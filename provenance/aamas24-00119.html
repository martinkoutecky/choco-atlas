  • Every bundle has positive budget, and every coordinate of every default is positive.
  • Every bundle has size at most three.
  • Every type has at most three nonsingleton bundles.

Thus every resolved profile in these instances is strictly positive. The masses have polynomial encoding length but need not belong to a fixed finite set.

I also prove an exact polynomial-time result:

Neither theorem settles the requested general classification.

1. Existence, masses, and rational answers

Let $K$ denote the set of nonnegative profiles satisfying all bundle budgets. The defaults give an element of $K$. Because the bundles partition the candidates and their budgets sum to one, the row-sum constraints follow from the bundle constraints.

The set $K$ is compact and convex. Define $F:K\to K$ by the resolution rule. For each positive-budget bundle,

$$ \left\|\mathbf d_{t,S}+w_{t,S}x_{\delta(t,S),S}\right\|_1 = b_{t,S}+w_{t,S}\sum_{c\in S}x_{\delta(t,S),c} \ge b_{t,S}>0. $$

Consequently, $F$ is continuous. Its output is nonnegative and has the prescribed total on each bundle. Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem therefore supplies a resolved profile.

The masses do not appear in $F$. Relabeling the $\tau$ support entries as $\tau$ voters leaves the resolution equations unchanged; no expansion according to mass denominators is required.

Short rational strong approximations exist. Given a resolved profile, choose an integer $r$ with $m2^{-r}\le\epsilon$. In each bundle, round all but one coordinate down to multiples of $2^{-r}$, and assign the last coordinate the exact budget remainder. The remainder is nonnegative. Its error is at most $(|S|-1)2^{-r}$, and the other errors are below $2^{-r}$. The resulting profile has polynomial encoding length and respects budgets exactly.

Finally, aggregation is exact rational arithmetic. If $\widehat x$ approximates $x^*$ as required, then

$$ \left|\widehat z_c-\sum_t\mu_t x^*_{t,c}\right| \le \sum_t\mu_t|\widehat x_{t,c}-x^*_{t,c}| \le\epsilon. $$

2. A bounded-use primitive circuit problem

Write

$$ [u]_+=\max\{0,u\}, \qquad \operatorname{clip}(u)=\min\{1,\max\{0,u\}\}. $$

Consider circuits with variables in $[0,1]$, exactly two constant nodes of values $1/4$ and $1/2$, and primitive gates

$$ N(u)=1-u, \qquad R(u,v)=[1-u-v]_+. $$

Every nonconstant node has exactly one producing gate. All nodes within a primitive gate are distinct. Constants may be used arbitrarily often.

Lemma 1. Finding an assignment satisfying every primitive gate within additive error $\delta=1/128$ is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hard, even when each nonconstant node is an input to at most two primitive gates.

2.1 Source problem

We use the following established result: Pure-Circuit remains $\mathrm{PPAD}$-complete with gates NOR, PURIFY, and COPY, even when every node is the input of exactly one gate. Each node has exactly one producing gate, and nodes within a gate are distinct. This is the choice $(X,Y)=(\mathrm{COPY},\mathrm{NOR})$ in [Pure-Circuit, Corollary 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.15149).

Its labels are $\{0,1,\bot\}$. NOR must output $1$ on two zero inputs and $0$ if either input is $1$. PURIFY copies a pure input to both outputs and always has at least one pure output. COPY copies a pure input. These conditions are precisely those used below.

2.2 Doubling and ramps

The function $D(u)=\min\{1,2u\}$ has the implementation

$$ a=N(u),\qquad b=N(a),\qquad c=R(u,b),\qquad o=N(c). $$

At exact gate values, $b=u$ and $o=D(u)$.

When every primitive gate has error at most $\delta$, the errors of $a,b,c,o$ relative to their ideal values at the actual input $u$ are at most

$$ \delta,\qquad 2\delta,\qquad 3\delta,\qquad 4\delta. $$

Indeed, $N$ is one-Lipschitz, and changing either argument of $R$ changes its output by at most the change in that argument.

For either exact constant $t\in\{1/4,1/2\}$, implement

$$ T_t(u)=D\bigl(D(R(N(u),t))\bigr) =\operatorname{clip}(4(u-t)). $$

The initial $N,R$ pair has error at most $2\delta$. Since $D$ is two-Lipschitz and its implementation adds at most $4\delta$, the two doublings yield error bounds

$$ 8\delta,\qquad 20\delta. $$

Thus each ramp approximates $T_t$ within $20\delta$. The ideal ramp is four-Lipschitz.

2.3 Simulating the source gates

Decode a real variable using

$$ \ell(u)= \begin{cases} 0,&u\le1/4,\\ 1,&u\ge3/4,\\ \bot,&1/4<u<3/4. \end{cases} $$

Implement NOR by $T_{1/4}(R(u,v))$. Its ideal output is

$$ \operatorname{clip}(3-4(u+v)), $$

and its error is at most

$$ 20\delta+4\delta=24\delta=\frac3{16}<\frac14. $$

If both inputs decode to zero, $u+v\le1/2$, so the ideal output is one. If either decodes to one, $u+v\ge3/4$, so the ideal output is zero. The error bound preserves the required decoded outputs.

Implement PURIFY by

$$ T_{1/4}(u),\qquad T_{1/2}(u). $$

For $u\le1/4$, both ideal outputs are zero; for $u\ge3/4$, both are one. For every $u$, at least one ideal output is an endpoint: the second is zero when $u\le1/2$, and the first is one when $u\ge1/2$. Since

$$ 20\delta=\frac5{32}<\frac14, $$

the required decoded properties hold.

Implement COPY by $T_{1/4}(u)$. Its ideal output is zero for $u\le1/4$ and one for $u\ge3/4$, and the same error bound applies.

All internal nodes are fresh. Every ramp uses the same two designated constant nodes; no additional constant nodes are introduced. Each source output is the terminal node of its corresponding gadget.

These arguments use actual input values locally, so they remain valid in cyclic circuits.

2.4 Bounding the number of uses

Each original source node is consumed by exactly one source gate. Its primitive uses are:

  • One use for a NOR or COPY simulation.
  • Two uses for a PURIFY simulation, one at the start of each ramp.

Inside a doubling gadget, its input is used twice, and every other internal node is used once. Between successive gadgets, a node is used at most twice. Therefore every nonconstant primitive node is an input to at most two primitive gates.

The construction has linear size. Decoding any valid primitive assignment solves the source instance, proving Lemma 1.

3. Reusing candidates through a coloring

Construct an undirected graph on the nonconstant primitive nodes. Join two nodes when they occur together in a primitive gate.

A node has at most two neighbors from its producing gate. It occurs as an input in at most two gates, each contributing at most two further neighbors. Hence its degree is at most six.

Greedy coloring therefore gives colors $1,\ldots,7$: when a vertex is colored, at most six colors are forbidden. Assign the two constant nodes colors $8$ and $9$.

Let $\kappa(i)$ denote a node’s color. All nodes within every primitive gate have distinct colors. In particular, if $J_i$ is the input set of nonconstant node $i$, then the colors in $\{i\}\cup J_i$ are pairwise distinct.

Introduce exactly the candidates

$$ C=\{a_h,c_h:1\le h\le9\}\cup\{*\}. $$

For convenience, write

$$ a_i=a_{\kappa(i)},\qquad c_i=c_{\kappa(i)}. $$

Different circuit nodes may now refer to the same candidate pair. Within each gate, however, the relevant pairs are disjoint. This local disjointness is all the society construction needs.

4. Constructing the positive society

$$ B=2^{-10},\qquad H=\frac14,\qquad W=2^{20}, \qquad \gamma=2^{-64}, \qquad k=\frac12-\frac HW. $$

Also set

$$ K=k+\frac HW=\frac12, \qquad \eta=\frac BW=2^{-30}, $$

$$ \alpha=\frac{BH}{B+K}=\frac1{2052}, \qquad \rho=\frac K{B+K}=\frac{512}{513}, \qquad \theta=\frac W{1+W}. $$

Every delegation weight is $W$.

Create a signal type $Q_i$ for every circuit node, and a feedback type $P_i$ for every nonconstant node.

4.1 Feedback types

For nonconstant node $i$, type $P_i$ has:

  • Self-delegated singleton $\{c_i\}$ with budget $k$.
  • A self-delegated singleton $\{*\}$, initially with budget $1-B-k$.

4.2 Signal types

For nonconstant node $i$, type $Q_i$ has:

  • Bundle $\{a_i,c_i\}$, budget $H$, delegate $P_i$, and default $(\gamma H,(1-\gamma)H)$.
  • For each $j\in J_i$, bundle $\{a_j,c_j\}$, budget $H$, delegate $Q_j$, and the same default.
  • A self-delegated singleton $\{*\}$, initially with budget $1-(|J_i|+1)H$.

For a constant node of value $t\in\{1/4,1/2\}$, type $Q_i$ instead has self-delegated singleton budgets

$$ \alpha t,\qquad H-\alpha t,\qquad 1-H $$

on $a_i,c_i,*$.

4.3 Positive padding

For each type, put every unused candidate in a self-delegated singleton of budget

$$ \zeta=\frac1{16\cdot19}. $$

Subtract the total padding budget from that type’s singleton budget on $*$.

The initial budget on $*$ is at least $1/4$ for every type. Padding costs at most $19\zeta=1/16$, so its final budget is positive.

Every singleton default equals its budget. Thus every bundle budget and every default coordinate is positive.

4.4 Validity and encoding

The coloring ensures that each type’s specified bundles are disjoint. Padding completes the partition. Budgets sum to one, and every nonsingleton delegates to another type. All self-delegations are singletons.

Feedback bundles have size at most three. Signal types have at most three nonsingleton bundles, each of size two. Since the candidate count is fixed, the possible padding counts are fixed. Consequently, all budgets, default entries, and weights range over fixed finite rational sets.

With $n$ circuit nodes, there are at most $2n$ types. Assign positive dyadic masses as follows. For

$$ D=2^{\lceil\log_2\tau\rceil}, $$

The entire construction is polynomial-time.

5. Every resolved profile approximately evaluates the circuit

Fix any resolved profile. For a nonconstant node $i$, abbreviate

$$ p=x_{P_i,a_i},\qquad q=q_i=x_{Q_i,a_i},\qquad X=\sum_{j\in J_i}x_{Q_i,a_j}. $$

Every signal type gives total support $H$ to its own pair, including constant types.

The feedback and signal equations are

$$ p=B\frac{(1-\gamma)\eta+q}{\eta+q+X}, \qquad q=H\frac{p+\gamma H/W}{K+p}. $$

For every copied input pair,

$$ x_{Q_i,a_j} = \theta q_j+\frac{\gamma H}{1+W}. $$

Budget feasibility gives

$$ 0\le p\le B,\qquad 0\le q\le H,\qquad 0\le X\le2H. $$

5.1 A perturbed quadratic equation

Rewrite the first two equations as

$$ p=B\frac{\eta+q}{\eta+q+X}+v, \qquad q=\frac{Hp}{K+p}+u, $$

where

$$ |v|\le\gamma B\le\gamma, \qquad |u|\le\frac{\gamma H^2}{WK}\le\gamma. $$

Because $Hp/(K+p)$ is increasing in $p$,

$$ q\le\frac{HB}{K+B}+\gamma=\alpha+\gamma. $$

Set $A=\eta+q+X$. Cross-multiplication gives

$$ pA=B(\eta+q)+vA, \qquad Kq=p(H-q)+u(K+p). $$

Multiplying the second identity by $A$ and using the first yields

$$ (B+K)q^2+ \bigl((B+K)\eta+KX-BH\bigr)q-BH\eta =E, $$

where

$$ E=vA(H-q)+uA(K+p). $$

Here $A\le\eta+3H<1$, and $H+K+B<1$. Therefore

$$ |E| \le\gamma A\bigl((H-q)+(K+p)\bigr) \le\gamma. $$

Define

$$ z_i=\frac{q_i}{\alpha}, \qquad \lambda=\frac{\eta}{\alpha}=\frac{513}{2^{28}}, \qquad h=\frac{\rho X}{\alpha}. $$

Dividing the quadratic identity by $(B+K)\alpha^2$ gives

$$ \left|z_i^2+(\lambda+h-1)z_i-\lambda\right| \le\frac{\gamma}{(B+K)\alpha^2}. $$

5.2 Controlling the positive root

Let $r$ be the positive root of

$$ P(z)=z^2+(\lambda+h-1)z-\lambda. $$

Because $P(0)=-\lambda<0$ and $P(1)=h\ge0$, we have $0<r\le1$. The other root is $-\lambda/r$. Thus, for $z_i\ge0$,

$$ |z_i-r| = \frac{|P(z_i)|}{z_i+\lambda/r} \le \frac{|P(z_i)|}{\lambda}. $$

Using

$$ (B+K)\alpha^2\lambda=BH\eta=2^{-42}, $$

we obtain

$$ |z_i-r|\le2^{42}\gamma. $$

The root equation can also be written

$$ r\bigl(r-(1-h)\bigr)=\lambda(1-r). $$

If $h\le1$, this implies $r\ge1-h$. Put $e=r-(1-h)$. Then $0\le e\le r$, so

$$ e^2\le re=\lambda(1-r)\le\lambda. $$

If $h\ge1$, the same equation gives

$$ r^2\le r(r+h-1)=\lambda(1-r)\le\lambda. $$

Hence, in both cases,

$$ 0\le r-[1-h]_+\le\sqrt{\lambda}. $$

5.3 Clipped signals and gate errors

Define $\xi_i=\operatorname{clip}(z_i)$ for every node; constant nodes have $z_i=\xi_i=t$ exactly.

Clipping fixes $r\in[0,1]$ and is one-Lipschitz. Therefore

$$ |\xi_i-[1-h]_+| \le2^{42}\gamma+\sqrt{\lambda}. $$

The bound $q_i\le\alpha+\gamma$ implies

$$ 0\le z_i-\xi_i\le\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}. $$

The copy equations give

$$ h = \rho\theta\sum_{j\in J_i}z_j + \frac{\rho|J_i|\gamma H}{(1+W)\alpha}. $$

Since $|J_i|\le2$ and $0\le\xi_j\le1$,

$$ \left|h-\sum_{j\in J_i}\xi_j\right| \le 2(1-\rho\theta)+\frac{4\gamma}{\alpha}. $$

Positive part is one-Lipschitz. Combining the estimates proves that every nonconstant node satisfies

$$ \left| \xi_i-\left[1-\sum_{j\in J_i}\xi_j\right]_+ \right| \le \sigma+2^{42}\gamma+\frac{4\gamma}{\alpha}, $$

where

$$ \sigma=\sqrt{\lambda}+2(1-\rho\theta). $$

This conclusion holds for every resolved profile.

6. Decoding a strong-approximation answer

$$ \widehat\xi_i = \operatorname{clip} \left(\frac{\widehat x_{Q_i,a_i}}{\alpha}\right). $$

For the clipped signals $\xi_i$ of $x^*$,

$$ |\widehat\xi_i-\xi_i|\le\frac{\epsilon}{\alpha}. $$

Constant nodes remain exact because their singleton budgets are respected exactly.

Each gate has at most two inputs. Consequently,

$$ \left| \widehat\xi_i- \left[1-\sum_{j\in J_i}\widehat\xi_j\right]_+ \right| \le \sigma+2^{42}\gamma+\frac{4\gamma}{\alpha} +\frac{3\epsilon}{\alpha}. $$

The constants satisfy

$$ \sqrt{\lambda}<\frac1{512}, $$

because $513/2^{28}<1/512^2$, and

$$ 2(1-\rho\theta) \le2(1-\rho)+2(1-\theta) < \frac1{256}+\frac1{2^{19}}. $$

$$ 2^{42}\gamma+\frac{4\gamma}{\alpha} = \frac{2^{42}+8208}{2^{64}} < 2^{-21}<\frac1{2048}. $$

Because every default coordinate is positive, each resolved coordinate is positive as well: its resolution numerator contains a positive default term, and its denominator is positive.

7. An exact polynomial-time class

Assume that for every nonsingleton bundle $S$ of type $t$, the set $S$ is a union of bundles in the partition of $u=\delta(t,S)$.

For every budget-feasible profile, the delegate’s total on $S$ is then the fixed rational number

$$ D_{t,S} = \sum_{\substack{R\in\mathcal S_u\\R\subseteq S}}b_{u,R}. $$

Hence the resolution equation becomes affine:

$$ x_{t,c} = \frac{b_{t,S}d_{t,S,c}}{b_{t,S}+w_{t,S}D_{t,S}} + \frac{b_{t,S}w_{t,S}}{b_{t,S}+w_{t,S}D_{t,S}} x_{u,c}. $$

We show that the resulting system has a unique solution.

For every coordinate in a positive-budget bundle, normalize by

$$ y_{t,c}=\frac{x_{t,c}}{b_{t,S_t(c)}}. $$

Singleton coordinates have $y_{t,c}=1$, irrespective of their delegate, because their budget fixes their only coordinate. Zero-budget coordinates are fixed at zero.

For a nonsingleton coordinate, let $R$ be the delegate’s bundle containing $c$. The union assumption implies $R\subseteq S$. If $b_{u,R}>0$, the normalized equation is

$$ y_{t,c} = \frac{d_{t,S,c}}{b_{t,S}+w_{t,S}D_{t,S}} + \beta_{t,c}y_{u,c}, $$

where

$$ \beta_{t,c} = \frac{w_{t,S}b_{u,R}} {b_{t,S}+w_{t,S}D_{t,S}} <1. $$

The strict inequality follows from $b_{u,R}\le D_{t,S}$ and $b_{t,S}>0$. If $b_{u,R}=0$, the delegate coordinate is zero and the coefficient can be omitted.

Thus the normalized equations have the form

$$ y=a+Ay, $$

where each row of $A$ has at most one nonzero entry, and every entry lies in $[0,1)$. There are finitely many rows, so

$$ q:=\|A\|_\infty<1. $$

If $y,y'$ are two solutions, then

$$ \|y-y'\|_\infty = \|A(y-y')\|_\infty \le q\|y-y'\|_\infty, $$

which forces $y=y'$. Section 1 supplies a resolved profile, so this unique linear-system solution respects all budgets and nonnegativity.

All coefficients are rational with polynomial encoding length. Exact rational linear algebra therefore computes the unique solution in polynomial time. To justify the bit bound, clear denominators row by row. If the resulting integer entries have at most $B_0$ bits and there are $n_0$ variables, determinants and minors have at most

$$ O(n_0B_0+n_0\log n_0) $$

bits, by expanding into at most $n_0!$ products. Fraction-free Gaussian elimination uses polynomially many operations on such minors. Therefore the solution and its computation have polynomial bit complexity.

This proves Theorem B.

For $m\le2$, every nonsingleton bundle is $C$, which is a union of every delegate’s bundles. Thus all such instances are exactly polynomial-time solvable. For $\tau=1$, self-delegation forces every bundle to be a singleton, so the profile is also fixed directly.

8. A general exponential upper bound

Let $N=m\tau$. On the nonnegative budget-feasible domain, resolved profiles are characterized by the quadratic equations

$$ x_{t,c} \left( b_{t,S}+w_{t,S}\sum_{c'\in S}x_{\delta(t,S),c'} \right) = b_{t,S} \left(d_{t,S,c}+w_{t,S}x_{\delta(t,S),c}\right) $$

for every positive-budget bundle and $c\in S$. Their denominators are strictly positive, so clearing them introduces no extra solutions.

Together with budgets and nonnegativity, there are $O(N)$ constraints of degree at most two. Clearing rational coefficient denominators gives polynomial total coefficient encoding length.

The strong-approximation theorem for quantifier-free real polynomial formulas, stated as [Proposition 5 in the AAMAS paper](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p1029.pdf), finds a rational point within $h$ of some satisfying assignment in time

$$ (PD)^{O(N)}\operatorname{poly}(L_{\mathrm{coeff}},\log(1/h)), $$

where $P$ is the number of constraints and $D$ their maximum degree. The hypotheses above give the required bounds.

Set $h=\epsilon/(m+1)$. Repair the returned rational vector $y$ separately on each positive-budget bundle of size $a$ and budget $b$. Put

$$ r_c=\max\{0,y_c\},\qquad R=\sum_{c\in S}r_c. $$

If $R>0$, output $\widehat x_c=br_c/R$. For the same exact profile $x^*$ approximated by $y$,

$$ |r_c-x_c^*|\le h,\qquad |R-b|\le ah, $$

and consequently

$$ |\widehat x_c-x_c^*| \le h+\frac{r_c}{R}|b-R| \le(a+1)h\le\epsilon. $$

If $R=0$, every exact coordinate in this bundle is at most $h$, so $b\le ah$. Output the default on that bundle. Both default and exact coordinates lie in $[0,b]$, giving error at most $ah\le\epsilon$.

Set zero-budget coordinates to zero. This repair preserves every budget exactly and remains close to the same exact resolved profile. Aggregation then completes the requested output.

The resulting general bound is

$$ (m\tau)^{O(m\tau)} \operatorname{poly}(L,\log(1/\epsilon)). $$

9. What remains open

Theorem A shows that a general polynomial-time algorithm would give polynomial-time algorithms for all $\mathrm{PPAD}$ search problems, even through instances with a fixed candidate count and strictly positive data.

It does not establish $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions. Nor does the exponential upper bound establish the requested polynomial-time alternative. The construction does not bound the largest strongly connected component size $s$, so the lower bound creates no contradiction with the stated dependence on that parameter.
