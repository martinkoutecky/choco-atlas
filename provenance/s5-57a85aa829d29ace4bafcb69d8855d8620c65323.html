The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite candidate set \(C\), committee size \(k\), approval-type set \(T\), and rational distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), compute \(x\in[0,1]^C\) with \(\sum_{c\in C}x_c=k\) that is Pareto-optimal for \(u_t(x)=\sum_{c\in A_t}x_c\), satisfies fractional AJR+ via \(\frac{\sum_t\rho_tu_t(x)}{\|\rho\|}\geq k\|\rho\|\) for every nonzero \(\rho\leq\mu\) supported on types approving any \(c\) with \(x_c<1\), is fractionally core-stable against deviations of total mass at most \(k\|\rho\|\), and satisfies GRP through a maximum flow with source capacities \(k\mu_t\), approval arcs, and unit candidate capacities.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\) candidates, let \(1\leq k\leq m\) be an integer, and let each approval type \(t\in T\) have a nonempty set \(A_t\subseteq C\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval sets; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A fractional committee is \(x\in[0,1]^C\) with \(\sum_c x_c=k\), and \(u_t(x)=\sum_{c\in A_t}x_c\). Pareto optimality is with respect to all such committees and all support types.
A coalition is a real vector \(0\leq\rho\leq\mu\) with \(\|\rho\|:=\sum_t\rho_t>0\); thus it may contain only part of a type's mass.
Fractional AJR+ requires \(\sum_t\rho_tu_t(x)/\|\rho\|\geq k\|\rho\|\) for every candidate \(c\) with \(x_c<1\) and every coalition supported on \(\{t:c\in A_t\}\).
Fractional core stability means there are no coalition \(\rho\) and deviation \(y\in[0,1]^C\) with \(\sum_c y_c\leq k\|\rho\|\) and \(u_t(y)>u_t(x)\) for every \(t\) with \(\rho_t>0\).
GRP means that some maximum flow \(f\) in the network with arcs \(s\to t\) of capacity \(k\mu_t\), approval arcs \(t\to c\) of infinite capacity, and arcs \(c\to q\) of capacity \(1\), satisfies \(f(c,q)\leq x_c\) for every candidate.

Can an exact binary-rational committee satisfying Pareto optimality, fractional AJR+, fractional core stability, and GRP simultaneously be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite voter lists, simultaneous existence of all four properties is established by [Jin–Liu–Tao](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01830v1).
[Suzuki–Vollen](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14907v2) give a polynomial-time algorithm for GRP and Pareto optimality; [Kroer–Peters](https://www.dominik-peters.de/publications/lindahl.pdf) give polynomial-time computation of approximate capped Lindahl equilibria with arbitrary positive budgets. These results do not settle the exact combined search problem above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Carried over nonempty approval sets and an integer committee size between one and the number of candidates.
  • Normalized total society mass to one, as required by the stated distribution.
  • Defined coalition mass as the sum of its coordinates and allowed real partial participation within each type.
  • Interpreted the AJR+ witness as one candidate commonly approved by every participating type, rather than a union of constituencies of different candidates.
  • Specified capped fractional deviations and strict improvement for every participating type, matching weak core stability.
  • Specified that GRP requires coordinatewise domination of a maximum flow, allowing additional committee mass beyond the flow.
  • Fixed exact binary-rational output to make the computational question precise; approximation is not substituted for the requested exact guarantees.

2. Coalition reductions and existence in the support model

For $S\subseteq T$, write
$$ \mu(S):=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t, \qquad b_S:=k\mu(S). $$

2.1. Core blocking reduces to full type masses

Lemma 1. A committee $x$ is core stable precisely when, for every nonempty $S\subseteq T$, no deviation $y\in[0,1]^C$ satisfies
$$ \sum_c y_c\leq b_S, \qquad u_t(y)>u_t(x)\quad(t\in S). $$

Proof. If a coalition $\rho$ blocks, let $S=\{t:\rho_t>0\}$. The same deviation strictly improves every type in $S$, and
$$ \sum_c y_c\leq k\sum_t\rho_t\leq k\mu(S). $$
It therefore blocks the coalition containing the full mass of every type in $S$.

Conversely, any deviation in the statement blocks the admissible coalition with $\rho_t=\mu_t$ on $S$ and zero elsewhere. $\square$

2.2. AJR+ also reduces to full type masses

Lemma 2. Fractional AJR+ is equivalent to the following inequalities: for every candidate $c$ with $x_c<1$, and every nonempty $S\subseteq\{t:c\in A_t\}$,
$$ \sum_{t\in S}\mu_tu_t(x)\geq k\mu(S)^2. $$

Proof. For a coalition supported on types approving $c$, multiply its defining AJR+ inequality by its positive mass. The required condition becomes
$$ F_x(\rho):= \sum_t\rho_tu_t(x)-k\left(\sum_t\rho_t\right)^2 \geq0. $$
This function is concave on the box given by $0\leq\rho_t\leq\mu_t$: its first term is linear, and the negative quadratic term is concave.

Every point of the box is a convex combination of its vertices. Consequently, nonnegativity at every vertex implies nonnegativity everywhere. The zero vertex gives equality; every other vertex takes the full masses of a nonempty subset of approving types. These vertex conditions are exactly the displayed inequalities. Necessity follows by taking those coalitions. $\square$

2.3. Applicable existence theorem

The supplied finite-voter theorem states: for any finite list of $n$ equally weighted voters with nonempty approval sets, $m$ candidates, and integer $1\leq k\leq m$, there is a fractional committee satisfying Pareto optimality, fractional AJR+, strict-improvement fractional core stability, and GRP simultaneously. Its fractional AJR+ condition uses unsaturated candidates and arbitrary real entitlement levels; core coalition budgets are $k|V|/n$; GRP has source capacities $k/n$. [Jin–Liu–Tao, Theorem 3 and fractional definitions](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01830v1)

Here is the extension needed for the present model.

Choose a common denominator $n$ for the masses, and replace each type $t$ by $n\mu_t$ identical voters. These multiplicities are positive integers. Approval sets remain nonempty, and the candidate and committee-size hypotheses are unchanged, so the theorem applies.

Pareto dominance is unchanged by duplication. A support-model blocker, by Lemma 1, would give a blocking coalition consisting of all copies of its participating types.

For AJR+, take all copies of the types in $S$. The finite-voter condition gives
$$ \frac{\sum_{t\in S}\mu_tu_t(x)}{\mu(S)} \geq k\mu(S). $$
Lemma 2 extends these inequalities to arbitrary real partial masses.

Finally, voter flows aggregate to type flows. Conversely, divide a type’s outgoing flow equally among its $n\mu_t$ copies. Each copy then sends at most
$$ \frac{k\mu_t}{n\mu_t}=\frac{k}{n}. $$
Candidate loads are unchanged. Thus the two networks have equal maximum-flow values and identical GRP committees.

This proves real simultaneous existence in the support model. The expansion is used only for existence.

3. Exact aggregation of candidates

For each candidate $c$, define its approval signature
$$ B(c):=\{t\in T:c\in A_t\}. $$
Let $\mathcal B$ be the set of signatures that occur, and put
$$ C_B:=\{c:B(c)=B\}, \qquad n_B:=|C_B|, \qquad r:=|\mathcal B|. $$
Then
$$ r\leq\min\{m,2^\tau\}. $$
The empty signature is included if unapproved candidates exist.

For a committee $x$, define
$$ z_B:=\sum_{c\in C_B}x_c. $$
The aggregate feasible set is
$$ P:= \left\{ z\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal B}: 0\leq z_B\leq n_B,\ \sum_Bz_B=k \right\}, $$
and type utilities become
$$ U_t(z):=\sum_{B:t\in B}z_B. $$

Every $z\in P$ has a realization, for example
$$ x_c=\frac{z_B}{n_B}\quad(c\in C_B). $$

Lemma 3. All four required properties depend only on $z$. In particular, an aggregate solution can be lifted uniformly by the preceding formula.

Proof.

For Pareto optimality, the attainable utility vectors are exactly
$$ \{(U_t(z))_{t\in T}:z\in P\}. $$
Thus Pareto dominance is identical before and after aggregation.

For core stability, aggregate deviations satisfy
$$ 0\leq Y_B\leq n_B, \qquad \sum_BY_B\leq b_S. $$
Every such deviation can be realized uniformly within each signature class, preserving both its cost and every type’s utility. Lemma 1 therefore applies in aggregate coordinates.

For AJR+, all candidates in $C_B$ have the same approving types. Moreover,
$$ z_B=n_B \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_c=1\text{ for every }c\in C_B. $$
If $z_B<n_B$, at least one candidate in the class is unsaturated, and its AJR+ conditions are the same as those of every other unsaturated candidate in the class. By Lemma 2, the exact aggregate conditions are
$$ \sum_{t\in S}\mu_tU_t(z)\geq k\mu(S)^2 \quad \text{for every }B\text{ with }z_B<n_B \text{ and every nonempty }S\subseteq B. $$

For GRP, merge the candidate nodes in $C_B$ into a node of capacity $n_B$. A type $t$ has an approval arc to this node precisely when $t\in B$. Original flows aggregate to merged flows. Conversely, a merged flow can be divided uniformly among the $n_B$ candidates, showing equality of the unrestricted maximum-flow values.

For domination by an arbitrary realization $x$ of $z$, suppose merged approval flows are $h_{tB}$ and
$$ \sum_{t\in B}h_{tB}\leq z_B. $$
When $z_B>0$, send
$$ h_{tB}\frac{x_c}{z_B} $$
from type $t$ to candidate $c\in C_B$. The candidate load is at most $x_c$, and the total outgoing flow of each type is unchanged. When $z_B=0$, all corresponding $h_{tB}$ are zero. Aggregation gives the converse implication. This proves the GRP assertion. $\square$

4. A finite, parameter-bounded description of core stability

Fix nonempty $S\subseteq T$. Define
$$ \Delta_S(z):= \max\left\{ \delta: 0\leq Y_B\leq n_B,\ \sum_BY_B\leq b_S,\ U_t(Y)-\delta\geq U_t(z)\quad(t\in S) \right\}. $$

The optimum exists and is finite: equivalently, it maximizes the continuous function
$$ \min_{t\in S}\bigl(U_t(Y)-U_t(z)\bigr) $$
over a nonempty compact deviation set. Because $S$ is finite, Lemma 1 implies that $S$ blocks exactly when $\Delta_S(z)>0$.

The dual feasible polyhedron is
$$ D_S:= \left\{ (\lambda,\alpha,w)\geq0: \sum_{t\in S}\lambda_t=1,\ \alpha+w_B\geq\sum_{t\in S\cap B}\lambda_t \quad(B\in\mathcal B) \right\}, $$
with objective
$$ b_S\alpha+\sum_Bn_Bw_B-\sum_{t\in S}\lambda_tU_t(z). $$

Indeed, the primal Lagrangian is
$$ \delta +\sum_{t\in S}\lambda_t\bigl(U_t(Y)-U_t(z)-\delta\bigr) +\alpha\left(b_S-\sum_BY_B\right) +\sum_Bw_B(n_B-Y_B). $$
Taking its supremum over unrestricted $\delta$ and $Y\geq0$ gives the normalization and inequalities defining $D_S$, together with the stated objective.

The primal is feasible with finite optimum. The dual is nonempty: any probability vector $\lambda$, together with $\alpha=1$ and $w=0$, is feasible. Rational linear-programming strong duality therefore applies.

Let $V_S$ be the vertex set of $D_S$. The dual optimum is attained at a vertex. To justify this despite possible unboundedness, its nonempty optimal face lies in the nonnegative orthant. Minimize the sum of coordinates on that face. A sublevel set bounded by any feasible point’s coordinate sum is compact, so the minimum is attained. Its minimizing face is compact and has an extreme point. That point is a vertex of the optimal face and hence of $D_S$.

Consequently,
$$ \Delta_S(z)= \min_{v\in V_S} \left( b_S\alpha_v+\sum_Bn_Bw_{vB} -\sum_{t\in S}\lambda_{vt}U_t(z) \right). $$
Thus $S$ does not block precisely when at least one $v\in V_S$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{t\in S}\lambda_{vt}U_t(z) \geq b_S\alpha_v+\sum_Bn_Bw_{vB}. $$

This is a disjunction of rational linear inequalities.

Enumerating the vertices

Write $s=|S|$. The polyhedron $D_S$ has
$$ d_S=s+r+1 $$
variables, and
$$ H_S=s+2r+2 $$
defining constraint hyperplanes, counting nonnegativity, the $r$ approval inequalities, and the normalization.

At a vertex, the active constraint normals span the ambient space. Otherwise a nonzero direction orthogonal to all active normals would permit sufficiently small movements in both directions, contradicting extremality. Therefore each vertex is obtained by solving some full-rank system of $d_S$ active equations.

Enumerate all such systems, solve them exactly, and retain the feasible solutions. This lists all vertices and gives
$$ |V_S|\leq H_S^{d_S}. $$

All defining coefficients and right-hand sides belong to $\{-1,0,1\}$. Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound
$$ |\det M|\leq d_S^{d_S/2} $$
give polynomial encoding length for vertex coordinates. Since $r\leq m$, this bound is polynomial in $m+\tau$.

Since also $r\leq2^\tau$, the number of systems to enumerate is bounded solely by a function of $\tau$.

5. Pareto optimality can be handled by enumerating faces

A coordinate pattern assigns each $B\in\mathcal B$ one of three statuses: fixed to zero, fixed to $n_B$, or free. Let $F$ be the corresponding closed face of $P$.

Lemma 4. If $z^0\in F$ is Pareto optimal and every free coordinate satisfies
$$ 0<z^0_B<n_B, $$
then every point of $F$ is Pareto optimal.

Proof. Suppose $z\in F$ is dominated by $y\in P$. For sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, put
$$ w=z^0+\varepsilon(y-z). $$
Its coordinate sum is $k$.

At a coordinate fixed to zero, $w_B=\varepsilon y_B$; at a coordinate fixed to $n_B$,
$$ w_B=n_B+\varepsilon(y_B-n_B). $$
Both respect their bounds for $\varepsilon\leq1$. Every free coordinate of $z^0$ lies strictly inside its bounds, so sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ preserves those bounds too. Hence $w\in P$.

Linearity gives
$$ U_t(w)-U_t(z^0) = \varepsilon\bigl(U_t(y)-U_t(z)\bigr). $$
Every difference is nonnegative and at least one is positive, contradicting Pareto optimality of $z^0$. $\square$

There are at most $3^r$ patterns. For each, its required interior point can be found explicitly.

Let $J$ be its free coordinates, and define
$$ R:=k-\sum_{B\text{ fixed upper}}n_B, \qquad N_J:=\sum_{B\in J}n_B. $$
If $J\neq\varnothing$, such an interior point exists exactly when $0<R<N_J$. In that case take
$$ z^0_B=\frac{Rn_B}{N_J}\quad(B\in J), $$
with the prescribed fixed coordinates. If $J=\varnothing$, the pattern is feasible exactly when $R=0$.

Test Pareto optimality of $z^0$ by the rational LP
$$ \max_{y\in P,\ U_t(y)\geq U_t(z^0)\ \forall t} \sum_t\bigl(U_t(y)-U_t(z^0)\bigr). $$
Its optimum is nonnegative because $z^0$ is feasible. It is positive exactly when a Pareto improvement exists. Retain precisely the patterns for which the optimum is zero.

Every retained face consists entirely of Pareto-optimal points, by Lemma 4.

6. The exact algorithm

Compute the maximum-flow value $\nu$ in the aggregated network of Section 3. Infinite approval capacities may be replaced by $k$, because the total source capacity is $k$.

Enumerate each retained Pareto face $F$. For every nonempty $S\subseteq T$, choose one vertex $v_S\in V_S$, enumerating all combinations of these choices.

For each combination, solve the following rational linear feasibility problem in variables $z_B$ and $h_{tB}$, where $h_{tB}$ exists only for $t\in B$.

First require
$$ z\in F. $$

For every signature $B$ that is not fixed to its upper bound by the pattern, impose all AJR+ inequalities
$$ \sum_{t\in S}\mu_tU_t(z)\geq k\mu(S)^2 \quad(\varnothing\neq S\subseteq B). $$

For every nonempty $S\subseteq T$, impose its chosen core inequality
$$ \sum_{t\in S}\lambda_{v_St}U_t(z) \geq b_S\alpha_{v_S}+\sum_Bn_Bw_{v_SB}. $$

Finally impose the GRP flow constraints
$$ h_{tB}\geq0, \qquad \sum_{B:t\in B}h_{tB}\leq k\mu_t, $$
$$ \sum_{t\in B}h_{tB}\leq z_B, \qquad \sum_{B}\sum_{t\in B}h_{tB}=\nu. $$

Stop at a feasible branch, obtain a rational vertex of its feasible polytope, and return
$$ x_c=\frac{z_B}{n_B}\quad(c\in C_B). $$

6.1. Soundness

Every returned aggregate committee lies in a retained face, so it is Pareto optimal.

If $z_B<n_B$, that signature cannot have been fixed upper. Its AJR+ inequalities were therefore imposed. Lemma 2 proves AJR+.

For each nonempty $S$, the imposed dual inequality certifies $\Delta_S(z)\leq0$. Lemma 1 proves core stability.

The flow constraints exhibit a feasible flow of value $\nu$ dominated by $z$. Hence they certify GRP.

Lemma 3 transfers all four properties to the returned committee.

6.2. Completeness

Take a real simultaneous committee, whose existence was proved in Section 2, and aggregate it to $z^\star$.

Choose the coordinate pattern that fixes exactly the coordinates of $z^\star$ equal to zero or $n_B$. Its other coordinates are strictly interior.

This pattern is retained by the Pareto test. Indeed, Lemma 4 applies with $z^\star$ as the interior Pareto-optimal point, and shows that the test point $z^0$ is Pareto optimal.

Whenever the pattern does not fix $B$ upper, $z^\star_B<n_B$, so all imposed AJR+ inequalities hold at $z^\star$.

For each $S$, core stability and Section 4 provide a vertex $v_S$ whose inequality holds at $z^\star$. The algorithm enumerates this combination.

Finally, GRP supplies the required flow variables. Thus at least one enumerated branch is feasible.

6.3. Rational encoding length

Each feasible branch is bounded: the $z_B$ have their box bounds, and the nonnegative flow variables have total sum $\nu\leq k$. It therefore has a rational vertex.

The number of variables is at most
$$ r+\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}|B| \leq m+m\tau. $$
All coefficients have polynomial encoding length in $m,\tau,L$:

  • Core dual vertices have polynomial encoding length by Section 4.
  • Each $\mu(S)$ has polynomial encoding length, using a common denominator formed from the input denominators.
  • Squaring $\mu(S)$ and multiplying by $k$ preserves polynomial encoding length.
  • The value $\nu$ is a minimum-cut capacity, hence a sum of input capacities and integer signature capacities.

A vertex is determined by a full-rank system of active constraints in the polynomial number of variables. Clearing denominators and applying Cramer’s rule gives polynomial encoding length for its coordinates, independently of the total number of constraints. Division by $n_B\leq m$ preserves that bound.

Thus the returned committee has total encoding length polynomial in $m,\tau,L$. This also reproves the general short-output existence result.

6.4. Running time

For every nonempty $S$,
$$ |V_S| \leq (\tau+2r+2)^{\tau+r+1}. $$
There are $2^\tau-1$ such subsets. Consequently, the number of face-and-vertex-choice branches is at most
$$ 3^r \left( (\tau+2r+2)^{\tau+r+1} \right)^{2^\tau-1}. $$
Using $r\leq2^\tau$, its logarithm is
$$ O(\tau4^\tau). $$

The vertex-enumeration preprocessing is smaller than this bound. Each branch contains at most
$$ r2^\tau+2^\tau+O(\tau r) $$
constraints, apart from constant-factor bookkeeping, and has polynomial-bit rational data. Exact rational linear programming therefore costs a function of $\tau$ times a polynomial in $m,L$ per branch.

Constructing signatures, computing $\nu$, testing faces, and expanding the output have the same form of bound. Altogether,
$$ \boxed{ 2^{O(\tau4^\tau)}\operatorname{poly}(m,L) } $$
time suffices.

7. What remains open

The proved algorithm handles arbitrary overlaps when the support size is fixed. It eliminates any need to expand binary-encoded masses into individual voters.

For unrestricted $\tau$, however, it still enumerates a separate dual certificate for every type coalition. Neither candidate aggregation nor the short-output bound removes this dependence.

A complete classification still requires a polynomial-time construction for unrestricted support size, or a hardness reduction for the simultaneous search problem. No such construction or reduction is established here.
