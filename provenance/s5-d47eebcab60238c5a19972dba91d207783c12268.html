The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) candidates, committee size \(k\), and a unit-mass distribution \(\mu\) over approval types \(t\subseteq C\), represented by \(\tau\) positive binary-rational masses, can a deterministic anonymous rule compute a size-\(k\) committee in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and satisfy both of these conditions? First, continuum JR: for every \(x_t\le\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t x_t\ge1/k\), if all types with positive \(x_t\) share an approved candidate, the committee contains a candidate approved by at least one such type. Second, for every decomposition into known masses, a designated focal atom of mass \(\epsilon\) and true type \(T\), and unknown mass \(\rho\), no report \(R\) is both safe across every allocation of the unknown mass and profitable for at least one allocation whenever \(\rho\ge7\epsilon\). A report transfers the focal mass from \(T\) to \(R\), and utility is \(|W\cap T|\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society \(\mu\) has total mass \(1\) and is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not \(2^m\), and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A deterministic anonymous rule \(F(C,k,\mu)\) selects exactly \(k\) candidates and depends only on aggregate type masses; no atom identities or decomposition are supplied to it. Candidate labels may resolve ties.
Continuum JR requires that every real vector \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t x_t\ge1/k\) and \(\bigcap_{t:x_t>0}t\ne\varnothing\) satisfy \(F(C,k,\mu)\cap\bigcup_{t:x_t>0}t\ne\varnothing\).
For the incentive condition, fix positive rational \(\epsilon\), rational \(\rho\ge7\epsilon\), and known nonnegative rational type masses \(D\) totaling \(1-\epsilon-\rho\ge0\). Unknown completions are every nonnegative rational type-mass vector \(U\) totaling \(\rho\), with unrestricted support.

The required guarantee forbids \(\Delta(U)\ge0\) for every completion together with \(\Delta(U)>0\) for some completion, for every such \(D,\epsilon,\rho,T,R\).

Does this committee-computation problem admit a deterministic anonymous rule satisfying both guarantees in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) (with \(k\) part of the input), or is computation under these simultaneous guarantees NP-hard?

Result

A deterministic anonymous polynomial-time rule exists. In fact, the construction below satisfies the incentive guarantee whenever the unknown mass is positive: the assumption \(\rho\ge 7\epsilon\) is unnecessary.

More precisely, the rule satisfies

\[ \Delta(U^\ast)>0\text{ for some completion }U^\ast \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Delta(U)<0\text{ for some completion }U. \]

The construction uses exact rational masses to encode two committee proposals. Each proposal is accepted only after a polynomial-time flow test certifies that it belongs to a suitable family of JR committees.

The fundability viewpoint also appears in [Ai and Tao, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15370v2). Below, all properties needed for the rational-mass construction are proved explicitly.

If \(k=m\), select \(C\). Both guarantees are immediate. Henceforth assume \(k<m\), and put \(q=1/k\).

1. JR and fundability

For any nonnegative type-mass vector \(\lambda\), define

\(h_\lambda(B)=\sum_{t:t\cap B\ne\varnothing}\lambda_t.\)

A committee \(W\) satisfies continuum JR exactly when, for every candidate \(c\),

\(\sum_{t:c\in t,\ t\cap W=\varnothing}\mu_t<q.\)

Indeed, a violation of the displayed inequality supplies a cohesive unrepresented group by taking the full masses of the indicated types. Conversely, every JR-violating vector has a common candidate \(c\), and all its positive coordinates belong to those indicated types. This equivalence includes groups with arbitrary real submasses.

Call \(S\subseteq C\) fundable at \(\lambda\) if there are nonnegative assignments \(z_{t,c}\), supported on approval edges, such that

\(\sum_{c\in S}z_{t,c}\le\lambda_t\) for every type \(t\), and \(\sum_tz_{t,c}=q\) for every \(c\in S\).

Write \(\mathcal H(\lambda)\) for the family of fundable sets.

Construct a flow network with source-to-type capacities \(\lambda_t\), approval edges from types to candidates in \(S\), and candidate-to-sink capacities \(q\). Let \(r_\lambda(S)\) be its maximum flow value. Thus

\(S\in\mathcal H(\lambda)\) if and only if \(r_\lambda(S)=q|S|.\)

The max-flow/min-cut theorem gives

\(r_\lambda(S)=\min_{B\subseteq S}\bigl(q|S\setminus B|+h_\lambda(B)\bigr).\)

For example, approval-edge capacity \(2\) suffices when the total mass is at most \(1\): an optimal cut has capacity at most \(1\), so it cuts no approval edge. Consequently,

\(S\in\mathcal H(\lambda)\) if and only if \(h_\lambda(B)\ge q|B|\) for every \(B\subseteq S.\)

All these flow problems have rational capacities, so rational maximum flows exist.

Every fundable set at a society of total mass \(1\) has size at most \(k\). If the society has positive empty-type mass, every fundable set has size at most \(k-1\).

Lemma 1. Every inclusion-maximal fundable set \(Q\) satisfies continuum JR. Therefore every size-\(k\) extension of \(Q\) satisfies JR.

Proof. If \(Q\) failed JR, some candidate \(c\) would have at least \(q\) mass of approvers whose types are disjoint from \(Q\). None of that mass is used by a funding assignment for \(Q\). Assigning \(q\) of it to \(c\) would fund \(Q\cup\{c\}\), contradicting maximality. Adding candidates preserves JR. \(\square\)

Define

\(\partial\lambda=\{c:S\cup\{c\}\in\mathcal H(\lambda)\text{ for every }S\in\mathcal H(\lambda)\}.\)

Because fundability is closed under taking subsets,

\(\partial\lambda=\bigcap\{Q:Q\text{ is inclusion-maximal in }\mathcal H(\lambda)\}.\)

To verify the nontrivial inclusion, if \(S\cup\{c\}\) is not fundable, extend \(S\) to a maximal fundable set. That extension cannot contain \(c\).

Members of \(\partial\lambda\) can be added successively to any fundable set. In particular, \(\partial\lambda\) itself is fundable.

2. Reachable profiles and committee families

Allow the following operations on a society, each involving a positive rational amount of mass:

  • Replace empty-type mass by any approval type.
  • Replace mass of type \(S\) by empty-type mass when \(|S|\ge m-k+1\) and \(C\setminus S\) is fundable at the current society.

A society is reachable from another if a finite sequence of these operations produces it.

Let \(\mathcal R(\mu)\) consist of all size-\(k\) committees that extend an inclusion-maximal fundable set at some society reachable from \(\mu\).

This family is nonempty: use a maximal fundable set at \(\mu\) itself.

Lemma 2. Every committee in \(\mathcal R(\mu)\) satisfies JR at \(\mu\). Moreover, if \(\nu\) is reachable from \(\mu\), then \(\mathcal R(\nu)\subseteq\mathcal R(\mu)\).

Proof. For a fixed size-\(k\) committee, replacing empty mass by another type can only increase its unrepresented approval masses.

In the second operation, every size-\(k\) committee intersects \(S\), because \(|C\setminus S|\le k-1\). Consequently, that operation changes none of its unrepresented approval masses. Thus JR at a reachable endpoint implies JR at the starting society. Apply Lemma 1 at the endpoint.

The family inclusion follows by concatenating operation sequences. \(\square\)

Full-type mass can always be made empty, since its complement is the fundable empty set. Hence exchanging mass between the empty and full types gives mutually reachable profiles.

3. A polynomial description of reachability

The definition above quantifies over operation sequences. We now eliminate that quantification.

Let

\(\widehat\mu=\mu+\mu_\varnothing(\delta_C-\delta_\varnothing).\)

Define the following set of original types:

\(J=\{\varnothing,C\}\cup\{t\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu):|t|\ge m-k+1,\ C\setminus t\in\mathcal H(\widehat\mu)\}.\)

Set \(f_t=0\) for \(t\in J\), and \(f_t=\mu_t\) otherwise. Write

\(\eta=1-\sum_tf_t.\)

Lemma 3. The societies reachable from \(\mu\) are exactly the vectors \(f+\nu\), where \(\nu\) is any nonnegative rational type-mass vector totaling \(\eta\).

Proof. First observe that replacing mass of \(S\) by full-type mass preserves the entire fundability family whenever \(C\setminus S\) is fundable.

To prove this observation, use the support inequalities from Section 1. For any \(B\) intersecting \(S\), its support mass is unchanged. For any \(B\) disjoint from \(S\), its original support mass already satisfies \(h_\lambda(B)\ge q|B|\), because \(B\subseteq C\setminus S\). Thus every previously deficient support inequality remains deficient, and no new deficiency is introduced.

Starting at \(\mu\), first make all empty mass full, producing \(\widehat\mu\). Next, for every proper type in \(J\), make all its original mass empty and then full. These operations are allowed; the observation shows that each resulting full replacement preserves fundability, so the remaining complement tests stay valid.

This produces

\(\Omega=f+\eta\delta_C,\)

with \(\mathcal H(\Omega)=\mathcal H(\widehat\mu)\). From \(\Omega\), make its full mass empty and redistribute it arbitrarily. Hence every \(f+\nu\) in the statement is reachable.

For the converse, track the original mass outside \(J\) only as an auxiliary proof device. Until any of it changes, the current profile consists of that unchanged mass \(f\), together with arbitrarily reported mass totaling \(\eta\). Replacing the latter mass by full approvals can only enlarge fundability. Therefore every currently fundable set is fundable at \(\Omega\).

A first change to original mass of a type \(S\notin J\) would require \(|S|\ge m-k+1\) and fundability of \(C\setminus S\) at the current profile. That complement would then be fundable at \(\Omega\), hence at \(\widehat\mu\), implying \(S\in J\). This is a contradiction.

Thus the original mass \(f\) stays fixed throughout every reachable sequence. \(\square\)

Computing \(J,f,\eta\) requires at most \(\tau\) fundability tests.

Also, whenever two societies differ only in the allocation of mass between \(\varnothing\) and \(C\), they have the same \(\widehat\mu\), the same proper-type masses, and therefore the same \(f,\eta\).

4. Polynomially checkable committee certificates

For \(Q\subseteq C\), write \(r_f(Q)\) for the maximum flow funded by the fixed mass vector \(f\).

Lemma 4. A size-\(k\) committee \(W\) belongs to \(\mathcal R(\mu)\) exactly when some \(Q\subseteq W\) satisfies both

\(q|Q|-r_f(Q)\le\eta,\)

and

\(r_f(Q\cup\{c\})-r_f(Q)<q\quad\text{for every }c\in C\setminus Q.\)

Thus a supplied certificate \(Q\) can be verified with at most \(m+1\) maximum-flow computations.

Proof. Fix a maximum flow from \(f\) into \(Q\). Let \(a_c\) be its inflow to candidate \(c\). Supply free singleton mass \(q-a_c\) at each \(c\in Q\), and leave any remaining free mass empty. The first inequality makes this allocation possible, and \(Q\) is fundable.

At this singleton profile, \(Q\cup\{c\}\) is fundable exactly when

\(r_f(Q\cup\{c\})=r_f(Q)+q.\)

Here is a detailed justification. Starting with a maximum fixed-mass flow into \(Q\), augment in the network expanded by candidate \(c\). Augmenting paths never decrease candidate-to-sink flows already present. Their total on \(Q\) cannot exceed \(r_f(Q)\), so each of those flows remains unchanged. Thus an increase of \(q\) supplies all of candidate \(c\)'s quota while preserving the singleton completion of \(Q\).

Conversely, any funding of \(Q\cup\{c\}\) at the singleton profile needs at least \(r_f(Q)+q\) fixed-mass flow, since the total nonempty free mass is exactly \(q|Q|-r_f(Q)\). The fixed-mass flow cannot exceed \(r_f(Q)+q\): restricting it to \(Q\) gives at most \(r_f(Q)\), and candidate \(c\) receives at most \(q\). This proves the equivalence.

Consequently, the second set of inequalities makes \(Q\) maximal. Lemma 3 then establishes sufficiency.

For necessity, suppose \(Q\subseteq W\) is maximal fundable at a reachable profile \(f+\nu\). Separate the fixed and free portions of a funding flow for \(Q\). Replace each used free portion by the singleton candidate it funds, and make unused free portions empty. This only removes approval edges, so \(Q\) remains fundable and maximal.

Augment the fixed-mass flow into \(Q\) to a maximum flow. Whenever its inflow to a candidate increases, reduce the singleton mass funding that candidate by the same amount and make that freed mass empty. Again, only free approval edges are removed, and \(Q\) remains fundable and maximal.

The resulting nonempty free mass is \(q|Q|-r_f(Q)\), proving the first inequality. The equivalence proved above and maximality prove every second inequality. \(\square\)

5. Every possible gain admits a strict-loss pair

We first need a transfer property.

Lemma 5. Let \(A=H+\epsilon\delta_T\) and \(B=H+\epsilon\delta_R\). If \(T\subseteq\partial B\), then

\(T\subseteq\partial A\) and \(\mathcal H(A)\subseteq\mathcal H(B).\)

Proof. Take \(S\in\mathcal H(A)\). Funding \(S\setminus T\) uses none of the distinguished mass \(\epsilon\delta_T\), so that set remains fundable at \(B\). Since \(T\subseteq\partial B\), adding its members gives \(S\cup T\in\mathcal H(B)\).

This union is also fundable at \(A\). For a subset \(E\subseteq S\cup T\) disjoint from \(T\), the required support inequality follows from fundability of \(S\) at \(A\). For an \(E\) intersecting \(T\), its support mass at \(A\) is at least its support mass at \(B\): the distinguished truthful mass supports it, whether or not the reported mass does. The inequalities at \(B\) therefore imply those at \(A\).

Thus every fundable \(S\) at \(A\) can be extended by all of \(T\), proving \(T\subseteq\partial A\). Also, \(S\cup T\) is fundable at \(B\), implying the family inclusion. \(\square\)

Lemma 6. Fix \(D,\epsilon,\rho\) as in the question, except that merely \(\rho>0\) is required. Suppose some completion admits committees

\(W\in\mathcal R(D+\epsilon\delta_T+U^\ast)\) and \(V\in\mathcal R(D+\epsilon\delta_R+U^\ast)\)

with \(u_T(V)>u_T(W)\). Then there are committees

\(W'\in\mathcal R(D+\epsilon\delta_T+\rho\delta_\varnothing)\) and \(V'\in\mathcal R(D+\epsilon\delta_R+\rho\delta_\varnothing)\)

with \(u_T(W')>u_T(V')\).

Proof. Put

\(A=D+\epsilon\delta_T+\rho\delta_\varnothing,\qquad B=D+\epsilon\delta_R+\rho\delta_\varnothing.\)

Replacing their unknown empty mass by \(U^\ast\) is allowed. Lemma 2 therefore places the given gain pair in \(\mathcal R(A),\mathcal R(B)\).

The gain implies \(T\ne\varnothing\). Set

\(t=|T|,\qquad g=\max(0,k-m+t),\qquad v=\max_{Z\in\mathcal R(A)}u_T(Z).\)

Every size-\(k\) committee has utility at least \(g\). Since \(A\) has positive empty mass, a maximal fundable set at \(A\) has size at most \(k-1\); extend it by a member of \(T\) if necessary. Hence \(g\le v\le t\) and \(v\ge1\).

Suppose no strict-loss pair exists. Then every committee in \(\mathcal R(B)\) has utility at least \(v\). We derive a contradiction in each possible case.

Case \(g<v<t\). Choose a maximal fundable set \(Q\) at \(B\). It has size at most \(k-1\). The minimum utility among its size-\(k\) extensions is

\(\max(|Q\cap T|,g).\)

All those extensions belong to \(\mathcal R(B)\), so \(|Q\cap T|\ge v\).

The set \(Q\cap T\) remains fundable when the distinguished report \(R\) is replaced by \(T\): every funding edge into its candidates remains available. Extend it to a maximal fundable set \(Q'\) at \(A\), again of size at most \(k-1\).

If \(|Q'\cap T|>v\), any size-\(k\) extension contradicts the definition of \(v\). Otherwise, add a candidate in \(T\setminus Q'\), which exists because \(v<t\), and fill the committee. This also gives utility greater than \(v\), a contradiction.

Case \(v=t\). Every committee in \(\mathcal R(B)\) contains \(T\). At every profile \(B'\) reachable from \(B\), every maximal fundable set therefore contains \(T\). Indeed, the intersection of all size-\(k\) extensions of a set \(Q\) of size at most \(k\) is exactly \(Q\), since \(m>k\). Thus \(T\subseteq\partial B'\).

Consider any allowed sequence starting at \(A\). We can mirror it starting at \(B\), keeping the distinguished mass \(\epsilon\) unchanged on both sides. Inductively, at corresponding profiles \(A',B'\), Lemma 5 gives

\(T\subseteq\partial A'\) and \(\mathcal H(A')\subseteq\mathcal H(B').\)

The distinguished truthful mass cannot be removed: such removal would require \(C\setminus T\) fundable at \(A'\), and adding \(T\subseteq\partial A'\) would make \(C\) fundable. This is impossible because \(qm>1\).

Every operation on other mass can be mirrored. Empty mass is available on both sides, and a complement fundable at \(A'\) remains fundable at \(B'\).

Hence every truthful reachable endpoint has \(T\) in all its maximal fundable sets. Every committee in \(\mathcal R(A)\) has utility \(t\), contradicting the assumed gain pair.

Case \(v=g\). Here \(g\ge1\). Every committee in \(\mathcal R(A)\) has utility \(g\), so it contains all of \(O=C\setminus T\). Applying the same intersection-of-extensions argument at \(A\) gives \(O\subseteq\partial A\), and therefore \(O\) is fundable.

Also, \(g\ge1\) implies \(|T|\ge m-k+1\). We may therefore make the distinguished mass of \(T\) empty and then replace it by \(R\). This makes \(B\) reachable from \(A\). Thus \(\mathcal R(B)\subseteq\mathcal R(A)\), and all reported committees also have utility \(g\), contradicting the gain pair.

The cases exhaust \(g\le v\le t\). \(\square\)

6. Encoding proposals in an aggregate rational mass

Fix a candidate order. For a proper nonempty type \(t\), let \(b(t)\) be its ordinary binary subset index. Set \(b(\varnothing)=b(C)=0\), and define

\(\sigma(\mu)=\sum_tb(t)\mu_t.\)

Thus \(\sigma\) ignores empty and full mass. Distinct proper nonempty types have distinct positive indices.

A table has two entries, each containing a nonnegative rational key and two subsets \(W,Q\subseteq C\). Use a fixed binary encoding: encode each integer by its binary length in unary followed by its binary representation, and encode subsets by their \(m\)-bit incidence vectors. This gives a polynomial-time encoder and decoder.

For a table payload \(s\) of length \(p\), form the bit string

\(1\,1^p\,0\,s,\)

read from least significant to most significant bit. Let \(e\) be its integer value. It is odd. A decoder supplied with sufficiently many bits recovers the payload and ignores trailing bits; malformed encodings are rejected.

Now define a decoder from a rational full-type mass \(x\). Write \(x=a/b\) in lowest terms and factor

\(b=2^N b_0,\qquad b_0\text{ odd}.\)

Put \(h=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), and extract

\(z=a\,b_0^{-1}\pmod{2^h},\qquad 0\le z<2^h.\)

Interpret its \(h\) bits using the table decoder. If parsing fails, there is no table.

Lemma 7. Given any table, any two nonnegative rationals \(a_1,a_2\), and any positive rational \(\rho\), there exists a rational \(\delta\) with \(0<\delta<\rho\) such that both \(a_1+\delta\) and \(a_2+\delta\) decode to that table.

Proof. Let \(e\) encode the table. Choose an integer \(h\ge1\) large enough that:

  • the complete encoding fits in \(h\) bits;
  • the powers of two dividing the reduced denominators of \(a_1,a_2\) are at most \(2^h\);
  • \(\delta=e/2^{2h}<\rho\).

Write either base rational as \(a_j=A_j/(2^{d_j}B_j)\), where \(B_j\) is odd and \(d_j\le h\). Then

\(a_j+\delta=\dfrac{A_j2^{2h-d_j}+eB_j}{2^{2h}B_j}.\)

Its numerator is odd, so its reduced denominator has exactly \(2h\) factors of two. Furthermore,

\(2^{2h}(a_j+\delta)=e+\dfrac{A_j2^{2h-d_j}}{B_j}\equiv e\pmod{2^h}.\)

The decoder therefore extracts \(e\) on both sides and recovers the table. \(\square\)

Crucially, table keys formed using \(\sigma\) do not depend on \(\delta\). There is no circularity between choosing the table and encoding it.

7. The rule

For \(k<m\), define \(F\) as follows.

  • Compute \(\sigma(\mu)\), and decode a table from \(\mu_C\).
  • For each decoded entry in its fixed order, check whether its key equals \(\sigma(\mu)\), whether \(|W|=k\) and \(Q\subseteq W\), and whether \(Q\) satisfies the certificate inequalities of Lemma 4. Return the first committee passing these checks.
  • If no entry passes, construct an inclusion-maximal fundable set at \(\mu\) by considering candidates in the fixed order and adding each whenever fundability is preserved. Extend it to size \(k\) in the fixed order.

The greedy set in the last step is maximal: once adding a candidate fails, adding further candidates cannot make that previously rejected extension fundable, because fundability is closed under subsets.

Every output belongs to \(\mathcal R(\mu)\). Accepted proposals do so by Lemma 4; the fallback does so using the zero-operation reachable profile. Thus Lemma 2 proves continuum JR.

The rule depends exclusively on aggregate rational masses and candidate labels. It is deterministic and anonymous.

8. Incentive guarantee

Fix admissible \(D,\epsilon,\rho,T,R\), with \(\rho>0\), and suppose a profitable completion exists.

The cases \(T=\varnothing\) and \(T=C\) cannot be profitable, since their utilities are constant. Hence \(T\) is proper and nonempty.

Both outputs at the profitable completion belong to their respective \(\mathcal R\) families. Lemma 6 gives a strict-loss pair \(W,V\) at

\(A=D+\epsilon\delta_T+\rho\delta_\varnothing,\qquad B=D+\epsilon\delta_R+\rho\delta_\varnothing.\)

Choose certificates \(Q_A,Q_B\) for these committees using Lemma 4. Form a table containing

\((\sigma(A),W,Q_A)\) and \((\sigma(B),V,Q_B).\)

The keys are distinct, because

\(\sigma(B)-\sigma(A)=\epsilon\bigl(b(R)-b(T)\bigr)\ne0.\)

Here \(b(T)>0\); either \(R\) is another proper nonempty type with a distinct index, or its index is zero.

Apply Lemma 7 to the base full-type masses \(A_C,B_C\). Obtain \(0<\delta<\rho\) such that both full-type masses after adding \(\delta\) decode to this table.

Use the common completion

\(U=\delta\delta_C+(\rho-\delta)\delta_\varnothing.\)

Relative to \(A,B\), these two resulting societies only transfer mass from the empty type to the full type. Section 3 shows that their respective \(f,\eta\) vectors are unchanged. Thus the certificates \(Q_A,Q_B\) remain valid. Their \(\sigma\) values are also unchanged.

The rule consequently selects \(W\) under truth and \(V\) under the report. Therefore

\(\Delta(U)=u_T(V)-u_T(W)<0.\)

Every profitable report has a losing completion. This proves the required incentive guarantee.

9. Polynomial running time

All operations use the supplied support; none enumerate all approval types.

The reachability preprocessing requires at most \(\tau\) flow tests. Checking each of the two table entries requires at most \(m+1\) flow computations, and the fallback requires at most \(m\). Each network has at most \(\tau+m+3\) vertices and polynomially many edges.

Rational flow computation is polynomial in the input bit length. Explicitly, clear denominators of the supplied capacities and \(q\). The common denominator has polynomial bit length, and this produces integer capacities, without expanding mass into voters. A polynomial max-flow algorithm therefore operates on a polynomial-size network with polynomial-bit capacities.

Computing \(\sigma\), reducing rationals, extracting powers of two, computing the modular inverse, and parsing the bounded table payload all take polynomial time. The number of extracted bits is at most the bit length of the full-type mass denominator.

Hence \(F\) runs in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time.

The construction relies on exact rational arithmetic and the availability of empty and full approval types, precisely as permitted in the question.
