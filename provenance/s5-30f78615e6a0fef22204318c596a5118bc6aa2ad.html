The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a population of total mass \(N\) with finite support \(T\) of feature-cost types \(t=(x_t,c_t)\), mass \(N\mu_t\), and per-capita budget \(b\), determine whether a truthful, individually rational, budget-feasible direct procurement mechanism can achieve an approximation factor below \(2\). The mechanism acts on individual cost reports and selects a fraction \(q_t\in[0,1]\) of each type, with aggregate design value \(V_N(q)=\log\det\!\left(I_d+N\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tq_tx_tx_t^\top\right)\) and benchmark \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}=\max\{V_N(q):0\le q_t\le1,\ \sum_t\mu_tc_tq_t\le b\}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless population of total mass \(N>0\), represented by the list \(T\) of its \(\tau\) positive-mass feature–cost types \(t=(x_t,c_t)\), with binary rational masses \(N\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Features \(x_t\in\mathbb Q^m\) are public and verifiable; costs and cost reports are positive rationals. Assume \(m\ge1\), \(0<\eta\le\|x_t\|_2^2\le1\), and positive binary rational \(N,b,\eta\); the total payment budget is \(Nb\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; running times use \(m,\tau,L\) and any precision parameters explicitly.
An anonymous deterministic direct mass mechanism observes the distribution \(\nu\) of reported feature–cost pairs and supplies menus \(q_\nu(x,r)\in[0,1]\) and \(p_\nu(x,r)\ge0\), including reports outside its support. These are fractional participation and payment per individual, with utility \(p-cq\); the true cost distribution is unavailable except through reports.
Individuals are nonatomic: one individual's deviation leaves \(\nu\) unchanged. Exact dominant-strategy truthfulness requires \(p_\nu(x,c)-cq_\nu(x,c)\ge p_\nu(x,r)-cq_\nu(x,r)\) for every admissible \(\nu,x,c,r\).
Require individual rationality \(p_\nu(x,r)\ge rq_\nu(x,r)\), normalization \(q_\nu(x,r)=0\Rightarrow p_\nu(x,r)=0\), and budget feasibility \(\sum_t\mu_t p_\nu(x_t,r_t)\le b\) for every reported society.
At truthful reports put \(q_t=q_\nu(x_t,c_t)\). Define \(V_N(q)=\log\det(I_m+N\sum_t\mu_tq_tx_tx_t^\top)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}=\max\{V_N(z):z\in[0,1]^\tau,\ \sum_t\mu_tc_tz_t\le b\}\).

Does there exist such a mechanism and a universal constant \(1\le\alpha<2\) satisfying \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}\le\alpha V_N(q)\) for every admissible society, or is every such factor impossible?

For indivisible experiments, deterministic truthful, individually rational, budget-feasible procurement mechanisms cannot achieve a factor strictly below \(2\), regardless of computational power ([Horel–Ioannidis–Muthukrishnan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.5724)).
For finitely many divisible strategic agents with additive value and individual costs bounded by the total budget, a \(2.62\)-approximation and a \(1.25\) lower bound are known ([Klumper–Schäfer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13124)); these concern a different valuation and strategic population. No result settling the exact atomless log-determinant problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted the society as atomless, with individual deviations leaving the aggregate report distribution unchanged; types are not strategic representatives controlling their entire mass.
  • Specified anonymous deterministic fractional participation menus and per-individual payments, with linear utility p minus cost times participation; menus also cover off-support reports.
  • Made the mechanism prior-free: the listed cost distribution describes an instance, while the mechanism receives costs only through individual reports.
  • Normalized the type weights to sum to one and set the total payment budget to N times the per-capita budget.
  • Specified positive rational costs and reports, rational feature coordinates, and positive rational total mass and budget; restored the positive lower and unit upper bounds on squared feature norms using eta.
  • Retained costs above the total budget: the discrete exclusion of such agents is not valid for the stated fractional benchmark.
  • Restored nonnegative payments and zero payments at zero participation, and required exact truthfulness and a uniform multiplicative guarantee without an additive error.
  • Kept the target independent of computational restrictions, matching the unconditional discrete mechanism lower bound.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Such a mechanism exists. In fact, the stated atomless model admits the universal approximation factor \(\alpha=e/(e-1)<2\).

The construction below proves this existence result. It also gives a finite algorithm using rational arithmetic with the slightly weaker factor \(\alpha=16/9<2\), together with an explicit running-time bound.

The essential premise is that an individual deviation leaves the reported distribution \(\nu\) unchanged. Thus a distribution-dependent menu may be constructed from \(\nu\), provided that each resulting fixed menu is truthful for every possible cost.

1. A posted-price lemma

Consider a normalized cost distribution with distinct positive costs \(d_1<\cdots<d_k\), masses \(w_i>0\), and \(\sum_iw_i=1\). Write

\(F_i=\sum_{j\le i}w_j,\qquad C=\sum_iw_id_i,\qquad \gamma=1-1/e.\)

There exist coefficients \(a_i\ge0\) satisfying

\(\sum_i a_i\le1,\qquad \sum_i a_id_iF_i\le C,\qquad \sum_i a_iF_i\ge\gamma.\)

Here \(a_i\) represents the weight assigned to an offer at price \(d_i\). These weights will describe deterministic fractional participation, without randomization.

To prove the claim, consider the finite linear program

\(\max\{\sum_i a_iF_i:\ a_i\ge0,\ \sum_i a_i\le1,\ \sum_i a_id_iF_i\le C\}.\)

Its dual is

\(\min\{\beta+\lambda C:\ \beta,\lambda\ge0,\ \beta+\lambda d_iF_i\ge F_i\text{ for all }i\}.\)

The primal is feasible and bounded, and the dual is feasible, for example at \((\beta,\lambda)=(1,0)\). Finite-dimensional linear-programming duality therefore applies.

Fix any dual-feasible \((\beta,\lambda)\). Define the quantile function \(d(u)=d_i\) for \(F_{i-1}<u\le F_i\), with \(F_0=0\). Then

\(\int_0^1d(u)\,du=C.\)

Dual feasibility implies, for every \(u>0\),

\(\beta\ge u(1-\lambda d(u)).\)

Indeed, when \(1-\lambda d(u)\ge0\), this follows from \(u\le F_i\) and the constraint at \(i\). When that expression is negative, it follows from \(\beta\ge0\). Consequently,

\(\lambda d(u)\ge\max\{0,1-\beta/u\}.\)

If \(\beta\ge1\), the dual objective is already at least \(1\). Otherwise, integrating gives

\(\lambda C\ge\int_\beta^1(1-\beta/u)\,du=1-\beta+\beta\log\beta,\)

where the expression at \(\beta=0\) is interpreted by continuity. Hence

\(\beta+\lambda C\ge1+\beta\log\beta\ge1-1/e.\)

The last inequality follows by minimizing \(\beta\log\beta\) on \([0,1]\): its derivative is \(1+\log\beta\), and its minimum is \(-1/e\), attained at \(\beta=1/e\).

Every dual-feasible objective is therefore at least \(\gamma\). Strong duality proves the lemma.

2. Choosing and arranging a benchmark allocation

Fix a finite reported society \(\nu\). In this section, all costs are its reported costs. Choose an optimizer \(z\) of its full-information problem.

Such an optimizer exists: the feasible region is nonempty and compact, and the objective is continuous. A deterministic choice can be made by successive lexicographic minimization over the compact set of optimizers.

Let \(\mathcal X\) be the set of distinct reported features. For each \(x\in\mathcal X\), define its selected normalized mass

\(M_x=\sum_{t:x_t=x}\mu_tz_t.\)

Rearrange the allocation within each feature so that it selects the cheapest individuals first, preserving \(M_x\). Denote the rearranged allocation by \(\widetilde z\).

This rearrangement preserves the covariance matrix because every individual in the group has the same feature. It also weakly decreases cost. Explicitly, suppose a feature group has increasing costs \(d_1<\cdots<d_h\), masses \(h_i\), selected masses \(v_i\), and total selected mass \(M\). Put \(H_i=\sum_{j\le i}v_j\). Then

\(\sum_i d_iv_i=d_hM-\sum_{i<h}(d_{i+1}-d_i)H_i.\)

Every allocation satisfies

\(H_i\le\min\{M,\sum_{j\le i}h_j\}.\)

The cheapest-first allocation attains all these upper bounds simultaneously and therefore minimizes cost.

Define the rearranged cost assigned to feature \(x\) by

\(C_x=\sum_{t:x_t=x}\mu_tc_t\widetilde z_t.\)

Thus

\(\sum_xC_x\le b,\qquad V_N(\widetilde z)=\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}.\)

For each feature with \(M_x>0\), the rearranged allocation fully selects all cost levels below a cutoff, selects a fraction \(\theta\in(0,1]\) at the cutoff, and selects nothing above it.

3. Constructing the complete menus

Fix a feature \(x\) with \(M_x>0\). Let \(d_1<\cdots<d_k\) be its selected cost levels, with \(d_k\) the cutoff. Normalize their selected masses by \(M_x\), obtaining weights

\(w_i=\frac{h_i\widetilde z_i}{M_x}.\)

Apply the lemma to this selected distribution. Its mean cost is \(C_x/M_x\). Obtain coefficients \(a_i\ge0\) such that

\(\sum_i a_i\le1,\qquad M_x\sum_i a_id_iF_i\le C_x,\qquad \sum_i a_iF_i\ge\gamma.\)

For every positive rational report \(r\), define a threshold allocation \(s_i(r)\) as follows:

  • \(s_i(r)=1\) when \(r<d_i\);
  • \(s_i(r)=0\) when \(r>d_i\);
  • at \(r=d_i\), set \(s_i(r)=1\) if \(i<k\), and \(s_k(d_k)=\theta\).

Now define the actual deterministic menu by

\(q_\nu(x,r)=\sum_i a_is_i(r),\qquad p_\nu(x,r)=\sum_i a_id_is_i(r).\)

Because \(\sum_i a_i\le1\), participation belongs to \([0,1]\).

For features with \(M_x=0\), and features absent from \(\nu\), define both menu entries to be zero at every report.

These formulas specify menus even for reports outside the support. All selections and tie-breaking depend only on the reported distribution and public parameters, so the mechanism is anonymous and deterministic.

If the reporting domain additionally contains distributions outside the finite rational input class, define the entire menu to be zero on those distributions. This also satisfies all required menu conditions there.

4. Truthfulness, individual rationality, normalization, and budget

First consider one threshold component, with price \(d\), allocation \(s(r)\), and payment \(ds(r)\). An individual of true cost \(c\) has utility

\((d-c)s(r).\)

If \(c<d\), truthful reporting yields allocation \(1\), which maximizes this nonnegative utility. If \(c>d\), truthful reporting yields allocation \(0\), which maximizes this nonpositive utility. If \(c=d\), every report gives utility zero.

Thus each component is exactly truthful for every pair of positive rational costs and reports. Taking a nonnegative weighted sum proves

\(p_\nu(x,c)-cq_\nu(x,c)\ge p_\nu(x,r)-cq_\nu(x,r).\)

This proves precisely the required dominant-strategy condition: an individual deviation keeps \(\nu\), and hence all component prices and coefficients, fixed.

For individual rationality, any component with \(s_i(r)>0\) has \(d_i\ge r\). Therefore

\(p_\nu(x,r)-rq_\nu(x,r)=\sum_i a_i(d_i-r)s_i(r)\ge0.\)

For normalization, every term \(a_is_i(r)\) is nonnegative. If their sum is zero, each is zero, and consequently \(p_\nu(x,r)=0\).

Finally, evaluated on the reported distribution, threshold component \(i\) purchases normalized mass exactly \(M_xF_i\). At the final cutoff, the equality fraction \(\theta\) ensures this identity also holds for the partially selected cost level. Thus the feature group’s total normalized payment is

\(\sum_{t:x_t=x}\mu_tp_\nu(x,c_t)=M_x\sum_i a_id_iF_i\le C_x.\)

Summing over features gives

\(\sum_t\mu_tp_\nu(x_t,c_t)\le\sum_xC_x\le b.\)

The argument applies afresh to every reported society. Multiplying by \(N\) gives total payment at most \(Nb\).

5. Log-determinant approximation

At truthful reports, let

\(Q_x=\sum_{t:x_t=x}\mu_tq_t.\)

The posted-price lemma gives \(Q_x\ge\gamma M_x\) for every feature. Hence, in the positive-semidefinite order,

\(A_q:=N\sum_xQ_xxx^\top\succeq\gamma N\sum_xM_xxx^\top=:\gamma A_z.\)

Log-determinant is increasing in this order. To verify the needed instance explicitly, the matrix

\((I_m+\gamma A_z)^{-1/2}(I_m+A_q)(I_m+\gamma A_z)^{-1/2}\)

is at least \(I_m\), so all its eigenvalues are at least \(1\). Its determinant is therefore at least \(1\), proving

\(\log\det(I_m+A_q)\ge\log\det(I_m+\gamma A_z).\)

Let \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\ge0\) be the eigenvalues of \(A_z\). Scalar concavity, together with \(\log(1+0)=0\), gives

\(\log(1+\gamma\lambda_j)\ge\gamma\log(1+\lambda_j).\)

Consequently,

\(V_N(q)\ge\sum_j\log(1+\gamma\lambda_j)\ge\gamma\sum_j\log(1+\lambda_j)=\gamma\,\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}.\)

Therefore

\(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}\le\frac{e}{e-1}V_N(q).\)

Since \(e>2\), this factor is strictly below \(2\).

6. A finite rational implementation

The existence proof above chooses an exact optimizer and permits real-valued outputs. The following implementation uses only rational arithmetic and achieves factor \(16/9\).

Choose any input type \(t_0\), and define

\(\rho=\min\{1,b/(\mu_{t_0}c_{t_0})\},\qquad y=N\mu_{t_0}\rho\|x_{t_0}\|_2^2,\qquad \ell=y/(1+y).\)

Selecting only fraction \(\rho\) of that type is feasible. Its value is \(\log(1+y)\), so

\(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}\ge\log(1+y)\ge\ell>0.\)

The second inequality follows by integrating \(1/(1+s)\ge1/(1+y)\) over \(s\in[0,y]\).

Set

\(K=\max\{1,\lceil10N/\ell\rceil\}.\)

Enumerate feasible allocations on the grid \(\{0,1/K,\ldots,1\}^{\tau}\), and choose one maximizing the rational determinant

\(\det(I_m+N\sum_t\mu_tz_tx_tx_t^\top).\)

This comparison is exact and requires no logarithm evaluation.

To prove its approximation guarantee, write \(A(u)=I_m+N\sum_t\mu_tu_tx_tx_t^\top\). For every \(u\in[0,1]^\tau\),

\(\frac{\partial V_N(u)}{\partial u_t}=N\mu_tx_t^\top A(u)^{-1}x_t\le N\mu_t,\)

because \(A(u)\succeq I_m\) and \(\|x_t\|_2^2\le1\).

Round an exact optimizer downward coordinatewise to this grid. Budget feasibility is preserved. Integrating the derivative bound along the segment between the rounded and original allocations shows that the value loss is at most

\(N\sum_t\mu_t/K=N/K\le\ell/10\le\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}/10.\)

Therefore the maximizing grid allocation has value at least \((9/10)\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}\).

Apply the same rearrangement and menu construction to that allocation. All quantities are now rational. Each posted-price linear program can be solved exactly by enumerating its vertices: a vertex has at most two positive coefficients, since there are only two resource constraints. With two positive coefficients, both resource constraints must be tight, so their values follow from two linear equations. There are only quadratically many candidates in the number of selected cost levels.

The resulting mechanism satisfies

\(V_N(q)\ge(9/10)(1-1/e)\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}\ge(9/16)\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}.\)

Here \(1-1/e>5/8\), since the first four terms of the exponential series sum to \(8/3\), and the remaining terms are positive. Thus \(\alpha=16/9\) is valid.

The running time is bounded by

\((K+1)^\tau\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log K)\)

bit operations. The rational numbers defining \(K\) have bit lengths polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\); determinant computation, rearrangement, and vertex enumeration also use polynomial bit complexity per grid allocation. No numerical precision parameter is required. This is a finite, generally exponential-time algorithm; polynomial-time efficiency is not claimed.

7. Why the finite-agent lower bounds do not transfer

The construction uses the reported distribution to choose menus, and it proves truthfulness with that distribution held fixed. In a finite strategic-agent model, an agent’s report changes the input from which its menu is constructed, so this proof would not establish truthfulness there. The cited papers concern [indivisible experiments](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.5724) and [finitely many divisible agents](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13124), respectively.

Under the atomless deviation rule specified in the question, every required mechanism property has been verified above.
