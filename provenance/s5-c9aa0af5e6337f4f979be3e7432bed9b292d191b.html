The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), per-capita voting weights \(w_t>0\), a common prior \(p=\Pr(X)<1/2\), and a quota \(Q\) with \(0<Q\le W=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\), choose a distribution over targeted-mass vectors \(a\in\prod_{t\in T}[0,\mu_t]\) for state \(Y\). In state \(X\), recommend \(x\) to everyone; in state \(Y\), recommend \(x\) to targeted mass \(a_t\) of each type and \(y\) to the rest. Require behavioral obedience, \(\mathbb{E}[a_t]\le\mu_t p/(1-p)\) for every type, and maximize the ex-ante probability that \(x\) passes, where a signal passes exactly when \(\sum_t w_ta_t\ge Q\).

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=2\) voting alternatives, \(x\) and \(y\), and states \(X\) and \(Y\). Inputs also include binary rationals \(w_t>0\), \(0<p=\Pr(X)<1/2\), \(0<Q\le W=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\), and a threshold \(b\in[0,1]\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Voters are behavioral: they vote for \(x\) when their posterior probability of \(X\) is at least \(1/2\), including equality, and otherwise vote for \(y\). Each voter observes only their own recommendation.
In state \(X\), everyone receives recommendation \(x\). In state \(Y\), the sender chooses a finitely supported probability distribution \(\nu\) over real vectors \(a\in\prod_{t\in T}[0,\mu_t]\); vector \(a\) recommends \(x\) to mass \(a_t\) of type \(t\) and \(y\) to the rest. Within each type, individuals have the same marginal probability of receiving \(x\).
Feasibility requires \(\mathbb E_\nu[a_t]\le\mu_t p/(1-p)\) for every \(t\). A realization implements \(x\) exactly when \(\sum_t w_ta_t\ge Q\). Define \(V^*=\sup_\nu\{p+(1-p)\Pr_\nu(\sum_t w_ta_t\ge Q)\}\), over feasible distributions.

Is deciding whether \(V^*\ge b\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite indivisible voters, optimal behavioral persuasion is characterized by an LP indexed by minimal winning coalitions; this characterization alone gives no polynomial bound in the compact weight input. [Kerman and Tenev](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00199-024-01599-0)

Result

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, the optimum is attained by a distribution supported on at most two mass vectors, and

\(V^*=\min\left\{1,\ p+\dfrac{pW}{Q}\right\},\qquad W=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t w_t.\)

The proof uses the divisibility of every type’s mass and the common proportional bound on its expected recommendation mass.

1. An upper bound

Define \(r=p/(1-p)\), and let \(\nu\) be any feasible distribution. Write

\(S(a)=\sum_{t\in T}w_ta_t,\qquad q=\Pr_\nu(S(a)\ge Q).\)

Since every coordinate and weight is nonnegative, \(S(a)\ge Q\,\mathbf{1}_{\{S(a)\ge Q\}}\). Taking expectations and then using feasibility gives

\(Qq\le \mathbb E_\nu[S(a)]=\sum_{t\in T}w_t\mathbb E_\nu[a_t]\le \sum_{t\in T}w_t\mu_t r=rW.\)

Together with \(q\le 1\), this proves

\(q\le \min\{1,rW/Q\}.\)

Consequently,

\(V^*\le p+(1-p)\min\{1,rW/Q\}.\)

2. A feasible distribution attaining the bound

Set

\(c=Q/W,\qquad a_t^+=c\mu_t\quad(t\in T),\qquad q^*=\min\{1,r/c\}.\)

The assumptions \(0<Q\le W\) imply \(0<c\le 1\). Therefore \(a^+\) belongs to the permitted box, and

\(\sum_{t\in T}w_ta_t^+=cW=Q.\)

Thus \(a^+\) is winning, including at equality as stipulated in the question. The zero vector is losing because \(Q>0\).

Choose \(a^+\) with probability \(q^*\) and the zero vector with probability \(1-q^*\), omitting the latter when its probability is zero. For each type,

\(\mathbb E_\nu[a_t]=q^*c\mu_t=\min\{c,r\}\mu_t\le r\mu_t.\)

Hence this finitely supported distribution is feasible, and its winning probability is exactly \(q^*\). It attains the upper bound.

For completeness, the behavioral condition also holds directly. The common marginal probability of receiving recommendation \(x\) in state \(Y\) is

\(h_t=\mathbb E_\nu[a_t]/\mu_t=q^*c\le r.\)

Because recommendation \(x\) occurs with probability one in state \(X\), Bayes’ rule gives

\(\Pr(X\mid x,t)=\dfrac{p}{p+(1-p)h_t}\ge \dfrac12.\)

Thus every recipient of recommendation \(x\) votes for \(x\), including when this inequality is an equality. Recommendation \(y\), whenever received, reveals state \(Y\).

It follows that

\(V^*=p+(1-p)\min\left\{1,\dfrac{pW}{(1-p)Q}\right\}=\min\left\{1,\ p+\dfrac{pW}{Q}\right\}.\)

In particular, the supremum in the definition is a maximum.

3. Exact polynomial-time decision

Because \(b\le 1\) and \(Q>0\), the formula yields the equivalences

\(V^*\ge b\ \Longleftrightarrow\ p+\dfrac{pW}{Q}\ge b\ \Longleftrightarrow\ p(W+Q)\ge bQ.\)

The algorithm therefore computes \(W=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tw_t\) and performs this exact rational comparison.

This requires polynomial time in the binary input length. Explicitly, write each mass and weight as a ratio of binary integers. A common denominator for the products \(\mu_tw_t\) is the product of all their input denominators; its bit length is bounded by the sum of those denominators’ bit lengths, hence by \(O(L)\). The corresponding numerator sum also has polynomial bit length. The remaining additions, multiplications, and integer comparisons likewise involve only polynomially many bits.

Thus the algorithm runs in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\), and therefore in \(m,\tau,L\) for the prescribed \(m=2\). No enumeration of winning coalitions is required.
