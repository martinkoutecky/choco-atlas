The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 5.1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite item catalogue \(O\), a finite set of complete valuation types \(T\), type masses \(\mu_t\), additive valuations \(v_t\), and per-capita item supplies \(q_o\), decide whether there exist nonnegative masses \(x_{t,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\subseteq O\) satisfying \(\sum_{B\subseteq O}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T,\,B\ni o}x_{t,B}=q_o\), such that every \(x_{t,B},x_{u,D}>0\) with \(v_t(B)<v_u(D)\) satisfies pointwise \(\mathrm{EQ1}\), and no measurable reassignment weakly improves every individual while strictly improving a positive mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(O\) be a catalogue with \(m\geq1\) items. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) complete valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types.
Each type specifies additive, signed integer item values encoded in binary; no valuation normalization is imposed. Supplies \(q_o\in[0,1]\) are binary rationals. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Individuals form an atomless population; each receives an integral bundle \(B\subseteq O\), possibly empty, with at most one copy of each item. An allocation consists of real masses \(x_{t,B}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{B\subseteq O}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:o\in B}x_{t,B}=q_o\).
Pointwise \(\mathrm{EQ1}\) means that whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\), \(x_{u,D}>0\), and \(v_t(B)<v_u(D)\), either some \(g\in D\) has \(v_u(g)\geq0\) and \(v_t(B)\geq v_u(D\setminus\{g\})\), or some \(c\in B\) has \(v_t(c)\leq0\) and \(v_t(B\setminus\{c\})\geq v_u(D)\).
A measurable reassignment is represented by real masses \(z_{t,B,D}\geq0\) with \(\sum_Dz_{t,B,D}=x_{t,B}\) and \(\sum_{t,B,D:o\in D}z_{t,B,D}=q_o\). It Pareto dominates the allocation if every positive entry satisfies \(v_t(D)\geq v_t(B)\) and \(\sum_{t,B,D:v_t(D)>v_t(B)}z_{t,B,D}>0\). Reassignments need not satisfy \(\mathrm{EQ1}\).

Is deciding whether such a society admits a pointwise \(\mathrm{EQ1}\), Pareto-optimal allocation solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents and indivisible items, existence of an \(\mathrm{EQ1}\), Pareto-optimal allocation is strongly NP-hard, even when all agents have equal total positive value and equal total negative value; a polynomial-time algorithm is known for values in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06799v3).
No result settling the exact atomless, integral-bundle problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one, interpreting supplies as per-capita quantities.
  • Specified signed binary-encoded integer valuations, following the discrete valuation domain; imposed no normalization restriction because the anchor concerns general instances.
  • Specified binary-rational supplies in [0,1], allowing zero supply, and retained bundles as subsets with empty bundles permitted.
  • Made the population atomless and allocation masses real; each individual receives an integral bundle.
  • Expanded pointwise EQ1 to include either removal of a nonnegative item from the richer bundle or removal of a nonpositive item from the poorer bundle.
  • Formalized measurable Pareto reassignments through masses indexed by type, original bundle, and new bundle, preserving supplies and individual utility comparisons; competing reassignments are unrestricted by fairness.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Proven result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with exactly two supported types, nonnegative integer valuations, equal total valuations, and supplies belonging to \(\{1/4,1\}\).

In the reduction below, every feasible pointwise \(\mathrm{EQ1}\) allocation is already Pareto optimal.

1. Reduction from partition

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\). [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

By doubling the input integers, we may assume the instance consists of positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) with

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i=2K,\qquad K\geq1.\)

The question is whether some \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i=K\). Doubling preserves the existence of an equal partition.

Construct a catalogue containing number items \(o_1,\ldots,o_n\) and two reference items \(r_1,r_2\). Let \(R=\{r_1,r_2\}\).

There are two types, \(A\) and \(H\), with masses \(\mu_A=\mu_H=1/2\). Their additive valuations are:

| Type | Number item \(o_i\) | Reference item \(r_j\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(A\) | \(a_i\) | \(0\) |
| \(H\) | \(0\) | \(K\) |

Set the supplies to

\(\displaystyle q_{o_i}=\frac14\quad\text{for every }i,\qquad q_{r_1}=q_{r_2}=1.\)

All valuations are nonnegative. Both types have total positive value \(2K\) and total negative value \(0\).

The construction has \(m=n+2\) items and \(\tau=2\) supported types. The bit length of \(K\) is polynomial in the source input length, and every mass and supply has constant bit length. Thus this is a polynomial-time construction.

2. EQ1 forces an exact partition bundle

Let \(x\) be any feasible pointwise \(\mathrm{EQ1}\) allocation in the constructed society.

First, every supported bundle contains both reference items. For each \(j\in\{1,2\}\), feasibility gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,B:r_j\notin B}x_{t,B} =\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}-q_{r_j} =1-1=0.\)

Nonnegativity therefore implies that \(x_{t,B}>0\) only if \(R\subseteq B\). Consequently, every supported type-\(H\) bundle has utility exactly \(2K\).

Second, every supported type-\(A\) bundle has utility at least \(K\). Suppose instead that \(x_{A,B}>0\) and \(v_A(B)<K\). Since \(\mu_H>0\), there is a supported type-\(H\) bundle \(D\). Its utility is \(2K\), so the ordered pair \((A,B),(H,D)\) requires an EQ1 witness.

Neither permitted witness exists:

  • Removing a reference item from \(D\) leaves type \(H\) with utility \(K>v_A(B)\). Removing a number item leaves utility \(2K>v_A(B)\).
  • Type \(A\) has no negative item values. Any item in \(B\) with nonpositive value has value zero, so its removal leaves utility \(v_A(B)<2K=v_H(D)\).

This contradicts pointwise \(\mathrm{EQ1}\). Hence \(v_A(B)\geq K\) whenever \(x_{A,B}>0\).

Third, the supply constraints force equality. Write

\(\displaystyle p_i=\sum_{B:o_i\in B}x_{A,B}\)

for the mass of type \(A\) receiving \(o_i\). Since the total supply of that item is \(1/4\), we have \(p_i\leq1/4\). Additivity and the lower bound just proved yield

\(\displaystyle \frac K2 =K\mu_A \leq\sum_Bx_{A,B}v_A(B) =\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_ip_i \leq\frac14\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i =\frac K2. \)

Thus every inequality is an equality. In particular,

\(\displaystyle \sum_Bx_{A,B}\bigl(v_A(B)-K\bigr)=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore every supported type-\(A\) bundle has utility exactly \(K\).

There is at least one such bundle because \(\mu_A=1/2>0\). For any one of them, the index set \(S=\{i:o_i\in B\}\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i\in S}a_i=v_A(B)=K.\)

Therefore, existence of a pointwise \(\mathrm{EQ1}\) allocation implies that the partition instance is a yes-instance.

3. A partition supplies an EQ1 allocation

Conversely, suppose \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i=K\). Its complement also sums to \(K\).

Assign:

  • mass \(1/4\) of type \(A\) the bundle \(R\cup\{o_i:i\in S\}\);
  • mass \(1/4\) of type \(A\) the bundle \(R\cup\{o_i:i\notin S\}\);
  • mass \(1/2\) of type \(H\) the bundle \(R\).

Set all other allocation entries to zero. These assignments realize the prescribed type masses. Each number item is received by mass \(1/4\), and each reference item is received by the whole population, so all supply constraints hold.

Every supported type-\(A\) bundle has utility \(K\), and the supported type-\(H\) bundle has utility \(2K\). Equal-utility pairs impose no EQ1 condition. For an unequal pair, removing either reference item from the richer type-\(H\) bundle leaves utility \(K\). Hence the allocation is pointwise \(\mathrm{EQ1}\).

4. Pareto optimality under the specified reassignment model

Define total welfare by

\(\displaystyle W(y)=\sum_{t,D}y_{t,D}v_t(D).\)

For every feasible allocation \(y\), both reference items occur in every supported bundle. Type \(H\) therefore contributes exactly \(\mu_H\,2K=K\) to welfare. Type \(A\)'s contribution is at most \(\sum_i a_iq_{o_i}=K/2\). Thus

\(\displaystyle W(y)\leq\frac{3K}{2}.\)

The allocation constructed above attains this bound. Indeed, the equality argument in Section 2 shows that every feasible pointwise \(\mathrm{EQ1}\) allocation in this construction attains it.

To verify that welfare maximization implies the required Pareto optimality, suppose a reassignment \(z\) Pareto dominates such an allocation \(x\). Define its resulting allocation by \(y_{t,D}=\sum_Bz_{t,B,D}\). The reassignment constraints ensure that \(y\) has the same type masses and supplies. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle W(y)-W(x) =\sum_{t,B,D}z_{t,B,D}\bigl(v_t(D)-v_t(B)\bigr)>0. \)

The strict inequality follows because all terms with positive reassignment mass are nonnegative, and strictly improving entries have positive total mass. The sum is finite. This contradicts the welfare upper bound.

Hence the constructed EQ1 allocation is Pareto optimal against every reassignment allowed in the question.

5. Conclusion

The partition instance is a yes-instance if and only if the constructed society admits a pointwise \(\mathrm{EQ1}\), Pareto-optimal allocation. This polynomial-time reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

Atomless splitting does not defeat the reduction: EQ1 requires every supported type-\(A\) bundle to meet a lower bound, while its aggregate supply permits exactly that average. Every supported bundle must therefore attain the partition target exactly. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The sign restrictions on witnesses are respected. In particular, allowing zero-valued items as witnesses does not invalidate the argument.

Thus every supported type-\(A\) bundle must have utility at least \(K\).

4. Aggregate supply forces exact equality

\[ \frac K2 \leq \sum_B x_{A,B}v_A(B) =\sum_i a_ip_i \leq \frac14\sum_i a_i =\frac K2. \]

The first inequality uses the pointwise lower bound and type-\(A\) mass \(1/2\). The identity uses additivity and the zero values of reference items for type \(A\). The upper bound uses positive \(a_i\) and the supply bounds.

\[ \sum_B x_{A,B}\bigl(v_A(B)-K\bigr) \]

is zero. Every positive allocation entry for type \(A\) consequently has utility exactly \(K\).

Positive type-\(A\) mass ensures at least one such bundle exists. Its number-item subset is an exact partition subset. The necessary direction is fully established.

The EQ1 check covers every supported ordered pair:

  • Equal-utility pairs do not trigger the condition.
