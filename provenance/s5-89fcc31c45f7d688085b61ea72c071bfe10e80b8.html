The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite candidate universe \(U\), initial candidate set \(C\subseteq U\), target \(p\in C\), rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete rankings of \(U\), and an addition or deletion budget \(q\), can at most \(q\) allowed candidates be added or deleted so that, after restricting each ranking to the resulting set \(S\), some rival \(c\ne p\) has \(k\)-approval mass score \(\sum_{\rho}\mu_\rho\mathbf{1}[c\in\operatorname{top}_k(\rho|_S)]\) strictly exceeding \(p\)'s?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix an integer \(k\geq 1\). Let \(U\) be a finite candidate universe with \(m=|U|\geq\max\{2,k\}\), initial candidates \(\varnothing\neq C\subseteq U\), distinguished candidate \(p\in C\), and binary-encoded integer budget \(q\geq 0\).
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct strict complete rankings of \(U\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_\rho\), where \(\sum_\rho\mu_\rho=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Consider two separate decision problems. Addition chooses \(A\subseteq U\setminus C\) with \(|A|\leq q\) and sets \(S=C\cup A\); deletion chooses \(D\subseteq C\setminus\{p\}\) with \(|D|\leq q\) and sets \(S=C\setminus D\). Candidates are indivisible and the society stays fixed.
Define \(\operatorname{top}_k(\rho|_S)\) to contain the first \(\min\{k,|S|\}\) candidates, and define \(s_S(c)=\sum_\rho\mu_\rho\mathbf{1}[c\in\operatorname{top}_k(\rho|_S)]\). All candidates attaining maximum score are winners. The decision condition is \(\exists c\in S\setminus\{p\}:s_S(c)>s_S(p)\).

For each fixed \(k\geq 1\), is each of these two destructive candidate-control problems in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,q\), making any dependence on \(k\) explicit.

For finite unweighted electorates, destructive control by adding candidates and by deleting candidates under \(k\)-approval are both \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for fixed positive \(k\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.4159). No separate result for the normalized rational support formulation is verified here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Treat k as a fixed positive integer, matching the anchor's family of election rules.
  • Interpret addition and deletion as separate problems, with all candidates outside C available for addition and all candidates in C except p available for deletion.
  • Make the candidate budget a nonnegative binary-encoded integer counting indivisible candidates.
  • Normalize the positive rational masses to sum to one and require strict complete rankings.
  • When fewer than k candidates remain, approve every remaining candidate.
  • Retain all tied maximum-score candidates as winners, so destructive success requires a strict score advantage.
  • Require at least two candidates in the universe; resulting candidate sets may be singletons, in which case destructive success is impossible.

Classification

For every fixed \(k\geq 1\), both destructive control by adding candidates and destructive control by deleting candidates are \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete in the normalized rational support formulation.

The proof uses the supplied unweighted hardness theorem and a polynomial-time normalization reduction. Normalization preserves every score comparison, including strict inequalities.

1. Exact score computation and membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Write the support as \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_\tau\), with masses \(\mu_i=a_i/b_i\), where \(a_i,b_i\) are positive binary integers. Define

\(Q=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}b_i,\qquad w_i=a_i(Q/b_i).\)

These are positive integers, and the normalization assumption gives \(\sum_i w_i=Q\). For any candidate set \(S\), define its integer scores by

\(t_S(c)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\,\mathbf 1[c\in\operatorname{top}_k(\rho_i|_S)].\)

Then \(t_S(c)=Q\,s_S(c)\). Since \(Q>0\),

\(s_S(c)>s_S(p)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t_S(c)>t_S(p).\)

Thus the strict inequality can be checked exactly.

The bit length of \(Q\) is at most \(1+\sum_i\log_2 b_i=O(L)\). Each weight and each score lies between zero and \(Q\), so all arithmetic uses \(O(L)\)-bit integers. Schoolbook multiplication and division compute all weights in \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations.

A certificate specifies the chosen addition or deletion set using an \(m\)-bit incidence vector. The verifier checks eligibility and the budget, constructs \(S\), and computes its scores. In each ranking, it scans candidates in order, awarding \(w_i\) to the first \(\min\{k,|S|\}\) candidates belonging to \(S\). It then checks whether any surviving rival strictly exceeds \(p\).

A conservative bit-operation bound for verification is

\(O\!\left(\tau L^2+\bigl(\tau m+\tau\min\{k,m\}+m\bigr)L\right).\)

This is polynomial in the input length, even when the budget is binary encoded. Hence both problems belong to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

If \(|S|\leq k\), every surviving candidate has score one. Such a set cannot satisfy the destructive condition; the verifier handles this case automatically.

2. Normalization preserves every candidate-control outcome

Consider an unweighted election with \(N\geq 1\) explicitly listed voters, each having a strict complete ranking of the full candidate universe \(U\). For addition, this universe includes both initial and available candidates; for deletion, take \(U=C\).

For each distinct full ranking \(\rho\), let \(n_\rho\) be its multiplicity. Construct the rational support by listing each distinct ranking once and assigning

\(\mu_\rho=n_\rho/N.\)

All listed masses are positive, and \(\sum_\rho\mu_\rho=1\). Keep \(U,C,p,q\) unchanged.

Let \(\operatorname{score}^{\mathrm{unw}}_S(c)\) denote the original unweighted approval score after restricting the election to \(S\). For every such \(S\) and every \(c\in S\),

\(\operatorname{score}^{\mathrm{unw}}_S(c)=\sum_\rho n_\rho\,\mathbf 1[c\in\operatorname{top}_k(\rho|_S)]=N\,s_S(c).\)

The first equality groups voters with identical full rankings. Their restrictions to every \(S\) are identical, so grouping is valid simultaneously for all candidate changes.

Consequently,

\(\operatorname{score}^{\mathrm{unw}}_S(c)>\operatorname{score}^{\mathrm{unw}}_S(p)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s_S(c)>s_S(p).\)

The permitted control sets are unchanged because the candidate universe, initial set, distinguished candidate, and budget are unchanged. Therefore this construction preserves both YES and NO instances, separately for addition and deletion.

It is a polynomial-time reduction. One can sort the explicitly listed rankings and count their multiplicities. The resulting support satisfies \(\tau\leq N\), and each numerator and denominator uses \(O(\log(N+1))\) bits. With candidate identifiers relabeled by integers, its total encoding length is

\(O\!\left(\tau m\log(m+1)+\tau\log(N+1)+m\log(m+1)+\log(q+2)\right),\)

which is polynomial in the original explicit-election input length.

3. Applying the unweighted hardness theorem

The supplied unweighted result states that, for each fixed positive \(k\), destructive control by either adding or deleting candidates under \(k\)-approval is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The source defines destructive success as making the distinguished candidate cease to be a winner, with all candidates of maximum score counted as winners. Its condition therefore agrees with the strict inequality required here. See [Lin, *The Complexity of Manipulating k-Approval Elections*, Section 5 and Theorem 20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.4159).

The relevant hypotheses are preserved by Section 2: rankings are strict and complete, voters remain fixed, candidates remain indivisible, the budget is unchanged, and deletion cannot remove \(p\). The source’s hard constructions also have sufficiently many candidates that the convention for elections with fewer than \(k\) candidates does not affect hardness.

For each fixed \(k\), composing that hardness reduction with the normalization reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness of each rational-support problem. Together with Section 1, this proves the stated \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

4. Deterministic running times

For either problem, let \(n\) be the number of eligible candidates:

  • Addition: \(n=|U\setminus C|\).
  • Deletion: \(n=|C\setminus\{p\}|\).

Set \(r=\min\{q,n\}\) and \(B(n,r)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom nj\). Enumerating all permitted control sets and computing their scores with the precomputed integer weights gives the deterministic bound

\(O\!\left(\tau L^2+B(n,r)\bigl(\tau m+\tau\min\{k,m\}+m\bigr)L\right).\)

Here \(B(n,r)\leq 2^n\). For \(r\geq 1\), also \(B(n,r)\leq(r+1)n^r\); when \(r=0\), there is one set to check. Thus every fixed budget admits a polynomial-time enumeration algorithm, while the unrestricted-budget problems are \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

The displayed bounds make the dependence on \(k\) explicit. They use the supplied support directly and never expand rational masses into individual voters. A binary budget larger than the eligible candidate count is simply capped at that count.
