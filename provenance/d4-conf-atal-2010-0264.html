The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \mu_t>0 summing to \(1\), rational target densities \pi_t\ge 0 with \sum_t\mu_t\pi_t=1, and rational \varepsilon>0, find rational non-increasing type weights w_1\ge\cdots\ge w_\tau\ge0 normalized by \sum_t\mu_tw_t=1 and a quota q\in(0,1] whose objective is within \varepsilon of its infimum. For each admissible replication size N, form a finite game with N\mu_t members of type t, each of weight w_t/N, and quota q; if \beta_{t,N}(w,q) is one such member’s normalized Banzhaf index, minimize \limsup_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\;\forall t}\sum_t\mu_t(N\beta_{t,N}(w,q)-\pi_t)^2.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: an ordered list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; the type order is fixed input.
The remaining input consists of binary-rational target densities \(\pi_t\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t\pi_t=1\), and binary-rational \(\varepsilon>0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; voting has two alternatives, so \(m=2\) is fixed.
A feasible design consists of rational weights \(w_1\ge\cdots\ge w_\tau\ge0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_tw_t=1\), and a rational quota \(q\in(0,1]\).
For every positive integer \(N\) satisfying \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\) for all types, the replicated game has \(N\mu_t\) members of type \(t\), each of weight \(w_t/N\). A coalition wins exactly when its total weight is at least \(q\), including equality.
Let \(\beta_{t,N}(w,q)\) be the normalized Banzhaf index of any one member of type \(t\). Define \(F(w,q)=\limsup_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\;\forall t}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\bigl(N\beta_{t,N}(w,q)-\pi_t\bigr)^2\); the design is held fixed throughout this limit.
The computational problem is to find a feasible rational design with \(F(w,q)\le\inf_{(u,r)\text{ feasible}}F(u,r)+\varepsilon\).

Is this problem solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), with \(m=2\) fixed and \(\varepsilon\) encoded in binary, or is it NP-hard?

Discrete canonical weighted voting game design with squared error has an exact anytime algorithm running in exponential time in the number of agents, for every exponential-time computable power index, including normalized Banzhaf ([source](https://www.pakvla.nl/bart/votingaamas.pdf)).
For growing electorates with finitely many positive integer weights, half quota, and gcd \(1\) among weights occurring infinitely often, Banzhaf power ratios converge to weight ratios ([source](https://www.lse.ac.uk/cpnss/assets/documents/voting-power-and-procedures/publications/2004/lindner.pdf)). Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact polynomial-time solvability

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. An exact optimal rational design is obtained by weighted isotonic regression of the target densities in the given type order, with quota \(q=\tfrac12\). Thus the running time has no dependence on \(1/\varepsilon\).

1. A lower bound valid for every feasible design

Define
\[ \mathcal C=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^\tau:x_1\ge\cdots\ge x_\tau\ge0,\ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tx_t=1\right\}, \qquad G(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t(x_t-\pi_t)^2. \]

First, consider any finite replicated game. Let \(f(S)\) denote the indicator that coalition \(S\) wins, and let \(\alpha_i\) denote voter \(i\)'s absolute Banzhaf index: the probability that \(i\) is pivotal when all other voters join independently with probability \(\tfrac12\).

For distinct voters \(i,j\), let \(R\) be a uniformly random coalition excluding both. Conditioning on whether the other voter joins gives
\[ \alpha_i=\frac12\mathbb E\!\left[f(R\cup\{i\})-f(R)+f(R\cup\{i,j\})-f(R\cup\{j\})\right]. \]
Subtracting the corresponding expression for \(\alpha_j\) yields
\[ \alpha_i-\alpha_j=\mathbb E\!\left[f(R\cup\{i\})-f(R\cup\{j\})\right]. \]
If \(i\)'s weight is at least \(j\)'s, then \(f(R\cup\{i\})\ge f(R\cup\{j\})\) for every \(R\). Consequently, absolute and normalized Banzhaf indices respect the weight order.

The normalization denominator is positive: the empty coalition loses and the grand coalition wins, so a chain adding voters one at a time contains a pivotal addition.

Members of the same type have equal indices by symmetry. Therefore, for every admissible replication size \(N\), the vector
\[ x_{t,N}=N\beta_{t,N}(w,q) \]
satisfies
\[ x_{1,N}\ge\cdots\ge x_{\tau,N}\ge0, \qquad \sum_t\mu_tx_{t,N}=\sum_tN\mu_t\beta_{t,N}(w,q)=1. \]
Thus \(x_N\in\mathcal C\), and hence
\[ F(w,q)=\limsup_NG(x_N)\ge\min_{x\in\mathcal C}G(x). \tag{1} \]

This argument does not require convergence of Banzhaf indices at arbitrary quotas.

2. Every rational vector in the constraint set is attained at half quota

We use the following known theorem: for nested growing electorates with fixed positive integer voter weights taking finitely many values, if the gcd of the weight values occurring infinitely often is \(1\), then at half quota the normalized Banzhaf ratio of any two fixed voters converges to their weight ratio. The convention is that equality with the quota wins. This is [Lindner–Machover, Theorem 3.6](https://www.lse.ac.uk/cpnss/assets/documents/voting-power-and-procedures/publications/2004/lindner.pdf).

Let \(w\in\mathcal C\) be rational. We check its applicability:

  • Removing zero-weight voters leaves positive voters' absolute and normalized Banzhaf indices unchanged: the removed voters are dummies.
  • Clearing denominators of the positive \(w_t\), then dividing by their gcd, gives positive integer weights \(a_t=cw_t\) whose gcd is \(1\).
  • Write admissible replication sizes as \(N=kD\), where \(D\) is the least common multiple of the denominators of the masses. These electorates can be nested by adding \(D\mu_t\) members of each type at each step.
  • Every positive-weight type occurs infinitely often because \(\mu_t>0\).
  • Scaling the replicated game by \(cN\) gives fixed individual weights \(a_t\), with quota exactly half their total.

Since \(w_1>0\), the theorem therefore gives, for positive-weight types,
\[ \frac{\beta_{t,N}(w,\tfrac12)}{\beta_{1,N}(w,\tfrac12)} \longrightarrow\frac{w_t}{w_1}. \]
For zero-weight types both the index and the corresponding weight ratio are zero. The denominator is positive for every \(N\), because a maximum-weight voter has positive power whenever any voter does.

Set \(r_{t,N}=\beta_{t,N}/\beta_{1,N}\). Normalization gives
\[ N\beta_{1,N}=\frac{1}{\sum_t\mu_tr_{t,N}} \longrightarrow \frac{1}{\sum_t\mu_tw_t/w_1}=w_1. \]
Consequently,
\[ N\beta_{t,N}(w,\tfrac12)\longrightarrow w_t \qquad\text{for every type }t. \]
Since the sum defining the objective has finitely many terms, this proves
\[ F(w,\tfrac12)=G(w) \qquad\text{for every rational }w\in\mathcal C. \tag{2} \]

3. Computing the optimal vector exactly

Here is an explicit weighted pool-adjacent-violators algorithm.

Start with consecutive singleton blocks. For each block \(B\), maintain its mass and target sum,
\[ A_B=\sum_{t\in B}\mu_t, \qquad T_B=\sum_{t\in B}\mu_t\pi_t, \]
and its mean \(a_B=T_B/A_B\).

Process types from left to right using a stack of blocks. Whenever the last two blocks \(I,J\) violate decreasing order, meaning \(a_I<a_J\), merge them and add their masses and target sums. Repeat until the violation disappears. At termination, set \(w_t=a_B\) for every \(t\in B\).

All means are rational and nonnegative. The final block means are nonincreasing, and
\[ \sum_t\mu_tw_t=\sum_BA_Ba_B=\sum_B T_B=\sum_t\mu_t\pi_t=1. \]
Thus the output belongs to \(\mathcal C\).

For completeness, we prove optimality of this algorithm.

The block invariant

Every block produced by the algorithm has the following property: the weighted target mean of every nonempty prefix of that block is at most the mean of the entire block.

It holds for singletons. Suppose blocks \(I,J\) satisfying it are merged, with means \(a<b\), masses \(A,B\), and merged mean
\[ c=\frac{Aa+Bb}{A+B}. \]
A prefix contained in \(I\) has mean at most \(a<c\). A prefix extending into \(J\), whose portion inside \(J\) has mass \(h\) and mean \(d\), satisfies \(d\le b\) and \(h\le B\). Its mean is therefore at most \((Aa+hb)/(A+h)\). Moreover,
\[ c-\frac{Aa+hb}{A+h} =\frac{A(B-h)(b-a)}{(A+B)(A+h)}\ge0. \]
The invariant is preserved.

Optimality certificate

Define residuals and prefix sums by
\[ \rho_t=\mu_t(w_t-\pi_t), \qquad R_k=\sum_{t=1}^{k}\rho_t. \]
Every final block has residual sum zero, because its fitted value is its weighted target mean. The block invariant implies that residual sums of prefixes within a block are nonnegative. Hence
\[ R_k\ge0\quad\text{for all }k, \qquad R_\tau=0. \]

Because \(w\) is constant on each block,
\[ \sum_t\rho_tw_t=0. \]
For any \(x\in\mathcal C\), summation by parts gives
\[ \sum_t\rho_tx_t =\sum_{k=1}^{\tau-1}R_k(x_k-x_{k+1})+R_\tau x_\tau \ge0. \]
Expanding the squares now yields
\[ G(x)-G(w) =\sum_t\mu_t(x_t-w_t)^2 +2\sum_t\rho_t(x_t-w_t) \ge0. \]
Thus \(w\) minimizes \(G\) over \(\mathcal C\). Since all masses are positive, the squared term also proves uniqueness.

4. Bit complexity and the optimal design

There are \(\tau\) block insertions and at most \(\tau-1\) merges, so the stack algorithm uses \(O(\tau)\) exact rational operations and comparisons.

All block quantities are sums of input masses or products \(\mu_t\pi_t\). A common denominator for each such sum can be chosen as a product of input denominators, whose binary length is \(O(L)\). Numerators also have polynomial length; indeed, the nonnegative block masses and target sums are at most \(1\). Block means and comparison cross-products therefore have polynomial bit length. Exact integer arithmetic implements the algorithm in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), and the output has polynomial encoding length.

Return this rational vector \(w\) and \(q=\tfrac12\). Combining (1), optimality of \(w\), and (2), we obtain
\[ \inf_{(u,r)\text{ feasible}}F(u,r) \ge\min_{x\in\mathcal C}G(x) =G(w) =F(w,\tfrac12). \]
Since the returned design is feasible, equality holds throughout. It attains the infimum exactly and therefore satisfies the requested guarantee for every input \(\varepsilon>0\).
