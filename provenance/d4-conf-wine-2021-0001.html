Result

For every fixed binary rational \(H>1\),
\(\boxed{R^*(H)=H}\).

More precisely, writing \(n=N/2\), every admissible deterministic OSP mechanism satisfies
\[ \sup_b\frac{\mathrm{SC}_{\mu_N(b)}(M_N(b))}{\mathrm{OPT}(\mu_N(b))} \ge \frac{nH}{n+H-1}. \]
Below, all displayed mathematics uses the requested inline delimiters.

1. A consequence of the OSP inequalities

Consider a query separating supplier \(i\)'s compatible costs \(1\) and \(H\). There cannot exist two profiles compatible with this query such that:

  • At profile \(b^\ell\), supplier \(i\) reports \(1\) and is unselected.
  • At profile \(b^h\), supplier \(i\) reports \(H\) and is selected.

Indeed, let \(P=p_i(b^\ell)\) and \(Q=p_i(b^h)\). Applying OSP with true cost \(1\), truthful profile \(b^\ell\), and alternative profile \(b^h\), gives
\(P\ge Q-1\).

Applying OSP in the reverse direction, with true cost \(H\), gives
\(Q-H\ge P\).

Consequently,
\(Q-H\ge P\ge Q-1\),
which implies \(H\le1\), a contradiction. This argument permits arbitrary real payments.

Thus, at such a query, at least one of the following statements holds:

  • Every compatible profile with \(b_i=1\) selects \(i\).
  • Every compatible profile with \(b_i=H\) leaves \(i\) unselected.

Because the only feasible allocations select an entire role, either statement fixes the allocation throughout the corresponding report branch.

2. A finite-population lower bound

Fix an admissible OSP mechanism \(M_N\), and let \(n=N/2\).

If the mechanism never makes an informative query, every profile follows the same history and receives the same allocation. Giving all suppliers in the selected role cost \(H\), and all suppliers in the other role cost \(1\), produces ratio \(H\).

Otherwise, consider its first informative query. All profiles reach this node: preceding queries, if any, have not distinguished any costs. The queried supplier \(i\) therefore has compatible domain \(\{1,H\}\), and every other supplier's cost remains unrestricted.

Let \(R\) be \(i\)'s role and \(S\) the other role. By Section 1, at least one of the following cases applies.

Case I: Every profile with \(b_i=1\) selects role \(R\).

Choose a profile with \(b_i=1\), all other suppliers in \(R\) having cost \(H\), and every supplier in \(S\) having cost \(1\). The mechanism selects \(R\), and
\(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu_N(b)}(x^R)=[1+(n-1)H]/N\),
whereas
\(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu_N(b)}(x^S)=n/N\).

Since \(1+(n-1)H>n\), the optimal allocation is \(x^S\). The resulting ratio is
\(a_n=[1+(n-1)H]/n=H-(H-1)/n\).

Case II: Every profile with \(b_i=H\) selects role \(S\).

Choose a profile with \(b_i=H\), all other suppliers in \(R\) having cost \(1\), and every supplier in \(S\) having cost \(H\). The mechanism selects \(S\), and
\(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu_N(b)}(x^S)=nH/N\),
whereas
\(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu_N(b)}(x^R)=[H+n-1]/N\).

The difference between the corresponding unnormalized costs is
\(nH-(H+n-1)=(n-1)(H-1)>0\).
Hence the optimal allocation is \(x^R\), and the ratio is
\(c_n=nH/(n+H-1)\).

Finally,
\(a_n-c_n=(n-1)(H-1)^2/[n(n+H-1)]>0\).

Therefore, in either case,
\(\displaystyle \sup_b\frac{\mathrm{SC}_{\mu_N(b)}(M_N(b))}{\mathrm{OPT}(\mu_N(b))}\ge\frac{nH}{n+H-1}=\frac{NH}{N+2(H-1)}\).

These witnesses use only three positive role-cost masses, namely \(1/N\), \((n-1)/N\), and \(1/2\). Thus the lower bound already holds on the normalized grid with support size \(\tau=3\).

3. Matching upper bound and asymptotic conclusion

For every report profile and either role \(r\), the role's mass is \(1/2\) and its suppliers' costs lie in \(\{1,H\}\). Therefore
\(1/2\le\mathrm{SC}_{\mu_N(b)}(x^r)\le H/2\).

It follows that every feasible allocation has approximation ratio at most \(H\).

An admissible OSP sequence exists: for every \(N\), use a mechanism with no queries, always select \(A\), and pay every supplier zero. OSP is vacuous for this mechanism, and its worst-case ratio is exactly \(H\).

For any admissible OSP sequence, define
\(q_N=\sup_b\mathrm{SC}_{\mu_N(b)}(M_N(b))/\mathrm{OPT}(\mu_N(b))\).
The preceding bounds give
\(\displaystyle \frac{NH}{N+2(H-1)}\le q_N\le H\).

Because \(H\) is fixed independently of \(N\), the lower bound tends to \(H\). Hence \(q_N\) converges to \(H\) along even population sizes for every admissible OSP sequence. Taking the infimum of their limit superiors proves
\(\boxed{R^*(H)=H}\).

4. Running time

With \(m=2\) and \(H\) fixed, the attaining mechanism makes zero cost queries and specifies its selected role in \(O(1)\) time. Explicitly outputting all allocation entries and zero payments takes \(O(N)\) time. Both bounds are independent of the society's support size \(\tau\) and input length \(L\); the mechanism does not receive the society's masses.
