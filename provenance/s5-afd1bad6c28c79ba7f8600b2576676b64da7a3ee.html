A society is given by its support: its \(\tau\) distinct locations with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; here the masses are positive multiples of \(1/5\) summing to one. \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Result

The supremum is \(\boxed{7-4\sqrt2}\), and it is attained by
\[ y=(0,0,a,a,\tfrac12),\qquad a=\frac{\sqrt2-1}{2}. \]
Equivalently, the attaining society is \(\mu=\frac25\delta_0+\frac25\delta_a+\frac15\delta_{1/2}\).

Global upper bound

We use the established five-agent PCD theorem: for every nonconstant five-agent profile on a circle of circumference one, allowing coincident locations, PCD’s expected total social cost is at most \((7-4\sqrt2)\) times the minimum total social cost. This is [Theorem 4 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17435v1#Thmtheorem4).

Its hypotheses match the present formulation. A support mass \(k/5\) expands into \(k\) coincident entries, so every admissible society corresponds to a five-agent profile. The lottery is precisely the same PCD lottery: entry \(i\) receives its facing gap \(g_{i+2}\), with probabilities aggregated at coincident locations.

Writing \(C_y(z)=\sum_{i=1}^5d(y_i,z)\), we have \(SC_{\mu_y}(z)=C_y(z)/5\). Consequently,
\[ \frac{\mathbb E[SC_{\mu_y}(Z_y)]}{\min_z SC_{\mu_y}(z)} = \frac{\mathbb E[C_y(Z_y)]}{\min_z C_y(z)} \le 7-4\sqrt2. \]
The denominator is positive: the continuous nonnegative function \(C_y\) attains its minimum on the compact circle, and a zero value would require all five entries to coincide.

Direct verification of attainment

For \(y=(0,0,a,a,\frac12)\), the clockwise gaps are
\[ (g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4,g_5) =(0,a,0,\tfrac12-a,\tfrac12). \]
Thus the aggregated PCD probabilities are
\[ \Pr(Z_y=0)=\tfrac12-a,\qquad \Pr(Z_y=a)=\tfrac12,\qquad \Pr(Z_y=\tfrac12)=a. \]

We first verify the optimum over the entire circle. Because \(0\) and \(\frac12\) are antipodal, \(d(0,z)+d(\frac12,z)=\frac12\) for every \(z\). Therefore, by the triangle inequality,
\[ \begin{aligned} C_y(z) &=2d(0,z)+2d(a,z)+d(\tfrac12,z)\\ &=\tfrac12+d(0,z)+2d(a,z)\\ &\ge \tfrac12+a+d(a,z) \ge \tfrac12+a. \end{aligned} \]
Equality holds at \(z=a\), so \(\min_z C_y(z)=\frac12+a\).

The costs at the three possible outcomes are
\[ C_y(0)=\tfrac12+2a,\qquad C_y(a)=\tfrac12+a,\qquad C_y(\tfrac12)=2-2a. \]
Hence
\[ \mathbb E[C_y(Z_y)] =(\tfrac12-a)(\tfrac12+2a) +\tfrac12(\tfrac12+a) +a(2-2a) =\tfrac12+3a-4a^2. \]
Our choice of \(a\) satisfies \(4a^2+4a=1\). The ratio is therefore
\[ \frac{\tfrac12+3a-4a^2}{\tfrac12+a} =\frac{7a-\tfrac12}{a+\tfrac12} =\frac{7\sqrt2-8}{\sqrt2} =7-4\sqrt2. \]

This attains the global upper bound. Rotations, reflections, and relabelings of this profile also attain it.
