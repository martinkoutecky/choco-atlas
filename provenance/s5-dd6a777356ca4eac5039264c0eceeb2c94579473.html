The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational finite-support distribution μ over complete utility types and an ordered stream of item kinds with per-capita supplies \(q_j\), run the paper’s randomized item-by-item ENVY rule on populations of size \(N\), with \(Nμ_t\) agents of type \(t\) and \(Nq_j\) copies at stage \(j\). In the \(N\to\infty\) expected mass-flow limit, is the utilitarian competitive ratio against the offline allocation unbounded, even with two utility types?

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) distinct utility types \(u_t\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{m}\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length, and any other parameters.
The input also gives an ordered stream of \(m\ge1\) item kinds with binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_j>0\); require \(\max_t u_{tj}>0\) for every kind. Utilities are additive, bids are truthful, and agents have no bundle-size constraints.
For each positive integer \(N\) making all \(N\mu_t,Nq_j\) integral, start with empty bundles and process \(Nq_j\) indivisible copies of kind \(j\) consecutively, in stream order. An agent of type \(t\) values each such copy at \(u_{tj}\).
For a current allocation \(A\), define the envy index \(E(A)=\frac{\sum_{a,b}\max\{0,u_a(A_b)-u_a(A_a)\}}{\sum_{a,b}u_a(A_b)}\), summing over all \(N\) agents. ENVY assigns the next copy uniformly among positive-value recipients whose receiving it minimizes the resulting index among positive-value recipients, conditional on the current realized allocation.
Let \(X^{(N)}_{tj}\) count copies of kind \(j\) assigned to type \(t\). The expected mass flow is \(x_{tj}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb E[X^{(N)}_{tj}]/N\), along admissible integers. Keep the rational input fixed while taking this limit.
When these limits exist, set \(W_{\mathrm{ENVY}}=\sum_{t,j}u_{tj}x_{tj}\) and \(W_{\mathrm{OPT}}=\sum_jq_j\max_tu_{tj}\). The utilitarian competitive ratio is \(W_{\mathrm{OPT}}/W_{\mathrm{ENVY}}\), interpreted as \(+\infty\) if the denominator is zero.

Does the expected mass-flow limit exist for every such input? In that limit, with \(\tau=2\), is the utilitarian competitive ratio unbounded over rational inputs, or is there a universal finite bound?

For finite populations of \(n\) agents, ENVY has utilitarian competitive ratio at least \(n/2\), already on instances with two utility types; its egalitarian competitive ratio is unbounded. [Source](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~tw/agwepia2019.pdf)

Conclusions

The expected mass-flow limit need not exist. A fixed rational input with $m=3$ and $\tau=3$ gives different deterministic normalized flows along two admissible population subsequences.

For $\tau=2$, the utilitarian competitive ratio is unbounded among inputs whose expected mass-flow limits exist. This already occurs with one item kind.

Both statements are proved below.

1. Exact marginal scores

For an allocation with agent bundles $b_a$, write its envy numerator as
$$ F=\sum_{a,b}[u_a(b_b)-u_a(b_a)]_+, \qquad [z]_+=\max\{0,z\}. $$

When the next copy has kind $j$, the denominator of the envy index increases by
$$ \sum_a u_{aj}, $$
independently of its recipient. This quantity is strictly positive under the input assumptions. Therefore ENVY chooses a positive-value recipient minimizing the change in $F$. This comparison is valid even when the current allocation is empty.

Define
$$ H_v(z)=\min\{v,\max\{0,z\}\}. $$
For a recipient $r$, put $v_a=u_{aj}$. Its exact marginal score is
$$ \Delta_r = \sum_a H_{v_a}\bigl(u_a(b_r)-u_a(b_a)+v_a\bigr) - \sum_b H_{v_r}\bigl(u_r(b_b)-u_r(b_r)\bigr) -v_r. $$

Indeed, allocating to $r$ increases incoming envy by the first sum and decreases outgoing envy by the second sum. The first sum includes a spurious self-contribution $v_r$, removed by the last term.

Equivalently, if $n_{h,c}$ agents have type $h$ and bundle $c$, the score of an agent of type $t$ with bundle $b$ is
$$ \Delta_j(t,b) = -u_{tj} + \sum_{h,c}n_{h,c} \left( H_{u_{hj}}\bigl(u_h(b)-u_h(c)+u_{hj}\bigr) - H_{u_{tj}}\bigl(u_t(c)-u_t(b)\bigr) \right). $$
Thus all agents in a type–bundle cell have the same score.

2. The one-kind allocation rule

Lemma. Starting from empty bundles and allocating copies of a single kind, ENVY serves positive-value agents in rounds. Each completed round gives every positive-value agent one copy. Within a round, agents are served in decreasing order of their value for that kind.

Proof. Write $v_a$ for an agent’s value and let $P$ be the positive-value agents. Zero-value agents receive nothing, contribute no envy as observers, and their empty bundles cannot be envied.

Suppose agents in $P$ currently have either $h$ or $h+1$ copies. Let $S$ be those with $h+1$ copies, and set
$$ C=\sum_{a\in P}v_a, \qquad C_S=\sum_{a\in S}v_a. $$

For an unserved agent $s\in P\setminus S$, the incoming-envy increase is $C-C_S-v_s$, and the outgoing-envy reduction is $|S|v_s$. Hence
$$ \Delta_s=C-C_S-(|S|+1)v_s. $$
For an already-served agent $r\in S$, the incoming increase is $C-v_r$, and outgoing envy does not decrease:
$$ \Delta_r=C-v_r. $$
Consequently,
$$ \Delta_r-\Delta_s = C_S-v_r+(|S|+1)v_s >0. $$
Here $C_S\ge v_r$ and $v_s>0$. Thus an already-served agent cannot receive another copy while an unserved agent remains.

Among unserved agents, the score is minimized by maximizing $v_s$. Starting from empty bundles, induction proves the claimed rule. $\square$

This lemma also applies to the first block of a longer stream, because all bundles are initially empty.

3. Unbounded ratio with two types

For an integer $k\ge1$, set $p=2^{-k}$. Consider one kind with supply $q_1=1$ and two types:

| Type | Mass | Utility |
|---|---:|---:|
| High | $p$ | $1$ |
| Low | $1-p$ | $p$ |

All input quantities are rational, both masses are positive, and both types value the kind positively.

For every admissible $N$, there are $N$ agents and $N$ copies. By the lemma, every agent receives exactly one copy. Therefore the normalized type flows are deterministic:
$$ \frac{X^{(N)}_{\mathrm{High},1}}{N}=p, \qquad \frac{X^{(N)}_{\mathrm{Low},1}}{N}=1-p. $$
In particular, their expected mass-flow limits exist.

The limiting welfare and optimal welfare are
$$ W_{\mathrm{ENVY}} =p+(1-p)p=2p-p^2, \qquad W_{\mathrm{OPT}}=1. $$
Thus
$$ \frac{W_{\mathrm{OPT}}}{W_{\mathrm{ENVY}}} = \frac{1}{2p-p^2} > \frac{1}{2p} = 2^{k-1}. $$
This proves unboundedness for $\tau=2$.

For each fixed $k$, the input remains fixed throughout the population limit. Only after establishing that limit do we vary $k$.
