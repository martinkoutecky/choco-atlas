The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite-support bidder types \(t=(v_t,B_t)\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), position classes \(j=1,\ldots,m\) with qualities \(q_j\) and rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_j\), and fixed \(\epsilon>0\), can one compute in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) an allocation of mass \(\lambda_{t,j,p}\) to position class \(j\) at integer price \(p\), or to no position at price \(0\), such that each type’s masses sum to \(\mu_t\), each class’s assigned mass is at most \(\sigma_j\), and every assigned bundle satisfies \(0\le p\le \min(B_t,v_tq_j)\)? Require every bundle assigned to type \(t\) to satisfy \(v_tq_j-p\ge v_tq_k-r-\epsilon B_{\max}\) against every positive-mass bundle \((k,r)\) with \(r\le B_t-\epsilon B_{\max}\). The returned social welfare \(\sum_{t,j,p}\lambda_{t,j,p}v_tq_j\) must be at least the maximum welfare attainable in the same mass model under exact envy-freeness, where the comparison has no additive loss and applies whenever \(r\le B_t\). Each unit of mass receives one integral position or none; the population mass, not the bundles, is continuous.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) bidder types \(t=(v_t,B_t)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Values and budgets are positive integers. There are \(m\ge1\) position classes with integer qualities \(q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_m>0\) and positive binary-rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_j\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length and \(B_{\max}=\max_t B_t\).
An allocation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(\lambda_{t,j,p}\), represented by their nonzero entries. Each individual receives one position at an integer payment, or the null bundle \((0,0)\), with \(q_0=0\). Require \(\sum_{j,p}\lambda_{t,j,p}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,p}\lambda_{t,j,p}\le\sigma_j\) for \(j\ge1\), and \(0\le p\le\min\{B_t,v_tq_j\}\) whenever \(\lambda_{t,j,p}>0\).
For fixed rational \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\), call an allocation relaxed envy-free if every \(\lambda_{t,j,p}>0\) satisfies \(v_tq_j-p\ge v_tq_k-r-\epsilon B_{\max}\) against every bundle \((k,r)\) assigned positive total mass with \(r\le B_t-\epsilon B_{\max}\). Comparisons include bundles received by the same type and the null bundle when assigned; unassigned positions impose no comparisons.
Exact envy-freeness instead requires \(v_tq_j-p\ge v_tq_k-r\) whenever \(r\le B_t\). Let \(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) be the maximum welfare \(\sum_{t,j,p}\lambda_{t,j,p}v_tq_j\) among exact envy-free allocations in this same model. Envy is evaluated for each realized bundle, rather than for a type's average allocation or payment.

For every fixed \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\), can a relaxed envy-free allocation with welfare at least \(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{EF}}\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with the polynomial allowed to depend on \(\epsilon\), or is obtaining this guarantee NP-hard for some fixed \(\epsilon\)?

For finite lists of bidders, Tang and Zhang establish a scheme polynomial in the bidder and position counts for fixed \(\epsilon\), with the same relaxed envy and welfare guarantees; they also establish a corresponding scheme for randomized allocations evaluated through expected clicks and payments. [Source](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/15/Papers/098.pdf)
No result settling this support-encoded mass model was located in the literature search.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

For every fixed rational \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\), the requested allocation can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The key is to round payments down to a constant-size integer grid. Once payments lie on that grid, only the highest assigned quality at each price matters for envy comparisons. Those qualities can be enumerated, and the remaining allocation problem is a rational linear program.

1. Rounding payments preserves the required guarantee

Write \(B=B_{\max}\) and \(D=\epsilon B\). Define the positive integer grid spacing
\[ s=\lfloor D\rfloor+1 \]
and the price set
\[ P=\{0,s,2s,\ldots,s\lfloor B/s\rfloor\}. \]

Here and below, displayed expressions use the same mathematical notation as inline expressions. In particular, the number of grid prices is \(K=|P|=\lfloor B/s\rfloor+1\). Since \(s>\epsilon B\), we have \(B/s<1/\epsilon\), and therefore
\[ K\le \lceil 1/\epsilon\rceil. \]

For an integer payment \(p\), let \(\rho(p)=s\lfloor p/s\rfloor\). Then
\[ 0\le p-\rho(p)\le s-1=\lfloor D\rfloor\le D. \]

Lemma. Rounding every payment in an exact envy-free allocation from \(p\) to \(\rho(p)\) produces a relaxed envy-free allocation with unchanged welfare.

Proof. Preserve each assigned quality and mass, merging entries when their rounded payments coincide. Payments remain nonnegative integers. Because payments only decrease, budget feasibility and individual rationality remain valid. Type masses, supplies, and welfare are unchanged.

Consider any realized rounded bundle \((j,\rho(p))\) of type \(t\), and any globally assigned rounded bundle \((k,\rho(r))\) satisfying \(\rho(r)\le B_t-D\). Choose original positive-mass entries witnessing these rounded bundles. The rounding bound gives
\[ r\le \rho(r)+D\le B_t. \]

Exact envy-freeness of the original allocation therefore implies \(v_tq_j-p\ge v_tq_k-r\). Consequently,
\[ v_tq_j-\rho(p) \ge v_tq_j-p \ge v_tq_k-r \ge v_tq_k-\rho(r)-D. \]

This is exactly the required relaxed envy inequality. The argument includes comparisons within a type and comparisons with the null bundle. \(\square\)

2. Enumerating the relevant comparisons

For a fixed price \(r\), every type prefers a larger quality at that price, because \(v_t>0\). Thus, if the largest quality assigned at price \(r\) is \(Q_r\), comparison against every bundle assigned at that price is equivalent to comparison against \((Q_r,r)\).

For each price \(p\in P\), enumerate one of these \(m+2\) choices:

  • The price is inactive, meaning no bundle may be assigned at that price.
  • The price is active, with a chosen upper quality \(Q_p\in\{q_0,q_1,\ldots,q_m\}\), where \(q_0=0\).

There are at most \((m+2)^K\) enumerations. Repeated qualities can simply produce duplicate enumerations.

A chosen upper quality need not actually be assigned. Requiring comparisons against such a hypothetical bundle only strengthens the constraints used by the algorithm. The correctness proof below shows that one enumeration uses the actual largest qualities of the rounded optimal allocation.

3. The linear program for one enumeration

Fix an enumeration. Call a triple \((t,j,p)\), with \(t\) a type, \(j\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\), and \(p\in P\), eligible when all the following conditions hold:

  • Price \(p\) is active and \(q_j\le Q_p\).
  • If \(j=0\), then \(p=0\).
  • The bundle is feasible: \(p\le B_t\) and \(p\le v_tq_j\).
  • For every active price \(r\) satisfying \(r\le B_t-D\),

\[ v_tq_j-p\ge v_tQ_r-r-D. \]

Eligibility is determined by arithmetic on the input and the enumeration; it does not depend on allocation variables.

Introduce a nonnegative variable \(\lambda_{t,j,p}\) for each eligible triple. Solve the linear program that maximizes
\[ \sum_{\text{eligible }(t,j,p)}\lambda_{t,j,p}v_tq_j \]
subject to the type-mass equations
\[ \sum_{\text{eligible }(t,j,p)}\lambda_{t,j,p}=\mu_t \qquad\text{for every type }t, \]
and the supply inequalities
\[ \sum_{\text{eligible }(t,j,p)}\lambda_{t,j,p}\le \sigma_j \qquad\text{for every }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}. \]

Discard infeasible enumerations. Return an optimal solution from the feasible enumeration with largest objective value.

At least one enumeration is feasible: activate only price zero with upper quality zero, and assign every type its entire mass to the null bundle.

4. Every returned allocation is relaxed envy-free

The linear-program constraints enforce type masses and supplies. Eligibility enforces integer payments, budget feasibility, individual rationality, and the restriction on the null bundle.

Now consider a positive entry \(\lambda_{t,j,p}\) and any globally assigned bundle \((k,r)\) with \(r\le B_t-D\). Since that bundle is assigned, price \(r\) is active and \(q_k\le Q_r\). Eligibility gives
\[ v_tq_j-p\ge v_tQ_r-r-D. \]
Because \(v_t>0\), it follows that
\[ v_tq_j-p\ge v_tq_k-r-D. \]

Thus every realized bundle satisfies every required comparison. This also covers bundles assigned to the same type and the null bundle when assigned.

5. The algorithm attains at least the exact optimum

First, the exact optimum is attained by a rational allocation. To see this, there are finitely many feasible integer-payment triples. For any subset of triples whose pairwise exact envy comparisons all hold, the mass and supply constraints define a bounded rational polytope, allowing some entries in the subset to have zero mass. Its allocations remain exact envy-free because deleting bundles removes comparisons. Every exact envy-free allocation belongs to one such polytope. Their finite union is nonempty, since it contains the all-null allocation, and each nonempty polytope has a rational welfare-maximizing vertex. Hence their maximum welfare is attained rationally.

Take an exact optimal allocation and apply the rounding lemma. Let \(\bar\lambda\) denote the resulting allocation. It is relaxed envy-free and has welfare \(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{EF}}\).

Choose the enumeration that makes a price active exactly when it occurs in \(\bar\lambda\), and, for each active price \(p\), sets \(Q_p\) to the largest quality actually assigned at that price.

Every positive entry of \(\bar\lambda\) is eligible:

  • Its price is active and its quality is at most \(Q_p\).
  • Its payment and null-bundle restrictions hold by construction.
  • For every active affordable price \(r\), the bundle with quality \(Q_r\) is actually assigned. Relaxed envy-freeness of \(\bar\lambda\) therefore gives precisely the eligibility inequality against \((Q_r,r)\).

Accordingly, \(\bar\lambda\) is feasible for this enumeration’s linear program. That program’s optimal welfare is at least \(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{EF}}\). The algorithm chooses the best enumeration, so its returned welfare is also at least \(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{EF}}\).

6. Bit complexity and output representation

Each enumeration has at most \(\tau(m+1)K\) variables. Eligibility requires at most \(K\) comparisons per candidate triple. There are \(\tau\) mass equations and \(m\) supply inequalities.

All arithmetic has bit length polynomial in \(L\), with constants allowed to depend on the fixed rational \(\epsilon\). In particular, grid payments are at most \(B\), valuation products are input-integer products, and masses and supplies retain their input rational representations.

Polynomial-time rational linear programming yields an optimal rational solution of polynomial encoding length. The output lists at most \(\tau(m+1)K\) nonzero entries.

The total running time is therefore bounded by
\[ (m+2)^{\lceil1/\epsilon\rceil} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L), \]
where the polynomial may depend on \(\epsilon\), as permitted.

This proof operates directly on support-encoded masses. It does not require expanding them into finite bidder lists or using the expected-click interpretation of randomized allocations in the [Tang–Zhang paper](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/15/Papers/098.pdf).
