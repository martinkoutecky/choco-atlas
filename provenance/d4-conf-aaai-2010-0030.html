The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over types \(\theta=(a,b,\phi)\), per-capita inventories \(q_j\), an event and tie-breaking order, and the score-gap condition from Theorem 2, compute the mass flow \(y_{\theta j}\) when infinitesimal agents arrive and best respond under SR as available inventory changes; determine whether this flow equals the fluid APSD flow.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; normalize \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\).
A type is \(\theta=(a,b,\phi)\), with integer periods \(1\le a\le b\) and a strict ranking \(\phi\) of \(m\ge1\) item categories; \(r_\phi(j)\) denotes the rank of category \(j\). Each agent demands one unit. Initial inventories are binary rationals \(q_j>0\) with \(\sum_jq_j=1\).
All inventory is initially available. Reservation removes inventory immediately; delivery occurs at departure and never replenishes inventory. Input orders resolve simultaneous arrivals between types and score ties between categories; each type's mass is processed consecutively in arrival order.
Scores are fixed at \(S_j=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta r_\phi(j)\), independently of reports and remaining inventory. For scores sorted as \(S_{(1)}\le\cdots\le S_{(m)}\), promise \(S_{(k+1)}-S_{(k)}<1\) for every \(1\le k<m\).
The scoring rule (SR), on a reported strict ranking \(\phi'\), reserves an available category minimizing \(r_{\phi'}(j)-S_j\), using the supplied category order for ties; availability means strictly positive remaining inventory.
Every infinitesimal agent best responds at the current inventory: among all strict-ranking reports, it selects one obtaining its most-preferred attainable category. Reports may satisfy \(a\le a'\le b'\le b\); the flow considered uses immediate reporting \(a'=a\) and truthful departure \(b'=b\).
Within each type's mass, recompute the best response whenever a category is exhausted. Let \(y_{\theta j}\) be the resulting reserved mass. Fluid arrival-priority serial dictatorship (APSD) uses the same processing order and inventories, with every infinitesimal agent reserving its most-preferred available category.

Can the exact SR flow \(y\) and its equality with fluid APSD be computed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length, or is the equality decision problem NP-hard? Under the score-gap promise, must the two flows always coincide?

For finite agents and indivisible items, strategic SR coincides with APSD under the strict adjacent-score-gap condition, and a statewise optimal ranking manipulation is known ([Zou, Gujar and Parkes, 2010](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/7630/7491)).
No result settling this exact fluid problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one and inventories to total supply one, matching the balanced assignment setting and avoiding undefined outcomes when supply is exhausted before all demand is served.
  • Interpreted items with per-capita inventories as categories; ranking positions range over categories, independently of their capacities.
  • Computed fixed population scores from the true preference marginal of the supplied distribution, rather than updating scores from strategic reports or remaining agents.
  • Specified integer arrival and departure periods, strict complete rankings, immediate inventory reservation, and irreversible consumption upon delivery.
  • Resolved simultaneous arrivals by consecutive processing of whole type masses using the supplied type order; resolved SR score ties using a supplied category order.
  • Interpreted best response as the statewise ranking manipulation used by the anchor. Retained the admissible timing-report domain but specified immediate reporting and truthful departure for the requested arrival-driven flow.
  • Interpreted successive-score gaps after sorting all category scores, with the strict inequality retained throughout inventory depletion.

Result

On every input satisfying the strict adjacent-score-gap promise, strategic SR and fluid APSD have exactly the same flow. The common flow can be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The proof below works directly in the stated fluid model; it does not require transferring the finite-agent result of [Zou, Gujar and Parkes (2010)](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/7630/7491).

1. Every category is attainable at every state

Write the categories in nondecreasing score order as \(c_1,\ldots,c_m\), breaking equal scores arbitrarily. Thus \(S_{c_k}=S_{(k)}\).

Fix a desired category \(c_t\). Consider the strict report that places \(c_t\) first and lists every other category in increasing score order:

\(c_t\succ c_1\succ\cdots\succ c_{t-1}\succ c_{t+1}\succ\cdots\succ c_m\).

We prove that this report makes \(c_t\) the unique SR winner when all categories are available.

The reported rank of \(c_t\) is \(1\). For any competing category \(c_k\), its SR objective minus that of \(c_t\) is

\(\bigl(r_{\phi'}(c_k)-S_{c_k}\bigr)-\bigl(1-S_{c_t}\bigr)=r_{\phi'}(c_k)-1-(S_{c_k}-S_{c_t})\).

There are two cases.

  • If \(k<t\), then \(r_{\phi'}(c_k)=k+1\) and \(S_{c_k}\le S_{c_t}\). Consequently, this difference is at least \(k>0\).
  • If \(k>t\), then \(r_{\phi'}(c_k)=k\). The promised adjacent gaps give

\(S_{c_k}-S_{c_t}=\sum_{\ell=t}^{k-1}(S_{c_{\ell+1}}-S_{c_\ell})<k-t\).
Consequently, the objective difference is strictly greater than
\((k-1)-(k-t)=t-1\ge0\).

Thus every competitor has a strictly larger objective. The supplied category tie order is immaterial.

Now let \(A\) be any nonempty set of available categories containing \(c_t\). The same report still makes \(c_t\) the unique minimizer over \(A\), because removing competitors preserves all the strict inequalities.

Therefore every available category is attainable at every inventory state. For \(m=1\), this conclusion holds immediately.

2. Best responses coincide with APSD choices

Fix a type \(\theta=(a,b,\phi)\) and a nonempty available set \(A\). By Section 1, the set of attainable categories is exactly \(A\): every member is attainable, and SR cannot reserve an unavailable category.

Since \(\phi\) is strict, there is a unique most-preferred category in \(A\), namely

\(j^*=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{j\in A}r_\phi(j)\).

The stipulated best response must therefore obtain \(j^*\). Fluid APSD reserves exactly this category.

This conclusion applies throughout a type’s processing. While the available set remains unchanged, its best-response category remains unchanged. When that category is exhausted, both rules select the same most-preferred category from the remaining set.

Starting from identical inventories, induction over type completions and category exhaustions therefore shows that the two processes reserve identical amounts at every step. In particular,

\(y_{\theta j}^{\mathrm{SR}}=y_{\theta j}^{\mathrm{APSD}}\)

for every type \(\theta\) and category \(j\).

The specified immediate-arrival and truthful-departure reports are feasible because \(a\le b\). Delivery times do not affect this induction: reservations remove inventory immediately, and departures never replenish it.

3. Exact computation

Order the supported types by arrival, using the supplied order for simultaneous arrivals. Initialize remaining inventories \(v_j=q_j\) and all flow entries \(y_{\theta j}=0\).

For each type \(\theta\), initialize its unprocessed mass to \(u=\mu_\theta\). While \(u>0\):

  • Choose its most-preferred category \(j\) with \(v_j>0\).
  • Set \(\delta=\min\{u,v_j\}\).
  • Increase \(y_{\theta j}\) by \(\delta\), and decrease both \(u\) and \(v_j\) by \(\delta\).

A category is always available whenever unprocessed mass remains. Indeed, after any amount has been processed, remaining total inventory equals remaining total demand: both started at \(1\), and each reservation decreases both by the same amount.

Every iteration completes the current type, exhausts a category, or does both. There are therefore at most \(\tau+m\) iterations. Using a pointer through each type’s preference list requires at most \(m\) category inspections per type, hence \(O(\tau m)\) inspections overall. The algorithm outputs the exact SR flow by Section 2.

Bit complexity

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses and inventories, under the usual binary numerator-denominator encoding. Its bit length is \(O(L)\).

Every mass, inventory, residual, and flow entry is an integer multiple of \(1/D\). All quantities used in the allocation simulation lie between \(0\) and \(1\), so their integer numerators have \(O(L)\) bits. The updates use only exact comparisons, additions, and subtractions.

If promise verification is desired, compute the scores using this same denominator. Their numerators satisfy

\(D S_j=\sum_\theta (D\mu_\theta)r_\phi(j)\le mD\),

so they have \(O(L+\log m)\) bits. Computing scores, sorting them, and checking adjacent gaps also take polynomial time. Arrival sorting uses binary integer comparisons and likewise takes polynomial time.

Thus the entire computation, including the explicit \(\tau\)-by-\(m\) flow matrix, has polynomial bit complexity.

4. Equality decision

On the promised input class, the equality decision always returns yes. Exact flow computation is polynomial-time solvable, and the two flows necessarily coincide. No subclaim remains open for the stated model.
