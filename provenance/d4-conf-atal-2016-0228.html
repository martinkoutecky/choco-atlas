The continuous mirror question, as graded

Result

The exact continuous weak least-core problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Both the optimal value and an optimal payoff-rate vector can be returned as exact rational numbers.

The proof constructs a polynomial-size rational linear program. Superadditivity is not needed for this construction.

1. Preparing the polyhedral pieces

Write the supplied pieces and their value formulas as

\(P_i=\{q\in\mathbb R^\tau:D_iq\le d_i\},\qquad v(q)=a_i^\top q+c_i\quad\text{on }P_i\cap[0,\mu].\)

Intersect every piece with the domain:

\(Q_i=P_i\cap[0,\mu]=\{q\in\mathbb R^\tau:A_iq\le b_i\}.\)

Here \(A_iq\le b_i\) includes the inequalities \(q\le\mu\) and \(-q\le0\). Test each \(Q_i\) for emptiness by rational LP feasibility and discard empty pieces. Let \(I\) index the remaining pieces, and let \(s_i\) be the number of rows of \(A_i\).

Every retained \(Q_i\) is nonempty and compact, and together they cover \([0,\mu]\). This preprocessing adds at most \(2m\tau\) inequalities and has polynomial encoding size.

Set \(V=v(\mu)\). We can compute \(V\) exactly by finding a piece containing \(\mu\) and evaluating its affine formula. The overlap agreement ensures that the result is independent of the chosen piece.

Let \(\mathbf1\in\mathbb R^\tau\) denote the all-ones vector. Since \(q\ge0\), we have \(\lVert q\rVert_1=\mathbf1^\top q\). Thus, on \(Q_i\), the deviation constraints are equivalent to

\((a_i-p-\epsilon\mathbf1)^\top q+c_i\le0\qquad\text{for every }q\in Q_i.\)

2. Exact finite certificates for each piece

Fix \(p,\epsilon\), and a retained piece \(i\). Consider the LP

\(\max\{(a_i-p-\epsilon\mathbf1)^\top q:A_iq\le b_i,\ q\in\mathbb R^\tau\}.\)

The variable \(q\) is unrestricted in this LP notation because its box constraints have already been included in \(A_iq\le b_i\).

Its feasible set is nonempty and compact, so its maximum is finite and attained. Its dual is

\(\min\{b_i^\top\lambda_i:A_i^\top\lambda_i=a_i-p-\epsilon\mathbf1,\ \lambda_i\ge0\}.\)

By LP strong duality, the dual also attains the same finite optimum. This theorem applies to general LPs, including those whose feasible sets have lower dimension. See the [LP duality theorem and unrestricted-variable formulation](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs.cmu.edu/academic/class/15859-f11/www/notes/lecture05.pdf).

Consequently, all deviation constraints on \(Q_i\) hold if and only if there exists \(\lambda_i\in\mathbb R^{s_i}\) satisfying

\(\lambda_i\ge0,\qquad A_i^\top\lambda_i=a_i-p-\epsilon\mathbf1,\qquad b_i^\top\lambda_i+c_i\le0.\)

For completeness, sufficiency follows directly: for any \(q\in Q_i\),

\((a_i-p-\epsilon\mathbf1)^\top q+c_i =\lambda_i^\top A_iq+c_i \le\lambda_i^\top b_i+c_i \le0.\)

For necessity, if every deviation constraint holds, the primal optimum is at most \(-c_i\). An attained optimal dual solution therefore satisfies \(b_i^\top\lambda_i+c_i\le0\).

3. The equivalent linear program

The original problem is exactly the following rational LP, with variables \(p,\epsilon\), and \(\lambda_i\) for \(i\in I\):

Minimize \(\epsilon\), subject to

  • \(p\ge0\) and \(\mu^\top p=V\);
  • \(\epsilon\ge0\);
  • \(\lambda_i\ge0\) for every \(i\in I\);
  • \(A_i^\top\lambda_i+p+\epsilon\mathbf1=a_i\) for every \(i\in I\);
  • \(b_i^\top\lambda_i+c_i\le0\) for every \(i\in I\).

The added constraint \(\epsilon\ge0\) does not change the problem. Indeed, the required deviation constraint at \(q=\mu\), together with efficiency and \(\mathbf1^\top\mu=1\), gives

\(V=p^\top\mu\ge v(\mu)-\epsilon\lVert\mu\rVert_1=V-\epsilon,\)

and hence \(\epsilon\ge0\).

Section 2 proves that the projection of this LP’s feasible set onto \((p,\epsilon)\) is precisely the original feasible set. Coverage of the domain ensures that every real deviation is included, including boundary points and deviations arbitrarily close to zero.

4. Feasibility and attainment

We next prove that the optimization has a finite, attained optimum.

Define the nonnegative rational number

\(C=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{a_{it}:i\in I,\ 1\le t\le\tau\}\bigr).\)

We claim that

\(v(q)\le C\mathbf1^\top q\qquad\text{for every }q\in[0,\mu].\)

For \(q=0\), this follows from \(v(0)=0\). Fix any other \(q\). For each piece, the set

\(T_i=\{t\in[0,1]:tq\in Q_i\}\)

is either empty or a closed interval, possibly a singleton, because \(Q_i\) is closed and convex. These finitely many intervals cover \([0,1]\).

Partition \([0,1]\) at all their endpoints, obtaining

\(0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_k=1.\)

Each segment \([t_{j-1},t_j]\) lies in some \(T_{i_j}\): choose an interior point and an interval containing it; that interval cannot start or end inside the segment, and its closedness includes the segment’s endpoints.

The affine formula therefore gives

\(v(t_jq)-v(t_{j-1}q)=(t_j-t_{j-1})a_{i_j}^\top q.\)

Since every coordinate of \(q\) is nonnegative and every coefficient \(a_{i_jt}\le C\),

\(a_{i_j}^\top q\le C\mathbf1^\top q.\)

Summing the increments and using \(v(0)=0\) proves the claim.

Now choose \(p=V\mathbf1\) and \(\epsilon=C\). Because \(V\ge0\) and \(\mathbf1^\top\mu=1\), these rates are nonnegative and efficient. Furthermore,

\(p^\top q+\epsilon\lVert q\rVert_1=(V+C)\mathbf1^\top q\ge C\mathbf1^\top q\ge v(q).\)

Thus the original problem, and hence the equivalent LP, is feasible. Its objective is bounded below by zero. A feasible LP with a bounded objective attains its optimum; with rational coefficients, it has a rational optimal solution.

5. Polynomial complexity and exactness

The constructed LP has

\(\tau+1+\sum_{i\in I}s_i\)

variables and \(O(\tau+\sum_{i\in I}s_i+m\tau)\) scalar constraints.

All coefficients come from the input, box inequalities, the rational value \(V\), or constants. Their total binary encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). In particular, computing \(V\) by rational dot-product arithmetic takes polynomial time and produces a number of polynomial bit length.

The standard exact rational LP theorem supplies a polynomial-time algorithm that detects infeasibility or unboundedness, or returns an exact rational optimum and optimizer. Exactness here is in the binary bit model; see [Gleixner and Steffy, Section 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.12820v1#S1.SS3). Our LP is feasible and bounded in objective by Section 4, so it returns an optimal \(\epsilon^\ast\) and \(p^\ast\).

Therefore the stated continuous optimization problem is polynomial-time solvable. The proof accommodates negative values, lower-dimensional pieces, and arbitrarily small deviations. It holds under all the given promises and, in fact, does not require superadditivity.
