Corollary A.1 (Polynomial-time best response) — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\), quota groups \(T_r\), normalized lower and upper quota counts \(\hat q^-_r,\hat q^+_r\), type dropout estimates \(\tilde d_t\), robustness radius \(\gamma\), and a fixed normalized inclusion intensity \(x_t\), compute a finite-support law \(\rho\) over dropout masses \(Z\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\) maximizing \(\mathbb E_{Z\sim\rho}[\max_{r,\sigma\in\{-,+\}} d_r^\sigma(x,Z)]\). Here \(A_r(x,Z)=\sum_{t\in T_r}x_t(\mu_t-Z_t)\), \(d^-_r=(\hat q^-_r-A_r)/\hat q^+_r\), and \(d^+_r=(A_r-\hat q^+_r)/\hat q^+_r\). Require \(\mathbb E[Z_t]/\mu_t\in[\max(0,\tilde d_t-\gamma),\min(1,\tilde d_t+\gamma)]\) for each type and \(\sum_t\mathbb E[Z_t]=\sum_t\mu_t\tilde d_t\). For a fixed-size panel, \(x_t=p_t/(k/n)\) and \(\hat q_r^\pm=q_r^\pm/k\), where \(p_t\) and \(q_r^\pm\) are the original inclusion probabilities and quota counts.

Result

The exact continuous best-response problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). An optimal law with rational probabilities and rational atoms can be computed, with at most \(2m\) atoms.

The proof uses an explicit linear program. It does not expand binary-encoded masses into individuals.

1. Expressing the loss as a maximum of affine functions

Let \(B=\prod_{t\in T}[0,\mu_t]\), and define
\[ \]
\( \mathcal K=\{(r,\sigma):1\le r\le m,\ \sigma\in\{-,+\}\}. \)

Thus \(|\mathcal K|=2m\). For each group, put \(M_r=\sum_{t\in T_r}x_t\mu_t\). The two deviations are affine functions of \(Z\):

\( d_r^-(x,Z)=\dfrac{\hat q_r^- -M_r}{\hat q_r^+}+\sum_{t\in T_r}\dfrac{x_t}{\hat q_r^+}Z_t, \)

\( d_r^+(x,Z)=\dfrac{M_r-\hat q_r^+}{\hat q_r^+}-\sum_{t\in T_r}\dfrac{x_t}{\hat q_r^+}Z_t. \)

Write these functions as \(f_k(Z)=c_k+\sum_t a_{kt}Z_t\), for \(k\in\mathcal K\), and let

\( F(Z)=\max_{k\in\mathcal K}f_k(Z). \)

Every coefficient is rational because the input is rational and \(\hat q_r^+>0\).

Define the bounds and required total mean by

\( \ell_t=\mu_t\max(0,\tilde d_t-\gamma),\qquad u_t=\mu_t\min(1,\tilde d_t+\gamma),\qquad D=\sum_t\mu_t\tilde d_t. \)

Since \(\mu_t>0\), admissibility is exactly the requirement that a law on \(B\) satisfy

\( \ell_t\le\mathbb E[Z_t]\le u_t\quad(t\in T),\qquad \sum_t\mathbb E[Z_t]=D. \)

The admissible set is nonempty: the deterministic atom \(Z_t=\mu_t\tilde d_t\) satisfies every constraint.

2. A compact linear program

For each \(k\in\mathcal K\), introduce a variable \(w_k\) and variables \(y_{kt}\), for \(t\in T\). Consider the following linear program, whose optimal value we denote by \(V_{\mathrm{LP}}\).

Maximize

\( \displaystyle J(w,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\left(c_kw_k+\sum_{t\in T}a_{kt}y_{kt}\right) \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle w_k\ge0\quad(k\in\mathcal K),\qquad \sum_{k\in\mathcal K}w_k=1, \)

\( \displaystyle 0\le y_{kt}\le\mu_tw_k\quad(k\in\mathcal K,\ t\in T), \)

\( \displaystyle \ell_t\le\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}y_{kt}\le u_t\quad(t\in T), \)

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}y_{kt}=D. \)

There are \(2m(\tau+1)\) variables and \(O(m\tau+\tau)\) constraints.

The feasible region is nonempty: choose any label \(k_0\), set \(w_{k_0}=1\) and \(y_{k_0t}=\mu_t\tilde d_t\), and set the remaining variables to zero. It is also closed and bounded, since \(0\le w_k\le1\) and \(0\le y_{kt}\le\mu_t\). Therefore the LP attains its optimum.

3. Every admissible law yields an LP solution with the same value

Fix any admissible law \(\rho\). Order the labels in \(\mathcal K\), and for every \(Z\in B\), let \(k(Z)\) be the first label attaining \(F(Z)\).

This choice is measurable: each label’s selection region is defined by finitely many strict or weak comparisons between continuous affine functions.

Set

\( w_k=\Pr_\rho(k(Z)=k),\qquad y_{kt}=\mathbb E_\rho[Z_t\mathbf1_{\{k(Z)=k\}}]. \)

The selection regions partition \(B\), so \(w_k\ge0\) and \(\sum_k w_k=1\). Since \(0\le Z_t\le\mu_t\),

\( 0\le y_{kt}\le\mu_tw_k. \)

Moreover,

\( \sum_k y_{kt}=\mathbb E_\rho[Z_t]. \)

Consequently, all marginal bounds and the expected-total equality hold in the LP.

Finally, the selected function equals the loss at each outcome. Affineness and the finite partition therefore give

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E_\rho[F(Z)]=\sum_k\mathbb E_\rho[f_k(Z)\mathbf1_{\{k(Z)=k\}}]=\sum_k\left(c_kw_k+\sum_ta_{kt}y_{kt}\right). \)

Thus, defining \(V=\sup_{\rho\text{ admissible}}\mathbb E_\rho[F(Z)]\), we obtain

\( V\le V_{\mathrm{LP}}. \)

No finite-support assumption was used here.

4. Recovering an optimal finite-support law

Conversely, take any feasible LP solution. For each label with \(w_k>0\), define an atom by

\( z_t^{(k)}=\dfrac{y_{kt}}{w_k}\quad(t\in T). \)

The homogeneous box constraints imply \(0\le z_t^{(k)}\le\mu_t\), so \(z^{(k)}\in B\). Define

\( \displaystyle \rho_{w,y}=\sum_{k:w_k>0}w_k\,\delta_{z^{(k)}}, \)

where \(\delta_z\) denotes the point mass at \(z\).

If \(w_k=0\), the box constraints force \(y_{kt}=0\) for every \(t\). Hence

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E_{\rho_{w,y}}[Z_t]=\sum_{k:w_k>0}w_kz_t^{(k)}=\sum_k y_{kt}. \)

The recovered law is therefore admissible, including the expected-total-dropout equality.

Because \(F(z)\ge f_k(z)\) for every label and every atom,

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E_{\rho_{w,y}}[F(Z)]\ge\sum_{k:w_k>0}w_kf_k(z^{(k)})=J(w,y). \)

Apply this construction to an optimal LP solution. Together with the preceding section, it gives

\( \displaystyle V_{\mathrm{LP}}\le\mathbb E_{\rho_{w,y}}[F(Z)]\le V\le V_{\mathrm{LP}}. \)

All three quantities are equal. This proves both that the supremum is attained and that the recovered law is optimal. Its support contains at most \(2m\) atoms.

The LP does not need constraints making label \(k\) active at atom \(z^{(k)}\). The inequalities above already establish exactness.

5. Exact computation and bit complexity

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly encoded LP with rational coefficients can be solved exactly in time polynomial in its encoding length, returning a rational optimizer when its feasible region is nonempty and its optimum is finite. Polynomial-time solvability in the rational bit model is established by the classical LP algorithms; see [Adler and Beling, *Polynomial Algorithms for Linear Programming over the Algebraic Numbers*](https://adler.ieor.berkeley.edu/ilans_pubs/lp_algebraic_stoc_1992.pdf).

All hypotheses hold here:

  • The LP has polynomially many variables and constraints.
  • Its rational coefficients can be computed in polynomial time and have polynomial bit length. Computing them requires products, sums of at most \(\tau\) terms, and division by positive rational quotas.
  • Its feasible region is nonempty and compact, as proved above.

For completeness, polynomial output size also follows from the usual determinant bounds. An optimal vertex exists. After clearing denominators, its coordinates are ratios of determinants of square integer systems of dimension at most \(2m(\tau+1)\). If the integer entries have at most \(H\) bits, determinants of dimension \(n\) have \(O(nH+n\log n)\) bits. Both \(n\) and \(H\) are polynomially bounded in \(m,\tau,L\).

Thus an exact rational optimizer can be obtained with polynomial encoding length. For every positive \(w_k\), computing \(y_{kt}/w_k\) also takes polynomial time and produces a rational number of polynomial bit length, even when \(w_k\) is numerically very small.

The resulting algorithm is:

  • Construct the LP above.
  • Solve it exactly.
  • For each positive \(w_k\), output probability \(w_k\) and atom \((y_{kt}/w_k)_{t\in T}\).

This settles the stated mass formulation. The proof accommodates the feature-value groups and the all-types group, and requires no normalization of \(\sum_t\mu_tx_t\).
