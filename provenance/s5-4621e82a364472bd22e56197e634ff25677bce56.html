The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many buyer and seller classes with shared finite-support value or cost distributions, rational class masses, a compatibility graph, and an explicit downward-closed polytope of feasible class-level trade volumes, determine the maximum expected gains from trade per capita over direct mechanisms for a continuum of agents. Each agent receives at most one integral trade; the mechanism may use the agent’s report and the aggregate report masses, must be BIC and interim IR, and must balance total buyer payments and seller receipts for every aggregate report profile. Is this optimum exactly represented by a finite LP over pairwise type flows \(x_{av,bc}\), with mass-capacity, compatibility, monotonicity, and virtual-budget constraints, and can an optimizing flow be implemented by such a mechanism?

2. Notation and the necessary budget inequality

Write the buyer support in class \(a\) as

\[ v_{a1}<\cdots<v_{an_a}, \]

with masses \(\mu_{ai}>0\). Write the seller support in class \(b\) as \(c_{b1}<\cdots<c_{b\ell_b}\), with masses \(\nu_{bj}>0\).

For an admissible mechanism, let \(q_{ai}\) be a supported buyer’s service probability and \(p_{ai}\) its payment at the truthful aggregate profile. Define its truthful expected utility by

\[ u_{ai}=v_{ai}q_{ai}-p_{ai}. \]

Similarly, let \(s_{bj}\), \(r_{bj}\), and \(w_{bj}=r_{bj}-c_{bj}s_{bj}\) denote seller service, receipt, and utility.

An individual deviation preserves aggregate report measures. Therefore BIC gives the usual single-parameter incentive inequalities at this fixed aggregate profile.

Adding the two buyer incentive inequalities between values \(v<v'\) gives

\[ (v'-v)\bigl(q_a(v')-q_a(v)\bigr)\geq0. \]

Thus buyer service is nondecreasing. The analogous seller calculation gives nonincreasing seller service.

Adjacent buyer incentive inequalities and IR imply

\[ u_{ai}\geq \sum_{k<i}(v_{a,k+1}-v_{ak})q_{ak}. \]

Indeed, \(u_{a1}\geq0\), and a buyer of value \(v_{a,k+1}\) can report \(v_{ak}\), giving

\[ u_{a,k+1}\geq u_{ak}+(v_{a,k+1}-v_{ak})q_{ak}. \]

Consequently,

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_i\mu_{ai}p_{ai} &\leq \sum_i\mu_{ai}v_{ai}q_{ai} -\sum_k(v_{a,k+1}-v_{ak})q_{ak}\sum_{i>k}\mu_{ai}\\ &=\sum_i\mu_{ai}\phi_a(v_{ai})q_{ai}. \end{aligned} \]

The last equality follows because

\[ \frac{\Pr_a[V>v_{ak}]}{f_a(v_{ak})} =\frac{\sum_{i>k}\mu_{ai}}{\mu_{ak}}. \]

For sellers, IR at the largest supported cost and successive downward incentive inequalities give

\[ w_{bj}\geq \sum_{k>j}(c_{bk}-c_{b,k-1})s_{bk}. \]

Therefore,

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_j\nu_{bj}r_{bj} &\geq \sum_j\nu_{bj}c_{bj}s_{bj} +\sum_k(c_{bk}-c_{b,k-1})s_{bk}\sum_{j<k}\nu_{bj}\\ &=\sum_j\nu_{bj}\psi_b(c_{bj})s_{bj}. \end{aligned} \]

Integrated balance at the truthful profile now implies

\[ \sum_{a,i}\mu_{ai}\phi_a(v_{ai})q_{ai} -\sum_{b,j}\nu_{bj}\psi_b(c_{bj})s_{bj}\geq0. \]

3. Every admissible mechanism gives an LP-feasible flow

Let \(x_{ai,bj}\) be the expected mass of trades between the indicated true-type populations.

Matching feasibility gives nonnegativity, compatibility, and the population capacity constraints. Anonymity gives

\[ \sum_{b,j}x_{ai,bj}=\mu_{ai}q_{ai}, \qquad \sum_{a,i}x_{ai,bj}=\nu_{bj}s_{bj}. \]

Hence the service-ratio monotonicity constraints hold. The inequality proved above becomes precisely the LP’s virtual-gains constraint.

Let \(Z\) denote the realized class-pair volume vector. Every realization satisfies \(Z\in D\). Taking expectations in each defining linear inequality of \(D\) shows that

\[ \mathbb E[Z] =\left(\sum_{i,j}x_{ai,bj}\right)_{(a,b)\in E} \in D. \]

Finally, the mechanism’s expected gains equal

\[ \sum_{ai,bj}(v_{ai}-c_{bj})x_{ai,bj}. \]

Thus

\[ \operatorname{OPT}\leq\text{LP optimum}. \]

4. Extending a feasible flow to the entire report domain

Fix any LP-feasible flow \(x\), and define

\[ q_{ai}=\frac{\sum_{b,j}x_{ai,bj}}{\mu_{ai}}, \qquad s_{bj}=\frac{\sum_{a,i}x_{ai,bj}}{\nu_{bj}}. \]

These numbers lie in \([0,1]\), with the required monotonicities.

Buyer rounding. Round a report \(t\) down to the largest supported value at most \(t\). Reports below the smallest supported value receive a dummy symbol. Define \(Q_a(t)\) to be the corresponding \(q_{ai}\), or zero at the dummy symbol.

Seller rounding. Round a report \(t\) up to the smallest supported cost at least \(t\). Reports above the largest supported cost receive a dummy symbol. Define \(T_b(t)\) to be the corresponding \(s_{bj}\), or zero at the dummy symbol.

Then \(Q_a\) is nondecreasing and \(T_b\) is nonincreasing on all of \([0,1]\).

Define utility potentials and baseline transfers by

\[ U_a(v)=\int_0^v Q_a(t)\,dt, \qquad P_a^0(v)=vQ_a(v)-U_a(v), \]

and

\[ W_b(c)=\int_c^1 T_b(t)\,dt, \qquad R_b^0(c)=cT_b(c)+W_b(c). \]

At supported types,

\[ U_a(v_{ai}) =\sum_{k<i}(v_{a,k+1}-v_{ak})q_{ak}, \]

and

\[ W_b(c_{bj}) =\sum_{k>j}(c_{bk}-c_{b,k-1})s_{bk}. \]

Thus these potentials attain the lower utility bounds used in Section 2. The same telescoping calculations now give equalities:

\[ \sum_{a,i}\mu_{ai}P_a^0(v_{ai}) =\sum_{ai,bj}\phi_a(v_{ai})x_{ai,bj}, \]

and

\[ \sum_{b,j}\nu_{bj}R_b^0(c_{bj}) =\sum_{ai,bj}\psi_b(c_{bj})x_{ai,bj}. \]

Set

\[ S=\sum_{ai,bj}\bigl(\phi_a(v_{ai})-\psi_b(c_{bj})\bigr)x_{ai,bj}\geq0. \]

Use transfers

\[ P_a(v)=P_a^0(v)-S, \qquad R_b(c)=R_b^0(c)+S. \]

The same subsidy \(S\) is given to every individual. This choice uses the normalization that total population mass is \(1\).

One further fact will ensure balance away from truth: each baseline transfer is constant within its rounded report bucket. For example, on a buyer bucket beginning at \(v_{ai}\),

\[ U_a(t)=U_a(v_{ai})+(t-v_{ai})q_{ai}, \]

so \(P_a^0(t)=P_a^0(v_{ai})\). The seller calculation is analogous. Baseline transfers at dummy reports are zero.

5. Realizing the flow by integral measurable matchings

We first establish the realization needed at an active profile.

Matching lemma. Suppose finitely many buyer buckets have masses \(M_i>0\), seller buckets have masses \(N_j>0\), and a nonnegative flow \(x_{ij}\) satisfies

\[ \sum_jx_{ij}\leq M_i, \qquad \sum_ix_{ij}\leq N_j. \]

There exists a randomized measurable integral matching such that every realization trades exactly mass \(x_{ij}\) between buckets \(i\) and \(j\). Every individual in buyer bucket \(i\) trades with probability \(\sum_jx_{ij}/M_i\), and analogously for sellers.

Proof. Give each buyer bucket a measure-preserving measurable bijection onto \([0,M_i)\), and each seller bucket one onto \([0,N_j)\).

Here the bijections can be defined at every point, which matters for the stated incentive requirement. The standard atomless Borel-space isomorphism theorem provides the bijection on a full-measure Borel subset. For a Lebesgue measurable bucket, extend it to the remaining null set as follows: remove the preimage of an uncountable null Cantor set from the existing bijection, then biject the enlarged null remainder onto that Cantor set. Both remainders have continuum cardinality, and completeness makes this extension and its inverse measurable. The Borel isomorphism theorem is [Kechris, Theorem 17.41](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4).

Independently rotate each coordinate interval by a uniform shift, modulo its length. In buyer interval \(i\), place disjoint half-open blocks of lengths \(x_{ij}\); leave the unused portion unmatched. In seller interval \(j\), place blocks of lengths \(x_{ij}\), indexed by buyers.

Pair corresponding buyer and seller blocks by equal displacement from their respective block beginnings. Pull these pairings back through the rotated coordinate bijections.

The resulting pairings are measurable, injective, and measure preserving. Every individual receives at most one trade, and every paired block has exactly its prescribed mass.

For every fixed individual, its rotated coordinate is uniform throughout its bucket. Its probability of entering any prescribed block is therefore the block’s length divided by bucket mass. This proves all claimed marginal probabilities, including for individuals in null subsets. \(\square\)

Apply the lemma to rounded report buckets using the fixed flow \(x\). Every realization then has the fixed class-pair volume vector

\[ z_{ab}=\sum_{i,j}x_{ai,bj}\in D. \]

Compatibility follows from the zero-flow restrictions.

The auxiliary coordinates do not affect an individual’s service distribution: its probability of trading with any opposite bucket is the prescribed flow divided by its own bucket mass. Thus the service lotteries are anonymous.

6. Defining the mechanism at every report profile

Call a measurable report profile active when, in every class, the masses of its rounded report buckets equal the given support masses. Require dummy-bucket masses to be zero.

At an active profile:

  • realize \(x\) between rounded buckets using the matching lemma;
  • charge each buyer \(P_a(t)\);
  • pay each seller \(R_b(t)\);
  • leave dummy reporters unmatched.

At any other profile, use zero trades and zero transfers.

Truthful reporting is active. Every unilateral deviation preserves the rounded aggregate masses because an individual has zero population mass. Consequently, every unilateral report considered in BIC remains active.

Profile-wise balance and feasibility

At an active profile, transfers are constant within rounded buckets, whose masses equal the input masses. Thus their baseline integrated difference is exactly \(S\).

Let

\[ \Lambda_A=\sum_{a,i}\mu_{ai}, \qquad \Lambda_B=\sum_{b,j}\nu_{bj}. \]

Since \(\Lambda_A+\Lambda_B=1\), the final integrated difference is

\[ S-S\Lambda_A-S\Lambda_B=0. \]

Dummy buckets have zero mass and do not affect this calculation. At inactive profiles, balance holds because all transfers are zero.

All transfers are bounded step functions, hence finite and integrable, and deterministic conditional on reports.

Active realizations have volume vector \(z\in D\). Inactive realizations have volume vector zero. Because \(D\) is nonempty and downward closed, \(0\in D\). Feasibility therefore holds at every report profile and every realization.

BIC and interim IR on the full domain

For a buyer with true value \(v\) who reports \(t\), expected utility is

\[ U_a(t)+S+(v-t)Q_a(t). \]

Monotonicity of \(Q_a\) implies, for every \(v,t\in[0,1]\),

\[ U_a(v)-U_a(t)\geq(v-t)Q_a(t). \]

For \(v\geq t\), this follows by integrating \(Q_a(r)\geq Q_a(t)\) over \([t,v]\). For \(v<t\), integrate \(Q_a(r)\leq Q_a(t)\) over \([v,t]\) and negate.

Truthful reporting therefore maximizes utility. Truthful utility is \(U_a(v)+S\geq0\).

For a seller with true cost \(c\) who reports \(t\), expected utility is

\[ W_b(t)+S+(t-c)T_b(t). \]

Because \(T_b\) is nonincreasing,

\[ W_b(c)-W_b(t)\geq(t-c)T_b(t). \]

If \(c\leq t\), integrate \(T_b(r)\geq T_b(t)\) over \([c,t]\). If \(c>t\), integrate \(T_b(r)\leq T_b(t)\) over \([t,c]\) and negate.

Thus truthful reporting maximizes seller utility, and truthful utility is \(W_b(c)+S\geq0\).

These arguments cover all true types and reports in \([0,1]\), including unsupported types and reports.

Let

\[ \tau_A=\sum_an_a, \qquad \tau_B=\sum_b\ell_b, \qquad \tau_A+\tau_B=\tau. \]

Omitting incompatible pairs, the LP has at most

\[ n\leq\tau_A\tau_B\leq\frac{\tau^2}{4} \]

variables.

Besides nonnegativity, it has:

  • \(\tau\) population capacity inequalities;
  • \(\tau-m\) adjacent service-monotonicity inequalities;
  • \(h\) inequalities inherited from \(D\);
  • one virtual-gains inequality.

Thus their total number is

\[ r=h+2\tau-m+1. \]

Monotonicity can be encoded without division. For example, if \(y_{ai}=\sum_{b,j}x_{ai,bj}\), the buyer inequality is

\[ \mu_{a,i+1}y_{ai}\leq\mu_{ai}y_{a,i+1}. \]

Likewise, the virtual coefficients can be computed directly from unconditional masses:

\[ \phi_a(v_{ai}) =v_{ai}-(v_{a,i+1}-v_{ai}) \frac{\sum_{k>i}\mu_{ak}}{\mu_{ai}}, \]

with the stated endpoint convention. The seller formula is analogous.

\[ O\!\left(m^2+\tau\log\tau+(h+1)\tau^2\right) \]

\[ \mathcal B =O\!\left((\tau^2+1)(h+\tau+1)(L+1)\right). \]

\[ \sum_{ai,bj}(v_{ai}-c_{bj})x_{ai,bj}\geq K \]

and test rational LP feasibility. Here all three parameters are polynomially bounded in the supplied input.

Consequently, decision and computation of an optimal rational flow take
