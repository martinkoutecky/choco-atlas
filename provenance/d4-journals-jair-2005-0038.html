The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 2 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) resource kinds, a finite set of utility types \(\mathcal T\) with binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational utility functions \(u_t\), per-capita supplies \(q_r\), and feasible initial and target configurations \(x^0,x^1\), decide whether a finite sequence of IR mass O-contracts reaches \(x^1\) from \(x^0\). A configuration \(x_{t,S}\) gives the mass of type \(t\) holding integral bundle \(S\subseteq R\), with \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:r\in S}x_{t,S}=q_r\). Each stage transfers some \(\delta>0\) mass from \((t,S)\) and \((t',S')\) to \((t,S\cup\{r\})\) and \((t',S'\setminus\{r\})\), where \(r\notin S\), \(r\in S'\), and both source cohorts have at least mass \(\delta\); the stage is IR when it strictly increases utilitarian welfare.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(R\) contain \(m\geq1\) resource kinds; each individual holds an integral bundle \(S\subseteq R\), possibly empty.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\geq1\) utility types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a monotone utility \(u_t:2^R\to\mathbb Q\), possibly negative, represented by a Boolean circuit taking the bundle's characteristic vector and outputting a signed binary numerator and a positive binary denominator.
Supplies \(q_r\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1]\) are per capita. A configuration has real coordinates \(x_{t,S}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq R}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:r\in S}x_{t,S}=q_r\).
The input gives feasible \(x^0,x^1\) as lists of their positive binary-rational coordinates, with omitted coordinates zero, and promises \(W(x^1)>W(x^0)\), where \(W(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}u_t(S)\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including circuits and configuration lists.
A mass O-contract chooses \(t,t',S,S',r\) with \(r\notin S\), \(r\in S'\), and a real \(0<\delta\leq\min\{x_{t,S},x_{t',S'}\}\); it subtracts \(\delta\) from both source coordinates and adds \(\delta\) to \((t,S\cup\{r\})\) and \((t',S'\setminus\{r\})\), adding coincident coordinate changes algebraically. The two cohorts may have the same type.
The contract is individually rational (IR) precisely when it strictly increases \(W\); compensatory payments have no budget restriction.

Is deciding whether a finite sequence of IR mass O-contracts reaches \(x^1\) exactly from \(x^0\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Discrete IR O-contract reachability is NP-hard even for two agents and monotone utilities with succinct circuit representations. [The Complexity of Contract Negotiation](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/michael.wooldridge/pubs/aij2005b.pdf).
Reachability for explicitly represented continuous Petri nets is polynomial-time decidable. [Logics for Continuous Reachability](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/christoph.haase/home/publication/bh-17/bh-17.pdf). No result for the exact mass problem specified here was located.

  • Utility encoding was unspecified: use succinct Boolean circuits, following the discrete complexity source, with signed numerator and positive denominator outputs to accommodate arbitrary rational values.
  • Carry over the anchor's monotone-utility restriction and its promise that the target deal strictly improves utilitarian welfare.
  • Initial and target configuration encoding was unspecified: list positive binary-rational coordinates and interpret omitted coordinates as zero; include all encoding lengths in L.
  • Intermediate mass and transfer domains were unspecified: allow nonnegative real configuration coordinates and positive real transfer amounts, with finite sequences and exact endpoint equality.
  • Supply domains were unspecified: require positive binary-rational per-capita supplies at most one, consistent with existing resource kinds and bundles containing at most one unit of each kind.
  • Allow empty bundles and signed utility values, as the discrete definitions do; compensation imposes no additional payment-budget condition.
  • Distinct participants need not have distinct utility types: allow transfers between separate cohorts of the same type and combine coincident coordinate changes algebraically; consequently the support may contain one type.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even for \(\tau=2\), equal masses, supplies \(q_r=\tfrac12\), and nonnegative, strictly monotone, integer-valued utilities. Both endpoint configurations can have just two positive coordinates.

There is also a deterministic algorithm with running time
\[ 2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L). \]
Thus fixed \(m\) gives polynomial-time decidability, whereas unrestricted \(m\) gives \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

1. A polynomial-time reduction from satisfiability

Let \(\varphi\) be a Boolean formula on \(n\geq1\) variables. Satisfiability remains \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under this restriction: an unused variable can always be added.

Introduce the resource set
\[ R=\{g,a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_n,b_n\},\qquad m=2n+1. \]
The resources \(a_i,b_i\) represent the two truth values of variable \(i\).

Define \(G(S)\) to hold precisely when:

  • \(g\in S\);
  • \(S\) contains exactly one of \(a_i,b_i\) for every \(i\);
  • the assignment making variable \(i\) true exactly when \(a_i\in S\) satisfies \(\varphi\).

Every bundle satisfying \(G\) has size \(n+1\).

Define an auxiliary function \(f:2^R\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\) by
\[ f(S)= \begin{cases} 0,& |S|=n+1\text{ and }G(S)\text{ is false},\\ |S|,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

There are two utility types, \(A\) and \(B\), with masses \(\mu_A=\mu_B=\tfrac12\). Put \(M=n+1\) and define
\[ u_A(S)=M|S|+f(S),\qquad u_B(S)=M|S|. \]

For every resource, set \(q_r=\tfrac12\). The initial and target configurations are
\[ x^0_{A,\varnothing}=x^0_{B,R}=\tfrac12,\qquad x^1_{A,R}=x^1_{B,\varnothing}=\tfrac12, \]
with all other coordinates zero.

These configurations satisfy every type-mass and resource-supply constraint.

2. Monotonicity, encoding, and the welfare promise

We verify strict monotonicity, despite the drop in \(f\).

For \(r\notin S\), let \(k=|S|\). The possible increments of \(f\) are:

  • If \(k<n\), the increment is \(1\).
  • If \(k=n\), the increment is either \(1\) or \(-n\).
  • If \(k=n+1\), the increment is either \(1\) or \(n+2\).
  • If \(k>n+1\), the increment is \(1\).

Consequently,
\[ u_A(S\cup\{r\})-u_A(S) =M+f(S\cup\{r\})-f(S)\geq M-n=1. \]
Also, \(u_B(S\cup\{r\})-u_B(S)=M>0\). Chaining single-resource additions proves strict monotonicity under every proper inclusion.

The utility circuits have polynomial size in the formula encoding length and \(n\): they count the bundle’s resources, check the literal-pair conditions, evaluate \(\varphi\), and perform binary arithmetic. They output denominator \(1\). Their values are at most \((n+2)m\), so their output bit lengths are \(O(\log m)\). The whole reduction takes polynomial time.

Since \(n\geq1\), we have \(m\geq n+2\), and therefore \(f(R)=m\). Thus
\[ W(x^0)=\frac{Mm}{2},\qquad W(x^1)=\frac{Mm+m}{2}, \]
giving
\[ W(x^1)-W(x^0)=\frac m2>0. \]
The welfare promise holds regardless of whether \(\varphi\) is satisfiable.

3. Satisfiability implies exact reachability

Suppose \(\varphi\) has a satisfying assignment. Let \(T\) contain its \(n\) selected literal resources, exactly one from each pair.

Order the resources by listing:

  • the resources of \(T\);
  • \(g\);
  • all remaining resources.

Let \(P_j\) be the prefix bundle after the first \(j\) resources. For every \(0\leq j\leq m\),
\[ f(P_j)=j. \]
Indeed, the only exceptional cardinality is \(n+1\), and \(P_{n+1}=T\cup\{g\}\) satisfies \(G\).

Starting from \(x^0\), transfer each resource in this order from type \(B\) to type \(A\), always using \(\delta=\tfrac12\). Before step \(j\), the two occupied coordinates are
\[ x_{A,P_{j-1}}=x_{B,R\setminus P_{j-1}}=\tfrac12. \]
Hence the selected resource is absent from the recipient bundle and present in the donor bundle, and the contract is enabled.

The common \(M|S|\) terms cancel between recipient and donor. The welfare increase is therefore
\[ \frac12\bigl(f(P_j)-f(P_{j-1})\bigr)=\frac12>0. \]
Every contract is IR. After exactly \(m\) contracts, the configuration is \(x^1\).

4. Unsatisfiability prevents even fractional crossing

Suppose \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable. Then \(G(S)\) is false for every bundle, so
\[ f(S)=|S|\quad\text{for }|S|\leq n,\qquad f(S)=0\quad\text{for }|S|=n+1. \]

We prove the invariant:

Every configuration reachable from \(x^0\) by finitely many IR contracts has \(x_{A,S}=0\) whenever \(|S|>n\).

The invariant holds initially. Assume it holds immediately before a contract.

A contract changes bundles only by adding or removing one resource. Therefore, to create a positive type-\(A\) coordinate of size greater than \(n\), its recipient must have type \(A\) and source bundle \(S\) of size exactly \(n\). Removing a resource from a type-\(A\) source cannot violate the invariant.

For such a recipient,
\[ f(S\cup\{r\})-f(S)=-n. \]

There are two possible donor types.

If the donor has type \(B\), its utility contribution beyond the common linear term is zero. The total welfare change is
\[ \Delta W=-n\delta<0. \]

If the donor has type \(A\), its positive source coordinate has bundle \(S'\) with \(1\leq|S'|\leq n\), by the induction hypothesis and \(r\in S'\). Hence
\[ f(S'\setminus\{r\})-f(S')=-1, \]
and the total welfare change is
\[ \Delta W=-(n+1)\delta<0. \]

In both cases, the contract fails IR. These calculations use the algebraic coordinate changes and remain valid when destinations coincide with other coordinates.

Thus an IR contract cannot violate the invariant. Induction proves it for every finite sequence, with arbitrary positive real transfer amounts.

But \(x^1_{A,R}=\tfrac12\) and \(|R|=2n+1>n\). Therefore \(x^1\) is unreachable when \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable.

We have established the equivalence
\[ \varphi\text{ is satisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^1\text{ is reachable from }x^0. \]
This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for the exact mass problem.

5. Running time via explicit continuous Petri nets

For completeness, the succinct problem admits an exponential-in-\(m\) upper bound.

Create one Petri-net place for each coordinate \((t,S)\). There are
\[ N=\tau2^m \]
places.

For every admissible tuple \((t,t',S,S',r)\), compute
\[ d=u_t(S\cup\{r\})+u_{t'}(S'\setminus\{r\})-u_t(S)-u_{t'}(S'). \]
Keep a transition precisely when \(d>0\). It consumes one unit from each source place and produces one unit at each destination place, retaining any input/output overlap.

The two source places are distinct because their bundles have different membership of \(r\). Firing this transition by amount \(\delta\) is therefore enabled exactly when
\[ 0<\delta\leq\min\{x_{t,S},x_{t',S'}\}, \]
and it has precisely the required coordinate effect. Its welfare increase is \(\delta d>0\). Every transition preserves type masses and resource supplies.

The number of candidate transitions is
\[ E=m\tau^2\,2^{m-1}2^{m-1} =m\tau^2\,4^{m-1}. \]
Circuit evaluations and rational sign comparisons take polynomial time in \(L\), so the explicit net can be constructed in \((N+E)\operatorname{poly}(L)\) time.

For explicitly encoded continuous Petri nets with integral incidence data and rational endpoint markings, finite rational reachability is polynomial-time decidable. This follows from Theorems 3.3 and 3.6 of [Blondin and Haase, *Logics for Continuous Reachability in Petri Nets*](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/christoph.haase/home/publication/bh-17/bh-17.pdf).

Here rational and real finite reachability agree. To verify this, fix the transition sequence of any finite real execution. Its transfer amounts satisfy finitely many rational linear equalities and weak inequalities, together with strict positivity constraints. At the real solution, impose every tight weak inequality as an additional equality. Gaussian elimination gives a rational parametrization of this affine solution space, whose rational points are dense. A sufficiently close rational point preserves all remaining strict inequalities, yielding a rational execution of the same sequence. The reverse implication is immediate.

Applying the explicit-net algorithm therefore gives total running time
\[ \bigl(L+m\tau^2 4^m\bigr)^{O(1)} =2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L). \]

The polynomial-time result for explicit continuous nets is consequently consistent with the hardness result: the bundle representation here is succinct.
