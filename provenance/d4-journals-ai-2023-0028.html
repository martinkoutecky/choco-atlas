The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), per-capita supply \(q\), integral bundle sizes \(j\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\), budgets \(B_t\), valuation schedules \(v_t(j)\), and an explicit finite rational price menu \(\mathcal P\), choose \(p\in\mathcal P\) and masses \(z_{t,j}\ge 0\). Require \(\sum_j z_{t,j}=\mu_t\), \(z_{t,j}=0\) unless \(j\) maximizes \(v_t(j)-pj\) among bundles satisfying \(pj\le B_t\), and \(\sum_{t,j}jz_{t,j}\le q\). For either the revenue objective \(p\sum_{t,j}jz_{t,j}\) or welfare objective \(\sum_{t,j}v_t(j)z_{t,j}\), compute a maximizing price and assignment.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Input also includes an integer bundle cap \(K\geq1\), per-capita supply \(q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), budgets \(B_t\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), explicitly tabulated values \(v_t(j)\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\) for \(j\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\), with \(v_t(0)=0\), and a nonempty explicit finite menu \(\mathcal P\subseteq\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\). No linearity or concavity is assumed.
For a uniform unit price \(p\), define \(D_t(p)=\arg\max\{v_t(j)-pj:j\in\{0,\ldots,K\},\ pj\leq B_t\}\).
An envy-free assignment consists of masses \(z_{t,j}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_jz_{t,j}=\mu_t\), \(z_{t,j}=0\) whenever \(j\notin D_t(p)\), and \(\sum_{t,j}jz_{t,j}\leq q\). Mass may split among tied demanded bundles; unsold supply is permitted.
For each objective separately, optimize over \(p\in\mathcal P\) and such assignments: revenue \(p\sum_{t,j}jz_{t,j}\), or welfare \(\sum_{t,j}v_t(j)z_{t,j}\). Report infeasibility if no menu price admits an assignment.
Is exact revenue maximization polynomial-time solvable or NP-hard, and is exact welfare maximization polynomial-time solvable or NP-hard? State running times in \(m:=K\), \(\tau\), \(|\mathcal P|\), and the total binary input length \(L\).
Discrete envy-free price-and-allocation optimization with general valuation schedules is stated to be NP-hard for both objectives in [Brânzei et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.08719).
A related model with finitely many buyers, linear valuations, and divisible goods has a polynomial-time revenue algorithm in [Brânzei et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.08719); it does not address the distributional problem above. No result settling this exact distributional problem was located.

  • Specified a positive integer bundle cap, positive rational per-capita supply, and a nonempty menu of nonnegative rational prices, including the possibility of price zero.
  • Made valuation schedules explicit tables, matching the general model's input representation; imposed no additional shape restriction.
  • Kept the graded question's bundle cap independent of per-capita supply and identified m with K for running-time notation.
  • Allowed mass splitting among utility-maximizing ties without a fixed tie-breaking order, consistent with optimizing over demanded allocations.
  • Added infeasibility reporting because an arbitrary finite price menu need not contain a feasible price.

Result

Both exact revenue maximization and exact welfare maximization are polynomial-time solvable. With \(m:=K\), both optimal values and corresponding assignments can be computed together using

\(O\!\left(|\mathcal P|\tau(m+1)\right)\)

exact arithmetic operations. A conservative bound with schoolbook integer arithmetic is

\(O\!\left(|\mathcal P|\tau(m+1)L^2\right)\)

bit operations.

The proof uses the permission to split a type’s mass among tied demanded bundles. It requires no linearity, monotonicity, or concavity of the valuation schedules.

1. Demand extrema and feasibility at a fixed price

Fix \(p\in\mathcal P\). Define the maximum affordable utility of type \(t\) by

\(u_t(p):=\max\{v_t(j)-pj:0\leq j\leq m,\ pj\leq B_t\}.\)

The feasible set in this maximum is nonempty because \(j=0\) is affordable. Consequently, \(D_t(p)\) is nonempty. Define

\(a_t(p):=\min D_t(p),\qquad b_t(p):=\max D_t(p),\)

and the aggregate quantities

\(Q_-(p):=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_t(p),\qquad Q_+(p):=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t b_t(p).\)

For any assignment satisfying the type-mass and demand constraints, let

\(Q(z):=\sum_{t,j}jz_{t,j}.\)

Every bundle receiving positive mass for type \(t\) lies between \(a_t(p)\) and \(b_t(p)\). Since its assigned masses sum to \(\mu_t\),

\(\mu_t a_t(p)\leq\sum_jjz_{t,j}\leq\mu_t b_t(p).\)

Summing over types gives

\(Q_-(p)\leq Q(z)\leq Q_+(p).\)

Conversely, every quantity in this interval is attainable. To prove this, take any \(s\in[Q_-(p),Q_+(p)]\).

If \(Q_-(p)=Q_+(p)\), allocating all type-\(t\) mass to \(a_t(p)\) attains \(s=Q_-(p)\).

Otherwise, define

\(\theta:=\dfrac{s-Q_-(p)}{Q_+(p)-Q_-(p)}\in[0,1].\)

For each type with \(a_t(p)<b_t(p)\), set

\(z_{t,a_t(p)}=(1-\theta)\mu_t,\qquad z_{t,b_t(p)}=\theta\mu_t,\)

and set its other masses to zero. For a type with \(a_t(p)=b_t(p)\), put all its mass on that bundle. Both endpoints belong to the demand set, and the resulting quantity is

\(Q(z)=(1-\theta)Q_-(p)+\theta Q_+(p)=s.\)

Thus the attainable quantities, before imposing supply, are exactly \([Q_-(p),Q_+(p)]\), even when individual demand sets have gaps.

It follows that

\(p\text{ admits an envy-free assignment}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Q_-(p)\leq q.\)

Necessity follows from the lower bound on \(Q(z)\). Sufficiency follows by assigning every type its smallest demanded bundle.

For a feasible price, the largest attainable quantity respecting supply is therefore

\(S(p):=\min\{q,Q_+(p)\}.\)

Indeed, feasibility ensures \(Q_-(p)\leq S(p)\leq Q_+(p)\), so the construction above attains \(S(p)\).

2. Exact optimal revenue and welfare at a fixed price

For revenue, every feasible assignment has value \(pQ(z)\). Because \(p\geq0\), this is maximized by attaining \(S(p)\). Hence

\(R^*(p)=pS(p).\)

For welfare, the defining property of demand gives, for every \(j\in D_t(p)\),

\(v_t(j)-pj=u_t(p),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad v_t(j)=u_t(p)+pj.\)

An envy-free assignment puts mass only on such bundles. Therefore its welfare satisfies

\(\displaystyle W(z)=\sum_{t,j}v_t(j)z_{t,j} =\sum_tu_t(p)\sum_jz_{t,j}+p\sum_{t,j}jz_{t,j} =\sum_t\mu_tu_t(p)+pQ(z).\)

Define

\(U(p):=\sum_t\mu_tu_t(p).\)

At the fixed price, \(U(p)\) is independent of the assignment. Since \(p\geq0\), welfare is also maximized by attaining \(S(p)\), giving

\(W^*(p)=U(p)+pS(p).\)

These formulas include \(p=0\): revenue is zero, and every feasible assignment has welfare \(U(0)\).

Thus one assignment attaining \(S(p)\) simultaneously maximizes both objectives at a fixed price, although the globally optimal menu prices may differ.

3. Optimization over the explicit menu

The algorithm processes every \(p\in\mathcal P\):

  • For each type, scan its \(m+1\) tabulated bundles. Among affordable bundles, compute \(u_t(p)\) and the smallest and largest utility maximizers \(a_t(p),b_t(p)\).
  • Compute \(Q_-(p),Q_+(p)\), and \(U(p)\).
  • If \(Q_-(p)>q\), discard the price.
  • Otherwise, compute \(S(p)=\min\{q,Q_+(p)\}\) and the two objective values derived above.

If every price is discarded, report infeasibility. Otherwise, independently select a feasible price maximizing each objective. Reconstruct its assignment using the interpolation in Section 1 with \(s=S(p)\).

The preceding proofs show that every discarded price is infeasible, every retained price has the computed optimum, and every reconstructed assignment attains it. Exhausting the explicit finite menu therefore yields both global optima.

4. Running time and exact bit complexity

Each price requires \(O(\tau(m+1))\) arithmetic operations and comparisons. Computing aggregates and reconstructing an assignment each require \(O(\tau)\) additional operations. The total arithmetic-operation bound is consequently

\(O\!\left(|\mathcal P|\tau(m+1)\right).\)

To justify polynomial bit complexity explicitly, represent each input rational by its binary numerator and positive denominator. Let \(D\) be the product of all input denominators. Their total encoded length is at most \(L\), so \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits.

For every input rational \(r\), write \(\widehat r:=Dr\), an integer. At a fixed price, compute

\(\widehat u_t(p)=\max\{\widehat v_t(j)-j\widehat p:j\widehat p\leq\widehat B_t,\ 0\leq j\leq m\}.\)

This integer equals \(Du_t(p)\). Similarly, compute

\(\widehat Q_-=\sum_t\widehat\mu_t a_t,\qquad \widehat Q_+=\sum_t\widehat\mu_t b_t,\qquad \widehat S=\min\{\widehat q,\widehat Q_+\}.\)

Feasibility is the integer comparison \(\widehat Q_-\leq\widehat q\). The exact objective values are

\(\displaystyle R^*(p)=\frac{\widehat p\,\widehat S}{D^2},\qquad W^*(p)=\frac{\sum_t\widehat\mu_t\widehat u_t(p)+\widehat p\,\widehat S}{D^2}.\)

All menu prices use this same denominator, so objective comparisons reduce to integer comparisons.

Every integer in these computations has \(O(L)\) bits: scaling input rationals contributes \(O(L)\) bits, multiplication by bundle indices contributes \(O(\log(m+1))\), and summation contributes \(O(\log\tau)\). Both logarithmic terms are \(O(L)\).

For reconstruction, when \(\widehat Q_+>\widehat Q_-\), use

\(\displaystyle\theta=\frac{\widehat S-\widehat Q_-}{\widehat Q_+-\widehat Q_-}.\)

Its numerator and denominator have \(O(L)\) bits, as do the resulting rational assignment masses. A sparse assignment uses at most two bundles per type; an explicit table can also be output within the stated bound.

Schoolbook multiplication and division of these integers take \(O(L^2)\) bit operations. Constructing \(D\), scaling the inputs, scanning the menu, and reconstructing the outputs therefore fit within

\(O\!\left(|\mathcal P|\tau(m+1)L^2\right).\)

Because the valuation schedules and menu are explicit, this bound is polynomial in the total input length \(L\). It has no dependence on the number of possible types outside the support.

5. Relation to the cited discrete model

The cited paper defines allocations for finitely many buyers by an integer vector and states general-valuation hardness in Theorem 14. Those integer allocation constraints differ from the splittable masses permitted here. [Brânzei et al., *Walrasian Pricing in Multi-unit Auctions*, Preliminaries and Appendix B.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.08719)

For a concrete illustration, take one type of mass \(1\), with \(m=2\), \(B=2\), values \(v(0)=v(1)=0,\ v(2)=2\), menu \(\mathcal P=\{1\}\), and supply \(q=1\). At price \(1\), demand is exactly \(\{0,2\}\). Splitting mass equally between these bundles attains quantity \(1\), revenue \(1\), and welfare \(1\). Requiring the entire type mass to choose one demanded bundle would permit only bundle \(0\) under the supply constraint.

The interval-attainment argument proves why this distinction makes the stated distributional problem polynomial-time solvable.
