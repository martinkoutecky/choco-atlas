The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite alternative set \(U\), an initial distribution \(\mu_0\) of binary-rational masses over complete rankings, and a stream of rational mass increments \((t,\delta)\), can an online algorithm output after every update a permutation \(\omega\) satisfying \(\mathbb{E}[F_\mu(\omega)]\le 2\min_{\pi\in S_U}F_\mu(\pi)\), where \(F_\mu(\omega)=\sum_t\mu_t\sum_{e\in U}|\omega(e)-t(e)|\), using \(O(n\log n)\) arithmetic operations per update and bit complexity polynomial in the rational input length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(U\) contain \(m\ge 2\) alternatives; types are strict complete rankings \(t\in S_U\), with \(t(e)\) denoting position. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
The initial society \(\mu_0\) has positive total mass. Each update supplies a complete ranking \(t\) and a positive binary-rational increment \(\delta\), replacing \(\mu(t)\) by \(\mu(t)+\delta\); absent types have mass zero. Masses need not sum to one.
Define \(F_\mu(\omega)=\sum_{t\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mu(t)\sum_{e\in U}|\omega(e)-t(e)|\). Updates are fixed independently of the algorithm’s randomness; outputs may be any permutations, with arbitrary resolution of cost ties.
Let \(L\) be the total binary encoding length of the initial input and updates received so far. Allow initialization polynomial in \(m,\tau_0,L_0\); measure subsequent costs in \(m,\tau,L\), including rational arithmetic and random-bit generation.

Does an online algorithm exist that, after every update, outputs \(\omega\in S_U\) satisfying \(\mathbb{E}[F_\mu(\omega)]\le 2\min_{\pi\in S_U}F_\mu(\pi)\), using \(O(m\log m)\) arithmetic operations per update and expected bit complexity polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For unit-mass insertions, dynamic footrule aggregation has an expected \(2\)-approximation with \(O(m\log m)\) update time and \(O(m^2)\) storage in the unit-cost arithmetic model. [Alimi, Mehrabiun and Zarei](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02885v1).
No result establishing the stated update bound together with rational-input bit complexity was located for positive rational-mass increments.

Answer

Yes. Mass-weighted reservoir sampling gives the required expected approximation, with \(O(m)\) arithmetic operations per update and expected bit complexity polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The arithmetic count below concerns operations on integers and rationals. The exact random sampler uses Boolean operations on binary digits, whose costs are included explicitly in the bit complexity. Its implementation keeps the arithmetic count bounded even when sampling requires retries.

The unit-mass reservoir rule appears in Section 3 of [Alimi, Mehrabiun and Zarei](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02885v1). We prove the weighted construction and its complexity directly.

1. Sampling by mass gives the approximation

Define the footrule distance by \(d(s,t)=\sum_{e\in U}|s(e)-t(e)|\), and let \(M=\sum_t\mu(t)>0\).

For any three rankings, the triangle inequality for absolute values, summed over alternatives, gives \(d(s,t)\le d(s,\pi)+d(\pi,t)\).

Suppose a random ranking \(R\) satisfies \(\Pr(R=s)=\mu(s)/M\). Because \(S_U\) is finite, there exists a minimizer \(\pi^*\) of \(F_\mu\). Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E[F_\mu(R)] =\frac1M\sum_{s,t}\mu(s)\mu(t)d(s,t) \le \frac1M\sum_{s,t}\mu(s)\mu(t)\bigl(d(s,\pi^*)+d(\pi^*,t)\bigr) =2F_\mu(\pi^*). \)

Here both sums range over the support. The last equality follows because each of the two terms factors into \(M F_\mu(\pi^*)\).

Thus maintaining a sample proportional to mass is sufficient.

2. The online reservoir

Maintain the total mass \(M\) and one ranking \(R\). On receiving an update \((t,\delta)\), replace \(R\) by \(t\) with probability \(p=\delta/(M+\delta)\), otherwise retain \(R\). Then replace \(M\) by \(M+\delta\) and output \(R\).

Assume the old sample has distribution \(\Pr(R=s)=\mu(s)/M\). Use fresh random bits for the replacement decision. For every ranking \(s\), the new sample satisfies

\( \displaystyle \Pr(R_{\mathrm{new}}=s) =\frac{M}{M+\delta}\frac{\mu(s)}M +\frac{\delta}{M+\delta}\mathbf 1_{\{s=t\}} =\frac{\mu(s)+\delta\mathbf 1_{\{s=t\}}}{M+\delta}. \)

This calculation also covers updates to types already present.

For initialization, start with the first positive-mass type and process the remaining initial types by the same weighted rule. The initial support is nonempty because the initial total mass is positive.

Induction proves the required distribution after every update. The assumption that updates are fixed independently of the algorithm’s randomness ensures that conditioning on the input sequence does not change this argument. Applying Section 1 proves the approximation guarantee at every update.

3. Maintaining rational totals with bounded arithmetic

Store the total mass as an unreduced fraction \(N/D\), where \(N,D\) are positive integers. Write the incoming increment as \(a/b\), with positive integers \(a,b\).

Compute

\( \displaystyle A=aD,\qquad C=Nb,\qquad B=C+A,\qquad D_{\mathrm{new}}=Db. \)

These computations require four integer arithmetic operations. The replacement probability is exactly

\( \displaystyle p=\frac{a/b}{N/D+a/b}=\frac{aD}{Nb+aD}=\frac AB. \)

After sampling the replacement decision, store \(N_{\mathrm{new}}=B\) and \(D_{\mathrm{new}}=Db\). No fraction reduction or greatest-common-divisor computation is needed.

Since the old total mass and the increment are positive, \(0<A<B\).

4. An exact coin with polynomial expected bit cost

We now implement a Bernoulli coin of probability \(A/B\), without adding an unbounded number of integer arithmetic operations.

Let \(k\) be the binary length of \(B\). In each trial, generate \(k\) independent fair bits, representing a uniformly distributed integer \(X\) in \(\{0,\ldots,2^k-1\}\).

Accept the trial when \(X<B\). On acceptance, return true exactly when \(X<A\). Otherwise repeat with fresh bits.

Conditioned on acceptance, \(X\) is uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,B-1\}\), so the probability of returning true is exactly \(A/B\). Moreover, \(2^{k-1}\le B<2^k\), so each trial succeeds with probability at least \(1/2\). Therefore the sampler terminates almost surely, uses at most two trials in expectation, and consumes at most \(2k\) random bits in expectation.

To keep the arithmetic count bounded, implement the comparisons by finite-state scans of binary digits:

  • Scan the digits of \(B\) from least significant to most significant, generating one fair bit per digit.
  • Simultaneously scan \(A\), using zero digits after its representation ends.
  • Maintain, separately for each threshold, one of three states: less, equal, or greater. Whenever the generated digit differs from the threshold digit, overwrite the state with their relation; otherwise retain it.

After the scan, the state records the relation at the most significant differing digit, hence the correct integer comparison. The transitions use a fixed Boolean lookup table. Retrying consists of rewinding the digit strings and resetting these states.

Thus each trial takes \(O(k)\) bit operations and performs no additional integer or rational arithmetic. The expected sampling cost is \(O(k)\) bit operations, including random-bit generation.

5. Encoding lengths and total complexity

Consider all \(r\) mass records processed so far, including the initial support entries, and write their masses as \(a_i/b_i\). The maintained integers satisfy

\( \displaystyle D=\prod_{i=1}^r b_i,\qquad N=\sum_{i=1}^r a_i\prod_{j\ne i}b_j. \)

These identities follow directly by induction from the update formulas.

Let \(\ell(x)\) denote the binary length of a positive integer. Since the numerical encodings form part of the input,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^r\bigl(\ell(a_i)+\ell(b_i)\bigr)\le L. \)

It follows that \(D<2^L\). Each summand defining \(N\) is also less than \(2^L\), and therefore \(N<r2^L\). Since \(r\le L\), both maintained integers have \(O(L)\) bits. The temporary integers used in an update likewise have \(O(L)\) bits.

Consequently:

  • The total-mass update uses four integer arithmetic operations. Reading, copying, and outputting a ranking requires \(O(m)\) ordinary element operations, giving an \(O(m)\), hence \(O(m\log m)\), arithmetic-operation bound per update.
  • Standard binary multiplication and addition take polynomial bit time on these \(O(L)\)-bit integers. The exact coin takes \(O(L)\) bit operations and random bits in expectation.
  • Initialization processes \(\tau_0\) weighted records, so its expected bit complexity is polynomial in \(m,\tau_0,L_0\).
  • Subsequent expected bit complexity is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Even the cumulative expected cost through the current prefix is polynomial, because the number of processed records is at most \(L\).

No enumeration of possible rankings is required. If the optimum is zero, every positive-mass type equals the same ranking; the reservoir necessarily outputs that ranking, so the guarantee also holds in this case.
