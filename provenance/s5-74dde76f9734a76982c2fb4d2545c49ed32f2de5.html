The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a population with type masses \(p_r>0\), \(\sum_r p_r=1\), and \(m\) indivisible good types, each type-good pair has an unknown value distribution shared by its members, with mean \(\theta_{rk}\in[a,b]\). Goods arrive over horizon \(H\); before each arrival, a history-dependent policy chooses probabilities \(q^t_{rk}\ge 0\), \(\sum_r q^t_{rk}=1\), then allocates the good to a randomly selected member of the chosen type and observes that member’s value. With per-capita allocation rate \(q^t_{rk}/p_r\), require type-level envy-freeness \(U_{r,r}(q^t)\ge U_{r,s}(q^t)\) for all \(r,s\), or proportionality \(U_{r,r}(q^t)\ge\sum_s p_s U_{r,s}(q^t)\), where \(U_{r,s}(q^t)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_k (q^t_{sk}/p_s)\theta_{rk}\). What policy satisfies the selected fairness condition at every round with probability at least \(1-1/H\), and what regret can it guarantee relative to the welfare-maximizing fair allocation, with dependence on \(H\), \(\tau\), \(m\), and the type masses made explicit?

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(p_r\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type is a nonatomic population of identical members.
There are \(m\ge1\) good types and \(H\ge2\) rounds. Known rational constants satisfy \(0<a\le b\) and \(\sigma>0\). Each pair \((r,k)\) has an unknown real-valued distribution with mean \(\theta_{rk}\in[a,b]\) and \(\sigma\)-sub-Gaussian centered noise; observations are independent fresh draws. Means need not satisfy any normalization.
Good types arrive independently and uniformly. Before observing the next good type, a policy uses only its past observations to choose \(q^t\ge0\) with \(\sum_r q^t_{rk}=1\). After observing type \(k\), it chooses recipient type \(r\) with probability \(q^t_{rk}\), samples a member according to normalized population measure within that type, and observes only that recipient's value.
Define \(U_{r,s}(q)=m^{-1}\sum_k(q_{sk}/p_s)\theta_{rk}\). The input selects either envy-freeness, \(U_{r,r}(q)\ge U_{r,s}(q)\) for every \(r,s\), or proportionality, \(U_{r,r}(q)\ge\sum_s p_sU_{r,s}(q)\) for every \(r\).
Let \(W(q)=m^{-1}\sum_{r,k}q_{rk}\theta_{rk}\), let \(W^*\) be its maximum over feasible allocations satisfying the selected fairness condition, and define random pseudo-regret \(R_H=HW^*-\sum_{t=1}^H W(q^t)\).
The required guarantee, uniformly over admissible distributions, is a single event of probability at least \(1-1/H\) on which every round is fair and \(R_H\le C\,H^{2/3}(\log H)^c\), where \(c\) is an absolute constant and \(C\) is independent of \(H\), with explicit dependence on \(m,\tau,p,a,b,\sigma\).
Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the known rational input. Report computation in \(m,\tau,H,L\) and any additional numerical-precision parameters; real-valued feedback is accessed through a sampling oracle, with arithmetic operation counts stated separately from bit complexity.

For each fairness choice, is achieving this guarantee possible in polynomial time in the stated input and precision parameters, or is achieving it NP-hard?

For finite players, a polynomially computable policy achieves \(\widetilde O(H^{2/3})\) regret and simultaneous roundwise fairness with probability \(1-1/H\); a worst-case \(\widetilde\Omega(H^{2/3})\) lower bound holds for both fairness notions. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01795v3)

Result and scope

Both envy-freeness and proportionality admit a polynomial-time policy satisfying the stated guarantee. The policy uses one robust linear program whose size depends on the support size, rather than on the denominators of the population masses.

The regret constant proved below is explicit. For proportionality it has moderate dependence on the masses. For envy-freeness it can be extremely large: the proof uses the quantitative finite-player slack lemma. Thus this settles the computational question under the stated allowance that the constant may depend arbitrarily on the known parameters; it does not establish polynomial dependence of that constant on the input length.

All fairness assertions concern the allocation probabilities specified in the question.

1. Definitions and constants

Write \(d=\tau m\), \(p_{\min}=\min_r p_r\), and let \(q^0_{rk}=p_r\). This allocation is fair for every possible mean matrix: every per-capita allocation vector is the all-ones vector.

For a matrix \(v\), define \(W_v(q)=m^{-1}\sum_{r,k}q_{rk}v_{rk}\).

The proportionality constraints simplify to

\(\displaystyle \sum_k(q_{rk}-p_r)\theta_{rk}\geq 0.\)

Indeed, \(\sum_s p_sU_{r,s}(q)=m^{-1}\sum_k\theta_{rk}\), because each column of \(q\) sums to one.

For envy-freeness, the constraints are

\(\displaystyle \sum_k\left(\frac{q_{rk}}{p_r}-\frac{q_{sk}}{p_s}\right)\theta_{rk}\geq 0.\)

Define the following constants for the two fairness choices:

\(\displaystyle K_{\mathrm{PR}}=\frac{4b}{ap_{\min}}.\)

For envy-freeness, let \(n\) be the least common multiple of the denominators of the masses, written in reduced form. Set

\(\displaystyle B=\frac ba,\qquad D=(4Bn^4)^{n^2}(2n)^{n^3},\qquad K_{\mathrm{EF}}=8D\left(n^4+\frac{bm}{a}\right).\)

These expressions depend only on the known input. In particular, they are independent of the horizon.

If there is only one population type, or only one good type, the baseline allocation is optimal and has zero regret. The argument below also covers these cases.

2. The compact robust linear program

Suppose estimates \(\widehat\theta\) and a rational radius \(\varepsilon>0\) satisfy

\(\displaystyle \max_{r,k}|\widehat\theta_{rk}-\theta_{rk}|\leq\varepsilon.\)

Consider the uncertainty box

\(\displaystyle \mathcal B=\{v:|v_{rk}-\widehat\theta_{rk}|\leq\varepsilon\text{ for every }r,k\}.\)

We maximize \(W_{\widehat\theta}(q)\) over allocations fair for every matrix in this box.

For envy-freeness, put \(h_{rsk}=q_{rk}/p_r-q_{sk}/p_s\). Robust fairness is equivalent to

\(\displaystyle \sum_k\widehat\theta_{rk}h_{rsk}\geq\varepsilon\sum_k|h_{rsk}|\qquad(r\ne s).\)

This follows by minimizing the left-hand fairness expression independently over each uncertain coordinate.

Introduce variables \(z_{rsk}\geq h_{rsk}\) and \(z_{rsk}\geq-h_{rsk}\), and impose

\(\displaystyle \sum_k\widehat\theta_{rk}h_{rsk}\geq\varepsilon\sum_kz_{rsk}.\)

These are linear constraints. Their projection onto the allocation variables is exactly the robust feasible set: one implication uses \(z_{rsk}\geq|h_{rsk}|\), and the reverse implication takes \(z_{rsk}=|h_{rsk}|\).

For proportionality, use \(h_{rk}=q_{rk}-p_r\) and the corresponding constraints

\(\displaystyle z_{rk}\geq h_{rk},\qquad z_{rk}\geq-h_{rk},\qquad \sum_k\widehat\theta_{rk}h_{rk}\geq\varepsilon\sum_kz_{rk}.\)

In both cases, also impose \(q\geq0\) and the column-sum equalities. The baseline allocation is always feasible, so the program is never infeasible.

The resulting rational linear programs have \(O(\tau^2m)\) variables and constraints for envy-freeness, and \(O(\tau m)\) for proportionality.

3. A robust approximation lemma for proportionality

Claim. If the estimation bound above holds, there exists an allocation fair throughout \(\mathcal B\) whose true welfare is at least \(W^*-K_{\mathrm{PR}}\varepsilon\).

Proof. Let \(q^*\) be an optimal truly proportional allocation. Such an optimum exists because the allocation polytope is compact and its fairness constraints are closed.

Define its unnormalized slacks by

\(\displaystyle S_r=\sum_k(q^*_{rk}-p_r)\theta_{rk}\geq0,\qquad S=\sum_rS_r.\)

Then

\(\displaystyle W^*-W_\theta(q^0)=\frac Sm.\)

Set \(\Gamma=4m\varepsilon\) and \(T=\Gamma/(ap_{\min})\).

If \(S\leq bT\), use the baseline allocation. It is robustly fair, and its welfare loss is at most

\(\displaystyle \frac{bT}{m}=\frac{4b}{ap_{\min}}\varepsilon.\)

Now suppose \(S>bT\). Write \(M_r=\sum_kq^*_{rk}\). Proportionality and positivity imply

\(\displaystyle bM_r\geq\sum_kq^*_{rk}\theta_{rk}\geq p_r\sum_k\theta_{rk}\geq amp_r>0.\)

Thus \(M_r>0\). Remove the amounts

\(\displaystyle \Delta_{rk}=\frac{q^*_{rk}}{M_r}\frac{S_r}{S}T,\)

and redistribute the removed allocation according to the masses:

\(\displaystyle q'_{rk}=q^*_{rk}-\Delta_{rk}+p_r\sum_s\Delta_{sk}.\)

To verify nonnegativity, observe that \(S_r\leq bM_r\) and

\(\displaystyle \frac{S_r}{S}T\leq\frac{S_r}{b}\leq M_r.\)

Hence \(\Delta_{rk}\leq q^*_{rk}\). Column sums remain one because \(\sum_rp_r=1\).

Type \(r\)'s utility loss from removal is at most

\(\displaystyle \sum_k\Delta_{rk}\theta_{rk}\leq bT\frac{S_r}{S}\leq S_r.\)

Also, \(\sum_{r,k}\Delta_{rk}=T\). Its utility gain from redistribution is therefore at least

\(\displaystyle p_r\sum_{s,k}\Delta_{sk}\theta_{rk}\geq ap_rT\geq\Gamma.\)

Consequently, every new proportionality slack is at least \(\Gamma\).

For any allocation, \(\sum_k|q_{rk}-p_r|\leq m+mp_r\leq2m\). Every \(v\in\mathcal B\) satisfies \(|v_{rk}-\theta_{rk}|\leq2\varepsilon\). Thus replacing the true means by \(v\) can decrease a proportionality slack by at most \(4m\varepsilon=\Gamma\). The allocation \(q'\) is robustly proportional.

Finally, its true welfare loss is at most the utility removed:

\(\displaystyle W^*-W_\theta(q')\leq\frac1m\sum_{r,k}\Delta_{rk}\theta_{rk}\leq\frac{bT}{m}=K_{\mathrm{PR}}\varepsilon.\)

This proves the claim. ∎

4. A robust approximation lemma for envy-freeness

We use one known geometric result, with its quantitative dependence made explicit.

Finite-player slack lemma. For \(n\) players, \(m\) good types, and means \(\nu_{ik}\in[1,B]\), let \(Y\) maximize unnormalized welfare among envy-free allocations. If \(0<\gamma<D^{-1}\), there exists an allocation \(X\) with welfare at least that of \(Y\) minus \(2n^4D\gamma\), such that each ordered pair of players either has identical allocation rows or satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_k(X_{ik}-X_{jk})\nu_{ik}\geq\gamma.\)

This is the slack construction in Lemma 1 and Appendix F of [*Honor Among Bandits*](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01795v3#S3). Its initial slack is \(D\gamma\); there are at most \(n^3\) iterations. Each iteration loses at most \(nD\gamma\) directly and \(nD\gamma/4\) through envy repair, giving the stated conservative constant.

Claim. If the estimation bound holds, there exists an allocation fair throughout \(\mathcal B\) whose true welfare is at least \(W^*-K_{\mathrm{EF}}\varepsilon\).

Proof. Introduce \(n_r=np_r\) formal copies of each population type. These are positive integers. Every copy of type \(r\) has normalized means \(\nu_{ik}=\theta_{rk}/a\), which lie in \([1,B]\).

An allocation \(q\) induces identical rows for the copies of each type:

\(\displaystyle X_{ik}=\frac{q_{rk}}{n_r}\quad\text{when }i\text{ has type }r.\)

Its columns sum to one. Finite-player envy-freeness is equivalent to the original envy-freeness, because

\(\displaystyle \sum_k(X_{ik}-X_{jk})\nu_{ik} =\frac1{an}\sum_k\left(\frac{q_{rk}}{p_r}-\frac{q_{sk}}{p_s}\right)\theta_{rk}.\)

Moreover, its unnormalized welfare is \(mW_\theta(q)/a\).

Conversely, average any finite-player envy-free allocation over permutations of copies within each population type. The true mean matrix is invariant under these permutations. Every permuted allocation is therefore envy-free with the same welfare. Convexity preserves envy-freeness upon averaging, and the averaged rows are identical within each type. Aggregating them yields an original feasible allocation.

Thus the finite-player optimum is exactly \(mW^*/a\).

Suppose first that \(\varepsilon<a/(8mD)\). Apply the finite-player lemma with

\(\displaystyle \gamma=\frac{4m\varepsilon}{a}<\frac1{2D}.\)

Consider arbitrary copy-specific means lying within \(\varepsilon/a\) of the corresponding estimated normalized means. Their distance from the true normalized means is at most \(2\varepsilon/a\).

For unequal allocation rows, perturbing the observer's means changes its envy-free slack by at most

\(\displaystyle \frac{2\varepsilon}{a}\sum_k|X_{ik}-X_{jk}| \leq\frac{4m\varepsilon}{a}=\gamma,\)

since allocation entries lie in \([0,1]\). Equal rows satisfy the constraint identically. Hence the lemma's allocation is envy-free throughout this copy-specific uncertainty box.

This robust feasible set is convex and invariant under permutations of copies within a type: the uncertainty intervals are identical for such copies. Averaging over those permutations therefore preserves robust feasibility and true welfare. Aggregation now yields an original allocation fair throughout \(\mathcal B\).

Its welfare loss is at most

\(\displaystyle \frac am\,2n^4D\gamma=8n^4D\varepsilon.\)

If instead \(\varepsilon\geq a/(8mD)\), use the baseline. Its welfare loss is at most \(b\), and

\(\displaystyle b\leq\frac{8bmD}{a}\varepsilon.\)

Both cases are bounded by \(K_{\mathrm{EF}}\varepsilon\). ∎

The copies are used only in this proof. The policy never constructs them, evaluates \(D\), or executes the finite-player slack algorithm.

5. Welfare of the computed robust optimizer

Let \(K\) denote the applicable constant above, and let \(\widehat q\) maximize the compact robust program.

On the estimation event, either approximation lemma supplies a robustly feasible \(q'\) with

\(\displaystyle W_\theta(q')\geq W^*-K\varepsilon.\)

For every allocation, the column-sum equalities imply

\(\displaystyle |W_\theta(q)-W_{\widehat\theta}(q)| \leq\frac{\varepsilon}{m}\sum_{r,k}q_{rk}=\varepsilon.\)

Optimality of \(\widehat q\) therefore gives

\(\displaystyle W_\theta(\widehat q) \geq W_{\widehat\theta}(\widehat q)-\varepsilon \geq W_{\widehat\theta}(q')-\varepsilon \geq W^*-(K+2)\varepsilon.\)

The computed allocation is also truly fair because \(\theta\in\mathcal B\).

6. Policy and its single success event

We use the convention that centered \(\sigma\)-sub-Gaussian noise satisfies

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E e^{\lambda(X-\theta)}\leq e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2/2}\quad\text{for every real }\lambda.\)

Define

\(\displaystyle s=\lceil H^{2/3}\rceil,\qquad \ell=\lceil\log_2(8dH)\rceil,\qquad \ell_y=\lceil\log_2(8H^2)\rceil.\)

If \(sp_{\min}/m<8\ell\), use the baseline throughout. Otherwise, use it for the first \(s\) rounds.

To specify finite precision, let

\(\displaystyle j=\lceil\log_2H\rceil,\qquad \eta=2^{-j}\leq H^{-1}.\)

Choose a rational \(M=b+\sigma\lceil\sqrt{2\ell_y}\rceil\), and let \(G=\eta\lceil M/\eta\rceil\). Quantize each exploration observation to the grid of spacing \(\eta\), saturating outside \([-G,G]\). The output is bounded, and its error is at most \(\eta\) whenever the observation lies in \([-M,M]\).

Let \(N_{rk}\) count exploration observations of pair \((r,k)\). If any count is smaller than \(sp_{\min}/(2m)\), use the baseline for all remaining rounds.

Otherwise, form each quantized empirical mean and project it onto \([a,b]\), obtaining \(\widehat\theta_{rk}\). Choose a rational \(\rho\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \sqrt{\frac{m\ell}{sp_{\min}}}\leq\rho\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{m\ell}{sp_{\min}}},\)

and set \(\varepsilon=2\sigma\rho+\eta\). Solve the robust program once and use its optimizer for every remaining round.

All allocation matrices are selected before observing the next good type.

We now verify the probability bound. During exploration, each pair has probability \(p_r/m\), independently across rounds. A multiplicative Chernoff bound gives

\(\displaystyle \Pr\!\left(\exists r,k:N_{rk}<\frac{sp_{\min}}{2m}\right) \leq d e^{-sp_{\min}/(8m)} \leq d e^{-\ell} \leq\frac1{8H}.\)

Conditional on the exploration labels and successful counts, the raw empirical means obey

\(\displaystyle \Pr\!\left(\exists r,k: |\overline X_{rk}-\theta_{rk}|> 2\sigma\sqrt{\frac{m\ell}{sp_{\min}}}\right) \leq2d e^{-\ell}\leq\frac1{4H}.\)

Here conditioning is legitimate because exploration decisions do not depend on observed values.

For each observation, sub-Gaussian concentration gives

\(\displaystyle \Pr(|X|>M)\leq2e^{-\ell_y}.\)

A union bound over at most \(H\) exploration observations yields probability at most \(1/(4H)\) that any exceeds this threshold.

Intersect these three events. Its probability is at least \(1-5/(8H)\), hence at least \(1-1/H\). On the intersection, quantization changes every empirical mean by at most \(\eta\). Projection onto \([a,b]\) cannot increase its distance from the true mean. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \max_{r,k}|\widehat\theta_{rk}-\theta_{rk}|\leq\varepsilon.\)

Every exploration round is deterministically fair; every subsequent round is fair on this same event.

7. Explicit regret guarantee

In the branch that estimates the means,

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon\leq 4\sigma\sqrt{\frac{m\ell}{sp_{\min}}}+\frac1H.\)

Since \(s\geq H^{2/3}\) and \(s\leq2H^{2/3}\), the preceding welfare bound gives, on the success event,

\(\displaystyle R_H \leq bs+(H-s)(K+2)\varepsilon \leq \left[2b+(K+2)\left(4\sigma\sqrt{\frac m{p_{\min}}}+1\right)\right] H^{2/3}\sqrt{\ell}.\)

The exploration loss bound uses \(W^*\leq b\) and nonnegative welfare.

In the baseline-only branch, its defining inequality implies

\(\displaystyle H^{1/3}<\sqrt{\frac{8m\ell}{p_{\min}}}.\)

Thus, deterministically,

\(\displaystyle R_H\leq bH \leq b\sqrt{\frac{8m}{p_{\min}}}\,H^{2/3}\sqrt{\ell}.\)

Define

\(\displaystyle C_0= 2b+(K+2)\left(4\sigma\sqrt{\frac m{p_{\min}}}+1\right) +b\sqrt{\frac{8m}{p_{\min}}}.\)

For every \(H\geq2\),

\(\displaystyle \ell\leq \frac{\log_2(8d)+2}{\ln2}\,\ln H.\)

Therefore the required guarantee holds with the absolute exponent \(c=1/2\) and the explicit constant

\(\displaystyle C= C_0\sqrt{\frac{\log_2(8\tau m)+2}{\ln2}}.\)

This constant is uniform over all admissible distributions.

8. Computation and oracle precision

The algorithm makes at most \(H\) feedback-oracle calls and solves at most one rational linear program.

All quantized feedback, estimates, confidence radii, and LP coefficients have bit length

\(\displaystyle B_0=O\!\left(L+\log H+\log(m\tau)\right).\)

For example, a power of two gives the required factor-two square-root approximation \(\rho\). The integers defining the exploration duration and logarithmic parameters are computable by integer arithmetic. Neither cloning nor evaluation of the enormous envy-free constant is required.

Using standard polynomial-time rational linear programming:

  • For envy-freeness, the LP has \(O(\tau^2m)\) variables and constraints.
  • For proportionality, it has \(O(\tau m)\) variables and constraints.
  • LP bit complexity, rational arithmetic-operation counts, and the encoding length of an exact rational optimizer are polynomial in those dimensions and \(B_0\).

Feedback quantization takes \(O(B_0)\) real comparisons per processed observation by binary search on the bounded grid. Thus the arithmetic-oracle computation uses \(O(HB_0)\) feedback comparisons, plus polynomially many rational operations for bookkeeping, optimization, and allocation sampling.

The internal rational bit computation is polynomial in \(m,\tau,H,L\). Exact sampling from rational allocation probabilities can be implemented in expected polynomial bit time using fair random bits, or treated as the categorical-sampling primitive already present in the model.

For an approximation-based feedback interface, it suffices to return the saturated observation to absolute accuracy \(\eta\); that oracle cost must be added separately. No finite bit encoding of an arbitrary exact real observation is assumed. Crucially, numerical approximation of feedback enlarges the confidence interval, while the final rational fairness constraints are solved exactly.

9. Conclusion

The compact robust programs, together with the proved approximation lemmas, give polynomial-time policies for both fairness choices. Each policy satisfies simultaneous roundwise fairness and the required regret bound on one event of probability at least \(1-1/H\).

The envy-free constant is quantitatively weak, but that weakness does not affect polynomial policy computation.
