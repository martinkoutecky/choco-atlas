The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) items, value bounds \(v_{\min},v_{\max}\), and at most \(\tau\) complete buyer types, an instance is an ordered online stream of cohorts with rational masses and type valuations \(v_t:2^{[m]}\to\{0\}\cup[v_{\min},v_{\max}]\). Supply is normalized to one unit per item. Each infinitesimal mass unit receives an integral bundle, so the online policy chooses a distribution over bundles for each arriving mass and must keep aggregate use of each item at most one. What is the best competitive ratio \(\alpha^*(m,\rho,\tau)\) achievable by a causal policy against the offline optimum, where \(\rho=v_{\max}/v_{\min}\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix \(m\geq1\), \(\tau\geq1\), and rational \(0<v_{\min}\leq v_{\max}\); write \(\rho=v_{\max}/v_{\min}\). There are no production costs and each item has supply one.
A society is represented by its support: the list of its types with positive total mass, each mass a positive binary rational. The support size is at most \(\tau\); this counts actual buyer types, not possible valuation functions. Total mass is unrestricted and need not sum to one.
Each type \(t\) has an explicitly tabulated rational valuation \(v_t:2^{[m]}\to\{0\}\cup[v_{\min},v_{\max}]\), with \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\); no monotonicity or other structural assumption is imposed.
The society arrives as a finite ordered stream of \(N\) cohorts \((t_i,q_i)\), with positive rational masses. Initially a policy knows only \(m,\tau,v_{\min},v_{\max}\); each cohort reveals its mass and complete valuation on arrival. Future types, total type masses, and the stopping time are unknown; types may recur.
For cohort \(i\), the policy irrevocably assigns nonnegative masses \(x_{i,S}\) to integral bundles \(S\subseteq[m]\), with \(\sum_S x_{i,S}=q_i\). Every prefix must satisfy \(\sum_i\sum_{S\ni j}x_{i,S}\leq1\) for each item \(j\). Welfare is \(W=\sum_{i,S}v_{t_i}(S)x_{i,S}\); \(\operatorname{OPT}\) maximizes the same welfare with full knowledge of the stream and the same mass allocation constraints.
Allow randomized causal policies against streams fixed independently of their randomness. Define \(\alpha^*(m,\rho,\tau)\) as the infimum of \(\alpha\geq1\) for which a policy guarantees \(\mathbb E[W]\geq\operatorname{OPT}/\alpha\) on every such stream, with no additive loss. There is no computational restriction; state any running times in \(m,\tau,N,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length.

What is the optimal worst-case competitive ratio \(\alpha^*(m,\rho,\tau)\) for this continuous online allocation problem?

For discrete buyers with unrestricted type support, even randomized algorithms cannot achieve \(o(\log m/\log\log m+\log\rho)\)-competitiveness. [Huang–Kim](https://arxiv.org/html/1411.4384)
For infinitesimal buyers with strictly convex differentiable production costs, optimal competitive ratios allowing additive loss are characterized; this does not characterize the finite-support, hard-capacity problem above. No exact characterization for that problem was verified. [Huang–Kim](https://arxiv.org/html/1411.4384)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Valuations and value bounds are binary rationals, and complete valuations are encoded as explicit bundle tables; L includes these tables and the cohort stream.
  • The empty bundle has value zero, and valuations otherwise retain the arbitrary, nonnegative domain of the discrete model.
  • Masses are unrestricted positive rationals rather than probabilities summing to one, matching unrestricted buyer counts.
  • The support representation describes the complete instance, but is revealed online through cohorts; aggregate masses, unseen types, and the stopping time are not supplied in advance.
  • Each cohort is homogeneous, its mass and valuation are revealed together, and a type may appear in multiple cohorts.
  • Mass assigned to a bundle consumes that mass of every item in the bundle and contributes mass times the bundle valuation; unserved mass receives the empty bundle.
  • The offline benchmark uses the same divisible-mass allocation model.
  • Randomized policies are evaluated in expectation against oblivious streams, without computational or incentive-compatibility restrictions.
  • Competitiveness is purely multiplicative: allowing an instance-independent additive loss at fixed bounded values and unit supplies would make the ratio vacuous.
  • The parameter tau bounds actual positive-mass support size; runtime statements additionally include the cohort count N and total input bit length L.

1. Status and proved results

The exact value of $\alpha^*(m,\rho,\tau)$ remains unresolved in this argument. However, the dependence on $m$ and $\rho$ for one recurring type can be determined up to universal constant factors.

Define
$$ R=\min\{m,\rho\}, \qquad h(R)=1+\frac{\ln R}{\ln(2+\ln R)}. $$

I prove
$$ \boxed{ \frac{h(R)}{100} \le \alpha^*(m,\rho,1) \le 4h(R). } $$

Thus
$$ \boxed{ \alpha^*(m,\rho,1) = \Theta\left( 1+\frac{\ln\min\{m,\rho\}} {\ln(2+\ln\min\{m,\rho\})} \right). } $$

In particular, $\alpha^*(m,\rho,1)=1$ whenever $m=1$ or $\rho=1$.

There is also a new explicit upper bound for arbitrary support size. For integer $\tau\ge1$, define
$$ U_\tau(R)= \begin{cases} \tau,&R=1,\\[2mm] \displaystyle \min_{k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}} \max\left\{ \tau(k+1),\left\lceil R^{1/k}\right\rceil \right\}, &R>1. \end{cases} $$
Then
$$ \boxed{ \alpha^*(m,\rho,\tau)\le U_\tau(R)\le4\tau h(R). } $$

The policies proving these upper bounds are deterministic, causal, and feasible for finite cohorts. Their guarantees have no additive loss.

If $\tau$ is not integral, replace it throughout by $\lfloor\tau\rfloor$.

2. Normalization and the offline curve for one type

Divide every valuation by $v_{\min}$. Both online and offline welfare scale by the same positive factor, so competitive ratios are unchanged. Henceforth every positive valuation lies in $[1,\rho]$.

We allocate only positive-valued bundles. Any remaining cohort mass receives the empty bundle. This preserves the required cohort mass equality and consumes no additional capacity.

Fix a type with valuation $v$. If all its valuations are zero, it contributes no welfare and receives only empty bundles. Otherwise, let
$$ \mathcal B=\{S\subseteq[m]:v(S)>0\}, \qquad p=\max_{S\in\mathcal B}v(S). $$
Every bundle in $\mathcal B$ is nonempty.

Define its full-capacity packing polytope
$$ \mathcal P= \left\{ a\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\mathcal B}: \sum_{S\ni j}a_S\le1 \text{ for every }j\in[m] \right\}. $$
For every $a\in\mathcal P$,
$$ \sum_Sa_S \le \sum_S|S|a_S = \sum_{j=1}^m\sum_{S\ni j}a_S \le m. $$
Consequently, $\mathcal P$ is compact.

For cumulative buyer mass $q\ge0$, define
$$ F(q)= \max\left\{ \sum_Sv(S)a_S: a\in\mathcal P,\ \sum_Sa_S\le q \right\}. $$
The maximum exists because its feasible set is nonempty and compact.

This is exactly the offline optimum for a stream consisting only of this type with total mass $q$. Indeed, any stream allocation aggregates to a feasible vector in this program. Conversely, if the cohort masses are $q_i$ and $\sum_iq_i=q>0$, an aggregate packing $a$ can be assigned by giving cohort $i$ the masses
$$ x_{i,S}=\frac{q_i}{q}a_S $$
and assigning its remaining mass to the empty bundle. Each prefix uses at most the final packing’s item loads.

The following properties will be used.

Monotonicity and concavity. The function $F$ is nondecreasing. If packings are feasible at masses $q$ and $q'$, their convex combination is feasible at the corresponding convex combination of these masses. Therefore $F$ is concave.

Continuity and decreasing average welfare. We have $F(0)=0$ and $F(q)\le pq$. For $0<a<b$, scaling an optimal packing at $b$ by $a/b$ gives
$$ F(a)\ge\frac abF(b). $$
Thus $F(q)/q$ is nonincreasing on $(0,\infty)$. Moreover,
$$ 0\le F(b)-F(a) \le \left(1-\frac ab\right)F(b) \le p(b-a). $$
Hence $F$ is continuous, including at zero.

Saturation mass. Let
$$ F_\infty=\max_{a\in\mathcal P}\sum_Sv(S)a_S $$
and let $q_*$ be the minimum total mass among packings attaining $F_\infty$. Both optimizations attain their optima. We have
$$ F(q_*)=F_\infty,\qquad q_*\le m, $$
and $F(q)<F_\infty$ whenever $q<q_*$.

For every $q\in(0,q_*)$, an optimal packing defining $F(q)$ has total mass exactly $q$. To prove this, suppose an optimum had mass $b<q$. Mix it with a welfare-maximizing packing of mass $q_*$. A sufficiently small positive mixture still has mass at most $q$, but has strictly larger welfare because $F(q)<F_\infty$. This is a contradiction. At $q_*$, the defining minimum-mass optimum also has mass exactly $q_*$.

Initial linear region. Let $q_0$ be the maximum mass packable using only bundles of value $p$. Then
$$ F(q)=pq\qquad(0\le q\le q_0). $$
The upper bound is $F(q)\le pq$; attainability follows by scaling a maximum-mass packing of value-$p$ bundles.

Also,
$$ F_\infty\ge pq_0, \qquad F_\infty\le pq_*, $$
so $q_0\le q_*$.

Define the terminal average welfare
$$ a_*=\frac{F_\infty}{q_*} $$
and the average-welfare range
$$ D=\frac p{a_*}. $$
Since positive valuations are at least one, $a_*\ge1$. A unit mass of a value-$p$ bundle is feasible, so $F_\infty\ge p$. Consequently,
$$ 1\le D \le\rho, \qquad D=\frac{pq_*}{F_\infty}\le q_*\le m. $$
Therefore
$$ \boxed{D\le R=\min\{m,\rho\}.} $$

If $D=1$, then $a_*=p$, and the inequalities above imply $q_*=q_0$.

3. A concatenation lemma

The central idea is to plan allocations for several possible cumulative masses using the revealed valuation table.

Lemma. Suppose
$$ q_0<q_1<\cdots<q_\ell=q_* $$
are masses with optimal packing profiles $a^{(i)}$ of total mass exactly $q_i$. Write
$$ b_i=\frac{F(q_i)}{q_i}. $$
Suppose a real number $c\ge1$ satisfies
$$ c\ge\ell+1, \qquad b_0=p, \qquad b_i\ge\frac{b_{i-1}}c \quad(1\le i\le\ell). $$
Then there is a causal policy for this type whose welfare at every cumulative mass $q$ is at least $F(q)/c$.

Each item’s total consumption by this policy is at most $(\ell+1)/c$.

Proof. Serve the scaled profiles
$$ \frac1c a^{(0)},\frac1c a^{(1)},\ldots,\frac1c a^{(\ell)} $$
consecutively as buyer mass arrives.

Batch $i$ has mass $q_i/c$. During that batch, distribute arriving mass proportionally to $a^{(i)}$. Thus the welfare rate per unit buyer mass is $b_i$. Once all batches are complete, give subsequent mass the empty bundle.

A finite cohort can cross several batch boundaries: assign its respective portions according to those batches and give any remainder the empty bundle. This directly defines a permissible allocation to the finite cohort.

Let
$$ T_i=\frac1c\sum_{h=0}^iq_h, \qquad T_{-1}=0. $$
Because the masses increase and $c\ge\ell+1$,
$$ T_i\le\frac{i+1}{c}q_i\le q_i. $$

During batch zero, the policy’s welfare is $pq$, which is at least $F(q)/c$.

Now consider batch $i\ge1$, at cumulative mass
$$ T_{i-1}\le q\le T_i. $$
Put $b=q_{i-1}$. At the batch’s start, the accumulated welfare is at least $F(b)/c$, and $T_{i-1}\le b$.

If $q\le b$, monotonicity gives
$$ W(q)\ge\frac{F(b)}c\ge\frac{F(q)}c. $$

If $q>b$, the constant welfare rate in this batch gives
$$ W(q)\ge\frac{F(b)}c+b_i(q-b). $$
Since $F(q)/q$ is nonincreasing,
$$ F(q)\le\frac{F(b)}bq = F(b)+b_{i-1}(q-b). $$
Using $b_i\ge b_{i-1}/c$ proves $W(q)\ge F(q)/c$.

After the last batch, accumulated welfare is at least
$$ \frac{F(q_\ell)}c=\frac{F_\infty}c, $$
which also suffices for every later mass.

Finally, each complete scaled profile consumes at most $1/c$ of every item. Every partial profile consumes no more than its complete profile. Thus every prefix uses at most $(\ell+1)/c$ of each item. $\square$

4. Selecting profiles and proving the upper bounds

Fix an integer $c\ge2$.

If $D=1$, use just the profile at $q_*=q_0$.

If $D>1$, set
$$ \ell=\left\lceil\log_cD\right\rceil. $$
For each $1\le i<\ell$, choose $q_i$ satisfying
$$ \frac{F(q_i)}{q_i}=\frac p{c^i}, $$
and put $q_\ell=q_*$.

These intermediate masses exist: the continuous, nonincreasing function $F(q)/q$ takes the value $p$ at $q_0$ and the value $a_*$ at $q_*$. The intermediate target values lie strictly between them. Their strict decrease also ensures
$$ q_0<q_1<\cdots<q_\ell. $$

The corresponding averages satisfy
$$ b_i=\frac{b_{i-1}}c\quad(1\le i<\ell) $$
and, because $D\le c^\ell$,
$$ b_\ell=a_* \ge\frac p{c^\ell} =\frac{b_{\ell-1}}c. $$
Therefore the concatenation lemma applies whenever $c\ge\ell+1$.

4.1 One recurring type

Define
$$ c(R)= \begin{cases} 1,&R=1,\\ \min\{c\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}:R\le c^{c-1}\},&R>1. \end{cases} $$

For $R>1$, take $c=c(R)$. Since $D\le R$,
$$ \ell=\left\lceil\log_cD\right\rceil\le c-1. $$
Thus the lemma supplies a feasible $c(R)$-competitive policy:
$$ \boxed{\alpha^*(m,\rho,1)\le c(R).} $$

If $R=1$, then $D=1$. The single unscaled profile at $q_*=q_0$ obtains $F(q)$ at every cumulative mass $q$. Hence
$$ \boxed{\alpha^*(m,\rho,1)=1\quad\text{when }R=1.} $$

4.2 Arbitrary support size

Suppose $R>1$. Choose integers $k\ge1$ and $c$ satisfying
$$ c\ge\tau(k+1), \qquad R\le c^k. $$

When a type first appears, construct its profiles as above and serve its subsequent cohorts using its own concatenation schedule scaled by $1/c$. Its number of profiles is at most
$$ 1+\left\lceil\log_cD\right\rceil\le k+1. $$
Therefore it consumes at most $(k+1)/c$ of every item. Since at most $\tau$ actual types appear, total consumption at every prefix is at most
$$ \frac{\tau(k+1)}c\le1. $$

Let $Q_t$ be the final mass of type $t$, and let $F_t$ be its single-type offline curve. The lemma gives
$$ W_t\ge\frac{F_t(Q_t)}c. $$
The joint offline allocation restricted to type $t$ is feasible for the single-type problem with full capacities. Consequently,
$$ \operatorname{OPT}\le\sum_tF_t(Q_t). $$
Summing the policy’s welfare yields
$$ W\ge\frac1c\sum_tF_t(Q_t) \ge\frac{\operatorname{OPT}}c. $$

Minimizing over $k$ gives
$$ \boxed{\alpha^*(m,\rho,\tau)\le U_\tau(R).} $$

For $R=1$, every nonzero type has $D=1$. Give each type its single profile scaled by $1/\tau$. The same argument gives competitive ratio $\tau$.

Finally, for $R>1$, the choice
$$ k=c(R)-1,\qquad c=\tau c(R) $$
satisfies the required inequalities. Thus, including the case $R=1$,
$$ \boxed{U_\tau(R)\le\tau c(R).} $$

All these policies know only the prescribed parameters before the stream begins. They compute a type’s profiles when that type first appears and never use its future mass or the stopping time.

5. Exact rational implementation and running time

The upper-bound construction can use rational profiles and allocations throughout.

First compute $F_\infty$ by a linear program over $\mathcal P$. Then compute $q_*$ by minimizing total mass subject to attaining welfare $F_\infty$. Compute $q_0$ by maximizing mass using only value-$p$ bundles.

For an intermediate target average $a$ satisfying
$$ a_*<a<p, $$
solve
$$ \max\left\{ \sum_Sx_S: x\in\mathcal P,\ \sum_Sv(S)x_S\ge a\sum_Sx_S \right\}. $$
Let its optimum mass be $q$.

No feasible packing in this program can have mass at least $q_*$: such a packing would have average welfare at most $F_\infty/q_*=a_*<a$. Hence $q<q_*$.

An optimal packing for $F(q)$ has mass exactly $q$. If $F(q)>aq$, mixing that packing with the saturation packing by a sufficiently small positive amount would increase its mass while retaining average welfare at least $a$. This contradicts maximality of $q$. Therefore
$$ F(q)=aq, $$
and the program supplies the required optimal profile.

Here every target average is $p/c^i$, a rational number.

An elementary finite implementation is available by enumerating vertices. Each relevant polytope has nonnegativity constraints, $m$ item constraints, and at most one additional welfare constraint or equality. Every vertex has at most $m+1$ positive coordinates: otherwise those coordinates admit a nonzero perturbation preserving all active additional constraints, contradicting extremality.

Enumerate supports of size at most $m+1$ and full-rank selections of active constraints. Solve the resulting linear systems, discard infeasible candidates, and select the first optimal candidate in a fixed enumeration. Singular systems are discarded; every vertex has a representation using a full-rank selection.

There are at most
$$ 2^{O(m^2)} $$
candidate systems per program. For a rational system of dimension $s\le m+1$ with coefficient bit lengths at most $B$, clearing all denominators and applying Cramer’s rule bounds solution-coordinate bit lengths by
$$ O(s^3B+s\log s). $$
The program for $q_*$ uses the already computed $F_\infty$; all subsequent level programs use the original valuations and $p/c^i$. Thus these finitely many preprocessing steps produce profiles of polynomial bit length.

For $R>1$, put
$$ k_0=\left\lceil\log_2R\right\rceil. $$
Taking $k=k_0$ and $c=\tau(k_0+1)$ is feasible. Hence the minimization defining $U_\tau(R)$ need only consider $1\le k\le k_0$, and each type needs at most $k_0+1$ profiles.

During the stream, maintain each type’s cumulative input mass and its position in the precomputed schedule. A sum of input rationals has a common denominator whose bit length is at most the sum of their denominator bit lengths. Likewise, the finitely many precomputed batch endpoints have polynomial bit length. All allocation computations therefore retain polynomial bit length over the finite stream.

This gives an exact rational implementation with total running time bounded by
$$ \boxed{ 2^{O(m^2)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,N,L). } $$
No optimization program uses loads produced by a preceding optimization program.

6. A stronger one-type lower-bound family

The following construction improves the dependence obtained from the square-root valuation family.

Proposition. For every integer $k\ge2$, if
$$ m\ge(2k)^k, \qquad \rho\ge k^k, $$
then, for every $\tau\ge1$,
$$ \boxed{\alpha^*(m,\rho,\tau)\ge k/4.} $$

The proof applies to randomized causal policies against fixed streams.

6.1 Valuation and stream

Set
$$ B=2k,\qquad A=k,\qquad M=B^k, \qquad a=\frac{\ln A}{\ln B}\in(0,1). $$
Use $M$ items and a single type with valuation
$$ v(S)= \begin{cases} A^\ell,&|S|=B^\ell \text{ for some }\ell\in\{0,\ldots,k\},\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
On every positive-valued bundle,
$$ v(S)=|S|^a. $$
All tabulated values are integers in $[1,A^k]$.

Give this type cohorts of masses
$$ q_0=1,\qquad q_i=B^i-B^{i-1}\quad(1\le i\le k). $$
The cumulative mass through cohort $i$ is
$$ Q_i=B^i. $$

6.2 Exact offline prefix optima

For any positive-valued allocation, let $P$ be its allocated mass and $R'$ its total item usage. Concavity of $s^a$, applied with weights proportional to the allocated masses, gives
$$ W=\sum_S|S|^ax_S \le P^{1-a}(R')^a. $$
At cumulative available mass $Q$, we have $P\le Q$ and $R'\le M$. Hence
$$ W\le Q^{1-a}M^a. $$

At prefix $i$, allocate mass $Q_i$ uniformly over bundles of size $B^{k-i}$. Every item’s load is
$$ \frac{Q_iB^{k-i}}M=1. $$
The welfare is
$$ V_i=Q_iA^{k-i} =A^k(B/A)^i =A^k2^i =M^aQ_i^{1-a}. $$
This aggregate allocation can be split proportionally among the available cohorts. Therefore
$$ \boxed{\operatorname{OPT}_i=V_i.} $$

6.3 Expected resource inequality

Let $W_i$ be the policy’s random welfare in cohort $i$, and let $R_i$ count item usage only in its positive-valued allocations. The preceding concavity inequality within that cohort gives
$$ W_i\le q_i^{1-a}R_i^a. $$
Let
$$ Z_i=\mathbb E[W_i], \qquad p'=\frac1a>1. $$
Since $r^a$ is concave and $0\le R_i\le M$, Jensen’s inequality gives
$$ Z_i \le q_i^{1-a}\bigl(\mathbb E[R_i]\bigr)^a. $$
Consequently,
$$ \frac{Z_i^{p'}}{q_i^{p'-1}}\le\mathbb E[R_i]. $$
Summing and using the hard capacities,
$$ \boxed{ \sum_{i=0}^k\frac{Z_i^{p'}}{q_i^{p'-1}}\le M. } $$

6.4 Prefix requirements and their minimum resource cost

Suppose the policy is $\alpha$-competitive. Causality implies that its allocations on a prefix have the same distribution whether or not later cohorts appear. Applying competitiveness to every stopped prefix gives
$$ \sum_{h=0}^iZ_h\ge\frac{V_i}{\alpha}. $$

Define
$$ d_0=\frac{V_0}{\alpha}, \qquad d_i=\frac{V_i-V_{i-1}}{\alpha} =\frac{V_i}{2\alpha}\quad(i\ge1). $$
Then
$$ H_i=\sum_{h=0}^i(Z_h-d_h)\ge0. $$

We claim
$$ \sum_i\frac{Z_i^{p'}}{q_i^{p'-1}} \ge \sum_i\frac{d_i^{p'}}{q_i^{p'-1}}. $$

To prove this, put
$$ \lambda_i=\left(\frac{d_i}{q_i}\right)^{p'-1}. $$
The ratios are
$$ \frac{d_0}{q_0}=\frac{A^k}{\alpha}, \qquad \frac{d_i}{q_i} = \frac{A^{k-i}}{2\alpha(1-1/B)} \quad(i\ge1). $$
They decrease: the first ratio drops by a factor $B-1$, and subsequent ratios drop by a factor $A$. Thus $\lambda_i$ is nonnegative and nonincreasing.

Summation by parts gives
$$ \sum_{i=0}^k\lambda_i(Z_i-d_i) = \lambda_kH_k+ \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(\lambda_i-\lambda_{i+1})H_i \ge0. $$
Convexity of $z^{p'}/q_i^{p'-1}$ gives the tangent inequality
$$ \frac{Z_i^{p'}}{q_i^{p'-1}} \ge \frac{d_i^{p'}}{q_i^{p'-1}} + p'\lambda_i(Z_i-d_i). $$
Summing proves the claim.

6.5 Evaluating the lower bound

Since
$$ V_i=M^aQ_i^{1-a}, $$
we have
$$ \frac{V_i^{p'}}{Q_i^{p'-1}}=M. $$
Therefore
$$ \frac{d_0^{p'}}{q_0^{p'-1}}=\frac M{\alpha^{p'}} $$
and, for every $i\ge1$,
$$ \frac{d_i^{p'}}{q_i^{p'-1}} = \frac M{\alpha^{p'}} \frac{2^{-p'}}{(1-1/B)^{p'-1}}. $$
Combining the resource inequality and the preceding minimization yields
$$ \alpha \ge \left( 1+ k\frac{2^{-p'}}{(1-1/B)^{p'-1}} \right)^a \ge \frac{k^a}{2}. $$
Finally,
$$ k^{1-a} = \exp\left( \frac{\ln2\,\ln k}{\ln(2k)} \right) \le2. $$
Hence $k^a\ge k/2$, giving
$$ \boxed{\alpha\ge k/4.} $$

For larger $m$, give value zero to every bundle involving an unused item. For the original valuation scale, multiply the table by $v_{\min}$. The assumptions $m\ge B^k$ and $\rho\ge A^k$ make the resulting instance admissible. It uses exactly one actual type. $\square$

7. Matching asymptotic bounds for one type

Here are explicit comparisons establishing the announced constants.

7.1 Upper comparison

Write $T=\ln R$. For $T\ge1$, set
$$ d=\left\lceil\frac{4T}{\ln(T+2)}\right\rceil. $$
For $u\ge1$, the inequality $\ln u\le\sqrt u$ follows by minimizing $\sqrt u-\ln u$, whose minimum occurs at $u=4$ and is positive.

Consequently,
$$ d\ge\frac{4T}{\sqrt{T+2}}\ge\sqrt{T+2}, $$
where the last inequality uses $T\ge1$. Thus
$$ \ln d\ge\frac12\ln(T+2). $$
Also $d\ge2$, so
$$ (d-1)\ln d \ge \frac d4\ln(T+2) \ge T. $$
Therefore $R\le d^{d-1}$ and
$$ c(R)\le d \le1+\frac{4T}{\ln(T+2)} \le4h(R). $$

For $0<T<1$, $R<e<9=3^2$, so $c(R)\le3\le4h(R)$. At $T=0$, $c(R)=1$. Hence
$$ \boxed{c(R)\le4h(R).} $$

7.2 Lower comparison

Suppose $T\ge64$ and choose
$$ k=\left\lfloor\frac{T}{2\ln(T+2)}\right\rfloor. $$
The inequality
$$ \ln(T+2)\le\sqrt{T+2}\le T/4 $$
ensures that the expression inside the floor is at least two. Thus
$$ k\ge\frac{T}{4\ln(T+2)}. $$
Also $2k\le T$, so
$$ k\ln(2k) \le \frac{T}{2\ln(T+2)}\ln(T+2) =\frac T2. $$
It follows that
$$ (2k)^k\le e^{T/2}\le R. $$
Since $m\ge R$ and $\rho\ge R$, the lower-bound proposition applies:
$$ \alpha^*(m,\rho,1) \ge\frac k4 \ge\frac{T}{16\ln(T+2)}. $$
The same inequalities imply $T/\ln(T+2)\ge4$, so
$$ h(R)\le\frac{2T}{\ln(T+2)}. $$
Therefore the lower bound is at least $h(R)/32$.

For $0\le T<64$, use $\alpha^*\ge1$. The elementary bound $\ln2\ge2/3$, obtained by integrating the tangent lower bound for the convex function $1/x$ on $[1,2]$, gives
$$ h(R)\le1+\frac{64}{\ln2}\le97. $$
Thus $\alpha^*\ge h(R)/100$ in this range as well.

Together with the upper comparison,
$$ \boxed{ \frac{h(R)}{100} \le \alpha^*(m,\rho,1) \le 4h(R). } $$

8. What remains unresolved

The single-type dependence on $m$ and $\rho$ is now established up to universal constant factors. Its exact value is not determined, except when $R=1$.
