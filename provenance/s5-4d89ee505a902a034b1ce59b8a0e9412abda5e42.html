The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given ranking types \(T\), binary-rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\in[0,1]\) with \(\sum_{j=1}^m q_j=1\), compute exactly the type-level probabilistic-serial allocation \(x_{tj}\). Each type’s mass eats its most-preferred category with positive residual supply at unit speed, so category \(j\) is consumed at rate \(\sum_{t:\,j\text{ is chosen by }t}\mu_t\); determine the running time in \(m\), \(\tau=|T|\), and input bit length \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\geq1\) categories be ranked by strict complete orders. A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of its \(\tau\) distinct ranking types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), encoded as binary rationals, with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
Category supplies are binary rationals \(q_j\in[0,1]\) satisfying \(\sum_{j=1}^m q_j=1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including rankings, masses, and supplies.
Starting at time zero, every individual consumes at unit speed its highest-ranked category with positive residual supply; consequently, a type's aggregate consumption rate is its mass, and category \(j\)'s rate is the sum of masses currently choosing it. Zero-supply categories are unavailable initially; all categories exhausted simultaneously are removed together. Consumption continues until all supplies are exhausted.
Define \(x_{tj}\) as the amount of category \(j\) consumed per individual of type \(t\), rather than its aggregate type consumption. Thus \(\sum_jx_{tj}=1\) and \(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}=q_j\). The computational problem is to compute every \(x_{tj}\) exactly as a binary rational.

Is this exact allocation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite agents and unit-supply objects, multi-unit-eating probabilistic serial is stated to run in linear time; its single-unit specialization is ordinary probabilistic serial ([Aziz](https://arxiv.org/html/1401.7700v3)).
Probabilistic serial has also been defined for continuum economies, with convergence from finite replicas established ([Che and Kojima](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/d1677.pdf)); this does not establish a bit-complexity bound for the support-encoded problem above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Ranking types are strict complete orders, following the discrete preference domain.
  • The undefined allocation entries are per-individual amounts, so aggregate type allocations are obtained by multiplying by type mass.
  • Zero-supply categories are unavailable initially, and simultaneous exhaustion removes all affected categories together.
  • The input length includes rankings and binary encodings of all rational numbers; exact allocation entries use binary-rational encoding.

Result

The exact allocation problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

The proof requires both an exact event simulation and a polynomial bound on the bit lengths of its intermediate numbers. We establish these directly. Rationals are represented by binary-encoded integer numerators and positive integer denominators.

1. Exact simulation of exhaustion events

Let \(r_j\) denote category \(j\)’s residual supply, and let \(y_{tj}\) denote the amount consumed so far per individual of type \(t\). Initially, \(r_j=q_j\) and \(y_{tj}=0\). Categories with zero residual supply are unavailable.

Whenever some supply remains, define \(c_t\) to be type \(t\)’s highest-ranked available category. Strict complete rankings make this choice unique. Define the aggregate rate

\(a_j=\sum_{t:c_t=j}\mu_t.\)

Every type chooses exactly one category, so \(\sum_j a_j=1\). In particular, at least one category has positive rate.

The time until the next exhaustion is

\(\displaystyle \delta=\min_{j:a_j>0}\frac{r_j}{a_j}.\)

All ratios in this minimum are positive. Until time \(\delta\), no available category disappears, so choices and rates remain constant. Consequently, the exact updates are

\(r'_j=r_j-a_j\delta,\)

\(y'_{tj}=y_{tj}+\delta\,\mathbf{1}_{\{c_t=j\}}.\)

Remove every category with \(r'_j=0\), simultaneously, and repeat. At least one category disappears at each event, so there are at most \(m\) events.

These updates reproduce the specified process: they use its constant rates until the first exhaustion and then remove precisely all categories exhausted at that time.

Termination time and feasibility

Let \(s\) denote elapsed time. Initially, \(\sum_jr_j=1-s=1\). At each event,

\(\displaystyle \sum_jr'_j=\sum_jr_j-\delta\sum_ja_j=\sum_jr_j-\delta.\)

Thus \(\sum_jr_j=1-s\) remains invariant. Residual supplies stay nonnegative by the definition of \(\delta\), so \(s\leq1\). When the simulation terminates, every residual supply is zero; hence \(s=1\).

Each type consumes at unit speed throughout every event interval. Therefore its final allocation satisfies \(\sum_jy_{tj}=1\). For each category, its residual decrement equals the mass-weighted allocation increment:

\(\displaystyle a_j\delta=\sum_t\mu_t\delta\,\mathbf{1}_{\{c_t=j\}}.\)

Summing over events gives \(\sum_t\mu_ty_{tj}=q_j\). Therefore the requested output is \(x_{tj}=y_{tj}\).

In particular, throughout the simulation, \(0\leq r_j\leq1\) and \(0\leq y_{tj}\leq1\).

2. Integer implementation and denominator bound

Write the input rationals as \(\mu_t=p_t/d_t\) and \(q_j=b_j/e_j\), with positive denominators. Set

\(\displaystyle D=\prod_{t\in T}d_t,\qquad E=\prod_{j=1}^{m}e_j,\qquad w_t=D\mu_t.\)

Each \(w_t\) is a positive integer, and \(\sum_tw_t=D\). Because all input denominator bits count toward \(L\),

\(\log_2D\leq L,\qquad \log_2E\leq L.\)

We maintain a common positive denominator \(P\), with integer numerators \(R_j\) and \(Y_{tj}\), such that

\(r_j=R_j/P,\qquad y_{tj}=Y_{tj}/P.\)

Initially, take \(P=E\), \(R_j=Eq_j\), and \(Y_{tj}=0\).

At an event, compute the integer rate weights

\(\displaystyle W_j=\sum_{t:c_t=j}w_t.\)

Then \(a_j=W_j/D\), and \(0\leq W_j\leq D\). Choose an index \(h\) minimizing \(R_j/W_j\) over categories with \(W_j>0\). These comparisons use integer cross-products: for positive \(W_i,W_j\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{R_i}{W_i}\leq\frac{R_j}{W_j}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_iW_j\leq R_jW_i.\)

The event duration is

\(\displaystyle \delta=\frac{DR_h}{PW_h}.\)

Using the old values on the right-hand sides, update

\(P'=PW_h,\)

\(R'_j=R_jW_h-W_jR_h,\)

\(Y'_{tj}=Y_{tj}W_h+DR_h\,\mathbf{1}_{\{c_t=j\}}.\)

These are integer computations. Dividing by \(P'\) verifies both update rules:

\(\displaystyle \frac{R'_j}{P'}=\frac{R_j}{P}-\frac{W_j}{D}\frac{DR_h}{PW_h}=r_j-a_j\delta,\)

\(\displaystyle \frac{Y'_{tj}}{P'}=\frac{Y_{tj}}{P}+\frac{DR_h}{PW_h}\mathbf{1}_{\{c_t=j\}}=y_{tj}+\delta\,\mathbf{1}_{\{c_t=j\}}.\)

Nonnegativity follows from the minimizing choice of \(h\). Simultaneous exhaustion is detected exactly by \(R'_j=0\).

Bit-length bound

Let \(P_k\) be the maintained denominator after \(k\) events. Each event multiplies it by an integer between \(1\) and \(D\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle P_k\leq ED^k\leq ED^m,\)

and hence

\(\displaystyle \log_2P_k\leq\log_2E+m\log_2D\leq(m+1)L.\)

Since residual supplies and accumulated allocations lie in \([0,1]\),

\(0\leq R_j\leq P_k,\qquad 0\leq Y_{tj}\leq P_k.\)

Thus all maintained integers have \(O((m+1)L)\) bits. Intermediate products multiply these integers by numbers at most \(D\); intermediate sums add at most two such products. Their bit lengths are also \(O((m+1)L)\).

This proves the necessary bit-complexity bound, without assuming that rational arithmetic has unit cost.

3. Running time and exact output

A straightforward implementation finds every \(c_t\) by scanning its ranking, using \(O(\tau m)\) operations per event. Computing rates, selecting the next exhaustion, updating residual supplies, and updating all \(\tau m\) allocation numerators also require \(O(\tau m)\) integer operations per event.

There are at most \(m\) events. Consequently, the simulation uses \(O(\tau m^2)\) integer operations, each on \(O((m+1)L)\)-bit integers. Integer addition, multiplication, comparison, and the exact divisions needed during initialization all have polynomial bit complexity. Thus the total running time is polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

At termination, output \(x_{tj}=Y_{tj}/P\). If reduced fractions are required, divide each numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor; this also takes polynomial time. The total output length is \(O(\tau m(m+1)L)\).

The proof covers zero supplies, categories temporarily receiving no consumption, simultaneous exhaustions, and \(m=1\).

4. Relation to the cited results

Aziz’s Theorem 4.1 states a linear-time guarantee for multi-unit-eating probabilistic serial. [Aziz](https://arxiv.org/html/1401.7700v3). Che and Kojima’s Theorem 1 establishes convergence to continuum probabilistic serial, including equality along exact replicas. [Che and Kojima](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/d1677.pdf).

For the support-encoded rational input here, the integer implementation and denominator bound above establish polynomial bit complexity directly. The exact allocation function therefore belongs to \(\mathrm{FP}\).
