The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational finite-support initial opinion distribution \(\mu_0\) on \(\mathbb{Q}^d\) and rational \(\varepsilon>\delta>0\), evolve \(\mu_t\) by \(\dot{\mu}_t=(F_{\mu_t})_\#\mu_t-\mu_t\), where \(F_\mu(x)\) is the mean opinion within distance \(\varepsilon\) of \(x\). Determine or approximate the first time \(T_\delta\) when every \(x,y\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_t)\) with \(\|x-y\|_2\le\varepsilon\) also satisfies \(\|x-y\|_2\le\delta\), including whether \(T_\delta\) is infinite.

Here \(\tau\) is the initial support size, not the number of possible types. All opinions interact through a complete social network. Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Both decision problems are in \(\mathrm P\). More precisely, for every evolution satisfying the stated weak equation,

\[ T_\delta= \begin{cases} 0,&\text{if no initial pair has }\delta<\|a_i-a_j\|_2\le\varepsilon,\\ \infty,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

The reason is specific to the measure jump equation: every initial atom retains positive mass at every finite time. This resolves the stated support-based hitting-time question, even though later measures may have infinite support. The proof does not require uniqueness of the evolution.

Persistence of initial atoms

Fix a bounded continuous test function \(\varphi\), and write

\[ u_\varphi(t)=\int\varphi\,d\mu_t, \qquad v_\varphi(t)=\int\varphi(F_{\mu_t}(x))\,d\mu_t(x). \]

The stipulated weak equation gives

\[ u_\varphi(t)=u_\varphi(0)+\int_0^t\bigl(v_\varphi(s)-u_\varphi(s)\bigr)\,ds. \]

Because both measures involved are probability measures, the integrand has absolute value at most \(2\|\varphi\|_\infty\). Consequently, \(u_\varphi\) is absolutely continuous and satisfies \(u_\varphi'=v_\varphi-u_\varphi\) almost everywhere. Multiplying by \(e^t\) and integrating proves

\[ u_\varphi(t)=e^{-t}u_\varphi(0)+\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)}v_\varphi(s)\,ds. \]

For nonnegative \(\varphi\), the second term is nonnegative, so

\[ \int\varphi\,d\mu_t\ge e^{-t}\int\varphi\,d\mu_0. \]

Now fix an initial opinion \(a_i\). For \(r>0\), take the bounded continuous function

\[ \varphi_r(x)=\max\left\{0,1-\frac{\|x-a_i\|_2}{r}\right\}. \]

Since \(\varphi_r(a_i)=1\), the preceding inequality implies \(\int\varphi_r\,d\mu_t\ge e^{-t}p_i\). As \(r\) decreases to zero, these functions converge pointwise to the indicator of \(\{a_i\}\) and are bounded by \(1\). Dominated convergence therefore yields

\[ \mu_t(\{a_i\})\ge e^{-t}p_i>0 \]

for every finite \(t\). In particular, every \(a_i\) belongs to the topological support of \(\mu_t\).

Determination of the hitting time

There are two exhaustive cases.

  • An initial violating pair exists. Suppose \(\delta<\|a_i-a_j\|_2\le\varepsilon\). Both points remain in the support at every finite time, and their locations do not change. They therefore violate the required condition at every finite time. The set defining \(T_\delta\) is empty, so \(T_\delta=\infty\).
  • No initial violating pair exists. Because all initial masses are positive, \(\operatorname{supp}\mu_0=\{a_1,\ldots,a_\tau\}\). The required condition holds at time zero, so \(T_\delta=0\), irrespective of subsequent behavior.

Thus, for every allowed \(h\ge0\), deciding \(T_\delta\le h\) amounts to checking that no initial violating pair exists. Deciding \(T_\delta=\infty\) amounts to checking that such a pair exists.

Exact algorithm and bit complexity

Compute, for every \(i<j\), the rational squared distance

\[ D_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(a_{ik}-a_{jk})^2. \]

A pair violates the criterion exactly when \(\delta^2<D_{ij}\le\varepsilon^2\). Squaring preserves these comparisons because distances and thresholds are nonnegative. Exact rational arithmetic handles both boundary equalities without approximation.

The algorithm uses \(O(m\tau^2)\) rational arithmetic operations. A conservative bit-complexity bound using schoolbook integer arithmetic is

\[ O\!\left(L+m\tau^2L^2\right). \]

To justify the operand lengths, write each input coordinate as an integer numerator over a positive integer denominator. For any fixed pair, an unreduced denominator for the squared-distance sum is the product of the squared coordinate denominators from that pair. Its bit length is \(O(L)\), since those denominator lengths are part of the total input length. The corresponding numerators and partial sums also have \(O(L)\) bits; summing \(m\) terms adds at most \(O(\log m)\) bits, which is absorbed by \(O(L)\). Each multiplication or comparison therefore costs \(O(L^2)\).

The threshold encodings are included in \(L\). There is no additional dependence on the numerical values of \(h\), \(\varepsilon/\delta\), or the masses. Both problems are polynomial-time solvable for variable \(m\) and \(\tau\).

Why convergence does not remove the obstruction

For a concrete instance, take \(m=1\), \(\mu_0=\frac12\delta_0+\frac12\delta_1\), \(\varepsilon=1\), and \(\delta=\frac12\). Then

\[ \mu_t=\frac{e^{-t}}2\delta_0+\frac{e^{-t}}2\delta_1+(1-e^{-t})\delta_{1/2} \]

satisfies the equation: all support points are within confidence distance, their mean is always \(\frac12\), and differentiation gives the required weak identity.

Although these measures converge weakly to consensus, both endpoints remain in the support at every finite time. Hence \(T_\delta=\infty\).

The cited [asynchronous finite-agent result](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00446-024-00467-3) concerns an expected update count. The polynomial-time classification here follows directly from persistence of atoms in the stipulated measure equation.
