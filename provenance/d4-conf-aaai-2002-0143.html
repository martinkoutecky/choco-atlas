The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) resource types, per-capita supplies \(b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^m\), and an atomless population with a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over reported types \((q,v)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^m\times\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), compute a truthful mechanism assigning each individual either their whole requested bundle or nothing, subject to \(\sum_t q_i^t x_t\le b_i\) for every resource \(i\), that maximizes reported welfare \(\sum_t v^t x_t\). Here \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) is the mass served from type \(t\), demand vectors are known or verifiable, and truthfulness is evaluated against an individual’s unilateral value-report deviation.

Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total binary input length.
Each individual has a known or verifiable demand vector and a privately known value. Service supplies the whole requested bundle; otherwise the individual receives nothing. Utilities are quasi-linear, and unserved individuals pay zero; require nonnegative payments and individual rationality at truthful reports.

A deterministic mechanism specifies finitely described measurable allocation and payment rules, including their response to every unilateral nonnegative rational value report, even one outside the support. Result

Yes. The model admits a deterministic truthful mechanism computable in polynomial time that achieves \(\mathrm{OPT}\) exactly. Consequently, it satisfies the requested \((m+1)\)-approximation.

The construction uses optimal LP resource prices and public identities to ration individuals whose reported values equal their bundle prices. Truthfulness relies on the stipulated zero mass of every individual and the permission of utility ties.

1. Optimal allocations and supporting prices

Consider the primal linear program

\(\displaystyle \max \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}v^t x_t,\qquad \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}q_i^t x_t\le b_i\ \ (i=1,\ldots,m),\qquad 0\le x_t\le\mu_t\ \ (t=1,\ldots,\tau).\)

Its optimum is precisely \(\mathrm{OPT}\). The feasible region is nonempty because it contains the zero vector, and compact because every variable lies in a bounded interval.

Introducing dual variables \(p_i\ge0\) for resource constraints and \(z_t\ge0\) for upper bounds gives the dual

\(\displaystyle \min \sum_{i=1}^{m}b_i p_i+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t z_t,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{m}q_i^t p_i+z_t\ge v^t\ \ (t=1,\ldots,\tau).\)

The dual is feasible: take \(p_i=0\) and \(z_t=v^t\). Strong LP duality therefore supplies optimal primal and dual solutions \(x^*\) and \((p^*,z^*)\) with equal objective values.

Define the bundle price

\(\displaystyle c_t=\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_i^t p_i^*.\)

These prices satisfy two crucial implications:

  • If \(v^t>c_t\), then \(x_t^*=\mu_t\).
  • If \(v^t<c_t\), then \(x_t^*=0\).

For completeness, both follow directly from the duality gap identity:

\(\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_i^*\left(b_i-\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}q_i^t x_t^*\right)+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}z_t^*(\mu_t-x_t^*)+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}x_t^*(c_t+z_t^*-v^t).\)

Every summand is nonnegative by primal and dual feasibility, so every summand vanishes.

If \(v^t>c_t\), dual feasibility gives \(z_t^*\ge v^t-c_t>0\). Hence \(z_t^*(\mu_t-x_t^*)=0\) forces \(x_t^*=\mu_t\).

If \(v^t<c_t\), then \(c_t+z_t^*-v^t>0\). Hence \(x_t^*(c_t+z_t^*-v^t)=0\) forces \(x_t^*=0\).

Thus partial service can occur only at equality \(v^t=c_t\).

2. The mechanism, including off-support reports

Represent the interval for type \(t\) as \([0,\mu_t)\), with local identity coordinate \(s\). The pair \((t,s)\) is immutable: changing a report does not move the individual into another interval.

Compute \(x^*\) and \(p^*\) from the positive-mass reported profile. Use a fixed deterministic LP algorithm and fixed input ordering. Reports made by individual identities as null exceptions do not enter this aggregate computation.

For the individual with identity \((t,s)\), whose fixed demand is \(q^t\), and every report \(r\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), define service by

\(\displaystyle a_t(s,r)= \begin{cases} 1,&r>c_t,\\ 1,&r=c_t\text{ and }s<x_t^*,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases}\)

Define the payment by

\(\displaystyle P_t(s,r)=c_t\,a_t(s,r).\)

A served individual receives the whole bundle \(q^t\). An unserved individual receives nothing and pays zero. Since \(p^*\ge0\) and \(q^t\ge0\), every payment is nonnegative.

The rule is defined even when \(r\) does not occur in the support. In particular, an off-support report is evaluated using the same identity, demand, bundle price, and identity cutoff.

The allocation and payment functions are measurable: their definitions use rational value thresholds and interval cutoffs.

3. Feasibility and exact welfare

At truthful reports, each interval has served mass exactly \(x_t^*\):

  • If \(v^t>c_t\), every individual is served, and the first implication above gives \(x_t^*=\mu_t\).
  • If \(v^t<c_t\), nobody is served, and the second implication gives \(x_t^*=0\).
  • If \(v^t=c_t\), precisely the prefix \([0,x_t^*)\) is served, of mass \(x_t^*\).

Consequently, resource consumption is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}q_i^t x_t^*\le b_i\)

for every resource \(i\), and welfare is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}v^t x_t^*=\mathrm{OPT}.\)

A unilateral report changes outcomes at at most one identity. A singleton has zero mass, so such a change leaves every aggregate resource integral unchanged. Feasibility therefore also holds after every unilateral deviation, including an off-support deviation.

4. Dominant-strategy truthfulness

Fix an individual and fix the reports of all other individuals. The positive-mass reported profile is independent of this individual's report, because the individual has zero mass. Thus the computed LP solutions, bundle price, and identity cutoff are identical under every report by this individual.

Write \(c\) for the fixed bundle price and \(h\in\{0,1\}\) for the fixed decision at equality. The individual faces the following menu:

  • Reports above \(c\) obtain service and pay \(c\).
  • Reports below \(c\) obtain no service and pay zero.
  • Reporting \(c\) obtains service exactly when \(h=1\), with payment \(c\).

Let \(w\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) be the individual's true value. Their utility from reporting \(r\) is

\(\displaystyle U_w(r)=(w-c)a(r).\)

There are three cases.

If \(w>c\): truthful reporting obtains service and utility \(w-c>0\). Every other report gives either that same utility or zero.

If \(w<c\): truthful reporting obtains no service and utility zero. Every report obtaining service gives utility \(w-c<0\); every other report gives zero.

If \(w=c\): every report gives utility zero, regardless of service.

Therefore

\(\displaystyle U_w(w)\ge U_w(r)\qquad\text{for every }r\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}.\)

This argument fixes arbitrary other reports in the model; it does not require them to be truthful. Truthful reporting is consequently a dominant strategy.

Truthful individual rationality also follows: truthful service occurs only when \(w\ge c\), and otherwise the individual pays zero.

5. Polynomial-time construction and evaluation

The standard rational-LP theorem provides deterministic algorithms that return exact optimal rational solutions, with polynomial encoding length, in time polynomial in the input encoding length. Exact optimization includes lower-dimensional feasible regions. See the treatment of polynomial solvability in [Nemirovski’s optimization notes, Section 8.4](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~nemirovs/OPTIII_LectureNotes2020.pdf).

Here the primal has \(\tau\) variables and \(m+2\tau\) inequalities. The dual has \(m+\tau\) variables and a polynomial-size rational description. The feasibility and bounded-optimum hypotheses were checked in Section 1. Thus both optimal solutions can be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Store, for each immutable interval label \(t\), the rational cutoff \(x_t^*\) and rational bundle price \(c_t\). This table has polynomial encoding length.

For a finitely encoded rational identity coordinate \(s\) and rational report \(r\), evaluation requires only comparisons of \(r\) with \(c_t\), and, at equality, of \(s\) with \(x_t^*\). Exact rational comparisons take polynomial time in the table size and query bit length. The interval formulas themselves define the rule for every real identity coordinate.

6. Why identity rationing is truthful here

The essential case is an individual rejected at an equality \(v^t=c_t\). Overreporting makes that individual served, but the payment remains \(c_t=v^t\), so utility remains zero.

For example, take one resource, demand \(q^1=1\), value \(v^1=1\), mass \(\mu_1=1\), and supply \(b_1=1/2\). An optimal solution has \(x_1^*=1/2\) and price \(c_1=1\). Half the interval receives service and pays one. A rejected individual can overreport and receive service, but gains no utility; the additional singleton service consumes zero aggregate supply.

Hence the construction satisfies the specified weak dominant-strategy truthfulness while attaining exact optimal welfare.
