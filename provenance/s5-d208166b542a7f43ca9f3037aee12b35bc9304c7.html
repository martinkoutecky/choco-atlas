The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(C\), \(k\), and a support \(T\) of \(\tau\) trichotomous ballot types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), find \(W\subseteq C\), \(|W|=k\), such that for every \(\ell\in[k]\) and \(J\subseteq T\), if \(\sum_{t\in J}\mu_t\ge\ell/k\) and \(\left|\bigcap_{t\in J}A_t^+\right|\ge\ell\), then \(\left|W\cap\bigcup_{t\in J}A_t^+\right|\ge\ell\); determine whether this can be done in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set of size \(m\), and let \(k\) be an integer with \(1\le k\le m\). A trichotomous type \(t\) partitions \(C\) into approval, indifference, and disapproval sets \(A_t^+,A_t^0,A_t^-\); any of these sets may be empty.
A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of its \(\tau\) distinct types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types, and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Find a committee \(W\subseteq C\), \(|W|=k\), such that for every \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and nonempty \(J\subseteq T\),
\(\sum_{t\in J}\mu_t\ge\ell/k\ \land\ |\bigcap_{t\in J}A_t^+|\ge\ell\ \implies\ |W\cap\bigcup_{t\in J}A_t^+|\ge\ell\).
Any committee satisfying this condition is an admissible output.

Is this committee search problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For explicitly listed, equally weighted voters, such a committee always exists and is polynomial-time computable ([Talmon and Page](https://preflib.github.io/gaiw2021/papers/GAIW_2021_paper_3.pdf)).
For positive real voter strengths, the identical condition is weighted PJR; efficient algorithms satisfying it are already known under assumptions excluding isolated approval-graph vertices and requiring \(k<m\), but the cited analysis does not explicitly state a binary-rational bit-complexity bound ([Cevallos and Stewart](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.12990)).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified the feasible committee-size domain as integers from one through the number of candidates.
  • Specified trichotomous types as partitions of the candidate set, allowing empty approval, indifference, or disapproval classes.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length.
  • Restricted quantified groups to nonempty subsets; the positive mass threshold already excludes the empty group.
  • Specified that any satisfying committee is acceptable, so no tie-breaking rule is required.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The search problem is solvable in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), even with empty approval sets, unsupported candidates, and \(k=m\).

We use weighted sequential Phragmén’s load rule, stated as Algorithm 8 in [Cevallos and Stewart](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.12990). Below, we prove its correctness directly and give an exact binary-rational implementation with polynomial bit complexity.

Only approval sets enter the required condition. Write \(A_t=A_t^+\).

1. Boundary cases and algorithm

Let \(C^+=\bigcup_{t\in T}A_t\).

If \(|C^+|\le k\), output any committee \(W\) containing \(C^+\) and having size \(k\). This satisfies the condition: whenever \(\left|\bigcap_{t\in J}A_t\right|\ge\ell\), we have

\[ \left|W\cap\bigcup_{t\in J}A_t\right| =\left|\bigcup_{t\in J}A_t\right| \ge\ell. \]

In particular, this handles \(k=m\).

Henceforth assume \(|C^+|>k\). Keep all types, including those with empty approval sets, and retain their original masses.

Initialize \(W=\varnothing\) and a load \(\lambda_t=0\) for each type \(t\). Perform \(k\) rounds. In each round:

  • For each \(c\in C^+\setminus W\), compute

\[ h(c)= \frac{1+\sum_{t:\,c\in A_t}\mu_t\lambda_t} {\sum_{t:\,c\in A_t}\mu_t}. \]

  • Choose a candidate \(c_j\) minimizing \(h(c)\), breaking ties arbitrarily, and put \(T_j=h(c_j)\).
  • Add \(c_j\) to \(W\), and set \(\lambda_t=T_j\) for every type approving \(c_j\). Leave all other loads unchanged.

Every denominator is positive because \(c\in C^+\) and all masses are positive. Since \(|C^+|>k\), every round has an available candidate.

2. Load invariants

Set \(T_0=0\). We prove that after round \(j\):

  • \(0\le\lambda_t\le T_j\) for every type;
  • loads never decrease;
  • every remaining candidate has score at least \(T_j\);
  • \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\lambda_t=j\).

For the first three assertions, use induction. Initially all loads are zero and all candidate scores are positive. Suppose the assertions hold after round \(j-1\).

The minimum score \(T_j\) is at least \(T_{j-1}\). Therefore every updated load increases or stays unchanged, and afterward every load is at most \(T_j\).

Before the update, every remaining candidate’s score was at least the minimum \(T_j\). Increasing loads cannot decrease any candidate score, because its numerator is a sum with nonnegative coefficients and its denominator is fixed. Consequently, every candidate remaining after this round still has score at least \(T_j\).

To prove the fourth assertion, let \(N_j=\{t:c_j\in A_t\}\), and denote the loads before this round by \(\lambda_t^{\mathrm{old}}\). The score equation gives

\[ T_j\sum_{t\in N_j}\mu_t -\sum_{t\in N_j}\mu_t\lambda_t^{\mathrm{old}}=1. \]

Thus the total weighted load increases by exactly one in each round. Starting from zero proves \(\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_t=j\).

In particular, at termination,

\[ \sum_t\mu_t\lambda_t=k, \qquad 0\le\lambda_t\le T_k. \]

Because \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), these imply \(T_k\ge k\).

3. Proof that the committee satisfies the required condition

Suppose, for contradiction, that the output violates the condition. Then there exist a nonempty \(J\subseteq T\) and an integer \(1\le\ell\le k\) such that, writing

\[ s=\sum_{t\in J}\mu_t, \qquad U_J=\bigcup_{t\in J}A_t, \qquad r=|W\cap U_J|, \]

we have \(s\ge\ell/k\), \(\left|\bigcap_{t\in J}A_t\right|\ge\ell\), and \(r\le\ell-1\).

Define the final weighted load of this group by

\[ H_J=\sum_{t\in J}\mu_t\lambda_t. \]

We first show \(H_J\le r\). A round whose selected candidate lies outside \(U_J\) changes no load in \(J\). In any other round, the increase in \(H_J\) is at most one: all load increments are nonnegative, and their total weighted sum over all types is exactly one. There are exactly \(r\) rounds selecting a member of \(U_J\). Hence \(H_J\le r\).

Next, there is an unelected candidate \(c\in\bigcap_{t\in J}A_t\). Otherwise, the committee would contain at least \(\ell\) candidates from \(U_J\), contradicting \(r<\ell\).

Let \(N_c=\{t:c\in A_t\}\). Since \(c\) remains unelected, its final score is at least \(T_k\). Therefore

\[ \sum_{t\in N_c}\mu_t(T_k-\lambda_t)\le1. \]

Every summand is nonnegative, and \(J\subseteq N_c\). It follows that

\[ sT_k-H_J =\sum_{t\in J}\mu_t(T_k-\lambda_t) \le1. \]

Combining the inequalities gives

\[ sT_k\le H_J+1\le r+1\le\ell. \]

Since \(s\ge\ell/k\), we obtain \(T_k\le k\). Section 2 gives the reverse inequality, so \(T_k=k\).

Now \(\lambda_t\le k\) for every type, while \(\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_t=k\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Since every mass is strictly positive, this forces \(\lambda_t=k\) for every type: any strictly smaller load would make the weighted average strictly smaller than \(k\).

Consequently,

\[ H_J=ks\ge\ell, \]

contradicting \(H_J\le r\le\ell-1\). Thus the output satisfies every required implication, including cases where the mass threshold holds with equality.

4. Exact binary-rational complexity

It remains to show polynomial running time in the binary input length, without expanding masses into individual voters.

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=p_t/q_t\), where \(p_t,q_t\) are positive integers. Set

\[ D=\prod_{t\in T}q_t, \qquad a_t=p_t(D/q_t). \]

Then \(a_t\) is a positive integer, \(\mu_t=a_t/D\), and \(\sum_t a_t=D\). The binary length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\), since \(\log_2D=\sum_t\log_2q_t\).

For each supported candidate, precompute

\[ S_c=\sum_{t:\,c\in A_t}a_t. \]

Thus \(1\le S_c\le D\).

Maintain all loads with a common denominator: \(\lambda_t=X_t/B\), where \(X_t\) is a nonnegative integer and \(B\) is a positive integer. Initially \(X_t=0\) and \(B=1\).

A candidate’s score is exactly

\[ h(c)=\frac{R_c}{BS_c}, \qquad R_c=DB+\sum_{t:\,c\in A_t}a_tX_t. \]

Because \(B\) is common to all candidates, compare their scores by cross multiplication: compare \(R_cS_d\) with \(R_dS_c\).

If \(c_j\) is selected, update

\[ B'=BS_{c_j}, \qquad X_t'= \begin{cases} R_{c_j},&c_j\in A_t,\\ X_tS_{c_j},&c_j\notin A_t. \end{cases} \]

These formulas represent exactly the loads specified by the algorithm. No fraction reduction is needed.

We now bound every integer’s binary length. After \(j\) rounds, \(B\le D^j\), because each round multiplies \(B\) by an integer at most \(D\).

Also, before round \(j\), every load is at most \(T_{j-1}\). For any available candidate,

\[ h(c) \le T_{j-1}+\frac{1}{\sum_{t:\,c\in A_t}\mu_t} \le T_{j-1}+D, \]

because its approving mass is \(S_c/D\ge1/D\). Induction yields \(T_j\le jD\). Hence throughout the algorithm,

\[ 0\le X_t=B\lambda_t\le kDB. \]

The temporary numerators satisfy

\[ R_c \le DB+S_c(kDB) \le DB(1+kD). \]

Using \(B\le D^k\), the maintained integers, temporary numerators, denominators, and comparison products therefore all have binary length

\[ O\!\left((k+2)\log_2D+\log_2(k+1)+1\right), \]

which is polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\), since \(k\le m\).

Computing all scores in a round uses \(O(m\tau)\) integer arithmetic operations; updating the loads uses \(O(\tau)\). There are \(k\le m\) rounds. Standard exact integer arithmetic on the polynomial-length operands above therefore gives total running time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\).

Conclusion

The algorithm always returns an admissible committee in polynomial time. It preserves the original normalization, accommodates isolated approval-graph vertices, and does not expand binary-encoded masses into voters.
