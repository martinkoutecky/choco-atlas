The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite supports of student types \(A_0\) and place types \(G_0\), positive rational masses \(\alpha_a\) and \(\beta_g\) summing to one on each side, and rational utilities \(u_{ag}\), compute an additive-\(\varepsilon\) approximation to the maximum of \(\sum_{a\in A_0}\alpha_a\log v_a(x)\), where \(x_{ag}\geq 0\), \(\sum_g x_{ag}=\alpha_a\), \(\sum_a x_{ag}=\beta_g\), and \(v_a(x)=\sum_g u_{ag}x_{ag}/\alpha_a\). Determine the running time in \(\tau_A=|A_0|\), \(\tau_G=|G_0|\), input bit length \(L\), accuracy \(\varepsilon\), and any necessary utility or mass conditioning parameters.

1. Result

The approximation problem is polynomial-time solvable in the binary input model. There is an algorithm returning a rational allocation that satisfies all feasibility constraints exactly and has additive objective gap at most \(\varepsilon\).

Write \(p=\tau_A\), \(\beta_{\min}=\min_g\beta_g\), and \(d=(p-1)(m-1)\). For \(p,m\geq2\), one implementation uses

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(d^2\left[\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log(1/\beta_{\min})+\log(pm+1)\right]\right)\)

ellipsoid iterations, each with polynomial bit cost. Consequently, total bit complexity is polynomial in \(p,m,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\).

No additional numerical bound on the smallest positive utility or student mass is required. The displayed dependence on place-mass conditioning is logarithmic. This is a polynomial-time approximation result, without a claim of strong polynomiality or exact optimization.

The proof below works directly with the listed supports.

2. Existence and a bound on optimal utilities

Index the supports by \(a\in\{1,\ldots,p\}\) and \(g\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\). Define

\(\displaystyle b_{ag}=\alpha_a\beta_g,\qquad q_a=\sum_g\beta_g u_{ag},\qquad q=\min_a q_a.\)

The allocation \(b\) is feasible, and \(v_a(b)=q_a\). Every row has a supported place with utility \(1\), so

\(\displaystyle 1\geq q_a\geq\beta_{\min}>0.\)

Every feasible allocation satisfies \(v_a(x)\leq1\), hence \(F(x)\leq0\). The feasible transportation polytope is compact, and the extended objective is upper semicontinuous: each \(\log v_a\), with value \(-\infty\) at zero, is upper semicontinuous. Thus a maximum is attained. Since \(F(b)\) is finite and every \(\alpha_a\) is positive, every maximizer \(x^*\) has strictly positive utilities.

We will prove the stronger bound

\(\displaystyle v_a(x^*)\geq\frac{q_a}{2}\quad\text{for every }a.\)

First consider the relaxation with row and column sums bounded above by their prescribed masses. Any allocation \(y\) in this relaxation can be completed to an allocation satisfying equality. Indeed, let

\(\displaystyle R_a=\alpha_a-\sum_g y_{ag},\qquad C_g=\beta_g-\sum_a y_{ag},\qquad T=\sum_a R_a=\sum_g C_g.\)

If \(T>0\), add \(R_aC_g/T\) to entry \(y_{ag}\); if \(T=0\), all equalities already hold. Nonnegative utilities imply that completion cannot decrease the objective. Therefore \(x^*\) also maximizes the objective over this relaxation.

Set \(c_{ag}=u_{ag}/v_a(x^*)\). For any relaxed feasible \(y\), the utilities along \((1-t)x^*+ty\) remain positive for \(t<1\). Optimality gives the right derivative inequality

\(\displaystyle 0\geq\left.\frac{d}{dt}F((1-t)x^*+ty)\right|_{t=0} =\sum_{a,g}c_{ag}(y_{ag}-x^*_{ag}).\)

Consequently, \(x^*\) maximizes the linear objective \(\sum_{a,g}c_{ag}y_{ag}\) over the relaxation. Its value is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a,g}c_{ag}x^*_{ag}=\sum_a\alpha_a=1.\)

This linear program is feasible and bounded. Its dual is feasible, for example by choosing \(r_a=\max_g c_{ag}\) and \(s_g=0\). Linear-programming strong duality therefore supplies optimal dual variables satisfying

\(\displaystyle r_a,s_g\geq0,\qquad r_a+s_g\geq c_{ag},\qquad \sum_a\alpha_a r_a+\sum_g\beta_g s_g=1.\)

Complementary slackness gives equality \(r_a+s_g=c_{ag}\) whenever \(x^*_{ag}>0\). Summing these equalities over a row yields

\(\displaystyle \alpha_a r_a+\sum_g x^*_{ag}s_g =\sum_g x^*_{ag}c_{ag}=\alpha_a.\)

Thus \(r_a\leq1\). Also \(\sum_g\beta_gs_g\leq1\), since all dual variables are nonnegative. Multiplying the dual inequalities by \(\beta_g\) and summing gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{q_a}{v_a(x^*)} =\sum_g\beta_g c_{ag} \leq r_a+\sum_g\beta_gs_g\leq2.\)

This proves the claimed utility bound.

3. A bounded convex program in independent coordinates

If \(p=1\) or \(m=1\), the feasible allocation is unique and can be returned directly. Henceforth assume \(p,m\geq2\), so \(d\geq1\).

Relabel students so that \(\alpha_p=\max_a\alpha_a\). Then \(\alpha_p\geq1/p\). Use per-student allocation fractions \(y_{ag}=x_{ag}/\alpha_a\), and introduce independent coordinates \(z_{ag}\) for \(a<p\), \(g<m\). Reconstruct the remaining fractions by

\(\displaystyle y_{ag}=z_{ag}\quad(a<p,\ g<m),\qquad y_{am}=1-\sum_{g<m}z_{ag}\quad(a<p),\)

\(\displaystyle y_{pg}=\frac{\beta_g-\sum_{a<p}\alpha_a z_{ag}}{\alpha_p}\quad(g<m),\qquad y_{pm}=1-\sum_{g<m}y_{pg}.\)

These formulas enforce all row and column equalities. Thus \(x_{ag}=\alpha_a y_{ag}(z)\) is feasible exactly when every reconstructed fraction is nonnegative.

Define the rational polytope

\(\displaystyle K=\left\{z:\ y_{ag}(z)\geq0\ \text{for all }a,g,\quad v_a(z):=\sum_g u_{ag}y_{ag}(z)\geq q_a/4\ \text{for all }a\right\}.\)

It has \(pm+p\) affine inequalities, with coefficients of polynomial encoding length. Section 2 shows that every original maximizer belongs to \(K\). Therefore minimizing

\(\displaystyle f(z)=-\sum_a\alpha_a\log v_a(z)\)

over \(K\) is equivalent to the original optimization problem.

On \(K\), utilities lie between \(q_a/4\) and \(1\). Hence

\(\displaystyle 0\leq f(z)\leq\log(4/\beta_{\min})\leq H,\qquad H:=2+\left\lceil\log_2(1/\beta_{\min})\right\rceil.\)

The integer \(H\) can be computed using exact rational comparisons with powers of two.

Membership in \(K\), together with a separating affine inequality when membership fails, is computable by rational arithmetic. At a rational point of \(K\), the gradient is also rational:

\(\displaystyle \frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{ag}} =\alpha_a\left( -\frac{u_{ag}-u_{am}}{v_a(z)} +\frac{u_{pg}-u_{pm}}{v_p(z)} \right),\qquad a<p,\ g<m.\)

Its encoding length is polynomial in the input length and the queried point’s encoding length.

4. An explicit ball of nearly optimal points

Let \(z^0_{ag}=\beta_g\). Its reconstructed fractions are \(y_{ag}(z^0)=\beta_g\) for every student.

For a perturbation \(w\), each reconstructed fraction changes by at most

\(\displaystyle |y_{ag}(z^0+w)-y_{ag}(z^0)|\leq pd\,\|w\|_2.\)

Here is a verification of that bound. The coefficient norms for ordinary entries and ordinary last-column entries are \(1\) and \(\sqrt{m-1}\). For the pivot student, they are bounded respectively by

\(\displaystyle \alpha_p^{-1}\sqrt{\sum_{a<p}\alpha_a^2}\leq p,\qquad \alpha_p^{-1}\sqrt{(m-1)\sum_{a<p}\alpha_a^2}\leq p\sqrt{m-1}.\)

All four bounds are at most \(pd\).

Set

\(\displaystyle r_0=\frac{\beta_{\min}}{4pmd}.\)

If \(\|w\|_2\leq r_0\), every fraction changes by at most \(\beta_{\min}/(4m)\), so all fractions remain positive. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle |v_a(z^0+w)-q_a| \leq\sum_g u_{ag}|y_{ag}(z^0+w)-\beta_g| \leq\beta_{\min}/4.\)

Since \(q_a\geq\beta_{\min}\), the perturbed utility is at least \(3q_a/4\). Therefore the Euclidean ball \(B(z^0,r_0)\) lies in \(K\).

An outer radius is also explicit. For \(z\in K\), each ordinary row has nonnegative entries summing to at most \(1\). Thus

\(\displaystyle \|z\|_2^2\leq p-1,\qquad K\subseteq B(0,R),\quad R:=p.\)

Let \(z^*\) minimize \(f\) over \(K\), and write \(f^*=f(z^*)\). Define

\(\displaystyle \theta=\frac{\varepsilon}{4H},\qquad z^\theta=(1-\theta)z^*+\theta z^0,\qquad r=\theta r_0=\frac{\varepsilon\beta_{\min}}{16Hpmd}.\)

Every point in \(B(z^\theta,r)\) has the form \((1-\theta)z^*+\theta y\), with \(y\in B(z^0,r_0)\). Convexity of \(K\) and \(f\), and the bounds \(0\leq f\leq H\), imply

\(\displaystyle f((1-\theta)z^*+\theta y) \leq(1-\theta)f^*+\theta H \leq f^*+\varepsilon/4.\)

Hence the sublevel set

\(\displaystyle S=\{z\in K:\ f(z)\leq f^*+\varepsilon/4\}\)

contains a ball of radius \(r\).

5. Ellipsoid algorithm and correctness

We use the standard rational central-cut ellipsoid method. Starting from a ball of radius \(R\), it maintains containment of any fixed target set retained by all cuts. With polynomially many precision bits, its volume decreases by a factor at most \(\exp(-1/(5d))\) per iteration. Thus \(O(d^2\log(R/r))\) cuts suffice to make its volume smaller than that of a radius-\(r\) ball. These containment, volume, and bit-complexity properties are proved in [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Section 3.2](https://www.mpi-inf.mpg.de/fileadmin/inf/d1/ellipsoid-lovasz.pdf). In dimension one, interval bisection gives the same required guarantee.

At each rational center \(c\):

  • If \(c\notin K\), use a violated affine inequality to obtain a central cut retaining \(K\).
  • If \(c\in K\), record \(c\) and use the objective cut

\(\nabla f(c)\cdot(z-c)\leq0\).

  • If the gradient is zero, return \(c\): the convex gradient inequality proves that it minimizes \(f\) over \(K\).

Run sufficiently many iterations to obtain the volume bound above.

Suppose, for contradiction, that every recorded center satisfies \(f(c)>f^*+\varepsilon/2\). Every feasibility cut retains \(S\). Every objective cut also retains \(S\), because for \(z\in S\), convexity gives

\(\displaystyle \nabla f(c)\cdot(z-c)\leq f(z)-f(c)<-\varepsilon/4.\)

Consequently, the final ellipsoid would still contain \(S\), including its radius-\(r\) ball. This contradicts the final volume bound. Therefore some recorded feasible center satisfies

\(\displaystyle f(c)\leq f^*+\varepsilon/2.\)

Approximate each recorded objective value to absolute error at most \(\varepsilon/8\), and choose a center \(\widehat z\) with the smallest approximate value. Comparing its approximate value with that of the best recorded center gives

\(\displaystyle f(\widehat z)\leq\min_{\text{recorded }c}f(c)+\varepsilon/4 \leq f^*+3\varepsilon/4.\)

The rational allocation \(\widehat x_{ag}=\alpha_a y_{ag}(\widehat z)\) satisfies all feasibility constraints exactly and obeys

\(\displaystyle F(x^*)-F(\widehat x)\leq3\varepsilon/4<\varepsilon.\)

6. Bit complexity and conditioning

All cuts use exact rational gradients or affine constraints. Logarithms are needed only to select the output.

For completeness, logarithms of positive rationals can be approximated in polynomial bit time. Write \(h=2^j w\), where \(1\leq w<2\). Then

\(\displaystyle \log h=j\log2+\log w,\qquad \log w=2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2k+1}}{2k+1},\quad t=\frac{w-1}{w+1}\in[0,1/3].\)

Truncating after \(K\) terms has error at most \(3^{-2K}\). The same series computes \(\log2\). Choosing accuracy to account for the factor \(j\) requires polynomially many terms in the rational’s bit length and the requested accuracy bits. Since \(\sum_a\alpha_a=1\), approximating each \(\log v_a\) to error \(\varepsilon/8\) also approximates the weighted objective to that error.

The iteration bound is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(d^2\log\frac{R}{r}\right) =O\!\left(d^2\log\frac{16Hp^2md}{\varepsilon\beta_{\min}}\right).\)

Using \(d\leq pm\) and the definition of \(H\) gives the bound stated in Section 1. The rational ellipsoid centers have polynomial encoding length; therefore all constraint checks, gradient calculations, ellipsoid updates, and objective evaluations have polynomial bit cost.

A positive binary rational encoded within \(L\) bits is at least \(2^{-O(L)}\). In particular, \(\log(1/\beta_{\min})=O(L)\). The total running time is consequently polynomial in the requested parameters.

The conditioning conclusions are:

  • Zero utilities and arbitrarily small positive utilities are allowed; no smallest-positive-utility parameter is needed.
  • Small student masses enter through their binary encodings, without an inverse-student-mass factor.
  • This algorithm has logarithmic dependence on \(1/\beta_{\min}\). A utility floor independent of place masses is unavailable: with one student type, utilities \((1,0)\), and place masses \((b,1-b)\), the unique feasible utility is \(b\). This example establishes the limitation of such a floor, without asserting a computational lower bound.

The finite-list polynomial-accuracy result is consistent with this conclusion. [Hosseini and Vazirani](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/opus/volltexte/2022/15682/pdf/LIPIcs-ITCS-2022-86.pdf) discuss ellipsoid approximation polynomial in input size and accuracy bits. Clearing denominators and expanding the present supports into individual students and places can create exponentially many individuals, so direct expansion does not establish the support-encoded bound.

The quoted conditional-gradient guarantee concerns a quadratic extension and requires a separate accuracy-transfer argument. [Panageas, Tröbst, and Vazirani, Theorem 24 and Lemma 23](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02024v3) give those guarantees. The proof here supplies polynomial dependence on accuracy bits directly for the original weighted logarithmic objective.
