The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many complete piecewise-constant valuation types \(\Theta\), masses \(\mu_\theta>0\) with \(\sum_{\theta}\mu_\theta=1\), and common-refinement intervals \(J_1,\ldots,J_k\) of lengths \(\ell_r\), choose aggregate allocations \(x_{\theta r}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{\theta}x_{\theta r}\le\ell_r\) to maximize \(\sum_{\theta}\mu_\theta\log U_\theta\), where \(U_\theta=\mu_\theta^{-1}\sum_r v_{\theta r}x_{\theta r}\), and provide a symmetric finite-agent realization for rational masses or its measure-valued limit, satisfying \(U_\theta\ge\mu_\phi^{-1}\sum_r v_{\theta r}x_{\phi r}\) for every pair \(\theta,\phi\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) valuation types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_\theta\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
The cake is \([0,1]\), partitioned into \(m\) common-refinement intervals \(J_r\) with positive binary-rational lengths \(\ell_r\). Type \(\theta\) has constant binary-rational density \(v_{\theta r}\ge0\) on \(J_r\), with \(\sum_r v_{\theta r}\ell_r>0\); every interval is valued positively by some type. Densities need not be normalized. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Compute an exact maximizer of \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\log U_\theta\), where \(U_\theta=\mu_\theta^{-1}\sum_r v_{\theta r}x_{\theta r}>0\), over \(x_{\theta r}\ge0\) with \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta r}\le\ell_r\). It must satisfy \(U_\theta\ge\mu_\phi^{-1}\sum_r v_{\theta r}x_{\phi r}\) for all \(\theta,\phi\).
A symmetric finite realization has \(N\mu_\theta\) equal-claim agents of each type, for an integer \(N\) making these counts integral; their disjoint cake pieces are finite unions of intervals, each receiving length \(x_{\theta r}/(N\mu_\theta)\) from \(J_r\). A compact description indexed by agent is allowed.
Alternatively, a measure-valued realization assigns each member of type \(\theta\) the same nonnegative cake measure \(B_\theta\), with \(B_\theta(J_r)=x_{\theta r}/\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta B_\theta\le\lambda\), where \(\lambda\) is Lebesgue measure; it is a weak limit of the finite realizations after multiplying each individual cake measure by \(N\).

Is computing such an exact maximizing allocation together with a symmetric finite realization or its measure-valued limit possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this problem NP-hard?

For finitely many agents, MEA computes a Pareto-optimal, envy-free allocation in polynomial time; its variable-claims extension optimizes a claim-weighted logarithmic utility objective and satisfies claim-adjusted envy-freeness. [Aziz–Ye](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.2908)
Exact equilibrium computation for finite linear Fisher markets with rational budgets and utilities is polynomial-time solvable; this result does not formulate the population realization requirement above. [Devanur et al.](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/market.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified binary-rational interval data and nonnegative finite densities, with total input bit length L; renamed the interval count k to m.
  • Excluded identically zero valuation types because their logarithmic utilities are undefined, and imposed the free-disposal convention that every retained interval is desired by some type.
  • Kept densities unnormalized, as the discrete valuation model does not require unit total value.
  • Interpreted symmetric finite realization as equal-claim clones receiving identical interval lengths within each type; allowed a compact indexed description because the common denominator of the masses can be exponentially large.
  • Defined the measure-valued limit using individual cake measures scaled by the population size, so its per-capita utility agrees with U_theta rather than vanishing as the population grows.
  • Required an exact maximizing allocation, without requiring an exact numerical representation of the logarithmic objective value.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\) under the permitted compact output representation. One can compute an exact rational maximizing allocation, a symmetric finite realization, and a measure-valued realization satisfying the stated weak-limit requirement.

The construction uses \(\tau\) Fisher-market buyers. It never explicitly expands the population into its potentially exponentially many members.

1. Reduction to an exact linear Fisher market

Define the aggregate utility of type \(\theta\) by

\(\displaystyle w_\theta(x)=\sum_r v_{\theta r}x_{\theta r}=\mu_\theta U_\theta(x).\)

A feasible allocation with every utility positive exists: take \(x_{\theta r}=\mu_\theta\ell_r\). Then

\(\displaystyle U_\theta(x)=\sum_r v_{\theta r}\ell_r>0.\)

Construct a Fisher market with one buyer for each supported type \(\theta\), budget \(\mu_\theta\), and one unit-supply good for each interval \(J_r\). Its utility coefficients are

\(\displaystyle a_{\theta r}=v_{\theta r}\ell_r.\)

Writing \(y_{\theta r}=x_{\theta r}/\ell_r\), the market feasibility constraints are precisely

\(\displaystyle y_{\theta r}\ge 0,\qquad \sum_\theta y_{\theta r}\le 1.\)

We use the following established theorem: a linear Fisher market with rational positive budgets and rational nonnegative utility coefficients, in which every buyer values some good and every good has an interested buyer, admits an exact rational equilibrium computable in polynomial time in its binary input length. Prices and a corresponding allocation have polynomial bit length. This is the exact algorithm of [Devanur, Papadimitriou, Saberi, and Vazirani, especially Section 2, Lemma 7.1, and Theorem 8.12](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/market.pdf).

All hypotheses hold here:

  • Each budget \(\mu_\theta\) is positive.
  • Each buyer values some good because \(\sum_r v_{\theta r}\ell_r>0\).
  • Every good has an interested buyer by the interval-positivity hypothesis.
  • Products \(v_{\theta r}\ell_r\) are rational, and the transformed input has polynomial bit length.

Compute an exact equilibrium allocation \(y\) and prices \(p\), and set

\(\displaystyle x_{\theta r}=\ell_r y_{\theta r},\qquad \pi_r=p_r/\ell_r.\)

Here \(\pi_r\) is the price per unit of cake length on \(J_r\).

2. A rational certificate of optimality and envy-freeness

We derive the required properties directly from equilibrium.

Every price \(p_r\) is positive. Indeed, some buyer has \(a_{\theta r}>0\); if \(p_r=0\), that buyer could obtain arbitrarily large utility by demanding arbitrarily much of this free good, contradicting utility maximization at a finite equilibrium bundle.

Define

\(\displaystyle \alpha_\theta=\max_r\frac{a_{\theta r}}{p_r}=\max_r\frac{v_{\theta r}}{\pi_r}>0.\)

For any bundle affordable with budget \(\mu_\theta\), its utility is at most \(\alpha_\theta\mu_\theta\). This bound is attainable by spending the entire budget on a good attaining the maximum ratio. Consequently, equilibrium utility equals that bound, and equilibrium expenditure equals the entire budget. Thus

\(\displaystyle w_\theta(x)=\mu_\theta\alpha_\theta,\qquad U_\theta(x)=\alpha_\theta,\qquad \sum_r\pi_r x_{\theta r}=\mu_\theta.\)

In particular, for all \(\theta,r\),

\(\displaystyle v_{\theta r}\le U_\theta(x)\pi_r.\)

Market clearing gives \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta r}=\ell_r\). Summing expenditures therefore yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_r\pi_r\ell_r=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1.\)

These are all exact rational identities or inequalities.

Optimality

Let \(z\) be any feasible allocation with every \(U_\theta(z)>0\). The inequality \(\log t\le t-1\), valid for every \(t>0\), gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_\theta\mu_\theta\log U_\theta(z)-\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\log U_\theta(x) \le \sum_\theta\frac{w_\theta(z)-w_\theta(x)}{U_\theta(x)}.\)

The negative terms sum to

\(\displaystyle \sum_\theta\frac{w_\theta(x)}{U_\theta(x)}=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1.\)

For the positive terms, the coefficient inequalities and feasibility imply

\(\displaystyle \sum_\theta\frac{w_\theta(z)}{U_\theta(x)} =\sum_{\theta,r}\frac{v_{\theta r}}{U_\theta(x)}z_{\theta r} \le\sum_r\pi_r\sum_\theta z_{\theta r} \le\sum_r\pi_r\ell_r =1. \)

Hence the objective difference is nonpositive. Therefore \(x\) is an exact global maximizer.

Required envy inequalities

For any pair \(\theta,\phi\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{\mu_\phi}\sum_r v_{\theta r}x_{\phi r} \le \frac{U_\theta(x)}{\mu_\phi}\sum_r\pi_r x_{\phi r} =U_\theta(x). \)

This proves precisely the required claim-adjusted envy-freeness.

3. Symmetric finite realization with polynomial-size description

Write each mass in lowest terms as \(\mu_\theta=c_\theta/d_\theta\), and choose

\(\displaystyle N_0=\operatorname{lcm}_\theta d_\theta,\qquad n_\theta=N_0\mu_\theta.\)

Each \(n_\theta\) is a positive integer, and \(\sum_\theta n_\theta=N_0\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \log_2 N_0\le\sum_\theta\log_2 d_\theta\le L,\)

so \(N_0\) is computable and representable with polynomially many bits.

Fix an ordering of the types. Write \(J_r=[a_r,a_r+\ell_r]\), with a consistent convention assigning boundary points to only one piece, and define

\(\displaystyle s_{\theta r}=\sum_{\psi<\theta}x_{\psi r}.\)

For agent \(h\) of type \(\theta\), where \(1\le h\le n_\theta\), assign within \(J_r\) the interval whose endpoints are

\(\displaystyle a_r+s_{\theta r}+\frac{(h-1)x_{\theta r}}{n_\theta} \quad\text{and}\quad a_r+s_{\theta r}+\frac{h x_{\theta r}}{n_\theta}. \)

Zero-length intervals are omitted. The agent’s piece is the union of these intervals over \(r\).

For each \(J_r\), consecutive type blocks have lengths \(x_{\theta r}\), and consecutive agents partition their type’s block. Since \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta r}=\ell_r\), these pieces partition the cake, up to the boundary convention. Every individual receives exactly

\(\displaystyle \frac{x_{\theta r}}{n_\theta}=\frac{x_{\theta r}}{N_0\mu_\theta}\)

from \(J_r\), as required.

Each agent of type \(\theta\) receives utility \(U_\theta(x)/N_0\). Their valuation of any type-\(\phi\) agent’s piece is

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{N_0\mu_\phi}\sum_r v_{\theta r}x_{\phi r}\le\frac{U_\theta(x)}{N_0}.\)

Thus the finite realization is envy-free among its equal-claim agents.

The endpoint formula is a compact description indexed by \((\theta,h)\). Its tables and parameters have polynomial size; any indexed agent’s at most \(m\) intervals can be computed in polynomial time.

4. Measure-valued realization and its weak-limit construction

Define the nonnegative measure \(B_\theta\) by its constant density on each refinement interval:

\(\displaystyle \frac{dB_\theta}{d\lambda}(t)=\frac{x_{\theta r}}{\mu_\theta\ell_r} \qquad\text{for }t\in J_r. \)

Then

\(\displaystyle B_\theta(J_r)=\frac{x_{\theta r}}{\mu_\theta}.\)

Furthermore, on every \(J_r\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_\theta\mu_\theta\frac{dB_\theta}{d\lambda} =\frac{\sum_\theta x_{\theta r}}{\ell_r}=1. \)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sum_\theta\mu_\theta B_\theta=\lambda.\)

Its own and cross-type utilities are exactly the quantities already proved envy-free.

It remains to establish the requested weak-limit property.

A sequence of symmetric finite realizations

For each positive integer \(k\), let

\(\displaystyle N_k=kN_0,\qquad n_\theta^{(k)}=N_k\mu_\theta.\)

Partition each \(J_r\) into \(k\) consecutive cells of equal length. Within each cell, allocate a consecutive block of length \(x_{\theta r}/k\) to type \(\theta\), in the fixed type order, and divide that block equally among its \(n_\theta^{(k)}\) agents.

Explicitly, agent \(h\) of type \(\theta\) receives, in cell \(t\), where \(0\le t<k\), the interval with endpoints

\(\displaystyle a_r+\frac{t\ell_r+s_{\theta r}}{k} +\frac{(h-1)x_{\theta r}}{k n_\theta^{(k)}} \quad\text{and}\quad a_r+\frac{t\ell_r+s_{\theta r}}{k} +\frac{h x_{\theta r}}{k n_\theta^{(k)}}. \)

Let \(A_{\theta h}^{(k)}\) denote the union of these intervals. These are disjoint finite unions, and every agent receives total length

\(\displaystyle \frac{x_{\theta r}}{n_\theta^{(k)}}=\frac{x_{\theta r}}{N_k\mu_\theta}\)

from \(J_r\).

Proof of weak convergence

Define the rescaled individual measure

\(\displaystyle \eta_{\theta h}^{(k)}=N_k\,\lambda|_{A_{\theta h}^{(k)}}.\)

On every cell \(C\subseteq J_r\), both \(\eta_{\theta h}^{(k)}\) and \(B_\theta\) have the same mass:

\(\displaystyle \eta_{\theta h}^{(k)}(C)=\frac{x_{\theta r}}{k\mu_\theta}=B_\theta(C). \)

For a continuous function \(f:[0,1]\to\mathbb R\), let

\(\displaystyle \omega_f(\delta)=\sup_{|s-t|\le\delta}|f(s)-f(t)|.\)

Every cell has diameter at most \(1/k\). Two nonnegative measures with equal mass on a cell have integrals of \(f\) differing by at most that mass times the oscillation of \(f\) on the cell. Summing over cells gives

\(\displaystyle \left|\int f\,d\eta_{\theta h}^{(k)}-\int f\,dB_\theta\right| \le B_\theta([0,1])\,\omega_f(1/k). \)

Uniform continuity implies \(\omega_f(1/k)\to0\). The bound is independent of \(h\), so convergence holds uniformly over all agents of type \(\theta\):

\(\displaystyle \eta_{\theta h}^{(k)}\Longrightarrow B_\theta.\)

This proves the population realization requirement.

5. Complexity conclusion

The exact market computation has polynomial bit complexity in \(m,\tau,L\). All subsequent quantities—allocation lengths, population counts, endpoint parameters, and measure densities—are obtained by polynomially many rational arithmetic operations on polynomial-length numbers.

Thus either requested realization can be output in polynomial time. An explicit listing of every agent could have exponential length, but the task expressly permits the compact indexed description constructed above.
