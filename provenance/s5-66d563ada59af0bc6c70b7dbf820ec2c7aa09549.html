The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(A\) and \(B\), rational masses \(\mu_a\) and \(\nu_b\) summing to \(1\) on each side, and each type's weak preference order over acceptable types on the other side, choose rational masses \(x_{ab}\ge 0\) on mutually acceptable pairs, subject to \(\sum_b x_{ab}\le\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}\le\nu_b\). For every acceptable pair \((a,b)\), define \(p_{ab}=\mu_a-\sum_{b'}x_{ab'}+\sum_{b':\,b\succ_a b'}x_{ab'}\) and \(q_{ab}=\nu_b-\sum_{a'}x_{a'b}+\sum_{a':\,a\succ_b a'}x_{a'b}\), and require \(p_{ab}=0\) or \(q_{ab}=0\). Find a stable flow whose total matched mass is at least \(2/3\) of the maximum stable mass, in time polynomial in the number of positive-mass types \(\tau\), the type-level preference description, and mass bit length \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be disjoint finite type supports. A society is given by its support: the list of its positive-mass types, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses satisfy \(\sum_a\mu_a=\sum_b\nu_b=1\).
Each type gives a weak preference order over an explicitly listed subset of the opposite support; ties and empty lists are allowed, and every acceptable type is strictly preferred to being unmatched. Let \(E\subseteq A\times B\) contain exactly the mutually acceptable pairs. Let \(m\) be the total preference-list length and \(L\) the total binary input length.
A feasible flow consists of rational \(x_{ab}\geq0\) for \((a,b)\in E\), with \(\sum_bx_{ab}\leq\mu_a\) and \(\sum_ax_{ab}\leq\nu_b\); set \(x_{ab}=0\) outside \(E\). There are no additional pair capacities.
For \((a,b)\in E\), define \(p_{ab}=\mu_a-\sum_{b'}x_{ab'}+\sum_{b':\,b\succ_a b'}x_{ab'}\) and \(q_{ab}=\nu_b-\sum_{a'}x_{a'b}+\sum_{a':\,a\succ_b a'}x_{a'b}\), where \(\succ\) denotes strict preference.
A flow is stable when every \((a,b)\in E\) satisfies \(p_{ab}=0\) or \(q_{ab}=0\), including pairs carrying positive flow. Write \(S(x)=\sum_{(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}\) and let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the maximum stable matched mass.

Does this problem admit an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) that returns a rational stable flow \(x\) with \(S(x)\geq\frac{2}{3}\mathrm{OPT}\), or is achieving this approximation guarantee NP-hard?

For discrete stable marriage with ties and incomplete lists, a \(\frac{3}{2}\)-approximation is computable in \(O(m)\) time ([Paluch](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.5660)).
For continuous bipartite stable allocation with strict preferences, polynomial-time minimum-cost optimization is known; that result excludes ties ([Dean–Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf)).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted acceptable pairs as mutually acceptable pairs, matching the discrete definition.
  • Specified that every acceptable partner is strictly preferred to being unmatched; ties do not create strict improvements.
  • Restricted the supplied type lists to positive-mass supports and defined tau as their combined size.
  • Allowed empty preference lists, as the discrete definition permits them.
  • Defined m as total preference-list length and L as total binary input length.

Result

Yes. There is an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) that returns a rational stable flow \(x\) satisfying \(S(x)\geq\frac23\mathrm{OPT}\).

The algorithm replaces each mutually acceptable pair with three strictly ranked contracts. It computes a stable allocation on those contracts without expanding masses into individuals. We prove both stability of the projection and the approximation guarantee.

1. The auxiliary contracts

Delete unilateral acceptabilities from the preference lists. This preserves the feasible flows and the stability conditions.

For each \(e=(a,b)\in E\), introduce three contracts \(X_e,Y_e,Z_e\), each connecting \(a\) and \(b\). Keep the original masses as vertex quotas.

Define strict preferences over the contracts as follows, with every block ordered internally in an arbitrary fixed strict order.

  • At each \(a\in A\), traverse its original indifference classes from best to worst. For each class, list its \(X\)-contracts, followed by its \(Y\)-contracts. After processing every class, append all \(Z\)-contracts in original class order.
  • At each \(b\in B\), use the same construction with \(X\) and \(Z\) interchanged.

This ordering follows the three-copy construction of [Yokoi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03076). We establish its continuous approximation guarantee directly below.

Write \(h_v\) for the original mass of vertex \(v\). For a feasible contract allocation \(f\), define

\(d_v(f)=\sum_{c\text{ incident to }v}f(c)\)

and, for a contract \(c\) incident to \(v\),

\(P_v(c;f)=h_v-d_v(f)+\sum_{c':\,c\succ_v^*c'}f(c'),\)

where \(\succ_v^*\) denotes the auxiliary strict order. We require auxiliary stability to mean that every contract \(c\) connecting \(a,b\) satisfies

\(P_a(c;f)=0\quad\text{or}\quad P_b(c;f)=0.\)

In particular, this condition applies to contracts carrying positive allocation.

We next show that such a rational allocation can be computed in polynomial time.

2. Computing the auxiliary allocation without population expansion

We use the polynomial-time stable-flow algorithm of [Cseh and Matuschke, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3701).

Its model is a directed capacitated network with source \(s\), sink \(t\), and strict rankings of incoming and outgoing arcs at every other vertex. A blocking walk consists entirely of unsaturated arcs; it starts at \(s\), or with an arc preferred to some positive-flow outgoing arc, and ends at \(t\), or with an arc preferred to some positive-flow incoming arc. Their algorithm finds a flow with no blocking walk using \(O(|V||F|)\) arithmetic operations, where \(F\) is the network arc set.

If \(E\) is empty, return the zero flow. Otherwise, omit isolated types from the following network; their output allocations are zero.

Construct a simple directed network containing:

  • an arc \(s\to a\) of capacity \(\mu_a\) for every remaining \(a\in A\);
  • an arc \(b\to t\) of capacity \(\nu_b\) for every remaining \(b\in B\);
  • for every auxiliary contract \(c\) connecting \(a,b\), a private vertex \(w_c\) and arcs \(a\to w_c\to b\), both of capacity \(2\).

At \(a\), rank the outgoing contract arcs by its auxiliary order. At \(b\), rank incoming contract arcs by its auxiliary order. All other incoming or outgoing lists are singletons.

The network is simple, all nonterminal vertices have incoming and outgoing arcs, and its rankings are strict. Thus the cited theorem applies.

Let \(g\) be the resulting stable network flow. Conservation at \(w_c\) gives a common value on its two incident arcs; call that value \(f(c)\). Conservation and the terminal capacities imply

\(d_a(f)\leq\mu_a,\qquad d_b(f)\leq\nu_b.\)

Since each original mass is at most \(1\), every contract arc carries at most \(1\), and hence is strictly below its capacity \(2\).

Claim. The resulting \(f\) is auxiliary-stable.

Proof. Suppose some contract \(c\) connecting \(a,b\) satisfies \(P_a(c;f)>0\) and \(P_b(c;f)>0\).

At \(a\), either:

  • there is unused quota, so \(s\to a\) is unsaturated; or
  • some positive-flow outgoing contract is strictly worse than \(c\).

Likewise, at \(b\), either \(b\to t\) is unsaturated or some positive-flow incoming contract is strictly worse than \(c\).

Therefore the walk through \(a\to w_c\to b\), extended to \(s\) and/or \(t\) whenever the corresponding endpoint has unused quota, is a blocking walk. Its contract arcs are unsaturated, and its endpoint conditions hold by construction. This contradicts stability of \(g\). \(\square\)

Binary encoding and running time

There are \(3|E|\) private vertices and \(6|E|\) contract arcs. Thus both network sizes are \(O(m+\tau)\).

To verify bit complexity explicitly, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses. Then \(\log D=O(L)\). Multiply every network capacity by \(D\), giving integer capacities bounded by \(2D\).

The cited algorithm starts with zero flow and augments by minimum residual capacities. Consequently, its intermediate flows remain integral and bounded by \(2D\), using \(O(L)\) bits per value. Its arithmetic operation count is polynomial in \(m+\tau\), independent of the numerical value of \(D\). Dividing the output by \(D\) gives rational \(f\) with polynomial encoding length.

This scaling changes numbers only; it does not create individuals or additional vertices.

3. Stability of the projected flow

Return

\(x_{ab}=f(X_{ab})+f(Y_{ab})+f(Z_{ab})\)

for \((a,b)\in E\), and zero elsewhere. Feasibility follows immediately from the auxiliary vertex quotas.

Claim. The projected flow \(x\) is stable under the problem’s definition.

Proof. Suppose \((a,b)\) blocks \(x\), meaning \(p_{ab}(x)>0\) and \(q_{ab}(x)>0\).

At \(a\), positive dissatisfaction means either unused quota or positive allocation to an originally strictly worse partner. The contract \(Y_{ab}\) is preferred to every auxiliary copy of any such worse pair:

  • its \(X\)- and \(Y\)-copies occur in later indifference classes;
  • its \(Z\)-copy occurs in the final part of the list.

Therefore \(P_a(Y_{ab};f)>0\).

The symmetric argument at \(b\), interchanging \(X\) and \(Z\), gives \(P_b(Y_{ab};f)>0\). This contradicts auxiliary stability. \(\square\)

4. A structural lemma excluding short improvements

Call a type deficient in \(x\) if its incident matched mass is strictly smaller than its quota.

Lemma. Suppose \(a_0\in A\) and \(b_0\in B\) are deficient in \(x\), \(x_{ab}>0\), and both \((a_0,b)\) and \((a,b_0)\) belong to \(E\). Then

\(b\succ_a b_0\qquad\text{and}\qquad a\succ_b a_0.\)

Proof.

First, \(f(X_{ab})=0\). Otherwise, consider \(Z_{a_0b}\). At \(b\), every \(Z\)-contract is preferred to every \(X\)-contract. Hence positive allocation on \(X_{ab}\) gives \(P_b(Z_{a_0b};f)>0\). Deficiency of \(a_0\) gives \(P_{a_0}(Z_{a_0b};f)>0\), contradicting auxiliary stability.

Symmetrically, \(f(Z_{ab})=0\): the contract \(X_{ab_0}\) would block it, using deficiency of \(b_0\).

Since \(x_{ab}>0\), it follows that

\(f(Y_{ab})=x_{ab}>0.\)

Suppose \(b\) is not strictly preferred to \(b_0\) by \(a\). Because the original preference order is weak, \(b_0\) is either tied with or strictly preferred to \(b\). In either case, \(X_{ab_0}\) precedes \(Y_{ab}\) in \(a\)’s auxiliary list. Thus \(P_a(X_{ab_0};f)>0\). Deficiency of \(b_0\) gives \(P_{b_0}(X_{ab_0};f)>0\), a contradiction. Therefore \(b\succ_a b_0\).

The symmetric argument using \(Z_{a_0b}\) proves \(a\succ_b a_0\). \(\square\)

Consequently, no stable comparison flow \(y\) can simultaneously have

\(y_{a_0b}>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad y_{ab_0}>0\)

under the lemma’s hypotheses. Indeed,

\(p_{ab}(y)\geq y_{ab_0}>0,\qquad q_{ab}(y)\geq y_{a_0b}>0,\)

so \((a,b)\) blocks \(y\). This argument also applies when \(y_{ab}>0\), using the problem’s requirement that stability includes positive-flow pairs.

5. The approximation bound

Let \(y\) be any rational stable flow. We prove

\(S(y)\leq\frac32 S(x).\)

For this proof only, choose a common denominator \(N\) for the coordinates of \(x\) and \(y\). On each original pair \(e\), cancel their common allocation and create:

  • \(N\max(y_e-x_e,0)\) red edge copies;
  • \(N\max(x_e-y_e,0)\) blue edge copies.

Let

\(C=N\sum_e\min(x_e,y_e)\)

be the canceled mass in these integer units.

At each vertex, pair red and blue incidences until one color is exhausted. These local pairings partition the edge copies into alternating trails and alternating cycles: each edge copy has at most one continuation at each end.

If an alternating trail has a red endpoint at vertex \(v\), maximality of the local pairing implies that the red degree exceeds the blue degree there. Thus

\(d_v(y)>d_v(x).\)

Since \(y\) is feasible, \(d_v(y)\leq h_v\), so \(v\) is deficient in \(x\).

We now exclude the two problematic trail lengths.

Length one. A single red edge joins two types deficient in \(x\). Both have positive unmatched mass, so that acceptable pair blocks \(x\), contradicting Section 3.

Length three with red endpoints. Such a trail has the form

\(a_0\;-\;b\;-\;a\;-\;b_0,\)

with red outer edges and a blue middle edge. Its endpoints are deficient in \(x\), its middle edge satisfies \(x_{ab}>0\), and its outer edges satisfy \(y_{a_0b}>0\) and \(y_{ab_0}>0\). Section 4 shows that \((a,b)\) then blocks \(y\), a contradiction.

The remaining components satisfy the required counting bound:

  • alternating cycles and even-length trails have equal numbers of red and blue edges;
  • odd-length trails with blue endpoints have fewer red than blue edges;
  • odd-length trails with red endpoints have \(k+1\) red and \(k\) blue edges, where the exclusions above give \(k\geq2\). Hence \(k+1\leq\frac32k\).

Summing over all components, if \(R,B\) denote the total numbers of red and blue copies, then

\(R\leq\frac32B.\)

Restoring the canceled mass gives

\(NS(y)=C+R\leq C+\frac32B\leq\frac32(C+B)=\frac32NS(x).\)

Thus \(S(x)\geq\frac23S(y)\) for every rational stable \(y\).

For completeness, the optimum is attained rationally. The real stable feasible set is a finite union of bounded rational polytopes: for every acceptable pair, choose one of its endpoint dissatisfaction expressions to equal zero. Each nonempty polytope has a rational vertex maximizing the linear objective. The constructed flow proves nonemptiness. Therefore we may take \(y\) to attain \(\mathrm{OPT}\).

Conclusion

The construction returns a rational stable flow with \(S(x)\geq\frac23\mathrm{OPT}\), in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Neither its network size nor its running time depends on the expanded population size.
