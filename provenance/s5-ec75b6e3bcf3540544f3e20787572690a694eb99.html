The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), a compatibility graph \(H\), binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), per-type load profiles, and an encoded \(m+a\) value formula extending the paper’s energy objective to type-mass vectors, determine the supremum of \(\sum_{x\in\mathcal P}v(x)\) to additive error \(\epsilon\). Here \(\mathcal P\) is any finite collection of nonzero vectors \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^{T}\) whose positive-support types induce a connected subgraph of \(H\), and \(\sum_{x\in\mathcal P}x=\mu\); return a feasible collection and a certified upper bound with gap at most \(\epsilon\).

Result

The additive approximation problem is NP-hard even for inverse-polynomial accuracy. This holds with \(m=1\), complete compatibility, equal dyadic masses, and quadratic value formulas.

The proof addresses the general formula class in the question. It does not establish hardness for the specific energy benchmark alone.

1. Construction from Maximum Cut

We reduce from Maximum Cut on simple unweighted graphs, whose decision version is NP-complete. [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1)

Let \(G=(V,F)\), with \(n=|V|\geq1\) and \(e=|F|\). Choose the smallest power of two \(N\) satisfying \(N\geq n+2\). Add isolated vertices to obtain a vertex set \(W\) of size \(r=N-2\). This preserves the maximum cut size, denoted by \(C^*\).

Create the types \(T=W\cup\{a,b\}\), where \(a,b\) are two additional types. Set:

  • \(\tau=N\) and \(\mu_t=1/N\) for every type;
  • \(H=K_N\), so every nonzero vector is feasible;
  • \(m=1\), all loads zero, and \(p_S=-2<p_F=-1<0\).

These energy data give \(E(x)=0\): every purchase has nonpositive value, and purchasing nothing is feasible.

Put \(B=r+1\) and \(M=2Br+1\). Supply the formulas

\(v^+(x)=B(x_a+x_b)\sum_{i\in W}x_i,\)

\(v^-(x)=-Mx_ax_b-\sum_{\{i,j\}\in F}x_ix_j.\)

These are permitted expression trees using only coordinates, integer constants, addition, subtraction, and multiplication. Both components vanish at zero.

Verification of the promised inequalities

For nonnegative vectors \(x,y\),

\(v^+(x+y)-v^+(x)-v^+(y) =B\left((x_a+x_b)\sum_{i\in W}y_i+(y_a+y_b)\sum_{i\in W}x_i\right)\geq0.\)

Likewise,

\(v^-(x+y)-v^-(x)-v^-(y) =-M(x_ay_b+y_ax_b)-\sum_{\{i,j\}\in F}(x_iy_j+y_ix_j)\leq0.\)

Thus the construction satisfies the required superadditivity and subadditivity promises, even without restricting these inequalities to feasible vectors.

2. Splitting cannot improve the optimum

Lemma. Every finite coalition family in the constructed instance can be replaced by a family in which each type belongs wholly to one coalition, without decreasing its total value.

Proof. Fix a family \((x^1,\ldots,x^k)\) summing to \(\mu\), and fix a type \(t\). Hold all coordinates of all other types fixed. Write \(z_h=x_t^h\).

Every monomial in \(v\) involves two distinct types. Consequently, the total value, as a function of the distribution of type \(t\), has the form

\(A+\sum_{h=1}^k c_hz_h,\qquad z_h\geq0,\qquad \sum_{h=1}^kz_h=\mu_t,\)

where \(A,c_1,\ldots,c_k\) do not depend on the \(z_h\).

Choose \(h_*\) maximizing \(c_h\). Moving all mass of type \(t\) into coalition \(h_*\) changes its contribution to \(\mu_tc_{h_*}\), and

\(\sum_{h=1}^kc_hz_h\leq c_{h_*}\sum_{h=1}^kz_h=\mu_tc_{h_*}.\)

Hence the total value does not decrease. Coordinate bounds and the total mass constraint remain satisfied. Every nonzero resulting vector is feasible because \(H\) is complete.

Perform this operation for every type. Subsequent operations never split a previously consolidated type. Delete zero vectors at the end. Each remaining coordinate is either \(0\) or \(\mu_t\), proving the lemma. ∎

It follows that the supremum over all finite real coalition families equals the maximum over ordinary partitions of \(T\). Indeed, every continuous family is dominated by such a partition, and every such partition is an admissible family. There are finitely many partitions, so the constructed optimum is attained by rational vectors.

3. Exact optimum of the constructed instance

For an ordinary partition \(P\) of \(T\), define its integer score by

\(Q(P)=N^2\sum_{C\in P}v(\mu\mathbf 1_C).\)

Direct substitution gives

\(Q(P)=B\sum_{i\in W}\left(\mathbf 1[i\sim_P a]+\mathbf 1[i\sim_P b]\right) -M\mathbf 1[a\sim_P b] -\sum_{\{i,j\}\in F}\mathbf 1[i\sim_P j],\)

where \(u\sim_P v\) means that \(u,v\) lie in the same block.

The two additional types must be separated

If \(a,b\) belong to the same block, the positive contribution is at most \(2Br\), while the edge contribution is nonpositive. Therefore

\(Q(P)\leq2Br-M=-1.\)

Conversely, the partition having one block \(W\cup\{a\}\) and the other block \(\{b\}\) has score \(Br-e>0\), because

\(Br-e\geq r(r+1)-r(r-1)/2=r(r+3)/2>0.\)

Thus every maximizing partition separates \(a\) from \(b\).

Every remaining type must join one of them

Suppose a partition separates \(a,b\) but contains a nonempty block \(D\subseteq W\) containing neither. Let \(A\subseteq W\) be the ordinary types in the block containing \(a\).

Merge \(D\) into that block. This creates a reward \(B|D|\). The only additional penalty comes from edges between \(D\) and \(A\), of which there are at most \(|D||A|\leq r|D|\). Hence the score increases by at least

\(B|D|-r|D|=(B-r)|D|=|D|>0.\)

No maximizing partition can therefore contain such a block. Every maximizing partition consists of two blocks, one containing \(a\), the other containing \(b\).

The remaining objective is Maximum Cut

Such a partition gives a cut of \(W\). If that cut crosses \(c\) edges, then exactly \(e-c\) edges have endpoints in the same block. Every ordinary type earns exactly one reward \(B\), so

\(Q(P)=Br-(e-c)=Br-e+c.\)

Conversely, every cut of \(W\) defines such a partition. Maximizing, and using the splitting lemma, yields the exact identity

\(\mathrm{OPT}=\dfrac{Br-e+C^*}{N^2}.\)

4. Recovering Maximum Cut from the approximation output

Choose the dyadic accuracy

\(\epsilon=\dfrac{1}{4N^2}=\dfrac{1}{4\tau^2}.\)

Suppose an algorithm returns the required rational family, with total value \(S\), and a rational upper bound \(U\). Its guarantees imply

\(S\leq\mathrm{OPT}\leq U,\qquad U-S\leq\epsilon.\)

Consequently,

\(\mathrm{OPT}\leq U\leq S+\epsilon\leq\mathrm{OPT}+\epsilon.\)

Substituting the exact optimum gives

\(C^*\leq N^2U-Br+e\leq C^*+\frac14.\)

Since \(C^*\) is an integer, it is recovered exactly by

\(C^*=\left\lfloor N^2U-Br+e\right\rfloor.\)

This calculation uses only the rational upper bound; it requires no numerical evaluation of the returned family.

5. Encoding size and conclusion

The construction has \(N<2(n+2)\) types. Its compatibility graph has polynomial size, its formula trees have size polynomial in \(n+e\), and all numerical constants have polynomial bit length. Because \(N\) is a power of two, the masses and accuracy are dyadic. Moreover, \(1/\epsilon=4N^2\).

An algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\) would therefore compute Maximum Cut exactly in polynomial time. Unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\), no such algorithm exists.

The unrestricted splitting and unbounded number of coalitions are fully covered by the rounding lemma.
