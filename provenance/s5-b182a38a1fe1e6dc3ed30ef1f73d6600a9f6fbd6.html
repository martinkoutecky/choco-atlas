The continuous mirror question, as graded

For fixed finite player-type and arm-type sets, rational player masses μ summing to \(1\), per-capita arm capacities ν, strict type preferences and priorities, and \(1\)-sub-Gaussian rewards with distinct means, does there exist a decentralized learning policy whose type-weighted regret against a player-pessimal stable flow is at most \(C\log^2 T\), with \(C\) independent of the number of workers in finite replications of these type masses? Workers have private reward histories and independent random seeds, observe aggregate service by type and arm each round, and arms serve proposed mass in priority order up to capacity.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge2\) arm types and \(\tau\ge1\) player types. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_i\), with \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Arm capacities \(\nu_j>0\) are binary rationals with \(\sum_j\nu_j\ge1\). Let \(L\) be the public input bit length; express running times in \(m,\tau,L,T\), and replication size \(N\).
Every arm has a publicly known strict priority order over player types. A served type-\(i\) worker at arm \(j\) receives an independent sample from a fixed \(1\)-sub-Gaussian distribution with unknown mean \(r_{ij}>0\); each row has distinct means, inducing the worker's unknown strict preferences. Rejection gives zero reward and no reward sample; every arm is acceptable.
A feasible flow has \(x_{ij}\ge0\), \(\sum_jx_{ij}=\mu_i\), and \(\sum_ix_{ij}\le\nu_j\). It is stable if no pair \((i,j)\) has both positive type-\(i\) mass assigned to arms worse than \(j\) and either spare capacity at \(j\) or positive mass there of a strictly lower-priority type.
Let \(x^-\) be the player-pessimal stable flow: for every type and every initial segment of its true arm ranking, its mass assigned to that segment is no greater than in any other stable flow. Equal-type workers are interchangeable; equal-type displacement alone is not blocking.
Interpret the mass model through all exact finite replications: for every \(N\) making \(N\mu_i,N\nu_j\) integers, there are \(N\mu_i\) workers of type \(i\), and arm \(j\) serves up to \(N\nu_j\) applicants per round in type-priority order. At a partially admitted type, choose a uniform subset, independently each round.
Workers start together, know their type, the public structural input, \(N\), and the round number, and use one common policy per type. Actions may depend only on their own action, admission and reward histories, independent private random seeds, and past aggregate admitted counts by type and arm; there is no direct communication or access to others' rewards.
For worker rewards \(X_w(t)\), define the signed type-weighted regret \(R_N(T)=T\sum_{i,j}x^-_{ij}r_{ij}-\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^T\sum_{w=1}^N\mathbb E[X_w(t)]\).
Does a decentralized learning policy exist that, for every admissible instance, satisfies \(R_N(T)\le C\log^2 T\) for every exact replication \(N\) and every \(T\ge2\), where \(C\) may depend on the fixed type-level instance but not on \(N\) or \(T\), or is this guarantee impossible?
[Known discrete result](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume22/20-1429/20-1429.pdf): CA-UCB achieves player-pessimal \(O(\log^2 T)\) regret with arbitrary strict individual preferences, observing arm winners, with a constant growing exponentially in market size.

1. Result

Such a decentralized policy exists. Below is a construction and a proof that its regret constant is independent of the exact replication size \(N\).

The policy alternates scheduled exploration with deferred acceptance using workers’ privately estimated rankings. Two facts make the bound uniform in \(N\):

  • A small mass of workers with incorrect rankings causes only a proportionally small loss relative to the player-pessimal benchmark.
  • Deferred acceptance incurs at most \(m-1\) rejection rounds per worker, even when its completion time grows with \(N\).

Write
\[ B=\sum_{i,j}x^-_{ij}r_{ij}, \qquad M=\max_{i,j}r_{ij}, \qquad \Delta=\min_i\min_{j\ne k}|r_{ij}-r_{ik}|>0. \]
Here and below, \(x^-\) is the flow stipulated in the question. The policy does not need to compute it or know \(M\) or \(\Delta\).

2. A robustness lemma for the benchmark

For any feasible flow \(x\), define its mean reward by
\[ W(x)=\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}r_{ij}. \]

First, every stable flow \(x\) satisfies \(W(x)\ge B\). Indeed, list type \(i\)’s arms as \(j_1,\ldots,j_m\) in decreasing order of their true means, and put
\[ F_i^x(\ell)=\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}x_{i,j_a}. \]
Using the row-sum constraint,
\[ \sum_jx_{ij}r_{ij} = \mu_i r_{i,j_m} + \sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1} \bigl(r_{i,j_\ell}-r_{i,j_{\ell+1}}\bigr)F_i^x(\ell). \]
Every coefficient in this last sum is positive. The defining prefix dominance of \(x^-\) therefore implies \(W(x)\ge W(x^-)=B\).

We need a quantitative extension when some workers have incorrect rankings.

Lemma. There is a finite constant \(K\), depending only on the fixed type-level instance, with the following property.

Suppose \(y,z\) are nonnegative flows such that \(x=y+z\) is feasible. Interpret \(y\) as the assignments of workers with correct rankings and \(z\) as the assignments of the remaining workers. Suppose that, for every pair \((i,j)\),
\[ \sum_{k:r_{ik}<r_{ij}}y_{ik}>0 \]
implies both
\[ \sum_hx_{hj}=\nu_j \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{h:\,j\text{ ranks }h\text{ below }i}x_{hj}=0. \]
Then, writing \(\varepsilon=\sum_{i,j}z_{ij}\),
\[ B-W(x)\le K\varepsilon. \]

Proof. The hypotheses describe a finite union of bounded polytopes in the \(d=2m\tau\) variables \((y,z)\). For each pair \((i,j)\), choose one of the following alternatives:

  • \(\sum_{k:r_{ik}<r_{ij}}y_{ik}=0\);
  • arm \(j\) is saturated and has zero assignment to types strictly below \(i\) in its priority order.

Together with feasibility and nonnegativity, each choice gives a polytope. These polytopes are bounded because every coordinate is at most its type’s mass.

Let \(D\) be a positive integer such that all \(D\mu_i\) and \(D\nu_j\) are integers. We can take
\[ K=M D d!. \]

To verify this bound, consider a vertex \(v\) of any nonempty polytope in the union. A vertex is determined by \(d\) linearly independent active constraints. Their coefficient matrix has integer entries of absolute value at most \(1\), and their right-hand sides have denominators dividing \(D\). Cramer’s rule shows that every coordinate of \(v\), and hence \(\varepsilon(v)\), has denominator dividing \(D|\det A|\). The determinant expansion gives \(|\det A|\le d!\).

Consequently, if \(\varepsilon(v)>0\), then
\[ \varepsilon(v)\ge\frac{1}{Dd!}. \]
Since the means are positive, \(W(v)\ge0\); also \(B\le M\). Thus
\[ B-W(v)\le M\le K\varepsilon(v). \]

If instead \(\varepsilon(v)=0\), nonnegativity forces \(z=0\). The disjunctions then say exactly that \(y=x\) is stable under the true rankings. Hence \(B-W(v)\le0\).

The desired inequality is affine in \((y,z)\). Every point of a bounded polytope is a convex combination of its vertices, so the inequality holds throughout every polytope in the union. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

The factorial bound is deliberately coarse. Its role is to establish that \(K\) is finite and independent of \(N\). No enumeration of these polytopes is part of the policy.

3. Deferred acceptance with frozen private rankings

Fix an exact replication and an arbitrary strict ranking of all arms for every worker. Different workers of the same type may have different rankings.

Run the following synchronous procedure:

  • Initially, every worker applies to its first-ranked arm.
  • An admitted worker reapplies to the same arm next round.
  • A rejected worker advances to the next arm in its frozen ranking.

Arms use exactly the admission rule in the question, including uniform selection within a partially admitted type.

Let \(c_j=N\nu_j\) denote arm \(j\)’s integer capacity.

Persistence after a rejection

The applicants at an arm include all workers it admitted in the preceding round. Therefore, for every cutoff in that arm’s type-priority order, the number admitted at or above the cutoff cannot decrease.

In particular, if a type-\(i\) worker is rejected by arm \(j\), that arm admits exactly \(c_j\) workers, all of types ranked at least as high as \(i\). Both facts persist in every subsequent round.

This assertion concerns type counts, so uniform displacement between equal-type workers does not affect it.

Termination and stability

No worker can exhaust its list. If a worker were rejected from its last arm, persistence after its earlier rejections would imply that every arm was full in that round. The total number admitted would then be
\[ \sum_jc_j\ge N, \]
although that worker was rejected and hence at most \(N-1\) workers were admitted—a contradiction.

Thus each worker is rejected at most \(m-1\) times. Every round before a rejection-free round contains a rejection, so a rejection-free round occurs within
\[ N(m-1)+1 \]
rounds. Once all workers are admitted, they keep their arms and all remain admitted.

The resulting assignment is stable with respect to the frozen worker rankings and strict priorities over types. Indeed, a worker preferring an arm to its final assignment must previously have applied to and been rejected by that arm. Persistence rules out both spare capacity and any strictly lower-priority type there.

In particular, if a worker’s frozen ranking is correct, it has no blocking pair under its true preferences.

Reward loss during the procedure

Let \(\beta\) be the fraction of workers whose frozen rankings are incorrect. Extend the procedure to completion, and let \(x^{\mathrm{fin}}\) be its final normalized flow.

Split this flow into assignments \(y\) of correctly ranked workers and assignments \(z\) of incorrectly ranked workers. The preceding stability argument verifies every hypothesis of the robustness lemma. Therefore
\[ B-W(x^{\mathrm{fin}})\le K\beta. \]

In procedure round \(s\), let \(a_s\) be the fraction rejected, and let \(W_s\) be the sum of admitted workers’ reward means divided by \(N\).

A correctly ranked admitted worker holds an arm at least as good as its eventual final arm: its pointer only advances down its ranking. For every incorrectly ranked worker, or every rejected worker, the deficit relative to its final reward mean is at most \(M\). Consequently,
\[ W(x^{\mathrm{fin}})-W_s\le M(\beta+a_s), \]
and hence
\[ B-W_s\le(K+M)\beta+Ma_s. \]

Over any prefix of the procedure,
\[ \sum_sa_s\le m-1, \]
because each worker experiences at most \(m-1\) rejections. Thus a prefix of length \(\ell\) has benchmark deficit at most
\[ (K+M)\ell\beta+M(m-1). \]

This bound applies even when the prefix ends before deferred acceptance completes. One may extend that prefix hypothetically using future admission draws; the argument holds for every such extension.

4. The decentralized policy

Number phases by \(k=1,2,\ldots\). Phase \(k\) consists of the following two blocks.

Exploration block

For every pair \((i,j)\), allocate \(k\) consecutive rounds:

  • All type-\(i\) workers apply to arm \(j\).
  • Every other type applies to a publicly fixed arm \(f(j)\ne j\).

Such a choice of \(f(j)\) exists because \(m\ge2\). The pairs are processed in a public fixed order, making this block’s length \(m\tau k\).

Workers retain only rewards obtained during exploration blocks in which their own type was the target type. They maintain a sample count and sum for each arm.

Exploitation block

Each worker sorts arms by its empirical means, using public arm labels to break ties and an arbitrary fixed convention when an arm has no samples.

It freezes this ranking, resets its deferred-acceptance pointer, and runs the procedure in Section 3 for exactly \(2^k\) rounds. Rewards from this block are not used in the ranking estimates.

The schedule is determined by the public round number. Every worker of a given type uses the same code; its actions depend only on its type, public information, and its own admission and exploration-reward histories.

5. Incorrect rankings have small expected mass

During exploration targeting \((i,j)\), only type \(i\) applies to arm \(j\). Each such worker is admitted with probability
\[ q_{ij}=\min\left\{1,\frac{\nu_j}{\mu_i}\right\}, \]
independently across these exploration rounds. Crucially, this probability does not depend on \(N\).

Put
\[ q_*=\min_{i,j}q_{ij}>0, \qquad S_k=\sum_{s=1}^ks=\frac{k(k+1)}2. \]
By the end of phase \(k\)’s exploration, each worker has made exactly \(S_k\) targeted exploration attempts at each arm.

For a fixed worker and arm, its number \(Z\) of admitted exploration samples is binomial with parameters \(S_k,q_{ij}\). The multiplicative binomial Chernoff bound, applied with relative deviation \(1/2\), gives
\[ \Pr\!\left(Z<\frac{q_{ij}S_k}{2}\right) \le \exp\left(-\frac{q_{ij}S_k}{8}\right). \]

Conditional on \(Z=n\ge1\), the samples are independent and \(1\)-sub-Gaussian. Thus their empirical mean satisfies
\[ \Pr\!\left(|\widehat r_{ij}-r_{ij}|\ge\frac{\Delta}{4}\,\middle|\,Z=n\right) \le2\exp\left(-\frac{n\Delta^2}{32}\right). \]
Combining these inequalities bounds the probability of insufficient samples or an estimation error by
\[ \exp\left(-\frac{q_{ij}S_k}{8}\right) + 2\exp\left(-\frac{q_{ij}S_k\Delta^2}{64}\right). \]

If every arm estimate is within \(\Delta/4\) of its true mean, the worker’s entire ranking is correct: every true pairwise gap is at least \(\Delta\), while the combined estimation error is less than \(\Delta/2\).

Define
\[ a=q_*\min\left\{\frac18,\frac{\Delta^2}{64}\right\}>0, \qquad p_k=3m\exp(-aS_k). \]
A union bound over the \(m\) arms shows that any fixed worker’s ranking is incorrect with probability at most \(p_k\).

Let \(\beta_k\) be the fraction of incorrectly ranked workers in phase \(k\). Linearity of expectation gives
\[ \mathbb E[\beta_k]\le p_k. \]
There is no union bound over workers, and no independence between workers is needed.

6. Uniform regret bound

An exploration round has expected reward at least zero, so its regret contribution is at most \(B\le M\).

For any prefix of phase \(k\)’s exploitation block, Section 3 and the bound on \(\mathbb E[\beta_k]\) give expected regret at most
\[ (K+M)2^kp_k+M(m-1). \]
To pass from reward means to expected realized rewards, condition on the history and current admission decisions. Every admitted worker’s fresh reward has the corresponding mean, so the normalized expected reward equals \(\mathbb E[W_s]\).

The series
\[ A=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^kp_k = 3m\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 2^k\exp\left(-\frac{ak(k+1)}2\right) \]
is finite: the ratio of consecutive summands is \(2\exp(-a(k+1))\), which tends to zero.

If phase \(k\) has started by time \(T\), the preceding exploitation blocks alone occupy \(2^k-2\) rounds. Therefore, with
\[ H=\left\lfloor\log_2(T+1)\right\rfloor, \]
at most \(H\) phases have started. Accounting also for a partially completed final block yields
\[ R_N(T) \le M m\tau\,\frac{H(H+1)}2 + M(m-1)H + (K+M)A. \]

Every term on the right is independent of \(N\). For \(T\ge2\), we have \(H\le2\log T/\log2\) and \(H(H+1)/2\le H^2\). For example, the finite constant
\[ C= \frac{ 4Mm\tau+2M(m-1)+(K+M)A }{ (\log2)^2 } \]
therefore satisfies
\[ R_N(T)\le C\log^2T \]
for every exact replication \(N\) and every \(T\ge2\).

This proves the requested guarantee.

7. Running time

The policy is independent of the horizon and uses no optimization oracle.

In the usual arithmetic model for reward observations, each worker performs
\[ O\bigl(T+m\log m\,\log(T+1)\bigr) \]
operations through round \(T\): constant work per round and one ranking sort per phase. It stores \(O(m)\) numerical registers, together with counters and public input. Public rational arithmetic takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,\log N\).

A direct simulation of all workers and the admission mechanism takes
\[ O\bigl(NT+Nm\log m\,\log(T+1)+Tm\tau\bigr) \]
word operations, in addition to polynomial public-input processing. Applicants can be bucketed by arm and type, processed in the public priority order, and uniformly sampled within a partially admitted type. Bit complexity for reward arithmetic requires a numerical encoding of samples, which the question does not specify.

A frozen-ranking deferred-acceptance run completes within \(N(m-1)+1\) rounds. The regret proof uses its total normalized rejection count rather than this completion-time bound.
