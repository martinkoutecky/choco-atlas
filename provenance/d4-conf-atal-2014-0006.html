The continuous mirror question, as graded

For fixed \(0\leq\alpha<1\), given candidates \(C\), target \(p\), a valid supplied single-peaked partition \(P\) of width at most \(2\), and binary-rational registered and eligible masses \(\rho_r,\sigma_r\) for supported rankings \(r\), with total mass \(1\), is there a choice of \(x_r\in[0,\sigma_r]\) with \(\sum_r x_r\leq\beta\) such that \(p\) wins after adding the selected mass? For the resulting mass \(w_r=\rho_r+x_r\), pairwise support is \(M(a,b)=\sum_{r:a\succ_r b}w_r\), and \(\mathrm{Copeland}_\alpha(a)=|\{b:M(a,b)>M(b,a)\}|+\alpha|\{b:M(a,b)=M(b,a)\}|\); the input specifies unique-winner or nonunique-winner.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix a rational \(\alpha\) with \(0\leq\alpha<1\). Candidates form a nonempty set \(C\), with \(m=|C|\) and target \(p\in C\); types are strict linear rankings of \(C\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here the list is the union of registered and eligible supports: each ranking \(r\) has nonnegative binary-rational masses \(\rho_r,\sigma_r\), with \(\rho_r+\sigma_r>0\) and \(\sum_r(\rho_r+\sigma_r)=1\). Thus \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
The input supplies an ordered partition \(P=(C_1,\ldots,C_q)\) into nonempty blocks of size at most \(2\). Every block is contiguous in every supported ranking, and contracting each block to one alternative gives rankings single-peaked on the supplied axis \(C_1,\ldots,C_q\).
Given a nonnegative binary-rational budget \(\beta\), eligible mass may be divided: choose real \(x_r\in[0,\sigma_r]\) with \(\sum_r x_r\leq\beta\), leaving registered mass fixed and setting \(w_r=\rho_r+x_r\).
Define \(M(a,b)=\sum_{r:a\succ_r b}w_r\) and \(s_\alpha(a)=|\{b\in C\setminus\{a\}:M(a,b)>M(b,a)\}|+\alpha|\{b\in C\setminus\{a\}:M(a,b)=M(b,a)\}|\).
The input selects the unique-winner condition \(s_\alpha(p)>s_\alpha(a)\) for every \(a\neq p\), or the nonunique-winner condition \(s_\alpha(p)\geq s_\alpha(a)\) for every \(a\neq p\); no tie-breaking is applied. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including the budget.

For each fixed \(\alpha\), is deciding whether such an addition makes \(p\) win in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard, for each of the two winner models?

Discrete constructive control by adding votes under \(\mathrm{Copeland}_\alpha\) is NP-complete at single-peaked width \(2\), for both winner models. [Yang–Guo](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2014/aamas/p629.pdf)
Fractional Copeland profiles and unrestricted vote addition have been studied in an analysis of influence likelihoods. [Xia](https://minbiaohan.github.io/LSA/papers/LSA-2022_paper_3.pdf) No complexity classification for exactly the bounded eligible-mass problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted the fixed score parameter alpha to rational values to specify an ordinary binary computational model; the graded question did not specify its arithmetic domain.
  • Interpreted total mass one as the combined registered and eligible mass, rather than requiring the resulting election to retain mass one.
  • Defined support as the union of registered and eligible ranking supports, allowing either component mass to be zero.
  • Required the supplied partition to be valid for every ranking in that union, with nonempty contiguous blocks and a common supplied axis after contraction.
  • Specified a nonnegative binary-rational budget and real-valued divisible additions.
  • Made the candidate set nonempty and the target a member; allowed empty registered or eligible populations because the discrete definition imposes no positivity requirement.
  • Specified strict rankings and score-based winner conditions without tie-breaking, and defined total input bit length.

Result

For every fixed rational \(\alpha\) with \(0\leq\alpha<1\), the decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for both the unique-winner and nonunique-winner models.

The key is an integrality gadget: adding any positive mass of a certain type gives one block an additional strict pairwise win. That block then prevents \(p\) from winning unless its internal contest is tied; the tie equation forces the added mass to equal its full capacity.

1. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

For distinct candidates \(a,b\), write their pairwise margin as

\(\Delta_{ab}(x)=M(a,b)-M(b,a)\).

This is an affine function of the addition variables, with rational coefficients of polynomial encoding length.

Suppose a real feasible addition makes \(p\) win. Fix the resulting sign of every pairwise margin. Consider the following rational linear program, with variables \(x_r\) and \(\varepsilon\):

  • \(0\leq x_r\leq\sigma_r\), \(\sum_r x_r\leq\beta\), and \(0\leq\varepsilon\leq1\);
  • \(\Delta_{ab}(x)=0\) for each prescribed tie;
  • \(\Delta_{ab}(x)\geq\varepsilon\) for each prescribed positive margin;
  • \(-\Delta_{ab}(x)\geq\varepsilon\) for each prescribed negative margin.

Maximize \(\varepsilon\). The original addition gives a feasible point with \(\varepsilon>0\): take the minimum of \(1\) and the finitely many nonzero absolute margins. If every margin is zero, take \(\varepsilon=1\).

The feasible polytope is compact, so the positive optimum is attained at a vertex. After clearing denominators, its defining coefficients have polynomial bit length. Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound imply that every coordinate of this vertex has polynomial bit length. Thus there is a polynomial-size rational addition preserving all pairwise outcomes.

A verifier checks its capacities, budget, pairwise margins, and Copeland scores using exact rational arithmetic. This establishes membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) for both models.

2. Source problem and construction

We reduce from Exact Cover by 3-Sets, the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete source problem also used by [Yang–Guo](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2014/aamas/p629.pdf).

Let the universe be \(U=\{u_1,\ldots,u_k\}\), where \(k=3t\), and let the available triples be \(S_1,\ldots,S_n\). We may restrict to \(t,n\geq1\).

Initially describe masses without normalization; normalization follows below.

Candidate blocks and supplied axis

Introduce the following blocks:

  • Singleton blocks \(H_j=\{h_j\}\), for \(1\leq j\leq n\).
  • Singleton endpoint blocks \(A=\{a\}\) and \(Z=\{z\}\).
  • Variable blocks \(V_j=\{v_j^+,v_j^-\}\), for \(1\leq j\leq n\).
  • Element blocks \(E_i=\{e_i^+,e_i^-\}\), for \(1\leq i\leq k\).
  • Singleton blocks \(G_j=\{g_j\}\), for \(1\leq j\leq n+1\).
  • A target block \(B\), defined as \(B=\{p\}\) for the nonunique-winner reduction and \(B=\{p,p'\}\) for the unique-winner reduction.

Supply the block axis

\(\bigl(H_n,\ldots,H_1,A,V_1,\ldots,V_n,E_1,\ldots,E_k,B,Z,G_{n+1},G_n,\ldots,G_1\bigr)\).

Call the consecutive blocks from \(A\) through \(Z\) the *middle blocks*.

Contracted rankings

Define a left ranking of blocks by

\(T_{\mathrm L}: A\succ H_1\succ V_1\succ H_2\succ V_2\succ\cdots\succ H_n\succ V_n\succ E_1\succ\cdots\succ E_k\succ B\succ Z\succ G_{n+1}\succ\cdots\succ G_1.\)

For each \(j\), let \(T_j\) be obtained from \(T_{\mathrm L}\) by swapping the adjacent blocks \(H_j,V_j\).

Define the right ranking by

\(T_{\mathrm R}: Z\succ B\succ E_k\succ\cdots\succ E_1\succ G_{n+1}\succ V_n\succ G_n\succ V_{n-1}\succ G_{n-1}\succ\cdots\succ V_1\succ G_1\succ A\succ H_1\succ\cdots\succ H_n.\)

These are strict rankings of the blocks. Expand each block contiguously, using the internal orders specified next.

Registered and eligible types

There are two registered types, each of mass \(1\):

  • One has contracted ranking \(T_{\mathrm L}\).
  • One has contracted ranking \(T_{\mathrm R}\).

Both rank \(v_j^-\succ v_j^+\) in every variable block and \(e_i^-\succ e_i^+\) in every element block.

For each \(j\), introduce an eligible type of capacity \(1\), with contracted ranking \(T_j\). Its internal orders are:

  • \(v_j^+\succ v_j^-\) in \(V_j\), and \(v_\ell^-\succ v_\ell^+\) in every \(V_\ell\) with \(\ell\neq j\);
  • \(e_i^+\succ e_i^-\) if \(u_i\in S_j\), and \(e_i^-\succ e_i^+\) otherwise.

Finally introduce one eligible type of capacity \(t\), with contracted ranking \(T_{\mathrm R}\), ranking the \(+\) candidate first in every variable and element block.

In the unique-winner construction, every type ranks \(p\succ p'\).

The unnormalized budget is \(2t\).

Normalization and input size

The total registered-plus-eligible mass is \(D=2+n+t\). Divide every mass, capacity, and the budget by \(D\).

This gives exactly the normalization required in the question. All numbers have polynomial binary encoding length. There are \(n+3\) supported types and \(O(n+t)\) candidates, so the construction has polynomial size.

Multiplying every final mass by \(D>0\) preserves all pairwise outcomes. We therefore analyze the unnormalized masses.

3. Verification of the width-two promise

Every block has size at most \(2\), and expansion makes every block contiguous in every supported ranking.

For a strict block ranking with a specified peak, single-peakedness follows when the blocks on each side of the peak occur in order of increasing distance from it: the ranking may interleave the two sides, but must preserve each side’s order.

For \(T_{\mathrm L}\), the peak is \(A\). Its left-side subsequence is \(H_1,\ldots,H_n\), and its right-side subsequence is

\(V_1,\ldots,V_n,E_1,\ldots,E_k,B,Z,G_{n+1},\ldots,G_1\).

Both have the required order. Swapping \(H_j,V_j\) interchanges blocks from opposite sides of \(A\), preserving each side’s subsequence. Thus every \(T_j\) is also single-peaked with peak \(A\).

For \(T_{\mathrm R}\), the peak is \(Z\). Its left-side subsequence is

\(B,E_k,\ldots,E_1,V_n,\ldots,V_1,A,H_1,\ldots,H_n\),

and its right-side subsequence is \(G_{n+1},\ldots,G_1\). Again both have the required order.

Hence the supplied partition satisfies every promise in the task.

4. Winning forces equal left and right mass

Let \(z_j\in[0,1]\) be the mass added from eligible type \(T_j\), and let \(y\in[0,t]\) be the added mass of the right eligible type. Put \(S=\sum_{j=1}^n z_j\).

The total mass of types with peak \(A\) is \(1+S\); the total mass of types with peak \(Z\) is \(1+y\).

If \(S>y\), more than half the final mass ranks \(a\) first. Consequently \(a\) strictly defeats every other candidate. Its score is \(m-1\), whereas each other candidate loses to \(a\) and has score at most \(m-2\). Thus \(p\) cannot win.

If \(y>S\), the same argument makes \(z\) the unique winner.

Therefore every successful addition satisfies

\(y=S.\)

Under this equality, the left and right masses are equal. Every left type orders the middle blocks as

\(A\succ V_1\succ\cdots\succ V_n\succ E_1\succ\cdots\succ E_k\succ B\succ Z\),

while every right type orders them in reverse. Thus candidates in distinct middle blocks always tie.

5. External strict wins of middle blocks

Assume \(y=S\). For a middle block \(Q\), let \(d(Q)\) count the outside candidates that a candidate in \(Q\) strictly defeats. Contiguity makes this count independent of the candidate chosen in \(Q\).

Every right type ranks every middle block above every \(H_j\). Since the right mass is half the total, a middle block strictly defeats \(h_j\) precisely when some positive-mass left type ranks that block above \(H_j\); otherwise they tie.

Likewise, every left type ranks every middle block above every \(G_j\). The two right types have the same contracted ranking, and the registered right type has positive mass. Hence a middle block strictly defeats \(g_j\) precisely when \(T_{\mathrm R}\) ranks that block above \(G_j\).

The resulting counts are:

| Middle block \(Q\) | Strict wins against the \(H\) candidates | Strict wins against the \(G\) candidates | \(d(Q)\) |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(A\) | \(n\) | \(0\) | \(n\) |
| \(V_j\) | \(n-j+\mathbf 1_{\{z_j>0\}}\) | \(j\) | \(n+\mathbf 1_{\{z_j>0\}}\) |
| \(E_i\) | \(0\) | \(n+1\) | \(n+1\) |
| \(B\) | \(0\) | \(n+1\) | \(n+1\) |
| \(Z\) | \(0\) | \(n+1\) | \(n+1\) |

Here \(\mathbf 1_{\{z_j>0\}}\) equals \(1\) when \(z_j>0\), and \(0\) otherwise.

To verify the only variable-dependent entry, \(T_{\mathrm L}\) ranks \(V_j\) above exactly \(H_{j+1},\ldots,H_n\). Among all eligible left rankings, only \(T_j\) changes a comparison involving \(V_j\) and an \(H\) block: it places \(V_j\) above \(H_j\). This produces one additional strict win exactly when \(z_j>0\).

All remaining comparisons of a middle candidate with outside candidates are ties, rather than losses.

6. Score formula and internal margins

Define the common baseline \(K=\alpha(m-1)\).

A singleton middle block with external count \(d\) has score

\(K+(1-\alpha)d.\)

For a two-candidate middle block with external count \(d\):

  • If its internal contest is tied, both candidates have score \(K+(1-\alpha)d\).
  • If its internal contest is decisive, its internal winner has score \(K+(1-\alpha)(d+1)\).

Indeed, the decisive internal winner scores \(1\) internally, defeats \(d\) outside candidates, and ties its remaining \(m-2-d\) opponents. Its score is

\(1+d+\alpha(m-2-d)=K+(1-\alpha)(d+1).\)

The internal loser scores exactly \(1\) less than the internal winner.

Variable-block margins

The two registered masses contribute \(-2\) to the margin of \(v_j^+\) against \(v_j^-\). Eligible left types contribute \(2z_j-S\), and the right eligible type contributes \(y\). Therefore, using \(y=S\),

\(\Delta(v_j^+,v_j^-)=-2+2z_j-S+y=2(z_j-1).\)

Thus \(V_j\) is internally tied exactly when \(z_j=1\). It is decisive whenever \(0\leq z_j<1\).

Element-block margins

Let \(c_i=\sum_{j:u_i\in S_j}z_j\). The same calculation gives

\(\Delta(e_i^+,e_i^-)=-2+2c_i-S+y=2(c_i-1).\)

Thus \(E_i\) is internally tied exactly when \(c_i=1\).

Target scores

For the nonunique-winner construction, \(B\) is a singleton, so

\(s_\alpha(p)=K+(1-\alpha)(n+1).\)

For the unique-winner construction, \(p\) strictly defeats \(p'\), so

\(s_\alpha(p)=K+(1-\alpha)(n+2).\)

7. The gadget forces integrality and exact coverage

Since \(\alpha<1\), we have \(1-\alpha>0\).

Suppose \(0<z_j<1\). Then \(V_j\) has external count \(n+1\) and a decisive internal contest. Its internal winner has score

\(K+(1-\alpha)(n+2).\)

This exceeds \(p\)’s score in the nonunique-winner construction and equals \(p\)’s score in the unique-winner construction. Both outcomes prevent the requested kind of victory.

Consequently, every successful addition satisfies

\(z_j\in\{0,1\}\qquad\text{for every }j.\)

Next, every element block has external count \(n+1\). If \(c_i\neq1\), its internal contest is decisive, and its internal winner again has score \(K+(1-\alpha)(n+2)\). Hence every successful addition also satisfies

\(\sum_{j:u_i\in S_j}z_j=1\qquad\text{for every }i.\)

These equations, together with integrality, say that the triples with \(z_j=1\) cover every element exactly once. Summing the equations over the \(3t\) elements gives

\(3\sum_{j=1}^n z_j=3t,\)

because each triple contains three elements. Thus exactly \(t\) triples are selected, forming an exact cover.

This proves that a successful addition implies a yes-instance of Exact Cover by 3-Sets.

8. An exact cover makes \(p\) win

Conversely, suppose an exact cover exists. Set \(z_j=1\) for its triples and \(z_j=0\) for all other triples, and set \(y=t\).

Then \(S=y=t\). All capacities are respected, and the added mass is \(S+y=2t\), exactly the budget.

Every element block is internally tied. A selected variable block is internally tied and has external count \(n+1\). An unselected variable block is decisive and has external count \(n\). Consequently, every candidate in a variable or element block has score at most

\(K+(1-\alpha)(n+1).\)

The singleton endpoints \(A,Z\) have scores \(K+(1-\alpha)n\) and \(K+(1-\alpha)(n+1)\), respectively.

It remains to check the outside candidates:

  • No outside candidate strictly defeats a middle candidate, by the half-mass comparison established in Section 5.
  • Every \(H\) candidate ties every \(G\) candidate: all left types prefer the \(H\) candidate, and all right types prefer the \(G\) candidate.
  • An \(H\) candidate therefore has at most \(n-1\) strict wins, and a \(G\) candidate has at most \(n\).

For any candidate with \(w\) strict wins and \(\ell\) strict losses, its score is

\(K+(1-\alpha)w-\alpha\ell.\)

Thus every outside candidate has score at most \(K+(1-\alpha)n\), which is strictly below \(p\)’s score in both constructions.

In the nonunique-winner construction, all opponents therefore have score at most \(p\)’s score. In the unique-winner construction, all candidates outside \(B\) have strictly smaller scores than \(p\); its twin \(p'\) scores exactly \(1\) less than \(p\).

Hence the exact cover produces the required victory in each model.

9. Conclusion

The construction is a polynomial-time reduction satisfying the supplied width-two partition, contiguous blocks, explicit support representation, bounded divisible eligible masses, and normalization. It works for every fixed rational \(0\leq\alpha<1\).

Together with membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for both winner models.
