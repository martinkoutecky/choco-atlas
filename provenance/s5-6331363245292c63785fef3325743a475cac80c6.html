The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item categories, a unit-mass atomless population with finite support \(T\) of submodular valuation types \(v_t:2^{[m]}\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), and value oracles for each \(v_t\), does there exist a finite-round posted-price mechanism and polynomial-time useful advice, computable in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), such that each infinitesimal buyer receives an integral bundle and every measurable strategy profile fixed by the advice attains expected welfare within \(O((\log\log m)^3)\) of the continuous optimum \(\max_x \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_j\)?

An allocation gives each buyer one integral bundle, possibly empty; \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) denotes the mass of type \(t\) receiving \(S\). Define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_x\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), subject to \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_j\).

Buyers have quasilinear utility for their final bundle and payment, including the effects of rationing.

Partial result: a constant approximation for a single supported valuation

The general question remains unresolved. I prove a polynomial-time posted-price mechanism with polynomial-time useful advice that achieves

$$ \frac{127}{128}\left(1-\frac1e\right)\mathrm{OPT} $$

when the support contains one valuation, including arbitrary monotone submodular valuations and arbitrary rational supplies.

For heterogeneous types, the same mechanism has rigorous bounds depending on the smallest type mass and the smallest positive supply. An explicit example shows that this mechanism alone cannot establish the general target.

1. Statement and supply reduction

Set

$$ \beta_j=\min\{b_j,1\}, \qquad J=\{j:\beta_j>0\}. $$

Replacing $b_j$ by $\beta_j$ preserves feasibility because the population has total mass $1$. Removing categories outside $J$ preserves optimal welfare: the mass receiving any zero-supply category is zero, and there are finitely many categories.

If $J$ is empty, then $\mathrm{OPT}=0$, and the empty allocation suffices. Henceforth suppose $J$ is nonempty, and restrict every valuation to $2^J$.

Define

$$ \alpha=\frac{127}{128}, \qquad \mu_\star=\min_t\mu_t, \qquad \theta=\min_{j\in J}\beta_j. $$

The mechanism constructed below satisfies, for every measurable advice-following profile,

$$ \mathbb E[W] \ge \alpha\max\left\{ \theta,\, \left(1-\frac1e\right)\mu_\star \right\}\mathrm{OPT}. $$

In particular, when $\tau=1$,

$$ \mathbb E[W] \ge \alpha\left(1-\frac1e\right)\mathrm{OPT}. $$

These statements will be proved without using results from the previous notes.

2. A supply-feasible posted-price mechanism with useful advice

2.1. Finite random entitlements

Use the interval realization $[0,1)$ with Lebesgue measure, partitioned into measurable type classes of masses $\mu_t$. The construction uses buyers’ coordinates, not their types.

Let

$$ B=L+7, \qquad Q=2^B. $$

For every $j\in J$, independently draw an integer $K_j$ uniformly from

$$ \{0,\ldots,Q-1\}, $$

using exactly $B$ unbiased random bits. Put $U_j=K_j/Q$.

The entitlement set for category $j$ is the circular interval

$$ I_j = \left\{ a\in[0,1): (a-U_j)\bmod 1\in[0,\beta_j) \right\}. $$

Its measure is exactly $\beta_j$ in every realized outcome. Buyer $a$ has entitlement bundle

$$ E(a)=\{j\in J:a\in I_j\}. $$

Reveal the public random seed and each buyer’s entitlement before the buyer chooses a bundle.

Post price zero for every category in $J$. If buyer $a$ requests $R\subseteq J$, deliver

$$ D(a)=R\cap E(a). $$

The final payment is zero, including for unsuccessful requests.

Thus the mechanism offers common nonnegative additive prices and applies a preannounced measurable rationing rule.

2.2. Feasibility and complexity

For every measurable request profile and every realized seed,

$$ \int \mathbf 1_{\{j\in D(a)\}}\,da \le \int \mathbf 1_{\{a\in I_j\}}\,da = \beta_j \le b_j. $$

Every delivered bundle is integral. Feasibility holds independently of whether buyers follow advice.

Each circular interval has at most two endpoints. Consequently, all entitlement bundles can be represented by a partition with at most $2|J|+1$ interval cells. Sorting the endpoints and recording the bundle on each cell takes polynomially many operations.

The seed uses $|J|(L+7)$ bits. All interval endpoints have polynomial rational encoding length. The mechanism has one offer round, polynomial state size, and no need to enumerate response classes. Delivery requires only intersecting the requested bundle with the recorded entitlement bundle.

2.3. Advice and its dominance proof

At a buyer’s decision history $h$, the entitlement bundle $E$ is known. Define

$$ A(v,h,R) = \begin{cases} E,&v(E)>v(R\cap E),\\ R,&v(E)=v(R\cap E). \end{cases} $$

These are the only cases because monotonicity gives

$$ v(R\cap E)\le v(E). $$

Advice uses at most two value queries and retains indifferent tentative actions.

The advice is idempotent. If it replaces $R$ by $E$, applying it again compares $v(E)$ with itself and retains $E$. If it retains $R$, the same comparison retains $R$ again.

To prove usefulness at the strategy level, fix a buyer and a strategy $s$. Entitlements depend only on the public random seed. Other buyers’ actions affect neither this buyer’s delivery nor payment. At every history where advice changes the action, the buyer’s actual final utility strictly increases from

$$ v(s(h)\cap E) $$

to $v(E)$. At every other history, utility is unchanged.

For a fixed buyer, the decision histories are precisely those generated by the finitely many public seeds. Every such seed has positive probability. Therefore, if $A_v[s]\ne s$, the utility improvement is strict at at least one positive-probability history. Expected utility is consequently strictly higher against every measurable profile of other buyers. This implies the required dominance condition.

Thus the advice is useful. The finite seed also avoids relying on a change occurring only at a probability-zero history.

Finally, if a strategy follows advice, its final value at every decision history satisfies

$$ v(R\cap E)=v(E). $$

Hence every measurable advice-following profile achieves exactly the entitlement welfare, almost everywhere. This remains true when strategies differ within a type.

3. Two submodular expectation lemmas

For a normalized monotone submodular valuation $v$ on $J$, define

$$ F_v(p)=\mathbb E[v(R_p)], \qquad p\in[0,1]^J, $$

where $R_p$ includes categories independently, with inclusion probabilities $p_j$.

The mechanism does not evaluate $F_v$. It is used only in the proof.

Lemma 1: thinning preserves a proportional fraction of value

For every $p\in[0,1]^J$ and $\lambda\in[0,1]$,

$$ F_v(\lambda p)\ge \lambda F_v(p). $$

Proof. First sample $R_p$, then retain each category of $R_p$ independently with probability $\lambda$, obtaining $T$. Its distribution is that of $R_{\lambda p}$.

Condition on $R_p=R$, and list its categories in a fixed order $j_1,\ldots,j_k$. Let

$$ R_i=\{j_1,\ldots,j_i\}, \qquad R_0=\varnothing. $$

When $j_i$ is retained, its predecessors in $T$ form a subset of $R_{i-1}$. Diminishing marginal values therefore give

$$ v(T) \ge \sum_{i=1}^k \mathbf 1_{\{j_i\in T\}} \bigl(v(R_i)-v(R_{i-1})\bigr). $$

Taking conditional expectation and telescoping,

$$ \mathbb E[v(T)\mid R_p=R] \ge \lambda \sum_{i=1}^k \bigl(v(R_i)-v(R_{i-1})\bigr) = \lambda v(R). $$

Averaging over $R_p$ proves the claim. $\square$

We also use monotonicity of $F_v$: if $p\le q$ coordinatewise, couple the two random sets using common independent uniform random variables to obtain $R_p\subseteq R_q$, and then apply monotonicity of $v$.

Lemma 2: independent inclusion approximates any distribution with bounded marginals

Let $O$ be an arbitrary random bundle satisfying

$$ \Pr(j\in O)\le p_j \quad\text{for every }j\in J. $$

Then

$$ F_v(p) \ge \left(1-\frac1e\right)\mathbb E[v(O)]. $$

Proof. Write $H=\mathbb E[v(O)]$. For deterministic bundles $R,O$, monotonicity and submodularity imply

$$ v(O) \le v(R) + \sum_{j\in O} \bigl(v(R\cup\{j\})-v(R)\bigr). $$

Indeed, first enlarge $O$ to $O\cup R$, then add the categories in $O\setminus R$ to $R$. Each resulting marginal is at most its marginal at $R$.

For $s\in[0,1]$, sample $R_{sp}$ independently of $O$. Taking expectations gives

$$ H \le F_v(sp) + \sum_j p_j \mathbb E\bigl[v(R_{sp}\cup\{j\})-v(R_{sp})\bigr]. $$

The multilinear polynomial $F_v$ satisfies

$$ \mathbb E\bigl[v(R_{sp}\cup\{j\})-v(R_{sp})\bigr] = (1-sp_j)\frac{\partial F_v}{\partial p_j}(sp). $$

Every partial derivative is nonnegative by monotonicity. Thus

$$ H \le F_v(sp) + \sum_j p_j\frac{\partial F_v}{\partial p_j}(sp). $$

Set $G(s)=F_v(sp)$. The chain rule yields

$$ G'(s)+G(s)\ge H, \qquad G(0)=v(\varnothing)=0. $$

Multiplying by $e^s$ and integrating from $0$ to $1$,

$$ eG(1)\ge H(e-1). $$

Therefore,

$$ F_v(p)=G(1)\ge \left(1-\frac1e\right)H. $$

All functions differentiated here are finite multilinear polynomials, so the calculation also applies when some coordinates of $p$ are zero or one. $\square$

4. Welfare analysis

4.1. Marginals of the finite random entitlements

For a fixed buyer $a$, write

$$ q_j(a)=\Pr(a\in I_j). $$

A circular interval of length $\beta_j$ contains at least $\lfloor Q\beta_j\rfloor$ points of any equally spaced $Q$-point grid. Consequently,

$$ q_j(a) \ge \frac{\lfloor Q\beta_j\rfloor}{Q} \ge \beta_j-\frac1Q. $$

Every positive encoded rational supply is at least $2^{-L}$. Therefore,

$$ \frac1Q=2^{-(L+7)}\le \frac{\beta_j}{128}, $$

and hence

$$ q_j(a)\ge \alpha\beta_j. $$

For each fixed buyer, entitlement events across categories are independent because the seeds $K_j$ are independent. Thus, writing $F_t=F_{v_t}$,

$$ \mathbb E[v_t(E(a))] = F_t(q(a)) \ge F_t(\alpha\beta) \ge \alpha F_t(\beta). $$

The last two inequalities use monotonicity and Lemma 1.

All values are bounded because there are finitely many types and bundles. Integrating the preceding inequality, and using the advice guarantee from Section 2, gives

$$ \mathbb E[W] \ge \alpha\sum_t\mu_tF_t(\beta). $$

4.2. The single-valuation case

Suppose $\tau=1$.

An optimal configuration vector exists: its feasible region is a nonempty compact polytope with finitely many coordinates. Its coordinates define a random bundle $O$ satisfying

$$ \Pr(j\in O)\le\beta_j, \qquad \mathbb E[v(O)]=\mathrm{OPT}. $$

Lemma 2 gives

$$ F_v(\beta) \ge \left(1-\frac1e\right)\mathrm{OPT}. $$

Therefore,

$$ \mathbb E[W] \ge \frac{127}{128}\left(1-\frac1e\right)\mathrm{OPT}. $$

This factor is strictly greater than $1/2$. For example, $e>8/3$ implies

$$ \frac{127}{128}\left(1-\frac1e\right) > \frac{127}{128}\cdot\frac58 = \frac{635}{1024} > \frac12. $$

The same argument applies whenever all supported types have the same valuation: draw $O$ from the aggregate bundle distribution of an optimal allocation.

4.3. A bound for heterogeneous types

Fix an optimal configuration vector. Conditional on type $t$, let $O_t$ have distribution

$$ \Pr(O_t=S)=\frac{x_{t,S}}{\mu_t}. $$

Put

$$ y_{t,j}=\Pr(j\in O_t), \qquad H_t=\mathbb E[v_t(O_t)]. $$

Feasibility implies

$$ \mu_t y_{t,j} \le \sum_u\mu_u y_{u,j} \le \beta_j. $$

Thus, by monotonicity and Lemmas 1 and 2,

$$ F_t(\beta) \ge F_t(\mu_t y_t) \ge \mu_t F_t(y_t) \ge \mu_t\left(1-\frac1e\right)H_t. $$

It follows that

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[W] &\ge \alpha\left(1-\frac1e\right) \sum_t\mu_t^2H_t\\ &\ge \alpha\left(1-\frac1e\right) \mu_\star\sum_t\mu_tH_t\\ &= \alpha\left(1-\frac1e\right) \mu_\star\mathrm{OPT}. \end{aligned} $$

4.4. A bound when supplies are plentiful

Because $\beta_j\ge\theta$ for every $j\in J$,

$$ F_t(\beta) \ge F_t(\theta\mathbf 1) \ge \theta v_t(J), $$

where the second inequality is Lemma 1 applied to $p=\mathbf 1$.

Every feasible allocation has welfare at most $\sum_t\mu_tv_t(J)$. Hence

$$ \mathbb E[W] \ge \alpha\theta\sum_t\mu_tv_t(J) \ge \alpha\theta\,\mathrm{OPT}. $$

Combining this with Section 4.3 proves the general bound stated in Section 1.

5. Why this does not resolve the general question

The following example shows that the entitlement mechanism can have an arbitrarily small approximation ratio even with two categories and two types.

Let $\varepsilon=2^{-k}$, where $k\ge2$. There are two types:

  • Type $H$ has mass $\varepsilon$ and valuation

$$ v_H(S)= \begin{cases} 0,&S=\varnothing,\\ 1,&S\ne\varnothing. \end{cases} $$

  • Type $Z$ has mass $1-\varepsilon$ and valuation identically zero.

Both categories have supply $\varepsilon$. These valuations are normalized, monotone, and submodular: for $v_H$, adding a category has marginal value one at the empty set and zero once another category is present.

Assigning category $1$ to every type-$H$ buyer is feasible and yields welfare $\varepsilon$. No allocation can exceed that welfare because only those buyers have positive value. Thus

$$ \mathrm{OPT}=\varepsilon. $$

Here $Q\varepsilon$ is an integer. Each buyer therefore receives each category as an entitlement with probability exactly $\varepsilon$, independently across categories. Every advice-following profile has expected welfare

$$ \mathbb E[W] = \varepsilon\bigl(1-(1-\varepsilon)^2\bigr) = \varepsilon(2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2). $$

Its approximation ratio is

$$ \frac{\mathbb E[W]}{\mathrm{OPT}} = 2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2, $$

which tends to zero.

This is a limitation of the constructed mechanism, not an impossibility result for the mechanism class in the question.

6. Proven progress and remaining question

The construction establishes a new constant guarantee for arbitrary submodular valuations when the support contains one valuation. Its advice is useful with respect to actual final utility, its finite randomness and state have polynomial size, and its supplies are respected in every outcome.

For the full task, the unresolved issue is serving heterogeneous types efficiently when valuable types and relevant supplies may have arbitrarily small masses. The bounds above do not control that case by a function of $m$ alone.
