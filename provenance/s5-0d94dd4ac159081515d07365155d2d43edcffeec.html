The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given qualified candidates \(C\), spoiler candidates \(D\), target \(p\in C\), action limit \(k\), and a finite support \(T\) of complete fallback ballot types over \(C\cup D\), with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), decide whether some \(D'\subseteq D\), \(|D'|\le k\), makes \(p\) the unique fallback winner. For each proposed \(D'\), restrict ballots to \(C\cup D'\), then compute level scores from the masses of types that approve each candidate within their first \(i\) approved positions; use the paper's strict-majority threshold \(1/2\), and its total-approval fallback if no level has a strict majority.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given disjoint finite candidate sets \(C,D\), a target \(p\in C\), and a positive integer \(k\) encoded in binary, put \(m=|C\cup D|\).
A ballot type specifies an approval subset \(A_t\subseteq C\cup D\), possibly empty, and a strict linear order on \(A_t\); all remaining candidates are disapproved and unranked.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For \(D'\subseteq D\), restrict each ballot to \(E=C\cup D'\) by removing absent candidates and retaining approval status and relative order; approved positions are then consecutive.
For \(a\in E\), its level score \(s_i(a)\) is the mass approving it within the first \(i\) approved positions, and its total approval score \(s(a)\) is the mass approving it.
At the smallest level \(1\le i\le |E|\) where some \(s_i(a)>1/2\), all candidates maximizing \(s_i\) win. If no such level exists, all candidates maximizing \(s\) win. Ties are retained.
The decision problem asks whether some \(D'\subseteq D\) with \(|D'|\le k\) makes \(p\) the unique winner.

Is this rational-mass decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? If it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, is it \(\mathrm{FPT}\) parameterized by \(k\)? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,k\).

For finite unweighted electorates, this control problem is [\(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1004.3398) and [\(\mathrm{W}[2]\)-hard parameterized by \(k\)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1004.3659).

Classification

The rational-mass problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete and $\mathrm{W}[2]$-hard parameterized by $k$. Consequently, it is not fixed-parameter tractable unless $\mathrm{FPT}=\mathrm{W}[2]$.

The proof consists of a parameter-preserving reduction from the finite-electorate problem and a polynomial-time verifier using exact arithmetic. No unconditional separation of these complexity classes is asserted.

1. Normalizing an explicit electorate preserves every outcome

Consider a finite unweighted instance with $N>0$ explicitly listed voters. Group identical ballots into distinct types $t$, and let $n_t$ be the multiplicity of type $t$. Construct the rational society with the same candidate sets, target, and budget, assigning mass

$$ \mu_t=\frac{n_t}{N}. $$

Every listed mass is positive, and their sum is $1$. The number of types satisfies $\tau\le N$. Each numerator and denominator requires $O(\log(N+1))$ bits, so grouping and normalization take polynomial time and produce an instance of polynomial length.

Fix any $D'\subseteq D$, and put $E=C\cup D'$. Let $v_i^E(a)$ denote the number of original voters approving $a$ within the first $i$ approved positions after restriction to $E$. Let $v^E(a)$ denote its total approval count. By construction,

$$ s_i^E(a)=\frac{v_i^E(a)}{N}, \qquad s^E(a)=\frac{v^E(a)}{N}. $$

These identities hold even if distinct original types become identical after restriction: their contributions simply add. Therefore,

$$ s_i^E(a)>\frac12 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_i^E(a)>\frac N2. $$

Division by the same positive number also preserves every score comparison and tie. Thus the two instances have the same first majority level and exactly the same winner set. If no majority level exists, they have the same approval-score maximizers.

This proves, simultaneously for every permitted $D'$,

$$ p\text{ is the unique winner in the original instance} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\text{ is the unique winner in the rational instance}. $$

The transformation leaves $k$ unchanged.

2. Transferring the hardness results

The finite-electorate results used here concern exactly constructive control by adding at most $k$ candidates, with success requiring the target to be the unique winner:

  • Theorem 3.7 of [*Control Complexity in Fallback Voting*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1004.3398) proves $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness.
  • Theorem 3.1 of [the parameterized-complexity paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1004.3659) proves $\mathrm{W}[2]$-hardness with parameter $k$; its Section 2 explicitly specifies the unique-winner model and positive budgets.

Their ballot and winner conventions agree with the task: only approved candidates are ranked, restriction retains approvals and relative order, majority is strict, and winning ties are retained.

Composing either hardness reduction with the normalization above gives a reduction to the stated rational-mass problem. Polynomial construction and preservation of yes-instances prove $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness. Since the normalization also preserves $k$, it is an FPT reduction and transfers $\mathrm{W}[2]$-hardness.

In particular, an algorithm with running time $f(k)L^c$, where $f$ is computable and $c$ is independent of $k$, would imply $\mathrm{FPT}=\mathrm{W}[2]$.

3. Exact verification and membership in $\mathrm{NP}$

Write each input mass as

$$ \mu_t=\frac{a_t}{b_t}, \qquad a_t,b_t\text{ positive integers}, $$

and define

$$ Q=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t, \qquad w_t=a_t\frac{Q}{b_t}. $$

The weights $w_t$ are integers satisfying

$$ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}w_t=Q. $$

The bit length of $Q$ is $O(L)$, because the sum of the input denominators’ bit lengths is at most $L$. Every $w_t$ and every score sum is at most $Q$, hence also has $O(L)$ bits. Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, computing $Q$ and all $w_t$ takes

$$ O(\tau L^2) $$

bit operations.

For a proposed $D'$, put $E=C\cup D'$ and $n=|E|\le m$. Scan each approved order, removing absent candidates and assigning consecutive positions to the retained candidates. Define

$$ H_{a,j} = \sum_{\substack{t:\ a\text{ has approved}\\ \text{position }j\text{ after restriction}}}w_t. $$

Building this table requires at most $O(\tau m)$ additions. Its prefix sums give integer level scores

$$ S_i(a)=\sum_{j=1}^{i}H_{a,j}=Q\,s_i(a). $$

Computing all prefix sums and inspecting every candidate at every level requires $O(m^2)$ additions or comparisons. Each costs $O(L)$ bit operations. Majority is checked exactly by

$$ 2S_i(a)>Q. $$

At the first majority level, accept precisely when $S_i(p)$ strictly exceeds every competing score. If no majority occurs, apply the same strict comparison to $S_n$, which equals the scaled total approval score.

Thus, after preprocessing the weights, verification takes

$$ O\bigl((\tau m+m^2)L\bigr) $$

bit operations. Including preprocessing, the bound is

$$ O\bigl(\tau L^2+(\tau m+m^2)L\bigr). $$

A certificate is a bit vector specifying $D'\subseteq D$. Its length is at most $m$, and its cardinality can be compared with the binary-encoded $k$ in polynomial time. This proves membership in $\mathrm{NP}$ and, together with Section 2, $\mathrm{NP}$-completeness.

4. Deterministic running time and the parameterized conclusion

Let

$$ d=|D|, \qquad K=\min\{k,d\}, \qquad B(d,K)=\sum_{j=0}^{K}\binom dj. $$

Enumerating all permitted subsets and verifying each, while computing the integer weights only once, gives the deterministic bound

$$ O\left( \tau L^2+ B(d,K)(\tau m+m^2)L \right). $$

We have

$$ B(d,K)\le 2^d \qquad\text{and}\qquad B(d,K)\le(d+1)^K\le m^K. $$

For the second bound, index the spoilers by $1,\ldots,d$ and encode each subset by its increasing list padded to length $K$ with zeros. This is an injection into the length-$K$ sequences over an alphabet of size $d+1$. The last inequality uses $p\in C$, so $d+1\le m$. These bounds also hold when $K=0$.

Consequently, a bound expressed only in the requested quantities is

$$ O\left( \tau L^2+ \min\left\{2^m,m^{\min\{k,m\}}\right\} (\tau m+m^2)L \right). $$

For each fixed $k$, this is polynomial, giving an $\mathrm{XP}$ algorithm. Its exponent depends on $k$, so this enumeration does not establish FPT. The transferred $\mathrm{W}[2]$-hardness excludes an FPT algorithm under the standard assumption $\mathrm{FPT}\ne\mathrm{W}[2]$.
