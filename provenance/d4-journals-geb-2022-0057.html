The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over ranking types and a normalized quota \(q\in(1/(k+1),1/k]\), let \(M(S)\) be the mass of types whose \(|S|\)-prefix is exactly \(S\). Run MD by adding candidates to satisfy demands \(\min\{\lfloor M(S)/q\rfloor,|S|\}\); does this mass-based procedure terminate with a committee of size \(k\), and are its possible outputs exactly the committees satisfying mass-PSC?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge 1\) candidates, let \(1\le k\le m\) be an integer, and let the rational quota satisfy \(q\in(1/(k+1),1/k]\). Committees are subsets of candidates, with no fractional membership.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) strict ranking types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_r\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_r\mu_r=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L,k\).
The \(j\)-prefix \(P_j(r)\) is the unordered set of the first \(j\) candidates in ranking \(r\); for nonempty \(S\subseteq C\), put \(M(S)=\sum_{r:P_{|S|}(r)=S}\mu_r\).
A coalition is a vector of submasses \(0\le a_r\le\mu_r\). It solidly supports \(S\) if every ranking with \(a_r>0\) places every candidate in \(S\) above every candidate outside \(S\). A size-\(k\) committee \(W\) satisfies mass-PSC if, for every nonempty \(S\subseteq C\), every positive integer \(\ell\), and every such coalition with \(\sum_r a_r\ge\ell q\), one has \(|W\cap S|\ge\min\{\ell,|S|\}\).
Mass-MD starts with \(W=\varnothing\) and processes stages \(j=1,\ldots,m\) in order. At stage \(j\), while some occurring \(j\)-prefix \(S\) has \(|W\cap S|<\min\{\lfloor M(S)/q\rfloor,|S|\}\), choose any such \(S\) and any \(c\in S\setminus W\), and add \(c\) to \(W\).
Advance only when no demand at that stage remains unmet; output after stage \(m\), including when \(k\) candidates have already been selected. Possible outputs range over all choices of deficient sets and candidates.
For every admissible input, does every mass-MD execution terminate with exactly \(k\) candidates, and are its possible outputs exactly the size-\(k\) mass-PSC committees, or is there a counterexample to either property?
For finite electorates, every MD execution terminates with exactly \(k\) candidates, and its possible outputs are exactly the PSC committees. [Aziz and Lee](https://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~haziz/dummett.pdf)

Result

Both properties hold for every admissible input. Every mass-MD execution terminates with exactly \(k\) candidates, and its possible outputs are precisely the size-\(k\) mass-PSC committees.

The proof uses an exact reduction to a finite electorate. Allowing fractional coalition submasses introduces no additional demands.

1. Mass-PSC is equivalent to prefix inequalities

For each nonempty \(S\subseteq C\), define
\(d(S)=\min\{\lfloor M(S)/q\rfloor,|S|\}\).

We first prove that a committee \(W\) satisfies mass-PSC if and only if
\(|W\cap S|\ge d(S)\)
for every nonempty \(S\subseteq C\).

Because rankings are strict, a ranking \(r\) places every member of \(S\) above every candidate outside \(S\) exactly when \(P_{|S|}(r)=S\). Consequently, every coalition solidly supporting \(S\) has total mass at most \(M(S)\). This maximum is attained by taking
\(a_r=\mu_r\) when \(P_{|S|}(r)=S\), and \(a_r=0\) otherwise.

For necessity, suppose \(W\) satisfies mass-PSC. Put \(h=\lfloor M(S)/q\rfloor\). If \(h=0\), the required inequality is automatic. Otherwise, the maximal supporting coalition has mass \(M(S)\ge hq\), so mass-PSC, applied with \(\ell=h\), gives
\(|W\cap S|\ge\min\{h,|S|\}=d(S)\).

For sufficiency, suppose all the prefix inequalities hold. If a coalition solidly supporting \(S\) has mass at least \(\ell q\), then \(M(S)\ge\ell q\), and hence \(\lfloor M(S)/q\rfloor\ge\ell\). Therefore
\(|W\cap S|\ge d(S)\ge\min\{\ell,|S|\}\),
as required.

Nonoccurring sets have \(M(S)=0\) and impose no constraint. Thus there are at most \(m\tau\) relevant prefix sets.

2. Exact scaling to a finite electorate

Write the input rationals as
\(\mu_r=u_r/v_r\) and \(q=a/b\),
with positive integer denominators. Choose
\(D=b\prod_{r=1}^{\tau}v_r\),
and define
\(n_r=D\mu_r\), \(n=D\), and \(Q=Dq\).

All these quantities are positive integers. Construct a finite electorate containing exactly \(n_r\) voters with ranking \(r\). Its total number of voters is
\(\sum_r n_r=D\sum_r\mu_r=D=n\).

The quota satisfies
\(n/(k+1)<Q\le n/k\),
because multiplying the assumed inequalities for \(q\) by \(D\) preserves them. The candidate set, strict rankings, and committee size are unchanged. Thus this finite instance satisfies the hypotheses of the finite-electorate MD theorem.

For every nonempty \(S\subseteq C\), let \(N(S)\) be the set of voters whose top-\(|S|\) set is \(S\). Then
\(|N(S)|=DM(S)\),
and therefore
\(\lfloor |N(S)|/Q\rfloor=\lfloor M(S)/q\rfloor\).

In the finite electorate, \(N(S)\) is the maximal solid coalition supporting \(S\). Applying the same maximal-coalition argument as in Section 1, finite \(Q\)-PSC is equivalent to
\(|W\cap S|\ge\min\{\lfloor |N(S)|/Q\rfloor,|S|\}\)
for every nonempty \(S\).

The displayed floor identity proves that finite \(Q\)-PSC and mass-PSC select exactly the same committees. This argument does not require an arbitrary fractional coalition to correspond to an integer voter subset.

3. Every execution is preserved

At stage \(j\), the occurring prefix sets are identical in the two instances: every supported ranking has \(n_r>0\), and no new ranking was introduced.

For any such prefix \(S\), its finite demand is
\(\min\{\lfloor |N(S)|/Q\rfloor,|S|\}=d(S)\).

Consequently, at every state \((j,W)\), both procedures have exactly the same deficient sets. After choosing a deficient \(S\), both permit exactly the candidates in \(S\setminus W\). Their conditions for advancing a stage and for terminating are also identical.

Hence every mass-MD execution corresponds, choice for choice, to a finite MD execution, and conversely. This correspondence includes processing the remaining stages after \(k\) candidates have been selected.

Aziz and Lee’s Lemma 1 establishes that every finite MD execution terminates with exactly \(k\) candidates; their Theorem 1 establishes that its possible outputs are exactly the PSC committees. Their procedure processes all stages, including after selecting \(k\) candidates. [Aziz and Lee, *A characterization of proportionally representative committees*, Lemma 1 and Theorem 1](https://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~haziz/dummett.pdf).

Applying those results to the scaled instance and using the two correspondences above proves both asserted properties.

4. Computation directly from the support

The scaled electorate need not be explicitly constructed. Although \(D\) can be exponentially large in value, its binary length is \(O(L)\), since its factors are input denominators.

Here is one elementary implementation and its bit-complexity bound.

Represent prefix sets by \(m\)-bit incidence vectors. At each stage, group equal prefixes by comparing each against previously encountered groups. Across all stages, this costs \(O(m^2\tau^2)\) bit operations. Sum the integer weights \(n_r\) in each group and compute its demand by dividing that sum by \(Q\). All operands have \(O(L)\) bits, so elementary integer arithmetic gives a total arithmetic cost of \(O(m\tau L^2)\), including denominator clearing.

During execution, rescan the current stage’s at most \(\tau\) groups after each addition and once to finish the stage. There are \(k\) additions and \(m\) stage completions. Computing each intersection size from incidence vectors costs \(O(m)\), giving \(O(m\tau(m+k))\) further bit operations.

Thus one execution can be computed in
\(O(m^2\tau^2+m\tau L^2+m\tau(m+k))\)
bit operations, directly from the supported rankings.

The same prefix table permits verification of a supplied size-\(k\) committee’s mass-PSC property in polynomial time. These bounds concern one execution or one committee verification; they make no assertion that all possible outputs can be enumerated in polynomial time.
