Theorem E.2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\), a finite catalogue \(\mathcal T\) of complete binary-XOS types \(v_t(S)=\max_{r\in R_t}|S\cap A_{t,r}|\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita item supplies \(q_j\), and \(k\ge2\), compute a feasible mass assignment \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_j\), with \(W(x)\ge(1/2-1/k)W^*(\mu,q)\), where \(W(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) and \(W^*\) is the optimum. Each unit of mass receives an integral bundle; only the aggregate assignment is fractional.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(\mathcal T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) items. Each type is specified by a nonempty, explicitly listed finite family \((A_{t,r})_{r\in R_t}\) of subsets of \([m]\), defining its complete valuation \(v_t(S)=\max_{r\in R_t}|S\cap A_{t,r}|\); empty clauses are allowed.
Inputs also include nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\) and an integer \(k\ge2\), encoded in unary. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including all clause descriptions.
An assignment has rational masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) with \(\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_j\). Each unit of mass receives an integral bundle, possibly empty; unused supply is allowed.
Write \(W(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) and \(W^*(\mu,q)=\max_x W(x)\) over feasible assignments. Represent an assignment by its nonzero entries, with all omitted entries zero.

Can a feasible assignment satisfying \(W(x)\ge(1/2-1/k)W^*(\mu,q)\) be computed in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,k)\), or is achieving this approximation guarantee NP-hard?

For finitely many binary-XOS bidders and one copy of each item, a randomized sequential communication protocol guarantees expected welfare at least \((1/2-1/k)\) times optimum; this is an approximation theorem, without a polynomial-time computation guarantee. Modeling choices made when packaging the task

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, even exactly. The explicitly listed clauses give a compact linear program, and any solution of this program can be converted into a feasible assignment with polynomially many nonzero entries and exactly the same welfare.

Thus the requested approximation guarantee holds for every \(k\ge 2\), with running time independent of \(k\).

1. A useful property of the valuations

For every type \(t\) and bundle \(S\), the definition gives \(v_t(S)\le |S|\).

If \(S\subseteq A_{t,r}\) for some clause \(r\), that clause attains \(|S|\), so
\[ v_t(S)=|S|. \]

Moreover, choose a maximizing clause \(r\) for an arbitrary bundle \(S\), and set \(B=S\cap A_{t,r}\). Then
\[ v_t(B)=|B|=v_t(S). \]
Replacing \(S\) by \(B\) therefore preserves welfare and can only decrease supply usage.

This permits us to focus on bundles contained in a selected clause.

2. A compact linear program

Introduce the following variables:

  • \(y_{t,r}\ge 0\): the mass of type \(t\) associated with clause \(r\);
  • \(z_{t,r,j}\ge 0\), for \(j\in A_{t,r}\): the mass associated with that clause receiving item \(j\).

Consider the linear program
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad &\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\sum_{r\in R_t}\sum_{j\in A_{t,r}}z_{t,r,j}\\ \text{subject to}\quad &\sum_{r\in R_t}y_{t,r}=\mu_t &&(t\in\mathcal T),\\ &0\le z_{t,r,j}\le y_{t,r} &&(t\in\mathcal T,\ r\in R_t,\ j\in A_{t,r}),\\ &\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\sum_{\substack{r\in R_t\\j\in A_{t,r}}}z_{t,r,j}\le q_j &&(j\in[m]),\\ &y_{t,r}\ge 0 &&(t\in\mathcal T,\ r\in R_t). \end{aligned} \]
Write \(P^*\) for its optimal value. We will prove \(P^*=W^*(\mu,q)\).

3. Every assignment gives an LP solution of equal value

Let \(x\) be any feasible assignment. For each pair \((t,S)\), choose a clause \(r(t,S)\) attaining \(v_t(S)\). Define
\[ y_{t,r}=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[m]\\r(t,S)=r}}x_{t,S}, \qquad z_{t,r,j}=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[m]\\r(t,S)=r,\ j\in S}}x_{t,S} \quad(j\in A_{t,r}). \]

The type-mass equalities follow because each bundle is associated with exactly one clause. Also, \(0\le z_{t,r,j}\le y_{t,r}\).

For each item \(j\), the sum of its \(z\)-variables counts only some of the original assignments receiving \(j\). Consequently,
\[ \sum_{t,r:\,j\in A_{t,r}}z_{t,r,j} \le \sum_{t,S:\,j\in S}x_{t,S} \le q_j. \]
Thus the LP solution is feasible.

Its objective is
\[ \sum_{t,r}\sum_{j\in A_{t,r}}z_{t,r,j} =\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}|S\cap A_{t,r(t,S)}| =\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S) =W(x). \]
Therefore \(P^*\ge W^*(\mu,q)\).

4. Every LP solution gives a sparse assignment of equal value

Fix a feasible rational LP solution \((y,z)\). We reconstruct bundles separately for each pair \((t,r)\).

Suppose \(A_{t,r}=\{j_1,\ldots,j_n\}\), ordered so that
\[ z_{t,r,j_1}\ge z_{t,r,j_2}\ge\cdots\ge z_{t,r,j_n}. \]
For this pair, abbreviate \(y=y_{t,r}\), \(z_i=z_{t,r,j_i}\), and set \(z_{n+1}=0\).

If \(n\ge 1\), form the prefix bundles \(B_0=\varnothing\) and \(B_i=\{j_1,\ldots,j_i\}\) for \(1\le i\le n\). Assign the masses
\[ \beta_0=y-z_1, \qquad \beta_i=z_i-z_{i+1}\quad(1\le i\le n) \]
to these bundles. All masses are nonnegative, because the coordinates are sorted and \(z_1\le y\). Their sum is
\[ \sum_{i=0}^{n}\beta_i=y-z_1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(z_i-z_{i+1})=y. \]

The mass receiving item \(j_\ell\) is exactly
\[ \sum_{i=\ell}^{n}\beta_i =\sum_{i=\ell}^{n}(z_i-z_{i+1}) =z_\ell. \]
Hence the reconstruction realizes every prescribed item marginal.

Every \(B_i\) is contained in \(A_{t,r}\), so Section 1 gives \(v_t(B_i)=i\). Its welfare contribution is therefore
\[ \sum_{i=0}^{n}\beta_i v_t(B_i) =\sum_{i=1}^{n}i(z_i-z_{i+1}) =\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_i. \]
The last equality follows by telescoping: each \(z_i\) has coefficient \(i-(i-1)=1\).

If the clause is empty, assign its entire mass \(y\) to the empty bundle. This realizes its zero marginals and zero objective contribution.

Perform this construction for all pairs \((t,r)\), merge repeated entries \((t,S)\) by adding their masses, and discard zero entries. Then:

  • each type receives total mass \(\sum_r y_{t,r}=\mu_t\);
  • each item’s usage equals the corresponding sum of \(z\)-variables and is at most its supply;
  • welfare equals the LP objective.

This proves \(W^*(\mu,q)\ge P^*\). Together with Section 3,
\[ W^*(\mu,q)=P^*. \]

5. Running time, encoding, and the guarantee

Let
\[ C=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}|R_t|, \qquad I=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\sum_{r\in R_t}|A_{t,r}|. \]
The LP has \(C+I\) variables and polynomially many constraints. Because every clause is explicitly listed, its entire encoding has size polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The LP is feasible: select one clause for each type, assign it mass \(\mu_t\), and set all \(z\)-variables to zero. Its feasible region is bounded, since every variable satisfies \(0\le z_{t,r,j}\le y_{t,r}\le\mu_t\). All coefficients are rational with polynomial encoding length.

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore supplies an exact optimal rational solution with polynomial encoding length.

The reconstruction uses at most
\[ \sum_{t,r}(|A_{t,r}|+1)=I+C \]
entries before merging. Sorting, rational subtraction, and merging take polynomial time; the resulting masses also have polynomial encoding length.

The algorithm consequently outputs a feasible sparse rational assignment \(x\) satisfying \(W(x)=W^*(\mu,q)\). Since \(W^*(\mu,q)\ge0\) and \(k\ge2\), it satisfies the requested inequality
\[ W(x)\ge(1/2-1/k)W^*(\mu,q). \]

The variables and constraints are well defined. In particular, \(z_{t,r,j}\le y_{t,r}\) is necessary for the proposed interpretation of item marginals.

Empty clauses introduce a \(y\)-variable and no \(z\)-variables. They therefore remain valid options for allocating type mass.

Choosing one maximizing clause for each pair \((t,S)\) is legitimate, including ties.

The type-mass equalities and bounds \(0\le z_{t,r,j}\le y_{t,r}\) follow from the definitions. For each item, the LP counts only original recipients whose selected clause contains that item, so the supply inequality has the correct direction.

The objective calculation counts exactly \(|S\cap A_{t,r(t,S)}|=v_t(S)\) for every original bundle. Thus every feasible assignment yields a feasible LP point of equal welfare, establishing \(P^*\ge W^*(\mu,q)\).

This conversion is a mathematical comparison of optima; the algorithm does not need to enumerate all bundles.

4. LP-to-assignment conversion

For a feasible rational LP solution, the sorted coordinates satisfy \(y\ge z_1\ge\cdots\ge z_n\ge0\). Consequently, every proposed mass is nonnegative.

The displayed telescoping calculations are correct:

  • The bundle masses sum to \(y\).
  • Item \(j_\ell\) receives total mass \(z_\ell\).
  • The welfare contribution equals \(\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_i\).

Each prefix lies inside the selected clause, so its valuation is exactly its cardinality under the complete valuation, regardless of other clauses.

The empty-clause case is handled explicitly. Zero clause mass, zero marginals, and tied coordinates cause no difficulty: they simply produce zero entries where appropriate.

Combining the constructions preserves type masses and realizes exactly the LP’s item marginals. Merging repeated bundles preserves welfare because the entries have the same type and bundle.

Therefore the reconstruction is valid and establishes the claimed equality \(W^*(\mu,q)=P^*\).

Because the families and their clauses are explicitly listed, both the clause count \(C\) and total incidence count \(I\) are polynomially bounded by the input description. This remains true for empty clauses: each is explicitly represented.

The LP has \(C+I\) variables and polynomially many constraints. Its coefficients and right-hand sides have polynomial binary encoding length.

The supplied feasibility argument is valid because every type has at least one clause. Boundedness follows from nonnegativity and \(\sum_r y_{t,r}=\mu_t\), together with \(z_{t,r,j}\le y_{t,r}\).

The invoked rational linear-programming theorem provides an exact rational optimum in polynomial time for such an explicitly encoded, feasible, bounded rational LP, with polynomial output encoding length. Its hypotheses are satisfied here.

The reconstruction creates at most \(I+C\) entries before merging. Each bundle has at most \(m\) items. Sorting, subtraction, and merging are polynomial-time operations on polynomial-length rationals; adding polynomially many such masses also retains polynomial encoding length.

The algorithm obtains \(W(x)=W^*(\mu,q)\). Valuations and assignment masses are nonnegative, so \(W^*(\mu,q)\ge0\). For every \(k\ge2\), the requested factor is at most one, proving the requested inequality.

The case \(k=2\), zero optimum, zero supplies, and types consisting entirely of empty clauses are all covered.
