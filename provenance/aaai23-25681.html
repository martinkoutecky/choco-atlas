The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\lambda_r\) of recipient types \(r\) with budgets \(B_r\), and rational supplies \(\eta_q\) of indivisible item types \(q\) with sizes \(s_q\) and values \(v_q\), find rational masses \(y_{r,b}\) over integral bundles \(b \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^Q\) satisfying \(s \cdot b \le B_r\), \(\sum_b y_{r,b} = \lambda_r\), and \(\sum_{r,b} y_{r,b} b_q \le \eta_q\). Treat unused supply as charity and require that every occupied bundle \(b\) of every recipient type is EF2 against every occupied bundle and every fitting integral charity subbundle, using the paper's additive-value envy predicate; decide whether such an allocation exists or output one.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) recipient types, each with positive binary-rational mass \(\lambda_r\), with \(\sum_r\lambda_r=1\), and positive rational budget \(B_r\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) item types \(q\), with positive binary-rational supplies \(\eta_q\), sizes \(s_q\), and values \(v_q\). All recipients share the additive valuation \(V(b)=\sum_qv_qb_q\); write \(S(b)=\sum_qs_qb_q\). Supplies need not sum to one, and equal value-to-size ratios are allowed.
An allocation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(y_{r,b}\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\) with \(S(b)\leq B_r\), satisfying \(\sum_b y_{r,b}=\lambda_r\) and \(\sum_{r,b}y_{r,b}b_q\leq\eta_q\). Its charity vector is \(c_q=\eta_q-\sum_{r,b}y_{r,b}b_q\); a bundle is occupied when \(y_{r,b}>0\).
EF2 means that for every occupied \((r,b)\) and every integral \(f\) with \(S(f)\leq B_r\) that satisfies either \(f\leq b'\) for some occupied bundle \(b'\), or \(f\leq c\), there exists integral \(d\) with \(0\leq d\leq f\), \(\sum_qd_q\leq2\), and \(V(b)\geq V(f-d)\). Vector inequalities are componentwise; removals count individual copies, including copies of the same type. Comparisons include occupied bundles of the same recipient type.

Is deciding whether such a rational EF2 allocation exists in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, where \(L\) is the total binary input length?

For explicitly listed finite agents and indivisible goods, an EF2 allocation always exists and is computable in polynomial time ([Barman et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.08168)).
For divisible goods and finitely many agents, feasible envy-freeness with charity can coexist with Pareto optimality ([Barman, Caragiannis, and Shyam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00411)); this concerns divisible bundles, rather than distributions over integral bundles. No result settling the precise mass model above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized recipient masses to sum to one, interpreting the society as a distribution; supplies remain unnormalized positive rational quantities.
  • Required nonempty recipient and item supports and strictly positive budgets, sizes, and values, following the discrete domains.
  • Restored comparisons against every budget-fitting integral subbundle of an occupied bundle, rather than only against the entire bundle.
  • Defined an integral charity subbundle by componentwise domination by the residual rational supply vector.
  • Made EF2 removals count at most two individual copies, possibly of the same item type; bundles with fewer than two items satisfy the condition automatically.
  • Applied comparisons across all occupied bundles, including those assigned to the same recipient type.
  • Allowed equal densities because the full version establishes that distinct densities are only an auxiliary algorithmic assumption.
  • Used the existence decision problem for the two-sided complexity target and defined total binary input length explicitly.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\): every valid input admits a rational EF2 allocation.

The finite-agent existence theorem transfers to this mass model by clearing denominators. The essential point is that normalization makes the charity vector componentwise smaller, so every required charity comparison remains covered.

Finite-agent theorem used

For finitely many agents and individual goods with positive rational sizes and values, identical additive valuations, and positive rational agent budgets, there exists a feasible allocation satisfying the following property: for every agent \(a\) and every budget-feasible subset \(F\) of another agent’s bundle or of the unallocated goods, removing at most two individual goods from \(F\) leaves value at most the value of \(a\)’s bundle.

This is Theorem 1 and Definition 1 of [Barman, Khan, Shyam, and Sreenivas](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.08168). Their Appendix A addresses equal densities. Subsets containing fewer than two goods satisfy the stated version simply by removing all their goods.

Clearing denominators and constructing the mass allocation

Choose a positive integer \(N\) such that all numbers

\(n_r:=N\lambda_r\quad\text{and}\quad K_q:=N\eta_q\)

are integers. Such an \(N\) exists because the masses and supplies are rational. Positivity implies that these integers are positive, and normalization gives

\(\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}n_r=N.\)

Construct a finite instance with \(n_r\) distinct agents of recipient type \(r\), each having budget \(B_r\), and \(K_q\) distinct individual goods of item type \(q\), each having size \(s_q\) and value \(v_q\). All agents have the given identical additive valuation. Thus every hypothesis of the finite-agent theorem holds.

Apply that theorem. For each finite agent \(a\), let \(A_a\) be its allocated set of goods, and let \(b(a)\) be its integral type-count vector. Define

\(k_{r,b}:=\bigl|\{a:\ a\text{ has type }r,\ b(a)=b\}\bigr|,\qquad y_{r,b}:=k_{r,b}/N.\)

These are nonnegative rational masses with finite support. Every occupied bundle satisfies its type’s budget because its corresponding finite agents do. Moreover,

\(\sum_b y_{r,b}=n_r/N=\lambda_r.\)

Let \(C_q\) denote the number of unallocated finite goods of item type \(q\). Counting allocated copies gives

\(\sum_{r,b}y_{r,b}b_q=(K_q-C_q)/N\leq\eta_q.\)

Consequently the mass allocation is feasible, and its charity vector satisfies

\(c_q=\eta_q-\sum_{r,b}y_{r,b}b_q=C_q/N.\)

Verification of every EF2 comparison

Fix any occupied pair \((r,b)\). Choose a finite agent \(a\) of type \(r\) whose allocated type-count vector is \(b\). Fix any integral \(f\) with \(S(f)\leq B_r\) that qualifies for a comparison in the question.

If \(\sum_q f_q\leq2\), take \(d=f\). Then \(V(f-d)=0\leq V(b)\). Hence assume \(\sum_q f_q>2\).

Comparison with an occupied bundle. Suppose \(f\leq b'\) for an occupied bundle \(b'\). Choose a finite agent \(a'\) receiving type-count vector \(b'\). There is a subset \(F\subseteq A_{a'}\) containing exactly \(f_q\) goods of each type \(q\). Its size is \(S(f)\leq B_r\).

If \(a'=a\), then \(f\leq b\), and positivity of values gives \(V(f)\leq V(b)\); take \(d=0\). Otherwise, the finite EF2 guarantee supplies a subset \(D\subseteq F\) of at most two individual goods such that the value of \(F\setminus D\) is at most \(V(b)\). Let \(d_q\) count the goods of type \(q\) in \(D\). Then

\(0\leq d\leq f,\qquad \sum_qd_q\leq2,\qquad V(f-d)\leq V(b).\)

This argument includes comparisons between occupied bundles of the same recipient type.

Comparison with charity. Suppose \(f\leq c\). Since \(N\geq1\) and \(C_q\geq0\),

\(f_q\leq c_q=C_q/N\leq C_q\)

for every item type \(q\). Therefore the finite charity contains a subset \(F\) with type-count vector exactly \(f\). It is budget-feasible for \(a\). Apply the finite EF2 guarantee against charity and count the removed goods by type to obtain the same three required inequalities for \(d\).

Individual copies remain distinct in the finite instance, so removing two copies of the same type is covered. These cases exhaust all comparisons in the definition. Thus the constructed rational allocation is EF2.

Decision complexity

On every input satisfying the stated hypotheses, the correct answer is YES. The decision algorithm therefore returns YES; if input validity must also be checked, those rational-arithmetic checks take polynomial time in the binary input length.

The expanded finite instance may be exponentially large. This limits the construction’s running time, but does not affect the polynomial-time decision result.
