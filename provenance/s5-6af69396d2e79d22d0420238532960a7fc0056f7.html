The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of room categories \(\mathcal O_t\) for each period, rational per-capita supplies \(q_{t,o}\) with \(\sum_{o\in\mathcal O_t}q_{t,o}=1\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) responsive, directionally separable preference types, find rational masses \(x_{\theta,b}\ge 0\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathcal B=\prod_t\mathcal O_t\). Require \(\sum_b x_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,b:\,b_t=o}x_{\theta,b}=q_{t,o}\). For every two positive-mass assignments \((\theta,b)\) and \((\theta',d)\), if \(d\succ_\theta b\), require a period \(t\) with \(b\succeq_\theta(\emptyset_t,d_{-t})\), and a reference bundle \(r\in\mathcal B\) and period \(s\) with \(b\succeq_\theta r\) and \((d_s,r_{-s})\succeq_\theta(r_s,d_{-s})\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(T\geq1\) be the number of periods, let \(\mathcal O_t\) be nonempty finite sets of available room categories, and put \(m=\sum_t|\mathcal O_t|\), \(\overline{\mathcal O}_t=\mathcal O_t\cup\{\emptyset_t\}\), and \(\mathcal B=\prod_t\mathcal O_t\). The outside option \(\emptyset_t\) is available without limit but cannot occur in allocated or reference bundles. Supplies are positive binary rationals \(q_{t,o}\) satisfying \(\sum_{o\in\mathcal O_t}q_{t,o}=1\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass \(\mu_\theta\) as a binary rational, with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type supplies an explicit weak-order table on \(\prod_t\overline{\mathcal O}_t\); \(L\) is the total input bit length, including these tables.
Preferences may have bundle indifferences. Responsiveness means that each period has a strict ranking of its categories and outside option, every category ranks above the outside option, and replacing one coordinate by a higher-ranked item strictly improves a bundle, independently of the other coordinates.
Directional separability means that, for \(2\leq t\leq T\), \(a\in\mathcal O_t\), and prefixes \(p,p'\in\prod_{u<t}\overline{\mathcal O}_u\), one has \(p\succeq_\theta p'\iff(p,a)\succeq_\theta(p',a)\). All omitted later coordinates are outside options. These preference restrictions are promises on the input.
Find rational masses \(x_{\theta,b}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_bx_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,b:b_t=o}x_{\theta,b}=q_{t,o}\). For every ordered pair \((\theta,b),(\theta',d)\) with positive assigned masses, including pairs with \(\theta=\theta'\), if \(d\succ_\theta b\), require both \(\exists t:\ b\succeq_\theta(\emptyset_t,d_{-t})\) and \(\exists r\in\mathcal B,\exists s:\ b\succeq_\theta r\text{ and }(d_s,r_{-s})\succeq_\theta(r_s,d_{-s})\). Here \(v_{-t}\) denotes all coordinates of \(v\) except \(t\); the two witnessing periods may differ, and the reference bundle need not be assigned.

Is finding such a rational allocation polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,T,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite agents with one available object per agent in each period, TTC followed by serial dictatorship produces an allocation satisfying EF1 and swap bounded envy under responsive, directionally separable preferences ([Echenique, Goel, and Lee](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09290v1)). No result settling this exact distribution-over-types search problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The preference representation was unspecified; use explicit weak-order tables over all bundles including outside options, and count their encoding in L.
  • Normalize total societal mass to one, matching the per-period supply normalization.
  • Treat room categories as available categories with positive supply; omit zero-supply categories before defining allocated and reference bundles.
  • Specify positive numbers of periods and nonempty category sets, and interpret m as the total number of period-specific categories.
  • Interpret responsiveness as context-independent strict within-period item rankings, with every real category above its outside option; allow indifferences between bundles.
  • Extend directional separability to prefixes containing outside options, as required by the discrete EF1 argument, and evaluate omitted coordinates as outside options.
  • Apply fairness to assignments within the same type as well as across types; allow independent EF1 and swap-bound witnessing periods and unassigned reference bundles.

Result

The search problem is polynomial-time solvable in the stated explicit-table input model.

The construction uses a mass-based version of TTC, followed by serial dictatorship in reverse TTC order. It accommodates bundle indifferences and enforces the requirements between recipients of the same type.

The TTC idea is motivated by [Echenique, Goel, and Lee](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09290v1). The proof below establishes the construction and its complexity directly for the distribution-over-types model.

1. Prefix allocations and the induction invariant

Write \(\mathcal P_t=\prod_{u=1}^t\mathcal O_u\). Interpret every prefix as a full bundle whose omitted coordinates are outside options. Thus prefix comparisons are available from the input tables.

A prefix allocation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(y_{\theta,p}\), for \(p\in\mathcal P_t\), satisfying

\[ \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_t}y_{\theta,p}=\mu_\theta, \qquad \sum_{\theta,p:p_u=o}y_{\theta,p}=q_{u,o} \quad (u\leq t). \]

Call this allocation *fair* if, whenever \(y_{\theta,p}>0\), \(y_{\eta,d}>0\), and \(d\succ_\theta p\), both of the following hold:

  • There is \(s\leq t\) such that \(p\succeq_\theta(\emptyset_s,d_{-s})\).
  • There are \(r\in\mathcal P_t\) and \(s\leq t\) such that

\(p\succeq_\theta r\) and
\((d_s,r_{-s})\succeq_\theta(r_s,d_{-s})\).

Here the subscript \(-s\) refers to the other coordinates of the prefix. Later coordinates remain outside options.

The algorithm constructs a fair prefix allocation for each \(t\). At \(t=T\), this is exactly the required allocation.

For \(t=1\), obtain any feasible transportation of the type masses to the first-period supplies by repeatedly matching residual type mass with residual category supply. It is fair: dropping the sole category from an envied bundle leaves the outside option, and the swap condition holds with the recipient’s own category as the reference bundle.

2. Mass-based TTC

Fix a fair allocation of prefixes of length \(t-1\), where \(t\geq2\).

For each positive entry, create a group \(h\) with:

  • type \(\theta_h\);
  • endowed prefix \(p_h\);
  • residual mass \(c_h=y_{\theta_h,p_h}\).

Groups retain these types and endowed prefixes throughout the TTC step.

At any stage, the residual stock of prefix \(p\) is

\[ S_p=\sum_{h:p_h=p}c_h. \]

Construct a directed graph with the positive-mass groups and the prefixes having positive residual stock as vertices:

  • Each group \(h\) points to a most-preferred prefix \(a_h\) among those with \(S_p>0\), according to \(\theta_h\).
  • Each prefix \(p\) points to any positive-mass group endowed with \(p\).

Ties between most-preferred prefixes are broken arbitrarily. Every vertex has one outgoing edge, so the finite graph contains a directed cycle.

Choose one cycle and put

\[ \delta=\min\{c_h:h\text{ is a group on the cycle}\}. \]

For each group \(h\) on that cycle, record an output group of type \(\theta_h\), assigned prefix \(a_h\), and mass \(\delta\). Reduce its residual mass \(c_h\) by \(\delta\). Rebuild the graph and continue until all residual masses vanish.

Number the cycles in their processing order. Each output group retains its cycle number.

Lemma 1: Feasibility, termination, and individual improvement

This procedure preserves every type’s total mass and the aggregate stock of every prefix. Each output group receives a prefix weakly preferred to its endowed prefix.

Proof. A cycle alternates between group vertices and prefix vertices. Each prefix on the cycle is received by its predecessor group and supplied by its successor group. Consequently, the recorded transfers consume exactly the endowed prefixes released by the reductions in residual masses.

Each participating group releases and receives the same mass \(\delta\), with its type unchanged. Thus type masses are preserved.

Before a positive-mass group \(h\) participates, its own endowed prefix \(p_h\) has positive residual stock. Its chosen prefix therefore satisfies

\[ a_h\succeq_{\theta_h}p_h. \]

Finally, each cycle exhausts at least one group, by the definition of \(\delta\). If there are \(N\) initial groups, there are at most \(N\) cycles. Thus the procedure terminates and accounts for all mass. \(\square\)

Lemma 2: The output prefix allocation remains fair

Proof. Consider two output groups: a type-\(\theta\) group receiving \(a\), originally endowed with \(p\), and another group receiving \(d\). Suppose \(d\succ_\theta a\).

Lemma 1 gives \(a\succeq_\theta p\), and hence \(d\succ_\theta p\).

The prefix \(d\) had positive stock in the original allocation, since TTC only redistributes existing prefixes. Therefore some original positive entry assigned \(d\) to some type. The original fairness invariant applies to the ordered pair consisting of \((\theta,p)\) and that entry.

Its removal witness remains valid because

\[ a\succeq_\theta p \succeq_\theta(\emptyset_s,d_{-s}). \]

Its reference-bundle witness also remains valid: if \(p\succeq_\theta r\), then \(a\succeq_\theta r\), while the swap comparison involving \(r\) and \(d\) is unchanged.

This applies regardless of whether the types coincide. \(\square\)

Lemma 3: Strict prefix envy determines reverse cycle order

If an output group of type \(\theta\), assigned prefix \(a\) in cycle \(k\), strictly prefers the prefix \(d\) assigned to another output group in cycle \(\ell\), then \(k>\ell\).

Proof. Suppose instead that \(k\leq\ell\). At the beginning of cycle \(k\), prefix \(d\) has positive residual stock.

For \(k=\ell\), this follows because \(d\) is selected in that cycle. For \(k<\ell\), it follows because \(d\) will be supplied in cycle \(\ell\), and residual stocks only decrease.

The first group selected a most-preferred prefix from the stock available in cycle \(k\). Therefore \(a\succeq_\theta d\), contradicting \(d\succ_\theta a\). \(\square\)

3. Assigning the next period

Process the TTC output groups in decreasing cycle number, with arbitrary order within each cycle.

For each group, repeatedly assign its type’s highest-ranked period-\(t\) category having positive residual supply. Assign the minimum of the group’s remaining mass and that category’s remaining supply. Continue until the entire group is assigned.

This may split a group across several categories. Afterward, aggregate entries with the same type and extended prefix.

Total remaining supply equals total remaining group mass throughout this procedure. Thus every group is filled, all period-\(t\) supplies are exhausted, and no outside option is assigned.

The following property is essential. Consider any two positive fragments after this assignment:

  • a type-\(\theta\) fragment receiving \((a,c)\);
  • another fragment receiving \((d,e)\).

If \(d\succ_\theta a\), Lemma 3 places the entire first TTC group before the entire second group in serial dictatorship. Category \(e\) still has positive supply when the first fragment chooses \(c\), because positive mass of \(e\) is assigned later. Therefore

\[ c\succeq_\theta e \]

in the strict period-\(t\) category ranking.

This reasoning includes two fragments belonging to the same type.

4. Preservation of both fairness conditions

Fix two such fragments and suppose

\[ (d,e)\succ_\theta(a,c). \]

There are two cases.

Case 1: \(a\succeq_\theta d\)

Responsiveness implies that adding category \(c\) improves the prefix \(a\). Hence

\[ (a,c)\succeq_\theta a \succeq_\theta d =(d,\emptyset_t). \]

Thus dropping period \(t\) from the envied bundle eliminates envy.

For the swap condition, use the recipient’s own bundle \(r=(a,c)\) as reference and swap period \(t\). Directional separability, applied separately with categories \(e\) and \(c\), gives

\[ (a,e)\succeq_\theta(d,e) \succ_\theta(a,c) \succeq_\theta(d,c). \]

Consequently,

\[ (d_t,r_{-t})=(a,e) \succeq_\theta(d,c) =(r_t,(d,e)_{-t}). \]

The reference contains no outside option.

Case 2: \(d\succ_\theta a\)

The serial-dictatorship property gives \(c\succeq_\theta e\).

By Lemma 2, the post-TTC prefix allocation is fair. Its removal condition supplies \(s<t\) such that

\[ a\succeq_\theta(\emptyset_s,d_{-s}). \]

Directional separability permits appending category \(c\) to this comparison, including to the prefix containing an outside option. Responsiveness then permits replacing \(c\) by the weakly lower-ranked category \(e\):

\[ (a,c) \succeq_\theta(\emptyset_s,d_{-s},c) \succeq_\theta(\emptyset_s,d_{-s},e). \]

This is the required removal witness.

The prefix swap condition supplies \(u\in\mathcal P_{t-1}\) and \(s<t\) satisfying

\[ a\succeq_\theta u, \qquad (d_s,u_{-s})\succeq_\theta(u_s,d_{-s}). \]

Choose the extended reference \(r=(u,e)\). Directional separability and responsiveness give

\[ (a,c)\succeq_\theta(u,c)\succeq_\theta(u,e)=r. \]

Appending \(e\) to the prefix swap comparison gives

\[ (d_s,u_{-s},e) \succeq_\theta (u_s,d_{-s},e). \]

These are precisely the bundles obtained by swapping coordinate \(s\) between \(r\) and the envied bundle \((d,e)\). Thus the swap condition holds, and \(r\in\mathcal P_t\).

Both cases establish the induction invariant. Starting with the first-period allocation and repeating the construction therefore produces a valid allocation at \(t=T\).

5. Polynomial operation count and bit complexity

Let \(K_t=|\mathcal O_t|\) and \(P_t=|\mathcal P_t|\).

Because each type’s preference table explicitly lists all tuples in \(\prod_t\overline{\mathcal O}_t\), the input length bounds the number of type-prefix pairs:

\[ \tau P_t \leq \tau P_T \leq \tau\prod_{u=1}^T(K_u+1) =O(L). \]

Preference ranks can be indexed during input processing.

At the start of round \(t\), aggregate by type and endowed prefix. The number of positive groups is then

\[ N\leq\tau P_{t-1}=O(L). \]

Each TTC cycle exhausts at least one group, so there are at most \(N\) cycles. Each creates at most \(N\) output groups. Thus TTC creates at most \(N^2\) records.

Constructing a graph requires only scanning residual groups and prefixes and comparing table ranks. Finding a directed cycle and updating its masses are polynomial-time operations.

If TTC produces \(M\) records, the serial-dictatorship step creates at most \(M+K_t\) positive fragments: each assignment exhausts a group or a category, or both. Aggregation again leaves at most \(\tau P_t\) entries.

For exact arithmetic, let \(D\) be the product of all denominators appearing in the input masses and supplies. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Scale every mass and supply by \(D\). All capacity operations are then integer minima, additions, and subtractions; every capacity lies between \(0\) and \(D\). Each arithmetic operation therefore uses numbers of \(O(L)\) bits.

The algorithm performs polynomially many such operations over the \(T\) rounds. Dividing the final integer entries by \(D\) produces rational output of polynomial bit length.

The explicit-table assumption is what makes the potentially large prefix spaces polynomial in the specified input size.

Conclusion

The algorithm constructs a feasible rational allocation satisfying both required fairness conditions for every ordered pair of positive entries, including pairs within one type. Its running time and output length are polynomial in \(m,\tau,T,L\).

Step-by-step verification

1. Prefix invariant and base case

The prefix invariant correctly restricts reference bundles to available categories and interprets omitted coordinates as outside options. At \(t=T\), it coincides with the two requirements in the task.

The first-period transportation procedure is feasible because total type mass and total first-period supply both equal \(1\).

Both fairness assertions for \(t=1\) are correct:

  • Using the recipient’s own category as reference makes the swap comparison exactly the assumed strict preference for the envied category.

These checks also cover distinct positive entries belonging to the same type.

2. Mass-based TTC: feasibility and improvement

The graph is well-defined. Every residual group has its endowed prefix available, and every positive-stock prefix has a positive residual owner. Thus every vertex has an outgoing edge, and a directed cycle exists.

On a cycle, each prefix is supplied by its successor group and received by its predecessor group. Reducing every participating group by the same mass therefore preserves the aggregate allocation of each prefix. Each group’s type is unchanged, so type masses are preserved as well.

The individual-improvement claim is justified: whenever a group participates, its endowed prefix remains available, and the selected prefix is a maximizer over the available prefixes.

Each cycle exhausts at least one residual group, and exhausted groups never return. The bound of at most \(N\) cycles is consequently valid.

3. Fairness after TTC

Lemma 2 correctly transfers both witnesses from the original allocation.

For an output recipient with endowed prefix \(p\) and received prefix \(a\), the inequalities \(d\succ_\theta a\) and \(a\succeq_\theta p\) imply \(d\succ_\theta p\). The received prefix \(d\) occurred with positive mass in the original allocation, so the original fairness invariant applies.

The removal witness survives by transitivity. The reference witness survives because only the comparison between the recipient’s bundle and the reference changes; the swap comparison remains unchanged.

There is no restriction here requiring different types, so the same-type requirement is covered.

4. Cycle order and serial dictatorship

Lemma 3 is correct. Residual stocks only decrease. If \(d\) is assigned in cycle \(\ell\), then it was available at the beginning of every cycle \(k\leq\ell\). A recipient selecting a most-preferred available prefix in such a cycle cannot strictly prefer \(d\) to its selection.

Consequently, strict prefix envy places the envious recipient’s entire TTC record earlier in reverse-order serial dictatorship.

The category comparison \(c\succeq_\theta e\) is justified: a category assigned with positive mass to a later record still has positive residual supply when the earlier fragment chooses. Selecting the highest-ranked available category therefore yields the claimed comparison.

Splitting records does not invalidate this argument, because the entire earlier record is processed before the later record.
