The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over provider types, where each type specifies a recurring workload, machine profile, and deadline profile under a fixed energy exponent \(\alpha\), let coalition mass \(\lambda_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) contribute proportionally scaled workloads and capacity. Let \(p(\lambda)=\sum_t\lambda_t p_t\), let \(C(\lambda)\) be the minimum energy to schedule that coalition, and set \(v(\lambda)=p(\lambda)-C(\lambda)\). Decide whether there are nonnegative type-symmetric payout rates \(q_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t q_t=v(\mu)\) that are stable against every positive-mass objection and its corresponding counter-objections, and compute such rates if they exist.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Type \(t\) supplies \(m_t\geq1\) identical speed-scalable machines and an explicitly listed batch of unit-work jobs with release time \(0\) and positive rational deadlines \(d_{tj}\). Fix a rational exponent \(\alpha>1\). Let \(m=\sum_t m_t\), let \(n\) count listed jobs, and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Represent providers by disjoint atomless intervals \(\Omega_t\) of lengths \(\mu_t\). A measurable coalition \(S\) has masses \(\lambda_t=|S\cap\Omega_t|\), supplies machine mass \(M(\lambda)=\sum_t\lambda_t m_t\), and contributes mass \(\lambda_t\) of each listed job. Jobs may be preempted and migrated, but each individual job uses at most one machine at a time.
Precisely, \(C(\lambda)\) is the infimum of \(\int_0^{D}\sum_{t,j}a_{tj}(u)s_{tj}(u)^\alpha\,du\), where \(D=\max_{t,j}d_{tj}\), measurable schedules satisfy \(s_{tj}\geq0\), \(0\leq a_{tj}\leq\lambda_t\), \(\sum_{t,j}a_{tj}\leq M(\lambda)\), \(a_{tj}(u)=0\) after \(d_{tj}\), and \(\int_0^{d_{tj}}a_{tj}s_{tj}\,du=\lambda_t\). Set \(C(0)=0\); an empty job list costs zero.
Let \(e_t\) be the unit vector, \(p_t=C(e_t)\), and \(v(S)=\sum_t\lambda_t p_t-C(\lambda)\). A type-symmetric allocation pays rate \(q_t\geq0\) on \(\Omega_t\) and satisfies \(\sum_t\mu_tq_t=v(\Omega)\).
An objection consists of disjoint positive-measure leader and respondent sets \(I,J\), a measurable coalition \(P\supseteq I\) disjoint from \(J\), and an integrable payoff \(y\) on \(P\), with \(\int_P y\leq v(P)\), \(y\geq q\) almost everywhere on \(P\), and \(y>q\) almost everywhere on \(I\).
A counter-objection is a measurable coalition \(Q\supseteq J\) disjoint from \(I\), with integrable payoff \(z\) satisfying \(\int_Qz\leq v(Q)\), \(z\geq q\) on \(Q\setminus P\), and \(z\geq y\) on \(Q\cap P\), almost everywhere. Stability means every objection has a counter-objection; only the initial allocation must be type-symmetric.

Is deciding whether such stable rates exist, and computing exact real-algebraic rates when they do, polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,n,L\) for fixed \(\alpha\), or is the existence problem NP-hard?

For finite providers, polynomial-time alliance detection and imputation computation are asserted for energy and total-completion-time objectives ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07111)). Migratory speed-scaling energy minimization separately has a polynomial-time algorithm ([source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000015000239)).
No complexity result for this exact mass scheduling problem with these objection and counter-objection conditions was located.

Result and exact-output convention

Stable rates exist for every instance. They can be computed in polynomial time as exact radical expressions. In fact, the rates constructed below admit no objection at all.

For exact algebraic output, I use finite expressions involving rational arithmetic and positive real roots of positive rationals. If \(\alpha=a/b>1\) is fixed and written in lowest terms, every output rate will be a rational linear combination of positive \(b\)-th roots of rational numbers. The number of terms and their bit lengths are polynomially bounded. For integer \(\alpha\), the rates are rational.

This specifies the output representation: the algorithm does not expand each rate into a minimal polynomial.

1. An exact finite-dimensional scheduling formulation

Index the listed jobs by \(i\), write \(t(i)\) for the type owning job \(i\), and write \(d_i\) for its deadline. Fix a nonzero mass vector \(\lambda\). Remove jobs belonging to types with zero mass, and put

\(\displaystyle w_i=\lambda_{t(i)},\qquad M=\sum_t\lambda_t m_t.\)

Let the distinct deadlines partition time into intervals of lengths \(\Delta_k>0\). Let \(E_k\) be the jobs available throughout interval \(k\); because all release times are zero, these are precisely the jobs whose deadlines occur at or after that interval’s right endpoint.

For a schedule, define its total occupation time for job \(i\) by

\(\displaystyle T_i=\int_0^{d_i}a_i(u)\,du.\)

The work requirement and Jensen’s inequality give

\(\displaystyle \int_0^{d_i}a_i(u)s_i(u)^\alpha\,du \ \geq\ T_i\left(\frac{w_i}{T_i}\right)^\alpha =\frac{w_i^\alpha}{T_i^{\alpha-1}}.\)

Here \(T_i>0\) whenever the schedule is feasible, since \(w_i>0\).

Introduce variables \(A_{ik}\), representing occupation time in interval \(k\). Their constraints are

\(\displaystyle 0\leq A_{ik}\leq\Delta_k w_i\quad(i\in E_k),\qquad \sum_{i\in E_k}A_{ik}\leq\Delta_k M,\qquad T_i=\sum_{k:i\in E_k}A_{ik}. \)

Conversely, any such variables with positive \(T_i\) give a feasible schedule: throughout interval \(k\), use occupation mass \(A_{ik}/\Delta_k\) and constant job speed \(w_i/T_i\). Its completed work is \(w_i\), and its energy attains the Jensen bound.

Consequently,

\[\displaystyle C(\lambda)= \min_A\sum_i\frac{w_i^\alpha}{T_i^{\alpha-1}} \tag{1} \]

subject to the displayed rational linear constraints.

The minimum exists. The feasible occupation-time set is compact; positive occupation times are feasible by assigning every job a sufficiently small positive occupation mass during the interval before the earliest deadline. The objective tends to infinity if any positive-work job’s occupation time tends to zero.

The summand in (1) is the convex perspective \(T_i(w_i/T_i)^\alpha\). Thus the formulation also proves that \(C\) is convex and positively homogeneous in the mass vector.

2. The feasible occupation times form a polymatroid

For a set \(B\) of active jobs, define

\[\displaystyle f(B)=\sum_k\Delta_k \min\left\{M,\sum_{i\in B\cap E_k}w_i\right\}. \tag{2} \]

The feasible occupation-time vectors are exactly

\[\displaystyle P(f)=\left\{T\geq0: \sum_{i\in B}T_i\leq f(B)\text{ for every }B\subseteq E \right\}. \tag{3} \]

To verify this, construct a flow network with:

  • an arc from the source to job \(i\), of capacity \(T_i\);
  • an arc from job \(i\) to each eligible interval \(k\), of capacity \(\Delta_k w_i\);
  • an arc from interval \(k\) to the sink, of capacity \(\Delta_k M\).

Fix the set \(B\) of job vertices on the source side of a cut. Minimizing over the interval vertices gives cut capacity

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i\notin B}T_i+f(B).\)

Therefore every cut has capacity at least \(\sum_iT_i\) exactly when (3) holds. The max-flow/min-cut theorem proves the asserted equivalence.

The function \(f\) is normalized, monotone, and submodular. Indeed, in each interval, the additional contribution from adding an eligible job \(i\) is

\(\displaystyle \Delta_k\min\left\{w_i, \left(M-\sum_{j\in B\cap E_k}w_j\right)_+ \right\}, \)

which decreases as \(B\) grows. Summing preserves this diminishing-returns property.

Also \(M\geq w_i\), because \(m_{t(i)}\geq1\). Hence

\(\displaystyle f(\{i\})=w_i d_i.\)

In particular, every feasible occupation time is bounded.

3. Optimal job durations are rational and computable in polynomial time

Define the duration per unit mass by \(h_i=T_i/w_i\). I next construct optimal \(h_i\) using only rational linear programs. This is a specialization of weighted lexicographic polymatroid allocation, whose general theory appears in [Fujishige’s paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.5.2.186).

3.1 Construction

Maintain a set \(F\) of jobs whose occupation times have been fixed. Initially \(F=\varnothing\). At every stage the fixed vector is feasible on \(F\) and satisfies

\(\displaystyle T(F)=f(F).\)

For the remaining set \(R=E\setminus F\), define the contracted rank function

\(\displaystyle f_F(B)=f(F\cup B)-f(F)\qquad(B\subseteq R).\)

Choose the largest \(\theta\) such that

\(\displaystyle \theta w(B)\leq f_F(B)\qquad(B\subseteq R).\)

Equivalently,

\[\displaystyle \theta=\min_{\varnothing\ne B\subseteq R} \frac{f_F(B)}{w(B)},\qquad w(B)=\sum_{i\in B}w_i. \tag{4} \]

Let \(B_*\) be the union of all sets attaining equality. Fix

\(\displaystyle T_i=\theta w_i\qquad(i\in B_*),\)

replace \(F\) by \(F\cup B_*\), and repeat.

The set \(B_*\) is nonempty and tight. To see closure under unions, the function

\(\displaystyle B\longmapsto f_F(B)-\theta w(B)\)

is nonnegative and submodular. If it vanishes on two sets, submodularity forces it to vanish on their union and intersection.

There are at most as many stages as active jobs.

3.2 Feasibility and increasing durations

We use the following elementary gluing property. Suppose \(x_F\) is feasible on \(F\), with \(x_F(F)=f(F)\), and \(x_R\in P(f_F)\). For every \(A\subseteq E\),

\(\displaystyle x_F(A\cap F)+x_R(A\setminus F) \leq f(A\cap F)+f(A\cup F)-f(F) \leq f(A), \)

where the last inequality is submodularity. Thus the combined vector lies in \(P(f)\).

Applying this property successively proves that the construction produces a feasible vector \(T^*\). If its successive blocks are \(B_1,\ldots,B_r\), with prefixes \(F_\ell=B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_\ell\), then

\[\displaystyle T^*(F_\ell)=f(F_\ell).\tag{5}\]

The stage values \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r\) are strictly increasing. After selecting a maximal tight block \(B_*\), any nonempty remaining set \(B\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle f_{F\cup B_*}(B)-\theta w(B) = f_F(B\cup B_*)-\theta w(B\cup B_*)>0. \)

Equality would contradict maximality of \(B_*\). Formula (4) therefore gives a strictly larger next value.

The first value is positive. Writing \(d_{\min}>0\) for the earliest deadline and \(W=\sum_iw_i\), every nonempty \(B\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \frac{f(B)}{w(B)} \geq d_{\min}\min\{1,M/W\}>0. \)

Thus every constructed duration is positive.

3.3 Energy optimality

For \(i\in B_\ell\), put

\(\displaystyle h_i=\theta_\ell,\qquad \eta_i=(\alpha-1)h_i^{-\alpha}. \)

The values of \(\eta_i\) strictly decrease between successive blocks.

For any \(T\in P(f)\), writing \(\eta_\ell\) for the common value on \(B_\ell\),

\[\displaystyle \sum_i\eta_iT_i = \eta_rT(E)+ \sum_{\ell=1}^{r-1}(\eta_\ell-\eta_{\ell+1})T(F_\ell) \leq \eta_rf(E)+ \sum_{\ell=1}^{r-1}(\eta_\ell-\eta_{\ell+1})f(F_\ell) = \sum_i\eta_iT_i^*. \tag{6} \]

The equality on the right follows from (5).

The convex function \(w_i^\alpha T_i^{1-\alpha}\) has derivative \(-\eta_i\) at \(T_i^*=w_i h_i\). Its supporting-line inequality, summed over jobs and combined with (6), gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_i\frac{w_i^\alpha}{T_i^{\alpha-1}} \geq \sum_i\frac{w_i^\alpha}{(T_i^*)^{\alpha-1}} -\sum_i\eta_i(T_i-T_i^*) \geq \sum_iw_i h_i^{1-\alpha}. \)

Therefore

\[\displaystyle C(\lambda)=\sum_iw_i h_i^{1-\alpha}.\tag{7}\]

Notably, the optimal durations do not depend on \(\alpha>1\).

3.4 Polynomial-time implementation

Neither the exponentially many inequalities in (3) nor subset enumeration in (4) is needed.

At a stage, maximize \(\theta\) over the occupation variables \(A_{ik}\), subject to the original interval constraints and

\[\displaystyle T_i=T_i^*\quad(i\in F),\qquad T_i\geq\theta w_i\quad(i\notin F). \tag{8} \]

This rational linear program has polynomial size.

Its feasible values of \(\theta\) are exactly those in (4):

  • Necessity follows from \(T(F)=f(F)\) and \(T(F\cup B)\leq f(F\cup B)\).
  • Sufficiency follows from the gluing property and the flow characterization.

At the optimal \(\theta\), maximize each remaining \(T_i\) separately, maintaining (8). Job \(i\) belongs to \(B_*\) exactly when this maximum equals \(\theta w_i\). Membership in a tight set forces equality. Conversely, if no tight set contains \(i\), all subset inequalities involving \(i\) have positive slack; there are finitely many, so \(T_i\) can be increased slightly.

Thus at most a quadratic number of polynomial-size rational linear programs constructs all durations.

Their bit lengths remain polynomial. For every block,

\(\displaystyle \theta_\ell= \frac{f(F_\ell)-f(F_{\ell-1})}{w(B_\ell)}. \)

Each rank value is a sum of polynomially many products and sums of input rationals. These ratios, and hence all subsequently fixed LP coefficients, have polynomial bit length.

4. An explicit supporting cost certificate

Apply the preceding construction at the grand mass vector \(\mu\), and retain its durations \(h_i\) and weights

\(\displaystyle \eta_i=(\alpha-1)h_i^{-\alpha}.\)

For each interval \(k\), choose nonnegative coefficients \(\beta_k,\gamma_{ik}\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \beta_k+\gamma_{ik}\geq\eta_i\qquad(i\in E_k)\)

and achieving the exact interval identity

\[\displaystyle \max_{\substack{0\leq a_i\leq\mu_{t(i)}\\ \sum_{i\in E_k}a_i\leq M(\mu)}} \sum_{i\in E_k}\eta_i a_i = \beta_kM(\mu)+ \sum_{i\in E_k}\gamma_{ik}\mu_{t(i)}. \tag{9} \]

These coefficients are explicit. Sort available jobs by increasing \(h_i\), equivalently decreasing \(\eta_i\).

  • If their total mass is at most \(M(\mu)\), choose \(\beta_k=0\).
  • Otherwise, fill the available job masses in that order up to machine capacity, and choose \(\beta_k\) equal to the weight \(\eta_i\) at the capacity threshold.
  • In both cases, set \(\gamma_{ik}=\max\{\eta_i-\beta_k,0\}\).

The greedy filling proves (9). The required comparisons use only rational durations and masses.

Define, for each type,

\[\displaystyle c_t= \sum_{i:t(i)=t}\alpha h_i^{1-\alpha} - \sum_k\Delta_k \left( m_t\beta_k+ \sum_{\substack{i\in E_k\\t(i)=t}}\gamma_{ik} \right). \tag{10} \]

I claim that

\[\displaystyle C(\lambda)\geq\sum_t c_t\lambda_t \quad\text{for every }\lambda\geq0, \qquad C(\mu)=\sum_t c_t\mu_t. \tag{11} \]

For the first assertion, the perspective supporting inequality is

\[\displaystyle \frac{w^\alpha}{T^{\alpha-1}} \geq \alpha h_i^{1-\alpha}w-\eta_iT. \tag{12} \]

For any feasible occupation variables at \(\lambda\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_i\eta_iT_i \leq \sum_k\Delta_k \left( \beta_kM(\lambda)+ \sum_{i\in E_k}\gamma_{ik}\lambda_{t(i)} \right). \)

Combining this with (12) and the exact scheduling formulation proves the first part of (11). For an absent job, \(w=T=0\), and the same inequality holds with both sides zero.

At \(\mu\), every job has \(T_i=\mu_{t(i)}h_i\), so (12) is an equality. Moreover, (6) says that this occupation vector maximizes the linear objective with weights \(\eta_i\). That linear maximization separates by intervals, so its value is exactly the sum of (9), multiplied by the interval lengths. Consequently all inequalities are equalities at \(\mu\), proving the second assertion in (11).

5. Stable nonnegative rates

Compute each standalone cost \(p_t=C(e_t)\) using (7), and define

\[\displaystyle q_t=p_t-c_t.\tag{13}\]

Applying (11) at \(e_t\) gives \(p_t\geq c_t\), hence \(q_t\geq0\).

Equality at \(\mu\) gives efficiency:

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_tq_t = \sum_t\mu_tp_t-C(\mu) = v(\Omega). \)

For every measurable coalition \(P\), with mass vector \(\lambda\), (11) yields

\[\displaystyle v(P) = \sum_t\lambda_tp_t-C(\lambda) \leq \sum_t\lambda_tq_t = \int_Pq. \tag{14} \]

Suppose an objection existed. Its integrable payoff would satisfy \(y-q\geq0\) almost everywhere on \(P\), and \(y-q>0\) almost everywhere on the positive-measure leader set \(I\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \int_Py>\int_Pq.\)

For completeness, strictness follows because the sets on which \(y-q\geq1/k\), for positive integers \(k\), cover \(I\) up to a null set; at least one has positive measure.

But (14) and the objection’s budget imply

\(\displaystyle \int_Py\leq v(P)\leq\int_Pq,\)

a contradiction. Thus these rates admit no objection, and satisfy the requested stability condition regardless of what nonsymmetric counter-objection payoffs are allowed.

6. Complexity and exact algebraic output

Run the rational duration algorithm once for \(\mu\), and once for each type with a nonempty job list to obtain \(p_t\). Types with empty lists have \(p_t=0\). If every list is empty, simply output \(q_t=0\).

There are at most \(n\) deadline intervals. Every linear program has polynomially many variables and constraints and polynomial coefficient bit length. The number of programs is polynomial in \(\tau,n\). Consequently the running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,n,L\).

Finally, write the fixed exponent as \(\alpha=a/b\). For a positive rational duration \(h\), introduce the positive algebraic number

\(\displaystyle r_h=\sqrt[b]{h^{-a}}.\)

Then

\(\displaystyle h^{1-\alpha}=h\,r_h,\qquad (\alpha-1)h^{-\alpha}=(\alpha-1)r_h. \)

Equations (7), (10), and (13) therefore give every rate as a rational linear combination of polynomially many such roots, with polynomial bit lengths. This is exact real-algebraic output.

Hence, with this explicit compact exact-output representation, the requested construction is polynomial-time solvable. The existence problem itself is even simpler: every valid instance has an affirmative answer.
