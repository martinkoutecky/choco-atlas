The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given types \(t\) with masses \(\mu_t\), strict post rankings, weights \(\omega_t\), a feasible current allocation \(x_{t,p}\), and efficiently represented per-capita matroid rank constraints \(\rho_p\), determine whether there is a reassignment flow \(z_{t,p,q}\) that preserves each unit of mass’s current-to-new comparison, yields a feasible allocation, and has positive weighted vote margin \(\sum_t \omega_t \sum_{p,q} z_{t,p,q}(\mathbf{1}[q \succ_t p]-\mathbf{1}[p \succ_t q])\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. There are \(m\) posts. Each type has an eligible set \(P_t\), a strict ranking \(\succ_t\) of that set, and a positive integer voting weight \(\omega_t\).
Each type has two distinct private last-resort posts, eligible only for that type, ranked below all its other eligible posts and strictly ordered relative to each other; each can accommodate its entire mass.
For each post \(p\), let \(T_p=\{t:p\in P_t\}\). Its per-capita rank constraint is a rational polymatroid rank function \(\rho_p:2^{T_p}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\), normalized, nondecreasing and submodular, with \(\rho_p(\{t\})>0\). An allocation \(y\) is feasible when \(y_{t,p}\geq0\), \(y_{t,p}=0\) for \(p\notin P_t\), \(\sum_p y_{t,p}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t\in S}y_{t,p}\leq\rho_p(S)\) for every \(p\) and \(S\subseteq T_p\).
The input includes a feasible binary-rational allocation \(x\) and descriptions evaluating each \(\rho_p(S)\) exactly in polynomial time, with polynomial output bit length. Let \(L\) include all input encodings and these descriptions; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
A reassignment is a nonnegative real flow \(z_{t,p,q}\), defined for \(p,q\in P_t\), satisfying \(\sum_q z_{t,p,q}=x_{t,p}\), whose destination allocation \(y_{t,q}=\sum_p z_{t,p,q}\) is feasible. Each flow portion compares its actual old post with its new post; improvements, deteriorations and staying put are all allowed.
Its weighted vote margin is \(\Delta(z)=\sum_t\omega_t\sum_{p,q\in P_t}z_{t,p,q}\bigl(\mathbf{1}[q\succ_t p]-\mathbf{1}[p\succ_t q]\bigr)\).

Is deciding whether such a reassignment with \(\Delta(z)>0\) exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Discrete weighted popularity under post matroid constraints has a structural characterization, and testing a given matching is polynomial-time solvable with independence oracles ([Kamiyama](https://doi.org/10.15807/jorsj.61.2)).
A related fractional common-independent-set model admits polynomial-time construction of a popular fractional solution ([Kavitha–Makino–Schlotter–Yokoi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3715329)); no result settling the specified flow-coupled mass problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to total mass one and expressed rank capacities in the same units.
  • Interpreted the undefined per-capita matroid ranks as rational polymatroid rank functions on eligible type sets, the aggregate mass analogue of matroid ranks; required positive singleton ranks to retain the exclusion of loops.
  • Specified exact polynomial-time rank evaluation with polynomial output bit length and included representation sizes in L.
  • Retained positive integer voting weights, potentially incomplete eligibility lists, full assignment, and two strictly ordered private last-resort posts; each private post accommodates its type's entire mass.
  • Specified real, divisible reassignment flows with prescribed old-post marginals and feasible destination allocations; allowed deteriorations and unchanged assignments as well as improvements.

Result

The decision problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, one can compute the maximum vote margin exactly and produce an optimal rational reassignment.

The proof uses the flow variables themselves. Their objective is linear, and the exponentially many post constraints admit polynomial-time separation by submodular function minimization.

1. An exact linear programming formulation

Let

\(E=\{(t,p,q):t\in T,\ p,q\in P_t\}\), and \(n=|E|=\sum_{t\in T}|P_t|^2\leq \tau m^2\).

For each coordinate define the integer coefficient

\(c_{t,p,q}=\omega_t\bigl(\mathbf{1}[q\succ_t p]-\mathbf{1}[p\succ_t q]\bigr)\).

Thus \(c_{t,p,q}\in\{-\omega_t,0,\omega_t\}\), and the vote margin is \(c^\mathsf{T}z\).

Define \(Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{E}\) by the following constraints:

  • \(z_{t,p,q}\geq0\) for every \((t,p,q)\in E\).
  • \(\sum_{q\in P_t}z_{t,p,q}=x_{t,p}\) for every \(t\in T\) and \(p\in P_t\).
  • For every post \(q\) and subset \(S\subseteq T_q\),

\(\sum_{t\in S}\sum_{p\in P_t}z_{t,p,q}\leq\rho_q(S)\).

These constraints are exactly the reassignment constraints in the question. Indeed, for \(z\in Q\), define

\(y_{t,q}=\sum_{p\in P_t}z_{t,p,q}\) for \(q\in P_t\), and \(y_{t,q}=0\) otherwise.

Nonnegativity and eligibility hold by construction. Conservation follows from the source equalities and feasibility of \(x\):

\(\sum_{q\in P_t}y_{t,q}=\sum_{p\in P_t}\sum_{q\in P_t}z_{t,p,q}=\sum_{p\in P_t}x_{t,p}=\mu_t\).

The third family of inequalities gives every required post constraint. Conversely, every reassignment in the question satisfies all defining constraints of \(Q\).

Consequently, the target question is precisely whether

\(\beta:=\max_{z\in Q}c^\mathsf{T}z>0\).

The formulation retains the actual source post of each portion of mass, so its objective implements the specified flow-coupled comparison.

2. Nonemptiness and boundedness

The reassignment that leaves all mass in place,

\(z^0_{t,p,q}=x_{t,p}\) if \(q=p\), and \(z^0_{t,p,q}=0\) otherwise,

belongs to \(Q\), because its destination allocation is \(x\). Moreover, \(c^\mathsf{T}z^0=0\). Thus \(Q\) is nonempty and \(\beta\geq0\).

For any \(z\in Q\), nonnegativity and the source equality imply

\(0\leq z_{t,p,q}\leq x_{t,p}\leq\mu_t\leq1\).

Hence \(Q\subseteq[0,1]^n\). It is a closed, bounded rational polyhedron, so it is a compact rational polytope and the maximum defining \(\beta\) is attained.

The private last-resort assumptions require no additional argument: feasibility of the supplied \(x\) already establishes nonemptiness.

3. Polynomial-time strong separation

A strong separation oracle, given a rational vector \(\widehat z\), must either certify \(\widehat z\in Q\) or return a rational inequality valid throughout \(Q\) and violated by \(\widehat z\).

First check all nonnegativity constraints and source equalities. There are polynomially many. A violated equality supplies a violated inequality by choosing the appropriate direction.

If these checks pass, compute the destination masses

\(\widehat y_{t,q}=\sum_{p\in P_t}\widehat z_{t,p,q}\).

For each post \(q\), define a function on its supported eligible types by

\(f_q(S)=\rho_q(S)-\sum_{t\in S}\widehat y_{t,q}\), for \(S\subseteq T_q\).

The second term is modular: writing \(a_q(S)=\sum_{t\in S}\widehat y_{t,q}\), one has

\(a_q(A)+a_q(B)=a_q(A\cup B)+a_q(A\cap B)\).

Therefore submodularity of \(\rho_q\) gives

\(f_q(A)+f_q(B)\geq f_q(A\cup B)+f_q(A\cap B)\).

Thus \(f_q\) is submodular. It need not be nondecreasing or nonnegative; neither property is required for unconstrained submodular minimization.

We use the standard theorem that a rational-valued submodular function given by an exact value oracle can be minimized, with a minimizing subset returned, in time polynomial in its ground-set size and the bit bound on its values, together with the oracle evaluation cost. See [Schrijver, Section 45.1](https://page.math.tu-berlin.de/~felsner/Lehre/SemMatS/Literatur/1-A.4.b.pdf).

The hypotheses hold here:

  • The ground set \(T_q\) has at most \(\tau\) elements.
  • Evaluating \(f_q(S)\) requires one supplied rank evaluation and a sum of at most \(\tau\) rational numbers.
  • Rank outputs have polynomial bit length by assumption. The additional rational arithmetic has bit length polynomial in the encoding length of \(\widehat z\) and in \(m,\tau,L\).

Minimize \(f_q\) and obtain a minimizing set \(S_q\). If \(f_q(S_q)<0\), return the violated valid inequality

\(\sum_{t\in S_q}\sum_{p\in P_t}z_{t,p,q}\leq\rho_q(S_q)\).

If the minimum is nonnegative, every subset constraint at post \(q\) holds. Repeating this for all posts either finds a violated constraint or certifies membership in \(Q\).

This constructs a polynomial-time strong separation oracle using at most \(m\) submodular minimizations per call.

4. Exact optimization and encoding bounds

We use the rational-polyhedral separation–optimization theorem: given a rational polyhedron with a known bound on the encoding length of each defining inequality, a polynomial-time strong separation oracle permits exact rational linear optimization in oracle-polynomial time. The theorem applies also to polyhedra of lower dimension; it does not require an interior ball in the ambient space. See [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Chapter 6, particularly Theorem 6.4.9](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4_7).

Here the required bounds are polynomial:

  • The dimension is \(n\leq\tau m^2\).
  • Replace each source equality by two inequalities. Every resulting constraint normal has entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
  • Every right-hand side is \(0\), a signed input entry \(x_{t,p}\), or an output \(\rho_q(S)\). Each has polynomial bit length under the stated input guarantees.
  • Each objective coefficient is a signed input weight or zero, so the objective vector has polynomial encoding length.

Accordingly, each individual defining inequality has polynomial encoding length, despite the exponential number of subset inequalities.

Applying the theorem with the oracle from Section 3 returns an optimal rational vector \(z^\star\). Its objective value

\(\beta=c^\mathsf{T}z^\star\)

is computed and compared with zero using exact rational arithmetic.

The number of oracle calls and the lengths of their rational query vectors are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Each oracle call also takes polynomial time in these parameters and its query length. Therefore the total running time is

\(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\).

This is a bit-complexity guarantee under the assumed polynomial-time rank evaluations.

5. Why strict positivity poses no precision problem

Exact optimization already suffices. A denominator bound additionally shows why a positive optimum cannot be arbitrarily close to zero relative to the input encoding.

Let \(B\) be a polynomial bound such that every defining right-hand side has a reduced denominator at most \(2^B\).

A linear objective on a nonempty compact polytope has an optimal vertex \(v\). At a vertex, the active constraint normals span \(\mathbb{R}^n\): otherwise a nonzero vector orthogonal to all active normals would allow sufficiently small perturbations in both directions while preserving every constraint, contradicting extremality. Thus select \(n\) independent active constraints and write

\(Av=b\),

where \(A\) is a nonsingular integer matrix with entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).

Let \(D\) be the product of the reduced denominators of the selected entries of \(b\). Then \(D\leq2^{nB}\) and \(Db\) is integral. The adjugate formula gives

\(D\det(A)v=\operatorname{adj}(A)(Db)\in\mathbb{Z}^n\).

Because \(c\) is integral, \(c^\mathsf{T}v\) is an integer divided by \(D\det(A)\). Hadamard’s determinant bound yields

\(|\det(A)|\leq n^{n/2}\).

Consequently, if \(\beta>0\), then

\(\beta\geq2^{-nB}n^{-n/2}\).

The negative binary logarithm of this bound is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Crucially, the denominator calculation uses only \(n\) active constraints, not all exponentially many rank values.

6. Decision algorithm and conclusion

Construct the objective and the separation oracle above, optimize exactly over \(Q\), and answer yes precisely when \(\beta>0\).

If the answer is yes, the returned rational optimizer is a reassignment with positive margin. If the answer is no, optimality and the feasible zero-margin reassignment imply \(\beta=0\), so no real reassignment has positive margin.

Thus the specified flow-coupled mass problem is polynomial-time solvable. No discrete popularity characterization or construction theorem for popular fractional solutions is needed.
