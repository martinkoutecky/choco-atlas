The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rooted tree \(T\), rational edge costs \(c_e\), population scale \(M\), and a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over customer types \(t=(v,b)\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), assume every branch has positive surplus. Determine the per-customer bills \(x_t\) that give an efficient allocation in the core against every coalition mass vector \(a_t\in[0,M\mu_t]\), and characterize the unique rule satisfying mass versions of SMP and NSD. A coalition's worth is the maximum of zero and its selected mass's total benefit minus the cost of the minimal subtree connecting the locations of types it selects, where selected masses \(q_t\) satisfy \(0\le q_t\le a_t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(T\) be a finite tree rooted at the supplier \(0\), with \(m\geq1\) nonroot vertices and fixed, nonnegative rational edge costs \(c_e\). Customers occupy nonroot vertices, each of which has positive customer mass; benefits per customer are positive rationals.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \(t=(v_t,b_t)\) with positive mass, each with its mass \(\mu_t\) as a binary rational, where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The support size \(\tau\) is not the number of possible types. The population scale \(M>0\) is rational, actual masses are \(n_t=M\mu_t\), and \(L\) is the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Write \(c_v\) for the cost of the edge entering \(v\), \(D_v\) for its strict descendants, \(C(D_v)=\sum_{u\in D_v}c_u\), \(B_v=\sum_{t:v_t=v}n_tb_t\), and \(W_v=\sum_{u\in D_v}B_u-C(D_v)\). Assume positive branch surplus: \(B_v+W_v-c_v>0\) at every nonroot vertex.
For any real coalition vector \(0\leq a_t\leq n_t\), let \(H(q)\) be the union of paths from \(0\) to vertices with \(q_t>0\), with empty-union cost zero. Its worth is \(V(a)=\max\{0,\max_{0\leq q\leq a}[\sum_tq_tb_t-\sum_{e\in H(q)}c_e]\}\); edge costs are incurred in full whenever used.
An anonymous billing rule assigns a real per-customer bill \(x_t\) to each type throughout this domain. Core requires \(\sum_tn_t(b_t-x_t)=V(n)\) and \(\sum_ta_t(b_t-x_t)\geq V(a)\) for every such coalition vector.
Mass SMP permits merging equal rational masses \(h>0\) from \(k\geq2\) customer blocks at one vertex, with benefits \(b_1,\ldots,b_k\), into one mass-\(h\) block with benefit \(\sum_i b_i\), and permits the inverse split. Neither operation may reduce the participants' total bill; equivalently, their total bills before and after coincide. Other masses and benefits stay fixed; recompute \(M\) and normalize \(\mu\) after each operation.
Put \(X_v=\sum_{t:v_t=v}n_tx_t\). Mass NSD requires, at every nonroot vertex, \(W_v=B_v\Longrightarrow\sum_{u\in D_v}X_u-C(D_v)=X_v\).

Does an anonymous billing rule satisfying core, mass SMP and mass NSD exist uniquely? Is exact computation of an efficient core allocation possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite customers, core, local split/merge-proofness and NSD are claimed to characterize the locally proportional billing rule, given by an explicit formula; the tree value-sharing game is also shown convex. [Verified source](https://www.tse-fr.eu/sites/default/files/TSE/documents/doc/wp/2022/wp_tse_1356.pdf).
No result settling this exact mass model was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Defined m as the number of nonroot tree vertices and L as the total binary input length.
  • Required positive rational benefits, positive rational population scale, nonnegative rational edge costs, and positive customer mass at every nonroot vertex; zero edge costs are allowed because the supplied text explicitly permits null-cost connections.
  • Interpreted positive surplus for every branch as subtree benefit minus all subtree edge costs, including the edge entering its root, being strictly positive.
  • Specified that coalition subtrees connect selected locations to the supplier and incur full fixed edge costs for any positive selected mass.
  • Allowed real coalition and selected masses, and real bills; efficiency and core constraints use actual masses M times mu.
  • Defined mass split/merge operations by parallel copies of the discrete operation: equal-mass blocks merge into one block whose per-customer benefit is their summed benefit. Total benefit is preserved, population scale changes, and the distribution is renormalized.
  • Required the axioms of a billing rule across all admissible societies, rather than only of an allocation at the given input.
  • Retained NSD as its stated conditional equality, using aggregate mass-weighted benefits and bills.

1. Answer

An anonymous rule satisfying core, mass SMP and mass NSD exists, but is not unique in general. An exact efficient core allocation can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Both conclusions hold for the stated model, including core constraints for all real coalition vectors. The nonuniqueness persists even among rules that depend continuously on the vertex benefits.

2. A locally proportional construction

Let \(T_v=\{v\}\cup D_v\), let \(p(v)\) be the parent of \(v\), and define

\(S_v=B_v+W_v,\qquad P_v=S_v-c_v.\)

The positive branch-surplus assumption states precisely that \(P_v>0\). If \(\operatorname{ch}(v)\) denotes the children of \(v\), then the definitions give

\(W_v=\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}P_w,\qquad S_v=B_v+\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}P_w.\)

Consequently, \(S_v>0\), and

\(r_v=\frac{P_v}{S_v}=1-\frac{c_v}{S_v}\)

satisfies \(0<r_v\leq1\).

Set \(R_0=1\) and recursively define \(R_v=R_{p(v)}r_v\). The proposed per-customer surplus and bill are

\(y_t=b_tR_{v_t},\qquad x_t=b_t(1-R_{v_t}).\)

Thus \(y_t=b_t-x_t>0\), and \(0\leq x_t<b_t\).

Edge-by-edge interpretation

For an ancestor \(v\) of \(v_t\), including \(v=v_t\), define

\(d_{t,v}=\frac{c_v}{S_v}\,b_t\prod_{u\in(v,v_t]}r_u,\)

where \((v,v_t]\) is the vertex path excluding \(v\), and an empty product equals \(1\). For other vertices, set \(d_{t,v}=0\).

These are nonnegative per-customer contributions to the edge entering \(v\). Telescoping along the path from \(v_t\) to the root gives

\(\sum_v d_{t,v}=b_t(1-R_{v_t})=x_t.\)

We next verify that each edge is paid exactly.

Define the aggregate residual benefit of a subtree, after charging all its edges, by

\(F_v=\sum_{t:v_t\in T_v}n_tb_t\prod_{u\in[v,v_t]}r_u.\)

Induction from leaves gives

\(F_v=r_v\left(B_v+\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}F_w\right)=r_vS_v=P_v.\)

Here the induction hypothesis is \(F_w=P_w\), and the identity defining \(S_v\) supplies the second equality. Therefore

\(\sum_t n_td_{t,v} =\frac{c_v}{S_v}\left(B_v+\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}F_w\right) =c_v.\)

Summing over edges proves budget balance:

\(\sum_t n_tx_t=\sum_{v\ne0}c_v.\)

3. Proof of core membership for every real coalition

For any real vector \(0\leq q\leq n\), an edge receiving a positive contribution from \(q\) belongs to \(H(q)\). Moreover, nonnegativity and \(q_t\leq n_t\) imply

\(\sum_t q_td_{t,v}\leq\sum_t n_td_{t,v}=c_v.\)

Hence

\(\sum_t q_tx_t =\sum_v\sum_t q_td_{t,v} \leq\sum_{e\in H(q)}c_e.\)

Now fix any coalition vector \(0\leq a\leq n\). For every \(0\leq q\leq a\), positivity of the surplus shares yields

\(\sum_t a_ty_t \geq\sum_t q_ty_t =\sum_t q_tb_t-\sum_t q_tx_t \geq\sum_t q_tb_t-\sum_{e\in H(q)}c_e.\)

Also \(\sum_t a_ty_t\geq0\). Taking the maximum in the definition of worth proves

\(\sum_t a_ty_t\geq V(a).\)

It remains to check efficiency. Every nonroot vertex has positive customer mass, so \(H(n)=T\). Choosing \(q=n\) gives

\(V(n)\geq\sum_t n_tb_t-\sum_{v\ne0}c_v=\sum_t n_ty_t.\)

The already proved coalition inequality, applied to \(a=n\), gives the reverse inequality. Thus

\(V(n)=\sum_t n_ty_t=\sum_{v\ne0}(B_v-c_v).\)

The construction is therefore an efficient core allocation.

4. Mass SMP and mass NSD

Mass SMP

Merging equal masses \(h\) at a vertex \(v\) preserves its aggregate benefit, because

\(\sum_{i=1}^{k}hb_i=h\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_i.\)

Every \(B_u\), and therefore every \(S_u,r_u,R_u\), remains unchanged. The participants’ bills before and after are respectively

\(h(1-R_v)\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_i \quad\text{and}\quad h(1-R_v)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_i\right).\)

They coincide. The same calculation proves invariance under the inverse split. Recomputing \(M\) and normalizing the masses does not affect these actual aggregate benefits.

The rule is anonymous because it assigns bills using only the society and the customer’s type.

Mass NSD

The subtree calculation above implies

\(\sum_{t:v_t\in T_v}n_ty_t=R_{p(v)}P_v.\)

Applying this identity to the children of \(v\) gives

\(\sum_{u\in D_v}\sum_{t:v_t=u}n_ty_t =R_v\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}P_w =R_vW_v.\)

Consequently,

\(\sum_{u\in D_v}X_u-C(D_v)=W_v(1-R_v),\)

whereas

\(X_v=B_v(1-R_v).\)

If \(W_v=B_v\), these quantities are equal, exactly as mass NSD requires.

This proves existence on the entire stated domain.

5. Nonuniqueness: a continuous family of rules

Consider the two-edge chain \(0\to1\to2\). Write

\(w=B_2-c_2.\)

Positive branch surplus is equivalent to

\(w>0,\qquad B_1+w>c_1.\)

Choose aggregate bills of the form

\(X_1=d,\qquad X_2=c_1+c_2-d,\)

and bill proportionally within each vertex:

\(x_t=\frac{X_{v_t}}{B_{v_t}}b_t.\)

We first establish a sufficient interval for core membership:

\(\max\{0,c_1-w\}\leq d\leq\min\{c_1,B_1\}.\)

Indeed, these inequalities imply \(0\leq X_1\leq B_1\) and \(0\leq X_2\leq B_2\), so every surplus share is nonnegative.

For any \(q\) supported only at vertex \(1\), its total bill is at most \(X_1\leq c_1\). If \(q\) has positive mass at vertex \(2\), its total bill is at most \(X_1+X_2=c_1+c_2\). The empty vector has zero bill. Thus, in every case,

\(\sum_t q_tx_t\leq\sum_{e\in H(q)}c_e.\)

The argument in Section 3 proves all real-coalition core inequalities. Budget balance proves efficiency.

Defining the family

Let

\(d_0=\frac{c_1B_1}{B_1+w},\qquad U=\min\{c_1,B_1\},\qquad f=\frac{(B_1-w)^2}{1+(B_1-w)^2}.\)

The locally proportional construction has \(X_1=d_0\). This value belongs to the sufficient interval: \(0\leq d_0\leq U\), and

\(d_0-(c_1-w)=\frac{w(B_1+w-c_1)}{B_1+w}>0.\)

For any fixed \(\theta\in[0,1]\), define

\(d_\theta=d_0+\theta f(U-d_0).\)

Because \(0\leq\theta f\leq1\), this remains in the sufficient interval. Therefore it defines an efficient core rule.

Its coefficients depend only on \(B_1,B_2,c_1,c_2\), which mass split/merge preserves. Hence it satisfies mass SMP.

At vertex \(1\), NSD is triggered precisely when \(B_1=w\). Then \(f=0\), so

\(d_\theta=d_0=\frac{c_1}{2},\qquad X_2-c_2=c_1-d_\theta=d_\theta=X_1.\)

At vertex \(2\), the NSD hypothesis is impossible because \(W_2=0<B_2\). Thus every member of the family satisfies mass NSD. Each also depends continuously on the vertex benefits.

For a rule on all trees, use this construction on two-edge chains and the rule from Section 2 on all other trees. The preceding proofs verify every required axiom throughout that domain.

Explicit difference

Take \(c_1=c_2=1\), with one type at each vertex, both having mass \(1\) and benefit \(3\). This is encoded by \(M=2\) and \(\mu_1=\mu_2=1/2\).

Here \(B_1=B_2=3\), \(w=2\), and

\(d_0=\frac35,\qquad U=1,\qquad f=\frac12.\)

The rules with \(\theta=0\) and \(\theta=1\) give respectively

\((X_1,X_2)=\left(\frac35,\frac75\right) \quad\text{and}\quad (X_1,X_2)=\left(\frac45,\frac65\right).\)

They are distinct anonymous rules satisfying all three axioms. Uniqueness therefore fails.

The underlying issue is substantive: split/merge preserves the vertex benefits, while conditional NSD constrains bills only on its equality locus. In the [cited paper, Appendix K, equation (121)](https://www.tse-fr.eu/sites/default/files/TSE/documents/doc/wp/2022/wp_tse_1356.pdf#page=48), an identity is attributed to NSD without imposing that equality hypothesis. Such an inference is unavailable under the axioms stated here.

6. Exact computational complexity

The allocation from Section 2 requires:

  • Computing \(n_t=M\mu_t\) and aggregating \(B_v\).
  • Computing \(P_v,S_v,r_v\) in a traversal from leaves toward the root.
  • Computing \(R_v=R_{p(v)}r_v\) in a traversal from the root toward the leaves.
  • Returning \(x_t=b_t(1-R_{v_t})\), or the surplus shares \(y_t=b_tR_{v_t}\).

This uses \(O(m+\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations.

To verify polynomial bit complexity, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input rationals. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Each term \(M\mu_tb_t\), and each edge cost, has denominator dividing \(D\). Thus all \(B_v,S_v,P_v\) have \(O(L)\)-bit numerators and denominators after reduction. Each \(r_v\) also has \(O(L)\)-bit numerator and denominator.

A path product contains at most \(m\) such factors, so every \(R_v\), and every output bill or surplus share, has \(O(mL)\)-bit numerator and denominator. Using ordinary exact integer arithmetic and fraction reduction, a conservative running-time bound is

\(O\!\left((m+\tau)(mL)^3\right)\)

bit operations.

Thus exact efficient core allocation is polynomial-time computable directly from the support representation, without expanding rational masses into individual customers.
