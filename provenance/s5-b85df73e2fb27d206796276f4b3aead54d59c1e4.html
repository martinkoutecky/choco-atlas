The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite bid set \(A\), exact rational ballot shares \(q_{r,a}\) for each round, the elimination and tie rules, and a distribution \(\mu\) over strict rankings, determine every winner under sincere exhaustive-ballot voting that is compatible with the observed shares and the paper’s assumptions about first-round choices and revealed strategic behavior.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) be a set of \(m\geq 2\) bids. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) strict rankings with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Masses sum to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
The input also specifies an observed sequence of \(R\) regular and auxiliary ballots, their available bid sets, and exact nonnegative rational shares \(q_{r,a}\) summing to \(1\) on each ballot. All agents participate in every ballot.
Exhaustive ballot elects a bid with share strictly greater than \(1/2\); otherwise it eliminates one bid with minimum share and repeats. If several bids have minimum share, an auxiliary ballot restricted to those bids determines which is eliminated: eliminate a bid with minimum auxiliary share. A supplied strict chair ranking resolves any remaining tie by eliminating its least preferred tied bid; a final two-bid tie elects its preferred bid. The chair ranking supplies no additional voting mass.
A sincere ballot selects the highest-ranked available bid. A ballot history is revealed strategic if, between successive regular ballots, it changes its vote while its previous choice remains available. Every history starts sincerely; histories never revealed strategic must be sincere throughout. Revealed strategic histories may otherwise select any available bid, including returning to sincere voting.
Compatibility means that each ranking's mass can be divided into nonnegative real masses assigned to such histories, preserving its total mass and reproducing every observed share. The observed stopping and elimination decisions must obey the specified rules. No bound on strategic mass is imposed.
For a compatible input, let \(W\) contain the winners obtained by rerunning exhaustive ballot with all mass voting sincerely, using the same tie rules; set \(W=\varnothing\) if the observations are incompatible. The sincere elimination sequence need not match the observed sequence. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,R,L\).

Is deciding, for a supplied bid \(c\in A\), whether \(c\in W\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it NP-hard? Equivalently, can every compatible sincere winner be determined in polynomial time?

For a particular family of reconstructed \(22\)-voter profiles from the 2022 host election, the discrete classification permits Qatar, Japan, or South Korea as sincere winners, depending on lower preferences and the chair's ranking; it establishes no general complexity classification. [Karabekyan's working paper](https://wp.hse.ru/data/2016/03/24/1127939325/130EC2016.pdf)
No result settling this exact mass-and-observed-history problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized society mass and ballot shares to 1, with full participation and no abstention.
  • Used a strict majority threshold rather than transferring an additive one-vote threshold to masses; the discrete example requires 12 of 22 votes.
  • Made observed available sets and auxiliary ballots explicit input, and required the observations to respect stopping and elimination rules.
  • Completed unresolved auxiliary-ballot ties using the supplied chair ranking, eliminating its least preferred tied bid; final two-bid ties favor its preferred bid.
  • Represented the chair's deciding preference separately without adding voting mass.
  • Interpreted nonrevealed histories as sincere throughout, matching the reconstruction's treatment of ordinary transfers after elimination.
  • The source supplies no general restriction on revealed strategic histories beyond sincere first choices and their identifying switches; allowed their subsequent ballots to be arbitrary and imposed no strategic-mass bound.
  • Allowed real subdivisions of each ranking's mass among ballot histories while preserving the supplied distribution exactly.
  • Kept the supplied distribution fixed and permitted the counterfactual sincere elimination sequence to differ from the observed sequence.
  • Defined the winner set to be empty for incompatible observations and used candidate membership as the decision version.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). Compatibility can be tested by a rational linear program with \(\mathrm{O}(\tau Rm^2)\) variables and constraints. Once compatibility is established, the sincere election can be simulated directly.

The total running time is

\(\displaystyle \bigl(L+\tau Rm^2\bigr)^{\mathrm{O}(1)}\)

bit operations.

Because the complete ranking distribution and the chair ranking are supplied, a compatible instance has exactly one sincere winner. The substantive issue is deciding compatibility without enumerating exponentially many voting histories.

1. Definitions and validation of the observed election

Write the supplied rankings as \(\succ_1,\ldots,\succ_\tau\), with masses \(p_1,\ldots,p_\tau\). Thus \(p_i>0\) and \(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}p_i=1\).

For each observed ballot \(r\), let \(B_r\subseteq A\) be its available set. Define

\(\displaystyle t_i(r)=\text{the highest-ranked bid in }B_r\text{ under }\succ_i.\)

A history for ranking \(i\) is a sequence \(h=(a_1,\ldots,a_R)\), where \(a_r\in B_r\). It must satisfy:

  • Its first vote is sincere: \(a_1=t_i(1)\).
  • If it contains no revealing change between successive regular ballots, then \(a_r=t_i(r)\) for every ballot \(r\), including auxiliary ballots.

These are whole-history conditions. In particular, an insincere vote before a later revealing change is permitted. The construction below preserves this possibility.

First validate the observed election using only its ballot types, available sets, shares, and chair ranking:

  • The initial regular ballot has available set \(A\).
  • A regular share strictly greater than \(1/2\) ends the election.
  • A final two-bid tie elects the chair’s preferred bid.
  • Otherwise, a unique minimum-share bid is eliminated directly.
  • If the minimum is attained by several bids, the required auxiliary ballot has exactly that tied set available. Its minimum-share bid is eliminated, with any remaining tie resolved by eliminating the chair’s least preferred tied bid.
  • The following regular ballot removes exactly the eliminated bid. No required auxiliary ballot is omitted, and no ballot follows termination.

All comparisons are exact rational comparisons. These checks take polynomial time; for example, the share comparisons require \(\mathrm{O}(Rm)\) rational operations. If validation fails, compatibility fails.

Henceforth assume that the observed election passes these checks.

2. A polynomial-size graph of admissible histories

Fix ranking \(i\). We construct a layered directed graph \(G_i\), with one layer after each observed ballot.

The state after a ballot is a triple \((b,e,d)\), where:

  • \(b\in A\cup\{\bot\}\) is the vote on the most recent regular ballot; \(\bot\) means that no regular ballot has occurred;
  • \(e\in\{0,1\}\) records whether a revealing change has occurred;
  • \(d\in\{0,1\}\) records whether any vote so far has been insincere.

The initial state is \((\bot,0,0)\).

Suppose the next ballot is \(r\), and the selected bid is \(a\in B_r\). On the first ballot, allow only \(a=t_i(1)\). On subsequent ballots, allow every \(a\in B_r\).

In either case, update the insincerity flag by

\(\displaystyle d'=d\lor \mathbf{1}\{a\ne t_i(r)\}.\)

For a regular ballot, update by

\(\displaystyle e'=e\lor\mathbf{1}\{b\ne\bot,\ b\in B_r,\ a\ne b\},\qquad b'=a.\)

For an auxiliary ballot, update by

\(\displaystyle e'=e,\qquad b'=b.\)

Thus auxiliary votes affect the sincerity flag but do not replace the remembered regular vote.

Each transition is labeled by its ballot \(r\) and selected bid \(a\). Retain parallel transitions when different selected bids produce the same destination state.

At the final layer, connect a state to a sink precisely when

\(\displaystyle e=1\quad\text{or}\quad d=0.\)

Lemma: accepted paths are exactly admissible histories

For every path, induction over the ballots proves that:

  • \(b\) equals the most recent regular vote;
  • \(e=1\) exactly when the history has made a revealing change between successive regular ballots;
  • \(d=1\) exactly when the history contains an insincere vote.

The update for \(e\) uses the previous regular vote even when an auxiliary ballot intervenes. It tests exactly whether that previous vote remains available and the new regular vote differs from it.

The first transition enforces sincerity at the start. The terminal condition accepts every revealed strategic history, and accepts an unrevealed history exactly when every vote was sincere. These are precisely the stipulated conditions. Therefore accepted paths and admissible histories correspond exactly. \(\square\)

There are at most \(4(m+1)\) states per layer and at most \(m\) outgoing vote transitions per state. Consequently, each graph has

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{O}(Rm)\text{ vertices and }\mathrm{O}(Rm^2)\text{ edges}.\)

3. The compatibility linear program

For each edge \(f\) of \(G_i\), introduce a nonnegative real flow variable \(x_{i,f}\).

Let \(s_i,z_i\) denote its source and sink. Impose:

  • Preservation of ranking mass:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{f\in\delta^+(s_i)}x_{i,f}=p_i,\qquad \sum_{f\in\delta^-(z_i)}x_{i,f}=p_i.\)

  • Flow conservation at every other vertex \(v\):

\(\displaystyle \sum_{f\in\delta^-(v)}x_{i,f} =\sum_{f\in\delta^+(v)}x_{i,f}.\)

  • Reproduction of every observed share: for each ballot \(r\) and bid \(a\in B_r\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{\tau} \sum_{\substack{f\text{ is a ballot }r\text{ transition}\\ f\text{ is labeled }a}} x_{i,f}=q_{r,a}.\)

Edges to the sink have no ballot label and do not enter the third set of equations.

Proposition: the linear program is feasible exactly when the observations are compatible

Compatibility implies feasibility. Suppose ranking \(i\)'s mass has been divided among admissible histories. By the lemma, each history determines an accepted source-to-sink path in \(G_i\). Send its assigned mass along that path and sum the resulting edge flows.

Each path conserves flow internally. Their total mass is \(p_i\). At ballot \(r\), paths labeled \(a\) carry exactly the mass voting for \(a\). Hence every equation above holds.

Feasibility implies compatibility. Suppose instead that feasible flows have been supplied. Each \(G_i\) is acyclic, so its nonnegative flow decomposes into source-to-sink path flows.

For completeness, this decomposition can be proved directly. If positive source flow remains, choose a positive outgoing edge. At every reached internal vertex, conservation guarantees a positive outgoing edge. Acyclicity ensures that this process reaches the sink. Subtract from the resulting path the minimum flow on its edges. This preserves nonnegativity and conservation, and makes at least one positive edge zero.

Repeating terminates after at most the original number of positive edges. No nonzero residual flow can remain after the source flow is exhausted: in an acyclic graph, an earliest vertex incident to residual positive outgoing flow would have no residual positive incoming flow, contradicting conservation.

The path masses therefore sum to \(p_i\) and reconstruct every edge flow. By the lemma, each path represents an admissible history. Assign its path mass to that history. The observed-share equations ensure that these histories reproduce every \(q_{r,a}\).

The observed decisions already passed rule validation. Thus these assignments establish compatibility. \(\square\)

This argument applies to real masses; no integrality assumption is introduced.

4. Polynomial-time feasibility and encoding length

Across all ranking types, the linear program has:

  • \(\mathrm{O}(\tau Rm^2)\) variables;
  • \(\mathrm{O}(\tau Rm)\) conservation and mass equations;
  • at most \(Rm\) observed-share equations;
  • one nonnegativity constraint per variable.

Every matrix coefficient belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). The nonzero right-hand sides are the supplied masses and shares.

Set \(N=1+\tau Rm^2\). Even a dense encoding of the resulting linear program has bit length \(\mathrm{O}(N^2+L)\). Its construction therefore takes polynomial time in \(N+L\).

The standard rational linear-programming theorem states that feasibility of a finite system of rational linear inequalities can be decided in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. Equalities can be replaced by pairs of inequalities. This theorem applies here because all coefficients and right-hand sides are rational and explicitly represented; it does not require a full-dimensional feasible region. See [Khachiyan’s original polynomial-time linear-programming result](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

Consequently, compatibility is decidable in

\(\displaystyle \bigl(L+\tau Rm^2\bigr)^{\mathrm{O}(1)}\)

bit operations.

5. Computing the sincere winner

Assume compatibility has been established. For any nonempty available set \(S\), define the sincere share of bid \(a\in S\) by

\(\displaystyle s_S(a)=\sum_{\substack{1\le i\le\tau\\ a\text{ is highest in }S\text{ under }\succ_i}}p_i. \)

These shares are fixed by the supplied society. The allocation of its mass among observed histories does not affect them.

Starting with \(S=A\), compute these shares and apply the specified election rules. When an auxiliary ballot is required on tied set \(T\), compute the same formula with \(S=T\). Strict rankings determine every sincere vote, and the strict chair ranking resolves every remaining tie. Thus each decision is unique.

Every nonterminal stage eliminates one bid. There are at most \(m\) regular ballots and at most \(m\) auxiliary ballots. Scanning each ranking to find its highest available bid gives a total of \(\mathrm{O}(\tau m^2)\) ranking-entry inspections.

Exact arithmetic also has polynomial bit complexity. Write \(p_i=u_i/v_i\) and take the common denominator

\(\displaystyle D=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}v_i.\)

Its bit length is \(\mathrm{O}(L)\). Represent ranking \(i\)'s mass by integer weight \(w_i=u_i(D/v_i)\). Then \(\sum_i w_i=D\), and every tally is an integer between zero and \(D\). Majority tests compare twice a tally with \(D\); minimum-share and tie tests compare integer tallies. No numerical approximation is needed.

The sincere simulation uses \(\mathrm{O}(m(\tau+m))\) additions and comparisons on \(\mathrm{O}(L)\)-bit integers, in addition to the ranking scans and polynomial preprocessing.

6. Decision procedure and conclusion

The complete algorithm is:

  • Validate the supplied data and observed election rules.
  • Test the flow linear program for feasibility.
  • If either test fails, return \(W=\varnothing\).
  • Otherwise, simulate the sincere election, obtaining its unique winner \(w\), and return \(W=\{w\}\).

For a supplied bid \(c\), membership is then decided by testing whether \(c=w\).

This settles the stated problem for an explicitly supplied support of size \(\tau\), with freely divisible real history masses. Its running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,R,L\), specifically \(\bigl(L+\tau Rm^2\bigr)^{\mathrm{O}(1)}\) bit operations.
