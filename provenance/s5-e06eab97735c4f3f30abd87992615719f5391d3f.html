The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two equally sized populations, finite sets of preference types ranking counterpart types, and an explicit finite list \(\mathcal E\) of balanced rational mass profiles closed under role swap, find a randomized type-report mechanism minimizing \(\max_{e\in\mathcal E} V_e\), and determine whether an optimal mechanism can be role-symmetric. For each profile \(e\), truthful type lotteries must induce feasible flows \(\mu_a^e q^S_{eab}=\nu_b^e q^C_{eba}=x^e_{ab}\), and satisfy first-order stochastic-dominance truthfulness when an individual report changes while the population masses stay fixed. Define \(z^e_{ab}=\max\{1-\sum_{b'\succeq_a b}q^S_{eab'}-\sum_{a'\succeq_b a}q^C_{eba'},0\}\) and \(V_e=\sum_{a,b}\mu_a^e\nu_b^e z^e_{ab}\).

A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is support size, not the number of possible types. Each side has total mass \(1\). Catalogs coincide with supports.
The input includes a nonempty explicit finite list \(\mathcal E\) closed under an involution \(e\mapsto e^{\leftrightarrow}\) that exchanges sides, catalogs, rankings, and masses. Let \(m\) be the maximum catalog size, \(\tau\) the maximum combined support size, and \(L\) the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L,|\mathcal E|\).
A mechanism specifies nonnegative report lotteries \(q^S_{eab},q^C_{eba}\) with \(\sum_b q^S_{eab}\leq1\) and \(\sum_a q^C_{eba}\leq1\); residual probability means unmatched. Truthful outcomes must satisfy \(\mu_a^e q^S_{eab}=\nu_b^e q^C_{eba}=x^e_{ab}\). Unmatched mass is permitted.
Individual reports leave \(e\) and its masses fixed: require \(\sum_{b'\succeq_a b}q^S_{eab'}\geq\sum_{b'\succeq_a b}q^S_{er b'}\) for every true type \(a\), report \(r\in A_e\), and cutoff \(b\in B_e\), and the analogous inequalities on the other side. Result

The optimization problem is polynomial-time solvable, and every instance admits a role-symmetric optimal mechanism.

The proof uses the stipulated interpretation that an individual report leaves the profile and its masses fixed. The explicit catalogs and profile list therefore determine all variables and incentive constraints.

The cited [finite-market result](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12431v3) gives related symmetrization conclusions. The proof below establishes both claims directly for the continuous model and the precise violation measure in the question.

1. An exact linear-programming formulation

For each profile \(e\), write \(s_e=|A_e|\), \(t_e=|B_e|\), and define the ranking prefixes

\(U^e_a(b)=\{b'\in B_e:b'\succeq_a b\}\), \(D^e_b(a)=\{a'\in A_e:a'\succeq_b a\}\).

Use variables \(q^S_{eab},q^C_{eba}\), auxiliary variables \(u^e_{ab}\), and a common objective variable \(T\). Minimize \(T\) subject to the following constraints.

Lottery feasibility:

\(q^S_{eab}\geq0,\quad q^C_{eba}\geq0,\quad \sum_{b\in B_e}q^S_{eab}\leq1,\quad \sum_{a\in A_e}q^C_{eba}\leq1.\)

Truthful mass consistency:

\(\mu_a^e q^S_{eab}=\nu_b^e q^C_{eba}\qquad(e\in\mathcal E,\ a\in A_e,\ b\in B_e).\)

Once these equalities hold, recover the matching masses by setting \(x^e_{ab}=\mu_a^e q^S_{eab}\).

Strategy-proofness on the first side:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{b'\in U^e_a(b)}q^S_{eab'}\geq\sum_{b'\in U^e_a(b)}q^S_{er b'}\qquad(a,r\in A_e,\ b\in B_e).\)

Strategy-proofness on the second side:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a'\in D^e_b(a)}q^C_{eba'}\geq\sum_{a'\in D^e_b(a)}q^C_{ed a'}\qquad(b,d\in B_e,\ a\in A_e).\)

Here the prefixes always use the true type’s ranking, as required.

Violation and worst-case bounds:

\(\displaystyle u^e_{ab}\geq1-\sum_{b'\in U^e_a(b)}q^S_{eab'}-\sum_{a'\in D^e_b(a)}q^C_{eba'},\qquad 0\leq u^e_{ab}\leq1,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a\in A_e,b\in B_e}\mu_a^e\nu_b^e u^e_{ab}\leq T\qquad(e\in\mathcal E),\qquad 0\leq T\leq1.\)

Every constraint is linear with rational coefficients.

Exact equivalence

For any feasible mechanism, both prefix sums defining \(z^e_{ab}\) are nonnegative. Consequently,

\(0\leq z^e_{ab}\leq1.\)

Moreover, the weights sum to one:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a,b}\mu_a^e\nu_b^e=\left(\sum_a\mu_a^e\right)\left(\sum_b\nu_b^e\right)=1.\)

Thus \(0\leq V_e\leq1\). Taking \(u^e_{ab}=z^e_{ab}\) and \(T=\max_e V_e\) extends any feasible mechanism to a feasible LP point with the same objective value.

Conversely, at any feasible LP point, the nonnegativity and affine lower bound on \(u^e_{ab}\) imply

\(u^e_{ab}\geq z^e_{ab}.\)

Since all weights are positive,

\(\displaystyle V_e\leq\sum_{a,b}\mu_a^e\nu_b^e u^e_{ab}\leq T.\)

The lottery variables satisfy exactly the original feasibility, consistency, and incentive conditions. Therefore the LP and the mechanism problem have equal optimal values, and the lottery variables of an LP optimum form an optimal mechanism.

The LP is nonempty: take all lottery probabilities zero, all \(u^e_{ab}=1\), and \(T=1\). It is also bounded and closed: lottery probabilities lie in \([0,1]\), and the remaining variables have explicit bounds. Hence an optimum exists.

2. Encoding size and running time

Let \(N=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}s_et_e\). The LP has \(3N+1\) variables, hence at most \(3|\mathcal E|m^2+1\).

The number of incentive inequalities is exactly

\(\displaystyle \sum_{e\in\mathcal E}(s_e^2t_e+t_e^2s_e)=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}s_et_e(s_e+t_e).\)

Because \(s_e,t_e\leq m\) and \(s_e+t_e\leq\tau\), the total number of constraints, including the other constraints above, is

\(O(|\mathcal E|m^2\tau).\)

Each coefficient has \(O(L)\) bits. In particular, a product \(\mu_a^e\nu_b^e\) has bit length at most the sum of its two factors’ bit lengths, up to a constant.

For completeness, the incentive rows contain at most

\(\displaystyle 2\sum_e s_e^2t_e^2+2\sum_e t_e^2s_e^2=4\sum_e s_e^2t_e^2\)

coefficient occurrences. Since \(s_et_e\leq m^2\) and \(s_et_e\leq\tau^2/4\), this is \(O(|\mathcal E|m^2\tau^2)\). All remaining rows fit within the same bound. A straightforward sparse rational encoding therefore has bit length

\(B=O(|\mathcal E|m^2\tau^2L).\)

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming supplies an exact rational optimal solution in time polynomial in the encoded LP length. Its hypotheses hold here: the LP is explicitly given, rational, feasible, bounded, and has a finite optimum. Thus the construction and exact optimization take

\((|\mathcal E|m^2\tau^2L)^{O(1)}\)

bit operations. Equivalently, the running time is polynomial in \(|\mathcal E|m^2\tau\) and \(L\).

Because the catalogs and profile list are explicit parts of the input, these parameters are bounded polynomially in \(L\). This is a polynomial-time algorithm in the stipulated input model; it does not enumerate rational-mass populations or unlisted types.

3. Role-symmetric optimality

Write \(\bar e=e^{\leftrightarrow}\), using the catalog identifications specified by the involution. In particular,

\(\mu_a^e=\nu_a^{\bar e},\qquad \nu_b^e=\mu_b^{\bar e}.\)

For a feasible mechanism \(q\), define its side-exchanged mechanism \(Rq\) by

\((Rq)^S_{eab}=q^C_{\bar e,a b},\qquad (Rq)^C_{eba}=q^S_{\bar e,b a}.\)

Side exchange preserves feasibility

Nonnegativity and lottery budgets are preserved because each first-side lottery becomes a second-side lottery at the exchanged profile, and conversely.

For mass consistency,

\(\mu_a^e(Rq)^S_{eab}=\nu_a^{\bar e}q^C_{\bar e,a b}=x^{\bar e}_{ba},\)

and

\(\nu_b^e(Rq)^C_{eba}=\mu_b^{\bar e}q^S_{\bar e,b a}=x^{\bar e}_{ba}.\)

Thus the matching masses of \(Rq\) are \((Rx)^e_{ab}=x^{\bar e}_{ba}\).

A first-side incentive inequality for \(Rq\) at \(e\) is precisely the second-side incentive inequality for \(q\) at \(\bar e\): the involution preserves the corresponding true ranking, report catalog, and prefix. The reverse statement handles the other side. Therefore \(Rq\) is feasible.

Every original feasibility condition is linear, so the average

\(\widehat q=\tfrac12(q+Rq)\)

is also feasible.

Averaging preserves the worst-case bound

Under side exchange, the two prefix sums in the violation formula exchange roles. Consequently,

\(z^e_{ab}(Rq)=z^{\bar e}_{ba}(q).\)

The masses exchange as well, giving

\(V_e(Rq)=V_{\bar e}(q).\)

For fixed \(e\), each violation term is the maximum of an affine function and zero, hence is convex in the lottery variables. A positively weighted sum of these terms is convex. Therefore,

\(\displaystyle V_e(\widehat q)\leq\tfrac12V_e(q)+\tfrac12V_e(Rq)=\tfrac12\bigl(V_e(q)+V_{\bar e}(q)\bigr)\leq\max_{f\in\mathcal E}V_f(q).\)

Taking the maximum over \(e\) proves

\(\displaystyle \max_eV_e(\widehat q)\leq\max_eV_e(q).\)

This establishes the required worst-case comparison; it does not require each individual profile’s violation to decrease.

The averaged mechanism is role-symmetric

Its matching masses satisfy

\(\displaystyle \widehat x^e_{ab}=\tfrac12\bigl(x^e_{ab}+x^{\bar e}_{ba}\bigr).\)

Since the profile map is an involution,

\(\displaystyle \widehat x^{\bar e}_{ba}=\tfrac12\bigl(x^{\bar e}_{ba}+x^e_{ab}\bigr)=\widehat x^e_{ab}.\)

Thus \(\widehat q\) is role-symmetric, including at profiles fixed by the involution.

Finally, apply this construction to an optimal mechanism, whose existence was proved above. The averaged mechanism is feasible and has objective no larger than the optimum, so it must also be optimal. Averaging and recovering its rational matching masses require polynomial time.
