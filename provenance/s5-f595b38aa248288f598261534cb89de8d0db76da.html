Result

Exact computation is polynomial-time solvable in the total binary input length \(L\) and the listed support size \(\tau\), for the fixed policy dimension \(m=2\). The entire equilibrium set has an exact representation by four rational median endpoints and the prescribed rational map.

We prove the characterization for arbitrary positive rational masses, including all majority ties, and then give a bit-complexity bound. The proof is self-contained; the cited [Borissov–Hanna–Lambrecht paper](https://eusp.org/sites/default/files/archive/ec_dep/wp/Ec-01_14.pdf) provides the motivating separate-voting framework.

1. Conditional policy preferences

Write the coefficients of the supplied utility function as

\(A_i=\dfrac{1-\beta_i+(1+\delta_i)\alpha\beta_i}{1-\beta_i}\), and \(B_i=\dfrac{(1+\delta_i)(1-\alpha)\beta_i}{1-\beta_i}\).

The input assumptions imply \(A_i>0\) and \(B_i>0\). Thus

\(U_i(\theta,\lambda)=\delta_i\ln\theta+A_i\ln(1-\theta-\lambda)+B_i\ln\lambda\).

Fix a feasible value of \(\lambda\). On the interval \(0<\theta<1-\lambda\),

\(\dfrac{\partial U_i}{\partial\theta}=\dfrac{\delta_i}{\theta}-\dfrac{A_i}{1-\lambda-\theta}\).

If \(\delta_i>0\), this derivative is positive below, and negative above, the unique conditional ideal point

\(p_i^\theta(\lambda)=\dfrac{\delta_i}{\delta_i+A_i}(1-\lambda)=\kappa_i(1-\lambda)\).

The last equality follows from

\(\delta_i+A_i=\dfrac{(1+\delta_i)(1-\beta_i+\alpha\beta_i)}{1-\beta_i}\).

If \(\delta_i=0\), utility is strictly decreasing in \(\theta\). We represent its boundary ideal point by \(p_i^\theta(\lambda)=0\), consistently with \(\kappa_i=0\).

Similarly, fixing a feasible value of \(\theta\),

\(\dfrac{\partial U_i}{\partial\lambda}=\dfrac{B_i}{\lambda}-\dfrac{A_i}{1-\theta-\lambda}\).

This derivative changes sign from positive to negative at

\(p_i^\lambda(\theta)=\dfrac{B_i}{A_i+B_i}(1-\theta)=\chi_i(1-\theta)\),

because \(A_i+B_i=\dfrac{1+\delta_i\beta_i}{1-\beta_i}\).

Consequently, both conditional preferences are strictly single-peaked, allowing a boundary ideal point at zero for the consumption coordinate.

2. Weighted medians characterize unbeaten policies

We use the following elementary lemma, proved here to account for strict majority voting and retained ties.

Lemma. Consider finitely many voters with positive weights summing to one, choosing a policy in an interval \((0,b)\). Suppose voter \(i\) has an ideal point \(p_i\in[0,b)\), with utility strictly increasing below \(p_i\) and strictly decreasing above \(p_i\). For \(p_i=0\), assume utility is strictly decreasing throughout the feasible interval. A feasible policy \(t\) is unbeaten by every strict-majority challenge if and only if

\(\mu(p\leq t)\geq\dfrac12\quad\text{and}\quad\mu(p\geq t)\geq\dfrac12\).

Proof. Suppose both inequalities hold. If a challenger \(s\) satisfies \(s>t\), every voter with \(p_i\leq t\) strictly prefers \(t\) to \(s\). Therefore the mass strictly preferring \(s\) is at most

\(\mu(p>t)=1-\mu(p\leq t)\leq\dfrac12\).

For a challenger \(s<t\), every voter with \(p_i\geq t\) strictly prefers \(t\), so the challenger’s supporting mass is at most \(\mu(p<t)\leq\dfrac12\). Neither challenge wins.

Conversely, if \(\mu(p\leq t)<\dfrac12\), the voters with \(p_i>t\) have mass greater than one half. Because the support is finite, choose

\(0<\varepsilon<\min\bigl\{b-t,\ \min_{i:p_i>t}(p_i-t)\bigr\}\).

Then \(t+\varepsilon\) is feasible and lies below every ideal point in that group. Every voter in the group strictly prefers \(t+\varepsilon\), yielding a winning challenge.

If \(\mu(p\geq t)<\dfrac12\), choose analogously

\(0<\varepsilon<\min\bigl\{t,\ \min_{i:p_i<t}(t-p_i)\bigr\}\).

The challenge \(t-\varepsilon\) wins. This includes voters with \(p_i=0\), whose utilities strictly increase when the policy decreases. These cases exhaust failure of the median conditions. \(\square\)

For a positive scalar \(s\), multiplication by \(s\) preserves order, so \(M_{sx}=sM_x\). Applying the lemma to the ideal points derived above gives:

  • The consumption coordinate is unbeaten exactly when \(\theta/(1-\lambda)\in M_\kappa\).
  • The productive-goods coordinate is unbeaten exactly when \(\lambda/(1-\theta)\in M_\chi\).

Hence a feasible pair is a separate-majority equilibrium exactly when both conditions hold.

3. Exact equilibrium-set characterization and interiority

From the coefficient expressions,

\(0\leq\kappa_i=\dfrac{\delta_i}{\delta_i+A_i}<1\), and \(0<\chi_i=\dfrac{B_i}{A_i+B_i}<1\).

Moreover, \(\kappa_i=0\) exactly when \(\delta_i=0\). Thus

\(\mu(\kappa=0)=\mu(\delta=0)<\dfrac12\).

Every weighted median of \(\kappa\) is therefore strictly positive: at zero, its lower cumulative mass is less than one half, and negative values have lower cumulative mass zero. Since the support is finite, both median sets are contained in the respective ranges of the statistics. Consequently,

\(M_\kappa\subset(0,1)\), and \(M_\chi\subset(0,1)\).

For any \(k\in M_\kappa\) and \(c\in M_\chi\), the equilibrium conditions become

\(\theta=k(1-\lambda)\), and \(\lambda=c(1-\theta)\).

The determinant of this linear system is \(1-kc>0\). Its unique solution is

\(F(k,c)=\left(\dfrac{k-kc}{1-kc},\dfrac{c-kc}{1-kc}\right)\).

Both coordinates are positive. Furthermore,

\(1-\theta-\lambda=\dfrac{(1-k)(1-c)}{1-kc}>0\),

so the solution is feasible and interior.

Conversely, every equilibrium determines \(k=\theta/(1-\lambda)\in M_\kappa\) and \(c=\lambda/(1-\theta)\in M_\chi\), and therefore equals \(F(k,c)\). We have proved the exact equality

\(E=\{F(k,c):k\in M_\kappa,\ c\in M_\chi\}\).

The inverse formulas also show that \(F\) is injective on this parameter domain. Thus the equilibrium is unique exactly when both median sets are singletons.

4. Computing the complete weighted median intervals

Let \(x\) be either statistic. Sort its distinct values as

\(v_1<\cdots<v_r\),

and let \(h_j>0\) be the total mass at \(v_j\). Define \(S_0=0\) and \(S_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}h_\ell\). Set

\(j_-=\min\{j:S_j\geq\dfrac12\}\), and \(j_+=\min\{j:S_j>\dfrac12\}\).

Both indices exist because \(S_r=1\). Then

\(M_x=[v_{j_-},v_{j_+}]\).

To verify this formula, if \(z<v_{j_-}\), then \(\mu(x\leq z)\leq S_{j_--1}<\dfrac12\). If \(z>v_{j_+}\), then \(\mu(x<z)\geq S_{j_+}>\dfrac12\), so \(\mu(x\geq z)<\dfrac12\).

For any \(v_{j_-}\leq z\leq v_{j_+}\), however,

\(\mu(x\leq z)\geq S_{j_-}\geq\dfrac12\),

and

\(\mu(x\geq z)\geq1-S_{j_+-1}\geq\dfrac12\).

This proves the formula, including every tie.

The algorithm therefore:

  • Computes each rational \(\kappa_i\) and \(\chi_i\).
  • Sorts each statistic, groups equal values, and accumulates their masses.
  • Computes the four endpoints \(k_-,k_+,c_-,c_+\).
  • Returns \(F(k_-,c_-)\) if both intervals are singletons; otherwise returns the exact representation \(E=F([k_-,k_+]\times[c_-,c_+])\).

This finite representation specifies the entire set even when it contains infinitely many policies. Equivalently, within the feasible policy domain, it is specified by the four rational linear inequalities

\(k_-(1-\lambda)\leq\theta\leq k_+(1-\lambda)\),

\(c_-(1-\theta)\leq\lambda\leq c_+(1-\theta)\).

5. Bit complexity

Use binary rational representations with positive denominators. Reduction to lowest terms is unnecessary for the algorithm.

Each statistic uses a fixed number of rational arithmetic operations. Its numerator and denominator therefore have bit length \(O(L)\). Comparisons of two statistics can be made exactly by integer cross-multiplication.

For cumulative weights, write \(w_i=p_i/q_i\) and form

\(Q=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}q_i\), and \(W_i=p_i(Q/q_i)\).

Because the denominator encodings are included in \(L\), the bit length of \(Q\) is \(O(L)\). The integers \(W_i\) are positive and satisfy \(\sum_iW_i=Q\). Every cumulative-weight comparison with one half can therefore be performed by comparing \(2\sum_iW_i\), over the relevant prefix, with \(Q\). All these integers have bit length \(O(L)\).

Using elementary integer multiplication and division, each required multiplication, division, or rational comparison costs \(O(L^2)\) bit operations. The two sorts use \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) comparisons. Computing the statistics, scaled weights, and cumulative sums requires \(O(\tau)\) further arithmetic operations. Thus a valid overall bound is

\(O\bigl((\tau\log(\tau+1)+1)L^2\bigr)\)

bit operations. The four rational endpoints, and the unique pair when applicable, have total output length \(O(L)\).

The binary precision of the masses does not require expanding them into individual voters. The algorithm operates directly on the \(\tau\) listed types. Competitive-path uniqueness is a given promise; with the supplied utilities, no competitive path or logarithm needs to be computed.

This establishes deterministic polynomial-time exact computation for the specified problem with \(m=2\).
