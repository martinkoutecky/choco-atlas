\(A_t=\sum_{j=1}^m v_{tj}q_j,\qquad A_*=\min_t A_t,\qquad \mu_*=\min_t\mu_t,\qquad S=\max_t\frac{A_t}{\mu_t}.\)

These quantities are positive rationals. Every feasible allocation satisfies \(a_{tj}\leq q_j/\mu_t\), so every feasible utility satisfies \(u_t\leq S\).

Let

\(f(B)=U_1(B)-U_0(B).\)

We will prove that \(f\) has opposite signs at two rational budgets of polynomial encoding length and satisfies

\(\lvert f(B)-f(B')\rvert\leq K\lvert B-B'\rvert\quad\text{for all }B,B'\geq1,\qquad K=\frac{2S^2}{\mu_*A_*}.\)

The constant \(K\) can be exponentially large numerically, but its logarithm is polynomially bounded. That is sufficient for polynomial-time bisection.

2. Equilibrium identities and a utility lower bound. Fix \(B\geq1\), and write \(b_t=b_{z_t}\).

The feasible allocation set is compact. The allocation \(a_{tj}=q_j/2\) has strictly positive utilities and strictly satisfies every supply constraint. An objective-maximizing sequence cannot converge to an allocation with any zero utility: the corresponding positive-weight logarithmic term would tend to negative infinity, while all other utilities are bounded above by \(S\). Consequently, a maximizer with positive utilities exists. Strict concavity in the utility vector gives uniqueness of that vector.

Slater’s condition holds at the allocation just described. Thus the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions yield supply multipliers \(p_j\) satisfying

\(p_j\geq \frac{b_t v_{tj}}{u_t(B)},\qquad a_{tj}(B)>0\ \Longrightarrow\ p_j=\frac{b_t v_{tj}}{u_t(B)}.\)

Here the factor \(\mu_t\) cancels between the objective derivative and the supply-constraint derivative.

All \(p_j\) are positive, because all valuations, budgets, and equilibrium utilities are positive. Complementary slackness therefore implies that every supply constraint is tight. Multiplying the equality on allocated items by their quantities and summing gives

\(p\cdot a_t(B)=b_t.\)

In particular, with \(W(B)=M_0+BM_1\),

\(p\cdot q=\sum_t\mu_t\,p\cdot a_t(B)=W(B).\)

Define \(\alpha_t=\max_j v_{tj}/p_j\). The displayed optimality conditions imply

\(u_t(B)=b_t\alpha_t.\)

They also imply that every nonnegative bundle \(x\) with \(p\cdot x\leq b_t\) satisfies \(v_t\cdot x\leq u_t(B)\).

Apply this observation to \(x=(b_t/W(B))q\), whose price is exactly \(b_t\). We obtain

\(u_t(B)\geq \frac{b_t}{W(B)}A_t.\)

Since \(M_0+M_1=1\) and \(B\geq1\), we have \(W(B)\leq B\). Hence, for every type in group \(1\),

\[u_t(B)\geq A_t\geq A_*.\tag{1}\]

This lower bound is uniform over the entire budget range.

3. A finite interval containing a zero of the gap. Define

\(v_-=\min_{t,j}v_{tj},\qquad v_+=\max_{t,j}v_{tj},\qquad R=\frac{v_+}{v_-},\qquad D=2R.\)

Using the two groups’ budgets,

\(u_s(B)\geq\frac{B}{R}u_t(B).\)

Average this inequality over group \(0\), and then over group \(1\), using the normalized mass weights. The result is

\(U_1(B)\geq\frac{B}{R}U_0(B).\)

\(f(D)\geq U_0(D)>0.\)

The promised assumption gives \(f(1)<0\). Section 4 proves continuity, so these endpoint signs will establish the existence of a zero in \([1,D]\). The encoding length of \(D\) is polynomial in \(L\).

4. An explicit Lipschitz bound. Let \(\mathcal U\) be the set of feasible utility vectors. It is convex and compact because it is the linear image of the feasible allocation set. For positive \(u\in\mathcal U\), define

\(F_B(u)=\sum_t\mu_t b_{z_t}\log u_t.\)

Its unique maximizer is \(u(B)\).

Fix \(B,B'\geq1\), and set \(x=u(B)\), \(y=u(B')\), and \(\Delta=x-y\). The segment between \(x\) and \(y\) lies in \(\mathcal U\) and has positive coordinates. First-order optimality along this segment gives

\(\nabla F_B(x)\cdot\Delta\geq0,\qquad \nabla F_{B'}(y)\cdot\Delta\leq0.\)

Consequently,

\[0\leq\bigl(\nabla F_B(x)-\nabla F_{B'}(y)\bigr)\cdot\Delta.\tag{2}\]

We bound the two parts of this gradient difference separately. First, since \(x_t,y_t\leq S\) and \(b_{z_t}\geq1\),

\[\begin{aligned} \bigl(\nabla F_B(x)-\nabla F_B(y)\bigr)\cdot\Delta &=\sum_t\mu_t b_{z_t}\left(\frac1{x_t}-\frac1{y_t}\right)(x_t-y_t)\\ &=-\sum_t\frac{\mu_t b_{z_t}}{x_ty_t}(x_t-y_t)^2\\ &\leq-\frac{\mu_*}{S^2}\|\Delta\|_2^2. \end{aligned}\tag{3}\]

Second, the coordinates of \(\nabla F_B(y)-\nabla F_{B'}(y)\) are zero on group \(0\) and equal to \(\mu_t(B-B')/y_t\) on group \(1\). By (1),

\[\begin{aligned} \|\nabla F_B(y)-\nabla F_{B'}(y)\|_2 &=|B-B'|\left(\sum_{t:z_t=1}\frac{\mu_t^2}{y_t^2}\right)^{1/2}\\ &\leq\frac{|B-B'|}{A_*}. \end{aligned}\tag{4}\]

The last inequality uses \(\sum_{t:z_t=1}\mu_t^2\leq(\sum_t\mu_t)^2=1\).

Combining (2)–(4) with Cauchy–Schwarz yields

\(0\leq-\frac{\mu_*}{S^2}\|\Delta\|_2^2+\frac{|B-B'|}{A_*}\|\Delta\|_2.\)

If \(\Delta\neq0\), divide by its norm; if \(\Delta=0\), the resulting bound already holds. Thus

\(\|u(B)-u(B')\|_2\leq\frac{S^2}{\mu_*A_*}|B-B'|.\)

Each group average is a convex combination of its types’ utilities. Therefore

\(|U_z(B)-U_z(B')|\leq\max_t|u_t(B)-u_t(B')|\leq\|u(B)-u(B')\|_2.\)

Adding the bounds for the two groups proves

\[|f(B)-f(B')|\leq K|B-B'|,\qquad K=\frac{2S^2}{\mu_*A_*}.\tag{5}\]

In particular, \(f\) is continuous.

5. Exact equilibrium computation with support-encoded masses. We use the established exact-computation theorem for linear Fisher markets: positive rational budgets, rational nonnegative valuations, and unit supplies admit polynomial-time exact equilibrium computation when every buyer values some good and every good has a buyer with positive valuation. The algorithm produces rational prices and a rational equilibrium allocation with polynomial encoding length. Rational-input scaling is discussed in Section 2, and the polynomial algorithm is established in Theorem 8.12 of [Devanur, Papadimitriou, Saberi, and Vazirani, *Market Equilibrium via a Primal–Dual Algorithm for a Convex Program*](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/market.pdf).

For a rational \(B\), introduce

\(y_{tj}=\frac{\mu_t a_{tj}}{q_j},\qquad c_{tj}=v_{tj}q_j,\qquad e_t=\mu_t b_{z_t}.\)

Then the supply constraints become \(\sum_t y_{tj}\leq1\), and

\(\sum_t\mu_t b_{z_t}\log(v_t\cdot a_t) =\sum_t e_t\log\left(\sum_j c_{tj}y_{tj}\right)-\sum_t e_t\log\mu_t.\)

The final sum is constant with respect to \(y\). Thus this is exactly a linear Fisher market with \(\tau\) buyers, budgets \(e_t\), and valuations \(c_{tj}\).

Every budget and valuation is a positive rational, so all participation hypotheses hold. The transformed instance has encoding length polynomial in the original input length and the encoding length of \(B\).

For completeness, converting its data to integers does not require expanding any mass. Multiply all budgets by one common denominator and, independently for each buyer, multiply that buyer’s valuation vector by a common denominator. The first operation scales the objective; each second operation adds a constant to it. Neither changes its maximizers. Products of the relevant denominators have bit lengths bounded by the sum of their bit lengths, hence polynomially bounded.

After computing a rational equilibrium \(y\), return

\(a_{tj}=\frac{q_jy_{tj}}{\mu_t}.\)

This is an exact rational equilibrium allocation for the requested instance. Its encoding length is polynomial, as are the lengths of its utilities and the exactly computed gap \(f(B)\).

6. Bisection and its bit-complexity bound. Choose the smallest nonnegative integer \(N\) satisfying

\(2^N\geq\frac{K(D-1)}{\epsilon}.\)

Compute exact equilibria at the initial endpoints \(\ell=1\) and \(r=D\). They satisfy \(f(\ell)<0<f(r)\).

Perform at most \(N\) iterations:

  • Set \(c=(\ell+r)/2\), compute an exact rational equilibrium at \(c\), and evaluate its rational gap.
  • If \(|f(c)|\leq\epsilon\), return \(c\) and that allocation.
  • Otherwise, replace \(\ell\) by \(c\) when \(f(c)<0\), or replace \(r\) by \(c\) when \(f(c)>0\). Retain the computed allocation for the replaced endpoint.

If no iteration returns, the final interval still has opposite endpoint signs and has width

\(r-\ell=\frac{D-1}{2^N}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{K}.\)

Continuity gives a zero \(\beta\in[\ell,r]\). By (5),

\(|f(\ell)|=|f(\ell)-f(\beta)|\leq K|\ell-\beta|\leq\epsilon.\)

Return the rational budget \(\ell\) and its stored exact equilibrium allocation.

It remains to bound \(N\) and the lengths of the queried budgets. Every positive input rational lies between \(2^{-L}\) and \(2^L\). Therefore

\(\mu_*\geq2^{-L},\qquad A_*\geq2^{-2L},\qquad S\leq m\,2^{3L},\qquad D\leq2^{2L+1}.\)

It follows that

\(K\leq2m^2\,2^{9L},\qquad \frac{K(D-1)}{\epsilon}\leq4m^2\,2^{12L}.\)

Hence \(N=O(L+\log m)\).

After \(k\) bisections, each endpoint lies on the grid

\(1+(D-1)\frac{h}{2^k},\qquad 0\leq h\leq2^k,\)

with integer \(h\). Thus every queried budget has encoding length \(O(\operatorname{bits}(D)+N)\), which is polynomial in the input length. Each exact Fisher solve consequently has polynomial bit complexity. All remaining rational arithmetic and sign comparisons also have polynomial bit complexity.

The algorithm uses at most \(N+2=O(L+\log m)\) exact equilibrium solves and returns the required rational budget and rational equilibrium allocation in polynomial total time.
