The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) with rational masses on a line segment, compute the lottery \(F(\mu)=\tfrac12\delta_{\bar x}+\tfrac12\mu\), where \(\bar x=\int x\,d\mu(x)\), and establish its exact worst-case miniSOS ratio and its realization as the limit of anonymous, exactly strategyproof finite mechanisms.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let the report and facility domain be a nondegenerate closed interval \(I=[a,b]\), with rational endpoints \(a<b\). A society is given by its support: \(\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}p_j\delta_{x_j}\), with distinct rational \(x_j\in I\), positive binary rational masses \(p_j\), and \(\sum_jp_j=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. There is no candidate-set parameter \(m\); state running times in \(\tau,L\), and population size \(n\) where applicable.
Define \(\bar x=\int_I x\,d\mu(x)\) and \(F(\mu)=\tfrac12\delta_{\bar x}+\tfrac12\mu\). Individual cost under lottery \(P\) is \(\mathbb E_{Y\sim P}|Y-x|\), whereas miniSOS social cost is \(C(P,\mu)=\mathbb E_{Y\sim P}\int_I|Y-x|^2\,d\mu(x)\).
Let \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)=\min_{y\in I}\int_I|y-x|^2\,d\mu(x)\) and \(\rho(F)=\sup_{\mu:\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)>0}C(F(\mu),\mu)/\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\). Point-mass societies remain admissible but are excluded from this quotient.
A finite mechanism \(M_n:I^n\to\Delta(I)\) is exactly strategyproof when \(\mathbb E_{Y\sim M_n(\mathbf x)}|Y-x_i|\leq\mathbb E_{Y\sim M_n(x_i',\mathbf x_{-i})}|Y-x_i|\) for every real report profile, agent, and alternative report in \(I\).
A limiting realization means one family of anonymous, exactly strategyproof \(M_n\) such that, for every admissible \(\mu\) and every sequence of profiles with \(n\to\infty\) and empirical distributions \(n^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{x_i^{(n)}}\) converging weakly to \(\mu\), the lotteries \(M_n(\mathbf x^{(n)})\) converge weakly to \(F(\mu)\).

What is the exact worst-case miniSOS approximation ratio \(\rho(F)\)? Can \(F(\mu)\) be computed exactly in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\), and does it admit a limiting realization as defined above?

For finite populations on the real line, the half-average, half-uniform-random-dictator mechanism is strategyproof and has exact ratio \(3/2\) whenever optimal cost is positive; no randomized strategyproof mechanism has a smaller worst-case ratio. [Feldman–Wilf](https://econcs.seas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum6346/files/econcs/files/feldman_ec13b.pdf)

Result

The exact ratio is \(\rho(F)=\frac32\). The lottery \(F(\mu)\) can be computed exactly in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\), and it admits a limiting realization by one family of anonymous, exactly strategyproof finite mechanisms.

1. Exact miniSOS ratio

Write \(c=\bar x=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}p_jx_j\) and \(V=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}p_j(x_j-c)^2\). Since \(c\) is a convex combination of points in \(I\), we have \(c\in I\).

For every \(y\in I\), expanding the square gives

\(\displaystyle \int_I(y-x)^2\,d\mu(x)=(y-c)^2+2(y-c)\int_I(c-x)\,d\mu(x)+V=(y-c)^2+V.\)

The cross term vanishes by the definition of \(c\). Consequently, the unique optimal facility is \(c\), and \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)=V\).

More generally, for any lottery \(P\) on \(I\),

\(\displaystyle C(P,\mu)=V+\int_I(y-c)^2\,dP(y).\)

Under \(F(\mu)=\frac12\delta_c+\frac12\mu\), the latter integral equals \(\frac12V\). Thus

\(\displaystyle C(F(\mu),\mu)=\frac32V.\)

Because all masses are positive, \(V=0\) holds exactly when every support point equals \(c\), hence exactly when \(\tau=1\). In that case \(F(\mu)=\mu\), and both costs are zero.

Every society with positive optimal cost therefore has ratio exactly \(\frac32\). Such societies exist: \(\mu=\frac12\delta_a+\frac12\delta_b\) is admissible and has variance \(\frac14(b-a)^2>0\). This proves \(\rho(F)=\frac32\).

2. Exact polynomial-time computation

The output has at most \(\tau+1\) support points. Compute \(c\), assign mass \(\frac12p_j\) to each \(x_j\), and assign mass \(\frac12\) to \(c\). If \(c=x_k\), merge those two masses, giving mass \(\frac12(1+p_k)\) at \(x_k\).

Here is an explicit bit-complexity bound. Express the binary-encoded rational inputs as \(x_j=u_j/v_j\) and \(p_j=r_j/s_j\), with positive denominators. Set

\(\displaystyle q_j=s_jv_j,\qquad D=\prod_{j=1}^{\tau}q_j,\qquad N=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}r_ju_j(D/q_j).\)

Each division \(D/q_j\) is exact, and \(c=N/D\). The bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\), since the sum of the bit lengths of its denominator factors is \(O(L)\). Also,

\(\displaystyle |N|\leq D\sum_jp_j|x_j|\leq D\max_j|x_j|,\)

so \(N\) has \(O(L)\) bits. The same bound applies to the magnitudes of partial sums used to compute \(N\).

Using schoolbook integer multiplication and division, this construction takes \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations. Checking whether \(c=x_j\) uses the exact comparison \(Nv_j=Du_j\), so all \(\tau\) checks fit the same bound. Constructing the output masses also fits this bound. Fractions need not be reduced to represent the lottery exactly.

Thus exact computation is polynomial in \(\tau,L\); no expansion of rational masses into a finite population is required.

3. An anonymous, exactly strategyproof family

For every positive integer \(n\) and every real report profile \(\mathbf x\in I^n\), define

\(\displaystyle c_n(\mathbf x)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i,\qquad M_n(\mathbf x)=\frac12\delta_{c_n(\mathbf x)}+\frac1{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{x_i}.\)

All output locations belong to \(I\), and permuting reports leaves the lottery unchanged. Hence each mechanism is well-defined and anonymous.

This is the finite rule in [Feldman–Wilf, Mechanism 1 and Theorem 4.6](https://econcs.seas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum6346/files/econcs/files/feldman_ec13b.pdf). We verify exact strategyproofness directly.

Fix an agent \(i\), true location \(x_i\), other reports \(\mathbf x_{-i}\), and alternative report \(r\in I\). Let \(c=c_n(\mathbf x)\) and \(c'=c_n(r,\mathbf x_{-i})\). Then \(c'-c=(r-x_i)/n\).

The agent’s expected cost under truthful reporting is

\(\displaystyle U_i(\mathbf x)=\frac12|c-x_i|+\frac1{2n}\sum_{k\ne i}|x_k-x_i|.\)

After reporting \(r\), the cost difference is

\(\displaystyle U_i(r,\mathbf x_{-i})-U_i(\mathbf x)=\frac12\bigl(|c'-x_i|-|c-x_i|\bigr)+\frac1{2n}|r-x_i|.\)

The triangle inequality implies

\(\displaystyle |c'-x_i|-|c-x_i|\geq-|c'-c|=-\frac1n|r-x_i|.\)

Substitution shows that the cost difference is nonnegative. This proves exactly the required strategyproofness inequality for every real profile and every alternative report.

The formula uses \(O(n)\) arithmetic operations and describes a lottery with at most \(n+1\) atoms.

4. Convergence for every permitted profile sequence

Fix an admissible \(\mu\), and let any sequence of profiles have empirical distributions

\(\displaystyle \nu_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{x_i^{(n)}}\)

converging weakly to \(\mu\). Let \(c_n=\int_Ix\,d\nu_n(x)\).

The function \(x\mapsto x\) is bounded and continuous on the compact interval \(I\). Therefore weak convergence gives \(c_n\to\int_Ix\,d\mu(x)=c\).

Now fix any continuous real-valued function \(f\) on \(I\). By the mechanism’s definition,

\(\displaystyle \int_I f\,dM_n(\mathbf x^{(n)})=\frac12f(c_n)+\frac12\int_I f\,d\nu_n.\)

Continuity gives \(f(c_n)\to f(c)\), and weak convergence gives \(\int_I f\,d\nu_n\to\int_I f\,d\mu\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \int_I f\,dM_n(\mathbf x^{(n)})\longrightarrow\frac12f(c)+\frac12\int_I f\,d\mu=\int_I f\,dF(\mu).\)

This is weak convergence of the output lotteries to \(F(\mu)\). The argument applies to every required sequence, including sequences converging to point-mass societies. The single family \((M_n)_{n\geq1}\) therefore supplies the stipulated limiting realization.
