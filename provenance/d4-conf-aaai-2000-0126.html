The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given ordered goods \(g_1,\ldots,g_m\), per-capita capacities \(q_j\), and a distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) bid types, where type \(t\) specifies an interval \(I_t\) and price \(p_t\), choose accepted masses \(x_t\) to maximize \(\sum_t p_t x_t\), subject to \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t:\,g_j\in I_t}x_t\le q_j\) for every good \(g_j\). Each individual receives either the entire interval or nothing; \(x_t\) is the mass of type \(t\) whose bid is accepted.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge1\) ordered goods be \(g_1,\ldots,g_m\), with nonnegative binary-rational per-capita capacities \(q_j\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Type \(t\) submits one bid with a nonempty interval \(I_t=\{g_{a_t},\ldots,g_{b_t}\}\), where \(1\le a_t\le b_t\le m\), and a nonnegative binary-rational price \(p_t\) for the entire interval. Each accepted individual consumes one unit of every good in that interval and pays \(p_t\); rejected individuals consume nothing and pay nothing.
Mass within each type is divisible: any real accepted mass \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) is permitted, while each individual receives the entire interval or nothing. Maximize \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}p_tx_t\), subject to \(\sum_{t:g_j\in I_t}x_t\le q_j\) for every \(j\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is computing an exact revenue-maximizing feasible vector \(x\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many bids, each requesting one unit of every good in a nonempty interval, revenue maximization under nonnegative integer good capacities is polynomial-time solvable.

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass 1, so capacities are measured per capita.

  • Made intervals nonempty and specified their inclusive endpoints, following the discrete interval-bid definition.
  • Interpreted divisible accepted masses as allowing arbitrary selection within each type, with one unit of each requested good consumed per accepted individual.
  • Defined L as the total binary input length.

Result

Computing an exact revenue-maximizing feasible vector is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\).

Divisibility makes the problem a rational linear program, which already establishes polynomial-time solvability. The interval structure gives a more explicit reduction to minimum-cost circulation. This reduction also explains why large rational denominators do not require expanding the population into individual bids.

1. Scale the rational data

Write every input rational with a positive integer denominator. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all capacities \(q_j\) and masses \(\mu_t\), and let \(E\) be the product of the denominators of all prices \(p_t\). Define

\(Q_j=Dq_j,\qquad U_t=D\mu_t,\qquad P_t=Ep_t.\)

These are nonnegative integers. The binary lengths of \(D\) and \(E\) are at most the sums of the binary lengths of their respective factors, hence are \(O(L)\). Consequently, every \(Q_j,U_t,P_t\) has binary length \(O(L)\), and all these numbers can be computed in polynomial time.

Writing \(X_t=Dx_t\), the original optimization problem is equivalent to maximizing

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{DE}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}P_tX_t\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle 0\le X_t\le U_t,\qquad \sum_{t:a_t\le j\le b_t}X_t\le Q_j\quad(1\le j\le m).\)

At this stage, the variables \(X_t\) remain real.

2. Construct a circulation network

Use vertices \(0,1,\ldots,m\), with two kinds of directed arcs:

  • For each good \(g_j\), add an arc \(j-1\to j\) with capacity \(Q_j\) and cost \(0\).
  • For each type \(t\), add an arc \(b_t\to a_t-1\) with capacity \(U_t\) and cost \(-P_t\).

Parallel arcs are allowed. A circulation is a nonnegative flow respecting arc capacities and satisfying flow conservation at every vertex.

Denote the flow on type \(t\)'s arc by \(X_t\), and the flow on good \(j\)'s arc by \(F_j\).

Every circulation gives a feasible allocation

Fix \(j\), and sum flow conservation over the vertices \(S_j=\{0,\ldots,j-1\}\). Internal arcs cancel.

The only arc leaving \(S_j\) is the good arc \(j-1\to j\). A type arc enters \(S_j\) precisely when

\(a_t-1\le j-1<b_t,\)

equivalently, \(a_t\le j\le b_t\). No type arc leaves \(S_j\), because every type arc points from a larger vertex to a smaller vertex. Therefore,

\(\displaystyle F_j=\sum_{t:a_t\le j\le b_t}X_t.\)

The arc capacities now imply all the scaled allocation constraints.

Every feasible allocation gives a circulation

Conversely, take any feasible real vector \(X\). Put flow \(X_t\) on each type arc and define

\(\displaystyle F_j=\sum_{t:a_t\le j\le b_t}X_t\)

on each good arc. All capacity constraints hold.

To check conservation, put \(F_0=F_{m+1}=0\). For each vertex \(k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\), the definition of the \(F_j\) gives

\(\displaystyle F_{k+1}-F_k=\sum_{t:a_t=k+1}X_t-\sum_{t:b_t=k}X_t.\)

Rearranging yields

\(\displaystyle F_k+\sum_{t:a_t=k+1}X_t=F_{k+1}+\sum_{t:b_t=k}X_t.\)

The left side is total incoming flow at vertex \(k\), and the right side is total outgoing flow. Thus the constructed flow is a circulation.

The feasible allocations and feasible circulations consequently correspond exactly. The circulation cost is

\(\displaystyle -\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}P_tX_t=-DE\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}p_tx_t.\)

Since \(DE>0\), minimizing cost is equivalent to maximizing revenue.

3. Exact solution and polynomial complexity

We use the standard minimum-cost circulation theorem:

For a finite directed graph with integer arc costs and finite nonnegative integer capacities, a minimum-cost circulation can be found in time polynomial in the graph size and the binary encoding length of the numerical data. Moreover, an optimal circulation can be chosen integral, and an integral optimum can be returned.

All hypotheses hold here:

  • The graph has \(m+1\) vertices and \(m+\tau\) arcs.
  • All capacities are finite nonnegative integers.
  • All costs are integers; negative costs are permitted.
  • The zero circulation is feasible.
  • The network’s total encoding length is \(O((m+\tau)L)\).

Apply this theorem and let \(X_t^*\) be the resulting integral flow on type \(t\)'s arc. Return

\(\displaystyle x_t^*=\frac{X_t^*}{D}\qquad(1\le t\le\tau).\)

The correspondence proved above shows that this vector is feasible and maximizes revenue over all real feasible allocations. Integrality of the returned flow does not restrict the optimum: the theorem guarantees an integral optimum for the circulation problem itself.

The output also has polynomial length. Since the masses are positive and sum to \(1\), each \(\mu_t\le1\), so

\(0\le X_t^*\le U_t=D\mu_t\le D.\)

Thus each output numerator and denominator has \(O(L)\) bits.

4. Why population expansion is unnecessary

Although \(D\) has polynomial binary length, its numerical value can be exponentially large. Replacing type \(t\) by \(D\mu_t\) separate bids would therefore not establish a polynomial-time algorithm.

The circulation construction uses exactly one arc per type, with its mass encoded as a binary integer capacity. It avoids this expansion and proves the required polynomial-time bound.
