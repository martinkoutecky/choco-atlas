The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support of complete types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and rational values \(v_t(s)\), realize each type as an atomless population \(\Omega_t\) of mass \(\mu_t\). For a measurable coalition \(B\), let a type-\(t\) member's utility be \(U_t(B)=\sum_{s\in T}v_t(s)\lambda(B\cap\Omega_s)\). Decide whether there is a measurable partition \(\Pi^*\) into positive-mass coalitions such that, against every partition \(\Pi'\) inequivalent to it modulo null sets, \(\Phi(\Pi^*,\Pi')=\int_\Omega \operatorname{sgn}(U_{t(x)}(\Pi^*(x))-U_{t(x)}(\Pi'(x)))\,d\lambda(x)>0\).

Valuations may have either sign, need not be symmetric, and include within-type valuations \(v_t(t)\). The support size \(\tau\) counts listed types, not all possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length. There is no separate parameter \(m\) in this model; state running times in \(\tau\) and \(L\).
Realize each type as a disjoint interval \(\Omega_t\) of Lebesgue mass \(\mu_t\), and let \(\Omega=\bigsqcup_{t\in T}\Omega_t\). Partial resolution: hardness and an exact parameterized algorithm

The requested classification remains unresolved under polynomial-time many-one reductions. I prove two results:

  • Strongly popular partition existence is $\mathrm{coNP}$-hard, even with equal masses and symmetric valuations. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.
  • For arbitrary inputs, existence is decidable in time

$$ f(\tau)L^{O(1)} $$
for a computable function $f$. Thus the problem is fixed-parameter tractable in the listed support size.

The second result is additional progress beyond the notes. Its main ingredient is an exact compression of countably infinite rivals, preserving their voting margin, including a zero margin.

1. Every strongly popular partition leaves types unsplit

Call a partition unsplit if, for each $t\in T$, one coalition contains $\Omega_t$ modulo a null set.

Lemma 1. Every strongly popular partition is unsplit.

Proof. Suppose distinct coalitions $B_1,B_2$ both contain positive masses of type $t$. By atomlessness, choose measurable sets
$$ A_i\subseteq B_i\cap\Omega_t,\qquad \lambda(A_i)=\delta>0, $$
where
$$ \delta<\frac12\min\{\lambda(B_1\cap\Omega_t),\lambda(B_2\cap\Omega_t)\}. $$
Exchange these sets:
$$ B'_1=(B_1\setminus A_1)\cup A_2,\qquad B'_2=(B_2\setminus A_2)\cup A_1. $$
Leave every other coalition unchanged.

Every coalition retains its type-mass vector. Thus unmoved members are indifferent. Members of $A_1$ compare the original type-$t$ utilities of $B_1$ and $B_2$, while members of $A_2$ make the reverse comparison. Their contributions cancel because their masses agree. Hence
$$ \Phi(\Pi,\Pi')=0. $$

The partitions are inequivalent. Indeed, $B'_1$ differs from $B_1$ by positive mass and retains positive overlap with $B_1$. It therefore cannot agree modulo null sets with $B_1$ or with any other original coalition.

This contradicts strong popularity. Finally, in a countable partition, a type not wholly contained in one coalition must have positive mass in at least two coalitions. Thus the argument covers every admissible partition. $\square$

Consequently, every candidate strongly popular partition corresponds to a set partition of $T$. There are at most $\tau^\tau$ candidates, each with at most $\tau$ coalitions.

2. Voting against an unsplit candidate depends only on coalition masses

Fix an unsplit candidate $\Pi$. Let $C(t)\subseteq T$ denote its block containing type $t$, and define its baseline utility by
$$ u_t=\sum_{s\in C(t)}v_t(s)\mu_s. $$

For a rival coalition, write its type-mass vector as
$$ b=(b_t)_{t\in T},\qquad b_t\geq0. $$
Its comparison pattern is
$$ \sigma(b)_t = \operatorname{sgn}\!\left(u_t-\sum_{s\in T}v_t(s)b_s\right) \in\{-1,0,1\}. $$
Define
$$ F_\sigma(b)=\sum_{t\in T}\sigma_t b_t. $$

If a rival has coalition vectors $b^j$, then
$$ \Phi(\Pi,\Pi')=\sum_j F_{\sigma(b^j)}(b^j). $$
This series converges absolutely, since
$$ \left|F_{\sigma(b^j)}(b^j)\right| \leq\sum_t b^j_t, \qquad \sum_j\sum_t b^j_t=1. $$

Because the candidate is unsplit, no further information about the locations of members is needed.

3. Exact compression of countably infinite rivals

Define
$$ V_t=\sum_{s\in T}|v_t(s)|\mu_s, $$
and
$$ R=\max\left(\{0\}\cup \left\{\frac{V_t}{|u_t|}:u_t\neq0\right\}\right), \qquad K=\lfloor R\rfloor+1. $$
Thus $K$ is a positive integer and, whenever $u_t\neq0$,
$$ K|u_t|>V_t. $$

Lemma 2. Fix any finite collection of distinguished coalitions of a rival. The remaining coalitions can be replaced, without changing their aggregate type masses or their total voting contribution, by at most $3^\tau$ families. Each family consists of at most $K$ coalitions with identical type-mass vectors.

Proof. Group the nondistinguished coalitions by comparison pattern. There are at most $3^\tau$ patterns. Consider one nonempty group with pattern $\sigma$, and let
$$ B=\sum_{j\text{ in the group}}b^j. $$
Coordinatewise,
$$ 0\leq B_s\leq\mu_s. $$
In particular,
$$ \left|\sum_s v_t(s)B_s\right|\leq V_t. $$

A finite group

Suppose the group has $N$ coalitions. Its average vector $B/N$ has pattern $\sigma$, because
$$ u_t-\sum_s v_t(s)\frac{B_s}{N} = \frac1N\sum_j \left(u_t-\sum_s v_t(s)b^j_s\right). $$
A finite average of quantities having the same strictly positive sign, strictly negative sign, or zero value retains that sign or value.

If $N\leq K$, replace the group by $N$ coalitions of vector $B/N$.

Suppose instead that $N>K$. For every $t$ with $u_t\neq0$,
$$ N|u_t|>V_t \geq\left|\sum_s v_t(s)B_s\right|. $$
Therefore
$$ \operatorname{sgn}\!\left(Nu_t-\sum_s v_t(s)B_s\right) =\operatorname{sgn}(u_t). $$
Since the average has pattern $\sigma$, this proves
$$ \sigma_t=\operatorname{sgn}(u_t) \quad\text{whenever }u_t\neq0. $$

The same conclusion holds with $N$ replaced by $K$, since $K|u_t|>V_t$. For $u_t=0$, the comparison sign is unchanged by positive scaling of $B$. Hence $B/K$ also has pattern $\sigma$. Replace the group by $K$ coalitions of vector $B/K$.

An infinite group

Enumerate its coalition vectors as $b^1,b^2,\ldots$. Their total masses tend to zero, because their positive masses have finite sum. Therefore, for each $t$,
$$ \sum_s v_t(s)b^j_s\longrightarrow0. $$
When $u_t\neq0$, the comparison sign is eventually $\operatorname{sgn}(u_t)$. All coalitions in the group have the same pattern, so
$$ \sigma_t=\operatorname{sgn}(u_t). $$

When $u_t=0$, all quantities
$$ -\sum_s v_t(s)b^j_s $$
have the same sign $\sigma_t$. Their series is absolutely convergent: the sum of their absolute values is at most $V_t$. Its sum consequently has that same sign, with strictness preserved whenever $\sigma_t\neq0$. Thus
$$ \operatorname{sgn}\!\left(-\sum_s v_t(s)B_s\right)=\sigma_t. $$

It follows that $B/K$ has pattern $\sigma$: for nonzero $u_t$, use $K|u_t|>V_t$; for zero $u_t$, use positive scaling. Replace the group by $K$ coalitions of vector $B/K$.

Preservation of votes and admissibility

In every case, the replacement retains aggregate vector $B$. Its voting contribution is
$$ N'F_\sigma(B/N')=F_\sigma(B) =\sum_jF_\sigma(b^j), $$
where $1\leq N'\leq K$. Each replacement coalition has positive total mass because the original group was nonempty.

These vectors can be realized as disjoint measurable coalitions: subdivide each type interval into pieces of the prescribed masses and join the corresponding pieces across types. The resulting partition is finite and admissible. $\square$

Corollary 3. If an unsplit candidate has an inequivalent rival with
$$ \Phi(\Pi,\Pi')\leq0, $$
then it has such a finite rival with at most
$$ 2+K3^\tau $$
coalitions.

Proof. If the rival is unsplit, it already has at most $\tau$ coalitions.

Otherwise, choose a type occurring with positive mass in two distinct rival coalitions. Retain those two coalitions as distinguished and apply Lemma 2 to the remainder. The resulting rival still splits that type, so it remains inequivalent to the unsplit candidate. Its voting margin is unchanged. $\square$

The numbers $u_t,V_t,R$, and $K$ can be computed by exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time in $L$. Their binary encoding lengths are $O(L)$: a common denominator for each row sum can be obtained by multiplying the input denominators appearing in that sum, whose combined encoding length is $O(L)$.

4. An exact algorithm with running time $f(\tau)L^{O(1)}$

The compression lemma gives a mixed integer linear formulation whose dimension depends only on $\tau$.

4.1. Unsplit rivals

For each candidate $\Pi$, enumerate all different set partitions of $T$. These are exactly its inequivalent unsplit rivals. Their utilities and voting margins are rational and can be calculated exactly.

It remains to detect split rivals with nonpositive margin.

4.2. Split rivals

Enumerate the following choices:

  • a type $q$ that will be split;
  • comparison patterns $\sigma^1,\sigma^2\in\{-1,0,1\}^\tau$ for two distinguished coalitions;
  • a subset $\mathcal A\subseteq\{-1,0,1\}^\tau$ of patterns used by the remaining families.

For these choices, introduce real vectors $b^1,b^2$ and $B^\sigma$ for $\sigma\in\mathcal A$, and integers $N_\sigma$.

Impose nonnegativity:
$$ b^1,b^2,B^\sigma\geq0. $$
Impose a genuine split:
$$ b^1_q>0,\qquad b^2_q>0. $$
For every $\sigma\in\mathcal A$, impose
$$ 1\leq N_\sigma\leq K, \qquad \sum_t B^\sigma_t>0. $$
Require all type masses to be allocated:
$$ b^1_t+b^2_t+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal A}B^\sigma_t=\mu_t \qquad(t\in T). $$

For the distinguished coalitions, require
$$ \operatorname{sgn}\!\left(u_t-\sum_s v_t(s)b^a_s\right) =\sigma^a_t \qquad(a\in\{1,2\},\ t\in T). $$
For each remaining family, require
$$ \operatorname{sgn}\!\left(N_\sigma u_t-\sum_s v_t(s)B^\sigma_s\right) =\sigma_t \qquad(\sigma\in\mathcal A,\ t\in T). $$
Each sign requirement means a strict linear inequality or a linear equality according as its prescribed sign is positive, negative, or zero.

Finally, require a nonpositive voting margin:
$$ \sum_t\sigma^1_t b^1_t +\sum_t\sigma^2_t b^2_t +\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal A}\sum_t\sigma_tB^\sigma_t \leq0. $$

All constraints are linear; in particular, $u_t$ is a known rational coefficient multiplying $N_\sigma$.

Lemma 4. An unsplit candidate has an inequivalent split rival with nonpositive margin if and only if one of these systems is feasible.

Proof. Given such a rival, retain two coalitions containing positive masses of one split type $q$. Lemma 2 compresses its remaining coalitions into families with multiplicities at most $K$. Their vectors, multiplicities, and patterns satisfy the system.

Conversely, a feasible solution specifies two distinguished coalitions and, for every $\sigma\in\mathcal A$, exactly $N_\sigma$ coalitions of vector
$$ B^\sigma/N_\sigma. $$
The sign constraints give the prescribed comparison patterns, and the voting inequality gives nonpositive margin. All coalitions have positive mass. The mass equations permit their realization by subdividing the type intervals.

The positive masses $b^1_q,b^2_q$ ensure that the rival splits type $q$, so it is inequivalent to the candidate. $\square$

4.3. Solving systems with strict inequalities

For completeness, strict inequalities can be handled exactly, without replacing them by a guessed numerical tolerance.

First replace equalities by two weak inequalities. Eliminate the real variables by Fourier–Motzkin elimination, retaining whether each bound is strict or weak. Combining a lower and an upper bound produces a strict comparison if either original bound was strict, and a weak comparison otherwise.

This rule is exact. For finitely many lower and upper bounds on one real variable, all pairwise comparisons are sufficient for existence of a value satisfying those bounds; equality of extremal bounds is permitted precisely when both relevant bounds are weak. If bounds occur on only one side, a sufficiently large or sufficiently small value exists.

After eliminating every real variable, the result is a finite conjunction of rational weak or strict inequalities in the integers $N_\sigma$. Clear denominators separately in each inequality. A strict inequality with integer coefficients becomes a weak one by shifting the integral right-hand side by one. We therefore obtain an ordinary integer linear feasibility problem.

Use the standard fixed-dimension integer-programming theorem: feasibility in $p$ integer variables and binary encoding length $S$ is decidable in time
$$ h(p)S^c, $$
where $h$ is computable and $c$ is an absolute constant. The requisite parameterized bound is a standard consequence of integer-programming algorithms; it is stated in the introduction of [Dadush, Eisenbrand, and Rothvoss, *From approximate to exact integer programming*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11870894/).

Its hypotheses hold here: after elimination and denominator clearing, the coefficients are integers, all remaining unknowns are integers, and only linear weak inequalities remain.

4.4. Running-time bound

Each system has at most
$$ 3^\tau $$
integer variables and at most
$$ \tau(3^\tau+2) $$
real variables. Its number of constraints depends only on $\tau$, and its coefficient lengths are $O(L)$.

During Fourier–Motzkin elimination, each step creates at most quadratically many constraints. Since the number of eliminated variables depends only on $\tau$, the final number of constraints also depends only on $\tau$. Each elimination step performs a bounded number of rational arithmetic operations per new coefficient, so final coefficient lengths are bounded by
$$ g(\tau)(L+1) $$
for a computable function $g$. Construction and integer feasibility consequently take time
$$ f_1(\tau)L^{O(1)}. $$

The numbers of candidates, unsplit rivals, distinguished patterns, split-type choices, and subsets $\mathcal A$ all depend only on $\tau$. Enumerating them changes only the parameter-dependent factor.

Theorem 5. Strongly popular partition existence for the stated atomless model is decidable in time
$$ f(\tau)L^{O(1)} $$
for a computable function $f$.

Proof. Enumerate the unsplit candidates justified by Lemma 1. Reject a candidate whenever an inequivalent unsplit rival has nonpositive margin or one of the split-rival systems is feasible.

By Lemma 4, an unrejected candidate has strictly positive margin against every inequivalent admissible rival, including countably infinite rivals. Thus it is strongly popular. Conversely, a strongly popular candidate is never rejected. The preceding bounds establish the running time. $\square$

This is a parameterized algorithm, not a polynomial-time algorithm when $\tau$ varies with the input.

5. A self-contained $\mathrm{coNP}$-hardness proof

I now reprove the lower bound from the notes, including the source-problem normalization.

5.1. The graph family

Let $G=(T,E)$ be a simple undirected graph with odd order $\tau\geq3$ and no isolated vertices. Give every type mass
$$ \mu_i=\frac1\tau. $$
Set
$$ M=4\tau,\qquad \eta=\frac1{2\tau}, $$
and define
$$ v_i(i)=-1, $$
$$ v_i(s)=M\quad\text{if }\{i,s\}\in E, $$
$$ v_i(s)=\eta\quad\text{if }i\neq s\text{ and }\{i,s\}\notin E. $$
These valuations are symmetric, and all off-diagonal valuations are strictly positive. The input length is
$$ L=O(\tau^2\log\tau). $$

Let $\alpha(G)$ be the maximum cardinality of an independent set, and let $\Gamma=\{\Omega\}$.

Theorem 6.
$$ \text{A strongly popular partition exists} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Gamma\text{ is strongly popular} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha(G)\leq\frac{\tau-1}{2}. $$

5.2. Only the grand coalition can be strongly popular

By Lemma 1, a strongly popular candidate must be unsplit.

If an unsplit candidate differs from $\Gamma$, every type belongs to a proper block $C\subsetneq T$. For $i\in C$,
$$ U_i(\Omega)-U_i(C) = \sum_{s\notin C}v_i(s)\mu_s>0, $$
because the sum is nonempty and consists entirely of strictly positive off-diagonal terms.

Every member prefers $\Gamma$, so
$$ \Phi(\Pi,\Gamma)=-1. $$
Thus only $\Gamma$ remains a possible strongly popular partition.

5.3. Small independence number guarantees strong popularity

Assume
$$ \alpha(G)\leq\frac{\tau-1}{2}. $$
Fix any rival $\Pi'$ inequivalent to $\Gamma$, with coalitions indexed by $j$. Define
$$ b_{j,i}=\lambda(B_j\cap\Omega_i), \qquad d_{j,i}=\frac1\tau-b_{j,i}. $$
Every deficit is nonnegative, and every vector $d_j$ is nonzero: a coalition with zero deficits would contain the entire society modulo null sets.

The utility difference is
$$ w_{j,i} = -d_{j,i} +M\sum_{s\in N(i)}d_{j,s} +\eta\sum_{s\notin N(i)\cup\{i\}}d_{j,s}. $$
Let
$$ S_j=\{i:w_{j,i}\leq0\}. $$

First, $S_j$ is independent. If adjacent $i,s$ both belonged to $S_j$, nonnegative deficits would imply
$$ d_{j,i}\geq Md_{j,s}, \qquad d_{j,s}\geq Md_{j,i}. $$
Since $M>1$, both deficits would vanish. Then $w_{j,i}\leq0$, together with strictly positive off-diagonal coefficients in row $i$, would force every other deficit to vanish. This contradicts $d_j\neq0$.

Second, a type belongs to at most one $S_j$. If $i\in S_j$, then every neighbor $s$ satisfies
$$ d_{j,s}\leq\frac{d_{j,i}}M\leq\frac1{\tau M}, $$
and therefore
$$ b_{j,s}\geq\frac{1-1/M}{\tau}>\frac1{2\tau}. $$
Since $i$ has a neighbor, membership in two different sets $S_j$ would place more than that neighbor’s available mass in two disjoint coalitions.

For each $i$ belonging to some $S_j$, denote that unique index by $j(i)$ and set
$$ a_i=\tau b_{j(i),i}. $$
Set $a_i=0$ when no such index exists. Then $0\leq a_i\leq1$.

Third, $\{i:a_i>1/M\}$ is independent. Suppose adjacent $i,s$ both have positive $a_i,a_s$. Their associated indices differ, by the first observation. Membership $s\in S_{j(s)}$ gives
$$ b_{j(s),i}\geq\frac{1-1/M}{\tau}. $$
Disjointness then gives
$$ b_{j(i),i}\leq\frac1{\tau M}, $$
so $a_i\leq1/M$. The same argument gives $a_s\leq1/M$.

Let $Q$ be the total mass of members who do not strictly prefer $\Gamma$. The uniqueness observation yields
$$ Q=\frac1\tau\sum_i a_i \leq\frac{\alpha(G)}{\tau}+\frac1M. $$
Hence
$$ Q\leq \frac{\tau-1}{2\tau}+\frac1{4\tau} = \frac12-\frac1{4\tau}. $$
The mass favoring $\Gamma$ is $1-Q$; the mass opposing it is at most $Q$, because $Q$ also includes indifferent members. Thus
$$ \Phi(\Gamma,\Pi') \geq1-2Q \geq\frac1{2\tau}>0. $$

All sums remain valid for countably many coalitions: they sum disjoint measurable masses, and each type contributes nonpositive votes in at most one coalition.

5.4. A large independent set defeats the grand coalition

Suppose an independent set $S$ has size
$$ k\geq\frac{\tau+1}{2}. $$
Every type has positive grand-coalition utility:
$$ U_i(\Omega) = \frac{-1+M\deg(i)+\eta(\tau-1-\deg(i))}{\tau}>0, $$
because $\deg(i)\geq1$.

Set
$$ \varepsilon=\frac1{4\tau k}. $$
Move mass $\varepsilon$ of each type in $S$ into a new coalition $B$, and let $A$ contain the remaining society. Both coalitions have positive mass.

For $i\in S$, independence gives
$$ U_i(\Omega)-U_i(A) = \varepsilon\bigl(-1+\eta(k-1)\bigr)<0, $$
since
$$ \eta(k-1)\leq\frac{\tau-1}{2\tau}<1. $$
Its members remaining in $A$ therefore prefer the rival.

For $i\notin S$, every valuation toward a removed type is strictly positive, so its members prefer $\Gamma$.

The moved members of type $i\in S$ have
$$ U_i(B)=\varepsilon\bigl(-1+\eta(k-1)\bigr)<0<U_i(\Omega), $$
so they prefer $\Gamma$. Consequently,
$$ \Phi(\Gamma,\{A,B\}) = \frac{\tau-2k}{\tau}+2k\varepsilon \leq-\frac1{2\tau}<0. $$
This defeats $\Gamma$. Together with Section 5.2, it completes the proof of Theorem 6.

5.5. Reduction from unsatisfiability

Use satisfiability of CNF formulas with at most three literals per clause, an $\mathrm{NP}$-complete problem from [Karp’s completeness results](https://www2.seas.gwu.edu/~simhaweb/champalg/tsp/papers/KarpOriginal.pdf).

We normalize to nonempty formulas having exactly three literal occurrences per clause, permitting repetitions:

  • Pad a nonempty clause with fewer than three occurrences by repeating a literal.
  • Replace a formula containing an empty clause by the fixed unsatisfiable formula

$$ (z\vee z\vee z)\wedge(\neg z\vee\neg z\vee\neg z). $$

  • Replace an empty conjunction by the fixed satisfiable clause

$$ z\vee\neg z\vee z. $$

This is polynomial time and preserves satisfiability. Thus the normalized satisfiability problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete, and its unsatisfiability problem is $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete.

Let a normalized formula $F$ have $c\geq1$ clauses. Form its clause graph $G_F$:

  • Create one vertex per literal occurrence.
  • Make the three vertices of each clause a triangle.
  • Join occurrences from different clauses when their literals are complementary.

An independent set contains at most one vertex per clause, so
$$ \alpha(G_F)\leq c. $$
Moreover,
$$ F\text{ is satisfiable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\alpha(G_F)=c. $$
A satisfying assignment permits selection of one true occurrence per clause, with no complementary selections. Conversely, an independent set of size $c$ selects one occurrence from every clause without selecting complementary literals. Making those selected literals true is consistent and satisfies every clause.

Let $H_F$ be the disjoint union of $G_F$ and $c+1$ copies of the three-vertex path. Each path has independence number two, so
$$ \tau=|V(H_F)|=6c+3, $$
$$ \alpha(H_F)=\alpha(G_F)+2(c+1). $$
The graph has odd order and no isolated vertices.

If $F$ is satisfiable,
$$ \alpha(H_F)=3c+2=\frac{\tau+1}{2}. $$
If $F$ is unsatisfiable,
$$ \alpha(H_F)\leq3c+1=\frac{\tau-1}{2}. $$
Applying Theorem 6 gives
$$ F\text{ is unsatisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed society admits a strongly popular partition}. $$
The construction is polynomial time and has
$$ L=O(\tau^2\log\tau). $$

This proves $\mathrm{coNP}$-hardness under polynomial-time many-one reductions. The graph-family restriction is also in $\mathrm{coNP}$: an independent set of size at least $(\tau+1)/2$ certifies nonexistence. Hence that restriction is $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete.

6. What these results settle, and what remains open

A polynomial-time existence algorithm would decide normalized unsatisfiability in polynomial time. By complementing its answer, it would also decide an $\mathrm{NP}$-complete satisfiability problem in polynomial time. Therefore polynomial-time solvability would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.

The same reduction establishes $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions: make one existence query and negate the answer. It does not establish many-one $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness.

The general algorithm proves exact decidability and fixed-parameter tractability in $\tau$, while accounting for arbitrary splitting, countably infinite rivals, and indifference. The requested many-one hardness classification remains open.
