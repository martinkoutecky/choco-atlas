Theorem 1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a society with type distribution \(\mu\), where each type records its signal laws, finite calibration counts, and communication role, and given time-varying continuum interaction kernels, does the paper’s uncertain-likelihood belief update converge almost surely for each hypothesis to the type-mass average of the corresponding log Dirichlet likelihood ratios?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by its support \(T\): the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu(a)\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let the signal alphabet have size \(m\geq2\) and the hypothesis set \(\Theta\) be finite and nonempty. Any running times are measured in \(m,\tau,L,|\Theta|\) and the other stated parameters, where \(L\) is the binary input length.
Each type \(a\) has a strictly positive signal probability vector \(p_a\) and, for each \(\theta\), fixed calibration counts \(z_{a\theta}\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\), with \(R_{a\theta}=\sum_{s=1}^{m}z_{a\theta,s}<\infty\). Its mass shares one signal stream \(S_{a,k}\), i.i.d. with law \(p_a\); streams are independent across types. Write \(n_{a,k,s}=\sum_{t=1}^{k}\mathbf1\{S_{a,t}=s\}\).
Communication roles are encoded by deterministic nonnegative kernels \(K_k(a,b)\), with receiver \(a\) and sender \(b\), satisfying \(\sum_a\mu(a)K_k(a,b)=1\). For fixed \(\eta>0\), every positive transfer \(\mu(a)K_k(a,b)\) is at least \(\eta\), including every diagonal transfer. For some integer \(B\geq1\), the union of the directed support graphs in each block of \(B\) consecutive times is strongly connected.
Initialize \(y_{a,0}=1\), \(b_{a,0}(\theta)=1\), and \(n_{a,0}=0\). For \(k\geq1\), set \(\ell_{a\theta,k}=\frac{(z_{a\theta,S_{a,k}}+n_{a,k,S_{a,k}})(m+k-1)}{(R_{a\theta}+m+k-1)n_{a,k,S_{a,k}}}\).
The mass update is \(y_{a,k+1}=\sum_b\mu(b)K_k(a,b)y_{b,k}\) and \(\log b_{a,k+1}(\theta)=\frac{\sum_b\mu(b)K_k(a,b)y_{b,k}\log b_{b,k}(\theta)+\log\ell_{a\theta,k+1}}{y_{a,k+1}}\); beliefs are positive and are not normalized across hypotheses.

Does every admissible instance satisfy, simultaneously for every \(a\in T\) and \(\theta\in\Theta\), \(\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\log b_{a,k}(\theta)=\sum_{c\in T}\mu(c)\log\frac{\operatorname{Dirichlet}(p_c;z_{c\theta}+\mathbf1)}{\operatorname{Dirichlet}(p_c;\mathbf1)}\) almost surely, or is there an admissible counterexample?

For finite agents, convergence to the equally weighted average of these log Dirichlet density ratios is established for the directed, time-varying push-sum update under uniform strong connectivity ([Uribe et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.04255)).
A later treatment establishes the corresponding geometric-mean limit for fixed connected undirected networks with aperiodic doubly stochastic weights ([Hare et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.09228)); no exact result for the mass update specified here was located.

  • Interpreted dynamics as type-indexed: each type's entire mass shares one i.i.d. signal stream; chose independent streams across types.
  • Conditioned on the realized finite calibration histograms, allowing zero calibration counts and zero total prior evidence.
  • Required strictly positive true signal probabilities to make the log Dirichlet density ratios finite and avoid undefined boundary evaluations.
  • Used the signal alphabet size in the likelihood update, resolving the supplied text's inconsistent use of alphabet and hypothesis cardinalities.
  • Specified kernel orientation and normalization relative to society mass, and replaced neighbor sums by mass-weighted sums in both push-sum recursions.
  • Carried uniform strong connectivity to the kernel support graphs and made positive diagonal transfers and a uniform lower bound explicit, preserving the stochastic mixing conditions intended by the discrete update.
  • Fixed initial beliefs and push-sum variables at one and initial signal counts at zero; the later treatment explicitly requires unit initial beliefs.
  • Used a convergence-versus-counterexample target because the anchor is an asymptotic convergence theorem, not a computational complexity result.

1. Exact convergence of each local likelihood product

Fix a type \(a\) and hypothesis \(\theta\). Temporarily suppress these indices, writing \(z_s=z_{a\theta,s}\), \(R=\sum_s z_s\), and \(n_{k,s}=n_{a,k,s}\). Define

\(P_k=\prod_{t=1}^{k}\ell_{a\theta,t},\qquad P_0=1.\)

Whenever signal \(s\) occurs, its count advances by one. Therefore, multiplying the likelihood factors gives the exact identity

\(P_k=\left(\prod_{t=1}^{k}\frac{m+t-1}{R+m+t-1}\right)\prod_{s=1}^{m}\prod_{r=1}^{n_{k,s}}\frac{z_s+r}{r}.\)

Using factorial identities, this becomes

\(P_k=C_z\, \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{z_s}(n_{k,s}+j)} {\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{R-1}(k+m+j)}, \qquad C_z=\frac{(R+m-1)!}{(m-1)!\prod_{s=1}^{m}z_s!}.\)

Empty products are \(1\), so this formula also covers \(R=0\).

By the strong law of large numbers, there is an event \(E\) of probability one on which

\(\displaystyle \frac{n_{a,k,s}}{k}\longrightarrow p_{a,s}\)

for every supported type \(a\) and every signal \(s\). Such a common event exists because there are finitely many pairs \((a,s)\).

On \(E\), divide the numerator and denominator in the product formula by \(k^R\). Since \(R\) and every \(z_s\) are fixed,

\(\displaystyle P_k\longrightarrow C_z\prod_{s=1}^{m}p_{a,s}^{z_s}>0.\)

For a positive parameter vector \(\alpha\), the Dirichlet density is

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Dirichlet}(p;\alpha) =\frac{\Gamma(\sum_s\alpha_s)}{\prod_s\Gamma(\alpha_s)} \prod_s p_s^{\alpha_s-1}. \)

Thus the limiting constant is exactly

\(\displaystyle C_z\prod_s p_{a,s}^{z_s} = \frac{\operatorname{Dirichlet}(p_a;z_{a\theta}+\mathbf1)} {\operatorname{Dirichlet}(p_a;\mathbf1)}. \)

Define

\(\displaystyle u_{a\theta,k}=\log\ell_{a\theta,k}, \qquad F_{a\theta,k}=\sum_{t=1}^{k}u_{a\theta,t}. \)

Taking logarithms yields, simultaneously for every \(a,\theta\) on \(E\),

\(\displaystyle F_{a\theta,k}\longrightarrow d_{a\theta}:= \log\frac{\operatorname{Dirichlet}(p_a;z_{a\theta}+\mathbf1)} {\operatorname{Dirichlet}(p_a;\mathbf1)}. \)

In particular,

\(\displaystyle u_{a\theta,k}=F_{a\theta,k}-F_{a\theta,k-1}\longrightarrow0.\)

This argument does not require absolute summability of the innovations.

2. The mass update is column-stochastic push-sum

Let \(D\) be the diagonal matrix with entries \(\mu(a)\), and define

\(\displaystyle A_k(a,b)=\mu(a)K_k(a,b).\)

The kernel normalization gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_a A_k(a,b)=1.\)

Hence each \(A_k\) is nonnegative and column-stochastic. Its support graph is the prescribed communication graph; every positive entry is at least \(\eta\), and every diagonal entry is at least \(\eta\).

Fix \(\theta\), and introduce the vectors

\(\displaystyle w_{a,k}=\mu(a)y_{a,k}, \qquad x_{a,k}=\mu(a)y_{a,k}\log b_{a,k}(\theta), \qquad v_{a,k}=\mu(a)u_{a\theta,k}. \)

Multiplying the two state updates by \(\mu(a)\) gives exactly

\(\displaystyle w_{k+1}=A_kw_k,\qquad x_{k+1}=A_kx_k+v_{k+1}.\)

The initialization is

\(\displaystyle w_0=(\mu(a))_{a\in T},\qquad x_0=0.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}w_k=1\)

at every time. Moreover, positive diagonal entries imply \(w_{a,k}>0\), so

\(\displaystyle \log b_{a,k}(\theta)=\frac{x_{a,k}}{w_{a,k}}\)

is well defined.

Writing \(s_k=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x_k\), column-stochasticity gives

\(\displaystyle s_k=\sum_{t=1}^{k}\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}v_t =\sum_{a\in T}\mu(a)F_{a\theta,k}. \)

Therefore, on \(E\),

\(\displaystyle s_k\longrightarrow s_\infty :=\sum_{a\in T}\mu(a)d_{a\theta}. \)

It remains to prove that each ratio \(x_{a,k}/w_{a,k}\) approaches \(s_k\).

3. Uniform mixing and a lower bound on the denominators

Suppose first that \(\tau\geq2\), and set

\(\displaystyle H=(\tau-1)B,\qquad \beta=\eta^H.\)

Claim. Every entry of every product of \(H\) consecutive matrices is at least \(\beta\).

To prove this, fix a starting time and source vertex. Positive diagonal entries allow a reachable vertex to remain reachable at every subsequent time. Consider a block of \(B\) times. If the set of vertices reachable at its beginning is proper, strong connectivity of the block’s union graph implies that some edge leaves this set. Its source remains reachable until that edge occurs, so at least one new vertex becomes reachable during the block.

Thus, after \(\tau-1\) blocks, every vertex is reachable. Each resulting path can be extended to length exactly \(H\) using diagonal edges. Every edge weight is at least \(\eta\); its contribution to the corresponding matrix entry is therefore at least \(\eta^H=\beta\). This proves the claim.

Since \(w_k\) is nonnegative with total mass \(1\), the claim implies

\(\displaystyle w_{a,k}\geq\beta\qquad\text{for every }a\text{ and }k\geq H.\)

We also obtain a contraction on vectors of zero coordinate sum. Let \(M\) be any such product of \(H\) matrices and write

\(\displaystyle M=\beta\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}+N.\)

Here \(N\) is nonnegative and each of its columns sums to \(1-\tau\beta\). For any vector \(r\) satisfying \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}r=0\),

\(\displaystyle \|Mr\|_1=\|Nr\|_1\leq(1-\tau\beta)\|r\|_1. \)

Column-stochasticity also shows \(\tau\beta\leq1\). Choose

\(\displaystyle q=1-\frac{\tau\beta}{2}\in(0,1).\)

Individual column-stochastic matrices do not increase the \(\ell^1\) norm and preserve zero coordinate sums. Partitioning a product into blocks of length \(H\) therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \|A_{j+r-1}\cdots A_j r_0\|_1 \leq q^{\lfloor r/H\rfloor}\|r_0\|_1 \)

whenever \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}r_0=0\). A product of length zero means the identity.

4. Vanishing disagreement

Define

\(\displaystyle e_k=x_k-w_ks_k.\)

Its coordinate sum is zero. Using the recurrences for \(x_k,w_k,s_k\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle e_{k+1}=A_ke_k+h_{k+1}, \qquad h_{k+1}=v_{k+1}-w_{k+1}\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}v_{k+1}. \)

The forcing vector \(h_{k+1}\) also has coordinate sum zero. Because \(w_{k+1}\) is nonnegative with total mass \(1\),

\(\displaystyle \|h_{k+1}\|_1 \leq\|v_{k+1}\|_1+ |\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}v_{k+1}| \leq2\|v_{k+1}\|_1. \)

On \(E\), every innovation tends to zero, so \(\|h_k\|_1\longrightarrow0\).

Since \(e_0=0\), expanding the recurrence and applying the contraction estimate yields

\(\displaystyle \|e_k\|_1 \leq\sum_{t=1}^{k} q^{\lfloor(k-t)/H\rfloor}\|h_t\|_1. \)

Here is an explicit justification that the right side tends to zero. Given \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(T\) so that \(\|h_t\|_1\leq\varepsilon\) for \(t\geq T\). The finitely many terms with \(t<T\) tend to zero individually. The remaining terms are bounded by

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}q^{\lfloor r/H\rfloor} =\frac{\varepsilon H}{1-q}. \)

Thus \(\limsup_k\|e_k\|_1\leq\varepsilon H/(1-q)\). Letting \(\varepsilon\) decrease to zero proves \(e_k\longrightarrow0\).

Using the denominator lower bound, for \(k\geq H\),

\(\displaystyle \left|\log b_{a,k}(\theta)-s_k\right| =\frac{|e_{a,k}|}{w_{a,k}} \leq\frac{\|e_k\|_1}{\beta} \longrightarrow0. \)

Combining this with the limit of \(s_k\) proves

\(\displaystyle \lim_{k\to\infty}\log b_{a,k}(\theta) = \sum_{c\in T}\mu(c) \log\frac{\operatorname{Dirichlet}(p_c;z_{c\theta}+\mathbf1)} {\operatorname{Dirichlet}(p_c;\mathbf1)}. \)

If \(\tau=1\), normalization forces \(\mu(a)=1\) and \(A_k=1\). Then \(y_{a,k}=1\) and \(\log b_{a,k}(\theta)=F_{a\theta,k}\), so Section 1 proves the conclusion directly.

All these arguments hold on the same probability-one event \(E\), simultaneously for every supported type and every hypothesis. This completes the proof.
