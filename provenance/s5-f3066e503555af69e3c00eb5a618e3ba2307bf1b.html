The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a candidate set \(C\), distinguished candidate \(p\), committee size \(k\), deletion budget \(\ell\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over approval types \(t\subseteq C\), does there exist \(D\subseteq C\setminus\{p\}\) with \(|D|\leq\ell\) such that \(p\) belongs to no maximizing \(k\)-committee in \(S=C\setminus D\) under \(\operatorname{SAV}_{\mu,S}(W)=\sum_{t:\,t\cap S\neq\varnothing}\mu_t\frac{|W\cap t|}{|t\cap S|}\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given a finite candidate set \(C\) with \(m=|C|\), a distinguished candidate \(p\in C\), and integers \(1\leq k\leq m\) and \(0\leq\ell\leq m-k\), consider destructive control by deleting candidates under satisfaction approval voting (SAV).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) distinct approval types \(t\subseteq C\) with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Empty approval types are allowed. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number \(2^m\) of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A permissible deletion is a set \(D\subseteq C\setminus\{p\}\) with \(|D|\leq\ell\) and \(|C\setminus D|\geq k\). Put \(S=C\setminus D\); each approval type becomes \(t\cap S\), with its mass unchanged.
For each \(W\subseteq S\) with \(|W|=k\), define \(\operatorname{SAV}_{\mu,S}(W)=\sum_{t:\,t\cap S\neq\varnothing}\mu_t\frac{|W\cap t|}{|t\cap S|}\). Types whose restricted approval set is empty contribute zero. Every maximizing committee is winning, with no tie-breaking.
The decision problem asks whether a permissible deletion exists after which \(p\) belongs to no winning committee. State running times in \(m,\tau,L,k,\ell\).

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For explicitly listed finite voters, destructive candidate deletion under SAV is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete and \(\mathrm{W[2]}\)-hard parameterized by \(\ell\), even with one distinguished candidate. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.11927).
No result settling exactly the rational-distribution formulation above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Fixed the unspecified numerical domains to integers 1 <= k <= m and 0 <= ell <= m-k, and required at least k surviving candidates.
  • Made the distribution normalization explicit: positive binary rational support masses sum to one.
  • Allowed empty approval types, preserved masses after candidate deletion, and assigned zero contribution to types that become empty, following the discrete SAV convention.
  • Retained every tied maximizing committee, following the destructive control definition.
  • Used the intended deletion-budget constraint, which the formal discrete question omits but the hardness proof explicitly enforces.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. It is also \(\mathrm{W[2]}\)-hard parameterized by \(\ell\). Both hardness results hold even when every mass is dyadic, meaning its denominator is a power of two.

The proof transfers the given explicit-voter hardness result to the support representation and establishes polynomial-time verification using exact arithmetic.

1. Characterizing successful deletions

Fix a permissible deletion \(D\), and put \(S=C\setminus D\). For each surviving candidate \(c\in S\), define its score by

\(\displaystyle q_S(c)=\sum_{t:\,c\in t}\frac{\mu_t}{|t\cap S|}.\)

Every denominator in this sum is positive because \(c\in t\cap S\). Rearranging the finite sums gives, for every committee \(W\subseteq S\),

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{SAV}_{\mu,S}(W)=\sum_{c\in W}q_S(c).\)

Thus winning committees consist of candidates with the highest individual scores, with all choices at the cutoff tie allowed.

Lemma. The candidate \(p\) belongs to no winning committee exactly when

\(\displaystyle \bigl|\{c\in S\setminus\{p\}:q_S(c)>q_S(p)\}\bigr|\geq k.\)

Proof. Suppose at least \(k\) surviving candidates have scores strictly above \(q_S(p)\). A committee containing \(p\) contains at most \(k-1\) of them. Replacing \(p\) with one outside the committee strictly increases its score. Hence no such committee is winning.

Conversely, suppose at most \(k-1\) candidates have scores strictly above \(q_S(p)\). Choose \(T\) to consist of the \(k-1\) highest-scoring candidates in \(S\setminus\{p\}\), breaking ties arbitrarily. Such a set exists because \(|S|\geq k\).

Every candidate outside \(T\cup\{p\}\) has score at most \(q_S(p)\): otherwise it would be among the at most \(k-1\) candidates strictly above \(p\), all of which must lie in \(T\). By the choice of \(T\), every candidate in \(T\) also has score at least that of every candidate outside \(T\cup\{p\}\). Consequently, replacing members of \(T\cup\{p\}\) cannot increase its total score. This is a winning committee containing \(p\). \(\square\)

The strict inequality is essential: a tie can allow \(p\) into a winning committee.

2. Exact verification and membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), with positive integers \(a_t,b_t\) encoded in binary. Define

\(\displaystyle B=L+\tau\left\lceil\log_2(m+1)\right\rceil+2.\)

For the proposed deletion, compute \(r_t=|t\cap S|\), and form the common denominator

\(\displaystyle Q=\prod_t b_t\max\{1,r_t\}.\)

Its bit length is at most \(B\): the input denominators collectively use at most \(L\) bits, and the additional factors contribute at most \(\tau\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil\) bits.

For each \(c\in S\), compute the integer

\(\displaystyle A_c=\sum_{t:\,c\in t}a_t\frac{Q}{b_t r_t}.\)

Each quotient is integral, and \(q_S(c)=A_c/Q\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle 0\leq q_S(c)\leq\sum_t\mu_t=1,\)

so \(0\leq A_c\leq Q\). Thus all comparisons can be performed exactly with integers of at most \(B\) bits. Types with \(r_t=0\) contribute to no candidate score.

Using ordinary quadratic-time integer multiplication and division, a conservative bound for computing the scores and applying the lemma is

\(\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{verify}}=O\!\left(L+m\tau B^2\right)\)

bit operations. This also covers checking the deletion constraints. The value of \(k\) enters only through the final threshold comparison.

A certificate is the deletion set \(D\), represented by an \(m\)-bit incidence vector. Its validity and success are therefore verifiable in polynomial time. The problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

3. Polynomial reduction from explicit voters

Use the given explicit-voter hardness result: destructive candidate deletion under SAV is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete and \(\mathrm{W[2]}\)-hard parameterized by the deletion budget, even with one distinguished candidate. The source uses exclusion from every winning committee; its parameterized hardness statement is Theorem 18. [Yang, *On the Parameterized Complexity of Controlling Approval-Based Multiwinner Voting*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.11927).

Consider such an instance with explicitly listed ballots \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\), where \(n\geq1\). Preserve \(C,p,k,\ell\).

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\geq n\). Conceptually pad the election with \(N-n\) empty ballots. For each type \(t\subseteq C\), let

\(\displaystyle h_t=\bigl|\{i:v_i=t\}\bigr|+(N-n)\mathbf{1}_{t=\varnothing}.\)

Output exactly the types with \(h_t>0\), assigning mass

\(\displaystyle \mu_t=\frac{h_t}{N}.\)

These types are distinct, their masses are positive dyadic rationals, and

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_t=\frac{\sum_t h_t}{N}=1.\)

There are at most \(n+1\) output types. Each mass requires \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits. Grouping equal ballots and constructing the masses takes polynomial time in the explicit input length; under a bit-vector encoding, grouping can be done in \(O(nm\log(n+1))\) bit comparisons, with polynomial additional counting and encoding work.

For every permissible deletion \(D\), put \(S=C\setminus D\). For every \(k\)-committee \(W\subseteq S\),

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{SAV}_{\mu,S}(W) =\frac1N\sum_{i:\,v_i\cap S\neq\varnothing} \frac{|W\cap v_i|}{|v_i\cap S|}. \)

Indeed, grouping identical ballots preserves their summed contributions, and every padding ballot contributes zero. The right-hand side is precisely the explicit-voter committee score multiplied by the positive constant \(1/N\).

Consequently, after every deletion, the two representations have exactly the same maximizing committees, including all ties. Their permissible deletion sets are also identical because \(C,p,k,\ell\) were preserved. Hence the reduction preserves both yes-instances and no-instances.

An explicit instance with no voters is always a no-instance: all committees tie, and some committee contains \(p\). It can be mapped to the fixed no-instance with \(C=\{p\}\), \(k=1\), \(\ell=0\), and mass one on the empty type.

This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness. Together with Section 2, it proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness. Because the reduction preserves \(\ell\) exactly, it also transfers \(\mathrm{W[2]}\)-hardness.

4. Running times

An exact algorithm enumerates all permissible deletion sets and applies the verifier. Since \(\ell\leq m-k\), every enumerated set of size at most \(\ell\) leaves at least \(k\) candidates. Its running time is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left[ \left(\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{m-1}{j}\right) \left(L+m\tau\left(L+\tau\left\lceil\log_2(m+1)\right\rceil+2\right)^2\right) \right]. \)

For fixed \(\ell\), this is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\); for unrestricted \(\ell\), it is bounded by \(2^{m-1}\) times the verification bound. The score calculation requires no enumeration of committees or of the \(2^m\) possible approval types.

Thus the support representation does not remove the hardness: the decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, and a polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
