The continuous mirror question, as graded

For each type, its allocation \(x_\theta\) must maximize \(u_\theta^\top x\) subject to \(x\geq 0\), \(p^\top x\leq w_\theta\), and its physical constraints; clearing requires \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_{\theta j}=\bar{s}_j\) and each type spends its budget. The output must also include BP-SOP multipliers \(r_{\theta q}\geq 0\) and perturbations \(\lambda_\theta=\sum_q r_{\theta q}\) satisfying the paper’s fixed-point correspondence. For a guaranteed-existence version, assume an unconstrained good and that every type values every good positively.

Theorem. For every fixed number of goods $m$, the promised exact equilibrium-and-fixed-point search problem is solvable in time polynomial in $\tau,k,L$. The output has polynomial-length real-algebraic encodings. The polynomial’s exponent may depend on $m$.

The proof treats binary-rational masses directly and allows arbitrary indifference among maximizing bundles. Its central structural result is that a clearing allocation can be obtained by mixing at most $m$ demand profiles, using the same mixing weights for every consumer type.

1. Definitions and admissible prices

Let $\Theta$ be the support of consumer types, with $|\Theta|=\tau$, masses $\mu_\theta>0$ satisfying $\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1$, positive budgets $w_\theta$, and positive utility coefficients $u_{\theta j}$. Supplies $\bar s_j$ are positive. All these data are rational.

The resource groups are pairwise disjoint nonempty subsets of the goods. Type $\theta$ is constrained by
$$ A_{\theta q}x=\sum_{j\in q}x_j\leq1, \qquad q\in T_\theta. $$
Fix a good $h$ outside every resource group.

Write
$$ W=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta w_\theta. $$
Let $J_0$ consist of the goods that are physically unconstrained for at least one type:
$$ J_0= \left\{ j:\text{there exists }\theta\text{ with } j\notin\bigcup_{q\in T_\theta}q \right\}. $$
In particular, $h\in J_0$.

At an equilibrium, $p_j>0$ for every $j\in J_0$. Indeed, if such a good had zero price, the corresponding type could increase its consumption without violating any constraint and strictly increase utility. It would therefore have no maximizing bundle.

Aggregating exhausted budgets and using clearing gives
$$ p^\top\bar s = \sum_\theta\mu_\theta p^\top x_\theta = W. $$
Consequently every equilibrium price belongs to
$$ \mathcal P= \left\{ p\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m: p_j>0\text{ for }j\in J_0,\quad p^\top\bar s=W \right\}. $$

For $p\in\mathcal P$, define the consumer’s feasible polytope and demand face by
$$ H_\theta(p)= \left\{ x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m: p^\top x\leq w_\theta,\quad A_{\theta q}x\leq1\text{ for }q\in T_\theta \right\}, $$
$$ D_\theta(p)= \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x\in H_\theta(p)} u_\theta^\top x. $$

These sets have the following properties.

  • $H_\theta(p)$ is nonempty, since it contains zero.
  • It is bounded: a physically constrained coordinate is at most one, while an unconstrained coordinate satisfies $x_j\leq w_\theta/p_j$, with $p_j>0$.
  • Hence $D_\theta(p)$ is a nonempty compact polytope.
  • Every $x\in D_\theta(p)$ exhausts the budget. Otherwise, increasing consumption of $h$ slightly would preserve feasibility and increase utility.

Define the weighted Minkowski sum
$$ K(p)= \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta D_\theta(p) = \left\{ \sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_\theta: x_\theta\in D_\theta(p) \right\}. $$
It is a nonempty compact polytope. Budget exhaustion implies
$$ K(p)\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb R^m:p^\top z=W\}. $$
Since $p_h>0$, this hyperplane has dimension $m-1$.

Thus $p\in\mathcal P$ is an equilibrium price exactly when
$$ \bar s\in K(p). $$

2. Equilibria supply precisely the required fixed-point certificates

The following result also verifies that finding an equilibrium suffices mathematically for the required BP-SOP output.

Proposition. Every equilibrium admits the specified nonnegative BP-SOP multipliers with
$$ \lambda_\theta=\sum_{q\in T_\theta}r_{\theta q}. $$
Conversely, a feasible BP-SOP KKT point satisfying this identity and $p\geq0$ is an equilibrium.

Equilibrium implies fixed-point KKT

Fix an equilibrium $(p,x)$. Each consumer linear program is feasible and has a finite attained optimum. Linear-programming strong duality supplies optimal dual variables
$$ \alpha_\theta\geq0,\qquad \gamma_{\theta q}\geq0 $$
satisfying
$$ \alpha_\theta p_j+ \sum_{\substack{q\in T_\theta\\j\in q}}\gamma_{\theta q} \geq u_{\theta j}, $$
$$ x_{\theta j} \left( \alpha_\theta p_j+ \sum_{\substack{q\in T_\theta\\j\in q}}\gamma_{\theta q} -u_{\theta j} \right)=0, $$
$$ \gamma_{\theta q}(A_{\theta q}x_\theta-1)=0. $$
Complementary slackness holds for the specified optimal allocation, including when it is one of several maximizing bundles.

The inequality for $h$ gives
$$ \alpha_\theta p_h\geq u_{\theta h}>0, $$
so $\alpha_\theta>0$. Define
$$ \beta_\theta=\frac1{\alpha_\theta}, \qquad r_{\theta q}=\frac{\gamma_{\theta q}}{\alpha_\theta}, \qquad R_{\theta j}= \sum_{\substack{q\in T_\theta\\j\in q}}r_{\theta q}. $$
Then
$$ d_{\theta j}:=p_j+R_{\theta j}-\beta_\theta u_{\theta j}\geq0, \qquad x_{\theta j}d_{\theta j}=0, $$
$$ r_{\theta q}(A_{\theta q}x_\theta-1)=0. $$

Put $U_\theta=u_\theta^\top x_\theta$. Positive expenditure and positive utility coefficients imply $U_\theta>0$. Multiplying the stationarity equalities by $x_{\theta j}$ and summing yields
$$ \beta_\theta U_\theta = p^\top x_\theta+ \sum_{q\in T_\theta}r_{\theta q}A_{\theta q}x_\theta = w_\theta+\sum_{q\in T_\theta}r_{\theta q}. $$
Therefore, with $\lambda_\theta=\sum_qr_{\theta q}$,
$$ \beta_\theta=\frac{w_\theta+\lambda_\theta}{U_\theta}. $$

For fixed $\lambda$, BP-SOP’s Lagrangian, with the conventions required in the question, is
$$ \mathcal L= \sum_\theta\mu_\theta(w_\theta+\lambda_\theta)\log U_\theta -\sum_jp_j\left(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_{\theta j}-\bar s_j\right) -\sum_{\theta,q}\mu_\theta r_{\theta q} (A_{\theta q}x_\theta-1). $$
Its derivative is
$$ \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial x_{\theta j}} = \mu_\theta(\beta_\theta u_{\theta j}-p_j-R_{\theta j}) = -\mu_\theta d_{\theta j}. $$
Thus the nonnegativity multipliers are $\mu_\theta d_{\theta j}$, and all KKT conditions hold with exactly the requested normalization.

These conditions also certify global optimality. For any other feasible allocation $y$ with positive type utilities, logarithmic concavity gives
$$ F(y)-F(x) \leq \sum_{\theta,j}\mu_\theta\beta_\theta u_{\theta j} (y_{\theta j}-x_{\theta j}). $$
Substitute $\beta_\theta u_{\theta j}=p_j+R_{\theta j}-d_{\theta j}$. The price contribution vanishes by clearing. Each physical-constraint contribution is nonpositive by feasibility and complementary slackness. The remaining contribution equals
$$ -\sum_{\theta,j}\mu_\theta d_{\theta j}y_{\theta j}\leq0. $$
An allocation with a zero type utility has logarithmic objective value $-\infty$.

Fixed-point KKT implies equilibrium

At such a KKT point, let
$$ \beta_\theta=\frac{w_\theta+\lambda_\theta}{U_\theta}>0. $$
Stationarity and complementary slackness give the same conditions involving $d_{\theta j}$. Taking their scalar product with $x_\theta$ gives
$$ w_\theta+\lambda_\theta = \beta_\theta U_\theta = p^\top x_\theta+\sum_qr_{\theta q} = p^\top x_\theta+\lambda_\theta. $$
Hence $p^\top x_\theta=w_\theta$.

For every consumer-feasible bundle $y_\theta$,
$$ \beta_\theta u_\theta^\top y_\theta \leq p^\top y_\theta+\sum_qr_{\theta q}A_{\theta q}y_\theta \leq w_\theta+\lambda_\theta = \beta_\theta U_\theta. $$
Division by $\beta_\theta>0$ proves consumer optimality. Clearing and nonnegative prices are already hypotheses. This proves the proposition.

3. A clearing allocation needs at most $m$ common demand profiles

Call $(v_\theta)_{\theta\in\Theta}$ an exposed demand profile at $p$ if there is a direction $a\in\mathbb R^m$ such that, for every type $\theta$, $v_\theta$ uniquely maximizes $a^\top x$ over $D_\theta(p)$.

Lemma. If $p\in\mathcal P$ and $\bar s\in K(p)$, there exist at most $m$ exposed demand profiles $(v_\theta^{(i)})_\theta$ and nonnegative weights $c_i$ summing to one such that
$$ \bar s= \sum_i c_i\sum_\theta\mu_\theta v_\theta^{(i)}. $$
Consequently,
$$ x_\theta=\sum_i c_i v_\theta^{(i)} $$
is a clearing equilibrium allocation at $p$.

Proof. First consider a vertex $z$ of $K(p)$. Every vertex of a polytope is uniquely exposed by a linear functional. Here is a direct justification. If the polytope has other vertices, let $C$ be their convex hull. Since $z$ is a vertex, $z\notin C$. Let $y$ be a point of $C$ closest to $z$ and set $a=z-y$. The minimizing property gives
$$ a^\top(v-y)\leq0 \qquad(v\in C). $$
Hence
$$ a^\top(z-v)\geq\|z-y\|^2>0 \qquad(v\in C). $$
Thus $a$ uniquely exposes $z$. For a singleton polytope, any direction suffices.

For each $\theta$, choose a maximizer $v_\theta$ of $a^\top x$ over $D_\theta(p)$. Their weighted sum maximizes $a^\top z$ over $K(p)$, so it equals the uniquely exposed vertex $z$.

Each type’s maximizer must also be unique. Otherwise, replacing one type’s bundle by a different maximizer, while holding all other bundles fixed, would produce a different aggregate maximizer because $\mu_\theta>0$. This contradicts uniqueness of $z$.

Therefore every vertex of $K(p)$ comes from an exposed demand profile.

Finally, $K(p)$ has affine dimension at most $m-1$. Any point in it is a convex combination of at most $m$ vertices. To verify this bound, start with a finite vertex combination. If more than $m$ vertices have positive weights, they are affinely dependent: there are scalars $b_i$, not all zero, with
$$ \sum_i b_i=0, \qquad \sum_i b_i z_i=0. $$
Both positive and negative $b_i$ occur. Setting
$$ \varepsilon=\min_{b_i>0}\frac{c_i}{b_i} $$
and replacing $c_i$ by $c_i-\varepsilon b_i$ preserves the represented point and total weight, keeps every weight nonnegative, and removes at least one positive weight. Repetition proves the bound.

Apply this to $\bar s$. Each resulting $x_\theta$ lies in the convex demand face $D_\theta(p)$, and their weighted sum is $\bar s$. This proves the lemma. $\square$

The lemma holds without fixing $m$. Fixing $m$ makes the number of profiles usable algorithmically.

4. Polynomially many descriptions of exposed profiles for fixed $m$

Assume henceforth that $m$ is fixed.

Because the resource groups are disjoint and exclude $h$, each type has at most $m-1$ physical constraints. Thus $H_\theta(p)$ has at most $2m$ defining inequalities: $m$ nonnegativity inequalities, at most $m-1$ physical inequalities, and one budget inequality.

Every vertex is obtained by making $m$ linearly independent defining inequalities tight. Enumerate all such candidate sets $B$. There are at most
$$ \binom{2m}{m} $$
candidates per type, a constant depending only on $m$.

For a candidate $B$, Cramer’s rule gives a vector
$$ v_{\theta B}(p)=\frac{N_{\theta B}(p)}{\Delta_{\theta B}(p)} $$
when $\Delta_{\theta B}(p)\neq0$. Here $N_{\theta B}$ is a vector of rational polynomials and $\Delta_{\theta B}$ is a rational polynomial.

Both have degree at most one: only the budget row of the candidate linear system depends on $p$, and determinants are multilinear in their rows. Candidates with identically zero determinant are discarded.

At a particular price, a candidate is a genuine feasible vertex exactly when its determinant is nonzero and its coordinates satisfy all defining inequalities. Degeneracy causes no omission: the active normals at a vertex span $\mathbb R^m$, so some independent active set appears in the enumeration. Indeed, if they did not span, a nonzero direction annihilating them would permit sufficiently small perturbations in both directions while preserving every inequality, contradicting extremality.

We now construct a polynomial family in the $2m$ variables $(p,a)$. It contains:

  • the price-domain polynomials $p_j$ and $p^\top\bar s-W$;
  • all candidate determinants and numerators needed to test feasibility;
  • all numerators of candidate utility differences

$$ u_\theta^\top v_{\theta B}(p) - u_\theta^\top v_{\theta C}(p); $$

  • all numerators of directional-score differences

$$ a^\top v_{\theta B}(p) - a^\top v_{\theta C}(p). $$

For example, the numerator of the last difference is
$$ \bigl(a^\top N_{\theta B}(p)\bigr)\Delta_{\theta C}(p) - \bigl(a^\top N_{\theta C}(p)\bigr)\Delta_{\theta B}(p). $$
Including the determinant signs makes every rational comparison unambiguous.

The family contains $O_m(\tau)$ polynomials, each of degree at most three and coefficient bit length polynomial in $L$. On a region where all their signs are specified, the following information is constant for every type:

  • which candidate vertices are feasible;
  • which maximize utility;
  • which of those maximize the directional score.

Break remaining ties by the first candidate in a fixed ordering. This selects one candidate index $B_\theta(\sigma)$ for each type, depending only on the sign vector $\sigma$.

The real-algebraic algorithmic fact used here is:

For a fixed number of variables, realizable sign conditions of rational polynomials can be enumerated, and a point satisfying a quantifier-free polynomial formula can be found exactly, in polynomial bit complexity in the number of polynomials, their degrees, and their coefficient encoding lengths. Sample points have polynomial-length algebraic representations.

This follows from standard cylindrical algebraic decomposition and its bit-complexity analysis. It includes zero signs and strict inequalities. See [Basu–Pollack–Roy, *Algorithms in Real Algebraic Geometry*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/3-540-33099-2) and [Basu’s survey, Section 2.1.2](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011_final.pdf).

Its hypotheses hold: there are $2m$ variables, $m$ is fixed, the coefficients are rational, and the degree and encoding bounds above are polynomial.

We can therefore enumerate polynomially many realizable sign vectors and retain those satisfying $p\in\mathcal P$. For each retained $\sigma$, let $C_\sigma(p,a)$ be the conjunction specifying its polynomial signs, and define
$$ v_{\theta\sigma}(p)=v_{\theta,B_\theta(\sigma)}(p), \qquad V_\sigma(p)=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta v_{\theta\sigma}(p). $$

Whenever $C_\sigma(p,a)$ holds, every $v_{\theta\sigma}(p)$ belongs to $D_\theta(p)$, so $V_\sigma(p)\in K(p)$. Moreover, every vertex of $K(p)$ is represented by some such selection: choose a direction uniquely exposing that vertex and use Section 3.

The sign region need not be connected. All selection decisions depend only on the recorded signs.

5. Exact clearing in a fixed number of variables

For each retained $\sigma$, write
$$ Q_\sigma(p)= \prod_{\theta\in\Theta} \Delta_{\theta,B_\theta(\sigma)}(p), $$
$$ H_{\sigma j}(p)= \sum_{\theta\in\Theta} \mu_\theta N_{\theta,B_\theta(\sigma),j}(p) \prod_{\eta\neq\theta} \Delta_{\eta,B_\eta(\sigma)}(p). $$
Then
$$ V_{\sigma j}(p)=\frac{H_{\sigma j}(p)}{Q_\sigma(p)}. $$
Both $Q_\sigma$ and $H_{\sigma j}$ have degree at most $\tau$. Their denominator is nonzero whenever $C_\sigma(p,a)$ holds.

Enumerate ordered $m$-tuples of retained sign vectors
$$ (\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_m). $$
There are polynomially many tuples for fixed $m$.

For each tuple, seek variables
$$ p\in\mathbb R^m,\qquad a_1,\ldots,a_m\in\mathbb R^m,\qquad c_1,\ldots,c_m\in\mathbb R $$
satisfying
$$ p\in\mathcal P,\qquad C_{\sigma_i}(p,a_i)\quad(1\leq i\leq m), $$
$$ c_i\geq0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^m c_i=1, $$
$$ \sum_{i=1}^m c_iV_{\sigma_i}(p)=\bar s. $$

The last equality is made polynomial by multiplying by the nonzero common denominator. For every good $j$, it becomes
$$ \sum_{i=1}^m c_iH_{\sigma_i j}(p) \prod_{\ell\neq i}Q_{\sigma_\ell}(p) - \bar s_j\prod_{\ell=1}^mQ_{\sigma_\ell}(p) =0. $$
Its degree is at most $m\tau+1$.

Each resulting system has
$$ m+m^2+m=m^2+2m $$
variables, a fixed number. It has polynomially many constraints and polynomial coefficient encoding length.

For clarity about the latter assertion, products of at most $m\tau$ affine polynomials in $m$ price variables have only polynomially many monomials when $m$ is fixed. Coefficient bit lengths grow polynomially under these products and the displayed sums. Thus the systems can be constructed explicitly in polynomial bit complexity.

Apply the fixed-dimensional real-algebraic algorithm to each system.

Soundness

From a sampled solution, define
$$ x_\theta=\sum_{i=1}^m c_i v_{\theta\sigma_i}(p). $$
Every summand belongs to $D_\theta(p)$, so convexity gives $x_\theta\in D_\theta(p)$. The clearing equations give
$$ \sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_\theta=\bar s. $$
Therefore $(p,x)$ is an equilibrium.

Completeness under the promise

The algorithm therefore finds an equilibrium.

6. Polynomial-time reconstruction of the fixed-point multipliers

It remains to construct the certificate from Section 2 with controlled encoding size.

Fix the computed price $p$ and allocation $x$. For type $\theta$, put
$$ U_\theta=u_\theta^\top x_\theta, \qquad d_\theta=1+|T_\theta|\leq m. $$
Its dual linear program has $d_\theta$ variables and is
$$ \min\left\{ \alpha w_\theta+\sum_q\gamma_q: \alpha,\gamma_q\geq0,\quad \alpha p_j+\sum_{\substack{q\in T_\theta\\j\in q}}\gamma_q \geq u_{\theta j}\text{ for every }j \right\}. $$
Strong duality applies because the primal is feasible, bounded, and attains its optimum. Thus the dual optimal face is nonempty and satisfies
$$ \alpha w_\theta+\sum_q\gamma_q=U_\theta. $$
It is bounded, since
$$ 0\leq\alpha\leq U_\theta/w_\theta, \qquad 0\leq\gamma_q\leq U_\theta. $$
Being a nonempty compact polytope, it has a vertex. Such a vertex is also a vertex of the dual feasible polyhedron: if it were a nontrivial convex combination of two dual-feasible points, optimality of the average would force both points to be optimal.

Enumerate sets of $d_\theta$ independent tight dual inequalities, solve the resulting systems, and check dual feasibility and objective value $U_\theta$. There are at most $2m$ dual inequalities, so only a constant number of candidates per type. The preceding argument guarantees an accepted candidate.

For every accepted candidate,
$$ \alpha_\theta p_h\geq u_{\theta h}>0, $$
hence $\alpha_\theta>0$. Output
$$ r_{\theta q}=\frac{\gamma_{\theta q}}{\alpha_\theta}, \qquad \lambda_\theta=\sum_qr_{\theta q}. $$
Section 2 proves that these are valid BP-SOP fixed-point multipliers.

Exact encoding and total complexity

The sampled tuple has a polynomial-length algebraic representation because the sampling system has fixed dimension. It can be represented in a common real number field of polynomial degree and polynomial coefficient bit length.

Every allocation coordinate and every dual candidate is obtained by rational arithmetic and determinants of fixed size in that field. The divisions defining $r_{\theta q}$ have nonzero denominators. These operations, sign tests, and equality tests have polynomial bit complexity, and the resulting coordinates retain polynomial-length standard real-algebraic encodings.

There are polynomially many sign vectors, polynomially many tuples for fixed $m$, polynomial-time sampling calls, and a constant number of dual candidates per type. All masses are used as rational coefficients; no replication into individual agents occurs.

This proves the theorem.

7. What remains open

The proved algorithm has running time of the form

The unresolved step is either to obtain an algorithm whose polynomial exponent is independent of $m$, including exact encoding bounds, or to establish the requested hardness while preserving all stated restrictions.
