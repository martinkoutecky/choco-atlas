The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite rational supports \(V_s,V_b\subseteq[0,1]\) and masses \(\mu_s,\mu_b\), let \(F_s(p)=\sum_{s\le p}\mu_s(s)\). For each buyer type \(b\ge r\), use a fixed tie rule to choose \(p_r(b)\in\arg\max_{p\in[r,b]}(b-p)F_s(p)\); for \(b<r\), do not trade. With type-pair mass \(\mu_s(s)\mu_b(b)\), let \(W(r)\) be aggregate welfare when a pair trades exactly when \(s\le p_r(b)\), and let \(W^*=\sum_{s,b}\mu_s(s)\mu_b(b)\max\{s,b\}\). Find a reserve maximizing \(W(r)\), or one satisfying \(W(r)\ge 0.746W^*\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There is one indivisible item, initially owned by the seller; thus \(m=1\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here the types are role-tagged values in nonempty finite rational sets \(V_s,V_b\subseteq[0,1]\), with \(\tau=|V_s|+|V_b|\).
The masses satisfy \(\sum_s\mu_s(s)=\sum_b\mu_b(b)=1\); seller and buyer values are independent. Let \(L\) be the total binary encoding length of the values and masses.
For a reserve \(r\in[0,1]\), define \(F_s(p)=\sum_{s\le p}\mu_s(s)\). A buyer with \(b<r\) does not trade; otherwise it chooses the smallest maximizer \(p_r(b)\in\arg\max_{p\in[r,b]}(b-p)F_s(p)\).
Trade occurs exactly when \(b\ge r\) and \(s\le p_r(b)\). Define \(x_r(s,b)\) as its indicator, \(W(r)=\sum_{s,b}\mu_s(s)\mu_b(b)[x_r(s,b)b+(1-x_r(s,b))s]\), and \(W^*=\sum_{s,b}\mu_s(s)\mu_b(b)\max\{s,b\}\).

Can a rational reserve \(r\) satisfying \(W(r)\ge0.746W^*\) always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is achieving this guarantee NP-hard?

The known approximation theorem guarantees a reserve attaining \(0.746W^*\) for every pair of independent value distributions, including discrete distributions and these mass distributions. [Dobzinski–Shaulker](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04890v1#S5)
That result states no running-time bound in the explicit rational-support bit model; the existence guarantee itself is already established on both sides.

  • Restricted reserves to [0,1] and requested a rational reserve for the computational problem.

Result

The guarantee is computable in polynomial time. In fact, an exact welfare-maximizing rational reserve can be computed in time \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)\), under the specified smallest-maximizer tie-breaking rule.

The proof constructs a polynomial-size set of rational reserves containing a representative of every possible allocation pattern.

1. Reducing each buyer’s choice to finitely many offers

Write the seller support as \(s_1<\cdots<s_n\), and let \(k=|V_b|\), so \(n+k=\tau\). Define the cumulative seller masses

\(A_i=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}\mu_s(s_\ell)=F_s(s_i)\), for \(1\le i\le n\).

All \(A_i\) are positive because all supported types have positive mass.

Fix a reserve \(r\) and a buyer value \(b\ge r\). Define

\(C_b(r)=\{r\}\cup\{s_i:r\le s_i\le b\}\).

Lemma. The smallest maximizer of \((b-p)F_s(p)\) over \(p\in[r,b]\) is the smallest maximizer over \(C_b(r)\).

Proof. Consider any \(p\in[r,b]\setminus C_b(r)\).

If \(F_s(p)>0\), let

\(q=\max\bigl(\{r\}\cup\{s_i:r\le s_i\le p\}\bigr)\).

Then \(q<p\), and there is no seller support value in \((q,p]\). Consequently \(F_s(q)=F_s(p)\), and

\((b-q)F_s(q)-(b-p)F_s(p)=(p-q)F_s(p)>0\).

Thus \(p\) cannot maximize the utility.

If \(F_s(p)=0\), its utility is zero. Every feasible offer has nonnegative utility. If the maximum utility is positive, \(p\) is not a maximizer. If the maximum is zero, \(r\) is also a maximizer and is smaller than \(p\).

The finite set \(C_b(r)\) therefore contains a global maximizer, and no point outside it can be the smallest global maximizer. \(\square\)

This lemma also proves that the prescribed smallest maximizer exists, despite the discontinuities of \(F_s\).

2. A polynomial-size set of breakpoints

For every \(b\in V_b\) and \(i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), define the rational number

\(t_{b,i,j}=b-\dfrac{(b-s_j)A_j}{A_i}\).

It is the unique solution in \(r\) of

\((b-r)A_i=(b-s_j)A_j\).

Construct the breakpoint set

\(D=\{0,1\}\cup V_s\cup V_b\cup\{t_{b,i,j}:b\in V_b,\ 1\le i,j\le n,\ 0\le t_{b,i,j}\le1\}\).

There are at most \(2+n+k+kn^2\) distinct breakpoints. Some of the utility crossings included here concern infeasible offers; including them only enlarges the set and does not affect correctness.

Sort its distinct elements as

\(0=d_0<d_1<\cdots<d_q=1\).

We next prove that the allocation is constant on each open interval \((d_h,d_{h+1})\).

3. Constancy of the allocation between breakpoints

Fix an open interval \(I=(d_h,d_{h+1})\) and a buyer value \(b\in V_b\).

Because every buyer support value belongs to \(D\), either every reserve in \(I\) exceeds \(b\), or every reserve in \(I\) is below \(b\). In the first case this buyer never trades throughout \(I\).

Consider the second case. Because every seller support value also belongs to \(D\), the following are constant throughout \(I\):

  • The seller support offers satisfying \(r\le s_j\le b\).
  • The set of seller types satisfying \(s\le r\).
  • The cumulative mass \(F_s(r)\).

For every feasible seller support offer \(s_j\), its utility is the constant

\(u_j=(b-s_j)A_j\).

Thus the comparisons between seller support offers, including ties, do not change within \(I\).

There remain two cases for the reserve offer.

Case 1: \(F_s(r)=0\) throughout \(I\). Its utility is constantly zero, so its comparisons with all seller support offers are constant.

Case 2: \(F_s(r)=A_i>0\) throughout \(I\), for some \(i\). Its utility is

\(u_r=(b-r)A_i\).

For each feasible seller support offer \(s_j\), equality \(u_r=u_j\) occurs only at \(t_{b,i,j}\). Any such point in \([0,1]\) belongs to \(D\), so no equality occurs inside \(I\). Hence the sign of every comparison is constant throughout \(I\).

Moreover, every feasible seller support offer is strictly larger than \(r\) throughout \(I\). Therefore, whenever the reserve offer ties a seller support offer, the smallest-price rule consistently favors the reserve. Ties between seller support offers are consistently resolved by their fixed numerical ordering.

By the lemma, the selected offer throughout \(I\) is consequently either:

  • The reserve \(r\) throughout the interval; or
  • One fixed seller support value throughout the interval.

If the selected offer is the reserve, the accepted seller types are constant because \(I\) contains no seller support value. If the selected offer is a fixed support value, the accepted seller types are plainly constant.

This holds for every buyer value. Therefore, for every supported pair \((s,b)\), the indicator \(x_r(s,b)\) is constant on \(I\). It follows directly from the definition of welfare that \(W(r)\) is constant on \(I\).

No continuity assertion at the breakpoints is needed.

4. Exact maximization over rational reserves

Define the candidate reserve set

\(R=D\cup\left\{\dfrac{d_h+d_{h+1}}{2}:0\le h<q\right\}\).

Every candidate is rational, and

\(|R|=2|D|-1=O(\tau^3)\).

For every real reserve \(r\in[0,1]\):

  • If \(r\in D\), it is itself a candidate.
  • Otherwise, \(r\) lies in one open interval between consecutive breakpoints, and its welfare equals that interval’s midpoint welfare.

Consequently,

\(\max_{r\in[0,1]}W(r)=\max_{r\in R}W(r)\).

In particular, the maximum is attained at a rational reserve.

The algorithm is now explicit:

  • Sort the seller support and compute the cumulative masses \(A_i\).
  • Construct and sort \(D\), then construct \(R\).
  • For each \(r\in R\), compute every eligible buyer’s price by enumerating \(C_b(r)\), maximizing utility exactly, and choosing the smallest maximizing price.
  • Compute \(W(r)\) exactly from the supported seller-buyer pairs.
  • Return a candidate with largest welfare.

Evaluating breakpoint reserves separately ensures that the algorithm respects the prescribed tie-breaking rule even where the allocation changes.

5. Bit complexity

Let \(Q\) be the product of the positive denominators of all input values and masses. The total binary encoding length is \(L\), so the binary length of \(Q\) is \(O(L)\).

Every input value and mass can be written with denominator \(Q\). Write

\(b=\beta_b/Q,\qquad s_j=\sigma_j/Q,\qquad A_i=a_i/Q\).

Because values and cumulative masses belong to \([0,1]\), the corresponding integer numerators lie between zero and \(Q\), with \(a_i>0\). Each utility-crossing breakpoint has the representation

\(t_{b,i,j}=\dfrac{\beta_ba_i-(\beta_b-\sigma_j)a_j}{Qa_i}\).

Its numerator and denominator have \(O(L)\) bits. The midpoint of two such breakpoints also has \(O(L)\) bits: its construction multiplies only a constant number of integers of that length.

For a fixed candidate reserve, all utility comparisons involve rational numbers with \(O(L)\)-bit numerators and denominators, and can be performed exactly by integer cross-multiplication.

Welfare evaluation also has polynomial bit complexity. Each summand in \(W(r)\) is a product of two input masses and one input value, selected according to \(x_r(s,b)\). Thus every summand has denominator dividing \(Q^3\). Their sum can be accumulated over that common denominator. Since welfare lies in \([0,1]\), the resulting numerator is at most \(Q^3\), and therefore has \(O(L)\) bits.

There are \(O(\tau^3)\) candidate reserves. Using the precomputed cumulative masses, evaluating one candidate takes \(O(kn)=O(\tau^2)\) arithmetic operations: enumerate the relevant offers for each buyer and then sum welfare over all supported pairs. Construction and sorting are polynomial as well.

Hence a direct implementation takes

\(O\!\left(\tau^5\operatorname{poly}(L)\right)\)

bit operations and returns a reserve of polynomial encoding length. Since \(m=1\), this is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

6. Obtaining the approximation guarantee

The existence guarantee supplied in the question states that some reserve \(\bar r\in[0,1]\) satisfies \(W(\bar r)\ge0.746W^*\); see [Dobzinski–Shaulker, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04890v1#S5). Its distributional hypotheses apply here: the masses define independent probability distributions supported in \([0,1]\), and the task explicitly grants applicability to these discrete distributions.

If \(r^\star\) is the exact maximizer returned by the algorithm, then

\(W(r^\star)\ge W(\bar r)\ge0.746W^*=\dfrac{373}{500}W^*\).

Thus the supplied existence guarantee becomes an exact polynomial-time construction in the explicit rational-support model.
