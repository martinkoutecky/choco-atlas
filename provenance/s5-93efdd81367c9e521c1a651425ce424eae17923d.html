The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given binary candidate positions \(c_i\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}\), a finite support \(T\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\ell}\) of voter types with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and the paper’s tie-breaking order, does there exist a shared salience vector \(w'\in[0,1]^{\ell}\) with \(\sum_k w'_k=1\) such that \(c_1\)'s vote mass is at least every other candidate’s? Each type votes for its nearest candidate under weighted \(L_1\) distance, with ties broken by the given order.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given \(m\geq2\) labeled candidates with positions \(c_i\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}\), where \(\ell\geq1\), a society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\ell}\) of \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A controller freely chooses one shared real vector \(w'\in[0,1]^{\ell}\) satisfying \(\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}w'_k=1\). There are no additional manipulation constraints.
Each type casts its entire mass for a nearest candidate under weighted \(L_1\) distance, breaking distance ties in the fixed order \(c_1\prec\cdots\prec c_m\), with the earlier candidate preferred. Let \(M_i(w')\) denote the resulting vote mass for \(c_i\).
The decision problem asks whether some feasible \(w'\) satisfies \(M_1(w')\geq M_i(w')\) for every \(i\); thus a tie for the largest vote mass counts as winning.

Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\ell,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For an explicitly listed finite electorate, the corresponding unrestricted binary-issue plurality-control problem is NP-complete, already with two candidates ([Estornell et al., 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v124/estornell20a/estornell20a.pdf)).
No result explicitly treating this rational-mass support formulation was located in the literature search.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, already for \(m=2\). Thus a polynomial-time algorithm in the stated input parameters would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. The finite-electorate hardness theorem applies

We use the finite-electorate result given in the question. More precisely, Theorem 2 of [Estornell et al. (2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v124/estornell20a/estornell20a.pdf) establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness of deciding whether the preferred candidate can win plurality, already with two candidates and binary issues, when the issue weights are unrestricted.

Its model matches the required conventions: weights are nonnegative and sum to one, distance ties follow candidate order, and the objective allows the preferred candidate to share the largest vote count. Taking their distance parameter \(p=1\) gives the distance in this question. Taking their interval constraint to be \([0,1]^\ell\) leaves exactly the feasible simplex required here.

2. Polynomial reduction to rational-mass support

Consider such a finite-electorate instance with \(n\geq1\) explicitly listed voters \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}\). Keep the candidate positions and their order unchanged.

Define the support and multiplicities by

\( T=\{v_j:1\leq j\leq n\}, \qquad n_t=|\{j:v_j=t\}|. \)

For each \(t\in T\), assign the binary-encoded rational mass

\( \mu_t=\frac{n_t}{n}. \)

These masses are positive, and \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Grouping the explicitly listed voters takes polynomial time. Also, \(\tau\leq n\), and each mass requires \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits for its numerator and denominator. Consequently, the output length is polynomial in the source input length.

To prove correctness, fix any feasible weight vector \(w'\). Let \(a(t,w')\) be the candidate selected by type \(t\), including the prescribed distance tie-breaking. Every listed voter at position \(t\) selects this same candidate.

If \(N_i(w')\) is candidate \(i\)'s vote count in the finite electorate, then

\( N_i(w')=\sum_{\substack{t\in T\\a(t,w')=i}}n_t, \qquad M_i(w')=\sum_{\substack{t\in T\\a(t,w')=i}}\frac{n_t}{n} =\frac{N_i(w')}{n}. \)

Because \(n>0\), for every candidate \(i\),

\( M_1(w')\geq M_i(w') \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N_1(w')\geq N_i(w'). \)

The feasible weights are unchanged, so this equivalence also holds after existentially quantifying over \(w'\). This is a polynomial many-one reduction and proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness already for \(m=2\).

The reduction aggregates the listed electorate directly; it never enumerates all \(2^\ell\) possible types.

3. Polynomial-size rational witnesses

We additionally prove membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), accounting for the controller’s ability to choose real weights.

Write

\( d_i(t,w)=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}b_{tik}w_k, \qquad b_{tik}=|t_k-c_{i,k}|\in\{0,1\}. \)

Suppose a real weight vector makes candidate \(1\) win, and let \(s(t)\) be the candidate selected by each type. The exact conditions for this assignment are

\( d_j(t,w)-d_{s(t)}(t,w)>0 \quad\text{for }j<s(t), \)

and

\( d_j(t,w)-d_{s(t)}(t,w)\geq0 \quad\text{for }j>s(t). \)

The first inequalities must be strict because an earlier candidate would otherwise win the distance tie. The second may be weak because \(s(t)\) precedes those competitors.

Introduce a variable \(\varepsilon\) and the closed polytope defined by

\( w_k\geq0,\qquad \sum_{k=1}^{\ell}w_k=1,\qquad 0\leq\varepsilon\leq1, \)

together with

\( d_j(t,w)-d_{s(t)}(t,w)\geq\varepsilon \quad\text{for }j<s(t), \)

and

\( d_j(t,w)-d_{s(t)}(t,w)\geq0 \quad\text{for }j>s(t). \)

The original real witness extends to a point with \(\varepsilon>0\): there are finitely many strict comparisons, so their positive gaps have a positive minimum. If there are no strict comparisons, choose \(\varepsilon=1\).

This polytope is nonempty and compact. Therefore, maximizing \(\varepsilon\) attains a positive optimum at a vertex.

Let \(q=\ell+1\), the number of variables including \(\varepsilon\). Every constraint has integer coefficients in \(\{-1,0,1\}\) and right-hand side in \(\{0,1\}\). At a vertex, there are \(q\) linearly independent active constraint rows. Indeed, otherwise a nonzero direction orthogonal to all active rows would permit sufficiently small perturbations in both directions, contradicting extremality.

These rows form a nonsingular integer system. By Cramer’s rule, every vertex coordinate is a ratio of integer determinants. Hadamard’s inequality bounds the absolute values of both the denominator determinant and the numerator determinants by \(q^{q/2}\), since every row has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt q\). Thus every coordinate has \(O(q\log(q+1))\) bits.

The resulting rational weight vector has polynomial encoding length and induces exactly the assignment \(s\), because its \(\varepsilon\) is positive. It consequently preserves all vote masses and the winning outcome.

4. Verification and conclusion

A verifier guesses such a rational weight vector, checks its simplex constraints, computes all distances exactly, and selects each type’s nearest candidate using the fixed order. It then sums the rational masses and checks \(M_1\geq M_i\) for every \(i\).

These computations take polynomial time. In particular, the product of all input mass denominators has bit length at most their total input bit length, so exact vote-mass comparisons require only polynomial-size integer arithmetic.

Hence the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). Combined with the reduction, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness already with two candidates.
