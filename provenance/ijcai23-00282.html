The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given issues with domains \(D_j\), a fixed common dependency graph \(G\) satisfying \(\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}(G)\le k\) for constant \(k\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete conditional-ballot types \(t\), execute cMES with initial per-unit budget \(m\), mass-weighted payments, largest participating mass as the secondary tie-break, and fixed lexicographic tie-breaking; compute the exact integral outcome \(w\in\prod_j D_j\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Each type \(t\) has rational mass \(\mu_t>0\), a directed dependency graph on binary issues, and for every issue \(I_j\) an approval predicate on the values of \(I_j\) and its in-neighbors. A type is satisfied with respect to \(I_j\) exactly when this predicate holds. Initially every type has budget \(b_t=m\). In each cMES round, consider every currently feasible purchase \((I',x)\) permitted by the dependency graphs; its eligible buyers are the positive-budget types satisfied with respect to its designated issue under \(x\). Choose the minimum \(p\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t\min\{p,b_t\}=|I'|\), charge eligible types \(\min\{p,b_t\}\), and select a minimum-price purchase by maximum eligible mass, then a fixed lexicographic order. Stop when no purchase is feasible and fill remaining issues by that fixed order.

Target. Is computing this cMES purchase sequence and final integral outcome for rational type masses, with every type's maximum indegree at most \(4\), NP-hard or solvable in polynomial time?

Nearest known results. For a finite electorate of unit-mass voters with possibly different dependency graphs, computing the cMES winning outcome is NP-hard even when every voter dependency graph has maximum indegree at most \(4\), under tie-breaking in favor of the largest buyer set. No result for the rational-mass version is stated here.

Theorem

Computing the cMES purchase sequence and final integral outcome is \(\mathrm{NP\text{-}hard}\), even when every type has mass \(1\) and every dependency graph has maximum indegree at most \(4\).

Reduction

We use the stated hardness result for finite electorates of unit-mass voters.

Let \(\mathcal I\) be such a finite-electorate instance, with voter set \(V\) and \(m\) binary issues. Construct a rational-mass instance \(\mathcal I^\ast\) as follows.

For every voter \(v\in V\), introduce one type \(t_v\) with

\[ \mu_{t_v}=1. \]

Copy from \(v\):

  • its directed dependency graph;
  • every issue-approval predicate;
  • its initial budget \(b_{t_v}=m\).

Also copy the fixed lexicographic order on purchases and issue completions. This construction is polynomial-time because every copied object retains its original representation and the rational number \(1\) has constant encoding length. The maximum indegree remains at most \(4\).

Exact preservation of the cMES execution

We prove by induction on the cMES rounds that \(\mathcal I\) and \(\mathcal I^\ast\) have identical partial outcomes and corresponding voters and types have identical remaining budgets.

This holds initially by construction.

Suppose it holds before some round. Consider any currently feasible purchase \(a=(I',x)\). Feasibility is identical because the partial outcomes and dependency graphs are identical. Moreover, voter \(v\) is an eligible buyer of \(a\) in \(\mathcal I\) if and only if type \(t_v\) is eligible in \(\mathcal I^\ast\): their approval predicates and positive-budget status coincide.

Let \(B(a)\subseteq V\) denote this common eligible set, under the correspondence \(v\leftrightarrow t_v\). In the finite instance, the price of \(a\) is the minimum \(p\) satisfying

\[ \sum_{v\in B(a)}\min\{p,b_v\}=|I'|. \]

In the rational-mass instance, its price is the minimum \(p\) satisfying

\[ \sum_{v\in B(a)} \mu_{t_v}\min\{p,b_{t_v}\} = \sum_{v\in B(a)} \min\{p,b_v\} = |I'|, \]

where the middle equality uses \(\mu_{t_v}=1\) and the induction hypothesis \(b_{t_v}=b_v\). Thus every feasible purchase has exactly the same price in both instances.

For each purchase \(a\), its eligible mass in \(\mathcal I^\ast\) is

\[ \sum_{v\in B(a)}\mu_{t_v} = \sum_{v\in B(a)}1 = |B(a)|. \]

Hence maximizing eligible mass in \(\mathcal I^\ast\) is exactly the same as maximizing the number of eligible buyers in \(\mathcal I\). The remaining lexicographic tie-breaking rule is also identical. Therefore both executions select the same purchase.

Each corresponding voter and type is then charged the same amount \(\min\{p,b_v\}\). Their updated budgets remain equal, completing the induction.

Consequently, both executions stop after the same purchase sequence. Since their partial outcomes are then identical and their fixed completion orders are identical, their final integral outcomes are identical as well.

Complexity consequence

The rational-mass problem therefore contains the known unit-mass problem as the special case \(\mu_t=1\) for every type. An algorithm computing the rational-mass cMES sequence and final outcome would, on the constructed instance, compute the winning outcome of the known \(\mathrm{NP\text{-}hard}\) finite-electorate problem.

Thus the target problem is \(\mathrm{NP\text{-}hard}\). This argument does not require expanding binary-encoded rational masses: hardness already holds for constant-encoding unit masses.
