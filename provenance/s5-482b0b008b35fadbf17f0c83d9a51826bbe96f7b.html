Scope and model

The stated asymptotic question is resolved. I interpret it as consensus halving between two recipients, under the cited paper’s online model.

Let \(\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3\) be non-atomic probability measures on \([0,1]\). For an allocation into unions of intervals \(U_1,U_2\), define discrepancy by \(D=\max_{1\leq j\leq3}|\mu_j(U_1)-\mu_j(U_2)|\).

Section 2 specifies reveal points \(0=x_1<\cdots<x_m=1\), with \(m\) polynomial in \(1/\epsilon\). At \(x_i\), the algorithm can query interval masses through \(x_{i+1}\), then irreversibly decide whether to cut at \(x_i\) and allocate the completed interval. Cuts are restricted to reveal points, and \(\mu_j([x_i,x_{i+1}])\leq\epsilon^2/(100\log_2 3)\). [Alon–Graur, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.16613#page=7).

Published bounds and hypothesis checks

Theorem 4.1 gives a deterministic online algorithm achieving discrepancy at most \(\epsilon\), using \(O(n\log n/\epsilon^2)\) cuts. Its proof states polynomial running time in \(n\), \(1/\epsilon\), and input size. Our three measures satisfy its hypotheses; setting \(n=3\) gives \(O(\epsilon^{-2})\) cuts. [Theorem 4.1 and its proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.16613#page=10).

Theorem 5.3 gives an adversarial lower bound of \(\Omega(\epsilon^{-2})\) cuts for every deterministic online algorithm with three measures in this model. [Theorem 5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.16613#page=19).

These are applications of published theorems; their proofs are not reproduced here.

Deduction of the optimum

Let \(\mathcal I_\epsilon\) denote the admissible inputs. For an algorithm \(A\) guaranteeing \(D\leq\epsilon\) on every such input, let \(K_A(I)\) be its cut count. Define
\(C_{\mathrm{det}}(\epsilon)=\inf_A\sup_{I\in\mathcal I_\epsilon}K_A(I)\).

The upper theorem supplies one eligible algorithm and constants \(C,\epsilon_1>0\) such that \(C_{\mathrm{det}}(\epsilon)\leq C\epsilon^{-2}\) whenever \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_1\).

The lower theorem supplies constants \(c,\epsilon_2>0\) such that, for every eligible \(A\), some admissible input forces at least \(c\epsilon^{-2}\) cuts whenever \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_2\). Thus \(C_{\mathrm{det}}(\epsilon)\geq c\epsilon^{-2}\). The adversarial input may depend on \(A\); a single input defeating all algorithms is unnecessary.

Consequently, for \(0<\epsilon<\min\{\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2\}\),
\(c\epsilon^{-2}\leq C_{\mathrm{det}}(\epsilon)\leq C\epsilon^{-2}\).
This proves \(C_{\mathrm{det}}(\epsilon)=\Theta(\epsilon^{-2})\). Restricting the infimum to polynomial-time algorithms gives the same conclusion: the upper-bound algorithm remains eligible, and the lower bound applies to every algorithm in that restricted class.

No open sub-claim remains for this asymptotic target.
