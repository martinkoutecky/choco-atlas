The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), hospitals \(H\), acceptable type sets \(A(h)\), strict doctor-type and hospital priorities, and rational quota functions \(p_h,q_h\) with \(\lvert C(p_h,q_h)\rvert\le4\) for each hospital, decide whether there is a mass assignment \(x_{t,h}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_h x_{t,h}\le\mu_t\) and \(p_h(B)\le\sum_{t\in B}x_{t,h}\le q_h(B)\) for every \(B\subseteq A(h)\), with no justified infinitesimal envy: no \(t,t',h\) has \(x_{t',h}>0\), \(t\succ_h t'\), and either unassigned mass \(\mu_t-\sum_g x_{t,g}>0\) or mass \(x_{t,g}>0\) at a hospital \(g\) with \(h\succ_t g\), while \(x_h+\varepsilon(e_t-e_{t'})\) remains quota-feasible for some sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\); if so, compute \(x\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) doctor types \(T\) with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=|H|\) hospitals. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Acceptability is given by \(A(h)\subseteq T\); put \(A(t)=\{h:t\in A(h)\}\). Each type strictly ranks \(A(t)\), every acceptable hospital is preferred to being unassigned, and each hospital has a strict priority order on \(A(h)\).
Write \(\mu(B)=\sum_{t\in B}\mu_t\). Hospital \(h\) has rational quota functions satisfying \(0\le p_h(B)\le q_h(B)\le\mu(B)\) for every \(B\subseteq A(h)\). Its explicitly listed constrained family is \(C_h=\{B:p_h(B)>0\text{ or }q_h(B)<\mu(B)\}\), with \(|C_h|\le4\); unlisted sets have \(p_h(B)=0\) and \(q_h(B)=\mu(B)\).
A mass assignment has real entries \(x_{t,h}\ge0\), zero on unacceptable pairs, with \(\sum_hx_{t,h}\le\mu_t\) and \(p_h(B)\le\sum_{t\in B}x_{t,h}\le q_h(B)\) for every hospital and subset. Types may split their mass among hospitals and unemployment.
Define \(r_{t,h}=\mu_t-\sum_gx_{t,g}+\sum_{g\in A(t):h\succ_t g}x_{t,g}\). Justified infinitesimal envy exists if some \(h\) and \(t,t'\in A(h)\) satisfy \(r_{t,h}>0\), \(x_{t',h}>0\), and \(t\succ_h t'\), and there is \(0<\delta\le\min\{r_{t,h},x_{t',h}\}\) such that, for every \(0<\varepsilon\le\delta\), replacing \(x_{t,h}\) by \(x_{t,h}+\varepsilon\) and \(x_{t',h}\) by \(x_{t',h}-\varepsilon\) preserves all quotas at \(h\). This test concerns only the receiving hospital's quotas.

Is deciding whether a feasible mass assignment without justified infinitesimal envy exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? In the polynomial-time case, can such an assignment also be computed exactly in polynomial time?

Discrete existence is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even with at most four constrained subsets per hospital; paramodular discrete quota pairs admit a polynomial-time existence algorithm. [Yokoi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.04888)
No result settling this exact mass problem was identified in the literature search.

  • Replaced the discrete quota ceiling given by subset cardinality with the available mass of that subset; nontrivial upper constraints and the four-set restriction use this ceiling.

  • Required zero assignment on unacceptable pairs and strict orders on acceptable partners, with unemployment below every acceptable hospital.

Classification

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even with dyadic rational masses and at most four constrained subsets per hospital. Thus allowing mass splitting does not yield a polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Every yes-instance has an exact rational solution of polynomial encoding length. Below, I prove the classification and give an exact exponential-time construction algorithm.

1. Characterizing infinitesimal envy

For a feasible assignment, write \(s_h(B)=\sum_{t\in B}x_{t,h}\). Rearranging the definition of residual mass gives

\(r_{t,h}=\mu_t-x_{t,h}-\sum_{g\in A(t):\,g\succ_t h}x_{t,g}\).

Consequently, \(0\le r_{t,h}\le\mu_t-x_{t,h}\).

Fix types \(t\succ_h t'\) with \(r_{t,h}>0\) and \(x_{t',h}>0\). Increasing \(x_{t,h}\) and decreasing \(x_{t',h}\) by the same amount changes \(s_h(B)\) as follows:

  • It increases when \(t\in B\) and \(t'\notin B\).
  • It decreases when \(t'\in B\) and \(t\notin B\).
  • It remains unchanged otherwise.

Therefore, an infinitesimal swap is blocked precisely when some listed set satisfies either

\(t\in B,\quad t'\notin B,\quad s_h(B)=q_h(B)\),

or

\(t'\in B,\quad t\notin B,\quad s_h(B)=p_h(B)\).

To justify sufficiency of this characterization, suppose no such binding constraint exists. Every affected listed upper or lower bound then has positive slack. Choose \(\delta>0\) no larger than all those slacks, \(r_{t,h}\), and \(x_{t',h}\). Every swap of size \(0<\varepsilon\le\delta\) respects the listed quotas.

It also respects the unlisted quotas: the receiving coordinate remains at most \(\mu_t\), the departing coordinate remains nonnegative, and all other coordinates remain between zero and their respective masses. Thus every subset sum remains between zero and its total mass.

Hence absence of justified infinitesimal envy is equivalent to requiring, for each priority-ordered pair, at least one of the following equalities:

\(r_{t,h}=0\), or \(x_{t',h}=0\), or a separating upper quota is binding, or a separating lower quota is binding.

There are at most six alternatives per pair, since \(|C_h|\le4\).

2. A gadget that forces a Boolean choice

I first describe masses in units of a positive scaling factor \(\lambda\).

For one variable, introduce internal types \(a,b,c,d\), each of mass \(\lambda\), and literal types \(e,f\), each of mass \(2\lambda\). Introduce hospitals \(U,V\).

The internal types accept only \(U,V\), with preference \(U\succ V\). At \(U\), use priority

\(a\succ_U b\succ_U c\succ_U d\succ_U e\succ_U f\).

Literal types will accept \(U\) and their associated clause hospitals, always preferring \(U\).

Hospital \(U\) accepts all six types and has exactly these three constrained sets:

| Set | Lower and upper quota |
|---|---|
| \(\{a,b,e\}\) | \(2\lambda\) |
| \(\{c,d,f\}\) | \(2\lambda\) |
| \(\{e,f\}\) | \(2\lambda\) |

Hospital \(V\) accepts only the internal types and has these four constrained sets:

| Set | Lower and upper quota |
|---|---|
| \(\{a,c\}\) | \(\lambda\) |
| \(\{a,d\}\) | \(\lambda\) |
| \(\{b,c\}\) | \(\lambda\) |
| \(\{b,d\}\) | \(\lambda\) |

Every displayed quota is an equality quota. All other quotas are trivial. These bounds satisfy the required inequalities: each set at \(U\) has mass \(4\lambda\), and each set at \(V\) has mass \(2\lambda\).

2.1. Every feasible assignment has one scalar parameter

The equalities at \(V\) imply, for some \(y\in[0,1]\),

\(x_{a,V}=x_{b,V}=\lambda y,\qquad x_{c,V}=x_{d,V}=\lambda(1-y)\).

Thus the total internal mass assigned to \(V\) is \(2\lambda\).

Adding the first two equalities at \(U\) and subtracting the third gives

\(x_{a,U}+x_{b,U}+x_{c,U}+x_{d,U}=2\lambda\).

The internal types therefore have total assigned mass \(4\lambda\), equal to their combined mass. Since each individual assignment is bounded by its mass, every internal type is fully assigned.

Put \(z=1-y\). Full assignment and the equalities at \(U\) now give

\(x_{a,U}=x_{b,U}=\lambda z,\qquad x_{c,U}=x_{d,U}=\lambda(1-z)\),

and

\(x_{e,U}=2\lambda(1-z),\qquad x_{f,U}=2\lambda z\).

2.2. Envy-freeness forces an endpoint

Suppose \(0<z<1\). Because \(U\) is \(a\)'s first choice,

\(r_{a,U}=\lambda-x_{a,U}=\lambda(1-z)>0\).

Also \(x_{b,U}=\lambda z>0\), and \(a\succ_U b\).

Types \(a,b\) belong to exactly the same constrained sets at \(U\). Therefore, increasing \(x_{a,U}\) and decreasing \(x_{b,U}\) equally preserves every listed quota there. Taking

\(\delta=\lambda\min\{z,1-z\}>0\)

also preserves all unlisted quotas, by Section 1. This is justified infinitesimal envy.

As specified in the question, this test checks only the receiving hospital \(U\)'s quotas.

Consequently, every envy-free assignment satisfies \(z\in\{0,1\}\).

2.3. Both endpoints avoid envy inside the gadget

Membership in the three equality sets at \(U\) gives these signatures:

| Type | Signature |
|---|---|
| \(a,b\) | \((1,0,0)\) |
| \(c,d\) | \((0,1,0)\) |
| \(e\) | \((1,0,1)\) |
| \(f\) | \((0,1,1)\) |

A swap between different signatures changes an equality-constrained sum, so it is impossible.

The only pairs with identical signatures are \(a,b\) and \(c,d\). At either endpoint, each such pair is either entirely absent from \(U\), or both types are fully assigned to their first choice \(U\). In the first case there is no departing positive mass; in the second there is no residual mass.

At \(V\), the signatures in the four equality sets are

\(a:(1,1,0,0),\quad b:(0,0,1,1),\quad c:(1,0,1,0),\quad d:(0,1,0,1)\).

They are pairwise distinct. Thus every swap at \(V\) violates an equality quota, regardless of its priority order.

This proves that both Boolean endpoints avoid justified envy at the variable hospitals.

3. Reduction from bounded-occurrence satisfiability

Use the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem \((3,\mathrm{B}2)\)-SAT: every clause contains three literals, and every variable occurs twice positively and twice negatively. This is the source problem used in [Yokoi’s Theorem 3.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.04888). The endpoint-forcing gadget above is the additional ingredient needed for the mass problem.

Let the formula have variables \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\) and clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_s\), with \(n\ge1\).

Choose

\(S=2^{\lceil\log_2(8n)\rceil},\qquad \lambda=S^{-1}\).

For each variable, create the gadget from Section 2, with indexed types and hospitals. The combined mass of these gadgets is \(8n\lambda\le1\). If this is less than one, add a type of mass \(1-8n\lambda\) that accepts no hospital.

All masses are positive dyadic rationals.

Interpret \(e_i\) as the positive literal \(v_i\), and \(f_i\) as the negative literal \(\neg v_i\).

For each clause \(C_j\), create hospital \(W_j\), accepting exactly its literal types. Each literal type accepts its variable hospital \(U_i\) and the clause hospitals containing that literal. It ranks \(U_i\) first and orders its clause hospitals strictly but arbitrarily.

At \(W_j\), impose:

  • Total lower quota \(\lambda\).
  • Upper quota \(\lambda\) on each acceptable singleton.

All remaining bounds are trivial. If the acceptable set is itself a singleton, combine the prescriptions into the equality quota \(\lambda\).

Thus a clause hospital has at most four constrained subsets. Its total lower quota is valid because it accepts at least one type of mass \(2\lambda\). Its priority order can be arbitrary.

The resulting instance has \(m=2n+s\) and \(\tau\le6n+1\). Every quota and mass has \(O(\log n)\) bits, and the entire reduction has polynomial encoding length and construction time.

3.1. Soundness

Suppose the constructed instance has a feasible envy-free assignment.

Section 2 forces each variable parameter \(z_i\) to lie in \(\{0,1\}\). Set \(v_i\) true exactly when \(z_i=1\).

The literal allocations at \(U_i\) are

\(x_{e_i,U_i}=2\lambda(1-z_i),\qquad x_{f_i,U_i}=2\lambda z_i\).

If \(v_i\) is false, \(e_i\) is fully assigned to \(U_i\), so it contributes no mass to any clause hospital. If \(v_i\) is true, the same statement holds for \(f_i\).

Every clause hospital receives at least \(\lambda>0\). Therefore, it receives positive mass from some acceptable literal type. That literal cannot be false, since false literal types are fully assigned to their variable hospitals.

Every clause is consequently satisfied.

3.2. Completeness

Conversely, suppose the formula has a satisfying Boolean assignment.

Choose the corresponding endpoint \(z_i\) for each variable and assign its internal and literal masses at \(U_i,V_i\) as in Section 2.

For every true literal type, assign mass \(\lambda\) to each distinct clause hospital containing that literal. This uses at most \(2\lambda\), because the literal occurs only twice. Leave any remaining mass unassigned.

False literal types already have their entire mass \(2\lambda\) at their variable hospital.

This assignment is feasible:

  • All variable equality quotas hold.
  • No type exceeds its mass.
  • Every clause has a true literal and hence receives at least \(\lambda\).
  • Every clause singleton receives at most \(\lambda\).

Section 2.3 proves absence of envy at variable hospitals.

At a clause hospital, a false literal type has zero residual mass: it is fully assigned to its first-choice variable hospital.

Every acceptable true literal type receives exactly \(\lambda\) at that clause hospital. Its singleton upper quota is therefore binding. Any proposed swap increasing its assignment violates that upper quota.

There is consequently no justified infinitesimal envy at any clause hospital either.

This proves the equivalence between satisfiability and existence of an envy-free mass assignment, establishing \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

4. Membership in NP and exact rational witnesses

Let \(d\le m\tau\) be the number of acceptable pairs. Feasibility is a rational linear system consisting of:

  • \(d\) nonnegativity inequalities;
  • \(\tau\) type-capacity inequalities;
  • at most \(8m\) listed quota inequalities.

Unlisted quotas follow from the type capacities and nonnegativity.

For every priority-ordered pair, Section 1 expresses envy-freeness as a disjunction of at most six linear equalities. A certificate selects one equality for each pair. There are at most

\(K=\sum_h\binom{|A(h)|}{2}\le m\tau(\tau-1)/2\)

such pairs.

The verifier adds the selected equalities to the feasibility system and tests rational linear feasibility in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

If an envy-free real assignment exists, choose, for each pair, an equality that it satisfies. The resulting linear system is nonempty.

Conversely, every solution of such a system is feasible and satisfies one blocking alternative for every pair, so it is envy-free.

This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

There is also a polynomial-size rational assignment certificate. The selected system defines a nonempty bounded rational polytope, so it has a vertex. All coefficient entries are in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and its right-hand sides are input masses or quota values.

Choose a common denominator \(D\) for these values; its bit length is \(O(L)\). At a vertex, select \(d\) linearly independent tight equations. Hadamard’s determinant bound and Cramer’s rule show that each coordinate has numerator and denominator bit length

\(O\!\left(L+d\log(d+1)\right)\).

Thus permitting real assignments introduces no obstruction to exact rational certification.

5. Running time and conclusion

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete with the stipulated limit of four constrained subsets per hospital.

An unconditional exact algorithm enumerates the blocking alternatives and solves the corresponding rational linear systems. Its running time is

\(6^{\,m\tau(\tau-1)/2}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\).

On a yes-instance it computes an exact rational envy-free assignment of polynomial encoding length.

A polynomial-time algorithm deciding existence, and therefore an algorithm deciding existence while constructing an exact assignment, would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
