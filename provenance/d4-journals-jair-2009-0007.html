The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite task catalogue \(D\), complete requester and performer types \(\mathcal Z\) with rational masses \(\mu_z\), and a fixed trust rule aggregating type-level EQOS reports over those masses, define feasible patterns \(a\) that assign one requester unit to a valuation and cover its tasks using performer units, each selecting at most one XOR cost bid. Let \(r(a)\) be the requester type, \(q_z(a)\) the number of performer units of type \(z\) used, and \(w_a\) the pattern's expected welfare under the trust rule. Find a rational mass assignment \(x_a\geq 0\) maximizing \(\sum_a w_a x_a\), subject to \(\sum_{a:r(a)=z}x_a\leq\mu_z\) and \(\sum_a q_z(a)x_a\leq\mu_z\) for each \(z\). Determine the complexity in the task catalogue size \(M\), support size \(\tau=|\mathcal Z|\), and rational input bit length \(L\), including whether the implicit pattern LP admits efficient pricing.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(D\) be a task catalogue with \(M\geq1\). A society is given by its support: the list \(\mathcal Z\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_z\), normalized by \(\sum_z\mu_z=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Each type \(z\) explicitly lists XOR requester valuations \(v_z(S)>0\) and XOR performer bids \((B,c_z(B))\), for nonempty subsets of \(D\), with nonnegative rational costs. Unlisted valuations, including \(v_z(\varnothing)\), are zero; unlisted cost bids are unavailable. A unit may select at most one valuation and, independently, at most one cost bid.
The input also lists rational EQOS reports \(\eta_h(r,z,t)\in[0,1]\): reporter type \(h\)'s success estimate for performer type \(z\) doing task \(t\) for requester type \(r\). Fix the mass-weighted trust rule \(p_{rzt}=\sum_h\mu_h\eta_h(r,z,t)\), and assume independent task successes.
A pattern \(a\) selects one requester unit of type \(r(a)\), one of its listed bundles \(S_a\), and performer units indexed by \(j\), of types \(z_j\). The nonempty sets \(T_j\) partition \(S_a\); unit \(j\) selects one listed bid \(B_j\supseteq T_j\), paying its full cost. Surplus tasks in \(B_j\) may be discarded. Multiple performer units may have the same type. Put \(q_z(a)=|\{j:z_j=z\}|\), and let \(\sigma_a(t)\) be the type assigned task \(t\).
The pattern welfare is \(w_a=\sum_{U\subseteq S_a}v_{r(a)}(U)\prod_{t\in U}p_{r(a),\sigma_a(t),t}\prod_{t\in S_a\setminus U}(1-p_{r(a),\sigma_a(t),t})-\sum_j c_{z_j}(B_j)\); costs are incurred irrespective of success.
Optimize \(\max_{x\geq0}\sum_a w_ax_a\), subject to \(\sum_{a:r(a)=z}x_a\leq\mu_z\) and \(\sum_a q_z(a)x_a\leq\mu_z\) for every \(z\), allowing unused mass. Patterns are implicit. Pricing, for nonnegative rational dual prices \(u_z,d_z\), asks whether \(\max_a\{w_a-u_{r(a)}-\sum_z d_zq_z(a)\}>0\).
Is this continuous welfare optimization problem solvable in polynomial time in \(M,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard? Is its pricing problem polynomial-time solvable or NP-hard, including the bit length of the supplied prices?
The discrete GTBM allocation decision problem is NP-complete, and its welfare optimization is NP-hard ([Ramchurn et al., 2009](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/267288/1/ramchurn09a.pdf)).
No result settling exactly this mass-pattern formulation was located in the literature search.

  • Instantiated the unspecified fixed trust rule as the mass-weighted mean of EQOS reports, following the weighted aggregation model.
  • Used independent task successes and valuation of the exact successfully completed subset, following the computational allocation model.
  • Allowed free disposal of surplus tasks in a selected cost bid while charging its full cost, following the default inequality in the integer programming formulation.
  • Made requester and performer capacities independent, allowed repeated performer types through distinct units, and allowed unused mass, as prescribed by the graded mass constraints.

Result

The continuous welfare optimization problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Its pricing decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, including the bit length of the supplied prices.

Both hardness results hold already with \(\tau=1\), mass \(1\), deterministic success, one listed requester valuation, and all performer bids having cost \(1\).

1. Reduction from Set Cover

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete Set Cover problem: given a nonempty universe \(D\), explicitly listed nonempty subsets \(B_1,\ldots,B_n\), and a nonnegative integer \(K\), decide whether at most \(K\) of these sets cover \(D\). See [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

We may restrict to instances satisfying \(\bigcup_i B_i=D\). Indeed, empty sets can be deleted, and an uncovered element certifies a no-instance, which can be mapped to the fixed coverable no-instance consisting of one element, its singleton set, and budget \(0\). An empty universe can similarly be mapped to a fixed yes-instance.

Let \(M=|D|\), and let \(\kappa\) denote the minimum number of sets covering \(D\). Construct the following society:

  • There is one type \(z\), with \(\mu_z=1\).
  • Its only listed requester valuation is \(v_z(D)=V\), where \(V=K+\frac12\).
  • Its performer bids are \((B_i,1)\), one for each listed set.
  • For every task \(t\), set \(\eta_z(z,z,t)=1\).

Thus \(p_{zzt}=1\) for every task. These are valid EQOS reports under the specified mass-weighted trust rule, and deterministic successes satisfy the independence assumption.

The construction has polynomial size. In particular, the numerator of \(V=(2K+1)/2\) has bit length linear in the bit length of \(K\).

Every pattern requests \(D\). If it uses \(q\) performer units, all tasks succeed and its welfare is

\[ w_a=V-q. \]

Here and below, each performer unit selects just one bid; different units of the same type may select different bids, as expressly permitted in the question.

2. Patterns encode minimum set covers

Lemma. The minimum number of performer units in a pattern is exactly \(\kappa\).

Proof. Suppose a pattern uses bids \(B_{i_1},\ldots,B_{i_q}\), assigned respective nonempty sets \(T_1,\ldots,T_q\). Since these assigned sets partition \(D\) and \(T_j\subseteq B_{i_j}\), we have

\[ D=\bigcup_{j=1}^{q}T_j\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{q}B_{i_j}. \]

Consequently, its selected bids cover \(D\). Removing repetitions gives a cover using at most \(q\) listed sets, so \(q\geq\kappa\).

Conversely, choose a minimum cover \(B_{i_1},\ldots,B_{i_\kappa}\). Each selected set has a private element: for every \(j\), there is some

\[ t_j\in B_{i_j}\setminus\bigcup_{\ell\ne j}B_{i_\ell}. \]

Otherwise that set could be deleted while preserving coverage, contradicting minimality. These private elements are distinct.

Assign \(t_j\) to performer unit \(j\), and assign each remaining task to any selected set containing it. This produces a partition into nonempty sets \(T_j\subseteq B_{i_j}\), hence a valid pattern with exactly \(\kappa\) performer units. Surplus bid tasks are discarded, as allowed. \(\square\)

3. Pricing is hard even at zero prices

Supply the nonnegative prices \(u_z=d_z=0\). By the lemma,

\[ \max_a\left\{w_a-u_{r(a)}-\sum_zd_zq_z(a)\right\} =V-\kappa. \]

Because \(\kappa\) and \(K\) are integers,

\[ V-\kappa>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K+\frac12-\kappa>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \kappa\leq K. \]

Thus the pricing decision answers the Set Cover instance. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness even when all supplied prices are zero and therefore have constant bit length.

4. Exact optimum of the continuous program

We now analyze the continuous program directly.

There is only one type, so its two capacity constraints are

\[ \sum_a x_a\leq1, \qquad \sum_a q(a)x_a\leq1, \]

where \(q(a)\) is the number of performer units in pattern \(a\). Every pattern has \(q(a)\geq\kappa\geq1\).

Define

\[ R=\max\left\{0,\frac{V}{\kappa}-1\right\}. \]

For every pattern, since \(V>0\) and \(q(a)\geq\kappa\),

\[ \frac{w_a}{q(a)} =\frac{V}{q(a)}-1 \leq\frac{V}{\kappa}-1 \leq R. \]

Therefore every feasible allocation satisfies

\[ \sum_a w_ax_a \leq R\sum_a q(a)x_a \leq R. \]

This upper bound also controls every mixture of patterns.

If \(V>\kappa\), use the pattern with \(\kappa\) performer units supplied by the lemma, and give it mass \(x_a=1/\kappa\). Its requester usage is \(1/\kappa\leq1\), and its performer usage is \(\kappa(1/\kappa)=1\). Its welfare is

\[ (V-\kappa)\frac1\kappa =\frac{V}{\kappa}-1 =R. \]

If \(V\leq\kappa\), all pattern welfares are nonpositive, and the zero allocation attains \(R=0\).

Hence the exact continuous optimum is

\[ \operatorname{OPT} =\max\left\{0,\frac{K+\frac12}{\kappa}-1\right\}. \]

In particular,

\[ \operatorname{OPT}>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \kappa\leq K. \]

Thus even deciding whether the continuous optimum is positive is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Exact welfare optimization is consequently \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

5. Pricing belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Consider an arbitrary instance, and let \(H\) be the total bit length of the supplied prices.

A certificate for a yes-instance is a pattern. Its performer units receive nonempty disjoint sets, so it has at most \(|S_a|\leq M\) performer units. The certificate specifies the requester and valuation indices, each performer’s type and bid index, and the assigned task sets. Its length is polynomial in \(M,\tau,L\).

The verifier checks that the requester valuation and all bids are listed, that the assigned sets partition the requested bundle, and that each assigned set is contained in its selected bid.

Let \(\mathcal V_r\) denote the explicitly listed valuation bundles of requester type \(r\). Since unlisted valuations vanish, the expected value can be evaluated as

\[ \sum_{\substack{U\in\mathcal V_{r(a)}\\U\subseteq S_a}} v_{r(a)}(U) \prod_{t\in U}p_{r(a),\sigma_a(t),t} \prod_{t\in S_a\setminus U} \left(1-p_{r(a),\sigma_a(t),t}\right). \]

This enumerates only explicitly listed valuations. Each probability is a sum of \(\tau\) rational products, and each summand in the displayed expression has at most \(M\) probability factors.

All arithmetic is exact and has polynomial bit complexity. Products have numerator and denominator bit lengths bounded by the sums of the corresponding input bit lengths; sums of polynomially many rationals likewise admit polynomial bit-length bounds using products of their denominators. Subtracting the costs and supplied prices preserves these bounds. The verifier can therefore check strict positivity of the reduced welfare in time polynomial in \(M,\tau,L,H\).

Pricing is in \(\mathrm{NP}\), and Section 3 proves its \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness. Hence it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

The reduction and the exact optimum calculation settle the specified mass-pattern formulation. Neither problem admits the requested polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
