The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of voter types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), strict score vectors, a positive-mass seed cohort sending one positive article on \(c_2\), a rational baseline contact-density matrix \(Q^0\), and an eligibility mask, choose a rational matrix \(Q\) with \(Q_{tu}\in[Q^0_{tu},1]\) on eligible pairs and \(Q_{tu}=Q^0_{tu}\) elsewhere. Interpret \(Q_{tu}\) as the probability that each possible directed contact from type \(t\) to type \(u\) is present, with certain transmission. In the dense-population limit, a type receives the article exactly when reachable from seed mass along type pairs with positive density. Maximize the gain in \(c_0\)’s vote margin over \(Q^0\), with complexity measured in \(|T|\) and the input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. There are \(m\geq3\) candidates \(C=\{c_0,\ldots,c_{m-1}\}\). Each type has pairwise distinct nonnegative integer scores \(\pi_t(c)\), encoded in binary.
A nonempty seed cohort \(S\subseteq T\) broadcasts one common positive article about \(c_2\). Let \(Q^0\in([0,1]\cap\mathbb Q)^{T\times T}\) and an eligibility mask \(A\subseteq T\times T\) be given. Choose rational \(Q\) with \(Q^0_{tu}\leq Q_{tu}\leq1\) on \(A\), and \(Q_{tu}=Q^0_{tu}\) elsewhere; there is no budget or cost constraint.
The entry \(Q_{tu}\) is a directed contact density with certain transmission. In the dense-population model, precisely the types reachable from \(S\) along pairs with \(Q_{tu}>0\) receive the article, including seeds via paths of length zero. Each receiving type increases its score for \(c_2\) by exactly \(1\), once regardless of paths or seed mass; all other scores stay fixed. Plurality votes follow the resulting scores, with ties favoring the tied candidate with lowest initial score.
Let \(v_i(Q)\) be the total mass voting for \(c_i\), \(M(Q)=v_0(Q)-\max_{1\leq i<m}v_i(Q)\), and \(\Delta(Q)=M(Q)-M(Q^0)\). Define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_Q\Delta(Q)\) and a multiplicative \(\rho\)-approximation by \(\Delta(Q)\geq\rho\,\mathrm{OPT}\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Does this problem admit a polynomial-time multiplicative approximation with any guaranteed factor \(0<\rho(m,\tau,L)\leq1\), or is every such positive-factor approximation NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

For finite voters, edge addition admits no polynomial-time positive-factor approximation unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\), even with three candidates, consecutive integer scores, one seed, certain transmission, restricted eligible edges, and unlimited budget. Result

Every strictly positive multiplicative approximation is NP-hard, already for \(m=3\). Consequently, unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\), there is no algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) that guarantees any factor \(0<\rho(m,\tau,L)\leq1\).

We prove this directly for the weighted support representation. The [cited finite-voter paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.06198v2) provides related background; its hardness theorem is not needed for the reduction.

More precisely, deciding whether \(\mathrm{OPT}>0\) is NP-complete. An exact algorithm runs in \(O\!\left(2^\tau(\tau^2+m\tau)L^2\right)\) bit operations.

1. Reduction from positive-integer subset sum

We use the standard NP-complete problem: given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n,B\), encoded in binary, decide whether some \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{j\in I}a_j=B\).

Set

\(\displaystyle W=\sum_{j=1}^n a_j,\qquad H=4W+8B+2.\)

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two strictly greater than \(H\). Construct \(m=3\) candidates and \(\tau=n+5\) types, with masses and initial scores as follows.

| Type | Mass | Base scores \((c_0,c_1,c_2)\) |
|---|---|---|
| Seed \(s\) | \(1/N\) | \((0,1,2)\) |
| Gateway \(g\) | \((2B-1)/N\) | \((2,0,1)\) |
| Item \(x_j\), for each \(j\) | \(2a_j/N\) | \((0,2,1)\) |
| Ballast \(b_1\) | \((6B+1)/N\) | \((0,2,1)\) |
| Ballast \(b_2\) | \((2W+1)/N\) | \((0,1,2)\) |
| Ballast \(b_0\) | \((N-H)/N\) | \((2,0,1)\) |

All masses are positive dyadic rationals. Their numerators sum to

\(\displaystyle 1+(2B-1)+2W+(6B+1)+(2W+1)+(N-H)=N,\)

so the masses sum to \(1\).

To ensure that every listed type has a distinct score vector, enumerate the types by distinct integers \(r_t\in\{0,\ldots,\tau-1\}\) and add \(3r_t\) to all three base scores of type \(t\). This preserves all voting behavior. Every type has three consecutive, distinct, nonnegative integer scores.

Take

\(\displaystyle S=\{s\},\qquad Q^0_{tu}=0\ \text{for every pair},\)

and permit exactly the contacts

\(\displaystyle A=\{(s,g)\}\cup\{(g,x_j):1\leq j\leq n\}.\)

Thus the eligible graph is acyclic, every seed-to-type path has length at most two, and all ballast types are unreachable.

Because transmission depends only on positivity, an eligible contact is effectively either inactive or active. Every choice of active eligible contacts is realizable by setting their densities to \(1\) and all other densities to \(0\).

2. Exactly which votes change

Initially, the gateway and \(b_0\) vote for \(c_0\); the items and \(b_1\) vote for \(c_1\); and the seed and \(b_2\) vote for \(c_2\).

The seed receives the article even at \(Q^0\), but continues voting for \(c_2\). Therefore, writing \(v_i^0=v_i(Q^0)\),

\(\displaystyle v_0^0=\frac{2B-1+N-H}{N},\qquad v_1^0=\frac{2W+6B+1}{N},\qquad v_2^0=\frac{2W+2}{N}. \)

In particular,

\(\displaystyle v_1^0-v_2^0=\frac{6B-1}{N}>0,\qquad M(Q^0)=v_0^0-v_1^0.\)

A receiving gateway has its \(c_2\) score increased from the middle score to the top score. The resulting tie favors \(c_2\), whose initial score was lower than \(c_0\)'s. Hence the gateway switches from \(c_0\) to \(c_2\). Each receiving item similarly switches from \(c_1\) to \(c_2\).

If \(Q_{sg}=0\), only the seed receives the article, regardless of the item-contact densities. Consequently, \(\Delta(Q)=0\).

If \(Q_{sg}>0\), the gateway receives the article, and precisely the items indexed by

\(\displaystyle I=\{j:Q_{g x_j}>0\}\)

receive it. Every subset \(I\) is possible. Put

\(\displaystyle k=\sum_{j\in I}a_j,\qquad y=\frac{2B-1}{N},\qquad x=\frac{2k}{N}.\)

The resulting vote masses are

\(\displaystyle v_0=v_0^0-y,\qquad v_1=v_1^0-x,\qquad v_2=v_2^0+y+x.\)

Using \(M=\min\{v_0-v_1,v_0-v_2\}\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle \Delta(Q) =\min\left\{x-y,\ (v_1^0-v_2^0)-2y-x\right\}. \)

Substituting the masses gives the exact identity

\(\displaystyle \Delta(Q) =\frac1N\min\{2(k-B)+1,\ 2(B-k)+1\} =\frac{1-2|k-B|}{N}. \)

Since \(k\) and \(B\) are integers:

  • If \(k=B\), then \(\Delta(Q)=1/N\).
  • If \(k\neq B\), then \(\Delta(Q)\leq-1/N\).

Together with the always-feasible choice \(Q=Q^0\), this proves

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{OPT}= \begin{cases} 1/N,&\text{if the subset-sum instance is a yes-instance},\\ 0,&\text{if it is a no-instance}. \end{cases} \)

3. Consequence for every positive factor

Suppose a polynomial-time algorithm returns feasible \(Q\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \Delta(Q)\geq\rho(m,\tau,L)\,\mathrm{OPT},\qquad \rho(m,\tau,L)>0.\)

Run it on the constructed instance and evaluate its output exactly.

On a yes-instance, the guarantee forces \(\Delta(Q)>0\). On a no-instance, no feasible output has positive improvement. Thus testing whether the returned improvement is positive decides subset sum.

This argument needs neither a lower bound on \(\rho\) nor an algorithm for evaluating \(\rho\). Its strict positivity alone suffices.

The reduction has polynomial binary length: \(N\leq2H\), so every mass has \(O(\log(W+B))\) bits, and every score has \(O(\log\tau)\) bits. With a dense encoding of \(Q^0\), the constructed input length is

\(\displaystyle L=O\!\left(n^2+n\log(W+B)+n\log(n+5)\right).\)

There are only \(n+5\) listed types; no expansion into \(N\) finite voters occurs. The reduction therefore establishes hardness in the requested parameters \(m,\tau,L\).

4. Finite certificates and exact running time

Define the forced and potentially available contact sets by

\(\displaystyle E_0=\{(t,u):Q^0_{tu}>0\},\qquad E_+=E_0\cup A.\)

Any feasible density matrix has the same receiving types as a canonical matrix whose eligible entries are each either their baseline value or \(1\): retain the baseline value for inactive contacts and set active eligible contacts to \(1\). This preserves every contact's positivity.

Hence the objective takes only finitely many values, and its maximum is attained. A choice of active eligible contacts provides a polynomial-size certificate. Reachability, voting, and the strict comparison \(\Delta(Q)>0\) are computable using exact rational arithmetic in \(O((\tau^2+m\tau)L^2)\) bit operations. Combined with the reduction, this proves NP-completeness of deciding \(\mathrm{OPT}>0\).

For an exact algorithm, enumerate all \(R\subseteq T\). A set \(R\) is realizable as the receiving set if and only if:

  • \(S\subseteq R\).
  • No edge of \(E_0\) leaves \(R\).
  • Every vertex of \(R\) is reachable from \(S\) in the graph \((R,E_+\cap(R\times R))\).

Necessity follows directly from reachability: receiving sets contain the seeds, are closed under forced contacts, and contain every vertex on a witnessing path.

For sufficiency, set every eligible contact within \(R\) to \(1\) and leave every other contact at baseline. Condition 3 makes every vertex of \(R\) reachable. Condition 2 ensures that no positive contact leaves \(R\): an eligible contact leaving \(R\) remains at baseline and must have baseline zero. Thus precisely \(R\) receives the article.

Each set can be checked and evaluated in \(O((\tau^2+m\tau)L^2)\) bit operations. Taking the best realizable set gives the stated exact running time

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(2^\tau(\tau^2+m\tau)L^2\right).\)
