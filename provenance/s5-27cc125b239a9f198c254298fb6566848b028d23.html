The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) good categories, \(\tau\) complete valuation types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), and normalized monotone \(a\)-submodular valuation oracles \(v_t\), define feasible allocations by nonnegative \(y_{t,D}\) with \(\sum_D y_{t,D}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,D:j\in D}y_{t,D}\le b_j\), where every agent receiving bundle \(D\) gets an integral bundle. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}\) be the maximum welfare over these allocations. Can an algorithm, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and with polynomially many value queries, output a polynomial-support allocation \(y\) and common nonnegative prices \(p\) such that unused supply has price zero, every positive-mass state \((t,D)\) satisfies \(v_t(D)\ge\sum_{j\in D}p_j\), every \(A\cap D=\varnothing\) satisfies \(\frac{1}{a}v_t(A\mid D)\le\sum_{j\in A}p_j\), and the welfare of \(y\) is at least \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}/(1+a)\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(X=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(m\ge1\). A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) complete valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Input also includes binary-rational supplies \(b_j\ge0\) and a binary-rational parameter \(a\ge1\). Each type has a value oracle \(v_t:2^X\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), normalized and monotone. Write \(v_t(A\mid D)=v_t(A\cup D)-v_t(D)\).
The promised meaning of \(a\)-submodularity is: for disjoint \(W,A\subseteq X\) and \(x\in X\setminus(W\cup A)\), \(v_t(A\cup\{x\}\mid W)\le v_t(A\mid W)+a\,v_t(\{x\}\mid W)\). Let \(L\) bound the total numerical input bit length and the bit length of every oracle answer.
An allocation consists of masses \(y_{t,D}\ge0\), indexed by \(D\subseteq X\), with \(\sum_Dy_{t,D}=\mu_t\) and \(u_j:=\sum_{t,D:j\in D}y_{t,D}\le b_j\). Each recipient gets the integral bundle \(D\), with at most one unit of each category; the empty bundle is allowed.
Define \(W(y)=\sum_{t,D}y_{t,D}v_t(D)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}=\max_y W(y)\) over feasible allocations.
An admissible output is a feasible rational allocation \(y\) and common rational prices \(p_j\ge0\), satisfying \(u_j<b_j\Rightarrow p_j=0\), and, for every \(y_{t,D}>0\), \(v_t(D)\ge\sum_{j\in D}p_j\) and \(a^{-1}v_t(A\mid D)\le\sum_{j\in A}p_j\) for every \(A\subseteq X\setminus D\).

Can an algorithm, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and with polynomially many value queries, always output an admissible pair \((y,p)\) with polynomially many positive entries of \(y\) and \(W(y)\ge\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}/(1+a)\)?

For finitely many agents and one indivisible object per category, polynomial-time greedy allocation with suitable prices achieves these individual-rationality and outward-stability conditions and a \((1+a)\)-approximation even against the fractional welfare optimum. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.00304v4).
No result settling this exact mass-society problem with common prices and value-oracle access was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the society to total mass one, interpreting supplies and welfare per capita.
  • Specified nonnegative rational supplies, a rational input parameter a at least one, and positive support masses.
  • Used the source definition of a-submodularity and explicitly defined marginal valuation.
  • Specified nonnegative rational oracle values and bounded oracle-answer bit lengths through L to make bit complexity meaningful.
  • Specified that bundles are subsets of categories, allow the empty bundle, and contain at most one unit of each category.

Result

Yes. There is an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), using \(O(m^2(\tau+m))\) value queries, that outputs an admissible pair with at most \(\tau+m\) positive allocation entries and welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}/(1+a)\).

The supplied [finite-agent result, Section 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.00304v4), uses historical marginal values to define prices. Here we use running minima of historical marginal values and split recipient masses only when a supply is exhausted. The proof below is self-contained.

1. Consequence of the valuation promise

Write \(v_t(j\mid S)=v_t(S\cup\{j\})-v_t(S)\).

The given promise implies that whenever \(S\subseteq T\subseteq X\setminus\{j\}\),

\[ v_t(j\mid T)\le a\,v_t(j\mid S). \]

Indeed, apply the promise with \(W=S\), \(A=T\setminus S\), and \(x=j\), and subtract \(v_t(A\mid S)\).

We will use this inequality directly, so that the factor \(a\) is incurred only once.

2. Algorithm

A group consists of a type \(t\), a positive rational mass \(q\), and a current bundle \(D\). It also maintains a history of bundles

\[ \varnothing=D_0\subset D_1\subset\cdots\subset D_k=D, \]

where each extension adds one category. For every \(j\notin D\), its score is

\[ c_g(j)=\min_{0\le r\le k}v_t(j\mid D_r). \]

All scores are nonnegative by monotonicity.

Initially, there is one group \((t,\mu_t,\varnothing)\) per type, with scores \(c_g(j)=v_t(\{j\})\). Let \(r_j=b_j\) denote remaining supply.

For categories with \(b_j=0\), immediately set

\[ p_j=\max_{1\le t\le\tau}v_t(\{j\}). \]

For other categories, initialize their prices to zero.

Repeat the following operation while there is a group-category pair satisfying \(j\notin D\), \(r_j>0\), and \(c_g(j)>0\):

  • Choose such a pair maximizing \(c_g(j)\). Write its score as \(\sigma\), and its group as \((t,q,D)\).
  • Set \(\delta=\min\{q,r_j\}\). Give category \(j\) to mass \(\delta\) of this group.
  • If \(\delta=q\), replace the group’s bundle by \(D'=D\cup\{j\}\). Otherwise, retain a group of mass \(q-\delta\) with bundle \(D\), and create a group of mass \(\delta\) with bundle \(D'\).
  • The group receiving \(j\) inherits the old history and appends \(D'\). Its remaining scores are updated by

\[ c_{g'}(k)=\min\{c_g(k),v_t(k\mid D')\}, \qquad k\notin D'. \]

  • Replace \(r_j\) by \(r_j-\delta\). If it becomes zero, set \(p_j=\sigma\).

At termination, categories with positive remaining supply retain price zero. Define \(y_{t,D}\) by summing the masses of all final groups with type \(t\) and bundle \(D\).

The histories need not be stored explicitly: their score minima suffice to implement the algorithm.

3. Feasibility, termination, and complexity

Every operation preserves each type’s total mass. It adds exactly \(\delta\) to the use of category \(j\), with \(\delta\le r_j\). Thus the final allocation satisfies

\[ \sum_D y_{t,D}=\mu_t, \qquad u_j=b_j-r_j\le b_j. \]

Each recipient receives a category at most once because additions require \(j\notin D\).

A split occurs only when \(\delta<q\). In that case \(\delta=r_j\), so the operation exhausts category \(j\). Each category can be exhausted only once. Consequently, there are at most \(m\) splits and at most \(\tau+m\) groups throughout the algorithm.

To bound the number of operations, consider the integer potential

\[ \Phi=\sum_{\text{current groups }g}|D_g|. \]

An operation without a split increases \(\Phi\) by one. A split of a group with bundle \(D\) increases it by \(|D|+1\). Since always \(\Phi\le m(\tau+m)\), there are at most \(m(\tau+m)\) operations.

Initialization uses \(m\tau\) value queries. Each operation requires at most \(m+1\) additional queries to update the receiving group’s scores. Hence the total query count is \(O(m^2(\tau+m))\). Scanning all group-category pairs to select an operation also takes polynomial time.

All arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity. Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses and supplies. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Every group mass and remaining supply is an integer multiple of \(1/Q\), with polynomial-size numerator. Each score is one of the differences of two oracle answers, rather than an iterated arithmetic expression, so its bit length is \(O(L)\). Prices have the same bound.

Finally, merging equal type-bundle pairs leaves at most \(\tau+m\) positive entries of \(y\).

4. Prices satisfy individual rationality

First observe that the selected scores \(\sigma\) form a nonincreasing sequence.

To verify this, consider one operation. Every eligible pair afterward either existed beforehand with the same score, or belongs to the receiving group and inherits a score no larger than its predecessor’s score. Remaining supplies only decrease, so no previously unavailable category becomes available. Therefore every score eligible afterward is at most the maximum score selected beforehand.

Consider a final group with type \(t\) and bundle \(D\). Along its history, write the category additions as \(j_1,\ldots,j_k\), with selected scores \(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_k\). At each addition,

\[ v_t(j_\ell\mid D_{\ell-1})\ge \sigma_\ell, \]

because the selected score is a minimum that includes the current marginal.

If \(j_\ell\) is eventually exhausted, its final price is a selected score from that same operation or a later operation. Nonincrease therefore gives \(p_{j_\ell}\le\sigma_\ell\). If it remains unexhausted, its price is zero and the same inequality holds. Categories of zero initial supply are never allocated.

Normalization and telescoping now give

\[ v_t(D) =\sum_{\ell=1}^k v_t(j_\ell\mid D_{\ell-1}) \ge\sum_{\ell=1}^k\sigma_\ell \ge\sum_{j\in D}p_j. \]

Thus every positive entry of \(y\) satisfies individual rationality.

5. Prices satisfy outward stability

We first prove a historical-witness property.

Claim. For every final group of type \(t\) with bundle \(D\), and every \(j\notin D\), there exists \(S_j\subseteq D\) such that

\[ v_t(j\mid S_j)\le p_j. \]

There are three cases.

Zero initial supply. If \(b_j=0\), take \(S_j=\varnothing\). The initial price definition gives the required inequality.

Positive supply exhausted. Consider the operation exhausting \(j\), whose selected score is \(p_j\). Trace the final group backward to its ancestor immediately before that operation. This ancestor lacked \(j\), since bundles only grow and the final group lacks \(j\). Its score for \(j\) was eligible and therefore at most the selected maximum \(p_j\).

That score is a minimum of marginals at historical bundles. Choose a historical bundle attaining the minimum as \(S_j\). It is contained in the ancestor’s bundle and hence in the final bundle \(D\). This argument also covers a final group descending from the unserved portion of the group split by the exhausting operation.

Supply unexhausted. At termination \(r_j>0\) and \(p_j=0\). Since the final group lacks \(j\), its score for \(j\) is eligible. Termination implies that score is zero. A historical bundle attaining its minimum supplies the required \(S_j\).

This proves the claim.

Now fix any \(A\subseteq X\setminus D\), and order its elements as \(j_1,\ldots,j_s\). Put \(T_\ell=D\cup\{j_1,\ldots,j_{\ell-1}\}\). Each witness satisfies \(S_{j_\ell}\subseteq T_\ell\), and \(j_\ell\notin T_\ell\). Section 1 therefore yields

\[ v_t(j_\ell\mid T_\ell) \le a\,v_t(j_\ell\mid S_{j_\ell}) \le a\,p_{j_\ell}. \]

Summing the telescoping marginals gives

\[ v_t(A\mid D)\le a\sum_{j\in A}p_j. \]

This is precisely the required outward-stability condition.

Also, \(u_j<b_j\) means \(r_j>0\), and the algorithm assigns price zero in that case. All admissibility conditions are established.

6. Welfare guarantee against the mass optimum

We prove that every admissible pair has the desired welfare guarantee.

Write \(p(E)=\sum_{j\in E}p_j\). For any type \(t\), any supported bundle \(D\) of its allocation, and any bundle \(E\), monotonicity and outward stability imply

\[ v_t(E) \le v_t(D\cup E) =v_t(D)+v_t(E\setminus D\mid D) \le v_t(D)+a\,p(E). \]

Define the mean allocated value of type \(t\) by

\[ \bar v_t=\frac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_D y_{t,D}v_t(D). \]

This is well-defined because \(\mu_t>0\). Averaging the preceding inequality with weights \(y_{t,D}/\mu_t\) gives, for every \(E\),

\[ v_t(E)\le\bar v_t+a\,p(E). \]

Let \(z\) be any feasible competing allocation, and write its category uses as \(u_j(z)\). Multiplying by \(z_{t,E}\) and summing gives

\[ W(z)\le W(y)+a\sum_j p_j u_j(z) \le W(y)+a\sum_j p_j b_j. \]

Admissibility gives \(p_j(b_j-u_j)=0\) for every category, so \(\sum_jp_jb_j=\sum_jp_ju_j\). Individual rationality, integrated over the allocation, gives

\[ \sum_jp_ju_j =\sum_{t,D}y_{t,D}p(D) \le W(y). \]

Consequently, \(W(z)\le(1+a)W(y)\) for every feasible \(z\).

The feasible allocation set has finitely many coordinates, is nonempty, closed, and bounded; therefore the welfare maximum exists. Applying the inequality to an optimal allocation proves

\[ W(y)\ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}}{1+a}. \]

1. Consequence of the valuation promise

The deduction
\[ v_t(j\mid T)\le a\,v_t(j\mid S) \]
is correct for \(S\subseteq T\subseteq X\setminus\{j\}\).

The proposed substitution satisfies every hypothesis: \(S\) and \(T\setminus S\) are disjoint, and \(j\) belongs to neither. Subtracting \(v_t(T\setminus S\mid S)\) leaves exactly the marginal at \(T\). No stronger submodularity assumption is used.

2. Algorithm and history invariant

The algorithm is well-defined. Whenever an operation is selected, both the group mass and remaining supply are positive, so \(\delta>0\).

The score update preserves the stated historical-minimum definition. A receiving group inherits its previous history and appends its new bundle; an unserved group retains its existing bundle and history. Consequently, every score minimum has an attaining historical bundle.

Exact comparisons and arbitrary tie-breaking suffice. No demand oracle or enumeration of all bundles is required.

3. Feasibility, termination, and complexity

The mass and supply conservation arguments are correct. An operation changes category use by precisely \(\delta\), and the condition \(j\notin D\) prevents repeated receipt of a category.

The splitting bound is correct: \(\delta<q\) forces \(\delta=r_j\), exhausting that category. Thus at most \(m\) splits occur, and the number of groups never exceeds \(\tau+m\).

The potential calculation is also correct:

  • Without a split, the potential increases by one.
  • With a split, its increase is \(|D|+1\).
  • Every group bundle has size at most \(m\), so the potential is bounded by \(m(\tau+m)\).

This proves the claimed operation bound, independently of how small positive masses or supplies are.

Initialization and the per-operation score updates yield the claimed \(O(m^2(\tau+m))\) query bound. Scanning all current pairs costs polynomial time.

The numerical argument is valid. The product of input denominators has bit length \(O(L)\); mass and supply updates preserve its rational grid. Group masses remain bounded by one, and remaining supplies remain bounded by their input values. Marginal differences have bit length \(O(L)\), and taking minima introduces no accumulating arithmetic expressions. Merging groups preserves polynomial output length.

4. Individual rationality

The selected scores are nonincreasing. Updated scores cannot increase, retained scores remain unchanged, and decreasing remaining supplies cannot introduce an available category.

Along every final group’s inherited history, the marginal at an addition is at least its selected score. Any positive-supply category’s final price is either zero or the score selected at its eventual exhaustion, which occurs at or after its allocation to that group. Hence the price is at most that group’s historical selected score.

The telescoping calculation therefore establishes
\[ v_t(D)\ge\sum_{j\in D}p_j. \]

Initially zero-supply categories never enter any allocated bundle. Merging identical type-bundle groups does not change the inequality.

5. Historical witnesses and outward stability

The three witness cases cover every category omitted from a final bundle.

For initially zero supply, the maximum singleton price gives the stated witness.

For exhausted positive supply, the final group has an ancestor immediately before exhaustion. That ancestor lacks the category because bundles only grow. Its historical minimum is bounded by the selected exhaustion score, including when the final group descends from an unserved split portion.

For unexhausted supply, termination and nonnegative scores force the relevant historical minimum to be zero.

The subsequent application of Section 1 satisfies its hypotheses: each witness lies inside the corresponding telescoping base bundle, and the category being added lies outside that base. Summing establishes outward stability for every \(A\subseteq X\setminus D\), including the empty set. The factor \(a\) is applied once to each marginal, without compounding.

The identity \(u_j=b_j-r_j\) also establishes the required implication from slack supply to zero price.

Minor issue: “Eligible” is ambiguous here. The loop requires a strictly positive score, but the unexhausted-supply case calls a pair eligible while concluding that its score is zero. The exhausted-supply argument similarly uses eligibility without distinguishing a zero score.

Minimum clarification: Define “eligible” as satisfying only \(j\notin D\) and \(r_j>0\), and describe loop candidates as eligible pairs with positive score. This is terminology clarification; no additional mathematical result is needed.

6. Welfare guarantee

The comparison with every competing bundle is valid:
\[ v_t(E)\le v_t(D)+a\,p(E). \]
Monotonicity justifies passing to \(D\cup E\), outward stability applies to \(E\setminus D\), and nonnegative prices justify replacing its price sum by \(p(E)\).

Averaging over allocated bundles is legitimate because every \(\mu_t>0\). Summing against a competing allocation uses exactly its prescribed type masses and supply constraints.

The complementary-slackness identity follows directly from admissibility, and integrated individual rationality bounds total allocated payments by \(W(y)\). These steps establish
\[ W(z)\le(1+a)W(y) \]
for every feasible competing allocation.

Finally, the feasible set is nonempty, closed, bounded, and finite-dimensional, and welfare is linear. Thus an optimum exists and the stated approximation follows.
