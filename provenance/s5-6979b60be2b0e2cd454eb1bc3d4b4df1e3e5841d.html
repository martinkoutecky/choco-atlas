The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item types, bidder types \(t\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), and explicitly represented XOS valuations \(v_t\), compute or approximate the maximum welfare \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) subject to \(x_{t,S}\ge0\), \(\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_j\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An explicitly represented XOS valuation is a list of nonnegative rational additive clauses \(a_{t,\ell,j}\), with \(v_t(S)=\max_\ell\sum_{j\in S}a_{t,\ell,j}\).
Given item types \(M\), bidder types \(t\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), and rational supplies \(b_j\), maximize \(\sum_{t,S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) subject to \(x_{t,S}\geq 0\), \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\leq b_j\).

Is computing this maximum welfare in P, or is it NP-hard?

For a finite list of bidders with explicitly represented XOS valuations, approximating maximum welfare within any constant factor smaller than \(\mathrm e/(\mathrm e-1)\) is NP-hard. The supplied result also gives a polynomial-time method for solving the unit-mass, unit-supply fractional allocation program using demand queries; it does not state a complexity result for the rational-mass, rational-supply formulation above.

Result

Computing the maximum welfare is in P, including for rational masses and rational supplies. A polynomial-size linear program gives its exact value.

Assume masses and supplies are nonnegative, as appropriate for the allocation problem, and each bidder type has at least one clause. Write \(L_t\) for the set of explicitly listed clauses of type \(t\), and let \(m=|M|\).

A polynomial-size linear program

Introduce variables:

  • \(y_{t,\ell}\): the mass of type \(t\) assigned to clause \(\ell\);
  • \(z_{t,\ell,j}\): the mass within that group receiving item \(j\).

Consider the linear program
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad& \sum_t\sum_{\ell\in L_t}\sum_{j\in M} a_{t,\ell,j}z_{t,\ell,j}\\ \text{subject to}\quad& \sum_{\ell\in L_t}y_{t,\ell}=\mu_t &&\text{for every }t,\\ & \sum_t\sum_{\ell\in L_t}z_{t,\ell,j}\le b_j &&\text{for every }j,\\ & 0\le z_{t,\ell,j}\le y_{t,\ell} &&\text{for every }t,\ell,j,\\ & y_{t,\ell}\ge0 &&\text{for every }t,\ell. \end{aligned} \]
Denote its optimum by \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{ext}}\), and the optimum in the question by \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{orig}}\).

Both programs are feasible: assign all mass to the empty bundle in the original program; in the new program, assign each type’s mass to any clause and set every \(z\) to zero. Both feasible regions are closed and bounded, so their optima are attained.

Every original allocation gives an equally valuable feasible solution

Let \(x\) be feasible for the original program. For each type \(t\) and bundle \(S\), choose a maximizing clause \(\ell(t,S)\), so that
\[ v_t(S)=\sum_{j\in S}a_{t,\ell(t,S),j}. \]
Define
\[ y_{t,\ell} =\sum_{S:\ell(t,S)=\ell}x_{t,S}, \qquad z_{t,\ell,j} =\sum_{\substack{S:\ell(t,S)=\ell\\j\in S}}x_{t,S}. \]
These variables satisfy all the new constraints. In particular, the total consumption of each item is unchanged. Their objective equals
\[ \sum_{t,\ell,j}a_{t,\ell,j}z_{t,\ell,j} = \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S} \sum_{j\in S}a_{t,\ell(t,S),j} = \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S). \]
Consequently, \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{orig}}\le\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{ext}}\).

Every new solution can be realized by bundles

Fix any feasible \((y,z)\). We first realize the prescribed item masses separately for each pair \((t,\ell)\).

Suppose \(m\ge1\). Order the items as \(j_1,\ldots,j_m\) so that
\[ z_{t,\ell,j_1}\ge\cdots\ge z_{t,\ell,j_m}. \]
Set
\[ c_0=y_{t,\ell},\qquad c_k=z_{t,\ell,j_k}\ \ (1\le k\le m),\qquad c_{m+1}=0. \]
The constraints ensure that this sequence is nonincreasing. For each \(k\) from zero through \(m\), assign mass
\[ w_k=c_k-c_{k+1} \]
to the prefix bundle \(S_k=\{j_1,\ldots,j_k\}\), with \(S_0=\varnothing\).

These masses are nonnegative and sum to \(y_{t,\ell}\). Item \(j_r\) occurs exactly in the bundles with \(k\ge r\), so its assigned mass is
\[ \sum_{k=r}^{m}w_k =\sum_{k=r}^{m}(c_k-c_{k+1}) =c_r =z_{t,\ell,j_r}. \]
If \(m=0\), simply assign mass \(y_{t,\ell}\) to the empty bundle.

Combine these constructions over all clauses and types, adding masses whenever the same type and bundle occur. The resulting \(x\) satisfies the original type-mass equalities and item-supply inequalities, because the construction preserves all corresponding totals.

For every constructed bundle, the definition of XOS gives
\[ v_t(S_k)\ge\sum_{j\in S_k}a_{t,\ell,j}. \]
Thus the welfare of the combined allocation is at least
\[ \sum_{t,\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{m} w_k\sum_{j\in S_k}a_{t,\ell,j} = \sum_{t,\ell,j}a_{t,\ell,j}z_{t,\ell,j}. \]
Applying this construction to an optimal new solution proves
\(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{orig}}\ge\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{ext}}\).
Therefore the two optima are equal.

Complexity and the supplied hardness result

The new program has \((m+1)\sum_t|L_t|\) variables and polynomially many constraints. Its coefficients consist of input rationals and the constants \(0,1,-1\), so its encoding length is polynomial in the explicit input length. The polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore computes its exact optimum in polynomial time.

The construction also yields an optimal original allocation with at most \((m+1)\sum_t|L_t|\) listed bundles, counting repetitions before aggregation.

The supplied finite-bidder welfare hardness result concerns integral allocations. The program here permits splitting each type’s mass among bundles and constrains aggregate item consumption. The proof establishes polynomial-time solvability of precisely this fractional formulation, without extending the supplied demand-query result by assumption.

In the extended program, \(0\le y_{t,\ell}\le\mu_t\) and \(0\le z_{t,\ell,j}\le y_{t,\ell}\). Both programs have finitely many variables and closed, bounded, nonempty feasible regions. Their linear objectives therefore attain finite optima.

  • Original allocation to extended solution.

A maximizing clause exists because each clause list is finite and nonempty. Choosing one for each pair \((t,S)\) partitions the bundles of each type. Consequently:

  • The resulting clause masses sum to \(\mu_t\).
  • Each item mass within a clause group lies between zero and that group’s mass.
  • Aggregate consumption of every item is preserved.
  • The objective is preserved exactly, because the chosen clause realizes \(v_t(S)\).

Thus the inequality \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{orig}}\le\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{ext}}\) is justified. This direction is an existence argument; it does not require enumerating all original bundles algorithmically.

  • Realization of extended variables by bundles.

After sorting the item masses, the constraints imply
\(c_0\ge c_1\ge\cdots\ge c_m\ge c_{m+1}=0\).
Therefore every \(w_k=c_k-c_{k+1}\) is nonnegative. The total mass telescopes to \(c_0=y_{t,\ell}\), and the mass receiving item \(j_r\) telescopes to \(c_r=z_{t,\ell,j_r}\). These computations are correct.

Tied item masses cause no problem: some bundle masses may simply be zero. Groups with \(y_{t,\ell}=0\) also work, since all their item masses and constructed bundle masses are zero. The case \(m=0\) is handled explicitly.

Aggregating the constructed masses by type and bundle preserves the required type masses and item consumptions. Each bundle’s XOS value is at least its value under the clause used to construct that group. Summing these inequalities remains valid even when several groups produce the same bundle, because welfare is linear in allocated mass.

The displayed objective identity follows by summing each item’s preserved marginal mass. Hence every extended feasible solution gives an original feasible allocation with at least its objective value. Applying this to an extended optimum establishes the reverse inequality and therefore equality of the two optima.
