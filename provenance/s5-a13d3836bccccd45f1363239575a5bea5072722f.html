The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of types \(T\), where each type \(t\) has a shared nonincreasing marginal-value schedule \(v_{t,1}\ge\cdots\ge v_{t,K}>0\), masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and supply \(\kappa\in(0,K)\) units per population member, compute an optimal mass allocation \(y_{tq}\). Each member receives an integer bundle \(q\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\), while \(y_{tq}\) may be fractional. Maximize \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{q=0}^{K}y_{tq}V_t(q)\), subject to \(\sum_q y_{tq}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,q}q\,y_{tq}=\kappa\), and \(y_{tq}\ge0\), where \(V_t(q)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}v_{t,i}\). For each assigned bundle \(q\), compute the limiting Vickrey charge \(P_t(q)=V_t(q)-D_t\), where \(D_t=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}[W(\mu,\kappa)-W(\mu-\epsilon e_t,\kappa)]/\epsilon\) and \(W\) is optimal welfare.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There is one homogeneous good. Set \(m=K\ge1\), the maximum integer bundle size; each type has an explicitly listed schedule of positive binary integer values \(v_{t,1}\ge\cdots\ge v_{t,K}>0\). Supply is a binary rational \(\kappa\in(0,K)\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Define \(V_t(0)=0\) and \(V_t(q)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}v_{t,i}\). Allocation mass \(y_{tq}\) gives bundle \(q\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\) to that mass of type \(t\).
For any nonnegative mass vector \(a\), define \(W(a,\kappa)=\max_y\sum_{t,q}y_{tq}V_t(q)\), subject to \(\sum_q y_{tq}=a_t\), \(\sum_{t,q}q y_{tq}=\kappa\), and \(y_{tq}\ge0\), whenever feasible.
Compute an exact optimal allocation at \(a=\mu\), allowing any optimum when values tie, and for each \(y_{tq}>0\) compute the charge \(P_t(q)=V_t(q)-D_t\), where \(D_t=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\bigl[W(\mu,\kappa)-W(\mu-\epsilon e_t,\kappa)\bigr]/\epsilon\) and \(e_t\) is the type coordinate vector.
Removal does not renormalize masses or change supply; use \(0<\epsilon<\min\{\mu_t,1-\kappa/K\}\). This preserves feasibility after infinitesimal removal, the counterpart of the no-monopoly condition.

Is computing this optimal mass allocation and all its specified limiting charges solvable in polynomial time in \(m=K\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite no-monopoly matroids with positive integer valuations, an ascending auction produces an efficient allocation and Vickrey payments; a later version gives a polynomial-time variant using a cocircuit oracle ([Bikhchandani et al.](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/schummer/ftp/research/jvick/jvick.pdf)).
Continuum VCG mechanisms have been characterized using individual marginal products ([Makowski–Ostroy](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304406892900194)); no result settling the exact computational problem above was identified.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(K\), \(\tau\), and \(L\). In fact, all specified charges have the form \(P_t(q)=pq\), where \(p\) is an exactly computable integer cutoff price. The strict inequality in the cutoff definition below is essential when supply falls exactly between marginal-value levels.

1. Defining the cutoff.

List the distinct values appearing in the schedules in decreasing order as \(w_1>\cdots>w_r>0\). Define their aggregate masses and cumulative masses by
\(B_j=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\,|\{i:v_{t,i}=w_j\}|\),
\(C_0=0\), and \(C_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}B_\ell\).

Each \(B_j\) is positive, and \(C_r=K\sum_t\mu_t=K>\kappa\). Hence there is a unique smallest index \(j^\ast\) satisfying \(C_{j^\ast}>\kappa\). Set \(p=w_{j^\ast}\).

For each type, define
\(h_t=|\{i:v_{t,i}>p\}|\) and \(e_t=|\{i:v_{t,i}=p\}|\).
Also set \(A=\sum_t\mu_t h_t\) and \(B=\sum_t\mu_t e_t\). By construction,
\(A=C_{j^\ast-1}\le\kappa<C_{j^\ast}=A+B\).
In particular, \(B>0\).

2. A welfare bound with an explicit equality condition.

For each type, define
\(U_t(p)=\max_{0\le q\le K}\{V_t(q)-pq\}\).

Because the marginal values are nonincreasing,
\(V_t(q)-pq=\sum_{i=1}^{q}(v_{t,i}-p)\)
increases through the first \(h_t\) terms, stays constant through the following \(e_t\) terms, and decreases thereafter. Thus
\(U_t(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\max\{v_{t,i}-p,0\}\),
and the maximizing integer bundles are exactly those satisfying
\(h_t\le q\le h_t+e_t\).

For any nonnegative mass vector \(a\) and any feasible allocation \(y\),
\(\sum_{t,q}y_{tq}V_t(q)\le p\kappa+\sum_t a_tU_t(p)\).
Indeed, multiply \(V_t(q)\le pq+U_t(p)\) by \(y_{tq}\), sum, and use the allocation constraints.

This bound is attained whenever
\(A(a)=\sum_ta_th_t\le\kappa\le\sum_ta_t(h_t+e_t)=A(a)+B(a)\),
where \(B(a)=\sum_ta_te_t>0\). To see this, set
\(\theta(a)=(\kappa-A(a))/B(a)\in[0,1]\).
For each type, assign mass \(a_t(1-\theta(a))\) to bundle \(h_t\) and mass \(a_t\theta(a)\) to bundle \(h_t+e_t\), combining the entries if these bundles coincide.

The type masses sum correctly, and the allocated supply is
\(\sum_ta_t[(1-\theta(a))h_t+\theta(a)(h_t+e_t)]=A(a)+\theta(a)B(a)=\kappa\).
Both assigned bundles maximize \(V_t(q)-pq\). Therefore
\(W(a,\kappa)=p\kappa+\sum_ta_tU_t(p)\)
under these conditions.

3. Computing an optimal allocation.

Apply the preceding construction with \(a=\mu\) and
\(\theta=(\kappa-A)/B\in[0,1)\).

Explicitly, initialize all entries to zero and, for each type, add \(\mu_t(1-\theta)\) to \(y_{t,h_t}\) and \(\mu_t\theta\) to \(y_{t,h_t+e_t}\). Combining coincident entries gives at most two positive entries per type.

The construction is feasible and attains the welfare bound, so it is optimal. In particular,
\(W(\mu,\kappa)=p\kappa+\sum_t\mu_tU_t(p)\).

4. Computing the removal derivatives exactly.

Fix a type \(t\), and write \(\sigma=A+B-\kappa>0\). Choose
\(0<\epsilon<\min\{\mu_t,\,1-\kappa/K,\,\sigma/K\}\)
and let \(a^\epsilon=\mu-\epsilon e_t\).

The first two restrictions are precisely of the permitted form in the question. Moreover,
\(A(a^\epsilon)=A-\epsilon h_t\le A\le\kappa\),
while
\(A(a^\epsilon)+B(a^\epsilon)=A+B-\epsilon(h_t+e_t)\ge A+B-\epsilon K>\kappa\).

Consequently, \(B(a^\epsilon)>0\), and the same price \(p\) still satisfies the conditions for attaining the welfare bound. Hence
\(W(\mu-\epsilon e_t,\kappa)=p\kappa+\sum_s\mu_sU_s(p)-\epsilon U_t(p)\).

Subtracting this identity from the baseline identity gives
\(W(\mu,\kappa)-W(\mu-\epsilon e_t,\kappa)=\epsilon U_t(p)\)
for every sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\). Thus the required limit exists, and
\(D_t=U_t(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\max\{v_{t,i}-p,0\}\).

This argument proves exact local linearity; it does not require differentiability across changes in the cutoff.

5. Charges, including arbitrary optimal tie choices.

Every positive entry in the constructed allocation uses a maximizing bundle, so
\(V_t(q)-pq=U_t(p)=D_t\).
Its specified charge is therefore \(P_t(q)=pq\).

The same conclusion holds for any optimal allocation. Indeed, its gap from the welfare bound equals
\(\sum_{t,q}y_{tq}[U_t(p)-(V_t(q)-pq)]\).
Every summand is nonnegative, and optimality makes their sum zero. Thus every entry with \(y_{tq}>0\) satisfies \(V_t(q)-pq=U_t(p)\), again giving \(P_t(q)=pq\).

To illustrate why the cutoff uses \(C_j>\kappa\), consider \(K=1\), two types of mass \(1/2\), values \(10\) and \(1\), and supply \(\kappa=1/2\). The cutoff is \(p=1\). Removing mass \(\epsilon\) of the high-value type requires replacing it with low-value mass, reducing welfare by \(9\epsilon\). Thus its derivative is \(9\), and its charge for bundle \(1\) is \(1\). Using the last allocated marginal value \(10\) would give the wrong derivative.

6. Exact polynomial-time complexity.

There are \(N=\tau K\) explicitly listed marginal values. Sort them and aggregate their rational masses by value to find the first cumulative mass strictly exceeding \(\kappa\). This takes \(O(N\log N)\) comparisons and polynomially many exact arithmetic operations. Then compute \(h_t,e_t,A,B,\theta\), the allocation entries, and the charges.

All intermediate and output numbers have polynomial encoding length. To verify this, write the input masses and supply as ratios of binary integers, and let \(Q\) be the product of their denominators. Its binary length is at most their total input length. Expressed over \(Q\), the numerators of \(A\) and \(B\) are at most \(KQ\), and that of \(\kappa\) is also at most \(KQ\). Therefore \(\theta=(\kappa-A)/B\) has numerator and denominator lengths \(O(L+\log K)\). Multiplying it by an input mass preserves polynomial encoding length.

The derivatives are sums of at most \(K\) integer differences, and the charges \(pq\) are integers; both have polynomial binary length. The allocation has at most \(2\tau\) positive entries, or can be output as the full table of \(\tau(K+1)\) entries.

Thus the algorithm computes an exact optimal allocation and every required limiting charge in polynomial time in \(K\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).
