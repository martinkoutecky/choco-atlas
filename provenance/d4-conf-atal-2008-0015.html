THEOREM 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite value set \(T\subseteq[L,U]\), a rational supply share \(q\in(0,1)\), and a finite rational prior \(\Pi\) over societies \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), allocate mass \(q\) efficiently to the highest reported values, splitting mass at the cutoff as needed. Find coefficients for a common per-capita rebate \(\rho(\mu)\), a finite linear combination of quantile values of \(\mu\), that maximizes \(\mathbb{E}_{\mu\sim\Pi}[\rho(\mu)]\), subject to dominant-strategy truthfulness for each infinitesimal bidder and the pointwise non-deficit constraint \(\rho(\mu)\le q\theta_q(\mu)\), where \(\theta_q(\mu)\) is the allocation cutoff.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(T\subseteq[\ell,u]\cap\mathbb Q\) be nonempty, with \(0\le\ell<u<\infty\), and write \(m=|T|\). There is unit total mass of nonatomic, unit-demand bidders with quasilinear utilities; values and reports belong to \(T\).
A society \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\) is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Support size means the number of listed types, not the number of possible types.
Input consists of rational supply \(q\in(0,1)\), an explicitly listed prior \(\Pi\) on \(h\) rational societies with positive rational probabilities summing to one, and a rational threshold \(B\). Let \(\tau\) denote the maximum input society support size and \(L\) the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,h,L\).
For \(0\le s<1\), define the descending quantile \(Q_s(\mu)=\max\{t\in T:\mu(\{x\in T:x\ge t\})>s\}\), and let \(\theta_q(\mu)=Q_q(\mu)\).
Allocate all mass above \(\theta_q(\mu)\), none below it, and the fraction \((q-\mu(\{x>\theta_q(\mu)\}))/\mu(\{\theta_q(\mu)\})\) of each bidder at the cutoff. Charge \(\theta_q(\mu)\) per allocated unit.
A common per-capita rebate has the form \(\rho(\mu)=c_0+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_jQ_{s_j}(\mu)\), where any finite \(k\ge0\), rational levels \(s_j\in[0,1)\), and signed rational coefficients are chosen by the designer and fixed across societies. No individual-rationality or rebate-nonnegativity constraint is imposed.
Require dominant-strategy truthfulness against every unilateral report by a measure-zero bidder, which leaves the aggregate society unchanged, and require \(\rho(\mu)\le q\theta_q(\mu)\) for every \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), including societies outside the prior support.
Is deciding whether such a mechanism attains \(\mathbb E_{\mu\sim\Pi}[\rho(\mu)]\ge B\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
For finite bidders, optimal expected redistribution among anonymous affine functions of the other bidders' ordered bids has an explicit polynomial-time solution given the expected order statistics, subject to efficiency, truthfulness, and non-deficit. [Guo–Conitzer](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/oelAIJ10.pdf)

1. Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). Its answer is yes exactly when
\(B\le q\,\mathbb E_{\mu\sim\Pi}[Q_q(\mu)]\).

The permitted choice \(s_1=q\) allows the rebate \(\rho(\mu)=qQ_q(\mu)\), which redistributes the entire revenue in every society. This construction works for arbitrary support sizes, so it settles the stated problem without an additional restriction or interpretation.

2. Cutoff allocation and truthfulness

Fix any society \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), and write \(\theta=Q_q(\mu)\), \(A=\mu(\{x>\theta\})\), and \(M=\mu(\{\theta\})\).

The defining maximum exists: at the minimum element of \(T\), the upper-tail mass is \(1>q\). By definition, \(A+M>q\).

Also \(A\le q\). Indeed, if \(A>q\), there is a smallest positive-mass type \(a>\theta\). Its upper-tail mass equals \(A>q\), contradicting maximality of \(\theta\). Consequently \(M>0\), and the cutoff allocation fraction satisfies
\(\alpha=(q-A)/M\in[0,1)\).

In particular, equality \(A=q\) is allowed and gives \(\alpha=0\). The total allocated mass is \(A+\alpha M=q\), so total revenue is \(q\theta\).

Now fix a measure-zero bidder with true value \(v\in T\). By hypothesis, every unilateral report leaves \(\mu\), hence \(\theta\), \(\alpha\), and \(\rho(\mu)\), unchanged. Reporting \(r\in T\) gives allocation
\(x(r)=1\) if \(r>\theta\), \(x(r)=\alpha\) if \(r=\theta\), and \(x(r)=0\) if \(r<\theta\).

The bidder’s quasilinear utility is
\(u(v,r)=(v-\theta)x(r)+\rho(\mu)\).

There are three cases:

  • If \(v>\theta\), the coefficient \(v-\theta\) is positive. Truthful reporting gives allocation \(1\), the largest possible allocation.
  • If \(v<\theta\), that coefficient is negative. Truthful reporting gives allocation \(0\), the smallest possible allocation.
  • If \(v=\theta\), utility equals \(\rho(\mu)\) under every report.

Thus truthful reporting is weakly optimal against every unilateral report. This proof applies to every common rebate depending only on the unchanged aggregate society.

3. A globally feasible optimal rebate

Choose the rational parameters
\(k=1\), \(c_0=0\), \(c_1=q\), and \(s_1=q\).

They are admissible because \(q\in(0,1)\). The resulting rebate is
\(\rho^*(\mu)=qQ_q(\mu)=q\theta_q(\mu)\).

Section 2 proves dominant-strategy truthfulness. Moreover, the non-deficit constraint holds with equality for every society, including societies outside the prior support. Since total bidder mass is one, total redistribution equals the per-capita rebate.

Conversely, every feasible rebate satisfies \(\rho(\mu)\le qQ_q(\mu)\) pointwise. Taking expectations therefore gives
\(\mathbb E_{\Pi}[\rho(\mu)]\le q\mathbb E_{\Pi}[Q_q(\mu)]\).

The rebate \(\rho^*\) attains this bound. Hence the maximum is attained and equals \(q\mathbb E_{\Pi}[Q_q(\mu)]\), proving the decision criterion in Section 1.

4. Exact algorithm and running time

Write the listed prior as \(\Pi=\{(\pi_i,\mu_i):1\le i\le h\}\). For each society, sort its positive-mass support in descending order:
\(t_{i,1}>\cdots>t_{i,\tau_i}\), with corresponding masses \(w_{i,j}>0\).

Compute cumulative masses \(C_{i,j}=\sum_{a=1}^{j}w_{i,a}\). Let \(j_i\) be the first index with \(C_{i,j_i}>q\); such an index exists because \(C_{i,\tau_i}=1\). Then
\(Q_q(\mu_i)=t_{i,j_i}\).

To verify this identity, the upper-tail mass at \(t_{i,j_i}\) exceeds \(q\), whereas every type strictly above it has upper-tail mass at most \(C_{i,j_i-1}\le q\). This also accounts for unlisted types in \(T\).

Compute the rational number
\(R=q\sum_{i=1}^{h}\pi_i t_{i,j_i}\)
and return yes exactly when \(R\ge B\).

There are \(O(h\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational comparisons and arithmetic operations. With fractions reduced after arithmetic, intermediate bit lengths are \(O(L)\): cumulative-mass denominators divide products of input mass denominators, and the expectation denominator divides the product of the prior-probability denominators and distinct selected-type denominators. Multiplication by \(q\) adds only its input bit length.

Using elementary integer arithmetic, a conservative bit-time bound is
\(O\!\left(L+h\tau\log(\tau+1)L^3\right)\).
There is no additional dependence on \(m\); only listed support types need processing.

5. Relation to the finite-bidder comparison

The [Guo–Conitzer paper](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/oelAIJ10.pdf) studies finite-bidder rebates expressed using other bidders’ ordered bids. Here the stipulated invariance of the aggregate under every unilateral report makes the aggregate revenue itself an admissible, report-independent rebate. The proof above directly verifies the consequences of that continuum assumption.
