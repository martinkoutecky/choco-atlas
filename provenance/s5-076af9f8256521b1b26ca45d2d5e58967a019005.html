The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(L\) and \(R\), rational masses \(\mu_i>0\) and \(\mu_j>0\) with \(\sum_{i\in L}\mu_i=\sum_{j\in R}\mu_j=1\), a compatibility relation, and a strict partial order for each type over compatible types on the other side, choose rational matched masses \(x_{ij}\ge 0\). Require \(\sum_j x_{ij}\le\mu_i\) and \(\sum_i x_{ij}\le\mu_j\). For each compatible pair \((i,j)\), define \(D_i(j;x)=\mu_i-\sum_{k:\neg(j\succ_i k)}x_{ik}\), and define \(D_j(i;x)\) symmetrically. Require \(D_i(j;x)=0\) or \(D_j(i;x)=0\) for every compatible pair, then maximize \(\sum_{(i,j)}x_{ij}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given disjoint finite type supports \(U,W\), a society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|U|+|W|\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_v\), with \(\sum_{u\in U}\mu_u=\sum_{w\in W}\mu_w=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Compatibility is an explicitly listed relation \(E\subseteq U\times W\); each type has an explicitly given strict partial order \(\succ_v\) over its compatible partners. Every compatible partner is acceptable; incomparability does not imply strict preference, and no tie-breaking is imposed.
Choose \(x\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{E}\) satisfying \(\sum_{w:(u,w)\in E}x_{uw}\le\mu_u\) and \(\sum_{u:(u,w)\in E}x_{uw}\le\mu_w\).
For \((u,w)\in E\), define \(D_u(w;x)=\mu_u-\sum_{k:(u,k)\in E,\,\neg(w\succ_u k)}x_{uk}\) and \(D_w(u;x)=\mu_w-\sum_{h:(h,w)\in E,\,\neg(u\succ_w h)}x_{hw}\).
Call \(x\) stable when \(D_u(w;x)=0\) or \(D_w(u;x)=0\) for every \((u,w)\in E\), including pairs with positive matched mass. Maximize \(M(x)=\sum_{(u,w)\in E}x_{uw}\), and denote the optimum by \(\mathrm{OPT}\).
Let \(m=|E|\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and the approximation parameter \(\delta\).

Does this problem admit a polynomial-time \((2-\delta)\)-approximation for some fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\), returning stable \(x\) with \(M(x)\ge\mathrm{OPT}/(2-\delta)\), or is achieving this guarantee NP-hard under the Unique Games Conjecture for every fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\)?

Discrete maximum stable matching with arbitrary partial orders admits a polynomial-time \(2\)-approximation, and beating \(2\) is NP-hard under the Unique Games Conjecture; the hardness formulation allows parallel edges. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.09583v3)
For fractional ordinal stability with weak orders and unit capacities, a stable matching can be found in polynomial time; this does not settle the approximation question above for arbitrary partial orders and rational masses. [Source](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0013.pdf)

Result

Assuming the Unique Games Conjecture, achieving the stated guarantee is NP-hard for every fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\). This holds even when all masses are equal dyadic rationals.

The proof needs two ingredients: rounding stable fractional matchings with equal masses, and eliminating parallel edges while preserving the approximation gap.

1. Stability certificates and rounding

For a feasible allocation, write \(d_v(x)\) for the total mass incident to \(v\). The definition gives

\(D_u(w;x)=\mu_u-d_u(x)+\sum_{k:\,w\succ_u k}x_{uk}\).

Both terms are nonnegative. Consequently, \(D_u(w;x)=0\) holds exactly when:

  • \(d_u(x)=\mu_u\);
  • every partner \(k\) receiving positive mass satisfies \(\neg(w\succ_u k)\).

The analogous statement holds at \(w\). Thus stability supplies, for every compatible pair, a saturated endpoint whose entire positive support prevents strict improvement.

We first record the integrality fact needed to preserve these certificates.

Lemma 1. For a finite bipartite graph, allowing parallel edges, the polytope

\(P=\{z\in\mathbb R_{\geq 0}^{E}:\sum_{e\ni v}z_e\leq 1\text{ for every }v\}\)

has only integral vertices.

Proof. Suppose a point has fractional coordinates, and consider the graph consisting of edges with coordinates strictly between \(0\) and \(1\).

If this graph contains a cycle, the cycle has even length. Alternately increasing and decreasing its coordinates by a sufficiently small positive amount gives two distinct feasible points whose average is the original point.

Otherwise, a component containing an edge is a tree. Choose a path between two leaves. Each endpoint is unsaturated: its only positive incident coordinate is its fractional path edge, because an incident coordinate equal to \(1\) would violate feasibility. Alternating perturbations along the path preserve the degrees of internal vertices and remain feasible at both endpoints when sufficiently small.

Thus a point with fractional coordinates cannot be a vertex. This argument also covers a cycle consisting of two parallel edges. \(\square\)

Lemma 2. Suppose every type has mass \(1/N\). From any stable rational allocation \(x\), one can compute a discrete stable matching \(Q\) satisfying \(|Q|\geq N M(x)\), in polynomial time.

Proof. Put \(p=Nx\), and let \(S=\{v:d_v(p)=1\}\). Consider the nonempty face of \(P\) defined by

\(z_e=0\text{ whenever }p_e=0,\qquad d_v(z)=1\text{ for every }v\in S\).

It contains \(p\). By Lemma 1, maximizing \(\sum_e z_e\) over this face gives an integral matching \(Q\) with \(|Q|\geq\sum_e p_e\).

For every original edge, choose an endpoint certifying stability of \(p\). That endpoint belongs to \(S\), so it remains matched in \(Q\). Its selected edge belongs to the support of \(p\), and therefore cannot be strictly worse than the candidate edge. Hence \(Q\) is stable, including for selected edges.

The optimization is a bipartite maximum-flow problem with integral lower bounds forcing the vertices in \(S\) to be covered. It is polynomial-time solvable. \(\square\)

In particular, with equal masses, fractional and discrete stable optima coincide after scaling. This alone does not establish the desired hardness, because the cited discrete formulation allows parallel edges.

2. The discrete hardness input

We use the following consequence of the supplied discrete hardness theorem:

Assuming the Unique Games Conjecture, for every fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), a polynomial-time \((2-\varepsilon)\)-approximation for maximum stable matching with arbitrary partial orders is NP-hard, even on balanced bipartite multigraphs with at most two parallel edges between any pair.

The restriction follows from the actual reduction in Section 4.2: each source vertex produces two agents on each side, and every constructed pair has multiplicity at most two. [Csáji, Király and Yokoi, Theorem 3 and Section 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.09583v3#S4.SS2)

Fix such an instance \(G_0=(A,B;F)\). Write \(r=|A|+|B|\), \(f=|F|\), and let \(h\) be its maximum discrete stable matching size. We can restrict to \(f\geq1\) and \(h\geq1\), as in the hardness construction.

We will replace \(G_0\) by a simple graph.

3. An explicit replacement for parallel edges

Choose a power of four \(q\), to be specified later, and put \(t=q-1\). Let \(I\) be the set of nonzero vectors in \(\mathbb F_2^d\), where \(q=2^d\).

Partition \(I\times I\) into two bipartite graphs:

\(R_b=\{(i,j)\in I\times I:i\cdot j=b\},\qquad b\in\{0,1\}\).

Here the dot product is over \(\mathbb F_2\).

Lemma 3. Each \(R_b\) has a perfect matching. Moreover, if subsets \(X,Y\subseteq I\) have no \(R_b\)-edge between them, then \(|X||Y|\leq q\).

Proof. The graph \(R_0\) is regular of degree \(q/2-1\), and \(R_1\) is regular of degree \(q/2\). These degrees are positive for \(q\geq4\). Every positive-degree regular bipartite graph with equally sized sides satisfies Hall’s condition: counting edges from a set \(X\) gives \(d|X|\leq d|N(X)|\). Hence both graphs have perfect matchings.

For the second assertion, index a matrix \(H\) by all vectors of \(\mathbb F_2^d\), with \(H_{ij}=(-1)^{i\cdot j}\). Character orthogonality gives \(HH^{\mathsf T}=q\,\mathrm{Id}\): distinct rows have inner product zero, because a nonzero linear functional takes each value equally often.

Consequently,

\(\left|\mathbf1_X^{\mathsf T}H\mathbf1_Y\right|\leq\sqrt{q|X||Y|}\).

If there is no \(R_b\)-edge between \(X\) and \(Y\), every relevant matrix entry has the same sign. Thus the left side equals \(|X||Y|\), implying \(|X||Y|\leq q\). \(\square\)

Replace each vertex \(v\) of \(G_0\) by \(t\) copies \(v^i\), indexed by \(I\).

  • A pair joined by one original edge is replaced by the complete bipartite graph between its copies.
  • A pair joined by two original edges is replaced by \(R_0\) for one edge and \(R_1\) for the other.

Every new edge carries its original edge as a label. The resulting graph \(G'\) is simple.

At a copy of \(v\), define preferences by the labels: a new edge labeled \(e\) is strictly preferred to one labeled \(g\) exactly when \(e\succ_v g\). These relations are strict partial orders, since irreflexivity and transitivity follow from the original orders. Distinct new edges with the same label are incomparable.

Every discrete stable matching of \(G_0\) lifts to a stable matching of \(G'\) of \(t\) times its size. Indeed, replace each selected original edge by a perfect matching in its replacement graph. Every copy then has the same original assignment label as its original vertex. Any lifted blocking edge would therefore give an original blocking edge. In particular,

\(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{disc}}(G')\geq th\).

The converse requires a quantitative argument.

4. Extracting an original stable matching

Put \(\rho=\sqrt q/t\). Let \(Q'\) be any discrete stable matching of \(G'\).

For an original edge \(e=(u,w)\), define \(X_e\) to be the copies of \(u\) that are either unmatched or assigned a label strictly worse than \(e\) at \(u\). Define \(Y_e\) similarly at \(w\).

There cannot be a replacement edge labeled \(e\) between \(X_e\) and \(Y_e\), since it would block \(Q'\). By Lemma 3,

\(\min\{|X_e|,|Y_e|\}\leq\sqrt q\).

For a singly represented original pair, the complete replacement graph gives the stronger conclusion that one set is empty.

Choose an endpoint \(c(e)\) attaining the displayed bound. Let \(S\) be the set of endpoints chosen for at least one edge. Thus, for every edge assigned to \(v\), at most \(\rho t\) copies of \(v\) are unmatched or receive a label strictly worse than that edge.

An original label \(g\) incident to \(v\) is allowed at \(v\) if

\(\neg(e\succ_v g)\quad\text{for every }e\text{ with }c(e)=v\).

Retain only original edges whose labels are allowed at both endpoints.

Project \(Q'\) to the original multigraph by setting

\(z_g=\frac{\text{number of edges of }Q'\text{ labeled }g}{t}\).

Then \(z\in P\) and \(\sum_g z_g=|Q'|/t\). Obtain \(y\) by deleting all coordinates corresponding to edges not retained.

For each original edge, its chosen endpoint has at most \(\rho t\) problematic copies. Taking the union over the \(f\) edges shows that the deleted projected mass is at most \(f\rho\). Therefore,

\(M(y)\geq |Q'|/t-f\rho\).

Before deletion, every vertex of \(S\) has unmatched projected mass at most \(\rho\), and \(|S|\leq f\). Deleting mass \(f\rho\) decreases the sum of degrees over \(S\) by at most \(2f\rho\). Hence

\(\eta:=\sum_{v\in S}(1-d_v(y))\leq3f\rho\).

Now set \(K=r+1\). On the retained original graph, assign edge \(g=(u,w)\) the integer weight

\(\omega_g=1+K\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{u\in S\}}+\mathbf1_{\{w\in S\}}\bigr)\).

Let \(Q\) be a maximum-weight matching. Lemma 1 implies that its weight is at least the fractional weight of \(y\):

\(\omega(Q)\geq M(y)+K(|S|-\eta)\).

Assume \(3Kf\rho<1\). This lower bound is strictly greater than \(K|S|-1\). A matching missing a vertex of \(S\), however, has weight at most

\(K(|S|-1)+r=K|S|-1\).

Thus \(Q\) covers every vertex of \(S\). Its selected labels are allowed, so \(c(e)\) certifies nonblocking for every original edge \(e\). Therefore \(Q\) is an original stable matching.

Since \(\omega(Q)=|Q|+K|S|\), we also obtain

\(|Q|\geq M(y)-K\eta\geq |Q'|/t-(3r+4)f\rho\).

This proves a deterministic extraction guarantee with a controllably small additive loss.

5. Preserving the hardness gap

Fix \(\delta\in(0,1)\). Choose \(q\) to be the least power of four satisfying

\(\sqrt q\geq16(3r+4)f/\delta\).

Since \(t=q-1\) and \(q\geq4\), we have \(\rho\leq2/\sqrt q\). Consequently,

\((3r+4)f\rho\leq\delta/8,\qquad 3Kf\rho<1\).

The extraction procedure therefore returns an original stable matching satisfying

\(|Q|\geq |Q'|/t-\delta/8\).

To turn \(G'\) into a society, let \(a=|A|=|B|\). Pad each side with isolated types to a common size \(N\), where \(N\) is the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\geq at\). Give every type mass \(1/N\). These masses are positive dyadic rationals, and each side has total mass one. Isolated types affect neither matching sizes nor stability.

Suppose the requested polynomial-time \((2-\delta)\)-approximation exists. The lifted original optimum supplies a stable society allocation of mass \(th/N\). Thus its returned allocation \(x\) satisfies

\(M(x)\geq th/[N(2-\delta)]\).

By Lemma 2, round it to a stable discrete matching \(Q'\) with

\(|Q'|\geq NM(x)\geq th/(2-\delta)\).

Extracting an original stable matching gives

\(|Q|\geq h/(2-\delta)-\delta/8\).

Finally,

\(\frac1{2-\delta}-\frac1{2-\delta/2}=\frac{\delta/2}{(2-\delta)(2-\delta/2)}>\delta/8\).

Since \(h\geq1\), it follows that

\(|Q|\geq h/(2-\delta/2)\).

We would therefore obtain a polynomial-time \((2-\delta/2)\)-approximation for the restricted discrete hardness problem, contradicting its UGC-conditional hardness. This settles the requested alternative.

6. Running times and encoding sizes

No denominator-sized expansion is used when rounding a society allocation.

For a society with parameters \(m,\tau,L\), Lemma 2 uses a lower-bound flow network with \(O(\tau)\) vertices and \(O(m+\tau)\) arcs. The integral flow computation takes \(O(\tau(m+\tau))\) augmenting-path operations. Exact support and saturation checks take polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\) and the returned allocation’s bit length.

For the hardness reduction, the original parameters satisfy

\(t=O((rf/\delta)^2),\qquad \tau<2rt,\qquad m\leq ft^2\).

Explicitly listing all lifted preferences requires at most \(O(t^3f^2)\) comparisons. After relabeling types, the constructed input length is

\(L=O((t^3f^2+rt)\log(rt+2))\).

Construction is polynomial in the original input length, \(r,f,1/\delta\). Extraction uses \(O(ft+f^2)\) preference checks followed by a polynomial-time weighted bipartite matching computation. Hence all transformations are polynomial for every fixed \(\delta\), with bounds depending on the explicit support rather than an expanded population.
