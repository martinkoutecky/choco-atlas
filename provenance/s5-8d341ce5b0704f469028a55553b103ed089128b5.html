Theorem 5 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Let \(\mu\) describe a unit-mass population with at most \(\tau\) true types \(t=(A_t,v_t)\), where \(A_t\subseteq J\) is public and \(v_t\) is private; the mechanism knows public eligibility-class masses but not masses split by private value. An adversarial stream of \(H\) batches \((j_h,q_h)\), with \(j_h\in J\), arrives online; the mechanism sees current reports, irrevocably assigns up to \(q_h\) mass to unmatched eligible agents, and sets payments immediately, while each individual receives at most one indivisible item. Require ex-post truthfulness for each individual under every random seed, and measure expected welfare against the offline maximum-weight matching of the true population and supplies. What is the minimum worst-case competitive ratio for randomized mechanisms, and can it be achieved in time polynomial in \(\tau\), \(|J|\), \(H\), and the input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless unit-mass population, specified by the support list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types \(t=(A_t,v_t)\), with \(A_t\subseteq J\), \(v_t\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\), and binary-rational masses \(a_t>0\) satisfying \(\sum_t a_t=1\). Here \(m=|J|\), \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types, and \(L\) is the total binary input length.
The full support describes the instance, not information supplied to the mechanism. Initially it knows public eligibility-class identifiers and their total masses, but neither future eligibility incidences nor the masses split by private value; eligibility for the arriving item category is revealed verifiably at arrival.
An oblivious adversary fixes a stream of at most \(H\) batches \((j_h,q_h)\), with \(j_h\in J\) and \(q_h\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\). The mechanism knows the bounds \(m,H\), but not future batches or the stopping time; agents may know the entire stream.
At each arrival, eligible agents report their values. Each agent reports one fixed nonnegative rational value from her first eligible batch onward and cannot misreport eligibility. The mechanism immediately and irrevocably serves a measurable subset of unmatched eligible agents of mass at most \(q_h\); unused supply expires.
Serving an individual gives her one indivisible item, and each individual can be served at most once. Subsets of a type may be served without dividing any individual's item. Mechanisms specify finitely encoded, pointwise allocation and payment rules, including their behavior under singleton report deviations; any auxiliary individual labels are independent of private values.
Payments are nonnegative, fixed upon service, and zero for unserved agents. Require ex-post individual rationality and, for every random seed, every individual, and every profile of others' reports, that truthful reporting maximizes her whole-stream quasi-linear utility.
Welfare is the integral of true values over served agents. The offline benchmark \(\mathrm{OPT}\) maximizes \(\sum_{t,h}v_t x_{t,h}\) subject to \(x_{t,h}\geq0\), \(x_{t,h}=0\) when \(j_h\notin A_t\), \(\sum_hx_{t,h}\leq a_t\), and \(\sum_tx_{t,h}\leq q_h\). A ratio \(\alpha\geq1\) means \(\mathrm{OPT}\leq\alpha\mathbb E[\mathrm{SW}]\) on every instance.

What is the infimum worst-case competitive ratio for randomized mechanisms satisfying these requirements, as a function of \(m,\tau,H,L\), and is it attainable in time polynomial in these parameters?

For finite populations, randomized ex-post truthful prompt mechanisms with publicly revealed eligibility have competitive ratio \(\Omega(\log\nu/\log\log\nu)\), where \(\nu=\min(n,M)\), \(n\) counts agents and \(M\) counts individual items; an \(O(\log n)\) prompt upper bound holds even with private eligibility. [Verified bounds](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-023-01202-3).
No result settling this exact atomless, individually indivisible, prompt-payment model was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Public eligibility means verifiable eligibility revealed at arrival, following the discrete model; initial eligibility-class identifiers and masses do not disclose future incidence vectors.
  • The full true support specifies the instance for analysis, while the mechanism receives only the stipulated public information and reports.
  • Values and reports are nonnegative rationals, supplies are positive rationals, and all numerical data contribute to the binary input length.
  • Agents are non-myopic; eligibility cannot be concealed, and their reported value is fixed from their first eligible batch.
  • The adversary is oblivious to the random seed, the horizon is an upper bound, and unused batch supply expires.
  • Type masses represent atomless sets of individuals: allocation may split a type's mass but must give each served individual one whole item.
  • Mechanisms have finitely encoded pointwise rules, including singleton deviations, so individual truthfulness is not merely an almost-everywhere condition; auxiliary labels carry no information about private values.
  • Restored ex-post individual rationality, nonnegative prompt payments, and zero payments for unserved individuals.
  • Defined the continuous offline benchmark through mass-capacitated matching and used an infimum because an optimal mechanism need not exist.
  • Did not replace the discrete agent count by support size; the continuous competitive ratio may depend on the stated continuous parameters.

Status and new results

The exact general infimum and its polynomial-time attainability remain unresolved. The following results substantially improve the previously established bounds.

Write
$$ \beta(k)=\max\{1,k-1\}. $$

For positive $m,H$:

  • There is a pointwise truthful, prompt mechanism with competitive ratio at most

$$ \min(m,H)\,\beta(\tau). $$
It does not need to know $\tau$ or the value distribution initially.

  • If all agents have the same eligibility set and there are at most two positive-mass values, competitive ratio $1$ is achievable.
  • In particular, the exact infimum is $1$ when $m=1$ and $\tau\leq2$, for arbitrary supply sequences and stopping times.
  • If every public eligibility class has a single value, a deterministic, zero-payment mechanism achieves competitive ratio $e/(e-1)$. This constant is optimal over that restricted family when parameter sizes are unbounded.
  • Consequently, every instance with $\tau\leq2$ admits a mechanism with competitive ratio at most $e/(e-1)$.

I also prove a precise exception to a critical-service-time argument: different reports can receive service at different batches when one report equals the common payment threshold.

All constructions below are finitely described pointwise rules. I do not establish their general polynomial-time evaluability.

Measure-theoretic conventions

Let $(X,\mu)$ be the population probability space. Individual singletons are assumed measurable and null. Consequently, changing one individual's report changes neither measurable report-level masses nor the measurability of a report profile.

Use an auxiliary measurable label
$$ \ell:X\longrightarrow[0,1] $$
such that every label level has mass zero. The labels are independent of private values, as permitted in the question.

For a measurable set $S$ of mass $a$, define its label rank by
$$ F_S(x)=\mu\{y\in S:\ell(y)<\ell(x)\}. $$

The restriction of the label distribution to $S$ has no atoms. Therefore, for every $u\geq0$,
$$ \mu\{x\in S:F_S(x)<u\}=\min\{u,a\}. $$

Here is a justification. The function
$$ F(s)=\mu\{y\in S:\ell(y)<s\} $$
is continuous and nondecreasing, with endpoint values $0$ and $a$. For any $t\in[0,a]$, choose a label quantile with cumulative mass $t$. A flat interval of $F$ carries zero $S$-mass, because its endpoint cumulative masses agree; its endpoints also carry zero mass. Thus the inverse image of any single cumulative rank has mass zero, and the set of ranks below $t$ has mass $t$.

This fact will give both measurable individual allocations and exact mass feasibility.

For a measurable rational report profile $b$ on a positive-mass set $E$, let
$$ S(b,E)=\{z\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}:\mu\{x\in E:b(x)=z\}>0\}. $$

Because the report domain is countable, report levels outside $S(b,E)$ together have mass zero. Every truthful finite-support profile has a finite, nonempty set $S(b,E)$.

In the cutoff constructions, a nonmeasurable profile or an infinite set $S(b,E)$ causes the mechanism to serve nobody in the relevant part of the stream. These conditions are invariant under singleton deviations, so this extension preserves truthfulness.

A truthful mechanism for repeated supply of one category

Definition of the mechanism

Fix one category $j$. The mechanism serves only occurrences of $j$.

At the first occurrence of $j$, let $E$ be its eligible population. Nobody has previously been served by this mechanism. If $\mu(E)=0$, serve nobody.

Otherwise, collect the fixed reports on $E$. If the profile is measurable and has finite positive-mass report levels, write them as
$$ w_1<w_2<\cdots<w_k. $$

Choose the cutoff $p$ as follows:

  • If $k=1$, set $p=w_1$.
  • If $k\geq2$, choose $I$ uniformly from $\{1,\ldots,k-1\}$ and set $p=w_I$.

The choice is made using a seed fixed independently of the instance. For every fixed seed, a singleton deviation leaves the selected index and cutoff unchanged, because it leaves all positive-mass report statistics unchanged.

Freeze this cutoff and define
$$ A=\mu\{x\in E:b(x)>p\}, \qquad B=\mu\{x\in E:b(x)=p\}. $$

For each individual $x\in E$, define the priority rank
$$ \rho(x)= \begin{cases} \mu\{y\in E:b(y)>p,\ \ell(y)<\ell(x)\},&b(x)>p,\\[2mm] A+\mu\{y\in E:b(y)=p,\ \ell(y)<\ell(x)\},&b(x)=p,\\[2mm] +\infty,&b(x)<p. \end{cases} $$

Let $Q_h$ be the cumulative supply of category $j$ through its current occurrence. At that occurrence, serve every previously unmatched eligible individual satisfying
$$ \rho(x)<Q_h. $$

Every served individual pays $p$ immediately. All other payments are zero.

Feasibility

The mass of individuals with report above $p$ and rank below $Q$ is
$$ \min\{Q,A\}. $$

The corresponding mass at report exactly $p$ is
$$ \min\{\max\{Q-A,0\},B\}. $$

Therefore, the cumulative selected mass is
$$ \min\{Q,A\}+\min\{\max\{Q-A,0\},B\} =\min\{Q,A+B\}. $$

The selected sets are nested as $Q$ increases. If the current batch adds supply $q$, the newly selected mass is consequently at most $q$.

The allocation is measurable, and every individual is served at most once. Selecting subsets by labels divides population mass without dividing anyone's item.

Pointwise whole-stream truthfulness

Fix the seed, the stream, one individual $x$, and every other individual's report.

A singleton change leaves $p$, $A$, $B$, and all label-prefix masses unchanged. Put
$$ r_>(x)=\mu\{y\in E:b(y)>p,\ \ell(y)<\ell(x)\}, $$
and
$$ r_=(x)=\mu\{y\in E:b(y)=p,\ \ell(y)<\ell(x)\}, $$
where either integral can equivalently exclude $x$.

For every report $z>p$, the individual's rank is $r_>(x)$, independently of the magnitude of $z$. For report $z=p$, her rank is $A+r_=(x)$. Moreover,
$$ r_>(x)\leq A\leq A+r_=(x). $$

Let $Q$ be the total category supply in the actual stream. There are two possibilities.

If $Q\leq r_>(x)$, no report above $p$ wins. The displayed inequality implies that reporting $p$ also cannot win. Thus every report loses.

If $Q>r_>(x)$, every report above $p$ wins and pays $p$. Reporting $p$ may win or lose, and reporting below $p$ always loses.

These alternatives prove truthfulness directly:

  • For true value $v>p$, truthful reporting either wins with utility $v-p$, or no report can win.
  • For $v<p$, truthful reporting loses; any winning deviation has negative utility.
  • For $v=p$, every possible outcome has utility zero.

This holds for every individual and every profile of others' reports, including zero-mass report anomalies. Payments are nonnegative, prompt, and individually rational under truthful reporting.

Welfare guarantee of the cutoff mechanism

Suppose the truthful eligible population has distinct values
$$ w_1<\cdots<w_k $$
with masses $a_1,\ldots,a_k$. Define tail masses
$$ T_r=\sum_{s=r}^k a_s. $$

Let $Q$ be the total supply of the selected category.

The single-category offline benchmark

Since every member of $E$ is eligible for every occurrence of this category, the offline problem reduces to choosing masses $x_r$ satisfying
$$ 0\leq x_r\leq a_r, \qquad \sum_r x_r\leq Q. $$

Any such aggregate allocation can be distributed among the category's batches by successively filling their supply capacities.

Set $w_0=0$. For every feasible allocation,
$$ \sum_{r=1}^k w_r x_r = \sum_{r=1}^k (w_r-w_{r-1})\sum_{s=r}^k x_s \leq \sum_{r=1}^k (w_r-w_{r-1})\min\{Q,T_r\}. $$

Allocating in decreasing value order simultaneously attains every tail bound. Hence the offline value is
$$ F(Q)=\sum_{r=1}^k (w_r-w_{r-1})\min\{Q,T_r\}. $$

Summing the cutoff guarantees

For cutoff $p=w_i$, let $W_i(Q)$ be the mechanism's welfare.

The mechanism serves high-report mass $\min\{Q,T_{i+1}\}$, all of whose true values are at least $w_{i+1}$. It also serves value-$w_i$ mass
$$ \min\{Q,T_i\}-\min\{Q,T_{i+1}\}. $$

Thus
$$ W_i(Q)\geq w_i\min\{Q,T_i\} +(w_{i+1}-w_i)\min\{Q,T_{i+1}\}. $$

For $i=1$, retain both terms. For $i\geq2$, retain just the second, nonnegative term. Summing yields
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{k-1}W_i(Q) \geq w_1\min\{Q,T_1\} +\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(w_{i+1}-w_i)\min\{Q,T_{i+1}\} =F(Q). $$

Consequently, when $k\geq2$,
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{SW}]\geq\frac{F(Q)}{k-1}. $$

When $k=1$, the mechanism serves mass $\min\{Q,a_1\}$ at the common value and achieves $F(Q)$ exactly.

Therefore the competitive ratio is at most $\beta(k)$.

For $k=2$, the only cutoff is $w_1$. The high-report population then has exactly value $w_2$, so the displayed welfare bound is an equality with the offline benchmark. This proves ratio $1$ for two values.

Consequences for general eligibility

A general upper bound depending on support size

Let
$$ b_0=\min(m,H). $$

Before arrivals, choose $R$ uniformly from $\{1,\ldots,b_0\}$. Select the $R$th distinct category to appear, and run the preceding cutoff mechanism on all occurrences of that category. If fewer than $R$ distinct categories appear, serve nobody.

This uses only the known bounds and arrival history.

For each appearing category $j$, let $Q_j$ be its total supply and $F_j(Q_j)$ its standalone offline value, allowing the entire eligible population to be used for that category.

The contribution of category $j$ to any globally feasible offline allocation is at most $F_j(Q_j)$. Therefore,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\leq\sum_j F_j(Q_j). $$

The eligible population for a category has at most $\tau$ positive-mass values. Conditional on selecting $j$, the cutoff guarantee gives
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{SW}\mid j\text{ selected}] \geq\frac{F_j(Q_j)}{\beta(\tau)}. $$

Each appearing category is selected with probability $1/b_0$. Hence
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{SW}] \geq \frac{1}{b_0\beta(\tau)}\sum_j F_j(Q_j) \geq \frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{b_0\beta(\tau)}. $$

This proves the general bound
$$ \alpha\leq\min(m,H)\max\{1,\tau-1\}. $$

The mechanism learns its cutoff distribution from observed reports; $\tau$ is used only in the analysis.

One common eligibility set

Suppose all agents have the same eligibility set $A$.

Initialize the cutoff mechanism at the first batch whose category lies in $A$. Thereafter, count the supply of every batch whose category lies in $A$. Every such batch has the same eligible population, so the preceding proof applies verbatim with
$$ Q=\sum_{h:j_h\in A}q_h. $$

This gives ratio $\beta(\tau)$, independently of $m$ and $H$. In particular, the ratio is exactly $1$ when there are at most two values.

When $m=1$, the population eligible for the sole category has a common eligibility set. If $\tau\leq2$, it has at most two values. Thus the exact infimum for this case is $1$.

If every eligible value is zero, the welfare statement remains valid: both mechanism welfare and offline welfare are zero.

Homogeneous eligibility classes admit the optimal uniform constant

Assume now that every public eligibility class has one common true value. Different classes may have different values and eligibility sets.

Let the classes be $C_1,\ldots,C_c$, with masses $a_c$ and common values $v_c$.

Learning class weights without individual incentives

At a class's first eligible batch, all its members are unmatched and report their fixed values.

Define its estimated weight to be the maximum positive-mass report value if the class report profile is measurable with finite positive-mass support. Otherwise, define its weight to be zero. Freeze that estimate.

Changing any one individual's report leaves this estimate unchanged. Under truthful reporting and the homogeneous-class assumption, the estimate is exactly $v_c$.

The allocation below uses only class weights, eligibility, class masses, and public labels. It never uses an individual's own report to select her within her class. Therefore each individual's allocation is invariant under her singleton report deviations, for every profile of others' reports. Charging zero proves pointwise truthfulness and individual rationality.

It remains to prove the welfare guarantee.

The allocation rule

Let $y_c\in[0,1]$ be the fraction of class $c$ already served. Initially $y_c=0$.

Define
$$ g(y)=\frac{e^y-1}{e-1}. $$

At an arriving batch, continuously distribute its supply among eligible classes with available positive-weight mass, always choosing a class with largest current score
$$ v_c\bigl(1-g(y_c)\bigr). $$

Stop when the batch supply is exhausted or all eligible positive-weight classes are full. Zero-weight classes can be left unserved.

This procedure has a finite description. Equivalently, choose a final score level and increase each eligible class's served fraction until its score reaches that level, respecting its initial fraction and upper bound $1$.

Within class $c$, the cumulative served set is
$$ \left\{x\in C_c: \mu\{z\in C_c:\ell(z)<\ell(x)\}<a_c y_c \right\}. $$

These sets are measurable, nested, and have mass exactly $a_c y_c$. Thus the class-level fractional allocation is realized by irrevocable service of individuals.

A complete primal-dual proof

Under the homogeneous-class assumption, the offline program has variables $x_{c,h}$, objective
$$ \sum_{c,h}v_c x_{c,h}, $$
class capacities $a_c$, and batch capacities $q_h$.

Define, after the stream,
$$ u_c=v_c g(y_c^{\mathrm{final}}). $$

For batch $h$, let
$$ z_h=\max_{c\text{ eligible at }h} v_c\bigl(1-g(y_c^{\mathrm{after}\ h})\bigr), $$
taking the maximum to be zero if there is no eligible positive-weight class.

Because served fractions never decrease, for every eligible pair $(c,h)$,
$$ u_c+z_h \geq v_c g(y_c^{\mathrm{after}\ h}) + v_c\bigl(1-g(y_c^{\mathrm{after}\ h})\bigr) =v_c. $$

All these numbers are nonnegative. Thus, for every feasible offline allocation,
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{c,h}v_c x_{c,h} &\leq\sum_{c,h}(u_c+z_h)x_{c,h}\\ &\leq\sum_c a_c u_c+\sum_h q_h z_h. \end{aligned} $$

We next bound this dual objective.

During a batch, the maximum available score is nonincreasing. Every infinitesimal allocation therefore occurs at score at least the batch's final maximum $z_h$. If all supply is used, this gives
$$ q_h z_h \leq \sum_c a_c v_c \int_{y_c^{\mathrm{before}\ h}}^{y_c^{\mathrm{after}\ h}} (1-g(y))\,dy. $$

If supply remains unused, all eligible positive-weight classes are full, so $z_h=0$ and the same inequality holds.

Also, since $g(0)=0$,
$$ \sum_c a_c u_c = \sum_c a_c v_c\int_0^{y_c^{\mathrm{final}}}g'(y)\,dy. $$

Summing the batch inequalities and adding gives
$$ \sum_c a_c u_c+\sum_h q_h z_h \leq \sum_c a_c v_c \int_0^{y_c^{\mathrm{final}}} \bigl(g'(y)+1-g(y)\bigr)\,dy. $$

Direct calculation shows
$$ g'(y)+1-g(y) = \frac{e^y}{e-1}+1-\frac{e^y-1}{e-1} = \frac{e}{e-1}. $$

Consequently,
$$ \mathrm{OPT} \leq \frac{e}{e-1}\sum_c a_c v_c y_c^{\mathrm{final}} = \frac{e}{e-1}\mathrm{SW}. $$

This proves the claimed deterministic, truthful, prompt bound.

A constant upper bound for every instance with at most two types

Interpret public eligibility classes in their usual sense: individuals are grouped by their common eligibility set. Let $c$ be the number of positive-mass public classes. Then
$$ c\leq\tau. $$

Use the following branch, determined entirely by the initially public class structure:

  • If $c=1$, use the common-eligibility cutoff mechanism.
  • If $c\geq2$, use the class-weight mechanism of the preceding section.

Both branches are truthful on arbitrary report profiles.

When $\tau\leq2$, the first branch has at most two positive-mass values and is optimal. In the second branch, necessarily $c=\tau=2$, so each class contains one positive-mass type and is homogeneous. Its ratio is at most $e/(e-1)$.

Thus
$$ \alpha\leq\frac{e}{e-1} \qquad\text{whenever }\tau\leq2. $$

This conclusion does not determine the exact ratio for two types with different eligibility sets.

The threshold-equality exception for prompt mechanisms

The following lemma holds without assuming that a mechanism depends only on aggregate statistics.

Lemma. Fix a random seed, an individual, others' reports, and a stream prefix. Suppose one report receives service during this prefix at payment $p$. In any continuation, every report that receives service only after this prefix must equal $p$.

Proof. Consider the stream that stops at the prefix. For any rational true value $z>p$, reporting the earlier winning report would give utility $z-p>0$. Truthfulness therefore requires truthful report $z$ to receive service during the prefix.

Next consider the continuation. Any two winning reports must pay the same amount: applying truthfulness with each winning report as the true value gives both inequalities between their payments. Promptness fixes the earlier winner's payment at $p$, so a later winner also pays $p$.

A later winning report $z$ did not win during the prefix. The first paragraph implies $z\leq p$. Individual rationality when $z$ is the true value implies $z\geq p$. Hence $z=p$. $\square$

The equality exception is real.

Take one category, value-$1$ and value-$2$ populations each of mass $1/2$, and public labels uniform within each population. Supply is $1/4$ at the first batch and $3/4$ at the second. Use the two-value cutoff mechanism, whose price is $1$.

For a value-$2$ individual with label $1/4$, reporting above $1$ gives rank
$$ \frac12\cdot\frac14=\frac18, $$
so she receives service at the first batch.

If she instead reports $1$, her rank is
$$ \frac12+\frac12\cdot\frac14=\frac58, $$
so she receives service only at the second batch. Both outcomes charge $1$.

The full truthfulness proof above applies to this mechanism. Thus a statement forbidding all report-dependent service times would be false here; the threshold-equality case must be retained.

A lower bound approaching the same constant

For completeness, the following lower bound is proved independently of the notes. It applies even without incentive constraints.

Oblivious instance distribution

Let $n\geq2$ be a power of two. There are $n$ public classes, each of mass $1/n$, and every individual has value $1$.

There are $n$ categories and $n$ batches, each supplying $1/n$. Choose a uniformly random permutation $\pi$ of the public classes. At batch $h$, the eligible classes are
$$ S_h=\{C_{\pi(h)},\ldots,C_{\pi(n)}\}. $$

Equivalently,
$$ A_{\pi(r)}=\{j_1,\ldots,j_r\}. $$

The permutation and whole stream are fixed before execution. Initially public identifiers and masses reveal no permutation information.

Each instance has
$$ m=H=\tau=n. $$

Masses and supplies are dyadic, and incidence bit vectors give polynomial description length.

Offline, batch $h$ can serve all of $C_{\pi(h)}$. Thus
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=1. $$

Constraints on expected online service

Let $R_h$ be unmatched mass in $S_h$ immediately before batch $h$, and let $Y_h$ be the served mass at that batch. Write
$$ r_h=\mathbb E[R_h], \qquad y_h=\mathbb E[Y_h]. $$

Conditional on the revealed history and actions, the next eliminated class is uniform among the $n-h+1$ active classes. This holds because reports are all $1$ and the seed is independent of the permutation. Hence
$$ r_{h+1}=\frac{n-h}{n-h+1}(r_h-y_h). $$

Since $r_1=1$, induction gives
$$ \frac{r_h}{n-h+1} = \frac1n-\sum_{k=1}^{h-1}\frac{y_k}{n-k+1}. $$

Final-batch feasibility $y_n\leq r_n$ implies
$$ \sum_{h=1}^n\frac{y_h}{n-h+1}\leq\frac1n. $$

Supply feasibility also gives
$$ 0\leq y_h\leq\frac1n. $$

Bounding total welfare

Define
$$ k_n=\max\left\{ k\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}: \sum_{h=1}^k\frac1{n-h+1}\leq1 \right\}, $$
and set
$$ s_n=n-k_n, \qquad D_n=\sum_{h=1}^{k_n}\frac1{n-h+1}, \qquad c_n=1-\frac{s_n}{n}D_n. $$

Write $k=k_n$ and $s=s_n$. The identity
$$ \sum_{h=1}^n y_h = s\sum_{h=1}^n\frac{y_h}{n-h+1} + \sum_{h=1}^n \left(1-\frac{s}{n-h+1}\right)y_h $$
has nonpositive coefficients in the second sum for $h\geq k+1$. For $h\leq k$, use $y_h\leq1/n$. Therefore,
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{h=1}^n y_h &\leq \frac{s}{n} + \frac1n\sum_{h=1}^k \left(1-\frac{s}{n-h+1}\right)\\ &=1-\frac{s}{n}D_n =c_n. \end{aligned} $$

Welfare equals served mass. Averaging over the finitely many permutations produces one fixed instance with
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{SW}]\leq c_n. $$

Every randomized mechanism consequently has worst-case ratio at least $1/c_n$ on this family. In particular,
$$ c_2=\frac34, $$
giving a lower bound $4/3$ with $m=H=\tau=2$.

The limiting constant

Maximality gives
$$ D_n\leq1<D_n+\frac1{s_n}. $$

Integral comparison gives
$$ \log\frac{n+1}{s_n+1} \leq D_n \leq\log\frac n{s_n}. $$

The first inequality implies
$$ s_n+1\geq\frac{n+1}{e}. $$

Thus $s_n\to\infty$, and the maximality inequalities imply $D_n\to1$. The two logarithmic bounds differ by at most $1/s_n$, so
$$ \log\frac n{s_n}\longrightarrow1. $$

It follows that
$$ \frac{s_n}{n}\longrightarrow\frac1e, \qquad c_n\longrightarrow1-\frac1e, \qquad \frac1{c_n}\longrightarrow\frac e{e-1}. $$

These instances have homogeneous eligibility classes. Together with the matching upper bound proved above, this establishes the exact optimal uniform constant $e/(e-1)$ for that restricted family over unbounded parameter sizes.

The retained horizon bound and degenerate cases

There is also a separate mechanism with competitive ratio $H$.

For $H\geq1$, choose $K$ uniformly from $\{1,\ldots,H\}$ and serve only at batch $K$. At that batch, rank eligible agents by decreasing report and then increasing public label:
$$ R_z(x)= \mu\{y\in E:b(y)>z\} + \mu\{y\in E:b(y)=z,\ \ell(y)<\ell(x)\}. $$

Serve $x$ precisely when
$$ R_{b(x)}(x)<q. $$

Reject nonmeasurable profiles or profiles with infinite positive-mass report support. Rejection is singleton-invariant.

For accepted profiles, the rank lemma shows that the rule serves mass $\min\{q,\mu(E)\}$ in decreasing reported-value order. Thus it achieves the truthful single-batch welfare optimum.

Charge each winner
$$ p_x=\inf\{z\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}:R_z(x)<q\}. $$

Fixing others' reports, all masses in this formula are singleton-invariant. Also, for $z'>z$,
$$ R_{z'}(x)\leq\mu\{y\in E:b(y)>z\}\leq R_z(x). $$

The winning set is a nonempty upper set; reports above all positive-mass report levels have rank zero.
