Lemma A.1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a cake \(C=[0,1]\), a finite set of \(\tau\) piecewise-uniform valuation types with rational interval endpoints, and positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), compute the CC-PUV-style allocation. In each round, with active types \(A\) and available intervals \(X\), choose a nonempty \(U\subseteq A\) minimizing \(\rho(U,X)=\frac{\sum_{I\in D(U,X)}|I|}{\sum_{t\in U}\mu_t}\). For \(r=\rho(U,X)\), find a rational flow that assigns only desired intervals to types in \(U\), gives each type \(t\in U\) aggregate cake measure \(r\mu_t\), and then removes \(U\) and \(D(U,X)\) before the next round. Determine whether this can be done exactly in time polynomial in \(\tau\), the number of intervals per type, and the input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not all possible types.
The cake is \(C=[0,1]\). Type \(t\) specifies a positive-length union \(B_t\) of at most \(\ell\) disjoint intervals with binary-rational endpoints and has normalized density \(v_t(x)=1/|B_t|\) on \(B_t\), zero elsewhere. Endpoints have measure zero.
Let \(W\) be the positive-length elementary intervals induced by all support endpoints and \(0,1\), let \(m=|W|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length. For \(X\subseteq W\), define \(D(U,X)=\{I\in X:I\subseteq B_t\text{ for some }t\in U\}\).
Start with \(A=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) and \(X=W\). Each round chooses a nonempty \(U\subseteq A\) minimizing \(\rho(U,X)=\bigl(\sum_{I\in D(U,X)}|I|\bigr)/\bigl(\sum_{t\in U}\mu_t\bigr)\), and sets \(r=\rho(U,X)\).
Allocate rational lengths \(f_{I,t}\geq0\) satisfying \(f_{I,t}=0\) unless \(I\subseteq B_t\), \(\sum_{t\in U}f_{I,t}=|I|\) for every \(I\in D(U,X)\), and \(\sum_{I\in D(U,X)}f_{I,t}=r\mu_t\) for every \(t\in U\). Realize these lengths as disjoint rational-endpoint subintervals.
Replace \(A\) by \(A\setminus U\) and \(X\) by \(X\setminus D(U,X)\), without renormalizing masses, until \(A=\varnothing\). Any minimizing subset and feasible allocation are permitted; cake desired by no type is discarded. The output consists of the selected subsets and allocations in all rounds.

Can this exact allocation problem be solved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,\ell,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many equal-weight agents, flow feasibility at a common quota \(c\geq0\) is equivalent to every nonempty subset having average demand at least \(c\); the resulting recursive allocation is polynomial-time computable ([Chen et al.](https://procaccia.info/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/justruth.pdf)).
Variable-claim CCEA and polynomial-time variable-claim market allocations are nearby results ([Aziz and Ye](https://arxiv.org/html/1307.2908)); no source verified here explicitly settles the exact weighted round-sequence problem above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required each valuation support to have positive total length and normalized its density, as required by the discrete valuation domain.
  • Defined the available intervals using the common endpoint partition, omitted zero-length cells, and treated endpoint ownership as immaterial.
  • Kept the original masses throughout recursion rather than renormalizing active masses.
  • Required every selected desired interval to be fully allocated, with disjoint pieces assigned only to types desiring them.
  • Allowed arbitrary minimizing subsets and feasible allocations, matching the original mechanism's arbitrary tie-breaking.
  • Specified rational subinterval allocations and the round sequence as the output, and permitted disposal of universally undesired cake.
  • Defined m as the number of elementary intervals, ell as the maximum number of support intervals per type, and L as total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact polynomial-time solvability

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,\ell,L\). We prove this by giving a flow algorithm that finds an exact minimizing subset in every round and produces rational allocations of polynomial bit length.

The argument extends the flow method in [Chen et al., Sections 3.3–3.4](https://procaccia.info/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/justruth.pdf). The weighted minimization and bit-complexity arguments below are proved directly.

1. Integer representations and separation of ratios

Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of all input interval endpoints, and let \(H\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses. These integers have bit length \(O(L)\), even when “binary-rational” means arbitrary rationals represented in binary.

For each elementary interval \(I\in W\) and each type \(t\), write

\( \displaystyle |I|=\frac{a_I}{Q},\qquad \mu_t=\frac{b_t}{H}, \)

where \(a_I,b_t\) are positive integers. Since the elementary intervals partition the cake up to endpoints and the masses sum to one,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{I\in W}a_I=Q,\qquad \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t=H. \)

Fix a round with nonempty active set \(A\) and remaining intervals \(X\). Define

\( \displaystyle \mu(U)=\sum_{t\in U}\mu_t,\qquad d_X(U)=\sum_{I\in D(U,X)}|I|. \)

For nonempty \(U\subseteq A\), set

\( \displaystyle a_U=\sum_{I\in D(U,X)}a_I,\qquad b_U=\sum_{t\in U}b_t. \)

Then

\( \displaystyle \rho(U,X)=\frac{H a_U}{Q b_U},\qquad 0\leq a_U\leq Q,\qquad 1\leq b_U\leq H. \)

Put \(K=QH\). Every possible ratio, in every round, therefore has reduced numerator and denominator at most \(K\).

Consequently, any two distinct possible ratios differ by at least \(1/K^2\). Indeed, if they are \(p/q\) and \(p'/q'\) in reduced form, then

\( \displaystyle \left|\frac pq-\frac{p'}{q'}\right|=\frac{|pq'-p'q|}{qq'}\geq\frac1{K^2}, \)

because the nonzero integer numerator has absolute value at least one.

This separation bound will let us extract an exact minimizer using only polynomially many flow computations.

2. The weighted flow criterion

For a rational \(c\in[0,H+1]\), construct a directed network with source \(s\), sink \(z\), a vertex for each \(t\in A\), and a vertex for each \(I\in X\). Its arcs and capacities are:

  • \(s\to t\), of capacity \(c\mu_t\);
  • \(t\to I\), of capacity \(M=H+3\), whenever \(I\subseteq B_t\);
  • \(I\to z\), of capacity \(|I|\).

Let \(F(c)\) denote its maximum-flow value.

Lemma. The following conditions are equivalent:

\( \displaystyle F(c)=c\mu(A), \)

and

\( \displaystyle d_X(U)\geq c\mu(U)\quad\text{for every nonempty }U\subseteq A. \)

Proof. The cut whose source side consists only of \(s\) has capacity \(c\mu(A)\leq H+1<M\). Thus no minimum cut crosses an arc of capacity \(M\).

For any minimum cut, let \(U\subseteq A\) and \(Y\subseteq X\) be its type vertices and interval vertices on the source side. Since no incidence arc crosses the cut, \(Y\supseteq D(U,X)\). Its capacity is therefore at least

\( \displaystyle c\mu(A\setminus U)+d_X(U). \)

Conversely, for every \(U\subseteq A\), the source side

\( \displaystyle \{s\}\cup U\cup D(U,X) \)

defines a cut of exactly that capacity. With the conventions \(\mu(\varnothing)=d_X(\varnothing)=0\), the minimum-cut value is consequently

\( \displaystyle c\mu(A)+\min_{U\subseteq A}\bigl(d_X(U)-c\mu(U)\bigr). \)

The minimum in this expression is at most zero, because the empty subset gives zero. By the max-flow/min-cut theorem, \(F(c)=c\mu(A)\) precisely when every term is nonnegative. This is the stated condition. \(\square\)

Define the exact minimum ratio

\( \displaystyle r_*=\min_{\varnothing\neq U\subseteq A}\rho(U,X). \)

The lemma proves that

\( \displaystyle F(c)=c\mu(A)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c\leq r_*. \)

Moreover,

\( \displaystyle 0\leq r_*\leq\frac{d_X(A)}{\mu(A)}\leq H, \)

because \(d_X(A)\leq1\) and \(\mu(A)\geq1/H\).

3. Finding an exact minimizing subset

Start with the feasible lower bound \(l=0\) and the infeasible upper bound \(h=H+1\). Repeatedly test \(c=(l+h)/2\) using the flow criterion:

  • If \(F(c)=c\mu(A)\), replace \(l\) by \(c\).
  • Otherwise, replace \(h\) by \(c\).

The invariant is

\( \displaystyle l\leq r_*<h. \)

Stop after enough iterations that

\( \displaystyle h-l<\frac1{K^2}. \)

Starting from width \(H+1\), this requires only

\( \displaystyle O\bigl(\log(H+1)+\log K\bigr)=O(L) \)

iterations.

Now compute a minimum cut at \(c=h\), and let \(U\) be its source-side type vertices. Since \(h>r_*\), its capacity is strictly less than \(h\mu(A)\).

No capacity-\(M\) arc crosses this cut. Its source-side interval set is exactly \(D(U,X)\): any additional interval vertex could be moved to the sink side, decreasing the cut capacity by its strictly positive length without introducing an incidence arc across the cut.

Thus the strict cut inequality is

\( \displaystyle h\mu(A\setminus U)+d_X(U)<h\mu(A), \)

or equivalently,

\( \displaystyle d_X(U)<h\mu(U). \)

This also shows that \(U\neq\varnothing\). Hence

\( \displaystyle r_*\leq\rho(U,X)<h. \)

Suppose \(\rho(U,X)>r_*\). Both quantities are possible subset ratios, so the separation bound gives

\( \displaystyle \rho(U,X)-r_*\geq\frac1{K^2}. \)

But the maintained bounds give

\( \displaystyle \rho(U,X)-r_*<h-r_*\leq h-l<\frac1{K^2}, \)

a contradiction. Therefore

\( \displaystyle \rho(U,X)=r_*. \)

We have found an exact nonempty minimizing subset. This extraction works for any minimum cut at the final upper bound; no particular tie-breaking rule is needed.

4. Constructing the required allocation

Let \(U\) be the selected minimizer, put \(r=\rho(U,X)\), and write \(D=D(U,X)\).

Construct the same network using only types in \(U\), intervals in \(D\), and quota \(c=r\). For every nonempty \(S\subseteq U\),

\( \displaystyle d_X(S)\geq r\mu(S), \)

by minimality of \(U\). Also \(D(S,X)\subseteq D\), so restricting the interval set to \(D\) preserves every such demand length. The weighted flow criterion therefore gives a flow of value \(r\mu(U)\).

By the definition of \(r\),

\( \displaystyle r\mu(U)=d_X(U)=\sum_{I\in D}|I|. \)

This value equals both the sum of the source-arc capacities and the sum of the sink-arc capacities. Consequently, every source arc and every sink arc is saturated.

For \(I\in D\) and \(t\in U\), define \(f_{I,t}\) as the flow on \(t\to I\), taking it to be zero when that arc is absent. Conservation and saturation give

\( \displaystyle \sum_{I\in D}f_{I,t}=r\mu_t\quad(t\in U), \)

and

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in U}f_{I,t}=|I|\quad(I\in D). \)

An incidence arc exists only when \(I\subseteq B_t\), so all required allocation constraints hold.

For each \(I\in D\), order the types in \(U\) and lay out consecutive pieces of lengths \(f_{I,t}\) from its left endpoint. These pieces fill \(I\), with disjoint interiors. A consistent half-open endpoint convention makes them disjoint; endpoint choices do not affect any valuation.

If \(r=0\), then \(d_X(U)=0\), and positivity of elementary-interval lengths implies \(D=\varnothing\). The zero allocation satisfies the same constraints.

5. Exact arithmetic, output size, and recursion

Flow computations have polynomial bit complexity

Each network has at most \(m+\tau+2\) vertices and \(m\tau+m+\tau\) arcs.

After \(j\) bisection iterations, every tested quota has a denominator dividing \(2^j\). All capacities can therefore be scaled to integers using the common denominator

\( \displaystyle 2^jHQ. \)

Since \(j=O(L)\) and \(Q,H\) have \(O(L)\) bits, the resulting integer capacities also have \(O(L)\) bits. An integer max-flow algorithm with a polynomial bound independent of capacity magnitudes, such as Edmonds–Karp, computes the flow and a minimum cut in polynomial bit time.

Allocations and cut points have polynomial bit length

For the selected subset \(U\),

\( \displaystyle r=\frac{H a_U}{Q b_U},\qquad r\mu_t=\frac{a_Ub_t}{Qb_U}. \)

Thus all allocation-network capacities become integers when multiplied by

\( \displaystyle \Delta=Qb_U\leq QH. \)

Computing an integral maximum flow in this scaled network gives each allocation length in the form

\( \displaystyle f_{I,t}=\frac{k_{I,t}}{\Delta},\qquad 0\leq k_{I,t}\leq\Delta. \)

These numbers have \(O(L)\) bits. Original interval endpoints have denominators dividing \(Q\), and \(Q\) divides \(\Delta\). Therefore the cut points formed by adding allocation lengths also have \(O(L)\)-bit representations.

The entire round sequence is polynomial

After producing the allocation, update exactly as specified:

\( \displaystyle A\leftarrow A\setminus U,\qquad X\leftarrow X\setminus D(U,X). \)

The original masses remain unchanged. The preceding arguments apply to every residual pair \(A,X\), so correctness follows inductively through all rounds.

Every round removes at least one type, giving at most \(\tau\) rounds. Each round uses \(O(L)\) flow tests, one final minimum-cut computation, and one allocation-flow computation.

Every elementary interval is allocated in at most one round and contributes at most \(\tau\) positive pieces. Thus there are at most \(m\tau\) output pieces, each with polynomial bit length. Zero entries can be specified implicitly.

Finally, sorting endpoints and computing all incidences \(I\subseteq B_t\) require polynomial time in \(m,\tau,\ell,L\), using exact rational comparisons.

Hence the algorithm computes a permitted complete round sequence and its exact rational allocations in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,\ell,L\).
