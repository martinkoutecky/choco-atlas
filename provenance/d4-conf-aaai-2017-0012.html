The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\) of \(\tau\) complete doctor types with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\), and a strict preference-comparison oracle over fractional accepted-mass vectors, determine the exact oracle-query complexity of computing the hospital’s unique choice \(C(\mu)=\max_{\succ}\{x:0\le x\le\mu\}\), for preferences whose rejected mass is coordinatewise nondecreasing as supply increases.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) doctor types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; types distinguish all attributes relevant to hospital preferences.
Consider one hospital, so \(m=1\), and let \(L\) be the total bit length of the support descriptions and masses. Accepted-mass vectors range over \(B(\mu)=\{x\in\mathbb R^T:0\leq x\leq\mu\}\), with coordinatewise inequalities.
The hospital has a strict total order \(\succ\) on \(B(\mu)\). For every supply \(s\in B(\mu)\), assume the maximum \(C(s)=\max_{\succ}\{x:0\leq x\leq s\}\) exists; additionally, \(C(s)\) is rational whenever \(s\) is rational.
Substitutability means \(s-C(s)\leq s'-C(s')\) whenever \(0\leq s\leq s'\leq\mu\). A comparison query supplies distinct binary-rational vectors \(x,y\in B(\mu)\) and returns which is preferred; no other preference access is available.
Compute \(C(\mu)\) exactly as a binary-rational vector. Use deterministic algorithms, with no imposed denominator bound on queries or choices; measure oracle calls separately from bit computation, and express bounds in \(m,\tau,L\).

What is the optimal worst-case comparison-query complexity of computing \(C(\mu)\): is it polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or necessarily superpolynomial, possibly with no finite uniform bound?

For \(n\) discrete doctors, strict substitutable preferences can require \(\Omega(2^n)\) oracle queries; an additional oracle deciding whether \(C(A)=A\) permits \(O(n^3)\) verification queries and \(O(n^2)\) comparisons. [Source](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/10595/10454).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total mass to one, following the distribution-over-types formulation; intermediate supplies need not have total mass one.
  • Treated complete doctor types as labels distinguishing every attribute relevant to the hospital; no additional matching-market data enter this single-hospital choice problem, and m=1.
  • Required a strict total order and attainment on every supply box, since strictness alone does not ensure a maximum on an infinite domain.
  • Retained real fractional feasible vectors but specified binary-rational comparison queries and promised rational choices at rational supplies, making exact binary output well-defined.
  • Specified deterministic worst-case query complexity, without introducing an unstated denominator bound or continuity assumption.
  • Used increasing rejection as supply increases, consistent with the discrete acceptance definition and its proof; the printed equivalent rejection inclusion has its direction reversed.

Result

There is no finite uniform comparison-query bound, already for fixed \(m=\tau=1\) and fixed input length \(L\).

More strongly, no deterministic comparison-oracle algorithm can be guaranteed to terminate with the exact answer on every admissible preference order. This holds even for an explicit family of single-peaked preferences with rational peaks.

1. An admissible family with fixed support

Fix one doctor type with mass \(\mu_1=1\). Thus \(B(\mu)=[0,1]\), and the support description and its bit length \(L=L_0\) remain fixed throughout.

For each dyadic rational \(a\in(0,1)\), define preferences by lexicographically comparing the pairs
\(\bigl(-(x-a)^2,-x\bigr)\), with larger pairs preferred.

Equivalently, the hospital prefers the amount closer to \(a\); when two distinct amounts are equally close, it prefers the smaller amount.

This defines a strict total order on all of \([0,1]\): lexicographic order is transitive and total, and distinct amounts have distinct second coordinates.

Claim. For every real supply \(s\in[0,1]\), the unique choice is
\(C_a(s)=\min\{s,a\}\).

Proof. If \(s<a\), then for \(0\leq x<y\leq s\), we have \(a-x>a-y\), so \(y\) has strictly greater first score than \(x\). Hence \(s\) is the unique maximum. If \(s\geq a\), the feasible amount \(a\) has first score zero, whereas every other feasible amount has strictly negative first score. Hence \(a\) is the unique maximum. This also covers \(s=a\). ∎

Consequently, maxima exist for every real supply, and choices are rational whenever supplies are rational.

Substitutability also holds. Indeed, the rejected mass is
\(s-C_a(s)=\max\{s-a,0\}\), which is nondecreasing in \(s\). Thus
\(s-C_a(s)\leq s'-C_a(s')\) whenever \(s\leq s'\).

Every member of this family therefore satisfies every preference promise in the task, and its target answer is \(C_a(1)=a\).

2. What a comparison reveals

Consider any legal query involving distinct amounts \(x,y\). Write
\(\ell=\min\{x,y\}\), \(h=\max\{x,y\}\), and \(q=(\ell+h)/2\).

The difference between their first scores is
\(-( \ell-a)^2+(h-a)^2=(h-\ell)(h+\ell-2a)\).

Since \(h-\ell>0\), the smaller amount has the greater first score exactly when \(a<q\). When \(a=q\), the first scores agree and the prescribed tie-breaking prefers the smaller amount. Therefore,

\(\ell\succ_a h\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\leq q.\)

Thus a comparison distinguishes \(a\leq q\) from \(a>q\). It does not distinguish equality from being slightly below the threshold. This remains true regardless of the denominators or bit lengths of the queried amounts.

3. Finite transcripts cannot certify the answer

Suppose, for contradiction, that a deterministic algorithm terminates and returns the exact choice on every admissible oracle.

Run it on the fixed support above with parameter \(a_0=1/2\). By the assumed guarantee, it terminates after finitely many comparisons, say \(k\), with corresponding thresholds \(q_1,\ldots,q_k\). It must output \(1/2\).

Define
\(\lambda=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{q_i:q_i<a_0,\ 1\leq i\leq k\}\bigr)\).

The set is finite, and every element is strictly below \(a_0\). Hence \(\lambda<a_0\).

Choose an integer \(N\geq2\) such that \(2^{-N}<a_0-\lambda\), and put
\(b=a_0-2^{-N}\). Then \(b\) is dyadic rational and
\(0\leq\lambda<b<a_0<1\).

Every comparison in the recorded execution receives the same answer under parameters \(a_0\) and \(b\):

  • If \(q_i<a_0\), then \(q_i\leq\lambda<b<a_0\). Both parameters exceed the threshold, so both oracles prefer the larger queried amount.
  • If \(q_i\geq a_0\), then \(b<a_0\leq q_i\). Both parameters are at most the threshold, so both oracles prefer the smaller queried amount.

To account for adaptivity, proceed inductively through the execution. The support inputs are identical. After identical answers to the first \(i-1\) queries, determinism gives the same internal state and the same \(i\)-th query. The preceding calculation gives the same answer to that query. Therefore the complete execution, including termination and output, is identical.

The algorithm consequently outputs \(1/2\) under parameter \(b\). But the correct answer there is \(C_b(1)=b\neq1/2\), a contradiction. ∎

4. Complexity consequence

The obstruction is stronger than an unbounded finite worst-case query requirement: a universally correct deterministic algorithm cannot have a terminating execution even at \(a_0=1/2\) within this family.

Accordingly, no finite function of \(m,\tau,L\) bounds the comparison queries needed for guaranteed exact recovery. Unlimited finite bit computation and arbitrarily long query encodings cannot overcome the indistinguishability proof.

This is a separate obstruction from the finite-domain exponential lower bound in [Deng, Panigrahi, and Waggoner](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/10595/10454). Here the support is fixed and contains only one type; the proof verifies attainment, rationality, strictness, and substitutability directly.
