The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) goods, \(q\), an explicitly represented finite set of buyer laws \(D_t\) over normalized monotone \(q\)-partitioning valuations, rational type masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), and a target \(\alpha\), does there exist a nonnegative static price vector whose worst-case expected welfare is at least \(\alpha\,\mathrm{OPT}\) in the following fluid market? Mass \(\mu_t\) of buyers has law \(D_t\); realized valuation mass arrives in any measurable order, each individual receives a whole bundle maximizing value minus posted price, and at stock depletion mass may be split only among utility-maximizing bundles. Here \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is the first-best measurable assignment of whole bundles subject to the per-capita supplies. Determine whether such prices exist and, when they do, find them; in particular, establish whether \(\alpha=\Omega(\log\log q/\log\log m\))\) is achievable.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge2\) and \(2\le q\le m\) be integers. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) buyer-law types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each law \(D_t\) explicitly lists finitely many valuations and binary-rational probabilities; each valuation is given by its complete table of nonnegative rational values, is monotone, and satisfies \(v(\varnothing)=0\). Let \(K\) count the listed valuation entries across laws and \(L\) be the total input bit length; measure running times in \(m,\tau,K,q,L\).
A valuation is \(q\)-partitioning if, for every \(S\subseteq[m]\), partition \((S_1,\ldots,S_q)\) into possibly empty parts, and weights \(\lambda_T\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{T\ni i}\lambda_T\ge1\) for every \(i\in[q]\), it satisfies \(v(S)\le\sum_{T\subseteq[q]}\lambda_Tv(\bigcup_{i\in T}S_i)\). All listed valuations are promised to satisfy this property.
The atomless population has valuation mass \(\mu_tD_t(v)\) for each pair \((t,v)\), and good \(j\) has rational supply \(0<b_j\le1\). These masses are fixed, rather than random aggregate draws.
An arrival order is any measurable parametrization of this population by \([0,1]\) preserving its masses. Static prices \(p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\) charge \(p(S)=\sum_{j\in S}p_j\); at each arrival, a buyer receives a whole bundle maximizing \(v(S)-p(S)\) among subsets of goods with positive residual supply, including the empty bundle.
Supply decreases by allocated buyer mass. At depletion, exhausted goods become unavailable and demand is recomputed; mass may be split among tied utility-maximizing bundles, but individual goods or bundles are never fractionally assigned to a buyer. All measurable tie-breaking choices are adversarial.
Let \(W(p)\) be the infimum of total valuation welfare over arrival orders and these choices. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}>0\) be the maximum welfare of a measurable whole-bundle assignment satisfying the supplies, without utility-maximization constraints. For rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), prices are \(\alpha\)-competitive when \(W(p)\ge\alpha\mathrm{OPT}\).
Write \(r(m,q)=(1+\log_2\log_2 q)/(1+\log_2\log_2 m)\).
Is an \(\Omega(r(m,q))\) welfare guarantee universally achievable by static prices in this fluid market, or is such a guarantee impossible?
If achievable, can prices attaining this guarantee be computed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,q,L\)?
For finite buyers with independently drawn \(q\)-partitioning valuations and one copy per good, static posted prices attain the corresponding double-logarithmic guarantee under adversarial arrival order, including \(\Omega(1/(1+\log\log m))\) at \(q=2\). [Bangachev–Weinberg](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol334-icalp2025/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.18/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.18.pdf).
For finite fractionally subadditive buyers, static prices attain half the optimal expected welfare. [Feldman–Gravin–Lucier](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.4916). No result settling this exact fluid-market guarantee was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted explicit buyer laws as finite rational probability lists over complete rational valuation tables; introduced K and total encoding length L.
  • Normalized total buyer mass to one and interpreted each law as prescribing exact realized valuation proportions, so welfare is an integral rather than an expectation over unspecified aggregate randomness.
  • Required positive supplies for every listed good, reflecting the discrete model's initially available goods; capped per-capita supplies at one because each buyer receives at most one copy of each good.
  • Made arrival orders measure-preserving measurable parametrizations, specified availability by positive residual supply, and required demand to be recomputed after depletion.
  • Settled unspecified tie-breaking adversarially; splitting distributes buyers among utility-maximizing whole bundles and does not fractionalize an individual's allocation.
  • Restricted alpha to rational values in the unit interval and excluded zero-optimum instances, where every nonnegative welfare guarantee is vacuous.
  • Used an endpoint-safe double-logarithmic ratio with added ones, consistent with the stated nonzero guarantee at q=2 and constant guarantee at q=m.

Result

The guarantee is universally achievable, and suitable prices can be computed in polynomial time under the stated complete-table input model.

More precisely, there is a polynomial-time algorithm producing nonnegative rational static prices satisfying

\( \displaystyle W(p)\ge \frac{r(m,q)}{384}\,\mathrm{OPT}. \)

The proof is direct for the fluid market. Its local thinning argument adapts the fractional-cover idea in Proposition 18 of [Bangachev–Weinberg](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol334-icalp2025/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.18/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.18.pdf). The passage from that argument to fluid prices is established below.

1. The offline optimum is a rational linear program

Index the positive-probability valuation occurrences across all laws by \(a\in[n]\), where \(n\le K\). Write their valuations as \(v_a\) and their population masses as \(\rho_a\). Thus \(\rho_a=\mu_tD_t(v_a)\) for the corresponding occurrence, and \(\sum_a\rho_a=1\).

Consider the configuration linear program with variables \(x_{a,S}\), representing the mass of population \(a\) assigned the whole bundle \(S\):

\( \displaystyle \max \sum_{a,S}x_{a,S}v_a(S), \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle x_{a,S}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{S\subseteq[m]}x_{a,S}=\rho_a\quad(a\in[n]), \)

and

\( \displaystyle \sum_{a,S:\,j\in S}x_{a,S}\le b_j\quad(j\in[m]). \)

Every feasible measurable assignment induces a feasible solution with the same welfare. Conversely, because each population class is atomless, it can be measurably partitioned into pieces of prescribed masses \(x_{a,S}\), each receiving the whole bundle \(S\). The supply inequalities make this assignment feasible.

Consequently, the LP optimum equals \(\mathrm{OPT}\). Fix a rational optimal solution \(x\).

2. A local thinning inequality

For \(S\subseteq[m]\) and \(t\in[0,1]\), let \(\Delta_S(t)\) be the distributions over subsets of \(S\) whose inclusion probability for each item is at most \(t\). Define

\( \displaystyle f_{a,S}(t)=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta_S(t)}\mathbb E_{R\sim\lambda}[v_a(R)]. \)

These maxima exist because the feasible sets are finite-dimensional compact polytopes.

Set

\( \displaystyle k=\lfloor\log_2q\rfloor,\qquad \gamma=\max\left\{2,\,1+\left\lfloor\frac{k}{4}\right\rfloor\right\}. \)

We prove the following inequality.

Thinning lemma. Let \(0<t\le1/16\), and let \(\lambda\) attain \(f_{a,S}(t)\). For every distribution \(\nu\in\Delta_{[m]}(t)\), independently sampling \(R\sim\lambda\) and \(T\sim\nu\) gives

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E[v_a(R\setminus T)]\ge \frac12\bigl(f_{a,S}(t)-f_{a,S}(t^\gamma)\bigr). \)

The case \(k\le3\)

First, the partitioning promise implies subadditivity on disjoint sets: use the two sets as partition parts, pad with empty parts, and give each part’s singleton index weight one.

Thus

\( \displaystyle v_a(R)\le v_a(R\setminus T)+v_a(R\cap T). \)

Independence gives, for each item \(j\),

\( \displaystyle \Pr(j\in R\cap T)=\Pr(j\in R)\Pr(j\in T)\le t^2. \)

The distribution of \(R\cap T\) therefore belongs to \(\Delta_S(t^2)\). Taking expectations yields

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E[v_a(R\setminus T)]\ge f_{a,S}(t)-f_{a,S}(t^2). \)

Here \(\gamma=2\). Since \(f_{a,S}\) is nondecreasing, this proves the claimed inequality.

The case \(k\ge4\)

Sample \(T_1,\ldots,T_k\) independently from \(\nu\), independently of \(R\). Their membership patterns partition \(R\) into \(2^k\le q\) parts. Let

\( \displaystyle B=\left\{j\in R:\left|\{i:j\in T_i\}\right|>\frac{k}{2}\right\}. \)

Give weight \(2/k\) to each union \(R\setminus T_i\), and weight one to \(B\). Every partition part outside \(B\) occurs in at least \(k/2\) of the complements, so its covering weight is at least one. Every part inside \(B\) is covered by \(B\). Any additional empty partition parts can be covered by their singleton indices at zero valuation cost.

The partitioning promise therefore gives

\( \displaystyle v_a(R)\le \frac{2}{k}\sum_{i=1}^k v_a(R\setminus T_i)+v_a(B). \)

For a fixed item \(j\), independence across the samples \(T_i\) and a union bound give

\( \displaystyle \Pr\left(\left|\{i:j\in T_i\}\right|>\frac{k}{2}\right)\le 2^k t^{k/2}. \)

Independence from \(R\) consequently gives

\( \displaystyle \Pr(j\in B)\le t\,2^k t^{k/2}\le t^{1+k/4}\le t^\gamma. \)

The middle inequality follows from \(t\le1/16\), which implies \(2^kt^{k/4}\le1\). The last follows from \(\gamma=1+\lfloor k/4\rfloor\le1+k/4\).

Hence the distribution of \(B\) belongs to \(\Delta_S(t^\gamma)\). Taking expectations in the fractional-cover inequality gives

\( \displaystyle f_{a,S}(t)\le 2\mathbb E[v_a(R\setminus T)]+f_{a,S}(t^\gamma), \)

which proves the lemma.

3. One common scale works for the whole society

Define the rational scales

\( \displaystyle t_i=16^{-\gamma^i}\quad(i\ge0), \)

and let \(N\) be the smallest integer satisfying

\( \displaystyle t_N\le\frac{1}{32m}. \)

In particular, \(N\ge1\) and \(t_{i+1}=t_i^\gamma\).

Aggregate the local optima using the offline allocation:

\( \displaystyle F(t)=\sum_{a,S}x_{a,S}f_{a,S}(t). \)

For each pair \((a,S)\), selecting \(S\) with probability \(1/16\) and the empty set otherwise shows that

\( \displaystyle f_{a,S}(t_0)\ge\frac{v_a(S)}{16}. \)

Also, any distribution in \(\Delta_S(t_N)\) satisfies

\( \displaystyle \Pr(R\ne\varnothing)\le |S|t_N\le mt_N. \)

By monotonicity and normalization,

\( \displaystyle f_{a,S}(t_N)\le mt_Nv_a(S)\le\frac{v_a(S)}{32}. \)

Multiplying by \(x_{a,S}\) and summing gives

\( \displaystyle F(t_0)\ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{16},\qquad F(t_N)\le\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{32}. \)

Thus telescoping yields

\( \displaystyle \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac12\bigl(F(t_i)-F(t_{i+1})\bigr)\ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{64}. \)

Choose an index \(i\) maximizing these differences, and put \(t=t_i\). Then

\( \displaystyle B_t:=\frac12\bigl(F(t)-F(t^\gamma)\bigr)\ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{64N}. \)

For every \((a,S)\), fix a distribution \(\lambda_{a,S}\) attaining \(f_{a,S}(t)\). Define, for \(D\subseteq[m]\),

\( \displaystyle H(D)=\sum_{a,S}x_{a,S}\mathbb E_{R\sim\lambda_{a,S}}[v_a(R\setminus D)]. \)

Applying the thinning lemma separately to every pair and summing shows that, for every \(\nu\in\Delta_{[m]}(t)\),

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E_{D\sim\nu}[H(D)]\ge B_t. \)

The shared scale is essential: this inequality holds simultaneously across the fixed population masses.

4. A dual certificate produces prices

Consider the finite LP

\( \displaystyle Z_t=\min_{\nu\in\Delta_{[m]}(t)}\sum_{D\subseteq[m]}\nu_DH(D). \)

The preceding section proves \(Z_t\ge B_t\). Its dual is

\( \displaystyle \max_{z\in\mathbb R,\;c\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m}\left(z-t\sum_jc_j\right), \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle H(D)+\sum_{j\in D}c_j\ge z\quad(D\subseteq[m]). \)

The primal is feasible and compact, with finite objective values. Finite-dimensional LP strong duality therefore supplies an optimal rational dual solution satisfying

\( \displaystyle z-t\sum_jc_j=Z_t\ge B_t. \)

Define the static prices by

\( \displaystyle p_j=\frac{c_j}{b_j}. \)

These prices are well defined and nonnegative because every \(b_j\) is strictly positive.

5. The certificate bounds every fluid execution

Fix any allowed arrival order and measurable adversarial tie-breaking choices. Let \(d_j\le b_j\) be the total mass allocated good \(j\), and define

\( \displaystyle D=\{j:d_j=b_j\},\qquad A=[m]\setminus D. \)

Every good in \(A\) has positive residual supply at the end, so it was available throughout the execution.

Let \(U\) denote total realized utility. A buyer with valuation \(v_a\) chooses a utility-maximizing bundle. For every comparison bundle \(R\), the bundle \(R\setminus D\) is always available. Therefore the buyer’s realized utility is at least

\( \displaystyle v_a(R\setminus D)-p(R\setminus D)\ge v_a(R\setminus D)-p(R). \)

For each population class \(a\), average these inequalities using the distributions \(\lambda_{a,S}\) and the weights \(x_{a,S}/\rho_a\). Integrating over its mass \(\rho_a\), then summing over \(a\), gives

\( \displaystyle U\ge H(D)-\sum_jp_j\ell_j, \)

where

\( \displaystyle \ell_j=\sum_{a,S}x_{a,S}\Pr_{R\sim\lambda_{a,S}}(j\in R). \)

The marginal constraints and offline supply feasibility imply

\( \displaystyle \ell_j\le t\sum_{a,S:\,j\in S}x_{a,S}\le tb_j. \)

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle U\ge H(D)-t\sum_jc_j. \)

Let \(\mathrm{Rev}\) denote total payments. Fully depleted goods contribute their entire supplies at the posted prices, and all other payments are nonnegative. Hence

\( \displaystyle \mathrm{Rev}=\sum_jp_jd_j\ge\sum_{j\in D}p_jb_j=\sum_{j\in D}c_j. \)

Welfare equals utility plus payments. Using the dual constraint for this particular depleted set \(D\), we obtain

\( \displaystyle \mathrm{Welfare}\ge H(D)+\sum_{j\in D}c_j-t\sum_jc_j\ge z-t\sum_jc_j=Z_t\ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{64N}. \)

This holds for every permitted execution. Taking the infimum proves

\( \displaystyle W(p)\ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{64N}. \)

All distributions used above are comparison devices in the proof. The mechanism itself uses deterministic static prices and assigns whole bundles according to the stated rules.

6. Relating the bound to the requested ratio

Write

\( \displaystyle a=1+\log_2\log_2m,\qquad b=1+\log_2\log_2q. \)

Then \(a\ge b\ge1\) and \(r(m,q)=b/a\).

Since \(k=\lfloor\log_2q\rfloor\ge(\log_2q)/2\),

\( \displaystyle \gamma\ge2,\qquad \gamma\ge\frac{k}{4}\ge\frac{\log_2q}{8}. \)

It follows that

\( \displaystyle \log_2\gamma\ge\max\{1,\log_2\log_2q-3\}\ge\frac b5. \)

For the last inequality, put \(u=\log_2\log_2q\). If \(u\le4\), use \(1\ge(1+u)/5\); if \(u\ge4\), use \(u-3\ge(1+u)/5\).

The definition of \(N\) gives

\( \displaystyle N=\left\lceil\frac{\log_2((5+\log_2m)/4)}{\log_2\gamma}\right\rceil. \)

Because \(\log_2m\ge1\),

\( \displaystyle \log_2((5+\log_2m)/4)\le1+\log_2\log_2m=a. \)

Therefore

\( \displaystyle N\le1+\frac{5a}{b}\le\frac{6a}{b}. \)

Combining the bounds proves

\( \displaystyle W(p)\ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{64N}\ge\frac{b}{384a}\,\mathrm{OPT}=\frac{r(m,q)}{384}\,\mathrm{OPT}. \)

7. Polynomial-time computation

The proof gives an explicit algorithm:

  • Solve the offline configuration LP to obtain \(x\).
  • For every scale \(t_i\), compute the values \(f_{a,S}(t_i)\), and choose the index maximizing \(F(t_i)-F(t_{i+1})\).
  • Obtain maximizing distributions \(\lambda_{a,S}\) at the chosen scale.
  • Compute \(H(D)\) for every subset \(D\).
  • Solve the dual certificate LP and output \(p_j=c_j/b_j\).

Each local maximization defining \(f_{a,S}(t_i)\) is a rational LP with at most \(2^m\) variables and \(m+1\) constraints. The offline LP has \(n2^m\) variables. The final dual has \(m+1\) variables and \(2^m\) constraints.

Since the input contains a complete valuation table, \(2^m=O(L)\). With \(n\le K\), all these LP sizes, their number, and the direct computation of all values \(H(D)\) are polynomial in the required parameters.

The scales also have polynomial bit length. Minimality of \(N\) implies

\( \displaystyle \gamma^{N-1}<\log_{16}(32m),\qquad \gamma^N<\gamma\log_{16}(32m). \)

Since \(\gamma=O(1+\log m)\), every denominator \(16^{\gamma^i}\) used by the algorithm has bit length \(O((1+\log m)^2)\).

The input masses, supplies, and valuation entries are rational. Standard polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore produces optimal solutions with polynomial bit length. Forming the finite sums and products defining \(H(D)\), and dividing \(c_j\) by positive \(b_j\), preserves polynomial bit length and running time.

Thus the algorithm computes rational prices with the proved guarantee in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,q,L\).
