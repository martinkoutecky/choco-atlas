1. Reward, scaling, and the omniscient benchmark

1.1 Reward equals consumed role mass

For every template $c$,

$$ w_c=\sum_s a_{s,c}. $$

For a cycle, both sides count its pair roles. For a chain with $t$ pairs, the right side counts those pairs and its altruist, while the reward is $t+1$, including the waiting-list transplant.

Consequently, every feasible history satisfies

$$ \sum_{r,c}w_cy_c^r = \sum_s\sum_{r,c}a_{s,c}y_c^r \leq \sum_s q_s. $$

The last inequality follows from permanent consumption and the capacity constraint for each role. Thus losing $a$ units of role mass implies losing at least $a$ units relative to the total-mass upper bound.

1.2 Uniform scaling preserves every ratio

Let $MI$ be obtained by multiplying all masses by a positive rational $M$. The correspondence

$$ y_c^r\longmapsto My_c^r $$

is a bijection between feasible histories of $I$ and $MI$. Residual capacities and every stage objective are multiplied by $M$. Hence it preserves stagewise maximization, including ties, in both directions.

Taking the adversarial infimum therefore gives, for every fixed $p$,

$$ \operatorname{VAL}_V(MI,p)=M\operatorname{VAL}_V(I,p), $$

and unrestricted optimization gives

$$ \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{fluid}}(MI) = M\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{fluid}}(I). $$

Every ratio is consequently unchanged. In particular, scaling introduces no asymptotic approximation: the examples below work for every positive integer $M$, and hence as $M\to\infty$.

1.3 The benchmark is one linear program

The common availability interval of a template $c$ is nonempty exactly when

$$ \max_{s:a_{s,c}>0}\alpha_s \leq \min_{s:a_{s,c}>0}\beta_s. $$

Discard templates failing this test. The benchmark is the value of

$$ \max_{x\geq0} \left\{ \sum_c w_cx_c: \sum_c a_{s,c}x_c\leq q_s\quad\text{for every }s \right\}. $$

To prove this, first aggregate any feasible history by setting $x_c=\sum_r y_c^r$. Permanent consumption gives all the displayed capacity inequalities.

Conversely, take any feasible $x$ and schedule each template’s entire flow at one stage in its common availability interval. For every role, consumption through any stage is at most its total scheduled consumption, which is at most $q_s$. All scheduled roles are present when used. The resulting history is therefore feasible and has the same reward.

The benchmark is computable in time

$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log H,\log C). $$

This includes checking availability and solving the explicit rational linear program.

$$ x\leftrightarrow y,\qquad z\leftrightarrow w,\qquad y\to z. $$

At stage $1$, also include $k$ disjoint, expiring two-cycles with ABO types $X,W$, and $k$ disjoint, expiring two-cycles with ABO types $Y,Z$.

$$ w\to u_1\to u_2\to\cdots\to u_{2k}\to x. $$

Every arc respects ABO compatibility. For example, $y\to z$ is an $\mathrm A$ donor donating to an $\mathrm A$ patient, and $u_{2k}\to x$ is an $\mathrm{AB}$ donor donating to an $\mathrm{AB}$ patient.

The long cycle is

$$ x\to y\to z\to w\to u_1\to\cdots\to u_{2k}\to x, $$

of length $2k+4$.

Benchmark. Clearing all expiring cycles first and the long cycle second consumes every role. The total mass is $6k+4$, so Section 1.1 proves

$$ \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{fluid}}(I)=6k+4. $$

If $h_1\geq0$ or $h_2\geq0$, adversarial clearing can saturate the corresponding persistent two-cycle at stage $1$. This is consistent with maximization: the available cycles belong to independent two-cycle components, and the one-way arc $y\to z$ creates no additional stage-$1$ cycle.

$$ (6k+4)-2k=4k+4. $$

$$ \max_p \frac{\operatorname{VAL}_V(I,p)} {\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{fluid}}(I)} = \frac{4k+4}{6k+4}. $$

If repeated role occurrences are permitted, repeating an independent two-cycle merely multiplies its consumption and adjusted reward by the same factor. A closed walk containing a new role has length at least $2k+4$; under the cap it must be the displayed long cycle. The proof therefore covers that convention as well.

3. A $\mathrm{coNP}$-hardness reduction

3.1 Source problem and construction

Use the NP-complete threshold version of Maximum Cut on nonempty simple cubic graphs. The restricted hardness is established by [Alimonti and Kann](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/aspnes/pinewiki/attachments/MarkovChains/alimanti-mis-cubic.pdf).

Let $G=(V,E)$ have

$$ n=|V|,\qquad e=|E|=\frac{3n}{2}, $$

and let $\kappa(G)$ denote its maximum cut size. The source threshold is $1\leq K\leq e$.

Every constructed role has unit mass and ABO type $\mathrm O\!-\!\mathrm O$. Consequently, every specified arc is ABO-compatible. Set $H=2$, forbid chains, and set $\ell=6$.

For each vertex $v$, order its three incident edges as $f_1,f_2,f_3$. Create one stage-$1$-only hub $h_v$. For each $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$, create three persistent roles $t_{v,f_i}^{\varepsilon}$ and two stage-$1$-only blockers $b_{v,1}^{\varepsilon},b_{v,2}^{\varepsilon}$. Add

$$ h_v\to t_{v,f_1}^{\varepsilon}\to b_{v,1}^{\varepsilon} \to t_{v,f_2}^{\varepsilon}\to b_{v,2}^{\varepsilon} \to t_{v,f_3}^{\varepsilon}\to h_v. $$

These two six-cycles share only their hub. Their first-stage flows satisfy precisely

$$ x_v^0\geq0,\qquad x_v^1\geq0,\qquad x_v^0+x_v^1\leq1. $$

Totally order $V$. For each edge $f=\{u,v\}$ with $u<v$, and each $\varepsilon$, create a stage-$2$-only role $z_f^\varepsilon$ and add the triangle

$$ t_{u,f}^{\varepsilon}\to z_f^\varepsilon \to t_{v,f}^{\varepsilon}\to t_{u,f}^{\varepsilon}. $$

At stage $1$, every available inter-variable arc strictly decreases the vertex order. Such arcs cannot belong to a cycle traversing distinct variable components. Within a variable component, switching between its two branches would require a walk of length at least $12$. Therefore the cap-$6$ first-stage possibilities are exactly the two specified local cycles.

At stage $2$, hubs and blockers have expired. The remaining triangles are disjoint: each persistent endpoint role is indexed by one incident edge and one value of $\varepsilon$.

Finally, add $3e$ isolated, stage-$1$-only two-cycles. Their total reward is

$$ R=6e. $$

There are $11n$ variable-component roles, $2e=3n$ triangle roles, and $6e=9n$ isolated-cycle roles. Thus

$$ \tau=23n. $$

All templates can be listed by checking directed sequences of length at most $6$, requiring $O(\tau^6)$ candidate checks. The reduction has polynomial size. Repeated two-cycles or twice-traversed triangles, if allowed, merely duplicate their ordinary aggregate-flow possibilities.

3.2 Exact omniscient optimum

Put

$$ t_v=x_v^0+x_v^1,\qquad 0\leq t_v\leq1. $$

Variable cycles contribute $6\sum_v t_v$. For an edge $\{u,v\}$, its triangles contribute at most

$$ 3\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}} \min\{1-x_u^\varepsilon,1-x_v^\varepsilon\} \leq 3\left(2-\frac{t_u+t_v}{2}\right). $$

The inequality applies $\min\{a,b\}\leq(a+b)/2$ separately to the two terms.

Since the graph is cubic,

$$ \sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}(t_u+t_v)=3\sum_v t_v. $$

Every history therefore has reward at most

$$ R+6\sum_v t_v+6e-\frac92\sum_v t_v = R+6e+\frac32\sum_v t_v \leq R+7e. $$

Equality is feasible: clear every isolated cycle; use $x_v^0=x_v^1=1/2$ for every vertex; and clear every final triangle with flow $1/2$. Hence

$$ \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{fluid}}(I)=R+7e=13e. $$

3.3 Exact adversarial value

Let $p_0$ be the potential on $\mathrm O\!-\!\mathrm O$. Every early template has coefficient $w_c(1-p_0)$.

For $p_0<1$, early maximization is unadjusted reward maximization. It clears all isolated cycles and forces $t_v=1$ for every variable component. Write $x_v=x_v^0$, so $x_v^1=1-x_v$.

The final reward of an edge is exactly

$$ 3\bigl( \min\{1-x_u,1-x_v\} + \min\{x_u,x_v\} \bigr) = 3(1-|x_u-x_v|). $$

The equality follows by considering $x_u\leq x_v$ and $x_v\leq x_u$. Disjointness of the triangles makes their individual capacity bounds simultaneously attainable.

Using $6n=4e$, total reward is therefore

$$ R+7e-3\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}|x_u-x_v|. $$

For every $x\in[0,1]^V$, define $S_t=\{v:x_v>t\}$. An edge’s endpoints lie on opposite sides for a set of thresholds of length $|x_u-x_v|$. Thus

$$ \sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}|x_u-x_v| = \int_0^1|\delta(S_t)|\,dt \leq \kappa(G). $$

A binary characteristic vector of a maximum cut attains equality. Consequently,

$$ \operatorname{VAL}_V(I,p)=13e-3\kappa(G) \qquad\text{when }p_0<1. $$

For $p_0>1$, every nonempty early clearing has negative adjusted reward. Empty clearing is uniquely optimal, and the final stage clears all $2e$ triangles with unit flow, giving $6e$.

For $p_0=1$, empty early clearing is an adversarially available maximizer, so the value is at most $6e$.

Since $\kappa(G)\leq e$,

$$ 13e-3\kappa(G)\geq10e>6e. $$

It follows that

$$ \max_p\operatorname{VAL}_V(I,p)=13e-3\kappa(G). $$

3.4 A binary threshold and the reduction direction

To accommodate even an interpretation requiring a finite binary expansion, choose a dyadic threshold. Put

$$ \theta=13e-3(K-1),\qquad Q=2^{\lceil\log_2(13e)\rceil}, $$

and set

$$ \rho= \frac{\left\lfloor Q\theta/(13e)\right\rfloor}{Q}. $$

This lies in $[0,1]$ and has polynomial bit length. Moreover,

$$ \theta-3<\rho\,13e\leq\theta, $$

because rounding down changes $\theta$ by less than $13e/Q\leq1$.

The exact value formula now gives

$$ \exists p:\operatorname{VAL}_V(I,p) \geq\rho\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{fluid}}(I) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \kappa(G)\leq K-1. $$

Indeed, if $\kappa(G)\leq K-1$, the best value is at least $\theta$. If $\kappa(G)\geq K$, it is at most $\theta-3$, strictly below the chosen threshold.

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from the complement of cubic Maximum Cut. It proves $\mathrm{coNP}$-hardness, with the restrictions stated above.

4. Horizon compression and evaluation of fixed potentials

4.1 At most $2\tau+2$ stages suffice

Partition the pre-final stages into maximal intervals with constant active-role sets. There are at most $2\tau+1$ such intervals. Keep the final stage separate, and write

$$ h\leq\min\{H,2\tau+2\} $$

for the resulting number of stages.

Consider one constant-availability interval. Let its first maximizing clearing have adjusted value $v$. Any later feasible clearing can be added to it: together they consume no more than the residual mass at the interval’s start.

No later clearing can have positive adjusted value, since adding it would contradict the first clearing’s optimality. Every later maximizing clearing therefore has adjusted value zero, because empty clearing is feasible.

Aggregating the interval’s clearings into its first stage preserves feasibility, optimality, total reward, and the residual state at the interval’s end. Conversely, after that aggregated maximizing clearing, empty clearing is optimal throughout the remaining interval.

Thus compression preserves all attainable adversarial total rewards. It is computable by sorting the binary arrival and expiration events.

4.2 Exact evaluation by complementary-slackness branches

Fix rational $p$. At a stage, let $A$ be the available-template capacity matrix, $b$ the residual-capacity vector, and $f(p)$ the adjusted reward vector. The primal and dual are

$$ \max\{f(p)^{\mathsf T}y:Ay\leq b,\ y\geq0\}, $$

$$ \min\{b^{\mathsf T}\lambda: A^{\mathsf T}\lambda\geq f(p),\ \lambda\geq0\}. $$

The primal is nonempty because $y=0$ is feasible. It is bounded because each template consumes some positive amount of a finite-mass role. Linear-programming strong duality therefore applies.

Optimality is equivalent to primal and dual feasibility and

$$ y_c\bigl((A^{\mathsf T}\lambda)_c-f_c(p)\bigr)=0, $$

$$ \lambda_s\bigl(b_s-(Ay)_s\bigr)=0. $$

At the final stage replace $f(p)$ by $w$.

For each product, select one factor to equal zero. There are at most

$$ 2^{h(m+\tau)} $$

choices across the compressed horizon. After fixing a choice, every constraint is linear: each residual capacity is an affine function of earlier flows.

Minimize total reward on each feasible branch and take the smallest value. This computes $\operatorname{VAL}_V(I,p)$ in time

$$ 2^{h(m+\tau)} \operatorname{poly} \bigl(m,\tau,h,L,\operatorname{bits}(p),\log C\bigr). $$

For fixed $p$, each branch is a polyhedron. Its projection onto flow histories is also a polyhedron. These projections form a finite union, and all flow coordinates are bounded by initial masses. Hence the set of maximizing histories is compact, and the adversarial infimum is attained.

4.3 Explicit certificate bounds

We use the following elementary rational-polyhedron fact.

Fact. Let a polyhedron in $n$ variables have rational constraint entries of at most $B$ bits. Each vertex has coordinate numerator and denominator lengths

$$ O(n^2B+n\log n). $$

To see this, a vertex is determined by $n$ independent active constraints. Clearing denominators separately in those rows gives integer entries of $O(nB)$ bits. Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound then give the stated estimate.

A nonempty polyhedron contained in the nonnegative orthant, on which a linear objective attains its minimum, has an optimal vertex. For completeness, minimize the sum of coordinates on its optimal face. A sublevel set bounded by the sum at any fixed feasible point is compact. The resulting minimum face is a nonempty polytope and has an extreme point. Since it is a face of a face of the original polyhedron, that point is a vertex of the original polyhedron.

These observations apply to each complementary-slackness branch: all flow and dual variables are nonnegative, and the reward minimum is attained by the projection argument above. A minimizing branch consequently has a rational solution of polynomial bit length.

Therefore, if

$$ \operatorname{VAL}_V(I,p)<T, $$

polynomial-length rational flows and dual variables certify a maximizing history whose total reward is below $T$. Exact rational arithmetic verifies feasibility, complementary slackness, and the strict reward inequality in polynomial time.

The threshold test for a supplied rational potential thus belongs to $\mathrm{coNP}$.

5.1 Capacity-independent optimality cones

In particular, all pre-final objectives are homogeneous in $u$.

For a feasible stage flow $y$, let $T$ be its set of tight capacity rows, and let $Z$ be its set of zero-flow coordinates. Complementary slackness says that $y$ is maximizing exactly when $u$ belongs to

$$ \begin{aligned} K_{T,Z}=\{u:\ &\exists\lambda\geq0,\quad \lambda_s=0\ \text{for }s\notin T,\\ &A^{\mathsf T}\lambda\geq D^{\mathsf T}u,\\ &(A^{\mathsf T}\lambda)_c=(D^{\mathsf T}u)_c \ \text{for }c\notin Z\}. \end{aligned} $$

Crucially, this cone depends on $A,D,T,Z$, and not on residual capacities. Over all compressed pre-final stages, there are at most

$$ h\,2^{m+\tau} $$

such cones.

5.2 Integer descriptions of the cones

Remove dual variables outside $T$ and write the remaining system as

$$ F\lambda\geq Eu. $$

Here $\lambda$ is unrestricted in this notation, with its nonnegativity included as rows of $F$. Equalities are represented by both signs. There are at most $2m+\tau$ rows, at most $\tau$ columns in $F$, and all entries of $F,E$ are integers of absolute value at most $C$.

Farkas’ lemma gives

$$ u\in K_{T,Z} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v^{\mathsf T}Eu\leq0 \quad\text{for every }v\geq0\text{ with }F^{\mathsf T}v=0. $$

It suffices to test extreme rays of the cone

$$ \{v\geq0:F^{\mathsf T}v=0\}. $$

Such a ray has support size at most $\tau+1$. Indeed, if its support has size $j$ and the restricted nullspace has dimension at least two, there is a nonproportional nullspace direction supported there. Small perturbations in both signs remain nonnegative, contradicting extremality. Thus the restricted matrix has rank $j-1$, and $j-1\leq\tau$.

Cofactors of $j-1$ independent rows supply an integer generator for the ray, with coordinates bounded by

$$ \tau!C^\tau. $$

The resulting normal vector $-E^{\mathsf T}v$ has coordinates bounded by

$$ U=(\tau+1)C\,\tau!C^\tau. $$

Hence every $K_{T,Z}$ is an intersection of homogeneous integer halfspaces whose coefficient bit length is at most

$$ D_*=\left\lceil\log_2(1+U)\right\rceil = O\bigl((\tau+1)\log((\tau+1)C)\bigr). $$

This also proves that each cone is closed. The description handles lower-dimensional cones: opposing inequalities can enforce their defining equalities.

The number of candidate extreme-ray supports is at most $(2m+\tau+1)^{\tau+1}$. Thus all nonzero boundary normals can be listed with total count bounded by

$$ N= h\,2^{m+\tau}(2m+\tau+1)^{\tau+1}. $$

5.3 Perturbing a potential cannot introduce new histories nearby

Fix $u_0$. Every cone not containing $u_0$ is closed, so some neighborhood of $u_0$ avoids it. There are finitely many cones, so one neighborhood avoids all such cones simultaneously.

For every $u$ in this neighborhood,

$$ u\in K_{T,Z}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u_0\in K_{T,Z}. $$

It follows that every stage flow maximizing at $u$ also maximizes at $u_0$, for every feasible residual-capacity vector. Applying this to successive stages gives inclusion of the entire history sets. The final objective is unchanged. Consequently,

$$ \operatorname{VAL}_V(I,\mathbf1-u) \geq \operatorname{VAL}_V(I,\mathbf1-u_0) $$

throughout that neighborhood.

The finitely many nonzero boundary hyperplanes cannot cover an open neighborhood. We can therefore perturb $u_0$ into a full-dimensional chamber while preserving any successful threshold guarantee.

Inside a chamber, every boundary-normal sign is fixed. Membership in every optimality cone is therefore fixed, so the complete set of maximizing histories—and its adversarial value—is constant throughout that chamber.

5.4 Every nonempty chamber has a small integer point

A chamber has a description

$$ \sigma_i n_i^{\mathsf T}u>0, $$

where $\sigma_i\in\{-1,1\}$ and every $n_i$ is integral with coordinates bounded by $U$.

By positive scaling, feasibility is equivalent to

$$ \sigma_i n_i^{\mathsf T}u\geq1 \quad\text{for every }i. $$

Write $u=u^+-u^-$ with $u^+,u^-\geq0$, and minimize

$$ \sum_b(u_b^++u_b^-) $$

subject to these inequalities. This LP has $2b_0=40$ variables. It is feasible, and its objective has compact sublevel sets, so it has an optimal vertex.

That vertex is determined by $40$ independent active constraints with integer coefficients bounded by $U$ and right sides in $\{0,1\}$. Cramer’s rule supplies a common denominator $\Delta$ and coordinate numerators, all bounded in absolute value by

$$ 40!\,U^{40}. $$

Multiplying $u$ by the positive integer $\Delta$ therefore produces an integer vector in the same chamber. Each coordinate has bit length

$$ O(D_*)= O\bigl((\tau+1)\log((\tau+1)C)\bigr), $$

where the constant uses the fixed value $b_0=20$.

Positive scaling of $u$ multiplies every pre-final objective by the same positive number, preserving its maximizers. Thus the integer vector gives exactly the same histories.

We have proved:

Integer-potential theorem. If any real $p$ satisfies the requested threshold, an integer $p$ satisfies it, with each coordinate having

$$ O\bigl((\tau+1)\log((\tau+1)C)\bigr) $$

bits. A successful potential can be chosen in a full-dimensional chamber.

This proof requires no vertex in an unrestricted polyhedron: splitting coordinates and minimizing their sum removes the lineality issue.

5.5 General complexity upper bounds

The integer-potential theorem supplies a polynomial-length existential witness. Section 4.3 supplies an NP certificate for failure of that witness. Therefore the general decision problem belongs to

$$ \Sigma_2^p. $$

There is also an explicit deterministic algorithm. Enumerate the cone normals, enumerate the full-dimensional chambers of their arrangement in $\mathbb R^{20}$, choose an integer representative of each chamber, and evaluate it by Section 4.2.

An arrangement of $N$ hyperplanes in fixed dimension $20$ has $N^{O(20)}$ chambers. They can be constructed incrementally: intersect each existing chamber with the two strict signs of the next hyperplane, testing feasibility by the homogeneous normalization used above. Only nonempty chambers are retained.

Combining this enumeration with complementary-slackness branching gives running time

$$ 2^{\,O\left(h(m+\tau)+\tau\log(2m+\tau+1)\right)} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,h,L,\log C), $$

where

$$ h\leq\min\{H,2\tau+2\}. $$

The constants in the exponential use the fixed number of ABO potential coordinates.

6. A restricted class is $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete for arbitrary horizons

Consider cycle-only instances whose roles all have the same patient–donor ABO type. Let its common potential be $p_0$.

There are only three relevant regimes:

  • If $p_0<1$, every pre-final stage maximizes unadjusted reward. Let the adversarial total value of this policy be $G_I$.
  • If $p_0>1$, every pre-final stage clears nothing. Let the final reward be $W_I$.
  • If $p_0=1$, every feasible pre-final clearing is maximizing. In particular, the adversary may use either a history realizing $G_I$ or the empty-early history realizing $W_I$.

Therefore

$$ \max_p\operatorname{VAL}_V(I,p)=\max\{G_I,W_I\}. $$

The value $W_I$ is computable by the final-stage reward LP. Failure of the threshold test means

$$ W_I<T \quad\text{and}\quad G_I<T. $$

The first inequality is decidable in polynomial time. The second has the polynomial certificate from Section 4.3, using $p_0=0$. The complement of the restricted decision problem is consequently in NP.

Section 3 proves hardness already within this class, with $H=2$, unit masses, and $\ell=6$. Hence the restricted problem is $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete. Its coNP membership holds for arbitrary horizons and cycle caps.

The fluid scaling question is settled: the three exact best-potential ratios are

$$ \frac{4k+4}{6k+4},\qquad \frac{4k+5}{6k+5},\qquad \frac56, $$

and remain unchanged after uniform mass multiplication.

It does not supply a many-one reduction preserving NP-source yes answers. No such reduction, or complete alternative classification, is proved here.
