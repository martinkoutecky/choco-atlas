1. The applicable theorem

Baïou–Balinski consider finite sets \(I,J\), strict preferences, positive supplies \(u_i\), positive demands \(v_j\), and nonnegative pair capacities \(c_{ij}\). Their algorithm finds an allocation \(y\) satisfying

\(0\le y_{ij}\le c_{ij},\qquad \sum_j y_{ij}\le u_i,\qquad \sum_i y_{ij}\le v_j.\)

Their stability condition is: whenever \(y_{ij}<c_{ij}\), either

\(\sum_{j':\,j'\succeq_i j}y_{ij'}=u_i\)

or

\(\sum_{i':\,i'\succeq_j i}y_{i'j}=v_j.\)

Here \(\succeq\) includes equality. The corrected paper proves existence and supplies a strongly polynomial inductive algorithm. Its Theorem 2 bounds allocation changes by \(3|I||J|+|J|\). [Corrected paper, Sections 1–2](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

We use this established theorem; we do not assume termination of ESMA.

2. Reduction and saturation

Set \(I=R_A\), \(J=R_B\), \(u_r=\mu_r\), \(v_s=\nu_s\), and \(c_{rs}=2\) for every pair. Retain all given preferences.

Every hypothesis holds: the supports are finite and nonempty, masses are positive, preferences are strict, and capacities are positive. Let \(x\) be the stable allocation returned by the theorem, and write \(T=\sum_{r,s}x_{rs}\). Feasibility gives \(T\le1\).

Claim. Every row and column is saturated.

Suppose \(T<1\). Since the total row supply is \(1\), some row \(r\) has \(\sum_s x_{rs}<\mu_r\). Similarly, some column \(s\) has \(\sum_r x_{rs}<\nu_s\). For this pair,

\(x_{rs}\le T<1<2=c_{rs}.\)

Moreover,

\(\sum_{s':\,s'\succeq_r s}x_{rs'}\le\sum_{s'}x_{rs'}<\mu_r,\)

and

\(\sum_{r':\,r'\succeq_s r}x_{r's}\le\sum_{r'}x_{r's}<\nu_s.\)

Both alternatives in the theorem’s stability condition fail, a contradiction. Therefore \(T=1\).

Each row deficit \(\mu_r-\sum_s x_{rs}\) is nonnegative, and their sum is \(1-T=0\). Consequently every row deficit is zero. The identical argument applies to columns. Thus

\(\sum_s x_{rs}=\mu_r,\qquad \sum_r x_{rs}=\nu_s.\)

3. Equivalence with the requested stability condition

For each pair define the nonnegative quantities

\(A_{rs}=\sum_{s':\,s\succ_r s'}x_{rs'},\qquad B_{rs}=\sum_{r':\,r\succ_s r'}x_{r's}.\)

Strict preferences partition each row into partners at least as preferred as \(s\) and partners strictly worse than \(s\). By saturation,

\(\sum_{s':\,s'\succeq_r s}x_{rs'}=\mu_r-A_{rs}.\)

Likewise,

\(\sum_{r':\,r'\succeq_s r}x_{r's}=\nu_s-B_{rs}.\)

Because \(x_{rs}\le1<2=c_{rs}\), the theorem’s stability condition applies to every pair. Substituting these identities, it says precisely

\(A_{rs}=0\quad\text{or}\quad B_{rs}=0.\)

Since both quantities are nonnegative, this is equivalent to \(A_{rs}B_{rs}=0\), exactly the required condition. Conversely, any matrix satisfying your conditions satisfies the theorem’s stability condition under these capacities.
