The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given complete pair, single-donor, and waiting-list types with the paper’s compatibility relation, binary-rational type masses, and sufficient waiting-list mass, what is the maximum mass of patients who can receive compatible kidneys using direct transplants and cycles or chains with at most four transplants, and what exchange-pattern schedule attains it?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(B=\{O,A,B,AB\}\), with \(m=|B|=4\). Blood compatibility is standard ABO kidney compatibility. Pair types are \((X,Y)^t\), recording patient blood type, donor blood type, and own-pair tissue compatibility \(t\in\{c,i\}\); single-donor and waiting-list types record blood type. Every blood-compatible transplant between different pairs, or involving a single donor or waiting-list patient, is tissue-compatible; an own-pair transplant additionally requires \(t=c\). Patients have dichotomous preferences over compatible and incompatible kidneys.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; absent types have zero mass. Masses are finite, nonnegative, divisible quantities and need not sum to one; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Write \(a_{XY}\) for total pair mass with blood types \((X,Y)\). Require \(a_{AB}>a_{BA}\), and, whenever \((X,Y)\) is blood-compatible and \(a_{XY}>0\), require positive mass of \((X,Y)^c\).
Let \(D\) be total single-donor mass and \(d_Y\) total donor mass of blood type \(Y\), counting paired and single donors. Sufficient waiting-list mass means its capacities can accommodate every terminal-donor vector \(q\) satisfying \(0\le q_Y\le d_Y\) and \(\sum_Y q_Y\le D\), through blood-compatible assignments.
Allowed patterns are compatible direct own-pair transplants, cycles involving between two and four pairs, and chains starting with one single donor, passing through between zero and three pairs, and ending at one waiting-list patient. Each pair in a cycle or chain receives one kidney and supplies one kidney; all transplant edges must be compatible. Different positions may have the same type but represent distinct members of that type.
A schedule assigns nonnegative real mass to each allowed pattern. A pattern used with mass \(x\) consumes \(x\) of each constituent position, counting repeated types with multiplicity; total consumption of each type cannot exceed its mass. Unused mass stays put. The objective is total transplanted patient mass, including waiting-list patients.

Is computing the exact maximum transplanted mass and an attaining exchange-pattern schedule solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is the threshold decision problem for a binary-rational target mass \(K\) NP-hard?

For finite societies under these compatibility and population conditions, Cheng and Yang give a sequential procedure attaining the exact maximum with exchanges of at most four transplants, together with an explicit formula ([discrete result](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/economics/documents/discussionpapers/2017/1713.pdf)).
No result settling exactly the divisible-mass problem stated here was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted complete pair types using blood types and own-pair tissue compatibility, with universal tissue compatibility for blood-compatible external transplants.
  • Carried over the strict mass inequality between (A,B) and (B,A) pairs and replaced the existence of a compatible pair in each populated blood-compatible category by positive compatible-pair mass.
  • Used unnormalized finite masses, reflecting population quantities rather than imposing a probability normalization.
  • Formalized sufficient waiting-list mass as capacity to absorb every possible terminal-donor composition bounded by available donors and total single-donor mass.
  • Defined continuous schedules by divisible pattern masses, allowing repeated types in distinct positions and charging their multiplicities.
  • Counted chain length by transplants, including the final waiting-list transplant; retained direct transplants and chains with no intermediate pair.
  • Fixed the blood-type domain at four types and introduced a binary-rational threshold for the decision version.

Conclusion

The exact maximum transplanted mass and an attaining exchange-pattern schedule are computable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). The threshold decision problem is therefore in \(\mathrm P\).

The proof uses the fixed bound on pattern length and the divisibility of masses. It does not require extending the finite-society procedure to divisible populations.

1. Enumerating the allowed patterns

Let \(T\) be the input support, with \(|T|=\tau\). Partition it into pair types \(T_P\), single-donor types \(T_S\), and waiting-list types \(T_W\). For each \(u\in T\), write \(b_u>0\) for its mass.

Write \(C(Y,X)\) when a donor of blood type \(Y\) can give to a patient of blood type \(X\). Standard ABO compatibility gives this relation explicitly.

We enumerate patterns using only supported types.

  • Direct own-pair transplants. Include a pattern for each \(u=(X,Y)^c\in T_P\) satisfying \(C(Y,X)\).
  • Cycles. For each \(r\in\{2,3,4\}\), enumerate every ordered tuple \((u_1,\ldots,u_r)\in T_P^r\). Write \(u_j=(X_j,Y_j)^{t_j}\). Include the tuple exactly when \(C(Y_j,X_{j+1})\) for every \(j\), with \(X_{r+1}=X_1\).
  • Chains. For each \(r\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), enumerate every tuple \((s,u_1,\ldots,u_r,w)\in T_S\times T_P^r\times T_W\). If \(r=0\), include it exactly when the single donor is blood-compatible with the waiting-list patient. If \(r>0\), check compatibility from the single donor to the first pair’s patient, from each pair’s donor to the next pair’s patient, and from the last pair’s donor to the waiting-list patient.

Repeated pair types are permitted. Their positions represent distinct members, as stipulated in the question. Therefore the own-pair tissue flag imposes no additional restriction on cycle or chain edges.

Let \(\mathcal P\) be the resulting pattern list and \(N=|\mathcal P|\). The number of candidates is at most

\(N\le \tau+\sum_{r=2}^{4}\tau^r+\sum_{r=0}^{3}\tau^{r+2}=O((1+\tau)^5).\)

Each candidate requires at most four compatibility checks. Thus enumeration takes polynomial time. Retaining different ordered descriptions of the same cycle is harmless: they simply give interchangeable variables in the optimization below.

For the stated model, \(m=4\). More generally, the same enumeration bound holds if compatibility on \(m\) blood labels is supplied as an explicit table; processing that table adds only polynomial dependence on \(m\).

2. An exact linear-programming formulation

For \(u\in T\) and \(p\in\mathcal P\), define \(A_{up}\) to be the number of constituent positions of type \(u\) in pattern \(p\). This counts repeated types with multiplicity.

Define \(v_p\), the number of transplanted patients per unit of pattern mass, by:

  • \(v_p=1\) for a direct own-pair transplant;
  • \(v_p=r\) for a cycle containing \(r\) pairs;
  • \(v_p=r+1\) for a chain containing \(r\) pairs, including its terminal waiting-list patient.

In particular, \(A_{up}\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\) and \(v_p\in\{1,2,3,4\}\).

Introduce a nonnegative real variable \(x_p\) for the mass assigned to pattern \(p\). Consider the linear program

\(V^*=\max\left\{\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}v_px_p:\ \sum_{p\in\mathcal P}A_{up}x_p\le b_u\text{ for every }u\in T,\quad x_p\ge0\text{ for every }p\in\mathcal P\right\}.\)

Claim. This linear program has exactly the feasible schedules and objective values specified in the question.

Proof. An allowed pattern containing an absent type cannot receive positive mass: it would consume positive mass of a type with zero capacity. Consequently, every positive-mass pattern in a feasible schedule is represented in \(\mathcal P\), up to an interchangeable ordered description.

Assigning mass \(x_p\) to pattern \(p\) consumes \(A_{up}x_p\) of type \(u\). Hence the displayed inequalities are precisely the schedule’s consumption constraints. Its transplanted patient mass is precisely \(\sum_pv_px_p\).

Conversely, a feasible vector \(x\) assigns nonnegative masses to allowed patterns and respects every type’s mass constraint, including repeated-position multiplicities. It is therefore a feasible schedule under the given definition. Its objective value is its transplanted patient mass. This proves the claim. \(\square\)

The assumption that all transplants between distinct members are tissue-compatible is essential to this correspondence: no unrecorded individual compatibility information is needed.

3. Existence and exact polynomial-time computation

The zero vector is feasible. Furthermore, every pattern contains at least one constituent position. For each pattern \(p\), choose a type \(u\) with \(A_{up}\ge1\). Nonnegativity and the corresponding capacity constraint imply

\(0\le x_p\le b_u/A_{up}\le b_u.\)

Thus the feasible region is closed and bounded. It is nonempty, so the objective attains its maximum. If there are no allowed patterns, the empty schedule attains maximum zero.

The linear program has \(N=O((1+\tau)^5)\) variables and \(\tau\) capacity constraints. Its matrix and objective coefficients are bounded integers; its right-hand side consists of the input rational masses. A dense encoding therefore has length \(O(\tau N+N+L)\), which is polynomial in \(\tau,L\).

We apply the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a linear program with rational data can be solved in time polynomial in its binary encoding length, returning an exact rational optimal solution when a finite optimum exists. This is the bit-complexity result, rather than an assertion about numerical approximation. See the discussion of polynomial bit complexity and exact LP algorithms in [Dadush, Huiberts, Natura, and Végh](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-023-01956-2).

All hypotheses hold here: the coefficients are rational, the encoding has polynomial length, and the feasible region is nonempty and bounded. Consequently, an exact optimal vector \(x^*\) and the exact value \(V^*\) are computable in polynomial time.

Outputting every pattern with \(x_p^*>0\), together with its rational mass, gives an attaining schedule.

4. Rational output size and sparse attainment

There is also a direct bound showing that exact attainment has a compact representation.

Introduce nonnegative slack variables \(s_u\), so that \(Ax+s=b\). The matrix \(H=[A\ I_\tau]\) has row rank \(\tau\). The feasible set in variables \((x,s)\) is compact, since \(x\) is bounded and \(0\le s\le b\). Hence there is an optimal vertex.

At any vertex, the columns of \(H\) corresponding to positive coordinates are linearly independent. Indeed, if they were dependent, there would be a nonzero vector \(h\), supported on those coordinates, with \(Hh=0\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both vectors \((x,s)+\varepsilon h\) and \((x,s)-\varepsilon h\) would remain nonnegative and satisfy the equations. Their midpoint would be the proposed vertex, a contradiction.

It follows that at most \(\tau\) coordinates are positive. In particular, some attaining schedule uses at most \(\tau\) patterns.

To bound its rational encoding, suppose there are \(k\le\tau\) positive coordinates. Choose \(k\) rows on which their columns form a nonsingular integer matrix \(M\). Its entries have absolute value at most four.

Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses. Then \(Qb\) is integral, and both \(Q\) and the entries of \(Qb\) have bit length \(O(L)\). Cramer’s rule expresses each positive coordinate as

\(y_j=\det(M^{(j)})/(Q\det M),\)

where \(M^{(j)}\) replaces column \(j\) of \(M\) by the selected entries of \(Qb\).

The determinant expansion gives \(|\det M|\le k!4^k\). If \(Z\) is the largest absolute selected entry of \(Qb\), it also gives \(|\det(M^{(j)})|\le k!4^{k-1}Z\). Therefore each positive coordinate has encoding length \(O(L+\tau\log(\tau+1))\). Exact attainment introduces no superpolynomial output-size obstruction.

5. Population conditions and threshold decision

The argument actually applies without the additional population promises. It retains the individual waiting-list capacities in the linear program and therefore needs neither the sufficient-waiting-list condition nor the inequalities and positivity conditions on pair masses. In particular, it applies to every society satisfying the stated promises.

For a binary-rational threshold \(K\), compute \(V^*\) and compare the two rational numbers exactly. The decision time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and the encoding length of \(K\).

Thus both requested optimization outputs are polynomial-time computable, and the threshold problem belongs to \(\mathrm P\). Under polynomial-time many-one reductions, NP-hardness would consequently imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
