The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_θ>0\) summing to \(1\), rational value vectors \(u_{θ j}\), per-capita budgets \(B_θ>0\), and rational separable stepwise-increasing seller costs \(C_j\) on normalized quantities, compute rational prices \(p_j>0\) and per-agent bundles \(x_{θ j}\ge 0\). Each type's bundle must maximize its quasi-linear utility subject to \(\sum_j p_j x_{θ j}\le B_θ\), and each aggregate quantity \(y_j=\sum_{θ\in T}\mu_θ x_{θ j}\) must maximize \(p_j z-C_j(z)\) over feasible \(z\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq1\) divisible goods. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), where \(\sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has values \(u_{\theta j}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\) and a per-capita budget \(B_\theta\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\).
For each good, the seller supplies a binary-rational list of breakpoints \(0=b_{j0}<b_{j1}<\cdots<b_{jK_j}\) and marginal costs \(0<d_{j1}<\cdots<d_{jK_j}\). The total cost \(C_j\) is continuous, satisfies \(C_j(0)=0\), and has slope \(d_{jk}\) on \((b_{j,k-1},b_{jk})\); quantities beyond \(b_{jK_j}\) are infeasible. Quantities are measured per unit total population, with no additional upper bound of \(1\).
An equilibrium consists of rational prices \(p_j>0\) and rational per-agent bundles \(x_{\theta j}\geq0\). Each bundle maximizes \(\sum_{j=1}^m(u_{\theta j}-p_j)x_{\theta j}\) over all nonnegative bundles satisfying \(\sum_{j=1}^m p_jx_{\theta j}\leq B_\theta\); any maximizing bundle is allowed, including partial spending when optimal.
For every good, \(y_j=\sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta x_{\theta j}\) must maximize \(p_jz-C_j(z)\) over \(0\leq z\leq b_{jK_j}\), independently of buyer budgets. Let \(K=\sum_{j=1}^mK_j\) and let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,K,L\).

Result

Computing an exact rational equilibrium is in \(\mathrm{P}\). The strongly polynomial result also transfers to this formulation.

The key is to represent each type by one buyer whose budget is its mass times its per-capita budget. No population expansion is needed.

1. Reduction to finitely listed buyers

For each \(\theta\in T\), create one buyer with values \(u_{\theta j}\) and budget

\[ \widehat B_\theta=\mu_\theta B_\theta. \]

Keep all goods, breakpoints, marginal costs, and supply caps unchanged. The resulting market has exactly \(\tau\) buyers, \(m\) goods, and \(K\) cost segments.

For any positive price vector \(p\), relate the original per-agent bundle \(x_\theta\) to this buyer’s bundle \(q_\theta\) by

\[ q_{\theta j}=\mu_\theta x_{\theta j}. \]

Here and below, these displayed formulas use the same mathematical notation as the inline formulas.

2. Exact preservation of buyer optimality

Fix \(\theta\) and \(p\). Because \(\mu_\theta>0\), the map \(x_\theta\mapsto q_\theta=\mu_\theta x_\theta\) is a bijection between the two feasible sets. Indeed,

\[ \sum_j p_jq_{\theta j} =\mu_\theta\sum_jp_jx_{\theta j} \leq\mu_\theta B_\theta =\widehat B_\theta \]

if and only if \(\sum_jp_jx_{\theta j}\leq B_\theta\).

The objectives satisfy

\[ \sum_j(u_{\theta j}-p_j)q_{\theta j} =\mu_\theta\sum_j(u_{\theta j}-p_j)x_{\theta j}. \]

Multiplication by the positive constant \(\mu_\theta\) preserves every comparison of objective values. Consequently,

\[ x_\theta\text{ is optimal with budget }B_\theta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q_\theta\text{ is optimal with budget }\widehat B_\theta. \]

This proves equivalence of the entire demand correspondences, including ties and partial spending. In particular, the construction imposes no additional requirement to exhaust a budget.

3. Exact preservation of seller optimality

The aggregate quantity in the finite market is

\[ \widehat y_j=\sum_{\theta\in T}q_{\theta j} =\sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta x_{\theta j} =y_j. \]

Thus both formulations give the seller exactly the same price and quantity for every good. Its maximization problem is unchanged:

\[ \max_{0\leq z\leq b_{jK_j}}\bigl(p_jz-C_j(z)\bigr). \]

Therefore \((p,x)\) is an equilibrium of the specified distribution model if and only if \((p,q)\), with \(q_{\theta j}=\mu_\theta x_{\theta j}\), is an equilibrium of the constructed finite market.

Conversely, recover an equilibrium from any finite-market equilibrium by setting

\[ x_{\theta j}=\frac{q_{\theta j}}{\mu_\theta}. \]

All these transformations preserve rationality.

4. Applicability of the finite-market algorithms

The finite-market model in Garg–Lock–Vazirani has nonnegative rational values, positive rational budgets, precisely these budget-constrained quasilinear buyer objectives, and separable stepwise increasing marginal costs with explicit supply caps. Its seller optimization is independent of buyer budgets. Theorem 2 supplies a polynomial-time exact equilibrium algorithm for this model. Our constructed instance satisfies these hypotheses: \(\widehat B_\theta>0\), and its seller data are unchanged. [Garg–Lock–Vazirani, Section 2 and Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09631v3)

For completeness, the additional positivity assumptions used by Baldwin–Klemperer–Lock can be ensured as follows:

  • Remove buyers whose values are all zero; their unique optimal bundle at positive prices is zero.
  • Remove goods valued at zero by every buyer. Restore each such good with allocation zero and price \(p_j=d_{j1}/2\).

For a restored good, every marginal profit slope satisfies \(p_j-d_{jk}<0\), so zero supply uniquely maximizes seller profit. Every buyer also strictly loses utility by purchasing it, so restoring it preserves buyer optimality. Removing these goods eliminates no positive buyer value.

The remaining instance satisfies their Assumption 1. Their strongly polynomial algorithm therefore applies. If nothing remains, the restored zero allocations and prices already constitute an equilibrium. [Baldwin–Klemperer–Lock, Assumption 1 and Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36346v1)

5. Encoding size and running time

Write each mass and budget as \(\mu_\theta=a_\theta/c_\theta\) and \(B_\theta=e_\theta/f_\theta\). Then

\[ \widehat B_\theta=\frac{a_\theta e_\theta}{c_\theta f_\theta}. \]

The numerator and denominator bit lengths are at most the sums of the corresponding input bit lengths, up to a constant. Since each mass is used in just one budget product and all other data are copied once, the transformed total binary length is \(L'=O(L)\).

The reduction uses \(\tau\) rational multiplications; recovering all bundles uses \(m\tau\) rational divisions. If the solver’s allocations have polynomial encoding length, division by the input masses preserves polynomial encoding length. Thus even very small masses cause no exponential output-size increase.

Accordingly, the total bit running time is

\[ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,L). \]

More concretely, substituting our parameters into Garg–Lock–Vazirani’s Theorem 2 gives at most

\[ O\!\left( (\tau+K)\tau m \bigl(m+\log(\tau+m)\bigr) (\tau+m)^5 \bigl(L+\log(\tau+m)\bigr) \right) \]

max-flow computations, with polynomial additional work and bit complexity. Their bit-length parameter is the maximum input coefficient length, which is \(O(L)\) here. [Garg–Lock–Vazirani, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09631v3)

Using Baldwin–Klemperer–Lock instead gives \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K)\) arithmetic operations, independent of coefficient bit lengths, while maintaining polynomial encoding lengths. The scaling and recovery operations preserve that guarantee. [Baldwin–Klemperer–Lock, Section 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36346v1)

Hence the explicitly listed support formulation is polynomial-time solvable, and indeed inherits a strongly polynomial algorithm.
