The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\) with service values \(v_t\in\{0,1\}\), rational population-mass profiles \(\mu\), a prior over those profiles, and black-box access to an anonymous downward-closed feasibility oracle returning lotteries over per-capita service rates \(q\in[0,1]^T\), determine the best welfare approximation achievable by a transformation with query and computation bounds polynomial in support size \(\tau\) and bit length \(L\). Require mass-level MIDR: for every true profile \(\mu\) and report pattern \(\rho\) with row sums \(\mu\) and report masses \(\nu\), truthful reporting maximizes expected welfare \(W_\mu(\rho,q)=\sum_{t,r}\rho_{t,r}v_tq_r\) over the transformed range, and compare its expected truthful welfare with the oracle's.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(T\) be a finite report alphabet with service values \(v_t\in\{0,1\}\). There is one service, \(m=1\), and a unit nonatomic population; profiles are binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\geq0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types and their binary-rational masses; \(\tau\) is support size, not the number of possible types. The alphabet is explicitly encoded, and the prior \(D\) is available through independent samples of rational profiles.
Feasibility is an unknown, nonempty family \(F\) of measurable service-rate functions on this population, valued in \([0,1]\), downward-closed and invariant under measure-preserving relabelings. It is independent of reports.
On any rational profile \(\nu\), querying \(A\) samples a feasible allocation constant at rate \(q_r\) within report class \(r\); oracle randomness specifies a lottery over such rate vectors. Sampled rates are binary rationals of encoding length polynomial in the query length and alphabet size.
A transformation \(B=M^A\) must remain feasible for every compatible hidden \(F\); it may randomize and discard service. Its range and randomness are fixed independently of the evaluated report.
A report pattern is a rational matrix \(\rho_{t,r}\geq0\) with \(\sum_r\rho_{t,r}=\mu_t\) and \(\nu_r=\sum_t\rho_{t,r}\). Applying \(q\sim B(\nu)\) gives rate \(q_r\) to the mass \(\rho_{t,r}\), with welfare \(W_\mu(\rho,q)=\sum_{t,r}\rho_{t,r}v_tq_r\).
Require exact mass-level MIDR: for every rational \(\mu\) and \(\rho\), \(\mathbb E_{q\sim B(\mu)}\sum_t\mu_tv_tq_t\geq\mathbb E_{q\sim B(\nu)}W_\mu(\rho,q)\). Equality is allowed, and rates for zero-mass report classes are irrelevant.
Write \(\operatorname{Wel}_D(C)=\mathbb E_{\mu\sim D,q\sim C(\mu)}\sum_t\mu_tv_tq_t\). An approximation factor \(\alpha\geq1\) means \(\operatorname{Wel}_D(B)\geq\operatorname{Wel}_D(A)/\alpha\) uniformly over oracles and priors with positive oracle welfare. Per evaluation, count all oracle queries, prior samples, and computation; \(L\) is the total binary encoding length encountered and \(\tau\) the maximum society support size encountered.
Target. What is the best worst-case welfare approximation factor achievable by exact mass-level MIDR black-box transformations using query, sampling, and computation bounds polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\)?
Nearest known results. For discrete binary single-parameter agents, constants \(\epsilon,c>0\) exist such that every MIDR transformation using at most \(\exp(n^\epsilon)\) queries suffers \(\operatorname{Wel}_D(M^A)\leq\operatorname{Wel}_D(A)/n^c\) on some downward-closed instance ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.10794)). No result settling this precise mass-level oracle model was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one and retained one service.
  • Allowed zero coordinates but encoded each society using only its positive-mass support; the finite report alphabet is explicitly encoded.
  • Specified prior access as independent sampling, following the discrete black-box model, rather than requiring an explicit enumeration of prior support.
  • Interpreted anonymity as invariance of a fixed feasibility family on a nonatomic population under measure-preserving relabelings; feasibility is independent of reported values.
  • Interpreted a returned rate as constant within each reported class, including after a report pattern splits a true type across report classes.
  • Required feasibility for every compatible hidden constraint and retained the ability to discard service afforded by downward closure.
  • Required the MIDR inequality on all rational profiles and report patterns, including profiles outside prior support, and included transformation randomness in its expectations.
  • Specified sampled rational oracle outputs with polynomial encoding length and counted prior samples alongside oracle queries and computation; support size and encountered bit length determine per-evaluation bounds.
  • Defined a multiplicative expected-welfare guarantee uniformly over positive-oracle-welfare instances.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

No finite worst-case welfare approximation factor exists in the stated model, even with unlimited queries and computation.

The decisive requirement is that each sampled output \(q\sim B(\nu)\) is itself a feasible allocation, assigning the constant rate \(q_r\) throughout report class \(r\). Randomization therefore gives a lottery over feasible class-constant functions.

I first give a zero-welfare obstruction, then strengthen it to an arbitrarily large loss in instances admitting strictly positive uniform service.

1. A feasible oracle with positive welfare

Take the population to be \([0,1]\) with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\), and let \(T=\{t_0,t_1\}\), with \(v_{t_0}=0\) and \(v_{t_1}=1\).

Define the hidden feasibility family by

\(F_0=\{f:[0,1]\to[0,1]\text{ measurable}:\lambda(\{x:f(x)>0\})\leq 1/2\}\).

This family satisfies every stipulated feasibility assumption:

  • It is nonempty because it contains the zero function.
  • It is downward-closed: if \(0\leq g\leq f\), then \(\{g>0\}\subseteq\{f>0\}\).
  • It is invariant under measure-preserving relabelings because such relabelings preserve the measure of the positive-service set.
  • Its definition does not depend on reports.

Define a deterministic oracle \(A\). On a rational profile \(\nu\), write \(p=\nu_{t_1}\). If \(p\leq 1/2\), return \(q_{t_1}=1\) and \(q_{t_0}=0\). Otherwise return both rates zero.

Every oracle output is feasible: its positive-service set has measure at most \(1/2\). All returned rates have constant binary encoding length.

Let \(D\) be the point mass at the profile \(\bar\mu\) with \(\bar\mu_{t_0}=\bar\mu_{t_1}=1/2\). Then \(\operatorname{Wel}_D(A)=1/2>0\).

2. Exact MIDR forces every transformed output to be zero

Let \(B\) be any feasible exact mass-level MIDR transformation for this oracle and hidden family.

Consider the rational profile \(\mu^\star\) consisting entirely of type \(t_1\). Its sole positive-mass report class is the entire population. Consequently, each sampled output of \(B(\mu^\star)\) is a constant function with rate \(q_{t_1}\).

If \(q_{t_1}>0\), its positive-service set has measure \(1\), contradicting feasibility in \(F_0\). Thus \(q_{t_1}=0\) almost surely, and truthful welfare at \(\mu^\star\) is zero.

Now fix any rational profile \(\nu\). Define the report pattern by \(\rho_{t_1,r}=\nu_r\) and \(\rho_{t_0,r}=0\) for every \(r\in T\). Its row sums are \(\mu^\star\), and its column sums are \(\nu\), so it is an admissible rational report pattern.

Every individual has true value one. The exact MIDR inequality therefore gives

\(0\geq \mathbb E_{q\sim B(\nu)}\sum_{r\in T}\nu_rq_r\).

The expression inside the expectation is nonnegative. Hence its expectation is zero. Moreover, each positive-mass class must have rate zero almost surely: a nonnegative random variable with expectation zero is zero almost surely.

This holds for every rational \(\nu\). In particular, \(\operatorname{Wel}_D(B)=0\).

For every finite \(\alpha\), the required inequality would assert \(0\geq (1/2)/\alpha>0\), which is impossible.

This proof uses neither computational restrictions nor restrictions on oracle access.

3. Strengthening: strictly positive uniform service

The first obstruction uses a family admitting no positive constant function. Arbitrarily large losses persist when positive uniform service is feasible and actually returned by the oracle.

Fix an integer \(k\geq2\), and put \(\varepsilon=2^{-k}\). Define

\(F_\varepsilon=F_0\cup\{f:[0,1]\to[0,1]\text{ measurable}:f\leq\varepsilon\text{ almost everywhere}\}\).

Both component families are downward-closed and invariant under measure-preserving relabelings, so their union has these properties. It contains the strictly positive constant function \(\varepsilon\).

Modify the preceding oracle at just one profile: when \(\nu_{t_1}=1/2+2^{-k}\), return the constant rate \(\varepsilon\) on both classes. All other outputs remain as before. This modified oracle is feasible in \(F_\varepsilon\). Its exceptional output has encoding length proportional to that exceptional query’s encoding length, so the stipulated encoding bound holds. At \(\bar\mu\), oracle welfare remains \(1/2\).

For rational \(h\in(1/2,1)\), let \(\mu^h_{t_1}=h\) and \(\mu^h_{t_0}=1-h\). Every feasible class-constant output at \(\mu^h\) has \(q_{t_1}\leq\varepsilon\):

  • If it belongs to \(F_0\), a positive rate on the class of mass \(h>1/2\) is impossible.
  • If it belongs to the other component, every rate is at most \(\varepsilon\).

Thus truthful expected welfare at \(\mu^h\) is at most \(h\varepsilon\).

Consider the report pattern from \(\mu^h\) to \(\bar\mu\) defined by

\(\rho_{t_1,t_1}=1/2,\quad \rho_{t_1,t_0}=h-1/2,\quad \rho_{t_0,t_1}=0,\quad \rho_{t_0,t_0}=1-h\).

Its row and column sums have the required values. Welfare under this report pattern is

\((1/2)q_{t_1}+(h-1/2)q_{t_0}\geq(1/2)q_{t_1}\).

Applying exact MIDR gives

\(\mathbb E_{q\sim B(\bar\mu)}(1/2)q_{t_1}\leq h\varepsilon\).

This holds for every rational \(h>1/2\). Taking a rational sequence decreasing to \(1/2\) yields \(\operatorname{Wel}_D(B)\leq\varepsilon/2\). Consequently,

\(\operatorname{Wel}_D(B)/\operatorname{Wel}_D(A)\leq\varepsilon=2^{-k}\).

Thus an approximation factor of at least \(2^k\) is necessary on these instances, although the alphabet and maximum support size are both two and positive uniform service is feasible.

4. Scope of the conclusion

The obstruction follows from the permitted nonconvex feasibility families, feasible sampled class-constant rates, and MIDR comparisons against every rational report pattern. These are explicit premises of the task.

The [cited discrete lower bound](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.10794) is therefore unnecessary for this conclusion. The stated mass-level model already admits an instance on which every feasible exact MIDR transformation has zero welfare despite positive oracle welfare.

Verdict

The output proves its claimed conclusion: no finite worst-case welfare approximation factor exists in the stated model. The first construction already establishes this, independently of query, sampling, or computation limits. The strengthening is also correct.

No major or minor gaps were found.

1. The feasibility interpretation

The proof relies on sampled transformed outputs being feasible functions that are constant within each positive-mass report class. This matches the task’s specification that applying \(q\sim B(\nu)\) gives rate \(q_r\) throughout the mass reporting \(r\).

In particular, on a profile with only one positive-mass report class, each sampled allocation is constant over the entire population. A lottery does not remove the requirement that its individual sampled allocations be feasible.

The argument establishes its obstruction for every feasible \(B\) satisfying the displayed MIDR inequalities. It therefore also applies to transformations satisfying the additional requirement that their range and randomness be independent of the evaluated report.

2. Verification of the first construction

Feasibility family

The family \(F_0\) satisfies the stipulated assumptions:

  • The zero function belongs to \(F_0\).
  • Pointwise reduction cannot enlarge the positive-service set, establishing downward closure.
  • Measure-preserving relabelings preserve the measure of that set.
  • Its definition refers only to service functions, independently of reports.

Neither convexity nor closure under averaging is required by the task. Thus the nonconvexity used here is permitted.

Oracle and prior

When \(p\leq 1/2\), the oracle serves only the class of mass \(p\), so the positive-service set has measure at most \(1/2\). When \(p>1/2\), it returns the feasible zero function. At \(p=0\), the rate assigned to the empty class is irrelevant.

The rates are binary rationals of constant encoding length. The chosen prior is supported on a rational profile and can be sampled deterministically. Its oracle welfare is correctly calculated as \(1/2\).

The all-value-one profile

At \(\mu^\star\), a positive rate on the sole positive-mass class would produce a positive-service set of measure \(1\). Feasibility therefore forces that rate to be zero almost surely. Truthful expected welfare is consequently zero.

This step is valid for lotteries: every sampled allocation must be feasible.

The deviation to an arbitrary profile

The proposed report pattern has row sums \(\mu^\star\), column sums \(\nu\), and rational nonnegative entries. It is admissible.

Since all true values equal one, its expected welfare is exactly

\[ \mathbb E_{q\sim B(\nu)}\sum_r \nu_rq_r. \]

The MIDR inequality places this nonnegative quantity below zero. Hence it equals zero.

For each positive-mass class, its nonnegative contribution must therefore have expectation zero, and its rate is zero almost surely. The alphabet is finite, so this also establishes simultaneous vanishing on all positive-mass classes of each fixed profile. The proof requires no common probability-one event across different profiles.

In particular, transformed welfare under the chosen prior is zero. Because oracle welfare is \(1/2\), every finite approximation factor fails. This settles the target even if a proposed finite factor depends on the input parameters.

3. Verification of the strengthening

Family and oracle

The union defining \(F_\varepsilon\) is nonempty, downward-closed, and invariant under measure-preserving relabelings. Its second component contains the positive constant function \(\varepsilon\).

The exceptional oracle output is feasible. Its query profile differs from \(\bar\mu\), so oracle welfare at \(\bar\mu\) remains \(1/2\).

The encoding assertion is sound: the exceptional profile contains masses with reduced denominator \(2^k\), so its binary-rational encoding length grows proportionally to \(k\). Returning \(\varepsilon=2^{-k}\) therefore satisfies the required polynomial output-length bound. Other outputs have constant-length rates.

Bound at profiles with majority value one

For \(h>1/2\), a feasible class-constant output in \(F_0\) cannot assign a positive rate to the class of mass \(h\). An output in the other component has rate at most \(\varepsilon\) on that positive-mass class.

Thus the claimed bound \(q_{t_1}\leq\varepsilon\), and consequently the truthful welfare bound \(h\varepsilon\), hold for every sampled feasible output.

Report-pattern calculation

The displayed pattern has nonnegative entries. Its row sums are \(h\) and \(1-h\), and both column sums are \(1/2\). Its welfare is correctly computed as

\[ \frac12 q_{t_1}+\left(h-\frac12\right)q_{t_0}, \]

which is at least \(\frac12 q_{t_1}\).

Applying MIDR gives the stated bound on truthful expected welfare at \(\bar\mu\). The left-hand side is fixed as \(h\) varies. Taking rational \(h\) decreasing to \(1/2\) therefore yields the bound \(\varepsilon/2\); no continuity assumption on \(B\) is needed.

Dividing by oracle welfare \(1/2\) gives the ratio bound \(2^{-k}\). The claimed necessary approximation factor \(2^k\) follows.

4. Scope and repair feedback

The proof fully addresses the target through a structural impossibility result. Its conclusion does not depend on the cited discrete lower bound, and it does not claim a computational hardness classification.

No additional mathematical clarification or repair is needed.
