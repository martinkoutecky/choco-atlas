The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many single types \(s\) with masses \(a_s\), couple types \(c\) with masses \(b_c\), hospitals with rational per-capita capacities \(q_h\), strict hospital priorities over acceptable doctor-role types, and complete responsive couple rankings \(P_c\), decide whether a stable mass allocation exists and compute one if it does. Require \(\sum_s a_s+2\sum_c b_c=1\); let \(y_{s,h}\) be single mass assigned to \(h\), and \(x_{c,h,h'}\) couple mass assigned with its two members at \(h,h'\), where \(h,h'\in H\cup\{\lambda\}\). Enforce type-mass conservation and hospital capacities. Require the mass-scaled extreme-altruism condition at every hospital with \(q_h<1\): if \(h'P_fh\), \(h'R_fh''\), and \(h''R_m\lambda\), then \((h',h'')P_c(h,h)\), with the symmetric condition for the other member. Stability means no positive mass of a single or couple type prefers a feasible alternative hospital or hospital pair that the relevant hospital or hospitals can accommodate by replacing lower-priority assigned mass and strictly improve their priority-based choice.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(H\) contain \(m\geq2\) hospitals, and let \(\lambda\) denote unemployment. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|S|+|C|\) single and couple types with positive binary-rational masses \(a_s,b_c\), where \(C\neq\varnothing\) and \(\sum_s a_s+2\sum_c b_c=1\). Support size means listed types, not possible types. Hospitals have positive rational capacities \(q_h\); \(\lambda\) accepts everyone without restriction. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Each single has an explicit strict ranking \(P_s\) of \(H\cup\{\lambda\}\). Each couple \(c\) has distinct role types \(f_c,m_c\), strict marginal rankings \(P_f,P_m\), and an explicit strict complete ranking \(P_c\) of \((H\cup\{\lambda\})^2\), responsive to those marginals. Write \(R\) for the corresponding weak ranking. Each hospital strictly ranks its acceptable single and couple-role types above vacancies; unacceptable roles cannot be assigned.
Require extreme-altruism: for every \(c\), every \(h\in H\) with \(q_h<1\), and every \(h',h''\in H\cup\{\lambda\}\), \(h'P_fh,\ h'R_fh'',\ h''R_m\lambda\) imply \((h',h'')P_c(h,h)\); also require the implication with \(f,m\) exchanged and conclusion \((h'',h')P_c(h,h)\).
An allocation consists of nonnegative real masses \(y_{s,h}\) and \(x_{c,h,h'}\), satisfying \(\sum_h y_{s,h}=a_s\), \(\sum_{h,h'}x_{c,h,h'}=b_c\), and \(\sum_s y_{s,h}+\sum_c\sum_k(x_{c,h,k}+x_{c,k,h})\leq q_h\) for each real hospital. Both coordinates range over \(H\cup\{\lambda\}\), so colocated couples consume twice their couple mass. Positive assignments must be hospital-acceptable and individually rational: singles weakly prefer their assignment to \(\lambda\), and couples weakly prefer theirs to \((\lambda,\lambda)\).
A block moves some \(\varepsilon>0\) from one positive assignment of a single or couple type to a strictly preferred hospital or ordered hospital pair. Only members changing hospitals require acceptance; moves to \(\lambda\) are always accepted.
Acceptance at each real destination means that all incoming role mass can simultaneously be partitioned into portions filling current vacancies or replacing equal amounts of strictly lower-priority incumbent role mass. Vacancy and replacement mass cannot be reused; unchanged members of the blocking cohort are protected, and departures are not credited when testing acceptance. Thus two incoming members at one hospital require joint acceptance. Displacing one member of another couple does not automatically remove its partner. An allocation is stable if it has no block.

Is deciding whether a stable mass allocation exists polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or NP-hard? Can an allocation be computed in polynomial time whenever one exists?

In finite markets with complete responsive hospital preferences over doctor sets, extreme-altruism guarantees stable existence, and individual doctor-proposing deferred acceptance constructs a stable matching; the stated characterization also gives a nonexistence extension when extreme-altruism fails. [Khare–Roy–Storcken](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07501v1)
Divisible schedule matching has stable-existence and algorithmic results under substitutable revealed-preference conditions, but uses bilateral schedule choices rather than coupled ordered hospital-pair assignments. [Alkan–Gale](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022053103000966)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified explicit strict single rankings, distinct couple-role types, strict marginal rankings, and explicit complete couple rankings; weak rankings include equality.
  • Carried over at least two hospitals and a nonempty couple population. Used positive rational hospital capacities; discrete lower bounds on doctor counts and capacities have no fixed positive analogue for divisible normalized mass.
  • Specified unemployment as universally acceptable and unrestricted, with no requirement that total real-hospital capacity equal total doctor mass.
  • Added individual rationality and hospital acceptability, including exclusion of assignments containing unacceptable roles.
  • Interpreted a positive-mass block as moving a common positive amount from one occupied assignment to one preferred alternative, with unchanged members requiring no additional acceptance.
  • Made the graded question's lower-priority replacement rule precise through simultaneous vacancy filling and equal-mass strictly lower-priority replacement, excluding displacement of equal-priority copies and protecting unchanged blocking members.
  • Followed the discrete destination-based blocking tests by not crediting departures during acceptance tests and by allowing displacement of a couple member without automatically removing its partner.
  • Retained the graded threshold q_h<1 for extreme-altruism rather than introducing a finite replication size.

1. Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. Every instance satisfying the stated hypotheses admits a stable mass allocation, and such an allocation can be computed in polynomial time.

In fact, extreme-altruism is unnecessary for this conclusion. Responsiveness suffices under the question’s rule that every incoming role must be accepted using vacancies or strictly lower-priority incumbent mass.

The proof constructs a stable allocation of the individual roles and then couples their marginal allocations.

2. An auxiliary bipartite allocation problem

Let \(T\) consist of one vertex for each listed single type and two role vertices for each listed couple type. Thus

\[ n:=|T|=|S|+2|C|\leq 2\tau. \]

Give these vertices demands

\[ d_s=a_s,\qquad d_{f_c}=d_{m_c}=b_c. \]

The normalization in the question gives \(\sum_{t\in T}d_t=1\).

We use the following established theorem.

Bipartite stable-allocation theorem. A finite bipartite graph with nonnegative edge capacities, positive vertex quotas, and strict preferences over incident edges admits a stable allocation. Here an edge blocks if its allocation is below its capacity and each endpoint either has unused quota or has positive allocation on a less-preferred incident edge. A stable allocation can be computed by a strongly polynomial algorithm. This is the stable-allocation theorem of [Baïou–Balinski, corrected version](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

Construct its instance as follows.

  • The left vertices are \(T\), with quotas \(d_t\).
  • The right vertices are the hospitals, with quotas \(q_h\), together with a private unemployment vertex \(\lambda_t\) for every \(t\), with quota \(d_t\).
  • Include an edge \(th\) precisely when \(h\) accepts the underlying type of \(t\) and \(hP_t\lambda\). Its capacity is \(\min(d_t,q_h)\).
  • Include \(t\lambda_t\), with capacity \(d_t\).
  • Each left vertex uses its marginal ranking, placing its private unemployment vertex last. Each hospital uses its given priority ranking. A private unemployment vertex has only one incident edge.

If an underlying hospital-ranked type occurs in several role vertices, order those occurrences arbitrarily within that type. This preserves every strict comparison between different original types.

All theorem hypotheses hold: the graph is finite and bipartite; quotas and edge capacities are positive rational numbers; and incident-edge preferences are strict. Let \(z\) be a stable allocation supplied by the theorem.

Every left quota is filled. Suppose instead that \(\sum_j z_{t,j}<d_t\). Then \(z_{t,\lambda_t}<d_t\), so the edge \(t\lambda_t\) is unsaturated. Both endpoints have unused quota, because \(\lambda_t\) has no other incident edge. That edge would block, a contradiction.

We henceforth write \(z_{t,\lambda}\) for \(z_{t,\lambda_t}\). Consequently,

\[ \sum_{u\in H\cup\{\lambda\}}z_{t,u}=d_t \]

for every \(t\). Every positive real-hospital assignment is acceptable and marginally preferred to unemployment.

3. The exclusion property of the marginals

For each real hospital define its vacancy mass

\[ v_h:=q_h-\sum_{t\in T}z_{t,h}. \]

For a role vertex \(t\) acceptable to \(h\), let \(B_h(t)\) be the total incumbent mass at \(h\) whose original type is strictly lower in \(h\)’s priority ranking than the type of \(t\).

We claim:

\[ z_{t,u}>0,\quad hP_tu,\quad h\text{ accepts }t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_h+B_h(t)=0. \tag{1} \]

To prove this, suppose the antecedent holds but \(v_h+B_h(t)>0\).

Because \(hP_tu\), we have \(h\neq u\), and therefore \(z_{t,h}<d_t\). Moreover,

\[ v_h+B_h(t)\leq q_h-z_{t,h}, \]

so \(z_{t,h}<q_h\). Thus \(th\) is below its edge capacity \(\min(d_t,q_h)\).

Since \(z_{t,u}>0\), the left endpoint has positive allocation on an edge less preferred than \(th\). The hospital endpoint either has unused quota, if \(v_h>0\), or positive allocation on a strictly worse edge, if \(B_h(t)>0\). Original strict priority comparisons remain strict in the auxiliary ranking.

Finally, \(uR_t\lambda\), because all auxiliary assignments are marginally individually rational. Hence \(hP_t\lambda\), so \(th\) is indeed an included edge.

Therefore \(th\) blocks \(z\), contradicting auxiliary stability. This proves (1).

4. Coupling the roles

For singles, set \(y_{s,u}:=z_{s,u}\).

For each couple \(c\), construct a nonnegative matrix \(x_{c,u,v}\) having row sums \(z_{f_c,u}\) and column sums \(z_{m_c,v}\). Such a matrix exists because both marginal totals equal \(b_c\).

An explicit construction repeatedly chooses a positive remaining row mass \(r_u\) and column mass \(s_v\), assigns

\[ x_{c,u,v}:=\min(r_u,s_v), \]

and subtracts this amount from both residuals. Every step exhausts at least one residual. The remaining row and column totals always agree, so the procedure exhausts both lists and produces the required marginals. It uses at most \(2m+1\) positive entries per couple.

The resulting allocation satisfies the couple mass equations. At every hospital \(h\), its load is

\[ \sum_s y_{s,h} +\sum_c\sum_k\bigl(x_{c,h,k}+x_{c,k,h}\bigr) = \sum_{t\in T}z_{t,h} \leq q_h. \]

This identity counts a colocated couple twice, as required.

Every positive assignment is hospital-acceptable. Furthermore, if \(x_{c,u,v}>0\), then \(uR_{f_c}\lambda\) and \(vR_{m_c}\lambda\). Responsiveness, applied successively to the two coordinates, implies

\[ (u,v)R_c(\lambda,\lambda). \]

Thus the constructed allocation satisfies all feasibility and individual-rationality requirements.

5. Proof of stability

A single block would move positive mass from some \(u\) to a strictly preferred destination \(h\).

The destination cannot be \(\lambda\), because every source assignment is marginally individually rational. If \(h\) is unacceptable, acceptance is impossible. Otherwise, \(z_{s,u}>0\), and (1) gives \(v_h+B_h(s)=0\). There is no vacancy or strictly lower-priority incumbent mass available for that incoming type. Acceptance is therefore impossible.

Now consider a proposed couple block from a positive assignment \((u,v)\) to \((h,k)\), with

\[ (h,k)P_c(u,v). \]

At least one coordinate strictly improves in its marginal ranking. Otherwise \(uR_{f_c}h\) and \(vR_{m_c}k\). Responsiveness would then imply, by changing the coordinates successively,

\[ (u,v)R_c(h,k), \]

contradicting the proposed strict improvement.

Suppose the improving coordinate is the first; the other case is identical. Then \(hP_{f_c}u\), and \(x_{c,u,v}>0\) implies \(z_{f_c,u}>0\).

The improving member changes hospitals. Its destination is real: \(uR_{f_c}\lambda\) rules out \(\lambda P_{f_c}u\). If \(h\) rejects its role type, the proposed block fails. Otherwise, (1) gives

\[ v_h+B_h(f_c)=0. \]

Acceptance of any positive incoming mass of this role would require some vacancy mass or strictly lower-priority incumbent mass. Both are zero. Protecting unchanged members cannot increase those resources, and departures are not credited under the stipulated acceptance test.

This also rules out a block sending both members to the same hospital: a simultaneous acceptance partition would still have to supply eligible resources for the improving member’s positive incoming mass.

Consequently, no single or couple block exists. The constructed allocation is stable.

6. Running time and relation to the cited finite model

The auxiliary instance has

\[ V=m+2n=O(m+\tau),\qquad E\leq n(m+1)=O(m\tau). \]

The Baïou–Balinski algorithm has an \(O(VE)\) arithmetic-operation implementation; [Dean–Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf) describe this bound and a faster implementation. The marginal computation therefore requires \(O(m\tau(m+\tau))\) arithmetic operations. Coupling requires \(O(\tau m)\) additional operations; writing every entry of a dense allocation requires \(O(\tau m^2)\) entries.

For an explicit conservative bit bound, multiply all numerical data by the product \(D\) of their input denominators. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). The augmenting-path algorithm uses additions, subtractions, comparisons, and minimum residual capacities, so scaled allocations remain integers. Quotas, allocations, and residual quantities have \(O(L)\) bits. The coupling procedure also preserves integrality. Using elementary arithmetic, the complete construction, including dense output, thus takes

\[ O\!\left(m\tau(m+\tau)L^2\right) \]

bit operations. Dividing by \(D\) gives an exact rational stable allocation of polynomial output length.

The difference from the finite model matters. [Khare–Roy–Storcken](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07501v1) permit a hospital to accept a couple according to a complete preference over doctor sets. Such a comparison need not provide a separate strictly lower-priority replacement for each incoming doctor. The acceptance rule here expressly requires those separate resources. That requirement makes the marginal exclusion property sufficient, without extreme-altruism.
