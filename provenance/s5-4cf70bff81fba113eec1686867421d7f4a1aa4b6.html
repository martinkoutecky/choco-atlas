The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational worker and firm cohort masses, an explicitly listed finite profile set \(\Omega\), payoff tables, information partitions, integer wages, and an initial mass matching, can an algorithm polynomial in the number of supported cohort and information types and the binary input length output a finite sequence of positive rational mass rematches and paper-consistent information updates ending at an individually rational, unblocked, information-stable state?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) worker and firm cohort/information types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses need not sum to one or balance across sides. Let \(m=|W|+|F|\), where \(W,F\) are the finite worker and firm productivity-type sets, and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
The input specifies public cohort labels \(A,B\), public firm types \(f:B\to F\), a nonempty explicitly listed set \(\Omega\subseteq W^A\), a realized profile \(\omega^*\in\Omega\), and signed rational payoff tables \(\nu,\phi:W\times F\to\mathbb Q\). Every member of worker cohort \(a\) has type \(\omega(a)\) under profile \(\omega\), and knows their realized type; profiles are possibilities, not a probability distribution.
An allocation is a finite list of positive rational matched and unmatched masses respecting cohort totals. Each matched record specifies a worker cohort, firm cohort, integer wage, and the firm's information partition over \(\Omega\); unmatched wages and payoffs are zero. Records may split, preserving cohort labels and inherited information. All allocations and partitions are public, and each firm's partition must distinguish profiles giving its current employee different types.
For a worker slice \(s\) from cohort \(a\) and a firm slice \(t\) from cohort \(b\), write \(U_s(\omega),V_t(\omega)\) for their current payoffs: a match at wage \(q\) gives \(\nu_{\omega(a),f(b)}+q\) to the worker and \(\phi_{\omega(a),f(b)}-q\) to the firm. Individual rationality requires nonnegative realized payoffs on every positive-mass record.
Slices not currently partnered block at an integer wage \(p\) when \(\nu_{\omega^*(a),f(b)}+p>U_s(\omega^*)\) and \(\phi_{\omega(a),f(b)}-p>V_t(\omega)\) for every \(\omega\in\Pi_t(\omega^*)\) satisfying \(\nu_{\omega(a),f(b)}+p>U_s(\omega)\), where \(\Pi_t\) is the firm's partition. Blocking at another profile is defined by replacing \(\omega^*\) throughout.
A pair rematch selects equal positive rational masses from such blocking slices, matches them at \(p\), and leaves their displaced partners unmatched; other matches remain unchanged. A negative-payoff slice may instead resign or dismiss its employee, breaking that mass of its match. After either move, every firm slice's partition may be any refinement of its inherited partition and its new employee-type observation partition.
At each step, a negative-payoff slice may leave; otherwise, if a blocking pair exists, one must rematch. If neither exists, keep the allocation and replace each \(\Pi_t\) by \(\Pi_t\vee\{N,\Omega\setminus N\}\), omitting empty cells, where \(N\) is the set of profiles at which the current state is individually rational and unblocked and \(\vee\) denotes common refinement.
Let \(K\) be the finest common coarsening of all positive-mass firm slices' partitions. A terminal state is stable when it is individually rational, unblocked, and \(K(\omega^*)\subseteq N\). The initial allocation and partitions are arbitrary subject to the stated consistency conditions.

Can a finite sequence of these rational mass rematches and information updates reaching a stable state always be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,|\Omega|,L\), including the explicit sequence's length and encoding, or is this path-construction problem NP-hard?

For discrete markets with integer wages, a finite learning-blocking path to stability exists from every initial state; this is an existence result without a polynomial running-time bound. [Chen–Hu (2020)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3982/TE3088)
No result settling this explicitly represented mass path-construction problem was located in the literature search.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exponential explicit paths are unavoidable

The universal polynomial-time construction requested in the question is impossible: there are instances of input length $O(r)$ for which every legal path to stability contains at least $2^r$ rematches.

This settles the polynomial-construction alternative negatively. It does not establish NP-hardness; that remaining classification is explicitly left open.

1. A constant-support family

Fix an integer $r\ge 1$ and put
$$ \varepsilon=2^{-r}. $$

There is one worker cohort $a$, of mass $1+\varepsilon$, and one firm cohort $b$, of mass $1$. There is one worker productivity type $w$ and one firm productivity type $f$. Set
$$ \nu_{w,f}=0,\qquad \phi_{w,f}=3. $$

Let
$$ \Omega=\{\omega^*\},\qquad \omega^*(a)=w, $$
and give every firm the unique partition of this singleton.

The initial allocation consists of:

  • a matched record of mass $1$, between cohorts $a$ and $b$, at integer wage $2$;
  • an unmatched worker record of mass $\varepsilon$.

Thus all firms are initially matched. Matched workers receive payoff $2$, matched firms receive payoff $1$, and unmatched workers receive payoff $0$. The initial state is individually rational.

The parameters satisfy
$$ m=2,\qquad \tau=2,\qquad |\Omega|=1. $$

All labels, payoffs, wages, and partitions have constant encoding size. The only growing data are the rational masses, whose numerators and denominators have $O(r)$ bits. Consequently, the total input length satisfies
$$ L_r=O(r). $$

2. Characterization of every legal move

We prove the following invariant for every state reachable from this initial state:

  • All firms remain matched.
  • Every matched wage is either $1$ or $2$.
  • The total unmatched worker mass is exactly $\varepsilon$.
  • Every payoff is nonnegative.

These properties hold initially. Suppose they hold at the current state.

A worker’s current payoff $U$ belongs to
$$ \{0,1,2\}. $$
A firm currently paying wage $q\in\{1,2\}$ receives payoff $3-q$.

Because information is complete, a proposed match at integer wage $p$ blocks precisely when
$$ p>U,\qquad 3-p>3-q. $$
Equivalently,
$$ U<p<q. $$

For the possible values of $U$ and $q$, the only integer solution is
$$ U=0,\qquad q=2,\qquad p=1. $$

Indeed, when $q=1$, no integer lies strictly between a nonnegative $U$ and $1$. When $q=2$, the only possible integer below $2$ and above a nonnegative $U$ is $1$, requiring $U=0$.

Therefore, every blocking pair consists of an unmatched worker slice and a firm slice currently paying wage $2$, and its wage must be $1$. Such slices are not currently partnered because the worker is unmatched.

Let a rematch select mass $\delta>0$. Its effects are:

  • unmatched workers of mass $\delta$ become matched at wage $1$;
  • the selected firms remain matched, now paying wage $1$;
  • those firms’ previous employees, of mass $\delta$, become unmatched.

Thus the unmatched worker mass remains $\varepsilon$. All firms remain matched, all matched wages remain in $\{1,2\}$, and the new payoffs are nonnegative. This proves preservation of the invariant under rematching.

There are no other allocation-changing moves: the invariant excludes negative payoffs, so resignation and dismissal are unavailable. Information refinements cannot change anything because $\Omega$ is a singleton. Record splitting also leaves all four invariant properties unchanged.

Finally, every rematch satisfies
$$ \delta\le\varepsilon, $$
because its selected workers must come from the total unmatched worker mass $\varepsilon$.

3. Stability forces exponentially many rematches

Let $X$ denote the total firm mass currently paying wage $2$.

If $X>0$, some positive-mass firm record pays wage $2$. There is also unmatched worker mass $\varepsilon>0$. These two slices block at wage $1$, so the state is not stable.

Conversely, if $X=0$, every firm pays wage $1$. The inequalities
$$ U<p<1 $$
have no integer solution for any current worker payoff $U\ge 0$. Hence there is no blocking pair. The state is individually rational by the invariant.

Since $\Omega$ is a singleton, individual rationality and absence of blocking imply
$$ N=\Omega,\qquad K(\omega^*)=\Omega. $$
The state is therefore stable.

We have proved that a reachable state is stable if and only if
$$ X=0. $$

Initially $X=1$. Every rematch of mass $\delta$ decreases $X$ by exactly $\delta$, and every such mass satisfies $\delta\le\varepsilon$.

Consequently, if a path reaches stability after $n$ rematches of masses $\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_n$, then
$$ 1=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_j \le n\varepsilon. $$
Therefore,
$$ n\ge\frac{1}{\varepsilon}=2^r. $$

This bound applies to every legal path, regardless of how records are split or which positive rational masses are selected.

4. The bound is attained

There is a legal path of exactly $2^r$ rematches.

At each step, select all unmatched worker mass $\varepsilon$ and mass $\varepsilon$ from the remaining wage-$2$ firm record, and rematch them at wage $1$.

After $k$ steps, the allocation has:

  • wage-$1$ matched mass $k\varepsilon$;
  • wage-$2$ matched mass $1-k\varepsilon$;
  • unmatched worker mass $\varepsilon$.

For every $k<2^r$, the remaining wage-$2$ mass satisfies
$$ 1-k\varepsilon=(2^r-k)\varepsilon\ge\varepsilon, $$
so the next selection is available. It is blocking by the characterization above.

After the final step, all firms pay wage $1$, and the state is stable. This construction requires no record consolidation: each step may retain its newly created matched record separately.

Thus the minimum number of rematches is exactly
$$ 2^r. $$

5. Balanced masses also permit the obstruction

Unequal total masses are not essential.

Add a second firm cohort, of mass $\varepsilon$, with a second firm type $f_0$ satisfying
$$ \nu_{w,f_0}=0,\qquad \phi_{w,f_0}=1. $$
Leave this additional cohort initially unmatched.

Worker and firm totals now both equal $1+\varepsilon$. The support size and number of productivity types remain constant:
$$ \tau=3,\qquad m=3. $$

The added firms can never participate in a blocking pair. As long as they remain unmatched, such a pair would require
$$ p>U\ge 0,\qquad 1-p>0, $$
and hence
$$ 0<p<1, $$
which has no integer solution.

Inductively, they remain unmatched throughout every legal path. They therefore introduce no additional moves, and the preceding invariant and lower-bound proof apply unchanged to the original firms.

So exponentially many rematches are unavoidable even with balanced total masses, nonnegative integer payoff tables, and strictly positive surplus for every worker–firm type pair.

6. Complexity consequence and remaining question

On this family, $m$, $\tau$, and $|\Omega|$ are fixed, while $L_r=O(r)$. Any explicit path must contain at least $2^r$ rematch entries. Writing those entries already requires exponentially many output operations.

Accordingly, no algorithm can always construct the requested explicit sequence in time polynomial in $m,\tau,|\Omega|,L$. This is an unconditional output-length obstruction, independent of any complexity assumption.

A succinct instruction describing repeated rematches could avoid writing every step, but it would not satisfy the question’s requirement to include the explicit sequence’s length and encoding.

The result resolves the notes’ open question about polynomial-size paths negatively. NP-hardness under an appropriate reduction notion remains unproved; exponential output length alone does not establish it.
