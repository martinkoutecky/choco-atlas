Given \(A\), \(O\), finite \(T\), rational \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), a strict principal preference, a symmetric strictly proper scoring rule \(s\), and hidden conditional distributions \(q_a\), each query elicits type-level conditional reports for a binary action partition through mass-weighted LSS scoring and \(D_\mu^{\mathrm{ISA}}\); output the unique \(a^\star\) satisfying \(q_{a^\star}\succ q_a\) for all \(a\ne a^\star\), while minimizing worst-case queries and determining whether \(O(\log |A|)\) comparisons suffice uniformly over \(\mu\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) be an explicitly listed action set with \(m=|A|\geq2\), and let \(O\) be a finite exhaustive set of mutually exclusive outcomes, with \(k=|O|\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\geq2\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type controls its entire mass's reports and knows every hidden rational distribution \(q_a\in\Delta(O)\). The principal has complete, transitive preferences over distributions, with fixed tie-breaking including action labels, and a unique best action \(a^\star\). Preferences are accessed through an exact comparison oracle.
Fix a symmetric strictly proper scoring rule \(s(r,o)\), finite on the rational simplex and accessed through an exact evaluation oracle. Mass-weighted LSS assigns per-unit-mass score \(S_t(p,o)=s(p_t,o)-\sum_u\mu_u s(p_u,o)\), with no additional report-dependent term; thus \(\sum_t\mu_tS_t=0\).
A query partitions the current action set into two nonempty subsets \(B_1,B_2\). Each type reports \(p_{t,B_j}\in\Delta(O)\), predicting the terminal outcome conditional on continuing the protocol within \(B_j\), rather than an average over its actions.
The rule \(D_\mu^{\mathrm{ISA}}\) retains the subset whose most preferred report, taken over all positive-mass types, is most preferred overall, using fixed tie-breaking. The protocol repeats until a singleton remains and executes that action.
After the terminal outcome is observed, each visited query is scored using its retained subset's reports; each type maximizes its mass times the sum of these scores. Reports and continuation strategies are evaluated at pure subgame-perfect equilibrium, and identification must hold in every such equilibrium. Equilibrium computation is outside the query model; no auxiliary prediction of the winning action is available.
Use rational reports. State oracle running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\), where \(L\) includes the binary encoding lengths of the public data, hidden distributions, and reports processed; count partition queries separately from preference-oracle calls.

Is equilibrium-correct identification of \(a^\star\) polynomial-time solvable in this oracle model, or is it NP-hard? What is the minimum worst-case number of partition queries, and do \(O(\log m)\) queries suffice uniformly over \(\mu\)?

For finitely many predictors sharing knowledge of the truth, zero-additive-term LSS scoring supports identification with \(O(\log m)\) partition queries. An augmented mechanism allowing an additional non-zero-sum prediction of the selected action achieves \(O(1)\) comparisons. Result

Under the deterministic oracle-protocol reading, equilibrium-correct identification is impossible for the specified cumulative-payoff mechanism. There is a counterexample with $m=3$, $\tau=2$, and $k=3$, using three distinct deterministic outcome distributions. It works for every positive pair of rational masses.

Consequently, the proposed polynomial-time versus NP-hardness dichotomy does not describe this mechanism: its equilibrium requirement fails independently of computation. There is no finite uniform worst-case partition-query bound, and $O(\log m)$ queries do not suffice uniformly over $\mu$.

Here, a deterministic protocol chooses partitions from public information and previous reporting histories; it cannot directly access the hidden distributions. Continuation strategies can depend on previous reports, as in the continuation subgames analyzed in the NOTES.

1. Scoring identities

Let the three outcomes be $o_1,o_2,o_3$, and let $e_i$ denote the distribution concentrated on $o_i$.

Symmetry of the scoring rule means invariance under simultaneous permutations of report coordinates and outcome labels. Consequently, there are finite constants $D,E$ such that

$$ s(e_i,o_i)=D,\qquad s(e_i,o_j)=E\quad\text{for }i\ne j. $$

Strict propriety at the deterministic distribution $e_i$ implies

$$ \delta:=D-E>0. $$

Strict propriety also gives, for every rational report $r$,

$$ s(r,o_i)\leq D, $$

with equality exactly when $r=e_i$.

Take two types of masses $w$ and $1-w$, where $0<w<1$ is rational. At any visited query, their mass-weighted payoffs are respectively

$$ w(1-w)\bigl(s(r_1,o)-s(r_2,o)\bigr) $$

and its negative. Thus, writing $V$ for the expected sum of type $1$'s base scores minus type $2$'s base scores over visited queries,

$$ U_1=w(1-w)V,\qquad U_2=-w(1-w)V. $$

Because $w(1-w)>0$, type $1$ maximizes $V$ and type $2$ minimizes it. Their strategic incentives therefore do not depend on the particular positive masses.

2. Preferences, actions, and the first partition

Choose the principal’s preference order so that

$$ e_1\succ e_2\succ r \quad\text{for every }r\notin\{e_1,e_2\}. $$

Order the remaining distributions lexicographically, and apply the fixed label tie-breaking when distributions coincide. This defines complete, transitive preferences. The order is discontinuous, which is permitted by the stated hypotheses.

Suppose the first partition is

$$ \{a\}\quad\text{versus}\quad\{b,c\}. $$

Name the two actions in the pair so that ties between identical reports favor $b$ over $c$. Set

$$ q_a=e_2,\qquad q_b=e_3,\qquad q_c=e_1. $$

The unique best action is therefore $c$. All three true distributions are distinct.

If the pair is retained, its next partition is necessarily $\{b\}$ versus $\{c\}$. We first specify an equilibrium of this continuation after every possible rational first-round reporting history.

3. A continuation equilibrium at every inherited history

Let $x,y$ be the first-round reports of types $1,2$ for the retained pair. Define the inherited expected score differences

$$ H_b=s(x,o_3)-s(y,o_3),\qquad H_c=s(x,o_1)-s(y,o_1). $$

For second-round reports $r_{t,b},r_{t,c}$, the cumulative value is

$$ V= \begin{cases} H_b+s(r_{1,b},o_3)-s(r_{2,b},o_3),&\text{if }b\text{ is selected},\\ H_c+s(r_{1,c},o_1)-s(r_{2,c},o_1),&\text{if }c\text{ is selected}. \end{cases} $$

The inherited terms remain relevant because they depend on the selected action.

Lemma. Every such continuation has a pure equilibrium with value

$$ v(H_b,H_c) = \min\left\{H_b+\delta,\, \max\{H_b-\delta,H_c\}\right\}. $$

One equilibrium is specified by the following table. Each ordered pair gives that type’s reports for $(b,c)$.

| Condition | Type $1$ | Type $2$ | Selected action | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $H_c<H_b-\delta$ | $(e_1,e_1)$ | $(e_3,e_1)$ | $b$ | $H_b-\delta$ |
| $H_b-\delta\leq H_c\leq H_b+\delta$ | $(e_3,e_1)$ | $(e_3,e_1)$ | $c$ | $H_c$ |
| $H_c>H_b+\delta$ | $(e_3,e_1)$ | $(e_1,e_1)$ | $b$ | $H_b+\delta$ |

Proof. The report $e_1$ is the principal’s uniquely most preferred distribution. A report of $e_1$ for $b$ therefore forces $b$, because ties with $c$ favor $b$.

In the middle case, both types report truthfully for both actions, and $c$ is selected.

For a deviation by type $1$, the opponent’s report $e_1$ for $c$ remains available. Thus:

  • If the deviation selects $c$, strict propriety bounds the value by $H_c$.
  • If it selects $b$, the deviator must report $e_1$ for $b$. The value is $H_b+E-D=H_b-\delta\leq H_c$.

For type $2$, selection of $c$ gives value at least $H_c$. Selection of $b$ requires its report $e_1$ for $b$ and gives $H_b+D-E=H_b+\delta\geq H_c$. Neither type improves.

In the lower case, type $1$ forces $b$. Against this strategy, every deviation by type $2$ still selects $b$, and

$$ V=H_b+E-s(r_{2,b},o_3)\geq H_b-\delta. $$

Thus type $2$ cannot reduce the value. If type $1$ retains its report $e_1$ for $b$, the value stays $H_b-\delta$. If it withdraws that report, the opponent’s report $e_1$ for $c$ makes $c$ win, giving

$$ V\leq H_c<H_b-\delta. $$

Thus type $1$ cannot increase the value.

In the upper case, type $2$ forces $b$. Every deviation by type $1$ still selects $b$, and

$$ V=H_b+s(r_{1,b},o_3)-E\leq H_b+\delta. $$

If type $2$ withdraws its forcing report, $c$ wins and gives

$$ V\geq H_c>H_b+\delta. $$

Thus neither type improves. The three equilibrium values give the displayed formula. $\square$

This proof establishes the required unilateral payoff bounds against arbitrary rational deviations, without invoking compactness or an equilibrium-existence theorem.

4. A full incorrect pure subgame-perfect equilibrium

At the first query, prescribe these reports:

| Type | Report for $\{a\}$ | Report for $\{b,c\}$ |
|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $e_2$ | $e_3$ |
| $2$ | $e_2$ | $e_1$ |

The pair is retained because $e_1\succ e_2$.

After every history retaining the pair, prescribe the continuation equilibrium from the lemma.

Equilibrium-path outcome and payoffs

On the displayed first-round history,

$$ H_b=D-E=\delta,\qquad H_c=E-D=-\delta. $$

Since $H_c<H_b-\delta=0$, the continuation reports are

$$ (r_{1,b},r_{1,c})=(e_1,e_1),\qquad (r_{2,b},r_{2,c})=(e_3,e_1). $$

Tie-breaking selects $b$, whose deterministic outcome is $o_3$. The first query contributes $\delta$ to $V$, and the second contributes $-\delta$. Hence

$$ V=0,\qquad U_1=U_2=0. $$

The executed action is $b$, although $c$ is uniquely best.

Every full deviation by type $1$ is unprofitable

Allow type $1$ to change both first-round reports and its entire continuation strategy.

If the singleton is retained, its outcome is $o_2$. Type $2$’s singleton report remains truthful, so the deviator’s payoff is

$$ w(1-w)\bigl(s(r,o_2)-D\bigr)\leq0. $$

If the pair is retained, write $x$ for type $1$’s new pair report. Type $2$’s pair report remains $e_1$, giving

$$ H_c=s(x,o_1)-D\leq0 $$

and

$$ H_b-\delta =s(x,o_3)-E-(D-E) =s(x,o_3)-D\leq0. $$

Therefore

$$ v(H_b,H_c)\leq0. $$

At this new history, type $2$ follows the lemma’s continuation strategy. The lemma proves that type $1$ cannot obtain more than its equilibrium value against that strategy, even by changing its continuation report. Its total payoff consequently remains nonpositive.

Every full deviation by type $2$ is unprofitable

If the singleton is retained, type $1$’s singleton report remains truthful, and type $2$’s payoff is again nonpositive by strict propriety.

If the pair is retained, type $1$’s pair report remains $e_3$, while its singleton report remains $e_2$. Consequently, type $2$’s new pair report $y$ must satisfy

$$ y\in\{e_1,e_2\}. $$

Every other distribution ranks below the unchanged singleton report $e_2$ and cannot make the pair win.

For $y=e_1$, the inherited values are

$$ (H_b,H_c)=(\delta,-\delta), $$

and for $y=e_2$, they are

$$ (H_b,H_c)=(\delta,0). $$

Both give

$$ v(H_b,H_c)=0. $$

The lemma’s type $1$ continuation strategy prevents type $2$ from reducing $V$ below this value. Thus type $2$ cannot obtain positive payoff.

Subgame perfection

The first-round strategies are a Nash equilibrium by the preceding full-deviation analysis. After every possible history retaining the pair, the prescribed continuation is a pure Nash equilibrium by the lemma. Histories retaining the singleton are terminal.

Thus the constructed strategy profile is a full pure subgame-perfect equilibrium. In particular, the harmful inherited history occurs on its equilibrium path, closing the principal gap identified in the NOTES.

All prescribed reports are rational simplex vertices.

5. Why choosing a different first partition cannot give a uniform guarantee

Fix three public action labels, their tie-breaking, two positive rational masses, the scoring rule, and the preference order above.

Consider any deterministic protocol using only the stipulated information channels. Before its first partition query, it has received no reports. Preference-oracle and score-oracle answers depend on the public preferences and scoring rule, not on which hidden distribution belongs to which action.

Its first partition therefore has some singleton $a$ and some pair. Within that pair, call the label favored by tie-breaking $b$ and the other label $c$. Choose the hidden input distributions to be

$$ q_a=e_2,\qquad q_b=e_3,\qquad q_c=e_1. $$

Section 4 supplies a full incorrect pure subgame-perfect equilibrium for this protocol. After the pair is retained, its only possible partition is the terminal comparison already analyzed.

Hence

$$ \text{every deterministic protocol has an admissible input with an incorrect pure SPE.} $$

The construction works for every $0<w<1$, including equal masses. It uses the first strategically nontrivial action-set size, distinct true distributions, and an existing full equilibrium; it does not exploit vacuous equilibrium correctness.

6. Query complexity and oracle running times

Equilibrium-correct identification

For the full stipulated input class, the class of uniformly equilibrium-correct deterministic protocols is empty. Its minimum worst-case partition-query count is therefore undefined, or $+\infty$ under the extended-cost convention.

In particular, uniform $O(\log m)$ equilibrium-correct identification is false. The counterexample itself follows a balanced tree and uses exactly two partition queries.

Execution costs

These remain efficient, but do not imply correctness. If a run visits $d$ queries, then

$$ d\leq m-1. $$

Balanced partitions give

$$ d\leq\lceil\log_2 m\rceil. $$

Finding the most preferred of the $2\tau$ reports at each query uses

$$ (2\tau-1)d $$

preference-oracle calls. Scoring the observed terminal outcome uses

$$ \tau d $$

score-oracle evaluations. Evaluating all retained reports against one fixed terminal distribution would use at most $\tau k d$ score-oracle evaluations.

Partition construction and rational report handling require polynomial overhead in $m,\tau,k,L$; balanced partition construction along the visited path uses $O(m)$ action-list operations. Arbitrary score-oracle implementations remain outside these overhead bounds.

The impossibility also holds with computationally elementary oracles. For example, the Brier score

$$ s(r,o)=2r(o)-\sum_z r(z)^2 $$

is symmetric, finite, and strictly proper because

$$ \sum_o q(o)\bigl(s(q,o)-s(r,o)\bigr)=\|q-r\|_2^2. $$

Its rational evaluations take polynomial bit time in $k,L$. The preference oracle above uses vertex equality tests followed by lexicographic comparison, also in polynomial bit time. Thus computational difficulty of the oracles is unnecessary for the counterexample.

The two-action boundary case

For completeness, $m=2$ does have equilibrium-correct identification with exactly one partition query.

Define the expected-score regret

$$ R(q,r)=\sum_o q(o)\bigl(s(q,o)-s(r,o)\bigr). $$

In a single terminal comparison, truthful reporting for both actions guarantees type $t$ nonnegative payoff:

$$ U_t=\mu_t\sum_{u\ne t}\mu_u R(q_a,p_{u,a})\geq0 $$

for whichever action the deviation selects. Since total payoff is zero, every equilibrium payoff is zero.

If the winning report for the selected action is untruthful, another type can replace its report for that action by truth. The original winning report remains, so the selected action cannot change. The deviator obtains a strictly positive weighted sum of regrets, a contradiction. Once the winning report is truthful, any other untruthful selected-action report would give the winning type strictly positive payoff. Thus all selected-action reports are truthful.

If the selected action is inferior, reporting truth for the best action makes it win. Every opponent’s report for that action must differ from truth; otherwise it would already outrank the selected truthful report. The deviation therefore gives strictly positive payoff, another contradiction.

Finally, against fully truthful opponents, any deviation yields

$$ -\mu_t(1-\mu_t)R(q_a,p_{t,a})\leq0. $$

Thus a truthful equilibrium exists, and every pure equilibrium is correct. Its execution uses $2\tau-1$ preference-oracle calls and $\tau$ score evaluations.
