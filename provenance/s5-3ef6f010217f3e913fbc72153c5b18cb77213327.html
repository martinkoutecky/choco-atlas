The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) non-rival services, supported monotone submodular valuations \(v_1,\ldots,v_\tau:2^{[n]}\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), and rational cohort shares \(\mu_i\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), choose a menu of lotteries \(X_i\) over integral bundles and expected payments \(p_i\) to maximize per-capita revenue \(\sum_i\mu_i p_i\), subject to \(v_i(X_i)-p_i\ge v_i(X_j)-p_j\) for all \(i,j\) and \(v_i(X_i)-p_i\ge 0\) for all \(i\). For support size \(\tau=\mathrm{poly}(n)\), can this be approximated within any \(1/\mathrm{poly}(n)\) factor in polynomial time, given value-oracle, demand-oracle, or explicit valuation access?

Their Theorem 3 and Corollary 10 cover all three access models. Their definition of approximation permits randomized algorithms with an expected-objective guarantee. Exact equivalence of the optimization problems

Given the society in the question, define a single buyer’s type distribution by

\( \Pr[V=v_i]=\mu_i,\qquad i\in[\tau]. \)

The positive masses summing to one make this a probability distribution. Its support has exactly \(\tau\) elements because the valuations are distinct.

From a mass-menu to a mechanism. On report \(j\), allocate a bundle sampled from \(X_j\) and charge the deterministic payment \(p_j\). Every realized allocation is feasible because every bundle in \(2^{[m]}\) is feasible.

For true type \(i\), the expected utility from report \(j\) is

\( u_i(j)=\mathbb{E}_{S\sim X_j}[v_i(S)]-p_j=v_i(X_j)-p_j. \)

Consequently, truthfulness requires exactly

\( v_i(X_i)-p_i\geq v_i(X_j)-p_j \quad\text{for every }i,j. \)

Individual rationality requires exactly

\( v_i(X_i)-p_i\geq0 \quad\text{for every }i. \)

Weak inequalities permit the designated truthful report, as required in the question. Expected revenue is exactly

\( \mathbb{E}[p_V]=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i p_i=R. \)

From a mechanism to a mass-menu. For each report \(j\), let \(X_j\) be the mechanism’s allocation distribution and let \(p_j\) be its expected payment. Even if allocation and payment are correlated, linearity of expectation gives

\( \mathbb{E}[v_i(S_j)-P_j] =v_i(X_j)-\mathbb{E}[P_j] =v_i(X_j)-p_j. \)

Thus truthfulness, individual rationality, and revenue again give precisely the menu’s constraints and objective.

Both transformations preserve every achievable revenue. In particular,

\( \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{menu}} =\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{single\ buyer}}. \)

There are no additional allocation restrictions to check: different reports are alternative choices by one buyer, and the society has no constraints coupling different consumers’ allocations.

Access models and running time

The identification leaves every valuation unchanged. A value query is therefore the same query in both formulations. A demand query likewise asks for a maximizer of the same expression,

\( v_i(S)-\sum_{a\in S}q_a. \)

An arbitrary maximizing answer remains valid in either formulation. Explicit valuation programs are also unchanged.

The lower bound already concerns polynomial-size support. Moreover, the paper’s reduction controls numerical encoding lengths: Theorem 7 bounds valuation and probability bit complexity, and Lemma 5 gives polynomial bounds for its hard instances. Hence allowing running time polynomial in \(L\) does not circumvent those instances. [Bit-complexity bounds](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.4002#page=24), [hard-instance bounds](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.4002#page=27).

Now fix \(c>0\) and suppose a menu algorithm had expected revenue at least \(m^{-c}\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{menu}}\). Applying it to the distribution above would produce a single-buyer mechanism satisfying

\( \mathbb{E}[R]\geq m^{-c}\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{single\ buyer}}, \)

with the same valuation access and polynomial overhead. This contradicts the respective oracle lower bound, or implies \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\) in the explicit-program model.

Scope of the support quantifier

The conclusion concerns the general polynomial-support class, not every separately fixed support bound.

Indeed, a simple menu always achieves a \(1/\tau\) fraction of optimum when full-bundle values are available. Put \(w_i=v_i([m])\). Individual rationality and monotonicity imply

\( p_i\leq v_i(X_i)\leq w_i, \qquad \mathrm{OPT}\leq\sum_i\mu_i w_i. \)

Choose \(i^\ast\) maximizing \(\mu_iw_i\), and offer the full bundle at price \(w_{i^\ast}\), alongside the free empty bundle. Type \(i^\ast\) may be designated to buy when indifferent. Revenue is therefore at least

\( \mu_{i^\ast}w_{i^\ast} \geq\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_i\mu_iw_i \geq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{\tau}. \)

Thus, for a fixed bound \(\tau\leq m^d\), this gives an \(m^{-d}\)-approximation in the value-oracle and explicit-program models. The stated hardness says that no fixed exponent works uniformly over the general class allowing polynomial support size. There is no contradiction.
