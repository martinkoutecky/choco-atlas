The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_A,\rho_B\), integer capacity \(K\), and a finite distribution \(\mu\) over complete agent types \(t=(d_{t,A},d_{t,B},w_t)\), find a finite-support mass allocation \(q_{t,r,s}\ge0\) to integral bundles \((r,s)\) with \(r+s\le K\), satisfying \(\sum_{r,s}q_{t,r,s}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,r,s}r q_{t,r,s}=\rho_A\), and \(\sum_{t,r,s}s q_{t,r,s}=\rho_B\), support-wise wEF1 \(\phi_t(r,s)/w_t\le d_t(r',s')/w_u\) whenever \(q_{t,r,s}q_{u,r',s'}>0\), where \(d_t(r,s)=r d_{t,A}+s d_{t,B}\) and \(\phi_t\) is the minimum disutility after removing one bundle item, and such that no feasible fractional reallocation weakly improves almost every holder and strictly improves a positive-mass set; output \(q\) or certify none exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) complete types \(t=(d_{t,A},d_{t,B},w_t)\) with positive mass \(\mu_t\), each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Assume \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), binary-rational disutilities \(d_{t,A},d_{t,B}\geq0\), and binary-rational weights \(w_t>0\), without weight normalization.
There are \(m=2\) chore categories, with nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_A,\rho_B\), and a common capacity \(K\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\) encoded in binary. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An allocation is an explicit finite list of positive binary-rational masses \(q_{t,r,s}\), where \((r,s)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^2\) and \(r+s\leq K\); omitted entries are zero. Require \(\sum_{r,s}q_{t,r,s}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,r,s}r q_{t,r,s}=\rho_A\), and \(\sum_{t,r,s}s q_{t,r,s}=\rho_B\).
Write \(d_t(a,b)=a d_{t,A}+b d_{t,B}\). Set \(\phi_t(0,0)=0\); for a nonempty integral bundle, \(\phi_t(r,s)\) is the minimum of \(d_t(r-1,s)\) when \(r>0\) and \(d_t(r,s-1)\) when \(s>0\).
Support-wise weighted EF1 requires \(\phi_t(r,s)/w_t\leq d_t(r',s')/w_u\) for every pair of occupied cells \((t,r,s)\) and \((u,r',s')\).
Interpret each occupied cell as an atomless set of holders of mass \(q_{t,r,s}\). A feasible fractional reallocation assigns each holder measurable real amounts \((a,b)\geq0\) with \(a+b\leq K\), preserving the two aggregate supplies; it need not satisfy weighted EF1.
An allocation is fractionally Pareto optimal if no such reallocation gives \(d_t(a,b)\leq d_t(r,s)\) almost everywhere and strict inequality on a set of positive mass.

Is the problem of outputting a support-wise weighted EF1, fractionally Pareto-optimal allocation, or reporting that none exists, solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\) (including the binary encoding of \(K\)), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many individually listed agents and two chore categories, without capacity constraints, a weighted EF1 and fractionally Pareto-optimal integral allocation always exists and is computable in polynomial time. [Garg, Murhekar, and Qin](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17173v1).

NP-hardness of the mass-allocation problem

The problem is NP-hard. This holds with two categories, strictly positive supplies and disutilities, and instances in which every feasible allocation is automatically fractionally Pareto optimal.

The reduction below addresses the main obstacle posed by atomless masses: weighted EF1 will force every type to occupy exactly one integral bundle. Thus splitting a type’s mass cannot relax the integer choices.

As in the supplied notes, rationals are encoded by binary numerators and denominators.

1. Encoding SUBSET SUM as a nonnegative integer equation

Use the standard NP-complete SUBSET SUM problem: given positive integers $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ and an integer $V$, determine whether some subset sums to $V$. We may assume $n\geq 1$ and $1\leq V\leq S$, where

$$ S=\sum_{j=1}^n v_j. $$

Instances outside these restrictions are trivial.

Set

$$ B=2n(S+1)+2,\qquad C=B^{n+2}. $$

For each $j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$, introduce two positive integer coefficients

$$ \alpha_{j,0}=C+B^j,\qquad \alpha_{j,1}=C+B^j+v_j, $$

and define

$$ T=nC+\sum_{j=1}^n B^j+V. $$

Relabel these $N=2n$ coefficients as $\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{N-1}$.

Lemma 1. The equation

$$ \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\alpha_i x_i=T,\qquad x_i\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}, $$

has a solution if and only if the SUBSET SUM instance is positive. Every solution satisfies

$$ x_{j,0}+x_{j,1}=1,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^n v_jx_{j,1}=V. $$

Proof. Write $X=\sum_i x_i$. Every coefficient lies between $C$ and $C+B^n+S$. The choice of $B$ gives

$$ C>n(B^n+S),\qquad C>\sum_{j=1}^n B^j+V. $$

Consequently, $X\geq n+1$ makes the left side exceed $T$, while $X\leq n-1$ makes it smaller than $nC$. Hence $X=n$.

Subtracting $nC$ leaves

$$ \sum_{j=1}^n B^j(x_{j,0}+x_{j,1}) +\sum_{j=1}^n v_jx_{j,1} = \sum_{j=1}^n B^j+V. $$

Each coefficient $x_{j,0}+x_{j,1}$ is at most $n<B$, and

$$ \sum_{j=1}^n v_jx_{j,1}\leq nS<B. $$

Thus neither side has carries in its base-$B$ expansion. Uniqueness of that expansion gives the two asserted identities. Conversely, any subset summing to $V$ supplies such a solution by choosing exactly one of $x_{j,0},x_{j,1}$ to equal $1$. ∎

We will also use

$$ 0<T\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\alpha_i, $$

which follows from $V\leq S$.

2. Full capacity makes efficiency automatic

Lemma 2. Suppose

$$ \rho_A+\rho_B=K $$

and every type satisfies $d_{t,A}>d_{t,B}$. Then every feasible allocation is fractionally Pareto optimal.

Proof. For an integral allocation,

$$ \sum_{t,r,s}q_{t,r,s}(K-r-s) = K-\rho_A-\rho_B=0. $$

All summands are nonnegative. Therefore every occupied bundle satisfies $r+s=K$.

Likewise, any feasible measurable fractional reallocation satisfies $a+b=K$ almost everywhere: the nonnegative function $K-a-b$ has integral zero.

For a holder originally receiving $(r,K-r)$, weak improvement consequently means

$$ (d_{t,A}-d_{t,B})(a-r)\leq 0. $$

Since $d_{t,A}-d_{t,B}>0$, this implies $a\leq r$ almost everywhere. Preservation of category-$A$ supply gives

$$ \int(r-a)=0. $$

Thus $a=r$ almost everywhere, and then $b=K-r$ almost everywhere. No strict improvement on positive mass is possible. ∎

3. A pair of types that forbids mass splitting

Fix integers $K\geq 4$, $R\geq 2$, and a positive weight $W$. Consider two positive-mass types with data

| Type | $d_A$ | $d_B$ | $w$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $1$ | $K+2R$ | $2R+2$ | $W$ |
| $2$ | $K+2R$ | $2R-1$ | $2W$ |

Both types have $d_A>d_B$. For a full bundle $(r,K-r)$, define its disutility under these two types by

$$ D_1(r)=(2R+2)K+(K-2)r, $$

$$ D_2(r)=(2R-1)K+(K+1)r. $$

Put $a=K+2R$. For either type, its EF1 residual satisfies

$$ \phi_\ell(r,K-r)\geq D_\ell(r)-a. $$

There is equality whenever $r>0$, because removing an $A$ chore then removes the greatest disutility. When $r=0$, only a $B$ chore can be removed, and its disutility is smaller than $a$.

Lemma 3. If both types occur in a weighted EF1 allocation and all their occupied bundles are full, then each type occupies a single bundle. Their category-$A$ counts satisfy

$$ r_2=2r_1+2R. $$

Proof. Take any occupied count $r$ of type $1$ and any occupied count $r'$ of type $2$. Weighted EF1 in the first direction implies

$$ \frac{D_1(r)-a}{W}\leq \frac{D_1(r')}{2W}. $$

After rearrangement,

$$ (K-2)(2r-r') \leq 2a-(2R+2)K =-2R(K-2). $$

Since $K-2>0$,

$$ r'\geq 2r+2R. $$

The reverse comparison implies

$$ \frac{D_2(r')-a}{2W}\leq\frac{D_2(r)}W, $$

and hence

$$ (K+1)(r'-2r) \leq a+(2R-1)K =2R(K+1). $$

Thus $r'\leq 2r+2R$, proving equality.

This holds for every pair of occupied counts. Fixing one occupied cell of either positive-mass type shows that the other type has only one occupied count. ∎

4. Constructing the society

Starting from the coefficients of Lemma 1, set $Q=256$ and define

$$ M= 2\left(\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}\alpha_i\right) \left(\prod_{h=1}^{N}(Q^h-1)\right). $$

For $i=0,\ldots,N-1$, set

$$ W_i=16^i,\qquad R_i=M Q^i=M W_i^2,\qquad p_i=R_i+1. $$

Let

$$ R_{\max}=R_{N-1},\qquad K=16R_{\max}. $$

For each $i$, create the two types from Section 3 with parameters $(K,R_i,W_i)$. There are exactly $\tau=2N=4n$ complete types: their weights $16^i$ and $2\cdot16^i$ are all distinct.

Coprime integers

The integers $p_i$ are pairwise coprime. Indeed, for $j>i$,

$$ p_j-Q^{j-i}p_i=1-Q^{j-i}. $$

Any common divisor of $p_i,p_j$ therefore divides $Q^{j-i}-1$, which divides $M$. But $p_i\equiv 1\pmod M$, so that common divisor is $1$.

Also, $\alpha_i$ divides $M$, so

$$ \gcd(\alpha_i,p_i)=1. $$

Define

$$ P=\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}p_i,\qquad H_i=\alpha_i\frac{P}{p_i},\qquad H=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}H_i,\qquad U=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}H_iR_i. $$

Give both types of pair $i$ mass

$$ \mu_{i,1}=\mu_{i,2}=\frac{H_i}{2H}. $$

These masses are positive and sum to $1$. Finally, set

$$ \rho_A=\frac{5U+3PT}{2H},\qquad \rho_B=K-\rho_A. $$

Both supplies are strictly positive. To check this, use $T\leq\sum_i\alpha_i$ to obtain

$$ PT\leq\sum_i H_ip_i. $$

Consequently,

$$ 0<\rho_A \leq \frac{\sum_iH_i(5R_i+3p_i)}{2H} = \frac{\sum_iH_i(8R_i+3)}{2H} \leq 4R_{\max}+\frac32 <K. $$

All constructed disutilities are positive, and their category differences are $K-2$ and $K+1$. Lemma 2 therefore applies to every feasible allocation of this society.

5. Any requested allocation yields a subset

Suppose the constructed society admits a weighted EF1 allocation.

Since total supply equals $K$, all occupied bundles are full. Lemma 3 forces pair $i$ to use single counts

$$ r_i,\qquad r'_i=2r_i+2R_i. $$

The category-$A$ supply equation becomes

$$ \rho_A = \frac{1}{2H}\sum_iH_i(3r_i+2R_i). $$

Substituting the definition of $\rho_A$ gives

$$ \sum_iH_ir_i=U+PT. $$

Reduce this identity modulo $p_i$. Every $H_j$ with $j\neq i$ is divisible by $p_i$, as is $P$. Moreover, $H_i$ is invertible modulo $p_i$, because the $p_i$ are pairwise coprime and $\gcd(\alpha_i,p_i)=1$. Hence

$$ r_i\equiv R_i\pmod{p_i}. $$

Because $r_i\geq 0$ and $0<R_i<p_i=R_i+1$, there is an integer $x_i\geq 0$ such that

$$ r_i=R_i+p_ix_i. $$

Substituting and cancelling $U$ yields

$$ \sum_iH_ip_ix_i=PT. $$

Since $H_ip_i=\alpha_iP$, division by $P$ gives

$$ \sum_i\alpha_ix_i=T. $$

Lemma 1 now produces a subset summing to $V$.

Thus even existence of a weighted EF1 allocation, without separately requiring efficiency, implies a positive SUBSET SUM instance.

6. A subset yields an allocation satisfying every EF1 comparison

Conversely, suppose SUBSET SUM is positive. Lemma 1 supplies $x_i\in\{0,1\}$ with

$$ \sum_i\alpha_ix_i=T. $$

Give all holders of the first type in pair $i$ the bundle with category-$A$ count

$$ r_i=R_i+p_ix_i, $$

and all holders of the second type the bundle with count

$$ r'_i=2r_i+2R_i=4R_i+2p_ix_i. $$

Fill each bundle to capacity with category-$B$ chores. These are valid integral bundles because

$$ 0<r_i\leq 2R_i+1,\qquad 0<r'_i\leq 6R_i+2\leq K. $$

The calculation in Section 5 reverses to show that category-$A$ supply is exactly $\rho_A$. Since every bundle is full, category-$B$ supply is then $\rho_B$.

It remains to prove all EF1 comparisons.

Comparisons within one pair

Both counts are positive, so residual disutility equals disutility minus $K+2R_i$. The two comparisons in Lemma 3 are therefore exact equalities when

$$ r'_i=2r_i+2R_i. $$

Comparison of a cell with itself holds because removing a chore cannot increase disutility.

Comparisons between different pairs

Fix a sender in pair $i$, and abbreviate

$$ R=R_i,\qquad W=W_i. $$

Write its disutilities as

$$ d_A=K+2R,\qquad d_B=2R+\varepsilon,\qquad c=d_A-d_B=K-\varepsilon, $$

where $\varepsilon=2$ for the first type and $\varepsilon=-1$ for the second.

For any type of weight $w$, its bundle at $x_j=0$ has category-$A$ count $Mw^2$. Under the fixed sender’s disutilities, its normalized disutility is

$$ \frac{d_BK}{w}+cMw. $$

Writing $w=zW$ and using $R=MW^2$, this becomes

$$ \frac{F_\varepsilon(z)}W, \qquad F_\varepsilon(z)= \frac{(2R+\varepsilon)K}{z} +(K-\varepsilon)Rz. $$

A target’s actual normalized category-$A$ count increases from its baseline by

$$ \frac{p_jx_j}{W_j} $$

for either type of pair $j$. Since $c>0$, its actual normalized disutility is at least the baseline value.

For a target in a different pair, the weight ratio satisfies

$$ z\leq\frac18 \quad\text{or}\quad z\geq16. $$

If $z\leq1/8$, then $2R+\varepsilon\geq R$, giving

$$ F_\varepsilon(z)\geq 8RK. $$

If $z\geq16$, then $K-\varepsilon\geq K-2\geq K/2$, again giving

$$ F_\varepsilon(z)\geq 8RK. $$

Therefore every target in a different pair has normalized disutility at least

$$ \frac{8RK}{W}. $$

We now bound the sender’s normalized residual. At $x_i=0$, multiplying this residual by $W$ gives, respectively,

$$ G_1=3RK+K-4R $$

for the first type, and

$$ G_2=3RK-K+R $$

for the second. These formulas follow by subtracting one $A$ chore from the respective baseline bundles. Both satisfy $G_\ell\leq4RK$.

Switching to $x_i=1$ increases either type’s normalized residual by $cp_i/W$. Since $p_i=R+1$,

$$ cp_i\leq(K+1)(R+1)\leq2KR. $$

The last inequality holds for $K\geq4$ and $R\geq2$. Thus either sender’s normalized residual is at most

$$ \frac{6RK}{W} < \frac{8RK}{W}. $$

This proves EF1 against every target in a different pair. Together with the within-pair comparisons, the allocation is support-wise weighted EF1.

It is fractionally Pareto optimal by Lemma 2.

7. Encoding length and conclusion

Every step of the construction has polynomial bit complexity in the original SUBSET SUM input.

The coefficients $\alpha_i$ and $T$ have polynomial bit length. Furthermore,

$$ \log M = O\left(\sum_i\log\alpha_i+N^2\right), $$

and consequently $R_i$, $W_i$, $p_i$, and the binary-encoded capacity $K$ have polynomial bit length. Also,

$$ \log P=O(N\log M+N^2). $$

Thus $H_i$, the masses, and both supplies have polynomial bit length. The construction uses a polynomial number of exact integer and rational operations and produces only $4n$ support types.

We have proved the equivalence

$$ \text{SUBSET SUM is positive} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed society admits a weighted EF1, fractionally Pareto-optimal allocation}. $$

Hence the existence decision problem is NP-hard. An algorithm that outputs the requested allocation or correctly reports nonexistence would decide these instances, so the requested output problem is NP-hard as well. A polynomial-time algorithm in $m,\tau,L$ would therefore imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.
