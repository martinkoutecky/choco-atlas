1. Source problem and construction

$$ \sum_{j=1}^m\sqrt{n_j}\le K. $$

Related square-root-sum comparison problems are studied in [Kayal and Saha, *On the Sum of Square Roots of Polynomials and Related Problems*](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/Sum20of20Square20Roots20ToCT.pdf). The reduction below is self-contained and assumes no complexity classification for this source problem.

First consider the nontrivial range

$$ m\le K<\sum_{j=1}^m n_j. $$

The remaining inputs will be handled in Section 5.

Write

$$ N=\max_j n_j,\qquad D=2N,\qquad a=\frac{1}{2D},\qquad S=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{1}{n_j}. $$

Construct a society with one type of mass $1$, one period, and $m$ auctions. Set

$$ \Delta=1,\qquad \underline\lambda=1,\qquad \overline\lambda=\frac{11}{10},\qquad M=\frac{21}{20},\qquad \eta=1,\qquad \alpha=\frac{1}{D^3}. $$

The initial-state law is the single atom

$$ (k_1,\lambda_1)=(2,M). $$

The valuation support is $[a,1]$, with density

$$ f(v)=\frac{2v}{1-a^2}. $$

Participation probabilities and capacities are

$$ \pi_j=\frac{1}{n_jS}, \qquad \kappa_j= \pi_j\frac{1-n_j/D^2}{1-a^2}. $$

Finally, choose the verification threshold

$$ \varepsilon= \frac{2}{3(1-a^2)} \left( \frac{K}{SD^3}-a^3 \right). $$

All these input quantities are rational. For the analysis, introduce

$$ h_j=\frac{\sqrt{n_j}}{D}. $$

The numbers $h_j$ are not part of the encoded output. They satisfy

$$ \frac{1}{D}\le h_j\le\frac12, \qquad h_j^2=\frac{n_j}{D^2}, \qquad \kappa_j=\pi_j\frac{1-h_j^2}{1-a^2}. $$

2. Verification of the elementary input promises

The valuation density is a rational polynomial and is continuously differentiable on $[a,1]$. Moreover,

$$ \int_a^1 f(v)\,dv = \frac{1-a^2}{1-a^2} =1, $$

$$ 0< \frac{2a}{1-a^2} \le f(v)\le \frac{2}{1-a^2}. $$

$$ \sum_{j=1}^m\pi_j = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{1}{n_j} =1. $$

$$ a^2<h_j^2<1. $$

Consequently,

$$ 0< \frac{1-h_j^2}{1-a^2} <1, $$

so every auction satisfies

$$ 0<\kappa_j<\pi_j. $$

This is precisely the capacity promise for a single type of mass $1$.

The threshold is also admissible. Since $S\le m$ and $K\ge m$,

$$ \frac{K}{SD^3}\ge\frac{1}{D^3}> \frac{1}{8D^3}=a^3, $$

$$ S\ge\frac{m}{N}, \qquad K<\sum_j n_j\le mN, \qquad D=2N. $$

It remains to establish the functional regularity promises. Those promises require a statement about all admissible multiplier profiles, rather than merely the constant initial profile.

3. Functional regularity on the entire promised domain

$$ H= \left\{ q:\int_Aq(a)\,da=M \right\}. $$

The valuation tail, for $a<y<1$, is

$$ \Pr(v>y)=\frac{1-y^2}{1-a^2}. $$

I claim that the uncapped clearing price in auction $j$ is

$$ p_j(q)=\frac{h_j}{\sqrt{R(q)}}. $$

$$ p_j(q)q(a) \ge h_j\frac{10}{11} \ge \frac{10}{11D} > \frac{1}{2D}=a, $$

$$ p_j(q)q(a) \le h_j\frac{11}{10} \le \frac{11}{20}<1. $$

The bounds on the thresholds have a uniform positive margin. Hence, in a neighborhood of this positive price, the tail is

$$ B_j((p,\infty)) = \pi_j\frac{1-p^2R(q)}{1-a^2}, $$

which is strictly decreasing in $p$. Every sufficiently close smaller price therefore has tail strictly greater than capacity. Monotonicity of the tail gives the same conclusion for every smaller nonnegative price. This proves the claimed price under the infimum definition in the task.

There are no bid atoms at these prices: conditional on any label’s multiplier, the uncapped bid has a density. Integrating these zero point masses over labels still gives zero.

$$ C_0= \frac{1}{1-a^2}\sum_{j=1}^m\pi_jh_j^3. $$

$$ \pi_jp_j(q) \frac{1-p_j(q)^2q(a)^2}{1-a^2}. $$

$$ \begin{aligned} \ell(q)(a) &= \sum_j \frac{\pi_jp_j(q)}{1-a^2} \left[ 1-p_j(q)^2q(a)^2-(1-h_j^2) \right]\\ &= \sum_j \frac{\pi_jh_j^3}{(1-a^2)\sqrt{R(q)}} \left[ 1-\frac{q(a)^2}{R(q)} \right]\\ &= C_0\frac{R(q)-q(a)^2}{R(q)^{3/2}}. \end{aligned} $$

The constant profile

$$ q^\star(a)=M $$

is interior and belongs to $H$. Since $R(q^\star)=M^2$, the displayed formula yields

$$ \ell(q^\star)(a)=0 $$

for every label.

$$ h=q-q', \qquad q_s=q'+sh,\qquad 0\le s\le1. $$

$$ \int_Ah\,da=0. $$

$$ D\ell(r)[h](a) = C_0\left[ -\frac{2r(a)h(a)}{R^{3/2}} + \left( -\frac{1}{R^{3/2}} +\frac{3r(a)^2}{R^{5/2}} \right) \int_A rh\,da \right]. $$

Pairing this derivative with $h$, and using $\int_Ah=0$, gives

$$ \int_AhD\ell(r)[h]\,da = C_0\left[ -\frac{2\int_A rh^2\,da}{R^{3/2}} + \frac{ 3\left(\int_A rh\,da\right) \left(\int_A r^2h\,da\right) }{R^{5/2}} \right]. $$

Here are explicit bounds for both terms. Write

$$ u=\frac{11}{10}, \qquad \delta=\frac{1}{20}, \qquad \|h\|_2^2=\int_Ah^2\,da. $$

Because $r\ge1$ and $R\le u^2$,

$$ \frac{2\int_A rh^2\,da}{R^{3/2}} \ge \frac{2}{u^3}\|h\|_2^2. $$

Also, $|r-M|\le\delta$. The zero integral of $h$ and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality imply

$$ \left|\int_A rh\,da\right| = \left|\int_A(r-M)h\,da\right| \le\delta\|h\|_2. $$

Similarly, since $|r^2-M^2|\le\delta(2M+\delta)$,

$$ \left|\int_A r^2h\,da\right| = \left|\int_A(r^2-M^2)h\,da\right| \le \delta(2M+\delta)\|h\|_2. $$

Using $R\ge1$, we therefore obtain

$$ \int_AhD\ell(r)[h]\,da \le -C_0 \left[ \frac{2}{u^3} - 3\delta^2(2M+\delta) \right] \|h\|_2^2. $$

The bracket is strictly greater than $1$, because

$$ \frac{2}{u^3} - 3\delta^2(2M+\delta) = \frac{2000}{1331}-\frac{129}{8000} = \frac{15828301}{10648000}>1. $$

Furthermore, $h_j\ge1/D$ and $\sum_j\pi_j=1$, so

$$ C_0 = \frac{\sum_j\pi_jh_j^3}{1-a^2} \ge \frac{1}{D^3} =\alpha. $$

It follows that

$$ \int_AhD\ell(r)[h]\,da \le -\alpha\|h\|_2^2. $$

All profiles and directions used here are bounded, and $R$ stays bounded away from zero. Thus differentiation under the label integral and integration of the derivative along the segment are justified by bounded domination. Integrating over $s$ proves

$$ \begin{aligned} \int_A(q-q')(\ell(q)-\ell(q'))\,da &= \int_0^1\int_AhD\ell(q_s)[h]\,da\,ds\\ &\le -\alpha\int_A(q-q')^2\,da. \end{aligned} $$

This is the required strong-monotonicity inequality on the entire promised domain.

Finally, $D\ge2$, and hence

$$ \eta=1\le\frac{D^3}{2}=\frac{1}{2\alpha}. $$

Every functional regularity promise is satisfied.
