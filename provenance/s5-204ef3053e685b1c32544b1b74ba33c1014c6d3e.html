The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational per capita supplies \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^d\) and supported types \(t\) with rational additive weights \(w_{t,e}\), compute each type’s largest threshold \(s_t\) such that, for every \(\alpha\in[0,1)\) and every mass distribution \(r\) of total mass \(\alpha\) over integral bundles \(z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^d\) with \(w_t\cdot z<s_t\) and aggregate use \(\sum_z r_z z\le q\), the residual inventory \(q-\sum_z r_z z\) can be assigned to mass \(1-\alpha\) as a distribution \(y\) over bundles satisfying \(w_t\cdot z\ge s_t\), with \(\sum_z y_z=1-\alpha\) and \(\sum_z y_z z=q-\sum_z r_z z\). Return \(s_t\) and a full mass partition at \(\alpha=0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society of total mass \(1\) is given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=d\ge1\) kinds of goods, binary-rational per capita supplies \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^m\) with \(\sum_e q_e>1\), and binary-rational additive weights \(w_{t,e}\ge0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Bundles are \(z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^m\), including the empty bundle, with value \(w_t\cdot z\). A mass distribution over bundles has nonnegative real coefficients and finite first moments; countable support is allowed. Inventory inequalities are coordinatewise, and all residual goods must be assigned.

For each type \(t\), let \(s_t\) be the largest threshold \(s\ge0\) such that for every \(\alpha\in[0,1)\) and every distribution \(r\) satisfying \(\sum_z r_z=\alpha\), \(\sum_z r_z z\le q\), and \(r_z>0\Rightarrow w_t\cdot z<s\), there exists a distribution \(y\) satisfying \(\sum_z y_z=1-\alpha\), \(\sum_z y_z z=q-\sum_z r_z z\), and \(y_z>0\Rightarrow w_t\cdot z\ge s\).

The computational problem is to compute every \(s_t\) exactly and, separately for each type, a full mass partition \(y^{(t)}\) witnessing its threshold at \(\alpha=0\), represented by finitely many integral bundles and binary-rational masses. These are self-partitions of the entire unit-mass society evaluated using one type's valuation.

Is this continuous threshold-and-partition problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies with nonnegative additive valuations, computing the residual maximin share and finding a corresponding acceptable partition are weakly NP-hard, already with two agents ([Feige](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19961)). A finite-agent integer-additive packing–covering characterization is also known ([Qin](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30257v1)); the search located no result settling the continuous problem defined above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted additive weights to nonnegative rationals, as required by the normalized monotone goods valuations in the discrete definition; zero weights remain allowed.
  • Normalized society mass to one and required positive supported type masses summing to one.
  • Used m=d for the number of good kinds and translated the discrete assumption that there are more goods than agents into total per capita item supply greater than one.
  • Allowed nonnegative real bundle masses with countable support and finite first moments in the quantified distributions; retained empty bundles and unrestricted integral bundle multiplicities.
  • Restricted thresholds to nonnegative values and retained strict rejection and weak acceptance inequalities.
  • Interpreted the requested full partition as a separate self-partition for each type, rather than one simultaneous allocation across different types.
  • Specified finite binary-rational representations for the returned partitions so that exact computational complexity is well-defined.

Partial result: polynomial time for a fixed number of weight levels

The unrestricted complexity question remains unresolved. I prove a new polynomial-time result covering arbitrary rational valuations with a fixed number of distinct positive weights. I also give an elementary exact formula when the two positive weights are integer multiples of one another.

Throughout, rational numbers are encoded by binary numerators and denominators, as in the notes.

1. The proved computational result

Theorem. Fix a constant $K$. Suppose that, for every type, the positively valued kinds having positive supply have at most $K$ distinct weights. Then every residual threshold and every requested full self-partition can be computed exactly in time polynomial in $m$, $\tau$, and $L$.

The polynomial may depend on $K$. Consequently, this proves polynomial-time solvability for every fixed number of goods kinds, and also for an unbounded number of kinds when each type has a fixed number of positive weight levels.

Reduction to weight levels

Fix one type with valuation $w$. Its mass $\mu_t$ does not occur in its threshold definition. Since the requested partitions are separate self-partitions, types can be processed independently.

A kind with zero supply cannot occur in any positive-mass bundle of a feasible removal or completion. A kind with zero weight can be assigned after completing the positively valued coordinates: assigning additional goods preserves acceptability, and the remaining population mass is positive whenever a completion is required.

If no positively valued supplied kind remains, the threshold is $0$. A positive threshold fails at zero removal because the average value is $0$.

Otherwise, let the distinct positive weights be $c_1,\ldots,c_k$, where $k\leq K$. Define
$$ I_i=\{e:q_e>0,\ w_e=c_i\}, \qquad Q_i=\sum_{e\in I_i}q_e. $$
An original bundle projects to the integral vector
$$ a_i=\sum_{e\in I_i}z_e, $$
and its value is $\sum_i c_i a_i$.

This aggregation preserves residual feasibility in both directions:

  • Projecting an original removal gives a removal whose inventory is bounded by $Q$.
  • Any aggregate removal can be lifted to original kinds by assigning each of its level-$i$ items independently to kind $e\in I_i$ with probability $q_e/Q_i$. If its aggregate consumption is $b_i$, the expected consumption of kind $e$ is $b_iq_e/Q_i\leq q_e$.
  • After any original removal, an aggregate completion can likewise be lifted using probabilities proportional to the actual residual supplies within each level. A level with zero residual supply has zero count in every positive-mass completion bundle and needs no lifting.

These constructions preserve mass, value, and inventory. They preserve finite first moments because the expected coordinate consumptions are the prescribed finite inventories. Zero-weight residual goods can then be assigned by the rounding construction proved below.

Thus it suffices to solve the problem in dimension $k$.

2. An exact finite characterization using integer-hull vertices

Integer scaling and the threshold grid

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of $c_1,\ldots,c_k$, and put
$$ W_i=Dc_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad V=W\cdot Q. $$
We work with scaled values $W\cdot a$. A scaled threshold $T$ corresponds to the original threshold $T/D$.

Every bundle has integral scaled value. Therefore all scaled thresholds in
$$ (j-1,j],\qquad j\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, $$
have the same good and bad bundles and hence the same feasibility status.

Feasibility is downward closed: lowering the threshold restricts permitted removals and enlarges the set of acceptable completion bundles. Threshold $0$ is feasible. Moreover, every feasible scaled threshold is at most $V$, because its required zero-removal partition has mass $1$ and average value $V$.

It follows that the maximum exists and is an integer
$$ T_*\in\{0,1,\ldots,\lfloor V\rfloor\}. $$
Indeed, feasibility anywhere in $(j-1,j]$ implies feasibility at $j$, and the average-value bound then gives $j\leq V$.

The integers $D$, $W_i$, and $\lfloor V\rfloor$ have polynomial encoding length.

Good and bad integer hulls

Fix an integer $T>0$. Define the bounded rational polytopes
$$ P_T^-=\{z\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^k:W\cdot z\leq T-1\}, $$
and
$$ P_T^+= \left\{ z\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^k: W\cdot z\geq T,\quad z_i\leq\left\lceil\frac{T}{W_i}\right\rceil \text{ for every }i \right\}. $$
Let
$$ F_T=\operatorname{vert}\!\left( \operatorname{conv}(P_T^-\cap\mathbb Z^k) \right), $$
and
$$ C_T=\operatorname{vert}\!\left( \operatorname{conv}(P_T^+\cap\mathbb Z^k) \right). $$
These vertices are integral bundles. Every member of $F_T$ is bad, and every member of $C_T$ is good.

Every bad bundle belongs to $\operatorname{conv}(F_T)$, directly from the definition of the integer hull.

Every good bundle contains a good bundle in $P_T^+\cap\mathbb Z^k$. To prove this, repeatedly delete individual items while retaining value at least $T$. The resulting bundle $a$ is coordinatewise minimal. If
$$ a_i>\left\lceil\frac{T}{W_i}\right\rceil, $$
then deleting one level-$i$ item leaves at least $\lceil T/W_i\rceil$ such items, which alone have value at least $T$. This contradicts minimality. Hence $a\in P_T^+$, and consequently $a\in\operatorname{conv}(C_T)$.

Completion capacity

For $v\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^k$, define
$$ c_T(v)= \max\left\{ \sum_{a\in C_T}x_a: x_a\geq0,\quad \sum_{a\in C_T}x_a a\leq v \right\}. $$
The maximum is attained. The feasible region is closed, and
$$ T\sum_a x_a \leq \sum_a x_a(W\cdot a) \leq W\cdot v, $$
so all coefficients are bounded.

Lemma. For $\beta>0$, a distribution of mass $\beta$ using exactly inventory $v$, supported on good bundles, exists if and only if $c_T(v)\geq\beta$.

Proof. Given a good distribution, replace each bundle by a contained minimal good bundle. The possible replacements form a finite set. Express each replacement as a convex combination of $C_T$ and aggregate these combinations. The result is a feasible packing of mass $\beta$, proving necessity.

Conversely, scale a packing of mass at least $\beta$ to mass exactly $\beta$. Let its inventory be $u\leq v$, and put
$$ h=\frac{v-u}{\beta}. $$
For a uniform variable $U\in[0,1)$, define
$$ \eta_i(U)= \lfloor h_i\rfloor+ \mathbf1_{\{U<h_i-\lfloor h_i\rfloor\}}. $$
This integral vector takes at most $k+1$ values and satisfies $\mathbb E\eta=h$. Independently adding it to each packed bundle assigns precisely the missing inventory $\beta h=v-u$. All augmented bundles remain good because the weights are nonnegative. The resulting distribution has finite support and finite first moments. $\square$

Compressing all permitted removals

Replacing a bad bundle by a convex combination of $F_T$ preserves both its mass and its inventory. Consequently, every possible removal inventory and mass can be represented by coefficients in
$$ R_T(Q)= \left\{ r\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{F_T}: \sum_b r_b\leq1,\quad \sum_b r_b b\leq Q \right\}. $$
Conversely, these coefficients describe actual bad-bundle distributions.

By the completion lemma, residual feasibility is therefore equivalent to
$$ c_T\!\left(Q-\sum_b r_b b\right)+\sum_b r_b\geq1 \qquad \text{for every }r\in R_T(Q). $$
At removal mass $1$, this inequality is automatic. Including that boundary does not assert the existence of a completion for a zero-mass population.

Dual tests

The dual of the capacity LP is
$$ c_T(v)= \min\{p\cdot v:p\in D_T\}, $$
where
$$ D_T= \{p\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^k:p\cdot a\geq1 \text{ for every }a\in C_T\}. $$
This polyhedron is nonempty because $W/T\in D_T$, and it contains no lines because $p\geq0$. Linear-programming duality gives an attained minimum; its optimal face contains a vertex. Thus, writing $E_T=\operatorname{vert}(D_T)$,
$$ c_T(v)=\min_{p\in E_T}p\cdot v. $$

Substitution into the residual inequality gives the exact characterization
$$ T\text{ is residual-feasible} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\cdot Q\geq 1+ \max_{r\in R_T(Q)} \sum_{b\in F_T}r_b(p\cdot b-1) \quad \text{for every }p\in E_T. $$
There is no exchange of different quantifier types here: the substituted condition simply requires the displayed inequality for every $r$ and every $p$.

3. Polynomial-time threshold computation for fixed dimension

The needed external result is the following standard integer-hull theorem.

Known theorem. For each fixed dimension $k$, the integer hull of a bounded rational polyhedron given by binary-encoded inequalities has polynomially many vertices, and all its vertices can be enumerated in polynomial time. The vertex bound is due to Cook, Hartmann, Kannan, and McDiarmid; polynomial-time enumeration is due to Hartmann. Both statements are recorded in [Basu–Hildebrand–Köppe, Theorem 3.4 and the following paragraph](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/07/3109.pdf).

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • Both $P_T^-$ and $P_T^+$ have dimension at most the fixed constant $k$.
  • Both are rational and bounded. For $P_T^-$, positivity of $W_i$ gives $z_i\leq(T-1)/W_i$.
  • Their defining inequalities have encoding length polynomial in the input length and the encoding length of $T$.
  • Both have integer points: the empty bundle belongs to $P_T^-$, and $\lceil T/W_i\rceil$ copies of any one level belong to $P_T^+$.

Therefore $F_T$ and $C_T$ can be computed in polynomial time and have polynomial size.

The polyhedron $D_T$ lies in fixed dimension $k$ and has $|C_T|+k$ explicit inequalities. Its vertices can be enumerated by choosing every set of $k$ linearly independent boundary equations, solving it, and checking feasibility. This is polynomially many choices for fixed $k$. Every vertex is found because its active constraint normals span $\mathbb R^k$.

For each such vertex, the maximization over $R_T(Q)$ is an explicit rational LP with polynomially many variables and constraints. It can therefore be solved exactly in polynomial time.

This proves polynomial-time residual-feasibility testing for an integer $T$. Downward closure then permits binary search over
$$ 0\leq T\leq\lfloor V\rfloor. $$
The number of queries is polynomial because $\lfloor V\rfloor$ has polynomial bit length. The exact original threshold is $T_*/D$.

This algorithm uses the universal residual condition itself, rather than only zero-removal feasibility.

4. Constructing the requested full partitions

It remains to show that the threshold algorithm also produces full partitions over the original kinds in polynomial time.

Suppose first that $T_*>0$. At zero removal, feasibility gives
$$ \lambda_a\geq0,\qquad \sum_{a\in C_{T_*}}\lambda_a=1,\qquad \sum_{a\in C_{T_*}}\lambda_a a\leq Q. $$
Choose a vertex of this feasible rational polytope.

At most $k+1$ coefficients are positive. Otherwise, the columns associated with positive coefficients admit a nonzero linear dependence preserving the mass equation and all $k$ inventory expressions. A sufficiently small perturbation in both directions preserves nonnegativity and contradicts extremality.

The supported integral bundles have polynomial bit length by the box bounds defining $P_{T_*}^+$. Their rational coefficients have polynomial bit length by Cramer’s rule applied to their defining systems.

Lifting a pooled bundle with polynomial support

Fix one supported pooled bundle $a$. For each original kind $e\in I_i$, prescribe the mean count
$$ \xi_e=a_i\frac{q_e}{Q_i}. $$
Within level $i$, order its kinds and form cumulative sums
$$ 0=H_0\leq H_1\leq\cdots\leq H_\ell=a_i, $$
with successive differences equal to their $\xi_e$.

For $U\in[0,1)$, assign to kind $e$ the number of points
$$ j+U,\qquad j=0,\ldots,a_i-1, $$
lying in its interval $[H_{e-1},H_e)$.

This gives exactly $a_i$ integral items within level $i$. Its expected count for kind $e$ is the interval length $\xi_e$: integrating over $U$ sums the lengths of the intersections of that interval with the unit intervals partitioning $[0,a_i)$.

Use the same $U$ for every level. Changes occur only at fractional parts of cumulative sums. Hence there are at most $m+1$ distinct resulting bundles, with rational probabilities given by interval lengths between consecutive breakpoints.

This construction does not enumerate the potentially enormous number $a_i$ of items. Away from breakpoints, each count is computed directly as
$$ \left\lceil H_e-U\right\rceil- \left\lceil H_{e-1}-U\right\rceil. $$
All computations therefore have polynomial bit complexity.

Every lifted bundle has exactly the pooled counts $a$ and consequently remains good. If the pooled packing uses inventory $u_i$, its lifted expected consumption of kind $e\in I_i$ is
$$ \frac{q_e}{Q_i}u_i\leq q_e. $$

Assigning all surplus goods

After lifting, let $u\leq q$ be the actual expected inventory used. Apply the rounding construction from Section 2 to $q-u$, with mass $1$, and add the resulting vectors to the lifted bundles.

This assigns every residual good, including zero-weight goods. It preserves acceptability and produces a rational full partition with at most
$$ (k+1)(m+1)^2 $$
bundles. All bundle coordinates and masses have polynomial encoding length.

Finally, keep this finite bundle list fixed and choose a vertex of the LP requiring nonnegative masses, total mass $1$, and exact inventory $q$. It has at most $m+1$ positive coefficients, by linear dependence among the columns consisting of mass and the $m$ coordinate counts. Cramer’s rule again gives polynomial-size rational coefficients.

For threshold $0$, apply rounding directly to the empty bundle with mean augmentation $q$.

Thus a full witness with at most $m+1$ integral bundles can be found in polynomial time. Processing the types independently proves the theorem.

5. An elementary exact formula for two divisible weight levels

The preceding theorem allows arbitrary rational ratios. The following special case additionally admits a closed formula and explicit infeasibility witnesses.

Suppose the two positive weights are $c$ and $kc$, where $c>0$ and $k\geq2$ is an integer. Let
$$ x=\sum_{e:w_e=c}q_e, \qquad y=\sum_{e:w_e=kc}q_e, \qquad V=x+ky. $$
We normalize values by $c$, so a pooled bundle $(i,j)$ has value $i+kj$.

Exact completion capacity

For an integer threshold $T>0$, write
$$ T=ak+r,\qquad 0\leq r<k. $$
For residual supplies $(u,v)$, the maximum packable good mass is
$$ c_T(u,v)= \begin{cases} (u+kv)/T, & r=0,\\[2mm] u/T+v, & a=0,\\[2mm] \displaystyle \min\left\{ \frac{u+kv}{T}, \frac{u/r+v}{a+1} \right\}, & a\geq1,\ r>0. \end{cases} $$

For $r=0$, the weighted-value bound is attained by packing the pure bundles $(T,0)$ and $(0,a)$.

For $a=0$, a good bundle either contains a high-weight item or at least $T$ low-weight items. The capacity is attained by $(T,0)$ and $(0,1)$.

For $a\geq1$ and $r>0$, every good bundle $(i,j)$ satisfies
$$ i+kj\geq T \quad\text{and}\quad i/r+j\geq a+1. $$
For the second inequality, if $j\geq a+1$ it is immediate. Otherwise,
$$ i\geq r+k(a-j), $$
and hence
$$ i/r+j \geq a+1+(k/r-1)(a-j) \geq a+1. $$
These inequalities give the two upper bounds in the formula.

They are attained as follows. If $v\leq au/r$, use masses
$$ \lambda_{(r,a)}=\frac va, \qquad \lambda_{(T,0)}=\frac{u-rv/a}{T}. $$
They use exactly $(u,v)$ and have total mass $(u+kv)/T$.

If $v\geq au/r$, use masses
$$ \lambda_{(r,a)}=\frac ur, \qquad \lambda_{(0,a+1)}=\frac{v-au/r}{a+1}. $$
They use exactly $(u,v)$ and have total mass $(u/r+v)/(a+1)$.

Comparing the two expressions shows that their order changes precisely at $v=au/r$. This proves the capacity formula.

Exact residual-feasibility test

Proposition. The integer threshold $T=ak+r>0$ is residual-feasible precisely under the following conditions:

| Case | Necessary and sufficient condition |
|---|---|
| $r=0$ | $V\geq T$ |
| $a=0$ | $x/T+y\geq1$ |
| $a\geq1,\ r>0$ | $V\geq T$ and $x/(T-1)+y/(a+1)\geq1$ |

Proof. When $r=0$, necessity follows from average value. A bad removal of mass $\alpha$ consumes value at most $\alpha(T-1)$, so
$$ u+kv\geq V-\alpha(T-1)\geq T(1-\alpha). $$
The capacity formula proves sufficiency.

When $a=0$, a bad bundle contains no high-weight item and at most $T-1$ low-weight items. Its consumption of the potential $u/T+v$ is at most $1$ per unit mass. Thus an initial potential at least $1$ leaves potential at least $1-\alpha$. Necessity follows from zero-removal capacity.

Now suppose $a\geq1$ and $r>0$. Average value again requires $V\geq T$. If
$$ \frac{x}{T-1}+\frac{y}{a+1}<1, $$
remove mass
$$ \alpha=\frac{x}{T-1}<1 $$
of the bad bundle $(T-1,0)$. This exhausts the low-weight supply. Every good residual bundle then needs at least $a+1$ high-weight items, but
$$ y<(a+1)(1-\alpha). $$
Completion is impossible. This proves the second necessary condition.

For sufficiency, define
$$ g(u,v)=\frac{u}{T-1}+\frac{v}{a+1}. $$
Every bad bundle $(i,j)$ satisfies
$$ \frac{i}{T-1}+\frac{j}{a+1} \leq 1+j\left(\frac1{a+1}-\frac{k}{T-1}\right) \leq1, $$
because $i+kj\leq T-1$ and $T-1<k(a+1)$.

Therefore any bad removal of mass $\alpha$ leaves
$$ g(u,v)\geq g(x,y)-\alpha\geq1-\alpha. $$
Also,
$$ T-1-r(a+1)=a(k-r)-1\geq0, $$
since $a\geq1$ and $k-r\geq1$. Consequently,
$$ \frac{u/r+v}{a+1}\geq g(u,v)\geq1-\alpha. $$
The weighted-value inequality gives
$$ \frac{u+kv}{T}\geq1-\alpha $$
as before. Both capacity bounds therefore meet the remaining mass. The completion lemma assigns the residual inventory exactly. $\square$

Closed formula for the maximum threshold

Let
$$ h=\left\lfloor\frac Vk\right\rfloor. $$
The exact original threshold is $s=cT_*$, where
$$ T_*= \begin{cases} \displaystyle \left\lfloor\frac{x}{1-y}\right\rfloor, & h=0,\\[4mm] \displaystyle \max\left\{ hk,\; \min\left\{ \lfloor V\rfloor,\; \left\lfloor 1+\frac{x(h+1)}{h+1-y} \right\rfloor \right\} \right\}, & h\geq1. \end{cases} $$
Both denominators are positive in their respective cases, since $y\leq V/k<h+1$.

To verify the formula, first suppose $h=0$. Every candidate positive threshold is below $k$, and the feasibility test becomes
$$ T\leq\frac{x}{1-y}. $$
Furthermore,
$$ \frac{x}{1-y}\leq V<k, $$
where the first inequality follows from
$$ V-\frac{x}{1-y} = y\left(k-\frac{x}{1-y}\right)\geq0. $$
Thus the stated floor gives the maximum.

For $h\geq1$, threshold $hk$ is feasible because $V\geq hk$. Any larger candidate satisfies
$$ hk<T\leq\lfloor V\rfloor<k(h+1), $$
so its quotient in division by $k$ is $h$, with positive remainder. Its additional feasibility condition is
$$ \frac{x}{T-1}+\frac{y}{h+1}\geq1, $$
equivalently
$$ T\leq1+\frac{x(h+1)}{h+1-y}. $$
Taking the greatest integer satisfying these bounds gives the formula.

For example, weights $(1,3)$ and supplies $(2,2)$ give $V=8$, $h=2$, and threshold $7$. Threshold $8$ fails: removing mass $2/7$ of $(7,0)$ leaves mass $5/7$ and only two high-weight items. Every acceptable residual bundle needs three such items, requiring $15/7>2$.

6. What remains open

These results fully solve the requested threshold-and-partition task when each type has a fixed number of distinct positive weight levels. They do not give a polynomial bound uniform in that number: the integer-hull enumeration and dual-vertex enumeration depend on the dimension.

For unrestricted valuations, I have neither a polynomial-time algorithm nor a hardness reduction whose YES instances satisfy every permitted removal. The general target therefore remains open in this write-up.
