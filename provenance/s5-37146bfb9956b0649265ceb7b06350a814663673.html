The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite list of exchange-network templates \(G_q\), each with rational edge weights, a valid initial message vector, positive mass \(\lambda_q\), and a unique Nash bargaining solution with positive gap \(\sigma_q\), suppose a continuum of copies of each template bargains only within its own copy. How many rounds suffice to compute role earnings \(\hat\gamma_{q,i}\) satisfying \(\max_{q,i}|\hat\gamma_{q,i}-\gamma^*_{q,i}|\le\varepsilon\), as a function of the support size, input length, gaps, weights, and \(\varepsilon\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) template types with positive binary-rational masses \(\lambda_q\), normalized by \(\sum_q\lambda_q=1\). The support size counts listed types, not possible types. Type \(q\) consists of a finite simple undirected graph \(G_q=(V_q,E_q)\) with nonempty edge set, positive rational edge weights, and rational initial messages. A continuum of copies of each template bargains exclusively within each copy, with identical initialization in every copy of a given type. Let \(m=\max_q|V_q|\), \(W_q=\max_{ij\in E_q}w_{q,ij}\), and let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including the rational tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\).
A Nash bargaining outcome is a matching \(M\) and nonnegative earnings \(\gamma\), with unmatched vertices earning zero, \(\gamma_i+\gamma_j=w_{ij}\) on matching edges, and \(\gamma_i+\gamma_j\ge w_{ij}\) on other edges. On every matching edge, balance requires \(\gamma_i-\max_{k\in\partial i\setminus\{j\}}(w_{ik}-\gamma_k)_+=\gamma_j-\max_{k\in\partial j\setminus\{i\}}(w_{jk}-\gamma_k)_+\). Here \((x)_+=\max\{x,0\}\), \(\partial i\) denotes the neighbors of \(i\), and an empty maximum is zero.
Each template is promised to have a unique Nash bargaining outcome \((M_q^*,\gamma_q^*)\) with positive structural gap \(\sigma_q\). The gap refers to its successive max-min slack decomposition: first fix unmatched earnings to zero; then maximize the minimum still-unfixed node slack \(\gamma_i\) and unmatched-edge slack \(\gamma_i+\gamma_j-w_{ij}\), over stable allocations respecting matching-edge equalities and previously fixed earnings. Successive limiting structures \(C_1,\ldots,C_k\) fix the remaining earnings; their internal vertex sets partition the matched vertices, and each structure is an alternating path, blossom, or bicycle. A blossom is an odd alternating cycle with an attached alternating stem; a bicycle consists of two odd alternating cycles joined by an alternating path. Boundary vertices already fixed may belong to a structure's edge endpoints without belonging to its internal vertex set.
For structure \(C_r\), write \(s_r\) for its slack, \(E_1(C_r)\) for its matching edges, and \(E_2(C_r)\) for its edges supplying positive best alternatives to internal vertices. Each internal vertex has surplus \(s_r\) over its best alternative; edge slack is zero on \(E_1(C_r)\) and \(s_r\) on \(E_2(C_r)\). Writing \(V_{\mathrm{ext}}(C_r)\) for all endpoints of its edges, the gap promise is \(\sigma_q\le\min\{s_1,s_2-s_1,\ldots,s_k-s_{k-1}\}\), together with \(\gamma_i^*+\gamma_j^*-w_{ij}\ge s_r+\sigma_q\) for every edge outside \(C_r\) whose endpoints both lie in \(V_{\mathrm{ext}}(C_r)\).
Fix damping \(\kappa=1/2\). In each synchronous round, suppressing the template index, compute \(o_{i\to j}^t=(w_{ij}-\alpha_{i\setminus j}^t)_+-\tfrac12(w_{ij}-\alpha_{i\setminus j}^t-\alpha_{j\setminus i}^t)_+\), then update \(\alpha_{i\setminus j}^{t+1}=\tfrac12\max_{k\in\partial i\setminus\{j\}}o_{k\to i}^t+\tfrac12\alpha_{i\setminus j}^t\). Initially \(\alpha_q^0\in[0,W_q]^{2|E_q|}\); round earnings are \(\gamma_{q,i}^t=\max_{k\in\partial i}o_{q,k\to i}^t\). All copies and templates advance simultaneously, and earnings are per role, without mass weighting.
Is computing rational role earnings satisfying \(\max_{q,i}|\hat\gamma_{q,i}-\gamma_{q,i}^*|\le\varepsilon\) polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or NP-hard? What worst-case number of the specified rounds guarantees this accuracy, as a function of \(m,\tau,L,(W_q)_q,(\sigma_q)_q,\varepsilon\)?
For a finite network, the verified anchor guarantees \(C n^7[W/\sigma+\log(1+\sigma/\varepsilon)]\) rounds; this is a numerical-parameter bound, not a polynomial bound in binary input length. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0911.1767v2).
Later work proves convergence from every valid initialization and gives a bound for approximate fixed-point residuals; residual accuracy is distinct from distance to the unique earnings vector. No source treating precisely the distribution-of-independent-copies formulation was located. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/1004.2079v2).

  • Normalized template-copy masses to sum to one; earnings are per-copy role earnings rather than mass-weighted totals.
  • Fixed the unspecified damping factor to one half, an allowed constant in the discrete dynamics.
  • Specified simultaneous rounds across templates and identical initialization for copies of the same template.
  • Required positive edge weights, nonempty edge sets, rational valid initial messages, and positive rational tolerance.
  • Required unmatched vertices to earn zero, as explicitly stated in the later treatment.
  • Interpreted each supplied gap as a positive rational lower bound on the structural gap, and included its encoding in the input length.
  • Defined the requested size parameter as the maximum template vertex count and distinguished bit complexity from dependence on numerical gaps and weights.

1. Result. Computing the requested role earnings is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, the promised earnings can be computed exactly as rationals, without simulating the bargaining dynamics.

Write \(n_q=|V_q|\). There is an absolute constant \(C\), for damping \(\kappa=1/2\), such that the following number of synchronous rounds suffices:

\( \displaystyle T=\left\lceil C\max_{1\le q\le\tau}n_q^7\left[\frac{W_q}{\sigma_q}+\log\left(1+\frac{\sigma_q}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\right\rceil. \)

Consequently, replacing every \(n_q\) by \(m\) gives another valid bound. This is a sufficient bound, without a claim of tightness. Its numerical-parameter dependence does not establish polynomial convergence time in binary input length.

2. Reduction to the listed templates. The update rule is deterministic and uses only messages within a copy. Identically initialized copies of a template therefore have identical messages and earnings at every round, by induction on the round number.

Thus the computational task consists of solving the \(\tau\) listed finite networks. The masses \(\lambda_q\) do not enter either the bargaining equations or the requested maximum error. For simultaneous dynamics, the required society-wide time is the maximum of the template-wise times.

For isolated vertices, take their round earnings to be zero, consistently with unmatched earnings.

3. Recovering the matching by linear programming. Fix one template and suppress its index. Let \((M^*,\gamma^*)\) denote its promised outcome.

For a matched vertex \(i\), with partner \(j\), define its surplus by

\( \displaystyle b_i(\gamma)=\gamma_i-\max_{k\in\partial i\setminus\{j\}}(w_{ik}-\gamma_k)_+. \)

The internal vertex sets partition the matched vertices, and the promise gives \(b_i(\gamma^*)=s_r\) when \(i\) is internal to \(C_r\). Since every \(s_r\ge s_1\ge\sigma\), every matched vertex satisfies

\( \displaystyle \gamma_i^*\ge b_i(\gamma^*)\ge\sigma>0. \)

Every edge outside \(M^*\) has a matched endpoint: an edge between two unmatched vertices would contradict stability because its weight is positive. For such an edge \(ik\), choose a matched endpoint \(i\). The definition of surplus gives

\( \displaystyle \gamma_i^*\ge\sigma+(w_{ik}-\gamma_k^*)_+\ge\sigma+w_{ik}-\gamma_k^*. \)

Hence every nonmatching edge has strictly positive slack:

\( \displaystyle \gamma_i^*+\gamma_k^*-w_{ik}\ge\sigma. \)

Now solve the fractional matching linear program:

\( \displaystyle \max\left\{\sum_{ij\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij}:x_{ij}\ge0,\ \sum_{j\in\partial i}x_{ij}\le1\text{ for every }i\right\}. \)

For a feasible vector \(x\), put \(d_i=\sum_{j\in\partial i}x_{ij}\). Direct calculation yields

\( \displaystyle \sum_i\gamma_i^*-\sum_{ij\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij} = \sum_i\gamma_i^*(1-d_i) +\sum_{ij\in E}(\gamma_i^*+\gamma_j^*-w_{ij})x_{ij}. \)

Every term on the right is nonnegative. The incidence vector of \(M^*\) attains equality, because unmatched earnings are zero and matching-edge earnings sum to their weights.

At any optimum, equality must therefore hold term by term. Strict nonmatching-edge slack forces \(x_{ij}=0\) outside \(M^*\). Positive earnings at every matched vertex force \(d_i=1\) there. Its sole remaining incident edge is its matching edge, so that edge has \(x_{ij}=1\).

The fractional matching LP consequently has the unique optimum equal to the incidence vector of \(M^*\). Solving it exactly recovers the matching.

4. Computing balanced earnings by maximizing slacks. Once \(M=M^*\) has been recovered, consider the stable-allocation polytope

\( \displaystyle P=\left\{\gamma: \gamma_i\ge0,\ \gamma_i=0\text{ if }i\text{ is unmatched},\ \gamma_i+\gamma_j=w_{ij}\text{ on }M,\ \gamma_i+\gamma_j\ge w_{ij}\text{ on }E\setminus M \right\}. \)

It is nonempty because it contains \(\gamma^*\). It is compact: each matched earning lies between zero and its matching-edge weight, and unmatched earnings are zero.

List the following affine slack functions:

  • \(f_i(\gamma)=\gamma_i\), for every vertex;
  • \(f_{ij}(\gamma)=\gamma_i+\gamma_j-w_{ij}\), for every edge outside \(M\).

For each allocation, sort this finite list in nondecreasing order. Choose an allocation whose sorted list is lexicographically maximal: first maximize its smallest entry, then its second smallest entry, and so forth. Such an allocation exists by compactness and successive maximization of the continuous order statistics.

We next prove that every such allocation is balanced. For a matching edge \(ij\),

\( \displaystyle b_i(\gamma)= \min\left(\{\gamma_i\}\cup \{\gamma_i+\gamma_k-w_{ik}:k\in\partial i\setminus\{j\}\}\right). \)

This identity follows from subtracting the maximum of zero and the quantities \(w_{ik}-\gamma_k\) from \(\gamma_i\).

Suppose \(b_i(\gamma)<b_j(\gamma)\). Choose

\( \displaystyle 0<\delta<b_j(\gamma)-b_i(\gamma), \)

and increase \(\gamma_i\) by \(\delta\) while decreasing \(\gamma_j\) by \(\delta\). Matching-edge equalities remain satisfied.

Every changed slack incident to \(i\), including its node slack, increases by \(\delta\). Every changed slack incident to \(j\) decreases by \(\delta\), but its original value was at least \(b_j(\gamma)\), so its new value remains strictly greater than \(b_i(\gamma)\ge0\). No listed edge slack belongs to both families, because \(ij\) is a matching edge and the graph is simple. Thus the perturbed allocation remains in \(P\).

At least one slack originally equal to \(b_i(\gamma)\) increases. No slack at or below that value decreases, and no decreased slack reaches that value. The sorted slack list therefore improves lexicographically, a contradiction. Interchanging \(i\) and \(j\) excludes the opposite inequality.

Hence \(b_i(\gamma)=b_j(\gamma)\) on every matching edge. The allocation is stable and balanced, so uniqueness forces it to equal \(\gamma^*\).

This argument supplies the needed correctness proof directly; polynomial computability of balanced outcomes is also established by [Kleinberg and Tardos](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/info/people/kleinber/stoc08-exchange.pdf).

5. Implementing the slack maximization in polynomial bit time. Let \(h=|V|+|E\setminus M|\), the number of listed slacks. The lexicographic maximization above can be implemented with polynomially many ordinary LPs.

Maintain a nonempty compact feasible polytope \(Q\), initially \(P\), and the set \(R\) of slack indices not yet frozen. At each stage:

  • Maximize \(z\) subject to \(\gamma\in Q\) and \(f_a(\gamma)\ge z\) for every \(a\in R\). Denote the optimum by \(\theta\).
  • Restrict to \(Q'=\{\gamma\in Q:f_a(\gamma)\ge\theta\text{ for every }a\in R\}\).
  • For each \(a\in R\), maximize \(f_a\) over \(Q'\). Freeze precisely those indices whose maximum equals \(\theta\), remove them from \(R\), and continue with \(Q=Q'\).

At least one index is frozen at every stage. Otherwise, for each \(a\in R\), there would be a point of \(Q'\) with \(f_a>\theta\). Averaging these finitely many points would make every remaining slack strictly greater than \(\theta\), contradicting optimality.

Moreover, averaging witnesses for the nonfrozen indices shows that all of them can simultaneously exceed \(\theta\). Thus the frozen indices are exactly the entries that must remain at the current minimum; the next stage optimizes the remaining sorted entries. Induction proves that this procedure implements the stated lexicographic maximization.

There are at most \(h\) stages and \(O(h^2)\) LP calls. At termination all node slacks, hence all earnings, are fixed.

For completeness, the intermediate rational data have polynomial encoding length. Let \(D\) be the product of the input weight denominators; its bit length is \(O(L)\). All constraint matrices in these stages have entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), with at most \(n+1\) variables. An optimal vertex therefore introduces, by Cramer’s rule, a denominator factor whose bit length is \(O(n\log(n+1))\), using Hadamard’s determinant bound. After at most \(h\) stages, a common denominator has bit length

\( \displaystyle O\bigl(L+hn\log(n+1)\bigr). \)

All slacks and optimized levels lie between zero and \(2W\), so their numerators also have polynomial bit length. The auxiliary LP optima used for testing introduce only another polynomial-size determinant factor.

The standard rational-LP theorem provides exact optimization in time polynomial in dimensions and binary encoding length; its rational setting is recalled in [Adler and Beling](https://adler.ieor.berkeley.edu/ilans_pubs/lp_algebraic_stoc_1992.pdf). Here every LP is feasible with bounded optimum, and the bounds above verify polynomial input size throughout.

Since \(h=O(m^2)\), performing this procedure for all \(\tau\) templates takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Outputting \(\hat\gamma_{q,i}=\gamma_{q,i}^*\) gives error zero. The algorithm requires neither the initialization nor a numerical gap value.

6. Guarantee for the specified rounds. The normalized finite-network theorem applies to a unique outcome with positive KT gap and every valid initialization. Its gap definition agrees with the stated structural promise, including the condition on external endpoints. For \(W=1\) and \(0<\eta<\sigma/4\), it guarantees earnings error at most \(\eta\) at every time after \(Cn^7[\sigma^{-1}+\log(\sigma/\eta)]\). See [Theorem 11 and Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/html/1004.2079v2).

Scaling weights and messages by \(W_q\) preserves the dynamics: positive parts, maxima, and the updates are positively homogeneous. The normalized gap and tolerance become \(\sigma_q/W_q\) and \(\varepsilon/W_q\).

To cover arbitrary positive tolerances, apply the theorem with physical tolerance

\( \displaystyle \delta_q=\min\{\varepsilon,\sigma_q/8\}. \)

This is below \(\sigma_q/4\), and accuracy \(\delta_q\) implies accuracy \(\varepsilon\). Also,

\( \displaystyle \log(\sigma_q/\delta_q) \le \log 8+\log(1+\sigma_q/\varepsilon). \)

Because \(\sigma_q\le s_1\le W_q\), the constant term is absorbed by \(W_q/\sigma_q\). Therefore, after adjusting the universal constant,

\( \displaystyle T_q= \left\lceil Cn_q^7 \left[\frac{W_q}{\sigma_q} +\log\left(1+\frac{\sigma_q}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\right\rceil \)

suffices for template \(q\), at every subsequent round. Taking \(T=\max_qT_q\) proves the society-wide guarantee in Section 1.

7. Binary complexity versus round complexity. The displayed guarantee can be exponential in input length even on a fixed-size graph. For example, take two disjoint edges of weights \(2\) and \(2^{1-B}\), with \(B\ge2\). Their unique balanced earnings are respectively \(1\) and \(2^{-B}\) at each endpoint. The two structures have slacks \(2^{-B}\) and \(1\), and satisfy the promise with \(\sigma=2^{-B}\). Thus \(W/\sigma=2^{B+1}\), although the input length is \(O(B)\).

This example demonstrates exponential numerical dependence of the bound; it does not prove that these dynamics actually require exponentially many rounds.

Each round uses \(O(\tau m^2)\) arithmetic operations, by maintaining the two largest incoming offers at each vertex. Exact messages after \(t\) rounds have \(O(L+t)\) bits: if a common initial denominator is \(D_0\), message denominators divide \(D_0\,2^{2t}\). Consequently, literal simulation has a bit cost depending on the numerical round count.

The exact LP algorithm establishes polynomial-time computability independently of that simulation cost. Approximate fixed-point residual guarantees alone are unnecessary for this conclusion and do not substitute for the earnings-distance guarantee proved above.
