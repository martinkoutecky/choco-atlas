The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given τ worker types with rational masses μ_t and rational linear utilities u_t(q) over team compositions q in the simplex Δ^{τ-1}, promised to satisfy a mass weak top-coalition condition on every nonzero residual mass vector v ≤ μ, compute a finite partition into teams x^j = λ_j q^j whose type masses sum to μ. A partition is mass-SIS if no positive mass can be removed from its teams and reassigned to new teams so that all deviating mass strictly prefers its new composition and all nondeviating mass in teams receiving members weakly prefers the resulting composition. Ask whether such a partition can be computed in time polynomial in the type count, input representation size, and rational bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Types represent divisible, nonatomic populations; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Input utilities are \(u_t(q)=\sum_{s=1}^{\tau}a_{ts}q_s\), with arbitrary signed rational coefficients; equal utilities mean indifference. Let \(m=\tau^2\) count utility coefficients and \(L\) be the total binary input length.
For any nonzero nonnegative real mass vector \(x\), write \(q(x)=x/\lVert x\rVert_1\). Every such vector is an admissible team when available; there are no team-size restrictions.
The mass weak top-coalition promise is: every nonzero real residual vector \(0\leq v\leq\mu\) admits a team \(0<x\leq v\) and finitely many nonzero nonnegative layers \(y^1,\ldots,y^\ell\) summing to \(x\), such that, whenever \(y^k_t>0\), every nonzero \(0\leq z\leq v-\sum_{h<k}y^h\) with \(z_t>0\) satisfies \(u_t(q(z))\leq u_t(q(x))\). Vector inequalities are coordinatewise.
A finite partition consists of nonzero team vectors \(x^1,\ldots,x^K\) with \(\sum_jx^j=\mu\); its computed representation must have rational entries. Different portions of one type may occupy different teams.
A deviation selects positive total mass from these teams and redistributes it among finitely many teams, allowing both newly formed teams and entry into existing teams. The nondeviating remainder of each original team must stay together, and remainders from distinct original teams cannot merge.
Every deviating portion must strictly prefer its final composition to its original composition. Every positive nondeviating portion in a team receiving deviators must weakly prefer its final composition; departures require no consent. Deviations may use real masses. A partition is mass-SIS if no such deviation exists.

Does every promised instance admit a finite rational mass-SIS partition?
Is computing such a partition polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or NP-hard?

For finite agents satisfying the weak top-coalition property, an SIS partition always exists and is computable in polynomial time in the agent count and the individually rational coalition-list representation size. [Published result](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/0005.pdf)
No result settling this exact continuous problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total mass to one, consistent with a distribution over types; retained strictly positive masses for listed support types.
  • Specified arbitrary signed rational utility coefficients and utility-induced weak preferences, permitting indifference.
  • Defined the unspecified mass weak top-coalition condition using finite positive mass layers: alternatives avoiding earlier layers must be feasible from the residual mass after those layers are removed.
  • Allowed real residuals, layer masses, and deviations, while requiring finite rational output teams for binary computation.
  • Restored deviations into existing teams as well as newly formed teams, and required nondeviating remainders to remain together without merging, matching discrete reachability.
  • Specified that departing teams need not consent, whereas all positive incumbent portions in teams receiving deviators must weakly approve.
  • Imposed no team-size bound or positive minimum team mass, since the discrete model provides neither.
  • Used the number of utility coefficients as m and total binary input length as L.
  • Made existence of finite rational partitions an explicit question because the discrete existence guarantee does not itself establish continuous existence.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Every promised instance admits a finite rational mass-SIS partition. Such a partition can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The construction below produces at most \(\tau\) teams, each containing at most two types, and is stable against deviations using real masses.

1. Characterizing the promise.

Write \(d_t=a_{tt}\). Define a directed graph on the supported types by putting an edge \(t\to s\) precisely when \(a_{ts}>d_t\).

We first prove that the mass weak top-coalition promise is equivalent to this graph being acyclic.

For a nonzero residual vector \(v\), let \(S=\{s:v_s>0\}\). For every \(t\in S\),

\(\displaystyle \sup_{\substack{0\leq z\leq v\\z_t>0}}u_t(q(z))=\max_{s\in S}a_{ts}.\)

Indeed, every admissible composition is supported on \(S\), so its utility is at most the displayed maximum. Conversely, for \(s\ne t\), the admissible vectors \(v_s e_s+\varepsilon e_t\), with positive \(\varepsilon\) tending to zero, have utilities tending to \(a_{ts}\). For \(s=t\), a pure type-\(t\) vector attains \(a_{tt}\). Here \(e_s\) denotes the coordinate unit vector.

Suppose the promise holds, and take a promised witness \(x,y^1,\ldots,y^\ell\) for \(v\). Choose \(t\) with \(y^1_t>0\). Its first-layer condition implies

\(\displaystyle u_t(q(x))\geq\max_{s\in S}a_{ts}.\)

Since \(x\leq v\), the reverse inequality holds. Thus \(u_t(q(x))\) equals this maximum. Moreover, \(x_t>0\); a convex combination of coefficients bounded above by this maximum can equal it only if every coefficient receiving positive weight equals it. In particular,

\(\displaystyle d_t=\max_{s\in S}a_{ts}.\)

Consequently, the induced graph on \(S\) has a sink.

Every nonempty set \(S\) occurs as a residual support: take \(v_s=\mu_s\) on \(S\) and zero elsewhere. Thus every nonempty induced subgraph has a sink. A directed cycle would contradict this property, because every vertex in its vertex set has an outgoing edge to another vertex in that set. The graph is therefore acyclic.

Conversely, suppose the graph is acyclic. For any residual support \(S\), choose a sink \(t\) in its induced graph. Then \(a_{ts}\leq d_t\) for every \(s\in S\). The singleton team \(x=v_t e_t\), with the single layer \(y^1=x\), satisfies the promise: its utility is \(d_t\), and every admissible team has type-\(t\) utility at most \(d_t\).

This proves the equivalence. Notice that necessity used only the first layer; no assertion about later layers is needed.

Relabel the types by repeatedly deleting a sink and assigning indices in deletion order. Then

\[\displaystyle a_{ir}\leq d_i\qquad\text{whenever }r\geq i. \tag{1}\]

All subsequent indices refer to this ordering.

2. Constructing the partition.

Process types in order \(1,\ldots,\tau\). Maintain a collection of pure teams and completed two-type teams. Completed two-type teams are never changed during the construction.

When processing \(t\), every existing pure team has the form \(\mu_s e_s\) for some \(s<t\). Define

\(\displaystyle E_t=\{s<t:\mu_s e_s\text{ is currently a pure team and }a_{st}=d_s\}.\)

Thus \(E_t\) consists of earlier pure types indifferent to admitting type \(t\).

If \(a_{ts}\leq d_t\) for every \(s\in E_t\), set \(b_t=d_t\) and create the pure team \(\mu_t e_t\).

Otherwise, define

\(\displaystyle F_t(b)=\sum_{s\in E_t}\mu_s\max\{a_{ts}-b,0\}-\mu_t(b-d_t).\)

Let \(M_t=\max_{s\in E_t}a_{ts}\), so \(M_t>d_t\). We have

\(\displaystyle F_t(d_t)>0,\qquad F_t(M_t)=-\mu_t(M_t-d_t)<0.\)

The function \(F_t\) is continuous and strictly decreasing: its summands in the first term are nonincreasing, while the last term is strictly decreasing because \(\mu_t>0\). Hence there is a unique root \(b_t\in(d_t,M_t)\).

Set

\(\displaystyle A_t=\{s\in E_t:a_{ts}>b_t\},\qquad p_s=\frac{\mu_s(a_{ts}-b_t)}{b_t-d_t}\quad(s\in A_t).\)

Every \(p_s\) is positive, and the root equation gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{s\in A_t}p_s=\mu_t.\)

For each \(s\in A_t\), replace the pure team \(\mu_s e_s\) by the completed team

\(\displaystyle x^{s,t}=\mu_s e_s+p_s e_t.\)

This allocates all mass of type \(t\). Call \(s\) the anchor and \(t\) the beneficiary of this team.

The two types’ utilities are exactly

\(\displaystyle u_s(q(x^{s,t}))=\frac{\mu_s d_s+p_s a_{st}}{\mu_s+p_s}=d_s,\)

and

\(\displaystyle u_t(q(x^{s,t}))=b_t.\)

For the second equality, use \(\mu_s(a_{ts}-b_t)=p_s(b_t-d_t)\).

The construction has the following properties.

  • Every portion of type \(t\) obtains the same utility \(b_t\), with \(b_t\geq d_t\). A type receiving \(b_t>d_t\) appears only as a beneficiary. A type initially receiving \(d_t\) either remains pure or later becomes an anchor, which preserves its utility \(d_t\).
  • Every final team is either pure or a pair \(x^{s,h}\) with \(s<h\). In a pair, the anchor receives \(d_s\), while the beneficiary receives \(b_h>d_h\).
  • Each anchor contributes its entire mass \(\mu_s\) to one pair. Label each final pair by its anchor and each final pure team by its type. These labels are distinct, so there are at most \(\tau\) teams.

We will also use this consequence of the construction:

If \(s<t\) was still pure when \(t\) was processed, \(a_{st}=d_s\), and \(s\) was not used at that step, then \(a_{ts}\leq b_t\). \[\tag{2}\]

When \(b_t=d_t\), this follows from the condition for keeping \(t\) pure. When \(b_t>d_t\), it follows because precisely the eligible anchors with \(a_{ts}>b_t\) were used.

3. Stability against arbitrary real deviations.

Suppose, for contradiction, that the resulting partition admits a deviation satisfying the requirements in the question. Let \(t\) be the smallest index with positive deviating mass.

Every type with index smaller than \(t\) is entirely nondeviating. All deviating portions have types \(r\geq t\). By (1),

\[\displaystyle a_{tr}\leq d_t\leq b_t\qquad(r\geq t). \tag{3}\]

Every deviating portion of type \(t\) originally receives \(b_t\), so its final utility must be strictly greater than \(b_t\).

Consider a final team receiving positive deviating mass of type \(t\). Because remainders from distinct original teams cannot merge, this final team contains either no nondeviating remainder or the remainder of one original team. We examine every possibility.

A final team with no nondeviating remainder. All its members are deviators, hence have indices at least \(t\). Equation (3) makes its type-\(t\) utility at most \(b_t\). It cannot receive a strictly improving type-\(t\) deviator.

A final team containing the remainder of a pure team \(\mu_s e_s\). If \(s\geq t\), again every type present has index at least \(t\), so (3) excludes strict improvement.

Suppose \(s<t\). No type-\(s\) mass deviates, so the whole mass \(\mu_s\) remains. Its original utility is \(d_s\). Every entrant has type \(r\geq t>s\), and (1) gives \(a_{sr}\leq d_s\). Consent therefore forces every positive entrant to have coefficient exactly \(d_s\). In particular,

\(\displaystyle a_{st}=d_s.\)

Since \(s\) is pure in the final constructed partition, it was pure when \(t\) was processed and was not used then. By (2), \(a_{ts}\leq b_t\). All other final members have indices at least \(t\), and satisfy (3). Thus the final type-\(t\) utility is at most \(b_t\).

A final team containing the remainder of a pair \(x^{s,h}\), where \(s<h\).

If \(h<t\), neither original type deviates. The beneficiary \(h\) retains its entire original team, whose utility to it is \(b_h>d_h\). Every entrant has type \(r\geq t>h\), so \(a_{hr}\leq d_h<b_h\). Any positive entry strictly lowers \(h\)’s utility, violating consent. Thus this team cannot receive type-\(t\) deviators.

If \(s\geq t\), every final member has index at least \(t\), and (3) excludes strict improvement.

Suppose \(s<t<h\). The entire anchor mass \(\mu_s\) remains. Any remaining original type-\(h\) mass has coefficient \(a_{sh}=d_s\) for the anchor. Every entrant has coefficient at most \(d_s\) by (1). Therefore consent again forces \(a_{st}=d_s\).

This pair was created when \(h>t\) was processed, so \(s\) was still pure when \(t\) was processed and was not used then. Equation (2) gives \(a_{ts}\leq b_t\). Every other final member has index at least \(t\), so its coefficient for \(t\) is at most \(d_t\leq b_t\). Strict improvement is impossible.

The only remaining possibility is \(s<t=h\): a type-\(t\) deviator enters one of the original pairs in which \(t\) itself is the beneficiary.

Conservation rules out these remaining destinations.

If \(b_t=d_t\), there are no original pairs with beneficiary \(t\), and the preceding cases already give a contradiction. Hence assume \(b_t>d_t\).

Let \(s\in A_t\), and consider the final team containing the remainder of \(x^{s,t}\). Its anchor is entirely nondeviating and remains with mass \(\mu_s\). Write its final type-\(t\) mass as \(\beta_s\), and its final masses of later types as \(\gamma_{s,r}\) for \(r>t\). Its vector is therefore

\(\displaystyle \mu_s e_s+\beta_s e_t+\sum_{r>t}\gamma_{s,r}e_r.\)

If this team receives a type-\(t\) deviator, strict improvement requires

\(\displaystyle \mu_s(a_{ts}-b_t)+\beta_s(d_t-b_t)+\sum_{r>t}\gamma_{s,r}(a_{tr}-b_t)>0.\)

By (1), \(a_{tr}\leq d_t<b_t\) for \(r>t\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle 0<\mu_s(a_{ts}-b_t)-\beta_s(b_t-d_t)=(b_t-d_t)(p_s-\beta_s),\)

and hence \(\beta_s<p_s\).

If this team receives no type-\(t\) deviator, it can only lose type-\(t\) mass, so \(\beta_s\leq p_s\).

The preceding case analysis shows that every type-\(t\) deviator must enter one of these original beneficiary teams. Their anchors remain nondeviating, and the prohibition on merging distinct remainders ensures that these remain distinct final teams. Thus all type-\(t\) mass is accounted for by the \(\beta_s\).

At least one team receives positive type-\(t\) deviating mass. Therefore one inequality is strict, and conservation would imply

\(\displaystyle \mu_t=\sum_{s\in A_t}\beta_s<\sum_{s\in A_t}p_s=\mu_t,\)

a contradiction.

No admissible deviation exists. The partition is mass-SIS. This argument uses no rationality assumption about deviations.

4. Rationality and polynomial running time.

The graph is constructed using \(m\) exact rational comparisons. Acyclicity and the required ordering can be computed in polynomial time.

At a processing step with \(b_t>d_t\), the root equation yields

\[\displaystyle b_t=\frac{\mu_t d_t+\sum_{s\in A_t}\mu_s a_{ts}}{\mu_t+\sum_{s\in A_t}\mu_s}. \tag{4}\]

To find \(A_t\) exactly, sort the eligible values \(a_{ts}>d_t\) in nonincreasing order. The active set \(\{s:a_{ts}>b_t\}\) is a nonempty prefix of this ordering. Enumerate the at most \(\tau\) nonempty prefixes, compute the candidate value in (4), and check whether that prefix is exactly the set of eligible values strictly exceeding the candidate. The unique root guarantees a consistent candidate.

Thus every \(b_t\), and then every \(p_s\), is rational and computable by polynomially many exact rational operations.

Each candidate in (4) uses at most \(\tau\) products and sums of original input rationals. Its binary length is bounded by a polynomial in \(\tau,L\). The same holds for each \(p_s=\mu_s(a_{ts}-b_t)/(b_t-d_t)\). Importantly, every anchor has its original mass \(\mu_s\); recursively subdivided anchor masses do not enter these formulas.

There are \(\tau\) processing steps and at most \(\tau\) output teams. Exact arithmetic, comparisons, and output writing therefore all take time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

This establishes both requested conclusions: finite rational existence and polynomial-time construction.
