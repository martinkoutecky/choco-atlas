The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given customer and provider types, rational arrival masses over \(H\) rounds, a population mass scale \(N\), and unknown fixed utilities in \([-1,1]\) for each ordered type pair, choose each round’s mass matching and zero-sum transfers to minimize expected cumulative Subset Instability. A matched mass \(x_{ij}\) yields aggregate utility feedback with variance proxy \(1/x_{ij}\); determine whether a policy achieves regret \(O\!\left(\tau\sqrt{nH\log(NH)}\right)\), where \(\tau\) is the number of types and \(n\) is the maximum arriving mass per round.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) and \(P\) be disjoint, nonempty customer and provider type sets, with \(m=|C|\) and \(\tau=|C|+|P|\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, with binary-rational population masses \(M_a\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Set \(N=\sum_a M_a\). Masses are not normalized probabilities.
Over \(H\ge1\) rounds, an arbitrary fixed arrival sequence has binary-rational masses \(0\le b_a^t\le M_a\), revealed at the start of round \(t\); set \(n=\max_t\sum_a b_a^t>0\). Unknown, fixed directed utilities \(u_{ij},u_{ji}\in[-1,1]\) depend only on types; unmatched utility is zero.
A policy chooses rational matched masses \(x_{ij}^t\ge0\), satisfying \(\sum_jx_{ij}^t\le b_i^t\) and \(\sum_ix_{ij}^t\le b_j^t\), and signed per-unit transfers \(p_{ij}^t\), received by customers and paid by providers. Unmatched mass receives no transfer; decisions use only arrivals observed so far and previous feedback.
After choosing the entire round's outcome, each positive \(x_{ij}^t\) produces two observations \(Y_{ij}^t=u_{ij}+\epsilon_{ij}^t\) and \(Y_{ji}^t=u_{ji}+\epsilon_{ji}^t\). Conditional on the past and current decision, noises are independent, mean-zero, and subgaussian with variance proxy \(1/x_{ij}^t\); zero matched mass gives no observation.
Partition the arriving mass into groups \(g\) by type and assigned partner type, including unmatched groups. Their masses are \(d_g\), and their per-unit realized payoffs are \(v_g=u_{ij}+p_{ij}\) for matched customers, \(v_g=u_{ji}-p_{ij}\) for matched providers, and zero for unmatched mass.
A coalition chooses arbitrary portions \(0\le z_g\le d_g\); write \(c_a(z)=\sum_{g:\operatorname{type}(g)=a}z_g\). Define \(W(c)=\max_{y\ge0}\{\sum_{i,j}y_{ij}(u_{ij}+u_{ji}):\sum_jy_{ij}\le c_i,\ \sum_iy_{ij}\le c_j\}\) and Subset Instability \(I_t=\max_{0\le z\le d}\{W(c(z))-\sum_gz_gv_g\}\). Regret is \(R_H=\sum_{t=1}^H I_t\).
Does this problem admit a policy, running in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,H,L\), that uniformly guarantees \(\mathbb E[R_H]=O(\tau\sqrt{nH\log(2+NH)})\), or is this guarantee unattainable by polynomial-time policies? Here \(L\) is the total binary encoding length of numerical inputs and feedback supplied to the policy.
For finite agents, an efficient typed-preference algorithm is claimed to achieve \(O(\tau\sqrt{nH\log(NH)})\) expected cumulative Subset Instability, with feedback available after each complete round ([full version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.08843v2)).

Conclusion

The requested uniform guarantee is unattainable even for policies with unlimited computation. Feedback after the complete round forces a worst-case expected regret of at least \(n/3\) before any learning occurs.

The construction below uses three types, nonnegative utilities, noiseless feedback, and arbitrarily large horizons.

1. A lower bound from the grand coalition

Fix any round and its chosen outcome. The coalition containing all arriving mass is admissible: choose \(z_g=d_g\) for every group. Its type masses are precisely the arrivals \(b_a^t\).

For every matched pair, the customer’s and provider’s transfers cancel. Unmatched mass has zero payoff. Thus

\(\displaystyle \sum_g d_gv_g=\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}^t(u_{ij}+u_{ji}).\)

Consequently, directly from the definition of Subset Instability,

\[\displaystyle I_t\ge W(b^t)-\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}^t(u_{ij}+u_{ji}).\tag{1}\]

Also, \(I_t\ge0\), since the empty coalition is admissible. Therefore \(R_H\ge I_1\).

2. Two indistinguishable markets

Let \(B\) and \(H\) be arbitrary positive integers. Set

\(\displaystyle C=\{c\},\qquad P=\{p,q\},\qquad M_c=M_p=M_q=B.\)

In every round, all three types arrive with their full masses:

\(\displaystyle b_c^t=b_p^t=b_q^t=B.\)

Hence \(m=1\), \(\tau=3\), and \(n=N=3B\).

Consider two possible fixed utility assignments:

| Market | \(u_{cp}\) | \(u_{pc}\) | \(u_{cq}\) | \(u_{qc}\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(A\) | \(1\) | \(1\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(D\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |

Use identically zero noise in both markets. This is permitted: constant zero random variables are conditionally independent and mean-zero, and for every positive matched mass \(x\) and every real \(\lambda\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[\exp(\lambda\epsilon)\mid\text{past, decision}]=1\le \exp\!\left(\frac{\lambda^2}{2x}\right).\)

Thus the specified subgaussian condition holds. All numerical inputs and feedback in this construction have finite binary encodings.

Fix any policy, allowing randomization and arbitrary computation. Write its first-round matched masses as

\(\displaystyle X=x_{cp}^1,\qquad Z=x_{cq}^1.\)

Customer feasibility requires

\[\displaystyle X\ge0,\qquad Z\ge0,\qquad X+Z\le B.\tag{2}\]

Before the first-round decision, the policy has received no feedback. Its support, population masses, horizon, and observed arrivals are identical in the two markets. Therefore the joint distribution of \((X,Z)\) is identical in both markets. Denote its expectations by \(\mu_X\) and \(\mu_Z\). Equation (2) implies

\[\displaystyle \mu_X+\mu_Z\le B.\tag{3}\]

3. Expected regret is at least \(B\) in one fixed market

In market \(A\), only the pair \((c,p)\) has positive total utility, namely \(2\). Matching all \(B\) customer mass to \(p\) is feasible and yields welfare \(2B\). No matching can yield more, because the customer mass is \(B\) and every pair’s total utility is at most \(2\). Therefore \(W_A(b^1)=2B\).

The chosen outcome’s total realized payoff is \(2X\), irrespective of its transfers. Applying (1) gives

\[\displaystyle \mathbb E_A[R_H]\ge\mathbb E_A[I_1]\ge2(B-\mu_X).\tag{4}\]

The same reasoning in market \(D\), whose beneficial pair is \((c,q)\), gives

\[\displaystyle \mathbb E_D[R_H]\ge2(B-\mu_Z).\tag{5}\]

Adding these inequalities and using (3),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_A[R_H]+\mathbb E_D[R_H]\ge4B-2(\mu_X+\mu_Z)\ge2B.\)

At least one of the two fixed markets therefore satisfies

\[\displaystyle \mathbb E[R_H]\ge B=\frac n3.\tag{6}\]

The selected market may depend on the policy, but it is fixed before the policy’s random choices and remains unchanged throughout all rounds. This is a valid worst-case instance.

4. Contradiction with the requested uniform rate

To show that the obstruction persists with growing horizons, take \(B=H^3\) and let \(H\) tend to infinity. Equation (6) gives, for every policy, an admissible instance with

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[R_H]\ge H^3.\)

On these instances, the proposed rate equals

\(\displaystyle \tau\sqrt{nH\log(2+NH)} =3\sqrt3\,H^2\sqrt{\log(2+3H^4)}.\)

The ratio of the proved lower bound to this expression is

\(\displaystyle \frac{H}{3\sqrt3\,\sqrt{\log(2+3H^4)}}\longrightarrow\infty.\)

Thus no universal constant can make the requested bound hold uniformly. In particular, no polynomial-time policy can provide it.

5. Relation to the cited finite-agent claim

The cited paper states its typed-preference bound in Theorem 5.2 and specifies feedback after the matching decision in Section 3. ([Full version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.08843v2))

For integer \(B\), the construction also has a finite-agent realization with \(B\) agents of each type. The same grand-coalition calculation and first-round indistinguishability argument apply. Consequently, the claimed finite-agent rate cannot hold uniformly in population size and horizon under that feedback timing either. A valid bound needs an additional term accounting for initial uncertainty, or a restriction on the parameter regime.

The impossibility established here follows directly from the given model and requires no computational hardness assumption.
