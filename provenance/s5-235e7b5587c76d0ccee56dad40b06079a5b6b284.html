Corollary 4 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite additive \(2\)-D valuation types \(T\), masses \(\mu_t\), item categories \(G\) with per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and integral bundles \(S\subseteq G\), decide whether there are variables \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni g}x_{t,S}=q_g\), such that every supported bundle for type \(t\) satisfies \(u_t(S)\ge(3/4)\lambda_t\) for correlated goods, or \(u_t(S)\ge(11/9)\lambda_t\) for correlated chores, where \(\lambda_t=\max_y\min_{y_S>0}u_t(S)\) over distributions \(y\) with \(\sum_S y_S=1\) and \(\sum_{S\ni g}y_S=q_g\); alternatively, optimize the common guarantee.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) item categories \(G\), with binary-rational per-capita supplies \(0<q_g\le1\). Bundles are subsets \(S\subseteq G\), including the empty bundle; each person receives at most one item from each category.
Each type has binary-rational additive receiving and nonreceiving values \((v_{tg},v'_{tg})\), and utility \(u_t(S)=\sum_{g\in S}v_{tg}+\sum_{g\notin S}v'_{tg}\). Consider separately correlated goods, with \(v_{tg}\ge v'_{tg}\ge0\), and correlated chores, with \(v_{tg}\le v'_{tg}\le0\).
Define \(Y(q)=\{y\ge0:\sum_{S\subseteq G}y_S=1,\ \sum_{S\ni g}y_S=q_g\text{ for every }g\}\) and \(\lambda_t=\max_{y\in Y(q)}\min_{S:y_S>0}u_t(S)\).
A mass allocation satisfies \(x_{t,S}\ge0\), \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni g}x_{t,S}=q_g\). Its required guarantee is \(u_t(S)\ge\frac34\lambda_t\) for goods, or \(u_t(S)\ge\frac{11}{9}\lambda_t\) for chores, whenever \(x_{t,S}>0\).
All bundles are implicit, and the benchmarks are determined by the input rather than supplied separately. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Do the \(\frac34\) goods and \(\frac{11}{9}\) chores guarantees always exist in this mass model, and can allocations attaining them be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For finite societies with additive correlated externalities, guarantees of \(\frac34+\frac{1}{12n}\) for goods and \(\frac{11}{9}\) for chores always exist, where \(n\) is the number of agents. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.12806v3)
Without externalities, the underlying goods result gives a strongly polynomial algorithm for \(\frac34\); the underlying chores result establishes \(\frac{11}{9}\) existence and a polynomial algorithm for \(\frac54\). [Goods](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00029), [chores](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.04505)

Result

Both guarantees always exist and can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, an allocation attaining every type’s exact benchmark \(\lambda_t\) always exists.

The existence proof is immediate from the common marginal constraints. The computational result requires more work: below, rounding and polynomial-size flow linear programs produce the required guarantees without computing the benchmarks exactly.

1. Exact-benchmark allocations always exist

First, \(Y(q)\) is nonempty. For example, include each category \(g\) independently with probability \(q_g\).

For any fixed type, the minimum utility of a positive-probability bundle belongs to the finite set of bundle utilities. Consequently, the maximum defining \(\lambda_t\) is attained. Choose an attaining distribution \(y^t\in Y(q)\), and define

\(x_{t,S}=\mu_t y^t_S.\)

Then

\(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t,\)

and, for every category \(g\),

\(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni g}x_{t,S}=\sum_t\mu_t q_g=q_g.\)

Every positive-mass bundle assigned to type \(t\) has utility at least \(\lambda_t\).

For correlated goods, all bundle utilities are nonnegative, so \(\lambda_t\ge0\) and \(\lambda_t\ge\frac34\lambda_t\). For correlated chores, all bundle utilities are nonpositive, so \(\lambda_t\le0\) and \(\lambda_t\ge\frac{11}{9}\lambda_t\).

Thus both requested existence statements hold. The remaining task is to compute suitable distributions in polynomial time.

2. Removing the externality constants

We can process each type separately because its output distribution will have marginals exactly \(q\).

Goods

For a fixed type, put

\(\beta=\sum_g v'_g,\qquad w_g=v_g-v'_g.\)

The correlation assumptions give \(\beta\ge0\) and \(w_g\ge0\). Write \(w(S)=\sum_{g\in S}w_g\), and define

\(\Lambda=\max_{y\in Y(q)}\min_{S:y_S>0}w(S).\)

Then \(u(S)=\beta+w(S)\) and \(\lambda=\beta+\Lambda\).

A distribution supported on bundles satisfying \(w(S)\ge\frac34\Lambda\) therefore guarantees

\(u(S)\ge\beta+\frac34\Lambda\ge\frac34(\beta+\Lambda)=\frac34\lambda.\)

Chores

Put

\(b=-\sum_g v'_g,\qquad c_g=v'_g-v_g.\)

Here \(b\ge0\), \(c_g\ge0\), and \(u(S)=-b-c(S)\), where \(c(S)=\sum_{g\in S}c_g\). Define

\(B=\min_{y\in Y(q)}\max_{S:y_S>0}c(S).\)

Then \(\lambda=-(b+B)\).

A distribution supported on bundles satisfying \(c(S)\le\frac{11}{9}B\) guarantees

\(u(S)\ge-b-\frac{11}{9}B\ge-\frac{11}{9}(b+B)=\frac{11}{9}\lambda.\)

It therefore suffices to solve the two nonnegative additive problems just defined.

3. A compact LP for rounded bundle families

We will use a standard flow representation of distributions over subsets.

Fix nonnegative integer weights \(d_1,\ldots,d_m\) and a nonnegative integer threshold \(H\). Process the categories in order through a layered directed acyclic graph. At each layer, an arc either omits the next category or includes it.

For the family

\(\{S:\sum_{g\in S}d_g\ge H\},\)

use states \(0,\ldots,H\), representing the accumulated sum capped at \(H\). Including category \(g\) moves state \(s\) to \(\min\{H,s+d_g\}\); omitting it preserves \(s\). Only final state \(H\) is accepted.

For the family

\(\{S:\sum_{g\in S}d_g\le H\},\)

use states \(0,\ldots,H\) without capping. Including category \(g\) is permitted only when \(s+d_g\le H\). Every final state is accepted.

Include and omit arcs remain distinct even when their endpoints coincide. Each accepted path therefore specifies a bundle. Both graphs have \(O(m(H+1))\) vertices and arcs.

Impose nonnegative unit flow from the initial state to the accepting sink, flow conservation at all other vertices, and the constraints

\(\sum_{\text{arcs including category }g}f_e=q_g\qquad(g\in G).\)

This LP is feasible if and only if there is a distribution in \(Y(q)\) supported on the specified bundle family:

  • A distribution induces flow by sending each bundle’s probability along its path.
  • Conversely, an acyclic unit flow decomposes into weighted source-to-sink paths. The displayed constraints make each category’s inclusion probability exactly \(q_g\).

Path decomposition takes polynomial time and produces at most the number of arcs many bundles: repeatedly remove a positive path with weight equal to its smallest arc flow, eliminating at least one positive arc each time.

All LP coefficients are \(0,1,-1\), and the right-hand sides are \(1\) and the input rationals \(q_g\). Standard polynomial-time rational LP algorithms therefore decide feasibility and return exact rational flows of polynomial encoding length.

4. Polynomial-time algorithm for goods

Fix a type’s weights \(w_g\ge0\). Set

\(N=4m,\qquad H=N-m=3m.\)

For each positive rational \(a\), define rounded weights

\(d_g(a)=\min\left\{N,\left\lfloor\frac{Nw_g}{a}\right\rfloor\right\}.\)

Call \(a\) feasible when there is a distribution in \(Y(q)\) supported on bundles satisfying

\(\sum_{g\in S}d_g(a)\ge H.\)

Section 3 tests this using an LP with \(O(m^2)\) variables and constraints.

Two properties establish the algorithm.

Property 1: Feasibility gives a utility guarantee

For every category,

\(d_g(a)\le\frac{Nw_g}{a}.\)

Thus every bundle accepted at threshold \(a\) satisfies

\(w(S)\ge\frac{a}{N}\sum_{g\in S}d_g(a)\ge\frac{aH}{N}=\frac34a.\)

Property 2: A polynomial candidate list contains a feasible threshold at least \(\Lambda\)

Consider the list

\(\mathcal A=\left\{\frac{Nw_g}{k}:w_g>0,\ 1\le k\le N\right\}.\)

It contains at most \(4m^2\) candidates. Suppose \(\Lambda>0\), and take a distribution attaining \(\Lambda\).

For every bundle \(S\) in its support,

\(\sum_{g\in S}\min\{w_g,\Lambda\}\ge\Lambda.\)

Indeed, if some category in \(S\) has weight at least \(\Lambda\), its capped weight already supplies \(\Lambda\); otherwise no weight in the sum is changed.

Also,

\(d_g(\Lambda)=\left\lfloor\frac{N\min\{w_g,\Lambda\}}{\Lambda}\right\rfloor.\)

Using \(\lfloor z\rfloor\ge z-1\), we obtain

\(\sum_{g\in S}d_g(\Lambda)\ge\frac{N}{\Lambda}\sum_{g\in S}\min\{w_g,\Lambda\}-|S|\ge N-m=H.\)

Hence \(\Lambda\) is feasible for the rounded test.

Write \(d_g=d_g(\Lambda)\). At least one \(d_g\) is positive, since the attaining distribution has accepted bundles and \(H>0\). Define

\(a_0=\min_{g:d_g>0}\frac{Nw_g}{d_g}.\)

Each \(d_g\) belongs to \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\) when positive, so \(a_0\in\mathcal A\). Moreover, \(d_g\le Nw_g/\Lambda\), which implies \(a_0\ge\Lambda\).

For every \(g\) with \(d_g>0\), the definition of \(a_0\) gives \(Nw_g/a_0\ge d_g\). Therefore

\(d_g(a_0)\ge d_g.\)

The same inequality is automatic when \(d_g=0\). The attaining distribution consequently passes the test at \(a_0\). This proves the property.

Algorithm and correctness

Sort \(\mathcal A\) and find its largest feasible candidate \(a^\star\). Feasibility is monotone as \(a\) increases: every \(d_g(a)\) is nonincreasing, so the accepted bundle family only shrinks. Binary search therefore uses \(O(\log(m+1))\) feasibility LPs.

If a feasible candidate exists, extract its distribution. When \(\Lambda>0\), Property 2 gives \(a^\star\ge\Lambda\), and Property 1 gives

\(w(S)\ge\frac34a^\star\ge\frac34\Lambda.\)

When \(\Lambda=0\), any resulting distribution also meets the requested nonnegative threshold.

If no candidate is feasible, Property 2 implies \(\Lambda=0\). Return any distribution in \(Y(q)\). One with at most \(m+1\) bundles is obtained by taking \(U\) uniformly in \([0,1]\) and setting

\(S(U)=\{g:U<q_g\}.\)

Its distribution is computed from the intervals between the sorted rational breakpoints \(0,q_1,\ldots,q_m,1\).

This completes the goods algorithm.

5. Polynomial-time algorithm for chores

Fix nonnegative costs \(c_g\), and put

\(C=\max_g c_g.\)

If \(C=0\), every bundle has cost zero, and any distribution in \(Y(q)\) suffices. Assume \(C>0\).

A lower bound on the benchmark

We have \(B\ge C\). To see this, fix any category \(g\). Since \(q_g>0\), every distribution in \(Y(q)\) assigns positive probability to some bundle containing \(g\). That bundle has cost at least \(c_g\). Thus every such distribution has maximum supported cost at least \(c_g\), and minimizing over distributions gives \(B\ge c_g\). Taking the maximum over \(g\) proves the claim.

Rounding and optimization

Set

\(K=\frac{2C}{9m},\qquad d_g=\left\lfloor\frac{c_g}{K}\right\rfloor,\qquad D=\sum_g d_g.\)

Because \(c_g\le C\),

\(D\le\frac92m^2.\)

Use the packing flow LP from Section 3 to find the smallest integer \(H^\star\in\{0,\ldots,D\}\) for which there is a distribution in \(Y(q)\) supported on bundles satisfying

\(\sum_{g\in S}d_g\le H^\star.\)

The endpoint \(D\) is feasible because every bundle satisfies this inequality. Feasibility is monotone in \(H\), so binary search needs \(O(\log(m+1))\) LPs, each with \(O(m^3)\) variables and constraints.

Let \(y\) attain the original benchmark \(B\). For every bundle in its support,

\(K\sum_{g\in S}d_g\le c(S)\le B.\)

Hence the maximum rounded sum in this support is a feasible integer threshold. By minimality of \(H^\star\),

\(KH^\star\le B.\)

Extract a distribution for threshold \(H^\star\). Each of its supported bundles satisfies

\(c(S)\le K\sum_{g\in S}d_g+|S|K\le KH^\star+mK\le B+\frac29C\le\frac{11}{9}B.\)

Here the first inequality follows from \(c_g<K(d_g+1)\), and the last uses \(C\le B\).

This completes the chores algorithm.

6. Combining types, output size, and running time

Apply the appropriate algorithm independently to every type, obtaining distributions \(y^t\in Y(q)\). Output

\(x_{t,S}=\mu_t y^t_S.\)

Section 1 verifies the mass and supply constraints, and Section 2 transfers the rounded additive guarantees to the original utilities.

The goods algorithm uses \(O(m^2)\) candidate thresholds per type and \(O(\log(m+1))\) LPs of size \(O(m^2)\). The chores algorithm uses \(O(\log(m+1))\) LPs of size \(O(m^3)\).

All differences, rounding scales, candidate thresholds, and comparisons are computed exactly with rational numbers of bit length polynomial in \(L+\log(m+1)\). The flow LPs have polynomial encoding length, and their rational solutions and path decompositions have polynomial encoding length.

Consequently, for either problem, the total running time is

\(\tau\,\operatorname{poly}(m,L),\)

hence polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The algorithms explicitly output at most \(O(\tau m^2)\) type–bundle entries for goods and \(O(\tau m^3)\) for chores, each with an exact rational mass.

Thus neither implicit bundles nor input-derived benchmarks prevent polynomial-time computation of the stated guarantees.
