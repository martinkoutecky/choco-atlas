The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite supports of boy and girl types with rational masses summing to one on each side and strict rankings over opposite-side types, let \(x^G\) be the girl-proposing stable flow in the truthful market and \(A_g=\{b:x^G_{bg}>0\}\). For each girl type \(g\), choose strategic mass \(\lambda_g\in[0,\mu_g]\), split from its truthful mass, and have it blacklist boy types outside \(A_g\). Given a threshold \(K\), decide whether some choice with \(\sum_g\lambda_g\le K\) makes boy-proposing type-capacitated deferred acceptance on the split market return exactly \(x^G\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(B,G\) be nonempty finite supports of boy and girl types, with positive binary-rational masses \(\nu_b,\mu_g\) satisfying \(\sum_b\nu_b=\sum_g\mu_g=1\). Every type has a complete strict ranking of opposite-side types, all preferred to being unmatched. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types and their binary-rational masses; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here set \(\tau=|B|+|G|\), \(m=\max\{|B|,|G|\}\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A feasible flow assigns nonnegative mass to acceptable pairs, with incident totals bounded by the corresponding type masses and no additional pair capacities. An acceptable pair blocks if each endpoint has either unmatched mass or positive mass assigned to a strictly worse partner; a stable flow has no blocking pair.
Mass deferred acceptance starts empty: an input-order-first proposer with free mass offers all that mass to its highest-ranked acceptable receiver that has not rejected it; receivers retain their best offered mass up to capacity and reject the rest. Partial rejection sends only the rejected mass onward; held mass remains until displaced. Stop when no further proposal is possible. Let \(x^G\) be the truthful girl-proposing outcome and \(A_g=\{b:x^G_{bg}>0\}\).
Choose real strategic masses \(0\le\lambda_g\le\mu_g\). Replace each girl type by truthful and strategic copies of masses \(\mu_g-\lambda_g\) and \(\lambda_g\), omitting zero-mass copies. The truthful copy accepts all boys; the strategic copy accepts exactly \(A_g\); both preserve the original boy ranking. Boys preserve their ordering of original girl types, placing the truthful copy immediately before the strategic copy within each type. Run boy-proposing mass deferred acceptance, and aggregate both copies' flows to obtain \(y_{bg}\).

Given a nonnegative binary-rational threshold \(K\), is deciding whether some \(\lambda\) satisfies \(\sum_g\lambda_g\le K\) and \(y_{bg}=x^G_{bg}\) for every \((b,g)\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Discrete winning coalitions are exactly those intersecting every minimal rotation's girl set, where these rotations are the cyclic partner changes from immediate upper covers of the girl-optimal matching in the boy-preference order; a minimum coalition is computable in \(O(n^2)\) time for \(n\) agents per side. [Ndiaye–Norin–Vetta](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.15748v2)

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). More precisely, writing \(x^B\) for truthful boy-proposing mass DA,

\(\displaystyle \text{YES}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K>0\ \text{or}\ \bigl(K=0\ \text{and}\ x^B=x^G\bigr).\)

The key fact is that every rational choice satisfying \(0<\lambda_g<\mu_g\) for every girl type forces the target outcome. Thus arbitrarily small positive budgets suffice. The stipulated ordering of truthful before strategic copies is essential to the proof.

A conservative worst-case bound is \(O(m^2\tau^3)\) arithmetic operations and \(O(m^2\tau^3L^2)\) bit operations. The positive-budget branch takes \(O(L)\) time.

1. Optimality of the specified mass DA

We first establish the property of DA needed below, including its applicability to partial rejections.

Consider any finite market with proposer quotas \(a_p\), receiver quotas \(c_q\), strict rankings, and no pair capacities. For a flow \(v\), define the proposer’s preference-prefix mass by

\(\displaystyle C_p(v;q)=\sum_{r\succeq_p q}v_{pr}.\)

Lemma 1. With rational quotas, the specified mass DA terminates at a stable flow \(h\). For every stable flow \(z\) in that market,

\(\displaystyle C_p(h;q)\ge C_p(z;q)\)

for every proposer \(p\) and acceptable receiver \(q\). Consequently its outcome is independent of the proposal order.

Proof. We maintain artificial bounds \(u_{pq}\), initially \(a_p\) on every acceptable pair. Whenever receiver \(q\) rejects any mass of proposer \(p\), set \(u_{pq}\) equal to the mass of \(p\) that \(q\) retains after that proposal.

Once a receiver has rejected a proposer, that proposer sends no new mass there. Consequently, on a previously rejected pair, \(u_{pq}\) always equals the currently held mass.

We prove the invariant that every stable flow \(z\) satisfies \(z_{pq}\le u_{pq}\). Initially this follows from the proposer quota.

At a current state, let \(r_p\) be the allocation obtained by greedily filling quota \(a_p\), in preference order, subject only to the current bounds \(u_{pq}\). For every flow \(z\) satisfying those bounds and the proposer quota,

\(\displaystyle C_p(z;q)\le \min\left\{a_p,\sum_{r\succeq_p q}u_{pr}\right\}=C_p(r_p;q).\)

All receivers preceding the proposer’s current proposal position have already rejected it. Their bounds equal their held masses. Thus \(r_p\) consists of its held row together with all its free mass placed at its next un-rejected receiver, if such a receiver exists.

Suppose a proposal is made to \(q\), and let \(\widehat h_{pq}\) denote the offered masses there before trimming. For every proposer,

\(\displaystyle \widehat h_{pq}\le r_{pq}.\)

If \(q\) rejects some mass of \(p\), its retained mass is

\(\displaystyle \bar h_{pq}=\max\left\{0,c_q-\sum_{s\succ_q p}\widehat h_{sq}\right\}.\)

Suppose, contrary to the invariant we want to preserve, that a stable flow \(z\) has \(z_{pq}>\bar h_{pq}\). Receiver feasibility then implies that some proposer \(s\succ_q p\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle z_{sq}<\widehat h_{sq}\le r_{sq}.\)

Indeed, otherwise the mass of proposers preferred to \(p\), together with \(z_{pq}\), would exceed \(c_q\).

The prefix inequality above now gives

\(\displaystyle C_s(z;q) \le \sum_{r\succ_s q}r_{sr}+z_{sq} < C_s(r_s;q) \le a_s. \)

Therefore proposer \(s\) has either unmatched mass or positive mass assigned below \(q\) in \(z\). Receiver \(q\) has positive mass \(z_{pq}\) assigned to the strictly worse proposer \(p\). Hence \((s,q)\) blocks \(z\), a contradiction. Updating every rejected pair’s bound therefore preserves the invariant.

For termination, choose a common denominator \(D\) for the rational quotas. Every held mass and bound remains a multiple of \(1/D\). Each proposal involving a rejection strictly decreases at least one bound by at least \(1/D\). The sum of the bounds is finite and nonnegative, so there are finitely many such proposals. A proposal without rejection exhausts its proposer’s free mass and creates no new free mass; therefore only finitely many proposals can occur between rejection events.

At termination, the greedy row \(r_p\) equals the held row \(h_p\): either the proposer has no free mass, or its list is exhausted. The invariant and the prefix inequality prove the asserted dominance over every stable \(z\).

Finally, suppose \((p,q)\) blocked \(h\). Since \(p\) has free mass or mass assigned below \(q\), it must previously have been rejected by \(q\). At that rejection, \(q\) became full with partners weakly preferred to \(p\), and subsequent proposals can only improve its retained partners. Thus \(q\) cannot have unmatched mass or a strictly worse partner. This contradiction proves stability.

Two DA outcomes each dominate the other in every preference prefix. Their rows, and hence their flows, must coincide. \(\square\)

In the original complete market, every stable flow matches all mass. Otherwise, equality of the two sides’ total masses would leave unmatched mass on both sides, producing a blocking pair.

In particular,

\(\displaystyle \sum_g x^G_{bg}=\nu_b,\qquad \sum_b x^G_{bg}=\mu_g.\)

2. The target’s positive-flow graph is a forest

Let \(x=x^G\), and define its support graph

\(\displaystyle H_x=\bigl(B\cup G,\{bg:x_{bg}>0\}\bigr).\)

Lemma 2. The graph \(H_x\) is a forest.

Proof. Suppose its support contains a simple cycle. Every cycle vertex strictly prefers one of its two incident cycle edges.

No edge can be preferred by both its endpoints: both would then have positive mass on their other, strictly worse cycle edges, so that pair would block \(x\).

A cycle has equally many vertices and edges. Each vertex selects one preferred edge, and no edge is selected twice. Consequently every edge is selected exactly once. Following these selections around the cycle shows that the girls’ preferred edges form one alternating matching of the cycle, and the boys’ preferred edges form the other.

Choose a positive rational \(\delta\) smaller than every mass on a cycle edge. Increase the girls’ preferred alternating edges by \(\delta\), and decrease the other alternating edges by \(\delta\). Call the resulting flow \(x'\).

Every incident total is unchanged, and every previously positive edge remains positive. Stability depends only on incident totals and on which edges carry positive mass: these determine unmatched mass and the existence of strictly worse partners. Thus \(x'\) remains stable.

Each girl on the cycle transfers \(\delta\) from a worse boy to a better boy, so at least one of her preference-prefix masses strictly increases. This contradicts Lemma 1 applied to girl-proposing DA, whose outcome \(x\) dominates every stable flow in every girl’s preference prefix. \(\square\)

We will also use the following elementary consequence.

Lemma 3. A flow supported on a forest is uniquely determined by its incident totals.

Proof. The difference of two such flows has incident sum zero at every vertex. At a leaf, its sole incident edge must therefore have difference zero. Remove that edge and repeat. This eliminates every edge of the forest. \(\square\)

3. A stable lift of the target to the copied market

Fix rational masses \(0<\lambda_g<\mu_g\). Denote the two copies by \(g^t\) and \(g^s\).

Construct a flow \(z\) in the copied market as follows. For each girl \(g\), give the truthful copy the best \(\mu_g-\lambda_g\) mass from her target column \(x_{\cdot g}\), in her boy ranking. Give the remaining \(\lambda_g\) mass to the strategic copy.

This construction fills both copies, preserves every boy’s total, and aggregates to \(x\). The strategic copy receives only boys in \(A_g\), so its assignments are acceptable.

The prefix construction has this useful property:

If \(z_{b,g^s}>0\), every boy assigned to \(g^t\) is weakly preferred by \(g\) to \(b\).

Lemma 4. The lifted flow \(z\) is stable in the copied market.

Proof. Suppose an acceptable pair consisting of boy \(b\) and a copy of \(g\) blocks \(z\).

If \(b\)’s willingness comes from mass assigned to an original girl type strictly below \(g\), then \(b\) is willing to trade with \(g\) in \(x\). Stability of \(x\) implies that every boy in \(g\)’s target column is weakly preferred to \(b\). Both copies contain only boys from that column, are full, and therefore are unwilling to trade with \(b\).

The only remaining source of willingness introduced by copying is that \(b\) holds positive mass at \(g^s\) and prefers \(g^t\). But the prefix property says that \(g^t\) holds no boy strictly worse than \(b\). It is full, so this pair cannot block either. \(\square\)

4. Positive strategic mass at every girl forces the target

Run boy-proposing DA in this copied market. Let \(h\) be its flow before aggregation and \(y\) its aggregation.

By Lemmas 1 and 4, every boy’s allocation in \(h\) dominates its allocation in \(z\) in every copied preference prefix. Since every boy is fully matched in \(z\), every boy is fully matched in \(h\).

The total receiver capacity is also \(1\). Thus every receiver copy is full. In particular, each strategic copy holds its positive mass \(\lambda_g\).

Aggregating the boy-prefix inequalities at boundaries between original girl types gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{r\succeq_b g}y_{br} \ge \sum_{r\succeq_b g}x_{br}. \)

We now prove that \(y\) uses only target-support edges.

Suppose \(y_{bg}>0\) but \(x_{bg}=0\). Let \(w_b\) be boy \(b\)’s worst positive-mass partner in \(x\). The prefix inequality at \(w_b\) implies that \(y\) assigns no mass below \(w_b\). Since \(x_{b,w_b}>0\), our new girl type \(g\) differs from \(w_b\), and therefore \(b\) strictly prefers \(g\) to \(w_b\).

Consequently \(b\) is willing to trade with \(g\) in \(x\). Stability of \(x\), together with \(x_{bg}=0\), implies that every boy in \(A_g\) is strictly preferred by \(g\) to \(b\).

The new mass \(y_{bg}>0\) must be held by \(g^t\), because \(g^s\) accepts only \(A_g\). Since \(g^s\) is full and has positive capacity, some boy \(c\in A_g\) satisfies \(h_{c,g^s}>0\).

Boy \(c\) prefers \(g^t\) to \(g^s\). The truthful copy holds positive mass of \(b\), whom it strictly ranks below \(c\). Thus \((c,g^t)\) blocks \(h\), contradicting Lemma 1.

We have proved

\(\displaystyle y_{bg}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ x_{bg}>0.\)

Both \(y\) and \(x\) have the same full incident totals. Their common containing support graph \(H_x\) is a forest by Lemma 2. Lemma 3 therefore yields

\(\displaystyle y=x=x^G.\)

This proves the claimed sufficiency of every rational vector with \(0<\lambda_g<\mu_g\).

5. Decision algorithm and running time

If \(K>0\), set

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=\min\{K/2,1/2\},\qquad \lambda_g=\varepsilon\mu_g.\)

These are rational masses satisfying

\(\displaystyle 0<\lambda_g<\mu_g,\qquad \sum_g\lambda_g=\varepsilon\le K.\)

Section 4 proves that their aggregated outcome equals \(x^G\). Thus the algorithm immediately answers YES. This branch requires only reading the input, taking \(O(L)\) time; the witness itself has polynomial encoding length.

If \(K=0\), nonnegativity forces every \(\lambda_g=0\). The copied market is then exactly the truthful original market. Hence feasibility holds exactly when \(x^B=x^G\).

For this branch, compute the two proposer-optimal stable allocations using the corrected Baïou–Balinski algorithms. Their inductive algorithm finds a stable allocation, and their row/column-optimal algorithms find the two optima; the relevant bounds are Theorem 2 and Corollary 4. Their optimality order implies cumulative dominance—mass gains precede mass losses in preference order—so Lemma 1 identifies these optima with the specified DA outcomes. The hypotheses apply: the market is finite, rankings are strict, quotas are positive rational numbers, and every pair can be given the redundant capacity \(2\), which never binds because total mass is \(1\).

For an explicit conservative implementation bound, put \(p=|B|\), \(q=|G|\), and \(E=pq\). The cited algorithms have \(O(E+\tau)\) allocation changes or terminal stages. Their search paths have \(O(\tau)\) vertices. Recomputing preference-prefix sums and scanning edges at each path step costs at most \(O(E\tau)\) arithmetic operations per stage. Thus computing both outcomes takes

arithmetic operations. Comparing them costs another \(O(E)\).

\(\displaystyle O(m^2\tau^3L^2).\)

These bounds depend on the numbers of positive-mass types, with no expansion into individual agents.

Therefore the target decision problem is polynomial-time solvable. The infimum successful strategic mass is always zero; it is attained precisely when \(x^B=x^G\).
