The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), a rational distribution \(\mu\), total mass \(M>2\), and type masses \(a_t=M\mu_t\), let \(V\) be a rational, nonnegative, monotone, superadditive value function on \(\prod_{t\in T}[0,a_t]\), represented by an explicit rational piecewise-affine subdivision. What is the minimum \(\Delta\ge 0\) for which there are per-unit type payoffs \(p_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t p_ta_t=V(a)+\Delta\) and \(\sum_t p_tq_t\ge V(q)\) for every \(q\le a\) with \(\sum_tq_t\le 2\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(a_t\). Here \(a_t=M\mu_t\), where \(M>2\) is rational and \(\mu_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input specifies \(V:\prod_t[0,a_t]\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) by \(m\) closed rational polytopes covering its domain, each given by rational linear inequalities and an affine formula with rational coefficients; formulas agree on overlaps. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
It is promised that \(V(0)=0\), that \(V\) is coordinatewise monotone, and that \(V(q+r)\ge V(q)+V(r)\) whenever \(q,r\ge0\) and \(q+r\le a\).
A coalition is any real mass vector \(0\le q\le a\); it may deviate precisely when \(\sum_tq_t\le2\). A subsidy \(\Delta\ge0\) stabilizes the society if there are per-unit payoffs \(p_t\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) with \(\sum_tp_ta_t=V(a)+\Delta\) and \(\sum_tp_tq_t\ge V(q)\) for every allowed coalition. Write \(\Delta^*\) for the minimum such subsidy.

Given a binary-rational threshold \(B\ge0\), is deciding whether \(\Delta^*\le B\) in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete nonnegative monotone superadditive TU games allowing deviations by singletons and pairs, a greedy algorithm constructs stable payoffs totaling at most \(2v(N)\). [Meir, Rosenschein, and Malizia](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/11/Papers/060.pdf)
No result settling this exact continuous formulation was located in the literature search.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). In fact, \(\Delta^*\) can be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The key is that each affine piece contributes a family of linear payoff constraints indexed by a rational polytope. Linear programming duality replaces that entire family by a polynomial-size system of linear constraints.

1. Restrict each piece to allowed coalitions

Identify the support with \(\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), and write the input pieces as

\[ P_i=\{q:A_iq\le b_i\}, \qquad V(q)=c_i^{\mathsf T}q+d_i\quad(q\in P_i), \qquad i=1,\ldots,m. \]

Define

\[ Q_i=P_i\cap\{q:0\le q\le a,\ \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}q\le2\}. \]

By appending the displayed inequalities to the description of \(P_i\), obtain a rational description

\[ Q_i=\{q:H_iq\le h_i\}. \]

Test each \(Q_i\) for feasibility by rational linear programming and discard empty ones. Let \(I\) denote the retained indices. Every retained \(Q_i\) is nonempty and compact, because it is closed and contained in the bounded box \([0,a]\). Lower-dimensional pieces are retained.

The covering promise implies

\[ \bigcup_{i\in I}Q_i = \{q:0\le q\le a,\ \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}q\le2\}. \]

Consequently, a nonnegative payoff vector \(p\) satisfies all deviation constraints exactly when, for every \(i\in I\),

\[ (c_i-p)^{\mathsf T}q+d_i\le0 \quad\text{for all }q\in Q_i. \]

Also compute \(v_a=V(a)\): find a piece containing \(a\) by checking its inequalities, and evaluate its affine formula. Coverage guarantees such a piece; agreement on overlaps guarantees a unique value.

2. Dualize the constraints on each piece

Fix \(i\in I\) and a payoff vector \(p\). Consider the linear program

\[ \max\{(c_i-p)^{\mathsf T}q:H_iq\le h_i\}. \]

Here \(q\) is treated as unrestricted in sign when deriving the dual: its nonnegativity constraints are already rows of \(H_iq\le h_i\). The dual is therefore

\[ \min\{h_i^{\mathsf T}y_i: H_i^{\mathsf T}y_i=c_i-p,\ y_i\ge0\}. \]

The primal is feasible and has a finite attained optimum, since \(Q_i\) is nonempty and compact. Strong LP duality therefore guarantees an attained dual optimum with the same value. This theorem applies without a full-dimensionality assumption. See the strong-duality and general-dual formulations in [MIT’s linear programming notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-854j-advanced-algorithms-fall-2005/d974aef980258f2a796171e96717d646_n11_lp.pdf).

It follows that the constraints on piece \(i\) are equivalent to the existence of \(y_i\) satisfying

\[ y_i\ge0, \qquad H_i^{\mathsf T}y_i=c_i-p, \qquad h_i^{\mathsf T}y_i+d_i\le0. \]

For completeness, both implications are explicit:

  • If such \(y_i\) exists, then every \(q\in Q_i\) satisfies

\[ (c_i-p)^{\mathsf T}q+d_i =y_i^{\mathsf T}H_iq+d_i \le y_i^{\mathsf T}h_i+d_i \le0. \]

  • Conversely, if the constraints hold throughout \(Q_i\), the primal optimum is at most \(-d_i\). An optimal dual vector then satisfies all three displayed conditions.

Crucially, these are linear constraints jointly in \(p\) and \(y_i\).

3. The minimum subsidy exists

We first prove that some finite subsidy stabilizes the society.

Let

\[ C=\max_{1\le i\le m,\ 1\le t\le\tau}|c_{i,t}|. \]

We claim that, throughout the box,

\[ V(q)\le C\sum_tq_t. \]

Fix \(q\in[0,a]\) and parameterize the segment from \(0\) to \(q\) by \(\lambda q\), where \(0\le\lambda\le1\). For each piece, the set

\[ J_i=\{\lambda\in[0,1]:\lambda q\in P_i\} \]

is empty or a closed interval, possibly a singleton. These intervals cover \([0,1]\).

Partition \([0,1]\) at all endpoints of the nonempty intervals. Each resulting subinterval of positive length is contained in some \(J_i\): choose an interior point and a covering interval; since no interval endpoint lies inside that subinterval, the covering interval contains its entirety, including the endpoints.

On such a subinterval \([\alpha,\beta]\), agreement with the affine formula gives

\[ V(\beta q)-V(\alpha q) =(\beta-\alpha)c_i^{\mathsf T}q \le(\beta-\alpha)C\sum_tq_t, \]

using \(q_t\ge0\). Summing over the partition and using \(V(0)=0\) proves the claim.

Thus the uniform payoff \(p_t=C\) dominates \(V(q)\) for every coalition in the entire box. Moreover,

\[ a^{\mathsf T}p=CM\ge V(a), \]

so it yields a valid subsidy \(\Delta_0=CM-v_a\ge0\).

The set of nonnegative payoff vectors satisfying all deviation constraints is closed, being an intersection of closed half-spaces. Restrict it further by

\[ v_a\le a^{\mathsf T}p\le CM. \]

This set is nonempty by the uniform-payoff construction. It is compact: every coordinate satisfies \(0\le p_t\le CM/a_t\), and \(a_t>0\). The continuous function \(a^{\mathsf T}p-v_a\) attains a minimum there. Any payoff outside this restriction with total exceeding \(CM\) has subsidy exceeding \(\Delta_0\), so this is also the global minimum \(\Delta^*\).

4. An exact finite linear program

Consider the following LP, with variables \(p,\Delta\), and one vector \(y_i\) for each \(i\in I\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad &\Delta\\ \text{subject to}\quad &p\ge0,\qquad \Delta\ge0,\\ &a^{\mathsf T}p=v_a+\Delta,\\ &y_i\ge0 &&(i\in I),\\ &H_i^{\mathsf T}y_i=c_i-p &&(i\in I),\\ &h_i^{\mathsf T}y_i+d_i\le0 &&(i\in I). \end{aligned} \]

By Section 2 and the covering property, a pair \((p,\Delta)\) extends to a feasible solution of this LP exactly when \(\Delta\) stabilizes the society using \(p\). Hence its optimal value is precisely \(\Delta^*\).

For the decision problem, simply add the constraint \(\Delta\le B\) and test feasibility. Section 3 ensures that the minimum is attained, so this test answers exactly whether \(\Delta^*\le B\).

5. Binary complexity and scope

Let \(r_i\) be the number of inequalities describing input piece \(P_i\), and let \(R=\sum_i r_i\). Each \(H_i\) has \(r_i+2\tau+1\) rows. Therefore the total number of multiplier variables is at most

\[ R+m(2\tau+1). \]

The final LP has \(\tau+1\) additional variables, at most \(m\tau\) dual equalities, at most \(m\) dual objective inequalities, and the budget and nonnegativity constraints. Its dimensions are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Its coefficients are input rationals, constants from the appended box and mass inequalities, and the computed rational \(v_a\). Evaluating \(v_a=c_i^{\mathsf T}a+d_i\) uses polynomially many rational arithmetic operations and produces polynomial binary length. Even a dense encoding of the constructed LP has polynomial total length.

Rational LP feasibility and optimization admit algorithms polynomial in binary encoding length; this is the relevant, weakly polynomial guarantee. See [MIT’s notes, Section 5.3](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-854j-advanced-algorithms-fall-2005/d974aef980258f2a796171e96717d646_n11_lp.pdf). Thus the preprocessing and final feasibility test run in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Exact optimization also computes \(\Delta^*\) and optimal rational payoffs in polynomial time.

The proof uses the explicit polyhedral affine representation, agreement on overlaps, \(V(0)=0\), and positive support masses. Monotonicity and superadditivity are unnecessary for this algorithm. The stated promised class is therefore fully covered.
