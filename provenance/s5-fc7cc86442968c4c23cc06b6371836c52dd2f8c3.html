The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite facilities \(J\), types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), strict rankings \(\succ_t\), per-person allocation costs \(g_{tj}\), capacity shares \(q_j\), and opening costs \(F_j\), choose \(y_j\in\{0,1\}\) and assignment masses \(x_{tj}\ge 0\) minimizing \(\sum_j F_jy_j+\sum_{t,j}g_{tj}x_{tj}\), subject to \(\sum_jx_{tj}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_tx_{tj}\le q_jy_j\). Require no mass to prefer an open undersubscribed facility, and no nonzero improving assignment circulation \(z_{tjk}\ge0\) with \(\sum_k z_{tjk}\le x_{tj}\), supported only on destinations \(k\succ_t j\), and balanced inflow and outflow at each facility. Find an optimal stable pair \((y,x)\), or report infeasibility.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(J\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) facilities. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; each type specifies a strict complete ranking \(\succ_t\) of \(J\) and its allocation costs.
Opening costs \(F_j\) and per-person allocation costs \(g_{tj}\) are nonnegative binary rationals; capacity shares \(q_j\) are positive binary rationals, with \(q_j<1\) for at least one facility. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Choose \(y_j\in\{0,1\}\) and real assignment masses \(x_{tj}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_jx_{tj}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_tx_{tj}\leq q_jy_j\).
Call \((y,x)\) stable if both following conditions hold. First, whenever \(x_{tj}>0\), \(y_k=1\), and \(k\succ_t j\), one has \(\sum_sx_{sk}=q_k\).
Second, there is no nonzero real array \(z_{tjk}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_kz_{tjk}\leq x_{tj}\), \(z_{tjk}=0\) unless \(y_k=1\) and \(k\succ_t j\), and \(\sum_{t,k}z_{tjk}=\sum_{t,k}z_{tkj}\) for every facility \(j\).
Minimize \(C(y,x)=\sum_jF_jy_j+\sum_{t,j}g_{tj}x_{tj}\) over stable pairs, or report infeasibility; any minimizing pair is acceptable.

Is the decision problem of whether a stable pair has \(C(y,x)\leq B\), for a nonnegative binary rational threshold \(B\), in P or NP-hard, with polynomial running time measured in \(m,\tau,L\) (including \(B\) in \(L\))?

For indivisible customers, assignment integrality can be relaxed in a reduced cyclic-coalition-stable formulation while opening and facility-filling-order variables remain binary. [Discrete integrality result](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21944v1#S6.SS2).

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even with one type, zero allocation costs, and \(0<q_j<1\) for every facility. Thus divisible assignments do not yield a polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof below concerns exactly the stated mass-assignment model.

1. The circulation condition is equivalent to acyclicity

For a feasible pair \((y,x)\), define a directed graph \(D(y,x)\) on \(J\). Include an arc \(j\to k\) precisely when some type \(t\) satisfies \(x_{tj}>0\), \(y_k=1\), and \(k\succ_t j\).

Lemma. The second stability condition holds if and only if \(D(y,x)\) is acyclic.

*Proof.* Suppose the graph contains a simple directed cycle \(j_1\to j_2\to\cdots\to j_r\to j_1\). For each arc, choose a witnessing type \(t_i\), and put \(j_{r+1}=j_1\). Define \(\varepsilon=\min_{1\leq i\leq r}x_{t_i j_i}>0\).

Set \(z_{t_i j_i j_{i+1}}=\varepsilon\), with every other entry zero. Because the cycle vertices are distinct, each source pair \((t,j)\) occurs at most once. Consequently, \(\sum_k z_{tjk}\leq x_{tj}\). Every selected move goes to a preferred open facility, and each cycle facility has incoming and outgoing mass \(\varepsilon\). This is a forbidden nonzero array.

Conversely, suppose such an array exists. Put \(w_{jk}=\sum_t z_{tjk}\). Then \(w\) is a nonzero nonnegative circulation supported on the arcs of \(D(y,x)\): conservation gives \(\sum_k w_{jk}=\sum_k w_{kj}\) at every vertex.

Starting with any positive-flow arc, its head has positive incoming flow and therefore positive outgoing flow. Repeating this argument produces a directed walk of positive-flow arcs. Since the graph is finite, a vertex repeats, yielding a directed cycle. This proves the lemma.

2. Hardness with one type and strictly subunit capacities

Reduce from positive-integer SUBSET SUM: given binary-encoded positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) and \(A>0\), decide whether some subset \(S\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i=A\). This is the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete exact-sum problem. [Karp’s original completeness paper](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Choose a power of two \(D>A+\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\). Construct facilities \(J=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}\), with:

  • One type of mass \(\mu_1=1\), ranking \(0\succ_1 1\succ_1\cdots\succ_1 n\).
  • Capacity \(q_0=1-A/D\) and opening cost \(F_0=0\).
  • Capacity and opening cost \(q_i=F_i=a_i/D\) for \(1\leq i\leq n\).
  • Allocation costs \(g_{1j}=0\) for every facility.
  • Threshold \(B=A/D\).

All capacities are positive and strictly below one. All numbers are dyadic rationals of polynomial bit length, and the construction has \(m=n+1\) and \(\tau=1\).

Suppose first that the SUBSET SUM instance has a solution \(S\). Open exactly facility \(0\) and the facilities in \(S\). Assign \(x_{10}=q_0\), \(x_{1i}=q_i\) for \(i\in S\), and zero elsewhere. The assigned mass is

\(q_0+\sum_{i\in S}q_i=1-A/D+A/D=1.\)

Every open facility is full, so the first stability condition holds. Every arc of \(D(y,x)\) points from a larger index to a smaller index, so the graph is acyclic. The lemma establishes the second condition. Finally, \(C(y,x)=\sum_{i\in S}a_i/D=A/D=B\).

Conversely, suppose the constructed instance has a stable pair of cost at most \(B\). Let \(S=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:y_i=1\}\). Because allocation costs vanish, the budget implies \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i\leq A\).

Facility \(0\) must be open. Otherwise, total open capacity would be at most \(A/D<1\), contradicting feasibility. With facility \(0\) open, feasibility requires

\(1\leq q_0+\sum_{i\in S}q_i=1-A/D+\sum_{i\in S}a_i/D.\)

Thus \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i\geq A\). Combining both inequalities gives \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i=A\), a SUBSET SUM solution.

This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness. The construction uses divisible masses throughout; its binary choices are precisely the facility-opening decisions.

3. Polynomial-size certificates despite real assignments

It remains to prove membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), since the problem permits arbitrary real assignments.

Take any real stable witness \((y,x)\) satisfying the budget. Define its positive support \(S_x=\{(t,j):x_{tj}>0\}\) and its set of full facilities \(H_x=\{k:\sum_t x_{tk}=q_k\}\).

Keeping \(y\) fixed, consider the polytope \(P\) of assignments \(u\) satisfying:

  • \(u_{tj}\geq0\) and \(\sum_j u_{tj}=\mu_t\).
  • \(\sum_t u_{tj}\leq q_jy_j\).
  • \(u_{tj}=0\) whenever \((t,j)\notin S_x\).
  • \(\sum_t u_{tk}=q_k\) whenever \(k\in H_x\).
  • \(\sum_jF_jy_j+\sum_{t,j}g_{tj}u_{tj}\leq B\).

This is a nonempty rational polytope because it contains \(x\). It is bounded because \(0\leq u_{tj}\leq\mu_t\).

Every \(u\in P\) is stable. Indeed, if \(u_{tj}>0\), then \(x_{tj}>0\). For any preferred open facility \(k\), stability of \(x\) implies \(k\in H_x\), and the imposed equality makes \(k\) full under \(u\). This proves the first condition. Moreover, \(D(y,u)\) is a subgraph of \(D(y,x)\), hence is acyclic. The lemma proves the second condition.

A nonempty bounded polytope has a vertex. Such a vertex of \(P\) has polynomial rational bit length, as follows. Write \(d=\tau m\). After clearing denominators in each constraint, all integer coefficients have bit length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). At a vertex, \(d\) linearly independent active constraints determine the coordinates. Cramer’s rule expresses each coordinate as a ratio of determinants of integer matrices of order \(d\). If their entries have absolute value at most \(M\), each determinant has absolute value at most \(d!M^d\). Its bit length is therefore at most \(O(d\log d+d\log M)\), which is polynomial.

Consequently, every yes-instance has a polynomial-size certificate consisting of \(y\) and a rational stable assignment. A verifier checks feasibility, the budget, and the first stability condition using exact rational arithmetic. It constructs \(D(y,x)\) by examining at most \(\tau m^2\) triples and checks acyclicity to verify the second condition. All these operations take polynomial time.

Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), completing the proof of \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

4. Relation to the discrete integrality result

The cited discrete result relaxes assignment integrality while retaining binary opening and filling-order variables. [Proposition 8 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21944v1#S6.SS2). The reduction above shows directly why continuous assignments can coexist with computational hardness: selecting which capacities to open already encodes SUBSET SUM.
