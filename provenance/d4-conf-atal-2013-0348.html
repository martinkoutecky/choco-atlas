The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) goods with rational per-capita supplies \(b_\ell\), and \(\tau\) types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), where type \(t\) has value \(v_t\) and an integral demanded bundle \(d_t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^m\), choose served masses \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) to maximize \(\sum_t v_t x_t\), subject to \(\sum_t d_t(\ell)x_t\le b_\ell\) for every good \(\ell\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses satisfy \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).
There are \(m\ge2\) goods, with \(m\) unrestricted, and positive rational per-capita supplies \(b_\ell\). Type \(t\) has a positive rational value \(v_t\) and a nonzero demanded bundle \(d_t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\), encoded in binary.
Serving mass \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) gives every served individual its entire demanded bundle; unserved individuals have value zero. Welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_t v_t x_t\).
Define \(\mathrm{OPT}(b)=\max\{W(x):0\le x_t\le\mu_t,\ \sum_t d_t(\ell)x_t\le b_\ell\text{ for every }\ell\}\). A \(\rho\)-approximation with supply relaxation \(\varepsilon\) returns such bounded masses satisfying \(\sum_t d_t(\ell)x_t\le(1+\varepsilon)b_\ell\) and \(W(x)\ge\mathrm{OPT}(b)/\rho\).
For fixed rational constants \(0<\delta<1/2\) and \(0\le\varepsilon<1\), is an \(m^{1/2-\delta}\)-approximation with supply relaxation \(\varepsilon\) computable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is achieving this guarantee NP-hard, where \(L\) is the total input bit length?
For discrete single-minded bidders, achieving an \(m^{1/2-\delta}\)-approximation is NP-hard for every fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\), even with supply relaxation \(0\le\varepsilon<1\). [Krysta, Telelis, and Ventre (2015)](https://ranger.uta.edu/~weems/NOTES6319/PAPERSTWO/Mechanisms_for_Multi-unit_Comb.pdf)

2. Exact linear programming formulation

Index the explicitly listed support by \(t\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\). Define the matrix \(D\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{m\times\tau}\) by \(D_{\ell t}=d_t(\ell)\), and let \(v=(v_1,\ldots,v_\tau)\) and \(\mu=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau)\).

Consider the rational linear program:

  • Maximize \(v^{\mathsf T}x\).
  • Subject to \(Dx\le b\).
  • Subject to \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\).

Its feasible set and objective coincide exactly with those defining \(\mathrm{OPT}(b)\). Thus this is an exact formulation of the given problem.

We apply the standard polynomial-time rational linear programming theorem: a linear program with explicitly given binary rational coefficients can be solved in time polynomial in its encoding length; when feasible with finite optimum, an exact rational optimizer can be returned. See [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239).

All hypotheses hold here:

  • Explicit polynomial size. There are \(\tau\) variables and \(m+2\tau\) inequalities. The demanded bundles, masses, supplies, and values are explicitly supplied. Writing the matrix and bound inequalities takes encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), even if the input uses a sparse representation.
  • Rational data. Every coefficient is an input rational, an input integer, or one of \(0,1,-1\). Binary-encoded demands are used directly as coefficients; no expansion into individual units is needed.
  • Feasibility. The vector \(x=0\) satisfies every constraint because all supplies and masses are positive.
  • Finite, attained optimum. The feasible set is closed and contained in the bounded box \(\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}[0,\mu_t]\), hence compact. The objective is continuous and satisfies \(0\le v^{\mathsf T}x\le\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}v_t\mu_t\).

The theorem therefore returns an exact optimizer \(x^\star\) in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), with \(W(x^\star)=\mathrm{OPT}(b)\).

For completeness, exact output does not require excessively long numbers. A linear objective on a nonempty compact polytope has an optimal vertex. Such a vertex is determined by \(\tau\) linearly independent active constraints. Clearing their rational denominators gives an integer system whose coefficients have polynomial bit length. Cramer’s rule and determinant bounds then give polynomial bit length for every coordinate of that vertex.

3. The requested guarantee

Return \(x^\star\) from the preceding algorithm. Its bounds satisfy \(0\le x_t^\star\le\mu_t\), and for every good \(\ell\),

\(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}d_t(\ell)x_t^\star\le b_\ell\le(1+\varepsilon)b_\ell\),

where the second inequality follows from \(\varepsilon\ge0\) and \(b_\ell>0\).

Since \(m\ge2\) and \(0<\delta<1/2\), the requested factor \(\rho=m^{1/2-\delta}\) satisfies \(\rho>1\). Nonnegativity of welfare therefore gives

\(W(x^\star)=\mathrm{OPT}(b)\ge\mathrm{OPT}(b)/m^{1/2-\delta}\).

Both required conditions hold. In fact, the algorithm achieves approximation factor \(1\) with supply relaxation \(0\).

4. Why the discrete hardness does not transfer

The cited hardness proof makes goods correspond to graph edges and bidders correspond to vertices. With unit supplies, serving both endpoints of an edge consumes two units, which exceeds \(1+\varepsilon\) when \(\varepsilon<1\). Its conclusion that served bidders form an independent set depends on each bidder’s binary service decision. See [Krysta, Telelis, and Ventre, Proposition 1](https://ranger.uta.edu/~weems/NOTES6319/PAPERSTWO/Mechanisms_for_Multi-unit_Comb.pdf).

The continuous formulation permits partial service of each type’s mass. A small instance illustrates the resulting difference. Take \(m=\tau=3\), masses \(\mu_t=1/3\), values \(v_t=1\), supplies \(b_\ell=1/3\), and demands

\(d_1=(1,1,0),\quad d_2=(1,0,1),\quad d_3=(0,1,1)\).

The continuous allocation \(x_1=x_2=x_3=1/6\) consumes exactly \(1/3\) of every good and has welfare \(1/2\). Summing the three supply constraints gives \(2(x_1+x_2+x_3)\le1\), so its welfare is optimal.

By contrast, if one imposed the additional restrictions \(x_t\in\{0,\mu_t\}\), any two served types would consume \(2/3\) of their shared good. Since \((1+\varepsilon)/3<2/3\), at most one type could be served even under the allowed relaxation, yielding welfare at most \(1/3\).

Thus whole bundles for served individuals are compatible with continuous type masses. The given problem has an exact polynomial-time solution without any integrality assumption.
