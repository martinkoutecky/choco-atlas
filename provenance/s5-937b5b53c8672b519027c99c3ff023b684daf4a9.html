The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support society μ with rational masses over complete buyer and seller types, gross-substitute valuations, and the paper's bundle and endowment limits, split every type's mass equally between two halves. Compute each half's Walrasian prices, run MIDA at the opposite half's prices, and allocate type mass among feasible integral bundles so each good clears. What is the resulting GFT ratio to the optimum, and can a witnessing mass allocation be computed in time polynomial in τ and input bit length for fixed G and M?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=G\geq1\) goods and an integer \(M\geq1\); write running times in \(G,\tau,L,M\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Each type specifies a buyer or seller role and an explicit table of normalized, nonnegative, weakly increasing gross-substitutes values \(v_t:\{0,\ldots,M\}^{G}\to\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\), with \(v_t(0)=0\). Sellers additionally specify a nonzero endowment \(E_t\in\{0,\ldots,M\}^{G}\); buyers have no goods and unlimited money.
At prices \(p\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^{G}\), a buyer choosing \(X\) gains \(v_t(X)-p\cdot X\); a seller selling an integral bundle \(0\leq X\leq E_t\) gains \(p\cdot X-v_t(E_t)+v_t(E_t-X)\).
A mass allocation assigns nonnegative rational masses \(a_{t,X}\) to feasible integral bundles, with \(\sum_Xa_{t,X}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t\text{ buyer},X}a_{t,X}X=\sum_{t\text{ seller},X}a_{t,X}X\). Its GFT is \(\sum_{t\text{ buyer},X}a_{t,X}v_t(X)-\sum_{t\text{ seller},X}a_{t,X}[v_t(E_t)-v_t(E_t-X)]\).
Let \(\operatorname{OPT}\) be the maximum GFT over these allocations. Restrict to \(\operatorname{OPT}>0\) and markets admitting an optimal allocation with positive traded quantity of every good.
Split each type into two masses \(\mu_t/2\). Compute a Walrasian price vector in each half and post it in the opposite half. The continuous MIDA execution assigns each half's type mass only to utility-maximizing feasible integral bundles, with purchases and sales clearing separately in each half; resolve demand ties centrally to maximize GFT among clearing selections. Prices need not be unique.

What is the tight worst-case ratio of the combined GFT of this equal-split continuous MIDA execution to \(\operatorname{OPT}\), over admissible societies and choices of Walrasian prices? Can the prices and a witnessing mass allocation be computed in time polynomial in \(\tau,L\) for fixed \(G,M\)?

For finite societies, random-halving MIDA has expected GFT ratio at least \(1-18M^{2G+2}c\ln(10Gk_{\max})/\sqrt{k_{\max}}-o(1/k_{\max})\), where \(k_x\) is the optimal traded quantity of good \(x\), \(k_{\max}=\max_xk_x\), and \(c=k_{\max}/\min_xk_x\); multi-good sellers are permitted in this approximation result. Centralized GFT-maximizing demand tie-breaking is also explicitly allowed. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/1604.06210v5)

Result

The tight worst-case ratio is \(1\), for every admissible society and every choice of Walrasian prices in the two halves.

Prices and a witnessing rational mass allocation can be computed in time \(\operatorname{poly}(G,\tau,(M+1)^G,L)\), hence in time polynomial in \(\tau,L\) for fixed \(G,M\). The proof actually does not require gross substitutes: divisible type masses and weakly increasing valuations suffice.

1. Utility maximization and clearing imply optimality

For each type \(t\), let \(\mathcal F_t\) be its feasible set of bundles: \(\{0,\ldots,M\}^G\) for a buyer, and \(\{X:0\leq X\leq E_t\}\) for a seller.

Define the signed net purchase vector \(q_t(X)\) and GFT contribution \(w_t(X)\) by

  • For buyers, \(q_t(X)=X\) and \(w_t(X)=v_t(X)\).
  • For sellers, \(q_t(X)=-X\) and \(w_t(X)=v_t(E_t-X)-v_t(E_t)\).

Thus the stipulated utility is \(w_t(X)-p\cdot q_t(X)\). Define

\( h_t(p)=\max_{X\in\mathcal F_t}\{w_t(X)-p\cdot q_t(X)\}. \)

For a mass vector \(\nu\), write \(\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)\) for its maximum GFT. A Walrasian price for \(\nu\) means a nonnegative rational price admitting a rational clearing allocation supported on utility-maximizing bundles.

For any feasible allocation \(a\) with type masses \(\nu_t\), clearing gives

\( \operatorname{GFT}(a) =\sum_{t,X}a_{t,X}[w_t(X)-p\cdot q_t(X)] \leq\sum_t\nu_t h_t(p). \)

If \(a\) assigns positive mass only to maximizing bundles, equality holds. Consequently, whenever \(p\) is Walrasian,

\( \operatorname{OPT}(\nu)=\sum_t\nu_t h_t(p), \)

and every clearing allocation supported on maximizing bundles is optimal.

A useful stronger conclusion follows. For any optimal allocation \(a^*\) and any Walrasian price \(p\),

\( 0=\sum_t\nu_t h_t(p)-\operatorname{GFT}(a^*) =\sum_{t,X}a^*_{t,X}[h_t(p)-w_t(X)+p\cdot q_t(X)]. \)

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore, whenever \(a^*_{t,X}>0\), the bundle \(X\) maximizes that type’s utility at \(p\). Thus every optimal allocation is supported by every Walrasian price.

2. Exact equal splitting preserves equilibrium prices

For every positive rational \(\lambda\), multiplication by \(\lambda\) is a bijection between feasible allocations with masses \(\mu\) and those with masses \(\lambda\mu\). It preserves clearing and multiplies GFT by \(\lambda\). Hence

\( \operatorname{OPT}(\lambda\mu)=\lambda\operatorname{OPT}(\mu). \)

Utility-maximizing bundles depend on valuations, feasible bundles, and prices, but not on type masses. Scaling a Walrasian witnessing allocation therefore proves that the set of Walrasian prices is unchanged under this scaling.

Both halves have exactly the mass vector \(\mu/2\). Let their independently chosen Walrasian prices be \(p^L\) and \(p^R\). Each price is therefore Walrasian for both halves.

When \(p^R\) is posted in the left half, a clearing selection of maximizing bundles exists. By Section 1, every such selection has GFT

\( \operatorname{OPT}(\mu/2)=\operatorname{OPT}/2. \)

The same conclusion holds when \(p^L\) is posted in the right half. Their combined GFT is consequently exactly \(\operatorname{OPT}\).

This also shows that centralized GFT-maximizing tie-breaking is sufficient but unnecessary for the welfare conclusion: all clearing selections of maximizing bundles have identical GFT.

The admissible class is nonempty for every \(G,M\geq1\). For example, take one buyer type and one seller type, each of mass \(1/2\), with buyer valuation \(v_b(X)=\sum_gX_g\), seller valuation identically zero, and seller endowment \((M,\ldots,M)\). These are gross-substitutes valuations; trading the full endowment gives \(\operatorname{OPT}=GM/2>0\) and positive trade in every good. Thus the worst-case ratio is exactly \(1\).

3. Existence of rational nonnegative equilibrium prices

It remains to justify existence and computation without assuming an equilibrium-existence theorem.

Consider the rational linear program

\( \text{maximize }\sum_{t,X\in\mathcal F_t}a_{t,X}w_t(X), \)

subject to

\( \sum_{X\in\mathcal F_t}a_{t,X}=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t, \qquad \sum_{t,X}a_{t,X}q_t(X)=0, \qquad a_{t,X}\geq0. \)

This is precisely the optimization defining \(\operatorname{OPT}\). Its feasible set is nonempty, since every type can select the zero bundle. It is compact: each variable lies between zero and its type mass. Therefore its optimum is finite and attained.

Its dual is

\( \text{minimize }\sum_t\mu_t z_t, \)

subject to

\( z_t+p\cdot q_t(X)\geq w_t(X) \quad\text{for every }t,\ X\in\mathcal F_t, \)

where \(z_t\) and the coordinates of \(p\) are initially unrestricted in sign.

By rational linear-programming duality, rational optimal primal and dual solutions exist. Since every \(\mu_t>0\), any optimal dual solution satisfies \(z_t=h_t(p)\): otherwise decreasing \(z_t\) to \(h_t(p)\) would strictly improve its objective.

For optimal primal and dual solutions, the duality gap is

\( 0=\sum_{t,X}a_{t,X}[z_t+p\cdot q_t(X)-w_t(X)]. \)

Every summand is nonnegative. Hence positive allocation mass occurs only on utility-maximizing bundles at \(p\).

We now prove that this optimal dual price is nonnegative. There is at least one buyer type: without buyers, clearing would force every seller’s allocated sale bundle to be zero, giving \(\operatorname{OPT}=0\).

Suppose \(p_g<0\) for some good \(g\).

  • Every maximizing buyer bundle must satisfy \(X_g=M\). If \(X_g<M\), increasing that coordinate by one changes utility by \(v_t(X+e_g)-v_t(X)-p_g>0\), using weak monotonicity.
  • Every maximizing seller bundle must satisfy \(X_g=0\). If \(X_g>0\), reducing that sale coordinate by one changes utility by \(-p_g+v_t(E_t-X+e_g)-v_t(E_t-X)>0\).

The optimal primal allocation is supported on these maximizing bundles. Its aggregate purchases of good \(g\) would therefore be \(M\sum_{t\text{ buyer}}\mu_t>0\), while aggregate sales would be zero. This contradicts clearing.

Thus \(p\geq0\), and the rational optimal primal allocation witnesses that \(p\) is Walrasian.

4. Polynomial-time computation

Let \(N=\sum_t|\mathcal F_t|\). Then \(N\leq\tau(M+1)^G\).

The primal has \(N\) variables and \(\tau+G\) equality constraints; the dual has \(\tau+G\) variables and \(N\) inequalities. Their rational coefficients are obtained from the input by table lookup and subtraction. Their encoding lengths are polynomial in \(G,\tau,(M+1)^G,L\).

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore computes exact rational optimal primal and dual solutions, of polynomial output bit length, in time

\( \operatorname{poly}(G,\tau,(M+1)^G,L). \)

The preceding proof verifies that the resulting price is nonnegative and the allocation is a Walrasian witness. Use this price in both halves and half of the computed allocation in each half.

If a witness trading every good positively is desired, it can also be computed. After obtaining \(\operatorname{OPT}\), solve a second linear program maximizing \(\eta\), subject to the original allocation constraints and

\( \operatorname{GFT}(a)=\operatorname{OPT}, \qquad \sum_{t\text{ buyer},X}a_{t,X}X_g\geq\eta \quad\text{for every }g, \qquad \eta\geq0. \)

The admissibility hypothesis supplies a feasible solution with \(\eta>0\). Also \(\eta\leq M\), so this program has an attained positive optimum and the same polynomial complexity. Its allocation is optimal and, by Section 1, supported by every Walrasian price.

The exact ratio follows from proportional splitting itself; it does not require a limiting argument from the [cited finite random-halving analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/1604.06210v5).
