The continuous mirror question, as graded

For fixed \(m\), let the voter types be complete rankings \(t\in S_m\). A distribution \(\mu\) is admissible when no forbidden configuration in the paper’s enriched group-separable characterization can be realized using distinct voter instances drawn from \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\). Find a distribution maximizing \(H(\mu)=-\sum_{t\in S_m}\mu_t\ln\mu_t\), and its optimal value.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

For each integer \(m\geq1\), candidates are \([m]\), and types \(t\in S_m\) are strict complete rankings, written from best to worst. A distribution has rational masses \(\mu_t\geq0\) summing to \(1\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not \(m!\), and \(L\) is the total encoding bit length.
Distinct voter instances may have the same positive-mass type; every such type supplies arbitrarily many instances. Restrictions of rankings retain their relative order.
Call a distribution admissible if neither of the following occurs: three distinct voter instances and three distinct candidates \(x_1,x_2,x_3\), with \(x_i\) in the middle of the restricted ranking of instance \(i\) for every \(i\); or two distinct voter instances and four distinct candidates \(a,b,c,d\) whose restricted rankings form one of \((abcd,badc)\), \((abcd,bdac)\), \((acbd,badc)\), \((acbd,bdac)\). Candidate labels and voter assignments may be chosen freely.
Define \(H_m=\max_{\mu\text{ admissible}}-\sum_{t\in S_m}\mu_t\ln\mu_t\), with \(0\ln0=0\). The optimization ranges over all supports in \(S_m\); only \(m\) is input. Exact entropy values are understood symbolically.

Is exact computation of \(H_m\) polynomial-time in \(m\), or is it NP-hard? Can an admissible maximizing distribution also be constructed in time polynomial in \(m\), its output support size \(\tau\), and its output encoding length \(L\)?

For finite elections with labeled voters, an exact recurrence and closed counting formula are known for these restrictions, including the case with one designated ranking fixed ([Ferrari](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.10553)). No result settling this exact distributional entropy problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized masses to sum to one and restricted them to binary rationals, following the required society convention.
  • Interpreted distinct voter instances as distinct individuals whose types may coincide; a positive-mass type supplies arbitrarily many instances.
  • Made candidate distinctness, arbitrary candidate relabeling, arbitrary voter assignments, and all four forbidden two-voter configurations explicit.
  • Retained optimization over all rankings rather than introducing an input list of permitted types; treated fixed m as defining each instance of a family with varying m.
  • Allowed m equal to one, where the forbidden configurations are vacuous.
  • Interpreted exact entropy values symbolically and separated polynomial-time value computation from construction time measured also in output support size and encoding length.

Result

For every \(m\geq 1\),

\(H_m=(m-1)\ln 2.\)

Thus exact symbolic computation is polynomial-time in \(m\). An admissible maximizing distribution has support size \(\tau=2^{m-1}\), assigns mass \(2^{-(m-1)}\) to each supported ranking, and can be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

1. Entropy reduces to maximum support size

Let \(D\subseteq S_m\) be the support of a distribution, and let \(\tau=|D|\). Admissibility depends only on \(D\): every supported type supplies arbitrarily many voter instances, independently of its positive mass.

By concavity of the logarithm,

\( -\sum_{t\in D}\mu_t\ln\mu_t =\sum_{t\in D}\mu_t\ln(1/\mu_t) \leq \ln\!\left(\sum_{t\in D}\mu_t/\mu_t\right) =\ln\tau. \)

Because the logarithm is strictly concave and every supported mass is positive, equality holds exactly when all masses equal \(1/\tau\).

Consequently, it suffices to determine the largest admissible support and exhibit a uniform distribution on such a support.

2. Every admissible support has at most \(2^{m-1}\) rankings

A family of rankings on a candidate set \(C\) is group-separable if, for every \(C'\subseteq C\) with \(|C'|\geq2\), there is a partition \(C'=A\sqcup B\) into nonempty sets such that every ranking restricted to \(C'\) places either all of \(A\) above all of \(B\), or all of \(B\) above all of \(A\).

We use the Ballester–Haeringer characterization, stated in [Ferrari, Proposition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.10553): a finite profile of strict complete rankings is group-separable if and only if it avoids both the three-voter configuration with three different middle candidates and the two-voter configuration \((abcd,bdac)\), with candidate labels and voter assignments arbitrary.

To apply it, form a finite profile containing one voter for each type in an admissible support \(D\). This profile avoids the three-voter configuration by the first restriction in the question. It avoids \((abcd,bdac)\) because that pair is explicitly among the four forbidden configurations. Its rankings are strict and complete. Thus the characterization applies, and \(D\) is group-separable.

We now prove a support bound for every group-separable family.

Lemma. A group-separable family of distinct rankings on \(n\geq1\) candidates has size at most \(2^{n-1}\).

Proof. Proceed by induction on \(n\). For \(n=1\), only one ranking exists.

Suppose \(n\geq2\). Group-separability gives a partition of the full candidate set into nonempty sets \(A,B\), with \(|A|=a\), \(|B|=b\), and \(a+b=n\). Let \(D_A,D_B\) be the sets of distinct restrictions of the rankings to these sets. Both families are group-separable: the required partitions for their candidate subsets follow directly from the definition for the original family.

Every original ranking is uniquely determined by:

  • its restriction to \(A\);
  • its restriction to \(B\);
  • whether \(A\) precedes \(B\) or \(B\) precedes \(A\).

Indeed, the ranking is the corresponding concatenation of its two restrictions. Therefore, by the induction hypothesis,

\( |D|\leq2|D_A||D_B| \leq2\cdot2^{a-1}\cdot2^{b-1} =2^{n-1}. \)

This completes the induction. \(\square\)

Applying the lemma to an admissible support yields \(\tau\leq2^{m-1}\). Section 1 consequently gives

\(H_m\leq(m-1)\ln2.\)

3. An admissible support attaining the bound

For each subset \(S\subseteq[m-1]\), define a ranking \(r_S\) by listing:

  • the elements of \(S\) in increasing order;
  • candidate \(m\);
  • the elements of \([m-1]\setminus S\) in decreasing order.

Let \(D_m=\{r_S:S\subseteq[m-1]\}\).

The subset \(S\) can be recovered from \(r_S\) as the set of candidates preceding \(m\). Hence these rankings are distinct, and

\(|D_m|=2^{m-1}.\)

The following property proves admissibility directly.

Claim. For every nonempty candidate subset \(C\subseteq[m]\), its smallest candidate is either first or last in every restricted ranking \(r_S|_C\).

Proof. Write \(u=\min C\). If \(C=\{m\}\), the assertion is immediate. Otherwise \(u<m\).

If \(u\in S\), then it precedes every other element of \(S\cap C\), candidate \(m\) when present, and every candidate in \(C\setminus S\). Thus it is first.

If \(u\notin S\), then it follows every candidate in \(S\cap C\), candidate \(m\) when present, and every other candidate in the decreasing final block restricted to \(C\). Thus it is last. \(\square\)

For any three candidates, their smallest candidate is therefore never in the middle, in any voter instance of any supported type. The forbidden three-voter configuration requires each of the three candidates to be middle in one assigned instance, which is impossible.

For any four candidates, their smallest candidate belongs to the set of the two extreme candidates in every restricted ranking. Thus the extreme-candidate sets of any two instances intersect. But each of the four forbidden pairs has disjoint extreme-candidate sets: the first ranking, either \(abcd\) or \(acbd\), has extremes \(\{a,d\}\), whereas the second, either \(badc\) or \(bdac\), has extremes \(\{b,c\}\). None can occur.

These arguments apply to arbitrary voter instances, including repeated types. Hence \(D_m\) is admissible.

Assigning mass \(2^{-(m-1)}\) to each ranking in \(D_m\) gives rational masses summing to one and entropy

\( -\sum_{r\in D_m}2^{-(m-1)}\ln\!\left(2^{-(m-1)}\right) =(m-1)\ln2. \)

Together with the upper bound, this proves the asserted formula.

4. Exact computation and construction complexity

The entropy algorithm outputs the symbolic expression \((m-1)\ln2\). This requires polynomial time in \(m\).

To construct a maximizing distribution explicitly, enumerate the \(2^{m-1}\) subsets \(S\subseteq[m-1]\), output \(r_S\), and attach mass \(1/2^{m-1}\). Each ranking can be generated by two scans through the subset’s membership bits. Candidate labels require \(O(\log(m+1))\) bits each, and the binary denominator \(2^{m-1}\) requires \(m\) bits. The resulting construction takes \(O(\tau m\log(m+1))\) bit operations under a standard explicit encoding, and therefore is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Moreover, every maximizing distribution must have exactly \(2^{m-1}\) supported types and be uniform: a smaller support gives strictly smaller entropy, and equality in the entropy bound requires uniform masses. Thus exponential support size is necessary for an explicit maximizing distribution.
