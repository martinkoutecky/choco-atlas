Theorem 2 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given τ valuation types \(t\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), \(m\) goods with rational per-capita capacities \(q_j\), explicit XOR bids with free disposal, and report-independent nested ranges \(\mathcal{B}_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{B}_K=2^G\) represented by GROWRANGE item-partition refinements, compute at each stage \(\ell\) an exact rational welfare-maximizing assignment of type mass to integral bundles in \(\mathcal{B}_\ell\), respecting type masses and capacities; also compute the optimum after deleting each type’s mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(G\) contain \(m\geq1\) goods, with binary-rational per-capita capacities \(q_j\geq0\). Each type has an explicit finite XOR bid list \(D_t\) of pairs \((A,b)\), where \(\varnothing\neq A\subseteq G\) and \(b\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\); its free-disposal valuation is \(v_t(S)=\max(\{0\}\cup\{b:(A,b)\in D_t,\ A\subseteq S\})\), so \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\).
The input includes \(K\geq1\) report-independent partitions \(P_1,\ldots,P_K\) into nonempty parts; each successive partition refines its predecessor by splitting parts, and \(P_K\) consists of singletons. Define \(\mathcal B_\ell=\{\bigcup_{C\in H}C:H\subseteq P_\ell\}\), including the empty bundle.
At stage \(\ell\), an assignment consists of nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,S}\) for \(S\in\mathcal B_\ell\), satisfying \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\leq q_j\). Mass may split among integral bundles; welfare is \(\sum_t\sum_Sv_t(S)x_{t,S}\).
Compute a welfare-maximizing assignment and its value at every stage, and the optimum value after deleting each type's entire mass. Deletion leaves other masses, capacities and partitions unchanged, without renormalization. Any maximizing assignment is acceptable; represent it by its nonzero entries. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including all bids and partitions.

Is this exact continuous optimization problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite agents, report-independent staged maximal-in-range VCG is strategyproof under an independent stage-based interruption and eventually implements an efficient allocation; this is an incentive and eventual-efficiency result, without a polynomial runtime guarantee ([Parkes–Schoenebeck](https://schoeneb.people.si.umich.edu/papers/GrowRange.pdf)).
For the related model of combinatorial exchanges with independent partially acceptable bids, surplus maximization is polynomial-time solvable; that model does not impose the type-mass XOR constraints and staged partition ranges above ([Solving Combinatorial Exchanges](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/www/partialBids.aaai02WS.pdf)). No result settling this exact continuous problem was identified in the search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational bid values and normalized empty-bundle value to zero, following the discrete valuation domain.
  • Defined free-disposal XOR values by the maximum contained atomic bid, with zero as the default; redundant lower-valued bids do not override free disposal.
  • Made the partition sequence explicit input, with nonempty parts, successive splitting refinements and a final singleton partition.
  • Specified nonnegative rational capacities, allowing zero capacity and unused supply.
  • Required every type's mass to receive one integral bundle, allowing the empty bundle and splitting mass among bundles.
  • Interpreted deletion as removing the entire selected type's mass without renormalizing remaining masses or changing supply or ranges.
  • Allowed arbitrary optimal tie-breaking and sparse assignment output; defined total input bit length to include bids and partitions.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The problem is polynomial-time solvable, including exact maximizing assignments at every stage and all whole-type deletion values. The essential fact is that each type needs only polynomially many candidate bundles.

1. Partition closures. Let \(T\) denote the support of types, and let \(B=\sum_{t\in T}|D_t|\) be the total number of listed bids.

For a stage \(\ell\) and a set \(A\subseteq G\), define its partition closure by

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{cl}_{\ell}(A)=\bigcup_{\substack{C\in P_\ell\\C\cap A\neq\varnothing}}C.\)

This closure belongs to \(\mathcal B_\ell\) and contains \(A\). Moreover, if \(S\in\mathcal B_\ell\) contains \(A\), then \(\operatorname{cl}_\ell(A)\subseteq S\): every partition part intersecting \(A\) must be included in \(S\).

For each type, define the candidate family

\(\displaystyle F_{t,\ell}=\{\varnothing\}\cup\{\operatorname{cl}_\ell(A):(A,b)\in D_t\},\)

removing duplicates. Thus \(|F_{t,\ell}|\leq 1+|D_t|\).

Lemma. For every \(S\in\mathcal B_\ell\), there exists \(R\in F_{t,\ell}\) such that \(R\subseteq S\) and \(v_t(R)=v_t(S)\).

Proof. If \(v_t(S)=0\), take \(R=\varnothing\). Otherwise, finiteness of the bid list implies that some \((A,b)\in D_t\) satisfies \(A\subseteq S\) and \(b=v_t(S)\). Take \(R=\operatorname{cl}_\ell(A)\). The closure property gives \(A\subseteq R\subseteq S\). Consequently, the bid on \(A\) gives \(v_t(R)\geq b\), while free-disposal monotonicity gives \(v_t(R)\leq v_t(S)=b\). Hence equality holds. \(\square\)

2. Restricting to candidates preserves welfare. Fix a stage and a set \(I\subseteq T\) of remaining types. Consider any feasible assignment \(x\) for these types.

For every \(t\in I\) and \(S\in\mathcal B_\ell\), choose a bundle \(R_{t,S}\) supplied by the lemma, and define

\(\displaystyle y_{t,R}=\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal B_\ell\\R_{t,S}=R}}x_{t,S}\qquad(R\in F_{t,\ell}).\)

The transformation preserves each type’s total assigned mass. For every good \(j\), it also satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in I}\sum_{\substack{R\in F_{t,\ell}\\j\in R}}y_{t,R} =\sum_{t\in I}\sum_{S\in\mathcal B_\ell}\mathbf 1_{\{j\in R_{t,S}\}}x_{t,S} \leq\sum_{t\in I}\sum_{S\in\mathcal B_\ell}\mathbf 1_{\{j\in S\}}x_{t,S} \leq q_j.\)

The first inequality follows from \(R_{t,S}\subseteq S\). Welfare is unchanged because

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in I}\sum_{R\in F_{t,\ell}}v_t(R)y_{t,R} =\sum_{t\in I}\sum_{S\in\mathcal B_\ell}v_t(R_{t,S})x_{t,S} =\sum_{t\in I}\sum_{S\in\mathcal B_\ell}v_t(S)x_{t,S}.\)

Conversely, every feasible assignment using candidate bundles is an allowed stage assignment. Thus restricting each type to \(F_{t,\ell}\) preserves the optimum exactly.

3. The explicit linear program. Compute the actual valuation of every candidate:

\(\displaystyle c_{t,R}=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{b:(A,b)\in D_t,\ A\subseteq R\}\bigr).\)

Using the actual valuation matters: the closure of one listed bid may contain another bid with a larger value.

For stage \(\ell\) and remaining types \(I\), solve the following rational linear program:

\(\displaystyle \max\ \sum_{t\in I}\sum_{R\in F_{t,\ell}}c_{t,R}y_{t,R},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{R\in F_{t,\ell}}y_{t,R}=\mu_t\qquad(t\in I),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in I}\sum_{\substack{R\in F_{t,\ell}\\j\in R}}y_{t,R}\leq q_j\qquad(j\in G),\)

and \(y_{t,R}\geq0\).

This program is feasible: assign all remaining mass to the empty bundle. It is bounded, since every variable satisfies \(0\leq y_{t,R}\leq\mu_t\). Its feasible set is therefore compact.

It has at most \(B+\tau\) variables and at most \(\tau+m\) equality and capacity constraints. All constraint coefficients are zero or one. Every objective coefficient is zero or a bid value already present in the input.

The standard rational linear-programming theorem supplies an exact rational optimum in time polynomial in the program’s binary encoding length. It applies here because the program is explicit, rational, feasible, and bounded; it does not require a full-dimensional feasible region. See [MIT’s linear-programming algorithm notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html).

By Section 2, the resulting rational assignment is feasible for the original problem and dominates every original feasible assignment. It is consequently an exact maximizing assignment for that stage.

4. Construction cost and output size. Represent bundles by their membership vectors on \(G\). Each closure is computable by scanning the partition. Each candidate valuation is computable by testing containment against the type’s explicit bid list.

Across all stages, these operations require at most a polynomial number of scans and rational comparisons; for example, candidate valuation evaluation uses at most \(O(KmB^2)\) elementary containment work. Comparisons of bid values have polynomial bit complexity. Constructing the constraint matrices also takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,L\).

For completeness, exact optimal assignments have polynomial binary descriptions independently of any numerical approximation argument. Introduce one nonnegative slack variable for each capacity constraint. The resulting equality system has \(r=|I|+m\) rows and a zero-one coefficient matrix. Its rows are independent: the slack columns isolate the capacity rows, and each type’s empty-bundle column then isolates its mass row.

At a vertex, positive-variable columns must be linearly independent. Otherwise, a nonzero dependence permits sufficiently small perturbations in both directions while preserving equality constraints and nonnegativity, contradicting extremality. Thus a vertex has at most \(r\) positive variables, including slacks.

For a basis matrix \(M\), its basic coordinates solve \(Mz=h\), where \(h\) contains the remaining masses and unchanged capacities. Choose a common denominator \(Q\) for the entries of \(h\). The bit length of \(Q\) is at most the total denominator encoding length in the input. Cramer’s rule gives

\(\displaystyle z_i=\frac{\det M_i(Qh)}{Q\det M}.\)

Hadamard’s inequality bounds the zero-one determinant by \(|\det M|\leq r^{r/2}\), and bounds the numerator determinant by \(r^{r/2}\max_k|(Qh)_k|\). Hence every vertex coordinate has bit length polynomial in \(r\) and \(L\).

An optimal vertex can also be found in polynomial time: after computing the optimum value, successively maximize each coordinate over the current optimal face and fix its attained value. After at most \(B+\tau\) additional LPs the face is a singleton, hence a vertex. Each intermediate face has vertices among the original vertices, so the newly fixed values retain the polynomial bit bounds just proved.

Consequently, one may return at most \(m+|I|\) nonzero assignment entries. Each entry consists of a type, an \(m\)-bit bundle, and a rational mass of polynomial bit length. The exact welfare value likewise has polynomial bit length.

5. All stages and whole-type deletions. For every stage \(\ell\), solve the program with \(I=T\), and output its maximizing assignment and value.

For every deleted type \(u\in T\), solve the same stage program with \(I=T\setminus\{u\}\). The other masses remain \(\mu_t\); the capacities, partitions, and candidate families for surviving types remain unchanged. This implements deletion without renormalization. If no types remain, the value is zero.

There are \(K(\tau+1)\) such optimization problems, each of polynomial encoding length. Their total exact running time and output length are therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\).

The refinement condition is compatible with this algorithm but is not needed for tractability: the proof works separately for every supplied partition.
