The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\), per-capita supplies \(\nu^1_a,\nu^2_b\), utilities \(u_{tab}\), and accuracy \(\epsilon>0\), with feasible positive utility for each positive-mass type, can one compute a feasible allocation \(x_{tab}\ge 0\) within additive error \(\epsilon\) of the maximum of \(\sum_t \mu_t\log U_t\), where \(U_t=\frac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_{a,b}u_{tab}x_{tab}\), subject to \(\sum_{a,b}x_{tab}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}x_{tab}=\nu^1_a\), and \(\sum_{t,a}x_{tab}=\nu^2_b\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are two nonempty finite sets of goods \(G_1,G_2\), with \(m=|G_1|+|G_2|\). Their positive binary-rational per-capita supplies satisfy \(\sum_a\nu^1_a=\sum_b\nu^2_b=1\).
The explicitly listed utilities \(u_{tab}\) are nonnegative binary rationals: a type-\(t\) agent receives utility \(u_{tab}\) from the pair \((a,b)\). Disagreement utilities are zero.
An allocation consists of divisible masses \(x_{tab}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{a,b}x_{tab}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}x_{tab}=\nu^1_a\), and \(\sum_{t,a}x_{tab}=\nu^2_b\). Define \(U_t(x)=\mu_t^{-1}\sum_{a,b}u_{tab}x_{tab}\).
Assume one feasible allocation has \(U_t>0\) for every type. Let \(F(x)=\sum_t\mu_t\log U_t(x)\), using natural logarithms and setting \(F(x)=-\infty\) if any utility is zero.
Given binary-rational \(0<\epsilon<1\), the computational problem is to produce a rational feasible allocation satisfying \(F(x)\geq\max_y F(y)-\epsilon\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is this problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely listed agents, fixed positive entropy regularization admits a generalized Frank–Wolfe algorithm with linear convergence in iterations; the unregularized two-good fractional model also has a stated polynomial-time additive-accuracy algorithm. [Hosseini–Vazirani](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.10704v4).
No result addressing exactly the rational-mass formulation above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized society mass to one and required each goods marginal to sum to one, reflecting one good from each set per capita.
  • Specified explicitly listed nonnegative binary-rational utilities and zero disagreement utilities, as in the linear Fisher two-good model.
  • Required nonempty goods sets and positive supplies; goods with zero supply can be discarded.
  • Interpreted positive-utility feasibility as simultaneous positivity for all supported types in one feasible allocation.
  • Allowed the two goods sets to have independently specified cardinalities and defined m as their total size.
  • Specified natural logarithms, rational allocation output, binary-rational accuracy between zero and one, and polynomial dependence on accuracy bit length.
  • Retained the fractional marginal constraints without imposing implementability as a lottery over integral three-way matchings.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\). The algorithm returns a rational allocation satisfying every marginal equality exactly.

The main issues are the logarithmic singularity at zero utility, the possible smallness of the rational masses, and the requirement of exact feasibility. The proof below addresses each of them.

1. A positive rational reference allocation

Let \(N=\tau|G_1||G_2|\), and let \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) be the allocation polytope defined in the question. Thus \(N\leq\tau m^2\).

Define
\[ \]
\(x^0_{tab}=\mu_t\nu^1_a\nu^2_b,\qquad \beta_t=U_t(x^0)=\sum_{a,b}\nu^1_a\nu^2_bu_{tab}.\)

The prescribed sums of the masses and supplies show directly that \(x^0\in P\). Every coordinate of \(x^0\) is strictly positive.

The positive-utility promise implies that every type has at least one strictly positive utility entry. Since all supplies are strictly positive, it follows that \(\beta_t>0\) for every type. These numbers and \(x^0\) are rational and computable with polynomial encoding length.

Put \(V=\max_{t,a,b}u_{tab}>0\). Every feasible allocation satisfies \(U_t(x)\leq V\).

An optimum \(x^\star\) exists and has strictly positive utilities. Indeed, take a maximizing sequence whose objective values are at least \(F(x^0)\), and extract a convergent subsequence using compactness of \(P\). If any utility at the limit were zero, its positive-weight logarithmic term would tend to negative infinity, while all other terms are bounded above by their corresponding weights times \(\log V\). This contradicts the lower bound \(F(x^0)\). Thus the limit has positive utilities, and continuity there proves attainment.

2. A rational lower bound on optimal utilities

Lemma. Every optimum satisfies \(U_t(x^\star)\geq\mu_t\beta_t\) for every type.

Proof. The allocation \((1-s)x^\star+sx^0\) is feasible for \(0\leq s\leq1\). Since its objective is maximized at \(s=0\), its right derivative there is nonpositive. Hence

\(\displaystyle 0\geq \sum_t\mu_t\frac{\beta_t-U_t(x^\star)}{U_t(x^\star)} = \sum_t\mu_t\frac{\beta_t}{U_t(x^\star)}-1. \)

Every summand in the last sum is nonnegative. Consequently each is at most one, which gives the claimed inequality. \(\square\)

Define \(\alpha_t=\mu_t\beta_t/2\), and introduce the rational polytope

\(\displaystyle P_\alpha=\{x\in P:U_t(x)\geq\alpha_t\text{ for every }t\}. \)

The lemma shows that \(P_\alpha\) contains an optimum. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \max_{x\in P_\alpha}F(x)=\max_{x\in P}F(x).\)

Furthermore, \(x^0\) satisfies every added utility inequality strictly, because

\(\displaystyle \beta_t-\alpha_t =\beta_t(1-\mu_t/2) \geq\beta_t/2 \geq\alpha_t. \)

Thus restricting to \(P_\alpha\) removes the logarithmic singularity without changing the optimum. All its defining coefficients have polynomial encoding length.

3. Exact coordinates, an interior ball, and a Lipschitz bound

Write the marginal equalities as \(Ax=b\). Rational Gaussian elimination gives a parametrization

\(\displaystyle x=x^0+Bz,\qquad z\in\mathbb R^d,\)

where the columns of \(B\) form a basis for \(\ker A\). Choose the free-variable parametrization, so that the rows of \(B\) corresponding to free coordinates form an identity matrix.

This computation has polynomial bit complexity. In particular, \(A\) has entries zero or one; its minors have polynomial encoding length by the determinant bound, and the elimination coefficients are ratios of such minors.

If \(d=0\), \(x^0\) is the unique feasible allocation, so returning it solves the problem. Assume henceforth that \(d\geq1\).

Let

\(\displaystyle K=\{z:x^0+Bz\in P_\alpha\},\qquad f(z)=F(x^0+Bz).\)

Every feasible coordinate \(x_i\) lies in \([0,1]\). Since each free coordinate satisfies \(z_j=x_i-x^0_i\), we have \(K\subseteq[-1,1]^d\). In particular, \(K\) lies in the Euclidean ball of radius \(R=N\) centered at zero.

We next give an explicit interior radius. Set

\(\displaystyle p=\min_i x^0_i,\quad \alpha=\min_t\alpha_t,\quad \mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t,\quad H=\max\{1,\sum_{i,j}|B_{ij}|\},\quad C=\frac{NV}{\mu_{\min}}. \)

Then \(\|B\|_2\leq H\), and the coefficient vector of each linear utility function \(U_t\) has Euclidean norm at most \(C\). Define

\(\displaystyle r=\min\left\{1,\frac{p}{2H},\frac{\alpha}{2CH}\right\}>0. \)

For \(\|z\|_2\leq r\), every coordinate of \(x^0+Bz\) is at least \(p-Hr\geq p/2\). Also,

\(\displaystyle |U_t(x^0+Bz)-\beta_t| \leq CHr\leq\alpha/2. \)

The utility slack at \(x^0\) is at least \(\alpha_t\geq\alpha\), so all utility inequalities remain satisfied. The marginal equalities hold identically. Therefore the closed ball of radius \(r\) centered at zero is contained in \(K\).

On \(P_\alpha\), the allocation-coordinate derivatives are

\(\displaystyle \frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{tab}}(x)=\frac{u_{tab}}{U_t(x)}. \)

Their absolute values are at most \(V/\alpha\). Consequently

\(\displaystyle \|\nabla f(z)\|_2 \leq\frac{HNV}{\alpha}\leq M, \qquad M=\max\left\{1,\frac{HNV}{\alpha}\right\}. \)

Integrating along line segments in the convex set \(K\) proves that \(f\) is \(M\)-Lipschitz on \(K\).

All the constants just constructed have polynomial encoding length. Thus the potentially small interior radius and large Lipschitz constant cause only polynomial logarithmic contributions to the runtime.

4. Rational objective and gradient oracles

Membership in \(K\) is checked by exact rational comparisons against its explicitly listed linear inequalities.

At a rational point \(z\in K\), the gradient \(\nabla f(z)\) is exactly rational and computable in polynomial time from the derivative formula above.

The value \(f(z)\) can be approximated to any rational absolute error \(h>0\) in time polynomial in the encoding length of \(z\), the input length, and \(\log(1/h)\). Here is an explicit justification.

For a positive rational \(s\), write \(s=2^kv\), where \(1\leq v<2\). With \(w=(v-1)/(v+1)\), we have \(0\leq w<1/3\) and

\(\displaystyle \log v=2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{2j+1}}{2j+1}. \)

The tail after index \(J\) has absolute value at most

\(\displaystyle \frac94\,3^{-(2J+3)}.\)

The same expansion evaluates \(\log2\), using \(w=1/3\). Thus evaluating \(\log s=k\log2+\log v\) requires only polynomially many rational operations for the requested precision, with polynomial intermediate encoding length.

Approximating every \(\log U_t\) to error \(h\) approximates their weighted sum to error at most \(h\), since \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).

5. Polynomial-time optimization with exact feasibility

We use the following standard rational ellipsoid fact.

Rational cut-or-stop ellipsoid theorem. Suppose a closed convex target set \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) lies in a known ball of rational radius \(R\). At each rational ellipsoid center, an oracle either stops or supplies a nonzero rational central cut containing \(Q\). For any rational volume threshold \(\eta>0\), the rational ellipsoid algorithm either obtains a stop or produces an enclosing ellipsoid of volume at most \(\eta\). Its runtime is polynomial in the dimension, the encoding lengths of \(R,\eta\), and the oracle complexity. The rational rounding needed for this conclusion is part of the theorem. This is the central-cut specialization of the shallow-cut ellipsoid theorem, Theorem 3.3.3 in [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1988.pdf).

We verify how this theorem gives our required guarantee.

Let \(f^\star=\max_K f\). For a rational threshold \(q\), consider the closed convex target set

\(\displaystyle Q_q=\{z\in K:f(z)\geq q\}.\)

Fix \(\delta=\epsilon/8\) and \(h=\epsilon/32\). At a rational ellipsoid center \(c\), use this oracle:

  • If \(c\notin K\), a violated linear inequality gives a rational central cut containing \(K\), hence containing \(Q_q\).
  • Otherwise compute \(\widehat f(c)\) with error at most \(h\). If \(\widehat f(c)\geq q-h\), stop and return \(x^0+Bc\). This allocation is exactly feasible and satisfies \(F(x^0+Bc)\geq q-2h\).
  • If \(\widehat f(c)<q-h\), then \(f(c)<q\). Concavity gives

\(\displaystyle f(z)\leq f(c)+\nabla f(c)^{\mathsf T}(z-c).\)

Therefore, when \(\nabla f(c)\neq0\), the rational central halfspace

\(\displaystyle \nabla f(c)^{\mathsf T}(z-c)\geq0\)

contains \(Q_q\). If the gradient is zero, the same concavity inequality proves \(Q_q\) empty, so we report failure.

It remains to bound the volume of \(Q_q\) when \(q\) is sufficiently below optimum.

Let \(z^\star\) maximize \(f\), and set

\(\displaystyle \theta=\frac{\delta}{2M(R+1)},\qquad \rho=\theta r. \)

Because \(K\) contains the ball of radius \(r\) centered at zero, convexity implies that \(K\) contains the ball of radius \(\rho\) centered at \((1-\theta)z^\star\). Every point \(z\) in this ball satisfies

\(\displaystyle \|z-z^\star\|_2 \leq\theta(R+r) \leq\frac{\delta}{2M}. \)

The Lipschitz bound consequently gives \(f(z)\geq f^\star-\delta/2\). In particular, if \(q\leq f^\star-\delta\), this entire ball lies in \(Q_q\).

That ball contains a cube of side length \(2\rho/d\), so its volume is at least \((2\rho/d)^d\). Run the ellipsoid theorem with

\(\displaystyle \eta=(\rho/d)^d.\)

An enclosing ellipsoid of volume at most \(\eta\) is then impossible when \(q\leq f^\star-\delta\). Hence the threshold procedure has these guarantees:

  • Success: it returns an exactly feasible rational allocation with objective at least \(q-2h\).
  • Failure: necessarily \(f^\star<q+\delta\).

The encoding length of \(\eta\) is polynomial, so each threshold procedure takes polynomial time.

To finish, choose an integer \(T\geq1\) with polynomial encoding length such that \(2^{-T}\leq\alpha\) and \(V\leq2^T\). These inequalities are obtained directly from the rational encodings. For every \(z\in K\),

\(\displaystyle -T\leq f(z)\leq T.\)

Maintain bounds \(\ell\leq f^\star\leq u\), together with a rational allocation of objective at least \(\ell\). Initially use \(\ell=-T\), \(u=T\), and \(x^0\).

Test \(q=(\ell+u)/2\). On success, replace \(\ell\) by \(\max\{\ell,q-2h\}\), retaining an allocation witnessing the new lower bound. On failure, replace \(u\) by \(\min\{u,q+\delta\}\).

The guarantees above preserve the invariant. If the previous interval width was \(w\), the new width is at most \(w/2+\delta\), because \(2h\leq\delta\). After \(k\) tests it is therefore at most

\(\displaystyle \frac{2T}{2^k}+2\delta.\)

Taking \(k\) so that \(2T/2^k\leq\epsilon/4\) leaves width at most \(\epsilon/2\). The retained allocation \(x\) then satisfies

\(\displaystyle F(x)\geq\ell\geq f^\star-\epsilon/2\geq f^\star-\epsilon.\)

6. Complexity conclusion

There are \(N\leq\tau m^2\) allocation variables. All preprocessing, oracle computations, ellipsoid parameters, and rational outputs have encoding length polynomial in \(N,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\). The number of threshold tests and the runtime of each test have the same polynomial bound.

The parametrization \(x=x^0+Bz\) enforces every marginal equality exactly, and allocations are returned only after exact verification of all linear inequalities.

Thus the specified rational-mass problem is polynomial-time solvable with the requested additive accuracy.
