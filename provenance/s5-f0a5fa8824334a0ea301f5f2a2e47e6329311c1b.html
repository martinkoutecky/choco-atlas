The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a candidate set \(C\), \(p\in C\), \(k\in\mathbb{N}\), and a finite support \(T\) of complete rankings of \(C\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), decide whether there is a set \(X\subseteq C\setminus\{p\}\), \(|X|\le k\), such that \(p\) is a nonunique Schulze winner on \(C\setminus X\). Compute pairwise margins as \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t(\mathbf{1}[a\succ_t b]-\mathbf{1}[b\succ_t a])\), using each ranking restricted to the remaining slate.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) distinct strict complete rankings of a nonempty candidate set \(C\), with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(m=|C|\), \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings, and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Given \(p\in C\) and a binary-encoded integer \(k\geq 0\), one may delete a set \(X\subseteq C\setminus\{p\}\) with \(|X|\leq k\); rankings are restricted to the remaining slate \(S=C\setminus X\).
For distinct \(a,b\in S\), set \(w(a,b)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t(\mathbf{1}[a\succ_t b]-\mathbf{1}[b\succ_t a])\). Use the complete directed graph on \(S\) with these weights, including zero and negative weights.
Let \(B_S(a,b)\) be the maximum, over simple directed paths from \(a\) to \(b\), of the minimum edge weight on the path. Candidate \(p\) is a Schulze winner if \(B_S(p,a)\geq B_S(a,p)\) for every \(a\in S\setminus\{p\}\); ties are accepted, and a singleton slate has its sole candidate as winner.

Is deciding whether an allowed deletion makes \(p\) a Schulze winner solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\) and \(\log(k+1)\), or is it NP-hard?

For explicitly listed unweighted voters, constructive control by deleting candidates in the nonunique-winner model was established as NP-hard by [Menton and Singh](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.2111); a later corrected proof establishes NP-completeness [Maushagen et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0326.pdf).
No result for exactly the normalized rational-support problem above was verified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted complete rankings as strict linear orders, consistent with the discrete setting.
  • Specified the margins-based Schulze variant using all directed edges, including zero and negative weights.
  • Made the nonunique-winner convention explicit: being any winner suffices, including being the sole winner.
  • Allowed singleton remaining slates and zero deletion budgets; encoded the budget in binary and defined L as total input bit length.
  • Retained the prohibition on deleting p, consistent with the intended constructive goal despite its omission from the original displayed discrete definition.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when every mass is a dyadic rational. Hence a polynomial-time algorithm in the specified input parameters exists if and only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

When \(k\geq m-1\), deleting every candidate except \(p\) gives an immediate yes-instance. The hardness concerns unrestricted instances with smaller deletion budgets.

1. The previously established hardness result applies to the required rule

We use the following established theorem: constructive control by deleting candidates for Schulze elections with explicitly listed unweighted voters is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete in the nonunique-winner model. This is Theorem 1 of [Maushagen et al., *Toward Completing the Picture of Control in Schulze and Ranked Pairs Elections*](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0326.pdf).

The conventions agree with the present task. Their Section 2 defines the graph using every ordered pair of distinct candidates, weighted by the integer pairwise margin; negative edges are omitted only from illustrations. Their winner condition compares maximum bottleneck path strengths using a weak inequality, so ties are accepted. Their voters provide strict complete rankings. [Source: Section 2 and Theorem 1](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0326.pdf).

If paths were interpreted as allowing repeated vertices, this would not change their maximum bottleneck strength between distinct endpoints. Removing a segment between two occurrences of the same vertex removes edges and therefore cannot decrease the minimum remaining edge weight. Repeated removal produces a simple path.

We now give a polynomial-time reduction from that established problem to the exact support representation in the question.

2. Reduction to distinct rankings with normalized dyadic masses

Let the source instance have candidate set \(C\), distinguished candidate \(p\), deletion budget \(k\), and an explicitly listed profile \(V\) of \(n\geq 1\) voters. Write its integer pairwise margins as

\(D_V(a,b)=\sum_{v\in V}\bigl(\mathbf{1}[a\succ_v b]-\mathbf{1}[b\succ_v a]\bigr).\)

We retain \(C\), \(p\), and \(k\).

First duplicate every voter. The resulting profile has \(2n\) voters and margins \(2D_V(a,b)\).

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\geq 2n\). Thus

\(2n\leq N<4n.\)

Choose any strict complete ranking \(r\) of \(C\), and let \(r^{\mathrm{rev}}\) be its reverse. Add \((N-2n)/2\) copies of the pair consisting of \(r\) and \(r^{\mathrm{rev}}\). This number is a nonnegative integer because both \(N\) and \(2n\) are even.

Each added pair contributes zero to every pairwise margin: for distinct \(a,b\), exactly one of its two rankings places \(a\) above \(b\). Consequently the padded profile \(V^\star\) has exactly \(N\) voters and satisfies

\(D_{V^\star}(a,b)=2D_V(a,b).\)

Let \(T\) be the set of distinct rankings occurring in \(V^\star\). For each \(t\in T\), let \(f_t\) be its positive multiplicity, and assign

\(\mu_t=f_t/N.\)

These masses are positive, sum to one, and are dyadic rationals because \(N\) is a power of two. Moreover, the rankings in \(T\) are distinct, as required.

The resulting society has margins

\(w(a,b)=\sum_{t\in T}\frac{f_t}{N}\bigl(\mathbf{1}[a\succ_t b]-\mathbf{1}[b\succ_t a]\bigr)=\frac{2}{N}D_V(a,b).\)

This construction has polynomial size. Indeed, \(\tau=|T|\leq N<4n\), and each mass requires \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits. With candidates indexed by integers, the output length is

\(O\bigl(nm\log(m+1)+n\log(n+1)+\log(k+1)+1\bigr).\)

All rankings can be grouped and their multiplicities counted in polynomial time. Crucially, this constructs only occurring rankings; it never enumerates the set of all possible rankings.

If empty voter profiles are permitted in the source problem, they have every margin equal to zero and are always yes-instances. They can be mapped separately to the singleton society with mass one.

3. Every allowed deletion preserves the answer

Fix any deletion set \(X\subseteq C\setminus\{p\}\), and let \(S=C\setminus X\).

Restricting a ranking does not change its comparison of two surviving candidates. Thus, for every distinct \(a,b\in S\), the margin identity remains

\(w(a,b)=\lambda D_V(a,b),\qquad \lambda=2/N>0.\)

Let \(B^V_S(a,b)\) denote the source election’s maximum bottleneck strength on the surviving slate. For every simple directed path \(P\) from \(a\) to \(b\),

\(\min_{e\in P}w(e)=\lambda\min_{e\in P}D_V(e).\)

This identity holds for positive, zero, and negative edge weights. Taking the maximum over the same finite collection of simple paths gives

\(B_S(a,b)=\lambda B^V_S(a,b).\)

Because \(\lambda>0\), for every \(a\in S\setminus\{p\}\),

\(B_S(p,a)\geq B_S(a,p)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ B^V_S(p,a)\geq B^V_S(a,p).\)

Therefore \(p\) is a winner after deleting \(X\) in the constructed society exactly when it is a winner after deleting \(X\) in the source election. Singleton slates also agree by definition.

The candidate set and deletion budget are unchanged, so this equivalence holds for precisely the same allowed deletion sets. The construction is consequently a polynomial-time many-one reduction, proving \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

4. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate is a subset \(X\subseteq C\setminus\{p\}\), encoded by at most \(m\) bits. We verify \(|X|\leq k\) and then verify the winner condition exactly.

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), with positive integers encoded in binary. Set

\(Q=\prod_{t\in T}b_t.\)

The bit length of \(Q\) is at most the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Compute the integer edge weights

\(A(a,b)=Qw(a,b)=\sum_{t\in T}a_t(Q/b_t)\bigl(\mathbf{1}[a\succ_t b]-\mathbf{1}[b\succ_t a]\bigr).\)

These computations take polynomial time. Since the masses sum to one, \(|w(a,b)|\leq 1\), so \(|A(a,b)|\leq Q\); the resulting weights have \(O(L)\) bits.

For completeness, maximum bottleneck strengths can be computed using threshold reachability. For each distinct surviving edge weight \(q\), form the directed graph containing exactly the edges with \(A(a,b)\geq q\). For distinct surviving candidates \(a,b\),

\(a\text{ reaches }b\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{there is a simple path from }a\text{ to }b\text{ with bottleneck at least }q.\)

The forward implication follows by removing repeated-vertex segments from a witnessing walk; the reverse implication follows directly from the graph definition.

Every simple path’s bottleneck is one of its edge weights. Consequently, the greatest threshold permitting reachability is exactly its maximum bottleneck strength. There are at most \(m(m-1)\) thresholds. Searches from \(p\) in each threshold graph and its reverse compute all strengths needed for the winner test in polynomial time, including when weights are negative.

Finally, dividing every integer strength by the positive number \(Q\) preserves all winner comparisons. A singleton slate is accepted immediately. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Thus the normalized rational-support problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.
