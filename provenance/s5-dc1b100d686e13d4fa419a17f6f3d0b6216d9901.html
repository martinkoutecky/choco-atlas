The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), nonnegative score rates \(a_{ut}\), quota groups \(g:T\to G\), and per-capita capacities \(\kappa_g\), compute a quota-feasible selected mass vector \(x\) maximizing \(\sum_{t\in T}x_t r_t\), where \(r_t=\sum_{u\in T}\mu_u a_{ut}\), subject to \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t:g(t)=g}x_t\le\kappa_g\). Can an anonymous randomized mechanism realize this optimum while each individual's inclusion probability remains unchanged when that individual's outgoing score row changes?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Represent it by an atomless probability space \((A,\lambda)\), partitioned into measurable classes \(A_t\) with \(\lambda(A_t)=\mu_t\). Public data specify \(g:T\to G\), \(m=|G|\), and binary rational capacities \(\kappa_g\ge0\); scores \(a_{ut}\ge0\) are binary rationals.
Each individual in \(A_u\) assigns score rate \(a_{ut}\) to each distinct individual in \(A_t\), and assigns zero to itself; \(a_{tt}\) may be positive. Let \(r_t=\sum_u\mu_u a_{ut}\).
Define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max\{\sum_t x_t r_t:0\le x_t\le\mu_t,\ \sum_{t:g(t)=g}x_t\le\kappa_g\text{ for every }g\}\).
A randomized mechanism chooses a measurable set \(S\subseteq A\), satisfying \(\sum_{t:g(t)=g}\lambda(S\cap A_t)\le\kappa_g\) almost surely, with expected score \(\mathbb E[\sum_t r_t\lambda(S\cap A_t)]\).
Anonymity means that individual inclusion probabilities are equivariant under measure-preserving relabelings of individuals and their public attributes and score profiles. Impartiality requires every individual's inclusion probability to remain unchanged after changing only that individual's outgoing score row, with its public class, incoming scores, and quota group fixed.
Mechanisms must also be defined on profiles obtained by such singleton deviations, although these deviations have zero mass. For \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), a mechanism is \(\alpha\)-optimal if its expected score is at least \(\alpha\mathrm{OPT}\) whenever \(\mathrm{OPT}>0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; computational running times refer to computing a finite description of the selection rule in \(m,\tau,L\).

Can an anonymous impartial randomized mechanism, computable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), guarantee approximation ratio \(\tfrac14\) on every instance, or is this guarantee impossible?
Can such a mechanism guarantee ratio \(1\), realizing the optimal selected mass vector in expectation, or is some approximation loss unavoidable?

For finite agents, an impartial randomized mechanism guarantees ratio \(\tfrac14\) under arbitrary independence-system constraints, including partition quotas; this is an approximation result, without a general polynomial-time claim ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20477v1)).
No result settling this atomless quota problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total mass to one, so capacities are fractions of the society; allowed nonnegative rational capacities, including zero.
  • Interpreted positive type masses as atomless classes of individuals, rather than as indivisible strategic agents.
  • Retained zero self-scores at the individual level while allowing positive within-type score rates between distinct individuals.
  • Required all score rates and capacities to be binary rationals and defined L as total input bit length; used m for the number of quota groups.
  • Defined individual deviations on singleton rows while fixing public classes, incoming scores, and quota groups; required the mechanism to extend to these zero-mass deviations.
  • Defined anonymity through equivariance of individual inclusion probabilities under relabeling, without requiring invariance of the entire joint lottery.
  • Required quota feasibility almost surely and interpreted realization of an optimal mass vector as realization in expectation.
  • Allowed arbitrary optimal tie choices subject to anonymity and impartiality; no label-based tie-breaking order was imposed.

Conclusion

Both guarantees are achievable. There is an anonymous, impartial randomized mechanism, computable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), whose expected score equals \(\mathrm{OPT}\). Consequently, it guarantees both ratio \(1\) and ratio \(\tfrac14\).

The essential fact is that a singleton deviation changes no integrated incoming score. The construction below explicitly defines inclusion probabilities for deviating individuals, even when their modified outgoing profiles have zero mass.

1. Integrated scores are invariant under singleton deviations

For a profile \(P\), write \(w_P(i,j)\) for the score rate assigned by individual \(i\) to individual \(j\), and define

\(R_P(j)=\int_A w_P(i,j)\,d\lambda(i).\)

On a given finite-support instance, every \(j\in A_t\) satisfies \(R_P(j)=r_t\). Indeed, the column for \(j\) has value \(a_{ut}\) on \(A_u\), except that its value at \(i=j\) is zero. Since that singleton has measure zero,

\(R_P(j)=\sum_u\mu_u a_{ut}=r_t.\)

Now let \(P'\) differ from \(P\) only in the outgoing row of one individual \(i_0\). For every fixed recipient \(j\), the functions \(w_P(\,\cdot\,,j)\) and \(w_{P'}(\,\cdot\,,j)\) differ only on the null singleton \(\{i_0\}\). Their integrals therefore agree:

\(R_{P'}(j)=R_P(j)\qquad\text{for every }j\in A.\)

This is a pointwise statement about all recipients, including \(i_0\). It holds even if the deviating row is unbounded, provided it is an admissible measurable row of nonnegative scores: its contribution is still supported on a null set.

We will use only the quota group and integrated incoming score to determine each individual's inclusion probability.

2. Compute an optimal, symmetric mass allocation

Fix a quota group \(g\) with positive total mass. List its distinct integrated score levels in decreasing order:

\(s_{g,1}>s_{g,2}>\cdots>s_{g,q_g}\geq 0.\)

Define their strata, masses, and cumulative masses by

\(B_{g,j}=\{i\in A:g(i)=g,\ R_P(i)=s_{g,j}\},\)

\(M_{g,j}=\lambda(B_{g,j}),\qquad C_{g,j}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}M_{g,\ell},\qquad C_{g,0}=0.\)

Every listed mass \(M_{g,j}\) is positive. Let

\(K_g=\min\{\kappa_g,C_{g,q_g}\}.\)

Allocate mass

\(z_{g,j}=\min\{K_g,C_{g,j}\}-\min\{K_g,C_{g,j-1}\}\)

to stratum \(B_{g,j}\), and give each individual in that stratum inclusion probability

\(p_{g,j}=\dfrac{z_{g,j}}{M_{g,j}}.\)

Because \(0\leq z_{g,j}\leq M_{g,j}\), these are valid probabilities. Their allocated masses sum to \(K_g\). All score levels above a possible cutoff have probability \(1\), all levels below it have probability \(0\), and at most one level has probability strictly between \(0\) and \(1\).

For each original type \(t\), set

\(x_t^*=\mu_t p_{g(t),j(t)},\)

where \(j(t)\) is its score level within its group. Thus types tied in score within the same group receive the same inclusion probability.

Proof of optimality

Let \(y_t\) be any feasible mass vector. In group \(g\), aggregate its selected masses by score level:

\(y_{g,j}=\sum_{t:g(t)=g,\ r_t=s_{g,j}}y_t,\qquad Y_{g,j}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}y_{g,\ell}.\)

The individual upper bounds and the group quota imply

\(Y_{g,j}\leq C_{g,j},\qquad Y_{g,j}\leq\kappa_g.\)

Consequently,

\(Y_{g,j}\leq\min\{K_g,C_{g,j}\}.\)

Set \(s_{g,q_g+1}=0\). Telescoping gives

\(\sum_{j=1}^{q_g}s_{g,j}y_{g,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{q_g}(s_{g,j}-s_{g,j+1})Y_{g,j}.\)

Every coefficient \(s_{g,j}-s_{g,j+1}\) is nonnegative. The cumulative allocated masses satisfy

\(\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}z_{g,\ell}=\min\{K_g,C_{g,j}\}.\)

Substituting the cumulative upper bounds into the telescoping identity proves

\(\sum_j s_{g,j}y_{g,j}\leq\sum_j s_{g,j}z_{g,j}.\)

Summing over groups proves that \(x^*\) is feasible and optimal:

\(\sum_t r_t x_t^*=\mathrm{OPT}.\)

Empty groups contribute nothing.

3. Realize the probabilities by feasible measurable sets

We need a lottery that selects exactly the prescribed mass from each stratum while giving every individual there the same inclusion probability.

Lemma. If \(B\) is a measurable set of positive measure in an atomless finite measure space, there exists a measurable function \(h_B:B\to[0,1)\) whose distribution under normalized measure on \(B\) is uniform.

Proof. First, atomlessness implies that every positive-measure set contains a subset of arbitrarily small positive measure: repeatedly split a positive-measure set into two positive-measure parts and retain a smaller part.

This implies divisibility. To obtain a subset of \(B\) of prescribed measure \(v\), where \(0<v<\lambda(B)\), start with \(F_0=\varnothing\). Whenever \(\lambda(F_n)<v\), let

\(\sigma_n=\sup\{\lambda(D):D\subseteq B\setminus F_n\text{ measurable},\ \lambda(D)\leq v-\lambda(F_n)\}.\)

The small-subset property gives \(\sigma_n>0\). Choose such a subset \(D_n\) with \(\lambda(D_n)>\sigma_n/2\), and set \(F_{n+1}=F_n\cup D_n\).

If \(F=\bigcup_nF_n\) had measure strictly below \(v\), the remaining set \(B\setminus F\) would contain a fixed subset \(D\) with

\(0<\lambda(D)\leq v-\lambda(F).\)

This \(D\) would be admissible in every supremum above. Hence every increment would have measure greater than \(\lambda(D)/2\), contradicting \(\lambda(F_n)\leq v\). Thus \(\lambda(F)=v\). The cases \(v=0\) and \(v=\lambda(B)\) are immediate.

Now recursively partition \(B\) into halves, then quarters, and so forth, using divisibility. Encode each individual's successive binary partition memberships as a binary expansion. The resulting measurable function has uniform distribution because every binary cylinder of length \(n\) has normalized measure \(2^{-n}\). Binary-expansion ambiguities form a null set; map the possible value \(1\) to \(0\). This yields the required \(h_B\). \(\square\)

For every stratum \(B_{g,j}\), fix such a coordinate \(h_{g,j}\). Draw independent uniform random variables \(U_{g,j}\in[0,1)\), and select

\(S_{g,j}=\{i\in B_{g,j}:(h_{g,j}(i)-U_{g,j})\bmod 1<p_{g,j}\}.\)

Set \(S=\bigcup_{g,j}S_{g,j}\). This is a measurable set.

For every fixed realization of the random variables, the selected coordinate interval has length \(p_{g,j}\). Uniformity of \(h_{g,j}\) therefore gives

\(\lambda(S_{g,j})=M_{g,j}p_{g,j}=z_{g,j}.\)

Thus every outcome satisfies the quotas:

\(\lambda(S\cap\{i:g(i)=g\})=\sum_jz_{g,j}=K_g\leq\kappa_g.\)

For every fixed individual \(i\in B_{g,j}\), uniform rotation gives

\(\Pr(i\in S)=p_{g,j}.\)

This equality holds for every individual, including any exceptional point of measure zero, regardless of its coordinate value.

The membership indicator is jointly measurable in the individual and the random seeds. Tonelli's theorem consequently yields

\(\mathbb E[\lambda(S\cap A_t)]=\int_{A_t}\Pr(i\in S)\,d\lambda(i)=\mu_t p_{g(t),j(t)}=x_t^*.\)

Therefore the mechanism realizes the optimal mass vector in expectation, and

\(\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_t r_t\lambda(S\cap A_t)\right]=\sum_t r_t x_t^*=\mathrm{OPT}.\)

In fact, the total score equals \(\mathrm{OPT}\) in every outcome, since each score stratum has a fixed selected mass.

4. Impartiality, including on singleton-deviation profiles

The rule above is also defined on profiles obtained through singleton deviations: compute integrated incoming scores, form the same score strata, and apply the same probability formula.

By Section 1, a singleton outgoing-row deviation leaves \(R_P(j)\) unchanged for every \(j\). Quota groups are fixed. Thus every stratum, its mass, and its allocated probability remain unchanged.

In particular, the deviator's inclusion probability remains unchanged. This proves impartiality pointwise, rather than merely almost everywhere.

The deviator need not belong to a positive-mass outgoing-profile type after its deviation. Its inclusion probability is evaluated through its quota group and integrated incoming score, so the mechanism remains defined for that individual.

The same argument covers any finite sequence of singleton deviations.

5. Anonymity

Let \(\phi\) be a measure-preserving relabeling that transports public attributes and the score profile. Write \(P^\phi\) for the transported profile. Change of variables in the incoming-score integral gives

\(R_{P^\phi}(\phi(i))=R_P(i).\)

Also, the transported quota group of \(\phi(i)\) equals the original quota group of \(i\). Therefore \(\phi\) transports each score stratum to a stratum with the same score and mass. Its allocated inclusion probability is unchanged:

\(\Pr_{P^\phi}(\phi(i)\in S)=\Pr_P(i\in S).\)

This is precisely the anonymity condition in the question.

The auxiliary coordinates need not themselves be invariant under relabeling. Every coordinate choice produces the same individual inclusion probabilities, which are the objects required to be equivariant.

6. Polynomial-time finite description

From the support input, compute the \(\tau\) rational scores

\(r_t=\sum_u\mu_u a_{ut}.\)

Then sort the types within quota groups by these scores, pool equal scores, and compute the rational masses \(M_{g,j}\), cumulative masses \(C_{g,j}\), and probabilities \(p_{g,j}\).

This uses \(O(\tau^2)\) rational arithmetic operations for the scores and \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) comparisons for sorting, together with polynomially many additional operations. Sums, products, comparisons, and the divisions used here all have polynomial bit complexity in \(m,\tau,L\).

For an explicit finite description of the lottery, use the canonical atomless representation

\(A=\bigsqcup_{t\in T}\bigl(\{t\}\times[0,1)\bigr),\)

with measure \(\mu_t\) times Lebesgue measure on each type interval. If a stratum consists of types \(t_1,\ldots,t_k\) and has mass \(M\), use the coordinate

\(h_B(t_\ell,z)=\dfrac{\sum_{a<\ell}\mu_{t_a}+\mu_{t_\ell}z}{M}.\)

These coordinates concatenate the type intervals into a uniform coordinate on the stratum. Their rational coefficients and the selection thresholds have polynomial total description length. Together with the circular-interval instruction, they give the required polynomial-time computable finite selection rule.

For any other atomless realization, the lemma supplies the corresponding measurable coordinates; the individual inclusion probabilities remain identical. On singleton-deviation profiles, the integrated scores and thresholds remain unchanged, so the same pointwise rule continues to apply.

7. Answer to both questions

The mechanism guarantees ratio \(1\), realizes \(x^*\) in expectation, and hence also guarantees ratio \(\tfrac14\). No approximation loss is unavoidable under the stated definitions.

The finite-agent approximation result [cited in the question](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.20477v1) is consistent with this conclusion. In a finite society, changing one outgoing row can change competitors' total scores. Here its contribution to every integrated score is zero. The proof concerns individual singleton deviations; it does not assert impartiality against coordinated changes by a positive-mass set.
