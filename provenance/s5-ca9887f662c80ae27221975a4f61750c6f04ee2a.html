The continuous mirror question, as graded

Input: \(m\ge2\) groups, each with two nominees, and a rational-mass distribution \(\mu\) over member types. Each type specifies a group affiliation and a complete utility vector over all nominees; define each group’s aggregate utility for nominee \(x\) by summing \(\mu_t v_t(x)\) over its types. Require the resulting instance to satisfy the paper’s egoism and monotonicity conditions and social-utility bound, and include a polynomial-time winning-probability procedure \(\Pi\) as in the paper. Decide whether there is a nomination profile \(s\) such that no group can increase its expected aggregate member utility by unilaterally changing nominees.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge2\) groups, with distinct nominees \(x_{i,1},x_{i,2}\) for each group \(i\). A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Each type has an affiliation \(g(t)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\) and binary-rational utilities \(v_t(x)\in\mathbb Q\) for all nominees. Every group has positive mass; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Define \(u_i(x)=\sum_{t:g(t)=i}\mu_t v_t(x)\) and \(U(x)=\sum_{i=1}^m u_i(x)\). The input includes a binary-rational bound \(\beta\ge1\), and satisfies \(0\le U(x)\le\beta\) for every nominee. Label nominees so that \(u_i(x_{i,1})\ge u_i(x_{i,2})\).
Egoism means \(u_i(x_{i,a})>u_i(x_{j,b})\) for all \(i\ne j\) and \(a,b\in\{1,2\}\); it is required only of aggregate utilities.
The input also includes a deterministic winning-probability procedure \(\Pi\). On a nomination profile \(s\in\{1,2\}^m\), it returns exact binary-rational probabilities \(p_i(s)\ge0\), with \(\sum_i p_i(s)=1\), in time bounded by a fixed polynomial in the total input bit length \(L\), which includes its description.
Monotonicity is promised: for every \(i\), fixed \(s_{-i}\), and \(a,b\in\{1,2\}\), \(U(x_{i,b})\ge U(x_{i,a})\) implies \(p_i(b,s_{-i})\ge p_i(a,s_{-i})\). No additional restriction on \(\Pi\) is imposed.
Group \(i\)'s payoff is \(r_i(s)=\sum_{j=1}^m p_j(s)u_i(x_{j,s_j})\). The decision problem asks whether some \(s\) satisfies \(r_i(s)\ge r_i(a,s_{-i})\) for every \(i\) and \(a\in\{1,2\}\).
Is this mass-based pure-strategy Nash equilibrium existence problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).
For the discrete aggregate-utility formulation with the probability procedure supplied as input, equilibrium existence is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even with two nominees per group, monotonicity, and \(\beta=200\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.14405v6)
No result settling exactly the support-encoded mass formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass 1 and required positive mass in every group.
  • Specified binary-rational individual utilities, allowing either sign because the discrete definition bounds social utility rather than individual or group utilities.
  • Made beta a binary-rational input with beta at least 1, retaining the discrete social-utility bound.
  • Applied strict egoism to aggregate group utilities, rather than imposing it separately on every member type.
  • Specified exact binary-rational probability outputs and a uniform polynomial evaluation bound to make payoff comparisons and input complexity precise.
  • Treated egoism, monotonicity, probability validity, and utility bounds as input promises.
  • Retained weak ordering of own-group nominees and allowed payoff ties, which do not constitute profitable deviations.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(\tau=m\) and \(\beta=200\). It also has a polynomial-time equilibrium verifier, so it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete in the promised-input sense. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm for all promised instances would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The essential point is that every aggregate-utility instance can be represented exactly by a mass-based society with one type per group.

Exact realization of aggregate utilities

Let an aggregate instance have utilities \(w_i(x)\), with the prescribed nominee labels, egoism, social-utility bounds, and probability procedure \(\Pi\). Write its encoding length as \(L_0\).

Set \(k=\lceil\log_2 m\rceil\), \(q=2^k\), and \(d=q-m\). Because \(m\ge2\), we have \(0\le d<m\).

Create exactly \(m\) types, denoted \(t_1,\ldots,t_m\), with affiliation \(g(t_i)=i\). Define their masses by

\( \displaystyle \mu_{t_i}=\begin{cases}2/q,&1\le i\le d,\\1/q,&d<i\le m.\end{cases} \)

Every mass is positive and dyadic, and

\( \displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^m\mu_{t_i}=\frac{2d+(m-d)}q=\frac{m+d}q=1. \)

For every nominee \(x\), assign

\( \displaystyle v_{t_i}(x)=\frac{w_i(x)}{\mu_{t_i}}. \)

Each reciprocal mass is a power of two. Thus these utilities are binary-encoded rationals; if the original utilities are dyadic, the new utilities remain dyadic.

There is exactly one type affiliated with each group, so the induced aggregate utility satisfies

\( \displaystyle u_i(x)=\mu_{t_i}v_{t_i}(x)=w_i(x). \)

This identity proves all the required preservation statements:

  • Every group has positive mass, and the support size is \(\tau=m\).
  • The nominee ordering is preserved because each own-group aggregate utility is unchanged.
  • Aggregate egoism is preserved because every utility appearing in its inequalities is unchanged.
  • Social utilities are unchanged: \(U(x)=\sum_i w_i(x)\). Hence the original bound, including \(\beta=200\), remains valid.
  • Retaining \(\Pi\) preserves monotonicity: both the social-utility comparisons and the returned probabilities are unchanged.

Finally, for every nomination profile \(s\),

\( \displaystyle r_i^{\mathrm{mass}}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^m p_j(s)u_i(x_{j,s_j})=\sum_{j=1}^m p_j(s)w_i(x_{j,s_j})=r_i^{\mathrm{aggregate}}(s). \)

Therefore every unilateral-deviation comparison is preserved. The two instances have exactly the same pure-strategy Nash equilibria.

Encoding cost

Since \(q<2m\), multiplying an original utility by a reciprocal mass increases its encoding length by at most \(O(\log m)\). There are \(2m^2\) utility entries. The constructed instance therefore has length

\( \displaystyle L=O(L_0+m^2\log m), \)

and can be written in \(O(L_0+m^2\log m)\) bit operations using binary shifts and copying. The original probability procedure is retained, with its original data embedded if its implementation needs them. Its evaluation time remains bounded by a fixed polynomial in the new input length.

Thus this is a polynomial-time reduction.

Application of the supplied hardness theorem

Theorem 1 of the cited paper states that equilibrium existence for the aggregate formulation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete with binary-encoded rational utilities and an input probability procedure, even with two nominees per group, monotonicity, egoism, and fixed \(\beta=200\). [Source: Theorem 1 and its input representation](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.14405v6#S4).

Apply the construction above to those hard instances. It preserves their probability procedure, all promises, and equilibrium existence, while producing support size \(\tau=m\).

This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for the mass formulation. Moreover, \(\tau=m\) is the smallest possible support size: because each type has one affiliation and every group has positive mass, every valid instance satisfies \(\tau\ge m\).

Verification and running times

Let \(P(L)\ge L\) be a fixed polynomial bounding one execution of \(\Pi\). Its output contains at most \(P(L)\) bits.

First compute the \(2m^2\) aggregate utilities. This requires \(O(m\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations: each type contributes to one group’s aggregate utility for each of the \(2m\) nominees.

Each aggregate has \(O(L)\) bits in an unreduced fraction representation. Indeed, for a fixed aggregate, the numerator and denominator lengths of its summands are bounded by the lengths of the relevant input masses and utilities; multiplying their denominators gives total bit length \(O(L)\). Schoolbook exact arithmetic consequently gives the preprocessing bound

\( \displaystyle O(m\tau L^2). \)

A certificate is a profile \(s\in\{1,2\}^m\), represented by \(m\) bits. Let \(s^{(i)}\) be the profile obtained by switching group \(i\)’s nominee. Evaluate \(\Pi\) at \(s\) and at the \(m\) profiles \(s^{(i)}\). Compute all baseline payoffs and each deviating group’s payoff, then check

\( \displaystyle r_i(s)\ge r_i(s^{(i)})\qquad\text{for every }i. \)

These checks are sufficient because there is only one alternative action per group.

Define \(H(L,m)=P(L)+mL\). A payoff, represented by combining its \(m\) rational summands without cancellation, has \(O(H(L,m))\) bits: probability encodings contribute at most \(P(L)\) bits in total, and aggregate utilities contribute \(O(mL)\). Thus exact payoff arithmetic and comparison cost \(O(H(L,m)^2)\) per operation.

The verification uses \(m+1\) procedure evaluations and \(O(m^2)\) rational operations. Including preprocessing, its bit running time is

\( \displaystyle O\!\left(m\tau L^2+mP(L)+m^2\bigl(P(L)+mL\bigr)^2\right). \)

This is polynomial in \(L\), establishing membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) on promised inputs. The verifier need not certify the global monotonicity or runtime promises.

For completeness, enumerating all \(2^m\) profiles gives a deterministic decision algorithm with bit running time

\( \displaystyle O\!\left(m\tau L^2+2^m\left[mP(L)+m^2\bigl(P(L)+mL\bigr)^2\right]\right). \)

Thus preprocessing is polynomial in the actual support size \(\tau\), while exhaustive equilibrium search is exponential in \(m\). The hardness reduction shows that the support encoding does not remove the general computational obstruction.
