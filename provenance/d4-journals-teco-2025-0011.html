The continuous mirror question, as graded

For fixed \(p\ge1\), take a unit-mass continuum of identical star cells with \(m\) tasks per cell, one root of cost vector \(r\) and one task-specific leaf of cost \(\ell_j\) for each task \(j\). Each task must be assigned whole to its root or its corresponding leaf in every cell. A direct mechanism receives the individual machines’ reports in each cell, assigns tasks and pays machines, and must make truthful reporting optimal for each machine holding the other reports fixed. Across cells, assignments may vary measurably, so let \(x_j\in[0,1]\) be the mass fraction of task \(j\) assigned to roots. Is \(2^{1-1/p}\) the best possible worst-case approximation ratio for deterministic truthful mechanisms, measured against \(\min_{x\in[0,1]^m}\left((\sum_j r_jx_j)^p+\sum_j(\ell_j(1-x_j))^p\right)^{1/p}\)?

Partial result: a sharper upper bound

I do not determine $\rho_p$ for $p>1$. I prove a sharper upper bound, including the exact worst-case ratio of the hybrid mechanism against the continuous benchmark.

and define, for $0<a\le 1$,
$$ D_p(a) = p a^{p-1}-(p-1)a^p-(p^2-1)z_p(a) +(p-1)z_p(a)\log z_p(a). $$
Here $\log$ is the natural logarithm, and $D_p(0)=0$ by continuity. Set

The following claims are proved below:

1. A truthful mechanism on the full report domains

For a root subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$, write
$$ r(S)=\sum_{j\in S}r_j, \qquad g_\ell(S)=\left(\sum_{j\notin S}\ell_j^p\right)^{1/p}. $$
In every cell, choose a minimizer of
$$ H_{r,\ell}(S)=r(S)+g_\ell(S), $$
using a fixed ordering of subsets to resolve ties. Assign $S$ to the root and every remaining task to its leaf.

This is the hybrid allocation rule from the [discrete star paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol198-icalp2021/LIPIcs.ICALP.2021.56/LIPIcs.ICALP.2021.56.pdf). Its truthfulness and the continuous bounds used here are proved directly.

Because the same rule is used at every label, assignments across cells are measurable. At homogeneous reports, the aggregate allocation is the indicator vector of the selected subset.

Root payments

Pay the root
$$ P_0=g_\ell(\varnothing)-g_\ell(S). $$
Fix all leaf reports and let $r$ be the root’s actual cost vector. If a root report produces subset $S$, its actual utility is
$$ g_\ell(\varnothing)-g_\ell(S)-r(S). $$
Truthful reporting chooses a subset minimizing $r(S)+g_\ell(S)$ and therefore maximizes this utility. This argument permits arbitrary multidimensional root deviations.

Leaf payments

Fix leaf $j$ and all other reports. Define
$$ A_j=\min_{S:\,j\in S}H_{r,\ell}(S). $$
This is independent of leaf $j$’s report. When that report is $z$, the minimum objective among allocations assigning its task to the leaf is
$$ B_j(z)= \min_{S:\,j\notin S} \left[ r(S)+ \left( z^p+\sum_{\substack{i\notin S\\i\ne j}}\ell_i^p \right)^{1/p} \right]. $$
Each function inside this finite minimum is continuous and strictly increasing in $z$. Consequently, $B_j$ is continuous and strictly increasing. Also, $B_j(z)\ge z$.

Thus the leaf receives its task below a finite threshold, loses it above that threshold, and may receive either outcome at the threshold. Pay an assigned leaf its threshold and an unassigned leaf zero. Receiving the task then gives utility equal to the threshold minus its actual cost; not receiving it gives zero utility. Truthful reporting is optimal, including at equality.

For completeness, the threshold has an explicit finite computation. For each $S$ not containing $j$, put
$$ d_S=A_j-r(S), \qquad c_S=\sum_{\substack{i\notin S\\i\ne j}}\ell_i^p. $$
If $d_S\ge0$ and $d_S^p\ge c_S$, this subset contributes the candidate threshold
$$ (d_S^p-c_S)^{1/p}. $$
The leaf threshold is the maximum of these candidates. If there are none, the leaf never receives its task and threshold zero suffices. This also handles candidates equal to zero.

All payments are finite at every finite real report profile.

Zero optimum and computation

If $\operatorname{OPT}_p=0$, any task with $\ell_j>0$ must have $r_j=0$: otherwise that task could not contribute zero load at both destinations. Assigning precisely these tasks to roots gives $H=0$. The mechanism therefore also selects an allocation with zero objective.

Subset enumeration, together with all threshold calculations above, takes
$$ O(\tau m2^m) $$
arithmetic operations and fixed-$p$ power evaluations, after reading the input of bit length $L$. Here $\tau=1$. This is an arithmetic-operation bound; for arbitrary fixed real $p$, it is not an unconditional exact bit-complexity claim.

2. Subset comparisons imply a fractional lower bound

The key improvement is to use all subset comparisons satisfied by the hybrid mechanism.

Let
$$ h=\min_S H_{r,\ell}(S). $$
On an instance with positive optimum, $h>0$: if $h=0$, its minimizing integral allocation would already have zero objective.

Scale every cost by $1/h$. Both objectives scale by the same factor, so this does not change their ratio. In the remainder of this section, therefore,
$$ \min_S H_{r,\ell}(S)=1. $$
Put $w_j=\ell_j^p$. Every subset satisfies
$$ r(S)+\left(\sum_{j\notin S}w_j\right)^{1/p}\ge1. $$

For $c>0$, define
$$ T(c)=\sum_j\min(r_j,cw_j). $$
Choosing independently, for each task, the smaller of its two terms gives the exact identity
$$ T(c)=\min_S\left[r(S)+c\sum_{j\notin S}w_j\right]. $$
For a subset whose remaining leaf weight is $v$, the preceding subset inequality gives
$$ r(S)\ge (1-v^{1/p})_+, $$
where $t_+=\max(t,0)$. Hence
$$ T(c)\ge G_p(c):=\min_{v\ge0}\left[(1-v^{1/p})_++cv\right]. $$

A direct minimization yields
$$ G_p(c)= \begin{cases} c,&0<c\le 1/p,\\[2mm] 1-\left(1-\dfrac1p\right)(pc)^{-1/(p-1)},&c\ge1/p. \end{cases} $$
Indeed, on $0\le v\le1$, the derivative of $1-v^{1/p}+cv$ vanishes at $v=(pc)^{-s}$ when $c\ge1/p$; when $c\le1/p$, the minimum is at $v=1$. On $v\ge1$, the minimum is also attained at $v=1$.

We will use the equivalent integral formula
$$ G_p(c)=\int_0^1\min(c,b(t))\,dt, \qquad b(t)=\frac1p\,t^{-(p-1)/p}. $$
For $c\le1/p$, the integrand equals $c$ throughout. For $c>1/p$, split the integral at $t=(pc)^{-s}$ and integrate $b(t)$ on the remaining interval. This gives exactly the displayed formula for $G_p$.

3. Proof of the upper bound $\rho_p\le K_p$

Let
$$ Q(x)= \left(\sum_jr_jx_j\right)^p +\sum_jw_j(1-x_j)^p. $$
Thus $\operatorname{OPT}_p^p=\min_{x\in[0,1]^m}Q(x)$.

Fix $a>0$. Convexity of $t\mapsto t^p$, applied at $a$, gives
$$ R^p\ge p a^{p-1}R-(p-1)a^p \qquad(R\ge0). $$
It follows that
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_p^p \ge -(p-1)a^p+ \sum_j \min_{0\le x_j\le1} \left[p a^{p-1}r_jx_j+w_j(1-x_j)^p\right]. $$

For $w_j>0$, put $b_j=r_j/w_j$. The scalar minimum is $w_j\psi_a(b_j)$, where
$$ \psi_a(b)= \begin{cases} p a^{p-1}b-(p-1)a^p b^s, &0\le b\le a^{-(p-1)},\\[2mm] 1,&b\ge a^{-(p-1)}. \end{cases} $$
To verify this, substitute $y=1-x$. The scalar minimizer is
$$ y=\min\left(1,a b^{1/(p-1)}\right). $$
Terms with $w_j=0$ have scalar minimum zero and can be omitted.

The function $\psi_a$ has the representation
$$ \psi_a(b)= \frac{p a^p}{p-1} \int_0^{a^{-(p-1)}} c^{(2-p)/(p-1)}\min(b,c)\,dc. $$
Both sides vanish at $b=0$. For $b<a^{-(p-1)}$, their derivatives equal
$$ p a^{p-1}-p a^p b^{1/(p-1)}; $$
above this cutoff, both derivatives are zero. The integral is finite at its lower endpoint because $(2-p)/(p-1)>-1$.

Using this representation, the bound on $T(c)$ from Section 2, and interchange of nonnegative integrals, we obtain
$$ \sum_{j:\,w_j>0}w_j\psi_a(b_j) \ge \frac{p a^p}{p-1} \int_0^{a^{-(p-1)}} c^{(2-p)/(p-1)}G_p(c)\,dc = \int_0^1\psi_a(b(t))\,dt. $$
Consequently,
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_p^p \ge -(p-1)a^p+\int_0^1\psi_a(b(t))\,dt. $$

For $0<a\le1$, let $z=z_p(a)=a^p/p^s$. The cutoff between the two formulas for $\psi_a(b(t))$ occurs at $t=z$. Therefore,
$$ \int_0^1\psi_a(b(t))\,dt = z+\int_z^1 \left[ a^{p-1}t^{-1+1/p}-(p-1)\frac zt \right]dt. $$
Evaluating the integrals gives
$$ \int_0^1\psi_a(b(t))\,dt = p a^{p-1}-(p^2-1)z+(p-1)z\log z. $$
Thus
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_p^p\ge D_p(a) \qquad(0\le a\le1), $$
and hence
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_p^p\ge\lambda_p. $$
The constant $\lambda_p$ is positive: as $a\downarrow0$,
$$ \frac{D_p(a)}{a^{p-1}}\longrightarrow p. $$

For the subset $S$ selected by the hybrid mechanism,
$$ F_p(r,\ell,\mathbf1_S) = \bigl(r(S)^p+g_\ell(S)^p\bigr)^{1/p} \le r(S)+g_\ell(S)=1. $$
Undoing the normalization proves
$$ F_p(r,\ell,\mathbf1_S) \le K_p\operatorname{OPT}_p(r,\ell). $$
Together with the full-domain truthfulness proof and zero-optimum property, this establishes
$$ \rho_p\le K_p. $$

4. The bound $K_p$ is sharp for this mechanism

This section concerns the hybrid mechanism, not the infimum over all truthful families.

For each positive integer $n$, take $m=n$ and define
$$ \ell_j=n^{-1/p}, $$
$$ r_j= \left(1-\frac{j-1}{n}\right)^{1/p} - \left(1-\frac jn\right)^{1/p}, \qquad 1\le j\le n. $$
The root costs are positive, nondecreasing in $j$, and sum to one. Monotonicity follows because these are consecutive increments of the concave function $t^{1/p}$, traversed toward zero.

Among subsets of size $k$, the first $k$ tasks have the smallest total root cost. Their costs telescope to
$$ \sum_{j=1}^k r_j = 1-\left(1-\frac kn\right)^{1/p}. $$
For every subset $S$ of size $k$,
$$ H(S)\ge 1-\left(1-\frac kn\right)^{1/p} +\left(1-\frac kn\right)^{1/p}=1. $$
The empty subset has $H=1$, so $\min_S H(S)=1$.

Limit of the fractional optimum

Put $b_j=nr_j$. For these instances, define
$$ D_n(a)= -(p-1)a^p+\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\psi_a(b_j). $$
Then
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_p(r,\ell)^p = \max_{0\le a\le1}D_n(a). $$

Here is a justification of equality. The argument in Section 3 gives the inequality from right to left. Conversely, let $x$ minimize $Q$, and let $R=\sum_jr_jx_j$. Since $\sum_jr_j=1$, we have $0\le R\le1$. The first-order conditions for this convex differentiable objective on the box imply that each $x_j$ minimizes
$$ pR^{p-1}r_jx_j+w_j(1-x_j)^p. $$
The supporting-line inequality for the root term is an equality at $a=R$. Thus $Q(x)=D_n(R)$, proving the reverse inequality.

Recall
$$ b(t)=\frac1p\,t^{-(p-1)/p}. $$
Each $b_j$ is the average of $b(t)$ over
$$ \left(\frac{n-j}{n},\frac{n-j+1}{n}\right). $$
Let $b_n$ be the corresponding step function. Then
$$ \|b_n-b\|_{L^1(0,1)}\longrightarrow0. $$
To check this despite the endpoint singularity, first restrict to $[\delta,1]$, where $b$ is uniformly continuous and interval averages converge uniformly. On the omitted interval, the relevant integrals are bounded using
$$ \int_0^\delta b(t)\,dt=\delta^{1/p}. $$
Letting first $n$ tend to infinity and then $\delta$ tend to zero proves the claim.

For $0\le a\le1$, the function $\psi_a$ is Lipschitz in $b$ with constant at most $p$, as follows from its derivative in Section 3. Hence
$$ \sup_{0\le a\le1}|D_n(a)-D_p(a)| \le p\|b_n-b\|_{L^1(0,1)} \longrightarrow0. $$
Uniform convergence on the compact interval implies convergence of maxima. Therefore,
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_p(r,\ell)^p\longrightarrow\lambda_p. $$

Unique allocations and encoded inputs

Multiply all root costs by $1+\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon>0$, while retaining the leaf costs. Every nonempty subset now satisfies
$$ H(S)\ge1+\varepsilon r(S)>1. $$
The empty subset is the unique minimizer, so the mechanism assigns every task to its leaf and has objective one.

For fixed $n$, as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the fractional optimum tends to the unperturbed optimum. This continuity can be checked uniformly over allocations: changing costs by $\Delta r,\Delta\ell$ changes $F_p$ by at most
$$ \left[ \left(\sum_j|\Delta r_j|\right)^p +\sum_j|\Delta\ell_j|^p \right]^{1/p}. $$
The same bound therefore controls the change in the minimum.

The costs used above need not be binary rationals. For fixed $n$ and $\varepsilon>0$, however, all finitely many strict subset comparisons persist under sufficiently small perturbations to nonnegative binary rational costs. The objective and optimum also vary continuously. Thus encoded homogeneous instances, each with $\tau=1$, can approximate the same ratios arbitrarily closely.

Letting $n$ tend to infinity proves that the hybrid mechanism’s worst-case ratio is exactly
$$ K_p. $$

6. A lower bound valid under arbitrary label mixing

I reproduce the incentive lower bound so the resulting interval is self-contained.

Aggregate weak monotonicity

Let $a_u(r,\ell)\in\{0,1\}^m$ denote the root allocation in cell $u$, and define
$$ x(r,\ell)=\int_0^1a_u(r,\ell)\,du. $$
Fix $\ell$ and compare root reports $r,r'$. The two truthful-reporting inequalities, added together, give
$$ (r-r')\cdot \bigl(a_u(r,\ell)-a_u(r',\ell)\bigr)\le0. $$
Payments cancel pointwise because they are finite. Integrating the bounded measurable allocations yields
$$ (r-r')\cdot \bigl(x(r,\ell)-x(r',\ell)\bigr)\le0. $$
No integrability assumption on payments is needed.

Definition of the lower-bound constant

Define on the entire interval $[0,1]$
$$ A_p(t)= 2^{1-1/p}\bigl(t^p+(1-t)^p\bigr)^{1/p}, $$
$$ B_p(t)= \left[ \frac{(2+t)^p+(1-t)^p}{2^p+1} \right]^{1/p}. $$
Set
$$ c_p=\min_{0\le t\le1/2}\max\{A_p(t),B_p(t)\}. $$
On $[0,1/2]$, $A_p$ strictly decreases from $2^{1-1/p}$ to one. Meanwhile $B_p$ strictly increases from one, because
$$ \frac{d}{dt}\bigl((2+t)^p+(1-t)^p\bigr) = p\bigl((2+t)^{p-1}-(1-t)^{p-1}\bigr)>0. $$
They therefore cross exactly once in $(0,1/2)$, and their common value is $c_p>1$.

Two-instance proof

Consider any truthful family with finite worst-case ratio $C$. For each positive integer $n$, put
$$ M_n=2^{2n},\qquad \delta_n=2^{-n}. $$
Use two tasks and leaf reports
$$ \ell^{(n)}=(M_n,1). $$
Compare root profiles
$$ r^{A,n}=(0,1+\delta_n), \qquad r^{B,n}=(2,1). $$
Each is an encoded homogeneous instance with $\tau=1$, positive optimum, and input bit length $O(n)$.

Write their aggregate allocations as $x^{A,n},x^{B,n}$ and put $q_n=x^{A,n}_2$. On instance $B$, the allocation assigning task one to roots and task two to leaves has objective
$$ J_p=(2^p+1)^{1/p}. $$
Thus
$$ M_n(1-x^{B,n}_1) \le F_p(B,x^{B,n}) \le CJ_p, $$
so $x^{B,n}_1\to1$.

Applying aggregate weak monotonicity with report difference $(2,-\delta_n)$ gives
$$ 2(x^{B,n}_1-x^{A,n}_1) -\delta_n(x^{B,n}_2-q_n)\le0. $$
Since $x^{A,n}_1\le1$, it follows that
$$ x^{B,n}_2 \ge q_n-\frac{2CJ_p}{\delta_nM_n} = q_n-\frac{2CJ_p}{2^n}. $$
Pass to a common subsequence on which $q_n\to q$ and $x^{B,n}_2\to t$. Then $t\ge q$.

On instance $A$, task one can be assigned to roots for free. Its optimum is therefore the one-task optimum for costs $1+\delta_n$ and one, which tends to $2^{1/p-1}$. Dropping leaf one’s load from the mechanism’s objective and taking limits gives
$$ C\ge A_p(q). $$
On instance $B$, dropping the same load and using $\operatorname{OPT}_p(B)\le J_p$ gives
$$ C\ge B_p(t)\ge B_p(q). $$
The last inequality holds because $B_p$ increases throughout $[0,1]$.

If $q\le1/2$, these two constraints imply $C\ge c_p$. If $q>1/2$, the second gives $C\ge B_p(1/2)>c_p$. Families with infinite ratio satisfy this lower bound automatically. Hence
$$ \rho_p\ge c_p>1. $$

For $p=2$, the crossing equation is
$$ 18t^2-22t+5=0. $$
Its root in $[0,1/2]$ is $(11-\sqrt{31})/18$, giving
$$ c_2=\frac{2\sqrt{29-\sqrt{31}}}{9} =1.0757078780\ldots. $$

7. Exactly solvable cases

The case $p=1$

The objective separates:
$$ F_1(r,\ell,x) = \sum_j\bigl(r_jx_j+\ell_j(1-x_j)\bigr). $$
Assign each task to its lower-cost destination, with deterministic ties. Pay the root $\ell_j$ for each task it receives, and pay leaf $j$ the amount $r_j$ when it receives its task.

For fixed leaf reports, root utility is the sum of $\ell_j-r_j$ over received tasks, and truthful reporting selects precisely the beneficial terms. Each leaf faces a threshold $r_j$ independent of its own report. Thus the rule is truthful on the full domains and attains the optimum, including zero optimum. Therefore,
$$ \rho_1=1. $$
For encoded inputs, comparisons and payments take polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$.

One task for $p>1$

For positive costs, the unique minimizer of
$$ r^p x^p+\ell^p(1-x)^p $$
is
$$ x^*=\frac{\ell^s}{r^s+\ell^s}. $$
For each $u\in(0,1)$, put
$$ \alpha(u)=\left(\frac{1-u}{u}\right)^{1/s}. $$
Assign the task to the root when $r\le\alpha(u)\ell$. Pay an assigned root $\alpha(u)\ell$ and an assigned leaf $r/\alpha(u)$; unassigned machines receive zero. Each machine faces a finite threshold independent of its own report.

The root condition is equivalent to $u\le x^*$, so its aggregate mass is exactly optimal. If one cost is zero and the other positive, the positive-cost destination receives zero mass. If both vanish, every assignment has zero objective. At the two endpoint labels, use the lower-cost rule from the $p=1$ case.

This gives measurable, truthful rules with finite payments at every label. A cell query uses a constant number of arithmetic operations and fixed-$p$ power evaluations.

8. What remains unresolved

The proved interval is
$$ c_p\le\rho_p\le K_p \qquad(p>1), $$
with $c_p>1$. The new upper bound is sharp for the hybrid mechanism itself, even on encoded homogeneous instances.

It is not proved sharp over all truthful mechanism families. In particular, mechanisms using different truthful rules at different labels may improve it. Neither the lower bound nor the sharp analysis of this particular mechanism establishes or refutes
$$ \rho_p=2^{1-1/p}. $$
