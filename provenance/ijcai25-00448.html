The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many resident types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), named hospitals \(H\), rational capacities \(q_h\), and hospital-side weak preferences over types, choose a fractional matching \(x_{t,h}\), unmatched masses, and capacity increases \(\Delta_h\ge0\) minimizing \(\sum_h\Delta_h\), subject to capacity feasibility and the paper's strong-stability condition lifted to positive mass: no type-hospital pair with positive mass assigned elsewhere can satisfy either strong-blocking alternative using positive occupant mass or unused capacity. For the forced-edge variant, also require \(x_{t,h}\ge\lambda_{t,h}\) for designated cohorts.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

An instance has resident types \(T\), each of rational mass \(\mu_t>0\), strict preferences over its acceptable hospitals, and hospitals with preferences over acceptable types that may contain ties. An aggregate matching has \(x_{th}\in\{0,\mu_t\}\), with \(\sum_h x_{th}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{th}\le q_h+\delta_h\), where \(q_h\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) and \(\delta_h\ge0\).
A positive submass of type \(t\) and hospital \(h\) strongly blocks if \(t\) prefers \(h\) to its assignment and \(h\) has unused positive capacity or weakly prefers \(t\) to some type assigned positive mass there; or if \(t\) is indifferent between \(h\) and its assignment and \(h\) strictly prefers \(t\) to some assigned positive mass.
Costs satisfy \(\kappa_h\in\{0,1\}\), and the objective is to minimize \(\sum_h\kappa_h\delta_h\) over strongly stable aggregate matchings and augmentations.

Target

Is this rational-mass MINSUM-COST problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results

For discrete HR-HT instances, weighted minimum-sum quota augmentation for a strongly stable matching is NP-hard even with costs in \(\{0,1\}\), and is inapproximable within every multiplicative factor.
No result for the rational-mass formulation above is known here.

Result

The rational-mass MINSUM-COST problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when every resident type has mass \(1\) and every original hospital quota is integral.

The proof is an exact, optimum-preserving reduction from the stated discrete HR-HT problem. The main point requiring proof is that allowing real-valued augmentations does not produce a cheaper solution.

Discrete hardness premise

Use the given result that discrete HR-HT weighted minimum-sum quota augmentation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard when:

  • residents have strict preferences;
  • hospitals may have ties;
  • hospital quotas \(q_h\) are nonnegative integers;
  • augmentations \(a_h\) are nonnegative integers; and
  • costs satisfy \(\kappa_h\in\{0,1\}\).

Its objective is \(\sum_h\kappa_h a_h\), subject to the augmented instance admitting the required strongly stable matching.

Construction

Let \(I\) be such a discrete instance, with resident set \(R\).

Construct a rational-mass instance \(I^\ast\) as follows.

For every resident \(r\in R\), introduce a distinct type \(t_r\) with mass \(\mu_{t_r}=1\). Copy \(r\)'s acceptable hospitals and strict preference order to \(t_r\). In every hospital preference order, replace \(r\) by \(t_r\), preserving all strict comparisons and ties. Copy every quota \(q_h\) and cost \(\kappa_h\).

This construction has size linear in the encoding of \(I\).

Because \(x_{t_rh}\in\{0,1\}\) and \(\sum_hx_{t_rh}\le 1\), aggregate matchings of \(I^\ast\) are exactly ordinary matchings of \(I\). Under an integral augmented capacity, a positive-submass strong block \((t_r,h)\) exists exactly when \((r,h)\) is a discrete strongly blocking pair: the assigned residents, preference comparisons, and vacancy condition are identical.

Canonicalization lemma

Lemma. Suppose \(x\) is a strongly stable aggregate matching of \(I^\ast\) under an arbitrary real augmentation vector \(\delta\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{H}\). For each hospital \(h\), let

\[ \ell_h=\sum_{r\in R}x_{t_rh}. \]

Define

\[ \widehat\delta_h=\max\{0,\ell_h-q_h\}. \]

Then \(\widehat\delta_h\) is a nonnegative integer, \(x\) remains feasible and strongly stable under \(\widehat\delta\), and \(\widehat\delta_h\le\delta_h\) for every \(h\).

Proof.

Since every assigned mass is \(1\), the load \(\ell_h\) is an integer. Since \(q_h\) is integral, \(\widehat\delta_h\) is also integral.

The resulting capacity is

\[ q_h+\widehat\delta_h=\max\{q_h,\ell_h\}, \]

which is at least \(\ell_h\). Thus \(x\) remains feasible.

Original feasibility gives \(\ell_h\le q_h+\delta_h\). If \(\ell_h\le q_h\), then \(\widehat\delta_h=0\le\delta_h\). If \(\ell_h>q_h\), then \(\delta_h\ge\ell_h-q_h=\widehat\delta_h\). Hence \(\widehat\delta_h\le\delta_h\) in all cases.

It remains to prove stability. Reducing capacity does not change the assigned types or any preference comparison. It therefore cannot create a blocking pair through either of the preference-versus-assigned-type conditions.

Suppose instead that a pair becomes blocking because \(h\) has unused capacity under \(\widehat\delta\). Then

\[ \ell_h<q_h+\widehat\delta_h=\max\{q_h,\ell_h\}. \]

This strict inequality is possible only when \(\ell_h<q_h\). But then the original capacity satisfies \(q_h+\delta_h\ge q_h>\ell_h\), so \(h\) already had unused positive capacity under \(\delta\). The same pair would therefore have blocked \(x\) under \(\delta\), contradicting its strong stability.

Thus every block under \(\widehat\delta\) would already have been a block under \(\delta\). Hence \(x\) remains strongly stable. \(\square\)

Equality of optimum values

Let \(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm D}(I)\) denote the discrete optimum and \(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm R}(I^\ast)\) the rational-mass optimum.

Every discrete feasible pair consisting of a matching and an integer augmentation vector is directly feasible for \(I^\ast\), with the same cost. Therefore

\[ \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm R}(I^\ast)\le \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm D}(I). \]

Conversely, apply the canonicalization lemma to any feasible rational solution \((x,\delta)\). The resulting \(\widehat\delta\) is integral, \(x\) is strongly stable under the integer capacities \(q_h+\widehat\delta_h\), and

\[ \sum_h\kappa_h\widehat\delta_h \le \sum_h\kappa_h\delta_h. \]

Through the matching correspondence, \((x,\widehat\delta)\) is a feasible solution of the discrete instance. Consequently,

\[ \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm D}(I)\le \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm R}(I^\ast). \]

Thus

\[ \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm R}(I^\ast)=\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm D}(I). \]

Complexity consequence

A polynomial-time algorithm for rational-mass MINSUM-COST would, when applied to \(I^\ast\), compute the optimum of the discrete \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard problem. Therefore rational-mass MINSUM-COST is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

The argument also shows that arbitrary real augmentations create no relaxation gap on the constructed instances. Moreover, because the reduction preserves the optimum exactly and every rational solution can be canonicalized without increasing its cost, the stated discrete multiplicative inapproximability transfers to this rational-mass formulation as well.
