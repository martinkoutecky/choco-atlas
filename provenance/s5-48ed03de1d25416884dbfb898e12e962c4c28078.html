The continuous mirror question, as graded

For each round, given advertiser types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), slot classes \(J\), eligibility sets \(S_t\), integral demand caps \(\kappa_t\), per-capita supplies \(s_j\), and feasible rational marginals \(x_{t,j}\), compute rational masses \(\lambda_{t,A}\geq 0\) for bundles \(A\subseteq S_t\), \(|A|\leq\kappa_t\), such that \(\sum_A\lambda_{t,A}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{A:j\in A}\lambda_{t,A}=x_{t,j}\), and \(\sum_t x_{t,j}=s_j\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
For one round, let \(J\) be a set of \(m\geq1\) slot classes. Each type has an explicitly listed eligibility set \(S_t\subseteq J\) and a positive integer demand cap \(\kappa_t\), encoded in binary; each individual can receive at most one slot from each class.
The input includes nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(s_j\) and aggregate marginal masses \(x_{t,j}\), promised to satisfy \(x_{t,j}=0\) for \(j\notin S_t\), \(0\leq x_{t,j}\leq\mu_t\), \(\sum_{j\in J}x_{t,j}\leq\mu_t\kappa_t\), and \(\sum_{t\in T}x_{t,j}=s_j\).
An allocation is a finite list of bundles \(A\subseteq S_t\), \(|A|\leq\kappa_t\), with positive binary-rational masses \(\lambda_{t,A}\); omitted masses are zero and the empty bundle is allowed. It must satisfy \(\sum_A\lambda_{t,A}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{A:j\in A}\lambda_{t,A}=x_{t,j}\) for every type and class.
Is computing such an allocation on every promised input possible in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? Measure running time in \(m\), \(\tau\), the total input bit length \(L\), and the binary-encoded caps \((\kappa_t)_{t\in T}\).
For the discrete version, a row-preserving swapping algorithm exists for every finite matrix whose individual labels each occur at most as many times as there are columns; its output has no repeated label within any column. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.6361)
A later version retains this discrete result. No result addressing exactly the mass-allocation problem above was identified in the search. [Later version](https://eprints.cs.univie.ac.at/3510/1/chls_updated.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one, so supplies and marginals are measured per capita and x_{t,j} denotes aggregate type mass rather than a conditional probability.
  • Made the feasible-marginals promise explicit through eligibility, individual class bounds, demand-cap bounds, and exact supply equalities.
  • Interpreted each slot class as permitting at most one slot per individual, as required by the stated subset bundles.
  • Used positive binary-encoded integer caps, nonnegative binary-rational supplies and marginals, and explicitly listed eligibility sets; empty eligibility sets and zero supplies are allowed.
  • Specified a finite representation listing only positive bundle masses, with omitted entries zero and the empty bundle allowed, to avoid requiring an exponentially large table of zero entries.
  • Treated one round as the computational instance and defined L as the total input bit length.

Result

The allocation can be computed in deterministic polynomial time. Moreover, there is always an allocation with at most \(\sum_{t\in T}(|S_t|+1)\leq\tau(m+1)\) positive bundle masses.

The construction below proves this directly, with polynomial output bit length and no dependence on the numerical magnitudes of the caps.

1. Reduction to one type

Different types can be handled independently. Once every type’s marginal masses equal \(x_{t,j}\), the aggregate supply constraints follow from the promise \(\sum_{t\in T}x_{t,j}=s_j\).

Fix a type and abbreviate its mass and cap by \(\mu>0\) and \(\kappa\). Enumerate its eligible classes as \(S=\{j_1,\ldots,j_n\}\), and write \(x_i=x_{t,j_i}\). The relevant promises are

\(0\leq x_i\leq\mu\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i\leq\mu\kappa.\)

We will construct at most \(n+1\) bundles for this type. The construction also covers \(n=0\), when the only output bundle is empty.

2. Exact construction

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators in the binary-rational encodings of \(\mu,x_1,\ldots,x_n\). Define integers

\(M=D\mu,\qquad X_i=Dx_i,\qquad H_0=0,\qquad H_i=\sum_{a=1}^{i}X_a.\)

Thus \(M>0\), \(0\leq X_i\leq M\), and \(H_n\leq\kappa M\).

For a real parameter \(u\in[0,M)\), define

\(f_i(u)=\bigl|\{q\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}:H_{i-1}\leq u+qM<H_i\}\bigr|.\)

We will prove that every \(f_i(u)\) is either zero or one. Hence the associated bundle is

\(A(u)=\{j_i:f_i(u)=1\}.\)

To obtain a finite allocation, form the integer breakpoint set

\(B=\{0,M\}\cup\{H_i\bmod M:0\leq i\leq n\}.\)

Sort its distinct elements as \(0=b_0<b_1<\cdots<b_r=M\). Because \(H_0\bmod M=0\), there are at most \(n\) additional interior breakpoints, so \(r\leq n+1\).

For each \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\), choose the midpoint

\(u_\ell=(b_{\ell-1}+b_\ell)/2,\)

and output the bundle \(A(u_\ell)\) with mass

\(\lambda_\ell=(b_\ell-b_{\ell-1})/D.\)

If the same bundle occurs more than once, combine its masses.

3. Every output bundle is feasible

No repeated class

The interval \([H_{i-1},H_i)\) has length \(X_i\leq M\). It cannot contain two points of the form \(u+qM\): distinct such points differ by at least \(M\), and a half-open interval of length at most \(M\) cannot contain both.

Therefore \(f_i(u)\in\{0,1\}\). Each bundle is a subset of the eligible classes, with no repeated class.

The demand cap

The intervals \([H_{i-1},H_i)\), including any empty ones, partition \([0,H_n)\). Consequently,

\(|A(u)|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_i(u)=\bigl|\{q\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}:0\leq u+qM<H_n\}\bigr|.\)

Since \(H_n\leq\kappa M\), any such \(q\) must belong to \(\{0,\ldots,\kappa-1\}\): if \(q\geq\kappa\), then \(u+qM\geq\kappa M\geq H_n\).

Thus \(|A(u)|\leq\kappa\), proving feasibility of every output bundle.

4. The masses and marginals are exact

Total mass

Every output mass is positive because consecutive breakpoints are distinct. Their sum is

\(\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\lambda_\ell=\frac{1}{D}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}(b_\ell-b_{\ell-1})=\frac{M}{D}=\mu.\)

Combining identical bundles preserves positivity and total mass.

Constancy between breakpoints

The value of \(f_i(u)\) can change only when a point \(u+qM\) crosses one of the endpoints \(H_{i-1}\) or \(H_i\). Such a crossing requires \(u\) to equal the corresponding endpoint modulo \(M\).

All these residues belong to \(B\). Therefore \(f_i\) is constant on each open interval \((b_{\ell-1},b_\ell)\), and its value there is \(f_i(u_\ell)\).

Marginal mass of a class

Fix \(i\). Split \([H_{i-1},H_i)\) at the integer multiples of \(M\). Translate each piece lying in \([qM,(q+1)M)\) by \(-qM\), obtaining pieces in \([0,M)\).

Translation preserves length, so their total length is \(X_i\). These translated pieces are disjoint: an overlap would mean that two points \(u+qM\) belong to \([H_{i-1},H_i)\), contradicting the no-repetition argument.

Their union is precisely \(\{u\in[0,M):f_i(u)=1\}\), whose length is therefore \(X_i\). Using constancy between breakpoints, and observing that finitely many endpoints have zero length, gives

\(\sum_{\ell:f_i(u_\ell)=1}(b_\ell-b_{\ell-1})=X_i.\)

Hence the output marginal mass of class \(j_i\) is

\(\sum_{\ell:j_i\in A(u_\ell)}\lambda_\ell=X_i/D=x_i.\)

Classes outside \(S\) occur in no bundle, matching their promised zero marginals. This completes the correctness proof for one type and, by independent application, for the entire society.

5. Polynomial running time and output length

The preceding construction uses intervals only to prove correctness. It can be implemented entirely with binary integers.

At a midpoint, neither endpoint \(H_i\) is congruent to \(u_\ell\) modulo \(M\). Counting the integers in the defining interval therefore gives the exact formula

\(f_i(u_\ell)=\left\lfloor\frac{2H_i-b_{\ell-1}-b_\ell}{2M}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{2H_{i-1}-b_{\ell-1}-b_\ell}{2M}\right\rfloor.\)

Here the floors are mathematical floors, including for negative numerators. The formula counts integers \(q\) between the two endpoint quotients; all counted integers are nonnegative because \((H_{i-1}-u_\ell)/M>-1\).

Thus the algorithm computes prefix sums and residues, sorts at most \(n+2\) distinct breakpoints, and evaluates at most \(n(n+1)\) membership indicators. It never enumerates the possible values of \(q\).

To verify bit complexity, let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

  • The product \(D\) has at most \(L+1\) bits, because its logarithm is bounded by the sum of the denominator encoding lengths.
  • Since \(\mu\leq1\) and \(x_i\leq\mu\), we have \(M,X_i\leq D\).
  • Since \(H_i\leq nD\), the arithmetic operands have \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits.
  • Every output mass has denominator \(D\) and numerator at most \(M\). After combining equal bundles, the same bound holds because their combined numerator cannot exceed the total \(M\).

If “binary rational” specifically means a dyadic rational, then \(D\) is a power of two, so the output masses are dyadic as well.

There are at most \(\tau(m+1)\) output entries, and each bundle can be encoded using an \(m\)-bit incidence vector. Sorting, exact integer arithmetic, combining identical bundles, and writing the output therefore all take polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

The numerical value of \(\kappa_t\) does not control any loop or output size. Its binary encoding is already included in \(L\).

6. Relation to the discrete result

The supplied matrix-swapping theorem appears as Theorem 4 in the [original paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.6361) and Theorem I.1 in the [later version](https://eprints.cs.univie.ac.at/3510/1/chls_updated.pdf). Expanding rational masses into individual matrix entries could produce an exponentially large matrix. The construction above instead uses at most \(|S_t|+1\) weighted bundles per type and operates directly on the binary-encoded numbers.

Consequently, the promised inequalities are sufficient for allocation, and computing an allocation on every promised input is polynomial-time solvable.
