The continuous mirror question, as graded

For \(m\) divisible public items with feasible allocation set \(Z\), utility reports in the paper’s admissible class, and a population supported on at most \(\tau\) utility types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb{Z}\), can one compute a randomized mechanism \(M_N\) in time polynomial in \(N,\tau,m,L\) that is asymptotically truthful under every one-resident report change of mass \(1/N\) and returns an asymptotic core allocation with high probability? Here, for true type \(t\), alternative report \(t'\), and other-resident mass vector \(\nu\), truthfulness compares \(\mathbb{E}[U_t(M_N(\nu+e_t/N))]\) with \((1-\epsilon_N)\mathbb{E}[U_t(M_N(\nu+e_{t'}/N))]-\delta_N\), where \(\epsilon_N,\delta_N\to0\). The core condition treats a coalition as masses \(a_t\in[0,\mu_t]\), with \(\alpha=\sum_t a_t>0\), and rules out allocations \(z'\in Z\) satisfying \(\alpha U_t(z')\ge(1+\eta_N)U_t(z)+\kappa_N\) for every participating type, with strict improvement on positive mass, where \(\eta_N,\kappa_N\to0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m,d\ge1\), let \(c,s_1,\ldots,s_m\) be positive binary rationals, and define \(Z=\{z\in[0,1]^m:\sum_jz_j\le1,\;cz_j\le s_j\ \forall j\}\); allocations specify fractions of the full capacity.
An admissible type has a binary-rational parameter vector \(u_t\in[0,1]^d\) specifying a differentiable, coordinatewise strictly increasing, concave utility \(U_t:Z\to[0,1]\), with \(U_t(0)=0\), positive somewhere, and \(\beta\)-Lipschitz gradient for a fixed common \(\beta\). The utility family is publicly specified; values and gradients at rational points can be approximated to absolute error \(2^{-b}\) in time polynomial in \(m,d,L,b\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). An integer \(N\ge2\) satisfies \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\); \(L\) is the total binary input length, and running time is measured in \(N,\tau,m,d,L\), allowing polynomial dependence on the numerical value of \(N\).
A randomized mechanism \(M_N\) samples a feasible rational allocation from the reported society. Reports may introduce admissible types absent from the original support. For every admissible \(t,t'\) and nonnegative rational vector \(\nu\) with \(\sum_r\nu_r=1-1/N\) and \(N\nu_r\in\mathbb Z\), require \(\mathbb E[U_t(M_N(\nu+e_t/N))]\ge(1-\epsilon_N)\mathbb E[U_t(M_N(\nu+e_{t'}/N))]-\delta_N\), where \(e_t\) denotes unit mass at type \(t\).
An allocation \(z\) satisfies the \((\eta_N,\kappa_N)\)-mass core if there are no real coalition masses \(a_t\in[0,\mu_t]\), with \(\alpha=\sum_ta_t>0\), and \(z'\in Z\) such that \(\alpha U_t(z')\ge(1+\eta_N)U_t(z)+\kappa_N\) for every \(t\) with \(a_t>0\), strictly for at least one such type. Coalition masses need not be multiples of \(1/N\).
Require this core condition with probability at least \(1-1/N-1/N^m\), with nonnegative, instance-uniform errors \(\epsilon_N,\delta_N,\eta_N,\kappa_N\to0\) as \(N\to\infty\) along \(m=o(\sqrt N)\).

Is sampling a mechanism satisfying these truthfulness and mass-core guarantees possible in time polynomial in \(N,\tau,m,d,L\), or is this computational task NP-hard?

For finite societies, PPGA is stated to achieve asymptotic truthfulness and asymptotic core membership with the stated probability in polynomial time under \(m=o(\sqrt N)\). [Kananian, Sujanani, and Zahedi](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/19414/27258/52390).

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Polynomial-time solvability

The task is polynomial-time solvable. A deterministic mechanism suffices: approximately maximize a smoothed Nash-welfare objective with a small quadratic regularizer. Smoothing bounds the effect of changing one report; regularization makes the optimizer stable.

The proof below includes rational output, polynomial bit complexity, reports introducing previously absent types, and arbitrary real coalition masses. It does not depend on implementing PPGA.

1. Real coalition masses and finite societies

First, arbitrary real coalition masses do not create an additional obstruction in this model.

Suppose an allocation is blocked by masses \((a_t)_t\), with total mass \(\alpha>0\), and alternative \(z'\). Let \(S=\{t:a_t>0\}\). Replace the coalition masses by

\( \widehat a_t=\mu_t \) for \(t\in S\), and \( \widehat a_t=0 \) otherwise.

Their total mass \(\widehat\alpha\) satisfies \(\widehat\alpha\ge\alpha\). Since utilities are nonnegative,

\( \widehat\alpha U_t(z')\ge\alpha U_t(z') \)

for every participating type. Thus every blocking inequality remains valid, including the strict inequality. Consequently, whenever a real-mass blocking coalition exists, one exists that includes the entire mass of every participating type.

Because \(N\mu_t\) is integral, this also proves equivalence between the stated mass-core condition and the corresponding finite-agent core condition for the population containing \(N\mu_t\) copies of each type. This explains why the coalition distinction alone does not prevent applying a finite-society result such as [the cited PPGA theorem](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/19414/27258/52390). We nevertheless give a direct construction to establish the required computational guarantees.

2. A normalization with uniform gradient bounds

Fix an integer constant \(B\ge 1+2\beta\). It depends only on the fixed common smoothness bound.

Define

\( w_j=\min\{1,s_j/c\},\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(w_1,\ldots,w_m), \)

and introduce the rational polytope

\( K=\{x\in[0,1]^m:\sum_j w_jx_j\le1\}. \)

The map \(x\mapsto Dx\) is a bijection from \(K\) to \(Z\). For every admissible type, put

\( V_t(x)=U_t(Dx). \)

We claim that, uniformly over all admissible types,

\( \|\nabla V_t(x)\|_2\le B\sqrt m,\qquad \|\nabla V_t(x)-\nabla V_t(y)\|_2\le\beta\|x-y\|_2. \)

For the first bound, the segment from \(0\) to \(w_je_j\) lies in \(Z\). Integrating the Lipschitz-gradient bound along that segment gives

\( U_t(w_je_j)\ge w_j\partial_jU_t(0)-\frac{\beta w_j^2}{2}. \)

Since \(U_t(w_je_j)\le1\), it follows that

\( w_j\partial_jU_t(0)\le1+\frac{\beta}{2}. \)

Also, \(\partial_jU_t(0)\ge0\), by coordinatewise monotonicity along this segment. For any \(z\in Z\), we have \(\|z\|_2\le1\), and therefore

\( |w_j\partial_jU_t(z)| \le w_j\partial_jU_t(0)+w_j\beta\|z\|_2 \le1+\frac{3\beta}{2}\le B. \)

The chain rule now gives the asserted gradient bound. For the second bound, use \(\|D\|_2\le1\):

\( \|\nabla V_t(x)-\nabla V_t(y)\|_2 =\|D(\nabla U_t(Dx)-\nabla U_t(Dy))\|_2 \le\beta\|x-y\|_2. \)

In particular, every \(V_t\) is \(G\)-Lipschitz on \(K\), where

\( G=B\sqrt m. \)

This normalization is essential: the original gradients need not have instance-uniform bounds when some capacities are very small.

3. The mechanism and its error parameters

Choose a dyadic rational \(r\in(0,1]\) as follows. If \(m^2>N\), set \(r=1\). Otherwise, let \(k\) be the largest nonnegative integer satisfying

\( 2^{4k}m^2\le N, \)

and set \(r=2^{-k}\). Thus, when \(m^2\le N\),

\( (m^2/N)^{1/4}\le r<2(m^2/N)^{1/4}, \)

and always \(r^{-1}\le N^{1/4}\).

Set

\( \lambda=\frac rm,\qquad h=\frac{r^3}{8B^2m^2}. \)

For a reported society \(\mu\), define

\( F_\mu(x)=\sum_t\mu_t\log(V_t(x)+r)-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x\|_2^2, \qquad x\in K. \)

Each logarithmic term is concave because \(V_t+r\) is positive and concave, and the logarithm is increasing and concave. Hence \(F_\mu\) is differentiable and \(\lambda\)-strongly concave. It has a unique maximizer \(x_\mu^*\), since \(K\) is compact.

The mechanism computes a rational point \(x_\mu\in K\) satisfying the directional-gradient certificate

\( g_\mu(x_\mu):= \max_{y\in K}\nabla F_\mu(x_\mu)\cdot(y-x_\mu)\le h, \)

and outputs \(z_\mu=Dx_\mu\). Section 6 proves that this computation takes polynomial time.

Use the following errors:

\( \epsilon_N=0,\qquad \delta_N=\min\left\{1,\frac{2B^2m^2}{Nr^2}+r\right\}, \)

\( \eta_N=2r,\qquad \kappa_N=(1+2r)r. \)

These bounds depend only on \(N,m\) and the fixed common \(\beta\).

4. Proof of the mass-core guarantee

Fix a reported society and write \(x=x_\mu\). For any \(y\in K\), concavity of \(V_t\) implies

\( V_t(y)\le V_t(x)+\nabla V_t(x)\cdot(y-x). \)

Divide by \(V_t(x)+r>0\), multiply by \(\mu_t\), and sum. Using the formula for \(\nabla F_\mu\), we obtain

\( \sum_t\mu_t\frac{V_t(y)}{V_t(x)+r} \le \sum_t\mu_t\frac{V_t(x)}{V_t(x)+r} +\nabla F_\mu(x)\cdot(y-x) +\lambda x\cdot(y-x). \)

The three terms on the right are bounded respectively by \(1\), \(h\), and \(\lambda m=r\). The last bound follows from \(x,y\in[0,1]^m\). Since \(h\le r\), this proves

\[ \sum_t\mu_t\frac{V_t(y)}{V_t(x)+r}\le1+2r. \tag{1} \]

Suppose, towards a contradiction, that real coalition masses \(a_t\in[0,\mu_t]\), of total mass \(\alpha>0\), and an alternative \(Dy\in Z\) block the output for the stated errors. Then every participating type satisfies

\( \alpha V_t(y)\ge(1+2r)V_t(x)+(1+2r)r =(1+2r)(V_t(x)+r), \)

with a strict inequality for at least one participating type. Dividing and summing with weights \(a_t\) gives

\( \sum_t a_t\frac{V_t(y)}{V_t(x)+r}>1+2r. \)

Every ratio is nonnegative and \(a_t\le\mu_t\), so this contradicts (1).

Thus the output always belongs to the \((\eta_N,\kappa_N)\)-mass core. No discretization of coalition masses was used.

5. Proof of approximate truthfulness

Accuracy relative to the exact optimizer

Concavity and the gradient certificate imply

\( F_\mu(x_\mu^*)-F_\mu(x_\mu) \le\nabla F_\mu(x_\mu)\cdot(x_\mu^*-x_\mu)\le h. \)

At a constrained maximizer, the first-order optimality condition is

\( \nabla F_\mu(x_\mu^*)\cdot(x-x_\mu^*)\le0 \quad\text{for all }x\in K. \)

Combining this condition with strong concavity gives

\( F_\mu(x_\mu^*)-F_\mu(x) \ge\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x-x_\mu^*\|_2^2. \)

Consequently,

\[ \|x_\mu-x_\mu^*\|_2\le\sqrt{2h/\lambda}. \tag{2} \]

Stability under one changed report

Consider any neighboring societies

\( p=\nu+e_t/N,\qquad q=\nu+e_{t'}/N. \)

Their supports may differ. Nevertheless, their objectives satisfy the exact identity

\( F_p(x)-F_q(x) =\frac1N\bigl(\log(V_t(x)+r)-\log(V_{t'}(x)+r)\bigr). \)

Since \(\|\nabla V_t\|_2\le G\), each logarithmic term is \(G/r\)-Lipschitz. Therefore \(F_p-F_q\) is \(2G/(Nr)\)-Lipschitz.

Apply the strong-concavity inequality at both exact maximizers and add:

\( \lambda\|x_p^*-x_q^*\|_2^2 \le (F_p-F_q)(x_p^*)-(F_p-F_q)(x_q^*) \le\frac{2G}{Nr}\|x_p^*-x_q^*\|_2. \)

If the distance is nonzero, divide by it; otherwise the following bound is immediate:

\[ \|x_p^*-x_q^*\|_2\le\frac{2G}{Nr\lambda}. \tag{3} \]

Using (2), (3), and the \(G\)-Lipschitz property of the true type’s utility yields

\( |V_t(x_p)-V_t(x_q)| \le\frac{2G^2}{Nr\lambda} +2G\sqrt{2h/\lambda} =\frac{2B^2m^2}{Nr^2}+r. \)

Utilities also lie in \([0,1]\), so the difference is at most \(1\). Hence

\( U_t(z_p)\ge U_t(z_q)-\delta_N. \)

This is exactly the required truthfulness inequality with \(\epsilon_N=0\). Viewing the deterministic output as a point-mass distribution gives the same inequality in expectation. The proof applies to every admissible \(t,t'\), including types absent from the original support.

6. Polynomial-time computation with rational output

We now construct the directional-gradient certificate using only the promised approximation oracles and rational arithmetic.

Smoothness and linear optimization

The gradient of \(F_\mu\) is Lipschitz with constant at most

\( H=\frac Br+\frac{B^2m}{r^2}+\lambda. \)

Indeed, for a logarithmic term, the gradient difference is bounded by the sum of \(\beta/r\) times the distance and \(G^2/r^2\) times the distance. Weighted summation and the quadratic term give the displayed bound, since \(B\ge\beta\).

The squared diameter of \(K\) is at most \(m\). Put

\( C=mH,\qquad \theta=\frac{h}{4C}. \)

These are positive rationals, and \(0<\theta\le1\).

A rational linear objective can be maximized over \(K\) by continuous knapsack: discard nonpositive coefficients, sort the remaining coordinates by coefficient divided by \(w_j\), and fill them in that order subject to \(x_j\le1\) and the remaining budget. The exchange argument proves optimality: budget assigned to a smaller ratio can be moved to an unsaturated larger ratio without violating feasibility and while increasing the objective. This algorithm uses polynomially many exact rational operations.

Approximate gradients

At a rational point \(x\in K\), approximate each utility value and gradient coordinate to error at most

\( \xi=\frac{hr^2}{4m(1+B)}. \)

Clip approximate utility values to \([0,1]\). Multiply approximate original gradient coordinates by \(w_j\), and clip the results to \([-B,B]\). Clipping preserves their error bounds because the true quantities lie in those intervals.

For a coordinate of a logarithmic gradient, the resulting error is at most

\( \frac{\xi}{r}+\frac{B\xi}{r^2} \le\frac{(1+B)\xi}{r^2} =\frac{h}{4m}. \)

Thus we can compute a rational vector \(\widehat g\) satisfying

\[ \|\widehat g-\nabla F_\mu(x)\|_\infty\le\frac{h}{4m}. \tag{4} \]

The iteration and termination

Start at \(x=0\). At each iteration, compute \(\widehat g\), and use linear optimization to choose

\( y\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{v\in K}\widehat g\cdot v. \)

Compute the rational number \(\widehat s=\widehat g\cdot(y-x)\).

If \(\widehat s\le h/2\), return \(x\). Otherwise update

\( x\leftarrow x+\theta(y-x). \)

Every iterate is rational and feasible.

By (4), for every \(v\in K\),

\( |(\widehat g-\nabla F_\mu(x))\cdot(v-x)|\le h/4. \)

Consequently, upon termination the true directional-gradient maximum is at most \(3h/4\), and hence at most \(h\), as required.

At a nonterminating iteration,

\( \nabla F_\mu(x)\cdot(y-x)>h/4. \)

Smoothness then gives

\( F_\mu(x+\theta(y-x))-F_\mu(x) \ge\theta h/4-C\theta^2/2 =\frac{h^2}{32C}. \)

Throughout \(K\), the range of \(F_\mu\) is at most

\( \log((1+r)/r)+r/2\le2/r. \)

Therefore the algorithm terminates after at most

\( \left\lceil\frac{64C}{rh^2}\right\rceil+1 \)

iterations.

This bound is polynomial in \(N,m\): \(B\) is fixed, \(r^{-1}\le N^{1/4}\), and \(h^{-1}=8B^2m^2r^{-3}\).

Bit complexity

The required oracle precision has

\( \log(1/\xi)=O(\log m+\log N+1) \)

bits, with constants depending only on \(B\). Each iteration evaluates only the listed support types, so its oracle work is polynomial in \(\tau,m,d\), the query encoding length, and this precision.

The knapsack solutions have polynomial rational encoding length: their coordinates are \(0\), \(1\), or a remaining rational budget divided by some \(w_j\). The fixed rational update factor \(\theta\) causes iterate encoding lengths to grow at most polynomially with the number of iterations and the input length. Thus all query points, intermediate arithmetic, and the final allocation \(Dx\) have polynomial encoding length.

Together with the assumed utility-oracle bounds, this proves total running time polynomial in \(N,\tau,m,d,L\).

7. Asymptotics and conclusion

Along any sequence with \(m=o(\sqrt N)\), put \(q=m^2/N\). Then \(q\to0\), and eventually

\( q^{1/4}\le r<2q^{1/4}. \)

Hence

\( \delta_N\le2B^2q^{1/2}+2q^{1/4}\to0,\qquad \eta_N=2r\to0,\qquad \kappa_N=(1+2r)r\to0, \)

while \(\epsilon_N=0\).

The mechanism always outputs a feasible rational allocation satisfying the stated mass-core condition. Its success probability is therefore \(1\), which exceeds \(1-1/N-1/N^m\). All four errors are instance-uniform in the required asymptotic regime.
