The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite item set \(M\), types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), explicit valuation tables \(v_t:2^M\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), and a fixed order of type cohorts, let each item \(j\) have a nondecreasing concave rational piecewise-linear cost \(f_j:[0,1]\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\) with \(f_j(0)=0\) and \(\alpha f_j(y)/y\ge f'_{j,+}(0)\) for \(y>0\). Process mass in cohort order, assigning type \(t\) at current loads \(y_j\) a lexicographically tie-broken bundle maximizing \(v_t(B)-\sum_{j\in B}f'_{j,+}(y_j)\), and increase each load by the mass assigned that item. Compute the resulting bundle masses \(x_{t,B}\) and payment density; determine whether this has a polynomial event-driven implementation and guarantees per-capita social cost \(\pi_\infty(x)=\sum_j f_j(y_j)+\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}[v_t(M)-v_t(B)]\le\alpha\pi_\infty^*\u005c), where \pi_\infty^*\u005c) is the minimum of that objective over x_{t,B}\ge0\) with \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(m\ge1\). A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has an explicit nondecreasing valuation table \(v_t:2^M\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\). A permutation of the types specifies their cohort order.
Each item has a continuous, nondecreasing, concave piecewise-linear cost \(f_j:[0,1]\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), specified by rational breakpoints and values, with \(f_j(0)=0\). A binary-rational parameter \(\alpha\ge1\) satisfies \(\alpha f_j(z)/z\ge f'_{j,+}(0)\) for every \(j\) and \(0<z\le1\). Let \(K\) count all linear pieces and \(L\) be the total input bit length, including the valuation tables.
Process each cohort infinitesimally, starting with loads \(y_j=0\). At current loads, choose \(B_t(y)=\operatorname{lexmin}\arg\max_{B\subseteq M}\{v_t(B)-\sum_{j\in B}f'_{j,+}(y_j)\}\); lexicographic order uses incidence vectors in item order, with \(0<1\).
While processing type \(t\), an infinitesimal mass \(ds\) receives \(B_t(y)\), increases each selected load by \(ds\), and pays density \(p(s)=\sum_{j\in B_t(y)}f'_{j,+}(y_j)\). Reevaluate choices as loads change, using the right slope at breakpoints; at \(1\), use the last segment's slope. Let \(x_{t,B}\) be the total mass of type \(t\) receiving \(B\).
For any feasible allocation, define \(y_j=\sum_{t,B:j\in B}x_{t,B}\) and \(\pi_\infty(x)=\sum_j f_j(y_j)+\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}[v_t(M)-v_t(B)]\). Let \(\pi_\infty^*=\min\{\pi_\infty(x):x_{t,B}\ge0,\ \sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\}\).

Does this continuous sequential mechanism always satisfy \(\pi_\infty(x)\le\alpha\pi_\infty^*\), or can this approximation guarantee fail?
Can its bundle masses and payment density be computed exactly by an event-driven implementation in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\)?

For finite agents, the sequential mechanism is budget balanced and \(\alpha\)-approximate for nondecreasing subadditive item costs satisfying \(\alpha c_j(S)/|S|\ge\max_{i\in S}c_j(\{i\})\) for every nonempty \(S\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06384).
No result settling this exact continuous mechanism was located in the literature search.

Result

The guarantee always holds under the stated hypotheses:

\(\displaystyle \pi_\infty(x)\le \alpha\pi_\infty^*.\)

Moreover, the bundle masses and payment density can be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\). The payment density has a representation with at most \(\tau+K-m\) constant intervals.

The proof below treats the continuous mechanism directly. It does not require a limiting argument from the [finite-agent result, Theorem 4.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06384).

1. Exact event construction

Relabel the types according to their prescribed order. Parameterize processing by cumulative mass \(s\in[0,1]\); type \(t\) occupies an interval of length \(\mu_t\).

Write the cost representation for item \(j\) as breakpoints

\(0=b_{j,0}<b_{j,1}<\cdots<b_{j,k_j}=1,\)

with slope \(a_{j,r}\) on the interval from \(b_{j,r-1}\) to \(b_{j,r}\). Thus \(\sum_j k_j=K\). Concavity and monotonicity give

\(a_{j,1}\ge a_{j,2}\ge\cdots\ge a_{j,k_j}\ge0.\)

At an internal breakpoint, use the slope of the segment beginning there, exactly as the question specifies.

Maintain the current loads \(y\), current type \(t\), and its remaining mass \(r>0\). One iteration is:

  • Compute the current prices \(q_j=f'_{j,+}(y_j)\), using the last slope at \(y_j=1\).
  • Enumerate the explicit valuation table and choose

\(B=\operatorname{lexmin}\arg\max_{C\subseteq M}\{v_t(C)-\sum_{j\in C}q_j\}\).

  • For each \(j\in B\), let \(b_j\) be its next breakpoint strictly greater than \(y_j\), including the endpoint \(1\). Set

\(\displaystyle \delta=\min\bigl(\{r\}\cup\{b_j-y_j:j\in B\}\bigr)\).
If \(B=\varnothing\), this means \(\delta=r\).

  • Assign mass \(\delta\) to \(B\), record payment density \(\sum_{j\in B}q_j\) on that interval, increase every selected load by \(\delta\), and decrease \(r\) by \(\delta\).
  • If the cohort has ended, advance to the next type. Otherwise, recompute prices and the bundle.

Before all mass has been processed, every load satisfies \(y_j\le s<1\). Consequently, the next breakpoint in step 3 exists and \(\delta>0\).

Correctness and event count

On an interval of length \(\delta\), no selected item reaches a breakpoint before its endpoint. Unselected loads remain constant. Therefore all prices remain constant throughout the interval, so the lexicographically selected maximizing bundle remains \(B\). Each selected load consequently increases at rate one, as required.

At the endpoint, simultaneously reached breakpoints are handled together using their right slopes. The construction therefore implements the prescribed reevaluation rule exactly.

Every iteration ends either a cohort or reaches an internal cost breakpoint. Loads are nondecreasing, so each internal breakpoint can be reached at most once. There are \(\tau\) cohort endings and

\(\displaystyle \sum_j(k_j-1)=K-m\)

internal breakpoints. Hence there are at most \(\tau+K-m\) positive-length allocation intervals.

The recorded half-open intervals specify the density at every processing time \(s<1\), with the newly computed bundle used at each event. If a terminal density at \(s=1\) is desired, one additional bundle computation using the prescribed terminal slopes supplies it; it affects no allocation mass or payment integral.

2. Exact budget balance

Let \(B(s)\) denote the assigned bundle and \(y_j^{\mathrm{fin}}\) the final load. Define total payments by

\(\displaystyle P=\int_0^1 p(s)\,ds.\)

For any recorded interval of length \(\delta\), a selected item \(j\) remains in one linear cost segment up to the interval’s endpoint. Continuity of \(f_j\) therefore gives

\(\displaystyle f_j(y_j+\delta)-f_j(y_j)=\delta q_j.\)

Unselected items contribute zero to both sides. Summing over items and then over all recorded intervals telescopes:

\(\displaystyle P=\sum_j\bigl(f_j(y_j^{\mathrm{fin}})-f_j(0)\bigr)=\sum_j f_j(y_j^{\mathrm{fin}}).\)

Thus the continuous mechanism is exactly budget balanced.

Define the loss density for type \(t\) by

\(\ell_t(C)=v_t(M)-v_t(C).\)

Since valuations are nondecreasing, \(\ell_t(C)\ge0\). Budget balance yields

\(\displaystyle \pi_\infty(x)=P+\sum_{t,C}x_{t,C}\ell_t(C).\)

3. Approximation proof

Let \(a_j=f'_{j,+}(0)\). Concavity implies that every price offered by the mechanism satisfies

\(0\le q_j(s)\le a_j.\)

Fix any feasible comparison allocation \(\bar x\), and write its loads as

\(\displaystyle \bar y_j=\sum_{t,C:j\in C}\bar x_{t,C}.\)

At any processing time belonging to type \(t\), utility maximization gives, for every bundle \(C\),

\(\displaystyle v_t(B(s))-\sum_{j\in B(s)}q_j(s)\ge v_t(C)-\sum_{j\in C}q_j(s).\)

Rearranging and then bounding the comparison prices by their initial slopes gives

\(\displaystyle \ell_t(B(s))+p(s)\le \ell_t(C)+\sum_{j\in C}q_j(s)\le \ell_t(C)+\sum_{j\in C}a_j.\)

Because \(\mu_t>0\), the numbers \(\bar x_{t,C}/\mu_t\) form a probability distribution over bundles. Average the preceding inequality against this distribution, and integrate over the cohort’s interval of length \(\mu_t\). The result is

\(\displaystyle \int_{\text{cohort }t}\bigl(\ell_t(B(s))+p(s)\bigr)\,ds \le \sum_C\bar x_{t,C}\ell_t(C)+\sum_C\bar x_{t,C}\sum_{j\in C}a_j.\)

Summing over types and using budget balance proves the comparison inequality

\(\displaystyle \pi_\infty(x)\le \sum_{t,C}\bar x_{t,C}\ell_t(C)+\sum_j a_j\bar y_j.\)

For \(\bar y_j>0\), the assumed average-cost condition gives

\(\displaystyle a_j\bar y_j\le\alpha f_j(\bar y_j).\)

For \(\bar y_j=0\), the same inequality holds because both sides are zero. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \pi_\infty(x)\le \sum_{t,C}\bar x_{t,C}\ell_t(C)+\alpha\sum_j f_j(\bar y_j)\le\alpha\pi_\infty(\bar x).\)

The last inequality uses \(\alpha\ge1\) and nonnegative losses.

Finally, the feasible allocation set is a nonempty, compact product of finite-dimensional simplices, and the objective is continuous. Hence a minimizing allocation exists. Choosing it as \(\bar x\) establishes

\(\displaystyle \pi_\infty(x)\le\alpha\pi_\infty^*.\)

This argument permits arbitrary cohort order and arbitrary nondecreasing valuation tables.

4. Polynomial bit complexity

The finite event bound alone does not establish polynomial running time; exact arithmetic also needs bounded bit length.

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses and breakpoint coordinates, represented as ordinary binary rationals. Their total encoding length is at most \(L\), so \(\log_2D=O(L)\).

Initially, all loads and processed masses are multiples of \(1/D\). Every event duration is either a remaining cohort mass or a difference between an input breakpoint and a current load. Updates use only addition and subtraction. Inductively, all event times, durations, loads, remaining masses, and output bundle masses are multiples of \(1/D\).

These quantities lie in \([0,1]\). Their numerators over denominator \(D\) therefore have \(O(L)\) bits.

Each slope is computed exactly as

\(\displaystyle a_{j,r}=\frac{f_j(b_{j,r})-f_j(b_{j,r-1})}{b_{j,r}-b_{j,r-1}}.\)

Its numerator and denominator have \(O(L)\) bits. Each bundle price sums at most \(m\) such slopes, so exact prices and utilities have \(O(mL)\)-bit representations. Rational comparisons, including exact tie detection, therefore take polynomial time.

There are at most \(\tau+K-m\) iterations. Each bundle computation uses at most \(O(m2^m)\) rational arithmetic operations. Although this contains \(2^m\), every type’s valuation table explicitly contains all \(2^m\) entries. In particular, \(2^m\le O(L)\), so this enumeration is polynomial in the stated input length.

The algorithm consequently runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\). It can output either all bundle masses or only their nonzero entries, together with an exact rational interval representation of the payment density.

5. The approximation factor can be attained

For completeness, the factor cannot generally be improved for this mechanism.

Take one item, one type of mass \(1\), and

\(v(\varnothing)=0,\qquad v(\{1\})=1,\qquad f(z)=\min\{z,1/\alpha\}.\)

For every admissible \(\alpha\ge1\), this cost satisfies all hypotheses. Its initial slope is \(1\), and

\(\displaystyle \alpha\frac{f(z)}z=\min\{\alpha,1/z\}\ge1.\)

Initially both bundles have utility zero. Lexicographic tie-breaking selects the empty bundle. The load therefore remains zero throughout, and the mechanism’s social cost is \(1\).

A feasible allocation is determined by the served mass \(z\). Its social cost is

\(\displaystyle \min\{z,1/\alpha\}+1-z.\)

For \(z\le1/\alpha\), this equals \(1\). For \(z\ge1/\alpha\), it equals \(1/\alpha+1-z\), whose minimum is \(1/\alpha\), attained at \(z=1\). Thus

\(\displaystyle \pi_\infty(x)=\alpha\pi_\infty^*.\)
