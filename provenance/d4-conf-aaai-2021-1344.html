The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite seed and recipient type sets \(E\) and \(R\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), a directed acyclic contact graph with rational rates \(\beta_{st}\), recipient communities \(\mathcal C\), and a rational seed-mass budget \(\kappa\), choose \(x_s\in[0,1]\) with \(\sum_{s\in E}\mu_s x_s\le\kappa\). Set \(r_s=x_s\) for seed types and, in topological order, \(r_t=1-\exp(-\sum_{s:(s,t)\in A}\mu_s\beta_{st}r_s)\) for downstream types. What is the maximum of \(\min_{C\in\mathcal C}\frac{\sum_{t\in C}\mu_t r_t}{\sum_{t\in C}\mu_t}\), or an additive-\(\epsilon\) approximation to it?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: disjoint nonempty seed and recipient type sets \(E,R\), with \(\tau=|E\cup R|\) positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
The input includes a directed acyclic graph \((E\cup R,A)\) with no arcs entering \(E\), nonnegative binary-rational contact rates \(\beta_{st}\), and an explicitly listed family \(\mathcal C\) of \(m\ge1\) nonempty subsets of \(R\), possibly overlapping.
The binary-rational budget satisfies \(0<\kappa\le\sum_{s\in E}\mu_s\). Feasible allocations satisfy \(x_s\in[0,1]\) and \(\sum_{s\in E}\mu_sx_s\le\kappa\); only seed types can receive allocations.
Set \(r_s=x_s\) for \(s\in E\), and in topological order set \(r_t=1-\exp(-\sum_{s:(s,t)\in A}\mu_s\beta_{st}r_s)\) for \(t\in R\), with an empty sum equal to \(0\).
Define \(F(x)=\min_{C\in\mathcal C}\frac{\sum_{t\in C}\mu_tr_t}{\sum_{t\in C}\mu_t}\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_xF(x)\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including a rational accuracy parameter \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\); computation uses the Turing bit model.

For each fixed \(\eta\in(0,1/e)\), can a feasible rational allocation satisfying \(F(x)\ge(1-1/e+\eta)\mathrm{OPT}-\epsilon\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\), or is obtaining this guarantee NP-hard?

For finite agents under Independent Cascade diffusion, independent seed selection with an expected cardinality budget has no polynomial-time multiplicative \((1-1/e+\eta)\)-approximation unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\), even on bipartite graphs with one recipient community; a randomized \((1-1/e)\)-approximation with arbitrarily small additive loss is known. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.03438)

  • Separated the fixed multiplicative improvement eta from the input additive accuracy epsilon, and specified rational outputs and bit-model running time to accommodate exponential activation values.

1. Result

The requested guarantee is computable in polynomial time. In fact, the specified mass recurrence admits a deterministic fully polynomial approximation scheme: a feasible rational allocation satisfying

\(F(x)\ge (1-\epsilon/2)\mathrm{OPT}\)

can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\).

The proof constructs a rational linear program. Its key ingredient is a multiplicative approximation to the scalar response function; this prevents large contact rates from amplifying approximation errors.

2. Properties of the recurrence

Write \(a_{st}=\mu_s\beta_{st}\ge0\), and define

\(h(z)=1-\exp(-z)\qquad(z\ge0).\)

The function \(h\) is increasing and concave, satisfies \(h(0)=0\), and takes values in \([0,1]\). Consequently, for \(c\in[0,1]\),

\(h(cz)\ge c h(z).\)

Indeed, concavity applied to \(cz=c z+(1-c)0\) gives this inequality.

The recurrence also makes every \(r_t(x)\) concave in the seed allocation. Seed responses are affine. Inductively, a nonnegative linear combination of predecessor responses is concave, and composition with the increasing concave function \(h\) preserves concavity. Thus every community average is concave, as is their pointwise minimum \(F\).

The feasible allocation set is nonempty and compact, and \(F\) is continuous. Therefore an optimal allocation \(x^*\) exists. Also,

\(0\le\mathrm{OPT}\le1.\)

Concavity alone does not establish the required bit complexity. The following explicit approximation supplies that part.

3. A rational approximation to the scalar response

Choose the smallest positive integer \(k\) such that

\(d=2^{-k}\le\epsilon/(4\tau),\)

and set

\(T=k,\qquad N=T/d=k2^k,\qquad \lambda=1-2d.\)

Since \(\tau\ge2\) and \(\epsilon<1\), we have \(d<1/8\) and \(0<\lambda<1\). Minimality of \(k\) gives

\(\epsilon/(8\tau)<d\le\epsilon/(4\tau).\)

We will construct a concave piecewise affine function \(\phi\), with rational coefficients, satisfying

\(\lambda h(z)\le\phi(z)\le h(z)\qquad\text{for every }z\ge0.\)

Rational affine upper bounds

For \(j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\), put \(q_j=jd\). Define

\(w=\dfrac{d^2}{4(1+2T)}.\)

Compute rational bounds \(\underline e_j,\overline e_j\) satisfying

\(0\le\underline e_j\le\exp(-q_j)\le\overline e_j\le1,\qquad \overline e_j-\underline e_j\le w.\)

A polynomial-time construction of these bounds is given in Section 6.

Set

\(A_j=\overline e_j,\qquad B_j=1-(1+q_j)\underline e_j,\)

and define

\(Q(z)=\min\bigl\{z,\ 1,\ A_jz+B_j:\ 1\le j\le N\bigr\},\qquad \phi(z)=\lambda Q(z).\)

The exact tangent to \(h\) at \(q_j\) is

\(\mathcal T_j(z)=\exp(-q_j)z+1-(1+q_j)\exp(-q_j).\)

Concavity gives \(\mathcal T_j(z)\ge h(z)\). For \(z\ge0\), the enclosure bounds imply

\(A_jz+B_j-\mathcal T_j(z) =(\overline e_j-\exp(-q_j))z +(1+q_j)(\exp(-q_j)-\underline e_j)\ge0.\)

Moreover, \(z\ge h(z)\) and \(1\ge h(z)\). Hence

\(Q(z)\ge h(z),\)

which proves \(\phi(z)\ge\lambda h(z)\).

Proof that \(\phi\) lies below \(h\)

There are three ranges.

For \(0\le z\le d\): Taylor’s theorem gives

\(h(z)\ge z-z^2/2\ge(1-d/2)z.\)

Since \(Q(z)\le z\) and \(\lambda=1-2d\le1-d/2\),

\(\phi(z)\le\lambda z\le h(z).\)

For \(d\le z\le T\): choose \(q_j=d\lceil z/d\rceil\). Then \(z\le q_j\le T\) and \(q_j-z\le d\). Because \(\lvert h''(u)\rvert=\exp(-u)\le1\) for \(u\ge0\), Taylor’s theorem yields

\(0\le\mathcal T_j(z)-h(z)\le d^2/2.\)

The rational coefficients contribute at most

\(A_jz+B_j-\mathcal T_j(z) \le w(z+1+q_j)\le w(1+2T)=d^2/4.\)

Therefore

\(Q(z)\le h(z)+3d^2/4.\)

Also, \(h(z)\ge h(d)\ge d-d^2/2\ge d/2\). It follows that

\(Q(z)\le(1+3d/2)h(z)\le(1+2d)h(z).\)

Consequently,

\(\phi(z)\le(1-2d)(1+2d)h(z)\le h(z).\)

For \(z\ge T\): since \(e>2\),

\(\exp(-T)=\exp(-k)\le2^{-k}=d.\)

Thus \(h(z)\ge1-d\), whereas

\(\phi(z)\le\lambda=1-2d\le1-d.\)

This completes the proof of

\(\lambda h(z)\le\phi(z)\le h(z)\qquad(z\ge0).\)

In particular, \(\phi(0)=0\) and \(0\le\phi(z)\le1\). All slopes used in \(Q\) are nonnegative, and the minimum of affine functions is concave, so \(\phi\) is increasing and concave.

4. The linear program

Introduce variables \(y_t\) for all listed types and a variable \(v\). For each recipient define the linear expression

\(z_t(y)=\sum_{s:(s,t)\in A}a_{st}y_s.\)

For each community write \(M_C=\sum_{t\in C}\mu_t>0\).

Maximize \(v\) subject to:

  • \(0\le y_t\le1\) for every type, and \(0\le v\le1\).
  • \(\sum_{s\in E}\mu_s y_s\le\kappa.\)
  • For every \(t\in R\),

\(y_t\le\lambda z_t(y)\), \(y_t\le\lambda\), and
\(y_t\le\lambda(A_jz_t(y)+B_j)\) for every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\).

  • For every \(C\in\mathcal C\),

\(vM_C\le\sum_{t\in C}\mu_t y_t.\)

The recipient constraints are exactly

\(y_t\le\phi(z_t(y)).\)

All constraints have rational coefficients. The zero vector is feasible: indeed, \(B_j\ge0\), because
\((1+q_j)\underline e_j\le(1+q_j)\exp(-q_j)\le1\).
The feasible region is bounded.

Every LP solution gives a valid lower bound

Given any feasible LP solution, output \(x_s=y_s\) for \(s\in E\). Its allocation constraints hold exactly.

Let \(r(x)\) be the true recurrence. We prove \(y_t\le r_t(x)\) in topological order. Equality holds for seeds. Assuming the inequality for predecessors, nonnegativity of the coefficients gives

\(z_t(y)\le\sum_{s:(s,t)\in A}a_{st}r_s(x).\)

Using the LP constraints, \(\phi\le h\), and monotonicity of \(h\),

\(y_t\le\phi(z_t(y)) \le h(z_t(y)) \le h\!\left(\sum_{s:(s,t)\in A}a_{st}r_s(x)\right) =r_t(x).\)

The community constraints now imply \(v\le F(x)\). Thus an optimal LP solution produces a feasible allocation whose true value is at least the LP optimum.

5. The LP loses only a multiplicative factor per recipient

Let \(n=|R|\), and list recipients in topological order as \(t_1,\ldots,t_n\). Starting from an optimal true allocation \(x^*\), define

\(\widetilde y_s=x_s^*\qquad(s\in E),\)

and recursively set

\(\widetilde y_{t_i}=\phi\!\left(\sum_{s:(s,t_i)\in A}a_{st_i}\widetilde y_s\right).\)

Together with the minimum community average of \(\widetilde y\) as its value of \(v\), this is an LP-feasible point.

We claim that

\(\widetilde y_{t_i}\ge\lambda^i r_{t_i}(x^*)\qquad(1\le i\le n).\)

Suppose the claim holds for earlier recipients. Every predecessor \(s\) of \(t_i\) satisfies

\(\widetilde y_s\ge\lambda^{i-1}r_s(x^*).\)

For seed predecessors this follows from equality and \(\lambda^{i-1}\le1\). For recipient predecessors \(t_j\), it follows from \(j\le i-1\), the induction hypothesis, and \(\lambda^j\ge\lambda^{i-1}\). Therefore, writing
\(Z_i=\sum_{s:(s,t_i)\in A}a_{st_i}r_s(x^*)\), we obtain

\(\widetilde y_{t_i} \ge\lambda h\!\left(\lambda^{i-1}Z_i\right) \ge\lambda^i h(Z_i) =\lambda^i r_{t_i}(x^*).\)

The first inequality uses \(\phi\ge\lambda h\) and monotonicity; the second uses \(h(cz)\ge c h(z)\). The same argument covers an empty predecessor set, whose sum is zero.

Since \(i\le n\), every recipient satisfies
\(\widetilde y_{t_i}\ge\lambda^n r_{t_i}(x^*)\). Taking community averages and then their minimum shows that the LP optimum is at least

\(\lambda^n\mathrm{OPT}.\)

Consequently, the allocation extracted from an optimal LP solution satisfies

\(F(x)\ge\lambda^n\mathrm{OPT}.\)

Bernoulli’s inequality and the choice of \(d\) give

\(\lambda^n=(1-2d)^n\ge1-2dn \ge1-\epsilon/2,\)

because \(n\le\tau\). This proves the advertised multiplicative guarantee.

6. Turing-model complexity and rational output

First,

\(k=O(\log(\tau/\epsilon)),\qquad N=k2^k<\dfrac{8k\tau}{\epsilon}.\)

Thus the number of affine pieces is polynomial in \(\tau,1/\epsilon\).

Here is an explicit construction of the exponential enclosures. Choose an integer \(p\) satisfying

\(p\ge6T,\qquad 2^{1-p}\le w,\)

with \(p=O(T+\log(1/w))=O(k)\). For each \(q_j\), compute the rational Taylor sum

\(S_j=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}\dfrac{(-q_j)^\ell}{\ell!}.\)

Taylor’s remainder bound gives

\(\lvert S_j-\exp(-q_j)\rvert\le q_j^p/p!.\)

The factorial bound \(p!\ge(p/e)^p\), together with \(q_j\le T\), \(p\ge6T\), and \(e<3\), implies

\(q_j^p/p!\le(eT/p)^p\le2^{-p}.\)

For completeness, the factorial bound follows from
\(\log(p!)\ge\int_1^p\log u\,du\ge p\log p-p\).

Therefore we may take

\(\underline e_j=\max\{0,S_j-2^{-p}\},\qquad \overline e_j=\min\{1,S_j+2^{-p}\}.\)

These enclose the true exponential and have width at most \(2^{1-p}\le w\).

Each \(q_j\) has denominator \(2^k\). A common denominator for its Taylor sum is
\(2^{k(p-1)}(p-1)!\), whose binary length is \(O(kp+p\log p)\). Numerator lengths are polynomial as well: the sum of the absolute Taylor terms is at most \(\exp(q_j)\le\exp(T)\). Hence every enclosure and affine coefficient is computable by rational arithmetic in polynomial time and has polynomial binary length.

The LP has \(\tau+1\) variables and
\(O(\tau(N+1)+m)\) constraints. Expanding their linear expressions gives polynomial total encoding length. Contact rates of large numerical magnitude enter only through the input rational coefficients \(a_{st}\); the approximation grid does not depend on their magnitudes.

We now apply the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a feasible bounded LP with rational data can be optimized exactly, returning a rational optimal solution, in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. This is the polynomial-time linear-programming result initiated by [Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319). The hypotheses hold here because the LP is explicitly rational, nonempty, and bounded.

Its seed coordinates therefore give the required rational allocation, with exact budget feasibility, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\).

7. Requested factor and comparison with Independent Cascade

For the stipulated \(\eta\), put \(\alpha=1-1/e+\eta<1\). Since \(\mathrm{OPT}\le1\),

\(F(x)\ge(1-\epsilon/2)\mathrm{OPT} \ge\mathrm{OPT}-\epsilon/2 \ge\alpha\mathrm{OPT}-\epsilon.\)

Thus the requested approximation guarantee is polynomial-time computable.

The [cited Independent Cascade result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.03438) concerns a different response function. For example, with two independently selected seeds and deterministic edges to one recipient, its activation probability is

\(f(x_1,x_2)=1-(1-x_1)(1-x_2).\)

This function is not concave: its values at \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) are both \(1\), while its value at their midpoint is \(3/4\). In the specified mass recurrence, the corresponding response is \(h(a_1x_1+a_2x_2)\), which is concave and admits the rational approximation proved above. The Independent Cascade hardness statement therefore does not contradict this algorithm.
