The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(P\), a horizon \(\ell\), finitely many approval-schedule types \(\theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\), and a rational threshold \(B\), does there exist an outcome \(\mathbf{o}\in P^\ell\) with \(\util_\mu(\mathbf{o})=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta\mathbf{1}[o_r\in A_{\theta,r}]\ge B\), such that for every nonzero coalition mass vector \(\mathbf{a}\) with \(0\le a_\theta\le\mu_\theta\) and every round set \(R\subseteq[\ell]\) on which all types with \(a_\theta>0\) have a common approved candidate in each round, the number of rounds in \(R\) whose chosen candidate is approved by positive coalition mass is at least \(\left\lfloor |R|\sum_{\theta\in T}a_\theta\right\rfloor\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P\) contain \(m\ge1\) candidates and let \(\ell\ge1\) be an integer horizon. A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\) distinct approval-schedule types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each schedule explicitly lists \(A_{\theta,r}\subseteq P\) for every round; empty approval sets are allowed. The threshold is \(B\in\mathbb Q\), encoded in binary, and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
An outcome is \(\mathbf o\in P^\ell\); candidates may repeat. Its welfare is \(\operatorname{Util}_\mu(\mathbf o)=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta\mathbf1[o_r\in A_{\theta,r}]\).
A coalition is a nonzero real vector \(\mathbf a\) with \(0\le a_\theta\le\mu_\theta\); write \(U(\mathbf a)=\{\theta:a_\theta>0\}\). It agrees on a nonempty round set \(R\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\ell\}\) when \(\bigcap_{\theta\in U(\mathbf a)}A_{\theta,r}\ne\varnothing\) for every \(r\in R\).
An outcome satisfies mass proportional justified representation when every such coalition and agreement set obey \(\left|\{r\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}:o_r\in\bigcup_{\theta\in U(\mathbf a)}A_{\theta,r}\}\right|\ge\left\lfloor |R|\sum_{\theta\in T}a_\theta\right\rfloor\).
Is deciding whether an outcome satisfying mass proportional justified representation has \(\operatorname{Util}_\mu(\mathbf o)\ge B\) solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,\ell,L\).
For finite voters, welfare-threshold feasibility under proportional justified representation is NP-complete even with static preferences and at most four approvers per candidate. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11157v1)

Conclusion

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with static schedules and at most four supporting types per candidate. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,\ell,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The key point is that allowing fractional coalitions does not strengthen the constraints beyond those imposed by taking the full mass of each included type.

1. Eliminating fractional coalitions

For every nonempty \(U\subseteq T\), define:

  • \(M(U)=\sum_{\theta\in U}\mu_\theta\);
  • \(G(U)=\{r:\bigcap_{\theta\in U}A_{\theta,r}\ne\varnothing\}\) and \(h(U)=|G(U)|\);
  • \(s_{\mathbf o}(U)=|\{r:o_r\in\bigcup_{\theta\in U}A_{\theta,r}\}|\).

Lemma. An outcome satisfies mass proportional justified representation if and only if, for every nonempty \(U\subseteq T\),
\[ s_{\mathbf o}(U)\ge \lfloor h(U)M(U)\rfloor. \]

Proof. Suppose first that the outcome satisfies the stated axiom. If \(h(U)=0\), the displayed inequality is automatic. Otherwise, take the coalition \(a_\theta=\mu_\theta\) for \(\theta\in U\), with all other coordinates zero, and take \(R=G(U)\). This coalition is nonzero, has support \(U\), and agrees on \(R\). The axiom gives the displayed inequality.

Conversely, suppose all displayed inequalities hold. Consider any allowed coalition \(\mathbf a\), let \(U=U(\mathbf a)\), and let \(R\) be any nonempty agreement set. Then \(R\subseteq G(U)\) and \(\sum_\theta a_\theta\le M(U)\). Since all quantities are nonnegative,
\[ \left\lfloor |R|\sum_\theta a_\theta\right\rfloor \le \lfloor h(U)M(U)\rfloor \le s_{\mathbf o}(U). \]
This is exactly the required constraint. \(\square\)

2. Exact reduction from finite voters

We use the finite-voter hardness result given in the question: welfare-threshold feasibility under temporal PJR is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard with static preferences and at most four approvers per candidate. This is [Theorem 4.3 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11157v1#Thmtheorem4.3).

Consider such a finite election with \(n\) explicitly listed voters and welfare threshold \(C\). Partition the voters into classes \(N_\theta\) having identical approval schedules. Construct the mass instance by setting
\[ \mu_\theta=\frac{|N_\theta|}{n}, \qquad B=\frac{C}{n}, \]
and retaining one copy of each distinct schedule.

These masses are positive binary-encoded rationals summing to one, and the resulting support size satisfies \(\tau\le n\).

Representation equivalence

Suppose an outcome satisfies finite-voter PJR. Fix a nonempty \(U\subseteq T\), and take the finite coalition
\[ S_U=\bigcup_{\theta\in U}N_\theta. \]
Its agreement rounds are exactly \(G(U)\), its represented-round count is \(s_{\mathbf o}(U)\), and \(|S_U|/n=M(U)\). Finite-voter PJR therefore gives
\[ s_{\mathbf o}(U)\ge\lfloor h(U)M(U)\rfloor. \]
By the lemma, the outcome satisfies mass PJR.

For the reverse direction, suppose the outcome satisfies mass PJR. Take any nonempty finite voter coalition \(S\) and any nonempty agreement set \(R\). Let
\[ U=\{\theta:S\cap N_\theta\ne\varnothing\}. \]
Identical schedules within each class imply that the union and intersection of approvals for \(S\) equal those for \(U\), in every round. Consequently, \(R\subseteq G(U)\), the represented-round count for \(S\) is \(s_{\mathbf o}(U)\), and
\[ \frac{|S|}{n}\le\frac{\sum_{\theta\in U}|N_\theta|}{n}=M(U). \]
Hence
\[ s_{\mathbf o}(U) \ge\lfloor h(U)M(U)\rfloor \ge\left\lfloor |R|\frac{|S|}{n}\right\rfloor. \]
Thus the outcome satisfies finite-voter PJR.

Welfare equivalence

For every outcome,
\[ \operatorname{Util}_\mu(\mathbf o) =\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{Util}_{\mathrm{finite}}(\mathbf o). \]
Therefore the mass welfare threshold is met exactly when the finite welfare threshold is met.

Encoding and restrictions

Grouping explicitly listed voters, computing the rational masses, and dividing the threshold by \(n\) take polynomial time in the finite input length. Every mass uses \(O(\log n)\) bits. The cited hardness construction has polynomially bounded horizon, so explicitly writing every round remains polynomial even if the source uses a compact static encoding.

Static schedules remain static. Moreover, every type approving a candidate contains at least one finite voter approving that candidate. Thus a candidate with at most four finite approvers has at most four supporting types after aggregation.

The reduction preserves the answer exactly and is polynomial, proving the claimed \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

3. Running times and upper bounds

All computations can use exact rational arithmetic. For example, multiplying the input denominators gives a common denominator with \(O(L)\) bits; mass sums, welfare comparisons, and floor computations then take polynomial bit time.

For a specified outcome, the lemma gives a verification algorithm: enumerate the \(2^\tau-1\) nonempty supports \(U\), compute their agreement and represented-round counts, and check their inequalities. A conservative bit-operation bound is
\[ O\!\left(2^\tau m\tau\ell L^2\right). \]
Enumerating all \(m^\ell\) outcomes consequently decides the problem in
\[ O\!\left(m^\ell 2^\tau m\tau\ell L^2\right) \]
bit operations.

For static schedules with at most four supporting types per candidate, verification is polynomial. Indeed, write \(S_c=\{\theta:c\in A_\theta\}\). Every support with positive agreement count is a nonempty subset of some \(S_c\), and its agreement count is \(\ell\). There are at most \(2^4-1=15\) subsets to check per candidate. Each check scans the outcome and uses at most four masses. Including input processing, a conservative verification bound is
\[ O\!\left((L+m\tau+15m\ell)L^2\right). \]
Since the explicitly listed horizon makes an outcome a polynomial-size certificate, this restricted mass problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

For unrestricted schedules, a violating support \(U\) is a polynomial-size certificate of failure of a specified outcome. Thus the unrestricted decision problem also has the upper bound \(\Sigma_2^p\). The hardness proof above already settles the requested polynomial-time-versus-hardness question.
