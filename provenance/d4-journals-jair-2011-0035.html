The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational weights \(w_t\), a quota rate \(q\), and a designated type \(t^\star\) with \(\mu_{t^\star}>0\), decide whether there exist rational \(0<\alpha\le\mu_{t^\star}\) and \(u,v>0\) with \(u+v=w_{t^\star}\) such that replacing mass \(\alpha\) of \(t^\star\) by mass \(\alpha\) of each of two labelled identity types of weights \(u\) and \(v\) makes their limiting total normalized Banzhaf power exceed \((\alpha/\mu_{t^\star})\) times the original limiting Banzhaf power of type \(t^\star\). The limit is taken over denominator-compatible \(N\)-player games with \(N\mu_t\) copies and quota \(Nq\), with the post-split finite measure of total mass \(1+\alpha\) normalized together with its quota.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Input comprises these masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), binary rational weights \(w_t\ge0\), a binary rational quota rate \(0<q\le\sum_t\mu_tw_t\), and a designated supported type \(t^\star\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length and \(m=2\) the fixed number of new identities; express running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
For any rational finite type measure \(\nu\), let \(G_N(\nu,q)\) have \(N\nu_t\) distinct players of weight \(w_t\) for each supported label \(t\), and quota \(Nq\); coalitions win when their weight meets or exceeds the quota. Take \(N\to\infty\) through all positive integers for which the counts and \(Nq\) are integral. Write \(B_A(\nu,q)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{i:\operatorname{type}(i)\in A}\beta_i(G_N(\nu,q))\), where \(\beta_i\) is the normalized Banzhaf index and \(A\) is a set of labels.
A split chooses rational \(\alpha,u,v\) with \(0<\alpha\le\mu_{t^\star}\), \(u,v>0\), and \(u+v=w_{t^\star}\). It reduces the mass of \(t^\star\) by \(\alpha\) and adds two fresh labels \(a,b\), each of mass \(\alpha\), with weights \(u,v\); all other masses and weights remain unchanged. Call the resulting measure \(\nu\), omitting zero masses. Its total mass is \(1+\alpha\); normalizing it means replacing \((\nu,q)\) by \((\nu/(1+\alpha),q/(1+\alpha))\), equivalently retaining the games just defined.
The decision problem asks whether such a split exists for which both relevant limits exist and \(B_{\{a,b\}}(\nu,q)>(\alpha/\mu_{t^\star})B_{\{t^\star\}}(\mu,q)\).

Is this continuous beneficial two-identity split problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

The discrete decision problem for normalized Banzhaf power is NP-hard even when splitting is restricted to two equal positive integer weights; the hardness also survives allowing fractional splits. [Aziz, Bachrach, Elkind and Paterson](https://www.dcs.warwick.ac.uk/~msp/papers/false_name.pdf).
For growing games with finitely many positive integer weight values, Lindner and Machover establish asymptotic proportionality of Banzhaf power to weight at half-total-weight quota under a gcd condition. This concerns power limits, rather than the split-existence decision problem. [Lindner and Machover](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/74105380/L). No result settling the decision problem defined here was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative weights and a positive quota no greater than total societal weight, retaining zero-weight dummy types and the grand-coalition-winning condition.
  • Required the split weights, as well as the split mass, to be rational.
  • Interpreted limiting power of a type as the aggregate normalized Banzhaf power of its copies, and kept new identity labels distinct even when weights coincide.
  • Made quota normalization explicit: divide both the post-split measure and its quota rate by its total mass, preserving the finite games after reindexing population size.
  • Specified the full denominator-compatible sequence and required existence of both compared limits for a split to qualify.
  • Used the number of new identities as the parameter m, fixed at two, and defined L as total input bit length.

Result

The problem is in $\mathrm{P}$. More precisely, let
$$ W=\sum_t\mu_tw_t. $$
A beneficial split exists if and only if
$$ w_{t^\star}>0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 2q\ne W. $$

When the answer is yes, two equal weights suffice. The freedom to choose an arbitrarily small positive rational split mass $\alpha$ is essential to the proof.

The decision algorithm uses $O((\tau+m)L^2)$ bit operations with elementary integer arithmetic, where $m=2$. It does not need to calculate any transcendental quantities.

1. The limiting Banzhaf formula

We first prove existence and identify the limits for every measure needed in the problem.

Let $\nu$ be a finite rational measure with positive supported masses, nonnegative rational weights, and
$$ W_\nu=\sum_t\nu_tw_t>0. $$
Its total mass need not equal one.

Suppose first that
$$ 0<q<W_\nu. $$
Define
$$ F_\nu(\theta) = \sum_t\nu_tw_t\frac{e^{\theta w_t}}{1+e^{\theta w_t}}. $$
This function is continuous, with limits $0$ and $W_\nu$ at negative and positive infinity, respectively. Moreover,
$$ F_\nu'(\theta) = \sum_{t:w_t>0} \nu_tw_t^2 \frac{e^{\theta w_t}}{(1+e^{\theta w_t})^2} >0. $$
Consequently, there is a unique real $\theta$ satisfying
$$ F_\nu(\theta)=q. $$
Since $F_\nu(0)=W_\nu/2$, this parameter is zero exactly at half-total-weight quota.

We will prove that, for every set of labels $A$,
$$ B_A(\nu,q) = \begin{cases} \displaystyle \frac{\sum_{t\in A}\nu_tw_t}{W_\nu}, & q=W_\nu/2,\\[10pt] \displaystyle \frac{\sum_{t\in A}\nu_t\tanh(\lambda w_t)} {\sum_t\nu_t\tanh(\lambda w_t)}, & q\ne W_\nu/2, \end{cases} $$
where
$$ \lambda=\frac{|\theta|}{2}>0 $$
in the second case. Unsupported labels contribute zero.

A lattice estimate

Because the positive weights are rational, there is a rational $h>0$ such that
$$ w_t=hk_t, \qquad k_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad \gcd\{k_t:w_t>0\}=1. $$

We need the following estimate. Fix probabilities $p_t\in(0,1)$ for the positive-weight labels. Let $S_N$ be the sum of independent Bernoulli variables, with $N\nu_t-d_t$ variables of weight $k_t$ for label $t$, where the nonnegative integers $d_t$ are fixed. Write
$$ a_N=\mathbb E S_N, \qquad V=\sum_{t:w_t>0}\nu_tk_t^2p_t(1-p_t)>0. $$
For integer $\ell_N$ satisfying $\ell_N-a_N=O(1)$,
$$ \Pr(S_N=\ell_N) = \frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi NV}}. $$
The estimate is uniform when the offsets remain in a fixed bounded interval.

Here is a proof, including the lattice condition. Let $\phi_N(x)$ be the characteristic function of $S_N-a_N$. Fourier inversion gives
$$ \Pr(S_N=\ell_N) = \frac1{2\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{-ix(\ell_N-a_N)}\phi_N(x)\,dx. $$
For each label,
$$ \left|1-p_t+p_te^{ik_tx}\right|^2 = 1-4p_t(1-p_t)\sin^2(k_tx/2). $$
All these factors have modulus one simultaneously only when every $k_tx$ is an integer multiple of $2\pi$. The gcd assumption implies that, on $[-\pi,\pi]$, this occurs only at $x=0$.

It follows by compactness, and by $N\nu_t-d_t\ge N\nu_t/2$ for sufficiently large $N$, that outside any fixed neighborhood of zero,
$$ |\phi_N(x)|\le \rho^N $$
for some $\rho<1$. In a sufficiently small neighborhood of zero, the displayed modulus formula also gives
$$ |\phi_N(x)|\le e^{-cNx^2} $$
for some $c>0$. Finally, expanding the logarithms of the centered characteristic factors yields
$$ \log\phi_N(x) = -\frac12\bigl(NV+O(1)\bigr)x^2 + O(N|x|^3). $$
Thus, for each fixed $y$,
$$ \phi_N(y/\sqrt N)\longrightarrow e^{-Vy^2/2}. $$
After substituting $x=y/\sqrt N$ into Fourier inversion, the bounded-offset assumption makes the extra phase tend to one. The bound $e^{-cy^2}$ permits dominated convergence, while the part outside the small neighborhood contributes $o(N^{-1/2})$. Therefore,
$$ \sqrt N\,\Pr(S_N=\ell_N) \longrightarrow \frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-Vy^2/2}\,dy = \frac1{\sqrt{2\pi V}}, $$
as claimed.

Applying exponential tilting

For a player of positive-weight label $t$, let $r_{N,t}$ be the probability that this player is pivotal when all other players independently join a coalition with probability $1/2$.

All players have the same factor relating pivotal probability to swing count. Hence the aggregate normalized Banzhaf index is exactly
$$ B_{A,N} = \frac{\sum_{t\in A}\nu_tr_{N,t}} {\sum_t\nu_tr_{N,t}}. $$
Zero-weight players have $r_{N,t}=0$.

Tilt the coalition distribution so that a player of label $s$ joins with probability
$$ p_s=\frac{e^{\theta w_s}}{1+e^{\theta w_s}}. $$
Set
$$ \sigma^2 = \sum_s\nu_sw_s^2p_s(1-p_s)>0, \qquad Z_N = \prod_s \left(\frac{1+e^{\theta w_s}}2\right)^{N\nu_s}. $$
For the coalition excluding one player of label $t$, let $S_{N,-t}$ denote its weight. At any attainable weight $s$,
$$ \Pr_{1/2}(S_{N,-t}=s) = Z_N\frac{2}{1+e^{\theta w_t}} e^{-\theta s} \Pr_\theta(S_{N,-t}=s). $$
This follows directly by comparing the probabilities of each coalition under the two independent-product distributions.

Under the tilted distribution,
$$ \mathbb E_\theta S_{N,-t}=Nq-w_tp_t. $$
Define
$$ C_N=\left\lceil\frac{Nq}{h}\right\rceil. $$
Because winning means meeting or exceeding the quota, the pivotal coalition weights are exactly
$$ h(C_N-j), \qquad j=1,\ldots,k_t. $$
Their distances from the tilted mean are bounded independently of $N$. Applying the lattice estimate in units of $h$ gives, uniformly over these finitely many values,
$$ \Pr_\theta(S_{N,-t}=h(C_N-j)) = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi N\sigma^2}}(1+o(1)). $$
Consequently,
$$ r_{N,t} = K_N \frac{2}{1+e^{\theta w_t}} \sum_{j=1}^{k_t}e^{\theta hj} (1+o(1)), $$
where the positive factor
$$ K_N = Z_Ne^{-\theta hC_N} \frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi N\sigma^2}} $$
is common to all labels. In particular, any dependence on the quota's position within the lattice lies in this common factor.

If $\theta=0$, the remaining factor is $k_t=w_t/h$. If $\theta\ne0$, the geometric sum gives
$$ \frac{2}{1+e^{\theta w_t}} \sum_{j=1}^{k_t}e^{\theta hj} = \frac{2e^{\theta h}}{e^{\theta h}-1} \tanh(\theta w_t/2). $$
This is a common positive multiple of $\tanh(|\theta|w_t/2)$: for negative $\theta$, both the prefactor and the hyperbolic tangent change sign.

Substitution into $B_{A,N}$ proves the asserted limiting formula. The argument holds along every admissible sequence of integers $N$, so it proves existence of the limits required in the question.

At half-total-weight quota this agrees with the proportionality result in [Lindner and Machover, Theorem 3.6](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/74105380/L); the calculation above also supplies the off-half-quota formula needed here.

Total-weight quota

If $q=W_\nu$, a coalition wins exactly when it contains every positive-weight player. All positive-weight players therefore have equal normalized Banzhaf indices; zero-weight players have index zero.

Writing
$$ M_\nu=\sum_{t:w_t>0}\nu_t, $$
we obtain, exactly for every admissible $N$,
$$ B_{A,N} = \frac{\sum_{t\in A:w_t>0}\nu_t}{M_\nu}. $$
Thus the limit exists at this endpoint too.

2. Instances with no beneficial split

Write
$$ w=w_{t^\star}, \qquad \mu_\star=\mu_{t^\star}. $$

If $w=0$, no legal split exists, because $u,v>0$ would imply $u+v>0$.

Now suppose $w>0$ and $q=W/2$. Every legal split preserves total weight:
$$ \sum_t\nu_tw_t = W-\alpha w+\alpha u+\alpha v = W. $$
The half-quota formula therefore gives
$$ B_{\{a,b\}}(\nu,q) = \frac{\alpha(u+v)}W = \frac{\alpha w}W. $$
For the original society,
$$ \frac{\alpha}{\mu_\star} B_{\{t^\star\}}(\mu,q) = \frac{\alpha}{\mu_\star} \frac{\mu_\star w}W = \frac{\alpha w}W. $$
These quantities are equal for every legal split. Strict benefit is impossible.

3. Beneficial splits at every interior non-half quota

Assume
$$ w>0, \qquad 0<q<W, \qquad q\ne W/2. $$
Fix the rational equal-weight split
$$ u=v=w/2. $$
We show that sufficiently small positive rational $\alpha$ works.

For the continuity argument, allow real $\alpha\in[0,\mu_\star]$ and define
$$ F_\alpha(x) = F_\mu(x) + \alpha \left( w\frac{e^{xw/2}}{1+e^{xw/2}} - w\frac{e^{xw}}{1+e^{xw}} \right). $$
This is the tilted expected-weight function for the split measure. Its total weight remains $W$, so it is strictly increasing from $0$ to $W$ and has a unique root $\theta_\alpha$ of
$$ F_\alpha(\theta_\alpha)=q. $$

We have
$$ \theta_\alpha\longrightarrow\theta_0 \qquad\text{as }\alpha\downarrow0. $$
Indeed, for each $\varepsilon>0$,
$$ F_0(\theta_0-\varepsilon)<q<F_0(\theta_0+\varepsilon). $$
Continuity in $\alpha$ preserves both inequalities for sufficiently small $\alpha$, and strict monotonicity then places $\theta_\alpha$ between those two arguments.

Also, $F_\alpha(0)=W/2$ for every $\alpha$. Hence all $\theta_\alpha$ are nonzero and have the same sign. Put
$$ \lambda_\alpha=\frac{|\theta_\alpha|}{2}, \qquad H_\alpha = \sum_t\mu_t\tanh(\lambda_\alpha w_t) + \alpha \left( 2\tanh(\lambda_\alpha w/2) - \tanh(\lambda_\alpha w) \right). $$
These quantities are continuous at zero, with $\lambda_0>0$ and $H_0>0$.

For rational positive $\alpha$, the proven limit formula gives
$$ B_{\{a,b\}}(\nu_\alpha,q) = \frac{2\alpha\tanh(\lambda_\alpha w/2)}{H_\alpha}, $$
whereas
$$ \frac{\alpha}{\mu_\star} B_{\{t^\star\}}(\mu,q) = \alpha\frac{\tanh(\lambda_0w)}{H_0}. $$
After cancelling $\alpha>0$, benefit is equivalent to
$$ \frac{2\tanh(\lambda_\alpha w/2)}{H_\alpha} > \frac{\tanh(\lambda_0w)}{H_0}. $$

This inequality holds strictly at the continuous extension $\alpha=0$. To verify it, let
$$ r=\tanh(\lambda_0w/2)\in(0,1). $$
The double-angle identity gives
$$ \tanh(\lambda_0w)=\frac{2r}{1+r^2}, $$
so the difference between the two sides at zero is
$$ \frac{2r}{H_0} - \frac{2r}{(1+r^2)H_0} = \frac{2r^3}{(1+r^2)H_0} >0. $$
Continuity therefore supplies a $\delta>0$ for which the inequality holds whenever $0<\alpha<\delta$ and $\alpha\le\mu_\star$.

Choose an integer $k$ sufficiently large that
$$ \alpha=\mu_\star/k<\delta. $$
This is a legal rational split mass, and the split is beneficial.

The order of limits is respected: for each chosen rational $\alpha>0$, the population limit has already been proved. Taking $\alpha\downarrow0$ is used only to establish existence of such an $\alpha$.

4. Beneficial splits at total-weight quota

Assume $w>0$ and $q=W$. Let
$$ M=\sum_{t:w_t>0}\mu_t. $$
The original designated type has limiting power
$$ B_{\{t^\star\}}(\mu,q)=\frac{\mu_\star}{M}. $$
After any legal positive-weight split, the positive-weight mass is $M+\alpha$, and the two fresh labels have total mass $2\alpha$. Thus
$$ B_{\{a,b\}}(\nu,q)=\frac{2\alpha}{M+\alpha}. $$
Benefit is equivalent to
$$ \frac{2\alpha}{M+\alpha}>\frac{\alpha}{M}, $$
or, equivalently,
$$ \alpha<M. $$
Choose
$$ \alpha=\mu_\star/2, \qquad u=v=w/2. $$
Since $0<\mu_\star\le M$, this choice satisfies $\alpha<M$ and is beneficial.

5. Decision algorithm and complexity

The preceding cases prove the exact criterion. On a valid input, the algorithm is:

  • Return no if $w_{t^\star}=0$.
  • Compute $W=\sum_t\mu_tw_t$ exactly.
  • Return yes if $2q\ne W$, and no otherwise.

For an explicit bit-complexity bound, take a common denominator equal to the product of the input denominators. Its bit length is $O(L)$; every denominator of a product $\mu_tw_t$ divides it. Computing the common denominator, the scaled summands, and the comparison uses $O(\tau)$ operations on $O(L)$-bit integers. Schoolbook multiplication and division therefore give
$$ O((\tau+m)L^2), \qquad m=2, $$
bit operations.

Only supported types are processed. Neither lattice scaling nor solving for $\theta$ is part of the decision algorithm.

The finite-game hardness in [Aziz, Bachrach, Elkind and Paterson, Theorem 11](https://www.dcs.warwick.ac.uk/~msp/papers/false_name.pdf) concerns a different decision problem. Here, the population limit and the ability to choose a sufficiently small positive split mass yield the polynomial-time criterion proved above.
