The continuous mirror question, as graded

For each integer \(N\), let an instance be a distribution \(\mu\) over location types in \([0,1]\), with masses in multiples of \(1/N\) and support size at most \(N\). A randomized rule \(F_N\) selects a facility location; truthfulness requires that an agent at \(x\) cannot improve expected distance by transferring mass \(1/N\) from \(x\) to any reported location \(x'\). Determine the best worst-case approximation ratio \(\alpha_N^*\) for expected minimum utility, where \(M_\mu(y)=\min_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}|x-y|\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu=\max_{y\in[0,1]}M_\mu(y)\); in particular, can the \(O(\sqrt N)\) guarantee be improved to a constant, or must the ratio grow?

1. Definitions and relation to the agent model

For a rule $F$, write its worst-case ratio as
$$ \mathcal A(F) = \sup_{\mu\in\mathcal D_N} \frac{\operatorname{OPT}_\mu} {\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\mu)}M_\mu(Y)}. $$

Let $\alpha_N^{\mathrm{RI}}$ restrict the infimum to report-independent rules. Let $\alpha_N^{\mathrm U}$ restrict it to distributions over deterministic truthful rules, with the distribution over rules independent of reports. Then
$$ \alpha_N^*\le\alpha_N^{\mathrm U}\le\alpha_N^{\mathrm{RI}}. $$
The second inequality holds because every report-independent distribution is a mixture of deterministic constant rules.

The mass model has exactly the same unrestricted optimum as the model with $N$ labeled agents. Indeed, every permitted measure is an empirical measure
$$ \mu_{\mathbf x}=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\delta_{x_i}. $$
Changing one coordinate changes this measure by precisely the deviation specified in the question. Moreover,
$$ \min_i|x_i-y|=M_{\mu_{\mathbf x}}(y). $$
Thus lifting a truthful mass rule preserves truthfulness and approximation.

Conversely, uniformly permute the coordinates before applying any truthful labeled mechanism. For each fixed permutation, a unilateral deviation changes one fixed coordinate, so its truthfulness inequality holds. Averaging preserves that inequality. The resulting distribution depends only on the multiset of reports, and permutation invariance of the objective preserves the approximation guarantee. This establishes equality of the unrestricted optima.

The supplied paper leaves the unrestricted asymptotic order open. [Chan, Lin, and Wang, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07261v1).

2. An explicit lower bound for arbitrary truthful randomized rules

For $N\ge3$, define
$$ A_N = \max_{1\le r\le\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor} \frac{r(2N-r-3)}{r(N-3)+N-1}. $$

Proposition. Every truthful randomized rule satisfies
$$ \boxed{\alpha_N^*\ge A_N.} $$
For $N=2$, the elementary lower bound $\alpha_2^*\ge1$ applies.

2.1 Three societies

Put $K=N-1$, and fix an integer $r$ with $1\le r\le K/2$. Define
$$ \mu^0 = \frac rN\delta_0+\frac rN\delta_1 + \frac1N\sum_{j=r}^{K-r}\delta_{j/K}. $$
Its total mass is one because
$$ 2r+(K-2r+1)=N. $$

Move $r-1$ individual units of mass from $0$ to the locations $j/K$, $1\le j\le r-1$. The resulting society is
$$ \mu^R = \frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{K-r}\delta_{j/K} +\frac rN\delta_1. $$
Reflecting this construction gives
$$ \mu^L = \frac rN\delta_0 +\frac1N\sum_{j=r}^{K}\delta_{j/K}. $$
Every intermediate society belongs to $\mathcal D_N$.

For a truthful rule, let
$$ z^a=\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\mu^a)}Y, \qquad a\in\{0,R,L\}. $$
An agent truly at $0$ has utility $Y$. Truthfulness therefore implies that moving one reporting unit away from $0$ cannot increase the expected facility location. Iterating gives $z^R\le z^0$.

An agent truly at $1$ has utility $1-Y$. Moving a reporting unit away from $1$ cannot decrease the expected facility location. Hence
$$ z^R\le z^0\le z^L. $$

2.2 Affine bounds on minimum distance

Both endpoints are occupied in $\mu^R$. Its gaps have length $1/K$, except for its rightmost gap of length $r/K$. Consequently,
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_{\mu^R} = \operatorname{OPT}_{\mu^L} = \frac r{2K}. $$

Set
$$ c=\frac{r-1}{2K-r-1}, \qquad b=\frac{1-c}{2K}. $$
Here $0\le c<1$.

We claim that, for every $y\in[0,1]$,
$$ M_{\mu^R}(y)\le b+cy. $$

On $[0,1-r/K]$, the occupied grid gives
$$ M_{\mu^R}(y)\le\min\left\{y,\frac1{2K}\right\}. $$
If $y\le1/(2K)$, then
$$ M_{\mu^R}(y)-cy \le(1-c)y\le b. $$
If $y\ge1/(2K)$, then
$$ M_{\mu^R}(y)-cy \le\frac1{2K}-cy\le b. $$

On the large gap,
$$ M_{\mu^R}(y) = \min\left\{y-\left(1-\frac rK\right),\,1-y\right\}. $$
Subtracting $cy$, the two branches have slopes $1-c>0$ and $-1-c<0$. Their maximum occurs at the gap midpoint
$$ y_* =1-\frac r{2K}. $$
The definitions of $b,c$ give
$$ b+cy_*=\frac r{2K}=M_{\mu^R}(y_*). $$
This proves the claimed bound.

Reflection yields
$$ M_{\mu^L}(y)\le b+c(1-y). $$

2.3 Combining truthfulness and approximation

Write
$$ E_R=\mathbb E_{F(\mu^R)}M_{\mu^R}(Y), \qquad E_L=\mathbb E_{F(\mu^L)}M_{\mu^L}(Y). $$
The affine bounds and $z^R\le z^L$ imply
$$ E_R+E_L \le 2b+c(z^R+1-z^L) \le2b+c. $$
Thus at least one of these societies has expected minimum distance at most $(2b+c)/2$. Since both optima equal $r/(2K)$,
$$ \mathcal A(F)\ge\frac{r/K}{2b+c}. $$
A direct calculation gives
$$ 2b+c = \frac{Kr+K-2r}{K(2K-r-1)}. $$
Therefore
$$ \mathcal A(F) \ge \frac{r(2K-r-1)}{Kr+K-2r}. $$
Taking the maximum over $r$ and substituting $K=N-1$ proves the proposition.

For sufficiently large $N$, choose
$$ r=\left\lfloor\sqrt{2(N-1)}\right\rfloor. $$
The resulting lower bound has expansion
$$ 2-\frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt N}+O(N^{-1}). $$
To verify it, observe that
$$ 2- \frac{r(2K-r-1)}{Kr+K-2r} = \frac{r^2-3r+2K}{r(K-2)+K}, $$
and substitute $r=\sqrt{2K}+O(1)$.

This establishes an explicit finite-population refinement of the asymptotic lower bound $2$.

3. An explicit report-independent upper bound

The following proof also supplies the upper bound needed for the restricted results.

Let
$$ s=\sqrt N,\qquad v=\sqrt{2N}, $$
and define
$$ q= \frac{v-s}{(s+1)(v+1)+2(v-s)}. $$
Because $v>s$, the denominator is greater than twice the numerator. Thus $0<q<1/2$.

Use the report-independent distribution that places probability $q$ at each endpoint and probability $1-2q$ uniformly on $[0,1]$. Report independence proves universal truthfulness.

Proposition. This rule has ratio at most
$$ \boxed{ R_N = \sqrt N+3- \frac{2(\sqrt N+1)}{\sqrt{2N}+1}. } $$
In particular,
$$ R_N=\sqrt N+3-\sqrt2+O(N^{-1/2}). $$

Proof

Sort the distinct support locations:
$$ x_1<\cdots<x_\tau. $$
Define boundary gaps $g_0=x_1$, $g_\tau=1-x_\tau$, and internal gaps
$$ g_i=x_{i+1}-x_i,\qquad 1\le i\le\tau-1. $$
Writing $\Delta=\operatorname{OPT}_\mu$, we have
$$ \Delta = \max\left\{g_0,g_\tau,\frac{g_1}{2},\ldots,\frac{g_{\tau-1}}2\right\}. $$
Integration over the gaps gives the uniform distribution’s expected minimum distance:
$$ U = \frac{g_0^2+g_\tau^2}{2} +\frac14\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}g_i^2. $$
The proposed distribution obtains
$$ E=q(g_0+g_\tau)+(1-2q)U. $$

Suppose an internal gap attains $\Delta$. Its length is $2\Delta$ and its contribution to $U$ is $\Delta^2$. For the remaining gaps, weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$ U\ge \Delta^2+\frac{(1-2\Delta)^2}{4(N-1)}. $$
Indeed, the sum of reciprocal coefficients for those gaps is
$$ 2+2+4(\tau-2)=4(\tau-1)\le4(N-1). $$
The identity
$$ t^2+\frac{(1-2t)^2}{4(N-1)} -\frac{t}{s+1} = \frac{(st-1/2)^2}{N-1} $$
therefore implies
$$ E\ge\frac{(1-2q)\Delta}{s+1}. $$

Suppose instead a boundary gap attains $\Delta$. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz on the other gaps gives
$$ U\ge \frac{\Delta^2}{2} +\frac{(1-\Delta)^2}{4N-2}, $$
because their reciprocal coefficients sum to $4\tau-2\le4N-2$. Now
$$ \frac{t^2}{2}+\frac{(1-t)^2}{4N-2} -\frac{t}{v+1} = \frac{(st-1/\sqrt2)^2}{2N-1}. $$
Consequently,
$$ E\ge \Delta\left(q+\frac{1-2q}{v+1}\right). $$

Our choice of $q$ makes the two guarantees equal:
$$ \frac{1-2q}{s+1} = q+\frac{1-2q}{v+1}. $$
Their common reciprocal is
$$ \frac{(s+1)(v+1)+2(v-s)}{v+1} = s+3-\frac{2(s+1)}{v+1} = R_N. $$
This proves the proposition.

4. Rotated rational societies

We now construct a family that sharpens the restricted lower bounds.

Fix $N\ge4$ and an integer $r\ge1$. Put
$$ K=N+2r-3,\qquad h=\frac rK,\qquad w=1-2h=\frac{N-3}{K}. $$

Temporarily identify the endpoints of $[0,1]$ to form a unit circle, with distance
$$ d_{\mathbb T}(a,b)=\min\{|a-b|,1-|a-b|\}. $$
Start with the $K$ circular grid points $j/K$, $0\le j<K$, and delete indices $1,\ldots,2r-1$. Exactly $N-2$ points remain. They have one circular gap of length $2h$ and $N-3$ gaps of length $1/K$.

Rotate these points so that the large gap is centered at $t$. Add the two interval endpoints $0,1$, assigning mass $1/N$ to each of the resulting $N$ reports. Coincident reports are combined. Denote the resulting society by $\mu_t$.

Restrict to
$$ I=[h,1-h]. $$
For $t$ in the interior of $I$, the large gap lies inside the interval. Adding endpoints can only split a small circular gap, so boundary gaps have length at most $1/K\le h$. Thus
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_{\mu_t}=h. $$

Let $R_t(y)$ be the distance from $y$ to the nearest rotated point using circular distance. Then
$$ M_{\mu_t}(y)\le R_t(y). $$
For a shortest circular path crossing the interval boundary, the added endpoint is reached before the circular support point. A shortest path that does not cross the boundary already gives an interval support point. These two cases prove the inequality.

4.1 Exact rotation average

Translation invariance gives, for every fixed $y$,
$$ \int_0^1R_t(y)\,dt = h^2+\frac{N-3}{4K^2} = \frac{r^2+(N-3)/4}{K^2}. $$
The large-gap tent has area $h^2$; each small-gap tent has area $1/(4K^2)$.

Nonnegativity therefore implies
$$ \frac1w\int_I R_t(y)\,dt \le \frac{r^2+(N-3)/4}{K^2w}. $$

4.2 Finite averaging

To avoid any assumption about measurability of the mechanism in its reports, use the rational midpoint parameters
$$ t_j=h+\left(j-\frac12\right)\frac wJ, \qquad 1\le j\le J. $$
Every $\mu_{t_j}$ is a rational society in $\mathcal D_N$, and its optimum is $h$.

For fixed $y$, the function $t\mapsto R_t(y)$ is $1$-Lipschitz: rotating the support by a distance at most $|t-t'|$ changes its distance function by at most that amount. Hence
$$ \left| \frac1J\sum_{j=1}^JR_{t_j}(y) -\frac1w\int_I R_t(y)\,dt \right| \le\frac w{2J}. $$
The same statement holds for $t\mapsto\max\{R_t(a),R_t(b)\}$, uniformly in $a,b$.

5. Sharp asymptotic ratio for report-independent rules

Fix a report-independent distribution $Q$. The preceding pointwise bounds imply
$$ \frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J \mathbb E_QM_{\mu_{t_j}}(Y) \le \frac{r^2+(N-3)/4}{K^2w}+\frac w{2J}. $$
Therefore some society has expected minimum distance at most the right-hand side.

Its optimum is $h$. Letting $J$ tend to infinity gives
$$ \boxed{ \alpha_N^{\mathrm{RI}} \ge \sup_{r\in\mathbb Z,\ r\ge1} \frac{r(N-3)}{r^2+(N-3)/4}. } $$

For an explicit consequence, put $p=\sqrt{N-3}/2$ and choose a positive integer $r$ with $|r-p|\le1/2$. Then
$$ \frac{r(N-3)}{r^2+(N-3)/4} = \sqrt{N-3} \left( 1-\frac{(r-p)^2}{r^2+p^2} \right). $$
Since $r^2+p^2\ge p^2=(N-3)/4$,
$$ \alpha_N^{\mathrm{RI}} \ge \sqrt{N-3}-\frac1{\sqrt{N-3}}. $$
Combining this with Section 3 proves
$$ \boxed{ \lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{\alpha_N^{\mathrm{RI}}}{\sqrt N}=1. } $$

Thus the leading constant $1$ is optimal within the report-independent class.

6. A stronger lower bound for universally truthful rules

We use the established deterministic characterization:

Every deterministic truthful mechanism on the full closed-interval domain, with distance utility and no payments, has at most two locations in its entire range.

The result allows labeled mechanisms and imposes no anonymity assumption. [Feigenbaum and Sethuraman, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.3414). Its interval normalization differs only by scaling, which multiplies both sides of each truthfulness inequality by the same positive constant. Lifting a deterministic mass rule gives a mechanism on the required full labeled domain, so the characterization applies.

6.1 Bounding two fixed locations

Let $B_t(y)$ be the circular distance to the full rotated $K$-point grid, before deletion, and define
$$ T_t(y)=\bigl(h-d_{\mathbb T}(y,t)\bigr)_+. $$
Outside the large gap, $R_t(y)=B_t(y)$; inside it, $R_t(y)=T_t(y)$. Therefore
$$ R_t(y)\le B_t(y)+T_t(y). $$
For any two fixed locations $a,b$,
$$ \max\{R_t(a),R_t(b)\} \le \max\{B_t(a),B_t(b)\}+T_t(a)+T_t(b). $$
Each tent integrates to $h^2$ over a full rotation.

For $0\le u\le1/(2K)$, uniform rotation gives
$$ \Pr_t[B_t(a)>u]=1-2Ku. $$
The union bound implies
$$ \Pr_t[\max\{B_t(a),B_t(b)\}>u] \le \min\{1,2(1-2Ku)\}. $$
Integrating this tail bound gives
$$ \int_0^1\max\{B_t(a),B_t(b)\}\,dt \le \int_0^{1/(2K)} \min\{1,2(1-2Ku)\}\,du = \frac3{8K}. $$
Consequently,
$$ \int_0^1\max\{R_t(a),R_t(b)\}\,dt \le \frac3{8K}+\frac{2r^2}{K^2}. $$

6.2 Applying the range characterization

Fix a deterministic truthful component $g$ with range contained in $\{a,b\}$. For every society in the finite family,
$$ M_{\mu_{t_j}}(g(\mu_{t_j})) \le \max\{R_{t_j}(a),R_{t_j}(b)\}. $$
Restricting the rotation integral to $I$ and using the finite averaging bound gives
$$ \frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J M_{\mu_{t_j}}(g(\mu_{t_j})) \le \frac{3/(8K)+2r^2/K^2}{w}+\frac w{2J}. $$
This bound is uniform over every pair $a,b$. We may therefore average it over a universally truthful mixture. Only finitely many actual output evaluations are integrated; no measurable choice of range locations is needed.

Some society has expected minimum distance at most this bound. Letting $J$ tend to infinity yields
$$ \boxed{ \alpha_N^{\mathrm U} \ge \sup_{r\in\mathbb Z,\ r\ge1} \frac{r(N-3)} {2r^2+\frac34r+\frac38(N-3)}. } $$
Choose integers satisfying
$$ \frac r{\sqrt{N-3}}\longrightarrow\frac{\sqrt3}{4}. $$
After dividing the displayed bound by $\sqrt N$, its limit is $1/\sqrt3$. Together with Section 3,
$$ \boxed{ \frac1{\sqrt3} \le \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\alpha_N^{\mathrm U}}{\sqrt N} \le \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\alpha_N^{\mathrm U}}{\sqrt N} \le1. } $$
In particular, $\alpha_N^{\mathrm U}=\Theta(\sqrt N)$.

This argument stops at universal truthfulness. An arbitrary rule truthful in expectation need not be presented as a mixture of deterministic truthful rules, so the range characterization cannot be applied to it.

7. A population comparison for unrestricted rules

There is also a monotonicity statement along integer multiples:
$$ \boxed{\alpha_{kN}^*\ge\alpha_N^*\qquad(k\ge1).} $$

Indeed, $\mathcal D_N\subseteq\mathcal D_{kN}$. Restrict any truthful $F_{kN}$ to $\mathcal D_N$. An allowed deviation of mass $1/N$ can be performed as $k$ successive deviations of mass $1/(kN)$.

At every step before the last, the original location $x$ still has positive mass. Thus each truthfulness inequality applies and compares utility at the same true location $x$. Chaining the inequalities proves truthfulness of the restricted rule under the $\mathcal D_N$ deviation definition.

The objective depends only on support, so restriction does not change its value on any retained society. Consequently, the restricted rule’s worst-case ratio is at most that of $F_{kN}$. Taking infima proves the claim.

8. Computation and the unresolved target

For rational input, sorting the $\tau$ support locations and computing the boundary gaps and half the internal gaps evaluates $\operatorname{OPT}_\mu$. A conservative schoolbook-arithmetic bound is
$$ O\!\left(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2\right) $$
bit operations.

The upper-bound rule has a constant-size symbolic distribution description involving $N$ and square roots. Reading the input and producing that description takes $O(L)$ bit operations, without expanding masses into $N$ agents. This describes the distribution; it is not a claim about producing an exact continuous real sample in finite time.

The unrestricted lower-bound societies have encoding length $O(N\log N)$. The finite rotation families have rational locations, support size at most $N$, and encoding length $O(N\log(NJ))$ when $r=O(\sqrt N)$.

The proved unrestricted bounds are
$$ A_N\le\alpha_N^*\le R_N. $$
They still leave a constant lower bound versus a $\sqrt N$ upper bound. Neither the sharp report-independent result nor the universal-truthfulness lower bound decides whether general truthfulness in expectation permits a constant approximation.
