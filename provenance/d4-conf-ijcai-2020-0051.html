The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite symmetric type graph \(H=(T,a)\), with \(a_{ts}\in\{0,1\}\) specifying friendship between types, including within-type relations; positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); and a proposed partition \(\Pi=(p^1,\ldots,p^r)\) with \(\sum_j p_t^j=\mu_t\), decide whether \(\Pi\) is core stable. For a coalition profile \(q\), define \(f_t(q)=\sum_s a_{ts}q_s\) and \(e_t(q)=\sum_s(1-a_{ts})q_s\). Extend selfish-first preferences by lexicographically comparing \((f_t(q),-e_t(q),\sum_s a_{ts}\sum_jp_s^j f_s(p^j),-\sum_s a_{ts}\sum_jp_s^j e_s(p^j))\). A blocker is a real selection \(z_t^j\in[0,p_t^j]\) of positive total mass; it forms \(y_t=\sum_jz_t^j\), leaving residual coalitions \(p^j-z^j\). Decide whether there is no such selection for which every selected unit strictly prefers the resulting partition to \(\Pi\).

Here \(m=|T|=\tau\), the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also gives \(\Pi=(p^1,\ldots,p^r)\), with binary-rational \(p_t^j\geq0\), \(\sum_jp_t^j=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_tp_t^j>0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,r,L\).
For a coalition profile \(q\), define \(f_t(q)=\sum_sa_{ts}q_s\) and \(e_t(q)=\sum_s(1-a_{ts})q_s\). For any finite coalition structure \(Q=(q^1,\ldots,q^k)\), define \(A_t(Q)=\sum_sa_{ts}\sum_{\ell=1}^kq_s^\ell f_s(q^\ell)\) and \(B_t(Q)=\sum_sa_{ts}\sum_{\ell=1}^kq_s^\ell e_s(q^\ell)\).
A type-\(t\) unit in coalition \(q\) of \(Q\) ranks its situation by the lexicographic vector \(U_t(q,Q)=(f_t(q),-e_t(q),A_t(Q),-B_t(Q))\), with larger vectors preferred and equal vectors indifferent.
A deviation chooses real \(0\leq z_t^j\leq p_t^j\) with \(\sum_{t,j}z_t^j>0\), forms \(y_t=\sum_jz_t^j\), and produces \(Q_z=(y,p^1-z^1,\ldots,p^r-z^r)\), omitting zero-mass coalitions. It blocks precisely when \(U_t(y,Q_z)>_{\mathrm{lex}}U_t(p^j,\Pi)\) for every \((t,j)\) with \(z_t^j>0\). The proposed structure is core stable precisely when no blocker exists.

Is continuous core-stability verification in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is its complementary problem of deciding whether a blocker exists \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Result

Blocker existence is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for the stated nonatomic model. Consequently, continuous core-stability verification is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hard and cannot be in \(\mathrm{P}\) unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The hardness holds even when every diagonal entry is \(1\), every input mass is dyadic, and each type initially belongs entirely to one coalition. The proof allows arbitrary real deviations.

1. Two preliminary observations

If a participating type strictly improves lexicographically, then necessarily its friend mass weakly increases. If its friend mass stays equal, its enemy mass must weakly decrease. These are necessary conditions regardless of the altruistic coordinates.

We may also perform calculations with unnormalized masses and normalize the constructed input afterward. Indeed, multiplying every mass by a positive constant \(\lambda\) gives

\[ \]

\(f_t(\lambda q)=\lambda f_t(q),\qquad e_t(\lambda q)=\lambda e_t(q),\)

\(A_t(\lambda Q)=\lambda^2A_t(Q),\qquad B_t(\lambda Q)=\lambda^2B_t(Q).\)

Thus each utility coordinate is multiplied by a positive constant, preserving every lexicographic comparison. Deviations correspond bijectively under \(z\mapsto\lambda z\), including the capacity constraints and the requirement of positive deviating mass.

2. Construction from Clique

We reduce from the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete Clique problem: given a simple graph \(G=(V,E)\) and an integer \(k\), decide whether it contains a clique of size at least \(k\). See [Karp’s original result](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Write \(n=|V|\). We restrict to \(2\leq k\leq n\); the excluded cases are decidable directly and can be mapped to fixed yes or no instances within this range.

Set

\(g=n+1,\qquad \epsilon=2^{-\ell},\)

where \(\ell\) is the smallest integer satisfying \(2^\ell\geq4(n+1)\). Hence

\(0<\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{4(n+1)}.\)

For each vertex \(v\), introduce a vertex type \(v\) and a private companion type \(w_v\). Introduce four additional types \(h,b,c,d\). Their unnormalized masses and initial coalitions are:

| Type | Unnormalized mass | Initial coalition |
|---|---:|---|
| \(v\), for each \(v\in V\) | \(1\) | \(C_v\) |
| \(w_v\), for each \(v\in V\) | \(g+k-\epsilon-1\) | \(C_v\) |
| \(h\) | \(g\) | \(C_0\) |
| \(b\) | \(g\) | \(C_0\) |
| \(c\) | \(1/2\) | \(C_0\) |
| \(d\) | \(k-1/2\) | \(C_0\) |

Each listed type places its entire mass in its indicated coalition.

Every type is friends with itself. The off-diagonal friendships are precisely:

  • \(h\) with \(b\) and \(c\), and \(c\) with \(d\);
  • \(h\) with every vertex type \(v\);
  • \(v\) with its companion \(w_v\);
  • two distinct vertex types \(u,v\) whenever \(\{u,v\}\in E\).

All other pairs are enemies. This specifies a symmetric binary friendship matrix.

Normalization

The total mass so far is

\(S_0=2g+k+n(g+k-\epsilon).\)

Choose a power of two \(P>S_0\). Introduce a final type \(\omega\), with mass \(P-S_0\), initially alone in \(C_\omega\). It is friends only with itself.

Divide every mass by \(P\), obtaining the actual input \((\mu,\Pi)\). All masses are positive dyadic rationals, their sum is \(1\), and all initial coalitions have positive mass.

For the proof, use unnormalized units, justified by the scaling observation. Since every type initially belongs entirely to one coalition, let \(x_t\) denote its mass in the deviating coalition \(y\). Thus \(0\leq x_t\leq\widehat\mu_t\), where \(\widehat\mu_t\) is its unnormalized mass.

3. Private companions and the filler cannot participate

Suppose \(w_v\) participates in a blocker. Its only friend types are \(w_v\) and \(v\), both initially entirely in \(C_v\). Its initial friend mass therefore equals its total friend mass in the society:

\(f_{w_v}(C_v)=g+k-\epsilon.\)

Consequently, its friend mass cannot strictly increase. Blocking requires it to remain equal, which forces

\(x_{w_v}=g+k-\epsilon-1,\qquad x_v=1.\)

Its initial enemy mass is zero. Blocking therefore also requires zero enemy mass in \(y\), excluding every other type from \(y\).

Thus \(y=C_v\), and the deviation merely recreates the original coalition using all its mass. The resulting coalition structure equals \(\Pi\), up to order and omitted zero profiles. Since \(A_t\) and \(B_t\) are invariant under those changes, every utility coordinate is unchanged. This contradicts strict improvement.

Therefore no private companion can participate in a blocker.

The identical argument applies to \(\omega\): its initial singleton coalition contains all its friends and no enemies, so its participation could only recreate that coalition without changing the structure. Hence

\(x_{w_v}=0\quad\text{for every }v,\qquad x_\omega=0\)

in every blocker.

4. Every blocker contains all of \(h\) and \(b\)

The initial friend masses of the remaining types are

\(f_h(C_0)=2g+\frac12,\qquad f_b(C_0)=2g,\)

\(f_c(C_0)=g+k,\qquad f_d(C_0)=k,\)

\(f_v(C_v)=g+k-\epsilon.\)

First, participation by \(h\) requires participation by \(b\). Otherwise,

\(f_h(y)=x_h+x_c+\sum_{v\in V}x_v \leq g+\frac12+n <2g+\frac12,\)

because \(g=n+1\). This would strictly decrease \(h\)'s friend mass.

Next, any nonempty blocker must contain \(h\). To see this, suppose \(x_h=0\).

  • Type \(b\) has friend mass at most \(g<2g\), so cannot participate.
  • Type \(c\)'s initial friend mass \(g+k\) is its global maximum. Participation would force all its friends, including all of \(h\), into \(y\).
  • Type \(d\)'s initial friend mass \(k\) is its global maximum. Participation would force all of \(c\), which in turn forces \(h\).
  • A participating vertex type, with its companion excluded, would have friend mass at most \(n<g+k-\epsilon\).

Together with Section 3, these exclusions leave no possible participant. Therefore every blocker contains \(h\) and \(b\).

Since \(b\)'s only friends are \(h\) and itself,

\(f_b(y)=x_h+x_b\leq2g=f_b(C_0).\)

Its participation forces equality, hence

\(x_h=x_b=g.\)

Its initial enemy mass is \(k\). Writing

\(W=\sum_{v\in V}x_v,\)

the necessary enemy-mass condition for \(b\) gives

\[W+x_c+x_d\leq k.\tag{1}\]

5. Types \(c,d\) cannot participate

Type \(c\)'s global maximum friend mass is \(g+k\), attained initially. Thus, if it participates, equality forces

\(x_h=g,\qquad x_c=\frac12,\qquad x_d=k-\frac12.\)

Similarly, participation by \(d\) forces \(x_c+x_d=k\), hence those same full masses.

In either case, inequality (1) forces \(W=0\). Section 4 already forces all of \(h,b\), so the deviating coalition is exactly \(C_0\). As in Section 3, this only recreates the original coalition and leaves all utilities unchanged, contradicting blocking.

Consequently, every blocker has

\[x_c=x_d=0,\qquad x_h=x_b=g,\qquad W\leq k.\tag{2}\]

It also has \(W>0\): if \(W=0\), type \(h\)'s new friend mass is \(2g\), below its initial \(2g+1/2\).

6. Any blocker yields a clique

For each vertex \(v\), let \(N[v]\) be its closed neighborhood in \(G\), including \(v\).

If \(x_v>0\), its private companion is absent, and (2) gives

\(f_v(y)=g+\sum_{u\in N[v]}x_u.\)

The necessary friend-mass condition therefore implies

\[\sum_{u\in N[v]}x_u\geq k-\epsilon.\tag{3}\]

Since some vertex participates, (3) implies \(W\geq k-\epsilon\).

Define the heavy vertices by

\(H=\{v\in V:x_v>\epsilon\}.\)

These vertices form a clique. Suppose distinct \(u,v\in H\) were nonadjacent. Then \(u\notin N[v]\), so (2) would give

\(\sum_{s\in N[v]}x_s\leq W-x_u<k-\epsilon,\)

contradicting (3) for \(v\).

Moreover, every vertex outside \(H\) has mass at most \(\epsilon\). Hence

\(\sum_{v\in H}x_v \geq W-n\epsilon \geq k-(n+1)\epsilon \geq k-\frac14 >k-1.\)

Each vertex type has capacity \(1\), so

\(|H|\geq\sum_{v\in H}x_v>k-1.\)

Because \(|H|\) is an integer, \(|H|\geq k\). Thus every blocker produces a clique of size at least \(k\).

This argument applies to arbitrary real deviations.

7. A clique yields a blocker

Conversely, suppose \(G\) contains a clique \(K\) of exactly \(k\) vertices. Choose

\(x_h=x_b=g,\qquad x_v=1-\frac{\epsilon}{2k}\quad(v\in K),\)

and set all other deviating masses to zero. These masses satisfy every capacity constraint. Their total vertex mass is

\(W=k-\frac{\epsilon}{2}.\)

Every participant strictly improves:

  • Vertex types. Because \(K\) is a clique and every diagonal entry is \(1\), each participating vertex is friends with all vertex mass in \(y\). Thus

\(f_v(y)=g+W>g+k-\epsilon=f_v(C_v)\).
Its first coordinate strictly improves.

  • Type \(h\). Its new friend mass is

\(f_h(y)=2g+W>2g+\frac12=f_h(C_0)\),
since \(k\geq2\) and \(\epsilon<1\). Its first coordinate strictly improves.

  • Type \(b\). Its friend mass remains \(2g\), while its enemy mass decreases:

\(e_b(y)=W<k=e_b(C_0)\).
Its second coordinate strictly improves.

Thus every participant improves within the first two coordinates. The values of \(A_t\) and \(B_t\) cannot affect these comparisons. After dividing the chosen masses by \(P\), this is a blocker for the normalized input.

We have proved the equivalence

\(G\text{ contains a clique of size at least }k \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Pi\text{ admits a blocker}.\)

8. Encoding size and running time

The constructed instance has

\(m=\tau=2n+5,\qquad r=n+2.\)

The exponent \(\ell\) is \(O(\log n)\), and \(S_0=O(n^2)\). Choosing the smallest power of two strictly above \(S_0\) gives \(P=O(n^2)\). Therefore every normalized mass has binary encoding length \(O(\log n)\).

The dense friendship matrix and coalition-profile array yield

\(L=O(n^2\log n).\)

Using straightforward binary arithmetic, the reduction takes

\(O\bigl((m^2+mr)(\log m)^2\bigr) =O\bigl((\tau^2+\tau r)(\log\tau)^2\bigr)\)

bit operations, hence polynomial time in the total input length.

The polynomial-time reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness of blocker existence for the exact problem stated. Taking complements proves \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness of continuous core-stability verification.
