The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a candidate set \(C=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), a binary-rational society \(\mu\) supported on \(\tau\) complete rankings, and distinct Borda scores under \(\mu\), compute the infimum mass that must be transferred between ranking types so the resulting Borda ranking differs from the original by Kendall–\(\tau\) distance at least \(1\). A fixed candidate order resolves ties. Transfers \(x_{\sigma,\pi}\ge 0\) have total outgoing mass at most \(\mu_\sigma\) for each source type, and the objective is to minimize \(\sum_{\sigma\ne\pi}x_{\sigma,\pi}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(m\ge2\), and let \(\mathfrak S_m\) be the strict complete rankings of \(C\). A society \(\mu\) is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each mass a binary rational, with \(\sum_\sigma\mu_\sigma=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Use Borda in the average-rank convention: \(s_i(\nu)=\sum_{\rho\in\mathfrak S_m}\nu_\rho\rho(i)\), where rank \(1\) is best. Let \(R(\nu)\) sort these scores increasingly, breaking ties by a fixed candidate order. Assume the scores under \(\mu\) are pairwise distinct.
For each supported source \(\sigma\), transfers \(x_{\sigma,\pi}\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) may go to any \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_m\setminus\{\sigma\}\), including initially absent types, subject to \(\sum_{\pi\ne\sigma}x_{\sigma,\pi}\le\mu_\sigma\). Extend transfers by zero for unsupported sources and set \(\nu_\rho=\mu_\rho-\sum_{\pi\ne\rho}x_{\rho,\pi}+\sum_{\sigma\ne\rho}x_{\sigma,\rho}\).
Define \(\varepsilon^*(\mu)=\inf\{\sum_{\sigma,\pi:\sigma\ne\pi}x_{\sigma,\pi}:d_\tau(R(\nu),R(\mu))\ge1\}\), over feasible transfers, using the unnormalized Kendall distance. The infimum need not be attained.

Is exact computation of \(\varepsilon^*(\mu)\) possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite equal-weight profiles, a stability-radius lower bound on the replacement fraction is established, without an exact complexity classification. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22847v2)
For ranking distributions, total-variation breakdown functions have general median lower bounds and a smoothed numerical estimation method; these do not settle this exact computational question. [Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/goibert23a/goibert23a.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize the society to total mass one, matching the probability-distribution domain.
  • Require at least two candidates so a positive Kendall-distance change is possible.
  • Allow destinations outside the initial support, matching arbitrary replacement rankings.
  • Allow nonnegative real transfers and retain an infimum, including when tie-breaking prevents attainment.
  • Use average ranks sorted increasingly and unnormalized Kendall distance, matching the discrete conventions.

Result

Exact computation of \(\varepsilon^*(\mu)\) is possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). It reduces to \((m-1)\) fractional knapsack problems, each with at most \(\tau\) items.

The proof accounts for initially absent destination rankings, the prohibition on self-transfers, and possible nonattainment of the infimum.

1. Reducing ranking changes to adjacent pairs

Let \(c_1,\ldots,c_m\) list the candidates in their initial Borda order. By the distinct-score hypothesis,

\( s_{c_1}(\mu)<s_{c_2}(\mu)<\cdots<s_{c_m}(\mu). \)

If another strict ranking orders every \(c_j\) before \(c_{j+1}\), transitivity forces it to equal the initial ranking. Consequently, whenever \(R(\nu)\ne R(\mu)\), some initially adjacent pair \(a=c_j\), \(b=c_{j+1}\) is reversed in \(R(\nu)\). Sorting by increasing scores then implies

\( s_b(\nu)\le s_a(\nu). \)

Conversely, the strict inequality \(s_b(\nu)<s_a(\nu)\) guarantees a ranking change, irrespective of tie-breaking.

We will compute the minimum cost of making each adjacent pair’s scores equal or reversed, and prove that strict reversal can be achieved with arbitrarily little additional cost.

2. Exact optimization for one pair

Fix candidates \(a,b\) satisfying \(s_a(\mu)<s_b(\mu)\). Define their initial gap and each supported source’s rank difference by

\( \Delta=s_b(\mu)-s_a(\mu)>0,\qquad d_\sigma=\sigma(b)-\sigma(a). \)

For any ranking \(\pi\),

\( -(m-1)\le \pi(b)-\pi(a)\le m-1. \)

Thus transferring one unit of mass from \(\sigma\) can decrease the score gap by at most

\( w_\sigma=d_\sigma+(m-1). \)

In particular, \(0\le w_\sigma\le 2(m-1)\).

For arbitrary feasible transfers, put \(y_\sigma=\sum_{\pi\ne\sigma}x_{\sigma,\pi}\). Expanding the score difference gives

\( s_b(\nu)-s_a(\nu) = \Delta- \sum_{\sigma,\pi:\,\pi\ne\sigma} x_{\sigma,\pi} \bigl(d_\sigma-[\pi(b)-\pi(a)]\bigr). \)

Since every coefficient in this sum is at most \(w_\sigma\), the condition \(s_b(\nu)\le s_a(\nu)\) implies

\( \sum_\sigma w_\sigma y_\sigma\ge\Delta. \)

The transfer cost is exactly \(\sum_\sigma y_\sigma\), and feasibility gives \(0\le y_\sigma\le\mu_\sigma\). Therefore every transfer reaching the pair’s boundary has cost at least

\( K_{ab} = \min\left\{ \sum_\sigma y_\sigma: 0\le y_\sigma\le\mu_\sigma,\ \sum_\sigma w_\sigma y_\sigma\ge\Delta \right\}. \)

This lower bound is achievable. Choose a ranking \(\pi^{ab}\) with

\( \pi^{ab}(b)=1,\qquad \pi^{ab}(a)=m, \)

and put the remaining candidates in any fixed order. For every source with \(w_\sigma>0\), this destination differs from \(\sigma\): equality would imply \(d_\sigma=-(m-1)\), hence \(w_\sigma=0\).

Variables with \(w_\sigma=0\) can be set to zero without impairing feasibility or increasing cost. Send \(y_\sigma\) from every positive-\(w_\sigma\) source to \(\pi^{ab}\). These transfers are allowed, even if \(\pi^{ab}\) is initially absent, and they yield

\( s_b(\nu)-s_a(\nu)=\Delta-\sum_\sigma w_\sigma y_\sigma. \)

Hence \(K_{ab}\) is exactly the minimum transfer cost for the weak crossing \(s_b(\nu)\le s_a(\nu)\).

Finally, the total available decrease is

\( \sum_\sigma\mu_\sigma w_\sigma = \sum_\sigma\mu_\sigma d_\sigma+(m-1)\sum_\sigma\mu_\sigma = \Delta+(m-1)>\Delta. \)

Thus the optimization is always feasible.

3. Explicit formula and proof of optimality

Discard zero-\(w_\sigma\) sources and index the remaining sources so that

\( w_1\ge w_2\ge\cdots\ge w_n>0. \)

Write their masses as \(p_1,\ldots,p_n\), and define

\( H_0=0,\qquad H_r=\sum_{i=1}^{r}w_i p_i. \)

Let \(k\) be the smallest index with \(H_k\ge\Delta\). Such an index exists by the total-decrease calculation above. Define

\( y_i^*= \begin{cases} p_i,&i<k,\\ (\Delta-H_{k-1})/w_k,&i=k,\\ 0,&i>k. \end{cases} \)

Minimality of \(k\) and \(H_k\ge\Delta\) imply \(0<y_k^*\le p_k\). This vector is feasible and satisfies \(\sum_iw_i y_i^*=\Delta\). Its cost is

\( \boxed{ K_{ab} = \sum_{i<k}p_i+ \frac{\Delta-H_{k-1}}{w_k}. } \)

Here is a direct optimality proof. Let \(z\) be any feasible vector on the positive-\(w\) sources, and let \(c=\sum_i y_i^*\). Then

\( \sum_iw_i(z_i-y_i^*) = w_k\left(\sum_i z_i-c\right) + \sum_i(w_i-w_k)(z_i-y_i^*). \)

Every term in the final sum is nonpositive:

  • For \(i<k\), \(w_i-w_k\ge0\), while \(z_i-y_i^*=z_i-p_i\le0\).
  • For \(i=k\), the coefficient is zero.
  • For \(i>k\), \(w_i-w_k\le0\), while \(z_i-y_i^*=z_i\ge0\).

Feasibility also gives \(\sum_iw_i(z_i-y_i^*)\ge0\). Therefore

\( 0\le\sum_iw_i(z_i-y_i^*) \le w_k\left(\sum_i z_i-c\right). \)

Since \(w_k>0\), we obtain \(\sum_i z_i\ge c\). Zero-\(w\) sources cannot improve this bound because they contribute cost without decreasing the gap. This proves the formula.

4. Strict crossings and the global infimum

The optimal transfers just constructed make the pair’s score gap exactly zero. Their remaining available decrease is

\( \sum_\sigma w_\sigma(\mu_\sigma-y_\sigma^*) = \Delta+(m-1)-\Delta = m-1>0. \)

Consequently, some source has both \(w_\sigma>0\) and \(y_\sigma^*<\mu_\sigma\).

Given any \(\eta>0\), increase that source’s transfer to \(\pi^{ab}\) by an amount \(t\) satisfying

\( 0<t<\min\{\eta,\mu_\sigma-y_\sigma^*\}. \)

The transfers remain feasible, their cost is less than \(K_{ab}+\eta\), and the resulting gap is \(-w_\sigma t<0\). Thus the infimum cost for strict reversal of this pair is also \(K_{ab}\).

Now define

\( K=\min_{1\le j<m}K_{c_jc_{j+1}}. \)

Every ranking-changing transfer reverses some initially adjacent pair in the output ranking. By Section 1, that pair has a weak score crossing, so its transfer cost is at least its corresponding \(K_{ab}\), and therefore at least \(K\).

Conversely, select an adjacent pair attaining \(K\). The preceding construction produces a strict reversal with cost less than \(K+\eta\) for every \(\eta>0\).

Together, these inequalities prove

\( \boxed{ \varepsilon^*(\mu)= \min_{1\le j<m}K_{c_jc_{j+1}}. } \)

The value is independent of the fixed tie-breaking order, although attainment can depend on that order.

5. Exact polynomial bit complexity

Write each input mass as \(\mu_\sigma=u_\sigma/v_\sigma\), with positive integer denominator, and take

\( D=\prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}v_\sigma. \)

The bit length of \(D\) is bounded by the sum of the input denominator bit lengths, hence is \(O(L)\). Write \(\mu_\sigma=A_\sigma/D\). Since the masses sum to one, \(\sum_\sigma A_\sigma=D\).

Each score has the form

\( s_i(\mu)=\frac{\sum_\sigma A_\sigma\sigma(i)}{D}, \)

whose numerator is at most \(mD\). Scores and gaps therefore have \(O(L+\log m)\) bits and can be compared exactly.

For a fixed pair, write \(\Delta=G/D\). The formula from Section 3 becomes

\( K_{ab} = \frac{ w_k\sum_{i<k}A_i+ G-\sum_{i<k}w_iA_i }{ Dw_k }. \)

Here \(1\le w_k\le2(m-1)\), so this rational number also has \(O(L+\log m)\) bits. Prefix sums and exact comparisons have the same polynomial bit bound.

The algorithm is:

  • Compute the Borda scores and sort the candidates.
  • For each initially adjacent pair, compute its at most \(\tau\) gains \(w_\sigma\), sort the positive gains, and apply the formula.
  • Return the minimum of these \((m-1)\) rational values.

This uses \(O(m\tau\log(\tau+1)+m\log m)\) arithmetic and comparison operations on numbers of polynomial bit length, together with polynomial preprocessing. Therefore its total running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

6. A nonattainment check

For \(m=2\), suppose all mass initially ranks \(a\) before \(b\), and the tie-breaking order also puts \(a\) before \(b\). Then \(\Delta=1\), the sole source has \(w_\sigma=2\), and the formula gives \(\varepsilon^*(\mu)=1/2\).

Transferring mass \(t\) to the reverse ranking leaves gap \(1-2t\). At \(t=1/2\), tie-breaking preserves the initial order; every \(t>1/2\) reverses it. Hence the infimum is indeed \(1/2\) and is not attained.
