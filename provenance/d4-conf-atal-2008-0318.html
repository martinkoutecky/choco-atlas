The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given registered candidates \(C\), addable candidates \(C'\), a target \(d\in C\), an addition limit \(r\), and a distribution \(\mu\) over complete rankings of \(C\cup C'\), does some \(A\subseteq C'\) with \(|A|\le r\) make \(d\) the unique highest-scoring candidate on the slate \(C\cup A\), where each type votes for its highest-ranked candidate on that slate?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) and \(C'\) be disjoint finite sets of registered and addable candidates, with \(d\in C\), \(m=|C\cup C'|\), and an integer addition limit \(0\le r\le |C'|\).
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct strict complete rankings of \(C\cup C'\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_i\), where \(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L,r\).
For a slate \(S\), each type assigns its entire mass to its highest-ranked candidate in \(S\). Write \(s_S(c)\) for the total mass assigned to \(c\). There is one seat, and a tie involving \(d\) counts as failure.

Is deciding whether there exists \(A\subseteq C'\) with \(|A|\le r\) such that \(s_{C\cup A}(d)>s_{C\cup A}(c)\) for every \(c\in(C\cup A)\setminus\{d\}\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete voters, constructive control by adding candidates under Bloc is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even with one seat, when Bloc coincides with plurality ([Meir, Procaccia, and Rosenschein](https://ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas08/proceedings/pdf/paper/AAMAS08_0008.pdf)).
No result explicitly treating this support-encoded, normalized rational-mass formulation was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted complete rankings as strict linear orders and registered and addable candidate sets as disjoint.
  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one and required every listed support mass to be positive.
  • Fixed one seat, as required by the graded unique-highest-score question; ties involving the target count as failure, consistent with adversarial tie-breaking.
  • Allowed a zero addition limit and bounded it by the number of addable candidates.
  • Defined m as the total number of registered and addable candidates and L as the total input bit length.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, and hence \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. This holds even when every mass is dyadic: its denominator is a power of two.

The cited paper establishes the discrete result in Proposition 4.8 and specifies adversarial tie-breaking in Remark 3.3. [Meir, Procaccia, and Rosenschein](https://ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas08/proceedings/pdf/paper/AAMAS08_0008.pdf). Below is a direct reduction that explicitly satisfies the support and normalization requirements.

Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) and running times

A certificate is a subset \(A\subseteq C'\) with \(|A|\le r\).

Write each input mass as \(\mu_i=a_i/b_i\), where \(a_i,b_i\) are positive integers encoded in binary. Set

\(D=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}b_i\), and \(w_i=a_i(D/b_i)\).

The bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\), because the sum of the denominator bit lengths is at most \(L\). Each \(w_i\) also has \(O(L)\) bits. These integers can be computed in \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations using ordinary integer multiplication and division.

For each ranking, scan until encountering a candidate in \(C\cup A\), and add \(w_i\) to that candidate’s integer score. These scores equal \(D s_{C\cup A}(c)\), so integer comparison checks the required strict inequalities exactly.

With indexed candidate identifiers, a conservative bound for certificate verification, including arithmetic preprocessing, is

\(O\!\left(\tau L^2+(\tau m+m)L\right)\)

bit operations. Checking \(|A|\le r\) fits within this bound. Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

For completeness, exhaustive enumeration gives a deterministic algorithm taking

\(O\!\left(\tau L^2+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{|C'|}{j}\right)(\tau m+m)L\right)\).

In particular, for each fixed \(r\), this is polynomial time. The hardness result concerns unrestricted input \(r\).

Reduction from exact cover by triples

Use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem Exact Cover by 3-Sets. Its input consists of a universe \(U\), with \(|U|=n=3q\), and a family \(\mathcal F=\{F_1,\ldots,F_t\}\) of three-element subsets of \(U\). The question is whether some \(q\) members partition \(U\).

We may restrict to \(q\ge1\), since the empty-universe case is trivial. If \(t<q\), the instance is necessarily negative; map it to the fixed negative election having \(C=\{d,x\}\), \(C'=\varnothing\), \(r=0\), and the single ranking \(x\succ d\) of mass \(1\). Hence assume \(t\ge q\).

Candidates and normalization

Create a registered candidate \(x_u\) for each \(u\in U\), and an addable candidate \(a_j\) for each \(F_j\). Set the addition limit to \(r=q\).

Initially construct rankings with positive integer weights:

  • One ranking with \(d\) first, of weight \(4\).
  • For each \(u\in U\), one ranking with \(x_u\) first, of weight \(3\).
  • For each \(u\in U\), one ranking of weight \(1\) placing all \(a_j\) satisfying \(u\in F_j\) above \(x_u\), and placing every other candidate below \(x_u\).

Use any fixed order within unspecified portions, so every ranking is strict and complete.

The total weight is \(T=4+4n\). Let \(B\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(B\ge T\), and put \(h=B-T\).

Add registered candidates \(z_1,\ldots,z_h\). For each \(z_\ell\), add one ranking of weight \(1\) with \(z_\ell\) first. Place these new candidates at the bottom of every previously constructed ranking. Complete each new ranking arbitrarily.

Thus

\(C=\{d\}\cup\{x_u:u\in U\}\cup\{z_1,\ldots,z_h\}\), and \(C'=\{a_1,\ldots,a_t\}\).

The total integer weight is now \(B\). Divide every weight by \(B\). All masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to \(1\).

Finally, merge any identical complete rankings by adding their masses. This produces a list of distinct rankings. Merging preserves every slate’s scores because identical rankings choose the same highest available candidate.

Scores for an arbitrary addition

Fix any \(A\subseteq C'\). Write \(s_A(c)=s_{C\cup A}(c)\).

The ranking with \(d\) first always supports \(d\). Every other ranking has an available registered candidate above \(d\). Consequently,

\(B s_A(d)=4\).

For an element \(u\), the weight-\(3\) ranking always supports \(x_u\). Its weight-\(1\) ranking supports an added candidate precisely when some selected set contains \(u\). No other ranking can support \(x_u\). Therefore,

\(B s_A(x_u)=3+\mathbf 1\{u\notin\bigcup_{a_j\in A}F_j\}\).

A selected candidate \(a_j\) can receive weight only from the weight-\(1\) element rankings for elements of \(F_j\). Since \(|F_j|=3\),

\(B s_A(a_j)\le3\).

Finally, each padding candidate receives exactly its own weight-\(1\) ranking:

\(B s_A(z_\ell)=1\).

These identities prove the exact equivalence

\(d\text{ is the strict winner on }C\cup A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\bigcup_{a_j\in A}F_j=U\).

Indeed, an uncovered element gives a rival \(x_u\) tying \(d\) at score \(4/B\), which is failure. If every element is covered, every rival has score at most \(3/B\), strictly below \(d\).

Correctness of the reduction

If the exact-cover instance is positive, add the \(q\) candidates corresponding to its exact cover. Every element is covered, so \(d\) wins strictly.

Conversely, suppose some \(A\) with \(|A|\le q\) makes \(d\) win strictly. The score calculation proves that its corresponding sets cover \(U\). Hence

\(3q=|U|=\left|\bigcup_{a_j\in A}F_j\right|\le\sum_{a_j\in A}|F_j|=3|A|\le3q\).

All inequalities are equalities. Thus \(|A|=q\), and the selected sets are pairwise disjoint: otherwise their union would have cardinality strictly smaller than the sum of their cardinalities. They therefore form an exact cover.

Encoding size and construction time

Because \(T\le B<2T\), we have \(h<T=4n+4\). Consequently, the constructed instance satisfies

\(m=1+n+t+h=O(n+t)\), \(\tau\le1+2n+h=O(n)\), and \(r=q=n/3\).

Each mass requires \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits. Writing all complete rankings and merging duplicates takes polynomial time; for example, a straightforward implementation takes \(O((n+t)^3\log(n+t+1))\) bit operations.

With indexed candidate names, the output length satisfies

\(L=O\!\left(n(n+t)\log(n+t+1)\right)\).

Thus this is a polynomial-time many-one reduction to exactly the required support-encoded, normalized formulation.

Conclusion

The reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness even for dyadic masses, and exact polynomial-time certificate verification proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\). Therefore the problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.
