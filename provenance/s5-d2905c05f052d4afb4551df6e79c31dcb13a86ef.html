The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), weights \(w_t\), tied rankings, and eligibility profiles, together with a normalized polymatroid rank function \(r_a\) for each of \(m\) categories, compute feasible allocation masses \(x_{ta}\). Require \(x_{ta}\ge 0\), \(\sum_a x_{ta}\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t\in S}x_{ta}\le r_a(S)\) for every \(S\subseteq T\), and \(x_{ta}=0\) for unacceptable assignments. Each unit of mass receives at most one indivisible category, while \(x\) induces a lottery for each type. Pareto optimality means that no feasible reassignment makes every type’s lottery first-order stochastically dominate its current lottery, with strict improvement for positive mass. Is there an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), given polynomial-time separation oracles for the \(r_a\), that returns a Pareto-optimal allocation with weighted matched mass at least \((1-1/e)\) times the maximum among feasible Pareto-optimal allocations?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(T\): the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each with binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. There are \(m\ge1\) categories. Each type has a binary-rational weight \(w_t\ge0\), an eligibility set, and a weak ranking of acceptable categories; all acceptable categories are strictly preferred to being unmatched.
For each category \(a\), a normalized, nondecreasing submodular rank function \(r_a:2^T\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) defines a polymatroid \(P_a\), supplied through an exact polynomial-time separation oracle. Let \(L\) include the explicit input bit length and a uniform bound on the bit lengths of rank values; oracle running times are polynomial in the query length and \(m,\tau,L\).
An allocation consists of masses \(x_{ta}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_a x_{ta}\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t\in S}x_{ta}\le r_a(S)\) for every \(a\) and \(S\subseteq T\), and \(x_{ta}=0\) unless \(a\) is both acceptable and eligible for \(t\). Each individual receives at most one category; type \(t\)'s lottery assigns probability \(x_{ta}/\mu_t\) to \(a\) and the remaining probability to being unmatched.
An allocation is Pareto optimal if no feasible allocation first-order stochastically dominates every type's lottery and strictly dominates at least one positive-mass type's lottery. Ties remain indifferences. Write \(W(x)=\sum_{t,a}w_t x_{ta}\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{PO}}=\max\{W(y):y\text{ is feasible and Pareto optimal}\}\).

Is there an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), given the separation oracles, that returns a feasible Pareto-optimal allocation \(x\) with \(W(x)\ge(1-1/e)\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{PO}}\)?

For discrete agents with ties, type-independent category weights per agent, and a matroid constraint at each category, a polynomial-time randomized, universally truthful, Pareto-optimal mechanism has approximation ratio \(e/(e-1)+O(1/\kappa)\), for every integer \(\kappa\ge1\); a later version states ratio \(e/(e-1)\). Result

Yes. In fact, a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm returns a Pareto-optimal allocation with \(W(x)=\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{PO}}\).

The crucial property is that stochastic dominance cannot decrease any type’s probability of being matched. Since the weights are nonnegative and depend only on the type, welfare cannot decrease under a Pareto improvement.

1. Expressing stochastic dominance by linear inequalities

Let \(F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m\tau}\) be the set of feasible allocations specified in the question.

For each type \(t\), write its acceptable categories as indifference classes \(C_{t,1},\ldots,C_{t,k_t}\), ordered from best to worst. Here \(k_t\le m\); take \(k_t=0\) if no category is acceptable. Define the cumulative assigned masses

\( H_{t,j}(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\sum_{a\in C_{t,\ell}}x_{ta}, \qquad 1\le j\le k_t. \)

The probability of receiving a category in the first \(j\) classes is \(H_{t,j}(x)/\mu_t\). Because \(\mu_t>0\), allocation \(y\) weakly first-order stochastically dominates allocation \(x\) for type \(t\) exactly when

\( H_{t,j}(y)\ge H_{t,j}(x) \quad\text{for every }1\le j\le k_t. \)

Indeed, these are precisely the nontrivial upper-contour probability comparisons for the finite ordered set consisting of the acceptable indifference classes and the unmatched outcome. The comparison including unmatched is always equality, since total probability is one. Strict dominance means that at least one of the displayed comparisons is strict. When \(k_t=0\), every feasible lottery for \(t\) is concentrated on unmatched.

Define the linear function

\( Q(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{j=1}^{k_t}H_{t,j}(x). \)

Consequently, if \(y\) dominates \(x\) for every type and strictly dominates it for at least one type, then \(Q(y)>Q(x)\). This objective respects ties: every category in \(C_{t,\ell}\) has the same coefficient \(k_t-\ell+1\).

Also, since infeasible and unacceptable assignments have zero mass,

\( H_{t,k_t}(x)=\sum_a x_{ta} \)

whenever \(k_t>0\). For \(k_t=0\), the right-hand side is zero. Therefore, dominance for every type implies

\( W(y)-W(x) =\sum_t w_t\left(\sum_a y_{ta}-\sum_a x_{ta}\right) \ge 0, \)

using \(w_t\ge0\).

2. The algorithm

Solve the following two linear optimization problems exactly, using separation oracles.

  • Compute \(V=\max\{W(z):z\in F\}\).
  • Return an allocation \(x\) maximizing \(Q(z)\) over the optimal-welfare face

\( F_V=\{z\in F:W(z)=V\}. \)

Both optimization problems attain their maxima. To see this, zero is feasible, all defining inequalities are closed, and the supply constraints imply \(0\le z_{ta}\le\mu_t\le1\). Thus \(F\) is a nonempty compact polytope. Its optimal-welfare face \(F_V\) is likewise nonempty and compact.

3. Proof of Pareto optimality and exact welfare optimality

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that a feasible allocation \(y\) dominates the returned \(x\) for every type and strictly dominates it for at least one type.

Section 1 gives \(W(y)\ge W(x)=V\). But \(V\) is the maximum welfare over all feasible allocations, so \(W(y)\le V\). Hence \(W(y)=V\), and therefore \(y\in F_V\).

Section 1 also gives \(Q(y)>Q(x)\), contradicting the second optimization step. Thus \(x\) is Pareto optimal.

Every feasible Pareto-optimal allocation has welfare at most \(V\). Conversely, the returned allocation is Pareto optimal and has welfare \(V\). Therefore the maximum defining \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{PO}}\) exists, and

\( W(x)=V=\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{PO}}. \)

Since this value is nonnegative, the requested guarantee follows:

\( W(x)\ge(1-1/e)\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{PO}}. \)

This argument includes types of weight zero: a strict improvement for such a type still strictly increases \(Q\).

4. Exact polynomial-time solvability in the oracle model

The relevant separation-to-optimization theorem states that, for a rational polyhedron given with its dimension and a bound on the encoding length of its defining inequalities, exact rational linear optimization can be performed in oracle-polynomial time using a strong separation oracle. This is the strong separation/strong optimization equivalence of Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver, Theorem 6.4.9; it applies also to polyhedra of lower dimension. [Geometric Algorithms and Combinatorial Optimization, Chapter 6](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4_7).

We verify the required hypotheses for both optimization steps.

Separation for \(F\). Let \(d=m\tau\). Given a rational query \(z\), first check nonnegativity, the type supply constraints, and the required zero coordinates. Each violated constraint directly provides a separating inequality. If these checks pass, query each category oracle on the vector \((z_{ta})_{t\in T}\). A separating inequality returned for \(P_a\) is lifted to the full allocation space by giving all other category coordinates coefficient zero. If every category oracle accepts, then \(z\in F\).

Thus separation uses at most \(m\) category-oracle calls and polynomial additional work. The stipulated oracle running-time bound ensures polynomial running time whenever the query has polynomial encoding length.

Bounded inequality encoding length. Although \(F\) has exponentially many rank inequalities, every defining inequality has coefficients in \(\{-1,0,1\}\) and a rational right-hand side of bit length bounded by \(L\), up to a fixed encoding constant. Its individual encoding length is therefore polynomial in \(d,L\). The theorem requires this bound, rather than an explicit enumeration of the inequalities.

Polynomial encoding length of \(V\). A linear objective over the nonempty bounded polytope \(F\) attains its maximum at a vertex. At a vertex, one can choose \(d\) linearly independent active constraints. Otherwise a nonzero direction orthogonal to all active constraint normals would permit sufficiently small feasible movement in both directions, contradicting extremality.

After clearing each selected row’s denominator, the resulting integer system has entries of magnitude at most \(2^{O(L)}\). Hadamard’s determinant bound and Cramer’s rule consequently give coordinate encoding lengths \(O(d(L+\log d))\). Evaluating the rational objective \(W\) at such a vertex shows that \(V\) has encoding length polynomial in \(d,L\).

Separation and description bounds for \(F_V\). Use the separation oracle for \(F\), together with the two additional inequalities

\( W(z)\le V, \qquad -W(z)\le -V. \)

Their encoding lengths are polynomial by the preceding paragraph. Hence \(F_V\) is also a rational polytope with a polynomial description bound and a polynomial-time exact separation oracle. It may have lower dimension, which is allowed by the stated theorem.

Finally, the coefficients of \(Q\) are integers between zero and \(m\), so its objective encoding length is polynomial. Applying the theorem twice yields an exact rational output and total running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). No expansion into individual agents or dependence on reciprocal masses is required.

5. Relation to the cited discrete result

The cited discrete result imposes universal truthfulness in addition to Pareto optimality and approximation. The present task asks for an allocation algorithm without that incentive requirement. Its stated welfare objective is monotone under stochastic Pareto improvements, enabling the exact two-stage optimization above.
