The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given courses \(C\) with integral credits \(O(c)\), types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita capacities \(q_c\), and an explicit allocation \(x_{t,A}\) of each type's mass to feasible integral bundles \(A\), decide whether the allocation is pair-size-stable. A block exists if some positive mass assigned to \(A\) can switch to \((A\setminus D)\cup\{c\}\), where \(c\notin A\), the type prefers \(c\) to every course in \(D\subseteq A\), \(O(c)\ge O(D)\), and \(O(A)-O(D)+O(c)\le T_t\); course \(c\) must have spare capacity or be able to replace lower-priority mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a set of \(m\) courses with positive integer credits \(O(c)\), and write \(O(A)=\sum_{c\in A}O(c)\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type \(t\) has a positive integer credit limit \(T_t\) and a strict ranking \(\succ_t\) of its acceptable courses \(P_t\). Each course has a positive rational capacity \(q_c\) and a strict priority order \(\succ_c\) over its acceptable types; acceptability is mutual. Members of the same type have equal priority.
A feasible integral bundle for \(t\) is any \(A\subseteq P_t\) with \(O(A)\le T_t\), including the empty bundle; there are no additional bundle constraints or lower quotas.
The input allocation explicitly lists its \(K\) positive binary-rational entries \(x_{t,A}\), with unlisted entries zero, feasible bundles only, \(\sum_A x_{t,A}=\mu_t\), and course loads \(\ell_c=\sum_{t,A:c\in A}x_{t,A}\le q_c\). All integers are binary encoded; \(L\) is the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,K,L\).
A block consists of \(t,A,c,D\) with \(x_{t,A}>0\), \(c\in P_t\setminus A\), and \(D\subseteq A\), possibly empty, such that \(c\succ_t d\) for every \(d\in D\), \(O(D)\le O(c)\), and \(O(A)-O(D)+O(c)\le T_t\).
It additionally requires a positive rational \(\varepsilon\le x_{t,A}\) satisfying \(\varepsilon\le q_c-\ell_c+\sum_{u:t\succ_c u}\sum_{B:c\in B}x_{u,B}\): this mass switches to \((A\setminus D)\cup\{c\}\), using vacancies and, if needed, displacing mass of strictly lower-priority types from \(c\). Bundles remain integral even though mass can be split. The allocation is pair-size-stable exactly when no block exists.

Is testing pair-size stability polynomial-time solvable, or is its complementary problem of detecting a block \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite societies, testing pair-size stability is weakly \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete, even with one student and no additional bundle constraints ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21229v2#S3)).
No complexity classification for this exact mass-input problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified positive binary-encoded integer credits and credit limits, and positive binary-rational capacities.
  • Supplied mutually acceptable pairs, strict type preferences, and strict course priorities over types; members of one type have equal priority and cannot displace one another.
  • Interpreted feasible bundles as acceptable subsets satisfying the credit limit, without additional bundle constraints or lower quotas, matching the anchor's setting.
  • Made the explicit allocation a sparse list of rational mass entries, including empty bundles when needed, and required mass conservation and capacity feasibility.
  • Allowed an empty drop set, as required by the discrete blocking definition.
  • Defined a block using an arbitrarily small positive rational mass, with admission through vacancies and displacement of strictly lower-priority assigned mass; splitting mass does not permit fractional course bundles.
  • Expressed the NP-hardness alternative for block detection because the anchor concerns co-NP-completeness of stability testing.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Classification. Detecting a block is weakly \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, and testing pair-size stability is weakly \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even when \(\tau=K=1\), every capacity is \(1\), and the sole type’s entire mass receives one bundle. Thus polynomial-time stability testing would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof below applies directly to the stated mass-input model.

1. Exact characterization of a block.

For each acceptable type-course pair, define its available mass by
\[ r_{t,c}=q_c-\ell_c+\sum_{u:t\succ_c u}\sum_{B:c\in B}x_{u,B}. \]
Here and below, displayed formulas are understood with the same mathematical delimiters as inline formulas.

Fix a listed entry \(x_{t,A}>0\) and a course \(c\in P_t\setminus A\). Define
\(E_{t,A,c}=\{d\in A:c\succ_t d\}\) and
\(a_{t,A,c}=\max\{0,O(A)+O(c)-T_t\}\).

Lemma. This entry and course admit a block exactly when \(r_{t,c}>0\) and there exists \(D\subseteq E_{t,A,c}\) satisfying
\(a_{t,A,c}\le O(D)\le O(c)\).

Proof. The preference requirement is precisely \(D\subseteq E_{t,A,c}\). The credit-limit inequality rearranges to
\(O(D)\ge O(A)+O(c)-T_t\).
Since \(O(D)\ge0\), combining it with the required inequality \(O(D)\le O(c)\) gives the stated interval.

The mass condition is equivalent to \(r_{t,c}>0\): necessity follows from \(0<\varepsilon\le r_{t,c}\). Conversely, when \(r_{t,c}>0\), the positive rational
\(\varepsilon=\min\{x_{t,A},r_{t,c}\}\)
satisfies both mass bounds. This proves both directions. \(\square\)

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

A certificate for block detection consists of a listed-entry index, a course index, and an \(m\)-bit indicator vector for \(D\). The lemma gives a polynomial-time verifier; no numerical certificate for \(\varepsilon\) is needed.

For completeness, exact rational arithmetic also has polynomial bit complexity. Put all input masses and capacities over the common denominator obtained by multiplying their input denominators. Its bit length is at most the total denominator bit length, hence at most \(L\). All relevant sums and comparisons consequently involve integers with \(O(L)\) bits. Credit sums likewise have \(O(L)\) bits.

More explicitly, compute
\(M_{u,c}=\sum_{B:c\in B}x_{u,B}\).
Scanning the allocation requires at most \(Km\) rational additions. For each course, suffix sums along its priority order compute every \(r_{t,c}\), using at most \(m\tau\) further additions. Preference ranks can be stored in the same polynomial preprocessing.

A conservative bound for preprocessing and checking one certificate is
\(O((Km+m\tau+m)L^3)\)
bit operations. Thus block detection belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), and stability testing belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\).

3. Hardness with one supported type and one allocation entry.

We reduce from Subset Sum with positive binary-encoded integers: given \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) and a positive target \(b\), decide whether some index set \(I\) satisfies
\(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=b\).
This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; it is also the source problem used in [Theorem 1 of Rodríguez and Manlove](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21229v2#S3). We may assume \(n\ge1\), since the empty-list cases are trivial.

Create courses \(d_1,\ldots,d_n,c^\ast\), with credits
\(O(d_i)=a_i\) and \(O(c^\ast)=b\).
There is one type \(t\), with
\(\mu_t=1\) and \(T_t=S:=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\).
It accepts every course and ranks \(c^\ast\) first, followed by the other courses in any strict order. Every course has capacity \(1\) and accepts only \(t\).

The sole allocation entry is
\(x_{t,A}=1\), where \(A=\{d_1,\ldots,d_n\}\).
This input is feasible: \(O(A)=S=T_t\), each \(d_i\) has load \(1\), and \(c^\ast\) has load \(0\). All required masses and capacities are positive binary-rational numbers.

The only course absent from the sole allocated bundle is \(c^\ast\). It has available mass \(r_{t,c^\ast}=1\) and is preferred to every course in \(A\). Therefore a block exists exactly when some \(D\subseteq A\) satisfies
\(O(D)\le b\) and \(S-O(D)+b\le S\).
The second inequality gives \(O(D)\ge b\), so these conditions are equivalent to \(O(D)=b\).

Consequently, a Subset Sum solution gives a block, with \(\varepsilon=1\); conversely, every block gives a Subset Sum solution.

The construction has \(m=n+1\) and \(\tau=K=1\). Computing \(S\) and writing the instance takes polynomial time in the source input length. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness of block detection and, by complementation, \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness of stability testing.

4. A pseudo-polynomial algorithm.

Let \(W=\max_{c\in C}O(c)\); if there are no courses, the allocation is immediately stable.

After the preprocessing above, inspect each of the at most \(Km\) candidate entry-course pairs. Discard candidates with \(r_{t,c}=0\). For every remaining candidate, use subset-sum dynamic programming on the credits of \(E_{t,A,c}\), retaining sums only through \(O(c)\).

Explicitly, enumerate these credits as \(w_1,\ldots,w_h\), where \(h\le m\). Set \(R_0=\{0\}\), and recursively define
\(R_i=R_{i-1}\cup\{s+w_i:s\in R_{i-1},\ s+w_i\le O(c)\}\).

By induction, \(R_i\) contains exactly the subset sums of the first \(i\) credits that are at most \(O(c)\). Positivity of the credits justifies truncation: a partial sum exceeding \(O(c)\) cannot later decrease.

The candidate admits a block precisely when \(R_h\) contains an integer in
\([a_{t,A,c},O(c)]\), by the lemma. A Boolean-array implementation uses \(O(m(W+1))\) updates per candidate. A conservative total running time is
\(O((Km+m\tau+Km^2(W+1))L^3)\)
bit operations.

This is pseudo-polynomial and becomes polynomial when course credits are unary encoded or bounded by a polynomial in \(L\). Combined with the hardness and membership proofs, it establishes the claimed weak completeness classification for the exact mass-input problem.
