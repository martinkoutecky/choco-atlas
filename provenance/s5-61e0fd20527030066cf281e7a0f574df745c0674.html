Theorem 2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational recipient-type masses \(\mu\in\Delta_\tau\), rational resource-type probabilities \(p\in\Delta_L\), utilities \(v_{a\ell}\in[0,1]\), initial per-capita utilities \(B_0(a)\), horizon \(T\), and \(\eta\in(1,4/3)\), each arriving resource type \(\ell_t\sim p\) must be allocated online using \(x_{t,a}\ge0\) with \(\sum_a\mu_a x_{t,a}=1\). Can one compute a nonanticipative policy using at most \(O(\log\log T)\) fluid re-solves whose expected regret against \(\max_x\min_{a:\mu_a>0}B_T(a)\) is \(O(1)\) in \(T\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) recipient types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\ge1\) resource types, with known positive rational probabilities \(p_\ell\) summing to \(1\), rational utilities \(v_{a\ell}\in[0,1]\), and rational initial per-capita utilities \(B_0(a)\ge0\). Let \(L\) be the input bit length, \(T\ge1\) an integer horizon, and \(\eta\in(1,4/3)\) rational.
At each period, an independent type \(\ell_t\sim p\) is revealed before a deterministic, immediate, irrevocable allocation \(x_{t,a}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_a\mu_a x_{t,a}=1\). Every recipient of type \(a\) receives the per-capita amount \(x_{t,a}\), with \(B_t(a)=B_{t-1}(a)+v_{a\ell_t}x_{t,a}\); allocations depend only on the revealed history.
For each realized arrival sequence, define \(\operatorname{OPT}_T=\max_{y_{t,a}\ge0,\,\sum_a\mu_a y_{t,a}=1}\min_a\{B_0(a)+\sum_{t=1}^T v_{a\ell_t}y_{t,a}\}\), allowing the maximizer to know all arrivals. Regret is \(\mathbb E[\operatorname{OPT}_T-\min_a B_T(a)]\).
A fluid solve at time \(s\) maximizes \(\min_a\{B_s(a)+(T-s)\sum_{\ell=1}^m p_\ell v_{a\ell}\xi_{\ell,a}\}\) subject to \(\xi_{\ell,a}\ge0\) and \(\sum_a\mu_a\xi_{\ell,a}=1\). Between solves, the policy uses a fixed feasible allocation table indexed by resource type; it may adjust the fluid solution before fixing that table, using deterministic tie-breaking.
Is constructing and executing such a nonanticipative policy with at most \(O(1+\log\log(T+2))\) fluid solves and regret bounded by \(C(\mu,p,v,\eta)\), independently of \(T\) and \(B_0\), possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,T\), or is this policy-construction problem NP-hard?
For finite lists of agents and divisible resources under known finite IID arrivals, backward infrequent re-solving with thresholding attains \(O(1)\) egalitarian regret with \(O(\log\log T)\) fluid solves; the bound does not require nondegeneracy. The epoch guarantee is uniform over nonnegative initial utilities. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12447).
No result explicitly addressing this recipient-mass formulation was verified in the literature search.

Conclusion

The policy can be constructed and executed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,T\). The recipient masses admit an exact change of variables to a finite-agent instance with precisely \(\tau\) agents. No expansion according to the masses’ denominators is needed.

Below, the re-solving schedule uses only integer arithmetic. It works for every permitted \(\eta\), with a regret bound that is actually independent of \(\eta\).

1. Exact reduction to a finite-agent instance

Let \(\delta=\min_a\mu_a>0\). Define total resource shares and normalized utilities by

\(z_{t,a}=\mu_a x_{t,a},\qquad b_t(a)=\delta B_t(a),\qquad \beta_{a\ell}=\delta v_{a\ell}/\mu_a.\)

The allocation constraint becomes \(z_{t,a}\ge0\) and \(\sum_a z_{t,a}=1\). Moreover,

\(b_t(a)=b_{t-1}(a)+\beta_{a\ell_t}z_{t,a}.\)

Because \(\delta\le\mu_a\) and \(v_{a\ell}\in[0,1]\), every \(\beta_{a\ell}\) belongs to \([0,1]\). Thus this is a finite-agent instance with \(n=\tau\) agents and finite known IID utility-vector arrivals.

The correspondence is bijective, with inverse \(x_{t,a}=z_{t,a}/\mu_a\). It preserves nonanticipativity. For every realized arrival sequence and every corresponding allocation,

\(\min_a b_T(a)=\delta\min_a B_T(a).\)

Consequently, if \(\operatorname{OPT}'_T\) denotes the transformed hindsight optimum, then

\(\operatorname{OPT}'_T=\delta\operatorname{OPT}_T.\)

The same substitution transforms each original fluid problem into

\(\max_{z_{\ell,a}\ge0,\ \sum_a z_{\ell,a}=1}\min_a\{b_s(a)+(T-s)\sum_\ell p_\ell\beta_{a\ell}z_{\ell,a}\}.\)

Its objective is exactly \(\delta\) times the original fluid objective. Therefore every transformed fluid solve is an original fluid solve under this coordinate change.

If several resource labels have identical vectors \(\beta_{\cdot\ell}\), merge them and add their probabilities. Let \(M\le m\) be the resulting number of distinct vectors, and let \(\mathcal P\) be their distribution. The constructed table assigns the same allocation to all original labels in a merged class.

2. The imported epoch guarantee

We use the following precise consequence of Algorithm 2 and Proposition 2 of [Balseiro–Xia, *Uniformly Bounded Regret in Dynamic Fair Allocation*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12447).

For \(n\) agents, finite IID distribution \(\mathcal P\) supported in \([0,1]^n\), initial utilities \(b\ge0\), and integers \(0<r<h\), solve the fluid problem with horizon \(h\). Set \(\gamma=r/(2n^2h)\). In each allocation row, retain a largest coordinate; delete other coordinates below \(\gamma\), transferring their total to the retained coordinate. Use this table for \(h-r\) periods.

Let \(V\) be the hindsight optimum before these allocations and \(V^+\) the hindsight continuation optimum afterward, including accumulated utilities. There is \(c_{\mathcal P}>0\), independent of \(b,h,r\), such that

\(\mathbb E[V-V^+\mid b]\le3M(h-r)\exp(-c_{\mathcal P}r^4/h^3).\)

All hypotheses hold after the reduction: the agent list is finite, arrivals are known finite IID vectors in \([0,1]^n\), and utilities remain nonnegative. In particular, the constant is uniform over the initial utilities; no nondegeneracy assumption is required.

3. An exactly computable policy

Define remaining horizons starting at \(h_0=T\). Whenever \(h_k>32\), set

\(h_{k+1}=\lceil h_k^{4/5}\rceil.\)

Stop at the first \(K\) with \(h_K\le32\), and define solve times \(s_k=T-h_k\).

At each \(s_k\) with \(k<K\):

  • Solve the transformed fluid problem with current utilities and horizon \(h_k\), using deterministic tie-breaking.
  • Apply the thresholding operation above with \(r=h_{k+1}\).
  • Use that fixed table until \(s_{k+1}\).

At \(s_K\), solve the fluid problem once more and use its unthresholded table for all remaining periods. Translate every allocation back by \(x_{t,a}=z_{t,a}/\mu_a\).

The policy is deterministic and nonanticipative: solve times are predetermined, each solve uses current utilities, and each subsequent allocation uses the fixed table entry for the revealed resource type. Thresholding preserves nonnegativity and row sums, so every allocation is feasible.

The ceiling is computable exactly: it is the smallest positive integer \(r\) satisfying \(r^5\ge h_k^4\). Binary search uses integers with \(O(\log(T+2))\) bits.

4. Number of fluid solves

For \(h>32\),

\(h^{9/10}-h^{4/5}=h^{4/5}(h^{1/10}-1)>16(\sqrt2-1)>1.\)

Therefore

\(\lceil h^{4/5}\rceil\le h^{4/5}+1<h^{9/10}<h.\)

The remaining horizons strictly decrease. While the recurrence continues,

\(\log h_k\le(9/10)^k\log T.\)

It reaches \(h_k\le32\) after \(O(1+\log\log(T+2))\) steps. Including the terminal solve, the policy uses \(K+1=O(1+\log\log(T+2))\) fluid solves.

5. Regret bound uniform over initial utilities

For a realized sequence, let \(V_k\) be the transformed hindsight continuation optimum at \(s_k\), including the utilities accumulated by our policy. Let \(A=\min_a b_T(a)\). Then

\(\operatorname{OPT}'_T-A=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(V_k-V_{k+1})+(V_K-A).\)

Every difference is nonnegative: a hindsight allocation can reproduce the policy’s next epoch and then follow an optimal continuation.

Conditional on the complete history through \(s_k\), future arrivals still have IID law \(\mathcal P\). The epoch guarantee applies to the current nonnegative utilities. Since \(h_{k+1}\ge h_k^{4/5}\),

\(\mathbb E[V_k-V_{k+1}\mid\text{history through }s_k]\le3M h_k\exp(-c_{\mathcal P}h_k^{1/5}).\)

For the terminal epoch, choose an agent whose utility at \(s_K\) is minimal. Each remaining period can increase that agent’s normalized utility by at most \(1\), under any allocation. Hence

\(V_K\le\min_a b_{s_K}(a)+h_K.\)

Our policy has nonnegative utility increments, so \(A\ge\min_a b_{s_K}(a)\). Thus \(V_K-A\le32\).

The nonterminal horizons are distinct integers exceeding \(32\). Taking expectations and summing gives

\(\mathbb E[\operatorname{OPT}'_T-A]\le32+3M\sum_{h=33}^{\infty}h\exp(-c_{\mathcal P}h^{1/5})=:C'.\)

This series converges. Indeed, \(\log h/h^{1/5}\to0\), so eventually \(c_{\mathcal P}h^{1/5}\ge3\log h\); the summand is then at most \(h^{-2}\).

Returning to the original utilities,

\(\mathbb E[\operatorname{OPT}_T-\min_a B_T(a)]\le C'/\delta.\)

This constant depends only on \(\mu,p,v\), and is independent of both \(T\) and \(B_0\).

6. Polynomial bit complexity

The reduction, probability merging, and integer schedule are polynomial-time operations on rational inputs. Each transformed fluid LP has \(M\tau+1\) variables, including its objective variable.

The remaining issue is growth of rational representations during execution. Fix a common denominator \(D\) for the transformed coefficients. Its bit length is polynomial in \(L\). After clearing coefficient denominators, the constraint matrix at horizon \(h\) has integer entries of polynomial bit length in \(L+\log(T+2)\).

Suppose the current utility vector has common denominator \(d\). Use a deterministic polynomial-time exact LP algorithm returning an optimal vertex. If \(A_I\) is a nonsingular active constraint matrix defining that vertex, Cramer’s rule gives a common coordinate denominator dividing \(d|\det A_I|\). The determinant’s bit length is polynomial in the matrix dimension and coefficient bit length.

Thresholding only deletes coordinates and adds their values to another coordinate. It therefore introduces no additional denominator. Updating utilities over the epoch gives a common denominator dividing

\(d|\det A_I|D.\)

Thus each epoch adds only polynomially many bits to a common denominator. There are \(O(1+\log\log(T+2))\) epochs. Also, each normalized utility is bounded above by its initial value plus \(T\), so numerators have polynomial bit length as well. Translating back by division by \(\mu_a\) preserves polynomial bit length.

Every LP and arithmetic operation consequently has polynomial bit complexity. Executing the policy requires \(T\) allocation steps, giving total time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,T\). The algorithm does not need to compute the analytical regret constant.

This proves the requested polynomial-time construction and execution guarantee.
