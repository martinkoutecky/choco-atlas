The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support rational society \(T=\{p_1,\ldots,p_\tau\}\subseteq\mathbb Q^2\) with mass vector \(\mu\), sample \(\theta\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,\pi/2)\), take weighted medians of the two coordinates of \(R_\theta p_t\), and return the inverse-rotated point; compute this mechanism, prove universal strategyproofness under finite-replication deviations, and determine \(\sup_{(T,\mu)}\mathbb E_\theta[\mathrm{SC}_\mu(M_\infty)]/\min_x\mathrm{SC}_\mu(x)=4/\pi\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: distinct types \(T=\{p_1,\ldots,p_\tau\}\subseteq\mathbb Q^m\), with \(m=2\), and positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Each agent's cost at facility \(x\in\mathbb R^2\) is Euclidean distance; define \(\mathrm{SC}_\mu(x)=\sum_t\mu_t\|p_t-x\|_2\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu=\min_{x\in\mathbb R^2}\mathrm{SC}_\mu(x)\).
For scalar reports \(a_t\), select the lower weighted median \(\min\{a_t:\sum_{s:a_s\le a_t}\mu_s\ge 1/2\}\), including when the median interval is nontrivial.
Independently of reports, sample \(\theta\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,\pi/2)\). Let \(R_\theta\) be counterclockwise rotation, take the selected weighted median in each coordinate of \(R_\theta T\), and inverse-rotate the resulting point; denote the output by \(M_\theta(T,\mu)\).
Exact mechanism computation means constructing a finite piecewise rational description of \(u\mapsto M_{\arctan u}(T,\mu)\) for \(u\in[0,\infty)\), with binary rational coefficients and breakpoints and specified values at breakpoints.
A finite-replication deviation replaces one report in any replication with \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\) agents at each \(p_t\): one agent at \(p_t\) reports \(y\in\mathbb Q^2\), moving mass \(1/N\) from \(p_t\) to \(y\). Universal strategyproofness requires that agent's true Euclidean cost never decrease, for every such \(N,t,y\) and every fixed \(\theta\).
Define \(\alpha=\sup_{(T,\mu):\mathrm{OPT}_\mu>0}\mathbb E_\theta[\mathrm{SC}_\mu(M_\theta(T,\mu))]/\mathrm{OPT}_\mu\).

Is exact mechanism computation in P or NP-hard, with running times stated in \(m=2\), \(\tau\), and \(L\)?
Is the mechanism universally strategyproof under finite-replication deviations, and is \(\alpha=4/\pi\), or does it have a different sharp approximation ratio?

For finite lists of agents, randomly rotated coordinate-wise median is universally strategyproof and takes \(O(d^3+nd^2)\) arithmetic operations; its planar expected approximation ratio is exactly \(4/\pi\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24750v2)

The mechanism is universally strategyproof under every finite-replication deviation in the question, and its sharp approximation ratio is \(\alpha=4/\pi\). Sharpness already holds for societies with three distinct rational types and dyadic masses.

The [cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24750v2) establishes the corresponding finite-list result. Below, all three conclusions are proved directly for the support encoding and the specified lower-median rule.

2. Exact computation from the support. Write \(p_t=(a_t,b_t)\). For \(u\in[0,\infty)\), put \(\theta=\arctan u\). Then

\(\displaystyle R_\theta p_t=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}(a_t-u b_t,\ b_t+u a_t).\)

Define the rational affine functions

\(\displaystyle f_t(u)=a_t-u b_t,\qquad g_t(u)=b_t+u a_t.\)

Because the common scaling factor is positive, the selected coordinate medians are obtained by finding the lower weighted medians of the \(f_t(u)\) and \(g_t(u)\).

Breakpoints and intervals. Form the set \(B\) consisting of \(0\) and every nonnegative solution of

\(\displaystyle f_s(u)=f_t(u)\quad\text{or}\quad g_s(u)=g_t(u),\qquad s<t.\)

Each equation is affine and has at most one solution. Neither can be an identity: an identity would imply \(a_s=a_t\) and \(b_s=b_t\), contrary to distinctness. Consequently, \(B\) contains at most \(1+2\binom{\tau}{2}\) elements, all rational.

Sort and deduplicate \(B\), obtaining \(0=\beta_0<\cdots<\beta_K\). On every open interval between consecutive breakpoints, and on \((\beta_K,\infty)\), the orders of both families of affine functions are constant. Indeed, a change in order would require a pairwise equality inside that interval.

For each interval, choose a rational sample point: its midpoint for a bounded interval, and \(\beta_K+1\) for the final interval. Sort each family at this sample point and scan the masses until the cumulative mass first reaches \(1/2\). Let \(r\) and \(s\) denote the resulting indices for the first and second coordinates. These indices select the weighted medians throughout the interval.

Rational formula. On that interval, the rotated output is

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}\bigl(f_r(u),g_s(u)\bigr).\)

Applying the inverse rotation gives

\(\displaystyle M_{\arctan u}(T,\mu)= \left( \frac{a_r+u(b_s-b_r)+u^2a_s}{1+u^2}, \frac{b_s+u(a_s-a_r)+u^2b_r}{1+u^2} \right).\)

Thus both coordinates are rational functions with rational coefficients.

At every \(\beta_j\), separately sort the exact values \(f_t(\beta_j)\) and \(g_t(\beta_j)\) and apply the prescribed lower-median rule. Tied values can be ordered arbitrarily: whichever tied index first reaches the threshold has the same scalar value. Substitution into the formula above therefore gives the exact rational output at that breakpoint.

This specifies all open intervals and every breakpoint, including \(0\). No endpoint at infinity is needed because the domain contains only finite \(u\).

Bit complexity. To make the arithmetic bounds explicit, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input coordinates, and let \(W\) be the product of the denominators of all masses. Set

\(\displaystyle A_t=Da_t,\qquad B_t=Db_t,\qquad w_t=W\mu_t.\)

These are integers with \(O(L)\) bits, and \(\sum_t w_t=W\). Weighted-median comparisons can therefore use the integer test \(2\sum w_t\ge W\).

Every breakpoint is a ratio of differences of the \(A_t,B_t\), so its numerator and denominator have \(O(L)\) bits. The sample points also have \(O(L)\)-bit encodings. If a sample is \(u=h/q\), with \(q>0\), the orders can be determined by sorting the integers

\(\displaystyle qA_t-hB_t\quad\text{and}\quad qB_t+hA_t.\)

All these integers have \(O(L)\) bits. Formula coefficients and separately specified breakpoint outputs likewise have \(O(L)\)-bit rational encodings.

There are \(O(\tau^2)\) intervals and breakpoint evaluations. Each requires \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) comparisons and \(O(\tau)\) mass additions. Including breakpoint construction and sorting, the total is \(O(\tau^3\log(\tau+1))\) arithmetic operations. Using unreduced numerator-denominator encodings and schoolbook integer arithmetic gives the stated bound of \(O(\tau^3\log(\tau+1)L^2)\) bit operations.

Each piece contains only a constant number of rational coefficients and endpoints, so the output length is \(O(\tau^2L)\). Hence the construction problem belongs to \(\mathrm{FP}\), the function analogue of \(\mathrm{P}\). No replication is constructed, and the runtime has no dependence on its population size.

3. Universal strategyproofness. Fix any allowed replication size \(N\), any deviating agent, and any fixed angle \(\theta\). In the replicated list, the selected scalar median is the order statistic of rank

\(\displaystyle k=\left\lceil N/2\right\rceil.\)

Indeed, a cumulative count first reaches half the total mass exactly when it first reaches \(N/2\). For even \(N\), this selects the lower median as required.

We first prove strategyproofness for this scalar order statistic. If \(N=1\), the output equals the report, and reporting the true scalar location gives zero cost. Suppose \(N\ge2\), and sort the other agents’ scalar reports as \(c_1\le\cdots\le c_{N-1}\). Put \(c_0=-\infty\).

For a report \(z\), the \(k\)-th order statistic is

\(\displaystyle h(z)=\min\{\max\{z,c_{k-1}\},c_k\}.\)

For \(k\ge2\), inserting \(z\le c_{k-1}\) makes the \(k\)-th value \(c_{k-1}\); inserting \(z\) between \(c_{k-1}\) and \(c_k\) makes it \(z\); and inserting \(z\ge c_k\) makes it \(c_k\). For \(k=1\), the formula reduces to \(h(z)=\min\{z,c_1\}\). These statements remain valid with ties.

Thus \(h(z)\) is the projection of \(z\) onto the interval \(I=[c_{k-1},c_k]\), interpreted as \((-\infty,c_1]\) when \(k=1\). For a true scalar location \(a\), \(h(a)\) is a closest point of \(I\) to \(a\). Since every possible output \(h(z)\) belongs to \(I\),

\(\displaystyle |a-h(a)|\le |a-h(z)|\qquad\text{for every report }z.\)

Now work in the coordinates rotated by the fixed \(\theta\). In each coordinate, the other agents’ reports remain fixed. Applying this scalar inequality separately to the deviator’s two true coordinates shows that neither coordinate’s absolute error can decrease under a deviation. Squaring and adding gives

\(\displaystyle \|R_\theta p_t-M_{\mathrm{rot}}^{\mathrm{truth}}\|_2 \le \|R_\theta p_t-M_{\mathrm{rot}}^{\mathrm{deviation}}\|_2. \)

Inverse rotation preserves Euclidean distance. Moreover, the weighted support description before and after the deviation gives exactly the same coordinate medians as the corresponding replicated lists, including after coincident reports are aggregated.

Therefore the agent’s true Euclidean cost cannot decrease. This holds for every allowed \(N,t,y\) and every fixed \(\theta\), proving universal strategyproofness.

4. Upper approximation bound. We first establish the weighted-median minimization property, including the lower-median tie rule.

Let \(q\) be a selected lower weighted median of scalar locations \(z_t\). By its definition, the mass at or below \(q\) is at least \(1/2\), and the mass strictly below \(q\) is less than \(1/2\). In particular, the mass strictly above \(q\) is at most \(1/2\).

For \(x>q\), each \(z_t\le q\) contributes exactly \(x-q\) to the difference \(|z_t-x|-|z_t-q|\); each \(z_t>q\) contributes at least \(-(x-q)\), by the triangle inequality. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_t\bigl(|z_t-x|-|z_t-q|\bigr) \ge (x-q)\left(\sum_{z_t\le q}\mu_t-\sum_{z_t>q}\mu_t\right) \ge0. \)

For \(x<q\), the corresponding argument partitions the locations into those at or above \(q\) and those strictly below \(q\). It gives the same nonnegativity conclusion. Hence \(q\) minimizes \(\sum_t\mu_t|z_t-x|\).

The Euclidean social cost has a minimizer \(x^\star\): it is continuous, and

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{SC}_\mu(x)\ge \|x\|_2-\sum_t\mu_t\|p_t\|_2,\)

so it is coercive.

For each \(\theta\), put \(q_\theta=R_\theta M_\theta(T,\mu)\). Applying the scalar minimization property in both coordinates yields

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} \mathrm{SC}_\mu(M_\theta) &=\sum_t\mu_t\|R_\theta p_t-q_\theta\|_2\\ &\le\sum_t\mu_t\|R_\theta p_t-q_\theta\|_1\\ &\le\sum_t\mu_t\|R_\theta(p_t-x^\star)\|_1. \end{aligned} \)

For a fixed nonzero vector \(v=r(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)\), the function \(|\cos z|+|\sin z|\) has period \(\pi/2\). Its integral over any interval of that length is therefore the same as over \([0,\pi/2]\), where the integral is \(2\). Thus

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_\theta\|R_\theta v\|_1 = \frac{2r}{\pi}\int_\phi^{\phi+\pi/2}(|\cos z|+|\sin z|)\,dz = \frac{4}{\pi}\|v\|_2. \)

The identity also holds for \(v=0\). Taking expectations in the preceding inequality gives

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_\theta[\mathrm{SC}_\mu(M_\theta)] \le \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_t\mu_t\|p_t-x^\star\|_2 = \frac{4}{\pi}\mathrm{OPT}_\mu. \)

These expectations are well-defined: the exact description proves measurability, and the displayed pointwise bound is uniformly finite. Consequently, \(\alpha\le4/\pi\).

5. Matching lower bound with three rational types. Let \(H=2^j\), where \(j\ge1\), and put \(\varepsilon=H^{-2}\). Consider

\(\displaystyle A=(-1,0),\qquad B=(1,0),\qquad C=(0,H),\)

with masses

\(\displaystyle \mu_A=\mu_B=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2},\qquad \mu_C=\varepsilon.\)

The types are distinct and rational; the masses are positive dyadic rationals summing to one.

For every facility \(x\), the triangle inequality gives \(\|A-x\|_2+\|B-x\|_2\ge2\). Evaluating the social cost at the origin also gives an upper bound. Together,

\(\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon\le\mathrm{OPT}_\mu\le1-\varepsilon+\varepsilon H.\)

In particular, \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu>0\).

Write \(c=\cos\theta\) and \(s=\sin\theta\). The rotated reports are

\(\displaystyle R_\theta A=(-c,-s),\qquad R_\theta B=(c,s),\qquad R_\theta C=(-Hs,Hc).\)

Consider the interval

\(\displaystyle a_H=\arctan(1/H)<\theta<b_H=\arctan H.\)

Here \(Hs>c\) and \(Hc>s\), so the first-coordinate order is \(C,A,B\), and the second-coordinate order is \(A,B,C\).

In the first coordinate, the mass at \(C\) is less than \(1/2\), while the cumulative mass through \(A\) is \((1+\varepsilon)/2>1/2\). Thus the selected median is \(-c\). In the second coordinate, the mass at \(A\) is less than \(1/2\), while the cumulative mass through \(B\) is \(1-\varepsilon>1/2\). Thus the selected median is \(s\).

The rotated output is therefore \((-c,s)\), and inverse rotation gives

\(\displaystyle M_\theta=(-\cos(2\theta),\sin(2\theta)).\)

Its distances to the two main types satisfy

\(\displaystyle \|M_\theta-A\|_2=2\sin\theta,\qquad \|M_\theta-B\|_2=2\cos\theta.\)

Ignoring the nonnegative contribution of \(C\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{SC}_\mu(M_\theta)\ge(1-\varepsilon)(\sin\theta+\cos\theta)\)

throughout this interval. Integrating only over this interval gives

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} \mathbb E_\theta[\mathrm{SC}_\mu(M_\theta)] &\ge\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)}{\pi} \int_{a_H}^{b_H}(\sin\theta+\cos\theta)\,d\theta\\ &=\frac{4(1-\varepsilon)}{\pi}\frac{H-1}{\sqrt{H^2+1}}. \end{aligned} \)

For the equality, use \(\sin a_H=1/\sqrt{H^2+1}\), \(\cos a_H=H/\sqrt{H^2+1}\), and the reversed values at \(b_H\).

Using the upper bound on the positive optimum,

\(\displaystyle \frac{\mathbb E_\theta[\mathrm{SC}_\mu(M_\theta)]}{\mathrm{OPT}_\mu} \ge \frac{4}{\pi} \frac{1-H^{-2}}{1-H^{-2}+H^{-1}} \frac{H-1}{\sqrt{H^2+1}}. \)

Both factors following \(4/\pi\) tend to one as \(j\) tends to infinity. These admissible three-type societies therefore have ratios tending to \(4/\pi\), proving \(\alpha\ge4/\pi\). Combined with the upper bound, this establishes \(\alpha=4/\pi\).
