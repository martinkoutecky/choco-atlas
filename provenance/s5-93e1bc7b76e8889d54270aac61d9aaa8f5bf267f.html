The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support of chapter-role types \(T\), positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), direct-voting probabilities \(p_t\), a fixed delegation map within each repeated chapter template, and a target type \(t^\star\) with \(p_{t^\star}>0\), compute the exact expected voting weight of an active member of \(t^\star\): \(\Phi(t^\star)=1+\sum_{s\ne t^\star}(\mu_s/\mu_{t^\star})r(s,t^\star)\), where \(r(s,t^\star)\) is the probability that a member of role \(s\) delegates along the template path to \(t^\star\), and is \(0\) if no such path exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) chapter-role types, with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types.
Types are partitioned into explicitly listed chapter templates, each containing at most \(m\) roles. Each template specifies a map \(D\) from its roles to themselves; delegations remain within a copy of that template.
Each role has a binary-rational direct-voting probability \(p_t\in[0,1]\). Voting decisions of distinct members are independent. An endpoint is represented by \(D(t)=t\) and must have \(p_t=1\).
An active member votes directly and suspends its outgoing delegation; an inactive member forwards its own weight and all received weight to its designated proxy. Weight trapped in an entirely inactive cycle is uncast.
Fix \(t^\star\in T\) with \(p_{t^\star}>0\), and condition on its corresponding template member being active. For \(s\ne t^\star\), let \(r(s,t^\star)\) be the probability that the source member's weight reaches this target: the source and every intermediate member on the first path to the target must be inactive. Set this probability to zero if the target is not reached before a role repeats, or if the types belong to different templates.
Define the mass-normalized active-member influence by \(\Phi(t^\star)=1+\sum_{s\in T\setminus\{t^\star\}}(\mu_s/\mu_{t^\star})r(s,t^\star)\). Masses are arbitrary relative abundances; the template specifies paths without imposing equal masses on its roles. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is exact computation of \(\Phi(t^\star)\) possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computation NP-hard?

Discrete: lottery-based expected weight and first-passage influence are equated for suspendible delegation profiles; the result states no complexity bound ([Grossi–Nitsche, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09789v1#S5.SS4)).
Later work describes expected voting weight as computationally tractable on finite functional delegation graphs with a common delegation probability ([Grossi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13188)).
No result settling this exact support-and-mass problem was located in the sources checked.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Chapter templates were undefined; interpret them as explicitly listed finite role graphs, with delegations confined to each repeated copy.
  • Probability encoding and dependence were unspecified; use binary-rational probabilities in the closed unit interval and independent member voting decisions.
  • Endpoint behavior follows the technical discrete definition: self-delegation represents an endpoint, which votes with probability one.
  • Make activity conditioning explicit, suspend the target's outgoing delegation, and require inactivity only of the source and intermediate members.
  • Resolve cycles using first arrival at the target; a repeated role before arrival means no reaching path, and entirely inactive cycles cast no weight.
  • Preserve arbitrary normalized masses and interpret the supplied formula as mass-normalized influence, without imposing equal role masses required by literal one-member-per-role chapter copies.
  • Define m as the maximum number of roles in a chapter template and L as total binary input length.

Result. Exact computation of \(\Phi(t^\star)\) is possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The proof below includes a bound on the size of the exact rational output.

1. First-passage probabilities.

Let \(C\subseteq T\) be the template containing \(t^\star\), and let \(n=|C|\leq m\). Define \(q_v=1-p_v\) for every role \(v\).

For \(s\in C\setminus\{t^\star\}\), follow its deterministic delegation sequence \(v_0=s\), \(v_{i+1}=D(v_i)\). Suppose it first reaches \(t^\star\) at \(v_k\), before any role repeats. Then

\[ r(s,t^\star)=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}q_{v_i}. \]

Indeed, conditional on the target being active, the source’s weight reaches it exactly when all of \(v_0,\ldots,v_{k-1}\) are inactive. An active role on this path casts the weight and suspends its outgoing delegation; if every role preceding the target is inactive, each forwards the weight to the next role. These roles are distinct and different from the target. Independence of voting decisions therefore gives the displayed product, and conditioning on the target’s activity does not change their probabilities.

If a role repeats before reaching the target, the target can never be reached. To see this, if \(v_i=v_j\) for \(i<j\), determinism gives \(v_{i+\ell}=v_{j+\ell}\) for every nonnegative integer \(\ell\). Thus the subsequent sequence stays in the already encountered cycle. By definition, \(r(s,t^\star)=0\). Sources outside \(C\) also have probability zero.

A successful path contains \(k+1\) distinct roles, so \(k\leq n-1\). An unsuccessful traversal detects a repetition after visiting at most \(n\) distinct roles.

2. Exact algorithm.

Write the input rationals as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\) and \(p_t=c_t/d_t\), where the denominators are positive integers and all integers are encoded in binary. Thus \(a_t>0\) and \(0\leq c_t\leq d_t\).

Construct the positive integer

\[ Q=\left(\prod_{t\in T}b_t\right)\left(\prod_{t\in T}d_t\right). \]

Initialize \(K=a_{t^\star}(Q/b_{t^\star})\). For each \(s\in C\setminus\{t^\star\}\), traverse its delegation sequence until reaching the target or encountering a repetition.

If the target is reached along \(v_0=s,\ldots,v_k=t^\star\), set

\[ N_s=a_s\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(d_{v_i}-c_{v_i}), \qquad H_s=b_s\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}d_{v_i}, \]

and add \(N_s(Q/H_s)\) to \(K\). If a repetition occurs first, add zero.

Every division here is exact. In particular, \(H_s\) divides \(Q\): the path roles are distinct, so its denominator product uses each input probability denominator at most once, together with the source’s mass denominator.

Consequently, after processing all sources,

\[ \frac KQ =\mu_{t^\star} +\sum_{s\in T\setminus\{t^\star\}}\mu_s r(s,t^\star). \]

Return the rational number

\[ \frac{K b_{t^\star}}{Q a_{t^\star}}. \]

Because \(\mu_{t^\star}>0\), this division is defined. Substituting the preceding identity proves that the returned value is exactly \(\Phi(t^\star)\).

This algorithm also covers targets lying on delegation cycles: each source traversal stops at its first arrival at the active target. It never follows the target’s outgoing delegation.

3. Bit complexity.

The sum of the binary lengths of all input denominators is at most \(L\). Therefore,

\[ \log_2 Q =\sum_{t\in T}\log_2 b_t+\sum_{t\in T}\log_2 d_t \leq L. \]

Thus \(Q\) has \(O(L)\) bits. Each \(H_s\) divides \(Q\), and \(0\leq N_s\leq H_s\): the latter follows from \(\mu_s\leq1\) and \(0\leq d_v-c_v\leq d_v\). Hence all path products and summands have \(O(L)\) bits.

The accumulator also remains small in binary length. All its summands are nonnegative, and

\[ 0<\frac KQ =\mu_{t^\star}+\sum_{s\ne t^\star}\mu_s r(s,t^\star) \leq\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s=1. \]

Every intermediate accumulator therefore lies between zero and \(Q\). Finally, both \(K b_{t^\star}\) and \(Q a_{t^\star}\) have \(O(L)\) bits. Reduction to lowest terms, if required, uses a polynomial-time integer gcd computation.

Constructing \(Q\) takes \(O(\tau)\) multiplications. The source traversals take \(O(\tau m)\) delegation steps and arithmetic operations altogether. Every arithmetic operation acts on integers of \(O(L)\) bits, so standard binary integer arithmetic gives a total running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

4. Relation to the cited work.

The first-passage product agrees with the formulation in [Grossi–Nitsche, Appendix B.3–B.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09789v1#S5.SS3). The later paper describes tractability for functional graphs with a common delegation probability. [Grossi et al., Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13188#S3).

The proof above independently establishes the requested exact complexity bound for heterogeneous probabilities and arbitrary positive masses. It processes only explicitly supported types; no enumeration of all possible types or voting configurations is needed.
