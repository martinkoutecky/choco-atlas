The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(Z\), rational masses \(\mu_z>0\), persistence \(\rho\), and a stationary type-level equilibrium weight matrix \(W\) with signal weights \(w_z^s\), compute each type’s tagged-member social influence in the block continuum, \(\mathrm{SI}_{\mu}(z)=\frac{w_z^s}{\mu_z}\left[\mu^\top\left((I-\rho W)^{-1}-I\right)\right]_z\), and determine when this resolvent is well-defined.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau=|Z|\geq1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_z\), normalized by \(\sum_z\mu_z=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Fix memory \(m=1\). The supplied stationary equilibrium profile consists of binary rational \(\rho\), \(W\in\mathbb{Q}^{\tau\times\tau}\), and signal weights \(0<w_z^s\leq1\), with \(0<|\rho|\leq1\) and \(w_z^s+\sum_yW_{zy}=1\); entries of \(W\) may be negative.
The continuum has blocks \(B_z\) of mass \(\mu_z\). For \(x\in B_z\), updating is \(a_t(x)=w_z^ss_t(x)+\rho\sum_yW_{zy}\bar a_{y,t-1}\), where \(\bar a_{y,t}=\mu_y^{-1}\int_{B_y}a_t(x)\,d\mu(x)\). Thus \(W_{zy}\) weights a block average, and the corresponding kernel is \(W_{zy}/\mu_y\). Equilibrium weights are input data; finding or certifying an equilibrium is outside the problem.
Tagged-member influence means the response of mass-weighted aggregate future actions per unit mass of a private-signal perturbation within a block, rather than a perturbation confined to a measure-zero singleton.
Let \(I\) be the identity matrix. When the inverse exists, the requested exact values are \(\mathrm{SI}_{\mu}(z)=\frac{w_z^s}{\mu_z}[\mu^{\top}((I-\rho W)^{-1}-I)]_z\). The temporal influence sum is separately defined as \(\frac{w_z^s}{\mu_z}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}[\mu^{\top}(\rho W)^k]_z\).

Is deciding whether the resolvent exists and computing every \(\mathrm{SI}_{\mu}(z)\) exactly polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard, where \(L\) is the total input bit length?
Under what necessary and sufficient conditions does the temporal influence sum converge and equal the resolvent expression?

Discrete: at every stationary linear equilibrium, social influence converges and equals \([\mathbf{1}^{\top}((I-\rho W)^{-1}-I)]_i w_i^s\), allowing signed weights. [Source](https://www.restud.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/MS28075manuscript.pdf).
This discrete result establishes convergence and an identity, but states no complexity classification for the mass-based computation.

  • Normalized masses to sum to one, consistent with a distribution over types and the discrete population-share convention.
  • Fixed memory to one period, as required by the supplied single-matrix resolvent.
  • Specified binary rational persistence and weights so exact computation has a finite input encoding; retained the persistence domain and weight normalization of the discrete model.
  • Interpreted W as weights on block averages, with kernel W_{zy}/mu_y, to fix the placement of mass factors.
  • Interpreted tagged-member influence as a response per unit perturbed mass, since a literal singleton perturbation has zero aggregate effect in an atomless continuum.
  • Kept equilibrium weights supplied rather than requiring equilibrium computation or certification.

1. Conclusion and notation

The resolvent decision problem is in \(\mathsf{P}\), and computing all requested exact values is in \(\mathsf{FP}\). This holds for arbitrary signed rational weights satisfying the input restrictions; equilibrium certification is unnecessary.

Convergence is a separate issue. Full matrix stability is sufficient, but mass-weighted influence can converge even when the matrix has unstable modes, provided the mass-weighted aggregate annihilates those modes.

Write \(n=\tau\), \(A=\rho W\), and \(u=\mu^\top\). All statements below concern the stipulated memory \(m=1\).

2. Why the mass normalization gives the stated influence

Consider a private-signal perturbation of amplitude \(h\) on a measurable subset of \(B_z\) having mass \(\varepsilon>0\), at time zero only. Its effect on the vector of block-average actions at time zero is

\(\displaystyle \delta\bar a_0=\frac{\varepsilon h\,w_z^s}{\mu_z}e_z.\)

Subsequent signals remain unchanged, so the updating equation gives

\(\displaystyle \delta\bar a_k=A^k\delta\bar a_0.\)

The corresponding aggregate-action response, divided by the signal perturbation’s integrated magnitude \(\varepsilon h\), is therefore

\(\displaystyle \frac{u\,\delta\bar a_k}{\varepsilon h}=\frac{w_z^s}{\mu_z}uA^ke_z.\)

Thus the temporal expression in the question is precisely the sum of these future responses. This calculation uses block averages and accounts explicitly for the factor \(\mu_z^{-1}\).

3. Exact polynomial-time computation

Let \(B=I-A\). The resolvent exists exactly when \(\det B\ne0\). If it exists, solve the single rational linear system

\(\displaystyle B^\top v=\mu.\)

Then \(v^\top=uB^{-1}\), and every requested value follows from

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{SI}_\mu(z)=\frac{w_z^s}{\mu_z}(v_z-\mu_z).\)

It remains to justify polynomial bit complexity, rather than merely a polynomial number of arithmetic operations.

Clearing denominators

Write each input rational with a positive integer denominator. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of \(\rho\), all entries of \(W\), and all masses \(\mu_z\). Because their encodings are included in the total input length \(L\), the bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\).

Every denominator occurring in \(B\) divides \(D\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle C=DB^\top,\qquad b=D\mu\)

have integer entries, and the system becomes \(Cv=b\). Their entries have \(O(L)\) bits: forming \(B\) requires only multiplying input rationals and subtracting them from identity entries.

Moreover, \(\det C=D^n\det B\), so singularity is unchanged.

Determinant and output bounds

For an integer matrix with entries bounded in absolute value by \(H\), the determinant of any \(r\times r\) submatrix satisfies

\(\displaystyle |\det M|\le r!H^r.\)

This follows directly from the determinant’s expansion into \(r!\) signed products. Here \(\log_2 H=O(L)\), so every such minor has bit length

\(\displaystyle O\bigl(n(L+\log n)\bigr).\)

Exact fraction-free Gaussian elimination computes determinants in a polynomial number of integer operations. Its intermediate entries are signed minors, or exact quotients of minors, and the preceding bound therefore gives polynomial intermediate bit lengths. Row interchanges handle zero pivots; if no nonzero pivot remains, the determinant is zero.

If \(\det C\ne0\), Cramer’s rule gives

\(\displaystyle v_z=\frac{\det C_z}{\det C},\)

where \(C_z\) replaces column \(z\) of \(C\) by \(b\). All these matrices have the same polynomial entry-size bound. Computing these \(n+1\) determinants therefore takes polynomial bit time, and each resulting rational has polynomial bit length.

Finally, subtracting \(\mu_z\), multiplying by \(w_z^s\), dividing by the positive rational \(\mu_z\), and reducing the fraction also take polynomial bit time. In particular, very small masses do not cause exponentially long output: reciprocation exchanges a rational’s numerator and denominator.

This proves polynomial-time decidability and exact computation in \((\tau,L)\), hence in \((m,\tau,L)\) for the stipulated \(m=1\). NP-hardness of the decision problem under polynomial-time reductions would consequently imply \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).

4. Necessary and sufficient convergence conditions

Work over \(\mathbb C\) to describe the spectrum of \(A\). For each eigenvalue \(\lambda\), define:

  • \(E_\lambda=\ker(A-\lambda I)^n\), its generalized eigenspace;
  • \(P_\lambda\), the projection onto \(E_\lambda\) along the other generalized eigenspaces;
  • \(d_\lambda\), the least positive integer for which \((A-\lambda I)^{d_\lambda}P_\lambda=0\).

These objects exist because the generalized eigenspaces give a direct-sum decomposition of \(\mathbb C^n\). The projections commute with \(A\).

Convergence for every type

The following conditions are equivalent:

  • Every temporal influence sum converges.
  • \(\displaystyle uA^k\longrightarrow0\).
  • \(\displaystyle uP_\lambda=0\quad\text{for every eigenvalue }\lambda\text{ with }|\lambda|\ge1.\)

Whenever these conditions hold, every temporal sum converges absolutely.

Thus the exact requirement is that \(u\) annihilate every generalized eigenspace whose eigenvalue lies outside the open unit disk.

Convergence for one specified type

For an individual type \(z\), the exact condition is

\(\displaystyle uP_\lambda(A-\lambda I)^je_z=0 \quad\text{for every }|\lambda|\ge1 \text{ and every }0\le j<d_\lambda.\)

This allows additional cancellations specific to the coordinate \(z\).

Proof of the conditions

Put \(s_k=uA^ke_z\). Since \(w_z^s/\mu_z>0\), convergence of the temporal influence sum is equivalent to convergence of \(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_k\).

If this series converges, then \(s_k\to0\).

Each spectral projection is a polynomial in \(A\): the polynomials \((t-\lambda)^{d_\lambda}\) are pairwise coprime, so the polynomial Chinese remainder theorem supplies a polynomial \(p_\lambda\) that is congruent to one modulo the factor for \(\lambda\), and zero modulo every other factor. It follows that \(P_\lambda=p_\lambda(A)\).

Writing \(p_\lambda(t)=\sum_r\alpha_rt^r\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle uA^kP_\lambda e_z=\sum_r\alpha_rs_{k+r}\longrightarrow0.\)

For \(\lambda\ne0\), the binomial expansion on \(E_\lambda\) gives

\(\displaystyle uA^kP_\lambda e_z =\sum_{j=0}^{d_\lambda-1} \binom{k}{j}\lambda^{k-j}c_{\lambda,j}, \qquad c_{\lambda,j}=uP_\lambda(A-\lambda I)^je_z.\)

Suppose some \(c_{\lambda,j}\ne0\), and let \(j_*\) be the largest such index. The right-hand side is \(\lambda^k\) times a polynomial in \(k\) of degree \(j_*\), with nonzero leading coefficient

\(\displaystyle \frac{c_{\lambda,j_*}}{j_*!\lambda^{j_*}}.\)

For \(|\lambda|\ge1\), this expression cannot tend to zero. Therefore convergence requires all the displayed coefficients for such eigenvalues to vanish.

Conversely, suppose those coefficients vanish. Every remaining nonzero-eigenvalue contribution has \(|\lambda|<1\), and its absolute value is bounded by a constant times \(k^j|\lambda|^k\). This is summable: the ratio of successive terms tends to \(|\lambda|<1\). The contribution from \(\lambda=0\) eventually vanishes by nilpotence. Hence \(\sum_k|s_k|<\infty\).

This proves the individual criterion, and also proves that \(s_k\to0\) suffices for convergence here.

Applying the criterion to every coordinate proves the vector statement. Indeed, its conditions with \(j=0\) give \(uP_\lambda e_z=0\) for every \(z\), equivalently \(uP_\lambda=0\); that row identity also annihilates all higher powers.

5. When the temporal sum equals the resolvent

For all types simultaneously, convergence and equality to the requested resolvent values hold exactly when both conditions below hold:

\(\displaystyle 1\notin\sigma(A),\qquad uP_\lambda=0\ \text{for every }\lambda\in\sigma(A)\text{ with }|\lambda|\ge1.\)

The first condition ensures that the resolvent exists. The second ensures temporal convergence.

To prove equality, let \(S_N=\sum_{k=1}^{N}uA^k\). Finite telescoping gives

\(\displaystyle S_N(I-A)=uA-uA^{N+1}.\)

Under the convergence condition, \(uA^{N+1}\to0\). If \(I-A\) is invertible, taking limits yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}uA^k =uA(I-A)^{-1} =u\bigl((I-A)^{-1}-I\bigr).\)

Taking coordinate \(z\) and multiplying by \(w_z^s/\mu_z\) proves the identity.

For one specified type \(z\), replace the second condition by the individual criterion in Section 4. Equality still follows. To check this without assuming convergence in other coordinates, observe that invertibility makes \((I-A)^{-1}=q(A)\) for some polynomial \(q\). Indeed, the minimal polynomial of \(A\) is coprime to \(1-t\), and Bézout’s identity supplies \(q\). Consequently, \(s_k\to0\) implies

\(\displaystyle uA^{N+1}(I-A)^{-1}e_z =\sum_r q_rs_{N+1+r}\longrightarrow0,\)

which eliminates the remainder in the finite telescoping identity for that coordinate.

6. Why invertibility and full stability must be distinguished

Full matrix stability, meaning that every eigenvalue of \(A\) has modulus strictly less than one, guarantees both invertibility and convergence. It is sufficient but stronger than necessary for the mass-weighted sums.

The following examples satisfy all displayed numerical restrictions. They are algebraic examples; they are not asserted to be Bayesian equilibria.

Take \(\rho=1\), \(w_1^s=w_2^s=1/2\), and

\(\displaystyle W= \begin{pmatrix} 5/4&-3/4\\ -3/4&5/4 \end{pmatrix}.\)

Its row sums are \(1/2\), and its eigenvalues are \(1/2\) and \(2\). Thus \(I-W\) is invertible.

For equal masses, \(u=(1/2,1/2)\), we have

\(\displaystyle uW^k=2^{-k}u.\)

Both temporal sums converge, and both influences equal \(1/2\), despite the unstable eigenvalue \(2\).

For unequal masses, \(u=(1/3,2/3)\), instead

\(\displaystyle uW^k =2^{-k}(1/2,1/2)+2^k(-1/6,1/6).\)

Neither coordinate tends to zero, so both temporal sums diverge although the resolvent exists.

Conversely, with equal masses and

\(\displaystyle W= \begin{pmatrix} 3/4&-1/4\\ -1/4&3/4 \end{pmatrix},\)

the eigenvalues are \(1/2\) and \(1\). Again \(uW^k=2^{-k}u\), so both temporal sums converge, but \(I-W\) is singular and the requested resolvent expression is undefined.

For comparison, if \(W\) is entrywise nonnegative, the supplied row normalization directly implies full stability:

\(\displaystyle \|A\|_\infty =|\rho|\max_z(1-w_z^s) \le1-\min_z w_z^s<1.\)

This argument fails for signed weights because row sums do not bound absolute row sums.
