The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given artists \(C\), a finite support of complete engagement types \(w^t \in \mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{|C|}\setminus\{0\}\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and \(k\) and \(\gamma>0\), decide whether there are \(U\subseteq C\), \(|U|\le k\), and masses \(0\le\nu_t\le\mu_t\) such that \(P_\mu(U,\nu)\ge\gamma\). Let \(q=\sum_t\nu_t\), \(\lambda=\mu-\nu\), \(a_t(U)=\sum_{j\in U}w^t_j\), and \(b_t=\sum_{j\in C}w^t_j\); then \(P_\mu(U,\nu)=\alpha\frac{\sum_t\mu_ta_t(U)}{\sum_t\mu_tb_t}-\alpha(1-q)\frac{\sum_t\lambda_ta_t(U)}{\sum_t\lambda_tb_t}-q\), with the second term set to zero when no mass remains.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) distinct complete engagement types \(w^t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^{m}\setminus\{0\}\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(C\) is the artist set, \(m=|C|\geq1\), and \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types; engagement vectors are not normalized.
The remaining inputs are an integer \(1\leq k\leq m\), a binary-rational revenue fraction \(0<\alpha\leq1\), and a binary-rational threshold \(\gamma>0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L,k\).
Subscription cost is one per unit mass. For a coalition \(U\subseteq C\), choose removed masses \(\nu_t\in\mathbb{R}\) with \(0\leq\nu_t\leq\mu_t\); the engagement vectors remain fixed. Put \(q=\sum_t\nu_t\), \(\lambda_t=\mu_t-\nu_t\), \(a_t(U)=\sum_{j\in U}w^t_j\), and \(b_t=\sum_{j\in C}w^t_j\).
Define the coalition's marginal revenue less subscription cost by \(P_\mu(U,\nu)=\alpha\frac{\sum_t\mu_ta_t(U)}{\sum_t\mu_tb_t}-\alpha(1-q)\frac{\sum_t\lambda_ta_t(U)}{\sum_t\lambda_tb_t}-q\), with the entire second term defined as zero when \(q=1\).

Is deciding whether there exist \(U\subseteq C\) with \(|U|\leq k\) and admissible \(\nu\) such that \(P_\mu(U,\nu)\geq\gamma\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it NP-hard?

For a finite list of users, where removal selects whole users and revenue and costs scale with user counts, this decision problem is NP-hard ([Ghosh et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04465v1)). No result settling exactly the divisible-mass problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The undefined revenue fraction alpha is a binary-rational input in (0,1], matching the discrete model's domain.
  • The cardinality parameter is an integer between 1 and m; the positive threshold is binary rational.
  • Removed masses may be arbitrary real numbers within their stated bounds, expressing divisible mass.
  • The input bit length L includes all engagement coordinates, masses, and numerical parameters.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The divisible-mass problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\). This holds even when \(\alpha=\tfrac12\), every engagement coordinate belongs to \(\{0,1\}\), and every type has total engagement either \(1\) or \(3\). Equal dyadic masses can also be imposed.

The proof below explicitly allows fractional removal throughout.

1. Reduction from Clique

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness of Clique. [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a simple graph, with \(n=|V|\), \(M=|E|\), and requested clique size \(h\). We may restrict to instances satisfying

\(3\leq h\leq n\), and \(M\geq \ell:=\binom h2\).

Indeed, excluded instances can be decided in polynomial time and replaced by a fixed instance satisfying these restrictions: a triangle for yes, or a four-cycle with requested size \(3\) for no.

Choose \(N\) to be the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\geq32M^2\). Thus \(32M^2\leq N<64M^2\).

Introduce two additional artists, \(z\) and \(d\), and set

\(C=V\cup\{z,d\}\), \(k=h+1\), \(\alpha=\tfrac12\), and \(\gamma=\dfrac{2\ell-1}{4N}\).

There are \(M+1\) supported types:

  • A background type with engagement vector \(e_d\) and mass \(1-M/N\).
  • For each edge \(\{u,v\}\in E\), a type with engagement vector \(e_u+e_v+e_z\) and mass \(1/N\).

Here \(e_j\) denotes the unit vector at artist \(j\).

These vectors are distinct and nonzero. All masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one; \(\gamma\) is also positive and dyadic. Background engagement totals \(1\), and every edge type totals \(3\). Moreover, \(k\leq |C|\).

We prove that this instance is yes exactly when \(G\) contains a clique of size \(h\).

2. A restoration lemma valid for fractional masses

Fix any coalition \(U\subseteq C\). Write \(a_t=a_t(U)\), and define

\(A_0=\sum_t\mu_ta_t\), \(B_0=\sum_t\mu_tb_t\).

For remaining masses \(\lambda_t\), put

\(s=\sum_t\lambda_t\), \(A=\sum_t\lambda_ta_t\), and \(B=\sum_t\lambda_tb_t\).

The remaining coalition revenue is \(R(\lambda)=\tfrac12 sA/B\) when \(s>0\), and \(R(0)=0\). Consequently,

\(P_\mu(U,\mu-\lambda)=\tfrac12 A_0/B_0-1+s-R(\lambda)\).

Since every \(b_t\) is either \(1\) or \(3\), whenever \(s>0\) we have

\(s\leq B\leq3s\), \(0\leq \rho:=A/B\leq1\), and \(\tfrac13\leq\eta:=s/B\leq1\).

Differentiating with respect to a remaining mass gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{\partial R}{\partial\lambda_t} =\frac12\left(\frac AB+\frac{s a_t}{B}-\frac{sA b_t}{B^2}\right) =\frac12\bigl(\eta a_t+(1-\eta b_t)\rho\bigr).\)

For the background type, \(b_t=1\) and \(a_t\in\{0,1\}\). Since \(1-\eta\geq0\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{\partial R}{\partial\lambda_t} \leq\frac12\bigl(\eta+(1-\eta)\bigr)=\frac12.\)

For an edge type whose entire support is not contained in \(U\), we have \(b_t=3\) and \(a_t\leq2\). Since \(1-3\eta\leq0\) and \(\rho\geq0\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{\partial R}{\partial\lambda_t} \leq\frac12\eta a_t\leq1.\)

Restoring mass \(\delta\) increases the term \(s\) by \(\delta\). The preceding derivative bounds therefore show that restoring background mass or uncovered edge mass cannot decrease profit.

This conclusion also holds when the starting remaining mass is zero: \(0\leq R(\lambda)\leq s/2\), so \(R\) is continuous there, and the derivative inequalities extend to the endpoint by the mean value theorem and continuity.

Thus, every admissible removal can be replaced, without decreasing profit, by one that retains all background mass and removes mass only from edge types whose entire support lies in \(U\).

Call such edge types covered. An edge \(\{u,v\}\) is covered precisely when \(u,v,z\in U\).

3. Bounding profit by the number of covered edges

Let \(c\) be the number of covered edge types. After applying the restoration lemma, write the total removed mass as

\(q=x/N\), where \(x\) is real and \(0\leq x\leq c\leq M\).

In particular, no integrality of removal is being assumed.

The initial total engagement is

\(B_0=1+2M/N\).

Since every removed type has total engagement \(3\), the remaining total engagement is \(B_0-3q\). Define the initial and remaining revenue coefficients

\(\displaystyle p_0=\frac{1}{2B_0}=\frac{N}{2(N+2M)}\),

\(\displaystyle p(q)=\frac{1-q}{2(B_0-3q)} =\frac{N-x}{2(N+2M-3x)}.\)

Their denominators are positive because \(x\leq M<N\). Direct subtraction gives

\(\displaystyle p(q)-p_0 =\frac{x(N-M)}{(N+2M)(N+2M-3x)}\geq0.\)

Also, \(x\leq M\) implies \(p(q)\leq\tfrac12\).

Let \(D\) be the remaining coalition engagement. Every removed type assigns all its engagement to \(U\), so the initial coalition engagement is \(D+3q\). Hence

\(\displaystyle P_\mu(U,\nu) =p_0(D+3q)-p(q)D-q =(3p_0-1)q-(p(q)-p_0)D.\)

Using \(D\geq0\), \(p(q)\geq p_0\), and \(p_0\leq\tfrac12\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle P_\mu(U,\nu)\leq\frac q2\leq\frac{c}{2N}.\)

The same bound holds for the original removal, since restoration only increased its profit.

4. No clique implies no profitable witness

Suppose \(G\) contains no clique of size \(h\), and let \(|U|\leq h+1\).

If \(z\notin U\), no edge is covered, so profit is at most zero.

If \(z\in U\), let \(S=U\cap V\). Then \(|S|\leq h\), and the covered edges are exactly those in the induced graph \(G[S]\).

If \(|S|<h\), then \(G[S]\) has fewer than \(\binom h2=\ell\) edges. If \(|S|=h\), the absence of an \(h\)-clique again implies that it has at most \(\ell-1\) edges. Thus \(c\leq\ell-1\).

The bound from Section 3 yields

\(\displaystyle P_\mu(U,\nu)\leq\frac{\ell-1}{2N} =\gamma-\frac{1}{4N}<\gamma.\)

This excludes every coalition and every fractional removal in a no-instance.

5. A clique supplies a witness above the threshold

Suppose \(S\subseteq V\) is a clique of size \(h\). Choose \(U=S\cup\{z\}\), and remove the full mass of its \(\ell\) internal edge types. Then \(|U|=k\) and \(q=\ell/N\).

Set

\(\displaystyle \delta=\frac12-p_0=\frac{M}{N+2M}\leq\frac MN.\)

The background artist \(d\) is outside \(U\), so the remaining coalition engagement satisfies \(0\leq D\leq3M/N\). Moreover,

\(0\leq p(q)-p_0\leq\tfrac12-p_0=\delta\).

Therefore,

\(\displaystyle P_\mu(U,\nu) \geq\left(\frac12-3\delta\right)\frac{\ell}{N}-\delta D =\frac{\ell}{2N}-\delta\left(\frac{3\ell}{N}+D\right).\)

Using \(\ell\leq M\), \(D\leq3M/N\), and \(\delta\leq M/N\),

\(\displaystyle P_\mu(U,\nu)\geq\frac{\ell}{2N}-\frac{6M^2}{N^2}.\)

Finally, \(N\geq32M^2\) implies

\(\displaystyle P_\mu(U,\nu) \geq\frac{\ell}{2N}-\frac{3}{16N} =\gamma+\frac{1}{16N}>\gamma.\)

Thus every yes-instance of Clique produces an admissible witness.

6. Input size, running time, and equal masses

For the construction above,

\(m=n+2\), \(\tau=M+1\), and \(k=h+1\).

Although \(N\) is of order \(M^2\), it appears only in binary rational numbers, whose encoding lengths are of order \(\log M\). Under the explicit vector-list encoding,

\(L=O(m\tau+\tau\log(\tau+1))\).

With a dense graph input and straightforward binary counters, the reduction can be implemented in

\(O(m^2+m\tau\log(m+\tau)+L)\)

bit operations. This is polynomial in the source input size and in \(m,\tau,L,k\).

To impose equal masses, replace the background type by \(N-M\) types, each of mass \(1/N\), each engaging with its own distinct auxiliary artist. Keep every edge type unchanged. All supported vectors remain distinct, and now every type has mass \(1/N\).

The restoration argument applies separately to each background type. The total engagement calculations and both directions of the reduction remain unchanged; the yes-witness includes none of the auxiliary artists. The resulting parameters are

\(\tau'=N\), \(m'=n+1+N-M\), and \(k'=h+1\).

Since \(N<64M^2\), this expanded construction also has polynomial size and running time.

The reduction is therefore complete. Divisibility of the masses does not remove the hardness: a decision algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
