The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support society of campaign-instance types with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), token distributions \(p_\theta\in\Delta(\mathcal{V})\), and fixed bids \(b_\theta\) for all but a focal type \(\theta^\star\) of positive mass \(\alpha\), let the focal cohort choose a common bid \(x\), with \(B_- = \sum_{\theta\ne\theta^\star}\mu_\theta b_\theta>0\) and \(q_x(t)=\frac{\alpha x p_{\theta^\star}(t)+\sum_{\theta\ne\theta^\star}\mu_\theta b_\theta p_\theta(t)}{\alpha x+B_-}\). Construct a sampler that outputs distribution \(q_x\) for every \(x\), and for each fixed random seed changes at most once as \(x\) rises, from a token with \(q_0(t)>p_{\theta^\star}(t)\) to one with \(q_0(u)<p_{\theta^\star}(u)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(\mathcal V\) be a finite token set with \(m=|\mathcal V|\geq1\). Each type has an explicitly listed rational distribution \(p_\theta\in\Delta(\mathcal V)\). All nonfocal types have fixed bids \(b_\theta\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\).
Fix a focal type \(\theta^\star\) of mass \(\alpha>0\), whose entire cohort chooses a common bid \(x\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\). Assume \(B_-:=\sum_{\theta\ne\theta^\star}\mu_\theta b_\theta>0\), and define \(q_x(t):=\frac{\alpha x p_{\theta^\star}(t)+\sum_{\theta\ne\theta^\star}\mu_\theta b_\theta p_\theta(t)}{\alpha x+B_-}\).
An exact stable sampler is a map \(\sigma(x,r)\in\mathcal V\), using an input-independent stream \(r\) of fair random bits, such that \(\Pr_r[\sigma(x,r)=t]=q_x(t)\) for every \(x,t\). For every fixed seed, its output is either constant or changes exactly once as \(x\) increases, from \(o\) with \(q_0(o)>p_{\theta^\star}(o)\) to \(u\) with \(q_0(u)<p_{\theta^\star}(u)\). At a change occurring at a rational bid, use the post-change token. Constant paths may output any token, including one whose probability never changes.
Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the society, distributions, and fixed bids, and let \(\ell\) be the bit length of a queried bid. Evaluation must terminate almost surely; exceptional seeds receive bid-independent outputs so that the map is defined and stable for every seed.

Can an exact stable sampler be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with expected evaluation time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\ell\), or is this construction problem NP-hard?

For finitely many agents, every monotone aggregation rule under robust preferences admits a stable implementation; linear bid-weighted aggregation is such a rule. This is an existence result without a stated bit-complexity bound. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10826v3)
No verified result located establishes the computational complexity of the support-encoded mass problem stated here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to sum to one, interpreting the society as a distribution over types.
  • Required explicitly listed rational token probabilities and nonnegative rational fixed and focal bids, matching the discrete bid domain and providing finite computational inputs.
  • Allowed constant sample paths, including tokens whose probabilities are unchanged, following the source's intended construction rather than its literal requirement that every path name both an oversampled and an undersampled token.
  • Retained strict undersampling for actual transition destinations; tokens with equality cannot gain probability under the specified aggregation.
  • Used the post-change token at a rational transition bid, without requiring the transition location itself to be rational.
  • Specified exact sampling with fair random bits, polynomial construction time, and expected polynomial evaluation time; assigned bid-independent outputs to exceptional seeds.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

An exact stable sampler admits the requested polynomial-time construction and expected polynomial-time evaluation. The proof uses an explicit coupling of the initial distribution and the focal distribution.

The exceptional-seed convention is used as stated: the evaluator realizes the total sampler almost surely, while a single bid-independent null set receives constant outputs.

1. The distributions follow a line segment

Write \(p=p_{\theta^\star}\) and define
\[ a(t)=q_0(t)=\frac{\sum_{\theta\ne\theta^\star}\mu_\theta b_\theta p_\theta(t)}{B_-}, \qquad \lambda(x)=\frac{\alpha x}{\alpha x+B_-}. \]
Then
\[ q_x(t)=(1-\lambda(x))a(t)+\lambda(x)p(t). \]

The assumptions \(\alpha>0\) and \(B_->0\) imply that \(\lambda(0)=0\), that \(\lambda(x)<1\) for every finite bid, and that \(\lambda\) increases strictly towards \(1\).

Partition the tokens into
\[ O=\{t:a(t)>p(t)\}, \qquad U=\{t:a(t)<p(t)\}, \qquad Z=\{t:a(t)=p(t)\}. \]
For every token set \(c_t=\min\{a(t),p(t)\}\). For \(o\in O\) and \(u\in U\), set
\[ d_o=a(o)-p(o)>0, \qquad e_u=p(u)-a(u)>0. \]
Because both distributions sum to \(1\),
\[ D:=\sum_{o\in O}d_o=\sum_{u\in U}e_u, \qquad \sum_t c_t=1-D. \]

If \(D=0\), then \(a=p\), and all bid distributions coincide. Sampling once from \(a\), independently of the bid, solves this case. Henceforth suppose \(D>0\).

2. An explicit distribution over stable paths

Introduce the following finite collection of path labels:

  • A constant label \(C_t\), with weight \(c_t\), for each token whose weight is positive.
  • A switching label \(S_{o,u}\), with weight

\[ w_{o,u}=\frac{d_o e_u}{D}, \]
for every \(o\in O\) and \(u\in U\).

These weights form a probability distribution. Indeed,
\[ \sum_{o,u}w_{o,u} =\frac{\left(\sum_o d_o\right)\left(\sum_u e_u\right)}{D} =D. \]
Their switching marginals are
\[ \sum_u w_{o,u}=d_o, \qquad \sum_o w_{o,u}=e_u. \]

Choose a label \(J\) according to these weights. Independently choose a uniform random variable \(V\) on \([0,1]\). Before specifying exceptional seeds, define the output by
\[ \widetilde\sigma(x)= \begin{cases} t,&J=C_t,\\ o,&J=S_{o,u}\text{ and }V>\lambda(x),\\ u,&J=S_{o,u}\text{ and }V\leq\lambda(x). \end{cases} \]

For a switching label and \(0<V<1\), the unique change threshold is
\[ \xi=\frac{B_-V}{\alpha(1-V)}. \]
Indeed, \(V\leq\lambda(x)\) is equivalent to \(x\geq\xi\). Thus the path changes exactly once, from an excess token \(o\in O\) to a deficit token \(u\in U\), using the post-change token at equality.

The marginals are exact. For \(o\in O\),
\[ \Pr[\widetilde\sigma(x)=o] =c_o+(1-\lambda(x))\sum_u w_{o,u} =p(o)+(1-\lambda(x))(a(o)-p(o)) =q_x(o). \]
For \(u\in U\),
\[ \Pr[\widetilde\sigma(x)=u] =c_u+\lambda(x)\sum_o w_{o,u} =a(u)+\lambda(x)(p(u)-a(u)) =q_x(u). \]
For \(t\in Z\), only its constant label outputs \(t\), with probability \(c_t=a(t)=q_x(t)\).

3. Fair bits and a total, stable map

Split the given fair-bit stream into its odd-position and even-position subsequences. These are independent fair-bit streams.

Use the odd-position stream to sample \(J\). All label weights are rational. Let \(Q\) be the product of their positive denominators, and write each weight as \(n_j/Q\). Then
\[ n_j\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad \sum_j n_j=Q. \]

If \(Q=1\), choose the sole label directly. Otherwise set \(K=\lceil\log_2 Q\rceil\). Repeatedly read \(K\) odd-position bits to obtain an integer uniform on
\[ \{0,\ldots,2^K-1\}. \]
Reject integers at least \(Q\). The first accepted integer is uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}\); use consecutive integer intervals of lengths \(n_j\) to select the label.

Acceptance probability is \(Q/2^K>1/2\). Consequently, sampling terminates almost surely and takes fewer than two trials in expectation.

From the even-position bits \(r^{(2)}_k\), define
\[ V(r)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r^{(2)}_k2^{-k}. \]
This is uniform on \([0,1]\): every dyadic interval of length \(2^{-n}\) has probability \(2^{-n}\), apart from endpoints of probability zero, and these intervals determine the uniform distribution.

Let \(E\) consist of the seeds for which either the label rejection procedure never accepts or \(V(r)\) is rational. The first event has probability zero by the acceptance bound. The second has probability zero because the rationals are countable and a uniform random variable has no atoms. Thus
\[ \Pr[E]=0. \]
Crucially, \(E\) is independent of the queried bid.

Fix any token \(t_0\). Define the total sampler by
\[ \sigma(x,r)= \begin{cases} t_0,&r\in E,\\ \widetilde\sigma(x,r),&r\notin E. \end{cases} \]

This establishes stability for every seed:

  • Exceptional seeds have constant output \(t_0\).
  • Constant labels give constant paths.
  • Every remaining switching seed has \(0<V<1\), so its threshold \(\xi\) is positive and finite, with output \(o\) below \(\xi\) and \(u\) at and above \(\xi\).

In fact, every remaining switching threshold is irrational: if \(\xi\) were rational, then
\[ V=\frac{\alpha\xi}{B_-+\alpha\xi} \]
would be rational. Hence no nonexceptional path changes at a rational bid, and the required rational-bid tie convention is satisfied.

Modification on \(E\) does not change any marginal probability. Therefore \(\Pr_r[\sigma(x,r)=t]=q_x(t)\) exactly.

4. Exact evaluation and its expected cost

For a queried rational bid \(x\), first reconstruct \(J\) from the odd-position stream. A constant label immediately determines the output.

For a switching label, compute the rational number \(\lambda=\lambda(x)\). After reading the first \(n\) even-position bits, let
\[ k_n=\sum_{i=1}^n r^{(2)}_i2^{n-i}, \qquad L_n=\frac{k_n}{2^n}, \qquad H_n=\frac{k_n+1}{2^n}. \]
The represented value satisfies \(L_n\leq V\leq H_n\). Apply these stopping rules:

  • If \(H_n\leq\lambda\), output \(u\).
  • If \(L_n>\lambda\), output \(o\).
  • Otherwise read another bit.

For every seed outside \(E\), \(V\ne\lambda\), since \(\lambda\) is rational. The shrinking intervals therefore eventually separate \(V\) from \(\lambda\), and the routine returns the specified output.

After \(n\) bits, failure to stop requires
\[ L_n\leq\lambda<H_n. \]
At most one of the \(2^n\) equally likely prefixes satisfies this condition. Thus, if \(N\) denotes the number of comparison bits,
\[ \Pr[N>n]\leq 2^{-n}, \qquad \mathbb E[N]\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-n}=2. \]

All comparisons use exact integer arithmetic. If \(\lambda=h/v\), with \(v>0\), they become
\[ (k_n+1)v\leq h2^n \quad\text{or}\quad k_nv>h2^n. \]
At step \(n\), the integers have bit length bounded by \(n\) plus the bit length of \(\lambda\). Standard integer arithmetic therefore gives a polynomial cost per step. The exponentially decreasing tail of \(N\) implies polynomial expected total comparison time.

The routine agrees with the total map on every seed outside the same bid-independent null set \(E\). On \(E\), the map has the constant values stipulated above. As permitted by the almost-sure evaluation requirement, the routine does not need to decide membership in \(E\).

5. Construction complexity

All preprocessing is deterministic rational arithmetic:

  • Compute \(B_-\) and \(a(t)\) from the explicitly listed support.
  • Compute \(O,U,Z\), the constant weights, and the excess and deficit masses.
  • Compute at most \(m^2\) switching weights.
  • Form \(Q\), the integer weights, and their cumulative sums.

There are polynomially many arithmetic operations. Their operand lengths remain polynomially bounded: products add operand bit lengths, and a sum of rational numbers can use the product of their denominators as a common denominator. Every sum here contains polynomially many terms. The product defining \(Q\) likewise contains at most \(m+m^2\) denominators of polynomial bit length.

Consequently, preprocessing time and the resulting sampler description have size polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). In particular, the construction never expands a cohort into individual agents.

For a queried bid of bit length \(\ell\), the bit length of
\[ \lambda(x)=\frac{\alpha x}{\alpha x+B_-} \]
is polynomial in \(L,\ell\). Label selection has polynomial cost per trial and fewer than two trials in expectation. The threshold comparison has polynomial expected cost as proved above. Splitting one sequential stream by parity can be implemented by buffering the interleaved bits, with linear overhead.

Thus evaluation has expected time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\ell\), and the requested construction is polynomial-time solvable.
