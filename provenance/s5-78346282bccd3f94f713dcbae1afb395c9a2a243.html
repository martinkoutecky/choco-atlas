The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) divisible bads, a finite type set \(\Theta\) with rational mass vector \(\mu\), common per-capita entitlement \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^m\), and complete SPLC disutilities \(d_\theta\), compute positive prices \(\pi\) up to scaling and bundles \(x_\theta\) such that \(x_\theta\in\arg\min_{x\ge0}\{d_\theta(x):\pi\cdot x\ge\pi\cdot q\}\) for every \(\theta\), while \(\left|\sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_{\theta j}-q_j\right|\le\varepsilon q_j\) for every bad \(j\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; assume \(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta=1\).
There are \(m\ge1\) divisible bads and a common per-capita endowment \(q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^m\), so aggregate supply is \(q\).
Each type has \(d_\theta(x)=\sum_{j=1}^m d_{\theta j}(x_j)\), with \(d_{\theta j}(0)=0\). Each coordinate function is continuous piecewise linear with finite nonnegative, nondecreasing rational slopes, positive rational lengths for its finite segments, and an unbounded final segment with positive slope; thus \(-d_\theta\) is SPLC. All type–bad pairs are specified, with no infinite disutilities.
Let \(\ell_{\theta j}\) be the length of the initial zero-disutility interval, possibly zero. Assume \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\ell_{\theta j}<q_j\) for every bad \(j\).
For rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), find rational \(\pi\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^m\), normalized by \(\sum_j\pi_j=1\), and per-capita bundles \(x_\theta\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m\) satisfying \(x_\theta\in\arg\min\{d_\theta(x):x\ge0,\ \pi\cdot x\ge\pi\cdot q\}\) and \(\left|\sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_{\theta j}-q_j\right|\le\varepsilon q_j\) for every \(j\). Any minimizing bundle is allowed, including ties.
Let \(S\) be the total number of segments and \(L\) the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,S,L\) and the accuracy parameter.

For every fixed \(c>0\), is this search problem at accuracy \(\varepsilon=(m+\tau)^{-c}\) solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,S,L\), or is it \(\mathsf{PPAD}\)-hard for some fixed \(c>0\)?

For finite lists of agents, equal-income competitive allocation of bads is \(\mathsf{PPAD}\)-hard at inverse-polynomial market-clearing error with exact individual optimality, even with at most three segments per agent–bad pair and constant slopes; exact equilibria admit a complementary pivot algorithm and belong to \(\mathsf{PPAD}\). Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized masses to sum to one, making the common per-capita endowment also the aggregate supply.
  • Interpreted SPLC disutilities as negatives of SPLC utilities: separable piecewise-linear convex costs with nonnegative, nondecreasing slopes. Retained zero initial slopes because the hardness construction uses them.
  • Specified rational segment data, finite disutilities for every type–bad pair, and an unbounded final segment rather than imposing consumption caps.
  • Carried over the exclusion of zero-price bads as a strict mass-weighted bound on initial zero-disutility capacity; final slopes are positive.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The search problem is \(\mathsf{PPAD}\)-hard for the fixed accuracy exponent \(c=10\). This holds even with dyadic masses, at most three segments per type–bad pair, and slopes from a fixed finite set.

The proof transfers the finite-agent hardness construction to the support representation. Identical agents are grouped into one type; no expansion of rational masses is needed.

1. The finite-agent hardness result used

Write \(n\) for the number of strategies in the source two-player game. The construction underlying Theorems 29–30 of [*Competitive Allocation of a Mixed Manna*, Section 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.02753#page=33) has:

  • \(m=2n+2\) bads and \(N=6n^2+6n+2\) agents.
  • Common endowment \(a\mathbf 1\), where \(a=1/n\).
  • Exact individual optimality and relative coordinatewise clearing tolerance \(\delta_n=1/(6nm^8)\).
  • At most three segments per agent–bad pair, with finite, nonnegative, nondecreasing constant slopes and positive final slopes.

Producing such an approximate equilibrium is \(\mathsf{PPAD}\)-hard. The common-endowment construction prepends a zero-disutility interval of length \(a-W_{ij}\) to agent \(i\)'s original strictly positive-slope disutility for bad \(j\). Here \(0\le W_{ij}\le a\), and \(E_j:=\sum_{i=1}^N W_{ij}>0\). Zero-length segments are omitted. These parameters and the accuracy conversion appear in the [proof of Theorem 29](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.02753#page=40).

We use this established finite-agent hardness result; the remaining argument is the representation reduction.

2. Padding with a type having a unique optimal bundle

To ensure dyadic masses, let

\(K=2^{\lceil\log_2 N\rceil}\).

Thus \(N\le K<2N\). Add \(K-N\) identical dummy agents, each with the same endowment \(a\mathbf 1\) and disutility

\(g(x)=\sum_{j=1}^m\max\{0,x_j-a\}\).

Each coordinate has two segments: slope zero over length \(a\), followed by an unbounded segment of slope one.

Lemma. At every strictly positive price vector \(\pi\), the dummy agent’s unique minimizing bundle under the constraint \(\pi\cdot x\ge\pi\cdot(a\mathbf 1)\) is \(a\mathbf 1\).

Proof. The bundle \(a\mathbf 1\) is feasible and has disutility zero. Consequently, every minimizer has disutility zero and satisfies \(x_j\le a\) for every \(j\). Therefore

\(\pi\cdot x\le\pi\cdot(a\mathbf 1)\).

Feasibility forces equality. Since every \(\pi_j>0\) and every \(a-x_j\ge0\), the equality \(\sum_j\pi_j(a-x_j)=0\) forces \(x_j=a\) for every \(j\). This proves uniqueness.

3. Constructing the support instance and checking its promises

Partition the padded population into classes of identical disutility functions. Let \(\Theta\) be these classes, and let \(k_\theta\) be the number of agents in class \(\theta\). Set

\(\mu_\theta=k_\theta/K,\qquad q=a\mathbf 1,\qquad d_\theta=d_i\text{ for any }i\in\theta.\)

Then every mass is positive and dyadic, and

\(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta=1,\qquad \tau=|\Theta|\le N+1.\)

All functions satisfy the required segment conditions. Grouping does not add segments, and the dummy functions have only two segments. Hence

\(S\le3m\tau.\)

It remains to verify the strict zero-disutility shortage. For an original agent, the initial zero interval has length \(\ell_{ij}=a-W_{ij}\); for a dummy agent, its length is \(a\). Therefore, for every bad \(j\),

\(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta\ell_{\theta j} =\frac1K\left(\sum_{i=1}^N(a-W_{ij})+(K-N)a\right) =a-\frac{E_j}{K}<a=q_j.\)

The strict inequality follows from \(E_j>0\). Thus the reduction produces a valid instance of the question.

Grouping identical agents is also compatible with ties. Their feasible sets coincide, and their disutilities are convex. Consequently, their minimizing bundles form a convex set: the average of minimizing bundles is again minimizing. Grouping therefore preserves the possibility of an equilibrium allocation.

4. Decoding any returned solution

Suppose the support solver returns normalized, strictly positive rational prices \(\pi\) and bundles \(x_\theta\).

Assign \(x_\theta\) to every padded agent belonging to class \(\theta\). Every assigned bundle is individually optimal because the agent has exactly the type’s disutility and common endowment \(q\).

The support clearing guarantee gives, for every \(j\),

\(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{K}x_{ij}-Ka\right| =K\left|\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta x_{\theta j}-a\right| \le K\varepsilon a.\)

By the lemma, every dummy consumes exactly \(a\mathbf 1\). Removing the dummies consequently leaves

\(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{ij}-Na\right|\le K\varepsilon a.\)

The original finite-agent market’s relative clearing error is therefore at most

\(\frac{K\varepsilon a}{Na}=\frac KN\varepsilon<2\varepsilon.\)

Individual optimality remains exact. Price normalization causes no difficulty: multiplying all prices by a common positive factor leaves every budget constraint unchanged.

This decoding works for every valid returned solution, including arbitrary choices among tied minimizing bundles.

5. Choosing the fixed exponent

Set the requested accuracy to

\(\varepsilon=(m+\tau)^{-10}.\)

Because \(m+\tau\ge m\), the decoded finite-agent clearing error is at most \(2m^{-10}\). Moreover, using \(m=2n+2\),

\(m^2-12n=4(n^2-n+1)>0.\)

It follows that

\(2\varepsilon\le2m^{-10}\le\frac1{6nm^8}=\delta_n.\)

Thus any valid answer to our support instance yields an answer at the accuracy required by the finite-agent hardness reduction. Composing the two reductions proves \(\mathsf{PPAD}\)-hardness for \(c=10\).

The same argument establishes hardness for every fixed integer \(c\ge10\).

6. Encoding and running times

Let \(S_0,L_0\) denote the segment count and binary input length of the finite-agent instance. Padding, grouping identical functions, and forming the masses take

\(\operatorname{poly}(m,N,S_0,L_0)\)

bit operations. The reduction produces parameters satisfying

\(\tau\le N+1,\qquad S\le3m\tau,\qquad L=\operatorname{poly}(m,N,S_0,L_0).\)

Each mass needs \(O(\log N)\) bits beyond its numerator, and the accuracy has encoding length

\(O(\log(1/\varepsilon))=O(\log(m+\tau))\)

for the fixed exponent \(10\). Decoding takes polynomial time in the source input length and the returned certificate length.

Consequently, an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,S,L)\) at this fixed accuracy would solve the source \(\mathsf{PPAD}\)-hard problem in polynomial time. Even a bound polynomial in \(m,\tau,S,L,1/\varepsilon\) would have that consequence, since here \(1/\varepsilon=(m+\tau)^{10}\).

The requested hardness alternative is therefore established; no polynomial-time algorithm for all fixed accuracy exponents follows from the finite-support representation.
