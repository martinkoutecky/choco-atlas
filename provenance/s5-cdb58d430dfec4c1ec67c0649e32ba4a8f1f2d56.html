The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), a team-size bound \(B\), and negative-valued embedded rules whose conditions test type counts and absences, compute the maximum normalized social welfare and an optimal feasible pair \((x,G)\). Here \(x_q\ge 0\) is the per-capita number of teams of composition \(q\), with \(\sum_q q_t x_q=\mu_t\); \(G\) is a finite set of zero-per-capita witness compositions drawn from the supported types. A rule applies to a focal team when its focal condition holds and each externality condition is witnessed either by positive mass of teams in \(x\) or by a composition in \(G\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also contains a binary-encoded integer \(B\geq1\) and \(m\) embedded rules. Allowed team compositions are \(Q=\{q\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{T}:1\leq\sum_t q_t\leq B\}\); compositions are not explicitly enumerated in the input.
A condition \(I=(p,N)\) has a nonzero integer requirement vector \(p\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{T}\) and an absence set \(N\subseteq T\), with \(p_t=0\) for \(t\in N\). It holds on \(q\) exactly when \(q_t\geq p_t\) for every \(t\) and \(q_t=0\) for every \(t\in N\).
Each rule \(r=(I_{r0}\mid I_{r1},\ldots,I_{rk_r}\to w_r)\) has binary-rational weight \(w_r<0\), with \(k_r\geq0\). Require \(\sum_{j=0}^{k_r}(\sum_t p_{rj,t}+|N_{rj}|)\geq2\), and require that no allowed composition satisfies both \(I_{r0}\) and \(I_{rj}\) for any \(j\geq1\).
A feasible pair consists of real team intensities \(x_q\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{q\in Q}q_t x_q=\mu_t\) for every \(t\), and a finite set \(G\subseteq Q\) of additional witness compositions; witnesses consume no mass and contribute no welfare.
Rule \(r\) applies to composition \(q\) when \(I_{r0}(q)\) holds and, for each \(j\geq1\), some \(h\in Q\) with \(x_h>0\) or \(h\in G\) satisfies \(I_{rj}\). Different externality conditions may share a witness.
Normalized social welfare is \(W(x,G)=\sum_{q\in Q}x_q\sum_{r\text{ applies to }q}w_r\). An optimal pair is represented by listing its positive intensities and its witnesses.
Is computing the maximum normalized social welfare and an optimal feasible pair polynomial-time solvable, or is this problem NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), the total input bit length \(L\), \(B\), and \(K=\sum_r k_r\).

Result

The problem is NP-hard, already when \(K=0\), every weight is \(-1\), every requirement entry belongs to \(\{0,1\}\), and every absence set has size at most three. More precisely, deciding whether the maximum welfare equals zero is NP-complete.

An optimal pair always exists with \(G=\varnothing\), at most \(\tau\) positive intensities, and rational intensities of polynomial bit length.

There are exact algorithms with running times

\[ \]
\(2^K\operatorname{poly}\!\left(m,K,\tau,L,\binom{B+\tau}{\tau}\right)\)

and

\[ \]
\((m+K+2)^{O(\tau^2)}\operatorname{poly}(m,K,\tau,L).\)

The second bound depends on \(B\) only through its binary encoding in \(L\). Thus the problem is polynomial-time solvable for each fixed support size \(\tau\), even with unrestricted \(K\) and binary-encoded \(B\).

The proof below concerns the given mass-and-witness model directly; it does not require transferring the cited [MaxSAT formulation](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/transinf/E97.D/7/E97.D_1812/_pdf) or [MIP formulation](https://d-nb.info/1165370204/34).

1. Witnesses can be discarded

For any feasible pair \((x,G)\), removing \(G\) can only deactivate rules. Since every rule weight is negative,

\(W(x,\varnothing)\geq W(x,G).\)

The mass constraints are unaffected. Consequently, it suffices to optimize over pairs with \(G=\varnothing\).

This does not make the problem trivial: a condition with a singleton requirement and a nonempty absence set can penalize singleton teams while satisfying the stipulated minimum condition size.

2. Attainment and a small optimal representation

Let

\(\mathcal J=\{(r,j):1\leq r\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq k_r\}\),

so \(|\mathcal J|=K\). For an intensity vector \(x\), define its external-condition activation set by

\(E(x)=\{(r,j)\in\mathcal J:\text{some }q\text{ with }x_q>0\text{ satisfies }I_{rj}\}.\)

For an arbitrary subset \(E\subseteq\mathcal J\), define

\(S_E=\{q\in Q:I_{rj}(q)\text{ is false for every }(r,j)\notin E\}\)

and

\(c_E(q)=\sum_{\substack{1\leq r\leq m\\(r,j)\in E\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq k_r}}w_r\,\mathbf 1_{\{I_{r0}(q)\}}.\)

For rules with \(k_r=0\), the condition on \(E\) is vacuous.

Consider the rational linear program

\(V_E=\max\{\sum_{q\in S_E}c_E(q)x_q:\sum_{q\in S_E}q_tx_q=\mu_t\text{ for every }t,\ x_q\geq0\}.\)

Set \(V_E=-\infty\) when this program is infeasible.

Every feasible intensity vector satisfies

\(\sum_{q\in Q}|q|x_q=\sum_t\mu_t=1,\qquad |q|=\sum_tq_t\geq1.\)

Hence \(\sum_qx_q\leq1\). Each feasible regime linear program therefore has a compact feasible set and attains its optimum.

Exactness of the regime programs

For any feasible \(x\), its support lies in \(S_{E(x)}\), and

\(W(x,\varnothing)=\sum_qc_{E(x)}(q)x_q\leq V_{E(x)}.\)

Thus every feasible welfare is at most \(M=\max_{E\subseteq\mathcal J}V_E\).

Conversely, choose an optimal solution \(y\) to a regime attaining \(M\). Because its support lies in \(S_E\), its actual activation set satisfies \(E(y)\subseteq E\). Removing assumed activations can only remove negative penalties, so

\(W(y,\varnothing)\geq\sum_qc_E(q)y_q=V_E=M.\)

The preceding upper bound applies to \(y\) as well. Therefore \(W(y,\varnothing)=M\). This proves both attainment and the identity

\(V^*=\max_{E\subseteq\mathcal J}V_E,\)

where \(V^*\) denotes the maximum actual welfare.

Sparsity and bit length

An optimal basic feasible solution of a regime program has at most \(\tau\) positive variables: its positive composition columns must be linearly independent in a matrix with \(\tau\) rows.

These columns have integer entries between zero and \(B\). If there are \(s\leq\tau\) positive columns, choose a nonsingular \(s\)-row minor. Cramer’s rule expresses the intensities using that integer minor and the corresponding masses.

A common denominator for the masses has bit length at most \(O(L)\). The determinant of the minor has absolute value at most \(s!B^s\). The same determinant expansion bounds the numerators after clearing the mass denominators. Consequently, each intensity has numerator and denominator bit length

\(O\!\left(L+\tau\log(B+1)+\tau\log(\tau+1)\right).\)

Thus an optimal pair has a polynomial-size rational representation with at most \(\tau\) positive intensities and no witnesses.

3. A direct reduction from satisfiability

We reduce from the standard NP-complete problem of satisfiability of a Boolean formula in conjunctive normal form with nonempty clauses containing at most three literals.

Let the formula have variables \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\), where \(n\geq1\), and clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_s\).

Types, capacity, and masses

Introduce one designated type \(a\) and two types \(t_i^0,t_i^1\) for each variable. Therefore,

\(T=\{a\}\cup\{t_i^0,t_i^1:1\leq i\leq n\},\qquad \tau=2n+1.\)

Set \(B=n+1\).

Let \(D\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(D\geq2n+1\). Assign mass \(1/D\) to every type except \(t_1^0\), and set

\(\mu_{t_1^0}=1-\frac{2n}{D}.\)

These masses sum to one. Every mass is positive and at least \(1/D\), and \(\mu_a=1/D\). They are dyadic rationals with \(O(\log n)\) bits each.

Write \(e_t\) for the unit requirement vector of type \(t\).

Rules

Every rule has weight \(-1\) and no external conditions.

For each variable \(v_i\), introduce two rules:

  • Missing value: requirement \(e_a\), absence set \(\{t_i^0,t_i^1\}\).
  • Both values: requirement \(e_{t_i^0}+e_{t_i^1}\), empty absence set.

For each clause \(C_\ell\), let \(A_\ell\) contain \(t_i^1\) for every positive literal \(v_i\) in the clause, and \(t_i^0\) for every negative literal \(\neg v_i\). Introduce the rule with requirement \(e_a\) and absence set \(A_\ell\).

There are \(m=2n+s\) rules and \(K=0\).

Every rule is valid:

  • its requirement is nonzero;
  • requirement coordinates vanish on its absence set;
  • its requirement-plus-absence size is at least two;
  • the local/external incompatibility requirement is vacuous because there are no external conditions.

All requirement entries are zero or one, and all absence sets have size at most three. The construction has polynomial input length.

Satisfiable formulas give zero welfare

Suppose \(\sigma\in\{0,1\}^n\) satisfies the formula. Use the composition

\(q^\sigma=e_a+\sum_{i=1}^ne_{t_i^{\sigma_i}},\)

which has size \(n+1=B\), with intensity \(1/D\).

This composition triggers no rule:

  • it contains a value for every variable;
  • it never contains both values of a variable;
  • for each clause, it contains a type corresponding to a true literal, so that clause’s absence condition fails.

Assign every remaining literal-type mass to its singleton composition. These residual masses are nonnegative because every literal-type mass is at least \(1/D\).

Literal-type singleton teams also trigger no rule: they contain no \(a\), and cannot contain both values of a variable. The resulting feasible pair has welfare zero.

All weights are negative, so every feasible welfare is at most zero. Hence

\(\text{formula satisfiable}\ \Longrightarrow\ V^*=0.\)

Unsatisfiable formulas give strictly negative welfare

Consider any composition \(q\) containing \(a\).

If no rule holds on \(q\), then the missing-value and both-values rules imply that, for every variable, exactly one of \(t_i^0,t_i^1\) is present. This defines an assignment.

Furthermore, each clause rule must fail. Since \(a\) is present, failure means that some type in \(A_\ell\) is present. The corresponding literal is true under the assignment. Therefore every clause is satisfied.

Consequently, if the formula is unsatisfiable, every composition containing \(a\) triggers at least one rule and has welfare coefficient at most \(-1\). All other compositions have coefficients at most zero.

For any feasible \(x\),

\(W(x,\varnothing)\leq-\sum_{q:q_a>0}x_q.\)

The mass constraint for \(a\), together with \(q_a\leq B\), gives

\(\mu_a=\sum_{q:q_a>0}q_ax_q\leq B\sum_{q:q_a>0}x_q.\)

Therefore,

\(W(x,\varnothing)\leq-\frac{\mu_a}{B}=-\frac{1}{D(n+1)}<0.\)

Adding witnesses cannot improve this bound. We have proved

\(V^*=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{the formula is satisfiable}.\)

This is a polynomial-time reduction and proves NP-hardness, with an explicit inverse-polynomial welfare gap.

4. The zero-welfare decision problem is NP-complete

NP-hardness follows from the reduction.

For membership in NP, Section 2 provides a polynomial-size certificate whenever \(V^*=0\): a feasible rational intensity vector with at most \(\tau\) positive compositions and welfare zero.

A verifier checks composition sizes, positivity of intensities, exact mass equalities, and rule applications by searching the listed support for each external condition. All arithmetic and condition checks take polynomial time in the certificate and input lengths.

The input restrictions themselves are polynomial-time checkable. In particular, two conditions \((p,N)\) and \((p',N')\) are simultaneously satisfiable by an allowed composition exactly when their coordinatewise maximum requirement vanishes on \(N\cup N'\) and its coordinate sum is at most \(B\). When those conditions hold, that maximum requirement vector is itself a valid simultaneous witness.

Thus deciding \(V^*=0\) on the specified input class is NP-complete.

5. Exact algorithms and dependence on capacity

Enumerating all compositions

The number of allowed compositions is

\(|Q|=\binom{B+\tau}{\tau}-1.\)

Enumerate these compositions, test their conditions, and solve the regime program from Section 2 for each of the \(2^K\) activation sets. Rational linear programming is polynomial-time in its encoded size; these programs are bounded whenever feasible.

This yields the exact running-time bound

\(2^K\operatorname{poly}\!\left(m,K,\tau,L,\binom{B+\tau}{\tau}\right).\)

Selecting an optimal basic solution in a maximizing regime returns an optimal pair with at most \(\tau\) positive intensities.

Because \(B\) is binary encoded, this bound can be very large. The following compression avoids enumerating all integer compositions.

Threshold cells

Put \(d=m+K\), the total number of local and external conditions.

For each coordinate \(t\), partition the integers from zero through \(B\) at:

  • zero versus positive values;
  • every distinct positive requirement \(p_{rj,t}\) that is at most \(B\).

There are at most \(d+2\) intervals per coordinate. Taking products gives at most

\(H\leq(d+2)^\tau\)

cells. Discard the all-zero cell and any cell whose lower bounds sum to more than \(B\).

Every condition has constant truth value on each remaining cell. Requirements exceeding \(B\) are automatically false.

For a cell with integer bounds \(a_t\leq q_t\leq b_t\), define

\(P_C=\{u\in\mathbb R^\tau:a_t\leq u_t\leq b_t\text{ for every }t,\ \sum_tu_t\leq B\}.\)

The allowed integer compositions in the cell are exactly \(P_C\cap\mathbb Z^\tau\).

Integrality of the cell polytope

Every vertex of \(P_C\) is integral.

To see this, if the sum constraint is not tight, a vertex must have every coordinate at a bound: otherwise that coordinate can be perturbed in both directions.

If the sum constraint is tight, at most one coordinate can lie strictly between its bounds. Two such coordinates could be perturbed in opposite directions while preserving the sum. All other coordinates are then at integer bounds, and the remaining coordinate equals \(B\) minus their integer sum.

Therefore,

\(P_C=\operatorname{conv}(P_C\cap\mathbb Z^\tau).\)

A compressed linear program

Fix a collection \(\mathcal C\) of cells, and let \(E_{\mathcal C}\) be the union of the external conditions true on those cells.

For each cell \(C\), introduce:

  • \(z_C\), its total team intensity;
  • \(y_{C,t}\), its total consumption of type \(t\).

Impose

\(z_C\geq0,\qquad a_{C,t}z_C\leq y_{C,t}\leq b_{C,t}z_C,\qquad \sum_ty_{C,t}\leq Bz_C,\)

and

\(\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}y_{C,t}=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t.\)

Let \(c_{E_{\mathcal C},C}\) be the constant value of \(c_{E_{\mathcal C}}(q)\) on cell \(C\). Maximize

\(\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}c_{E_{\mathcal C},C}z_C.\)

These constraints exactly describe the aggregation of intensities over compositions in the chosen cells:

  • Aggregating genuine composition intensities gives these constraints.
  • Conversely, if \(z_C>0\), then \(y_C/z_C\in P_C\). Cell integrality expresses this average as a convex combination of allowed integer compositions. Multiplying the combination weights by \(z_C\) reconstructs the required intensities.
  • If \(z_C=0\), the coordinate bounds force \(y_C=0\).

A reconstructed support may leave some chosen cells unused. Its actual activation set is then a subset of \(E_{\mathcal C}\), so its actual welfare is at least the compressed objective.

The compressed program is bounded: every retained cell has some lower bound at least one, so \(\sum_ty_{C,t}\geq z_C\), and hence \(\sum_Cz_C\leq1\).

Enumerating at most \(\tau\) cells

Section 2 proves that an optimum uses at most \(\tau\) compositions, hence at most \(\tau\) cells.

Enumerate every collection of at most \(\tau\) retained cells and solve its compressed program. There are at most \((H+1)^\tau\) such collections.

Each compressed objective is at most \(V^*\), because it reconstructs a feasible pair with at least that welfare. Conversely, the cells occupied by a sparse optimum occur among the enumerated collections. For that collection, the optimum itself gives a compressed solution of value \(V^*\).

Thus the largest compressed objective is exactly \(V^*\). Each program has at most \(\tau(\tau+1)\) variables and polynomial coefficient bit length. The resulting bound is

\((m+K+2)^{O(\tau^2)}\operatorname{poly}(m,K,\tau,L).\)

Recovering the listed optimal pair

The convex decompositions used above can be computed in polynomial time.

For completeness, take a rational point \(u\in P_C\). Keep the coordinate bounds tight at \(u\), and also keep the sum equality if it is tight. A vertex in this face can be found by putting remaining coordinates at lower bounds and, when the sum equality must hold, filling them successively toward upper bounds. This produces an integer vertex \(v\).

If \(u\neq v\), extend the ray from \(v\) through \(u\) until it reaches a new boundary point \(u'\). Then \(u\) is a convex combination of \(v\) and \(u'\). Previously tight constraints remain tight, and at least one new independent constraint becomes tight. Repeating therefore takes at most \(\tau\) steps and produces at most \(\tau+1\) integer vertices.

All steps use rational arithmetic of polynomial bit length. Indeed, if a current point has common denominator \(D_0\), the next ray intersection can be written with common denominator at most \(2\tau B D_0\): the facet normals are coordinate vectors or the all-ones vector, and all coordinates lie between zero and \(B\). Denominator bit length therefore grows by only \(O(\log(B+1)+\log(\tau+1))\) per step.

Decompose the winning compressed solution, and solve its surrogate linear program once more over the resulting finite list of integer compositions. An optimal basic solution has at most \(\tau\) positive intensities. Its surrogate welfare is \(V^*\), and its actual welfare is at least \(V^*\), hence exactly \(V^*\). Output this support and \(G=\varnothing\).

6. Running-time summary

All bounds are for exact bit computation and include recovery of an optimal pair.

| Method | Running time |
|---|---|
| Explicit compositions and external activation sets | \(2^K\operatorname{poly}\!\left(m,K,\tau,L,\binom{B+\tau}{\tau}\right)\) |
| Threshold cells and external activation sets | \(2^K(m+K+2)^{O(\tau)}\operatorname{poly}(m,K,\tau,L)\) |
| Collections of at most \(\tau\) threshold cells | \((m+K+2)^{O(\tau^2)}\operatorname{poly}(m,K,\tau,L)\) |

For the middle bound, replace each regime program by its cell aggregation; its allowed cells are those satisfying no external condition outside the guessed activation set.

The last two bounds use \(B\) only through comparisons, interval endpoints, and coefficients with \(O(\log(B+1))\) bits, already included in \(L\). The hardness reduction has \(B=n+1\), so hardness does not depend on an exponentially large numerical capacity.
