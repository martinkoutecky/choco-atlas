The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational \(L<U\), a rational bias \(b\), a finite target set \(T\subseteq\mathbb{R}\), and rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), form, for each denominator-compatible \(N\), a game with \(N\mu_t\) players of target \(t\). Each chooses \(x_i\in[L,U]\), has weight \(1/N\) in \(\hat{x}_N=b+\frac{1}{N}\sum_i x_i\), and incurs loss \((\hat{x}_N-t_i)^2\). Characterize all weak limits \((\nu,h)\) of empirical target-report distributions and aggregates along pure Nash equilibria as \(N\to\infty\): \(\nu\) has target marginal \(\mu\), \(h=b+\int x\,d\nu(t,x)\), mass with \(t<h\) reports \(L\), mass with \(t>h\) reports \(U\), and if \(h\in T\), reports of type \(h\) have the mean needed to make the aggregate equal \(h\).

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\subset\mathbb Q\) of \(\tau\) distinct target types, each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(m=1\) be the report dimension and \(\mathcal L\) the total input bit length.
Given binary rationals \(L<U\) and \(b\), for every positive integer \(N\) satisfying \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\) for all \(t\), form a complete-information game with \(N\mu_t\) players of target \(t\). Each reports \(x_i\in[L,U]\), the aggregate is \(\hat x_N=b+N^{-1}\sum_i x_i\), and player \(i\)'s loss is \((\hat x_N-t_i)^2\). Targets and bias may have either sign; targets need not lie in \([L,U]\).
Let \(\mathcal E_\infty\) consist of pairs \((\nu,h)\) obtainable along some compatible sequence \(N_k\to\infty\) of pure Nash equilibria, where \(N_k^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{(t_i,x_i)}\) converges weakly to \(\nu\) and \(\hat x_{N_k}\to h\).
Define \(\mathcal C\) as the pairs with \(\nu\) a probability measure on \(T\times[L,U]\), target marginal \(\mu\), and \(h=b+\int x\,d\nu(t,x)\), such that \(\nu(\cdot\mid t)=\delta_L\) for \(t<h\) and \(\nu(\cdot\mid t)=\delta_U\) for \(t>h\). Result

Yes: \(\mathcal E_\infty=\mathcal C\). For the stated report dimension \(m=1\), an exact finite description is computable in polynomial time in \(\tau\) and \(\mathcal L\).

Moreover, there is a unique equilibrium aggregate \(h_*\), shared by every pure equilibrium at every compatible population size. Only the type whose target equals \(h_*\), if present, can have a conditional report law not forced to an endpoint.

1. Exact finite-player equilibrium conditions

Fix a compatible population size \(N\), a report profile, and its aggregate \(h=b+N^{-1}\sum_i x_i\). If player \(i\) changes its report by a feasible amount \(\Delta\), its loss changes by

\(\displaystyle \left(h+\frac{\Delta}{N}-t_i\right)^2-(h-t_i)^2=\frac{2(h-t_i)\Delta}{N}+\frac{\Delta^2}{N^2}.\)

Consequently, the profile is a pure Nash equilibrium if and only if

\(\displaystyle t_i<h\ \Longrightarrow\ x_i=L,\qquad t_i>h\ \Longrightarrow\ x_i=U.\)

There is no additional restriction on an individual report when \(t_i=h\), beyond the report interval and the aggregate identity.

To prove necessity, suppose \(t_i<h\) and \(x_i>L\). Choose

\(\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<\min\{x_i-L,\,2N(h-t_i)\}.\)

The feasible deviation \(\Delta=-\varepsilon\) strictly decreases loss. Similarly, if \(t_i>h\) and \(x_i<U\), choosing

\(\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<\min\{U-x_i,\,2N(t_i-h)\}\)

makes \(\Delta=\varepsilon\) strictly profitable.

For sufficiency, when \(t_i<h\) and \(x_i=L\), every feasible deviation satisfies \(\Delta\geq0\), so both terms in the loss-change formula are nonnegative. When \(t_i>h\) and \(x_i=U\), every feasible deviation satisfies \(\Delta\leq0\), again making both terms nonnegative. When \(t_i=h\), the loss change is \(\Delta^2/N^2\), which is nonnegative. This proves the characterization directly; compare [Lemma 8 of the supplied source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29010v1#S3.SS3).

Pure equilibria exist for every compatible \(N\). Indeed, define

\(\displaystyle \Phi_N(x)=\left(b+\frac1N\sum_i x_i\right)^2-\frac2N\sum_i t_i x_i.\)

A unilateral deviation changes \(\Phi_N\) by exactly the deviating player's loss change. The continuous function \(\Phi_N\) attains a minimum on the compact box \([L,U]^N\). Any such minimizer is therefore a pure equilibrium.

2. There is exactly one feasible aggregate

For each type \(t\), let \(\rho_t=\nu(\cdot\mid t)\) and let \(a_t=\int x\,d\rho_t(x)\). The defining conditions of \(\mathcal C\) require

\(\displaystyle a_t\in A_t(h),\qquad A_t(h)= \begin{cases} \{U\},&h<t,\\ [L,U],&h=t,\\ \{L\},&h>t, \end{cases}\)

together with \(h=b+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_t\).

Suppose two pairs in \(\mathcal C\) have aggregates \(h_1<h_2\). For every type \(t\), every element of \(A_t(h_1)\) is at least every element of \(A_t(h_2)\). This follows from \(U\geq a\geq L\) for \(a\in[L,U]\): as the aggregate increases past a target, its permitted mean changes from \(U\), through \([L,U]\), to \(L\). Thus \(a_t^{(1)}\geq a_t^{(2)}\) for every \(t\), implying

\(\displaystyle h_1=b+\sum_t\mu_t a_t^{(1)}\geq b+\sum_t\mu_t a_t^{(2)}=h_2,\)

a contradiction. Hence \(\mathcal C\) has at most one aggregate.

It also has at least one. Let \(D\) be a common multiple of the denominators of the masses, so \(D\) is compatible. By Section 1, the game with \(D\) players has a pure equilibrium. Its empirical measure and aggregate satisfy the conditions defining \(\mathcal C\).

Denote this unique feasible aggregate by \(h_*\). Section 1 also shows that every finite equilibrium's empirical pair belongs to \(\mathcal C\). Therefore every finite equilibrium has aggregate exactly \(h_*\).

3. Every equilibrium limit belongs to \(\mathcal C\)

Take a pair \((\nu,h)\in\mathcal E_\infty\), witnessed by equilibrium empirical measures \(\nu_k\) and aggregates \(h_k\).

All measures are supported on the compact space \(T\times[L,U]\). Their target marginals equal \(\mu\), so the weak limit has the same marginal. Since \(T\) is finite, weak convergence also gives convergence of each conditional report law.

The report coordinate is bounded and continuous on this space. Hence

\(\displaystyle h=\lim_k h_k=b+\lim_k\int x\,d\nu_k(t,x)=b+\int x\,d\nu(t,x).\)

If \(t<h\), then \(t<h_k\) for all sufficiently large \(k\). Section 1 forces every type-\(t\) player in those equilibria to report \(L\), giving \(\rho_t=\delta_L\). Similarly, \(t>h\) implies \(\rho_t=\delta_U\).

If \(h\in T\), substituting these forced laws into the aggregate identity gives

\(\displaystyle \int x\,d\rho_h(x)= \frac{h-b-L\sum_{t<h}\mu_t-U\sum_{t>h}\mu_t}{\mu_h}.\)

These are precisely the conditions defining \(\mathcal C\). Thus \(\mathcal E_\infty\subseteq\mathcal C\).

4. Mean-preserving empirical approximation

The reverse inclusion requires exact preservation of the conditional mean.

Lemma. Let \(\rho\) be any probability measure on \([L,U]\), with mean \(q\). For every positive integer \(n\), there exist points \(y_{n,1},\ldots,y_{n,n}\in[L,U]\) such that

\(\displaystyle \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n y_{n,j}=q,\qquad \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\delta_{y_{n,j}}\ \longrightarrow\ \rho\)

weakly as \(n\to\infty\).

Proof. Write \(F(x)=\rho([L,x])\), and define its quantile function by

\(\displaystyle Q(s)=\inf\{x\in[L,U]:F(x)\geq s\},\qquad 0<s<1.\)

Right-continuity of \(F\) gives \(Q(s)\leq x\) if and only if \(s\leq F(x)\). Thus, if \(S\) is uniform on \((0,1)\), then \(Q(S)\) has law \(\rho\).

Set

\(\displaystyle y_{n,j}=n\int_{(j-1)/n}^{j/n}Q(s)\,ds.\)

Every point lies in \([L,U]\), and summation gives

\(\displaystyle \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n y_{n,j}=\int_0^1Q(s)\,ds=q.\)

To prove convergence, let \(Y_n(s)=y_{n,j}\) on the \(j\)-th subinterval. Let \(\alpha_j\) and \(\beta_j\) be the essential infimum and supremum of \(Q\) there. Because \(Q\) is nondecreasing,

\(\displaystyle \beta_j\leq\alpha_{j+1},\qquad \sum_{j=1}^n(\beta_j-\alpha_j)\leq U-L.\)

Both \(Q(s)\) and \(Y_n(s)\) lie in \([\alpha_j,\beta_j]\) almost everywhere on that subinterval. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \int_0^1|Q(s)-Y_n(s)|\,ds \leq\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n(\beta_j-\alpha_j) \leq\frac{U-L}{n}.\)

For any continuous function \(f\) on \([L,U]\), uniform continuity now implies \(\int_0^1 f(Y_n(s))\,ds\to\int_0^1 f(Q(s))\,ds\). Explicitly, for any \(\delta>0\), the set where \(|Y_n-Q|>\delta\) has measure at most \((U-L)/(n\delta)\); on its complement, uniform continuity bounds \(|f(Y_n)-f(Q)|\). The law of \(Y_n(S)\) is the required equally weighted empirical measure. This proves the lemma.

5. Every pair in \(\mathcal C\) is an equilibrium limit

Fix \((\nu,h)\in\mathcal C\), and choose compatible sizes \(N_k=kD\).

If \(h\notin T\), all conditional report laws are forced endpoint masses. At every size \(N_k\), assign those endpoint reports to the respective types. The aggregate is exactly \(h\), and Section 1 proves that the profile is an equilibrium. Its empirical measure equals \(\nu\) at every size.

Now suppose \(h\in T\). Let \(\rho_h=\nu(\cdot\mid h)\), with its prescribed mean \(q\). The number \(n_k=N_k\mu_h\) of type-\(h\) players is an integer and tends to infinity because \(\mu_h>0\).

Apply the lemma with \(n=n_k\), and assign its reports to these players. Assign \(L\) to every type below \(h\), and \(U\) to every type above \(h\). Exact mean preservation gives aggregate

\(\displaystyle b+L\sum_{t<h}\mu_t+U\sum_{t>h}\mu_t+\mu_hq=h.\)

Section 1 therefore proves that each profile is a pure equilibrium. Its type-\(h\) conditional empirical law converges to \(\rho_h\), while every other conditional law already equals its required endpoint mass. Thus the joint empirical measures converge to \(\nu\).

This proves \(\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal E_\infty\), and hence \(\mathcal E_\infty=\mathcal C\).

6. Polynomial-time exact description

Sort the targets as \(t_1<\cdots<t_\tau\), and write \(\mu_i=\mu_{t_i}\). Define

\(\displaystyle p_j=\sum_{i=1}^j\mu_i,\qquad r_j=b+Lp_j+U(1-p_j),\qquad 0\leq j\leq\tau.\)

Here \(p_0=0\). In particular,

\(\displaystyle r_{i-1}-r_i=(U-L)\mu_i>0.\)

There are two kinds of candidates.

  • Aggregate in a gap. For \(0\leq j\leq\tau\), test

\(\displaystyle t_j<r_j<t_{j+1},\)
using \(t_0=-\infty\) and \(t_{\tau+1}=+\infty\) solely as comparison conventions. If this holds, \(h_*=r_j\). Types with indices at most \(j\) report \(L\); all others report \(U\).

  • Aggregate at a target. For \(1\leq i\leq\tau\), test

\(\displaystyle r_i\leq t_i\leq r_{i-1}.\)
If this holds, \(h_*=t_i\), and its conditional mean is
\(\displaystyle q_i=L+\frac{t_i-r_i}{\mu_i}.\)
The tested inequalities and the identity for \(r_{i-1}-r_i\) imply \(L\leq q_i\leq U\).

These tests are exhaustive: any feasible aggregate either lies in a target gap, where its value must equal the corresponding \(r_j\), or equals a target, where feasibility is exactly the second test. Existence and uniqueness from Section 2 imply that exactly one candidate succeeds.

The algorithm outputs \(h_*\) and writes

\(\displaystyle \nu=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i(\delta_{t_i}\otimes\rho_i),\)

where each \(\rho_i\) is a probability measure satisfying the rational constraints

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{supp}(\rho_i)\subseteq \begin{cases} \{L\},&t_i<h_*,\\ [L,U],&t_i=h_*,\\ \{U\},&t_i>h_*, \end{cases}\)

and

\(\displaystyle \int x\,d\rho_i(x)= \begin{cases} L,&t_i<h_*,\\ q_i,&t_i=h_*,\\ U,&t_i>h_*. \end{cases}\)

This finite description permits arbitrary probability laws satisfying these constraints, including laws with infinite support.

Sorting takes \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) rational comparisons. Prefix sums, candidate tests, and output construction require \(O(\tau)\) additional rational arithmetic operations. All resulting rationals have polynomial bit length: a common denominator for the mass sums can be taken as the product of the input mass denominators, whose bit length is at most their total input length. The subsequent formulas use only a bounded number of further rational operations per entry. Exact rational arithmetic therefore gives polynomial running time and polynomial output length.

The requested description problem is consequently polynomial-time solvable for \(m=1\).
