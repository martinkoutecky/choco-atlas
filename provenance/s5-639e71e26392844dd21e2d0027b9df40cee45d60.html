The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given goods \(G\), a finite support \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and each type's rational per-capita budget \(b_t\) and utilities \(u_{tj}\), assume each type values some good positively and each good is desired by some type. Compute rational prices \(p_j>0\), per-capita allocations \(x_{tj}\geq 0\), and returned money \(s_t\geq 0\) such that \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_{tj}=1\) for every good \(j\), \(\sum_{j\in G}p_jx_{tj}+s_t=b_t\) for every type \(t\), and each \((x_t,s_t)\) maximizes \(\sum_j u_{tj}y_j+r\) over \(y_j,r\geq 0\) subject to \(\sum_jp_jy_j+r\leq b_t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) contain \(m\geq1\) divisible goods, each with total supply \(1\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a binary-rational per-capita budget \(b_t>0\) and utilities \(u_{tj}\geq0\). Every type values some good positively, and every good is valued positively by some type. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An equilibrium consists of rational prices \(p_j>0\), per-capita allocations \(x_{tj}\geq0\), and per-capita returned money \(s_t\geq0\), satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_{tj}=1\) for each good and \(\sum_{j\in G}p_jx_{tj}+s_t=b_t\) for each type.
For every type \(t\), \((x_t,s_t)\) must maximize \(\sum_{j\in G}u_{tj}y_j+r\) over real \(y_j,r\geq0\) with \(\sum_{j\in G}p_jy_j+r\leq b_t\). Any maximizing bundle is allowed, including splits among tied goods and retained money; there is no individual quantity cap.

Is computing such an exact rational equilibrium possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For a finite list of buyers, exact Arctic Auction equilibrium computation has a combinatorial polynomial-time algorithm ([Vazirani](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21637v2)); a subsequent result gives a strongly polynomial algorithm ([Garg, Taherijam, and Vazirani](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24756)).
No result explicitly treating this normalized, rational-support mass formulation was verified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required strictly positive budgets: the literal allowance of zero budgets can invalidate positive-price market clearing under the stated desirability assumptions and makes the algorithm's initialization degenerate.
  • Specified nonnegative rational utilities, following the discrete model's parameter convention.
  • Required nonempty goods and support, and defined L as the total binary input bit length, including masses.
  • Made explicit that allocations and returned money are per capita, demand has no individual quantity cap, and any utility-maximizing split at ties is permitted.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

An exact rational equilibrium can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). One aggregate buyer per type suffices. The reduction preserves every equilibrium condition and does not expand rational masses into individual buyers.

Here numerical inputs, including utilities, are understood to be rational numbers encoded in binary.

1. The finite-buyer theorem used

We use the following established result: a finite Arctic Auction with positive rational buyer budgets, nonnegative rational utilities, and unit supplies admits an exact rational equilibrium computable in polynomial time, provided every buyer values some good positively and every good is positively valued by some buyer. Buyers maximize linear utility from goods plus returned money, without quantity caps. This is the model in Definition 1 and the algorithmic result in Sections 5–6 of [Vazirani’s paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21637v2).

Thus, it remains to give a polynomial-size reduction to that model and prove that its output solves the stated problem.

2. Reduction and preservation of individual optimality

Construct a finite Arctic Auction with buyer set \(T\), the same goods and utilities, and buyer budgets

\[ B_t:=\mu_t b_t. \]

Each \(B_t\) is positive and rational. Both positivity assumptions on the utility matrix are unchanged, so the finite-buyer theorem applies.

Fix any positive price vector \(p\). Define the per-capita feasible set for type \(t\) by

\[ F_t(p):=\left\{(y,r)\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{m}\times\mathbb R_{\geq0}: \sum_{j\in G}p_jy_j+r\leq b_t\right\}, \]

and the aggregate buyer’s feasible set by

\[ \widehat F_t(p):=\left\{(Y,R)\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{m}\times\mathbb R_{\geq0}: \sum_{j\in G}p_jY_j+R\leq B_t\right\}. \]

Because \(\mu_t>0\), the map

\[ (y,r)\longmapsto(\mu_t y,\mu_t r) \]

is a bijection from \(F_t(p)\) to \(\widehat F_t(p)\), with inverse division by \(\mu_t\). Indeed, multiplying the per-capita budget inequality by \(\mu_t\) gives exactly the aggregate budget inequality.

Moreover, the aggregate objective satisfies

\[ \sum_{j\in G}u_{tj}(\mu_t y_j)+\mu_t r =\mu_t\left(\sum_{j\in G}u_{tj}y_j+r\right). \]

Multiplication by the positive number \(\mu_t\) preserves all objective comparisons. Therefore, \((y,r)\) maximizes the per-capita objective if and only if \((\mu_t y,\mu_t r)\) maximizes the aggregate objective. This also preserves arbitrary splits among tied goods and returned money.

3. Recovering the required equilibrium

Run the finite-buyer algorithm, obtaining rational prices \(p_j>0\), aggregate quantities \(X_{tj}\geq0\), and aggregate returned money \(S_t\geq0\). Define

\[ x_{tj}:=\frac{X_{tj}}{\mu_t}, \qquad s_t:=\frac{S_t}{\mu_t}. \]

These numbers are rational and nonnegative. Aggregate market clearing gives, for each good \(j\),

\[ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_{tj} =\sum_{t\in T}X_{tj} =1. \]

For each type \(t\), the aggregate budget identity gives

\[ \sum_{j\in G}p_jx_{tj}+s_t =\frac{\sum_{j\in G}p_jX_{tj}+S_t}{\mu_t} =\frac{B_t}{\mu_t} =b_t. \]

Individual optimality follows from the bijection proved in Section 2. Thus the recovered tuple satisfies every equilibrium requirement in the question.

Conversely, multiplying any equilibrium’s allocations and returned money by the corresponding masses produces a finite-buyer equilibrium. Hence the transformation establishes an exact correspondence between the two equilibrium formulations.

4. Bit complexity

Write \(\mu_t=a_t/d_t\) and \(b_t=c_t/e_t\), with positive integer numerators and denominators. Then

\[ B_t=\frac{a_tc_t}{d_te_t}. \]

The numerator and denominator bit lengths of this product are bounded by the sums of the corresponding input bit lengths, up to a constant. Consequently, constructing all aggregate budgets takes polynomial time and produces a transformed instance of polynomial input length. It has exactly \(\tau\) buyers and \(m\) goods.

The finite-buyer algorithm therefore runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and returns rational data of polynomial bit length. Dividing each returned allocation and refund by its encoded positive mass requires polynomial-time rational arithmetic and increases bit length by at most the mass’s encoding length, up to a constant.

This proves polynomial-time computability of the requested exact rational equilibrium. No separate algorithm for rational-support societies is needed: the finite-buyer theorem applies through the explicit reduction above.
