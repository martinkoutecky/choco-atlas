The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given bipartite type sets \(U,V\), positive rational masses \(\mu_i\), supported pairs \(E\), rational flow capacities \(\kappa_{ij}\), and type-specific separable, concave, piecewise-affine payoff functions, find a feasible flow \(q\) with \(0\le q_{ij}\le\kappa_{ij}\), per-type budgets \(\sum_j q_{ij}\le\beta_i\mu_i\), and symmetric per-capita allocations \(q_{ij}/\mu_i\) and \(q_{ij}/\mu_j\). Require full stability on this type-level network: no type can improve by reducing its incident flows, and no supported pair of types can both improve by increasing their shared flow while reducing other incident flows, subject to feasibility. Does an exact finite-step or polynomial-time algorithm find such a flow, in terms of the type count, supported pairs, and input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: disjoint nonempty type sets \(U,V\), with \(\tau=|U|+|V|\) positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_i\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Input also includes a nonempty supported-pair set \(E\subseteq U\times V\), \(m=|E|\), nonnegative rational capacities \(\kappa_{ij}\), and positive rational per-capita budgets \(\beta_i\).
For each endpoint \(i\) of each pair \(e\), a continuous concave piecewise-affine function \(f_{i,e}:[0,\kappa_e/\mu_i]\to\mathbb R\) is explicitly specified by rational breakpoints and affine coefficients; values and slopes may have either sign. Let \(K\) count all affine pieces and \(L\) be the total binary input length.
A flow is feasible when \(0\le q_e\le\kappa_e\) and \(\sum_{e\ni i}q_e\le\beta_i\mu_i\) for every type \(i\). Allocation is uniform within each type: pair \(e=\{i,j\}\) gives per-capita amounts \(q_e/\mu_i\) and \(q_e/\mu_j\), representing the same total flow.
Type \(i\)'s payoff is \(u_i(q)=\sum_{e\ni i}f_{i,e}(q_e/\mu_i)\); there are no externalities.
A feasible flow is fully stable if no type strictly improves through a feasible simultaneous decrease of any subset of its incident flows, with all other flows fixed.
It must also admit no feasible deviation by a supported pair \(\{i,j\}\) that weakly increases its shared flow, weakly decreases any other flows incident to either endpoint, fixes all remaining flows, and strictly improves both endpoints' payoffs. Indifference does not block; no tie-breaking rule is imposed.

Can an exact fully stable flow always be found in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For integral capacities and budgets and concave piecewise-affine payoffs with integral breakpoints, generalized deferred acceptance produces an integral fully stable network in at most \(\sum_{e\in E}\alpha_e\) iterations; this is a numerical, rather than binary-input polynomial, bound. [Castera–Fixary–Laraki](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18779v1)
For finite bipartite networks with continuous separable concave payoffs and no externalities, existence and convergence of generalized deferred acceptance are established, while finite-time computation is left open. [Castera–Fixary–Laraki](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18779v1)
Ordinal stable allocation with strict partner rankings and real capacities and quotas has a strongly polynomial algorithm. [Baïou–Balinski, corrected version](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total supported mass to one, interpreting the society as a probability distribution.
  • Require nonempty bipartition classes and a nonempty supported-pair set; allow zero capacities and require positive budgets, following the discrete domains.
  • Specify payoff as an additive function of per-capita incident allocations, with no externalities, as required by the anchor.
  • Interpret symmetry as a common total pair flow and uniform allocations within each type, without requiring equal per-capita allocations across types of different masses.
  • Require continuous payoff pieces with explicit rational breakpoints and coefficients; allow signed values and slopes without imposing monotonicity.
  • Restore weak shared-flow increases, simultaneous incident-flow reductions, unchanged nonincident flows, and strict improvement for every deviating endpoint.

Result

An exact fully stable flow can always be found in deterministic time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\).

The proof reduces the problem to ordinal stable allocation. Concavity ensures that the allocation fills each pair’s marginal intervals in order. It also ensures that every strictly profitable finite pair deviation would produce an ordinal blocking edge.

Ties are refined only for the auxiliary computation; the proof establishes stability under the original strict-improvement definition.

1. Expressing payoffs in total-flow units

For every type \(i\), define its total-flow budget by

\(b_i=\beta_i\mu_i>0.\)

For an incident pair \(e\), define

\(g_{i,e}(t)=f_{i,e}(t/\mu_i),\qquad 0\leq t\leq\kappa_e.\)

Then \(u_i(q)=\sum_{e\ni i}g_{i,e}(q_e)\), and feasibility becomes

\(0\leq q_e\leq\kappa_e,\qquad \sum_{e\ni i}q_e\leq b_i.\)

Each \(g_{i,e}\) is continuous, concave, and piecewise affine. A breakpoint \(z\) of \(f_{i,e}\) becomes \(\mu_i z\); an affine slope \(a\) becomes \(a/\mu_i\). These transformations use exact rational arithmetic with polynomial encoding length.

For each positive-capacity pair \(e=\{i,j\}\), merge its two endpoints’ transformed breakpoints to obtain

\(0=t_{e,0}<t_{e,1}<\cdots<t_{e,n_e}=\kappa_e.\)

On interval \(k\), both endpoint functions are affine. Write their slopes as \(a_{i,e,k}\) and \(a_{j,e,k}\), and the interval length as

\(d_{e,k}=t_{e,k}-t_{e,k-1}>0.\)

Concavity implies that, at either endpoint, these slopes are nonincreasing in \(k\).

Retain precisely the initial intervals on which both endpoint slopes are strictly positive. They form an initial sequence because both slope sequences are nonincreasing. Let \(r_e\) be its length and define

\(c_e=t_{e,r_e},\)

where \(c_e=0\) if no interval is retained. For a zero-capacity pair, also set \(c_e=0\).

Two properties will be used:

  • Each \(g_{i,e}\) is nondecreasing on \([0,c_e]\).
  • If \(c_e<\kappa_e\), some endpoint \(i\) satisfies

\(g_{i,e}(t)\leq g_{i,e}(c_e)\) for every \(t\in[c_e,\kappa_e]\).

For the second property, an endpoint has nonpositive slope immediately after \(c_e\). Concavity makes all its subsequent slopes nonpositive as well.

The total number \(N=\sum_e r_e\) of retained intervals is \(O(K+m)\).

2. The auxiliary ordinal allocation problem

Replace each retained interval by a separate edge, or contract, \(h=(e,k)\), between the original endpoints of \(e\). Give it capacity \(d_{e,k}\), and give every original vertex \(i\) quota \(b_i\).

At endpoint \(i\), rank contracts by decreasing slope \(a_{i,e,k}\). Refine equal slopes by a fixed ordering of pair labels, then by increasing interval index within a pair.

Thus, for every pair \(e\), both endpoints strictly prefer each earlier retained interval to every later retained interval. This follows from concavity when the slopes differ and from the refinement when they agree.

For an allocation \(x\), say that vertex \(i\) wants an incident contract \(h\) if either:

  • its total allocation is strictly below its quota; or
  • some positive allocated contract is ranked below \(h\).

An ordinal allocation is stable if no unsaturated contract is wanted by both endpoints.

We use the following established theorem: on a finite simple bipartite graph with positive vertex quotas, nonnegative edge capacities, and strict preference orders, a stable allocation can be computed by a strongly polynomial algorithm. This applies to arbitrary real quotas and capacities. See the corrected [Baïou–Balinski paper, Section 2, Theorem 2 and Corollary 1](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

Our interval graph can have parallel edges. The following reduction removes them, so no extension of that theorem is assumed.

Lemma: parallel contracts can be removed with constant-size gadgets

Consider a contract \(h\) between \(u\in U\) and \(v\in V\), with capacity \(\gamma>0\).

Introduce four fresh vertices \(A,B,C,D\), placing \(A,C\) on the \(V\) side and \(B,D\) on the \(U\) side. Introduce the six edges

\(uA,\quad Bv,\quad AB,\quad BC,\quad CD,\quad DA.\)

Every new edge has capacity \(\gamma\), and every auxiliary vertex has quota \(\gamma\).

At \(u\), edge \(uA\) occupies the original rank of \(h\); at \(v\), edge \(Bv\) occupies its original rank. The auxiliary preferences, from best to worst, are:

| Vertex | Preference order |
|---|---|
| \(A\) | \(AB\succ uA\succ AD\) |
| \(B\) | \(BC\succ Bv\succ BA\) |
| \(C\) | \(CD\succ CB\) |
| \(D\) | \(DA\succ DC\) |

Apply this construction separately to every contract. The resulting bipartite graph is simple.

Proof that every stable gadget allocation projects correctly.

First, all four auxiliary vertices must have full quotas. Suppose an auxiliary vertex \(W\) has total allocation below \(\gamma\). Along the directed cycle

\(A\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow A,\)

its predecessor \(Z\) ranks \(ZW\) first. Since \(W\) is under quota, the allocation on \(ZW\) is less than \(\gamma\). Vertex \(Z\) therefore either has unused quota or has positive allocation on a worse edge. Both \(Z\) and \(W\) want the unsaturated edge \(ZW\), contradicting stability.

Let \(p\) and \(r\) be the allocations on \(uA\) and \(Bv\). Summing the full quotas on \(A,C\), and separately on \(B,D\), gives

\(2\gamma=\sum_{\text{cycle edges }a}\widehat x_a+p =\sum_{\text{cycle edges }a}\widehat x_a+r.\)

Hence \(p=r\). Define the projected contract allocation by \(x_h=p=r\). These projected values satisfy the original capacities and quotas.

It remains to prove their ordinal stability. Suppose \(h\) were unsaturated and wanted by both original endpoints. Set

\(z=\widehat x_{AB},\qquad w=\widehat x_{AD}.\)

The full quota at \(A\) gives

\(z+w=\gamma-p>0.\)

If \(w>0\), vertex \(A\) wants \(uA\), because \(AD\) is worse. Together with \(u\), this blocks \(uA\).

If \(z>0\), vertex \(B\) wants \(Bv\), because \(BA\) is worse. Together with \(v\), this blocks \(Bv\).

At least one case applies, contradicting gadget stability. Therefore the projected allocation is ordinally stable. This proves the lemma.

Consequently, we can compute a stable allocation \(x\) on the interval graph using the cited simple-graph algorithm.

3. Stability forces intervals to be filled in order

Lemma. For any pair \(e\), if \(x_{e,\ell}>0\), then every earlier interval \(k<\ell\) is full.

Proof. Otherwise, \((e,k)\) is unsaturated while a later contract \((e,\ell)\) has positive allocation. Both endpoints rank \((e,k)\) above \((e,\ell)\), so both want \((e,k)\). This contradicts ordinal stability.

Define the output flow by

\(q_e=\sum_{k=1}^{r_e}x_{e,k}.\)

It is feasible: \(q_e\leq c_e\leq\kappa_e\), and its incident totals equal those in the interval allocation.

The lemma means that the interval allocation is exactly the prefix representation of this flow:

\(x_{e,k}=\min\{d_{e,k},\max\{0,q_e-t_{e,k-1}\}\}.\)

Thus, the first unsaturated interval gives the right-hand slope at \(q_e<c_e\), and the last positively allocated interval gives the left-hand slope at \(q_e>0\).

4. The output flow is fully stable

Unilateral decreases

For every incident pair \(h\), we have \(q_h\leq c_h\), and \(g_{i,h}\) is nondecreasing on \([0,c_h]\). Therefore, decreasing any subset of incident flows cannot increase any corresponding payoff summand.

No type can strictly improve by a unilateral simultaneous decrease.

Pair deviations

Suppose, for contradiction, that pair \(e=\{i,j\}\) has a feasible deviation \(q'\) strictly improving both endpoints. Write

\(\delta=q'_e-q_e\geq0.\)

All other changed incident flows decrease. Their payoff contributions cannot increase, by the preceding argument. Hence \(\delta>0\).

If \(q_e=c_e=\kappa_e\), increasing \(e\) is infeasible. If \(q_e=c_e<\kappa_e\), property 2 from Section 1 supplies an endpoint whose payoff contribution from \(e\) cannot increase; its other changed contributions cannot increase either. That endpoint cannot strictly improve.

We therefore have \(q_e<c_e\).

Let \(s=(e,k)\) be the first unsaturated retained interval. At endpoint \(i\), its slope is

\(a_i=a_{i,e,k}>0.\)

Concavity gives the finite-increase bound

\(g_{i,e}(q_e+\delta)-g_{i,e}(q_e)\leq a_i\delta.\)

Indeed, every affine slope to the right of \(q_e\) is at most \(a_i\).

We show that endpoint \(i\) wants contract \(s\).

If \(i\) has unused quota at \(q\), this holds by definition. Otherwise,

\(\sum_{h\ni i}q_h=b_i.\)

For every other incident pair \(h\), put \(D_h=q_h-q'_h\geq0\). Feasibility of the deviation implies

\(\sum_{h\ni i,\ h\ne e}D_h\geq\delta.\)

For a pair with \(D_h>0\), necessarily \(q_h>0\). Let \(\ell_{i,h}\) be the left-hand slope of \(g_{i,h}\) at \(q_h\). It is the slope of the last positively allocated interval of \(h\). Concavity yields

\(g_{i,h}(q_h)-g_{i,h}(q_h-D_h)\geq\ell_{i,h}D_h,\)

because every slope on the withdrawn interval is at least \(\ell_{i,h}\).

If every withdrawn pair satisfied \(\ell_{i,h}\geq a_i\), the endpoint’s total payoff change would be at most

\(a_i\delta-\sum_{h:D_h>0}\ell_{i,h}D_h \leq a_i\bigl(\delta-\sum_hD_h\bigr)\leq0.\)

This contradicts its strict improvement. Therefore, some withdrawn pair satisfies

\(\ell_{i,h}<a_i.\)

Its last positively allocated contract is ranked strictly below \(s\), since its actual slope is smaller. Thus \(i\) wants \(s\).

The same argument applies independently to \(j\). Both endpoints consequently want the unsaturated contract \(s\), contradicting ordinal stability.

This rules out every permitted pair deviation. Together with unilateral stability, it proves full stability.

5. Exact binary-input complexity

The construction has polynomial size:

  • there are \(N=O(K+m)\) retained contracts;
  • their gadgets introduce \(4N\) vertices and \(6N\) edges;
  • all breakpoints, slopes, capacities, and quotas have polynomial rational encoding length.

The hypotheses of the ordinal theorem hold: the gadget graph is finite, simple, and bipartite; every quota is positive; every edge capacity is positive; and every preference list is strict. If a complete preference list is required, absent edges can be assigned zero capacity and ranked arbitrarily.

Exact rational computation also has polynomial bit complexity. The Baïou–Balinski inductive algorithm uses additions, subtractions, comparisons, and minima, with polynomially many arithmetic operations; these operations are explicit in [Section 2 of the corrected paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

To see the encoding bound directly, multiply all constructed capacities and quotas by the product \(D\) of their denominators. The binary length of \(D\) is polynomial in the constructed input length. Positive scaling preserves ordinal stability. On the scaled integer instance, the algorithm’s operations preserve integrality. After polynomially many additions and subtractions, intermediate integers still have polynomial binary length: each elementary addition or subtraction increases the maximum required length by at most one bit. Dividing the resulting allocation by \(D\) recovers an exact rational allocation.

Finally, summing its interval allocations produces each \(q_e\) exactly in polynomial time.

If no interval is retained, the output is simply \(q=0\); the stability proof above applies unchanged. Thus all permitted inputs are covered.
