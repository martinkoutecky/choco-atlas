The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses μ_t over student types, per-capita hospital capacities κ_h, additive hospital values u_h(t), and a stable flow x with ∑_h x_{th} ≤ μ_t and ∑_t x_{th} ≤ κ_h, does there exist a profile of hospital reports, each an ordered list of types, such that mass-based student-proposing deferred acceptance returns x and no hospital can improve its value by changing its report? Each type records a student’s ranking of hospitals and the priority ranks assigned by every hospital; mass-based acceptance may split a type at a capacity boundary.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(H\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) hospitals. A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a strict ranking of \(H\cup\{0\}\), where \(0\) means unmatched, and a true priority rank at every hospital. Hospital capacities \(\kappa_h>0\) are fixed binary rationals. Values \(u_h(t)\) are binary rationals, nonzero and pairwise distinct for each hospital; true hospital priorities rank types by decreasing value, with \(0\) valued at zero. Thus a type is hospital-acceptable exactly when \(u_h(t)>0\).
A feasible flow satisfies \(x_{th}\geq0\), \(\sum_hx_{th}\leq\mu_t\), and \(\sum_tx_{th}\leq\kappa_h\); put \(x_{t0}=\mu_t-\sum_hx_{th}\). Stability means positive assignments are mutually acceptable, and no positive mass of a type assigned to a less-preferred hospital or unmatched wants an acceptable hospital that has spare capacity or holds positive mass of a strictly lower-priority type.
Students report truthfully. Each hospital may report any strict ordered list of a subset of \(T\), including the empty list; omitted types are rejected. Reports treat all mass of a type identically and may include truly unacceptable types.
Mass-based student-proposing deferred acceptance proceeds in simultaneous rounds: initially all mass proposes to its favorite student-acceptable hospital; subsequently rejected mass proposes to its next such hospital. Each hospital retains, from held mass and new proposals, listed types in reported order up to capacity, splitting the boundary type and rejecting the rest. Mass exhausting its list stays unmatched. Denote the resulting flow by \(\operatorname{DA}(Q)\).
Hospital \(h\) evaluates a flow \(y\) by \(U_h(y)=\sum_tu_h(t)y_{th}\). A report profile \(Q\) is a pure Nash equilibrium when \(U_h(\operatorname{DA}(Q))\geq U_h(\operatorname{DA}(Q'_h,Q_{-h}))\) for every hospital and every alternative report.

Given a binary-rational flow \(x\), promised stable under the true preferences, is deciding whether some pure Nash equilibrium \(Q\) satisfies \(\operatorname{DA}(Q)=x\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? Measure running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the total input bit length \(L\).

In finite many-to-one markets with responsive hospital preferences, every stable matching is induced by a pure Nash equilibrium consisting of reports obtained by deleting students from true acceptable lists while preserving their order. [Jaramillo–Kayı–Klijn](https://bw.bse.eu/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/686-file.pdf)
Continuum stable matchings have been characterized through supply and demand equations in a different model; this does not settle the report-equilibrium decision problem above, for which no exact result was identified. [Azevedo–Leshno](https://eduardomazevedo.github.io/papers/Azevedo-Leshno-Supply-and-Demand-Matching.pdf)

1. Structural facts about mass-based DA

For a report profile $Q$, call a flow *reported-stable* if stability is evaluated using the listed types and their reported priorities, while retaining the students’ true preferences.

For two flows $z,w$, say that $z$ *student-dominates* $w$ if, for every type and every preference threshold, the mass assigned at or above that threshold is at least as large under $z$.

Lemma 1: stability and student optimality

For every report profile $Q$, the flow $y=\operatorname{DA}(Q)$ is reported-stable and student-dominates every rational reported-stable flow.

Proof. Choose a positive integer $D$ clearing the denominators of the masses and capacities. Replace type $t$ by $D\mu_t$ individual students, numbered consecutively. Hospital $h$ has capacity $D\kappa_h$ and ranks students by their reported type order, breaking ties within each type by increasing individual number.

This is a finite many-to-one market with strict preferences and responsive hospital choice. Its simultaneous student-proposing DA, aggregated by type and divided by $D$, reproduces the stated mass-based algorithm: each hospital retains successive type blocks up to capacity, splitting the boundary block.

We use the standard finite DA theorem: student-proposing DA is stable and gives every student a weakly preferred assignment relative to every stable matching. The finite model and this student-optimality property are described in [Jaramillo–Kayı–Klijn](https://bw.bse.eu/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/686-file.pdf). The expansion satisfies its hypotheses: finite populations, integer capacities, strict student preferences, and strict responsive hospital priorities.

Aggregation preserves stability. Indeed, a mass-level block involving a vacancy or a strictly lower-priority type would supply an individual blocking pair in the expanded market.

Now let $z$ be a rational reported-stable flow, and enlarge $D$ to clear its denominators too. Within each type, assign consecutive individuals to destinations in decreasing student-preference order, putting unmatched individuals last.

This individual matching is stable. A blocking individual–hospital pair involving a vacancy or a different lower-priority type would contradict reported stability of $z$. A replacement involving the same type cannot block: every individual assigned to the proposed hospital has a smaller number than any same-type individual assigned somewhere worse.

Finite student optimality therefore applies to this representation of $z$. Summing its individual comparisons within each type gives the asserted cumulative mass inequalities. $\square$

Lemma 2: every DA outcome has forest support

For a flow $y$, let $G(y)$ be the undirected bipartite graph on $T$ and $H\cup\{0\}$, with edge $th$ exactly when $y_{th}>0$. If $y=\operatorname{DA}(Q)$, then $G(y)$ is a forest.

Proof. Suppose a simple cycle exists. Each cycle type chooses its preferred one of its two incident cycle destinations.

No hospital can be chosen by both adjacent cycle types. Both would have positive mass at a worse destination; the higher-ranked one would block with the hospital’s positive assignment of the lower-ranked one. The destination $0$ cannot be chosen, because positive hospital assignments are student-acceptable.

The cycle has equally many type and destination vertices. Thus it cannot contain $0$, and every cycle hospital is chosen exactly once.

Choose a positive rational $\varepsilon$ smaller than every cycle-edge assignment. Transfer $\varepsilon$ for each cycle type from its worse cycle destination to its better one. Every type total and every hospital load remains unchanged. Every positive edge remains positive, and no new edge appears.

Reported stability depends on positive support, hospital slack, acceptability, and priority comparisons. All are unchanged, so the perturbed flow remains reported-stable. It strictly improves the cumulative allocation of every cycle type at some threshold and worsens none. This contradicts Lemma 1. $\square$

2. Polynomial-time evaluation and a stronger complexity upper bound

Proposition 3: exact DA evaluation is polynomial-time

Given $Q$, the exact flow $\operatorname{DA}(Q)$ can be computed in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

The relevant established algorithmic result is the following.

Stable allocation theorem. A bipartite stable allocation instance with strict preference lists, vertex quotas, and edge upper bounds admits a strongly polynomial algorithm computing the allocation that lexicographically maximizes each proposing vertex’s allocation vector among stable allocations. An elementary implementation uses polynomially many additions, subtractions, minima, and comparisons; integer inputs remain integral. This is the job-optimal allocation algorithm of [Dean–Munshi, Sections 2–3](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

Here lexicographic comparison lists destinations from best to worst. I verify the reduction and its correspondence with the specified DA outcome.

Proof. Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the masses and capacities, and set

$$ a_t=D\mu_t,\qquad b_h=D\kappa_h,\qquad A=\sum_ta_t=D,\qquad B=\sum_hb_h. $$

These are integers with polynomial binary length.

Create a real job for each type, with quota $a_t$, and a real machine for each hospital, with quota $b_h$. Include real edge $th$ exactly when $h$ is student-acceptable to $t$ and $Q_h$ lists $t$. Its upper bound is $\min(a_t,b_h)$. Use the student and reported hospital orders on these edges.

To accommodate unmatched mass and unused capacity, add a dummy job $d$ of quota $B$ and a dummy machine $z$ of quota $A$. Add edges:

  • $tz$, with upper bound $a_t$;
  • $dh$, with upper bound $b_h$;
  • $dz$, with upper bound $\min(A,B)$.

Every real job ranks $z$ last; every real machine ranks $d$ last. The dummy job ranks all real machines above $z$, and the dummy machine ranks all real jobs above $d$, using arbitrary strict orders otherwise.

Every feasible scaled original flow $w$ has a unique extension filling all quotas:

$$ w_{tz}=a_t-\sum_hw_{th},\qquad w_{dh}=b_h-\sum_tw_{th},\qquad w_{dz}=\sum_{t,h}w_{th}. $$

The last quantity is at most both $A$ and $B$, so all edge bounds are respected.

This extension is stable exactly when the original flow is reported-stable. Dummy edges cannot block: on each such edge, at least one endpoint ranks the other last. On a real edge, a worse allocation at the job means a worse hospital or unmatched mass, while a worse allocation at the machine means a lower-ranked real type or spare capacity.

The real edge upper bound causes no discrepancy. An original blocking pair has positive worse mass of the type and either spare capacity or positive lower-priority mass at the hospital. Consequently its current edge allocation is strictly below both $a_t$ and $b_h$.

Apply the stable allocation theorem and restrict its output to real edges, obtaining $v$. Integer arithmetic makes $v$ integral.

Let $y=D\operatorname{DA}(Q)$. DA uses only additions, subtractions, and minima, so $y$ is integral. It is reported-stable by Lemma 1 and therefore extends to a stable allocation in the constructed instance.

Job optimality says that each real type’s vector under $v$ is lexicographically at least its vector under $y$. Conversely, Lemma 1 says that $y/D$ student-dominates $v/D$, which implies the reverse lexicographic comparison. Hence every real type’s vector is equal:

$$ v=D\operatorname{DA}(Q). $$

The constructed graph has $\tau+m+2$ vertices and at most $m\tau+m+\tau+1$ edges. All scaled quotas and bounds have polynomial binary length. The elementary stable allocation algorithm performs polynomially many arithmetic operations on integer flow and residual quantities bounded by the total quotas. Thus its bit running time is polynomial. Dividing the real allocation by $D$ gives the exact DA outcome. $\square$

Corollary 4: membership in $\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}$

The general equilibrium-attainability problem belongs to $\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}$.

Proof. A report profile has encoding length

$$ O\!\left(m\tau\log(\tau+1)\right). $$

A hospital index and one alternative report also have polynomial encoding length. By Proposition 3, the predicate

$$ \operatorname{DA}(Q)=x \quad\text{and}\quad U_h\!\left(\operatorname{DA}(Q'_h,Q_{-h})\right)\le U_h(x) $$

is decidable in polynomial time. The utility comparisons are exact binary-rational comparisons.

The requested condition is therefore an existential choice of $Q$ followed by a universal choice of $(h,Q'_h)$, with a polynomial-time predicate. Invalid universal encodings can be accepted automatically, while invalid existential encodings are rejected. This is the defining form of $\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}$. $\square$

There is also a deterministic bound without exponential dependence on the numerical denominator. One hospital has

$$ R_\tau=\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}\frac{\tau!}{(\tau-k)!} $$

possible reports. Enumerating profiles and unilateral deviations, using Proposition 3, gives running time

$$ R_\tau^m(1+mR_\tau)\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) = 2^{O((m+1)\tau\log(\tau+1))} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

Neither bound establishes membership in $\mathrm P$ or $\mathrm{NP}$.

3. A new sufficient condition for a supporting equilibrium

Let $G_H(x)$ be the positive-assignment graph with the unmatched destination omitted. Call a type *shared* if it has positive assignments at at least two hospitals.

Theorem 5

Suppose $x$ is true-stable and each connected component of $G_H(x)$ contains at most one shared type. Then $x$ is induced by a pure Nash equilibrium. Such reports can be constructed in polynomial time.

We first record the consequence of stability that controls the construction.

Boundary observation

Call an incumbent type $t$ a *boundary type at $h$* if $x_{th}>0$ and some positive mass of $t$ is assigned to a destination worse than $h$.

At each hospital:

  • there is at most one boundary type;
  • if one exists, the hospital is full;
  • its true value is strictly below that of every other incumbent type.

Indeed, a boundary type wants more of $h$. Stability excludes spare capacity and any lower-priority incumbent. Distinct values give the strict comparison. Two boundary types would require each to have lower value than the other.

Construction of the reports

Write

$$ F_h=\{t:x_{th}=\mu_t\},\qquad M_h=\sum_{t\in F_h}\mu_t. $$

Consider a component containing a shared type $c$. Every hospital in that component must be adjacent to $c$: without another shared type, there is no other way to connect distinct hospitals. Order these hospitals by $c$’s preference:

$$ h_1\succ_c h_2\succ_c\cdots\succ_c h_k, \qquad p_i=x_{ch_i}>0. $$

Every other positively assigned type in the component is assigned to just one hospital. It is either in $F_h$ or has positive unmatched mass.

For every $i<k$, type $c$ is a boundary type at $h_i$. Therefore

$$ \kappa_{h_i}=M_{h_i}+p_i, $$

and no other partially assigned type can occur there.

At $h_k$, if $x_{c0}>0$, the same statements hold. If $x_{c0}=0$, there may instead be one additional type $r$ assigned partly to $h_k$ and partly to $0$. This $r$ is the boundary type.

At every hospital, report:

  • all types in $F_h$, in true priority order;
  • its component’s shared type, if positively assigned there;
  • its additional type with positive unmatched mass, if one exists.

Omit all other types. Components without a shared type consist of one hospital and its singly assigned types, so the same construction applies.

The reports induce $x$

The total supply of $F_h$ is $M_h\le\kappa_h$, and these types precede all other listed types. Hospital $h$ therefore never rejects them. Each is listed only at its assigned hospital, so all its mass eventually reaches that hospital and is accepted.

A shared type $c$ consequently passes through its supported hospitals in the order $h_1,\ldots,h_k$. Omitted hospitals can delay proposals but retain no mass.

For $i<k$, after the full types are assigned, the capacity available for $c$ is exactly $p_i$. Inductively, at least $p_i$ of $c$ reaches $h_i$, because

$$ \mu_c=\sum_{j=1}^kp_j+x_{c0}. $$

Thus $h_i$ retains exactly $p_i$.

If $x_{c0}>0$, the last hospital also has residual capacity exactly $p_k$. If $x_{c0}=0$, exactly $p_k$ reaches the last hospital and is accepted before any additional unmatched-boundary type.

An additional boundary type $r$ is listed only at its assigned hospital. That hospital is full in $x$, and its residual capacity after the preceding types equals $x_{rh}$. It therefore retains exactly that amount. Types assigned entirely unmatched are omitted everywhere.

Hence $\operatorname{DA}(Q)=x$.

Two bounds under an arbitrary deviation

Fix a hospital $h$, an arbitrary report $Q'_h$, and let

$$ y=\operatorname{DA}(Q'_h,Q_{-h}). $$

At every fixed hospital $g$, its full types $F_g$ remain protected: their entire supply fits its capacity and precedes all other listed types.

For later use, suppose a shared type $c$ sends total proposal mass $P$ to a fixed supported hospital $g$. Let $M'_g\le M_g$ be the total full-type mass actually reaching $g$. Since $c$ precedes any additional boundary type, its final retained mass is

$$ \min(P,\kappa_g-M'_g)\ge \min(P,x_{cg}). $$

Thus the total mass of $c$ rejected there is at most

$$ \max(P-x_{cg},0). $$

This holds for the entire DA run: the hospital retains the highest-ranked mass among its cumulative proposals, and no full type can be rejected.

Bound A: every positive-value newcomer wants $h$ under $x$.

Suppose $x_{th}=0$, $y_{th}>0$, and $u_h(t)>0$.

If $x_{t0}>0$, the unmatched mass witnesses that $t$ wants $h$, because a positive DA assignment makes $h$ student-acceptable.

If $t$ is fully assigned to one other hospital $g$, protection at $g$ prevents it from reaching a less-preferred $h$. Hence it must prefer $h$ to $g$.

The remaining case is a shared type $c$ with $x_{c0}=0$. Suppose it does not want $h$ under $x$. Then every supported hospital $h_i$ is preferred to $h$, and all these hospitals are fixed. Applying the rejection bound successively shows that proposal mass reaching $h_i$ is at most

$$ \mu_c-\sum_{j<i}p_j. $$

At $h_k$, this is at most $p_k$, and all of it is accepted. No mass reaches $h$, a contradiction.

Bound B: every incumbent that is not a boundary type satisfies $y_{th}\le x_{th}$.

For a full type, this follows from $x_{th}=\mu_t$.

The only other possibility is a shared type $c$. If it is not a boundary type at $h$, then $x_{c0}=0$ and $h$ is its least-preferred supported hospital. All preceding supported hospitals are fixed. The same successive rejection bound shows that at most $x_{ch}$ reaches $h$, proving the assertion.

A singly assigned type with positive unmatched mass is always a boundary type, so there are no further cases.

No deviation increases utility

If $h$ has slack in $x$, it has no boundary type. Bound A and true stability exclude every positive-value newcomer. Bound B prevents any incumbent’s allocation from increasing. Since incumbents have positive values and all remaining newcomers have negative values,

$$ U_h(y)\le U_h(x). $$

Now suppose $h$ is full.

If it has a boundary type $b$, put $v=u_h(b)>0$ and let $I$ be its other incumbents. Otherwise, let $I$ be all incumbents and put

$$ v=\min_{t\in I}u_h(t)>0. $$

The latter minimum exists because $\kappa_h>0$ and $h$ is full.

In either case, every $t\in I$ has $u_h(t)\ge v$ and satisfies $y_{th}\le x_{th}$ by Bound B. Every positive-value newcomer has value strictly below every incumbent: by Bound A it wants $h$, and otherwise it would block $x$. Negative-value newcomers also have value below $v$. A boundary incumbent, when present, has value exactly $v$.

Capacity feasibility therefore gives

$$ U_h(y) \le \sum_{t\in I}u_h(t)y_{th} + v\left(\kappa_h-\sum_{t\in I}y_{th}\right). $$

Full occupancy of $x$ gives equality in the corresponding expression:

$$ U_h(x) = \sum_{t\in I}u_h(t)x_{th} + v\left(\kappa_h-\sum_{t\in I}x_{th}\right). $$

Subtracting,

$$ U_h(y)-U_h(x) \le \sum_{t\in I}(u_h(t)-v)(y_{th}-x_{th}) \le 0. $$

The deviation was arbitrary. Thus $Q$ is a pure Nash equilibrium.

The component condition, the relevant type classes, and the reports are computable by graph traversal, rational comparisons, and sorting in polynomial time. $\square$

4. Complete characterization for two hospitals

Corollary 6

If $m=2$ and $x$ is promised true-stable, then

$$ x\text{ is induced by a pure Nash equilibrium} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G(x)\text{ is a forest}. $$

Consequently, the two-hospital problem is polynomial-time solvable.

Proof. Necessity follows from Lemma 2.

For sufficiency, if two distinct types were shared between the two hospitals, their four positive hospital-assignment edges would form a cycle. Hence a forest $G(x)$ allows at most one shared type. In particular, every component of $G_H(x)$ satisfies Theorem 5, which constructs a supporting equilibrium. $\square$

This result permits unequal masses, split assignments, unmatched mass, and arbitrary binary-rational capacities.

5. Why the remaining case is genuinely strategic

The sufficient condition does not cover a component with two shared types. Such a component can already fail with three hospitals.

Let $\mu_t=\mu_s=1/2$, with capacities

$$ \kappa_a=\kappa_c=\frac14,\qquad \kappa_b=\frac12. $$

Students rank

$$ t:\ a\succ b\succ c\succ0,\qquad s:\ c\succ b\succ a\succ0. $$

Use true values:

| Hospital | $u_h(t)$ | $u_h(s)$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| $a$ | $1$ | $2$ |
| $b$ | $2$ | $1$ |
| $c$ | $2$ | $1$ |

The target has

$$ x_{ta}=x_{tb}=x_{sb}=x_{sc}=\frac14, $$

with all other assignments zero. Its support is the path $a-t-b-s-c$.

It is true-stable. All hospitals are full, and the only desired improvements are $t$ from $b$ to $a$ and $s$ from $b$ to $c$. Each desired hospital holds only that same type.

Reports $(t)$ at $a$, $(t,s)$ at $b$, and $(s)$ at $c$ induce the target. Nevertheless, no inducing profile is an equilibrium.

Every inducing profile must list $t$ at $a$, both types at $b$, and $s$ at $c$.

  • If $b$ reports $t$ above $s$, hospital $a$ deviates to $(s)$. All $1/2$ of $t$ then reaches and remains at $b$. Hospital $c$ retains $1/4$ of $s$, and the remaining $1/4$ passes through $b$ to $a$. Hospital $a$’s utility increases from $1/4$ to $1/2$.
  • If $b$ reports $s$ above $t$, hospital $c$ deviates to $(t)$. Hospital $b$ retains all $s$, hospital $a$ retains $1/4$ of $t$, and the remaining $1/4$ reaches $c$. Its utility likewise increases from $1/4$ to $1/2$.

Additional entries at the fixed outer hospital do not affect these deviations: the other type is retained entirely at $b$.

Thus forest support alone cannot settle equilibrium attainability once three hospitals and two shared types in one component are allowed.

6. Remaining open question

The general problem now has polynomial-time outcome evaluation and a $\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}$ upper bound. Theorem 5 supplies a supporting equilibrium for a substantial class of split flows and completely resolves the two-hospital case.

For general inputs with at least three hospitals, I have neither a polynomial-time decision algorithm nor an $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness reduction. In particular, deciding equilibrium attainability for stable forest-supported flows whose hospital-assignment components contain multiple shared types remains open here.
