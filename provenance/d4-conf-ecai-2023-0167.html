The continuous mirror question, as graded

Input a finite set of exposure types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), a symmetric rational contact matrix \(A\), thresholds \(\theta_t\), initial black, white, and gray masses \(z_t^b,z_t^w,z_t^g\) summing to \(\mu_t\), per-turn mass capacities \(r_b,r_w\), and target \(\alpha\). Assume every type has positive total exposure. Type \(t\) is eligible for opinion \(p\in\{b,w\}\) when \(\sum_u A_{tu}z_u^p\ge\theta_t\sum_u A_{tu}\mu_u\). On each turn, a polynomial-size rational-circuit policy chooses transfers from currently eligible gray types, bounded by their available gray mass and totaling the lesser of the player's capacity and the total eligible gray mass; apply the transfers, then alternate turns starting with black until no player can move. Decide whether there exists a black policy such that, for every white policy, terminal black mass is at least \(\alpha\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) exposure types \(T\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). The support size counts listed types, not all possible types. Let \(m=\tau\) be the contact-matrix dimension and \(L\) the total input bit length.
Input a symmetric nonnegative rational matrix \(A\), rational thresholds \(\theta_t\in[0,1]\), nonnegative rational initial masses \(z_t^b,z_t^w,z_t^g\) with \(z_t^b+z_t^w+z_t^g=\mu_t\), positive rational capacities \(r_b,r_w\), rational target \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), and a unary policy-description bound \(s\). Require \(\sum_u A_{tu}\mu_u>0\) for every \(t\); diagonal contacts are allowed.
At state \(z\), let \(E_p(z)=\{t\in T:z_t^g>0,\ \sum_u A_{tu}z_u^p\ge\theta_t\sum_u A_{tu}\mu_u\}\), for \(p\in\{b,w\}\).
Players alternate turns, starting with black. A turn starts with remaining capacity \(c=r_p\). The player's policy outputs rational transfers \(x_t\), with \(0\le x_t\le z_t^g\), \(x_t=0\) outside \(E_p(z)\), and \(\sum_t x_t=\min\{c,\sum_{t\in E_p(z)}z_t^g\}\). Simultaneously set \(z_t^p\leftarrow z_t^p+x_t\), \(z_t^g\leftarrow z_t^g-x_t\), and \(c\leftarrow c-\sum_t x_t\).
Recompute eligibility and repeat within that turn until capacity is exhausted or no eligible gray mass remains. Empty turns pass; play ends when neither player has eligible gray mass. Adopted opinions never change.
A policy is a deterministic circuit depending only on the current masses and remaining turn capacity, with total binary description length at most \(s\). Gates perform exact rational arithmetic, comparisons, and conditional selection; division must be defined, and outputs must satisfy the transfer constraints at every admissible rational state. Both players have the same bound.
Is deciding whether some black policy guarantees terminal black mass \(\sum_t z_t^b\ge\alpha\) against every white policy solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,s\) and any numerical capacity dependence.
The discrete cautious opinion-maximization problem with compact, configuration-dependent strategies is \(\Sigma_2^p\)-complete, hence NP-hard ([source](https://doi.org/10.3233/FAIA230248)). No complexity result for this precise mass game was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative contacts and initial masses, thresholds and target in the closed unit interval, and strictly positive capacities, following the discrete contact, threshold, coverage, and diffusion-speed meanings.
  • Allowed diagonal contact entries to represent exposure between distinct individuals of the same type; positive total exposure remains required.
  • Restored reevaluation within a turn: newly eligible mass can be transferred before the player's remaining capacity expires, matching the discrete definition's iterative moves.
  • Specified simultaneous application within each transfer batch, passing on empty turns, and termination only when both players lack eligible gray mass.
  • Made the unspecified polynomial policy-size restriction explicit through a unary description bound s, so the policy-size bound is polynomial in the encoded input length.
  • Specified exact rational arithmetic, comparison and conditional gates, rational constants counted in description length, and validity on every admissible rational state.
  • Policies use the current masses and remaining capacity, without additional history; the remaining capacity records the progress of the current turn.
  • Used m equal to tau for the contact-matrix dimension because the question has no separate candidate or alternative dimension.

Proven result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\), even when:

  • \(A\) is symmetric with entries in \(\{0,1\}\);
  • every threshold belongs to \(\{0,1\}\);
  • all numerical inputs are dyadic rationals with numerators and denominators bounded by \(2m\);
  • \(r_w=1\) and \(1/r_b<2m\);
  • the common policy bound is \(s=O(\tau\log(\tau+2))\).

Consequently, a decision algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,s,1/r_b,1/r_w\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). The proof explicitly constructs policies valid at every admissible rational state.

1. Reduction from CLIQUE

We use the classical \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete CLIQUE problem: given a simple undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\) and an integer \(k\), decide whether \(G\) contains a clique of size \(k\). See [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Write \(n=|V|\), \(\ell=|E|\), and \(q=\binom{k}{2}\). We may restrict to instances satisfying

\(\displaystyle 2\le k\le n,\qquad \ell\ge q.\)

Indeed, the excluded cases are immediately decidable. An excluded NO instance can be replaced by the four-vertex star with \(k=3\), which satisfies these restrictions and contains no such clique; an excluded YES instance can be replaced by a triangle with \(k=3\). Thus this restriction preserves hardness.

Create the following listed types:

  • one anchor type \(a\);
  • one vertex type \(v\) for each \(v\in V\);
  • one edge type \(e_{uv}\) for each \(\{u,v\}\in E\).

Set

\(\displaystyle m=\tau=n+\ell+1,\qquad h=\lceil\log_2m\rceil,\qquad \delta=2^{-h}.\)

Every vertex and edge type has mass \(\delta\). The anchor has mass

\(\displaystyle \mu_a=1-(n+\ell)\delta=1-(m-1)\delta.\)

Since \(2^h\ge m\), we have \(\mu_a\ge\delta>0\). These masses sum to one.

Initially, the anchor is entirely black; every other type is entirely gray. All initial white masses are zero.

Define the symmetric contact matrix by

\(\displaystyle A_{aa}=1,\qquad A_{vv}=1\quad(v\in V),\)

and, for each edge \(\{u,v\}\in E\),

\(\displaystyle A_{e_{uv},a}=A_{a,e_{uv}}=A_{e_{uv},u}=A_{u,e_{uv}}=A_{e_{uv},v}=A_{v,e_{uv}}=1.\)

All other entries are zero. Set

\(\displaystyle \theta_a=\theta_v=0,\qquad \theta_{e_{uv}}=1.\)

Every contact row has positive mass: the anchor and vertex rows have positive diagonal contributions, while every edge row has total contact mass \(\mu_a+2\delta>0\).

Finally, set

\(\displaystyle r_b=k\delta,\qquad r_w=1,\qquad \alpha=\mu_a+(k+q)\delta.\)

The target is admissible because

\(\displaystyle 1-\alpha=(n-k+\ell-q)\delta\ge0.\)

The policy bound will be fixed in Section 3.

2. Exact behavior of the constructed game

Edge eligibility

The anchor stays entirely black throughout play because adopted opinions never change.

For an edge type \(e_{uv}\), its black exposure is therefore

\(\displaystyle \sum_t A_{e_{uv},t}z_t^b=\mu_a+z_u^b+z_v^b.\)

Its threshold is \(\mu_a+2\delta\). Since \(z_u^b,z_v^b\le\delta\), a gray edge type is black-eligible exactly when

\(\displaystyle z_u^b=z_v^b=\delta.\)

Thus both endpoint types must be entirely black.

Conversely, its white exposure always satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_t A_{e_{uv},t}z_t^w=z_u^w+z_v^w\le2\delta<\mu_a+2\delta.\)

No edge type is ever white-eligible.

Every vertex type with positive gray mass is eligible for both players because its threshold is zero.

Black’s first turn

Initially, black’s eligible types are exactly the vertex types. Their total gray mass is \(n\delta\). Since \(k\le n\), the first transfer must satisfy

\(\displaystyle 0\le x_v\le\delta,\qquad \sum_{v\in V}x_v=k\delta.\)

This transfer exhausts black’s capacity, so black cannot adopt newly eligible edge masses during the same turn.

Let

\(\displaystyle S=\{v\in V:x_v=\delta\}.\)

Because each vertex in \(S\) receives mass \(\delta\), we have \(|S|\le k\). This conclusion allows arbitrary fractional transfers to the other vertices.

White’s first turn

At white’s first turn, the eligible gray types are exactly the remaining gray vertex types. Their total mass is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{v\in V}(\delta-x_v)=(n-k)\delta<1.\)

White’s capacity is one. The transfer rule therefore forces white to adopt all this mass. Componentwise, this follows because each transfer is at most its vertex’s gray mass and their sum equals the sum of those gray masses.

Afterward, every vertex has zero gray mass, and its black mass remains \(x_v\). White has no eligible gray type for the rest of play.

The black-eligible edge types are now exactly those whose two endpoints belong to \(S\). Their eligibility cannot change: their contact rows involve only the fixed anchor and the now fixed vertex masses.

Let \(e_G(S)\) denote the number of edges induced by \(S\). Black must eventually adopt all \(e_G(S)\delta\) eligible edge mass. Each subsequent black turn adopts the smaller of its capacity and the remaining eligible mass, so this requires at most

\(\displaystyle \left\lceil\frac{e_G(S)}{k}\right\rceil\)

additional black turns. All other edge types remain gray.

Consequently, for every pair of valid policies, the terminal black mass is exactly

\(\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{term}}=\mu_a+k\delta+e_G(S)\delta.\)

In particular, fractional first-turn transfers cannot compensate for missing induced edges: the first-turn black increment is always \(k\delta\).

3. Globally valid policies within the description bound

We now verify the policy requirement at every admissible rational state, rather than only along the constructed play.

For either player \(p\), fix any ordering \(t_1,\ldots,t_m\) of the types. Define the available mass

\(\displaystyle a_t^p(z)= \begin{cases} z_t^g,&t\in E_p(z),\\ 0,&t\notin E_p(z). \end{cases}\)

For our instances, this can be computed with a constant number of gates per type:

  • for the anchor and vertex types, \(a_t^p=z_t^g\);
  • for an edge type \(e_{uv}\), select \(z_{e_{uv}}^g\) if

\(\displaystyle z_a^p+z_u^p+z_v^p\ge\mu_a+2\delta\),
and select zero otherwise.

These formulas use the *current* anchor mass. Hence they remain correct even at admissible states other than those reachable from our initial state.

Starting with \(d_0=c\), let

\(\displaystyle x_{t_j}=\min\{a_{t_j}^p,d_{j-1}\},\qquad d_j=d_{j-1}-x_{t_j}\quad(1\le j\le m).\)

Each minimum uses a comparison and conditional selection. No division occurs.

For every admissible state and \(c\ge0\), induction gives

\(\displaystyle d_j=\max\left\{c-\sum_{i=1}^j a_{t_i}^p,0\right\}.\)

Therefore the outputs are nonnegative, bounded by the corresponding gray masses, and zero outside eligibility. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \sum_t x_t=c-d_m =\min\left\{c,\sum_{t\in E_p(z)}z_t^g\right\}.\)

Thus these are globally valid policies depending only on the current masses and remaining capacity.

They use \(O(m)\) gates. Under a standard binary encoding of indexed gates, their descriptions have length \(O(m\log(m+2))\): wire indices require \(O(\log m)\) bits, and the rational constants require \(O(\log m)\) bits.

Choose, uniformly for the reduction,

\(\displaystyle s=Cm\lceil\log_2(m+2)\rceil,\)

where the fixed constant \(C\) covers this encoding. This bound accommodates every priority ordering for both players. In particular, valid white policies exist within the bound.

4. Correctness of the reduction

If \(G\) contains a clique of size \(k\): black uses the valid priority policy that places those \(k\) vertex types first. Its first transfer adopts each of them completely. Thus \(S\) is that clique, and against every valid white policy,

\(\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{term}}=\mu_a+k\delta+\binom{k}{2}\delta=\alpha.\)

If a black policy guarantees the target: take any valid white policy within the bound. The outcome formula implies

\(\displaystyle e_G(S)\ge q=\binom{k}{2}.\)

But \(|S|\le k\), and simplicity of the graph gives

\(\displaystyle e_G(S)\le\binom{|S|}{2}\le\binom{k}{2}.\)

Since \(k\ge2\), equality forces \(|S|=k\) and every pair in \(S\) to be an edge. Hence \(S\) is a clique of size \(k\).

This proves the required equivalence and therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

5. Encoding size and capacity dependence

The reduction uses exactly \(m=\tau=n+\ell+1=O(n^2)\) listed types. With the matrix represented explicitly,

\(\displaystyle s=O(\tau\log(\tau+2)),\qquad L=O(m^2+\tau\log(\tau+2)+s)=O(m^2).\)

The instance can be constructed in \(O(m^2+\tau\log(\tau+2)+s)\) bit operations under standard encodings, hence in \(O(n^4)\) time.

Every numerical input has numerator and denominator bounded by \(2m\), since \(2^h<2m\). Also,

\(\displaystyle \frac1{r_b}=\frac{2^h}{k}<2m,\qquad \frac1{r_w}=1.\)

Thus allowing polynomial numerical dependence on inverse capacities does not yield a polynomial decision algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

6. General termination and simulation bounds

These bounds concern execution of given valid policies, not the decision problem.

Let

\(\displaystyle G_0=\sum_t z_t^g,\qquad r_{\min}=\min\{r_b,r_w\}.\)

Every positive transfer batch has one of two properties:

  • It exhausts the remaining capacity. The completed turn has then transferred exactly \(r_p\). There are at most \(\lfloor G_0/r_{\min}\rfloor\) such turns because the total mass transferred during play is at most \(G_0\).
  • It leaves positive remaining capacity. The rule then forces adoption of all currently eligible gray mass, permanently exhausting at least one gray type. There are at most \(\tau\) such batches.

Therefore the number of positive transfer batches is at most

\(\displaystyle \tau+\left\lfloor\frac{G_0}{r_{\min}}\right\rfloor.\)

Two consecutive empty turns imply termination: neither changes the state, so neither player has eligible gray mass. Consequently, the number of turns is also \(O(\tau+1+G_0/r_{\min})\).

Set

\(\displaystyle H=1+\tau+\left\lfloor\frac{G_0}{r_{\min}}\right\rfloor.\)

Computing eligibility directly costs \(O(m\tau)\) rational operations per state, while evaluating a policy costs \(O(s)\) gates. Simulation therefore uses

\(\displaystyle O\bigl((m\tau+s)H\bigr)\)

rational arithmetic and comparison operations.

This is not automatically a polynomial bit-time bound. For ordinary binary arithmetic gates \(+,-,\times,\div\), one circuit evaluation can increase rational bit lengths by a factor \(2^{O(s)}\). Summing the \(\tau\) transfers adds a further factor \(O(\tau+1)\). Starting from input bit lengths at most \(L\), iteration over at most \(H\) updates therefore gives the conservative exact simulation bound

\(\displaystyle 2^{O(H(s+\log(\tau+1)))}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,s,H). \)

The proof establishes the requested hardness classification. It does not assert membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) or \(\Sigma_2^p\), nor require an algorithm for checking global validity of arbitrary policy circuits.
