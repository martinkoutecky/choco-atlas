The continuous mirror question, as graded

For fixed \(J\), rational per-capita supply \(q\), valuation domain \(\mathcal V\), and support cap \(\tau\), determine the largest \(\alpha\) achievable by one family of deterministic mechanisms \((M_N)\). Each \(M_N\) receives \(N\) reports, allocates integral bundles subject to \(Nq_j\) copies of each item \(j\), and is dominant-strategy truthful on every profile in \(\mathcal V^N\). For every rational society \(\mu\) with support at most \(\tau\) and positive benchmark, require \(\liminf_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{Rev}(M_N;\mu)/N \ge \alpha\,\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)\), along admissible \(N\) for which all \(N\mu_t\) and \(Nq_j\) are integers. Here \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)\) is the maximum of \(\sum_{t,S}y_{t,S}v_t(S)\) over \(y_{t,S}\ge0\), with \(\sum_S y_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}y_{t,S}\le q_j\).

Partial solution: exactly when ratio $1$ is possible

The exact numerical supremum remains unresolved in some parameter regimes. However, the arguments below give a new exact characterization of all regimes in which $\alpha^*=1$.

Write
$$ T=\lfloor\bar\tau\rfloor,\qquad Q=\sum_{j\in J}q_j,\qquad q_{\min}=\min_{j\in J}q_j. $$
For $0<Q<1$, define
$$ G(Q)= \begin{cases} 0,&Q\le \frac12,\\[2mm] Q-\frac12,&\frac12<Q\le\frac34,\\[2mm] \bigl(1-\sqrt{1-Q}\bigr)^2,&\frac34<Q<1. \end{cases} $$

The following statements are proved here.

| Support cap | Exact characterization of $\alpha^*=1$ |
|---|---|
| $T=1$ | Always |
| $T=2$ | Exactly when either every $q_j\ge1$, or $Q<1$ and $q_{\min}\ge G(Q)$ |
| $T\ge3$ | Exactly when every $q_j\ge1$ |

In particular, for two equally supplied items,
$$ T=2,\qquad q=(c,c),\qquad 0<c<1, $$
we obtain
$$ \boxed{\alpha^*(J,q,\bar\tau)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c\le\frac49.} $$

Thus, for example, supplies $(1/10,1/10)$ admit asymptotically optimal revenue when the support cap is two, but do not when the support cap is three.

We also prove a strengthened general upper bound. Put
$$ c_j=\min\{1,q_j\},\qquad C=\min\left\{1,\sum_j c_j\right\}. $$
For each scarce item, meaning $c_j<1$, let
$$ B_j=\max\{C-c_j,\sqrt{c_j}\}. $$
Whenever $T\ge2$,
$$ \boxed{ \alpha^*(J,q,\bar\tau) \le \min\left\{ 1,\; \min_{j:c_j<1} \frac{B_j+c_j/B_j-c_j}{C} \right\}. } $$
The inner minimum is omitted if there are no scarce items.

For completeness, we also establish the general lower bound from the notes:
$$ \boxed{ \alpha^*(J,q,\bar\tau) \ge \max\left\{ \min_j c_j,\; \frac1{\min\{m,\,1+|\{j:q_j<1\}|\}} \right\}. } $$

All mechanisms constructed below are deterministic, individually rational, and truthful on the entire report domain, including profiles exceeding the support cap.

1. Benchmark, integrality, and the universal ceiling

For an admissible replica size $N$, let $W_N$ be the maximum total value of a feasible integral allocation to the society’s replica profile. Set
$$ V_{\max}=\max_t v_t(J). $$
Then
$$ N\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)-(\tau+m)V_{\max} \le W_N \le N\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q). $$

For the upper bound, aggregate any integral allocation by type and bundle and divide the counts by $N$. This produces a feasible benchmark solution.

For the lower bound, choose an optimal extreme point $y$ of the benchmark polytope. This polytope is nonempty, because everyone can receive the empty bundle, and compact, because
$$ 0\le y_{t,S}\le\mu_t. $$
An extreme point has at most $\tau+m$ positive coordinates. Indeed, if it had more, there would be a nonzero direction supported on those coordinates that preserves the $\tau$ type equalities and every tight resource inequality. Sufficiently small movements in either direction would remain feasible, contradicting extremality.

Allocate $\lfloor Ny_{t,S}\rfloor$ copies of every nonempty bundle $S$ to type $t$. Assign all remaining bidders the empty bundle. Rounding down preserves resource constraints and type populations. Each positive coordinate loses less than $V_{\max}$ in total value, proving the claim.

Consequently,
$$ \frac{W_N}{N}\longrightarrow\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q). $$
There are infinitely many admissible $N$, because all relevant masses and supplies are rational.

Individual rationality gives
$$ p_i\le v_i(S_i). $$
Therefore every allowed mechanism satisfies
$$ \frac{\operatorname{Rev}(M_N;\mu)}N \le\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q), $$
and hence $\alpha^*\le1$.

For computational statements, I use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly given, nonempty bounded rational polytope admits deterministic linear optimization in polynomial bit time. An optimal extreme point can be selected deterministically by successively optimizing coordinates on the optimal face. Its encoding length is polynomial in the input length, by the determinant bounds for basic solutions.

The benchmark has $\tau2^m$ variables. Its bit running time is therefore
$$ \operatorname{poly}(\tau2^m,m,L), $$
and also $\operatorname{poly}(\bar\tau2^m,m,L)$.

2. Two elementary exact cases

2.1. Every item has supply at least one

If every $q_j\ge1$, give every bidder $J$ and charge its reported value of $J$.

The allocation is independent of that bidder’s report and is feasible. A truthful bidder obtains utility $v_i(J)$. Any deviation either yields the same value affordably or produces an unaffordable payment. Thus the mechanism is truthful and individually rational.

Free disposal gives
$$ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)=\sum_t\mu_t v_t(J). $$
The mechanism extracts this value exactly, so $\alpha^*=1$.

2.2. Support cap one

For every valuation $u$, construct a deterministic schedule
$$ B_1(u),\ldots,B_N(u) $$
for $N$ identical bidders by solving the homogeneous benchmark and rounding as in Section 1. It satisfies
$$ \sum_i u(B_i(u)) \ge N\operatorname{OPT}(u,q)-(m+1)u(J). $$

For $N\ge3$, define a mechanism on all profiles as follows. Bidder $i$ receives $B_i(u)$ if all other reports equal $u$; otherwise it receives the empty bundle. Charge the bidder its reported value of its assigned bundle.

Each bidder’s bundle depends only on the other reports, so the mechanism is truthful.

At a unanimous profile, the allocation is the feasible schedule. At a nonunanimous profile, at most one bidder passes the test. To see this, suppose distinct bidders $i,h$ pass, with respective common other-report valuations $u,w$. A third bidder’s report gives $u=w$. The tests then force every report to equal $u$, a contradiction. A single scheduled bundle is feasible.

For smaller $N$, assign everyone the empty bundle. At a fixed homogeneous society, the revenue loss divided by $N$ tends to zero. Thus $\alpha^*=1$ for $T=1$.

With total report bit length $R$, this mechanism can be implemented in
$$ \operatorname{poly}(N,2^m,m,L,R) $$
bit time.

3. A counting upper bound without anonymity

We first prove an upper-bound lemma that allows a third, zero-valued type.

Fix an item $j$ with
$$ c=q_j<1, \qquad D=\sum_{k\ne j}\min\{1,q_k\}. $$
For rational $\varepsilon>0$, define two valuations:

  • Type $a$ has value $1$ for bundles containing $j$, and value $0$ otherwise.
  • Type $b$ has value $1+\varepsilon$ for bundles containing $j$, value $1$ for nonempty bundles not containing $j$, and value $0$ for the empty bundle.

An optional third type has value identically zero. These valuations are normalized and satisfy free disposal.

Let the masses of $a,b$ be $r,b>0$, with $r+b\le1$; if the inequality is strict, give the remaining mass to the zero type.

For every truthful mechanism family and this fixed society,
$$ \limsup_{N\to\infty} \frac{\operatorname{Rev}(M_N;\mu)}N \le b+\frac{cr}{b}+\varepsilon c. $$

Proof. At an admissible $N$, write $n_a=Nr$ and $n_b=Nb$. Let $\mathcal P_N$ contain all labeled profiles with the prescribed counts. Let $\mathcal H_N$ contain profiles obtained by reducing the type-$a$ count by one and increasing the type-$b$ count by one.

For $P\in\mathcal P_N$, let $A(P)$ count its type-$a$ bidders receiving item $j$.

If bidder $i$ is counted in $A(P)$, change only its report from $a$ to $b$, producing $H\in\mathcal H_N$. At $H$, a true type-$b$ bidder can report $a$ and obtain its outcome at $P$. That outcome contains $j$, and individual rationality at $P$ bounds its payment by $1$. The deviation is therefore affordable and gives utility $1+\varepsilon$.

Truthfulness forces bidder $i$ to receive a bundle containing $j$ at $H$. Each $H$ can have at most $Nc$ such recipients. The map from predecessor pairs $(P,i)$ to $(H,i)$ is injective. Consequently,
$$ \sum_{P\in\mathcal P_N}A(P) \le Nc\,|\mathcal H_N|. $$
The multinomial counts give
$$ \frac{|\mathcal H_N|}{|\mathcal P_N|} = \frac{n_a}{n_b+1}. $$
Thus some ordering $P$ satisfies
$$ A(P)\le Nc\,\frac{n_a}{n_b+1}. $$

At this ordering, type-$a$ payments total at most $A(P)$. Type-$b$ payments total at most
$$ n_b+\varepsilon Nc, $$
and zero-type payments vanish. Therefore
$$ \frac{\operatorname{Rev}(M_N;\mu)}N \le b+c\frac{Nr}{Nb+1}+\varepsilon c. $$
Taking the upper limit proves the lemma. $\square$

The neighboring profiles need not be replicas of the original society. The argument is valid because truthfulness is required on every profile.

3.1. Strengthened two-type upper bound

Take $r=1-b$ and
$$ C=\min\{1,c+D\}. $$
If
$$ b\ge C-c, $$
then the benchmark is at least $C$.

To verify this, assign item $j$ to mass $\min\{r,c\}$ of type $a$, and assign its remaining mass $c-\min\{r,c\}$ to type $b$. Assign singleton bundles from the other items to total type-$b$ mass $C-c$. Such a distribution exists because $C-c\le D$.

The total type-$b$ mass used is
$$ C-\min\{r,c\}\le b: $$
if $r\le c$, this follows from $C\le1$; if $r>c$, it is the imposed condition $b\ge C-c$. The allocation is feasible and has value at least $C$.

If a family achieves ratio $\alpha$, the counting lemma therefore gives
$$ \alpha C \le b+\frac{c(1-b)}b+\varepsilon c. $$
The family must serve every positive rational $\varepsilon$, so
$$ \alpha \le \frac{b+c/b-c}{C}. $$

The function
$$ h_c(b)=b+\frac cb-c $$
has derivative $1-c/b^2$. Its minimum over $C-c\le b<1$ is attained, or approached, at
$$ B=\max\{C-c,\sqrt c\}. $$
Rational masses are dense, and dyadic masses are also dense if that is the intended meaning of “binary-rational.” Thus
$$ \alpha^*\le\frac{B+c/B-c}{C}. $$
Applying this to every scarce item proves the upper bound stated at the beginning.

3.2. A third type rules out ratio one whenever an item is scarce

Suppose $T\ge3$ and $c=q_j<1$. Since $m\ge2$ and every supply is positive,
$$ \min\{1,c+D\}>c. $$
Choose admissible rational masses through numbers satisfying
$$ c<\rho<\min\{1,c+D\}, \qquad \max\{c,\rho-c\}<b<\rho, $$
and set
$$ r=\rho-b,\qquad z=1-\rho. $$
All three masses are positive, $r<c$, and $b>c$.

A benchmark allocation of value at least $\rho$ assigns:

  • item $j$ to type-$a$ mass $r$;
  • item $j$ to type-$b$ mass $c-r$;
  • other singleton items to type-$b$ mass $\rho-c$.

The total type-$b$ mass used is $b$, and the other-item demand is feasible because $\rho-c<D$.

The counting lemma and the limit $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ yield
$$ \alpha^* \le \frac{b+c(\rho-b)/b}{\rho}. $$
But
$$ b+\frac{c(\rho-b)}b = \rho-(\rho-b)\left(1-\frac cb\right) <\rho. $$
Therefore $\alpha^*<1$.

Together with Section 2.1, this proves the claimed characterization for $T\ge3$.

For example, with two items of supply $c<1/2$, let $\rho$ approach $2c$ from below and $b$ approach $\sqrt2\,c$. The conditions above remain satisfied, and the resulting bound approaches
$$ \boxed{\alpha^*\le\sqrt2-\frac12\qquad(T\ge3,\ q=(c,c),\ 0<c<1/2).} $$

4. A rounding lemma

The new positive result uses the following finite rounding lemma.

Lemma. Let variables $x_e\in[0,1]$ be partitioned into groups, with
$$ \sum_{e\in H}x_e=1 $$
for every group $H$. Consider additional linear sums with coefficients in $\{0,1\}$. Suppose each variable occurs in at most $d$ additional sums.

Then the variables can be rounded to $\widehat x_e\in\{0,1\}$ so that:

  • every group still sums to $1$;
  • initially integral variables remain unchanged;
  • every additional sum changes by at most $2d$.

Proof. Maintain the group equalities exactly. Also maintain some additional equalities exactly, initially all of them.

Whenever an additional equality contains at most $2d$ currently fractional variables, stop maintaining it. If $F$ fractional variables remain, each group containing fractional variables contains at least two: otherwise its sum could not be the integer $1$. Hence there are at most $F/2$ relevant group equalities.

Every maintained additional equality now contains more than $2d$ fractional variables. Since the total number of incidences with additional equalities is at most $dF$, there are fewer than $F/2$ maintained additional equalities.

Thus fewer than $F$ equalities constrain the $F$ fractional variables. A nonzero direction preserves all these equalities. Move along it until a variable reaches $0$ or $1$. This preserves the box constraints and decreases the number of fractional variables.

After finitely many steps, all variables are integral. When an additional equality was dropped, at most $2d$ of its variables could still change. Each changes by at most $1$, so its final error is at most $2d$. $\square$

All choices can be made deterministically using fixed coordinate orders and rational linear algebra.

5. Asymptotically optimal revenue with two types

We now prove the substantial new positive result.

Theorem. Suppose
$$ T=2,\qquad Q<1,\qquad q_{\min}\ge G(Q). $$
Then a single deterministic truthful family achieves ratio $1$ for every permitted society.

5.1. The meaning of $G$

For $b\ge1/2$, define
$$ g_Q(b)=\frac{b}{1-b}(Q-b)_+, $$
where $(x)_+=\max\{x,0\}$. Then
$$ G(Q)=\sup_{1/2\le b<1}g_Q(b). $$

If $Q\le1/2$, the expression vanishes. Otherwise, on $1/2\le b<Q$,
$$ \frac{d}{db}\frac{b(Q-b)}{1-b} = \frac{Q-2b+b^2}{(1-b)^2}. $$
The relevant critical point is
$$ b_*=1-\sqrt{1-Q}. $$
If $Q\le3/4$, then $b_*\le1/2$, so the maximum is at $b=1/2$, with value $Q-1/2$. If $Q>3/4$, the maximum is at $b_*$; using $Q=2b_*-b_*^2$, its value is $b_*^2$. This proves the displayed formula for $G$.

5.2. Selecting separated societies

Fix a canonical binary encoding of the allowed societies. Equal valuation tables are identified by exact rational equality. If masses must be dyadic, enumerate only dyadic encodings.

For each sufficiently large $N$, set
$$ \ell_N=\left\lfloor\frac14\log_2N\right\rfloor. $$
Call a society selected at $N$ if its canonical encoding has length at most $\ell_N$, its support has at most two types, and its replica counts at $N$ are integral.

There are finitely many selected societies. Distinct selected societies have replica histograms at distance at least $3$ for sufficiently large $N$, where
$$ d(h,h')=\frac12\sum_v|h(v)-h'(v)|. $$
This distance is the minimum number of bidder reports that must change to transform one histogram into the other.

Indeed, encoded mass denominators are at most $2^{\ell_N}$. If supports differ, some mass difference is at least $2^{-\ell_N}$. If supports agree but masses differ, some difference is at least $2^{-2\ell_N}$. Since histogram differences sum to zero, half their $\ell_1$ distance is at least any individual coordinate difference. Therefore
$$ d(h,h')\ge N2^{-2\ell_N}\ge\sqrt N. $$
Also, every positive type count is at least
$$ N2^{-\ell_N}\ge N^{3/4}. $$

Every fixed permitted society is selected eventually along all its admissible replica sizes, because $\ell_N\to\infty$.

5.3. Fractional allocations and an exchange property

Let
$$ K=2(m+1). $$
Choose an integer $D$ satisfying
$$ D>K2^m+K+3+\frac{Q}{1-Q}. $$
This depends only on $J,q$.

Take $N$ large enough that every $Nq_j\ge D+1$. For each selected society, solve its benchmark with reduced supplies
$$ q'_j=q_j-\frac DN. $$
Use only the empty bundle and bundles having positive value for their assigned type; excluding zero-valued nonempty bundles cannot reduce the optimum.

Among welfare-optimal solutions, maximize the nonempty allocation mass of the more numerous type, with deterministic further tie-breaking. Denote the resulting solution by $y$.

Suppose the two types have masses
$$ a<b,\qquad a+b=1, $$
and denote them by $t,u$, respectively. A bundle $S$ assigned to $t$ is called accessible to $u$ when
$$ v_u(S)\ge v_t(S)>0. $$

If any accessible bundle has positive $y_{t,S}$, then
$$ y_{u,\varnothing}=0. $$
For otherwise, transfer a sufficiently small positive mass of $S$ from $t$ to an empty-assigned bidder of type $u$. Type masses and resource usage remain unchanged. Welfare strictly increases if $v_u(S)>v_t(S)$; if the values are equal, the secondary objective strictly increases. Both contradict the choice of $y$.

The total nonempty allocation mass is at most total item usage, hence at most
$$ Q'_N=Q-\frac{mD}{N}. $$
Therefore, if accessible bundles are used, all mass $b$ of type $u$ is assigned nonempty bundles, and the entire nonempty allocation mass of type $t$ is at most $Q'_N-b$.

In particular, for every item $j$,
$$ a_{t,j}:= \sum_{\substack{S\ni j\\v_u(S)\ge v_t(S)>0}}y_{t,S} \le (Q'_N-b)_+. $$

5.4. Rounding simultaneously across all orderings

Fix one selected histogram $h$. Consider every labeled profile $P$ with histogram $h$.

For bidder $i$ of type $t$ at $P$, introduce variables indexed by bundles:
$$ x_{P,i,S}=\frac{y_{t,S}}{\mu_t}. $$
The variables for each pair $(P,i)$ form a group summing to one.

Use two classes of additional sums.

First, for every $P,t,S$, use the bundle-count sum
$$ \sum_{i:\,P_i=t}x_{P,i,S}. $$
Its initial value is $Ny_{t,S}$.

Second, consider every labeled neighboring profile $H$ obtained by changing one report from a type $t$ in the support to the other type $u$. For each item $j$, use the sum
$$ \sum_{i:\,H_i=u} \ \sum_{\substack{S\ni j\\v_u(S)\ge v_t(S)>0}} x_{P(H,i),i,S}, $$
where $P(H,i)$ changes bidder $i$ in $H$ back to type $t$.

Each variable belongs to one bundle-count sum and at most $m$ neighboring-profile sums. Thus the rounding lemma applies with $d=m+1$, giving error at most $K$.

The rounded variables define a deterministic bundle $S_i(P)$ for every bidder at every labeled profile with histogram $h$.

Feasibility at selected profiles

The total usage of item $j$ differs from its fractional usage by at most
$$ K\tau2^{m-1}\le K2^m. $$
Consequently, it is at most
$$ Nq_j-D+K2^m\le Nq_j. $$

Feasibility of accessible bundles at neighboring profiles

Suppose $H$ changes a report from $t$ to $u$, whose original counts are $n_t,n_u$.

If $n_t\ge n_u$, then $n_t\ge N/2$. The initial neighboring sum for item $j$ is at most
$$ \frac{n_u+1}{n_t}(Nq_j-D) \le Nq_j-D+2q_j < Nq_j-D+2. $$
Its rounded value is at most $Nq_j$ by the choice of $D$.

If $n_t<n_u$, write $\mu_u=b>1/2$. The initial neighboring sum is
$$ \frac{Nb+1}{1-b}a_{t,j}. $$
If $a_{t,j}=0$, it vanishes. Otherwise the exchange property gives
$$ a_{t,j}\le Q-b-\frac{mD}{N}. $$
Also $b<Q$, so $1-b>1-Q$. Hence
$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{Nb+1}{1-b}a_{t,j} &\le N\frac{b(Q-b)}{1-b} -mD\frac{b}{1-b} +\frac{a_{t,j}}{1-b}\\ &\le NG(Q)-mD+\frac{Q}{1-Q}\\ &\le Nq_j-mD+\frac{Q}{1-Q}. \end{aligned} $$
After rounding, the sum is still at most $Nq_j$.

Thus, at every internal neighboring profile, all accessible scheduled bundles together are feasible.

5.5. Extending the schedules to a truthful mechanism on the full domain

For each bidder $i$, inspect only the other reports.

If these reports can be completed by one report to a selected histogram $h$, the histogram is unique: two such histograms would have distance at most one, contradicting their separation. The missing type $t$ is also unique. Let $P$ be the completed labeled profile.

Offer bidder $i$ the scheduled bundle $S_i(P)$ with threshold
$$ v_t(S_i(P)). $$
If this bundle is empty, make no nonempty offer. Otherwise, for bidder $i$’s actual report $w$:

  • if $w(S_i(P))\ge v_t(S_i(P))$, assign that bundle and charge $w(S_i(P))$;
  • otherwise assign the empty bundle at zero.

If the other reports admit no completion, assign the empty bundle at zero.

The offer and its threshold depend only on the other reports.

Truthfulness. Fix the other reports. There is at most one offered nonempty bundle $S$, with fixed threshold $z$.

If the true valuation satisfies $v(S)\ge z$, truthfulness obtains value $v(S)$. A deviation can only obtain $S$ or the empty bundle, and cannot improve that value.

If $v(S)<z$, truthfulness obtains the empty bundle. Any deviation obtaining $S$ is charged its reported value of $S$, which is at least $z>v(S)$, and is therefore unaffordable.

This proves dominant-strategy truthfulness. Truthful payments equal true allocated values, so individual rationality holds.

Feasibility on arbitrary profiles. If the actual profile has a selected histogram, the mechanism implements its feasible rounded schedule.

Otherwise, suppose some bidder has a nonempty offer. Every offer must correspond to the same selected histogram: two completed profiles each differ from the actual profile in at most one report, so their histograms have distance at most two.

The actual histogram therefore differs from this selected histogram by replacing one type $t$ with a valuation $w$. Only bidders reporting $w$ can have offers.

If $w$ is the other supported type, the allocations are exactly the accessible bundles bounded in Section 5.4.

If $w$ is outside the selected support, there is exactly one such bidder. It receives at most one bundle, which is feasible because every item capacity is at least one.

If no bidder has an offer, feasibility is immediate. This covers every profile in $\mathcal V^N$.

For the finitely many smaller replica sizes excluded above, assign everyone the empty bundle.

5.6. Revenue and implementation

Reducing each supply by $D/N$ loses at most
$$ \frac{mD}{N}V_{\max} $$
in benchmark value. To verify this, start from an original optimal allocation. 6. Completing the characterization of ratio one for support cap two

Sufficiency was proved in Sections 2 and 5. We prove necessity.

Suppose an item $j$ is scarce, with $c=q_j<1$.

If $Q\ge1$, then
$$ C=\min\left\{1,\sum_k\min\{1,q_k\}\right\}=1. $$
Choose
$$ \max\{c,1-c\}<b<1. $$
The two-type benchmark construction applies, and
$$ b+\frac{c(1-b)}b = 1-(1-b)\left(1-\frac cb\right)<1. $$
Thus $\alpha^*<1$.

Now suppose $Q<1$ and
$$ c=q_{\min}<G(Q). $$
Because $G(Q)$ is the maximum of $g_Q$, there exists $b\in(1/2,Q)$ with
$$ g_Q(b)>c. $$
We can also arrange $b\ge Q-c$.

Indeed, if an initial choice already satisfies this, retain it. Otherwise $Q-c>1/2$, and at $b=Q-c$,
$$ g_Q(b)=\frac{bc}{1-b}>c. $$
Continuity then permits a choice slightly above $Q-c$, still below $Q$. Choose the masses rational, or dyadic, within the resulting strict interval.

Since $b\ge Q-c$, the two-type benchmark is at least $Q$. Moreover,
$$ g_Q(b)>c \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b+\frac{c(1-b)}b<Q. $$
The counting upper bound consequently gives $\alpha^*<1$.

This proves the stated necessary and sufficient conditions for $\alpha^*=1$ when $T=2$.

Equal supplies

7. A general constructive lower bound

Define
$$ c_{\min}=\min_j\min\{1,q_j\},\qquad s=|\{j:q_j<1\}|,\qquad \kappa=\min\{m,s+1\}. $$

Fix a priority order on bidder identities. Repeatedly let $A$ be the set of items having a remaining copy. Select an unprocessed bidder maximizing its reported value of $A$, with fixed identity tie-breaking. Give it $A$, charge its reported value of $A$, and decrease the corresponding capacities by one.

This is feasible and individually rational on all profiles.

For truthfulness, compare a truthful and a deviating execution. Until the first differing selected bidder, the executions coincide.

If both select the deviator in that round, its bundle is the same. If truthfulness selects it first, any later bundle under deviation is a subset of the then-available set, so free disposal prevents an improvement. If deviation selects it first, its deviating score must exceed its true value of the available set: a weakly smaller score cannot reverse the fixed-priority comparison. Its payment is therefore unaffordable. This proves truthfulness, including ties.

During the first $Nc_{\min}$ rounds, every item is available. These rounds allocate $J$ to the bidders with the highest grand-bundle values. Thus
$$ \operatorname{Rev} \ge Nc_{\min}\sum_t\mu_t v_t(J) \ge Nc_{\min}\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q). $$

For the second guarantee, start with an optimal integral reference allocation. Maintain its undiscarded bundles for unprocessed bidders, feasible under remaining capacities.

When the greedy winner receives $A$ and pays $w$, discard its maintained reference bundle $T$, if nonempty. Decrease capacities on $A$ by one. Whenever an item constraint becomes violated, discard a maintained bundle containing that item. One discard per violated item suffices, and items in $T$ cannot require an additional discard.

Every discarded bundle lies in $A$ and belongs to a then-unprocessed bidder, so its value is at most $w$.

At most $m$ nonempty bundles are discarded: if $T$ is nonempty, the bound is
$$ 1+|A\setminus T|\le|A|\le m; $$
if $T$ is empty, it is at most $|A|$.

An item with $q_j\ge1$ never needs an additional discard. With $n$ unprocessed bidders, its remaining capacity is at least $n$; after the round it can still accommodate every remaining reference bundle. Thus at most $s$ additional discards are needed besides $T$.

Each round discards reference value at most $\kappa w$. All nonempty reference bundles are eventually discarded, giving
$$ W_N\le\kappa\operatorname{Rev}. $$
Section 1 then proves
$$ \alpha^*\ge\max\{c_{\min},1/\kappa\}. $$

The implementation uses $O(N^2)$ table lookups and rational comparisons and $O(Nm)$ capacity operations, with bit running time
$$ \operatorname{poly}(N,m,L,R), $$
independently of the support cap.
