The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a graph \(G=(V,E)\) and a finite-support report measure over edge-cost roles, consider repeated network deployments whose aggregate hired vector must lie in \(\mathcal{B}(G)/m\), where \(\mathcal{B}(G)\) is the spanning-tree base polytope. What is the minimum worst-case approximation ratio achievable by a no-money mechanism that assigns each tagged agent an inclusion probability \(q_e(d;\nu)\), is truthful under binding reports, and satisfies aggregate feasibility?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a connected, loopless undirected multigraph with \(|V|\ge 2\), \(m=|E|\), and \(r=|V|-1\). Edge roles are public and cannot be misreported; costs and cost reports belong to \(\mathbb Q_{\ge 0}\), with no metric restriction.
A nonatomic society is a finite-support probability measure \(\nu\) on edge–cost types \((e,c)\), with \(\nu(\{e\}\times\mathbb Q_{\ge0})=1/m\) for every edge. It is given by its \(\tau\) positive-mass types and their binary rational masses; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; running times are measured in \(m,\tau,L,r\).
A no-money mechanism assigns a tagged agent of role \(e\) reporting \(d\) an inclusion probability \(q_e(d;\nu)\in[0,1]\), including for reports outside the support. A single agent's deviation leaves \(\nu\) unchanged. Binding reports give expected cost \(\max\{c,d\}q_e(d;\nu)\); truthfulness requires \(c q_e(c;\nu)\le\max\{c,d\}q_e(d;\nu)\) for every \(e,c,d,\nu\).
Write \(\mathcal B(G)\) for the spanning-tree base polytope. Aggregate feasibility requires \(x_e=\sum_{c:(e,c)\in\operatorname{supp}\nu}\nu(e,c)q_e(c;\nu)\) and \(x\in\mathcal B(G)/m\) for every report measure; deployments have no additional feasibility constraints.
At truthful reports, define \(\operatorname{SC}_M(\nu)=\sum_{(e,c)}c\nu(e,c)q_e(c;\nu)\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)=\min\{\sum_{(e,c)}c y_{e,c}:0\le y_{e,c}\le\nu(e,c),\ (\sum_c y_{e,c})_{e\in E}\in\mathcal B(G)/m\}\).
Let \(A(m,r)\) be the infimum, over mechanisms satisfying these conditions on all such graphs and societies, of their worst-case approximation ratio. A ratio \(\alpha\) means \(\operatorname{SC}_M(\nu)\le\alpha\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)\) for every society, including those with zero optimum.

What is \(A(m,r)\), the optimal worst-case approximation ratio achievable by truthful no-money mechanisms in this nonatomic model?

For discrete edge agents, a polynomial-time truthful-in-expectation mechanism achieves \((m-r)/2+1\), and a graphical family rules out a better general guarantee ([Pountourakis–Schäfer](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~schaefer/ftp/pdf/SAGT14-Matroid-MD.pdf)).
No result settling exactly the nonatomic model above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified connected, loopless undirected multigraphs with at least two vertices, so spanning-tree bases exist and every edge is an eligible role; parallel edges are allowed.
  • Fixed m as the number of graph edges and r as the spanning-tree rank, independently of the society's support size.
  • Normalized total mass to one and each edge-role mass to 1/m, preserving one equally weighted role per discrete edge and ensuring the stated scaled feasibility domain is available.
  • Made edge roles public and immutable, with unrestricted nonnegative rational costs and cost reports, allowing finite binary input encodings and zero costs.
  • Interpreted tagged agents as nonatomic individuals whose deviations leave the aggregate report measure unchanged; required truthfulness for off-support reports as well.
  • Defined the benchmark using mass capacities and the same aggregate feasibility constraints, without imposing additional deployment constraints.
  • Evaluated approximation at truthful reports and used an inequality covering zero optimum; the literal comparison of binding costs under arbitrary underreports against declared-cost optimum would invalidate the discrete positive guarantees.
  • Defined the optimum guarantee as an infimum over mechanisms and a worst case over graphs and societies with fixed m and r, without presuming attainment or imposing a computational restriction.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

For every admissible pair \(1\le r\le m\), the answer is \(A(m,r)=1\) when \(m=r\), and \(A(m,r)=+\infty\) when \(m>r\).

The unbounded lower bound holds even with strictly positive rational costs and support size \(\tau=m+2\).

Tree case. Suppose \(m=r\). Connectivity implies that \(G\) is a tree, so its only spanning tree is \(E\). Aggregate feasibility therefore requires, for each edge \(e\),

\(\sum_c \nu(e,c)q_e(c;\nu)=1/m=\sum_c\nu(e,c)\).

Because every supported type has positive mass and every inclusion probability is at most \(1\), this forces \(q_e(c;\nu)=1\) at every supported type. The optimum likewise selects every supported type fully. Consequently, every feasible mechanism has social cost equal to the optimum.

The mechanism \(q_e(d;\nu)=1\) for all reports is truthful: its truthfulness inequality is \(c\le\max\{c,d\}\). Thus \(A(m,r)=1\).

A fixed graph for the unbounded lower bound. Now fix \(m>r\), and put \(k=m-r+1\), so \(k\ge2\).

Take vertices \(v_0,\ldots,v_r\), with \(k\) parallel edges between \(v_0\) and \(v_1\), and the \(r-1\) edges \(v_1v_2,\ldots,v_{r-1}v_r\). Denote the parallel-edge set by \(P\), and distinguish two of its edges, \(a\) and \(b\).

Every spanning tree contains all \(r-1\) path edges and exactly one edge of \(P\). These statements also hold for every vector in the spanning-tree base polytope, since that polytope is the convex hull of spanning-tree incidence vectors.

Choose an integer \(n\ge2\), and define \(\varepsilon=1/n\), \(H=n\), and \(\delta=1/(rn^2)=\varepsilon^2/r\). In particular, \(0<\delta<1<H\).

Define the society by the following conditional cost distributions within each role. Each listed conditional mass is multiplied by \(1/m\) to obtain its mass under \(\nu\).

| Edge role | Cost | Conditional mass |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(a\) | \(1\) | \(\varepsilon\) |
| \(a\) | \(H\) | \(1-\varepsilon\) |
| \(b\) | \(\delta\) | \(1-\varepsilon\) |
| \(b\) | \(H\) | \(\varepsilon\) |
| Each edge in \(P\setminus\{a,b\}\) | \(H\) | \(1\) |
| Each path edge | \(\delta\) | \(1\) |

All masses and costs are positive rationals. Every role has total mass \(1/m\), and the support size is \(\tau=m+2\).

The optimum. Work in units multiplied by \(m\). Each path edge must contribute inclusion mass \(1\), at cost \(\delta\), giving total cost \((r-1)\delta\).

The parallel roles must contribute total inclusion mass \(1\). Their available costs, in increasing order, are:

  • conditional mass \(1-\varepsilon\) at cost \(\delta\);
  • conditional mass \(\varepsilon\) at cost \(1\);
  • all remaining mass at cost \(H\).

Since \(\delta<1<H\), the minimum cost is obtained by taking the first two groups fully. This is feasible: their role totals are \(1-\varepsilon\) on \(b\) and \(\varepsilon\) on \(a\), a convex combination of the two corresponding spanning trees. Therefore,

\(m\,\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)=\varepsilon+(r-\varepsilon)\delta>0\).

A lower bound for every truthful mechanism. Fix any truthful feasible mechanism, and suppress \(\nu\) in its notation. Write \(u=q_a(1)\), \(v=q_a(H)\), and \(z=q_b(\delta)\). Let

\(h=(1-\varepsilon)v+\varepsilon q_b(H)+\sum_{e\in P\setminus\{a,b\}}q_e(H)\)

be the total selected conditional mass of cost-\(H\) types.

Aggregate feasibility on \(P\) gives

\(\varepsilon u+(1-\varepsilon)z+h=1\).

Since \(z\le1\), it follows that \(h\ge\varepsilon(1-u)\).

Truthfulness for a cost-\(1\) agent of role \(a\) reporting \(H\), with the society unchanged, gives \(u\le Hv\). Hence

\(Hh\ge H(1-\varepsilon)v\ge(1-\varepsilon)u\).

Set \(s=m\,\operatorname{SC}_M(\nu)\). Dropping the nonnegative contributions of cost-\(\delta\) types yields \(s\ge\varepsilon u+Hh\). The preceding two bounds imply

\(s\ge u\)

and

\(s\ge\varepsilon u+H\varepsilon(1-u)=H\varepsilon-\varepsilon(H-1)u\).

Because \(H>1\) and \(u\le s\), the second inequality further gives

\(s\ge H\varepsilon-\varepsilon(H-1)s\).

Rearranging,

\(s\ge \dfrac{H\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon(H-1)}=\dfrac{1}{2-\varepsilon}\).

This bound uses only necessary consequences of truthfulness and feasibility, so it applies to every mechanism in the question.

Divergence of the approximation ratio. Since \(\delta=\varepsilon^2/r\),

\(m\,\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)=\varepsilon+(r-\varepsilon)\delta\le\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2\).

Consequently,

\(\dfrac{\operatorname{SC}_M(\nu)}{\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)} \ge\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon)} =\dfrac{n}{(2-1/n)(1+1/n)} >\dfrac n3\).

For any finite proposed ratio \(\alpha\), choose an integer \(n>3\alpha\). The resulting society violates that guarantee. The graph and support size remain fixed as \(n\) increases.

The mechanism class is nonempty: selecting a fixed spanning tree with report-independent probabilities is truthful and feasible. Nevertheless, every mechanism has unbounded worst-case ratio when \(m>r\). Thus \(A(m,r)=+\infty\).

The [Pountourakis–Schäfer discrete guarantee](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~schaefer/ftp/pdf/SAGT14-Matroid-MD.pdf) concerns one cost-bearing agent per edge. Here, heterogeneous costs within a role let the optimum select only cheap types, while truthfulness forces inclusion of expensive types in that same role. The construction proves that this distinction prevents every finite guarantee, even with strictly positive costs and positive optimum.
