The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_t\) over finitely many opponent types, a finite score set \(S\), balanced baseline matchup profiles, and a distinguished target's baseline profile against each type, determine whether mass transfers \(x_{tu}(r,s)\) among type-pair games and \(z_t(r,s)\) in target-versus-type games obeying the paper's per-game manipulation restrictions admit a balanced realization in which the target's limiting score per opponent is at least the common per-capita score of every opponent type.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) opponent types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Each type has a coalition-membership flag, as does a distinguished target of zero mass. Let \(S\subseteq\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^2\) be a nonempty explicitly listed scoring model closed under coordinate reversal; write \(m=|S|\), \(r^*=(r_2,r_1)\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For every ordered type pair, the input gives nonnegative rational baseline game masses \(B_{tu}(r)\), satisfying \(\sum_{r\in S}B_{tu}(r)=\mu_t\mu_u\) and \(B_{ut}(r^*)=B_{tu}(r)\), including \(t=u\). Target-versus-type baseline masses \(D_t(r)\), oriented with the target first, satisfy \(\sum_rD_t(r)=\mu_t\).
A transition from baseline outcome \(r\) to outcome \(s\in S\) is allowed as follows: with neither endpoint in the coalition, only \(s=r\); with both endpoints in the coalition, any \(s\); with only the first endpoint in the coalition, \(s_1\leq r_1\) and \(s_2\geq r_2\); with only the second, reverse these inequalities. Unchanged outcomes are always allowed.
Choose nonnegative real transfer masses \(x_{tu}(r,s)\) and \(z_t(r,s)\), zero on forbidden transitions, with \(\sum_sx_{tu}(r,s)=B_{tu}(r)\), \(x_{ut}(r^*,s^*)=x_{tu}(r,s)\), and \(\sum_sz_t(r,s)=D_t(r)\). Define \(Q_{tu}(s)=\sum_rx_{tu}(r,s)\) and \(H_t(s)=\sum_rz_t(r,s)\).
A realization consists of disjoint atomless intervals \(I_t\) of lengths \(\mu_t\), measurable baseline and final outcomes for each opponent pair, reversed when its endpoints are exchanged, and measurable baseline and final outcomes for each target game. Their joint outcome masses on \(I_t\times I_u\) and \(I_t\) must respectively equal \(x_{tu}(r,s)\) and \(z_t(r,s)\); self-games on the measure-zero diagonal are omitted.
Balanced means that, for almost every opponent in \(I_t\), its baseline and final outcome masses against \(I_u\) equal \(B_{tu}(r)/\mu_t\) and \(Q_{tu}(s)/\mu_t\), respectively. Inputs are promised to admit a balanced baseline realization; its microscopic arrangement is not fixed.
The target's limiting score per opponent is \(a_0=\sum_{t,s}s_1H_t(s)\); every member of type \(t\) has limiting score \(a_t=\mu_t^{-1}\sum_{u,s}s_1Q_{tu}(s)\). The single target game contributes zero to an opponent's limiting score. Ties count as winning, so feasibility requires \(a_0\geq a_t\) for every supported type.

Is deciding whether such transfers admit a balanced realization with \(a_0\geq a_t\) for all \(t\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

Discrete constructive round-robin manipulation is NP-complete for general scoring models, while Copeland and chess scoring admit polynomial-time algorithms ([Russell–Walsh](https://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~twalsh/rwadt09.pdf)). No result settling this exact continuous problem was identified in the search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified coalition membership for every supported type and for the target; membership is homogeneous within a type.
  • Normalized opponent mass to one and treated the distinguished target as a single zero-mass competitor, whose game does not affect opponents' limiting scores.
  • Interpreted the finite score set as explicitly listed nonnegative rational outcome pairs, closed under endpoint reversal; used m for its cardinality because there is no finite team count.
  • Defined baseline profiles and transfers as ordered-pair masses, including within-type games, with reversal consistency and conservation of each baseline outcome's mass.
  • Made balance precise by requiring identical baseline and final outcome profiles against each opposing type almost everywhere; required a measurable realization rather than merely aggregate score constraints.
  • Treated baseline profiles as the complete input, permitting the microscopic baseline arrangement to be chosen subject to those profiles, and promised balanced baseline realizability.
  • Retained arbitrary changes between two coalition members, weak monotonicity for games with one coalition member, unchanged outcomes, and co-winner semantics.
  • Allowed real-valued transfers with no manipulation budget or integrality restriction.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\) for every explicitly listed scoring model satisfying the stated hypotheses. Its running time is \((\tau m L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations.

The essential point is that every admissible transfer table has a balanced realization. Thus balance imposes no additional constraints beyond the finite linear equations in the question. The proof includes games within a single type.

1. A stronger realization statement

Write \(C=S\times S\). A label \(c=(r,s)\in C\) specifies a game's baseline and final outcomes. Define its reversal by \(c^*=(r^*,s^*)\).

Call a transfer table admissible if it satisfies nonnegativity, the forbidden-transition restrictions, the baseline marginal equations, and reversal symmetry. No winning inequalities are required in this definition.

Lemma. Every admissible transfer table \(x,z\) has a measurable realization such that, for every type \(t\), almost every player \(v\in I_t\), every type \(u\), and every label \((r,s)\), the mass of players in \(I_u\) giving that joint label against \(v\) is

\[ \frac{x_{tu}(r,s)}{\mu_t}. \]

In particular, the realization is balanced in both its baseline and final outcomes.

Proof: coordinates and reversal

Choose disjoint half-open intervals \(I_t\) of lengths \(\mu_t\). Identify each interval with \([0,1)\) by an affine coordinate \(\theta_t\). Under this identification, physical Lebesgue measure on \(I_t\) is \(\mu_t\) times Lebesgue measure on \([0,1)\).

Exchanging endpoints preserves admissibility after reversing both outcomes. Indeed, the cases with neither or both endpoints in the coalition are invariant under reversal, while the two cases with exactly one coalition endpoint are exchanged.

Distinct types

Fix \(t<u\), and define the probability distribution

\[ p_{tu}(r,s)=\frac{x_{tu}(r,s)}{\mu_t\mu_u}. \]

Its entries sum to one because the transfer marginal equations give

\[ \sum_{r,s}x_{tu}(r,s)=\sum_r B_{tu}(r)=\mu_t\mu_u. \]

Partition \([0,1)\) measurably into intervals labeled by \((r,s)\), with lengths \(p_{tu}(r,s)\). Zero-length intervals can be omitted. Let \(F_{tu}\) be the resulting label function.

For players with coordinates \(a\in[0,1)\) in type \(t\) and \(b\in[0,1)\) in type \(u\), assign the label

\[ F_{tu}\bigl((b-a)\bmod 1\bigr). \]

For the opposite ordering of the same players, assign its reversed label.

For fixed \(a\), the map \(b\mapsto(b-a)\bmod1\) preserves Lebesgue measure. Consequently, the physical mass of partners in \(I_u\) with label \((r,s)\) is

\[ \mu_u p_{tu}(r,s)=\frac{x_{tu}(r,s)}{\mu_t}. \]

For fixed \(b\), the map \(a\mapsto(b-a)\bmod1\) also preserves Lebesgue measure. The corresponding reverse-oriented section therefore has mass

\[ \mu_t p_{tu}(r,s) =\frac{x_{tu}(r,s)}{\mu_u} =\frac{x_{ut}(r^*,s^*)}{\mu_u}. \]

Integrating the sections gives exactly the prescribed joint masses on both ordered blocks.

Games within one type

Fix \(t\), and put

\[ p_t(c)=\frac{x_{tt}(c)}{\mu_t^2}. \]

Then \(\sum_c p_t(c)=1\), and reversal symmetry gives \(p_t(c)=p_t(c^*)\).

Partition the labels into orbits of the involution \(c\mapsto c^*\). An orbit is either a singleton or a pair.

Construct a measurable label function \(F_t\) first on \((0,\tfrac12)\):

  • For each two-element orbit \(\{c,c^*\}\), choose one representative \(c\) and allocate it an interval of length \(p_t(c)\).
  • For each singleton orbit \(\{c\}\), allocate it an interval of length \(p_t(c)/2\).

These lengths sum to one half, since

\[ \sum_{\text{two-element orbit representatives }c}p_t(c) +\frac12\sum_{c=c^*}p_t(c) =\frac12\sum_{c\in C}p_t(c) =\frac12. \]

Extend the function to \((\tfrac12,1)\) by requiring

\[ F_t(d)=F_t(1-d)^*. \]

Interval endpoints can be assigned any admissible label, with their reflected endpoints assigned the reversed label. This preserves the displayed identity and does not affect any label's measure.

A two-element orbit then contributes measure \(p_t(c)\) to each of its labels. A singleton contributes measure \(p_t(c)/2\) in each half. Thus every label \(c\) has total measure \(p_t(c)\).

For distinct players with coordinates \(a,b\), provided \((b-a)\bmod1\ne\tfrac12\), assign the label

\[ F_t\bigl((b-a)\bmod1\bigr). \]

Reversal holds because exchanging the players changes a nonzero circle difference \(d\) into \(1-d\).

There remains the exceptional case of antipodal players, whose circle difference is \(\tfrac12\). Choose any \(r\in S\), and let \(c_0=(r,r)\). This unchanged transition is always admissible. For an antipodal pair, assign \(c_0\) when \(a<b\), and \(c_0^*\) when \(a>b\). Reversal now holds for these pairs as well. The diagonal is omitted as stipulated.

The antipodal set has two-dimensional measure zero, and its section at any fixed player contains only one partner. Hence this assignment changes neither joint masses nor section masses.

For every fixed player, circle translation preserves measure. Its physical mass of partners with label \(c\) is therefore

\[ \mu_t p_t(c)=\frac{x_{tt}(c)}{\mu_t}. \]

This proves the required assertion for self-type blocks.

Target games and balance

For each type \(t\), partition \(I_t\) into measurable intervals labeled by \((r,s)\), with lengths \(z_t(r,s)\). Such a partition exists because

\[ \sum_{r,s}z_t(r,s)=\sum_r D_t(r)=\mu_t. \]

Assign the target game's baseline and final outcomes according to these labels, oriented with the target first.

All constructed functions are measurable. Positive-measure labels are admissible, and any exceptional assignments use admissible unchanged transitions.

Finally, summing the opponent section masses over final outcomes gives the baseline section mass

\[ \sum_s\frac{x_{tu}(r,s)}{\mu_t} =\frac{B_{tu}(r)}{\mu_t}, \]

while summing over baseline outcomes gives the final section mass

\[ \sum_r\frac{x_{tu}(r,s)}{\mu_t} =\frac{Q_{tu}(s)}{\mu_t}. \]

Thus the realization is balanced. This completes the proof of the lemma.

2. The exact linear program

Use variables \(x_{tu}(r,s)\) and \(z_t(r,s)\), and impose the following rational linear constraints:

  • All variables are nonnegative.
  • Variables for forbidden transitions equal zero.
  • For every ordered type pair and baseline outcome,

\[ \sum_s x_{tu}(r,s)=B_{tu}(r). \]

  • For every ordered type pair and pair of outcomes,

\[ x_{ut}(r^*,s^*)=x_{tu}(r,s). \]

  • For every target-versus-type baseline outcome,

\[ \sum_s z_t(r,s)=D_t(r). \]

  • For every supported type \(t\), impose

\[ \mu_t\sum_{v=1}^{\tau}\sum_{r,s\in S}s_1z_v(r,s) \ \geq\ \sum_{u=1}^{\tau}\sum_{r,s\in S}s_1x_{tu}(r,s). \]

The last constraint is exactly \(a_0\geq a_t\). To see this, substitute the definitions of \(H_v\) and \(Q_{tu}\), then multiply the desired score comparison by the strictly positive mass \(\mu_t\). This multiplication preserves the inequality.

Equivalence. The original instance is feasible if and only if this linear program is feasible.

For necessity, any feasible realization supplies transfers satisfying constraints 1–5, and its winning score comparisons give constraint 6.

For sufficiency, a feasible LP solution is an admissible transfer table. The lemma constructs its balanced realization. Its target score is the left-hand score expression, and every opponent of type \(t\), except possibly on a null set, has score

\[ \frac1{\mu_t}\sum_{u,r,s}s_1x_{tu}(r,s). \]

Constraint 6 therefore ensures that the target ties or beats every supported type, exactly as required.

3. Running time and encoding size

Let

\[ N=(\tau^2+\tau)m^2. \]

The formulation has exactly \(N\) variables before eliminating forbidden transitions, and \(O(N)\) linear constraints. This includes nonnegativity, forbidden-transition equations, marginal equations, reversal equations, and the \(\tau\) winning inequalities.

Reversal indices and allowed transitions can be computed using \(O(\tau^2m^2)\) rational comparisons and arithmetic operations. Every coefficient and right-hand side has \(O(L)\) bits: coefficients are input rationals, signs, or products \(\mu_t s_1\), whose encoding lengths grow only by a constant factor.

Thus:

  • Constructing the sparse LP takes \(O(\tau^2m^2\operatorname{poly}(L))\) bit operations.
  • A dense LP encoding has length \(O(N^2L)\), which is polynomial in the original input length.
  • Exact rational linear-programming feasibility is decidable in polynomial time in the numbers of variables and constraints and the binary encoding length. This theorem applies to systems with equalities and lower-dimensional feasible regions. See [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239).

Applying that theorem gives total decision time

\[ \operatorname{poly}(\tau^2m^2,L) =(\tau m L)^{O(1)} \]

bit operations. This is a bit-polynomial bound; no strongly polynomial bound is claimed.

When feasible, exact LP algorithms can also return rational transfers of polynomial encoding length. The realization above then has a polynomial-size description using rational interval endpoints and circle-difference formulas.

4. Why balance does not preserve the discrete obstruction

The construction uses the stated freedom to choose the microscopic baseline arrangement. It realizes even the joint baseline/final transfer proportions uniformly in every opponent section, which is stronger than the requested balance.

In fact, applying the lemma to unchanged transfers \(x_{tu}(r,r)=B_{tu}(r)\) shows that the balanced-baseline promise already follows from the stated mass and reversal conditions.

Although individual game outcomes remain elements of the finite set \(S\), atomless partner sets permit arbitrary transfer proportions. The realization lemma proves that these proportions are implementable simultaneously and symmetrically, so the LP is an exact formulation of this continuous problem.
