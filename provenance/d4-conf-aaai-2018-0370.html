The continuous mirror question, as graded

For an \(n\)-day repeated cut-and-choose service on the square, does there exist a non-adaptive forced-cut protocol using \(3n\) cuts such that, after every reachable history of divisions and exact aggregate choice shares, each legal next division admits two finite-support rational distributions over chooser types consistent with that history whose next shares choosing one side are respectively below and above \(1/2\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(n\geq1\) and \(C=(0,1)^2\). A chooser type is a nonnegative Borel probability measure \(u\) on \(C\), absolutely continuous with respect to area, together with a deterministic tie rule selecting the same physical piece whenever a division is repeated. Types remain unchanged across days and choose myopically.
A society \(\mu\) is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, with masses summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; rationality concerns masses, not valuations. Any running-time bounds must specify dependence on \(n\), \(m=2\) offered pieces, \(\tau\), and binary data length \(L\), and identify the representation of valuations.
A non-adaptive forced-cut protocol fixes before play each day's forced straight-line cuts, permitted cutter-cut directions, and slice assignments. Forced cuts partition the square into segments of positive area; the cutter makes one straight-line cut through each segment's interior, producing two slices of positive area. Every interior offset in each permitted direction is legal, and each segment contributes one slice to each offered piece. Count forced cuts and segment-cut operations separately, summed over all days.
For a legal division \(D=(X,C\setminus X)\), let \(c_u(D)\in\{0,1\}\) indicate whether the type chooses \(X\), including its tie rule, and define \(s_\mu(D)=\sum_{u\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mu(u)c_u(D)\).
A history \(H=((D_1,q_1),\ldots,(D_{t-1},q_{t-1}))\) is reachable if its divisions are legal and some such society satisfies \(s_\mu(D_j)=q_j\) for every earlier day. Only divisions and exact aggregate shares are observed; alternative consistent societies may have different supports.
Call a protocol aggregate non-exploitable if, for every \(1\leq t\leq n\), every reachable history \(H\) (including the empty history), and every legal next division \(D\), there exist finite-support societies \(\mu^-\) and \(\mu^+\) consistent with \(H\) such that \(s_{\mu^-}(D)<\tfrac12<s_{\mu^+}(D)\).

For every \(n\geq1\), does an aggregate non-exploitable non-adaptive forced-cut protocol on \(C\) using at most \(3n\) cuts exist, or is there some \(n\) for which none exists?

For a single persistent chooser, a non-adaptive non-exploitable square protocol using \(3n\) cuts is known: every legal next division permits either individual choice consistently with previous choices. [Tamuz, Vardi, and Ziani](https://www.tamuz.caltech.edu/papers/repeated_cake_cutting.pdf)
No result settling the exact aggregate-share formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Chooser types use the intended absolutely continuous probability valuations; zero density on regions is allowed, and types persist across days.
  • Society masses are positive binary rationals summing to one; valuation measures themselves need not have rational descriptions.
  • Consistent deterministic tie-breaking is included in each type rather than imposing an arbitrary universal tie preference.
  • Reachability means realizability by a finite-support rational-mass society, including histories with aggregate shares equal to zero or one.
  • Alternative societies may use different types and support sizes; the original support is not disclosed to the cutter.
  • Cuts must produce slices of positive area, excluding boundary cuts and empty segments; permitted directions and slice assignments are fixed before play, with all interior offsets legal.
  • The cut budget is interpreted as at most three times the number of days, counting each segment-cut operation separately.
  • The target retains protocol existence because the anchor supplies a construction, not a polynomial-time decision result.

Result

Yes. For every \(n\geq1\), there is an aggregate non-exploitable non-adaptive protocol using exactly \(n\) forced cuts and \(2n\) segment-cut operations.

In fact, for every reachable history and every legal next division, consistent societies can make the next aggregate share equal to \(0\) or \(1\). The crucial point is that the witnesses may have different supports.

1. The protocol

Use the geometry of Protocol 5.1 of [Tamuz, Vardi, and Ziani](https://www.tamuz.caltech.edu/papers/repeated_cake_cutting.pdf). We give a self-contained proof of the stronger aggregate property.

Before play, fix \(a_j=j/(n+1)\) for every day \(j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\). On day \(j\):

  • Make the forced vertical cut \(x=a_j\).
  • Require a horizontal cutter-cut in each of the two resulting segments, at independently chosen heights \(\alpha_j,\beta_j\in(0,1)\).
  • Offer the piece

\(X_j=((0,a_j)\times(0,\alpha_j))\cup((a_j,1)\times(\beta_j,1))\)
and its complement.

Assign points on the cut lines by a fixed convention. Their assignment does not affect any absolutely continuous valuation.

Both segments and all four slices have positive area. Every interior horizontal offset is legal. The forced cuts, permitted directions, and slice assignments are fixed before play.

Summed over all days, the protocol uses \(n\) forced cuts and \(2n\) segment-cut operations, hence \(3n\) cuts in total.

2. Every choice sequence has a strict valuation witness

Lemma. Fix \(r\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), any legal divisions \(D_1,\ldots,D_r\) under this protocol, and any prescribed choices \(b_1,\ldots,b_r\in\{0,1\}\). There is an absolutely continuous Borel probability measure \(u\) such that, for every \(j\leq r\),

\(u(X_j)=\frac12+\frac{2b_j-1}{4(r+1)}.\)

Consequently, the myopic choices are exactly the prescribed choices, irrespective of the tie rule.

Proof. Define the extreme cutter heights

\(\ell=\min_{1\leq j\leq r}\{\alpha_j,\beta_j\},\qquad h=\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\{\alpha_j,\beta_j\}.\)

Legality gives \(0<\ell\leq h<1\).

For this proof, set \(a_0=0\), retain \(a_i=i/(n+1)\) for \(1\leq i\leq r\), and set \(a_{r+1}=1\). Thus the last strip extends from the last observed forced cut to the right boundary. Define the positive-area rectangles

\(R_i^-=(a_{i-1},a_i)\times(0,\ell),\qquad R_i^+=(a_{i-1},a_i)\times(h,1)\)

for \(1\leq i\leq r+1\).

Set

\(e_0=e_{r+1}=0,\qquad e_i=2b_i-1\quad(1\leq i\leq r),\)

and define

\(B=\frac{1}{2(r+1)},\qquad \varepsilon=\frac{1}{4(r+1)},\qquad d_i=\varepsilon(e_i-e_{i-1}).\)

Assign rectangle masses

\(u(R_i^-)=B+\frac{d_i}{2},\qquad u(R_i^+)=B-\frac{d_i}{2}.\)

These assignments define a density by making it constant on each rectangle, with value equal to the assigned mass divided by its area, and zero elsewhere.

We verify that this is a probability measure. Since \(|e_i-e_{i-1}|\leq2\), we have \(|d_i|\leq2\varepsilon=B\). Each assigned mass is therefore at least \(B/2>0\). Telescoping gives

\(\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}d_i=\varepsilon(e_{r+1}-e_0)=0.\)

Consequently,

\(\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}u(R_i^-)=\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}u(R_i^+)=(r+1)B=\frac12.\)

The density is nonnegative, Borel measurable, and integrates to \(1\), so it defines an absolutely continuous Borel probability measure.

Now fix a day \(j\leq r\). For \(i\leq j\), the rectangle \(R_i^-\) lies in \(X_j\), while \(R_i^+\) lies in its complement. For \(i>j\), the reverse holds. This follows because every lower rectangle lies below both cutter heights and every upper rectangle lies above both cutter heights.

All valuation mass is on these rectangles. Hence

\(u(X_j)=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(B+\frac{d_i}{2}\right)+\sum_{i=j+1}^{r+1}\left(B-\frac{d_i}{2}\right).\)

Using \(\sum_i d_i=0\), this simplifies to

\(u(X_j)=\frac12+\sum_{i=1}^{j}d_i=\frac12+\varepsilon e_j.\)

If \(b_j=1\), the value is strictly above \(1/2\); if \(b_j=0\), it is strictly below \(1/2\). This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

Each witness can be equipped with a deterministic tie rule—for example, among equally valued pieces, select the piece containing a fixed point of \(C\). Because the offered pieces form a partition, this selects the same physical piece whenever a division is repeated. The lemma’s choices are all strict.

3. Preserving exact aggregate shares

Fix any day \(t\leq n\), any reachable history

\(H=((D_1,q_1),\ldots,(D_{t-1},q_{t-1})),\)

and any legal next division \(D_t\).

By reachability, there is a finite-support society consistent with \(H\). Write its distinct types as \(u_1,\ldots,u_\tau\), with positive binary-rational masses \(w_1,\ldots,w_\tau\). Thus

\(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i=1,\qquad q_j=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i c_{u_i}(D_j)\quad(j<t).\)

For each \(b\in\{0,1\}\) and each original type \(u_i\), prescribe the length-\(t\) choice sequence

\((c_{u_i}(D_1),\ldots,c_{u_i}(D_{t-1}),b).\)

Apply the lemma with \(r=t\) to obtain a replacement type \(v_i^{(b)}\) realizing this entire sequence with strict preferences.

Define a society \(\mu^{(b)}\) by placing mass \(w_i\) on \(v_i^{(b)}\). If replacement types coincide, merge their masses. Merging preserves positive binary-rational masses and total mass \(1\); the resulting support size is at most \(\tau\).

For every earlier day \(j<t\),

\(s_{\mu^{(b)}}(D_j)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i c_{v_i^{(b)}}(D_j)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i c_{u_i}(D_j)=q_j.\)

Thus both replacement societies preserve every exact observed aggregate share. On the next day, all replacement types make choice \(b\), so

\(s_{\mu^{(b)}}(D_t)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i b=b.\)

Taking \(\mu^-=\mu^{(0)}\) and \(\mu^+=\mu^{(1)}\) gives

\(s_{\mu^-}(D_t)=0<\frac12<1=s_{\mu^+}(D_t).\)

For \(t=1\), the same argument starts with any singleton society; there are no earlier constraints. Hence the required property holds for the empty history as well.

This proves aggregate non-exploitability for every \(n\geq1\). \(\square\)

4. Representation and scope

The protocol offers \(m=2\) pieces. Its cut count is independent of the witnessing society’s support size \(\tau\) and binary data length \(L\).

The constructed valuations have an explicit representation as piecewise constant densities on \(2(t+1)\) rectangles. Their rectangle coordinates may be real, as legal cutter offsets are unrestricted real numbers. The society masses retain the original binary-rational weights, with merging if needed.

This is an existence proof, with no running-time claim for arbitrary valuations or real cut coordinates.
