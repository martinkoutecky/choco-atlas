The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite supports \(A_h\) and \(B_h\) with rational population masses \(\mu_{h,a}\) and \(\nu_{h,b}\), known feature maps, discrete context and action spaces, and unknown linear type-pair utilities and context transitions, design an online policy for \(K\) episodes. At each stage the policy chooses a platform action and feasible flows \(x_{ab}\geq 0\), with \(\sum_b x_{ab}\leq\mu_{h,a}\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}\leq\nu_{h,b}\); flows maximize welfare under the true utilities and admit supporting dual prices. Under a specified finite observation protocol, does there exist a policy with high-probability planner regret sublinear in \(K\), with a bound depending on feature dimension, horizon, and support size rather than the number of possible contexts?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

At each stage \(h\in\{1,\ldots,H\}\), two nonempty societies have fixed supports \(A_h,B_h\), repeated across episodes. A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Each side has total mass \(1\); \(\tau=\max_h(|A_h|+|B_h|)\) is support size, not the number of possible types.
Contexts form a finite set \(\mathcal C\); the explicitly listed platform action set \(E\) has size \(m\). Known rational feature maps \(\psi:\mathcal C\times E\to\mathbb R^d\) and \(\phi:A_h\times B_h\to\mathbb R^d\) have Euclidean norm at most \(1\). Contexts have finite binary labels, and features are evaluable in polynomial time from their descriptions and query labels.
Write \(\Phi(C,e,a,b)=\operatorname{vec}(\psi(C,e)\phi(a,b)^\top)\). Unknown stage parameters satisfy \(u_h=\langle\Phi,\theta_h\rangle\), \(v_h=\langle\Phi,\gamma_h\rangle\), \(\|\theta_h\|_2,\|\gamma_h\|_2\le d\), and \(u_h,v_h\in[-1,1]\); unmatched mass receives zero utility.
Unknown transitions satisfy \(P_h(C'\mid C,e)=\langle\psi(C,e),q_h(C')\rangle\), are valid probability kernels, and obey \(\|\sum_{C'}f(C')q_h(C')\|_2\le\sqrt d\) whenever \(\|f\|_\infty\le1\). Transitions depend only on the context and platform action, not on flows or transfers.
Feasible flows satisfy \(x_{ab}\ge0\), \(\sum_bx_{ab}\le\mu_{h,a}\), and \(\sum_ax_{ab}\le\nu_{h,b}\). For pair weights \(w_{ab}\), supporting prices mean primal optimality together with dual optimality of \(p_a,q_b\ge0\), \(p_a+q_b\ge w_{ab}\), minimizing \(\sum_a\mu_{h,a}p_a+\sum_b\nu_{h,b}q_b\). Transfers are unrestricted and budget balanced; ties may be resolved arbitrarily.
In each of \(K\) episodes, starting from a fixed known context \(C_1\), the learner chooses platform actions, estimated utilities, and flows with supporting prices for those estimates. True-utility optimal flows and prices define the benchmark; they are not required of the learner during exploration.
After each stage, every pair with \(x_{ab}>0\) yields exactly one observation of each per-unit utility, regardless of its flow mass, with independent centered \(1\)-subgaussian noise; no other utility observations are available. The learner also observes the next context. Feedback is rounded to the nearest multiple of \(K^{-2}\); \(L\) denotes the maximum binary encoding length of input descriptions, queried labels, and recorded feedback.
Define \(\bar r_h(C,e)=\max_x\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}(u_h+v_h)(C,e,a,b)\), \(\bar V_1^\pi(C_1)=\mathbb E_\pi[\sum_h\bar r_h(C_h,e_h)]\), and \(R^P(K)=\sum_{k=1}^K[\max_\pi\bar V_1^\pi(C_1)-\bar V_1^{\pi_k}(C_1)]\), where \(\pi_k\) is the platform policy selected from preceding episodes.

Does this model admit an online policy running in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,d,H,K,\log(1/\delta)\) with \(R^P(K)\le\operatorname{poly}(d,H,\tau)\operatorname{polylog}(K/\delta)K^\alpha\), for some universal \(\alpha<1\), with probability at least \(1-\delta\) for every \(0<\delta<1\), independently of \(|\mathcal C|\); or is such a guarantee impossible?

For finite agents, a verified guarantee is \(R^P(K)=\widetilde O(d^{5/2}H(\sum_hW_h)\sqrt K)\) with high probability under the corresponding linear-feature and noisy-feedback assumptions, where \(W_h>0\) bounds stage optimal welfare. [Published result](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/7e0af0d1bc0ec2a90fc294be2e00447e-Paper-Conference.pdf).
No result settling this precise mass-and-feedback model was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized each side's masses to one, interpreting the societies as distributions; stage welfare is consequently bounded by two.
  • Restored the tensor-product utility features, parameter bounds, signed utility domain, and bounded linear transition model.
  • Fixed stage supports and masses across episodes, made transitions independent of flows, and chose a fixed known initial context.
  • Resolved the graded question's true-utility optimality requirement as a benchmark condition: implemented flows have supporting prices for learned utilities, as in the discrete learning model.
  • Specified one noisy per-unit utility observation per positive-flow pair per stage, independent of flow size, with no observations on zero-flow pairs.
  • Made feedback finitely encoded by rounding to a grid of spacing K^(-2), and specified succinct context labels and polynomial-time feature evaluation.
  • Defined m as the platform action count, tau as the maximum combined stage support size, and L as the maximum relevant encoding length.
  • Specified nonnegative supporting dual prices, unrestricted budget-balanced transfers, zero outside utility, arbitrary tie resolution, and the pseudo-value definition of planner regret.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

A positive result

The model admits the requested guarantee, with \(\alpha=1/2\). A polynomial-time policy achieves
\[ \]
\(R^P(K)\le C H^2\sqrt d\,(d\tau+d+1)\,\ell\sqrt K\)
with probability at least \(1-\delta\), where \(C\) is a universal constant and
\[ \]
\(\ell=\left\lceil\log_2\!\left(\frac{128(d+1)(H+1)(\tau+1)(K+1)}{\delta}\right)\right\rceil.\)

The displayed empty display delimiters above carry no mathematics; all mathematical expressions below use the requested inline delimiters.

The proof uses the distinction specified in the question: exploration flows need supporting prices for the learner’s estimates, whereas platform regret is evaluated using true optimal welfare. In particular, the observation rule permits sampling every supported pair at every visited context.

1. Feasible exploration that observes every pair

Fix a stage \(h\). Announce utility estimates identically zero and choose
\[ \]
\(x_{ab}=\mu_{h,a}\nu_{h,b},\qquad p_a=q_b=0.\)

Indeed,
\[ \]
\(\sum_bx_{ab}=\mu_{h,a},\qquad \sum_ax_{ab}=\nu_{h,b}.\)

Every entry is positive because \(A_h,B_h\) are supports. For zero estimated pair weights, every feasible flow is primal optimal, and zero prices are feasible and dual optimal. Zero transfers are budget balanced.

Thus this is an allowed exploration outcome, and it supplies one observation of each utility for every pair. Its encoding length is polynomial in the encoding length of the masses. No lower bound on a positive mass is needed.

The policy below uses these announced estimates and flows throughout. Its internal regressions determine the platform actions. This is permitted because the question imposes no exploration welfare requirement, and transitions depend only on context and platform action.

2. Pair utilities share a design matrix

Write \(z(C,e)=\psi(C,e)\), and let
\[ \]
\(w_{h,ab}(C,e)=u_h(C,e,a,b)+v_h(C,e,a,b).\)

Reshape \(\theta_h+\gamma_h\) as a matrix \(T_h\) so that
\[ \]
\(w_{h,ab}(C,e)=z(C,e)^\top t_{h,ab},\qquad t_{h,ab}=T_h\phi(a,b).\)

The assumptions give
\[ \]
\(\|t_{h,ab}\|_2\le\|T_h\|_{\mathrm F}\|\phi(a,b)\|_2\le2d,\qquad |w_{h,ab}(C,e)|\le2.\)

Although the pair coefficients differ, their observed regressors are identical. For episode \(k\), define
\[ \]
\(z_{h,i}=\psi(C_{h,i},e_{h,i}),\qquad \Lambda_{h,k}=I_d+\sum_{i<k}z_{h,i}z_{h,i}^\top,\)
and
\[ \]
\(\rho_{h,k}(C,e)=\sqrt{z(C,e)^\top\Lambda_{h,k}^{-1}z(C,e)}.\)

This matrix serves both utility estimation and transition estimation.

For the algorithm and proof, choose a sufficiently large universal integer \(c\), and set
\[ \]
\(\beta_r=c(d+1)\lceil\sqrt\ell\rceil,\qquad \beta_P=cH(d\tau+d+1)\lceil\sqrt\ell\rceil.\)

The choice of \(c\) will be justified by the concentration estimates below.

3. Optimistic transportation rewards

Clip each recorded individual utility observation to the interval \([-M,M]\), where
\[ \]
\(M=10\lceil\sqrt\ell\rceil.\)

Let \(Y_{h,ab,i}\) be the sum of the two clipped observations, and form
\[ \]
\(\widehat t_{h,ab,k}=\Lambda_{h,k}^{-1}\sum_{i<k}z_{h,i}Y_{h,ab,i}.\)

Define the ideal optimistic pair weight
\[ \]
\(\widetilde w_{h,ab,k}(C,e)=\operatorname{clip}_{[-2,2]}\!\left(z(C,e)^\top\widehat t_{h,ab,k}+\beta_r\rho_{h,k}(C,e)\right).\)

Let \(F_h\) denote the feasible flow polytope. The ideal optimistic reward is
\[ \]
\(\widetilde r_{h,k}(C,e)=\max_{x\in F_h}\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}\widetilde w_{h,ab,k}(C,e).\)

Two elementary properties will be used repeatedly. First,
\[ \]
\(\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}\le1\quad\text{for every }x\in F_h.\)

Second, if \(G_h(s)=\max_{x\in F_h}\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}s_{ab}\), then
\[ \]
\(|G_h(s)-G_h(s')|\le\max_{a,b}|s_{ab}-s'_{ab}|.\)

To prove the latter, evaluate the difference of the two objectives at any feasible \(x\); its absolute value is at most \(\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}\|s-s'\|_\infty\le\|s-s'\|_\infty\). Taking maxima in both directions proves the claim. Also,
\[ \]
\(0\le\bar r_h(C,e)\le2,\qquad 0\le\widetilde r_{h,k}(C,e)\le2.\)

Utility confidence

With probability at least \(1-\delta/2\), simultaneously for every stage, pair, episode, context and action,
\[ \]
\(\left|z(C,e)^\top\widehat t_{h,ab,k}-w_{h,ab}(C,e)\right|\le\beta_r\rho_{h,k}(C,e).\)

Here are the details.

A union bound over at most \(2HK\tau^2\) individual observations, using the subgaussian tail bound and the rounding error, shows that clipping changes no observation with probability at least \(1-\delta/4\).

On this event, the summed observation has the form
\[ \]
\(Y_{h,ab,i}=z_{h,i}^\top t_{h,ab}+\xi_{h,ab,i}+b_{h,ab,i},\qquad |b_{h,ab,i}|\le K^{-2},\)
where \(\xi_{h,ab,i}\) is conditionally centered and \(\sqrt2\)-subgaussian. The regressors are predictable because the platform policy is chosen before the episode.

We use the following self-normalized inequality. If predictable vectors \(z_i\) have norm at most one, \(\xi_i\) are conditionally centered \(R\)-subgaussian, and \(\Lambda_n=I+\sum_{i\le n}z_iz_i^\top\), then, for a fixed \(n\), with probability at least \(1-\varepsilon\),
\[ \]
\(\left\|\sum_{i\le n}z_i\xi_i\right\|_{\Lambda_n^{-1}}\le R\sqrt{\log\det\Lambda_n+2\log(1/\varepsilon)}.\)

For completeness, for every fixed vector \(\lambda\), the conditional subgaussian assumption makes
\[ \]
\(\exp\!\left(\lambda^\top\sum_{i\le n}z_i\xi_i/R-\frac12\lambda^\top(\Lambda_n-I)\lambda\right)\)
a nonnegative supermartingale with expectation at most one. Integrating against a standard Gaussian distribution in \(\lambda\) gives expectation at most one for
\[ \]
\((\det\Lambda_n)^{-1/2}\exp\!\left(\frac{\|\sum_{i\le n}z_i\xi_i\|_{\Lambda_n^{-1}}^2}{2R^2}\right).\)

Markov’s inequality proves the stated bound.

Moreover,
\[ \]
\(\log\det\Lambda_n\le d\log(1+n/d),\)
by the trace bound and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality.

The rounding contribution satisfies
\[ \]
\(\left\|\sum_{i<k}z_{h,i}b_{h,ab,i}\right\|_{\Lambda_{h,k}^{-1}}\le\left(\sum_{i<k}b_{h,ab,i}^2\right)^{1/2}\le\sqrt K\,K^{-2}.\)

To justify the first inequality, put the regressors into a matrix \(Z\). All singular values of \((I+ZZ^\top)^{-1/2}Z\) are at most one.

Finally, ridge regularization contributes at most \(\|t_{h,ab}\|_2\le2d\). Applying the self-normalized inequality and union bounding over stages, pairs and episode indices proves the confidence statement for sufficiently large \(c\).

Clipping the optimistic weights preserves optimism because the true weights belong to \([-2,2]\). Consequently,
\[ \]
\(0\le\widetilde w_{h,ab,k}-w_{h,ab}\le2\beta_r\rho_{h,k},\)
and therefore
\[ \]
\(0\le\widetilde r_{h,k}(C,e)-\bar r_h(C,e)\le2\beta_r\rho_{h,k}(C,e).\)

4. The planning algorithm, including finite precision

Choose a dyadic rational \(\eta\) satisfying
\[ \]
\(0<\eta\le\frac1{16(K+1)^3}.\)

At a queried context–action pair, compute a rational upper approximation \(s_{h,k}(C,e)\) such that
\[ \]
\(\rho_{h,k}(C,e)\le s_{h,k}(C,e)\le\rho_{h,k}(C,e)+\frac{\eta}{\beta_r+\beta_P}.\)

Replace \(\rho\) by \(s\) in the optimistic pair weights and solve the rational transportation LP exactly. Denote its value by \(r_{h,k}^{\mathrm{num}}(C,e)\). Monotonicity and the preceding Lipschitz property give
\[ \]
\(\widetilde r_{h,k}(C,e)\le r_{h,k}^{\mathrm{num}}(C,e)\le\widetilde r_{h,k}(C,e)+\eta.\)

At the beginning of episode \(k\), perform backward induction. Set \(V_{H+1,k}=0\), and define
\[ \]
\(\widehat b_{h,k}=\Lambda_{h,k}^{-1}\sum_{i<k}z_{h,i}V_{h+1,k}(C_{h+1,i}).\)

For a real number \(a\), let \(\lceil a\rceil_\eta\) mean rounding upward to the nearest multiple of \(\eta\). Set
\[ \]
\(Q_{h,k}(C,e)=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,\,2(H-h+1)]}\!\left(\left\lceil r_{h,k}^{\mathrm{num}}(C,e)+z(C,e)^\top\widehat b_{h,k}+\beta_Ps_{h,k}(C,e)\right\rceil_\eta\right),\)
and
\[ \]
\(V_{h,k}(C)=\max_{e\in E}Q_{h,k}(C,e).\)

The episode policy \(\pi_k\) chooses an action attaining this maximum, using a fixed rule for ties. Its entire representation is computed from preceding episodes.

In particular, every computed value lies in \([0,2H]\) and on a fixed rational grid.

5. Uniform transition confidence

The values used in backward regression depend on the transition data. A concentration bound for a single fixed value function would therefore be insufficient. We establish the necessary uniform bound.

For any bounded function \(f\), write
\[ \]
\(q_h[f]=\sum_{C'\in\mathcal C}f(C')q_h(C').\)

The transition assumptions imply
\[ \]
\(P_hf(C,e)=z(C,e)^\top q_h[f],\qquad \|q_h[f]\|_2\le\sqrt d\,\|f\|_\infty.\)

We will prove that, with probability at least \(1-\delta/4\), simultaneously for all episodes, stages, contexts and actions,
\[ \]
\(\left|z(C,e)^\top\widehat b_{h,k}-P_hV_{h+1,k}(C,e)\right|\le\beta_P\rho_{h,k}(C,e).\)

A deterministic cover of the possible value functions

Let \(n_h=|A_h||B_h|\le\tau^2\). Deterministically,
\[ \]
\(\|\widehat t_{h,ab,k}\|_2\le2KM,\qquad \|\widehat b_{h,k}\|_2\le2HK,\qquad 0\preceq\Lambda_{h,k}^{-1}\preceq I_d.\)

The first two inequalities follow from \(\|\Lambda^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1\), \(\|z_i\|_2\le1\), \(|Y_i|\le2M\), and \(0\le V_i\le2H\).

Consider ideal value functions obtained by:

  • taking arbitrary pair coefficient vectors of norm at most \(2KM\);
  • taking an arbitrary transition coefficient vector of norm at most \(2HK\);
  • using arbitrary positive semidefinite matrices \(U,T\) with operator norm at most one for the reward and transition bonuses;
  • solving the transportation LP, adding the transition estimate and bonus, clipping, and maximizing over \(E\).

Allowing \(U,T\) to differ enlarges the class and thus is harmless.

For positive semidefinite \(U,U'\) and \(\|z\|_2\le1\),
\[ \]
\(\left|\sqrt{z^\top Uz}-\sqrt{z^\top U'z}\right|\le\sqrt{\|U-U'\|_{\mathrm{op}}}.\)

Together with the LP Lipschitz bound, nonexpansiveness of clipping, and nonexpansiveness of taking a maximum, this shows that parameter approximations yield a uniform approximation over all contexts. The maximum over actions introduces no factor depending on \(m\).

More explicitly, a uniform error of at most \(\varepsilon\) follows by approximating each pair vector and the transition vector within \(\varepsilon/4\), and approximating \(U,T\) in Frobenius norm within
\[ \]
\(\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{4\beta_r}\right)^2,\qquad \left(\frac{\varepsilon}{4\beta_P}\right)^2,\)
respectively.

The standard volumetric bound for a Euclidean ball gives a deterministic cover of cardinality \(N_h\) satisfying
\[ \]
\(\log N_h\le C_0(d\tau^2+d^2+d)\ell\)
when \(\varepsilon=(K+1)^{-2}\). To check this estimate, the pair-vector contribution is at most
\[ \]
\(dn_h\log(1+16KM/\varepsilon),\)
the transition-vector contribution is at most
\[ \]
\(d\log(1+16HK/\varepsilon),\)
and the two matrix contributions are each bounded by their ambient dimension \(d^2\) times the logarithm of one plus their radius-to-accuracy ratio. Their Frobenius radii are at most \(\sqrt d\). Every logarithm here is \(O(\ell)\).

The constant \(C_0\) depends on the fixed bonus constant \(c\) only through \(O(1+\log c)\).

For the same parameter values, the numerical value function differs from its ideal counterpart by at most \(3\eta\): at most \(\eta\) from the reward, at most \(\eta\) from the transition square root, and at most \(\eta\) from upward grid rounding.

Thus every value function the algorithm actually uses is within \(\varepsilon+3\eta\) of a member of this deterministic finite cover.

Concentration over the cover

For a fixed cover member \(f\), the variables
\[ \]
\(f(C_{h+1,i})-P_hf(C_{h,i},e_{h,i})\)
are conditionally centered and \(2H\)-subgaussian, because \(0\le f\le2H\).

Apply the self-normalized inequality from Section 3 and union bound over cover members, stages and episode indices. This bounds
\[ \]
\(\left\|\sum_{i<k}z_{h,i}\bigl[f(C_{h+1,i})-P_hf(C_{h,i},e_{h,i})\bigr]\right\|_{\Lambda_{h,k}^{-1}}\)
by
\[ \]
\(2H\sqrt{d\log(1+K/d)+2\log\!\left(\frac{4HK\max_hN_h}{\delta}\right)}.\)

Replacing a cover member by the algorithm’s value function changes this norm by at most
\[ \]
\(2K(\varepsilon+3\eta)\le1.\)

This follows directly from the uniform approximation, \(\|z_i\|_2\le1\), and \(\|\Lambda^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1\).

Ridge regularization contributes at most
\[ \]
\(\|q_h[V_{h+1,k}]\|_2\le2H\sqrt d.\)

Finally,
\[ \]
\(d\tau^2+d^2+d\le(d\tau+d+1)^2.\)

These estimates prove the claimed transition confidence bound for sufficiently large universal \(c\). Such a fixed choice exists: the required squared bonus grows as \(c^2\), while the covering constant grows at most as \(1+\log c\).

This argument controls data-dependent fitted values without enumerating \(\mathcal C\).

6. Optimism and regret

Work on the utility and transition confidence events. At every context–action pair,
\[ \]
\(0\le Q_{h,k}(C,e)-\bigl[\bar r_h(C,e)+P_hV_{h+1,k}(C,e)\bigr]\le2(\beta_r+\beta_P)\rho_{h,k}(C,e)+3\eta.\)

Indeed, utility optimism bounds the reward error by \(2\beta_r\rho+\eta\). Transition confidence bounds the transition estimate plus its bonus between the true transition value and that value plus \(2\beta_P\rho+\eta\). Upward grid rounding adds at most \(\eta\).

Clipping preserves both inequalities: the comparison quantity belongs to \([0,2(H-h+1)]\).

Backward induction now gives
\[ \]
\(V_{h,k}(C)\ge\bar V_h^*(C)\)
for every \(h,C\).

Define
\[ \]
\(\Delta_{h,k}(C)=V_{h,k}(C)-\bar V_h^{\pi_k}(C).\)

Then \(0\le\Delta_{h,k}\le2H\). For the greedy action selected by \(\pi_k\),
\[ \]
\(\Delta_{h,k}(C)\le2(\beta_r+\beta_P)\rho_{h,k}(C,\pi_{h,k}(C))+3\eta+P_h\Delta_{h+1,k}(C,\pi_{h,k}(C)).\)

Along the actual episode, introduce the martingale difference
\[ \]
\(D_{h,k}=P_h\Delta_{h+1,k}(C_{h,k},e_{h,k})-\Delta_{h+1,k}(C_{h+1,k}).\)

The policy and fitted functions are fixed before the episode, so this difference is conditionally centered. Also \(|D_{h,k}|\le2H\). Telescoping the preceding inequality gives
\[ \]
\(R^P(K)\le2(\beta_r+\beta_P)\sum_{k,h}\rho_{h,k}(C_{h,k},e_{h,k})+3HK\eta+\sum_{k,h}D_{h,k}.\)

Azuma’s inequality bounds the last sum, with probability at least \(1-\delta/4\), by
\[ \]
\(2H\sqrt{2HK\log(4/\delta)}.\)

For the confidence-radius sum, let
\[ \]
\(s_{h,k}=z_{h,k}^\top\Lambda_{h,k}^{-1}z_{h,k}\in[0,1].\)

The matrix determinant lemma and \(\log(1+s)\ge s/2\) for \(s\in[0,1]\) imply
\[ \]
\(\sum_{k=1}^Ks_{h,k}\le2\log\det\Lambda_{h,K+1}\le2d\log(1+K/d).\)

By Cauchy–Schwarz,
\[ \]
\(\sum_{k=1}^K\rho_{h,k}(C_{h,k},e_{h,k})\le\sqrt{2Kd\log(1+K/d)}.\)

Substituting the bonuses and summing over stages proves
\[ \]
\(R^P(K)\le C H^2\sqrt d\,(d\tau+d+1)\ell\sqrt K.\)

The clipping, utility-confidence, transition-confidence and regret-martingale failure probabilities sum to at most \(\delta\).

7. Polynomial running time

The implementation requires only:

  • \(d\)-dimensional rational regression and matrix inversion;
  • transportation LPs with at most \(\tau^2\) variables and \(\tau\) capacity constraints;
  • maximization over the explicitly listed \(m\) actions;
  • evaluation of fitted value functions at observed context labels.

Rational LPs can be solved exactly in time polynomial in their dimensions and input bit lengths. Square roots are approximated from above by rational bisection; the required precision has polynomial bit length.

The grid rounding in Section 4 prevents recursive growth of value-target denominators. Every backward-regression target belongs to a fixed grid in \([0,2H]\). Consequently, the regression coefficients, LP inputs and matrix inverses all have encoding lengths polynomial in \(L,d,H,K,\log(1/\delta)\).

At episode \(k\), backward regression evaluates the next-stage value at at most \(k-1\) stored contexts per stage. Each evaluation examines \(m\) actions and solves the corresponding transportation LPs. Summing over episodes therefore still gives polynomial total running time.

No operation enumerates \(\mathcal C\). Small positive masses affect their encoding lengths, but neither the statistical bound nor the running time contains an inverse mass.

8. Conclusion

The requested guarantee holds for the stated model. The observation rule supplies complete pair feedback under an allowed fractional exploration flow, and uniform concentration justifies efficient optimistic planning with the resulting nonlinear transportation rewards.

Since logarithms of \(d,H,\tau\) can be absorbed into a polynomial prefactor, the proved bound has the requested form with the universal exponent \(\alpha=1/2\).
