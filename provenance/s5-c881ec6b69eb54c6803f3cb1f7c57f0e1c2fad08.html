The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a shared finite-horizon MDP with rational linear features, a society μ over complete reward types, a focal cohort of mass \(\delta>0\), fixed reports from the remaining mass with mean feature count \(F_-\), and the cohort’s target feature expectation \(\Phi^\star\), compute a feasible trajectory distribution \(q\) minimizing \(\left\|F_-+\delta\,\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim q}[\phi(\omega)]-\Phi^\star\right\|_2^2\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S,A\) be nonempty finite sets, and let \(P\) and \(\mu_0\) be rational transition probabilities and an initial distribution for a shared MDP with horizon \(H\geq1\), encoded in unary. Features are \(\phi:S\to\mathbb{Q}^m\), with unrestricted signs; for a trajectory \(\omega\), write \(\phi(\omega)=\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\phi(s_t)\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) complete reward types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; a type is a rational vector \(w\in\mathbb{Q}^m\) specifying reward \(R_w(s,a)=\phi(s)^\top w\).
A designated cohort has rational mass \(0<\delta\leq1\). The supplied rational vector \(F_-\) is the mass-weighted contribution of fixed feasible reports from the remaining society, without dividing by \(1-\delta\); it is zero when \(\delta=1\). A supplied rational target \(\Phi^\star\) is an attainable expected feature count in this MDP.
A feasible trajectory distribution \(q\) is induced by a randomized policy starting from \(\mu_0\) and obeying \(P\); actions may depend on time and observed history, but the policy cannot control transition randomness. Represent the output by a time-dependent randomized Markov policy, and allow any minimizer.
Compute a feasible \(q\) minimizing \(\left\|F_-+\delta\,\mathbb{E}_{\omega\sim q}[\phi(\omega)]-\Phi^\star\right\|_2^2\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length apart from the unary horizon.

Conclusion

Computing an exact minimizer is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,|S|,|A|,H,L\). An exact minimizing time-dependent randomized Markov policy can be output using rational probabilities of polynomial total encoding length.

The proof is a reduction to rational convex quadratic programming. Crucially, the occupancy constraints preserve transition randomness and describe exactly the expected feature counts achievable by the allowed policies.

1. Feasible occupancy measures

For any allowed policy, define its occupancy variables by

\(x_{t,s,a}=\Pr(s_t=s,\ a_t=a)\),

where \(0\leq t\leq H-1\), \(s\in S\), and \(a\in A\). There are \(N=H|S||A|\) variables.

Let \(X\) be the set specified by the following rational linear constraints:

\(x_{t,s,a}\geq0\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a\in A}x_{0,s,a}=\mu_0(s)\quad(s\in S)\),

and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a\in A}x_{t+1,s',a}=\sum_{s\in S}\sum_{a\in A}P(s'\mid s,a)x_{t,s,a}\quad(0\leq t\leq H-2,\ s'\in S)\).

When \(H=1\), the last family is empty.

Every feasible policy gives a point of \(X\). Nonnegativity and the initial constraints follow from the definition. For the flow constraints, the MDP assumption implies that, conditional on the entire history and the current state-action pair \((s,a)\), the next-state distribution is \(P(\cdot\mid s,a)\). Summing these conditional probabilities over histories gives precisely the stated flow equation. Thus history dependence cannot circumvent these constraints.

Conversely, every point of \(X\) is realized by a time-dependent randomized Markov policy. To prove this, fix \(x\in X\), define

\(\displaystyle d_t(s)=\sum_{a\in A}x_{t,s,a}\),

and choose any fixed action \(a^\circ\in A\). Set

\(\displaystyle \pi_t(a\mid s)=\frac{x_{t,s,a}}{d_t(s)}\quad\text{if }d_t(s)>0\).

If \(d_t(s)=0\), set \(\pi_t(a^\circ\mid s)=1\) and all other action probabilities to zero.

These are probability distributions. We prove by induction that this policy has occupancy \(x\). Initially, \(\Pr(s_0=s)=\mu_0(s)=d_0(s)\). Whenever \(\Pr(s_t=s)=d_t(s)\), for a state with positive \(d_t(s)\),

\(\Pr(s_t=s,a_t=a)=d_t(s)\pi_t(a\mid s)=x_{t,s,a}\).

If \(d_t(s)=0\), both sides are zero, since nonnegativity forces every \(x_{t,s,a}=0\). The flow equation then gives

\(\displaystyle \Pr(s_{t+1}=s')=\sum_{s,a}P(s'\mid s,a)x_{t,s,a}=d_{t+1}(s')\),

completing the induction.

Therefore \(X\) describes exactly the occupancy measures of feasible policies.

Finally, \(X\) is nonempty: always choosing \(a^\circ\) defines a feasible policy. Summing the flow equations and using \(\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)=1\) shows inductively that

\(\displaystyle \sum_{s,a}x_{t,s,a}=1\)

at every time. Consequently all variables lie in \([0,1]\). As a closed, bounded set defined by finitely many linear constraints, \(X\) is compact.

2. Expected features are linear in occupancy

Define the rational matrix \(B\in\mathbb{Q}^{m\times N}\) by

\(B_{j,(t,s,a)}=\phi_j(s)\).

For a policy with occupancy \(x\), linearity of expectation gives, coordinate by coordinate,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}_q[\phi(\omega)]=\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\sum_{s,a}x_{t,s,a}\phi(s)=Bx\).

By the two directions proved above, the feasible expected feature-count set is exactly

\(\mathcal{C}=\{Bx:x\in X\}\).

In particular, replacing history-dependent policies with occupancy variables neither enlarges nor shrinks the achievable set of expected feature counts.

Write \(c=F_- - \Phi^\star\). The original optimization problem is therefore exactly

\(\displaystyle \min_{x\in X} f(x),\qquad f(x)=\|c+\delta Bx\|_2^2\).

3. The resulting quadratic program is convex

Expanding the objective gives

\(f(x)=\delta^2x^\top B^\top Bx+2\delta c^\top Bx+c^\top c\).

Discarding the constant term yields the quadratic-programming form

\(\displaystyle \min_{x\in X}\left(\frac12x^\top Qx+g^\top x\right)\),

where

\(Q=2\delta^2B^\top B,\qquad g=2\delta B^\top c\).

Both coefficients are rational. For every \(v\in\mathbb{R}^N\),

\(v^\top Qv=2\delta^2\|Bv\|_2^2\geq0\).

Thus \(Q\) is symmetric positive semidefinite, regardless of the signs of the features. The objective is continuous, and \(X\) is nonempty and compact, so a minimizer exists.

4. Exact optimization and binary complexity

We use the following standard theorem.

Exact convex quadratic programming theorem. Given a rational symmetric positive semidefinite matrix \(Q\), rational linear objective coefficients, and finitely many rational linear constraints, an exact rational minimizer can be computed in time polynomial in the total binary encoding length whenever the feasible set is nonempty and the minimum is attained. This includes singular \(Q\) and feasible sets of lower dimension. The theorem is due to Kozlov, Tarasov, and Khachiyan; their result concerns exact optimization in the Turing model. [Original paper](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189).

Their integer-data formulation also covers rational data: multiply each constraint by a positive common denominator and the objective by a positive common denominator. These operations preserve feasibility and minimizers. The binary length of a product of denominators is at most the sum of their binary lengths, so this conversion has polynomial encoding length.

All hypotheses hold here:

  • \(Q\) is rational, symmetric, and positive semidefinite.
  • The occupancy constraints are rational and linear.
  • \(X\) is nonempty and compact, so the minimum is attained.

It remains to check the size of the constructed instance. There are \(N=H|S||A|\) variables, \(N\) nonnegativity constraints, and \(H|S|\) flow and initial equalities. Equalities can be represented by pairs of inequalities.

The flow coefficients are copied from \(P\) and \(\mu_0\). Each entry of \(Q\) or \(g\) is obtained by summing at most \(m\) products of a fixed number of input rational numbers. Products add binary lengths. A sum of \(m\) rationals can be represented using the product of their denominators, whose binary length is bounded by the sum of their denominator lengths. Hence these coefficients have polynomial binary length and can be computed by polynomial-time rational arithmetic.

There are \(N^2\) entries of \(Q\). Consequently the entire quadratic program has encoding length polynomial in \(m,|S|,|A|,H,L\). The unary encoding of \(H\) ensures that introducing one layer per time step is a polynomial-size construction.

The theorem therefore produces an exact rational minimizing occupancy vector \(x^\star\) in polynomial time. Its encoding length is polynomial as well, since the algorithm outputs it within polynomial time.

5. Producing the requested policy

Apply the construction from Section 1 to \(x^\star\):

\(\displaystyle d_t^\star(s)=\sum_a x^\star_{t,s,a},\qquad \pi_t^\star(a\mid s)=\frac{x^\star_{t,s,a}}{d_t^\star(s)}\)

when \(d_t^\star(s)>0\), using the fixed action \(a^\circ\) when the denominator is zero.

Summation, exact comparison with zero, and rational division all take polynomial time on these polynomial-length rational numbers. The output contains \(H|S||A|\) action probabilities and has polynomial total encoding length.

The induction already proved shows that its induced trajectory distribution \(q^\star\) has occupancy \(x^\star\). Thus

\(\displaystyle \left\|F_-+\delta\,\mathbb{E}_{q^\star}[\phi(\omega)]-\Phi^\star\right\|_2^2=f(x^\star)\),

which is the minimum over every allowed feasible trajectory distribution. This is the requested exact solution.

6. Society data and the cited claim

For this explicitly supplied objective, the reward types and their masses impose no additional constraints on \(q\). Their influence is already summarized by the supplied \(F_-\), \(\delta\), and \(\Phi^\star\). Reading their support list is polynomial in its input length; neither enumerating possible reward types nor expanding rational masses into finitely many demonstrators is necessary.

Target attainability also causes no difficulty. If \(\delta=1\), then \(F_-=0\), and attainability implies that the minimum is zero. For smaller \(\delta\), attainability of \(\Phi^\star\) alone does not assert that zero is achievable, but the optimization proof applies unchanged.

The cited paper’s Proposition 1 and Equation (6) formulate a related occupancy-based constrained least-squares problem for finitely many demonstrators. [Fickinger et al., Section 2.2](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/neurips20ws-mpag). The proof above establishes the stated rational mass-society problem directly, including exact arithmetic and recovery of a feasible randomized Markov policy.
