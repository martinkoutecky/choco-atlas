The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), integer weights \(a_t\), and rational quota fraction \(q\), consider every integer \(N\) for which \(N\mu_t\) and \(Nq\) are integers. Form the weighted voting game with \(N\mu_t\) players of type \(t\), each of weight \(a_t\), and quota \(Nq\). If \(\eta_t^{(N)}\) is the common nucleolus payoff of type \(t\), determine whether \(u_t^{(N)}=N\eta_t^{(N)}\) converges along admissible \(N\to\infty\); compute the limit if it exists, and otherwise determine nonconvergence.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Each type has a binary-encoded weight \(a_t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). Put \(A=\sum_t\mu_ta_t\), and require a binary-rational quota \(0<q\le A\).
For each admissible positive integer \(N\), meaning \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\) for every type and \(Nq\in\mathbb Z\), form a game with \(N\mu_t\) players of weight \(a_t\). A coalition has value \(1\) exactly when its weight is at least \(Nq\), and otherwise value \(0\).
Use the nucleolus over nonnegative payoff vectors summing to \(1\), without additional singleton lower bounds. Let \(\eta_t^{(N)}\) denote the common payoff of players of type \(t\), and put \(u_t^{(N)}=N\eta_t^{(N)}\). The computational problem is to decide convergence of this vector along all admissible \(N\to\infty\) and, when it converges, compute its limit to any requested coordinatewise error \(2^{-k}\).
Is this convergence-and-limit problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? Measure running time in \(m\), \(\tau\), \(L\), and \(k\), where \(m\) is the number of distinct supported weights and \(L\) is the total binary input length.
Discrete nucleolus computation has a pseudopolynomial algorithm polynomial in the number of players and maximum integer weight ([Elkind–Pasechnik](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.0298)); binary-weight computation is NP-hard ([Elkind et al.](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/paul.goldberg/papers/wtgames.pdf)).
For strictly interior normalized quotas, eventual coincidence of replicated-game nucleoli with normalized weights is already established ([Kurz–Napel–Nohn](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.0004)); this covers the replication family above when \(0<q<A\).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted integer weights to nonnegative values, as required by the discrete definition; zero weights remain allowed.
  • Required a nonempty support and 0<q<=A, preserving a winning grand coalition and excluding a winning empty coalition; retained the boundary q=A.
  • Interpreted q as quota per unit population, so its upper bound is A rather than necessarily 1.
  • Specified the source's feasible payoff domain: nonnegative payoffs summing to 1, without singleton-value lower bounds.
  • Specified limit computation through arbitrary binary precision k because the graded question supplied no representation for potentially nonrational limits.
  • Defined m as the number of distinct supported weight values and L as total binary input length.

1. Result

The convergence-and-limit problem is polynomial-time solvable. Every valid input has a limit, and that limit can be computed exactly as a rational vector.

Define the total mass of positive-weight types by \( p=\sum_{t:a_t>0}\mu_t. \)
Since \(A\ge q>0\), we have \(p>0\). The limit is
\( u_t^\infty= \begin{cases} a_t/A,&0<q<A,\\ 1/p,&q=A\text{ and }a_t>0,\\ 0,&q=A\text{ and }a_t=0. \end{cases} \)

The interior case follows from the supplied replication theorem. The boundary case requires a different formula, proved below.

2. Admissibility and the interior case

Let \(D\) be the least common multiple of the reduced denominators of all masses and of the quota. For a reduced rational number \(b/d\), its product with an integer \(N\) is integral exactly when \(d\) divides \(N\). Consequently, the admissible positive integers are exactly the positive multiples of \(D\). In particular, they form an unbounded sequence.

Suppose \(0<q<A\). Set
\( M=\max_t a_t,\qquad Q=q/A,\qquad \theta=\min\{Q,1-Q\}>0. \)
For each distinct supported weight \(b\), define
\( \sigma_b=\sum_{t:a_t=b}\mu_t,\qquad \sigma_{\min}=\min_b\sigma_b>0. \)
In the game for an admissible \(N\), weight \(b\) occurs \(N\sigma_b\) times. Its normalized quota is \(Q\), and a player of type \(t\) has normalized weight \(a_t/(NA)\).

Proposition 2 of [Kurz–Napel–Nohn](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.0004) states that, for integer weights, the nucleolus equals normalized weights whenever the minimum weight multiplicity times the smaller normalized quota margin exceeds twice the number of distinct weights times the squared maximum weight. Here its sufficient condition is
\( \theta N\sigma_{\min}>2mM^2. \)
All these parameters except \(N\) are fixed, so this condition holds for every sufficiently large admissible \(N\).

There is one domain convention to check. The cited paper imposes singleton lower bounds. If \(Nq>M\), every singleton is losing, so those lower bounds are precisely nonnegativity. Thus its domain agrees with the domain in the question for every sufficiently large admissible \(N\).

It follows that eventually
\( \eta_t^{(N)}=\frac{a_t}{NA}, \qquad u_t^{(N)}=N\eta_t^{(N)}=\frac{a_t}{A} \)
for every supported type \(t\). This proves convergence along all admissible integers, including for zero-weight types.

3. Boundary case: a direct nucleolus proof

Suppose \(q=A\), and fix any admissible \(N\). Let \(I\) be the player set, \(P\) its positive-weight players, and \(Z=I\setminus P\). Write
\( r=|P|=Np\ge1,\qquad d=|Z|. \)

The total player weight is \(NA=Nq\). Omitting any positive-weight player decreases coalition weight strictly below the quota; omitting zero-weight players does not change it. Therefore
\( v(S)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P\subseteq S. \)

We prove that the unique lexicographic minimizer of the coalition excesses is
\( x_i^*= \begin{cases} 1/r,&i\in P,\\ 0,&i\in Z. \end{cases} \)
For any feasible payoff vector \(x\), write \(x(S)=\sum_{i\in S}x_i\) and \(e(S,x)=v(S)-x(S)\). Excesses are sorted in nonincreasing order.

Payoffs to zero-weight players must vanish

At \(x^*\), every winning coalition has excess zero, and every losing coalition has nonpositive excess. Hence its maximum excess is zero.

If a feasible vector \(x\) gives positive total payoff \(z=x(Z)>0\) to zero-weight players, then
\( e(P,x)=1-x(P)=z>0. \)
Its sorted excess vector is therefore lexicographically worse than that of \(x^*\). Any minimizer must satisfy \(x(Z)=0\). By nonnegativity, each player in \(Z\) then receives zero.

If \(r=1\), feasibility already forces the sole positive-weight player to receive one. Thus the formula is proved in this case.

Positive-weight players must share equally

Assume \(r\ge2\), and consider a feasible \(x\) with \(x(Z)=0\).

First suppose some \(i\in P\) has \(x_i=0\). Every winning coalition and every coalition contained in \(Z\) has excess zero. These two disjoint families contain \(2^d+2^d\) coalitions. In addition, the losing singleton \(\{i\}\) has excess zero.

At \(x^*\), a losing coalition has zero excess exactly when it is contained in \(Z\). Thus \(x^*\) has exactly \(2^{d+1}\) zero excesses, while \(x\) has strictly more. All excesses at either vector are nonpositive. Immediately after the common initial block of zeros, \(x\) still has zero while \(x^*\) has a negative entry. Hence \(x\) is lexicographically worse.

It remains to consider vectors with \(x_i>0\) for every \(i\in P\). Such vectors have exactly the same \(2^{d+1}\) zero excesses as \(x^*\). Put
\( c=\min_{i\in P}x_i. \)
Every losing coalition whose excess is negative contains a positive-weight player, so its payoff is at least \(c\). Conversely, a singleton attaining \(c\) is losing because \(r\ge2\). Consequently, the largest negative excess is exactly \(-c\).

Since the positive-player payoffs sum to one, \(c\le1/r\), with equality only when every such payoff is \(1/r\). Therefore, if \(x\ne x^*\), then \(c<1/r\). Its first negative excess, \(-c\), is strictly larger than the first negative excess \(-1/r\) at \(x^*\). Again, \(x\) is lexicographically worse.

This proves the claimed nucleolus formula for every admissible \(N\). Consequently,
\( u_t^{(N)}= \begin{cases} N/r=1/p,&a_t>0,\\ 0,&a_t=0. \end{cases} \)
The boundary vector is already constant along the admissible sequence.

4. Polynomial-time computation

The algorithm computes \(A=\sum_t\mu_ta_t\) exactly, compares \(q\) with \(A\), and returns the corresponding formula from Section 1. In the boundary case, it also computes \(p=\sum_{t:a_t>0}\mu_t\).

These operations have polynomial bit complexity. Indeed, the product of the input mass denominators has bit length at most their total encoded length. Using that product as a common denominator, the numerators for \(A\) and \(p\), and the rational coordinates above, all have bit length polynomial in \(L\) and \(\tau\). Exact comparison, addition, multiplication, and division therefore suffice in polynomial time.

Exact rational output already satisfies every requested error. If dyadic approximations are required, output
\( \widehat u_t=2^{-k}\left\lfloor 2^k u_t^\infty\right\rfloor. \)
Then \(0\le u_t^\infty-\widehat u_t<2^{-k}\), and the required integer arithmetic has bit length polynomial in \(L+\tau+k\).

Thus the running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k\). Computing any large replicated game, or reaching the eventual-coincidence threshold, is unnecessary. The hardness of computing a particular finite game's nucleolus does not obstruct this explicit limit computation.
