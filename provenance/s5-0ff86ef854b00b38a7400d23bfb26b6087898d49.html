The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type graph \(H=(T,E)\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), capacities \(b_t\), and deal revenues \(w_{tu}\), define each coalition's worth by \(\Gamma(z)=\max_{q\ge 0}\sum_{\{t,u\}\in E}w_{tu}q_{tu}\), subject to \(\sum_{u\ne t:\{t,u\}\in E}q_{tu}+2q_{tt}\le b_tz_t\) for every \(t\), where \(0\le z\le\mu\) and \(q_{tt}\) counts deals between distinct companies of type \(t\). Compute the largest \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) and nonnegative per-company payments \(p_t\) such that \(\sum_t\mu_tp_t\le\Gamma(\mu)\) and \(\sum_tz_tp_t\ge\alpha\Gamma(z)\) for every coalition composition \(z\le\mu\).

The input includes an undirected type graph \(H=(T,E)\), possibly with loops, positive integer per-company capacities \(b_t\), and rational deal revenues \(w_{tu}\). Let \(m=|E|\) and let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Result

The largest universally guaranteed factor is \(\boxed{\alpha=1}\). Every instance admits an exact core payment, and such a payment can be computed exactly in \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations.

The proof applies directly to the stated continuous model, including loops, arbitrary rational revenues, and all real coalition compositions.

The capacity LP and its dual

Define the matrix \(A\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times E}\) as follows. A nonloop edge \(\{t,u\}\) has a column with entries \(1\) at \(t\) and \(u\), and zero elsewhere. A loop \(\{t,t\}\) has a column with entry \(2\) at \(t\), and zero elsewhere. Also define \(c(z)_t=b_tz_t\).

Then the given coalition value is

\(\displaystyle \Gamma(z)=\max\{w^\top q:Aq\le c(z),\ q\ge0\}.\)

This LP is feasible because \(q=0\) is feasible. Every column of \(A\) sums to \(2\), so every feasible \(q\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle 2\sum_{e\in E}q_e=\sum_{t\in T}(Aq)_t\le\sum_{t\in T}b_tz_t.\)

Thus its feasible region is closed and bounded, and its maximum is finite and attained. This also covers negative revenues; in particular, \(\Gamma(z)\ge0\).

The dual LP is

\(\displaystyle \min\left\{\sum_{t\in T}b_tz_ty_t:y\ge0,\ A^\top y\ge w\right\}.\)

Its edge constraints are precisely

\(\displaystyle y_t+y_u\ge w_{tu}\qquad\text{for every nonloop }\{t,u\}\in E,\)

and

\(\displaystyle 2y_t\ge w_{tt}\qquad\text{for every loop }\{t,t\}\in E.\)

The dual feasible region is independent of \(z\). It is nonempty: if \(M=\max(\{0\}\cup\{w_e:e\in E\})\), the vector \(y_t=M\) satisfies every constraint.

Strong LP duality therefore applies: the primal is feasible with finite optimum, and the dual has an attained optimum with the same value.

Construction and proof of the exact core

Choose an optimal dual solution \(y^*\) for the grand coalition \(z=\mu\), and define

\(\displaystyle p_t=b_ty_t^*\qquad(t\in T).\)

Since \(b_t>0\) and \(y_t^*\ge0\), these payments are nonnegative. Strong duality gives exact budget balance:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\mu_tp_t=\sum_{t\in T}b_t\mu_ty_t^*=\Gamma(\mu).\)

Now fix an arbitrary real composition \(0\le z\le\mu\), and let \(q\) be any feasible deal vector for that coalition. Dual feasibility, followed by primal feasibility, gives

\(\displaystyle w^\top q\le (A^\top y^*)^\top q=(y^*)^\top Aq\le(y^*)^\top c(z)=\sum_{t\in T}z_tp_t.\)

The first inequality uses \(A^\top y^*\ge w\) and \(q\ge0\); the second uses \(Aq\le c(z)\) and \(y^*\ge0\). Maximizing over feasible \(q\) yields

\(\displaystyle \Gamma(z)\le\sum_{t\in T}z_tp_t.\)

Because \(z\) was arbitrary, \(p\) satisfies every coalition inequality with \(\alpha=1\).

This is the largest factor in the stipulated interval. Moreover, whenever \(\Gamma(\mu)>0\), the grand-coalition inequality and budget constraint themselves imply

\(\displaystyle \alpha\Gamma(\mu)\le\sum_t\mu_tp_t\le\Gamma(\mu),\)

hence \(\alpha\le1\), even if larger factors were permitted.

A finite characterization of the coalition inequalities

The infinitely many coalition inequalities also have an exact finite characterization. For any fixed \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) and \(p\ge0\), they hold if and only if

\(\displaystyle \frac{p_t}{b_t}+\frac{p_u}{b_u}\ge\alpha w_{tu}\qquad\text{for every nonloop }\{t,u\}\in E,\)

and

\(\displaystyle \frac{2p_t}{b_t}\ge\alpha w_{tt}\qquad\text{for every loop }\{t,t\}\in E.\)

Sufficiency. Set \(y_t=p_t/b_t\). These constraints say \(A^\top y\ge\alpha w\). For every coalition-feasible \(q\),

\(\displaystyle \alpha w^\top q\le y^\top Aq\le y^\top c(z)=z^\top p.\)

Taking the maximum over \(q\) proves \(z^\top p\ge\alpha\Gamma(z)\).

Necessity for a nonloop. Since both masses are positive, choose

\(\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon\le\min\{b_t\mu_t,b_u\mu_u\}.\)

Consider the composition with \(z_t=\varepsilon/b_t\), \(z_u=\varepsilon/b_u\), and all other coordinates zero. The deal vector consisting of \(\varepsilon\) units on this edge is feasible, so \(\Gamma(z)\ge\varepsilon w_{tu}\). The coalition inequality therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon\left(\frac{p_t}{b_t}+\frac{p_u}{b_u}\right)=z^\top p\ge\alpha\Gamma(z)\ge\alpha\varepsilon w_{tu}.\)

Dividing by \(\varepsilon>0\) proves the required constraint.

Necessity for a loop. Choose \(0<\varepsilon\le b_t\mu_t/2\), let \(z_t=2\varepsilon/b_t\), and set all other coordinates to zero. The feasible deal vector \(q_{tt}=\varepsilon\) gives, by the same argument, \(2p_t/b_t\ge\alpha w_{tt}\).

The coefficient \(2\) is exactly the loop convention in the question: each loop deal consumes two units of that type’s capacity.

Polynomial-time computation and the discrete comparison

The algorithm solves the grand-coalition dual once and then computes \(p_t=b_ty_t^*\). The dual has \(\tau\) variables, \(m\) edge constraints, and \(\tau\) nonnegativity constraints.

Its coefficients are rational. Computing \(b_t\mu_t\) increases their binary lengths by at most the combined lengths of the corresponding input numbers. Even a dense encoding of the LP has length \(O(L+\tau(m+\tau))\). The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming returns an exact rational optimum in time polynomial in the LP dimensions and encoding length; its hypotheses hold here by the feasibility and finite-optimum arguments above. Consequently, the total running time, including forming the payments, is \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations. See [Khachiyan’s polynomial-time LP theorem](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

This algorithm uses only the listed support. It requires no expansion of rational masses into players or of capacities into individual capacity units.

The discrete comparison does not impose a \(\frac23\) limitation here. In the discrete game, coalition worth is defined by integral deal vectors, while the cited mechanism uses a fractional LP relaxation. Here the defining worth is already the fractional LP optimum, so strong duality pays for the full dual allocation without rounding or an integrality loss. The integral model and its fractional relaxation are distinguished explicitly in [Xiao, Lu and Fang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.01442v2).
