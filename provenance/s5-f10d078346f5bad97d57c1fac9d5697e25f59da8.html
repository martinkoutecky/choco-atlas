The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given weak-ranking types \(t\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and a finite allocation cross-section of positive masses \(\eta_{t,\mathbf b}\) receiving integral bundles \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^m\), decide whether every positive-mass recipient’s bundle SD-dominates every other recipient’s bundle according to the evaluator’s type. For SD-EF1, allow deletion of one good from each compared recipient bundle before testing dominance.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq1\) good categories. A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) weak-ranking types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
Each type specifies a weak total order \(\succcurlyeq_t\) on the categories, with ties explicitly represented. Supplies \(q_g\geq0\) are binary rationals per capita.
The given allocation cross-section consists of \(K\) distinct pairs \((t,\mathbf b)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\eta_{t,\mathbf b}\), where \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^m\) has binary-encoded coordinates. It satisfies \(\sum_{\mathbf b}\eta_{t,\mathbf b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,\mathbf b}\eta_{t,\mathbf b}b_g=q_g\) for every category \(g\).
Define \(\mathbf b\succcurlyeq_t^{SD}\mathbf c\) when \(\sum_{h:\,h\succcurlyeq_t g}b_h\geq\sum_{h:\,h\succcurlyeq_t g}c_h\) for every category \(g\); tied categories are included together.
SD-EF requires \(\mathbf b\succcurlyeq_t^{SD}\mathbf c\) for every ordered pair of positive-mass cells \((t,\mathbf b),(s,\mathbf c)\), including cells of the same type.
SD-EF1 requires, for each such comparison with \(\mathbf c\neq\mathbf0\), some category \(g\) with \(c_g>0\) such that \(\mathbf b\succcurlyeq_t^{SD}(\mathbf c-\mathbf e_g)\), where \(\mathbf e_g\) is the unit vector. Empty compared bundles automatically satisfy the condition. Deletion is from the compared bundle only and may depend on the ordered pair.
Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,K,L\).

For each of SD-EF and SD-EF1, is verification of a given allocation cross-section in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete allocations of indivisible goods, SD-EF and SD-EF1 verification with weak rankings admits an \(\mathcal O(nm)\)-time algorithm, where \(n\) counts agents and \(m\) counts individual items ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16816v1)). No result settling exactly the mass-input verification problems above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one so that supplies have their stated per-capita meaning.
  • Required the cross-section to exhaust each type's mass and allocate every category's supply, matching the discrete complete-allocation requirement.
  • Treated categories as repeatable goods with nonnegative integer multiplicities, as specified by the graded question; encoded multiplicities in binary.
  • Made ties explicit and included entire indifference classes in each SD threshold.
  • Interpreted SD-EF1 deletion as one copy from the compared recipient's bundle, chosen separately for each ordered comparison.
  • Made comparisons against empty bundles automatically satisfied, resolving the literal existential-deletion defect in accordance with the intended up-to-one-good condition and verification algorithm.
  • Included comparisons between different bundles received by the same type, and defined K and L to account for the explicit allocation input.

Result

Both \(\mathrm{SD\text{-}EF}\) and \(\mathrm{SD\text{-}EF1}\) verification are in \(\mathrm P\). Each can be verified using \(\mathcal O(\tau Km)\) integer additions and comparisons, or \(\mathcal O(\tau KmL)\) bit operations.

The [cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16816v1) treats allocations of individually listed items. The proof below operates directly on the binary-encoded category counts, without expanding either bundle multiplicities or population masses.

1. Cumulative counts at indifference classes

For each type \(t\), write its weak ranking as ordered, nonempty indifference classes \(C_{t,1},\ldots,C_{t,r_t}\), from best to worst, where \(r_t\leq m\). Define \(P_{t,i}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{i}C_{t,j}\), and, for a bundle \(\mathbf x\), define its cumulative count by

\(B_{t,i}(\mathbf x)=\sum_{g\in P_{t,i}}x_g.\)

If \(g\in C_{t,i}\), then the set of categories weakly preferred to \(g\) is exactly \(P_{t,i}\). Consequently, the given definition implies

\(\mathbf b\succcurlyeq_t^{SD}\mathbf c\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B_{t,i}(\mathbf b)\geq B_{t,i}(\mathbf c)\text{ for every }i\in\{1,\ldots,r_t\}.\)

Thus only complete indifference-class prefixes are checked.

2. A complete criterion for a single comparison

Lemma. For any type \(t\) and nonnegative integer bundles \(\mathbf b,\mathbf c\):

  • The \(\mathrm{SD\text{-}EF}\) comparison holds exactly when \(B_{t,i}(\mathbf c)-B_{t,i}(\mathbf b)\leq0\) for every \(i\).
  • The \(\mathrm{SD\text{-}EF1}\) comparison, with the stipulated convention for an empty compared bundle, holds exactly when \(B_{t,i}(\mathbf c)-B_{t,i}(\mathbf b)\leq1\) for every \(i\).

Proof. The first assertion is the cumulative-count characterization above.

For the second assertion, first suppose that \(\mathbf c=\mathbf0\). The comparison automatically holds, and every difference is \(-B_{t,i}(\mathbf b)\leq0\), so the proposed inequalities also hold.

Now suppose that \(\mathbf c\neq\mathbf0\).

For necessity, let \(g\) be a permissible deletion witnessing the comparison. For every prefix,

\(B_{t,i}(\mathbf c-\mathbf e_g)=B_{t,i}(\mathbf c)-\mathbf1_{\{g\in P_{t,i}\}}.\)

The dominance inequalities therefore give

\(B_{t,i}(\mathbf c)-B_{t,i}(\mathbf b)\leq\mathbf1_{\{g\in P_{t,i}\}}\leq1.\)

For sufficiency, assume that every cumulative-count difference is at most one. Let \(j\) be the first indifference class containing a category with positive count in \(\mathbf c\), and choose such a category \(g\in C_{t,j}\).

If \(i<j\), then \(B_{t,i}(\mathbf c)=0\) and the deleted category is outside the prefix. Hence

\(B_{t,i}(\mathbf c-\mathbf e_g)=0\leq B_{t,i}(\mathbf b).\)

If \(i\geq j\), the deleted category belongs to the prefix, so the assumed inequality yields

\(B_{t,i}(\mathbf c-\mathbf e_g)=B_{t,i}(\mathbf c)-1\leq B_{t,i}(\mathbf b).\)

Every required dominance inequality holds. This proves sufficiency and the lemma. \(\square\)

In particular, deleting any good from the compared bundle’s highest occupied indifference class suffices whenever any deletion can suffice.

3. Checking all comparisons through extrema

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the set of the \(K\) positive-mass cells, and let \(\mathcal A_t=\{(s,\mathbf x)\in\mathcal A:s=t\}\).

Every \(\mathcal A_t\) is nonempty: otherwise its cell masses would sum to zero, contradicting \(\mu_t>0\).

For each type \(t\) and prefix \(i\), define

\(M_{t,i}=\max_{(s,\mathbf c)\in\mathcal A}B_{t,i}(\mathbf c),\)

\(a_{t,i}=\min_{(t,\mathbf b)\in\mathcal A_t}B_{t,i}(\mathbf b).\)

Proposition. The cross-section satisfies:

  • \(\mathrm{SD\text{-}EF}\) exactly when \(M_{t,i}\leq a_{t,i}\) for every \(t,i\).
  • \(\mathrm{SD\text{-}EF1}\) exactly when \(M_{t,i}\leq a_{t,i}+1\) for every \(t,i\).

Proof. Fix \(d\in\{0,1\}\). By the lemma, the corresponding property is equivalent to

\(B_{t,i}(\mathbf c)\leq B_{t,i}(\mathbf b)+d\)

for every type \(t\), prefix \(i\), own cell \((t,\mathbf b)\in\mathcal A_t\), and compared cell \((s,\mathbf c)\in\mathcal A\).

If \(M_{t,i}\leq a_{t,i}+d\), then every such pair satisfies

\(B_{t,i}(\mathbf c)\leq M_{t,i}\leq a_{t,i}+d\leq B_{t,i}(\mathbf b)+d.\)

Conversely, the finite, nonempty sets defining both extrema contain cells attaining them. Applying the universal pairwise inequality to these two cells gives \(M_{t,i}\leq a_{t,i}+d\). Comparisons within the same type are included throughout. \(\square\)

4. Algorithm and running time

For each type \(t\), perform the following steps.

  • Assign every category its indifference-class index.
  • Initialize arrays for the prefix maxima \(M_{t,i}\) and own-type minima \(a_{t,i}\).
  • For each of the \(K\) cells \((s,\mathbf x)\), accumulate its category counts into the classes of \(t\), then take cumulative sums. Update all prefix maxima; if \(s=t\), also update all own-type minima.
  • Check the inequalities in the proposition, using threshold zero for \(\mathrm{SD\text{-}EF}\) or threshold one for \(\mathrm{SD\text{-}EF1}\).

A cell requires \(\mathcal O(m+r_t)=\mathcal O(m)\) integer operations. Thus the total is \(\mathcal O(\tau Km)\), including the \(\mathcal O(\tau m)\) class-index preparation and final comparisons.

For bit complexity, let \(H\) be the largest bit length of a bundle coordinate. Every cumulative count is a sum of at most \(m\) such coordinates and has at most \(H+\lceil\log_2 m\rceil+1\) bits. Since the rankings and bundle coordinates are explicitly encoded, \(H\leq L\) and \(\log_2 m=\mathcal O(L)\). Additions and comparisons therefore cost \(\mathcal O(L)\) bit operations each. Including input reading gives the claimed \(\mathcal O(\tau KmL)\) bit bound.

The rational masses and supplies do not enter the fairness comparisons: validity of the cross-section is a premise, and the definitions quantify over its positive-mass cells. No denominator expansion or enumeration of individual goods is required.

This completely resolves both verification problems as polynomial-time solvable. An additional claim of \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under polynomial-time reductions would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
