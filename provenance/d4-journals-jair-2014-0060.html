The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite worker types \(T\), job classes \(G\), rational masses \(\mu\), rational per-capita supplies \(\rho\), capacities \(\zeta_t\), verified values \(v_{tg}\), and an optimal mass allocation \(y^\star\) over integral bundles \(b\), define \(F_\rho(\nu)=\max\{\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}v_t(b):\sum_b y_{t,b}=\nu_t,\ \sum_{t,b}b_g y_{t,b}\le\rho_g,\ y_{t,b}\ge0\}\) for \(\nu\le\mu\), where \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{G}\) and \(\sum_g b_g\le\zeta_t\). Under replication with \(N\mu_t\) workers of type \(t\) and \(N\rho_g\) jobs of class \(g\), compute exactly \(u_t=\int_0^1D_tF_\rho(\lambda\mu)\,d\lambda\) and \(P_t=\mu_tu_t-\sum_b y^\star_{t,b}v_t(b)\) for every \(t\) with \(\mu_t>0\), using verified values on the allocated support.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) worker types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\) job classes \(G\), binary-encoded integer capacities \(\zeta_t\ge1\), and additive signed rational values \(v_t(b)=\sum_{g\in G}b_gv_{tg}\). Jobs within a class are identical. Bundles belong to \(B_t=\{b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}:\sum_gb_g\le\zeta_t\}\), including the empty bundle.
The input includes an optimal allocation \(y^\star\), represented by its nonzero rational entries. For payment computation, only allocated jobs are available: \(G\) consists of the positively used classes and \(\rho_g=\sum_{t,b}b_gy^\star_{t,b}>0\). Verification supplies \(v_{tg}\) for every worker type and every such class, including types receiving no jobs of that class.
For \(\nu\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\), define \(F_\rho(\nu)=\max\{\sum_{t,b\in B_t}y_{t,b}v_t(b):\sum_{b\in B_t}y_{t,b}=\nu_t,\ \sum_{t,b\in B_t}b_gy_{t,b}\le\rho_g,\ y_{t,b}\ge0\}\); \(y^\star\) is optimal at \(\nu=\mu\).
Define \(D_tF_\rho(\nu)=\lim_{h\downarrow0}(F_\rho(\nu+he_t)-F_\rho(\nu))/h\), where \(e_t\) is the coordinate unit vector, and \(u_t=\int_0^1D_tF_\rho(\lambda\mu)\,d\lambda\), \(P_t=\mu_tu_t-\sum_b y^\star_{t,b}v_t(b)\). Positive \(P_t\) is a transfer to type \(t\). If no jobs are allocated, all outputs are zero.
Is exact computation of all \(u_t\) and \(P_t\) polynomial-time solvable, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), and the capacities \(\zeta_t\).
For finite agents with indivisible jobs and additive valuations, exact allocation-game Shapley values and their associated payments are reported to be \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete ([Greco–Scarcello](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.4224)).
For continuous transportation cost games, an algorithm computes Aumann–Shapley prices ([Samet–Tauman–Zang](https://www.tau.ac.il/~samet/papers/transpotation.pdf)); this is a nearby result, not a verified complexity classification of the exact problem above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized worker masses to sum to one, so job supplies are quantities per unit total worker mass.
  • Specified positive integer capacities, signed rational additive valuations, homogeneous job classes, and a sparse rational encoding of the supplied optimal allocation.
  • Interpreted the payment-game supplies as allocated quantities, rather than the original available supplies, because the discrete payment rule permits coalition reallocations only among allocated jobs.
  • Specified that verification reveals every worker type's value for each allocated job class, including values for jobs assigned to other types.
  • Retained empty bundles: the stated continuous model allows them, and the discrete discussion and hardness construction require unassigned agents despite the literal nonempty-allocation wording.
  • Defined the unspecified derivative as the right coordinate derivative and extended the value function to all nonnegative mass vectors.
  • Included the zero-output convention when the allocated support is empty and fixed the sign convention for transfers.

Let

When $A>0$, let $k$ be the number of distinct strictly positive values among the $v_{tg}$. Also, let $d>0$ be a rational number such that every strictly positive valuation is an integer multiple of $d$, and put
$$ M=A/d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}. $$
Such a $d$ can be computed in polynomial bit time by clearing denominators and taking a greatest common divisor.

The following are proven bit-complexity bounds:

| Task or restriction | Running time |
|---|---|
| Welfare and all right coordinate derivatives at a rational point $\nu$ | $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L+\operatorname{bits}(\nu))$ |
| Exact utilities and payments, unrestricted | $2^{O(m+\tau)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ |

All these algorithms use the capacities through their binary encodings. They do not expand a capacity into individual jobs or slots.

1. Transportation formulation and exact derivatives

For each type and class, define
$$ x_{tg}=\sum_{b\in B_t}b_g y_{t,b}. $$
The bundle formulation is exactly equivalent to
$$ F_\rho(\nu)= \max\left\{ \sum_{t,g}v_{tg}x_{tg}: \sum_gx_{tg}\le\zeta_t\nu_t,\quad \sum_tx_{tg}\le\rho_g,\quad x_{tg}\ge0 \right\}. $$

Indeed, a feasible bundle allocation satisfies
$$ \sum_gx_{tg} =\sum_b y_{t,b}\sum_gb_g \le\zeta_t\nu_t. $$
Its class quantities and welfare are preserved.

Conversely, from any feasible $x$, use the empty bundle and the bundles $\zeta_t e_g$, assigning masses
$$ y_{t,\zeta_t e_g}=\frac{x_{tg}}{\zeta_t}, \qquad y_{t,0}=\nu_t-\frac{\sum_gx_{tg}}{\zeta_t}. $$
These masses are nonnegative, sum to $\nu_t$, and reproduce $x$ and its welfare. This construction is valid for signed valuations.

The supplied allocation therefore gives, in polynomial bit time,
$$ x^\star_{tg}=\sum_b b_g y^\star_{t,b}, \qquad W_t=\sum_gv_{tg}x^\star_{tg}, \qquad \rho_g=\sum_tx^\star_{tg}. $$
Its optimality implies
$$ \sum_tW_t=F_\rho(\mu). $$

Negative valuations can be replaced by zero when computing $F_\rho$. An allocation on a negative-valued edge can be deleted without violating any upper-bound constraint and with an increase in welfare. Zero-valued edges can likewise be deleted without changing welfare. Hence this replacement preserves the optimum.

The dual is
$$ F_\rho(\nu)= \min\left\{ \sum_t\zeta_t\nu_tq_t+\sum_g\rho_gp_g: q_t+p_g\ge v_{tg},\quad q_t,p_g\ge0 \right\}. $$
The primal is feasible and bounded, so strong duality applies. Clipping any dual coordinate above $A$ to $A$ preserves feasibility and cannot increase the objective. Consequently, the value is unchanged by restricting the dual to the compact polytope
$$ Q=\left\{ (q,p)\in[0,A]^{T\cup G}:q_t+p_g\ge v_{tg} \right\}. $$

Let $Q^\star(\nu)$ denote its optimal face. Then
$$ D_tF_\rho(\nu) =\zeta_t\min_{(q,p)\in Q^\star(\nu)}q_t. $$

To justify this formula, express the dual value as the minimum of the finitely many affine functions associated with the vertices of $Q$. At a fixed $\nu$, every inactive vertex has a strictly positive objective gap. For a sufficiently small positive increment in coordinate $t$, only currently active vertices can determine the minimum. The right derivative is therefore the smallest active coefficient $\zeta_tq_t$. Minimizing a linear coordinate over the optimal face gives the same minimum.

The computational theorem used here is the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem: a rational LP can be solved in polynomial bit time, and a finite attained optimum has an optimal rational value and an optimal solution of polynomial encoding length. Our LPs have polynomially many variables and constraints, polynomial-size rational data, and nonempty compact feasible regions or faces. Thus the theorem applies both to the dual optimization and to optimization over its optimal face.

2. Ordered dual optima along the ray

Set
$$ c_t=\zeta_t\mu_t>0. $$
Introduce transformed coordinates
$$ r_t=-q_t. $$
Dual feasibility becomes
$$ p_g-r_t\ge v_{tg}, \qquad -A\le r_t\le0, \qquad 0\le p_g\le A. $$
In these coordinates the ray objective is
$$ f_\lambda(r,p) =-\lambda\sum_tc_tr_t+\sum_g\rho_gp_g. $$

2.1 The feasible set and optimal faces are lattices

For vectors in the $(r,p)$ coordinates, let $\wedge$ and $\vee$ denote componentwise minimum and maximum.

Both operations preserve feasibility. For example, for the componentwise maximum, choose an input vector attaining the larger $r_t$. Its own $p_g$ is at most the larger $p_g$, so
$$ \max(p_g,p'_g)-\max(r_t,r'_t)\ge v_{tg}. $$
For the componentwise minimum, choose an input vector attaining the smaller $p_g$. Its own $r_t$ is at least the smaller $r_t$, giving the same inequality.

For any linear objective,
$$ f_\lambda(z\wedge z')+f_\lambda(z\vee z') =f_\lambda(z)+f_\lambda(z'). $$
If $z,z'$ are optimal, both terms on the left are at least the optimum. Their sum is twice the optimum, so both are optimal.

Every optimal face therefore has a greatest vector in this componentwise order. To construct it abstractly, choose an optimal vector maximizing each coordinate separately and take their finite componentwise maximum. Compactness ensures that the coordinate maxima are attained.

Write this greatest vector as $z^+(\lambda)=(r^+(\lambda),p^+(\lambda))$, and put $q^+(\lambda)=-r^+(\lambda)$. It simultaneously minimizes every worker coordinate $q_t$ over the optimal face.

For a fixed $q$, optimality and $\rho_g>0$ force
$$ p_g=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{v_{tg}-q_t:t\in T\}\bigr). $$
Otherwise, reducing $p_g$ would strictly improve the objective. Thus $p$ is uniquely determined by an optimal $q$.

It follows that $z^+(\lambda)$ is the unique minimizer of $\sum_tq_t$ over the optimal face. Hence all derivatives can be obtained with two LPs: optimize the dual objective, then minimize $\sum_tq_t$ subject to equality with that optimal value.

2.2 Monotonicity theorem

Proven claim. If $\lambda_1<\lambda_2$, then every dual optimum $(q^1,p^1)$ at $\lambda_1$ and every dual optimum $(q^2,p^2)$ at $\lambda_2$ satisfy
$$ q^1_t\ge q^2_t\quad\text{for every }t, \qquad p^1_g\le p^2_g\quad\text{for every }g. $$

Proof. Use transformed vectors $z_1,z_2$, and define
$$ a=z_1\wedge z_2, \qquad b=z_1\vee z_2, \qquad \Delta_t=\max(r^1_t-r^2_t,0). $$
A direct calculation gives
$$ f_{\lambda_1}(a)+f_{\lambda_2}(b) -f_{\lambda_1}(z_1)-f_{\lambda_2}(z_2) =-(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)\sum_tc_t\Delta_t. $$
Optimality makes the left-hand side nonnegative; the right-hand side is nonpositive. Since all $c_t>0$, equality forces $\Delta_t=0$ for every $t$. Thus $r^1\le r^2$, equivalently $q^1\ge q^2$.

The two optimality inequalities must each be equalities. In particular,
$$ f_{\lambda_2}(b)=f_{\lambda_2}(z_2). $$
Because the worker coordinates of $b$ equal those of $z_2$, this equality says
$$ \sum_g\rho_g\bigl(\max(p^1_g,p^2_g)-p^2_g\bigr)=0. $$
Every summand is nonnegative and every $\rho_g>0$. Therefore $p^1\le p^2$. $\square$

Consequently,
$$ D_tF_\rho(\lambda\mu)=\zeta_tq_t^+(\lambda) $$
is nonincreasing in $\lambda$ for every type. This is stronger than concavity along the ray, which alone only controls the weighted sum of these derivatives.

3. Vertex coordinates and the new tractable cases

The greatest optimal vector $z^+(\lambda)$ is a vertex of $Q$. Indeed, if it were a nontrivial convex combination of two feasible vectors, linearity of the objective would make both vectors optimal. Both would be componentwise at most the greatest optimal vector. Their convex combination could equal it only if both equaled it.

We now bound the possible coordinates of vertices.

Construct a graph whose vertices are the $n$ dual coordinates $r_t,p_g$, and whose edges are the tight inequalities
$$ p_g-r_t=v_{tg}. $$
Mark a coordinate when one of its bounds is tight.

Every connected component of this graph has a marked coordinate. Otherwise, adding or subtracting a sufficiently small common amount from all coordinates of an unmarked component preserves its tight differences, its bounds, and every strictly slack inequality. This would express the point as the midpoint of two distinct feasible points, contradicting that it is a vertex.

Choose a simple path from any coordinate to a marked coordinate in its component. The path has at most $n-1$ edges. Successively using the tight differences expresses the chosen coordinate as an anchor from $\{-A,0,A\}$ plus a signed sum of at most $n-1$ valuations.

Negative valuations were already removed; zero valuations contribute nothing. If the distinct positive levels are $w_1,\ldots,w_k$, then $A$ is one of these levels. Every coordinate $q_t$ or $p_g$ at a vertex consequently belongs to
$$ \mathcal V= \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^k a_iw_i: a_i\in\mathbb Z,\quad |a_i|\le n \right\}. $$
In particular,
$$ |\mathcal V|\le(2n+1)^k. $$

Let $K$ be the number of distinct vectors $q^+(\lambda)$ attained as $\lambda$ ranges over $[0,1]$, including endpoint states. These vectors form a finite sequence: $Q$ has finitely many vertices, and changes of the active affine functions occur at finitely many parameters.

By monotonicity, each coordinate can strictly decrease at most $|\mathcal V|-1$ times. Every change of vector decreases at least one coordinate. Therefore
$$ K\le1+\tau\bigl((2n+1)^k-1\bigr). $$

If the positive valuations are multiples of $d$, the same path argument shows that every vertex coordinate is a multiple of $d$. Since $0\le q_t\le A=Md$, each coordinate can strictly decrease at most $M$ times. Thus
$$ K\le1+\tau M. $$

These bounds will yield the asserted algorithms once exact integration is shown to take time polynomial in $K$ and the input length.

4. Exact integration with a number of LP calls proportional to $K$

For a selected dual vector $z=(q,p)$, define its ray line
$$ \ell_z(\lambda)=S_z\lambda+B_z, \qquad S_z=\sum_tc_tq_t, \qquad B_z=\sum_g\rho_gp_g. $$
Write $H(\lambda)=F_\rho(\lambda\mu)$. It is a concave, continuous, piecewise-affine function, and
$$ H(\lambda)=\min_{z\text{ a vertex of }Q}\ell_z(\lambda). $$

The vector $z^+(\lambda)$ is also the state immediately to the right of $\lambda$. To see this, its $q$ coordinates simultaneously minimize all worker coordinates on the optimal face. Since $c_t>0$, it uniquely minimizes the slope $S_z$ among the active dual vectors. The inactive vertex lines have positive gaps, so this state remains optimal on a sufficiently short interval to the right.

Recursive algorithm

For an interval $[a,b]$, suppose its selected endpoint states are $z_L=z^+(a)$ and $z_R=z^+(b)$.

If their $q$ vectors coincide, monotonicity makes the marginal vector constant throughout the interval. Its contribution is
$$ u_t^{[a,b]}=\zeta_tq^L_t(b-a). $$

Otherwise, monotonicity gives $q^L\ge q^R$ with at least one strict inequality, so
$$ S_L>S_R. $$
Intersect the two lines at
$$ s=\frac{B_R-B_L}{S_L-S_R}. $$
Endpoint optimality gives $s\in[a,b]$. In fact $s>a$: equality at $a$ would make $z_R$ optimal there with a smaller slope than the selected right-hand state $z_L$.

Compute $H(s)$ and $z_M=z^+(s)$ exactly.

If
$$ H(s)=\ell_{z_L}(s)=\ell_{z_R}(s), $$
then concavity proves
$$ H(\lambda)=\ell_{z_L}(\lambda)\quad(a\le\lambda\le s), $$
and
$$ H(\lambda)=\ell_{z_R}(\lambda)\quad(s\le\lambda\le b). $$
For the first equality, the chord between $(a,H(a))$ and $(s,H(s))$ is $\ell_{z_L}$. Concavity gives $H\ge\ell_{z_L}$ there, whereas dual feasibility gives $H\le\ell_{z_L}$. The second equality follows identically.

On the interiors of these affine intervals, the corresponding right coordinate derivatives are $\zeta_tq^L_t$ and $\zeta_tq^R_t$. Here is a justification that does not assume that the ray slope determines the entire gradient: an active vertex line touching an affine interval at an interior point must have that interval’s slope, or it would fall below $H$ on one side. Comparing optimal vectors at two distinct interior parameters, monotonicity and equality of their slopes imply equality of every $q_t$, because all $c_t>0$.

The exact interval contribution is therefore
$$ u_t^{[a,b]} =\zeta_t\left(q^L_t(s-a)+q^R_t(b-s)\right). $$

If instead
$$ H(s)<\ell_{z_L}(s), $$
recurse on $[a,s]$ with states $z_L,z_M$, and on $[s,b]$ with states $z_M,z_R$.

In this second case, $s<b$. The new state differs from both endpoint states: equality of $q$ would also imply equality of $p$ and hence of the line. Thus
$$ S_L>S_M>S_R. $$
Moreover, $z_M$ occurs on an open interval immediately to the right of $s$.

Termination and bit complexity

Every unsuccessful intersection query introduces a new state strictly between its endpoint slopes. Such a state cannot be introduced again in another branch: descendant slopes lie strictly between their respective endpoint slopes, and sibling slope ranges are disjoint apart from their shared endpoint. There are at most $K$ states. The recursion therefore uses $O(K)$ queries, each requiring two rational LPs.

Every selected state is a vertex of the input-defined polytope $Q$, so its coordinates have polynomial bit length in $L$. The coefficients $S_z,B_z$, their intersection parameters, and the individual integration contributions consequently have polynomial bit length. Summing $O(K)$ rational contributions has bit complexity polynomial in $K,m,\tau,L$, including the cost of writing the outputs.

Thus the exact algorithm takes
$$ (K+1)^{O(1)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
bit time. Combined with Section 3, this proves the bounds for fixed $k$ and bounded scaled valuation range $M$.

5. An unrestricted exponential upper bound

For completeness, an explicit breakpoint bound gives the stated unrestricted exponential algorithm.

For subsets of types and classes, write
$$ c(I)=\sum_{t\in I}c_t, \qquad \rho(J)=\sum_{g\in J}\rho_g, $$
and define
$$ \mathcal R= \left\{ \frac{\rho(J)}{c(I)}: \varnothing\ne I\subseteq T,\quad \varnothing\ne J\subseteq G \right\}. $$

Proven claim. Every marginal vector is constant on each open interval between consecutive elements of
$$ \{0,1\}\cup(\mathcal R\cap(0,1)). $$

Proof. Add worker and class slacks to the transportation LP:
$$ \sum_gx_{tg}+s_t=\lambda c_t, \qquad \sum_tx_{tg}+r_g=\rho_g. $$
Negate the class rows. The resulting matrix is a reduced node–arc incidence matrix on vertices $T\cup G\cup\{o\}$. Transportation variables connect types to classes; slack variables connect their respective vertices to $o$.

The slack columns establish row rank $n$. A basis therefore consists of $n$ independent incidence columns. Its edges contain no cycle and hence form a spanning tree on the $n+1$ vertices.

Delete a basic edge and take the component not containing $o$. If its type and class sets are $I,J$, summing its balance equations shows that the corresponding basic variable is
$$ \pm\bigl(\lambda c(I)-\rho(J)\bigr). $$
This expression is not identically zero. A nonempty component containing types has $c(I)>0$; one containing classes has $\rho(J)>0$. Its only possible positive zero belongs to $\mathcal R$.

At a positive parameter outside $\mathcal R$, every feasible basis is therefore nondegenerate. Choose an optimal basic feasible solution. Strict positivity of its basic variables implies that all reduced costs are nonpositive: otherwise a sufficiently small increase in a nonbasic variable would preserve feasibility and improve welfare.

Reduced costs do not depend on the right-hand side. Within an interval avoiding $\mathcal R$, none of the basic variables can cross zero, so this basis stays feasible and optimal. Its strict positivity also persists under sufficiently small perturbations of the worker masses, giving a constant gradient throughout that interval. $\square$

There are at most
$$ (2^\tau-1)(2^m-1) $$
ratios. Enumerating and sorting them, evaluating derivatives at each intervening midpoint, and summing interval contributions gives an exact algorithm in
$$ 2^{O(m+\tau)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
bit time.

Each individual ratio and midpoint has polynomial encoding length. The final rational outputs may have exponentially many bits; the stated bound includes exact summation and output writing.

6. Fixed-dimension bounds

For fixed class prices, eliminate the worker coordinates:
$$ q_t(p)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{v_{tg}-p_g:g\in G\}\bigr). $$
Then
$$ F_\rho(\nu) =\min_{p\in[0,A]^m} \left( \sum_g\rho_gp_g+\sum_t\zeta_t\nu_tq_t(p) \right). $$
The following hyperplanes partition the cube into cells on which every $q_t(p)$ is affine:
$$ p_g=0,\quad p_g=A,\quad p_g=v_{tg}, \quad p_g-p_h=v_{tg}-v_{th}. $$
Their number is at most
$$ H_m=2m+\tau m+\tau\binom m2. $$

On a cell closure, the objective is affine and attains its minimum at a vertex. Every such vertex is the intersection of $m$ linearly independent defining hyperplanes. Enumerating these intersections supplies at most $H_m^m$ candidate prices sufficient for every $\nu$.

Each candidate produces a feasible dual vector. Hence $F_\rho$ is exactly the minimum of the resulting finite family of affine functions on the nonnegative mass domain. Restricting them to the ray, enumerating their pairwise intersections, and using the minimum active coordinate coefficient for each right derivative computes the integrals in
$$ H_m^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
bit time. Rational linear algebra gives polynomial encoding lengths for the candidate intersections.

For fixed support size, instead eliminate
$$ p_g(q)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{v_{tg}-q_t:t\in T\}\bigr) $$
and use the hyperplanes
$$ q_t=0,\quad q_t=A,\quad q_t=v_{tg}, \quad q_t-q_s=v_{tg}-v_{sg}. $$
There are at most
$$ H_\tau=2\tau+m\tau+m\binom\tau2 $$
hyperplanes, yielding the analogous bound
$$ H_\tau^{O(\tau)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

7. Payments, budget balance, and the one-class case

Once the utilities have been computed, the payments require only
$$ P_t=\mu_tu_t-W_t. $$

On every open interval where the selected state is constant, the ray slope is
$$ H'(\lambda)=\sum_tc_tq_t^+(\lambda) =\sum_t\mu_tD_tF_\rho(\lambda\mu). $$
Integrating the finitely many affine intervals gives
$$ \sum_t\mu_tu_t=H(1)-H(0)=F_\rho(\mu), $$
because zero worker mass permits only $x=0$. Optimality of the supplied allocation therefore gives
$$ \sum_tP_t=0. $$

If $A=0$, welfare and all utilities vanish. Each $W_t$ is nonpositive, and their sum is the optimal welfare zero, so every $W_t$ and payment also vanish. The stipulated no-allocated-jobs case likewise has all outputs zero.

For one class, let its supply be $\rho$, and list the distinct positive valuations as
$$ w_1>w_2>\cdots>w_k>0, \qquad w_{k+1}=0. $$
Define
$$ I_i=\{t:v_{t1}\ge w_i\}, \qquad C_i=\sum_{t\in I_i}\zeta_t\mu_t. $$
Then
$$ u_t= \zeta_t \sum_{i:t\in I_i} (w_i-w_{i+1}) \min\left\{1,\frac{\rho}{C_i}\right\}. $$
Types with nonpositive valuations have zero utility.

To prove this formula while accounting correctly for signed valuations, first delete all nonpositive-valued assignments. This preserves feasibility and weakly increases welfare. For the remaining allocation, telescoping the positive levels expresses welfare as the sum of $(w_i-w_{i+1})$ times the quantity assigned to $I_i$. Each prefix quantity is at most
$$ \min\left\{\rho,\sum_{t\in I_i}\zeta_t\nu_t\right\}. $$
Greedy allocation in decreasing valuation order attains all these prefix bounds simultaneously. Consequently,
$$ F_\rho(\nu)= \sum_{i=1}^k (w_i-w_{i+1}) \min\left\{\rho,\sum_{t\in I_i}\zeta_t\nu_t\right\}. $$
Taking right coordinate derivatives and integrating along the ray gives the displayed utility formula. At equality with a supply threshold, the right derivative of the capped term is zero; this endpoint convention does not change its integral.

Sorting and cumulative sums evaluate all one-class utilities in $O(\tau\log\tau)$ rational arithmetic operations and polynomial bit time.

8. What remains open

The new monotonicity theorem and integration algorithm establish polynomial time for a fixed number of valuation levels and pseudo-polynomial time in the scaled valuation range. They do not bound $K$ polynomially for unrestricted binary-rational valuations.

The nearby [Samet–Tauman–Zang paper](https://www.tau.ac.il/~samet/papers/transpotation.pdf), particularly Section 5, describes gradient integration through a parametric transportation procedure. Its stated procedure does not supply the missing polynomial bit-complexity bound for this input model. Likewise, the discrete Shapley-value hardness result supplied in the question does not itself constitute a reduction to these ray integrals.

An unrestricted polynomial-time algorithm or a valid hardness reduction is still required.
