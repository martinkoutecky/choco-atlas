The continuous mirror question, as graded

Here \(m=\tau\) is the mass-vector dimension; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. The input includes a polynomial \(F\in\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_m]\), given by its nonzero monomials with binary coefficients and exponents, satisfying \(F(0)=0\) and \(a_t:=\partial_tF(0)\geq0\); other coefficients may have either sign.
A state \(P=(\mathcal C,s)\) consists of a finite multiset of nonzero coalition vectors \(x\in\mathbb R_+^m\) and singleton mass \(s\in\mathbb R_+^m\), with \(s+\sum_{x\in\mathcal C}x=\mu\). The initial state is rational and has \(k\) coalitions. Singleton mass has payoff density \(a_t\); within coalition \(x\), use the Aumann–Shapley density \(p_t(x)=\int_0^1\partial_tF(\lambda x)\,d\lambda\). An existing coalition \(x\) has value \(F(x)\), whereas a singleton packet \(y\) has value \(a\cdot y\). A round consists of one parallel split followed by merges until none is profitable. An infinite merge phase is a nonterminating execution. Write \(U(P)\) for the possible results of completed rounds.
Define \(\Psi(P)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C}\sum_t x_t\max\{0,-p_t(x)\}\), \(\Phi(P)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C}F(x)+a\cdot s\), and \(V(P)=(-\Psi(P),\Phi(P))\). Inputs are promised to satisfy lexicographic nondecrease of \(V\) on every completed round, with strict increase whenever that round executes a merge.

Partial resolution: a sharper complexity classification

I use polynomial-time many-one reductions as the intended hardness convention.

The following results are proved below:

  • The promised decision problem is $\mathrm{co}\exists\mathbb R$-complete, already when $k=0$, $d\leq 10$, and every feasible coalition is fair.
  • Its restriction to $d\leq 2$ is $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete.
  • On every affirmative instance, every execution reaches a fixed point after at most $\max\{1,k\}$ rounds. This statement does not require the Lyapunov promise.
  • Many-one $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness of the full problem is equivalent to

$$ \mathrm{coNP}\subseteq\exists\mathbb R. $$

Here $\exists\mathbb R$ is the class of languages reducible in polynomial time to the existential theory of the reals, and $\mathrm{co}\exists\mathbb R$ consists of their complements.

These results substantially sharpen the notes, but they do not establish the requested many-one $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness or a polynomial-time algorithm. The remaining classification issue is stated precisely in the last section.

1. Singleton profitability forces a possible infinite merge phase

Write
$$ g(x)=F(x)-a\cdot x. $$

A coalition $x$ and a singleton packet $y$ merge profitably exactly when
$$ g(x+y)>g(x). $$

Two singleton packets whose combined vector is $w$ merge profitably exactly when
$$ g(w)>0. $$

Consequently, a merge-stage state $(\mathcal C,s)$ admits a profitable merge involving singleton mass exactly when at least one of the following holds:
$$ \exists y:\quad 0\leq y\leq s,\qquad g(y)>0, $$
or, for some $x\in\mathcal C$,
$$ \exists y:\quad 0\leq y\leq s,\qquad g(x+y)>g(x). $$

The first condition can be implemented by merging $y/2$ with $y/2$. Both conditions automatically exclude $y=0$.

Lemma 1. If a merge-stage state admits a profitable merge involving singleton mass, it admits an infinite sequence of profitable merges.

Proof. First suppose that a coalition $x$ can profitably absorb a packet $y\leq s$. Define
$$ f(t)=g(x+ty),\qquad 0\leq t\leq1. $$
Then $f(1)>f(0)$.

Choose $c$ strictly between these two values. By continuity, the set of points satisfying $f(t)=c$ is nonempty and compact. Let $u$ be its largest element. Then $u<1$, and
$$ f(t)>c\qquad(u<t\leq1). $$
Indeed, a later value at or below $c$, followed by $f(1)>c$, would produce a later preimage of $c$.

The mean value theorem on $[u,1]$ gives a point $t_*\in(u,1)$ such that
$$ f'(t_*)=\frac{f(1)-c}{1-u}>0. $$
Also $f(t_*)>c>f(0)$. Since $f'$ is continuous, there is $\delta>0$ such that
$$ t_*+\delta<1,\qquad f'(t)>0\quad(t_*\leq t\leq t_*+\delta). $$

First merge $x$ with $t_*y$. This is profitable. Set
$$ t_j=t_*+\frac{\delta}{2}(1-2^{-j}),\qquad j\geq0. $$
Subsequently merge the current coalition with $(t_{j+1}-t_j)y$. Every such packet is nonzero, and every merge is profitable because $f$ is strictly increasing on the specified interval. The total draw is bounded coordinatewise by
$$ \left(t_*+\frac{\delta}{2}\right)y\leq y\leq s. $$
Thus the sequence is legal and infinite.

For a profitable merge of two singleton packets, let $w$ be their combined vector and use
$$ f(t)=g(tw). $$
Here $f(0)=0<f(1)$. The same construction supplies $t_*$ and $\delta$. First merge $t_*w/2$ with $t_*w/2$, producing $t_*w$, and then absorb the successive packets above. The combined draw remains bounded by $w\leq s$. $\square$

This lemma uses the polynomial regularity of $F$ and the permission to choose arbitrary real packets. It uses neither fairness nor the Lyapunov promise.

2. Exact characterization and a linear round bound

Call a profitable merge involving singleton mass a singleton opportunity. Reachability below refers to legal execution prefixes ending at a merge-stage state, possibly immediately after a split.

Theorem 2. The requested universal property holds if and only if no state reachable without a singleton merge has a singleton opportunity.

If the property holds, every execution reaches a fixed point after at most $\max\{1,k\}$ completed rounds.

Proof. If a singleton opportunity is reachable without a singleton merge, Lemma 1 supplies an execution with an infinite merge phase. The universal property fails.

Conversely, suppose no such opportunity is reachable. An execution cannot perform a first singleton merge: its preceding state would be a forbidden reachable opportunity. Hence every merge in every execution is a coalition–coalition merge.

Splitting never increases the number of coalitions. Each coalition–coalition merge decreases that number by one. Therefore, throughout an execution, there are at most
$$ \max\{k-1,0\} $$
merges in total. In particular, every merge phase terminates.

Consider a completed round containing no merge. Its result $P$ is fair because the split phase retains only fair coalitions. It also has no profitable merge, by the definition of a completed merge phase. Its next split and merge phase therefore leave it unchanged:
$$ U(P)=\{P\}. $$
Taking $W=\{P\}$ in the definition of $I$ shows $P\in I$. All subsequent states equal $P$, so $V$ is constant.

Every round preceding this fixed-point round contains a merge. There can be at most $k-1$ such rounds when $k\geq1$, and none when $k=0$. The asserted bound follows. $\square$

Thus, for this continuum model, the universal property reduces entirely to excluding singleton opportunities. The finite-state Lyapunov argument in the notes is valid, but unnecessary: coalition count gives a stronger bound.

For $d\leq1$, we have $F(x)=a\cdot x$. Every coalition is fair, every possible merge has zero gain, and every initial state is already a fixed point.

3. Short certificates without universal termination tests

A failure certificate cannot naively assert that previous merge phases ended legally: absence of singleton opportunities is itself a quantified condition. The following relaxation avoids that difficulty.

Start by performing the prescribed first split. Thereafter permit:

  • Any profitable coalition–coalition merge.
  • Advancement to the next split when no coalition–coalition merge is profitable, provided the current phase has performed at least one coalition–coalition merge.

For these relaxed paths, advancement does not test absence of singleton opportunities.

Lemma 3. The requested property fails exactly when some relaxed path reaches a singleton opportunity.

A witnessing path requires at most $\max\{k-1,0\}$ coalition–coalition merges and $\max\{1,k\}$ split phases.

Proof. By Theorem 2, failure has a legal prefix without singleton merges ending at a singleton opportunity. Every earlier completed round must contain a coalition–coalition merge. Otherwise that round ends at a fixed point, from which the opportunity cannot subsequently arise. Thus this prefix is also a relaxed path.

Conversely, suppose a relaxed path reaches an opportunity. If every advancement was legal under the original dynamics, its endpoint is legally reachable.

Otherwise consider the first illegal advancement. Everything before it is legal. At that boundary there is no profitable coalition–coalition merge, by the relaxed rule. The advancement can therefore be illegal only because a singleton opportunity exists there. This already supplies a legally reachable opportunity, and Theorem 2 implies failure.

Every coalition–coalition merge reduces coalition count. Every advancement requires a merge since the previous split. These observations give the bounds. $\square$

Before any singleton merge, coalition coordinates are sums of subsets of the initial coordinate masses. More explicitly, regard each nonzero initial coordinate
$$ x^{(i)}_t e_t $$
as a labeled atom. A coalition–coalition merge combines atoms; a split releases or retains all atoms belonging to a given coordinate of a coalition. Released atoms cannot return while singleton merges are excluded.

A common denominator for all initial rational masses has $O(L)$ bits. Every subsequent coordinate lies between $0$ and $1$ and has denominator dividing that common denominator. Thus each coordinate has $O(L)$ bits. This applies to relaxed paths as well.

4. Membership in $\mathrm{co}\exists\mathbb R$ and general running time

Exact polynomial evaluation with binary exponents

For a monomial $c_\alpha x^\alpha$, put $|\alpha|=\sum_t\alpha_t$. Direct integration gives
$$ p_t(x)= \sum_{\alpha:\alpha_t>0} \frac{c_\alpha\alpha_t}{|\alpha|} x^{\alpha-e_t}. $$

Indeed, its derivative at $\lambda x$ contributes
$$ c_\alpha\alpha_t\lambda^{|\alpha|-1}x^{\alpha-e_t}, $$
and the integral of $\lambda^{|\alpha|-1}$ is $1/|\alpha|$.

Binary exponentiation evaluates powers using $O(1+\log(d+1))$ multiplication gates. All densities can share a circuit of size
$$ O\bigl(hm(1+\log(d+1))\bigr). $$
For each monomial, compute its coordinate powers and their prefix and suffix products. The derivative contribution in coordinate $t$ is then computed using $x_t^{\alpha_t-1}$ and the product of all other coordinate powers. This uses no division by $x_t$, so it remains valid on coordinate boundaries.

For explicit bounds, define
$$ A=2+m+hm\bigl(1+\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil\bigr), $$
and
$$ B=2+L+\lceil\log_2(km+2)\rceil +\lceil\log_2(d+2)\rceil. $$

Arithmetic circuits become existential real formulas by introducing a variable for each gate. Addition and multiplication gates impose, respectively,
$$ z=u+v,\qquad z=uv. $$
These equations uniquely enforce the circuit outputs. Individual evaluation or singleton-opportunity tests have formula length $O(AB)$, with polynomial degree at most two after gate encoding.

Encoding the relaxed path

Use $k$ coalition slots, with a zero vector representing an empty slot, and existentially quantify the coordinates of successive states. A Boolean flag records whether a coalition–coalition merge has occurred since the most recent split.

Each transition is a finite Boolean combination of polynomial constraints:

  • A selected pair must be nonzero and satisfy

$$ F(x_i+x_j)>F(x_i)+F(x_j). $$
The new state replaces these slots by their sum and a zero slot.

  • A phase advancement requires the flag and, for every pair of nonzero slots,

$$ F(x_i+x_j)\leq F(x_i)+F(x_j). $$

  • The split rule is also encoded exactly. For each slot $x$, introduce $r$ satisfying, coordinatewise,

$$ \bigl(p_t(x)\geq0\ \wedge\ r_t=x_t\bigr) \ \vee\ \bigl(p_t(x)<0\ \wedge\ r_t=0\bigr). $$
Fairness of $r$ is the formula
$$ H(r)=\bigwedge_t\bigl(r_t=0\ \vee\ p_t(r)\geq0\bigr). $$
The output slot is $r$ when $H(r)$ holds and zero otherwise. Singleton mass receives the released mass.

This unified split formula also handles initially fair coalitions: their occupied coordinates all have nonnegative density, so $r=x$ and $H(r)$ holds.

Allow identity transitions to pad a shorter path. Unrolling $2k+2$ transitions suffices by Lemma 3. Finally impose one of the singleton-opportunity formulas from Section 1 at the endpoint.

All quantifiers are existential. The finite conjunction over coalition pairs introduces no universal real quantifier. A conservative bound for the entire formula length is
$$ S=O\bigl((k+2)^4AB\bigr). $$
The factor accounts for unrolling the path, pair choices, state updates, and circuit copies. Since $m,k,h,\log(d+1)$ are polynomially bounded in $L$, the formula is constructible in polynomial time.

Lemma 3 proves that it is satisfiable exactly when the requested property fails. Therefore the property belongs to $\mathrm{co}\exists\mathbb R$.

Deterministic bit complexity

Canny’s theorem decides existential real formulas in polynomial space. Its hypotheses apply: our formulas have explicitly encoded rational coefficients, polynomial equations and inequalities, and only existential quantifiers. [Canny, *Some Algebraic and Geometric Computations in PSPACE*](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1988/6041.html).

A sharper time bound follows by directly exploring the execution tree. At each merge-stage state:

  • Test every singleton opportunity; reject if any exists.
  • Otherwise branch over profitable coalition–coalition merges.
  • When no merge exists, perform the next split if the phase contained a merge; otherwise finish at the fixed point.

There are at most $2k+2$ relevant operations, with at most $k^2+2$ choices per operation. Each local test has existential-formula length $O(AB)$. Consequently a conservative deterministic bit-time bound is
$$ 2^{\,O(k\log(k+2))+(AB)^{O(1)}}. $$
The algorithm uses polynomial space and has time $2^{L^{O(1)}}$. Here $m=\tau$ throughout; no types outside the supplied support are enumerated.

5. $\mathrm{co}\exists\mathbb R$-hardness at fixed degree

We use the following known theorem, with its precise representation and domain.

Polynomial positivity theorem. Given a rational polynomial $f$ of total degree at most eight, represented explicitly by its monomials, deciding whether
$$ \exists u\in[0,1]^n:\quad f(u)>0 $$
is $\exists\mathbb R$-complete. This is POS-8 in [Ouaknine and Worrell, Theorem 8, full version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.1914v3).

Let $f$ be such an input, with $H$ monomials and encoding length $L_f$. Choose a power of two $N$ satisfying
$$ n+1\leq N<2(n+1). $$
Set
$$ m=\tau=N,\qquad \mu_i=\frac1N, $$
and start entirely with singleton mass, so $k=0$.

Use coordinate $n+1$ as an auxiliary coordinate and define
$$ g(x)=x_{n+1}^2f(Nx_1,\ldots,Nx_n). $$
Write its explicit expansion as
$$ g(x)=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha x^\alpha. $$
Set
$$ M=1+\sum_\alpha |c_\alpha|\,|\alpha|, \qquad F(x)=M\sum_{i=1}^N x_i+g(x). $$

Input validity and the promise

Every monomial of $g$ contains $x_{n+1}^2$. Hence
$$ F(0)=0,\qquad a_i=M>0,\qquad d\leq10. $$
The masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one.

For every feasible coalition, $0\leq x_i\leq1$. Therefore, for $0\leq\lambda\leq1$,
$$ |\partial_i g(\lambda x)| \leq \sum_\alpha |c_\alpha|\alpha_i \leq M-1. $$
Here each remaining monomial factor has magnitude at most one. Thus
$$ p_i(x) =M+\int_0^1\partial_i g(\lambda x)\,d\lambda \geq1. $$
Every feasible coalition is fair.

Consequently all splits are identities and $\Psi=0$ in every feasible state. Each profitable merge strictly increases $\Phi$ by its defining inequality. Every completed round therefore satisfies the full Lyapunov promise, including strictness whenever it contains a merge.

Correctness of the reduction

We have
$$ \exists x\in[0,\mu]:\quad g(x)>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists u\in[0,1]^n:\quad f(u)>0. $$

For the forward implication, positivity implies $x_{n+1}>0$, and $u_i=Nx_i$ lies in $[0,1]$.

For the reverse implication, take
$$ x_i=\frac{u_i}{N}\quad(i\leq n), \qquad x_{n+1}=\frac1N, $$
and set the remaining coordinates to zero. This is feasible and gives
$$ g(x)=\frac{f(u)}{N^2}>0. $$

Since the initial state has no coalitions, a first merge is possible precisely when some $x\leq\mu$ satisfies $g(x)>0$. Lemma 1 makes such an opportunity a certificate of failure. In its absence, the initial state is a fixed point.

Thus
$$ \boxed{ \text{constructed instance has the property} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(u)\leq0\ \text{for every }u\in[0,1]^n. } $$

This is a many-one reduction from the complement of POS-8. Together with Section 4, it proves $\mathrm{co}\exists\mathbb R$-completeness.

Reduction size

Scaling a degree-eight monomial multiplies its coefficient by $N^{|\alpha|}$; it does not increase monomial count. The auxiliary square also preserves that count. Therefore
$$ k=0,\qquad d\leq10,\qquad m=\tau=N,\qquad h=H+N. $$
Coefficient bit lengths, including that of $M$, are $O(L_f+\log N)$. Even with full exponent vectors, the output length is bounded by
$$ O\bigl((H+N)(N+L_f+\log N)\bigr). $$
The construction takes polynomial bit time in $L_f$.

6. Complete classification of the quadratic case

Polynomial certificates for strict quadratic positivity

Lemma 4. Let $q$ be a rational quadratic polynomial and let $s\geq0$ be rational. If
$$ \exists y\in[0,s]:\quad q(y)>0, $$
then such a rational $y$ exists with encoding length polynomial in the encoding length of $(q,s)$.

Proof. Write
$$ q(y)=c+b^{\mathsf T}y+y^{\mathsf T}Qy, $$
where $Q$ is rational and symmetric.

The box is compact, so $q$ has a maximizer. Choose one, $y^*$, with the smallest possible number of coordinates strictly between their bounds. Let $J$ be these free coordinates; all other coordinates are rational box endpoints.

If $J$ is nonempty, stationarity within the corresponding face gives
$$ 2Q_{JJ}y^*_J+b_J+2Q_{J\bar J}y^*_{\bar J}=0. $$

The matrix $Q_{JJ}$ is nonsingular. Otherwise choose a nonzero vector $v$ in its kernel and extend it by zero outside $J$. Stationarity and $Q_{JJ}v=0$ imply
$$ q(y^*+tv)=q(y^*) $$
for every $t$. Moving along this line until a free coordinate first reaches a box boundary gives another maximizer with fewer free coordinates, a contradiction.

Hence the displayed rational linear system uniquely determines $y^*_J$. Cramer’s rule gives rational coordinates of polynomial encoding length. Indeed, clearing denominators produces integer matrices with polynomial bit lengths, and a determinant of order at most $m$ increases bit length only polynomially.

If $J$ is empty, $y^*$ is already a rational vertex. Since the maximum is positive, $y^*$ supplies the required witness. $\square$

When $d\leq2$, both singleton-opportunity polynomials
$$ g(y),\qquad g(x+y)-g(x) $$
are quadratic. Along a relaxed path, the states have polynomial encoding length by Section 3.

A certificate of failure consists of the short relaxed path and a rational packet witnessing its final opportunity. Lemma 4 bounds the packet’s encoding length polynomially. Splits, profitability, and absence of profitable coalition–coalition merges at advancement points are all checkable by exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

Thus the quadratic restriction belongs to $\mathrm{coNP}$.

Quadratic hardness construction

Let $G$ have $n_0$ vertices, and let $2\leq r\leq n_0$. Pad it with isolated vertices to obtain a power-of-two vertex count $n$, with
$$ n_0\leq n<2n_0. $$
This preserves whether an $r$-clique exists.

Let $A_G$ be its adjacency matrix. Define
$$ B_r=2r(r-1),\qquad C_r=B_r-(2r-1), $$
and
$$ q(x)=B_r x^{\mathsf T}A_Gx-C_r\left(\sum_i x_i\right)^2, $$
$$ F(x)=C_r\sum_i x_i+q(x). $$
Take $m=\tau=n$, $\mu_i=1/n$, and $k=0$.

Here $a_i=C_r>0$. Integration of the quadratic derivatives gives
$$ p_i(x) =C_r+B_r\sum_{j:\{i,j\}\in E(G)}x_j-C_r\sum_jx_j. $$
Every feasible coalition has $\sum_jx_j\leq1$, so
$$ p_i(x)\geq C_r\left(1-\sum_jx_j\right)\geq0. $$
All feasible coalitions are fair, and the Lyapunov promise follows exactly as in Section 5.

Let $\omega(G)$ denote the maximum clique size. We now prove
$$ \max_{\substack{z\geq0\\\sum_i z_i=1}} z^{\mathsf T}A_Gz =1-\frac1{\omega(G)}. $$

A maximizer exists by compactness. Choose one of minimum support. If supported vertices $i,j$ are nonadjacent, keeping $z_i+z_j$ fixed and redistributing their weights changes the objective affinely: there are no square terms or $z_iz_j$ term. An endpoint has objective at least as large, hence also maximal, and smaller support. This contradicts the choice.

The support is therefore a clique of some size $\ell$. On that support,
$$ z^{\mathsf T}A_Gz =1-\sum_i z_i^2 \leq1-\frac1\ell \leq1-\frac1{\omega(G)}, $$
where the first inequality follows from Cauchy–Schwarz. Uniform weights on a maximum clique attain equality.

The threshold lies strictly between the consecutive clique values:
$$ 1-\frac1{r-1} < \frac{C_r}{B_r} < 1-\frac1r. $$
Its distance from each endpoint is $1/(2r(r-1))$.

For any nonzero $x\geq0$, put $z=x/\sum_i x_i$. Then
$$ q(x)= B_r\left(\sum_i x_i\right)^2 \left(z^{\mathsf T}A_Gz-\frac{C_r}{B_r}\right). $$
Consequently,
$$ \exists x\geq0:\quad q(x)>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \omega(G)\geq r. $$

If $\omega(G)<r$, no initial singleton merge is profitable, so the property holds.

If $\omega(G)\geq r$, choose an $r$-clique and set $w_i=1/n$ on its vertices and zero elsewhere. Then $w\leq\mu$ and $q(w)>0$. This is a singleton opportunity, so Lemma 1 implies failure.

Therefore
$$ \boxed{ \text{constructed instance has the property} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \omega(G)<r. } $$
This proves many-one $\mathrm{coNP}$-hardness and hence $\mathrm{coNP}$-completeness for $d\leq2$.

The construction has
$$ d=2,\qquad k=0,\qquad m=\tau=n, \qquad h=\frac{n(n+3)}2. $$
Coefficients have $O(\log(n_0+1))$ bits. A conservative construction-time and output-length bound is
$$ O\bigl(n_0^3\log(n_0+1)\bigr). $$

Deterministic quadratic running time

Lemma 4 also gives a direct algorithm for each local quadratic test. Enumerate the at most $3^m$ faces of the box, specifying each coordinate as lower-bound, upper-bound, or free. On each face with nonsingular free quadratic matrix, solve the stationary linear system and test the resulting feasible point. Include all vertices.

The lemma proves that one of these candidates attains the global maximum; singular free matrices can be skipped. Each candidate is checked in polynomial bit time.

Combining this with the short execution-tree search gives
$$ 2^{\,O(k\log(k+2)+m)} \bigl((k+2)(m+2)(h+2)(L+2)\bigr)^{O(1)} $$
deterministic bit time for $d\leq2$. The failure-certificate verifier takes polynomial time in the displayed parameters.

7. What remains unresolved under many-one reductions

Let $\mathcal D$ denote the promised decision problem.

The established completeness result gives the exact equivalence
$$ \mathcal D\text{ is many-one }\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathrm{coNP}\subseteq\exists\mathbb R. $$

For the forward implication, an $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness reduction to $\mathcal D$, composed with the existential encoding of its complement, places the complement of every $\mathrm{NP}$ language in $\exists\mathbb R$.

For the reverse implication, complementing the assumed inclusion gives
$$ \mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{co}\exists\mathbb R. $$
The hardness reduction in Section 5 then reduces every $\mathrm{NP}$ language to $\mathcal D$, always producing promised inputs.

Likewise,
$$ \mathcal D\in\mathrm P \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists\mathbb R=\mathrm P. $$
A polynomial-time algorithm for $\mathcal D$ would decide POS-8 by constructing the instance in Section 5 and negating its answer. Conversely, if $\exists\mathbb R=\mathrm P$, the existential encoding of failure yields a polynomial-time algorithm.

In particular, a polynomial-time algorithm would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$. The quadratic reduction also establishes one-query polynomial-time Turing $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness, by negating the property answer.

The open point is therefore not a missing polarity reversal in the clique construction. Under the many-one convention, proving the requested $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness would establish the class inclusion above. This write-up does not prove that inclusion or the corresponding polynomial-time collapse.
