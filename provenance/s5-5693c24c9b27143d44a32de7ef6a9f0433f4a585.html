Theorem 1.4 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item categories, finite supports \(T_X,T_{SM}\) of XOS and single-minded types, rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), and private type valuations \(v_t\), find an \(\alpha\)-approximation to \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_x\sum_{t\in T_X\cup T_{SM}}\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), subject to \(x_{t,S}\ge0\), \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_j\); characterize communication complexity in \(m,\tau,L\), especially whether exact optimization is polynomial or the Theorem 1.4 approximation threshold remains hard for \(\alpha=1/(1-(1-1/|T_X|)^{|T_X|})+0.001\), with \(|T_X|\ge2\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The support is partitioned into publicly labelled sets \(T_X,T_{SM}\), with \(n=|T_X|\ge2\) and \(c=|T_{SM}|\ge1\). There are \(m\ge1\) item categories and public binary-rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\ge0\).
Each type has one cooperative representative privately holding its monotone valuation \(v_t:2^{[m]}\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\): XOS for \(t\in T_X\), single-minded for \(t\in T_{SM}\). Single-minded weights are nonnegative and desired sets may be empty.
Let \(L\) be the total public input bit length plus the maximum bit length of any valuation value; an entire private valuation need not have polynomial description length.
An allocation consists of rational bundle masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\), satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le b_j\). Define \(W(x)=\sum_t\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_x W(x)\).
Use randomized number-in-hand blackboard communication with unrestricted local computation; communication cost is worst-case expected bits, including transmission of the allocation's nonzero entries. An \(\alpha\)-approximation always returns a feasible allocation and satisfies \(\mathbb E[W(x)]\ge\mathrm{OPT}/\alpha\).

For \(\alpha=\frac{1}{1-(1-1/n)^n}+0.001\), does this mass-allocation problem admit an \(\alpha\)-approximation using \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) communication, or is superpolynomial communication necessary?

Discrete welfare maximization with \(n\ge2\) XOS bidders and \(c\) single-minded bidders requires \(2^{\Omega(\min\{c,\sqrt m\})}\) communication at this approximation factor, while a \(2\)-approximation uses polynomial communication. [Qiu–Weinberg–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06585v1)
For fractional allocations, the configuration LP with unit bidder bounds and unit item capacities is solvable in polynomial communication for discretized valuations; this is a nearby result with different right-hand sides. [Nisan–Segal](https://web.stanford.edu/~isegal/prices.pdf)

  • Used disjoint valuation-class labels even though the mathematical classes overlap; n counts XOS-labelled supported types.
  • Required at least one single-minded supported type to retain the additional-bidder setting; retained the stipulated requirement of at least two XOS types.

  • Used the discrete expected-welfare approximation convention and worst-case expected blackboard communication, with unrestricted local computation.

Answer

Polynomial communication suffices. In fact, an exact optimal mass allocation can be computed and transmitted using \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) deterministic communication.

The proof applies to arbitrary monotone, nonnegative rational valuations; it does not require their XOS or single-minded structure.

1. A packing LP with the same optimum

Let \(T=T_X\cup T_{SM}\) and \(d=\tau+m\). Consider the packing LP whose variables are \(y_{t,S}\):

\(\displaystyle \max \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}v_t(S)y_{t,S},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\subseteq[m]}y_{t,S}\le\mu_t\quad(t\in T),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\ni j}y_{t,S}\le b_j\quad(j\in[m]),\)

and \(y_{t,S}\ge0\). Denote its optimum by \(P^\star\).

Every allocation in the question is feasible for this LP, so \(P^\star\ge\mathrm{OPT}\). Conversely, given any feasible \(y\), define

\(\displaystyle x_{t,\emptyset}=y_{t,\emptyset}+\mu_t-\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}y_{t,S},\qquad x_{t,S}=y_{t,S}\quad(S\ne\emptyset).\)

This adds only empty bundles, so \(x\) satisfies all mass equalities and item constraints. Since \(v_t(\emptyset)\ge0\), it also satisfies \(W(x)\ge W(y)\). Therefore \(\mathrm{OPT}\ge P^\star\), proving

\(\displaystyle P^\star=\mathrm{OPT}.\)

This argument permits positive values for empty bundles, including single-minded types whose desired set is empty.

The packing LP is feasible and bounded: the zero vector is feasible, and each variable lies between zero and its type’s mass. Thus an optimum exists.

2. The dual has only \(d\) variables

The dual is

\(\displaystyle \min \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t q_t+\sum_{j=1}^m b_jp_j,\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle q_t+\sum_{j\in S}p_j\ge v_t(S)\quad(t\in T,\ S\subseteq[m]),\)

and \(q_t,p_j\ge0\).

Each representative first announces \(v_t([m])\). Put

\(\displaystyle V=\max_{t\in T}v_t([m]).\)

Monotonicity gives \(v_t(S)\le V\) for every bundle. If \(V=0\), assigning all mass to empty bundles is optimal, so assume \(V>0\).

For the full dual, and for any dual obtained by retaining only a subset of its bundle inequalities, an optimum can be sought in the public box

\(\displaystyle 0\le q_t\le V,\qquad 0\le p_j\le V.\)

Indeed, take any feasible nonnegative vector and cap every coordinate at \(V\). For a retained inequality, either none of its coordinates changes, or one of its coordinates becomes \(V\). In the latter case its left-hand side is at least \(V\), hence at least \(v_t(S)\). Feasibility is preserved. Because all objective coefficients are nonnegative, the objective cannot increase.

The vector \(q_t=V,\ p_j=0\) is feasible. Consequently, restricting to this box preserves the optimum, even when some supplies are zero.

3. Exact separation has polynomial communication cost

Let \(B\) be the maximum binary encoding length of a valuation value. If this bound is not initially public, each representative can compute its own maximum encoding length locally and announce that integer; their maximum determines \(B\). This uses polynomial communication, and \(B\le L\).

At a rational query point \((q,p)\), representative \(t\) computes locally a bundle

\(\displaystyle S_t\in\arg\max_{S\subseteq[m]}\left(v_t(S)-\sum_{j\in S}p_j\right).\)

The maximum exists because the bundle domain is finite. Finding it may require exponential computation, which the communication model permits.

If

\(\displaystyle v_t(S_t)-\sum_{j\in S_t}p_j>q_t,\)

the representative announces \(S_t\) and \(v_t(S_t)\). These identify an original violated dual inequality using \(O(m+B)\) bits. Otherwise it announces that none of its bundle inequalities is violated. If all representatives make the latter announcement, every private inequality is satisfied. Public constraints, including the box and any objective threshold, are checked publicly.

We use the following standard rational ellipsoid facts:

  • A bounded rational LP in \(d\) variables can be optimized exactly using polynomially many separation calls and query points of polynomial encoding length. The bound depends on \(d\), the maximum encoding length of an inequality, and the public objective and bounding data, rather than on the number of inequalities.
  • The certificate-producing feasibility procedure, when reporting infeasibility, can provide a polynomial-size infeasible subsystem consisting of public inequalities and inequalities returned by the separation oracle.

These are the oracle LP facts behind configuration-LP optimization and primal recovery; the latter recovery is described explicitly in [Roughgarden, Section 8.3, footnote 9](https://www.nowpublishers.com/article/DownloadSummary/TCS-085).

Their hypotheses hold here. Each private inequality has coefficients in \(\{0,1\}\) and a right-hand side of length at most \(B\); the objective coefficients and bounding box have polynomial encoding length. The oracle returns actual violated inequalities.

Thus the exact optimum \(z^\star=P^\star\) is computable with \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) communication. The different right-hand sides change the dual objective, but preserve its dimension and separation oracle. This is the extension needed beyond the unit-bound formulation in [Nisan–Segal, Section 7.3](https://web.stanford.edu/~isegal/prices.pdf).

Computing the value alone is insufficient. The following argument recovers an allocation.

4. Recovering a polynomial-size optimal primal

We first establish a rational gap bound, valid both for the full packing LP and for every restriction to a subset of bundle variables.

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all public masses and supplies, written in reduced form. Then \(\log_2 D\le L\).

After adding slack variables, the packing LP has \(d\) equality constraints and a matrix with entries in \(\{0,1\}\). Any basic feasible solution is determined by a nonsingular \(d\times d\) basis matrix \(A_0\). The determinant expansion gives

\(\displaystyle 1\le|\det A_0|\le d!.\)

By Cramer’s rule, every basic coordinate has denominator dividing \(D|\det A_0|\). There are at most \(d\) positive original variables. Each of their valuation coefficients has denominator at most \(2^B\). Hence the objective value of a basic solution has reduced denominator at most

\(\displaystyle K=D\,d!\,2^{dB}.\)

Every restricted packing LP is feasible and bounded and therefore has an optimal basic feasible solution. Its optimum, and the full optimum, consequently have denominators at most \(K\). Two distinct such values differ by at least \(1/K^2\), since

\(\displaystyle \left|\frac ab-\frac ce\right|=\frac{|ae-bc|}{be}\ge\frac1{K^2}\)

whenever \(b,e\le K\) and the fractions differ. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \log_2 K=O(L+d\log d+dB),\)

which is polynomial in the stated parameters.

Now run certificate-producing dual feasibility with the additional public constraint

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_tq_t+\sum_jb_jp_j\le z^\star-\frac1{2K^2}.\)

This system is infeasible, by the definition of \(z^\star\). Let \(F\) be the polynomial-size collection of bundle inequalities in its infeasibility certificate. Their bundles and valuation values have already been communicated.

Form the restricted packing LP using only variables indexed by \(F\), and let its optimum be \(z_F^\star\). Its dual retains precisely those bundle inequalities. The box preserves this restricted dual’s optimum by the capping argument in Section 2. Certificate infeasibility and strong duality therefore give

\(\displaystyle z^\star-\frac1{2K^2}<z_F^\star\le z^\star.\)

The upper bound holds because the restricted primal has fewer variables. If its optimum differed from \(z^\star\), the rational gap bound would instead imply

\(\displaystyle z^\star-z_F^\star\ge\frac1{K^2},\)

a contradiction. Thus \(z_F^\star=z^\star\).

All coefficients of this polynomial-size restricted LP are public. A designated representative can solve it locally and select an optimal basic feasible solution \(y^\star\). This solution has at most \(d\) positive bundle variables.

Finally, fill unused type masses with empty bundles as in Section 1. The resulting \(x^\star\) is feasible and has welfare at least \(z^\star=\mathrm{OPT}\); optimality forces equality.

5. Transmitting the allocation and concluding

The allocation \(x^\star\) has at most \(d+\tau=m+2\tau\) nonzero entries.

All coordinates of \(y^\star\) share a denominator dividing \(D|\det A_0|\). The public masses have denominators dividing \(D\), so filling unused mass preserves this common denominator. Each output mass lies in \([0,1]\) and therefore has encoding length

\(\displaystyle O(L+d\log d).\)

An entry additionally identifies its type and its bundle, requiring \(O(\log\tau+m)\) bits. Transmitting every nonzero entry thus costs polynomially many bits. Together with the oracle calls, the entire protocol has deterministic worst-case communication \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\).

A deterministic protocol is a special case of the permitted randomized model. Since \(W(x^\star)=\mathrm{OPT}\), it satisfies the requested approximation guarantee.

The discrete lower bound concerns allocations that cannot split one bidder across bundles. Here the LP’s fractional bundle masses are themselves permissible outputs, so no integral rounding is required. Accordingly, that lower bound does not rule out this protocol. The discrete distinction and configuration-LP approach appear in [Qiu–Weinberg–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06585v1).
