Corollary 4.6 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given fixed \(k\), rational \(\alpha,\eta>0\), and a finite-support rational society \(\mu\) over complete types \((u,r)\), where \(u\) records a voter’s true utility type and \(r\in\Sigma_k\) is their current reported ranking, let \(\nu_r=\sum_u\mu_{u,r}\). For each candidate \(j\), let \(d_j(\nu)\) be the minimum mass of reports that must change to make \(j\) win plurality under the paper’s tie-breaking rule; transfers must preserve \(u\) and respect the available mass of each type. Can one compute the exact lottery \(G(j)=[1-\rho d_j(\nu)]_+/\sum_\ell[1-\rho d_\ell(\nu)]_+\), with \(\rho=\eta/[k(k+1)\alpha^2]\), in time polynomial in \(k,\tau,L\), and establish that every supported outcome is within mass \(1/\rho\) of a plurality win and no coalition of mass at most \(\alpha\) can increase its integrated expected true utility by more than \(\eta\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix \(m=k\ge2\) candidates, strict reported rankings \(r\in\Sigma_k\), and binary rational parameters \(0<\alpha<1\) and \(\eta>0\). A true utility type is a vector \(u\in\mathbb Q^k\cap[0,1]^k\); true utilities remain fixed when reports change.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types \((u,r)\) with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_{u,r}\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length, including utilities and parameters. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Write \(\nu_r=\sum_u\mu_{u,r}\). Use plurality with the smallest candidate index winning ties.
A report change is a nonnegative real transfer array \(x_{u,r,s}\) satisfying \(\sum_s x_{u,r,s}=\mu_{u,r}\), where destination rankings \(s\in\Sigma_k\) need not occur in the input. Its changed mass is \(\sum_{u,r}\sum_{s\ne r}x_{u,r,s}\), and its resulting report masses are \(\nu'_s=\sum_{u,r}x_{u,r,s}\).
Define \(d_j(\nu)\) as the infimum changed mass over transfers making \(j\) the plurality winner. Set \(\rho=\eta/[k(k+1)\alpha^2]\) and \(G_\mu(j)=\max\{1-\rho d_j(\nu),0\}/\sum_{\ell=1}^k\max\{1-\rho d_\ell(\nu),0\}\).
A coalition selects masses \(0\le c_{u,r}\le\mu_{u,r}\) with \(\sum_{u,r}c_{u,r}\le\alpha\), redistributes only those reports while preserving each true utility type, and leaves all other reports fixed. Its integrated expected utility under a lottery \(G\) is \(U_c(G)=\sum_{u,r}c_{u,r}\sum_{j=1}^k u_jG(j)\).

Is exact computation of \(G_\mu\) possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
Does this lottery always have both guarantees: every \(j\) with \(G_\mu(j)>0\) can be made a plurality winner by changing mass at most \(1/\rho\), and every coalition deviation producing \(\mu'\) satisfies \(U_c(G_{\mu'})-U_c(G_\mu)\le\eta\)?

For finite electorates and fixed \(k\), the analogous lottery is polynomial-time computable for efficiently computable rules invariant under voter permutations; a related discrete construction also bounds total coalition utility gain ([Birrell–Pass](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~rafael/papers/IJCAI11.pdf)).
No result settling this exact continuous formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one and restricted the coalition bound to 0<alpha<1, matching the discrete restriction that the protected coalition size is below the electorate size.
  • Represented true utility types by binary rational outcome-utility vectors in [0,1]^k, evaluated unchanged after deviations; current reports may be arbitrary strict rankings, and utility ties are permitted.
  • Selected smallest-index tie-breaking because both supplied and full versions allow an unspecified deterministic tie-breaking rule.
  • Replaced minimum by infimum: deterministic tie-breaking can make a candidate's winning region nonclosed, so a minimum changed mass need not exist.
  • Allowed divisible real transfers to any strict ranking, including rankings absent from the input support; transfers preserve true utility types and available source masses.
  • Defined coalition membership as selectable submass of each complete type and coalition utility as an unnormalized integral over that selected mass.
  • Set m=k, required at least two candidates, and included all utilities and rational parameters in the input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Exact computation is possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), even when \(m\) varies. Both guarantees hold. In fact, every admissible coalition deviation satisfies the stronger bound

\[ U_c(G_{\mu'})-U_c(G_\mu)\le \frac{\eta}{m+1}. \]

The proof below accounts explicitly for divisible masses, unsupported destination rankings, and possibly unattained winning-cost infima.

1. Report changes reduce to transport of first-place masses

Write \(p_i=\sum_{(u,r):\,r\text{ ranks }i\text{ first}}\mu_{u,r}\). Thus \(p\) belongs to the probability simplex \(\Delta_m\), and plurality depends only on \(p\).

For \(p,z\in\Delta_m\), define their total variation distance by

\[ \delta(p,z)=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^m|p_i-z_i| =\sum_{i=1}^m(p_i-z_i)_+, \]

where \(a_+=\max\{a,0\}\).

Transport lemma. Starting from the given society, the minimum changed report mass needed to obtain first-place masses \(z\) is exactly \(\delta(p,z)\). This is achievable while preserving true utilities.

Proof. In any transfer, the mass whose original and destination rankings both place \(i\) first is at most \(\min\{p_i,z_i\}\). Consequently, at least

\[ 1-\sum_i\min\{p_i,z_i\}=\delta(p,z) \]

changes its first-place candidate. Every such transfer changes its ranking, proving the lower bound.

For achievability, put \(a_i=(p_i-z_i)_+\), \(b_i=(z_i-p_i)_+\), and \(h=\sum_i a_i=\sum_i b_i\). If \(h=0\), leave all reports unchanged.

Otherwise, for every surplus candidate \(i\), select the fraction \(a_i/p_i\) of each input type whose ranking places \(i\) first. Redistribute each selected portion among deficit candidates \(j\) in proportions \(b_j/h\), using any fixed ranking placing \(j\) first. Surplus and deficit candidates are distinct, so all selected reports change. Each transfer retains its source utility vector. The resulting first-place masses are \(z\), and the changed mass is exactly \(h=\delta(p,z)\). \(\square\)

2. Winning-cost infima and tie-breaking

Define the closed weak-winner region

\[ W_j=\{z\in\Delta_m:z_j\ge z_i\text{ for every }i\}. \]

Under the prescribed tie-breaking, the actual winner region additionally requires \(z_j>z_i\) whenever \(i<j\).

Claim. The winning-cost infimum is

\[ d_j(p)=\min_{z\in W_j}\delta(p,z). \]

Proof. The minimum exists because \(W_j\) is nonempty and compact and \(\delta(p,\cdot)\) is continuous. Every profile where \(j\) actually wins lies in \(W_j\), so the transport lemma gives the lower bound.

Conversely, let \(z\in W_j\) attain the displayed minimum. Let \(e_j\) assign all mass to candidate \(j\), and, for \(0<\varepsilon<1\), put \(z^\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)z+\varepsilon e_j\). For every \(i\ne j\),

\[ z^\varepsilon_j-z^\varepsilon_i =(1-\varepsilon)(z_j-z_i)+\varepsilon>0. \]

Thus \(j\) is the unique plurality winner. Moreover,

\[ \delta(p,z^\varepsilon) \le \delta(p,z)+\delta(z,z^\varepsilon) =\delta(p,z)+\varepsilon\delta(z,e_j) \le \delta(p,z)+\varepsilon. \]

The transport lemma realizes these profiles, and letting \(\varepsilon\) approach zero proves the upper bound on the infimum. \(\square\)

In particular, the infimum depends only on first-place masses, although its attainment can depend on tie-breaking.

3. An explicit formula for every distance

Fix \(j\), and define, for \(t\ge0\),

\[ F_j(t)=\sum_{i\ne j}(p_i-p_j-t)_+. \]

The function \(F_j(t)-t\) is continuous and strictly decreasing. Its value at zero is nonnegative, and \(F_j(1-p_j)=0\). Hence there is a unique \(t_j\in[0,1-p_j]\) satisfying \(F_j(t_j)=t_j\).

Claim. We have \(d_j(p)=t_j\).

Proof. Let \(z\in W_j\), and set \(t=\delta(p,z)\). Each coordinate changes by at most \(t\), so \(z_j\le p_j+t\). Since \(z_i\le z_j\),

\[ (p_i-p_j-t)_+\le(p_i-z_i)_+. \]

Summing over \(i\ne j\) gives \(F_j(t)\le t\). Strict decrease of \(F_j(t)-t\) therefore implies \(t\ge t_j\).

For the reverse inequality, define

\[ z_j=p_j+t_j,\qquad z_i=p_i-(p_i-p_j-t_j)_+=\min\{p_i,p_j+t_j\}\quad(i\ne j). \]

All coordinates are nonnegative, and

\[ \sum_i z_i=1+t_j-F_j(t_j)=1. \]

Also \(z_j\ge z_i\), so \(z\in W_j\). Candidate \(j\) gains mass \(t_j\), and all other candidates together lose exactly \(F_j(t_j)=t_j\). Therefore \(\delta(p,z)=t_j\), proving the claim. \(\square\)

To compute this root exactly, observe that

\[ F_j(t)= \max_{S\subseteq[m]\setminus\{j\}} \left(\sum_{i\in S}p_i-|S|p_j-|S|t\right). \]

Consequently, \(F_j(t)\le t\) is equivalent to

\[ t\ge \frac{\sum_{i\in S}p_i-|S|p_j}{|S|+1} \quad\text{for every }S\subseteq[m]\setminus\{j\}. \]

For a fixed cardinality, the largest numerator uses the largest available scores. Let \(b_{j,1}\ge\cdots\ge b_{j,m-1}\) be the scores excluding \(p_j\), and let \(B_{j,q}=\sum_{h=1}^q b_{j,h}\), with \(B_{j,0}=0\). We obtain the formula

\[ \boxed{ d_j(p)= \max_{0\le q\le m-1} \frac{B_{j,q}-q p_j}{q+1}. } \]

The term for \(q=0\) is zero, so the maximum is always nonnegative.

4. Exact computation and bit complexity

Aggregate the first-place masses and sort them once. For each candidate \(j\), traverse this sorted list while skipping \(j\), calculate its prefix sums, and evaluate the formula above. This uses \(O(\tau+m^2+m\log m)\) arithmetic operations, in addition to reading the input.

Here is an explicit polynomial bound on the required bit operations.

Index the support by \(t\), and write \(\mu_t=A_t/B_t\) with positive integer denominators. Set \(D=\prod_t B_t\). Its bit length is \(O(L)\), and each first-place mass can be represented as \(p_i=n_i/D\), where \(0\le n_i\le D\).

The distance formula then represents each optimum as

\[ d_j=\frac{N_j}{D h_j}, \qquad N_j\ge0,\qquad 1\le h_j\le m, \]

with \(N_j\) of bit length \(O(L+\log m)\). These representations are obtained by comparing the prefix-sum fractions exactly.

Write \(\rho=A/B\), where \(A,B\) are positive integers of bit length \(O(L+\log m)\). Let \(Q=m!\), and define integer weights

\[ W_j= \max\{BDh_j-AN_j,0\}\frac{Q}{h_j}. \]

Because \(h_j\) divides \(Q\),

\[ \frac{W_j}{BDQ}=\max\{1-\rho d_j,0\}. \]

Thus the exact output is

\[ G_\mu(j)=\frac{W_j}{\sum_{\ell=1}^mW_\ell}. \]

The denominator is positive: the current plurality winner has \(d_j=0\), hence unnormalized weight one.

All integers used here have bit length \(O(L+m\log(m+1))\). Using ordinary quadratic-time integer arithmetic gives, for example, the valid bound

\[ O\!\left( L+(\tau+m^2)\bigl(L+m\log(m+1)\bigr)^2 \right) \]

bit operations. No enumeration of the \(m!\) rankings is required; \(m!\) above is merely a common-denominator integer.

5. Both guarantees

Supported candidates are attainable within the stated budget

If \(G_\mu(j)>0\), then \(1-\rho d_j>0\), so \(d_j<1/\rho\).

Choose \(0<\varepsilon<\min\{1,1/\rho-d_j\}\). Section 2 and the transport lemma provide an actual report change making \(j\) the unique winner at cost at most \(d_j+\varepsilon<1/\rho\).

Thus the first guarantee holds despite possible nonattainment of \(d_j\).

Coalition gain is bounded

First, distances to the fixed set \(W_j\) are Lipschitz:

\[ |d_j(p)-d_j(q)|\le\delta(p,q). \]

Indeed, if \(z\in W_j\) minimizes the distance from \(q\), the triangle inequality gives \(d_j(p)\le\delta(p,q)+d_j(q)\); interchange \(p\) and \(q\) for the reverse bound.

Let \(w_j(p)=(1-\rho d_j(p))_+\) and \(Z(p)=\sum_jw_j(p)\). The positive-part function is Lipschitz with constant one, so

\[ |w_j(p)-w_j(q)|\le\rho\delta(p,q). \]

Also \(Z(p)\ge1\) for every profile. Set \(A_0=\sum_j|w_j(q)-w_j(p)|\). Normalization therefore satisfies

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_j|G_q(j)-G_p(j)| &\le \frac{A_0+|Z(q)-Z(p)|}{Z(q)}\\ &\le 2A_0. \end{aligned} \]

Hence

\[ \delta(G_p,G_q)\le A_0\le m\rho\delta(p,q). \]

Now consider an admissible coalition. Write \(c=\sum_{u,r}c_{u,r}\le\alpha\), and let \(p'\) be the resulting first-place masses. Only selected mass can change reports, so the transport lower bound implies \(\delta(p,p')\le c\).

Define its aggregate candidate utilities by \(a_j=\sum_{u,r}c_{u,r}u_j\). Because true utilities remain fixed and lie in \([0,1]\), these same coefficients describe its utility before and after the deviation, and \(0\le a_j\le c\). Therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} U_c(G_{\mu'})-U_c(G_\mu) &=\sum_j a_j\bigl(G_{\mu'}(j)-G_\mu(j)\bigr)\\ &\le c\sum_{j:\,G_{\mu'}(j)>G_\mu(j)} \bigl(G_{\mu'}(j)-G_\mu(j)\bigr)\\ &=c\,\delta(G_\mu,G_{\mu'})\\ &\le m\rho c^2\\ &\le m\rho\alpha^2 =\frac{\eta}{m+1} \le\eta. \end{aligned} \]

This proves the second guarantee for every admissible deviation.
