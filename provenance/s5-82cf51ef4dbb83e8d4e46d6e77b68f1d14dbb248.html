The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(\Theta\) of size \(\tau\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\ge0\) summing to \(1\), \(T\) rounds, type-level values \(v_{\theta,t}\), and predictions \(\widetilde V_\theta\) for \(V_\theta=\sum_{t=1}^T v_{\theta,t}\), reveal only the current values and history online, without splitting or repartitioning types. At each round choose intensities \(a_{\theta,t}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{\theta}\mu_\theta a_{\theta,t}=1\), and determine the best worst-case competitive ratio for \(\operatorname{NSW}_\mu(a)=\exp(\sum_{\theta}\mu_\theta\log u_\theta)\), where \(u_\theta=\sum_t v_{\theta,t}a_{\theta,t}\), under perfect or bounded-error predictions.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=T\ge1\) rounds, each supplying one unit of divisible resource. Values \(v_{\theta,t}\ge0\) are binary rationals, and every \(V_\theta=\sum_{t=1}^T v_{\theta,t}\) is positive.
Initially a deterministic online policy knows the support, \(T\), and positive binary-rational predictions \(\widetilde V_\theta\). An adaptive adversary reveals current values; the policy sees these and history, then irrevocably chooses one intensity per fixed type, without splitting or repartitioning types.
Feasibility requires \(a_{\theta,t}\ge0\) and \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta a_{\theta,t}=1\); there is no additional upper bound on an intensity. Utilities are \(u_\theta(a)=\sum_t v_{\theta,t}a_{\theta,t}\), and \(\operatorname{NSW}_\mu(a)=\exp(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\log u_\theta(a))\), interpreted as zero if any utility is zero.
Predictions concern \(V_\theta\) itself. Perfect predictions satisfy \(\widetilde V_\theta=V_\theta\); bounded errors satisfy \(V_\theta/d_\theta\le\widetilde V_\theta\le c_\theta V_\theta\), with binary-rational \(c_\theta,d_\theta\ge1\). A policy's competitive ratio is \(\sup_{v,\widetilde V}\operatorname{OPT}_\mu(v)/\operatorname{NSW}_\mu(A(v,\widetilde V))\), over admissible adaptive instances; a zero denominator gives infinite ratio. State running times in \(m=T\), \(\tau\), total binary input length \(L\), and any additional algorithmic parameters.

What is the optimal worst-case competitive ratio achievable by deterministic online policies, as a function of \(\mu\), \(\tau\), \(T\), and \((c_\theta,d_\theta)_{\theta\in\Theta}\), under perfect and bounded-error predictions?

Proved progress and scope

The optimal competitive ratio for arbitrary prescribed masses and heterogeneous prediction errors is not determined here.

The following results substantially sharpen the previous bounds:

  • A composition theorem gives stronger upper bounds by running separate policies on fixed subsets of the support.
  • Equal-mass blocks transfer uniform-support lower bounds to nonuniform societies.
  • A causal rounding argument repairs the binary-rational gap in the imported lower bound.
  • For the family consisting of one type of mass $1-\delta$ and $n$ types of mass $\delta/n$, the achievable logarithmic exponent is $\delta$, up to arbitrarily small losses in the lower bound.

In particular, an optimal ratio proportional to entropy is not a valid general characterization.

All logarithms below are natural. “Rational” means a rational number represented by binary-encoded numerator and denominator; the rounding construction produces dyadic values.

1. Resource shares and a comparison inequality

Define resource shares by
$$ x_{\theta,t}=\mu_\theta a_{\theta,t}. $$
Since every mass is positive, this is an invertible transformation. Feasibility becomes
$$ x_{\theta,t}\ge 0, \qquad \sum_\theta x_{\theta,t}=1. $$
It neither splits nor changes any type.

Write
$$ z_\theta(x)=\sum_{t=1}^T v_{\theta,t}x_{\theta,t}, \qquad W_\mu(z)=\prod_\theta z_\theta^{\mu_\theta}. $$
Then
$$ u_\theta(x)=\frac{z_\theta(x)}{\mu_\theta}, \qquad \operatorname{NSW}_\mu(x) = \frac{W_\mu(z(x))} {\prod_\theta\mu_\theta^{\mu_\theta}}. $$
The denominator is independent of the allocation, so competitive ratios can be calculated using $W_\mu$.

An offline maximizer exists: the feasible allocation space is compact, and the weighted product, extended to zero when a coordinate is zero, is continuous. Its maximum is positive because $x_{\theta,t}=\mu_\theta$ gives
$$ z_\theta=\mu_\theta V_\theta>0. $$

For any positive vector $r$ and any feasible allocation $x^*$, weighted arithmetic–geometric mean gives
$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{W_\mu(z(x^*))}{W_\mu(r)} &\le \sum_\theta\mu_\theta\frac{z_\theta(x^*)}{r_\theta}\\ &= \sum_t\sum_\theta x^*_{\theta,t} \frac{\mu_\theta v_{\theta,t}}{r_\theta}\\ &\le \sum_t\max_\theta \frac{\mu_\theta v_{\theta,t}}{r_\theta}. \end{aligned} $$
The last inequality follows separately in each round from the simplex constraint.

2. A baseline policy and its guarantees

This section proves the baseline results from NOTES, which will be used in the composition theorem.

Define
$$ H(\mu)=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\log\frac1{\mu_\theta}, \qquad C=\prod_\theta c_\theta^{\mu_\theta}, \qquad D=\max_\theta d_\theta. $$

2.1 Policy

For a nonzero round, let
$$ I_t=\{\theta:v_{\theta,t}>0\}, \qquad M_t=\sum_{\theta\in I_t}\mu_\theta. $$
Reserve half the resource using shares
$$ r_{\theta,t}= \begin{cases} \displaystyle\frac{\mu_\theta}{2M_t},&\theta\in I_t,\\ 0,&\theta\notin I_t. \end{cases} $$

Initialize
$$ Y_{\theta,0}=\frac{\mu_\theta\widetilde V_\theta}{2}. $$
Choose the other half, $g_t$, to maximize
$$ \sum_\theta\mu_\theta \log\bigl(Y_{\theta,t-1}+v_{\theta,t}g_{\theta,t}\bigr) $$
subject to
$$ g_{\theta,t}\ge0, \qquad \sum_\theta g_{\theta,t}=\frac12. $$
Update
$$ Y_{\theta,t}=Y_{\theta,t-1}+v_{\theta,t}g_{\theta,t}, \qquad x_{\theta,t}=r_{\theta,t}+g_{\theta,t}. $$

In an all-zero round, use $r_{\theta,t}=g_{\theta,t}=\mu_\theta/2$. Fix an ordering to resolve all ties.

The greedy objective is continuous on its compact feasible set because every initial $Y_{\theta,0}$ is positive. Thus a maximizer exists. The policy uses no error bounds.

Let
$$ G_\theta=\sum_t v_{\theta,t}g_{\theta,t}. $$
Since $M_t\le1$, its final share utilities satisfy
$$ z_\theta^A \ge \frac{\mu_\theta V_\theta}{2}+G_\theta>0. $$

2.2 Potential bound

Define
$$ P_t=\max_\theta\frac{\mu_\theta v_{\theta,t}}{Y_{\theta,t}}. $$
At a greedy optimum, every coordinate with positive $g_{\theta,t}$ has derivative $P_t$. Otherwise a small feasible transfer to a coordinate of larger derivative would improve the objective. Therefore
$$ \frac{P_t}{2} = \sum_\theta\mu_\theta \frac{Y_{\theta,t}-Y_{\theta,t-1}}{Y_{\theta,t}}. $$
The identity also holds in an all-zero round.

For $a\ge b>0$,
$$ \frac{a-b}{a}\le\log\frac ab. $$
Summing this inequality gives
$$ \sum_t P_t\le2\Phi, \qquad \Phi= \sum_\theta\mu_\theta \log\left(1+\frac{2G_\theta} {\mu_\theta\widetilde V_\theta}\right). $$

The overprediction inequalities imply
$$ Y_{\theta,T} = \frac{\mu_\theta\widetilde V_\theta}{2}+G_\theta \le \frac{c_\theta\mu_\theta V_\theta}{2}+G_\theta \le c_\theta z_\theta^A. $$
Consequently,
$$ W_\mu(Y_T)\le C W_\mu(z^A). $$
Applying the comparison inequality and using $Y_{\theta,T}\ge Y_{\theta,t}$ yields
$$ \frac{W_\mu(z^*)}{W_\mu(z^A)} \le C\sum_t\max_\theta \frac{\mu_\theta v_{\theta,t}}{Y_{\theta,T}} \le C\sum_tP_t \le2C\Phi. $$

Because $g_{\theta,t}\le1/2$,
$$ G_\theta\le\frac{V_\theta}{2}. $$
Hence
$$ \Phi\le \sum_\theta\mu_\theta \log\left(1+\frac{d_\theta}{\mu_\theta}\right). $$

Weighted concavity of logarithm also gives
$$ \Phi \le \log\left(1+2\sum_\theta\frac{G_\theta}{\widetilde V_\theta}\right). $$
Moreover,
$$ \begin{aligned} 2\sum_\theta\frac{G_\theta}{\widetilde V_\theta} &= 2\sum_t\sum_\theta g_{\theta,t}\frac{v_{\theta,t}}{\widetilde V_\theta}\\ &\le \sum_t\max_\theta \frac{v_{\theta,t}}{\widetilde V_\theta}\\ &\le TD. \end{aligned} $$
Thus
$$ \Phi\le\log(1+TD). $$

2.3 Bounds independent of the predictions

Every offline share utility obeys $z_\theta^*\le V_\theta$. Therefore the reserve guarantee gives
$$ \frac{W_\mu(z^*)}{W_\mu(z^A)} \le \prod_\theta\left(\frac2{\mu_\theta}\right)^{\mu_\theta} = 2e^{H(\mu)}. $$

Also, whenever $v_{\theta,t}>0$,
$$ z_\theta^A\ge \frac{\mu_\theta v_{\theta,t}}{2M_t}. $$
The comparison inequality consequently gives
$$ \frac{W_\mu(z^*)}{W_\mu(z^A)} \le2\sum_tM_t\le2T. $$

Combining the four bounds, this single policy has competitive ratio at most
$$ B(\mu,T,c,d) = 2\min\left\{ e^{H(\mu)}, T, C\sum_\theta\mu_\theta \log\left(1+\frac{d_\theta}{\mu_\theta}\right), C\log(1+TD) \right\}. $$

With perfect predictions,
$$ \sum_\theta\mu_\theta \log\left(1+\frac1{\mu_\theta}\right) \le H(\mu)+\log2, $$
so
$$ B(\mu,T,1,1) \le 2\min\{H(\mu)+\log2,\log(1+T)\}. $$
Finally,
$$ H(\mu)\le \log\left(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\frac1{\mu_\theta}\right) =\log\tau. $$

All these inequalities hold on each realized history, so they apply to adaptive revelation.

3. A stronger upper bound by composition

3.1 Composition theorem

Let
$$ \mathcal P=\{\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_k\} $$
be any fixed partition into nonempty subsets. Define
$$ p_j=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_j}\mu_\theta, \qquad \nu_\theta^{(j)}=\frac{\mu_\theta}{p_j} \quad(\theta\in\Theta_j). $$

Suppose policy $A_j$ has competitive ratio $R_j$ for the conditional society with masses $\nu^{(j)}$. Run these policies independently, giving block $j$ resource fraction $p_j$ every round.

This is a valid policy on the original fixed types. Its competitive ratio is at most
$$ \boxed{ \exp(H(p))\prod_{j=1}^kR_j^{p_j} }, \qquad H(p)=\sum_jp_j\log\frac1{p_j}. $$

Proof. Let $W_j^*$ be the offline optimum weighted share product for conditional masses $\nu^{(j)}$ and one full resource per round.

The restriction of any original offline allocation to block $j$ uses at most one resource per round. Completing its shares within that block can only increase utilities. Thus
$$ W_\mu(z^*)\le\prod_j(W_j^*)^{p_j}. $$

If $z^{A_j}$ denotes the share utilities of conditional policy $A_j$, the composed policy has share utilities $p_jz_\theta^{A_j}$ on block $j$. Hence
$$ W_\mu(z^A) = \prod_j\left[p_jW_j(z^{A_j})\right]^{p_j}. $$
Dividing gives
$$ \frac{W_\mu(z^*)}{W_\mu(z^A)} \le \prod_jp_j^{-p_j}R_j^{p_j}. $$
This is the claimed expression. $\square$

The partition is a computational choice; it does not split types or change their identities.

3.2 Explicit guarantees with unknown error bounds

For a singleton block, use its forced allocation and set $B_j=1$.

For every other block, use Section 2 with conditional masses $\nu^{(j)}$. Define
$$ C_j=\prod_{\theta\in\Theta_j} c_\theta^{\nu_\theta^{(j)}}, \qquad D_j=\max_{\theta\in\Theta_j}d_\theta, $$
and
$$ B_j= 2\min\left\{ e^{H(\nu^{(j)})}, T, C_j\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_j}\nu_\theta^{(j)} \log\left(1+\frac{d_\theta}{\nu_\theta^{(j)}}\right), C_j\log(1+TD_j) \right\}. $$
The resulting policy satisfies
$$ \boxed{ \frac{\operatorname{OPT}_\mu} {\operatorname{NSW}_\mu(A)} \le e^{H(p)}\prod_j B_j^{p_j}. } $$

Every fixed partition gives such a policy, and none of its component policies receives $c_\theta$ or $d_\theta$. This statement does not assume that the policy can select a partition using unknown error bounds.

With perfect predictions, each nonsingleton block can use
$$ B_j\le 2\min\left\{ H(\nu^{(j)})+\log2,\log(1+T) \right\}. $$

4. Exact small cases and a strict general lower bound

4.1 Exact ratio one

The optimal ratio is $1$ in each of these cases:

  • $\tau=1$, with arbitrary prediction errors;
  • $T=1$, with arbitrary prediction errors;
  • $T=2$, with perfect predictions.

For $\tau=1$, feasibility forces intensity $1$.

For $T=1$, every value is positive. Removing allocation-independent factors leaves the objective
$$ \prod_\theta x_{\theta,1}^{\mu_\theta}, $$
which is maximized at $x_{\theta,1}=\mu_\theta$.

For $T=2$ with perfect predictions, the first reveal determines the second column:
$$ v_{\theta,2}=\widetilde V_\theta-v_{\theta,1}. $$
Thus the entire instance is known before the first allocation.

For completeness, an exact rational offline construction follows. Write
$$ b_\theta=v_{\theta,1}, \qquad h_\theta=v_{\theta,2}, \qquad \rho_\theta=\frac{b_\theta}{b_\theta+h_\theta}. $$
If a column is identically zero, allocate the other resource in shares $\mu_\theta$. Otherwise choose $\pi\in(0,1)$ such that
$$ \sum_{\rho_\theta>\pi}\mu_\theta \le\pi\le \sum_{\rho_\theta\ge\pi}\mu_\theta. $$

Such a price exists: scan the finitely many thresholds. Between thresholds the mass strictly above the price is constant, and the price increases continuously. At a crossing inside an interval, take that constant mass as $\pi$; at a threshold crossing, the displayed inequalities hold. Both nonzero columns ensure the crossing is strictly between zero and one.

Give type $\theta$ expenditure budget $\mu_\theta$. Types above the threshold spend on resource one, types below it spend on resource two, and tied budgets are split so that total spending on resource one is $\pi$. Divide expenditures by the prices $\pi$ and $1-\pi$ to obtain shares.

The resources clear, and every type spends only on resources maximizing value per price. Its share utility is therefore
$$ z_\theta= \mu_\theta\max\left\{ \frac{b_\theta}{\pi}, \frac{h_\theta}{1-\pi} \right\}. $$
It follows that
$$ \frac{\mu_\theta b_\theta}{z_\theta}\le\pi, \qquad \frac{\mu_\theta h_\theta}{z_\theta}\le1-\pi. $$
The comparison inequality bounds every competing allocation’s welfare ratio by $\pi+(1-\pi)=1$.

4.2 A strict lower bound for every nontrivial mass vector

Suppose $\tau\ge2$ and $T\ge3$. For every nonempty proper subset $S$, put
$$ p=\sum_{\theta\in S}\mu_\theta, \qquad q=1-p. $$
Every deterministic policy has competitive ratio at least
$$ \boxed{ \exp\left(\frac{(pq)^3}{128}\right)>1. } $$
This holds with perfect predictions and every total equal to $1$.

Proof. Define
$$ \beta=pq, \qquad \alpha=1-\beta, \qquad K=1+\frac{\beta}{2}. $$
Every prediction is $1$. Round one gives value $\alpha$ to every type. Let $x$ be the policy’s total first-round resource share allocated to $S$.

The adversary chooses one of two continuations:
$$ \begin{array}{c|cc} &\text{values in }S&\text{values outside }S\\ \hline \text{A, round two}&\beta/2&\beta\\ \text{A, round three}&\beta/2&0\\ \hline \text{B, round two}&\beta&\beta/2\\ \text{B, round three}&0&\beta/2 \end{array} $$
Each type’s total is exactly $1$.

Let
$$ Z_1=\sum_{\theta\in S}\mu_\theta u_\theta, \qquad Z_2=\sum_{\theta\notin S}\mu_\theta u_\theta. $$
Weighted logarithmic concavity gives
$$ \log\operatorname{NSW}_\mu \le p\log(Z_1/p)+q\log(Z_2/q). $$
Equality is attained by allocating each group’s shares proportionally to its members’ masses.

Write
$$ F(Z_1,Z_2)=p\log Z_1+q\log Z_2. $$
The constants omitted from this expression cancel in welfare ratios.

In continuation A, give the third resource entirely to its positively valued group; this can only increase welfare. If $y$ is the round-two share allocated to $S$, then
$$ Z_1=\frac{\beta}{2}+\alpha x+\frac{\beta y}{2}, \qquad Z_2=\beta+\alpha(1-x)-\beta y. $$
The offline choice
$$ x_A=\frac{pK-\beta/2}{\alpha}, \qquad y=0, $$
gives $(Z_1,Z_2)=(pK,qK)$.

In continuation B, let $y$ be the round-two share allocated outside $S$. Then
$$ Z_1=\beta+\alpha x-\beta y, \qquad Z_2=\frac{\beta}{2}+\alpha(1-x)+\frac{\beta y}{2}. $$
The corresponding offline choice is
$$ x_B=\frac{pK-\beta}{\alpha}, \qquad y=0. $$

Both choices are strictly feasible. Indeed,
$$ pK-\beta>p-\beta=p^2>0, $$
and
$$ \alpha-(pK-\beta/2) = q\left(1-\frac{p(1+p)}2\right)>0. $$
Moreover,
$$ x_A-x_B=\frac{\beta}{2\alpha}. $$

In either continuation,
$$ Z_1+Z_2=K-\frac{\beta y}{2}\le K. $$
The supporting-line inequality for logarithm therefore yields
$$ F(Z_1,Z_2) \le F(pK,qK)+\frac{Z_1+Z_2-K}{K} \le F(pK,qK). $$
Thus the stated offline allocations are globally optimal.

Each aggregate utility is at most $1$. For $0<z,z^*\le1$, the second derivative of logarithm gives
$$ \log z\le \log z^*+\frac{z-z^*}{z^*} -\frac{(z-z^*)^2}{2}. $$
In continuation A, writing $h=x-x_A$, this implies
$$ \begin{aligned} F(pK,qK)-F(Z_1,Z_2) \ge{}& \frac{\beta y}{2K}\\ &+\frac12\left[ p\left(\alpha h+\frac{\beta y}{2}\right)^2 +q(\alpha h+\beta y)^2 \right]. \end{aligned} $$
The first term is nonnegative. Minimizing the quadratic over all real $y$ gives
$$ \alpha^2h^2 \left( 1-\frac{(p/2+q)^2}{p/4+q} \right) = \frac{pq\,\alpha^2h^2}{p+4q} \ge\frac{pq\,\alpha^2h^2}{4}. $$
Consequently,
$$ F(pK,qK)-F(Z_1,Z_2) \ge \frac{pq\,\alpha^2(x-x_A)^2}{8}. $$
Continuation B gives the same bound with $x_B$; its quadratic denominator is $4p+q\le4$.

At least one distance from $x$ to $x_A,x_B$ is at least $\beta/(4\alpha)$. Choosing that continuation gives logarithmic welfare loss at least
$$ \frac{pq\,\alpha^2}{8} \left(\frac{\beta}{4\alpha}\right)^2 = \frac{(pq)^3}{128}. $$
Zero online utility already gives infinite ratio. Otherwise exponentiation proves the claim. For larger horizons, append all-zero rounds. $\square$

Perfect predictions are admissible under every stated error bound, so this lower bound also applies to bounded-error guarantees.

5. Repairing the rational-input lower-bound transfer

A continuity argument applied directly to an arbitrary policy would be invalid: a deterministic policy may be discontinuous in the revealed values. The following simulation avoids that assumption.

5.1 Causal dyadic simulation lemma

Fix rational masses $\mu$, horizon $T$, and known totals
$$ V_\theta=1 $$
for every type. Suppose a deterministic policy $B$ has ratio at most $R$ on all dyadic valuation tables with these totals.

For every $\varepsilon>0$, there is a deterministic policy on arbitrary real-valued tables with the same totals and ratio at most
$$ (1+\varepsilon)R. $$

Proof. The case $T=1$ is already exact. Assume $T\ge2$, and put
$$ \mu_{\min}=\min_\theta\mu_\theta. $$
Choose $0<\eta<1$ and a dyadic mesh $\Delta$. For an arbitrary real-valued instance $v$, define causally
$$ w_{\theta,t} = \Delta\left\lfloor\frac{v_{\theta,t}}{\Delta}\right\rfloor \quad(t<T), $$
and
$$ w_{\theta,T} = 1-\sum_{t<T}w_{\theta,t}. $$
These are nonnegative dyadic values with row totals exactly $1$. Nonnegativity of the last entry follows from
$$ \sum_{t<T}w_{\theta,t} \le \sum_{t<T}v_{\theta,t}\le1. $$

For each row,
$$ \sum_t|v_{\theta,t}-w_{\theta,t}| \le E, \qquad E=2(T-1)\Delta. $$
Indeed, all rounding losses in the first $T-1$ entries are added to the last entry.

Therefore, for every feasible allocation $x$,
$$ |z_\theta(v,x)-z_\theta(w,x)|\le E, $$
because each share is at most $1$.

Simulate $B$ on the table $w$, obtaining shares $b_t$. Execute instead
$$ x_{\theta,t}=(1-\eta)b_{\theta,t}+\eta\mu_\theta. $$
Keep the simulation’s history separately. This is a deterministic causal policy.

Let
$$ \kappa=\frac{E}{\eta\mu_{\min}}<1. $$
On $w$, the executed allocation has
$$ z_\theta(w,x)\ge\eta\mu_\theta. $$
Hence
$$ z_\theta(v,x) \ge (1-\kappa)z_\theta(w,x) \ge (1-\kappa)(1-\eta)z_\theta(w,b). $$

Write $Q(v)$ for the optimal weighted share product on $v$. Take an offline optimum $x^*$ on $v$ and smooth it:
$$ x'=(1-\eta)x^*+\eta\mu. $$
Then
$$ z_\theta(v,x')\ge\eta\mu_\theta, \qquad z_\theta(v,x')\ge(1-\eta)z_\theta(v,x^*). $$
The same perturbation estimate gives
$$ z_\theta(w,x')\ge(1-\kappa)z_\theta(v,x'). $$
Thus
$$ Q(w)\ge (1-\kappa)(1-\eta)Q(v). $$

Combining the inequalities,
$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{Q(v)}{W_\mu(z(v,x))} &\le \frac{Q(w)} {(1-\kappa)^2(1-\eta)^2W_\mu(z(w,b))}\\ &\le \frac{R}{(1-\kappa)^2(1-\eta)^2}. \end{aligned} $$
First choose $\eta$ sufficiently small, then choose $\Delta$ sufficiently small, so that the last factor is at most $(1+\varepsilon)R$. $\square$

Every mapped history is an admissible dyadic instance for $B$. Thus the proof also covers adaptive adversaries.

5.2 Consequences for encoding and the imported theorem

Restriction to dyadic inputs cannot increase the optimal ratio. Conversely, the lemma transfers any finite dyadic-input guarantee to arbitrary real inputs with an arbitrarily small multiplicative loss. Consequently, for fixed rational $\mu,T$ and known unit totals, the two infimum competitive ratios are equal.

For a constant-factor transfer, take $\eta=1/4$ and choose
$$ \Delta\le\frac{\mu_{\min}}{32(T-1)}. $$
Then $\kappa\le1/4$, and the loss factor is at most $(4/3)^4$. The dyadic denominator requires only
$$ O\left(\log T+\log\frac1{\mu_{\min}}\right) $$
bits.

The finite-agent theorem states that no deterministic online policy has an $O((\log n)^{1-\epsilon})$ guarantee for fixed $\epsilon>0$, even with known unit totals; its construction has $T=n+1$. [Theorem 3 and Section 5.2 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.03564v3#S5.SS2).

Its hypotheses match the real-valued uniform-share model. Uniform masses change every utility by the same factor $n$, preserving ratios. The simulation lemma therefore transfers this impossibility to dyadic valuations with exact unit totals. For uniform masses and $T=n+1$, each mapped valuation needs $O(\log n)$ bits, so the complete table has polynomial binary length.

This repairs the encoding gap identified in the verifier report.

6. Transferring lower bounds to equal-mass blocks

6.1 Block embedding theorem

Suppose the fixed support contains disjoint nonempty subsets
$$ G_1,\ldots,G_k $$
of equal mass
$$ \sum_{\theta\in G_j}\mu_\theta=\frac Pk, \qquad 0<P\le1. $$
Let $U$ be the remaining support.

A uniform-support lower bound $R$ on $k$ types and $T_0$ rounds transfers to a lower bound
$$ \boxed{R^P} $$
for the original masses, using $T_0+1$ rounds and perfect unit-total predictions. When $P=1$, the extra round is unnecessary.

Proof. Given a uniform $k$-type instance with values $w_{j,t}$ and unit totals, give every type in $G_j$ value $w_{j,t}$ during its first $T_0$ rounds. Give types in $U$ value zero in those rounds.

If $P<1$, append a round with value $1$ for every type in $U$ and zero for the other types. Every original type has total $1$.

Take any deterministic policy $A$ on this society. Simulate its first $T_0$ rounds. Let
$$ y_{j,t}=\sum_{\theta\in G_j}x_{\theta,t}, \qquad \sum_jy_{j,t}\le1. $$
Define a uniform policy $B$ by completing these shares:
$$ b_{j,t} = y_{j,t}+\frac{1-\sum_\ell y_{\ell,t}}{k}. $$
The simulation maintains $A$’s own history, so $B$ is a valid deterministic online policy.

Put
$$ Z_j=\sum_t w_{j,t}y_{j,t}, \qquad z_j^B=\sum_t w_{j,t}b_{j,t}. $$
Then $Z_j\le z_j^B$. Moreover,
$$ Z_j=\sum_{\theta\in G_j}\mu_\theta u_\theta^A. $$
Weighted logarithmic concavity within each group gives
$$ \prod_{\theta\in G_j}(u_\theta^A)^{\mu_\theta} \le \left(\frac{kZ_j}{P}\right)^{P/k}. $$

The remaining types obtain utility only in the appended round. Their aggregate resource share is at most $1$, so their welfare contribution is at most
$$ (1-P)^{-(1-P)}. $$
Consequently,
$$ \operatorname{NSW}_\mu(A) \le P^{-P}(1-P)^{-(1-P)} \left[\operatorname{NSW}_{\mathrm{unif}}(B)\right]^P. $$
For $P=1$, omit the factor involving $1-P$.

An original offline allocation can implement a uniform offline optimum by assigning each group its uniform share and dividing that share proportionally to the fixed masses within the group. In the appended round, divide the resource proportionally among $U$. This attains
$$ P^{-P}(1-P)^{-(1-P)} \left[\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{unif}}\right]^P. $$
Dividing proves
$$ \frac{\operatorname{OPT}_\mu}{\operatorname{NSW}_\mu(A)} \ge \left[ \frac{\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{unif}}} {\operatorname{NSW}_{\mathrm{unif}}(B)} \right]^P. $$
An adaptive uniform adversary may choose its next values from $B$’s history; the simulation converts this into an admissible adaptive instance for $A$. $\square$

No types are split in this reduction. The groups are used to assign identical valuation sequences and to aggregate shares in the analysis.

7. A nonuniform family with logarithmic exponent $\delta$

Fix a rational constant $0<\delta<1$. Consider
$$ \mu^{(n)} = \left( 1-\delta, \underbrace{\frac{\delta}{n},\ldots,\frac{\delta}{n}}_{n\text{ types}} \right), \qquad T=n+2. $$
Define binary entropy
$$ h(\delta)= -(1-\delta)\log(1-\delta)-\delta\log\delta. $$

7.1 Upper bound

Partition the support into the heavy singleton and the $n$ light types. The singleton policy has ratio $1$. The conditional light-type masses are uniform.

Section 3 therefore supplies a deterministic policy with perfect-prediction ratio at most
$$ \boxed{ e^{h(\delta)} \left[2\log(n+3)\right]^\delta. } $$
Thus its ratio is
$$ O_\delta((\log n)^\delta). $$

The partition depends only on the given masses. The policy requires neither future values nor prediction-error bounds.

7.2 Lower bound on the exponent

The light singleton groups have equal mass $\delta/n$ and total mass $\delta$. Section 6 embeds any uniform $n$-type instance on $n+1$ rounds into this society on $n+2$ rounds.

Suppose a family of deterministic policies achieved
$$ O((\log n)^{\delta(1-\epsilon)}) $$
for some fixed $0<\epsilon<1$. The simulated uniform policies would then satisfy
$$ \frac{\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{unif}}} {\operatorname{NSW}_{\mathrm{unif}}} \le \left[ O((\log n)^{\delta(1-\epsilon)}) \right]^{1/\delta} = O((\log n)^{1-\epsilon}). $$
This contradicts the rational-input impossibility established in Section 5.

Therefore, on this family, no deterministic policy achieves
$$ \boxed{ O((\log n)^{\delta(1-\epsilon)}) } $$
for any fixed $0<\epsilon<1$, even with dyadic valuations and perfect unit-total predictions.

The upper and lower results identify exponent $\delta$ up to arbitrarily small exponent losses. They do not establish a matching constant-factor lower bound.

7.3 Entropy is not a matching general lower bound

For this family,
$$ H(\mu^{(n)})=h(\delta)+\delta\log n. $$
Hence
$$ \min\{H(\mu^{(n)}),\log(1+T)\} = \Theta_\delta(\log n). $$
But the achievable ratio is $O_\delta((\log n)^\delta)$, and
$$ \frac{(\log n)^\delta}{\log n}\longrightarrow0. $$

Thus there cannot be a positive universal constant lower bound proportional to
$$ \min\{H(\mu),\log(1+T)\} $$
for every prescribed mass vector and horizon.

This rules out a direct entropy characterization suggested by the earlier upper bound.

8. Running times

The upper-bound policies can use exact rational allocations; no numerical evaluation of logarithms is needed.

In a nonzero greedy round, write $B_\theta=Y_{\theta,t-1}$. The solution has the form
$$ g_\theta= \max\left\{ 0,\mu_\theta h-\frac{B_\theta}{v_{\theta,t}} \right\} \quad(v_{\theta,t}>0), $$
and zero-valued types receive no greedy share. The scalar $h$ is chosen so that the shares sum to $1/2$.

Sort the thresholds
$$ \frac{B_\theta}{\mu_\theta v_{\theta,t}}. $$
For a candidate active set $J$,
$$ h= \frac{1/2+\sum_{\theta\in J}B_\theta/v_{\theta,t}} {\sum_{\theta\in J}\mu_\theta}. $$
Scanning the sorted thresholds identifies a consistent active set. The marginal conditions and concavity prove that its allocation is optimal.

For a supplied partition with block sizes $s_j$, the arithmetic-operation count is
$$ O\left( T\sum_j s_j\log(2s_j) \right) \subseteq O(T\tau\log(2\tau)). $$
There is no partition-search claim here; the partition is an additional algorithmic parameter.

For binary input length $L$, conditional masses have $O(L)$-bit representations: putting the original masses over a common denominator makes a block mass a sum of their numerators, and normalization divides by that sum.

Maintain each block’s utility coordinates over a common denominator. In one round, the denominator can be enlarged using the current value numerators and denominators, the fixed conditional-mass denominator, and the active-set mass numerator. These contribute $O(L)$ additional bits per round. Utility magnitudes are bounded by $2^{O(L)}$, since
$$ Y_{\theta,t} \le \frac{\mu_\theta\widetilde V_\theta}{2}+\frac{V_\theta}{2}. $$
The required integers therefore have $O(TL)$ bits.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic gives the conservative bit-time bound
$$ \boxed{ O\left(T\tau\log(2\tau)(TL)^2\right), \qquad m=T. } $$
The exact two-round construction also runs in polynomial bit time, using $O(\tau\log(2\tau))$ rational arithmetic operations. No accuracy parameter is required.

9. Remaining unresolved questions
