The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite hierarchy with team cohorts indexed by types \(t\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), per-team harmonic budgets \(b_t^r\), a resource-conversion graph, and mass-normalized supplies \(s_v^r\), compute an \(\epsilon\)-accurate harmonic market equilibrium. A type records its hierarchy position \(v(t)\) and complete budget profile; define the mass-weighted subtree budget \(\beta_v^r=\sum_{t:\,v(t)\in\operatorname{SubT}(v)}\mu_t b_t^r\). At each node \(v\), with its parent’s prices fixed, solve the paper’s local concave price program using \(\beta_v^r\) in place of the finite subtree budget, recover conversion amounts from the dual variables, and recursively recover flows. The resulting type allocation is \(x_t^r=b_t^r/p_{v(t)}^r\); seek a running-time bound in the hierarchy size, \(m\), \(\tau\), input bit length \(L\), and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\).

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable under the stated promises. In fact, an exact rational equilibrium can be computed in time polynomial in \(h,m,\tau,L\). Consequently, the required dependence on \(\log(1/\epsilon)\) is satisfied.

The key is to aggregate mass-weighted budgets, rather than expand rational masses into individual agents. Below, reciprocal prices give a rational quadratic-programming formulation, including a polynomial bound on encoding lengths throughout the hierarchy.

1. Exact aggregation of the support

Define the aggregate local budget

\[ B_v^r=\sum_{t:v(t)=v}\mu_t b_t^r. \]

Then

\[ A_v^r=\sum_{t:v(t)=v}\mu_t\frac{b_t^r}{p_v^r} =\frac{B_v^r}{p_v^r}, \qquad \beta_v^r=\sum_{u\in\operatorname{SubT}(v)}B_u^r. \]

Thus every equilibrium equation depends on the support only through the array \(B\). Conversely, any equilibrium for aggregate demands \(B_v^r/p_v^r\) lifts to the original society by setting \(x_t^r=b_t^r/p_{v(t)}^r\).

This is an exact reduction to a harmonic instance with \(h\) hierarchy nodes. It uses no replication of agents. It places the problem within the structural setting of [Section 3 of the cited finite-agent paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22159v1). The remaining proof establishes rational computation and bit complexity directly.

All entries of \(B\) and \(\beta\) can be computed by rational arithmetic in polynomial time and have polynomial encoding length. Indeed, each is a sum of polynomially many products of input rationals; using products of their denominators as common denominators gives polynomial bit-length bounds.

2. Elimination of hierarchy flows

Let \(o\) denote the root. In any exact positive-price equilibrium, for every nonroot node,

\[ p_{\pi(v)}^r f_v^r=\beta_v^r. \]

To prove this, proceed upward from the leaves. At a leaf, money conservation gives

\[ p_{\pi(v)}^r f_v^r=p_v^r A_v^r=B_v^r=\beta_v^r. \]

At an internal nonroot node, assuming the identity for its children,

\[ p_{\pi(v)}^r f_v^r =p_v^r A_v^r+\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}p_v^r f_w^r =B_v^r+\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}\beta_w^r =\beta_v^r. \]

Introduce reciprocal prices \(z_v^r=1/p_v^r\), and define

\[ a_o^r=s_o^r, \qquad a_v^r=s_v^r+\beta_v^r z_{\pi(v)}^r \quad(v\ne o). \]

Because \(A_v^r+\sum_w f_w^r=\beta_v^r z_v^r\), physical conservation becomes

\[ \beta_v^r z_v^r =a_v^r+\sum_{q:(q,r)\in C}c_v^{qr} -\sum_{q:(r,q)\in C}c_v^{rq}. \tag{1} \]

The conversion conditions become

\[ z_v^r\leq z_v^q,\qquad c_v^{rq}\geq0,\qquad (z_v^r-z_v^q)c_v^{rq}=0. \tag{2} \]

For positive reciprocal prices, equality in reciprocal prices is equivalent to equality in prices. Hence these are precisely the original conversion conditions.

3. A local rational quadratic program

Once the parent reciprocal prices are fixed, consider the program

\[ \operatorname{minimize}_{z\in\mathbb R^m} Q_v(z)=\frac12\sum_r\beta_v^r z_r^2-\sum_r a_v^r z_r, \]

subject to

\[ z_r\geq0,\qquad z_r\leq z_q \quad\text{for every }(r,q)\in C. \tag{3} \]

Its Hessian is the positive semidefinite diagonal matrix with entries \(\beta_v^r\), so this is a convex quadratic program with rational data.

At a strictly positive optimizer, the nonnegativity constraints are inactive. The first-order conditions for the order constraints give nonnegative multipliers \(c^{rq}\) satisfying

\[ \beta_v^r z_r-a_v^r +\sum_{q:(r,q)\in C}c^{rq} -\sum_{q:(q,r)\in C}c^{qr}=0, \]

and \(c^{rq}=0\) whenever \(z_r<z_q\). These are exactly (1) and (2).

These multiplier conditions remain valid when \(C\) has cycles. To justify this without a strict-feasibility assumption, let \(D\) contain the rows \(e_r-e_q\) for constraints tight at the optimizer. Since the optimizer is positive, every direction \(d\) with \(Dd\leq0\) is feasible for sufficiently small positive steps. Optimality implies

\[ \nabla Q_v(z)\cdot d\geq0. \]

Farkas' lemma therefore gives \(-\nabla Q_v(z)=D^\mathsf{T}c\) for some \(c\geq0\), which is the asserted stationarity condition.

Conversely, any positive \(z\) and nonnegative \(c\) satisfying (1) and (2) make \(z\) optimal in (3). For any feasible \(y\),

\[ \nabla Q_v(z)\cdot(y-z) =-\sum_{(r,q)\in C}c^{rq} \bigl((y_r-y_q)-(z_r-z_q)\bigr)\geq0. \]

Here complementary slackness eliminates the second difference, and feasibility gives \(y_r-y_q\leq0\). Convexity then implies \(Q_v(y)\geq Q_v(z)\).

4. Positive optimizers and zero-budget coordinates

Fix one positive-price exact equilibrium, whose existence is promised, and denote its reciprocal prices by \(z^*\). This equilibrium is used only to prove correctness; the algorithm does not need it as input.

Uniqueness on positive-budget coordinates

Every two optimizers \(u,z\) of the same local program agree wherever \(\beta_v^r>0\). Indeed,

\[ Q_v\!\left(\frac{u+z}{2}\right) =\frac{Q_v(u)+Q_v(z)}2 -\frac18\sum_r\beta_v^r(u_r-z_r)^2. \]

The midpoint is feasible. If the final sum were positive, it would have objective value below the minimum. Thus \(u_r=z_r\) whenever \(\beta_v^r>0\).

A consequence of nondegeneracy

For a resource \(r\), let \(R(r)\) be the resources reachable from \(r\), including \(r\) itself. For every node \(v\) and resource \(r\), there is some \(q\in R(r)\) with \(\beta_v^q>0\).

Otherwise, the nonempty set

\[ \operatorname{SubT}(v)\times R(r) \]

would be closed under downward hierarchy edges and conversion edges. Every local budget in this set would be zero: this follows from \(\beta_v^q=0\) and nonnegativity of local budgets. That contradicts the stated nondegeneracy assumption.

Choosing positive zero-budget coordinates

Suppose a local optimizer \(z\) has positive coordinates wherever \(\beta_v^r>0\). Put \(P_v=\{r:\beta_v^r>0\}\), retain these coordinates, and replace every other coordinate by

\[ \widehat z_r=\min_{q\in R(r)\cap P_v}z_q. \tag{4} \]

The minimum is over a nonempty set, so every coordinate of \(\widehat z\) is positive.

Moreover, \(\widehat z_r\geq z_r\) on zero-budget coordinates: the original order inequalities imply \(z_r\leq z_q\) for every reachable \(q\).

The replacement preserves each edge inequality:

  • If both endpoints have positive budget, their coordinates are unchanged.
  • If only the destination has positive budget, its value is among the candidates in (4).
  • If both have zero budget, \(R(q)\subseteq R(r)\) for an edge \((r,q)\), so \(\widehat z_r\leq\widehat z_q\).
  • If only the source has positive budget, \(z_r\leq z_q\leq\widehat z_q\).

For a zero-budget coordinate, \(a_v^r=s_v^r\geq0\). Consequently,

\[ Q_v(\widehat z)-Q_v(z) =-\sum_{r\notin P_v}s_v^r(\widehat z_r-z_r)\leq0. \]

Since \(z\) was already optimal and \(\widehat z\) is feasible, \(\widehat z\) is also optimal.

Correctness of the root-to-leaf computation

At the root, \(z_o^*\) is a positive optimizer by Section 3. Therefore every optimizer agrees with it on \(P_o\), and the replacement (4) produces a positive optimizer.

Inductively, suppose the computed parent vector agrees with \(z_{\pi(v)}^*\) wherever \(\beta_{\pi(v)}^r>0\). If \(\beta_v^r>0\), then \(\beta_{\pi(v)}^r>0\). Hence the computed coefficient

\[ a_v^r=s_v^r+\beta_v^r z_{\pi(v)}^r \]

equals its coefficient in the reference equilibrium. If \(\beta_v^r=0\), that coefficient is simply \(s_v^r\), independently of the parent.

Thus the entire local program has the same coefficients as the reference program. It has the positive optimizer \(z_v^*\), all optimizers agree with it on \(P_v\), and (4) again produces a positive optimizer.

This establishes the root-to-leaf algorithm.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

We use the following standard algorithmic theorem: a convex quadratic objective with rational positive semidefinite Hessian, minimized over a rational polyhedron with an attained finite minimum, admits computation of an exact rational optimizer in polynomial time in its binary encoding length. This is the exact polynomial-time result of [Kozlov, Tarasov, and Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189).

Every local program satisfies these hypotheses. Section 4 proves existence of an optimizer, and its Hessian is positive semidefinite. However, a separate argument is needed to prevent encoding lengths from growing excessively across successive nodes.

A uniform bound on all computed reciprocal prices

At each processed node, form an undirected graph consisting of conversion edges whose endpoints have equal computed reciprocal prices. Call its connected components \(K\).

Every component contains a resource with positive \(\beta_v^r\). To see this, take a zero-budget resource \(r\). In (4), choose a reachable positive-budget resource \(q\) attaining the minimum. Along a directed path from \(r\) to \(q\), all computed reciprocal prices lie between the equal endpoint values. Every edge of that path is therefore tight.

Let \(y_{v,K}\) denote the common reciprocal price on component \(K\). Summing stationarity over \(K\) cancels multipliers on internal edges. Multipliers on edges crossing between components are zero, because those edges are not tight. Hence

\[ \left(\sum_{r\in K}\beta_v^r\right)y_{v,K} -\sum_{r\in K}\beta_v^r z_{\pi(v)}^r =\sum_{r\in K}s_v^r, \tag{5} \]

where the parent term is omitted at the root.

For any processed prefix of the hierarchy, these equations form a square linear system in the component values \(y_{v,K}\). In hierarchy order, its matrix is lower triangular. Its diagonal entries are

\[ \sum_{r\in K}\beta_v^r>0, \]

so the system is nonsingular.

There are at most \(hm\) unknowns. Every coefficient is a sum of original aggregate budgets or supplies and therefore has polynomial encoding length. Clearing denominators, Cramer's rule and Hadamard's determinant bound show that every component value has polynomial encoding length in \(h,m,\tau,L\).

This bound applies to every processed prefix. Thus each succeeding local quadratic program has polynomial input length. There are \(h\) such programs; replacement (4) requires only graph reachability and rational comparisons.

Computing conversions

After choosing a positive local optimizer, compute compatible conversions by solving the rational linear feasibility problem

\[ \sum_{q:(r,q)\in C}c^{rq} -\sum_{q:(q,r)\in C}c^{qr} =a_v^r-\beta_v^r z_v^r, \]

with

\[ c^{rq}\geq0,\qquad c^{rq}=0\ \text{if }z_v^r<z_v^q. \]

Section 3 proves feasibility. Rational linear programming computes a rational solution of polynomial encoding length in polynomial time.

Thus both prices and conversions are computed exactly with polynomial bit complexity.

6. Recovery and verification of the equilibrium

Output

\[ p_v^r=\frac1{z_v^r},\qquad f_o^r=0,\qquad f_v^r=\beta_v^r z_{\pi(v)}^r\quad(v\ne o), \qquad x_t^r=b_t^r z_{v(t)}^r, \]

together with the computed conversions.

All entries are rational with polynomial encoding length. Prices are strictly positive; flows, conversions, and allocations are nonnegative.

Physical conservation follows from (1), because

\[ A_v^r+\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}f_w^r =\left(B_v^r+\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}\beta_w^r\right)z_v^r =\beta_v^r z_v^r. \]

For each nonroot node, money conservation holds exactly:

\[ p_v^r\left(A_v^r+\sum_{w\in\operatorname{ch}(v)}f_w^r\right) =\beta_v^r =p_{\pi(v)}^r f_v^r. \]

The order inequalities give \(p_v^r\geq p_v^q\) on every conversion edge. Any positive conversion has equal endpoint reciprocal prices and therefore equal endpoint prices, so every complementary product is zero. Finally, \(x_t^r=b_t^r/p_{v(t)}^r\) holds exactly.

The output is consequently an exact equilibrium of the original support-encoded society. Taking its allocation as \(x^*\), the allocation-distance requirement is

\[ \sum_{t,r}\mu_t|x_t^r-x_t^{r,*}|=0. \]

Every conservation residual and complementary-product residual is also zero. The output is therefore \(\epsilon\)-accurate for every requested \(\epsilon>0\).

Conclusion

Binary rational masses introduce no replication cost: their effect is captured exactly by aggregate local budgets. The algorithm computes an exact rational equilibrium in polynomial time in \(h,m,\tau,L\), and hence computes an \(\epsilon\)-accurate equilibrium within the requested time bound.
