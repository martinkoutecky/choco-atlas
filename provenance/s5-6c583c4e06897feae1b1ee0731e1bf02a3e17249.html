The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\) and rational per-unit population contributions \(a_t>0\), let \(P(x)=\sum_t\mu_t x_t\), \(R(x)=\sum_t\mu_t a_t x_t/\sum_t\mu_t a_t\), and \(x\in[0,1]^\tau\) record each type's fraction of yes votes. Define \(F(x)=1\) iff \((P(x)\ge 11/20\land R(x)\ge 13/20)\lor(P(x)\ge 25/28)\). A weighted rule is \(u_{\lambda,q}(x)=1\) iff \(\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_t x_t\ge q\), for \(\lambda_t\ge0\). Find the minimum size of a monotone \(\wedge/\vee\) formula over weighted rules that agrees with \(F\) for every \(x\in[0,1]^\tau\), and give the formula and a proof of minimality.

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\) encoded as binary rationals; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a binary-rational per-unit population contribution \(a_t>0\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).
For \(x\in[0,1]^\tau\), let \(x_t\) be the fraction of type \(t\) voting yes, \(P(x)=\sum_t\mu_t x_t\), and \(R(x)=\frac{\sum_t\mu_t a_t x_t}{\sum_t\mu_t a_t}\).
Define \(F(x)=1\) exactly when \((P(x)\ge11/20\land R(x)\ge13/20)\lor P(x)\ge25/28\), and otherwise set \(F(x)=0\).
An admissible weighted rule satisfies \(u_{\lambda,q}(x)=1\) exactly when \(\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_t x_t\ge q\), with real \(\lambda_t\ge0\) and \(0<q\le\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_t\).

Exact classification

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). The minimum and an attaining formula can also be computed in polynomial time.

Write \(\alpha=11/20\), \(\beta=13/20\), \(\gamma=25/28\), and \(T=\sum_t\mu_ta_t>0\). Define

  • \(A(x)=\mathbf 1_{\{P(x)\ge\alpha\}}\);
  • \(B(x)=\mathbf 1_{\{R(x)\ge\beta\}}\);
  • \(C(x)=\mathbf 1_{\{P(x)\ge\gamma\}}\).

Thus \(F=(A\wedge B)\vee C\). These are admissible leaves: \(A,C\) use \(\lambda_t=1\), with quotas \(\alpha,\gamma\), and \(B\) uses \(\lambda_t=a_t\), with quota \(\beta T\).

Let \(U=\max\{R(x):x\in[0,1]^\tau,\ P(x)=\alpha\}\) and \(V=\min\{R(x):x\in[0,1]^\tau,\ P(x)=\gamma\}\). Both extrema exist because their feasible sets are nonempty and compact.

Theorem. The exact minimum is \(b(F)=1+\mathbf 1_{\{U>\beta\}}+\mathbf 1_{\{V<\beta\}}\). More explicitly:

| Condition | Minimum | Attaining formula |
|---|---:|---|
| \(U\le\beta\) | \(1\) | \(B\) |
| \(U>\beta\) and \(V\ge\beta\) | \(2\) | \(A\wedge B\) |
| \(V<\beta\) | \(3\) | \((A\wedge B)\vee C\) |

The last condition implies \(U>\beta\), as proved below. All statements include threshold equality.

Removing redundant leaves

Suppose first that \(U\le\beta\). We claim that \(R(x)\ge\beta\) implies \(P(x)\ge\alpha\).

Indeed, if \(P(x)<\alpha\), choose \(s=(\alpha-P(x))/(1-P(x))\) and set \(y=(1-s)x+s\mathbf 1\). Then \(0<s<1\), \(P(y)=\alpha\), and \(R(y)>R(x)\). The last inequality follows from \(R(x)<1\): since \(P(x)<1\), some coordinate is below \(1\), and every \(\mu_ta_t\) is positive. Consequently \(R(x)<R(y)\le U\le\beta\), proving the claim. Hence \(A\wedge B=B\).

Next suppose that \(V\ge\beta\). If \(P(x)\ge\gamma\), set \(y=(\gamma/P(x))x\). Then \(y\in[0,1]^\tau\), \(P(y)=\gamma\), and \(R(x)=(P(x)/\gamma)R(y)\ge V\ge\beta\). Also \(P(x)\ge\gamma>\alpha\). Thus \(C\) implies \(A\wedge B\), so \(F=A\wedge B\).

For these particular thresholds, \(V<\beta\) forces \(U>\beta\). To prove this, choose \(x\) with \(P(x)=\gamma\) and \(R(x)<\beta\), and put \(y=\mathbf 1-x\). Then \(P(y)=3/28\) and \(R(y)>7/20\).

Set \(s=62/125\) and \(z=(1-s)y+s\mathbf 1\). Direct calculation gives \(P(z)=11/20=\alpha\) and \(R(z)>(63/125)(7/20)+62/125=1681/2500>13/20=\beta\). Therefore \(U>\beta\).

These implications establish every formula in the table, including when \(U=\beta\) or \(V=\beta\).

Lower bounds from exposed boundary patches

We now prove optimality against all admissible weighted leaves, including leaves with arbitrary real weights.

Boundary-covering lemma

For a leaf with weights \(w_t=\mu_t\lambda_t\), let \(H=\{x:\sum_tw_tx_t=q\}\). Its normal vector is nonzero because \(q>0\) and \(q\le\sum_tw_t\).

For any finite formula, every boundary point of its winning set inside \((0,1)^\tau\) lies on a threshold hyperplane of some leaf. Otherwise every leaf, and hence the formula, would be constant throughout a sufficiently small neighborhood of that point.

Moreover, if this boundary contains a nonempty relatively open subset of a hyperplane \(H_0\), some leaf hyperplane must equal \(H_0\). For \(\tau\ge2\), the intersection of a different hyperplane with \(H_0\) is empty or has lower dimension. A finite union of such intersections cannot cover a relatively open subset of \(H_0\): each has zero measure in \(H_0\), whereas an open subset has positive measure. For \(\tau=1\), hyperplanes are singleton points, so the conclusion follows directly.

The three possible patches

The boundary always contains a relatively open patch of \(H_B=\{x:R(x)=\beta\}\). At \(x=\beta\mathbf 1\), all coordinates are interior and \(P(x)=R(x)=\beta\). Since \(\alpha<\beta<\gamma\), throughout a sufficiently small neighborhood \(F=B\). Thus the portion of \(H_B\) in that neighborhood is a boundary patch.

If \(U>\beta\), the boundary also contains a relatively open patch of \(H_A=\{x:P(x)=\alpha\}\). Let \(x^*\) attain \(U\). For sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), the point \(x_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)x^*+\varepsilon\alpha\mathbf 1\) lies in \((0,1)^\tau\), satisfies \(P(x_\varepsilon)=\alpha\), and has \(R(x_\varepsilon)>\beta\). In a small neighborhood of this point, \(R>\beta\) and \(P<\gamma\), so \(F=A\).

If \(V<\beta\), the boundary similarly contains a relatively open patch of \(H_C=\{x:P(x)=\gamma\}\). Mix a minimizer of \(V\) with \(\gamma\mathbf 1\) by a sufficiently small positive amount. The resulting point is interior, has \(P=\gamma\), and retains \(R<\beta\). In a small neighborhood, \(F=C\).

Finally, these three hyperplanes are distinct. The hyperplanes \(H_A,H_C\) have different quotas. Write \(c_t=\mu_t\) and \(d_t=\mu_ta_t/T\); both coefficient vectors sum to \(1\). If \(H_B\) equalled \(H_A\) or \(H_C\), equality of hyperplanes would force \(d=sc\). Summing coordinates gives \(s=1\), which would force respectively \(\beta=\alpha\) or \(\beta=\gamma\), a contradiction.

The boundary-covering lemma therefore requires at least \(1+\mathbf 1_{\{U>\beta\}}+\mathbf 1_{\{V<\beta\}}\) distinct leaf hyperplanes, and hence at least that many leaf occurrences. The formulas already constructed attain this bound, completing the theorem.

Exact algorithm and running time

Both extrema are fractional-knapsack calculations.

For a fixed \(P(x)=s\), put \(y_t=\mu_tx_t\). Then \(0\le y_t\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_ty_t=s\), and \(TR(x)=\sum_ta_ty_t\).

To maximize this sum, sort the types by decreasing \(a_t\) and fill their capacities in that order. This is optimal because whenever \(a_i\ge a_j\), \(y_i<\mu_i\), and \(y_j>0\), transferring \(\delta=\min\{\mu_i-y_i,y_j\}\) from \(j\) to \(i\) changes the objective by \(\delta(a_i-a_j)\ge0\). Repeating these transfers produces the prefix-fill solution. Sorting increasingly gives the minimum by the same argument with the inequality reversed.

For either ordering, let \(S_j=\sum_{i=1}^j\mu_i\), and choose the first \(j\) with \(S_j\ge s\). The extremal numerator is \(\sum_{i<j}\mu_i a_i+(s-S_{j-1})a_j\). Use decreasing order with \(s=\alpha\) to obtain \(U\), and increasing order with \(s=\gamma\) to obtain \(V\). One sort suffices for both passes.

The algorithm uses \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) exact rational arithmetic operations and comparisons. Reduced intermediate rationals have \(O(L)\) bits: their denominators divide products of input denominators and fixed threshold denominators, and their numerator lengths are also \(O(L)\).

Using elementary integer arithmetic and a conservative \(O(L^3)\) bound per rational operation, a valid bit-time bound is \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^3+\log(k+1))\). The attaining formula has \(O(L)\) output bits. There is no dependence on the numerical value of \(k\) beyond reading its encoding.

Specifically:

  • For \(k=1\), return yes exactly when \(U\le\beta\).
  • For \(k=2\), return yes exactly when \(V\ge\beta\).
  • For \(k\ge3\), return yes.

Thus both decision and optimization are polynomial-time solvable.

Sharpness and scope

All three minima occur:

| Masses | Contributions | \(U\) | \(V\) | \(b(F)\) |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| \(\mu=(1)\) | \(a=(1)\) | \(11/20\) | \(25/28\) | \(1\) |
| \(\mu=(1/2,1/2)\) | \(a=(3,1)\) | \(31/40\) | \(47/56\) | \(2\) |
