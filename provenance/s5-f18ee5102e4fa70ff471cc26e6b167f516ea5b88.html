The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational applicant-type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_s\) of job types \(s\), their interval and processing data, and each type's utilities \(u_t(s)\), define \(M_t\) as the largest \(z\) for which a unit mass of type-\(t\) recipients can be assigned feasible integral bundles with utility at least \(z\), using at most \(q_s\) units of each job type in aggregate. Can one find, in time polynomial in the type supports, input bit length \(L\), and \(1/\epsilon\), finite-support assignment masses \(x_{t,B}\) such that \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B} n_s(B)x_{t,B}\le q_s\) for every \(s\), and every positive-mass bundle satisfies \(u_t(B)\ge 0.24(1-\epsilon)M_t\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) applicant types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) supported job types, with positive binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_s\), not necessarily summing to \(1\), and nonnegative binary-rational utilities \(u_t(s)\). Each has binary-encoded integers \(r_s,d_s,p_s\geq1\) with \(p_s\leq d_s-r_s+1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including rational \(0<\epsilon<1\).
An integral bundle \(B\) is a multiset with binary-encoded counts \(n_s(B)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\). It is feasible if its copies can each occupy \(p_s\) consecutive slots in \(\{r_s,\ldots,d_s\}\), without overlap on one recipient; repeated copies of a type are allowed. Write \(\mathcal B\) for these bundles, including the empty bundle, and \(u_t(B)=\sum_s n_s(B)u_t(s)\).

This hypothetical unit society consists entirely of type \(t\); \(M_t\) is not supplied as input.

Can an assignment satisfying \(x_{t,B}>0\Rightarrow u_t(B)\geq0.24(1-\epsilon)M_t\) always be found in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\)?

For finite agents and individually listed jobs, a polynomial-time \(0.24(1-\epsilon)\)-MMS guarantee is stated by [Li, Li and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.11648); [Hummel](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.11582) questions its scheduling approximation premise and proves a polynomial-time \(2/7\)-MMS guarantee and \(1/2\)-MMS existence.

A polynomial-time construction

Yes. An assignment satisfying the requested guarantee can be found in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon$.

The proof combines three ingredients:

  • An exact reduction of repeated job types to polynomially many explicitly listed block jobs.
  • A budgeted scheduling procedure derived from a known weighted throughput approximation.
  • A rational cutting-plane procedure that converts the budgeted procedure into a distribution satisfying every supply inequality exactly.

Throughout, “binary-rational” means a rational represented by binary-encoded numerator and denominator, as in the supplied notes.

1. Benchmarks and reduction to one applicant type

Put
$$ K_s=\left\lfloor\frac{d_s-r_s+1}{p_s}\right\rfloor. $$

Every feasible bundle satisfies $n_s(B)\leq K_s$: its copies of type $s$ occupy disjoint intervals inside a window of length $d_s-r_s+1$. Thus there are finitely many feasible count vectors, and $\mathcal B$ is finite.

Consequently, each $M_t$ is attained. Indeed, every feasible distribution witnesses a threshold equal to the minimum utility among its supported bundles. That minimum belongs to the finite set of feasible bundle utilities. Threshold zero is feasible, so there is a largest feasible threshold.

It suffices to solve the problem for one applicant type. Suppose that, for each $t$, we construct rational weights $y^t_B$ such that
$$ \sum_B y^t_B=1,\qquad \sum_B n_s(B)y^t_B\leq q_s, $$
and every positively weighted bundle meets the desired utility guarantee for $t$. Define
$$ x_{t,B}=\mu_t y^t_B. $$
Then $\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t$, and
$$ \sum_{t,B}n_s(B)x_{t,B} \leq \sum_t\mu_tq_s =q_s. $$
The utility guarantees are preserved.

Henceforth fix one applicant type, write its utility as $u$, and write its benchmark as $M$.

We may replace each supply by $\min\{q_s,K_s\}$. Every distribution over feasible bundles already has expected count at most $K_s$, so this replacement changes neither feasible distributions nor $M$. Retain the notation $q_s$ for these clipped supplies. With
$$ D=\max_s d_s, $$
we now have $0<q_s\leq K_s\leq D$.

2. Exact compression into explicitly listed block jobs

Lemma 1: a bounded number of runs

Every feasible bundle has an integer schedule consisting of at most
$$ R=6m^2+2m $$
runs. A run consists of consecutive copies of one job type, with no intervening idle time.

Proof. Represent a copy starting at integer $a$ by the half-open interval $[a,a+p_s)$, inside $[r_s,d_s+1)$. Let
$$ E=\{r_s,d_s+1:s=1,\ldots,m\}. $$

Mark a scheduled copy if its interval contains some point of $E$ strictly inside it. At most one copy can contain any particular point strictly inside its interval, because copies do not overlap. Therefore at most $2m$ copies are marked.

Cut the time line at every point of $E$ and at both endpoints of every marked copy. There are at most $6m$ distinct cut points.

An unmarked copy cannot cross a point of $E$. It also cannot cross an endpoint of a marked copy, since that would cause overlap with that copy. Thus every unmarked copy lies inside one cell between consecutive cut points.

If a type occurs in a cell, its availability window contains the entire cell: both window endpoints are cut points, and the window contains an interval of positive length inside that cell. The total processing time of the unmarked copies in the cell is at most its length.

Repack those copies consecutively from the beginning of the cell, grouped by type. This remains inside every relevant window and does not intersect a marked copy. It creates at most $m$ runs per cell. Keep marked copies in place, treating each as one additional run.

There are fewer than $6m$ cells and at most $2m$ marked copies. The asserted bound follows. All repacked start times are integers. $\square$

Lemma 2: a polynomial-size block representation

Construct an explicitly listed scheduling instance as follows. For every type $s$ and every integer
$$ 0\leq j\leq\lfloor\log_2K_s\rfloor, $$
include $R$ distinct block jobs. Each such block has:

  • availability window $[r_s,d_s+1)$;
  • processing time $2^jp_s$;
  • utility $2^ju(s)$;
  • represented count $2^j$ of original type $s$.

The number of listed blocks is
$$ N=R\sum_s\bigl(1+\lfloor\log_2K_s\rfloor\bigr), $$
which is polynomial in $m,L$. Every block has polynomial encoding length.

The feasible original bundles are exactly the count vectors represented by schedulable subsets of these blocks.

Proof. Splitting any scheduled block into $2^j$ consecutive original copies gives a feasible original bundle.

Conversely, take the schedule from Lemma 1. Decompose the multiplicity of each run into its binary expansion, and split that run into consecutive blocks of the corresponding sizes. Each run uses at most one block of any size. There are at most $R$ runs in total, so the list contains enough distinct blocks of every type and size. This represents the original bundle exactly. $\square$

In particular, any additive utility or additive price attached to original copies is preserved by this representation. The block list can be constructed without knowing any feasible bundle or schedule.

3. The scheduling approximation used

We use the following established theorem.

Weighted throughput theorem. Given explicitly listed jobs with processing times, weights, and allowed start-time windows, for every fixed rational $0<\delta<1$, a polynomial-time algorithm returns a nonoverlapping subset of weight at least
$$ \frac{1-\delta}{2}\,\mathrm{OPT}. $$
Its running time is polynomial in the number of windows, their binary encoding length, and $1/\delta$.

The time-window implementation and its guarantee are given in Sections 3.2.1 and 3.3.1 of [Bar-Noy, Bar-Yehuda, Freund, Naor and Schieber, *A Unified Approach to Approximating Resource Allocation and Scheduling*](https://csaws.cs.technion.ac.il/~reuven/PDF/BarBar.pdf). The implementation uses polynomially many rational arithmetic operations; profit updates and window endpoints retain polynomial bit length.

Here is how its time model applies to our integer-slot jobs. For a job with integer release $r$, processing time $p$, and completion deadline $d+1$, give the theorem the allowed start window
$$ [r,d+2-p). $$
Every feasible integer start is included. If the algorithm returns real starts, round all of them down. For consecutive scheduled jobs,
$$ a_i+p_i\leq a_{i+1} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lfloor a_i\rfloor+p_i\leq\lfloor a_{i+1}\rfloor, $$
because $p_i$ is integral. Also,
$$ r_i\leq\lfloor a_i\rfloor\leq d_i+1-p_i. $$
Thus rounding preserves nonoverlap and yields feasible integer-slot schedules.

Fix
$$ \delta=\frac1{25}. $$
The resulting weighted scheduling algorithm has guarantee
$$ \beta=\frac{12}{25}. $$
It applies to the block instance, including after blocks are removed or their nonnegative weights are changed.

4. A budgeted scheduling procedure

Lemma 3: meeting a utility target within an additive budget

Give each listed block $i$ a nonnegative rational utility $v_i$ and cost $c_i$. Given rational $z>0$ and $C\geq0$, there is a polynomial-time procedure that either reports failure or returns a schedulable subset $A$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{i\in A}c_i\leq C, \qquad \sum_{i\in A}v_i\geq\frac6{25}z. $$
It never fails if a schedulable subset of cost at most $C$ and utility at least $z$ exists.

Proof. First suppose $C=0$. Remove all positive-cost blocks and run the weighted scheduling algorithm with weights $v_i$. Under the stated promise, the optimum remaining weight is at least $z$, so the returned utility is at least $(12/25)z$. Check the required utility inequality and return the result if it holds.

Now suppose $C>0$. Set
$$ \lambda=\frac{z}{2C}. $$
Remove blocks with $c_i>C$, and then remove blocks with $v_i-\lambda c_i\leq0$. Run the weighted scheduling algorithm on the remaining blocks with weights
$$ w_i=v_i-\lambda c_i>0. $$

Under the promise, take a feasible subset $A^\star$ of cost at most $C$ and utility at least $z$. None of its blocks has cost greater than $C$. Its total residual weight is at least
$$ \sum_{i\in A^\star}(v_i-\lambda c_i) \geq z-\lambda C =\frac z2. $$
Deleting its nonpositive residual-weight blocks preserves feasibility and cannot decrease that residual sum. Therefore the scheduling algorithm returns a subset $A$ with
$$ \sum_{i\in A}w_i \geq \frac{12}{25}\frac z2 =\frac6{25}z. $$

If $A$ has cost at most $C$, its utility is at least its residual weight, and we return it.

Suppose instead that its cost exceeds $C$. Every block in $A$ has cost at most $C$ and satisfies $v_i>\lambda c_i$.

If some block has cost at least $C/2$, its singleton is within budget and has utility at least $z/4$. Otherwise every block costs less than $C/2$. Add blocks until their total cost first reaches $C/2$. This is possible because $A$ costs more than $C$. The selected subset has cost less than $C$ and utility greater than
$$ \lambda\frac C2=\frac z4. $$
Removing blocks preserves scheduling feasibility. Since $1/4>6/25$, either case gives the required result.

For instances without the promise, the procedure checks the returned cost and utility and reports failure if necessary. Thus every successful output is valid. $\square$

5. From a benchmark distribution to a pricing oracle

Fix a candidate threshold $z>0$. For any nonnegative rational price vector $\pi=(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_m)$, give each original copy of type $s$ price $\pi_s$. Its blocks consequently have costs $2^j\pi_s$. Set
$$ C=\sum_s\pi_sq_s. $$

If $z\leq M$, take a benchmark distribution supported on bundles of utility at least $z$. Its expected price is at most $C$, so at least one supported bundle has price at most $C$.

Lemma 2 represents that bundle exactly by scheduled blocks. Lemma 3 therefore succeeds and returns a feasible original bundle $B$ satisfying
$$ u(B)\geq\frac6{25}z, \qquad \sum_s\pi_sn_s(B)\leq\sum_s\pi_sq_s. $$

We have obtained a polynomial-time pricing procedure with the following properties:

  • Every successful output is a feasible bundle of utility at least $(6/25)z$ and price at most $\pi\cdot q$.
  • If $z\leq M$, the procedure succeeds for every nonnegative rational $\pi$.

The next lemma supplies exact rational distribution weights from this procedure.

6. Recovering a distribution with exact supply bounds

Lemma 4: rational recovery from a pricing procedure

Let $\mathcal A$ be a finite, possibly implicitly represented subset of $\{0,\ldots,D\}^m$, and let $q\in(0,D]^m$ be rational.

Suppose a polynomial-time procedure, on input any nonnegative rational vector $\pi$, either reports failure or returns $a\in\mathcal A$ with
$$ \pi\cdot a\leq\pi\cdot q. $$

There is a polynomial-time algorithm that either reports failure or produces polynomially many vectors $a^j\in\mathcal A$ and rational weights $y_j\geq0$ satisfying
$$ \sum_jy_j=1, \qquad \sum_jy_ja^j\leq q $$
coordinatewise. If the pricing procedure never fails, recovery succeeds.

Proof. We give the precision and termination argument explicitly.

Let $Q$ be the product of the denominators of $q_s$, and define
$$ b_a=Q(a-q),\qquad H=QD. $$
Then every $b_a$ is an integer vector with coordinates of absolute value at most $H$. Put
$$ T=(m+1)!(H+1)^{m+1}, \qquad g=\frac1{2T}, \qquad \rho=\frac1{32T(mH+1)}. $$
All these numbers have polynomial encoding length.

We first establish a uniform geometric fact for any nonempty collected family $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal A$.

Suppose its desired distribution does not exist. Equivalently,
$$ \operatorname{conv}\{b_a:a\in\mathcal C\} \cap\mathbb R_{\leq0}^m =\varnothing. $$
The first set is compact and the second is closed and convex. Strict separation gives a nonnegative vector $\pi$ such that
$$ \min_{a\in\mathcal C}\pi\cdot b_a>0. $$
The separating vector must be nonnegative because the second set is unbounded in every negative coordinate direction. Normalize it to $\sum_s\pi_s=1$.

Consider the bounded linear program maximizing $\gamma$ subject to
$$ \pi\geq0,\qquad \sum_s\pi_s=1,\qquad b_a\cdot\pi\geq\gamma\quad(a\in\mathcal C), \qquad -H\leq\gamma\leq H. $$
Its optimum is positive. At an optimal vertex, the $m+1$ coordinates solve a nonsingular integer system whose coefficients have absolute value at most $H+1$. Cramer’s rule bounds its denominator by
$$ (m+1)!(H+1)^{m+1}=T. $$
Its positive objective value is therefore at least $1/T$. Thus some $\pi^\star$ in the simplex satisfies
$$ b_a\cdot\pi^\star\geq\frac1T \quad(a\in\mathcal C). $$

Set
$$ \theta=\frac1{8T(mH+1)}, \qquad \pi'=(1-\theta)\pi^\star+\theta(1/2,\ldots,1/2). $$
Every coordinate of $\pi'$ is at distance at least $\theta/2$ from the boundary of $[0,1]^m$. Furthermore,
$$ b_a\cdot\pi' \geq \frac{1-\theta}{T}-\frac{\theta mH}{2} \geq\frac7{8T}. $$
For any $\pi$ with $\|\pi-\pi'\|_2\leq\rho=\theta/4$,
$$ |b_a\cdot(\pi-\pi')| \leq \sqrt m\,H\rho \leq mH\rho \leq\frac1{32T}. $$
Consequently the polytope
$$ P_{\mathcal C} = \left\{ \pi\in[0,1]^m: b_a\cdot\pi\geq g\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal C \right\} $$
contains a Euclidean ball of radius $\rho$. For an empty family, this conclusion holds directly.

Now run a rational ellipsoid cutting-plane procedure, beginning with a ball of radius $m$ containing $[0,1]^m$. We use the standard ellipsoid theorem in its following form: given rational half-space cuts excluding queried centers, polynomially many cuts suffice to reduce the enclosing volume below that of a prescribed ball of radius $\rho$; the required number is
$$ O\bigl(m^2(1+\log(m/\rho))\bigr). $$
The rational implementation has polynomial bit complexity. In dimension one, ordinary interval halving gives the same conclusion.

At a queried center $\pi$:

  • If $\pi\notin[0,1]^m$, use a violated coordinate bound as the cut.
  • Otherwise call the pricing procedure. If it fails, report failure.
  • If it returns $a$, record $a$. We have $b_a\cdot\pi\leq0<g$, so impose the cut

$$ b_a\cdot w\geq g. $$
A central relaxation of this cut excludes the queried center and retains every point satisfying the displayed inequality.

If $b_a=0$, the singleton distribution on $a=q$ already solves the problem.

After the prescribed number of cuts, solve the explicit rational linear program
$$ y_a\geq0,\qquad \sum_{a\in\mathcal C}y_a=1,\qquad \sum_{a\in\mathcal C}y_aa\leq q $$
over the collected vectors.

If this program were infeasible, the geometric fact above would imply that $P_{\mathcal C}$ contains a ball of radius $\rho$. Every cut made during the procedure retains $P_{\mathcal C}$: coordinate cuts retain its box, and bundle cuts retain its defining inequalities. Hence the final ellipsoid would contain that ball, contradicting its volume bound.

Thus, whenever the pricing procedure has not failed, the explicit program is feasible. Polynomial-time rational linear programming returns the required weights with polynomial encoding length. Only polynomially many vectors were collected, and all cut coefficients have the polynomial encoding bounds established above. $\square$

Applying the lemma

For our candidate $z$, let
$$ \mathcal A(z)= \left\{ (n_1(B),\ldots,n_m(B)): B\in\mathcal B,\ u(B)\geq\frac6{25}z \right\}. $$
This is finite, and its coordinates are at most $D$. The pricing procedure from Section 5 supplies exactly the oracle required by Lemma 4.

We therefore have a polynomial-time threshold test with two rigorously established properties:

  • If $z\leq M$, it succeeds.
  • Every successful test returns a rational distribution satisfying the supplies exactly and supported on feasible bundles of utility at least $(6/25)z$.

The test may succeed when $z>M$; this causes no problem.

7. Choosing thresholds without computing the benchmark

First determine whether $M=0$. Let
$$ S^+=\{s:u(s)>0\}. $$
Then
$$ M>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_{s\in S^+}q_s\geq1. $$

For necessity, every positive-utility bundle contains at least one positive-utility copy. Any distribution supported on such bundles therefore satisfies
$$ 1 \leq \sum_B y_B\sum_{s\in S^+}n_s(B) \leq \sum_{s\in S^+}q_s. $$

For sufficiency, choose rational singleton masses $a_s$ with
$$ 0\leq a_s\leq q_s,\qquad \sum_{s\in S^+}a_s=1. $$
Such masses can be chosen greedily. Every singleton is schedulable by the input hypothesis. Their utilities are positive.

If $M=0$, return the empty bundle. Otherwise define
$$ u_{\min}=\min_{s\in S^+}u(s), \qquad U=\sum_sK_su(s). $$
The singleton construction and the bundle-count bounds give
$$ u_{\min}\leq M\leq U. $$

Test every geometric threshold
$$ z_j=u_{\min}(1+\epsilon)^j $$
up to and including the first threshold at least $U$. Retain the distribution from the largest successful threshold.

The number of thresholds is
$$ O\left( \frac{1+\log(U/u_{\min})}{\epsilon} \right), $$
and both that number and every threshold’s rational encoding length are polynomial in $m,L,1/\epsilon$.

Thus every bundle in the retained distribution has utility at least
$$ \frac6{25}z_\star \geq \frac6{25(1+\epsilon)}M \geq \frac6{25}(1-\epsilon)M = 0.24(1-\epsilon)M. $$
The second inequality follows from $(1+\epsilon)(1-\epsilon)\leq1$.
