The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\), platforms \(P\), eligibility sets \(N_t\), rankings \(R_t\), a disjoint group map \(g\), rational mass quotas \(\ell_{p,h},u_{p,h}\), and bounds \(L_{t,k},U_{t,k}\), choose a finite-support, type-symmetric distribution over measurable assignments of atomless agents, each assigned to at most one eligible platform, such that every realization satisfies \(\ell_{p,h}\le X_{p,h}\le u_{p,h}\), every individual of type \(t\) has top-\(k\) assignment probability in \([L_{t,k},U_{t,k}]\), and expected matched mass is maximized; return an optimum or an infeasibility certificate.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses need not sum to one. Represent type \(t\) by the atomless interval \(A_t=[0,\mu_t)\) with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\), keeping these intervals disjoint.
There are \(m\geq1\) platforms \(P\), eligibility sets \(N_t\subseteq P\), strict rankings \(R_t\) of all platforms, and a surjective group map \(g:T\to\{1,\ldots,\chi\}\). Write \(R_{t,k}\) for the first \(k\) platforms in \(R_t\), for \(1\leq k\leq m\).
Input bounds are binary rationals satisfying \(0\leq\ell_{p,h}\leq u_{p,h}\) and \(0\leq L_{t,k}\leq U_{t,k}\leq1\). Platforms have no additional capacity constraints; unmatched agents are allowed.
An assignment is a measurable map \(f\) with \(f(a)\in N_t\cup\{\bot\}\) for \(a\in A_t\), where \(\bot\) denotes being unmatched. Define \(X_{p,h}(f)=\sum_{t:g(t)=h}\lambda(\{a\in A_t:f(a)=p\})\).
A feasible lottery \(D\) has finite support on assignments, every supported assignment satisfies \(\ell_{p,h}\leq X_{p,h}(f)\leq u_{p,h}\), and every individual \(a\in A_t\) satisfies \(L_{t,k}\leq\Pr_{f\sim D}[f(a)\in R_{t,k}]\leq U_{t,k}\). Type symmetry means that \(\Pr_{f\sim D}[f(a)=p]\) is identical for all individuals of type \(t\), for each \(p\in P\cup\{\bot\}\).
The objective is to maximize \(\mathbb E_{f\sim D}[\sum_{t\in T}\lambda(\{a\in A_t:f(a)\neq\bot\})]\), returning an optimum or reporting infeasibility. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is this continuous optimization problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,\chi,L\).

For discrete items belonging to exactly one group, an exact polynomial-time algorithm computes an optimal lottery satisfying individual probability bounds and ex-post lower and upper group quotas, or reports infeasibility ([discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.09951v4)). No result settling exactly the atomless finite-support problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Mass normalization was unspecified; masses are unnormalized positive rationals, preserving the interpretation of total population size.
  • The atomless population was unspecified; each type is a disjoint interval with Lebesgue measure.
  • Rankings and the range of top-k constraints were unspecified; rankings are strict orders of all platforms, and constraints apply for every k from 1 through m, with eligibility enforced separately.
  • The domains of fairness bounds were unspecified; group quotas are nonnegative ordered rationals, and individual probability bounds are ordered rationals in [0,1].
  • Type symmetry was undefined; it means identical assignment marginals for all individuals of a type, with individual constraints holding pointwise.
  • The disjoint group map is required to cover every type exactly once; group labels denote nonempty groups.
  • Additional platform capacities were absent from the continuous question; they are unrestricted, although the full version also accommodates such capacities.

Result and output convention

Feasibility and the optimal value are computable in polynomial time. An optimal finite lottery also has a polynomial-size exact description. However, explicitly listing every supported assignment can require exponentially many entries.

I interpret “returning an optimum” as permitting a compact description of the lottery: a binary-encoded integer specifying its support size, together with a formula for its assignments and probabilities. Below I prove this result and also settle the interpretation requiring explicit support enumeration.

Let \(e=\sum_{t\in T}|N_t|\), \(n=m\tau+m\chi+2\), and \(d=m\tau+e+m\chi\). A sufficient bound for computing the optimum and its compact lottery description is

\(O(nd^2L+L^3)=O\!\left(m^3(\tau+\chi)^3L+L^3\right)\)

bit operations. In particular, the associated feasibility and objective-threshold decision problems are in \(\mathrm{P}\).

1. Necessary aggregate constraints

For an assignment \(f\), define

\(x_{t,p}(f)=\lambda(\{a\in A_t:f(a)=p\})\).

For any feasible finite lottery \(D\), put \(x_{t,p}=\mathbb E_D[x_{t,p}(f)]\). These expected masses satisfy the following linear constraints:

  • Eligibility and nonnegativity:

\(x_{t,p}\geq 0\), with \(x_{t,p}=0\) whenever \(p\notin N_t\).

  • Available type mass:

\(\sum_{p\in P}x_{t,p}\leq\mu_t\).

  • Ranking-prefix bounds:

\(\mu_tL_{t,k}\leq\sum_{p\in R_{t,k}}x_{t,p}\leq\mu_tU_{t,k}\).

  • Group quotas:

\(\ell_{p,h}\leq\sum_{t:g(t)=h}x_{t,p}\leq u_{p,h}\).

To justify the prefix constraints, write the finite lottery as \(D=\{(w_j,f_j)\}_{j=1}^K\). Finite summation commutes with integration, so

\(\displaystyle \sum_{p\in R_{t,k}}x_{t,p} =\int_{A_t}\sum_{j=1}^K w_j\mathbf 1_{\{f_j(a)\in R_{t,k}\}}\,d\lambda(a). \)

The integrand lies between \(L_{t,k}\) and \(U_{t,k}\) for every individual. Integrating gives the claimed inequalities. The group inequalities follow by averaging the ex-post quotas.

The objective becomes

\(\displaystyle F(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{p\in P}x_{t,p}\).

Thus maximizing \(F(x)\) subject to these aggregate constraints gives an upper bound on the lottery optimum. Section 3 proves that a rational aggregate optimum is exactly realizable by a finite, type-symmetric lottery.

2. The aggregate optimization is a maximum-flow problem

Write the ranking of type \(t\) as \(p_{t,1},\ldots,p_{t,m}\). Construct a directed network with source \(s\), sink \(z\), type nodes \(v_{t,k}\), and group-platform nodes \(w_{p,h}\).

An edge bound \([a,b]\) means that its flow must lie between \(a\) and \(b\). Add these edges:

| Edge | Lower and upper bounds |
|---|---|
| \(s\longrightarrow v_{t,m}\) | \([\mu_tL_{t,m},\,\mu_tU_{t,m}]\) |
| \(v_{t,k+1}\longrightarrow v_{t,k}\), for \(k<m\) | \([\mu_tL_{t,k},\,\mu_tU_{t,k}]\) |
| \(v_{t,k}\longrightarrow w_{p_{t,k},g(t)}\), when \(p_{t,k}\in N_t\) | \([0,\mu_t]\) |
| \(w_{p,h}\longrightarrow z\) | \([\ell_{p,h},u_{p,h}]\) |

There are exactly \(n\) vertices and \(d\) edges.

Equivalence with the aggregate constraints

Given an aggregate feasible vector \(x\), send \(x_{t,p_{t,k}}\) along the corresponding eligibility edge. Send

\(\displaystyle C_{t,k}=\sum_{i=1}^k x_{t,p_{t,i}}\)

along the edge entering \(v_{t,k}\). Send \(\sum_{t:g(t)=h}x_{t,p}\) along \(w_{p,h}\longrightarrow z\).

Conservation at each type node follows from

\(C_{t,k}=C_{t,k-1}+x_{t,p_{t,k}}\),

where \(C_{t,0}=0\). Conservation at each group-platform node follows from its definition. All edge bounds follow from the aggregate constraints.

Conversely, given a feasible flow, define \(x_{t,p_{t,k}}\) as the flow along its eligibility edge, or as zero if that edge is absent. Conservation, starting at \(v_{t,1}\) and proceeding up the chain, shows that the flow entering \(v_{t,k}\) equals \(C_{t,k}\). Therefore the chain bounds enforce exactly the ranking-prefix constraints, and the group-platform edges enforce exactly the group quotas.

The total-mass constraint follows from the final prefix bound:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{p\in P}x_{t,p}\leq\mu_tU_{t,m}\leq\mu_t\).

Finally, the source-to-sink flow value equals \(F(x)\). Hence the aggregate optimization is exactly maximum flow with lower and upper edge bounds.

Exact feasibility and optimization

For completeness, lower bounds can be handled as follows. Add a return edge \(z\longrightarrow s\) with bounds \([0,M]\), where \(M=\sum_t\mu_t\). A feasible source-to-sink flow exists precisely when this augmented network has a feasible circulation.

For each edge \(a\), initially assign its lower bound \(l_a\), and give its remaining flow capacity \(u_a-l_a\). At each vertex define

\(\displaystyle b(v)=\sum_{a\text{ entering }v}l_a-\sum_{a\text{ leaving }v}l_a.\)

Introduce a super-source \(S\) and super-sink \(Z\):

  • If \(b(v)>0\), add \(S\longrightarrow v\) with capacity \(b(v)\).
  • If \(b(v)<0\), add \(v\longrightarrow Z\) with capacity \(-b(v)\).

A maximum flow saturating every edge leaving \(S\) gives precisely the additional flows needed to restore conservation. Conversely, any feasible circulation supplies such a saturating flow. Failure to saturate therefore certifies infeasibility.

After obtaining a feasible circulation, remove the return edge and the auxiliary vertices. Optimize the resulting feasible source-to-sink flow in its residual network. For an original edge carrying \(f_a\), the forward and backward residual capacities are \(u_a-f_a\) and \(f_a-l_a\), respectively. Thus every augmentation preserves both bounds. When no augmenting path remains, the residual cut certifies optimality.

These are two ordinary maximum-flow computations.

3. Exact finite realization of rational aggregate masses

Lemma. Every rational aggregate feasible vector \(x\) is realized by a finite, type-symmetric lottery in which every supported assignment has type-platform masses exactly \(x_{t,p}\).

Proof. Introduce unmatched as an additional outcome and define

\(\displaystyle q_{t,p}=\frac{x_{t,p}}{\mu_t}, \qquad q_{t,\bot}=1-\sum_{p\in P}q_{t,p}. \)

These numbers are nonnegative rationals summing to one for each type. Choose a positive integer \(Q\) such that

\(b_{t,c}=Qq_{t,c}\)

is an integer for every type \(t\) and outcome \(c\in P\cup\{\bot\}\).

Fix an ordering of these outcomes. For each type, partition the residue set \(\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}\) into consecutive blocks \(B_{t,c}\) of sizes \(b_{t,c}\); zero-size blocks are empty.

For each \(j\in\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}\), define an assignment \(f_j\) by

\(\displaystyle f_j(a)=c \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \left(\left\lfloor\frac{Qa}{\mu_t}\right\rfloor+j\right)\bmod Q\in B_{t,c}, \qquad a\in A_t. \)

Each \(f_j\) is measurable: its outcome sets are finite unions of half-open intervals. If \(p\notin N_t\), then \(x_{t,p}=0\), so \(B_{t,p}\) is empty and no individual is assigned to an ineligible platform.

Give each index \(j\) probability \(1/Q\).

For any fixed individual \(a\in A_t\), adding \(j\) runs through all residues exactly once. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \Pr[f_j(a)=c]=\frac{|B_{t,c}|}{Q}=q_{t,c}.\)

This holds for every individual, including interval boundaries, because the intervals are half-open and \(a<\mu_t\). It proves type symmetry and all individual prefix bounds.

For any fixed index \(j\), the \(Q\) cells

\(\displaystyle \left[\frac{i\mu_t}{Q},\frac{(i+1)\mu_t}{Q}\right), \qquad i\in\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}, \)

have equal measure. Exactly \(b_{t,p}\) cells receive platform \(p\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \lambda(\{a\in A_t:f_j(a)=p\}) =\frac{\mu_tb_{t,p}}{Q} =x_{t,p}. \)

Every supported assignment consequently satisfies every group quota, and its matched mass is \(F(x)\). This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

Different indices may specify the same assignment; combining their probabilities still gives a finite lottery.

4. Running time and size of the returned lottery

Use the usual binary encoding of rational numerators and denominators. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all rational input entries.

Every network bound becomes an integer after multiplication by \(D\). In particular, the denominator of \(\mu_tL_{t,k}\) divides the product of the input denominators of \(\mu_t\) and \(L_{t,k}\), which divides \(D\). The same argument applies to upper prefix bounds and all other capacities.

The scaled capacities and auxiliary balances have \(O(L)\) bits. With integer capacities, maximum-flow algorithms maintain integer flows. Hence the resulting exact optimum has

\(x_{t,p}=k_{t,p}/D\)

for nonnegative integers \(k_{t,p}\).

The [Edmonds–Karp algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1145/321694.321699) takes \(O(nd^2)\) arithmetic operations on a network of this size; its bound is independent of capacity magnitudes. Here each addition, subtraction, or comparison operates on \(O(L)\)-bit integers. The two maximum-flow computations therefore take \(O(nd^2L)\) bit operations. Constructing the scaled instance using ordinary integer arithmetic takes at most \(O(L^3)\) additional bit operations.

To bound the lottery description, write each mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/c_t\), with positive integers \(a_t,c_t\), and choose

\(\displaystyle Q=D\prod_{t\in T}a_t.\)

Indeed,

\(\displaystyle Qq_{t,p} = \left(D\prod_{r\in T}a_r\right) \frac{k_{t,p}c_t}{Da_t} = k_{t,p}c_t\prod_{r\neq t}a_r \)

is an integer. The unmatched block size is also an integer because it is \(Q\) minus the platform block sizes.

Both \(\log D\) and \(\sum_t\log a_t\) are \(O(L)\), so \(\log Q=O(L)\). The returned description consists of \(Q\), the block sizes, and the assignment formula from Section 3. Its size is \(O(\tau(m+1)L)\) bits, and constructing it fits within the stated polynomial bound.

Thus:

  • If the flow instance is infeasible, no feasible lottery exists.
  • Otherwise, its rational optimum yields an exact finite lottery attaining the aggregate upper bound.

This proves equality of the aggregate and lottery optima, including attainment.

5. Explicit support enumeration can require exponential time

This limitation occurs even with \(m=\tau=\chi=1\).

Take one type of mass \(1\), eligible for the sole platform \(p\), and let \(\varepsilon=2^{-b}\). Set

\(\ell_{p,1}=u_{p,1}=\varepsilon\),

and

\(L_{t,1}=U_{t,1}=\varepsilon\).

Consider any feasible lottery with \(K\) supported assignments. Let \(S_j\) be the individuals assigned to \(p\) in its \(j\)-th assignment. The ex-post quota requires \(\lambda(S_j)=\varepsilon\).

Every individual has positive matching probability, so every individual belongs to at least one \(S_j\). Hence

\(\displaystyle 1=\lambda\!\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^K S_j\right) \leq\sum_{j=1}^K\lambda(S_j) =K\varepsilon. \)

Therefore \(K\geq 2^b\). This bound is attained: partition the unit interval into \(2^b\) equal half-open intervals, and choose uniformly which single interval is assigned to \(p\).

The input length is \(L=\Theta(b)\), so explicit support output requires \(2^{\Omega(L)}\) entries in this family.

For the general construction, \(Q\leq 2^{O(L)}\). Each assignment can be described using \(O(\tau(m+1))\) intervals: a cyclic shift of a consecutive residue block produces at most two intervals. Consequently, full enumeration is possible in \(2^{O(L)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,\chi,L)\) time.

This is an output-size obstruction. The feasibility and value problems remain polynomial-time solvable; they are not \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
