Partial result: a stronger profile-dependent construction

The general optimum remains unresolved. I prove a composition rule that improves the profile-dependent upper bound in the notes and gives an infinite family with exactly determined query complexity:

$$ u_0=\frac12,\qquad u_{j+1}=u_j(1-u_j) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q((u_j,1-u_j))=j+1, \qquad D((u_j,1-u_j))=2^{2^j}. $$

The same construction produces balanced two-type profiles with complexity $\Theta(\log\log D)$. In contrast, profiles $(1/D,1-1/D)$ require $\Theta(\log D)$ queries. Thus the denominator alone does not determine the complexity, even with two positive types.

All constructions and reductions below apply to every fixed tidy kitchen in the question.

1. Positive support and merging

Write

$$ P=\{t\in T:E_t>0\},\qquad k=|P|. $$

Since $\sum_t E_t=1$, we have $1\leq k\leq\tau$.

Proposition 1: zero entitlements can be deleted

Deleting zero-entitlement types preserves the optimal query count.

To prove one direction, run a protocol for $P$, ignore appraisal replies from the other types, and give those other types empty allocations.

For the converse, choose $r\in P$. Simulate a protocol for the full support by assigning every zero-entitlement type the same measure as $r$. A query addressed to such a type is simulated by addressing $r$. The returned cut is identical, and all required appraisal replies are obtained by copying $r$’s values.

At termination, transfer every zero-entitlement allocation to $r$. This preserves disjointness and coverage, and no positive-entitlement type loses value. Every reduced profile consistent with the simulated transcript extends to a consistent full profile by the stated duplication, so the guarantee holds for all consistent reduced profiles.

Consequently,

$$ k=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q(E)=0. $$

Proposition 2: merging gives lower bounds

Let $\mathcal P$ be a partition of $P$ into nonempty blocks, and define

$$ e_B=\sum_{t\in B}E_t. $$

Then

$$ Q((e_B)_{B\in\mathcal P})\leq Q(E). $$

Indeed, for any block-measure profile $(\mu_B)$, assign measure $\mu_B$ to every original type in block $B$. Simulate each original query by querying its block and copying the block appraisal replies. Finally, give each block the union of its original types’ allocations. Finite additivity and disjointness give

$$ \mu_B\!\left(\bigcup_{t\in B}X_t\right) =\sum_{t\in B}\mu_B(X_t) \geq\sum_{t\in B}E_t=e_B. $$

The simulation preserves complete transcripts, so it also establishes the required guarantee for every profile consistent with the replies.

These two propositions justify treating each positive type as one agent. The masses and intensities enter the allocation requirement through $E_t=\lambda_ta_t$.

2. Exact cuts and local guarantees

For a kitchen measure $\nu$, serving $S$, and knife $\kappa$, define

$$ F(x)=\nu(S\cap\kappa_x). $$

This function is nondecreasing. Knife divisibility says that it attains every value in $[0,\nu(S)]$. Therefore

$$ F(0)=0,\qquad F(1)=\nu(S), $$

and $F$ is continuous: a jump, including an endpoint discontinuity, would omit some intermediate value.

It follows that the cut returned by a query satisfies

$$ \nu(S\cap\kappa_b)=p\nu(S). $$

This remains true when $\nu(S)=0$.

For the constructions below, a *local two-type protocol with share* $e$ partitions an arbitrary serving $S$ into $A,B$ and guarantees

$$ \nu_1(A)\geq e\nu_1(S),\qquad \nu_2(B)\geq(1-e)\nu_2(S). $$

No normalization by $\nu_i(S)$ is required. In particular, this definition and the following proofs include null servings.

3. A composition lemma

Proposition 3: parallel composition of shares

Suppose local two-type protocols for shares $r,s\in(0,1]$ use at most $q_r,q_s$ queries. Then there is a local protocol for share

$$ e=\frac{rs}{r+s} $$

using at most

$$ 1+\max\{q_r,q_s\} $$

queries.

Proof. Define

$$ \alpha=\frac{s}{r+s},\qquad \beta=\frac{r}{r+s}. $$

Thus $\alpha+\beta=1$, and

$$ \frac e\alpha=r,\qquad \frac e\beta=s. $$

Ask type $1$ to cut $S$ at fraction $\alpha$. Let the returned piece be $L$ and put $R=S\setminus L$. Their values to type $1$ are exactly $\alpha\nu_1(S)$ and $\beta\nu_1(S)$.

Type $2$ values at least one of these pieces at least its corresponding fraction of $\nu_2(S)$. Otherwise,

$$ \nu_2(L)<\alpha\nu_2(S),\qquad \nu_2(R)<\beta\nu_2(S) $$

would contradict finite additivity.

Give such a piece $Y$ to type $2$, breaking ties deterministically. Both relevant values are available from the single query. Write $Z=S\setminus Y$ and let $w\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$ be its fraction according to type $1$. Then

$$ \nu_1(Z)=w\nu_1(S),\qquad \nu_2(Y)\geq(1-w)\nu_2(S). $$

On $Z$, use the local protocol with type $1$’s share $e/w$, which equals either $r$ or $s$. Let its allocations be $A$ to type $1$ and $B$ to type $2$. Type $1$ receives

$$ \nu_1(A)\geq\frac ew\nu_1(Z)=e\nu_1(S). $$

Type $2$ receives $Y\cup B$. Its value is at least

$$ \begin{aligned} \nu_2(Y)+\left(1-\frac ew\right)\nu_2(Z) &=\left(1-\frac ew\right)\nu_2(S)+\frac ew\nu_2(Y)\\ &\geq\left(1-\frac ew+\frac ew(1-w)\right)\nu_2(S)\\ &=(1-e)\nu_2(S). \end{aligned} $$

All coefficients used here are nonnegative because $e/w\in(0,1]$. The calculation also applies when either measure assigns zero value to $S$. Disjointness and coverage follow from the two partitions. ∎

Interchanging the two types gives the same bound for share $1-e$.

4. A computable upper bound for every rational share

Define finite sets of rational shares recursively:

$$ \mathcal A_0=\{0,1\}, $$

and

$$ \mathcal A_{q+1} = \mathcal A_q \cup \left\{ \frac{rs}{r+s}, \,1-\frac{rs}{r+s} : r,s\in\mathcal A_q\setminus\{0\} \right\}. $$

By induction using Proposition 3, every $e\in\mathcal A_q$ admits a local protocol using at most $q$ queries. The base cases assign all of $S$ to one type.

For rational $e\in[0,1]$, define

$$ J(e)=\min\{q:e\in\mathcal A_q\}. $$

The next proposition proves that this minimum always exists.

Proposition 4: denominator bound for the construction

If $e$ has reduced denominator $M$, then

$$ J(e)\leq\lceil\log_2M\rceil. $$

Proof. Use strong induction on $M$. For $M=1$, the share is $0$ or $1$ and belongs to $\mathcal A_0$.

The sets $\mathcal A_q$ are closed under complementation, so for $M\geq2$ we may write

$$ e=\frac aM,\qquad 1\leq a\leq\left\lfloor\frac M2\right\rfloor. $$

Put

$$ h=\left\lfloor\frac M2\right\rfloor,\qquad r=\frac ah,\qquad s=\frac a{M-h}. $$

Both $r,s$ lie in $(0,1]$, their reduced denominators are at most $\lceil M/2\rceil$, and

$$ \frac{rs}{r+s}=\frac aM=e. $$

If $q=\lceil\log_2M\rceil$, then

$$ \left\lceil\frac M2\right\rceil\leq2^{q-1}. $$

The induction hypothesis therefore puts both $r,s$ in $\mathcal A_{q-1}$. The defining composition puts $e$ in $\mathcal A_q$. ∎

Thus

$$ Q((e,1-e))\leq J(e)\leq\lceil\log_2\operatorname{den}(e)\rceil. $$

The quantity $J$ is a construction bound; I do not claim that it equals the unrestricted optimum. It is computable by finite enumeration through the level guaranteed by Proposition 4. No polynomial running-time claim is made.

The first levels are

$$ \mathcal A_1=\left\{0,\frac12,1\right\}, $$

$$ \mathcal A_2= \left\{0,\frac14,\frac13,\frac12,\frac23,\frac34,1\right\}. $$

Two useful compositions are

$$ \frac{(1/2)(3/4)}{1/2+3/4}=\frac3{10}, \qquad \frac{(2/3)(3/4)}{2/3+3/4}=\frac6{17}. $$

They give three-query protocols for both shares.

5. The resulting upper bound for a full entitlement profile

For a permutation $\pi$ of $P$, put

$$ s_j=\sum_{i=1}^jE_{\pi(i)}. $$

Define

$$ U_J(E)= \min_\pi \sum_{j=2}^k (j-1)J\!\left(\frac{E_{\pi(j)}}{s_j}\right). $$

Proposition 5

For $k\geq2$,

$$ Q(E)\leq U_J(E). $$

Proof. Start by assigning $C$ to $\pi(1)$. Inductively, suppose the first $j-1$ types have a partition $(Y_i)_{i<j}$ satisfying

$$ \nu_{\pi(i)}(Y_i)\geq\frac{E_{\pi(i)}}{s_{j-1}}. $$

For each $i<j$, divide $Y_i$ between its owner and the new type $\pi(j)$ using the local protocol whose share for the new type is

$$ \beta_j=\frac{E_{\pi(j)}}{s_j}. $$

The owner retains a fraction at least

$$ \alpha_j=1-\beta_j=\frac{s_{j-1}}{s_j} $$

of its value of $Y_i$. Its new value is therefore at least

$$ \alpha_j\nu_{\pi(i)}(Y_i) \geq \frac{s_{j-1}}{s_j}\frac{E_{\pi(i)}}{s_{j-1}} = \frac{E_{\pi(i)}}{s_j}. $$

The new type receives the union of its pieces. Since the $Y_i$ partition $C$, its value is at least

$$ \sum_{i<j}\beta_j\nu_{\pi(j)}(Y_i)=\beta_j. $$

Thus the induction invariant holds for the first $j$ types. At $j=k$, we have $s_k=1$, giving the required entitlements.

Stage $j$ uses at most $(j-1)J(\beta_j)$ queries. Minimizing over the ordering proves the result. ∎

By Proposition 4, this improves or equals the ordering bound in the notes:

$$ U_J(E)\leq \min_\pi \sum_{j=2}^k (j-1) \left\lceil \log_2\operatorname{den}\!\left(\frac{E_{\pi(j)}}{s_j}\right) \right\rceil. $$

6. Unavoidable bounds

Assume $k\geq2$. Define

$$ D=D(E),\qquad H(E)= \max_{\emptyset\neq A\subsetneq P} \operatorname{den}\!\left(\sum_{t\in A}E_t\right), $$

$$ \varepsilon=\min_{t\in P}E_t,\qquad f=\left\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}\right\rceil-1, $$

and, for integers $r,d\geq2$, define

$$ b_r(d)= 1+\left\lfloor \log_2\log_2\!\left(2(d-1)^{1/(r-1)}\right) \right\rfloor. $$

These quantities are defined: $D,H(E)\geq2$, and $\varepsilon\leq1/2$ implies $f\geq1$.

6.1 Denominator and small-entitlement bounds

I use the following published results in their kitchen-query formulation:

  • For $n\geq2$ and integer $c\geq1$, an entitlement denominator exceeding $c$ forces a query count strictly exceeding $\lfloor\log_2\log_2(2c^{1/(n-1)})\rfloor$.
  • For two positive entitlements $(u,1-u)$, if integer $f\geq1$ satisfies $u<1/f$, then the query count strictly exceeds $\log_3f$.

These are respectively Theorem 1 and the two-type case of Theorem CF2 in [Brandl–Mackenzie](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09004v1#S4.SS1). Their setting fixes an arbitrary tidy kitchen, allows unrestricted kitchen-measure profiles, and uses the same appraisal-inclusive queries.

Apply the first theorem to the positive support with $c=D-1$. Integer rounding gives

$$ Q(E)\geq b_k(D). $$

For each nonempty proper $A\subset P$, merge $A$ and its complement. The two resulting entitlements have common denominator $\operatorname{den}(\sum_{t\in A}E_t)$. Proposition 2 and the same theorem give

$$ Q(E)\geq b_2(H(E)). $$

Finally, merge a minimum-entitlement type against the others. Since

$$ f<\varepsilon^{-1}, $$

we have $\varepsilon<1/f$, so

$$ Q(E)\geq1+\lfloor\log_3f\rfloor. $$

6.2 A support lower bound allowing arbitrary servings

The following bound does not restrict query servings or output servings:

$$ Q(E)\geq\left\lceil\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rceil. $$

Here is a complete proof.

First, positive finite reweightings preserve kitchen measures. Fix a kitchen measure $\mu$ and a finite serving partition $(A_\ell)$. For positive weights $w_\ell$ satisfying $\sum_\ell w_\ell\mu(A_\ell)=1$, define

$$ \mu_w(B)=\sum_\ell w_\ell\mu(B\cap A_\ell). $$

This is a finitely additive probability measure, and

$$ (\min_\ell w_\ell)\mu(B) \leq\mu_w(B) \leq(\max_\ell w_\ell)\mu(B). $$

Hence its null servings are exactly the slivers. Each knife-value function for $\mu_w$ is a finite sum of the continuous knife-value functions established in Section 2. Its endpoints are $0$ and $\mu_w(S)$, so the intermediate value theorem supplies knife divisibility.

Moreover, suppose a query under $\mu$ returns $R=S\cap\kappa_b$, and $\mu_w$ agrees with $\mu$ on $S,R$. Its cut reply is unchanged. If the target is zero, both infima are $0$. Otherwise, for every $x<b$,

$$ \mu(S\cap\kappa_x)<\mu(R). $$

Thus $R\setminus(S\cap\kappa_x)$ has positive $\mu$-measure and therefore positive $\mu_w$-measure. Since $\mu_w(R)$ is the unchanged target, every earlier position remains below it, while $b$ attains it.

Now run any valid protocol on a profile where all types have the same fixed kitchen measure $\mu$. Suppose it uses $q$ queries, with queried servings $S_j$, returned servings $R_j$, and final allocations $X_t$. Disjointness, coverage, and validity imply

$$ \mu(X_t)=E_t $$

for every type: all inequalities $\mu(X_t)\geq E_t$ must be equalities because both sums are $1$.

Consider the finite algebra generated by all $S_j,R_j,X_t$. On its positive-$\mu$ atoms, regard serving indicators as vectors with inner product

$$ \langle u,v\rangle=\int uv\,d\mu, $$

where the integral is a finite weighted sum. Set

$$ V= \operatorname{span} \{\mathbf1_C,\mathbf1_{S_1},\mathbf1_{R_1},\ldots, \mathbf1_{S_q},\mathbf1_{R_q}\}. $$

If $\mathbf1_{X_t}\notin V$ for some $t\in P$, let

$$ g=\mathbf1_{X_t}-\operatorname{proj}_V\mathbf1_{X_t}. $$

Then $g\neq0$ and is orthogonal to every listed indicator. Choose $\eta>0$ sufficiently small that $1-\eta g$ is positive on every positive-measure atom, and set $g=0$ on null atoms. Replace only type $t$’s measure by

$$ \mu'_t(B)=\int(1-\eta g)\mathbf1_B\,d\mu. $$

Orthogonality to $\mathbf1_C$ ensures normalization. The reweighting argument proves that $\mu'_t$ is a kitchen measure. Orthogonality to the query and reply indicators preserves all its appraisal values, and the cut-preservation argument preserves every cut it makes.

Consequently, determinism produces the same complete transcript and allocation. But

$$ \begin{aligned} \mu'_t(X_t) &=E_t-\eta\langle g,\mathbf1_{X_t}\rangle\\ &=E_t-\eta\|g\|^2\\ &<E_t, \end{aligned} $$

a contradiction. Therefore $\mathbf1_{X_t}\in V$ for every $t\in P$.

These $k$ indicators are nonzero and mutually orthogonal, hence linearly independent. Thus

$$ k\leq\dim V\leq2q+1, $$

which proves the bound.

7. An infinite family with exact query complexity

Define

$$ u_0=\frac12,\qquad u_{j+1}=u_j(1-u_j) \qquad(j\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}). $$

Every $u_j$ lies in $(0,1/2]$.

Proposition 6

For every $j\geq0$,

$$ D((u_j,1-u_j))=2^{2^j}, \qquad Q((u_j,1-u_j))=j+1. $$

Proof of the denominator formula. Write $u_j=a_j/d_j$ in lowest terms. Initially $a_0=1$ and $d_0=2$. If $a_j$ is odd and $d_j$ is a power of two, then

$$ u_{j+1}=\frac{a_j(d_j-a_j)}{d_j^2}. $$

The numerator is odd, so it is coprime to the power-of-two denominator. Hence

$$ a_{j+1}=a_j(d_j-a_j),\qquad d_{j+1}=d_j^2. $$

Induction gives $d_j=2^{2^j}$. The complementary entitlement has the same reduced denominator.

Proof of the upper bound. We have $u_0\in\mathcal A_1$. If $u_j\in\mathcal A_{j+1}$, then its complement also belongs to that set, and

$$ \frac{u_j(1-u_j)}{u_j+(1-u_j)}=u_{j+1}. $$

Therefore $u_{j+1}\in\mathcal A_{j+2}$. This proves

$$ Q((u_j,1-u_j))\leq j+1. $$

Proof of the lower bound. For $j=0$, the denominator bound gives one query. For $j\geq1$, put $d=2^{2^j}$. Since $d>2$,

$$ 2^{2^j}<2(d-1)<2^{2^j+1}. $$

Consequently,

$$ j< \log_2\log_2(2(d-1)) < j+1, $$

where the upper inequality follows from $2^j+1<2^{j+1}$. Thus $b_2(d)=j+1$, completing the proof. ∎

The first shares are

$$ \frac12,\qquad \frac14,\qquad \frac3{16},\qquad \frac{39}{256},\qquad \frac{8463}{65536}. $$

Their exact query counts are respectively $1,2,3,4,5$.

The denominator bound also settles the two compositions exhibited earlier:

$$ Q\!\left(\left(\frac3{10},\frac7{10}\right)\right)=3, \qquad Q\!\left(\left(\frac6{17},\frac{11}{17}\right)\right)=3. $$

Indeed, the constructions use three queries, and $b_2(d)\geq3$ for both denominators.

8. Balanced profiles attaining the logarithmic-denominator lower-bound order

Define

$$ v_j=\frac{1-u_j}{2-u_j}. $$

Since $0<u_j\leq1/2$,

$$ \frac13\leq v_j<\frac12. $$

Thus both entitlements of $(v_j,1-v_j)$ stay uniformly away from zero.

Compose shares $1-u_j$ and $1$ using Proposition 3. The first has a local protocol using $j+1$ queries, and the second needs none. Therefore

$$ Q((v_j,1-v_j))\leq j+2. $$

Write $u_j=a_j/d_j$ as above. Then

$$ v_j=\frac{d_j-a_j}{2d_j-a_j}. $$

This fraction is reduced because

$$ \gcd(d_j-a_j,2d_j-a_j) =\gcd(d_j-a_j,d_j) =\gcd(a_j,d_j)=1. $$

Its denominator is therefore

$$ d'_j=2d_j-a_j, \qquad \frac32d_j\leq d'_j<2d_j. $$

By monotonicity of $b_2$ and Proposition 6,

$$ j+1\leq Q((v_j,1-v_j))\leq j+2. $$

Also,

$$ \log_2\log_2d'_j=j+O(1). $$

Hence this balanced family satisfies

$$ Q((v_j,1-v_j)) =\Theta(\log\log d'_j). $$

For comparison, at the same denominators $d'_j$, the profiles

$$ \left(\frac1{d'_j},1-\frac1{d'_j}\right) $$

satisfy

$$ \log_3(d'_j-1) < Q\!\left(\left(\frac1{d'_j},1-\frac1{d'_j}\right)\right) \leq\lceil\log_2d'_j\rceil. $$

The lower bound uses $f=d'_j-1$; the upper bound is Proposition 4. Their complexity is therefore $\Theta(\log d'_j)$.

This proves that profiles with identical support size and identical denominator can have substantially different optimal query complexity.

9. Combined bounds and what remains open

For $k\geq2$, the established bounds are

$$ \boxed{ \max\left\{ \left\lceil\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rceil,\, b_k(D(E)),\, b_2(H(E)),\, 1+\lfloor\log_3f\rfloor \right\} \leq Q(E) \leq \min\left\{ 2(k-1)\lceil\log_2D(E)\rceil,\, U_J(E) \right\}. } $$

The first upper bound is the supplied rational-entitlement bound, applied after deleting zero types; see [Cseh–Fleiner](https://hpi.de/friedrich/docs/publications/2020/TALG.pdf). The second is proved here.

The least common denominator divides the product of the input denominators of the masses and intensities. Consequently,

$$ \log_2D(E)=O(L), $$

and the uniform upper bound gives $O(\tau L)$ queries. This is solely a query-count statement; no separate running-time bound is asserted.

The new construction settles an infinite family exactly and shows that the $\log\log D$ lower-bound order is achievable even for balanced two-type profiles. It does not establish that every balanced profile admits that order, nor that $J$ describes all optimal protocols.
