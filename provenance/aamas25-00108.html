The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite network \(G\), polynomial edge latencies \(c_e\), total fleet mass \(M\), common fleet size \(w\), type masses \(\mu_t\), route menus \(\mathcal P_t\), and unit-route masses \(y_{t,p}\) satisfying \(\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_t}y_{t,p}=w\mu_t\), with seed envy-ratio at most \(\beta\), find a succinct bundle-mass distribution \(\lambda_{t,B}\) over \(B\in\mathcal P_t^w\) such that \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}\#_p(B)=y_{t,p}\), and the resulting fleet envy-ratio is at most \(1+(\beta-1)/w\). Here \(L_e=M\sum_t\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_t:e\in p}y_{t,p}\), bundle cost is \(C(B)=\sum_{p\in B}\sum_{e\in p}c_e(L_e)\), and social cost is \(\sum_eL_ec_e(L_e)\).

The search problem is to find such a distribution succinctly: list its positive-mass bundles using binary-encoded path multiplicities and rational masses, with total encoding length polynomial in the input length. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Conclusion

With the prescribed type-by-type marginals, the search problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even if feasibility is additionally promised. This holds with two types, identical menus, constant latencies, and \(\beta=2\).

There is also a defect in the stated promise: it does not guarantee that a required distribution exists. For example, take \(w=2\), \(\beta=2\), two types of mass \(1/2\), and a common menu containing routes of constant costs \(1\) and \(2\). Prescribe that the first type uses only the first route and the second only the second route. Their forced bundle costs are \(2\) and \(4\), whereas the required ratio is \(3/2\).

The following reduction addresses the substantive question after adding a feasibility promise.

1. Source problem and numerical encoding

We reduce from \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\): given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), decide whether some subset has sum half the total. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

We may assume \(n\ge2\) and that the total is even: multiplying every input integer by \(2\) preserves the answer and ensures an even total.

Let \(w\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(w\ge n\). Pad the sequence with zeros to obtain \(a_1,\ldots,a_w\), and define

\[ \]
<!-- All mathematical expressions below use the requested inline delimiters. -->

\(S=\sum_{i=1}^{w}a_i,\qquad H=wS+1,\qquad R=w+1.\)

Define positional weights and a positive baseline by

\(\Delta_i=H R^{i-1},\qquad U=\Delta_w+\max_i a_i,\qquad K=4U.\)

For each index introduce two routes with costs

\(\ell_{p_i^0}=K+\Delta_i,\qquad \ell_{p_i^1}=K+\Delta_i+a_i.\)

Finally, define

\(T=wK+\sum_{i=1}^{w}\Delta_i+S/2,\qquad \theta=T/w,\qquad g=1+1/w.\)

Introduce one further route \(q\), whose cost is

\(\ell_q=g\theta.\)

The positional weights will force an exact-cost bundle to choose exactly one route from each pair.

2. Construction and verification of the seed promise

Both types have the same explicitly listed menu

\(\mathcal P=\{p_i^0,p_i^1:1\le i\le w\}\cup\{q\}.\)

Their masses are \(\mu_0=\mu_1=1/2\). Set \(M=1\) and \(\beta=2\). Prescribe the marginals as follows:

  • Type \(0\): \(y_{0,p_i^0}=y_{0,p_i^1}=1/4\) for every \(i\), and \(y_{0,q}=0\).
  • Type \(1\): \(y_{1,q}=w/2\), and every other marginal is zero.

Thus each type’s marginals sum to \(w/2=w\mu_t\).

To realize these costs in a simple undirected network, create distinct internal vertices \(v_p\), one for each route \(p\). Route \(p\) is the two-edge path \(s,v_p,t\). Assign constant latency \(\ell_p\) to \(\{s,v_p\}\) and zero latency to \(\{v_p,t\}\). All coefficients are nonnegative, and the latencies have degree \(d=0\).

We now verify the seed promise. Every paired-route cost belongs to \([K,K+U]\). Also,

\(\theta=K+\frac1w\sum_{i=1}^{w}(\Delta_i+a_i/2),\)

so \(K\le\theta\le K+U\). Since \(w\ge2\),

\(K\le\ell_q=g\theta\le\frac32(K+U)=\frac{15}{8}K<2K.\)

Consequently, every used route has cost between \(K\) and \(2K\). Any two used routes therefore satisfy \(\ell_p\le2\ell_{p'}\), establishing the seed promise with \(\beta=2\).

The required bundle envy bound is exactly \(g=1+1/w\).

3. Every valid distribution yields a partition

Consider any distribution satisfying the prescribed marginals and the envy bound.

The anchor bundle is forced. Type \(1\) has zero marginal on every route other than \(q\). Because masses and multiplicities are nonnegative, every positive-mass bundle of this type consists of \(w\) copies of \(q\). Its cost is

\(w\ell_q=wg\theta=gT.\)

Therefore, for every positive-mass type-\(0\) bundle \(B\), the envy inequality from the anchor bundle to \(B\) gives

\(gT\le gC(B),\)

and hence \(C(B)\ge T\).

The fixed mean forces equality. The type-\(0\) marginals give

\(\sum_B\lambda_{0,B}C(B)=\frac14\sum_{i=1}^{w}(\ell_{p_i^0}+\ell_{p_i^1})=\frac12T.\)

Since the total type-\(0\) bundle mass is \(1/2\), its conditional average bundle cost is \(T\). Every positive-mass bundle has cost at least \(T\); thus every such bundle must have cost exactly \(T\).

Fix one of these bundles. It cannot contain \(q\), because \(y_{0,q}=0\). Write

\(x_i^b=\#_{p_i^b}(B),\qquad r_i=x_i^0+x_i^1.\)

The bundle size and exact-cost equations are

\(\sum_{i=1}^{w}r_i=w\)

and

\(\sum_{i=1}^{w}r_i\Delta_i+\sum_{i=1}^{w}x_i^1a_i=\sum_{i=1}^{w}\Delta_i+S/2.\)

Every \(\Delta_i\) is divisible by \(H\). Reducing the latter equation modulo \(H\) gives

\(\sum_i x_i^1a_i\equiv S/2\pmod H.\)

Both quantities lie in \([0,H)\): indeed,

\(0\le\sum_i x_i^1a_i\le w\max_i a_i\le wS<H.\)

Thus the congruence is an equality:

\(\sum_i x_i^1a_i=S/2.\)

Subtracting this equality from the cost equation and dividing by \(H\) yields

\(\sum_{i=1}^{w}r_iR^{i-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{w}R^{i-1}.\)

Each \(r_i\) is an integer satisfying \(0\le r_i\le w<R\). Uniqueness of base-\(R\) representation therefore implies \(r_i=1\) for every \(i\). Explicitly, reduction modulo \(R\) gives \(r_1=1\); subtracting and dividing by \(R\), and repeating, gives all remaining equalities.

Consequently, each \(x_i^1\) belongs to \(\{0,1\}\). The indices with \(x_i^1=1\) form a partition of total weight \(S/2\). Removing padded zero entries gives a partition of the original instance.

This proves that every valid output encodes a solution to the source instance, despite bundles being allowed to repeat routes.

4. Every partition yields a succinct valid distribution

Conversely, suppose \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,w\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=S/2\).

Construct two type-\(0\) bundles:

  • \(B_I\) chooses \(p_i^1\) for \(i\in I\) and \(p_i^0\) otherwise.
  • \(B_{\overline I}\) makes the opposite choice at every index.

Give each bundle mass \(1/4\). Each paired route occurs in exactly one of these bundles, so its prescribed marginal is \(1/4\). Both bundle costs equal \(T\), because \(I\) and its complement each have weight \(S/2\).

For type \(1\), give the bundle consisting of \(w\) copies of \(q\) mass \(1/2\). Its cost is \(gT\).

The only positive bundle costs are therefore \(T\) and \(gT\), so the envy-ratio is exactly \(g\). All marginals are satisfied, and hence edge loads and social cost are preserved.

This output uses only three bundles, encoded by path multiplicities and rational masses.

5. Encoding bounds and search hardness

The construction has \(\tau=2\), menu size \(2w+1\), and \(m=2(2w+1)\). Its graph has \(2w+3\) vertices and \(2(2w+1)\) edges.

Because \(w<2n\), its numerical encoding is polynomial in the source input length. In particular,

\(\log_2\Delta_w=O(\log_2 S+w\log_2(w+1)).\)

All masses are dyadic. Since \(w\) is a power of two and \(T\) is an integer, \(\theta=T/w\) and \(\ell_q=(w+1)T/w^2\) are also dyadic. Thus the reduction works even under the interpretation that “binary-rational” requires a power-of-two denominator.

We have proved the exact equivalence:

\(\text{source instance has a partition}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{constructed instance has a valid distribution}.\)

Moreover, every valid distribution yields a partition by inspecting any positive-mass type-\(0\) bundle.

For precision, suppose an algorithm is guaranteed to produce a valid output in polynomial time whenever a constructed instance is feasible. Run it for its polynomial time bound and inspect its output for a valid partition. On a yes-instance it necessarily supplies one. On a no-instance no candidate can pass the partition check, regardless of the algorithm’s behavior outside its feasibility promise. This would decide \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) in polynomial time.

Therefore, even the search problem with an additional feasibility promise is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

6. Why discrete regrouping does not establish feasibility here

The cited regrouping algorithm reallocates routes among compatible fleets while preserving aggregate traffic and social cost. [Gollapudi et al., Theorem 4.1 and Algorithm 1](https://ifaamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2025/pdfs/p923.pdf).

The present task additionally requires preservation of each type’s individual route marginals. Those constraints fix each type’s average bundle cost. The reduction exploits precisely this requirement: the anchor imposes a lower bound, and the other type’s fixed mean forces every bundle to attain that bound exactly.

Thus aggregate load preservation and type-by-type marginal preservation lead to different problems. The hardness above concerns the exact latter formulation.
