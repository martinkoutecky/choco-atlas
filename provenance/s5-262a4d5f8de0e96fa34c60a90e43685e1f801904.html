The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given resource kinds \(R\), complete utility types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_r\), rational \(k\)-additive utility coefficients, and a rational threshold \(K\), decide whether there exist masses \(x_{t,B}\ge0\) for bundles \(B\subseteq R\) such that \(\sum_{B\subseteq R}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) for each \(t\), \(\sum_{t\in T,\,B\ni r}x_{t,B}=q_r\) for each \(r\), and \(\sum_{t\in T,\,B\subseteq R}x_{t,B}u_t(B)>K\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(R\) be a set of \(m\) resource kinds and let \(2\le k\le m\). Each individual receives a bundle \(B\subseteq R\), containing at most one unit of each kind; empty bundles are allowed.
A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of the \(\tau\) complete utility types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Type \(t\) is represented by a sparse list of signed binary-rational coefficients \(\alpha_{t,S}\) for \(S\subseteq R\) with \(|S|\le k\); omitted coefficients are zero, and \(u_t(B)=\sum_{S\subseteq B,\,|S|\le k}\alpha_{t,S}\). The empty-set coefficient is allowed, and utilities need not be nonnegative or monotone.
The remaining inputs are binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_r\in[0,1]\) and a signed binary-rational threshold \(K\). An allocation consists of real masses \(x_{t,B}\ge0\), with \(\sum_{B\subseteq R}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T,\,B\ni r}x_{t,B}=q_r\) for every \(r\); all supplies must be allocated.
The decision problem asks whether such an allocation satisfies \(\sum_{t\in T,\,B\subseteq R}x_{t,B}u_t(B)>K\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

Is this continuous decision problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard, for each fixed \(k\ge2\)?

The discrete welfare-threshold problem with signed rational utilities in this representation is NP-complete for every fixed \(k\ge2\), even with two agents, one having identically zero utility. [Chevaleyre et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-008-0335-0)
Related discrete winner determination is NP-hard already with pairwise dependent valuations. [Conitzer et al.](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/k-wise.aaai05.pdf) No result settling the exact continuous problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified sparse coefficient encoding, with omitted coefficients zero, to preserve the succinct k-additive representation.
  • Allowed signed rational coefficients, signed thresholds, nonzero empty-set utility, and nonmonotone utilities, following the discrete utility domain.
  • Restricted normalized per-capita supplies to [0,1], since each individual can receive at most one unit of each resource kind; zero supplies remain allowed.
  • Made allocation masses real and retained exact supply equalities and the strict welfare threshold.
  • Asked the complexity question for each fixed k at least 2, matching the anchor; restricted to m at least k, since larger k adds no expressive power.
  • Allowed support size one: the discrete requirement of at least two individuals does not require two distinct utility types in an atomless society.

Result

For every fixed \(k\ge 2\), the continuous decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even when \(\tau=1\), the single type has mass \(1\), every supply equals \(1/2\), and all nonzero utility coefficients have degree at most \(2\).

Thus a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,k,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Hardness: encoding MAX-CUT with exact supplies

We reduce from unweighted MAX-CUT: given a simple undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\) and an integer \(h\), decide whether some cut has at least \(h\) edges. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, as established by [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1).

Fix \(k\ge 2\). Add isolated vertices if necessary so that \(m=|V|\ge k\); this does not change any cut size.

Take \(R=V\). Introduce one utility type \(t\), with \(\mu_t=1\), and set \(q_v=1/2\) for every \(v\in V\). Its coefficients are

\( \alpha_{t,\{v\}}=\deg_G(v),\qquad \alpha_{t,\{v,w\}}=-2\ \text{when }\{v,w\}\in E, \)

with every other coefficient zero. These coefficients are permitted for every \(k\ge 2\).

For a bundle \(B\subseteq V\), let \(z_v=1\) when \(v\in B\), and \(z_v=0\) otherwise. Then

\( u_t(B)=\sum_{v\in V}\deg_G(v)z_v-2\sum_{\{v,w\}\in E}z_vz_w =\sum_{\{v,w\}\in E}(z_v+z_w-2z_vz_w). \)

For each edge, its summand is \(1\) exactly when its endpoints lie on opposite sides of \(B\), and is \(0\) otherwise. Consequently,

\( u_t(B)=|\delta_G(B)|, \)

where \(\delta_G(B)\) denotes the edges with exactly one endpoint in \(B\).

Let \(C(G)=\max_{B\subseteq V}|\delta_G(B)|\). Any feasible allocation satisfies

\( \sum_{B\subseteq V}x_{t,B}u_t(B)\le C(G)\sum_{B\subseteq V}x_{t,B}=C(G). \)

Conversely, choose a maximizing bundle \(B^\star\). Allocate mass \(1/2\) to \(B^\star\) and mass \(1/2\) to \(V\setminus B^\star\). Each resource occurs in exactly one of these bundles, so its allocated mass is exactly \(1/2\). Both bundles define the same cut. Hence this feasible allocation has welfare \(C(G)\).

We have therefore proved the exact identity

\( \operatorname{OPT}=C(G). \)

Set \(K=h-1/2\). Because \(C(G)\) is an integer,

\( \operatorname{OPT}>K\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C(G)\ge h. \)

The reduction produces at most \(m+|E|\) nonzero coefficients, with polynomial binary encoding length, and is computable in polynomial time. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for each fixed \(k\ge 2\).

2. Polynomial-size certificates despite real allocation masses

We now prove membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), including for the strict inequality.

Write the allocation constraints as \(Ax=b\), \(x\ge 0\). There are \(d=m+\tau\) rows and \(N=\tau 2^m\) columns. The column indexed by \((t,B)\) has a \(1\) in the row for type \(t\), a \(1\) in each resource row belonging to \(B\), and zeros elsewhere. Thus \(A\) is a \(0\)-\(1\) matrix. The vector \(b\) lists the masses and supplies.

The feasible set is nonempty: the allocation

\( x_{t,B}=\mu_t\prod_{r\in B}q_r\prod_{r\notin B}(1-q_r) \)

satisfies every type-mass constraint and every supply constraint. It is also compact, since its defining constraints are closed and \(0\le x_{t,B}\le\mu_t\). Therefore welfare attains a maximum.

An optimum uses at most \(m+\tau\) positive variables

Among all maximizing allocations, choose \(x^\star\) with the fewest positive variables. Let \(I\) be its support.

Suppose the columns indexed by \(I\) were linearly dependent. There would then be a nonzero vector \(v\), supported on \(I\), with \(Av=0\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both \(x^\star+\varepsilon v\) and \(x^\star-\varepsilon v\) would be feasible.

Let \(c_{t,B}=u_t(B)\). Optimality implies \(c^\mathsf{T}v=0\): otherwise one of these two allocations would improve welfare.

The type rows of \(Av=0\) imply \(\sum_i v_i=0\), so \(v\) has both positive and negative entries. Put

\( \lambda=\min_{i:v_i<0}\frac{x^\star_i}{-v_i}>0. \)

Then \(x^\star+\lambda v\) remains feasible, has the same welfare, and has strictly smaller support. This contradicts the choice of \(x^\star\).

Thus its positive columns are independent, and \(p=|I|\le d\).

The positive masses have polynomial binary length

Choose \(p\) rows on which these columns form a nonsingular square matrix \(H\). Their masses satisfy

\( Hx^\star_I=b', \)

where \(b'\) consists of the corresponding entries of \(b\).

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all entries of \(b\). Its binary length is \(O(L)\), and \(Db'\) is integral. Since every entry of \(b'\) lies in \([0,1]\), every entry of \(Db'\) lies between \(0\) and \(D\).

Cramer's rule gives

\( x^\star_{I,j}=\frac{\det H_j}{D\det H}, \)

where \(H_j\) replaces column \(j\) of \(H\) by \(Db'\). Hadamard's inequality yields

\( |\det H|\le p^{p/2},\qquad |\det H_j|\le Dp^{p/2}. \)

Hence each positive mass has a rational encoding of length

\( O\!\left(L+d\log(d+1)\right). \)

A certificate therefore consists of at most \(d=m+\tau\) type–bundle pairs and their rational masses. Its total length is

\( O\!\left(d\left[m+\log(\tau+1)+L+d\log(d+1)\right]\right). \)

The verifier checks nonnegativity, every allocation equality, and welfare strictly exceeding \(K\), using exact rational arithmetic. Each listed bundle's utility is computed directly from its type's sparse coefficient list. Verification takes \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L)\) time.

If a yes-instance has any real allocation exceeding \(K\), its maximizing allocation also exceeds \(K\); the argument above supplies a polynomial-size rational certificate. Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

3. Running times and conclusion

An exact general algorithm can explicitly construct the allocation linear program with \(\tau 2^m\) variables and \(m+\tau\) equality constraints. Evaluating its objective coefficients and applying polynomial-time rational linear programming takes

\( \operatorname{poly}(\tau 2^m,m,\tau,k,L) =2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L). \)

Certificate verification takes \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L)\) time. Combining membership with the reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for every fixed \(k\ge 2\).

The decisive feature of the reduction is complement symmetry: mixing a maximum-cut bundle with its complement enforces all prescribed marginals without reducing welfare.
