The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many seller classes \(t\), each with public value \(v_t\), and finite cost supports \(C_t\) with rational population masses \(\mu_{t,c}\), determine the maximum aggregate buyer utility achievable by a DSIC-IR mechanism when each seller receives an integral allocation and total payments are at most a per-capita budget \(b\) for every measurable report profile. What is the optimal mechanism and its computational complexity in the support size and input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \((t,c)\) with positive mass \(\mu_{t,c}\), each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\) public seller classes, with nonempty finite cost supports \(C_t\), nonnegative rational values \(v_t\) and costs, and rational per-capita budget \(b>0\); masses sum to \(1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type comprises an atomless population of mass \(\mu_{t,c}\). Every seller supplies one indivisible service, has private cost \(c\), and has a public priority \(z\in[0,1]\) uniformly distributed within every type; only class and priority are public.
A direct mechanism observes a seller's class, priority, own reported cost, and the aggregate measure of public priorities and reports. It may randomize, but each realized allocation is in \(\{0,1\}\), and payments are nonnegative. Reports range over \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}\).
Require weak DSIC and IR in expectation over mechanism randomness for every seller and every measurable report profile. Seller utility is payment minus cost times allocation; a unilateral report change changes that seller's outcome but not the aggregate report measure.
For every measurable report profile and every realization of mechanism randomness, the population integral of payments must be at most \(b\).
At truthful reports, define aggregate buyer utility by \(U(M)=\mathbb E[\sum_t\sum_{c\in C_t}\mu_{t,c}\int_0^1(v_t x_M(t,c,z)-p_M(t,c,z))\,dz]\). Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be its supremum over all mechanisms satisfying these conditions.

Does a mechanism computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) guarantee \(U(M)\ge0.2015\,\mathrm{OPT}\) on every such society, or is obtaining this approximation guarantee NP-hard?

For finitely many independent sellers with regular cost distributions, an efficient DSIC-IR mechanism achieves at least a \(0.2015\) approximation to optimal expected buyer utility under a hard budget; randomized posted prices extend this guarantee to irregular distributions. [Verified full version](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00101v2).
No result settling this precise atomless formulation was identified in the targeted literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one, so the per-capita budget is the aggregate payment bound; required a positive budget.
  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational values and costs, nonempty class supports, and unrestricted nonnegative cost reports, following the procurement domain.
  • Made populations atomless and introduced public priorities uniformly distributed within each private type, allowing integral service decisions to distinguish otherwise identical sellers without revealing costs.
  • Specified a distribution-based mechanism interface using public class, priority, own report, and aggregate report measure; private type membership is unavailable to the mechanism.
  • Allowed randomized mechanisms with integral realized allocations, interpreted DSIC and IR in expectation over internal randomness, and imposed the hard budget separately for every randomness realization.
  • Defined truthful aggregate utility and used a supremum benchmark to avoid assuming that an optimal mechanism is attained.
  • Retained arbitrary finite cost supports because the verified full version extends the anchor's approximation guarantee beyond regular distributions.

Status and partial result

The mathematical optimum has a polynomial-size linear programming characterization and is attained by a deterministic mechanism. The remaining issue is the cost of executing the mechanism on arbitrary aggregate report measures.

A further result removes the need for exact aggregate comparison: certified aggregate estimates with polynomially many bits suffice for a deterministic mechanism attaining
$$ U(M)=\frac12\,\mathrm{OPT}. $$
This gives the requested approximation under an explicitly stated aggregate-access model. I do not establish that this computational access model follows from the task’s observation assumption alone.

I interpret “computable in polynomial time” as including mechanism execution, as required by the verifier’s objection.

1. A finite linear program

For each class $t$, list its positive-mass supported costs in increasing order:
$$ c_{t,1}<\cdots<c_{t,k_t}. $$
Write $\mu_{t,i}$ for their masses, and define
$$ F_{t,j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\mu_{t,i}. $$
These are unnormalized cumulative masses, and $\sum_t k_t=\tau$.

Consider the linear program
$$ V=\max_w\sum_{t=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{k_t} (v_t-c_{t,j})F_{t,j}w_{t,j} $$
subject to
$$ w_{t,j}\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{k_t}w_{t,j}\le 1 \quad\text{for every }t, $$
and
$$ \sum_{t=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{k_t} c_{t,j}F_{t,j}w_{t,j}\le b. $$

The zero vector is feasible. Every feasible variable belongs to $[0,1]$, so the feasible region is compact. Consequently, an optimum exists and $V\ge 0$.

The interpretation of $w_{t,j}$ is the length of a priority interval receiving posted price $c_{t,j}$. At truthful reports, uniform priorities within each type give accepted mass $w_{t,j}F_{t,j}$.

2. Proven upper bound for every admissible mechanism

Fix an admissible mechanism and its truthful aggregate report measure $\gamma^*$.

For class $t$, priority $z$, and supported report $c_{t,i}$, let $q_{t,i}(z)$ and $p_{t,i}(z)$ denote expected allocation and payment with aggregate input $\gamma^*$. The observation restriction makes these functions independent of the seller’s true cost except through the report. A unilateral deviation leaves $\gamma^*$ unchanged.

Nonnegative payments and the realized hard-budget constraint imply, by Tonelli’s theorem,
$$ P:=\sum_t\sum_i\mu_{t,i} \int_0^1p_{t,i}(z)\,dz\le b. $$
Because every listed mass is positive, all these payment functions are finite outside a common null set. Work outside that set and fix $t,z$, suppressing these indices.

For $i<j$, DSIC gives
$$ p_i-c_iq_i\ge p_j-c_iq_j, \qquad p_j-c_jq_j\ge p_i-c_jq_i. $$
Adding yields
$$ (c_j-c_i)(q_i-q_j)\ge 0. $$
Thus
$$ 1\ge q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_{k_t}\ge 0. $$

Set $q_{k_t+1}=0$ and
$$ d_j=q_j-q_{j+1}. $$
Then $d_j\ge 0$ and $\sum_jd_j=q_1\le 1$.

The DSIC inequality for a cost-$c_i$ seller reporting the next higher supported cost gives
$$ p_i\ge p_{i+1}+c_i(q_i-q_{i+1}). $$
IR at the largest supported cost gives
$$ p_{k_t}\ge c_{k_t}q_{k_t}. $$
Iterating these inequalities therefore proves
$$ p_i\ge \sum_{j=i}^{k_t}c_j(q_j-q_{j+1}) =\sum_{j=i}^{k_t}c_jd_j. $$

Define
$$ \bar w_{t,j}=\int_0^1d_{t,j}(z)\,dz. $$
These numbers are nonnegative and satisfy $\sum_j\bar w_{t,j}\le 1$. Integrating the payment bound and rearranging finite sums gives
$$ P\ge \sum_t\sum_jc_{t,j}F_{t,j}\bar w_{t,j}. $$
Hence $\bar w$ satisfies the LP’s budget constraint.

Since $q_{t,i}=\sum_{j=i}^{k_t}d_{t,j}$, expected buyer value equals
$$ \sum_t\sum_i\mu_{t,i}v_t\int_0^1q_{t,i}(z)\,dz = \sum_t\sum_jv_tF_{t,j}\bar w_{t,j}. $$
Subtracting the payment lower bound proves
$$ U(M)\le \sum_t\sum_j(v_t-c_{t,j})F_{t,j}\bar w_{t,j} \le V. $$

This argument does not require independence of mechanism randomness across sellers. Taking the supremum gives
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\le V. $$

3. Proven mathematical attainment

Let $w^*$ be an optimal LP solution. For each class, partition an initial portion of $[0,1)$ into disjoint half-open intervals $I_{t,j}$ of lengths $w^*_{t,j}$. Priorities outside these intervals, including priority $1$, receive no offer.

Let $\gamma$ denote the observed aggregate measure on class, priority, and report. Define
$$ S(\gamma)= \sum_t\sum_jc_{t,j}\, \gamma\bigl(\{t\}\times I_{t,j}\times[0,c_{t,j}]\bigr). $$

If $S(\gamma)\le b$, a seller in $I_{t,j}$ trades and receives payment $c_{t,j}$ exactly when its report is at most $c_{t,j}$. If $S(\gamma)>b$, all allocations and payments are zero.

For every report profile, the payment integral is either $S(\gamma)\le b$ or zero. Thus the realized hard budget holds.

Fix a seller and the other sellers’ reports. Its unilateral report cannot change $\gamma$, so it cannot change the cancellation decision. If offers are honored, accepting price $p$ gives utility $p-c$, whereas rejecting gives zero. Truthful reporting accepts precisely when $c\le p$ and therefore maximizes utility and gives nonnegative utility. Cancellation and no offer give zero utility.

This proves DSIC and IR, including for nonnegative true costs outside the supplied supports.

At truthful reports,
$$ \gamma^*\bigl(\{t\}\times I_{t,j}\times[0,c_{t,j}]\bigr) =w^*_{t,j}F_{t,j}. $$
LP feasibility therefore prevents cancellation, and truthful utility is $V$. Together with Section 2, this establishes the unconditional mathematical identity
$$ \boxed{\mathrm{OPT}=V.} $$

This attainment argument does not assert polynomial-time execution of the exact aggregate test.

4. Polynomial-time construction from the society input

The LP has $\tau$ variables, $m$ class constraints, and one budget constraint, besides nonnegativity. Its coefficients are rational sums and products of input rationals and have polynomial total encoding length.

The standard rational linear programming theorem provides exact optimization in time polynomial in the rational input encoding length. The rational case is covered by [Adler and Beling’s polynomial algorithms for linear programming](https://adler.ieor.berkeley.edu/ilans_pubs/lp_algebraic_stoc_1992.pdf). Here its hypotheses hold: the data are rational and the feasible region is nonempty and bounded.

An optimal solution can have polynomial encoding length. Indeed, choose an optimal vertex. After clearing denominators, its coordinates solve a nonsingular integer system with polynomial-bit coefficients. Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound
$$ |\det A|\le \tau! H^\tau, $$
where $H$ bounds the absolute matrix entries, give polynomial-bit numerators and denominators.

Thus the optimal weights, interval endpoints, and a finite description of the mechanism can all be constructed in polynomial time. This fact alone does not bound the cost of aggregate evaluation during execution.

5. New result: certified finite precision suffices

Explicit access assumption

For this section only, assume the following aggregate oracle.

Given rational interval endpoints, a rational price, and an integer precision $n$, the oracle returns a dyadic number $a\in[0,1]$ satisfying
$$ |a-\gamma(R)|\le 2^{-n}, $$
where
$$ R=\{t\}\times I\times[0,p]. $$
The answer is a deterministic function of the aggregate measure and the query. Its computational cost is polynomial in the query encoding length and $n$.

Individual comparisons of priorities and reports against rational thresholds are also counted as available primitives. The result below is conditional on these access assumptions.

Construction

Scale the optimal weights by one half:
$$ \widehat w_{t,j}=\frac12w^*_{t,j}. $$
Form disjoint half-open intervals $\widehat I_{t,j}$ with these lengths and use the same posted prices $c_{t,j}$.

Set
$$ H=\sum_t\sum_jc_{t,j}. $$
Choose $n$ so that, with $\delta=2^{-n}$,
$$ 2H\delta\le \frac b2. $$
If $H=0$, this condition holds for every $n$.

For each rectangle
$$ \widehat R_{t,j} =\{t\}\times\widehat I_{t,j}\times[0,c_{t,j}], $$
query a certified approximation $a_{t,j}$ and define
$$ u_{t,j}=\min\{1,a_{t,j}+\delta\}. $$
Writing $R_{t,j}=\gamma(\widehat R_{t,j})$, the approximation guarantee implies
$$ R_{t,j}\le u_{t,j}\le R_{t,j}+2\delta. $$
The first inequality follows from $R_{t,j}\le a_{t,j}+\delta$ and $R_{t,j}\le 1$; the second follows from $a_{t,j}\le R_{t,j}+\delta$.

Compute the rational upper bill
$$ T(\gamma)=\sum_t\sum_jc_{t,j}u_{t,j}. $$
Honor all posted-price acceptances if $T(\gamma)\le b$; otherwise cancel all trade.

Hard budget, DSIC, and IR

The actual bill if offers are honored is
$$ \widehat S(\gamma)= \sum_t\sum_jc_{t,j}R_{t,j}. $$
Since costs are nonnegative,
$$ \widehat S(\gamma)\le T(\gamma) \le\widehat S(\gamma)+2H\delta. $$
Thus every honored profile has actual payment at most $b$, and every canceled profile has payment zero.

A unilateral report changes neither the aggregate measure nor the oracle answers. Consequently, the gate is fixed under unilateral deviations. The posted-price argument from Section 3 proves DSIC and IR for every report profile.

Truthful performance

At truth, uniform priorities give
$$ \widehat S(\gamma^*)= \frac12\sum_t\sum_j c_{t,j}F_{t,j}w^*_{t,j} \le\frac b2. $$
Therefore
$$ T(\gamma^*)\le \widehat S(\gamma^*)+2H\delta \le b. $$
Truthful trade is always honored, and
$$ U(M)= \frac12\sum_t\sum_j (v_t-c_{t,j})F_{t,j}w^*_{t,j} =\frac12V =\frac12\,\mathrm{OPT}. $$
Since $\mathrm{OPT}=V\ge 0$,
$$ U(M)\ge 0.2015\,\mathrm{OPT}. $$

Polynomial precision and arithmetic

Under ordinary binary rational encoding, every input cost is at most $2^L$ and $b\ge 2^{-L}$. Hence
$$ H\le\tau 2^L. $$
For example, choose
$$ n=2L+\lceil\log_2\tau\rceil+3. $$
Then
$$ 2H2^{-n} \le\frac{2^{-L}}4 \le\frac b4, $$
which is more than sufficient.

There are at most $\tau$ oracle queries. Their endpoints have polynomial encoding length, and their requested precision is polynomial in $L$ and $\log\tau$. Every subsequent operation uses rational numbers of polynomial encoding length. In particular, comparison of $T(\gamma)$ with $b$ is an exact rational comparison.

Thus, under the stated access assumptions, construction and execution require polynomially many operations with polynomial bit complexity. Exact comparison of an arbitrary real aggregate bill with the budget is unnecessary.

6. Why the access assumption remains substantive

Finite-precision aggregate evaluation is not automatically implementable by finitely many pointwise queries to an arbitrary measurable report profile.

For a concrete proof, take one class and one supported type of mass $1$, parameterized by uniform priority $z\in[0,1]$. Consider the aggregate mass of reports in $[0,1]$.

Suppose a deterministic algorithm queries the report function at finitely many priorities and promises absolute error strictly less than $1/2$. Run it on
$$ r_0(z)=0. $$
Let $Q$ be its finite set of queried priorities. Define another measurable profile by
$$ r_1(z)= \begin{cases} 0,&z\in Q,\\ 2,&z\notin Q. \end{cases} $$
The algorithm has exactly the same execution on these profiles: by induction, each query receives the same answer, so its subsequent queries and final output agree.

But the accepted masses are respectively $1$ and $0$, because $Q$ has Lebesgue measure zero. No common output can approximate both with error strictly less than $1/2$. This is a contradiction.

This establishes a limitation of pointwise profile access, not an approximation-hardness theorem.

7. Remaining computational question

The mathematical optimization problem is fully characterized, and the requested approximation follows with polynomial oracle complexity using only certified finite-precision aggregate estimates.

What remains unproved is that the task’s stipulated observation supplies those estimates—and the relevant individual threshold comparisons—at polynomial computational cost. Without a specified representation or access model, the unconditional execution-time classification remains unresolved. No NP-hardness result is established here.
