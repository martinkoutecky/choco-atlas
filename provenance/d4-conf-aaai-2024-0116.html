The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite directed cohort graph \(H=(Q,E)\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_q\) summing to \(1\), where each cohort has a shared epistemic state and an activated edge updates the receiving cohort together, and a satisfiable non-tautological target formula \(\varphi\), add a campaign source holding \(\varphi\) with edges to a chosen set \(S\subseteq Q\). What set \(S\) of minimum mass \(\sum_{q\in S}\mu_q\) makes \(\varphi\) unanimously accepted in every admissible run, for every BFN with cohort graph \(H\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(Q\) of \(\tau\) cohort types, each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_q\), with \(\sum_{q\in Q}\mu_q=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. The input also gives a loopless directed graph \(H=(Q,E)\) and a satisfiable, non-tautological propositional formula \(\varphi\) over \(m\) variables; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Each cohort has one shared epistemic state \(\Psi\), a belief formula \(B(\Psi)\), and its own change operator \(\circ\); initial beliefs are consistent. States and operators are universally quantified, not supplied as input. Define \(\Psi\circ^k\alpha\) by repeated updating and \(\Psi\star\alpha\) as the first positive iterate whose beliefs entail \(\alpha\).
An allowed operator has a bound \(k_\circ\ge1\) such that \(B(\Psi\circ^{k_\circ}\alpha)\models\alpha\) for every state and formula, and satisfies the following conditions for all states and formulas:
\(B(\Psi)\land\alpha\not\models\bot\Rightarrow B(\Psi\star\alpha)\equiv B(\Psi)\land\alpha\); \(\alpha\not\models\bot\Rightarrow B(\Psi\circ\alpha)\not\models\bot\); replacing each input in any finite update sequence by a logically equivalent formula preserves the resulting beliefs up to equivalence.
\(B(\Psi\star\alpha)\land\beta\models B(\Psi\star(\alpha\land\beta))\); if the left side is consistent, the converse entailment also holds.
\((\alpha\models\beta\text{ or }\alpha\models\neg\beta)\Rightarrow B((\Psi\circ\beta)\star\alpha)\equiv B(\Psi\star\alpha)\); \(B(\Psi\star\alpha)\not\models\neg\beta\Rightarrow B((\Psi\circ\beta)\star\alpha)\models\beta\).
Choose whole cohorts \(S\subseteq Q\), costing \(\sum_{q\in S}\mu_q\), and add a source \(c\) with beliefs equivalent to \(\varphi\), no incoming edges, and exactly the outgoing edges \(\{(c,q):q\in S\}\). The source has no society mass or additional cost.
A run activates one augmented edge \((u,v)\) per step and updates only the receiving cohort by \(\Psi_v^{t+1}=\Psi_v^t\circ_v B(\Psi_u^t)\). An admissible run contains every finite word over the augmented edge set as a contiguous block; if that set is empty, the sole run leaves all states constant.
Call \(S\) successful if, for every allowed initial state and operator profile and every admissible run, there exists \(T\) such that \(B(\Psi_q^t)\models\varphi\) for all \(q\in Q\) and \(t\ge T\).

Is computing a successful \(S\) of minimum mass solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,|E|,L\), or is the associated decision problem—whether a successful \(S\) of mass at most a binary-rational budget \(b\in[0,1]\) exists—\(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite agents with equal selection costs, a universally successful campaign of minimum cardinality is computable in polynomial time in the graph size. [Source](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/28941/29789)

Result

The minimum-mass campaign is computable in polynomial time. Let $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{src}}$ be the set of source strongly connected components of $H$, meaning components with no incoming edge from another component. Then
$$ S\text{ is successful} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\cap C\ne\varnothing \text{ for every }C\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{src}}. $$
Consequently, the optimum mass is
$$ \operatorname{OPT} = \sum_{C\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{src}}} \min_{q\in C}\mu_q. $$

This graph criterion also appears in Proposition 8 of the [cited paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/28941/29789). Below is a direct proof for the precise axioms and admissible runs in the question.

1. A consequence of the operator axioms

For a formula $\gamma$, write $[\gamma]$ for its set of satisfying assignments. Fix an allowed operator $\circ$, a state $\Psi$ with consistent beliefs, and a satisfiable input $\beta$. Put
$$ D=B(\Psi), \qquad D'=B(\Psi\circ\beta). $$
The consistency axiom ensures that $D'$ is consistent.

We prove
$$ [D']\subseteq[D]\cup[\beta]. $$
We also prove the stronger statement
$$ D\land\beta\not\models\bot \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D'\equiv D\land\beta. $$

First suppose $D\land\beta$ is consistent. The conjunction axiom for $\star$ gives
$$ B(\Psi\star\top)\equiv D. $$
Thus $B(\Psi\star\top)\not\models\neg\beta$. Applying the final iteration axiom with $\alpha=\top$ yields
$$ B((\Psi\circ\beta)\star\top)\models\beta. $$
Because $D'$ is consistent, the conjunction axiom identifies the left-hand beliefs with $D'$. Hence $D'\models\beta$.

The iteration axiom for $\alpha\models\beta$, applied with $\alpha=\beta$, gives
$$ B((\Psi\circ\beta)\star\beta) \equiv B(\Psi\star\beta). $$
Using the conjunction axiom on both sides, together with $D'\models\beta$, now gives
$$ D'\equiv D\land\beta. $$

Next suppose $D\land\beta$ is inconsistent. Then $D\models\neg\beta$. The iteration axiom for $\alpha\models\neg\beta$, applied with $\alpha=\neg\beta$, gives
$$ B((\Psi\circ\beta)\star\neg\beta) \equiv B(\Psi\star\neg\beta) \equiv D. $$
If $D'\land\neg\beta$ is consistent, the conjunction axiom therefore gives
$$ D'\land\neg\beta\equiv D. $$
If it is inconsistent, $D'\models\beta$. In either case, every model of $D'$ satisfies $D$ or $\beta$, proving the claimed inclusion.

In particular, we have preservation of common entailments:
$$ D\models\varphi,\quad \beta\models\varphi \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D'\models\varphi. $$

Throughout any run, all beliefs remain consistent: initially they are consistent, the source believes the satisfiable formula $\varphi$, and every subsequent input is the consistent belief formula of its sender.

2. Reachability from the source is sufficient

Suppose every cohort is reachable from $c$ in the augmented graph. Fix an arbitrary allowed initial profile and operator profile. For each cohort $q$, choose its uniform bound $k_q$.

Choose a directed spanning tree rooted at $c$. For each cohort $q$, let $p(q)$ be its parent. Process the cohorts in an order in which parents precede children, and form a finite edge word $W$ by concatenating, for each $q$, exactly $k_q$ repetitions of
$$ (p(q),q). $$

We claim that executing $W$ from any consistent profile makes every cohort believe $\varphi$.

During the block for $q$, only $q$ is updated. Its parent is distinct from $q$, so the parent's state stays fixed throughout that block. The parent already believes $\varphi$: this holds initially for $c$ and, inductively, holds for every processed parent. Writing $\beta$ for that fixed parent's belief formula, the uniform bound gives
$$ B(\Psi_q\circ_q^{k_q}\beta)\models\beta\models\varphi. $$
Previously processed cohorts are unchanged during this block. Induction over the tree order proves the claim.

Every admissible run contains $W$ as a contiguous block. Let $T$ be the time immediately after such a block. Then every cohort believes $\varphi$ at time $T$. Every later sender, including $c$, also believes $\varphi$. The preservation result from Section 1 therefore implies, inductively, that
$$ B(\Psi_q^t)\models\varphi \qquad \text{for every }q\in Q\text{ and every }t\ge T. $$

The profile and run were arbitrary. Thus reachability of every cohort from $c$ implies universal success.

3. Missing a source component prevents success

We first construct an allowed operator explicitly, so that the counterexample does not require any additional assumptions about operators.

Let $\Omega$ be the finite set of assignments to the propositional variables. A state consists of a total preorder $\preceq$ on $\Omega$ and a flag $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$. Its belief models are
$$ [B(\preceq,\varepsilon)] = \begin{cases} \min_{\preceq}\Omega,&\varepsilon=1,\\ \varnothing,&\varepsilon=0. \end{cases} $$
Every such model set is definable by a propositional formula.

For an input $\alpha$ with model set $A$:

  • If $A\ne\varnothing$, put all worlds in $A$ strictly before all worlds outside $A$, preserving the previous ordering within both groups, and set the flag to $1$.
  • If $A=\varnothing$, preserve the preorder and set the flag to $0$.

This is lexicographic revision, extended to inconsistent inputs. Its resulting beliefs satisfy
$$ [B(\Psi\circ\alpha)]=\min_{\preceq}A, $$
where the minimum of the empty set is empty. Hence $\star$ is the first update and the uniform bound is $1$.

Here are the checks of all remaining axioms.

  • Conjunction when consistent. If the original belief models intersect $A$, the flag is $1$ and

$$ \min_{\preceq}A = \min_{\preceq}\Omega\cap A. $$
This is precisely the required conjunction.

  • Consistency and equivalence. A nonempty $A$ has nonempty minima. Updates depend only on model sets, so replacing inputs by equivalent formulas leaves the state, and therefore all resulting beliefs, unchanged.
  • The two conjunction entailments. For arbitrary model sets $A,F$,

$$ \min_{\preceq}A\cap F \subseteq \min_{\preceq}(A\cap F). $$
If the left-hand set is nonempty, equality holds: an element of that set witnesses that the minimum rank available in $A\cap F$ equals the minimum rank in $A$. These are exactly the two required entailments.

  • The two iteration equalities. If $A\subseteq[\beta]$ or $A\subseteq[\neg\beta]$, updating by $\beta$ preserves the ordering within $A$. Thus its minima, and consequently the beliefs after $\star\alpha$, are unchanged.
  • The final iteration entailment. Its antecedent says that

$$ \min_{\preceq}A\cap[\beta]\ne\varnothing. $$
After updating by $\beta$, all $\beta$-worlds precede all other worlds. The minima within $A$ therefore belong to $[\beta]$, as required.

Thus this operator is allowed on its entire state space, including inconsistent states.

Now suppose $C$ is a source strongly connected component of $H$ with $S\cap C=\varnothing$. Since $\varphi$ is non-tautological, choose a world
$$ w_-\models\neg\varphi. $$
Give every cohort initial beliefs whose sole model is $w_-$, and give every cohort the allowed operator just constructed. Such initial beliefs are represented by a preorder with $w_-$ as its unique minimum and flag $1$. The source can likewise be represented with $[\varphi]$ as its minimum class.

There are no incoming edges into $C$ from outside $C$: none from another cohort component because $C$ is a source component, and none from $c$ because $S$ misses $C$.

Inductively, every cohort in $C$ retains the singleton belief model set $\{w_-\}$. Indeed, any update received in $C$ comes from another cohort in $C$, whose input has that same singleton model set. Revision by that input again produces exactly $\{w_-\}$.

Therefore no cohort in $C$ ever believes $\varphi$, and success fails.

For completeness, when the augmented edge set is nonempty, admissible runs exist by concatenating an enumeration of all finite edge words. The counterexample works for every such run. When the edge set is empty, it works for the stipulated constant run.

4. The exact graph criterion

The condensation graph of $H$ is a finite directed acyclic graph. Every component is reachable from a source component: repeatedly following incoming edges backwards must terminate at a source.

If $S$ intersects every source component, then $c$ reaches a selected vertex in each such component. Strong connectivity lets it reach the whole component, and paths in the condensation let it reach every remaining component. Section 2 therefore proves success.

Conversely, if $S$ misses a source component, Section 3 proves failure. This establishes
$$ S\text{ is successful} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\cap C\ne\varnothing \text{ for every }C\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{src}}. $$

5. Minimum mass and computational complexity

The source components are disjoint. Every successful $S$ must therefore satisfy
$$ \sum_{q\in S}\mu_q \ge \sum_{C\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{src}}} \min_{q\in C}\mu_q. $$
Choose a minimum-mass cohort $q_C$ from each source component and set
$$ S^\ast=\{q_C:C\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{src}}\}. $$
This set is successful and achieves the lower bound, proving the optimum formula.

The algorithm computes the strongly connected components, identifies those with no incoming edge from another component, and chooses a minimum-mass cohort in each. The graph computation takes time
$$ O(\tau+|E|). $$
Comparing the binary-rational masses, summing the chosen masses, and comparing the result with $b$ use exact rational arithmetic with polynomial bit complexity in the total input length.

No model enumeration or propositional satisfiability test is needed; the promised properties of $\varphi$ suffice. Thus both the optimization problem and the associated decision problem are polynomial-time solvable in the requested parameters.
