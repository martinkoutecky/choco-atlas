The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), integers \(k,d\), \(0<\epsilon<1\), and a rational society \(\mu\) over types \(t=(A_t,\lambda^t)\) with \(|A_t|\le d-1\) whenever \(\mu_t>0\), construct in time \(f(d,k,\epsilon)\operatorname{poly}(m+\tau+L)\) a rational weighted instance \((C',T',\mu',k)\) and a lifting map such that \(|C'|\le(dk/\epsilon)^{O(d^2)}\), \(|T'|\le(dk/\epsilon)^{O(d^3)}\), and every \(\beta\)-approximate committee for the reduced instance lifts to \(S\subseteq C\) with \(|S|\le k\) and \(F_\mu(S)\ge(1-\epsilon)\beta\operatorname{OPT}_\mu(k)\), where \(F_\mu(S)=\sum_t\mu_t\sum_{j=1}^{|S\cap A_t|}\lambda^t_j\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set with \(m=|C|\), let \(k\geq1\) and \(d\geq2\) be integers, and let \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\) be a binary rational.
A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Each type is \(t=(A_t,\lambda^t)\), where \(\varnothing\ne A_t\subseteq C\), \(|A_t|\leq d-1\), and \(\lambda^t\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^{|A_t|}\) satisfies \(1\geq\lambda^t_1>0\) and \(\lambda^t_j\geq\lambda^t_{j+1}\).
For a committee \(S\subseteq C\), define \(F_\mu(S)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\sum_{j=1}^{|S\cap A_t|}\lambda^t_j\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu(k)=\max_{|S|\leq k}F_\mu(S)\). Reduced types and masses obey the same approval, vector, and normalization conditions; define their score and optimum analogously.

Does there exist a reduction running in \(f(d,k,\epsilon)\operatorname{poly}(m+\tau+L)\), for some computable \(f\), producing a rational weighted instance \((C',T',\mu',k)\) with \(|C'|\leq(dk/\epsilon)^{O(d^2)}\) and \(|T'|\leq(dk/\epsilon)^{O(d^3)}\), together with a polynomial-time lifting algorithm?
For every \(\beta\in(0,1]\), must every \(S'\subseteq C'\) with \(|S'|\leq k\) and \(F_{\mu'}(S')\geq\beta\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu'}(k)\) lift to \(S\subseteq C\) with \(|S|\leq k\) and \(F_\mu(S)\geq(1-\epsilon)\beta\operatorname{OPT}_\mu(k)\)?

For discrete voters, Gupta et al. establish a parameter-preserving \((1-\epsilon)\)-approximate kernel with these candidate and voter bounds whenever no \(d\) voters jointly approve \(d\) candidates. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12699v2).
No result establishing or excluding exactly this rational-mass compression guarantee was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational, non-increasing OWA vectors of length equal to the approval-set size, with first entry at most one, following the discrete scoring domain.
  • Excluded empty approval sets and all-zero scoring vectors, following the discrete optimization model's removal of voters with zero contribution; consequently used d at least two.
  • Normalized input and reduced masses to sum to one, interpreting a distribution over types as a probability distribution.
  • Specified positive integer k and binary-rational epsilon, and defined L as the total input bit length.
  • Required the reduced instance to retain the bounded approval sets and scoring-vector domain, and required polynomial-time solution lifting as in approximate preprocessing.
  • Interpreted the requested size bounds as candidate and support counts, without adding a parameter-only bound on rational encoding lengths.

Result

Yes. Under the stated hypothesis \(|A_t|\leq d-1\), there is a polynomial-time reduction with the stronger bounds

\(\displaystyle |C'|\leq \frac{2dk}{\epsilon},\qquad |T'|\leq \left(\frac{2dk}{\epsilon}\right)^{d-1}.\)

The lifting algorithm is the identity: retain the original names of candidates in \(C'\) and return \(S=S'\).

The proof uses bounded approval-set size directly. The cited [Gupta et al. paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12699v2) addresses the broader class of profiles in which no \(d\) voters jointly approve \(d\) candidates.

1. Candidate reduction

Write \(r=d-1\), \(F=F_\mu\), and \(M=\operatorname{OPT}_\mu(k)\). For each candidate \(c\), define its singleton score

\(\displaystyle w(c)=F(\{c\})=\sum_{t:\,c\in A_t}\mu_t\lambda^t_1.\)

Set

\(\displaystyle N=k+\left\lceil\frac{kr}{\epsilon}\right\rceil.\)

Retain the \(\min\{m,N\}\) candidates with largest singleton scores, breaking ties arbitrarily, and call this set \(C'\).

We prove

\(\displaystyle \max_{\substack{S\subseteq C'\\ |S|\leq k}}F(S)\geq(1-\epsilon)M.\)

If \(m\leq N\), then \(C'=C\), so the assertion is immediate. Assume henceforth that \(m>N\), and consequently \(|C'|=N\).

Exchange bound

Let \(S\subseteq C\) satisfy \(|S|\leq k\), and suppose \(x\in S\setminus C'\). Put \(U=S\setminus\{x\}\) and \(R=C'\setminus S\). In particular,

\(\displaystyle |R|\geq N-k>0.\)

For \(y\in R\), define the overlap penalty

\(\displaystyle P_U(y)=\sum_{\substack{t:\,y\in A_t\\ U\cap A_t\neq\varnothing}}\mu_t\lambda^t_1.\)

Because \(y\notin U\), its marginal score is exactly

\(\displaystyle F(U\cup\{y\})-F(U)=\sum_{t:\,y\in A_t}\mu_t\lambda^t_{|U\cap A_t|+1}.\)

The index exists because \(y\in A_t\setminus U\). If \(U\cap A_t=\varnothing\), the corresponding contribution is \(\mu_t\lambda^t_1\); otherwise it is nonnegative. Therefore

\(\displaystyle F(U\cup\{y\})-F(U)\geq w(y)-P_U(y).\)

Similarly, nonincreasingness of every vector gives

\(\displaystyle F(S)-F(U)=\sum_{t:\,x\in A_t}\mu_t\lambda^t_{|U\cap A_t|+1}\leq w(x).\)

Since \(y\in C'\) and \(x\notin C'\), the definition of \(C'\) implies \(w(y)\geq w(x)\). Combining these inequalities yields

\(\displaystyle F(U\cup\{y\})\geq F(S)-P_U(y).\)

It remains to find a replacement with small overlap penalty. Interchanging the finite sums gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{y\in R}P_U(y)=\sum_{t:\,U\cap A_t\neq\varnothing}\mu_t\lambda^t_1\,|A_t\cap R|.\)

Using \(|A_t|\leq r\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle \sum_{y\in R}P_U(y)\leq r\sum_{t:\,U\cap A_t\neq\varnothing}\mu_t\lambda^t_1\leq rF(U)\leq rM.\)

The second inequality holds because every type whose approval set intersects \(U\) contributes at least \(\mu_t\lambda^t_1\) to \(F(U)\). The last inequality follows from \(|U|\leq k\).

By averaging, some \(y\in R\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle P_U(y)\leq\frac{rM}{|R|}\leq\frac{rM}{N-k}.\)

Thus replacing \(x\) with this \(y\) preserves committee size and loses at most \(rM/(N-k)\).

Preserving a near-optimal committee

Start with an optimal committee \(O\), where \(|O|\leq k\). Apply the exchange bound whenever the current committee contains a candidate outside \(C'\).

Each exchange decreases the number of outside candidates by one. Every intermediate committee still has size at most \(k\), so the exchange bound remains applicable. After at most \(k\) exchanges, we obtain \(O'\subseteq C'\) with

\(\displaystyle F(O')\geq M-\frac{krM}{N-k}\geq(1-\epsilon)M,\)

where the last inequality uses \(N-k=\lceil kr/\epsilon\rceil\geq kr/\epsilon\). This proves the candidate-reduction guarantee.

2. Exact type compression and normalization

For every original type \(t\), let \(B_t=A_t\cap C'\). Let

\(\displaystyle \mathcal B=\{B_t:t\in T,\ B_t\neq\varnothing\}.\)

For each \(B\in\mathcal B\), define

\(\displaystyle a_B=\sum_{t:\,B_t=B}\mu_t,\qquad b_{B,j}=\sum_{t:\,B_t=B}\mu_t\lambda^t_j\quad(1\leq j\leq |B|).\)

These coordinates exist because \(|B|\leq |A_t|\) for every type in the group.

Also define the mass of types whose restricted approval set is empty:

\(\displaystyle e=\sum_{t:\,B_t=\varnothing}\mu_t.\)

There is at least one group in \(\mathcal B\). Indeed, some original candidate has positive singleton score because the masses sum to one, every approval set is nonempty, and every \(\lambda^t_1\) is positive. The retained set contains a candidate with maximum singleton score.

Choose any \(B_*\in\mathcal B\). Define reduced masses by

\(\displaystyle \rho_B=\begin{cases}a_B+e,&B=B_*,\\ a_B,&B\neq B_*.\end{cases}\)

For each \(B\in\mathcal B\), create one reduced type with approval set \(B\), mass \(\mu'_B=\rho_B\), and vector

\(\displaystyle \lambda^{\prime B}_j=\frac{b_{B,j}}{\rho_B}\quad(1\leq j\leq |B|).\)

Validity of the reduced instance

Every reduced approval set is nonempty and has size at most \(r=d-1\). Moreover, \(\rho_B>0\), and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{B\in\mathcal B}\rho_B=\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}a_B+e=1.\)

For every group, the original vector conditions imply

\(\displaystyle 0<b_{B,1}\leq a_B\leq\rho_B,\qquad b_{B,j}\geq b_{B,j+1}\geq0.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle 1\geq\lambda^{\prime B}_1>0,\qquad \lambda^{\prime B}_j\geq\lambda^{\prime B}_{j+1}\geq0.\)

All coefficients are rational. The reduced masses are sums of original masses; thus they also remain dyadic if “binary rational” is interpreted as requiring dyadic masses.

Exact preservation of restricted scores

For every \(X\subseteq C'\),

\(\displaystyle F_{\mu'}(X)=\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}\rho_B\sum_{j=1}^{|X\cap B|}\frac{b_{B,j}}{\rho_B}=\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}\sum_{j=1}^{|X\cap B|}b_{B,j}.\)

Expanding the definition of \(b_{B,j}\) gives

\(\displaystyle F_{\mu'}(X)=\sum_{t:\,B_t\neq\varnothing}\mu_t\sum_{j=1}^{|X\cap B_t|}\lambda^t_j=F_\mu(X).\)

Here \(X\subseteq C'\) implies \(X\cap B_t=X\cap A_t\), and every type with \(B_t=\varnothing\) contributes zero.

Thus the reduced score agrees exactly with the original score on every retained committee, despite the reassignment of empty-approval mass.

3. Lifting every approximate solution

The exact score identity and Section 1 imply

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{OPT}_{\mu'}(k)=\max_{\substack{X\subseteq C'\\ |X|\leq k}}F_\mu(X)\geq(1-\epsilon)\operatorname{OPT}_\mu(k).\)

Let any \(\beta\in(0,1]\) and any feasible \(S'\subseteq C'\) satisfy

\(\displaystyle F_{\mu'}(S')\geq\beta\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu'}(k).\)

Lift by returning \(S=S'\), with its original candidate names. Then \(|S|\leq k\), and

\(\displaystyle F_\mu(S)=F_{\mu'}(S')\geq\beta\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu'}(k)\geq(1-\epsilon)\beta\operatorname{OPT}_\mu(k).\)

This proves the required guarantee simultaneously for every \(\beta\).

4. Size and running time

Put \(q=dk/\epsilon\). Since \(d\geq2\), \(k\geq1\), and \(\epsilon<1\), we have \(q>2\). Also,

\(\displaystyle N\leq k+\frac{k(d-1)}{\epsilon}+1\leq q+1.\)

Hence \(|C'|\leq N\leq2q\).

There is one reduced type per distinct nonempty restricted approval set. Therefore, writing \(n'=|C'|\),

\(\displaystyle |T'|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{r,n'\}}\binom{n'}{i}\leq(n'+1)^r\leq(N+1)^r\leq(2q)^r.\)

The middle counting bound follows by encoding each subset as its increasing list of candidate indices, padded with zeros to length \(r\). Since \(q>2\), these bounds imply the requested \((dk/\epsilon)^{O(d^2)}\) candidate bound and \((dk/\epsilon)^{O(d^3)}\) type bound.

The algorithm computes singleton scores, sorts candidates, intersects approval sets with \(C'\), and groups the resulting nonempty sets. It processes only groups that actually occur; it never enumerates all possible approval sets.

All arithmetic can be exact with polynomial bit complexity. To see this, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses and vector entries. Its bit length is at most \(L\). Every mass sum and every sum of products \(\mu_t\lambda^t_j\) has denominator dividing \(D\); these quantities lie in \([0,1]\), so their numerators over denominator \(D\) have \(O(L)\) bits. Ratios \(b_{B,j}/\rho_B\) likewise have \(O(L)\)-bit numerator and denominator. Standard exact rational arithmetic therefore suffices.

The reduction runs in \(\operatorname{poly}(m+\tau+L)\) time, which is stronger than the requested running time. Identity lifting is polynomial-time.
