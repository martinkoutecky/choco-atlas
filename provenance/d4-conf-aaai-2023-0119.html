The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite driver and request type sets, rational offline masses, rational per-period arrival masses, edge data, group memberships, and a finite horizon, characterize the competitive-ratio vectors achievable by causal fluid matching policies. Each policy may split mass among allowed probe sequences, tracking patience states and splitting probed mass into successful and failed fractions according to edge success probabilities; compare its operator-profit and group-fairness values with the separate fluid optima. Determine whether every achievable three-objective vector satisfies \(r_O+r_U+r_V\leq 1\), for group fairness and for the corresponding per-unit and per-arrival-cohort individual-fairness objectives.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(U,V\) be finite driver and request type sets, with allowed edges \(E\), horizon \(T\geq1\), initial driver masses \(a_u>0\), and known arrival masses \(b_{v,t}\geq0\), where \(\sum_v b_{v,t}=1\) and \(n_v=\sum_t b_{v,t}>0\). Stationary arrivals mean \(b_{v,t}=b_v\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau=|U|+|V|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. All arrival masses, edge probabilities \(p_e\in[0,1]\), and utilities \(w_e^O,w_e^U,w_e^V\geq0\) are binary rationals; patience limits \(\Delta_u,\Delta_v\geq1\) are integers. State running times in \(m=|E|\), \(\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), \(T\), and the patience limits.
Each side is partitioned into nonempty groups. A causal fluid policy knows the input but uses only currently arrived requests and available drivers; it may reject mass or split it among adaptive probe sequences within its arrival period.
A probe couples equal driver and request masses \(q\) on an allowed edge: \(p_eq\) succeeds, earns the three edge utilities per matched unit, and removes both matched fractions; the remaining \((1-p_e)q\) increments both failure counters. Driver failure counts persist across periods; requests depart at the end of their arrival period. Either unit departs upon success or upon reaching its patience limit. The same driver unit cannot be probed twice by the same request unit; different driver units of one type may occur in a sequence.
Initially identical driver units receive symmetric treatment. Let \(x_{uv,t}\) be the total successful mass on edge \((u,v)\) in period \(t\), and write \(D_u=\sum_{v,t}w^U_{uv}x_{uv,t}\), \(R_v=\sum_{u,t}w^V_{uv}x_{uv,t}\), and \(O=\sum_{u,v,t}w^O_{uv}x_{uv,t}\).
Group fairness values are \(F_U=\min_g\frac{\sum_{u\in U(g)}D_u}{\sum_{u\in U(g)}a_u}\) and \(F_V=\min_g\frac{\sum_{v\in V(g)}R_v}{\sum_{v\in V(g)}n_v}\). Individual fairness instead uses \(F_U=\min_u D_u/a_u\) and arrival-cohort fairness \(F_V=\min_t\sum_{u,v}w^V_{uv}x_{uv,t}\), averaging across request types within each period.
For each instance \(I\), let \(\operatorname{OPT}_j(I)\) be the separate supremum of objective \(j\in\{O,U,V\}\) over feasible fluid schedules obeying these same temporal and probing restrictions. Restrict to instances with all three benchmarks positive. A vector \(r\in[0,1]^3\) is achievable if one causal policy family guarantees \(F_j(I)\geq r_j\operatorname{OPT}_j(I)\) on every instance, with \(F_O=O\).

Which competitive-ratio vectors are achievable for this fluid model, separately for group fairness and individual fairness, with stationary and with time-dependent arrivals? Must every achievable vector satisfy \(r_O+r_U+r_V\leq1\), or can a policy guarantee a vector whose sum exceeds \(1\)?

For discrete stochastic online matching, simultaneous worst-case competitive guarantees for profit and both fairness objectives cannot have sum exceeding \(1\); this is a competitive trade-off bound. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.06021v3).
No result settling these guarantees for the precise fluid model above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted competitive-ratio vectors as uniform guarantees across instances, rather than objective ratios on a single instance, following the discrete competitive-ratio definition.
  • Normalized total arrival mass to one in each period, matching the discrete model's one arrival per round; offline masses need not sum to one.
  • Used nonnegative rational utilities, positive integer patience limits, nonempty group partitions, positive total arrival mass for each supported request type, and positive separate benchmarks to make the ratios well-defined.
  • Defined separate fluid optima over schedules respecting arrival times and the same probing and patience restrictions, rather than using the discrete benchmark LPs as definitions of fluid feasibility.
  • Made failure counters explicit on both sides, retained driver counters across periods, and required unmatched requests to depart within their arrival period.
  • Allowed repeated driver types in a request's probe sequence when they represent distinct driver units, while excluding repeated probes of the same driver-request pair.
  • Defined per-unit offline fairness through symmetric treatment of initially identical units and utility divided by initial type mass; defined online individual fairness over whole period cohorts, averaging across types, as in the discrete definition.
  • Kept stationary rational arrival masses without an integrality requirement on their horizon totals, since the continuous analogue uses divisible masses.

Partial characterization and an algorithm for interior guarantees

1. Proven results

I use the nonatomic fluid interpretation implicit in allowing arbitrary mass splitting: units retain identities, but there is no minimum positive mass that a policy must allocate.

For each of the four settings—group or individual fairness, with stationary or time-dependent arrivals—let $\mathcal A$ denote the achievable-ratio region, and define
$$ \mathcal S=\{r\in[0,1]^3:r_O+r_U+r_V\leq1\}, \qquad \mathcal S_{<}=\{r\in[0,1]^3:r_O+r_U+r_V<1\}. $$

The following conclusions are proved below:

  • $\mathcal S_{<}\subseteq\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal S$, and consequently $\overline{\mathcal A}=\mathcal S$.
  • No policy can guarantee a vector whose coordinates sum to more than $1$. This holds even for stationary arrivals with four supported types.
  • For arbitrary probabilities and patience limits, a finite rational linear program computes each separate benchmark supremum exactly.
  • Every rational vector in $\mathcal S_{<}$ has an explicitly computable finite causal policy. A running-time bound is given in Section 7.
  • The entire closed simplex is achievable when either side has patience one, or when all edge probabilities belong to $\{0,1\}$.

The unrestricted boundary remains open in this write-up. In particular, computing an exact supremum does not prove that a schedule attains it.

The principal advance beyond the notes is the general benchmark LP and its finite approximation construction.

2. A stationary instance forces the sum bound

Fix any horizon $T\geq1$. Take one driver type $u$ with initial mass
$$ a_u=\frac14, $$
and three request types $v_O,v_U,v_V$, with stationary masses
$$ b_{v_O}=\frac12, \qquad b_{v_U}=b_{v_V}=\frac14. $$
Allow all three edges. Set every success probability and every patience limit to one. Give the edges utility triples
$$ (w^O,w^U,w^V)_{uv_O}=(1,0,0), $$
$$ (w^O,w^U,w^V)_{uv_U}=(0,1,0), $$
$$ (w^O,w^U,w^V)_{uv_V}=(0,0,1). $$
For group fairness, use one group on each side. This is a valid binary-rational instance with support size $\tau=4$.

Write
$$ X_j=\sum_{t=1}^T x_{uv_j,t}, \qquad j\in\{O,U,V\}. $$
Each successful match consumes driver mass, so every feasible schedule satisfies
$$ X_O+X_U+X_V\leq\frac14. $$

Profit and driver fairness are
$$ O=X_O, \qquad F_U=4X_U. $$
Matching all driver mass to the corresponding request type in the first period proves
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_O=\frac14, \qquad \operatorname{OPT}_U=1. $$

Under request group fairness,
$$ F_V=\frac{X_V}{T}, \qquad \operatorname{OPT}_V=\frac{1}{4T}. $$
The optimum is attained by matching all driver mass to $v_V$ in the first period.

Under arrival-cohort fairness,
$$ F_V=\min_t x_{uv_V,t}. $$
The driver budget gives
$$ T\min_t x_{uv_V,t}\leq X_V\leq\frac14. $$
Matching mass $1/(4T)$ to $v_V$ in every period attains this bound: each period has $1/4$ such request mass, and total driver consumption is $1/4$. Thus here also
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_V=\frac{1}{4T}. $$
All three benchmarks are positive.

For group fairness, therefore,
$$ \frac{O}{\operatorname{OPT}_O} +\frac{F_U}{\operatorname{OPT}_U} +\frac{F_V}{\operatorname{OPT}_V} =4(X_O+X_U+X_V)\leq1. $$
For individual and cohort fairness,
$$ \frac{O}{\operatorname{OPT}_O} +\frac{F_U}{\operatorname{OPT}_U} +\frac{F_V}{\operatorname{OPT}_V} =4X_O+4X_U+4T\min_t x_{uv_V,t} \leq1. $$

Any family guaranteeing $r$ must satisfy its three guarantees on this instance. Adding them proves
$$ r_O+r_U+r_V\leq1. $$
Because the instance is stationary, this obstruction applies to all four settings.

3. A general state-balance LP

We next construct a polytope whose projection is the closure of the feasible successful-mass tensors.

3.1 Variables and balances

A driver state is $(u,k)$ with $0\leq k<\Delta_u$, and a current-period request state is $(v,\ell)$ with $0\leq\ell<\Delta_v$.

Let
$$ q_{u,k,v,\ell,t}\geq0 $$
be the proposed probe mass between those states in period $t$, for $(u,v)\in E$. Put $f_{uv}=1-p_{uv}$, and define
$$ Q^D_{u,k,t}=\sum_{v,\ell}q_{u,k,v,\ell,t}, \qquad H^D_{u,k,t}=\sum_{v,\ell}f_{uv}q_{u,k,v,\ell,t}, $$
$$ Q^R_{v,\ell,t}=\sum_{u,k}q_{u,k,v,\ell,t}, \qquad H^R_{v,\ell,t}=\sum_{u,k}f_{uv}q_{u,k,v,\ell,t}. $$

Let $s_{u,k,t}\geq0$ be driver mass at the beginning of period $t$, and let $h_{v,\ell,t}\geq0$ be request mass left immediately before the period ends. Impose
$$ s_{u,k,1}=a_u\mathbf 1_{\{k=0\}}, $$
$$ s_{u,k,t+1} =s_{u,k,t}+H^D_{u,k-1,t}-Q^D_{u,k,t}, $$
$$ h_{v,\ell,t} =b_{v,t}\mathbf 1_{\{\ell=0\}} +H^R_{v,\ell-1,t}-Q^R_{v,\ell,t}, $$
where the terms indexed by $-1$ are zero.

Failures at the last permitted counter state depart, so they generate no subsequent state. The proposed success tensor is
$$ x_{uv,t}=p_{uv}\sum_{k,\ell}q_{u,k,v,\ell,t}. $$

3.2 Which states are reachable?

We impose zero variables for states that cannot be reached causally.

These states can be computed by a finite closure procedure. In period $t$, begin with the driver states reachable at the end of period $t-1$, and the states $(v,0)$ with $b_{v,t}>0$. Whenever both inputs of a probe are reachable and $f_{uv}>0$, add its surviving output states, if their counters remain below their patience limits. Repeat until no state is added. Carry the resulting driver states to period $t+1$.

The initial reachable driver states are precisely $(u,0)$.

For clarity, reachability here has a particularly simple interpretation. Let $c_u$ be the earliest period in which some incident edge $(u,v)$ has
$$ b_{v,t}>0,\qquad p_{uv}<1, $$
or let $c_u=\infty$ if no such period exists. Then:

  • Driver state $(u,0)$ is always reachable.
  • Every higher driver state is reachable within period $t$ exactly when $c_u\leq t$, and at its beginning exactly when $c_u<t$.
  • If $b_{v,t}=0$, no request state of type $v$ is reachable in period $t$.
  • If $b_{v,t}>0$, higher request states are reachable exactly when $v$ has an incident edge with probability less than one.

To verify the positive assertions, use sufficiently small probes while retaining positive mass in every previously reached state. Higher driver states can be generated successively using fresh state-zero requests; higher request states can be generated using fresh state-zero drivers. The negative assertions follow because a higher state requires an earlier failure involving that type.

Set $q_{u,k,v,\ell,t}=0$ whenever either input state is unreachable within period $t$. Also set beginning driver states and end request states to zero outside their corresponding reachable sets.

Call the resulting state-balance polytope $\mathcal P$.

3.3 Necessity and compactness

Every feasible schedule supplies a point of $\mathcal P$: each probe removes its input mass from its current states, its failed fraction enters the next states, and its successful fraction departs. Requests have no carryover between periods. Voluntarily unused driver mass may be retained idle, without affecting successes.

The polytope is bounded. Indeed, request balances imply
$$ Q^R_{v,0,t}\leq b_{v,t}, $$
and, successively,
$$ Q^R_{v,\ell,t} \leq H^R_{v,\ell-1,t} \leq Q^R_{v,\ell-1,t} \leq b_{v,t}. $$
Writing $d_V=\max_v\Delta_v$, we obtain
$$ \sum_{u,k,v,\ell}q_{u,k,v,\ell,t} \leq\sum_v b_{v,t}\Delta_v \leq d_V. $$
Summing driver balances over counter states shows that total retained driver mass never increases. Hence every $s_{u,k,t}$ is at most $a_u$. The request remainders are also bounded by their arrival masses.

Thus $\mathcal P$ is a nonempty compact rational polytope. Its projection onto $x$ is compact.

These facts alone do not prove exact executability of a point of $\mathcal P$. We now prove approximate executability.

4. Two feasibility lemmas

4.1 Pairing can avoid all previous partners

Suppose two available nonatomic reservoirs, of equal mass $q>0$, are to be coupled. Each unit has previously probed only finitely many partners, because its patience is finite.

Identify each reservoir, up to null sets, with the circle $[0,q)$ equipped with Lebesgue measure. For a shift $c\in[0,q)$, pair coordinate $z$ in the first reservoir with coordinate $z+c$ modulo $q$ in the second.

For each fixed first-side unit, only finitely many shifts pair it with a previous partner. That set has measure zero. By Fubini’s theorem, for almost every shift, the mass of units paired with a previous partner is zero. Choose such a shift and discard any exceptional null set.

Consequently, any prescribed finite sequence of type-and-counter-state probe batches can be implemented without repeating a driver–request pair, provided sufficient masses are available at each batch. A measurable subset of coupled mass $p_{uv}q$ supplies the successful fraction, and the complement supplies the failed fraction.

Initially identical drivers can receive symmetric treatment: uniformly rotate the initial identity coordinates within each driver type before applying the construction. Every driver of that type then has the same treatment distribution. This relabeling preserves all mass totals and pair restrictions.

4.2 A finite seed schedule creates positive buffers

There exists a finite schedule $S^\circ$ such that, immediately before requests depart in each period, every state reachable within that period contains positive mass.

Construct it period by period. Whenever a probe can create an as-yet-unreached state, fire it on mass
$$ q=\frac12\min\{\text{its two input masses}\}. $$
Both input states retain positive mass, and every surviving output has positive mass. Repeating this operation realizes the reachability closure. At the end of the period, retain all drivers and let requests depart.

Each such probe creates at least one new state. Define
$$ D_U=\sum_u\Delta_u,\qquad D_V=\sum_v\Delta_v, \qquad K=T(D_U+D_V). $$
The seed uses at most $K$ probe batches.

A quantitative buffer bound will be useful. Let
$$ h_*=\min\bigl(\{a_u:u\in U\}\cup \{b_{v,t}:b_{v,t}>0\}\bigr), $$
and let $f_*$ be the minimum positive $1-p_e$, setting $f_*=1$ if none exists. Every positive seed buffer is at least
$$ \mu_*=h_*\left(\frac{f_*}{2}\right)^K. $$
This follows inductively: a seed probe retains at least half of each input mass, while a newly created state receives at least $f_*/2$ times the smaller input mass.

Because positive input rationals and positive $1-p_e$ are at least $2^{-L}$,
$$ \mu_*\geq2^{-(K+1)(L+1)}. $$

5. Every LP allocation has a finite causal approximation

Proposition. Let $(q,s,h)\in\mathcal P$, with success tensor $x$, and let $0<\varepsilon<1$. There is a finite causal schedule whose tensor is
$$ x^\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)x+\varepsilon x^\circ, $$
where $x^\circ$ is the tensor of the seed schedule.

Proof

Fix an integer
$$ N\geq\frac{2d_V}{\varepsilon\mu_*}. $$

We maintain the following driver-state masses at the beginning of each period:
$$ (1-\varepsilon)s_t+\varepsilon s^\circ_t. $$
This holds initially because both schedules have the same initial masses.

First carve out driver submasses $\varepsilon s^\circ_t$ and request submasses $\varepsilon b_t$. Execute the scaled seed schedule for that period. Keep all other mass idle during this step.

Afterward, the driver and request states together have mass vector
$$ \varepsilon z^\circ_{\mathrm{end}} +(1-\varepsilon)z_{\mathrm{start}}, $$
where $z_{\mathrm{start}}$ consists of the LP beginning driver states and fresh state-zero requests. The seed vector $z^\circ_{\mathrm{end}}$ has mass at least $\mu_*$ in every permitted probe-input state.

Next perform $N$ cycles. In every cycle, execute every proposed probe batch in any fixed order, on mass
$$ \frac{1-\varepsilon}{N}q_{u,k,v,\ell,t}. $$

Let $C_tq_t$ denote the net state change of all proposed probes in this period, including departures. After $j$ complete cycles, the state vector is
$$ \varepsilon z^\circ_{\mathrm{end}} +(1-\varepsilon) \left(z_{\mathrm{start}}+\frac jN C_tq_t\right). $$
The expression in parentheses is nonnegative: it is the convex combination
$$ \left(1-\frac jN\right)z_{\mathrm{start}} +\frac jN z_{\mathrm{end}} $$
of the LP beginning and ending state vectors.

Thus every permitted input state contains at least $\varepsilon\mu_*$ at the beginning of each cycle. During one cycle, the total mass consumed from any individual state is at most
$$ \frac{1-\varepsilon}{N}\sum_{u,k,v,\ell}q_{u,k,v,\ell,t} \leq\frac{d_V}{N} \leq\frac{\varepsilon\mu_*}{2}. $$
Production during the cycle can only help availability. Every prescribed batch is therefore executable. Apply the pairing lemma to avoid previous partners.

At the end of the $N$ cycles, driver-state masses are exactly
$$ (1-\varepsilon)s_{t+1}+\varepsilon s^\circ_{t+1}. $$
Carve these into the corresponding two portions for the next period. Requests depart.

The seed contributes $\varepsilon x^\circ$, and the cycles contribute $(1-\varepsilon)x$. This proves the asserted tensor.

Every batch uses currently available drivers and requests from the current period. Each unit obeys its actual counter history, and the pairing lemma prevents repeated pairs. Hence the schedule is causal and feasible. ∎

6. Exact benchmark suprema

Every fairness objective in the question is the minimum of finitely many linear functions of $x$, using the original instance’s denominators. Write these functions as $\ell_{j,h}$. Profit is linear.

For nonnegative weights $\alpha_i$,
$$ \min_h\ell_{j,h}\left(\sum_i\alpha_i x^{(i)}\right) \geq \sum_i\alpha_i\min_h\ell_{j,h}(x^{(i)}). $$
In particular, nonnegative utilities and the approximation proposition give
$$ F_j(x^\varepsilon)\geq(1-\varepsilon)F_j(x). $$

Let $M_j$ be the maximum of objective $j$ over the projected polytope $\mathcal P$. Necessity gives
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_j\leq M_j. $$
Apply the approximation proposition to an LP maximizer. For every $\varepsilon>0$,
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_j\geq(1-\varepsilon)M_j. $$
Letting $\varepsilon$ decrease to zero proves
$$ \boxed{\operatorname{OPT}_j=M_j.} $$

More generally, the proposition and necessity prove
$$ \boxed{ \overline{\{\text{feasible success tensors}\}} =\operatorname{proj}_x\mathcal P. } $$

To optimize fairness, introduce $z\geq0$ and require every relevant linear utility to be at least its denominator times $z$. For example, driver group fairness uses
$$ \sum_{u\in U(g),v,t}w^U_{uv}x_{uv,t} \geq z\sum_{u\in U(g)}a_u, $$
request group fairness uses
$$ \sum_{v\in V(g),u,t}w^V_{uv}x_{uv,t} \geq z\sum_{v\in V(g)}n_v, $$
individual driver fairness uses
$$ \sum_{v,t}w^U_{uv}x_{uv,t}\geq za_u, $$
and arrival-cohort fairness uses
$$ \sum_{u,v}w^V_{uv}x_{uv,t}\geq z \qquad\text{for every }t. $$
Maximize $z$ for the relevant benchmark.

The LP maxima exist by compactness and continuity. This proves attainment in the LP, not necessarily by a fluid schedule.

7. Algorithms and running times

Set
$$ P=1+T\left( \sum_{(u,v)\in E}\Delta_u\Delta_v+D_U+D_V \right). $$
Writing $d_U=\max_u\Delta_u$, we have
$$ P\leq1+T\bigl(md_Ud_V+\tau(d_U+d_V)\bigr). $$
The LP has $O(P)$ variables and constraints. Its rational coefficient encoding length is polynomial in $P$ and $L+\log T$: coefficients consist of input masses, probabilities, products of probabilities and utilities, and fairness-denominator sums.

I use the following standard rational LP theorem: a rational linear program can be solved exactly in time polynomial in its binary encoding length; when an optimum exists, a rational optimal solution of polynomial encoding length can be returned. This is the exact polynomial-time LP result established by [Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239).

The hypotheses hold here: the data are rational, the feasible region is nonempty, and the relevant objectives have finite attained LP maxima. Therefore all three benchmark values and corresponding LP allocations are computable in
$$ \operatorname{poly}(P,L+\log T) $$
bit operations.

For rational $\varepsilon$, choose
$$ N= \left\lceil \frac{2d_V\,2^{(K+1)(L+1)}}{\varepsilon} \right\rceil. $$
The construction uses at most $K+NP$ probe batches.

For completeness, the pairing step can also be implemented with finite rational data. Represent reservoirs as finite unions of identity intervals and use their ordered mass coordinates. Previous pairings are finite unions of graphs of translations. A new rotation can agree with an old pairing on positive mass only at one of finitely many rational shifts, obtained by comparing the interval pieces. Choose a rational shift outside that list.

Each probe introduces only a bounded number of new endpoints in the current state partitions: endpoints for the selected mass and its success fractions. With $B$ batches, these partitions have $O(B+\tau)$ intervals, and the recorded pairing graphs have polynomially many pieces. Candidate forbidden shifts can consequently be enumerated in polynomial time in $B$ and the rational encoding lengths. Choosing shifts as midpoints of rational gaps keeps those lengths polynomial in the number of batches and the original plan’s encoding length.

Thus a conservative bound for constructing and executing the finite approximation is
$$ \operatorname{poly}\bigl(P,N,L+\log T+\ell_\varepsilon\bigr), $$
where $\ell_\varepsilon$ is the encoding length of $\varepsilon$.

This is an explicit, potentially exponential bound. The benchmark LP is polynomial in the numerical patience limits; no claim is made that it is polynomial in their binary encoding lengths alone.

8. Every strict-interior vector is achievable

8.1 Mixing schedules

Let $S_i$ be feasible schedules for the same instance, and let $\lambda_i\geq0$ satisfy $\sum_i\lambda_i\leq1$.

Split every initial driver-type mass and every arriving request-type mass into those proportions. Execute a scaled copy of $S_i$ within component $i$ and leave the remainder unused. Scaling preserves successes, failures, counter histories, and temporal restrictions. Components use disjoint units, so pair restrictions are preserved. Symmetric initial component assignment preserves the required driver symmetry.

The combined tensor is
$$ x=\sum_i\lambda_i x^{(i)}. $$
Using the original denominators, linearity of profit and the minimum-of-linear-functions inequality give
$$ F_j(x)\geq\sum_i\lambda_i F_j(x^{(i)}). $$

8.2 Applying the approximation algorithm

Fix a rational vector $r$ with
$$ s=r_O+r_U+r_V<1. $$
Set
$$ \varepsilon=\frac{1-s}{2}, \qquad \lambda_j=\frac{r_j}{1-\varepsilon}. $$
Then
$$ \sum_j\lambda_j=\frac{2s}{1+s}<1. $$

For each objective $j$, compute its LP maximizer and use Sections 5–7 to construct a feasible schedule $S_j$ satisfying
$$ F_j(S_j)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\operatorname{OPT}_j. $$
Mix the three schedules with weights $\lambda_j$. Nonnegativity allows us to retain just the contribution from $S_j$, obtaining
$$ F_j\geq\lambda_jF_j(S_j) \geq r_j\operatorname{OPT}_j. $$

This is one causal policy family for the prescribed vector. It plans from the known input and executes only on current resources.

If $\ell_r$ is the ratio vector’s encoding length, a running-time bound is
$$ \operatorname{poly}\left( P,\ L+\log T+\ell_r,\ \left\lceil \frac{2d_V\,2^{(K+1)(L+1)}}{(1-s)/2} \right\rceil \right). $$

Every real strict-interior vector is also achievable: choose a rational vector coordinatewise above it whose sum remains below one, and apply that rational vector’s policy.

Together with Section 2,
$$ \mathcal S_{<}\subseteq\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal S, \qquad \overline{\mathcal A}=\mathcal S $$
in all four settings.

9. Classes where the boundary is fully achievable

Whenever all three separate benchmarks are attained by feasible schedules, mixing their optimizers with weights $r_O,r_U,r_V$ proves every vector in $\mathcal S$ achievable. The following classes have that property.

9.1 Deterministic edge probabilities

Suppose $p_e\in\{0,1\}$ for every edge.

A probability-zero probe never earns utility or removes a successful fraction. Omitting it leaves both units with no larger failure counts. Replaying all other probes on the same units therefore remains feasible: patience can only improve, and removing probes cannot introduce a repeated pair.

Thus probability-zero edges can be ignored. All remaining probes succeed immediately, and feasible success tensors are exactly those satisfying
$$ x_{uv,t}\geq0, \qquad \sum_{v,t}x_{uv,t}\leq a_u, \qquad \sum_u x_{uv,t}\leq b_{v,t}, $$
on probability-one edges.

Sufficiency follows by executing these allocations period by period. The total driver inequality leaves enough mass for each current allocation, and the request inequality leaves enough current request mass. Every allocated unit departs upon success.

This compact rational polytope has attained benchmark maxima. The three optimizations and their mixed schedule take
$$ \operatorname{poly}(T(m+\tau),L+\log T+\ell_r) $$
time for rational $r$. Patience limits do not affect this bound.

9.2 Every request has patience one

Assume $\Delta_v=1$ for every request type. Use variables $q_{uv,k,t}$ and driver states $s_{u,k,t}$. The exact constraints are
$$ s_{u,k,t+1} =s_{u,k,t} +\sum_v(1-p_{uv})q_{uv,k-1,t} -\sum_vq_{uv,k,t}, $$
with the initial states as before, together with
$$ \sum_{u,k}q_{uv,k,t}\leq b_{v,t}. $$
Successes are
$$ x_{uv,t}=p_{uv}\sum_kq_{uv,k,t}. $$

To execute any feasible point, reserve disjoint request submasses for all prescribed probes in a period. Process driver states in increasing $k$. Upon reaching state $k$, available mass is its beginning mass plus the failed mass just generated at $k-1$. The nonnegative end-state balance guarantees enough mass for all its prescribed probes.

Each request is probed once, so pair repetition is impossible. This proves exact executability and benchmark attainment.

The resulting running time is
$$ \operatorname{poly}(T(m+\tau)d_U,L+\log T+\ell_r). $$

9.3 Every driver has patience one

Assume $\Delta_u=1$ for every driver type. Every probed driver departs, whether the probe succeeds or fails.

Let $q_{uv,\ell,t}$ denote probes from request state $\ell$, and impose
$$ \sum_{v,\ell,t}q_{uv,\ell,t}\leq a_u, $$
