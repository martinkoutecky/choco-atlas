The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given schools \(B\), supported student types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), preference lists and consent status, school priority orders over types, and rational capacity shares \(\kappa_b\), compute the terminal allocation of mass EADAM. Run deferred acceptance in rounds, splitting mass proportionally among history cohorts when a priority cutoff falls within a type; track a cohort’s hold and rejection history, remove the latest-round interrupting pairs involving consenting mass, and rerun until none remain. Is this output computable in time polynomial in the number of schools, \(\tau=|T|\), the number of acceptable type-school pairs, and input bit length \(L\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) schools \(B\), a set \(E\subseteq T\times B\) of mutually acceptable pairs, and binary-rational capacity shares \(0<\kappa_b\leq1\), with no requirement that capacities sum to one. Each type has strict preferences over its acceptable schools, all preferred to being unmatched, and a Boolean consent status; each school has a strict priority order over its acceptable types.
Mass is divisible. A cohort consists of mass with the same original type, remaining preference list, and proposal, hold, and rejection history; every split preserves the parent's history and original school priorities.
In each deferred-acceptance run, start all mass unmatched. In synchronous rounds, each unmatched cohort with an untried acceptable school proposes its whole mass to its highest remaining untried school; schools reconsider previously held mass together with new proposals and retain mass up to capacity in type-priority order.
If a cutoff type has total considered mass \(w>0\) and remaining capacity \(r\), each considered cohort of that type contributes the fraction \(r/w\) of its mass to the retained part; reject the remainder. Retained and rejected parts become separate cohorts. Stop the run when no unmatched mass has an untried school.
A positive-mass fragment rejected by school \(b\) in round \(k'\) is interrupting at \(b\) if it was held there in some round \(k<k'\) and \(b\) rejected positive mass during some round \(j\) with \(k\leq j<k'\).
After a run, select the largest rejection-round index having consenting interrupting mass, and simultaneously delete the corresponding school from the remaining list of every such fragment at that index. Deletions affect only those fragments and persist in their descendants; rerun from scratch, resetting run histories. Stop when no consenting interrupting mass remains.
The output is the exact aggregate allocation \(x_{tb}\), together with each type's unmatched mass, at this terminal run; cohort histories are not part of the output. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is computing this terminal mass-EADAM allocation possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,|E|,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For explicitly listed students with strict preferences, strict school priorities, integer quotas, and any consenting subset, discrete EADAM is computable in \(O(|E_{\mathrm{disc}}|)\) time, where \(E_{\mathrm{disc}}\) is the set of acceptable student-school pairs. [Faenza–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.08506).
No result settling the complexity of this precise proportional-cohort mass rule was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total student mass to one and interpreted school capacity shares as positive binary rationals at most one; total capacity need not equal total mass.
  • Specified mutually acceptable, possibly incomplete lists, strict student preferences, strict school priorities over types, and preference for every acceptable school over being unmatched.
  • Made consent uniform within each supported type and preserved original type priorities after cohort splitting.
  • Applied proportional cutoff splitting to all considered cohorts of the cutoff type, including previously held mass and new proposals.
  • Translated interruption into an earlier hold followed by rejection, with a positive-mass rejection at the same school between the hold round inclusively and the fragment's rejection round exclusively.
  • Made deletions fragment-specific and persistent across reruns, while resetting proposal, hold, and rejection histories for each fresh run.
  • Specified exact aggregate type-school masses and unmatched masses as the output, rather than an explicit list of terminal cohorts.

Result

The stated procedure need not terminate. Consequently, its terminal allocation is not defined on every valid input, so the proposed polynomial-time versus NP-hard dichotomy does not apply as stated.

The obstruction occurs with \(m=2\), \(\tau=3\), and dyadic input masses and capacities. The first DA run terminates, but the prescribed deletion produces a rerun with infinitely many positive rejection waves.

A valid instance

There are two schools, \(A\) and \(B\), with capacities \(\kappa_A=4/8\) and \(\kappa_B=3/8\). The types are:

| Type | Mass | Strict preferences | Consenting |
|---|---:|---|---|
| \(X\) | \(3/8\) | \(B\succ A\) | Yes |
| \(Z\) | \(2/8\) | \(B\succ A\) | No |
| \(Y\) | \(3/8\) | \(A\succ B\) | No |

Every type-school pair is acceptable. School priorities are \(Z\succ_A X\succ_A Y\) and \(Y\succ_B Z\succ_B X\).

The masses are positive and sum to one. Both capacities lie in the required interval.

Throughout the calculations below, one mass unit means \(1/8\). Thus the type masses are \(3,2,3\), and capacities are \(4,3\). An expression such as \(2X+Y\) denotes two units of type \(X\) and one unit of type \(Y\).

Aggregating cohorts in these calculations is only bookkeeping. At a cutoff type, the prescribed common retention fraction applies to every considered cohort, so summing their retained masses gives exactly the aggregate calculation.

First run and the required deletion

The first DA run proceeds as follows. Holdings are shown after each round.

| Round | New proposals | Held at \(A\) | Held at \(B\) | Rejected |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| \(1\) | \(3X+2Z\) to \(B\); \(3Y\) to \(A\) | \(3Y\) | \(2Z+X\) | \(2X\) at \(B\) |
| \(2\) | \(2X\) to \(A\) | \(2X+2Y\) | \(2Z+X\) | \(Y\) at \(A\) |
| \(3\) | \(Y\) to \(B\) | \(2X+2Y\) | \(2Z+Y\) | \(X\) at \(B\) |
| \(4\) | \(X\) to \(A\) | \(3X+Y\) | \(2Z+Y\) | \(Y\) at \(A\) |
| \(5\) | \(Y\) to \(B\) | \(3X+Y\) | \(Z+2Y\) | \(Z\) at \(B\) |
| \(6\) | \(Z\) to \(A\) | \(Z+3X\) | \(Z+2Y\) | \(Y\) at \(A\) |
| \(7\) | \(Y\) to \(B\) | \(Z+3X\) | \(3Y\) | \(Z\) at \(B\) |
| \(8\) | \(Z\) to \(A\) | \(2Z+2X\) | \(3Y\) | \(X\) at \(A\) |

Each row follows by admitting considered mass in the specified type-priority order. In round \(8\), for example, \(A\) considers two units of \(Z\) and three units of \(X\). After retaining \(Z\), its remaining capacity is two, so every considered \(X\)-cohort is retained with fraction \(2/3\). Exactly one unit of \(X\) is rejected.

This rejected unit is consenting interrupting mass:

  • Every fragment of it was held at \(A\) in round \(4\), when all three units of \(X\) were held there.
  • \(A\) rejected one unit of \(Y\) in round \(6\).
  • Its rejection from \(A\) occurs in round \(8\), and \(4\leq6<8\).

The rejected \(X\)-mass has exhausted its list, so the run terminates after round \(8\). Since this is its last rejection round and contains consenting interrupting mass, the selected index is \(8\).

The required deletion therefore removes \(A\) from exactly one unit of \(X\). The next run starts with:

| Original type and fragment class | Mass units | Remaining list |
|---|---:|---|
| \(X_f\) | \(2\) | \(B\succ A\) |
| \(X_s\) | \(1\) | \(B\) |
| \(Z\) | \(2\) | \(B\succ A\) |
| \(Y\) | \(3\) | \(A\succ B\) |

Here \(X_f\) and \(X_s\) are fragment classes of the same original type \(X\). They retain its consent status and school priorities. Run histories are reset as required.

The rerun has infinitely many rounds

In round \(1\) of the rerun, \(B\) retains its two units of \(Z\), leaving one unit of capacity for three considered units of \(X\). Proportional retention gives:

  • \(2/3\) unit of \(X_f\) held at \(B\);
  • \(1/3\) unit of \(X_s\) held at \(B\);
  • \(4/3\) units of \(X_f\) rejected and able to propose to \(A\);
  • \(2/3\) unit of \(X_s\) rejected with no remaining school.

Meanwhile, \(A\) holds all three units of \(Y\).

In round \(2\), the \(4/3\) units of \(X_f\) propose to \(A\). Since \(X\succ_A Y\), \(A\) holds \(4/3\) units of \(X\) and \(8/3\) units of \(Y\), rejecting \(1/3\) unit of \(Y\). That rejected mass proposes to \(B\) next.

We now prove an exact invariant for all subsequent rounds. Set \(a=2/3\) and \(d_n=(1/3)a^{n-1}\) for every integer \(n\geq1\).

Invariant. At the end of round \(2n\):

  • \(A\) holds \(2-a^n\) units of \(X_f\) and \(2+a^n\) units of \(Y\).
  • \(B\) holds two units of \(Z\), \(1-a^{n-1}\) units of \(Y\), and \(a^{n-1}\) units of \(X\).
  • The \(X_f:X_s\) ratio within \(B\)'s held \(X\)-mass is \(2:1\).
  • Exactly \(d_n\) units of unmatched \(Y\) will propose to \(B\) in the next round; no other unmatched mass has an untried school.

The round-\(2\) calculation establishes the invariant for \(n=1\).

Suppose it holds for some \(n\geq1\). In round \(2n+1\), the arriving \(Y\)-mass makes \(B\)'s total considered \(Y\)-mass equal to
\(1-a^{n-1}+d_n=1-a^n\).
This is strictly less than one. Therefore \(B\) retains all this \(Y\)-mass and all two units of \(Z\), leaving exactly \(a^n\) units of capacity for its previously held \(a^{n-1}\) units of \(X\).

Thus \(X\) remains the cutoff type, and every held \(X\)-cohort receives retention fraction \(a\). The total rejected \(X\)-mass is
\(a^{n-1}-a^n=d_n\).
Proportional rejection preserves the \(2:1\) composition of the surviving \(X\)-mass. Among the rejected mass, the \(X_f\) portion is
\((2/3)d_n=d_{n+1}\).
It will propose to \(A\); the rejected \(X_s\) portion has exhausted its list.

In round \(2n+2\), \(A\)'s considered \(X\)-mass is consequently
\(2-a^n+d_{n+1}=2-a^{n+1}\).
This is less than two and hence less than \(A\)'s capacity four. No \(Z\)-mass arrives, so \(A\) retains all its \(X\)-mass. Since \(A\) was already full, it rejects exactly \(d_{n+1}\) units of \(Y\), leaving \(2+a^{n+1}\) units of \(Y\) held.

That rejected \(Y\)-mass has \(B\) untried. No other new rejection occurs. This establishes every part of the invariant for \(n+1\).

By induction, \(d_n=(1/3)(2/3)^{n-1}>0\) for every finite \(n\). Hence the rerun always has positive unmatched mass with an untried school. Its stopping condition is never satisfied.

There is also no largest consenting interruption index in this infinite run. Every \(X\)-fragment rejected at \(B\) in round \(2n+1\) descends from mass held there in round \(1\). School \(B\) rejected positive mass in round \(1\), so each such rejection is consenting and interrupting. These indices are unbounded.

Consequence for the complexity question

This is an admissible constant-size instance on which the prescribed procedure has no terminal run. Although the rejection waves tend to zero, convergence is not the stated stopping condition. Moreover, treating the infinite run through its limiting allocation would still leave the required “largest rejection-round index” nonexistent.

The [Faenza–Zhang result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.08506) concerns explicitly listed students with strict individual priorities. It supplies no termination guarantee for this proportional-cohort rule.

A complexity classification requires an additional rule addressing infinite runs and unbounded interruption indices. For the precise procedure given, the terminal allocation is not defined on all valid inputs.

Step-by-step verification

1. Admissibility and proportional aggregation

The three masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one. Both capacity shares belong to the required interval. All six type-school pairs are acceptable, both preference orders and school priorities are strict, and the consent statuses are specified.

Using units of one eighth is a valid rescaling of all masses and capacities.

The aggregate calculations respect the cohort rule. Within any cutoff type, every considered cohort receives the same retention fraction. Consequently, summing the cohort-level retained amounts produces exactly the stated aggregate retained mass. The argument separately tracks the distinction between fragments with different remaining lists when that distinction becomes relevant.

2. First DA run

Every row of the first-run table is correct:

  • Round 1: School \(B\) retains two units of \(Z\) and one of \(X\), rejecting two units of \(X\). School \(A\) retains all three units of \(Y\).
  • Round 2: At \(A\), the two arriving units of \(X\) leave capacity for two units of \(Y\), so one unit of \(Y\) is rejected.
  • Round 3: At \(B\), the arriving unit of \(Y\), together with the two held units of \(Z\), fills capacity and displaces the held unit of \(X\).
  • Round 4: At \(A\), the arriving unit of \(X\) brings its total to three, leaving capacity for one unit of \(Y\). One unit of \(Y\) is rejected.
  • Round 5: At \(B\), two units of \(Y\) leave capacity for one of the two considered units of \(Z\). One unit of \(Z\) is rejected.
  • Round 6: At \(A\), the arriving unit of \(Z\) and three held units of \(X\) fill capacity, displacing the last held unit of \(Y\).
  • Round 7: At \(B\), three units of \(Y\) fill capacity, displacing the remaining held unit of \(Z\).
  • Round 8: At \(A\), two units of \(Z\) leave capacity for two of the three considered units of \(X\). The retention fraction for every considered \(X\)-cohort is therefore \(2/3\), and exactly one unit of \(X\) is rejected.

All rejected mass before round 8 has the next school indicated in the table untried. The \(X\)-mass rejected in round 8 has already tried both schools, so the run terminates then.

3. Interruption and required deletion

The entire three units of \(X\) were held at \(A\) after round 4 and remained held there through round 7. Therefore every positive fragment rejected from \(A\) in round 8 inherits a history of being held there in round 4.

School \(A\) rejected positive \(Y\)-mass in round 6. Thus the interruption definition is satisfied with
\[ k=4,\qquad j=6,\qquad k'=8, \]
and the required inequalities hold. Type \(X\) consents.

Because round 8 is the final rejection round and contains consenting interrupting mass, it is necessarily the largest qualifying index. Earlier interruptions do not affect this selection.

Exactly one unit of \(X\) has \(A\) deleted. The remaining two units retain the list \(B\succ A\). Fragmentation by earlier histories does not invalidate the two displayed classes: within each class, the remaining list, original type, and consent status agree, and histories reset before the rerun.

4. Rerun base case

At \(B\) in round 1, the three considered units of original type \(X\) share one unit of residual capacity. Applying retention fraction \(1/3\) gives:

  • held \(X_f\)-mass \(2/3\);
  • held \(X_s\)-mass \(1/3\);
  • rejected \(X_f\)-mass \(4/3\);
  • rejected \(X_s\)-mass \(2/3\).

Only the rejected \(X_f\)-mass can propose to \(A\).

In round 2, \(A\) retains \(4/3\) units of \(X_f\) and \(8/3\) units of \(Y\), rejecting \(1/3\) unit of \(Y\). These values verify all four invariant clauses for \(n=1\), including the assertion that no other unmatched mass has an untried school.

5. Inductive calculation

The constants and recurrence are consistent:
\[ a=\frac23,\qquad d_n=\frac13a^{n-1},\qquad d_{n+1}=a\,d_n. \]

Assuming the invariant at the end of round \(2n\), the considered \(Y\)-mass at \(B\) in the next round is
\[ 1-a^{n-1}+d_n=1-a^n. \]
This is strictly less than one for every finite \(n\geq1\). Hence \(B\) retains all considered \(Y\)-mass and both units of \(Z\), leaving \(a^n\) capacity for \(a^{n-1}\) held units of \(X\).

The common retention fraction is \(a\), and the rejected mass is
\[ a^{n-1}-a^n=d_n. \]
The held \(X_f:X_s\) ratio remains \(2:1\). The rejected \(X_f\)-portion is therefore
\[ \frac23d_n=d_{n+1}, \]
as claimed.

When this portion reaches \(A\), the considered \(X_f\)-mass becomes
\[ 2-a^n+d_{n+1}=2-a^{n+1}. \]
It is below capacity, so all of it is retained. Since \(A\) was full, it rejects exactly \(d_{n+1}\) units of \(Y\), leaving \(2+a^{n+1}\) units held.

The history and remaining-list assertions also hold: rejected \(X_f\)-mass has \(A\) untried; rejected \(X_s\)-mass has no untried school; and the \(Y\)-mass rejected at \(A\) has \(B\) untried. No additional proposal source is omitted. This establishes the complete invariant.

6. Nontermination

For every finite \(n\),
\[ d_n=\frac13\left(\frac23\right)^{n-1}>0. \]
At the end of each even round, positive unmatched \(Y\)-mass can propose to \(B\). At the end of each subsequent odd round, positive rejected \(X_f\)-mass can propose to \(A\).

Thus the stopping condition fails after every round of the rerun. Convergence of the wave sizes to zero does not satisfy the explicitly stated stopping condition.

7. Unbounded interruption indices

Every \(X\)-fragment rejected at \(B\) in round \(2n+1\) descends from mass held there in round 1. Its inherited history therefore contains that holding.

School \(B\) rejected positive mass in round 1. The definition explicitly permits the rejection witness to occur in the holding round, because it requires \(k\leq j<k'\). Taking \(k=j=1\) satisfies this condition for every \(n\geq1\).

All such \(X\)-mass consents, and the rejection indices are unbounded. Consequently, even interpreting the infinite run through a limiting allocation would not supply the prescribed largest interruption index.

Scope and feedback

The counterexample establishes failure of the precise procedure on a valid constant-size input. The discrete EADAM comparison is contextual; the proof does not rely on applying that theorem to proportional cohorts.

No repair or additional clarification is needed for the submitted nontermination argument. A rule governing infinite runs would be needed to formulate a total allocation problem, but that would change the task being classified.
