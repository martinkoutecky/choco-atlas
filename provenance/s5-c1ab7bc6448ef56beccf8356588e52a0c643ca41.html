The anchor (named result in the paper)

Corollary 2 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), a proposed committee \(W\) with \(|W|=k\), and finite approval-type support \(\mathcal T\subseteq 2^C\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_A\) summing to \(1\), decide whether, for every \(\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}\) and every coalition submass vector \(0\le\lambda_A\le\mu_A\) with \(\sum_A\lambda_A\ge\ell/k\), the condition \(\left|\bigcap_{\lambda_A>0}A\right|\ge\ell\) implies \(\left|W\cap\bigcup_{\lambda_A>0}A\right|\ge\ell\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set with \(m\ge2\), let \(1\le k\le m\) be an integer, and let \(W\subseteq C\) satisfy \(|W|=k\). Approval types are nonempty proper subsets of \(C\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal T\) of \(\tau\) distinct types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_A\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval sets; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A coalition is any real submass vector \((\lambda_A)_{A\in\mathcal T}\) with \(0\le\lambda_A\le\mu_A\); write \(S(\lambda)=\{A\in\mathcal T:\lambda_A>0\}\).
Continuous PJR verification asks whether, for every integer \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and every coalition with \(\sum_A\lambda_A\ge\ell/k\),
\(\left|\bigcap_{A\in S(\lambda)}A\right|\ge\ell\ \Longrightarrow\ \left|W\cap\bigcup_{A\in S(\lambda)}A\right|\ge\ell\).

Is continuous PJR verification in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is its complement \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, equivalently verification \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hard?

For finite lists of voters, verification of generalised PSC and its weak version is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete even for dichotomous preferences; at the Hare quota these properties coincide with PJR. [Aziz–Lee](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~haziz/prsolution.pdf)
Discrete PJR verification is independently established as \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete. [Aziz et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11478) No result settling exactly the submass problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified integer committee size with 1 <= k <= m, excluding the undefined zero-quota denominator.
  • Restricted approval types to nonempty proper subsets and m >= 2, carrying over the later full version's exclusion of voters completely indifferent among all candidates.
  • Specified coalition coordinates as real numbers, allowing arbitrary divisible submasses.
  • Defined L as total input bit length.
  • Expressed the hardness alternative through the complement because the anchor concerns coNP-complete verification.

Result

Continuous PJR verification is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete, and its complement is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, under polynomial-time many-one reductions. Thus a verification algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\) exists only if \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The key observation is that every violating coalition can be replaced by one taking the entire mass of each type in its support.

1. Eliminating fractional coalitions

For a nonempty subset \(S\subseteq\mathcal T\), define

\(\displaystyle M(S)=\sum_{A\in S}\mu_A,\qquad I(S)=\bigcap_{A\in S}A,\qquad U(S)=\bigcup_{A\in S}A.\)

Lemma. Continuous PJR fails if and only if there exist an integer \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and a nonempty subset \(S\subseteq\mathcal T\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle M(S)\ge\frac{\ell}{k},\qquad |I(S)|\ge\ell,\qquad |W\cap U(S)|<\ell.\)

Proof. Suppose a coalition \(\lambda\) violates PJR at level \(\ell\). Its support \(S=S(\lambda)\) is nonempty because its mass is at least \(\ell/k>0\). Since \(\lambda_A\le\mu_A\),

\(\displaystyle M(S)\ge\sum_{A\in\mathcal T}\lambda_A\ge\frac{\ell}{k}.\)

Its intersection and union are exactly \(I(S)\) and \(U(S)\), so the remaining inequalities hold.

Conversely, given such \(S\) and \(\ell\), set \(\lambda_A=\mu_A\) for \(A\in S\), and \(\lambda_A=0\) otherwise. Positivity of the listed masses implies \(S(\lambda)=S\). This coalition satisfies all three inequalities and therefore violates PJR. \(\square\)

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{coNP}\)

By the lemma, a certificate of failure consists of \(\ell\) and a subset \(S\) of the listed support. These require at most \(\tau+O(\log k)\) bits.

The intersection and union can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau\). The mass comparison also takes polynomial time in \(L\): if the masses have denominators \(q_A\), their product has bit length at most the sum of the denominators’ bit lengths. Using that product as a common denominator therefore permits exact summation and comparison with \(\ell/k\) using integers of polynomial bit length.

Thus the complement belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), and verification belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\).

3. Preparing discrete instances with valid types

We use the established theorem that discrete PJR verification is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete. Its discrete definition quantifies over voter subsets \(V\) with \(|V|\ge\ell n/k\), requiring the same intersection and committee-union inequalities as here. See Theorem 3 and the PJR definition in [Aziz et al., *On the Complexity of Extended and Proportional Justified Representation*](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11478/11337).

The discrete theorem allows arbitrary approval ballots. We first show explicitly that the required nonempty-proper restriction does not obstruct the reduction.

Take a discrete instance with voters \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), candidate set \(C_0\), ballots \(B_i\subseteq C_0\), and committee \(W\) of size \(k\ge1\). Choose \(w\in W\). Add a fresh candidate \(z\), obtaining \(C=C_0\cup\{z\}\), and define

\(\displaystyle A_i=B_i\quad\text{if }B_i\ne\varnothing,\qquad A_i=\{w\}\quad\text{if }B_i=\varnothing.\)

Keep \(W\) and \(k\) unchanged. Every \(A_i\) is nonempty and proper: no voter approves \(z\). Also \(|C|\ge2\) and \(k\le|C|\).

This transformation preserves PJR failure:

  • Any voter subset containing an originally empty ballot has empty intersection in the original instance, so it cannot witness failure.
  • In the transformed instance, any subset containing a modified ballot has intersection contained in \(\{w\}\). At levels \(\ell\ge2\), it is therefore not cohesive. At level \(\ell=1\), its union contains the committee member \(w\), so it cannot violate PJR.
  • For every subset containing no originally empty ballot, the ballots, intersection, committee-union size, and quota condition are unchanged.

Consequently, the original and transformed discrete instances have exactly the same answer.

4. Compressing voters into support masses

Group the transformed ballots by equality. Let \(\mathcal T\) be their distinct types, and let

\(\displaystyle t_A=|\{i:A_i=A\}|,\qquad \mu_A=\frac{t_A}{n}\quad(A\in\mathcal T).\)

Every mass is positive, and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{A\in\mathcal T}\mu_A=1.\)

This is a valid instance of the specified continuous problem.

We prove equivalence in both directions.

Discrete failure implies continuous failure. Let \(V\) witness discrete failure at level \(\ell\), and put \(S=\{A_i:i\in V\}\). Grouping identical ballots does not change their intersection or union. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle M(S)=\frac{\sum_{A\in S}t_A}{n}\ge\frac{|V|}{n}\ge\frac{\ell}{k}.\)

Thus \(S,\ell\) satisfy the lemma’s three conditions, giving continuous failure.

Continuous failure implies discrete failure. By the lemma, choose \(S,\ell\) satisfying those conditions. Take every voter whose ballot belongs to \(S\):

\(\displaystyle V=\{i:A_i\in S\}.\)

Every listed type occurs, so this voter subset has exactly the same intersection and union as \(S\). Its size satisfies

\(\displaystyle |V|=\sum_{A\in S}t_A=nM(S)\ge\frac{\ell n}{k}.\)

It therefore witnesses discrete PJR failure.

The construction takes polynomial time. There are at most \(n\) distinct types, only one candidate is added, and each numerator and denominator in \(t_A/n\) uses \(O(\log n)\) bits. Since the source contains an explicit list of voters, the output length is polynomial in the source length.

We have therefore given a polynomial-time reduction from discrete PJR failure to continuous PJR failure. The former is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete by the cited theorem, so the latter is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Together with Section 2, this proves the claimed completeness results.
