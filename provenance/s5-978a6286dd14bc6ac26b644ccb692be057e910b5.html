The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given bipartite type sets \(A,B\), rational masses \(\mu_a,\mu_b\) summing to \(1\), eligible pairs \(E\), and rational surpluses \(w_{ab}\), compute a polynomial-size description of all stable mass outcomes. Each outcome has contract masses \(x_{ab}\geq 0\) with incident mass at each type bounded by its \(\mu\); its allocation maximizes \(\sum_{(a,b)\in E}w_{ab}x_{ab}\), and its per-agent payoff rates \(p_a,p_b\geq 0\) form a dual optimum satisfying \(p_a+p_b\geq w_{ab}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: disjoint type sets \(A,B\), with \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) positive masses \(\mu_t\) encoded as binary rationals and satisfying \(\sum_{t\in A\cup B}\mu_t=1\). Support size counts listed types, not possible types.
Eligible contracts are \(E\subseteq A\times B\), with \(m=|E|\) and positive binary-rational surpluses \(w_{ab}\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length. Each individual can undertake at most one contract; mass is divisible.
A stable mass outcome is a real pair \((x,p)\). Contract masses \(x_{ab}\geq0\) satisfy \(\sum_{b:(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}\leq\mu_a\) and \(\sum_{a:(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}\leq\mu_b\), and maximize \(\sum_{(a,b)\in E}w_{ab}x_{ab}\).
Payoff rates \(p_t\geq0\) are constant within each type and minimize \(\sum_{t\in A\cup B}\mu_t p_t\) subject to \(p_a+p_b\geq w_{ab}\) for every \((a,b)\in E\). Unmatched mass is permitted.
A description of all outcomes means an exact rational system of linear equalities and inequalities, possibly with auxiliary real variables whose projection is precisely the outcome set.

Can a polynomial-size description of all stable mass outcomes be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computation NP-hard?

For finite agents with integer contract capacities, all stable network bargaining solutions admit a polynomial-time construction ([Bateni et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1004.4317v1)). No result settling this exact support-encoded continuous description problem was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted masses summing to one as normalization across the entire society, rather than separately on each side.
  • Restricted eligible-pair surpluses to positive rationals; zero-surplus pairs are ineligible under the discrete definition.
  • Made explicit the unit contract capacity implicit in the graded question's incident-mass bounds, with divisible mass and no additional pair capacity.
  • Specified the dual objective as the mass-weighted sum of payoff rates and interpreted rates as constant within each type.
  • Specified an exact description by rational linear constraints with optional auxiliary variables; outcomes themselves may have real coordinates.
  • Defined m as the number of eligible pairs and L as the total input bit length.

Result

A polynomial-size description of all stable mass outcomes can be constructed in polynomial time. It has \(m+\tau\) variables, \(2m+2\tau\) inequalities, and one equality. No auxiliary variables or optimization calls are needed.

The proof applies to the full support-encoded continuous problem as defined.

1. The explicit description

Write \(T=A\cup B\). Output the following system in the real variables \(x_{ab}\), for \((a,b)\in E\), and \(p_t\), for \(t\in T\):

\( \begin{aligned} x_{ab}&\geq 0 &&((a,b)\in E),\\ \sum_{b:(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}&\leq\mu_a &&(a\in A),\\ \sum_{a:(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}&\leq\mu_b &&(b\in B),\\ p_t&\geq0 &&(t\in T),\\ p_a+p_b&\geq w_{ab} &&((a,b)\in E),\\ \sum_{(a,b)\in E}w_{ab}x_{ab} &=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p_t.& \end{aligned} \)

All coefficients are rational. In particular, the last equality is linear: masses and surpluses are input constants.

2. Duality and existence of optima

Define the incidence matrix \(M\in\{0,1\}^{T\times E}\) by \(M_{t,e}=1\) exactly when type \(t\) is an endpoint of contract \(e\). Every column has two ones.

The contract optimization problem is

\( \max\{w^{\mathsf T}x: Mx\leq\mu,\ x\geq0\}. \)

Its linear-programming dual is

\( \min\{\mu^{\mathsf T}p: M^{\mathsf T}p\geq w,\ p\geq0\}. \)

Indeed, each primal capacity constraint supplies one nonnegative dual variable, and the dual constraint for edge \((a,b)\) is precisely \(p_a+p_b\geq w_{ab}\).

We use the standard strong-duality theorem: a feasible linear program with finite optimal value has an optimal dual solution with the same value. See [MIT’s notes on LP duality](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/18/Notes/n12-duality.html).

Its hypotheses hold here. The primal is feasible because \(x=0\) is feasible. Its feasible region is closed and bounded: every edge coordinate satisfies \(0\leq x_{ab}\leq\mu_a\). Thus its continuous objective attains a finite maximum. The dual is also explicitly feasible: setting every \(p_t\) to \(W=\max(\{0\}\cup\{w_{ab}:(a,b)\in E\})\) satisfies every covering constraint.

Consequently, both optimal values are attained and equal.

3. Proof that the system describes exactly all outcomes

For any primal-feasible \(y\) and dual-feasible \(q\), nonnegativity and feasibility give

\( \begin{aligned} \sum_{(a,b)\in E}w_{ab}y_{ab} &\leq\sum_{(a,b)\in E}(q_a+q_b)y_{ab}\\ &=\sum_{t\in T}q_t(My)_t\\ &\leq\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tq_t. \end{aligned} \)

This proves weak duality directly.

Every pair satisfying the output system is stable. Let \((x,p)\) satisfy it, and denote its common objective value by \(v\). For every primal-feasible \(y\), the preceding inequality with \(q=p\) gives

\( w^{\mathsf T}y\leq\mu^{\mathsf T}p=v=w^{\mathsf T}x. \)

Therefore \(x\) is primal-optimal. For every dual-feasible \(q\), the same inequality with \(y=x\) gives

\( \mu^{\mathsf T}q\geq w^{\mathsf T}x=v=\mu^{\mathsf T}p. \)

Therefore \(p\) is dual-optimal. These are exactly the two optimality requirements in the task.

Every stable outcome satisfies the output system. Its feasibility gives the first five families of constraints. Its primal and dual optimality, together with strong duality established above, gives the last equality.

Thus the system’s real solution set is precisely the entire outcome set, including any irrational outcomes.

4. Encoding size and construction time

There are \(m+\tau\) variables. The nonnegativity constraints contribute \(m+\tau\) inequalities, the capacity constraints contribute \(\tau\), and the covering constraints contribute \(m\). There is one additional equality.

The coefficient matrix has \(6m+2\tau\) nonzero entries. Every nonconstant numerical input coefficient appears only twice: each mass appears as a capacity bound and in the final equality; each surplus appears as a covering bound and in that equality. All other coefficients belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\).

With indexed variables and sparse encoding, the description therefore has bit length

\( O\!\left(L+(m+\tau)\log(m+\tau+2)\right). \)

A single traversal of the listed types and edges constructs all rows, including the final equality. Indexing and copying rational coefficients take polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). No denominator clearing, expansion into individuals, or computation of an optimal value is required.

This settles the requested computation as polynomial-time computable.
