The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over party-ballot types, per-capita candidate supplies \(s_{it}\), and a house rate \(H\), let \(q_i(\mu)\) be the share of ballots for party \(i\). Decide whether there are selected candidate masses \(x_{it}\) with \(x_{i\mathrm f}+x_{i\mathrm m}=Hq_i(\mu)\) for every party, \(\sum_i x_{i\mathrm f}=\sum_i x_{i\mathrm m}=H/2\), and \(0\le x_{it}\le s_{it}\) for every party and type.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge 1\) be the number of parties. Each ballot type names exactly one party. A society \(\mu\) is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) ballot types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, with total mass \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Define \(q_i(\mu)\) as the total mass of ballots naming party \(i\). Candidates have two types, \(t\in\{\mathrm f,\mathrm m\}\). The input also gives binary rational supplies \(s_{it}\ge 0\) and a binary rational house rate \(H>0\), measured per unit of society mass; supplies need not sum to \(1\).
Feasibility means existence of real selected masses \(x_{it}\) satisfying \(x_{i\mathrm f}+x_{i\mathrm m}=Hq_i(\mu)\) for every party, \(\sum_i x_{i\mathrm f}=\sum_i x_{i\mathrm m}=H/2\), and \(0\le x_{it}\le s_{it}\) for every party and candidate type. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Result

The feasibility problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, it admits an explicit necessary-and-sufficient criterion and a polynomial-time construction of a feasible rational selection.

1. Reduce each party’s constraints to an interval

Aggregate the supported ballot masses by their named party to compute \(q_i=q_i(\mu)\). Set
\[ a_i=Hq_i. \]
The premises imply \(a_i\ge 0\) and
\[ \sum_{i=1}^m a_i=H. \]

Write \(y_i=x_{i\mathrm f}\). The party equality forces
\[ x_{i\mathrm m}=a_i-y_i. \]
Consequently, the two supply constraints for party \(i\) are equivalent to
\[ 0\le y_i\le s_{i\mathrm f}, \qquad 0\le a_i-y_i\le s_{i\mathrm m}. \]
Define
\[ \ell_i=\max\{0,a_i-s_{i\mathrm m}\}, \qquad u_i=\min\{a_i,s_{i\mathrm f}\}. \]
Thus party \(i\)’s constraints are exactly
\[ \ell_i\le y_i\le u_i. \]

It remains to impose \(\sum_i y_i=H/2\). This also guarantees the required male total, because
\[ \sum_i x_{i\mathrm m} =\sum_i a_i-\sum_i y_i =H-H/2 =H/2. \]

2. Exact feasibility criterion

Proposition. The instance is feasible if and only if
\[ \ell_i\le u_i\quad\text{for every }i, \qquad \sum_i\ell_i\le H/2\le\sum_i u_i. \]

Necessity. Any feasible selection satisfies \(\ell_i\le y_i\le u_i\). Each interval must therefore be nonempty, and summing these inequalities gives the claimed bounds on \(H/2\).

Sufficiency. Suppose the stated conditions hold. Define the capacities and the remaining amount by
\[ c_i=u_i-\ell_i\ge 0, \qquad R_0=H/2-\sum_i\ell_i. \]
The aggregate inequalities imply
\[ 0\le R_0\le\sum_i c_i. \]

Process the parties in order, setting
\[ d_i=\min\{c_i,R_{i-1}\}, \qquad R_i=R_{i-1}-d_i. \]
Since the residuals are nonnegative,
\[ R_i=\max\{0,R_{i-1}-c_i\}. \]
Induction therefore gives
\[ R_i=\max\left\{0,R_0-\sum_{j=1}^i c_j\right\}. \]
In particular, \(R_m=0\). Hence \(0\le d_i\le c_i\) and
\[ \sum_i d_i=R_0-R_m=R_0. \]

Now choose
\[ y_i=\ell_i+d_i, \qquad x_{i\mathrm f}=y_i, \qquad x_{i\mathrm m}=a_i-y_i. \]
The inequalities \(0\le d_i\le c_i\) place \(y_i\) in its required interval, and
\[ \sum_i y_i=\sum_i\ell_i+R_0=H/2. \]
Section 1 then verifies every original constraint. This proves sufficiency.

Equivalently, the criterion can be written directly in terms of the input as
\[ Hq_i\le s_{i\mathrm f}+s_{i\mathrm m} \quad\text{for every }i, \]
together with
\[ \sum_i\min\{Hq_i,s_{i\mathrm f}\}\ge H/2, \qquad \sum_i\min\{Hq_i,s_{i\mathrm m}\}\ge H/2. \]
Indeed, nonnegativity of the supplies makes the first condition equivalent to \(\ell_i\le u_i\), while
\[ \ell_i=a_i-\min\{a_i,s_{i\mathrm m}\}, \qquad \sum_i a_i=H. \]

3. Polynomial bit complexity

To verify that exact rational arithmetic is polynomial, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input rationals: supported ballot masses, supplies, and \(H\). Every such denominator divides \(D\), and the binary length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\), since the sum of the denominator encoding lengths is at most \(L\).

The quantities
\[ Q_i=Dq_i, \qquad J=DH, \qquad S_{it}=Ds_{it} \]
are integers. Compute \(Q_i\) by aggregating the integer-scaled supported ballot masses. Because the masses sum to \(1\),
\[ \sum_i Q_i=D. \]

Use the common scale \(B=2D^2\). Under this scale, party demands, supplies, and the required female total become the integers
\[ A_i=Ba_i=2JQ_i, \qquad F_i=Bs_{i\mathrm f}=2DS_{i\mathrm f}, \qquad M_i=Bs_{i\mathrm m}=2DS_{i\mathrm m}, \qquad T=B(H/2)=JD. \]
Thus the algorithm checks
\[ \lambda_i=\max\{0,A_i-M_i\}, \qquad \upsilon_i=\min\{A_i,F_i\}, \]
and accepts precisely when
\[ \lambda_i\le\upsilon_i\quad\text{for every }i, \qquad \sum_i\lambda_i\le T\le\sum_i\upsilon_i. \]

Computing \(D\), scaling the inputs, aggregating the support, and checking these inequalities require \(O(m+\tau)\) arithmetic operations. All intermediate integers have \(O(L+\log(m+\tau))\) bits: input numerators and \(D\) have \(O(L)\) bits, each displayed product has only a constant number of such factors, and summation adds at most \(O(\log(m+\tau))\) bits. Ordinary integer arithmetic therefore implements the algorithm in polynomial time.

The construction in Section 2 can also be performed on these scaled integers. Dividing its selected masses by \(B\) yields a feasible rational witness of polynomial encoding length.

4. Conclusion

The distributional formulation is polynomial-time solvable by the interval criterion above. This proof is independent of the supplied result for discrete candidates; no divisor-method assumption or parity argument is needed.
