Proposition 5 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given three productivity types with rational masses \(\mu_h,\mu_m,\mu_l\) summing to \(1\), their productivity levels \(\lambda_h>\lambda_m>\lambda_l\), and a minimum productive organization mass \(\alpha\), decide whether a measurable partition into organizations exists in which every productive organization has mass at least \(\alpha\), its mass median selects meritocracy or egalitarianism under the paper’s voting rule, and no positive-mass group can form a blocking organization; construct such a partition when one exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless probability space partitioned into three measurable productivity types, with positive masses \(\mu_h+\mu_m+\mu_l=1\) and productivities \(\lambda_h>\lambda_m>\lambda_l\geq0\). All input numbers, including the threshold \(0<\alpha\leq1\), are binary rationals.
The society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass and their masses. Here the number of productivity levels is \(m=3\), and the support size is \(\tau=3\); support size means listed positive-mass types, not possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\), and any dependence on \(\alpha\).
An organizational structure is a finite or countable partition into positive-mass measurable organizations, covering society up to a null set; types may be split arbitrarily among organizations.
For any positive-mass coalition \(G\), write \(s_G=\mu(G)\) and \(a_G=s_G^{-1}\int_G\lambda\,d\mu\). It is productive exactly when \(s_G\geq\alpha\), producing \(s_Ga_G\); otherwise every member receives zero.
Define its upper mass median by \(d_G=\max\{\lambda_j:\mu(G\cap\{\lambda\geq\lambda_j\})\geq s_G/2\}\). A productive coalition chooses meritocracy when \(d_G\geq a_G\), paying each type \(j\) the amount \(\lambda_j\); otherwise it chooses egalitarianism, paying everyone \(a_G\).
Individuals compare organizations lexicographically by their own payment and then the organization's mean productivity \(a_G\), including for unproductive organizations.
A blocking coalition is any positive-mass measurable group whose members almost everywhere strictly prefer its resulting organization to their assigned organization. Such a group may draw arbitrary portions from several organizations and is subject to the same production and voting rules, even when unproductive. A partition is core stable if no blocking coalition exists.

Is deciding whether a core-stable partition exists solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? If it is in polynomial time, can a stable partition also be constructed in polynomial time whenever one exists?

For finite three-type societies, a necessary-and-sufficient existence characterization and constructions of stable partitions are known, without a stated complexity classification. The authors also report continuum robustness, but give no computational classification for the problem above; no such classification was located in the search. [Barberà, Beviá and Ponsatí](https://bw.bse.eu/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/737-file.pdf).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified an atomless probability space so each productivity type can be divided into arbitrary measurable portions.
  • Required all three masses to be positive, retaining the nondegenerate three-type setting, and specified binary-rational nonnegative productivities and a binary-rational threshold between zero and one.
  • Defined organizations as positive-mass measurable cells of a finite or countable partition, ignoring null sets, to avoid undefined mean productivity and median for zero-mass cells.
  • Extended the higher-productivity median convention to the upper mass median and retained meritocracy when median productivity equals mean productivity.
  • Restored individual payments and the lexicographic preference for higher mean productivity at equal payment, including in unproductive organizations.
  • Allowed blocking coalitions to split types and draw from multiple organizations, required strict improvement almost everywhere, and retained unproductive blocking moves.

1. Answer and representation of a constructed partition

For \(m=\tau=3\), existence is decidable in polynomial time, and a stable partition can be constructed in polynomial time whenever one exists. The construction outputs a finite table of exact real algebraic type masses.

A mass table is the appropriate representation because the input specifies only type masses. If its entries are \(x_{ij}\), row \(i\) describes an organization containing mass \(x_{ij}\) of type \(j\). On any atomless society with the given support, this table can be realized by measurable subsets.

The central result is that, whenever a stable partition exists, one exists with at most

\[ K=(m+3)(2^\tau-1) \]

organizations. Thus \(K=42\) here. Crucially, this bound is independent of \(\alpha\).

The proof below establishes this compression result and then gives an exact algorithm.

2. Preliminary facts

Let \(J\) be the set of listed types, so \(|J|=\tau\). For an organization \(G\), define its type support by

\[ S(G)=\{j\in J:\mu(G\cap T_j)>0\}, \]

where \(T_j\) is the measurable set of type \(j\).

Write an individual’s outcome as \((p,a)\), consisting of payment and organizational mean, ordered lexicographically.

We use the standard divisibility theorem for atomless finite measures: if \(A\) is measurable and \(0\leq t\leq\mu(A)\), there is a measurable subset of \(A\) with measure \(t\). Restrictions of the society’s measure to measurable type and organization intersections remain finite and atomless. Consequently, all portions selected below exist.

Voting identity. If \(G\) has mean \(a\), then

\[ d_G\geq a \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mu(G\cap\{\lambda\geq a\})\geq s_G/2. \]

Indeed, if \(d_G\geq a\), the upper tail at \(a\) contains the upper tail at \(d_G\). Conversely, if the upper tail at \(a\) has at least half the mass, let \(\lambda_j\) be the smallest listed productivity at least \(a\). Its upper tail is precisely the upper tail at \(a\), so \(d_G\geq\lambda_j\geq a\).

Thus productive meritocracy is characterized by the weak inequality in this identity, and productive egalitarianism by its strict complement.

Monotonicity of stability. Suppose a stable partition is replaced by a partition under which almost every individual’s outcome weakly improves. The replacement remains stable.

To prove this, a coalition blocking the replacement would give its members outcomes strictly above their replacement outcomes, hence strictly above their original outcomes by transitivity of the lexicographic order. It would therefore block the original partition. Coalition outcomes depend only on their membership, so they are identical in this comparison.

3. The exchange lemma

Lemma. Suppose two organizations \(G,H\) have the same type support and belong to the same category: unproductive, productive egalitarian, or productive meritocratic. Suppose their means satisfy \(A=a_G>B=a_H\).

Then the partition is blocked in either of the following circumstances:

  • Both organizations are unproductive.
  • Both are productive egalitarian.
  • Both are productive meritocratic and \(A<d_G\).

Proof. The common support must contain at least two productivity levels: otherwise both means would be identical.

Let \(\lambda_+\) and \(\lambda_-\) be its highest and lowest productivities, and put \(\Delta=\lambda_+-\lambda_->0\). Remove mass \(\varepsilon\) of type \(\lambda_-\) from \(G\), and replace it with mass \(\varepsilon\) of type \(\lambda_+\) from \(H\). Atomlessness permits this for every sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\).

The resulting coalition \(G_\varepsilon\) has unchanged mass and mean

\[ A_\varepsilon=A+\frac{\varepsilon\Delta}{s_G}>A>B. \]

We check each category.

Unproductive organizations. Since mass is unchanged, \(G_\varepsilon\) remains unproductive. Every participant still receives zero and obtains a strictly higher mean than in their assigned organization. Hence every participant strictly improves.

Productive egalitarian organizations. By the voting identity,

\[ \delta=\frac{s_G}{2}-\mu(G\cap\{\lambda\geq A\})>0. \]

Choose \(\varepsilon<\delta\), in addition to the availability constraints. Replacing mass \(\varepsilon\) can increase any upper-tail mass by at most \(\varepsilon\), and \(A_\varepsilon>A\). Therefore

\[ \mu(G_\varepsilon\cap\{\lambda\geq A_\varepsilon\}) \leq \mu(G\cap\{\lambda\geq A\})+\varepsilon < s_G/2. \]

Thus \(G_\varepsilon\) remains egalitarian. Its payment \(A_\varepsilon\) exceeds both original payments \(A\) and \(B\), so every participant strictly improves.

Productive meritocratic organizations with \(A<d_G\). Choose \(\varepsilon\) small enough that \(A_\varepsilon<d_G\). Replacing a lowest-productivity member by a highest-productivity member cannot decrease the upper-tail mass at \(d_G\). Consequently,

\[ \mu(G_\varepsilon\cap\{\lambda\geq d_G\})\geq s_G/2, \]

and the new median is at least \(d_G>A_\varepsilon\). Thus \(G_\varepsilon\) remains meritocratic. Every participant keeps their productivity payment and obtains a strictly higher mean than before.

In all three cases, \(G_\varepsilon\) blocks. This proves the lemma.

4. Compression to at most \(42\) organizations

Theorem. If a core-stable finite or countable partition exists, there is a core-stable finite partition with at most \(K=(m+3)(2^\tau-1)\) organizations.

Proof. Fix a stable partition and a nonempty type support \(S\).

The exchange lemma implies that all unproductive organizations with support \(S\) have the same mean. It likewise implies that all productive egalitarian organizations with support \(S\) have the same mean.

Consider productive meritocratic organizations with support \(S\). There are at most \(m\) distinct means equal to a productivity level. There is at most one additional distinct mean. Indeed, if two additional means satisfy \(A>B\), the organization with mean \(A\) is meritocratic and \(A\) is not a productivity level. Its median is a productivity level, so

\[ A\leq d_G \quad\text{and}\quad A\neq d_G, \]

giving \(A<d_G\). The exchange lemma then contradicts stability.

Therefore, for each support, there are at most \(m+3\) classes specified by category and mean. There are at most \(2^\tau-1\) nonempty supports. Hence there are at most \(K\) such classes.

Merge all organizations within each class. Countable unions are measurable. If the common mean is \(A\), their union also has mean \(A\), by countable additivity of mass and productivity integrals.

For a productive meritocratic class, each organization has at least half its mass at productivities at least \(A\). Summing these inequalities proves the same property for the union. The union remains productive and meritocratic, preserving every member’s outcome.

For a productive egalitarian class, each organization has strictly less than half its mass at productivities at least \(A\). The sum remains strictly less than half: the sum of the positive deficits is positive. The union remains productive and egalitarian, again preserving outcomes.

For an unproductive class, the union retains mean \(A\). If its total mass is below \(\alpha\), outcomes are unchanged. If its mass reaches \(\alpha\), it becomes productive. Every new payment is nonnegative, because all productivities are nonnegative. Thus every member weakly improves from their original outcome \((0,A)\).

The merged partition weakly improves everyone’s outcome. By monotonicity of stability, it is stable. It has at most \(K\) organizations.

For \(m=\tau=3\), this gives \(K=42\).

5. Exact algebraic encoding of stability

Fix \(n\), with \(1\leq n\leq K\). We describe a formula whose solutions are precisely stable partitions with \(n\) organizations.

For organization \(i\), introduce masses \(x_{ij}\), mean \(a_i\), and type payments \(p_{ij}\). Set

\[ s_i=\sum_{j\in J}x_{ij}. \]

Impose

\[ x_{ij}\geq0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{ij}=\mu_j,\qquad s_i>0,\qquad s_i a_i=\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_jx_{ij}. \]

Atomless divisibility shows that every mass table satisfying these constraints is realizable as a measurable partition.

For any nonnegative type-mass vector \(z\) with positive total mass and mean \(c\), define the polynomial predicate

\[ \mathsf M(z,c)= \bigvee_{j\in J} \left( c\leq\lambda_j \ \wedge\ 2\sum_{r:\lambda_r\geq\lambda_j}z_r \geq \sum_r z_r \right). \]

By the definition of the upper median, this says exactly that the median is at least \(c\).

For each organization impose the following disjunction:

\[ \begin{aligned} & \left(s_i<\alpha\ \wedge\ \bigwedge_j p_{ij}=0\right) \\ {}\vee{}& \left(s_i\geq\alpha\ \wedge\ \mathsf M(x_i,a_i) \ \wedge\ \bigwedge_j p_{ij}=\lambda_j\right) \\ {}\vee{}& \left(s_i\geq\alpha\ \wedge\ \neg\mathsf M(x_i,a_i) \ \wedge\ \bigwedge_j p_{ij}=a_i\right). \end{aligned} \]

Call the resulting partition predicate \(\mathsf{Part}_n(x,a,p)\).

To encode a potential blocker, introduce variables \(y_{ij}\), describing how much type-\(j\) mass it draws from organization \(i\), together with its mean \(b\) and payments \(q_j\). Impose

\[ 0\leq y_{ij}\leq x_{ij},\qquad t=\sum_{i,j}y_{ij}>0,\qquad tb=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_jy_{ij}. \]

Apply the same production and voting disjunction to the blocker’s type masses \(z_j=\sum_i y_{ij}\), total mass \(t\), mean \(b\), and payments \(q_j\).

Finally, impose strict preference on every source cell used with positive mass:

\[ \bigwedge_{i,j} \left[ y_{ij}=0 \ \vee\ q_j>p_{ij} \ \vee\ \left(q_j=p_{ij}\ \wedge\ b>a_i\right) \right]. \]

Let \(\mathsf{Block}_n(x,a,p;y,b,q)\) denote these constraints.

This encoding is exact. Every measurable blocker supplies such variables. Conversely, every satisfying assignment supplies a measurable blocker: independently select the prescribed mass from each disjoint organization–type cell using atomless divisibility. Within each cell, assigned outcomes are constant, so the preference constraints are precisely strict preference almost everywhere.

Consequently, a stable partition with \(n\) organizations exists exactly when the following real-algebraic sentence is true:

\[ \exists x,a,p\ \left[ \mathsf{Part}_n(x,a,p) \ \wedge\ \neg\exists y,b,q\ \mathsf{Block}_n(x,a,p;y,b,q) \right]. \]

By the compression theorem, the original question is equivalent to the truth of this sentence for at least one \(n\leq K\).

6. Running time and construction

We use the standard algorithmic result that cylindrical algebraic decomposition decides polynomial formulas over the reals and produces exact algebraic sample points. With fixed numbers of variables and polynomials and fixed degrees, its bit complexity is polynomial in coefficient bit length. Quantifier elimination complexity and coefficient-size bounds are given in [Basu’s survey, Theorems 2.4 and 2.18](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011_final.pdf); algebraic sampling is treated in [Basu–Pollack–Roy](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-662-05355-3).

Here the hypotheses are satisfied:

  • The number of variables is \(O(K(\tau+1))\).
  • The number of polynomial predicates and the Boolean formula length are \(O(K(m+\tau+1))\).
  • Polynomial degrees are at most \(2\).
  • After clearing rational denominators, coefficient bit lengths are \(O(L)\).

A deliberately coarse bound for decision and construction is

\[ T(m,\tau,L) \leq \left[(m+\tau+1)K(L+1)\right]^{\,2^{O(K(\tau+1))}}, \qquad K=(m+3)(2^\tau-1). \]

The factor for trying all \(n\leq K\) is absorbed in this bound.

For \(m=\tau=3\), all parameters other than \(L\) are constants. Therefore the running time is polynomial in \(L\). The threshold \(\alpha\) appears only as a rational coefficient; there is no dependence on its numerical reciprocal \(1/\alpha\).

For construction, order the decomposition variables so that partition variables precede blocker variables. Evaluating the quantified blocker condition identifies cells consisting of stable partitions. If such a cell exists, its algebraic sample point yields the required mass table. For fixed \(m=\tau=3\), that table and its exact algebraic encoding have polynomial bit length.

For a concrete realization, represent each type by a separate interval of length \(\mu_j\) and split it into consecutive intervals of lengths \(x_{1j},\ldots,x_{nj}\). Organization \(i\) is the union of its pieces across types. On an arbitrary atomless society, the same mass table is realized by the divisibility theorem.

Thus both requested tasks are polynomial-time solvable under the given support representation.
