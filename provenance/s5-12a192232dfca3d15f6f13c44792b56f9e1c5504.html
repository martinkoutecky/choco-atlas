Scope and status

The supplied task does not define an encoded computational problem. Consequently, neither a polynomial-time classification nor an NP-hardness claim is presently meaningful.

The intended mathematical question is the conditional transfer from learning a scoring function to approximate truthfulness. Below I prove that transfer, including its resistance to inaccurate learning under strategic reports. This establishes a statistical guarantee, without assigning a computational complexity.

The published anchor is Theorem 16 of [Schoenebeck and Yu, *Learning and Strongly Truthful Multi-Task Peer Prediction*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol185-itcs2021/LIPIcs.ITCS.2021.78/LIPIcs.ITCS.2021.78.pdf). Its general measurable-space conclusion is approximate truthfulness; its stated strong-truthfulness conclusion adds finite signal spaces and convexity hypotheses.

1. Definitions and assumptions

Let \(\mathcal X,\mathcal Y\) be measurable signal spaces, and let \(P\) be a probability law on their product. Write \(Q=P_X\otimes P_Y\). Assume \(P\ll Q\), and define \(r=dP/dQ\).

Let \(\Phi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\) be continuous and convex, with \(\Phi(1)=0\). Define its conjugate by

\(\Phi^*(k)=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{tk-\Phi(t)\}\).

Assume the divergence \(I_\Phi(P)=\int\Phi(r)\,dQ\) is finite.

A reporting strategy consists of measurable probability kernels \(A(x,da)\) and \(B(y,db)\). The agents randomize independently conditional on their signals, and use these same kernels independently on every task. Thus the joint reporting kernel is

\(T((x,y),d(a,b))=A(x,da)B(y,db)\).

Write \(R=PT\) for the joint report law. Because \(Q\) is a product law and \(T\) acts separately on the two coordinates,

\(QT=R_X\otimes R_Y=:Q_R\).

For a measurable score \(K\), define

\(V(R,K)=\int K\,dR-\int\Phi^*(K)\,dQ_R\).

Throughout, scores are assumed to make these integrals finite. Whenever payments are averaged over training data, assume the resulting payments are integrable as well.

2. Proven payment bound

Lemma. For every such reporting strategy and score,

\(V(R,K)\leq D_\Phi(R\Vert Q_R)\leq I_\Phi(P)\).

Proof. First, \(R\ll Q_R\): if \(Q_R(E)=0\), then \(T(z,E)=0\) for \(Q\)-almost every \(z\), hence also for \(P\)-almost every \(z\). Consequently \(R(E)=0\).

Let \(r_R=dR/dQ_R\). The definition of the conjugate gives the pointwise Fenchel inequality

\(r_R K-\Phi^*(K)\leq\Phi(r_R)\).

Integrating against \(Q_R\) proves the first inequality.

For the second inequality, introduce the probability measure

\(\Lambda(dz,dw)=Q(dz)T(z,dw)\).

Under this measure,

\(r_R(W)=\mathbb E_\Lambda[r(Z)\mid\sigma(W)]\), almost surely.

Indeed, integration over every event determined by \(W\) gives the same value on both sides: weighting \(\Lambda\) by \(r(Z)\) changes its \(W\)-marginal from \(Q_R\) to \(R\).

Conditional Jensen’s inequality therefore gives

\(\Phi(r_R(W))\leq\mathbb E_\Lambda[\Phi(r(Z))\mid\sigma(W)]\).

Integrating yields

\(D_\Phi(R\Vert Q_R)\leq\int\Phi(r)\,dQ=I_\Phi(P)\).

This proves the lemma. In particular, the bound requires no learning accuracy under \(R\). \(\square\)

3. Proven reduction using two additional tasks

Suppose a learner uses \(m=S(\delta,\epsilon)\) independent samples from \(P\), where \(0<\delta<1\) and \(\epsilon>0\), and outputs a score satisfying

\(\Pr[V(P,K)\geq I_\Phi(P)-\epsilon]\geq 1-\delta\).

Use \(m\) tasks for training and two independent additional tasks for scoring. Give both agents the payment

\(M=K(\widehat X_1,\widehat Y_1)-\Phi^*(K(\widehat X_1,\widehat Y_2))\).

Let \(D\) denote the training reports together with the learner’s random seed. Conditional on \(D\), the score \(K\) is fixed. Independence across tasks implies that:

  • \((\widehat X_1,\widehat Y_1)\) has law \(R\);
  • \((\widehat X_1,\widehat Y_2)\) has law \(Q_R\).

Hence

\(\mathbb E[M\mid D]=V(R,K)\leq D_\Phi(R\Vert Q_R)\)

for every strategy, almost surely.

Under truthful reporting, the learner receives independent samples from \(P\). Its assumed accuracy therefore gives

\(\Pr[\mathbb E[M\mid D]\geq I_\Phi(P)-\epsilon]\geq 1-\delta\).

Define the benchmark payment mapping

\(F(A,B;P)=D_\Phi(PT\Vert (PT)_X\otimes(PT)_Y)\).

We have proved two distinct statements:

  • Every strategy’s conditional expected payment is bounded above by its benchmark, regardless of the learner’s accuracy.
  • Truthful reporting achieves its benchmark within \(\epsilon\), with probability at least \(1-\delta\) over truthful training data.

Moreover, Section 2 shows that truth maximizes \(F\). Thus \(2+S(\delta,\epsilon)\) tasks suffice for this precise approximate-truthfulness guarantee on the stated measurable spaces.

Limitation. The high-probability lower bound does not alone imply an unconditional expected-payment lower bound. Payments on the failure event could be arbitrarily negative. Such a conclusion needs additional control of that event.

4. Proven strong benchmark in the finite case

Assume now that \(\mathcal X,\mathcal Y\) are finite, every marginal probability is positive, and \(\Phi\) is strictly convex.

Assume also stochastic relevance: distinct Alice signals induce distinct conditional distributions of Bob’s signal, and conversely.

I claim that \(F(A,B;P)<I_\Phi(P)\) whenever the strategy profile is not a pair of deterministic permutations.

For output signals \(a,b\) with \(Q_R(a,b)>0\),

\(r_R(a,b)=\sum_{x,y}w_{xy}^{ab}r(x,y)\),

where

\(w_{xy}^{ab}=\dfrac{P_X(x)P_Y(y)A(x,a)B(y,b)}{Q_R(a,b)}\).

These weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Strict convexity implies that equality in the divergence bound requires \(r(x,y)\) to be constant among all pairs with \(w_{xy}^{ab}>0\), for every such \(a,b\).

Suppose two distinct inputs \(x,x'\) can produce the same output \(a\). For every \(y\), choose an output \(b\) with \(B(y,b)>0\). Both corresponding weights are positive, so equality would imply

\(r(x,y)=r(x',y)\).

Since \(P(Y=y\mid X=x)=P_Y(y)r(x,y)\), this contradicts stochastic relevance.

Thus distinct rows of \(A\) have disjoint supports. There are \(|\mathcal X|\) nonempty supports inside an output set of size \(|\mathcal X|\); each support must therefore be a singleton. Hence \(A\) is a deterministic permutation. The same argument applies to \(B\).

Consequently, every non-permutation strategy strictly lowers \(F\). Permutations preserve the divergence by relabeling. The benchmark is therefore strongly truthful, and Section 3 transfers learner accuracy to approximation of this strongly truthful benchmark.

This does not assert a uniform positive payment gap over all non-permutation strategies.

5. What remains unspecified

The task still lacks the ingredients needed for a complexity classification:

  • Encodings or oracle interfaces for the signal spaces, distributions, score class, and convex function.
  • A specified computational output and approximation criterion.
  • A running-time model accounting for learning, score evaluation, numerical precision, and sampling.

The proved task bound counts observations. It does not bound the computation required to obtain or evaluate the learned score.
