The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given schools \(S\), finite complete student types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), true school rankings, immutable category maps \(a_s(t)\) and scores \(\sigma_s(t)\), per-capita capacities \(\kappa_s\), and rational non-increasing piecewise-linear adjustments \(\alpha_{s,a}\), does there exist an exact polynomial-time direct mechanism with the following properties? For every reported mass profile \(\widehat\mu_{t,R}\), it returns assignment masses \(x_{t,R,s}\) summing to \(\widehat\mu_{t,R}\), respects \(\sum_{t,R}x_{t,R,s}\le\kappa_s\), and is stable at reported rankings: no positive mass assigned to a less-preferred option can move to a preferred school by displacing positive incumbent mass with lower adjusted priority, and no acceptable applicant mass can take an unfilled seat. At school \(s\), type \(t\)’s priority is \(\sigma_s(t)+\alpha_{s,a_s(t)}(z_{s,a_s(t)})\), where \(z_{s,a}\) is the mass of category \(a\) assigned there. The mechanism must also prevent every positive-measure coalition from jointly changing reports so that almost every coalition member strictly improves under their true ranking.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) schools, with unmatched option \(\varnothing\). A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) complete student types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Type \(t\) has a strict true ranking \(P_t\) over \(S\cup\{\varnothing\}\), immutable school-specific categories \(a_s(t)\), and rational scores \(\sigma_s(t)\in[0,1]\).
Each school has rational capacity \(0<\kappa_s\le1\), rational acceptance threshold \(b_s\), and continuous non-increasing piecewise-linear adjustments \(\alpha_{s,a}:[0,1]\to\mathbb R\), specified by rational breakpoints and coefficients. Adjustments may have either sign.
A direct mechanism observes immutable attributes and reported rankings, but not true rankings. Reports partition each type into masses \(\widehat\mu_{t,R}\ge0\), with \(\sum_R\widehat\mu_{t,R}=\mu_t\). It returns nonnegative assignment masses satisfying \(\sum_{u\in S\cup\{\varnothing\}}x_{t,R,u}=\widehat\mu_{t,R}\) and \(\sum_{t,R}x_{t,R,s}\le\kappa_s\).
Write \(z_{s,a}=\sum_{t,R:a_s(t)=a}x_{t,R,s}\) and \(p_s(t)=\sigma_s(t)+\alpha_{s,a_s(t)}(z_{s,a_s(t)})\). Stability requires individual rationality: \(x_{t,R,s}>0\) implies \(s\succ_R\varnothing\) and \(p_s(t)\ge b_s\). If \(x_{t,R,u}>0\) and \(s\succ_R u\), then \(p_s(t)\ge b_s\) implies that \(s\) is full, and every incumbent group \((t',R')\) with \(x_{t',R',s}>0\) satisfies \(p_s(t)\le p_s(t')\). Comparisons use current category masses; priority ties do not block.
Every member of a positive-mass report group receives the lottery \((x_{t,R,u}/\widehat\mu_{t,R})_u\). Strict improvement means strict stochastic dominance under the member's true ranking. Weak group strategy-proofness excludes any measurable positive-mass coalition whose members jointly change rankings, with outsiders truthful, so that almost every coalition member strictly improves; immutable attributes cannot be changed.
The mechanism is defined for real mass partitions. On rational inputs it must return exact binary-rational masses in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,\rho,K,L\), where \(\rho\) is the reported support size, \(K\) the total number of adjustment pieces, and \(L\) the total input bit length.

Does this domain admit a stable, weakly group strategy-proof direct mechanism with the stated exact polynomial running time, or is no such mechanism possible?

For finite students, a stable, weakly group strategy-proof mechanism exists for adjusted scoring priorities; its stated implementation takes \(O(n^4m)\) priority comparisons. [Kitahara–Okumura](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00182-026-01000-5).
Fractional stability and fairness are established for discrete-concave markets, but that model uses different choice-based stability and does not establish the coalition property specified here; no exact result settling this target was located. [Imamura–Kawase](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18574v1).

Conclusion

No such mechanism exists. Under the stated stability definition, a stable assignment need not exist, even with one school, one type, and dyadic rational inputs. This obstruction precedes both the coalition requirement and the running-time requirement.

An admissible counterexample

Let \(S=\{s\}\). There is one student type \(t\), with:

  • Mass \(\mu_t=1\) and true ranking \(s\succ_{P_t}\varnothing\).
  • One category \(a_s(t)=a\) and score \(\sigma_s(t)=0\).
  • School capacity \(\kappa_s=3/4\) and threshold \(b_s=1/2\).
  • Adjustment \(\alpha_{s,a}(z)=1-z\) for \(z\in[0,1]\).

The adjustment is continuous, non-increasing, and linear with rational coefficients. Every numerical input is dyadic rational.

Consider truthful reporting. Write \(x=x_{t,P_t,s}\) for the assigned school mass. Feasibility gives \(0\le x\le3/4\) and unmatched mass \(1-x\). Because there is only one category, \(z_{s,a}=x\), so the current adjusted priority is \(p_s(t)=1-x\).

Proof that no assignment is stable

Suppose first that \(x>1/2\). Then \(x>0\) and \(p_s(t)=1-x<1/2=b_s\). This violates individual rationality.

Consequently, every stable assignment would have to satisfy \(x\le1/2\). But then:

  • The unmatched group has positive assignment mass \(1-x\ge1/2\).
  • Its reported ranking places \(s\) above \(\varnothing\).
  • Its current adjusted priority satisfies \(p_s(t)=1-x\ge1/2=b_s\).

The blocking condition in the question therefore requires \(s\) to be full. This would mean \(x=\kappa_s=3/4\), contradicting \(x\le1/2\).

These cases exhaust all feasible assignments. Thus there is no stable assignment at truthful reports.

The obstruction is not an isolated parameter choice

More generally, consider one school and one type of mass \(1\), ranking the school above being unmatched. Let its capacity be \(\kappa\), and write \(p(z)=\sigma+\alpha(z)\). Assume \(p\) is non-increasing and \(p(0)\ge b>p(\kappa)\).

If \(x=0\), the unmatched students are eligible at a vacant school, violating stability. If \(x>0\), individual rationality requires \(p(x)\ge b\). Since \(p(\kappa)<b\), this implies \(x<\kappa\le1\). Positive unmatched mass remains eligible, so stability again requires \(x=\kappa\), a contradiction.

Our example satisfies the stronger endpoint inequalities \(p(0)>b>p(\kappa)\). The impossibility therefore holds throughout a nontrivial family of inputs.

The decisive feature is that eligibility at equality, \(p_s(t)=b_s\), still blocks vacant seats, while admission beyond that boundary violates individual rationality. The finite-student existence result does not resolve this obstruction under the explicitly stated fractional stability conditions.

Because a stable direct mechanism must return a stable assignment at truthful reports, this counterexample rules out the requested mechanism regardless of computational power or incentive properties.
