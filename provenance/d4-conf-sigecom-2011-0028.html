The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support of private value vectors and public budgets with rational population masses \(\mu_t\), a finite service set \(J\), and per-capita total supply \(q\), does a \(\mathrm{poly}(|J|,\tau,L)\)-time algorithm output an anonymous direct-revelation mechanism with indivisible service lotteries and feasible mass rationing that is DSIC, ex-post IR, budget-feasible, and earns a fixed constant fraction of optimal expected revenue per capita? Require supply feasibility at every report-mass profile and evaluate revenue under the truthful type distribution \(\mu\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is nonatomic and has total mass \(1\). It is given by its support: \(\tau\) types \(t=(v_t,B_t)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Values and public budgets are nonnegative binary rationals. Conditional on each public budget, the value coordinates are independent.
There are \(m=|J|\geq1\) service kinds. Agents are unit-demand; receiving service \(j\) gives value \(v_{tj}\), and receiving nothing gives value \(0\). The binary-rational supply \(q\in[0,1]\) bounds total served mass, with no separate capacity for a service kind. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For each public budget \(b\), let \(T_b=\{t:B_t=b\}\) and \(\beta_b=\sum_{t\in T_b}\mu_t\). An agent with budget \(b\) may report any value vector in \(T_b\); budgets cannot be misreported. A report-mass profile is any \(\rho\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{r\in T_b}\rho_r=\beta_b\) for every \(b\).
An anonymous direct mechanism assigns each report \(r\), at each profile \(\rho\), a lottery over receiving one indivisible service or nothing, jointly with a realized payment \(P\). Identical reports with identical public budgets receive identical lotteries. Include the option to receive nothing and pay nothing.
Let \(x_{rj}(\rho)\) be the probability of service \(j\). Mass rationing must implement these probabilities while serving total mass at most \(q\) in every realization, at every report-mass profile; in particular, \(\sum_r\rho_r\sum_jx_{rj}(\rho)\leq q\).
Payments are nonnegative and never exceed the public budget. Ex-post IR requires \(P\leq v_{rj}\) whenever truthful report \(r\) receives \(j\), and \(P=0\) when it receives nothing. Utility is expected realized value minus payment, and is \(-\infty\) if a payment exceeds the true budget.
DSIC requires, for every \(\rho\) and every \(t,r\in T_b\), that type \(t\) weakly prefers its truthful lottery to report \(r\)'s lottery. A single nonatomic agent's deviation leaves \(\rho\) unchanged; lotteries for zero-mass reports must also be defined. Truthful reporting may be selected when utilities tie.
Revenue is \(R(M)=\sum_t\mu_t\mathbb E[P\mid t,\rho=\mu]\). Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be its supremum over all anonymous DSIC, ex-post IR, budget-feasible mechanisms satisfying the same reporting and supply requirements.
Does there exist an absolute constant \(c>0\) and an algorithm running in \(\mathrm{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time that outputs such a mechanism with \(R(M)\geq c\,\mathrm{OPT}\), or is obtaining any fixed positive constant fraction NP-hard? The output mechanism must have a finite description evaluable in polynomial time in these parameters and the bit length of a rational report-mass profile.
For finite societies with independent unit-demand value coordinates, public budgets, and matroid feasibility, a polynomial-time computable DSIC constant-factor revenue approximation is stated in [Chawla–Malec–Malekian](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.6280).
[Richter](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0899825619300193) characterizes optimal revenue mechanisms for a continuum with a divisible good and without unit-demand. This does not settle the indivisible-service problem above; no result for this precise formulation was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one and interpreted per-capita total supply as a single aggregate capacity q in [0,1], with no service-specific capacities.
  • Specified nonnegative rational values and public budgets, unit-demand consumption, and a nonempty service set.
  • Carried over coordinate independence by requiring the conditional value distribution within each public budget class to be a product distribution.
  • Restricted value reports to the listed support within the agent's public budget class; admissible report-mass profiles preserve each public budget class's total mass.
  • Defined nonatomic DSIC with aggregate reports fixed by an individual deviation, including lotteries for reports currently having zero mass.
  • Required realized budget feasibility and ex-post IR, prohibited negative payments, and included voluntary nonparticipation.
  • Interpreted feasible mass rationing as implementing individual indivisible-service lotteries with aggregate capacity respected in every realization.
  • Defined optimal revenue over anonymous DSIC mechanisms satisfying the same constraints, with truthful reporting selected in utility ties.
  • Required a finite, polynomial-time evaluable mechanism description to make the algorithmic output requirement precise.

Result

The problem is exactly solvable in polynomial time. There is an algorithm producing an admissible mechanism with \(R(M)=\mathrm{OPT}\), so one may take \(c=1\).

The proof uses the explicitly listed support and the stipulated nonatomic deviation model. It does not require conditional independence of the value coordinates.

1. A finite linear program

Write \(T\) for the listed support and define \(a_{tj}=\min\{B_t,v_{tj}\}\).

For each \(t\in T\) and \(j\in J\), introduce variables:

  • \(x_{tj}\), the probability that report \(t\) receives service \(j\);
  • \(z_{tj}\), the expected payment attributable to receiving service \(j\).

Consider the linear program maximizing

\(\displaystyle V=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\sum_{j\in J}z_{tj}\)

subject to the following constraints.

For every \(t\in T\),

\(\displaystyle x_{tj}\geq0,\qquad \sum_{j\in J}x_{tj}\leq1.\)

For every \(t\in T\) and \(j\in J\),

\(\displaystyle 0\leq z_{tj}\leq a_{tj}x_{tj}.\)

For every public budget \(b\) and every \(t,r\in T_b\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j\in J}v_{tj}x_{tj}-\sum_{j\in J}z_{tj}\ \geq\ \sum_{j\in J}v_{tj}x_{rj}-\sum_{j\in J}z_{rj}.\)

Finally,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\sum_{j\in J}x_{tj}\leq q.\)

The zero vector is feasible. The feasible region is closed and bounded: every allocation variable lies in \([0,1]\), and every payment variable lies in \([0,a_{tj}]\). Thus an optimal solution \((x^*,z^*)\) exists. Denote its objective value by \(V^*\).

Every admissible mechanism gives a feasible LP solution

Take any admissible mechanism and examine its lotteries at the truthful profile \(\rho=\mu\). Set

\(\displaystyle x_{tj}=\Pr(t\text{ receives }j),\qquad z_{tj}=\mathbb E[P\,\mathbf 1_{\{t\text{ receives }j\}}].\)

Unit demand gives the allocation constraints. Nonnegative payments, budget feasibility, and truthful ex-post IR give

\(\displaystyle 0\leq z_{tj}\leq \min\{B_t,v_{tj}\}\Pr(t\text{ receives }j)=a_{tj}x_{tj}.\)

Because receiving nothing entails zero payment, the expected payment for report \(t\) is \(\sum_jz_{tj}\).

For a deviation from \(t\) to \(r\in T_b\), every realized payment remains within the true budget \(b\). Consequently, expected utility from that deviation is exactly

\(\displaystyle \sum_jv_{tj}x_{rj}-\sum_jz_{rj}.\)

The DSIC requirements therefore imply the LP incentive inequalities. Realization-wise supply feasibility implies the LP supply constraint by taking expectations.

The LP objective equals the mechanism’s revenue. Hence

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{OPT}\leq V^*.\)

2. Extending the optimal lotteries to every profile

Define conditional service prices by

\(\displaystyle p_{rj}=\frac{z^*_{rj}}{x^*_{rj}}\quad\text{if }x^*_{rj}>0,\)

and set \(p_{rj}=0\) when \(x^*_{rj}=0\). The LP constraints ensure

\(\displaystyle 0\leq p_{rj}\leq\min\{B_r,v_{rj}\}.\)

Let \(s_r=\sum_jx^*_{rj}\). At any admissible report-mass profile \(\rho\), define

\(\displaystyle D(\rho)=\sum_r\rho_rs_r.\)

Set \(\alpha(\rho)=1\) when \(D(\rho)\leq q\), and set \(\alpha(\rho)=q/D(\rho)\) otherwise. The second branch has a positive denominator, so this also defines the rule when \(q=0\).

For report \(r\), prescribe the following lottery:

  • receive service \(j\) and pay \(p_{rj}\) with probability \(\alpha(\rho)x^*_{rj}\);
  • receive nothing and pay nothing with probability \(1-\alpha(\rho)s_r\).

Also provide the outside option of receiving nothing and paying nothing.

These probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one.

Budget feasibility and ex-post IR

Whenever truthful report \(r\) receives service \(j\), its payment satisfies \(p_{rj}\leq B_r\) and \(p_{rj}\leq v_{rj}\). Receiving nothing costs zero. Thus the required payment restrictions hold at every profile.

DSIC, including zero-mass reports

Fix any profile \(\rho\), budget \(b\), and types \(t,r\in T_b\). Expected utility of type \(t\) from report \(r\) is

\(\displaystyle U_t(r;\rho)=\alpha(\rho)\left(\sum_jv_{tj}x^*_{rj}-\sum_jz^*_{rj}\right).\)

Here we used \(x^*_{rj}p_{rj}=z^*_{rj}\), including when \(x^*_{rj}=0\).

The LP incentive inequality, multiplied by the common nonnegative factor \(\alpha(\rho)\), gives

\(\displaystyle U_t(t;\rho)\geq U_t(r;\rho).\)

Under the stipulated nonatomic deviation model, a single agent’s deviation leaves \(\rho\), and therefore \(\alpha(\rho)\), unchanged. This proves exactly the required DSIC condition.

Truthful utility is nonnegative because each \(z^*_{tj}\leq v_{tj}x^*_{tj}\). Thus truthful reporting also weakly dominates the outside option.

All formulas apply to every listed report, whether or not its current mass is positive.

Planned served mass

Writing \(w_{rj}=\alpha(\rho)x^*_{rj}\), the prescribed total served mass is

\(\displaystyle \sum_r\rho_r\sum_jw_{rj}=\alpha(\rho)D(\rho)\leq q.\)

We next implement this bound in every realization.

3. Realization-wise nonatomic rationing

The following construction implements any prescribed probabilities \(w_{rj}\geq0\) with \(\sum_jw_{rj}\leq1\), while allocating exactly mass \(\rho_rw_{rj}\) to service \(j\) within each report class.

For every positive-mass report class \(A_r\), choose a normalized coordinate \(u_r:A_r\to[0,1)\) whose pushforward of the class’s normalized mass is Lebesgue measure. Such a coordinate exists for every finite nonatomic measure space: the nonatomic divisibility theorem permits repeated equal bisections, and the resulting binary partition labels give a uniform coordinate. Its hypotheses hold because restricting the society’s nonatomic measure to \(A_r\) preserves nonatomicity.

For a zero-mass report class, assign coordinate zero to its agents, if any.

Partition \([0,1)\) into consecutive half-open service intervals \(I_{rj}\) of lengths \(w_{rj}\), followed by a no-service interval of length \(1-\sum_jw_{rj}\). Draw one common uniform shift \(U\in[0,1)\). Give an agent in class \(r\) service \(j\) precisely when

\(\displaystyle (u_r+U)\bmod1\in I_{rj}.\)

Charge \(p_{rj}\) upon receiving service \(j\), and zero otherwise.

For each fixed agent, translation of a uniform shift is uniform. Thus its service probability is exactly \(w_{rj}\), regardless of its coordinate. Identical reports therefore receive identical lotteries.

For each fixed shift, translation preserves Lebesgue measure. The mass receiving service \(j\) in class \(r\) is consequently exactly \(\rho_rw_{rj}\). Summing across report classes and services proves that, in every realization, total served mass is at most \(q\).

Zero-mass classes contribute no served mass but still have the prescribed lotteries. The population coordinates only correlate allocations; they do not affect any agent’s marginal lottery.

4. Polynomial description and evaluation

The LP has \(2\tau m\) variables and \(O(\tau m+\tau^2)\) constraints. Its coefficients are rational, and its total encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly represented rational LP can be solved exactly in time polynomial in its encoding length; a bounded nonempty rational polytope has an optimal rational solution of polynomial encoding length. Polynomial LP algorithms and the vertex-size bounds underlying exact optimization are described in [MIT’s linear-programming notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html).

The theorem applies here because the LP is explicit, rational, nonempty, and bounded. Hence \((x^*,z^*)\) and the conditional prices \(p_{rj}\) have polynomial encoding length and are computable in polynomial time.

The output consists of these tables, the scaling formula, and the cyclic rationing rule. Given a rational profile \(\rho\), evaluating \(D(\rho)\), \(\alpha(\rho)\), all service probabilities, and all interval endpoints takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\) and the profile’s bit length.

There is also a finite randomization implementation at every rational profile. Let \(K\) be the product of the denominators of the rational probabilities \(w_{rj}\), and write \(n_{rj}=Kw_{rj}\). The bit length of \(K\) is polynomial; its numerical magnitude need not be polynomial.

Partition each report class into cells labeled by \(\lfloor Ku_r\rfloor\). These cells each have mass \(\rho_r/K\). Draw a common integer \(H\) uniformly from \(\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), and cyclically shift cell labels by \(H\). Assign consecutive blocks of \(n_{rj}\) shifted labels to service \(j\).

Every agent visits every shifted label with equal probability, giving probability \(n_{rj}/K=w_{rj}\) for service \(j\). In every realization, exactly \(n_{rj}\) cells receive that service, giving mass \(\rho_rw_{rj}\).

Computing this rationing description, and evaluating its label rule given \(H\), uses polynomial time and polynomial-length integers. It requires no enumeration of the \(K\) shifts or cells. At irrational profiles, the continuous-shift construction defines the mechanism and proves feasibility.

5. Optimal revenue

The LP supply constraint gives \(D(\mu)\leq q\), so \(\alpha(\mu)=1\). Therefore the constructed mechanism earns

\(\displaystyle R(M)=\sum_t\mu_t\sum_jx^*_{tj}p_{tj}=\sum_t\mu_t\sum_jz^*_{tj}=V^*.\)

The mechanism is admissible at every report-mass profile. Combining its revenue with the earlier upper bound yields

\(\displaystyle V^*=R(M)\leq\mathrm{OPT}\leq V^*.\)

Thus the supremum is attained, and exact optimization—and hence any fixed positive constant approximation up to \(c=1\)—is polynomial-time solvable for the formulation in the question.
