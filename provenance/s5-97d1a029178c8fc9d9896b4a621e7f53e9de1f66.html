Conclusions

Exact maximization is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with two placement categories and with all numerical input data dyadic. Its rational-threshold decision version is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

Every feasible instance has an exact optimal allocation of polynomial bit length. However, the existence of polynomial-size, polynomial-time verifiable optimality certificates for every feasible instance is equivalent to \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\), when certificates mean arbitrary sound and complete verification witnesses.

Thus the computational question is settled. An unconditional affirmative or negative answer to the universal certificate question would settle \(\mathrm{NP}\) versus \(\mathrm{coNP}\). Below I prove these statements and give an explicit rank-sensitive optimality condition, whose full certificate can have exponential size.

1. The objective is convex in allocation masses

Write \(\mathcal P\) for the feasible allocation polytope and \(e=|E|\leq \tau m\). List the distinct eligible-edge utilities as \(v_1<\cdots<v_N\), where \(N\leq e\). Define

\[ p_i(x)=\sum_{(t,j)\in E:\,u_{tj}=v_i}x_{tj}, \qquad C_i(x)=\sum_{h=1}^{i}p_h(x), \qquad W(z)=\int_0^z\omega(s)\,ds. \]

Because feasible allocations have total mass one, \(C_N(x)=1\). Pooling equal utilities gives

\[ G_\omega(x) =\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_i\bigl(W(C_i(x))-W(C_{i-1}(x))\bigr) =v_NW(1)-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(v_{i+1}-v_i)W(C_i(x)), \]

where \(C_0(x)=0\).

The function \(W\) is concave and piecewise linear: its slopes are the nonincreasing values of \(\omega\). Each \(C_i\) is linear in \(x\), and every utility gap \(v_{i+1}-v_i\) is positive. Consequently, \(G_\omega\) is convex and piecewise linear on \(\mathcal P\).

This differs from applying monotone OWA to a fixed vector of linear criteria. The formulation in [Ogryczak–Śliwiński](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221702003995) concerns that fixed-vector setting; here the masses occupying utility ranks vary with the allocation.

Feasibility can be checked by a rational maximum-flow computation: source-to-type capacities are \(\mu_t\), eligible type-to-category capacities can be one, and category-to-sink capacities are \(q_j\). A flow of value one is equivalent to a feasible allocation. This takes \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) bit operations.

Two easy cases are worth separating. With \(m=1\), a feasible allocation is uniquely determined. With \(K=1\), the objective is a positive constant times total utility and is solvable by weighted transportation optimization in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time. The hardness below uses \(m=2\) and variable \(K\).

2. A dyadic reduction from \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\)

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\): given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), determine whether some subset sums to half their total. See, for example, these [MIT lecture notes](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/6.1400-2025/lec19-before-class.pdf).

Let \(A=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\), and choose a power of two \(M>A\).

Construction

There are \(n\) ordinary types and one dummy type \(d\), with masses

\[ \mu_i=\frac{a_i}{M}\quad(1\leq i\leq n), \qquad \mu_d=1-\frac{A}{M}. \]

Both categories are eligible for every ordinary type. The dummy is eligible only for category one. Supplies are

\[ q_2=\frac{A}{2M}, \qquad q_1=1-\frac{A}{2M}. \]

For each ordinary type, set \(w_i=4^{\,n-i+1}\) and utilities

\[ u_{i1}=i, \qquad u_{i2}=i+\frac{1}{w_i}. \]

The dummy utility is \(u_{d1}=n+1\). Since \(w_i\geq4\), all utilities of type \(i\) lie strictly below all utilities of type \(i+1\).

Put \(s_0=0\) and \(s_i=\sum_{h=1}^{i}\mu_h\). Define the rank weight function by

\[ \omega(s)= \begin{cases} \frac{3w_i}{2},&s_{i-1}\leq s<s_{i-1}+\frac{\mu_i}{2},\\ \frac{w_i}{2},&s_{i-1}+\frac{\mu_i}{2}\leq s<s_i, \end{cases} \]

on each ordinary-type block, and set \(\omega(s)=1\) on \([s_n,1]\).

These weights are positive and nonincreasing. Indeed, between consecutive ordinary blocks,

\[ \frac{w_i}{2}\geq\frac{3w_{i+1}}{2}, \]

because \(w_{i+1}=w_i/4\); the final ordinary weight is \(w_n/2=2\), followed by the dummy weight one.

All data are dyadic. Their encodings have polynomial length: \(M\) needs \(O(\log A)\) bits, and \(w_i\) needs \(O(n)\) bits. The construction has

\[ m=2,\qquad \tau=n+1,\qquad K=2n+1. \]

It is always feasible: assign half of each ordinary type to each category, and assign the dummy entirely to category one.

Exact objective calculation

Let \(y_i=x_{i2}\). Feasibility is equivalent to

\[ 0\leq y_i\leq\mu_i, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n}y_i=\frac{A}{2M}, \]

together with the forced dummy allocation.

The utilities of different types never interleave. Therefore, type \(i\) always occupies the quantile interval \([s_{i-1},s_i]\). Its baseline utility \(i\) contributes \(i\mu_iw_i\), because the average weight on that interval is \(w_i\).

Its higher utility occupies the last \(y_i\) units of that interval. The weight integral over those units is

\[ \begin{cases} \frac{w_i}{2}y_i,&0\leq y_i\leq\frac{\mu_i}{2},\\ \frac{3w_i}{2}y_i-\frac{w_i}{2}\mu_i, &\frac{\mu_i}{2}\leq y_i\leq\mu_i. \end{cases} \]

Multiplying by the utility increment \(1/w_i\), the additional contribution is

\[ y_i-\frac12\min\{y_i,\mu_i-y_i\}. \]

Define the rational constants

\[ C=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i\mu_iw_i+(n+1)\mu_d, \qquad B=C+\frac{A}{2M}. \]

Every feasible allocation consequently satisfies the exact identity

\[ G_\omega(x) =B-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min\{y_i,\mu_i-y_i\}. \]

Each summand is nonnegative. Hence \(G_\omega(x)\leq B\), with equality precisely when \(y_i\in\{0,\mu_i\}\) for every ordinary type.

Under that condition, the supply equation becomes

\[ \sum_{i:\,y_i=\mu_i}a_i=\frac A2. \]

Thus

\[ \max_{x\in\mathcal P}G_\omega(x)\geq B \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (a_1,\ldots,a_n)\text{ is a yes-instance of }\mathrm{PARTITION}. \]

The construction and threshold have polynomial encoding length. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness, including the claimed dyadic, two-category restriction.

3. Polynomial bit length and threshold verification

An optimum exists at a transportation vertex

For a feasible instance, \(\mathcal P\) is nonempty and compact, and \(G_\omega\) is continuous. Hence an optimum exists.

Every point of \(\mathcal P\) is a convex combination of its vertices. Convexity gives, for \(x=\sum_r\lambda_r z^r\),

\[ G_\omega(x)\leq\sum_r\lambda_rG_\omega(z^r) \leq\max_rG_\omega(z^r). \]

Therefore some vertex is optimal.

Vertex coordinates have polynomial bit length

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all masses and supplies. Its binary length is \(O(L)\).

The positive-support graph of a transportation vertex is a forest. Otherwise, an even cycle permits sufficiently small positive and negative alternating perturbations, preserving every row and column sum; this expresses the allocation as the midpoint of two distinct feasible allocations, contradicting extremality.

Remove a positive-support edge from its tree. Sum the type balances minus the category balances over either resulting component. Internal flows cancel, and the removed edge is the only positive flow crossing the component boundary. Thus its flow, up to sign, is a sum of masses minus a sum of supplies.

Every positive coordinate therefore has denominator dividing \(D\). Since coordinates lie in \([0,1]\), each needs \(O(L)\) bits. There are at most \(\tau+m-1\) positive coordinates.

Consequently, every feasible instance admits a polynomial-size exact optimal allocation, with dense encoding length \(O(\tau mL)\).

Exact evaluation and membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

For a rational allocation with encoding length \(H\), feasibility and the pooled-utility formula can be checked exactly in

\[ \bigl(\tau m\log(\tau m+1)+K\bigr)\operatorname{poly}(L+H) \]

bit operations. Sort utilities, pool their masses, and traverse the cumulative masses and weight breakpoints to compute \(W\).

If any feasible allocation reaches a rational threshold \(b\), an optimal vertex reaches it and has polynomial encoding length. Guessing that vertex and evaluating its objective proves that the threshold problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Together with Section 2, this establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness of rational-threshold decision.

4. Universal polynomial optimality certificates

Here “certificate” means a uniform polynomial-time verifier that accepts only exact optimal allocations, and for which every feasible instance has some polynomial-size optimal allocation and accepted polynomial-size certificate.

Such certificates imply \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\)

Apply the reduction in Section 2.

For a no-instance of \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\), the constructed allocation instance is feasible, but its optimal value is strictly below \(B\). Strictness follows from attainment of the maximum and the equality characterization proved above.

Suppose universal polynomial optimality certificates exist. A nondeterministic verifier for the complement of \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) can:

  • Construct the allocation instance and \(B\).
  • Guess an allocation and its optimality certificate.
  • Verify certified optimality and check that its exact objective is below \(B\).

Soundness follows because a yes-instance has optimum \(B\). Completeness follows from the assumed certificate guarantee.

Thus the complement of the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), implying \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\).

This implication applies to any proposed rank-sensitive certificate format.

Conversely, \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\) implies such certificates exist

Consider the language of pairs \((I,x)\) for which \(x\) is feasible and exactly optimal for instance \(I\).

Its complement belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). Infeasibility of the proposed allocation is directly checkable. If \(x\) is feasible but suboptimal, a better transportation vertex is a polynomial-size witness, by Section 3; its superiority is exactly verifiable.

The optimality language therefore belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\). If \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\), it has polynomial-size witnesses verified in polynomial time. Choose a polynomial-size optimal vertex to obtain the required certificate for every feasible instance.

Hence the unrestricted certificate property is equivalent to \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\). This is a complexity-theoretic existence statement, not a claim that a particular concise dual-witness format exists.

5. An explicit rank-sensitive optimality condition

There is an exact condition combining weighted transportation optima and dual witnesses, but generally involving exponentially many rank choices.

Let the weight intervals have endpoints \(0=r_0<\cdots<r_K=1\) and values \(a_1\geq\cdots\geq a_K>0\). Define

\[ b_k=W(r_{k-1})-a_kr_{k-1}. \]

Concavity gives the exact representation

\[ W(z)=\min_{1\leq k\leq K}(a_kz+b_k). \]

Each affine function extends one segment of \(W\), lies above \(W\), and equals it on that segment.

For \(\sigma\in\{1,\ldots,K\}^{N-1}\), put \(\Delta_i=v_{i+1}-v_i\) and define

\[ F_\sigma(x)=c_\sigma+\sum_{(t,j)\in E}d^\sigma_{tj}x_{tj}, \]

where

\[ c_\sigma=v_NW(1)-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\Delta_i b_{\sigma_i}, \qquad d^\sigma_{tj} =-\sum_{\substack{1\leq i<N\\u_{tj}\leq v_i}} \Delta_i a_{\sigma_i}. \]

Substitution into the pooled-utility formula yields

\[ G_\omega(x)=\max_\sigma F_\sigma(x). \]

The choices \(\sigma_i\) refer directly to the weight intervals containing cumulative masses below utility ranks.

For each \(\sigma\), the weighted transportation dual is

\[ \min_{\alpha^\sigma,\beta^\sigma} \left(\sum_t\mu_t\alpha^\sigma_t+\sum_jq_j\beta^\sigma_j\right), \qquad \alpha^\sigma_t+\beta^\sigma_j\geq d^\sigma_{tj}\quad((t,j)\in E). \]

The potentials are unrestricted in sign.

A feasible \(x^*\) is optimal if and only if, for every \(\sigma\), there exist dual-feasible potentials satisfying

\[ c_\sigma+\sum_t\mu_t\alpha^\sigma_t+\sum_jq_j\beta^\sigma_j \leq G_\omega(x^*). \]

For sufficiency, weak duality bounds every \(F_\sigma(x)\) by \(G_\omega(x^*)\); taking their maximum bounds \(G_\omega(x)\). For necessity, optimality bounds every \(F_\sigma\) over \(\mathcal P\). Strong duality supplies the required potentials because each weighted transportation problem is feasible and bounded.

Each individual dual witness has polynomial bit length by rational linear-programming theory. The number of witnesses, however, can be \(K^{N-1}\). Enumerating these weighted problems gives an exact algorithm with running time

\[ K^{N-1}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,L) \leq K^{\tau m-1}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,L). \]

Checking only a branch active at \(x^*\) does not establish this condition: each \(F_\sigma\) is a global lower bound on \(G_\omega\). Its dual bounds that branch, not all other branches.

The discrete sufficient condition in [Nguyen–Weng](https://arxiv.org/html/1801.07544v1) therefore cannot simply be transferred into a universal single-branch certificate for the variable-mass objective.
