The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(\Theta\), rational masses \(\mu\), finite reactive modules, bounded integer energy levels, LTL goals \(\gamma_\theta\), and an anonymous finite public-state transition rule, decide whether there exists a feasible measurable pre-play energy-transfer kernel \(x\), respecting donor budgets and recipient capacities, and a measurable population assignment of finite-state Mealy strategies such that the induced continuum game has a stage-2 core-stable population outcome and \(x\) is stage-1 Core-stable. Stage-2 deviations are by measurable positive-mass coalitions, with complement responses as in the paper; each agent's goal is evaluated on its induced run, and the paper's no/some/all stable-continuation preference is interpreted agentwise almost everywhere.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Realize it as the atomless space \(A=\bigsqcup_\theta\{\theta\}\times[0,\mu_\theta)\) with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\).
Each type has Boolean local variables, a finite reactive module, an LTL goal \(\gamma_\theta\) over its local and public variables, and binary-encoded integers \(0\le e_\theta\le c_\theta\). There is a nonempty finite public state space with a specified initial state. Let \(m\ge1\) count all public and type-local Boolean variables and \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Commands have Boolean guards and simultaneous local Boolean assignments, leaving unassigned variables unchanged, and signed integer energy increments \(\eta\). A command requires a true guard and \(E+\eta\ge0\), and sets energy to \(\min\{E+\eta,c_\theta\}\). Initialization uses unconditional initial commands, including one with nonnegative increment; subsequent rounds include an always-enabled skip with nonnegative increment. Local variables start false.
The deterministic anonymous public update is encoded by a finite Boolean circuit whose tests are rational affine inequalities in masses of agents satisfying listed Boolean predicates of type, current local valuation, and chosen command; predicates are supplied as Boolean circuits. Comparisons are exact, including equality. Commands execute synchronously, followed by the public update; initialization produces the first valuation in each agent's run.
A transfer profile is a nonnegative measurable density \(x:A\times A\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), with \(x(a,a)=0\), \(O_x(a)=\int_Ax(a,b)\,d\lambda(b)\le e_{\theta(a)}\), and \(x(a,b)+e_{\theta(b)}\le c_{\theta(b)}\) almost everywhere. Put \(I_x(a)=\int_Ax(b,a)\,d\lambda(b)\) and \(E_x(a)=\min\{e_{\theta(a)}-O_x(a)+I_x(a),c_{\theta(a)}\}\); require \(E_x(a)\) to be integer almost everywhere. Aggregate excess receipts are discarded by this saturation rule.
Strategies are deterministic finite-state Mealy machines observing the current public valuation, own local valuation, and own energy, with initialization used only once and legal outputs on every reachable observation history. A population profile is a measurable assignment of machines from their countable discrete set; machine sizes have no common imposed bound. It induces a unique measurable population evolution and individual LTL runs; write \(w_\sigma(a)\in\{0,1\}\) for goal satisfaction.
A profile belongs to \(K(x)\), the stage-2 core, when every measurable coalition \(C\) with \(\lambda(C)>0\) and \(w_\sigma=0\) almost everywhere on \(C\), and every measurable replacement strategy assignment on \(C\), admit a measurable complement response for which a positive-mass subset of \(C\) still fails its goal. The grand coalition is allowed.
If \(K(x)=\varnothing\), set \(r_x=0\). Otherwise take the essential supremum \(u_x\) and essential infimum \(\ell_x\) of \(\{w_\sigma:\sigma\in K(x)\}\), and set \(r_x=0\) where \(u_x=0\), \(r_x=1\) where \(u_x=1,\ell_x=0\), and \(r_x=2\) where \(\ell_x=1\). Agents strictly prefer larger ranks and are indifferent at equal ranks.
A transfer profile is stage-1 Core-stable if every positive-mass measurable coalition \(C\) and every alternative collection of its donor rows that extends to a feasible full profile admit feasible replacement donor rows for the complement, producing \(y\) with \(\lambda(\{a\in C:r_y(a)\le r_x(a)\})>0\). Both sides may withdraw all previous offers and reallocate; responses follow the coalition's offers, and feasibility uses original endowments.
Is deciding whether there exists a feasible stage-1 Core-stable \(x\) with \(K(x)\ne\varnothing\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? Express running times in \(m,\tau,L\), maximum capacity \(B=\max_\theta c_\theta\), and maximum goal length \(g=\max_\theta|\gamma_\theta|\).
For finite agent lists, Core–Core offer existence is \(\mathrm{2EXPTIME}\)-complete; existential and universal core verification with bounded energy are also \(\mathrm{2EXPTIME}\)-complete. [Gutierrez et al., 2024](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0008.pdf).
No classification of this precise atomless, anonymously aggregated problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized masses to one and used an atomless interval realization, allowing agents of the same type to receive different transfers and strategies.
  • Specified a finite input encoding for the anonymous transition rule using Boolean circuits and rational affine comparisons of population masses; fixed exact comparison semantics and the initial public state.
  • Made module variables local to each agent, allowed goals and guards to inspect local and public variables, and specified the finite observations available to Mealy strategies.
  • Interpreted the transfer kernel as a real-valued density integrated against population measure. Retained the individual-offer recipient bound and aggregate saturation from the discrete definition, rather than imposing an aggregate incoming-energy bound; required integer post-transfer energies to retain the stated integer energy domain.
  • Restricted partial offer deviations to those admitting a feasible completion, and complement responses to feasible completions, because integer post-transfer energies impose a joint feasibility condition.
  • Included withdrawal and reallocation of all prior offers by both deviators and responders, using original endowments for donor budgets.
  • Used positive-mass blocking subsets for both core definitions and included the grand coalition. Corrected the inconsistent stage-1 coalition indices and interpreted the blocking witness as belonging to the deviating coalition, as the surrounding explanation requires.
  • Defined no/some/all continuation ranks through essential extrema of measurable goal-satisfaction indicators, making the agentwise almost-everywhere interpretation invariant under null-set changes.
  • Required a nonempty stage-2 core in addition to stage-1 stability, and gave empty continuation sets the lowest preference rank.
  • Defined m as the total number of public and type-local Boolean variables and L as the complete finite input bit length; imposed no uniform strategy-memory bound.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with \(\tau=7\), \(B=8\), and goal lengths bounded by an absolute constant. Thus a polynomial-time algorithm in \(L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The reduction below treats measurable population splits and feasible complement responses directly. It does not rely on the [finite-agent theorem cited in the question](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0008.pdf).

1. Construction from satisfiability

Let \(\varphi(v_1,\ldots,v_n)\) be a propositional CNF formula. Introduce a further variable \(z\), and define \(\psi=\varphi\wedge z\). Then \(\psi\) is satisfiable exactly when \(\varphi\) is satisfiable, and the all-false valuation falsifies \(\psi\).

Construct seven types:

| Types | Mass of each type | Endowment | Capacity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Donors \(D_1,D_2,D_3\) | \(1/8\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |
| Reporters \(R_1,R_2,R_3\) | \(1/8\) | \(0\) | \(8\) |
| Certificate type \(Q\) | \(1/4\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |

Their masses sum to \(1\). Write \(A_T\) for the measurable set occupied by type \(T\).

Modules

Each donor has only a zero-increment initial no-op and the subsequent zero-increment skip.

Reporter \(R_i\) has a local Boolean variable \(b_i\), initially false. Its initial commands are:

  • a zero-increment no-op;
  • a command setting \(b_i\) true, with increment \(-2\).

Subsequently, its only command is skip. Consequently, the second initial command is legal exactly when its transferred initial energy is at least \(2\).

Type \(Q\) has local variables \(v_1,\ldots,v_n,z,q\). Its two zero-increment initial commands, called \(\alpha_0,\alpha_1\), set \(q\) false and true, respectively. Subsequent zero-increment commands can set any one of \(v_1,\ldots,v_n,z\) true; skip is also available. Thus a finite-state machine can reach any specified valuation of \(v_1,\ldots,v_n,z\), starting from the all-false valuation.

All commands have unconditional guards. The construction satisfies the required initialization and skip conditions.

Public update

The public variables encode:

  • a mode \(p\in\{1,2,3\}\);
  • Boolean flags \(h,s\);
  • a Boolean flag recording whether initialization has occurred.

During initialization, let \(a_i\) be the mass of reporters of type \(R_i\) choosing their \(-2\) reporting command. Set \(p\) to an index maximizing \(a_i\), breaking ties by the smallest index. Set \(h\) true exactly when a positive mass of \(Q\)-agents chooses \(\alpha_1\). Thereafter, \(p\) and \(h\) remain unchanged.

Initially \(s\) is false. At every public update, it becomes true if it was already true or if a positive mass of \(Q\)-agents has current local valuation satisfying \(\psi\).

These updates use only the permitted predicates and rational affine comparisons: comparisons between \(a_i\), and tests that a listed predicate has positive mass. Strict positivity can also be expressed by negating a nonpositivity test.

Goals

Let \(P_i\) be the fixed Boolean formula saying that the public mode is \(i\). Define:

  • reporter \(R_i\)'s goal as \(b_i\);
  • type \(Q\)'s goal as \(\mathrm F s\);
  • the donor goals as follows.

| Type | Goal |
|---|---|
| \(D_1\) | \(P_1\vee(P_3\wedge h)\vee\mathrm F s\) |
| \(D_2\) | \(P_2\vee(P_1\wedge h)\vee\mathrm F s\) |
| \(D_3\) | \(P_3\vee(P_2\wedge h)\vee\mathrm F s\) |

These goals have constant length. The formula \(\psi\) occurs only in a predicate circuit.

2. Transfer properties

Fix any feasible transfer profile \(x\).

Because donors have \(e=c=1\), the pointwise recipient constraint forces all incoming transfers to donors to be zero almost everywhere. The same holds for \(Q\), whose capacity is zero.

Reporters and \(Q\) have zero endowment. Nonnegativity and their outgoing-budget constraints therefore force their donor rows to vanish almost everywhere.

Thus only donors send transfers, and only reporters receive them. Every donor has transferred initial energy \(1-O_x(a)\), while every reporter has transferred initial energy \(\min\{I_x(a),8\}\).

Define the measurable sets and their masses by \(H_i(x)=\{a\in A_{R_i}:E_x(a)\ge 2\}\) and \(t_i(x)=\lambda(H_i(x))\). Let \(j(x)\) maximize \(t_i(x)\), with the prescribed tie-breaking rule.

3. Stage-2 core analysis

Lemma 1: Core profiles report exactly the eligible reporters

In every \(\sigma\in K(x)\), almost every agent in \(H_i(x)\) chooses the reporting command.

Indeed, suppose a positive-mass measurable set of eligible reporters chooses the no-op. Every member fails its goal \(b_i\). That set can form a coalition and replace its strategies with a finite-state machine that reports during initialization and skips thereafter. Every member then satisfies \(b_i\), independently of every complement response. This contradicts stage-2 core membership.

Conversely, reporters outside \(H_i(x)\) cannot legally report and therefore always fail.

Consequently, every core profile has initial reporting masses \(t_i(x)\), and hence public mode \(j(x)\).

Lemma 2: If \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable, \(K(x)\) is nonempty and donor ranks cycle

Assume \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable. Then \(\psi\) is unsatisfiable, so \(s\) remains false under every strategy profile.

Consider a profile in which every eligible reporter reports, donors skip, and all \(Q\)-agents choose the same initial command \(\alpha_h\) and subsequently skip. This realizes either value of \(h\).

We verify core membership. Let \(C\) be a positive-mass coalition of losing agents.

If \(C\) contains a positive mass of \(Q\)-agents or ineligible reporters, those agents have impossible goals and remain a positive-mass failing subset under every replacement.

Otherwise, up to a null set, \(C\) consists only of losing donors. Donors have no effective stage-2 choices. The complement can let eligible reporters report and let \(Q\) choose \(\alpha_0\), so that \(h\) is false. The mode remains \(j(x)\). Every donor in \(C\) is a nontop donor and therefore fails.

Thus both constructed profiles belong to \(K(x)\).

Lemma 1 also shows that the top donor wins in every core profile and the bottom donor loses in every core profile. The two constructed values of \(h\) show that the middle donor both wins and loses across core profiles. Therefore the donor ranks are exactly:

| Mode | \(D_1\) | \(D_2\) | \(D_3\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(1\) | \(2\) | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| \(2\) | \(0\) | \(2\) | \(1\) |
| \(3\) | \(1\) | \(0\) | \(2\) |

These are statements about essential ranks on the entire respective type sets.

Lemma 3: If \(\varphi\) is satisfiable, every donor and \(Q\)-agent has rank \(2\)

First, \(K(x)\) is nonempty. Let eligible reporters report, and let all \(Q\)-agents follow a finite sequence reaching a satisfying valuation of \(\psi\). The public flag \(s\) eventually becomes true. Every donor and \(Q\)-agent wins. The only losing agents are ineligible reporters, whose goals are impossible. Therefore this profile belongs to \(K(x)\).

Next, every core profile must satisfy \(\mathrm F s\). Otherwise all of \(A_Q\) is a positive-mass losing coalition. It can assign the finite-state certificate strategy just described to every member. Regardless of the complement response, a mass \(1/4\) of agents eventually satisfies \(\psi\), forcing \(s\) true and making every coalition member win. This contradicts core membership.

Hence every donor and \(Q\)-agent wins in every core profile and has rank \(2\).

4. Unsatisfiable formulas yield no stage-1 stable profile

Assume \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable, and fix an arbitrary feasible \(x\). Its donor ranks have one of the three rows in the preceding table.

Suppose first that the mode is \(1\). Take the coalition \(C=A_{D_2}\cup A_{D_3}\). Replace each coalition donor row by density \(8\) to \(A_{R_2}\), and zero elsewhere.

Each such donor spends exactly \(8\cdot(1/8)=1\). The recipient density bound is respected because \(R_2\) has capacity \(8\) and endowment zero.

These rows admit a feasible completion: set all complement rows to zero. Every \(R_2\)-agent then receives energy \(8\cdot(1/4)=2\), and all transferred initial energies are integral.

Now consider any feasible complement response, producing \(y\). Every \(R_2\)-agent still receives at least \(2\), so \(t_2(y)=1/8\).

Only the remaining donor type \(D_1\) can send additional transfers. Its aggregate outgoing budget is at most \(1/8\). For either \(i\ne 2\), the coalition sends nothing to \(R_i\), and therefore \(2t_i(y)\le\int_{A_{R_i}}I_y(a)\,d\lambda(a)\le 1/8\). The first inequality holds because every agent counted by \(t_i(y)\) must receive at least \(2\); the second follows from Tonelli's theorem and the remaining donors' aggregate budget.

Thus \(t_i(y)\le 1/16<1/8=t_2(y)\). Mode \(2\) is forced against every feasible response.

By Lemma 2, all \(D_2\)-agents improve from rank \(1\) to \(2\), and all \(D_3\)-agents improve from rank \(0\) to \(1\). The coalition therefore blocks \(x\).

The other cases are identical:

  • from mode \(2\), coalition \(A_{D_3}\cup A_{D_1}\) forces mode \(3\);
  • from mode \(3\), coalition \(A_{D_1}\cup A_{D_2}\) forces mode \(1\).

The same density and budget calculation applies in each case. Every feasible profile is blocked. Hence no stage-1 Core-stable profile exists.

5. Satisfiable formulas yield a stable zero-transfer profile

Assume \(\varphi\) is satisfiable, and let \(x^0\) be the zero-transfer profile.

By Lemma 3, \(K(x^0)\ne\varnothing\), and all donors and \(Q\)-agents have rank \(2\). Every reporter has energy zero, cannot report, and has rank \(0\).

Consider any positive-mass measurable coalition \(C\) and any alternative donor rows that admit a feasible completion.

If \(C\) contains a positive mass of donors or \(Q\)-agents, that subset already has maximal rank \(2\). Take any feasible completion guaranteed by the premise. Those agents cannot strictly improve, providing the required positive-mass subset.

Otherwise, up to a null set, \(C\) contains only reporters. Every reporter has original endowment zero. Its alternative donor row must consequently vanish almost everywhere. Set all complement rows to zero. This produces a feasible profile equal to \(x^0\) almost everywhere, so every coalition reporter retains rank \(0\).

Thus \(x^0\) is stage-1 Core-stable and has a nonempty stage-2 core.

Combining the two directions, \(\varphi\) is satisfiable exactly when the constructed society has the requested transfer profile.

6. Parameters and running-time consequence

For a source formula of encoded length \(N\) on \(n\) variables, the construction has \(m=n+10\), \(\tau=7\), \(B=8\), and \(g\le g_0\), where \(g_0\) is an absolute constant. There are five public bits, three reporter bits, and \(n+2\) certificate bits.

Under an indexed circuit-and-command encoding, the construction has \(L=O(N+n\log(n+2))\) and takes \(O(N+n\log(n+2))\) time.

Accordingly, an algorithm with running time \(T(m,\tau,L,B,g)\) would solve satisfiability in the reduction time plus \(T(n+10,7,L,8,g_0)\). In particular, a bound \(f(\tau,B,g)L^{O(1)}\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

This settles the requested hardness alternative. It does not establish membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) or a matching general upper bound.
