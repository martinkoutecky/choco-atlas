Theorem 5.1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite candidate and vacancy type sets \(I,J\), positive rational masses \(a_i,b_j\) summing to one on each side, strict rankings of acceptable opposite-side types, and a rational threshold \(K\), decide whether there is a stable mass assignment \(x_{ij}\geq 0\) with \(\sum_j x_{ij}\leq a_i\), \(\sum_i x_{ij}\leq b_j\), and \(\max\{D_I(x),D_J(x)\}\leq K\), where \(D_I(x)=\sum_{ij}r_i(j)x_{ij}\) and \(D_J(x)=\sum_{ij}s_j(i)x_{ij}\). For every acceptable pair \((i,j)\), stability requires \(U_{ij}=0\) or \(V_{ij}=0\), where \(U_{ij}\) is the mass of type \(i\) unmatched or assigned below \(j\), and \(V_{ij}\) is the mass of type \(j\) unmatched or assigned below \(i\). Let \(\Omega_I,\Omega_J\) be the minimum respective dissatisfaction over stable mass assignments and define the rational excess \(\theta=K-\max\{\Omega_I,\Omega_J\}\); determine the decision problem's complexity and whether a meaningful parameterization by this excess requires a specified population scale.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I,J\) be nonempty finite candidate and vacancy type sets, with positive binary rational masses \(a_i,b_j\) satisfying \(\sum_i a_i=\sum_j b_j=1\). Each society is given by its support, listing only positive-mass types; \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) is the total support size, not the number of possible types. Put \(m=\max\{|I|,|J|\}\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Acceptability is mutual, described by \(E\subseteq I\times J\); each type strictly ranks its acceptable opposite-side types. Let \(r_i(j),s_j(i)\) be their consecutive list positions, starting at \(1\). Every acceptable partner is preferred to being unmatched.
A mass assignment has real entries \(x_{ij}\geq0\) on \(E\), zero entries outside \(E\), and satisfies \(\sum_jx_{ij}\leq a_i\), \(\sum_ix_{ij}\leq b_j\).
For \((i,j)\in E\), define \(U_{ij}=a_i-\sum_hx_{ih}+\sum_{h:r_i(h)>r_i(j)}x_{ih}\) and \(V_{ij}=b_j-\sum_hx_{hj}+\sum_{h:s_j(h)>s_j(i)}x_{hj}\), with sums restricted to acceptable pairs. Stability means \(U_{ij}=0\) or \(V_{ij}=0\) for every acceptable pair.
Define \(D_I(x)=\sum_{(i,j)\in E}r_i(j)x_{ij}\) and \(D_J(x)=\sum_{(i,j)\in E}s_j(i)x_{ij}\); unmatched mass contributes zero. Given a nonnegative binary rational \(K\), decide whether a stable assignment satisfies \(\max\{D_I(x),D_J(x)\}\leq K\).
Let \(\Omega_I,\Omega_J\) be the respective minimum costs over stable assignments and \(\theta=K-\max\{\Omega_I,\Omega_J\}\). For \(\theta\geq0\), define \(p_N=\lceil N\theta\rceil\), where \(N\geq1\) is a binary-encoded integer population scale; \(N=1\) denotes normalized units. Scaling changes cost units, without imposing indivisibility. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and any parameter used.

Is this continuous decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\) or NP-hard?
If NP-hard, is it FPT parameterized by \(p_N\), and does \(N=1\) suffice or must a population scale be supplied?

Discrete Balanced Stable Marriage is reported NP-hard, and its parameterization by the threshold minus the larger side-optimal cost is W[1]-hard ([Gupta et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.09545)).
A single linear cost can be minimized over bipartite stable allocations in polynomial time ([Dean–Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf)); no result settling this exact two-sided threshold problem was identified.

Classification

The continuous decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Moreover, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard already on instances with \(p_N=1\), for every fixed positive integer \(N\). Consequently, it is not FPT parameterized by \(p_N\) unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

This holds with dyadic masses, preference lists of length at most four, and assignments allowed to split continuously. Supplying a population scale does not restore tractability under the stated assumptions: there is no requirement that positive masses be at least \(1/N\).

1. A gadget that forces endpoint choices

Fix a positive mass \(q\). There are four candidate types \(A,B,C,D\) and four vacancy types \(a,b,c,d\), each of mass \(q\). Their complete acceptable-partner lists are:

| Candidate | Preference list | Vacancy | Preference list |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(A\) | \(a\succ d\succ b\succ c\) | \(a\) | \(B\succ A\) |
| \(B\) | \(b\succ a\) | \(b\) | \(C\succ D\succ A\succ B\) |
| \(C\) | \(c\succ b\) | \(c\) | \(A\succ C\) |
| \(D\) | \(d\succ b\) | \(d\) | \(D\succ A\) |

Acceptability is mutual, and all ranks are consecutive list positions.

Characterization of every stable assignment

Because \(D\) and \(d\) rank each other first,

\[ U_{Dd}=V_{Dd}=q-x_{Dd}. \]

Stability therefore forces \(x_{Dd}=q\). The capacity constraints then give \(x_{Ad}=x_{Db}=0\).

Write \(\alpha_A,\alpha_B,\alpha_C\) for the unmatched masses of the remaining candidates. On the pair \((A,c)\), candidate \(A\) ranks \(c\) last and vacancy \(c\) ranks \(A\) first. Hence

\[ U_{Ac}=\alpha_A,\qquad V_{Ac}=q-x_{Ac}. \]

If \(\alpha_A>0\), feasibility gives \(x_{Ac}\leq q-\alpha_A\), so both quantities are positive, contradicting stability. Thus \(\alpha_A=0\). The same argument on \((B,a)\) and \((C,b)\) gives \(\alpha_B=\alpha_C=0\).

All three candidate rows therefore have total mass \(q\). Since their three vacancy columns have capacities summing to \(3q\), every column also has total mass \(q\).

Set \(t=x_{Ba}\) and \(u=x_{Ac}\). The row and column equations determine the entire assignment:

| | \(a\) | \(b\) | \(c\) | \(d\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(A\) | \(q-t\) | \(t-u\) | \(u\) | \(0\) |
| \(B\) | \(t\) | \(q-t\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(C\) | \(0\) | \(u\) | \(q-u\) | \(0\) |
| \(D\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(q\) |

Nonnegativity is equivalent to \(0\leq u\leq t\leq q\).

For \((A,b)\), the dissatisfied masses are

\[ U_{Ab}=u,\qquad V_{Ab}=q-t. \]

Its stability condition is therefore \(u=0\) or \(t=q\).

Every other acceptable pair automatically satisfies stability:

  • On \((A,a),(B,b),(C,c),(A,d)\), the vacancy has no unmatched or worse-partner mass.
  • On \((A,c),(B,a),(C,b),(D,b)\), the candidate has no unmatched or worse-partner mass.
  • On \((D,d)\), both dissatisfied masses are zero.

Thus the stable assignments are exactly

\[ \{(t,0):0\leq t\leq q\}\ \cup\ \{(q,u):0\leq u\leq q\}. \]

This explicitly includes every possible fractional stable assignment.

Costs and the strict intermediate penalty

Using the consecutive ranks in the table gives

\[ D_I=4q+3t+2u,\qquad D_J=9q-2t-3u. \]

For example, candidate \(A\)'s contribution is
\((q-t)+3(t-u)+4u=q+2t+u\); adding the other three candidate contributions gives the first formula. On the vacancy side, the contributions of \(a,b,c,d\) are respectively \(2q-t,\ 4q-t-2u,\ 2q-u,\ q\), giving the second formula.

Consequently,

\[ D_I+D_J=13q+t-u\geq13q. \]

Equality requires \(t=u\). Combined with \(u=0\) or \(t=q\), this leaves exactly two possibilities:

| Endpoint | \((t,u)\) | \(D_I\) | \(D_J\) |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | \((0,0)\) | \(4q\) | \(9q\) |
| Second | \((q,q)\) | \(9q\) | \(4q\) |

Every other stable assignment has strictly larger total cost. This is the mechanism that prevents fractional splitting from bypassing the reduction.

2. Reduction from Partition

Use the classical \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete Partition problem: given positive binary integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\), with \(W=\sum_{\ell=1}^n w_\ell\), decide whether some subset \(S\) satisfies

\[ \sum_{\ell\in S}w_\ell=W/2. \]

Fix any positive integer scale \(N\). Let \(H\) be the smallest power of two strictly greater than \(4NW\), and put

\[ q_\ell=\frac{w_\ell}{H},\qquad c_0=1-\frac{4W}{H}. \]

For each \(\ell\), create an independent copy of the gadget with mass \(q_\ell\) at each of its eight types. Add one isolated candidate–vacancy pair, each of mass \(c_0\), accepting only each other.

All masses are positive dyadic rationals. On each side their sum is

\[ 4\sum_{\ell=1}^n q_\ell+c_0=1. \]

The isolated pair must receive mass \(c_0\), contributing \(c_0\) to each cost. Set

\[ K=1+\frac{5W}{2H}. \]

For any stable assignment, the gadget inequality gives

\[ D_I+D_J \geq13\sum_{\ell=1}^n q_\ell+2c_0 =2+\frac{5W}{H} =2K. \]

If both side costs are at most \(K\), this inequality must be equality. Because each gadget's excess \(t_\ell-u_\ell\) is nonnegative, every gadget must individually attain equality. Thus every gadget is at one of its two endpoints.

Let \(S\) contain exactly the gadgets at their second endpoint, and write \(z=\sum_{\ell\in S}w_\ell\). Their combined costs are

\[ D_I=1+\frac{5z}{H},\qquad D_J=1+\frac{5(W-z)}{H}. \]

Therefore,

\[ D_I\leq K\ \Longleftrightarrow\ z\leq W/2, \qquad D_J\leq K\ \Longleftrightarrow\ z\geq W/2. \]

Both inequalities hold precisely when \(z=W/2\).

Conversely, any partition subset selects these endpoints and produces a stable assignment with both costs exactly \(K\). The reduction is therefore valid in both directions.

The constructed instance has

\[ m=4n+1,\qquad \tau=8n+2,\qquad |E|=10n+1. \]

Its encoding length is \(O(n(1+\log n+\log W+\log N))\), and the construction takes polynomial time in that length. No zero-mass or implicitly represented types are used.

3. The parameter is always one

The gadget formulas imply \(D_I\geq4q\), with equality at its first endpoint. They also imply \(D_J\geq4q\), since \(2t+3u\leq5q\), with equality at its second endpoint.

Choosing all first endpoints minimizes the candidate-side cost; choosing all second endpoints minimizes the vacancy-side cost. Hence the constructed instance satisfies

\[ \Omega_I=\Omega_J =4\sum_{\ell=1}^n q_\ell+c_0 =1. \]

It follows that

\[ \theta=\frac{5W}{2H}. \]

By the choice \(H>4NW\),

\[ 0<N\theta=\frac{5NW}{2H}<\frac58<1, \]

and consequently \(p_N=\lceil N\theta\rceil=1\).

Thus, for every fixed \(N\), the slice \(p_N=1\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. An algorithm with running time

\[ f(p_N)\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \]

would solve Partition in polynomial time on these instances. Such an FPT algorithm therefore implies \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

In particular, the parameterization is para-\(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard already for normalized units \(N=1\).

4. Membership in NP and an exact upper bound

Let \(e=|E|\). For each acceptable pair, guess which equality certifies stability: \(U_{ij}=0\) or \(V_{ij}=0\).

For a fixed collection of these choices, impose:

  • nonnegativity and all row and column capacity constraints;
  • the chosen stability equalities;
  • \(D_I\leq K\) and \(D_J\leq K\).

This is a rational linear feasibility problem with \(e\) variables and \(O(e+\tau)\) constraints. Its feasible region is bounded because \(0\leq x_{ij}\leq\min(a_i,b_j)\). If nonempty, it has a rational vertex of polynomial encoding length: after clearing denominators, the usual determinant bounds on a basic feasible solution give this bit-length bound.

A certificate consists of the equality choices and such a rational assignment. Exact rational arithmetic verifies all constraints in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time. Hence the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), completing the proof of \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

Enumerating all equality choices also gives an exact algorithm running in

\[ O\!\left(2^{|E|}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\right). \]

Since \(|E|\leq\min\{m^2,\lfloor\tau^2/4\rfloor\}\), this is FPT in support size, despite the hardness for \(p_N\).

5. What supplying a scale changes

Under the stated model, supplying \(N\) merely rescales costs. The reduction above works for every supplied \(N\) while keeping \(p_N=1\).

A further promise such as \(Na_i,Nb_j\in\mathbb Z\) would impose mass granularity, even while assignments remained divisible. That promise is absent here; the result above settles the problem as stated.

There is no conflict with the polynomial algorithm for a single linear cost over bipartite stable allocations proved by [Dean–Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf). In this reduction, minimizing \(D_I+D_J\) is easy: choose either endpoint of each gadget. Requiring both side costs to meet the same threshold encodes Partition among those minimum-total-cost allocations.
