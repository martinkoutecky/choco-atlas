The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many alternatives at rational positions on a line and, for each of \(k\) districts, a finite-support rational probability measure \(\mu_d\) over voter locations, let \(q_d\) be its left \(\alpha\)-quantile. Each district selects the alternative preferred by a voter at \(q_d\), using the paper's tie-breaking, and the mechanism returns the rightmost district representative \(w\). Does every such society satisfy \(\max_d \int |z-w|\,d\mu_d(z) \le \max\{\frac{3-\alpha}{1-\alpha},\frac{2}{\alpha}-1\}\min_{x\in A}\max_d\int |z-x|\,d\mu_d(z))\? In particular, does the bound become 2+\sqrt{5}\) at \(\alpha=(3-\sqrt{5})/2\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\subset\mathbb Q\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) alternative positions, and let \(k\geq1\) districts have finitely supported probability measures \(\mu_d\) on \(\mathbb Q\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; a type specifies a district and voter location. Thus \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types, and masses sum to \(1\) in each district. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L,k\) and any additional parameters.
For each real \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), define \(q_d=\min\{z\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu_d):\mu_d(({-\infty},z])\geq\alpha\}\). Voters rank alternatives by increasing absolute distance, resolving equal distances by arbitrary fixed strict tie orders supplied for each type.
Each district selects the highest-ranked alternative \(y_d\) at \(q_d\), and the winner is \(w=\max_d y_d\). Define \(C(x)=\max_d\int |z-x|\,d\mu_d(z)\), \(C^*=\min_{x\in A}C(x)\), and \(B(\alpha)=\max\{(3-\alpha)/(1-\alpha),\,2/\alpha-1\}\).

Does every such society, for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) and every permitted tie order, satisfy \(C(w)\leq B(\alpha)C^*\), including when \(C^*=0\)?
In particular, does \(C(w)\leq(2+\sqrt5)C^*\) always hold at \(\alpha=(3-\sqrt5)/2\)?

For finite electorates, the corresponding order-statistic mechanism has a stated max-of-average distortion bound \(B(\alpha)\), giving \(2+\sqrt5\) at the specified parameter ([published result](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/35118/1/1-s2.0-S0167637723000469-main.pdf)).
No source explicitly treating this finite-support mass formulation was located in the literature search.

Conclusion

Yes. For every society in the question, every real \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and every permitted tie order,
\[ C(w)\leq \max\left\{\frac{3-\alpha}{1-\alpha},\frac{2}{\alpha}-1\right\}C^*. \]
This includes \(C^*=0\). Consequently, the bound at \(\alpha=(3-\sqrt5)/2\) is \(2+\sqrt5\).

The finite-electorate analogue appears in Theorem 3.1 of the [published paper](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/35118/1/1-s2.0-S0167637723000469-main.pdf). The following proof works directly with masses, without expanding the support into individual voters.

Quantile and geometric facts

Write \(f_d(x)=\int |z-x|\,d\mu_d(z)\), so \(C(x)=\max_d f_d(x)\). Because \(A\) is finite and nonempty, choose \(o\in A\) with \(C(o)=C^*\).

Fix a district \(d\). Its finite support and total mass \(1\) ensure that \(q_d\) exists. Its defining minimality gives
\[ \mu_d((-\infty,q_d))<\alpha \quad\text{and}\quad \mu_d((-\infty,q_d])\geq\alpha. \]
Indeed, if there is a support point immediately preceding \(q_d\), its cumulative mass is strictly below \(\alpha\); otherwise the mass strictly below \(q_d\) is zero. Consequently,
\[ \mu_d([q_d,\infty))>1-\alpha. \]

We also use the following elementary fact: for real \(a<b\),
\[ |z-a|\leq |z-b| \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z\leq \frac{a+b}{2}. \]
To verify it, square both nonnegative sides and cancel \(z^2\); the resulting inequality is \(2(b-a)z\leq(b-a)(a+b)\).

Since \(y_d\) is a highest-ranked alternative at \(q_d\), distance-based ranking implies
\[ |q_d-y_d|\leq |q_d-x| \qquad\text{for every }x\in A. \]
Arbitrary tie orders preserve this weak inequality. No further property of the tie orders will be needed.

Case \(o<w\)

Let \(D=w-o>0\). Since \(w=\max_d y_d\), some district \(e\) has \(y_e=w\). The preceding distance inequality, applied to \(o\), gives
\[ |q_e-w|\leq |q_e-o|, \]
hence \(q_e\geq(o+w)/2\).

Every \(z\geq q_e\) therefore satisfies \(|z-o|\geq D/2\). Integrating over that set yields
\[ C^*=C(o)\geq f_e(o) \geq \mu_e([q_e,\infty))\frac D2 \geq (1-\alpha)\frac D2. \]
Since \(1-\alpha>0\), it follows that \(D\leq 2C^*/(1-\alpha)\).

For every district \(d\), the triangle inequality and total mass \(1\) give \(f_d(w)\leq f_d(o)+D\). Taking the maximum over districts,
\[ C(w)\leq C^*+D \leq \left(1+\frac{2}{1-\alpha}\right)C^* =\frac{3-\alpha}{1-\alpha}C^*. \]

Case \(w<o\)

Let \(D=o-w>0\), and fix any district \(d\). Because \(y_d\leq w<o\), the inequality \(|q_d-y_d|\leq|q_d-o|\) gives
\[ q_d\leq\frac{y_d+o}{2}\leq\frac{w+o}{2}. \]

Set \(S_d=(-\infty,q_d]\) and \(p_d=\mu_d(S_d)\). The quantile property gives \(p_d\geq\alpha>0\).

For every \(z\in S_d\), we have \(z\leq(w+o)/2\). Thus both
\[ |z-w|\leq|z-o| \quad\text{and}\quad |z-o|\geq D/2 \]
hold. On the complement of \(S_d\), the triangle inequality gives \(|z-w|\leq|z-o|+D\). Integrating these pointwise inequalities,
\[ f_d(w)\leq f_d(o)+(1-p_d)D, \qquad f_d(o)\geq p_dD/2. \]

Because \(p_d>0\), the second inequality implies \(D\leq2f_d(o)/p_d\). Substituting into the first,
\[ f_d(w) \leq \left(1+\frac{2(1-p_d)}{p_d}\right)f_d(o) =\left(\frac{2}{p_d}-1\right)f_d(o) \leq\left(\frac{2}{\alpha}-1\right)f_d(o). \]
The last step uses \(p_d\geq\alpha\) and \(f_d(o)\geq0\). Taking the maximum over districts proves
\[ C(w)\leq\left(\frac{2}{\alpha}-1\right)C^*. \]

If \(w=o\), then \(C(w)=C^*\). These three cases establish the asserted bound.

Zero optimal cost and the specified parameter

The proof never divides by \(C^*\), so it already includes zero optimal cost. Explicitly, if \(C^*=0\), then \(f_d(o)=0\) for every district. Each integral is a finite sum of nonnegative terms with positive support masses; therefore every support location equals \(o\). Since \(o\in A\) and alternative positions are distinct, \(o\) is the unique alternative at distance zero. Every district selects \(o\), and \(C(w)=0\).

For \(\alpha_0=(3-\sqrt5)/2\in(0,1)\), direct substitution gives
\[ \frac{3-\alpha_0}{1-\alpha_0} =\frac{2}{\alpha_0}-1 =2+\sqrt5. \]
Hence \(C(w)\leq(2+\sqrt5)C^*\).

Computation from the support

No voter replication is needed. With rational \(\alpha\) of bit length \(b\), one can sort support locations within districts, accumulate masses to find the quantiles, scan the alternatives to select each representative, and evaluate every alternative’s district costs.

This uses \(O(\tau\log\tau+km+m\tau)\) exact rational arithmetic and comparison operations. With reduced rational arithmetic, intermediate bit lengths are polynomial in \(L+b\); thus the total bit running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k,b\).

For the fixed parameter \(\alpha_0\), comparisons are also exact: for a rational cumulative mass \(r\in[0,1]\),
\[ r\geq\alpha_0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (3-2r)^2\leq5, \]
because \(3-2r>0\). The resulting bit running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k\). For an arbitrary real \(\alpha\), a computational running time requires a specified representation or comparison oracle; the proved inequality requires neither.
