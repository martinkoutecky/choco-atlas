The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite item categories \(G\), valuation types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), weights \(\eta_t\), and monotone submodular valuations \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), choose masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) of type-\(t\) agents receiving each integral bundle \(S\subseteq G\), subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le b_j\), maximizing \(\left(\prod_{t,S}v_t(S)^{\eta_t x_{t,S}}\right)^{1/\sum_t\eta_t\mu_t}\), or determine the best achievable approximation factor.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a set of \(m\geq 1\) item categories. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\geq 1\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Normalize \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).
Each type has a positive rational weight \(\eta_t\) and a nonnegative monotone submodular valuation \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\), accessed through a value oracle. Supplies \(b_j\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\) are per capita.
An allocation specifies real masses \(x_{t,S}\geq 0\) for integral bundles \(S\subseteq G\), with \(\sum_{S\subseteq G}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\leq b_j\).
Its welfare is \(W(x)=\left(\prod_{t,S}v_t(S)^{\eta_t x_{t,S}}\right)^{1/\sum_t\eta_t\mu_t}\). Omit factors with zero mass; any positive mass receiving a zero-valued bundle makes welfare zero. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_x W(x)\).
An approximation factor \(\alpha\geq 1\) means welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/\alpha\). State time and oracle-query bounds in \(m,\tau,L\) and any approximation parameters, where \(L\) is the encoding length of the rational input plus a uniform bound on the bit length of oracle answers.

What is the optimal polynomial-time approximation factor for this mass-allocation problem? In particular, can a factor strictly below \(\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)\) be achieved, or is achieving such a factor NP-hard?

For discrete indivisible-item allocation, approximation within \(\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)\) is ruled out unless \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\), even with identical valuations and equal weights. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.12541).
For the continuous special case \(\mu_t=1/\tau\) and \(b_j=1/\tau\), a configuration-relaxation result gives factor \(\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)+\epsilon\) for every fixed \(\epsilon>0\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02942).

Result

The optimal polynomial-time approximation factor is \(\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)\).

More precisely:

  • A deterministic algorithm achieves factor \(\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)\), with running time and value-oracle query count \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\). It outputs a feasible allocation supported on at most \(m+\tau\) bundles.
  • For every fixed \(\alpha<\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)\), achieving factor \(\alpha\) is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard. This holds already for equal weights, equal masses \(\mu_t=1/\tau\), equal supplies \(b_j=1/\tau\), and explicitly represented coverage valuations.

The hardness proof below bounds every feasible mass allocation directly. It therefore survives the additional freedom to split type masses.

1. Positive welfare and the configuration linear program

Define
\[ \]
\(A=\sum_t\eta_t\mu_t\), \(q_t=\eta_t\mu_t/A\), and \(y_{t,S}=x_{t,S}/\mu_t\). Thus \(\sum_tq_t=1\), and each \(y_t\) is a probability distribution over bundles.

First, positive-welfare feasibility can be decided in polynomial time.

For a type with \(v_t(\varnothing)>0\), assigning its entire mass the empty bundle suffices. For every remaining type, submodularity gives

\(v_t(S)\leq\sum_{j\in S}v_t(\{j\})\),

because \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\). Consequently, every positive-valued bundle contains a positive-valued singleton.

It follows that a positive-welfare allocation exists exactly when there are nonnegative singleton masses \(z_{t,j}\), for the types with \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\), satisfying

\(\sum_{j:v_t(\{j\})>0}z_{t,j}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_tz_{t,j}\leq b_j\).

Necessity follows by replacing each positive-valued bundle by one positive-valued singleton it contains. Sufficiency follows by allocating the indicated singletons. This is a capacitated bipartite-flow feasibility problem. It requires at most \(\tau(m+1)\) valuation queries and polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

If it is infeasible, then \(\mathrm{OPT}=0\), and assigning everyone the empty bundle meets every approximation guarantee. Henceforth suppose it is feasible.

Restrict configurations to bundles with positive value. The logarithm of optimal welfare is exactly the optimum \(F\) of

\(\displaystyle \max_y\sum_tq_t\sum_{S:v_t(S)>0}y_{t,S}\ln v_t(S)\),

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S:v_t(S)>0}y_{t,S}=1\quad\text{for every }t,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_t\sum_{S\ni j}y_{t,S}\leq b_j\quad\text{for every }j,\)

and \(y_{t,S}\geq0\).

In particular, \(\mathrm{OPT}=\exp(F)\). This finite linear program is feasible and bounded. Its feasible solutions are the desired mass allocations themselves.

The dual has item prices \(p_j\geq0\) and unrestricted type variables \(u_t\):

\(\displaystyle \min_{u,p}\sum_tu_t+\sum_jb_jp_j,\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle u_t+\mu_t\sum_{j\in S}p_j\geq q_t\ln v_t(S)\quad\text{whenever }v_t(S)>0.\)

An approximate separation oracle therefore needs to maximize, approximately,

\(\displaystyle q_t\ln v_t(S)-\mu_t\sum_{j\in S}p_j.\)

The configuration-LP approach is also the basis of the cited relaxation result. The following argument handles arbitrary rational masses and supplies. [Feng, Li, and Li, Lemma 3.1 and Appendix B.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02942).

2. A finite-ground-set margin in submodular knapsack

Write

\(\rho=1-\mathrm{e}^{-1}\), and \(\beta=\frac23-\rho=\mathrm{e}^{-1}-\frac13>0\).

We use the following strengthening of the usual knapsack guarantee.

Lemma. For a nonnegative monotone submodular function on at most \(m\) elements, subject to one nonnegative rational knapsack constraint, a deterministic algorithm returns a feasible set of value at least

\(\displaystyle \left(\rho+\frac{\beta}{m}\right)\)

times the optimum. It uses \(O(m^5)\) value queries and polynomial bit complexity.

Proof. Use Sviridenko’s algorithm, which enumerates feasible sets of size at most three and greedily completes the triples. Its standard guarantee is \(\rho\). We retain the positive term present in its analysis. [Sviridenko, Theorem 1 and its proof](https://thibaut.horel.org/submodularity/papers/sviridenko2004.pdf).

Suppose first that the function \(f\) is normalized and costs are positive. Let \(O\) be an optimal set. If \(|O|\leq3\), enumeration finds an optimum.

Otherwise, let \(Y\) consist of the first three elements of \(O\), ordered by successively largest marginal value within \(O\). The analysis of the run seeded by \(Y\) gives either an optimum, or a feasible set \(T\supseteq Y\) and an auxiliary element \(e\notin T\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle f(T\cup\{e\})-f(Y)\geq\rho\bigl(f(O)-f(Y)\bigr),\)

and

\(\displaystyle f(T\cup\{e\})-f(T)\leq\frac{f(Y)}3.\)

These are the estimates immediately preceding the concluding inequality in Sviridenko’s proof. Subtracting the second estimate from the first gives

\(\displaystyle f(T)\geq\rho f(O)+\left(\frac23-\rho\right)f(Y)=\rho f(O)+\beta f(Y).\)

The first element of \(Y\) maximizes singleton value within \(O\). By submodularity and normalization,

\(\displaystyle f(O)\leq\sum_{j\in O}f(\{j\})\leq |O|\max_{j\in O}f(\{j\})\leq m f(Y).\)

Thus \(f(T)\geq(\rho+\beta/m)f(O)\).

For a nonzero baseline, apply the normalized result to \(f-f(\varnothing)\). Restoring the baseline preserves the guarantee because \(\rho+\beta/m<1\).

All zero-cost elements can first be included and contracted. The residual function remains monotone and submodular, and has at most \(m\) elements. If none remain, optimization is exact. Finally, rational costs can be scaled to integers without changing feasibility; their resulting bit lengths are polynomial in the original encoding length. \(\square\)

The margin \(\beta/m\) will absorb numerical and linear-programming errors. Its dependence on \(m\) is consistent with hardness against every fixed improvement over \(\rho\).

3. Approximate logarithmic demand

Choose positive rational numbers \(s,r,\delta,h\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \frac{\beta}{4m}\leq s\leq\frac{\beta}{2m},\qquad \rho+s\leq r\leq\rho+2s,\qquad \delta=\frac{s}{4},\qquad h=\frac{s}{32}.\)

Such numbers can be computed using polynomial-time approximations to \(\mathrm{e}\), with \(O(\log m)\) bits of precision. In particular,

\(\displaystyle r\leq\rho+\frac{\beta}{m}.\)

We first construct a covering subroutine.

Covering subroutine. Given nonnegative rational item costs \(c_j\) and a rational target \(V>0\) with \(v(G)\geq V\), find a set \(R\) such that

\(\displaystyle v(R)\geq rV,\qquad c(R)\leq\min_{S:v(S)\geq V}c(S).\)

To do this, scale costs to nonnegative integers and binary-search the budget. At each budget, run the preceding knapsack algorithm and declare success if its output has value at least \(rV\).

Let \(C^*\) be the minimum cost of a set attaining value at least \(V\). Every budget at least \(C^*\) succeeds, by the knapsack guarantee. Maintain an unsuccessful lower budget and a successful upper budget. Every unsuccessful budget is strictly below \(C^*\); therefore, when the two integer budgets become adjacent, the successful upper budget is at most \(C^*\).

This argument does not require the particular algorithm outputs to be monotone in the budget. Zero-cost solutions are checked first. The number of knapsack calls is polynomial in the cost encoding length.

Now consider logarithmic demand for a valuation \(v\) and arbitrary nonnegative rational costs \(c\). Every positive oracle value lies between \(2^{-L}\) and \(2^L\). Enumerate targets

\(\displaystyle V_a=2^{-L}(1+\delta)^a\)

up to \(2^L\), skipping targets exceeding \(v(G)\), and run the covering subroutine for each target.

For every positive-valued bundle \(S\), one target satisfies

\(\displaystyle V_a\leq v(S)<(1+\delta)V_a.\)

Its returned set \(R\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle c(R)\leq c(S),\qquad v(R)\geq\frac{r}{1+\delta}v(S).\)

Consequently, writing \(D=\ln((1+\delta)/r)\),

\(\displaystyle \ln v(R)-c(R)\geq\ln v(S)-c(S)-D.\)

Thus the candidate list contains a logarithmic-demand solution within additive error \(D\).

There are \(O(1+L/\delta)\) targets. If each item cost has at most \(B\) bits, scaling and binary search use \(O(1+mB+\log m)\) knapsack calls per target. Hence the query bound for one candidate list is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(m^5(1+L/\delta)(1+mB+\log m)\right).\)

For dual separation of type \(t\), use costs \(c_j=\mu_tp_j/q_t\). The resulting guarantee is

\(\displaystyle q_t\ln v_t(R)-\mu_tp(R)\geq q_t\ln v_t(S)-\mu_tp(S)-q_tD.\)

4. Polynomial-time LP recovery and the exact factor

Approximate every positive logarithmic value by a rational number \(\ell_{t,S}\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle |\ell_{t,S}-\ln v_t(S)|\leq h.\)

Let \(\widehat F\) be the optimum of the rational configuration LP with objective coefficients \(q_t\ell_{t,S}\). Since \(\sum_tq_t=1\) and every \(y_t\) is a probability distribution,

\(\displaystyle |\widehat F-F|\leq h,\)

and the two objectives differ by at most \(h\) at every feasible allocation.

For any dual candidate \((u,p)\), generate the demand candidate lists from Section 3. Return any genuinely violated rational constraint. If none of those constraints is violated, then, for every positive-valued bundle \(S\),

\(\displaystyle q_t\ell_{t,S}-\mu_tp(S)\leq u_t+q_t(D+2h).\)

Indeed, passing from a logarithm to its rational approximation loses at most \(q_th\) on each side of the demand comparison.

Choose a rational \(\Delta\) with

\(\displaystyle D+2h\leq\Delta\leq D+3h.\)

Whenever the oracle accepts a candidate, shifting its type variables to \(u_t+q_t\Delta\) makes it feasible for the entire rational dual. This increases the dual objective by exactly

\(\displaystyle \sum_tq_t\Delta=\Delta.\)

This is the hypothesis needed for approximate dual separation with primal recovery: the rational ellipsoid method produces a feasible restricted primal solution \(\widehat y\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \widehat F(\widehat y)\geq\widehat F-\Delta-h.\)

For completeness, the recovery argument uses objective cutoffs. At a cutoff \(C\), acceptance certifies \(\widehat F\leq C+\Delta\), through the shifted feasible dual. Failure of the rational feasibility procedure gives a finite set of generated configuration constraints whose restricted primal optimum exceeds \(C\), within the chosen numerical tolerance. Binary-searching between these outcomes, retaining the generated columns, and solving the resulting explicit restricted LP gives the stated bound; the feasibility and search tolerances are chosen to total at most \(h\).

The standard rational-LP hypotheses hold here:

  • There are only \(m+\tau\) dual variables.
  • All rational constraint coefficients have uniformly polynomial bit length.
  • The singleton-flow allocation from Section 1 supplies a feasible restricted primal.
  • All objective values lie in a known interval of length \(O(L)\).
  • Determinant bounds provide a box of radius \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)}\) containing an optimal dual solution for every restricted LP containing the seed columns.

Thus ellipsoid separation and primal recovery require polynomially many calls, and the queried prices have polynomial bit length. No expansion by mass denominators is used.

Returning to the true logarithmic objective gives

\(\displaystyle \ln W(\widehat y)\geq F-D-6h.\)

We now check that this loss is strictly less than \(\ln(1/\rho)\). Since \(\rho+s<1\),

\(\displaystyle \ln(r/\rho)\geq\ln(1+s/\rho)\geq\frac{s}{\rho+s}\geq s.\)

Also, \(\ln(1+\delta)\leq\delta=s/4\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle \ln(1/\rho)-D=\ln(r/\rho)-\ln(1+\delta)\geq\frac{3s}{4}.\)

But \(6h=3s/16<3s/4\). Hence

\(\displaystyle W(\widehat y)\geq\rho\,\mathrm{OPT}=\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)}.\)

All precision parameters have inverses \(O(m)\). Combining the oracle bounds with polynomial-time ellipsoid optimization gives running time and oracle-query count

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L).\)

Finally, select an optimal basic solution of the explicit restricted LP. It has at most \(m+\tau\) positive configuration variables, because there are \(\tau\) type equalities and \(m\) supply inequalities. Its masses are rational with polynomial encoding length. Setting \(x_{t,S}=\mu_ty_{t,S}\) yields the required output allocation.

5. Hardness that survives mass splitting

We use the following strengthened Max-Cover gap.

For every fixed \(c_0>0\) and \(\gamma>0\), it is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard to distinguish set systems partitioned into \(k\) equally sized groups between:

  • YES: one set from each group covers the universe.
  • NO: every collection of \(\ell\leq c_0k\) sets covers at most a fraction \(1-(1-1/k)^\ell+\gamma\) of the universe.

The groups can have the same constant number \(n\) of sets. This precise form is stated in Section 2 of Kapralov, Post, and Vondrák. [Online submodular welfare maximization: Greedy is optimal](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kapralov/papers/online-alloc.pdf).

Set \(c_0=3\), and write the sets as \(A_{a,r}\), where \(a\) indexes the \(k\) groups and \(r\in\mathbb Z_n\).

Construct \(m=kn\) item categories \((a,r)\), and \(\tau=n\) types indexed by \(t\in\mathbb Z_n\). Set

\(\displaystyle \mu_t=\frac1n,\qquad \eta_t=1,\qquad b_j=\frac1n.\)

Define

\(\displaystyle v_t(S)=\frac{\left|\bigcup_{(a,r)\in S}A_{a,r+t}\right|}{|U|},\)

where addition in the second index is modulo \(n\).

These are nonnegative monotone submodular coverage valuations: the marginal contribution of an item is the number of newly covered universe elements, which decreases as the existing bundle grows. Their value oracles can be implemented in polynomial time from the explicit set system, with polynomial answer bit length.

YES instances

Choose covering sets \(A_{a,r_a}\), one from each group. Give the entire mass of type \(t\) the bundle

\(\displaystyle B_t=\{(a,r_a-t):a\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\}.\)

For each group \(a\), the indices \(r_a-t\) run through all of \(\mathbb Z_n\). Hence every item belongs to exactly one \(B_t\), and its consumption is exactly \(1/n\). The allocation is feasible.

Each type’s bundle covers the universe, so every assigned value is \(1\). Since all valuations are at most \(1\),

\(\mathrm{OPT}=1.\)

NO instances: a bound for every bundle size

Assume \(k\geq2\), and define

\(\displaystyle a_k=(1-1/k)^k,\qquad \rho_k=1-a_k,\qquad d_k=-a_k\ln(1-1/k)>0.\)

The function

\(\displaystyle g_k(z)=1-(1-1/k)^z\)

is increasing and concave on the nonnegative reals. Its tangent at \(k\) is

\(\displaystyle T_k(z)=\rho_k+d_k(z-k).\)

Concavity implies \(g_k(z)\leq T_k(z)\). Thus, for every bundle of size at most \(3k\), the NO guarantee gives

\(\displaystyle v_t(S)\leq T_k(|S|)+\gamma.\)

The same bound holds for larger bundles. Indeed,

\(\displaystyle kd_k=a_k\bigl[-k\ln(1-1/k)\bigr]\geq a_k,\)

using \(-\ln(1-u)\geq u\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle T_k(3k)=1-a_k+2kd_k\geq1+a_k>1.\)

Since \(T_k\) is increasing and \(v_t(S)\leq1\), all bundles satisfy

\(\displaystyle v_t(S)\leq\rho_k+d_k(|S|-k)+\gamma.\)

NO instances: a bound for every mass allocation

Let \(x\) be any feasible allocation, allowing arbitrary real masses. Its average bundle size satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}|S|=\sum_j\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\leq\sum_jb_j=\frac{kn}{n}=k.\)

Also, \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}=1\). Averaging the preceding affine bound therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\leq\rho_k+\gamma.\)

With equal weights, weighted AM–GM gives

\(\displaystyle W(x)\leq\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\leq\rho_k+\gamma.\)

If a positive mass receives zero value, the first inequality holds because \(W(x)=0\). Otherwise it follows from concavity of the logarithm.

This bound applies directly to every feasible mixture.

Separating the cases

Fix any \(\alpha<1/\rho\). Then \(1/\alpha>\rho\). Since \(\rho_k\) converges to \(\rho\), choose a sufficiently small fixed rational \(\gamma>0\) and a sufficiently large constant \(K\) such that

\(\displaystyle \rho_k+\gamma<1/\alpha\quad\text{for all }k\geq K.\)

The gap remains \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard under the restriction \(k\geq K\): instances with \(k<K\) can be decided by enumerating the possible \(k\)-set covers in polynomial time, because \(K\) is fixed.

On a YES instance, an \(\alpha\)-approximation must return welfare at least \(1/\alpha\), and therefore arithmetic average value at least \(1/\alpha\). On a NO instance, every feasible allocation has arithmetic average value at most \(\rho_k+\gamma<1/\alpha\).

A fixed rational threshold between these bounds distinguishes the cases. For a listed rational allocation, its arithmetic average value is computable directly in polynomial time. Thus such an approximation algorithm would solve the \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard gap problem.

Conclusion

Factor \(\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)\) is achievable in polynomial time for arbitrary binary-encoded rational masses, weights, and supplies. Every fixed strictly smaller factor is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard, already in the equal-mass, equal-supply special case with coverage valuations.
