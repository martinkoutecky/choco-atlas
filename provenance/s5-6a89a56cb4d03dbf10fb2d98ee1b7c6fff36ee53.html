The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite item categories \(G\), valuation types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), weights \(\eta_t\), and monotone submodular valuations \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), choose masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) of type-\(t\) agents receiving each integral bundle \(S\subseteq G\), subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le b_j\), maximizing \(\left(\prod_{t,S}v_t(S)^{\eta_t x_{t,S}}\right)^{1/\sum_t\eta_t\mu_t}\), or determine the best achievable approximation factor.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a set of \(m\geq 1\) item categories. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\geq 1\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Normalize \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).
Each type has a positive rational weight \(\eta_t\) and a nonnegative monotone submodular valuation \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\), accessed through a value oracle. Supplies \(b_j\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\) are per capita.
An allocation specifies real masses \(x_{t,S}\geq 0\) for integral bundles \(S\subseteq G\), with \(\sum_{S\subseteq G}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\leq b_j\).
Its welfare is \(W(x)=\left(\prod_{t,S}v_t(S)^{\eta_t x_{t,S}}\right)^{1/\sum_t\eta_t\mu_t}\). Omit factors with zero mass; any positive mass receiving a zero-valued bundle makes welfare zero. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_x W(x)\).
An approximation factor \(\alpha\geq 1\) means welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/\alpha\). State time and oracle-query bounds in \(m,\tau,L\) and any approximation parameters, where \(L\) is the encoding length of the rational input plus a uniform bound on the bit length of oracle answers.

What is the optimal polynomial-time approximation factor for this mass-allocation problem? In particular, can a factor strictly below \(\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)\) be achieved, or is achieving such a factor NP-hard?

For discrete indivisible-item allocation, approximation within \(\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)\) is ruled out unless \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\), even with identical valuations and equal weights. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.12541).
For the continuous special case \(\mu_t=1/\tau\) and \(b_j=1/\tau\), a configuration-relaxation result gives factor \(\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)+\epsilon\) for every fixed \(\epsilon>0\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02942).

1. Result and computational convention

Write
$$ \rho=1-\mathrm{e}^{-1}. $$

The optimal constant approximation factor is
$$ \boxed{\frac{1}{\rho}=\frac{\mathrm{e}}{\mathrm{e}-1}}. $$

More precisely:

  • A deterministic algorithm attains this factor in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ time and value-oracle queries. It outputs at most $m+\tau$ occupied type–bundle pairs, with rational masses of polynomial encoding length.
  • For every fixed $\alpha<\mathrm{e}/(\mathrm{e}-1)$, obtaining an $\alpha$-approximation is $\mathsf{NP}$-hard. This holds already for equal weights, masses $\mu_t=1/\tau$, supplies $b_j=1/\tau$, and explicitly represented coverage valuations.

Here, polynomial-time allocation algorithms use the usual output convention: they list occupied type–bundle pairs and binary rational masses. The mathematical optimum continues to allow arbitrary real masses. The hardness proof bounds every feasible real allocation, and uses the output convention only to compute its distinguishing statistic.

The main repair to the previous argument is an explicit LP recovery procedure. Its termination certificate is a volume certificate with a specified objective margin; it does not assert that a lower-dimensional feasible region is empty.

2. Positive welfare and the configuration LP

Supplies can first be replaced by $\min\{b_j,1\}$: because total population mass is one, no allocation consumes more than one unit of any category.

For a type with $v_t(\varnothing)=0$, submodularity implies
$$ v_t(S)\leq\sum_{j\in S}v_t(\{j\}). $$
To verify this, order the elements of $S$ and telescope their marginal contributions. Each marginal is at most the corresponding marginal at the empty set.

Consequently, every positive-valued bundle contains a positive-valued singleton. A positive-welfare allocation exists exactly when nonnegative masses $z_{t,j}$ satisfy
$$ \sum_{j:v_t(\{j\})>0}z_{t,j}=\mu_t \quad\text{for every }t\text{ with }v_t(\varnothing)=0, $$
and
$$ \sum_{t:v_t(\varnothing)=0}z_{t,j}\leq b_j \quad\text{for every }j. $$

Necessity follows by replacing each occupied positive-valued bundle by one positive-valued singleton it contains; this cannot increase consumption. Types with positive empty-bundle value can instead receive the empty bundle. Conversely, these singleton masses, together with those empty-bundle assignments, give positive welfare.

This is a rational capacitated-flow problem. It uses at most $\tau(m+1)$ valuation queries and polynomial time. If it is infeasible, then $\mathrm{OPT}=0$, and the all-empty allocation suffices.

Henceforth suppose positive welfare is feasible. Define
$$ A=\sum_t\eta_t\mu_t,\qquad q_t=\frac{\eta_t\mu_t}{A},\qquad y_{t,S}=\frac{x_{t,S}}{\mu_t}. $$
All $q_t$ are positive and $\sum_tq_t=1$. Restrict configurations to bundles with positive value. The logarithm of welfare is
$$ F(y)=\sum_tq_t\sum_{S:v_t(S)>0}y_{t,S}\ln v_t(S). $$

Thus $\ln\mathrm{OPT}$ is the optimum $F^*$ of the finite LP
$$ \begin{aligned} \max\quad& \sum_tq_t\sum_{S:v_t(S)>0}y_{t,S}\ln v_t(S),\\ \text{subject to}\quad& \sum_{S:v_t(S)>0}y_{t,S}=1 &&\text{for every }t,\\ & \sum_t\mu_t\sum_{S\ni j}y_{t,S}\leq b_j &&\text{for every }j,\\ &y_{t,S}\geq0. \end{aligned} $$

The feasible region is compact and nonempty, so its optimum is attained. Excluding zero-valued bundles loses no positive-welfare optimum.

Its dual is
$$ \begin{aligned} \min\quad&\sum_tu_t+\sum_jb_jp_j,\\ \text{subject to}\quad& u_t+\mu_t\sum_{j\in S}p_j\geq q_t\ln v_t(S) &&\text{whenever }v_t(S)>0,\\ &p_j\geq0. \end{aligned} $$
The variables $u_t$ are unrestricted. Approximate separation will therefore require approximate maximization of
$$ \ln v_t(S)-\frac{\mu_t}{q_t}\sum_{j\in S}p_j. $$

3. A finite-size knapsack margin and logarithmic demand

3.1. Knapsack lemma

Let
$$ \beta=\frac23-\rho=\mathrm{e}^{-1}-\frac13>0. $$

Lemma. For a nonnegative monotone submodular function on at most $m$ elements, under one nonnegative rational knapsack constraint, a deterministic algorithm returns a feasible set whose value is at least
$$ \left(\rho+\frac{\beta}{m}\right) $$
times the optimum. It uses $O(m^5)$ value queries and polynomial bit complexity.

Proof. Use the enumeration-and-density algorithm of [Sviridenko](https://thibaut.horel.org/submodularity/papers/sviridenko2004.pdf): enumerate feasible sets of cardinality at most three, and greedily complete each triple by repeatedly considering the remaining element of largest marginal-value-to-cost ratio, adding it if feasible and otherwise discarding it.

First suppose the function $f$ is normalized and all costs are positive. Let $O$ be optimal. If $|O|\leq3$, enumeration finds an optimum.

Otherwise, choose the first three elements of $O$ successively by largest marginal contribution within $O$, and let their set be $Y$. Write
$$ M=f(O)-f(Y),\qquad B'=B-c(Y)>0. $$
Consider the greedy run seeded by $Y$.

If no element of $O$ is rejected, the final set contains $O$ and is optimal. Otherwise, let $e\in O$ be the first rejected element of $O$, and let $T$ be the set just before its rejection.

Before this rejection, every element of $O$ outside the current set remains available. If the current set is $T'$ and the next considered element has density $d$, submodularity and the density choice give
$$ M\leq f(T')-f(Y) +\sum_{j\in O\setminus T'}f(j\mid T') \leq f(T')-f(Y)+B'd. $$

Ignore previously rejected elements outside $O$. Interpolate the gains of the accepted elements, followed by the entire marginal contribution of $e$, linearly over their costs. Let $H(z)$ denote this interpolated gain. On every segment, its slope is the density $d$ of that segment, and
$$ H'(z)\geq\frac{M-H(z)}{B'}. $$
Indeed, the preceding inequality holds at the segment's start, and $H$ only increases within the segment. Integrating, or differentiating $\mathrm{e}^{z/B'}(M-H(z))$, yields
$$ H(z)\geq M(1-\mathrm{e}^{-z/B'}). $$
Because $e$ is infeasible, the total interpolated cost exceeds $B'$. Therefore
$$ f(T\cup\{e\})-f(Y)\geq\rho\bigl(f(O)-f(Y)\bigr). $$

Let the three chosen marginal contributions be $a_1,a_2,a_3$. Their greedy ordering and submodularity imply
$$ a_1\geq a_2\geq a_3. $$
Since $e\in O\setminus Y$ and $T\supseteq Y$, its marginal at $T$ is at most $a_3$, hence at most $f(Y)/3$. It follows that
$$ f(T)\geq \rho f(O)+\left(\frac23-\rho\right)f(Y). $$
Moreover,
$$ f(O)\leq\sum_{j\in O}f(\{j\}) \leq m\max_{j\in O}f(\{j\}) \leq m f(Y). $$
The required guarantee follows.

For a nonzero baseline, apply this argument to $f-f(\varnothing)$ and restore the baseline. This preserves the guarantee because $\rho+\beta/m<1$. Include and contract all zero-cost elements first. Rational costs can be scaled to integers using a common denominator of polynomial bit length.

There are $O(m^3)$ seeds and $O(m^2)$ marginal queries per greedy run, proving the query bound. All comparisons involve polynomial-length rationals. $\square$

3.2. A covering subroutine

Choose positive rational parameters satisfying
$$ \frac{\beta}{4m}\leq s\leq\frac{\beta}{2m},\qquad \rho+s\leq r\leq\rho+2s,\qquad \delta=\frac{s}{4},\qquad h=\frac{s}{32}. $$
They are computable with $O(\log m)$ bits of precision. In particular,
$$ r\leq\rho+\frac{\beta}{m}. $$

Given nonnegative rational costs $c_j$ and a target $V>0$ with $v(G)\geq V$, we can find $R$ such that
$$ v(R)\geq rV,\qquad c(R)\leq\min_{S:v(S)\geq V}c(S). $$

To prove this, scale the costs to integers and let $C^*$ be the minimum cost of a set attaining $V$. At each tested budget, run the knapsack algorithm and declare success if its output attains $rV$. Every budget at least $C^*$ succeeds.

Check budget zero first. Otherwise binary-search between an unsuccessful lower budget and a successful upper budget. Every unsuccessful budget is strictly below $C^*$. When the integer endpoints become adjacent, the successful upper endpoint is therefore at most $C^*$.

This argument does not assume monotonicity of the algorithm's particular outputs.

3.3. Logarithmic demand

Every positive oracle value is between $2^{-L}$ and $2^L$. Enumerate the rational targets
$$ V_a=2^{-L}(1+\delta)^a $$
up to $2^L$, skipping targets exceeding $v(G)$, and apply the covering subroutine.

For every positive-valued bundle $S$, there is a target with
$$ V_a\leq v(S)<(1+\delta)V_a. $$
Its returned set $R$ satisfies
$$ c(R)\leq c(S),\qquad v(R)\geq\frac{r}{1+\delta}v(S). $$
Consequently, with
$$ D=\ln\frac{1+\delta}{r}, $$
the candidate list satisfies
$$ \ln v(R)-c(R)\geq\ln v(S)-c(S)-D. $$

There are $O(1+L/\delta)$ targets. If each cost has at most $P$ bits, one candidate list uses
$$ O\!\left( m^5(1+L/\delta)(1+mP+\log m) \right) $$
value queries and polynomial time. The target rationals also have polynomial bit length.

For type $t$, use costs $c_j=\mu_tp_j/q_t$. The resulting error in the dual demand objective is $q_tD$.

4. Rationalization and the acceptance certificate

For each positive oracle value, compute a deterministic rational approximation
$$ |\ell_{t,S}-\ln v_t(S)|\leq h. $$
Such approximations are computable in polynomial time. For example, after writing a positive rational as $2^kz$ with $1\leq z<2$, use
$$ \ln z= 2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2i+1} \left(\frac{z-1}{z+1}\right)^{2i+1}. $$
The ratio has absolute value at most $1/3$, so polynomially many bits and terms suffice; $\ln2$ can be handled by the same series.

Let $\widehat F(y)$ be the rounded objective, and let $\widehat F^*$ be its optimum. Since the total objective weight is one,
$$ |\widehat F(y)-F(y)|\leq h \quad\text{for every feasible }y, \qquad |\widehat F^*-F^*|\leq h. $$

Choose a rational $\Delta$ satisfying
$$ D+2h\leq\Delta\leq D+3h. $$

At a rational dual candidate $(u,p)$ with $p\geq0$, generate the demand lists for every type. Return a genuinely violated rounded configuration constraint if one is found.

If none is found, every positive-valued bundle $S$ satisfies
$$ q_t\ell_{t,S}-\mu_tp(S) \leq u_t+q_t(D+2h) \leq u_t+q_t\Delta. $$
Indeed, the demand comparison loses $q_tD$, and passing between the two rounded logarithms loses at most $2q_th$.

Thus acceptance certifies that
$$ (u_t+q_t\Delta,p_j) $$
is feasible for the entire rounded dual. Its objective exceeds the candidate's objective by exactly $\Delta$.

In particular, acceptance at a candidate satisfying
$$ \sum_tu_t+\sum_jb_jp_j\leq C $$
certifies
$$ \widehat F^*\leq C+\Delta. $$

5. Explicit LP recovery, including thin cutoff regions

5.1. The finite-precision volume procedure

We first specify the ellipsoid fact used below, including its finite-precision content.

Volume procedure. In dimension $d\geq2$, start from a ball of rational radius $R$. Suppose each unsuccessful query supplies a nonzero rational normal $a$ and a halfspace
$$ a^\mathsf{T}(z-c)\geq0 $$
through the current rational center $c$. Given rational $r_0<R$, there is a deterministic rational implementation that, after
$$ N=O\!\left(d^2(1+\log(R/r_0))\right) $$
cuts, produces an ellipsoid of volume smaller than a ball of radius $r_0$. It contains every point in the initial ball that satisfies all supplied halfspaces. Its arithmetic and query-point bit lengths are polynomial in $d$, $N$, the cut-normal bit bound, and $\log R$.

Here is a verification. Represent an ellipsoid by its center $c$ and positive-definite shape matrix $Q$. The exact central-cut update is
$$ c^*=c+\frac{Qa}{(d+1)\sqrt{a^\mathsf{T}Qa}}, $$
$$ Q^*=\frac{d^2}{d^2-1} \left( Q-\frac{2}{d+1} \frac{Qaa^\mathsf{T}Q}{a^\mathsf{T}Qa} \right). $$
After transforming to the unit ball and putting the cut in the first coordinate direction, containment follows from
$$ \frac{((d+1)z_1-1)^2+(d^2-1)(1-z_1^2)}{d^2} = 1+\frac{2(d+1)}{d^2}(z_1^2-z_1) \leq1 $$
for $0\leq z_1\leq1$.

The exact volume ratio is
$$ \left(\frac{d^2}{d^2-1}\right)^{d/2} \sqrt{\frac{d-1}{d+1}} \leq \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2(d+1)}\right). $$
For the inequality, use $\ln(1+u)\leq u$ and
$\ln((1-u)/(1+u))\leq-2u$, with $u=1/d$ in the latter; the resulting bound is at most $-1/(2(d+1))$ for $d\geq2$.

To implement rationally, put
$$ \zeta=\frac{1}{12d(d+1)}. $$
Approximate $c^*$ by a rational $\bar c$ within $\zeta$ in the norm defined by $(Q^*)^{-1}$. Then the exact updated ellipsoid is contained in the ellipsoid centered at $\bar c$ with shape $(1+\zeta)^2Q^*$.

Round this shape matrix entrywise to a dyadic grid of spacing $\kappa$, and add $d\kappa I$. The rounding error has operator norm at most $d\kappa$, so this operation preserves containment. Choose the grid fine enough that the resulting matrix $\bar Q$ satisfies
$$ (1+\zeta)^2Q^* \preceq\bar Q \preceq(1+\zeta)^3Q^*. $$
The volume increase is at most $(1+\zeta)^{3d/2}$. Hence each rational update decreases volume by at least the factor
$$ \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{4(d+1)}\right). $$

These precision choices have polynomial encoding length. Indeed,
$$ Q^*\succeq\frac49Q,\qquad \bar Q\preceq2Q. $$
Starting from $Q=R^2I$, throughout $N$ updates the eigenvalues are bounded below by $R^2 2^{-2(N+1)}$ and above by $R^2 2^N$. These bounds specify dyadic grids of polynomial bit length for both centers and matrices. The positive rational $a^\mathsf{T}Qa$ has polynomial bit length and a polynomial-bit lower bound, so its inverse square root can be approximated to the required precision in polynomial time. Intermediate expressions have polynomial bit length, and rounding at every update prevents accumulation of denominator lengths.

Finally, choose $N$ so that
$$ R^d\exp\!\left(-\frac{N}{4(d+1)}\right)<r_0^d. $$
This proves the stated volume and complexity guarantees.

5.2. Uniform bounds for restricted dual optima

Let $J_0$ contain the configurations occupied by the feasible allocation from Section 2. For every configuration set $J\supseteq J_0$, let $\widehat F_J$ denote the rounded primal optimum restricted to $J$.

Every such primal is feasible and compact. Its dual is
$$ \begin{aligned} \min\quad&\sum_tu_t+\sum_jb_jp_j,\\ \text{subject to}\quad& u_t+\mu_tp(S)\geq q_t\ell_{t,S} &&\text{for }(t,S)\in J,\\ &p\geq0. \end{aligned} $$

Put $d=m+\tau$ and $H=L+1$. Since $|\ell_{t,S}|\leq H$,
$$ -H\leq\widehat F_J\leq H. $$

Let $B$ be a uniform bound on the bit length of coefficients in these dual constraints and their right-hand sides. It is polynomial in $L$ and $\log m$.

Each restricted dual has an optimal vertex. To check the relevant hypothesis, its lineality space is zero: a lineality direction must have zero price coordinates because of $p\geq0$; the presence of at least one seed configuration for every type then forces every type coordinate to be zero. The dual is therefore pointed. Strong LP duality gives an attained finite optimum, and its optimal face, being pointed, contains a vertex.

A vertex solves $d$ independent active constraints. Clearing denominators row by row and applying Cramer's rule bounds its coordinates by
$$ d!\,2^{d(d+2)B}. $$
For example, the polynomially describable number
$$ M_0=2^{4d^2(B+\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil+1)} $$
bounds every such optimal vertex coordinate in absolute value. This bound is independent of $|J|$.

5.3. Cutoff acceptance or a restricted-primal certificate

Fix a rational cutoff $C$ and a rational tolerance $0<\varepsilon<1$. Put
$$ M=2M_0,\qquad \lambda=\frac{\varepsilon}{4d},\qquad r_0=\frac{\varepsilon}{8d^2},\qquad R=dM. $$

Run the volume procedure inside the box $[-M,M]^d$. At each center, check, in order:

  • the box constraints, $p\geq0$, and the objective cutoff;
  • the seed and already generated configuration constraints;
  • the approximate demand lists from Section 4.

For any violated inequality, return its central halfspace retaining the side satisfying that inequality. If no listed or generated demand constraint is violated, accept.

Acceptance gives the already proved certificate
$$ \widehat F^*\leq C+\Delta. $$

Suppose instead that the procedure reaches its volume limit. Let $J$ consist of the seed configurations and all configuration cuts generated in this run. The final ellipsoid contains
$$ K_J(C)= \left\{ (u,p): \begin{array}{l} (u,p)\in[-M,M]^d,\quad p\geq0,\\ u_t+\mu_tp(S)\geq q_t\ell_{t,S}\quad ((t,S)\in J),\\ \sum_tu_t+\sum_jb_jp_j\leq C \end{array} \right\}. $$
Every supplied cut is valid for this final set, including cuts generated adaptively.

We claim that termination certifies
$$ \widehat F_J>C-\varepsilon. $$

Otherwise, take an optimal restricted dual vertex $(u^*,p^*)$, whose coordinates are bounded by $M_0$. Increase every coordinate by $\lambda$.

Each configuration inequality then has slack at least $\lambda$, and each price is at least $\lambda$. The objective increases by at most
$$ \lambda\left(\tau+\sum_jb_j\right) \leq\lambda d=\frac{\varepsilon}{4}, $$
so its cutoff slack is at least $3\varepsilon/4$.

Each configuration normal and the objective normal has Euclidean norm at most $d$. A ball of radius
$$ r_0=\frac{\lambda}{2d} $$
around the shifted point therefore remains feasible for all these inequalities and for $p\geq0$. It also lies inside the box, since the original vertex lies in $[-M_0,M_0]^d$ and $\lambda+r_0<1$.

Thus $K_J(C)$ would contain a ball of radius $r_0$, contradicting the final ellipsoid's smaller volume. The claim follows.

This proves the required rejection guarantee even when $K_J(C)$ itself is lower-dimensional. No inference from small volume to exact infeasibility is made.

5.4. Recovering an exactly feasible allocation

Use the preceding procedure with
$$ \varepsilon=\frac h2. $$
Binary-search cutoffs between $-H$ and $H$:

  • on acceptance, replace the upper endpoint by the tested cutoff;
  • on volume termination, replace the lower endpoint by the tested cutoff and retain its generated configurations.

Collect all configurations from these runs, together with $J_0$, into $J_{\mathrm{all}}$. Stop when the endpoint difference is at most $h/2$.

The upper endpoint $U$ satisfies
$$ \widehat F^*\leq U+\Delta. $$
The lower endpoint $C_-$ satisfies
$$ \widehat F_{J_{\mathrm{all}}}\geq C_- -\frac h2. $$
For the initial lower endpoint this follows from seed feasibility and the bound $-H$; for every updated lower endpoint it follows from the volume certificate and inclusion of its generated columns.

Therefore
$$ \widehat F_{J_{\mathrm{all}}}\geq\widehat F^*-\Delta-h. $$

There are $O(1+\log(H/h))$ cutoff runs, each producing polynomially many columns. Solve the explicit restricted rational LP exactly. The standard rational-LP algorithm applies: its numbers of variables and constraints and its coefficient bit lengths are polynomial, and it is feasible and bounded. It returns an optimal rational basic feasible solution $\widehat y$.

This solution satisfies every original supply and type-mass constraint exactly. Selecting a basic optimum gives at most $m+\tau$ positive configuration variables.

6. Approximation factor and complexity

Returning from rounded to true logarithms gives
$$ F(\widehat y) \geq F^*-\Delta-3h \geq F^*-D-6h. $$

The finite-size knapsack margin absorbs this loss. Indeed,
$$ \ln\frac r\rho \geq\ln\left(1+\frac s\rho\right) \geq\frac{s}{\rho+s} \geq s, $$
because $\rho+s<1$. Also,
$$ \ln(1+\delta)\leq\delta=\frac s4. $$
Consequently,
$$ \ln\frac1\rho-D =\ln\frac r\rho-\ln(1+\delta) \geq\frac{3s}{4}, $$
whereas
$$ 6h=\frac{3s}{16}<\frac{3s}{4}. $$
It follows that
$$ W(\widehat y)\geq\rho\,\mathrm{OPT}. $$

Finally output $x_{t,S}=\mu_t\widehat y_{t,S}$.

All parameters satisfy $\delta^{-1},h^{-1}=O(m)$. The logarithms, targets, LP coefficients, ellipsoid centers, and demand costs have polynomial bit length. The volume procedure makes polynomially many cuts because $\log M_0$ is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$. Combining this with the explicit demand-query bound proves
$$ \text{running time}=\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L), \qquad \text{value queries}=\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

The algorithm processes the $\tau$ supported types directly. It never expands rational masses into individual agents.

7. Hardness that survives arbitrary mass splitting

7.1. The coverage gap used

We use the following strengthened Max-Cover gap, stated in Section 2 of [Kapralov, Post, and Vondrák](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kapralov/papers/online-alloc.pdf).

For fixed constants $c_0>0$ and $\gamma>0$, it is $\mathsf{NP}$-hard to distinguish set systems partitioned into $k$ groups, each containing the same constant number $n$ of sets, between:

  • YES: one set from each group covers the universe;
  • NO: every collection of $\ell\leq c_0k$ sets covers at most a fraction

$$ 1-(1-1/k)^\ell+\gamma $$
of the universe.

Set $c_0=3$, and write the sets as $A_{a,r}$, with $a\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$ and $r\in\mathbb Z_n$.

Create $m=kn$ item categories $(a,r)$ and $\tau=n$ types $t\in\mathbb Z_n$. Set
$$ \mu_t=\frac1n,\qquad \eta_t=1,\qquad b_j=\frac1n, $$
and define
$$ v_t(S)= \frac{ \left|\bigcup_{(a,r)\in S}A_{a,r+t}\right| }{|U|}. $$
Indices in $\mathbb Z_n$ are added modulo $n$.

These are monotone, nonnegative submodular coverage valuations: an item's marginal is the number of newly covered elements, which decreases as the existing bundle grows. Their oracles are implementable in polynomial time from the explicit set system and return polynomial-length rationals.

7.2. YES instances

Choose covering sets $A_{a,r_a}$, one from each group. Assign the entire mass of type $t$ the bundle
$$ B_t=\{(a,r_a-t):1\leq a\leq k\}. $$
For each group, the indices $r_a-t$ run through $\mathbb Z_n$. Thus every item occurs in exactly one such bundle, and its consumption is exactly $1/n$.

Every occupied bundle has value one. Since all values are at most one,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=1. $$

7.3. A NO bound valid for every bundle size

Assume $k\geq2$, and define
$$ a_k=(1-1/k)^k,\qquad \rho_k=1-a_k,\qquad d_k=-a_k\ln(1-1/k)>0. $$
The function
$$ g_k(z)=1-(1-1/k)^z $$
is increasing and concave on the nonnegative reals: its first derivative is positive and its second derivative is negative. Hence its tangent at $k$ gives
$$ g_k(z)\leq T_k(z):=\rho_k+d_k(z-k). $$

For $|S|\leq3k$, the NO promise therefore implies
$$ v_t(S)\leq T_k(|S|)+\gamma. $$

This bound also holds for larger bundles. Indeed,
$$ kd_k=a_k[-k\ln(1-1/k)]\geq a_k $$
by $-\ln(1-u)\geq u$. Thus
$$ T_k(3k)=1-a_k+2kd_k\geq1+a_k>1. $$
Since $T_k$ is increasing and $v_t(S)\leq1$, every bundle satisfies
$$ v_t(S)\leq\rho_k+d_k(|S|-k)+\gamma. $$

7.4. The bound for every real mass allocation

Let $x$ be any feasible allocation, with arbitrary real masses. Its total mass is one, and its average bundle size satisfies
$$ \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}|S| = \sum_j\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S} \leq\sum_jb_j=k. $$
Averaging the affine bundle bound gives
$$ \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\leq\rho_k+\gamma. $$

Because all weights equal one, the welfare is the geometric mean with exponents $x_{t,S}$ summing to one. If an occupied bundle has zero value, its welfare is zero. Otherwise, concavity of the logarithm gives
$$ \ln W(x) =\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}\ln v_t(S) \leq \ln\left(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\right). $$
Thus, in every case,
$$ W(x)\leq\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\leq\rho_k+\gamma. $$

This is a bound on all feasible mixtures, so it does not depend on integral allocation hardness transferring automatically.

7.5. Separating the cases

Fix $\alpha<1/\rho$. Since
$$ \rho_k\longrightarrow\rho<1/\alpha, $$
choose a fixed rational $\gamma>0$ and a constant $K$ such that, for all $k\geq K$,
$$ \rho_k+\gamma<1/\alpha. $$
Choose also a fixed rational threshold strictly between a uniform upper bound on these NO values and $1/\alpha$.

Restricting to $k\geq K$ preserves hardness. Instances with $k<K$ can be decided by enumerating the possible choices of one set from each group, in polynomial time because $K$ is fixed.

In a YES instance, an $\alpha$-approximation must return welfare at least $1/\alpha$, so its arithmetic average value is also at least $1/\alpha$. In a NO instance, every allocation's arithmetic average value is below the chosen threshold.

For a listed rational allocation, this arithmetic average is computable in polynomial time using its occupied bundles and valuation queries. Therefore such an approximation algorithm distinguishes the $\mathsf{NP}$-hard gap.

The upper and lower bounds settle the optimal constant factor. A size-dependent improvement tending to the threshold is compatible with the hardness statement; a fixed strictly smaller factor is $\mathsf{NP}$-hard.
