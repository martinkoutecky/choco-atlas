The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite item categories \(G\), valuation types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), weights \(\eta_t\), and monotone submodular valuations \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), choose masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) of type-\(t\) agents receiving each integral bundle \(S\subseteq G\), subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le b_j\), maximizing \(\left(\prod_{t,S}v_t(S)^{\eta_t x_{t,S}}\right)^{1/\sum_t\eta_t\mu_t}\), or determine the best achievable approximation factor.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be finite item categories and \(T\) finite valuation types. Type \(t\) has rational mass \(\mu_t\geq 0\), positive rational weight \(\eta_t\), and a monotone submodular valuation \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\) given by a polynomial-time evaluator. Category \(j\) has rational supply \(b_j\geq 0\), and \(\sum_t\eta_t\mu_t>0\).
Choose masses \(x_{t,S}\geq 0\), where \(S\subseteq G\) is an integral bundle, with \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\leq b_j\). Maximize \(\left(\prod_{t,S}v_t(S)^{\eta_t x_{t,S}}\right)^{1/\sum_t\eta_t\mu_t}\); a zero valuation assigned positive mass makes the product zero.
Is finding an optimal feasible \(x\) polynomial-time solvable, or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
For finite agents and indivisible items, maximizing this welfare is hard to approximate within \(\mathrm e/(\mathrm e-1)\), even with identical valuations and equal weights. For the unit-mass, unit-supply case, the corresponding configuration LP has a polynomial-time \((\mathrm e/(\mathrm e-1)+\varepsilon)\)-approximation under value-oracle access.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

Finding an optimal feasible allocation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with two unit-mass types having identical valuations, equal weights, and unit supplies. The reduction below accounts explicitly for fractional allocations.

Reduction and valuation. Start with an unweighted simple graph \(H=(V,E)\), where \(n=|V|\geq 1\). Finding a maximum cut in such a graph is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer, *Some simplified NP-complete graph problems*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1).

Introduce a set \(D\) of \(n\) dummy categories, disjoint from \(V\), and set \(G=V\cup D\). For \(S\subseteq G\), let \(c(S)\) denote the number of edges of \(H\) having exactly one endpoint in \(S\cap V\). Write \(C=\max_{A\subseteq V}c(A)\).

Construct an allocation instance with:

  • Two types, each having mass \(1\) and weight \(1\).
  • Supply \(b_j=1\) for every category \(j\in G\).
  • Identical valuations \(v_1=v_2=v\), defined by \(v(S)=n|S|+c(S)\).

The construction has polynomial size, and evaluating \(v(S)\) takes polynomial time by counting selected categories and crossing edges.

We will prove that its optimal welfare is exactly \(n^2+C\).

Verification of the valuation hypotheses. The valuation is nonnegative and integer-valued, with \(v(\varnothing)=0\).

View the dummy categories as isolated vertices. For \(j\notin S\), let \(d(j)\) be its degree and \(N(j)\) its neighbor set. Adding \(j\) changes the valuation by

\(v(S\cup\{j\})-v(S)=n+d(j)-2|N(j)\cap S|\).

Since \(|N(j)\cap S|\leq d(j)\leq n-1\), this marginal is at least \(n-d(j)\geq 1\). Thus \(v\) is monotone.

Moreover, if \(A\subseteq B\subseteq G\) and \(j\notin B\), then \(|N(j)\cap A|\leq |N(j)\cap B|\). Consequently,

\(v(A\cup\{j\})-v(A)\geq v(B\cup\{j\})-v(B)\).

This is the diminishing-returns characterization of submodularity on a finite ground set. Hence both types have valid monotone submodular valuations.

Upper bound for every fractional allocation. Let \(x\) be any feasible allocation. Define \(p_S=(x_{1,S}+x_{2,S})/2\). The mass constraints imply \(p_S\geq 0\) and \(\sum_Sp_S=1\), so these numbers form a probability distribution over bundles.

For each category, feasibility gives

\(\sum_{S\ni j}p_S=(x_{1,\{S:j\in S\}}+x_{2,\{S:j\in S\}})/2\leq 1/2\),

where, more explicitly, the numerator means \(\sum_{S\ni j}(x_{1,S}+x_{2,S})\). Therefore,

\(\mathbb E_p[|S|]=\sum_{j\in G}\sum_{S\ni j}p_S\leq |G|/2=n\).

Because every bundle induces a cut of \(H\), we also have \(\mathbb E_p[c(S)]\leq C\). It follows that

\(\mathbb E_p[v(S)]=n\mathbb E_p[|S|]+\mathbb E_p[c(S)]\leq n^2+C\).

The welfare of \(x\) is \(W(x)=\prod_{S:p_S>0}v(S)^{p_S}\). If a positively weighted bundle has zero valuation, then \(W(x)=0\). Otherwise, concavity of the logarithm gives

\(\log W(x)=\sum_Sp_S\log v(S)\leq \log\bigl(\sum_Sp_Sv(S)\bigr)\).

Thus, in all cases, \(W(x)\leq \mathbb E_p[v(S)]\leq n^2+C\). This bound applies to every feasible fractional allocation.

An allocation attaining the bound. Choose a maximum-cut side \(A\subseteq V\), so \(c(A)=C\). Since \(|D|=n\), choose \(D_A\subseteq D\) with \(|D_A|=n-|A|\), and put \(S=A\cup D_A\).

Then \(|S|=|G\setminus S|=n\). Complementing a cut preserves its size, and dummy categories affect no edges, so

\(c(S)=c(G\setminus S)=C\).

Consequently, \(v(S)=v(G\setminus S)=n^2+C\).

Assign all of type \(1\)'s mass to \(S\), and all of type \(2\)'s mass to \(G\setminus S\). Every category is used exactly once, so this allocation is feasible. Its welfare is

\(\bigl(v(S)v(G\setminus S)\bigr)^{1/2}=n^2+C\).

Together with the upper bound, this proves \(\mathrm{OPT}=n^2+C\).

Hardness of finding an optimizer. The identity already proves hardness of computing the exact optimal value: for any integer threshold \(K\), the graph has a cut of size at least \(K\) if and only if \(\mathrm{OPT}\geq n^2+K\).

An optimal allocation also directly reveals a maximum cut. Indeed, for an optimal \(x\), the preceding inequalities imply

\(n^2+C=W(x)\leq n\mathbb E_p[|S|]+\mathbb E_p[c(S)]\leq n^2+C\).

Hence

\(n\bigl(n-\mathbb E_p[|S|]\bigr)+\bigl(C-\mathbb E_p[c(S)]\bigr)=0\).

Both summands are nonnegative, so \(\mathbb E_p[c(S)]=C\). Since \(c(S)\leq C\) for every bundle, every bundle with \(p_S>0\) must satisfy \(c(S)=C\).

Thus, from any optimal allocation returned as an explicit list of positive bundle masses, selecting any listed bundle and intersecting it with \(V\) yields a maximum-cut side in polynomial time. The constructed instance even admits an optimizer with just two positive entries.

Therefore exact optimization is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Fractional allocation does not remove the hardness in this construction. This conclusion is consistent with the stated approximation guarantee; the reduction establishes exact hardness without asserting the same approximation hardness as in the finite-agent problem.
