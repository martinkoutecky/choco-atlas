Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a number-equivalent population scale \(N\), \(d\) good categories with \(Nq_g\) integral copies of category \(g\), a rational type distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) complete valuation types, and monotone subadditive valuations \(v_t(b)\) on integral bundles \(b\), characterize the worst-case ratio between unrestricted maximum welfare and maximum EF1 welfare, and determine the complexity of computing a maximum-welfare EF1 allocation. In the mass model, variables \(x_{t,b}\ge 0\) satisfy \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\); EF1 must hold between every pair of positive-mass type-bundle configurations. Give the price and algorithmic bounds in \(N,d,\tau\), and input bit length \(L\), for scaled and unscaled valuations.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Inputs are integers \(N\geq1\), \(d\geq1\), positive rational supplies \(q_g\) with integral \(C_g=Nq_g\), and \(m=\sum_g C_g\) total copies; copies within a category are interchangeable. Bundles belong to \(B=\prod_{g=1}^d\{0,\ldots,C_g\}\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; no integrality of \(N\mu_t\) is required.
Each type has a complete table of binary rational values \(v_t:B\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\), with \(v_t(0)=0\), inducing a monotone subadditive valuation on the copies. Scaled means \(v_t(C)=1\) for every type; unscaled imposes no common scale. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including these tables.
An allocation consists of real masses \(x_{t,b}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\) for every category. It is EF1 if, for every pair \((t,b),(s,c)\) with positive mass and \(c\neq0\), some category \(g\) with \(c_g>0\) satisfies \(v_t(b)\geq v_t(c-e_g)\), where \(e_g\) is the unit vector.
Define \(W(x)=\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}v_t(b)\), and let \(U\) and \(F\) be its unrestricted and EF1 maxima. The EF1 price is the supremum of \(U/F\) over instances with \(U>0\), taken separately for scaled and unscaled valuations; set the ratio to \(+\infty\) if no positive-welfare EF1 allocation exists.

What are the tight worst-case EF1 prices and the best polynomial-time factors \(\alpha\) for computing an EF1 allocation with \(W(x)\geq U/\alpha\), for scaled and unscaled valuations? State bounds and running times in \(N,m,d,\tau,L\).

For finite societies of \(n\) agents, the EF1 prices are \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\) when scaled and \(\Theta(n)\) when unscaled, even with additive lower bounds; polynomial-time algorithms attain the corresponding welfare guarantees with demand-oracle access to subadditive valuations. [Source](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~nisarg/papers/price-of-fairness.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made N a positive integer, normalized the type masses to sum to one, and allowed arbitrary positive rational masses without requiring integral N times each mass.
  • Removed zero-supply categories, defined m as the total number of copies, bounded each individual bundle by the full inventory, and treated category copies as interchangeable.
  • Specified nonnegative rational valuations, zero value for the empty bundle, and scaling by the value of the full inventory.

Partial resolution: all instances with at most two categories

The full question remains unresolved for arbitrary dimension. However, the gap identified in the notes can be closed for all instances with $d\leq2$, including nonintegral supplies and arbitrary rational type masses.

Write
$$ E=\min\{N,m\}. $$

The following bounds are proved below.

| Regime | Scaled price | Unscaled price | Polynomial-time welfare factors |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| $m\leq N$, arbitrary $d$ | $\Theta(\sqrt m)$ | $\Theta(m)$ | $\sqrt m$ and $m$ |
| $d\leq2$, arbitrary supplies | $\Theta(\sqrt E)$ | $\Theta(E)$ | $O(\sqrt E)$ and $E$ |
| Arbitrary $d$ satisfying either rounding condition below | Upper bound proved below | $\Theta(E)$ over this family | Explicit scaled factor below; unscaled factor $E$ |
| Every $q_g$ integral | Tight scaled order still open in unbounded dimension | Exact supremum $N$ | $\min\{N,1+\sqrt{dN}\}$ and $N$ |

The lower bounds in the first two rows use two types. All the displayed approximation algorithms run in $\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bit time. Their allocation LPs have $O(\tau d)$ variables.

There is also an exact algorithm for arbitrary instances taking
$$ |B|^\tau\operatorname{poly}(L) $$
time. It determines EF1 feasibility and, when feasible, computes $F$.

The two rounding conditions are
$$ R:=\sum_g\bigl(q_g-\lfloor q_g\rfloor\bigr)\leq1 $$
and
$$ H:=\sum_g\bigl(\lceil q_g\rceil-q_g\bigr)\leq1. $$
The second condition supplies the new construction needed to cover all two-category instances.

If the supremum in the question is taken over unrestricted values of all parameters, both global prices are infinite. The results here address the intended parameter-dependent question.

1. Preliminary facts

For a bundle $b$, write
$$ |b|=\sum_g b_g. $$
Subadditivity on copies implies, whenever $b+c\leq C$ coordinatewise,
$$ v_t(b+c)\leq v_t(b)+v_t(c). $$
In particular,
$$ v_t(b)\leq\sum_g b_gv_t(e_g). $$
For scaled valuations, monotonicity also gives
$$ 0\leq v_t(b)\leq1. $$

An unrestricted feasible allocation always exists: give each type a fraction $1/N$ of its mass bundle $C$, and the remaining mass bundle $0$. When $N=1$, feasibility forces every supported bundle to equal $C$, because its average equals the coordinatewise upper bound. Thus both prices equal $1$ when $N=1$.

We subsequently allow arbitrary positive rational masses $\mu_t$. No argument requires $N\mu_t$ to be integral.

2. An exact finite characterization

Let
$$ K=|B|=\prod_g(C_g+1). $$
For each type, define
$$ r_t(0)=0,\qquad r_t(c)=\min_{g:c_g>0}v_t(c-e_g) \quad\text{for }c\neq0. $$
The EF1 requirement is equivalent to
$$ v_t(b)\geq r_t(c) $$
for every supported own bundle $(t,b)$ and every supported comparison bundle $c$.

Let
$$ V_t=\{v_t(b):b\in B\}. $$
For each vector $a\in\prod_t V_t$, consider the allocation LP that permits a variable $x_{s,c}$ only when
$$ v_s(c)\geq a_s \quad\text{and}\quad r_t(c)\leq a_t\quad\text{for every }t. $$
Its remaining constraints are the type-mass and supply equalities from the question.

Claim. The EF1 feasible set is exactly the union of these LP feasible sets.

Proof. Every allocation in a threshold LP is EF1, since a supported own bundle has value at least $a_t$, whereas every supported comparison bundle has removal value at most $a_t$.

Conversely, suppose $x$ is EF1. Positive type masses ensure that every type has nonempty support. Set
$$ a_t=\min_{b:x_{t,b}>0}v_t(b). $$
This minimum belongs to $V_t$. Every supported own bundle satisfies the first restriction, and EF1 gives the second restriction for every supported comparison bundle. Hence $x$ belongs to this threshold LP. $\square$

Each LP feasible set is a compact rational polytope: every variable lies between $0$ and its type mass. Therefore the EF1 feasible set is compact, and $F$ is attained whenever it is feasible.

An optimal basic solution of an optimal threshold LP has at most $\tau+d$ positive variables, since there are $\tau+d$ equality constraints. Its rational encoding length is polynomial in $L$, by the determinant bounds for rational linear systems.

Precomputing all $r_t(c)$ requires $O(\tau Kd)$ table accesses. There are at most $K^\tau$ threshold vectors. Thus exact optimization takes
$$ K^\tau\operatorname{poly}(L) $$
time. The complete-table representation ensures $\tau K=O(L)$. In particular, this algorithm is polynomial-time for fixed $\tau$.

The unrestricted optimum $U$ is computable by one rational allocation LP in $\operatorname{poly}(L)$ time.

3. Downward rounding: a common bundle plus at most one copy

Define
$$ r_g=\lfloor q_g\rfloor,\qquad f_g=q_g-r_g,\qquad R=\sum_g f_g. $$
Assume $R\leq1$.

Restrict allocations to bundles
$$ r,\qquad r+e_g\quad\text{for }f_g>0. $$
Let $y_{t,g}$ denote type-$t$ mass receiving $r+e_g$. The allocation LP is
$$ \sum_t y_{t,g}=f_g,\qquad \sum_g y_{t,g}\leq\mu_t,\qquad y_{t,g}\geq0, $$
with remaining type mass receiving $r$. Maximize the actual welfare of this allocation. Denote its optimum by $W_\downarrow$.

The LP is feasible because $y_{t,g}=\mu_t f_g$ satisfies its constraints.

3.1 EF1 and the unscaled guarantee

Every allowed bundle contains $r$. If the comparison bundle is $r+e_g$, deleting its extra copy leaves $r$, whose value is no greater than the recipient’s own value. If the comparison bundle is $r$, monotonicity gives the same conclusion after deleting any available copy. Thus every feasible solution of this LP is EF1.

Furthermore,
$$ Nf_g=C_g-Nr_g $$
is an integer, and
$$ \sum_g Nf_g=NR\leq N. $$
Consequently, $C$ can be partitioned into $N$ bundles, each equal to $r$ or to $r+e_g$: begin with $N$ copies of $r$ and distribute the remaining copies into distinct bundles.

For each type, subadditivity applied to this partition gives
$$ v_t(C)\leq N\left((1-R)v_t(r)+\sum_g f_gv_t(r+e_g)\right). $$
The expression in parentheses is exactly that type’s expected value under the feasible independent assignment $y_{t,g}=\mu_t f_g$. Hence
$$ W_\downarrow\geq\frac1N\sum_t\mu_t v_t(C). $$
Since
$$ U\leq\sum_t\mu_t v_t(C), $$
we obtain
$$ W_\downarrow\geq U/N. $$
In the scaled case, this also proves
$$ W_\downarrow\geq1/N. $$

The partition into $N$ bundles is only a proof device; the algorithm does not enumerate these bundles.

3.2 A scaled bound

Define
$$ G_+=\{g:r_g\geq1\},\qquad G_0=\{g:r_g=0\}, $$
and
$$ m_0=\sum_{g\in G_0}C_g,\qquad D_\downarrow=\sum_{g\in G_+}\frac{q_g}{r_g}. $$
An empty sum is zero.

Set
$$ a_t=v_t(r),\qquad A=\sum_t\mu_ta_t. $$
Every allowed bundle contains $r$, so
$$ W_\downarrow\geq A. $$

Split an unrestricted bundle $b$ into its $G_+$ and $G_0$ parts, denoted $b^+$ and $b^0$.

For $b^+$, put
$$ z(b)=\max_{g\in G_+}\frac{b_g}{r_g}, $$
with $z(b)=0$ if $G_+$ is empty. Partitioning $b^+$ into at most $\lceil z(b)\rceil$ bundles bounded coordinatewise by $r$ gives
$$ v_t(b^+)\leq(1+z(b))a_t. $$
Together with $v_t(b^+)\leq1$, this implies
$$ v_t(b^+) \leq a_t+\min\{1,z(b)a_t\} \leq a_t+\sqrt{z(b)a_t}. $$

For an unrestricted optimum $x$, the supply equalities give
$$ \sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}z(b) \leq\sum_{g\in G_+}\frac{q_g}{r_g} =D_\downarrow. $$
Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz therefore yields
$$ U_+:=\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}v_t(b^+) \leq A+\sqrt{D_\downarrow A}. $$

We next bound
$$ U_0:=\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}v_t(b^0). $$
Let $S_0$ be the optimal singleton welfare using just the supplies in $G_0$, with type capacities $\mu_t$. These supplies have total mass
$$ \sum_{g\in G_0}q_g=m_0/N\leq R\leq1. $$

For every nonempty $b^0$, distribute mass $x_{t,b}$ among its singleton categories in proportions $b_g/|b^0|$. Subadditivity shows that the resulting singleton welfare is at least
$$ J_0:=\sum_{t,b:b^0\neq0} x_{t,b}\frac{v_t(b^0)}{|b^0|}. $$
This assignment respects type capacities and does not exceed any category supply. It can be completed to a full singleton assignment: its remaining supply mass is no greater than its remaining type capacity, because the total singleton supply is at most $1$.

Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$ U_0^2 \leq J_0 \sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}v_t(b^0)|b^0| \leq J_0\,m_0/N. $$
Thus, when $m_0>0$,
$$ S_0\geq NU_0^2/m_0. $$
When $m_0=0$, simply $U_0=0$.

Any feasible $G_0$ singleton assignment can be extended to the downward-rounding LP by assigning the remaining extra-copy supplies within the remaining type capacities. The total extra-copy supply is at most $1$. Moreover,
$$ v_t(r+e_g)\geq v_t(e_g). $$
Consequently,
$$ W_\downarrow\geq S_0, \qquad U_0\leq\sqrt{(m_0/N)W_\downarrow}. $$

Subadditivity gives $U\leq U_++U_0$. Combining the bounds,
$$ U\leq W_\downarrow+ \left(\sqrt{D_\downarrow}+\sqrt{m_0/N}\right) \sqrt{W_\downarrow}. $$
Using $W_\downarrow\geq1/N$, we obtain the factor
$$ \boxed{ \alpha_\downarrow = \min\left\{ N,\, 1+\sqrt{ND_\downarrow}+\sqrt{m_0} \right\}. } $$

For reference,
$$ ND_\downarrow =\sum_{g\in G_+}\frac{C_g}{r_g} \leq m-m_0, $$
and $D_\downarrow\leq2|G_+|$. Therefore
$$ \alpha_\downarrow \leq \min\{N,\ 1+\sqrt{2m},\ 1+\sqrt{2N|G_+|}+\sqrt N\}. $$

4. Upward rounding: a common bundle with at most one omission

Define
$$ s_g=\lceil q_g\rceil,\qquad h_g=s_g-q_g,\qquad H=\sum_g h_g. $$
Assume $H\leq1$.

Restrict allocations to
$$ s,\qquad s-e_g\quad\text{for }h_g>0. $$
These are valid bundles: $1\leq s_g\leq C_g$.

Let $y_{t,g}$ be type-$t$ mass receiving $s-e_g$. Use the LP
$$ \sum_t y_{t,g}=h_g,\qquad \sum_g y_{t,g}\leq\mu_t,\qquad y_{t,g}\geq0, $$
with the remaining mass receiving $s$. Maximize its welfare and call the optimum $W_\uparrow$. The independent assignment $y_{t,g}=\mu_t h_g$ proves feasibility.

4.1 EF1 and the unscaled guarantee

Consider own bundle $s-e_g$ and comparison bundle $s-e_j$.

If $g\neq j$, deleting a copy of category $g$ from the comparison bundle leaves
$$ s-e_j-e_g\leq s-e_g. $$
If $g=j$, the bundles are equal. Comparing $s-e_g$ with $s$ is resolved by deleting category $g$. An own bundle $s$ weakly dominates every other allowed bundle. Monotonicity therefore proves EF1 for every feasible LP solution.

Also,
$$ Nh_g=Ns_g-C_g $$
is integral, and $\sum_g Nh_g\leq N$. Starting with $N$ copies of $s$, omit the required copies from distinct bundles. The resulting $N$ bundles partition $C$.

Subadditivity, exactly as in the downward construction, proves
$$ W_\uparrow \geq\frac1N\sum_t\mu_t v_t(C) \geq U/N. $$
For scaled valuations,
$$ W_\uparrow\geq1/N. $$

4.2 A value comparison for the independent assignment

There is at most one category satisfying
$$ s_g=1,\qquad q_g<1/2, $$
because such a category has $h_g>1/2$, whereas $H\leq1$. Call this category exceptional if it exists.

First suppose there is no exceptional category. We prove that the independent assignment gives each type expected value at least $v_t(s)/2$.

Fix a type and write
$$ V=v_t(s),\qquad a_g=v_t(s-e_g). $$
For distinct categories $g,j$, subadditivity and monotonicity imply
$$ V\leq a_g+a_j, $$
because $(s-e_g)+e_g=s$ and $e_g\leq s-e_j$.

Furthermore, if $s_g\geq2$, then $e_g\leq s-e_g$, so
$$ V\leq a_g+v_t(e_g)\leq2a_g. $$

If every omitted-bundle value is at least $V/2$, the independent expected value is at least
$$ (1-H)V+HV/2\geq V/2. $$
Otherwise choose a minimum-value omission $j$ with $a_j<V/2$. The preceding inequality forces $s_j=1$. Absence of an exceptional category then gives $h_j\leq1/2$.

For every other omission, $a_g\geq V-a_j$. Hence the independent expected value is at least
$$ (1-h_j)V+(2h_j-H)a_j. $$
If $2h_j-H\geq0$, this is at least $(1-h_j)V\geq V/2$. If $2h_j-H<0$, using $a_j<V/2$ bounds it below by
$$ (1-H/2)V\geq V/2. $$
This proves the claim.

Consequently, in this case, with
$$ u=s,\qquad A=\sum_t\mu_t v_t(u), $$
we have
$$ W_\uparrow\geq A/2. $$

If an exceptional category $g_*$ exists, instead set
$$ u=s-e_{g_*},\qquad A=\sum_t\mu_t v_t(u). $$
The independent assignment gives bundle $u$ with probability $h_{g_*}>1/2$, so again
$$ W_\uparrow\geq A/2. $$

4.3 Scaled welfare bound

If no exceptional category exists, let
$$ D_\uparrow=\sum_g q_g/s_g,\qquad m_0=0. $$
If an exceptional category exists, let
$$ D_\uparrow=\sum_{g\neq g_*}q_g/s_g,\qquad m_0=C_{g_*}. $$

On the coordinates present in $u$, the partition and Cauchy–Schwarz argument from Section 3 gives
$$ U_+\leq A+\sqrt{D_\uparrow A} \leq2W_\uparrow+\sqrt{2D_\uparrow W_\uparrow}. $$

It remains to justify the exceptional-coordinate bound when $g_*$ exists. Put $q_*=q_{g_*}<1/2$. Any singleton assignment of this category has type masses $z_t$ satisfying
$$ 0\leq z_t\leq\mu_t,\qquad \sum_tz_t=q_*. $$
It can be realized inside the upward-rounding construction:

  • Give mass $\mu_t-z_t$ the bundle $s-e_{g_*}$.
  • Distribute all other omissions among the masses $z_t$.
  • Give their remaining mass bundle $s$.

This is possible because
$$ \sum_{g\neq g_*}h_g =H-h_{g_*} \leq1-h_{g_*} =q_*. $$
Every bundle allocated to the masses $z_t$ contains one copy of $g_*$. Therefore the optimized upward-rounding welfare is at least the optimal singleton welfare of this category.

The singleton extraction calculation from Section 3 now gives
$$ U_0\leq\sqrt{(m_0/N)W_\uparrow}. $$
In the nonexceptional case, $U_0=0$.

Combining these estimates and using $W_\uparrow\geq1/N$ proves
$$ \boxed{ \alpha_\uparrow = \min\left\{ N,\, 2+\sqrt{2ND_\uparrow}+\sqrt{m_0} \right\}. } $$
Here $D_\uparrow\leq d$, and $ND_\uparrow\leq m-m_0$. Thus
$$ \alpha_\uparrow \leq \min\{N,\ 2+\sqrt{3m},\ 2+\sqrt{2dN}+\sqrt N\}. $$

5. Consequences for two categories and integral supplies

Let $k$ be the number of nonintegral supplies. Then
$$ R+H=k. $$
If $d\leq2$, we have $k\leq2$, so at least one of $R\leq1$ and $H\leq1$ holds.

Therefore every two-category instance admits an EF1 allocation, and the constructions give
$$ W\geq U/N $$
for unscaled valuations.

For scaled valuations, the bounds above give the simple uniform factor
$$ \min\{N,\ 2+3\sqrt N\}. $$
When $m\leq N$, the stronger singleton bounds proved next apply. Combining regimes yields factors
$$ O(\sqrt E)\quad\text{and}\quad E $$
for scaled and unscaled valuations, respectively.

If every supply is integral, the downward construction has only the bundle $q$. Its unscaled guarantee is $N$. In its scaled bound,
$$ m_0=0,\qquad D_\downarrow=d, $$
giving
$$ \min\{N,1+\sqrt{dN}\}. $$

The unscaled supremum for any fixed positive integral supply vector is exactly $N$. To prove the lower bound, use valuations depending only on the first category:
$$ v_H(b)=Mb_1,\qquad v_L(b)=b_1, $$
with masses $1/N$ and $1-1/N$, where $N\geq2$.

The unrestricted optimum is $Mq_1$. Strict increase in the first-coordinate count forces all supported first-coordinate counts in an EF1 allocation to differ by at most one. Since their average $q_1$ is integral, they must all equal $q_1$. Thus
$$ F=q_1\left(M/N+1-1/N\right), $$
and
$$ \frac UF =\frac{M}{M/N+1-1/N}\longrightarrow N. $$
Other categories can be allocated at their common integral supplies and contribute zero value.

6. Singleton allocations when $m\leq N$

An allocation supported on empty bundles and singletons is automatically EF1. Its optimal welfare $S$ is given by
$$ \max\sum_{t,g}y_{t,g}v_t(e_g) $$
subject to
$$ \sum_g y_{t,g}\leq\mu_t,\qquad \sum_t y_{t,g}=q_g,\qquad y_{t,g}\geq0. $$
It is feasible because $y_{t,g}=\mu_tq_g$ and $\sum_gq_g=m/N\leq1$.

Given an unrestricted optimum, extract singleton masses from every nonempty bundle in proportions $b_g/|b|$. The resulting welfare is at least
$$ J=\sum_{t,b\neq0}x_{t,b}\frac{v_t(b)}{|b|}. $$
The extracted assignment can be completed, by the same residual-capacity argument used in Section 3. Hence $S\geq J$.

Since $|b|\leq m$,
$$ S\geq U/m. $$

For scaled valuations, weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$ U^2 \leq J\sum_{t,b\neq0}x_{t,b}v_t(b)|b| \leq Jm/N. $$
Also, the independent singleton assignment has welfare
$$ \frac1N\sum_t\mu_t\sum_g C_gv_t(e_g) \geq\frac1N\sum_t\mu_t v_t(C) =\frac1N. $$
Thus
$$ S\geq\max\{1/N,\ NU^2/m\}\geq U/\sqrt m. $$

This establishes the advertised factors $m$ and $\sqrt m$.

7. Matching lower bounds, including arbitrary mass splitting

It suffices first to use one category, with $C=m$ and $q=C/N$.

7.1 Unscaled lower bound

For $N\geq2$, use masses $1/N$ and $1-1/N$ and valuations
$$ v_H(b)=Mb,\qquad v_L(b)=b. $$
Giving all $C$ copies to the high type uses exactly supply $q$, so
$$ U=Mq. $$

Let $h$ be the smallest supported bundle size in any EF1 allocation. Strict increase implies every supported size is at most $h+1$. Since the average is $q$, every supported size is at most $\lceil q\rceil$. For integral $q$, the average additionally forces every supported size to equal $q$.

It follows that
$$ F\leq\frac{M}{N}\lceil q\rceil+q. $$
Letting $M$ increase through positive integers gives the price lower bound
$$ \frac{C}{\lceil C/N\rceil}. $$
This equals $m$ when $m\leq N$ and is at least $N/2$ when $m\geq N$.

Thus the unscaled lower bound is $\Omega(E)$.

7.2 Scaled lower bound

Choose an integer $K$ satisfying $q<K\leq C$, and use
$$ v_H(b)=\min\{b/K,1\},\qquad v_L(b)=b/C, $$
with high-type mass
$$ \delta=q/K. $$
These valuations are monotone, subadditive, and scaled.

Giving bundle $K$ to all high-type mass and zero to the low type is feasible. Both valuations are bounded above by $b/K$, so
$$ U=q/K. $$

In an EF1 allocation, the minimum supported size $h$ satisfies $h\leq q<K$. Whichever type receives size $h$, its valuation is strictly increasing below that level’s cap. Consequently every supported size is at most $h+1$, and hence at most $\lceil q\rceil$.

If $C\leq N$ and $C\geq4$, choose $K=\lfloor\sqrt C\rfloor$. EF1 allocations then use only sizes zero and one; when $q=1$, only size one occurs. Assigning singletons first to all high-type mass gives
$$ F=\delta/K+(q-\delta)/C. $$
Therefore
$$ \frac UF =\frac{CK}{C+K^2-K} \geq K/2 \geq\sqrt C/4. $$

If $C\geq N$ and $N\geq4$, choose
$$ K=\lceil q\sqrt N\rceil. $$
This satisfies $q<K\leq C$. Since all supported sizes are at most $\lceil q\rceil$,
$$ F\leq \delta\frac{\lceil q\rceil}{K}+\frac qC \leq\frac{q(q+1)}{K^2}+\frac1N \leq\frac3N. $$
Also,
$$ U=q/K\geq\frac1{\sqrt N+1}. $$
Hence
$$ \frac UF \geq\frac{N}{3(\sqrt N+1)} \geq\frac{\sqrt N}{6}. $$
The remaining bounded values of $E$ are covered by the elementary lower bound $1$.

Thus the scaled lower bound is $\Omega(\sqrt E)$.

7.3 Embedding in two categories

For fixed $d=2$ and $m\geq2$, take
$$ C_1=m-1,\qquad C_2=1, $$
and let both valuations depend only on the first category. The one-category welfare calculations remain valid.

To allocate the second category, split every supported mass into fractions $1/N$ receiving its additional copy and $1-1/N$ receiving none. This preserves EF1: if a comparison bundle has positive first-coordinate count, use its first-category EF1 removal; otherwise its value is zero.

Moreover,
$$ \min\{N,m-1\}\geq\frac12\min\{N,m\}. $$
Thus the same asymptotic lower bounds hold for fixed $d=2$.

Together with the upper bounds, this proves the tight prices
$$ \boxed{ \Theta(\sqrt E)\text{ scaled},\qquad \Theta(E)\text{ unscaled} } $$
for $d=1$ and $d=2$.

These lower bounds constrain every algorithm, regardless of running time, because its EF1 welfare cannot exceed $F$. Therefore the polynomial-time factors above are optimal in asymptotic order in these regimes.

8. Running times and remaining open questions

The singleton, downward-rounding, and upward-rounding algorithms each use a rational LP with at most $\tau(d+1)$ allocation variables and $\tau+d$ equality constraints. Their coefficients come directly from the input and a polynomial number of table lookups. They run in $\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bit time and can return rational allocations with at most $\tau+d$ positive entries.

No algorithm iterates over $N$ agents or uses a common denominator of the type masses. Thus the running times remain polynomial even when $N$ or the mass denominators are large in binary encoding. In particular, they are polynomial in $N,m,d,\tau,L$ as requested.

The scaled one-category lower-bound tables have encoding length
$$ O(m\log(m+1)+\log N). $$
