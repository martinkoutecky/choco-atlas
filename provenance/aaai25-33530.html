The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses over finitely many additive valuation types, rational per-capita supplies of finitely many good kinds, and a cardinality cap k, compute a mass allocation of each type to integral bundles of size at most k, meeting all supplies and maximizing total utilitarian welfare.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) additive valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(a_t\), where \(\sum_t a_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) good kinds with positive binary-rational per-capita supplies \(s_j\), and a binary-encoded integer cap \(k\geq1\), with \(\sum_j s_j\leq k\). Type \(t\) has nonnegative binary-rational values \(v_{tj}\), normalized by \(\sum_j s_jv_{tj}=1\).
An integral bundle is \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^m\) with \(\sum_j b_j\leq k\); multiple copies of a kind and the empty bundle are allowed. Its value to type \(t\) is \(v_t\cdot b\).
A mass allocation assigns nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,b}\), satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=a_t\) for every type and \(\sum_{t,b}b_jx_{t,b}=s_j\) for every kind. Its utilitarian welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}(v_t\cdot b)\). Represent allocations by listing only positive masses and their bundles.

Can an allocation maximizing \(W(x)\) be computed in polynomial time, or is this optimization problem NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), the total input bit length \(L\), and \(k\), distinguishing polynomial dependence on \(\log k\) from dependence on \(k\).

For finitely many agents and explicitly listed indivisible goods, a utilitarian-optimal allocation under a common cardinality cap is computable in polynomial time. [Lam, Li, and Sun](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.01660).
The search identified no result settling this precise mass-allocation formulation.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one so supplies are per capita; each supported type has strictly positive mass.
  • Carried over nonnegative additive utilities and normalized each type's value of the per-capita endowment to one.
  • Removed zero-supply kinds and required at least one remaining kind; carried over feasibility as total per-capita supply at most the positive integer cap.
  • Interpreted good kinds as permitting repeated copies within an integral bundle, including the empty bundle; supplies constrain aggregate consumption rather than individual bundle coordinates.
  • Specified that every type's entire mass is allocated and every supply is exhausted exactly.
  • Specified binary encoding of the cap and a sparse rational representation of the output allocation.

Result

The optimization problem is polynomial-time solvable, including when the cap \(k\) is binary encoded. An exact optimal allocation can be computed in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(k+1))\) bit operations and represented using at most \(\tau(m+1)\) positive masses.

The decisive property is that multiple copies of a kind and the empty bundle are allowed.

1. A polynomial-size linear program

For each type \(t\) and kind \(j\), let \(y_{tj}\) denote the total quantity of kind \(j\) assigned to type \(t\). Consider the following rational linear program:

\(\displaystyle \text{maximize }\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{tj}y_{tj}\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}y_{tj}=s_j \qquad (j=1,\ldots,m),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=1}^{m}y_{tj}\leq k a_t \qquad (t=1,\ldots,\tau),\)

and \(y_{tj}\geq 0\).

It has \(m\tau\) variables, \(m\) supply equalities, and \(\tau\) capacity inequalities, in addition to nonnegativity.

It is feasible: set \(y_{tj}=a_t s_j\). The supply equalities follow from \(\sum_t a_t=1\), and each capacity inequality follows from

\(\displaystyle \sum_j a_t s_j=a_t\sum_j s_j\leq k a_t.\)

It is bounded: nonnegativity and the supply equalities imply \(0\leq y_{tj}\leq s_j\). Thus its feasible region is nonempty and compact, and its linear objective attains a maximum.

2. Every mass allocation gives a feasible LP solution

Given a feasible mass allocation \(x\), define

\(\displaystyle y_{tj}=\sum_b b_jx_{t,b}.\)

The original supply constraints give \(\sum_t y_{tj}=s_j\). Since every allowed bundle satisfies \(\sum_j b_j\leq k\), we have

\(\displaystyle \sum_j y_{tj} =\sum_b x_{t,b}\sum_j b_j \leq k\sum_b x_{t,b} =k a_t.\)

Thus \(y\) is LP-feasible. Additivity gives exact preservation of welfare:

\(\displaystyle W(x) =\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}\sum_jv_{tj}b_j =\sum_{t,j}v_{tj}y_{tj}.\)

Consequently, the LP optimum is an upper bound on the welfare of every feasible mass allocation.

3. Every rational LP solution gives a mass allocation

Let \(y\) be any rational feasible LP solution. Write \(e_j\) for the \(j\)-th standard unit vector. For each type \(t\), assign

\(\displaystyle x_{t,k e_j}=\frac{y_{tj}}{k}\qquad(j=1,\ldots,m)\)

and

\(\displaystyle x_{t,0}=a_t-\frac{1}{k}\sum_j y_{tj}.\)

Assign zero mass to all other bundles and omit zero entries from the representation.

These masses are nonnegative and rational. For the empty bundle, nonnegativity follows from the type-capacity inequality. Their sum for type \(t\) is exactly \(a_t\).

Every listed bundle is integral and respects the cap: \(0\) has cardinality zero, and \(k e_j\) has cardinality \(k\). This uses the hypothesis that multiple copies of a kind are permitted.

For every kind \(j\), the allocated supply is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,b}b_jx_{t,b} =\sum_t k\frac{y_{tj}}{k} =s_j.\)

Finally, the resulting welfare is

\(\displaystyle W(x) =\sum_{t,j}\frac{y_{tj}}{k}\bigl(v_t\cdot k e_j\bigr) =\sum_{t,j}v_{tj}y_{tj}.\)

Therefore the transformation preserves feasibility and objective value exactly. Applying it to an exact rational LP optimum produces an optimal mass allocation. Together with Section 2, this proves equality of the two optimization problems.

4. Running time and output size

The algorithm is to construct the LP, solve it exactly over the rationals, and apply the formulas in Section 3.

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming computes an exact rational optimum in time polynomial in the number of variables, constraints, and total coefficient encoding length, whenever the feasible region is nonempty and the optimum is finite. Both hypotheses were verified above. Rational data can be converted to integer data by clearing denominators with polynomial growth in encoding length; polynomial-time feasibility and optimization are described in [MIT’s ellipsoid-method lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-251j-introduction-to-mathematical-programming-fall-2009/e13f5e6650f7be1e21101a2e7bd0d5b8_MIT6_251JF09_lec18.pdf).

For an explicit bound, let \(N=m\tau\). Even with a dense constraint matrix, the constructed LP has encoding length

\(\displaystyle B=O\!\left(L+\tau\log(k+1)+N(m+\tau+1)\right).\)

Indeed, its constraint coefficients are zero or one, and computing the right-hand sides \(k a_t\) adds at most \(O(\log(k+1))\) bits per type. Hence the complete algorithm takes

\(\displaystyle \left(L+\tau\log(k+1)+m\tau(m+\tau+1)\right)^{O(1)}\)

bit operations. Since \(L\) already includes the explicit valuation table and the binary encoding of \(k\), this is polynomial in the total input length.

The output contains at most \(\tau(m+1)\) positive entries. Each bundle is encoded as its integer coordinate vector; encoding \(k e_j\) requires \(O(m\log(k+1))\) bits, without enumerating its copies. Exact rational LP solutions have polynomial encoding length, and the divisions by \(k\) and subtractions used in reconstruction preserve polynomial encoding length.

Thus no polynomial dependence on the numerical value of \(k\) is needed. The potentially large set of integral bundles is never enumerated. The valuation normalization is compatible with the argument but is not needed for this tractability result.
