The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of programs \(P\), supported single-doctor types \(S\), couple types \(K\), rational type masses \(\alpha_s,\beta_k\), and rational per-capita quotas \(q_p\), decide whether there are rational assignment flows \(x_{s,p}\) and \(y_{k,(p_1,p_2)}\) that exhaust each type’s mass, respect quotas and individual acceptability, and admit no blocking deviation. Each type specifies its complete relevant preferences; programs rank doctor types, with members of a type sharing a priority. A blocking deviation is a positive mass of a type moving to a preferred option that the destination programs can accept under responsive choice, with both members accepted together when a couple chooses the same program. If such flows exist, produce one. Measure complexity in the number of programs \(m\), supported types \(\tau=|S|+|K|\), input bit length \(L\), and preference-list lengths.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) programs, \(S\) single-doctor types, and \(K\) ordered couple types. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|S|+|K|\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Normalize doctor mass by \(\sum_s\alpha_s+2\sum_k\beta_k=1\). Each program has a positive binary-rational per-capita quota \(q_p\).
Write \(P^+=P\cup\{\bot\}\), where \(\bot\) denotes unemployment. Singles strictly rank their listed acceptable options in \(P^+\); couples strictly rank listed ordered pairs in \((P^+)^2\). The options \(\bot\) and \((\bot,\bot)\), respectively, are always listed last; pairs with exactly one unemployed member may also be listed. Unlisted options are unacceptable.
Programs strictly rank acceptable doctor categories \(s\) and \((k,i)\), where \(i\in\{1,2\}\) identifies a couple member; all doctors within a category have equal priority. Assignment to a real program requires its acceptance of that category.
An assignment consists of nonnegative rational flows on acceptable options satisfying \(\sum_a x_{s,a}=\alpha_s\), \(\sum_{(a,b)}y_{k,(a,b)}=\beta_k\), and \(\sum_s x_{s,p}+\sum_k\sum_{(a,b)}(\mathbf1_{a=p}+\mathbf1_{b=p})y_{k,(a,b)}\le q_p\). Couple flow measures couples, so assigning both members to one program consumes twice that flow.
A blocking deviation selects a positive rational mass \(\delta\) from one type's flow at one current option and sends it to a strictly preferred listed option. Each destination applies responsive choice to its original incumbent doctor mass together with the arriving members, retaining acceptable mass up to quota in priority order. Equal-priority arrivals cannot displace incumbents; acceptance means some such selection retains every deviating member required at that destination. Members already there are counted once. Departures do not first free capacity in these acceptance tests; \(\bot\) accepts without limit.
For couples, both members must be accepted at their respective destinations, including when one stays in place; at a common destination both masses must be retained together. A stable assignment has no blocking deviation. Decide whether one exists and, if so, produce rational flows. State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), and total preference-list length \(\ell\).

Is deciding whether a rational stable assignment exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete: [Drummond, Perrault, and Bacchus](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/15/Papers/079.pdf) establish a bijection between stable matchings and satisfying assignments of a SAT encoding.
Fractional: [Biró and Fleiner](https://real.mtak.hu/26264/7/BF16do_last.pdf) prove existence of stable allocations with contributions under their domination-based stability definition and discuss applications to couples.
Related complexity: [Ishizuka and Kamiyama](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2018.11) prove \(\mathrm{PPAD}\)-completeness of finding stable fractional hypergraph matchings even at maximum degree three; this concerns a different search problem.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The mass normalization was unspecified; normalize total doctor mass to one, counting each couple twice, and interpret quotas in the same units.
  • The domains of quotas and flows were unspecified; require positive rational quotas and nonnegative rational flows, retaining the discrete definition's positive capacities.
  • Exhausting mass omitted unemployment; include an unlimited null program, allow listed partially unemployed couple options, and place complete unemployment last.
  • Complete relevant preferences did not specify strictness or acceptability; use strict orders over listed acceptable options, with unlisted options unacceptable.
  • Program priorities did not distinguish couple members; rank single categories and ordered couple-member categories separately, with equal priority within each category.
  • Responsive choice did not specify treatment of equal-priority masses; give incumbents precedence over equal-priority arrivals and allow any responsive selection consistent with that convention.
  • Blocking did not specify the source portion or departure accounting; select positive rational mass from one current option and test acceptance against original incumbents plus arrivals, counting members already present once, as in the discrete union-based acceptance definition.
  • The common-destination requirement did not explicitly cover a member already there; require retention of both members together, including the incumbent member.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result

The existence decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\): every valid instance admits a rational stable assignment.

Rational flows can be constructed in time
\((2\tau+1)^m\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,\ell,L)\).
Thus construction is polynomial-time for every fixed \(m\). This proof does not establish polynomial-time construction when both \(m\) and \(\tau\) vary.

The essential step is a refinement of the auxiliary program preferences used in the contribution-allocation theorem. It handles deviations in which a couple member stays at a program.

2. The existence theorem used

We use the following contribution-allocation theorem.

Let \(V\) be a finite vertex set and \(E\) a finite indexed collection of activities. Activity \(e\) has capacity \(c_e>0\) and positive contribution \(a_{ve}\) at each incident vertex \(v\). Vertex \(v\) has capacity \(b_v>0\) and strictly orders its incident activities. Then there exists an allocation \(z\) satisfying

\( 0\le z_e\le c_e, \qquad \sum_{e:\,v\in e}a_{ve}z_e\le b_v, \)

such that every activity \(e\) with \(z_e<c_e\) is dominated at some incident vertex \(v\), meaning

\( \sum_{f:\,v\in f,\ f\succeq_v e}a_{vf}z_f=b_v. \)

This is [Biró–Fleiner, Theorem 13](https://real.mtak.hu/26264/7/BF16do_last.pdf), with the domination definition in Section 3.3. Indexed activities may share incidence sets: the Scarf-matrix construction uses a separate column and capacity constraint for each activity and requires no distinct-incidence assumption.

We use only existence from this theorem. Rationality and an explicit running time will be proved separately.

3. Constructing the auxiliary instance

Write \(T\) for the supported single and couple types. Let \(\gamma_t\) denote their respective masses \(\alpha_s\) or \(\beta_k\).

First remove every listed option assigning a member to a program that does not accept that member’s category. Such an option cannot receive flow or be an accepted blocking destination. Unemployment remains available.

For each remaining option \(u\) of type \(t\), create an activity \(e=(t,u)\). Its incident vertices are \(t\) and the real programs used by \(u\). Set

\( b_t=\gamma_t,\qquad b_p=q_p,\qquad c_e=\gamma_t. \)

The contribution at the type vertex is \(a_{te}=1\). At a program vertex, \(a_{pe}\) is the number of members assigned there: either \(1\) or \(2\). Thus a common destination consumes twice the couple flow, exactly as required.

The unemployment activity is incident only with its type vertex. A partially unemployed couple activity has the type vertex and its one real destination.

At each type vertex, order activities by the given option preferences.

Auxiliary program preferences

For an activity \(e=(t,u)\) incident with program \(p\), define \(w_p(e)\) to be the worst-priority doctor category that \(u\) assigns to \(p\).

Order incident activities at \(p\) as follows:

  • Prefer the activity whose \(w_p(e)\) has higher original program priority.
  • If the worst categories coincide, prefer the activity that its type prefers.

The second rule is well-defined: a doctor category identifies its type. Consequently, two activities with the same worst category belong to the same type, whose option preferences are strict. These rules therefore give a strict order on all incident activities.

These auxiliary orders are a proof device. Stability will be checked using the original program priorities.

All vertex and activity capacities are positive, all incident contributions are positive, and the activity collection is finite. The theorem therefore applies.

4. Transferring domination to the stated blocking rule

Let \(z\) be an allocation supplied by the theorem.

Every type’s entire mass is assigned

Let \(e_t^\bot\) denote type \(t\)’s unemployment activity.

If \(z_{e_t^\bot}=\gamma_t\), the type capacity constraint implies that its total assigned mass is \(\gamma_t\).

Otherwise, \(e_t^\bot\) is unsaturated. Its only incident vertex is \(t\), so it must be dominated there. Since unemployment is last, every activity of \(t\) is weakly preferred to it. Domination gives

\( \sum_{e:\,t\in e}z_e=\gamma_t. \)

Thus all type equalities hold. Program capacity constraints also hold by construction, so \(z\) is a feasible assignment.

A preferred target cannot be dominated at its type

Suppose \(z_{(t,u)}>0\), and let \(v\) be a strictly preferred option of the same type.

Since total type flow is \(\gamma_t\),

\( z_{(t,v)} \le \gamma_t-z_{(t,u)} <\gamma_t=c_{(t,v)}. \)

Hence the target activity \((t,v)\) is unsaturated and must be dominated at some incident vertex.

It cannot be dominated at \(t\): the positive flow at the strictly worse option \(u\) is excluded from the domination sum, so that sum is strictly below \(\gamma_t\). Therefore the target is dominated at a real destination \(p\).

Domination at \(p\), together with its capacity constraint, implies both that its load is \(q_p\) and that every positive-flow activity incident with \(p\) is weakly preferred there to \((t,v)\). Indeed, any positive contribution from a strictly worse activity would make the domination sum strictly smaller than the total load.

The worst target member at the dominating program must be arriving

Let \(g=w_p((t,v))\).

Suppose the member of category \(g\) stays at \(p\) in the deviation from \(u\) to \(v\). Then \(u\) also assigns that member to \(p\). Consequently, \(w_p((t,u))\) has priority no higher than \(g\).

There are two cases.

  • If \(w_p((t,u))\) has strictly lower priority than \(g\), the primary auxiliary comparison ranks \((t,u)\) below \((t,v)\).
  • If their worst categories coincide, the secondary comparison ranks \((t,u)\) below \((t,v)\), because \(v\) is strictly preferred by type \(t\).

Both contradict the domination consequence above, since \((t,u)\) has positive flow and is incident with \(p\).

Therefore the member of category \(g\) is genuinely arriving at \(p\).

That arriving member is rejected

Every positive-flow activity at \(p\) is weakly preferred in the auxiliary order to \((t,v)\). Its worst doctor category therefore has original priority at least as high as \(g\). Hence every incumbent doctor at \(p\) has priority at least as high as \(g\), and their total mass is \(q_p\).

Under the original responsive choice rule, these incumbents precede a new arrival of category \(g\): higher-priority incumbents have priority, and equal-priority incumbents precede equal-priority arrivals. They already fill the quota. Other arrivals cannot reduce the mass ordered ahead of the arriving category-\(g\) member.

Thus no positive mass of that arriving member can be retained. The proposed deviation fails.

This argument applies to singles, distinct couple destinations, common destinations, and partial unemployment. For a common destination, the arriving worst-priority member’s rejection prevents both members from being retained together.

Therefore \(z\) has no blocking deviation, even allowing real-valued deviations. In particular, it satisfies the stated rational-deviation stability condition.

5. An explicit algorithm producing rational flows

We now give a finite rational linear-programming construction.

Let \(N\) be the number of remaining options, including unemployment. Then

\( N\le \ell, \qquad N\le |S|(m+1)+|K|(m+1)^2 \le \tau(m+1)^2. \)

At program \(p\), retain only supported doctor categories in its acceptance list and number them from best to worst:

\( 1,\ldots,d_p, \qquad d_p\le |S|+2|K|\le 2\tau. \)

Enumerate one state \(h_p\) for each program:

  • \(h_p=*\), imposing no rejection certificate;
  • \(h_p=r\in\{1,\ldots,d_p\}\), imposing a full quota with no doctor category worse than rank \(r\).

For a fixed state vector, construct an LP with one variable \(z_{t,u}\) per option.

Include nonnegativity, all type mass equalities, and all program capacity inequalities. For every \(h_p=r\), additionally impose:

  • program \(p\)’s load equals \(q_p\);
  • any option assigning a category of rank greater than \(r\) to \(p\) has zero flow.

In this state, call an arriving category \(g\) at \(p\) certifiably rejected if

\( h_p=r\ne *, \qquad \operatorname{rank}_p(g)\ge r. \)

Indeed, all incumbent mass at \(p\) then has priority at least as high as \(g\), and it fills \(q_p\).

For each current option \(u\) and strictly preferred target \(v\), identify the members who genuinely enter real programs. A couple member \(i\) is entering if its target destination \(v_i=p\) is real and its current destination satisfies \(u_i\ne p\).

If none of these entering members is certifiably rejected, impose

\( z_{t,u}=0. \)

There are at most two entering members per comparison. If there are none, this rule also imposes zero flow.

Solve the resulting rational LP and return any feasible solution. Continue enumerating states until one is feasible.

Soundness

For a positive-flow current option \(u\), every strictly preferred target \(v\) must have a certifiably rejected entering member; otherwise the LP would have forced \(z_{t,u}=0\).

The certificate ensures rejection under the original responsive rule for every positive deviation mass. Consequently, every feasible LP solution is stable.

A feasible state exists

Take the real stable assignment constructed in Section 4.

At each full program, choose \(h_p\) to be the rank of its worst positive-mass incumbent category. At every other program choose \(h_p=*\). This assignment satisfies the state’s capacity and zero-flow restrictions.

Moreover, the Section 4 proof established something stronger than stability: for every positive-flow current option and every preferred target, some genuinely arriving category \(g\) faces a destination full of incumbents with priority at least as high as \(g\).

At that destination, the chosen worst-incumbent rank is no greater than \(\operatorname{rank}_p(g)\). Thus \(g\) is certifiably rejected. No additional zero-flow constraint excludes the assignment.

Therefore at least one enumerated LP is feasible.

Rationality and output size

Every candidate LP has rational right-hand sides and integer coefficients of absolute value at most \(2\). Its feasible set is bounded because every option flow is bounded by its type mass.

A nonempty candidate LP therefore has a rational vertex.

More quantitatively, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses and quotas. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). At a vertex, choose \(N\) linearly independent active constraints. Their coefficient matrix \(B\) is integral, with entries of absolute value at most \(2\). Hadamard’s inequality gives

\( |\det B|\le (2\sqrt N)^N. \)

After multiplying the right-hand side by \(D\), Cramer’s rule bounds the numerator and denominator length of each flow by

\( O\!\left(L+N\log(N+1)\right). \)

Thus the algorithm produces an explicitly encoded rational stable assignment of polynomial total size.

6. Running times and scope of the conclusion

There are

\( \prod_{p\in P}(d_p+1)\le (2\tau+1)^m \)

state vectors.

For each state, checking all current/target preference comparisons takes \(O(\ell^2+m\tau)\) elementary operations. The resulting LP has \(N\le\ell\) variables, \(O(N+m+\tau)\) constraints, and polynomial encoding length. Rational LP feasibility and vertex extraction take polynomial bit time.

Consequently:

  • Existence decision: on promised valid inputs, read the input and answer yes, in \(O(L)\) time.
  • Construction: \((2\tau+1)^m\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,\ell,L)\) bit time.
  • Each output flow: \(O(L+\ell\log(\ell+1))\) bits.
  • Fixed \(m\): construction is polynomial-time.
  • Parameter \(m+\tau\): construction is fixed-parameter tractable.

The decision classification follows from universal rational existence. The construction bound above is exponential in general; no polynomial-time search bound is claimed.

The theorem supplies existence over the reals. The prover correctly postpones rationality to its separate LP argument.

The activity bound \(c_e=\gamma_t\) is consistent with the type-capacity bound. It introduces no restriction beyond the maximum possible flow for that option.

The auxiliary program order is well-defined and strict. A worst category uniquely identifies its type, including for the categories \((k,1)\) and \((k,2)\). Therefore activities with identical worst categories can be compared by that type’s strict option preferences. Activities with different worst categories are compared by strict program priority.

The argument correctly separates the two possibilities for the unemployment activity.

Thus the theorem’s allocation satisfies the required type equalities, as well as the program-capacity inequalities.

The prover correctly derives both consequences:

The domination sum is a sub-sum of total program load. It equals quota, while total load is at most quota. Thus the two sums coincide. Since incident contributions are positive, no strictly worse activity can have positive flow.

The prover covers both possible comparisons:

  • The same worst category places the current activity below the target through the type-preference tie-break.

Both contradict the consequence of domination, because the current activity has positive flow.

This reasoning respects member identities in ordered couples. It also applies when the target sends both members to the same program.
