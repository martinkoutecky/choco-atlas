The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite graph \(G=(V,E)\) with primary vertices \(V_p\), backbone vertices \(V_b\), and standard server-role types \(T=V_s\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), thresholds \(0<\theta_t\le\mu_t\), nonnegative reward densities \(q_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_tq_t=1\), and rational \(\epsilon\), let \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) be participating mass and declare role \(t\) active iff \(x_t\ge\theta_t\). Decide whether every \(x\) for which \(V_p\) is connected in \(G[V_p\cup V_b\cup\{t\in T:x_t\ge\theta_t\}]\) satisfies \(\sum_tq_tx_t\ge1-\epsilon\); equivalently, decide whether \(\min\{\sum_tq_tx_t:x\in[0,\mu],\ v_\theta(x)=1\}\ge1-\epsilon\), and return a violating \(x\) when not.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite simple undirected graph with a partition \(V=V_p\sqcup V_b\sqcup T\) into primary vertices, backbone vertices, and standard server roles; put \(m=|V|\). Assume \(|V_p|\ge2\) and that all primary vertices lie in one connected component of \(G\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type corresponds to one standard vertex. Require \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\).
Input also includes binary rational thresholds \(0<\theta_t\le\mu_t\), reward densities \(q_t\ge0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_tq_t=1\), and \(\epsilon\in\mathbb Q\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A coalition is any real vector \(x\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\). Role \(t\) is available exactly when \(x_t\ge\theta_t\); primary and backbone vertices are always available, including as intermediate vertices on paths.
Define \(v_\theta(x)=1\) exactly when all primary vertices lie in one connected component of \(G[V_p\cup V_b\cup\{t:x_t\ge\theta_t\}]\), and otherwise set \(v_\theta(x)=0\). Its allocated reward is \(p(x)=\sum_tq_tx_t\).
Decide whether every winning coalition satisfies \(p(x)\ge1-\epsilon\); on a negative answer, return a rational winning coalition with \(p(x)<1-\epsilon\).

Is this decision-and-witness problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?

Discrete \(\epsilon\)-core membership is coNP-complete even with equal rewards and a single backbone vertex; it is polynomial-time solvable on trees. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.0572)
No result settling this exact thresholded mass problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified a simple undirected graph and a disjoint exhaustive partition of vertices.
  • Required at least two primary vertices and connectivity of all primary vertices in the full graph, excluding the degenerate domains identified in the discrete model and ensuring consistency with unit total reward.
  • Used only positive-mass standard roles as the explicitly listed support; zero-mass entries are omitted.
  • Specified binary rational encoding for thresholds and reward densities, and defined m as the number of graph vertices and L as total input bit length.
  • Allowed arbitrary real participating masses while requiring a rational violating vector as output.
  • Preserved arbitrary rational epsilon and the weak membership inequality, so equality is accepted and violations are strict.
  • Specified polynomial-time Turing reductions for NP-hardness because membership has universal, coNP polarity.

Result

The decision-and-witness problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. More precisely, its decision part is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions.

Hardness holds even with a single backbone vertex, equal masses, reward densities \(q_t=1\), and strictly interior thresholds \(\theta_t=\mu_t/2\). Thus the result does not depend on taking the discrete boundary case \(\theta_t=\mu_t\).

1. Exact reduction to subsets of roles

Call \(S\subseteq T\) winning if all primary vertices belong to one connected component of \(G[V_p\cup V_b\cup S]\). Define its cost by

\[ c(S)=\sum_{t\in S}q_t\theta_t. \]

For each subset \(S\), define the rational coalition \(x^S\) by \(x^S_t=\theta_t\) when \(t\in S\), and \(x^S_t=0\) otherwise.

Because \(0<\theta_t\le\mu_t\), this coalition is feasible and its available roles are exactly \(S\). Consequently, \(x^S\) is winning exactly when \(S\) is winning, and \(p(x^S)=c(S)\).

Conversely, take any real coalition \(x\), and put \(S(x)=\{t\in T:x_t\ge\theta_t\}\). The coalitions \(x\) and \(x^{S(x)}\) have exactly the same available vertices. Moreover, nonnegativity of every \(q_t\) gives

\[ p(x)=\sum_{t\in T}q_tx_t \ge\sum_{t\in S(x)}q_t\theta_t =c(S(x)). \]

Therefore,

\[ \exists x\text{ winning with }p(x)<1-\epsilon \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists S\subseteq T\text{ winning with }c(S)<1-\epsilon. \]

In particular, every negative instance has a rational witness whose coordinates are copied from the input thresholds or are zero.

A subset \(S\) is a polynomial-size certificate: connectivity can be checked by graph traversal, and its cost can be compared with \(1-\epsilon\) using exact rational arithmetic. Products and sums of the input rationals have polynomial bit length. Hence the complement of the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), and the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\).

2. Reduction from Vertex Cover

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness of Vertex Cover, also used in the discrete connectivity-game hardness proof in [Bachrach, Porat, and Rosenschein, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.0572). The following construction handles the thresholded masses and the strict inequality explicitly.

Let \(H=(U,F)\) be a finite simple graph, with \(n=|U|\), and let \(k\) be the requested cover size. We may restrict to \(n\ge2\), \(F\ne\varnothing\), and \(0\le k<n\). Excluded instances have immediately decidable answers and can be replaced by fixed yes or no instances satisfying these restrictions, so the restricted problem remains \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\ge n\). Then \(n\le N<2n\).

Graph construction

Construct the target graph as follows:

  • There is one backbone vertex \(b\).
  • There is a primary vertex \(a\), and one primary vertex \(p_e\) for each edge \(e\in F\).
  • There is a standard vertex \(t_u\) for each \(u\in U\), together with \(N-n\) additional standard vertices.
  • Join \(a\) to \(b\), and join every standard vertex to \(b\).
  • For each edge \(e=\{u,v\}\in F\), join \(p_e\) to \(t_u\) and \(t_v\).
  • Add no other edges.

Thus \(\tau=N\), \(|V_p|=|F|+1\ge2\), and \(m=N+|F|+2\). The entire graph is connected: every standard vertex and \(a\) is adjacent to \(b\), and every \(p_e\) has a path to \(b\) through an endpoint’s standard vertex.

Numeric construction

For every standard role \(t\), set

\[ \mu_t=\frac1N,\qquad \theta_t=\frac1{2N},\qquad q_t=1. \]

These values satisfy all requirements:

\[ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1,\qquad \sum_{t\in T}\mu_tq_t=1,\qquad 0<\theta_t=\frac{\mu_t}{2}<\mu_t. \]

Set

\[ \epsilon=1-\frac{2k+1}{4N}. \]

All these numbers are dyadic rationals with encoding length \(O(\log n)\). Because \(N<2n\), both the explicit support and the entire constructed instance have polynomial size.

3. Correctness of the reduction

For \(S\subseteq T\), define the corresponding original vertices by \(C(S)=\{u\in U:t_u\in S\}\).

Claim. The subset \(S\) is winning exactly when \(C(S)\) is a vertex cover of \(H\).

Proof. Suppose \(S\) is winning. For an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), if neither \(t_u\) nor \(t_v\) belongs to \(S\), then \(p_e\) is isolated in the available induced graph. It cannot be connected to the distinct primary vertex \(a\), contradicting winning. Hence at least one endpoint of every edge belongs to \(C(S)\).

Conversely, suppose \(C(S)\) covers every edge. Each primary vertex \(p_e\) then has an available path \(p_e,t_u,b\) through a selected endpoint \(u\). The primary vertex \(a\) is directly adjacent to \(b\). Thus all primary vertices belong to the component containing \(b\), so \(S\) is winning. \(\square\)

The additional standard vertices have no incident edges to any \(p_e\), so they cannot help cover an original edge.

Every subset has cost \(c(S)=|S|/(2N)\). Therefore,

\[ c(S)<1-\epsilon \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{|S|}{2N}<\frac{2k+1}{4N} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2|S|<2k+1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |S|\le k, \]

where the last equivalence uses that \(|S|\) and \(k\) are integers.

If \(H\) has a vertex cover \(C\) of size at most \(k\), select precisely the roles \(t_u\) with \(u\in C\). The resulting canonical coalition is winning and satisfies \(p(x^S)<1-\epsilon\).

Conversely, if any real winning coalition violates the reward inequality, Section 1 yields a winning subset \(S\) with \(|S|\le k\). By the claim, \(C(S)\) is a vertex cover, and \(|C(S)|\le|S|\le k\).

We have proved

\[ (H,k)\text{ is a yes-instance of Vertex Cover} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed instance has answer no}. \]

4. Complexity and witnesses

The reduction proves that the complement of the decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Together with Section 1, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; equivalently, the decision problem is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete.

A single call to an oracle for the requested decision-and-witness problem decides Vertex Cover: construct the instance and reverse its decision answer. This establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions.

The real-valued domain creates no witness-size obstruction. Every violating coalition can be replaced by one with \(x_t\in\{0,\theta_t\}\), of polynomial encoding length. Thus the hardness is already present in choosing which roles to activate. A polynomial-time algorithm for the full problem would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Always-available primary vertices do not invalidate either implication. An uncovered edge’s primary vertex remains isolated, regardless of possible paths through other primary vertices. Padding roles cannot change this because they have no adjacency to any \(p_e\).
