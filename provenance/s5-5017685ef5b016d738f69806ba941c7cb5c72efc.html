Each type values at least one good, and every good is valued by some type. Zero coefficients omit goods from the utility; at \(\rho_t=0\), use Cobb–Douglas with coefficients normalized to sum to \(1\).
Let \(x_t(p)\) be the utility-maximizing bundle affordable with budget \(e_t\), and let \(z_j(p)=\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}(p)-1\). A market equilibrium has utility-maximizing per-capita bundles whose mass-weighted aggregate clears every positively priced good and does not exceed supply for any zero-priced good.
Start from positive prices. Updates are locally finite, every good is updated indefinitely, and its first update and every subsequent gap occur within one time unit. At an update time \(s\), let \(\alpha_j(s)\) be the previous update time for that good, or \(0\) initially, and choose \(\widetilde z_j\) between the minimum and maximum of \(z_j(p(r))\) over \(r\in(\alpha_j(s),s)\).
Update only that price by \(p_j(s)=p_j(s^-)[1+\lambda\min\{\widetilde z_j,1\}(s-\alpha_j(s))]\); simultaneous updates use an arbitrary fixed ordering. Set \(E=\max\{1/(1-\max_t\rho_t),1\}\) and fix \(0<\lambda\leq1/(26E)\).
For the convergence measure, define \(\phi(p)=\sum_jp_j+\sum_t\mu_t e_t\log\widehat u_t(p)\), where \(\widehat u_t(p)\) is optimal utility with unit spending. Linear convergence means convergence to an equilibrium with \(\phi(p(s))-\phi(p^*)\leq C\exp(-cs)\) for some \(C,c>0\) depending on the instance and initial prices. 1. Exact reduction to a finite market

Write \(u_t\) for type \(t\)’s utility. Construct a Fisher market with the same goods and supplies and exactly \(\tau\) buyers. Buyer \(t\) has utility \(u_t\) and budget

\(b_t:=\mu_t e_t>0.\)

Every utility in the question is homogeneous of degree one:

\(u_t(c x)=c\,u_t(x)\qquad(c>0).\)

For a nonzero CES exponent, this follows by factoring \(c^{\rho_t}\) from the defining sum. For Cobb–Douglas, it follows because the normalized exponents sum to one. Omitting zero coefficients does not affect this identity.

For any nonnegative price vector \(p\), the map \(x\mapsto y=\mu_t x\) is a bijection between the corresponding budget-feasible sets, since

\(p\cdot y\leq b_t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\cdot x\leq e_t.\)

Moreover, \(u_t(y)=\mu_t u_t(x)\), so this bijection preserves utility comparisons. Consequently, whenever maximizing bundles exist, their sets satisfy

\(\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{p\cdot y\leq b_t}u_t(y) =\mu_t\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{p\cdot x\leq e_t}u_t(x).\)

In particular, at positive prices the finite buyer’s demand is

\(y_t(p)=\mu_t x_t(p).\)

There is no ambiguity from demand selection at positive prices. On the valued coordinates, maximizing CES utility is equivalent to maximizing a strictly concave sum when \(0<\rho_t<1\), minimizing a strictly convex sum when \(\rho_t<0\), or maximizing a strictly concave weighted logarithmic sum when \(\rho_t=0\). In the latter two cases, a feasible bundle positive on every valued coordinate has positive utility, whereas a bundle missing a valued coordinate has zero utility. Thus these optimization arguments apply on the positive domain. Strict monotonicity forces full spending and excludes expenditure on unvalued goods.

It follows that the finite market’s excess demand is exactly

\(z_j^{\mathrm{fin}}(p)=\sum_t y_{tj}(p)-1 =\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}(p)-1=z_j(p).\)

2. Every admissible trajectory is preserved

First, prices remain positive at every finite time. Nonnegative demand gives \(z_j(p)\geq-1\), hence every permitted stale value satisfies \(\widetilde z_j\geq-1\). Writing \(\Delta=s-\alpha_j(s)\), the update multiplier obeys

\(1+\lambda\min\{\widetilde z_j,1\}\Delta \geq1-\lambda>0,\)

because \(\Delta\leq1\) and \(\lambda\leq1/(26E)\leq1/26\). Local finiteness allows induction over the finitely many updates in each bounded time interval.

Now give the constructed finite market the same initial prices, update times, simultaneous-update ordering, and stale values. Whenever the price histories coincide, the identity of excess demands makes their permitted stale-demand intervals identical. Their next updates therefore coincide. Induction proves equality of the entire trajectories.

3. Application of the finite CES theorem

Cheung–Cole’s Theorem 2 establishes linear convergence for finite CES Fisher markets with \(\max_t\rho_t<1\), \(\min_t\rho_t>-\infty\), and step size \(\lambda\leq1/(26E)\), under their rule (2). That rule permits arbitrary stale values between the interval’s minimum and maximum excess demands and assumes update gaps at most one. Section 3 explicitly allows simultaneous updates through arbitrary tie-breaking. Their potential analysis gives exponential decay in elapsed time, with constants depending on the starting prices. [Cheung–Cole, Theorem 2 and Sections 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/1806.10952v1).

All hypotheses hold here: there are finitely many buyers, their budgets are positive, every exponent is finite and less than one, the prescribed step-size bound is identical, and the timing and stale-demand conditions match. Thus the constructed market’s price trajectory converges to an equilibrium \(p^*\).

Its potential is exactly the question’s potential. Indeed, unit-spending optimal utility depends on the utility function and prices, and those are unchanged. Therefore

\(\phi_{\mathrm{fin}}(p) =\sum_jp_j+\sum_t b_t\log\widehat u_t(p) =\sum_jp_j+\sum_t\mu_t e_t\log\widehat u_t(p) =\phi(p).\)

Subtracting the equilibrium value merely normalizes the potential by a constant. Hence, with the chosen \(\lambda\) fixed, the theorem yields constants \(C,c>0\) such that

\(0\leq\phi(p(s))-\phi(p^*)\leq C\exp(-cs).\)

Finally, let \(y_t^*\) be equilibrium bundles in the finite market and set \(x_t^*:=y_t^*/\mu_t\). The optimization bijection proves that these are utility-maximizing per-capita bundles. Their aggregates satisfy

\(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}^*=\sum_t y_{tj}^*.\)

Thus they clear every positively priced good and do not exceed supply for any zero-priced good. The limiting equilibrium is therefore an equilibrium of the original society.

4. Scope of the conclusion

This settles convergence for every admissible instance and trajectory. It does not establish a polynomial bound in \(m,\tau,L\), or a bound on the number of updates: the guaranteed decay is in elapsed time, and local finiteness imposes no uniform bound on update frequency.
