The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(C\), \(k\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) approval types, compute a partial committee \(P\subseteq C\), with \(|P|\le k\), such that every size-\(k\) completion \(W\supseteq P\) has no type-weighted fractional \(\mathrm{FJR}+\) violation, where \(Z=\sum_t\mu_t z_t\), \(Y=\sum_c y_c\), and a Hare violation requires \(Z\ge Y/k\); likewise, require no Droop violation with \(Z>Y/(k+1)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge1\) candidates and let \(1\le k\le m\) be an integer. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) distinct approval types \(A_t\subseteq C\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval sets; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For a size-\(k\) committee \(W\), write \(u_t(W)=|A_t\cap W|\). A level-\(\ell\) fractional witness, where \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), consists of nonnegative real variables \(z_t,x_{tc},y_c\) satisfying
\(z_t=0\) whenever \(u_t(W)\ge\ell\); \(x_{tc}=0\) whenever \(c\notin A_t\); \(\sum_{c\in C}x_{tc}=\ell z_t\); \(x_{tc}\le y_c\) for every \(t,c\); and \(x_{tw}\le z_t\) for every \(t\) and \(w\in W\).
Set \(Z=\sum_t\mu_t z_t\) and \(Y=\sum_{c\in C}y_c\). A Hare violation requires \(Z>0\) and \(Z\ge Y/k\); a Droop violation requires \(Z>0\) and \(Z>Y/(k+1)\). There are no further upper bounds on witness variables.
For either quota variant, the computational problem is to output \(P\subseteq C\), with \(|P|\le k\), such that every size-\(k\) completion \(W\supseteq P\) has no violation of that variant at any level.

For the Hare and Droop variants separately, is this computational problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite explicitly listed electorates, Residual-Budget Greedy computes such a partial committee in polynomial time for each quota variant, with the guarantee holding for every permitted execution.
For finite electorates, checking a given committee for either kind of fractional violation is also polynomial-time solvable.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one and retained only distinct approval types with positive mass.
  • Restored all five fractional-witness constraints, including the elected-candidate bound, and required positive weighted voter mass.
  • Used unbounded nonnegative real witness variables and integer representation levels from one through k, matching the discrete definition.
  • Restricted k to an integer between one and the number of candidates; empty approval sets remain allowed.
  • Interpreted the Hare and Droop requests as separate quota variants, matching the two statements of the anchor.
  • Defined L as total input bit length and measured complexity in the support encoding rather than an expanded electorate.

Result

Both the Hare and Droop variants are solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

The essential step is to implement Residual-Budget Greedy using aggregate budgets for approval types. Expanding rational masses into individual voters is needed only for the correctness proof, not for the computation.

1. The finite-electorate theorem used

We use the finite-electorate guarantee supplied in the question: every permitted execution of Residual-Budget Greedy returns a partial committee whose every size-\(k\) completion has no fractional violation of the chosen variant.

For clarity, the permitted execution rules are as follows. Every voter starts with budget \(1\). Levels \(h=k,k-1,\ldots,1\) are processed in descending order. Given the currently selected set \(P\) and remaining voter budgets \(b_i\), an unelected candidate \(c\) has score
\(\displaystyle \sum_{i:\,c\in A_i,\ u_i(P)<h}\frac{b_i}{h-u_i(P)}.\)
A candidate may be purchased when this score reaches the common price. Its payments may be any nonnegative amounts totaling that price, with each eligible voter paying at most their displayed contribution limit. Purchases stop once \(k\) candidates have been selected. These are the RBG rules in [Teh, Section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11500v1#S3.SS2).

We will verify that our compressed algorithm implements such an execution exactly.

2. Algorithm on the support

Choose the normalized candidate price
\(\displaystyle q=\frac1k\)
for Hare, and
\(\displaystyle q=\frac1{k+1}\)
for Droop.

Maintain a selected set \(P\) and an aggregate residual budget \(r_t\) for each type. Initialize
\(\displaystyle P=\varnothing,\qquad r_t=\mu_t.\)

Fix candidate and type orders to make the algorithm deterministic. Process levels \(h=k,k-1,\ldots,1\). At each level, while \(|P|<k\), compute for every \(c\notin P\)
\(\displaystyle s_h(c)=\sum_{\substack{t:\,c\in A_t\\u_t(P)<h}}\frac{r_t}{h-u_t(P)}.\)

If no score is at least \(q\), advance to the next level. Otherwise choose the first candidate \(c\) whose score is at least \(q\), and purchase it as follows.

Start with an unpaid remainder \(R=q\). Process the eligible types in their fixed order. For each such type, define its aggregate payment by
\(\displaystyle d_t=\min\left\{\frac{r_t}{h-u_t(P)},R\right\},\)
and replace \(R\) by \(R-d_t\). Set \(d_t=0\) for ineligible types.

Because the sum of the contribution limits is at least \(q\), this procedure finishes with
\(\displaystyle \sum_t d_t=q.\)
Moreover, eligibility gives \(h-u_t(P)\ge1\), so
\(\displaystyle 0\le d_t\le\frac{r_t}{h-u_t(P)}\le r_t.\)
Thus the budget updates \(r_t\leftarrow r_t-d_t\) remain nonnegative. Add \(c\) to \(P\), recompute the scores, and continue.

Return \(P\) when all levels have been processed or \(|P|=k\). By construction, \(|P|\le k\).

3. Exact simulation of a finite electorate

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), where \(a_t,b_t\) are positive integers. Set
\(\displaystyle D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t,\qquad n_t=D\mu_t.\)
Every \(n_t\) is a positive integer, and normalization of the masses gives
\(\displaystyle \sum_t n_t=D.\)

Consider the conceptual finite electorate containing \(n_t\) voters of approval type \(A_t\). Its total number of voters is \(D\). We never explicitly construct this electorate.

We claim that the compressed algorithm simulates a permitted RBG execution on this electorate, with candidate price \(Dq\).

The simulation maintains the invariant that every voter of type \(t\) has the same remaining budget
\(\displaystyle b^{\mathrm{ind}}_t=\frac{r_t}{\mu_t}.\)
Initially this budget equals \(1\), as required.

Suppose the invariant holds before a purchase. For any eligible type \(t\), its \(n_t\) voters together contribute the following amount to the finite-electorate score:
\(\displaystyle n_t\frac{b^{\mathrm{ind}}_t}{h-u_t(P)} = D\frac{r_t}{h-u_t(P)}. \)
Consequently, the finite-electorate score of any candidate \(c\) is exactly \(D s_h(c)\). Candidate affordability therefore agrees in the two executions.

When the compressed algorithm charges type \(t\) the aggregate amount \(d_t\), charge each conceptual voter of that type
\(\displaystyle p^{\mathrm{ind}}_t=\frac{d_t}{\mu_t}.\)
The individual contribution limit is respected, because
\(\displaystyle p^{\mathrm{ind}}_t \le \frac{r_t/\mu_t}{h-u_t(P)} = \frac{b^{\mathrm{ind}}_t}{h-u_t(P)}. \)
Only eligible approvers pay. The candidate receives total payment
\(\displaystyle \sum_t n_t p^{\mathrm{ind}}_t = D\sum_t d_t = Dq. \)
Finally, every type-\(t\) voter’s new remaining budget is
\(\displaystyle b^{\mathrm{ind}}_t-p^{\mathrm{ind}}_t = \frac{r_t-d_t}{\mu_t}, \)
so the invariant persists.

Thus every purchase, level transition, and stopping decision agrees with a permitted finite-electorate execution. The finite prices are \(D/k\) for Hare and \(D/(k+1)\) for Droop, exactly the required prices. The supplied theorem therefore applies to the returned \(P\).

4. Transfer of the fractional-witness guarantee

Fix any size-\(k\) completion \(W\supseteq P\). Suppose it admits a support-level witness at some level \(\ell\).

Give every conceptual voter of type \(t\) the same witness variables \(z_t\) and \(x_{tc}\), and retain the candidate variables \(y_c\). Each individual witness constraint holds:

  • All voters of type \(t\) have utility \(u_t(W)\), so the required zero weights are preserved.
  • Approval restrictions and the row equality \(\sum_c x_{tc}=\ell z_t\) are preserved.
  • The inequalities \(x_{tc}\le y_c\) and \(x_{tw}\le z_t\) are preserved.

The finite-electorate total voter weight becomes
\(\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{fin}} = \sum_t n_t z_t = D\sum_t\mu_t z_t = DZ, \)
while the candidate weight remains \(Y\).

For Hare, \(Z>0\) and \(Z\ge Y/k\) therefore imply
\(\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{fin}}>0,\qquad Z_{\mathrm{fin}}\ge\frac{D}{k}Y.\)
This is a finite-electorate Hare violation.

For Droop, \(Z>0\) and \(Z>Y/(k+1)\) imply
\(\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{fin}}>0,\qquad Z_{\mathrm{fin}}>\frac{D}{k+1}Y.\)
This is a finite-electorate Droop violation; the strict inequality is preserved exactly.

Either case contradicts the finite-electorate guarantee. Since \(W\) and \(\ell\) were arbitrary, every size-\(k\) completion of the returned \(P\) has no violation at any level.

This argument permits arbitrary real witness variables and introduces no additional upper bounds.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

There are at most \(k\) purchases and \(k\) level transitions. Computing all candidate scores at one decision point takes \(O(m\tau)\) rational operations. Collecting a payment and updating utilities takes \(O(\tau)\) operations. Hence the total number of arithmetic operations is \(O(km\tau)\).

It remains to bound their operand lengths.

Let
\(\displaystyle Q=(k+1)!.\)
Every possible positive gap \(h-u_t(P)\), and both possible price denominators, divide \(Q\).

We prove by induction that after \(j\) purchases, every residual-budget denominator divides \(DQ^j\). Initially all mass denominators divide \(D\). Before the next purchase, dividing a residual budget by a positive gap produces a denominator dividing \(DQ^{j+1}\). The price \(q\) also has denominator dividing this integer. Thus all scores, unpaid remainders, payments selected by minimum operations, and updated residual budgets have denominators dividing \(DQ^{j+1}\). This establishes the induction.

All these quantities lie between \(0\) and \(1\): residual budgets are nonnegative and sum to at most \(1\), contribution limits do not exceed their residual budgets, and candidate scores do not exceed their sum. Their numerators therefore have the same asymptotic bit bound as their denominators.

Since
\(\displaystyle \log_2 D\le L,\qquad \log_2 Q=O(k\log(k+1)),\)
every rational manipulated by the algorithm has bit length
\(\displaystyle O\!\left(L+k^2\log(k+1)\right).\)
Exact arithmetic and comparisons consequently take polynomial time. Combined with the operation count, this proves the claimed polynomial-time bound for both variants.
