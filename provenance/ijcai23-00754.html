The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given fixed \(p,q\), rational \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^p\) with \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\), rational per-capita item supply \(\rho\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^q\), and integer valuations \(v_{a,b}\), compute the largest \(T\) for which there exists a finite-support family \(x_{a,z}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\), indexed by \(a\in[p]\) and \(z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^q\), satisfying \(\sum_zx_{a,z}=\mu_a\), \(\sum_{a,z}z_bx_{a,z}=\rho_b\), and \(x_{a,z}>0\Rightarrow\sum_bv_{a,b}z_b\ge T\), together with such an allocation plan.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Write \(p=\tau\) and \(m=q\) for the number of item types; both are fixed positive integers.
The input also gives binary-rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_b\ge0\), with \(\sum_{b=1}^{q}\rho_b\ge1\), and binary-encoded valuations \(v_{a,b}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). Bundle \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{q}\) has utility \(\sum_{b=1}^{q}v_{a,b}z_b\) for type \(a\), including utility zero for the empty bundle.
An allocation plan is a finite list of positive binary-rational masses \(x_{a,z}\), with all unlisted entries zero, satisfying \(\sum_zx_{a,z}=\mu_a\) for every \(a\) and \(\sum_{a,z}z_bx_{a,z}=\rho_b\) for every \(b\). There is no individual bundle-size bound.
Its guaranteed utility is the minimum utility among its positive-mass bundles. The computational problem is to compute the largest feasible guaranteed utility \(T\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) and an attaining allocation plan. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

For every fixed \(p,q\), is this exact optimization problem solvable in polynomial time in \(L\), or is it NP-hard for some fixed \(p,q\)?

Discrete max-min fair allocation with nonnegative additive integer valuations is polynomial-time solvable when both agent-type and item-type counts are fixed ([verified result](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0754.pdf)). No complexity result for precisely the mass model above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted the unspecified signs of integer valuations to nonnegative integers, matching the discrete goods model.
  • Removed zero-mass types from the supplied list and set p equal to the support size tau.
  • Carried over the discrete assumption that items number at least agents as total per-capita supply at least one.
  • Specified a nonnegative integer threshold, since every bundle utility is a nonnegative integer.
  • Used m=q for the number of item types in the continuous encoding, which has no finite total item count.
  • Specified binary encoding of valuations and an explicit finite-list representation of the allocation plan; retained unbounded integer bundles as in the graded question.

Result and encoding convention

The exact optimization problem is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed \(m,\tau\). In fact, fixing \(m\) alone suffices: an algorithm computes the optimum and an attaining plan in time

\(2^{O(m^3)}(\tau+m+L)^{O(1)}.\)

An attaining plan can be chosen with at most \(\tau+m\) positive entries, each of polynomial encoding length.

I interpret “binary-rational” as a rational represented by binary-encoded numerator and denominator. Thus allocation masses may have arbitrary rational denominators. This is the encoding convention used throughout the proof.

1. Exact supplies can be replaced by upper bounds

Call a finite rational plan a packing plan if it assigns each type its full mass and uses at most the available supply:

\(\sum_z x_{a,z}=\mu_a\quad\text{for every }a,\qquad \sum_{a,z}z_bx_{a,z}\le\rho_b\quad\text{for every }b.\)

Lemma 1. Every packing plan can be converted into a plan using exactly the supplies, without decreasing any recipient’s utility. If the packing plan has \(N\) positive entries, the construction produces at most \(N+m\) positive entries.

Proof. Let the unused supply of item type \(b\) be

\(r_b=\rho_b-\sum_{a,z}z_bx_{a,z}\ge0.\)

Write \(r_b=k_b+d_b\), where \(k_b=\lfloor r_b\rfloor\) and \(0\le d_b<1\).

Arrange the \(N\) entries as consecutive intervals partitioning \([0,1)\), with each interval’s length equal to its entry’s mass. This is possible because total population mass is \(1\). Split these intervals at the points \(d_1,\ldots,d_m\). There are at most \(N+m\) resulting nonempty intervals, all with rational endpoints.

On every resulting interval, add \(k_b\) items of type \(b\) to its original bundle. Add one further item of type \(b\) exactly on the intervals contained in \([0,d_b)\). These additions consume

\(k_b\cdot1+d_b=r_b\)

units of per-capita supply of type \(b\). Thus the new plan uses every supply exactly and preserves every type’s mass. All bundle additions are integral. Since all valuations are nonnegative, no utility decreases. \(\square\)

This construction uses the absence of an individual bundle-size bound.

2. Threshold feasibility has bounded configurations

Fix an integer threshold \(t\ge0\), and define

\(C_a(t)=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}: z_b\le t\text{ for every }b,\ \sum_bv_{a,b}z_b\ge t\}.\)

These sets are finite. Every configuration has encoding length \(O(m\log(t+2))\), although their number may be exponentially large.

Lemma 2. Threshold \(t\) is feasible in the original model if and only if there is a packing plan supported on the sets \(C_a(t)\).

Proof. First suppose an original plan guarantees utility at least \(t\). Replace every bundle \(z\) assigned to type \(a\) by the coordinatewise truncation

\(z'_b=\min\{z_b,t\}.\)

This preserves the utility threshold. Indeed:

  • If no coordinate with positive valuation is truncated, the utility is unchanged.
  • Otherwise, some truncated coordinate \(b\) satisfies \(v_{a,b}\ge1\), because valuations are nonnegative integers. That coordinate alone then contributes \(v_{a,b}t\ge t\).

For \(t=0\), the assertion holds directly. Truncation can only reduce resource consumption. Coalescing identical resulting entries therefore gives a packing plan supported on \(C_a(t)\).

Conversely, apply Lemma 1 to any such packing plan. Every bundle initially has utility at least \(t\), and adding items preserves this property. The resulting plan satisfies the original supply equalities. \(\square\)

3. A finite configuration LP decides feasibility

Consider the following LP, with one nonnegative variable for every \(a\) and \(z\in C_a(t)\):

\(\displaystyle F(t)=\max\sum_{a=1}^{\tau}\sum_{z\in C_a(t)}x_{a,z},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{z\in C_a(t)}x_{a,z}\le\mu_a\qquad(a=1,\ldots,\tau),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a=1}^{\tau}\sum_{z\in C_a(t)}z_bx_{a,z}\le\rho_b\qquad(b=1,\ldots,m),\)

and \(x_{a,z}\ge0\).

The zero vector is feasible, and every feasible vector has objective at most \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\). Because this is a finite LP with every variable bounded by its type’s mass, its optimum is attained.

Lemma 3. Threshold \(t\) is feasible if and only if \(F(t)=1\).

Proof. A packing plan supported on \(C_a(t)\) is an LP solution of objective \(1\).

Conversely, if an LP solution has objective \(1\), define the nonnegative type deficits

\(\delta_a=\mu_a-\sum_{z\in C_a(t)}x_{a,z}.\)

Their sum is \(1-1=0\). Consequently every deficit is zero, so each type receives its full mass. The solution is therefore a packing plan, and Lemma 2 applies.

Since the LP data are rational, an optimal basic solution is rational. Thus this argument supplies masses allowed by the stated encoding convention. \(\square\)

The dual LP is

\(\displaystyle \min\sum_a\mu_a\alpha_a+\sum_b\rho_bc_b,\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \alpha_a+\sum_bc_bz_b\ge1\qquad(a=1,\ldots,\tau,\ z\in C_a(t)),\)

and \(\alpha_a\ge0,\ c_b\ge0\).

It has only \(\tau+m\) variables. It is feasible, for example at \(\alpha_a=1,\ c_b=0\), with objective \(1\).

4. Exact dual separation uses only \(m\) integer variables

We construct a strong separation oracle that returns an actual violated defining inequality.

Given rational \((\alpha,c)\), first check all nonnegativity inequalities. If they hold, then for each type \(a\), test whether there exists an integer vector \(z\) satisfying

\(0\le z_b\le t\quad(b=1,\ldots,m),\qquad \sum_bv_{a,b}z_b\ge t,\qquad \alpha_a+\sum_bc_bz_b<1.\)

The strict rational inequality can be expressed as a non-strict integer inequality. Choose a positive common denominator \(D\) for \(\alpha_a,c_1,\ldots,c_m\), and set

\(A=D\alpha_a\in\mathbb Z,\qquad C_b=Dc_b\in\mathbb Z.\)

For integer \(z\), the strict inequality is equivalent to

\(\displaystyle \sum_bC_bz_b\le D-A-1.\)

We have therefore obtained an integer linear feasibility problem with exactly \(m\) variables and \(2m+2\) inequalities. Its encoding length is polynomial in the original data, \(\log(t+2)\), and the oracle query’s encoding length. Clearing denominators does not cause an exponential increase in bit length: the bit length of their product is at most the sum of their bit lengths.

Fixed-dimensional integer programming decides this feasibility problem and returns a witness when one exists. A bound \(2^{O(m^3)}\operatorname{poly}(\ell)\), where \(\ell\) is the integer program’s encoding length, suffices. This is the classical fixed-dimensional integer-programming bound discussed in [Kannan’s primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/800061.808749).

A witness \(z\) identifies a violated dual constraint. If none exists for any type, every dual constraint holds. Thus exact separation costs at most \(\tau\) fixed-dimensional integer-programming calls.

Exact optimization and primal recovery

We use the standard exact ellipsoid theorem with dual recovery: for a rational LP with \(r\) rows, uniformly bounded column encoding length, and a strong separation oracle for its dual that returns defining inequalities, one can compute the exact optimum and recover an optimal primal solution supported on polynomially many columns in oracle-polynomial time. This follows from the strong optimization and oracle-inequality dual-recovery results in Chapter 6, particularly Lemma 6.5.15, of [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4).

All required hypotheses hold here:

  • The configuration LP is finite, feasible, and bounded.
  • It has \(r=\tau+m\) rows.
  • Its columns are \((e_a,z)\), with integer entries bounded by \(\max\{1,t\}\).
  • Every oracle output is an original nonnegativity or configuration inequality, with uniformly polynomial encoding length.
  • The objective coefficients and right-hand sides are explicitly given rationals.

Consequently, \(F(t)\) can be computed exactly, and, when \(F(t)=1\), a rational packing plan can be recovered. Taking an optimal basic solution of the recovered finite LP gives at most \(\tau+m\) positive configuration variables: after introducing slack variables, there are only \(\tau+m\) equations.

The threshold test runs in time

\(2^{O(m^3)}\bigl(\tau+m+L+\log(t+2)\bigr)^{O(1)}.\)

5. Binary search finds the optimum

For each item type, let \(V_b=\max_a v_{a,b}\), and define

\(U=\left\lfloor\sum_bV_b\rho_b\right\rfloor.\)

For any original plan guaranteeing utility \(t\), total population mass is \(1\), so

\(\displaystyle t\le\sum_{a,z}x_{a,z}\sum_bv_{a,b}z_b\le\sum_bV_b\sum_{a,z}z_bx_{a,z}=\sum_bV_b\rho_b.\)

Thus every feasible threshold satisfies \(t\le U\).

Threshold \(0\) is feasible: start with the empty bundle for every type and apply Lemma 1. Feasibility is monotone in the threshold, since a plan guaranteeing \(t\) also guarantees every smaller nonnegative threshold. Therefore binary search over the integers in \([0,U]\) finds the largest feasible threshold \(T\).

The integer \(U\) has \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits. Hence binary search makes polynomially many threshold tests, each with \(\log(t+2)=O(L+\log(m+1))\). The total running time is

\(2^{O(m^3)}(\tau+m+L)^{O(1)}.\)

6. Constructing a sparse, polynomial-size attaining plan

Recover a basic packing plan for threshold \(T\). It has at most \(\tau+m\) positive entries. Apply Lemma 1 to obtain an exact-supply plan with at most \(\tau+2m\) entries, all guaranteeing utility at least \(T\).

For every resulting bundle, its coordinate of item type \(b\) is at most

\(T+\lfloor\rho_b\rfloor+1.\)

Indeed, the original configuration coordinate is at most \(T\), and the surplus construction adds at most \(\lfloor r_b\rfloor+1\le\lfloor\rho_b\rfloor+1\). These coordinates therefore have polynomial bit length.

We can compress the resulting plan further. Retain its finite set of bundle entries and solve the explicit rational feasibility LP imposing the \(\tau\) type-mass equalities and \(m\) supply equalities. The constructed plan witnesses feasibility. A basic feasible solution has at most \(\tau+m\) positive entries, and all retained bundles still guarantee utility at least \(T\).

The masses also have polynomial encoding length. To verify this directly, let \(r=\tau+m\), and let \(M\ge1\) bound the entries of the final equality matrix. That matrix is integral, and \(\log M=O(L+\log(m+1))\). Clear all input denominators with a common denominator \(D\), whose bit length is at most \(L\). For a nonsingular basis of rank \(s\le r\), Hadamard’s inequality gives

\(\lvert\det B\rvert\le s^{s/2}M^s.\)

Cramer’s rule expresses every basic mass with denominator dividing \(D\lvert\det B\rvert\). Each mass lies in \([0,1]\), so its numerator and denominator have

\(O\bigl(L+r\log(r+1)+r\log M\bigr)\)

bits. The complete output thus has polynomial length.

Finally, the returned plan’s guaranteed utility is exactly \(T\). If its minimum utility exceeded \(T\), integrality of bundle utilities would make threshold \(T+1\) feasible, contradicting the binary-search optimum.

This proves both exact optimization and construction of an attaining allocation plan.
