The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_1,\mu_2\), rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_o\), nonnegative additive values \(v_t(o)\), and fixed \(\epsilon>0\), find a finite-support assignment \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\) over integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^q\), with \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}B_o=\rho_o\). For each \((t,B)\) with \(x_{t,B}>0\), require either \(v_t(B)\ge v_t(C)-v_t(o)\) for every occupied bundle \(C\) and every \(o\) with \(C_o>0\), or \(v_t(B)\ge (1-\epsilon)M_t(\rho)\), where \(M_t(\rho)=\max_y\min_{B:y_B>0}v_t(B)\) over finite-support distributions satisfying \(\sum_B y_B=1\) and \(\sum_B y_BB_o=\rho_o\).

Result and interpretation

For every fixed rational $\epsilon\in(0,1)$, the problem is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, the construction gives every occupied type–bundle pair its $(1-\epsilon)$-MMS guarantee; the EFX alternative is unnecessary.

As in the notes, “binary-rational” means a rational whose numerator and denominator are encoded in binary.

The proof works for any finite number of valuation types. Its key feature is that each type can receive a different distribution with the same mean supply vector $\rho$.

1. Separating the valuation types

Suppose that, for each type $t$, we construct a finite-support rational distribution $y^{(t)}$ satisfying
$$ \sum_B y^{(t)}_B=1, \qquad \sum_B y^{(t)}_B B_o=\rho_o, $$
and
$$ v_t(B)\geq(1-\epsilon)M_t(\rho) \quad\text{whenever }y^{(t)}_B>0. $$

Define
$$ x_{t,B}=\mu_t y^{(t)}_B, $$
omitting zero masses. Then
$$ \sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t $$
and, for every category $o$,
$$ \sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}B_o =\sum_t\mu_t\rho_o =\rho_o. $$
Every occupied pair satisfies the required MMS alternative.

Thus it suffices to solve the following single-valuation problem in polynomial time:

Given nonnegative rational values $a_o$ and supplies $\rho_o$, construct a rational finite-support distribution with mean $\rho$ whose minimum supported value is at least $(1-\epsilon)M(\rho)$.

Write
$$ a(B)=\sum_o a_oB_o, \qquad U=\sum_o a_o\rho_o. $$

2. Attainment and bounds for the benchmark

Choose a positive integer $d$ clearing all valuation denominators, so that $da_o$ is integral for every $o$. The product of the input denominators suffices, and its binary encoding has polynomial length.

For every feasible distribution $y$, additivity gives
$$ \sum_B y_Ba(B)=U. $$
Consequently, its minimum supported value is at most $U$.

Every integral bundle has value in $d^{-1}\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$. Hence the attainable supported minima belong to the finite set
$$ \left\{\frac jd:0\leq j\leq\lfloor dU\rfloor\right\}. $$

The feasible-distribution set is nonempty: independently round each coordinate of $\rho$ to its floor or ceiling, preserving its expectation. This produces a finite-support distribution with mean $\rho$.

The set of attainable minima is therefore finite and nonempty. Its maximum is attained. In particular,
$$ 0\leq M(\rho)\leq U, $$
and
$$ M(\rho)>0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M(\rho)\geq\frac1d. $$

We will use these bounds to obtain a polynomial-length list of candidate thresholds.

3. A rounding lemma with exact coordinate means

The next lemma converts a nonnegative rational vector into integral bundles while losing at most one item’s value.

Lemma. Let $s\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^r$ and let $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ be nonnegative rational weights. Put
$$ A=\max_i a_i, $$
with $A=0$ when $r=0$. In polynomial time, one can construct a rational distribution on at most $r+1$ integral vectors $R$ such that
$$ \mathbb E[R]=s $$
and every supported vector satisfies
$$ a(R)\geq a(s)-A. $$
Each coordinate $R_i$ is either $\lfloor s_i\rfloor$ or $\lceil s_i\rceil$.

Proof. The case $r=0$ is immediate. Otherwise write
$$ n_i=\lfloor s_i\rfloor, \qquad f_i=s_i-n_i. $$
Start with mass $1$ on the vector $n$.

Process the coordinates one at a time. When processing coordinate $i$, order the current atoms by their values $a(R)$. Select the lowest-valued mass of total size $f_i$, splitting the atom at the selection boundary if necessary. Add one unit of coordinate $i$ to precisely this selected mass.

This operation increases the expectation of coordinate $i$ by $f_i$ and changes no other coordinate’s expectation. Each coordinate is processed once, so the final mean is $s$.

There is at most one boundary split per coordinate. Thus the final distribution has at most $r+1$ atoms.

We prove that the range of supported values never exceeds the largest weight processed so far. Suppose the old range is $D$. Before an insertion, every selected atom has value at most every unselected atom.

Values within either group retain their previous differences. For a selected atom of old value $z$ and an unselected atom of value $w$, we have $z\leq w$, and after insertion
$$ (z+a_i)-w\leq a_i, $$
while
$$ w-(z+a_i)\leq w-z\leq D. $$
Therefore the new range is at most $\max\{D,a_i\}$. Initially the range is zero, so the final range is at most $A$.

The final average value equals $a(s)$. A finite distribution whose value range is at most $A$ has minimum value at least its average minus $A$. This proves the claimed lower bound.

All operations are rational comparisons, additions, and subtractions on a polynomial number of atoms. To verify the encoding bound, let $Q$ be the product of the denominators of the $f_i$. Every atom mass remains an integer multiple of $1/Q$ between zero and one. Thus masses have polynomial bit length. Bundle coordinates are floors or ceilings of the input coordinates, so their bit lengths are polynomial as well. $\square$

4. A polynomial-size relaxation for a candidate threshold

Fix a rational constant $\delta\in(0,1)$ and a positive rational threshold $T$. We construct a linear program with these two properties:

  • If $T\leq M(\rho)$, the program is feasible.
  • From any feasible solution, we can construct an exact-mean distribution whose supported values are all at least $(1-\delta)T$.

Partition the categories into
$$ G=\{o:a_o\geq\delta T\}, \qquad S=\{o:a_o<\delta T\}. $$
Call them large and small categories, respectively. Zero-valued categories belong to $S$.

Set
$$ K=\left\lceil\frac1\delta\right\rceil. $$
Enumerate the configurations
$$ \mathcal D= \left\{ D\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^m: D_o=0\text{ for }o\in S,\ \sum_{o\in G}D_o\leq K \right\}. $$
For fixed $\delta$,
$$ |\mathcal D| =\binom{|G|+K}{K} \leq(m+1)^K, $$
so this enumeration is polynomial in $m$.

Define
$$ b_D=\max\{0,T-a(D)\}, \qquad W=\sum_{o\in S}a_o\rho_o. $$

Use one variable $p_D$ for each configuration and impose
$$ \begin{aligned} p_D&\geq0 &&\text{for every }D\in\mathcal D,\\ \sum_{D\in\mathcal D}p_D&=1,\\ \sum_{D\in\mathcal D}p_DD_o&\leq\rho_o &&\text{for every }o\in G,\\ \sum_{D\in\mathcal D}p_Db_D&\leq W. \end{aligned} $$

The last inequality treats small goods through their total available value. We now justify both properties.

4.1 Every attainable threshold is accepted

Assume $T\leq M(\rho)$. By Section 2, there is a feasible finite-support distribution $y$ with every supported bundle worth at least $T$.

For each supported bundle $B$, choose a subbundle $D$ of its large goods as follows.

If the large goods in $B$ have total value below $T$, retain all of them. Their number is less than $1/\delta$, because each has value at least $\delta T$. Hence $D\in\mathcal D$.

If the large goods have total value at least $T$, take large goods until their total first reaches $T$. At most $K$ goods are needed: any $K$ large goods have total value at least
$$ K\delta T\geq T. $$
Again $D\in\mathcal D$, and now $b_D=0$.

In both cases $D\leq B$ coordinatewise. Moreover,
$$ b_D\leq\sum_{o\in S}a_oB_o. $$
For the first case this follows from $a(B)\geq T$; for the second case the left side is zero.

Aggregate the masses of bundles mapped to each configuration. The resulting $p_D$ sum to one, satisfy the large-category supply inequalities, and satisfy
$$ \sum_Dp_Db_D \leq \sum_By_B\sum_{o\in S}a_oB_o =W. $$
Thus the linear program is feasible.

4.2 Every accepted threshold yields an approximate distribution

Let $p$ be a rational feasible solution.

First suppose $W>0$. For each configuration with $p_D>0$, define a vector of small-good means by
$$ s^D_o= \begin{cases} \dfrac{b_D}{W}\rho_o,&o\in S,\\ 0,&o\in G. \end{cases} $$
Its value is exactly
$$ a(s^D)=b_D. $$

Apply the rounding lemma to $s^D$, restricted to the small categories. Every resulting integral small-good bundle $R$ satisfies
$$ a(R)\geq b_D-A_S, $$
where
$$ A_S=\max_{o\in S}a_o, $$
with $A_S=0$ if $S$ is empty. By the definition of small categories,
$$ A_S\leq\delta T. $$
Therefore every conditional bundle $D+R$ satisfies
$$ \begin{aligned} a(D+R) &\geq a(D)+b_D-A_S\\ &\geq T-\delta T\\ &=(1-\delta)T. \end{aligned} $$

Choose $D$ with probability $p_D$, then use its conditional rounding distribution. Let $z$ be the resulting mean vector. For every large category,
$$ z_o=\sum_Dp_DD_o\leq\rho_o. $$
For every small category,
$$ z_o =\frac{\sum_Dp_Db_D}{W}\rho_o \leq\rho_o. $$
Thus $z\leq\rho$ coordinatewise.

If $W=0$, feasibility and nonnegativity imply
$$ p_D>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_D=0. $$
In this case use $D$ itself, with no small-good additions. Every supported configuration has value at least $T$, and its mean again satisfies $z\leq\rho$.

In either case, put
$$ r=\rho-z\geq0. $$
Apply the rounding lemma to $r$ to obtain an integral-vector distribution with mean $r$. Take this residual vector independently of the previously constructed bundle and add it to that bundle.

The resulting mean is exactly $\rho$. All added coordinates and all values are nonnegative, so every supported value remains at least $(1-\delta)T$.

This construction is explicit and has polynomial support. There are at most
$$ |\mathcal D|(m+1)^2 $$
supported bundles before merging duplicates: at most $m+1$ conditional rounding outcomes per configuration, followed by at most $m+1$ residual outcomes.

We have proved:
$$ T\leq M(\rho) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{the program is feasible}, $$
and
$$ \text{the program is feasible} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{we can construct a distribution of minimum value at least }(1-\delta)T. $$

The program need not decide exact feasibility at threshold $T$. These implications are sufficient.

5. Obtaining the required multiplicative guarantee

Set
$$ \delta=\frac{\epsilon}{2}. $$
This is a fixed positive rational constant.

If $U<1/d$, Section 2 implies $M(\rho)=0$. Return any rational floor-or-ceiling distribution with mean $\rho$.

Otherwise enumerate the thresholds
$$ T_j=\frac1d(1+\delta)^j, \qquad j=0,\ldots,J, $$
where $J$ is the largest index satisfying $T_J\leq U$. Test the linear program from Section 4 at each threshold.

If no threshold is accepted, then $M(\rho)=0$: otherwise $M(\rho)\geq1/d$, and Section 4.1 would ensure acceptance of $T_0$. Again return any exact-mean floor-or-ceiling distribution.

If at least one threshold is accepted, choose the largest accepted threshold $\widehat T$ and construct its distribution using Section 4.2.

When $M(\rho)>0$, let $j^\star$ be the largest index satisfying
$$ T_{j^\star}\leq M(\rho). $$
This index belongs to the enumerated list because $M(\rho)\leq U$. Its program is feasible, so
$$ \widehat T\geq T_{j^\star}. $$
The spacing of the thresholds gives
$$ M(\rho)<(1+\delta)T_{j^\star}. $$
Consequently, the constructed minimum value is at least
$$ (1-\delta)\widehat T \geq \frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta}M(\rho). $$
Finally,
$$ \frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta} =1-\frac{2\delta}{1+\delta} \geq1-2\delta =1-\epsilon. $$
Thus every supported bundle has value at least $(1-\epsilon)M(\rho)$.

The case $M(\rho)=0$ satisfies the same guarantee by nonnegativity.

6. Polynomial running time and output length

I use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: feasibility of a finite rational linear program can be decided in time polynomial in its dimensions and total coefficient bit length, and a feasible rational point of polynomial encoding length can be returned.

Here its hypotheses and encoding bounds hold as follows.

The integer $d$ has polynomial bit length. Also $U$ has polynomial bit length. For fixed $\delta$, the number of candidate thresholds is
$$ O_\delta\bigl(1+\log(dU)\bigr), $$
which is polynomial in the input length. Each threshold is obtained by a polynomial number of multiplications by the fixed rational $1+\delta$, so each has polynomial bit length.

For each threshold, $K=\lceil1/\delta\rceil$ is constant. The program has at most $(m+1)^K$ variables and at most $m+2$ constraints besides nonnegativity. Every configuration coordinate is at most $K$, and all coefficients $b_D$, $W$, and $\rho_o$ have polynomial bit length. Rational linear programming therefore applies in polynomial time.

The subsequent construction uses only polynomially many rational arithmetic operations. Division by nonzero $W$ preserves polynomial encoding length: the numerator and denominator of $W$ already have polynomial bit length. The rounding lemma provides polynomial-size distributions with polynomial-length masses and bundle coordinates. Their finite products and aggregation preserve these bounds.

Finally, perform this algorithm independently for each type and scale its masses by $\mu_t$, as in Section 1. The running time is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$ for every fixed $\epsilon$.

The output can additionally be compressed to at most $m+\tau$ occupied pairs. To see this, restrict variables to the polynomially many pairs already constructed and impose
$$ z_{t,B}\geq0, \qquad \sum_Bz_{t,B}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_{t,B}z_{t,B}B=\rho. $$
This is a nonempty bounded rational polytope. A basic feasible solution has at most $m+\tau$ positive variables, because its positive columns $(e_t,B)$ are linearly independent in a space of dimension $m+\tau$. Such a solution can be computed by rational linear programming in polynomial time. Every retained bundle already satisfies its type’s approximate MMS threshold, so this compression preserves the guarantee.

7. Conclusion

Every valid instance admits an assignment, and such an assignment can be computed directly from the compressed rational input in polynomial time for every fixed rational $\epsilon\in(0,1)$.

The construction gives every occupied pair $(t,B)$
$$ v_t(B)\geq(1-\epsilon)M_t(\rho). $$
It therefore meets the required disjunction, including all conventions concerning empty bundles and zero-valued categories.
