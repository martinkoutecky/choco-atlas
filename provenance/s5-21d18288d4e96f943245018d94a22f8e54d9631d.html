Theorem 1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given explicit time slots \(H\), a finite support \(T\) of request types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), per-capita capacities \(\kappa_q\), and one-operation bids with common duration \(p\), prices \(v_t\), release times \(r_t\), and deadlines \(d_t\) satisfying \(d_t-r_t-p\le 2\), compute maximum revenue. For each type \(t\), let \(S_t\) be its feasible start slots and \(I_{t,s}\) the slots occupied at start \(s\). Choose masses \(x_{t,s}\ge0\) to maximize \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{s\in S_t}v_tx_{t,s}\), subject to \(\sum_{s\in S_t}x_{t,s}\le\mu_t\) for each \(t\), and \(\sum_{t,s:q\in I_{t,s}}x_{t,s}\le\kappa_q\) for each \(q\in H\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) request types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
There is one resource with an explicitly listed horizon \(H=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\) of unit slots and binary-rational per-capita capacities \(\kappa_q\ge0\).
Each type requests one nonpreemptive operation of common integer duration \(p\ge1\), has binary-rational price \(v_t\ge0\), and integer release time and deadline satisfying \(0\le r_t<d_t\le m\) and \(0\le d_t-r_t-p\le2\).
Define \(S_t=\{s\in\mathbb Z:r_t\le s\le d_t-p\}\) and \(I_{t,s}=\{s,\ldots,s+p-1\}\): completion exactly at the deadline is allowed.
An allocation chooses real masses \(x_{t,s}\ge0\) with \(\sum_{s\in S_t}x_{t,s}\le\mu_t\) for every type and \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{s\in S_t:q\in I_{t,s}}x_{t,s}\le\kappa_q\) for every slot. Mass may be divided among starts or left unserved; every served individual receives an entire contiguous operation.
Revenue is \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{s\in S_t}v_tx_{t,s}\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

Is computing the exact maximum revenue for this continuous problem polynomial-time solvable in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

The discrete winner-determination problem is NP-hard even with one resource, one nonpreemptive operation per bid, common duration, and slack at most \(2\). [Wang, Ghenniwa, and Shen (2009)](https://publications-cnrc.canada.ca/eng/view/accepted/?id=2b69f79c-187e-497a-aea1-0185ae8f6a5e).
No result specifically addressing the mass-allocation problem defined above was located in the search.

Result. Computing the exact maximum revenue is polynomial-time solvable in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\). The continuous allocation problem has an exact rational linear-programming formulation with at most \(3\tau\) variables.

1. Exact linear-programming formulation

Let \(J=\{(t,s):t\in T,\ s\in S_t\}\), and let \(n=|J|\). Because the release times, deadlines, and duration are integers,

\[ |S_t|=d_t-p-r_t+1\in\{1,2,3\}, \]

so \(n\le 3\tau\). Moreover, every allowed operation lies within the horizon: if \(s\in S_t\), then \(s\ge0\) and \(s+p\le d_t\le m\).

Introduce one real variable \(x_{t,s}\) for each \((t,s)\in J\). Consider the linear program with objective

\[ \max \sum_{(t,s)\in J}v_t x_{t,s}, \]

subject to:

  • For every type \(t\), \(\sum_{s\in S_t}x_{t,s}\le\mu_t\).
  • For every slot \(q\), \(\sum_{(t,s)\in J:\,s\le q<s+p}x_{t,s}\le\kappa_q\).
  • For every \((t,s)\in J\), \(x_{t,s}\ge0\).

These are precisely the allocation constraints in the question. In particular, a variable represents a mass receiving an entire contiguous operation at its specified start; dividing mass between variables does not divide any individual's operation. Thus feasible allocations and feasible LP solutions coincide, with identical revenue.

The LP is feasible because the zero vector satisfies every constraint. Every feasible solution satisfies \(0\le x_{t,s}\le\mu_t\le1\). Consequently, its feasible region is a nonempty compact polytope, and the continuous objective attains a finite maximum.

2. Encoding size and running time

There are \(n\le3\tau\) variables and \(m+\tau+n\) inequalities, including nonnegativity. In inequality form, every matrix coefficient belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). The right-hand sides are the given capacities, masses, and zeros; each price appears at most three times in the objective.

Therefore a dense binary encoding of this LP has length

\[ B=O\!\left(L+n(m+\tau+n)\right) =O\!\left(L+\tau(m+\tau)\right). \]

The LP can be constructed in polynomial time: enumerate each type's at most three starts, and determine each slot coefficient by testing \(s\le q<s+p\).

We apply the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly encoded LP with rational coefficients can be solved exactly in time polynomial in its binary encoding length, returning a rational optimum when the optimum is attained and finite. This is a bit-complexity guarantee, not merely an approximation guarantee; see [Trevisan's linear-programming lecture, Section 2.4](https://lucatrevisan.wordpress.com/2011/01/24/cs261-lecture-5-linear-programming/).

All hypotheses hold here: the coefficients are rational, the encoding is explicit, and feasibility and attainment were established above. Hence an exact optimal allocation and its revenue can be computed using

\[ \left(L+\tau(m+\tau)\right)^{O(1)} \]

bit operations, which is polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

3. Why exact output has polynomial length

For completeness, the required exact answer cannot have an exponentially long rational representation.

A linear objective on a nonempty compact polytope has an optimal vertex. At any vertex, the active constraint rows span the variable space: otherwise a nonzero vector orthogonal to every active row would permit sufficiently small perturbations in both directions, preserving all constraints and contradicting extremality. Thus an optimal vertex \(x^*\) satisfies \(Cx^*=b'\) for an invertible \(n\times n\) matrix \(C\) with entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).

Let \(D\) be the product of the positive denominators of all input masses and capacities. Its binary length is \(O(L)\), and \(Db'\) is integral. Cramer's rule gives

\[ x^*=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(C)\,Db'}{D\det C}. \]

Hadamard's inequality gives \(1\le|\det C|\le n^{n/2}\). Therefore all coordinates have a common positive denominator \(D|\det C|\) of bit length \(O(L+n\log(n+1))\). Since their values lie between zero and one, their numerators have the same bound.

Let \(E\) be the product of the positive denominators of the prices. The revenue consequently has a denominator dividing \(ED|\det C|\). Also, summing the type constraints gives total served mass at most one, so revenue is at most \(\max_t v_t\). These facts bound both numerator and denominator lengths by \(O(L+n\log(n+1))\).

4. Relation to discrete winner determination

Discrete winner determination imposes indivisible acceptance and scheduling choices. Those restrictions are absent from the stated mass-allocation problem, which explicitly permits arbitrary real masses and unserved mass. Its feasible set is exactly the polytope above, so discrete NP-hardness does not establish hardness of this continuous problem.

No integrality property of the LP is needed for the polynomial-time conclusion.
