The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a party partition \(\mathcal P\), distinguished party \(P^\star\), and a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete rankings of \(C\), decide whether some nomination \(\eta(P)\in P\) makes \(p^\star=\eta(P^\star)\) the unique Maximin winner, where \(w_\mu(a,b)=\sum_{\theta:a\succ_\theta b}\mu_\theta\) and \(\operatorname{MM}_{\mu,\eta}(a)=\min_{b\ne a}w_\mu(a,b)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be partitioned into parties \(\mathcal P\), each of size at most \(2\), with distinguished party \(P^\star\). The input gives an explicit rational distribution \(\mu\) over strict complete rankings \(t\) of \(C\). Put \(w_\mu(a,b)=\sum_{t:a\succ_t b}\mu_t\); \(a\) defeats \(b\) iff \(w_\mu(a,b)>1/2\). For a nomination vector \(x\) with \(x_P\in P\), the Copeland score of a nominee is its number of defeated fellow nominees; it is a unique Copeland winner if its score is strictly maximal.

Target. Is it NP-hard, or solvable in polynomial time, to decide whether some nomination vector makes \(x_{P^\star}\) the unique Copeland winner?

Nearest known results. For finite elections with exactly three voters, the corresponding Possible President problem for Copeland is NP-complete even when every party has size at most \(2\). No result for the distribution-over-rankings version is assumed here.

Theorem

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, and hence \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when the distribution has support size at most \(3\) and every probability has denominator \(3\).

1. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate specifies one nominee \(x_P\in P\) for every party \(P\in\mathcal P\). Because every party has size at most \(2\), the certificate has polynomial length.

Let the explicit support of the distribution be \(t_1,\ldots,t_s\), with rational probabilities

\[ \mu_{t_i}=\frac{p_i}{q_i}. \]

For every ordered pair of nominees \(a,b\), compute

\[ w_\mu(a,b)=\sum_{i:a\succ_{t_i}b}\frac{p_i}{q_i}. \]

This comparison can be performed exactly in polynomial time. Indeed, putting \(D=\prod_{i=1}^s q_i\), we have

\[ w_\mu(a,b)>\frac12 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2\sum_{i:a\succ_{t_i}b}p_i\frac{D}{q_i}>D. \]

Although \(D\) can be numerically large, its binary length is at most \(\sum_i\lceil\log_2 q_i\rceil\), which is bounded by the input length. Thus all required integer arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity.

After determining every pairwise defeat relation among the nominees, one computes each nominee’s Copeland score and checks whether

\[ \operatorname{score}(x_{P^\star}) > \operatorname{score}(x_P) \]

for every other nominated candidate \(x_P\). Consequently, the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

2. Reduction from the three-voter problem

Use the stated \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete Possible President problem for Copeland with exactly three voters and parties of size at most \(2\).

Consider such an instance, consisting of:

  • a candidate set \(C\);
  • a partition \(\mathcal P\) of \(C\), with \(|P|\leq 2\);
  • a distinguished party \(P^\star\);
  • three strict complete rankings \(t_1,t_2,t_3\).

Preserve \(C\), \(\mathcal P\), and \(P^\star\). Define a distribution \(\mu\) by taking the empirical distribution of the three voters:

\[ \mu_t=\frac{|\{i\in\{1,2,3\}:t_i=t\}|}{3} \]

for every ranking \(t\) that occurs among \(t_1,t_2,t_3\). Repeated rankings are therefore aggregated. This produces an explicit rational distribution with support size at most \(3\), and the construction is polynomial-time.

3. Preservation of pairwise defeats

Fix distinct candidates \(a,b\in C\). By construction,

\[ w_\mu(a,b) = \sum_{t:a\succ_t b}\mu_t = \frac{|\{i\in\{1,2,3\}:a\succ_{t_i}b\}|}{3}. \]

Therefore,

\[ w_\mu(a,b)>\frac12 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |\{i:a\succ_{t_i}b\}|>\frac32. \]

The quantity on the right is an integer, so this is equivalent to

\[ |\{i:a\succ_{t_i}b\}|\geq 2. \]

Thus \(a\) defeats \(b\) under \(\mu\) if and only if at least two of the three original voters prefer \(a\) to \(b\). This is precisely the strict pairwise-majority relation of the original election.

Because there are three voters, no pairwise majority tie is possible.

4. Preservation of nomination outcomes

Let \(x\) be any nomination vector. The preceding argument applies to every pair of candidates in the nominated set

\[ \{x_P:P\in\mathcal P\}. \]

Hence the pairwise-defeat digraph induced by the nominees is identical in the original three-voter election and in the constructed distributional instance. Since the Copeland score used here is the outdegree in this digraph, every nominee has exactly the same score in both instances.

In particular,

\[ x_{P^\star}\text{ is the unique Copeland winner under }\mu \]

if and only if it is the unique Copeland winner in the original three-voter election.

Thus the original instance admits a successful nomination vector if and only if the constructed distributional instance does.

5. Conclusion

The construction is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from the stated three-voter \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem. Therefore the distribution-over-rankings problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Together with membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

The hardness already holds for empirical distributions of three voters, so allowing arbitrary explicitly represented rational distributions does not make the problem easier.
