The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) divisible goods and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over finitely many complete trader types \(\theta=(w_\theta,u_\theta)\), with strongly connected economy graph and \(u_\theta(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{\theta,j}(x_j)\) having at most \(2\) segments, \(27\)-bounded slopes, and \(23\)-sparse support, find a normalized rational price vector \(p\) and per-type bundles \(x_\theta\in\mathbb{R}_+^n\) such that each \(x_\theta\) maximizes \(u_\theta\) subject to \(p\cdot x_\theta\le p\cdot w_\theta\), and \(\left|\sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_{\theta,j}-\sum_\theta\mu_\theta w_{\theta,j}\right|\le n^{-13}\sum_\theta\mu_\theta w_{\theta,j}\) for every good \(j\).

Partial result: equivalence and an algorithm for small support

For unrestricted support size, I establish $\mathrm{PPAD}$-completeness, but neither of the two requested alternatives. I also prove an additional algorithmic result: for an absolute constant $C$, an exact equilibrium can be computed in time
$$ C^\tau\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$
Consequently, the problem is polynomial-time solvable for fixed $\tau$, and even when $\tau=O(\log(m+L))$.

The arguments below are self-contained apart from the explicitly stated finite-market theorems and standard polynomial-time algorithms for rational linear programming.

1. External theorems and conventions

Write $\mathcal F$ for the finite-list version: traders are listed individually, aggregate quantities are unweighted sums, and the output consists of normalized rational prices admitting an allocation with exact individual optimality and relative clearing tolerance $m^{-13}$.

Chen, Dai, Du, and Teng’s Theorem 3 states that $\mathcal F$ is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-complete for strongly connected, $2$-linear, $27$-bounded, $23$-sparse markets. Their definitions impose the two sparsity bounds separately and require relative clearing for every good. Their Theorem 2 also supplies an exact equilibrium for strongly connected markets with these utilities; exact equilibrium clears positive-price goods, while permitting leftover zero-price supply. These statements are from [Sections 2.2–2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/0904.0644v1).

The hypotheses match the task: every nonzero component here has a positive final slope, so “nonzero component” and the source’s “strictly monotone component” define the same graph edges.

Throughout, a polynomial-time search reduction means polynomial-time maps producing valid target instances and decoding every valid target solution correctly. Polynomial-length solutions are used, as in the supplied search theorem.

2. Exact equivalence between the two encodings

2.1. Absorbing masses into trader data

Given a support instance, create one finite-list trader for each type $\theta$, with
$$ \widehat w_\theta=\mu_\theta w_\theta, \qquad \widehat r_{\theta,j}(y)=\mu_\theta r_{\theta,j}(y/\mu_\theta). $$

For a two-segment component with slopes $a>c$ and breakpoint $b>0$,
$$ r(t)=a\min\{t,b\}+c\max\{t-b,0\}. $$
Therefore
$$ \mu_\theta r(y/\mu_\theta) = a\min\{y,\mu_\theta b\} +c\max\{y-\mu_\theta b,0\}. $$
The slopes remain unchanged, and the breakpoint becomes $\mu_\theta b>0$. Linear and zero components remain linear and zero, respectively.

Because each mass is positive, endowment supports and nonzero utility supports remain unchanged. Thus both sparsity bounds and every economy-graph edge are preserved. The construction has exactly $\tau$ traders.

Products of binary rationals have encoding lengths bounded by the sums of the operand lengths, up to constants. There are polynomially many such products, so this is a polynomial-time construction with polynomial encoding size.

Fix any price vector $p$. The bijection
$$ y_\theta=\mu_\theta x_\theta $$
preserves affordability:
$$ p\cdot y_\theta\le p\cdot\widehat w_\theta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\cdot x_\theta\le p\cdot w_\theta. $$
Furthermore,
$$ \widehat u_\theta(y_\theta)=\mu_\theta u_\theta(x_\theta). $$
Multiplication by a positive constant preserves all utility comparisons. Hence this bijection preserves the entire set of maximizing bundles.

For every good $j$,
$$ \sum_\theta y_{\theta,j}-\sum_\theta\widehat w_{\theta,j} = \sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_{\theta,j} -\sum_\theta\mu_\theta w_{\theta,j}. $$
The transformed supply equals the original weighted supply. Consequently, the clearing inequalities are identical, including when supply or price is zero. Prices and their normalization are unchanged.

Thus the support problem reduces to $\mathcal F$, once allocations can be reconstructed from prices. Section 3 proves that reconstruction.

2.2. Grouping a finite list into distinct types

Conversely, let a finite-list instance contain $N$ traders. Group traders with identical complete types into classes $C_\theta$, and put
$$ k_\theta=|C_\theta|, \qquad \mu_\theta=\frac{k_\theta}{N}. $$
Keep each class’s common endowment and utility. The resulting complete types are distinct, and their masses are positive and sum to one.

Equality of types can be checked in polynomial time by comparing reduced rational endowments and canonical component representations. The number of classes is at most $N$, and the mass encodings have polynomial length.

Strong connectivity survives grouping. Indeed, project an original directed path onto its sequence of classes and remove consecutive repetitions. Every remaining transition between distinct classes is a support-graph edge, because all members of a class have identical endowments and utilities. This gives a directed walk between any two distinct classes.

Given support bundles $x_\theta$, assign $x_\theta$ to every original trader in $C_\theta$. Each assigned bundle is optimal for its recipient. Moreover,
$$ \sum_{i=1}^N x_{i,j}-\sum_{i=1}^N w_{i,j} = N\left( \sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_{\theta,j} -\sum_\theta\mu_\theta w_{\theta,j} \right). $$
Supply scales by the same factor $N$, so relative clearing is preserved.

Grouping also preserves existence. If identical traders originally receive different optimal bundles, their average is affordable by convexity of their common budget set. If their common optimal value is $V$, concavity gives
$$ u_\theta\left(\frac{1}{k_\theta}\sum_{i\in C_\theta}x_i\right) \ge \frac{1}{k_\theta}\sum_{i\in C_\theta}u_\theta(x_i) =V. $$
Affordability bounds the left-hand side above by $V$. Thus the average is optimal and preserves aggregate consumption.

This proves the reverse search reduction.

3. Rational optimal bundles can be reconstructed

Fix rational prices $p$ admitting the required allocation.

For a two-segment component, introduce segment quantities $z^1,z^2$ satisfying
$$ 0\le z^1\le b, \qquad z^2\ge0. $$
Consumption is $z^1+z^2$, and represented utility is $az^1+cz^2$. For fixed consumption $t$, its largest represented utility is attained at
$$ z^1=\min\{t,b\}, \qquad z^2=\max\{t-b,0\}, $$
and equals $r(t)$. This follows because transferring quantity from the second segment to an available first segment increases utility by the positive slope difference $a-c$.

Use one nonnegative variable for each linear or zero component. Each type’s individual optimization is therefore a rational linear program: maximize represented utility subject to segment bounds and the budget constraint.

Let its optimum be $V_\theta$. The assumed witnessing allocation ensures that the optimum is finite and attained. Standard rational linear programming computes $V_\theta$ exactly in polynomial time.

Next solve a joint rational feasibility program imposing:

  • every type’s budget and segment bounds;
  • represented utility equal to $V_\theta$ for every type;
  • both weighted clearing inequalities for every good.

The witnessing allocation, with canonical segment splits, proves feasibility. A rational feasible solution of polynomial encoding length can therefore be computed in polynomial time.

For any reconstructed bundle, true utility is at least its represented utility, namely $V_\theta$. Affordability bounds true utility above by $V_\theta$. Thus the reconstructed bundle is exactly optimal.

This includes zero-utility components and zero-supply goods. Since masses and quantities are nonnegative and masses are positive, a zero-supply clearing constraint forces every type’s consumption of that good to be zero.

Together with Section 2, this proves that the support problem is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-complete.

4. Additional result: an exact algorithm exponential only in support size

The following construction gives further progress beyond the encoding equivalence.

Proposition. There is an absolute constant $C$ such that an exact equilibrium, including rational bundles, can be computed in time
$$ C^\tau\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

4.1. The singleton case

Suppose $\tau=1$, so its mass is one. Set $x=w$.

For each nonzero component, choose its right-hand slope $d_j$ at $w_j$. For a two-segment component this means
$$ d_j= \begin{cases} a,&w_j<b,\\ c,&w_j\ge b. \end{cases} $$
For a linear component use its slope, and for a zero component put $d_j=0$.

For every $t\ge0$,
$$ r_j(t)\le r_j(w_j)+d_j(t-w_j). $$
To verify this, when $t>w_j$, every traversed slope is at most $d_j$; when $t<w_j$, every traversed slope is at least $d_j$. Summing the corresponding segment increments gives the inequality.

If some component is nonzero, take $p=d$ and normalize it. Every affordable bundle then satisfies
$$ u(x)\le u(w)+d\cdot(x-w)\le u(w). $$
Thus $w$ is optimal and clears exactly. If utility is identically zero, use $p_j=1$ for every good; again $x=w$ suffices.

Hence the singleton case is polynomial-time solvable.

4.2. A useful price form for larger supports

Assume $\tau\ge2$, and define
$$ D=\{j:r_{\theta,j}\not\equiv0\text{ for some }\theta\}, \qquad S_j=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta w_{\theta,j}. $$
The utility sparsity bound gives
$$ |D|\le23\tau. $$

Strong connectivity ensures that every type has an incoming edge and an outgoing edge. Consequently, every type has nonzero utility and owns a positive quantity of some good in $D$.

Any prices admitting optimal bundles must satisfy $p_j>0$ for every $j\in D$. Otherwise, some type could obtain arbitrarily large utility from a free good with a positive final slope.

At such prices every type exhausts its budget: any positive slack can purchase additional quantity of a valued good and strictly increase utility. Also, no type consumes a positive-price good that it does not value. Removing such consumption and spending the released money on a valued good would strictly improve utility.

Apply exact-equilibrium existence to the mass-scaled finite market from Section 2 and decode its allocation. For a good outside $D$ with positive price, the preceding observation makes aggregate consumption zero. Exact clearing of positive-price goods therefore forces its supply to be zero. Its price can then be changed to zero without changing incomes or utilities.

For every good outside $D$, give each type its original endowment of that good. These goods are now free and have zero utility for everyone, so optimality is unchanged and they clear exactly.

Thus an exact equilibrium exists with
$$ p_j>0\quad(j\in D), \qquad p_j=0\quad(j\notin D), $$
and exact clearing of every good. Rescale so that $p_j\ge1$ on $D$.

4.3. Segment expenditure variables

For type $\theta$, let $A_\theta$ be its set of positive-slope utility segments. A linear component contributes one unbounded segment; a two-segment component contributes one bounded segment and one unbounded segment. Therefore
$$ 1\le |A_\theta|\le46. $$
For a segment $s\in A_\theta$, write $j(s)$ for its good and $a_{\theta,s}>0$ for its slope. A bounded segment also has a quantity capacity $b_{\theta,s}>0$.

Let $q_{\theta,s}$ be money spent on segment $s$. At positive prices, its quantity and utility contribution are
$$ \frac{q_{\theta,s}}{p_{j(s)}}, \qquad \frac{a_{\theta,s}}{p_{j(s)}}q_{\theta,s}. $$
Define the utility per unit of expenditure by
$$ v_{\theta,s}=\frac{a_{\theta,s}}{p_{j(s)}}. $$

The segment bounds and budget equality become
$$ q_{\theta,s}\ge0, \qquad q_{\theta,s}\le b_{\theta,s}p_{j(s)} \quad\text{for bounded segments}, $$
$$ \sum_{s\in A_\theta}q_{\theta,s} = B_\theta, \qquad B_\theta=\sum_{j\in D}p_jw_{\theta,j}. $$
Because each type owns a positive quantity of a good in $D$, $B_\theta>0$.

4.4. Every optimum has a segment threshold

Fix prices and an optimal segment allocation. It spends the entire positive budget. Choose a segment $t_\theta$ of minimum $v_{\theta,s}$ among segments receiving positive expenditure, and put
$$ \lambda_\theta=v_{\theta,t_\theta}. $$

Every segment with $v_{\theta,s}>\lambda_\theta$ must be bounded and fully purchased. Otherwise, transferring a sufficiently small positive expenditure from $t_\theta$ to that segment would be feasible and strictly increase utility. In particular, an unbounded segment cannot have ratio above $\lambda_\theta$.

Every segment with $v_{\theta,s}<\lambda_\theta$ receives zero expenditure, by the choice of $t_\theta$.

Segments with ratio equal to $\lambda_\theta$ may receive any expenditure within their bounds. This description includes all price ties and breakpoint optima.

4.5. Encoding a threshold choice by a linear program

For each type choose a reference segment $t_\theta$ and write $k_\theta=j(t_\theta)$. Assign every segment one of three statuses:

  • $H$: bounded and fully purchased;
  • $L$: receives zero expenditure;
  • $E$: has the reference utility-per-expenditure ratio.

Force the reference segment to have status $E$. Unbounded segments may have only status $L$ or $E$.

Define the linear expression
$$ R_{\theta,s} = a_{\theta,s}p_{k_\theta} -a_{\theta,t_\theta}p_{j(s)}. $$
Since the prices involved are positive, its sign equals the sign of $v_{\theta,s}-\lambda_\theta$.

For a chosen collection of references and statuses, impose
$$ p_j\ge1\qquad(j\in D), $$
the segment bounds and budget equalities from Section 4.3, and the following status constraints:
$$ H:\quad R_{\theta,s}\ge0,\qquad q_{\theta,s}=b_{\theta,s}p_{j(s)}, $$
$$ L:\quad R_{\theta,s}\le0,\qquad q_{\theta,s}=0, $$
$$ E:\quad R_{\theta,s}=0. $$
Finally impose exact clearing in expenditure units:
$$ \sum_\theta\mu_\theta \sum_{\substack{s\in A_\theta\\j(s)=j}}q_{\theta,s} = S_jp_j \qquad(j\in D). $$

All these constraints are linear with rational coefficients. Weak inequalities suffice; no strict-inequality feasibility procedure is needed.

4.6. Every feasible program produces optimal bundles

Consider a feasible solution of one such program. For a fixed type, abbreviate its expenditure vector by $q$, its budget by $B$, and its threshold by $\lambda>0$.

Let $e$ be any competing feasible segment expenditure vector. For an $H$ segment,
$$ (v_s-\lambda)(e_s-q_s)\le0, $$
because $v_s-\lambda\ge0$ and $q_s$ equals the capacity. For an $L$ segment the same inequality holds because $v_s-\lambda\le0$, $q_s=0$, and $e_s\ge0$. For an $E$ segment the expression is zero.

Using $\sum_s q_s=B$ and $\sum_s e_s\le B$ gives
$$ \sum_s v_s(e_s-q_s) = \lambda\left(\sum_s e_s-B\right) +\sum_s(v_s-\lambda)(e_s-q_s) \le0. $$
Thus $q$ maximizes represented segment utility.

Recover quantities by
$$ x_{\theta,j} = \frac{1}{p_j} \sum_{\substack{s\in A_\theta\\j(s)=j}}q_{\theta,s} \qquad(j\in D), $$
and set $x_{\theta,j}=w_{\theta,j}$ outside $D$.

These bundles are affordable. Any affordable competing bundle has a canonical segment representation whose utility is bounded above by the represented utility of $q$. Conversely, the true utility of the recovered bundle is at least its represented utility. Applying the preceding upper bound to the recovered bundle itself proves equality and exact individual optimality.

For $j\in D$, the clearing equation yields
$$ \sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_{\theta,j} = \frac{1}{p_j} \sum_\theta\mu_\theta \sum_{\substack{s\in A_\theta\\j(s)=j}}q_{\theta,s} =S_j. $$
Outside $D$, clearing follows from $x_{\theta,j}=w_{\theta,j}$. Zero-supply goods in $D$ cause no exception: their clearing equations force all corresponding expenditures to zero.

Finally, set prices outside $D$ to zero and divide all positive prices by their minimum. This preserves every budget set and gives the required normalization.

4.7. Completeness and running time

Take the exact equilibrium established in Section 4.2. Its canonical segment allocation is individually optimal. Section 4.4 supplies reference segments and statuses satisfying one of the enumerated programs. Hence at least one program is feasible.

Each type has at most $46$ reference choices and at most $3^{46}$ status assignments. Therefore at most
$$ (46\cdot3^{46})^\tau $$
programs need to be solved.

Each program has polynomial encoding size: its coefficients use only input rationals, their products, and polynomially many sums. Rational linear-program feasibility is polynomial-time decidable and, when feasible, yields a rational solution of polynomial encoding length. Division by positive rational prices preserves polynomial encoding length.

This proves the proposition with
$$ C=46\cdot3^{46}. $$
The dependence on mass denominators is through $L$, without expanding masses into trader copies.

If $\tau\le K\log(m+L)$ for a fixed constant $K$, then $C^\tau$ is polynomial in $m+L$. This proves the stated logarithmic-support case.

5. Consequences for the requested alternatives

The equivalence establishes
$$ \text{a polynomial-time algorithm for the unrestricted problem exists} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathrm{PPAD}\subseteq\mathrm{FP}. $$
The algorithm in Section 4 does not settle this question because its running time is exponential in unrestricted $\tau$.

There is also a precise obstruction to proving $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness under the search-reduction convention stated in Section 1.

Suppose satisfying-assignment search reduced to this market problem through maps $f,g$. Thus $f(F)$ is a valid market instance for every Boolean formula $F$, and whenever $F$ is satisfiable, every valid market solution $y$ must make $g(F,y)$ a satisfying assignment.

An unsatisfiability certificate would consist of a polynomial-length valid solution $y$ of $f(F)$ for which $g(F,y)$ fails to satisfy $F$. Verification is polynomial-time: rational arithmetic checks prices, budgets and clearing, while the individual linear programs in Section 3 check exact optimality.

Such a certificate cannot exist for a satisfiable formula, by reduction correctness. For an unsatisfiable formula, a short valid market solution exists; its decoded output necessarily fails to satisfy the formula. Thus unsatisfiability would belong to $\mathrm{NP}$, implying
$$ \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}. $$

This is a conditional implication, not a proof that $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness is impossible. The requirement that reductions produce valid target instances even on unsatisfiable formulas is essential.

6. Remaining open question

The unrestricted polynomial-time versus $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness question remains unsettled by these arguments. The proved results are the exact encoding equivalence, rational allocation reconstruction, and an exact algorithm exponential only in support size.
