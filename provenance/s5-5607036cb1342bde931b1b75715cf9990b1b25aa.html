The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(d\), \(R\), an \(R\)-fat estate \(C_0\), a finite set \(T\) of complete valuation types with masses \(\mu_t\), and a bounded integrable density \(v_t\) for each type, let \(N=k^d\), choose counts \(n_t(k)\) with \(\sum_t n_t(k)=N\) and \(n_t(k)/N\to\mu_t\), and set \(C_k=kC_0\) with density \(v_{t,k}(x)=v_t(x/k)\). Partial resolution: equality cases and an arithmetic reduction

I do not settle the unrestricted $\mathrm P$-versus-$\mathrm{NP}$-hardness question. I prove three additional results:

  • If a zero-valued type occurs with positive mass, the threshold equality cases are polynomial-time decidable for both $\beta<1$ and $\beta>1$.
  • For $\beta>1$, geometry can be eliminated uniformly, including at equality: eventual feasibility is equivalent to an exact additive feasibility problem with integer type counts.
  • For $\beta>1$, the problem is polynomial-time decidable when the positive-valued types have pairwise disjoint supports.

I also prove an arithmetic criterion for using one fixed additive allocation template. An explicit example shows why that criterion alone does not decide the unrestricted problem: bundles may have to vary with $k$.

1. Interpretation, notation, and elementary cases

I use the intended estate-allocation interpretation that every allotment is a subset of $C_k$.

Let the input simplices be $\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_m$, and define

$$ w_s=\operatorname{vol}(\Delta_s)>0,\qquad a_{t,s}=v_t|_{\operatorname{int}(\Delta_s)},\qquad W_t=\sum_s a_{t,s}w_s. $$

Thus $W_t=V_t(C_0)$. Simplex boundaries are Lebesgue-null and do not affect any integral.

Write

$$ I=\{t:W_t>0\},\qquad Z=T\setminus I. $$

Because densities are nonnegative, a type in $Z$ has zero density almost everywhere. Its utility from every allotment is zero, so it may receive an empty allotment.

If $\alpha=0$, or if $I$ is empty, empty allotments prove eventual feasibility. Counts converging to the masses can be obtained by rounding. All thresholds below are defined only when $I$ is nonempty.

For a normalized set $Y\subseteq C_0$, change of variables gives

$$ \int_{kY}v_t(x/k)\,dx = N\int_Yv_t(z)\,dz,\qquad N=k^d. $$

Scaling preserves fatness.

The exact classifications proved below for inputs with $Z\neq\varnothing$ are:

| Exponent | Eventual feasibility |
|---|---|
| $0\leq\beta<1$ | Exactly when $\alpha\leq\gamma_F$ |
| $\beta>1$ | Exactly when $\alpha\leq\gamma_A$ |

The thresholds $\gamma_F,\gamma_A$ are defined and computed below. Both classifications have bit running time $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,d,L)$.

2. An exact additive necessary condition

For a positive population vector $p=(p_t)_{t\in T}$ with $\sum_t p_t=1$, define $\mathcal A_p(\alpha)$ by variables $b_{t,s}\geq0$ and constraints

$$ \sum_t p_t b_{t,s}\leq w_s \qquad(s=1,\ldots,m), $$

$$ \sum_s a_{t,s}b_{t,s} \geq \sum_s a_{t,s}b_{r,s} \qquad(t,r\in T), $$

$$ \sum_s a_{t,s}b_{t,s}\geq\alpha W_t \qquad(t\in T). $$

Here $b_{t,s}$ represents physical volume in density region $s$ per type-$t$ agent.

Lemma 1: finite feasibility implies exact additive feasibility

Suppose a feasible allocation at $k$ has $n_t>0$ agents of every type. Set $p_t=n_t/N$. Then $\mathcal A_p(\alpha)$ is feasible.

Proof. Envy-freeness between agents of the same type forces all their own utilities to be equal. Denote the common utility of type $t$ by $u_t$.

Utility is bounded above by additive value. Therefore null overlap gives

$$ n_tu_t \leq \sum_{i:t_i=t}\int_{X_i}v_t(x/k)\,dx \leq NW_t. $$

For each positive integer $j$, choose, inside every positive-utility allotment $X_i$, a usable plot $Y_i^{(j)}$ whose value to its owner is at least $u_{t_i}-1/j$. For a zero-utility allotment, choose the empty set. This does not require attainment of the utility supremum.

Define

$$ b_{t,s}^{(j)} = \frac{N}{n_t} \sum_{i:t_i=t} \operatorname{vol}\bigl(k^{-1}Y_i^{(j)}\cap\Delta_s\bigr). $$

The selected plots have null overlap, so

$$ \sum_t p_t b_{t,s}^{(j)}\leq w_s. $$

Their average own value satisfies

$$ u_t-\frac1j \leq \sum_s a_{t,s}b_{t,s}^{(j)} \leq u_t. $$

For an observer of type $t$, every nonempty selected plot belonging to an agent of type $r$ is admissible: the fatness restriction is common to all types. Hence envy-freeness implies

$$ \sum_s a_{t,s}b_{r,s}^{(j)}\leq u_t. $$

Finally,

$$ 0\leq b_{t,s}^{(j)}\leq \frac{w_s}{p_t}. $$

The vectors therefore have a convergent subsequence. Its limit satisfies all constraints of $\mathcal A_p(\alpha)$, because $u_t\geq\alpha W_t$. $\square$

The limiting additive threshold

Define $\gamma_A$ as the maximum $\gamma\geq0$ for which $\mathcal A_\mu(\gamma)$ is feasible.

This maximum exists and is finite. Indeed,

$$ 0\leq b_{t,s}\leq \frac{w_s}{\mu_t}, $$

and, for every $t\in I$,

$$ \gamma W_t \leq \sum_s a_{t,s}b_{t,s} \leq \frac{W_t}{\mu_t}. $$

The feasible region is closed. Also, setting $b_{t,s}=w_s$ for every type proves feasibility at $\gamma=1$.

If an input is eventually feasible, Lemma 1 supplies certificates for populations $p_t(k)=n_t(k)/N$. These certificates are uniformly bounded because $p_t(k)\to\mu_t>0$. A convergent subsequence yields a certificate for $\mathcal A_\mu(\alpha)$. Consequently,

$$ \text{eventual feasibility}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha\leq\gamma_A $$

for every $\beta$.

The program defining $\gamma_A$ has $m\tau+1$ variables and $O(m\tau+\tau^2+m)$ constraints. Its exact solution has bit running time $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,d,L)$.

3. Uniform elimination of geometry when $\beta>1$

The following strengthens strict-slack constructions: no capacity slack is required.

Lemma 2: uniform realization of additive certificates

Assume $\alpha>0$, $I\neq\varnothing$, and $\beta>1$. There exists $K$, depending only on the input, such that the following holds for every $k\geq K$.

For any positive integer counts $n_t$ summing to $N=k^d$, if $\mathcal A_{n/N}(\alpha)$ is feasible, then those counts admit a feasible geometric allocation.

Proof. Choose an axis-parallel cube $B^+$ of side $D$ containing $C_0$, and put

$$ \ell_k=\frac{D}{Rk^\beta}. $$

Let

$$ M=\max_{t,s}a_{t,s}>0,\qquad c=\frac{\alpha\min_{t\in I}W_t}{mM}>0. $$

Take any additive certificate $b$. Replace the vectors of zero-valued types by zero. This preserves capacities, thresholds, and envy inequalities.

For each $t\in I$,

$$ \sum_s a_{t,s}b_{t,s}\geq\alpha W_t. $$

Thus some index $s(t)$ satisfies

$$ a_{t,s(t)}b_{t,s(t)}\geq\frac{\alpha W_t}{m}, \qquad b_{t,s(t)}\geq c. $$

Each simplex contains a fixed axis-parallel interior cube. The number of grid cubes of side $\ell_k$ that fit in such an interior cube, divided by $N$, tends to infinity, because

$$ N\ell_k^d = \frac{D^d}{R^d}k^{d(1-\beta)} \longrightarrow0. $$

Therefore, for all sufficiently large $k$, every simplex can accommodate $N$ cores of side $\ell_k$ with disjoint interiors. Also,

$$ \ell_k^d\leq\frac{c}{N} $$

eventually.

Give each positive-valued agent of type $t$ a distinct core in simplex $s(t)$. Its core volume does not exceed its prescribed volume $b_{t,s(t)}/N$.

In simplex $s$, the total prescribed normalized volume is

$$ \sum_t n_t\frac{b_{t,s}}{N} = \sum_t p_t b_{t,s} \leq w_s. $$

After removing the cores, all remaining individual prescriptions are nonnegative, and their sum does not exceed the remaining volume. They can therefore be realized by Borel portions.

For completeness, this partitioning fact follows from coordinate cuts. For a bounded Borel set $E$, the function

$$ c'\longmapsto\operatorname{vol}\bigl(E\cap\{x_1\leq c'\}\bigr) $$

is continuous, since each hyperplane has zero Lebesgue measure. It ranges from zero to $\operatorname{vol}(E)$, so repeated cuts realize any finite list of nonnegative prescribed volumes with admissible total.

Let $Y_i$ be the union of an agent’s portions and core. Then

$$ B_i^-\subseteq Y_i\subseteq B^+, \qquad \frac{\operatorname{len}(B^+)} {\operatorname{len}(B_i^-)} = Rk^\beta. $$

Thus $Y_i$ is usable. Set $X_i=kY_i$.

Every whole allotment is usable, so nonnegativity gives, for every observer $r$,

$$ U_{r,k}(X_i) = \int_{X_i}v_r(x/k)\,dx = \sum_s a_{r,s}b_{t_i,s}. $$

The additive envy inequalities and thresholds give the desired geometric inequalities. Zero-valued agents receive empty allotments.

The required lower bound on $k$ used only $c$, the fixed interior cubes, and the input parameters. It is independent of the certificate and counts. $\square$

Exact arithmetic reformulation

Combining Lemmas 1 and 2 proves:

For $\beta>1$, eventual feasibility is equivalent to the existence of integer count vectors $n(k)$, with $n(k)/k^d\to\mu$, such that $\mathcal A_{n(k)/k^d}(\alpha)$ is feasible for every sufficiently large $k$.

This equivalence holds at $\alpha=\gamma_A$. It eliminates geometry, but it does not yet give a polynomial-time test for the quantified count condition.

Strict threshold and zero-valued types

If $\alpha<\gamma_A$, choose a certificate with guarantee $\gamma>\alpha$, and scale it by $\theta<1$ sufficiently close to one that $\theta\gamma>\alpha$. The scaled certificate has strict capacities,

$$ \sum_t\mu_t b_{t,s}<w_s. $$

Ordinary rounded populations converging to $\mu$ satisfy these capacities eventually. Lemma 2 proves eventual feasibility.

If $Z\neq\varnothing$, equality can also be handled. For every positive-valued type choose

$$ n_t(k)=\lfloor N\mu_t\rfloor. $$

For zero-valued types, round their counts and assign the remaining count to one designated zero-valued type. All proportions converge to their prescribed masses.

Take a certificate for $\mathcal A_\mu(\alpha)$, with zero vectors for zero-valued types. Then

$$ \sum_t\frac{n_t(k)}N b_{t,s} = \sum_{t\in I}\frac{\lfloor N\mu_t\rfloor}{N}b_{t,s} \leq \sum_{t\in I}\mu_t b_{t,s} \leq w_s. $$

Its envy inequalities and thresholds are unchanged. Lemma 2 applies.

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ Z\neq\varnothing,\ \beta>1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{eventual feasibility}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \alpha\leq\gamma_A. } $$

4. The Fisher threshold and its equality case with zero-valued types

Construct a linear Fisher market with buyers $t\in I$, budgets $\mu_t$, supplies $w_s$, and utility densities $a_{t,s}$. Remove goods valued at zero by every buyer. Importantly, the retained budgets are not renormalized.

Let $x^*_{t,s}$ be equilibrium allocated volumes and $\pi_s^*>0$ equilibrium prices per unit volume. Define

$$ u_t^*=\frac{\sum_s a_{t,s}x^*_{t,s}}{\mu_t}. $$

The equilibrium conditions are

$$ \sum_t x^*_{t,s}=w_s,\qquad \sum_s\pi_s^*x^*_{t,s}=\mu_t, $$

$$ \frac{a_{t,s}}{\pi_s^*}\leq u_t^*, \qquad x^*_{t,s}>0 \Longrightarrow \frac{a_{t,s}}{\pi_s^*}=u_t^*. $$

Every retained buyer values a good, and every retained good is valued by a buyer. Thus the positive-incidence hypotheses for exact linear Fisher equilibrium computation hold. Converting goods to unit supplies replaces utilities by $a_{t,s}w_s$; clearing rational denominators produces integral data of polynomial binary length. Orlin’s algorithm then computes an exact equilibrium in $O((m+\tau)^4\log(m+\tau))$ arithmetic operations and polynomial bit time. [Orlin, *Improved Algorithms for Computing Fisher’s Market Clearing Prices*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/16dagstuhl-seminar-proceedings/dsp-vol10171/DagSemProc.10171.2/DagSemProc.10171.2.pdf).

Define

$$ \gamma_F=\min_{t\in I}\frac{u_t^*}{W_t}. $$

The arguments below also establish that this definition is independent of the chosen equilibrium.

4.1. Localization below exponent $1$

Assume $\alpha>0$, $\beta<1$, and eventual feasibility.

Let $u_t(k)$ be the common own utility of type $t$. As in Lemma 1,

$$ \alpha W_t\leq u_t(k)\leq\frac{W_t}{n_t(k)/N}, $$

so these utilities are bounded.

Choose usable plots approaching their owners’ utilities with errors $\varepsilon_k\to0$, and normalize them into $C_0$. For large $k$, every positive-type selected plot has physical value at least

$$ c_0=\frac{\alpha}{2}\min_{t\in I}W_t>0. $$

Fix $\delta>0$. If a selected normalized plot has diameter at least $\delta$, its outer cube has side at least $\delta/\sqrt d$, and its inner cube has side at least $\delta/(\sqrt d\,Rk^\beta)$. Its volume is therefore at least

$$ \left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt d\,Rk^\beta}\right)^d. $$

Null overlap bounds the number of such plots by a constant times $k^{\beta d}\delta^{-d}$. Their fraction among $N=k^d$ agents tends to zero.

Let $H$ be the union of simplex boundaries, and $H_\delta$ its $\delta$-neighborhood. A plot of diameter less than $\delta$ that is not contained in any one simplex lies entirely in $H_\delta$. Indeed, a point farther than $\delta$ from every boundary would force every point of the plot into that point’s simplex.

Every selected positive-type plot has normalized volume at least $c_0/(NM)$. Hence the fraction of small, nonlocalized plots is at most

$$ \frac{M}{c_0}\operatorname{vol}(C_0\cap H_\delta). $$

The boundary union is null, so this quantity tends to zero as $\delta\to0$. First taking the large-$k$ limit for fixed $\delta$ proves that all but $o(N)$ positive-type selected plots lie in individual simplices.

Pass to a subsequence on which $u_t(k)\to u_t$ and the numbers of localized type-$t$ plots in simplex $s$, divided by $N$, converge to $\lambda_{t,s}$. Then

$$ u_t\geq\alpha W_t,\qquad \sum_s\lambda_{t,s}=\mu_t, $$

and $\lambda_{t,s}=0$ if $a_{t,s}=0$.

A localized plot has normalized volume between

$$ \frac{u_t(k)-\varepsilon_k}{Na_{t,s}} \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{u_t(k)}{Na_{t,s}}. $$

Summing the lower bounds and passing to the limit gives

$$ \sum_{t:a_{t,s}>0} \lambda_{t,s}\frac{u_t}{a_{t,s}}\leq w_s. $$

An observer of type $r$ values such a plot at least

$$ \frac{a_{r,s}}{a_{t,s}} \bigl(u_t(k)-\varepsilon_k\bigr). $$

Envy-freeness bounds this by $u_r(k)$. Consequently,

$$ \lambda_{t,s}>0 \Longrightarrow \frac{a_{r,s}u_t}{a_{t,s}}\leq u_r \qquad(r\in I). $$

These conclusions use neither connectedness nor a fixed cube orientation.

4.2. Equilibrium utility dominance

Suppose $u,\lambda$ satisfy the preceding conditions. Put

$$ \widehat\pi_s=\max_{t\in I}\frac{a_{t,s}}{u_t}, \qquad z_{t,s}= \lambda_{t,s}\frac{u_t}{a_{t,s}} $$

on positive pairs, and $z_{t,s}=0$ otherwise.

Whenever $z_{t,s}>0$, the cross-type inequalities imply

$$ \widehat\pi_s=\frac{a_{t,s}}{u_t}. $$

Thus

$$ \sum_s\widehat\pi_s z_{t,s}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_t z_{t,s}\leq w_s. $$

I claim that $u_t\leq u_t^*$ for every buyer. Suppose otherwise, and define

$$ \rho=\max_t\frac{u_t}{u_t^*}>1, \qquad H=\left\{t:\frac{u_t}{u_t^*}=\rho\right\}. $$

Let $S$ be the goods on which buyers in $H$ spend in the candidate allocation $z$.

For $s\in S$, choose a candidate recipient $r\in H$. Then

$$ \pi_s^*\geq\frac{a_{r,s}}{u_r^*} = \rho\widehat\pi_s. $$

Every equilibrium recipient of $s$ must belong to $H$. Otherwise, for a recipient $t\notin H$,

$$ \frac{a_{t,s}}{u_t} = \frac{\pi_s^*}{u_t/u_t^*} > \frac{\pi_s^*}{\rho} \geq\widehat\pi_s, $$

contradicting the definition of $\widehat\pi_s$.

Set $\mu_H=\sum_{t\in H}\mu_t>0$. All candidate spending by $H$ is on $S$, and all equilibrium revenue from $S$ is paid by $H$. Therefore

$$ \mu_H = \sum_{s\in S}\widehat\pi_s\sum_{t\in H}z_{t,s} \leq \sum_{s\in S}\widehat\pi_sw_s \leq \frac1\rho\sum_{s\in S}\pi_s^*w_s \leq \frac{\mu_H}{\rho}, $$

a contradiction.

Applying this comparison in both directions between two equilibria proves uniqueness of their $u^*$ vectors. It also proves

$$ \boxed{ \beta<1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{eventual feasibility requires }\alpha\leq\gamma_F. } $$

4.3. Strict sufficiency for every exponent

Define equilibrium expenditure masses

$$ \lambda^*_{t,s}=\pi_s^*x^*_{t,s}. $$

Their row sums are $\mu_t$, and their column sums are $\pi_s^*w_s$.

Suppose $\alpha<\gamma_F$. Choose $\theta<1$ such that

$$ \theta u_t^*>\alpha W_t \qquad(t\in I). $$

In simplex $s$, use grid cubes of normalized volume

$$ h_s(k)^d=\frac{\theta}{N\pi_s^*}. $$

If $P_s(k)$ is the number of grid cubes contained in the simplex, then

$$ P_s(k)h_s(k)^d\longrightarrow w_s. $$

Indeed, apart from grid faces, every point farther than $\sqrt d\,h_s(k)$ from the simplex boundary belongs to a contained grid cube. The omitted boundary neighborhood has volume tending to zero.

Consequently,

$$ \frac{P_s(k)}N\longrightarrow\frac{\pi_s^*w_s}{\theta} > \pi_s^*w_s. $$

Round the category masses $\lambda^*_{t,s}$, together with masses $\mu_z$ for zero-valued types, to integer counts summing to $N$. Each category has bounded rounding error, and no zero-mass category needs an agent. The strict packing margin supplies enough cubes eventually.

A type-$t$ agent assigned to simplex $s$ has physical own value

$$ Na_{t,s}h_s(k)^d = \theta\frac{a_{t,s}}{\pi_s^*} = \theta u_t^*. $$

Every observer $r$ values that cube at most $\theta u_r^*$. Whole cubes are usable for every $Rk^\beta\geq1$, so these comparisons are comparisons of actual utilities.

Thus $\alpha<\gamma_F$ is sufficient for every $\beta\geq0$.

4.4. Equality with a zero-valued type

Now suppose $Z\neq\varnothing$ and $\alpha\leq\gamma_F$. Use cubes with

$$ h_s(k)^d=\frac{1}{N\pi_s^*}. $$

Their grid capacities satisfy

$$ \frac{P_s(k)}N\longrightarrow\pi_s^*w_s. $$

Define

$$ D_k= \max_s \max\left\{ 0,\, 1-\frac{P_s(k)}{N\pi_s^*w_s} \right\}, \qquad \varepsilon_k=D_k+N^{-1/2}. $$

Then $\varepsilon_k\to0$, and for sufficiently large $k$ it lies between zero and one. Assign

$$ q_{t,s}(k)= \left\lfloor(1-\varepsilon_k)N\lambda^*_{t,s}\right\rfloor $$

agents of type $t$ to cubes in simplex $s$. These demands fit, because

$$ \sum_tq_{t,s}(k) \leq (1-\varepsilon_k)N\pi_s^*w_s \leq P_s(k). $$

Give the remaining agents zero-valued types. More precisely, round all but one zero-valued type to $\lfloor N\mu_z\rfloor$, and give the designated zero-valued type the remaining count. That count is nonnegative: the positive-type total is at most $(1-\varepsilon_k)N\sum_{t\in I}\mu_t$.

All type proportions converge to $\mu$. Every positive-type agent has own utility exactly $u_t^*$, and every observer $r$ values its cube at most $u_r^*$. Thus envy-freeness and the thresholds hold exactly.

Combining necessity and sufficiency proves

$$ \boxed{ Z\neq\varnothing,\ 0\leq\beta<1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{eventual feasibility}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \alpha\leq\gamma_F. } $$

The equality construction is also sufficient when $\beta=1$ or $\beta>1$.

For comparison, the thresholds satisfy

$$ 1\leq\gamma_F\leq\gamma_A. $$

To prove the first inequality, let $M_I=\sum_{t\in I}\mu_t\leq1$. Market clearing gives $\sum_s\pi_s^*w_s=M_I$, and therefore

$$ W_t\leq u_t^*M_I. $$

For the second, set $b_{t,s}=x^*_{t,s}/\mu_t$ for positive-valued types and zero otherwise. Capacities hold, and

$$ \sum_s a_{t,s}b_{r,s} \leq u_t^*\sum_s\pi_s^*\frac{x^*_{r,s}}{\mu_r} = u_t^*. $$

These are additive envy-free bundles with own utilities $u_t^*$.

5. An exact arithmetic criterion for a fixed template

Fix a rational certificate $b$ for $\mathcal A_\mu(\alpha)$. Its admissible population polytope is

$$ P_b= \left\{ p\in\mathbb R^\tau: p_t\geq0,\ \sum_t p_t=1,\ \sum_t p_tb_{t,s}\leq w_s\text{ for every }s \right\}. $$

It contains $\mu$.

Proposition 3: fixed-template criterion

There exist integer counts $n(k)$ with

$$ \sum_tn_t(k)=k^d,\qquad \frac{n(k)}{k^d}\in P_b,\qquad \frac{n(k)}{k^d}\longrightarrow\mu $$

for every sufficiently large $k$ if and only if the affine hull of $P_b$ contains an integer vector.

The integer vector in the affine hull is not required to be nonnegative.

Proof. Write the rational affine hull as

$$ \operatorname{aff}(P_b)=\{p:Ap=c\}, $$

where $A,c$ have integer entries after clearing denominators. Include the equation $\sum_t p_t=1$.

Consider the subgroup

$$ D=\{N\in\mathbb Z: An=cN\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb Z^\tau\}. $$

Because $\mu$ is rational and $A\mu=c$, this subgroup contains a positive integer. Hence $D=h\mathbb Z$ for some $h\geq1$.

If the stated counts exist, then $h$ divides $k^d$ for every sufficiently large $k$. Taking arbitrarily large $k$ congruent to $1$ modulo $h$ forces $h=1$. Therefore there is an integer vector $n_0$ satisfying $An_0=c$.

Conversely, assume such $n_0$ exists. Choose $p_0$ in the relative interior of $P_b$, and put

$$ y_N=(1-N^{-1/2})\mu+N^{-1/2}p_0. $$

For every defining inequality that is not an equality throughout $P_b$, its slack at $y_N$ is at least $N^{-1/2}$ times its strictly positive slack at $p_0$. The coordinates of $y_N$ remain bounded away from zero because $\mu_t>0$.

The rational vector space $\ker A$ has a real basis of integer vectors $e_1,\ldots,e_q$: obtain a rational basis by elimination and clear each vector’s denominators. Express

$$ N(y_N-n_0)=\sum_{j=1}^q\eta_j e_j $$

and round each $\eta_j$ to its nearest integer. Define

$$ n(N)=Nn_0+\sum_{j=1}^q\operatorname{round}(\eta_j)e_j. $$

Then $An(N)=cN$, and $\|n(N)-Ny_N\|$ is bounded by a constant independent of $N$. Thus

$$ \frac{n(N)}N=y_N+O(N^{-1}). $$
