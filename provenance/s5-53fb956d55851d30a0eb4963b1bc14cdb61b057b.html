The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(T_L,T_R\), positive rational masses \(\mu_a,\nu_b\) summing to \(1\) on each side, positive rational valuations \(v_{ab},w_{ba}\), a rational \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and a welfare threshold \(\Omega\), does there exist a mass matching \(x_{ab}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_b x_{ab}\le\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}\le\nu_b\), such that \(\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}(v_{ab}+w_{ba})\ge\Omega\) and, for every pair \((a,b)\), at least one of \(\mu_a-\sum_{b':v_{ab'}\ge\alpha v_{ab}}x_{ab'}\) and \(\nu_b-\sum_{a':w_{ba'}\ge\alpha w_{ba}}x_{a'b}\) is zero?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass encoded as a binary rational. Let the two nonempty supports be \(T_L,T_R\), let \(\tau=|T_L|+|T_R|\) be the support size, not the number of possible types, and let \(m=|T_L||T_R|\).
Masses satisfy \(\mu_a,\nu_b>0\) and \(\sum_a\mu_a=\sum_b\nu_b=1\). Every pair is compatible, with positive rational valuations \(v_{ab},w_{ba}\); equal valuations are allowed without tie-breaking. The input also contains rational \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) and \(\Omega>0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A mass matching consists of real numbers \(x_{ab}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_bx_{ab}\le\mu_a\) and \(\sum_ax_{ab}\le\nu_b\). Unmatched mass has valuation zero.
Call it \(\alpha\)-stable if, for every \((a,b)\in T_L\times T_R\), either \(\mu_a-\sum_{b':v_{ab'}\ge\alpha v_{ab}}x_{ab'}=0\) or \(\nu_b-\sum_{a':w_{ba'}\ge\alpha w_{ba}}x_{a'b}=0\). Equality at a valuation threshold prevents blocking.
The decision problem asks whether such a matching exists with \(\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}(v_{ab}+w_{ba})\ge\Omega\).

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), including the encoded input parameter \(\alpha\).

For discrete societies with \(n\) agents on each side, complete positive cardinal preferences, and ties allowed, the welfare-threshold problem is NP-complete under \((n-1)/n<\alpha<1\). [Fernandez Abad, Klumper, and Schäfer](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17949v1#S5)
Welfare optimization for approximately stable fractional matchings is also NP-hard under a different definition based on expected utilities; that result does not address the mass-blocking condition above. [Caragiannis et al.](https://cs.au.dk/~iannis/publ/C96-ec19b.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The welfare threshold's domain was unspecified; require a positive binary rational, following the discrete decision problem.
  • Specify real matching masses, total support size tau, pair count m, and total binary input bit length L.
  • Make explicit that valuation ties are allowed without tie-breaking and that unmatched mass has valuation zero.
  • Retain the graded question's input range alpha in (0,1): normalized masses supply no underlying discrete agent count n to which the anchor's near-one restriction could be attached.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when both supports have size \(n\) and every mass is \(1/n\). An exact algorithm runs in
\[ \min\!\left\{|T_R|^{|T_L|},\,|T_L|^{|T_R|}\right\} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \subseteq 2^{O(\tau\log\tau)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). \]
Here \(L\) includes the binary encoding of \(\alpha\). Thus the problem is fixed-parameter tractable in the support size \(\tau\), but a polynomial-time algorithm for unrestricted inputs would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Full matching and the meaning of the blocking condition

Write \(p=|T_L|\), \(q=|T_R|\), and define the threshold sets
\[ G^L_{ab}=\{b':v_{ab'}\geq\alpha v_{ab}\}, \qquad G^R_{ab}=\{a':w_{ba'}\geq\alpha w_{ba}\}. \]

First, every \(\alpha\)-stable mass matching matches all mass. Indeed, suppose
\[ s:=\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}<1. \]
Since the masses on each side sum to \(1\), some \(a\) has positive unmatched mass, and some \(b\) has positive unmatched mass. Consequently,
\[ \mu_a-\sum_{b'\in G^L_{ab}}x_{ab'} \geq \mu_a-\sum_{b'}x_{ab'}>0, \]
and the analogous deficit for \(b\) is positive. This contradicts stability for \((a,b)\). Hence every row and column is saturated:
\[ \sum_bx_{ab}=\mu_a, \qquad \sum_ax_{ab}=\nu_b. \]

For such a matching,
\[ \mu_a-\sum_{b'\in G^L_{ab}}x_{ab'} = \sum_{b':\,v_{ab'}<\alpha v_{ab}}x_{ab'}. \]
By nonnegativity, this expression vanishes exactly when every positive entry in row \(a\) has valuation at least \(\alpha v_{ab}\). The analogous statement holds for columns.

It follows that if \(x\) is stable and \(z\) has the same row and column sums with
\[ z_{ab}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ x_{ab}>0, \]
then \(z\) is stable: for each pair, whichever endpoint protects that pair under \(x\) still protects it under \(z\).

2. Uniform masses admit welfare-preserving discrete rounding

Assume now that \(p=q=n\) and every mass is \(1/n\). Let \(x\) be stable and put \(Y=nx\). The preceding result shows that \(Y\) is doubly stochastic.

We claim that \(Y\) is a convex combination of permutation matrices supported on its positive entries:
\[ Y=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_kP^{(k)}, \qquad \lambda_k>0, \qquad \sum_k\lambda_k=1. \]

For completeness, consider the bipartite graph of positive entries of \(Y\). For any set \(S\) of rows, its neighbor set \(N(S)\) satisfies
\[ |S| = \sum_{i\in S,j}Y_{ij} \leq \sum_{j\in N(S),i}Y_{ij} = |N(S)|. \]
Hall’s theorem therefore supplies a supported perfect matching, with permutation matrix \(P\). Set
\[ \varepsilon=\min_{i}Y_{i,P(i)}>0. \]
If \(\varepsilon=1\), then \(Y=P\). Otherwise,
\[ Y'=\frac{Y-\varepsilon P}{1-\varepsilon} \]
is doubly stochastic and has strictly fewer positive entries. Induction on the number of positive entries proves the claimed decomposition.

Each \(P^{(k)}/n\) has the same margins as \(x\) and support contained in that of \(x\); hence it is mass-stable by Section 1. For a permutation matching \(M_k\), its mass-stability condition is exactly discrete \(\alpha\)-stability:
\[ v_{a,M_k(a)}\geq\alpha v_{ab} \quad\text{or}\quad w_{b,M_k(b)}\geq\alpha w_{ba} \]
for every pair \((a,b)\).

Let
\[ W(M_k)=\sum_{(a,b)\in M_k}(v_{ab}+w_{ba}). \]
Linearity gives
\[ n\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}(v_{ab}+w_{ba}) = \sum_k\lambda_kW(M_k). \]
At least one \(M_k\) therefore satisfies
\[ W(M_k)\geq n\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}(v_{ab}+w_{ba}). \]

Thus any stable mass matching with welfare at least \(\Omega\) yields a discrete stable matching with welfare at least \(n\Omega\). Conversely, any discrete stable matching \(M\) yields a stable mass matching \(x=P_M/n\), with welfare \(W(M)/n\).

3. Polynomial-time reduction and \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness

Use the discrete welfare-threshold problem from the question. Its \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness holds for complete positive cardinal preferences with ties and
\[ \frac{n-1}{n}<\alpha<1. \]
The cited proof permits the explicit rational choice
\[ \alpha_n=1-\frac{1}{2n}, \]
whose encoding uses \(O(\log n)\) bits. [Fernandez Abad, Klumper, and Schäfer, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17949v1#S5)

Given such a discrete instance with threshold \(W>0\), construct the mass instance by:

  • retaining all valuations and \(\alpha_n\);
  • assigning one supported type to each discrete agent;
  • setting every mass to \(1/n\);
  • setting \(\Omega=W/n\).

All required masses and valuations are positive, and both total masses equal \(1\). Every discrete stable matching is perfect: otherwise there would be an unmatched agent on each side, and their pair would block because both valuations are positive.

Section 2 proves the exact equivalence
\[ \exists\,M\text{ discrete stable with }W(M)\geq W \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists\,x\text{ mass-stable with welfare }\geq W/n. \]

The reduction has
\[ \tau=2n,\qquad m=n^2. \]
If the discrete input length is \(L_D\), the output length is \(O(L_D+n\log n)\), and the construction takes polynomial bit time. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness with binary-encoded \(\alpha\).

4. Exact algorithm and membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

For each \(a\), let
\[ V_a=\{v_{ab}:b\in T_R\}. \]
Enumerate vectors \(s=(s_a)_{a\in T_L}\) with \(s_a\in V_a\). There are at most \(q^p\) vectors.

For a fixed vector, define
\[ h_b= \max\!\left( \{\,\alpha w_{ba}:s_a<\alpha v_{ab}\,\}\cup\{0\} \right). \]
Allow exactly the edges
\[ E_s=\{(a,b):v_{ab}\geq s_a,\ w_{ba}\geq h_b\}. \]
Solve the rational linear feasibility problem
\[ x_{ab}\geq0,\qquad x_{ab}=0\text{ outside }E_s, \]
\[ \sum_bx_{ab}=\mu_a,\qquad \sum_ax_{ab}=\nu_b, \qquad \sum_{a,b}x_{ab}(v_{ab}+w_{ba})\geq\Omega. \]

We verify both directions.

*Soundness.* For any pair \((a,b)\), if \(s_a\geq\alpha v_{ab}\), every positive entry in row \(a\) meets that pair’s left threshold. Otherwise, the definition gives \(h_b\geq\alpha w_{ba}\), so every positive entry in column \(b\) meets its right threshold. Since all margins are saturated, the corresponding deficit is zero. Every feasible solution is therefore stable.

*Completeness.* Given a stable matching \(x\), choose
\[ s_a=\min\{v_{ab}:x_{ab}>0\}. \]
These minima exist because every row has positive, saturated mass. If \(s_a<\alpha v_{ab}\), row \(a\) contains positive mass below that threshold. Stability then forces every positive entry in column \(b\) to have valuation at least \(\alpha w_{ba}\). Taking the maximum over all such \(a\) shows that every positive entry in column \(b\) meets \(h_b\). Thus \(x\) is feasible in its enumerated branch.

Each branch has at most \(m\) variables and \(\tau\) marginal equalities, plus the welfare inequality. Its rational encoding has polynomial length in \(m,\tau,L\), so polynomial-time rational linear programming applies. All threshold comparisons are exact rational comparisons, including equality; their bit complexity is polynomial in \(L\).

Exchanging the two sides gives an algorithm with \(p^q\) branches. Choosing the better orientation yields
\[ \min\{q^p,p^q\}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \leq \tau^{\tau/2}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) = 2^{O(\tau\log\tau)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). \]

Finally, a certificate can specify just one cutoff index for each left type, using \(O(p\log(q+1))\) bits. A deterministic verifier constructs that branch and checks its rational linear feasibility in polynomial time. Soundness and completeness above prove that this is an \(\mathrm{NP}\) verifier, despite the original matching variables being real.

Together with Section 3, this establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.
