The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\tau\ge4\) complete bivalent cost types with rational population distribution \(\mu\), rational per-capita chore supplies \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{m}\), and \(c_t(e)\in\{a,b\}\), decide or construct a finite-support mass allocation \(x_{t,z}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{m}\) satisfying \(\sum_z x_{t,z}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,z}z_e x_{t,z}=q_e\), and \(c_t(z-\mathbf{1}_e)\le(\tau-1)c_t(z')\) for every supported \(x_{t,z}>0\), \(x_{u,z'}>0\), and every \(e\) with \(z_e>0\), where \(c_t(z)=\sum_e z_e c_t(e)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge4\) distinct complete cost types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) chore categories, with per-capita supplies \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{m}\). Global binary-rational values \(0\le a\le b\) specify \(c_t(e)\in\{a,b\}\) for every type and category; costs are additive and are not normalized.
Each person receives an integral bundle \(z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{m}\), allowing multiple copies of a category and the empty bundle; write \(c_t(z)=\sum_e z_ec_t(e)\).
A mass allocation has finitely many nonzero binary-rational entries \(x_{t,z}\ge0\), with \(\sum_zx_{t,z}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,z}z_ex_{t,z}=q_e\) for every category.
It is \((\tau-1)\)-EFX if \(c_t(z-\mathbf{1}_e)\le(\tau-1)c_t(z')\) whenever \(x_{t,z}>0\), \(x_{u,z'}>0\), and \(z_e>0\), including comparisons within one type and removal of zero-cost copies; \(\mathbf{1}_e\) is the unit vector.

Can existence of such a \((\tau-1)\)-EFX mass allocation be decided, and an allocation constructed when one exists, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is deciding existence NP-hard? Here \(L\) is the total binary input length.

For finitely many agents, a complete \((n-1)\)-EFX allocation for common bivalued costs is always computable in \(O(nm)\) time when \(n\ge4\). [Source](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/0110.pdf)
For positive discrete costs \(\{1,k\}\), \(k>1\), later work establishes \((2-1/k)\)-EFX allocations that are fractionally Pareto optimal. [Source](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0440.pdf)
No result settling this exact distribution-over-types problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the population distribution to total mass one and required every listed type to have positive mass; identical cost vectors are merged into one support type.
  • Specified global nonnegative binary-rational cost values with a≤b, allowing zero costs and imposing no cost normalization, as the full version dictates.
  • Interpreted the m coordinates as repeatable chore categories, retained integral bundle counts and empty bundles, and allowed zero supplies.
  • Specified rational allocation masses and an explicit finite list of nonzero entries for construction.
  • Retained all supported-pair comparisons, including within-type comparisons, and required removal of every present copy, including copies with zero cost.
  • Defined L as total binary input length and retained the graded approximation factor τ−1.

Partial resolution and further polynomial-time constructions

1. Status and conventions

The existence decision is polynomial-time solvable:

  • If allocation masses may be arbitrary rationals encoded in binary, every valid instance admits an allocation.
  • If “binary-rational” means dyadic, meaning a rational with denominator a power of two, an allocation exists exactly when every supply is dyadic.

The unresolved component is polynomial-time construction for all inputs. Below I prove additional construction criteria, including an exact EFX construction using a capacitated flow and a common integral bundle.

Write
$$ \alpha=\tau-1\ge3. $$
The promise of distinct complete cost types implies $a<b$: if $a=b$, all complete cost vectors would coincide.

All constructions below represent bundle coordinates in binary. They never enumerate a potentially enormous number of copies.

2. A rounding lemma

Lemma. For any $v\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{m}$, a rational distribution on at most $m+1$ integral bundles can be constructed in polynomial time such that its mean is $v$ and every supported bundle has either $\lfloor V\rfloor$ or $\lceil V\rceil$ copies, where
$$ V=\sum_{e=1}^{m}v_e. $$

Proof. Define prefix sums
$$ S_0=0,\qquad S_e=\sum_{j=1}^{e}v_j. $$
For $u\in[0,1)$, let
$$ Z_e(u) = \left|\left\{j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:S_{e-1}\le u+j<S_e\right\}\right| = \lceil S_e-u\rceil-\lceil S_{e-1}-u\rceil. $$
These coordinates are nonnegative integers.

For each coordinate,
$$ \int_0^1 Z_e(u)\,du = \sum_{j\ge0}\int_0^1 \mathbf 1_{[S_{e-1},S_e)}(u+j)\,du = S_e-S_{e-1} = v_e. $$
The second equality follows because the intervals $[j,j+1)$ partition the nonnegative real line. Only finitely many summands can contribute.

Write $V=r+f$, where $r=\lfloor V\rfloor$ and $0\le f<1$. The total copy count is
$$ \sum_e Z_e(u) = \begin{cases} r+1,&u<f,\\ r,&u\ge f. \end{cases} $$
Indeed, it counts the points $u+j$ in $[0,V)$.

The bundle changes only at fractional parts of the prefix sums $S_e$. Partition $[0,1)$ at these breakpoints, choose a parameter strictly inside each positive-length interval, and assign its bundle probability equal to the interval length. There are at most $m+1$ such intervals. Their probabilities sum to one, and the integral identities give the required mean.

All prefix sums, breakpoints, probabilities, and ceiling calculations have polynomial binary length. Each coordinate is at most $\lceil v_e\rceil$, so large numerical coordinates cause no output-size problem. This proves the algorithmic claim.

We will also need the following dyadic observation. Suppose $\mu>0$ and every $\mu v_e$ are dyadic. For a breakpoint $u=\{S_e\}$,
$$ \mu u = \sum_{j=1}^{e}\mu v_j-\mu\lfloor S_e\rfloor. $$
Thus every breakpoint multiplied by $\mu$ is dyadic. The resulting masses, equal to $\mu$ times differences of consecutive breakpoints, are dyadic.

3. A new exact EFX construction using a common integral bundle

Assume $a>0$. Choose an integral vector $D$ satisfying
$$ 0\le D_e\le q_e $$
for every category. Define the remaining supply and its total by
$$ v=q-D,\qquad V=\sum_e v_e, $$
and put
$$ r=\lfloor V\rfloor,\qquad s=\lceil V\rceil. $$

Call a category cheap for type $t$ when $c_t(e)=a$.

Consider the following feasibility system, whose variable $y_{t,e}$ is the total mass of copies of category $e$ assigned to type $t$ after giving everyone $D$:
$$ y_{t,e}\ge0, $$
$$ y_{t,e}=0\quad\text{if }c_t(e)=b, $$
$$ \sum_t y_{t,e}=v_e, $$
$$ r\mu_t\le\sum_e y_{t,e}\le s\mu_t \quad\text{for every }t. $$

Proposition. Feasibility of this system can be decided in polynomial time. Whenever it is feasible, an exact EFX mass allocation can be constructed in polynomial time, with at most $\tau(m+1)$ nonzero entries.

Moreover, feasibility is necessary and sufficient for an allocation of the following specified form: everyone receives $D$, every additional copy is cheap for its recipient, and every additional bundle has $r$ or $s$ copies.

Flow formulation

Construct a network with a source, category vertices, type vertices, and a sink:

  • The source-to-category edge has lower and upper capacity $v_e$.
  • A category-to-type edge exists exactly when that category is cheap for that type; give it capacity $V$.
  • The type-to-sink edge has lower capacity $r\mu_t$ and upper capacity $s\mu_t$.
  • Add a sink-to-source edge with lower and upper capacity $V$.

A feasible circulation is precisely a feasible vector $y$. Standard lower-capacity reduction gives a maximum-flow feasibility problem on a network of polynomial size.

For completeness concerning binary complexity, one may clear all capacity denominators using their product. Its binary length is polynomial in the input length. A maximum-flow algorithm with a polynomial number of arithmetic operations then works with integers of polynomial binary length. It does not require time proportional to the numerical capacities.

If all supplies and masses are dyadic, all capacities are dyadic. Clearing them with a power of two and computing an integral flow produces dyadic values $y_{t,e}$.

Construction and supply verification

For each type, define its additional per-person mean vector
$$ v^t_e=\frac{y_{t,e}}{\mu_t}. $$
Its total lies in $[r,s]$. Apply the rounding lemma to $v^t$, obtaining a distribution on bundles $W$.

Because $r$ and $s$ are consecutive integers, or the same integer, every supported $W$ has $r$ or $s$ copies. Coordinates with $y_{t,e}=0$ remain zero, so every additional copy is cheap for type $t$.

If $W$ has probability $\rho_{t,W}$, set
$$ x_{t,D+W}=\mu_t\rho_{t,W}, $$
merging repeated bundles.

The type masses are correct. The total supply of category $e$ is
$$ \sum_t\mu_tD_e+\sum_t y_{t,e} = D_e+v_e = q_e. $$

Exact EFX verification

Fix a source type $t$ and write
$$ C_t=c_t(D). $$
A source bundle $D+W$ has owner cost
$$ c_t(D+W)=C_t+a|W|. $$
Every permitted removed copy costs at least $a$. Therefore
$$ c_t(D+W-\mathbf 1_e) \le C_t+sa-a. $$
Since $s\le r+1$,
$$ C_t+sa-a\le C_t+ra. $$

Every target bundle is $D+W'$, with $|W'|\ge r$. Its additional copies need not be cheap for evaluator $t$, but each costs at least $a$. Consequently,
$$ c_t(D+W')\ge C_t+ra. $$
Combining these bounds gives
$$ c_t(D+W-\mathbf 1_e)\le c_t(D+W'). $$
This includes removal from the common bundle $D$ and all within-type comparisons.

Necessity for the specified form

Conversely, suppose an allocation has the specified form. Define
$$ y_{t,e}=\sum_W W_e x_{t,D+W}. $$
The supply equations give $\sum_t y_{t,e}=q_e-D_e$. Cheapness gives the required zero coordinates. Finally,
$$ r\mu_t \le \sum_W |W|x_{t,D+W} \le s\mu_t. $$
Thus $y$ satisfies the flow system.

Encoding and useful choices of the common bundle

The rounding lemma gives at most $m+1$ bundles per type. The means $y_{t,e}/\mu_t$ and all output coordinates have polynomial binary length.

Under dyadic requirements, the lemma’s scaled-breakpoint observation applies because $\mu_tv^t_e=y_{t,e}$ is dyadic. Hence output masses are dyadic.

Two useful choices of $D$, each immediately available in polynomial time, are
$$ D=0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad D_e=\lfloor q_e\rfloor. $$
The latter removes the entire common integral part before solving the flow problem. In particular, integral supplies always admit an immediate exact EFX allocation: give bundle $q$ to every person.

An example beyond the previous bounded-ratio construction

Take four types with equal masses and four categories. Type $t$ sees category $t$ at cost $a$ and all other categories at cost $b$. Let
$$ q_e=\frac38 \quad\text{for every }e. $$
Give each type half its people one copy of its cheap category and half two copies. The two entries for each type have mass $1/8$.

Every target bundle is nonempty and therefore costs at least $a$ to every evaluator. Every nonsingleton source bundle consists of two owner-cheap copies, so any removal leaves cost $a$. This is exact EFX for arbitrarily large $b/a$.

4. A further construction criterion from the common integral part

The flow criterion need not hold. Nevertheless, a sufficiently costly common integral bundle supplies another polynomial construction.

Assume $a>0$ and define
$$ D_e=\lfloor q_e\rfloor,\qquad f_e=q_e-D_e, $$
$$ F=\sum_e f_e,\qquad h=\lfloor F\rfloor,\qquad k=\lceil F\rceil, $$
$$ C_t=c_t(D). $$

Proposition. A polynomial-time $\alpha$-EFX construction exists whenever
$$ (\alpha-1)C_t \ge kb-(\alpha h+1)a \quad\text{for every type }t. $$

Proof. Apply the rounding lemma to $f$, and give every type the same distribution on bundles $D+W$. Every $W$ has $h$ or $k$ copies.

For any source type $t$ and permitted removal,
$$ c_t(D+W-\mathbf 1_e) \le C_t+kb-a. $$
Every target bundle satisfies
$$ c_t(D+W')\ge C_t+ha. $$
The assumed inequality is exactly
$$ C_t+kb-a\le\alpha(C_t+ha). $$
It therefore proves every required EFX comparison.

The common distribution has mean $q$, so all supply and mass equations hold. There are at most $\tau(m+1)$ entries, and the rounding lemma supplies the polynomial running time and dyadic preservation.

This criterion applies outside the regime $b\le\alpha a$. Since $F<m$, its right-hand side depends on at most $m$ additional copies, rather than on the potentially enormous total supply.

Why uniform rounding does not solve every instance

Take
$$ \tau=4,\qquad \mu_t=\frac14,\qquad a=1,\qquad b=100, $$
with two categories and cost rows
$$ (1,100),\quad(100,1),\quad(1,1),\quad(100,100). $$
Let
$$ q=\left(\frac34,\frac34\right). $$
Systematic rounding in category order produces
$$ (1,1),\quad(1,0),\quad(0,1) $$
with positive probabilities. Giving this same distribution to every type fails $3$-EFX: for the first type, removing the first category from $(1,1)$ leaves cost $100$, whereas target bundle $(1,0)$ costs $1$.

The common integral part is zero, so the criterion above also fails. The cheap-flow criterion fails because the fourth type has no cheap category but would need an additional bundle of at least one copy.

The instance itself is easily solvable: give the first two types two copies of their respective cheap category, and give each of the last two types one copy, split equally between the categories. Each category receives supply
$$ \frac12+\frac18+\frac18=\frac34. $$
The only nonsingleton sources are owner-cheap pairs; all targets are nonempty. Thus this allocation is exact EFX.

This example identifies a limitation of the proposed general constructions without suggesting nonexistence.

5. Other polynomial-time construction regimes

Small total supply

If
$$ Q=\sum_e q_e\le1, $$
rounding $q$ produces only empty bundles and singletons. Every permitted removal leaves the empty bundle, so the allocation is exact EFX for all nonnegative costs.

This includes $q=0$, which will be excluded from the finite-agent theorem application below.

Bounded positive cost ratio

Suppose $a>0$ and
$$ b\le\alpha a. $$
Round $q$ and give every type the same distribution. Put $r=\lfloor Q\rfloor$. Every source has at most $r+1$ copies, so every removal leaves cost at most $rb$. Every target contains at least $r$ copies, so its cost is at least $ra$. Hence
$$ c_t(z-\mathbf 1_e) \le rb \le\alpha ra \le\alpha c_t(z'). $$
This constructs an $\alpha$-EFX allocation in polynomial time.

Zero low cost

Suppose $a=0<b$. Define
$$ H=\{e:c_t(e)=b\text{ for every }t\},\qquad J=\{1,\ldots,m\}\setminus H. $$
For every $e\in J$, choose a type $t(e)$ with $c_{t(e)}(e)=0$.

Let
$$ U=\sum_{e\in H}q_e,\qquad r=\lfloor U\rfloor,\qquad f=U-r,\qquad \ell=1-f>0. $$
Use the rounding lemma’s parameterized construction to obtain $Z^H(u)$ with mean $q$ restricted to $H$. Its universally costly copy count is $r+1$ when $u<f$ and $r$ when $u\ge f$.

For each type define
$$ w_t=\mu_t\ell, $$
and let $Z^t(v)$ be the rounding construction with mean vector
$$ v^t_e= \begin{cases} q_e/w_t,&e\in J\text{ and }t(e)=t,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
All copies in $Z^t$ cost zero to type $t$.

For type $t$, prescribe
$$ Z_t(u)= \begin{cases} Z^H(u),&u<f,\\ Z^H(u)+Z^t((u-f)/\ell),&u\ge f. \end{cases} $$
Partition the parameter interval at the finitely many breakpoints and give each bundle mass $\mu_t$ times its interval length.

Type masses sum correctly. Universal categories have total mean $q_e$ because every type uses $Z^H$. For $e\in J$, its designated type contributes
$$ \mu_{t(e)} \int_f^1 Z^{t(e)}_e((u-f)/\ell)\,du = \mu_{t(e)}\ell\,v^{t(e)}_e = q_e. $$

Every target contains at least $r$ universally costly copies and therefore costs at least $rb$ to every evaluator.

A source from $u<f$ contains exactly $r+1$ universal copies and no others, so every removal leaves cost $rb$. A source from $u\ge f$ has owner cost $rb$, with all additional copies owner-zero-cost. Removing any copy, including a zero-cost copy, leaves cost at most $rb$. Thus this is exact EFX.

There are at most $|H|$ universal breakpoints and at most $|\{e\in J:t(e)=t\}|$ additional breakpoints for type $t$. The boundary $f$, when nonzero, is already a universal breakpoint. Hence each type needs at most $m+1$ bundles. All arithmetic has polynomial binary length.

For dyadic supplies and masses, universal breakpoints give dyadic masses. A transformed additional breakpoint has the form
$$ u=f+\ell\left(\frac{P}{w_t}-j\right), $$
where $P$ is a prefix supply sum and $j$ is an integer. Therefore
$$ \mu_tu=\mu_tf+P-jw_t $$
is dyadic. Differences of consecutive scaled breakpoints are consequently dyadic.

6. General existence and the decision problem

The external result used here is the following finite-agent theorem: for common positive costs $\{1,k\}$, with arbitrary real $k>1$, every finite instance admits a $(2-1/k)$-EFX allocation. Its EFX definition requires every source-item removal. The paper also permits an all-high row to be rescaled to an all-one row. These are the needed hypotheses and conventions. [Lin, Wu, and Zhou, Section 2 and Theorem 3.14](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0440.pdf).

Proposition. For $0<a<b$, every rational instance admits a $(2-a/b)$-EFX mass allocation.

Proof. If $q=0$, assign the empty bundle to everyone. Otherwise choose a positive integer $N$ clearing the denominators of all masses and supplies.

Create $N\mu_t$ finite agents of each type and $Nq_e$ distinct copies of each category. There are $N$ agents, and all agent counts are positive integers.

Divide costs by $a$, giving common values $\{1,k\}$ with $k=b/a>1$. If a row is all-high on the supplied items, divide that row additionally by $k$. The resulting costs still belong to $\{1,k\}$. Positive rescaling of an evaluator’s entire cost function preserves all of its EFX inequalities.

Apply the cited theorem and restore the row scalings. Let $n_{t,z}$ count agents of type $t$ receiving category-count vector $z$. Set
$$ x_{t,z}=\frac{n_{t,z}}{N}. $$
Aggregation gives
$$ \sum_zx_{t,z}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,z}z_ex_{t,z}=q_e. $$

For supported bundles belonging to distinct witnessing agents, the finite-agent inequality gives
$$ c_t(z-\mathbf 1_e)\le(2-a/b)c_t(z'). $$
A comparison of a bundle with itself holds directly because costs are nonnegative and $2-a/b>1$. Thus aggregation also preserves within-type comparisons.

Finally,
$$ 2-a/b<2<3\le\alpha. $$
The allocation meets the requested approximation factor.

This proves existence, but the numerical values of $N$ and $N\sum_e q_e$ can be exponential in the input length. Replication is therefore not a polynomial-time construction for the succinct input.

Dyadic allocation masses

Necessity follows from finite support:
$$ q_e=\sum_{t,z}z_ex_{t,z} $$
is a finite integer linear combination of dyadic numbers, hence dyadic.

For sufficiency, when supplies and masses are dyadic, choose $N$ to be a power of two. The positive-cost replication construction then has dyadic entries. The zero-low-cost construction was proved dyadic above.

Consequently, under the dyadic interpretation, existence is equivalent to all supplies being dyadic. Reducing a fraction and checking whether its denominator is a power of two is polynomial-time.

Under arbitrary rational output encoding, all valid instances are affirmative. Thus the decision problem cannot be NP-hard under ordinary polynomial-time reductions unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.

7. Polynomial-size certificates for general positive costs

The exponential replication proof nevertheless yields a polynomial-size certificate, including under dyadic requirements.

Use its denominator $N$, and define
$$ M=N\sum_e q_e,\qquad K=\max\{1,M\},\qquad d=\tau+m. $$
For each supported pair define the integer column
$$ v_{t,z}=(\mathbf e_t,z)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{d}. $$
Every coordinate is at most $K$. The replicated allocation gives integer coefficients satisfying
$$ \sum_{t,z}n_{t,z}v_{t,z}=(N\mu,Nq). $$

Set
$$ h=\lceil\log_2(2dK)\rceil,\qquad k=4dh. $$
If at least $k$ columns have positive coefficients, select $k$ of them. Their $2^k$ subset sums take at most $(kK+1)^d$ values. Since
$$ dK\le2^{h-1},\qquad h\le2^{h-1}, $$
we have
$$ (kK+1)^d \le(5dKh)^d \le(5\cdot2^{2h-2})^d < 2^{4dh} = 2^k. $$
Two distinct subsets therefore have equal sums. Cancel their intersection, obtaining disjoint subsets $A,B$ with
$$ \sum_{i\in A}v_i=\sum_{i\in B}v_i. $$
Both are nonempty: summing the type coordinates shows that equal column sums have equal cardinalities.

Let $\delta=\min_{i\in B}n_i$. Increase coefficients in $A$ by $\delta$ and decrease coefficients in $B$ by $\delta$. This preserves the represented vector, integrality, and nonnegativity, and eliminates at least one positive coefficient. It introduces no new column.

Repeating leaves fewer than
$$ 4(\tau+m)\left\lceil\log_2\bigl(2(\tau+m)K\bigr)\right\rceil $$
columns. Dividing the coefficients by $N$ gives the allocation.

EFX is preserved because the remaining support is a subset of the original support. Every coefficient is at most $N$, by the preserved type-mass equations, and every bundle coordinate is at most $M$. The quantities $N$ and $M$ have polynomial binary length. Thus this certificate has polynomial total length and can be verified in polynomial time.

The argument establishes a size bound. It does not provide a polynomial-time method for finding the subset-sum collisions or the initial EFX support.

8. Remaining construction question

The new flow criterion gives exact EFX whenever a balanced, cheap assignment exists after removing a specified common integral bundle. The common-integral-part inequality gives another polynomial construction for arbitrary cost ratios.

These results extend the previously established construction regimes, but they do not cover every positive-cost instance. What remains is a construction algorithm polynomial in $m,\tau,L$ for the remaining inputs. Neither replication nor the certificate sparsification supplies that algorithm.
