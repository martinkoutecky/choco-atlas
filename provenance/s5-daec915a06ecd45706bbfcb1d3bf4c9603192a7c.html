The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type-interaction graph \(H\), rational masses \(\mu_t\geq 0\) summing to \(1\), a threshold \(B\), and a fixed positive integer score \(\alpha\), decide whether the mass can be partitioned into finitely many coalition vectors \(x^j\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{T}\) such that \(\sum_j x^j_t=\mu_t\) for every type \(t\), each coalition’s positive support is a clique of \(H\), and \(\alpha\sum_j(\sum_t x^j_t)^2\geq B\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix a positive integer score \(\alpha\). Let \(H=(T,E)\) be a finite simple undirected compatibility graph, with \(m=|E|\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Distinct agents of the same type are mutually compatible; agents of distinct types are compatible exactly when their types are adjacent in \(H\).
The input also contains a positive binary rational threshold \(B\); let \(L\) be the total bit length of the masses and threshold.
An outcome consists of finitely many nonzero vectors \(x^1,\ldots,x^k\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^{T}\), with \(\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_t^j=\mu_t\) for every \(t\), such that each positive support \(\{t:x_t^j>0\}\) is a clique of \(H\). Types may split their mass between coalitions, and singleton supports are allowed.
Its welfare is \(W=\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\sum_{t\in T}x_t^j)^2\). The decision problem asks whether an outcome with \(W\geq B\) exists.

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, for each fixed \(\alpha>0\)? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

For indivisible agents, deciding whether a clique partition has welfare \(\alpha\sum_C|C|(|C|-1)\) at least a given positive integer threshold is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for every fixed positive integer \(\alpha\), including variants requiring individual rationality or Nash stability. [Discrete hardness result](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07632v1).
No result settling exactly the divisible-mass decision problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified H as a simple undirected graph, matching the discrete social network; m denotes its edge count.
  • Removed zero-mass types from the supplied support, so tau counts exactly the positive-mass types.
  • Specified B as a positive binary rational, preserving the discrete definition's positive threshold while allowing normalized mass welfare.
  • Made within-type compatibility explicit: distinct agents of one type are mutually compatible, as required by the stated clique-support condition and squared-total-mass welfare.

Result

For every fixed positive integer \(\alpha\), the decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, already for equal masses. Thus a polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The key structural fact is that allowing types to split their mass does not increase the maximum welfare. An exact algorithm runs in \(3^\tau\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)+\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time.

1. Eliminating splits

Lemma. Every feasible outcome can be replaced by a feasible outcome in which each type places all its mass in one coalition, with welfare at least as large. If the original outcome splits any type, the replacement can have strictly larger welfare.

Proof. Fix a feasible outcome \(x^1,\ldots,x^k\) and a type \(t\). Let \(J=\{j:x_t^j>0\}\), and define, for \(j\in J\),

\(y_j=x_t^j,\qquad a_j=\sum_{u\ne t}x_u^j.\)

Thus \(\sum_{j\in J}y_j=\mu_t\). Choose \(r\in J\) maximizing \(a_j\). Move all the mass of \(t\) into coalition \(r\), leaving every other type’s allocation unchanged.

This operation is feasible. Coalition \(r\) already contained \(t\), so its positive support remains unchanged. Every other affected coalition loses \(t\), and a subset of a clique remains a clique. Any zero coalition may be deleted.

The welfare change, divided by \(\alpha\), is

\(\displaystyle (a_r+\mu_t)^2-a_r^2-\sum_{j\in J}\bigl((a_j+y_j)^2-a_j^2\bigr) =\mu_t^2-\sum_{j\in J}y_j^2+2\sum_{j\in J}y_j(a_r-a_j).\)

The last sum is nonnegative by the choice of \(r\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \mu_t^2-\sum_{j\in J}y_j^2 =2\sum_{\substack{i,j\in J\\i<j}}y_i y_j,\)

which is nonnegative and is strictly positive when \(|J|>1\).

Apply this operation successively to all types. Processing one type never splits a previously processed type. After at most \(\tau\) operations, every type belongs to exactly one coalition. This proves the lemma.

Consequently, if \(\mathcal P(H)\) denotes the finite set of partitions of \(T\) into nonempty cliques, then the maximum welfare is exactly

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{OPT}(H,\mu) =\alpha\max_{\Pi\in\mathcal P(H)} \sum_{C\in\Pi}\left(\sum_{t\in C}\mu_t\right)^2.\)

In particular, a maximum exists and is attained using at most \(\tau\) coalitions. This conclusion follows directly from the lemma and the finiteness of \(\mathcal P(H)\); no compactness assumption about the original outcome space is needed.

2. Polynomial reduction from the discrete problem

Use the discrete hardness result stated in the question: for fixed \(\alpha\), deciding whether a graph admits a clique partition with welfare at least a positive integer \(b\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. This is also the specialization of [Theorem 6 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07632v1#S4.SS1) to the scoring vector \((\alpha)\): admissible coalitions are cliques, with welfare \(\alpha|C|(|C|-1)\).

Let \(G\) be such a discrete instance on \(n\geq1\) vertices. Set

\(N=2^{\lceil\log_2 n\rceil}.\)

Construct \(H\) by adding \(N-n\) isolated vertices to \(G\). Make every vertex a distinct type and set

\(\displaystyle \mu_t=\frac1N\quad\text{for every }t,\qquad B=\frac{b+\alpha N}{N^2}.\)

All masses are positive and sum to one, and \(B>0\). Both the masses and threshold are dyadic rationals.

Every added isolated vertex must be a singleton in a clique partition. For any clique partition \(\Pi\) of \(H\), let \(S(\Pi)\) be its discrete welfare. Since the blocks partition all \(N\) vertices,

\(\displaystyle S(\Pi) =\alpha\sum_{C\in\Pi}|C|(|C|-1) =\alpha\sum_{C\in\Pi}|C|^2-\alpha N.\)

The corresponding unsplit divisible outcome therefore has welfare

\(\displaystyle W(\Pi) =\frac{\alpha}{N^2}\sum_{C\in\Pi}|C|^2 =\frac{S(\Pi)+\alpha N}{N^2}.\)

Hence \(W(\Pi)\geq B\) if and only if \(S(\Pi)\geq b\). The added singletons contribute zero discrete welfare, so \(S(\Pi)\) equals the welfare of the restricted partition of \(G\).

This proves both directions:

  • A discrete yes-instance yields a divisible yes-instance by assigning each type’s entire mass to its discrete coalition.
  • A divisible outcome reaching \(B\) can, by the lemma, be replaced by an unsplit outcome reaching \(B\). Its clique partition restricts to a discrete partition of \(G\) reaching \(b\).

The construction is polynomial. Its support size satisfies \(\tau=N<2n\), its edge count is unchanged, and, for fixed \(\alpha\), its rational encoding length satisfies

\(L=O\bigl(\tau\log(\tau+1)+\log(b+1)\bigr).\)

Thus the divisible problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for every fixed positive integer \(\alpha\).

3. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

By the lemma, every yes-instance has a certificate consisting of a clique partition of the support. Assigning a block label to each type uses \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) bits.

The verifier checks that every pair of distinct types in a common block is adjacent, computes the block masses, and compares their squared sum with \(B/\alpha\).

For exact arithmetic, write \(\mu_t=p_t/q_t\), with positive integer denominators, and set

\(\displaystyle D=\prod_{t\in T}q_t,\qquad a_t=p_t(D/q_t).\)

Then \(a_t\) are positive integers, \(\sum_t a_t=D\), and the bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). Writing \(B=p_B/q_B\), the certificate passes precisely when

\(\displaystyle \alpha q_B\sum_{C\in\Pi}\left(\sum_{t\in C}a_t\right)^2\geq p_BD^2.\)

All integers in this comparison have \(O(L)\) bits because \(\alpha\) is fixed. Thus verification takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), proving membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

4. Exact algorithm and running time

Using the integers \(a_t\) above, define \(A(S)=\sum_{t\in S}a_t\). For each \(S\subseteq T\), let \(F(S)\) be the maximum of \(\sum_C A(C)^2\) over clique partitions of \(S\).

Set \(F(\varnothing)=0\). For nonempty \(S\), choose its least type \(v(S)\) under a fixed ordering and use

\(\displaystyle F(S)= \max_{\substack{C\subseteq S,\ v(S)\in C\\C\text{ is a clique}}} \bigl(A(C)^2+F(S\setminus C)\bigr).\)

This recurrence is exact: every partition has a unique block containing \(v(S)\), and removing that block leaves a clique partition of \(S\setminus C\). Conversely, every candidate combines such a block with a feasible partition of the remainder.

Precompute clique indicators and subset sums for all \(2^\tau\) subsets. Across all nonempty states, the number of candidate subsets examined is at most

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq T}2^{|S|-1}=\frac{3^\tau-1}{2}.\)

Also \(F(S)\leq A(S)^2\leq D^2\), so stored values have \(O(L)\) bits. Finally, test \(\alpha q_BF(T)\geq p_BD^2\).

A conservative bound, including graph preprocessing, subset manipulation, and schoolbook integer arithmetic, is

\(O\bigl((m+\tau^2+3^\tau)(L+\tau+1)^2\bigr)\)

bit operations. The algorithm therefore is fixed-parameter tractable in the actual support size \(\tau\), while the unrestricted decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.
