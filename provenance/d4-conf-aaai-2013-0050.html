The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a rational background distribution \(b\) over rankings, strategic mass \(\delta>0\) with \(\sum_\rho b_\rho+\delta=1\), preferred candidate \(p\), and threshold \(q\), choose one common report \(\sigma\in\mathcal R(C)\) for the strategic mass. Is \(\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal R(C)}\Pr_{\nu^\sigma}[p\text{ wins under RV-Borda}]\ge q\), where \(\nu^\sigma_\rho=b_\rho+\delta\mathbf 1[\rho=\sigma]\) and a voter is sampled by mass before selecting their highest-ranked Borda co-winner?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) candidates, \(p\in C\), and \(\mathcal R(C)\) the strict total orders on \(C\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
The input gives background masses \(b_\rho\ge0\) by this convention, a binary rational strategic mass \(0<\delta\le1\), and a binary rational threshold \(q\in[0,1]\), with \(\sum_\rho b_\rho+\delta=1\); unlisted background masses are zero.
The strategic mass chooses one common report \(\sigma\in\mathcal R(C)\), yielding \(\nu^\sigma_\rho=b_\rho+\delta\mathbf1[\rho=\sigma]\).
Apply Borda with scores weighted by mass, and let \(W(\nu^\sigma)\) be its full set of co-winners. RV tie-breaking samples a ranking according to \(\nu^\sigma\), including the strategic mass, and elects its highest-ranked member of \(W(\nu^\sigma)\).
Write \(P_p(\sigma)\) for the resulting probability that \(p\) wins, and \(L\) for the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is deciding whether \(\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal R(C)}P_p(\sigma)\ge q\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite unweighted societies with one strategic voter, this threshold problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete ([Aziz et al., 2013](https://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~sergeg/papers/AzizGMNW13aaai.pdf)).
No result for exactly the rational-mass, common-report problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified rankings as strict total orders and the candidate set as nonempty, with the preferred candidate belonging to it.
  • Specified the strategic mass and probability threshold as binary rationals, with the threshold in [0,1]; normalization permits strategic mass 1 and empty background support.
  • Interpreted the background distribution as a subprobability distribution of total mass 1 minus the strategic mass, represented by positive support entries with omitted entries zero.
  • Extended Borda totals by weighting each report's score by its mass and retained every candidate tied for the maximum score.
  • Defined tau as the background support size and L as the total binary input length; the resulting society has at most tau plus one supported ranking.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. The finite unweighted problem embeds exactly into the stated rational-mass model by normalizing voter multiplicities. This preserves both Borda co-winners and RV winning probabilities, including the contribution of the strategic report.

Here “binary rational” means a rational whose numerator and denominator are encoded in binary.

1. Polynomial reduction from the unweighted problem

We use the known result stated in the question: given an explicitly listed profile of nonstrategic voters, one strategic voter, a preferred candidate, and a rational threshold, deciding whether a strategic vote achieves that threshold under Borda with RV tie-breaking is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. The paper defines RV by sampling from the submitted votes, so the strategic vote participates in tie-breaking. See its formal background and Theorem 9 in [Aziz et al. (2013)](https://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~sergeg/papers/AzizGMNW13aaai.pdf).

Consider such an instance with candidate set \(C\), preferred candidate \(p\), nonstrategic rankings \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_{N-1}\), and threshold \(t\). Thus there are \(N\) voters after the strategic voter submits a report.

For each ranking \(\rho\), let \(k_\rho\) be its multiplicity among the nonstrategic votes. Construct the target instance by setting

\(b_\rho=k_\rho/N,\qquad \delta=1/N,\qquad q=t.\)

List only rankings with \(k_\rho>0\). These masses satisfy all required hypotheses:

\(b_\rho\ge 0,\qquad 0<\delta\le 1,\qquad \sum_\rho b_\rho+\delta=((N-1)+1)/N=1.\)

The support size is \(\tau\le N-1\). Each mass has an encoding of length \(O(\log(N+1))\). Grouping identical explicitly listed rankings and computing their multiplicities takes polynomial time in the source input length. The constructed input length is

\(O\bigl(\tau m\log(m+1)+(\tau+1)\log(N+1)+\operatorname{bits}(t)\bigr).\)

Preservation of Borda co-winners

For a ranking \(\rho\), define \(\operatorname{pos}_\rho(c)\) as the position of candidate \(c\), starting at one, and define its Borda score by

\(s_\rho(c)=m-\operatorname{pos}_\rho(c).\)

Fix any strategic report \(\sigma\). In the unweighted election, candidate \(c\) has total score

\(S^\sigma(c)=\sum_\rho k_\rho s_\rho(c)+s_\sigma(c).\)

In the constructed rational-mass election, its score is

\(B^\sigma(c)=\sum_\rho b_\rho s_\rho(c)+\delta s_\sigma(c)=S^\sigma(c)/N.\)

Every candidate’s score is multiplied by the same positive factor. Consequently, the two elections have exactly the same full co-winner set for every report \(\sigma\).

Preservation of RV probabilities

Denote this common co-winner set by \(W^\sigma\). Define \(I_\rho(W)\) to equal one when \(p\in W\) and \(\rho\) ranks \(p\) above every member of \(W\setminus\{p\}\), and zero otherwise.

In the unweighted election, exactly

\(\sum_\rho k_\rho I_\rho(W^\sigma)+I_\sigma(W^\sigma)\)

of the \(N\) submitted votes elect \(p\) when sampled for RV tie-breaking. Therefore its winning probability is

\(\bigl(\sum_\rho k_\rho I_\rho(W^\sigma)+I_\sigma(W^\sigma)\bigr)/N.\)

In the rational-mass election, that probability is

\(P_p(\sigma)=\sum_\rho b_\rho I_\rho(W^\sigma)+\delta I_\sigma(W^\sigma),\)

which is the same quantity. This calculation also covers the case where \(\sigma\) coincides with a listed background ranking: their masses simply add.

Thus every report has exactly the same winning probability in both instances. In particular,

\(\max_\sigma P_p(\sigma)\ge q\)

holds precisely when the source instance is a yes-instance. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) and running times

A certificate is one ranking \(\sigma\), requiring \(O(m\log(m+1))\) bits.

Verification computes every candidate’s weighted Borda score, determines the full co-winner set, and evaluates

\(P_p(\sigma)=\sum_{\rho\text{ listed}}b_\rho I_\rho(W^\sigma)+\delta I_\sigma(W^\sigma).\)

All comparisons can be performed exactly. To see the bit complexity, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all listed masses, \(\delta\), and \(q\). Since these denominators are part of the input, \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits. Multiplication by \(D\) makes every mass and the threshold integral. Scaled Borda scores have \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits because each is at most \(D(m-1)\).

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, a conservative verification bound is

\(O\bigl((\tau+1)m(L+\log(m+1))^2\bigr)\)

bit operations. This includes constructing the common denominator, computing scores, scanning the listed rankings and the certificate, and comparing the probability with \(q\). Hence the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Enumerating all reports gives a deterministic exact algorithm with running time

\(O\bigl(m!(\tau+1)m(L+\log(m+1))^2\bigr).\)

Neither verification nor enumeration expands rational masses into individual voters.

3. Conclusion

The reduction and verification establish \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for exactly the stated support-encoded, rational-mass, common-report problem. A polynomial-time algorithm for all such inputs would therefore imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
