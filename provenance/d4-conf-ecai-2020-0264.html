Fair in the Eyes of Others (d4-conf-ecai-2020-0264)

Answer: \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-complete}\)

The anchor (named result in the paper)

Corollary 2 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite goods set \(O\), a finite utility-type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), per-capita supplies \(q_o\), and additive utilities \(u_t\), decide whether nonnegative masses \(x_{t,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\subseteq O\) satisfy \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:o\in B}x_{t,B}=q_o\), while no two positive-mass bundles \(B,D\) have an assigned type \(t\) with \(x_{t,B}>0\), \(u_t(D)>u_t(B)\), and \(u_r(D)>u_r(B)\) for every active type \(r\) with \(\mu_r>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\) utility types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also gives a finite goods set \(O\), with \(m=|O|\), binary rational per-capita supplies \(q_o\in[0,1]\), and nonnegative binary rational additive item utilities \(u_t(o)\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Bundles are all subsets \(B\subseteq O\), including the empty bundle; they are implicit rather than listed in the input. Each recipient receives an integral bundle, with at most one copy of each good.
A mass allocation consists of real numbers \(x_{t,B}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{B\subseteq O}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\), and \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{B\ni o}x_{t,B}=q_o\) for every \(o\); all supply must be allocated.
Call it free of unanimous envy if there are no \(t,s\in T\) and \(B,D\subseteq O\) with \(x_{t,B}>0\), \(x_{s,D}>0\), \(u_t(D)>u_t(B)\), and \(u_r(D)>u_r(B)\) for every \(r\in T\). Comparisons include recipients of the same type; indifference does not approve envy.

Is deciding whether such a mass allocation free of unanimous envy exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many agents with nonnegative rational additive utilities and a complete allocation of indivisible goods, existence of an allocation free of unanimous envy is the intended NP-complete decision problem of the [discrete hardness result](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/13778/26864/32482).
No result settling exactly the mass problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified supplies as binary rationals in [0,1], the feasible per-capita range when bundles contain at most one copy of each good; retained exact allocation of all supply.
  • Specified item utilities as nonnegative binary rationals, allowing zeros as in the discrete examples.
  • Specified allocation masses as nonnegative real numbers and all integral subsets, including the empty bundle, as implicitly available bundles.
  • Made positive mass of the compared bundle D explicit through an assigned type s; comparisons also apply within a utility type, and all strict comparisons include the recipient types.
  • Removed zero-mass types from the supplied support and defined L as total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even with exactly two distinct, nonproportional utility types, strictly positive item utilities, masses \(\mu_1=\mu_2=\tfrac12\), and supplies \(q_o=\tfrac12\) for every good.

The proof below applies directly to the specified mass model.

1. Aggregating across types

For bundles \(B,D\subseteq O\), write \(B\prec D\) if \(u_r(B)<u_r(D)\) for every \(r\in T\). Call a family of bundles an antichain if it contains no pair satisfying \(B\prec D\).

Given a mass allocation, define its aggregate bundle masses by

\(y_B=\sum_{t\in T}x_{t,B}\).

They satisfy

\(\sum_{B\subseteq O}y_B=1,\qquad \sum_{B\ni o}y_B=q_o\quad\text{for every }o\in O.\)

Lemma. A mass allocation free of unanimous envy exists if and only if there are nonnegative aggregate masses satisfying these equations whose positive support is an antichain.

Proof. Suppose \(x\) is free of unanimous envy. If \(y_B>0\) and \(y_D>0\), there are types \(t,s\) with \(x_{t,B}>0\) and \(x_{s,D}>0\). If \(B\prec D\), every type—including \(t\)—strictly prefers \(D\) to \(B\). This would violate the definition. Thus the positive support of \(y\) is an antichain.

Conversely, given such aggregate masses, set

\(x_{t,B}=\mu_t y_B.\)

Then

\(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_t\sum_{B\ni o}x_{t,B}=\left(\sum_t\mu_t\right)q_o=q_o.\)

Any unanimous-envy violation would involve two positive aggregate masses with \(B\prec D\), contradicting the antichain property. \(\square\)

In particular, the positive type masses impose no additional restriction on existence beyond their sum being one. The utility types determine which bundle pairs are forbidden.

2. Hardness with two distinct utility types

We reduce from the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem PARTITION: given positive binary integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), decide whether some index set \(I\) satisfies

\(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^n a_i.\)

We may restrict to \(n\geq2\): inputs with fewer items are decidable directly, so this restriction preserves hardness.

Create goods \(O=\{o_1,\ldots,o_n\}\), two types of mass \(\tfrac12\) each, and supplies \(q_{o_i}=\tfrac12\). Define utilities by

\(u_1(o_i)=a_i\quad\text{for every }i,\)

\(u_2(o_1)=a_1+\tfrac12,\qquad u_2(o_i)=a_i\quad\text{for }i\geq2.\)

These are strictly positive binary rationals. The two utility vectors are distinct and nonproportional: their common positive value on \(o_2\) would force any proportionality factor to be one, whereas their values on \(o_1\) differ. The construction has polynomial encoding length.

For any bundle \(B\), let

\(w(B)=\sum_{o_i\in B}a_i,\qquad A=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i.\)

We first establish the exact comparison rule

\(B\prec D\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w(B)<w(D).\)

The forward implication follows from \(u_1=w\). For the reverse implication, integer weights give \(w(D)-w(B)\geq1\), while

\(u_2(D)-u_2(B)=w(D)-w(B)+\tfrac12\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{o_1\in D}-\mathbf{1}_{o_1\in B}\bigr)\geq\tfrac12>0.\)

Thus both types strictly prefer \(D\).

A feasible allocation implies a partition

Suppose a mass allocation free of unanimous envy exists, and aggregate it as in Section 1. All occupied bundles must have the same integer weight, say \(c\): otherwise the comparison rule would make the smaller-weight bundle unanimously dominated.

Using the supply equations and exchanging finite sums gives

\(c=\sum_B y_Bw(B)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\sum_{B\ni o_i}y_B=\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^n a_i=\tfrac A2.\)

Here the first equality uses that the occupied bundles all have weight \(c\) and their masses sum to one.

At least one bundle has positive mass. Its weight is therefore \(A/2\), providing a solution to PARTITION.

A partition implies a feasible allocation

Conversely, suppose \(B\subseteq O\) has \(w(B)=A/2\). Its complement \(C=O\setminus B\) also has weight \(A/2\).

Set \(y_B=y_C=\tfrac12\), with all other aggregate masses zero. Every good belongs to exactly one of \(B,C\), so its supply is exactly \(\tfrac12\). Since \(u_1(B)=u_1(C)\), neither bundle strictly unanimously dominates the other.

The lemma therefore supplies a valid allocation. Explicitly, each type receives mass \(\tfrac14\) of each bundle.

This proves both directions of the reduction and establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under the stated restrictions. The same reduction also works with a single type having utility \(u_1=w\).

3. Polynomial certificates despite implicit bundles

It remains to prove membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\). We show that every yes-instance admits a certificate containing at most \(m+1\) bundles and polynomial-length rational masses.

Start with any feasible aggregate distribution whose support is an antichain. Among all feasible aggregate distributions supported within this finite family, choose one with minimum support size. Write its positive masses as \(y_1,\ldots,y_k\), on bundles \(B_1,\ldots,B_k\).

For each bundle define the column

\(v_j=(1,\mathbf{1}_{o_1\in B_j},\ldots,\mathbf{1}_{o_m\in B_j})^{\mathsf T}.\)

The aggregate constraints are

\(\sum_{j=1}^k y_jv_j=b,\qquad b=(1,q_{o_1},\ldots,q_{o_m})^{\mathsf T}.\)

Bounding the number of bundles

These columns are linearly independent. Indeed, otherwise there would be nonzero real coefficients \(\alpha_j\) with \(\sum_j\alpha_jv_j=0\). The first coordinate gives \(\sum_j\alpha_j=0\), so some coefficients are positive and some negative.

Define

\(\delta=\min_{\alpha_j>0}\frac{y_j}{\alpha_j},\qquad y'_j=y_j-\delta\alpha_j.\)

All new masses are nonnegative, at least one becomes zero, and the aggregate constraints remain satisfied. Their support remains within the original antichain. This contradicts minimum support size.

Consequently \(k\leq m+1\).

Bounding the rational encoding length

The matrix with columns \(v_j\) has column rank \(k\). Select \(k\) rows forming a nonsingular square matrix \(C\), and let \(b'\) be the corresponding coordinates of \(b\). The masses are uniquely determined by

\(Cy=b'.\)

All entries of \(C\) are zero or one. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the input supplies. Its binary length is \(O(L)\), and every coordinate of \(Db'\) is an integer between zero and \(D\).

Let \(C_j\) replace column \(j\) of \(C\) by \(Db'\). Cramer’s rule gives

\(y_j=\frac{\det C_j}{D\det C}.\)

The determinant expansion yields \(|\det C|\leq k!\) and \(|\det C_j|\leq Dk!\): every product for \(C_j\) contains exactly one entry from its replaced column. Since \(\det C\neq0\), each mass therefore has a rational encoding of length \(O(L+k\log k)\).

These rational values equal the original masses, because the selected system has a unique solution. Hence they satisfy all aggregate equations, including the rows not selected.

Verification

A certificate lists the at most \(m+1\) bundles as incidence vectors and their rational masses. A verifier checks:

  • nonnegative masses summing to one;
  • every good’s supply equation;
  • for every ordered pair of listed bundles, that at least one utility type weakly prefers the first bundle to the second.

The last condition is exactly the antichain condition. All checks use polynomially many exact rational operations on polynomial-length numbers. By the lemma, acceptance certifies a valid mass allocation.

Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

4. Conclusion

The specified mass-allocation existence problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even under the two-type restrictions proved above. Therefore a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
