The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite woman-type and man-type sets \(A\) and \(B\), rational masses \(\mu\) and \(\nu\), and strict rankings of opposite-side types, find a nonnegative mass matching \(x=(x_{ab})\) with \(\sum_b x_{ab}=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}=\nu_b\) whose type-level blocking pairs are absent, or whose aggregate divorce distance \(d_\infty(x)=1-\max_{y\in\mathcal S}\sum_{a,b}\min\{x_{ab},y_{ab}\}\) is at most fixed \(\varepsilon<\tfrac12\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be nonempty finite sets of woman-types and man-types, each with a strict complete ranking of opposite-side types; all partners are acceptable.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) positive-mass types, each with its mass as a binary rational. Require \(\sum_a\mu_a=\sum_b\nu_b=1\); let \(m=\max\{|A|,|B|\}\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
A mass matching is a nonnegative matrix satisfying \(\sum_bx_{ab}=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_ax_{ab}=\nu_b\).
A pair \((a,b)\) blocks \(x\) precisely when \(\sum_{b':\,b\succ_a b'}x_{ab'}>0\) and \(\sum_{a':\,a\succ_b a'}x_{a'b}>0\). Let \(\mathcal S\) be the set of mass matchings without blocking pairs.
For fixed \(0\leq\varepsilon<\tfrac12\), construct a rational mass matching satisfying \(d_\infty(x)=1-\max_{y\in\mathcal S}\sum_{a,b}\min\{x_{ab},y_{ab}\}\leq\varepsilon\); exact stability is the case \(\varepsilon=0\).

Is this construction problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Discrete: finding an approximately stable marriage requires \(\Omega(n^2)\) separate-side Boolean queries, including for randomized algorithms; this is a query lower bound, not NP-hardness. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/1405.7709).
Continuous: Baïou–Balinski give a strongly polynomial algorithm for stable allocations under this blocking definition, covering the stated normalized complete market. Consequently, this construction target is already settled in polynomial time. [Source](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

1. The stable-allocation theorem

We use the following known result.

Baïou–Balinski stable-allocation theorem. Given finite row and column sets, strict preferences, positive quotas \(s_a,d_b\), and nonnegative entry capacities \(c_{ab}\), their inductive algorithm constructs an allocation \(z\) satisfying
\(\sum_b z_{ab}\leq s_a\), \(\sum_a z_{ab}\leq d_b\), and \(0\leq z_{ab}\leq c_{ab}\), with the stability condition
\[ z_{ab}<c_{ab} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sum_{b'\succeq_a b}z_{ab'}=s_a \ \text{or}\ \sum_{a'\succeq_b a}z_{a'b}=d_b. \]
Here weak preference means strict preference or equality. The algorithm is strongly polynomial; Theorem 2 bounds its allocation changes by \(3E+q\). [Baïou–Balinski, corrected paper, §2 and Theorem 2](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

Apply this theorem with row set \(A\), column set \(B\), quotas \(s_a=\mu_a\), \(d_b=\nu_b\), and capacities \(c_{ab}=2\). All hypotheses hold: the sets are finite, quotas are positive, preferences are strict and complete, and capacities are positive.

2. Every returned allocation exhausts all masses

Let \(z\) be the stable allocation returned by the theorem, and define \(T=\sum_{a,b}z_{ab}\). Its quota inequalities imply \(T\leq1\).

Suppose \(T<1\). Since both sides have total quota \(1\), some row \(a\) and some column \(b\) have deficits:
\(\sum_{b'}z_{ab'}<\mu_a\) and \(\sum_{a'}z_{a'b}<\nu_b\).

Consequently,
\(\sum_{b'\succeq_a b}z_{ab'}<\mu_a\) and
\(\sum_{a'\succeq_b a}z_{a'b}<\nu_b\).
Moreover, \(z_{ab}\leq T<1<2=c_{ab}\). These three inequalities contradict the theorem’s stability condition. Therefore \(T=1\).

Each row deficit is nonnegative, and their sum is
\(\sum_a\mu_a-T=0\); hence every row deficit is zero. The identical argument applies to columns. Thus
\(\sum_bz_{ab}=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_az_{ab}=\nu_b\):
the returned allocation is a mass matching.

3. Its stability is exactly the requested stability

Because \(z_{ab}\leq T=1<2\), the theorem’s stability implication applies to every pair.

Using the row equality,
\(\mu_a-\sum_{b'\succeq_a b}z_{ab'} =\sum_{b':\,b\succ_a b'}z_{ab'}\).
Similarly,
\(\nu_b-\sum_{a'\succeq_b a}z_{a'b} =\sum_{a':\,a\succ_b a'}z_{a'b}\).

The theorem therefore says that, for every pair \((a,b)\), at least one of these two worse-partner sums equals zero. They can never both be positive. This is precisely the absence of blocking pairs in the question, so \(z\in\mathcal S\).

Output \(x=z\). Choosing \(y=x\) gives
\(\sum_{a,b}\min\{x_{ab},y_{ab}\}=\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}=1\).
For every mass matching \(y\), the overlap is at most \(\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}=1\). Hence the maximum equals \(1\), and \(d_\infty(x)=0\leq\varepsilon\).

4. Rational output and bit complexity

Write the input masses as binary-encoded fractions, and let \(Q\) be the product of their denominators. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Multiply every quota and capacity by \(Q\), giving integer quotas and capacities \(2Q\).

The inductive algorithm’s numerical operations are additions, subtractions, comparisons, and minima, so integer input produces integer allocations. Its updates preserve feasibility. [Baïou–Balinski, §2](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302). Dividing its output by \(Q\) therefore gives rational entries with \(O(L)\) bits each.

For completeness, a conservative implementation bound follows from the algorithm’s structure. Between allocation changes, its alternating sequence has distinct rows and columns, hence length \(O(\tau)\). Each extension can be implemented by scanning the matrix and recomputing preference-prefix sums in \(O(E)\) operations. Together with \(O(E)\) allocation changes and the row initialization, this gives \(O(E^2\tau)\) arithmetic operations.

After scaling, feasible entries and quota sums are bounded by \(Q\), and capacities by \(2Q\). The auxiliary differences and sums consequently have \(O(L)\) bits. Each addition, subtraction, or comparison costs \(O(L)\) bit operations. Computing \(Q\) and converting the quotas costs at most \(O(\tau L^2)\) using elementary integer arithmetic.

Thus construction takes \(O(\tau L^2+E^2\tau L)\) bit operations, with output length \(O(EL)\). No expansion into individual agents is required.

5. The discrete query lower bound

The cited \(\Omega(n^2)\) lower bound concerns separate-side Boolean queries, including randomized algorithms. It does not establish NP-hardness and is compatible with polynomial-time construction from explicitly supplied preferences. [Gonczarowski–Nisan–Ostrovsky–Rosenbaum](https://arxiv.org/html/1405.7709).
