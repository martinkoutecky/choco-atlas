The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given three facilities at rational positions on a line, normalized rational capacities \(\kappa_j\), a threshold \(\alpha\), and rational masses \(\mu_t\) on supported report profiles \(t=(\pi_t,A_t)\), let \(R_t\) be the locations consistent with each report. A deterministic rule observes the report masses and chooses \(x_{t,j}\geq 0\) with \(\sum_j x_{t,j}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{t,j}=\kappa_j\). Nature may place each assigned portion of report mass at any location in its \(R_t\); compare the resulting assignment cost with the full-information minimum cost for that same placement and capacities. What is the infimum, over such rules, of their worst-case distortion over inputs and consistent placements; is it \(2\)?

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) report types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; state any running times in \(m,\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), and any additional parameters.

Its consistency region \(R_t\subseteq\mathbb R\) consists exactly of positions \(z\) whose distances \(|z-q_j|\) are nondecreasing along \(\pi_t\), and for which \(A_t=\{j:|z-q_j|\leq\alpha\min_k|z-q_k|\}\). Distance ties may be ranked arbitrarily; require \(R_t\neq\varnothing\).

Exact value

The divisible-mass model has \(D^*=2\). Threshold \(\alpha=3\) attains this value. Moreover, every other fixed rational threshold has worst-case distortion strictly greater than \(2\).

The upper bound uses the finite-agent guarantee stated in the question, namely that at threshold \(3\) a deterministic rule achieves cost at most twice the optimal cost for every consistent placement. This is [Theorem 5.6 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05905v1#S5.SS3). The main issue is proving that this guarantee survives arbitrary splitting and measure-valued placements.

1. An exact finite characterization of measure-valued violations

Fix an admissible input. Write \(d_j(z)=|z-q_j|\), and let

\( \displaystyle P=\left\{x\geq0:\ \sum_jx_{t,j}=\mu_t,\quad \sum_tx_{t,j}=\kappa_j\right\}. \)

For every report type and pair of facilities, define

\( \displaystyle a_{t,j,k}=\sup_{z\in R_t}\bigl(d_j(z)-2d_k(z)\bigr). \)

These numbers are finite. Indeed, the triangle inequality gives

\( \displaystyle d_j(z)-2d_k(z)\leq |q_j-q_k|-d_k(z)\leq |q_j-q_k|, \)

and \(R_t\neq\varnothing\) supplies a finite lower bound on the supremum.

For \(x\in P\), define the transportation polytope

\( \displaystyle Y(x)=\left\{y\geq0:\ \sum_ky_{t,j,k}=x_{t,j},\quad \sum_{t,j}y_{t,j,k}=\kappa_k\right\}, \)

and its optimal value

\( \displaystyle H(x)=\max_{y\in Y(x)}\sum_{t,j,k}a_{t,j,k}y_{t,j,k}. \)

The set \(Y(x)\) is nonempty and compact: for example, \(y_{t,j,k}=x_{t,j}\kappa_k\) is feasible.

Lemma. For every feasible \(x\),

\( \displaystyle H(x)=\sup_{\{\nu_{t,j}\}}\left(C-2\operatorname{OPT}(\nu,\kappa)\right), \)

where the supremum ranges over precisely the placements allowed in the question. Consequently, \(x\) guarantees distortion at most \(2\) if and only if \(H(x)\leq0\).

Proof. First fix an allowed placement. Consider any feasible reassignment of its aggregate measure, represented by measures \(\beta_k\) satisfying

\( \displaystyle \sum_k\beta_k=\nu,\qquad \beta_k(\mathbb R)=\kappa_k. \)

Since \(\beta_k\leq\nu\), write \(p_k=d\beta_k/d\nu\). These densities are nonnegative and satisfy \(\sum_kp_k=1\) almost everywhere with respect to \(\nu\).

Every \(\nu_{t,j}\) is absolutely continuous with respect to \(\nu\). Thus

\( \displaystyle d\gamma_{t,j,k}=p_k\,d\nu_{t,j} \)

defines a splitting of each original assigned portion according to its reassigned facility. Setting \(y_{t,j,k}=\gamma_{t,j,k}(\mathbb R)\) gives \(y\in Y(x)\). If \(B\) is the reassignment cost, then

\( \displaystyle C-2B =\sum_{t,j,k}\int\bigl(d_j(z)-2d_k(z)\bigr)\,d\gamma_{t,j,k}(z) \leq\sum_{t,j,k}a_{t,j,k}y_{t,j,k} \leq H(x). \)

All integrals are finite by the first-moment assumption. Taking the infimum over reassignment costs yields

\( \displaystyle C-2\operatorname{OPT}(\nu,\kappa)\leq H(x). \)

Conversely, choose a maximizing \(y\in Y(x)\). For any \(\varepsilon>0\) and each positive cell, choose \(z_{t,j,k}\in R_t\) with

\( \displaystyle d_j(z_{t,j,k})-2d_k(z_{t,j,k})>a_{t,j,k}-\varepsilon. \)

Let \(\delta_z\) denote unit point mass at \(z\), and set

\( \displaystyle \nu_{t,j}=\sum_k y_{t,j,k}\delta_{z_{t,j,k}}. \)

This is an allowed finite atomic placement. Reassigning its cell indexed by \(k\) to facility \(k\) gives a feasible cost \(B\). Since \(\sum_{t,j,k}y_{t,j,k}=1\),

\( \displaystyle C-2\operatorname{OPT}(\nu,\kappa) \geq C-2B >H(x)-\varepsilon. \)

Letting \(\varepsilon\) tend to zero proves the identity.

Finally, under the specified zero-cost conventions, distortion at most \(2\) is equivalent to \(C\leq2\operatorname{OPT}\) for every placement. This proves the last assertion. \(\square\)

2. Transferring the finite-agent upper bound

Fix \(\alpha=3\) and an arbitrary admissible rational input. Choose a positive integer \(N\) such that every \(N\mu_t\) and \(N\kappa_j\) is an integer.

Construct a finite-agent instance with \(N\mu_t\) labeled agents reporting type \(t\), and facility capacities \(N\kappa_j\). Run the finite-agent rule with distortion at most \(2\). Let \(n_{t,j}\) be the number of type-\(t\) agents it assigns to facility \(j\), and define

\( \displaystyle x_{t,j}=\frac{n_{t,j}}{N}. \)

We claim that \(H(x)\leq0\).

Suppose instead that \(H(x)>0\). After multiplication by \(N\), the row margins and column margins of \(Y(x)\) are integers. A linear objective over a transportation polytope has an integral optimal solution when its margins are integral. Therefore a maximizing \(y\) can be chosen with every \(Ny_{t,j,k}\) integral.

For completeness, this integrality follows from the support graph of a vertex: a positive cycle would permit a nonzero alternating perturbation preserving all margins, so the support is a forest. Successive removal of leaves determines every edge value from integer margins, making every edge value integral.

Choose consistent positions \(z_{t,j,k}\) approximating the defining suprema closely enough that

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t,j,k}y_{t,j,k}\bigl(d_j(z_{t,j,k})-2d_k(z_{t,j,k})\bigr)>0. \)

Among the labeled type-\(t\) agents originally assigned to \(j\), place exactly \(Ny_{t,j,k}\) agents at \(z_{t,j,k}\). The row equations ensure that all agents are placed, and their reports remain consistent. Because the rule observes only the unchanged reports and facility data, its assignment remains unchanged.

Reassigning these agents to their indexed facilities \(k\) is feasible by the column equations. Let its normalized cost be \(B\), and let the rule’s normalized cost be \(C\). The displayed inequality says \(C>2B\). But the finite-agent guarantee gives

\( \displaystyle C\leq2\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{finite}}\leq2B, \)

a contradiction.

Thus \(H(x)\leq0\). By the lemma, this \(x\) works against all allowed Borel measures, including measures that split individual assigned portions among different locations. Hence

\( \displaystyle \inf_{\mathcal A}D(3,\mathcal A)\leq2. \)

3. A rule computable from the support

The preceding existence proof expands the population to \(N\) agents. That expansion is unnecessary.

First, \(H\) is concave on \(P\). Indeed, if \(y^{(1)}\in Y(x^{(1)})\) and \(y^{(2)}\in Y(x^{(2)})\), their convex combination belongs to the corresponding \(Y\) for the convex combination of the two matrices. Using maximizing solutions gives

\( \displaystyle H\bigl(\theta x^{(1)}+(1-\theta)x^{(2)}\bigr) \geq \theta H(x^{(1)})+(1-\theta)H(x^{(2)}). \)

Express the matrix established in Section 2 as a convex combination of vertices of \(P\). Concavity implies that at least one such vertex \(v\) satisfies \(H(v)\leq0\).

A deterministic rule can therefore enumerate the vertices of \(P\) in a fixed order and return the first one passing this test.

Here are explicit computational bounds:

  • At threshold \(3\), each \(R_t\) is a finite union of intervals and singleton points with rational boundaries. To construct it, partition the line at facility positions, ranking-equality points, and roots of \(|z-q_j|=3|z-q_{\pi_t(1)}|\), then test every open interval and boundary point. There are \(O(m^2)\) candidate boundaries per type.
  • On each resulting interval, \(d_j-2d_k\) is affine after splitting at facility positions. On either unbounded tail it tends to negative infinity. Thus every \(a_{t,j,k}\) is exactly computable as a rational supremum, including when a boundary is excluded from \(R_t\). Its encoding length is polynomial in \(L\).
  • A vertex of \(P\) has forest support in the bipartite graph on the \(\tau\) report nodes and \(m\) facility nodes. Its support extends to a spanning tree. Enumerating edge subsets, testing spanning trees, and solving their margin equations therefore finds every vertex.
  • Each test \(H(v)\leq0\) is an exact rational linear program with \(\tau m^2\) variables.

This gives running time

\( \displaystyle 2^{m\tau}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L), \)

without a population-denominator parameter.

The parameter \(\tau\) here is the actual support size. Additionally, consistency forces every approval set to be a nonempty prefix of its ranking, so \(\tau\leq m\,m!\). For the stipulated \(m=3\), this gives \(\tau\leq18\); consequently the rule is polynomial-time in \(L\), with a potentially large constant.

4. Lower bounds that force the assignment masses

All constructions below have one report type of mass \(1\). Therefore every rule must choose \(x_{t,j}=\kappa_j\). Fractional assignments provide no freedom on these inputs.

We use feasible alternative assignments to upper-bound \(\operatorname{OPT}\); proving those alternatives optimal is unnecessary.

4.1 Threshold \(\alpha=1\)

Choose rational \(\varepsilon>0\), and set

\( \displaystyle (q_1,q_2,q_3)=(-1-2\varepsilon,\ 1,\ 1+\varepsilon), \qquad (\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3)=\left(\frac14,\frac12,\frac14\right). \)

The common report has ranking \(2\succ3\succ1\) and approval set \(\{2\}\). Both locations \(0\) and \(1\) are consistent:

  • At \(0\), the distances in ranking order are \(1,\ 1+\varepsilon,\ 1+2\varepsilon\).
  • At \(1\), they are \(0,\ \varepsilon,\ 2+2\varepsilon\).

Nature places the portions assigned to facilities \(1\) and \(2\) at \(1\), and the portion assigned to facility \(3\) at \(0\). The rule’s cost is

\( \displaystyle C=\frac{3+3\varepsilon}{4}. \)

Swapping the two outer-facility portions gives a feasible reassignment of cost

\( \displaystyle B=\frac{1+3\varepsilon}{4}. \)

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle D(1,\mathcal A)\geq\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\frac{C}{B}=3. \)

4.2 Threshold \(1<\alpha<3\)

Choose positive rational \(\varepsilon,\eta\), with \(\eta<1\), and put

\( \displaystyle (q_1,q_2,q_3)=(-\alpha-\varepsilon,\ 1,\ \alpha), \qquad (\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3)=(s,\eta,s), \qquad s=\frac{1-\eta}{2}. \)

The common report has ranking \(2\succ3\succ1\) and approval set \(\{2,3\}\).

Consider the two locations

\( \displaystyle a=0,\qquad b=\frac{\alpha+1}{2}. \)

At \(a\), the distances to facilities \(2,3,1\) are \(1,\alpha,\alpha+\varepsilon\), so the report is consistent.

At \(b\), those distances are

\( \displaystyle h=\frac{\alpha-1}{2},\qquad h,\qquad g+\varepsilon,\quad g=\frac{3\alpha+1}{2}. \)

The tied facilities may be ranked \(2\succ3\). Facility \(1\) is unapproved because

\( \displaystyle g-\alpha h=\frac{-\alpha^2+4\alpha+1}{2}>0. \)

For the last inequality, \(1<\alpha<3\) implies \(\alpha^2<3\alpha\), so its numerator exceeds \(\alpha+1\).

Nature puts the portions assigned to facilities \(1\) and \(2\) at \(b\), and the portion assigned to facility \(3\) at \(a\). Thus

\( \displaystyle C=s(g+\varepsilon)+\eta h+s\alpha. \)

A feasible reassignment sends the mass at \(a\) to facility \(1\), and divides the mass at \(b\) between facilities \(2\) and \(3\). Its cost is

\( \displaystyle B=s(\alpha+\varepsilon)+(s+\eta)h. \)

Taking positive rational \(\varepsilon,\eta\) tending to zero yields

\( \displaystyle D(\alpha,\mathcal A) \geq\frac{5\alpha+1}{3\alpha-1} >2, \)

where the strict inequality follows from

\( \displaystyle \frac{5\alpha+1}{3\alpha-1}-2=\frac{3-\alpha}{3\alpha-1}>0. \)

4.3 Threshold \(\alpha\geq3\)

Choose rational \(0<\varepsilon<(\alpha-1)/2\), and set

\( \displaystyle (q_1,q_2,q_3)=(0,\ 1,\ 1+\varepsilon), \qquad (\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3)=\left(\frac12,\frac14,\frac14\right). \)

The common report has ranking \(1\succ2\succ3\) and approves all three facilities.

Let

\( \displaystyle u=\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\alpha-1},\qquad a=-u,\qquad b=\frac12. \)

At \(a\), the distances are \(u,\ 1+u,\ 1+\varepsilon+u\). The last equals \(\alpha u\), so all three facilities are approved and the ranking is consistent.

At \(b\), the distances are \(1/2,\ 1/2,\ 1/2+\varepsilon\). The chosen restriction on \(\varepsilon\) makes all facilities approved, and the first tie permits the reported ranking.

Nature puts the portion assigned to facility \(1\) at \(b\), and both other portions at \(a\). Its cost is

\( \displaystyle C=\frac34+\frac u2+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}. \)

Reassign the mass at \(a\) to facility \(1\), and divide the mass at \(b\) between facilities \(2\) and \(3\). This costs

\( \displaystyle B=\frac14+\frac u2+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}. \)

Therefore,

\( \displaystyle D(\alpha,\mathcal A) \geq\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\frac{C}{B} =\frac{3\alpha-1}{\alpha+1}. \)

This equals \(2\) at \(\alpha=3\), and exceeds \(2\) when \(\alpha>3\).

All positions and capacities in these constructions are rational. The mass parameters can be chosen dyadic, every capacity is positive, and every adversarial placement is finite atomic with finite first moment.

5. Conclusion

Every admissible threshold and deterministic rule has distortion at least \(2\). At threshold \(3\), the transportation-integrality argument transfers the finite-agent upper bound to every allowed measure-valued placement. Hence

\( \displaystyle \boxed{D^*=2}. \)

The lower bounds also establish that \(\alpha=3\) is the unique rational threshold at which distortion \(2\) can be attained.
