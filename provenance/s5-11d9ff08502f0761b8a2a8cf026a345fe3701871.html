Result

Let \(\alpha=4\sqrt3-6\). The tight ratio is

\[ R(\lambda)=\max\{3+\lambda,\;4-\alpha\lambda\}. \]

Equivalently, it is \(4-\alpha\lambda\) for \(0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_0\), and \(3+\lambda\) for \(\lambda_0\leq\lambda\leq1\), where

\[ \lambda_0=\frac1{1+\alpha}=\frac{5+4\sqrt3}{23}. \]

The unique minimizing real mixing weight is \(\lambda_0\), with ratio

\[ R(\lambda_0)=3+\lambda_0=\frac{74+4\sqrt3}{23}. \]

Exact computation of both costs, and the accompanying comparison problem, is polynomial-time in the support encoding. The proofs below apply even when the allowed masses are restricted to dyadic rationals.

1. Optimal facilities can be chosen from the support

For a nonempty consecutive block of support indices, define

\[ B(i,j)=\min_{y\in\mathbb R}\sum_{t=i}^{j}\mu_t|a_t-y|. \]

A weighted median minimizes this expression. Indeed, away from the support locations its slope is the mass strictly to the left minus the mass strictly to the right. A support location with at most half the block mass strictly on either side therefore minimizes the convex objective. Such a location exists: choose the first index whose cumulative block mass reaches half the total.

Now take any two facilities \(u\leq v\). Their nearest-facility assignment is consecutive: locations below their midpoint are assigned to \(u\), and locations above it to \(v\). Midpoint ties can be assigned consistently. Replace each facility serving a nonempty block by a weighted median of that block. The assigned cost does not increase, and the nearest-facility cost is no larger than the assigned cost.

Consequently,

\[ \operatorname{OPT}_2(\mu) =\min_{1\leq s\leq t\leq\tau}c_\mu(\{a_s,a_t\}). \]

In particular:

  • If \(\tau\geq3\), every candidate pair leaves some positive-mass support location uncovered, so \(\operatorname{OPT}_2(\mu)>0\).
  • For fixed support size, \(\operatorname{OPT}_2\) is continuous in the ordered locations and positive masses: it is the minimum of finitely many continuous functions.

Write \(C_P\) and \(C_D\) for the expected costs of Proportional and Pairwise-Distance. For \(s<t\), their unordered pair probabilities are

\[ p^P_{st}=\mu_s\mu_t d_{st}\left(\frac1{S_s}+\frac1{S_t}\right), \qquad p^D_{st}=\frac{\mu_s\mu_t d_{st}}Z. \]

On profiles with at least two distinct positive-mass locations, every \(S_s\) and \(Z\) is positive. Thus \(C_P,C_D,C_\lambda\), and—for \(\tau\geq3\)—their approximation ratios are continuous in the profile parameters.

2. Exact transfer between rational masses and finite profiles

Choose a common denominator \(N\) for the masses and put \(n_s=N\mu_s\). Construct a finite profile containing \(n_s\) agents at \(a_s\).

For Proportional, the probability of choosing an anchor at \(a_s\) is \(n_s/N=\mu_s\). The finite-profile distance sum from that anchor is \(NS_s\). Hence the probability that the second selected location is \(a_t\), conditional on this anchor, is

\[ \frac{n_t d_{st}}{NS_s}=\frac{\mu_t d_{st}}{S_s}. \]

For Pairwise-Distance, the total weight of agent pairs at distinct locations \(a_s,a_t\) is \(n_sn_td_{st}\). Pairs at the same location contribute zero. The total pair weight is \(N^2Z\), giving location-pair probability

\[ \frac{n_sn_td_{st}}{N^2Z}=\frac{\mu_s\mu_td_{st}}Z. \]

For every facility set \(F\),

\[ \operatorname{SC}(F)=Nc_\mu(F), \qquad \operatorname{OPT}^{\mathrm{fin}}_2=N\operatorname{OPT}_2(\mu). \]

Thus replication preserves both mechanisms’ outcome distributions and their approximation ratios exactly. Replication is used only in the proof, not in the algorithms.

Conversely, consider any finite line profile with positive optimum. Group its repeated locations into distinct support locations, with masses equal to their multiplicities divided by the population size. There are at least three support locations.

If necessary, approximate its locations by strictly ordered rational locations. Approximate its masses by positive dyadic masses summing to one. Explicitly, for sufficiently large \(k\), round the first \(\tau-1\) masses down to multiples of \(2^{-k}\), and let the last mass be the remainder. All masses are eventually positive and converge to the original masses.

The continuity proved above implies convergence of the approximation ratios. Therefore finite-profile lower bounds on the line remain valid for the specified rational societies, including the dyadic restriction.

3. Tight upper and lower bounds

We use the following published finite-profile results. For every finite line profile, both component mechanisms have cost at most \(4\operatorname{OPT}^{\mathrm{fin}}_2\), and the mixture at \(\lambda_0\) has cost at most \((3+\lambda_0)\operatorname{OPT}^{\mathrm{fin}}_2\). For every \(\lambda\), the supremum over finite line profiles is at least \(\max\{3+\lambda,4-\alpha\lambda\}\). These are the component bounds, Theorem 2, and Proposition 1 of the [cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22484v1#S3).

Their hypotheses match the replicated profiles: repeated locations are permitted, the facility budget is two, and the objective is total nearest-facility distance on the real line. Section 2 therefore gives

\[ C_P\leq4\operatorname{OPT}_2,\qquad C_D\leq4\operatorname{OPT}_2,\qquad C_{\lambda_0}\leq(3+\lambda_0)\operatorname{OPT}_2. \]

For \(0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_0\), linearity in the mixing weight gives

\[ C_\lambda =\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_0}C_{\lambda_0} +\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_0}\right)C_D. \]

Consequently,

\[ \frac{C_\lambda}{\operatorname{OPT}_2} \leq4-\frac{1-\lambda_0}{\lambda_0}\lambda =4-\alpha\lambda. \]

For \(\lambda_0\leq\lambda\leq1\), let \(\theta=(1-\lambda)/(1-\lambda_0)\). Then

\[ C_\lambda=\theta C_{\lambda_0}+(1-\theta)C_P, \]

and therefore

\[ \frac{C_\lambda}{\operatorname{OPT}_2} \leq4-\theta(1-\lambda_0)=3+\lambda. \]

Finally, Section 2 transfers the published finite-line lower bounds to rational societies. Thus

\[ R(\lambda)\geq\max\{3+\lambda,4-\alpha\lambda\}, \]

which matches the upper bounds.

Because \(\alpha>0\), the first branch strictly decreases and the second strictly increases. Their unique intersection is \(\lambda_0\), proving the asserted minimizer. If the mixing weight itself must be rational, the same optimal value is an infimum but is not attained, since \(\lambda_0\) is irrational.

4. Exact expected-cost computation

For the computational question, assume that \(\rho\) has a binary rational encoding, and include its encoding and that of \(\lambda\) in \(L\). An arbitrary real threshold without a finite representation does not specify a Turing-model decision problem.

Define \(c_{st}=c_\mu(\{a_s,a_t\})\). Directly,

\[ C_\lambda =\sum_{s<t}\mu_s\mu_td_{st}c_{st} \left[ \lambda\left(\frac1{S_s}+\frac1{S_t}\right) +\frac{1-\lambda}{Z} \right]. \]

We next give an implementation using \(O(\tau^2)\) arithmetic operations, with polynomially bounded bit lengths.

Integer scaling and pair costs

Choose positive common denominators \(D,Q\) for the locations and masses, respectively, and set

\[ A_t=Da_t,\qquad W_t=Q\mu_t,\qquad \sum_tW_t=Q. \]

These are integers. Products of the input denominators suffice, so all these integers have \(O(L)\) bits. Put

\[ b_{st}=|A_s-A_t|, \qquad T_{st}=\sum_rW_r\min\{b_{sr},b_{tr}\}. \]

Then \(c_{st}=T_{st}/(QD)\).

Define integer prefixes

\[ M_j=\sum_{r\leq j}W_r,\qquad H_j=\sum_{r\leq j}W_rA_r, \]

with \(M_0=H_0=0\). For \(s<t\), let \(h\) be the largest index satisfying \(2A_h\leq A_s+A_t\). Thus \(s\leq h<t\). Splitting the nearest-facility cost into four consecutive ranges gives

\[ \begin{aligned} T_{st}={}&A_sM_s-H_s\\ &+(H_h-H_s)-A_s(M_h-M_s)\\ &+A_t(M_t-M_h)-(H_t-H_h)\\ &+(H_\tau-H_t)-A_t(M_\tau-M_t). \end{aligned} \]

The four ranges are respectively \(r\leq s\), \(s<r\leq h\), \(h<r\leq t\), and \(r>t\). This verifies the formula, including midpoint ties.

For fixed \(s\), the index \(h\) moves only forward as \(t\) increases. All pair costs can therefore be computed in \(O(\tau^2)\) integer operations.

Grouped expectation formula

Set

\[ U_s=\sum_tW_tb_{st},\qquad V=\sum_{s<t}W_sW_tb_{st}, \]

\[ E_s=\sum_tW_tb_{st}T_{st},\qquad J=\sum_{s<t}W_sW_tb_{st}T_{st}. \]

Substituting the scaled sampling probabilities yields

\[ C_P=\frac1{Q^2D}\sum_s\frac{W_sE_s}{U_s}, \qquad C_D=\frac{J}{QDV}, \qquad C_\lambda=\lambda C_P+(1-\lambda)C_D. \]

All integer quantities in these formulas have \(O(L)\) bits: each is a sum of at most polynomially many products of a bounded number of scaled input integers. The only potentially longer denominator comes from summing the \(\tau\) row fractions in \(C_P\). A common denominator divides \(\prod_sU_s\), whose bit length is \(O(\tau L)\). Numerators and subsequent mixture operations have the same polynomial bound.

Since \(\tau\leq L\), ordinary exact integer arithmetic therefore gives

\[ \boxed{\text{Exact }C_\lambda:\quad O(\tau^2\operatorname{poly}(L))\text{ bit time}.} \]

In particular, the exact rational output has polynomial length.

5. Exact optimum and comparison complexity

The consecutive-block argument from Section 1 gives, for \(\tau\geq2\),

\[ \operatorname{OPT}_2 =\min_{1\leq j<\tau}\bigl(B(1,j)+B(j+1,\tau)\bigr). \]

For completeness, the equality follows in both directions. Any facility pair induces consecutive blocks, whose optimal one-facility costs do not exceed its cost. Conversely, facilities at the medians of any proposed blocks have nearest-facility cost no larger than their assigned block cost.

For \(m=2\), all prefix and suffix weighted medians can be found with forward scans. In scaled weights, a prefix median crosses the increasing threshold \(M_j/2\); a suffix median crosses the increasing threshold \((M_\tau+M_{j-1})/2\). Prefix moments then evaluate each block cost in constant time. Hence

\[ \boxed{\text{Exact }\operatorname{OPT}_2:\quad O(\tau\operatorname{poly}(L))\text{ bit time}.} \]

To make the dependence on the facility budget explicit, the same method computes \(\operatorname{OPT}_m\). Precompute all interval costs in \(O(\tau^2)\) operations using weighted medians. For \(m<\tau\), use

\[ D(r,j)=\min_{r-1\leq h<j} \bigl(D(r-1,h)+B(h+1,j)\bigr), \]

with \(D(0,0)=0\) and infeasible states assigned infinity. This minimizes over \(r\) nonempty consecutive blocks. Allowing at most \(m\) blocks gives the same value as exactly \(m\): splitting a block cannot increase its optimal assigned cost.

If \(m\geq\tau\), the optimum is zero. Otherwise,

\[ \boxed{\text{Exact }\operatorname{OPT}_m:\quad O(m\tau^2\operatorname{poly}(L))\text{ bit time}.} \]

Finally, compute \(C_\lambda\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}_2\), then compare the resulting rationals by cross-multiplication of positive denominators. Their bit lengths are polynomially bounded, so

\[ \boxed{\text{Deciding }C_\lambda\leq\rho\operatorname{OPT}_2 \text{ is in }\mathrm P,\text{ with }O(\tau^2\operatorname{poly}(L))\text{ bit time}.} \]

For \(\tau\leq2\), both rules cover every support location and both costs are zero; these cases are handled directly.
