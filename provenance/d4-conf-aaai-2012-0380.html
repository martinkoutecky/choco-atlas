The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), per-unit resource weights \(\omega_t\), and an explicit task list \(j=1,\ldots,M\), with distinct required type sets \(A_j\subseteq T\) and a singleton task for each type, find a welfare-maximizing allocation \(W_j\geq 0\) satisfying \(\sum_{j:t\in A_j}W_j\leq\mu_t\omega_t\), together with an optimistic-core payoff allocation. In the continuous society, a coalition may withdraw resources from any measurable submass of each type, form new task throughput, and compensate affected nondeviators according to the optimistic arbitration rule; does the allocation admit a payoff that no such coalition can profitably block, and can an allocation and such payoff be computed in time polynomial in \(\tau\), \(M\), and the input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). The support size \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Represent type \(t\) by an atomless interval of measure \(\mu_t\), with resource density \(\omega_t\geq0\). There are \(m=M\) explicitly listed tasks, with distinct nonempty required sets \(A_j\subseteq T\), revenue rates \(\pi_j\geq0\), and a singleton task \(k(t)\) for every type. All numerical input is binary rational; \(L\) is its total bit length.
For a coalition's aggregate resource vector \(r\geq0\), define \(v(0)=0\); otherwise \(v(r)=\pi_j\min_{t\in A_j}r_t\) if \(\operatorname{supp}(r)=A_j\), and \(v(r)=0\) if no such task exists. Resources may be split among finitely many coalitions, and task repetitions are allowed.
An initial allocation consists of throughputs \(W_j\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{j:t\in A_j}W_j\leq\mu_t\omega_t\), with welfare \(\sum_j\pi_jW_j\). Each positive-throughput task receives contribution density \(W_j/\mu_t\) from each required type \(t\).
A payoff allocation is specified by type totals \(x_{tj}\geq0\), distributed uniformly within each type: \(x_{tj}=0\) for \(t\notin A_j\), \(\sum_t x_{tj}=\pi_jW_j\), and \(\sum_jx_{tj}\geq\mu_t\omega_t\pi_{k(t)}\). Thus type \(t\)'s payoff density from task \(j\) is \(x_{tj}/\mu_t\).
A deviating coalition is any measurable set \(S\) of positive measure, with type masses \(s_t\). It may reclaim all resources from tasks wholly controlled by \(S\), use its unused resources, abandon any mixed task by withdrawing all its contributions and forfeiting its payoff there, and withdraw arbitrary measurable portions of its contributions from other mixed tasks.
For a retained mixed task, let \(d_{tj}\) be the aggregate withdrawal, so \(0\leq d_{tj}\leq s_tW_j/\mu_t\). Optimistic arbitration gives \(S\) the residual payment \(\max\{0,v(r^j-d^j)-\sum_t(1-s_t/\mu_t)x_{tj}\}\), where \(r^j_t=W_j\) for \(t\in A_j\) and is zero otherwise. The coalition may form new coalitions using its available resources and redistribute its resulting revenue and residual payments. It blocks if almost every member receives strictly more than its initial payoff density; an optimistic-core outcome admits no such block.
Does every instance admit a welfare-maximizing optimistic-core outcome, and is finding such an outcome or determining nonexistence polynomial-time solvable in \(m=M\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), or NP-hard?
Finite-agent linear bottleneck games admit a welfare-maximizing optimistic-core outcome computable in polynomial time ([verified result](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/8256/8115)). A [later treatment](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.0420) also establishes this for finitely many players with divisible resources; it does not formulate the atomless measurable-submass model above.

Result. Every instance admits a welfare-maximizing optimistic-core outcome, and such an outcome can be computed in time polynomial in \(m=M\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

The construction uses optimal resource prices, as in the finite-player result in [Section 7 of the later treatment](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.0420). The argument below proves stability directly for the stated measurable-submass model, including unequal withdrawals and withdrawals that change a task’s support.

1. Welfare optimization and resource prices. Put \(b_t=\mu_t\omega_t\), the total resource of type \(t\). Consider the primal linear program

\[ \max_{W\geq 0}\ \sum_j\pi_jW_j, \qquad \sum_{j:t\in A_j}W_j\leq b_t\quad(t\in T), \]

and its dual

\[ \min_{\gamma\geq 0}\ \sum_t b_t\gamma_t, \qquad \sum_{t\in A_j}\gamma_t\geq\pi_j\quad(j=1,\ldots,m). \]

The primal is feasible, since \(W=0\) is feasible. Its feasible region is bounded: because every \(A_j\) is nonempty, each variable satisfies \(W_j\leq\min_{t\in A_j}b_t\). The dual is feasible, for example by taking every \(\gamma_t=\max_j\pi_j\). Thus optimal solutions exist, and linear-programming strong duality applies.

Fix optimal solutions \(W\) and \(\gamma\), and define

\[ C_t=\sum_{j:t\in A_j}W_j, \qquad u_t=b_t-C_t. \]

Equality of the optimal objective values gives

\[ 0=\sum_t\gamma_tu_t+ \sum_jW_j\left(\sum_{t\in A_j}\gamma_t-\pi_j\right). \]

Every summand is nonnegative. Consequently,

\[ \gamma_tu_t=0\quad(t\in T), \qquad W_j\left(\sum_{t\in A_j}\gamma_t-\pi_j\right)=0 \quad(j=1,\ldots,m). \]

The primal also captures welfare obtainable through arbitrary finite task repetitions. Indeed, from any production coalition with resource vector \(q\) and listed support \(A_j\), retain throughput \(\min_{t\in A_j}q_t\). Summing these throughputs over repetitions gives feasible primal variables and preserves their revenue. Coalitions with unlisted support have zero revenue and can be discarded. Conversely, any feasible \(W\) is realized by the specified uniform contribution densities \(W_j/\mu_t\). Therefore the chosen allocation maximizes welfare under the model’s production rules.

2. Construction of a valid payoff allocation. Define

\[ x_{tj}= \begin{cases} \gamma_tW_j,&t\in A_j,\\ 0,&t\notin A_j. \end{cases} \]

These payments are nonnegative and have the required support. The identities above imply

\[ \sum_t x_{tj} =W_j\sum_{t\in A_j}\gamma_t =\pi_jW_j. \]

Moreover,

\[ \sum_jx_{tj}=\gamma_tC_t=\gamma_tb_t. \]

The singleton dual constraint is \(\gamma_t\geq\pi_{k(t)}\). Hence

\[ \sum_jx_{tj}\geq b_t\pi_{k(t)}, \]

which verifies individual rationality. In particular, the initial total payoff density within type \(t\) is

\[ p_t=\frac{\sum_jx_{tj}}{\mu_t} =\gamma_t\omega_t. \]

3. A valuation bound valid for every support. Write \(\gamma\cdot q=\sum_t\gamma_tq_t\). For every nonnegative resource vector \(q\),

\[ v(q)\leq\gamma\cdot q. \]

To prove this, the cases \(q=0\) and unlisted support follow from \(v(q)=0\) and \(\gamma\geq0\). Otherwise, let \(\operatorname{supp}(q)=A_j\) and \(z=\min_{t\in A_j}q_t\). Dual feasibility and nonnegativity give

\[ v(q)=\pi_jz \leq z\sum_{t\in A_j}\gamma_t \leq\sum_{t\in A_j}\gamma_tq_t =\gamma\cdot q. \]

It follows that any finite collection of new coalitions using aggregate resources at most \(a\) earns total revenue at most \(\gamma\cdot a\).

This bound also applies when withdrawal changes an existing task’s support: it does not assume that the remainder still performs the original task.

4. Stability against every measurable deviation. Fix any measurable deviating coalition \(S\) of positive measure. Let \(s_t\) be its type masses and put

\[ \alpha_t=\frac{s_t}{\mu_t}\in[0,1]. \]

For each positive-throughput initial task, write

\[ r^j_t= \begin{cases} W_j,&t\in A_j,\\ 0,&t\notin A_j, \end{cases} \qquad B^j_t=\alpha_tr^j_t. \]

Uniform contributions imply that \(B^j\) is exactly the aggregate resource vector contributed by members of \(S\) to task \(j\).

Partition the positive-throughput tasks into \(F\), the wholly controlled or abandoned tasks, and \(R\), the retained mixed tasks. A wholly controlled task that is preserved can equivalently be reproduced using its reclaimed resources and counted among the coalition’s new production.

For each \(j\in R\), let \(d^j\) be the withdrawal vector. By the stipulated withdrawal limits,

\[ 0\leq d^j\leq B^j. \]

The outsiders’ initial payment from this task is

\[ H_j=\sum_t(1-\alpha_t)x_{tj} =\gamma\cdot(r^j-B^j). \]

The preceding valuation bound gives

\[ v(r^j-d^j)-H_j \leq\gamma\cdot(r^j-d^j)-\gamma\cdot(r^j-B^j) =\gamma\cdot(B^j-d^j). \]

The final expression is nonnegative. Thus optimistic arbitration’s residual payment satisfies

\[ \max\{0,v(r^j-d^j)-H_j\} \leq\gamma\cdot(B^j-d^j). \]

This proves the required bound for arbitrary withdrawals, including those that remove types from the remaining support.

The unused resources belonging to \(S\) form the vector \(U\), where \(U_t=\alpha_tu_t\). Resources available for new production are therefore at most

\[ a=U+\sum_{j\in F}B^j+\sum_{j\in R}d^j. \]

Let \(Q\) be the coalition’s total revenue from new production and residual payments. Abandoned tasks provide no residual payment, so

\[ Q\leq\gamma\cdot a+ \sum_{j\in R}\gamma\cdot(B^j-d^j) =\gamma\cdot U+\sum_{j\in F\cup R}\gamma\cdot B^j. \]

Because \(\gamma_tu_t=0\), we have \(\gamma\cdot U=0\). Consequently,

\[ Q\leq\sum_t\alpha_t\gamma_tC_t =\sum_t\alpha_t\sum_jx_{tj} =\sum_ts_tp_t. \]

The right-hand side is exactly \(S\)’s initial aggregate payoff.

To finish, let \(f\) be any measurable payoff density obtainable after the deviation. If almost every member strictly improved, then \(h=f-p\) would be positive almost everywhere on \(S\), where \(p\) is the initial density. Since \(S\) has positive measure, some measurable set \(\{h\geq1/n\}\) has positive measure: these sets, over positive integers \(n\), cover \(S\) up to a null set. Therefore \(\int_Sh>0\), and hence

\[ \int_Sf>\int_Sp=\sum_ts_tp_t. \]

This contradicts the aggregate revenue bound. Thus no measurable coalition blocks, proving that the constructed outcome belongs to the optimistic core.

5. Polynomial-time construction. The primal has \(m\) variables and \(\tau\) resource constraints; the dual has \(\tau\) variables and \(m\) task constraints. Their incidence coefficients are explicit zeros and ones. Forming the rational products \(b_t=\mu_t\omega_t\) produces an encoding of size polynomial in \(L\).

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming yields exact rational optimal solutions of polynomial encoding length in time polynomial in the LP dimensions and encoding length. Its hypotheses hold here by the feasibility and finite optimum verified above. Computing the products \(x_{tj}=\gamma_tW_j\) then takes polynomial time and produces a polynomial-size output.

Thus the requested search problem is polynomial-time solvable. On every input satisfying the stated hypotheses, a welfare-maximizing optimistic-core outcome exists.
