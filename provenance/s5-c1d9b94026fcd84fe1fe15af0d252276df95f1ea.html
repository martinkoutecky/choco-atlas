The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) alternatives, finitely many interaction cohorts \(A\), rational masses \(\mu_{a,\sigma}\) over cohort-ranking pairs, a symmetric cohort interaction-rate matrix \(q\), a score embedding and decoder, and an error target \(\eta>0\), consider the mean-field pairwise-gossip process in which each agent starts at the embedding of its ranking, interacts at per-agent rates specified by \(q\), and both participants replace their score estimates by their average. Compute a finite per-capita time \(T\) and certificate such that the expected mass-average Kendall-\(\tau\) error of agents’ decoded rankings at time \(T\), relative to the ranking decoded from the population mean embedding, is at most \(\eta\).

Rankings are strict and complete over \(m\geq 1\) alternatives; \(A\) is a nonempty finite set of positive-mass cohorts, with \(w_a=\sum_\sigma\mu_{a,\sigma}\).

The score map \(\psi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^m\) is supplied as a finite circuit using rational affine operations, comparisons, and conditional selection. Result. The certified-time problem is solvable in polynomial time on the promised inputs. The returned process time can be exponentially large, but its binary encoding and the certificate have polynomial length.

The key point is that the promise of local ranking stability, together with the restricted circuit operations, implies an explicit stability radius whose encoding has polynomial length. Computing the largest stability radius is unnecessary.

1. An explicit radius from the stability promise.

Write \(n=|A|\), and let \(s\) denote the circuit size, counting scalar gates and coefficient incidences in affine gates. Set

\(N=L+s+d+m+\tau+n+10,\qquad \rho=2^{-8N^2}.\)

We prove that, on every promised input,

\(\|y-\bar x\|_2\leq\rho\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D(y)=D(\bar x).\)

An affine gate here has fixed rational coefficients. Conditional selection chooses between previously computed values; it does not multiply two variable values.

Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of all rational input literals, including masses, embeddings, and circuit coefficients. Because their total encoding length is at most \(L\),

\(Q\leq 2^L.\)

Each coordinate of \(\bar x=\sum_{a,\sigma}\mu_{a,\sigma}\phi(\sigma)\) has denominator dividing \(Q^2\).

Freeze every comparison outcome and every conditional choice in the circuit. Every numerical wire then becomes a rational affine function of the input. Consider all such affine functions, including those obtained from assignments of outcomes that are not realizable.

Their coefficients have denominators dividing \(Q^{s+1}\). Indeed, every affine gate increases the required denominator exponent by at most one, while selection merely copies a predecessor.

Their coefficient magnitudes are at most

\(\bigl((N+1)2^L\bigr)^{s+1}\leq 2^{2N^2}.\)

To see this, input-coordinate coefficients have magnitude at most one, rational literals have magnitude at most \(2^L\), and an affine gate increases the maximum coefficient magnitude by at most the factor \((N+1)2^L\).

Now consider any affine difference \(g(x)=a\cdot x+b\) that can arise either in a circuit comparison or between two output scores. The preceding bounds imply

\(\|a\|_2\leq 2^{3N^2}.\)

Also, \(g(\bar x)\) has denominator dividing \(Q^{s+3}\). Consequently,

\(g(\bar x)\neq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |g(\bar x)|\geq Q^{-(s+3)}\geq 2^{-N^2}.\)

For every \(y\) with \(\|y-\bar x\|_2\leq\rho\),

\(|g(y)-g(\bar x)|\leq 2^{3N^2}\rho=2^{-5N^2}<\tfrac12\,2^{-N^2}.\)

Thus every such affine difference that is nonzero at \(\bar x\) retains its sign throughout this ball. These bounds hold uniformly over all frozen outcomes; the algorithm need not enumerate them.

Fix \(v\) with \(0<\|v\|_2\leq\rho\), and consider the points \(\bar x+tv\), where \(0<t\leq1\). We show that the circuit’s comparison outcomes are independent of \(t\).

Proceed through the circuit in topological order. Once all preceding comparison outcomes agree along the ray, each numerical predecessor is represented by the same affine function along that ray. For their comparison difference \(g\):

  • If \(g(\bar x)\neq0\), its sign is constant by the preceding clearance bound.
  • If \(g(\bar x)=0\), then \(g(\bar x+tv)=t\,a\cdot v\), whose sign, including whether it equals zero, is independent of positive \(t\).

This proves the induction, including comparisons involving equality. Conditional choices therefore agree along the ray. Applying the same argument to every output-score difference proves that the sorted ranking, with the specified index tie-breaking, is independent of positive \(t\).

By the local-stability promise, this ranking equals \(D(\bar x)\) for sufficiently small positive \(t\). Hence it equals \(D(\bar x)\) also at \(t=1\). The center itself is immediate, proving the claimed radius.

This argument explicitly handles comparison boundaries through \(\bar x\). It does not assume that comparisons or scores are strict there.

2. Compact support and legitimate moment calculations.

Let

\(K=\operatorname{conv}\{\phi(\sigma):(a,\sigma)\text{ is supported}\}.\)

This set is compact and closed under averaging. The laws remain supported on \(K\).

For completeness, this follows directly from the measure equation. Define

\(r_a=\sum_b q_{ab}w_b,\)

and let \(B(\nu,\xi)\) be the law of \((X+Y)/2\) for independent \(X\sim\nu\), \(Y\sim\xi\). The corresponding integral equation is

\(f_a(t)=e^{-r_at}f_a(0)+\int_0^t e^{-r_a(t-u)}\sum_b q_{ab}w_b B(f_a(u),f_b(u))\,du.\)

It maps paths of probability measures on \(K\) to paths of probability measures on \(K\): all terms are nonnegative, and their total mass is

\(e^{-r_at}+\int_0^t e^{-r_a(t-u)}r_a\,du=1.\)

Moreover, for probability measures, the total-variation norm satisfies

\(\|B(\nu,\xi)-B(\nu',\xi')\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\|\nu-\nu'\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+\|\xi-\xi'\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.\)

This follows by expanding the difference of product measures and using that pushforward does not increase total variation. The integral operator is therefore a contraction on a sufficiently short time interval. Repeating the construction gives a global probability-valued solution supported on \(K\), with uniqueness.

The specified weak formulation has the same uniqueness: bounded continuous tests determine measures, and its integral form yields a total-variation Lipschitz bound in time; the preceding Lipschitz estimate then gives uniqueness by Grönwall’s inequality.

Because the support is compact, coordinate and quadratic functions can be replaced by bounded continuous functions agreeing with them on \(K\). Their moment calculations are therefore justified under the stated test-function formulation.

3. Dispersion decay at the specified per-capita rate.

Define the cohort means and conditional variances by

\(z_a(t)=\int x\,df_a(t)(x),\qquad v_a(t)=\int\|x-z_a(t)\|_2^2\,df_a(t)(x).\)

The mean equations are

\(z_a'(t)=\tfrac12\sum_b q_{ab}w_b(z_b(t)-z_a(t)).\)

Symmetry of \(q\) gives

\(\dfrac{d}{dt}\sum_a w_a z_a(t)=\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}q_{ab}w_aw_b(z_b-z_a)=0.\)

Thus the global mean remains \(\bar x\).

Let

\(V(t)=\sum_a w_a\int\|x-\bar x\|_2^2\,df_a(t)(x).\)

Using symmetry and the identity

\(\left\|\frac{x+y}{2}-\bar x\right\|_2^2-\frac{\|x-\bar x\|_2^2+\|y-\bar x\|_2^2}{2}=-\frac14\|x-y\|_2^2,\)

we obtain the exact dissipation identity

\(-V'(t)=\frac14\sum_{a,b}q_{ab}w_aw_b\iint\|x-y\|_2^2\,df_a(x)\,df_b(y).\)

In particular, this uses the time scale in the question, including its convention for self-interactions.

Independence of partners gives

\(\iint\|x-y\|_2^2\,df_a(x)\,df_b(y)=v_a+v_b+\|z_a-z_b\|_2^2.\)

Put

\(U=\sum_a w_av_a,\qquad H=\sum_a w_a\|z_a-\bar x\|_2^2.\)

Then \(V=U+H\), and

\(-V'=\tfrac12\sum_a w_ar_av_a+\tfrac14\sum_{a,b}q_{ab}w_aw_b\|z_a-z_b\|_2^2.\)

Let

\(q_{\min}=\min\{q_{ab}:q_{ab}>0\},\qquad w_{\min}=\min_a w_a,\qquad \lambda=\frac{q_{\min}w_{\min}^2}{2n}.\)

These quantities are positive under the graph hypotheses.

Suppose first that \(n\geq2\). Every cohort has a positive-rate neighbor, so \(r_a\geq q_{\min}w_{\min}\). Therefore the conditional-variance contribution is at least

\(\tfrac12q_{\min}w_{\min}U.\)

Choose a spanning tree \(\mathcal T\) of the positive-rate graph, ignoring self-loops. The weighted variance identity gives

\(H=\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}w_aw_b\|z_a-z_b\|_2^2.\)

The tree path between any two vertices has at most \(n-1\) edges. Cauchy–Schwarz along that path therefore gives

\(\|z_a-z_b\|_2^2\leq(n-1)\sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal T}\|z_u-z_v\|_2^2.\)

Since \(\sum_{a,b}w_aw_b=1\),

\(H\leq\frac{n-1}{2}\sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal T}\|z_u-z_v\|_2^2.\)

Both orientations of each tree edge occur in the dissipation sum. Hence its cohort-mean contribution is at least

\(\frac{q_{\min}w_{\min}^2}{2}\sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal T}\|z_u-z_v\|_2^2\geq\frac{q_{\min}w_{\min}^2}{n-1}H.\)

Our \(\lambda\) is no larger than either coefficient, so \(-V'\geq\lambda V\).

If \(n=1\), the non-bipartite promise ensures a positive self-loop. Here \(H=0\), \(w_1=1\), and the exact identity gives \(-V'=q_{11}V/2=\lambda V\).

Thus, in every case,

\(V(t)\leq V(0)e^{-\lambda t},\qquad V(0)=\sum_{a,\sigma}\mu_{a,\sigma}\|\phi(\sigma)-\bar x\|_2^2.\)

4. Algorithm and certificate.

Let \(M=\binom m2\), the maximum unnormalized Kendall distance. The radius proved in Section 1 implies the pointwise bound

\(d_\tau(D(x),\sigma_\star)\leq M\,\mathbf1_{\{\|x-\bar x\|_2>\rho\}}\leq\frac{M}{\rho^2}\|x-\bar x\|_2^2.\)

Consequently,

\(E(t)\leq\frac{M V(0)}{\rho^2}e^{-\lambda t}.\)

The analogous conversion from dispersion to expected ranking error appears in Theorem 2 of the [discrete gossip paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22847v2); the continuous-time dissipation and computational radius used here were proved above.

The algorithm is:

  • Compute the cohort masses, \(\bar x\), \(\sigma_\star=D(\bar x)\), and \(V(0)\), exactly.
  • If \(M=0\), or \(\eta\geq M\), return \(T=0\) with the certificate \(E(0)\leq M\leq\eta\). If \(V(0)=0\), return \(T=0\): positivity of every supported mass implies every initial state equals \(\bar x\), so \(E(0)=0\).
  • Otherwise compute \(\rho,q_{\min},w_{\min},\lambda\), and find a spanning tree when \(n\geq2\).
  • Find the least nonnegative integer \(k\) satisfying

\(M V(0)\leq\eta\rho^2\,2^k.\)

This requires only integer bit-length calculations and exact comparisons.

  • Return the integer

\(T=\left\lceil\frac{k}{\lambda}\right\rceil.\)

An integer is, in particular, a finite binary-rational output. Since \(\lambda T\geq k\) and \(e\geq2\),

\(E(T)\leq\frac{M V(0)}{\rho^2}e^{-\lambda T}\leq\frac{M V(0)}{\rho^2}2^{-k}\leq\eta.\)

A certificate consists of the computed rational data, the spanning tree when applicable, and \(k,T\). Its numerical checks are the exact inequalities

\(M V(0)\leq\eta\rho^2\,2^k,\qquad \lambda T\geq k.\)

The verifier recomputes the masses, mean, target ranking, initial dispersion, and rate bound, and checks the tree. Soundness then follows from the proved radius and dissipation lemmas. As appropriate for this promise problem, soundness assumes the stated local-stability promise; the verifier need not decide whether that promise holds.

5. Computation time and returned process time.

All required rational operands have \(O(N^2)\) bits. In particular, circuit evaluation has polynomial bit complexity by the same coefficient and denominator bounds used in Section 1.

The algorithm uses

\(O\bigl(L+\tau d+s+m\log(m+1)+n^2\bigr)\)

arithmetic, comparison, and graph-processing operations, with polynomial-length operands. Using schoolbook integer arithmetic and standard gcd-based rational reduction, one valid conservative bit-time bound is

\(\widetilde O\!\left(\bigl(L+\tau d+s+m\log(m+1)+n^2\bigr)N^4\right).\)

Here \(\widetilde O\) suppresses logarithmic factors. This is polynomial in every requested parameter, and includes the encoding of \(\eta\) through \(L\). No enumeration of unsupported rankings or circuit branches is performed.

For the magnitude of the returned time, every positive input rational is at least \(2^{-L}\). Consequently,

\(q_{\min}\geq2^{-L},\qquad w_{\min}\geq2^{-L},\qquad \lambda^{-1}\leq2n\,2^{3L}.\)

Also, each embedding coordinate and each mean coordinate has magnitude at most \(2^L\), so

\(V(0)\leq4d\,2^{2L}.\)

Together with \(\eta\geq2^{-L}\) and \(\rho^{-2}=2^{16N^2}\), this gives \(k=O(N^2)\). Therefore

\(T=O\!\left(nN^2\,2^{3L}\right),\)

while the binary encoding of \(T\) has only \(O(L+\log n+\log N)\) bits. The full certificate has polynomial length.

6. Exponentially large process times can be necessary.

This distinction is intrinsic. Take \(m=2\), \(d=1\), one cohort of mass one, and two supported rankings with masses \(1/2\) and embeddings \(-1\) and \(1\). Then \(\bar x=0\). Set

\(\psi(x)=(0,1)\ \text{if }-\tfrac12<x<\tfrac12,\qquad \psi(x)=(1,0)\ \text{otherwise}.\)

This is an allowed circuit, and it is locally ranking-stable at zero, for example with radius \(1/4\). Let the single self-interaction rate be \(q_{11}=2^{-b}\), and set \(\eta=1/4\). The loop makes the cohort graph connected and non-bipartite.

The integral equation contains the nonnegative contribution \(e^{-q_{11}t}f_1(0)\). Both initial states have Kendall error one relative to \(D(0)\), so

\(E(t)\geq e^{-2^{-b}t}.\)

Any valid time must consequently satisfy

\(T\geq2^b\log4.\)

These instances have input length \(O(b)\), demonstrating that polynomial computation time cannot imply polynomial process time.
