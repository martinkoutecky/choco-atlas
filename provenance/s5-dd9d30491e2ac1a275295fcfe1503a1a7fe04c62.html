The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite recipient types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational positive weight densities \(q_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_tq_t=1\), good classes \(G\) with rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_g\), and bivalued valuations \(v_t(g)\in\{1,k\}\), compute a distribution \(x_{t,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{G}\). Require \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}B_g=\sigma_g\), and, for every positively represented \(B\) of type \(t\), \(B'\) of type \(s\), and \(g\) with \(B'_g>0\), \(\frac{v_t(B)}{q_t}\ge\frac{v_t(B')-v_t(g)}{q_s}\); the resulting population allocation must also be fractionally Pareto optimal.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|T|\) recipient types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type specifies a positive rational weight density \(q_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_tq_t=1\), and additive valuations taking values in \(\{1,k\}\), for a common input rational \(k>1\).
There are \(m=|G|\) good classes, with nonnegative rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_g\); copies within a class are identical. All numerical inputs use binary encoding, and \(L\) is their total input bit length.
An allocation is a finitely supported family of nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,B}\), indexed by integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{G}\), satisfying \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}B_g=\sigma_g\). Empty bundles are allowed.
Require weighted envy-freeness up to any good: whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\), \(x_{s,B'}>0\), and \(B'_g>0\), require \(v_t(B)/q_t\geq (v_t(B')-v_t(g))/q_s\), including comparisons within a type.
Require fractional Pareto optimality at the recipient level: there must be no fractional bundles \(y_{t,B}\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{G}\) for the positively represented groups satisfying \(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}y_{t,B,g}=\sigma_g\) and \(v_t(y_{t,B})\geq v_t(B)\) for every such group, with strict inequality for at least one group.

Is computing such an allocation, or determining that none exists, solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), with \(k\) included in the binary input, or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For a finite list of \(n\) agents and \(M\) individual goods with common positive bivalued valuations and positive normalized weights, a WEFX and fPO allocation can be computed in \(O(\min\{k,M\}n^2M^2)\) time. [Verified discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08345v1).
No result settling this exact mass-supported problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized recipient masses to sum to one, making supplies per capita for the entire population.
  • Specified the common valuation ratio k as a binary-encoded rational input strictly greater than one.
  • Specified supplies as nonnegative and copies within each good class as identical; m counts classes.
  • Specified allocations as finitely supported rational mass families, allowing empty bundles and unrestricted nonnegative integral multiplicities.
  • Defined population fPO by preserving each positively represented recipient group's utility under fractional reassignment, with a strict improvement on positive mass, rather than by preserving only each type's average utility.
  • Included determining nonexistence because the discrete existence guarantee does not itself establish existence in the mass model.

Partial results: a new fPO characterization and two polynomial-time cases

The general allocation search problem remains unresolved. I prove three additional results:

  • An allocation is recipient-level fPO exactly when its aggregate allocation maximizes the quantity assigned on $k$-valued edges, subject to fixing each type’s total quantity of copies.
  • Every fPO allocation admits a price certificate consisting of bounded integer powers of $k$.
  • Polynomial-time constructions exist when the total supply is at most one, and when all types have identical valuation vectors.

I also give self-contained proofs of universal existence and polynomial-size verifiable witnesses, including the encoding detail identified by the verifier.

1. Aggregate efficiency and supporting prices

Write
$$ a_{tg}=v_t(g)\in\{1,k\}. $$
For a feasible allocation $x$, define its aggregate quantities and utilities by
$$ z_{tg}=\sum_Bx_{t,B}B_g, \qquad U_t=\sum_ga_{tg}z_{tg}. $$

Lemma 1: recipient-level and aggregate fPO coincide

The allocation is recipient-level fPO if and only if there is no nonnegative matrix $Z$ satisfying
$$ \sum_tZ_{tg}=\sigma_g, \qquad \sum_ga_{tg}Z_{tg}\geq U_t $$
for every type, with strict inequality for at least one type.

Proof. A recipient-level improvement gives such a matrix by
$$ Z_{tg}=\sum_Bx_{t,B}y_{t,B,g}. $$
A strictly improved group has positive mass, so its type’s aggregate utility strictly increases.

Conversely, suppose such a matrix $Z$ exists. Put $u_{t,B}=v_t(B)$. For a type with $U_t>0$, assign
$$ y_{t,B,g}=\frac{u_{t,B}}{U_t}Z_{tg}. $$
These assignments realize $Z$, since
$$ \sum_Bx_{t,B}y_{t,B,g} =\frac{\sum_Bx_{t,B}u_{t,B}}{U_t}Z_{tg} =Z_{tg}. $$
Their utilities satisfy
$$ v_t(y_{t,B}) =\frac{u_{t,B}}{U_t}\sum_ga_{tg}Z_{tg} \geq u_{t,B}. $$
If this type’s aggregate utility strictly increases, at least one represented group has positive initial utility and strictly improves.

If $U_t=0$, positivity of valuations implies that every represented bundle of this type is empty. Assign
$$ y_{t,B,g}=\frac{Z_{tg}}{\mu_t}. $$
This realizes $Z$ and strictly improves these groups whenever the new aggregate utility is positive. Thus an aggregate improvement yields a recipient-level improvement. $\square$

Lemma 2: positive supporting prices characterize fPO

The allocation is fPO if and only if there are numbers $\alpha_t\geq1$ and prices $p_g$ satisfying
$$ p_g\geq\alpha_ta_{tg} \quad\text{for every }t,g, $$
and
$$ p_g=\alpha_ta_{tg} \quad\text{whenever }z_{tg}>0. $$

Proof. For necessity, consider the rational linear program
$$ \max_{Z\geq0}\ \sum_{t,g}a_{tg}Z_{tg} $$
subject to
$$ \sum_tZ_{tg}=\sigma_g, \qquad \sum_ga_{tg}Z_{tg}\geq U_t. $$
It is feasible because $z$ is feasible, and bounded because $Z_{tg}\leq\sigma_g$. Under fPO, Lemma 1 implies that its optimum equals $\sum_tU_t$.

Its dual has unrestricted variables $p_g$ and nonnegative variables $\beta_t$, with constraints
$$ p_g\geq(1+\beta_t)a_{tg} $$
and objective
$$ \min\ \sum_gp_g\sigma_g-\sum_t\beta_tU_t. $$
Finite-dimensional linear-programming strong duality applies. Set $\alpha_t=1+\beta_t$ in an optimal dual solution. Equality of the primal and dual values gives
$$ \sum_gp_g\sigma_g=\sum_t\alpha_tU_t. $$
Consequently,
$$ \sum_{t,g}(p_g-\alpha_ta_{tg})z_{tg}=0. $$
Every summand is nonnegative, so equality holds on every allocated edge.

For sufficiency, any aggregate allocation $Z$ with the prescribed supplies satisfies
$$ \sum_t\alpha_t\sum_ga_{tg}Z_{tg} \leq\sum_gp_g\sigma_g =\sum_t\alpha_tU_t. $$
Because every $\alpha_t$ is positive, simultaneous weak improvement with one strict improvement is impossible. Apply Lemma 1. $\square$

2. A new characterization by fixed-quantity transportation

Define the high-value indicators and type quantities by
$$ h_{tg}= \begin{cases} 1,&a_{tg}=k,\\ 0,&a_{tg}=1, \end{cases} \qquad r_t=\sum_gz_{tg}. $$
Thus $a_{tg}=k^{h_{tg}}$.

Consider the transportation problem
$$ \max_{Z\geq0}\ \sum_{t,g}h_{tg}Z_{tg} $$
subject to
$$ \sum_gZ_{tg}=r_t, \qquad \sum_tZ_{tg}=\sigma_g. $$

Theorem 3: exact transportation characterization

A feasible allocation is recipient-level fPO if and only if its aggregate matrix $z$ is optimal for this transportation problem.

Moreover, whenever it is fPO, it admits a supporting-price certificate
$$ \alpha_t=k^{d_t}, \qquad p_g=k^{d_g}, $$
where every exponent is an integer satisfying
$$ 0\leq d_t,d_g\leq\tau+m-1. $$

Proof, fPO implies transportation optimality. Take the prices from Lemma 2 and define
$$ \lambda_t=\log_k\alpha_t, \qquad \pi_g=\log_kp_g. $$
These logarithms exist because the prices and multipliers are positive. Since $k>1$, the price inequalities imply
$$ h_{tg}\leq\pi_g-\lambda_t, $$
with equality wherever $z_{tg}>0$.

For any transportation-feasible matrix $Z$,
$$ \sum_{t,g}h_{tg}Z_{tg} \leq\sum_g\pi_g\sigma_g-\sum_t\lambda_tr_t =\sum_{t,g}h_{tg}z_{tg}. $$
Thus $z$ is optimal.

Proof, transportation optimality implies fPO. Construct a directed graph with one vertex for each type and each good class:

  • For every pair $(t,g)$, include an arc $t\to g$ of weight $h_{tg}$.
  • Whenever $z_{tg}>0$, include an arc $g\to t$ of weight $-h_{tg}$.

This graph has no positive-weight directed cycle. Otherwise, choose a positive simple directed cycle. Every reverse arc on it corresponds to a positive entry of $z$. For sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, add $\varepsilon$ on the forward edges of the cycle and subtract $\varepsilon$ on its reverse edges. This preserves every row sum and column sum and preserves nonnegativity. Its objective improvement equals $\varepsilon$ times the cycle weight, contradicting optimality.

Add a source with a zero-weight arc to every vertex. Let $d_u$ be the maximum weight of a directed walk from this source to $u$.

These maxima are finite and attained: removing a nonpositive cycle cannot decrease a walk’s weight, so a maximum can be attained by a simple path. With $N=\tau+m$ original vertices, such a path has at most $N-1$ original arcs. All arc weights are integers at most one, and the direct source arc has weight zero. Therefore
$$ d_u\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}. $$
For every arc $u\to v$ of weight $w$,
$$ d_v\geq d_u+w. $$
In particular,
$$ d_g\geq d_t+h_{tg}, $$
and, on every allocated edge,
$$ d_t\geq d_g-h_{tg}. $$
Hence equality holds on allocated edges.

Set
$$ \alpha_t=k^{d_t}, \qquad p_g=k^{d_g}. $$
Then
$$ p_g\geq\alpha_tk^{h_{tg}}=\alpha_ta_{tg}, $$
with equality whenever $z_{tg}>0$. Also $\alpha_t\geq1$. Lemma 2 proves fPO. $\square$

Consequences

First, fPO depends only on the allocated type–good edges, once supply feasibility holds. The masses and bundle composition affect feasibility and WEFX, but the above graph determines efficiency.

Second, fPO can be checked by detecting a positive directed cycle. This takes polynomial time using integer arc weights. If no such cycle exists, the longest-path labels above provide a certificate.

Third, the certificate has polynomial encoding length even when $k$ is binary encoded. If $k=a/b$ in lowest terms, every price is of the form
$$ \frac{a^d}{b^d}, \qquad d\leq\tau+m-1, $$
whose encoding length is bounded by a polynomial in $\tau,m,L$.

This characterization does not yet give a general allocation algorithm: the type quantities $r_t$ and a compatible WEFX bundle support still have to be chosen.

3. Polynomial-time computation when total supply is at most one

Let
$$ S=\sum_g\sigma_g. $$

Theorem 4

If $S\leq1$, a WEFX and recipient-level fPO allocation can be computed in polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$, for arbitrary valuation types and weights.

Proof. If $S=0$, give every type only the empty bundle. Assume $S>0$.

Choose type quantities
$$ r_t=\mu_tS. $$
Solve the transportation linear program from Theorem 3 with these row sums and the input column sums.

The program is feasible: the matrix
$$ Z^0_{tg}=\mu_t\sigma_g $$
has the required row and column sums. Its feasible region is bounded, since each entry is at most its column supply. Its dimensions and rational coefficient lengths are polynomial in the input size. The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore returns an optimal rational matrix $z$ of polynomial encoding length.

For each class $g$, let $e_g$ denote the bundle containing one copy of that class. Output
$$ x_{t,e_g}=z_{tg}, \qquad x_{t,0}=\mu_t(1-S), $$
omitting zero masses.

The type masses are correct because
$$ \sum_gz_{tg}+\mu_t(1-S)=\mu_t, $$
and the supplies are correct because $\sum_tz_{tg}=\sigma_g$.

Every represented bundle is empty or a singleton. Removing any good from a compared singleton leaves the empty bundle, so every required WEFX inequality has a zero right-hand side. Thus WEFX holds for all positive weights.

The aggregate matrix is transportation-optimal, so Theorem 3 proves recipient-level fPO. The output has at most $\tau(m+1)$ represented pairs and polynomial encoding length. $\square$

The supply restriction is substantive. The same fixed row sums cannot generally be used above this range.

For example, take one good class, two types, and
$$ \mu_1=\mu_2=\frac12, \qquad q_1=\frac12, \qquad q_2=\frac32, \qquad \sigma=2. $$
Give both types per-copy value one. The weights satisfy the required normalization.

The choice $r_t=\mu_tS$ forces each type’s mean bundle count to equal two. Finite support then gives a represented type-$1$ count $n_1\geq2$ and a represented type-$2$ count $n_2\leq2$. WEFX would require
$$ \frac{n_2}{q_2}\geq\frac{n_1-1}{q_1}, $$
but
$$ \frac{n_2}{q_2}\leq\frac43<2\leq\frac{n_1-1}{q_1}. $$
Thus no WEFX decomposition can have those row sums, although an allocation with different row sums exists.

4. Polynomial-time computation for identical valuation vectors

Assume now that
$$ v_t(g)=a_g\in\{1,k\} $$
for every type $t$. Weights and supplies remain arbitrary binary-encoded rationals.

Every feasible allocation is fPO in this case. Indeed, a recipient-level improvement would weakly increase every group’s common utility and strictly increase one positive-mass group’s utility. Summing these utilities with their masses would strictly increase
$$ \sum_ga_g\sigma_g, $$
which is fixed.

The task is therefore to construct WEFX bundles without enumerating copies.

4.1. A weighted filling lemma

Suppose there are finitely many groups indexed by $i$, with positive rational masses $\rho_i$, positive rational weights $q_i$, and nonnegative rational base values $b_i$. Assume
$$ \sum_i\rho_iq_i=1. $$
We wish to distribute a rational supply $C>0$ of identical unit-valued copies. A group may be split into subgroups receiving different integer counts $j$.

Lemma 5. In polynomial time, each original group can be split into at most two groups, with total distributed quantity $C$, so that some rational threshold $r$ satisfies
$$ \frac{b_i+j}{q_i}\geq r $$
for every output group, and
$$ \frac{b_i+j-1}{q_i}\leq r \quad\text{whenever }j>0. $$
The threshold also satisfies
$$ r\geq\min_i\frac{b_i}{q_i}. $$

Proof. Define
$$ F(r)=\sum_i\rho_i\max\{0,\lfloor rq_i-b_i\rfloor+1\}, $$
and
$$ A(r)=\sum_i\rho_i\max\{0,\lceil rq_i-b_i\rceil\}. $$
The nondecreasing step function $F$ has breakpoints
$$ \frac{b_i+j}{q_i}, \qquad j\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}. $$
At every breakpoint, its left limit is $A(r)$.

Set
$$ \ell=\min_i\frac{b_i}{q_i}-1, \qquad u=\max_i\frac{b_i}{q_i}+C. $$
Then $F(\ell)=0<C$. Also $uq_i-b_i\geq Cq_i>0$, so
$$ F(u) =u-\sum_i\rho_ib_i +\sum_i\rho_i\bigl(1-\{uq_i-b_i\}\bigr) >C. $$
Here the normalization gives the coefficient of $u$, and
$$ \sum_i\rho_ib_i \leq\max_i\frac{b_i}{q_i}\sum_i\rho_iq_i =\max_i\frac{b_i}{q_i}. $$

To locate a suitable breakpoint efficiently, write
$$ b_i=\frac{u_i}{v_i}, \qquad q_i=\frac{a_i}{d_i} $$
in lowest terms, and set
$$ H=\max_i v_i a_i. $$
Every breakpoint has reduced denominator at most $H$. Therefore two distinct breakpoints differ by at least $1/H^2$.

Bisect the interval, preserving
$$ F(\ell)<C\leq F(u), $$
until $u-\ell<1/H^2$. This requires polynomially many iterations: the starting interval and $H$ have polynomial binary encoding length.

There is exactly one distinct breakpoint in $(\ell,u]$. It is
$$ r^*= \min_i \frac{b_i+\max\{0,\lfloor\ell q_i-b_i\rfloor+1\}}{q_i}. $$
Consequently,
$$ A(r^*)=F(\ell)<C\leq F(r^*). $$

Put
$$ j_i=\max\{0,\lceil r^*q_i-b_i\rceil\}, $$
and let
$$ J=\{i:r^*q_i-b_i\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\}. $$
The jump size is
$$ F(r^*)-A(r^*)=\sum_{i\in J}\rho_i. $$
Choose rational numbers $d_i\in[0,\rho_i]$ for $i\in J$, summing to
$$ C-A(r^*), $$
by sequentially filling these intervals. Set $d_i=0$ outside $J$.

Give mass $\rho_i-d_i$ the count $j_i$, and mass $d_i$ the count $j_i+1$. Total consumption equals $C$.

For the lower count, the ceiling formula gives
$$ b_i+j_i\geq r^*q_i, $$
and, whenever $j_i>0$,
$$ b_i+j_i-1\leq r^*q_i. $$
An upper count occurs only when $r^*q_i-b_i=j_i$, so it satisfies the same two conclusions. The first possible breakpoint is $\min_i b_i/q_i$, proving the last assertion.

All floor evaluations, bisection endpoints, counts, and masses have polynomial encoding length. $\square$

4.2. Allocate high copies, then low copies

Let
$$ G_H=\{g:a_g=k\}, \qquad G_L=\{g:a_g=1\}, $$
and
$$ C_H=\sum_{g\in G_H}\sigma_g, \qquad C_L=\sum_{g\in G_L}\sigma_g. $$

If $C_H>0$, apply Lemma 5 to the original types, with $\rho_i=\mu_t$, $q_i=q_t$, $b_i=0$, and supply $C_H$. Denote the resulting integer high-copy counts by $h_i$ and threshold by $r_H$. Then
$$ \frac{h_i}{q_i}\geq r_H, \qquad \frac{h_i-1}{q_i}\leq r_H \quad\text{when }h_i>0. $$
If $C_H=0$, use $h_i=0$ and $r_H=0$.

If $C_L>0$, apply Lemma 5 again, now with bases
$$ b_i=kh_i $$
and supply $C_L$. Let $\ell_i$ be the resulting low-copy counts and $r_L$ its threshold.

Every resulting bundle has
$$ \frac{kh_i+\ell_i}{q_i}\geq r_L. $$
If $\ell_i>0$, removing a low copy gives
$$ \frac{kh_i+\ell_i-1}{q_i}\leq r_L. $$
Removing a high copy leaves no larger value, since $k>1$.

For a high-only bundle, the first phase gives
$$ \frac{k(h_i-1)}{q_i}\leq kr_H. $$
Moreover,
$$ r_L\geq\min_i\frac{kh_i}{q_i}\geq kr_H. $$
Thus removing any copy from a high-only bundle also leaves normalized value at most $r_L$.

All recipients consequently have normalized value at least $r_L$, while removing any good from any nonempty bundle leaves normalized value at most $r_L$. This proves every WEFX comparison.

If $C_L=0$, the first-phase inequalities directly prove WEFX with threshold $kr_H$.

There are at most $4\tau$ groups before recovering the individual class coordinates.

4.3. Recovering class-specific bundles

We must realize the prescribed supplies within the high and low pools while preserving each group’s integer counts. This can be done with polynomial support.

Consider one pool, whose group counts are $c_i$ and whose class supplies sum to
$$ \sum_i\rho_ic_i. $$
Lay out consecutive group blocks of lengths $\rho_ic_i$. Independently partition the same interval into consecutive class intervals of lengths $\sigma_g$.

Within group $i$, use a recipient coordinate $w\in(0,\rho_i)$. Its $c_i$ slots occupy the positions
$$ A_i+j\rho_i+w, \qquad j\in\{0,\ldots,c_i-1\}, $$
where $A_i$ is the block’s starting position. Label each slot by the class interval containing its position.

A class boundary inside this block has local coordinate
$$ c=j\rho_i+w_0, \qquad 0\leq w_0<\rho_i. $$
It changes the group’s class-count bundle only at $w=w_0$. Therefore each class boundary introduces at most one recipient-coordinate breakpoint in one group. Split groups at these breakpoints.

The class quantities are exact: as $w$ traverses its interval, each slot traverses one interval of length $\rho_i$, and these slot intervals partition the group block. Hence the mass-weighted number of slots assigned to a class equals that class interval’s length.

The construction does not enumerate slots. For a class’s local intersection $[a,b)$ with a block, and a midpoint $w$ of a refined recipient interval, its bundle coordinate is
$$ \operatorname{clip}_{c_i} \left(\left\lceil\frac{b-w}{\rho_i}\right\rceil\right) - \operatorname{clip}_{c_i} \left(\left\lceil\frac{a-w}{\rho_i}\right\rceil\right), $$
where
$$ \operatorname{clip}_{c}(z)=\min\{c,\max\{0,z\}\}. $$
This uses exact rational arithmetic with polynomial encoding length.

Apply this construction to both pools, taking the union of their recipient-coordinate breakpoints. Across both pools there are at most $m$ additional breakpoints. Thus the final support has at most $4\tau+m$ pairs. Refinement preserves every group’s utility and weight, so WEFX is preserved.

We have proved the following.

Theorem 6

When all valuation vectors are identical, an allocation can be computed in polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$, including binary-encoded $k$, weights, and supplies.

The same argument applies whenever the valuation vectors are positive scalar multiples of a common vector taking values in $\{1,k\}$: the observer’s scalar cancels from WEFX, and every feasible allocation remains fPO. In particular, it includes the one-good-class case even when different types assign different values to that class.

5. Universal existence and compact witnesses

These results concern every valid input, including instances not covered by the algorithms above.

5.1. Existence by exact replication

Choose a positive integer $D$ clearing all mass and supply denominators. Form a finite instance with $D\mu_t$ agents of type $t$ and $D\sigma_g$ copies of class $g$. Give every type-$t$ agent weight $q_t/D$. These weights are positive and normalized:
$$ \sum_tD\mu_t\frac{q_t}{D}=1. $$

The supplied finite-agent theorem applies to these common positive bivalued valuations and positive normalized weights. It produces a WEFX and fPO allocation. [Liu and Zhang, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08345v1).

If $c_{t,B}$ agents receive the class-count bundle $B$, set
$$ x_{t,B}=\frac{c_{t,B}}D. $$
The required masses and supplies follow immediately. Cancelling the common factor $D$ in the finite WEFX inequalities gives exactly the society’s inequalities. A comparison of a group with itself is automatic.

For fPO, suppose fractional bundles $y_{t,B}$ dominated this allocation. Assign that bundle to each of the corresponding finite agents. Their total class consumption is
$$ \sum_{t,B}c_{t,B}y_{t,B,g}=D\sigma_g. $$
For a class with $N_g=D\sigma_g>0$ copies, an agent consuming quantity $y_g$ can receive fraction $y_g/N_g$ of every copy. Each copy is fully allocated because total class consumption equals $N_g$. Zero-supply classes have zero consumption by nonnegativity.

This produces a fractional Pareto improvement of the finite allocation, a contradiction. Thus every valid instance has a solution.

Replication may be exponentially large, so this is an existence proof rather than a polynomial-time construction.

5.2. Polynomial bounds on bundle coordinates

Let $B'$ be a represented nonempty bundle of type $s$, and remove one copy of any class present in it. For every represented pair $(t,B)$, WEFX implies
$$ v_t(B) \geq\frac{q_t}{q_s}\bigl(|B'|-1\bigr), $$
because every remaining copy is worth at least one.

Multiply by $x_{t,B}$ and sum. Using weight normalization and supply feasibility gives
$$ \frac{|B'|-1}{q_s} \leq\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}v_t(B) \leq kS. $$
Hence
$$ |B'|\leq1+kq_sS. $$
Every bundle coordinate is therefore bounded by
$$ N=\left\lceil1+kq_{\max}S\right\rceil. $$
Its binary encoding length is polynomial in the input length.

5.3. Support reduction

Associate each represented pair with the integer column
$$ c_{t,B}=(e_t,B)\in\mathbb Z^{\tau+m}. $$
Feasibility says
$$ \sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}c_{t,B}=(\mu,\sigma). $$

If these columns are dependent, choose a nonzero rational dependence $d$. Its type coordinates imply
$$ \sum_{t,B}d_{t,B}=0, $$
so it has both positive and negative entries. Set
$$ \theta=\min_{d_{t,B}>0}\frac{x_{t,B}}{d_{t,B}}, \qquad x'_{t,B}=x_{t,B}-\theta d_{t,B}. $$
This preserves feasibility and nonnegativity and removes at least one represented pair.

No new bundle is introduced, so WEFX is preserved. No new allocated type–good edge is introduced either, so the same supporting prices from Lemma 2 certify fPO.

Repeating leaves linearly independent columns, hence at most $\tau+m$ represented pairs.

5.4. Explicit encoding bound for the remaining masses

Let the remaining support size be $r\leq\tau+m$. Select $r$ rows forming a nonsingular integer matrix $C$. The masses satisfy
$$ Cx=b, $$
where $b$ consists of selected input masses and supplies.

Put $N_0=\max\{1,N\}$. Every entry of $C$ has absolute value at most $N_0$, so
$$ |\det C|\leq(\sqrt r\,N_0)^r. $$

Let $D_b$ be the product of the denominators of the selected entries of $b$, and define
$$ \widetilde b=D_bb, \qquad R=\max_i|\widetilde b_i|, \qquad K=\max\{N_0,R\}. $$
Both $D_b$ and $R$ have polynomial encoding length: clearing these denominators multiplies only polynomially many input integers whose total encoding length is bounded by the input length.

Let $C_j(\widetilde b)$ replace column $j$ of $C$ by $\widetilde b$. Its entries are bounded by $K$, so
$$ |\det C_j(\widetilde b)|\leq(\sqrt r\,K)^r. $$
Cramer’s rule gives
$$ x_j=\frac{\det C_j(\widetilde b)}{D_b\det C}. $$
Both numerator and denominator consequently have polynomial encoding length. This explicitly accounts for the replacement columns missing from the previous round’s determinant estimate.

Thus every valid instance has a solution with polynomial total encoding length and at most $\tau+m$ represented pairs.

5.5. Polynomial verification

For a proposed polynomial-length allocation:

  • Feasibility is checked by exact rational arithmetic.
  • WEFX is checked for all represented pairs and classes present in the compared bundle.
  • fPO is checked by the positive-cycle criterion of Theorem 3.
