The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(s+1\) populations, each of total mass \(1\), with finite supports of rational-mass preference types, where side \(0\)'s types are partitioned into the \(m\) classes of an input Latin rectangle and type-level preferences satisfy the paper's three compatibility conditions, find a joint mass assignment \(x_q \ge 0\) over feasible type-tuples \(q\). Require each type's marginal to equal its input mass, and require that no type-tuple \(a\) has positive mass \(b_j(a;x)=\sum_{q:q_j=a_j,\ a\succ_{a_j}q}x_q\) of agents on every side who prefer \(a\) to their assigned tuple.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(s\geq1\). A society is given by its support: disjoint lists \(T_0,\ldots,T_s\) of types with positive binary-rational masses \(w_t\), with \(\sum_{t\in T_j}w_t=1\) for every side. Write \(\tau=\sum_{j=0}^s|T_j|\); this is the support size, not the number of possible preference types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The input includes a Latin rectangle \(R=(r_{ik})\) with \(1\leq m\leq s\), each row a permutation of \(\{1,\ldots,s\}\), and distinct entries in each column, together with a partition \(T_0=C_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_m\) into nonempty classes.
All tuples in \(Q=\prod_{j=0}^sT_j\) are feasible. Each type \(t\in T_j\) has an explicitly listed strict total order \(\succ_t\) on tuples containing it; individuals of the same type share this order and distinguish partners only by type.
Compatibility means the following three conditions. First, each \(t\in C_i\) has supplied strict orders on each \(T_{r_{ik}}\), and its tuple order is lexicographic in the coordinate sequence \(r_{i1},\ldots,r_{is}\).
Second, each \(t\in T_j\), \(j\geq1\), has a supplied strict order \(\succ_t^\star\) on \(T_0\): whenever \(u\succ_t^\star v\), every tuple containing \(t\) with coordinate \(u\) on side zero is preferred to every such tuple with coordinate \(v\). Rankings within a fixed side-zero type are otherwise arbitrary strict orders.
Third, define \(p_i(j)\) by \(r_{i,p_i(j)}=j\). If \(p_i(j)<p_h(j)\), then every \(t\in T_j\) ranks every member of \(C_i\) above every member of \(C_h\) under \(\succ_t^\star\).
A mass assignment has \(x_q\geq0\) for \(q\in Q\), with \(\sum_{q:q_j=t}x_q=w_t\) for every side and type. It is stable if, for every \(a\in Q\), at least one \(j\in\{0,\ldots,s\}\) satisfies \(b_j(a;x)=0\), where \(b_j(a;x)=\sum_{q\in Q:q_j=a_j,\ a\succ_{a_j}q}x_q\).
Is finding such a stable mass assignment (or determining that none exists) solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,s,L\), or is it NP-hard?
For finite societies with equally many agents on each side and these compatibility conditions, a stable matching always exists and is computable in polynomial time. [Anholcer](https://ord.pwr.edu.pl/assets/papers_archive/ord2027vol37no1_3.pdf)
For graph-based fractional matching with unit vertex capacities, ordinal stability permits polynomial-time maximization of the number of fully matched vertices under strict preferences; this concerns pairs rather than the tuples and arbitrary type masses above. [Chen, Roy and Sorge](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0013.pdf)
No complexity classification for the exact continuous problem above was located in the literature search.

$$ \sum_{f\in E:\,v\in f}y_f\leq1 \qquad\text{for every vertex }v. $$
It is stable if, for every $e\in E$, some $v\in e$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{\substack{f\in E:\,v\in f\\f\succeq_v e}}y_f=1. $$

Scarf’s lemma and computational bound. Suppose $B=[I_h\mid B']$ has more than $h$ columns, $b\geq0$, and $\{\alpha\geq0:B\alpha=b\}$ is bounded. Suppose an ordinal matrix $C$ satisfies
$$ C_{ii}\leq C_{ik}\leq C_{ij} \qquad (i,j\leq h,\ i\neq j,\ k>h). $$
There is a set $J$ of $h$ columns supporting a feasible vector such that every column $k$ has a row $i$ with
$$ C_{ik}\leq C_{i\ell}\qquad(\ell\in J). $$

Applying the lemma

Set
$$ B=[I_\tau\mid A],\qquad b=w. $$

$$ z_t+\sum_{q:\,t\in q}x_q=w_t. $$
The feasible set is nonempty, since $(z,x)=(w,0)$ is feasible. It is bounded: each $z_t\leq w_t$, and each $x_q$ is bounded by every incident type’s mass.

  • score $N+2$ for other identity columns;
  • score $\rho_t(q)$ for an incident tuple column;
  • score $N+1$ for a nonincident tuple column.

These entries satisfy Scarf’s inequalities, and all have polynomial encoding length.

Let $J$ and a supported feasible vector $(z,x)$ satisfy the conclusion. Fix a tuple $a$, and choose its domination row $t$.

Because $w_t>0$, some positive variable contributes to row $t$. Its column belongs to $J$ and has ordinal score at most $N$. If $a$ did not contain $t$, its score would be $N+1$, contradicting domination. Hence $t$ is a coordinate of $a$.

Moreover, $z_t=0$: otherwise its score-zero identity column would contradict the positive score of $a$. Every positive incident tuple $q$ satisfies
$$ \rho_t(q)\geq\rho_t(a). $$
Therefore no positive mass at $t$ lies on a tuple strictly worse than $a$.

$$ M=\sum_{q\in Q}x_q. $$

$$ \sum_{t\in T_j}z_t=1-M. $$

The preceding domination argument gives a coordinate type of $a$ whose unused mass is zero, contradicting the choice of all its coordinates.

Thus $M=1$. Each side’s nonnegative unused masses sum to zero, so every $z_t=0$. The resulting $x$ has the required marginals. The domination argument proves that every tuple has a coordinate with blocking quantity zero.

This proves existence for every valid input, without needing compatibility.
