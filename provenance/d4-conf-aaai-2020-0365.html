The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), compact rational action intervals \(D_t\), and rational polynomial unary and pairwise costs, determine the complexity of producing rational finite-support action distributions \(\pi_t\) whose per-capita cost is within a supplied rational \(\varepsilon>0\) of the global minimum, where \(J(\pi)=\sum_t\mu_t\int u_t(x)\,d\pi_t(x)+\sum_{t,s}\mu_t w_{ts}\int\!\int f_{ts}(x,y)\,d\pi_t(x)\,d\pi_s(y)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type controls one scalar action, so \(m=\tau\), with domain \(D_t=[a_t,b_t]\), where \(a_t,b_t\in\mathbb Q\) and \(a_t\leq b_t\). Inputs include nonnegative rational interaction weights \(w_{ts}\) and unary and pairwise polynomials \(u_t,f_{ts}\) with rational coefficients of either sign, given by explicit dense coefficient arrays; let \(d\) be their maximum degree and \(L\) the total input bit length, including a binary-rational \(\varepsilon>0\).
A feasible choice assigns each type a Borel probability measure \(\pi_t\) on \(D_t\). Define \(J(\pi)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\int u_t(x)\,d\pi_t(x)+\sum_{t,s\in T}\mu_t w_{ts}\iint f_{ts}(x,y)\,d\pi_t(x)\,d\pi_s(y)\). The pair sum is ordered, with no symmetry assumption; even when \(t=s\), the two actions are independent draws.
The computational problem is to produce measures with finite rational action supports and nonnegative rational probabilities summing to \(1\), satisfying \(J(\pi)\leq J^*+\varepsilon\), where \(J^*=\min_{\rho_t\in\mathcal P(D_t)}J(\rho)\).

Is this additive approximation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,d,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite-agent F-DCOPs, PFD has an anytime guarantee: its incumbent objective value never increases; this does not establish polynomial-time global approximation ([source](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/6198/6198-13-9423-1-10-20200516.pdf)).

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\), even with \(d=2\), all domains equal to \([0,1]\), equal dyadic masses, zero unary terms, zero self-interactions, and the fixed tolerance \(\varepsilon=1/4\). Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm with the stated guarantee would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Construction from maximum cut

We use the known \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness of the following decision problem: given a simple unweighted undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\) and an integer \(K\), determine whether some cut has at least \(K\) edges. This is Simple Max Cut in [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(76)90059-1).

Let \(V=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), with \(n\geq1\), and write \(M(G)\) for the maximum cut size. Choose

\( N=2^{\lceil\log_2 n\rceil}. \)

Add isolated vertices until there are \(N\) vertices. This preserves \(M(G)\).

Construct a society with \(m=\tau=N\), setting, for every type \(i\),

\( \mu_i=1/N,\qquad D_i=[0,1],\qquad u_i(x)=0. \)

For every ordered pair, set

\( f_{ij}(x,y)=2xy-x-y. \)

Define the interaction weights by

\( w_{ij}= \begin{cases} N,& i<j\text{ and }\{i,j\}\in E,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \)

Finally, set \(\varepsilon=1/4\).

These are valid inputs. In particular, the masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one; the weights are nonnegative integers; and each edge contributes exactly once to the ordered pair sum. All self-interaction weights vanish.

The construction has polynomial encoding length: \(N\leq2n\), there are \(N^2\) ordered pairs, each polynomial has a constant-size dense coefficient array, and each nonzero weight uses \(O(\log n)\) bits.

2. The optimum over measures equals negative maximum cut

Take any feasible family of Borel probability measures \(\pi_i\). Define its means by

\( p_i=\int_{[0,1]}x\,d\pi_i(x)\in[0,1]. \)

For an interacting pair, integration against the product measure gives

\( \iint(2xy-x-y)\,d\pi_i(x)\,d\pi_j(y) =2p_ip_j-p_i-p_j. \)

Since \(\mu_iw_{ij}=1\) on each selected edge, the objective is therefore

\( J(\pi) =-\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}(p_i+p_j-2p_ip_j). \)

To interpret this sum, let \(B_1,\ldots,B_N\) be independent Bernoulli random variables with \(\Pr(B_i=1)=p_i\). Each realization defines a cut. For every edge,

\( \Pr(B_i\neq B_j) =p_i(1-p_j)+(1-p_i)p_j =p_i+p_j-2p_ip_j. \)

Consequently,

\( -J(\pi) =\mathbb E[\text{number of edges crossing the random cut}] \leq M(G). \)

The inequality holds because every realized cut has at most \(M(G)\) crossing edges. Thus every feasible family satisfies \(J(\pi)\geq-M(G)\).

Conversely, choose a maximum cut and let \(b_i\in\{0,1\}\) encode its sides. The feasible measures \(\pi_i=\delta_{b_i}\) give \(J(\pi)=-M(G)\). These measures already have rational action supports and rational probabilities. Hence the exact optimum is

\( J^*=-M(G). \)

This proves the equality for the full feasible set of measures in the constructed instance, not merely for measures supported at the endpoints.

3. Recovering the exact cut value from an approximate output

Suppose an algorithm produces the required finite rational measures in polynomial time. Applied to the constructed instance, its output satisfies

\( -M(G)\leq J(\pi)\leq-M(G)+1/4. \)

Equivalently,

\( M(G)-1/4\leq-J(\pi)\leq M(G). \)

Because \(M(G)\) is an integer, it follows that

\( M(G)=\lceil-J(\pi)\rceil. \)

This value can be computed in polynomial time from the output. Explicitly, if

\( \pi_i=\sum_{r=1}^{k_i}q_{ir}\delta_{x_{ir}}, \)

then \(p_i=\sum_r q_{ir}x_{ir}\), and the formula above evaluates \(J(\pi)\) using rational arithmetic. A polynomial-time algorithm has polynomial output length; the rational sums and products used here also have polynomial bit length. Thus exact evaluation and taking the ceiling require polynomial time.

Comparing the recovered \(M(G)\) with \(K\) decides Simple Max Cut. This establishes the claimed \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

4. Bounded weights and the anytime guarantee

The hardness also persists if all weights are restricted to \(\{0,1\}\), provided the tolerance remains part of the input. Replace each nonzero weight \(N\) by \(1\) and set \(\varepsilon=1/(4N)\). The resulting objective is the preceding objective divided by \(N\), so its optimum is \(-M(G)/N\), and an approximate output again yields

\( M(G)=\lceil-NJ(\pi)\rceil. \)

This tolerance is dyadic and has \(O(\log n)\) encoding length.

The cited PFD paper proves monotonic improvement of its incumbent in Proposition 1. That statement supplies no polynomial bound on the time needed to achieve a prescribed global additive error, so it is compatible with the hardness proved here. [PFD paper](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/6198/6198-13-9423-1-10-20200516.pdf)
