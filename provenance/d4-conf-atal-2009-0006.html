The anchor (named result in the paper)

Proposition 6.4 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), positive rational per-capita weights \(a_t\) normalized by \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_ta_t=1\), a quota fraction \(q\in(1/2,1)\), and a cohort \(S\subset T\) with \(0<A_S<1\), where \(A_S=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_ta_t\), consider replication games with \(N\mu_t\) voters of type \(t\), each of weight \(a_t/N\). Determine whether, in the limit along replication sizes with integral \(N\mu_t\), the Shapley–Shubik index of the bloc formed by merging every voter in \(S\) exceeds the pre-merge sum of Shapley–Shubik indices of those voters. The pre-merge limit is \(\alpha_S=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\); the post-merge limit is \(\bigl(\min(q,1-A_S)-\max(0,q-A_S)\bigr)/(1-A_S)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a positive binary-rational per-capita weight \(a_t\), with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_ta_t=1\).
The input also contains a binary-rational quota \(q\in(1/2,1)\) and a specified cohort \(S\subset T\), with \(0<A_S<1\), where \(A_S=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_ta_t\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; running times are measured in \(\tau\) and \(L\), with no candidate parameter \(m\) in this model.
For each positive integer \(N\) satisfying \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\) for every type, let \(G_N\) be the weighted voting game with \(N\mu_t\) voters of type \(t\), each of weight \(a_t/N\), and quota \(q\); meeting the quota is winning.
Let \(C_N\) contain every voter whose type belongs to \(S\). Form \(G_N^S\) by replacing \(C_N\) with one bloc \(b\) of weight \(A_S\), retaining all other voters and the quota. Write \(\phi_i(G)\) for the standard Shapley–Shubik index.
Continuous beneficial merging asks whether \(\lim_{N\to\infty}\phi_b(G_N^S)>\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{i\in C_N}\phi_i(G_N)\), taking both limits along the admissible replication sizes; equality is a negative instance.

Is continuous beneficial merging solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite weighted voting games, beneficial Shapley–Shubik merging is [\(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.3869), and subsequently was shown [\(\mathrm{PP}\)-complete even for two equal-weight merging players](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.1691). [Shapiro–Shapley](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.3.1.1) establish limiting values for fixed players when other voting weight is fragmented into vanishing pieces; this is a limit theorem rather than a complexity classification of the support-encoded decision problem.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The quota's encoding was unspecified; require a binary rational.
  • Make merging explicit: replace all cohort voters by one player with their total weight, keeping the quota and all outside voters unchanged.
  • Interpret strict improvement as comparison of the limiting indices, with equality rejected, rather than eventual strict improvement at finite replication sizes.
  • The graded pre-merge population-share formula is incompatible with unequal per-capita weights; define the comparison through the actual Shapley–Shubik replication limits instead of assuming the supplied formulas.
  • Use support size and total input bit length as the runtime parameters; this voting-game model has no candidate parameter.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result

Continuous beneficial merging is solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\). More precisely, it is beneficial exactly when
\[ q<1-A_S+A_S^2. \]

We prove both limits and then give an exact arithmetic algorithm. Throughout, write \(A=A_S\) and \(B=1-A\); by hypothesis, \(A,B>0\). All limits below are along admissible replication sizes.

2. The cohort’s power before merging

For a weighted voting game, the Shapley–Shubik index of a voter is the probability that this voter is pivotal in a uniformly random permutation: the preceding voters are losing, and adding this voter makes them winning.

We use the following established theorem, in its normalized form.

Neyman’s approximation theorem. For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(K_\varepsilon>0\) such that a finite weighted voting game with nonnegative weights \(w_i\), total weight \(1\), and quota \(r\) satisfies
\[ \sum_i|\phi_i-w_i|<\varepsilon \]
whenever
\[ K_\varepsilon\max_i w_i\le r\le1-K_\varepsilon\max_i w_i. \]
The winning convention is that meeting the quota suffices. This statement appears as Proposition 3 in [Neyman and Smorodinsky, *Asymptotic Values of Vector Measure Games*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5101152_Asymptotic_Values_of_Vector_Measure_Games).

In \(G_N\), the total weight is exactly
\[ \sum_{t\in T}(N\mu_t)\frac{a_t}{N} =\sum_{t\in T}\mu_ta_t=1. \]
Moreover,
\[ \max_i w_i=\frac{\max_{t\in T}a_t}{N}\longrightarrow0. \]
For each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), the theorem’s quota inequalities therefore hold for all sufficiently large admissible \(N\), because \(q\) and \(1-q\) are positive constants. Consequently,
\[ \sum_i|\phi_i(G_N)-w_i|\longrightarrow0. \]

The total weight of \(C_N\) is exactly \(A\). Hence the triangle inequality gives
\[ \left|\sum_{i\in C_N}\phi_i(G_N)-A\right| \le\sum_i|\phi_i(G_N)-w_i|\longrightarrow0. \]
Thus
\[ \boxed{\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{i\in C_N}\phi_i(G_N)=A.} \]

This is a weight share; unequal per-capita weights need not give the cohort’s population share.

3. The bloc’s power after merging

Assign every player in \(G_N^S\) an independent uniform arrival time in \([0,1]\). Ordering by these times generates a uniformly random permutation, since ties have probability zero and every ordering is equally likely.

Let \(U\) be the bloc’s arrival time. Conditional on \(U=u\), each outside voter independently precedes the bloc with probability \(u\). Let \(V_N(u)\) denote their total preceding weight. The bloc is pivotal precisely when
\[ q-A\le V_N(u)<q. \]
Therefore
\[ \phi_b(G_N^S) =\int_0^1\Pr\bigl(q-A\le V_N(u)<q\bigr)\,du. \]

The expectation and variance are
\[ \mathbb E[V_N(u)]=uB \]
and
\[ \operatorname{Var}(V_N(u)) =u(1-u)\sum_{t\notin S}(N\mu_t)\left(\frac{a_t}{N}\right)^2 =\frac{u(1-u)}{N}\sum_{t\notin S}\mu_ta_t^2. \]
The final sum is finite for the fixed input. Chebyshev’s inequality consequently implies that \(V_N(u)\) converges in probability to \(uB\).

Except at the at most two values of \(u\) satisfying \(uB=q-A\) or \(uB=q\), this yields
\[ \Pr\bigl(q-A\le V_N(u)<q\bigr) \longrightarrow \mathbf 1_{\{q-A<uB<q\}}. \]
Indeed, away from those boundaries, sufficiently small deviations from \(uB\) preserve membership or nonmembership in the interval.

The integrands are bounded by \(1\). Dominated convergence therefore gives
\[ \lim_{N\to\infty}\phi_b(G_N^S) =\operatorname{length}\{u\in[0,1]:q-A<uB<q\}. \]
The interval has positive length: its upper endpoint before truncation is positive, its lower endpoint is less than \(1\) because \(q<1\), and their difference is \(A/B>0\). Thus the limit equals
\[ F_q(A)= \min\left\{1,\frac{q}{1-A}\right\} -\max\left\{0,\frac{q-A}{1-A}\right\}. \]

Since \(q>1/2\), the thresholds satisfy \(1-q<q\). Evaluating the truncations gives
\[ F_q(A)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{A}{1-A},&0<A\le1-q,\\[4pt] \dfrac{1-q}{1-A},&1-q<A<q,\\[4pt] 1,&q\le A<1. \end{cases} \]

4. Exact beneficial-merging criterion

By Sections 2–3, merging is beneficial exactly when \(F_q(A)>A\).

Case 1: \(0<A\le1-q\). Here
\[ F_q(A)-A=\frac{A^2}{1-A}>0. \]
Also,
\[ q\le1-A<1-A+A^2, \]
so the claimed criterion holds.

Case 2: \(1-q<A<q\). Here
\[ F_q(A)-A =\frac{1-q-A(1-A)}{1-A}. \]
The denominator is positive. Therefore
\[ F_q(A)>A \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q<1-A+A^2. \]

Case 3: \(q\le A<1\). Here \(F_q(A)-A=1-A>0\). Moreover,
\[ 1-A+A^2-A=(1-A)^2>0, \]
so again \(q\le A<1-A+A^2\).

These cases exhaust the hypotheses, proving
\[ \boxed{\text{Merging is beneficial}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q<1-A_S+A_S^2.} \]

Equality is handled exactly. If \(q=1-A+A^2\), then
\[ q-(1-A)=A^2>0,\qquad q-A=(1-A)^2>0. \]
Hence this equality lies in Case 2, where \(F_q(A)=A\), and is a negative instance.

5. Polynomial-time algorithm

Compute \(A_S=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_ta_t\) and test the proved strict inequality using exact rational arithmetic.

For an explicit bit bound, let \(D\) be the product of all input denominators occurring in the masses, per-capita weights, and quota. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Each denominator of a product \(\mu_ta_t\) divides \(D\), so one can compute integers \(P,Q\) with
\[ A_S=\frac{P}{D},\qquad q=\frac{Q}{D}. \]
The decision becomes the integer comparison
\[ QD<D^2-PD+P^2. \]

Because \(0<P<D\) and \(0<Q<D\), all integers in this comparison have \(O(L)\) bits. Constructing \(D,P,Q\) requires \(O(\tau)\) arithmetic operations on integers of polynomial bit length. Standard integer arithmetic therefore gives polynomial running time in \(\tau,L\).

The algorithm never constructs a replicated game or computes a finite-game power index.
