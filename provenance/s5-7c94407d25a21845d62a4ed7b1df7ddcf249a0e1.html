The continuous mirror question, as graded

For each scale \(N=q^4\), fix an atomless society with speed masses \(\mu_1=\mu_\alpha=1/2\), speeds \(\{1,\alpha\}\), and \(\alpha=\sqrt{N}/4\). Per unit of population, provide \(s_{\mathrm{long}}=N^{-1/2}\) jobs of length \(\alpha\) and \(s_{\mathrm{short}}=1/2+N^{-1/4}\) jobs of length \(1\). A feasible randomized oracle takes the aggregate report measure and a tagged infinitesimal agent's report, and returns a distribution over measurable schedules; each agent receives at most one integral job, and every realized schedule respects the aggregate supplies. Given oracle access to any such algorithm \(A\) with expected makespan \(O(1)\), does a uniform transformation using \(\operatorname{poly}(L,\tau,|J|)\) queries exist that returns only queried schedule kernels and produces a BIC mechanism with expected makespan at most \(N^{o(1)}\phi(A)\)? The tagged agent's deviation leaves \(\mu\) unchanged, and BIC is defined by its expected payment-minus-processing-time utility.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by the support list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, with masses encoded as binary rationals; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For each integer \(q\geq 3\), set \(N=q^4\) and \(\alpha=q^2/4\). The atomless population has total mass \(1\), speeds \(\{1,\alpha\}\), and truthful masses \(\mu_1=\mu_\alpha=1/2\). There are \(m=|J|=2\) job types: length \(\alpha\) with supply mass \(N^{-1/2}\), and length \(1\) with supply mass \(1/2+N^{-1/4}\).
Each agent receives either no job or one whole job. Every realized schedule must assign exactly both supply masses; jobs cannot be discarded or divided between agents.
An aggregate report is \(\nu=(1-h,h)\), with rational \(h\in[0,1]\); individual reports belong to \(\{1,\alpha\}\). Represent agents by report fibres \(\{1,\alpha\}\times[0,1]\), carrying measure \(\nu\) times Lebesgue measure. A schedule is a measurable function \(x(r,u)\in\{0,1,\alpha\}\).
An oracle query returns a replayable joint probability kernel over schedules feasible for its queried report measure, together with its tagged-agent marginals. These marginals depend only on the tagged report, agree with the schedule marginals almost everywhere within each positive-mass report fibre, and are also defined for reports of zero aggregate mass.
The transformation may adaptively query any such report measure and randomize over returned kernels, but must replay a selected kernel intact on the actual report fibres, without relabelling agents, editing allocations, or conditioning its internal randomness. Its resulting schedules must be feasible at the actual report measure. Kernels are oracle objects; the resource bound counts queries, with polynomial-length rational query inputs.
For truthful speeds \(v\), define \(\phi(M)=\mathbb E[\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{a}x(a)/v(a)]\), with expectation over all mechanism randomness. Payments are unrestricted finite real transfers to agents. Writing \(X_r,P_r\) for the tagged agent's resulting job length and payment, BIC requires \(\mathbb E[P_v-X_v/v]\geq\mathbb E[P_r-X_r/v]\) for every \(v,r\in\{1,\alpha\}\) at \(\mu\); a tagged deviation leaves \(\mu\) unchanged.
Does a uniform black-box transformation exist which, for every feasible oracle family \(A\) with \(\phi(A)=O(1)\), uses \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,|J|,L)\) queries and produces a feasible BIC mechanism satisfying \(\phi(T(A))\leq N^{o(1)}\phi(A)\) uniformly as \(N\to\infty\), or is this guarantee impossible?
For finite populations, every polynomial-time black-box BIC transformation has some instance and oracle with expected makespan \(O(1)\) whose transformed expected makespan is \(\Omega(n^{1/4})\). [Chawla–Immorlica–Lucier](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~shuchi/papers/black-box.pdf)

  • Normalized population mass to 1 and interpreted job quantities as masses of whole-job assignments, rather than fractional processing of individual jobs.
  • Defined makespan by essential supremum and took expectations over oracle and transformation randomness at the fixed truthful society.
  • Allowed oracle queries at all binary-rational aggregate report measures over the two speeds, including boundary measures, and defined tagged responses for absent report types.
  • Resolved aggregate-only access by using anonymous report fibres with coherent tagged marginals, excluding exceptional null-agent treatment.

  • Counted m and |J| as the two distinct job types, and included the binary encoding of q, speeds, supplies, and masses in L.

Conclusion and interpretation

Under the standard interpretation of BIC, in which the displayed expected utilities are finite real numbers, the guarantee is impossible. In fact, for every \(q\geq 5\), there is an admissible oracle family with \(\phi(A)=1\) for which every feasible BIC transformation using intact kernel replay has makespan

\(\displaystyle \phi(T(A))=\alpha=\frac{N^{1/2}}4.\)

This holds even with unlimited queries.

I use the finite-expected-utility interpretation below. If finite transfers are required only almost surely and infinite expected utilities are admitted, the literal inequalities instead allow a trivial positive result; this distinction is addressed at the end.

1. Constructing homogeneous schedule kernels

Write the long-job and short-job supply masses as

\(\displaystyle \ell=\frac1{q^2},\qquad s=\frac12+\frac1q.\)

For each report \(r\), choose nonnegative fractions \(a_r,b_r\) with \(a_r+b_r\leq 1\). Draw independent uniform random variables \(U_1,U_\alpha\) on \([0,1)\), and define \(t_r(u)=(u+U_r)\bmod 1\). Assign

\(\displaystyle x(r,u)= \begin{cases} \alpha,&0\leq t_r(u)<a_r,\\ 1,&a_r\leq t_r(u)<a_r+b_r,\\ 0,&a_r+b_r\leq t_r(u)<1. \end{cases}\)

This is a measurable random schedule. Rotation preserves Lebesgue measure, so every realization assigns fractions \(a_r\) and \(b_r\) of fibre \(r\) to long and short jobs, respectively.

For every fixed tagged coordinate \(u\), \(t_r(u)\) is uniform. Thus the tagged probabilities are exactly \(a_r,b_r,1-a_r-b_r\), independently of \(u\). These marginals agree with the schedule marginals throughout every positive-mass fibre and remain defined when a fibre has zero aggregate mass.

2. Two kernels

Define the good kernel \(G\) by

\(\displaystyle a_1=0,\quad b_1=1,\qquad a_\alpha=\frac2{q^2},\quad b_\alpha=\frac2q.\)

The fast-fibre fractions are admissible because, for \(q\geq 3\),

\(\displaystyle \frac2{q^2}+\frac2q\leq\frac89<1.\)

At the truthful measure \(\mu=(1/2,1/2)\), every realization assigns long-job mass

\(\displaystyle \frac12a_1+\frac12a_\alpha=\frac1{q^2}=\ell\)

and short-job mass

\(\displaystyle \frac12b_1+\frac12b_\alpha=\frac12+\frac1q=s.\)

Every slow agent receives a short job, and every long job goes to a fast agent. Consequently every realized makespan is \(1\), so

\(\displaystyle \phi(G)=1.\)

The expected tagged job lengths are

\(\displaystyle w_1^G=1,\qquad w_\alpha^G=\alpha\frac2{q^2}+\frac2q=\frac12+\frac2q.\)

Next define the fallback kernel \(B\) by identical fractions in both report fibres:

\(\displaystyle a_1=a_\alpha=\ell,\qquad b_1=b_\alpha=s.\)

These fractions are admissible because

\(\displaystyle \ell+s=\frac12+\frac1q+\frac1{q^2}\leq\frac{17}{18}<1.\)

For every aggregate report \(\nu=(1-h,h)\), each realization assigns long-job mass

\(\displaystyle (1-h)\ell+h\ell=\ell\)

and short-job mass \((1-h)s+hs=s\). Hence \(B\) is feasible at every queried measure and remains feasible when replayed at \(\mu\).

Both tagged reports have the same expected job length:

\(\displaystyle w_1^B=w_\alpha^B=\alpha\ell+s=\frac34+\frac1q.\)

At \(\mu\), every realization assigns long jobs to a positive mass \(\ell/2\) of slow agents. Their processing time is \(\alpha\), and no processing time exceeds \(\alpha\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \phi(B)=\alpha.\)

The kernel \(B\), with zero payments, is BIC: changing one's report leaves the expected assigned length unchanged.

3. The oracle family

For every rational \(h\in[0,1]\), let the oracle return

\(\displaystyle A(h)= \begin{cases} G,&h=1/2,\\ B,&h\neq 1/2. \end{cases}\)

The preceding calculations verify feasibility at every queried measure, including the endpoints. They also verify all required tagged-marginal properties.

At the truthful measure, \(A\) returns \(G\), so \(\phi(A)=1\).

There is no regularity requirement on the dependence of the oracle on \(h\), so this family is admissible.

4. BIC forces exclusive use of the fallback kernel

First derive the necessary BIC condition. Let \(w_r=\mathbb E[X_r]\) and \(p_r=\mathbb E[P_r]\). The two incentive inequalities imply

\(\displaystyle p_\alpha-p_1\leq w_\alpha-w_1,\qquad p_\alpha-p_1\geq\frac{w_\alpha-w_1}{\alpha}.\)

Since \(\alpha>1\), these inequalities can hold only if

\(\displaystyle w_\alpha\geq w_1.\)

Thus faster agents must receive at least as much expected job length.

Now consider any transformation permitted by the problem. Every returned kernel is either \(G\) or \(B\). Adaptive queries cannot enlarge this set. Because the transformation must replay a selected kernel intact and cannot condition its internal randomness, its resulting kernel at \(\mu\) is a mixture of \(G\) and \(B\).

Let \(\lambda\) be its probability of selecting \(G\), including all randomness used in queries and selection. The tagged-deviation convention leaves \(\mu\) unchanged, so the resulting tagged marginals are those of this same mixture. Hence

\(\displaystyle w_1=\lambda+(1-\lambda)\left(\frac34+\frac1q\right)\)

and

\(\displaystyle w_\alpha=\lambda\left(\frac12+\frac2q\right) +(1-\lambda)\left(\frac34+\frac1q\right).\)

Subtracting gives

\(\displaystyle w_\alpha-w_1=\lambda\left(\frac2q-\frac12\right).\)

For \(q\geq 5\), the parenthesized quantity is strictly negative. The necessary BIC condition therefore forces \(\lambda=0\).

Every feasible BIC transformation must consequently replay \(B\) almost surely. This obstruction is not caused by an absence of BIC outputs: \(B\) itself is feasible and BIC.

5. Failure of the requested approximation guarantee

For this oracle family and every \(q\geq 5\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{\phi(T(A))}{\phi(A)} =\alpha=\frac{q^2}{4}=\frac{N^{1/2}}4.\)

This exceeds every uniformly subpolynomial bound \(N^{o(1)}\) for sufficiently large \(N\). Indeed, if \(\varepsilon(N)\to 0\), then eventually \(\varepsilon(N)\leq 1/4\), and

\(\displaystyle \frac{N^{1/2}/4}{N^{\varepsilon(N)}}\geq\frac{N^{1/4}}4\longrightarrow\infty.\)

Therefore the requested transformation does not exist under finite-expected-utility BIC. The proof applies regardless of its query budget.

6. If infinite expected utilities are allowed

Almost-sure finiteness of payments alone does not ensure finite expected utility.

To see the consequence, query \(A\) at \(\mu\) and replay its kernel intact. Independently draw a common payment \(Z\) with

\(\displaystyle \Pr(Z=2^k)=2^{-k},\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,\)

and pay \(Z\) to every agent, regardless of report. Every realized transfer is finite, but

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[Z]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}1=+\infty.\)

Because processing costs are bounded, every expected utility in the displayed BIC inequalities is \(+\infty\). If those inequalities are interpreted in the extended-real order, they all hold. This one-query transformation preserves \(\phi(A)\) exactly.

Thus the intended, finite-expected-utility version is impossible; allowing infinite expected utilities makes the literal guarantee possible through vacuous incentive inequalities.
