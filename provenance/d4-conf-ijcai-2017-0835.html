Manipulating Opinion Diffusion in Social Networks (d4-conf-ijcai-2017-0835)

NEW RESULT — discrete: hard → continuous: hard · adversarial review: proved

Answer: \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-complete}\)

The anchor (named result in the paper)

Corollary 1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), initial opinions \(o_t\in\{0,1\}\), rational block-exposure coefficients \(a_{tu}\), a quota \(Q\), and a budget \(K\), can one choose bribe masses \(b_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_t b_t\le K\) so that every stable endpoint reachable by finitely many asynchronous moves of positive eligible mass and maximizing \(\sum_t x_t\) has \(\sum_t x_t\ge Q\), where a type may move mass toward black exactly when \(B_t(x)>W_t(x)\) and toward white when \(W_t(x)>B_t(x)\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has initial opinion \(o_t\in\{0,1\}\). Input exposures satisfy \(a_{tu}=a_{ut}\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]\); diagonal entries describe interaction within a type. Input quota and budget satisfy \(Q\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1]\) and \(K\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\).
Let \(m\) count unordered type pairs with positive exposure, including diagonal pairs, and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
A state records black masses \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\), with exposures \(B_t(x)=\sum_{u\in T}a_{tu}x_u\) and \(W_t(x)=\sum_{u\in T}a_{tu}(\mu_u-x_u)\).
A bribe chooses real masses \(b_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_t b_t\le K\), flips their initial opinions once, and produces \(x_t^0=\mu_to_t+(1-2o_t)b_t\); bribed mass subsequently follows the same update rules.
An asynchronous move changes one coordinate: increase \(x_t\) by any real \(0<\delta\le\mu_t-x_t\) if \(B_t(x)>W_t(x)\), or decrease it by any real \(0<\delta\le x_t\) if \(W_t(x)>B_t(x)\). Eligibility is evaluated before the move; ties permit no move.
A state is stable if no move is eligible. Let \(R(b)\) contain stable states reachable from \(x^0\) by finitely many moves, including zero moves; an optimistic endpoint is an element of \(R(b)\) attaining its maximum total black mass.
The decision problem asks whether a feasible bribe has at least one optimistic endpoint and every optimistic endpoint satisfies \(\sum_t x_t\ge Q\).

Is this continuous optimistic opinion-diffusion bribery problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite agents on simple undirected graphs, unit-cost initial-opinion bribery with optimistic stable updates is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; it is polynomial-time solvable on forests. [Source](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0124.pdf)
No result settling exactly the mass-based problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Undefined block exposures were interpreted as symmetric rational interaction densities in [0,1], preserving undirected, nonnegative influence; diagonal entries allow interaction between distinct individuals within a type.
  • Undefined black and white exposures were defined as the corresponding exposure-weighted masses, without row normalization.
  • Bribery flips initial opinions in either direction at unit cost per mass and does not make bribed individuals stubborn.
  • Bribe quantities and move quantities may be arbitrary real masses; a move changes one type and tests eligibility at its starting state.
  • The quota is a positive rational at most total mass, and the budget is a positive rational, carrying over the discrete problem's positive quota and budget.
  • Optimistic endpoints maximize over finitely reachable stable endpoints, including an initially stable state; their existence is required explicitly to prevent vacuous acceptance if no maximum is attained.
  • The size parameter m counts positive unordered interaction blocks, including diagonal blocks; L denotes total binary input length.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, hence \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

The proof handles arbitrary real-valued bribes. It also establishes that every initial state has a unique optimistic endpoint, reachable in at most \(2\tau\) moves. Hardness holds even with dyadic masses and \(m<3\tau\).

1. A greatest reachable stable state always exists

Write the exposure difference as
\[ h_t(x)=B_t(x)-W_t(x)=\sum_{u\in T}a_{tu}(2x_u-\mu_u). \]
Because all exposures are nonnegative, \(x\le y\) coordinatewise implies \(h_t(x)\le h_t(y)\) for every \(t\).

Lemma. From any initial state \(x^0\), the following procedure reaches a stable state \(D\) that coordinatewise dominates every reachable stable state.

  • Whenever \(x_t<\mu_t\) and \(h_t(x)>0\), set \(x_t=\mu_t\). Continue until no such move remains; call the resulting state \(U\).
  • Starting from \(U\), whenever \(x_t>0\) and \(h_t(x)<0\), set \(x_t=0\). Continue until no such move remains; call the resulting state \(D\).

Each phase makes at most \(\tau\) moves.

Proof. Every move is permitted by the specified rules. In the first phase, each changed coordinate becomes full and never changes again during that phase. Thus the phase terminates after at most \(\tau\) moves, and
\[ U_t<\mu_t\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h_t(U)\le0. \]

First, \(U\) bounds every state reachable from \(x^0\), including states reached using partial moves. Initially \(x^0\le U\). Suppose a reachable state \(x\) satisfies \(x\le U\). A decrease preserves this inequality. An increase also preserves it when \(U_t=\mu_t\). When \(U_t<\mu_t\), however,
\[ h_t(x)\le h_t(U)\le0, \]
so an increase of coordinate \(t\) is impossible. Induction on the number of moves proves the bound.

The second phase also terminates after at most \(\tau\) moves. Throughout this phase all exposure differences weakly decrease. A coordinate set to zero therefore retains a strictly negative exposure difference. A coordinate left below its maximum already had a nonpositive exposure difference at \(U\), and retains one. Consequently no increase is eligible at \(D\); termination of the second phase ensures that no decrease is eligible either. Thus \(D\) is stable.

Finally, let \(y\le U\) be any stable state. We show that \(y\) remains below every state in the second phase. Suppose the current state is \(z\ge y\), and coordinate \(t\) is about to be set to zero. Then
\[ h_t(y)\le h_t(z)<0. \]
Stability forces \(y_t=0\), since otherwise a decrease would be eligible at \(y\). Hence \(y\) remains below the next state. Induction gives \(y\le D\).

Every reachable stable state lies below \(U\), and hence below \(D\). Since \(D\) itself is reachable, it is the greatest reachable stable state. \(\square\)

In particular, the maximum defining an optimistic endpoint is always attained. Moreover, the optimistic endpoint is unique: if a reachable stable state \(y\le D\) has the same total black mass as \(D\), every coordinate difference \(D_t-y_t\) must be zero.

Thus the decision question is equivalent to asking whether some feasible bribe produces \(D\) with total black mass at least \(Q\).

2. Reduction parameters

We reduce from Vertex Cover, using its standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness ([Karp, 1972](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~madhusudan/courses/Fall2020/Lectures/L19-after.pdf)).

Let the input be a simple undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\), with \(n=|V|\), \(r=|E|\), and cover bound \(k\). We may restrict to
\[ n\ge2,\qquad r\ge1,\qquad 1\le k<n. \]
The excluded cases are decidable immediately and can be mapped to fixed restricted yes- or no-instances.

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\ge n\), and define
\[ M=n+1,\qquad c=\frac1{16N},\qquad \eta=\frac1{128N^2}=\frac{c}{8N},\qquad \varepsilon=\frac{2\eta}{rM},\qquad \kappa=k(c+2\eta). \]

The following bounds will be used:
\[ 0<\eta<c,\qquad c+2\eta<1, \]
\[ \kappa\le N(c+2\eta)=\frac1{16}+\frac1{64N}<\frac12, \]
and
\[ (k+1)(c-\eta)-\kappa =c-(3k+1)\eta =c\left(1-\frac{3k+1}{8N}\right)>0. \]
The last inequality follows from \(k\le N\).

Choose the smallest power of two \(H\) satisfying
\[ H\ge n+(r+1)M, \]
and put
\[ B=H-n-rM. \]
Then \(B\ge M=n+1\).

3. Construction

There is one type for each vertex, one type for each edge, and one additional anchor type \(z\). Their masses are
\[ \mu_v=\frac1H,\qquad \mu_e=\frac MH,\qquad \mu_z=\frac BH. \]
They are positive dyadic rationals summing to one.

Initially all vertex and edge types are white, while the anchor is black.

The positive exposures are:

  • \(a_{vv}=1\) for every vertex \(v\), and \(a_{zz}=1\).
  • \(a_{vz}=a_{zv}=(1-2c)/B\) for every vertex \(v\).
  • For every edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), \(a_{eu}=a_{ue}=a_{ev}=a_{ve}=\varepsilon\).
  • \(a_{ez}=a_{ze}=\varepsilon/B\) for every edge \(e\).

All other exposures are zero. These exposures are symmetric and belong to \([0,1]\): \(1-2c>0\), \(B\ge3\), and \(0<\varepsilon<1\).

Set
\[ K=\frac{\kappa}{H}, \qquad Q=\frac{B+rM}{H}. \]
Thus \(K>0\) and \(0<Q<1\).

The support size and positive-pair count are
\[ \tau=n+r+1,\qquad m=2n+3r+1<3\tau. \]
Every nonzero numerical entry has \(O(\log(n+r))\) bits. The construction therefore has polynomial encoding length and can be generated in polynomial time, including under a dense exposure-matrix encoding.

Exposure calculations

It is convenient to use unnormalized black masses \(y_t=Hx_t\). Their capacities are \(1\), \(M\), and \(B\), respectively. Define
\[ \widehat h_t(y)=Hh_t(y/H). \]
Multiplication by \(H>0\) preserves eligibility signs.

For a vertex \(v\),
\[ \widehat h_v(y) =2y_v-1+\frac{1-2c}{B}(2y_z-B) +\varepsilon\sum_{e\ni v}(2y_e-M). \]
Since \(y_z\le B\), \(y_e\le M\), and the degree \(d(v)\le r\),
\[ \widehat h_v(y) \le2y_v-2c+\varepsilon d(v)M \le2y_v-2c+2\eta. \tag{1} \]
When \(y_z=B\), the corresponding lower bound is
\[ \widehat h_v(y)\ge2y_v-2c-2\eta. \tag{2} \]

For an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\),
\[ \widehat h_e(y) =\varepsilon\bigl(2(y_u+y_v)-2\bigr) +\frac{\varepsilon}{B}(2y_z-B) \le\varepsilon\bigl(2(y_u+y_v)-1\bigr). \tag{3} \]
Equality holds in (3) whenever \(y_z=B\).

4. Completeness: a vertex cover gives a successful bribe

Suppose \(C\subseteq V\) is a vertex cover with \(|C|\le k\). Bribe unnormalized mass
\[ \beta_v=Hb_v=c+2\eta \]
at each vertex \(v\in C\), and bribe nothing elsewhere. The total normalized cost is at most
\[ \frac{k(c+2\eta)}H=K. \]

The anchor remains at \(y_z=B\). By (2), each chosen vertex initially satisfies
\[ \widehat h_v(y)\ge2(c+2\eta)-2c-2\eta=2\eta>0. \]
Set every chosen vertex to its full black mass \(1\). These increases cannot reduce any exposure difference, so all these moves remain eligible.

Every edge has a chosen endpoint. Consequently, after these moves, each edge satisfies
\[ \widehat h_e(y)=\varepsilon\bigl(2(y_u+y_v)-1\bigr)\ge\varepsilon>0. \]
Set every edge type to its full black mass \(M\).

We verify stability of the resulting state.

  • An unchosen vertex has black mass zero and, by (1),

\[ \widehat h_v(y)\le-2c+2\eta<0. \]

  • A chosen vertex is full and, by (2),

\[ \widehat h_v(y)\ge2-2c-2\eta>0. \]

  • Every edge is full and has positive exposure difference, as above.
  • The anchor is full. Its exposure difference satisfies

\[ \widehat h_z(y) =B+\frac{1-2c}{B}\sum_{v\in V}(2y_v-1) +\frac{\varepsilon}{B}\sum_{e\in E}(2y_e-M) \ge B-\frac{n+2\eta}{B}>0. \]
Here \(\varepsilon rM=2\eta\), and \(B\ge n+1\) implies \(B^2>n+2\eta\).

Thus the state is stable and finitely reachable. Its total black mass is
\[ \frac{B+rM+|C|}{H}\ge Q. \]
By the lemma, an optimistic endpoint exists and has at least this much black mass. The constructed instance is therefore a yes-instance.

5. Soundness: arbitrary fractional bribes give a vertex cover

Consider any feasible bribe, allowing bribes to every type. Write \(\beta_t=Hb_t\), so
\[ \sum_t\beta_t\le\kappa. \]
Initially \(y_v=\beta_v\) and \(y_e=\beta_e\), while \(y_z=B-\beta_z\).

Apply the increasing phase of the lemma, using unnormalized coordinates, and call its endpoint \(U\). Define
\[ S=\{v\in V:U_v=1\}. \]

At most \(k\) vertex types become full

We claim that every \(v\in S\) satisfies
\[ \beta_v>c-\eta. \tag{4} \]

If \(v\) was initially full, then \(\beta_v=1>c-\eta\). Otherwise, immediately before its increasing-phase move, its black mass is still \(\beta_v\): that phase changes a coordinate only by setting it directly to its maximum. Eligibility and (1) give
\[ 0<\widehat h_v(y)\le2\beta_v-2c+2\eta, \]
which proves (4).

If \(|S|\ge k+1\), the budget would therefore satisfy
\[ \kappa\ge\sum_{v\in S}\beta_v >(k+1)(c-\eta)>\kappa, \]
a contradiction. Hence \(|S|\le k\).

An uncovered edge must be white at every reachable stable state

Suppose \(S\) is not a vertex cover, and choose an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\) with neither endpoint in \(S\).

Neither endpoint was changed during the increasing phase, so
\[ U_u=\beta_u,\qquad U_v=\beta_v,\qquad U_u+U_v\le\kappa<\frac12. \]
By (3),
\[ \widehat h_e(U)\le\varepsilon(2\kappa-1)<0. \]

Every reachable state lies coordinatewise below \(U\), by the lemma. Therefore any reachable stable state \(y\) satisfies
\[ \widehat h_e(y)\le\widehat h_e(U)<0. \]
Stability forces \(y_e=0\).

Such a stable state has total black mass at most
\[ \frac{B+(r-1)M+n}{H} =\frac{B+rM}{H}-\frac{M-n}{H} =Q-\frac1H<Q. \]
Thus no feasible successful bribe can leave an uncovered edge.

Every successful bribe consequently yields a vertex cover \(S\) with \(|S|\le k\). This proves the reduction and establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

6. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) and running times

The remaining issue is that bribes may be arbitrary real numbers. We prove that every yes-instance admits a polynomial-size rational bribe.

Take a successful real bribe and its canonical execution from the lemma. Record:

  • the ordered list of full increases;
  • the ordered list of full decreases;
  • whether each final coordinate is zero, strictly interior, or full.

There are at most \(\tau\) moves in each phase. For this fixed execution pattern, every intermediate coordinate is either an affine function of the bribe variables, zero, or its capacity.

Consequently, the following requirements are rational linear equalities or inequalities:

  • bribe bounds and budget;
  • strict exposure-sign and positive-move-size conditions at every move;
  • the final quota;
  • final stability, expressed according to the recorded status as

\[ w_t=0,\ h_t(w)\le0; \qquad 0<w_t<\mu_t,\ h_t(w)=0; \qquad w_t=\mu_t,\ h_t(w)\ge0. \]

The number of constraints and their coefficient encoding lengths are polynomial in \(L\).

Introduce a variable \(s\in[0,1]\), and replace every strict inequality \(f(b)>0\) by \(f(b)\ge s\). The original real witness satisfies all finitely many strict inequalities, so this bounded rational polytope has a feasible point with \(s>0\).

Maximize \(s\). The optimum is positive and is attained at a vertex. Such a vertex is determined by linearly independent active constraints in at most \(\tau+1\) variables. Clearing denominators and applying Cramer’s rule shows that its coordinates have polynomial encoding length: the relevant determinants have polynomial dimension and polynomial-bit integer entries. Thus a successful rational bribe of total encoding length \(C=\operatorname{poly}(L)\) exists.

A verifier receives this rational bribe, checks its cost, runs the canonical procedure, and checks its endpoint quota.

After constructing adjacency lists, each phase can maintain exposure differences and eligible coordinates in \(O(m+\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations. Each coordinate changes at most once per phase, and each incident exposure is updated only a constant number of times. For a rational bribe of encoding length \(C\), the resulting bit running time is
\[ O\bigl((m+\tau)\operatorname{poly}(L+C)\bigr). \]
For the polynomial-size certificates just proved, this is polynomial in the input length. Therefore the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

For completeness, a deterministic exact algorithm can enumerate the two ordered move lists and the final coordinate statuses, and solve the corresponding slack-maximization linear programs. There are at most
\[ 9\cdot3^\tau(\tau!)^2 \]
patterns, giving running time
\[ 2^{O(\tau\log(\tau+1))}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). \]

Together with the reduction, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.
