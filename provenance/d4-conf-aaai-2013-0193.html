The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a symmetric rational interaction matrix \(a\), and \(\epsilon>0\), treat each type as an atomless population of mass \(\mu_t\). For a coalition with type-mass profile \(q\), define per-capita value \(\phi(q)=\sum_{s<t}a_{st}q_sq_t+\frac12\sum_t a_{tt}q_t^2\), corresponding to pair weights scaled by \(1/N\) in an \(N\)-agent realization. Output a finite rational decomposition into coalition profiles, with residual mass assigned to infinitesimal singleton coalitions, whose welfare is within \(\epsilon\) of the supremum over measurable partitions; measure complexity in \(|T|\), input bit length \(L\), and \(1/\epsilon\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; there is no separate ground-set parameter \(m\).
Each type is an atomless population. Input also includes a symmetric matrix \(a\in\mathbb Q^{\tau\times\tau}\), with unrestricted signs and diagonal entries, and a binary rational \(\epsilon>0\); \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A coalition's profile \(q\) records its absolute type masses, and its contribution to welfare is \(\phi(q)=\sum_{s<t}a_{st}q_sq_t+\tfrac12\sum_t a_{tt}q_t^2\).
Types may be split freely among disjoint measurable coalitions, with no bound on their number or additional feasibility constraints. Welfare is the sum of \(\phi(q)\) over the at most countably many positive-mass coalitions; zero-mass coalitions contribute zero.
Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the supremum of this welfare over measurable partitions. The computational problem is to output finitely many nonzero rational profiles \(q^1,\ldots,q^k\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_i q^i_t\le\mu_t\) for every type and \(\sum_i\phi(q^i)\ge\mathrm{OPT}-\epsilon\); residual mass is partitioned into singleton coalitions of value zero.

Is this additive approximation problem solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\), \(L\), and \(1/\epsilon\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even when \(1/\epsilon\) is polynomially bounded in the remaining input length?

For finite agents, deciding whether a partition's total internal signed edge weight can reach a rational threshold is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, including on planar graphs. [Verified source](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/bkkz_aaai2013.pdf).
No result settling this exact atomless additive approximation problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made epsilon a positive binary rational so that its encoding and the input bit length are defined.
  • Interpreted q as absolute coalition masses and phi(q) as that coalition's contribution to welfare per unit of total society mass. Welfare sums these contributions without an additional coalition-mass factor, consistently with the stated finite-population scaling.
  • Allowed arbitrary signed rational interaction coefficients, including diagonal coefficients representing interactions between distinct individuals of the same type.
  • Defined measurable-partition welfare by summing over positive-mass coalitions, with zero-mass coalitions contributing zero; the output's residual population is partitioned into actual zero-mass singletons.

  • Used tau as the only structural size parameter because this problem has no separate ground set measured by m.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even when all diagonal entries are zero, all coefficients have absolute value at most one, and \(1/\epsilon=4\tau^3\). Consequently, an algorithm polynomial in \(\tau\), \(L\), and \(1/\epsilon\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The essential fact is that, with zero diagonal entries, splitting types cannot improve the optimal welfare.

1. A rounding lemma for zero diagonals

Assume \(a_{tt}=0\) for every type \(t\). Let \((q^i)_{i\in I}\) be any finite or countable family of positive-mass coalition profiles satisfying \(\sum_{i\in I}q^i_t\leq\mu_t\). Its welfare is

\[ W=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{s<t}a_{st}q^i_sq^i_t. \]

This sum is absolutely convergent. Indeed, for each pair of types,

\[ \sum_{i\in I}q^i_sq^i_t \leq \left(\sum_{i\in I}q^i_s\right) \left(\sum_{i\in I}q^i_t\right) \leq\mu_s\mu_t, \]

and therefore

\[ \sum_{i\in I}\left|\phi(q^i)\right| \leq\sum_{s<t}|a_{st}|\mu_s\mu_t<\infty. \]

For each type \(t\), independently choose a label \(X_t\). Assign probability \(q^i_t/\mu_t\) to coalition label \(i\in I\). Assign the remaining probability to a private label \(r_t\), distinct from every coalition label and every other private label.

Group whole type populations according to their chosen labels. This produces a measurable partition with at most \(\tau\) coalitions. Its welfare is

\[ F(X)=\sum_{s<t}a_{st}\mu_s\mu_t\,\mathbf{1}_{\{X_s=X_t\}}. \]

For distinct types \(s,t\), independence and the private-label construction give

\[ \Pr(X_s=X_t) =\sum_{i\in I}\frac{q^i_s}{\mu_s}\frac{q^i_t}{\mu_t}. \]

Consequently,

\[ \mathbb{E}[F(X)] =\sum_{s<t}a_{st}\sum_{i\in I}q^i_sq^i_t =W. \]

Let \(\mathcal{P}(T)\) denote the finite set of partitions of the type set. Every realization of \(X\) induces a member of \(\mathcal{P}(T)\), so its welfare is at most

\[ M=\max_{P\in\mathcal{P}(T)} \sum_{B\in P}\sum_{\substack{s<t\\s,t\in B}} a_{st}\mu_s\mu_t. \]

Thus \(W=\mathbb{E}[F(X)]\leq M\). Conversely, every partition of whole types is a permissible measurable partition. We have proved the exact identity

\[ \boxed{\mathrm{OPT}=M.} \]

In particular, the supremum is attained by at most \(\tau\) rational profiles when the input masses are rational. This argument covers both countably many positive-mass coalitions and residual singleton mass.

2. An explicit reduction from Independent Set

We reduce from the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete Independent Set decision problem: given a simple graph \(G=(V,E)\) with \(n\geq1\) vertices and an integer \(K\), decide whether its independence number \(\alpha(G)\) satisfies \(\alpha(G)\geq K\). Independent Set is also the starting point of the finite-agent hardness reduction in Theorem 3 of [Bachrach, Kohli, Kolmogorov, and Zadimoghaddam](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/bkkz_aaai2013.pdf).

Let \(\tau\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(\tau\geq n+1\). Then \(n+1\leq\tau<2(n+1)\).

Create one type for each vertex, one additional hub type \(h\), and \(\tau-n-1\) dummy types. Every type has mass \(\mu_t=1/\tau\). Define the symmetric matrix by

\[ a_{hv}=\frac1\tau\quad(v\in V),\qquad a_{uv}=-1\quad(\{u,v\}\in E), \]

with every other entry, including all diagonal entries, equal to zero. Set

\[ \epsilon=\frac{1}{4\tau^3}. \]

All masses, coefficients, and the accuracy parameter are dyadic rationals. Every type has positive mass, and \(|a_{st}|\leq1\).

We now prove

\[ \boxed{\mathrm{OPT}=\frac{\alpha(G)}{\tau^3}.} \]

By the rounding lemma, it suffices to consider partitions of whole types.

For such a partition, let \(S\subseteq V\) be the vertices sharing the hub’s coalition, and let \(e(S)\) be the number of graph edges induced by \(S\). The hub coalition has welfare

\[ \frac{|S|}{\tau^3}-\frac{e(S)}{\tau^2} =\frac{|S|-\tau e(S)}{\tau^3}. \]

Every other coalition has nonpositive welfare, since its only possible nonzero interactions are negative graph edges.

If \(S\) is independent, the total welfare is at most \(|S|/\tau^3\leq\alpha(G)/\tau^3\).

If \(S\) is not independent, then \(e(S)\geq1\), and hence

\[ |S|-\tau e(S)\leq n-\tau\leq-1. \]

The total welfare is then negative, so it is again at most \(\alpha(G)/\tau^3\). This proves the upper bound for every partition.

For the matching lower bound, place the hub together with a maximum independent set, and put every remaining type in its own coalition. There are exactly \(\alpha(G)\) positive hub interactions and no internal negative interactions. The resulting welfare is \(\alpha(G)/\tau^3\), proving the identity.

3. The additive approximation distinguishes the decision cases

Suppose an algorithm satisfies the requested guarantee. Run it on the constructed instance and compute the exact rational welfare \(W\) of its output.

Feasibility and the approximation guarantee imply

\[ \frac{\alpha(G)-1/4}{\tau^3} \leq W \leq \frac{\alpha(G)}{\tau^3}. \]

Compare \(W\) with the rational threshold

\[ R=\frac{K-1/2}{\tau^3}. \]

If \(\alpha(G)\geq K\), then

\[ W\geq\frac{K-1/4}{\tau^3}>R. \]

If \(\alpha(G)<K\), integrality gives \(\alpha(G)\leq K-1\), so

\[ W\leq\frac{K-1}{\tau^3}<R. \]

Thus a single call to the approximation algorithm decides Independent Set.

The construction has \(\tau<2(n+1)\), input length \(L=O(\tau^2\log\tau)\), and \(1/\epsilon=4\tau^3\). Its encoding and construction time are polynomial in the graph input size. Moreover, the input length excluding \(\epsilon\) is at least \(\tau\), so \(1/\epsilon\) is polynomially bounded in that remaining length.

If the approximation algorithm ran in polynomial time, its output would also have polynomial bit length. Exact evaluation of its finitely many rational profiles and comparison with \(R\) would therefore take polynomial time.

This completes the reduction and settles the stated question.
