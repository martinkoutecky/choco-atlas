Result

The stated continuous decision problem is in $\mathrm{P}$. In fact, its optimum has an explicit formula: either a zero-cost control makes the objective unbounded above, or the entire budget can be assigned to one control type with the best utility gain per unit cost.

The proof uses the fixed control set and the given balance equations directly.

1. Eliminate the steady-state opinions

Write $k=|I|$, so $\tau=m+k$. Define
$$ A=(W_{ts}\mu_s\lambda_s)_{t,s\in O}, \qquad M=\omega\operatorname{Id}_m-A, \qquad G=(W_{ts}\mu_s)_{t\in O,\ s\in I}. $$
Let $p=(\mu_t)_{t\in O}$ and $\alpha=(\alpha_t)_{t\in O}$, both column vectors.

Since $M=-D$, the stability promise implies that $M$ is invertible: otherwise $0$ would be an eigenvalue of $D$, contradicting its strictly negative real part.

For each control type, define its net posting rate
$$ u_s=h_s^+-h_s^-. $$
The balance equations become
$$ Mx=\omega\alpha+Gu. $$
Consequently, every choice of finite rates determines exactly one steady-state vector,
$$ x=M^{-1}(\omega\alpha+Gu). $$
There are no imposed opinion bounds, so this vector introduces no additional feasibility condition.

Solve the single adjoint system
$$ M^{\mathsf T}y=p. $$
Then $p^{\mathsf T}M^{-1}=y^{\mathsf T}$, and the utility equals
$$ -p^{\mathsf T}x = -\omega y^{\mathsf T}\alpha-y^{\mathsf T}Gu = b+\sum_{s\in I}a_su_s, $$
where
$$ b=-\omega y^{\mathsf T}\alpha, \qquad a_s=-\mu_s\sum_{t\in O}y_tW_{ts}. $$
All these coefficients are rational.

2. Characterize feasible net posting rates

Define the effective cost
$$ d_s=\mu_sc_s\ge0. $$
A vector $u\in\mathbb R^k$ is induced by feasible posting rates if and only if
$$ \sum_{s\in I}d_s|u_s|\le C. $$

For necessity, nonnegativity of the posting rates gives
$$ |h_s^+-h_s^-|\le h_s^++h_s^-. $$
Multiplying by $d_s$ and summing proves the required inequality.

For sufficiency, set
$$ h_s^+=\max\{u_s,0\}, \qquad h_s^-=\max\{-u_s,0\}. $$
These rates have difference $u_s$ and sum $|u_s|$, so they satisfy the original budget constraint.

Thus the problem is exactly whether some $u$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{s\in I}d_s|u_s|\le C, \qquad b+\sum_{s\in I}a_su_s\ge q. $$

3. Compute the optimum exactly

Case A: an effective zero-cost control exists

Suppose some $s\in I$ satisfies
$$ d_s=0, \qquad a_s\ne0. $$
For any $R\ge0$, choose
$$ u_s=\operatorname{sgn}(a_s)R, \qquad u_r=0\quad(r\ne s). $$
Its cost is zero and its utility is $b+|a_s|R$. Hence the utility is unbounded above, and every finite threshold $q$ is attainable, including when $C=0$.

Explicitly, the rational choice
$$ R=\max\left\{0,\frac{q-b}{|a_s|}\right\} $$
already attains the threshold.

Case B: every zero-cost control has zero utility coefficient

Assume
$$ d_s=0\ \Longrightarrow\ a_s=0. $$
Define
$$ \rho= \max\left( \{0\}\cup \left\{\frac{|a_s|}{d_s}:s\in I,\ d_s>0\right\} \right). $$
This definition includes $I=\varnothing$.

For every feasible $u$,
$$ \sum_{s\in I}a_su_s \le \sum_{s\in I}|a_s|\,|u_s| \le \rho\sum_{s\in I}d_s|u_s| \le \rho C. $$
The second inequality also holds for zero-cost types because their coefficients vanish. Therefore, every feasible utility is at most $b+C\rho$.

This bound is attained:

  • If $\rho=0$, then all $a_s$ vanish, and $u=0$ attains utility $b$.
  • If $\rho>0$, select a type $s^\star$ attaining the maximum and set

$$ u_{s^\star} = \operatorname{sgn}(a_{s^\star})\frac{C}{d_{s^\star}}, \qquad u_s=0\quad(s\ne s^\star). $$
Its cost is $C$, and its utility is $b+C\rho$.

Consequently, in Case B the answer is exactly
$$ \boxed{b+C\rho\ge q}. $$
The optimum is attained, so equality at the threshold causes no difficulty.

4. Running time and exact arithmetic

Constructing $M$, solving $M^{\mathsf T}y=p$, computing all coefficients, and selecting the best ratio require
$$ O\!\left(m^3+m|I|+\tau\right) $$
rational arithmetic operations using Gaussian elimination. Reading the input additionally takes $O(L)$ bit operations. To establish membership in $\mathrm{P}$, we must also bound the integer sizes used by an exact implementation.

Here is a conservative explicit bit-time bound.

Let $Q$ be the product of the positive denominators of all input rationals. Its binary length is $O(L)$. Each entry of $M$ is a difference between an input rational and a product of three input rationals, whose denominator divides $Q$. Thus
$$ H=QM^{\mathsf T}, \qquad r=Qp $$
are an integer matrix and integer vector, with entries of binary length $O(L)$.

Since $H$ is nonsingular, Cramer’s rule gives
$$ y_j=\frac{\det H_j}{\det H}, $$
where $H_j$ replaces column $j$ of $H$ by $r$. Hadamard’s inequality bounds the binary lengths of these determinants, and of all their minors, by
$$ O\!\left(m\bigl(L+\log(m+1)\bigr)\right). $$

Use pivoted fraction-free elimination to compute these $m+1$ determinants. This standard exact determinant algorithm takes $O(m^3)$ integer arithmetic operations per determinant; its intermediate entries are signed minors, and its divisions are exact. It applies to both singular and nonsingular integer matrices, so it also handles any singular $H_j$. Hence the total determinant computation takes $O(m^4)$ such operations on integers of the bounded length above.

For completeness, subsequent coefficient sums also retain polynomial length. Write each input rational $v$ as $\widehat v/Q$, and let
$$ \Delta=\det H, \qquad z_j=\det H_j. $$
Then
$$ b= -\frac{\widehat\omega\sum_{t\in O}z_t\widehat\alpha_t} {\Delta Q^2}, \qquad a_s= -\frac{\widehat\mu_s\sum_{t\in O}z_t\widehat W_{ts}} {\Delta Q^2}, \qquad d_s=\frac{\widehat\mu_s\widehat c_s}{Q^2}. $$
These formulas use common denominators. Their numerators and denominators have binary length
$$ O\!\left(m\bigl(L+\log(m+1)\bigr)\right). $$
All signs, ratios, and the final threshold can therefore be compared exactly by cross multiplication with integers of that same asymptotic length.

Using schoolbook integer multiplication and division yields the explicit bit-time upper bound
$$ O\!\left( L+ \bigl(m^4+m|I|+\tau\bigr) m^2\bigl(L+\log(m+1)\bigr)^2 \right). $$
This bound is conservative but polynomial. No expansion into individual agents or unlisted possible types is involved.

The algorithm need not test stability: it uses the input promise to obtain invertibility.
