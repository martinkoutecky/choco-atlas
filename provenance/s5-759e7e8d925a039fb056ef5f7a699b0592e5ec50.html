The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite simple graph \(G=(V,E)\) with no isolated vertices and positive rational type masses \(\mu_e\) summing to \(1\), let each edge type \(e\) have an atomless population \(\Omega_e\) of mass \(\mu_e\). For each measurable coalition \(X\), let \(F(X)=\{e:\lambda(X\cap\Omega_e)=\mu_e\}\), \(V_X=\{v:\delta(v)\subseteq F(X)\}\), and \(\gamma_\mu(X)=\alpha(G[V_X])\). Does there exist a family of nonnegative measurable payoff densities \(f_X\) satisfying \(\int_X f_X\,d\lambda=\gamma_\mu(X)\) for every \(X\), and \(f_X(i)\le f_Y(i)\) almost everywhere whenever \(X\subseteq Y\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite simple undirected graph with no isolated vertices and \(E\ne\varnothing\). Set \(m=|V|\). A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|E|\) edge types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_e\), where \(\sum_{e\in E}\mu_e=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Realize the society as the disjoint union \(\Omega=\bigsqcup_{e\in E}\Omega_e\), where \(\Omega_e=\{e\}\times[0,\mu_e]\), with completed Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\). Coalitions are all measurable subsets of \(\Omega\).
For a coalition \(X\), define \(F(X)=\{e\in E:\lambda(X\cap\Omega_e)=\mu_e\}\), \(V_X=\{v\in V:\delta(v)\subseteq F(X)\}\), and \(\gamma_\mu(X)=\alpha(G[V_X])\). Here \(\delta(v)\) is the set of incident edges, and \(\alpha\) is the independence number, with \(\alpha(G[\varnothing])=0\).
A population monotonic allocation scheme is a family of nonnegative measurable integrable densities \(f_X:X\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), one for every measurable coalition, such that \(\int_X f_X\,d\lambda=\gamma_\mu(X)\) and, for every \(X\subseteq Y\), \(f_X\le f_Y\) almost everywhere on \(X\). The exceptional null set may depend on the pair \((X,Y)\).

Is deciding whether this society admits a population monotonic allocation scheme in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For the discrete game with edge players and coalition worth \(\alpha(G[\{v:\delta(v)\subseteq S\}])\), existence of a nonnegative efficient population monotonic allocation scheme is decidable in polynomial time ([Wang et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.17041)).
No result settling this exact atomless decision problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The atomless measure spaces were unspecified; use disjoint labeled intervals with completed Lebesgue measure, so they require no additional input.
  • The domains and regularity of payoff densities were implicit; define each density on its coalition, with nonnegative real values and integrability.
  • The scope of almost-everywhere monotonicity was unspecified; allow the exceptional null set to depend on each nested pair of coalitions.
  • The size parameter and bit-length convention were unspecified; use m for the number of vertices, tau for the edge-type support size, and L for total input bit length.
  • Require a nonempty edge set, as already forced by positive masses summing to one, and specify independence number zero for the empty induced graph.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). In fact, the atomless society admits a population monotonic allocation scheme exactly when the discrete edge game does. The positive masses affect the densities used to realize a scheme, but do not affect its existence.

1. The discrete game

For \(S\subseteq E\), define
\[ W(S)=\{v\in V:\delta(v)\subseteq S\}, \qquad g(S)=\alpha(G[W(S)]). \]
Since there are no isolated vertices, \(W(\varnothing)=\varnothing\), so \(g(\varnothing)=0\).

A discrete population monotonic allocation scheme consists of nonnegative numbers \(a_{S,e}\), for every nonempty \(S\subseteq E\) and \(e\in S\), satisfying
\[ \sum_{e\in S}a_{S,e}=g(S), \qquad a_{S,e}\le a_{T,e}\quad\text{whenever }e\in S\subseteq T. \]

By the definitions in the question, every measurable coalition \(X\) satisfies
\[ \gamma_\mu(X)=g(F(X)). \]

2. Equivalence of the two existence problems

Proposition. The atomless society admits a population monotonic allocation scheme if and only if the discrete game \((E,g)\) does.

From an atomless scheme to a discrete scheme

Suppose an atomless scheme \(\{f_X\}\) exists. For each \(S\subseteq E\), let
\[ A_S=\bigsqcup_{e\in S}\Omega_e. \]
Every mass is positive. Consequently, \(F(A_S)=S\), and hence \(\gamma_\mu(A_S)=g(S)\).

For nonempty \(S\) and \(e\in S\), define
\[ a_{S,e}=\int_{\Omega_e}f_{A_S}\,d\lambda. \]
These numbers are finite and nonnegative by integrability and nonnegativity. The finite disjoint decomposition of \(A_S\), together with efficiency, gives
\[ \sum_{e\in S}a_{S,e} =\int_{A_S}f_{A_S}\,d\lambda =g(S). \]

If \(e\in S\subseteq T\), then \(A_S\subseteq A_T\). Atomless monotonicity gives \(f_{A_S}\le f_{A_T}\) almost everywhere on \(A_S\), and therefore almost everywhere on \(\Omega_e\). Integrating yields \(a_{S,e}\le a_{T,e}\).

Thus \(\{a_{S,e}\}\) is a discrete scheme. Pair-dependent exceptional null sets cause no difficulty: each required inequality is integrated separately.

From a discrete scheme to an atomless scheme

Conversely, suppose a discrete scheme \(\{a_{S,e}\}\) exists. For every measurable coalition \(X\), define, at a point \(\omega\in X\cap\Omega_e\),
\[ f_X(\omega)= \begin{cases} a_{F(X),e}/\mu_e,& e\in F(X),\\ 0,& e\notin F(X). \end{cases} \]
When \(F(X)=\varnothing\), this definition simply gives the zero function.

For each fixed \(X\), this is a nonnegative measurable function, constant on each of finitely many measurable pieces. Every constant is finite because \(\mu_e>0\). Moreover,
\[ \int_X f_X\,d\lambda =\sum_{e\in F(X)} \frac{a_{F(X),e}}{\mu_e}\lambda(X\cap\Omega_e) =\sum_{e\in F(X)}a_{F(X),e} =g(F(X)) =\gamma_\mu(X). \]
For empty \(F(X)\), both the sum and the worth are zero. Thus efficiency and integrability hold for every measurable coalition.

Now let \(X\subseteq Y\). For every edge \(e\),
\[ \lambda(X\cap\Omega_e)\le\lambda(Y\cap\Omega_e)\le\mu_e, \]
so \(F(X)\subseteq F(Y)\).

Fix \(\omega\in X\cap\Omega_e\). If \(e\notin F(X)\), then \(f_X(\omega)=0\le f_Y(\omega)\). Otherwise, \(e\in F(X)\subseteq F(Y)\), and discrete monotonicity gives
\[ f_X(\omega) =\frac{a_{F(X),e}}{\mu_e} \le\frac{a_{F(Y),e}}{\mu_e} =f_Y(\omega). \]
The constructed scheme therefore satisfies monotonicity pointwise. This argument also covers coalitions containing an edge type only up to a null subset. The proposition is proved.

3. Recognition criterion

A pendant vertex has degree one, and a pendant edge has a pendant endpoint. A type I edge is a non-pendant edge sharing no endpoint with any pendant edge.

Theorem 9 of [Wang, Xiao, Du, and Xu (2022)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.17041) states that the discrete independent set game admits a nonnegative efficient population monotonic allocation scheme exactly when:

  • Every vertex is within distance two of a pendant vertex.
  • The type I edges form a matching.

Its hypotheses hold here: \(G\) is finite, simple, undirected, and has no isolated vertices; its edge players and coalition worth are exactly those defining \(g\). By the proposition, these two conditions also characterize the atomless society.

4. Algorithm and running time

Compute all degrees and let \(P=\{v:\deg(v)=1\}\). Run a multi-source breadth-first search from \(P\), rejecting if any vertex has distance greater than two or is unreachable.

Next mark \(H=P\cup N(P)\). These are exactly the vertices incident to pendant edges. Thus an edge is type I precisely when both endpoints lie outside \(H\). Scan those edges and reject if any vertex belongs to more than one of them. Otherwise accept.

Degrees, breadth-first search, marking, and edge scanning take \(O(m+\tau)\) word operations with adjacency lists. Including reading the binary input gives \(O(m+\tau+L)\) time on a standard word RAM. Charging logarithmic bit cost per word operation gives the conservative bound \(O((m+\tau+L)\log(m+\tau+L))\) in the bit-cost RAM model.

No arithmetic on the rational masses is required: their positivity and normalization are input promises, and existence depends only on the support graph.
