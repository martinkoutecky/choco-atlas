The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite family \(Q\) of explicit multigraph templates, each with \(n\) roles, indivisible edge-goods, and cancelable endpoint-local valuations, and binary-rational masses \(\lambda_q>0\) summing to \(1\), consider a continuum of copies of template \(q\) indexed by \(x\in[0,\lambda_q)\). Role \(i\) in a copy has type \((q,i)\) and mass \(\mu_{q,i}=\lambda_q/n\). Can one compute a measurable policy assigning every copy a complete allocation of its own goods such that, for almost every copy and all roles \(i,j\) and goods \(g\in X_j^q(x)\), \(v_{q,i}(X_j^q(x)\setminus\{g\})\le v_{q,i}(X_i^q(x))\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Input: a nonempty finite family \(Q\) of explicit loopless multigraphs \(G_q=([n],M_q)\), with \(n\geq 1\), indivisible edge-goods, and positive binary-rational weights \(\lambda_q\) summing to \(1\). Write \(m=\sum_q|M_q|\).
Each valuation \(v_{q,i}:2^{M_q}\to\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\) is encoded by a Boolean circuit returning a binary-rational value from a bundle's incidence vector; valuations are promised monotone and endpoint-local: \(v_{q,i}(S)=v_{q,i}(S\cap E_{q,i})\), where \(E_{q,i}\) contains the edges incident to \(i\).
Cancelability means that for all \(S,T\subseteq M_q\) and \(g\notin S\cup T\), \(v_{q,i}(S\cup\{g\})>v_{q,i}(T\cup\{g\})\) implies \(v_{q,i}(S)>v_{q,i}(T)\). No normalization of empty-bundle values is imposed.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=n|Q|\) types \((q,i)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_{q,i}=\lambda_q/n\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Copies of \(q\) are indexed by \(x\in[0,\lambda_q)\), with each role carrying measure \(dx/n\). Goods stay within their copy and may be assigned to any role in that copy.
A policy assigns each copy a complete partition \((X_1^q(x),\ldots,X_n^q(x))\) of \(M_q\). Represent it by finitely many rational half-open intervals per template, each labeled with an allocation.
Require, for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\), all \(i,j\in[n]\), and every \(g\in X_j^q(x)\), that \(v_{q,i}(X_j^q(x)\setminus\{g\})\leq v_{q,i}(X_i^q(x))\), including goods with zero value to \(i\). Comparisons concern roles within the same copy.

Can such a policy be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,n,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

Known: complete EFX allocations for finite multigraph instances with monotone cancelable endpoint-local valuations always exist and can be computed in polynomial time. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18665v1)

2. Exact equivalence with finite EFX search

For a template $q$, define a finite EFX allocation to be a complete partition $A^q=(A_1^q,\ldots,A_n^q)$ of $M_q$ satisfying

$$ v_{q,i}(A_j^q\setminus\{g\})\leq v_{q,i}(A_i^q) $$

for every $i,j\in[n]$ and every $g\in A_j^q$. This definition includes removal of goods having zero value to the comparing role.

Proof. Given finite EFX allocations, label the single interval $[0,\lambda_q)$ by $A^q$ for each $q$. Every required comparison then holds everywhere.

Conversely, fix a template in a valid policy. Sort all its interval endpoints, including $0$ and $\lambda_q$, and refine the representation into cells between consecutive endpoints. On the interior of each cell, the allocation is constant.

Every positive-length cell must carry a finite EFX allocation. Otherwise, some fixed comparison would fail throughout that cell, contradicting almost-everywhere feasibility. The measure factor $1/n$ is positive, so it does not change which sets have measure zero.

Because $\lambda_q>0$ and there are finitely many cells, at least one cell has positive length. Extract its allocation. Sorting explicit rational endpoints and reading an allocation label take polynomial time. Repeat for every template. $\square$

3. A hardness result under the literal promises

3.1. External theorem used

Goldberg, Høgh, and Hollender prove that computing a complete EFX allocation for two identical agents with a normalized monotone submodular valuation encoded by a Boolean circuit is $\mathrm{PLS}$-hard. Their circuit model permits binary-encoded nonnegative integer outputs. Their EFX definition quantifies over every removed good, and the hardness construction satisfies monotonicity; it does not use certificates that monotonicity fails. See Definition 4.3 and Theorem 4.2 of [*The frontier of intractability for EFX with two agents*](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/paul.goldberg/papers/TCS-GHH.pdf).

Submodularity here means that, whenever $S\subseteq T$ and $g\notin T$,

$$ v(S\cup\{g\})-v(S) \geq v(T\cup\{g\})-v(T). $$

I use this published hardness theorem as an external result.

4. Complete classification of the two-role, one-template restriction

For completeness, I prove the matching $\mathrm{PLS}$ upper bound directly, including rational-output bit complexity.

4.1. Identical monotone valuations

Let $M$ contain $k$ goods, and let both roles have the same monotone valuation $v$. No normalization is needed.

Choose a polynomially bounded integer $B$ such that every circuit output, written as a nonnegative fraction $p/d$ with $d\geq1$, satisfies

$$ p<2^B,\qquad d<2^B. $$

Such a bound follows from the explicit output length of the circuit.

For two distinct output values $a=p/d$ and $b=p'/d'$, the integer $pd'-p'd$ is nonzero. Consequently,

$$ |a-b| = \frac{|pd'-p'd|}{dd'} \geq \frac1{dd'} > 2^{-2B}. $$

Define an integer encoding

$$ R(a)=\left\lfloor 2^{2B+1}a\right\rfloor. $$

For circuit output values, $R$ preserves strict order: if $a<b$, then the scaled difference exceeds $2$, so $R(a)<R(b)$. Equal values receive equal encodings. Since $a<2^B$, computing $R(a)$ requires only $O(B)$ bits and polynomial-time integer arithmetic.

For each bundle $S$, put

$$ c(S)=(k+1)R(v(S))+|S|. $$

Strictly larger valuation implies strictly larger $c$, because the cardinality term varies by at most $k$. Equal valuation is broken in favor of larger cardinality.

Use complete ordered partitions $(A,B)$ as local-search states, with objective

$$ \Phi(A,B)=\min\{c(A),c(B)\}. $$

The neighbors are the partitions obtained by transferring one good from its current bundle to the other bundle. Initialization, objective evaluation, and examination of all $k$ neighbors take polynomial time. The objective is a nonnegative integer of polynomial bit length.

Lemma. Every local maximum of $\Phi$ is EFX.

Proof. Suppose a partition is not EFX. Self-comparisons cannot fail, because monotonicity gives $v(S\setminus\{g\})\leq v(S)$. Thus, after naming the bundles appropriately, there is $g\in B$ such that

$$ v(A)<v(B\setminus\{g\}). $$

Monotonicity also gives

$$ v(B)\geq v(B\setminus\{g\})>v(A). $$

Therefore $c(B)>c(A)$ and $\Phi(A,B)=c(A)$.

Transfer $g$ to $A$. Monotonicity and the increase in cardinality imply

$$ c(A\cup\{g\})\geq c(A)+1. $$

Strict order preservation gives

$$ R(v(B\setminus\{g\}))\geq R(v(A))+1. $$

Using $|B\setminus\{g\}|-|A|\geq-k$, we obtain

$$ \begin{aligned} c(B\setminus\{g\})-c(A) &=(k+1)\bigl(R(v(B\setminus\{g\}))-R(v(A))\bigr) +|B\setminus\{g\}|-|A|\\ &\geq(k+1)-k\\ &=1. \end{aligned} $$

Both new bundles therefore have $c$-value strictly above the old minimum. Hence

$$ \Phi(A\cup\{g\},B\setminus\{g\})>\Phi(A,B), $$

contradicting local maximality. $\square$

This proves a reduction to polynomial local search. It also proves existence: the finite state space has a global maximum, which is a local maximum and hence EFX. It does not prove that repeated improving transfers terminate in polynomially many steps.

4.2. Different monotone valuations

Now let the roles have arbitrary monotone valuations $v_1,v_2$.

$$ v_1(A)\geq v_1(B\setminus\{g\}) \quad\text{for every }g\in B, $$

and

$$ v_1(B)\geq v_1(A\setminus\{g\}) \quad\text{for every }g\in A. $$

Give role $2$ whichever of $A,B$ it weakly prefers under $v_2$, and give role $1$ the other bundle.

Role $1$ satisfies EFX by the two displayed conditions, regardless of which bundle remains. If role $2$ receives $C$ and the other bundle is $D$, its choice and monotonicity give

$$ v_2(C)\geq v_2(D)\geq v_2(D\setminus\{g\}) \quad\text{for every }g\in D. $$

Self-comparisons follow from monotonicity. The resulting allocation is therefore EFX, and this final conversion takes polynomial time.

Finally, place the allocation on the single interval $[0,1)$. Thus the one-template, two-role policy problem reduces to a $\mathrm{PLS}$ problem. Combined with Section 3, this restriction is $\mathrm{PLS}$-complete, and every promised instance in the restriction has a valid policy.

$$ w_{q,i}(C)\leq w_{q,i}(D) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_{q,i}(C)\leq v_{q,i}(D). $$

Each valuation operation is implemented by circuit evaluation followed, when necessary, by exact rational subtraction or comparison. These operations have polynomial bit complexity. The finite theorem uses polynomially many such operations, so the total running time is bounded by

6. What remains unsettled

Monotonicity and endpoint locality do not imply cancelability. For example, with two parallel edges $a,b$, the valuation

$$ v(S\cup\{g\})=1>0=v(T\cup\{g\}), \qquad v(S)=v(T)=0, $$

violating cancelability.

Accordingly, the polynomial-time construction in Section 5 cannot be applied to every literally promised input.
