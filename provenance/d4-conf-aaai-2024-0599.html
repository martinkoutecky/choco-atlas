The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), per-capita supplies \(q_j\), and SPLC valuations \(u_t(b)=\sum_j\sum_{r=1}^{b_j}v_{tjr}\) on integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{J}\), define \(\sigma_t(q)\) as the supremum of \(z\) for which a distribution over integral bundles has expected use of each good \(j\) at most \(q_j\) and gives every bundle in its support value at least \(z\). Find finite bundle masses \(\lambda_{t,b}\ge 0\) such that \(\sum_b\lambda_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}\lambda_{t,b}b_j\le q_j\) for each good, and every bundle with positive mass satisfies \(u_t(b)\ge \sigma_t(q)/2\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) kinds of goods, with nonnegative binary rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\). For each good, an explicitly listed finite marginal sequence specifies each type's SPLC valuation: \(v_{tj1}\geq\cdots\geq v_{tjK_j}\geq0\), all binary rationals; set \(v_{tjr}=0\) for \(r>K_j\). Integral bundles are \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\), including the empty bundle, and \(u_t(b)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{r=1}^{b_j}v_{tjr}\).
Define \(\sigma_t(q)=\sup\{z\geq0:\exists\text{ a probability distribution }P\text{ on integral bundles with }\mathbb E_P[b_j]\leq q_j\text{ for every }j,\ P(b)>0\Rightarrow u_t(b)\geq z\}\).
The allocation problem is to output finitely many nonzero binary rational masses \(\lambda_{t,b}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_b\lambda_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}\lambda_{t,b}b_j\leq q_j\), and \(\lambda_{t,b}>0\Rightarrow u_t(b)\geq\sigma_t(q)/2\). Supplies may be left unused. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\), and \(\sum_jK_j\).
Can this \(\tfrac12\)-share allocation be computed in polynomial time for every instance, or is computing such an allocation NP-hard?
For finite societies with SPLC valuations, a \(\tfrac12\)-MMS allocation exists and is computable in polynomial time; the guarantee also holds for symmetric APS ([verified source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08504)). No result settling the precise distribution-over-types problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The infinite marginal sequences had no finite encoding. Use explicitly listed finite sequences, as in the discrete SPLC model, and extend them by zero beyond their listed lengths.
  • Specify nonnegative, nonincreasing binary rational marginal values and nonnegative binary rational supplies, retaining monotonicity and concavity.
  • Retain the graded question's unrestricted integral bundle domain; the marginal-list lengths do not impose additional bundle caps.
  • Require output masses to be binary rationals with finite support, making the computational output concrete.
  • Use m for the number of kinds of goods and L for the total input bit length; type masses describe population frequencies rather than unequal individual entitlements.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The allocation problem is polynomial-time solvable. Let \(N=\sum_{j=1}^{m}K_j\). The construction below runs in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,N)\) bit operations and outputs polynomially many rational masses, each encoded with polynomially many bits.

The key observation is that type-specific lotteries can be combined without exceeding the supplies. The main work is therefore to construct, for each type, a lottery guaranteeing half its benchmark.

1. Reduction to a single type

Suppose that, for every type \(t\), we construct a finitely supported rational probability distribution \(P_t\) satisfying

\(\mathbb E_{P_t}[b_j]\leq q_j\) for every good \(j\),

and

\(P_t(b)>0\Longrightarrow u_t(b)\geq \sigma_t(q)/2.\)

Define \(\lambda_{t,b}=\mu_tP_t(b)\), omitting zero entries. Then

\(\sum_b\lambda_{t,b}=\mu_t,\)

and, for every good \(j\),

\(\sum_{t,b}\lambda_{t,b}b_j=\sum_t\mu_t\mathbb E_{P_t}[b_j]\leq\sum_t\mu_tq_j=q_j.\)

The utility guarantee also holds for every positive allocation mass. Thus it suffices to solve the following single-type problem: construct a rational lottery with expected bundle at most \(q\), all of whose supported bundles have utility at least \(\sigma(q)/2\).

Fix one type and suppress its subscript.

2. The benchmark is attained on a finite bundle set

Define

\(\mathcal B=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\{0,1,\ldots,K_j\}.\)

For any integral bundle \(b\), let \(\bar b_j=\min\{b_j,K_j\}\). Because all marginals beyond \(K_j\) are zero,

\(u(\bar b)=u(b)\quad\text{and}\quad \bar b_j\leq b_j.\)

Pushing any lottery forward under this truncation preserves utilities and weakly decreases expected consumption. Consequently, the definition of \(\sigma(q)\) is unchanged if lotteries are restricted to the finite set \(\mathcal B\).

Let \(\mathcal V=\{u(b):b\in\mathcal B\}\). A feasible lottery on \(\mathcal B\) has a minimum supported utility belonging to \(\mathcal V\). Conversely, that minimum is itself a feasible threshold. Since the empty-bundle lottery makes threshold zero feasible, it follows that

\(\sigma(q)=\max\{x\in\mathcal V:\text{some lottery on }\mathcal B\text{ has expected bundle at most }q\text{ and supported utilities at least }x\}.\)

In particular, this maximum is attained. This argument establishes attainment; the algorithm will not enumerate \(\mathcal B\) or compute \(\sigma(q)\) exactly.

For each good, define its prefix utility

\(U_j(k)=\sum_{r=1}^{k}v_{jr},\qquad 0\leq k\leq K_j.\)

Let \(U=\sum_jU_j(K_j)\). Then \(\sigma(q)\leq U\).

If \(U=0\), the empty-bundle lottery solves the problem. Otherwise, let \(\ell\) be the smallest positive listed marginal. Every positive bundle utility is at least \(\ell\). By attainment,

\(\sigma(q)>0\Longrightarrow \ell\leq\sigma(q)\leq U.\)

3. A polynomial-size test for a candidate threshold

Fix a positive rational number \(a\). Set

\(\delta=\frac{a}{4m},\qquad H=3m,\)

and define rounded prefix scores

\(w_j(k)=\min\left\{H,\left\lfloor\frac{U_j(k)}{\delta}\right\rfloor\right\}.\)

Call a bundle \(b\in\mathcal B\) accepted when

\(\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_j(b_j)\geq H.\)

The following two implications provide the approximation guarantee.

Lemma 1: Bundles of utility at least \(a\) are accepted

Suppose \(u(b)\geq a\). If any score \(w_j(b_j)\) equals \(H\), acceptance follows immediately.

Otherwise, none of the scores is capped, so

\(\sum_jw_j(b_j)=\sum_j\left\lfloor U_j(b_j)/\delta\right\rfloor\geq\sum_jU_j(b_j)/\delta-m=u(b)/\delta-m\geq4m-m=H.\)

Thus \(b\) is accepted.

Lemma 2: Accepted bundles have utility at least \(3a/4\)

For every prefix, \(w_j(k)\leq U_j(k)/\delta\). Hence an accepted bundle satisfies

\(u(b)=\sum_jU_j(b_j)\geq\delta\sum_jw_j(b_j)\geq\delta H=3a/4.\)

Together, the lemmas show that the accepted bundles contain every bundle of utility at least \(a\), while every accepted bundle has utility at least \(3a/4\).

A flow formulation for accepted-bundle lotteries

Construct a directed acyclic multigraph with vertices

\((j,s),\qquad 0\leq j\leq m,\quad 0\leq s\leq H.\)

For every good \(j\), score \(s\), and quantity \(k\in\{0,\ldots,K_j\}\), introduce an arc

\((j-1,s)\longrightarrow\bigl(j,\min\{H,s+w_j(k)\}\bigr),\)

labelled with quantity \(k\) of good \(j\). Distinct quantity choices remain distinct arcs even if their endpoints coincide.

The source is \((0,0)\), and the sink is \((m,H)\). A path chooses exactly one quantity for every good. Its final score is \(\min\{H,\sum_jw_j(b_j)\}\). Therefore, source-to-sink paths correspond exactly to accepted bundles.

Introduce a nonnegative flow variable \(f_e\) for every arc. Require unit source-to-sink flow, with conservation at every other vertex. For each good \(j\), additionally require

\(\sum_{e\text{ in layer }j}k(e)f_e\leq q_j,\)

where \(k(e)\) is the quantity labelling arc \(e\).

This rational LP is feasible if and only if an accepted-bundle lottery with expected bundle at most \(q\) exists:

  • A lottery gives a unit flow by assigning each bundle’s probability to its path. The resource expressions are precisely its expected quantities.
  • A feasible flow decomposes into source-to-sink paths because the graph is acyclic. The path weights sum to one and define a lottery with the same expected quantities.

For completeness, decomposition is constructive. Follow positive-flow arcs from the source to the sink, subtract the minimum flow on that path, and repeat. Conservation ensures that a positive-flow path cannot stop at an intermediate vertex. Every subtraction eliminates at least one positive arc, so at most the number of arcs many paths are produced.

The graph has \((m+1)(H+1)=O(m^2)\) vertices and

\((H+1)\sum_j(K_j+1)=O\bigl(m(N+m)\bigr)\)

arcs. Thus exact rational LP feasibility and, when feasible, a rational accepted-bundle lottery can be computed in polynomial time.

Two consequences will be used:

  • If \(a\leq\sigma(q)\), the LP is feasible. Indeed, an attaining lottery from Section 2 uses only bundles of utility at least \(a\), all of which are accepted by Lemma 1.
  • Every lottery extracted from a feasible LP guarantees utility at least \(3a/4\), by Lemma 2.

4. Finding a sufficient threshold without computing the benchmark

Assume \(U>0\). Test all thresholds

\(a_i=\ell(3/2)^i,\qquad i=0,1,\ldots,\quad a_i\leq U.\)

For each threshold, solve the LP from Section 3.

If no LP is feasible, output the empty-bundle lottery. This is correct: if \(\sigma(q)>0\), then \(\ell\leq\sigma(q)\), and the LP for \(a_0=\ell\) would be feasible. Thus the absence of a feasible candidate implies \(\sigma(q)=0\).

Otherwise, choose the largest feasible threshold \(a\) and output its flow-decomposition lottery.

To prove its guarantee, first consider \(\sigma(q)>0\). Let \(a_*\) be the largest grid threshold satisfying \(a_*\leq\sigma(q)\). Such a threshold exists, and maximality gives

\(a_*\leq\sigma(q)<(3/2)a_*.\)

The LP for \(a_*\) is feasible. Consequently, the chosen feasible threshold satisfies \(a\geq a_*\). Every output bundle therefore has utility at least

\(3a/4\geq3a_*/4>\sigma(q)/2.\)

If \(\sigma(q)=0\), the empty-bundle lottery suffices; indeed, no positive candidate can be feasible, since feasibility would itself provide a strictly positive feasible benchmark threshold.

The algorithm tests all candidates and does not assume that LP feasibility is monotone along the grid.

5. Bit complexity and output size

All computations use rationals represented by binary numerators and denominators.

Every positive input marginal is at least \(2^{-L}\), and every input marginal is at most \(2^L\). Hence, when \(U>0\),

\(U/\ell\leq N\,2^{2L}.\)

The number of grid thresholds is therefore \(O(L+\log(N+1))\). Each threshold has \(O(L+\log(N+1))\) bits: its numerator and denominator are obtained from those of \(\ell\) by multiplying by powers of three and two of that order.

Prefix utilities, rounded scores, and graph construction are computable by polynomial-time rational arithmetic. Each feasibility LP has:

  • \(O\bigl(m(N+m)\bigr)\) variables;
  • \(O(m^2+m)\) constraints;
  • coefficient bit length \(O(L+\log(N+m+1))\).

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming applies directly: these are explicitly represented rational linear constraints. It decides feasibility exactly and, when feasible, returns a rational feasible point with polynomial encoding length.

Flow decomposition uses at most \(O\bigl(m(N+m)\bigr)\) paths. Its rational subtractions also have polynomial bit complexity. To see the latter explicitly, put the returned arc flows over the common denominator obtained by multiplying their denominators. This denominator has polynomial bit length; all subsequent path weights and residual flows retain that common denominator.

Performing the construction for all types gives the running-time bound

\(\tau\,O(L+\log(N+1))\cdot\operatorname{poly}\bigl(m(N+m),\,L+\log(N+m+1)\bigr),\)

which is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,N\).

There are at most \(O\bigl(\tau m(N+m)\bigr)\) output entries before merging repeated bundles. Multiplication of each path probability by \(\mu_t\) preserves polynomial encoding length.

6. Conclusion

For each type, the algorithm constructs a finite rational lottery with expected quantities at most \(q\) and supported utilities at least \(\sigma_t(q)/2\). Weighting these lotteries by the type masses yields exactly the required allocation.

Thus the precise distribution-over-types problem in the question admits a polynomial-time algorithm.
