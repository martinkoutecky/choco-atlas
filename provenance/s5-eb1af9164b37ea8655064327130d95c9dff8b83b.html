The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite bipartite graph of worker and firm types with rational masses, rational type-pair capacities and worker quotas, and exact rational piecewise-linear type-level choice maps satisfying the continuous analogues of the paper’s axioms and gapless condition, construct a finite weighted poset and a stable base allocation that represent every stable mass allocation uniquely as \(x=x_{\min}+\sum_{L\in R}\lambda_L d_L\), with \(0\leq\lambda_L\leq\theta_L\) and \(L\prec L'\), \(\lambda_{L'}>0\) implying \(\lambda_L=\theta_L\). Give a running-time bound polynomial in the number of supported types, the rational input bit length, and the choice-map description size or oracle cost.

Each edge has an aggregate capacity \(b_e\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\). Worker \(w\) has a per-unit quota \(q_w\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), aggregate quota \(Q_w=\mu_wq_w\), and a strict linear order on incident edges \(E_w\). Its choice map \(C_w\) greedily retains offered quantities in this order up to \(Q_w\). Firms have no separate quota constraints.
Write \(B_v=\prod_{e\in E_v}[0,b_e]\). Each firm choice map \(C_f:B_f\to B_f\) acts on aggregate quantities and is continuous, given explicitly by a finite rational polyhedral subdivision and rational affine formulas agreeing on overlaps. Let \(D\) be the description size and \(L\) the total binary input length.
For every firm, \(0\le C_f(z)\le z\); consistency requires \(z\ge z'\ge C_f(z)\Rightarrow C_f(z')=C_f(z)\); substitutability requires \(z\ge z'\Rightarrow C_f(z)\wedge z'\le C_f(z')\); size monotonicity requires \(z\ge z'\Rightarrow\sum_e C_f(z)_e\ge\sum_e C_f(z')_e\). Inequalities and \(\wedge,\vee\) are coordinatewise.
A local vector \(z\) is acceptable if \(C_v(z)=z\). For distinct acceptable vectors, define \(z\prec_v z'\) by \(C_v(z\vee z')=z'\). A pair of distinct edges \((a,c)\) is a tandem at acceptable \(z\) if some \(\delta>0\) satisfies \(z+t\mathbf e_a\in B_f\) and \(C_f(z+t\mathbf e_a)=z+t\mathbf e_a-t\mathbf e_c\) for every \(0<t\le\delta\), where \(\mathbf e_a\) is the coordinate unit vector.
Assume gaplessness: whenever \(z_1\prec_f z_2\prec_f z_3\), and \((a,c_i)\) is a tandem at \(z_i\) for \(i=1,2,3\), then \(c_1=c_3\Rightarrow c_1=c_2\).
An allocation is any real vector \(x\in\prod_{e\in E}[0,b_e]\) with \(C_v(x_v)=x_v\) for every vertex. Edge \(e=wf\) blocks it if, for each \(v\in\{w,f\}\), there exists \(t_v>0\) with \(x_v+t_v\mathbf e_e\in B_v\) and \(C_v(x_v+t_v\mathbf e_e)_e>x_e\). Stability means absence of blocking edges.
The representation problem asks for a finite poset \((R,\prec)\), positive rational weights \(\theta_L\), rational directions \(d_L\in\mathbb Q^E\), and a stable base \(x_{\min}\) least under firm revealed preferences. 1. Preliminary facts about choice maps

All maps below act on capacity boxes and satisfy the three choice axioms in the question.

The worker maps satisfy these hypotheses. Indeed, for an incident edge $e$,
$$ C_w(z)_e = \min\left\{ z_e,\, \max\left\{Q_w-\sum_{g>_w e}z_g,0\right\} \right\}. $$
This formula establishes continuity and substitutability: decreasing offers weakly increases the residual quota available at each edge. The total chosen quantity is
$$ \sum_e C_w(z)_e=\min\left\{Q_w,\sum_e z_e\right\}, $$
which establishes size monotonicity.

For consistency, greedy choice retains an initial sequence of offers completely, possibly retains one further offer partially, and rejects the remaining offers. Reducing offers while keeping them above their chosen quantities leaves the completely retained offers unchanged and leaves enough of the partially retained offer to exhaust the same residual quota. Thus the chosen vector remains unchanged.

Lemma 1: the single-coordinate cutoff

Let $C:B\to B$ be continuous and consistent, with $0\le C(z)\le z$. Fix all offered coordinates except $e$ at $u$. Write $y(s)=(s,u)$, with the first coordinate corresponding to $e$, and define
$$ h(s)=C(y(s))_e, \qquad k=h(b_e). $$
Then
$$ h(s)=\min\{s,k\} \qquad (0\le s\le b_e). $$

Proof. If $s\ge k$, then
$$ y(b_e)\ge y(s)\ge C(y(b_e)). $$
Consistency gives $C(y(s))=C(y(b_e))$, so $h(s)=k$.

Suppose $s<k$. Since choice is bounded by the offer, $h(s)\le s$. If $h(s)<s$, choose
$$ h(s)<\alpha<s. $$
Continuity and $h(b_e)=k>\alpha$ give an $r\in(s,b_e)$ with $h(r)=\alpha$. Every other coordinate of $C(y(r))$ is bounded by its unchanged offer, while its $e$ coordinate is less than $s$. Hence
$$ y(r)\ge y(s)\ge C(y(r)). $$
Consistency gives $h(s)=h(r)=\alpha$, a contradiction. Thus $h(s)=s$. The case $b_e=0$ is immediate. $\square$

Define
$$ \kappa_{v,e}(x) = C_v\bigl(x_v+(b_e-x_e)\mathbf e_e\bigr)_e. $$
The argument of this expression replaces the offer on $e$ by its capacity; therefore $\kappa_{v,e}(x)$ depends only on the other coordinates of $x_v$.

At an acceptable allocation, Lemma 1 gives $x_e\le\kappa_{v,e}(x)$. Endpoint $v$ is willing to increase $e$ exactly when
$$ \kappa_{v,e}(x)>x_e. $$
Indeed, a positive increment no larger than the difference is retained, whereas equality makes every feasible increment on that coordinate ineffective.

Consequently, an acceptable allocation is stable exactly when
$$ x_e=\min\{\kappa_{w,e}(x),\kappa_{f,e}(x)\} \qquad(e=wf). $$
This also gives an exact stability test using offers at capacity, without infinitesimal computations.

Lemma 2: fixing offers and projecting preserves the axioms

Partition the coordinates of a choice map $C$ into $A$ and $S$. Fix any feasible offer vector $p$ on $A$, and define
$$ D(z)=C(p,z)_S. $$
Then $D$ is continuous, bounded between zero and its offer, and satisfies consistency, substitutability, and size monotonicity.

Proof. Continuity and the bounds follow directly from those of $C$.

For consistency, suppose $z\ge z'\ge D(z)$. The fixed coordinates satisfy $p\ge C(p,z)_A$, so
$$ (p,z)\ge(p,z')\ge C(p,z). $$
Consistency of $C$ gives $D(z')=D(z)$.

Substitutability follows by applying substitutability of $C$ to $(p,z)\ge(p,z')$ and restricting to $S$.

For size monotonicity, substitutability also gives
$$ C(p,z)_A\le C(p,z')_A \qquad(z\ge z'), $$
because the offers on $A$ are unchanged. Therefore
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{i\in S}D(z)_i-\sum_{i\in S}D(z')_i &= \left(\sum_i C(p,z)_i-\sum_i C(p,z')_i\right)\\ &\quad+ \sum_{i\in A}\left(C(p,z')_i-C(p,z)_i\right) \ge0. \end{aligned} $$
Both terms are nonnegative by the respective axioms. $\square$

When $C$ is rational piecewise affine, so is $D$. For evaluation, its subdivision need not be constructed: evaluate $C$ at $(p,z)$ and project.

2. A leaf-elimination bijection

Consider a graph with a leaf $u$, incident to the single edge $e=uv$. This statement allows general choice maps at both endpoints.

By Lemma 1, the leaf’s scalar choice has the form
$$ C_u(s)=\min\{s,a\}, \qquad a=C_u(b_e). $$
Let $S=E_v\setminus\{e\}$. Delete $u$ and $e$, and replace the choice at $v$ by
$$ D_v(z)=C_v(a,z)_S. $$
All other choices remain unchanged. Lemma 2 shows that the reduced instance retains the required axioms.

Theorem 3: exact correspondence under leaf deletion

Restriction to the surviving edges is a bijection between the stable allocations of the original and reduced instances. The inverse extends a reduced stable allocation $y$ by
$$ x_e=C_v(a,y_S)_e. $$

Proof.

First, let $x$ be stable in the original instance, and put $h=x_e$. Leaf acceptability gives $h\le a$. Applying the cutoff characterization of stability to $e$ yields
$$ h=\min\{a,\kappa_{v,e}(x)\}. $$
Lemma 1, with the other offers fixed at $x_S$, therefore gives
$$ C_v(a,x_S)_e=h. $$

Original acceptability says $C_v(h,x_S)=(h,x_S)$. Size monotonicity, applied when the offer on $e$ is increased from $h$ to $a$, gives
$$ \sum_i C_v(a,x_S)_i \ge h+\sum_{i\in S}x_i. $$
Its $e$ coordinate is $h$, and every other output coordinate is at most the corresponding $x_i$. Thus equality holds coordinatewise:
$$ C_v(a,x_S)=(h,x_S). $$
In particular, the restriction is acceptable for $D_v$.

We next verify that willingness on surviving edges is preserved. Fix $j\in S$, and let
$$ x_S^j=x_S+(b_j-x_j)\mathbf e_j. $$
Substitutability gives
$$ C_v(a,x_S^j)_e\le C_v(a,x_S)_e=h. $$
All its other coordinates are bounded by $x_S^j$. Hence
$$ (a,x_S^j)\ge(h,x_S^j)\ge C_v(a,x_S^j). $$
Consistency gives
$$ C_v(a,x_S^j)=C_v(h,x_S^j). $$
Thus the original and reduced choices give the same output on $j$ at its offer at capacity. Lemma 1 shows that their willingness to increase $j$ agrees. Consequently, the restricted allocation is stable.

Conversely, let $y$ be stable in the reduced instance, and define
$$ h=C_v(a,y_S)_e. $$
Reduced acceptability gives
$$ C_v(a,y_S)=(h,y_S). $$
Since $0\le h\le a$, consistency implies
$$ C_v(h,y_S)=(h,y_S). $$
The extension is therefore acceptable at $v$ and at the leaf.

Lemma 1 gives
$$ h=\min\{a,\kappa_{v,e}(h,y_S)\}. $$
If $h=a$, the leaf refuses an increase. Otherwise the neighbor’s cutoff equals $h$, so the neighbor refuses. Thus $e$ does not block.

The preceding substitutability and consistency argument applies again to every surviving edge at $v$, proving that its willingness agrees with that in the reduced instance. All other endpoint choices are unchanged. Therefore the extension is stable.

The displayed formula determines the removed coordinate uniquely, proving bijectivity. $\square$

3. Polynomial-time solution on forests

Theorem 4: uniqueness and construction

Every forest instance has exactly one stable allocation. For the rational input model in the question, this allocation is computable in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,D$.

Proof of uniqueness and existence. Repeatedly delete a leaf using Theorem 3. Eventually the graph has no edges. Its empty allocation is uniquely stable. Reversing the deletions gives a unique stable allocation at every preceding stage.

Algorithm. During deletion, store each removed edge and its threshold $a$. A residual choice at a vertex always has the form
$$ z\longmapsto C_v(p,z)_{\text{remaining coordinates}}, $$
where $p$ records fixed offers on previously deleted incident edges. Thus each leaf threshold is obtained by one evaluation of the original choice map, using capacity on the leaf’s remaining edge.

During reconstruction, each removed quantity is obtained by one further original-choice evaluation, as in Theorem 3. There are at most $2m$ such evaluations.

An explicitly supplied firm map can be evaluated by testing membership in its rational polyhedral cells and applying a containing cell’s affine formula. Agreement on overlaps removes ambiguity. Worker maps are evaluated directly by their greedy formulas.

For completeness, the rational bit lengths remain polynomial. After computing the aggregate quotas, let $M$ be the product of the positive denominators of all rational input constants and affine coefficients. Its binary length is polynomial in the input length. All initial quantities have denominators dividing $M$. An affine evaluation whose arguments have denominators dividing $M^k$ produces outputs with denominators dividing $M^{k+1}$. Worker evaluation has the same property.

There are at most $2m$ sequential evaluations, so all stored outputs have denominators dividing $M^{2m+1}$. Their values lie within the input capacity bounds; hence their numerators also have polynomial binary length. Cell-membership tests and intermediate arithmetic consequently have polynomial cost. $\square$

The unique allocation is automatically least under firm revealed preferences. Taking
$$ R=\varnothing $$

Every stable allocation $x$ of the original instance satisfies
$$ x=F(x_H). $$

$$ D_v^p(x_{K\cap E_v})=x_{K\cap E_v}. $$

The map $F$ is continuous and rational piecewise affine on its entire parameter box.

These inverse images are rational polyhedra, and the composed formula is rational affine on each resulting piece. The forest algorithm uses finitely many compositions. $\square$

Let
$$ P=\{p:F(p)\text{ is stable in the original instance}\}. $$
Then Theorem 5 gives
$$ \mathcal S=F(P), $$
where $\mathcal S$ denotes the stable set.

The set $P$ is a finite union of rational polytopes. Indeed, acceptability is expressed by the equalities
$$ C_v(F(p)_v)=F(p)_v. $$
Subject to these equalities, stability on each edge of $H$ is the disjunction that at least one endpoint’s output at the offer at capacity equals $p_e$. All the functions involved are continuous rational piecewise affine. Refining their finitely many domains makes each selected equality linear. Selecting a refusing endpoint on each edge gives a finite union of rational polyhedra, bounded by the parameter box.

6. A necessary width bound for the requested poset

The preceding uniqueness property constrains every possible representation of the form requested in the question.

Proposition 7: width is at most the number of cycle edges

$$ \{(d_L)_H:L\in A\} $$
are linearly independent. In particular,
$$ \operatorname{width}(R)\le |H|, $$
and hence $\operatorname{width}(R)\le\beta$ for a spanning-forest complement.

Proof. Define the strict predecessor ideal
$$ I=\{L\in R:L\prec L'\text{ for some }L'\in A\}. $$
Because $A$ is an antichain, $I\cap A=\varnothing$.

$$ \sum_{L\in A}u_L(d_L)_H=0. $$
