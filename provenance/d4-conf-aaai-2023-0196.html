The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational mass vector \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) complete agent types, a finite anonymous mechanism with rational piecewise-polynomial aggregate transition and outcome rules, type utilities, and target per-type lotteries \(F_t\), does there exist a memoryless strategy kernel \(x_t(v)\in\Delta(A)\) that is a Bayesian best response for every positive-mass type, induces \(F_t\), and gives every such type nonnegative interim utility? A unilateral deviation has zero effect on the aggregate action masses \(q_{t,a}(v)=\mu_t x_t(v,a)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) complete types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; the masses are the entire population specification, with no additional random population composition.
The input contains nonempty finite sets of observable states \(V\), actions \(A\), and individual alternatives \(B\), an initial state \(v_0\), nonempty legal-action sets \(A(v)\), and absorbing terminal states, each labelled by exactly one alternative. Write \(n=|V|\), \(k=|A|\), and \(m=|B|\).
An anonymous mechanism specifies representative-agent transition kernels \(K_t(v,a,q)\in\Delta(V)\), depending on the type, the individual's legal action, and the aggregate action masses at that state. These kernels include the individual's outcome rule through terminal labels; a deviation can change the individual's transitions and outcome while leaving the aggregate field fixed.
Each kernel entry is a rational piecewise-polynomial function on \(q_{t,a}\geq0\), \(\sum_{a\in A(v)}q_{t,a}=\mu_t\), with illegal-action coordinates zero. Pieces are explicitly listed using polynomial inequality guards and polynomial monomial lists with binary coefficients and exponents; the first matching guard applies, and guards cover the domain. The input is promised to define probability distributions and to make every supported path from \(v_0\) reach a terminal state under every admissible aggregate field and legal strategy.
Each type has signed binary-rational quasi-linear utility \(u_t(b)=w_t(c_b)-p_b\), where \(b=(c_b,p_b)\), with signed binary-rational valuations and payments, and a target binary-rational lottery \(F_t\in\Delta(B)\). The outside-option utility is zero.
A candidate consists of real-valued memoryless kernels \(x_t(v)\in\Delta(A(v))\), defining \(q_{t,a}(v)=\mu_t x_t(v,a)\). For any legal memoryless deviation \(y\), let \(D_t(y;q)\) be the terminal-label lottery from \(v_0\) in the Markov chain with transition \(\sum_a y(v,a)K_t(v,a,q(v))\), and put \(U_t(y;q)=\sum_bD_t(y;q)(b)u_t(b)\).
Feasibility requires, for every supported type, \(D_t(x_t;q)=F_t\), \(U_t(x_t;q)\geq0\), and \(U_t(x_t;q)\geq U_t(y;q)\) for every legal memoryless \(y\); the entire aggregate field \(q\) stays fixed during a unilateral deviation. These comparisons are interim comparisons from the initial state.

Is this feasibility problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,n,k\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length, including all polynomial descriptions.

For finite-agent mechanisms, equilibrium implementation together with interim individual rationality has a [Probabilistic Strategy Logic characterization](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/26373/26145); this is not a polynomial-time or hardness classification.
[Memoryless PSL model checking](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0005.pdf) is decidable in space exponential in the system size and triple-exponential in the formula size. No result settling the exact mass feasibility problem above was located.

Result

The feasibility problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(\tau=1\), \(m=2\), \(n=3\), every run terminates after one transition, and every kernel entry is a single polynomial of degree at most six.

Thus a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,n,k\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

For the general promised problem, an existential-real formulation gives a deterministic decision algorithm with running time

\(\displaystyle 2^{\left(L+\tau n^3(m+k+1)\right)^{O(1)}}\)

and space polynomial in \(L+\tau n^3(m+k+1)\). The hardness proof and the upper bound follow.

1. Reduction from Boolean satisfiability

Let \(\Phi\) be a Boolean formula in conjunctive normal form with variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_d\) and clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_s\), each containing three literals. We reduce from \(\mathrm{3SAT}\), restricting to \(d\geq1\).

For a literal \(\ell\), define its *falsity polynomial* by

\(\displaystyle f_\ell(z)= \begin{cases} 1-z_i,&\ell=z_i,\\ z_i,&\ell=\neg z_i. \end{cases}\)

On Boolean inputs, \(f_\ell(z)\) equals one exactly when \(\ell\) is false. Define

\(\displaystyle R(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}z_i^2(1-z_i)^2+ \sum_{j=1}^{s}\left(\prod_{\ell\in C_j}f_\ell(z)\right)^2.\)

Every summand is a square. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle R(z)=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z\in\{0,1\}^{d}\text{ and }z\text{ satisfies }\Phi.\)

Indeed, vanishing of the first sum forces each \(z_i\) to be zero or one. On such inputs, a clause summand vanishes exactly when at least one of its literals is true.

Encoding the variables in aggregate masses

Use one supported type, of mass \(\mu_1=1\), and actions \(a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_d\). At the initial state all actions are legal. Write \(q_i=q_{1,a_i}(v_0)\), and substitute

\(\displaystyle z_i=dq_i\qquad(1\leq i\leq d).\)

The aggregate domain is the simplex

\(\displaystyle q_i\geq0,\qquad \sum_{i=0}^{d}q_i=1.\)

Every Boolean vector \(\zeta\in\{0,1\}^{d}\) has a corresponding admissible aggregate vector:

\(\displaystyle q_i=\frac{\zeta_i}{d}\quad(1\leq i\leq d),\qquad q_0=1-\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\zeta_i.\)

The last coordinate is nonnegative because \(\sum_i\zeta_i\leq d\).

Making the polynomial a valid probability

Throughout the aggregate simplex, \(0\leq z_i\leq d\). Since \(d\geq1\), both \(|z_i|\) and \(|1-z_i|\) are at most \(d\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle 0\leq R(z)\leq d^5+sd^6.\)

Choose a power of two \(C\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle C\geq d^5+sd^6.\)

Its binary length is \(O(\log(d+s+2))\). Define

\(\displaystyle p(q)=\frac{R(dq_1,\ldots,dq_d)}{C}.\)

Then \(0\leq p(q)\leq1\) everywhere on the aggregate domain.

The mechanism

There are three states: the initial state \(v_0\) and two absorbing terminals \(v_G,v_H\). Their labels are two distinct alternatives \(G,H\).

For every initial action \(a\), set

\(\displaystyle K_1(v_0,a,q)(v_G)=1-p(q),\qquad K_1(v_0,a,q)(v_H)=p(q),\qquad K_1(v_0,a,q)(v_0)=0.\)

At each terminal, allow only \(a_0\), with a deterministic self-loop.

Give both alternatives payment zero and valuation zero. Thus

\(\displaystyle u_1(G)=u_1(H)=0.\)

Set the target lottery to \(F_1=\delta_G\).

This instance satisfies every promise:

  • Its sole supported mass is positive and normalized.
  • All transition rows are probability distributions throughout their domains.
  • Every supported run reaches a terminal after one transition.
  • All coefficients are binary-rational; each polynomial has degree at most six.
  • Each entry needs only one piece, with an always-true guard.

2. Correctness and size of the reduction

For any candidate \(x_1\), its aggregate field at the initial state is \(q=x_1(v_0)\). Because the initial transition row is identical for every action,

\(\displaystyle D_1(x_1;q)(H)=p(q).\)

All utilities are zero. Hence individual rationality holds, and every legal deviation has exactly the same utility, zero, with the aggregate field fixed. Feasibility therefore reduces to the target-lottery condition:

\(\displaystyle \text{feasible} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p(q)=0\text{ for some admissible }q. \)

If \(\Phi\) is satisfiable, the aggregate vector constructed from a satisfying Boolean assignment gives \(p(q)=0\), and hence a feasible candidate.

Conversely, if a feasible candidate exists, then \(R(dq_1,\ldots,dq_d)=0\). The sum-of-squares argument proves that \((dq_1,\ldots,dq_d)\) is a Boolean satisfying assignment for \(\Phi\).

Thus

\(\displaystyle \Phi\text{ is satisfiable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed instance is feasible}.\)

For the input-size check, each variable summand has three monomials. Each clause summand is a product of three squared affine factors, so expansion produces at most \(27\) monomials. Coefficient lengths are polynomially bounded, and substituting \(z_i=dq_i\) does not increase the degree. Repeating these descriptions for the \(d+1\) actions still produces polynomial total length, even if exponent vectors are written densely.

The reduction is therefore computable in polynomial time. Its parameters are

\(\displaystyle m=2,\qquad \tau=1,\qquad n=3,\qquad k=d+1,\qquad L=\operatorname{poly}(|\Phi|).\)

This proves the claimed \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

3. A finite-horizon consequence of the termination promise

For the upper bound, use the stated promise that every supported path reaches a terminal.

Fix a type \(t\) and an admissible aggregate field \(q\). Put an edge from \(v\) to \(w\) whenever

\(\displaystyle K_t(v,a,q(v))(w)>0\)

for some legal action \(a\). Consider the subgraph reachable from \(v_0\).

This subgraph has no directed cycle consisting of nonterminal states. To prove this, choose a memoryless strategy assigning positive probability to every legal action at every state. Every graph edge then has positive transition probability. A reachable nonterminal cycle would yield an infinite supported path that repeatedly traverses the cycle, contradicting the promise.

Therefore every supported path, under any strategy, reaches a terminal within \(n\) transitions. This conclusion concerns pathwise termination; almost-sure termination alone would not justify it.

Moreover, for this fixed \(q\), an optimal terminal-utility strategy can be chosen deterministic and memoryless. On the reachable nonterminal graph, reverse topological induction defines the value of a state as the maximum, over its legal actions, of the expected successor value. Choosing a maximizing action at each state attains these values. The same induction bounds the utility of every history-dependent strategy.

4. Existential-real encoding

Introduce candidate variables \(x_t(v,a)\), enforcing the legal-action simplex constraints, and impose

\(\displaystyle q_{t,a}(v)=\mu_t x_t(v,a).\)

Introduce transition variables \(\kappa_{t,v,a,w}\) equal to the specified kernel entries evaluated at \(q(v)\).

For an entry whose listed guards and polynomials are \(G_i,p_i\), first-match semantics are expressed by

\(\displaystyle \bigvee_i\left( G_i(q(v))\land \bigwedge_{j<i}\neg G_j(q(v)) \land \kappa_{t,v,a,w}=p_i(q(v)) \right). \)

This is a Boolean combination of polynomial conditions. Guard coverage is part of the input promise.

Candidate outcome probabilities

Set

\(\displaystyle P_t(v,w)=\sum_{a\in A(v)} x_t(v,a)\kappa_{t,v,a,w}.\)

Introduce \(g_{t,h,v,b}\) for horizons \(h\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\). If \(v\) is terminal with label \(\lambda(v)\), impose

\(\displaystyle g_{t,h,v,b}=\mathbf{1}_{\lambda(v)=b}\)

for every horizon. At a nonterminal state impose

\(\displaystyle g_{t,0,v,b}=0,\qquad g_{t,h,v,b}=\sum_w P_t(v,w)g_{t,h-1,w,b}\quad(h\geq1).\)

Induction on \(h\) shows that these are the probabilities of reaching each terminal label within \(h\) transitions. By the preceding termination bound,

\(\displaystyle g_{t,n,v_0,b}=D_t(x_t;q)(b).\)

Require \(g_{t,n,v_0,b}=F_t(b)\).

Incentive certificates

Write the prescribed target utility as

\(\displaystyle f_t=\sum_b F_t(b)u_t(b).\)

Reject immediately if any \(f_t<0\).

Introduce real variables \(W_{t,h,v}\). At terminals require

\(\displaystyle W_{t,h,v}=u_t(\lambda(v)).\)

At nonterminals require \(W_{t,0,v}=0\), and for every legal action and every positive horizon impose

\(\displaystyle W_{t,h,v}\geq \sum_w\kappa_{t,v,a,w}W_{t,h-1,w}. \)

Finally impose

\(\displaystyle W_{t,n,v_0}\leq f_t.\)

These constraints are sufficient: induction on the horizon shows that \(W_{t,h,v}\) bounds the expected terminal utility obtainable within that horizon under any strategy, assigning utility zero if termination has not yet occurred. Every deviation from \(v_0\) terminates within \(n\) transitions, so

\(\displaystyle U_t(y;q)\leq W_{t,n,v_0}\leq f_t=U_t(x_t;q).\)

They are also necessary. Given a feasible candidate, choose the \(W\)-variables to be the exact finite-horizon optimal values. They satisfy the displayed inequalities. At \(v_0\), the horizon-\(n\) optimum equals the optimum attained by a deterministic memoryless strategy, by Section 3. Feasibility therefore implies \(W_{t,n,v_0}\leq f_t\).

The resulting existential formula is consequently equivalent to feasibility on promised inputs.

5. Running-time bound and scope

Binary-encoded exponents cause no exponential expansion: compute each power by repeated squaring and introduce a variable for each arithmetic gate. Gate equations have degree at most two, and their number is polynomial in the original description length.

The outcome recurrences use \(O(\tau n^3m)\) arithmetic operations; the incentive inequalities use \(O(\tau n^3k)\). Kernel evaluation, guard selection, and input constants add polynomial overhead in \(L\). Hence, with

\(\displaystyle S=L+\tau n^3(m+k+1),\)

the existential-real sentence has length \(S^{O(1)}\).

The existential theory of the reals is decidable in polynomial space, by [Canny’s theorem](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1988/6041.html). Its hypotheses apply here: all variables are real, all quantifiers are existential, and the remaining formula is a finite Boolean combination of rational polynomial equalities and inequalities. Applying that theorem gives polynomial space in \(S\), and therefore a deterministic time bound \(2^{S^{O(1)}}\).

The requested classification is thus settled as \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The argument does not assert membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).
