The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two oceanic weighted voting games with the same number \(m\) of named major players, a designated player \(i\), rational major-player weights and quota, and an atomless homogeneous ocean of rational total weight \(A>0\) in each game, decide whether \(\Phi_i(G')>\Phi_i(G'')\). For each game, \(\Phi_i\) is the limiting Shapley–Shubik power under random arrival priorities: \(\Phi_i=\sum_{S\subseteq[m]\setminus\{i\}}\int_{\ell_{i,S}}^{u_{i,S}} t^{|S|}(1-t)^{m-1-|S|}\,dt\), where \(\ell_{i,S}=\max(0,(q-w(S)-w_i)/A)\), \(u_{i,S}=\min(1,(q-w(S))/A)\), and empty intervals contribute zero.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An input consists of two oceanic weighted voting games \(G',G''\), each with the same named major players \([m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), where \(m\geq1\), and a designated major player \(i\in[m]\). Each game \(G=(w_1,\ldots,w_m;q;A)\) has nonnegative rational weights and quota, and an atomless homogeneous ocean of total voting weight \(A>0\); the two games may have different numerical data. Coalitions win when their total weight is at least \(q\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here the ocean has one type, \(\tau=1\), of mass \(A\) measured in voting-weight units; the named atomic players are listed separately. Masses and weights need not sum to one. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
For each game define \(w(S)=\sum_{j\in S}w_j\), \(\ell_{i,S}=\max\{0,(q-w(S)-w_i)/A\}\), and \(u_{i,S}=\min\{1,(q-w(S))/A\}\).
The designated player's limiting Shapley–Shubik power under independent uniform arrival priorities is \(\Phi_i(G)=\sum_{S\subseteq[m]\setminus\{i\}}\int_{\ell_{i,S}}^{u_{i,S}}t^{|S|}(1-t)^{m-1-|S|}\,dt\), with a term defined as zero whenever \(\ell_{i,S}\geq u_{i,S}\).

Is deciding whether \(\Phi_i(G')>\Phi_i(G'')\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions?

For finite weighted voting games with nonnegative integer weights, strict comparison of a designated player's Shapley–Shubik power in two games with the same number of players is \(\mathrm{PP}\)-complete. [Faliszewski–Hemaspaandra](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.4585)
For oceanic games, Leech gives the corresponding subset-integral formula and evaluation by enumerating subsets of major players; this does not classify the comparison problem above. No complexity classification of this exact comparison problem was located. [Leech](https://www.lse.ac.uk/cpnss/assets/documents/voting-power-and-procedures/publications/2002/leech2.pdf)

Result

The comparison problem is \(\mathrm{PP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. In particular, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, already when both oceans have the fixed mass \(A=\tfrac12\) and support size \(\tau=1\).

The hardness reduction preserves power values exactly; no approximation or limiting argument is needed.

1. Exact embedding of finite games

Let \(H=(w_1,\ldots,w_m;q)\) be a finite weighted voting game with nonnegative integer weights and integer quota. Construct the oceanic game
\[ G_H=(w_1,\ldots,w_m;q;\tfrac12). \]
Thus the named major players retain their weights, and the ocean consists of one homogeneous type of positive mass.

Fix the designated player \(i\), and a subset \(S\subseteq[m]\setminus\{i\}\). Write \(s=w(S)\) and \(k=|S|\). Because the weights are nonnegative, precisely one of the following three cases occurs.

  • The coalition is already winning: \(s\geq q\). Then \(u_{i,S}\leq0\), whereas \(\ell_{i,S}\geq0\). Its integral contribution is therefore zero.
  • Adding \(i\) changes a losing coalition into a winning coalition: \(s<q\leq s+w_i\). Integrality gives \(q-s\geq1\). Consequently,

\[ \ell_{i,S}=\max\{0,2(q-s-w_i)\}=0, \qquad u_{i,S}=\min\{1,2(q-s)\}=1. \]
Its contribution is
\[ \int_0^1t^k(1-t)^{m-1-k}\,dt =\frac{k!(m-1-k)!}{m!}. \]
The identity follows by repeated integration by parts: for nonnegative integers \(k,h\), the integrals satisfy
\[ I(k,h)=\frac{k}{h+1}I(k-1,h+1) \quad(k>0), \qquad I(0,h)=\frac1{h+1}. \]

  • The coalition remains losing after adding \(i\): \(s+w_i<q\). Integrality gives \(q-s-w_i\geq1\), so \(\ell_{i,S}\geq2>u_{i,S}\). Its contribution is zero.

It follows directly from the given subset-integral formula that
\[ \Phi_i(G_H) = \sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[m]\setminus\{i\}\\ w(S)<q\leq w(S)+w_i}} \frac{|S|!(m-1-|S|)!}{m!}. \]

This sum is exactly the finite Shapley–Shubik power \(\operatorname{SS}_i(H)\). Indeed, for a fixed predecessor set \(S\), precisely \(|S|!(m-1-|S|)!\) permutations put the members of \(S\) before \(i\) and all other players after \(i\); player \(i\) is pivotal exactly under the displayed swing condition. Hence
\[ \boxed{\Phi_i(G_H)=\operatorname{SS}_i(H).} \]

The argument also covers zero weights and \(m=1\).

2. Polynomial-time many-one hardness reduction

The finite comparison problem is \(\mathrm{PP}\)-complete: its inputs are two integer weighted voting games with the same number of players and a common designated index, and it asks whether the first designated power strictly exceeds the second. These are precisely Definition 1.1 and Theorem 2.1 of [Faliszewski–Hemaspaandra](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.4585).

Map a finite comparison instance \((H',H'',i)\) to
\[ (G_{H'},G_{H''},i), \]
using the construction above. Both outputs have the same named major players \([m]\), nonnegative rational numerical data, and one ocean type of mass \(\tfrac12>0\). Thus every output satisfies the task’s hypotheses.

The exact embedding proves
\[ \operatorname{SS}_i(H')>\operatorname{SS}_i(H'') \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Phi_i(G_{H'})>\Phi_i(G_{H''}). \]

The construction copies the finite inputs and adds two constant-length ocean descriptions. Its time and output length are \(O(m+L_{\mathrm{fin}})\), where \(L_{\mathrm{fin}}\) is the source input length; the output has \(\tau=1\). This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction, establishing \(\mathrm{PP}\)-hardness and therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

3. Membership in \(\mathrm{PP}\) for rational inputs

We now prove the matching upper bound without enumerating all subsets.

For one input game, let \(B\) be the product of the denominators in its rational weights, quota, and ocean mass. Define integers
\[ W_j=Bw_j,\qquad Q=Bq,\qquad a=BA. \]
Since \(A>0\), \(a\) is a positive integer. All these integers have \(O(L)\) bits: the bit length of a product is bounded by the sum of its factors’ bit lengths, up to an additive constant per factor.

Set
\[ D=m!\,a^m. \]
Its bit length is \(O(mL+m\log(m+1))\).

For a subset \(S\), write \(k=|S|\), \(W(S)=\sum_{j\in S}W_j\), and define integer endpoint numerators
\[ c=\max\{0,Q-W(S)-W_i\}, \qquad b=\min\{a,Q-W(S)\}. \]
The given endpoints are exactly \(c/a\) and \(b/a\). Let \(J_S\) denote the integral contribution, including its stipulated zero value when \(c\geq b\).

If \(c<b\), then \(0\leq c<b\leq a\). Expanding the integrand gives
\[ J_S= \sum_{r=0}^{m-1-k} (-1)^r\binom{m-1-k}{r} \frac{b^{k+r+1}-c^{k+r+1}} {(k+r+1)a^{k+r+1}}. \]
Consequently, \(N_S=DJ_S\) is an integer, because its summands are
\[ (-1)^r\binom{m-1-k}{r} \frac{m!}{k+r+1} \bigl(b^{k+r+1}-c^{k+r+1}\bigr) a^{m-k-r-1}, \]
and every integer \(k+r+1\) appearing here lies between \(1\) and \(m\), hence divides \(m!\).

If \(c\geq b\), set \(N_S=0\). In every case,
\[ 0\leq N_S\leq D. \]
Indeed, a nonzero \(J_S\) integrates a function bounded between zero and one over a subinterval of \([0,1]\).

For a specified \(S\), \(N_S\) is computable exactly in time \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\): there are at most \(m\) summands, their exponents are at most \(m\), and all intermediate integers have polynomial bit length.

For the two games, use superscripts to distinguish \(D',D''\) and \(N'_S,N''_S\), and put
\[ H=D'D''. \]
Choose an integer \(r\geq1\) such that \(2^r>H\), with \(r\) polynomially bounded in the input length. Consider this randomized algorithm:

  • Choose a fair bit \(e\), a uniformly random subset \(S\subseteq[m]\setminus\{i\}\), and a uniformly random integer \(z\in\{0,\ldots,2^r-1\}\).
  • If \(e=0\), accept exactly when \(z<N'_SD''\).
  • If \(e=1\), accept exactly when \(z\geq N''_SD'\).

All thresholds lie in \([0,H]\), so the number of accepting random strings is exactly
\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_S\bigl(N'_SD''+2^r-N''_SD'\bigr) &=2^{m-1+r} +D''\sum_SN'_S-D'\sum_SN''_S\\ &=2^{m-1+r} +H\bigl(\Phi_i(G')-\Phi_i(G'')\bigr). \end{aligned} \]
There are \(2^{m+r}\) random strings in total. Acceptance probability therefore strictly exceeds one half exactly when \(\Phi_i(G')>\Phi_i(G'')\). The algorithm runs in polynomial time on every random string, proving membership in \(\mathrm{PP}\).

4. Running times and conclusion

The comparison problem is thus \(\mathrm{PP}\)-complete, even though the ocean support size is fixed at \(\tau=1\). The hardness comes from the separately listed major players.

For completeness, the same integer formulas give a deterministic exact comparison algorithm with running time
\[ 2^{m-1}(m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}. \]
It enumerates the major-player subsets, sums their integer contributions for each game, and compares the resulting rational values by cross multiplication. The sums still have polynomial bit length, since each summand is at most its corresponding \(D\).

A deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for the problem would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{PP}\), and hence \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
