The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(\mathcal T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a rational seed-mass budget \(\kappa\), and rational piecewise-linear monotone diminishing-returns maps \(F_t:\prod_{s\in\mathcal T}[0,\mu_s]\to[0,1]\) with \(F_t(0)=0\), choose seed masses \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) such that \(\sum_t x_t\le\kappa\). Set \(a^{(0)}=x\) and \(a_t^{(r+1)}=x_t+(\mu_t-x_t)F_t(a^{(r)})\), and define \(\Phi(x)=\sum_t a_t^\infty\). Determine the complexity of finding a feasible rational \(x\) with \(\Phi(x)\ge(1-1/e-\epsilon)\max_{y}\Phi(y)\), where \(y\) ranges over the same seed-mass constraints.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Let \(D=\prod_{t\in\mathcal T}[0,\mu_t]\). Each activation map \(F_t:D\to[0,1]\) is continuous and piecewise affine, encoded by an explicit rational polyhedral subdivision and rational affine formulas agreeing on overlaps. Let \(m\) count all listed cells and \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The maps are promised coordinatewise monotone, satisfy \(F_t(0)=0\), and have diminishing returns: \(F_t(u+h e_s)-F_t(u)\ge F_t(v+h e_s)-F_t(v)\) whenever \(u\le v\), \(h\ge0\), and all four arguments lie in \(D\); \(e_s\) is the coordinate unit vector.
Given binary-rational \(0<\kappa\le1\) and \(0<\epsilon<1-1/e\), let \(K=\{x\in D:\sum_t x_t\le\kappa\}\). Feasible outputs are rational vectors in \(K\).
For every real \(x\in K\), set \(a^{(0)}=x\) and \(a_t^{(r+1)}=x_t+(\mu_t-x_t)F_t(a^{(r)})\). Define \(a^\infty=\lim_{r\to\infty}a^{(r)}\), \(\Phi(x)=\sum_t a_t^\infty\), and \(\mathrm{OPT}=\sup_{y\in K}\Phi(y)\).

Does this problem admit a randomized algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon)\) that returns a feasible rational \(x\) satisfying \(\Phi(x)\ge(1-1/e-\epsilon)\mathrm{OPT}\) with probability at least \(2/3\), or is achieving this guarantee NP-hard for some fixed \(\epsilon\) in the stated range?

For finite agents with independent uniform thresholds and normalized monotone submodular activation functions, cardinality influence maximization admits a randomized greedy \((1-1/e-\epsilon)\)-approximation ([Mossel–Roch](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0612046)).
A related fractional-incentive model also admits this approximation, but uses additive incentives in individual threshold tests rather than the deterministic mass recurrence specified here ([Demaine et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.7970)); it does not settle this target.

Result

The stated problem admits a deterministic polynomial-time approximation algorithm. Specifically, it returns a feasible rational vector \(x\) satisfying
\( \Phi(x)\ge (1-1/e-\epsilon)\mathrm{OPT} \)
in time \( \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon) \). Its success probability is therefore \(1\).

The proof uses the explicit piecewise affine encoding to obtain an exact value oracle at strictly positive seed vectors. A discretization with a positive baseline then reduces the problem to monotone submodular maximization under one knapsack constraint.

1. The limiting cascade preserves diminishing returns

For this proof, extend the recurrence defining \(a^{(r)}(x)\) and \(\Phi(x)\) to every \(x\in D\). This uses the same formulas and agrees with the given objective on \(K\).

Write
\( G_x(a)_t=x_t+(\mu_t-x_t)F_t(a) \).
Because \(F_t\) takes values in \([0,1]\), the map \(G_x\) maps \(D\) into \(D\). It is coordinatewise monotone in \(a\).

We have \(a^{(1)}(x)\ge a^{(0)}(x)=x\). Monotonicity of \(G_x\) then gives
\( a^{(r+1)}(x)\ge a^{(r)}(x) \)
for every \(r\). Since these vectors are bounded above by \(\mu\), their coordinatewise limit \(p(x)\) exists. Continuity gives \(G_x(p(x))=p(x)\).

Moreover, \(p(x)\) is the least fixed point of \(G_x\) in \(D\): every fixed point \(q\) satisfies \(q\ge x\), and induction gives \(a^{(r)}(x)\le q\), hence \(p(x)\le q\).

We next prove that every coordinate of \(a^{(r)}(x)\), and consequently \(\Phi(x)\), is monotone and has diminishing returns.

1.1. Composition preserves diminishing returns

Let \(A:D\to D\) have monotone coordinates with diminishing returns, and let \(F:D\to\mathbb R\) be monotone with diminishing returns. Then \(F\circ A\) has the same properties.

First, coordinate diminishing returns implies diminishing returns for any common nonnegative vector increment \(w\):
\( F(u+w)-F(u)\ge F(v+w)-F(v) \)
whenever \(u\le v\) and the arguments lie in \(D\). To see this, add the coordinates of \(w\) in a fixed order and apply coordinate diminishing returns to each pair of corresponding increments.

Now take \(u\le v\) and a feasible coordinate increment \(h e_s\). Put
\( \Delta_u=A(u+h e_s)-A(u) \)
and
\( \Delta_v=A(v+h e_s)-A(v) \).
The hypotheses on \(A\) give
\( \Delta_u\ge\Delta_v\ge0 \)
and \(A(u)\le A(v)\). Therefore,
\( F(A(u)+\Delta_u)-F(A(u)) \ge F(A(u)+\Delta_v)-F(A(u)) \ge F(A(v)+\Delta_v)-F(A(v)) \).
The first inequality uses monotonicity; the second uses the common-vector increment property. All intermediate arguments lie in \(D\), because \(D\) is a box. This proves the claim.

1.2. The seed-dependent update preserves diminishing returns

Suppose \(H:D\to[0,1]\) is monotone with diminishing returns. Fix a coordinate \(t\), and define
\( Q(x)=x_t+(\mu_t-x_t)H(x) \).

For \(s\ne t\), its marginal is
\( Q(x+h e_s)-Q(x)=(\mu_t-x_t)\bigl(H(x+h e_s)-H(x)\bigr) \).
Both factors are nonnegative and decrease when the base point increases coordinatewise. Thus this marginal is nonnegative and satisfies diminishing returns.

For \(s=t\), direct calculation gives
\( Q(x+h e_t)-Q(x) =h\bigl(1-H(x+h e_t)\bigr) +(\mu_t-x_t)\bigl(H(x+h e_t)-H(x)\bigr) \).
Again, both summands are nonnegative. When the base point increases, \(1-H(x+h e_t)\), \(\mu_t-x_t\), and the marginal of \(H\) all decrease. Hence this marginal also satisfies diminishing returns.

Starting with the identity map \(a^{(0)}(x)=x\), apply these two preservation statements inductively to
\( H_t(x)=F_t(a^{(r)}(x)) \).
Every coordinate of \(a^{(r)}\) is therefore monotone with diminishing returns. Passing to the limit in each defining inequality proves the same properties for \(p_t(x)\) and their sum \(\Phi(x)\).

Finally, \(F_t(0)=0\) implies \(p(0)=0\), so \(\Phi(0)=0\).

2. Piecewise affine activation maps are concave

The following consequence of the encoding is crucial.

Lemma. A continuous function on a full-dimensional box that is piecewise affine on a finite polyhedral subdivision and satisfies the stated diminishing-returns condition is jointly concave.

Proof. Diminishing returns applied to two consecutive equal increments in one coordinate gives
\( F(z+h e_i)+F(z-h e_i)-2F(z)\le0 \)
whenever these points lie in the box.

Consider an interior interface between two affine regions. Locally, write its hyperplane as \(n\cdot z=c\), with affine gradients \(g_-\) and \(g_+\) on the negative and positive sides. Continuity on the interface implies
\( g_+-g_-=\alpha n \)
for some scalar \(\alpha\).

Choose a point \(z\) in the relative interior of the interface and a coordinate \(i\) with \(n_i\ne0\). For sufficiently small \(h>0\), the preceding symmetric difference equals
\( \alpha h|n_i| \).
It follows that \(\alpha\le0\).

Consequently, along any line crossing this interface transversely, the slope cannot increase. Indeed, for direction \(d\), the slope change is \(\alpha n\cdot d\) when crossing from the negative side to the positive side, and \(-\alpha n\cdot d\) in the reverse direction; both are nonpositive in their respective crossing directions.

To handle the entire subdivision, refine it conceptually by the finite collection of its supporting hyperplanes. A generic interior line segment crosses only one hyperplane at a time and avoids their intersections. Along every such segment, the restriction of \(F\) is continuous and piecewise affine with nonincreasing slopes, hence concave. Arbitrary segments are limits of generic interior segments, so continuity extends the concavity inequality to them. The refinement is used only in this proof and need not be constructed algorithmically. \(\square\)

Apply this lemma to each \(F_t\). Retain only the listed cells with nonempty full-dimensional interior, and denote their affine formulas by \(\ell_{tj}\). Then
\( F_t(a)=\min_j\ell_{tj}(a) \)
for every \(a\in D\).

Here is a verification of this representation. If \(F_t=\ell_{tj}\) near an interior point \(z\) of a cell, then concavity implies \(F_t(y)\le\ell_{tj}(y)\) for every \(y\in D\). Explicitly, choose a sufficiently small \(\lambda>0\) so that \(z'=z+\lambda(z-y)\) remains inside that cell. Applying concavity at
\( z=(z'+\lambda y)/(1+\lambda) \)
and using the affine formula at \(z,z'\) yields the inequality. Conversely, every point of \(D\) belongs to the closure of a full-dimensional cell, where continuity gives equality with that cell’s formula.

Full-dimensionality of a rational polyhedral cell can be tested in polynomial time by rational linear programming. Thus the retained formulas can be identified without constructing the refinement used in the proof.

2.1. A scaling inequality

Concavity and \(F_t(0)=0\) give
\( F_t(\lambda a)\ge\lambda F_t(a) \)
for \(0\le\lambda\le1\).

It follows directly from the recurrence that
\( a^{(r)}(\lambda y)\ge\lambda a^{(r)}(y) \).
For the induction step, monotonicity and the preceding inequality give
\( a_t^{(r+1)}(\lambda y) \ge \lambda y_t+\lambda(\mu_t-\lambda y_t)F_t(a^{(r)}(y)) \ge \lambda a_t^{(r+1)}(y) \).
The base case is equality. Passing to the limit yields
\( \Phi(\lambda y)\ge\lambda\Phi(y) \).
This argument does not require continuity of \(\Phi\) at the boundary.

3. Exact evaluation at positive rational seeds

Fix a rational vector \(x\in D\) with \(x_t>0\) for every supported type. Because the \(F_t\) are concave, \(G_x\) is concave coordinatewise as a function of \(a\).

We first prove uniqueness of its fixed point. Let \(p=p(x)\) be its least fixed point, and let \(q\) be any other fixed point. We know \(q\ge p\ge x>0\). If \(q\ne p\), put
\( \lambda=\min_t p_t/q_t \).
Then \(0<\lambda<1\), \(p\ge\lambda q\), and equality holds in some coordinate \(i\). Monotonicity and concavity give
\( p=G_x(p)\ge G_x(\lambda q) \ge\lambda G_x(q)+(1-\lambda)G_x(0) =\lambda q+(1-\lambda)x \).
In coordinate \(i\), this contradicts \(p_i=\lambda q_i\) and \(x_i>0\). Hence the fixed point is unique.

Now solve the rational linear program
\( \max \sum_t a_t \)
subject to
\( 0\le a_t\le\mu_t \)
and
\( a_t\le x_t+(\mu_t-x_t)\ell_{tj}(a) \)
for every \(t,j\).

The affine-minimum representation shows that these constraints are equivalent to \(a\le G_x(a)\). From any feasible \(a\), iteration of \(G_x\) increases to a fixed point, which must be \(p(x)\). Thus every feasible vector satisfies \(a\le p(x)\). Since \(p(x)\) itself is feasible, it is the unique maximizer.

The linear program has at most \(m\) activation inequalities, in addition to the box constraints. Its rational coefficients have bit length polynomial in the input length and the encoding length of \(x\). Standard exact rational linear programming therefore computes \(p(x)\), and hence \(\Phi(x)\), in polynomial time.

Strict positivity is essential to this oracle argument. For example, with one type, \(\mu_1=1\), and \(F_1(a)=a\), the objective equals \(0\) at \(x=0\) and \(1\) at every \(x>0\). The algorithm below ensures positivity at every oracle query.

4. A polynomial-size discretization with a positive baseline

Choose
\( q=\lceil2/\epsilon\rceil \)
and \( \eta=1/q \). Thus \(0<\eta\le\epsilon/2\).

For each supported type define
\( d_t=\min\{\mu_t,\kappa/\tau\} \),
\( b_t=\eta d_t \),
and
\( c_t=\min\{\mu_t,\kappa\} \).

Every \(b_t\) is strictly positive. Also,
\( \sum_t b_t\le\eta\kappa \)
and \(b_t\le\eta c_t\).

Let
\( N_t=\lfloor(c_t-b_t)/b_t\rfloor \).
Create \(N_t\) distinct items of type \(t\), each with cost \(b_t\). Denote the resulting ground set by \(E\). For a subset \(S\subseteq E\), let \(n_t(S)\) count its items of type \(t\), and define
\( x_t(S)=b_t\bigl(1+n_t(S)\bigr) \).

By construction, \(0<x_t(S)\le c_t\le\mu_t\), even for subsets exceeding the budget. Hence
\( f(S)=\Phi(x(S)) \)
is defined on every subset of \(E\) and is computable by the positive-seed oracle.

The knapsack budget is
\( B=\kappa-\sum_t b_t>0 \).
Its constraint is exactly feasibility of the corresponding seed vector:
\( \sum_{e\in S}\operatorname{cost}(e)\le B \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_t x_t(S)\le\kappa \).

The ground set has polynomial size. If \(\mu_t\le\kappa/\tau\), then \(c_t/b_t=q\). Otherwise, \(c_t/b_t\le q\tau\). Therefore
\( |E|=\sum_t N_t\le q\tau^2=O(\tau^2/\epsilon) \).

4.1. The induced set function is submodular

Monotonicity of \(\Phi\) implies monotonicity of \(f\).

For \(S\subseteq T\) and an item \(e\notin T\) of type \(t\), we have \(x(S)\le x(T)\), and adding \(e\) adds the same coordinate increment \(b_t e_t\) to both vectors. Diminishing returns of \(\Phi\) therefore gives
\( f(S\cup\{e\})-f(S) \ge f(T\cup\{e\})-f(T) \).
This is the diminishing-returns characterization of set submodularity.

4.2. The grid approximates every real feasible vector

Take any \(y\in K\), and define
\( \widehat x_t=b_t+b_t\lfloor(1-\eta)y_t/b_t\rfloor \).

Coordinatewise,
\( (1-\eta)y_t\le\widehat x_t \le(1-\eta)y_t+b_t \).
Since \(y_t\le c_t\) and \(b_t\le\eta c_t\), we have \(\widehat x_t\le c_t\). Consequently, \(\widehat x=x(S_y)\) for some subset \(S_y\subseteq E\).

Furthermore,
\( \sum_t\widehat x_t \le(1-\eta)\sum_t y_t+\sum_t b_t \le(1-\eta)\kappa+\eta\kappa=\kappa \),
so \(S_y\) satisfies the knapsack constraint.

Monotonicity and the scaling inequality give
\( f(S_y)=\Phi(\widehat x) \ge\Phi((1-\eta)y) \ge(1-\eta)\Phi(y) \).

Taking the supremum over all \(y\in K\) proves
\( \max_{S:\,\operatorname{cost}(S)\le B}f(S) \ge(1-\eta)\mathrm{OPT} \).
No attainment assumption for \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is needed.

5. Approximation guarantee and running time

Use the following established theorem: a nonnegative, normalized, monotone submodular set function on \(n\) items, accessible through a value oracle, admits a deterministic \((1-1/e)\)-approximation under one knapsack constraint, using \(O(n^5)\) value evaluations. This is a specialization of [Sviridenko’s Theorem 1](https://thibaut.horel.org/submodularity/papers/sviridenko2004.pdf).

Apply it to
\( g(S)=f(S)-f(\varnothing) \).
The previous section verifies monotonicity and submodularity; monotonicity also gives \(g(S)\ge0\), and \(g(\varnothing)=0\). Rational costs and budget can be converted to integers by multiplying by a common denominator of polynomial bit length.

Writing \(\alpha=1-1/e\), the returned subset \(S\) satisfies
\( g(S)\ge\alpha\max_{\operatorname{cost}(T)\le B}g(T) \).
Since \(f(\varnothing)\ge0\), this implies
\( f(S)\ge\alpha\max_{\operatorname{cost}(T)\le B}f(T) \).
Combining with the discretization bound,
\( \Phi(x(S)) \ge\alpha(1-\eta)\mathrm{OPT} \ge(\alpha-\epsilon)\mathrm{OPT} \),
because \(\alpha\eta\le\eta\le\epsilon/2\).

There are \(O(\tau^2/\epsilon)\) items. Every queried vector is a positive rational vector whose coordinates are integer multiples of the \(b_t\), with multipliers at most \(q\tau\); its encoding length is polynomial in \(L,\tau,\log(1/\epsilon)\). Every oracle evaluation is an exact rational linear program of polynomial size. The knapsack algorithm makes polynomially many such evaluations and performs rational arithmetic on numbers of polynomial bit length.

Thus the complete algorithm runs in
\( \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon) \),
outputs a feasible rational vector, and achieves the requested guarantee deterministically.
