The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two nonatomic voter types, geographic and functional, each of mass \(1/2\), let \(x,y\in[0,1/2]\) be the masses supporting a bill in the respective chambers, and let \(z\in\{0,1\}\) indicate whether the government proposed it. The bill passes when \((z=1\text{ and }x+y>1/2)\) or \((z=0\text{ and }x>1/4\text{ and }y>1/4)\). Find the minimum \(k\) such that this rule, for every allowed \(x,y,z\), is exactly the conjunction of \(k\) quota tests \(a_i x+b_i y+c_i z>q_i\), with nonnegative weights and strict quotas.

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass encoded as a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here the geographic and functional types each have mass \(1/2\), so \(\tau=2\) and total voter mass is \(1\).
A coalition is described by arbitrary real supporting masses \(x,y\in[0,1/2]\) and a separate government indicator \(z\in\{0,1\}\). The government has no voter mass. The bill passes exactly when \((z=1\land x+y>1/2)\lor(z=0\land x>1/4\land y>1/4)\); equality at a majority threshold loses.
A representation consists of finitely many tests \(a_i x+b_i y+c_i z>q_i\), with fixed real coefficients \(a_i,b_i,c_i\geq0\) and \(q_i\geq0\). Passage must equal their conjunction for every allowed coalition, including boundary points. Define the dimension as the minimum positive number of tests, or \(\infty\) if no finite representation exists. Any running-time claim should use support size \(\tau\), input bit length \(L\), and any quota-count parameter; this fixed bill rule has no varying candidate-count parameter \(m\).

What is the dimension of this continuous voting rule: what is the smallest \(k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\) permitting such an exact representation, or is no finite \(k\) possible?

For the discrete analogue with two chambers of \(n\) voters each, passage requires more than \(n\) ordinary supporters when the government proposes the bill, and more than \(n/2\) supporters in each chamber otherwise. Its dimension is \(1\) for \(n=1,2\), \(2\) for \(n=3,4\), and \(3\) for \(n\geq5\). [Cheung and Ng](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.4795)

The dimension is exactly \(\boxed{3}\). The following proof applies directly to the continuous rule and includes all boundary coalitions.

Three tests suffice

Use the conjunction of

\[ x+\frac12 z>\frac14,\qquad y+\frac12 z>\frac14,\qquad x+y>\frac12. \]

All coefficients and quotas are nonnegative.

When \(z=0\), the first two tests require \(x>\frac14\) and \(y>\frac14\). These inequalities imply the third, so the conjunction gives exactly the required passage condition.

When \(z=1\), the first two tests hold automatically because \(x,y\geq0\). The conjunction therefore reduces to \(x+y>\frac12\), again exactly the required condition. All majority equalities lose as prescribed.

Thus the dimension is at most \(3\).

At least three tests are necessary

Suppose an exact representation exists. Every individual test must hold at every winning coalition. Consider any one of its tests, written as \(ax+by+cz>q\), where \(a,b,c,q\geq0\).

For every \(0<\varepsilon<\frac14\), the coalition \((\frac14+\varepsilon,\frac14+\varepsilon,0)\) wins. Consequently,

\[ q<(a+b)\left(\frac14+\varepsilon\right). \]

Taking \(\varepsilon\) to zero gives \(q\leq\frac{a+b}{4}\). Also \(a+b>0\): otherwise this test would fail at every coalition with \(z=0\).

We now identify three losing coalitions that require three distinct tests.

First coalition: \(U=(\frac14,\frac12,0)\).

Some test must fail at \(U\). For that test,

\[ \frac a4+\frac b2\leq q\leq\frac{a+b}{4}. \]

Hence \(b=0\), \(a>0\), and \(q=\frac a4\).

Second coalition: \(V=(\frac12,\frac14,0)\).

Similarly, some test must fail at \(V\), and that test necessarily has \(a=0\), \(b>0\), and \(q=\frac b4\). It is distinct from every test failing at \(U\).

Third coalition: \(W=(\frac14,\frac14,1)\).

This coalition loses because its total supporting mass equals \(\frac12\). A test failing at \(W\) satisfies

\[ \frac{a+b}{4}+c\leq q\leq\frac{a+b}{4}. \]

Nonnegativity of \(c\) therefore forces \(c=0\) and \(q=\frac{a+b}{4}\).

For every \(0<\varepsilon<\frac14\), both \((\varepsilon,\frac12,1)\) and \((\frac12,\varepsilon,1)\) win. Since \(c=0\), validity of this test at those coalitions, followed by taking \(\varepsilon\) to zero, gives

\[ q\leq\frac b2,\qquad q\leq\frac a2. \]

Substituting \(q=\frac{a+b}{4}\) yields \(a\leq b\) and \(b\leq a\). Thus \(a=b>0\). This test is a positive multiple of \(x+y>\frac12\), and is distinct from the tests required to reject \(U\) and \(V\).

Every exact representation consequently contains at least three tests. Combined with the explicit construction, this proves that the dimension is \(3\).
