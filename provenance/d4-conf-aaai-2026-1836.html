The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of complete contribution roles \(T\), rational role masses \(\mu\), and a succinct monotone Boolean value functional \(V:[0,1]^T\to\{0,1\}\) on arrived role masses, define finite replicas by \(v_n(S)=V((|S\cap N_t|/n)_{t\in T})\), with \(|N_t|/n\to\mu_t\). Under uniform arrival orders and tagged-agent delay deviations, does EVS converge to a measurable per-capita policy that minimizes the limit of \(n\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\sum_i(\phi_i(v_n,\pi)-\mathrm{SV}_i(v_n))^2]\) among anonymous policies satisfying the limiting SF, OIR, I4EA, and MOS conditions, and can that policy be computed from the chosen encoding of \(V\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(T\): the list of its \(\tau\) complete contribution roles with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible roles. Roles determine all contributions, with no additional player attributes.
The input value functional \(V:[0,1]^T\to\{0,1\}\) is an AND/OR circuit of size \(m\), whose leaves are rational affine threshold tests with nonnegative coefficients and explicitly specified strict or weak comparisons. Assume \(V(0)=0\) and \(V(\mu)=1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For every positive integer \(n\) with \(n\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\), form \(N=\bigsqcup_tN_t\), with \(|N_t|=n\mu_t\), and \(v_n(S)=V((|S\cap N_t|/n)_t)\). Promise that every such replica is solvable: no winning coalition \(S\) has a unique indispensable player \(i\) with \(v_n(\{i\})=0\), where indispensable means \(v_n(S\setminus\{i\})=0\).
An online policy uses only the ordered arrived coalition and its restricted game, assigns nonnegative cumulative shares summing to its value at every prefix, and is anonymous under relabelings preserving the game. SF means expected shares under uniform orders equal Shapley values; OIR means cumulative shares never decrease; I4EA means moving one player later while fixing everyone else's relative order cannot increase her final share; MOS means an earlier symmetric player receives a weakly larger share. These requirements apply also to restricted games; symmetry means equal marginal effects on every coalition excluding the two players.
Define EVS as follows. At the first winning prefix \(P\), let \(i\) be its last arrival and \(C=\{j\in P:v_n(P\setminus\{j\})=0\}\). Let \(\widehat P=Q\cup\{i\}\), where \(Q\) is the shortest prefix preceding \(i\) for which \(v_n(Q\cup\{i\})=1\), and put \(r=|\{j\in\widehat P:v_n(\widehat P\setminus\{j\})=0\}|\). Give \(i\) share \(1/r\), each other member of \(C\) share \((1-1/r)/(|C|-1)\), and everyone else zero; if \(|C|=1\), give \(i\) one. Shares are zero before winning and frozen afterward.
Generate uniform orders using independent marks \(U_i\sim\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]\); ties have probability zero. A per-capita policy is a nonnegative measurable field \(q_t(u,s)\) for \(0\leq u\leq s\leq1\), describing cumulative payment density at cutoff \(s\) to role \(t\) arriving at \(u\).
Define convergence to \(q\) by \(\mathbb E[n^{-1}\sum_{i:U_i\leq s}(n\phi_i(v_n|_{A_s},\pi|_{A_s})-q_{t(i)}(U_i,s))^2]\to0\) for every fixed \(s\in[0,1]\), where \(A_s=\{i:U_i\leq s\}\). A limiting admissible policy is such a limit of anonymous online policies satisfying SF, OIR, I4EA and MOS exactly on solvable finite games; this specifies the limiting axioms, including tagged-player delay deviations.
For an admissible policy with witnessing sequence \(\phi^{(n)}\), use the objective \(J=\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb E_\pi[\sum_i(\phi_i^{(n)}(v_n,\pi)-\mathrm{SV}_i(v_n))^2]\), when it exists; all limits run through the exact replicas above. Computational evaluation means approximating \(\int_a^b q_t(u,1)\,du\) to additive error \(\varepsilon\), for rational \(0\leq a<b\leq1\) and \(\varepsilon>0\).
Does EVS converge to an admissible measurable per-capita policy whose objective limit exists, depends only on that policy, and is minimal among admissible policies with existing objective limits?
On inputs admitting such an EVS limit, is its computational evaluation polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L\), the query bit length and \(\log(1/\varepsilon)\), or is it NP-hard?
For finite solvable monotone Boolean games, EVS satisfies SF, OIR, I4EA and MOS and minimizes expected squared Shapley distance among anonymous mechanisms satisfying these axioms. [Ge, Zhang and Zhao](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11023v1).

Result

Universal convergence is false. On inputs admitting the specified EVS limit, evaluation is polynomial-time. In fact, those inputs admit a precise characterization: near full participation, the circuit must reduce to unanimity on one nonempty set of roles. For every such input, EVS is admissible and its objective is \(J=0\), hence minimal.

I use the intended monotone interpretation of the leaves: each tests \(\mathbf a\cdot x\ge b\) or \(\mathbf a\cdot x>b\), with \(\mathbf a\ge0\). An affine constant is absorbed into \(b\).

The requirement of convergence at every fixed cutoff, including the cutoff at which the population crosses a threshold, is essential to this result.

1. What the convergence definition implies

Fix a cutoff \(s\). Write \(\Phi_{n,i}(s)\) for player \(i\)’s cumulative share in the restricted game at \(s\), extending it by zero for unarrived players.

Suppose a sequence converges to \(q\) as specified. Then, for every role \(t\),

\[ \text{[Equation omitted to respect the requested delimiter format.]} \]

More explicitly, the following convergence holds in mean square:

\(R_{n,t}(s):=\sum_{i\in N_t}\Phi_{n,i}(s)\ \longrightarrow\ \mu_t\int_0^s q_t(u,s)\,du.\)

Here and below, all limits run through exact replicas.

To prove this, first observe that \(q_t(\cdot,s)\) is square-integrable on \([0,s]\). Indeed, every finite share lies between zero and one, so \(n\Phi_{n,i}(s)\) is bounded. If \(q_t(\cdot,s)\) had infinite squared integral, the expected squared approximation error would be infinite for every replica.

Define \(f_t(u)=q_t(u,s)\mathbf1_{\{u\le s\}}\) and

\(\widehat R_{n,t}(s)=n^{-1}\sum_{i\in N_t}f_t(U_i).\)

Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\(\mathbb E|R_{n,t}(s)-\widehat R_{n,t}(s)|^2\le \mu_t\,\mathbb E\!\left[n^{-1}\sum_{i:U_i\le s}(n\Phi_{n,i}(s)-q_{t(i)}(U_i,s))^2\right]\longrightarrow0.\)

Independence of the marks gives

\(\mathbb E\widehat R_{n,t}(s)=\mu_t\int_0^s q_t(u,s)\,du,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\widehat R_{n,t}(s))=\frac{\mu_t}{n}\operatorname{Var}(f_t(U))\longrightarrow0.\)

This proves the assertion.

Summing over roles and using efficiency, we obtain a necessary condition:

At every fixed \(s\), the random value \(v_n(A_s)\) must converge in mean square to a deterministic constant.

In particular, convergence is impossible if the probability that \(A_s\) wins has a limit strictly between zero and one.

The objective is determined by the limiting policy

There is also a general positive conclusion, independent of the classification below.

For any admissible policy, put \(g_t(u)=q_t(u,1)\). Mean-square convergence implies

\(n\mathbb E\sum_i(\Phi_{n,i}(1))^2\longrightarrow\sum_t\mu_t\int_0^1g_t(u)^2\,du.\)

For justification, regard \(n\Phi_{n,i}(1)\) and \(g_{t(i)}(U_i)\) as vectors with squared norm \(\mathbb E[n^{-1}\sum_i(\cdot)^2]\). Their difference tends to zero in norm, while the latter vector has squared norm exactly \(\sum_t\mu_t\int_0^1g_t^2\).

Players within a role are symmetric, so write their common Shapley value as \(\mathrm{SV}_{n,t}\). SF and the role-total convergence above imply

\(n\mathrm{SV}_{n,t}\longrightarrow\int_0^1g_t(u)\,du.\)

Expanding the squared distance and using SF therefore proves

\(J(q)=\sum_t\mu_t\left[\int_0^1g_t(u)^2\,du-\left(\int_0^1g_t(u)\,du\right)^2\right].\)

Thus every admissible policy has an existing, finite objective limit, and that limit depends only on its full-participation field, up to equality almost everywhere.

2. A nondegenerate counterexample to universal convergence

Take one role, with mass \(\mu_1=1\), and let

\(V(x)=\mathbf1_{\{x\ge1/2\}}.\)

The replica is the symmetric threshold game with winning threshold \(k_n=\lceil n/2\rceil\).

Every replica satisfies the solvability promise. A winning coalition of size \(k_n\) has all its members indispensable; a larger winning coalition has none. Unique indispensability occurs only when \(k_n=1\), in which case that player’s singleton wins.

EVS gives each of the first \(k_n\) arrivals share \(1/k_n\), and everyone else zero.

At the fixed cutoff \(s=1/2\),

\(v_n(A_{1/2})=\mathbf1_{\{\operatorname{Binomial}(n,1/2)\ge\lceil n/2\rceil\}}.\)

The central limit theorem gives

\(\mathbb P(v_n(A_{1/2})=1)\longrightarrow1/2.\)

The necessary condition from Section 1 fails. Hence EVS cannot converge to a field of the specified kind.

This failure persists despite an existing finite objective limit. Symmetry gives \(\mathrm{SV}_i=1/n\), and direct calculation yields

\(n\mathbb E\sum_i(\phi_i^{\mathrm{EVS}}-\mathrm{SV}_i)^2=\frac{n}{k_n}-1\longrightarrow1.\)

So finite optimality and existence of the numerical objective limit do not imply the requested policy convergence.

3. The saturation circuit

For each threshold leaf, define

\(A=\mathbf a\cdot\mu,\qquad D=\{t:a_t>0\}.\)

Construct a monotone Boolean circuit \(H\) on subsets of roles by retaining the original AND/OR gates and replacing its leaves as follows.

| Original leaf | Condition | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| \(\mathbf a\cdot x\ge b\) | \(b<A\) | Constant \(1\) |
| \(\mathbf a\cdot x\ge b\) | \(b=A\) | Test \(D\subseteq S\) |
| \(\mathbf a\cdot x\ge b\) | \(b>A\) | Constant \(0\) |
| \(\mathbf a\cdot x>b\) | \(b<A\) | Constant \(1\) |
| \(\mathbf a\cdot x>b\) | \(b\ge A\) | Constant \(0\) |

An empty conjunction is constant \(1\). Since \(V(\mu)=1\), we have \(H(T)=1\).

For a feasible vector \(0\le x\le\mu\), define its completed roles by

\(F(x)=\{t:x_t=\mu_t\}.\)

Two properties will be used.

Global domination: \(V(x)\le H(F(x))\).

Indeed, a weak saturated leaf satisfies

\(\mathbf a\cdot x\ge\mathbf a\cdot\mu\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_t=\mu_t\text{ for every }t\in D,\)

because all terms \(a_t(\mu_t-x_t)\) are nonnegative. Every other replacement either preserves the leaf or increases it to constant \(1\). AND/OR gates preserve this inequality.

Local equality: there exists \(\delta\in(0,1)\) such that

\(V(x)=H(F(x))\quad\text{whenever}\quad (1-\delta)\mu_t\le x_t\le\mu_t\text{ for every }t.\)

For every leaf with \(b<A\) and \(A>0\), choose \(\delta\) small enough that \((1-\delta)A>b\). There are finitely many leaves, so one positive \(\delta\) works simultaneously. Leaves with \(A=0\) are constant. Saturated and impossible leaves already have the asserted exact behavior.

4. Why an admitting input must reach its threshold only at full participation

Define the deterministic ray threshold

\(c=\inf\{s\in[0,1]:V(s\mu)=1\}.\)

Monotonicity and the endpoint assumptions imply \(c\in[0,1]\).

Interior thresholds cannot admit any limiting policy

Suppose \(0<c<1\), and write

\(X_n(c)=(|A_c\cap N_t|/n)_t.\)

The coordinates are independent scaled binomial variables. The multivariate central limit theorem gives

\(\sqrt n(X_n(c)-c\mu)\ \Rightarrow\ Z,\)

where \(Z\) is a centered Gaussian vector with positive diagonal covariance

\(\operatorname{diag}(\mu_t c(1-c))_{t\in T}.\)

A nonconstant threshold leaf with \(b\ne cA\) has an asymptotically fixed truth value. An active leaf with \(b=cA\) asymptotically tests the sign of \(\mathbf a\cdot Z\). Strict and weak comparisons have the same limit because this nonzero Gaussian linear form equals zero with probability zero.

Substitute these limiting tests into the circuit, obtaining a Boolean function \(G(Z)\). Its discontinuities lie in finitely many Gaussian-null hyperplanes, so

\(\mathbb P(V(X_n(c))=1)\longrightarrow p:=\mathbb P(G(Z)=1).\)

All active tests are false on the strictly negative orthant and true on the strictly positive orthant. The resulting circuit values are respectively zero and one: they agree with \(V((c-\eta)\mu)\) and \(V((c+\eta)\mu)\) for sufficiently small positive \(\eta\).

The Gaussian has positive density everywhere. Consequently, both orthants have positive probability, and \(0<p<1\).

Section 1 therefore rules out convergence for any efficient policy to a specified field at this cutoff.

A threshold at zero cannot admit an EVS limit

Suppose \(c=0\). Then \(V(s\mu)=1\) for every \(s>0\).

For all sufficiently large replicas,

\(V((1/n)_{t\in T})=1.\)

To verify this, compare leaf truth values near the origin. Positive thresholds remain false for sufficiently large \(n\); negative thresholds remain true; weak zero thresholds are true; and strict zero thresholds with nonzero coefficients are true when every coordinate is positive. These are exactly the leaf values on a sufficiently small positive multiple of \(\mu\).

Consider the event that the first \(\tau\) arrivals contain exactly one player from every role. Its probability is

\(\frac{\tau!\prod_t(n\mu_t)}{n(n-1)\cdots(n-\tau+1)}\longrightarrow\tau!\prod_t\mu_t>0.\)

On this event, the first winning prefix has at most \(\tau\) players. EVS’s final shares are supported on that prefix and sum to one, so Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\(\sum_i(\phi_i^{\mathrm{EVS}})^2\ge1/\tau.\)

It follows that \(n\mathbb E\sum_i(\phi_i^{\mathrm{EVS}})^2\) diverges. Mean-square convergence at full participation would make this quantity bounded, as proved in Section 1. This is a contradiction.

Therefore, every input admitting the specified EVS limit has \(c=1\). By local equality along the ray, this also implies \(H(\varnothing)=0\).

5. Exact characterization of the admitting inputs

Theorem. Within the stated solvability promise, EVS admits the specified limit if and only if \(H\) has exactly one inclusion-minimal winning role set \(B\), and \(B\ne\varnothing\).

A high-probability regularity event

Choose \(\delta\) from Section 3 and put \(s_0=1-\delta/2\). Let \(G_n\) be the event that, for every role \(t\),

\((1-3\delta/4)\mu_t\le |A_{s_0}\cap N_t|/n\le(1-\delta/4)\mu_t.\)

For each role, the count is binomial with \(n\mu_t\) trials and success probability \(s_0\). Hoeffding’s inequality and a union bound give

\(\mathbb P(G_n^c)\le2\tau\exp(-n\delta^2\min_t\mu_t/8).\)

For sufficiently large \(n\), on \(G_n\):

  • Every role has at least two players still unarrived at \(s_0\).
  • Every coalition containing \(A_{s_0}\), even after removing one player, lies in the local-equality region.
  • \(A_{s_0}\), and \(A_{s_0}\cup\{i\}\) for any unarrived player \(i\), have no completed roles.

Since \(H(\varnothing)=0\), all those latter coalitions lose. Thus both the first winning prefix and EVS’s minimal critical prefix occur after \(s_0\). Their values, and the values after deleting any one member, are determined exactly by \(H\) on completed roles.

Multiple minimal winning sets prevent convergence

For each role, let \(M_{n,t}=\max_{i\in N_t}U_i\), its completion time. Independence across roles and

\(\mathbb P(n(1-M_{n,t})>x)=(1-x/n)^{n\mu_t}\longrightarrow e^{-\mu_t x}\)

show that the scaled completion gaps converge jointly to independent exponential variables with rates \(\mu_t\).

For any nonempty proper role set \(B\), the event

\(E_{n,B}=\{\max_{t\in B}M_{n,t}<\min_{t\notin B}M_{n,t}\}\)

therefore has a limiting probability strictly greater than zero. The comparison boundary has probability zero in the exponential limit, and the required strict ordering holds on a nonempty open rectangle.

Now let \(B\) be an inclusion-minimal winning set of \(H\). On \(E_{n,B}\cap G_n\), precisely the roles in \(B\) are complete at the first winning prefix. Every proper subset of \(B\) loses. Hence its indispensable players are exactly all players in those roles.

The minimal critical prefix has the same completed-role set: it cannot win with a proper subset of \(B\), and no outside role has completed yet. Its indispensable players are again exactly all players in \(B\).

Writing \(\mu(B)=\sum_{t\in B}\mu_t\), EVS therefore gives exactly \(1/(n\mu(B))\) to each player in these roles and zero to everyone else.

Suppose \(H\) has two distinct minimal winning sets \(B,B'\). They are incomparable, so choose \(t\in B\setminus B'\). On events of probabilities bounded away from zero, the final role total \(R_{n,t}(1)\) equals respectively

\(\mu_t/\mu(B)\quad\text{and}\quad0.\)

Intersecting with \(G_n\) preserves those positive probability bounds. These distinct values prevent convergence in mean square to a deterministic constant, contrary to Section 1.

Together with Section 4, this proves necessity.

One nonempty minimal winning set gives convergence

Suppose \(H\) has a unique minimal winning set \(B\ne\varnothing\). Monotonicity then implies

\(H(S)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B\subseteq S.\)

Indeed, every winning set contains a minimal winning subset, which must be \(B\); every superset of \(B\) wins.

Global domination shows that every winning coalition in every replica contains all players belonging to roles in \(B\).

On \(G_n\), the first winning prefix and minimal critical prefix consequently have the same indispensable players: exactly all players in roles \(B\). EVS gives them the uniform allocation

\(\psi_{n,i}=\mathbf1_{\{t(i)\in B\}}/(n\mu(B)).\)

Thus \(\phi^{\mathrm{EVS}}=\psi_n\) on \(G_n\). Everywhere, nonnegative final shares sum to one, and hence

\(\sum_i(\phi_i^{\mathrm{EVS}}-\psi_{n,i})^2\le2+2/(n\mu(B)).\)

It follows that

\(n\mathbb E\sum_i(\phi_i^{\mathrm{EVS}}-\psi_{n,i})^2 \le(2n+2/\mu(B))\mathbb P(G_n^c)\longrightarrow0.\)

Define the field, on \(0\le u\le s\le1\), by

\(q_t(u,s)=\begin{cases}1/\mu(B),&s=1\text{ and }t\in B,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\)

This is nonnegative and measurable. The preceding estimate proves convergence at \(s=1\).

For every fixed \(s<1\), winning requires all \(n\mu(B)\) players in those roles to have arrived. Therefore,

\(\mathbb P(v_n(A_s)=1)\le s^{n\mu(B)}.\)

Since the squared sum of nonnegative shares is at most their total value,

\(\mathbb E\!\left[n^{-1}\sum_{i:U_i\le s}(n\Phi_{n,i}^{\mathrm{EVS}}(s))^2\right] \le n s^{n\mu(B)}\longrightarrow0.\)

This proves convergence at every cutoff and completes the characterization.

6. Admissibility and minimality

The replicas are monotone Boolean games and are solvable by promise. Every restriction is also solvable: a forbidden winning coalition in a restriction would be the same forbidden coalition in the original game.

The finite EVS theorem consequently supplies SF, OIR, I4EA and MOS on every relevant game and restriction. EVS is anonymous because its construction uses only the ordered restricted game. Thus EVS itself provides the required finite witnesses, including the task’s tagged-player delay requirements. See [Ge, Zhang and Zhao, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11023v1).

For the field proved above, \(q_t(u,1)\) is constant in \(u\) for every role. The objective formula in Section 1 gives \(J=0\).

Every competing objective is a limit of nonnegative squared distances and is therefore nonnegative. EVS is consequently minimal among admissible policies. Its objective depends only on the policy, and the same independence holds for every admissible policy by Section 1.

7. Polynomial-time evaluation

The characterization does not require enumeration of winning role sets.

Construct \(H\) by exact rational comparisons and define

\(B_*=\{t\in T:H(T\setminus\{t\})=0\}.\)

A role belongs to \(B_*\) exactly when every winning role set contains it. Indeed, a winning set omitting \(t\) exists exactly when \(T\setminus\{t\}\) wins, by monotonicity.

Consequently:

  • If \(H\) has a unique minimal winning set \(B\), then \(B_*=B\).
  • If \(H(B_*)=1\), then \(B_*\) itself is winning and contained in every winning set, so it is the unique minimal winning set.

Thus the admitting-input criterion is precisely

\(B_*\ne\varnothing\quad\text{and}\quad H(B_*)=1.\)

Each saturated leaf becomes a conjunction of at most \(\tau\) role variables. Constructing \(H\), evaluating it on the \(\tau\) sets \(T\setminus\{t\}\), and evaluating it on \(B_*\) all take polynomial time. The rational sums and comparisons have polynomial bit complexity in the stated input length.

On an admitting input, the requested integral is exactly

\(\int_a^bq_t(u,1)\,du= \begin{cases}(b-a)/\mu(B_*),&t\in B_*,\\0,&t\notin B_*.\end{cases}\)

This rational number has polynomial encoding length and can be computed exactly. Hence evaluation is polynomial-time in the requested parameters, with no approximation-dependent numerical work.
