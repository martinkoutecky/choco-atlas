The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational type-mass arrival rates over a horizon, ads with type-specific click and continuation probabilities, and a finite typed independent-cascade model with a specified population scaling and limiting diffusion law, allocate each arriving type mass among integral ad sequences without observing prior cascades. What competitive ratio can an online policy achieve against an offline allocation that knows the full arrival stream, and can a greedy mass-allocation policy attain \(1/2\) with efficient approximation in the number of types, ads, input bit length, and requested accuracy?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types \(T\) with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total binary input length.
There are \(m\geq1\) ads, rational revenues \(r_i>0\), rational sharing and continuation probabilities \(q_{it},c_{it}\in[0,1]\), and a slot bound \(1\leq k\leq m\). An integral sequence contains at most \(k\) distinct ads, including the empty sequence; its sharing probability is \(p_{it}(\sigma)=q_{it}\prod_{\ell<j}c_{\sigma(\ell),t}\) when \(\sigma(j)=i\), and zero when \(i\) is absent. Sharing and continuation are independent.
The horizon consists of \(H\) unit intervals. Nonnegative rational arrival rates \(a_{ht}\) satisfy \(\sum_h a_{ht}\leq\mu_t\); each arriving individual appears once. Rates for interval \(h\) are revealed at its start, with future rates unknown. Immediately and irrevocably split each arriving type mass among integral sequences; observe no sharing or diffusion outcomes.
For cumulative assigned masses \(x_{t\sigma}\), seed masses are \(s_{it}=\sum_\sigma x_{t\sigma}p_{it}(\sigma)\). The typed IC scaling has \(N\mu_t\) individuals of type \(t\) and independent directed transmission probabilities \(\min\{\lambda_{iut}/N,1\}\), where the rational intensities \(\lambda_{iut}\geq0\) are known; sequence assignments within each type are exchangeable.
Use the following specified limiting diffusion law: \(z_i\) is the componentwise least solution in \([s_i,\mu]\) of \(z_{it}=s_{it}+(\mu_t-s_{it})(1-\exp(-\sum_u\lambda_{iut}z_{iu}))\). Final normalized revenue is \(F(x)=\sum_i r_i\sum_t z_{it}\); each individual contributes at most once per ad, and ads have separate cascades and no budget caps.
Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the supremum revenue of feasible allocations knowing all arrival rates but no random outcomes. A policy is \(\beta\)-competitive when \(\mathbb E[F(x)]\geq\beta\mathrm{OPT}\) for every fixed admissible arrival stream.
Define greedy refinement \(G_n\) by subdividing each interval into \(n\) equal pieces. In each piece, process types in their listed order and assign its \(a_{ht}/n\) mass to a sequence maximizing the increase in \(F\), breaking ties lexicographically; these assignments prescribe service during that piece. Its limiting revenue is \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}F(G_n)\).

What worst-case competitive ratio can an online mass-allocation policy achieve?
Does greedy's limiting revenue always reach \(\tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}\), and is there an online implementation attaining \(\tfrac12-\varepsilon\) in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,H,k,L,1/\varepsilon\), for rational \(0<\varepsilon<\tfrac12\), or does either guarantee fail?

Discrete non-adaptive sequencing has a greedy \(\tfrac12\)-competitive guarantee; sequence optimization uses polynomial-time dynamic programming given influence marginals, which are estimated by simulation ([Tang, 2018](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/11306/11165)).
Continuous submodular online learning has approximation-regret guarantees against a fixed hindsight decision, a different benchmark ([Chen–Hassani–Karbasi, 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v84/chen18f.html)). No result settling the specified mass-allocation model was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one and revenue per unit population.
  • Specified sparse exchangeable typed IC scaling with transmission intensity divided by population scale, and selected the least-fixed-point limiting diffusion law; neither was specified in the supplied question.
  • Represented rational arrival rates by H unit intervals, revealing only the current interval's rates, and required cumulative arrivals of each type not to exceed its population mass.
  • Carried over distinct arriving individuals, immediate irrevocable allocation, independent sharing and continuation, separate ad cascades, and counting each influenced individual once per ad.
  • Specified all sequences of distinct ads up to a slot bound, including the empty sequence, since no additional sequence restrictions were supplied.
  • Used positive rational revenues, probabilities including endpoints, nonnegative rational diffusion intensities, and the main model without advertiser budget caps.
  • Defined greedy through finite mass refinements and a liminf revenue, with fixed processing and tie-breaking orders, so its meaning does not require differentiability of the limiting law.
  • Used an offline supremum with full arrival information but no random-outcome information, and interpreted efficient approximation as a multiplicative competitive ratio loss of epsilon.

Proven guarantees and a sharper obstruction

Both proposed guarantees hold:

  • Greedy’s specified limiting revenue is at least $\tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}$.
  • For every rational $\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac12)$, a deterministic online policy achieves $\tfrac12-\varepsilon$ in polynomial time.

Moreover, greedy’s factor $\tfrac12$ is sharp. A different argument bounds the competitive factor of every online policy, including randomized policies, by $\tfrac34$. Thus, if $\beta^\star$ denotes the supremum of achievable uniform competitive factors, the results proved below give
$$ \frac12\leq\beta^\star\leq\frac34. $$

The exact value of $\beta^\star$ remains unresolved. The upper bound improves the bound in the notes.

1. The least fixed point has diminishing returns

Write $S=(s_{it})_{i,t}$ and
$$ \Phi(S)=\sum_i r_i\sum_t z_{it}(S_i). $$
Thus $F(x)=\Phi(S(x))$. A seed array is within its coordinate bounds when $0\leq s_{it}\leq\mu_t$.

1.1 A branching-tree representation

Fix an ad $i$. Construct a rooted multitype tree as follows. A vertex of type $t$ has, independently for each $u$, a Poisson number of type-$u$ children with mean $\lambda_{iut}\mu_u$. Independently mark each type-$u$ vertex with probability $s_{iu}/\mu_u$.

Let $z^{(D)}_{it}/\mu_t$ be the probability of a mark within distance $D$ of a type-$t$ root. Independence and the Poisson generating function give
$$ z^{(0)}_{it}=s_{it}, $$
$$ z^{(D+1)}_{it} =s_{it}+(\mu_t-s_{it}) \left(1-\exp\left(-\sum_u\lambda_{iut}z^{(D)}_{iu}\right)\right). $$

These iterates increase and remain bounded by $\mu$. Their limit is a fixed point, by continuity of the displayed map. Every fixed point in $[s_i,\mu]$ dominates every iterate: this follows inductively from monotonicity of that map. Consequently, their limit is exactly the specified least fixed point.

This argument uses the specified diffusion law directly; it makes no assertion about convergence of finite random graphs.

Condition on a finite-depth tree containing $N_u$ vertices of type $u$. Its marking probability is
$$ h_N(s_i)=1-\prod_u\left(1-\frac{s_{iu}}{\mu_u}\right)^{N_u}. $$
For $N_v>0$,
$$ \frac{\partial h_N}{\partial s_{iv}} = \frac{N_v}{\mu_v} \left(1-\frac{s_{iv}}{\mu_v}\right)^{N_v-1} \prod_{u\ne v}\left(1-\frac{s_{iu}}{\mu_u}\right)^{N_u}. $$
For $N_v=0$, this derivative is zero. Every derivative is nonnegative and nonincreasing in every seed coordinate. Integrating these derivatives along nonnegative increments proves diminishing returns for $h_N$.

Expectations preserve the resulting finite-difference inequalities. Passing to the depth limit also preserves them, because only bounded function values occur in those inequalities. Hence $\Phi$ is nondecreasing and has diminishing returns.

1.2 Clipping and concavity

Extend $\Phi$ to nonnegative arrays by
$$ \widehat\Phi(S)=\Phi(S\wedge\mu), $$
where the clipping is performed separately for each ad and type.

For each finite-tree marking polynomial, clipping changes a coordinate derivative to zero beyond its upper boundary. The derivatives remain nonincreasing. The preceding expectation and limit arguments therefore prove that, whenever $A\leq B$ and $V\geq0$,
$$ \widehat\Phi(A+V)-\widehat\Phi(A) \geq \widehat\Phi(B+V)-\widehat\Phi(B). $$

Also, $\Phi(0)=0$: iteration from zero remains zero.

Two concavity properties follow from the same finite-tree calculation.

  • Along a nonnegative ray, $\Phi$ is concave. In particular,

$$ \Phi(\alpha S)\geq\alpha\Phi(S), \qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq1. $$

  • If only one type-$t$ column varies, then $\Phi$ is concave in that entire column. Indeed, for each ad only one seed coordinate varies, and the concave ad contributions add.

These assertions do not assume continuity at zero seed mass.

2. Greedy’s limiting half guarantee

2.1 Comparison with one sequence per mass piece

Consider any finite ordered list of mass pieces. Let $D_j$ be greedy’s seed increment on piece $j$, and let $E_j$ be the increment obtained by assigning an alternative sequence to the same piece. Define
$$ P_j=\sum_{\ell<j}D_\ell,\qquad S_G=\sum_jD_j,\qquad S_O=\sum_jE_j. $$

Monotonicity, telescoping, and diminishing returns imply
$$ \begin{aligned} \Phi(S_O) &\leq \widehat\Phi(S_G+S_O)\\ &\leq \Phi(S_G) +\sum_j\left[\widehat\Phi(S_G+E_j)-\widehat\Phi(S_G)\right]\\ &\leq \Phi(S_G) +\sum_j\left[\Phi(P_j+E_j)-\Phi(P_j)\right]. \end{aligned} $$

For the second inequality, telescope the $E_j$ increments starting from $S_G$, then move each increment’s base point down to $S_G$. For the third, use $P_j\leq S_G$.

The arrays $P_j+E_j$ are within their seed bounds: they use earlier processed masses and an alternative assignment of the current piece. Thus the last line uses the original, unclipped function.

Greedy’s maximizing property gives
$$ \Phi(P_j+E_j)-\Phi(P_j) \leq \Phi(P_j+D_j)-\Phi(P_j). $$
The right-hand gains telescope. Therefore
$$ \Phi(S_O)\leq2\Phi(S_G). $$

2.2 Approximation of arbitrary fractional allocations

For each type $t$, define
$$ v_t(\sigma)=\bigl(p_{1t}(\sigma),\ldots,p_{mt}(\sigma)\bigr), \qquad P_t=\operatorname{conv}\{v_t(\sigma):\sigma\text{ feasible}\}. $$
The empty sequence supplies the zero vector.

Let $A_t=\sum_h a_{ht}$. Any offline allocation gives total type-$t$ seeds $A_tv_t$ for some $v_t\in P_t$, provided $A_t>0$. Carathéodory’s theorem yields
$$ v_t=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_t}\theta_{t\ell}v_t(\sigma_{t\ell}), \qquad d_t\leq m+1, \qquad \theta_{t\ell}\geq0, \qquad \sum_\ell\theta_{t\ell}=1. $$

Put $D=m+1$ and take $n>D$. In every interval, assign
$$ N_{t\ell}=\left\lceil(n-D)\theta_{t\ell}\right\rceil $$
of that interval’s $n$ type-$t$ pieces to $\sigma_{t\ell}$. This is feasible because
$$ \sum_\ell N_{t\ell}\leq n-D+d_t\leq n. $$
Assign remaining pieces to the empty sequence.

The resulting seed array $S^{[n]}$ satisfies
$$ S^{[n]}\geq\left(1-\frac{D}{n}\right)S_O. $$
Consequently, monotonicity and ray concavity give
$$ \Phi(S^{[n]}) \geq \left(1-\frac{D}{n}\right)\Phi(S_O). $$

The comparison in Section 2.1 applies to this allocation and $G_n$. Since $S_O$ was arbitrary,
$$ F(G_n)\geq \frac12\left(1-\frac{m+1}{n}\right)\mathrm{OPT}, \qquad n>m+1. $$
Taking the lower limit proves
$$ \boxed{\liminf_{n\to\infty}F(G_n)\geq\tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}.} $$

No offline maximizer or continuity at an activation boundary was assumed.

3. A two-stream obstruction: every policy has factor at most $\tfrac34$

The construction also proves sharpness of greedy’s half guarantee.

Take two ads, $k=1$, two intervals, and three types $A,B,C$. Choose rational parameters
$$ 0<b<\frac12,\qquad t>0, $$
and put
$$ \mu_A=M=1-2b,\qquad \mu_B=\mu_C=b. $$
Both revenues are one. Sharing probabilities are
$$ q_{1A}=q_{2A}=1,\qquad q_{1B}=1,\quad q_{2B}=0,\qquad q_{1C}=0,\quad q_{2C}=1. $$
Set all continuation probabilities to zero. The only positive intensities are
$$ \lambda_{1,B,A}=\frac{t}{b}, \qquad \lambda_{2,C,A}=\frac{t}{b}. $$

The first interval brings all mass $M$ of type $A$. The second brings either all mass $b$ of type $B$ or all mass $b$ of type $C$. Both streams are admissible and have the same revealed prefix.

3.1 Exact revenue formulas

There are no incoming transmission edges to $B$ or $C$, so their final activated masses equal their seed masses. The equations for type $A$ are therefore explicit:
$$ z_{1A} =s_{1A}+(M-s_{1A}) \left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{t}{b}s_{1B}\right)\right), $$
$$ z_{2A} =s_{2A}+(M-s_{2A}) \left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{t}{b}s_{2C}\right)\right). $$
These equations determine the unique fixed point in this acyclic instance.

Let $Y,W$ be the possibly random first-interval masses assigned to ads one and two. Then
$$ Y+W\leq M. $$

On the stream ending with $B$, the best possible later decision assigns all $b$ mass to ad one, by monotonicity. Conditional on $Y,W$, any policy’s final revenue is consequently at most
$$ b+M(1-e^{-t})+e^{-t}Y+W. $$
On the stream ending with $C$, the corresponding upper bound is
$$ b+M(1-e^{-t})+Y+e^{-t}W. $$

The law of $(Y,W)$ is the same on both streams. Averaging the conditional upper bounds and using $Y+W\leq M$ gives
$$ \frac{\mathbb E[F_B]+\mathbb E[F_C]}{2} \leq b+\frac{M(3-e^{-t})}{2}. $$

For the $B$ stream, offline assigns all type-$A$ mass to ad two and all type-$B$ mass to ad one. This is optimal: with $Y+W=M$, the best final revenue equals
$$ b+M(2-e^{-t})-(1-e^{-t})Y, $$
which is maximized at $Y=0$. By symmetry, the $C$ stream has the same optimum:
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=b+M(2-e^{-t}). $$

At least one of the two fixed streams thus has expected competitive ratio at most
$$ R(b,t)= \frac{b+M(3-e^{-t})/2} {b+M(2-e^{-t})}. $$

Take, for example, rational $b_j=1/(j+3)$ and $t_j=j$. Then
$$ \lim_{j\to\infty}R(b_j,t_j)=\frac34. $$
It follows that
$$ \boxed{\beta^\star\leq\tfrac34.} $$
This argument applies to randomized policies against fixed admissible streams.

3.2 Greedy’s factor is exactly $\tfrac12$

Use the same instance and choose the stream ending with $B$.

Before the second interval, revenue equals $Y+W$: no source type $B$ or $C$ has been seeded. Each positive type-$A$ piece has equal gains from the two singleton sequences. Lexicographic tie-breaking assigns it to ad one. Hence every refinement assigns all $M$ mass to ad one.

In the second interval, assigning a type-$B$ piece to ad one yields its direct-seed revenue. Type $A$ is already saturated for that ad. Therefore, for every $n$,
$$ F(G_n)=M+b. $$
Its ratio is
$$ \frac{M+b}{b+M(2-e^{-t})}. $$
Along the rational parameter sequence above, this tends to $\tfrac12$.

Together with Section 2, this proves that greedy’s limiting worst-case factor is exactly $\tfrac12$.

4. Fractional block greedy reduces the algorithmic question to concave optimization

Process the types of each interval in their listed order. A block consists of the interval’s entire mass $d=a_{ht}$ of one type $t$. The rates are known at the interval’s start, so these decisions can be computed then and prescribed for service during the interval.

Let $E_tv$ denote the seed array whose type-$t$ column is $v$, with other columns zero. At current seeds $S$, define
$$ M(S,d,t) = \sup_{v\in P_t} \left[\Phi(S+dE_tv)-\Phi(S)\right]. $$
This is maximization of a concave function on $P_t$, by Section 1.2.

Suppose block $j$ obtains gain at least $M_j-\eta_j$. The telescoping comparison from Section 2.1 applies also to fractional block increments, because those increments are nonnegative. It gives
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\leq2\Phi(S_G)+\sum_j\eta_j. $$

For each positive block put
$$ b_j=d_j\max_i r_iq_{it_j}. $$
Choosing a maximizing singleton on every block yields direct-seed revenue $\sum_j b_j$. Diffusion cannot decrease it, so
$$ B:=\sum_j b_j\leq\mathrm{OPT}. $$
If $b_j=0$, that type cannot seed any ad and the block can be skipped.

For the remaining blocks choose
$$ \eta_j=2\varepsilon b_j. $$
Then
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\leq2\Phi(S_G)+2\varepsilon\mathrm{OPT}, $$
and hence
$$ \Phi(S_G)\geq \left(\frac12-\varepsilon\right)\mathrm{OPT}. $$

It remains to prove that the required block approximations are computable in polynomial time.

5. Polynomial-time evaluation of the specified least fixed point

We prove a value-oracle lemma, including the zero-seed and numerical-conditioning cases.

Lemma. For rational seeds $0\leq s_t\leq\mu_t$, the quantity $\sum_tz_t(s)$ for one ad can be approximated to additive error $\xi>0$ in time polynomial in the rational input length, $\tau$, and $\log(1/\xi)$.

We use the standard ellipsoid oracle results: weak separation permits weak linear optimization of a compact convex body with explicit inner and outer balls; bounded-encoding rational polytopes admit exact separation, optimization, and constructive vertex decomposition from a linear optimization oracle. These are the relevant consequences of Theorems 3.1, 3.8, and 3.9 of [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/10046/10046D.pdf). The requisite balls and numerical oracles are supplied below.

5.1 Restricting to seeded reachable types

Suppress the ad index. Form the graph with edge $u\to t$ when $\lambda_{ut}>0$, and let $\mathcal R$ be the set reachable from $\{t:s_t>0\}$.

Iteration from Section 1 shows that
$$ z_t=0\quad\text{outside }\mathcal R, \qquad z_t>0\quad\text{on }\mathcal R. $$
If there are no positive seeds, return zero immediately.

Let $T_s$ be the fixed-point map. For every $\alpha>1$ and every $t\in\mathcal R$,
$$ T_s(\alpha z)_t<\alpha T_s(z)_t. $$
If $s_t>0$, strictness follows from the positive constant term. If $s_t=0$, positivity of $z_t$ implies $\sum_u\lambda_{ut}z_u>0$, and strictness follows from
$$ 1-e^{-\alpha x}<\alpha(1-e^{-x}), \qquad x>0. $$

Every nonnegative vector $v$ supported on $\mathcal R$ with $v\leq T_s(v)$ is dominated by $z$. Otherwise,
$$ \alpha=\max_{t\in\mathcal R}\frac{v_t}{z_t}>1. $$
At a maximizing coordinate,
$$ v_t\leq T_s(v)_t \leq T_s(\alpha z)_t <\alpha z_t=v_t, $$
a contradiction.

5.2 Convex constraints

Fix $v_t=\mu_t$ when $s_t=\mu_t$, and fix $v_t=0$ outside $\mathcal R$. If no variables remain, return the sum of these fixed coordinates.

For every remaining coordinate, $s_t<\mu_t$. The postfixed inequality is equivalent, for $v_t<\mu_t$, to
$$ g_t(v):= \log\frac{\mu_t-s_t}{\mu_t-v_t} -\sum_u\lambda_{ut}v_u \leq0. $$
Each $g_t$ is convex. Fixed coordinates are included in the linear sum.

The domination proved above implies that maximizing the sum of coordinates subject to these constraints recovers the least fixed-point value. We next introduce caps that make numerical evaluation well conditioned.

5.3 An explicit strict interior point

Let $\mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t$, let $s_{\min}$ be the smallest positive seed, and let $\lambda_{\min}$ be the smallest positive intensity, using $1$ if none exists. Define
$$ b_0=\frac14\min\{s_{\min},\mu_{\min}\}, \qquad \alpha_0=\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{\mu_{\min}\lambda_{\min}}4\right\}. $$
For each variable coordinate, let $\ell(t)$ be its shortest distance from a positively seeded type and set
$$ w_t=b_0\alpha_0^{\ell(t)}. $$
Retain the prescribed values on fixed coordinates.

If $s_t>0$, then $T_s(w)_t\geq s_t\geq4w_t$. Otherwise, a predecessor on a shortest path has value at least $w_t/\alpha_0$. Using
$$ 1-e^{-x}\geq\min\{x/2,1/2\},\qquad x\geq0, $$
and $w_t\leq\mu_{\min}/4$, gives $T_s(w)_t\geq2w_t$.

Thus, for every variable coordinate,
$$ \begin{aligned} g_t(w) &=\log\frac{\mu_t-T_s(w)_t}{\mu_t-w_t}\\ &\leq-\frac{T_s(w)_t-w_t}{\mu_t-w_t} \leq-w_t. \end{aligned} $$
The last inequality uses $\mu_t-w_t\leq1$.

Write $w_{\min}$ for the smallest variable coordinate of $w$. All shortest-path distances are at most $\tau-1$, so $w$ and $w_{\min}$ have polynomial encoding length.

For $0<\theta<1/4$, let $C_\theta$ be the convex set defined by the $g_t$ constraints and
$$ 0\leq v_t\leq(1-\theta/2)\mu_t $$
on variable coordinates.

Let $\lambda_{\max}$ be the largest intensity, or zero if all are zero. An inner-ball radius around $w$ is
$$ r= \min\left\{ \frac{w_{\min}}4,\, \frac{\mu_{\min}}8,\, \frac{w_{\min}}{4(2/\mu_{\min}+\tau\lambda_{\max})} \right\}. $$
Indeed, throughout this ball, variable coordinates remain positive and below $\mu_t/2$, and
$$ \|\nabla g_t\|_1\leq2/\mu_{\min}+\tau\lambda_{\max}. $$
The resulting change in $g_t$ is at most $w_{\min}/4$, preserving negative slack. The box has diameter at most one, giving an explicit outer-radius bound.

The feasible vector
$$ (1-\theta)z+\theta w $$
satisfies the caps, since $w_t\leq\mu_t/4$. Its objective loss is at most $\theta$, because $\sum_t\mu_t=1$. Therefore, including fixed-coordinate contributions,
$$ \sum_tz_t-\theta \leq \max_{v\in C_\theta}\sum_tv_t \leq \sum_tz_t. $$

5.4 A weak separation oracle and accuracy accounting

On the capped box,
$$ \|\nabla g_t\|_1\leq M_g:=\frac{2}{\theta\mu_{\min}}+\tau\lambda_{\max}. $$
Gradients are rational and computable exactly.

For a requested weak-separation tolerance $\delta$, evaluate each $g_t$ with error at most
$$ \gamma=\frac{\delta w_{\min}}8. $$
First separate any violated box constraint. Otherwise:

  • If an estimated $g_t(v)$ exceeds $\gamma$, its true value is positive. Convexity makes its gradient a separating normal. Divide the normal by $\max\{1,\|\nabla g_t(v)\|_1\}$ to give norm at most one.
  • If no estimate exceeds $\gamma$, then all true violations are at most $2\gamma$. Mixing $v$ with $w$ with weight

$$ \frac{2\gamma}{w_{\min}+2\gamma} $$
makes every constraint nonpositive. Its distance from $v$ is at most $2\gamma/w_{\min}\leq\delta/4$, because the box diameter is at most one.

This is a valid weak separation oracle with polynomial bit complexity.

For $0<\xi<1$, choose $\theta=\xi/8$. Request weak optimization with distance and objective errors at most
$$ \kappa= \min\left\{ \frac14,\, \frac{\xi}{16(\tau+1+M_g/w_{\min})} \right\}. $$
Clip the returned vector to the capped box. Clipping loses at most $\tau\kappa$ in the objective and leaves violations at most $M_g\kappa$. Mixing with $w$ with weight
$$ \frac{M_g\kappa}{w_{\min}+M_g\kappa} $$
repairs feasibility and loses at most $M_g\kappa/w_{\min}$.

Together with the cap loss and optimization error, the total loss is at most
$$ \theta+(\tau+1)\kappa+\frac{M_g\kappa}{w_{\min}}<\xi. $$
The repaired vector is rational, feasible, and dominated by $z$, so its coordinate sum gives the required approximation.

If the remaining variable dimension is one, apply the convex-body theorem to $C_\theta\times[-1,1]$ with zero objective coefficient on the added coordinate. The zero-dimensional case was already terminated explicitly. If $\xi\geq1$, returning zero suffices.

Finally, logarithms of positive rationals admit polynomial-time approximation. For example, reduce the argument to $2^ev$ with $1\leq v<2$, and use
$$ \log v = 2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{2j+1}}{2j+1}, \qquad w=\frac{v-1}{v+1}, \qquad 0\leq w<\frac13. $$
The geometric tail bound gives polynomial dependence on the input and accuracy bit lengths. This completes the lemma.

Applying the lemma separately to ads, with error budgets accounting for their rational revenues, supplies a polynomial-time additive value oracle for $\Phi$.

6. Polynomial-time optimization of an arriving block

6.1 Linear optimization over the sequence polytope

For rational weights $w_i$, linear optimization over $P_t$ asks for
$$ \max_\sigma \sum_j w_{\sigma(j)}q_{\sigma(j),t} \prod_{\ell<j}c_{\sigma(\ell),t}. $$

Ads with nonpositive weighted reward can be removed without decreasing the objective. To justify this, remove the last such ad first: its suffix contains only positive weighted rewards, and deletion removes both the nonpositive reward and a continuation discount.

For the remaining ads put $b_i=w_iq_{it}>0$. Interchanging adjacent ads $i,j$ changes the local objective by
$$ b_i(1-c_{jt})-b_j(1-c_{it}). $$
Thus an optimum exists with selected ads ordered by nonincreasing
$$ \frac{b_i}{1-c_{it}}, $$
placing positive-reward ads with $c_{it}=1$ first.

After sorting the remaining $M'$ ads, use
$$ V_{M'+1,\ell}=0\quad(0\leq\ell\leq k), \qquad V_{j,0}=0, $$
and, for $1\leq j\leq M'$ and $1\leq\ell\leq k$,
$$ V_{j,\ell} = \max\{V_{j+1,\ell},\ b_j+c_{jt}V_{j+1,\ell-1}\}. $$
Backtracking returns a maximizing sequence. The running time and rational bit complexity are polynomial.

Delete coordinates with $q_{it}=0$, and let $a$ be the remaining dimension. The polytope contains
$$ \operatorname{conv}\{0,q_{it}e_i:q_{it}>0\}. $$
For $a\geq1$, an explicit interior point and inner radius are
$$ u_i^0=\frac{q_{it}}{2a}, \qquad r_P=\frac{q_{\min}}{4a^2}. $$
The radius follows by checking nonnegativity and $\sum_i u_i/q_{it}\leq1$ throughout the ball.

Every sequence-vector coordinate is a product of at most $k$ input probabilities, so vertices have polynomial encoding length. The rational-polytope oracle results therefore provide strong separation and a constructive decomposition of a rational point into at most $a+1$ sequence vectors. Sequence witnesses are retained from the linear-oracle calls used to produce vertices.

If $a=0$, the block is useless. If $a=1$, its maximizing singleton is optimal by monotonicity. Below assume $a\geq2$, so also $m\geq2$.

6.2 Regularization and a Lipschitz bound

Consider a block of mass $d>0$ with desired gain error $\eta>0$. Put $R_0=\sum_i r_i$ and use the exploration vector
$$ v_i^0=\frac{q_{it}}m, $$
which is implemented by equal allocation to all singleton sequences.

Choose a positive dyadic $\rho$ satisfying
$$ \frac{\eta}{32R_0}<\rho\leq\frac{\eta}{16R_0}. $$
For the blocks in Section 4, $\eta<R_0$, so $\rho<1/16$.

Optimize
$$ g_\rho(u) = \Phi\bigl(S+dE_t((1-\rho)u+\rho v^0)\bigr)-\Phi(S), \qquad u\in P_t. $$
Concavity and nonnegative gains give
