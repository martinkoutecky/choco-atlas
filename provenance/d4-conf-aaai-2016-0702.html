The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(A\) of complete person types and room classes \(Q\), with binary happiness values \(h_{ab}\) and room values \(v_{aq}\), rational type masses \(\mu_a \ge 0\) summing to \(1\), and rational room supplies \(\rho_q \ge 0\) summing to \(\tfrac12\), choose nonnegative rational masses \(x_{abq}\) for \(a \le b\) and \(q \in Q\) to maximize \(\sum_{q \in Q}\sum_{a \le b} x_{abq}(h_{ab}+h_{ba}+v_{aq}+v_{bq})\), subject to \(\sum_{a \le b}x_{abq}=\rho_q\) for each \(q\) and \(2\sum_q x_{aaq}+\sum_{b<a,q}x_{baq}+\sum_{b>a,q}x_{abq}=\mu_a\) for each \(a\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(A\): the list of its \(\tau\) complete person types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(Q\) contain \(m\) room classes with binary-rational supplies \(\rho_q\geq 0\), summing to \(\tfrac12\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For every \(a,b\in A\) and \(q\in Q\), the input specifies directed happiness \(h_{ab}\in\{0,1\}\) and room value \(v_{aq}\in\{0,1\}\). The value \(h_{aa}\) concerns two distinct people of the same type; happiness need not be symmetric.
Fix any ordering of \(A\). An allocation consists of divisible, nonnegative rational room masses \(x_{abq}\) for \(a\leq b\): each such room houses one person of each indicated type, or two distinct people of type \(a\) when \(a=b\).
Require \(\sum_{a\leq b}x_{abq}=\rho_q\) for every \(q\), and \(2\sum_qx_{aaq}+\sum_{b<a}\sum_qx_{baq}+\sum_{b>a}\sum_qx_{abq}=\mu_a\) for every \(a\).
Maximize welfare \(W(x)=\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{a\leq b}x_{abq}(h_{ab}+h_{ba}+v_{aq}+v_{bq})\). No stability or envy-freeness condition is imposed; rents do not enter welfare.

Is exact welfare maximization for this mass allocation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies with two distinct people per room, exact welfare maximization is NP-hard even when all happiness and room values belong to \(\{0,1\}\). This discrete result does not classify the mass problem stated above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Omit zero-mass person types from the support, as required by the support-input convention; retain nonnegative room supplies.
  • Interpret same-type pairs as two distinct people of that type, with binary diagonal happiness h_aa specified as input.
  • Use an arbitrary ordering of the person types solely to index unordered pairs.
  • Define m as the number of room classes and L as the total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Claim. Exact welfare maximization is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Both an optimal rational allocation and its exact welfare can be computed.

Linear-program formulation. Let \(I=\{(a,b,q):a,b\in A,\ a\leq b,\ q\in Q\}\). There are \(N=m\tau(\tau+1)/2\) variables. For each index, define \(c_{abq}=h_{ab}+h_{ba}+v_{aq}+v_{bq}\), so \(c_{abq}\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\).

Write the allocation constraints as \(Mx=d\), where the rows consist of the \(m\) room-supply equations and the \(\tau\) person-mass equations. The column for \(x_{abq}\) has:

  • coefficient \(1\) in room row \(q\);
  • coefficients \(1\) in person rows \(a,b\) when \(a<b\);
  • coefficient \(2\) in person row \(a\) when \(a=b\);
  • zero in every other row.

Thus the optimization problem is precisely \(\max\{c^\mathsf{T}x:Mx=d,\ x\geq0\}\), with the additional requirement that its output be rational. All matrix coefficients belong to \(\{0,1,2\}\), and \(d\) consists of the input supplies and masses. Even a dense encoding of this linear program has length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Feasibility and existence of an optimum. Initially allow real coordinates and denote the resulting feasible set by \(P\). Define \(x^0_{aaq}=\mu_a\rho_q\) and \(x^0_{abq}=0\) for \(a<b\). For every room class, \(\sum_a x^0_{aaq}=\rho_q\sum_a\mu_a=\rho_q\). For every person type, \(2\sum_qx^0_{aaq}=2\mu_a\sum_q\rho_q=\mu_a\). Hence \(P\) is nonempty, and this particular allocation is rational.

Every feasible coordinate satisfies \(0\leq x_{abq}\leq\rho_q\leq\tfrac12\). Consequently, \(P\) is closed and bounded, hence compact. The linear objective therefore attains a maximum over \(P\). Moreover, \(0\leq W(x)\leq4\sum_q\rho_q=2\).

Exactness and output size. We next show that some maximizer is rational with polynomial encoding length.

Choose a maximizer \(x^*\) with the smallest possible number \(k\) of positive coordinates, and let \(S\) be its support. The columns of \(M\) indexed by \(S\) are linearly independent. Indeed, otherwise there would be a nonzero vector \(z\), supported on \(S\), satisfying \(Mz=0\). Both \(x^*+\varepsilon z\) and \(x^*-\varepsilon z\) are feasible for sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), because all coordinates of \(x^*\) in \(S\) are positive. Optimality forces \(c^\mathsf{T}z=0\); otherwise one of these perturbations would improve welfare.

Change the sign of \(z\), if necessary, so that some coordinate is positive. Set \(t=\min_{j\in S:z_j>0}x^*_j/z_j\). Then \(x^*-tz\) is feasible, has the same welfare, and has strictly fewer positive coordinates. This contradicts the choice of \(x^*\), proving column independence.

In particular, \(k\leq m+\tau\). Select \(k\) rows on which these columns form a nonsingular integer matrix \(B\). The positive coordinates satisfy \(Bx^*_S=d'\), where \(d'\) is the selected portion of \(d\).

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses and supplies. Its binary length is \(O(L)\), and \(Dd'\) is integral. Cramer’s rule gives \(x^*_j=\det(B_j)/(D\det B)\), where \(B_j\) replaces the corresponding column of \(B\) by \(Dd'\).

Every entry of \(B\) has absolute value at most \(2\), and every entry of \(Dd'\) has absolute value at most \(D\). Expanding determinants therefore gives \(|\det B|\leq k!2^k\) and \(|\det B_j|\leq k!2^{k-1}D\). Each coordinate consequently has encoding length \(O(L+k\log(k+1))\). Thus \(x^*\) is a legal rational allocation of polynomial total encoding length, and the maximum over real allocations equals the maximum over the required rational allocations.

Polynomial-time computation. The exact rational linear-programming theorem states that an explicitly specified rational linear program can be solved in time polynomial in its encoding length, returning an exact rational optimum when the feasible set is nonempty and the optimum is finite. This is the standard ellipsoid-method guarantee; see [Theorem 1 in Yang Cai’s thesis](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/cai/publication/thesis/thesis.pdf#page=38).

All its hypotheses hold here: the program is explicitly specified, has rational coefficients, has polynomial encoding length, is feasible, and is bounded. Applying that theorem returns an optimal rational allocation in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Evaluating \(c^\mathsf{T}x^*\) by exact rational arithmetic also takes polynomial time.

The stated discrete NP-hardness result concerns additional indivisibility requirements. Those requirements are absent from this mass problem, whose feasible allocations are exactly the rational points of the linear program above.
