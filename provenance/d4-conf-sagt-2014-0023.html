The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item kinds, \(\tau\) buyer types with normalized monotone super-additive valuations \(v_t:2^M\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge0\) summing to \(1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\ge0\), compute a welfare-maximizing market-clearing equilibrium. The allocation variables \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) must satisfy \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_j\); each positive-mass bundle \(S\) must maximize \(v_t(S)-\sum_{j\in S}p_j\) at item prices \(p_j\ge0\), and unused supply must have price zero. What is the complexity in \(m,\tau\), input bit length, and valuation-oracle access?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) contain \(m\ge1\) item kinds. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible valuations. Supplies are binary rationals \(b_j\ge0\).
Each type has a normalized monotone super-additive valuation \(v_t:2^M\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\). Buyers consume at most one copy of each kind. Access consists of value queries and exact demand queries at nonnegative rational item prices; a demand query may return any maximizing bundle.
An allocation consists of rational masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\), with \(\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_j\). The empty bundle is permitted.
An equilibrium additionally has rational prices \(p_j\ge0\): whenever \(x_{t,S}>0\), \(S\in\arg\max_{T\subseteq M}\{v_t(T)-\sum_{j\in T}p_j\}\); any kind with unused supply has price zero. These are item prices, with no seller-chosen prebundling.
Write \(W(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), and let \(W^*\) be the maximum welfare over feasible allocations without equilibrium constraints. Let \(L\) include the input encoding length and a bound on the binary length of each valuation returned by an oracle.

Can an exact equilibrium with welfare at least \(W^*/(C\sqrt m)\), for a universal constant \(C\), always be computed in time and oracle calls polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this approximation guarantee impossible within those bounds?

1. Answer

An exact equilibrium can always be computed in time and oracle calls polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Moreover, its welfare is exactly \(W^*\). Thus the requested guarantee holds with \(C=1\).

The allocation model permits rational masses of buyers to receive different bundles. This makes the configuration linear program the actual welfare optimization problem. The proof below extends the fractional optimality conditions discussed by [Nisan–Segal, Section 9.1](https://web.stanford.edu/~isegal/prices.pdf) to the stated rational masses and supplies.

2. The welfare program and its dual

Write \(p(S)=\sum_{j\in S}p_j\). Consider the finite, though exponentially large, linear program \(P\):

\[ \max_x \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{S\subseteq M}v_t(S)x_{t,S}, \]

subject to

\[ \sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t \quad\text{for every }t, \]

\[ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{S\subseteq M:\,j\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_j \quad\text{for every }j, \qquad x_{t,S}\ge0. \]

This program is feasible: assign every type its entire mass on the empty bundle. Its feasible region is compact, since every coordinate satisfies \(0\le x_{t,S}\le\mu_t\). Its data are rational, so an optimal rational solution exists. Consequently, its optimum is precisely the \(W^*\) in the question.

Associate a dual variable \(u_t\) with each type equality and a nonnegative dual variable \(p_j\) with each supply inequality. The dual program \(D\) is

\[ \min_{u,p} F(u,p) =\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tu_t+\sum_{j\in M}b_jp_j, \]

subject to

\[ u_t+p(S)\ge v_t(S) \quad\text{for every }t,\ S\subseteq M, \qquad p_j\ge0. \]

Although equality-constraint dual variables are initially unrestricted, the constraint for \(S=\varnothing\) gives \(u_t\ge0\), because normalization means \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\).

The dual is feasible: take \(p=0\) and \(u_t=v_t(M)\), using monotonicity. Strong linear programming duality therefore gives attained primal and dual optima with common value \(W^*\).

3. Optimal solutions give exactly the required equilibrium

For any primal-feasible \(x\) and dual-feasible \((u,p)\), the duality gap has the following exact decomposition:

\[ F(u,p)-W(x) = \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}\bigl(u_t+p(S)-v_t(S)\bigr) + \sum_jp_j\left(b_j-\sum_{t,S:\,j\in S}x_{t,S}\right). \]

To verify the identity, expand the right side, use \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\), and cancel the two occurrences of total payments. Every summand is nonnegative by feasibility.

Now take optimal primal and dual solutions. Their gap is zero, so every summand is zero.

If \(x_{t,S}>0\), it follows that

\[ v_t(S)-p(S)=u_t. \]

Dual feasibility also gives

\[ v_t(T)-p(T)\le u_t \quad\text{for every }T\subseteq M. \]

Thus every positively allocated bundle maximizes that type’s utility.

If kind \(j\) has unused supply, its supply slack is strictly positive. The corresponding zero product in the gap decomposition forces \(p_j=0\).

These are precisely the equilibrium conditions in the question. Hence optimal primal and dual solutions produce an equilibrium of welfare \(W^*\).

Conversely, suppose \((x,p)\) is any equilibrium and define

\[ u_t=\max_{S\subseteq M}\bigl(v_t(S)-p(S)\bigr). \]

The empty bundle ensures \(u_t\ge0\), and these variables are dual feasible. The equilibrium demand condition makes every positively weighted bundle term in the gap decomposition zero. The unused-supply condition makes every supply term zero. Therefore \(F(u,p)=W(x)\); weak duality implies \(W(x)=W^*\).

Thus every equilibrium under the stated definition is welfare optimal.

4. A bounded dual and separation using permitted queries

Query every \(v_t(M)\), and let

\[ V=\max_{1\le t\le\tau}v_t(M). \]

If \(V=0\), monotonicity and nonnegativity imply that all valuations vanish. Assigning everyone the empty bundle at zero prices is then an optimal equilibrium. Henceforth assume \(V>0\).

Bounding prices and utilities

The dual optimum is unchanged by adding

\[ 0\le u_t\le V,\qquad 0\le p_j\le V. \]

Indeed, start with any dual-feasible \((u,p)\) and replace each coordinate by its minimum with \(V\). Consider a constraint \(u_t+p(S)\ge v_t(S)\).

If none of its participating coordinates exceeds \(V\), the constraint is unchanged. Otherwise, at least one participating coordinate becomes \(V\); all others remain nonnegative. Its left side is therefore at least \(V\), while monotonicity gives \(v_t(S)\le V\). So the clipped vector remains feasible.

Every objective coefficient is nonnegative, so clipping cannot increase the objective. This argument also applies when only an arbitrary subset of the bundle constraints is retained—a fact needed for allocation recovery.

Exact separation

Given a rational candidate \((u,p)\), first check the coordinate bounds. If they hold, all prices are nonnegative, so the allowed demand oracle can be used.

For each type \(t\), request any bundle

\[ S_t\in\arg\max_{S\subseteq M}\bigl(v_t(S)-p(S)\bigr), \]

and query its value. If \(v_t(S_t)-p(S_t)>u_t\), return the violated original constraint

\[ u_t+p(S_t)\ge v_t(S_t). \]

Otherwise every bundle constraint for that type holds, because the returned bundle attains the maximum utility. After checking all types, either a violated constraint has been returned or feasibility has been established.

One separation call uses at most \(\tau\) demand queries and \(\tau\) value queries. Arbitrary tie-breaking causes no difficulty: every maximizing bundle gives the same maximum utility.

5. Exact optimization and allocation recovery

We use the following standard rational linear programming theorem, in its constraint-generation form.

Oracle LP theorem. Let a finite rational linear program have \(d\) variables, a known rational bounding box, and polynomial bounds on the binary lengths of its objective and inequality coefficients. Suppose a strong separation oracle either certifies feasibility or returns a violated original inequality. Then exact optimization requires polynomial time and polynomially many oracle calls. Moreover, one can obtain polynomially many original inequalities such that retaining them together with the bounding box preserves the optimum.

The last assertion is the usual primal-recovery property: retained dual constraints identify enough primal variables to recover an optimal primal solution. See [Lecture 8, Section 3, “Using the Dual”](https://www.dcs.warwick.ac.uk/~u2470130/E0249/Notes/lec8.pdf).

Checking the hypotheses and exactness

Here the dimension is \(d=m+\tau\). The bounding box is \([0,V]^d\). Bundle inequalities have coefficients in \(\{0,1\}\), and their right sides have binary length bounded by \(L\). Objective coefficients are the explicitly supplied rational masses and supplies. Section 4 supplies the required strong separation oracle.

For completeness, the exponential number of inequalities does not introduce exponentially long optimum values. At any vertex of the boxed dual, select \(d\) linearly independent tight inequalities. Their coefficient matrix \(A\) has entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), so Hadamard’s inequality gives

\[ |\det A|\le d^{d/2}. \]

Each selected right side has denominator at most \(2^L\). Clearing these denominators and applying Cramer’s rule shows that the coordinates have a common denominator at most \(2^{dL}d^{d/2}\). The objective coefficients have a common denominator at most \(2^{dL}\). Therefore an optimum value has denominator at most

\[ Q=2^{2dL+d^2}. \]

The same bound applies to every restriction obtained by deleting bundle constraints while keeping the box. Coordinate magnitudes are bounded by \(V\), so numerators also have polynomial binary length. In particular, distinct such optimum values differ by at least \(Q^{-2}\). The rational oracle theorem therefore applies to exact optimization, with polynomial precision requirements.

It returns an exact optimal dual vector \((u^*,p^*)\) and a polynomial-size set \(\mathcal K\) of type–bundle constraints preserving the boxed optimum. Add every empty-bundle constraint if necessary; adding these constraints preserves that optimum.

Recovering the allocation

Let \(D_{\mathcal K}\) retain only the bundle constraints indexed by \(\mathcal K\), together with nonnegativity. The clipping argument shows that adding the box does not change its optimum. Hence the *unboxed* \(D_{\mathcal K}\) also has optimum \(W^*\).

Its primal \(P_{\mathcal K}\) is exactly the welfare program of Section 2 restricted to variables \(x_{t,S}\) with \((t,S)\in\mathcal K\). It has polynomially many variables and constraints. It is feasible because all empty-bundle variables were included, and bounded because the type masses bound every coordinate.

Strong duality gives optimum \(W^*\) for \(P_{\mathcal K}\). Solve this explicit rational program exactly and extend its solution by zero on all omitted variables. The resulting \(x^*\) is feasible and optimal for the full welfare program.

Pairing \(x^*\) with the full dual optimizer \((u^*,p^*)\), Section 3 proves that \((x^*,p^*)\) is the required exact equilibrium.

This recovery step determines the appropriate mixture of bundles; it does not require a demand oracle to enumerate all bundles tied at the final prices.

6. Output size and conclusion

The allocation can be returned sparsely, listing only positive masses. Introduce one slack variable for each of the \(m\) supply inequalities. The restricted primal then has \(m+\tau\) equality rows. An optimal basic feasible solution therefore has at most \(m+\tau\) positive variables, counting supply slacks, and hence at most \(m+\tau\) positive allocation masses. Such an optimal basic solution can be found by standard polynomial-time rational LP methods.

Its masses have polynomial binary length: each basis uses at most \(m+\tau\) columns with entries in \(\{0,1\}\), and its right side consists of the input masses and supplies, so Cramer’s rule again supplies polynomial bounds. Prices likewise have polynomial binary length by the boxed-dual argument.

The entire computation consequently uses polynomial time and polynomially many permitted oracle calls in \(m,\tau,L\), without expanding rational masses into individual buyers.

The proof does not require super-additivity. The discrete seller-bundling approximation concerns allocations subject to different constraints; the fractional masses permitted here make exact welfare optimization possible. The computed equilibrium satisfies \(W(x^*)=W^*\), and therefore

\[ W(x^*)\ge \frac{W^*}{\sqrt m} \qquad\text{for every }m\ge1. \]
