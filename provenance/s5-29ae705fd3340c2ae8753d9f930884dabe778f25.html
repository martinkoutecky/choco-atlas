The continuous mirror question, as graded

Input candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), and a finite set of approval types \(\mathcal T\subseteq 2^C\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Decide whether there is a committee \(W\subseteq C\), \(|W|=k\), such that for every \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), every \(S\subseteq C\) with \(|S|=\ell\), and every submass vector \(\nu\) with \(0\le\nu_t\le\mu_t\), \(\nu_t=0\) whenever \(S\nsubseteq t\), and \(\sum_t\nu_t\ge\ell/k\), the weighted average satisfaction \(\frac{\sum_t\nu_t|W\cap t|}{\sum_t\nu_t}\) is at least \(\ell\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Input a candidate set \(C\) with \(m=|C|\) and an integer committee size \(1\le k\le m\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal T\subseteq 2^C\) of \(\tau\) distinct approval types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\mu_t=1\). Empty approval types are allowed. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L,k\).
A committee is a subset \(W\subseteq C\) with \(|W|=k\); type \(t\)'s satisfaction is \(|W\cap t|\).
Call \(W\) mass-AJR if, for every integer \(1\le\ell\le k\), every \(S\subseteq C\) with \(|S|=\ell\), and every real submass vector \(\nu\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal T}\) satisfying \(0\le\nu_t\le\mu_t\), \(\nu_t=0\) whenever \(S\nsubseteq t\), and \(\sum_t\nu_t\ge\ell/k\), one has \(\frac{\sum_t\nu_t|W\cap t|}{\sum_t\nu_t}\ge\ell\).

Is deciding whether a mass-AJR committee exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,k\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For explicitly listed finite voters, AJR committee existence is \(\Sigma_2^p\)-complete. [Ai and Tao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29643)
No result settling the exact mass-AJR existence problem defined here was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted the committee size to an integer between 1 and the number of candidates, as required by the discrete election definition.
  • Specified that universally quantified submass coordinates are real, so each type's mass is arbitrarily divisible.
  • Explicitly allowed empty approval types, as the discrete model allows empty ballots.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Mass-AJR existence is \(\Sigma_2^p\)-complete

In particular, the problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The proof uses a feature of the cited finite-voter reduction: its output has an integral Hare quota. On such instances, allowing divisible submasses does not change AJR.

1. Eliminating the quantification over real submasses

Fix a committee \(W\), an integer \(1\leq\ell\leq k\), and a candidate set \(S\) of size \(\ell\). Write
\(E_S=\{t\in\mathcal T:S\subseteq t\}\),
\(s_t=|W\cap t|\), and \(q=\ell/k\).

If \(\sum_{t\in E_S}\mu_t<q\), there is no eligible submass, so this pair imposes no constraint.

Otherwise, define
\(\Phi(W,S)=\min\{\sum_{t\in E_S}x_ts_t:0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t,\ \sum_{t\in E_S}x_t=q\}\).
The feasible region is nonempty and compact, so the minimum exists.

Lemma 1. The mass-AJR requirement for this pair is equivalent to
\(\Phi(W,S)\geq\ell^2/k\).

Proof. Any violating submass \(\nu\) of total mass \(a\geq q\) can be scaled to \(x=(q/a)\nu\). This preserves eligibility and the capacity bounds, gives total mass \(q\), and preserves average satisfaction. Consequently,
\(\sum_t x_ts_t<\ell q=\ell^2/k\).
Conversely, a feasible vector attaining a value below \(\ell^2/k\) is itself a violating submass. \(\square\)

Lemma 2. The minimum defining \(\Phi(W,S)\) is obtained by taking eligible mass in nondecreasing order of satisfaction, stopping at total mass \(q\).

Proof. Order the eligible types as \(t_1,\ldots,t_p\), with satisfactions \(s_1\leq\cdots\leq s_p\). Let \(g\) be the stated greedy vector. For every feasible \(x\) and every \(r\),
\(\sum_{i=1}^r x_i\leq\min\{q,\sum_{i=1}^r\mu_{t_i}\}=\sum_{i=1}^r g_i\).
The identity
\(\sum_{i=1}^p s_ix_i=s_pq-\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}(s_{r+1}-s_r)\sum_{i=1}^r x_i\)
then shows that the objective at \(g\) is at most the objective at \(x\). \(\square\)

Thus, a fixed pair can be checked exactly in polynomial time.

2. Exact correspondence for an integral Hare quota

Consider an explicitly listed profile of \(n\) voters, where type \(t\) occurs \(n_t\) times. Aggregate it into masses \(\mu_t=n_t/n\), keeping the candidates and committee size unchanged.

Lemma 3. If \(k\mid n\), a committee satisfies finite-voter AJR if and only if it satisfies mass-AJR in the aggregated instance.

Proof. A finite-voter violation immediately gives a mass violation: if the violating group contains \(a_t\) voters of type \(t\), use \(\nu_t=a_t/n\). Its mass and average satisfaction are exactly the normalized size and average satisfaction of that group.

For the other direction, suppose there is a mass violation for \(\ell,S\). By Lemma 1, there is a violation of total mass exactly \(\ell/k\). Multiply its coordinates by \(n\). We obtain a feasible vector for
\(\min\{\sum_{t\in E_S}z_ts_t:0\leq z_t\leq n_t,\ \sum_{t\in E_S}z_t=Q\}\),
where \(Q=\ell n/k\), whose value is strictly below \(\ell Q\).

By the divisibility assumption, \(Q\) is an integer. All capacities \(n_t\) are integers too. The greedy minimizer from Lemma 2 therefore has integer coordinates: each full coordinate is an integer capacity, and the final coordinate is an integer remaining quota.

Choose that many actual voters of each type. The resulting group has exactly \(Q\) voters, all approve \(S\), and its total satisfaction is strictly below \(\ell Q\). It is a finite-voter AJR violation. \(\square\)

The divisibility hypothesis is what makes this transfer valid; aggregation of an arbitrary finite profile alone would not establish equivalence.

3. The finite hardness reduction has the required divisibility

The following is an inference from the published reduction, rather than an assumption about arbitrary finite profiles.

[Ai and Tao, version 2, Lemmas 3.8 and 3.12](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29643v2#S3.SS3), establish \(\Sigma_2^p\)-hardness for the auxiliary \(\lambda\)-AJR existence problem with an integer quota \(2<\lambda\leq n\). Lemma 3.13 converts it into ordinary finite-voter AJR. Its output satisfies \(n'=\lambda k'\) in every case:

  • If \(\lambda k\geq n\), the construction gives \(k'=k\) and \(n'=\lambda k\).
  • Otherwise, put \(d=n-\lambda k\) and \(\xi=(\lambda-1)d\). The construction gives \(k'=k+\xi\) and

\(n'=n+\xi(\lambda-1)+(\lambda-2)d=\lambda(k+\xi)=\lambda k'\).

These are polynomial-size constructions because \(\lambda\leq n\). Thus their reduction proves finite AJR existence \(\Sigma_2^p\)-hard even under \(k'\mid n'\). The reduction equivalences themselves are the cited known results.

4. Polynomial reduction to mass-AJR

Apply the preceding finite-voter reduction, then aggregate identical ballots and assign mass \(n_t/n'\) to each occurring type.

The output meets every input requirement:

  • Its types are distinct and have positive rational masses summing to \(1\).
  • Empty ballots, if present, become an allowed empty type.
  • Candidates and committee size are preserved.
  • Its support size satisfies \(\tau\leq n'\).
  • Using candidate incidence vectors, its encoding length is

\(L=O(m\tau+\tau\log(n'+1)+\log(m+1))\).

Aggregation and rational encoding take polynomial time in the explicitly listed profile size. Since \(k'\mid n'\), Lemma 3 preserves the qualifying committees exactly. Composing the reductions proves \(\Sigma_2^p\)-hardness of mass-AJR existence.

This argument never expands a general rational-mass input into voters.

5. Membership and running times

For completeness, the real submass quantification also permits a matching upper bound.

Let \(D\) be the product of the input mass denominators, and set \(b_t=D\mu_t\). These are integers, and \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits. For a fixed pair \(\ell,S\), multiply the exact-quota optimization by \(kD\). Its capacities become \(kb_t\), its required total becomes \(\ell D\), and the satisfaction threshold becomes \(\ell^2D\).

Lemma 2 computes its minimum directly, without constructing voters. Satisfaction values lie in \(\{0,\ldots,k\}\), so bucket ordering takes \(O(\tau+k)\) operations. Put
\(B=L+\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil+1\).
With elementary integer arithmetic, a sufficient bit-operation bound for checking a fixed committee and pair is
\(O((m\tau+\tau+k)B^2)\).
This includes scanning approval sets, ordering types, and performing exact arithmetic.

Consequently, non-mass-AJR has a polynomial certificate consisting only of \(\ell,S\). Equivalently, existence has the form
\(\exists W\subseteq C,\ |W|=k,\ \forall(\ell,S)\ P(W,\ell,S)\),
where \(P\) is the polynomial-time predicate supplied by Lemmas 1 and 2; invalid pairs are accepted automatically. This proves membership in \(\Sigma_2^p\).

An explicit exhaustive algorithm has running time
\(O\!\left(\binom{m}{k}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\binom{m}{\ell}\right)(m\tau+\tau+k)B^2\right)\).
In particular, fixed \(k\) gives polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), while the general problem is \(\Sigma_2^p\)-complete.
