The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) and a tree character \(\chi\), construct rational masses \(\mu_A\geq 0\), summing to \(1\), on voter types \(t_A\) with utility \(u_A(x)=\mathbf{1}[|x\cap A|\text{ is even}]\), for nonempty \(A\subseteq[n]\), such that \(\sum_A\mu_Au_A\) has character exactly \(\chi\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given \(n\geq1\), let \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and let outcomes be subsets of \([n]\). The input is an explicitly listed family \(\chi\subseteq\mathcal P([n])\), containing \(\emptyset\) and \([n]\), whose members are pairwise nested or disjoint; call this a tree character. Encode its \(k=|\chi|\) members as incidence vectors, and let \(L\) be the input bit length.
For every nonempty \(A\subseteq[n]\), type \(t_A\) has utility \(u_A(x)=\mathbf1[|x\cap A|\text{ is even}]\). Construct nonnegative rational masses with \(\sum_A\mu_A=1\), and define \(U(x)=\sum_A\mu_Au_A(x)\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number \(2^n-1\) of possible types; omitted types have zero mass.
Preferences satisfy \(x\succeq y\iff U(x)\geq U(y)\); equal utilities remain tied. A set \(S\subseteq[n]\) is separable if, for every \(X,Y\subseteq S\) and \(Z\subseteq[n]\setminus S\), \(U(X)\geq U(Y)\iff U(X\cup Z)\geq U(Y\cup Z)\). The character is the collection of all separable sets.
The required output is a society whose aggregate utility has character exactly \(\chi\). State bit running times in \(m=n\), \(k\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), accounting for the binary output length; polynomial time means polynomial in the input length.

Can this society be constructed in polynomial time, or is this construction problem NP-hard under polynomial-time search reductions?

Every tree character is admissible as a character of preferences on finite binary outcomes ([Beveridge–Calaway](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11083-021-09553-8)). Rational mass realizations also exist by their positive rational construction; the verified result supplies no polynomial bit-time bound for the support representation ([full preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.08332)).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required n to be positive, excluding the empty ground set, which has no permitted voter types on which to place total mass one.
  • Required the tree character to contain both the empty and full sets and to be pairwise nested or disjoint, following the intended definition clarified in the published abstract.
  • Encoded the input character as an explicit list of incidence vectors and distinguished its size k from the output society's support size tau.
  • Defined character through weak utility comparisons, retaining ties and quantifying over every subset of the complement, correcting the supplied definition's element-versus-subset slip.
  • Interpreted the computational target as constructing an explicit binary rational support representation; polynomial time is measured against input length, and hardness concerns search rather than the already guaranteed existence of a realization.

1. Polynomial coefficient length for any fixed support

Proposition. Suppose a character is realized by positive masses on distinct nonempty types $A_1,\ldots,A_\tau$. Then the same entire weak preference order can be realized on that support by masses
$$ \mu_i=\frac{w_i}{\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}w_j}, $$
where the $w_i$ are positive integers satisfying
$$ w_i\leq \tau^{\tau/2}. $$
Consequently, the binary support representation has length
$$ O\!\left(\tau m+\tau^2\log(\tau+1)\right). $$

Proof. Let $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_\tau)$ be the original positive mass vector. For every ordered pair of outcomes $x,y$, define
$$ d(x,y)_i=u_{A_i}(x)-u_{A_i}(y)\in\{-1,0,1\}. $$
Thus their utility difference is $d(x,y)\cdot a$.

Consider the finite system in variables $z_1,\ldots,z_\tau$ consisting of:

  • $z_i\geq1$ for every $i$;
  • $d(x,y)\cdot z=0$ whenever $d(x,y)\cdot a=0$;
  • $d(x,y)\cdot z\geq1$ whenever $d(x,y)\cdot a>0$.

Negative comparisons are covered by reversing the ordered pair.

This system is feasible. Indeed, among the finitely many positive numbers consisting of the $a_i$ and the positive comparison differences, take their minimum $\eta>0$. Then $a/\eta$ satisfies every constraint.

Let $P$ be its feasible polyhedron. Minimizing $\sum_i z_i$ over $P$ attains an optimum: intersecting $P$ with the sublevel set determined by any feasible point gives a nonempty compact set, since every coordinate is at least $1$.

The optimal face has an extreme point $v$ that is also extreme in $P$. For completeness, successively minimize coordinates $z_1,\ldots,z_\tau$ over the compact optimal face. The resulting point is extreme: any convex decomposition must preserve the optimal objective, and then each successively minimized coordinate, forcing both endpoints to equal that point.

At $v$, the active constraint rows span $\mathbb R^\tau$. Otherwise, a nonzero vector orthogonal to all active rows would permit sufficiently small feasible perturbations in both directions, contradicting extremality. Choose $\tau$ independent active rows. They give
$$ Bv=b, $$
where every entry of $B$ belongs to $\{-1,0,1\}$ and every entry of $b$ belongs to $\{0,1\}$.

Cramer’s rule gives
$$ v_i=\frac{\det B_i}{\det B}, $$
where $B_i$ replaces column $i$ by $b$. Hadamard’s inequality applies because every row of either matrix has Euclidean norm at most $\sqrt{\tau}$:
$$ |\det B|,\ |\det B_i|\leq\tau^{\tau/2}. $$
Define
$$ w_i=\operatorname{sgn}(\det B)\det B_i. $$
Since $v_i\geq1$, these are positive integers. Moreover, $w=|\det B|v$, so every zero comparison remains zero and every positive comparison remains positive.

Their sum is at most $\tau^{\tau/2+1}$. Each numerator and denominator therefore requires $O(\tau\log(\tau+1))$ bits. Positive normalization preserves all comparisons, including ties, proving the proposition. $\square$

This is an existence bound, not a polynomial-time construction: the system above uses the realized preference order, which is not supplied by the tree-character input. Nevertheless, it removes coefficient length as an independent existential obstacle once polynomial support size is established.

2. A polynomial construction with branching and nesting

Let $D_1,D_2,D_3,D_4$ be a partition of $[m]$ into four nonempty blocks. Consider
$$ \chi= \{\emptyset,[m],D_1,D_2,D_3,D_4,D_1\cup D_2\}. $$
This tree has a branching root and a nested branching member.

Define positive integer weights through
$$ F(x)=20\sum_{i=1}^{4}u_{D_i}(x) +u_{[m]}(x) +\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq4}c_{ij}u_{D_i\cup D_j}(x), $$
where
$$ c_{12}=c_{13}=c_{23}=1,\qquad c_{14}=c_{24}=2,\qquad c_{34}=4. $$
The total weight is
$$ 4\cdot20+1+(1+1+1+2+2+4)=92. $$
Output each displayed weight divided by $92$. The eleven supported types are distinct and nonempty.

2.1. Every listed set is separable

The empty set and the root are automatically separable.

Fix a block $D_i$. Every supported type contains all of $D_i$ or avoids it. Hence two outcomes on $D_i$ with equal parity remain tied under every exterior outcome.

For opposite parities, the term $20u_{D_i}$ contributes a difference of magnitude $20$. The root and incident pair terms have combined absolute contribution at most
$$ 1+\max_i\sum_{j\neq i}c_{ij}=9. $$
Thus the comparison retains its strict sign for every exterior outcome. Every $D_i$ is separable.

Now let $S=D_1\cup D_2$. For an outcome $X\subseteq S$, put
$$ r(X)=u_{D_1}(X)+u_{D_2}(X)\in\{0,1,2\}. $$
If $r(X)\neq r(Y)$, the two block terms contribute a difference of magnitude at least $20$. All remaining terms that could differ have combined weight at most
$$ 1+\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}=12. $$
Therefore the sign is independent of the exterior.

If $r(X)=r(Y)\in\{0,2\}$, both block parities agree individually, so every contribution cancels.

If $r(X)=r(Y)=1$, the block parities either agree individually or are exchanged. In the exchanged case, the pair $D_1\cup D_2$ and the root have equal parity in both outcomes. Contributions involving $D_3$ cancel because $c_{13}=c_{23}$; those involving $D_4$ cancel because $c_{14}=c_{24}$. The outcomes remain tied under every exterior outcome.

This proves separability of $S$, with ties preserved exactly.

2.2. A separable set cannot split a block

Suppose $T$ contains $a\in D_i$ and excludes $b\in D_i$. Every supported type contains both $a,b$, or neither.

Let $W_a$ be the sum of weights of supported types containing $a$. It is positive. Directly,
$$ F(\emptyset)-F(\{a\})=W_a>0, $$
whereas
$$ F(\{b\})-F(\{a,b\})=-W_a<0. $$
Taking $X=\emptyset$, $Y=\{a\}$, and exterior outcome $Z=\{b\}$ proves that $T$ is nonseparable.

It remains to examine unions of whole blocks.

2.3. Every unlisted union of blocks is nonseparable

Write
$$ p_l(x)=(-1)^{|x\cap D_l|}. $$
Choose two included blocks $i,j$ and outcomes $X,Y$ whose parities on those blocks are
$$ (p_i(X),p_j(X))=(-1,1),\qquad (p_i(Y),p_j(Y))=(1,-1), $$
with identical outcomes on every other included block.

The block terms cancel, as does the root term. Using
$$ u_{D_a\cup D_b}(x)=\frac{1+p_a(x)p_b(x)}2, $$
the exact difference is
$$ F(X\cup Z)-F(Y\cup Z) =\sum_{l\neq i,j}(c_{jl}-c_{il})p_l. $$

The following table covers every unlisted proper union. Initially all exterior blocks have parity $+1$; subsequently every exterior block has parity $-1$. The indicated shared interior parities are feasible because the blocks are nonempty.

| Included block indices | Exchanged indices $(i,j)$ | Shared interior choice | Exact difference | Initially | Subsequently |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| $\{1,3\}$ | $(1,3)$ | — | $2p_4$ | $2$ | $-2$ |
| $\{2,3\}$ | $(2,3)$ | — | $2p_4$ | $2$ | $-2$ |
| $\{1,4\}$ | $(1,4)$ | — | $p_2+3p_3$ | $4$ | $-4$ |
| $\{2,4\}$ | $(2,4)$ | — | $p_1+3p_3$ | $4$ | $-4$ |

| $\{1,2,4\}$ | $(1,4)$ | $p_2=1$ | $p_2+3p_3$ | $4$ | $-2$ |

| $\{2,3,4\}$ | $(3,4)$ | $p_2=-1$ | $p_1+p_2$ | $0$ | $-2$ |

Each row violates the required weak-comparison equivalence. The last two rows specifically use a tie becoming a strict inequality.

Every subset either splits a block or is a union of whole blocks. The preceding arguments therefore prove that the character is exactly $\chi$.

2.4. Bit complexity

Here $k=7$ and $\tau=11$. All mass numerators and denominators have constant bit length, so output length is $O(\tau m)$.

Identifying the containment structure and blocks by incidence-vector comparisons, then writing the support, takes
$$ O(L+mk^2+m\tau) $$
bit operations. Thus this family admits polynomial-time construction.

3. An obstruction to the displayed depth-and-sibling formula

A tempting route to a general algorithm is to compress the positive coefficients in the preprint’s explicit depth-and-sibling construction. Its displayed formula assigns $\alpha^{g(A)}$ to tree members and small dyadic coefficients to sibling unions, with $\alpha=2-2^{-(m-1)}$. [Beveridge–Calaway, preprint, Section 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.08332).

The following calculation shows why that formula cannot be used verbatim without resolving a correctness discrepancy.

Take
$$ \chi_0= \{\emptyset,\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2\},\{3\},[4]\}. $$
Its sibling unions, including the root’s residual block $\{4\}$, are
$$ \mathcal B= \{\{1,2\},\{1,2,3\},\{1,2,4\},\{3,4\}\}. $$
Put $a=15/8$ and consider
$$ F_0= u_{[4]}+a(u_{\{1,2\}}+u_{\{3\}}) +a^2(u_{\{1\}}+u_{\{2\}}). $$
For any positive coefficients satisfying
$$ \varepsilon=\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}\delta_B<\frac{15}{64}, $$
define
$$ F=F_0+\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}\delta_Bu_B. $$

Claim. The unlisted set $S=\{1,2,3\}$ is separable for $F$.

Proof. The part of $F_0$ supported inside $S$ is
$$ g=a(u_{\{1,2\}}+u_{\{3\}}) +a^2(u_{\{1\}}+u_{\{2\}}). $$
Its possible values and total parities are:

| Parities on $\{1\},\{2\}$ | Parity on $\{3\}$ | $64g$ | Total parity |
|---|---|---:|---|
| both even | even | $690$ | even |
| both even | odd | $570$ | odd |
| one odd | even | $345$ | odd |
| one odd | odd | $225$ | even |
| both odd | even | $240$ | even |
| both odd | odd | $120$ | odd |

For opposite total parities, the smallest absolute difference between values of $g$ is $105/64$. The root term contributes at most $1$ in absolute value under either exterior outcome. The perturbation contributes at most $\varepsilon$. Therefore every such comparison retains the sign of its $g$-difference, since
$$ \frac{105}{64}-1-\varepsilon>0. $$

For equal total parity, the root contributions cancel. Every nonzero $g$-difference has magnitude at least $15/64$, so the perturbation cannot change its sign.

Finally, equal $g$-values arise only from identical parity assignments or exchanging the parities of coordinates $1,2$. Every supported perturbation type contains both those coordinates or neither. The two individual terms have equal coefficients. Consequently these comparisons remain tied for both exterior outcomes.

Every comparison on $S$ therefore has an exterior-independent sign, including zero. This proves separability. $\square$

The displayed preprint’s sibling coefficients satisfy the required smallness bound in this example. Thus compression, which preserves preferences, would also preserve this extra separability. This calculation concerns that explicit formula; it does not refute the admissibility premise supplied in the task.

4. What remains open

The new coefficient bound applies to arbitrary realizations and preserves ties exactly. The explicit construction above also goes beyond chains and trees with disjoint proper members.

Neither result supplies a polynomial-time algorithm for arbitrary tree characters. In particular, polynomial coefficient existence on a prescribed support does not establish that an appropriate support and its coefficients can be found efficiently.
