The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite item catalogue \(J\), a periodic turn word \(w\) over at most eleven agent types, an \(\mathit{OXS}\) valuation template \(u_t\) for each type, rational per-capita supplies \(r_j\), and accuracy \(\varepsilon>0\), consider every admissible scale \(N\): \(N\) agents follow \(w\), each category \(j\) has \(Nr_j\) distinct indivisible copies, and agents play the resulting finite round-robin game. For each scale, form the type-conditioned histograms of integral bundles over all SPNEs. Compute a rational histogram within \(\varepsilon\) in \(L_1\) distance of some subsequential limit of these histograms as \(N\to\infty\).

1. Definitions and deterministic constraints

Write
$$ R=\sum_{j\in J}r_j,\qquad d=\lfloor R\rfloor,\qquad \theta=R-d. $$
Thus $0\leq\theta<1$.

For a category-count bundle $b$, write
$$ |b|=\sum_{j\in J}b_j. $$

For each slot of type $t$, define
$$ v_{t,s}=\max_{j\in J}a_{t,s,j}. $$
Let
$$ S_t^+=\{s:v_{t,s}>0\},\qquad \ell_t=|S_t^+|,\qquad M_t=\sum_{s\in S_t^+}v_{t,s}. $$

Here $M_t$ is the maximum utility obtainable with unrestricted category multiplicities. Indeed, every matching contributes at most $v_{t,s}$ at slot $s$, so
$$ u_t(b)\leq M_t $$
for every bundle. Conversely, choosing one maximizing-category copy for each positive slot gives a matching of weight $M_t$.

Call a bundle saturated for type $t$ if $u_t(b)=M_t$. Equality requires every positive slot to be matched to a category attaining its slot maximum: the difference between $M_t$ and the matching weight is a sum of nonnegative slot deficits. Consequently, every saturated bundle has at least $\ell_t$ copies.

At an admissible scale $N$, there are exactly
$$ NR=dN+\theta N $$
picks. Every agent therefore receives $d$ copies, and precisely the first $\theta N$ agents receive one additional copy.

Let $f_t$ be the number of occurrences of type $t$ in $w$. The input assumption gives $p_t=f_t/q$. In any prefix of the periodic agent order, the type-$t$ count differs from its expected count by at most $q$. Thus, if $E_t(N)$ counts type-$t$ agents receiving the additional copy,
$$ |E_t(N)-\theta Np_t|\leq q. $$

It follows that every $h\in\mathcal H$ satisfies
$$ \sum_b h_t(b)=1, $$
$$ \sum_{|b|=d}h_t(b)=1-\theta,\qquad \sum_{|b|=d+1}h_t(b)=\theta, $$
and
$$ \sum_t p_t\sum_b b_jh_t(b)=r_j \qquad(j\in J). $$

These identities pass to limits because every finite histogram is supported on the same finite set
$$ B_R=\{b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^m:|b|\in\{d,d+1\}\}. $$

Also, $\mathcal H$ is nonempty. Admissible scales are unbounded; backward induction gives a pure SPNE at each scale; and the resulting histograms have a convergent subsequence in the finite-dimensional compact space
$$ \prod_{t=1}^{\tau}\Delta(B_R). $$

2. A saturated allocation path extends to an SPNE

Lemma 1. At any finite admissible scale, suppose a legal allocation path gives every type-$t$ agent utility $M_t$. Then some pure SPNE follows that path.

Proof. At every subtree entered by first departing from the prescribed path, choose a pure SPNE by backward induction.

Now work backward along the prescribed path. At a node where a type-$t$ agent acts, following the prescribed action and continuation gives that agent final utility $M_t$. Every alternative action, followed by its selected off-path equilibrium, gives utility at most $M_t$. Hence the prescribed action maximizes the acting agent’s payoff.

Inductively, all child continuations are SPNEs, and the selected action maximizes the acting agent’s continuation payoff. This is precisely the backward-induction condition for an SPNE. Therefore the completed profile is a pure SPNE. $\square$

The lemma concerns existence. Constructing all of its off-path continuations is unnecessary for the histogram search problem.

3. Exact characterization of saturated limit histograms

Let $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sat}}$ be the set of histograms satisfying all of the following:

  • Each $h_t$ is a probability distribution on $B_R$.
  • Its mass on bundles of size $d+1$ is $\theta$.
  • Category supplies satisfy

$$ \sum_t p_t\sum_b b_jh_t(b)=r_j. $$

  • Every positively weighted bundle is saturated:

$$ h_t(b)>0\ \Longrightarrow\ u_t(b)=M_t. $$

Theorem 2.
$$ \mathcal P_{\mathrm{sat}} = \{h\in\mathcal H:\text{every positively weighted bundle is saturated}\}. $$

Thus, on this part of the histogram space, the deterministic constraints are also sufficient for equilibrium attainability.

3.1. Rational histograms are exactly realizable

First suppose $h\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sat}}$ is rational.

Choose $N$ divisible by:

  • $q$;
  • $q$ times a denominator of $\theta$;
  • every supply denominator;
  • every denominator of $p_t h_t(b)$.

Then $N$ is admissible, $\theta N$ is a multiple of $q$, and
$$ n_{t,b}=Np_t h_t(b) $$
is an integer for every supported pair.

Because the prefix of length $\theta N$ contains complete periods of $w$, it contains exactly $\theta Np_t$ type-$t$ agents. Assign the bundles of size $d+1$ to those agents according to the counts $n_{t,b}$. Assign the bundles of size $d$ to the remaining type-$t$ agents. The required counts agree because
$$ \sum_{|b|=d+1}n_{t,b}=\theta Np_t. $$

Supply conservation gives
$$ \sum_t\sum_b n_{t,b}b_j=Nr_j. $$
Therefore the distinct category-$j$ copies can be partitioned among the assigned bundles.

Prescribe that every acting agent picks one of its assigned copies. Along this path, every such copy remains available until its owner picks it. The number of prescribed copies equals the agent’s number of scheduled turns, so this is a legal path allocating all copies.

Every final bundle is saturated. Lemma 1 therefore extends the path to a pure SPNE whose histogram is exactly $h$.

The same argument works at every positive multiple of the chosen $N$. Hence
$$ h\in\mathcal H. $$

3.2. Real histograms follow by rational approximation

Now let $h\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sat}}$ have real probabilities.

Restrict coordinates to pairs $(t,b)$ having $h_t(b)>0$. On these coordinates, the probability, bundle-size, and supply constraints form a consistent rational linear system
$$ Az=c. $$
Gaussian elimination gives a rational particular solution and a rational basis for the homogeneous solution space. Approximating the coefficients expressing $h$ in this basis by rational numbers gives rational solutions converging to $h$.

There are only finitely many retained coordinates, and each is strictly positive. Therefore sufficiently close approximations remain positive. Keeping all other coordinates zero gives rational histograms
$$ h^{(n)}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sat}},\qquad \|h^{(n)}-h\|_1\longrightarrow0. $$

By the preceding construction, each $h^{(n)}$ is exactly realizable by a finite SPNE at arbitrarily large admissible scales. Choose such a scale $N_n\geq n$. These SPNE histograms converge to $h$, proving $h\in\mathcal H$.

The reverse inclusion follows from the deterministic constraints in Section 1 and the assumed saturation. $\square$

4. A polynomial flow criterion

For every positive slot, let
$$ A_{t,s}=\{j:a_{t,s,j}=v_{t,s}\} $$
be its nonempty set of maximizing categories.

Construct a capacitated network with:

  • a source;
  • one node for each positive slot $(t,s)$;
  • one node for each category $j$;
  • a sink.

The arcs and capacities are:

| Arc | Capacity |
|---|---:|
| Source to positive slot $(t,s)$ | $p_t$ |
| Slot $(t,s)$ to $j\in A_{t,s}$ | $p_t$ |
| Category $j$ to sink | $r_j$ |

Define the total slot demand
$$ A=\sum_t p_t\ell_t. $$

Theorem 3. A saturated histogram exists in $\mathcal H$ if and only if
$$ d\geq\max_t\ell_t $$
and this network admits a flow of value $A$.

When both conditions hold, an exact rational member of $\mathcal H$ can be computed in polynomial time.

4.1. Necessity

Suppose a saturated $h\in\mathcal H$ exists.

Each type has probability $1-\theta>0$ of receiving exactly $d$ copies. Since a saturated bundle needs at least $\ell_t$ copies,
$$ d\geq\ell_t $$
for every supported type.

For each supported saturated bundle $b$, choose a maximum-weight matching. Every positive slot is matched to a maximizing category. Define
$$ x_{t,s,j} = p_t\sum_b h_t(b) \mathbf 1\{\text{the chosen matching assigns category }j\text{ to slot }s\}. $$
Then
$$ x_{t,s,j}=0\quad\text{if }j\notin A_{t,s}, $$
$$ \sum_j x_{t,s,j}=p_t, $$
and
$$ \sum_t\sum_{s\in S_t^+}x_{t,s,j} \leq \sum_t p_t\sum_b b_jh_t(b) = r_j. $$
The inequality holds because the matching uses no more category-$j$ copies than the bundle contains.

These quantities define a feasible flow saturating every source-to-slot arc. Its value is $A$.

4.2. From a feasible flow to saturated bundle distributions

Conversely, assume the two conditions hold. Choose a rational flow of value $A$, and denote its slot-to-category values by $x_{t,s,j}$.

For a positive slot, set
$$ P_{t,s,j}=\frac{x_{t,s,j}}{p_t}. $$
This is a probability distribution supported on $A_{t,s}$.

For each type, we now construct a distribution over saturated bundles of exactly $\ell_t$ copies with these slot marginals.

Fix the same category order for all slots. Define cumulative probabilities
$$ F_{t,s,j}=\sum_{a=1}^{j}P_{t,s,a}. $$
Partition $[0,1]$ at all distinct cumulative probabilities for the positive slots of type $t$, including $0$ and $1$.

On each open interval of this partition, select for each slot the category containing that interval in its cumulative distribution. Let $g$ be the resulting category-count bundle, and assign it probability equal to the interval’s length.

Every selected category maximizes its slot’s weight. Hence
$$ |g|=\ell_t,\qquad u_t(g)=M_t. $$
Moreover, the resulting distribution, denoted $\mu_t$, satisfies
$$ \mathbb E_{\mu_t}[g_j] = \sum_{s\in S_t^+}P_{t,s,j}. $$

There are at most
$$ 1+\ell_t(m-1) $$
intervals, because each slot contributes at most $m-1$ interior partition points. Thus $\mu_t$ has at most that many supported bundles. For $\ell_t=0$, take $\mu_t=\delta_0$.

4.3. Assigning the remaining copies

Define the category supplies left after these saturation bundles by
$$ z_j=r_j-\sum_t\sum_{s\in S_t^+}x_{t,s,j}. $$
Flow feasibility gives $z_j\geq0$. Their total is
$$ C=\sum_j z_j = R-\sum_t p_t\ell_t. $$

Case $C=0$

Since $d\geq\ell_t$,
$$ 0=C=\theta+\sum_t p_t(d-\ell_t). $$
Every term is nonnegative. Therefore
$$ \theta=0,\qquad \ell_t=d\quad\text{for every }t. $$
Also, every $z_j=0$.

Taking
$$ h_t=\mu_t $$
satisfies all probability, size, supply, and saturation constraints. Theorem 2 gives $h\in\mathcal H$.

Case $C>0$

Set
$$ \beta_j=\frac{z_j}{C},\qquad \gamma_0=1-\theta,\qquad \gamma_1=\theta. $$

For each type $t$, independently select:

  • a bundle $g$ according to $\mu_t$;
  • $\eta\in\{0,1\}$ with probabilities $\gamma_\eta$;
  • a category $j$ with probabilities $\beta_j$.

Output the bundle
$$ b=g+(d+\eta-\ell_t)e_j. $$
Its probability before merging repetitions is
$$ \mu_t(g)\gamma_\eta\beta_j. $$

The filler count is nonnegative because $d\geq\ell_t$. Each bundle contains $g$, so its utility remains $M_t$. Its size is
$$ |b|=d+\eta. $$
Thus its bundle-size probabilities are exactly the required ones.

For category $j$, the aggregate expected consumption is
$$ \sum_t p_t\mathbb E[b_j] = \sum_t\sum_{s\in S_t^+}x_{t,s,j} + \beta_j\sum_t p_t(d+\theta-\ell_t). $$
The last sum equals $C$, giving
$$ \sum_t p_t\mathbb E[b_j] = \sum_t\sum_{s\in S_t^+}x_{t,s,j}+\beta_jC = r_j. $$

Hence the constructed rational histogram belongs to $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{sat}}$, and Theorem 2 establishes
$$ h\in\mathcal H. $$
This proves sufficiency and completes Theorem 3. $\square$
