Lemma EC.2 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many roles \(t\in\mathcal T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), symmetric nonnegative interaction weights \(K_{tu}\), group signs \(r_t\in\{-1,+1\}\), rational opinions \(s_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t s_t=0\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t s_t^2=1\), and a rational seed-mass budget \(\beta\), consider an atomless population partitioned into role blocks of masses \(\mu_t\), with a role-\(t\) user interacting with each role-\(u\) user at weight \(K_{tu}/N\) in an \(N\)-user approximation. Choose a measurable seed set \(S\) of mass at most \(\beta\) and a real opinion shift \(\delta\) supported on \(S\) to maximize the reduction in disparity \(\|(I+L)^{-1}(r(s+\delta))\|_{L^2}^2\); given a rational threshold \(q\), decide whether the maximum reduction is at least \(q\). Equivalently, determine the complexity of minimizing the proponent’s displayed role-mass objective over \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_t x_t\le\beta\), and aggregate shifts \(v_t\), with \(v_t=0\) when \(x_t=0\).

A society is specified by its support: \(\tau\) roles with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts represented roles, not possible types. Represent it by Lebesgue measure on \([0,1]\), partitioned into intervals \(B_t\) of lengths \(\mu_t\).
Input also includes symmetric rational weights \(K_{tu}\ge0\), signs \(r_t\in\{-1,+1\}\) with both signs present, rational opinions \(s_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_ts_t=0\) and \(\sum_t\mu_ts_t^2=1\), a rational budget \(\beta\in[0,1]\), and a rational threshold \(q\). Extend \(r\) and \(s\) constantly over each role block.
For \(a\in B_t\), define the continuum Laplacian by \((\mathcal L f)(a)=\sum_uK_{tu}\int_{B_u}(f(a)-f(b))\,db\), including within-role interactions. Define conditional disparity by \(D(h)=\|(I+\mathcal L)^{-1}(rh)\|_{L^2([0,1])}^2\), where multiplication by \(r\) is pointwise.
An intervention chooses any measurable seed set \(S\subseteq[0,1]\) of measure at most \(\beta\) and any real \(\delta\in L^2([0,1])\) vanishing almost everywhere outside \(S\). Shifts have no magnitude bound or penalty; the shifted opinions need not retain the initial normalization or zero mean.
The exact decision problem asks whether some intervention satisfies \(D(s)-D(s+\delta)\ge q\). Measure running times in \(\tau\), the number \(m\) of positive unordered interaction entries, and the total input bit length \(L\).

Is this exact threshold decision problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Finite-agent unconstrained seeding with a fixed correlation structure is NP-hard, while optimization over shifts for a fixed seed set is polynomial-time ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07480v2#Sx4)). No result settling this exact atomless mass-budget problem was identified in the literature search.

Result

The exact threshold decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions.

This holds even when all role masses are equal dyadic rationals, the budget is \(\beta=\tfrac12\), the opinions satisfy \(s_t=r_t\), all within-role weights vanish, and all positive interaction weights are equal.

The proof gives a uniform bound over arbitrary fractional seed allocations and arbitrary measurable interventions.

1. Reduction from MAX-CUT

Use unweighted MAX-CUT: given a finite simple undirected graph \(G\) and an integer \(c\), decide whether \(G\) has a cut containing at least \(c\) edges. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer, *Some simplified NP-complete graph problems*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(76)90059-1).

Let \(G\) have \(n\ge1\) vertices. Choose the smallest power of two \(N\) satisfying \(N\ge2n\), and add \(N-n\) isolated vertices. Denote the resulting graph by \(\widehat G\), its adjacency matrix by \(A\), its degrees by \(d_i\), and its maximum degree by \(\Delta\). Put

\(\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(d_i)-A,\qquad \varepsilon=\dfrac{1}{1024N^3}.\)

Construct a society with \(N\) roles and parameters

\(\mu_i=\dfrac1N,\qquad K_{ij}=N\varepsilon A_{ij},\qquad \beta=\dfrac12.\)

Assign \(r_i=+1\) to exactly half the roles and \(r_i=-1\) to the other half, and set \(s_i=r_i\). Thus

\(\sum_i\mu_i s_i=0,\qquad \sum_i\mu_i s_i^2=1.\)

Both signs are present. Moreover, \(rs=1\) everywhere. Since \(\mathcal L1=0\), the initial disparity is

\(D(s)=1.\)

Set the decision threshold to

\(q=\dfrac12+\dfrac{\varepsilon}{N}(2c-1).\)

We will prove that the society admits an intervention with gain at least \(q\) exactly when \(G\) has a cut of size at least \(c\).

2. Exact optimization for a prescribed seed allocation

Fix any measurable seed set \(S\). Write

\(p_i=N\,|S\cap B_i|,\qquad 0\le p_i\le1.\)

The budget condition is \(\sum_i p_i\le N/2\).

For a feasible shift, let \(e=r\delta\). Multiplication by \(r\) preserves support and square integrability, and

\(r(s+\delta)=1+e.\)

Define the role averages

\(y_i=N\int_{B_i}e(a)\,da,\)

and write \(e=y_i+w\) on \(B_i\), so that \(\int_{B_i}w=0\).

The subspace of functions constant on each role and its orthogonal complement are invariant under \(\mathcal L\). On role-constant functions, \(\mathcal L\) acts as \(\varepsilon\Lambda\). On functions whose integral vanishes in every role, it acts on \(B_i\) as multiplication by \(\varepsilon d_i\). Consequently, with

\(C=(I+\varepsilon\Lambda)^{-2},\qquad \gamma_i=(1+\varepsilon d_i)^{-2},\)

orthogonality gives the exact identity

\(D(s+\delta)=\dfrac1N(1+y)^\mathsf TC(1+y)+\sum_i\gamma_i\int_{B_i}w(a)^2\,da.\)

Here and below, \(1\) also denotes the vector of all ones.

For \(p_i>0\), Cauchy–Schwarz on \(S\cap B_i\) yields

\(\int_{B_i}e(a)^2\,da\ge\dfrac{\left(\int_{B_i}e(a)\,da\right)^2}{|S\cap B_i|}=\dfrac{y_i^2}{Np_i}.\)

Hence

\(\int_{B_i}w(a)^2\,da=\int_{B_i}e(a)^2\,da-\dfrac{y_i^2}{N}\ge\dfrac{y_i^2}{N}\left(\dfrac1{p_i}-1\right).\)

Equality is attained by taking \(e=y_i/p_i\) on \(S\cap B_i\) and \(e=0\) elsewhere in \(B_i\). If \(p_i=0\), feasibility forces \(y_i=0\).

Therefore the optimal value depends only on \(p\), and is attained by a shift constant on the seeded portion of each role.

To express this value without divisions by zero, put

\(P=\operatorname{diag}(p_i),\qquad u=P^{1/2}1,\)

and define

\(M(p)=P^{1/2}CP^{1/2}+\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\gamma_i(1-p_i)\bigr).\)

This matrix is positive definite: for any nonzero vector \(z\),

\(z^\mathsf TM(p)z=(P^{1/2}z)^\mathsf TC(P^{1/2}z)+\sum_i\gamma_i(1-p_i)z_i^2>0.\)

Indeed, the first term is positive if \(P^{1/2}z\ne0\); otherwise, every nonzero coordinate of \(z\) has \(p_i=0\), making the second term positive.

Substitute \(y=P^{1/2}z\). Since \(C1=1\), the minimum disparity becomes the minimum of

\(1+\dfrac1N\bigl(2u^\mathsf Tz+z^\mathsf TM(p)z\bigr).\)

For a coordinate with \(p_i=0\), the corresponding row of \(M(p)\) has only the diagonal entry \(\gamma_i\), and \(u_i=0\). Thus the unconstrained minimizer automatically has \(z_i=0\), as required.

Completing the square proves that the maximum attainable gain for allocation \(p\) is exactly

\(\Gamma(p)=\dfrac1N u^\mathsf TM(p)^{-1}u.\)

Every \(p\in[0,1]^N\) is realizable by measurable subsets of the role intervals. The optimizing shifts above belong to \(L^2\): there are finitely many roles, and each positive \(p_i\) gives a finite amplitude. Thus this formula concerns attained interventions, not merely infima.

3. A uniform perturbation bound

Define the fractional cut expression

\(F(p)=\sum_i d_i p_i-p^\mathsf TAp=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(\widehat G)}(p_i+p_j-2p_ip_j).\)

We prove, uniformly for \(p\in[0,1]^N\), that

\(\left|\Gamma(p)-\dfrac{\sum_i p_i}{N}-\dfrac{2\varepsilon}{N}F(p)\right|\le65\varepsilon^2\Delta^2.\)

All matrix norms in this section are Euclidean operator norms.

For \(x\ge0\), the function \(f(x)=(1+x)^{-2}\) satisfies \(f'(0)=-2\) and \(0<f''(x)\le6\). Taylor’s theorem therefore gives

\(\left|(1+x)^{-2}-(1-2x)\right|\le3x^2.\)

The graph Laplacian is positive semidefinite, with \(\|\Lambda\|_2\le2\Delta\). To verify the latter bound, for every real vector \(v\),

\(v^\mathsf T\Lambda v=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(\widehat G)}(v_i-v_j)^2\le2\sum_i d_i v_i^2\le2\Delta\|v\|_2^2.\)

Applying the scalar Taylor bound to its eigenvalues gives

\(C=I-2\varepsilon\Lambda+R_C,\qquad \|R_C\|_2\le12\varepsilon^2\Delta^2.\)

Likewise,

\(\gamma_i=1-2\varepsilon d_i+\rho_i,\qquad |\rho_i|\le3\varepsilon^2\Delta^2.\)

Substitution into \(M(p)\) yields

\(M(p)=I-2\varepsilon T(p)+R(p),\)

where

\(T(p)=\operatorname{diag}(d_i)-P^{1/2}AP^{1/2},\qquad \|R(p)\|_2\le15\varepsilon^2\Delta^2.\)

Since \(\|A\|_2\le\Delta\) and \(\|P^{1/2}\|_2\le1\), we have \(\|T(p)\|_2\le2\Delta\). The adjacency bound follows, for example, from

\(\left|v^\mathsf TAv\right|=\left|2\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(\widehat G)}v_iv_j\right|\le\sum_i d_i v_i^2\le\Delta\|v\|_2^2.\)

Put \(\eta=\varepsilon\Delta\) and \(U=M(p)-I\). Our choice of \(\varepsilon\) ensures \(\eta\le1/16\), so

\(\|U\|_2\le4\eta+15\eta^2\le5\eta<\dfrac12.\)

Using the exact identity

\((I+U)^{-1}=I-U+U^2(I+U)^{-1},\)

we obtain

\(\left\|M(p)^{-1}-I-2\varepsilon T(p)\right\|_2\le15\eta^2+\dfrac{25\eta^2}{1-\|U\|_2}\le65\eta^2.\)

Finally,

\(\|u\|_2^2=\sum_i p_i,\qquad u^\mathsf TT(p)u=\sum_i d_i p_i-p^\mathsf TAp=F(p).\)

The claimed gain bound follows by inserting this inverse expansion into the exact formula for \(\Gamma(p)\), and using \(\sum_i p_i\le N\).

Because \(\Delta\le N\), its error bound satisfies

\(65\varepsilon^2\Delta^2\le65\varepsilon^2N^2=\dfrac{65}{1024}\dfrac{\varepsilon}{N}<\dfrac{\varepsilon}{4N}.\)

This bound includes allocations with zero coordinates and arbitrarily small positive coordinates.

4. Bounding every fractional allocation by MAX-CUT

Let \(C_{\max}\) be the maximum cut size of \(\widehat G\), which equals that of \(G\).

For any \(p\in[0,1]^N\), select each vertex independently with probability \(p_i\). An edge \(\{i,j\}\) crosses the resulting random cut with probability

\(p_i(1-p_j)+(1-p_i)p_j=p_i+p_j-2p_ip_j.\)

By linearity of expectation, its expected cut size is \(F(p)\). Every realization is a cut of size at most \(C_{\max}\); hence

\(F(p)\le C_{\max}\)

for every fractional allocation. This argument bounds fractional seeding without assuming that an optimal seed set consists of whole roles.

Conversely, every cut of \(G\) extends to a cut of \(\widehat G\) whose selected side contains exactly \(N/2\) vertices. Indeed, if its selected side contains \(j\) original vertices, add \(N/2-j\) isolated vertices. This is possible because

\(0\le j\le n\le N/2,\qquad N-n\ge N/2.\)

The added vertices do not change the cut size.

5. Correctness of the reduction

Suppose first that \(G\) has a cut of size at least \(c\). Extend it as above and let \(p\) be the indicator of its selected side. Then

\(\sum_i p_i=N/2,\qquad F(p)\ge c.\)

The optimal intervention for this allocation exists by Section 2, and Section 3 gives

\(\Gamma(p)\ge\dfrac12+\dfrac{2\varepsilon c}{N}-65\varepsilon^2\Delta^2>q.\)

The last inequality holds because \(q=\tfrac12+2\varepsilon c/N-\varepsilon/N\), while the error is smaller than \(\varepsilon/(4N)\). Thus the constructed instance is a yes-instance.

Now suppose \(G\) has no cut of size at least \(c\). Cut sizes are integers, so \(C_{\max}\le c-1\). For every feasible seed allocation,

\(\sum_i p_i\le N/2,\qquad F(p)\le c-1.\)

Consequently,

\(\Gamma(p)\le\dfrac12+\dfrac{2\varepsilon(c-1)}{N}+65\varepsilon^2\Delta^2<q.\)

Every shift supported on such a seed set has gain at most \(\Gamma(p)\). Therefore no measurable intervention reaches the threshold.

The reduction is valid for the exact non-strict threshold question because both directions have strict separation from \(q\).

6. Input size and conclusion

The padding satisfies \(2n\le N<4n\). All role masses are positive dyadic rationals, and every positive interaction weight equals

\(N\varepsilon=\dfrac1{1024N^2}.\)

The threshold is also dyadic. These numbers have bit lengths polynomial in the source input size. The construction has \(\tau=N\) represented roles and \(m=|E(G)|\) positive unordered interaction entries, and its total encoding length \(L\) is polynomial.

We have therefore established a polynomial-time many-one reduction from MAX-CUT. The exact atomless mass-budget decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard; a running time polynomial in \(\tau,m,L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
