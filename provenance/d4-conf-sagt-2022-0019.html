The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) tasks with positive durations \(p_i\) and a society supported on \(\tau\) preferred schedules, each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), compute a permutation \(S\) minimizing \(\Phi_\mu(S)=\sum_t \mu_t\sum_i |C_i(S)-d_{i,t}|\), where \(C_i(S)\) is task \(i\)’s completion time in \(S\), and \(d_{i,t}\) is its completion time in type \(t\)’s preferred schedule.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq 1\) tasks with positive binary-rational durations \(p_i\). Every schedule is a permutation of all tasks, executed on one machine from time \(0\), without idle time or preemption.
A society is given by its support: \(\tau\geq 1\) distinct preferred schedules \(V_t\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number \(m!\) of possible types.
Let \(C_i(S)\) be task \(i\)'s completion time in schedule \(S\), and set \(d_{i,t}=C_i(V_t)\), using the same durations in every schedule.
The problem is to compute a permutation \(S\) minimizing \(\Phi_\mu(S)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\sum_{i=1}^{m}|C_i(S)-d_{i,t}|\); any minimizing permutation is acceptable. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is exact minimization of \(\Phi_\mu\) polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a finite electorate with equally weighted voters, minimizing total absolute completion-time deviation is strongly NP-hard. With exactly two equally weighted voters, either preferred schedule is optimal.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The duration encoding was unspecified; durations are positive binary rationals, allowing the positive integer durations used by the discrete hardness result.
  • Schedules start at time zero on one machine, without idle time or preemption, as in the discrete definition.
  • The treatment of optimal ties was unspecified; returning any minimizing permutation suffices.
  • The input is nonempty: there is at least one task and one supported type. L denotes total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Exact minimization is NP-hard when the support size \(\tau\) is part of the input. This follows from the finite-electorate hardness premise in the question by aggregating identical votes. The conclusion also holds if “binary-rational” is interpreted strictly as requiring dyadic masses.

The reduction does not establish hardness for any particular fixed support size.

1. Aggregating identical votes

Use the stated hardness result as a premise. Consider an instance with \(n\) equally weighted voters whose preferred permutations are \(R_1,\ldots,R_n\). Its objective is
\[ F(S)=\sum_{r=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|C_i(S)-C_i(R_r)|. \]

Let \(V_1,\ldots,V_\tau\) be the distinct permutations appearing among these votes, and let \(c_t\) be the multiplicity of \(V_t\). Thus
\[ c_t\geq 1,\qquad \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}c_t=n,\qquad \tau\leq n. \]
Keep the durations unchanged and assign mass
\[ \mu_t=\frac{c_t}{n}. \]
These masses are positive and sum to one. For every permutation \(S\),
\[ \Phi_\mu(S) =\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\frac{c_t}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{m}|C_i(S)-C_i(V_t)| =\frac{F(S)}{n}. \]
Consequently, the two instances have exactly the same minimizing permutations. In particular, any minimizer returned for the support instance solves the original instance.

This transformation has polynomial size and running time. Identical permutations can be grouped by lexicographic sorting, using
\[ O\!\left(nm\log(n+1)\log(m+1)\right) \]
bit operations for comparisons of task labels. Each mass has an encoding of \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits. The output contains at most \(n\) permutations, so it is polynomial in the original input length.

Therefore, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would yield a polynomial-time algorithm for the given NP-hard finite-electorate problem.

2. If the masses must be dyadic

The fractions \(c_t/n\) need not be dyadic. The following modification proves hardness under that stricter interpretation too.

Let \(D\) be a common denominator of the source durations; for example, take the product of their encoded denominators. Define
\[ q_i=Dp_i,\qquad Q=\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_i. \]
All \(q_i\) and \(Q\) are positive integers. For each support permutation, put
\[ a_t(S)=D\sum_{i=1}^{m}|C_i(S)-C_i(V_t)|. \]
Every scaled completion time is an integer between \(0\) and \(Q\), hence
\[ a_t(S)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}, \qquad a_t(S)\leq mQ. \]

The original normalized, scaled objective is
\[ H_0(S)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\frac{c_t}{n}a_t(S) =\frac{DF(S)}{n}. \]
Since \(DF(S)\) is an integer, any two distinct values of \(H_0\) differ by at least \(1/n\).

Choose a power of two \(M\) satisfying
\[ M>4n\tau mQ. \]
For \(t<\tau\), define
\[ k_t=\left\lfloor\frac{Mc_t}{n}\right\rfloor, \qquad k_\tau=M-\sum_{t<\tau}k_t, \qquad \widehat\mu_t=\frac{k_t}{M}. \]
Because \(M>n\) and \(c_t\geq 1\), every \(k_t\) with \(t<\tau\) is positive. Moreover,
\[ k_\tau\geq \frac{Mc_\tau}{n}>0. \]
Thus these are positive dyadic masses summing to one.

For \(t<\tau\), the rounding error has absolute value less than \(1/M\). The last coordinate compensates for their sum. Therefore,
\[ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau} \left|\widehat\mu_t-\frac{c_t}{n}\right| \leq \frac{2\tau}{M}. \]
Writing
\[ \widehat H(S)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\widehat\mu_t a_t(S) =D\Phi_{\widehat\mu}(S), \]
we obtain, uniformly over all permutations,
\[ |\widehat H(S)-H_0(S)| \leq \frac{2\tau mQ}{M} <\frac{1}{2n}. \]

Let \(T\) minimize \(\widehat H\), and let \(S^*\) minimize \(H_0\). If \(T\) were not an original minimizer, the integer gap would give
\[ H_0(T)-H_0(S^*)\geq \frac1n. \]
But optimality of \(T\) for \(\widehat H\) gives
\[ \begin{aligned} H_0(T)-H_0(S^*) &=[H_0(T)-\widehat H(T)] +[\widehat H(T)-\widehat H(S^*)] +[\widehat H(S^*)-H_0(S^*)]\\ &<\frac1{2n}+0+\frac1{2n} =\frac1n, \end{aligned} \]
a contradiction. Hence every dyadic-instance minimizer is an original minimizer.

Finally, this is a polynomial-size construction. If the source input length is \(L_0\), then \(D\) and \(Q\) have polynomially many bits. Taking the smallest suitable power of two gives
\[ \log M=O\!\left(\log(n\tau m)+\log Q\right), \]
so all constructed masses have polynomial encoding length. This completes the reduction even under the dyadic interpretation.

3. Exact running-time bounds

For completeness, the problem admits an exact subset dynamic program.

Define the individual completion-time cost
\[ f_i(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t|x-d_{i,t}|. \]
For a subset \(A\) of tasks, let
\[ P(A)=\sum_{i\in A}p_i, \]
and let \(B(A)\) be the minimum total cost of ordering precisely the tasks in \(A\), starting at time zero. Then
\[ B(\varnothing)=0, \qquad B(A)=\min_{i\in A} \left[B(A\setminus\{i\})+f_i(P(A))\right]. \]

To justify the recurrence, if task \(i\) is last, it completes at \(P(A)\). Removing it leaves a schedule of \(A\setminus\{i\}\), with all other completion times unchanged. Conversely, appending \(i\) to an optimal ordering of that smaller subset attains the corresponding candidate value.

All arithmetic can be performed exactly by clearing the duration and mass denominators. The resulting integers have \(O(L)\) bits: products of input denominators have bit lengths bounded by their total encoded length, and summing the objective terms adds only logarithmically many bits.

Using ordinary quadratic-time integer arithmetic, this yields:

  • Evaluation of one permutation: \(O(m\tau L^2)\) bit operations.
  • Exact optimization: \(O(2^m m\tau L^2)\) bit operations.
  • Dynamic-programming storage, including preferred completion times: \(O((2^m+m\tau)L)\) bits.

Recording a minimizing last task at each subset reconstructs a minimizing permutation.

4. The two-voter case

The hardness conclusion concerns unrestricted support size and is consistent with the stated two-voter result.

Indeed, for two schedules \(V_1,V_2\) of equal mass, the triangle inequality gives
\[ \Phi_\mu(S) \geq \frac12\sum_{i=1}^{m}|C_i(V_1)-C_i(V_2)|. \]
Both \(S=V_1\) and \(S=V_2\) attain this bound.

Thus the target problem is NP-hard in general, despite this easy special case.
