Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), and rational piecewise-polynomial valuations \(V_t(b,\sigma)\) satisfying the paper’s monotonicity and SOS conditions on finite replications, can one construct in time polynomial in \(\tau=|T|\) and the input length finite piecewise-rational descriptions of allocation and payment intensities \(a_t(b,\sigma)\) and \(p_t(b,\sigma)\)? Here \(b\in\{0,1\}\) is the tagged agent’s signal and \(\sigma_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) is the mass of other type-\(t\) agents with signal \(1\); intensities may grow as a signal group’s mass approaches zero. For every state \(\sigma\), require feasibility \(\sum_{t,b}\mu_{t,b}a_t(b,\sigma)\leq 1\), ex-post IC and IR using the same other-agent state under a deviation, and welfare \(\sum_{t,b}\mu_{t,b}a_t(b,\sigma)V_t(b,\sigma)\geq\tfrac12\max_{t,b:\mu_{t,b}>0}V_t(b,\sigma)\), where \(\mu_{t,1}=\sigma_t\) and \(\mu_{t,0}=\mu_t-\sigma_t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Consider a single-item auction, so \(m=1\). A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a publicly known valuation \(V_t:\{0,1\}\times D\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}\), where \(D=\prod_u[0,\mu_u]\); the first argument is its private binary signal, and \(\sigma\in D\) records other agents’ masses with signal \(1\). Values are weakly increasing in every mass coordinate and satisfy \(V_t(1,\sigma)>V_t(0,\sigma)\).
The SOS promise means the following: for every positive integer \(N\) with all \(N\mu_u\) integral, form disjoint groups \(E_{N,u}\) of \(N\mu_u\) bidders. For each \(i\in E_{N,t}\), the set function \(v_i(S)=V_t(\mathbf1_{i\in S},(|(S\setminus\{i\})\cap E_{N,u}|/N)_{u\in T})\) is monotone and submodular on \(\bigcup_uE_{N,u}\).
Input valuations are piecewise polynomial on explicitly listed semialgebraic partitions of \(D\). Cells use Boolean formulas of rational polynomial sign conditions; polynomials use sparse monomial lists with binary coefficients and exponents. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; these conditions are promises.
Seek allocation intensities \(a_t(b,\sigma)\geq0\) and expected payment intensities \(p_t(b,\sigma)\in\mathbb R\), described by explicit finite partitions of the same kind with rational functions on cells. Denominators must be nonzero on their cells, including boundary cells. Intensities are finite at every state but may be unbounded near a vanishing signal group.
A single agent’s report leaves \(\sigma\) unchanged. Ex-post IC and IR require, for every \(t,\sigma,b,c\), \(a_t(b,\sigma)V_t(b,\sigma)-p_t(b,\sigma)\geq a_t(c,\sigma)V_t(b,\sigma)-p_t(c,\sigma)\) and \(a_t(b,\sigma)V_t(b,\sigma)-p_t(b,\sigma)\geq0\), including counterfactual reports into zero-mass groups.
Write \(\mu_{t,1}=\sigma_t\) and \(\mu_{t,0}=\mu_t-\sigma_t\). At every real state \(\sigma\in D\), require \(\sum_{t,b}\mu_{t,b}a_t(b,\sigma)\leq1\) and \(\sum_{t,b}\mu_{t,b}a_t(b,\sigma)V_t(b,\sigma)\geq\tfrac12\max_{t,b:\mu_{t,b}>0}V_t(b,\sigma)\); zero-mass groups contribute zero.

Can such an ex-post IC-IR mechanism achieving a \(2\)-approximation at every state be constructed for every promised input in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), including its explicit output description?

For finitely many bidders with binary signals and SOS values, a randomized ex-post IC-IR \(2\)-approximation exists; its construction is polynomial in the explicit valuation-table input size. [Amer–Talgam-Cohen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08806)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to sum to one, interpreting the society as a distribution over types.
  • Made finite replications precise using N times each type mass bidders and mass 1/N per bidder, excluding the tagged bidder from the other-agent signal counts.
  • Carried over nonnegative valuations, weak increase in other signals, and strict increase in the agent’s own binary signal.
  • Specified explicit semialgebraic partitions and sparse binary polynomial encodings for the input and piecewise-rational output; L includes the entire input representation.
  • Required finite values at every boundary state while permitting unbounded intensities nearby; IC and IR apply to both reports even when a signal group has zero mass.
  • Allowed signed expected payments, as the discrete mechanism definition imposes no separate payment-sign restriction; allocation intensities have only nonnegativity and aggregate feasibility constraints.
  • Treated SOS and monotonicity as input promises and required guarantees at every real state.

Result

Yes. Under the stated convention that an individual report leaves \(\sigma\) unchanged, a polynomial-time construction achieves exact optimal welfare at every state. The SOS promise is unnecessary for this construction.

The two features that make this possible are the unchanged-state IC condition and the permission for intensities to grow without bound near a vanishing signal group. All intensities below are nevertheless finite at every state.

1. A pointwise IC-IR payment rule

Fix a type \(t\) and a state \(\sigma\). Write \(v_b=V_t(b,\sigma)\), so \(v_1>v_0\).

Adding the two IC inequalities gives

\[ (a_1-a_0)(v_1-v_0)\geq 0. \]

Consequently, IC requires \(a_1\geq a_0\). Conversely, any nonnegative allocations satisfying this inequality admit the payments

\[ p_0=a_0v_0,\qquad p_1=a_1v_0. \]

To verify this directly:

  • A bidder with true signal \(0\) obtains utility \(0\) from either report.
  • A bidder with true signal \(1\) obtains utility \(a_1(v_1-v_0)\) from reporting \(1\), and \(a_0(v_1-v_0)\) from reporting \(0\).

Thus truthful reporting maximizes utility, and truthful utility is nonnegative. This verification also applies to reports into zero-mass groups.

It therefore suffices to construct allocations satisfying \(a_t(1,\sigma)\geq a_t(0,\sigma)\).

2. Selecting a highest-valued present group

Order the types as \(1,\ldots,\tau\). Define

\[ W_t(\sigma)= \begin{cases} V_t(0,\sigma),&\sigma_t=0,\\ V_t(1,\sigma),&\sigma_t>0. \end{cases} \]

This is the maximum value among the present signal groups of type \(t\):

  • If \(\sigma_t=0\), only signal \(0\) has positive mass.
  • If \(\sigma_t>0\), signal \(1\) has positive mass and its value strictly exceeds that of signal \(0\).

Hence, writing \(\operatorname{OPT}(\sigma)\) for the benchmark in the question,

\[ \operatorname{OPT}(\sigma)=\max_{t\in T}W_t(\sigma). \]

Let \(w(\sigma)\) be the smallest index attaining this maximum.

3. Allocation and payment intensities

For every type \(t\neq w(\sigma)\), set both allocation and payment intensities to zero.

For the winning type \(t=w(\sigma)\), use the following rules.

If \(\sigma_t>0\), set

\[ a_t(0,\sigma)=0,\qquad a_t(1,\sigma)=\frac{1}{\sigma_t}, \]

\[ p_t(0,\sigma)=0,\qquad p_t(1,\sigma)=\frac{V_t(0,\sigma)}{\sigma_t}. \]

If \(\sigma_t=0\), set

\[ a_t(0,\sigma)=a_t(1,\sigma)=\frac{1}{\mu_t}, \]

\[ p_t(0,\sigma)=p_t(1,\sigma)=\frac{V_t(0,\sigma)}{\mu_t}. \]

In every case, allocations are nonnegative, \(a_t(1,\sigma)\geq a_t(0,\sigma)\), and \(p_t(b,\sigma)=a_t(b,\sigma)V_t(0,\sigma)\). Section 1 therefore proves ex-post IC and IR for every type, state, and counterfactual report.

Feasibility and welfare

If the winning type satisfies \(\sigma_t>0\), its signal-\(1\) group consumes total allocation

\[ \mu_{t,1}a_t(1,\sigma) =\sigma_t\frac{1}{\sigma_t}=1. \]

Every other present group receives zero allocation. Welfare is therefore

\[ V_t(1,\sigma)=W_t(\sigma)=\operatorname{OPT}(\sigma). \]

If the winning type satisfies \(\sigma_t=0\), its signal-\(0\) group consumes total allocation

\[ \mu_{t,0}a_t(0,\sigma) =\mu_t\frac{1}{\mu_t}=1. \]

Its counterfactual signal-\(1\) group has zero mass and contributes zero. Welfare is

\[ V_t(0,\sigma)=W_t(\sigma)=\operatorname{OPT}(\sigma). \]

Thus feasibility holds with equality, and the mechanism achieves exact welfare at every real state.

All intensities are finite: the denominator \(\sigma_t\) is used only where \(\sigma_t>0\), and \(\mu_t>0\) by hypothesis.

4. Polynomial-size explicit representation

The remaining issue is representing the winner regions and payments without enumerating all combinations of input cells.

For each \(t,b\), write the supplied piecewise-polynomial description as cells \(F_{tbj}\) with polynomial values \(P_{tbj}\). All formulas below are interpreted on \(D\).

A piecewise description of \(W_t\) consists of the branches

\[ F_{t0j}\land(\sigma_t=0), \quad\text{with polynomial }P_{t0j}, \]

and

\[ F_{t1j}\land(\sigma_t>0), \quad\text{with polynomial }P_{t1j}. \]

Denote these branches by \((G_{ti},Q_{ti})\). They are disjoint and cover \(D\).

For distinct types \(t,u\) and either comparison relation \(\bowtie\in\{>,\geq\}\), define the Boolean formula

\[ \mathsf C_{tu}^{\bowtie} = \bigvee_{i,j} \left( G_{ti}\land G_{uj}\land (Q_{ti}-Q_{uj}\bowtie 0) \right). \]

At each state, exactly one branch for each type applies. Therefore this formula holds exactly when \(W_t(\sigma)\bowtie W_u(\sigma)\).

The region where type \(t\) wins is consequently described by

\[ \mathsf R_t = \left(\bigwedge_{u<t}\mathsf C_{tu}^{>}\right) \land \left(\bigwedge_{u>t}\mathsf C_{tu}^{\geq}\right). \]

The strict comparisons against smaller indices implement the specified tie-breaking. The regions \(\mathsf R_t\) are disjoint and cover \(D\).

A common partition for all output functions

For each input cell \(F_{t0j}\), output the two case formulas

\[ \mathsf R_t\land F_{t0j}\land(\sigma_t=0), \]

\[ \mathsf R_t\land F_{t0j}\land(\sigma_t>0). \]

These cases are mutually disjoint and cover \(D\). Empty cases impose no conditions and can be retained as vacuous branches in the formula-based description; their nonempty realizations form the partition.

On the first case, specify the winning type’s allocations as \(1/\mu_t\) and its payments as \(P_{t0j}/\mu_t\). On the second case, specify its allocations as \(0,1/\sigma_t\) and its payments as \(0,P_{t0j}/\sigma_t\). All other types’ outputs are zero.

Every output is thus a rational function on its cell. Every denominator is nonzero throughout its cell, including its included boundary points.

Description length and construction time

Let \(K\) be the total number of branches used to describe all the \(W_t\). The explicit input encoding gives \(K=O(L)\).

Across all ordered type pairs, the comparison formulas contain at most \(K^2\) branch-pair disjuncts. Each disjunct copies input formulas and compares a difference of two input polynomials. Sparse subtraction has polynomial bit complexity: it merges the monomial lists, retains binary exponents, and performs rational coefficient arithmetic of polynomial bit length.

There are at most twice as many output cases as input cells for the signal-\(0\) valuations. Expanding all copied formulas, and listing all allocation and payment functions on every case, therefore still uses polynomially many bits and polynomial construction time in \(\tau,L\).

The construction performs only symbolic operations. It does not require evaluating polynomial signs at arbitrary real states or testing semialgebraic feasibility.

Conclusion

The stated mass model admits a polynomial-time, explicitly represented ex-post IC-IR mechanism achieving exact optimal welfare, and hence the requested \(2\)-approximation. Its IC proof is pointwise because an individual report leaves \(\sigma\) unchanged.
