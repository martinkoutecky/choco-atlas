The continuous mirror question, as graded

Fix \(m\) items, rational per-capita supplies \(q\in[0,1]^m\), and a domain \(\mathcal V\) of capped-additive valuations with rational parameters. An instance is a supported distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) complete valuation types, with rational masses summing to \(1\). Each type receives integral bundles, but its mass may be split among bundles: \(x_{t,S}=\mu_t\pi_{\mu,t}(S)\), where \(\pi_{\mu,t}\) is a lottery over \(S\subseteq[m]\), \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}=q_j\) for every item \(j\). Does there exist, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), a polynomial-time truthful maximal-in-range direct mechanism, with a report-independent family of allocation rules and per-agent payments, whose allocation is feasible and has welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\infty}/(2-\varepsilon)\)? Truthfulness means that, holding \(\mu\) fixed under a unilateral zero-mass deviation, every true type \(t\) weakly prefers its truthful report to every report \(r\): \(\sum_S\pi_{\mu,t}(S)v_t(S)-p_{\mu,t}\geq\sum_S\pi_{\mu,r}(S)v_t(S)-p_{\mu,r}\). The running time is measured in \(m,\tau\), and the input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\geq1\), let \(q\in[0,1]^m\) be rational per-capita supplies, and let \(\mathcal V_m\) contain all capped-additive valuations \(v_t(S)=\min\{B_t,\sum_{j\in S}a_{tj}\}\), with nonnegative rational parameters \(B_t,a_{tj}\); both the cap and item values are private.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) complete valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
An allocation specifies a lottery \(\pi_{\mu,r}\) over integral bundles \(S\subseteq[m]\) for every report \(r\in\mathcal V_m\), including reports outside the support. Write \(x_{t,S}=\mu_t\pi_{\mu,t}(S)\); feasibility requires \(x_{t,S}\geq0\), \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}=q_j\) for every item \(j\), so all supplied mass is allocated.
Define \(W_\mu(\pi)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}_\infty=\max_{x\text{ feasible}}\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), without incentive constraints.
For each public \((m,q,\varepsilon)\), a maximal-in-range mechanism fixes a family \(\mathcal R\), independent of reported types and masses, of allocation rules \(A:(\mu,r)\mapsto A_{\mu,r}\). Every rule is feasible at every supported society, and the mechanism selects \(A\in\arg\max_{A\in\mathcal R}W_\mu(A)\), using a fixed tie-breaking rule.
The selected rule supplies \(\pi_{\mu,r}=A_{\mu,r}\) and per-agent payments \(p_{\mu,r}\). Truthfulness requires \(\sum_S\pi_{\mu,t}(S)v_t(S)-p_{\mu,t}\geq\sum_S\pi_{\mu,r}(S)v_t(S)-p_{\mu,r}\) for every \(t,r\in\mathcal V_m\), holding \(\mu\) fixed under a unilateral zero-mass deviation. Payments are unrestricted transfers, and caps constrain valuations rather than payments.
Polynomial time means polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) for the supported allocation and payments, and additionally in the queried report's bit length for an unsupported report; lotteries are represented by finite lists of bundles with binary-rational probabilities, and payments are binary rationals.

For every fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1\), does such a polynomial-time truthful maximal-in-range mechanism guarantee \(W_\mu\geq\mathrm{OPT}_\infty/(2-\varepsilon)\) on every instance, or is this guarantee impossible unless \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{P}/\mathrm{poly}\)?

For two finite bidders with capped-additive valuations and mandatory allocation of all items, achieving a factor \(2-\varepsilon\) by a polynomial-time maximal-in-range mechanism is impossible unless \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{P}/\mathrm{poly}\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.1995)

Result

The guarantee is achievable under the definitions in the question. More strongly, for every fixed rational \(0<\delta<1\), there is a polynomial-time truthful maximal-in-range mechanism satisfying
\[ W_\mu\geq(1-\delta)\mathrm{OPT}_\infty. \]

Its lotteries have polynomial-size supports, all supplies are allocated exactly, and truthfulness holds for every supported and unsupported report.

The range definition permits a singleton containing an entire allocation rule. The substantive work below constructs such a rule with exact truthfulness and the stated approximation. Its menu may depend on the society, because a unilateral zero-mass deviation holds that society fixed.

1. A finite-menu truthfulness lemma

Fix a society \(\mu\) and a finite collection of bundles \(D\subseteq2^{[m]}\). Suppose an allocation using only bundles in \(D\) is feasible. Consider the linear program with variables \(x_{t,S}\), for supported types \(t\) and bundles \(S\in D\):

\[ \max\ \sum_t\sum_{S\in D}x_{t,S}v_t(S), \]

subject to

\[ x_{t,S}\geq0,\qquad \sum_{S\in D}x_{t,S}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_t\sum_{\substack{S\in D\\j\in S}}x_{t,S}=q_j. \]

It is feasible by assumption and bounded because \(0\leq x_{t,S}\leq\mu_t\). Its dual has unrestricted variables \(u_t\) and \(\lambda_j\):

\[ \min\ \sum_t\mu_tu_t+\sum_jq_j\lambda_j, \]

subject to

\[ u_t+\sum_{j\in S}\lambda_j\geq v_t(S) \qquad(t\text{ supported},\ S\in D). \]

Write \(\lambda(S)=\sum_{j\in S}\lambda_j\). Choose optimal primal and dual solutions \(x^*,(u,\lambda)\). Complementary slackness gives

\[ x^*_{t,S}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ v_t(S)-\lambda(S)=u_t. \]

Since \(\mu_t>0\), some \(x^*_{t,S}\) is positive. Together with dual feasibility, this proves

\[ u_t=\max_{S\in D}\bigl(v_t(S)-\lambda(S)\bigr). \]

For a supported report \(t\), assign the lottery
\(\pi_{\mu,t}(S)=x^*_{t,S}/\mu_t\) and charge

\[ p_{\mu,t}=\sum_{S\in D}\pi_{\mu,t}(S)\lambda(S). \]

For an unsupported report \(r\), assign a bundle maximizing
\(v_r(S)-\lambda(S)\) over \(S\in D\), using a fixed ordering to break ties, and charge \(\lambda(S)\).

Thus every truthful report \(t\), supported or unsupported, obtains utility

\[ \max_{S\in D}\bigl(v_t(S)-\lambda(S)\bigr). \]

Every report \(r\) produces a lottery on \(D\), with payment equal to its expected \(\lambda\)-price. Consequently,

\[ \sum_S\pi_{\mu,r}(S)v_t(S)-p_{\mu,r} = \sum_S\pi_{\mu,r}(S)\bigl(v_t(S)-\lambda(S)\bigr) \leq \max_{S\in D}\bigl(v_t(S)-\lambda(S)\bigr). \]

This proves the required truthfulness inequality for every \(t,r\in\mathcal V_m\).

Computational consequence. Given any feasible allocation with polynomially many bundles, take \(D\) to be the union of its bundle supports and solve the preceding LP. The resulting allocation has at least the original welfare and extends to an exactly truthful rule at this fixed society. All computations are polynomial if bundle values can be evaluated in polynomial time.

The remaining task is therefore to compute a sufficiently good feasible allocation with polynomially many bundles.

2. A polynomial-size approximation LP

Canonical valuation parameters

For each supported type, put

\[ b_{tj}=\min\{B_t,a_{tj}\}, \qquad C_t=\min\left\{B_t,\sum_jb_{tj}\right\}. \]

Then

\[ v_t(S)=\min\left\{C_t,\sum_{j\in S}b_{tj}\right\}. \]

Indeed, truncating an individual item value at the original cap does not change any capped bundle value; reducing the cap to the value of the grand bundle also changes no value.

Moreover, \(b_{tj}=v_t(\{j\})\) and \(C_t=v_t([m])\). Hence these parameters are canonical: equality of two capped-additive valuation functions can be tested by comparing their canonical parameters. Their binary encodings have polynomial length.

Rounded cumulative values

Fix rational \(0<\delta<1\), and set \(\eta=\delta/m\).

Consider a type with \(C_t>0\). Let
\(\ell_t=\min\{b_{tj}:b_{tj}>0\}\), and define the rational grid

\[ G_t= \{0,C_t\} \cup \left\{\ell_t(1+\eta)^k: k\text{ is a nonnegative integer},\ \ell_t(1+\eta)^k\leq C_t\right\}. \]

Let \(R_t(z)\) be the largest grid point at most \(z\). For every
\(\ell_t\leq z\leq C_t\),

\[ \frac{z}{1+\eta}\leq R_t(z)\leq z. \]

To verify the lower bound, take the largest geometric grid point \(g\leq z\). Its successor is \((1+\eta)g\), and maximality gives \(z<(1+\eta)g\), unless \(z\) itself is the final grid point \(C_t\).

For a bundle \(S\), process the items in their fixed order, starting at \(s_0=0\), and define

\[ s_j= R_t\!\left( \min\{C_t,s_{j-1}+b_{tj}\mathbf1_{\{j\in S\}}\} \right). \]

Set \(\widetilde v_t(S)=s_m\). Every positive argument to \(R_t\) in this recurrence is at least \(\ell_t\), so the preceding rounding bound applies.

Let

\[ z_j=\min\left\{C_t,\sum_{\substack{i\leq j\\i\in S}}b_{ti}\right\}. \]

We claim that

\[ (1+\eta)^{-j}z_j\leq s_j\leq z_j. \]

The upper bound follows inductively from rounding downward. For the lower bound, suppose \(s_{j-1}\geq\alpha z_{j-1}\), where
\(\alpha=(1+\eta)^{-(j-1)}\). With
\(b=b_{tj}\mathbf1_{\{j\in S\}}\), nonnegativity implies

\[ \min\{C_t,s_{j-1}+b\} \geq \alpha\min\{C_t,z_{j-1}+b\} = \alpha z_j. \]

Rounding loses at most another factor \(1+\eta\), proving the induction.

Finally, \((1+\eta)^{-1}\geq1-\eta\), and Bernoulli’s inequality gives
\((1-\eta)^m\geq1-m\eta=1-\delta\). Therefore, for every bundle,

\[ (1-\delta)v_t(S)\leq\widetilde v_t(S)\leq v_t(S). \]

For a zero valuation, define \(\widetilde v_t(S)=0\).

Polynomial encoding size

The number of grid points is at most

\[ 2+ \left\lfloor \frac{\log(C_t/\ell_t)}{\log(1+\eta)} \right\rfloor. \]

The binary input encoding implies \(\log(C_t/\ell_t)=O(L)\), while
\(\log(1+\eta)\geq\eta/2\) for \(0<\eta<1\). Thus each grid has
\(O(1+mL/\delta)\) points.

Each grid point is obtained by raising a binary-encoded rational to an exponent of this polynomial size. Its numerator and denominator therefore have polynomial bit length. This construction depends polynomially on encoding lengths, rather than on the numerical magnitudes of the valuation parameters.

The flow formulation

For each supported type, construct a layered directed acyclic graph. Layer \(j\) records the rounded state after processing items \(1,\ldots,j\). The initial node is state \(0\) in layer \(0\).

From state \(s\) in layer \(j-1\), provide two labeled arcs:

  • an exclusion arc to \(R_t(\min\{C_t,s\})\);
  • an inclusion arc to \(R_t(\min\{C_t,s+b_{tj}\})\).

Keep these arcs distinct even if they have the same endpoints. Add a common terminal node after layer \(m\), with an arc from every final state \(s\) to that terminal node. Give this terminal arc reward \(s\).

Every source-to-terminal path specifies a bundle by its inclusion labels, and its reward is precisely \(\widetilde v_t(S)\).

Introduce nonnegative flows \(f_{t,e}\), with source-to-terminal flow value \(\mu_t\) and conservation at every internal node. Impose the exact supply equations

\[ \sum_t \sum_{\substack{e\text{ is an inclusion arc}\\ e\text{ processes item }j}} f_{t,e}=q_j \qquad(j\in[m]). \]

Maximize the total terminal reward,

\[ \sum_t\sum_{s\in G_t}s\,f_{t,(m,s)\to\mathrm{terminal}}. \]

This is a rational LP of polynomial size.

It is feasible. For example, let \(U\) be uniform on \([0,1]\), and give every type the bundle
\(S(U)=\{j:U\leq q_j\}\). This lottery has at most \(m+1\) distinct bundles, rational probabilities, and item marginals \(q_j\). Routing its bundles through each type’s graph supplies feasible flows.

Every edge flow is at most \(\mu_t\), so the feasible flow polytope is bounded. Standard exact rational linear programming therefore computes an optimal solution in polynomial time, with polynomial-length rational coordinates.

3. Welfare and finite lottery representation

The original allocation polytope is nonempty and compact: it has finitely many variables, and each variable lies between \(0\) and \(\mu_t\). Thus \(\mathrm{OPT}_\infty\) is attained.

Map an optimal original allocation through the graphs, sending its mass on each bundle along that bundle’s path. The supply equations are preserved exactly. By the pointwise approximation proved above, its rounded objective is at least

\[ (1-\delta)\mathrm{OPT}_\infty. \]

Consequently, the optimal flow LP has objective at least this value.

Decompose each type’s optimal flow into paths. Because the graph is acyclic, repeatedly extracting a positive-flow path and subtracting its smallest edge flow decomposes the entire flow. Each extraction eliminates at least one positive edge, so there are at most as many paths as graph edges.

Replacing every path by its bundle gives a feasible allocation \(x^0\) with polynomially many bundles and binary-encoded rational probabilities. Path decomposition preserves both type masses and every inclusion-arc total, so all supply equalities remain exact.

Since \(v_t(S)\geq\widetilde v_t(S)\) for every bundle,

\[ W_\mu(x^0)\geq(1-\delta)\mathrm{OPT}_\infty. \]

Apply the finite-menu lemma with \(D\) equal to the union of these bundle supports. The resulting supported allocation \(x^*\) satisfies

\[ W_\mu(x^*)\geq W_\mu(x^0) \geq(1-\delta)\mathrm{OPT}_\infty, \]

and its payments and unsupported-report extension satisfy exact truthfulness.

4. The fixed range and computational requirements

For public \(m,q,\delta\), define one allocation rule \(A^\delta\) by the preceding algorithm:

  • Compute the rounded-value flow allocation at the input society.
  • Form its finite bundle menu \(D\).
  • Solve the true-welfare menu LP and its dual.
  • Return the supported lottery for a supported report, and a utility-maximizing menu bundle for an unsupported report.

Use deterministic LP algorithms and fixed orderings throughout. Define

\[ \mathcal R_{m,q,\varepsilon}=\{A^\delta\}. \]

This family is fixed independently of the input types and masses. Its single element is a function of the society and report, exactly as allowed in the question. The rule is feasible at every supported society, and selecting its sole element satisfies the stipulated maximal-in-range condition with fixed tie-breaking.

The menu, prices, and supported allocation are computed from \(\mu\). They remain fixed when evaluating a unilateral zero-mass report, which is the hypothesis used in the truthfulness proof.

For fixed \(\delta\), both LPs, the decompositions, the supported lotteries, and the payments have polynomial size and are computable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). An unsupported query requires evaluating its capped-additive valuation on polynomially many bundles, so its additional running time is polynomial in the report’s bit length. Prices may be negative; unrestricted transfers permit this.

Given fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1\), choose a fixed rational

\[ 0<\delta<\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}. \]

Then \(1-\delta>1/(2-\varepsilon)\), proving the requested guarantee.

5. Why the finite-bidder barriers do not contradict this result

The cited finite-auction results concern mechanisms maximizing over fixed sets of allocation outcomes. For example, Definition 2.3 of the later paper fixes a set of item allocations and maximizes reported welfare over that set. Here, the range elements are entire society-dependent allocation rules. Moreover, the stated incentive constraints compare reports while holding the society fixed. These are different hypotheses from those of the finite-bidder barriers. [Buchfuhrer et al., definitions and lower bounds](https://theory.stanford.edu/~shaddin/papers/mir_soda10.pdf)

The construction uses both distinctions explicitly: its range contains one society-dependent rule, and its exact incentive proof holds a society-dependent menu and prices fixed during deviations. Hence the finite-bidder impossibility does not extend to the model as defined.
