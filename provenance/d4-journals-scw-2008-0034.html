The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(k\ge 3\) and positive rational type masses \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_k\) summing to \(1\), construct a game on atomless cohorts of those masses whose measurable coalitions win exactly when they include the full mass of at least \(k-1\) types; verify its axioms and that its Nakamura number is \(k\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau=k\geq3\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_k\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. There is no alternative-count parameter \(m\).
Realize type \(i\) as the atomless cohort \(T_i=\{i\}\times[0,\mu_i)\), with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\), and let \(N=\bigcup_i T_i\). Coalitions are all Lebesgue-measurable subsets of \(N\).
A simple game is a family \(\omega\) of winning coalitions. Require \(S\in\omega\) exactly when \(\#\{i:\lambda(S\cap T_i)=\mu_i\}\geq k-1\); thus full mass means inclusion up to a null set.
Require \(\emptyset\notin\omega\), \(N\in\omega\), monotonicity, properness, nonstrongness, and nonweakness: winning supersets remain winning; a winning coalition's complement loses; some coalition and its complement both lose; and \(\bigcap_{S\in\omega}S=\emptyset\).
The Nakamura number \(\nu(\omega)\) is the minimum cardinality of a family of winning coalitions with empty intersection, or \(+\infty\) if no such family exists. Intersections and emptiness are literal set operations, not operations modulo null sets.
Construction means producing a finite symbolic description of the game, rather than enumerating measurable coalitions.

Is existence of a game satisfying these requirements and \(\nu(\omega)=k\) decidable in polynomial time in \(\tau=k\) and \(L\), with polynomial-time construction on yes-instances, or is the existence problem NP-hard?

For every \(k\geq3\), finite computable monotonic, proper, nonstrong, nonweak games with Nakamura number \(k\) exist; the same existence result holds for countably infinite computable games ([Kumabe–Mihara](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.0439)).
No prior result settling this exact atomless, full-cohort-mass construction problem was identified in the literature search.

  • Specified the atomless society as disjoint Lebesgue intervals of the prescribed masses, with all Lebesgue-measurable coalitions allowed.
  • Interpreted full mass as equality of coalition mass and cohort mass, permitting null omissions; retained literal empty intersections for nonweakness and the Nakamura number, following the discrete definitions.
  • Expanded the unspecified axioms to monotonicity, properness, nonstrongness, and nonweakness, and required the empty coalition to lose and the grand coalition to win.
  • Specified construction as a finite symbolic description; did not transfer computability on indices of recursive subsets of a countable player set to arbitrary measurable coalitions.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, and every admissible input is a yes-instance. The required winning rule uniquely determines the game. For every positive mass vector in the question, that game satisfies all the stated properties and has \(\nu(\omega)=k\).

The proof uses positivity of the masses, but no numerical relation among them beyond the given assumptions.

1. The prescribed game

Let \([k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}\), and let \(\mathcal M(N)\) denote the Lebesgue-measurable subsets of \(N\). For \(S\in\mathcal M(N)\), define its set of full cohorts by

\[ F(S)=\{i\in[k]:\lambda(S\cap T_i)=\mu_i\}. \]

Define

\[ \omega=\{S\in\mathcal M(N):|F(S)|\geq k-1\}. \]

This is exactly the rule required by the question. We verify its properties directly.

2. Verification of the game properties

Empty coalition and grand coalition. Since every \(\mu_i>0\), we have \(F(\emptyset)=\emptyset\), whereas \(F(N)=[k]\). Hence \(\emptyset\notin\omega\) and \(N\in\omega\).

Monotonicity. Suppose \(S\subseteq R\subseteq N\), with both coalitions measurable. For each \(i\in F(S)\),

\[ \mu_i=\lambda(S\cap T_i)\leq\lambda(R\cap T_i)\leq\lambda(T_i)=\mu_i. \]

Thus \(F(S)\subseteq F(R)\), so a measurable superset of a winning coalition wins.

Properness. If \(S\in\omega\), then for every \(i\in F(S)\),

\[ \lambda((N\setminus S)\cap T_i) =\mu_i-\lambda(S\cap T_i)=0. \]

Since \(\mu_i>0\), none of these indices belongs to \(F(N\setminus S)\). Therefore

\[ |F(N\setminus S)|\leq k-|F(S)|\leq1<k-1, \]

where the final inequality uses \(k\geq3\). Consequently, the complement of every winning coalition loses.

Nonstrongness. Consider the measurable coalition

\[ H=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\bigl(\{i\}\times[0,\mu_i/2)\bigr). \]

For every \(i\), both \(H\cap T_i\) and \((N\setminus H)\cap T_i\) have measure \(\mu_i/2<\mu_i\). Thus \(F(H)=F(N\setminus H)=\emptyset\), and both coalitions lose.

Nonweakness with literal intersection. For each \(i\in[k]\), put

\[ W_i=N\setminus T_i. \]

Each \(W_i\) is measurable and has precisely \(k-1\) full cohorts, so \(W_i\in\omega\). Because the cohorts partition \(N\),

\[ \bigcap_{i=1}^{k}W_i =N\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_i =\emptyset. \]

Since these coalitions belong to \(\omega\), the intersection of all winning coalitions is also literally empty.

3. Exact Nakamura number

The coalitions \(W_1,\ldots,W_k\) above already prove \(\nu(\omega)\leq k\). We now prove the matching lower bound, allowing arbitrary measurable winners and arbitrary null-set omissions.

Let \(1\leq r<k\), and let \(S_1,\ldots,S_r\in\omega\). Define

\[ B_a=[k]\setminus F(S_a),\qquad 1\leq a\leq r. \]

Each winner has at most one cohort that is not full, so \(|B_a|\leq1\). Consequently,

\[ \left|\bigcup_{a=1}^{r}B_a\right| \leq\sum_{a=1}^{r}|B_a| \leq r<k. \]

Choose \(j\in[k]\setminus\bigcup_{a=1}^{r}B_a\). Then every \(S_a\) has full mass in \(T_j\). Equivalently, each measurable set \(T_j\setminus S_a\) has measure zero. Using the literal set identity

\[ T_j\setminus\bigcap_{a=1}^{r}S_a =\bigcup_{a=1}^{r}(T_j\setminus S_a), \]

finite subadditivity gives

\[ \lambda\left(T_j\setminus\bigcap_{a=1}^{r}S_a\right)=0. \]

It follows that

\[ \lambda\left(T_j\cap\bigcap_{a=1}^{r}S_a\right)=\mu_j>0. \]

In particular, \(\bigcap_{a=1}^{r}S_a\neq\emptyset\) as a literal set. Thus fewer than \(k\) winners cannot have empty intersection. Combining both bounds yields

\[ \nu(\omega)=k. \]

This argument explicitly handles the distinction between full mass and literal inclusion: finitely many null-set omissions cannot exhaust a positive-measure cohort.

4. Decision and symbolic construction

On an input satisfying the stated promises, the decision algorithm returns yes.

For construction, output the supplied masses together with the finite specification

\[ T_i=\{i\}\times[0,\mu_i),\qquad N=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_i,\qquad \omega=\left\{S\in\mathcal M(N): \left|\{i\in[k]:\lambda(S\cap T_i)=\mu_i\}\right|\geq k-1\right\}. \]

This consists of the input data and a fixed formula template. Its length and the time needed to produce it are polynomial in \(k\) and \(L\). If promise validation is desired, positivity and the sum condition can also be checked in polynomial time by exact rational arithmetic.

This is a symbolic construction, as requested; it does not require enumerating measurable coalitions or deciding membership from arbitrary descriptions of measurable sets.

The cited [Kumabe–Mihara paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.0439) studies computable games on countable players with recursive coalitions. No transfer of that result to the present measurable setting is needed: the construction and all required properties have been proved directly above.
