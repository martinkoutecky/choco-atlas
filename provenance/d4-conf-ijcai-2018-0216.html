The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), a total utility \(T\), and two coalition-value functions \(\nu_i(q)=\max_{j\in[Q_i]}(b_{ij}+\sum_t a_{ijt}q_t)\) on \(D(\mu)=\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\), compute the minimum \(\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2\) and a type-symmetric per-unit division \(x\) attaining it, subject to \(\sum_t\mu_t x_t=T\), \(\epsilon_i\ge 0\), and \(\sum_t q_t x_t\ge\nu_i(q)-\epsilon_i\) for every \(q\in D(\mu)\) and \(i\in\{1,2\}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
A coalition is any mass vector \(q\in D(\mu)=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}[0,\mu_t]\), including the empty and grand coalitions.
The input includes \(T\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) and two explicitly listed functions \(\nu_i(q)=\max_{1\le j\le Q_i}(b_{ij}+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}a_{ijt}q_t)\), with \(Q_i\ge1\) and signed rational coefficients.
Promise that each \(\nu_i\) is nonnegative on \(D(\mu)\), satisfies \(\nu_i(0)=0\) and \(\nu_i(\mu)\le T\), and is lattice-supermodular: \(\nu_i(q)+\nu_i(r)\le\nu_i(q\wedge r)+\nu_i(q\vee r)\), where meet and join are coordinatewise minimum and maximum.
A type-symmetric per-unit division is \(x\in\mathbb R^{\tau}\), with no sign restriction, satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t x_t=T\).
Compute the minimum \(\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2\) and an attaining division, subject to \(\epsilon_i\ge0\) and \(\sum_tq_tx_t\ge\nu_i(q)-\epsilon_i\) for every \(q\in D(\mu)\) and \(i\in\{1,2\}\).
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(\tau,Q_1,Q_2,L\). There is no separate \(m\) parameter.

Is computing this minimum and an attaining division in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

For finite supermodular two-scenario games, the minimum total nonnegative slack is computable in polynomial time, and an attaining division can also be computed in polynomial time ([Hatano and Yoshida, 2018](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2018/0043.pdf)).
No published complexity result for exactly the stated mass problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored supermodularity as a promise on the coalition-mass lattice; a maximum of affine functions does not by itself guarantee this property.
  • Restored normalized, nonnegative coalition values, nonnegative total utility, and the grand-coalition conditions ν_i(μ) ≤ T from the discrete model.
  • Specified that all affine coefficients and T are binary rationals, with signed affine coefficients and explicitly listed nonempty families of pieces.
  • Specified unrestricted real per-unit payments, matching the discrete definition of a value division rather than imposing nonnegative payments.
  • Defined L as total binary input length and used τ, Q_1, Q_2, L as the running-time parameters because this model has no separate m parameter.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The minimum and an attaining division are computable in \( \operatorname{poly}(\tau(Q_1+Q_2),L) \) time. The explicit max-affine representation suffices; supermodularity is not needed for this algorithm.

1. Exact reduction of the coalition constraints

Write \( Q=Q_1+Q_2 \). Introduce total payments \( y_t=\mu_t x_t \) and coefficients \( c_{ijt}=\mu_t a_{ijt} \). Since each \( \mu_t>0 \), this change of variables is invertible, and the budget becomes \( \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}y_t=T \).

Fix a scenario \( i \) and an affine piece \( j \). For every division \( x \),

\( \displaystyle \max_{q\in D(\mu)} \left(b_{ij}+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}(a_{ijt}-x_t)q_t\right) = b_{ij}+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\max\{a_{ijt}-x_t,0\} = b_{ij}+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\max\{c_{ijt}-y_t,0\}. \)

Indeed, the box constraints allow each coordinate to be chosen independently: choose \( q_t=\mu_t \) when \( a_{ijt}-x_t>0 \), and \( q_t=0 \) when it is negative; either endpoint works when it is zero.

Furthermore, a number dominates a maximum of finitely many affine pieces exactly when it dominates every piece. Consequently, the original constraints for scenario \( i \) are equivalent to

\( \displaystyle \epsilon_i\ge b_{ij}+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\max\{c_{ijt}-y_t,0\} \qquad (1\le j\le Q_i). \)

This equivalence includes all coalitions, including the empty and grand coalitions.

2. A polynomial-size linear program

For every triple \( (i,j,t) \), introduce an auxiliary variable \( u_{ijt} \). Solve the following rational linear program:

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad &\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2\\ \text{subject to}\quad &\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}y_t=T,\\ &\epsilon_i\ge0 &&(i\in\{1,2\}),\\ &u_{ijt}\ge0 &&(i\in\{1,2\},\ 1\le j\le Q_i,\ 1\le t\le\tau),\\ &u_{ijt}\ge c_{ijt}-y_t &&(i\in\{1,2\},\ 1\le j\le Q_i,\ 1\le t\le\tau),\\ &\epsilon_i\ge b_{ij}+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}u_{ijt} &&(i\in\{1,2\},\ 1\le j\le Q_i). \end{aligned} \)

The variables \( y_t \) have no sign restriction.

To verify exactness in both directions:

  • Given a feasible original division and slacks, set \( y_t=\mu_t x_t \) and \( u_{ijt}=\max\{c_{ijt}-y_t,0\} \). Section 1 proves every LP constraint.
  • Given a feasible LP solution, its auxiliary constraints imply \( u_{ijt}\ge\max\{c_{ijt}-y_t,0\} \). Hence its last constraints imply the inequalities in Section 1. Thus \( x_t=y_t/\mu_t \), together with the same slacks, is feasible for the original problem.

The objectives agree under both transformations. Therefore the two problems have exactly the same attainable objective values.

3. Feasibility, attainment, and exact output

The LP is feasible. Explicitly, take

\( \displaystyle y_t^0=\mu_t T,\qquad u_{ijt}^0=\max\{c_{ijt}-y_t^0,0\},\qquad \epsilon_i^0= \max\left\{0,\max_{1\le j\le Q_i} \left(b_{ij}+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}u_{ijt}^0\right)\right\}. \)

The budget holds because \( \sum_t\mu_t=1 \), and the remaining constraints hold by construction. All these quantities are finite rationals.

The objective is bounded below by zero because both slacks are nonnegative. The standard rational linear-programming theorem therefore applies: a nonempty rational LP with an objective bounded below has an attained rational optimum, and an optimal solution can be computed in polynomial time in its explicit binary encoding length.

If \( (y^\ast,\epsilon^\ast,u^\ast) \) is an optimal solution, output

\( \displaystyle \epsilon_1^\ast+\epsilon_2^\ast \quad\text{and}\quad x_t^\ast=\frac{y_t^\ast}{\mu_t}\quad(1\le t\le\tau). \)

The equivalence already proved establishes that this division attains the original minimum.

4. Running time

The LP has \( \tau+2+\tau Q \) variables, \( 2\tau Q+Q+2 \) inequalities, and one equality.

Constructing it requires \( O(\tau Q) \) rational multiplications and \( O(\tau Q) \) coefficient entries. Each product \( c_{ijt}=\mu_t a_{ijt} \) has \( O(L) \) bits, so the entire explicit LP encoding has length

\( \displaystyle O\!\left(\tau Q\bigl(L+\log(\tau Q+2)\bigr)\right). \)

Polynomial-time rational LP optimization and the subsequent divisions by the positive input masses therefore take \( \operatorname{poly}(\tau(Q_1+Q_2),L) \) bit operations and produce outputs of polynomial binary length. No additional population-size parameter is involved.
