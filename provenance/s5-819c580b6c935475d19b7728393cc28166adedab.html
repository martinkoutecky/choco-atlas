Established theorem

Suk and Agarwal’s Theorem 2 gives a policy, Algorithm 2 with its fixed uniform weight specification, satisfying

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E[R_T^{\mathrm B}]\leq \widetilde O\!\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}K^{1/3}(\sigma_{i+1}-\sigma_i)^{2/3}\right), \)

where \( \sigma_0=1 \), \( \sigma_m=T+1 \), and the boundaries are the significant Borda switches of their Definition 1. The preference matrices may vary arbitrarily over time, and the policy does not require knowledge of these boundaries. [Theorem 2 and model setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12950v2#S4.SS1).

We use this established theorem; the remaining argument verifies that ranking populations satisfy its hypotheses.

Exact reduction from ranking populations

Let \( K\geq 2 \), and let \( \mathcal S_K \) be the set of strict rankings of the alternatives \( [K]=\{1,\ldots,K\} \). Fix any sequence of population distributions \( \mu_1,\ldots,\mu_T \) on \( \mathcal S_K \). The distributions may have rational masses and may change at every round.

For distinct alternatives \( a,b \), define

\( \displaystyle P_t(a,b)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal S_K}\mu_t(\pi)\mathbf 1\{a\succ_\pi b\}. \)

Because every ranking is strict, exactly one of \( a\succ_\pi b \) and \( b\succ_\pi a \) holds. Consequently,

\( P_t(a,b)\in[0,1],\qquad P_t(a,b)+P_t(b,a)=1. \)

Set \( P_t(a,a)=1/2 \), using the usual self-comparison convention.

Suppose the policy chooses \( (I_t,J_t) \) from its past observations and internal randomness. A fresh ranking \( \Pi_t\sim\mu_t \) is then sampled independently of that information. For \( I_t\neq J_t \), the observed comparison is

\( Y_t=\mathbf 1\{I_t\succ_{\Pi_t}J_t\}. \)

Let \( \mathcal H_{t-1} \) denote the history available before the current action. Independence of the fresh sample gives, for every action and history having positive probability,

\( \displaystyle \Pr(Y_t=1\mid\mathcal H_{t-1},I_t=a,J_t=b)=P_t(a,b). \)

Since \( Y_t \) is binary, this identifies its conditional distribution as \( \operatorname{Bernoulli}(P_t(a,b)) \). If the policy selects the same alternative twice, an independent fair coin implements \( P_t(a,a)=1/2 \).

Thus the ranking experiment has precisely the required conditional feedback law. Dependence among different comparisons within one ranking causes no difficulty: only one comparison from each fresh ranking is observed.

More formally, use the same policy in the ranking experiment and the corresponding Bernoulli experiment. Their initial histories agree. Whenever their history distributions agree through round \( t-1 \), the policy gives the same conditional action distribution, and the calculation above gives the same conditional feedback distribution. Induction therefore proves equality of their entire action–feedback history distributions.

Regret is preserved

Define the Borda score and its maximum by

\( \displaystyle b_t(a)=\frac1K\sum_{b=1}^{K}P_t(a,b),\qquad b_t^\star=\max_{a\in[K]}b_t(a). \)

The relevant regret is

\( \displaystyle R_T^{\mathrm B}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[b_t^\star-\frac{b_t(I_t)+b_t(J_t)}2\right]. \)

These quantities depend only on the induced preference matrices and the selected actions. Equality of the action-history laws therefore preserves expected regret.

The score also has a direct population interpretation. If \( \operatorname{rank}_\pi(a) \) numbers positions from best to worst, then \( a \) beats exactly \( K-\operatorname{rank}_\pi(a) \) alternatives. Hence

\( \displaystyle b_t(a)=\frac{K-\mathbb E_{\Pi_t\sim\mu_t}[\operatorname{rank}_{\Pi_t}(a)]+1/2}{K}. \)

Thus maximizing this score is equivalent to minimizing expected population rank.

All hypotheses needed for the feedback reduction hold, so Theorem 2 supplies the claimed guarantee. Rationality imposes no additional obstacle; the argument also works for arbitrary real probability masses.

Computational scope and conclusion

The paper reports total algorithmic complexity \( O(KT^2) \) for the Borda setting. The reduction requires no enumeration of rankings or reconstruction of population distributions: the policy consumes comparison outcomes directly. This transfers the reported arithmetic complexity, without establishing an additional bit-complexity bound for explicitly encoded rational inputs. [Remark 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12950v2#S6.SS2).

Therefore, restricting the preference matrices to those induced by ranking populations does not leave the stated existence guarantee open.
