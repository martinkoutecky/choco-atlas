1. Answer and scope

The approximation guarantee is attainable. Correlations permit us to retain or zero each entire background-type interval, giving a finite description with size polynomial in the support size \(\tau\).

For every admissible oracle, a single-call reduction can guarantee tagged truthfulness, IR, no positive transfers, and expected welfare at least \((1-\gamma)W_0\). Its sampled payments can be negative; the payment guarantees are in expectation.

The vanishing-mass question has a different answer. Expected per-advertiser charges are uniformly bounded, but convergence does not follow from the stated oracle assumptions. Moreover:

  • If \(\gamma\geq 1/2\), a correlated reduction guarantees \(p_\epsilon=0\) for every oracle and every \(\epsilon\).
  • If \(\gamma<1/2\), no reduction can guarantee convergence of expected per-advertiser charges for every admissible family of oracles, even with one background type and one slot class.

We prove these statements directly. The [cited finite-advertiser paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.6134) provides the relevant single-call context.

Throughout, polynomial sampling overhead means exact rational sampling in expected polynomial bit time. The distinction from bounded worst-case fair-bit sampling is explained in Section 5.

2. Exact characterization of tagged incentives

Fix \(\epsilon\), the background reports, and a report-independent thinning law. Write \(r=1-\gamma\). Let \(B\in\{0,1\}\) indicate retention of the whole tagged interval, so \(\Pr(B=1)=r\). Let \(S\) describe the retained background cells, and let \(\nu_1,\nu_0\) be its conditional laws given \(B=1,B=0\), respectively.

For a lottery \(D\) in the oracle’s fixed range, define:

  • \(a_\epsilon(D)\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m\): its expected tagged assignments;
  • \(H_\epsilon(S,D)\): its expected welfare on retained background cells.

Feasibility and bounded values give \(\sum_s a_{\epsilon,s}(D)\leq\epsilon\) and \(0\leq H_\epsilon(S,D)\leq V\).

For a tagged report \(b\in[0,V]^m\), put

\(\displaystyle F_{\epsilon,S}(b)=\max_{D\in\mathcal R_\epsilon}\bigl[H_\epsilon(S,D)+b\cdot a_\epsilon(D)\bigr].\)

The maximum is attained because the stipulated MIDR oracle selects a maximizer. Let \(D_{\epsilon,S}(b)\) denote its selected lottery when the tag is retained.

Define

\(\displaystyle f_\epsilon(b)=\mathbb E_{S\sim\nu_1}[F_{\epsilon,S}(b)],\qquad a_\epsilon^1(b)=\mathbb E_{\nu_1}[a_\epsilon(D_{\epsilon,S}(b))].\)

When the tag is zeroed, the queried profile is independent of its original report. Let \(a_\epsilon^0\) be its expected tagged assignment vector in that branch, averaging over \(\nu_0\) and oracle randomness. Consequently, the implemented expected assignment is

\(\displaystyle X_\epsilon(b)=r\,a_\epsilon^1(b)+\gamma a_\epsilon^0.\)

The second term is necessary: a zeroed tagged interval may still receive slots.

2.1 A convex potential

Set

\(\displaystyle G_\epsilon(b)=r f_\epsilon(b)+\gamma b\cdot a_\epsilon^0.\)

For any reports \(b,c\), maximality gives

\(\displaystyle F_{\epsilon,S}(c)\geq F_{\epsilon,S}(b)+(c-b)\cdot a_\epsilon(D_{\epsilon,S}(b)).\)

Taking expectations yields

\(\displaystyle G_\epsilon(c)\geq G_\epsilon(b)+(c-b)\cdot X_\epsilon(b).\)

Thus \(G_\epsilon\) is convex and \(X_\epsilon(b)\) is a subgradient at every report, including reports at ties.

2.2 Expected payments are uniquely determined

Let \(Q_\epsilon(b)=\mathbb E[P_\epsilon(b)]\), and define total expected truthful utility

\(\displaystyle U_\epsilon(b)=b\cdot X_\epsilon(b)-Q_\epsilon(b).\)

Tagged truthfulness is exactly the condition

\(\displaystyle U_\epsilon(c)\geq U_\epsilon(b)+(c-b)\cdot X_\epsilon(b)\)

for all reports \(b,c\). We now verify that this forces \(U_\epsilon-G_\epsilon\) to be constant.

Fix \(b,c\), write \(d=c-b\), and partition their segment into \(b_j=b+jd/n\). Applying the supporting inequalities to consecutive points shows that both \(U_\epsilon(c)-U_\epsilon(b)\) and \(G_\epsilon(c)-G_\epsilon(b)\) lie between

\(\displaystyle \frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}d\cdot X_\epsilon(b_j)\quad\text{and}\quad\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{n}d\cdot X_\epsilon(b_j).\)

The difference between these bounds is

\(\displaystyle \frac1n d\cdot\bigl(X_\epsilon(c)-X_\epsilon(b)\bigr),\)

which tends to zero because both assignment vectors have total mass at most \(\epsilon\). Hence the two increments agree.

At the zero tagged type, IR implies \(-Q_\epsilon(0)\geq0\), while no positive transfers implies \(Q_\epsilon(0)\geq0\). Therefore \(Q_\epsilon(0)=0\), and

\(\displaystyle U_\epsilon(b)=G_\epsilon(b)-G_\epsilon(0).\)

It follows that the necessary and sufficient expected-payment identity for truthfulness, IR, and no positive transfers is

\(\displaystyle Q_\epsilon(b)=r\bigl[F_\epsilon^1(0)-F_\epsilon^1(b)+b\cdot a_\epsilon^1(b)\bigr],\qquad F_\epsilon^1=f_\epsilon.\)

Equivalently, writing \(D_{\epsilon,S}(0)\) for any maximizer of retained-background welfare,

\[\displaystyle Q_\epsilon(b)=r\,\mathbb E_{\nu_1}\!\left[H_\epsilon(S,D_{\epsilon,S}(0))-H_\epsilon(S,D_{\epsilon,S}(b))\right].\tag{1}\]

The background-welfare value at the zero tagged report is independent of which maximizing lottery tie-breaking selects.

For sufficiency, the potential inequality proves truthfulness. Also, \(f_\epsilon(b)\geq f_\epsilon(0)\), so truthful utility is nonnegative.

Finally, maximality at reports \(0\) and \(b\) gives, for each \(S\),

\(\displaystyle 0\leq H_\epsilon(S,D_{\epsilon,S}(0))-H_\epsilon(S,D_{\epsilon,S}(b))\leq b\cdot a_\epsilon(D_{\epsilon,S}(b)).\)

Consequently,

\[\displaystyle 0\leq Q_\epsilon(b)\leq r\epsilon V,\qquad 0\leq\mathbb E[p_\epsilon(b)]\leq rV.\tag{2}\]

These are statements about expectations. They do not determine the distribution of sampled payments.

3. A polynomial-overhead single-call construction

Represent the background intervals by \(I_1,\ldots,I_\tau\), of lengths \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau\), and the tagged interval by \(T_\epsilon\).

Independently sample retention bits for the \(\tau\) background intervals and the tagged interval, each with retention probability \(r\). Retain an interval wholly when its bit is one.

This is jointly measurable and report-independent. Every background point has retention probability \(r\), even though points within a type interval are perfectly correlated.

Make exactly one query, zeroing the reports on nonretained intervals. Keep the population, capacities, and oracle range unchanged, and implement the sampled assignment returned by the oracle.

Let \(z_{t,s}\) be the returned assigned mass from retained background interval \(I_t\) to class \(s\). Compute the realized retained-background welfare

\(\displaystyle H=\sum_{t:\,I_t\text{ retained}}\sum_{s=1}^m v_{t,s}z_{t,s}.\)

Charge the tagged advertiser

\[\displaystyle P_\epsilon=\begin{cases}-H,&B=1,\\[2pt]\dfrac r\gamma H,&B=0.\end{cases}\tag{3}\]

Because the background retention law is the same in both tagged branches,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[P_\epsilon]=r\,\mathbb E_S\!\left[H_\epsilon(S,D_{\epsilon,S}(0))-H_\epsilon(S,D_{\epsilon,S}(b))\right].\)

This is precisely (1), proving tagged truthfulness, IR, and no positive transfers.

The payment is finite and integrable: \(H\leq V\), so

\(\displaystyle |P_\epsilon|\leq V\max\{1,r/\gamma\}.\)

It is also normalized. If everyone receives zero reported value, every retained-background contribution is zero, and hence \(H=P_\epsilon=0\).

3.1 Sampled-payment freedom and correlated thinning

For this construction, all other integrable payments with the same incentive guarantees have the form \(P_\epsilon+Z_\epsilon\), where

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[Z_\epsilon]=0\)

for every tagged report, background profile, and admissible oracle. Normalization additionally requires \(Z_\epsilon=0\) on the outcomes where everyone receives zero reported value.

For a general correlated law, a useful sufficient condition is \(\nu_1\ll\nu_0\). If \(h=d\nu_1/d\nu_0\), then the estimator

\[\displaystyle P_\epsilon=\begin{cases}-H,&B=1,\\[2pt]\dfrac r\gamma h(S)H,&B=0\end{cases}\tag{4}\]

also has expectation (1). It is integrable because

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E|P_\epsilon|\leq rV+rV\int h\,d\nu_0=2rV.\)

For finitely supported masks, this condition is simply \(\nu_1(S)>0\Longrightarrow\nu_0(S)>0\), with \(h(S)=\nu_1(S)/\nu_0(S)\).

Absolute continuity is sufficient, but not necessary: when retained-background welfare is identically zero in the tagged-retained branch, the required expected payment can be zero without it.

3.2 Some correlations cannot support a universal payment rule

Consider the thinning law that retains everyone with probability \(r\) and zeroes everyone with probability \(\gamma\).

Take one background type of mass one, one class with \(q_1=1\), and rational values \(0<c<b\leq V\). Fix \(0<\epsilon<1\). Consider deterministic assignments:

  • \(C\): assign nobody;
  • \(A\): assign a background interval of length \(\epsilon\);
  • \(D\): assign the whole tagged interval.

Compare oracle ranges \(\{C,D\}\) and \(\{C,A,D\}\), with fixed tie-breaking selecting \(C\) when all reports are zero.

At the queried truthful profile, both oracles select \(D\), because \(b\epsilon>c\epsilon\). At the all-zero query, both select \(C\). Thus the reduction observes identical transcripts under both oracles.

Nevertheless, (1) requires expected payment zero for the first oracle and \(rc\epsilon\) for the second. Hence no single-call payment rule based on these transcripts can satisfy the required guarantees for both.

The characterization is therefore the expected externality identity (1), together with integrability and normalization; prescribed retention marginals alone do not make every correlation usable.

4. Welfare guarantee

This proof applies to every report-independent thinning law with the prescribed pointwise marginals.

Let \(D^0_\epsilon\) be the oracle’s selected lottery on the unmodified truthful profile, whose expected welfare is \(W_0\). Let \(w_K(D)\) denote expected welfare using the zeroed profile.

For every realization of \(K\), \(D^0_\epsilon\) remains in the same feasible range. MIDR maximality therefore gives

\(\displaystyle w_K(D_K)\geq w_K(D^0_\epsilon).\)

Actual truthful welfare of the implemented assignment is at least its zeroed-profile welfare, since all values are nonnegative. Thus

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[W_{\mathrm{implemented}}]\geq\mathbb E_K[w_K(D^0_\epsilon)].\)

Joint measurability and bounded welfare permit interchange of expectations and the mass integral. Every point, including every tagged point, is retained with probability \(r\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_K[w_K(D^0_\epsilon)]=rW_0.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[W_{\mathrm{implemented}}]\geq(1-\gamma)W_0.\)

No independence between background points is used here.

5. Encoding and reduction overhead

Let \(A\) be the bit length of the returned rational aggregates.

The construction uses exactly \(\tau+1\) rational intervals. Their endpoints are cumulative sums of the input masses, followed by the tagged endpoint \(1+\epsilon\). Forming these endpoints and the capacities \(q_s(1+\epsilon)\) takes polynomial bit time.

The reduction uses:

  • \(\tau+1\) exact rational Bernoulli samples;
  • \(O(m(\tau+1))\) report entries in the query;
  • \(O(m\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations to compute \(H\);
  • one multiplication by \(r/\gamma\), when needed.

All intermediate rational bit lengths are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,A\). Hence reduction overhead is \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,A)\), with oracle computation and realization cost separate. No individual background advertiser is enumerated.

For exact rational coins, rejection sampling from binary integers has expected polynomial bit cost. If \(\gamma\) is non-dyadic, an algorithm using a uniformly bounded number of fair bits cannot realize its probability exactly: every such algorithm has dyadic output probabilities. Thus a strict bounded worst-case fair-bit interpretation would already make the prescribed marginals impossible. The substantive result uses exact rational sampling with expected polynomial bit overhead, or a rational-coin primitive.

6. Limits for a fixed background profile and oracle family

Fix background reports, \(\gamma\), and the tagged vector \(b\). The oracle range may vary with \(\epsilon\), as the question permits.

6.1 Expected-price convergence

For a general thinning law, define

\(\displaystyle d_\epsilon(S)=\frac{H_\epsilon(S,D_{\epsilon,S}(0))-H_\epsilon(S,D_{\epsilon,S}(b))}{\epsilon}.\)

The argument in Section 2 gives \(0\leq d_\epsilon(S)\leq V\). Every reduction satisfying the incentive requirements must obey

\[\displaystyle \mathbb E[p_\epsilon]=r\,\mathbb E_{\nu_{1,\epsilon}}[d_\epsilon(S)].\tag{5}\]

Therefore expected-price convergence holds exactly when the right-hand side converges. Boundedness alone does not imply convergence.

For the independent whole-type construction, the background mask law \(\pi\) is fixed and finite. A useful sufficient condition is convergence of \(d_\epsilon(S)\) for every mask. Then

\(\displaystyle \lim_{\epsilon\to0}\mathbb E[p_\epsilon]=r\sum_S\pi(S)d_0(S).\)

For example, this follows if expected retained-background welfare has first-order expansions with a common leading term:

\(\displaystyle H_\epsilon(S,D_{\epsilon,S}(i))=h(S)+\epsilon h_i(S)+o(\epsilon),\qquad i\in\{0,b\}.\)

In that case, \(d_0(S)=h_0(S)-h_b(S)\). These expansions are additional stability assumptions, not consequences of MIDR.

6.2 Exact distributional criterion for payment (3)

Let \(H_\epsilon^1\) and \(H_\epsilon^0\) denote the realized retained-background welfare conditional on \(B=1\) and \(B=0\). Set \(Z_\epsilon^i=H_\epsilon^i/\epsilon\). Both variables are nonnegative.

The law of \(p_\epsilon\) is the mixture

\[\displaystyle p_\epsilon\sim\begin{cases}-Z_\epsilon^1,&\text{with probability }r,\\[2pt]\dfrac r\gamma Z_\epsilon^0,&\text{with probability }\gamma.\end{cases}\tag{6}\]

Because both mixture weights and both nonzero scaling coefficients are fixed:

Payment (3) converges in distribution to a finite random variable if and only if both conditional laws \(Z_\epsilon^0,Z_\epsilon^1\) converge weakly to finite nonnegative random variables.

Sufficiency follows immediately from (6). For necessity, tightness of the mixture implies tightness of each conditional law. Moreover, for every \(t>0\),

\(\displaystyle \Pr(Z_\epsilon^0>t)=\gamma^{-1}\Pr\!\left(p_\epsilon>\frac r\gamma t\right),\qquad \Pr(Z_\epsilon^1>t)=r^{-1}\Pr(p_\epsilon<-t).\)

Thus any subsequential conditional limits are uniquely determined by the positive and negative tails of the limiting price law. Their remaining probability is the atom at zero, proving convergence of both conditional laws.

In particular, finite distributional convergence requires \(H_\epsilon^i=O_{\mathsf P}(\epsilon)\). If either branch has background welfare bounded below by a positive constant with probability bounded away from zero along a sequence, the prices are not tight.

6.3 Convergence of expectations to the limiting expectation

If \(p_\epsilon\) converges weakly to an integrable \(p_0\), uniform integrability is sufficient for

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[p_\epsilon]\longrightarrow\mathbb E[p_0].\)

For (3), uniform integrability is equivalent to uniform integrability of both nonnegative families \(Z_\epsilon^0,Z_\epsilon^1\), since

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E\!\left[|p_\epsilon|\mathbf1_{\{|p_\epsilon|>M\}}\right]=r\,\mathbb E\!\left[Z_\epsilon^1\mathbf1_{\{Z_\epsilon^1>M\}}\right]+r\,\mathbb E\!\left[Z_\epsilon^0\mathbf1_{\{Z_\epsilon^0>(\gamma/r)M\}}\right].\)

Uniform integrability is not necessary for signed payments. An exact condition uses the clipping function \(T_M(x)=\max\{-M,\min\{M,x\}\}\): assuming weak convergence to an integrable \(p_0\), convergence of expectations to \(\mathbb E[p_0]\) is equivalent to

\(\displaystyle \lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{\epsilon\to0}\left|\mathbb E[p_\epsilon-T_M(p_\epsilon)]\right|=0.\)

Indeed, bounded continuity gives \(\mathbb E[T_M(p_\epsilon)]\to\mathbb E[T_M(p_0)]\), and integrability gives \(\mathbb E[T_M(p_0)]\to\mathbb E[p_0]\).

6.4 Examples

Bounded expectations with no finite distributional limit.
Take a singleton range assigning all background mass to the one class and assigning no tagged mass. Let the background value be \(c>0\).

Under (3), \(p_\epsilon=-c/\epsilon\) with probability \(r^2\), and \(p_\epsilon=rc/(\gamma\epsilon)\) with probability \(\gamma r\); otherwise it is zero. Its expectation is zero for every \(\epsilon\), but its laws are not tight.

A finite, nonzero limit.
Instead let the range contain two assignments: one assigns only a background interval of length \(\epsilon\), and the other assigns only the tagged interval. Choose \(0<c<b\leq V\).

Payment (3) then satisfies \(p_\epsilon=rc/\gamma\) with probability \(\gamma r\), and is zero otherwise. Its distribution is independent of \(\epsilon\), and its expectation is \(r^2c\).

Distributional behavior also depends on the payment estimator. For example, independently multiplying any admissible payment by \(J_\epsilon/\delta_\epsilon\), where \(J_\epsilon\) is Bernoulli with mean \(\delta_\epsilon\to0\), preserves expected payments and normalization while making the resulting per-advertiser charge converge to zero in probability. Its expected charge still obeys (5). This illustrates why weak convergence and convergence of expectations must be checked separately.

7. Universal finite-price convergence: the exact threshold

7.1 If \(\gamma\geq1/2\), zero payments suffice

Here \(r\leq1/2\). Choose

  • \(K\) equal to the entire background, with probability \(r\);
  • \(K=T_\epsilon\), with probability \(r\);
  • \(K=\varnothing\), with probability \(1-2r\).

Every background point and the whole tagged interval have retention probability \(r\). Make the one query and set \(P_\epsilon=0\).

When \(K=T_\epsilon\), the oracle maximizes \(b\cdot a_\epsilon(D)\) over its fixed range. Reporting the true vector therefore maximizes tagged utility in this branch. In the other branches, the queried profile and resulting assignment are independent of the tagged report.

This proves tagged truthfulness. Nonnegative values give IR, and zero payments give no positive transfers and normalization. Section 4 proves the welfare guarantee.

Moreover, \(p_\epsilon=0\) identically. Thus both its distribution and expectation converge for every admissible oracle family.

7.2 If \(\gamma<1/2\), universal expected-price convergence is impossible

Fix one background type on \(I=[0,1)\), one class with \(q_1=1\), and rational values \(0<c<b\leq V\).

For \(0<\epsilon<1\), let \(J_\epsilon=[0,\epsilon)\). Define assignments:

  • \(A_\epsilon\): assign all background mass and no tagged mass;
  • \(D_\epsilon\): assign \(I\setminus J_\epsilon\) and the whole tagged interval.

Both assign total mass one, within capacity \(1+\epsilon\).

Consider the deterministic range \(\{A_\epsilon,D_\epsilon\}\), with ties selecting \(A_\epsilon\). For any retained background set \(K\cap I\), write

\(\displaystyle \ell_\epsilon(K)=|K\cap J_\epsilon|.\)

When the tag is retained, the zeroed-profile welfare advantage of \(D_\epsilon\) is

\(\displaystyle b\epsilon-c\ell_\epsilon(K)\geq(b-c)\epsilon>0.\)

Thus the oracle selects \(D_\epsilon\). When the tag is zeroed, it selects \(A_\epsilon\). The retained-background externality is exactly \(c\ell_\epsilon(K)\).

By (1), every reduction satisfying the incentive requirements must therefore have

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[p_\epsilon]=\frac c\epsilon\,\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbf1_{\{B=1\}}\ell_\epsilon(K)\right].\)

For each background point \(u\), the prescribed marginals imply

\(\displaystyle \Pr(B=1,\ u\in K)\geq\Pr(B=1)+\Pr(u\in K)-1=2r-1.\)

Joint measurability permits integration over \(J_\epsilon\), yielding

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E\!\left[\mathbf1_{\{B=1\}}\ell_\epsilon(K)\right]\geq(2r-1)\epsilon.\)

Hence, independently of all thinning correlations,

\[\displaystyle \mathbb E[p_\epsilon]\geq c(2r-1)=c(1-2\gamma)>0.\tag{7}\]

By contrast, for the singleton range \(\{A_\epsilon\}\), the tagged allocation is identically zero. The characterization in Section 2 forces expected payment zero.

Now define an admissible oracle family using the singleton range at \(\epsilon=2^{-2n}\), and the two-assignment range at \(\epsilon=2^{-(2n+1)}\); define its range arbitrarily at other positive rational masses. Each range is report-independent and unchanged under zeroing.

Along the first sequence, every qualifying reduction has expected per-advertiser charge zero. Along the second, (7) bounds that expectation below by the fixed positive constant \(c(1-2\gamma)\). Therefore expectations cannot converge as \(\epsilon\to0\).

This obstruction applies to every reduction satisfying the stipulated incentive requirements, regardless of its payment estimator or thinning correlations. Thus universal finite distributional convergence together with convergent expectations is possible exactly when \(\gamma\geq1/2\).
