The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite propositional language, a satisfiable integrity constraint \(\kappa\), a query \(\psi\), and a finite support of types \(t=(K_t,X_t)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\lambda_t\) summing to \(1\), take each type's priority-level function \(\ell_t(w)\) as available after the paper's assumed offline stratification. Let \(R\) be the largest priority level and, for each world \(w\models\kappa\), set \(F_w(j)=\sum_{t:\ell_t(w)\le j}\lambda_t\) for \(j=1,\ldots,R-1\). Select the worlds lexicographically maximizing \((F_w(1),\ldots,F_w(R-1))\); decide whether every selected world satisfies \(\psi\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix a propositional language with \(m\) atoms and worlds \(\Omega=\{0,1\}^m\). Input includes propositional formulas \(\kappa,\psi\), with \(\kappa\) satisfiable.
A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types \(t=(K_t,X_t)\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\lambda_t\), where \(\sum_t\lambda_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each \(K_t\) is a finite sequence of nonempty strata of propositional formulas, ordered from most to least important; inconsistency is allowed. Its ordering strategy \(X_t\) supplies a total preorder on all worlds.
Offline stratification is supplied as a Boolean circuit computing \(\ell_t(w)\) in binary and a binary integer \(R_t\). These are promised to give the consecutive ranks \(1,\ldots,R_t\) of the preorder's nonempty equivalence classes, with rank \(1\) best; ranks are computed over all \(\Omega\), before imposing \(\kappa\). Computing or checking this preprocessing is outside the problem.
Put \(R=\max_tR_t\). For \(w\models\kappa\), define \(F_w(j)=\sum_{t:\ell_t(w)\le j}\lambda_t\) for \(1\le j<R\). Select every world lexicographically maximizing \((F_w(1),\ldots,F_w(R-1))\); retain all ties, and use the empty vector when \(R=1\).
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including formulas, masses, rank circuits, and rank bounds. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), and \(\log R\).

Is deciding whether every selected world satisfies \(\psi\) solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

For finite multisets of bases, the corresponding entailment problem is \(\Theta_2^p\)-complete for best-out, maxsat, and leximin strategies under offline stratification ([Qi, Liu, and Bell, 2006](https://aaai.org/Papers/AAAI/2006/AAAI06-045.pdf)). No result settling the exact rational-mass problem with the input encoding above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The representation of available offline priority functions was unspecified. Supply binary-output Boolean circuits and binary rank bounds, count their size in L, and promise their correctness.
  • Ordering strategies were unspecified. Treat each as a total preorder supplied through its offline rank circuit, retaining the general strategy domain.
  • Priority levels are consecutive ranks of nonempty equivalence classes over all worlds, rather than ranks recomputed among models of the integrity constraint.
  • Retain every maximizing world without tie-breaking; when R=1, every model of the integrity constraint is selected.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

For unbounded \(m\), the problem is \(\Delta_2^p=\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{NP}}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions, on the promised inputs. In particular, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and has no polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The hardness already holds with \(R=2\), dyadic masses, and bases consisting of one singleton stratum. For genuinely fixed \(m\), exhaustive enumeration gives polynomial time in the remaining input size.

I first give a direct \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness reduction, then prove the stronger classification and running-time bounds.

1. Direct hardness with one type and two ranks

Let \(\varphi\) be an arbitrary propositional formula. Introduce fresh atoms \(s,y\), and define

\(\kappa=s\land y,\qquad \psi=\varphi,\qquad g=\neg s\lor(y\land\varphi).\)

Use one type of mass \(1\), with rank circuit

\(\ell(w)=1\) if \(w\models g\), and \(\ell(w)=2\) otherwise.

Set \(R_1=2\). This preorder can be induced by the base with the single stratum \(\{g\}\), using any of best-out, maxsat, or leximin: satisfying \(g\) is strictly preferable to falsifying it.

Both offline rank classes are nonempty independently of \(\varphi\): worlds with \(s=0\) have rank \(1\), while worlds with \(s=1,y=0\) have rank \(2\). Also, \(\kappa\) is always satisfiable.

On worlds satisfying \(\kappa\), the formula \(g\) is equivalent to \(\varphi\). Consequently:

  • If \(\varphi\) is satisfiable, feasible rank-\(1\) worlds exist. Exactly those worlds are selected, and they all satisfy \(\psi\).
  • If \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable, every feasible world has rank \(2\). All are selected, and all falsify \(\psi\).

Thus the constructed instance answers yes exactly when \(\varphi\) is satisfiable. The construction has polynomial size and satisfies every promise. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness even with \(\tau=1\) and \(R=2\).

2. A polynomial-length encoding of the objective

The apparent obstacle to a polynomial oracle algorithm is that \(R\) can be exponentially large. We can avoid constructing the cumulative vector.

Write the masses in their ordinary binary rational encoding as \(\lambda_t=a_t/b_t\). Define

\(D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t,\qquad A_t=a_t(D/b_t).\)

Then every \(A_t\) is a positive integer, and \(\sum_t A_t=D\). Moreover, the bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). Finite binary fractions are covered by this construction.

For a world \(w\), define its integer rank histogram by

\(H_w(r)=\sum_{t:\ell_t(w)=r}A_t,\qquad 1\le r\le R.\)

It satisfies

\(D F_w(j)=\sum_{r=1}^{j}H_w(r),\qquad \sum_{r=1}^{R}H_w(r)=D.\)

Histogram comparison

Suppose two histograms first differ at rank \(r\). Their cumulative sums agree before \(r\), and their difference at \(r\) is exactly the difference between their histogram entries at \(r\).

That first differing rank cannot be \(R\): equality at every earlier rank, together with equal total mass \(D\), forces equality at \(R\).

Therefore lexicographic comparison of the cumulative vectors is exactly lexicographic comparison of the full histograms

\((H_w(1),\ldots,H_w(R)).\)

This also preserves ties. When \(R=1\), all histograms equal \((D)\), as required by the empty-vector rule.

Sparse encoding

Each histogram has at most \(\tau\) positive entries. List them as

\((r_1,h_1),\ldots,(r_k,h_k),\qquad r_1<\cdots<r_k,\qquad k\le\tau.\)

Pad this list to length \(\tau\) using \((R+1,0)\). Encode the list by the coordinate sequence

\(E(w)=(R+1-r_1,h_1,\ldots,R+1-r_\tau,h_\tau).\)

To verify its ordering, consider the first pair where two padded lists differ.

  • If the ranks differ, the smaller next rank contributes positive mass at an earlier histogram position, while the other histogram contributes zero there. The smaller rank is therefore preferable, and its coordinate \(R+1-r_i\) is larger.
  • If the ranks agree, the larger mass is preferable in both representations.

Thus lexicographic maximization of \(E(w)\) selects exactly the worlds prescribed in the question.

Use a fixed width \(b=\lceil\log_2(R+1)\rceil\) for each rank coordinate and a fixed width \(B=\lceil\log_2(D+1)\rceil\) for each mass coordinate. Concatenating their unsigned binary representations gives a bit string of length

\(s=\tau(b+B)=O\!\left(\tau(L+\log(R+1))\right).\)

Because all coordinate widths are fixed, lexicographic comparison of these bit strings preserves the coordinate ordering.

Sorting the supplied ranks, grouping equal ranks, and adding the corresponding integers computes \(E(w)\) in polynomial time.

3. Membership in \(\Delta_2^p\)

Find the lexicographically maximum feasible encoding one bit at a time.

Suppose the already chosen prefix is \(p\). Ask the following \(\mathrm{NP}\) question:

“Does there exist \(w\models\kappa\) whose encoding \(E(w)\) begins with \(p1\)?”

A witness is a world assignment. Verification evaluates \(\kappa\), the rank circuits, and the polynomial-time encoding procedure.

Choose the next bit to be \(1\) precisely when the answer is yes. Otherwise choose \(0\). The invariant that the chosen prefix has a feasible extension holds initially because \(\kappa\) is satisfiable, and is preserved at every step. After \(s\) queries, the resulting string \(e^\star\) is the maximum feasible encoding.

Finally ask whether there exists a world satisfying

\(w\models\kappa\land\neg\psi,\qquad E(w)=e^\star.\)

This is another \(\mathrm{NP}\) query. Answering its negation decides the requested universal entailment, because equality with \(e^\star\) characterizes all selected worlds.

The algorithm uses at most

\(O\!\left(\tau(L+\log(R+1))\right)+1\)

adaptive \(\mathrm{NP}\)-oracle queries and polynomial additional work. Hence the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{NP}}\).

For complexity measured solely by \(L\), unused atoms can be ignored: only atoms occurring in \(\kappa,\psi\), or the rank circuits affect the decision. Their number is bounded by the ordinary explicit input length.

4. \(\Delta_2^p\)-hardness with dyadic masses and \(R=2\)

We give a direct reduction from an arbitrary language \(A\in\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{NP}}\).

Encoding an adaptive oracle computation

Fix a deterministic polynomial-time machine \(M\) deciding \(A\) with a satisfiability oracle. On input \(x\), choose a polynomial bound \(q=q(|x|)\ge1\) on its number of queries.

Pad computations to exactly \(q\) queries by asking a fixed unsatisfiable formula after the original computation halts, while preserving its output.

Introduce answer atoms \(a_1,\ldots,a_q\). Given a hypothetical answer sequence \(a\), simulate \(M(x)\) using those answers. This defines polynomial-time functions:

  • \(Q_i(x,a_1,\ldots,a_{i-1})\), the formula submitted at query \(i\);
  • \(O(x,a_1,\ldots,a_q)\), the final acceptance bit.

For each query introduce a polynomial-length certificate block \(u_i\). Let \(V_i(x,a_1,\ldots,a_{i-1},u_i)\) verify that \(u_i\) satisfies the query formula \(Q_i\).

Construct a Boolean circuit expressing

\(C_x(a,u,z)= \left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{q}(\neg a_i\lor V_i(x,a_1,\ldots,a_{i-1},u_i))\right) \land \left(z\leftrightarrow O(x,a_1,\ldots,a_q)\right).\)

All these circuits have polynomial size: the machine and verifier have fixed polynomial running-time bounds, and their bounded computations can be unrolled into Boolean circuits.

Convert \(C_x\) into a propositional formula \(\kappa_x\) by introducing a variable for each gate, imposing its defining equivalence, and requiring the output gate to be true. Thus an assignment to \(a,u,z\) extends to a model of \(\kappa_x\) exactly when it satisfies \(C_x\).

The formula \(\kappa_x\) is satisfiable: take every \(a_i=0\), arbitrary certificates, and \(z=O(x,0,\ldots,0)\). Every certificate implication then holds.

The lexicographically maximum feasible answers are correct

Let \(a^\star\) be the lexicographically maximum answer sequence extendible to a model of \(\kappa_x\). We prove inductively that it equals the actual oracle transcript.

Assume its first \(i-1\) answers are correct. The circuit \(Q_i\) therefore describes the actual next query.

If this query is unsatisfiable, no certificate \(u_i\) makes \(V_i\) true. Hence every feasible extension of this prefix has \(a_i=0\).

If this query is satisfiable, choose a satisfying certificate and set \(a_i=1\). The earlier positive answers also have certificates, since they are actual positive oracle answers. Set every later answer to \(0\), so that all later certificate implications hold automatically, and choose \(z\) according to \(O\). This produces a feasible extension with \(a_i=1\). Lexicographic maximality therefore forces \(a_i^\star=1\).

This proves the induction. Every model with answer sequence \(a^\star\) consequently has \(z=1\) exactly when \(M\) accepts \(x\).

Implementing lexicographic answer maximization

Create \(q\) types, one for each answer atom. Type \(i\) has the singleton-stratum base \(\{a_i\}\), mass

\(\lambda_i=2^{-i},\)

and ranks

\(\ell_i(w)=1\) if \(a_i=1\), and \(\ell_i(w)=2\) otherwise.

Add one type with the singleton-stratum base \(\{\top\}\), constant rank \(1\), and mass

\(\lambda_0=2^{-q}.\)

All masses are positive dyadic rationals, and

\(\lambda_0+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\lambda_i=2^{-q}+(1-2^{-q})=1.\)

Each answer type has both rank classes nonempty over all worlds, since its answer atom can independently be true or false before imposing \(\kappa_x\). The additional type has exactly one nonempty class. Therefore all offline rank promises hold, and \(R=2\).

There is only one cumulative coordinate:

\(F_w(1)=2^{-q}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}2^{-i}a_i.\)

This strictly orders answer sequences lexicographically. Indeed, if two sequences first differ at position \(i\), with the first having \(1\), their score difference is at least

\(2^{-i}-\sum_{j=i+1}^{q}2^{-j}=2^{-q}>0.\)

Consequently, the selected worlds are exactly the models of \(\kappa_x\) with answer sequence \(a^\star\).

Set \(\psi=z\). Every selected world satisfies \(\psi\) exactly when \(M\) accepts \(x\), equivalently when \(x\in A\).

The construction has polynomial size, including all \(q+1\) binary-encoded masses. This proves \(\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{NP}}\)-hardness under polynomial-time many-one reductions, and completes the classification.

5. Running times and the mass encoding

An unconditional deterministic algorithm enumerates all \(2^m\) worlds, computes their sparse encodings, and maintains the best encoding together with whether any tied world falsifies \(\psi\).

Using elementary integer arithmetic, one valid bit-operation bound is

\(O\!\left( \tau L^2+ 2^m\left[m+L+\tau L+ \tau\log_2(\tau+1)\lceil\log_2(R+1)\rceil\right] \right).\)

The preprocessing term covers conversion to common-denominator integer masses. Per world, the remaining terms cover formula and circuit evaluation, sorting ranks, grouping masses, and comparing encodings.

The oracle algorithm instead takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,\log(R+1)\), with \(O(\tau(L+\log(R+1)))\) adaptive \(\mathrm{NP}\)-oracle calls.

The hardness construction also explains why the [multiset result quoted in the question](https://aaai.org/Papers/AAAI/2006/AAAI06-045.pdf) does not yield the same upper bound here. Realizing its dyadic masses as equal-mass individuals requires a population size divisible by \(2^q\), because one mass is \(2^{-q}\). The rational support representation has polynomial size, whereas that explicit expansion has exponential size in \(q\).
