The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses μ_t over finitely many advertiser types \(t=(v_t,b_t)\), rational slot qualities \(α_1>\cdots>α_m>0\), and per-capita supply \(q\) of each slot class, can one compute in time polynomial in \(m,τ,L,\log(1/ε)\) a compact randomized direct mechanism? Each advertiser receives an integral bundle \(S\subseteq M\) and has value \(v_t\sum_{j\in S}α_j\); the mechanism must respect supply \(q\), be ex-interim individually rational and incentive compatible for a tagged advertiser facing a fixed background distribution of other reports, and be ex-post no-positive-transfers, budget-feasible, and Pareto optimal.

Types are \(t=(v_t,b_t)\), with rational \(v_t>0\) and \(b_t\geq0\); both coordinates are private, and equal valuations are allowed.
There are \(m\geq1\) slot classes \(M=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), rational qualities \(\alpha_1>\cdots>\alpha_m>0\), and rational per-capita supply \(0<q\leq1\) of each class. Each advertiser receives an integral bundle \(S\subseteq M\), with no unit-demand restriction, and pays \(p\); utility is \(v\sum_{j\in S}\alpha_j-p\) when \(p\leq b\), and \(-\infty\) otherwise.
A direct mechanism supplies jointly randomized measurable allocations and payments for the population and a lottery for a tagged advertiser reporting any \((\hat v,\hat b)\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^2\). A tagged deviation has zero mass and leaves the background report distribution fixed; its truthful lottery agrees with that of an advertiser of the same type in the population.

Expectations are over mechanism randomization, with any positive probability of exceeding the true budget giving expected utility \(-\infty\). Every realized outcome must be Pareto optimal for the reported society: no alternative measurable integral allocation with the same supplies and integrable payments satisfying \(p'\leq b\) may weakly improve utility almost everywhere and weakly increase the auctioneer's total payment, with strict improvement on a positive-mass set or a strict revenue increase. For finite societies, a polynomial-time randomized mechanism exists with private budgets, ex-interim IR and IC, and ex-post NPT and Pareto optimality, for divisible or indivisible items ([Dütting–Henzinger–Starnberger](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.6448)).

8. Impossibility under bounded fair-bit sampling

The sampling distinction cannot be removed merely by finding a different economic mechanism.

Proposition

Assume an actual tagged lottery must be sampled exactly using a worst-case bounded number of fair bits. Under the type-agreement requirement, no mechanism satisfies the question’s requirements for every allowed input.

Proof. Consider the input

$$ m=\tau=1,\qquad \mu_1=1,\qquad \alpha_1=v_1=1,\qquad b_1=0,\qquad q=\frac13. $$

Budget feasibility and nonnegative payments force every original payment to equal zero.

In every realized population outcome, Pareto optimality forces assigned mass to equal $q$. Indeed, if assigned mass were $z<q$, atomlessness would permit assigning the item to a positive-mass subset of unallocated advertisers, of mass at most $q-z$. Keeping every payment zero would strictly improve those advertisers’ utilities and leave revenue unchanged.

Let $\pi$ be the truthful tagged advertiser’s winning probability. By type agreement, almost every population advertiser has winning probability $\pi$. Joint measurability and Tonelli’s theorem give

$$ \pi = \int \mathbb E[\mathbf 1_{\{\text{advertiser receives item}\}}]\,da = \mathbb E\left[ \int\mathbf 1_{\{\text{advertiser receives item}\}}\,da \right] = q = \frac13. $$

Now fix this input, the truthful tagged report, and any allowed precision parameter. A worst-case running-time bound implies a finite upper bound $H$ on the number of fair bits used.

Pad every computation with unused bits to length $H$. The sample is then a deterministic function of $H$ fair bits. Its winning probability must consequently be

$$ \frac{N}{2^H} $$

for an integer $N$. Such a probability cannot equal $1/3$: otherwise $3N=2^H$, contradicting divisibility.

This proves impossibility. ∎

The proposition concerns exact sampling of an outcome. Returning a symbolic description of a lottery with probability $1/3$ does not sample that lottery. Numerical precision also does not avoid the obstruction because the economic requirements remain exact.
