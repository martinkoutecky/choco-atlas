Theorem 19 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given bipartite worker and slot types \(I\) and \(J\), allowed pairs \(E\), nonnegative rational masses \(\mu_i,\nu_j\), and rational surpluses \(w_{ij}\), choose matching masses \(x_{ij}\geq 0\) subject to \(\sum_j x_{ij}\leq\mu_i\) and \(\sum_i x_{ij}\leq\nu_j\). Let \(W\) be the maximum of \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij}\). Compute payoff rates \(u_i,v_j\geq 0\) satisfying \(\sum_i\mu_i u_i+\sum_j\nu_j v_j=W\) and \(u_i+v_j\geq w_{ij}\) for every allowed pair, with rates zero for nonessential types. Among these continuous core imputations, find one minimizing the largest essential-type rate, one maximizing the smallest essential-type rate, and one minimizing their spread.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I\) and \(J\) be disjoint finite sets of worker and slot types, with a nonempty set of allowed pairs \(E\subseteq I\times J\) and strictly positive rational surpluses \(w_{ij}\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) positive-mass types, each with its mass \(\mu_i\) or \(\nu_j\) encoded as a binary rational. Masses need not sum to one. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(m=|E|\) and \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A matching consists of real masses \(x_{ij}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{j:(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}\leq\mu_i\) and \(\sum_{i:(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}\leq\nu_j\). Let \(W\) be the maximum of \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij}\).
A type is essential if its capacity constraint holds with equality in every matching attaining \(W\). Let \(A_*\subseteq I\cup J\) denote the essential types, and assume \(A_*\neq\varnothing\).
A continuous core imputation is a vector of nonnegative real payoff rates \((u,v)\) satisfying \(\sum_i\mu_i u_i+\sum_j\nu_j v_j=W\) and \(u_i+v_j\geq w_{ij}\) for all \((i,j)\in E\), with rates zero on nonessential types. Write \(r_i=u_i\) and \(r_j=v_j\).
Compute three imputations, optimizing separately: minimize \(\max_{a\in A_*}r_a\), maximize \(\min_{a\in A_*}r_a\), and minimize \(\max_{a\in A_*}r_a-\min_{a\in A_*}r_a\). Any optimizer is acceptable when there are ties.
Is this computation problem solvable exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
For finite assignment games with unit-capacity agents and positive edge weights, min-max fair, max-min fair, and minimum-spread core imputations are computable in polynomial time. Their minimum and spread objectives range over agents matched in every maximum-weight matching.
That result concerns individual unit-capacity agents; it does not state the complexity of the arbitrary rational-mass, payoff-rate problem defined here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Defined an essential type as one whose entire mass is matched in every optimal matching, extending the discrete requirement of being matched in every optimum.
  • Retained only positive-mass types in the support and allowed arbitrary total mass without normalization.
  • Restricted allowed-pair surpluses to strictly positive rationals and required a nonempty allowed-pair set, carrying over the intended positive-worth assignment-game domain.
  • Explicitly required a nonempty essential-type set so that the minimum and spread objectives are defined.
  • Defined m as the number of allowed pairs and L as the total input bit length.
  • Specified three separate optimizations and allowed any optimizer in case of ties.

All three imputations are computable exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). An algorithm uses \(\tau+4\) rational linear programs, each of polynomial encoding length.

1. Matching optimization and its dual.

Write \(T=I\cup J\), and define the capacity vector by \(c_i=\mu_i\) for \(i\in I\) and \(c_j=\nu_j\) for \(j\in J\). Let \(B\) be the vertex-edge incidence matrix: the column for \((i,j)\in E\) has a \(1\) in rows \(i,j\) and zeros elsewhere. Thus \((Bx)_a\) is the matching mass incident to type \(a\).

The welfare problem is the linear program

\(\displaystyle W=\max\{w^{\mathsf T}x:Bx\leq c,\ x\geq0\}.\)

Its feasible region is nonempty, since it contains \(x=0\), and compact: each coordinate satisfies \(0\leq x_{ij}\leq\min(c_i,c_j)\). Consequently, its optimum is finite and attained. Moreover, \(W>0\), because an allowed edge has positive weight and both endpoints have positive capacity.

The dual program is

\(\displaystyle \min\{c^{\mathsf T}r:B^{\mathsf T}r\geq w,\ r\geq0\}.\)

Here \(B^{\mathsf T}r\geq w\) means precisely \(r_i+r_j\geq w_{ij}\) on every allowed pair. By linear programming strong duality, the dual has an attained optimum equal to \(W\).

We next verify that its optimal solutions already satisfy the required zero rates on nonessential types. For every primal-feasible \(x\) and dual-feasible \(r\),

\(\displaystyle c^{\mathsf T}r-w^{\mathsf T}x =\sum_{a\in T}r_a\bigl(c_a-(Bx)_a\bigr) +\sum_{(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}(r_i+r_j-w_{ij}).\)

Every summand on the right is nonnegative. If both \(x\) and \(r\) are optimal, the left side is zero, so every summand is zero.

If \(a\) is nonessential, there exists an optimal matching \(x\) with \(c_a-(Bx)_a>0\). Pairing this matching with any optimal dual solution forces \(r_a=0\). Thus every optimal dual solution is zero on every nonessential type.

It follows that the continuous core in the question is exactly the nonempty polytope

\(\displaystyle \mathcal C=\{r\geq0:B^{\mathsf T}r\geq w,\ c^{\mathsf T}r=W,\ r_a=0\text{ for }a\notin A_*\}.\)

The last equalities are redundant, but we may include them after computing \(A_*\).

2. Computing the essential types exactly.

First compute \(W\). The set of welfare-optimal matchings is the nonempty compact polytope

\(\displaystyle F=\{x\geq0:Bx\leq c,\ w^{\mathsf T}x=W\}.\)

For each \(a\in T\), solve the linear program

\(\displaystyle \delta_a=\max_{x\in F}\bigl(c_a-(Bx)_a\bigr).\)

The slack being maximized is nonnegative throughout \(F\). Therefore:

  • If \(\delta_a=0\), every matching in \(F\) saturates type \(a\), so \(a\) is essential.
  • If \(\delta_a>0\), an attained maximizer leaves positive slack at type \(a\), so \(a\) is nonessential.

Hence

\(\displaystyle A_*=\{a\in T:\delta_a=0\}.\)

This procedure uses \(\tau\) additional linear programs. The comparisons with zero are exact rational comparisons.

3. Linear programs for the three objectives.

For every \(r\in\mathcal C\), nonnegativity and \(c^{\mathsf T}r=W\) imply \(0\leq r_a\leq W/c_a\). Define the rational bound

\(\displaystyle M=\max_{a\in T}\frac{W}{c_a}.\)

Because \(A_*\neq\varnothing\), the quantities

\(\displaystyle H(r)=\max_{a\in A_*}r_a,\qquad K(r)=\min_{a\in A_*}r_a\)

are defined and satisfy \(0\leq K(r)\leq H(r)\leq M\).

Solve the following three programs separately, always including \(r\in\mathcal C\):

| Requested imputation | Linear objective | Additional constraints |
|---|---|---|
| Minimize the maximum rate | Minimize \(h\) | \(0\leq h\leq M\), and \(r_a\leq h\) for every \(a\in A_*\) |
| Maximize the minimum rate | Maximize \(\ell\) | \(0\leq\ell\leq M\), and \(\ell\leq r_a\) for every \(a\in A_*\) |
| Minimize the spread | Minimize \(h-\ell\) | \(0\leq\ell\leq h\leq M\), and \(\ell\leq r_a\leq h\) for every \(a\in A_*\) |

We verify each formulation.

For the first program, every feasible extension of \(r\) has \(h\geq H(r)\), while \(h=H(r)\) is feasible. Thus optimizing over the auxiliary variable gives exactly the objective \(H(r)\).

For the second program, every feasible extension has \(\ell\leq K(r)\), while \(\ell=K(r)\) is feasible. Thus its objective is exactly \(K(r)\).

For the third program, every feasible extension satisfies

\(\displaystyle h-\ell\geq H(r)-K(r).\)

Equality is achieved by taking \(h=H(r)\) and \(\ell=K(r)\). These choices obey all the bounds. Hence minimizing over the auxiliary variables gives exactly the spread \(H(r)-K(r)\).

In each case, the rate vector of any optimal LP solution is a requested optimizer. All three feasible regions are nonempty and compact: core rates are bounded as above, and the auxiliary variables are explicitly bounded. Therefore all three optima are attained.

4. Exact polynomial-time complexity.

We use the standard exact rational linear programming theorem: an LP with binary-encoded rational data can be solved in time polynomial in its encoding length; for a nonempty bounded feasible region, an exact rational optimizer of polynomial encoding length can be returned. Exact polynomial-time algorithms and rational reconstruction are discussed in [Gleixner and Steffy, Section 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.12820v1#S1.SS3).

Every application here meets those hypotheses:

  • The welfare LP has \(m\) variables and \(m+\tau\) inequality constraints, with rational input coefficients.
  • Each essentiality LP adds one rational equality to those constraints and has a nonempty compact feasible region.
  • Each fairness LP has at most \(\tau+2\) variables and \(O(m+\tau)\) constraints, again with a nonempty compact feasible region.

The computed number \(W\) has polynomial encoding length. Indeed, a welfare optimum can be chosen at a vertex of its rational feasible polytope; its coordinates have polynomial encoding length by Cramer’s rule applied to linearly independent active constraints. Taking their rational scalar product with \(w\) preserves polynomial encoding length. Consequently, inserting \(W\) into later LPs does not cause an exponential increase in input length.

Likewise, \(M\) has polynomial encoding length: it is one of the \(\tau\) rational numbers \(W/c_a\), each computable and comparable in polynomial time. All coefficients in all subsequent programs therefore have polynomial encoding length in \(m,\tau,L\).

The algorithm performs one welfare optimization, \(\tau\) essentiality optimizations, and three fairness optimizations. Its total running time and the encoding length of its three output imputations are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The argument works directly with rational masses and payoff rates, without expanding masses into individual agents.
