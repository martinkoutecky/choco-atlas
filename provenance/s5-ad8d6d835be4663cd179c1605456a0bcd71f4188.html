Theorem 1 (General Graph) — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a connected comparison graph \(G=([n],E)\) and an explicitly listed rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete rankings of the \(n\) items, with support size \(\tau\), compute the normalized BTL score vector when the induced pairwise marginals \(q_{ij}=\sum_{\sigma:\,j\succ_\sigma i}\mu_\sigma\) satisfy \(q_{ij}=w_j/(w_i+w_j)\) on every edge. Equivalently, form the transition matrix from these marginals and return its stationary distribution.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=n\geq2\) be the number of items and \(G=([m],E)\) a connected simple undirected comparison graph.
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) strict complete rankings with positive masses \(\mu_\sigma\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_\sigma\mu_\sigma=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number \(m!\) of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
For each oriented comparison edge, define \(q_{ij}=\sum_{\sigma:\,j\succ_\sigma i}\mu_\sigma\). The input is promised to admit strictly positive BTL scores \(w\in\mathbb R_{>0}^{m}\) satisfying \(q_{ij}=w_j/(w_i+w_j)\) on every edge.
Let \(d_{\max}\) be the maximum degree of \(G\). Define \(P_{ij}=q_{ij}/d_{\max}\) for distinct adjacent items, \(P_{ij}=0\) for distinct nonadjacent items, and \(P_{ii}=1-\sum_{j\ne i}P_{ij}\).
The required output is the exact normalized score vector \(\pi_i=w_i/\sum_jw_j\), represented by binary rationals; equivalently, the stationary distribution of \(P\).

Is this promised exact score-computation problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely sampled BTL comparisons, Rank Centrality has a high-probability relative score-error bound on connected graphs under sufficient sampling and a positive absolute spectral gap for the simple random walk. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.1688).
For exact BTL edge marginals, the normalized scores are already known to equal the stationary distribution of the transition matrix above; this result does not address the explicit rational-support input model and its bit complexity. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.1688).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Set m=n as the item-count parameter and require at least two items, so the maximum-degree normalization is defined.
  • Specify a simple undirected comparison graph and strict complete rankings.
  • Require positive binary-rational support masses summing to one.
  • Interpret BTL consistency as a promise of strictly positive real scores, normalized to sum to one.
  • Specify the maximum-degree transition normalization, zero transitions between nonadjacent distinct items, and diagonal self-loops.
  • Interpret compute as exact output in binary-rational form, with L the total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact score computation is polynomial-time solvable

We use the standard encoding of a rational number as a numerator and denominator written in binary. Under the stated promise, a spanning tree determines all score ratios exactly. The main issue is bounding the sizes of the integers used in this computation.

1. Compute exact edge marginals

Write the input masses as \(\mu_\sigma=a_\sigma/b_\sigma\), with positive integers \(a_\sigma,b_\sigma\). Define

\(D=\prod_{\sigma=1}^{\tau}b_\sigma,\qquad c_\sigma=a_\sigma(D/b_\sigma).\)

Every \(c_\sigma\) is a positive integer. The normalization of the masses gives

\(\sum_{\sigma=1}^{\tau}c_\sigma=D.\)

Because the total bit length of the input denominators is at most \(L\), we have \(\log_2 D\leq L\). Thus \(D\) and every \(c_\sigma\) have \(O(L)\) bits.

For an oriented edge \((i,j)\), define

\(A_{ij}=\sum_{\sigma:\,j\succ_\sigma i}c_\sigma.\)

Then

\(q_{ij}=A_{ij}/D.\)

Strict complete rankings place exactly one of \(i,j\) above the other, so \(A_{ji}=D-A_{ij}\). The positive BTL promise implies \(0<q_{ij}<1\); consequently,

\(1\leq A_{ij}\leq D-1.\)

The score ratio is therefore the exact rational

\(\displaystyle \frac{w_j}{w_i}=\frac{q_{ij}}{1-q_{ij}}=\frac{A_{ij}}{D-A_{ij}}.\)

These marginals are computed directly from the listed support. No enumeration of the other possible rankings is required.

2. Recover the scores along a spanning tree

Since \(G\) is connected, choose a spanning tree \(T\), root it at an arbitrary vertex \(r\), and orient its edges from parent to child.

For each oriented tree edge \(e=(u,v)\), set

\(\alpha_e=A_{uv},\qquad \beta_e=D-A_{uv}.\)

Both integers are positive, and the promise gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{\alpha_e}{\beta_e}=\frac{w_v}{w_u}.\)

Let \(H_i\) be the set of edges on the unique tree path from \(r\) to \(i\). Define

\(\displaystyle x_i=\prod_{e\in H_i}\frac{\alpha_e}{\beta_e},\)

where the empty product gives \(x_r=1\). Multiplying the edge ratios along that path yields

\(\displaystyle x_i=\frac{w_i}{w_r}.\)

It follows that

\(\displaystyle \frac{x_i}{\sum_jx_j}=\frac{w_i}{\sum_jw_j}.\)

This also proves uniqueness of the required normalized vector: every positive score vector satisfying the edge equations has the same ratios to the root.

3. An integer construction with bounded output length

To avoid uncontrolled denominator growth during normalization, define the positive integer

\(\displaystyle W_i=\left(\prod_{e\in H_i}\alpha_e\right)\left(\prod_{e\in T\setminus H_i}\beta_e\right).\)

Let \(K=\prod_{e\in T}\beta_e\). Direct cancellation gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{W_i}{K}=\prod_{e\in H_i}\frac{\alpha_e}{\beta_e}=x_i.\)

Hence the algorithm can output

\(\displaystyle S=\sum_{j=1}^{m}W_j,\qquad \pi_i=\frac{W_i}{S}.\)

Each \(W_i\) is a product of exactly \(m-1\) integers, each at most \(D\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle 1\leq W_i\leq D^{m-1},\qquad S\leq mD^{m-1}.\)

Every output numerator and denominator consequently has

\(\displaystyle O(mL+\log m)\)

bits. The whole vector has encoding length \(O(m^2L+m\log m)\). If reduced fractions are required, divide \(W_i\) and \(S\) by their greatest common divisor; this cannot increase their bit lengths.

Thus exact output itself poses no exponential-length obstruction.

4. Polynomial bit complexity

The construction can be implemented as follows.

  • Build the position of every item in every listed ranking.
  • Find a rooted spanning tree of \(G\).
  • Compute \(D\) and the integer masses \(c_\sigma\).
  • For each of the \(m-1\) tree edges, scan the support and compute its integer marginal \(A_{uv}\).
  • Compute the products \(W_i\), their sum \(S\), and the output fractions.

The position tables require \(O(\tau m)\) entries. Finding the tree requires \(O(m+|E|)\) graph operations. Computing the tree marginals requires \(O(\tau m)\) preference tests and integer additions. Computing all \(W_i\) directly requires \(O(m^2)\) integer multiplications.

Every intermediate integer in these steps has \(O(mL+\log m)\) bits: the masses and marginals are bounded by \(D\), the product intermediates by \(D^{m-1}\), and the final sum by \(mD^{m-1}\). Binary integer addition, multiplication, exact division, and greatest-common-divisor computation all take polynomial time in their operand lengths.

Since \(|E|\leq m(m-1)/2\), these bounds establish deterministic running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

5. Verification of the stationary-distribution interpretation

The exact-BTL stationary-distribution identity appears in the cited [Rank Centrality paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.1688). It can also be checked directly here.

For every comparison edge,

\(\displaystyle \pi_iP_{ij}=\frac{w_iw_j}{d_{\max}(w_i+w_j)\sum_kw_k}=\pi_jP_{ji}.\)

For nonedges both sides vanish, and for diagonal entries the identity is automatic. Moreover, \(d_{\max}\geq1\), and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j\ne i}P_{ij}\leq\frac{\deg(i)}{d_{\max}}\leq1,\)

so \(P\) is stochastic. Summing detailed balance gives

\(\displaystyle (\pi P)_j=\sum_i\pi_iP_{ij}=\pi_j\sum_iP_{ji}=\pi_j,\)

where the last sum is the sum of row \(j\).

Every graph edge has positive transition probabilities in both directions. Connectedness therefore makes \(P\) irreducible. The theorem that a finite irreducible stochastic matrix has a unique stationary distribution applies, so the computed vector is that distribution.

No sampling condition or spectral-gap bound is needed for this exact algebraic computation.
