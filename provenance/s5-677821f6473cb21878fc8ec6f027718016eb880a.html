Theorem 7 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Fix \(r=3\) and \(m\ge4\). Given candidates \(C\), supported types \(t=(A_t,w_t)\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a unique incumbent winner \(d\), and rational per-capita budgets \(f,b\in[0,1]\), does there exist protected mass \(a_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_t a_t\le f\) such that no rival can be made strictly higher scoring? For each \(i\ne d\), define \(g_{ti}=w_t(1-\mathbf{1}[i\in A_t]+\mathbf{1}[d\in A_t])\). The condition is that for every \(i\ne d\), \(\mathrm{sc}_{\mu}(i)+\max\{\sum_t g_{ti}y_t: 0\le y_t\le\mu_t-a_t,\ \sum_t y_t\le b\}\le\mathrm{sc}_{\mu}(d)\), where \(y_t\) is the mass bribed from type \(t\) and reballoted to approve \(i\) while excluding \(d\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix \(r=3\) and let \(C\) contain \(m\ge4\) candidates. A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types \(t=(A_t,w_t)\) with \(A_t\subseteq C\), \(|A_t|=3\), positive integer weights \(w_t\), and positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Use weighted \(3\)-approval, with \(\mathrm{sc}_{\mu}(c)=\sum_{t:c\in A_t}\mu_tw_t\). The input includes a unique initial winner \(d\) and binary rational budgets \(f,b\in[0,1]\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Protection and bribery each cost one per unit of population mass, independently of weight. The defender chooses real masses \(a_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_ta_t\le f\); protected mass retains its ballot and cannot be bribed. The attacker may split and reballot any remaining mass to arbitrary three-candidate approval sets, preserving weights, with total bribed mass at most \(b\). An attack succeeds only if some rival strictly out-scores \(d\); a tie is safe.
Define \(g_{ti}=w_t(1-\mathbf{1}_{i\in A_t}+\mathbf{1}_{d\in A_t})\). The decision problem asks whether such a protection exists satisfying, for every \(i\ne d\),
\(\mathrm{sc}_{\mu}(i)+\max\{\sum_tg_{ti}y_t: y\in\mathbb{R}^{\tau},\ 0\le y_t\le\mu_t-a_t,\ \sum_ty_t\le b\}\le\mathrm{sc}_{\mu}(d)\).

Is this continuous destructive protection decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

The discrete version with positive integer weights, unit protection and bribery prices, and an unrestricted number of candidates is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete already for fixed \(3\)-approval. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.02533).
Continuous priced bribery for scoring protocols, without a defender, is polynomial-time solvable; this concerns a different problem. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25444v1). No result settling the protection problem defined above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The approval-set domain was unspecified; each initial and replacement ballot approves exactly three distinct candidates.
  • The weight domain was unspecified; weights are positive integers encoded in binary, matching the discrete definition.
  • The cost interpretation was implicit; both actions cost one per unit of population mass, independently of weight.
  • The domains of action variables were implicit; protection and bribery masses are real and freely divisible, and bribery preserves weights.
  • Only approval sets are recorded because ordering within the approved and unapproved candidates does not affect this rule.

1. Status and results

I do not settle the complexity of the unrestricted problem. I establish the following additional tractable cases.

Let
$$ \mathcal D=\{t:d\in A_t\}, \qquad M_D=\sum_{t\in\mathcal D}\mu_t. $$

  • Common winner-ballot weight, with bounded heavier support. Suppose $f\le M_D$ and all types in $\mathcal D$ have the same weight $v$. Let

$$ h=\bigl|\{t\notin\mathcal D:w_t>v\}\bigr|. $$
For every fixed $h$, the problem is polynomial-time solvable, with no restriction on $m$, $\tau$, or the number of distinct weights below $v$. An explicit running-time bound is given in Section 5.

  • No heavier types. Under the preceding hypotheses with $h=0$, one rational linear program suffices.
  • Common weight outside winner ballots. Suppose $f\ge M_D$, every type outside $\mathcal D$ has the same weight $v$, and every type in $\mathcal D$ has weight at least $v$. Again, one rational linear program suffices.

Consequently, equal-weight instances are polynomial-time solvable for all budgets, including the regime $b<1-f$ left open in the notes.

These results do not transfer the discrete hardness theorem to divisible masses. The cited [discrete protection paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.02533) concerns a different feasible set of protection choices.

2. Exact finite representation of attack values

Write
$$ \Delta_i=\mathrm{sc}_{\mu}(d)-\mathrm{sc}_{\mu}(i)>0 $$
for each rival $i$. For protection $a$, define
$$ u_t=\mu_t-a_t, \qquad F_i(a)= \max\left\{ \sum_tg_{ti}y_t: 0\le y_t\le u_t,\ \sum_ty_t\le b \right\}. $$

Before bribery, a unit of type $t$ contributes
$$ w_t\bigl(\mathbf 1_{i\in A_t}-\mathbf 1_{d\in A_t}\bigr) $$
to the score difference between $i$ and $d$. A replacement ballot can contribute $w_t$: because $m\ge4$, it can approve $i$ and exclude $d$. Thus its maximum improvement is exactly
$$ g_{ti}=w_t\bigl(1-\mathbf 1_{i\in A_t}+\mathbf 1_{d\in A_t}\bigr). $$

In particular, all gains are nonnegative.

For $x\in\mathbb R$, let $x_+=\max(x,0)$, and put
$$ \Lambda_i=\{0\}\cup\{g_{ti}:1\le t\le\tau\}. $$

Lemma 1. For every admissible protection,
$$ F_i(a)= \min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_i} \left[ b\lambda+\sum_t(g_{ti}-\lambda)_+(\mu_t-a_t) \right]. $$

Proof. For any $\lambda\ge0$ and feasible attack,
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_tg_{ti}y_t &=\lambda\sum_ty_t+\sum_t(g_{ti}-\lambda)y_t\\ &\le b\lambda+\sum_t(g_{ti}-\lambda)_+u_t. \end{aligned} $$

If the available mass having positive gain totals at most $b$, bribing all that mass attains the bound for $\lambda=0$.

Otherwise, for $b>0$, take available mass in decreasing order of gain until exactly $b$ mass has been selected. Let $\lambda$ be the gain at the cutoff. All mass with larger gain is selected, all mass with smaller gain is omitted, and cutoff-gain mass fills the remaining budget. The displayed bound is then attained, and $\lambda\in\Lambda_i$.

For $b=0$, choosing the largest gain gives bound zero, which is the attack value. This covers every case. $\square$

Define
$$ T_{i\lambda} = b\lambda+\sum_t(g_{ti}-\lambda)_+\mu_t-\Delta_i. $$

Lemma 1 shows that safety against $i$ is equivalent to the existence of $\lambda\in\Lambda_i$ satisfying
$$ \sum_t(g_{ti}-\lambda)_+a_t\ge T_{i\lambda}. $$

All inequalities are weak, preserving the convention that a tie is safe.

For completeness, this also proves the general $\mathrm{NP}$ upper bound. A certificate specifies a threshold for each rival; a deterministic verifier checks the resulting rational linear system in polynomial time.

One may retain only
$$ k\le\min(m-1,3\tau) $$
rival constraints. Indeed, if $S=\bigcup_tA_t$, then $|S|\le3\tau$, and all candidates outside $S$ have score zero and identical gain vectors. Also $d\in S$, since positive masses and weights give some candidate positive score. Thus only one representative outside $S$ is needed.

Enumerating thresholds consequently gives the exact general bound
$$ (\tau+1)^k\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

Every linear system used here has $\tau$ variables and polynomially many rational coefficients of polynomial bit length. Therefore the polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming applies.

3. A protection exchange lemma

The following observation supplies the tractable structure.

Lemma 2. Suppose two types $p,q$ satisfy
$$ g_{pi}\ge g_{qi} \qquad\text{for every rival }i. $$
If $a_q\ge\varepsilon$ and $a_p+\varepsilon\le\mu_p$, replacing
$$ a_p\ \text{by}\ a_p+\varepsilon, \qquad a_q\ \text{by}\ a_q-\varepsilon $$
cannot increase any attack value.

Proof. The new residual capacities are
$$ u'_p=u_p-\varepsilon, \qquad u'_q=u_q+\varepsilon. $$

Fix a rival and any attack $y'$ feasible under the new capacities. Let
$$ \eta=\max(0,y'_q-u_q). $$
Then $0\le\eta\le\varepsilon$. Form an attack under the old capacities by moving $\eta$ bribed mass from $q$ to $p$:
$$ y_p=y'_p+\eta, \qquad y_q=y'_q-\eta, $$
with other coordinates unchanged.

We have
$$ y_p\le u_p-\varepsilon+\eta\le u_p, \qquad y_q\le u_q. $$
The total bribed mass is unchanged. Its gain changes by
$$ \eta(g_{pi}-g_{qi})\ge0. $$
Thus every new attack has gain at most that of some old attack. Taking maxima proves the claim. $\square$

If $p$ approves $d$ and $q$ does not, then
$$ g_{pi}\ge w_p, \qquad g_{qi}\le w_q $$
for every rival. Hence $w_p\ge w_q$ is sufficient for Lemma 2.

We will also use the following budget normalization. Any safe protection can be extended coordinatewise to have total mass exactly $f$: if its current total is $s<f$, the unused protection capacity totals $1-s\ge f-s$. Such an extension preserves safety because it shrinks the attacker’s feasible set.

4. Reduction to a common-weight protection pool

Assume throughout this section that
$$ f\le M_D, \qquad w_t=v\quad\text{for every }t\in\mathcal D. $$

Partition the remaining types into
$$ E=\{t\notin\mathcal D:w_t>v\}, \qquad B=\{t\notin\mathcal D:w_t\le v\}. $$
Thus $|E|=h$.

Lemma 3. A safe protection exists if and only if one exists satisfying
$$ \sum_ta_t=f, \qquad a_t=0\quad(t\in B). $$

Proof. Normalize the total protection to $f$. If some type in $B$ remains protected, then some type in $\mathcal D$ is not fully protected. Otherwise the protection total would exceed $M_D\ge f$.

Move protection from that type in $B$ to the unfilled type in $\mathcal D$. Lemma 2 applies because their weights satisfy $v\ge w_t$. Choose the amount moved to empty the former protection coordinate or fill the latter coordinate.

Repeating terminates after finitely many steps: each step permanently empties one coordinate in $B$ or fills one coordinate in $\mathcal D$. Safety and total protection are preserved. The converse is immediate. $\square$

We now express all remaining disjunctions using only the $h$ protection coordinates on $E$.

Write
$$ z_t=a_t\quad(t\in E), \qquad x_i=\sum_{\substack{t\in\mathcal D\\i\in A_t}}a_t. $$
The total protection on $\mathcal D$ is
$$ \sum_{t\in\mathcal D}a_t=f-\sum_{t\in E}z_t. $$

For a threshold $\lambda$, define
$$ \alpha_\lambda=(2v-\lambda)_+, \qquad \beta_\lambda=(v-\lambda)_+, \qquad c_\lambda=\alpha_\lambda-\beta_\lambda. $$

A type in $\mathcal D$ has gain $v$ if it approves $i$, and gain $2v$ otherwise. Therefore
$$ \sum_t(g_{ti}-\lambda)_+a_t = \alpha_\lambda f-c_\lambda x_i +\sum_{t\in E} \bigl((g_{ti}-\lambda)_+-\alpha_\lambda\bigr)z_t. $$

Put
$$ B_{i\lambda}(z) = \alpha_\lambda f +\sum_{t\in E} \bigl((g_{ti}-\lambda)_+-\alpha_\lambda\bigr)z_t -T_{i\lambda}. $$

The threshold safety inequality is exactly
$$ c_\lambda x_i\le B_{i\lambda}(z). $$

If $\lambda<2v$, then $c_\lambda>0$. Define the affine function
$$ \phi_{i\lambda}(z)=\frac{B_{i\lambda}(z)}{c_\lambda}. $$
If $\lambda\ge2v$, then $c_\lambda=0$; write
$$ \psi_{i\lambda}(z)=B_{i\lambda}(z). $$

Consequently, safety against $i$ is equivalent to
$$ \left[ \text{some }\psi_{i\lambda}(z)\ge0 \right] \quad\text{or}\quad \left[ x_i\le \max_{\substack{\lambda\in\Lambda_i\\\lambda<2v}} \phi_{i\lambda}(z) \right]. $$

The maximum is nonempty: $\lambda=0$ belongs to $\Lambda_i$ and has $c_0=v>0$.

No heavier types: one linear program

When $h=0$, all the functions above are constants.

For each rival, if some $\psi_{i\lambda}\ge0$, its constraint is automatic. Otherwise define
$$ K_i= \max_{\substack{\lambda\in\Lambda_i\\\lambda<2v}} \phi_{i\lambda}. $$
Its complete safety condition is simply
$$ x_i\le K_i. $$

Thus one solves the rational linear system
$$ 0\le a_t\le\mu_t\quad(t\in\mathcal D), \qquad \sum_{t\in\mathcal D}a_t=f, $$
together with the nonautomatic inequalities
$$ \sum_{\substack{t\in\mathcal D\\i\in A_t}}a_t\le K_i. $$

Computing all thresholds and constants uses polynomial time; direct computation requires $O(k\tau^2)$ rational arithmetic operations. The linear program has at most $\tau$ variables and $O(\tau+k)$ constraints, with polynomial encoding length.

This proves polynomial-time solvability when the winner-ballot weight is common, no other type is heavier, and $f\le M_D$.

5. Polynomial time for a fixed number of heavier types

The affine formulation also gives an exact algorithm when $h$ is fixed.

Theorem 4. Under the hypotheses of Section 4, let
$$ N_0=\frac{k(\tau+1)(\tau+2)}2. $$
The decision problem is solvable in time
$$ (2N_0+1)^{h+1}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$
In particular, it is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed $h$.

Proof. In $\mathbb R^h$, form the following affine hyperplanes:

  • $\psi_{i\lambda}(z)=0$ for every zero-coefficient threshold;
  • $\phi_{i\lambda}(z)-\phi_{i\lambda'}(z)=0$ for every pair of positive-coefficient thresholds belonging to the same rival.

Constant functions need no hyperplane: their signs or ordering are already fixed.

Each rival has at most $\tau+1$ thresholds. Therefore the number $N$ of hyperplanes satisfies
$$ N\le k\left((\tau+1)+\binom{\tau+1}{2}\right) =N_0. $$

A face of this arrangement is a nonempty set specified by assigning each hyperplane’s defining affine function one of the signs negative, zero, or positive. These faces include all dimensions, so boundary points are included.

On each face:

  • every $\psi_{i\lambda}$ has a fixed sign;
  • the ordering of the $\phi_{i\lambda}$ for each rival is fixed.

Thus, for each rival, either a zero-coefficient threshold is valid throughout the face, or one fixed positive-coefficient threshold realizes the maximum throughout the face.

Choose those thresholds and solve one rational linear program. Its variables are $a_t$ for $t\in\mathcal D$ and $z_t$ for $t\in E$. Its constraints are
$$ 0\le a_t\le\mu_t, \qquad 0\le z_t\le\mu_t, \qquad \sum_{t\in\mathcal D}a_t+\sum_{t\in E}z_t=f, $$
the selected threshold safety inequalities, and membership of $z$ in the closure of the face. The last condition is encoded by replacing strict sign inequalities with weak ones and retaining zero equalities.

This use of closures is sound: every selected safety inequality is itself a sufficient threshold witness, including at boundary points. It is complete because any safe normalized protection has a vector $z$ lying in some face. On that face, the selected automatic threshold or selected maximizing threshold certifies its safety.

It remains to justify enumeration and its running time.

An arrangement of $N$ affine hyperplanes in $\mathbb R^h$ has at most
$$ \sum_{j=0}^h2^j\binom Nj \le(2N+1)^h $$
nonempty faces, counting all dimensions. Here is a proof of the bound. When a hyperplane is inserted, each old face that it crosses splits into two side faces and one intersection face, increasing the count by two. The crossed faces inject into the faces of the arrangement induced on the inserted hyperplane, whose dimension is $h-1$. If $A(N,h)$ denotes the maximum face count, this gives
$$ A(N,h)\le A(N-1,h)+2A(N-1,h-1). $$
Together with $A(0,h)=A(N,0)=1$, induction yields the displayed binomial sum.

Faces can be enumerated incrementally using rational linear programming. For each proposed sign pattern, impose its zero equalities and replace every strict condition
$$ s_j\ell_j(z)>0 $$
by
$$ s_j\ell_j(z)\ge\varepsilon, \qquad 0\le\varepsilon\le1. $$
Maximize $\varepsilon$. The sign pattern is nonempty exactly when the optimum is positive. Indeed, any point satisfying the finitely many strict inequalities has a positive minimum slack.

At each insertion there are at most $(2N+1)^h$ existing faces and three proposed extensions per face. Hence enumeration requires
$$ O\bigl((N+1)(2N+1)^h\bigr) $$
linear-programming checks. There is also one protection linear program per final face.

All affine coefficients and all linear systems have polynomial bit length: they are formed from input rationals, integer gains, sums over at most $\tau$ terms, and division by positive integer $c_\lambda$. Exact rational linear programming therefore gives the claimed bound. $\square$

This is a polynomial-time result for fixed $h$. The displayed bound does not establish fixed-parameter tractability in $h$, because the exponent of the input-dependent factor grows with $h$.

6. A complementary regime and explicit equal-weight formulas

Common weight outside winner ballots

Theorem 5. Suppose
$$ f\ge M_D, $$
every type outside $\mathcal D$ has weight $v$, and every type in $\mathcal D$ has weight at least $v$. Then the decision problem is polynomial-time solvable.

Proof. Normalize protection to total mass $f$. Every type in $\mathcal D$ dominates every type outside $\mathcal D$ in the sense of Lemma 2.

If a winner-ballot type is not fully protected, some protection lies outside $\mathcal D$: otherwise the total protection would be strictly below $M_D\le f$. Repeated exchanges therefore produce a safe protection satisfying
$$ a_t=\mu_t\quad(t\in\mathcal D), \qquad \sum_{t\notin\mathcal D}a_t=f-M_D. $$

Only types outside $\mathcal D$ remain available for bribery. Their total residual mass is
$$ R=1-f. $$
For each rival define
$$ N_i=\sum_{\substack{t\notin\mathcal D\\i\in A_t}}\mu_t, \qquad q_i=\sum_{\substack{t\notin\mathcal D\\i\in A_t}}a_t. $$
The residual mass approving $i$ is $N_i-q_i$. Such mass has gain zero; all other residual mass has gain $v$. Consequently,
$$ F_i(a)=v\min\bigl(b,R-N_i+q_i\bigr). $$

If $\Delta_i\ge vb$, the rival is automatically safe. Otherwise safety is equivalent to
$$ q_i\le\frac{\Delta_i}{v}-R+N_i. $$

These inequalities, the coordinate bounds, and the required total protection outside $\mathcal D$ form one rational linear program of polynomial encoding length. $\square$

Equal weights for arbitrary budgets

Suppose every weight equals $v$. Divide scores and attack gains by $v$, and define
$$ D=\sum_{t:d\in A_t}\mu_t, \qquad I_i=\sum_{t:i\in A_t}\mu_t, $$
$$ H_i=\sum_{t:d,i\in A_t}\mu_t, \qquad N_i=I_i-H_i, \qquad \delta_i=D-I_i. $$

Here $D=M_D$ and $\Delta_i=v\delta_i$.

For $f\le D$, protection may be restricted to winner ballots. Let
$$ p_i=\sum_{t:d,i\in A_t}a_t. $$
The residual mass with gain $2$ against $i$ is
$$ D-H_i-f+p_i. $$
The total residual gain is
$$ 1+\delta_i-2f+p_i. $$
Lemma 1, using thresholds $0,1,2$ after normalization, therefore gives
$$ \frac{F_i(a)}v = \min\left( 2b,\ b+D-H_i-f+p_i,\ 1+\delta_i-2f+p_i \right). $$

If $\delta_i\ge2b$, safety is automatic. Otherwise the two remaining threshold conditions are
$$ p_i\le f-b-N_i \quad\text{or}\quad p_i\le2f-1. $$
Because they bound the same scalar, their union is exactly
$$ p_i\le\max(f-b-N_i,\,2f-1). $$

Hence the complete linear program in this regime is
$$ 0\le a_t\le\mu_t\quad(t\in\mathcal D), \qquad \sum_{t\in\mathcal D}a_t=f, $$
with
$$ \sum_{\substack{t\in\mathcal D\\i\in A_t}}a_t \le\max(f-b-N_i,\,2f-1) $$
for every rival with $\delta_i<2b$.

For $f\ge D$, protect all winner ballots and use variables only outside $\mathcal D$. Theorem 5 gives automatic safety when $\delta_i\ge b$. For every other rival, the required bound becomes
$$ q_i\le\delta_i-(1-f)+N_i =f+D-H_i-1. $$
Together with
$$ 0\le a_t\le\mu_t\quad(t\notin\mathcal D), \qquad \sum_{t\notin\mathcal D}a_t=f-D, $$
these are again linear constraints.

Thus equal-weight instances are decided in
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
time for every $f,b\in[0,1]$. Both descriptions retain weak inequalities, including at their boundary cases.

7. Remaining open question

The unrestricted problem remains in $\mathrm{NP}$ by Section 2, but no polynomial algorithm or hardness reduction is proved here for arbitrary weight patterns.

The new algorithms cover unrestricted numbers of candidates and support types in several regimes excluded by neither budget inequality. Their central requirement is that protection can be concentrated on a common-weight pool, with only a fixed number of exceptional coordinates.

For arbitrary weights, that exchange structure need not hold, and the affine arrangement may have dimension growing with $\tau$. The resulting enumeration bound then ceases to be polynomial. Resolving that remaining simultaneous threshold selection problem is still necessary for the requested unrestricted classification.
