The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite road graph \(G=(V,E)\), a time horizon, a finite support \(\Theta\) of rider types \(\theta=(o,d,\gamma)\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), and a fleet with initial locations and capacities measured in normalized mass units, choose feasible vehicle routes and type-level assignments \(x_{\theta p}\ge 0\) to feasible travel plans \(p\), with \(\sum_p x_{\theta p}=\mu_\theta\). Each unit of mass must receive an integral travel plan, assignments must match vehicle routes and respect capacity at every time, and taxi plans have the paper’s taxi cost. What is the complexity of finding a feasible solution minimizing \(\sum_{\theta,p}x_{\theta p}c_\theta(p)+\beta\sum_k T^c_k\), under a stated normalization of fleet supply and route costs?

All riders are ready at time zero. For every supported type, \(o,d\in V\), \(\gamma\in[0,\gamma_{\max}]\) is rational, and the shortest-path distance \(D_\theta\) from \(o\) to \(d\) is finite and at most \(H\). The rational inputs satisfy \(\gamma_{\max}\geq0\), \(\alpha\geq0\), and \(\beta>0\).
An integral rider plan starts at \(o\) and ends at \(d\) by \(H\). It either takes an unlimited-supply taxi immediately along a shortest path, or waits and traverses every moving edge aboard exactly one fleet vehicle following that same edge at that time. Transfers at a common vertex are instantaneous; taxi and fleet travel cannot be combined.
Types may split among whole plans: \(x_{\theta p}\geq0\) and \(\sum_p x_{\theta p}=\mu_\theta\). At every time, the total mass aboard each vehicle is at most \(q\); vehicle routes themselves cannot split.

1. Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds already when \( K=1,\quad q=1,\quad \beta=1,\quad \gamma_{\max}=0 \), and every supported type has the same destination, equal to the vehicle’s initial vertex.

For fixed vehicle routes, the continuous rider allocation can be computed in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,H,L)\) time. Enumerating vehicle routes gives an exact deterministic algorithm with running time
\[ (m+1)^{KH}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,H,L). \]
Thus a polynomial-time algorithm for unrestricted inputs would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

2. Hardness reduction

We reduce from directed Hamiltonian cycle, whose \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness is established in [Karp’s original paper](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

We may restrict the source problem to strongly connected, loopless directed graphs with at least two vertices. Indeed, a graph admitting a Hamiltonian cycle is strongly connected. A graph that is not strongly connected can therefore be replaced by a fixed strongly connected no-instance: the three-vertex path with both orientations of each edge. Removing loops does not affect Hamiltonian cycles on at least two vertices. The finitely many smaller instances can be decided directly.

Let \( F=(W,J) \) be such a source instance, with \( n=|W|\geq2 \). Choose a vertex \( w\in W \), and enumerate the remaining vertices as \( v_1,\ldots,v_\tau \), where \( \tau=n-1 \).

Use \( G=F \) as the road graph. The single vehicle starts at \( w \). Set
\[ H=n,\qquad K=1,\qquad q=1,\qquad \beta=1,\qquad \gamma_{\max}=0,\qquad B=n. \]

There is one supported type \( \theta_i=(v_i,w,0) \) for each \( i\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\} \).

To ensure that the masses are dyadic rationals, let \( r=2^{\lceil\log_2\tau\rceil} \) and define
\[ \mu_{\theta_i}= \begin{cases} 1/r,&i<\tau,\\ 1-(\tau-1)/r,&i=\tau. \end{cases} \]
These masses sum to \( 1 \), are positive, and satisfy \( \mu_{\theta_i}\geq1/r \). Set
\[ \alpha=r(n+1). \]

Strong connectivity guarantees that every type has finite shortest-path distance, with \( 1\leq D_{\theta_i}\leq n-1<H \). Hence the constructed instance satisfies all input promises.

The construction has polynomial size. Specifically, \( r<2\tau \), \( \alpha=O(n^2) \), the unary horizon has length \( n \), and every mass has \( O(\log n) \) bits.

3. Correctness of the reduction

A Hamiltonian cycle gives an allocation of cost \(\boldsymbol{n}\).

Suppose the source graph has a Hamiltonian cycle
\[ w,u_1,\ldots,u_{n-1},w. \]
The vehicle follows this cycle, picking up the entire mass originating at each visited vertex and delivering everyone to \( w \) at time \( n \).

The onboard mass never exceeds the total population mass \( 1=q \). Every rider has zero time value, and no rider uses a taxi. The vehicle traverses exactly \( n \) moving edges, so \( C=\beta n=n=B \).

An allocation of cost at most \(\boldsymbol{n}\) gives a Hamiltonian cycle.

Suppose a feasible allocation satisfies \( C\leq n \). Sending the entire mass of any type \( \theta_i \) by taxi would alone cost
\[ \mu_{\theta_i}(\alpha+1)D_{\theta_i} \geq \frac{\alpha+1}{r} =n+1+\frac1r >n. \]
All objective terms are nonnegative. Therefore, every type must receive a strictly positive amount of fleet service.

For each \( i \), positive fleet service implies that the vehicle visits \( v_i \) and subsequently visits \( w \). To justify this implication, choose any positive-mass fleet plan of that type. Its first moving edge departs from \( v_i \) aboard the single vehicle. Its first arrival at \( w \) also occurs aboard that vehicle on a moving edge, since \( v_i\neq w \) and waiting cannot change location.

Let \( t_* \) be the vehicle’s last visit to \( w \) within the horizon. The route prefix ending at \( t_* \) starts and ends at \( w \) and visits every other vertex.

Delete all waiting steps from this prefix. The resulting closed walk has vertex sequence \( z_0,\ldots,z_s \), where \( z_0=z_s=w \). Its \( s-1 \) interior positions must contain all \( n-1 \) other vertices. Consequently,
\[ s-1\geq n-1,\qquad\text{so}\qquad s\geq n. \]
On the other hand,
\[ s\leq t_*\leq H=n. \]
Thus \( s=n \). Its \( n-1 \) interior positions contain each vertex other than \( w \) exactly once. The walk is therefore a directed Hamiltonian cycle.

This argument permits arbitrary mass splitting: even a very small positive fleet-served fraction forces the necessary origin visit.

The reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for the stated restricted instances.

4. Fixed vehicle routes admit a polynomial-size linear program

Fix integral vehicle routes \( s_{k,0},\ldots,s_{k,H} \). Write each type as \( \theta=(o_\theta,d_\theta,\gamma_\theta) \).

For each type, construct a time-expanded directed network with nodes \( (v,t) \), for \( v\in V \) and \( 0\leq t\leq H \), and a terminal sink. Include:

  • Waiting arcs \( (v,t)\to(v,t+1) \).
  • For every moving vehicle slot \( (k,t) \), an arc labelled by that vehicle from \( (s_{k,t},t) \) to \( (s_{k,t+1},t+1) \).
  • Arrival arcs from \( (d_\theta,t) \) to the terminal sink.

Delete waiting and moving arcs leaving destination nodes. Thus fleet flow terminates upon first arrival at its destination.

Let \( y_\theta\in[0,\mu_\theta] \) denote taxi mass. Require a nonnegative flow of value \( \mu_\theta-y_\theta \) from \( (o_\theta,0) \) to the terminal sink, with flow conservation at every other node.

For every moving vehicle slot, impose the shared capacity constraint
\[ \sum_\theta f_{\theta,k,t}\leq q. \]
Here \( f_{\theta,k,t}=0 \) when that vehicle arc is absent from the type’s network.

Let \( z_{\theta,t} \) be flow on the arrival arc at time \( t \), and let \( M \) be the total number of moving vehicle slots. Impose
\[ \sum_\theta (\alpha+\beta+\gamma_\theta)D_\theta y_\theta +\sum_\theta\sum_{t=0}^{H}\gamma_\theta t\,z_{\theta,t} +\beta M \leq B. \]

Exactness of the formulation.

Every feasible allocation induces these flows: terminate each fleet plan at first arrival, and represent all waiting as waiting at the vertex. Removing onboard waiting cannot increase any vehicle’s load.

Conversely, each type’s time-expanded network is acyclic. Its nonnegative flow decomposes into weighted source-to-sink paths: repeatedly subtract the smallest positive flow along a positive-flow source-to-sink path; each subtraction eliminates an arc, and acyclicity excludes residual circulations. Each path specifies a whole fleet plan, including the vehicle used on every moving edge. Transfers are valid because consecutive arcs meet at the same vertex and time.

The capacity constraints bound onboard mass during each moving step. During waiting steps, everyone may wait outside the vehicle. At integer times, riders may alight and then board instantaneously, consistently with the transfer rule. Arrival-arc costs equal the specified first-arrival costs. Taxi mass uses only the taxi option.

Hence the linear program is feasible exactly when the fixed routes admit an allocation with \( C\leq B \).

The program has \( O\bigl(\tau(H+1)(m+K)\bigr) \) variables and constraints. Its rational coefficients have bit length \( O(L+\log(KH+1)) \). The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear-program feasibility therefore applies, giving running time \( \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,H,L) \).

5. Membership and running times

A certificate consists solely of the vehicle routes. Its length is \( O(K(H+1)\log(m+1)) \) bits. Because vehicles are individually listed and \( H \) is unary encoded, this length is polynomial in the input length.

A deterministic verifier checks the route edges and initial vertices, then decides feasibility of the preceding rational linear program in polynomial time. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\). Combined with the reduction, the decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

For an explicit exact algorithm, each vehicle has at most \( m+1 \) choices per time step, including waiting. Enumerating all route profiles and solving their linear programs takes
\[ (m+1)^{KH}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,H,L) \]
time. Shortest-path distances can be precomputed in \( O(\tau m) \) graph operations.
