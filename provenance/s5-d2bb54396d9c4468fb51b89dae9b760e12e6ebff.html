The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over a finite support \(T\) of buyer types, per-capita supplies \(q_j>0\), and each type’s budget \(M_t\), global cap \(c_t\), and values \(u_{tj}\), compute a Fisher equilibrium \((p,(x_t)_{t\in T})\) maximizing \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\min(c_t,\sum_j u_{tj}x_{tj})\). Require \(p_j\ge 0\), type-level demand \(x_t\in\arg\max_{z\ge 0}\{\min(c_t,\sum_j u_{tj}z_j):\sum_jp_jz_j\le M_t\}\), aggregate feasibility \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}\le q_j\), and Walras’ law \(p_j(q_j-\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj})=0\) for every good \(j\).

There are \(m\ge1\) divisible goods with per-capita supplies \(q_j>0\). Each type has a per-buyer budget \(M_t>0\), a global utility cap \(c_t>0\), and values \(u_{tj}\ge0\); all input numbers are binary rationals. Let \(L\) be their total encoding length and \(U_t(z)=\min\{c_t,\sum_j u_{tj}z_j\}\).
An allocation assigns the same bundle \(x_t\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\) to every buyer of type \(t\). An equilibrium consists of \(p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\) and these bundles satisfying
\(x_t\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m,\;\sum_jp_jz_j\le M_t}U_t(z)\), \(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}\le q_j\), and \(p_j(q_j-\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj})=0\) for every \(t\) and \(j\), as applicable.
Demand has no supply bounds; any maximizing bundle is allowed, including bundles with \(\sum_j u_{tj}x_{tj}>c_t\) and bundles leaving budget unspent.

Is computing an equilibrium maximizing \(\sum_t\mu_tU_t(x_t)\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many buyers with unit supplies and these utility functions, computing a social-welfare-maximizing equilibrium is NP-hard; an equilibrium with a Pareto-optimal allocation can nevertheless be computed in polynomial time. Classification

Computing an equilibrium maximizing \(\sum_t\mu_tU_t(x_t)\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The hardness holds even when every supply is \(q_j=1\) and every mass is dyadic. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof is a polynomial-time reduction that preserves equilibrium conditions and welfare exactly.

1. The finite-buyer hardness theorem

We use Theorem 19 of [Bei, Garg, Hoefer and Mehlhorn](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol057-esa2016/LIPIcs.ESA.2016.8/LIPIcs.ESA.2016.8.pdf): computing a social-welfare-maximizing equilibrium is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for finitely many buyers with unit supplies and utilities
\[ \]
\(V_i(y)=\min\{C_i,\sum_jv_{ij}y_j\}\),
where \(B_i>0\) is buyer \(i\)'s budget, \(C_i>0\), and \(v_{ij}\geq0\).

The paper’s definition on page 8:2 matches the demand convention here: buyers optimize over all nonnegative affordable bundles, without supply bounds; linear utility exceeding the cap is expressly allowed. Its equilibrium conditions are feasibility and complementary market clearing. Thus, the source theorem concerns the required unrestricted notion of equilibrium.

For completeness, this hardness also holds with integer numerical parameters. Starting with rational parameters, choose positive integers \(D,E\) clearing, respectively, all denominators of the values and caps, and all denominators of the budgets. Replace
\(v_{ij},C_i,B_i\) by \(Dv_{ij},DC_i,EB_i\).

Under the price transformation \(P\mapsto EP\), affordable bundles are unchanged, while every utility and the welfare objective are multiplied by \(D\). Hence equilibria and welfare maximizers correspond. Products of the input denominators have polynomial binary encoding length, so this transformation is polynomial-time.

2. Constructing normalized masses

Take an integer source instance with buyers indexed by \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\).

If \(n=1\), set \(\mu_1=1\). Otherwise set

\(\displaystyle \mu_i=2^{-i}\quad(1\leq i<n),\qquad \mu_n=2^{-(n-1)}.\)

These masses are positive, and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_i=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}2^{-i}+2^{-(n-1)}=1.\)

Initially create one type per source buyer, with parameters

\(\displaystyle q_j=1,\qquad M_i=\frac{B_i}{\mu_i},\qquad c_i=\frac{C_i}{\mu_i},\qquad u_{ij}=v_{ij}.\)

Every reciprocal \(1/\mu_i\) is an integer power of two. Thus all budgets, caps, values, and supplies are integers, while the masses have finite binary expansions. The construction therefore satisfies even the restrictive interpretation of “binary rational.”

For every nonnegative bundle \(z\), the constructed utility satisfies the identity

\(\displaystyle U_i(z)=\min\left\{\frac{C_i}{\mu_i},\sum_jv_{ij}z_j\right\}=\frac{1}{\mu_i}V_i(\mu_i z).\)

3. Equilibrium correspondence

Keep prices unchanged: \(p=P\). Relate allocations by

\(\displaystyle y_i=\mu_i x_i.\)

We check every equilibrium condition in both directions.

Individual optimality

The map \(z\mapsto w=\mu_i z\) is a bijection between the two nonnegative bundle spaces. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \sum_jp_jz_j\leq M_i \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_jP_jw_j\leq B_i.\)

Under this bijection, \(U_i(z)=V_i(w)/\mu_i\). Multiplication by the positive constant \(1/\mu_i\) preserves maximizers. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle x_i\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{z\geq0,\;p\cdot z\leq M_i}U_i(z) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y_i\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{w\geq0,\;P\cdot w\leq B_i}V_i(w).\)

This argument includes zero prices, unspent budgets, and bundles whose linear utility exceeds the cap.

Supply feasibility and clearing

For each good \(j\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_i\mu_i x_{ij}=\sum_i y_{ij}.\)

Since both instances have unit supplies, their feasibility inequalities coincide. Their complementary clearing conditions also coincide:

\(\displaystyle p_j\left(1-\sum_i\mu_i x_{ij}\right) =P_j\left(1-\sum_i y_{ij}\right).\)

Thus the allocation transformation gives a bijection between source equilibria and equilibria of the constructed list of types.

Welfare

The utility identity gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_i\mu_iU_i(x_i)=\sum_iV_i(\mu_i x_i)=\sum_iV_i(y_i).\)

Therefore the correspondence preserves welfare exactly, and it preserves welfare-maximizing equilibria.

4. Ensuring a genuine support of distinct types

If a support must contain distinct types, merge any constructed entries having identical budgets, caps, and value vectors.

To justify this operation, consider such a group \(G\), with total mass \(\mu_G=\sum_{i\in G}\mu_i\). At any equilibrium, all its bundles maximize the same utility over the same budget set. Let their common optimal utility be \(h\), and define

\(\displaystyle \bar x_G=\frac{1}{\mu_G}\sum_{i\in G}\mu_i x_i.\)

The budget set is convex, so \(\bar x_G\) is affordable. The utility \(U\), being the minimum of a constant and a linear function, is concave. Hence

\(\displaystyle U(\bar x_G)\geq\frac{1}{\mu_G}\sum_{i\in G}\mu_iU(x_i)=h.\)

Affordability and the definition of \(h\) give the reverse inequality. Thus \(\bar x_G\) is also a maximizing bundle. Merging preserves aggregate consumption and welfare.

Conversely, assign a merged type’s bundle to every constituent entry. This preserves individual optimality, consumption, and welfare. Consequently, merging preserves the attainable equilibrium welfare values, and a merged equilibrium can be expanded before applying \(y_i=\mu_i x_i\).

The resulting support has size \(\tau\leq n\), and its masses remain positive dyadic rationals summing to one.

5. Encoding length and conclusion

Each mass initially needs at most \(n\) binary digits in its denominator. Scaling a budget or cap by \(1/\mu_i\) adds at most \(n\) bits. Thus the construction has polynomial encoding length; identifying and merging identical types is also polynomial-time.

Suppose a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) computed a welfare-maximizing equilibrium for the mass formulation. Apply it to the constructed support, expand any merged types, and return

\(\displaystyle P=p,\qquad y_i=\mu_i x_i.\)

The preceding checks prove that this is a source equilibrium, and exact welfare preservation proves that it maximizes source social welfare. This would solve the cited \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard problem in polynomial time.

Therefore the precise mass formulation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Its normalized masses and explicit support representation do not remove the finite-buyer hardness.
