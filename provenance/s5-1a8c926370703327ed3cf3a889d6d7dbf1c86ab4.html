The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given posts \(P\), supported types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), each type’s complete weak preference order over eligible posts and its own last resort \(\ell_t\), and rational per-capita capacity bounds \(b_p \leq u_p\), does there exist a capacity vector \(q\) and assignment masses \(\alpha_{t,p}\) such that \(b_p \leq q_p \leq u_p\), \(\sum_{p}\alpha_{t,p}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t\alpha_{t,p}\leq q_p\) for every real post \(p\), with last resorts unlimited? Require that no feasible reassignment \(\beta_{t,p,r}\), respecting the same capacities, has positive vote margin \(\sum_{t,p,r}\beta_{t,p,r}\bigl(\mathbf{1}[r\succ_t p]-\mathbf{1}[p\succ_t r]\bigr)>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P\) be a set of \(m\) real posts. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type specifies eligible posts \(E_t\subseteq P\) and a complete weak order on \(S_t=E_t\cup\{\ell_t\}\). Its private last resort \(\ell_t\) is strictly worse than every eligible real post and has unlimited capacity. Only applicants vote; indifference contributes zero.
For every real post, the input gives finite binary-rational bounds \(0<b_p\leq u_p\). Capacities and assignment masses may be real: \(b_p\leq q_p\leq u_p\), \(\alpha_{t,p}\geq0\), \(\sum_{p\in S_t}\alpha_{t,p}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t:p\in E_t}\alpha_{t,p}\leq q_p\). The bounds constrain capacity, not occupancy.
A reassignment consists of real masses \(\beta_{t,p,r}\geq0\), indexed by \(p,r\in S_t\), satisfying \(\sum_{r\in S_t}\beta_{t,p,r}=\alpha_{t,p}\) and \(\sum_{t:r\in E_t}\sum_{p\in S_t}\beta_{t,p,r}\leq q_r\) for each real destination \(r\). Staying put is allowed, and the capacity vector remains fixed.
An assignment is popular if every such reassignment has vote margin \(\sum_t\sum_{p,r\in S_t}\beta_{t,p,r}(\mathbf{1}[r\succ_t p]-\mathbf{1}[p\succ_t r])\leq0\).

Is deciding whether some admissible capacity vector admits a popular assignment solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Known discrete results: in the graph of first-choice edges, call a post even if it is reachable by an even alternating path from an unmatched vertex relative to a maximum matching. Starting from an instance without a popular matching, posts outside every applicant’s first-choice and best-even-post sets can retain their original multiplicities in any successful copy extension. Discrete variable-copy feasibility is also [\(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304397510007759).
[Popular mixed matchings](https://cse.iitm.ac.in/~meghana/papers/TCS11-2.pdf) always exist and are computable in polynomial time, but compare independently sampled matchings by expected votes; they do not impose the arbitrary reassignment comparison defined here. No result settling this precise mass problem was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required positive finite capacity lower bounds, reflecting the discrete requirement that every real post retains at least one copy; normalization permits different positive per-capita baselines.
  • Specified that each last resort is private, strictly worst, and unlimited, and that assignments and reassignments use only eligible destinations.
  • Defined reassignment feasibility by conserving every type-and-origin assignment mass, including moves that stay put, while imposing destination capacity constraints.
  • Allowed nonnegative real assignment and reassignment masses and real capacities; only the input data must be binary rational.
  • Interpreted capacity lower bounds as bounds on available capacity rather than minimum occupancy, with no aggregate copying budget.
  • Did not impose a promise that the baseline capacity vector lacks a popular assignment: that hypothesis belongs to the discrete dominance statement, whereas the graded question asks unrestricted feasibility.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even when all numerical data are dyadic rationals and, for a common positive unit \(\delta\), every post has bounds \(b_p=\delta\) and \(u_p\in\{\delta,2\delta\}\).

The key step is an integrality result: allowing real capacities and assignment masses does not create additional feasible instances when all data are integral multiples of a common unit.

1. A binary witness for popularity

Write
\[ \]
\(v_t(p,r)=\mathbf{1}_{\{r\succ_t p\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{p\succ_t r\}}\),
and let \(x_p=\sum_{t:p\in E_t}\alpha_{t,p}\) denote occupancy.

For a vector indexed by real posts, extend its notation by setting its value at every private last resort to zero.

Lemma 1. An admissible assignment \(\alpha\) is popular at capacities \(q\) if and only if there exists \(h\in\{0,1\}^{P}\) satisfying:

  • Whenever \(\alpha_{t,p}>0\), for every \(r\in S_t\),

\(v_t(p,r)\leq h_r-h_p.\)

  • Whenever \(h_p=1\), \(x_p=q_p\).

Proof: necessity. Fix \((q,\alpha)\). Maximizing the vote margin over reassignments is the linear program

\(\displaystyle \max_{\beta\geq0}\sum_t\sum_{p,r\in S_t}v_t(p,r)\beta_{t,p,r},\)

subject to the source equalities and destination capacity inequalities in the question.

This program is feasible because staying put is allowed. It is bounded because each variable satisfies \(0\leq\beta_{t,p,r}\leq\alpha_{t,p}\). Staying put has objective zero, so popularity is equivalent to its optimal value being zero.

The dual has unrestricted variables \(z_{t,p}\) for the source equalities and nonnegative variables \(y_r\) for real destination capacities:

\(\displaystyle \min \sum_t\sum_{p\in S_t}\alpha_{t,p}z_{t,p}+\sum_{r\in P}q_ry_r,\)

subject to

\(z_{t,p}+y_r\geq v_t(p,r)\qquad(t,\ p,r\in S_t),\)

where \(y_{\ell_t}=0\).

If \(\alpha\) is popular, strong linear-programming duality gives an optimal dual solution with objective zero. The staying constraints give \(z_{t,p}+y_p\geq0\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle 0=\sum_t\sum_{p\in S_t}\alpha_{t,p}(z_{t,p}+y_p)+\sum_{p\in P}y_p(q_p-x_p).\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore,

\(\alpha_{t,p}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ z_{t,p}=-y_p,\)

and

\(y_p>0\ \Longrightarrow\ x_p=q_p.\)

For every occupied real post \(p\), choose a type \(t\) with \(\alpha_{t,p}>0\). The dual constraint for destination \(\ell_t\), together with the strict inferiority of the last resort, gives

\(-y_p=z_{t,p}\geq v_t(p,\ell_t)=-1.\)

Thus \(y_p\leq1\). If \(p\) is unoccupied, then \(q_p>0\), so \(y_p(q_p-x_p)=0\) implies \(y_p=0\). Hence every real post satisfies \(0\leq y_p\leq1\).

On every occupied source \((t,p)\), dual feasibility now reads

\(y_r-y_p\geq v_t(p,r)\qquad(r\in S_t).\)

Define \(h_p=1\) if \(y_p>0\), and \(h_p=0\) otherwise. These inequalities survive this rounding:

  • If \(v_t(p,r)=1\), the bounds \(0\leq y_p,y_r\leq1\) force \(y_p=0\) and \(y_r=1\).
  • If \(v_t(p,r)=0\), then \(y_r\geq y_p\), which implies \(h_r\geq h_p\).
  • If \(v_t(p,r)=-1\), every binary difference satisfies \(h_r-h_p\geq-1\).

Also, \(h_p=1\) implies \(y_p>0\), hence \(x_p=q_p\). This proves necessity.

Proof: sufficiency. Suppose such an \(h\) exists, and let \(\beta\) be any feasible reassignment. A source with zero assigned mass has zero outgoing reassignment mass, so condition 1 yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,p,r}\beta_{t,p,r}v_t(p,r)\leq\sum_{t,p,r}\beta_{t,p,r}(h_r-h_p).\)

Let \(d_p\) be the total reassigned mass whose destination is real post \(p\). Using the source equalities and the zero values at last resorts, the right-hand side equals

\(\displaystyle \sum_{p\in P}h_p(d_p-x_p)\leq\sum_{p\in P}h_p(q_p-x_p)=0.\)

The inequality uses \(d_p\leq q_p\); the equality uses condition 2. Thus every reassignment has nonpositive margin. \(\square\)

2. Fixing the witness leaves a flow problem

For \(H\subseteq P\), set \(h_p=\mathbf{1}_{\{p\in H\}}\), with \(h_{\ell_t}=0\), and define

\(A_t(H)=\{p\in S_t: v_t(p,r)\leq h_r-h_p\text{ for every }r\in S_t\}.\)

These are the origins at which type \(t\) may receive positive assignment mass under this witness.

Consider the following feasibility system:

\(\alpha_{t,p}\geq0,\qquad \alpha_{t,p}=0\text{ for }p\notin A_t(H),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{p\in S_t}\alpha_{t,p}=\mu_t\qquad\text{for every }t,\)

\(\displaystyle b_p\leq\sum_{t:p\in E_t}\alpha_{t,p}\leq u_p\qquad(p\in H),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t:p\in E_t}\alpha_{t,p}\leq u_p\qquad(p\notin H).\)

Lemma 2. Some admissible capacity vector admits a popular assignment if and only if this system is feasible for some \(H\subseteq P\).

Proof. Given a popular assignment, take the witness from Lemma 1 and let \(H=\{p:h_p=1\}\). Its support is contained in \(A_t(H)\). Posts in \(H\) are full, so their occupancies lie between their capacity bounds. The displayed system follows.

Conversely, suppose the system is feasible, and denote its occupancies by \(x_p\). Choose

\(q_p=x_p\quad(p\in H),\qquad q_p=\max\{b_p,x_p\}\quad(p\notin H).\)

These capacities satisfy \(b_p\leq q_p\leq u_p\). Every positive assignment lies in an allowed origin, and every post with \(h_p=1\) is full. Lemma 1 proves popularity. \(\square\)

The system is a circulation feasibility problem. Explicitly, introduce a source \(s\), a sink \(d\), one vertex per type, and destination vertices for all real posts and private last resorts:

  • The arc \(s\to t\) has lower and upper bounds both \(\mu_t\).
  • Include \(t\to p\) exactly when \(p\in A_t(H)\), with bounds zero and one.
  • The arc \(p\to d\) for a real post has bounds \(b_p,u_p\) when \(p\in H\), and \(0,u_p\) otherwise.
  • Every private-last-resort arc to \(d\) has bounds zero and one.
  • The arc \(d\to s\) has lower and upper bounds both one.

Flow conservation identifies the type-to-destination flows with the assignment masses. Thus this network realizes precisely the displayed system.

3. Integrality despite real-valued capacities

Lemma 3. Suppose every mass \(\mu_t\) and every bound \(b_p,u_p\) is an integer multiple of a positive rational number \(\delta\). If some admissible real capacity vector admits a popular real assignment, then there is such a solution in which every capacity and assignment mass is an integer multiple of \(\delta\).

Proof. By Lemma 2, some \(H\) has a feasible circulation of the form above. Divide all flows and arc bounds by \(\delta\).

Every scaled bound is integral. In particular, \(1/\delta\) is integral because

\(\displaystyle 1/\delta=\sum_t\mu_t/\delta.\)

Apply the integral circulation theorem: a finite directed network with integral lower and upper arc bounds has an integral feasible circulation whenever it has a real feasible circulation. Its hypotheses hold here: the network is finite, all scaled bounds are finite integers, and feasibility follows from the given solution. This theorem follows from total unimodularity of the directed incidence matrix, with coordinate bounds appended.

Scaling an integral circulation back gives an assignment \(\alpha'\) whose entries and occupancies \(x'_p\) are multiples of \(\delta\). Define

\(q'_p=x'_p\quad(p\in H),\qquad q'_p=\max\{b_p,x'_p\}\quad(p\notin H).\)

These capacities are also multiples of \(\delta\). Lemma 2 proves admissibility and popularity. \(\square\)

The rounding is existential and need not preserve the original assignment or capacities. It preserves the binary witness \(H\).

4. Reduction from discrete variable-copy feasibility

We use the following known result, in its precise bounded form: deciding whether an instance admits a popular matching for some integer item capacities \(k_p\) satisfying \(1\leq k_p\leq c_p\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even when every \(c_p\in\{1,2\}\). Weak preferences are allowed. This is [Kavitha–Nasre, *Popular matchings with variable item copies*, Theorem 2](https://www.cse.iitm.ac.in/~meghana/papers/TCS11-1.pdf).

Copies of an item have the same preference rank. Therefore matching to copies is equivalent to assigning applicants to that item subject to an integer capacity. Unmatched applicants may equivalently receive their private, strictly inferior last resorts.

Take such a discrete instance with \(n\geq1\) applicants. Let \(D\) be the least power of two strictly larger than \(n\), and set \(\delta=1/D\). Then \(n<D\leq2n\).

Group applicants with identical eligible real posts and identical weak orders on those posts. For a group \(t\) containing \(n_t\) applicants, create a type of mass

\(\mu_t=n_t\delta,\)

with the group’s real preferences and a private last resort strictly below all eligible real posts.

Add one type with no eligible real posts and mass

\(\mu_0=(D-n)\delta>0.\)

Its only option is its private last resort. Finally, set

\(b_p=\delta,\qquad u_p=c_p\delta.\)

All masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one. The support size is at most \(n+1\), and every numerical value has a representation of length polynomial in the discrete input length. Grouping and constructing the instance take polynomial time.

Forward implication

Suppose the discrete instance has a popular matching \(M\) at integer capacities \(k_p\).

Choose \(q_p=k_p\delta\). Assign each type the masses obtained by counting its applicants’ destinations in \(M\) and multiplying those counts by \(\delta\). Interpret unmatched applicants as receiving the type’s last resort. Put the additional type entirely at its last resort.

We must verify popularity against arbitrary real reassignments.

Scale any reassignment by \(1/\delta\). Its origin supplies are integers: they are precisely the numbers of applicants assigned by \(M\) to each type-origin pair. Its real destination capacities are the integers \(k_p\).

The maximum-margin reassignment problem is therefore a transportation flow problem with integral supplies and capacities. Last-resort capacities can be bounded by \(D\), since the total scaled mass is \(D\). The feasible flow polytope is consequently bounded.

The integral optimal-flow theorem applies: a bounded feasible network-flow linear program with integral supplies and arc bounds has an integral optimum for any linear objective. Thus the maximum margin is attained by an integral scaled reassignment.

Such a reassignment can be implemented on the individual discrete applicants. For each type-origin group, partition its applicants according to the integral destination counts. Identical preferences within a type ensure that the resulting discrete vote margin equals the scaled flow margin. A last-resort destination means assigning the applicant to their own private last resort. The extra type stays at its sole option and contributes zero.

Since \(M\) is popular, every resulting discrete margin is nonpositive. Hence the integral optimum, and therefore the optimum over all real reassignments, is nonpositive. The constructed mass assignment is popular.

Reverse implication

Suppose the constructed mass instance admits a popular assignment at some admissible real capacities.

Every mass and capacity bound is an integer multiple of \(\delta\). Lemma 3 therefore gives a popular solution \((q',\alpha')\) with capacities and assignment masses that are multiples of \(\delta\).

Set

\(k_p=q'_p/\delta.\)

These are integers satisfying \(1\leq k_p\leq c_p\).

For each original type, the numbers \(\alpha'_{t,p}/\delta\) are nonnegative integers summing to \(n_t\). Assign its \(n_t\) individual applicants to destinations with exactly these counts, translating type-last-resort assignments into unmatched applicants. This produces a discrete matching \(M'\) respecting capacities \(k_p\).

Any discrete reassignment from \(M'\) induces a feasible mass reassignment by aggregating its transitions within types and multiplying their counts by \(\delta\). Keep the extra type at its sole option. The mass vote margin is exactly \(\delta\) times the discrete vote margin.

Popularity of \(\alpha'\) therefore rules out every positive discrete margin. Thus \(M'\) is popular.

Both implications hold, establishing a polynomial-time many-one reduction and proving \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

5. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate is simply the subset \(H\subseteq P\), encoded with \(m\) bits.

Given \(H\), compute every \(A_t(H)\) by checking its defining inequalities. This requires at most \(O(\tau(m+1)^2)\) preference comparisons. Then test the circulation from Section 2 for feasibility. Rational circulation feasibility is decidable in polynomial time in the network size and the binary encoding length of its bounds.

By Lemma 2, the verifier accepts some certificate exactly when the instance admits an admissible popular assignment. Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Together with the reduction, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.
