The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(p,\alpha,\beta\) and an exact finite rational polyhedral subdivision of \(\Delta_{k-1}\times\Delta_{\ell-1}\), with each cell labeled by a subset of candidates, decide whether the simplex vertices can be bijectively labeled by \(X_\alpha=\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{p}:\sum_a x_a=\alpha\}\) and \(Y_\beta=\{y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{p}:\sum_a y_a=\beta\}\) so that at every \((q,r)\) the supplied label equals \(\arg\max_a(\sum_i q_i x^{(i)}_a+\sum_j r_j y^{(j)}_a)\). If so, output the bijections.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A=\{1,\ldots,p\}\), with \(m=p\ge2\), and let \(\alpha,\beta\) be positive integers encoded in binary. Set \(X_\alpha=\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{p}:\sum_a x_a=\alpha\}\), \(Y_\beta=\{y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{p}:\sum_a y_a=\beta\}\), \(k=\binom{\alpha+p-1}{p-1}\), and \(\ell=\binom{\beta+p-1}{p-1}\).
The input is an explicit finite rational polyhedral subdivision of \(\Delta_{k-1}\times\Delta_{\ell-1}\), including all faces; each cell is specified by rational linear inequalities and equalities and labeled by a nonempty subset of \(A\). A label applies to the relative interior of its cell, so boundary faces have their own labels. Let \(H(q,r)\) denote the resulting function.
Each simplex factor represents a society of total mass \(1\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here types are indexed simplex vertices; write \(\tau_q,\tau_r\) for the two support sizes and \(\tau=\tau_q+\tau_r\).
Recognition asks whether there are bijections \(i\mapsto x^{(i)}\in X_\alpha\) and \(j\mapsto y^{(j)}\in Y_\beta\) such that, for every real \((q,r)\in\Delta_{k-1}\times\Delta_{\ell-1}\), \(H(q,r)=\arg\max_{a\in A}(\sum_{i=1}^{k}q_i x^{(i)}_a+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}r_j y^{(j)}_a)\). All maximizing candidates are included, with no tie-breaking; on acceptance, output the bijections.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length and \(N\) the number of listed cells. State running times in \(m,k,\ell,N,L,\alpha,\beta\), and, for operations on individual societies, their actual support size \(\tau\).

Is this continuous recognition problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

For the discrete version, an explicit \(k\times\ell\) matrix of candidate subsets can be recognized and its row and column bijections recovered in polynomial time, for arbitrary positive \(\alpha,\beta\). [Discrete recognition result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.15730).
No result settling this exact continuous recognition problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified at least two candidates and positive integer powers, with binary encoding, following the intended discrete domain.
  • Fixed the simplex dimensions by the cardinalities of the complete strategy sets.
  • Specified an explicit rational inequality-and-equality encoding of every subdivision cell, including all faces; labels apply to relative interiors so ties on boundaries are representable.
  • Required nonempty candidate labels and retained every maximizer without tie-breaking, following correspondence semantics.
  • Normalized each of the two societies separately to total mass one, as dictated by the product of simplices; required equality at all real points while using binary rational masses for encoded individual societies.
  • Defined total input bit length, cell count, and actual society support sizes separately from the numbers of possible strategy types.

Result

The continuous recognition problem is solvable in polynomial time, with exact rational arithmetic. On acceptance, the algorithm recovers the required bijections. In fact, those bijections are unique.

The proof below does not rely on the discrete recognition algorithm in [Boros–Čepek–Gurvich–Makino, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.15730). Its essential additional information comes from the continuously observed tie regions.

1. Notation and a polyhedral computation

Write \(m=p\), and let
\(P=\Delta_{k-1}\times\Delta_{\ell-1}\).
Its affine hull \(E\) has dimension
\(d=k+\ell-2\).
Let
\(\bar q=(1/k,\ldots,1/k)\) and
\(\bar r=(1/\ell,\ldots,1/\ell)\).

We use the following standard exact linear-programming theorem: feasibility and optimization for rational linear programs are computable in time polynomial in their binary description length. For a nonempty bounded rational polyhedron, an optimal rational point of polynomial encoding length can be returned.

In particular, for every listed cell \(C\), we can compute its affine hull, dimension, and a rational point \(z_C\in\operatorname{relint}C\) in polynomial time. Here is an explicit procedure, which also handles redundant inequalities.

Include the defining constraints of \(P\). For each inequality \(u_t(z)\leq b_t\), maximize its slack \(b_t-u_t(z)\) over \(C\).

  • If the maximum is zero, the inequality is an equality throughout \(C\).
  • Otherwise, retain an optimizing point with positive slack.

Average these retained points together with any feasible point. The resulting point \(z_C\) satisfies every non-universal inequality strictly: its slack is an average of nonnegative slacks, including a positive one. It satisfies all universal equalities.

Let \(E_C\) be the affine space defined by the supplied equalities and the universally tight inequalities. A sufficiently small neighborhood of \(z_C\) within \(E_C\) satisfies all remaining inequalities strictly. Thus
\(\operatorname{aff}C=E_C\) and
\(z_C\in\operatorname{relint}C\).
Gaussian elimination now supplies the dimension and a basis of the affine hull.

The hypotheses for these linear programs hold: each cell is nonempty and is contained in the compact polytope \(P\).

2. Every candidate pair exposes its score-difference hyperplane

For this section, assume that a realizing pair of bijections exists. Define

\(s_a(q,r)=\sum_i q_i x^{(i)}_a+\sum_j r_j y^{(j)}_a\),

and, for distinct candidates \(a,b\), define
\(g_{ab}=s_a-s_b\).

Uniform societies give equal scores

Coordinate permutations act bijectively on \(X_\alpha\), so the average of \(x_a\) over \(X_\alpha\) is independent of \(a\). Since every type has coordinate sum \(\alpha\), this average is \(\alpha/m\). The corresponding average over \(Y_\beta\) is \(\beta/m\). Consequently,

\(s_a(\bar q,\bar r)=(\alpha+\beta)/m\)

for every candidate \(a\).

Score perturbations are attainable in the interior

For each candidate \(c\), let \(i_c\) denote the row assigned the type \(\alpha e_c\). These rows exist because the assignment covers all of \(X_\alpha\).

Given a sufficiently small vector \(h\in\mathbb R^m\) with \(\sum_c h_c=0\), modify \(\bar q\) by adding \(h_c/\alpha\) to coordinate \(i_c\), leaving the other coordinates unchanged. The resulting \(q\) still has coordinate sum one. For sufficiently small \(h\), every coordinate remains positive. With \(r=\bar r\), its score vector is

\(s(q,\bar r)=((\alpha+\beta)/m)\mathbf 1+h\).

For \(m\geq3\), choose

\(h_a=h_b=(m-2)\varepsilon,\qquad h_c=-2\varepsilon\quad(c\notin\{a,b\})\),

where \(\varepsilon>0\) is sufficiently small. The coordinate sum is zero, and precisely \(a,b\) maximize the scores. The resulting point lies in \(\operatorname{relint}P\).

For \(m=2\), the uniform point itself has precisely the two candidates tied.

A listed cell spans the entire equality hyperplane

The function \(g_{ab}\) is nonconstant on \(E\). Indeed, assigning both societies entirely to their types concentrated on \(a\) gives value \(\alpha+\beta\); concentrating both on \(b\) gives value \(-(\alpha+\beta)\).

Hence
\(Z_{ab}=\{z\in E:g_{ab}(z)=0\}\)
is an affine hyperplane of dimension \(d-1\).

At the interior point constructed above, all inequalities against candidates outside \(\{a,b\}\) are strict. They remain strict in a sufficiently small neighborhood. Thus \(Z_{ab}\) contains a nonempty relatively open set \(U\), contained in \(\operatorname{relint}P\), throughout which \(H=\{a,b\}\).

The relative interiors of cells labeled exactly \(\{a,b\}\) cover \(U\). Every such cell lies in \(Z_{ab}\): the score difference vanishes on its relative interior and therefore, by continuity, on its closure.

If all these cells had dimension at most \(d-2\), their finitely many affine hulls could not cover a relatively open subset of the \((d-1)\)-dimensional affine space \(Z_{ab}\). Therefore at least one has dimension \(d-1\).

We have proved:

Hyperplane lemma. For every distinct \(a,b\), a valid input contains a cell of dimension \(d-1\) labeled exactly \(\{a,b\}\). The affine hull of every such cell is \(Z_{ab}\).

Singleton labels orient the hyperplanes

Every singleton label also occurs. Let \(i_a,j_a\) index the types \(\alpha e_a,\beta e_a\), and take

\(q=(1-t)e_{i_a}+t\bar q,\qquad r=(1-t)e_{j_a}+t\bar r\),

with \(0<t<1\). For each \(b\neq a\),

\(s_a(q,r)-s_b(q,r)=(1-t)(\alpha+\beta)>0\).

Thus some listed cell has label \(\{a\}\). At every point of that cell’s relative interior, \(g_{ab}>0\).

3. Recovering the score differences exactly

Fix candidate \(m\) as a reference. Perform the following construction independently for each \(a<m\).

  • Find a cell \(C_{am}\) of dimension \(d-1\) labeled exactly \(\{a,m\}\). If none exists, reject.
  • Compute a nonzero rational affine function \(f_a\) on \(E\) whose zero set is \(\operatorname{aff}C_{am}\).
  • Find a cell \(C_a\) labeled \(\{a\}\). If none exists, reject. Evaluate \(f_a(z_{C_a})\). Reject if it is zero; otherwise change the sign of \(f_a\), if necessary, so that this value is positive.
  • Compute \(M_a=\max_{z\in P}f_a(z)\), and set

\(G_a=((\alpha+\beta)/M_a)f_a\).

The maximum in step 4 is positive because \(f_a(z_{C_a})>0\). It can be computed directly: writing

\(f_a(q,r)=c+\sum_i u_iq_i+\sum_j v_jr_j\)

on \(E\), one has
\(M_a=c+\max_i u_i+\max_j v_j\).

We now justify this construction on every valid input.

By the hyperplane lemma, \(f_a\) and \(g_{am}\) are nonzero affine functions on \(E\) with the same zero hyperplane. Therefore
\(f_a=t_a g_{am}\)
for some nonzero scalar \(t_a\). Their values at \(z_{C_a}\) are both positive, so \(t_a>0\).

Every type satisfies
\(x_a-x_m\leq\alpha\) and
\(y_a-y_m\leq\beta\).
These bounds are simultaneously attained by the types concentrated on \(a\). Therefore

\(\max_{z\in P}g_{am}(z)=\alpha+\beta\).

It follows that \(M_a=t_a(\alpha+\beta)\), and hence

\(G_a=g_{am}\).

Thus each independently recovered hyperplane acquires its exact scale. No compatibility assumption between the initially chosen hyperplane normals is needed.

4. Recovering the two bijections

Evaluate the recovered functions at one pure society and one uniform society:

\(D^X_{ia}=G_a(e_i,\bar r),\qquad D^Y_{ja}=G_a(\bar q,e_j)\quad(a<m)\).

On a valid input, the uniform society contributes equally to all candidates, giving

\(D^X_{ia}=x^{(i)}_a-x^{(i)}_m,\qquad D^Y_{ja}=y^{(j)}_a-y^{(j)}_m\).

The prescribed coordinate sums therefore determine every coordinate:

\(x^{(i)}_m=(\alpha-\sum_{a<m}D^X_{ia})/m,\qquad x^{(i)}_a=x^{(i)}_m+D^X_{ia}\quad(a<m)\),

and

\(y^{(j)}_m=(\beta-\sum_{a<m}D^Y_{ja})/m,\qquad y^{(j)}_a=y^{(j)}_m+D^Y_{ja}\quad(a<m)\).

Compute these vectors exactly. Reject unless:

  • every reconstructed coordinate is a nonnegative integer;
  • every reconstructed row has coordinate sum \(\alpha\), and every reconstructed column has coordinate sum \(\beta\);
  • the reconstructed rows are pairwise distinct, and the reconstructed columns are pairwise distinct.

These checks establish bijectivity without enumerating the type sets. There are \(k=|X_\alpha|\) reconstructed rows belonging to \(X_\alpha\); if they are distinct, they exhaust \(X_\alpha\). The same argument applies to \(Y_\beta\).

For a valid input, the formulas recover its realizing assignments, so these checks succeed. They also show that the realizing bijections are unique: any realization must give the same normalized score-difference functions and therefore the same vectors.

5. Exact verification on every relative interior

The preceding reconstruction is necessary for acceptance, but arbitrary input labels could still be inconsistent. We therefore verify the reconstructed rule everywhere.

We first record the precise strict-inequality test.

Relative-interior lemma. Let \(C\) be a nonempty polytope, let \(z_C\in\operatorname{relint}C\), and let \(h\) be affine. Then

\(h>0\text{ on }\operatorname{relint}C \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h\geq0\text{ on }C\text{ and }h(z_C)>0\).

Proof. If \(h\) is positive on the relative interior, continuity and the density of \(\operatorname{relint}C\) in \(C\) imply nonnegativity on \(C\).

Conversely, suppose \(h\geq0\) on \(C\) and \(h(z_C)>0\). If \(h(u)=0\) at some \(u\in\operatorname{relint}C\), then, for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\),

\(w=u+\varepsilon(u-z_C)\in C\).

Affineness gives
\(h(w)=-\varepsilon h(z_C)<0\), a contradiction. This proves the lemma.

Now define the scores from the reconstructed vectors. For each cell \(C\) with label \(B\), choose an anchor \(a_0\in B\).

  • For every \(b\in B\setminus\{a_0\}\), check that \(s_{a_0}-s_b\) vanishes identically on \(\operatorname{aff}C\). This is a rational linear-algebra check using an affine basis.
  • For every \(b\notin B\), minimize \(s_{a_0}-s_b\) over \(C\). Require its minimum to be nonnegative and its value at \(z_C\) to be strictly positive.

By the relative-interior lemma, these conditions are exactly the statement that the maximizing set on \(\operatorname{relint}C\) is \(B\).

The relative interiors of all listed cells partition \(P\). Consequently, passing every cell check proves the required equality for every real point of \(P\). Zero score gaps on a cell’s boundary cause no difficulty: the boundary’s own cells are checked separately.

This proves soundness. The hyperplane and reconstruction arguments, together with the necessity of the cell checks, prove completeness.

6. Running times and support sizes

All complexity statements below concern bit operations and the promised explicit subdivision.

Put \(D=k+\ell\). Let \(\mathsf{LP}(D,B)\) denote a polynomial bit-complexity bound for exact rational linear programming with at most \(D\) variables and description length at most \(B\).

The algorithm uses:

  • \(O(L+ND)\) linear programs to compute cell affine hulls and relative-interior points, allowing for the addition of the common simplex constraints;
  • at most \(N(m-1)\) further linear programs for final verification;
  • polynomial rational linear algebra, evaluations, and sorting.

Every intermediate rational number has encoding length bounded by a fixed polynomial in
\(L+D+\log(1+\alpha+\beta)\).
This follows from the rational LP output bound, Gaussian elimination, and the polynomial number of rational arithmetic operations.

Thus, for some polynomially bounded \(B\), the total running time has the form

\(O((L+ND+mN)\mathsf{LP}(D,B)) +\operatorname{poly}(m,k,\ell,N,L,\log(1+\alpha),\log(1+\beta))\).

In particular, recognition is polynomial in the listed parameters, with no additional dependence on the numerical magnitudes of \(\alpha,\beta\).

It is also polynomial in \(L\) alone under the explicit input convention. Indeed, every one of the \(k\ell\) vertices of \(P\) must occur as a listed zero-dimensional cell: an extreme point of \(P\) belongs to some subdivision cell, is an extreme point of that cell, and its singleton face is included. Hence \(N\geq k\ell\). Moreover, \(m\leq\min\{k,\ell\}\), and the explicit records imply \(N=O(L)\).

The output requires
\(O(m(k+\ell)\log(1+\max\{\alpha,\beta\}))\)
bits.

For an individual society pair, after recognition, only its actual supports need to be visited. With
\(\tau=\tau_q+\tau_r\), computing the score vector and its maximizing set takes \(O(m\tau)\) exact arithmetic operations.

More explicitly, if the society descriptions have total binary length \(S\), all intermediate numerators and denominators can be kept at length
\(O(S+\log(1+\alpha+\beta))\).
Using elementary integer arithmetic gives the conservative bit bound

\(O(m\tau(S+\log(1+\alpha+\beta))^2)\).

Thus evaluation depends on the actual support size \(\tau\), rather than requiring a scan of all \(k+\ell\) possible types.
