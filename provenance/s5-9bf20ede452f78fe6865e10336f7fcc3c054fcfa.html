4. Proven consequence of an $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness reduction

Let $\mathrm{ETR}$ denote the following decision problem: determine whether an existential formula over real variables is true, where its quantifier-free part is a Boolean combination of polynomial equations and inequalities with rational coefficients. Let $\exists\mathbb R$ denote the class of decision problems polynomial-time many-one reducible to $\mathrm{ETR}$.

The following conditional statement makes explicit an obstacle to completing the requested hardness argument. The corresponding general observation for $\mathrm{FIXP}$ appears in Etessami–Yannakakis. [Section 8](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/kousha/nash_focs07_full_j_spec_issue_sub.pdf)

Proposition

Suppose there is a polynomial-time transformation sending every Boolean formula $\varphi$ to a valid society instance $I_\varphi$, together with a polynomial-time exact-arithmetic decoder $D$, such that, for every equilibrium $x$ of $I_\varphi$,
$$ D(\varphi,x)= \begin{cases} 1,&\varphi\text{ is satisfiable},\\ 0,&\varphi\text{ is unsatisfiable}. \end{cases} $$
Allow the decoder rational arithmetic and comparisons.

Then
$$ \mathrm{coNP}\subseteq\exists\mathbb R. $$

The hypothesis is the usual decision interpretation of an $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness reduction to a total search problem: any returned solution must decide the source instance. It requires valid target instances on both satisfiable and unsatisfiable inputs.

Proof: existential encoding of fixed points

By Section 3 and $\mathrm{FIXP}$ membership, construct in polynomial time from $I_\varphi$ a continuous circuit map
$$ H_\varphi:P_\varphi\longrightarrow P_\varphi, $$
where $P_\varphi$ is a nonempty compact convex rational polytope. There is also a polynomially described separable affine map $\gamma_\varphi$ such that every fixed point $z$ of $H_\varphi$ decodes to an equilibrium
$$ x=\gamma_\varphi(z). $$
Brouwer’s theorem applies because $P_\varphi$ is nonempty, compact, and convex and $H_\varphi$ is a continuous self-map. Hence a fixed point exists.

Introduce a real variable for every circuit gate. Its correct evaluation has a polynomial-size existential encoding. If $u,v$ are gate inputs and $w$ is its output, use:

| Gate | Polynomial conditions |
|---|---|
| Addition | $w=u+v$ |
| Subtraction | $w=u-v$ |
| Multiplication | $w=uv$ |
| Division | $wv=u$ and $v\rho=1$, with an auxiliary variable $\rho$ |
| Minimum | $w\leq u$, $w\leq v$, and $(w-u)(w-v)=0$ |
| Maximum | $w\geq u$, $w\geq v$, and $(w-u)(w-v)=0$ |

For division, $v\rho=1$ enforces $v\neq0$, so $wv=u$ enforces exactly the intended quotient. For minimum, the product equation makes $w$ equal to an input, and the inequalities force it to equal the smaller input. Maximum is analogous.

Add the linear inequalities defining $P_\varphi$ and equations identifying each output coordinate of $H_\varphi$ with its corresponding input coordinate. Call the resulting quantifier-free formula $\Psi_\varphi(z,w)$.

Because the circuit is acyclic, its gate values are determined successively by its inputs. The gate conditions therefore prove
$$ \exists w\;\Psi_\varphi(z,w) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z\in P_\varphi\text{ and }H_\varphi(z)=z. $$
The formula has polynomial size; expanding the displayed products introduces only a constant number of monomials per gate.

Proof: existential encoding of the decoder

The affine equations for $x=\gamma_\varphi(z)$ have polynomial size.

A polynomial-time rational-arithmetic decoder with comparisons also has a polynomial-size existential computation encoding. To see this, unroll its polynomially bounded computation into successive register states. Arithmetic instructions use the gate conditions above.

For a comparison branch, encode its two alternatives as a disjunction. For example, branching on whether a register $r$ is nonnegative uses one alternative containing $r\geq0$ and the corresponding next control state, and another containing $r<0$ and the other next control state. Boolean control variables satisfy
$$ e(e-1)=0. $$
At each time step, exactly one instruction is active. Its guarded constraints impose its arithmetic operation, register updates, and next control state. Since there are polynomially many steps and registers, this produces a polynomial-size Boolean combination of polynomial conditions.

These constraints describe precisely the decoder’s actual computation: the initial state is specified, each arithmetic operation is enforced, and each branch follows its tested condition. Include the condition that its output is zero.

Consequently, we can construct a polynomial-size existential formula asserting
$$ \exists z:\quad z\in P_\varphi,\quad H_\varphi(z)=z,\quad D\bigl(\varphi,\gamma_\varphi(z)\bigr)=0. $$

If $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable, a fixed point exists and every decoded equilibrium causes rejection, so this formula is true.

If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, every decoded equilibrium causes acceptance, so this formula is false.

Thus $\mathrm{UNSAT}$ polynomial-time many-one reduces to $\mathrm{ETR}$. Since $\mathrm{UNSAT}$ is $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete,
$$ \mathrm{coNP}\subseteq\exists\mathbb R. $$
This proves the proposition.

This implication does not disprove $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness. It identifies an additional consequence that a qualifying hardness proof would establish.
