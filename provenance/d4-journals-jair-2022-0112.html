The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a population of \(N\) workers, type masses \(\mu_t\), per-type annotation costs \(c_t\), DRC moment bounds \(V_{t,y}\) and \(D_{t,y}\), and tolerance \(\varepsilon\), choose query rates \(q_t\in[0,1]\) to minimize \(N\sum_t\mu_t c_tq_t\), subject for each label \(y\) to \(\frac{1}{N}\sum_t\mu_t\left(\frac{V_{t,y}}{q_t}+\left(\frac{1}{q_t}-1\right)D_{t,y}\right)\le\varepsilon^2\). Rates are applied independently to workers of each type, and a zero rate is permitted only when the direct estimate is exact for every label.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

For one fixed item, there are \(m\ge 1\) labels and a population scale \(N\ge 1\), encoded as a binary integer. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. No integrality of \(N\mu_t\) is required.
For each type and label, a worker's real-valued score \(Z_{t,y}\) has finite variance, and its direct estimate \(a_{t,y}\) is deterministic. The input gives nonnegative binary-rational bounds \(V_{t,y}\ge\operatorname{Var}(Z_{t,y})\) and \(D_{t,y}\ge(\mathbb E Z_{t,y}-a_{t,y})^2\), nonnegative binary-rational annotation costs \(c_t\), tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\), and budget \(B\ge0\).
Worker annotations are mutually independent conditional on the fixed item. Query indicators are independent of annotations and of one another; type \(t\) is queried with real probability \(q_t\in[0,1]\). For positive rates, doubly robust estimation uses the score contribution \(a_{t,y}+I_t(Z_{t,y}-a_{t,y})/q_t\).
A zero rate is admissible precisely when \(V_{t,y}=D_{t,y}=0\) for every label; its contribution to each variance bound is defined as zero. Otherwise all rates must be positive.
The cost minimization problem minimizes \(N\sum_t\mu_t c_tq_t\) subject to \(\frac1N\sum_t\mu_t\bigl(V_{t,y}/q_t+(1/q_t-1)D_{t,y}\bigr)\le\varepsilon^2\) for every label \(y\). Its decision version asks whether admissible rates exist with cost at most \(B\); inequalities are exact, and \(L\) is the total binary input length.

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For a finite list of workers, doubly robust estimation is unbiased and has variance equal to the sum of the worker variance and squared mean-discrepancy terms above, divided by the squared population size; this is a statistical identity, not a complexity classification ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.08591)).
No result settling the exact support-encoded decision problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Used m for the number of labels, since N already denotes population size.
  • Interpreted D as a bound on squared mean discrepancy from the deterministic direct estimate, rather than a residual second moment, following the variance calculation.
  • Specified nonnegative rational costs and moment bounds, positive rational tolerance, and real query rates.
  • Made the zero-rate condition certifiable from the supplied bounds: all V and D entries for that type must vanish; defined its variance contribution as zero.
  • Introduced a nonnegative rational budget and the exact decision version of cost minimization to make the complexity target precise.

Partial classification of the exact decision problem

I do not establish either a uniform polynomial-time algorithm or $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness. The following strengthened partial results are proved below:

The first, third, and fourth statements strengthen the results in the supplied notes. All arguments used here are included.

1. Encoding assumption and normalization

I use the intended finite encoding: $\varepsilon$ and $B$ are binary-encoded rationals, and their encoding lengths are included in $L$. Without a specified finite encoding for these quantities, a Turing-machine complexity classification is not defined.

Call a type inactive if $V_{t,y}=D_{t,y}=0$ for every label. Assigning an inactive type rate zero minimizes its nonnegative cost and contributes zero to every constraint. Delete these types. If none remain, the answer is yes.

For every remaining type, put
$$ A_{t,y}=\mu_t(V_{t,y}+D_{t,y}),\qquad b_t=N\mu_tc_t, $$
and, for each label, put
$$ h_y=N\varepsilon^2+\sum_t\mu_tD_{t,y}. $$
The decision inequalities become
$$ 0<q_t\le1,\qquad \sum_t\frac{A_{t,y}}{q_t}\le h_y\quad\text{for every }y, \qquad \sum_tb_tq_t\le B. $$
Every $A_{t,y}$ is nonnegative, every $h_y$ is positive, and every remaining type has a positive coefficient for some label.

These coefficients have polynomial total encoding length. In particular, multiplying by binary-encoded $N$ requires arithmetic on its encoding, without expanding a population of $N$ workers.

Variance feasibility and attainment

The variance constraints are feasible if and only if
$$ \sum_tA_{t,y}\le h_y\quad\text{for every }y. $$
Indeed, $q_t\le1$ implies $A_{t,y}/q_t\ge A_{t,y}$, and setting every rate to one simultaneously minimizes all the left-hand sides. Equivalently, the criterion is
$$ \sum_t\mu_tV_{t,y}\le N\varepsilon^2\quad\text{for every }y. $$

Suppose this criterion holds. Define
$$ \delta_t=\max_y\frac{A_{t,y}}{h_y}>0. $$
Every feasible rate satisfies $q_t\ge\delta_t$, because each nonnegative summand $A_{t,y}/q_t$ is at most $h_y$. Consequently, the variance-feasible set is a closed subset of
$$ \prod_t[\delta_t,1]. $$
It is compact, and the continuous linear cost attains its minimum. Thus prohibited zero rates cannot arise as limits needed to attain the optimum.

2. Removing forced rates gives strict feasibility

This preprocessing will justify the exact dual certificate in Section 3.

First check the variance-feasibility criterion. If it fails, reject.

A label is tight at one if
$$ \sum_tA_{t,y}=h_y. $$
For such a label, every type with $A_{t,y}>0$ must have rate one in every feasible solution: any smaller rate would make that label’s left-hand side strictly larger than $h_y$.

Also, any remaining type with $b_t=0$ may be assigned rate one. This leaves its cost unchanged and decreases every variance expression.

Let $E$ consist of these two classes of types. Fix their rates to one, and define
$$ C_0=\sum_{t\in E}b_t,\qquad \widehat B=B-C_0,\qquad \widehat h_y=h_y-\sum_{t\in E}A_{t,y}. $$
For a label tight at one, all its positive coefficients have been removed and its residual constraint is $0\le0$. Delete that constraint. Delete any other residual constraint having no positive coefficient.

Renaming the remaining coefficients, the residual optimization problem is
$$ C=\min\left\{ \sum_{i=1}^kb_iq_i: 0<q_i\le1,\ \sum_{i=1}^k\frac{a_{i,y}}{q_i}\le h_y \text{ for every remaining }y \right\}. $$
Here $b_i>0$, each coordinate has a positive coefficient somewhere, and
$$ h_y>\sum_{i=1}^ka_{i,y} $$
for every remaining label. The strict inequality follows because removing fixed coordinates subtracts the same quantity from the right-hand side and from the sum of coefficients.

If $k=0$, compare $C_0$ with $B$ directly. Otherwise, at least one label remains. Set
$$ d=\min_y\left(1-\frac{\sum_i a_{i,y}}{h_y}\right)>0, \qquad \bar q_i=1-\frac d2. $$
Then $0<\bar q_i<1$, and
$$ \frac{\sum_i a_{i,y}}{h_y\bar q_i} \le \frac{1-d}{1-d/2}<1. $$
Thus every residual variance inequality and every upper-rate inequality is strict at $\bar q$.

For completeness, the residual optimizer is unique. Under $x_i=1/q_i$, the feasible set becomes the compact convex polytope
$$ x_i\ge1,\qquad \sum_i a_{i,y}x_i\le h_y, $$
and its objective is $\sum_i b_i/x_i$. Its Hessian is diagonal with entries $2b_i/x_i^3>0$, so the objective is strictly convex. Two distinct minimizers would have a midpoint with strictly smaller objective.

3. Existential certificates for both answers

Let $\exists\mathbb R$ denote the class of decision problems polynomial-time many-one reducible to feasibility of existential polynomial equalities and inequalities over the reals. Membership in $\operatorname{co}\exists\mathbb R$ means that the complement has such a reduction.

3.1. Acceptance has a quadratic existential formulation

For each retained type, introduce $s_t$ and impose
$$ 0\le q_t\le1,\qquad s_t\ge0,\qquad q_ts_t\ge1, $$
together with
$$ \sum_tA_{t,y}s_t\le h_y\quad\text{for every }y, \qquad \sum_tb_tq_t\le B. $$
This system is equivalent to the decision problem.

Given feasible rates, choose $s_t=1/q_t$. Conversely, the product inequality forces $q_t>0$ and $s_t\ge1/q_t$; nonnegativity of $A_{t,y}$ then implies the original variance inequalities.

All constraints have degree at most two and polynomial encoding length after clearing positive denominators. Hence the problem belongs to $\exists\mathbb R$.

The product constraint also has the rational second-order cone representation
$$ \left\|(2,q_t-s_t)\right\|_2\le q_t+s_t. $$
Under $q_t,s_t\ge0$, squaring gives exactly $q_ts_t\ge1$.

3.2. An explicit dual lower bound

Consider the strictly feasible residual problem from Section 2. For nonnegative multipliers $\lambda_y$ and $\eta_i$, write
$$ z_i=\sum_y\lambda_ya_{i,y}. $$
Its Lagrangian, with domain $(0,\infty)^k$, is
$$ \mathcal L(q) = \sum_i\left((b_i+\eta_i)q_i+\frac{z_i}{q_i}\right) -\sum_y\lambda_yh_y-\sum_i\eta_i. $$
For every feasible $q$,
$$ \mathcal L(q)\le\sum_i b_iq_i. $$
For $u>0$ and $z\ge0$,
$$ \inf_{q>0}\left(uq+\frac zq\right)=2\sqrt{uz}. $$
For $z>0$, this follows from
$$ uq+\frac zq-2\sqrt{uz} = \left(\sqrt{uq}-\sqrt{z/q}\right)^2, $$
with equality at $q=\sqrt{z/u}$. For $z=0$, the infimum is zero as $q$ tends to zero.

Therefore every nonnegative multiplier pair gives the lower bound
$$ D(\lambda,\eta) = 2\sum_i\sqrt{(b_i+\eta_i)z_i} -\sum_y\lambda_yh_y-\sum_i\eta_i \le C. $$

3.3. Proof that the dual bound attains the optimum

Here is a separation argument establishing the required strong duality.

List all residual constraint functions as $f_1,\ldots,f_r$, including both the variance residuals
$$ \sum_i a_{i,y}/q_i-h_y $$
and the upper-rate residuals $q_i-1$. They are convex on $(0,\infty)^k$. Define
$$ U= \left\{ (u,v): \text{there exists }q\in(0,\infty)^k \text{ with }f_j(q)<u_j\text{ for every }j, \quad \sum_i b_iq_i<v \right\}. $$
This set is nonempty and open. It is convex: convex combinations of witnessing rates remain positive, convexity bounds each constraint function, and the cost is linear.

The point $(0,C)$ does not belong to $U$, since its membership would give a feasible rate vector of cost below $C$.

The finite-dimensional separation theorem for an open convex set and a point outside it gives a nonzero vector $(\alpha,\beta)$ satisfying
$$ \alpha\cdot u+\beta v\ge\beta C \quad\text{for every }(u,v)\in U. $$
The set $U$ remains unchanged under arbitrary positive increases of any coordinate. Consequently, every component of $\alpha$, and also $\beta$, is nonnegative; a negative coefficient would violate the displayed lower bound under such increases.

Moreover, $\beta>0$. Otherwise $\alpha\ne0$, and the strictly feasible point $\bar q$ from Section 2 permits a vector $u$ with every component negative and $f_j(\bar q)<u_j$. This would give $\alpha\cdot u<0$, contradicting the separation inequality.

Divide by $\beta$. By choosing $u_j$ decreasing to $f_j(q)$ and $v$ decreasing to the cost, we obtain nonnegative constraint multipliers such that
$$ \mathcal L(q)\ge C \quad\text{for every }q\in(0,\infty)^k. $$
Let $q^*$ be an attained optimum. Its feasibility gives $\mathcal L(q^*)\le C$, so
$$ \mathcal L(q^*)=C=\inf_{q>0}\mathcal L(q). $$
The coordinatewise infimum calculation above now proves
$$ D(\lambda,\eta)=C. $$
Thus the dual maximum is attained and equals the primal minimum.

3.4. Rejection has a quadratic existential formulation

For a nontrivial residual instance, the original answer is no exactly when $C>\widehat B$.

Consider the following existential system:
$$ \lambda_y\ge0,\qquad \eta_i\ge0,\qquad z_i\ge0,\qquad w_i\ge0, $$
$$ z_i=\sum_y\lambda_ya_{i,y}, \qquad w_i^2\le(b_i+\eta_i)z_i, $$
$$ 2\sum_iw_i-\sum_y\lambda_yh_y-\sum_i\eta_i>\widehat B. $$
A solution supplies a dual lower bound exceeding $\widehat B$, because
$$ w_i\le\sqrt{(b_i+\eta_i)z_i}. $$
Conversely, if $C>\widehat B$, use attaining dual multipliers and choose
$$ w_i=\sqrt{(b_i+\eta_i)z_i}. $$
They satisfy the system.

This is a polynomial-size existential system of degree at most two. The preliminary rationally decidable cases can be mapped to fixed true or false existential formulas. Hence the complement belongs to $\exists\mathbb R$, proving
$$ \boxed{\text{the decision problem belongs to } \exists\mathbb R\cap\operatorname{co}\exists\mathbb R.} $$

The existential theory of the reals is decidable in polynomial space, so the problem also belongs to $\mathrm{PSPACE}$. The hypotheses are met by the polynomial-size integer-coefficient formulas obtained above. See [Canny, *Some Algebraic and Geometric Computations in PSPACE*](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1988/6041.html).

These are certificates using real variables; no polynomial bound on rational certificate length has been proved.

4. Polynomial time for fixed support size

The acceptance formulation uses at most $2\tau$ variables, degree at most two, and $m+O(\tau)$ constraints.

The fixed-dimensional real-algebraic decision theorem states that, for fixed numbers of variables and fixed degree, feasibility of polynomial equalities and inequalities with integer coefficients is decidable in time polynomial in the number of constraints, formula length, and coefficient bit length. This is a consequence of the decision algorithms and intermediate bit-size bounds in [Basu, Pollack, and Roy, *On the Combinatorial and Algebraic Complexity of Quantifier Elimination*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221499271_On_the_combinatorial_and_algebraic_complexity_of_quantifier_elimination).

Fix any constant $\tau_0$. For instances with $\tau\le\tau_0$, the formulation has at most $2\tau_0$ variables and degree two. Clearing denominators preserves polynomial encoding length. The theorem therefore gives an exact algorithm polynomial in $m$ and $L$.

The polynomial’s exponent may depend on $\tau_0$. This does not establish the uniform polynomial bound requested when $\tau$ is unbounded.

5. Square-root-sum hardness with one label

Define square-root-sum comparison to ask, for positive binary integers $d_1,\ldots,d_n$ and a nonnegative binary integer $K$, whether
$$ S=\sum_{i=1}^n\sqrt{d_i}\le K. $$
This is the classical exact-arithmetic comparison problem discussed in [Allender and coauthors, *On the Complexity of Numerical Analysis*](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2005/037/revision/1/download). The reduction below uses no assumed classification of it.

Let $M$ be the least power of two at least $n$. Pad the radicands with ones:
$$ e_i=d_i\quad(i\le n),\qquad e_i=1\quad(n<i\le M). $$
Set
$$ K'=K+M-n,\qquad S'=\sum_{i=1}^M\sqrt{e_i}=S+M-n. $$
Thus $S\le K$ exactly when $S'\le K'$.

Let $d_{\max}=\max_i e_i$. Choose a positive integer $r$ with $2^r>Md_{\max}$ and put $H=2^{2r}$. Construct
$$ m=N=1,\qquad \tau=M,\qquad \mu_i=\frac1M, $$
$$ c_i=M,\qquad V_{i,1}=Me_i,\qquad D_{i,1}=0, \qquad \varepsilon=2^r,\qquad B=\frac{(K')^2}{H}. $$
All quantities are integers or dyadic rationals. The normalized inequalities are
$$ \sum_i\frac{e_i}{q_i}\le H,\qquad \sum_iq_i\le\frac{(K')^2}{H}, \qquad 0<q_i\le1. $$

If rates are feasible, Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$ (S')^2 = \left(\sum_i\sqrt{e_i/q_i}\sqrt{q_i}\right)^2 \le \left(\sum_i e_i/q_i\right)\left(\sum_iq_i\right) \le(K')^2. $$
Since both sides’ square roots are nonnegative, $S'\le K'$.

Conversely, if $S'\le K'$, choose
$$ q_i=\frac{S'\sqrt{e_i}}H. $$
These rates are positive and satisfy the caps, since
$$ S'\sqrt{e_i}\le Md_{\max}<H. $$
Furthermore,
$$ \sum_i\frac{e_i}{q_i}=H,\qquad \sum_iq_i=\frac{(S')^2}{H}\le B. $$

The construction has polynomial size: $M\le2n$, and $r$ can be chosen proportional to the binary length of $Md_{\max}$. It proves a polynomial-time many-one reduction to the one-label restriction.

6. Exact oracle algorithm when types affect only one label

Assume each active type has a positive coefficient $A_{t,y}$ for exactly one label. Apply Sections 1 and 2. The residual variables partition into disjoint groups $G_y$, and the variance constraints become
$$ \sum_{i\in G_y}\frac{a_i}{q_i}\le h_y. $$
The minimum total cost is the sum of the group minima, plus the fixed cost $C_0$.

6.1. Solving one group

For a nonempty group, the residual problem is
$$ \min\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_iq_i \quad\text{subject to}\quad \sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{a_i}{q_i}\le h,\qquad 0<q_i\le1, $$
where $a_i,b_i>0$ and $h>\sum_i a_i$.

Maintain a set $T$ of capped coordinates and its complement $F$. Define
$$ R=h-\sum_{i\in T}a_i,\qquad S_F=\sum_{i\in F}\sqrt{a_ib_i},\qquad x=\frac{S_F}{R}. $$
Initially $T$ is empty. Always,
$$ R>\sum_{i\in F}a_i>0 $$
while $F$ is nonempty.

Choose $j\in F$ minimizing the rational threshold
$$ \rho_j=\frac{b_j}{a_j}. $$
Test whether
$$ x^2\le\rho_j. $$
This is equivalent to the radical comparison
$$ \sum_{i\in F}\sqrt{\frac{a_ib_i}{\rho_j}}\le R. $$
If the test succeeds, stop. Otherwise move $j$ to $T$.

To verify this update, let $x'$ be the subsequent value. Direct subtraction gives
$$ x'-x = \frac{a_jx-\sqrt{a_jb_j}}{R-a_j}>0. $$
The denominator is positive by the invariant; the numerator is positive because the failed test gives $x>\sqrt{b_j/a_j}$. Thus every previously capped threshold remains below subsequent values of $x^2$.

The procedure stops before $F$ becomes empty. With one coordinate $i$ remaining, $R>a_i$ implies
$$ x=\frac{\sqrt{a_ib_i}}R<\sqrt{\frac{b_i}{a_i}}, $$
so its test succeeds. There are at most $\ell$ tests.

At termination, define
$$ q_i^*=1\quad(i\in T),\qquad q_i^*=x\sqrt{a_i/b_i}\quad(i\in F). $$
The successful minimum-threshold test ensures all free rates are at most one. The update argument gives $x^2\ge b_i/a_i$ for capped coordinates.

For each $i$, differentiation shows that the strictly convex function
$$ b_iq+x^2a_i/q $$
has its minimum over $0<q\le1$ at
$$ \min\{1,x\sqrt{a_i/b_i}\}=q_i^*. $$
Also,
$$ \sum_i\frac{a_i}{q_i^*} = \sum_{i\in T}a_i+\frac{S_F}{x} =h. $$
For any feasible $q$, coordinatewise minimization therefore gives
$$ \sum_i b_iq_i \ge \sum_i b_iq_i+x^2\left(\sum_i a_i/q_i-h\right) \ge \sum_i b_iq_i^*. $$
The group minimum is consequently
$$ C_y=C_{T_y}+\frac{S_{F_y}^2}{R_y}, \qquad C_{T_y}=\sum_{i\in T_y}b_i. $$

6.2. Combining all groups

Put $v_i=a_ib_i$. Expanding each square yields
$$ C_y = C_{T_y} +\frac{\sum_{i\in F_y}v_i}{R_y} +\sum_{\substack{i<j\\i,j\in F_y}} \sqrt{\frac{4v_iv_j}{R_y^2}}. $$
Hence the overall minimum has the form
$$ \operatorname{OPT} = C_{\mathrm{rat}}+\sum_{\nu=1}^p\sqrt{r_\nu}, $$
where $C_{\mathrm{rat}}$ is rational, every $r_\nu$ is a positive rational, and
$$ p\le\binom{k}{2}. $$
All these rational quantities have polynomial encoding length.

If $B-C_{\mathrm{rat}}<0$, reject. Otherwise the budget question is one rational-radical comparison:
$$ \sum_{\nu=1}^p\sqrt{r_\nu}\le B-C_{\mathrm{rat}}. $$
An empty radical list is handled by rational comparison.

6.3. Reduction of oracle queries to integer radicands

For a comparison
$$ \sum_{\nu=1}^p\sqrt{p_\nu/d_\nu}\le u/v, $$
where $p_\nu,d_\nu,v$ are positive integers and $u\ge0$, set
$$ Q=\prod_{\nu=1}^p d_\nu,\qquad z_\nu=\frac{p_\nu Q^2}{d_\nu}. $$
Every $z_\nu$ is an integer, and the comparison is equivalent to
$$ \sum_{\nu=1}^p\sqrt{v^2z_\nu}\le uQ. $$
The binary length of $Q$ is at most the sum of the denominator lengths, so this transformation has polynomial size.

The group algorithms use at most $k$ threshold queries in total, followed by at most one final budget query. Thus this restriction is polynomial-time decidable with a square-root-sum oracle.

The one-label reduction in Section 5 lies within this restriction. Therefore:
$$ \boxed{\text{When each active type affects only one label, the problem is polynomial-time Turing equivalent to square-root-sum comparison.}} $$
The same statement holds for the one-label restriction.

7. Hardness even for disjoint pairs of types

The preceding lower bound can be strengthened to instances in which every label involves exactly two active types.

Given $d_1,\ldots,d_n,K$, let $M$ be the least power of two at least $2n$. Use $M$ types of equal mass $1/M$, with
$$ N=1,\qquad c_t=M,\qquad D_{t,y}=0. $$
For label $i$, set
$$ V_{2i-1,i}=Md_i,\qquad V_{2i,i}=M, $$
and set all other variance bounds to zero. Types beyond $2n$ are inactive.

Let $d_{\max}=\max_i d_i$. Choose $r$ with $2^r>2d_{\max}$ and set
$$ H=2^{2r},\qquad \varepsilon=2^r,\qquad B=\frac{\sum_i d_i+n+2K}{H}. $$
For each label, the normalized constraint is
$$ \frac{d_i}{q_{2i-1}}+\frac1{q_{2i}}\le H. $$
Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$ q_{2i-1}+q_{2i}\ge\frac{(\sqrt{d_i}+1)^2}{H}. $$
This bound is attained by
$$ q_{2i-1}=\frac{d_i+\sqrt{d_i}}H,\qquad q_{2i}=\frac{\sqrt{d_i}+1}H. $$
Both rates are positive and below one: their numerators are at most $2d_{\max}$, whereas $H>2d_{\max}$.

Set inactive rates to zero. Since the pairs are disjoint, the exact optimum is
$$ \operatorname{OPT} = \frac{\sum_i d_i+n+2\sum_i\sqrt{d_i}}H. $$
Thus $\operatorname{OPT}\le B$ if and only if $\sum_i\sqrt{d_i}\le K$.

All data are integers or dyadic rationals of polynomial encoding length, and $M<4n$. This proves a polynomial-time many-one reduction even with two active types per label and one affected label per active type. Section 6 supplies the matching oracle upper bound for this restriction.

8. What remains unresolved

The general classification remains unresolved in this argument.

The exact conic formulation and the attained dual establish real-algebraic upper bounds; they do not provide a uniform polynomial bit-complexity algorithm. The fixed-support algorithm allows an exponent depending on the support bound.

Likewise, the reductions prove square-root-sum hardness, without proving $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness. A polynomial-time algorithm for the full problem would, by Section 5, give a polynomial-time algorithm for square-root-sum comparison.

For arbitrarily overlapping label supports and unbounded support size, neither requested classification has been established.
