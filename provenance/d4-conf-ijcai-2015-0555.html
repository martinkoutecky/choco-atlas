The anchor (named result in the paper)

Proposition 1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type graph \(H=(T,F)\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\), weights \(w_t\), and quota \(q\), determine for each type \(t\) the exponential rate of its normalized Banzhaf index in the denominator-compatible finite blow-ups, where type \(s\) has \(k\mu_s\) agents and the quota is \(\lceil kq\rceil\). In particular, establish whether the rate equals the infimum of \(\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s D(x_s/\mu_s\|1/2)\), with \(D(p\|1/2)=p\ln(2p)+(1-p)\ln(2(1-p))\), over masses \(0\le x_s\le\mu_s\) and active supports \(A\subseteq T\), such that each component \(D\) of \(H[A]\) has \(\sum_{s\in D}w_sx_s\le q\), while the component containing \(t\) after adding the target type reaches quota. Allow \(A\) to include zero-mass bridge types, and report infinity when no pivotal configuration is feasible.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_s\), normalized by \(\sum_s\mu_s=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Input also includes a simple undirected graph \(H=(T,F)\), binary-encoded integer weights \(w_s\ge0\), and a rational quota satisfying \(\frac12\sum_s\mu_sw_s<q\le\sum_s\mu_sw_s\).
For each positive integer \(k\) with all \(k\mu_s\) integral, replace type \(s\) by a clique of \(k\mu_s\) agents, each of weight \(w_s\); join two distinct type cliques completely exactly when their types are adjacent in \(H\). Set the quota to \(Q_k=\lceil kq\rceil\). A coalition wins exactly when one of its induced connected components has weight at least \(Q_k\).
For one designated agent of type \(t\), let \(B_t(k)\) be its probability of changing a losing coalition into a winning one when every other agent is included independently with probability \(1/2\). This is the normalized Banzhaf index used here. Its proposed rate is \(R_t=\lim_{k\to\infty}-k^{-1}\ln B_t(k)\), along the admissible integers, with \(-\ln0=+\infty\).
Define \(I_t\) as the infimum of \(\sum_s\mu_sD(x_s/\mu_s\|1/2)\), where \(D(p\|1/2)=p\ln(2p)+(1-p)\ln(2(1-p))\), with \(0\ln0=0\). The infimum ranges over \(A\subseteq T\) and \(0\le x_s\le\mu_s\), with \(x_s=0\) outside \(A\), every component \(C\) of \(H[A]\) satisfying \(\sum_{s\in C}w_sx_s\le q\), and the component \(C_t\) containing \(t\) in \(H[A\cup\{t\}]\) satisfying \(\sum_{s\in C_t}w_sx_s\ge q\). Types in \(A\) may have zero selected mass; the added target agent contributes zero mass in these constraints. An empty feasible set gives \(I_t=+\infty\).

Do the rates \(R_t\) exist for every input, and do they equal \(I_t\)?
Is computing all rates to additive error \(\varepsilon\), while distinguishing \(+\infty\), possible in polynomial time in \(m=|F|\), \(\tau\), the input bit length \(L\), and \(\log(1/\varepsilon)\), or is this problem \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite graph-restricted weighted voting games, computing the Banzhaf and Shapley–Shubik indices is \(\#\mathrm{P}\)-complete after their respective counting normalizations; bounded-treewidth instances admit a pseudo-polynomial algorithm. [Source](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/15/Papers/095.pdf)
No result settling this specific blow-up rate problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to sum to one, fixing the scale used in the rate.
  • Retained nonnegative integer agent weights and the proper-game quota condition from the discrete definition.
  • Specified clique replacement within each type and complete joins across type edges; the supplied question did not define the blow-up adjacency.
  • Used all positive integers making the type populations integral, without imposing an additional quota-denominator restriction.
  • Interpreted normalized Banzhaf as the pivotal probability, rather than dividing an agent's swing count by the total swing count.
  • Defined the rate per blow-up scale k using natural logarithms, and left existence of its limit as a question.
  • Specified the active-support constraints, including zero selected masses, absent-type masses, and the target's zero limiting mass.
  • Made computation of potentially irrational rates precise through additive error, with a separate infinity outcome; defined m as the number of type-graph edges.

Results

For the stated input class:

  • Every \(R_t\) exists as an extended real number.
  • The equality \(R_t=I_t\) holds for positive-weight targets, but fails in general for zero-weight targets—even when their rates are finite.
  • Computing all rates while distinguishing \(+\infty\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. In fact, deciding whether one designated rate is finite is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

The zero-weight case is essential to the hardness result below.

1. Preliminaries and an entropy calculation

Write \(W(S)=\sum_{s\in S}\mu_sw_s\), \(\mu(S)=\sum_{s\in S}\mu_s\), and \(W=W(T)\). The quota hypothesis gives \(0<W/2<q\le W\).

For a coalition, let its occupied support be the set of types represented by at least one selected agent. Because type cliques and cross-type joins are complete, its connected components correspond exactly to the components of the graph induced by its occupied support.

For any \(S\subseteq T\), define

\( J(S)=\inf\left\{\sum_{s\in S}\mu_sD(x_s/\mu_s\|1/2):0\le x_s\le\mu_s,\ \sum_{s\in S}w_sx_s\ge q\right\}. \)

We use the convention \(J(S)=+\infty\) when the constraint is infeasible.

Explicit minimizers

If \(W(S)<q\), then \(J(S)=+\infty\).

Suppose \(q<W(S)\). There is a unique \(\lambda>0\) satisfying

\( \sum_{s\in S}\mu_sw_s\frac{e^{\lambda w_s}}{1+e^{\lambda w_s}}=q. \)

Indeed, the left side is continuous and strictly increasing: its derivative is

\( \sum_{s\in S}\mu_sw_s^2\frac{e^{\lambda w_s}}{(1+e^{\lambda w_s})^2}>0. \)

Its value at zero is \(W(S)/2<q\), and its limit is \(W(S)>q\).

Set \(p_s^\ast=e^{\lambda w_s}/(1+e^{\lambda w_s})\) and \(x_s^\ast=\mu_sp_s^\ast\). Convexity of binary relative entropy gives, for every \(p\in[0,1]\),

\( D(p\|1/2)\ge \lambda w_sp-\ln\!\left(\frac{1+e^{\lambda w_s}}2\right), \)

with equality at \(p=p_s^\ast\). This follows by minimizing the difference: its derivative on the interior is \(\ln(p/(1-p))-\lambda w_s\), and its second derivative is positive.

Consequently,

\( J(S)=\lambda q-\sum_{s\in S}\mu_s\ln\!\left(\frac{1+e^{\lambda w_s}}2\right), \)

and \(x^\ast\) is a minimizer. Every coordinate is strictly between zero and its upper bound.

If \(q=W(S)\), feasibility forces \(x_s=\mu_s\) for every positive-weight type. Zero-weight coordinates minimize their entropy at \(\mu_s/2\). Hence

\( J(S)=\sum_{\substack{s\in S\\w_s>0}}\mu_s\ln2. \)

In both finite cases, the specified minimizer has positive coordinates and weighted sum exactly \(q\).

Two elementary probability facts

Let \(N_s(k)=k\mu_s-\mathbf1_{\{s=t\}}\) be the number of available agents of type \(s\). For selected counts \(n_s(k)\), their joint probability is

\( P_k(n)=\prod_{s\in T}2^{-N_s(k)}\binom{N_s(k)}{n_s(k)}. \)

If \(n_s(k)/k\to x_s\), then

\( -\frac1k\ln P_k(n)\longrightarrow \Phi(x):=\sum_{s\in T}\mu_sD(x_s/\mu_s\|1/2). \)

To justify this, for \(0\le j\le N\) the elementary binomial bounds give

\( \frac{e^{-ND(j/N\|1/2)}}{N+1} \le 2^{-N}\binom Nj \le e^{-ND(j/N\|1/2)}. \)

The logarithmic prefactors divided by \(k\) vanish, and continuity of \(D\), including at its endpoints, gives the asserted limit.

We also need a tail bound with no designated-agent deletion. For independent \(Z_s\sim\operatorname{Bin}(k\mu_s,1/2)\), whenever \(J(S)\) is finite,

\( \Pr\!\left(\sum_{s\in S}w_sZ_s\ge Q_k\right)\le e^{-kJ(S)}. \)

For \(q<W(S)\), apply the exponential Markov inequality with the \(\lambda\) above and use \(Q_k\ge kq\). If \(q=W(S)\), then \(kq\) is integral, and reaching quota requires selecting every positive-weight agent in \(S\). Its probability is exactly \(e^{-kJ(S)}\).

2. The proposed quantity \(I_t\)

Let \(U_t\) be the connected component of \(H\) containing \(t\). Then, for every target, including zero-weight targets,

\( \boxed{I_t=J(U_t).} \)

Proof. For any feasible pair in the definition of \(I_t\), the component \(C_t\) is contained in \(U_t\). Nonnegative weights therefore imply

\( \sum_{s\in U_t}w_sx_s\ge \sum_{s\in C_t}w_sx_s\ge q. \)

The entropy contribution on \(U_t\) is at least \(J(U_t)\), and all remaining contributions are nonnegative. Thus \(I_t\ge J(U_t)\).

If \(W(U_t)<q\), feasibility is impossible.

Otherwise, take \(A=T\), use the minimizer of \(J(U_t)\) on \(U_t\), and set \(x_s=\mu_s/2\) outside \(U_t\). The component \(U_t\) has selected weight exactly \(q\). Every other component \(V\) has selected weight \(W(V)/2\le W/2<q\). This is feasible and has entropy \(J(U_t)\), proving equality. \(\square\)

Thus the proposed quantity depends on the graph only through the full component containing the target.

3. Positive-weight targets

Suppose \(w_t>0\). Then

\( \boxed{R_t=J(U_t)=I_t.} \)

We prove existence as well as equality.

If \(W(U_t)<q\), even the full component containing the added target has weight below \(Q_k\). Adding that agent cannot create a winning component, so \(B_t(k)=0\) for every admissible \(k\).

Now suppose \(W(U_t)\ge q\).

Upper bound on pivotal probability

If the target is pivotal, the total selected weight in \(U_t\), before its addition, is at least \(Q_k-w_t\).

When \(q<W(U_t)\), use the optimizing \(\lambda\) from Section 1. Put \(a_s=\ln((1+e^{\lambda w_s})/2)\). The exponential Markov inequality gives

\( B_t(k)\le \exp\!\left(-\lambda(Q_k-w_t)+k\sum_{s\in U_t}\mu_sa_s-a_t\right) \le e^{-kJ(U_t)}e^{\lambda w_t-a_t} \le 2e^{-kJ(U_t)}. \)

The final inequality follows from \(e^{\lambda w_t-a_t}=2e^{\lambda w_t}/(1+e^{\lambda w_t})\le2\).

When \(q=W(U_t)\), the maximum available weight before adding the target is \(kq-w_t\). The necessary threshold condition forces every available positive-weight agent in \(U_t\) to be selected. Its probability is \(2e^{-kJ(U_t)}\).

Therefore,

\( \liminf_k-\frac1k\ln B_t(k)\ge J(U_t). \)

Pivotal counts achieving the exponent

First suppose \(q<W(U_t)\), and let \(x^\ast\) be its entropy minimizer. For \(s\in U_t\setminus\{t\}\), choose counts \(n_s(k)\) with \(n_s(k)/k\to x_s^\ast\). Write \(b_k=\sum_{s\in U_t\setminus\{t\}}w_sn_s(k)\), and choose

\( n_t(k)=\left\lceil\frac{Q_k-b_k}{w_t}\right\rceil-1. \)

This ensures

\( b_k+w_tn_t(k)<Q_k\le b_k+w_t(n_t(k)+1). \)

Moreover, \(n_t(k)/k\to x_t^\ast\). Since \(0<x_t^\ast<\mu_t\), this count lies between zero and \(k\mu_t-1\) for all sufficiently large admissible \(k\).

Every type in \(U_t\) is occupied for sufficiently large \(k\). Its selected agents therefore form one connected component, which loses before the addition and wins afterward.

Outside \(U_t\), choose counts approaching \(\mu_s/2\). Every other full graph component \(V\) has capacity

\( W(V)\le W-W(U_t)\le W-q<q, \)

so it cannot win, regardless of its selected counts. The constructed count vector is pivotal, and its probability exponent tends to \(J(U_t)\).

Finally, suppose \(q=W(U_t)\). Select every available positive-weight agent in \(U_t\), and select approximately half of each zero-weight type. For sufficiently large \(k\), all types in \(U_t\) are occupied. Its weights before and after addition are respectively \(kq-w_t\) and \(kq=Q_k\). Choose approximately half of every outside type. This is again pivotal, with probability exponent \(J(U_t)\).

Thus the matching upper limit holds. These constructions work for every sufficiently large admissible \(k\), so they also rule out any lattice-induced failure of convergence.

4. Zero-weight targets: the correct rate formula

Suppose \(w_t=0\).

If another agent of type \(t\) is selected, adding the target changes neither connectivity nor component weights: the existing type-\(t\) clique already supplies all its connections. Therefore a pivotal coalition must contain no other type-\(t\) agent.

For \(A\subseteq T\setminus\{t\}\), let \(\mathcal C_t(A)\) be the components of \(H[A]\) containing a neighbor of \(t\), and put

\( S_t(A)=\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal C_t(A)}C. \)

These are precisely the components merged when the target is added.

Call \(A\) admissible for merging if:

  • \(W(S_t(A))\ge q\);
  • at least two components in \(\mathcal C_t(A)\) have positive capacity.

Let \(\mathcal G_t\) denote this finite family. The correct formula is

\( \boxed{ R_t=\min_{A\in\mathcal G_t} \left[\mu(T\setminus A)\ln2+J(S_t(A))\right], } \)

where an empty minimum is \(+\infty\).

Necessity and the probability upper bound

Consider a pivotal coalition with occupied support \(A\). The merged components have total selected weight at least \(Q_k\), so their capacity is at least \(q\).

At least two of these components must have positive selected weight. Otherwise one component already carries the entire post-addition weight and would already win. Hence \(A\in\mathcal G_t\).

For a fixed such support \(A\), the probability that every available agent outside \(A\) is absent is

\( 2^{-(k\mu(T\setminus A)-1)} =2e^{-k\mu(T\setminus A)\ln2}. \)

The subtraction of one accounts for the designated agent, which is not sampled. Independently, the selected weight in \(S_t(A)\) must reach quota. Section 1 consequently gives

\( \Pr(\text{pivotal with support }A) \le 2e^{-k[\mu(T\setminus A)\ln2+J(S_t(A))]}. \)

If \(\mathcal G_t\) is empty, no pivotal coalition exists for any \(k\).

Otherwise, writing the claimed minimum as \(\rho_t\) and summing over at most \(2^{\tau-1}\) supports gives

\( B_t(k)\le2^\tau e^{-k\rho_t}, \qquad \liminf_k-\frac1k\ln B_t(k)\ge\rho_t. \)

Matching pivotal counts

Fix \(A\in\mathcal G_t\), and abbreviate \(S=S_t(A)\).

On \(S\), use the entropy minimizer of \(J(S)\); on \(A\setminus S\), set \(x_s=\mu_s/2\); outside \(A\), set \(x_s=0\). Every coordinate in \(A\) is positive.

The total selected weight on \(S\) is \(q\). Each joined component has selected weight strictly below \(q\): there are at least two positive-capacity components, and the minimizer assigns positive selected weight to each of them.

Every nonjoined component \(C\) satisfies

\( W(C)\le W-W(S)\le W-q<q. \)

Now choose \(n_s(k)=\lceil kx_s\rceil\) for \(s\in A\), and zero counts outside \(A\). These counts respect their capacities because \(k\mu_s\) is integral and \(x_s\le\mu_s\). For sufficiently large \(k\), their occupied support is exactly \(A\).

Rounding upward ensures that the merged weight is at least \(kq\), hence at least \(Q_k\). Each pre-addition component remains below quota for sufficiently large \(k\), because its limiting selected weight is strictly below \(q\). Thus the count vector is pivotal.

Its probability exponent tends to

\( \Phi(x)=\mu(T\setminus A)\ln2+J(S_t(A)). \)

Choosing a minimizing support proves the matching upper limit. This proves the formula and existence of every zero-weight target’s rate, including the boundary case \(q=W(S)\).

5. Equality fails even for a genuinely pivotal target

There is an immediate dummy counterexample: in a complete type graph, a zero-weight target cannot change connectivity or weight, although \(I_t\) can be finite.

More substantially, take the three-type path \(a-t-b\), with

\( \mu_a=\mu_b=\frac14,\qquad \mu_t=\frac12,\qquad w_a=w_b=1,\qquad w_t=0,\qquad q=\frac38. \)

Here \(W=1/2\), so the quota hypothesis holds.

Section 2 gives

\( I_t=\frac12D(3/4\|1/2). \)

For the target to be pivotal, every other type-\(t\) agent must be absent. Without type \(t\), the two endpoint cliques are separate, and neither can reach quota because each has capacity \(1/4<3/8\). Adding the target makes their combined weight decisive. Hence, exactly,

\( B_t(k)= 2^{-(k/2-1)} \Pr\!\left(\operatorname{Bin}(k/2,1/2)\ge\lceil3k/8\rceil\right). \)

The zero-target formula yields

\( R_t=\frac12\ln2+\frac12D(3/4\|1/2)>I_t. \)

Thus both quantities are finite, and the target is genuinely pivotal.

In fact, whenever \(w_t=0\) and \(I_t\) is finite, the compulsory absence of the other type-\(t\) agents gives the general bound

\( R_t\ge \mu_t\ln2+I_t>I_t. \)

To verify it directly, pivotality implies both their absence and total selected weight at least \(Q_k\) in \(U_t\). These events are independent because type \(t\) has weight zero. Their probability is at most \(2e^{-k[\mu_t\ln2+J(U_t)]}\).

Consequently, equality holds precisely when \(w_t>0\) or \(W(U_t)<q\); in the latter case both quantities are \(+\infty\).

6. Computational hardness

We prove hardness already for distinguishing finite rates from \(+\infty\), with:

  • a connected type graph;
  • weights in \(\{0,1\}\);
  • a designated zero-weight target of degree two;
  • quota equal to the full society’s weight.

Source problem

Use monotone satisfiability: each clause consists either entirely of positive literals or entirely of negative literals. Monotone three-literal satisfiability is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even when each clause uses three distinct variables and every variable occurs twice positively and twice negatively. These stronger restrictions are established in [Döcker’s paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08032). The construction below accepts every instance of that restricted class.

Let the formula have \(n\) variables and \(c\) clauses.

Construction

Create the following types:

  • the designated target \(t\), and roots \(a,b\);
  • types \(u_i,v_i\) for each variable;
  • one type \(d_j\) for each clause.

Add edges \(ta,tb\), and, for every variable, add the path edges

\( au_i,\quad u_iv_i,\quad v_ib. \)

For a positive clause, join its clause type to the \(u_i\) corresponding to its variables. For a negative clause, join its clause type to the corresponding \(v_i\). Add no other edges.

Assign weight one to \(a,b\) and all clause types; assign weight zero to all other types. Let \(h=c+2\), so \(\tau=h+2n+1\).

Choose a power of two \(D\) with \(\tau\le D<2\tau\), and assign

\( \mu_s=\frac1D\quad(s\ne t), \qquad \mu_t=1-\frac{h+2n}{D}. \)

All masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one. Set

\( q=W=\frac hD. \)

The quota hypothesis holds. The graph, masses, weights, and quota have polynomial encoding length.

A finite rate implies satisfiability

Suppose a pivotal coalition exists.

Since \(Q_k=kq\) equals the entire society’s weight, winning requires every positive-weight agent to belong to one connected component. Thus every agent of types \(a,b,d_j\) must be selected.

No other type-\(t\) agent can be selected. Since the target’s only neighbors are \(a,b\), these roots must lie in different pre-addition components; otherwise adding the zero-weight target changes nothing. Every clause type must belong to one of those two components.

Both \(u_i\) and \(v_i\) cannot be occupied, because the path \(a-u_i-v_i-b\) would connect the roots.

Define a truth assignment by setting a variable true when \(u_i\) is occupied and false when \(v_i\) is occupied. If neither is occupied, assign it arbitrarily. This is consistent.

A positive clause type can connect to a root only through an occupied \(u_i\), so that clause has a true variable. A negative clause type similarly requires an occupied \(v_i\), so it has a false variable. Every clause is satisfied.

If the formula is unsatisfiable, therefore, \(B_t(k)=0\) for every admissible \(k\), and \(R_t=+\infty\).

Satisfiability implies a finite rate

Conversely, take a satisfying assignment.

Select every positive-weight agent. For each variable, occupy \(u_i\) when it is true and \(v_i\) when it is false, leaving the opposite type empty. Leave every other type-\(t\) agent absent.

Every positive clause connects to \(a\), and every negative clause connects to \(b\). The roots remain disconnected because each variable path has an absent internal type.

Both root components lose: each omits the other root’s positive weight. Adding the target joins them and collects all positive weight, reaching quota. This construction works for every admissible \(k\).

It also supplies a merging-admissible support, so Section 4 proves that the rate is finite.

Indeed, its value is explicit. Any merging-admissible support must retain all \(h\) positive-weight types and delete at least one type from every variable pair. A satisfying assignment achieves exactly those \(n\) deletions. Therefore

\( R_t= \left(\mu_t+\frac{h+n}{D}\right)\ln2 = \left(1-\frac nD\right)\ln2 \)

for satisfiable formulas.

Complexity conclusion

The reduction establishes

\( \text{formula satisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_t<+\infty. \)

The finiteness decision is also in \(\mathrm{NP}\). For positive-weight targets, Section 3 reduces it to checking \(W(U_t)\ge q\). For zero-weight targets, a support \(A\in\mathcal G_t\) is a certificate: its components, capacities, and adjacency to \(t\) can all be checked in polynomial time.

Hence deciding whether a designated rate is finite is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. An algorithm computing all rates with the required infinity distinction would solve this decision problem even with fixed \(\varepsilon\). Thus the requested computational problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and cannot have the proposed polynomial running time unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Preliminaries and entropy calculation

The quota hypothesis implies \(W>0\) and \(W/2<q\le W\). The correspondence between coalition components and components of the occupied type support is correct: each occupied type induces a connected clique, and adjacency between occupied types is exactly adjacency in \(H\).

The calculation of \(J(S)\) is valid in all three cases:

  • If \(W(S)<q\), the constraint is infeasible.
  • If \(q<W(S)\), the displayed function of \(\lambda\) starts at \(W(S)/2<q\), increases strictly, and converges to \(W(S)>q\). Its derivative is positive because \(W(S)>0\) guarantees a positive-weight type in \(S\).

The entropy inequality and its equality condition are correct, including zero weights and endpoint values of \(p\). Summing it establishes the claimed minimum because \(\lambda>0\) and the weighted-sum constraint is a lower bound.

The binomial probability bounds give the asserted exponent for individual count vectors. The designated-agent deletion changes each normalized population size by at most \(1/k\), which vanishes in the limit. The finitely many initial instances in which an available population is zero do not affect this argument.

The tail estimate is also correct. For the interior case, exponential Markov bounds apply with the stated optimizer. At the boundary \(q=W(S)\), admissibility makes \(kq\) integral, and reaching quota forces selection of all positive-weight agents in \(S\).

2. Identification of \(I_t\)

Both inequalities proving \(I_t=J(U_t)\) hold.

For the upper bound, the proposed choice \(A=T\) is permitted by the task. The optimizer gives selected weight exactly \(q\) in \(U_t\), while every other full component has selected weight at most \(W/2<q\). Thus all feasibility constraints hold.

If \(W(U_t)<q\), both the asserted infeasibility and the resulting infinite value are correct.

3. Positive-weight targets

The probability upper bounds establish the required lower bound on the rate.

When \(q<W(U_t)\), pivotality requires pre-addition selected weight at least \(Q_k-w_t\) within \(U_t\). The moment-generating-function calculation correctly accounts for the omitted target through the term \(-a_t\). The remaining multiplicative factor is at most \(2\).

When \(q=W(U_t)\), this necessary threshold forces selection of every available positive-weight agent in \(U_t\). Their probability is exactly \(2e^{-kJ(U_t)}\).

The matching count constructions are valid:

  • The ceiling formula places the pre-addition weight strictly below quota and the post-addition weight at or above quota.
  • The normalized target count converges to the interior optimizer, so it respects the available population for every sufficiently large admissible \(k\).
  • Every type in \(U_t\) is eventually occupied, ensuring the claimed connectivity.
  • Outside components cannot win because their full capacities satisfy \(W(V)\le W-q<q\).

The boundary construction likewise reaches \(Q_k=kq\) exactly after adding the target. Its entropy cost includes full selection of positive-weight types and half selection of zero-weight types.

These constructions work along every sufficiently large admissible integer, so they establish convergence rather than merely a subsequential bound.

4. Zero-weight targets

The compulsory absence of all other type-\(t\) agents is correctly justified. If type \(t\) is already occupied, adding another zero-weight member supplies neither new connections nor additional weight.

The characterization by merging-admissible supports is sound.

For necessity, the merged selected weight must reach quota. If only one merged component had positive selected weight, that component would already reach quota before addition, contradicting pivotality. Consequently, at least two merged components have positive capacity.

For a fixed support, the absence probability correctly subtracts the unsampled designated agent. The tail event on \(S_t(A)\) is independent of those absences. Summing the resulting bounds over finitely many supports gives the claimed exponent bound with factor \(2^\tau\).

The matching construction checks all necessary conditions:

  • Every retained coordinate is positive.
  • The optimizer gives total merged weight exactly \(q\).
  • At least two merged components receive positive selected weight, so every individual merged component has selected weight strictly below \(q\).
  • Every nonmerged component has capacity strictly below \(q\).
