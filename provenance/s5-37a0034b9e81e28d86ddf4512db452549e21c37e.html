The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of interchangeable radio types \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\), the paper’s channel and utility parameters, a coalition-size bound \(B\), and an initial distribution over coalition patterns and history states, does every asynchronous sequence of legal switches terminate? A legal switch transfers some mass \(\varepsilon>0\) of type \(t\) from a pattern \(Q\) to a pattern \(R\), updating the pattern masses and histories; the mover must strictly improve its per-radio utility, and destination members must not lose utility under the resulting population externalities. Termination means that no legal switch remains.

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) radio types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\ge1\) channels and a bound \(B\ge1\) on the number of roles in a coalition.
Each type supplies a nonempty known-channel set \(K_t\subseteq[m]\) and nonnegative rational gains \(g_{t,k}\) on every channel. Channel availability indicators are independent, with rational probabilities \(\theta_k\in[0,1]\); common rational parameters satisfy \(\widetilde P>0\), \(\sigma^2>0\), and \(0<\alpha\le1/m\). Parameters remain fixed throughout a run.
A coalition template \(Q\) is a tuple of at most \(B\) typed roles; repeated types are allowed. A decorated pattern additionally assigns each role a history of previously departed nonsingleton type multisets. Its mass \(z_Q\) is the mass of coalition copies, so each role contributes \(z_Q\) radio mass; the initial decorated-pattern support is explicitly listed with rational masses satisfying the type-mass constraints. Empty coalitions are discarded.
For each undecorated template and channel, input nonnegative rational coefficients specify measured external interference \(I_{Q,k}(z)=a_{Q,k}+\sum_P c_{Q,P,k}z_P\), where decorated copies of a template are aggregated in this sum. All coefficient tables are explicit.
Members share the union of their known channels. At each rank, every unassigned role proposes its highest-weight unused channel, with weight \(\theta_k g_{t,k}\); uncontested proposals are accepted, and each contested channel goes to its highest-weight proposer. Remaining roles repeat while excluding channels already assigned at that rank; if none remain eligible, a role takes its highest-weight unused channel regardless of conflicts. Fixed channel and role orders resolve ties.
For each availability realization, each role selects its first available channel, or remains idle if none is available. Roles selecting at the same rank form a group \(G\), whose usable channels are the distinct channels selected by its members. A singleton group spends \(\widetilde P\) on its selected channel; every larger group chooses nonnegative powers summing to \(\widetilde P\) for each member to maximize its sum of rates, conditional on the other groups' powers, with rates \(C_{i,k}=\log_2(1+g_{t_i,k}p_{i,k}/(\sigma^2+I_{Q,k}(z)+\sum_{j\ne i}g_{t_j,k}p_{j,k}))\). Powers on unusable channels are zero. Instances are promised to admit a simultaneous group-optimal power profile at every feasible population state; select the lexicographically least such profile.

For its channel order \((k_1,\ldots,k_q)\), define \(s_{Q,i}=\sum_{r=1}^q r\alpha\theta_{k_r}\prod_{h<r}(1-\theta_{k_h})+\prod_{r=1}^q(1-\theta_{k_r})\) and per-radio utility \(u_{Q,i}(z)=(1-s_{Q,i})\overline C_{Q,i}(z)\).
A switch selects a source pattern \(Q\), one type-\(t\) role, and a destination pattern \(R\) or the empty destination. For any feasible real \(\varepsilon>0\), replace \(\varepsilon\) copies of each selected nonempty pattern by \(\varepsilon\) copies of \(Q\) without the mover and \(R\) with the mover, preserving all other roles and histories; selecting the same pattern twice requires \(2\varepsilon\) copies. The resulting patterns must respect \(B\).
The mover carries its history and adds the departed template's type multiset if it was nonsingleton. A nonsingleton destination's resulting type multiset must not already be in that history; singleton destinations are always history-eligible. Evaluated before and after the entire mass transfer, the mover's utility must strictly increase and every destination incumbent's utility must weakly increase. Source incumbents need not consent; histories never reset.
A sequence consists only of legal switches and is maximal if it is infinite or ends at a state admitting no legal switch. Termination means finite termination at such a state, rather than convergence of population masses.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized radio masses to one; coalition-copy masses count once for each occupied role, rather than themselves forming a probability distribution.
  • Interpreted the coalition-size bound as an integer bound on finite typed roles, with continuous masses of coalition copies; switches modify both source and destination copies and preserve radio mass.
  • Used typed roles and fixed role orders to resolve sorting ties among interchangeable radios; histories record underlying type multisets and travel only with the moving role.
  • Allowed every feasible positive real transfer size, including transfers between distinct copies of the same pattern; imposed the corresponding mass-availability constraint.
  • Retained departures into singleton coalitions, the exemption of singletons from history restrictions, and the recording of departed nonsingleton coalitions; excluded environmental changes and history resets during a run.

  • The discrete model treats external interference as measured data without specifying its continuous population response; supplied explicit nonnegative rational affine response tables as additional input.

1. One-channel utility comparisons

Throughout the construction, set
$$ \theta_1=1,\qquad \widetilde P=\sigma^2=1,\qquad \alpha=\frac12, \qquad K_t=\{1\}. $$
Every type has gain either $0$ or $1$.

With one channel, every role selects channel $1$ at rank one. Every active member must put its entire power on that channel. Thus the power profile is uniquely determined, is simultaneously group-optimal, and satisfies the equilibrium-existence promise at every feasible population state.

For an undecorated template $T$, define its effective load by
$$ H_T(z)=1+I_{T,1}(z)+\sum_{i\in T}g_{t_i,1}. $$
A gain-$1$ role has rate denominator $H_T(z)-1$. Its sensing factor is $s_{T,i}=1/2$, so its utility is
$$ u_{T,i}(z)=f(H_T(z)), \qquad f(h)=\frac12\log_2\!\left(\frac{h}{h-1}\right). $$
For $h>1$,
$$ f'(h)=-\frac{1}{2\ln(2)\,h(h-1)}<0. $$
Every gain-$0$ role has utility zero.

Therefore:

  • A gain-$1$ mover strictly improves exactly when its destination load after the transfer is smaller than its source load before the transfer.
  • A gain-$1$ destination incumbent weakly improves exactly when the resulting destination load is at most its previous load.
  • A gain-$0$ role cannot be a legal mover.

These statements account for the entire mass transfer, as required by the task.

2. Reduction from Boolean unsatisfiability

Let
$$ F=C_1\land\cdots\land C_q $$
be a Boolean formula on variables $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, with exactly three literal occurrences in each clause. We assume $n,q\ge1$ and remove unused variables.

We use the standard theorem that satisfiability of such $3$-CNF formulas is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete. Its complement, unsatisfiability, is consequently $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete.

We construct an instance $\mathcal I(F)$ satisfying
$$ F\text{ is unsatisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{every maximal legal-switch sequence in }\mathcal I(F)\text{ terminates}. $$

2.1. Types, gains, and initial masses

For each variable $x_i$, introduce four distinct types:
$$ \mathsf V_i,\quad \mathsf S_i,\quad \mathsf T_i,\quad \mathsf F_i. $$
Their respective gains are $1,0,1,1$. The type $\mathsf V_i$ makes the assignment choice; $\mathsf S_i$ is its initial companion; $\mathsf T_i$ and $\mathsf F_i$ represent its two destinations.

For each clause $C_j$, introduce three distinct types:
$$ \mathsf C_j,\quad \mathsf E_j,\quad \mathsf D_j, $$
with gains $1,0,1$. These implement a clause-certification switch.

Finally, introduce types $\mathsf M,\mathsf Z$, with gains $1,0$, for the nontermination mechanism. All types introduced here are distinct.

Let $N$ be the least power of two satisfying
$$ N\ge 4n+3q+2. $$
Add gain-$0$ padding types until the support size is exactly $\tau=N$, and put
$$ a=\frac1N,\qquad \mu_t=a $$
for every supported type.

All initial histories are empty. Give coalition-copy mass $a$ to each of the following patterns:

| Purpose | Initial patterns |
|---|---|
| Variable $i$ | $(\mathsf V_i,\mathsf S_i)$, $(\mathsf T_i)$, $(\mathsf F_i)$ |
| Clause $j$ | $(\mathsf C_j,\mathsf E_j)$, $(\mathsf D_j)$ |
| Nontermination mechanism | $(\mathsf M,\mathsf Z)$ |
| Each padding type | Its singleton |

Every supported type appears in exactly one initial role. Hence each type contributes radio mass $a$, and the total radio mass is $Na=1$. All type and pattern masses are positive dyadic rationals.

2.2. Aggregate mass coordinates

All coordinates below aggregate both role orders and all histories. Define:

  • $W_i$: mass of pairs with multiset $\{\mathsf V_i,\mathsf S_i\}$;
  • $X_i^+$: mass of pairs with multiset $\{\mathsf V_i,\mathsf T_i\}$;
  • $X_i^-$: mass of pairs with multiset $\{\mathsf V_i,\mathsf F_i\}$;
  • $U_j$: mass of pairs with multiset $\{\mathsf C_j,\mathsf E_j\}$;
  • $Y_j$: mass of pairs with multiset $\{\mathsf C_j,\mathsf D_j\}$.

For a literal $\ell$, write
$$ X(\ell)= \begin{cases} X_i^+,&\ell=x_i,\\ X_i^-,&\ell=\neg x_i. \end{cases} $$
If a literal occurs repeatedly in a clause, it is counted repeatedly.

Set
$$ h_0=q+10,\qquad h_*=h_0+10. $$

2.3. Effective-load tables

Assign the following effective loads to both orders of every listed pair:

| Template multiset $T$ | Effective load $H_T(z)$ |
|---|---|
| $\{\mathsf V_i,\mathsf S_i\}$ | $h_0+1$ |
| $\{\mathsf V_i,\mathsf T_i\}$ | $h_0+NW_i$ |
| $\{\mathsf V_i,\mathsf F_i\}$ | $h_0+NW_i$ |
| $\{\mathsf C_j,\mathsf E_j\}$ | $h_0-\frac12+N\sum_{\ell\in C_j}X(\ell)$ |
| $\{\mathsf C_j,\mathsf D_j\}$ | $h_0+NU_j$ |
| $\{\mathsf M,\mathsf Z\}$ | $h_0-q+\frac12+N\sum_{j=1}^qY_j$ |

The singleton loads are
$$ H_{\{\mathsf T_i\}} =H_{\{\mathsf F_i\}} =H_{\{\mathsf D_j\}} =H_{\{\mathsf M\}} =h_0. $$
Every other singleton, and every unlisted pair, has constant load $h_*$.

These loads specify valid interference tables by setting
$$ I_{T,1}(z)=H_T(z)-1-\sum_{i\in T}g_{t_i,1}. $$
Indeed, all mass coefficients in $H_T$ are nonnegative. Every constant term of $H_T$ is at least
$$ h_0-q+\frac12=\frac{21}{2}, $$
whereas
$$ 1+\sum_{i\in T}g_{t_i,1}\le3. $$
Thus every interference constant is nonnegative as well.

The coefficients refer only to aggregated undecorated-template masses, exactly as required. All tables, including zero entries and unlisted templates, are supplied explicitly.

3. Intended switches and their exact conditions

3.1. Assignment switches consume a whole block

Suppose variable $i$ is unassigned. Its source pair has mass $W_i=a$. Consider moving $\mathsf V_i$ into the singleton $\mathsf T_i$ with transfer amount $\varepsilon$.

After the transfer, the resulting pair has load
$$ h_0+N(a-\varepsilon). $$
The destination incumbent $\mathsf T_i$ previously had singleton load $h_0$. Its consent condition is therefore
$$ h_0+N(a-\varepsilon)\le h_0. $$
This requires $\varepsilon\ge a$. Feasibility requires $\varepsilon\le a$, so
$$ \varepsilon=a. $$
At that amount, the mover’s load decreases from $h_0+1$ to $h_0$, giving strict improvement.

The same calculation applies to destination $\mathsf F_i$.

Thus an assignment switch chooses exactly one of
$$ (X_i^+,X_i^-)=(a,0) \qquad\text{or}\qquad (X_i^+,X_i^-)=(0,a). $$
The mover records $\{\mathsf V_i,\mathsf S_i\}$. Its chosen destination multiset is different, so the switch is history-eligible.

3.2. A clause can be certified exactly when a selected literal satisfies it

Suppose clause $j$ has not been certified, so $U_j=a$. Move $\mathsf C_j$ from its initial pair into singleton $\mathsf D_j$.

The destination incumbent’s consent condition is
$$ h_0+N(a-\varepsilon)\le h_0. $$
Again, feasibility and consent force $\varepsilon=a$.

At that amount, the destination load is $h_0$. Strict mover improvement holds exactly when
$$ h_0<h_0-\frac12+N\sum_{\ell\in C_j}X(\ell), $$
or equivalently,
$$ N\sum_{\ell\in C_j}X(\ell)>\frac12. $$
Each selected literal contributes $a$, and each unselected literal contributes zero. Since $Na=1$, this condition holds exactly when at least one literal of $C_j$ has been selected as true.

The mover records $\{\mathsf C_j,\mathsf E_j\}$, which differs from its resulting destination multiset. The switch is history-eligible.

A certification changes $Y_j$ from zero to $a$.

3.3. The monitor can depart exactly when all clauses have been certified

Let $k$ be the number of certified clauses. Then
$$ N\sum_{j=1}^qY_j=k, $$
and the monitor pair has load
$$ h_0-q+\frac12+k. $$
A departure of $\mathsf M$ to the empty destination produces a singleton of load $h_0$. It is strictly beneficial exactly when
$$ h_0<h_0-q+\frac12+k, $$
that is,
$$ k>q-\frac12. $$
Because $k$ is an integer between zero and $q$, this holds exactly when $k=q$.

When all clauses have been certified, every positive feasible departure amount is legal. There are no destination incumbents, and a singleton destination is always history-eligible.

4. Exhaustive reachable-state analysis

The reduction requires excluding unintended switches. We prove the following invariant and switch classification together by induction.

Every reachable state has this form:

  • Each variable is either unassigned, with its original pair of mass $a$, or assigned, with exactly one chosen destination pair of mass $a$.
  • Each clause is either uncertified, with its original pair of mass $a$, or certified, with its destination pair of mass $a$.
  • A departed variable or clause source companion is a singleton of mass $a$.
  • Unused positive-gain destination companions remain singletons of mass $a$.
  • The monitor consists of original pair mass $r$, singleton $\mathsf M$ mass $a-r$, and singleton $\mathsf Z$ mass $a-r$, for some $r\in[0,a]$.
  • Padding types remain singletons.

An assigned variable mover has history containing its original source multiset. A certified clause mover has the analogous history. Every singleton $\mathsf M$ has history containing $\{\mathsf M,\mathsf Z\}$. All remaining original monitor-pair copies retain empty histories.

The invariant holds initially. Under it, every possible mover belongs to one of the following categories.

4.1. Gain-zero roles

These roles have utility zero in every coalition. None can strictly improve, so none can move legally.

This covers source companions, the monitor companion, and padding types.

4.2. Positive-gain destination companions

Each such role is either a singleton of load $h_0$ or a member of its completed destination pair, also of load $h_0$.

Its only listed pair template contains its designated variable or clause mover. That mover is never a singleton under the invariant. Since $B=2$, it cannot join a pair containing that mover.

Consequently, any feasible nonempty destination produces an unlisted pair of load $h_*>h_0$. A departure to the empty destination gives singleton load $h_0$. Neither possibility strictly improves utility.

Thus these companions never move.

4.3. Variable and clause movers

While in an original source pair, a variable mover has load $h_0+1$. A clause mover has load at most
$$ h_0-\frac12+3Na=h_0+\frac52. $$
Their singleton load is $h_*$, and every unlisted pair has load $h_*$. Because
$$ h_*>h_0+\frac52, $$
neither an empty destination nor an unlisted pair can improve utility.

For a variable mover in its original pair, the only available listed destinations are its two assignment companions. Section 3.1 proves that these are precisely the two whole-block assignment switches.

For a clause mover in its original pair, the only available listed destination is its certification companion. Section 3.2 gives its exact whole-block legality condition.

After assignment, a variable mover has load $h_0$. Its original source multiset is in its history, so returning to that source is forbidden. Entering its alternative assignment pair would also give load $h_0$, because $W_i=0$, and therefore cannot strictly improve utility. Empty and unlisted destinations give load $h_*$.

After certification, a clause mover likewise has load $h_0$. Returning to its original source is history-forbidden, and all other feasible destinations either are unavailable or have load $h_*$.

Thus each variable or clause mover makes at most one legal switch.

4.4. The monitor mover

The original monitor-pair load is at most $h_0+1/2$, which is smaller than $h_*$. Every unlisted destination pair is therefore unprofitable.

Its only listed destination pair has multiset $\{\mathsf M,\mathsf Z\}$. A mover departing the original monitor pair adds this very multiset to its history before the destination test. Hence joining a singleton $\mathsf Z$ is forbidden. It is also forbidden for an existing singleton $\mathsf M$, whose history already contains that multiset.

A singleton $\mathsf M$ cannot improve by moving to the empty destination, because that changes neither its template nor any aggregate population mass.

The only remaining possibility is departure from an original monitor pair to the empty destination. Section 3.3 proves that this is legal exactly when all clauses have been certified.

This completes the switch classification. Every permitted switch preserves the stated invariant, completing the induction.

5. Equivalence with unsatisfiability

5.1. Unsatisfiable formulas give universal finite termination

Suppose $F$ is unsatisfiable.

The assignment switches select consistent truth values: each variable selects at most one value and can never change it. A certification is possible only when some selected literal satisfies that clause.

If every clause were certified, the selected partial assignment would satisfy every clause. Extending it arbitrarily to the remaining variables would then satisfy $F$, contradicting unsatisfiability.

Therefore the monitor is never enabled.

By the exhaustive classification, every legal switch is an assignment or certification switch, and each associated mover can switch at most once. Every legal sequence consequently has length at most
$$ n+q. $$
In particular, no infinite legal sequence exists. Every maximal sequence is finite and, by maximality, ends at a state admitting no legal switch.

Thus every maximal sequence terminates.

5.2. Satisfiable formulas give an infinite legal sequence

Suppose $F$ has a satisfying assignment.

First perform the $n$ assignment switches corresponding to that assignment. Each is legal by Section 3.1. Next certify all $q$ clauses. Each has a selected satisfying literal, so these switches are legal by Section 3.2.

All clauses are now certified. The original monitor pair has mass $a$, load $h_0+1/2$, and empty histories on its remaining copies.

For switch numbers $h=1,2,\ldots$, depart from original monitor-pair copies to the empty destination with amount
$$ \varepsilon_h=a\,2^{-h}. $$
After $H$ such departures, the remaining original monitor-pair mass is
$$ a-\sum_{h=1}^H a\,2^{-h}=a\,2^{-H}>0. $$
Each next departure is feasible. Its mover’s load decreases from $h_0+1/2$ to $h_0$, so it strictly improves. There are no destination incumbents, and the singleton destination is history-eligible.

This is an infinite legal sequence. It is maximal under the task’s definition and does not terminate.

We have proved
$$ F\text{ unsatisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathcal I(F)\text{ has universal finite termination}. $$

6. Input size and complexity consequences

There are initially $4n+3q+2$ nonpadding types, and
$$ 4n+3q+2\le\tau=N<2(4n+3q+2). $$
Thus $\tau=O(n+q)$.

With $m=1$ and $B=2$, the number of ordered undecorated templates is
$$ T=\tau+\tau^2. $$
A dense explicit interference table has $T^2=O(\tau^4)$ coefficient entries. Every nonzero coefficient and constant in the construction has $O(\log\tau)$ bits. The initial support has $O(\tau)$ patterns, all with masses $1/\tau$ and empty histories.

Consequently, the output length and construction time can both be bounded by
$$ O(\tau^4\log\tau). $$
The reduction is polynomial in the binary length of the input formula. It uses actual supported types throughout and does not implicitly enumerate a larger type universe.

It follows that:

  • Universal finite termination is $\mathrm{coNP}$-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions, even with fixed $m=1$ and $B=2$.
  • Existence of an infinite legal sequence is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions.
  • Universal finite termination is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions: construct $\mathcal I(F)$, query universal termination, and negate the answer to decide satisfiability.
  • A polynomial-time algorithm for the full termination problem would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.

On the constructed family, a satisfying assignment is a polynomial-size certificate for failure of universal termination: it specifies a finite legal prefix followed by the explicit geometric monitor sequence. This gives a matching $\mathrm{coNP}$ upper bound on that family, but not on the full model.

7. Immediate terminality remains polynomial for one channel

For completeness, the distinction between immediate terminality and universal termination can be proved directly.

Allow arbitrary rational gains and affine interference, still with $m=1$. Write
$$ H_T(z)=\sigma^2+I_{T,1}(z) +\widetilde P\sum_{i\in T}g_{t_i,1}. $$
The same forced-power calculation gives
$$ u_{T,i}(z) =\theta_1^2(1-\alpha) \log_2\!\left( \frac{H_T(z)} {H_T(z)-\widetilde P g_{t_i,1}} \right). $$
If the prefactor is zero, or the mover’s gain is zero, strict improvement is impossible. Otherwise its utility is strictly decreasing in $H_T$.

Fix a source role and a destination pattern, after checking size and history eligibility. Let $D$ be the resulting destination template and let $\Delta$ be the aggregate template-mass change per unit transfer. Then
$$ H_D(z+\varepsilon\Delta)=H_D(z)+\lambda_D\varepsilon $$
for a rational $\lambda_D$.

Legality is exactly the intersection of:

  • the positive transfer and available-copy constraints;
  • the mover inequality

$$ H_D(z)+\lambda_D\varepsilon<H_Q(z); $$

  • if there is any positive-gain destination incumbent, the consent inequality

$$ H_D(z)+\lambda_D\varepsilon\le H_R(z). $$

Zero-gain incumbents impose no restriction. These are rational linear inequalities in one real variable, whose feasibility is decidable exactly.

If the supplied state contains $S$ decorated patterns and
$$ b=\min\{B,L\}, $$
there are at most $bS(S+1)$ candidate choices. Here $S\le L$, and all listed roles, histories, and coefficient tables are part of the input. Exact rational arithmetic gives a conservative bit-complexity bound
$$ O\!\left((b+1)S(S+1)L^6\right)\subseteq O(L^9). $$
Indeed, each load evaluation sums at most $O(L)$ rational terms; unreduced intermediate numerators and denominators have polynomial bit length, and history checks and interval tests also take polynomial time.

Thus immediate terminality is polynomial-time decidable for $m=1$, while universal finite termination is already $\mathrm{coNP}$-hard for $m=1$, $B=2$.

8. What remains unresolved

The reduction establishes a substantial obstruction to polynomial-time solvability. It does not prove many-one $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness of the universal-termination decision itself: its many-one direction is from unsatisfiability, not satisfiability.

No general upper bound or full-model decision procedure is established here.
