Corollary 5 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite location grid \(L\subseteq[0,1]\), fixed \(K\), and tolerance \(\eta>0\), construct an anonymous direct mechanism mapping every reported distribution \(\nu\in\Delta(L)\) to a distribution over integral facility tuples \(s\in L^K\), with report-dependent integral nearest-facility reactions, such that for every true \(\mu\in\Delta(L)\) truthful reporting is strictly dominant and \(\mathbb{E}[C(\mu,s)]\leq\min_{s\in L^K}C(\mu,s)+\eta\), where \(C(\mu,s)=\sum_{x\in L}\mu_x\min_{j\leq K}|x-s_j|\); determine the mechanism and its complexity in the explicit input size.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Write \(G\subseteq[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q\) for the given location grid, with \(m=|G|\geq2\); fix \(K\geq2\) and a binary rational tolerance \(\eta>0\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) denotes the total input bit length.
An anonymous direct mechanism assigns each reported distribution \(\nu\in\Delta(G)\) a lottery \(P_\nu\) over integral tuples \(s\in G^K\), allowing repeated facility locations. Reports and masses are rational. There are no transfers. An individual is nonatomic, so changing her report leaves \(\nu\) unchanged.
For report \(y\), the mechanism imposes the single facility \(a(y,s)\) nearest to \(y\), breaking distance ties toward the smaller location. A true type \(x\) has utility \(-|x-a(y,s)|\). Strict truthfulness means \(\mathbb E_{s\sim P_\nu}|x-a(x,s)|<\mathbb E_{s\sim P_\nu}|x-a(y,s)|\) for every \(\nu\) and every \(x\ne y\) in \(G\), regardless of others’ reports.
Define \(C(\mu,s)=\sum_{x\in G}\mu_x\min_{1\leq j\leq K}|x-s_j|\). Require \(\mathbb E_{s\sim P_\mu}C(\mu,s)\leq\min_{s\in G^K}C(\mu,s)+\eta\) for every society \(\mu\). Computational evaluation means producing the rational probability table of \(P_\nu\) from the grid, tolerance, and reported support.

For each fixed \(K\geq2\), can such strictly truthful mechanisms achieve every additive tolerance \(\eta>0\), or is a positive additive error unavoidable?
If every tolerance is achievable, can the lotteries be evaluated in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\eta\)?

For finite populations on the uniform grid with \(r+1\) locations, strict truthfulness and additive average-cost error \(\frac{6r}{\sqrt n}\sqrt{\ln\!\left(\frac{n(r+1)^K}{2r^2}\right)}\) are established for sufficiently large \(n\). Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Every positive additive tolerance is achievable. For each fixed \(K\ge2\), the required rational probability table can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), even without dependence on \(1/\eta\).

If \(m\le K\), opening every grid location gives exact optimality and strict truthfulness. The construction below also handles the substantive case \(m>K\). In that case, exact optimality for every society is impossible, although there is no positive lower bound on the achievable additive tolerance.

1. Truthful reporting is weakly optimal for every fixed tuple

For \(x,y\in G\) and \(s\in G^K\), define the excess distance from reporting \(y\) instead of \(x\) by
\(D_{x,y}(s)=|x-a(y,s)|-|x-a(x,s)|\).

By the definition of nearest-facility assignment,
\[ |x-a(x,s)|=\min_{1\le j\le K}|x-s_j|. \]
Since \(a(y,s)\) is one of the facilities in \(s\), it follows that \(D_{x,y}(s)\ge0\).

Consequently, for any fixed lottery over tuples, truthful reporting weakly minimizes expected distance. Distance ties do not affect this argument.

Here the nonatomic assumption is essential: an individual’s deviation leaves \(\nu\), and therefore the lottery \(P_\nu\), unchanged.

2. A lottery that makes every incentive strict

For each ordered pair \(u,v\in G\), let
\(t(u,v)=(u,v,\ldots,v)\in G^K\).
This tuple exists because \(K\ge2\), and repetitions are permitted.

Let \(Q\) assign probability \(1/m^2\) to each of these tuples. The tuples are distinct because their first two coordinates identify their ordered pairs.

Fix distinct \(x,y\in G\). At the tuple \(t(x,y)\), reporting \(x\) assigns the facility at \(x\), while reporting \(y\) assigns the facility at \(y\). Thus
\(D_{x,y}(t(x,y))=|x-y|>0\).

All other tuples have nonnegative excess distance by Section 1. Therefore
\[ \mathbb E_{s\sim Q}D_{x,y}(s)\ge\frac{|x-y|}{m^2}>0. \]

This argument covers all grid types, including types absent from the reported support.

3. Construction and additive-cost guarantee

Set \(\varepsilon=\min\{\eta,1/2\}\), which is a positive rational number.

For every rational reported distribution \(\nu\), choose a tuple \(s^\star_\nu\) minimizing \(C(\nu,s)\) over \(G^K\). Resolve multiple minimizers by a fixed lexicographic rule. Define
\(P_\nu=(1-\varepsilon)\delta_{s^\star_\nu}+\varepsilon Q\),
where \(\delta_s\) denotes the point mass at \(s\).

This mechanism is anonymous because its lottery depends only on the reported distribution.

For every \(\nu\) and distinct \(x,y\), Section 1 gives a nonnegative incentive contribution from the optimal tuple, and Section 2 gives
\[ \mathbb E_{s\sim P_\nu}D_{x,y}(s) \ge\varepsilon\frac{|x-y|}{m^2}>0. \]
Hence the mechanism satisfies the stated strict-truthfulness requirement, regardless of others’ reports.

Now fix any society \(\mu\), and write
\(c^\star_\mu=\min_{s\in G^K}C(\mu,s)\).
Every facility distance lies in \([0,1]\), and the masses sum to \(1\). Therefore \(0\le C(\mu,s)\le1\) for every tuple. It follows that
\[ \mathbb E_{s\sim P_\mu}C(\mu,s)-c^\star_\mu =\varepsilon\bigl(\mathbb E_{s\sim Q}C(\mu,s)-c^\star_\mu\bigr) \le\varepsilon \le\eta. \]

Thus the same mechanism achieves the required guarantee for every society.

4. Exact polynomial-time evaluation

Given the reported support, evaluate each of the \(m^K\) tuples using
\(C(\nu,s)=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)}\nu_x\min_j|x-s_j|\).
Each evaluation takes \(O(\tau K)\) rational arithmetic operations. Exact comparison of these costs, together with the fixed tie rule, determines \(s^\star_\nu\).

The arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity. To see this explicitly, write each grid coordinate and each reported mass as a rational with a positive integer denominator. Let \(B_G\) be the product of all grid-coordinate denominators, and let \(B_\nu\) be the product of the reported-mass denominators. Every grid distance has denominator dividing \(B_G\), so every weighted distance, and hence every tuple cost, has denominator dividing \(B_GB_\nu\).

The binary length of this product is \(O(L)\). Since each cost lies in \([0,1]\), its numerator over this common denominator also has \(O(L)\) bits. Thus all cost evaluations and comparisons can be performed exactly with polynomially many bit operations.

To output the lottery, give each tuple \(t(u,v)\) probability \(\varepsilon/m^2\), and add probability \(1-\varepsilon\) to \(s^\star_\nu\). If the optimal tuple is already among those tuples, combine the two contributions in its table entry. The table has at most \(m^2+1\) positive entries, each with polynomial encoding length. If a table including zero-probability entries is required, it has \(m^K\) entries, which is still polynomial for fixed \(K\).

The resulting running time is
\(O(m^K\tau K)\) arithmetic operations with polynomial bit overhead. This proves the requested polynomial bound in \(m,\tau,L,1/\eta\), and in fact proves the stronger bound independent of the numerical magnitude of \(1/\eta\).

5. Why exact optimality can nevertheless fail

Suppose \(m>K\). Choose a subset \(A\subset G\) with exactly \(K\) locations, and a society \(\mu\) giving positive mass to every location in \(A\) and zero mass elsewhere.

Its optimal cost is zero: place one facility at each location of \(A\). Conversely, any zero-cost tuple must contain every location of \(A\), because each has positive mass. Since there are exactly \(K\) facility slots, such a tuple has facility set precisely \(A\).

If a mechanism were exactly optimal at this society, its lottery would be supported entirely on these zero-cost tuples: costs are nonnegative, so expected cost zero precludes positive probability on any positive-cost tuple.

Choose \(b\in G\setminus A\), and let \(c\in A\) be its nearest location in \(A\), using the prescribed tie rule. For every tuple in that lottery, reporting either \(b\) or \(c\) assigns the same location \(c\). A true type \(b\) therefore obtains equal expected distance from those two reports, contradicting strict truthfulness.

For \(m\le K\), the opposite conclusion holds: a tuple containing every grid location, padded by repetitions, has zero cost for every society. Truthful reporting gives distance zero, while any distinct report \(y\) gives distance \(|x-y|>0\).

Thus exact optimality is possible precisely when \(m\le K\); arbitrarily small positive error is achievable in all the stipulated cases.

The construction uses the optimal/elicitation lottery pattern described by [Nissim, Smorodinsky and Tennenholtz](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1004.2888). Its guarantees here follow directly from the nonatomic assumption and the preceding proof.

5. Exact-optimality characterization

For \(m>K\), a society supported positively on exactly \(K\) locations has optimal cost zero. A tuple has zero cost only if it contains every supported location. With exactly \(K\) slots, its facility set must therefore equal that support.

Nonnegative costs and the finite outcome space justify the conclusion that an exactly optimal lottery assigns positive probability only to such tuples.

The chosen outside location \(b\) and its tie-broken nearest supported location \(c\) receive the same assignment under every tuple in that lottery. Using an absent type here is legitimate because the task expressly requires strictness for all grid types at every distribution.

For \(m\le K\), a tuple containing every grid location gives zero social cost and strictly distinguishes every pair of reports. Padding by repetitions is permitted. Both directions of the characterization are established.
