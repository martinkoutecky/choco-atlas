The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given slot categories \(E\), valuation types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and type valuations \(v_t:2^E\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) that are \(\{a,b\}\)-bivalued submodular with \(a\mid b\), choose masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) assigned to whole bundles \(S\subseteq E\), subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:g\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_g\). First maximize the mass receiving positive utility, \(\sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S}\); among those solutions, maximize \(\sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(E\) contain \(m\ge1\) slot categories. A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of its \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Supplies \(q_g\ge0\) are binary rationals measured per capita. Common binary integers satisfy \(0<a<b\) and \(a\mid b\).
Each type has a submodular valuation \(v_t:2^E\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), with \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\) and \(v_t(S\cup\{g\})-v_t(S)\in\{a,b\}\) for every \(g\notin S\). Valuations are supplied by Boolean circuits mapping bundle incidence vectors to binary values, promised to satisfy these conditions.
An allocation assigns masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) to whole bundles \(S\subseteq E\), with \(\sum_{S\subseteq E}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:g\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_g\). A bundle contains at most one slot of each category; empty bundles and unused supply are allowed.
Lexicographically maximize \((A(x),B(x))\), where \(A(x)=\sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S}\) and \(B(x)=\sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S)\). Any maximizing allocation is acceptable. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including the valuation circuits.

Is computing an exact maximizing allocation solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many agents and indivisible goods, exact MNW—first maximizing the number of positive utilities, then their product—is computable in polynomial time for these valuations when \(a\mid b\), with polynomial-time valuation evaluation ([Cousins, Viswanathan and Zick](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03087v3)).
No verified prior classification of this precise continuous problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive type masses to sum to one, interpreting the society as a distribution and supplies as per-capita quantities.
  • Required common positive integers 0<a<b with a dividing b, and normalized every valuation at the empty bundle, as required by the discrete valuation class.
  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational supplies; zero supplies and unused capacity remain permitted.
  • Used succinct Boolean circuits for valuation evaluation, with their descriptions included in L, to make the polynomial-time versus NP-hard question a standard binary-input question without requiring exponential valuation tables.
  • Interpreted bundles as subsets with at most one slot per category, allowed empty bundles, and imposed no additional tie-breaking among objective maximizers.
  • Made exact optimization the computational problem and required nonempty society support and at least one slot category.

Status and new partial results

I do not resolve the classification for arbitrary $m$. I prove two further results:

  • An exact maximizing allocation can be computed in time

$$ (m+1)^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L). $$
The exponent in the polynomial is absolute. In particular, exact optimization is polynomial-time solvable whenever
$$ m\log(m+1)=O(\log L). $$

  • Exact optimization is polynomial-time solvable for unrestricted $m$ and $\tau$ under the additional promise that every valuation depends only on bundle cardinality. Equivalently, each associated matroid is uniform.

The first result avoids bounds on general linear forms in logarithms. It uses an elementary lower bound for nonzero reduced costs, whose integer coefficients can be bounded independently of $\tau$ and of the input denominators. The second result uses a circulation formulation whose improving cycles require only polynomial-size product comparisons.

Both results hold without the assumption $a\mid b$.

1. Preliminary reductions

Write
$$ \bar q_g=\min\{q_g,1\}, \qquad Q=\sum_{g\in E}\bar q_g. $$
Replacing $q_g$ by $\bar q_g$ preserves the feasible set: the total mass receiving any category is at most the total population mass, which is one.

1.1 The first objective

Every nonempty bundle has positive value, because each marginal gain is at least $a>0$. Consequently,
$$ A(x)=\sum_{t,S\ne\varnothing}x_{t,S}. $$
For every feasible allocation,
$$ A(x)\le 1, \qquad A(x)\le \sum_{t,S}|S|x_{t,S}\le Q. $$

Conversely, choose rational numbers $\lambda_g$ satisfying
$$ 0\le\lambda_g\le\bar q_g, \qquad \sum_g\lambda_g=\min\{1,Q\}. $$
They can be chosen greedily. The allocation
$$ x_{t,\{g\}}=\mu_t\lambda_g, \qquad x_{t,\varnothing}=\mu_t\left(1-\sum_g\lambda_g\right) $$
is feasible and attains positive-utility mass $\min\{1,Q\}$. Therefore,
$$ A^*=\min\{1,Q\}. $$

Let $P^*$ denote the feasible allocation polytope with the additional equality $A(x)=A^*$. The remaining problem is to maximize $B$ over $P^*$.

1.2 Matroid-rank representation

For each type, define
$$ r_t(S)=\frac{v_t(S)-a|S|}{b-a}. $$
Along an ordering of $S$, this expression counts the marginal gains equal to $b$. Thus $r_t$ is integer-valued, $r_t(\varnothing)=0$, and its marginals belong to $\{0,1\}$. It is submodular because subtracting a modular function and dividing by a positive constant preserve submodularity.

Here is a verification that $r_t$ is a matroid rank function. Suppress the type subscript and define
$$ \mathcal I=\{I\subseteq E:r(I)=|I|\}. $$
The marginal bound gives $r(S)\le |S|$. If $J\subseteq I\in\mathcal I$, then
$$ |I|=r(I)\le r(J)+|I\setminus J| \le |J|+|I\setminus J|=|I|. $$
Hence $J\in\mathcal I$.

For augmentation, let $I,J\in\mathcal I$ with $|I|<|J|$. If every element of $J\setminus I$ had zero marginal at $I$, submodularity would imply $r(I\cup J)=r(I)$. Monotonicity would then give
$$ |J|=r(J)\le r(I\cup J)=|I|, $$
a contradiction. Thus some element of $J\setminus I$ augments $I$.

Finally, scan any set $S$, retaining an element exactly when it increases the rank of the retained set. The retained set $I$ satisfies $r(I)=|I|$. Every discarded element has zero marginal at the final retained set, by submodularity. Therefore $r(S)=r(I)=|I|$. Conversely, every independent subset of $S$ has size at most $r(S)$ by monotonicity. This identifies $r$ with the rank function of the matroid $\mathcal I$.

Consequently,
$$ v_t(S)=a|S|+(b-a)r_t(S). $$
The valuation circuit supplies a polynomial-time rank oracle.

2. Exact optimization for rational surrogate scores

We first justify the rational optimization subroutine used later.

Suppose rational scores $F_t(k,i)$ are given for $0\le i\le k\le m$, with $F_t(k,i)$ nondecreasing in $i$ for each fixed $k$, and $F_t(0,0)=0$. Consider maximizing
$$ \sum_{t,S}F_t(|S|,r_t(S))x_{t,S} $$
over $P^*$.

2.1 Bundle pricing

Fix a matroid rank function $r$, rational category prices $p_g$, and integers $0\le i\le k\le m$. We can minimize
$$ \sum_{g\in S}p_g $$
subject to $|S|=k$ and $r(S)\ge i$ in polynomial time.

Indeed, set $j=k-i$ and define
$$ \mathcal J_{k,i} = \{U\subseteq E:|U|\le k,\ |U|-r(U)\le j\}. $$
Nullity $|U|-r(U)$ is monotone under inclusion, because every rank increment is at most one. Thus this family is hereditary.

For augmentation, take $U,V\in\mathcal J_{k,i}$ with $|U|<|V|$. If $|U|-r(U)<j$, adding any element of $V\setminus U$ preserves membership. Otherwise $|U|-r(U)=j$. If no element of $V\setminus U$ increased rank at $U$, then
$$ r(V)\le r(U\cup V)=r(U), $$
and hence
$$ |V|-r(V)\ge |V|-r(U)>|U|-r(U)=j, $$
a contradiction. A rank-increasing element therefore augments $U$.

Thus $\mathcal J_{k,i}$ is a matroid. If $r(E)\ge i$, an independent set of size $i$ can be enlarged to cardinality $k$; the resulting set belongs to $\mathcal J_{k,i}$. Its bases are therefore precisely the requested bundles. Minimum-weight matroid-basis greedy computes their minimum price. If $r(E)<i$, the requested bundle is infeasible.

Let this minimum price be $C_{t,k,i}(p)$. Then
$$ \max_{S\subseteq E} \left(F_t(|S|,r_t(S))-\sum_{g\in S}p_g\right) = \max_{k,i}\left(F_t(k,i)-C_{t,k,i}(p)\right), $$
omitting infeasible pairs.

To prove equality, a minimum-price bundle for $(k,i)$ has actual rank at least $i$, so its actual score is at least $F_t(k,i)$. Conversely, for any bundle $S$, use $k=|S|$ and $i=r_t(S)$; the corresponding minimum price is at most the price of $S$.

There are $O(m^2)$ pairs, and greedy uses $O(m)$ rank evaluations per pair. The demand computation therefore takes polynomial time and returns an actual maximizing bundle.

2.2 The LP oracle and recovery of an allocation

Introduce supply slacks. The surrogate primal has
$$ d=\tau+m+1 $$
equality rows: one per type, one per category, and the equality $A=A^*$. All its column entries belong to $\{0,1\}$.

Its dual is
$$ \min\left\{ \sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t+\sum_g\bar q_gp_g+A^*\beta \right\}, $$
subject to $p_g\ge0$ and
$$ \alpha_t+\beta\,\mathbf 1_{\{S\ne\varnothing\}} +\sum_{g\in S}p_g \ge F_t(|S|,r_t(S)). $$
The variables $\alpha_t$ and $\beta$ are unrestricted. The pricing algorithm separates these constraints exactly at rational dual points.

For clarity, the generally unbounded dual can be reduced to the bounded, full-dimensional setting of the rational-polytope optimization and dual-recovery theorem. I use that standard theorem, including recovery of an optimal nonnegative combination of defining inequalities; see [Grötschel, Lovász and Schrijver, Section 3, particularly Theorem 3.10](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/10046/10046D.pdf).

Here are the details of the reduction.

The dual has no lines. A line direction must have zero $p$ coordinates because of the constraints $p_g\ge0$. Empty-bundle constraints then force its $\alpha$ coordinates to be zero, and any singleton constraint forces its $\beta$ coordinate to be zero. Thus the dual is pointed.

The primal is feasible and bounded, so the dual has a finite attained optimum. Its optimal face, being pointed, contains a vertex. If the score encoding length is at most $H$, Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s inequality give an effective bound
$$ 2^{\operatorname{poly}(d,H)} $$
on the absolute coordinates of every dual vertex: its defining coefficient matrix has integer entries of absolute value at most one, and its right-hand sides are rational scores.

Choose a rational $R$, with polynomial encoding length, strictly larger than this bound and than the coordinates of the following strict feasible point:
$$ p_g=1,\qquad \beta=0,\qquad \alpha_t=M+2, $$
where $M$ bounds the absolute values of all scores. Intersect the dual with the box $[-R,R]^d$. This is a bounded, full-dimensional rational polytope with a known interior point and a rational interior ball of polynomial encoding length. Separation remains polynomial-time.

The box preserves the optimum, and an original optimal dual vertex lies strictly inside it. In any optimal dual-recovery certificate for the boxed problem, the multipliers of the box inequalities must therefore be zero: complementary slackness applied at that strictly interior optimal vertex forces this. The remaining certificate is exactly an optimal solution of the original surrogate primal, including its supply slacks.

We can return an optimal basic feasible allocation, with at most $d$ positive allocation variables. Thus:

Rational-surrogate lemma. The surrogate problem is exactly solvable in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,H$.

3. An exact algorithm with explicit exponential dependence on $m$

The main improvement is to bound reduced-cost comparisons independently of $\tau$ and of the masses’ denominators.

3.1 A small determinant controls every basis

Write the standard-form constraint matrix as $C$. An allocation column for $(t,S)$ consists of:

  • the type vector $e_t$;
  • the category-incidence vector $\mathbf 1_S$;
  • the entry $\mathbf 1_{\{S\ne\varnothing\}}$.

A supply-slack column has a single one in its category row.

This matrix has full row rank: empty-bundle columns span the type rows, slack columns span the supply rows, and a singleton column supplies the remaining $A$ direction.

Set
$$ h=m+1, \qquad \Delta=h^h, \qquad N=2(h+1)\Delta. $$

Consider any nonsingular basis matrix $C_{\mathcal B}$. It must contain an allocation column for every type, or that type’s row would be zero. Choose one such column as an anchor for each type. There are $\tau$ anchors and $h$ other basis columns.

Subtract the corresponding anchor from each nonanchor allocation column, leaving slack columns unchanged. After ordering columns, the transformed basis matrix is
$$ \begin{pmatrix} I_\tau&0\\ R&H \end{pmatrix}, $$
where $H$ is an $h\times h$ integer matrix with entries in $\{-1,0,1\}$. These column operations preserve the determinant, so
$$ \det C_{\mathcal B}=\det H, \qquad 0<|\det H|\le h^{h/2}\le\Delta. $$

For any candidate column $j$, subtract its type anchor if it is an allocation column. Its remaining lower part is a vector $z_j\in\{-1,0,1\}^h$. Its coefficients on the nonanchor basis columns are
$$ \eta_\ell = \frac{\det H_\ell(z_j)}{\det H}, $$
where $H_\ell(z_j)$ replaces column $\ell$ of $H$ by $z_j$. Every numerator has absolute value at most $\Delta$.

3.2 Integer logarithmic forms for reduced costs

Let $c_j$ be the true objective coefficient: $\log v_t(S)$ for nonempty allocation columns, and zero for empty or slack columns.

For an allocation column $j$ of type $t$, its reduced cost is
$$ \rho_j = c_j-c_{\mathrm{anchor}(t)} -\sum_{\ell=1}^{h} \eta_\ell \left(c_\ell-c_{\mathrm{anchor}(\mathrm{type}(\ell))}\right). $$
For a slack column, the initial anchor term is absent. A nonanchor slack column contributes its own cost zero without an anchor subtraction.

Multiplying by $\det H$ gives an integer linear form in logarithms:
$$ (\det H)\rho_j=\sum_u n_u\log u, \qquad \sum_u|n_u|\le N. $$
Indeed, the initial difference contributes at most $2\Delta$ to the coefficient sum, and each of the $h$ remaining differences contributes at most $2\Delta$.

Every logarithm base is a positive integer at most
$$ U=bm. $$

This bound involves neither $\tau$ nor the right-hand-side denominators.

3.3 An elementary separation bound

Suppose
$$ \Gamma=\sum_u n_u\log u\ne0, \qquad \sum_u|n_u|\le N, \qquad 1\le u\le U. $$
Define integers
$$ P_+=\prod_{n_u>0}u^{n_u}, \qquad P_-=\prod_{n_u<0}u^{-n_u}. $$
Both are at most $U^N$, and $\Gamma=\log(P_+/P_-)$.

If $P_+>P_-$, then
$$ \Gamma \ge \frac{P_+-P_-}{P_+} \ge \frac1{P_+} \ge U^{-N}, $$
using $\log z\ge(z-1)/z$ for $z\ge1$. Interchanging the products proves the same bound for $|\Gamma|$ when $P_- > P_+$. Therefore,
$$ \Gamma\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |\Gamma|\ge U^{-N}. $$

No theorem about linear forms in logarithms is needed.

3.4 Rounding preserves every strict reduced-cost sign

Approximate every possible positive-bundle logarithm to error at most
$$ \delta=\frac{1}{4NU^N}. $$
The possible utility values belong to
$$ \mathcal U = \{ak+(b-a)i:1\le k\le m,\ 0\le i\le k\}, $$
a set of size $O(m^2)$.

Sort these utilities, clamp the approximations below at zero, and take successive prefix maxima. This gives rational approximations $\widehat\ell(u)$ that remain within $\delta$ of $\log u$ and are nondecreasing in $u$. To verify the upper error bound, every preceding utility $w\le u$ has approximation at most $\log w+\delta\le\log u+\delta$. The lower bound follows from retaining the approximation at $u$ itself.

Use the rational surrogate coefficients
$$ \widehat c_{t,S} = \widehat\ell\bigl(a|S|+(b-a)r_t(S)\bigr) $$
for nonempty bundles, and zero for empty bundles and slacks.

For every basis and every column, the determinant-scaled reduced cost changes by at most
$$ N\delta=\frac14U^{-N}. $$
Hence every nonzero true reduced cost retains its sign. Exact zero reduced costs may acquire either sign; this causes no difficulty below.

Solve this rational surrogate exactly and return an optimal vertex.

We need the following standard LP fact: every optimal vertex of a full-row-rank standard-form LP admits a basis with all reduced costs nonpositive for maximization. Here it follows directly from duality. Choose an optimal dual vertex. Its tight columns span the row space. Complementary slackness makes every positive column of the chosen primal vertex tight, and those positive columns are linearly independent. Extend them to a basis using tight columns. This basis represents the chosen primal vertex, and its reduced costs are nonpositive.

Apply this fact to the surrogate optimum. If its resulting basis had a strictly positive true reduced cost, sign preservation would give a strictly positive surrogate reduced cost, a contradiction. Thus this basis also has all true reduced costs nonpositive. Its allocation is therefore an exact maximizer of $B$ over $P^*$.

3.5 Running time and output size

The precision required is
$$ \log_2(1/\delta) = O\bigl(N\log U+\log N\bigr), $$
and
$$ N=2(m+2)(m+1)^{m+1}. $$

Integer logarithms can be approximated in time polynomial in their input length and the requested precision. For completeness, write $u=2^s z$ with $1\le z<2$ and use
$$ \log z = 2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \frac{w^{2j+1}}{2j+1}, \qquad w=\frac{z-1}{z+1}. $$
Here $0\le w<1/3$, so the tail decreases geometrically. The same series computes $\log2$ using $w=1/3$. Accounting for multiplication by $s$, a polynomial number of rational-arithmetic operations suffices at the required precision.

The rational-surrogate lemma now gives the claimed running time:
$$ (m+1)^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L). $$

The returned allocation has at most $\tau+m+1$ positive type–bundle entries. Its masses also have short rational encodings. Let $D$ be the product of the input denominators; it clears $\mu_t$, $\bar q_g$, and $A^*$. The basis elimination above shows that every basic coordinate has denominator dividing
$$ D\det H. $$
Allocation coordinates and clipped-supply slacks lie between zero and one. Their encoding lengths are therefore
$$ O\bigl(L+m\log(m+1)\bigr). $$

Proven conclusion. Exact optimization is polynomial-time solvable on every family satisfying $m\log(m+1)=O(\log L)$, even when $\tau$ is unrestricted.

4. A polynomial-time case with unrestricted $m$: cardinality valuations

Assume now the additional promise that each $v_t(S)$ depends only on $|S|$.

Submodularity makes its cardinality marginals nonincreasing. Since every marginal is either $b$ or $a$, there is an integer $R_t\in\{0,\ldots,m\}$ such that
$$ v_t(S)=u_t(|S|), \qquad u_t(k)=ak+(b-a)\min\{k,R_t\}. $$
Thus the associated rank function is that of a uniform matroid. The parameter $R_t=r_t(E)$ is obtained by one circuit evaluation.

We give an exact polynomial-time algorithm for this subclass.

4.1 A circulation formulation

Construct a directed network with source node $s$, sink node $z$, one node for each category, and one node for each type.

Its arcs are:

  • $s\to g$, of capacity $\bar q_g$;
  • $g\to t$, of capacity $\mu_t$, for every category and type;
  • $m$ parallel arcs $t\to z$, indexed by $k=1,\ldots,m$, each of capacity $\mu_t$;
  • $z\to s$, of capacity $m$.

All arcs except the type-to-sink arcs have objective pair $(0,0)$. Give the type-to-sink arcs the lexicographic objective pairs
$$ (1,\log u_t(1)) \quad\text{for }k=1, $$
and
$$ \left(0,\log\frac{u_t(k)}{u_t(k-1)}\right) \quad\text{for }k\ge2. $$

Every allocation induces a feasible circulation. Set
$$ z_{t,g}=\sum_{S:g\in S}x_{t,S}, \qquad y_{t,k}=\sum_{S:|S|\ge k}x_{t,S}. $$
Use $z_{t,g}$ on $g\to t$ and $y_{t,k}$ on layer $k$ of $t\to z$. Conservation at type $t$ follows from
$$ \sum_gz_{t,g} = \sum_S|S|x_{t,S} = \sum_{k=1}^m y_{t,k}. $$
The capacities $z_{t,g}\le\mu_t$ enforce the one-slot-per-category condition at the marginal level.

The first objective coordinate of this circulation is $A(x)$. Telescoping logarithms shows that the second is $B(x)$.

4.2 Exact lexicographic circulation optimization

Let
$$ n=m+\tau+2, \qquad U=bm, \qquad \ell=\left\lceil\log_2(U+1)\right\rceil, \qquad C=2n\ell. $$
Replace an arc’s objective pair $(\alpha,\beta)$ provisionally by the real scalar cost $C\alpha+\beta$.

Consider any simple residual cycle. Its length is at most $n$. Its primary cost $\alpha_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ is an integer, and its secondary cost satisfies
$$ |\beta_{\mathrm{cyc}}|\le n\log U. $$
Thus, when $\alpha_{\mathrm{cyc}}\ne0$, the sign of
$$ C\alpha_{\mathrm{cyc}}+\beta_{\mathrm{cyc}} $$
is the sign of $\alpha_{\mathrm{cyc}}$.

When $\alpha_{\mathrm{cyc}}=0$, the secondary cost is an integer linear form in logarithms with coefficient sum at most $2n$: each arc contributes at most two logarithms, with signs reversed on reverse residual arcs. The elementary product argument from Section 3 therefore gives
$$ \beta_{\mathrm{cyc}}\ne0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |\beta_{\mathrm{cyc}}|\ge U^{-2n}. $$

Approximate each secondary arc cost by a rational number to error at most
$$ \eta=\frac{1}{4nU^{2n}}. $$
The resulting scalar cost of a simple residual cycle differs from its real scalar cost by at most $n\eta$. Consequently, every lexicographically improving simple residual cycle has strictly positive rounded scalar cost.

The precision required is
$$ O(n\log U+\log n), $$
which is polynomial in the input length. Solve the resulting polynomial-size rational circulation LP exactly.

Its optimum has no positive rounded-cost residual cycle. Hence it has no lexicographically improving residual cycle.

To justify that this implies lexicographic optimality, subtract the returned circulation from any competing circulation. Orient positive differences forward and negative differences backward. Conservation allows this difference to be decomposed into nonnegative multiples of simple residual cycles. If no cycle is lexicographically positive, every cycle has nonpositive primary cost. A competing circulation with larger primary objective is therefore impossible. If the primary objectives are equal, every cycle used with positive weight must have primary cost zero; their secondary costs are then all nonpositive. A larger secondary objective is also impossible.

Thus the computed circulation is exactly lexicographically optimal.

4.3 Optimal layer flows are consecutive

For $k\ge2$, the secondary layer costs strictly decrease with $k$.

Indeed, writing
$$ d_k=u_t(k)-u_t(k-1), $$
we have $d_k\ge d_{k+1}>0$, and
$$ u_t(k)^2-u_t(k-1)u_t(k+1) = u_t(k)(d_k-d_{k+1})+d_kd_{k+1}>0. $$
Therefore,
$$ \log\frac{u_t(k)}{u_t(k-1)} > \log\frac{u_t(k+1)}{u_t(k)}. $$
The first layer strictly precedes every later layer lexicographically because its primary cost is one.

If an earlier layer were not full while a later layer carried positive flow, shifting flow between those two parallel arcs would improve the objective. This is impossible at the computed lexicographic optimum.

Hence, writing
$$ \sum_gz_{t,g}=\mu_t(k_t+\theta_t), \qquad k_t\in\{0,\ldots,m\}, \qquad 0\le\theta_t<1, $$
the layer flows are $\mu_t$ through layer $k_t$, then $\mu_t\theta_t$ through the next layer when it exists, and zero thereafter.

4.4 Recovering whole bundles

It remains to realize these marginal flows by whole bundles.

Fix a type and put
$$ p_g=\frac{z_{t,g}}{\mu_t}. $$
Then $0\le p_g\le1$ and $\sum_gp_g=k+\theta$. Order the categories and define prefix sums
$$ s_0=0, \qquad s_j=\sum_{g=1}^jp_g. $$

For $u$ uniformly distributed in $(0,1)$, include category $g$ precisely when the half-open interval
$$ [s_{g-1},s_g) $$
contains a point of the lattice $u+\mathbb Z$.

An interval has length at most one, so it contains at most one such point, apart from irrelevant endpoints. Its inclusion probability is exactly its length $p_g$: reduction modulo one preserves its length and is injective except at endpoints.

The union of the intervals is $[0,k+\theta)$. It contains $k+1$ lattice points when $u<\theta$, and $k$ otherwise. The resulting bundle therefore has cardinality $k+1$ with probability $\theta$ and $k$ with probability $1-\theta$.

The bundle changes only when $u$ crosses a fractional part of a prefix sum. Consequently, this construction gives at most $m+1$ bundles, with rational probabilities computable exactly. Multiplying those probabilities by $\mu_t$ yields the required allocation masses.

The construction realizes the category marginals $z_{t,g}$ and exactly the consecutive layer flows described above. Thus its objectives equal those of the optimal circulation.

Every original allocation maps to a feasible circulation with identical objectives, while the computed optimal circulation maps back to a feasible allocation with identical objectives. This proves:

Cardinality-valuation theorem. Under the additional promise that every valuation depends only on bundle cardinality, an exact lexicographically maximizing allocation is computable in polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$.

5. What remains open

The general exact classification remains unresolved.

The first theorem supplies an unconditional exact algorithm, but its precision requirement contains
$$ N=2(m+2)(m+1)^{m+1}. $$
This produces an exponential dependence on $m$. The bound does not show that such precision is necessary.

The circulation theorem removes that dependence for uniform matroids because simple residual cycles have coefficients bounded by one and polynomial length. I have not proved an analogous formulation or optimality criterion for arbitrary matroid-rank valuations.

Accordingly, neither a general polynomial-time exact algorithm nor an NP-hardness reduction is established here.
