The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite EIP set \(M\), a finite support \(T\) of service-provider types, rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), type budgets \(b_t>0\), valuations \(v_{tj}\ge 0\), and per-capita capacities \(c_j>0\), compute a Fisher equilibrium: prices \(p_j\ge 0\) and per-member allocations \(x_{tj}\ge 0\) such that each type's allocation maximizes \(\sum_{j\in M} v_{tj}x_{tj}\) subject to its budget, aggregate demand satisfies \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_{tj}\le c_j\), positive-price EIPs clear capacity, and type budgets are spent. For the paper's linear utilities, this is represented by maximizing \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t b_t\log(\sum_{j\in M}v_{tj}x_{tj})\) subject to those capacity constraints and nonnegative allocations.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) service-provider types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge 1\) divisible goods indexed by a finite set \(M\), representing edge infrastructure providers' resources. Input budgets \(b_t>0\), per-capita capacities \(c_j>0\), and per-unit valuations \(v_{tj}\ge 0\) are binary rationals; \(L\) is the total input bit length. Every type values some good positively, and every good is valued positively by some type.
An equilibrium consists of prices \(p_j\ge 0\) per resource unit and per-member allocations \(x_{tj}\ge 0\). For each \(t\), the bundle \(x_t\) maximizes \(\sum_j v_{tj}z_j\) over \(z\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_j p_jz_j\le b_t\), and satisfies \(\sum_jp_jx_{tj}=b_t\); any maximizing bundle is permitted.
Require \(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}\le c_j\) and \(p_j(c_j-\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj})=0\) for every \(j\). Compute an exact equilibrium.

Is computing this equilibrium possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is the problem NP-hard?

For finitely listed buyers with divisible resources, an Eisenberg–Gale optimum and its capacity multipliers give equilibrium allocations and prices, with Pareto-optimal allocations.
Exact equilibrium computation for finite linear Fisher markets is polynomial-time solvable. [Devanur et al.](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/market.pdf)
A continuous Fisher-market result establishes equilibrium and optimization properties for finitely many buyers and a continuum of goods; that is a different population model. [Gao and Kroer](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16684)

Reduction to a finite Fisher market

Construct a market with one buyer for each \(t\in T\) and one unit of each good \(j\in M\). Give buyer \(t\) budget and valuations
\[ B_t=\mu_tb_t,\qquad a_{tj}=c_jv_{tj}. \]
Here and below, these displayed formulas mean \(B_t=\mu_tb_t\) and \(a_{tj}=c_jv_{tj}\).

For clarity, write the remaining formulas using inline delimiters. If \(q_j\) denotes the price of a normalized good and \(w_{tj}\) its allocation to buyer \(t\), define the corresponding society prices and allocations by

\(p_j=q_j/c_j,\qquad x_{tj}=c_jw_{tj}/\mu_t.\)

All divisions are valid because \(c_j>0\) and \(\mu_t>0\).

We prove that this transformation gives an exact correspondence between equilibria.

Individual optimality and budget exhaustion

Fix corresponding price vectors, so \(q_j=c_jp_j\). For each type \(t\), the map

\(z_j\longmapsto h_j=\mu_tz_j/c_j\)

is a bijection between nonnegative bundles. It satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_jq_jh_j=\mu_t\sum_jp_jz_j.\)

Consequently, \(z\) satisfies the per-member budget constraint precisely when \(h\) satisfies the finite buyer’s constraint:

\(\displaystyle \sum_jp_jz_j\le b_t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_jq_jh_j\le B_t.\)

Their utilities satisfy

\(\displaystyle \sum_ja_{tj}h_j=\mu_t\sum_jv_{tj}z_j.\)

Multiplication by the positive number \(\mu_t\) preserves comparisons of utilities. Thus a bundle maximizes utility in one problem if and only if its corresponding bundle maximizes utility in the other. This argument permits all ties and every maximizing bundle.

Moreover, if the finite buyer exhausts its budget, then

\(\displaystyle \sum_jp_jx_{tj}=\frac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_jq_jw_{tj}=\frac{B_t}{\mu_t}=b_t.\)

Capacity constraints and complementary slackness

For every good \(j\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}=c_j\sum_tw_{tj}.\)

Therefore the society capacity constraint is equivalent to the unit-supply constraint \(\sum_tw_{tj}\le1\). Also,

\(\displaystyle p_j\left(c_j-\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}\right)=q_j\left(1-\sum_tw_{tj}\right).\)

Thus complementary slackness is preserved exactly. Nonnegativity is preserved as well.

These identities prove both directions: a finite-market equilibrium yields a society equilibrium, and a society equilibrium yields a finite-market equilibrium.

Exact computation and bit complexity

We use the following established theorem: a finite linear Fisher market with positive rational budgets, rational nonnegative valuations, and unit supplies admits an exact equilibrium computable in polynomial time, provided every buyer values some good positively and every good has a potential buyer. An exact rational equilibrium with polynomial encoding length can be returned. This is the finite-market result of [Devanur, Papadimitriou, Saberi, and Vazirani](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/market.pdf), particularly Section 2 and Theorem 8.12.

All hypotheses hold for the constructed market:

  • \(B_t=\mu_tb_t>0\).
  • \(a_{tj}=c_jv_{tj}\ge0\) is rational.
  • Because \(c_j>0\), \(a_{tj}>0\) if and only if \(v_{tj}>0\). Hence every buyer values some good positively, and every good is valued positively by some buyer.

The construction preserves polynomial input length. Indeed, multiplying two binary-encoded rationals requires polynomial time, and the numerator and denominator bit lengths of their product are at most the sums of the corresponding input bit lengths, up to a constant. There are only \(\tau\) budgets and \(m\tau\) valuation entries. Hence the transformed encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Run the exact finite Fisher-market algorithm, obtaining \(q,w\), and return

\(p_j=q_j/c_j,\qquad x_{tj}=c_jw_{tj}/\mu_t.\)

These final rational arithmetic operations also take polynomial time and preserve polynomial output length. In particular, a small mass causes no expansion into individual buyers: its reciprocal is handled through its binary rational representation.

Conclusion

The reduction and recovery procedure compute exactly the equilibrium required in the question, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). No continuum-of-goods theorem is needed; the listed support produces a finite market with \(\tau\) buyers.
