The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite agent types \(\Theta\) with rational mass distribution \(\mu\), finite item types \(Q\) with rational per-capita supplies \(\rho\), and additive values \(v_{\theta q}\in[-1,1]\), does there always exist, and can one compute from the compressed input, finitely many occupied classes \((\lambda_a,\theta_a,B^a,p_a)\) with integral bundles \(B^a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{Q}\), satisfying \(\sum_{a:\theta_a=\theta}\lambda_a=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_a\lambda_a B^a_q=\rho_q\), \(0\le p_a\le1\), and \(v_{\theta_a}(B^a)+p_a\ge v_{\theta_a}(B^b)+p_b\) for every occupied pair \(a,b\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), where \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|Q|\) item types, with binary rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_q\ge0\) and binary rational additive values \(v_{\theta q}\in[-1,1]\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An outcome is a finite list of occupied classes \((\lambda_a,\theta_a,B^a,p_a)\), with positive binary rational masses \(\lambda_a\), types \(\theta_a\in\Theta\), binary-encoded integral bundles \(B^a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{Q}\), and binary rational subsidies \(p_a\in[0,1]\). Empty bundles are allowed, and a type may occupy multiple classes.
Require \(\sum_{a:\theta_a=\theta}\lambda_a=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_a\lambda_aB^a_q=\rho_q\). Subsidies come from an external sponsor, with no total-budget constraint.
Writing \(v_\theta(B)=\sum_qv_{\theta q}B_q\), require \(v_{\theta_a}(B^a)+p_a\ge v_{\theta_a}(B^b)+p_b\) for every ordered pair of occupied classes, including classes of the same type; indifference is permitted.

Does every input admit such an outcome? Is deciding feasibility in \(\mathrm P\) or NP-hard, and can a feasible outcome be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For an explicitly listed finite society and explicitly listed indivisible items with additive marginal values in \([-1,1]\), an envy-free allocation with subsidies in \([0,1]\) always exists and is computable in polynomial time ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10089)).
No prior result settling this exact compressed mass model was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the mass distribution to total mass one and required a nonempty positive-mass support.
  • Specified nonnegative rational supplies without normalizing their sum; supplies measure item counts per capita.
  • Required values to be binary rationals, following the discrete computational model.
  • Specified positive rational occupied-class masses and rational subsidies, with bundle counts encoded in binary, to make exact computation well-defined.
  • Explicitly allowed empty bundles and splitting a type across classes; retained weak envy inequalities between all occupied classes, including those of the same type.
  • Specified externally funded subsidies without a total-budget constraint, matching the anchored guarantee.

Throughout, “binary rational” means a rational number whose numerator and denominator are represented in binary.

2. Rational subsidies for fixed bundles

Consider a finite list of $r$ classes with fixed types $\theta_a$ and integral bundles $B^a$. Define
$$ w_{ab}=v_{\theta_a}(B^b)-v_{\theta_a}(B^a). $$
The subsidy constraints are exactly
$$ p_a\ge w_{ab}+p_b \qquad\text{for all }a,b. $$

Lemma. If these bundles admit real subsidies in $[0,1]$, then they admit binary rational subsidies in $[0,1]$. Such subsidies can be computed in polynomial time in the encoding length of the class list and the input values.

Proof. Let $\widehat p\in[0,1]^r$ be feasible. For a directed walk
$$ a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_k, $$
summing its envy inequalities gives
$$ \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}w_{a_i a_{i+1}} \le \widehat p_{a_0}-\widehat p_{a_k} \le 1. $$
Every closed walk therefore has nonpositive weight. Removing a closed segment cannot decrease a walk’s weight.

For each $a$, let $h_a$ be the maximum weight of any walk starting at $a$, allowing the empty walk. Removing closed segments reduces this maximization to finitely many simple paths. Consequently,
$$ 0\le h_a\le 1. $$
Prepending the edge $a\to b$ to a maximizing walk from $b$ gives
$$ h_a\ge w_{ab}+h_b. $$
Thus $h$ is a feasible subsidy vector.

Let $V$ be the product of the denominators of all input values. Every edge weight belongs to $V^{-1}\mathbb Z$, because bundles are integral. Hence
$$ h_a\in V^{-1}\mathbb Z, \qquad 0\le h_a\le 1. $$
The numerator of $h_a$, when represented with denominator $V$, lies between $0$ and $V$. The bit length of $V$ is bounded by the total bit length of the input denominators.

Finally, a maximizing simple path has at most $r-1$ edges. Dynamic programming computes the maximum walk weights using at most $r-1$ edges, including the empty walk. This uses polynomially many exact rational operations, with polynomial bit lengths. $\square$

3. Universal feasibility and decision complexity

The external theorem used here states that explicitly listed agents and indivisible items with additive marginal values in $[-1,1]$ admit an envy-free allocation with subsidies in $[0,1]$. It also supplies a polynomial-time algorithm for the explicit representation. [Lu, Mackenzie, and Suzuki, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.10089v1)

Theorem. Every valid compressed-model input admits a feasible outcome.

Proof. Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of all masses and supplies. Then
$$ n_\theta=D\mu_\theta\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad M_q=D\rho_q\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}. $$
Moreover,
$$ \sum_\theta n_\theta=D. $$

Construct an explicit finite society with $n_\theta$ agents of type $\theta$ and $M_q$ copies of item type $q$. Each agent of type $\theta$ values each copy of $q$ at $v_{\theta q}$. These utilities are additive, and every marginal value lies in $[-1,1]$, so the finite theorem applies.

If there are no items, the empty allocation with zero subsidies suffices. Otherwise, apply the theorem and let $B^i_q$ count the copies of item type $q$ allocated to agent $i$. By Section 2, the resulting bundles admit rational subsidies in $[0,1]$.

Make each agent a class of mass $1/D$. Then
$$ \sum_{i:\theta_i=\theta}\frac1D =\frac{n_\theta}{D} =\mu_\theta $$
and
$$ \sum_i\frac1D B^i_q =\frac{M_q}{D} =\rho_q. $$
Every class envy inequality is an envy inequality in the finite society, including comparisons between classes of the same type. This is a feasible outcome. $\square$

Thus a decision algorithm answers YES on every valid input. If input validation is included, positivity, normalization, and the stated rational bounds can all be checked in polynomial time. Hence feasibility decision is in $\mathrm P$.

The construction above does not establish polynomial-time search in the compressed representation: the numerical value of $D$ can be exponential in $L$.

4. Polynomial-size feasible outcomes

Theorem. Every input admits a feasible outcome with at most $m+\tau$ classes and polynomial total encoding length.

Proof. Start with the rational outcome from Section 3. For each class $a$, define
$$ c_a=(e_{\theta_a},B^a)\in\mathbb Z^{\tau+m}, $$
where $e_{\theta_a}$ is the unit vector identifying its type. Let $A$ have these columns and write
$$ b=((\mu_\theta)_\theta,(\rho_q)_q). $$
The balance constraints are
$$ A\lambda=b. $$

If the occupied columns are dependent, choose a nonzero rational vector $z$ with $Az=0$. Summing the type rows gives
$$ \sum_a z_a=0. $$
Therefore $z$ has both positive and negative entries. Set
$$ t=\min_{a:z_a>0}\frac{\lambda_a}{z_a}, \qquad \lambda'_a=\lambda_a-tz_a. $$
All new masses are nonnegative, at least one is zero, and
$$ A\lambda'=b. $$
Delete the zero-mass classes. Changing masses preserves envy inequalities, and deleting classes removes comparisons.

Repeating this operation leaves linearly independent occupied columns. Their number $r$ satisfies
$$ r\le m+\tau. $$

For the encoding bound, retain the denominator $D$ from Section 3 and define
$$ C=\max\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{M_q:q\in Q\}\bigr). $$
This definition also covers an empty item set. Every retained bundle satisfies
$$ 0\le B^a_q\le M_q. $$
Since $\log D=O(L)$ and every supply has input bit length at most $L$, all bundle coordinates have $O(L)$ bits.

Choose $r$ rows on which the retained columns form an invertible square matrix $H$. The corresponding balance vector is $u/D$ for an integral vector $u$. Entries of $H$ have absolute value at most $C$, and entries of $u$ have absolute value at most $DC$. Thus
$$ \lambda=H^{-1}u/D. $$
Cramer’s rule represents each mass with denominator bounded in absolute value by
$$ D\,r!\,C^r $$
and numerator bounded in absolute value by
$$ r!\,D\,C^r. $$
Each mass therefore has bit length
$$ O(\log D+r\log C+r\log r), $$
which is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

Section 2 supplies polynomial-length rational subsidies for the retained classes. $\square$

All balances, range restrictions, and ordered-pair envy inequalities can consequently be verified in polynomial time for these compact outcomes. The proof establishes their existence; it does not efficiently discover their bundles.

5. A polynomial construction for rank-one valuations

Assume the matrix $(v_{\theta q})_{\theta,q}$ has rank at most one over $\mathbb Q$.

5.1 Normalizing the scalar representation

We can compute rational numbers $c_\theta,w_q$ such that
$$ v_{\theta q}=c_\theta w_q, \qquad |c_\theta|\le 1, \qquad |w_q|\le 1. $$

If all values are zero, take all these numbers to be zero. Otherwise, choose an entry
$$ d=v_{\theta_0 q_0} $$
of maximum absolute value and put $A=|d|>0$. Define
$$ w_q=\frac{v_{\theta_0 q}}{A}, \qquad c_\theta=\operatorname{sgn}(d)\,v_{\theta q_0}. $$
Rank at most one implies that every relevant two-by-two minor vanishes:
$$ v_{\theta q}d=v_{\theta q_0}v_{\theta_0 q}. $$
Consequently,
$$ c_\theta w_q =\frac{v_{\theta q_0}v_{\theta_0 q}}{d} =v_{\theta q}. $$
The choice of $d$ gives
$$ |w_q|\le 1, \qquad |c_\theta|\le A\le 1. $$
These numbers have polynomial bit length.

For a bundle $B$, write its scalar value as
$$ x(B)=\sum_q w_qB_q. $$
Then every type evaluates it as
$$ v_\theta(B)=c_\theta x(B). $$

5.2 Balancing scalar values

First construct untyped bundle classes of total mass one and average bundle $\rho$.

Set
$$ K_q=\lfloor\rho_q\rfloor, \qquad f_q=\rho_q-K_q\in[0,1). $$
Start with one class of mass one holding bundle $K$.

Process each coordinate $q$ once. If $f_q=0$, do nothing. Otherwise:

  • If $w_q\ge 0$, select classes of total mass exactly $f_q$ having the lowest current scalar values.
  • If $w_q<0$, select classes of total mass exactly $f_q$ having the highest current scalar values.

Selection is performed by sorting the classes and taking a prefix or suffix, splitting one boundary class if necessary. Add one copy of $q$ to every selected class.

This increases the average quantity of $q$ by exactly $f_q$. Therefore the final average bundle is $\rho$. Every resulting bundle satisfies
$$ B_q\in\{\lfloor\rho_q\rfloor,\lceil\rho_q\rceil\}. $$
Each coordinate creates at most one additional class, so there are at most $m+1$ classes.

Balancing lemma. The final scalar values have range at most one.

Proof. Suppose the current scalar values have range at most $R$.

Consider a step with $w_q=w\ge 0$. If classes $a,b$ are respectively selected and unselected, their old values satisfy
$$ x_a\le x_b. $$
Their new values are $x_a+w$ and $x_b$. Hence
$$ (x_a+w)-x_b\le w, $$
while
$$ x_b-(x_a+w)\le x_b-x_a\le R. $$
For two selected classes, or two unselected classes, their difference is unchanged. Thus the new range is at most
$$ \max(R,w). $$

For a negative step, replace each scalar value by its negative. Selecting the highest original values becomes selecting the lowest reflected values, and the preceding argument applies with increment $|w_q|$.

Initially all scalar values are equal. Induction therefore bounds the final range by
$$ \max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{|w_q|:f_q>0\}\bigr)\le 1. $$
$\square$

The argument allows arbitrary positive and negative $w_q$. For example, with one type, values $(1,-1)$, and supplies $(1/2,1/2)$, it produces mass $1/2$ on each of $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$; both bundles have value zero.

5.3 Assigning bundles to types

Let the resulting bundle classes have masses $\nu_j$ and scalar values $x_j$. Order them so that
$$ x_1\le x_2\le\cdots\le x_s. $$
Order the supported types so that their multipliers are nondecreasing.

Partition $[0,1)$ into consecutive bundle intervals of lengths $\nu_j$, in bundle order. Independently partition it into consecutive type intervals of lengths $\mu_\theta$, in multiplier order. Each nonempty intersection becomes an occupied class, carrying the corresponding bundle and type.

This preserves every supply and gives each type exactly its required mass. The two partitions have at most $s+\tau-2$ distinct interior endpoints, so their common refinement has at most
$$ s+\tau-1\le m+\tau $$
classes.

Index these classes in interval order. Write their scalar values and multipliers as $x_a,c_a$. Then
$$ x_1\le\cdots\le x_r, \qquad c_1\le\cdots\le c_r. $$

5.4 Constructing subsidies and checking every comparison

Define preliminary payments by
$$ P_1=0, $$
and, for each adjacent pair, set
$$ t_a=\frac{c_a+c_{a+1}}2, \qquad P_{a+1}=P_a-t_a(x_{a+1}-x_a). $$

For $i<j$, telescoping gives
$$ P_j-P_i =-\sum_{a=i}^{j-1}t_a(x_{a+1}-x_a). $$
The ordering of the multipliers implies
$$ c_i\le t_a\le c_j \qquad\text{for }i\le a<j. $$
All scalar increments are nonnegative. Therefore
$$ -c_j(x_j-x_i) \le P_j-P_i \le -c_i(x_j-x_i). $$
The upper bound implies
$$ c_ix_i+P_i\ge c_ix_j+P_j. $$
The lower bound implies
$$ c_jx_j+P_j\ge c_jx_i+P_i. $$
These are exactly the two ordered envy inequalities between classes $i$ and $j$. Since the pair was arbitrary, every required comparison holds.

In particular, same-type comparisons are included. If $c_i=c_j$, both inequalities are equalities.

It remains to bound the payment range. Put
$$ C_*=\max_\theta|c_\theta|\le 1. $$
For $i<j$,
$$ |P_j-P_i| \le\sum_{a=i}^{j-1}|t_a|(x_{a+1}-x_a) \le C_*(x_j-x_i) \le 1, $$
using the balancing lemma. Thus the whole payment vector has range at most one. Define
$$ p_a=P_a-\min_b P_b. $$
Then
$$ 0\le p_a\le 1. $$
A common payment shift preserves every envy inequality. The constructed outcome is feasible.

5.5 Running time and representation

There are at most $m+1$ bundle classes during balancing. Each coordinate requires sorting this polynomial-size list and at most one split. Type assignment produces at most $m+\tau$ classes, and payment construction uses a linear number of arithmetic operations afterward.

All quantities are exact rationals of polynomial bit length:

  • Bundle coordinates are floors or ceilings of input supplies.
  • Balancing masses remain multiples of the reciprocal of the product of the supply denominators.
  • Intersecting with type intervals introduces only the input mass denominators.
  • Scalar values and payments are polynomially long sums and products of polynomial-length rationals.

Hence the complete construction runs in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

Theorem. For valuation matrices of rank at most one, a feasible outcome is computable in polynomial time, with at most $m+\tau$ classes and
$$ B^a_q\in\{\lfloor\rho_q\rfloor,\lceil\rho_q\rceil\} $$
for every occupied class and coordinate.

6. A reduction for the remaining construction question

For arbitrary valuations, large supplies can be removed from the construction problem by giving everyone a common integral bundle.

Set
$$ K_q=\lfloor\rho_q\rfloor, \qquad \rho'_q=\rho_q-K_q. $$
Suppose an outcome for supplies $\rho'$ has bundles $C^a$. Replace them by
$$ B^a=C^a+K. $$
Since total class mass is one,
$$ \sum_a\lambda_aB^a_q =\rho'_q+K_q =\rho_q. $$
For every ordered pair,
$$ v_{\theta_a}(B^a)-v_{\theta_a}(B^b) = v_{\theta_a}(C^a)-v_{\theta_a}(C^b). $$
Thus the same subsidies remain feasible.

This transformation is polynomial-time. Consequently, a polynomial construction for all instances satisfying
$$ 0\le\rho_q<1 $$
would suffice for the general problem.

The reduction does not solve the remaining difficulty: small average supplies can still require large bundles on classes of very small mass. The rank-one proof controls those choices using a single scalar ordering. No corresponding construction for general valuation matrices is established here.
