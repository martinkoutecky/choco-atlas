The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational cohort-location masses μ_{ij} ≥ 0 for three cohorts and q ordered cells, with ∑_j μ_{ij} = 1/3 for each cohort i, reveal the cells from left to right. Require each normalized cell mass 3μ_{ij} ≤ ε^2/(100 log 3); a deterministic online policy may cut only between cells and must assign each completed consecutive block immediately to one of two districts. What is the minimum worst-case number of cuts needed to ensure that, for every cohort i and district d, the normalized share 3∑_{j assigned to d} μ_{ij} lies in [1/2-ε/2, 1/2+ε/2]?

Partial determination of the minimax cut budget

The exact value of $C(\varepsilon,q)$ is not determined here outside the one-cut regime. I prove that regime exactly, give uniform sufficient budgets, and construct rational adversarial streams forcing explicit lower budgets.

Compared with the bounds in the notes, the results below add a five-cut policy for sufficiently large tolerance, an obstruction to every two-cut allocation, and a sharper adversarial bound that benefits from additional cells.

1. Normalization and statements

I interpret “binary rational” as a rational number whose numerator and denominator are encoded in binary. Requiring dyadic masses would make the stipulated cohort total $1/3$ impossible.

Put
$$ a_{ij}=3\mu_{ij}, \qquad \delta=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{100\log_2 3}, \qquad \alpha=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}, \qquad \beta=\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}. $$
Thus each normalized cohort has total $1$, and every normalized cell mass is at most $\delta$.

Identify the two districts with signs. If cell $j$ receives sign $\sigma_j\in\{-1,+1\}$, its final discrepancy is
$$ D_i=\sum_{j=1}^q \sigma_j a_{ij}. $$
The two normalized district shares are $(1+D_i)/2$ and $(1-D_i)/2$. Consequently, the required allocation condition is exactly
$$ |D_i|\leq\varepsilon \qquad \text{for }i=1,2,3. $$

Define
$$ N=\left\lceil\frac1\delta\right\rceil, \qquad U(\varepsilon)= \left\lceil\frac{3}{\varepsilon^2/3-\delta}\right\rceil-1. $$

The following claims are proved below.

  • Uniform sufficient budget:

$$ C(\varepsilon,q)\leq \min\{q-1,U(\varepsilon)\} \leq \left\lceil\frac{450}{49\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil-1. $$

  • Exact one-cut regime:

$$ C(\varepsilon,q)=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \left\lceil\frac q2\right\rceil\delta\leq\beta. $$

  • An obstruction to two cuts: writing

$$ K=\left\lfloor\frac{\beta}{\delta}\right\rfloor, $$
we have
$$ q\geq3K+1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(\varepsilon,q)\geq3. $$
The constructed stream admits no successful two-cut allocation even offline.

  • A five-cut sufficient budget:

$$ 6\alpha+5\delta\leq1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(\varepsilon,q)\leq5. $$
Equivalently, this holds when
$$ \varepsilon\geq\frac23+\frac53\delta. $$
In particular, $\varepsilon\geq7/10$ suffices.

  • A quantitative online lower bound: define

$$ n=q-N, \qquad h=\frac1{\max\{N,n\}}, \qquad T=nh, \qquad b=2\varepsilon+h, \qquad \lambda=b+h, $$
and
$$ F=2b^2+4\lambda b+2hT, \qquad G(\varepsilon,q)= \frac{(T-8\varepsilon-2h)_+^2}{2F}, $$
where $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$. Then
$$ C(\varepsilon,q)\geq \max\{1,\lceil G(\varepsilon,q)\rceil\}. $$
In particular,
$$ 0<\varepsilon\leq\frac1{32}, \qquad q\geq2N \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(\varepsilon,q)\geq \left\lceil\frac1{90\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil. $$

The potential construction is related to the online lower-bound method in [Alon and Graur, Section 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.16613). The argument here proves the required rational-cell and reveal-timing details directly.

2. Admissible lengths

First, $\log_2 3$ is irrational. Otherwise, writing it as a positive rational $r/s$ would give
$$ 2^r=3^s, $$
contradicting unique prime factorization.

Since $\varepsilon$ is positive and rational, $\delta$ is therefore irrational. A normalized cohort of total $1$ requires
$$ q\delta\geq1. $$
Equality is impossible because $q\delta$ is irrational. Conversely, if $q\delta>1$, the rational uniform stream
$$ a_{ij}=\frac1q $$
is admissible. Thus the admissible lengths are exactly
$$ q\geq N. $$
Moreover,
$$ \frac1N<\delta, \qquad 1<N\delta<1+\delta. $$

We will also use
$$ \log_2 3>\frac32, $$
which follows from $3^2>2^3$. Hence
$$ \delta<\frac{\varepsilon^2}{150}. $$

3. A uniform online policy

Set
$$ g=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{3}. $$
Maintain the mass vector $A$ of the current unassigned block and the signed discrepancy vector $S$ of all assigned blocks.

Upon revealing cell $j$, with mass vector $a_j$:

  • If every coordinate of $A+a_j$ is at most $g$, append that cell.
  • Otherwise, cut immediately before cell $j$. Assign the preceding block with a sign $s$ satisfying

$$ s\,S\cdot A\leq0. $$
Then replace $S$ by $S+sA$ and start the new block with cell $j$.

At termination, assign the final block using the same sign rule. Resolve ties deterministically.

Because every cell coordinate is at most $\delta<g$, the first cell fits in a block, and every resulting block has coordinate masses at most $g$. The policy uses only revealed information and assigns a completed block immediately when cutting.

Discrepancy guarantee

For each assigned block,
$$ \|S+sA\|_2^2 = \|S\|_2^2+2s\,S\cdot A+\|A\|_2^2 \leq \|S\|_2^2+\|A\|_2^2. $$
Starting with $S=0$ and summing over all blocks gives
$$ \|D\|_2^2 \leq \sum_{\text{blocks }A}\sum_{i=1}^3 A_i^2. $$
Since $0\leq A_i\leq g$,
$$ A_i^2\leq gA_i. $$
Each cohort has total $1$, so
$$ \|D\|_2^2 \leq g\sum_{i=1}^3\sum_{\text{blocks }A}A_i = 3g = \varepsilon^2. $$
Therefore every $|D_i|\leq\varepsilon$.

Cut count

Whenever a cut occurs, some coordinate satisfies
$$ A_i+a_{ij}>g. $$
Since $a_{ij}\leq\delta$, the completed block has
$$ A_i>g-\delta. $$
If the policy makes $c$ cuts, summing over their completed blocks yields
$$ c(g-\delta) < \sum_{\text{nonfinal blocks }A}\sum_i A_i \leq3. $$
Thus
$$ c\leq \left\lceil\frac3{g-\delta}\right\rceil-1 = U(\varepsilon). $$
Also $c\leq q-1$, because only internal boundaries count.

Finally,
$$ g-\delta> \frac{\varepsilon^2}{3}-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{150} = \frac{49\varepsilon^2}{150}, $$
giving the numerical bound stated in Section 1.

4. Exactly when one cut suffices

Sufficiency

Suppose
$$ \left\lceil\frac q2\right\rceil\delta\leq\beta. $$
Use the predetermined boundary after
$$ k=\left\lfloor\frac q2\right\rfloor $$
cells.

For any cohort, its prefix mass $P_i$ satisfies
$$ P_i\leq k\delta\leq\beta. $$
Its suffix contains at most $\lceil q/2\rceil$ cells, so
$$ P_i \geq 1-\left\lceil\frac q2\right\rceil\delta \geq 1-\beta = \alpha. $$
Thus the prefix and suffix both satisfy every cohort requirement. The policy makes its cut when cell $k+1$ is revealed.

Zero cuts cannot succeed: one district would receive zero mass, whereas $\alpha>0$. Therefore $C(\varepsilon,q)=1$.

Necessity

Suppose instead that
$$ \left\lceil\frac q2\right\rceil\delta>\beta. $$
Choose a rational number $u$ with
$$ \max\left\{ \frac1q,\, \frac{\beta}{\lceil q/2\rceil} \right\} <u<\delta. $$
Such a choice exists by admissibility and the strict displayed inequality.

Pack the first cohort as early as possible:
$$ a_{1j} = \min\{u,\max\{1-(j-1)u,0\}\}. $$
Reverse this sequence for the second cohort, and set $a_{3j}=1/q$.

These masses are rational, each lies below $\delta$, and each cohort has total $1$. At the boundary after $k$ cells, the first two prefix masses are
$$ P_1(k)=\min\{ku,1\}, \qquad P_2(k)=\max\{1-(q-k)u,0\}. $$

If $k\geq\lceil q/2\rceil$, then $P_1(k)>\beta$. If $k\leq\lfloor q/2\rfloor$, then $P_2(k)<\alpha$. These cases cover every internal boundary. Consequently, no one-cut allocation succeeds, even offline.

This proves the equivalence.

The smallest admissible length

At $q=N$,
$$ \left\lceil\frac N2\right\rceil\delta \leq \frac{(N+1)\delta}{2} < \frac{1+2\delta}{2} < \frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}, $$
where the last inequality follows from $2\delta<\varepsilon$. Hence
$$ C(\varepsilon,N)=1. $$

Thus a uniform lower bound of order $\varepsilon^{-2}$ cannot hold over all admissible parameter pairs.

5. A rational stream ruling out two cuts

Let
$$ K=\left\lfloor\frac{\beta}{\delta}\right\rfloor, \qquad q\geq3K+1. $$
Because $\beta$ is positive and rational while $\delta$ is irrational, $\beta/\delta$ is not an integer. In particular,
$$ K\delta<\beta<(K+1)\delta. $$

Choose a rational $u<\delta$ sufficiently close to $\delta$ that
$$ u>\frac1q, \qquad \left\lfloor\frac{\beta}{u}\right\rfloor=K, \qquad (K+1)u>\beta. $$
The first condition is possible because the pair is admissible. The other conditions follow by choosing $u$ close enough to $\delta$.

Put
$$ r=q-2K. $$
Then $r\geq K+1$, so $ru>\beta$.

Define the first cohort by early packing with cap $u$, as in Section 4, and define the third cohort by reversing it. For the second cohort, put
$$ c=\min\{1,ru\}. $$
Give each of the central cells
$$ K+1,\ldots,q-K $$
mass $c/r$, and each of the other $2K$ cells mass $(1-c)/(2K)$.

All these masses are rational. The central entries are at most $u$. If $c<1$, then $c=ru$ and
$$ 1-c=1-ru<2Ku, $$
because $qu>1$. Thus the outside entries are also at most $u$. The second cohort has total $1$, and its central mass satisfies
$$ c>\beta. $$

Consider any contiguous interval comprising cells $k+1,\ldots,\ell$ whose first- and third-cohort masses are both at least $\alpha$.

For the first cohort, the mass before the interval must be at most $\beta$. Early packing therefore gives
$$ k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\beta}{u}\right\rfloor=K. $$
For the third cohort, the mass after the interval must be at most $\beta$, giving
$$ q-\ell\leq K. $$
Hence the interval contains every central cell. Its second-cohort mass consequently exceeds $\beta$.

No contiguous interval can therefore have all three cohort masses in $[\alpha,\beta]$.

An allocation with at most two cuts has at most three blocks. If both districts receive mass, at least one district receives exactly one block, hence a contiguous interval. That interval would have to meet all three cohort requirements, which is impossible. Therefore
$$ C(\varepsilon,q)\geq3. $$

6. A five-cut policy for large tolerance

Assume
$$ 6\alpha+5\delta\leq1. $$

For each cohort, maintain a label count initially equal to zero. A cohort is called active until its count reaches two.

Build a block until some active cohort has block mass at least $\alpha$. Mark that block with one such cohort’s label, using a deterministic tie rule, and increase that cohort’s label count.

If any cohort remains active, then at the next cell reveal:

  • Cut immediately before that cell.
  • Assign the marked block to a district that has not yet received a block carrying its label.
  • Start the next block.

When all cohorts become inactive, make no further cuts. At termination, assign the final block, including any subsequent cells, to the district lacking its label.

A cohort receives only two labels, so a district lacking the current label always exists. This procedure respects the required timing: the threshold is detected after a cell reveal, and the block is assigned immediately at the next permitted cut.

All six labels must occur

Before a block reaches its threshold, every active cohort has block mass below $\alpha$. The cell that first reaches a threshold adds at most $\delta$ to each coordinate. Thus every cohort active during a marked block has mass at most
$$ \alpha+\delta $$
in that block.

Suppose some cohort $i$ remained active at termination. It was active throughout the process. There could have been at most five marked blocks, because six labels would make every cohort inactive.

If there is an unmarked terminal block, its cohort-$i$ mass is below $\alpha$. Therefore the entire cohort-$i$ total would be strictly below
$$ 5(\alpha+\delta)+\alpha = 6\alpha+5\delta \leq1, $$
a contradiction.

If the terminal block itself is marked, there are at most five marked blocks and no unmarked remainder. Its total would then be at most
$$ 5(\alpha+\delta) < 6\alpha+5\delta \leq1, $$
again a contradiction.

Thus every cohort receives exactly two labels.

Allocation guarantee and cut count

The two blocks carrying a given cohort’s label are assigned to different districts. Each has that cohort’s mass at least $\alpha$. Consequently, both districts receive at least $\alpha$ of every cohort.

Since each cohort’s total is $1$, each district also receives at most $1-\alpha=\beta$. The allocation is successful.

There are six marked blocks, and the sixth is assigned at termination. Hence at most five cuts occur.

Finally,
$$ 6\alpha+5\delta\leq1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \varepsilon\geq\frac23+\frac53\delta. $$
Because $\delta<1/150$, every $\varepsilon\geq7/10$ satisfies this condition.

7. A sharper rational online adversary

Fix a deterministic policy that succeeds on every admissible stream. We construct a stream forcing the lower bound in Section 1.

Use the first
$$ n=q-N $$
cells as an active phase, leaving exactly $N$ cells for a reserved tail. Define
$$ h=\frac1{\max\{N,n\}}, \qquad T=nh\leq1. $$
Every active cell has third-cohort mass zero and combined first-two-cohort mass $h$. Because $h\leq1/N<\delta$, its individual coordinates satisfy the cell cap.

When $n=0$, the lower bound below is zero and the elementary lower bound $C\geq1$ applies. We may therefore discuss the construction for $n>0$.

A bound on every committed active state

Suppose the policy cuts immediately before active cell $j$. Let $v_i$ be its committed discrepancy after assigning the preceding block, and let $Q_i$ be the corresponding committed unsigned mass. The current cell has already been revealed; write its vector as $w$, where
$$ w_3=0, \qquad 0\leq w_i\leq h. $$

If some $|v_i|>b=2\varepsilon+h$ for $i=1,2$, complete every subsequent cell with vector
$$ \left( \frac{1-Q_1-w_1}{q-j}, \frac{1-Q_2-w_2}{q-j}, \frac1{q-j} \right). $$
The residual masses are nonnegative: the combined active mass revealed so far is at most $T\leq1$. Also $q-j\geq N$, so every continuation coordinate is at most $1/N<\delta$.

Let $\sigma$ be the eventual sign of the current cell, and put
$$ R_i=1-Q_i-w_i. $$
Every subsequent cohort-$i$ mass is $R_i$ times its third-cohort mass. Since all earlier third-cohort masses are zero, the final discrepancies satisfy
$$ D_i=v_i+\sigma w_i+R_iD_3. $$
Success would imply
$$ |v_i| \leq |D_i|+w_i+R_i|D_3| \leq 2\varepsilon+h=b, $$
a contradiction.

Thus a universally successful policy must satisfy
$$ |v_1|,|v_2|\leq b $$
after every cut before an active cell. This continuation preserves the already-revealed current cell.

Choosing active directions

Put
$$ \lambda=b+h $$
and define
$$ M(x,y)=x^2+y^2+2\lambda(x-y). $$

Immediately before revealing an active cell, let $v=(x,y)$ be the discrepancy of already-assigned blocks. Reveal
$$ w= h\left( \frac{\lambda-y}{2\lambda+x-y}, \frac{\lambda+x}{2\lambda+x-y}, 0 \right). $$
The entries are rational. Because $|x|,|y|\leq b<\lambda$, both first-two-cohort entries are positive, their denominator is positive, and their sum is $h$.

Furthermore,
$$ \nabla M(x,y)=(2x+2\lambda,\,2y-2\lambda), $$
so direct substitution gives
$$ \nabla M(v)\cdot(w_1,w_2)=0. $$

Generate all active cells this way unless the losing continuation above applies. For a universally successful policy it cannot apply. After the active phase, distribute each residual cohort total uniformly over the $N$ tail cells. That continuation is admissible because every residual lies in $[0,1]$.

8. Potential growth with the reveal timing included

Suppose there are $r$ cuts before active cells. Let $P_k$ be the first-two-cohort mass vector of the block completed at the $k$-th such cut, let $s_k$ be its assigned sign, and write
$$ v_k=v_{k-1}+s_kP_k, \qquad v_0=0. $$

For $k\geq2$, the first cell $z_k$ of block $P_k$ was revealed before the preceding cut. Its direction was therefore chosen using $v_{k-2}$. Every other cell of $P_k$ was generated using $v_{k-1}$.

Consequently,
$$ \nabla M(v_{k-1})\cdot P_k = \bigl(\nabla M(v_{k-1})-\nabla M(v_{k-2})\bigr)\cdot z_k. $$
The Hessian of $M$ is twice the identity, so
$$ \nabla M(v_{k-1})\cdot P_k = 2s_{k-1}P_{k-1}\cdot z_k. $$
Since the coordinates of $z_k$ are at most $h$,
$$ \left|\nabla M(v_{k-1})\cdot P_k\right| \leq 2h\|P_{k-1}\|_1. $$
For the first block, the corresponding inner product is zero.

Expanding the quadratic potential gives
$$ M(v_k)-M(v_{k-1}) = \|P_k\|_2^2+ s_k\nabla M(v_{k-1})\cdot P_k. $$
The completed active blocks have combined mass at most $T$. Summing therefore yields
$$ M(v_r) \geq \sum_{k=1}^r\|P_k\|_2^2-2hT. $$

Each committed state has coordinate magnitudes at most $b$, so
$$ M(v_r)\leq2b^2+4\lambda b. $$
Combining these inequalities,
$$ \sum_{k=1}^r\|P_k\|_2^2 \leq 2b^2+4\lambda b+2hT = F. $$

The entire uncompleted active suffix

Define $B$ to be all active mass not contained in $P_1,\ldots,P_r$, measured at the end of the active phase. If $r=0$, it is the whole active phase.

This definition includes all cells revealed after the last active cut. They belong to one unassigned block at the end of the active phase, and therefore eventually receive one common sign $\sigma$.

Write
$$ R_i=1-\sum_{k=1}^r(P_k)_i-B_i. $$
The uniform tail has cohort-$i$ mass $R_i$ times its third-cohort mass. Thus, regardless of later cuts inside the tail,
$$ D_i=(v_r)_i+\sigma B_i+R_iD_3. $$
Success implies
$$ B_i\leq b+2\varepsilon=4\varepsilon+h. $$
Hence the combined completed active mass
$$ A=\sum_{k=1}^r\|P_k\|_1 = T-\|B\|_1 $$
satisfies
$$ A\geq T-8\varepsilon-2h. $$

If this lower bound is positive, then $r>0$. For two-dimensional nonnegative vectors,
$$ \|P_k\|_2^2\geq\frac{\|P_k\|_1^2}{2}. $$
Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives
$$ \sum_{k=1}^r\|P_k\|_2^2 \geq \frac{A^2}{2r}. $$
Using the upper bound $F$ proves
$$ r\geq \frac{(T-8\varepsilon-2h)^2}{2F}. $$
When the numerator’s unsquared expression is nonpositive, the positive-part bound simply asserts $r\geq0$.

Thus
$$ C(\varepsilon,q)\geq\lceil G(\varepsilon,q)\rceil. $$

Every constructed mass is rational. Dividing the normalized masses by $3$ gives the required original cohort totals. The construction imposes no upper bound on its encoding length, consistent with the definition of $C$.

9. Explicit large-length consequence

Assume
$$ q\geq2N, \qquad 0<\varepsilon\leq\frac1{32}. $$
Then $T=1$, and
$$ h\leq\frac1N<\delta<\frac{\varepsilon^2}{150}. $$
In particular,
$$ b<\frac{301}{150}\varepsilon, \qquad \lambda<\frac{302}{150}\varepsilon. $$
Therefore
$$ \begin{aligned} F &= 2b^2+4\lambda b+2h\\ &< \left( 2\left(\frac{301}{150}\right)^2+ 4\frac{302}{150}\frac{301}{150}+ \frac1{75} \right)\varepsilon^2\\ &= \frac{54511}{2250}\varepsilon^2 < 25\varepsilon^2. \end{aligned} $$
Also,
$$ 1-8\varepsilon-2h > \frac34-\frac1{76800} > \frac{749}{1000}. $$
Consequently,
$$ G(\varepsilon,q) > \frac{(749/1000)^2}{50\varepsilon^2} > \frac1{90\varepsilon^2}, $$
where the last inequality follows from
$$ 90\left(\frac{749}{1000}\right)^2>50. $$

Together with the uniform policy,
$$ \left\lceil\frac1{90\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil \leq C(\varepsilon,q) \leq \left\lceil\frac{450}{49\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil-1. $$
Thus the order $\Theta(\varepsilon^{-2})$ is established in this range.

10. Running times and the support representation

These are running times for the explicit allocation policies, not for computing the unknown exact minimax value.

Here $m=2$, there are three cohorts, and $\tau$ counts positive cohort-location entries. Let $L$ include the binary encodings of the support, masses, $q$, and $\varepsilon$.

The uniform policy performs $O(\tau)$ nonzero mass updates and
$$ O\bigl(\min\{\tau,\varepsilon^{-2}\}\bigr) $$
block assignments. Its arithmetic consists of rational additions, multiplications, and comparisons. The five-cut policy performs $O(\tau)$ mass updates and at most six block assignments.

The rational quantities maintained by these policies have $O(L)$ bit length. Indeed, denominators of accumulated masses divide products of input denominators; their total bit length is bounded by $L$. The sign comparisons involve only a fixed number of products of such quantities.

Using elementary exact arithmetic, a conservative bit-operation bound for the uniform policy is
$$ O\left( qL+ m\bigl(\tau+\min\{\tau,\varepsilon^{-2}\}\bigr)L^2 \right), \qquad m=2. $$
For the five-cut and predetermined one-cut policies, it is
$$ O(qL+m\tau L^2). $$

The $qL$ term accommodates a protocol explicitly revealing every cell, including empty cells. With chronological support and encoded empty gaps, the processing bounds reduce to $O(m\tau L^2)$: the policies can process the nonzero entries and record any scheduled boundary without expanding all $3q$ possible types. No future nonzero mass is used before its reveal.

11. What remains unresolved

The one-cut regime is settled exactly. Outside it, the proved statements identify sufficient budgets and budgets that a successful deterministic policy must exceed, but they do not always coincide.

For example, when $\varepsilon\geq7/10$ and
$$ q\geq3\left\lfloor\frac{\beta}{\delta}\right\rfloor+1, $$
the results give
$$ 3\leq C(\varepsilon,q)\leq5, $$
without deciding which value occurs.

Likewise, the large-length bounds establish matching asymptotic order for small tolerance, but leave the exact constants and finite-$q$ transition undetermined.
