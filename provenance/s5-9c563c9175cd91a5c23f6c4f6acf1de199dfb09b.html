The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_o\) for item categories \(o\in O\), priorities \(q_t>0\), and equal-magnitude additive values \(v_t(o)\in\{-a_t,0,a_t\}\), let \(\bar q=\sum_t\mu_tq_t\), \(u_t=v_t/a_t\), \(r_t=\sum_o\sigma_ou_t(o)\), \(\Lambda_t=\max\{\lambda:\sum_s\mu_s\lceil\lambda q_s/\bar q\rceil\le r_t\}\), and \(h_t=\lceil(q_t/\bar q)\Lambda_t\rceil\). Compute a mass distribution \(\pi_{t,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{O}\) satisfying \(\sum_B\pi_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B}\pi_{t,B}B_o=\sigma_o\), \(u_t(B)\ge h_t\) whenever \(\pi_{t,B}>0\), and maximum normalized welfare \(\sum_{t,B}\pi_{t,B}u_t(B)=\sum_o\sigma_o\max_tu_t(o)\), in time polynomial in the type and category counts and the input bit length.

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). There are \(m=|O|\) item categories, nonnegative rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_o\), positive rational priorities \(q_t\), and additive values \(v_t(o)\in\{-a_t,0,a_t\}\), where \(a_t>0\) is rational; set \(a_t=1\) for an identically zero valuation. All input numbers are encoded in binary; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Define \(u_t=v_t/a_t\), \(\bar q=\sum_{s\in T}\mu_sq_s\), and \(r_t=\sum_{o\in O}\sigma_ou_t(o)\). Let \(\Lambda_t=\max\{\lambda\in\mathbb R:\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\lceil\lambda q_s/\bar q\rceil\le r_t\}\) and \(h_t=\lceil(q_t/\bar q)\Lambda_t\rceil\).
A feasible allocation is a finitely supported distribution of nonnegative rational masses \(\pi_{t,B}\) on types and integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{O}\), including the empty bundle, satisfying \(\sum_B\pi_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B}\pi_{t,B}B_o=\sigma_o\), and \(u_t(B)\ge h_t\) whenever \(\pi_{t,B}>0\). Polynomial-time feasibility and construction

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). When feasible, an allocation can be constructed with at most \(\tau(m+1)\) nonzero entries, all of polynomial encoding length.

The proof has three ingredients: exact computation of the thresholds, a polynomial-size linear program, and an explicit rounding lemma that converts its solution into distributions over integral bundles.

1. Exact polynomial-time computation of the thresholds

Write \(c_s=q_s/\bar q\) and define \(F(\lambda)=\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\lceil c_s\lambda\rceil\). Since \(c_s>0\) and \(\sum_s\mu_sc_s=1\), the elementary inequalities \(z\le\lceil z\rceil<z+1\) give

\[ \lambda\le F(\lambda)<\lambda+1. \]

Here and below, the argument allows negative thresholds and negative values of \(r_t\).

Fix a type \(t\), and abbreviate \(r=r_t\). The function \(F\) is nondecreasing and left-continuous. The preceding inequalities imply that \(r-1\) is feasible for \(F(\lambda)\le r\), whereas every \(\lambda>r\) is infeasible. Consequently, the feasible set has a finite supremum in \([r-1,r]\). Left-continuity shows that this supremum is feasible, so the maximum \(\Lambda_t\) in the question exists.

Moreover, \(\Lambda_t\) belongs to one of the breakpoint grids:

\[ \Lambda_t\in\bigcup_{s\in T}\{k/c_s:k\in\mathbb Z\}. \]

Indeed, these grids are locally finite. If \(\Lambda_t\) belonged to none of them, every summand of \(F\) would be constant in a neighborhood of \(\Lambda_t\). A slightly larger argument would then remain feasible, contradicting maximality.

For every \(s\), compute

\[ K_{t,s}=\max\{k\in\mathbb Z:F(k/c_s)\le r_t\}. \]

This is an integer binary search with feasible lower endpoint \(k^-=\lfloor c_s(r_t-1)\rfloor\) and infeasible upper endpoint \(k^+=\lceil c_s(r_t+1)\rceil\). To check these endpoints, observe that \(k^-/c_s\le r_t-1\), hence \(F(k^-/c_s)<r_t\), while \(k^+/c_s\ge r_t+1\), hence \(F(k^+/c_s)>r_t\).

Every \(K_{t,s}/c_s\) is feasible and therefore at most \(\Lambda_t\). Since \(\Lambda_t\) belongs to some breakpoint grid, equality holds for at least one \(s\). Thus

\[ \Lambda_t=\max_{s\in T}\frac{K_{t,s}}{c_s}, \qquad h_t=\lceil c_t\Lambda_t\rceil. \]

All numbers in these searches have polynomial bit length. Specifically, the rational numbers \(c_s,r_t\) have polynomial bit length, and the integer endpoints have magnitude at most \(c_s(|r_t|+1)+1\). Binary search therefore uses polynomially many iterations. Each predicate evaluation consists of \(\tau\) exact rational operations and ceiling computations.

Hence every \(h_t\) is computable exactly in polynomial time and has polynomial bit length.

2. Welfare equality and the linear program

For every category \(o\), define \(w_o=\max_{t\in T}u_t(o)\), and for every type define its permitted categories by \(J_t=\{o\in O:u_t(o)=w_o\}\).

For any distribution satisfying the supply equations, its welfare shortfall equals

\[ \sum_o\sigma_ow_o-\sum_{t,B}\pi_{t,B}u_t(B) = \sum_{t,B,o}\pi_{t,B}B_o\bigl(w_o-u_t(o)\bigr). \]

Every summand on the right is nonnegative. Therefore welfare equality holds if and only if every positive-mass bundle assigned to type \(t\) has zero coordinates outside \(J_t\).

Introduce variables \(x_{t,o}\), representing the total mass of category \(o\) assigned to type \(t\). Consider the following rational linear feasibility system:

  • \(x_{t,o}\ge0\) for all \(t,o\).
  • \(x_{t,o}=0\) whenever \(o\notin J_t\).
  • \(\sum_{t\in T}x_{t,o}=\sigma_o\) for every \(o\).
  • \(\sum_{o\in O}u_t(o)x_{t,o}\ge\mu_th_t\) for every \(t\).

A feasible allocation necessarily gives a solution: set \(x_{t,o}=\sum_B\pi_{t,B}B_o\). The supply and permitted-category conditions follow immediately. Averaging the bundlewise utility inequality gives

\[ \sum_o u_t(o)x_{t,o} = \sum_B\pi_{t,B}u_t(B) \ge h_t\sum_B\pi_{t,B} = \mu_th_t. \]

The converse requires converting mean bundles into integral bundles. The next lemma establishes exactly what is needed.

3. Constructive integral-bundle lemma

Lemma. Let \(a\in\{-1,0,1\}^d\), \(h\in\mathbb Z\), and \(y\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^d\) satisfy \(a\cdot y\ge h\). Then one can construct, in polynomial time, a rational probability distribution supported on at most \(d+1\) bundles \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d\) such that:

  • its mean is \(y\);
  • every supported bundle satisfies \(a\cdot B\ge h\);
  • every coordinate satisfies \(\lfloor y_i\rfloor\le B_i\le\lceil y_i\rceil\).

In particular, coordinates with \(y_i=0\) stay zero.

Proof. For \(d=0\), use the empty bundle. Assume \(d>0\), and reorder coordinates so that the \(p\) nonzero coefficients of \(a\) come first.

For each coordinate define an integer \(b_i\) and a fractional part \(f_i\) as follows:

  • If \(a_i=-1\), set \(b_i=\lceil y_i\rceil\) and \(f_i=b_i-y_i\).
  • If \(a_i\in\{0,1\}\), set \(b_i=\lfloor y_i\rfloor\) and \(f_i=y_i-b_i\).

Thus \(0\le f_i<1\). Define \(S_0=0\) and \(S_i=\sum_{j=1}^i f_j\). For a parameter \(\theta\in[0,1)\), let

\[ z_i(\theta)=\lfloor S_i+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor S_{i-1}+\theta\rfloor. \]

Because \(S_i-S_{i-1}=f_i\in[0,1)\), each \(z_i(\theta)\) belongs to \(\{0,1\}\). Define \(B_i(\theta)=b_i-z_i(\theta)\) when \(a_i=-1\), and \(B_i(\theta)=b_i+z_i(\theta)\) otherwise.

These bundles are integral and nonnegative. If \(y_i\) is integral, then \(f_i=0\), so \(z_i(\theta)=0\). Otherwise the two possible coordinates are precisely \(\lfloor y_i\rfloor\) and \(\lceil y_i\rceil\).

To verify the utility bound, put

\[ C=\sum_{i:a_i=1}b_i-\sum_{i:a_i=-1}b_i. \]

The definitions give \(a\cdot y=C+S_p\). Since the nonzero coefficients come first, telescoping gives

\[ a\cdot B(\theta) = C+\sum_{i=1}^p z_i(\theta) = C+\lfloor S_p+\theta\rfloor. \]

The integer \(h-C\) satisfies \(S_p\ge h-C\), so \(\lfloor S_p+\theta\rfloor\ge h-C\). Hence every \(B(\theta)\) satisfies \(a\cdot B(\theta)\ge h\).

For the mean, temporarily take \(\theta\) uniformly distributed on \([0,1)\). The identity \(\mathbb E[\lfloor s+\theta\rfloor]=s\), valid for every real \(s\), yields \(\mathbb E[z_i(\theta)]=S_i-S_{i-1}=f_i\). Therefore \(\mathbb E[B_i(\theta)]=y_i\).

Finally, this distribution has an explicit finite representation. Each function \(\lfloor S_i+\theta\rfloor\) has at most one breakpoint inside \((0,1)\). The union of these breakpoints partitions \((0,1)\) into at most \(d+1\) intervals. On each interval the entire bundle \(B(\theta)\) is constant. Assign that bundle probability equal to the interval’s length, evaluating it at the interval’s rational midpoint. Endpoints have zero probability.

All breakpoints are rational fractional parts of \(-S_i\). Computing and sorting them, evaluating the bundles, and forming the probabilities require polynomial time and produce numbers of polynomial bit length. This proves the lemma. ∎

4. From an LP solution to the required allocation

Suppose the linear program has a rational feasible solution \(x\). For every type \(t\), define its conditional mean bundle by \(y^{(t)}_o=x_{t,o}/\mu_t\). This is well-defined because every listed type has positive mass.

The type constraint gives \(u_t\cdot y^{(t)}\ge h_t\). Apply the lemma with \(a=u_t\), \(h=h_t\), and \(y=y^{(t)}\). It produces bundles \(B^{(t,j)}\) with rational probabilities \(\alpha_{t,j}\) satisfying

\[ \sum_j\alpha_{t,j}=1, \qquad \sum_j\alpha_{t,j}B^{(t,j)}=y^{(t)}, \qquad u_t(B^{(t,j)})\ge h_t. \]

Set \(\pi_{t,B^{(t,j)}}=\mu_t\alpha_{t,j}\), combining duplicate entries if necessary.

The type masses are correct. For every category \(o\), the total allocated supply is

\[ \sum_{t,j}\mu_t\alpha_{t,j}B^{(t,j)}_o = \sum_t\mu_ty^{(t)}_o = \sum_t x_{t,o} = \sigma_o. \]

Every supported bundle satisfies its type’s utility threshold.

Furthermore, if \(o\notin J_t\), then \(x_{t,o}=y^{(t)}_o=0\). The lemma preserves that zero coordinate in every bundle. Therefore every allocated item goes to a maximizing type, establishing the required welfare equality.

This proves that the original allocation problem is feasible exactly when the linear program is feasible.

5. Complexity and conclusion

The linear program has at most \(m\tau\) variables and polynomially many constraints. Section 1 proves that its coefficients and right-hand sides have polynomial bit length. It is also bounded: nonnegativity and the supply equations imply \(x_{t,o}\le\sigma_o\).

We use the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: feasibility can be decided, and a rational feasible point produced when one exists, in time polynomial in the system’s encoding length. Its rationality and finite-encoding hypotheses hold here; boundedness also holds as just verified. Polynomial-time LP algorithms and the polynomial encoding bounds for basic feasible solutions are described in these [MIT lecture notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html).

Dividing the resulting coordinates by the positive rational masses preserves polynomial bit length. The rounding lemma then produces at most \(m+1\) entries per type, using polynomial time and polynomial-size bundle coordinates and masses.

Thus both feasibility and construction are polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). The argument is direct for the stated mass-and-integral-bundle model and does not require transferring the finite-agent existence theorem.
