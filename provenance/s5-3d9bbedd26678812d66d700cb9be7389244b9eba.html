Theorem 2.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite hospital set \(\mathcal H\), a finite set \(T\) of complete resident types, rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), and rational per-capita quotas \(0\leq \ell_h\leq u_h\), decide whether there is a feasible assignment of the atomless mass of each type to an acceptable hospital or to \(\bot\) that is robustly mass-popular. Each type specifies its resident ranking and its priority class at every hospital; hospitals rank classes, with residents in the same class tied. An assignment is a measurable function from each type's mass space \(\Omega_t\), of measure \(\mu_t\), to an acceptable hospital or \(\bot\), with hospital assignment mass between \(\ell_h\) and \(u_h\). Compare two assignments by integrating resident votes pointwise and, for each hospital, padding to \(u_h\) with \(\bot\), canceling common assigned mass, and pairing the remaining outgoing and incoming mass by any measure-preserving correspondence; hospital votes follow its priority classes. Find an assignment that no feasible alternative defeats under any admissible set of correspondences, or report that none exists. Determine whether this can be done in time polynomial in \(|\mathcal H|\), \(\tau=|T|\), and the input bit length \(L\), with a finite rational description of the assignment.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(m=|\mathcal H|\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type specifies a mutually acceptable set \(A_t\subseteq\mathcal H\), a strict ranking of \(A_t\), and its priority class at each acceptable hospital. Each hospital strictly orders its classes; residents in the same class are tied. Every acceptable partner is preferred to \(\bot\). Quotas are binary rationals with \(0\leq\ell_h\leq u_h\) and \(u_h>0\).
Realize type \(t\) as \(\Omega_t=[0,\mu_t)\) with Lebesgue measure. An assignment \(M\) is measurable, with \(M(x)\in A_t\cup\{\bot\}\); it is feasible when every hospital receives mass in \([\ell_h,u_h]\). Alternatives use the same resident spaces and may be arbitrary measurable assignments.
For assignments \(N,M\), resident votes are integrated pointwise. At each hospital, cancel residents assigned there in both assignments and cancel common vacancy mass \(\min\{u_h-|N(h)|,u_h-|M(h)|\}\); pad the residual sides with the remaining vacancy mass labeled \(\bot\), and pair them by any measure-preserving bijection modulo null sets.
Each resident or paired hospital position contributes \(+1\) if it prefers the outcome in \(N\), \(-1\) if it prefers the outcome in \(M\), and \(0\) for indifference. Let \(\Delta_C(N,M)\) be the sum of resident and hospital vote integrals for correspondences \(C\). Assignment \(M\) is robustly mass-popular if \(\Delta_C(N,M)\leq0\) for every feasible \(N\) and every admissible \(C\).
A finite rational description consists of a partition of each \(\Omega_t\) into finitely many intervals with rational endpoints, each labeled by its assigned hospital or \(\bot\).

Is deciding whether a feasible robustly mass-popular assignment exists in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? In the polynomial-time case, can an assignment with a finite rational description, or a report that none exists, be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For discrete hospital–resident instances with strict preferences, positive integer upper quotas, and nonnegative integer lower quotas, a feasible matching popular for every hospital correspondence can be computed in polynomial time whenever feasibility holds ([A M et al.](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol103-sea2018/LIPIcs.SEA.2018.9/LIPIcs.SEA.2018.9.pdf)).
Popular half-integral matchings under expected voting in strict stable-marriage instances admit polynomial-time minimum-cost computation ([Kavitha](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol055-icalp2016/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.22/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.22.pdf)); this uses a different comparison rule. No result settling the exact atomless problem above was located.

  • Made acceptability mutual and placed every acceptable partner above being unmatched or having a vacant position.

Result

Deciding whether a feasible robustly mass-popular assignment exists is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. This holds even when all lower quotas are zero and all hospitals have the same dyadic rational upper quota.

The proof must account for atomless splitting: merely replacing each discrete resident by an interval would not, by itself, establish hardness. We prove that, for the constructed instances, an atomless robustly popular assignment exists if and only if the original discrete instance admits a popular matching.

Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm that computes a finite rational assignment or correctly reports nonexistence would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. The discrete hardness theorem

We use the following theorem of Cseh, Huang, and Kavitha:

Given a bipartite graph \(\smash{G=(A\cup B,E)}\), deciding whether it admits a popular matching is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete when every vertex of \(A\) has strict preferences and every vertex of \(B\) either has strict preferences or ties all its neighbors.

Here every vertex casts one vote, indifference contributes zero, and an acceptable partner is preferred to being unmatched. This is Theorem 3 of [*Popular Matchings with Two-Sided Preferences and One-Sided Ties*](https://hpi.de/friedrich/docs/publications/2017/SIAMDM.pdf).

For matchings \(Q,K\), write their discrete vote margin as

\[ D(Q,K)=\sum_{a\in A}v_a(Q(a),K(a))+\sum_{b\in B}v_b(Q(b),K(b)), \]

where \(v_z(c,d)\in\{-1,0,1\}\) is vertex \(z\)'s vote for \(c\) against \(d\), and an unmatched vertex has partner \(\bot\). Thus \(K\) is popular precisely when \(D(Q,K)\leq0\) for every matching \(Q\).

The theorem's hypotheses match the required resident preferences and hospital priority classes: a strict hospital preference becomes ordered singleton classes, while a hospital tying all neighbors has one class.

2. The reduction

Take an instance of this discrete problem with \(n=|A|\geq1\). Let \(q\) be the least power of two satisfying \(q\geq n\), and put \(\delta=1/q\). Add \(q-n\) isolated applicants. They are always unmatched and cast zero votes between any two matchings, so this preserves the existence of a popular matching. Denote the augmented applicant set by \(A'\).

Construct the society as follows:

  • The hospitals are the vertices of \(B\).
  • Every hospital has quotas \(\ell_b=0\) and \(u_b=\delta\).
  • Each applicant has resident mass \(\delta\), with its discrete acceptable set and strict ranking.
  • Hospital priority classes reproduce the discrete preferences.

If several applicants have identical type data, merge them into one supported type. A type containing \(k_t\) applicants has mass \(\mu_t=k_t\delta\). Its interval \(\Omega_t\) can be partitioned into intervals \(I_a\), one for each constituent applicant, each of measure \(\delta\). These intervals serve only as labels in the proof.

All masses and quotas are dyadic rationals. Their total resident mass is \(q\delta=1\), every supported mass is positive, and \(u_b>0\). Moreover, the all-unmatched assignment is feasible.

The construction is polynomial: \(q<2n\) unless \(q=n=1\), the support size is at most \(q\), and the rational numbers require \(O(\log q)\) bits. In particular, the reduction does not enumerate possible types outside the support.

3. A decomposition lemma

Let

\[ P(G)=\left\{x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{E}: \sum_bx_{ab}\leq1\text{ for every }a,\quad \sum_ax_{ab}\leq1\text{ for every }b\right\}. \]

Every \(x\in P(G)\) is a finite convex combination of incidence vectors of discrete matchings:

\[ x=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_i\chi^{K_i}, \qquad \lambda_i>0,\qquad \sum_i\lambda_i=1. \]

Justification. The constraint matrix consists of the vertex-edge incidence matrix of a bipartite graph, together with nonnegativity constraints. Negating the rows indexed by applicants turns the incidence matrix into a directed incidence matrix, which is totally unimodular. Adding signed unit rows preserves total unimodularity. With integral right-hand sides, every vertex of \(P(G)\) is therefore integral. Its integral points are incidence vectors of matchings. Since \(P(G)\) is bounded, it is the convex hull of its vertices.

We also define \(x_{a\bot}=1-\sum_bx_{ab}\). The same decomposition then gives

\[ x_{a\bot}=\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf1_{\{K_i(a)=\bot\}}. \]

4. An atomless robust assignment implies a discrete popular matching

Let \(M\) be any feasible robustly mass-popular assignment in the constructed society. No rationality or regularity beyond measurability is assumed.

For each original applicant label \(a\in A'\), define

\[ x_{ab}=\frac{|I_a\cap M^{-1}(b)|}{\delta}, \qquad x_{a\bot}=\frac{|I_a\cap M^{-1}(\bot)|}{\delta}. \]

Each row sums to one, and hospital feasibility gives \(\sum_ax_{ab}\leq1\). Consequently, the decomposition lemma applies. Fix any component \(K=K_i\) with coefficient \(\lambda=\lambda_i>0\).

We prove that \(K\) is popular. Suppose instead that a discrete matching \(Q\) satisfies \(D(Q,K)>0\).

For every applicant \(a\), the decomposition implies

\[ |I_a\cap M^{-1}(K(a))|\geq\lambda\delta. \]

By atomlessness, choose a measurable subset \(S_a\) of this set of measure exactly \(\lambda\delta\). Define an alternative \(N\) by assigning all residents of \(S_a\) to \(Q(a)\), and leaving everyone else as in \(M\).

Feasibility of the alternative

Let \(o_b(J)\in\{0,1\}\) indicate whether matching \(J\) occupies hospital \(b\). The new hospital load is

\[ |N(b)|=|M(b)|+\lambda\delta\bigl(o_b(Q)-o_b(K)\bigr) =\delta\left(\lambda o_b(Q)+\sum_{j\ne i}\lambda_j o_b(K_j)\right). \]

The expression in parentheses lies in \([0,1]\). Hence \(0\leq|N(b)|\leq\delta\), so \(N\) is feasible. Its assignments are acceptable because \(Q\) is a matching in the original graph.

Resident votes

Every resident of \(S_a\) changes from \(K(a)\) to \(Q(a)\). Thus the resident vote integral is

\[ \lambda\delta\sum_{a\in A'}v_a(Q(a),K(a)). \]

Hospital votes, including vacancy cancellation

At each hospital \(b\), there are four possibilities.

  • The partner is unchanged: \(K(b)=Q(b)\). No resident changes membership at \(b\), so its hospital vote is zero.
  • Both partners are real and distinct: \(K(b)=a\) and \(Q(b)=c\). Exactly \(\lambda\delta\) mass leaves from \(S_a\), and exactly that mass enters from \(S_c\). All other assigned residents cancel. Occupancy is unchanged, so all vacancy mass cancels. Every residual pair has vote \(v_b(c,a)\).
  • The hospital becomes occupied: \(K(b)=\bot\) and \(Q(b)=c\). Exactly \(\lambda\delta\) mass enters and no mass leaves. After common vacancies cancel, the entering mass is paired with \(\lambda\delta\) vacancy mass on the \(M\)-side, producing vote \(+\lambda\delta\).
  • The hospital becomes unoccupied: \(K(b)=a\) and \(Q(b)=\bot\). Exactly \(\lambda\delta\) mass leaves. The residual vacancy is on the \(N\)-side, producing vote \(-\lambda\delta\).

In every case, the hospital contribution is \(\lambda\delta\,v_b(Q(b),K(b))\). The residual resident sets have constant priority classes, so their scores do not depend on the chosen correspondence. Measure-preserving bijections exist modulo null sets between the equal-mass residual spaces.

Therefore

\[ \Delta_C(N,M)=\lambda\delta D(Q,K)>0. \]

This contradicts robust popularity. Hence \(K\) is a discrete popular matching.

In particular, atomless splitting cannot create a robustly popular assignment when the discrete instance has none.

5. A discrete popular matching gives an atomless robust assignment

Conversely, let \(K\) be a discrete popular matching. Define \(M_K\) by assigning the entire interval \(I_a\) to \(K(a)\).

This is feasible: every occupied hospital receives exactly \(\delta\) mass, and every other hospital is empty.

Let \(N\) be an arbitrary feasible measurable alternative, and define its normalized marginals

\[ p_{ab}=\frac{|I_a\cap N^{-1}(b)|}{\delta}, \qquad p_{a\bot}=\frac{|I_a\cap N^{-1}(\bot)|}{\delta}. \]

Again \(p\in P(G)\). We show that its total vote against \(M_K\), divided by \(\delta\), is an affine function of these marginals, independent of the hospital correspondences.

Resident contributions

Because all residents of \(I_a\) have the same incumbent outcome \(K(a)\), the normalized resident contribution is

\[ R_K(p)=\sum_{a\in A'}\sum_{c\in A_a\cup\{\bot\}} p_{ac}v_a(c,K(a)). \]

Hospital contributions

If \(b\) is empty in \(M_K\), all entering residents are paired with vacancies. Its normalized vote is

\[ H_{b,K}(p)=\sum_ap_{ab}. \]

If \(K(b)=r\), the hospital is full in \(M_K\), and every departing resident belongs to \(I_r\). After common residents cancel, all remaining \(M_K\)-side positions therefore have the same priority class. The residual \(N\)-side consists of incoming residents and vacancy mass \(\delta-|N(b)|\). Its normalized contribution is

\[ H_{b,K}(p)= \sum_ap_{ab}v_b(a,r) -\left(1-\sum_ap_{ab}\right). \]

The term with \(a=r\) is zero. These expressions remain valid with hospital ties, and every admissible correspondence yields the same score.

Thus, for an affine function \(F_K\),

\[ \frac{\Delta_C(N,M_K)}{\delta} =F_K(p) :=R_K(p)+\sum_bH_{b,K}(p). \]

For the incidence vector of any discrete matching \(Q\), these formulas give precisely \(F_K(\chi^Q)=D(Q,K)\).

Decompose \(p=\sum_j\alpha_j\chi^{Q_j}\). Since the coefficients sum to one, affinity and the popularity of \(K\) imply

\[ \frac{\Delta_C(N,M_K)}{\delta} =\sum_j\alpha_jD(Q_j,K)\leq0. \]

This holds for every measurable feasible \(N\) and every admissible \(C\). Hence \(M_K\) is robustly mass-popular.

6. Complexity and rational descriptions

We have proved the exact equivalence

\[ G\text{ admits a discrete popular matching} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed society admits a robustly mass-popular assignment}. \]

Together with the discrete hardness theorem and the polynomial construction, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

Every yes-instance produced by the reduction also has a finite rational description. The assignment \(M_K\) uses intervals with endpoints that are multiples of \(\delta\). Within each merged type, the constituent intervals can be ordered by assigned outcome and adjacent equal labels combined. This gives at most \(|A_t|+1\) intervals per type, with endpoints requiring \(O(\log q)\) bits. Reordering residents within an identical type preserves all vote integrals, intersections, and admissible correspondences by a measure-preserving relabeling.

Thus hardness persists on a family whose yes-instances have polynomial-size rational descriptions. A polynomial-time algorithm producing such an assignment or correctly reporting that none exists would decide the discrete \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem, and therefore would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
