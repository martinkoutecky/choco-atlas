Theorem 1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), positive rational per-member powers \(g_t\), and rational \(\alpha\in[1/2,1)\), let a coalition be a mass vector \(x\) with \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) and power \(\Gamma(x)=\sum_t g_t x_t\). Does there exist a scale-covariant correspondence \(\phi\) on every nonzero subpopulation \(x\le\mu\) that satisfies the paper’s translated Inclusion, Power, Enforcement, and Group Rationality axioms, equivalently selecting minimum-power winning self-enforcing subcoalitions? If so, characterize and compute \(\phi(\mu)\) in terms of the type count, support size, and input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), positive binary rational per-member powers \(g_t\), and binary rational \(\alpha\in[1/2,1)\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. There is no separate parameter \(m\).
Let \(D_\mu=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{T}:0\neq x\leq\mu\}\), with inequalities interpreted coordinatewise, and let \(\Gamma(x)=\sum_tg_tx_t\). Mass is divisible: all real vectors in this domain are allowed, and subpopulations are not renormalized.
A correspondence \(\phi\) assigns a set of coalitions to every \(x\in D_\mu\). Scale covariance means \(\phi(cx)=\{cy:y\in\phi(x)\}\) whenever \(c>0\) and \(x,cx\in D_\mu\).
Inclusion requires \(\varnothing\neq\phi(x)\subseteq\{y:0\neq y\leq x\}\). Power requires \(\Gamma(y)>\alpha\Gamma(x)\) for every \(y\in\phi(x)\); equality is losing.
Enforcement requires \(y\in\phi(y)\) for every \(y\in\phi(x)\); a coalition \(z\) is self-enforcing precisely when \(z\in\phi(z)\).
Group Rationality requires, for every \(y\in\phi(x)\) and every \(0\neq z\leq x\) with \(\Gamma(z)>\alpha\Gamma(x)\) and \(z\in\phi(z)\), that \(z\notin\phi(x)\Longleftrightarrow\Gamma(y)<\Gamma(z)\). Thus all tied minimum-power eligible coalitions must be selected.

Does every such society admit a scale-covariant correspondence satisfying these four axioms, or can existence fail?
When one exists, is it unique, and how can \(\phi(\mu)\) be characterized and computed, with running time expressed in \(\tau\) and \(L\)?

For finite societies of indivisible agents, these axioms determine a unique correspondence, constructible by induction over coalition size; it is single-valued when distinct coalitions have distinct powers. [Discrete result](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/egorov/ftp/Coalition%20Formation%20in%20Non-Democracies.pdf).
No result settling this exact divisible-mass formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Undefined translated axioms were replaced by their explicit coordinatewise versions, including the complete Group Rationality biconditional and selection of all ties.
  • Coalition coordinates range over real masses, although the input is rational; every nonzero subpopulation is in the domain and is not renormalized.
  • Scale covariance was interpreted as covariance under uniform multiplication of subpopulation masses whenever both vectors lie in the domain, keeping per-member powers and the threshold fixed.
  • No genericity assumption was imposed because discrete existence and uniqueness do not require it; equal-power outcomes remain permitted.
  • The unspecified type-count parameter was identified with the support size; this model has no separate parameter m.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Conclusion

No society satisfying the stated hypotheses admits such a correspondence. The obstruction applies to every support size \(\tau\geq 1\): scale covariance contradicts Group Rationality once Enforcement supplies a self-enforcing coalition.

Consequently, there is no correspondence whose uniqueness or value \(\phi(\mu)\) can be studied under these axioms.

Proof

Suppose, for contradiction, that \(\phi\) satisfies all the requirements.

By Inclusion, choose \(y\in\phi(\mu)\). Then \(0\neq y\leq\mu\), so \(y\in D_\mu\). Because all powers \(g_t\) are positive, \(\Gamma(y)>0\). Enforcement gives \(y\in\phi(y)\).

Choose \(c=(1+\alpha)/2\). Since \(\alpha<1\), we have \(\alpha<c<1\). Define \(z=cy\). Then \(0\neq z\leq y\leq\mu\), so \(z\in D_\mu\), and linearity gives \(\Gamma(z)=c\Gamma(y)\). In particular, \(\alpha\Gamma(y)<\Gamma(z)<\Gamma(y)\).

Scale covariance at \(y\) gives \(\phi(z)=\{cw:w\in\phi(y)\}\). Since \(y\in\phi(y)\), it follows that \(z=cy\in\phi(z)\). Thus \(z\) is self-enforcing.

Now apply Group Rationality twice, both times with ambient population \(y\).

  • Selected coalition \(y\), competing coalition \(z\). The competitor satisfies every eligibility condition: \(0\neq z\leq y\), \(\Gamma(z)>\alpha\Gamma(y)\), and \(z\in\phi(z)\). Hence Group Rationality states that \(z\notin\phi(y)\) if and only if \(\Gamma(y)<\Gamma(z)\). The latter inequality is false, so \(z\in\phi(y)\).
  • Selected coalition \(z\), competing coalition \(y\). The competitor is eligible because \(0\neq y\leq y\), \(\Gamma(y)>\alpha\Gamma(y)\), and \(y\in\phi(y)\). Hence Group Rationality states that \(y\notin\phi(y)\) if and only if \(\Gamma(z)<\Gamma(y)\). The latter inequality is true, so \(y\notin\phi(y)\).

This contradicts \(y\in\phi(y)\), proving nonexistence.

Consequences and computation

The contradiction uses only positivity of power, \(\alpha<1\), and the availability of smaller positive multiples in the domain. It therefore applies throughout the specified parameter range, including \(\alpha=1/2\), and already when \(\tau=1\).

For inputs promised to satisfy the stated hypotheses, the existence decision algorithm always returns “does not exist”, in \(O(1)\) time, independently of \(\tau\) and \(L\). There is no \(\phi(\mu)\) to characterize or compute. Uniqueness is only vacuous.

The indivisible setting does not admit the crucial scaling step: a smaller positive multiple of a coalition need not be an admissible coalition. Thus this obstruction does not contradict the stated discrete result.
