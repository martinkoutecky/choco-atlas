The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) goods of unit supply and a finite support \(T\) of buyer types, with rational total population mass \(N>0\), type proportions \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), and rational per-buyer budgets \(b_t\), utilities \(u_{tj}\), and transaction costs \(c_{tj}\), find prices \(p_j\ge\epsilon\) and per-member allocations \(x_{tj}\ge 0\) such that \(\sum_j(p_j+c_{tj})x_{tj}=b_t\) for every type \(t\); \(\sum_t N\mu_t x_{tj}\le 1\) for every good \(j\), with \(\sum_t N\mu_t x_{tj}\ge 1/(1+\epsilon)\) whenever \(p_j>\epsilon\); and \(x_{tj}>0\) only if \(u_{tj}\ge\alpha_t(p_j+c_{tj})/(1+\epsilon)\), where \(\alpha_t=\max_j u_{tj}/(p_j+c_{tj})\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) buyer types, each with positive binary-rational mass \(a_t\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(N=\sum_t a_t>0\) and \(\mu_t=a_t/N\), without requiring \(N=1\).
There are \(m\geq1\) divisible goods, each of unit supply. Type \(t\) has linear utilities with rational coefficients \(u_{tj}\geq0\), at least one positive, rational per-member budget \(b_t>0\), and finite rational transaction costs \(c_{tj}\geq0\) per unit. Its effective unit price is \(p_j+c_{tj}\).
The accuracy parameter \(0<\epsilon\leq1\) is binary rational. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including masses, budgets, utilities, costs, and \(\epsilon\).
An \(\epsilon\)-approximate equilibrium consists of rational prices \(p_j\geq\epsilon\) and nonnegative rational per-member allocations \(x_{tj}\), with \(\alpha_t=\max_j u_{tj}/(p_j+c_{tj})\), satisfying
\(\sum_j(p_j+c_{tj})x_{tj}=b_t\) for every type, and \(\sum_t a_t x_{tj}\leq1\) for every good;
\(p_j>\epsilon\Rightarrow\sum_t a_t x_{tj}\geq1/(1+\epsilon)\), and \(x_{tj}>0\Rightarrow u_{tj}\geq\alpha_t(p_j+c_{tj})/(1+\epsilon)\). Ties impose no additional restriction.

Is computing such an \(\epsilon\)-approximate equilibrium in \(\mathrm{P}\), with bit running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this search problem \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For an explicit list of \(n\) buyers, an algorithm computes the same approximation in \(O(\epsilon^{-1}(n+\log m)mn\log(B/\epsilon))\) operations, where \(B=(1+\epsilon)\sum_i B_i\). A convex formulation also yields a claimed polynomial-time approximation to exact equilibrium, with logarithmic dependence on numerical precision. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/1001.0393v2)

Result

The search problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\): an equilibrium satisfying all the stated conditions can be computed with bit running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The proof requires more than substituting the support size into the auction algorithm. We use convex optimization to approximate prices, then recover rational allocations by an exactly feasible rational linear program.

1. Absorbing the masses

Define aggregate budgets and allocations by
\[ W_t=a_tb_t,\qquad y_{tj}=a_tx_{tj}. \]

Then the budget and supply constraints become
\[ \sum_j(p_j+c_{tj})y_{tj}=W_t,\qquad \sum_t y_{tj}\leq1. \]

The market-clearing and approximate-optimality conditions are unchanged, because \(a_t>0\). Conversely, aggregate allocations give per-member allocations through \(x_{tj}=y_{tj}/a_t\).

These transformations preserve polynomial encoding length. Thus it suffices to solve a market with \(\tau\) buyers and rational budgets \(W_t\). No expansion into individual society members is needed.

We also divide each utility row by its positive maximum. Henceforth,
\[ 0\leq u_{tj}\leq1,\qquad \max_j u_{tj}=1. \]
This normalization preserves every bang-per-buck comparison and the required approximation condition.

2. An auxiliary equilibrium with a price floor

Set
\[ k=\epsilon/8,\qquad W'_t=(1+k)W_t,\qquad S'=\sum_tW'_t. \]

Consider the following convex program, with natural logarithms:
\[ \min_{p,\beta}\left\{\sum_jp_j-\sum_tW'_t\log\beta_t\right\}, \]
subject to
\[ p_j\geq\epsilon,\qquad \beta_t>0,\qquad p_j+c_{tj}\geq u_{tj}\beta_t. \]

This is the price–inverse-bang-per-buck formulation from Appendix A of the [cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1001.0393v2), with inflated budgets and a positive price floor. We establish the properties needed here explicitly.

For fixed \(\beta\), the minimizing prices are
\[ P_j(\beta)=\max\left\{\epsilon,\max_t(u_{tj}\beta_t-c_{tj})\right\}. \]
Consequently, the program reduces to minimizing
\[ f(\beta)=\sum_jP_j(\beta)-\sum_tW'_t\log\beta_t \]
over the positive orthant.

Existence. Put \(C=\max_{t,j}c_{tj}\) and \(H=\max_t\beta_t\). A type attaining \(H\) has some utility coefficient equal to \(1\), so
\[ f(\beta)\geq H-C-S'\log H. \]
This lower bound tends to infinity as \(H\) tends to infinity. If \(H\) stays bounded and any coordinate \(\beta_t\) tends to zero, its negative logarithmic term tends to infinity, while the other terms are bounded below. Thus finite sublevel sets are bounded and stay away from the boundary of the positive orthant. Continuity therefore gives a minimizer \(\beta^*\), with prices \(p^*=P(\beta^*)\).

Equilibrium allocations. The original program satisfies Slater’s condition: choose any positive \(\beta\), then choose all prices sufficiently large to make every inequality strict. Its differentiable convex objective and affine constraints therefore admit KKT multipliers at the optimum.

Let \(q^*_{tj}\geq0\) be the multipliers for
\(u_{tj}\beta_t-p_j-c_{tj}\leq0\), and let \(\rho_j\geq0\) be those for \(\epsilon-p_j\leq0\). Stationarity and complementary slackness give
\[ \sum_tq^*_{tj}+\rho_j=1,\qquad \sum_ju_{tj}q^*_{tj}=W'_t/\beta^*_t, \]
\[ q^*_{tj}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ p^*_j+c_{tj}=u_{tj}\beta^*_t,\qquad \rho_j(p^*_j-\epsilon)=0. \]

It follows that
\[ \sum_tq^*_{tj}\leq1,\qquad p^*_j>\epsilon\ \Longrightarrow\ \sum_tq^*_{tj}=1, \]
and, for every type,
\[ \sum_j(p^*_j+c_{tj})q^*_{tj} =\beta^*_t\sum_ju_{tj}q^*_{tj}=W'_t. \]

Every type receives a positive allocation somewhere because its budget is positive. The price inequalities, together with equality on allocated goods, show that
\[ \alpha^*_t=\max_j\frac{u_{tj}}{p^*_j+c_{tj}}=\frac1{\beta^*_t}. \]
Thus \(q^*\) allocates only exact maximizers at \(p^*\). These auxiliary prices and allocations may be irrational; they will serve only as witnesses.

3. Approximating the auxiliary prices in polynomial bit time

First obtain explicit bounds. If \(p^*_j>\epsilon\), full allocation and nonnegative transaction costs imply
\[ p^*_j =p^*_j\sum_tq^*_{tj} \leq\sum_t(p^*_j+c_{tj})q^*_{tj} \leq S'. \]
Therefore \(p^*_j\leq\max\{\epsilon,S'\}\).

Since some \(u_{tj}=1\) in every row,
\[ \beta^*_t\leq\max\{\epsilon,S'\}+C. \]
Also \(q^*_{tj}\leq1\), so the KKT utility equation gives
\[ W'_t/\beta^*_t=\sum_ju_{tj}q^*_{tj}\leq m, \qquad \beta^*_t\geq W'_t/m. \]

Define the rational numbers
\[ d=\min\left\{1,\frac{\min_tW'_t}{2m}\right\},\qquad R=S'+C+\epsilon+1,\qquad Q=[d,R]^\tau. \]
Then \(\beta^*\in Q\).

On this box, \(f\) is strongly convex with parameter
\[ \gamma=\frac{\min_tW'_t}{R^2}>0. \]
Indeed, the maximum-affine price terms are convex, and the Hessian of the logarithmic part has diagonal entries \(W'_t/\beta_t^2\geq\gamma\). Strong convexity and minimality imply
\[ f(\beta)-f(\beta^*)\geq \frac{\gamma}{2}\|\beta-\beta^*\|_2^2 \qquad(\beta\in Q). \]

Choose
\[ \eta=\epsilon^2/128,\qquad \Delta=\gamma\eta^2/2. \]
We next justify that a rational \(\widehat\beta\in Q\) with
\[ f(\widehat\beta)\leq f(\beta^*)+\Delta \]
can be computed in polynomial bit time.

The weak-optimization hypotheses

We use the standard ellipsoid weak-optimization theorem: given a compact convex body with explicitly known inner and outer balls and a polynomial-time weak separation oracle, rational weak linear optimization is polynomial in the dimension, geometric encoding lengths, and accuracy encoding length. It returns a point within the requested distance of the body whose linear objective is within the requested additive error of the optimum. This is the separation–optimization theorem of [Grötschel, Lovász and Schrijver](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/10046/10046D.pdf).

Here are explicit hypotheses for its application.

A rational subgradient of \(f\) at any rational \(\beta\in Q\) is computable exactly: choose an active affine piece for each \(P_j\), then add the logarithmic gradient \((-W'_t/\beta_t)_t\). Its Euclidean norm is at most
\[ G=m+S'/d. \]
The price terms each have subgradients of norm at most \(1\), and the logarithmic gradient has norm at most its sum of absolute coordinates, which is at most \(S'/d\). Consequently \(f\) is \(G\)-Lipschitz on \(Q\).

Since \(d\leq1<R\),
\[ |f(\beta)|\leq M:=mR+S'(R+1/d)\qquad(\beta\in Q). \]
Consider the compact convex epigraph truncation
\[ K=\{(\beta,z):\beta\in Q,\ f(\beta)\leq z\leq M+2\}. \]

It contains a ball centered at
\[ \left(\frac{d+R}{2}\mathbf1,\ M+1\right) \]
of radius \(\min\{(R-d)/4,1/2\}\), and it lies in an origin-centered ball of radius \(\tau R+M+2\). All these parameters are rational and have polynomial encoding length.

For completeness, its weak separation oracle works as follows. A point outside \(Q\), or above \(z=M+2\), has an exact coordinate separator. Otherwise evaluate \(f(\beta)\) to additive error \(h/4\), obtaining \(v\). If \(v\leq z+h/2\), raising \(z\) to \(\max\{z,f(\beta)\}\) places the point in \(K\) within distance \(3h/4\). If \(v>z+h/2\), then \(f(\beta)>z\), and an exact rational subgradient \(g\) supplies the separator with normal \((g,-1)\). Indeed, every \((\beta',z')\in K\) satisfies
\[ g\cdot(\beta'-\beta)-(z'-z) \leq z-f(\beta)<0. \]
Logarithms of positive rationals can be evaluated to arbitrary additive accuracy in polynomial bit time. Thus this is a polynomial-time weak separation oracle.

Apply weak optimization to minimize \(z\), with accuracy
\[ \xi=\Delta/(G+2). \]
It returns a rational \((v,z)\) within distance \(\xi\) of \(K\), with \(z\leq f(\beta^*)+\xi\). Clamp each coordinate of \(v\) to \([d,R]\), obtaining \(\widehat\beta\).

There is \((\beta_0,z_0)\in K\) within distance \(\xi\) of \((v,z)\). Coordinatewise clamping cannot increase distance to \(\beta_0\), so
\[ f(\widehat\beta) \leq f(\beta_0)+G\xi \leq z_0+G\xi \leq z+(G+1)\xi \leq f(\beta^*)+\Delta. \]

All numerical parameters above have polynomial encoding length. This proves the required polynomial bit-time approximation. Strong convexity now yields
\[ \|\widehat\beta-\beta^*\|_2\leq\eta. \]
Define \(\widehat p_j=P_j(\widehat\beta)\). Since normalized utilities are at most \(1\),
\[ |\widehat p_j-p^*_j|\leq\eta \qquad\text{for every }j. \]

4. Handling the strict price-floor condition

Set the output prices to
\[ p_j= \begin{cases} \epsilon,&\widehat p_j\leq\epsilon+\eta,\\ \widehat p_j,&\widehat p_j>\epsilon+\eta. \end{cases} \]

These are rational and satisfy \(p_j\geq\epsilon\). Moreover,
\[ |p_j-p^*_j|\leq2\eta. \]
Crucially,
\[ p_j>\epsilon\ \Longrightarrow\ p^*_j\geq\widehat p_j-\eta>\epsilon. \]
Thus every good for which the output requires approximate clearing was fully allocated in the auxiliary equilibrium.

Put \(r=\epsilon/64\). Because \(p^*_j+c_{tj}\geq\epsilon\),
\[ (1-r)(p^*_j+c_{tj}) \leq p_j+c_{tj} \leq(1+r)(p^*_j+c_{tj}). \]

5. A feasible allocation witness

For each type, let
\[ D_t=\sum_j(p_j+c_{tj})q^*_{tj},\qquad \lambda_t=W_t/D_t,\qquad \widetilde y_{tj}=\lambda_tq^*_{tj}. \]
The effective-price bounds imply
\[ (1-r)W'_t\leq D_t\leq(1+r)W'_t, \]
hence
\[ \frac1{(1+k)(1+r)} \leq\lambda_t \leq\frac1{(1+k)(1-r)}. \]

For \(0<\epsilon\leq1\), our constants satisfy
\[ (1+k)(1-r) =1+\frac{7\epsilon}{64}-\frac{\epsilon^2}{512}\geq1, \]
\[ (1+k)(1+r) =1+\frac{9\epsilon}{64}+\frac{\epsilon^2}{512} \leq1+\epsilon. \]
Therefore
\[ \frac1{1+\epsilon}\leq\lambda_t\leq1. \]

The witness \(\widetilde y\) spends exactly \(W_t\) for every type. Its supply use is at most that of \(q^*\), so it never exceeds unit supply. If \(p_j>\epsilon\), then \(\sum_tq^*_{tj}=1\), giving
\[ \sum_t\widetilde y_{tj}\geq\frac1{1+\epsilon}. \]

It also satisfies approximate optimality. For every auxiliary allocated edge,
\[ \frac{u_{tj}}{p_j+c_{tj}}\geq\frac{\alpha^*_t}{1+r}, \]
while all edges satisfy
\[ \frac{u_{tj}}{p_j+c_{tj}}\leq\frac{\alpha^*_t}{1-r}. \]
Writing \(\alpha_t=\max_j u_{tj}/(p_j+c_{tj})\), we obtain
\[ \widetilde y_{tj}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{u_{tj}}{p_j+c_{tj}} \geq\alpha_t\frac{1-r}{1+r} \geq\frac{\alpha_t}{1+\epsilon}. \]
The last inequality follows from
\(r(2+\epsilon)\leq\epsilon\), which holds for \(r=\epsilon/64\).

Thus a feasible allocation exists at the computed rational prices. The witness itself need not be computed.

6. Recovering rational allocations exactly

Compute each \(\alpha_t\) exactly from the rational output prices, and define
\[ E=\left\{(t,j): \frac{u_{tj}}{p_j+c_{tj}}\geq\frac{\alpha_t}{1+\epsilon}\right\}. \]

Solve the rational linear feasibility problem
\[ y_{tj}\geq0,\qquad y_{tj}=0\text{ for }(t,j)\notin E, \]
\[ \sum_j(p_j+c_{tj})y_{tj}=W_t\qquad\text{for every }t, \]
\[ \sum_ty_{tj}\leq1\qquad\text{for every }j, \]
\[ \sum_ty_{tj}\geq\frac1{1+\epsilon} \qquad\text{for every }j\text{ with }p_j>\epsilon. \]

Section 5 proves real feasibility. This is a bounded rational polytope with \(\tau m\) variables, polynomially many constraints, and polynomial-length coefficients. Polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore returns an exactly feasible rational allocation of polynomial encoding length.

Finally output \(x_{tj}=y_{tj}/a_t\). The mass transformation proves that these allocations satisfy every original requirement, including exact budget exhaustion and exact supply feasibility. The allowed-edge definition imposes precisely the requested approximate-optimality condition and adds no tie restriction.

7. Bit complexity

Mass aggregation, utility normalization, all accuracy parameters, the computed prices, and the final linear program have polynomial encoding length. Weak convex optimization uses accuracy whose binary encoding length is polynomial in the input length; it therefore has logarithmic dependence on \(1/\epsilon\). The final rational linear program is also solvable in polynomial bit time.

Consequently, the stated support-encoded search problem is polynomial-time solvable.
