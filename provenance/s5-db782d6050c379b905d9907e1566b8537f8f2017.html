Result and computational model

Every fixed ratio $\rho\in(0,1]$ is achievable. In fact, there is a uniform randomized MIWR family whose expected normalized allocation converges coordinatewise to a welfare optimum.

The construction below uses independent fair random bits and has polynomial running time on every execution. It repairs the issue identified in the verifier report by using dyadic implementation probabilities and a buyer ordering generated from finitely many random bits.

The absence of a convergence-rate requirement is essential.

1. A report-independent weighted range

Order the bundles as $S_0,\ldots,S_{D-1}$, where $D=2^m$ and $S_0=\varnothing$. Write $s(S_\ell)=\ell$. Fix the lexicographic order on labeled allocations induced by this bundle order.

For a public replication size $N$, define
$$ h=\left\lfloor\frac{\log_2 N}{2m}\right\rfloor. $$
If $h=0$, the mechanism uses only the all-empty allocation, with weight $1$.

Suppose henceforth that $h>0$. Let $q$ and $a$ be the largest powers of two satisfying
$$ q^4\le h,\qquad a^8\le h, $$
and set $\delta=1/a$. Let $P$ be the smallest power of two satisfying $P\ge N$. Thus
$$ N\le P<2N. $$

We generate a random ordering of the buyers using a bounded number of fair bits. Set
$$ B=4\left\lceil\log_2(N+1)\right\rceil. $$
Give each buyer an independent uniform $B$-bit key, and sort by key, resolving equal keys by buyer label. Divide this ordering into
$$ g=\lfloor N/h\rfloor $$
full blocks of size $h$, with $r=N-gh<h$ leftover buyers.

Independently assign each buyer a rank
$$ z_i\in\left\{\frac{1}{2q},\frac{3}{2q},\ldots, \frac{2q-1}{2q}\right\} $$
uniformly. This uses exactly $\log_2 q$ fair bits per buyer.

An allocation $A$ is allowed if:

  • every leftover buyer receives $\varnothing$;
  • in every full block, at most $\lfloor hb_j\rfloor$ buyers receive good $j$.

These allocations are globally feasible because
$$ g\lfloor hb_j\rfloor\le ghb_j\le Nb_j. $$

For an allowed allocation, define
$$ K(A)=\sum_i s(A_i)(2qz_i), \qquad c(A)=\frac{K(A)}{2qN}. $$
Here $K(A)$ is a nonnegative integer and
$$ 0\le c(A)\le D-1. $$

Define the power of two
$$ M=a(2q)PD $$
and give $A$ the weight
$$ w(A)=1-\frac{K(A)}M =1-\beta c(A), \qquad \beta=\frac{N}{aPD}. $$
Since
$$ K(A)\le (2q-1)N(D-1)<2qPD\le M, $$
every weight belongs to $(0,1]$. Moreover,
$$ \frac{\delta}{2D}\le\beta\le\frac{\delta}{D}, \qquad 0\le1-w(A)\le\delta. $$

The randomly selected range consists of all pairs $(w(A),A)$ obtained this way. Its construction depends only on public parameters, buyer labels, and report-independent coins. The mechanism exactly maximizes weighted reported welfare over this range, using the fixed lexicographic tie order and the stipulated VCG payments.

2. Exact optimization and bounded polynomial running time

Each block has at most
$$ D^h=2^{mh}\le\sqrt N $$
candidate allocations. Enumerate these candidates, check their block feasibility, and evaluate their reported welfare.

For block $b$ and candidate $C$, record
$$ W_b(C)=\sum_{i\in b}\widehat v_i(C_i), \qquad K_b(C)=\sum_{i\in b}s(C_i)(2qz_i). $$
The choices in different blocks are independent subject to their block restrictions.

Use a dynamic program indexed by blocks and accumulated integer cost. For each attainable cost $k$, retain the maximum total reported welfare attainable at that cost, together with the lexicographically first allocation attaining it. The cost index satisfies
$$ 0\le k\le K_{\max}:=(2q-1)N(D-1). $$
Because $h>0$ implies $D\le\sqrt N$, and $q\le h^{1/4}\le N^{1/4}$,
$$ K_{\max}\le 2N^{7/4}. $$
Thus the number of states and transitions is polynomial in the permitted parameters.

After processing all blocks, maximize
$$ \left(1-\frac{k}{M}\right)W_k $$
over the retained states. For fixed $k$, the weight is strictly positive, so retaining only the largest welfare at that cost loses no weighted optimum.

Tie handling is exact. Replacing a partial allocation by a lexicographically earlier allocation of the same cost and welfare preserves lexicographic precedence under every identical assignment to the remaining blocks. Consequently, retaining the earliest witness at each state and resolving final ties lexicographically produces the prescribed global tie order.

All arithmetic is polynomial in bit length. A retained welfare is a sum of at most $N$ evaluated rational values. The assumed evaluation and value-length bounds therefore give polynomial bounds on its reduced numerator and denominator lengths. Cost indices and weights also have polynomial bit length. Exact addition, comparison, and multiplication consequently take polynomial time.

For VCG counterfactuals, retain the same range and set one buyer’s welfare contribution to zero. Running the same optimization for all $N$ buyers remains polynomial. Expanding a support representation into labeled buyers also takes polynomial time in $N,\tau,L$.

Finally, implementation is exact and bounded: draw a uniform integer $U$ from $\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$ using exactly $\log_2 M$ fair bits, and implement $A$ precisely when
$$ U<M-K(A). $$
This event has probability exactly $w(A)$. No rejection sampling is used anywhere.

Hence one algorithm implements the family in $\operatorname{poly}(N,m,\tau,L)$ time on every execution and every supported report profile.

3. A strictly convex rule for selecting the limit

Fix a truthful society and supply vector. All subsequent asymptotics keep this input fixed.

Let $\mathcal F$ be the feasible set of the fractional program, and write
$$ W(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S), \qquad V=\max_{x\in\mathcal F}W(x). $$
The set $\mathcal F$ is nonempty because the all-empty allocation is feasible. It is closed and bounded, since
$$ 0\le x_{t,S}\le\mu_t. $$
Thus it is compact. It is also convex because its constraints are linear. Its welfare-optimal face
$$ \mathcal F^*=\{x\in\mathcal F:W(x)=V\} $$
is nonempty, compact, and convex.

For a probability vector $p=(p_0,\ldots,p_{D-1})$, define
$$ Q_\ell(p)=\sum_{j=\ell}^{D-1}p_j, \qquad F(p)=\frac12\sum_{\ell=1}^{D-1}Q_\ell(p)^2. $$

We first establish a cost interpretation of $F$. Consider a unit mass of buyers with ranks uniform on $[0,1]$, assigning bundle index $\ell$ cost $\ell z$ at rank $z$. For a fractional assignment with bundle frequencies $p$, let $f_\ell(z)$ be the fraction at rank $z$ receiving an index at least $\ell$. Then
$$ 0\le f_\ell(z)\le1, \qquad \int_0^1 f_\ell(z)\,dz=Q_\ell(p), $$
and its total cost is
$$ \sum_{\ell=1}^{D-1}\int_0^1 zf_\ell(z)\,dz. $$

For any $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ with integral $Q$,
$$ \int_0^1 zf(z)\,dz\ge\frac{Q^2}{2}. $$
Indeed,
$$ (z-Q)\bigl(f(z)-\mathbf 1_{\{z<Q\}}\bigr)\ge0 $$
pointwise. Integration and the equality of the two functions’ integrals give the claimed inequality.

These lower bounds are simultaneously attained by
$$ f_\ell(z)=\mathbf 1_{\{z<Q_\ell(p)\}}. $$
They are nested because the $Q_\ell(p)$ decrease with $\ell$, and therefore specify a valid assignment with frequencies $p$. Its cost is $F(p)$.

The tail vector $(Q_1(p),\ldots,Q_{D-1}(p))$ determines $p$ uniquely on the probability simplex. The squared Euclidean norm is strictly convex, so $F$ is strictly convex there.

Define
$$ R(x)=\sum_t\mu_t F\left(\left(\frac{x_{t,S}}{\mu_t}\right)_S\right). $$
Every $\mu_t$ is positive. Therefore $R$ is strictly convex on the affine set satisfying the type-mass equalities. Continuity and compactness imply that there is a unique minimizer
$$ x^*=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{x\in\mathcal F^*}R(x). $$
We will prove convergence to $x^*$.

4. Sampling and empirical rank-cost bounds

Buyer-ordering error

Let $E_N$ be the event that all random keys are distinct. A union bound gives
$$ \Pr(E_N^c) \le\frac{N(N-1)}{2^{B+1}} \le\frac{N^2}{2(N+1)^4} =:p_N. $$
Conditional on $E_N$, the sorted ordering is a uniform random permutation: the distinct independent keys have a distribution invariant under every permutation of buyer labels.

Write $n_{t,b}$ for the number of type-$t$ buyers in full block $b$, and define
$$ \Delta_N=\frac1N\sum_{b,t}|n_{t,b}-h\mu_t|. $$
Conditional on $E_N$, each block is a uniform sample without replacement. Thus
$$ \mathbb E[n_{t,b}\mid E_N]=h\mu_t, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(n_{t,b}\mid E_N)\le h\mu_t. $$
Using $\mathbb E|Z-\mathbb EZ|\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(Z)}$ and
$\sum_t\sqrt{\mu_t}\le\sqrt{\tau}$ gives
$$ \mathbb E[\Delta_N\mid E_N]\le\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{h}}. $$
Always $\Delta_N\le2gh/N\le2$, so
$$ \mathbb E[\Delta_N]\le\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{h}}+2p_N. $$

Empirical rank costs

Consider $n>0$ buyers of one type in a block. Let $\nu$ be their empirical rank distribution, and let $u_q$ be the uniform distribution on the rank grid. Denote by $G_\nu(p)$ the minimum fractional rank cost per buyer for bundle frequencies $p$.

Uniformly over $p$,
$$ |G_\nu(p)-F(p)| \le(D-1)\left(\operatorname{TV}(\nu,u_q)+\frac1q\right). $$
To prove this, couple $\nu$ and $u_q$ so their ranks disagree with probability $\operatorname{TV}(\nu,u_q)$. Couple a uniform grid rank to a continuous uniform rank inside its grid interval, with displacement at most $1/q$. Transfer any fractional assignment through these couplings while preserving its bundle frequencies. Changing a rank from $z$ to $z'$ changes any bundle’s cost by at most $(D-1)|z-z'|$. Transferring an optimal assignment in either direction proves the bound.

For $n$ independent grid ranks, let $n_j$ count rank category $j$. Then
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[n\,\operatorname{TV}(\nu,u_q)] &=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^q\mathbb E|n_j-n/q|\\ &\le\frac12\sum_{j=1}^q\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(n_j)}\\ &\le\frac12\sqrt{qn}. \end{aligned} $$

Let $e_N$ be the sum of the preceding empirical error bounds over full-block type groups, normalized by $N$; groups with no buyers contribute zero. Conditional on the partition, all ranks remain independent. Cauchy–Schwarz yields
$$ \mathbb E[e_N] \le(D-1)\left(\frac1q+\frac12\sqrt{\frac{q\tau}{h}}\right). $$

For an allowed allocation $A$, let $Y(A)$ be its normalized type-bundle counts before cancellation. Its actual rank cost in each group is at least the fractional minimum for that group’s frequencies. Applying the uniform bound and then convexity of $F$ within each type gives
$$ c(A)\ge R(Y(A))-e_N. $$
For this convexity step, include leftover buyers as an additional group receiving only $S_0$, with cost $F(e_0)=0$. The group weights for each type then sum exactly to $\mu_t$.

5. A feasible comparison allocation

Write
$$ p_t^*=\left(\frac{x^*_{t,S}}{\mu_t}\right)_S. $$
In block $b$, initially assign each nonempty bundle $S$ fractionally in amount
$$ \min(n_{t,b},h\mu_t)\,p_{t,S}^*, $$
and give the remaining type-$t$ mass the empty bundle.

For every nonempty bundle, this amount is at most $hx^*_{t,S}$. Hence every good’s fractional block load is at most $hb_j$.

Among assignments with these frequencies, minimize fractional rank cost. Express the assignment as counts indexed by type, rank category, and bundle. Round every nonempty-bundle count down to an integer, assigning all remaining buyers in each type-rank category the empty bundle.

Call the resulting allocation $B_N$. Rounding decreases both good loads and rank cost. Each good’s resulting load is an integer at most $hb_j$, hence at most $\lfloor hb_j\rfloor$. Therefore $B_N$ belongs to the selected range.

Let
$$ V_{\max}=\max_t v_t([m]), \qquad \eta_N=V-W(Y(B_N)). $$
Global feasibility implies $\eta_N\ge0$. Monotonicity bounds every bundle value by $V_{\max}$. Type shortages, rounding fewer than one buyer per nonempty type-rank-bundle count, and leftovers give
$$ 0\le\eta_N \le V_{\max}\left( \Delta_N+\frac{q\tau(D-1)}h+\frac hN \right). $$

For fixed $m$, as $N\to\infty$,
$$ h\to\infty,\qquad q\to\infty,\qquad h^{-1/8}\le\delta<2h^{-1/8}. $$
Since $q\le h^{1/4}$ and $p_N=O(N^{-2})$, the preceding estimates imply
$$ \mathbb E[\eta_N/\delta]\to0. $$
More explicitly, the terms are bounded by fixed constants times
$$ h^{-3/8},\qquad h^{1/8}p_N,\qquad h^{-5/8},\qquad \frac{h^{9/8}}N, $$
all of which vanish.

We also need a cost bound. From the definition of $F$,
$$ 0\le F(p)\le\frac{D-1}{2}, \qquad |F(p)-F(p')|\le(D-1)\|p-p'\|_1. $$
The second inequality follows by bounding each tail difference by $\|p-p'\|_1$ and using
$$ \frac12|u^2-v^2|\le|u-v| \quad\text{for }u,v\in[0,1]. $$

Put $a_t=n_{t,b}/h$. If $a_t>0$, the initial comparison frequencies are
$$ p=\min(1,\mu_t/a_t)p_t^* +\bigl(1-\min(1,\mu_t/a_t)\bigr)e_0. $$
They satisfy
$$ |a_tF(p)-\mu_tF(p_t^*)| \le3(D-1)|a_t-\mu_t|. $$
For $a_t\le\mu_t$, use $p=p_t^*$ and the bound on $F$. For $a_t>\mu_t$, use
$$ \|p-p_t^*\|_1\le2(1-\mu_t/a_t) $$
and the Lipschitz bound; the resulting constant is at most $5(D-1)/2$. The case $a_t=0$ follows directly from the bound on $F$.

Applying this estimate and the empirical rank bound before rounding, and observing that rounding decreases cost, gives
$$ c(B_N)\le R(x^*)+3(D-1)\Delta_N+e_N. $$
Here the full blocks contribute $(gh/N)R(x^*)\le R(x^*)$, explaining why no additional leftover term is needed.

6. Convergence to the unique welfare optimum

Let $A_N$ be the selected allocation before cancellation, and abbreviate
$$ Y_N=Y(A_N),\qquad C_N=c(A_N),\qquad C_N^B=c(B_N). $$
Every $Y_N$ belongs to $\mathcal F$.

Suppose first that $V>0$. Exact weighted optimality gives
$$ W(Y_N)(1-\beta C_N) \ge(V-\eta_N)(1-\beta C_N^B). $$
All factors $1-\beta c(A)$ are positive and at most $1$. Consequently,
$$ W(Y_N)\ge V-\eta_N-\beta V(D-1). $$
Since $W(Y_N)\le V$, $\mathbb E[\eta_N]\to0$, and $\beta\to0$,
$$ \mathbb E[V-W(Y_N)]\to0. $$

Using $W(Y_N)\le V$ in the same optimality inequality gives
$$ V(1-\beta C_N) \ge(V-\eta_N)(1-\beta C_N^B). $$
Expanding and dividing by $\beta V$ yields
$$ C_N \le C_N^B+\frac{\eta_N}{\beta V} -\frac{\eta_N C_N^B}{V} \le C_N^B+\frac{\eta_N}{\beta V}. $$
Because $\beta\ge\delta/(2D)$,
$$ \mathbb E[\eta_N/\beta]\to0. $$
Combining the lower cost bound with the comparison bound therefore gives
$$ R(Y_N) \le R(x^*)+3(D-1)\Delta_N+2e_N +\frac{\eta_N}{\beta V}. $$
The nonnegative error on the right tends to zero in expectation.

We now justify convergence of the allocation itself. For $\varepsilon>0$, consider the compact set
$$ \mathcal K_\varepsilon =\{x\in\mathcal F:\|x-x^*\|_1\ge\varepsilon\}. $$
If this set is nonempty, the continuous nonnegative function
$$ G(x)=V-W(x)+\max\{R(x)-R(x^*),0\} $$
has a strictly positive minimum there. Indeed, $G(x)=0$ would imply $x\in\mathcal F^*$ and $R(x)\le R(x^*)$, which by uniqueness forces $x=x^*$.

The estimates above give $\mathbb E[G(Y_N)]\to0$. Markov’s inequality then proves
$$ Y_N\to x^* \quad\text{in probability}. $$
Every coordinate is bounded by its type mass. Thus convergence in probability implies coordinatewise convergence of expectations:
$$ \mathbb E[Y_N]\to x^*. $$

If $V=0$, every feasible truthful allocation has zero welfare. The fixed lexicographic tie order selects the all-empty allocation. This is also the unique minimizer of $R$ on $\mathcal F$, because $R\ge0$, with equality only when every type receives the empty bundle. Hence the same conclusion holds.

Finally, conditional on the selected range and allocation, actual implementation satisfies
$$ \mathbb E[X^{(N)}_{t,S}\mid A_N] =w(A_N)Y_{N,t,S} \quad(S\ne\varnothing), $$
and
$$ \mathbb E[X^{(N)}_{t,\varnothing}\mid A_N] =w(A_N)Y_{N,t,\varnothing} +(1-w(A_N))\mu_t. $$
Each conditional coordinate differs from its pre-cancellation coordinate by at most $\delta\mu_t$. Since $\delta\to0$,
$$ \mathbb E[X^{(N)}]\to x^* $$
coordinatewise.

Every bound applies along all admissible replication sizes. The limiting allocation is feasible and satisfies
$$ \sum_{t,S}x^*_{t,S}v_t(S)=\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,b). $$

7. Consequence for the complexity barrier

The construction achieves ratio $1$, and therefore every requested fixed ratio $\rho\in(0,1]$. It requires no complexity assumption; the APX-hardness premise does not obstruct these asymptotic guarantees.

There is no conflict with the supplied [fixed-buyer randomized MIWR hardness theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.1948). That theorem concerns a finite-instance approximation guarantee for discrete auctions. Our family can perform poorly at finite replication sizes: it returns the empty allocation whenever $\log_2 N<2m$. For each fixed society, increasing $N$ eventually permits exhaustive optimization inside growing blocks while retaining polynomial time in $N$. Increasing $N$ also increases item supplies.

Thus no ratio in the specified interval faces the stated complexity barrier under this precise convergence-only formulation.
