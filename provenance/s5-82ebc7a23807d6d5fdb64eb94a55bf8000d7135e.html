The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given colleges \(P\), a finite set of applicant types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\), a fixed state \(s_i\), fixed rival strategies, type utilities \(u_{i\theta}=v_\theta+e_{i\theta}\), acceptance probabilities \(\pi_{i\theta}=\pi_i(s_i,v_\theta)\), per-capita capacity \(\kappa_i\), and penalty \(\gamma_i>\max_\theta u_{i\theta}\), compute offer fractions \(x_{i\theta}\in[0,1]\) maximizing \(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta x_{i\theta}u_{i\theta}\pi_{i\theta}-\gamma_i\max\{\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta x_{i\theta}\pi_{i\theta}-\kappa_i,0\}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P=\{P_1,\ldots,P_m\}\) be colleges and fix \(i\). A society is given by its support: a list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has binary rational attributes \(v_\theta,e_{i\theta}\in[0,1]\), with utility \(u_{i\theta}=v_\theta+e_{i\theta}\). The state \(s_i\in[0,1]\) and rival strategies are fixed; their conditional acceptance probabilities \(\pi_{i\theta}=\pi_i(s_i,v_\theta)\in[0,1]\) are supplied as binary rationals, equal for equal scores.
The binary rational capacity satisfies \(0<\kappa_i\leq1\), and the binary rational penalty satisfies \(\gamma_i>\max_\theta u_{i\theta}\). Each type's mass is divisible: an offer fraction \(x_{i\theta}\in[0,1]\) selects that fraction of its mass. Utility ties impose no restriction on these fractions.
Define \(F_i(x)=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta x_{i\theta}u_{i\theta}\pi_{i\theta}-\gamma_i\max\{\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta x_{i\theta}\pi_{i\theta}-\kappa_i,0\}\), and \(\mathrm{OPT}_i=\max_{x\in[0,1]^\tau}F_i(x)\). The penalty applies to expected enrollment mass. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Can the continuum guarantee of zero additive loss, \(\mathrm{OPT}_i-F_i(x)=0\), be achieved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is achieving this guarantee NP-hard?

Dai and Jordan establish an additive near-optimality guarantee for a utility cutoff strategy with indivisible applicants; they also state that its additive loss vanishes for a continuum of applicants when acceptance probabilities are continuous in score. Their statement does not specify computational complexity for a society encoded by rational support masses. [Source](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume22/20-1221/20-1221.pdf)

Result

Zero additive loss is achievable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). An exact optimizer is obtained by sorting types by utility and filling expected enrollment capacity, allowing a fractional selection of one boundary type.

The argument below applies directly to the stated rational-support model; it requires no continuity assumption on acceptance probabilities.

1. No optimizer exceeds capacity

Fix \(i\), and abbreviate
\(u_\theta=u_{i\theta}\), \(\pi_\theta=\pi_{i\theta}\), \(\kappa=\kappa_i\), and \(\gamma=\gamma_i\).
Define
\(a_\theta=\mu_\theta\pi_\theta\),
\(E(x)=\sum_\theta a_\theta x_\theta\), and
\(R(x)=\sum_\theta a_\theta u_\theta x_\theta\).
Thus
\(F_i(x)=R(x)-\gamma\max\{E(x)-\kappa,0\}\).

Suppose \(E(x)>\kappa\). Set
\(x'_\theta=(\kappa/E(x))x_\theta\).
Because \(0<\kappa/E(x)<1\), this is a permitted offer vector, and \(E(x')=\kappa\). Consequently,

\( F_i(x')-F_i(x) =(E(x)-\kappa)\left(\gamma-\frac{R(x)}{E(x)}\right). \)

The ratio \(R(x)/E(x)\) is a weighted average of the utilities: its weights are \(a_\theta x_\theta/E(x)\), which are nonnegative and sum to \(1\). Hence
\(R(x)/E(x)\leq\max_\theta u_\theta<\gamma\).
Both factors in the displayed difference are strictly positive.

Therefore every vector exceeding capacity is strictly worse than a permitted vector at capacity. Since \(F_i\) is continuous on the compact box \([0,1]^\tau\), its maximum exists, and

\( \mathrm{OPT}_i =\max\left\{\sum_\theta a_\theta u_\theta x_\theta: 0\leq x_\theta\leq1,\ \sum_\theta a_\theta x_\theta\leq\kappa\right\}. \)

2. Exact algorithm

Types with \(a_\theta=0\) contribute neither enrollment nor reward; set their offer fractions to \(0\). Index the remaining types by \(1,\ldots,n\), where \(n\leq\tau\), and sort them so that
\(u_1\geq u_2\geq\cdots\geq u_n\).
Break utility ties arbitrarily, as permitted by the hypotheses.

Introduce the expected enrollment variables \(y_j=a_jx_j\). Because \(a_j>0\), this gives a bijection between \(x_j\in[0,1]\) and \(y_j\in[0,a_j]\). The problem becomes

\( \max\left\{\sum_{j=1}^n u_jy_j: 0\leq y_j\leq a_j,\ \sum_{j=1}^n y_j\leq\kappa\right\}. \)

Let \(A=\sum_{j=1}^n a_j\).

If \(A\leq\kappa\), choose \(y_j^*=a_j\) for every \(j\). This is feasible and optimal because all utilities are nonnegative, so every feasible \(y\) satisfies
\(\sum_j u_jy_j\leq\sum_j u_ja_j\).
This also covers \(n=0\).

If \(A>\kappa\), let \(r\) be the smallest index for which
\(\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\geq\kappa\).
Choose:

  • \(y_j^*=a_j\) for \(j<r\);
  • \(y_r^*=\kappa-\sum_{j<r}a_j\);
  • \(y_j^*=0\) for \(j>r\).

Minimality of \(r\) gives \(0<y_r^*\leq a_r\), and the selected enrollment sums to \(\kappa\). Return \(x_j^*=y_j^*/a_j\). Thus at most one type has an offer fraction strictly between \(0\) and \(1\).

3. Proof of optimality

Consider the case \(A>\kappa\), and let \(y\) be any feasible enrollment vector. Put \(\lambda=u_r\), which is nonnegative. Since \(\sum_jy_j^*=\kappa\),

\( \sum_j u_j(y_j^*-y_j) =\sum_j(u_j-\lambda)(y_j^*-y_j) +\lambda\left(\kappa-\sum_jy_j\right). \)

Every term on the right is nonnegative:

  • For \(j<r\), sorting gives \(u_j-\lambda\geq0\), and \(y_j^*-y_j=a_j-y_j\geq0\).
  • For \(j=r\), the coefficient \(u_r-\lambda\) is \(0\).
  • For \(j>r\), both \(u_j-\lambda\leq0\) and \(y_j^*-y_j=-y_j\leq0\).
  • The final term is nonnegative because \(\lambda\geq0\) and \(y\) respects capacity.

Therefore \(\sum_j u_jy_j^*\geq\sum_j u_jy_j\) for every feasible \(y\). Together with Section 1, this proves

\( F_i(x^*)=\mathrm{OPT}_i, \qquad \mathrm{OPT}_i-F_i(x^*)=0. \)

4. Binary running time

Exact arithmetic suffices, with polynomially bounded intermediate and output lengths.

Each utility \(u_j=v_j+e_{ij}\) has \(O(L)\) binary length. Sorting requires \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational comparisons, each implementable by cross-multiplying integers of \(O(L)\) bits.

For enrollment arithmetic, write \(a_j=p_j/q_j\), using the product of the input denominators of \(\mu_j\) and \(\pi_j\) as \(q_j\); reduction is unnecessary. Write \(\kappa=p_\kappa/d_\kappa\), and set
\(D=d_\kappa\prod_{j=1}^n q_j\).
The sum of the binary lengths of these input denominators is bounded by \(L\), so \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits.

The numbers \(b_j=Da_j\) and \(K=D\kappa\) are integers. Moreover,
\(\sum_j a_j\leq\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\),
so all enrollment prefix sums after scaling are at most \(D\). They therefore have \(O(L)\) bits. Locate the boundary by comparing these integer prefix sums with \(K\), and compute

\( x_r^*=\frac{K-\sum_{j<r}b_j}{b_r}. \)

Both numerator and denominator have \(O(L)\) bits. All other returned fractions are \(0\) or \(1\).

Constructing these integers and scanning the sorted list uses \(O(\tau)\) arithmetic operations on integers of \(O(L)\) bits. Standard integer arithmetic therefore gives polynomial bit complexity and a polynomial-size exact output. Since the college and rival strategies are fixed and the probabilities are supplied, no additional optimization over the \(m\) colleges is required.

5. Relation to the continuum statement

Dai and Jordan’s Theorem 3 states an additive cutoff guarantee and its vanishing loss under their continuum and continuity assumptions. [Source, Theorem 3, p. 13](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume22/20-1221/20-1221.pdf#page=13)

For the present encoding, the direct proof above establishes computational tractability. Divisibility permits selecting precisely the required portion of a boundary type even when the support is finite. Supplied rational acceptance probabilities need not be continuous in score for this algorithm to work.
