The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given vehicle types \(r=1,\ldots,\tau\) with fleet masses \(\mu_r\), \(k\) charging stations, a fixed station-mass profile \(q_1,\ldots,q_k\) with \(\sum_j q_j=1\), and rational workload coefficients \(h_{rj}\), choose allocations \(x_{rj}\geq 0\) satisfying \(\sum_j x_{rj}=\mu_r\) and \(\sum_r x_{rj}=q_j\) to minimize the maximum station workload \(\max_j \sum_r h_{rj}x_{rj}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) vehicle types with positive masses \(\mu_r\) encoded as binary rationals, with \(\sum_r\mu_r=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=k\geq1\) labeled charging stations. The input includes binary rational station masses \(q_j\geq0\), with \(\sum_jq_j=1\), and finite nonnegative rational workload coefficients \(h_{rj}\), already incorporating charging time and station capacity. Empty stations are allowed.
Mass may be split arbitrarily: choose real allocations \(x_{rj}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_jx_{rj}=\mu_r\) and \(\sum_rx_{rj}=q_j\). The objective is to minimize \(\max_j\sum_rh_{rj}x_{rj}\); there is no secondary objective. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is exact minimization of the maximum station workload solvable in time polynomial in \(m=k\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For discrete agents, PISA has a stated \(O(n^4k^2)\) search bound for one prescribed ordered coalition-size profile; it samples that profile and does not guarantee its optimum. [Source](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.3233/FAIA230527)
No result settling this exact continuous problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required nonnegative finite workload coefficients because they represent charging times divided by positive station capacities; retained the graded question's arbitrary coefficient matrix.
  • Allowed zero station masses, consistent with the discrete definition allowing empty coalitions.
  • Made station masses binary rationals and defined L as total binary input length.
  • Identified m with k, the number of stations, and allowed any positive support size and station count.
  • Retained the graded question's maximum-workload objective alone; the application also mentions total workload but supplies no ordering or scalarization of its two objectives.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Proven claim. Exact minimization is solvable in time polynomial in \(m=k\), \(\tau\), and \(L\). Both the optimal value and an optimal allocation can be returned as binary rationals.

1. Feasibility and attainment. Let
\[ \mathcal X=\left\{x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{\tau\times m}: \sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{rj}=\mu_r\ \text{for every }r,\quad \sum_{r=1}^{\tau}x_{rj}=q_j\ \text{for every }j \right\}. \]
This set is nonempty: \(x_{rj}=\mu_rq_j\) satisfies both families of equalities because both mass vectors sum to \(1\).

Every feasible coordinate satisfies \(0\leq x_{rj}\leq\mu_r\leq1\). Thus \(\mathcal X\) is closed and bounded, hence compact. The function
\[ W(x)=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}h_{rj}x_{rj} \]
is continuous, being the maximum of finitely many linear functions. Consequently its minimum is attained.

Set \(H=\max_{r,j}h_{rj}\). For every feasible allocation and every station,
\[ 0\leq\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}h_{rj}x_{rj} \leq H\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}x_{rj} =Hq_j\leq H. \]
In particular, \(0\leq W(x)\leq H\), including when \(H=0\).

2. Exact linear-programming formulation. Introduce one real variable \(t\) and consider
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad &t\\ \text{subject to}\quad &\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{rj}=\mu_r &&(1\leq r\leq\tau),\\ &\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}x_{rj}=q_j &&(1\leq j\leq m),\\ &\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}h_{rj}x_{rj}\leq t &&(1\leq j\leq m),\\ &x_{rj}\geq0 &&(1\leq r\leq\tau,\ 1\leq j\leq m),\\ &0\leq t\leq H. \end{aligned} \]

For every \(x\in\mathcal X\), the pair \((x,W(x))\) is feasible for this program. Conversely, every feasible pair satisfies \(x\in\mathcal X\) and \(W(x)\leq t\). These two implications prove that the program’s optimal value equals \(\min_{x\in\mathcal X}W(x)\).

Moreover, if \((x^*,t^*)\) is an optimal solution, then
\[ \min_{x\in\mathcal X}W(x)\leq W(x^*)\leq t^* =\min_{x\in\mathcal X}W(x). \]
Hence \(x^*\) solves the original problem exactly.

Empty stations cause no difficulty: if \(q_j=0\), nonnegativity and the column-sum equality force every \(x_{rj}=0\), so that station’s workload is zero.

3. Polynomial complexity and exactness. We use the rational linear-programming theorem: a linear program with binary rational data can be solved in polynomial time in its total encoding length; when feasible with a finite optimum, an exact rational optimal solution can be produced. This is the standard bit-complexity result of [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239).

All its hypotheses hold here:

  • There are \(\tau m+1\) variables and \(O(\tau m+\tau+m)\) constraints, counting nonnegativity and replacing equalities by pairs of inequalities.
  • Every coefficient belongs to the input or is one of \(0,1,-1\); \(H\) is an input coefficient selected by rational comparisons.
  • Consequently, the total LP encoding length \(S\) is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), even with a dense encoding.
  • The feasible region is nonempty and compact by the preceding bounds, so the optimum is finite and attained.

Exact output also has polynomial length. To see this directly, choose an optimal vertex of the feasible polytope and put \(N=\tau m+1\). Its coordinates solve a nonsingular system of \(N\) tight constraints. Clearing denominators in each selected equation produces integer entries of magnitude at most \(2^{O(S)}\). Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s determinant bound then give numerator and denominator bit lengths \(O(N(S+\log N))\) for every coordinate. This establishes polynomial output size; the cited theorem supplies the polynomial-time algorithm to find it.

Thus exact continuous optimization is polynomial-time solvable for every prescribed station-mass vector. The discrete PISA search bound is unnecessary for this conclusion. Under standard polynomial-time reductions, NP-hardness of the associated threshold decision problem would therefore imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
