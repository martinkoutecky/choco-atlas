The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \([\tau]\), rational population masses \(\mu\), and a coalition-value oracle \(V(x)\) for \(x\in[0,\mu]\), define \(F(z)=V(\mu\odot z)\) and the total Shapley allocation to type \(t\) by \(\Phi_t=\int_0^1 \partial_t F(s\mathbf{1})\,ds\). Assuming a stated smoothness bound, determine the worst-case number of coalition-value queries needed to estimate every \(\Phi_t\) within additive error \(\varepsilon\), and whether the paper's sampled \(k\)-additive least-squares approach can attain that bound.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq2\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; there is no separate \(m\) parameter.
An exact value oracle returns the signed real number \(V(x)\) at any rational \(x\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\); derivatives are unavailable. Set \(F(z)=V(\mu\odot z)\), where \(\odot\) denotes componentwise multiplication, and normalize \(V(0)=0\).
Assume \(F\) extends to a twice continuously differentiable function on a neighborhood of \([0,1]^\tau\), with \(\sup_z\max_{i,j}|\partial_i\partial_jF(z)|\leq B\), for a supplied positive rational \(B\). Define the total allocation \(\Phi_t=\int_0^1\partial_tF(s\mathbf1)\,ds\).
For positive rational \(\varepsilon\), let \(N^*(\tau,B,\varepsilon)\) be the least worst-case query budget of an adaptive randomized algorithm satisfying \(\Pr(\max_t|\widehat\Phi_t-\Phi_t|\leq\varepsilon)\geq2/3\) for every admissible oracle. Exact real arithmetic has unit cost; state arithmetic running times and rational query-coordinate lengths in \(\tau,L,B,\varepsilon\) and any surrogate degree \(k\), where \(L\) is the binary input length.
The sampled surrogate procedure uses \(1\leq k\leq\min\{3,\tau\}\) and \(Q(z)=\sum_{|S|\leq k}c_S\prod_{t\in S}z_t\), the multilinear extension of a game with no interactions above order \(k\).
It queries both endpoints and samples distinct nonempty proper subsets \(A\subset[\tau]\), successively with probability proportional to \(w_A=\binom{\tau-2}{|A|-1}^{-1}\) among remaining subsets, querying \(F(\mathbf1_A)\).
It minimizes \(\sum_{A\text{ sampled}}w_A(F(\mathbf1_A)-Q(\mathbf1_A))^2\) subject to \(Q(\mathbf1)-Q(0)=F(\mathbf1)-F(0)\), chooses the minimizer with smallest Euclidean coefficient norm, and returns \(\widehat\Phi_t=\int_0^1\partial_tQ(s\mathbf1)\,ds\).

What is the asymptotically optimal query complexity \(N^*(\tau,B,\varepsilon)\), and can the specified sampled \(k\)-additive least-squares procedure attain it?

Discrete: using every coalition, efficiency-constrained weighted least squares with these weights recovers exact Shapley allocations for \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\); this gives no sampled-query guarantee. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04763v2).
Continuous: the allocation is the Aumann–Shapley or integrated-gradients attribution, for which gradient-based numerical evaluation is established. [Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/sundararajan17a.html). No result settling the specified value-only minimax problem was located.

1. Proven results and unresolved question

The sharp joint asymotics of $N^*(\tau,B,\varepsilon)$ remain unresolved. The following bounds and the obstruction to the sampled surrogate are proved below.

Write
$$ r=\frac{\tau B}{\varepsilon}, \qquad \lambda=\log\!\bigl(2+\tau(1+r)\bigr), $$
where $\log$ denotes the natural logarithm. There are universal constants $c,C>0$ such that
$$ c\max\left\{ \tau,\; r^{2/3},\; \tau r^{2/3} \left(\frac{\log(2\tau)}{\lambda}\right)^{1/3} \right\} \leq N^*(\tau,B,\varepsilon) $$
and
$$ N^*(\tau,B,\varepsilon) \leq C\tau\left( 1+\min\left\{ r,\; r^{2/3}\bigl(\log(2\tau)\bigr)^{1/3} \right\} \right). $$

The joint lower bound follows from the stronger implicit inequality
$$ \bigl(N^*(\tau,B,\varepsilon)\bigr)^3 \log\!\bigl(2+N^*(\tau,B,\varepsilon)\bigr) \geq c\tau^3r^2\log(2\tau). $$
This improves the joint lower bound in the notes by a factor of $\log(2\tau)$ in this implicit inequality.

For every fixed $\tau$, these bounds give the complete conclusion
$$ N^*(\tau,B,\varepsilon) = \Theta_\tau\left(1+(B/\varepsilon)^{2/3}\right). $$

The specified sampled least-squares procedure cannot attain the minimax complexity in general: for every permitted $k$ and every sample budget, it fails with probability one on an admissible oracle whenever
$$ \varepsilon<\frac{B(\tau-1)}{18}. $$
This remains true when every coalition is queried. More generally, complete coalition data leave an unavoidable allocation uncertainty of order $\tau B$.

2. Reduction and value-only upper bounds

2.1. Reduction to the cube

Since every $\mu_t$ is positive and rational, a rational cube query $z$ can be implemented by querying $V$ at
$$ x=\mu\odot z. $$
Conversely, a rational query $x$ to $V$ corresponds to the rational cube point $z_t=x_t/\mu_t$. Thus the masses affect query-coordinate lengths, but not query complexity.

For lower bounds, any admissible cube function gives a society oracle by setting
$$ V(x)=F(x_1/\mu_1,\ldots,x_\tau/\mu_\tau). $$

Define
$$ g_i(s)=\partial_iF(s\mathbf 1), \qquad D=\tau B. $$
The chain rule and the Hessian bound imply
$$ |g_i'(s)| = \left|\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\partial_i\partial_jF(s\mathbf 1)\right| \leq D. $$
Consequently, each $g_i$ is $D$-Lipschitz. Its magnitude need not be bounded, because arbitrary affine functions are admissible.

The fundamental theorem of calculus also gives
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\Phi_i=F(\mathbf 1)-F(0). $$

2.2. Derivative approximation from values

Choose $h$ to be the largest dyadic power satisfying
$$ h\leq \min\left\{\frac12,\frac{\varepsilon}{2B}\right\}. $$
In particular, $h$ is within a factor of two of this threshold.

For $s\in[0,1]$, put
$$ \beta(s)= \begin{cases} 1,&s\leq 1/2,\\ -1,&s>1/2, \end{cases} $$
and define
$$ d_i(s)= \frac{F(s\mathbf 1+\beta(s)he_i)-F(s\mathbf 1)} {\beta(s)h}. $$
Both query points lie in the cube. Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus along coordinate $i$ gives
$$ |d_i(s)-g_i(s)| \leq \frac1h\int_0^h Bu\,du = \frac{Bh}{2} \leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}. $$
Thus $\tau+1$ value queries suffice to approximate the entire gradient at one diagonal point.

2.3. Deterministic quadrature

Let $n$ be the smallest power of two satisfying
$$ n\geq\max\{1,r\}. $$
Query at the midpoints
$$ s_j=\frac{j+1/2}{n}, \qquad j=0,\ldots,n-1, $$
and return
$$ \widehat\Phi_i=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}d_i(s_j). $$

For a $D$-Lipschitz function, midpoint quadrature has error at most $D/(4n)$. Indeed, on a cell of length $1/n$ with midpoint $s_j$,
$$ \int_{\text{cell}}|g_i(s)-g_i(s_j)|\,ds \leq D\int_{\text{cell}}|s-s_j|\,ds = \frac{D}{4n^2}. $$
Summing over the cells proves
$$ \left|\frac1n\sum_jg_i(s_j)-\Phi_i\right| \leq\frac{D}{4n}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}. $$
Together with the finite-difference error, this yields error at most $\varepsilon/2$ in every coordinate.

The query count is
$$ (\tau+1)n=O\bigl(\tau(1+r)\bigr). $$

2.4. Randomized stratified quadrature

Let
$$ \ell=\left\lceil\log_2(6\tau)\right\rceil. $$
Then $\ell\geq\log(6\tau)$ and $\ell=O(\log(2\tau))$.

Choose $n$ to be the smallest power of two satisfying
$$ n\geq1, \qquad n^3\geq 2r^2\ell. $$
Choose $M$ to be the smallest power of two satisfying
$$ M\geq\max\{1,r/n\}. $$
These near-threshold choices are part of the algorithm.

Independently for each stratum, draw $J_j$ uniformly from $\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$ and set
$$ S_j=\frac{j+(J_j+1/2)/M}{n}. $$
All sampling points are rational. Return
$$ \widehat\Phi_i=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}d_i(S_j). $$

First consider
$$ Z_i=\frac1n\sum_jg_i(S_j). $$
Its expectation is composite midpoint quadrature on $nM$ cells, so
$$ |\mathbb EZ_i-\Phi_i| \leq\frac{D}{4nM} \leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}. $$

Within one stratum, $g_i$ has range length at most $D/n$. Therefore each independent summand $g_i(S_j)/n$ has range length at most $D/n^2$. Hoeffding’s inequality gives
$$ \Pr\left(|Z_i-\mathbb EZ_i|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right) \leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{n^3\varepsilon^2}{2D^2}\right) \leq\frac1{3\tau}. $$
A union bound over the coordinates shows that, with probability at least $2/3$, every coordinate has total error at most
$$ \frac{\varepsilon}{4} +\frac{\varepsilon}{2} +\frac{\varepsilon}{4} =\varepsilon. $$

The query count is
$$ O\left( \tau\left[1+r^{2/3}\bigl(\log(2\tau)\bigr)^{1/3}\right] \right). $$
Selecting the cheaper of the deterministic and randomized constructions proves the stated upper bound.

2.5. Arithmetic time and coordinate lengths

Let $q=(\tau+1)n$ be the query budget of the selected construction, and set
$$ H_{\mathrm{coord}} = L+\log(2+\tau)+\log(2+B/\varepsilon). $$

The specified dyadic choices imply that every query coordinate, after multiplication by its input mass, has binary length
$$ O(H_{\mathrm{coord}}). $$
To see this, the exponents determining $h,n,M$ are all
$$ O\bigl(\log(2+\tau)+\log(2+B/\varepsilon)\bigr), $$
and multiplying by $\mu_i$ adds at most $O(L)$ bits.

Parameter selection by successive doubling takes $O(H_{\mathrm{coord}})$ arithmetic operations. Including explicit construction of all $\tau$ coordinates of every query, deterministic arithmetic time is
$$ O(\tau q+H_{\mathrm{coord}}). $$
The randomized construction uses exactly $\log_2M$ random bits per stratum. Its arithmetic and random-bit operation count is
$$ O(\tau q+nH_{\mathrm{coord}}+H_{\mathrm{coord}}). $$
These counts use the stipulated unit-cost exact real arithmetic model.

3. A smooth family for lower bounds

Define
$$ P(u)= \begin{cases} u^3(1-u)^3,&0\leq u\leq1,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} $$
and
$$ R(v)= \begin{cases} v(1-v^2)^3,&|v|\leq1,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
The functions and their derivatives through order two vanish at their support boundaries. Hence both are globally twice continuously differentiable. Also,
$$ R(0)=0,\qquad R'(0)=1, \qquad I:=\int_0^1P(u)\,du =\frac{3!\,3!}{7!} =\frac1{140}. $$

Let
$$ K= \|P''\|_\infty\|R\|_\infty + 2\|P'\|_\infty\|R'\|_\infty + \|P\|_\infty\|R''\|_\infty. $$
This is a fixed positive finite constant.

For an integer $n\geq1$, choose vectors $a_j\in[-1,1]^\tau$, for $j=0,\ldots,n-1$, satisfying
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}a_{ji}=0. $$
Put
$$ s(z)=\frac1\tau\sum_i z_i, \qquad q_j(z)=\frac1\tau\sum_i a_{ji}z_i, $$
and define
$$ F_a(z)= \frac{B\tau^2}{Kn^2} \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} P(ns(z)-j)R(nq_j(z)). $$

At any point, at most one summand has a nonzero derivative of order at most two. At boundaries between the supports, all such derivatives vanish. For the contributing summand, the product rule gives
$$ \partial_i\partial_\ell F_a = \frac BK \left[ P''R + (a_{ji}+a_{j\ell})P'R' + a_{ji}a_{j\ell}PR'' \right], $$
where the arguments are $ns(z)-j$ and $nq_j(z)$. The coefficient bounds and the definition of $K$ imply
$$ |\partial_i\partial_\ell F_a|\leq B $$
globally. Also $F_a(0)=0$. Thus this is an admissible family.

On the diagonal, the zero-sum condition gives $q_j(s\mathbf1)=0$. Differentiating before restricting to the diagonal yields
$$ \partial_iF_a(s\mathbf1) = \frac{B\tau}{Kn}\sum_j a_{ji}P(ns-j). $$
Integration therefore gives
$$ \Phi_i=\delta_n\sum_j a_{ji}, \qquad \delta_n=\frac{BI\tau}{Kn^2}. $$

Crucially, every value query involves at most one block $a_j$, including queries away from the diagonal.

4. Affine and scalar lower bounds

4.1. At least $\tau$ queries are necessary

Restrict to affine functions
$$ F_a(z)=a\cdot z, \qquad a\in\mathbb R^\tau. $$
They are admissible, and their allocations equal $a$.

Suppose an algorithm uses $N<\tau$ queries. Give $a$ a centered Gaussian distribution with covariance $R_0^2I_\tau$, where
$$ R_0=4\varepsilon\sqrt{\tau}. $$
Condition on the algorithm’s random seed.

Each query is a linear measurement of $a$. Although its direction may depend on previous responses, that direction is fixed after conditioning on the preceding transcript. Sequential Gaussian conditioning shows that the posterior covariance is $R_0^2$ times the orthogonal projection onto the common nullspace of the measured directions: each new measurement removes variance only in its newly measured direction.

This nullspace has positive dimension. Choose a unit vector $v$ in it, measurably from the transcript. Conditional on the transcript, $v\cdot a$ has standard deviation $R_0$. Maximum-norm success implies
$$ |v\cdot(\widehat a-a)| \leq\varepsilon\|v\|_1 \leq\varepsilon\sqrt{\tau}. $$
A Gaussian with standard deviation $R_0$ has density at most $1/(R_0\sqrt{2\pi})$. Hence conditional success probability is at most
$$ \frac{2\varepsilon\sqrt{\tau}}{R_0\sqrt{2\pi}} = \frac14\sqrt{\frac2\pi} <\frac23. $$
Averaging contradicts the required guarantee on every affine input. Thus
$$ N^*(\tau,B,\varepsilon)\geq\tau. $$

4.2. A scalar lower bound with dimension amplification

Let $p=\lfloor\tau/2\rfloor$, and choose $a\in[-1,1]^\tau$ with its first $p$ entries equal to $1$ and all remaining entries equal to
$$ -\frac{p}{\tau-p}. $$
Then $\sum_i a_i=0$ and $a_1=1$.

In the family of Section 3, set
$$ a_j=\sigma_j a, $$
where the $\sigma_j$ are independent uniform signs. Grant the algorithm the entire sign associated with each queried block. This strengthens its information.

After $N$ adaptive queries, at least $m\geq n-N$ signs remain independent and uniform conditional on the transcript. The first allocation is
$$ \Phi_1=\delta_n\sum_j\sigma_j. $$

The largest atom of a sum of $m$ uniform signs is at most $1/\sqrt{m+1}$. For completeness, write
$$ p_r=\frac{\binom{2r}{r}}{2^{2r}}. $$
The recurrence
$$ p_{r+1}=p_r\frac{2r+1}{2r+2} $$
and
$$ (2r+1)(2r+3)\leq(2r+2)^2 $$
prove $p_r\leq1/\sqrt{2r+1}$ by induction. The odd case follows from its central atom
$$ p_r\frac{2r+1}{2r+2}\leq\frac1{\sqrt{2r+2}}. $$

The remaining allocation has lattice spacing $2\delta_n$. An interval of radius $\varepsilon$ contains at most $\varepsilon/\delta_n+1$ possible values. Therefore conditional success probability is at most
$$ \frac{\varepsilon/\delta_n+1}{\sqrt{m+1}}. $$

Choose $n=32(N+1)$. Then $m\geq31N+32$. If
$$ \varepsilon\leq\frac{\delta_n\sqrt{n-N}}4, $$
the preceding probability is at most
$$ \frac14+\frac1{\sqrt{33}}<\frac23. $$
Consequently, a successful algorithm must satisfy
$$ \varepsilon> c\frac{B\tau}{(N+1)^{3/2}} $$
for a universal $c>0$. Combining this with $N\geq\tau\geq2$ yields
$$ N^*(\tau,B,\varepsilon)\geq c r^{2/3}. $$

5. A Gaussian maximum-norm lemma

The strengthened joint lower bound needs simultaneous uncertainty in many coordinates.

Lemma. There is a universal $a_0>0$ with the following property. If a centered Gaussian vector $G\in\mathbb R^d$ has covariance $K_0$ satisfying
$$ 0\preceq K_0\preceq nI_d, \qquad \operatorname{tr}K_0\geq\frac{nd}{2}, $$
then
$$ \Pr\left( \|G\|_\infty\leq a_0\sqrt{n\log(2d)} \right)\leq\frac14. $$
Consequently, for every center $b\in\mathbb R^d$,
$$ \Pr\left( \|G-b\|_\infty \leq \frac{a_0}{\sqrt2}\sqrt{n\log(2d)} \right)\leq\frac12. $$

5.1. Selecting many weakly correlated coordinates

Let
$$ T=\{i:(K_0)_{ii}\geq n/4\}. $$
Since every diagonal entry is at most $n$,
$$ \frac{nd}{2} \leq\operatorname{tr}K_0 \leq n|T|+\frac n4(d-|T|). $$
Thus $|T|\geq d/3$.

Form a graph on $T$, joining $i$ and $j$ when
$$ |(K_0)_{ij}|>n/16. $$
Because the eigenvalues of $K_0$ lie in $[0,n]$,
$$ \sum_{i,j}(K_0)_{ij}^2 = \operatorname{tr}(K_0^2) \leq n\operatorname{tr}K_0 \leq n^2d. $$
Each edge contributes twice to this sum, so the graph has at most $128d$ edges.

A graph with $m$ vertices and $e$ edges has an independent set of size at least $m^2/(2e+m)$. To verify this, take a uniformly random ordering and select each vertex that precedes all its neighbors. The selected set is independent, and its expected size is
$$ \sum_v\frac1{\deg(v)+1} \geq\frac{m^2}{2e+m} $$
by Cauchy–Schwarz.

Hence there is an independent set $J$ of size
$$ M:=|J|\geq\frac d{2313}. $$
For $i\in J$, standardize
$$ W_i=\frac{G_i}{\sqrt{(K_0)_{ii}}}. $$
These variables have variance one, and their off-diagonal covariances are at most $1/4$.

5.2. Gaussian comparison, with proof

We use the following comparison: if centered Gaussian vectors $X,Y$ have equal coordinate variances and
$$ \operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j) \leq \operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Y_j) \quad(i\ne j), $$
then
$$ \Pr(\max_iX_i\leq t) \leq \Pr(\max_iY_i\leq t). $$

Here is a proof. For independent $X,Y$, set
$$ Z(u)=\sqrt{1-u}\,X+\sqrt u\,Y. $$
Gaussian integration by parts gives, for smooth bounded $f$,
$$ \frac d{du}\mathbb Ef(Z(u)) = \frac12\sum_{i,j} \left( \operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Y_j) - \operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j) \right) \mathbb E[\partial_i\partial_jf(Z(u))]. $$
This identity follows by differentiating and applying
$$ \mathbb E[Z_i h(Z)] = \sum_j\operatorname{Cov}(Z_i,Z_j) \mathbb E[\partial_jh(Z)], $$
which is integration by parts in the Gaussian density. Singular covariance matrices follow by adding independent Gaussian noise and taking a limit.

Take $f(x)$ to be a product of smooth decreasing approximations to the indicators of $x_i\leq t$, with values in $[0,1]$. Its mixed second derivatives are nonnegative. The diagonal covariance differences vanish, so the interpolation derivative is nonnegative. Taking the indicator limit proves the comparison.

Apply it to $W$ and
$$ Y_i=\frac12Z_0+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}Z_i, $$
where $Z_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_M$ are independent standard Gaussians.

If $\|G\|_\infty\leq a\sqrt{n\log(2d)}$, then
$$ W_i\leq 2a\sqrt{\log(2d)} \quad(i\in J). $$
Since $d\leq2313M$, a sufficiently small universal $a$ ensures, for $M\geq2$, that
$$ 2a\sqrt{\log(2d)} \leq\frac18\sqrt{\log M}. $$

On $Z_0\geq-3$, the event $\max_iY_i\leq t$ implies
$$ \max_iZ_i\leq\frac{t+3/2}{\sqrt3/2}. $$
For all sufficiently large $M$, with the above choice of $t$, this upper threshold is at most $\frac12\sqrt{\log M}$.

Moreover,
$$ \Pr\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq M}Z_i \leq\frac12\sqrt{\log M}\right)\longrightarrow0. $$
Indeed, for $u\geq0$,
$$ \Pr(Z_1>u) \geq \frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-(u+1)^2/2}, $$
by integrating the density over $[u,u+1]$. At $u=\frac12\sqrt{\log M}$, the product of this lower bound and $M$ tends to infinity; independence then gives the asserted limit.

Choose a universal $M_0$ large enough that this maximum probability is at most $1/8$, and that the preceding threshold comparison holds, for every $M\geq M_0$. Since
$$ \Pr(Z_0<-3)\leq e^{-9/2}, $$
the required central-box probability is less than $1/4$ whenever $d\geq2313M_0$.

For the remaining finitely bounded dimensions, some coordinate has variance at least $n/2$. Its density bound gives
$$ \Pr\left(\|G\|_\infty\leq a\sqrt{n\log(2d)}\right) \leq 2a\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d)}{\pi}}. $$
Decreasing $a$ if necessary makes this at most $1/4$ for every $d<2313M_0$. This proves the first assertion with a universal $a_0>0$.

Finally, let $G'$ be an independent copy of $G$. If
$$ p=\Pr(\|G-b\|_\infty\leq\eta), $$
then
$$ p^2\leq\Pr(\|G-G'\|_\infty\leq2\eta). $$
The covariance of $G-G'$ is $2K_0$, so the first assertion, with $n$ replaced by $2n$, makes the right-hand side at most $1/4$ when
$$ \eta\leq\frac{a_0}{\sqrt2}\sqrt{n\log(2d)}. $$
Thus $p\leq1/2$, proving the lemma.

6. The strengthened joint lower bound

Consider any successful algorithm with worst-case budget $N$. Section 4 gives $N\geq\tau$.

Set
$$ d=\lfloor\tau/2\rfloor, \qquad n=\left\lceil\frac{2N}{d}\right\rceil. $$
Draw independent standard Gaussian vectors $b_j\in\mathbb R^d$, and put
$$ H=\sqrt{2\log(24nd)}. $$
Define paired coefficients
$$ a_{j,2\ell-1}=\frac{b_{j\ell}}H, \qquad a_{j,2\ell}=-\frac{b_{j\ell}}H, \qquad \ell=1,\ldots,d. $$
When $\tau$ is odd, set the final coefficient to zero.

Let $E$ be the event that all $nd$ Gaussian entries have magnitude at most $H$. The Gaussian tail bound and a union bound give
$$ \Pr(E)\geq1-2nd\,e^{-H^2/2}=\frac{11}{12}. $$
On $E$, all coefficient vectors satisfy the hypotheses of Section 3, so the resulting oracle is admissible.

For the probabilistic argument, also define these functions outside $E$, and force the algorithm to stop after $N$ queries there. This does not affect its guarantee on admissible inputs.

A query in block $j$ depends on $b_j$ through the single linear measurement
$$ \sum_{\ell=1}^d b_{j\ell}(z_{2\ell-1}-z_{2\ell}). $$
Grant the algorithm that measurement. Given it, the original response can be computed, so this is a stronger oracle.

Condition on the random seed and the strengthened transcript. Sequential Gaussian conditioning shows that the posterior block covariances are orthogonal projections $P_j$. They remain block diagonal, because each measurement acts on one block. Every query removes at most one unit of covariance trace, hence
$$ \sum_j\operatorname{tr}P_j \geq nd-N \geq\frac{nd}{2}. $$
For
$$ S=\sum_jb_j, $$
the posterior covariance is
$$ K_0=\sum_jP_j, \qquad 0\preceq K_0\preceq nI_d, \qquad \operatorname{tr}K_0\geq\frac{nd}{2}. $$

The odd-indexed allocations satisfy
$$ \Phi_{2\ell-1}=\frac{\delta_n}{H}S_\ell. $$
Thus maximum-norm allocation success requires $S$ to lie in a box of radius $\varepsilon H/\delta_n$ around a transcript-dependent center. The shifted-box assertion of Section 5 applies to its conditional Gaussian law; its conditional mean is absorbed into the center.

Consequently, conditional success probability is at most $1/2$ whenever
$$ \frac{\varepsilon H}{\delta_n} \leq \frac{a_0}{\sqrt2}\sqrt{n\log(2d)}. $$
But the guarantee on admissible inputs requires unconditional success probability at least
$$ \frac23\Pr(E)\geq\frac{11}{18}>\frac12. $$
Therefore a successful algorithm must satisfy
$$ \varepsilon> c\frac{B\tau\sqrt{\log(2d)}}{Hn^{3/2}}. $$

Because $N\geq\tau$,
$$ n\leq\frac{3N}{d}, \qquad nd\leq3N, \qquad H^2\leq2\log(72N). $$
Squaring and rearranging gives
$$ N^3\log(72N) \geq c\frac{B^2\tau^2d^3}{\varepsilon^2}\log(2d). $$
For $\tau\geq2$, $d\geq\tau/3$ and $\log(2d)$ is within universal constant factors of $\log(2\tau)$. Hence
$$ N^3\log(2+N) \geq c\tau^3r^2\log(2\tau). $$

Apply this to $N=N^*$. The deterministic upper bound gives
$$ N^*\leq C\tau(1+r), $$
so
$$ \log(2+N^*)\leq C'\lambda. $$
It follows that
$$ N^* \geq c\tau r^{2/3} \left(\frac{\log(2\tau)}{\lambda}\right)^{1/3}. $$
Together with Section 4, this proves all the lower bounds stated in Section 1.

In particular, for every fixed $A>0$, the parameter range
$$ 1\leq r\leq\tau^A $$
has the lower bound
$$ N^*\geq c_A\tau r^{2/3}. $$
The upper bound can still exceed this by a logarithmic factor.

7. Coalition data cannot determine the continuous allocation accurately

7.1. A dimension-amplified counterexample

7.2. An upper bound on the ambiguity of complete vertex data

The preceding obstruction has the correct order in $\tau B$.

For a vertex game $v(A)=F(\mathbf1_A)$, define its Shapley allocation by
$$ \operatorname{Sh}_i(v) = \sum_{A\subseteq[\tau]\setminus\{i\}} \frac{|A|!(\tau-|A|-1)!}{\tau!} \bigl(v(A\cup\{i\})-v(A)\bigr). $$
We prove
$$ |\operatorname{Sh}_i(v)-\Phi_i| \leq\frac{B\tau}{3}. $$

For $s\in[0,1]$, let the coordinates of $U_{-i}$ be independent Bernoulli variables with parameter $s$. The beta integral
$$ \int_0^1s^a(1-s)^b\,ds=\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b+1)!} $$
for nonnegative integers $a,b$ follows by repeated integration by parts. Applying it with $a=|A|$ and $b=\tau-1-|A|$ gives
$$ \operatorname{Sh}_i(v) = \int_0^1 \mathbb E_s\bigl[F(U_{-i},1)-F(U_{-i},0)\bigr]\,ds. $$
The fundamental theorem of calculus then gives
$$ \operatorname{Sh}_i(v) = \int_0^1\int_0^1 \mathbb E_s[\partial_iF(U_{-i},u)]\,du\,ds. $$

Changing coordinates one at a time inside the cube, the Hessian bound implies
$$ |\partial_iF(U_{-i},u)-\partial_iF(s\mathbf1)| \leq B\left( |u-s|+\sum_{j\ne i}|U_j-s| \right). $$
Now
$$ \mathbb E_s|U_j-s|=2s(1-s), $$
and
$$ \int_0^1 2s(1-s)\,ds=\frac13, \qquad \int_0^1\int_0^1|u-s|\,du\,ds=\frac13. $$
Integrating the derivative-difference bound proves
$$ |\operatorname{Sh}_i(v)-\Phi_i| \leq B\left(\frac13+\frac{\tau-1}{3}\right) =\frac{B\tau}{3}. $$

Consequently, complete vertex data admit an estimator with maximum-norm error at most $B\tau/3$, whereas Section 7.1 gives a lower obstruction of $B(\tau-1)/18$. Their unavoidable accuracy scale is therefore
$$ \Theta(\tau B). $$

8. Arithmetic cost of the sampled surrogate

Let $T$ be the number of sampled nonempty proper coalitions, with
$$ 0\leq T\leq2^\tau-2, $$
and let
$$ D_k=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{\tau}{j}. $$
For the permitted degrees, $D_k=\Theta(\tau^k)$.

The procedure makes $T+2$ value queries. Every society query coordinate is either zero or an input mass, so its binary length is $O(L)$.

For a sampled coalition $A$, the design row is
$$ X_{A,S}=\mathbf1_{\{S\subseteq A\}}, \qquad |S|\leq k. $$
Forming the weighted normal equations takes $O(TD_k^2)$ arithmetic operations. The efficiency constraint is
$$ \sum_{S\ne\varnothing}c_S=F(\mathbf1)-F(0). $$
It is always feasible, for example by putting the right-hand side into one singleton coefficient.

Elimination on the constrained normal equations gives a particular minimizing coefficient vector and a basis for all homogeneous minimizing directions. Orthogonal projection off that homogeneous space selects the minimum-norm minimizer. Explicitly, if the independent basis vectors are the columns of $Z$ and $c_0$ is a particular minimizer, the selected vector is
$$ c_0-Z(Z^\mathsf TZ)^{-1}Z^\mathsf Tc_0. $$
The Gram matrix is invertible because the columns of $Z$ are independent. These operations take $O(D_k^3)$ time, including rank-deficient cases.

For a multilinear monomial indexed by nonempty $S$,
$$ \int_0^1\partial_i\prod_{t\in S}z_t\bigg|_{z=s\mathbf1}\,ds = \begin{cases} 1/|S|,&i\in S,\\ 0,&i\notin S. \end{cases} $$
Hence the returned allocations are
$$ \widehat\Phi_i=\sum_{S\ni i}\frac{c_S}{|S|}, $$
and can be formed in $O(kD_k)$ operations.

The required sampling distribution also has an implementation without enumerating all coalitions. Precompute binomial coefficients, and use the common denominator
$$ D_{\mathrm w} = \prod_{a=1}^{\tau-1}\binom{\tau-2}{a-1}. $$
Its binary length is $O(\tau^2)$. Each coalition of size $a$ then has integer weight
$$ u_a=\frac{D_{\mathrm w}}{\binom{\tau-2}{a-1}}. $$

Maintain the remaining count $m_a$ at each size. Select size $a$ with probability proportional to $m_au_a$, then select a uniform unused coalition of that size. Each remaining coalition consequently receives probability proportional to its required weight.

Exact uniform integers can be sampled by rejection from the next power-of-two range, with fewer than two expected trials. The relevant weight totals have $O(\tau^2)$ bits. Store removed combinatorial ranks in sorted lists; mapping an unused rank to its original rank and updating the lists takes $O(T)$ operations per draw. Combinatorial unranking takes $O(\tau)$ operations using the precomputed binomial coefficients.

A conservative total expected arithmetic and random-bit operation bound is therefore
$$ O\left( L+\tau^2+T\tau^2+T^2+TD_k^2+D_k^3 \right). $$
This running-time statement supplies no continuous-allocation accuracy guarantee.
