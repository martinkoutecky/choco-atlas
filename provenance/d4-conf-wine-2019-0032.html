Theorem O.3 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an explicit alternative set \(\mathcal A\), rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), rational valuations \(v_t(A)\), and a disagreement alternative \(D\), compute the lexicographically maximal ascending utility quantile among per-type utilities \(u_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_tu_t=B=\max_{A\in\mathcal A}\sum_t\mu_tv_t(A)\), \(u_t\ge v_t(D)\), and \(\sum_t x_tu_t\le\max_{A\in\mathcal A}\sum_t x_tv_t(A)\) for every \(x\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\), where \(B\) is maximum welfare. The selected alternative must attain \(B\), and transfers \(p_t=u_t-v_t(A^*)\) must balance in mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), encoded in binary and summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also gives an explicit nonempty alternative set \(\mathcal A\), with \(m=|\mathcal A|\), a disagreement alternative \(D\in\mathcal A\), and valuations \(v_t(A)\in\mathbb Q\) of either sign; \(L\) is the total binary input length.
Each type receives a common utility \(u_t\in\mathbb R\). Define \(B=\max_{A\in\mathcal A}\sum_t\mu_tv_t(A)\). Feasibility requires \(\sum_t\mu_tu_t=B\), \(u_t\ge v_t(D)\), and \(\sum_tx_tu_t\le\max_{A\in\mathcal A}\sum_tx_tv_t(A)\) for every \(x\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\); coalitions may contain any fraction of each type.
Normalize disagreement utilities before the fairness comparison: let \(g_t=u_t-v_t(D)\). The ascending mass quantile is \(Q_g(q)=\min\{z:\sum_{t:g_t\le z}\mu_t>q\}\) for \(q\in[0,1)\). Compare two such step functions on their common breakpoint partition, preferring the larger value on the first interval where they differ.
The computational problem is to report infeasibility or compute an exact feasible utility vector maximizing this quantile lexicographically, together with any \(A^*\) attaining \(B\) and unrestricted signed transfers \(p_t=u_t-v_t(A^*)\), satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_tp_t=0\).

Is this computational problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies, computing the corresponding disagreement-normalized lexicographic welfare-sharing solution is NP-hard even with polynomially many explicit alternatives and a deterministic disagreement alternative; submodularity of coalition maximum welfare permits a strongly polynomial algorithm. [Discrete results](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.11346)
No complexity result for exactly the mass formulation above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The graded question omitted disagreement normalization in its objective. Compare gains u_t-v_t(D), following the discrete selection definition, while retaining the stated constraints on original utilities.
  • Define the ascending quantile using mass rather than an unweighted ordering of types, and compare step functions on their common breakpoint partition.
  • Handle an empty feasible set by reporting infeasibility, since welfare-sharing feasibility is not guaranteed for arbitrary signed valuations.
  • Require D to belong to the explicit nonempty alternative set; allow signed rational valuations, unrestricted signed transfers, and any welfare-maximizing alternative in a tie.
  • Interpret computation as exact computation, as requested by the graded question, rather than introducing the numerical tolerance used in the discrete algorithm discussion.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Fractional coalitions yield a polynomial-size description of the feasible set, and successive linear programs compute its unique lexicographically maximal mass quantile.

1. Fractional coalitions characterize the feasible utility vectors

Write \(v^A=(v_t(A))_{t=1}^{\tau}\), \(\mu=(\mu_t)_{t=1}^{\tau}\), and let
\(\mathcal O=\{A\in\mathcal A:\mu\cdot v^A=B\}\).
This set is nonempty and can be computed by exact rational arithmetic.

We first prove the characterization
\[ \text{feasible utilities} = \operatorname{conv}\{v^A:A\in\mathcal O\} \cap\{u:u_t\ge v_t(D)\text{ for every }t\}. \]
Here and below, an equation displayed on its own line is understood with the same mathematical delimiters as the surrounding text.

More explicitly, the claimed equality is
\(\mathcal U=\operatorname{conv}\{v^A:A\in\mathcal O\}\cap\{u:u_t\ge v_t(D)\ \forall t\}\).

For necessity, suppose \(u\) satisfies efficiency and every fractional-coalition inequality. Fix an arbitrary direction \(h\in\mathbb R^\tau\), and consider
\(x(\varepsilon)=\mu/2+\varepsilon h\).

Because every \(\mu_t\) is positive, \(x(\varepsilon)\) lies in \(\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\) for sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\). At \(\varepsilon=0\), every alternative in \(\mathcal O\) has coalition welfare \(B/2\), whereas every alternative outside \(\mathcal O\) has strictly smaller welfare. Since there are finitely many alternatives, these strict inequalities persist for sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\). Consequently,
\(\max_{A\in\mathcal A}x(\varepsilon)\cdot v^A=B/2+\varepsilon\max_{A\in\mathcal O}h\cdot v^A\).

The coalition inequality and \(\mu\cdot u=B\) give
\(B/2+\varepsilon h\cdot u\le B/2+\varepsilon\max_{A\in\mathcal O}h\cdot v^A\).
Subtracting \(B/2\) and dividing by \(\varepsilon\) yields
\(h\cdot u\le\max_{A\in\mathcal O}h\cdot v^A\).

This holds for every \(h\in\mathbb R^\tau\). The convex hull of the finitely many vectors \(v^A\), \(A\in\mathcal O\), is compact and convex. If \(u\) were outside it, the strict separation theorem would supply a direction \(h\) violating the preceding inequality. Thus \(u\) belongs to that convex hull. Disagreement domination supplies the remaining inequalities in the characterization.

For sufficiency, suppose
\(u=\sum_{A\in\mathcal O}\theta_Av^A\),
where \(\theta_A\ge0\) and \(\sum_A\theta_A=1\), and suppose \(u_t\ge v_t(D)\). Then
\(\mu\cdot u=\sum_A\theta_A B=B\).
For every coalition \(x\),
\(x\cdot u=\sum_A\theta_A(x\cdot v^A)\le\max_{A\in\mathcal A}x\cdot v^A\).
All feasibility requirements therefore hold.

2. A polynomial-size feasibility formulation

Define \(d_t=v_t(D)\) and \(s_t(A)=v_t(A)-d_t\). By the characterization above, the feasible gain vectors form
\(\mathcal P=\operatorname{conv}\{s(A):A\in\mathcal O\}\cap\mathbb R^\tau_{\ge0}\).

In particular, feasibility is exactly the feasibility of the following rational linear system:

  • \(\theta_A\ge0\) for \(A\in\mathcal O\);
  • \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal O}\theta_A=1\);
  • \(g_t=\sum_{A\in\mathcal O}\theta_As_t(A)\) for every \(t\);
  • \(g_t\ge0\) for every \(t\).

It has at most \(m+\tau\) variables and polynomially many constraints, with coefficients of polynomial binary encoding length. Exact rational linear programming therefore decides feasibility in polynomial time.

If this system is infeasible, the algorithm reports infeasibility. Otherwise, \(\mathcal P\) is a nonempty compact convex polytope.

3. Successive minimum-gain optimization

We now give an algorithm for any nonempty compact convex polytope \(\mathcal P\subseteq\mathbb R^\tau_{\ge0}\) with the preceding explicit formulation.

Initially, put \(C_0=\mathcal P\) and \(R_0=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\). The set \(R_j\) will contain the types whose gains have not yet been fixed.

At stage \(j\), provided \(R_{j-1}\ne\varnothing\), compute
\(\ell_j=\max_{g\in C_{j-1}}\min_{t\in R_{j-1}}g_t\).

This is a linear program: maximize \(z\), subject to \(g\in C_{j-1}\) and \(g_t\ge z\) for every \(t\in R_{j-1}\). Compactness ensures attainment. Define
\(C_j=\{g\in C_{j-1}:g_t\ge\ell_j\ \forall t\in R_{j-1}\}\)
and
\(I_j=\{t\in R_{j-1}:g_t=\ell_j\text{ for every }g\in C_j\}\).

Each membership test for \(I_j\) is another linear program: maximize \(g_t\) over \(C_j\). Since every such gain is at least \(\ell_j\), the type belongs to \(I_j\) exactly when this maximum equals \(\ell_j\).

We claim that \(I_j\) is nonempty. Otherwise, for every \(t\in R_{j-1}\), there would be a point \(g^{(t)}\in C_j\) with \(g^{(t)}_t>\ell_j\). Their average belongs to \(C_j\) by convexity. For each remaining coordinate, every summand is at least \(\ell_j\) and one is strictly larger. Hence the average has every remaining gain strictly larger than \(\ell_j\), contradicting its definition.

Fix the gains of types in \(I_j\) at \(\ell_j\), and put \(R_j=R_{j-1}\setminus I_j\). At least one type is removed at every stage, so the process stops after at most \(\tau\) stages. Its final set \(C_K\) contains exactly one gain vector, denoted \(\widehat g\): every coordinate has been fixed.

The successive levels are strictly increasing whenever another stage exists. Indeed, for every \(t\in R_j\), its exclusion from \(I_j\) supplies a point of \(C_j\) with \(g_t>\ell_j\). Averaging these points gives a point with every coordinate in \(R_j\) strictly above \(\ell_j\). Thus \(\ell_{j+1}>\ell_j\).

4. Proof of optimality for the mass quantile

Let \(g\in\mathcal P\) differ from \(\widehat g\). Since \(C_K=\{\widehat g\}\), there is a first stage \(j\) for which
\(g\in C_{j-1}\) but \(g\notin C_j\).
Set
\(r=\min_{t\in R_{j-1}}g_t\).
Then \(r<\ell_j\).

Let \(F=I_1\cup\cdots\cup I_{j-1}\), and let \(M=\sum_{t\in F}\mu_t\). The gains of every type in \(F\) agree between \(g\) and \(\widehat g\).

If \(j>1\), membership in \(C_{j-1}\) also implies \(r\ge\ell_{j-1}\). Thus every gain of a type in \(F\) is at most \(r\). Meanwhile, every type outside \(F\) has gain at least \(r\) under \(g\) and at least \(\ell_j\) under \(\widehat g\).

It follows that the two quantile functions agree for \(q<M\). To justify the possible boundary tie: additional remaining types can have gain \(\ell_{j-1}\) under \(g\), but they merely extend the last already fixed plateau; they cannot change a quantile value before the total fixed mass \(M\).

At \(q=M\), the strict inequality in the quantile definition gives
\(Q_g(M)=r\) and \(Q_{\widehat g}(M)=\ell_j\).

For the first equality, the fixed types contribute mass \(M\), and the remaining types attaining \(r\) contribute additional positive mass. For the second, the nonempty set \(I_j\) contributes positive mass at \(\ell_j\). Also \(M<1\), because \(R_{j-1}\ne\varnothing\).

Both functions are finite step functions, so these unequal values persist on a nonempty interval beginning at \(M\). Hence their first differing interval favors \(\widehat g\), since \(\ell_j>r\).

Therefore \(\widehat g\) strictly defeats every distinct feasible gain vector under the specified mass-quantile comparison. It is the unique maximizer. The argument uses positivity of the masses, without expanding them into individual agents.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

The algorithm uses at most \(\tau\) stage linear programs and at most \(\tau(\tau+1)/2\) coordinate tests. We must additionally bound the encoding lengths of the recursively computed levels.

Let
\(H=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{s_t(A):t=1,\ldots,\tau,\ A\in\mathcal O\}\bigr)\).
Every feasible gain and every computed level lies in \([0,H]\).

For any stage \(j\), consider a single auxiliary polytope with variables \(\theta,g,h_1,\ldots,h_j\). Include the original feasibility formulation and add:

  • \(0\le h_1\le\cdots\le h_j\le H\);
  • \(g_t=h_k\) for \(t\in I_k\), \(k<j\);
  • \(g_t\ge h_j\) for \(t\in R_{j-1}\).

This is a nonempty bounded rational polytope with polynomially many variables and constraints. Its coefficients come from the original formulation, \(H\), and the integers \(0,1,-1\). In particular, its description does not contain any previously computed level.

Sequentially maximizing \(h_1,\ldots,h_j\) over this polytope gives precisely \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_j\). Here is the verification. Once \(h_1,\ldots,h_{k-1}\) equal their computed levels, the constraints place \(g\) in \(C_{k-1}\) and require every type in \(R_{k-1}\) to have gain at least \(h_k\). Therefore \(h_k\le\ell_k\). Conversely, any point of \(C_j\), together with \(h_k=\ell_k\), satisfies all auxiliary constraints. Thus every bound is attained.

Each sequential maximization selects a nonempty face of the preceding face. The final face contains a vertex of the auxiliary polytope, whose \(h\)-coordinates are the computed levels. Standard determinant bounds for vertices of rational polytopes consequently give polynomial binary encoding length for every \(\ell_k\), uniformly in \(j\).

All linear programs actually solved by the algorithm therefore have polynomial encoding length. The polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming applies: feasibility, exact bounded optima, and exact rational feasible points can all be computed in time polynomial in their encoded descriptions. Together with the polynomial number of calls, this proves polynomial running time in \(m,\tau,L\).

6. Utilities, transfers, and the discrete distinction

Return \(u_t=d_t+\widehat g_t\). Choose any \(A^*\in\mathcal O\), and set \(p_t=u_t-v_t(A^*)\). These are exact rational outputs of polynomial encoding length. Their budget balance follows from
\(\sum_t\mu_tp_t=\mu\cdot u-\mu\cdot v^{A^*}=B-B=0\).

The discrete hardness result concerns coalitions consisting of subsets of whole agents; see [Babaioff and Feige, Theorem O.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.11346). The decisive additional condition here is the availability of every sufficiently small perturbation of the interior coalition \(\mu/2\). It forces efficient utilities into the convex hull of welfare-maximizing valuation vectors, producing the polynomial-size formulation above.
