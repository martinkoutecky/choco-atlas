The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) indivisible goods, a known support \(T\) of \(\tau\) valuation types with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and an unknown normalized, free-disposal valuation \(v_t\) in an atomic language \(L\) for each type, with value and demand queries available to a representative of each type, recover every \(v_t\) and thus the exact weighted measure \(\nu=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\delta_{v_t}\). If \(q_t=\mathrm{size}_L(v_t)\), can this be done with at most \(\sum_t(q_t(m+1)+1)\) demand queries and \(\sum_t q_t\) value queries?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) contain \(m\geq1\) indivisible goods. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Fix a known atomic language \(\mathcal L\). A representation is \((B,w)\), where \(B\subseteq2^G\) and \(w:B\to\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\); its semantics are \(v(S)=\phi_{\mathcal L}(S;B,w)\). The empty representation denotes zero. For valid representations, the evaluator agrees with atomic weights, depends only on contained atoms, and is monotone under compatible addition of atoms: \(\phi_{\mathcal L}(A;B,w)=w(A)\) for \(A\in B\), \(\phi_{\mathcal L}(S;B,w)=\phi_{\mathcal L}(S;B|_S,w|_{B|_S})\), and \(\phi_{\mathcal L}(S;B\cup B',w')\geq\phi_{\mathcal L}(S;B,w)\) when \(w'|_B=w\), where \(B|_S=\{A\in B:A\subseteq S\}\).
Each unknown \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\) is normalized, satisfies free disposal, has an \(\mathcal L\)-representation, and satisfies \(v_t(S)<K\) for a known positive binary rational \(K\). Let \(q_t\) be its minimum number of atoms and \(Q=\sum_tq_t\).
A representative of each type answers truthful queries. \(\operatorname{VALUE}_t(S)\) returns \(v_t(S)\). \(\operatorname{DEMAND}_t(S,p)\) returns YES exactly when \(S\) maximizes \(v_t(R)-p(R)\); otherwise it returns any \(R\) with strictly greater utility. Ties require YES, and the empty bundle is permitted.
Prices are nonnegative functions on bundles, with succinct symbolic descriptions using \(\mathcal L\)-representations, rational constants, and piecewise bundle-containment tests. Oracle calls have unit cost; writing queries, reading answers, and other computation count toward running time. Let \(L\) include the encoded support and bound and a bound on the bit length of exact valuation answers. Recovery means producing an \(\mathcal L\)-representation of each \(v_t\), specifying \(\nu=\sum_t\mu_t\delta_{v_t}\).

Is exact recovery polynomial-time solvable in this oracle model, with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,Q\) and at most \(\sum_t(q_t(m+1)+1)\) demand queries and \(Q\) value queries, or is it NP-hard even with these oracles?

For one discrete agent, exact learning in an atomic language is known to require at most \(q(m+1)+1\) demand queries and \(q\) value queries; this is a query-complexity guarantee. [Lahaie, Constantin and Parkes](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/05/Papers/1481.pdf)
No separate result for the specified distribution-input problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored the known strict upper bound K on all valuation values, which the continuous question omitted.
  • Restricted valuation values and atomic weights to binary rationals and introduced an answer-bit-length parameter to make polynomial running time meaningful; the discrete model permits real values.
  • Fixed the known atomic language and made recovery mean an exact atomic representation, rather than an exponentially large table of bundle values.
  • Specified the empty representation as zero and interpreted the atomic-language axioms on valid representations and compatible extensions.
  • Specified the nonstandard demand oracle, including arbitrary strictly improving counterexamples, YES on utility ties, and permission to return the empty bundle.
  • Allowed succinct symbolic nonlinear prices using atomic representations and piecewise containment tests, since the discrete definition permits general bundle prices and uses such restricted prices.
  • Counted oracle calls at unit cost while charging for query descriptions, responses, and local computation; polynomial running time is measured also in total minimum representation size Q.
  • Allowed different listed types to have identical valuations; their weighted Dirac terms still specify the exact measure.

  • An additional, explicit structural condition yields polynomial-time recovery with exactly the requested query bounds. This condition holds for XOR and OR.
  • Passing from one unknown valuation to an explicitly listed support introduces no additional recovery difficulty.

These results do not settle polynomial-time recovery or oracle-relative NP-hardness for every language allowed by USER_TASK. In particular, the counterexample concerns the proposed implementation; it does not rule out another learner satisfying the supplied single-agent query-complexity guarantee.

1. An exponential counterexample to the proposed learner

1.1 A fixed admissible language

For every finite goods set $G$, define
$$ c_B(S)=\bigl|\{A\in B:A\subseteq S,\ |A|=1\}\bigr| $$
and
$$ \phi_{\mathcal L}(S;B,w) = \max\left( \{0,c_B(S)-1\} \cup \{w(A):A\in B,\ A\subseteq S\} \right). $$

Declare $(B,w)$ valid precisely when, for every $A\in B$,
$$ w(A)\geq \max\{0,c_B(A)-1\} $$
and
$$ w(A)\geq w(C) \qquad \text{for every }C\in B\text{ with }C\subseteq A. $$

This is one fixed language: the same rule applies for every number of goods.

It satisfies the required semantic properties:

  • Atomic agreement. At an atom $A$, its own weight is included in the maximum. The validity inequalities ensure that every other term is at most $w(A)$.
  • Dependence on contained atoms. Both the singleton count and the weight terms depend only on $B|_S$.
  • Monotonicity under compatible addition. Adding atoms cannot decrease the singleton count or remove any weight term.
  • Empty representation. With no atoms, the evaluation is zero.

Moreover, every subrepresentation of a valid representation is valid. Deletion can only decrease singleton counts and remove inequalities involving deleted atoms.

Every represented function satisfies free disposal: if $S\subseteq R$, then the singleton count cannot decrease and every weight term available at $S$ remains available at $R$.

1.2 A target whose minimum representation has $m$ atoms

Let $m\geq3$, and define
$$ v(S)=\max\{0,|S|-1\}. $$

The representation consisting of all singleton atoms, each with weight zero, represents $v$. It is valid because each singleton contains exactly one singleton atom. Thus its minimum representation size satisfies $q\leq m$.

For the reverse inequality, consider any valid representation of $v$. By atomic agreement, every singleton atom has weight zero; an empty atom, if present, also has weight zero.

Let $k$ be the number of singleton atoms. Consider a two-element bundle $P$ that does not have both of its singletons among those atoms. Then
$$ c_B(P)\leq1. $$
If $P$ itself were absent from $B$, its contained atoms could only be singleton atoms and an empty atom, all of weight zero. Consequently,
$$ \phi_{\mathcal L}(P;B,w)=0, $$
contrary to $v(P)=1$.

Therefore every pair except possibly those entirely among the $k$ singleton atoms must itself be an atom. Hence
$$ |B| \geq k+\binom m2-\binom k2. $$
For $0\leq k\leq m$ and $m\geq3$,
$$ k+\binom m2-\binom k2-m = \frac{(m-k)(m+k-3)}2 \geq0. $$
It follows that $|B|\geq m$, proving
$$ q=m. $$

The target is normalized and rational. Taking $K=m$ gives
$$ v(S)\leq m-1<K. $$
One type of mass $1$ therefore gives an admissible society.

1.3 An execution that adds every bundle of size at least two

Consider precisely the learner in NOTES. Its manifest starts empty. An outer demand query uses manifest prices $h$. A returned discrepancy bundle $S$ is then tested using
$$ p_{H,S}(R)= \begin{cases} h(R),&R\subsetneq S,\\ K,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
If this demand query returns YES, the learner adds $(S,v(S))$.

Order all bundles of size at least two by nondecreasing cardinality, breaking ties arbitrarily. The outer demand oracle can return them in this order.

To verify this assertion, suppose the next bundle is $S$, of cardinality $r\geq2$. All previously added atoms have weight $|A|-1$, and none is a singleton. The manifest therefore evaluates as
$$ h(R)= \max\left( \{0\}\cup \{|A|-1:A\text{ was previously added},\ A\subseteq R\} \right). $$

Every previously added atom contained in $S$ is a proper subset of $S$. Consequently,
$$ h(S)\leq r-2<r-1=v(S). $$
The empty bundle has utility zero, so $S$ is a permitted strict improvement in the outer demand query.

On the other hand, every proper subset $R\subsetneq S$ satisfies
$$ h(R)=v(R). $$
Indeed, this is immediate for $|R|\leq1$. If $|R|\geq2$, then $R$ was already added, so its own atom contributes $|R|-1$; every other contained atom contributes at most that value.

For the shrinking query, every proper subset of $S$ consequently has utility zero. Every other bundle has utility
$$ v(R)-K<0. $$
Thus the empty bundle maximizes utility, and the oracle must return YES. The learner adds $S$ immediately.

Every intermediate representation is valid: it contains no singleton atoms, and weights $|A|-1$ increase under containment.

After all bundles of size at least two have been added, the manifest equals $v$ everywhere, so the next outer query returns YES.

The resulting counts are therefore
$$ N_V=2^m-m-1 $$
and
$$ N_D=2(2^m-m-1)+1. $$

Already at $m=3$, the value-query count is $4>q=3$. At $m=4$, both requested bounds fail:
$$ N_V=11>4, \qquad N_D=23>4(4+1)+1=21. $$

Thus the proposed implementation can have exponentially many rounds even though $q=m$.

1.4 What this establishes

Agreement on every proper subset of a discrepancy bundle does not imply that the bundle belongs to a fixed minimum representation. Undiscovered zero-weight atoms can affect larger bundles without creating discrepancies on their own bundles.

The cited paper’s proof uses this atom-identification inference when establishing its learning bound. The displayed semantic axioms do not justify that inference. See [Lahaie, Constantin and Parkes, Section 3.1](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/05/Papers/1481.pdf).

This does not prove a lower bound against all possible learners.

2. A sufficient condition that repairs the proof

Consider a language satisfying the stated axioms and these additional properties:

  • Deletion preserves validity. Every subrepresentation of a valid representation is valid.
  • Local redundancy implies global redundancy. For any valid $(B,w)$ and $A\in B$, if

$$ \phi_{\mathcal L}(A;B\setminus\{A\},w)=w(A), $$
then
$$ \phi_{\mathcal L}(S;B\setminus\{A\},w) = \phi_{\mathcal L}(S;B,w) \qquad\text{for every }S\subseteq G. $$

Restrictions of $w$ are implicit where atoms have been deleted.

Under these additional hypotheses, the proposed learner is correct and achieves the requested bounds.

2.1 Missing atoms necessarily create discrepancies

Fix a minimum representation $(B_*,w_*)$ of the target $v$.

For every $A\in B_*$, monotonicity under addition and atomic agreement give
$$ \phi_{\mathcal L}(A;B_*\setminus\{A\},w_*) \leq w_*(A). $$
Equality would, by the second additional property, allow deletion of $A$ without changing $v$. This would contradict minimum cardinality. Therefore
$$ \phi_{\mathcal L}(A;B_*\setminus\{A\},w_*) < w_*(A). $$

Suppose the current manifest uses some $C\subseteq B_*$ with the target weights:
$$ h(S)=\phi_{\mathcal L}(S;C,w_*). $$
For every missing atom $A\in B_*\setminus C$, monotonicity gives
$$ h(A) \leq \phi_{\mathcal L}(A;B_*\setminus\{A\},w_*) < w_*(A) = v(A). $$

Thus every missing atom is a strict discrepancy on its own bundle. This is the property absent from the counterexample.

2.2 Correctness of discovery

Initially $C=\varnothing$. Inductively suppose $C\subseteq B_*$ and its weights are correct. Validity and monotonicity give
$$ 0\leq h(S)\leq v(S) \qquad\text{for every }S\subseteq G. $$
Normalization then implies $h(\varnothing)=0$.

For the outer query $\operatorname{DEMAND}(\varnothing,h)$:

  • YES implies $v(S)-h(S)\leq0$ for every $S$. Together with $h\leq v$, this proves $h=v$.
  • Any returned bundle $S$ satisfies $v(S)>h(S)$ and is nonempty.

Apply the shrinking price displayed in Section 1.3. The empty bundle has utility zero. Any returned strict improvement must satisfy
$$ R\subsetneq S, \qquad v(R)>h(R), $$
because bundles outside the proper subsets have strictly negative utility.

Eventually a shrinking query returns YES. At that point,
$$ v(R)=h(R) \qquad\text{for every }R\subsetneq S, $$
while the current bundle still satisfies $v(S)>h(S)$.

By dependence on contained atoms, this strict discrepancy implies that some missing target atom $A\in B_*\setminus C$ satisfies $A\subseteq S$. Otherwise the target and manifest would have identical contained representations at $S$.

Section 2.1 gives $v(A)>h(A)$. Since all proper subsets of $S$ agree, $A$ cannot be a proper subset. Therefore
$$ A=S. $$

A value query on $S$ returns $w_*(S)$, so adding that atom preserves the induction invariant.

Each successful round adds a distinct atom of $B_*$. Hence there are at most $q$ rounds.

2.3 Query and computation bounds

Each successful round uses one outer demand query. Its shrinking phase uses at most $m$ demand queries: each non-YES response strictly decreases the cardinality of a nonempty discrepancy bundle, and a singleton’s shrinking query must return YES.

Including the terminating outer query gives
$$ N_D\leq q(m+1)+1, \qquad N_V\leq q. $$

The learner submits prices symbolically. It never needs to evaluate $h$ locally. A stored atom consists of an $m$-bit bundle and a queried rational weight with bit length bounded by $L$. Thus the manifest encoding has size
$$ O\bigl(q(m+L)\bigr). $$
A shrinking price additionally contains $S$, $K$, and the proper-containment test. Proper containment is expressible through the permitted tests:
$$ R\subsetneq S \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R\subseteq S\ \text{and}\ S\not\subseteq R. $$

Writing queries, reading answers, and updating representations therefore take polynomial time in $m,L,q$. No local optimization or semantic evaluation is required.

For $q=0$, the target is zero, and the initial outer query terminates immediately.

3. The sufficient condition holds for XOR and OR

3.1 XOR

Define XOR evaluation by
$$ \phi_{\mathrm{XOR}}(S;B,w) = \max\left(\{0\}\cup\{w(A):A\in B,\ A\subseteq S\}\right). $$
Use representations satisfying atomic agreement.

Subrepresentations remain valid: at a retained atom $A$, deletion cannot increase the evaluation above its original value $w(A)$, while $A$ itself still supplies $w(A)$.

Suppose deletion of $A$ leaves its own value equal to $w(A)$. Write $h$ for the evaluation after deletion. XOR evaluations satisfy free disposal, so for every $S\supseteq A$,
$$ h(S)\geq h(A)=w(A). $$
Restoring $A$ therefore does not change the maximum at $S$. For $S$ not containing $A$, restoring $A$ has no effect by containment.

This proves local redundancy implies global redundancy. Consequently Section 2 establishes the requested polynomial-time recovery guarantee for XOR.

3.2 OR

Define OR evaluation by
$$ \phi_{\mathrm{OR}}(S;B,w) = \max \left\{ \sum_{A\in D}w(A): D\subseteq B,\ A\subseteq S\text{ for every }A\in D,\ D\text{ is pairwise disjoint} \right\}. $$
The empty packing is permitted. Again, use representations satisfying atomic agreement.

For normalized targets, an empty atom has weight zero and can be omitted without changing evaluation. We may therefore work with nonempty atoms.

Subrepresentations remain valid. At a retained atom $A$, the one-atom packing achieves $w(A)$; deleting other atoms cannot increase the original evaluation above $w(A)$.

Now suppose deleting $A$ leaves its own value equal to $w(A)$. Since there are finitely many packings, some packing $D$ of remaining atoms contained in $A$ attains
$$ \sum_{C\in D}w(C)=w(A). $$

Consider any feasible packing $E$ for any bundle $S$ in the original representation.

If $A\notin E$, the packing remains available after deletion. If $A\in E$, replace $A$ by $D$. Every member of $D$ is contained in $A$, so it is disjoint from every member of $E\setminus\{A\}$. The replacement is feasible and preserves total weight.

Every original packing value is therefore achievable after deletion. Conversely, deletion cannot increase the optimum. The evaluations agree for every $S$.

Thus OR also satisfies the sufficient condition, and Section 2 establishes the requested polynomial-time recovery guarantee for OR.

4. Explicit-support recovery reduces to single-type recovery

This reduction does not require either additional structural condition.

Suppose a single-type learner for the fixed language runs in polynomial time in $m,L,q$ and uses at most
$$ q(m+1)+1 $$
demand queries and $q$ value queries.

Run it separately for each listed type $t$. Since
$$ q_t\leq Q \qquad\text{and}\qquad \sum_tq_t=Q, $$
the sum of its running times is polynomial in $m,\tau,L,Q$. The total query counts are
$$ N_D\leq Q(m+1)+\tau = \sum_t\bigl(q_t(m+1)+1\bigr) $$
and
$$ N_V\leq Q. $$

Output the original masses together with the recovered representations. This specifies exactly
$$ \nu=\sum_t\mu_t\delta_{v_t}. $$
Repeated valuations may retain separate entries; this specifies the same measure without requiring representation-equivalence testing.

Conversely, any recovery algorithm for the support-input problem applies to the single-type case by taking $\tau=1$ and mass $1$.

Thus explicit support and masses introduce no additional obstruction. The unresolved issue is the single-type computational guarantee for the full class of languages in the question.

5. Remaining gap

The stated axioms do not supply the deletion property used in Section 2. Indeed, in Section 1, deleting a zero-weight singleton preserves its own value but changes larger-bundle values.

Accordingly, the repaired proof resolves XOR, OR, and languages satisfying the additional conditions, but it does not resolve arbitrary $\mathcal L$ under the original assumptions. A complete answer still requires either a different polynomial-time learner with the specified bounds or a hardness argument that remains valid in the presence of the permitted symbolic-price demand oracle.
