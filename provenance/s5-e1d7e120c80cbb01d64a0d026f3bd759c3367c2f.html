The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite customer and 3PL type sets \(I,J\), rational type masses \(a_i,b_j\), and the paper’s rational evaluation values, acceptability thresholds, trading possibilities, trading-psychology classes, and waiting costs for each type, is there a mass flow \(x_{ij}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_j x_{ij}\le a_i\) and \(\sum_i x_{ij}\le b_j\) that is stable and satisfies \(\sum_{i,j}p^A_{ij}x_{ij}\ge q_A\) and \(\sum_{i,j}p^B_{ij}x_{ij}\ge q_B\)? Define residual masses \(u_i=a_i-\sum_jx_{ij}\) and \(v_j=b_j-\sum_ix_{ij}\). Stability means that no types \(i,j\) and incumbent statuses block with positive mass: unmatched \(i,j\) with \(u_i,v_j>0\) and \(g_{ij}\ge\alpha_i^c,\ h_{ij}\ge\beta_j^c\); unmatched \(i\) and matched \(j\) with \(u_i>0,\ x_{kj}>0\), \(j\) non-eager, \(g_{ij}\ge\alpha_i^c,\ h_{ij}>h_{kj}+c_j\); matched \(i\) and unmatched \(j\) with \(x_{i\ell}>0,\ v_j>0\), \(i\) non-eager, \(g_{ij}>g_{i\ell}+c_i,\ h_{ij}\ge\beta_j^c\); or both matched with \(x_{i\ell},x_{kj}>0\), both non-eager, \(g_{ij}>g_{i\ell}+c_i,\ h_{ij}>h_{kj}+c_j\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I,J\) be nonempty customer and provider type sets, with \(m=|I|\), \(n=|J|\), and \(\tau=m+n\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each mass a binary rational. Support size counts listed types, not possible types; masses \(a_i,b_j>0\) need not sum to one or have equal totals. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Inputs include nonnegative rational evaluation values \(g_{ij},h_{ij}\), acceptability thresholds \(\alpha_i^c,\beta_j^c\), rational trading scores \(p^A_{ij},p^B_{ij}\in[0,1]\), and nonnegative rational targets \(q_A,q_B\). Scores are supplied directly, with \(p^A_{ij}=0\) if \(g_{ij}<\alpha_i^c\), and \(p^B_{ij}=0\) if \(h_{ij}<\beta_j^c\).
Evaluation values have supplied representations \(g_{ij}=w_i r_j+(1-w_i)R_j\) and \(h_{ij}=z_j s_i+(1-z_j)S_i\), where all quantities are rational, \(0\le r_j\le R_j\), \(0\le s_i\le S_i\), and \(w_i,z_j\in[0,1]\).
Each type belongs to exactly one class: eager, neutral, or patient. Waiting costs \(c_i^A,c_j^B\) are \(+\infty\) for eager types, positive rationals for neutral types, and zero for patient types.
A feasible flow has real entries \(x_{ij}\ge0\), satisfies \(\sum_jx_{ij}\le a_i\), \(\sum_ix_{ij}\le b_j\), and is individually rational: \(x_{ij}>0\) requires \(g_{ij}\ge\alpha_i^c\) and \(h_{ij}\ge\beta_j^c\). Define \(u_i=a_i-\sum_jx_{ij}\) and \(v_j=b_j-\sum_ix_{ij}\).
Customer \(i\) has unmatched status \(\bot\) with mass \(u_i\), and incumbent status \(\ell\in J\) with mass \(x_{i\ell}\); provider \(j\) has unmatched status \(\bot\) with mass \(v_j\), and incumbent status \(k\in I\) with mass \(x_{kj}\).
Customer \(i\) is willing to pair with \(j\) from status \(\bot\) exactly when \(g_{ij}\ge\alpha_i^c\), and from status \(\ell\) exactly when it is non-eager and \(g_{ij}>g_{i\ell}+c_i^A\). Provider \(j\) is willing from status \(\bot\) exactly when \(h_{ij}\ge\beta_j^c\), and from status \(k\) exactly when it is non-eager and \(h_{ij}>h_{kj}+c_j^B\).
A flow is stable if no pair \(i,j\) has positive-mass statuses from which both are willing to pair. Equal evaluation values are allowed; the displayed weak acceptability and strict improvement comparisons govern ties.
Is deciding whether a stable feasible flow satisfies \(\sum_{i,j}p^A_{ij}x_{ij}\ge q_A\) and \(\sum_{i,j}p^B_{ij}x_{ij}\ge q_B\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,n,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
For discrete one-to-one matching, [Yuan et al. (2023)](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a02e/3b2cb4471105021b2fce2dae892d4e51773f.pdf) establish sufficient binary stability constraints indexed by trading classes, without a complexity classification.
[Caragiannis et al.](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/29049/1/Stable-Fractional-Matchings-AIJ.pdf) prove NP-hardness of welfare maximization for fractional matching under aggregate-utility blocking, a different stability notion. No result settling the exact decision problem above was located.

  • Masses were not normalized in the graded question; retain arbitrary positive rational totals, including unequal totals across the two sides.
  • Require individual rationality of every positive flow, reflecting the intended exclusion of unacceptable assignments in the discrete model.
  • Retain the structured evaluation-value formulas through supplied rational real values, public values, and effort levels, rather than silently allowing arbitrary evaluation matrices.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when every type is patient and every mass equals \(1\). The hardness construction satisfies the supplied evaluation representations, including \(r_j\le R_j\) and \(s_i\le S_i\).

Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,n,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Let \(E\) be the set of mutually acceptable pairs:

\[ E=\{(i,j):g_{ij}\ge\alpha_i^c,\ h_{ij}\ge\beta_j^c\}. \]

For this proof, all formulas below use the prescribed comparisons exactly.

For each \((i,j)\in E\), define the incumbent statuses from which the customer is willing:

\[ \mathcal W^A_{ij} =\{\ell\in J:i\text{ is non-eager and }g_{ij}>g_{i\ell}+c_i^A\}. \]

Define \(\mathcal W^B_{ij}\) analogously. For eager types, the corresponding set is empty, so no arithmetic with \(+\infty\) is needed.

The total willing masses are

\[ D^A_{ij}(x)=a_i-\sum_{\ell\in J}x_{i\ell} +\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal W^A_{ij}}x_{i\ell}, \]

and

\[ D^B_{ij}(x)=b_j-\sum_{k\in I}x_{kj} +\sum_{k\in\mathcal W^B_{ij}}x_{kj}. \]

These are nonnegative on every feasible flow. Because the status sets are finite and their masses are nonnegative, a pair blocks exactly when both quantities are positive. Thus stability for \((i,j)\in E\) is precisely the disjunction

\[ D^A_{ij}(x)=0\quad\text{or}\quad D^B_{ij}(x)=0. \]

Pairs outside \(E\) cannot block. Indeed, an endpoint that finds the proposed pair unacceptable rejects it when unmatched. When matched, its incumbent is acceptable by individual rationality, and a proposed value below its threshold cannot strictly exceed its incumbent value plus a nonnegative waiting cost.

A nondeterministic algorithm guesses, for every pair in \(E\), one of the two displayed equalities. It then checks feasibility of the resulting rational linear system, including capacities, individual rationality, and both score targets.

There are polynomially many variables and constraints, all with polynomial encoding length. The strict evaluation comparisons are computed before constructing the linear system; the system itself contains only non-strict inequalities and equalities. Polynomial-time rational linear-programming feasibility therefore applies. A successful branch is exactly a stable flow satisfying the targets, proving membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

2. A gadget that excludes fractional mixtures

Consider three customers \(C_1,C_2,C_3\) and three providers \(P_1,P_2,P_3\), all patient and of unit mass. Assume temporarily that every pair in this gadget is acceptable.

The following tables specify their evaluation comparisons. Later we realize them through the required evaluation formulas.

| Customer comparison values | \(P_1\) | \(P_2\) | \(P_3\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(C_1\) | \(3\) | \(1\) | \(2\) |
| \(C_2\) | \(3\) | \(5\) | \(4\) |
| \(C_3\) | \(3\) | \(9\) | \(6\) |

| Provider comparison values | \(P_1\) | \(P_2\) | \(P_3\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(C_1\) | \(6\) | \(6\) | \(6\) |
| \(C_2\) | \(12\) | \(4\) | \(8\) |
| \(C_3\) | \(10\) | \(0\) | \(5\) |

Customer comparisons are made within rows of the first table; provider comparisons are made within columns of the second.

Define two perfect matchings:

\[ A=\{(C_1,P_1),(C_2,P_2),(C_3,P_3)\}, \]

\[ B=\{(C_1,P_2),(C_2,P_3),(C_3,P_1)\}. \]

Both pure matchings are stable within the gadget

In \(A\), customers \(C_1,C_2\) receive their best providers. The only improving alternative for \(C_3\) is \(P_2\), but \(P_2\) prefers its incumbent \(C_2\) to \(C_3\), since \(4>0\). Thus \(A\) is stable.

In \(B\), the complete list of customer improvements is:

  • \(C_1\) would improve by switching to \(P_1\) or \(P_3\).
  • \(C_2\) would improve by switching to \(P_2\).
  • \(C_3\) would improve by switching to \(P_2\) or \(P_3\).

Each proposed provider prefers its incumbent:

\[ 10>6,\qquad 8>6,\qquad 6>4,\qquad 6>0,\qquad 8>5, \]

respectively. Hence \(B\) is stable.

Every nontrivial mixture is unstable

Suppose a flow saturates all six types and uses only edges in \(A\cup B\). Writing \(\theta=x_{C_1P_1}\), the row and column equations successively imply

\[ x_{C_1P_1}=x_{C_2P_2}=x_{C_3P_3}=\theta, \]

\[ x_{C_1P_2}=x_{C_2P_3}=x_{C_3P_1}=1-\theta, \]

with \(0\le\theta\le1\).

If \(0<\theta<1\), then \((C_1,P_3)\) blocks:

  • \(C_1\) has positive mass \(1-\theta\) with incumbent \(P_2\), and strictly prefers \(P_3\), since \(2>1\).
  • \(P_3\) has positive mass \(\theta\) with incumbent \(C_3\), and strictly prefers \(C_1\), since \(6>5\).

All costs are zero. Therefore such a flow is stable within the gadget if and only if \(\theta\in\{0,1\}\), corresponding to \(B\) and \(A\).

3. Realizing and isolating arbitrarily many gadgets

We now construct \(K\) gadgets that satisfy the required evaluation representations and have no acceptable pairs between different gadgets.

Write their types as \(C_{kr},P_{kj}\), where \(1\le k\le K\) and \(1\le r,j\le3\). Set

\[ \Delta=\frac1{K+1},\qquad t_k=k\Delta,\qquad \varepsilon=\frac{\Delta^2}{100}, \]

and define the affine function

\[ F_t(y)=10-4y+2ty-t^2. \]

Use coefficient pairs

\[ (\lambda_1,\mu_1)=(3,0),\quad (\lambda_2,\mu_2)=(1,4),\quad (\lambda_3,\mu_3)=(2,2), \]

and

\[ (\gamma_1,\nu_1)=(6,0),\quad (\gamma_2,\nu_2)=(4,4),\quad (\gamma_3,\nu_3)=(0,5). \]

Set \((\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3)=(2,0,1)\).

Supplied representations

Choose customer weights and provider weights as

\[ w_{C_{kr}}=t_k+\varepsilon(r-1),\qquad z_{P_{kj}}=t_k+\varepsilon\eta_j. \]

For provider \(P_{kj}\), supply

\[ R_{P_{kj}}=F_{t_k}(0)+\varepsilon^2\lambda_j-\varepsilon\mu_jt_k, \]

\[ r_{P_{kj}}=F_{t_k}(1)+\varepsilon^2\lambda_j +\varepsilon\mu_j(1-t_k). \]

For customer \(C_{kr}\), supply

\[ S_{C_{kr}}=F_{t_k}(0)+\varepsilon^2\gamma_r-\varepsilon\nu_rt_k, \]

\[ s_{C_{kr}}=F_{t_k}(1)+\varepsilon^2\gamma_r +\varepsilon\nu_r(1-t_k). \]

Define the supplied evaluation matrices from these representations exactly as in the question.

These choices satisfy all representation restrictions. First, \(0<t_k<1\) and \(2\varepsilon<\Delta\), so every \(w,z\) lies in \([0,1]\).

For either endpoint-value construction, write its coefficient pair as \((a,b)\). Here \(0\le a\le6\) and \(0\le b\le5\). The lower endpoint value is at least \(6\), because

\[ F_t(1)=6+2t-t^2\ge6. \]

The upper endpoint value is at least \(9-5\varepsilon>0\). Moreover, lower minus upper equals

\[ -4+2t+\varepsilon b<-2+5\varepsilon<0. \]

Thus \(0\le r_j\le R_j\) and \(0\le s_i\le S_i\). All evaluation values are consequently nonnegative.

Set thresholds

\[ \alpha^c_{C_{kr}}=F_{t_k}(w_{C_{kr}}),\qquad \beta^c_{P_{kj}}=F_{t_k}(z_{P_{kj}}). \]

The local comparisons are exactly those of the gadget

Within gadget \(k\), substitution gives

\[ g_{C_{kr},P_{kj}} =F_{t_k}(w_{C_{kr}}) +\varepsilon^2\bigl(\lambda_j+\mu_j(r-1)\bigr), \]

\[ h_{C_{kr},P_{kj}} =F_{t_k}(z_{P_{kj}}) +\varepsilon^2\bigl(\gamma_r+\nu_r\eta_j\bigr). \]

The parenthesized values are exactly the two tables in Section 2. The common row or column term does not affect comparisons, and \(\varepsilon^2>0\).

Every parenthesized value is nonnegative. Hence every within-gadget pair meets both thresholds. In particular, the zero entry in the provider table is acceptable by the specified weak comparison.

Every cross-gadget pair is unacceptable

The following calculation applies to either side. An observer in gadget \(k\) has parameter

\[ y=t_k+\varepsilon e,\qquad e\in\{0,1,2\}. \]

Its evaluation of an alternative in gadget \(\ell\ne k\) has the form

\[ F_{t_\ell}(y)+\varepsilon^2a+\varepsilon b(y-t_\ell), \]

where \(0\le a\le6\) and \(0\le b\le5\).

The difference between the two base terms is

\[ F_{t_\ell}(y)-F_{t_k}(y) =-(t_\ell-t_k)^2+2(t_\ell-t_k)\varepsilon e. \]

Since \(|t_\ell-t_k|\ge\Delta\) and \(|t_\ell-t_k|<1\), this is at most \(-\Delta^2+4\varepsilon\). Also \(y,t_\ell\in[0,1]\), so the remaining perturbation is at most \(6\varepsilon^2+5\varepsilon\).

Consequently, cross-gadget evaluation minus the observer’s threshold is at most

\[ -\Delta^2+9\varepsilon+6\varepsilon^2<0. \]

For the final strict inequality, use \(\varepsilon=\Delta^2/100\) and \(\Delta\le1\):

\[ 9\varepsilon+6\varepsilon^2 \le \left(\frac9{100}+\frac6{10000}\right)\Delta^2 <\Delta^2. \]

Thus both endpoints find every cross-gadget pair unacceptable.

Cross-gadget pairs also cannot block from incumbent statuses: every feasible incumbent is acceptable, whereas the proposed cross-gadget evaluation is below the endpoint’s threshold. Therefore feasibility and stability separate across gadgets.

4. Reduction from \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\)

We reduce from the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\): given positive binary integers \(d_1,\ldots,d_K\), determine whether some subset sums to half their total. This is one of the classical complete problems in [Karp’s reduction theorem](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Let

\[ T=\sum_{k=1}^K d_k,\qquad D=\max_{1\le k\le K}d_k. \]

Construct the \(K\) isolated gadgets above. Every type has mass \(1\), is patient, and has waiting cost zero.

For gadget \(k\), denote its two matchings by \(A_k,B_k\). Assign scores as follows:

  • Each edge in \(A_k\) has \(p^A=d_k/D\) and \(p^B=0\).
  • Each edge in \(B_k\) has \(p^A=0\) and \(p^B=d_k/D\).
  • Every other edge has both scores zero.

The two edge sets are disjoint. All scores lie in \([0,1]\), and nonzero scores occur only on acceptable pairs. Set

\[ q_A=q_B=\frac{3T}{2D}. \]

The targets force full flow on the six cycle edges

For a feasible flow, define

\[ Y_k=\sum_{(i,j)\in A_k\cup B_k}x_{ij}. \]

Because gadget \(k\) has three unit-mass customers, \(Y_k\le3\). Writing the two objective values as \(V_A,V_B\), the score definitions give

\[ V_A+V_B=\sum_{k=1}^K\frac{d_k}{D}Y_k \le\frac{3T}{D}. \]

If both targets are met, then also

\[ V_A+V_B\ge q_A+q_B=\frac{3T}{D}. \]

Equality must therefore hold throughout. Since every \(d_k>0\), it follows that \(Y_k=3\) for every gadget.

The cycle-edge flow alone consequently saturates all three customers and all three providers in each gadget. No mass remains for other edges or unmatched statuses. By Section 2, stability now forces each gadget to use precisely \(A_k\) or \(B_k\).

Also, equality of the objective sum and the two lower bounds implies

\[ V_A=V_B=\frac{3T}{2D}. \]

Forward implication

Suppose the constructed instance has a stable feasible flow meeting both targets. Let \(U\) be the gadgets using \(A_k\). By the preceding argument, all remaining gadgets use \(B_k\), and

\[ V_A=\frac3D\sum_{k\in U}d_k=\frac{3T}{2D}. \]

Hence

\[ \sum_{k\in U}d_k=\frac T2. \]

The original \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) instance is a yes-instance.

Reverse implication

Suppose a subset \(U\) satisfies \(\sum_{k\in U}d_k=T/2\). Use matching \(A_k\) in gadgets indexed by \(U\), and matching \(B_k\) in all other gadgets.

Each gadget matching is stable by Section 2. Cross-gadget pairs cannot block by Section 3. The resulting flow is therefore globally stable and feasible.

Its scores satisfy

\[ V_A=\frac3D\sum_{k\in U}d_k=\frac{3T}{2D}, \]

and

\[ V_B=\frac3D\sum_{k\notin U}d_k=\frac{3T}{2D}. \]

Both targets are met.

5. Encoding size and conclusion

The construction has \(m=n=3K\) and \(\tau=6K\).

All representation parameters, thresholds, and evaluation values have encoding length \(O(\log(K+1))\): they use a fixed number of rational operations on \(t_k=k/(K+1)\) and \(\varepsilon=1/(100(K+1)^2)\). The scores and targets have encoding length polynomial in the binary encoding length of the integers \(d_k\). Supplying the full evaluation and score matrices requires only \(O(K^2)\) entries.

Thus the construction is a polynomial-time many-one reduction. Together with membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), it proves that the exact decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. The proof uses the stated positive-mass status blocking rule directly.
