Partial resolution and a stronger fixed-horizon guarantee

The continuous diminishing-returns question has a complete affirmative answer. The optimal approximation threshold for unrestricted unary-encoded $h$ remains unresolved.

The results proved below are:

  • $F_h$ is monotone and continuously diminishing-returns submodular on the entire specified box, including diminishing returns in each coordinate itself.
  • For every rational $0<\zeta<1/4$, a deterministic algorithm achieves ratio $1-1/e-\zeta$ in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,h,1/\zeta$.
  • For every fixed horizon $h$, a polynomial-time algorithm achieves the strictly larger ratio

$$ c_h=1-\left(1-\frac1{2^h}\right)^{2^h}. $$
In particular, the general one-round problem has a $3/4$ approximation, with arbitrary initial colours, stubbornness flags, masses, and self-interaction.

  • The fixed-horizon algorithm does not give a uniform polynomial-time improvement for variable $h$: its running time grows with the computation-tree enumeration, and $c_h$ tends to $1-1/e$.
  • Fractional seeding can outperform whole-type seeding by an unbounded factor, so the cited discrete hardness theorem requires an additional reduction before it can establish the requested fractional hardness.

If $\alpha=1$, seeding every type completely gives objective value $1$. The arguments below include all budgets $0<\alpha\le1$.

1. An exact computation-tree representation

Write
$$ x_t=\frac{a_t}{\mu_t}, \qquad 0\le x_t\le1. $$
The budget becomes $\sum_t\mu_t x_t\le\alpha$.

First, the recurrence preserves probability distributions. If the previous-round coordinates sum to $1$, then for a non-stubborn type,
$$ \begin{aligned} p^{s+1}_{t,b}+p^{s+1}_{t,r}+p^{s+1}_{t,u} &=\pi^s_{t,b}+\pi^s_{t,r} +(p^s_{t,b}+p^s_{t,r}+p^s_{t,u})\pi^s_{t,u}\\ &=\pi^s_{t,b}+\pi^s_{t,r}+\pi^s_{t,u}\\ &=1. \end{aligned} $$
Every coordinate is nonnegative. The initial distributions have these properties, and stubborn types retain them.

1.1 Independent computation trees

For type $t$ and time $s$, define a random ordered tree $T(t,s)$:

  • If $s=0$, or $t$ is stubborn, use a single leaf labelled $t$.
  • Otherwise, choose $v$ with probability $P_{tv}$. The first subtree is an independent copy of $T(v,s-1)$, and the second is an independent copy of $T(t,s-1)$.

Independently colour every leaf labelled $t$ according to $p^0_{t,\cdot}$. At an internal vertex, return the first subtree’s colour if it is coloured; otherwise return the second subtree’s colour.

Induction shows that the returned distribution is exactly $p^s_{t,\cdot}$. Indeed, the first subtree has distribution $\pi^{s-1}_{t,\cdot}$, the second has distribution $p^{s-1}_{t,\cdot}$, and their independence gives
$$ \Pr(\text{blue}) =\pi^{s-1}_{t,b} +\pi^{s-1}_{t,u}p^{s-1}_{t,b}, $$
with the analogous red and uncoloured formulas.

This remains valid when $v=t$: the two subtrees are independent copies. Thus the construction represents the algebraic recurrence in the question, including self-interaction.

The tree has at most $2^s$ leaves. Reading its leaves in their ordered sequence, its output is the first non-uncoloured leaf’s colour, or uncoloured if there is none.

1.2 Conditioning on the neighbour choices

Fix a tree and consider its leaf sequence with the original, unseeded colours.

If the first originally coloured leaf is blue, the output remains blue under every seeding allocation. Earlier uncoloured leaves can only become blue.

If the first originally coloured leaf is red, the output is blue exactly when at least one leaf in the prefix ending at that red leaf is seeded. If all leaves were originally uncoloured, the output is blue exactly when at least one leaf is seeded.

Let $k_t$ count occurrences of type $t$ in the relevant prefix. Repeated occurrences are independently seeded in this auxiliary construction, so the conditional blue probability is
$$ 1-\prod_t(1-x_t)^{k_t}. $$

Mixing the root type with probabilities $\mu_t$ and averaging over neighbour choices yields a finite representation
$$ F_h(a)=\beta+\sum_{j=1}^N w_j \left[1-\prod_t\left(1-\frac{a_t}{\mu_t}\right)^{k_{jt}}\right], $$
where
$$ \beta=F_h(0),\qquad w_j\ge0,\qquad \beta+\sum_jw_j=1, $$
and
$$ k_{jt}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}, \qquad 1\le\sum_tk_{jt}\le K, \qquad K=2^h. $$
The representation is used analytically below; the uniform approximation algorithm will not enumerate its terms.

2. Proven: continuous diminishing returns

Consider one term
$$ G(x)=1-\prod_t(1-x_t)^{k_t}. $$
For $k_i\ge1$,
$$ \frac{\partial G}{\partial x_i} =k_i(1-x_i)^{k_i-1} \prod_{t\ne i}(1-x_t)^{k_t}\ge0. $$
For distinct $i,j$ with positive exponents,
$$ \frac{\partial^2G}{\partial x_i\partial x_j} =-k_ik_j(1-x_i)^{k_i-1}(1-x_j)^{k_j-1} \prod_{t\ne i,j}(1-x_t)^{k_t}\le0. $$
For $k_i\ge2$,
$$ \frac{\partial^2G}{\partial x_i^2} =-k_i(k_i-1)(1-x_i)^{k_i-2} \prod_{t\ne i}(1-x_t)^{k_t}\le0. $$
A derivative involving an exponent zero vanishes. The diagonal second derivative also vanishes when $k_i=1$. These are polynomial expressions, so the inequalities hold at boundary points as well.

Nonnegative weighted sums preserve these signs. Rescaling by the positive masses therefore gives
$$ \frac{\partial F_h}{\partial a_i}\ge0, \qquad \frac{\partial^2F_h}{\partial a_i\partial a_j}\le0 \quad\text{for every }i,j. $$

Consequently, every first derivative is coordinatewise nonincreasing. Explicitly, if $a\le b$ coordinatewise and both $a+d e_i$ and $b+d e_i$ belong to the box, then
$$ \begin{aligned} F_h(a+d e_i)-F_h(a) &=\int_0^d \frac{\partial F_h}{\partial a_i}(a+v e_i)\,dv\\ &\ge\int_0^d \frac{\partial F_h}{\partial a_i}(b+v e_i)\,dv\\ &=F_h(b+d e_i)-F_h(b). \end{aligned} $$
This proves continuous diminishing-returns submodularity on the entire box.

The representation also gives
$$ 0\le\frac{\partial F_h}{\partial a_i} \le\frac{K}{\mu_i}. $$
With
$$ \mu_{\min}=\min_i\mu_i, \qquad D=\frac{2^h}{\mu_{\min}}, $$
integration along a line segment in the box yields
$$ |F_h(a)-F_h(b)| \le D\sum_i|a_i-b_i|. $$

Diminishing returns does not imply joint concavity

Take two equally massive, initially uncoloured, non-stubborn types, each interacting only with the other. At $h=1$, both have blue probability
$$ x_1+x_2-x_1x_2, $$
so this is also $F_1$ in the $x$ coordinates. Its Hessian is
$$ \begin{pmatrix} 0&-1\\ -1&0 \end{pmatrix}, $$
whose eigenvalues are $1$ and $-1$. Thus joint concavity fails even in this small example.

3. New result: a ratio strictly above $1-1/e$ for every fixed horizon

Define
$$ c_K=1-\left(1-\frac1K\right)^K, \qquad K=2^h. $$

We first prove a pointwise comparison that accounts for repeated type labels.

3.1 A bounded-prefix coverage inequality

For a term with exponent vector $k$, put
$$ z=\sum_tk_tx_t. $$
Then
$$ c_K\min\{1,z\} \le1-\prod_t(1-x_t)^{k_t} \le\min\{1,z\}. $$

For the upper bound, view the expression as the probability that at least one of the independently seeded prefix leaves is seeded. The union bound gives an upper bound $z$, and every probability is at most $1$.

For the lower bound, list the $d=\sum_tk_t$ occurrences separately. Pad the list to length $K$ with variables equal to zero. The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives
$$ \prod_t(1-x_t)^{k_t} \le\left(1-\frac zK\right)^K, $$
because the $K$ nonnegative factors have sum $K-z$.

The function
$$ g_K(z)=1-\left(1-\frac zK\right)^K, \qquad 0\le z\le K, $$
is nondecreasing and concave, with $g_K(0)=0$. For $0\le z\le1$, concavity therefore gives
$$ g_K(z)\ge z g_K(1)=c_Kz. $$
For $1\le z\le K$, monotonicity gives $g_K(z)\ge c_K$. These two cases prove the lower bound.

3.2 A linear-programming approximation

Enumerate the representation from Section 1 and solve the rational linear program
$$ \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad& \beta+\sum_jw_j y_j\\ \text{subject to}\quad& 0\le x_t\le1,\qquad \sum_t\mu_t x_t\le\alpha,\\ &0\le y_j\le1,\qquad y_j\le\sum_tk_{jt}x_t. \end{aligned} $$
Its feasible region is nonempty and compact. Its coefficients are rational, so the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming supplies an optimal rational solution in time polynomial in its total binary encoding length.

Let $U$ be its optimal objective value. For any feasible $x$, the upper bound from Section 3.1 implies
$$ F_h(\mu_1x_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau x_\tau) \le \beta+\sum_jw_j\min\left\{1,\sum_tk_{jt}x_t\right\}. $$
The right-hand side is a feasible LP objective. Hence
$$ U\ge\mathrm{OPT}. $$

Let $(\widehat x,\widehat y)$ be an optimal LP solution, and output $\widehat a_t=\mu_t\widehat x_t$. It satisfies the original constraints. The lower bound from Section 3.1 gives
$$ \begin{aligned} F_h(\widehat a) &\ge\beta+ c_K\sum_jw_j\min\left\{1,\sum_tk_{jt}\widehat x_t\right\}\\ &\ge\beta+c_K\sum_jw_j\widehat y_j\\ &=c_KU+(1-c_K)\beta\\ &\ge c_K\mathrm{OPT}+(1-c_K)F_h(0). \end{aligned} $$
In particular, the multiplicative ratio is $c_K$ for the total blue mass.

3.3 Running time and the limitation for variable $h$

A complete binary tree of depth $h$ has $K-1$ internal positions. Each active position has at most $\tau$ neighbour choices. Padding unused positions by a fixed symbol shows that there are at most $\tau^{K-1}$ possible trees for one root, and hence
$$ N\le\tau^K. $$

Each tree probability is a product of a root mass and at most $K-1$ interaction probabilities. Its rational encoding length is $O(KL)$. A common denominator for all these weights can be obtained by raising the product of the input denominators to the power $K$; its encoding length is also $O(KL)$. Thus aggregating the constant term $\beta$ does not introduce uncontrolled denominator growth.

A conservative bound for construction and rational LP solution is
$$ \left[ m+\tau+L+ K\tau^{K+1}(L+h+1) \right]^{O(1)}, \qquad K=2^h, $$
where the polynomial exponent is absolute. This is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$ for every fixed $h$.

For $h=1$, there are at most $m+\tau$ root–neighbour or stubborn-root cases, and each prefix has at most two occurrences. The LP therefore has encoding length polynomial in $m,\tau,L$ and gives
$$ c_2=\frac34 $$
in time $(m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}$.

For every finite $K>1$,
$$ \log\left(1-\frac1K\right)<-\frac1K, $$
so
$$ c_K>1-\frac1e. $$
However,
$$ \lim_{K\to\infty}c_K=1-\frac1e. $$
Both the running-time growth and this vanishing advantage prevent the result from establishing a fixed improvement for unrestricted unary-encoded $h$.

The pointwise comparison itself is sharp: with one initially uncoloured, non-stubborn type and $P_{11}=1$,
$$ F_h(a)=1-(1-a)^K. $$
At $a=1/K$, the coverage expression equals $c_K$, while its capped-linear surrogate equals $1$. This is sharpness of the surrogate bound, not hardness of the optimization problem.

4. A uniform polynomial-time $1-1/e-\zeta$ approximation

For completeness, this section gives a self-contained proof of the uniform guarantee, including the corrected choice of grid size.

4.1 A lower bound for the total objective

The continuous optimum exists because its feasible region is compact and $F_h$ is continuous. Moreover,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\ge\alpha. $$

To prove this, choose $a_t=\alpha\mu_t$. Every initial blue probability is at least $\alpha$. If every blue probability at some round is at least $\alpha$, row stochasticity gives $\pi^s_{t,b}\ge\alpha$. The blue update is at least $\pi^s_{t,b}$, and stubborn types retain their initial blue probabilities. Induction therefore gives $F_h(a)\ge\alpha$.

This lower bound will turn additive numerical losses into multiplicative losses.

4.2 A grid of polynomial encoding length

Fix a rational $0<\zeta<1/4$, and set
$$ s=\left\lceil\frac8\zeta\right\rceil, \qquad \eta=\frac1s. $$
Let $R$ be the least power of two satisfying
$$ R\ge\frac{\tau D}{\eta}. $$
Set
$$ \delta=\frac{\alpha}{R}, \qquad c_t=\min\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{\mu_t}{\delta}\right\rfloor,R\right\}. $$

Consider the integer-grid objective
$$ G(y)=F_h(\delta y), \qquad 0\le y_t\le c_t,\qquad \sum_ty_t\le R. $$
It is monotone and has integer diminishing returns by Section 2.

Let $M$ be its optimum. Rounding a continuous optimum down coordinatewise to this grid preserves feasibility. Each coordinate loses less than $\delta$, so the sensitivity bound gives
$$ M\ge\mathrm{OPT}-\tau D\delta \ge\mathrm{OPT}-\eta\alpha. $$

The least-power choice gives
$$ R<\frac{2\tau D}{\eta}, $$
and consequently
$$ \log_2R =O\left(L+h+\log\tau+\log(1/\zeta)\right). $$
The number $R$ may be exponentially large, but its encoding length is polynomial.

4.3 Batched threshold greedy with an approximate oracle

A one-unit grid gain is at most
$$ U=\delta D. $$
Use decreasing rational thresholds starting at $\theta_0=U$, with each consecutive ratio at least $1-\eta$. Stop at the first threshold $\theta_J$ satisfying
$$ \theta_J\le\frac{\eta\alpha}{4R}. $$

Such a sequence with short encodings is obtained by dividing each interval from $U/2^j$ to $U/2^{j+1}$ into $s$ equal parts. It has
$$ O\left(\eta^{-1}\log(D/\eta)\right) $$
thresholds.

Start with $y=0$. At each threshold $\theta$, process each coordinate $t$. Define
$$ K_t=\min\left\{c_t-y_t,\ R-\sum_i y_i\right\}. $$
The true unit gains
$$ d_t(k)=G(y+k e_t)-G(y+(k-1)e_t), \qquad 1\le k\le K_t, $$
are nonincreasing in $k$.

Evaluate each objective value with absolute error at most
$$ v=\frac{\eta\theta_J}{16}. $$
The estimated unit gains then have error at most
$$ \gamma=2v=\frac{\eta\theta_J}{8}. $$

Binary-search the batch length using sentinel endpoints $0$ and $K_t+1$: accept a queried index when its estimated gain is at least $\theta$, and otherwise reject it. At termination, let $k$ be the accepted lower endpoint and add $k e_t$ to $y$.

If $k>0$, its acceptance certifies $d_t(k)\ge\theta-\gamma$. True diminishing returns then gives this lower bound for every unit in the batch. If $k<K_t$, rejection of $k+1$ certifies
$$ d_t(k+1)\le\theta+\gamma. $$
These certificates do not require the numerical estimates themselves to be monotone.

4.4 Approximation analysis

During a pass at threshold $\theta$, every coordinate with remaining capacity has current one-unit gain at most
$$ B_\theta=\frac{\theta}{1-\eta}+\gamma. $$
For the first pass, this follows from the bound $U$. For later passes, it follows from the previous threshold’s rejection certificate and diminishing returns. A coordinate already at capacity needs no such bound.

Let $z$ be a grid optimum. The coordinatewise maximum $y\vee z$ remains inside the box, although it can exceed the budget. Monotonicity and diminishing returns give
$$ \begin{aligned} M-G(y) &\le G(y\vee z)-G(y)\\ &\le\sum_t(z_t-y_t)_+ \bigl(G(y+e_t)-G(y)\bigr)\\ &\le R B_\theta. \end{aligned} $$
Only coordinates with remaining capacity contribute, and
$$ \sum_t(z_t-y_t)_+\le\sum_tz_t\le R. $$

Every accepted batch has average gain at least $\theta-\gamma$. Since $\gamma/\theta\le\eta/8$,
$$ \frac{\theta-\gamma}{B_\theta} \ge \frac{1-\eta/8}{1/(1-\eta)+\eta/8} \ge1-2\eta. $$
For the final inequality, multiplying numerator and denominator by $1-\eta$ gives the lower bound
$$ (1-\eta)\frac{1-\eta/8}{1+\eta/8} \ge(1-\eta)(1-\eta/4) \ge1-2\eta. $$

Put $\kappa=1-2\eta$. A batch of length $k$ therefore satisfies
$$ G(y+k e_t)-G(y) \ge\frac{\kappa k}{R}\bigl(M-G(y)\bigr). $$

If the algorithm fills the budget, the batch lengths sum to $R$. Thus
$$ \begin{aligned} M-G(y_{\mathrm{out}}) &\le\bigl(M-G(0)\bigr) \prod_{\text{batches}}\left(1-\frac{\kappa k}{R}\right)\\ &\le M e^{-\kappa}. \end{aligned} $$
Here $G(0)\ge0$ is sufficient; the proof concerns the total objective.

If it finishes all thresholds without filling the budget, every coordinate with remaining capacity has marginal at most $\theta_J+\gamma$. The same residual comparison yields
$$ M-G(y_{\mathrm{out}}) \le R(\theta_J+\gamma) \le\frac{\eta\alpha}{3}. $$

In either case,
$$ G(y_{\mathrm{out}}) \ge(1-e^{-\kappa})M-\frac{\eta\alpha}{3}. $$
Because $2\eta\le1$,
$$ e^{-1+2\eta}-e^{-1} =\int_0^{2\eta}e^{-1+t}\,dt \le2\eta. $$
Hence $1-e^{-\kappa}\ge1-1/e-2\eta$. Combining this with
$$ M\ge\mathrm{OPT}-\eta\alpha, \qquad \alpha\le\mathrm{OPT}, $$
gives
$$ G(y_{\mathrm{out}}) \ge(1-1/e-4\eta)\mathrm{OPT} \ge(1-1/e-\zeta)\mathrm{OPT}. $$

4.5 Numerical evaluation and running time

Exact rational iteration can require exponentially many bits: the one-type example from Section 3 has
$$ F_h(1/2)=1-2^{-2^h}. $$

Instead, store all probabilities on a dyadic grid of precision $q$, rounding after each update and clipping to $[0,1]$. Compute interaction sums exactly from the stored values.

If the maximum stored-coordinate error is $E$, row stochasticity bounds the interaction-coordinate error by $E$. For two factors in $[0,1]$, the product error is at most $2E$. Thus the blue and red updates have error at most $3E$ before rounding, and the uncoloured update has error at most $2E$. Clipping cannot increase error relative to a true value in $[0,1]$.

Including initial rounding, the final objective error is at most
$$ 3^{h+1}2^{-q}. $$
It suffices to choose
$$ q=O\left(h+\log(1/v)\right). $$

For an explicit conservative bit bound, define
$$ B= 1+L+h+ \left\lceil\log_2(\tau/\zeta)\right\rceil +\operatorname{bits}(\zeta). $$
The grid coordinates, thresholds, and required numerical precision have encoding length $O(B)$.

There are
$$ O\left(\frac{\tau B^2}{\zeta}\right) $$
objective evaluations: the number of thresholds is $O(B/\zeta)$, and each coordinate search takes $O(\log R)=O(B)$ queries.

Using common denominators for input rows, each evaluation takes $O(h(m+\tau))$ arithmetic operations on $O(B)$-bit numbers. Elementary integer arithmetic therefore gives the conservative total bit running time
$$ O\left( \frac{h(m+\tau)\tau B^4}{\zeta} \right). $$
In particular, the algorithm is polynomial in the support representation and never expands masses into individual agents.

5. Why the discrete hardness does not yet settle this problem

The cited [discrete adoption-maximization theorem](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0442.pdf) excludes fixed improvements over $1-1/e$ for its seed-set problem under the stated complexity assumption. The following example proves that a transfer based on rounding fractional allocations to fully seeded types cannot preserve objective values within a constant factor.

Let $\ell\ge2$ be a power of two. Introduce stubborn source types $s_1,\ldots,s_\ell$ and non-stubborn follower types $o_1,\ldots,o_\ell$. Every type is initially uncoloured and has mass
$$ \mu_t=\frac1{2\ell}. $$
Each follower interacts only with its corresponding source. Give stubborn rows self-loops. Set
$$ \alpha=\frac1{2\ell}, \qquad h=\ell^2. $$

5.1 Whole-type optimum

The budget permits fully seeding at most one type. Seeding a source makes that source and its follower blue, giving value $1/\ell$. Seeding a follower affects only itself, giving $1/(2\ell)$. Therefore
$$ \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{whole}}=\frac1\ell. $$

5.2 A fractional allocation

Seed each source to fraction $1/\ell$ and seed no follower. Its cost is exactly $\alpha$.

Each source retains blue probability $1/\ell$. A follower’s uncoloured probability satisfies
$$ p^{s+1}_{o_i,u} =p^s_{o_i,u}(1-1/\ell), \qquad p^0_{o_i,u}=1. $$
There are no red probabilities, so
$$ F_h(a) =\frac1{2\ell} +\frac12\left[1-(1-1/\ell)^{\ell^2}\right]. $$
Using $(1-1/\ell)^{\ell^2}\le e^{-\ell}$ gives
$$ \frac{F_h(a)}{\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{whole}}} \ge \frac12+\frac{\ell}{2}(1-e^{-\ell}), $$
which tends to infinity.

All masses are positive dyadic rationals, every type is seedable, and the unary horizon is polynomial in the support size. Thus the example satisfies the stated restrictions.

This proves an unbounded fractional-versus-whole-type gap. It does not exclude a different reduction establishing the requested fractional hardness.

6. What is settled and what remains open

The continuous diminishing-returns question is settled: yes, on the entire box and with all the features allowed in the question.

The new fixed-horizon result shows that $1-1/e$ can be exceeded when the horizon is bounded by a fixed constant. Its general one-round guarantee is $3/4$.

For unrestricted unary-encoded horizons, the proved uniform guarantee remains $1-1/e-\zeta$ for every positive accuracy parameter $\zeta$. No argument above establishes a fixed improvement, and no argument establishes the requested conditional exclusion. The optimal uniform approximation threshold therefore remains open in this write-up.
