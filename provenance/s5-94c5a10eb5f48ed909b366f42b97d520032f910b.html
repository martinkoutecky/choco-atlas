The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given positive rational type masses \(\mu_i\) summing to \(1\), a rational row-stochastic contact matrix \(P\) reversible with respect to \(\mu\), and initial blue fractions \(x_i^0\in[0,1]\), suppose the nonconstant spectral norm of \(P\) in \(L^2(\mu)\) is at most a fixed \(r_0<1/4\) and \(\sum_i\mu_i x_i^0\le 1/2-2r_0\). At each round let \(q_i^t=\sum_j P_{ij}x_j^t\), set \(x_i^{t+1}=1\) when \(q_i^t>1/2\), set it to \(0\) when \(q_i^t<1/2\), and retain \(x_i^t\) when \(q_i^t=1/2\). For rational \(\varepsilon>0\), compute the least \(t\) such that \(\sum_i\mu_i x_i^t\le\varepsilon\), together with \(x^t\).

Input consists of this society, a nonnegative rational row-stochastic matrix \(P\in\mathbb{Q}^{\tau\times\tau}\) satisfying \(\mu_iP_{ij}=\mu_jP_{ji}\), rational initial blue fractions \(x_i^0\in[0,1]\), and rational \(\varepsilon>0\). Diagonal entries of \(P\) are allowed.
Write \(\|f\|_{2,\mu}^2=\sum_i\mu_i f_i^2\). Inputs are promised to satisfy \(\|Pf\|_{2,\mu}\leq r_0\|f\|_{2,\mu}\) whenever \(\sum_i\mu_i f_i=0\), and \(\sum_i\mu_i x_i^0\leq1/2-2r_0\).

Result

The promised computation is solvable in polynomial time. For fixed \(r_0\) and \(m=2\), an exact algorithm uses \(O(\tau^2L^3)\) bit operations, equivalently \(O(m\tau^2L^3)\) on the stated domain.

When \(r_0>0\), the total blue mass decreases by at least a factor of four every round. The boundary case \(r_0=0\) is also directly solvable, but can have a stationary state of blue mass exactly \(1/2\).

1. Stationarity and a one-round estimate

Write \(\mathbf 1\) for the all-ones vector and define the blue mass of a state \(x\in[0,1]^\tau\) by \(b=\sum_i\mu_i x_i\).

Detailed balance and row-stochasticity imply stationarity:

\[ \sum_i\mu_iP_{ij} =\mu_j\sum_iP_{ji} =\mu_j. \]

Consequently, \(P\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1\) and \(\sum_i\mu_i(Px)_i=b\).

Let \(f=x-b\mathbf 1\). Its weighted mean is zero, so the promised contraction gives

\[ \sum_i\mu_i\bigl((Px)_i-b\bigr)^2 =\|Pf\|_{2,\mu}^2 \leq r_0^2\|f\|_{2,\mu}^2. \]

Since \(0\leq x_i\leq1\), we have \(x_i^2\leq x_i\). Hence

\[ \|f\|_{2,\mu}^2 =\sum_i\mu_i x_i^2-b^2 \leq b(1-b). \]

Let \(y\) be the state obtained from \(x\) after one update, and let \(S=\{i:(Px)_i\geq1/2\}\). Every coordinate outside \(S\) becomes zero, while every coordinate inside \(S\) remains at most one. This includes ties, even when a tied coordinate retains a fractional value. Therefore

\[ b':=\sum_i\mu_i y_i\leq\sum_{i\in S}\mu_i. \]

If \(b<1/2\), every \(i\in S\) satisfies \((Px)_i-b\geq1/2-b\). Thus

\[ (1/2-b)^2\sum_{i\in S}\mu_i \leq\sum_i\mu_i\bigl((Px)_i-b\bigr)^2 \leq r_0^2b(1-b). \]

Combining these inequalities proves the one-round bound

\[ b'\leq\frac{r_0^2b(1-b)}{(1/2-b)^2}. \]

2. Geometric decay when \(r_0>0\)

Suppose \(r_0>0\) and \(b\leq1/2-2r_0\). Then \(1/2-b\geq2r_0\), so the preceding bound yields

\[ b'\leq\frac{b(1-b)}4\leq\frac b4. \]

In particular, \(b'\leq b\leq1/2-2r_0\), so the hypothesis needed for this estimate persists.

Writing \(b_t=\sum_i\mu_i x_i^t\), induction from the initial promise now gives

\[ b_t\leq4^{-t}b_0\leq\frac12\,4^{-t} \qquad\text{for every }t\geq0. \]

This proof uses neither equal masses nor binary initial states.

Under the standard binary numerator-and-denominator encoding, every positive input rational satisfies \(\varepsilon\geq2^{-L}\): its numerator is at least one and its denominator has at most \(L\) bits. Therefore

\[ b_L\leq\frac12\,4^{-L}<2^{-L}\leq\varepsilon. \]

Thus a qualifying round always exists and its least value is at most \(L\). Simulating rounds in increasing order and checking their masses returns the least qualifying round.

3. The boundary case \(r_0=0\)

The promise now says \(Pf=0\) for every weighted-mean-zero vector \(f\). Because all masses are positive, zero weighted norm implies that every coordinate is zero.

Applying this to \(f=x-b\mathbf 1\) shows that \(Px=b\mathbf 1\) for every state \(x\). The initial promise gives \(b_0\leq1/2\).

There are three possibilities:

  • If \(b_0\leq\varepsilon\), return \(t=0\) and \(x^0\).
  • If \(\varepsilon<b_0<1/2\), every coordinate becomes zero in the first round. Return \(t=1\) and the zero vector.
  • If \(\varepsilon<b_0=1/2\), every coordinate ties in the first round and retains its initial value. The same happens thereafter, so report that no qualifying round exists.

This completely handles the permitted degenerate boundary case.

4. Exact arithmetic and running time

It remains to ensure that exact simulation has polynomial bit complexity.

Each coordinate always belongs to the set \(\{0,1,x_i^0\}\), because an update either assigns zero, assigns one, or retains the previous value. Thus the dynamics never generates new fractional coordinate values.

For an explicit implementation, let \(D\) be the product of all denominators appearing in the input. The sum of their binary lengths is at most \(L\), so \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits. Express the input as

\[ \mu_i=\frac{u_i}{D},\qquad P_{ij}=\frac{v_{ij}}{D},\qquad x_i^0=\frac{a_i}{D},\qquad \varepsilon=\frac eD. \]

Maintain \(x_i^t=z_i^t/D\), where \(z_i^t\in\{0,D,a_i\}\). Then

\[ q_i^t=\frac{\sum_jv_{ij}z_j^t}{D^2}. \]

The update is determined exactly by comparing \(2\sum_jv_{ij}z_j^t\) with \(D^2\). Equality retains \(z_i^t\); the other comparisons assign \(D\) or zero. Use a separate next-state vector to perform the updates simultaneously.

Similarly, the target condition is tested exactly as

\[ \sum_i\mu_i x_i^t\leq\varepsilon \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_i u_i z_i^t\leq eD. \]

All these integers have \(O(L)\) bits. Indeed, \(u_i,v_{ij},z_i^t\leq D\), and nonnegativity and stochasticity bound each opinion numerator by \(D^2\); the mass numerator is likewise at most \(D^2\). The scaled numerator \(e\) also has \(O(L)\) bits.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, each multiplication or division of these integers costs \(O(L^2)\) bit operations. Forming the common-denominator representation costs \(O(\tau^2L^2)\). Each simulated round costs \(O(\tau^2L^2)\), including its mass test.

For \(r_0>0\), at most \(L\) updates are needed. For \(r_0=0\), only the direct checks above are needed. The total running time is therefore \(O(\tau^2L^3)\). The returned vector is exact; its coordinates can be output using zero, one, or their original input fractions.

No spectral verification is required: the algorithm is analyzed on the promised inputs.
