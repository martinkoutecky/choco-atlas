The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational bounded-degree planar role graph \(H\) with \(\tau\) roles, at most four goods, and the normalized linear-influence parameters and endowments \(w_t\) of each role, form a continuum of copies of \(H\), with mass \(1/\tau\) on each role. Each role has per-agent endowment \(\tau w_t\); in its utility, replace each neighbor-allocation coefficient \(a_{t,i,s,j}\) by \(a_{t,i,s,j}/\tau\). Find a normalized common price vector \(p\) and one bundle \(y_t\) per role such that each role’s budget violation and optimality regret are at most \(\epsilon=\tau^{-16}\), and mass-weighted market-clearing error is at most \(\epsilon\).

Here \(1\le m\le4\), and the types are roles of a directed graph \(H\) whose underlying undirected graph is planar and has degree bounded by a fixed constant. Require \(\max_t c_{t,i}>0\) for every good, and strong connectivity of the economy graph with edge \(s\to t\) whenever some \(i\) satisfies \(w_{s,i}>0\) and \(c_{t,i}>0\).

4. Consequence for $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness

Here is the reduction convention under which the obstruction holds.

A promise-preserving SAT search reduction consists of polynomial-time functions $f,g$ such that:

  • For every Boolean formula $\varphi$, including unsatisfiable formulas, $f(\varphi)$ is a valid instance of the economy problem.
  • If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, then for every acceptable output $s$ of $f(\varphi)$, the value $g(\varphi,s)$ is a satisfying assignment.

This is the usual total-search obstruction, specialized below to the present relation. [Megiddo–Papadimitriou](https://theory.stanford.edu/~megiddo/pdf/papadimX.pdf).

Proposition. Such a reduction implies $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$.

Proof. Consider the following certificate for unsatisfiability of $\varphi$:

  • A polynomial-size acceptable output $s$ for $f(\varphi)$.
  • The condition that $g(\varphi,s)$ is not a satisfying assignment of $\varphi$.

The verifier computes $f(\varphi)$, checks that $s$ is acceptable using Section 1, computes $g(\varphi,s)$, and checks its failure to satisfy $\varphi$. All these operations take polynomial time.

If $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable, Section 3 supplies a polynomial-size acceptable $s$, and no decoder output can satisfy $\varphi$. Thus a certificate exists.

If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, the reduction guarantees that every acceptable $s$ decodes to a satisfying assignment. Thus no certificate exists.

Hence UNSAT belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$. Since UNSAT is $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete, $\mathrm{coNP}\subseteq\mathrm{NP}$. Taking complements gives the reverse inclusion, so $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$. $\square$

The same proof extends to polynomial-time oracle search reductions that make promised-valid queries along every valid answer history: guess and verify the entire polynomial-size answer transcript.

The promise requirement matters. If a proposed reduction only guarantees that its target instance is valid when $\varphi$ is satisfiable, the preceding certificate argument does not apply.

5.3 Decoding every acceptable output

Let $(\widehat p,y)$ be any acceptable target output, and put
$$ \widehat x_r=\frac{y_r}{\tau}, \qquad \epsilon=\tau^{-16}. $$
By Section 1, its transformed finite budget and utility errors are at most $\epsilon/\tau$, and its clearing errors are at most $\epsilon$.

Define
$$ D_0=\sum_i\lambda_i\widehat p_i, \qquad p_i^0=\frac{\lambda_i\widehat p_i}{D_0}. $$
These prices are normalized, and $D_0\ge\lambda_-$.

For unsplit traders, decode
$$ x_{t,i}^0=\frac{\widehat x_{t,i}}{\lambda_i}. $$
For $T$, decode
$$ x_{T,i}^0 = \frac1{\lambda_i}\sum_{\ell=1}^q\widehat x_{T_\ell,i}. $$

Budgets. An unsplit trader’s decoded budget error is at most
$$ \frac{\epsilon}{\tau D_0}. $$
Summing the replacement budgets gives error at most $q\epsilon/(\tau D_0)$ for $T$. Since $q\le\tau$, every decoded budget error is at most $\epsilon/\lambda_-$.

Utility for unsplit traders. The goods transformation maps feasible deviations exactly:
$$ \widehat p\cdot\widehat v=D_0\,p^0\cdot v, \qquad \widehat p\cdot\widehat w_t=D_0\,p^0\cdot w_t^0. $$
Because $T$ has no outgoing influence, aggregating its replacements changes no other utility. The utility scaling identity therefore bounds decoded regret by $K_0\epsilon/\tau$.

Utility for $T$. Let
$$ \widehat\rho_T = \max_i\frac{\widehat c_{T,i}}{\widehat p_i}. $$
Each replacement satisfies
$$ (\widehat p\cdot\widehat w_{T_\ell})\widehat\rho_T \le \widehat c_T\cdot\widehat x_{T_\ell} +\frac{\epsilon}{\tau}. $$
Summing these inequalities proves that the aggregate transformed bundle has regret at most $q\epsilon/\tau$. Undoing utility scaling gives decoded regret at most
$$ K_0q\epsilon/\tau\le K_0\epsilon. $$

Clearing. Aggregation preserves total transformed allocations, so
$$ \left|\sum_t x_{t,i}^0-W_i^0\right| \le\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda_i} \le\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda_-}. $$

All decoded errors are therefore at most $C_0\epsilon$, and
$$ C_0\epsilon = C_0\tau^{-16} \le C_0R^{-16}N^{-16} \le N^{-16}. $$

Every acceptable target output thus decodes to a valid source output, without tie-breaking. This proves $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hardness of the stated continuum search problem.
