The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite complete agent types \(T\) with masses \(\mu_t\), entitlement intensities \(\alpha_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t=1\), additive per-unit values \(v_t(e)\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_e\) of item classes \(E\), choose a mass \(y_{t,b}\) of type-\(t\) agents receiving each integral bundle \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{E}\), subject to \(\sum_b y_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}b_e=\rho_e\). With \(P_t=\alpha_t\sum_e\rho_ev_t(e)\), minimize \(\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}(P_t-v_t(b))^+\), and determine exact, approximation, or parameterized algorithms and whether the optimum is always at most \(\frac14\max_{t,e}|v_t(e)|\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) complete types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) item classes \(E\). Each type specifies binary-rational entitlement intensity \(\alpha_t>0\) and additive values \(v_t(e)\in\mathbb{Q}\), allowing positive, negative and zero values; \(\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t=1\). Supplies \(\rho_e\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\) are per capita.
An allocation consists of nonnegative real masses \(y_{t,b}\) over all integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}^{E}\), satisfying \(\sum_b y_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}b_e=\rho_e\) for every \(e\); empty bundles and arbitrarily large finite bundles are allowed.
Set \(v_t(b)=\sum_e b_ev_t(e)\), \(P_t=\alpha_t\sum_e\rho_ev_t(e)\), and \(R=\max_{t,e}|v_t(e)|\). Nonnegative monetary subsidies enter utility additively, and the proportional benchmark excludes subsidy payments.
The total subsidy is \(S(y)=\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}\max\{P_t-v_t(b),0\}\). The decision input additionally contains a binary-rational budget \(B\geq0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is deciding whether a feasible allocation satisfies \(S(y)\leq B\) in \(\mathsf{P}\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard?
Does every instance admit a feasible allocation with \(S(y)\leq R/4\)?

For finite societies with \(n\) agents, a weighted proportional outcome with total subsidy at most \(\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor R/n\) can be computed in polynomial time for each fixed \(n\); this is a subsidy guarantee, not exact minimization. [Garg, Sharma and Wu](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15208v1).
Searches located no hardness classification for that guaranteed-bound computation problem and no result settling precisely the mass model defined above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized society mass to one so that supplies and total subsidy are per capita.
  • Required positive binary-rational entitlement intensities, arbitrary signed binary-rational values, and nonnegative binary-rational supplies.
  • Made complete types include both valuations and entitlement intensity.
  • Retained the unrestricted integral-bundle domain and allowed countably supported nonnegative real allocation masses; exact supply constraints ensure finite first moments.
  • Expressed exact subsidy minimization through existence under a rational budget, without assuming that an infimum is attained.
  • Used the proportional benchmark excluding total subsidy, with nonnegative subsidies entering utility additively.

1. Results

The decision problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard, already when \(\tau=1\), all values are positive integers, every supply equals \(\frac12\), and \(B=0\). Consequently, a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).

Every instance admits a feasible allocation with \(S(y)\leq R/4\). This bound is sharp, even for \(\tau=m=1\).

2. Zero subsidy for a single type

When \(\tau=1\), the normalizations force \(\mu_1=\alpha_1=1\). Write \(v,P,y_b\) for this type’s values, benchmark, and allocation masses.

For every feasible allocation,
\( \sum_b y_bv(b)=\sum_e\rho_ev(e)=P. \)
This identity is valid even for allocations with infinite support: indeed,
\( \sum_b y_b|v(b)|\leq\sum_e|v(e)|\sum_b y_bb_e=\sum_e|v(e)|\rho_e<\infty. \)

Lemma. A feasible allocation has zero subsidy if and only if every bundle with positive mass has value exactly \(P\).

Proof. If \(S(y)=0\), each nonnegative summand \(y_b\max\{P-v(b),0\}\) vanishes. Thus \(v(b)\geq P\) whenever \(y_b>0\). The preceding expectation identity gives
\( \sum_b y_b(v(b)-P)=0. \)
All summands on positive-mass bundles are nonnegative, so each must vanish. Hence \(v(b)=P\) on every such bundle. The converse follows directly from the subsidy formula. \(\square\)

3. Polynomial reduction proving hardness

We reduce from PARTITION: given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\), decide whether some subset sums to half their total. We may assume the total is even, because multiplying every input integer by \(2\) preserves the answer and ensures this condition.

Put
\( T=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^k a_i,\qquad A=\max_i a_i,\qquad D=kA+k+2,\qquad H=D^{k+1}. \)
In particular,
\( T<D,\qquad kA<D,\qquad k<D. \)

Construction

Create one type, with \(\mu_1=\alpha_1=1\), and \(2k\) item classes \(e_i^+,e_i^-\). Define
\( v(e_i^+)=H+D^i+a_i,\qquad v(e_i^-)=H+D^i. \)
Give every class supply \(\frac12\), and set \(B=0\).

All values are positive integers, and the benchmark is
\( P=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^k\bigl(v(e_i^+)+v(e_i^-)\bigr) =kH+\sum_{i=1}^kD^i+T. \)

Exact-value bundles encode partitions

Consider an integral bundle, writing \(x_i=b_{e_i^+}\) and \(z_i=b_{e_i^-}\). We prove that \(v(b)=P\) holds precisely when
\( x_i+z_i=1\ \text{for every }i,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^k a_ix_i=T. \)

Let \(h=\sum_i(x_i+z_i)\). Because \(T<D\), the number
\( Q=\sum_{i=1}^kD^i+T \)
has valid base-\(D\) digits in positions \(0,\ldots,k\), and therefore \(0\leq Q<H\). Consequently,
\( P=kH+Q<(k+1)H. \)

Every item has value at least \(H\). Thus an exact-value bundle satisfies \(hH\leq P<(k+1)H\), which implies \(h\leq k\).

Its value can now be written as
\( v(b)=hH+\sum_{i=1}^k(x_i+z_i)D^i+\sum_{i=1}^ka_ix_i. \)
The lower coefficients satisfy
\( 0\leq x_i+z_i\leq h\leq k<D,\qquad 0\leq\sum_i a_ix_i\leq Ah\leq kA<D. \)
Hence none of these coefficients produces a carry in base \(D\).

Uniqueness of base-\(D\) representation, applied to \(v(b)=P\), now gives
\( h=k,\qquad x_i+z_i=1\ \text{for every }i,\qquad \sum_i a_ix_i=T. \)
Since \(x_i,z_i\) are nonnegative integers, each pair is either \((1,0)\) or \((0,1)\). Thus the indices with \(x_i=1\) form a partition witness.

Conversely, choosing \((x_i,z_i)=(1,0)\) on a partition witness and \((0,1)\) elsewhere gives a bundle of value \(P\).

Equivalence of the decision instances

Suppose the constructed society admits an allocation with \(S(y)\leq B=0\). By the lemma, every positive-mass bundle has value \(P\). At least one such bundle exists because the masses sum to \(1\). The preceding calculation extracts a partition witness.

Conversely, suppose a partition witness exists, and let \(b\) be its encoded bundle. Define its coordinatewise complement by \(\bar b_e=1-b_e\). Both bundles are integral and nonnegative. Moreover,
\( v(\bar b)=\sum_ev(e)-v(b)=2P-P=P. \)
Assign mass \(\frac12\) to each of \(b,\bar b\). Every class then has total consumption \(\frac12\), so the allocation is feasible, and both bundles require zero subsidy.

This proves the required equivalence.

Encoding length and running time

Each constructed value has
\( O\bigl(k\log D\bigr)=O\bigl(k(\log k+\log A)\bigr) \)
bits. The constructed input has \(m=2k\), \(\tau=1\), and total length
\( L=O\bigl(k^2(\log k+\log A)\bigr). \)
The construction uses integer arithmetic of polynomial bit complexity and therefore runs in polynomial time in the source input length.

Thus the mass model’s exact decision problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard, even with fixed support size \(\tau=1\).

4. Every instance admits subsidy at most \(R/4\)

We apply the finite-society theorem stated in the task: for \(n\) agents with additive signed values and positive weights summing to \(1\), an allocation exists whose total subsidy is at most
\( \frac{\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor}{n}R. \)
Its benchmark excludes subsidy payments, as required here. This is [Garg, Sharma and Wu, Theorem 4.20](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15208v1#S4.Thmtheorem20).

Choose a positive integer \(N\) that clears the denominators of all \(\mu_t\) and \(\rho_e\). Such an \(N\) exists because these numbers are rational.

Construct a finite society containing:

  • \(N\mu_t\) agents of type \(t\), each with weight \(\alpha_t/N\);
  • \(N\rho_e\) distinct copies of item class \(e\).

There are exactly \(N\) agents, and their weights are positive and satisfy
\( \sum_t(N\mu_t)\frac{\alpha_t}{N} =\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t=1. \)
Each item copy has its class’s original value, so the value range remains \(R\).

The finite proportional benchmark of every type-\(t\) agent equals
\( \frac{\alpha_t}{N}\sum_e(N\rho_e)v_t(e) =\alpha_t\sum_e\rho_ev_t(e)=P_t. \)
Thus all hypotheses and benchmark conventions of the finite theorem match.

Apply that theorem. For each type \(t\) and integral class-count bundle \(b\), let \(c_{t,b}\) count the agents receiving \(b\), and set
\( y_{t,b}=c_{t,b}/N. \)
Then
\( \sum_b y_{t,b}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}b_e=\rho_e. \)
Hence \(y\) is feasible in the mass model.

The minimal subsidy needed by each finite agent is exactly \(\max\{P_t-v_t(b),0\}\). Dividing the finite total subsidy by \(N\) therefore yields
\( S(y)\leq\frac1N\frac{\lfloor N^2/4\rfloor}{N}R =\frac{\lfloor N^2/4\rfloor}{N^2}R \leq\frac R4. \)

This produces an actual allocation with finite support; no limiting or attainment argument is needed. If every supply is zero, assigning empty bundles directly gives \(S(y)=0\).

A construction-time bound

Clearing denominators takes \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time. We can choose \(N\leq2^{O(L)}\), and the expanded number of items
\( M=N\sum_e\rho_e \)
also satisfies \(M\leq2^{O(L)}\).

An explicit, albeit slow, construction enumerates all assignments of these \(M\) distinct items to the \(N\) agents and selects one meeting the guaranteed bound. Its running time is
\( \max\{2,N\}^{M}\operatorname{poly}(N,M,m,\tau,L) \leq2^{\,2^{O(L)}}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). \)
The theorem ensures this search succeeds. This running-time bound makes no claim that guaranteed-bound computation requires such time.

5. Sharpness

Take one type and one class, with
\( \mu_1=\alpha_1=1,\qquad v_1(e)=R>0,\qquad \rho_e=\frac12. \)
The benchmark is \(P_1=R/2\).

Only the empty bundle requires subsidy: its deficit is \(R/2\), whereas every nonempty integral bundle has value at least \(R\). Let \(y_0\) denote the empty-bundle mass. Feasibility implies
\( \frac12=\sum_{b\geq1}by_b\geq\sum_{b\geq1}y_b=1-y_0. \)
Therefore \(y_0\geq\frac12\), and
\( S(y)=\frac R2y_0\geq\frac R4. \)
Assigning mass \(\frac12\) to the empty bundle and mass \(\frac12\) to the one-item bundle attains equality.

Thus \(R/4\) is the exact universal subsidy bound.
