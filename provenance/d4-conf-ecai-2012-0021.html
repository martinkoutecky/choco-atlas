Proposition 1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\) of complete rankings over options and rational comparative constraints \(V\) on type shares, decide whether every positive distribution \(\mu\) satisfying \(V\) has \(\mu(I)>\mu(J)\), where \(I\) contains types ranking \(a\) above \(b\) and \(J\) contains types ranking \(b\) above \(a\). Return a violating distribution when the implication fails.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(O\) contain \(m\geq 2\) options, let \(a,b\in O\) be distinct, and let \(T\neq\varnothing\) be an explicitly listed set of strict complete rankings of \(O\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; the input specifies \(T\) and constraints on its unknown shares.
For \(E\subseteq T\), write \(\mu(E)=\sum_{t\in E}\mu_t\). A positive distribution satisfies \(\mu_t>0\) for every \(t\in T\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\).
The input \(V\) consists of strict comparative constraints \(\mu(E)>\mu(F)\), with explicitly listed disjoint \(E,F\subseteq T\); their coefficients are rational. Promise that some positive distribution satisfies \(V\).
Set \(I=\{t\in T:a\text{ ranks above }b\}\) and \(J=T\setminus I\). Decide whether every positive real distribution satisfying \(V\) has \(\mu(I)>\mu(J)\); if this fails, return a positive binary-rational distribution satisfying \(V\) and \(\mu(I)\leq\mu(J)\).

Is this implication problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with a violating distribution computable in polynomial time when it fails, or is its decision version \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,|V|\), and the total input bit length \(L\).

For finitely many weighted criteria, strict coalition implication under consistent comparative information has a characterization by feasibility of an integer system of nonnegative combination coefficients; this is not a hardness classification ([original result reproduced by an author](https://wassilaouerdane.github.io/HDR_Ouerdane_2022.pdf#page=272)).
No complexity classification for exactly the positive-support distribution problem above was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted comparative constraints as strict comparisons of sums over disjoint type coalitions, matching the discrete definition; rationality does not introduce arbitrary thresholds or coefficient weights.
  • Normalized total mass to one and interpreted positivity as strictly positive mass on every listed type; quantified distributions may have real coordinates, while returned witnesses use binary-rational coordinates.
  • Carried over consistency as a promise that a positive distribution satisfying the constraints exists.
  • Interpreted complete rankings as strict total orders, required a nonempty support and two distinct queried options, and made I and J the exact complementary sets supporting the two directions.
  • The support and constraints are the input because shares are unknown; the fixed binary-rational society encoding applies to returned distributions.

1. Result

The implication problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). When the implication fails, a positive rational violating distribution can be computed in polynomial time, with polynomial output bit length. This holds for arbitrary rational coefficients in the listed strict constraints.

The algorithm uses the explicitly listed \(\tau\) rankings; it never enumerates all possible rankings.

2. A linear program that preserves strictness

Enumerate \(T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_\tau\}\), and put \(s=|V|\). Write the constraints in \(V\) as
\[ A_r x>0,\qquad r=1,\ldots,s, \]
where \(x_i\) denotes the share of \(t_i\). For an unweighted constraint \(\mu(E_r)>\mu(F_r)\), the coefficient \(A_{ri}\) is \(1\) on \(E_r\), \(-1\) on \(F_r\), and \(0\) elsewhere. Rational weighted coefficients are handled identically.

Define \(c_i=1\) when \(t_i\in I\), and \(c_i=-1\) when \(t_i\in J\). Thus
\[ c^\mathsf{T}x=\mu(I)-\mu(J). \]

Consider the following rational linear program, with variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_\tau,\delta\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad &\delta\\ \text{subject to}\quad &\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}x_i=1,\\ &x_i\geq\delta &&(i=1,\ldots,\tau),\\ &A_r x\geq\delta &&(r=1,\ldots,s),\\ &c^\mathsf{T}x\leq0,\\ &\delta\geq0. \end{aligned} \tag{LP} \]

The target violation remains nonstrict; only positivity and the strict premises receive a common slack.

If feasible, this program has a compact feasible region. Indeed, its constraints imply \(0\leq x_i\leq1\), and summing \(x_i\geq\delta\) gives \(0\leq\delta\leq1/\tau\). The feasible region is closed because all its constraints are nonstrict linear inequalities or equalities. Hence its optimum is attained.

3. Exact correctness proof

Lemma. A positive real distribution satisfying \(V\) and violating the target exists if and only if (LP) is feasible and its optimal value is positive.

Proof. Suppose \(x\) is such a distribution. Every \(x_i\) is positive, and every \(A_r x\) is positive. Because there are finitely many such quantities and \(\tau\geq1\), their minimum
\[ \delta_0=\min\bigl(\{x_i:1\leq i\leq\tau\} \cup\{A_r x:1\leq r\leq s\}\bigr) \]
exists and is positive. This definition also covers \(s=0\).

Normalization and the violation give \(\sum_i x_i=1\) and \(c^\mathsf{T}x\leq0\). Therefore \((x,\delta_0)\) is feasible for (LP), so its optimal value is positive.

Conversely, suppose \((x,\delta)\) is feasible with \(\delta>0\). Then \(x_i\geq\delta>0\) for every type, and \(A_r x\geq\delta>0\) for every premise. Normalization makes \(x\) a distribution, and \(c^\mathsf{T}x\leq0\) makes it a violation. This proves both directions. \(\square\)

The algorithm consequently returns:

  • Implication holds if (LP) is infeasible or its optimum is zero.
  • Implication fails, together with an optimal \(x\), if its optimum is positive.

The promise of consistency is compatible with this argument but is not needed for the reduction. The degenerate cases are also covered: \(I=T\) makes the target automatic, while \(I=\varnothing\) makes every consistent distribution a violation.

4. Exact rational computation and witness size

We use the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: in the binary bit model, one can decide feasibility and compute an exact rational optimum and an optimal rational solution whenever a finite optimum exists. Polynomial-time solvability with respect to dimension and binary encoding length is recalled in [Adler and Beling’s paper](https://adler.ieor.berkeley.edu/ilans_pubs/lp_algebraic_stoc_1992.pdf).

Its hypotheses hold here: every coefficient is rational, the program is explicitly represented, and any feasible region is bounded. Thus the test of whether the optimum is positive is exact.

For completeness, polynomial witness size can also be seen directly. Let \(n=\tau+1\), and let \(B\) be the binary encoding length of (LP). A nonempty optimal face of a bounded polyhedron has a vertex. At that vertex, \(n\) linearly independent active constraints determine the solution.

Clear denominators separately in those constraints. The resulting integer coefficients and right-hand sides have absolute value at most \(H=2^{O(B)}\). By Cramer’s rule, each coordinate is a ratio of integer determinants. The determinant expansion bounds both numerator and denominator in absolute value by \(n!H^n\). Consequently, each coordinate has
\[ O\bigl(n(B+\log n)\bigr) \]
bits. In particular, a positive optimal slack cannot require a superpolynomial number of bits to distinguish from zero.

When the implication fails, the exact LP solution therefore supplies the requested positive distribution, with numerators and denominators encoded in binary.

5. Running time

Scanning the rankings to determine \(I\) takes \(O(m\tau)\) ranking-entry operations. Constructing the constraint matrix takes \(O(\tau s)\) coefficient positions, besides reading the input.

The program has \(\tau+1\) variables and \(\tau+s+4\) inequalities when normalization is represented by two inequalities. With a dense encoding, its bit length satisfies
\[ B=O\bigl(L+\tau(\tau+s+1)\bigr). \]

Thus decision and witness computation take
\[ \operatorname{poly}\bigl(m\tau,\ \tau+s,\ L+\tau(\tau+s+1)\bigr) \]
bit operations. Equivalently, a convenient bound is
\[ \bigl(L+m\tau+\tau(\tau+|V|+1)\bigr)^{O(1)}. \]

Because rankings and constraints are explicitly listed, all these parameters are polynomially bounded in \(L\). The total running time is therefore polynomial in \(L\), and the witness has polynomial bit length.

6. If “binary-rational” requires dyadic shares

The conclusion also holds under the stronger interpretation that every output share must have denominator a power of two.

Take a rational witness \(x\) with common slack \(\delta>0\), and define
\[ M=\max\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{\|A_r\|_1:1\leq r\leq s\}\bigr), \qquad \eta=\frac{\delta}{4M}. \]

If \(I\) and \(J\) are both nonempty, choose \(i\in I\), \(j\in J\), and transfer mass \(\eta\) from coordinate \(i\) to coordinate \(j\), obtaining \(z\). Every coordinate remains at least \(3\delta/4\). Each premise changes by at most \(M\eta=\delta/4\), so its slack remains at least \(3\delta/4\). Moreover,
\[ c^\mathsf{T}z=c^\mathsf{T}x-2\eta\leq-2\eta. \]
If \(I=\varnothing\), take \(z=x\); then \(c^\mathsf{T}z=-1\leq-2\eta\). The case \(J=\varnothing\) cannot admit a witness.

Choose a power of two \(N>4M\tau/\eta\). Round the first \(\tau-1\) coordinates of \(z\) downward to multiples of \(1/N\), and set the last coordinate to one minus their sum. Call the result \(y\). Then
\[ \|y-z\|_1<\frac{2\tau}{N}<\frac{\eta}{2M}. \]

This perturbation preserves positivity, changes each premise by less than \(\eta/2\), and changes the target difference by less than \(\eta/2\). Hence \(y\) is a positive dyadic distribution satisfying every premise and still violating the target strictly. The required \(\log N\), and therefore the construction time and output size, are polynomial in the input length.

7. Relation to the integer characterization

The cited [Proposition 1, reproduced on PDF page 272](https://wassilaouerdane.github.io/HDR_Ouerdane_2022.pdf#page=272), gives an integer-coefficient characterization of coalition implication. Such a characterization does not establish hardness: the explicit positive-support problem here is decided directly by the rational linear program above.

Thus its decision version is in \(\mathrm{P}\); under polynomial-time many-one reductions, it could be \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
