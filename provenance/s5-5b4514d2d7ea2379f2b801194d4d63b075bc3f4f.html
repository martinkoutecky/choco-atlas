The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(a_0\prec a_1\), a rich domain \(\mathcal D\) of classical preferences, and finite support \(\{(R_t,\mu_t)\}_{t=1}^{\tau}\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), where \(r_t=V^{R_t}(a_1,(a_0,0))\), choose a deterministic DSIC and individually rational mechanism on \(\mathcal D\) that maximizes revenue per capita for a continuum of customers with type masses \(\mu_t\). Equivalently, find \(q^\star\in\arg\max_{q\in\{0,r_1,\ldots,r_\tau\}}q\sum_{t:r_t\ge q}\mu_t\) and the posted-price rule that serves type \(t\) when \(r_t\ge q^\star\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A=\{a_0,a_1\}\), with \(a_1\succ a_0\), and \(m=|A|=2\). A bundle \((a,p)\) has payment \(p\in\mathbb R\) from the customer. Classical preferences are complete, transitive, continuous, strictly prefer smaller payments for each alternative, strictly prefer \(a_1\) at equal payments, and permit compensation between any bundle and either alternative.
Fix a domain \(\mathcal D\) of classical preferences satisfying the following richness condition for some \(\varepsilon>0\): whenever \(v,w\in\mathbb R^2\) satisfy \(w_i>v_i\) for both coordinates and \(0<w_1-w_0<v_1-v_0\), with \((v_1-v_0)-(w_1-w_0)<\varepsilon\), some preference in \(\mathcal D\) makes both pairs \((a_0,v_0),(a_1,v_1)\) and \((a_0,w_0),(a_1,w_1)\) indifferent.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each supported type \(R_t\in\mathcal D\) is supplied through a positive binary rational \(r_t\), uniquely defined by \((a_1,r_t)\,I_t\,(a_0,0)\); \(I_t\) denotes indifference. The domain is fixed background data; \(L\) is the total binary input length.
Choose a deterministic mechanism \((f,p):\mathcal D\to A\times\mathbb R\), with \(f\) onto, that is DSIC and individually rational relative to \((a_0,0)\) throughout \(\mathcal D\), maximizing per-capita revenue \(\sum_t\mu_t p(R_t)\). Customers are served independently, without capacity constraints or production costs; indifference between offered alternatives is resolved in favor of \(a_1\).

Is this exact revenue-maximization problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

The known characterization replaces DSIC and IR revenue maximization by optimization over canonical mechanisms satisfying a mechanism-level monotonicity condition; it asserts neither discrete hardness nor a polynomial running time.

Exact polynomial-time solution for \(m=2\)

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. Its optimal revenue is

\[ \max_{q\in\{r_1,\ldots,r_\tau\}} q\sum_{t:r_t\ge q}\mu_t. \]

An optimal mechanism offers \((a_0,0)\) and \((a_1,q)\), choosing \(a_1\) at indifference. The scalar cutoff description agrees with Section 7 of the [cited paper](https://www.econtheory.org/ojs/index.php/te/article/viewFile/2910/27013/773). Below is a direct proof, including feasibility throughout the domain and the binary-input complexity bound.

1. Willingness to pay and richness

For every \(R\in\mathcal D\), let \(r(R)\) be the payment satisfying

\((a_1,r(R))\,I_R\,(a_0,0)\).

Existence follows from compensation and continuity; uniqueness follows from strict payment monotonicity. Moreover, \(r(R)>0\): any nonpositive payment for \(a_1\) makes it strictly preferable to \((a_0,0)\).

Strict payment monotonicity and transitivity therefore imply

\((a_1,q)\,R\,(a_0,0)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\le r(R)\),

with indifference exactly when \(q=r(R)\).

Richness implies that every positive willingness to pay occurs in \(\mathcal D\). To prove this, fix \(s>0\), and set

\(\delta=\min\{s/2,\varepsilon/2\},\qquad v=(0,s),\qquad w=(2\delta,s+\delta)\).

Both coordinates of \(w\) strictly exceed those of \(v\), and

\(0<w_1-w_0=s-\delta<s=v_1-v_0,\qquad (v_1-v_0)-(w_1-w_0)=\delta<\varepsilon\).

Thus the assumed richness supplies a preference containing \(v\) as an indifference vector. That preference has \(r(R)=s\).

2. Every positive posted price gives a feasible onto mechanism

Fix \(q>0\). Define the mechanism throughout \(\mathcal D\) by assigning \((a_1,q)\) when \(r(R)\ge q\), and \((a_0,0)\) otherwise.

The comparison established above shows that each type receives its preferred bundle from this menu, with the stipulated tie-breaking rule. Any misreport produces another bundle from the same menu, so it cannot strictly improve the customer’s outcome. This proves DSIC.

The mechanism is IR because \((a_0,0)\) is always available.

Finally, richness supplies types with willingness to pay \(q/2\) and \(2q\). They receive \(a_0\) and \(a_1\), respectively, so the allocation rule is onto. These witness types need not belong to the society’s support.

The revenue of this mechanism is

\(F(q)=q\sum_{t:r_t\ge q}\mu_t\).

3. A universal upper bound for feasible mechanisms

Consider any deterministic, onto, DSIC and IR mechanism \((f,p)\).

DSIC forces a constant payment for each alternative. Indeed, if two reports receive the same alternative at different payments, the type charged the larger payment can report the other type and obtain that same alternative at a strictly smaller payment. This contradicts DSIC.

Since \(f\) is onto, both constants exist. Denote the payment for \(a_0\) by \(b\), and that for \(a_1\) by \(c\).

IR for any type assigned \(a_0\) implies

\((a_0,b)\,R\,(a_0,0)\),

hence \(b\le0\) by strict payment monotonicity.

If \(c\le0\), every payment is nonpositive, and revenue is nonpositive.

Now suppose \(c>0\). For every supported type assigned \(a_1\), IR implies

\((a_1,c)\,R_t\,(a_0,0)\),

so \(r_t\ge c\). Write

\(W=\sum_{t:f(R_t)=a_1}\mu_t\).

Since the total mass is one, revenue satisfies

\(\operatorname{Rev}(f,p)=cW+b(1-W)\le cW\le c\sum_{t:r_t\ge c}\mu_t=F(c)\).

Section 2 supplies a feasible onto mechanism attaining \(F(c)\). Thus every feasible mechanism with a positive payment for \(a_1\) is weakly dominated in revenue by a positive posted-price mechanism.

There is also a feasible mechanism with strictly positive revenue: take \(q=\min_t r_t\), so every supported type buys and revenue equals \(q>0\). Consequently, mechanisms with \(c\le0\) cannot be optimal.

4. Only supported willingness-to-pay values need consideration

Let the distinct supported values be

\(0<s_1<\cdots<s_k,\qquad k\le\tau\),

and let

\(\nu_j=\sum_{t:r_t=s_j}\mu_t,\qquad S_j=\sum_{\ell=j}^{k}\nu_\ell\).

For any positive price \(q\) with a supported buyer, let \(s_j\) be the smallest supported value satisfying \(s_j\ge q\). There is no supported willingness to pay in the interval from \(q\) up to \(s_j\), excluding \(s_j\). Because indifference favors \(a_1\), the buying sets at prices \(q\) and \(s_j\) coincide. Therefore

\(F(q)=qS_j\le s_jS_j=F(s_j)\).

Prices exceeding \(s_k\) yield zero revenue. Hence

\(\operatorname{OPT}=\max_{1\le j\le k}s_jS_j\).

Selecting any maximizing index \(j^\ast\) and posting \(q^\ast=s_{j^\ast}\) attains this upper bound. This proves global optimality over all mechanisms in the task.

5. Exact binary-input algorithm

The algorithm sorts the supported values, groups equal values, computes suffix masses, and selects the largest product \(s_jS_j\). It returns the rational price \(q^\ast\), the optimum revenue, and the mechanism described in Section 2.

There are \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) rational comparisons and \(O(\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations. To verify polynomial bit complexity, write the input masses as \(\mu_t=u_t/d_t\), and use the common denominator

\(D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}d_t\).

Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Each scaled mass

\(M_t=u_t(D/d_t)\)

is an integer, and \(\sum_tM_t=D\). Every suffix mass can therefore be represented as \(N_j/D\), with \(0<N_j\le D\).

Writing \(s_j=x_j/y_j\), each candidate revenue is represented exactly as

\(s_jS_j=\dfrac{x_jN_j}{y_jD}\).

All these integers, and the cross-products used to compare candidates, have \(O(L)\) bits. Ordinary exact integer arithmetic consequently implements the algorithm in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\). The output price is an input rational, and the optimum revenue also has polynomial binary length.

Thus the exact revenue-maximization problem posed here is polynomial-time solvable for the fixed value \(m=2\).
