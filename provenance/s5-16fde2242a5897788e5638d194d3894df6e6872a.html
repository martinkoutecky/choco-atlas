The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational mass vector \(\mu\), promised click mass \(\rho\), residual type demands \(d\), and a type-symmetric polymatroid \(P_\mu=\{X\ge0:X(S)\le f_\mu(S)\ \forall S\subseteq T\}\) induced by per-capita keyword and slot data, compute exactly for each \(t\in T\) the type-mass clinch \(\delta_t=\max_{X\in P_{\rho,d}}\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}X-\max_{X\in P_{\rho,d},\,X_t=0}\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}X\), where \(P_{\rho,d}=\{X\ge0:\rho+X\in P_\mu,\ X\le d\}\), and determine whether the computation and its payments admit a consistent finite-clone lifting for \(N\mu_t\) identical advertisers.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\) keywords. Keyword \(k\) has eligible types \(E_k\subseteq T\), eligible mass \(M_k=\sum_{t\in E_k}\mu_t\), and a nonnegative nonincreasing step function \(a_k:[0,M_k]\to\mathbb R_+\), specified by rational breakpoints and heights; zero heights are allowed and click rates do not depend on advertiser identity.
Write \(F_k(q)=\int_0^q a_k(u)\,du\), \(X(S)=\sum_{t\in S}X_t\), and \(f_\mu(S)=\sum_{k=1}^m F_k(\sum_{t\in S\cap E_k}\mu_t)\). The feasible click-mass polymatroid is \(P_\mu=\{X\ge0:X(S)\le f_\mu(S)\text{ for every }S\subseteq T\}\).
Inputs also include rational promised masses \(\rho\in P_\mu\), finite rational residual demands \(d\ge0\), and rational type prices \(p\ge0\). Set \(P_{\rho,d}=\{X\ge0:\rho+X\in P_\mu,\ X\le d\}\) and \(\delta_t=\max_{X\in P_{\rho,d}}X(T)-\max_{X\in P_{\rho,d},\,X_t=0}X(T)\); the payment-mass increment is \(p_t\delta_t\).
For each positive integer \(N\) with all \(N\mu_t\) integral, replace type \(t\) by \(N\mu_t\) identical advertisers, each with promise \(\rho_t/\mu_t\), demand \(d_t/\mu_t\), and price \(p_t\). Keyword \(k\) has \(NM_k\) slots with click rates \(\alpha^k_j=N\int_{(j-1)/N}^{j/N}a_k(u)\,du\).
On these advertisers use the polymatroid rank \(g_N(A)=\sum_k\sum_{j=1}^{|A\cap C_k|}\alpha^k_j\), where \(C_k\) is the set eligible for keyword \(k\). Compute each advertiser's clinch by the same maximum-difference formula, deleting that advertiser alone, and charge its price times its clinch, simultaneously from the initial state.
A consistent finite-clone lifting means that, for every such \(N\), the sum of advertiser clinches of each type divided by \(N\) equals \(\delta_t\), and the similarly normalized payment sum equals \(p_t\delta_t\).

Is exact computation of all type clinches and payment increments polynomial-time, or NP-hard, with running time measured in \(m,\tau,L\), and the total number of slot-function pieces, where \(L\) is the total input bit length?
Does this update admit a consistent finite-clone lifting for every valid input?

For finitely many advertisers with divisible clicks, the maximum-difference clinching formula is computable in polynomial time with a submodular value oracle; multiple-keyword slot constraints yield a polymatroid. [Goel, Mirrokni, and Paes Leme](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.0404).
No result settling the specified mass update together with this finite-clone consistency condition was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to sum to one and treated allocations and demands as aggregate type masses.
  • Made per-capita slot data explicit as nonnegative nonincreasing rational step functions; their integrals define keyword ranks, preserving identity-independent click rates and allowing zero-rate slots.
  • Required feasible nonnegative rational promises and finite nonnegative rational demands, as in the discrete clinching subproblem.
  • Added nonnegative rational type prices to define payment increments; the question concerns one simultaneous clinching update, rather than an unspecified complete auction trajectory.
  • Defined clone promises and demands per advertiser by dividing aggregate type masses by the type mass, scaled total keyword supply by the clone population, and obtained slot rates by averaging the slot function over equal mass intervals.
  • Interpreted consistent finite-clone lifting as exact agreement of normalized type totals for every integral clone population, with each advertiser independently clinching against all other advertisers.

1. Resolution

Exact computation of all type clinches and payment increments is polynomial-time in the specified input parameters. However, the update does not admit a consistent finite-clone lifting for every valid input.

We prove polynomial-time computability using a compact flow network, then give a counterexample with two types, positive demands, and strictly positive click rates.

2. A compact network representing the mass polymatroid

Let \(K\) be the total number of step-function pieces. Keywords with \(M_k=0\) contribute nothing and can be omitted.

For each remaining keyword, write its breakpoints and heights as

\(0=b_{k,0}<b_{k,1}<\cdots<b_{k,r_k}=M_k\),

\(c_{k,1}\ge c_{k,2}\ge\cdots\ge c_{k,r_k}\ge0\).

Values at individual breakpoints do not affect the integral. Define

\(\gamma_{k,i}=c_{k,i}-c_{k,i+1}\ge0\), for \(1\le i<r_k\).

Then, for \(0\le q\le M_k\),

\(F_k(q)=c_{k,r_k}q+\sum_{i=1}^{r_k-1}\gamma_{k,i}\min\{q,b_{k,i}\}\).

Indeed, both sides vanish at zero. On the interior of piece \(j\), the derivative of the right-hand side is

\(c_{k,r_k}+\sum_{i=j}^{r_k-1}(c_{k,i}-c_{k,i+1})=c_{k,j}\).

Both sides are continuous, so the identity follows.

Define

\(\beta_t=\mu_t\sum_{k:t\in E_k}c_{k,r_k}\).

Construct a directed network with source \(s\), sink \(z\), one vertex for each type, and an auxiliary vertex \(v_{k,i}\) for each pair with \(1\le i<r_k\). Its arcs and capacities are:

  • \(s\to t\), with capacity \(\beta_t\);
  • \(s\to v_{k,i}\), with capacity \(\gamma_{k,i}b_{k,i}\);
  • \(v_{k,i}\to t\), for \(t\in E_k\), with capacity \(\gamma_{k,i}\mu_t\).

Given any vector \(y\ge0\), additionally put an arc \(t\to z\) of capacity \(y_t\).

Representation lemma. All terminal arcs \(t\to z\) can be saturated simultaneously if and only if \(y\in P_\mu\).

Proof. Fix the set \(S\subseteq T\) of type vertices on the sink side of a cut. Terminal arcs contribute \(y(T\setminus S)\), and direct source-to-type arcs contribute \(\sum_{t\in S}\beta_t\).

Each auxiliary vertex can independently be placed on either side. Placing \(v_{k,i}\) on the sink side costs \(\gamma_{k,i}b_{k,i}\); placing it on the source side costs

\(\gamma_{k,i}\sum_{t\in S\cap E_k}\mu_t\).

Consequently, the minimum cut capacity with this fixed set \(S\) is

\(y(T\setminus S)+\sum_{t\in S}\beta_t+\sum_{k,i}\gamma_{k,i}\min\left\{b_{k,i},\sum_{t\in S\cap E_k}\mu_t\right\}\)

\(=y(T\setminus S)+f_\mu(S)\).

Thus the network’s minimum cut capacity is

\(\min_{S\subseteq T}\bigl[y(T\setminus S)+f_\mu(S)\bigr]\).

By the maximum-flow/minimum-cut theorem, the terminal arcs can all be saturated exactly when every cut has capacity at least \(y(T)\). This is equivalent to \(y(S)\le f_\mu(S)\) for every \(S\subseteq T\), precisely the defining inequalities of \(P_\mu\). The theorem applies because the graph is finite and every capacity is finite and nonnegative. [Ford and Fulkerson](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/lans/readings/routing/ford-max_flow-1956.pdf). \(\square\)

3. Computing residual optima and clinches

Apply the representation lemma with \(y=\rho\). Since \(\rho\in P_\mu\), an ordinary maximum-flow calculation produces a flow \(h\) satisfying

\(h(t,z)=\rho_t\) for every \(t\).

Fix this flow. For any nonnegative demand vector \(b\), retain the upstream capacities and give each terminal arc the bounds

\(\rho_t\le h'(t,z)\le\rho_t+b_t\).

The representation lemma implies that admissible flows \(h'\) correspond exactly, through their terminal flows, to vectors \(X\) satisfying

\(X\ge0,\qquad X\le b,\qquad \rho+X\in P_\mu\),

where \(X_t=h'(t,z)-\rho_t\).

We now reduce optimization over these flows to ordinary maximum flow. Form the residual network around \(h\), respecting the stated lower bounds:

  • For an upstream arc of capacity \(u_e\), give its forward residual arc capacity \(u_e-h_e\) and its reverse residual arc capacity \(h_e\).
  • For a terminal arc, give its forward residual arc capacity \(b_t\) and its reverse residual arc capacity zero.

Let \(W(b)\) be the maximum residual flow value from \(s\) to \(z\). Then

\(W(b)=\max\{X(T):X\ge0,\ X\le b,\ \rho+X\in P_\mu\}\).

To verify this equality in both directions:

  • A residual flow augments \(h\) by its forward flows minus its reverse flows. The residual capacity bounds ensure that the resulting flow satisfies every original upper and lower bound. Its increase in value equals \(X(T)\).
  • Conversely, for any admissible \(h'\), represent each positive difference \(h'_e-h_e\) on the corresponding forward residual arc and each negative difference on its reverse residual arc. These differences respect all residual capacities and conserve flow at every internal vertex. Their net value is \(X(T)\). Any cycles can be removed without changing that value.

Therefore the residual optimum is exactly the required residual-mass optimum.

For each type \(t\), define \(d^{(-t)}\) by

\(d^{(-t)}_t=0,\qquad d^{(-t)}_u=d_u\quad(u\ne t)\).

The requested outputs are consequently

\(\delta_t=W(d)-W(d^{(-t)})\),

and

\(p_t\delta_t\).

This requires one maximum-flow calculation to realize \(\rho\), followed by \(\tau+1\) residual maximum-flow calculations.

Bit complexity

The constructed graph has \(O(\tau+K)\) vertices and \(O(\tau+\tau K)\) arcs. Its capacities are obtained from input rationals by polynomially many additions, subtractions, and multiplications; their total encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\).

Exact maximum flow is polynomial-time on these rational networks. For example, clear all denominators using their product, whose bit length is at most the sum of their bit lengths, and apply the shortest augmenting-path algorithm. Its number of augmentations is polynomial in the graph size, independently of capacity magnitudes. Intermediate integers have polynomial bit length. [Edmonds and Karp](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~pettie/matching/Edmonds-Karp-network-flow.pdf).

The final subtractions and price multiplications also take polynomial time and produce rationals of polynomial encoding length. This proves the asserted exact polynomial-time algorithm.

4. Counterexample to finite-clone consistency

Take two types with

\(T=\{1,2\},\qquad \mu_1=\mu_2=\tfrac12\).

There is one keyword, eligible for both types, with click-rate function

\(a(u)=1\) for \(0\le u<\tfrac34\),

and

\(a(u)=\tfrac12\) for \(\tfrac34\le u\le1\).

Set

\(\rho_1=\rho_2=0,\qquad d_1=d_2=\tfrac12,\qquad p_1=p_2=1\).

Every mass is positive, every demand is positive, both click-rate heights are positive, and all input numbers are binary rationals. The promise vector is feasible.

Type clinches

Here

\(F(\tfrac12)=\tfrac12,\qquad F(1)=\tfrac78\).

Thus the mass feasibility constraints are

\(X_1\le\tfrac12,\qquad X_2\le\tfrac12,\qquad X_1+X_2\le\tfrac78\).

The maximum total residual mass is \(\tfrac78\), attained by \(X_1=X_2=\tfrac7{16}\).

When either type is deleted, the maximum is \(\tfrac12\), attained by assigning that amount to the other type. Therefore

\(\delta_1=\delta_2=\tfrac78-\tfrac12=\tfrac38\).

The two payment-mass increments are also \(\tfrac38\).

Advertiser clinches for a valid clone population

Choose \(N=4\). Each type has two advertisers, each with promise zero, demand

\(d_t/\mu_t=1\),

and price one.

The prescribed slot rates are

\((\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3,\alpha_4)=(1,1,1,\tfrac12)\).

All four advertisers are eligible. Hence \(g_4(A)=|A|\) when \(|A|\le3\), while the rank of all four advertisers is \(\tfrac72\).

The maximum total advertiser allocation is \(\tfrac72\). The full-set rank gives the upper bound, and assigning \(\tfrac78\) to each advertiser attains it: every subset of at most three advertisers receives at most its cardinality, and the full set receives exactly \(\tfrac72\).

Now constrain any one advertiser’s increment to zero. The other three have total rank \(3\), so the total allocation is at most \(3\). Assigning one click to each of those three advertisers attains this bound and satisfies all rank inequalities.

Thus every advertiser’s clinch is

\(\tfrac72-3=\tfrac12\).

Each type has two advertisers, so its normalized clone-clinch sum is

\(\dfrac{2\cdot\tfrac12}{4}=\tfrac14\ne\tfrac38=\delta_t\).

Prices equal one, so the normalized payment sum also equals \(\tfrac14\), whereas the required payment-mass increment equals \(\tfrac38\).

Failure for this valid \(N\) disproves consistency for every valid input.

The discrepancy arises from the deletion operation. Full type blocks have correctly scaled ranks: if \(B_N(S)\) contains all clones of types in \(S\), telescoping the slot integrals gives

\(g_N(B_N(S))=Nf_\mu(S)\).

Nevertheless, deleting a whole type and summing the effects of deleting its advertisers individually yield different maximum differences.
