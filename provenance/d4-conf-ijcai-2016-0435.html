The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite alternative set \(M\), a rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete rankings \(\mathcal{S}(M)\), and fixed tie-breaking, form the majority graph \(E_\mu\), compute its uncovered set \(S_\mu\), and compute a lottery \(p\) minimizing \(p_{\max}\) subject to \(p_Z\ge0\), \(\sum_{Z\in S_\mu}p_Z=1\), and \(\sum_{(Y,Z)\in E_\mu}p_Z\le p_{\max}\) for every \(Y\in S_\mu\). Determine the worst-case value of \(\sum_{A\in M}p_A\operatorname{med}_\lambda(A)/\min_{B\in M}\operatorname{med}_\lambda(B)\) over all metric population measures \(\lambda\) whose ranking push-forward is \(\mu\), and whether the factor \(4\) guarantee is tight.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) be finite and let \(\mu\) be a rational probability distribution on the complete rankings of \(M\). Direct \(Y\to Z\) when \(\mu\{r:Y\succ_r Z\}>1/2\), resolving equality using a fixed total order; let \(S_\mu\) be the alternatives reaching every alternative in at most two directed steps.
Choose the lexicographically first optimum \(p\) of \(\min p_{\max}\) subject to \(p_Z\ge0\), \(\sum_{Z\in S_\mu}p_Z=1\), and \(\sum_{Z\in S_\mu:(Y,Z)\in E_\mu}p_Z\le p_{\max}\) for each \(Y\in S_\mu\), with \(p_A=0\) outside \(S_\mu\).
A metric population is a probability measure \(\lambda\) on a metric space containing \(M\); its induced ranking orders alternatives by nondecreasing distance, with ties resolved by the fixed order. Require this ranking push-forward to equal \(\mu\), and define \(\operatorname{med}_\lambda(A)=\inf\{t:\lambda(\{x:d(x,A)\le t\})\ge1/2\}\). Let \(D_\mu(p)\) be the supremum of \(\frac{\sum_{A\in M}p_A\operatorname{med}_\lambda(A)}{\min_{B\in M}\operatorname{med}_\lambda(B)}\) over such \(\lambda\).

Target: Is the rational-threshold problem of deciding \(D_\mu(p)\le q\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it NP-hard?
Is \(\sup_\mu D_\mu(p)=4\)?

Known discrete results: for finite voter profiles, this LP-based rule is computable in polynomial time and has expected median distortion at most \(4\). Every randomized rule has worst-case median distortion at least \(3\) in the discrete model.

Results and scope

The proposed universal bound is false under the stated median and majority-tie conventions: a fixed rational distribution on two rankings has unbounded distortion, even using only populations with strictly positive optimal median.

I also determine the distortion completely for two alternatives. The complexity question for an unrestricted number of alternatives remains open here.

There is a minor definitional issue: the displayed ratio is undefined when its denominator is zero. Below, suprema are taken over populations with positive denominator. The unboundedness construction uses only such populations, so any extension that includes their ratios remains unbounded.

1. A fixed two-alternative instance with unbounded distortion

Let \(M=\{A,B\}\), naming the alternatives so that the fixed order resolves a majority tie in favor of \(A\). Fix the rational ranking distribution

\(\mu(A\succ B)=\mu(B\succ A)=\tfrac12.\)

The directed tournament has the single edge \(A\to B\). Consequently, \(S_\mu=\{A\}\): \(A\) reaches both alternatives, whereas \(B\) cannot reach \(A\). The normalization constraint therefore forces \(p_A=1\) and \(p_B=0\). There is no ambiguity from lexicographic optimization.

For each \(0<\varepsilon<1\), use the real line with its usual distance. Place the alternatives at \(B=0\) and \(A=2\), and define

\(\lambda_\varepsilon=\tfrac12\delta_\varepsilon+\tfrac12\delta_3,\)

where \(\delta_x\) denotes unit probability mass at \(x\). The distances are:

| Population point | Mass | Distance to \(A\) | Distance to \(B\) | Ranking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(\varepsilon\) | \(\tfrac12\) | \(2-\varepsilon\) | \(\varepsilon\) | \(B\succ A\) |
| \(3\) | \(\tfrac12\) | \(1\) | \(3\) | \(A\succ B\) |

Both rankings are strict, and the ranking push-forward is exactly the fixed distribution \(\mu\).

For two equally weighted atoms, the specified lower median is the smaller of the two distances: below that distance the ball has mass zero, and at that distance it has mass at least \(\tfrac12\). Thus

\(\operatorname{med}_{\lambda_\varepsilon}(A)=1,\qquad \operatorname{med}_{\lambda_\varepsilon}(B)=\varepsilon.\)

The distortion ratio is therefore

\(\displaystyle \frac{\sum_Cp_C\operatorname{med}_{\lambda_\varepsilon}(C)}{\min_C\operatorname{med}_{\lambda_\varepsilon}(C)}=\frac1\varepsilon.\)

For every finite positive \(K\), choosing \(0<\varepsilon<\min\{1,1/K\}\) makes this ratio exceed \(K\). Hence this single rational instance satisfies \(D_\mu(p)=+\infty\). In particular,

\(\displaystyle \sup_\mu D_\mu(p)=+\infty,\)

rather than \(4\).

This construction already consists of two equally weighted voters. Thus the stated finite-profile bound cannot apply unchanged to the simultaneous use of this lower median and this majority-tie convention. The published bound for Uncovered Set Min-Cover does not justify transferring the guarantee without reconciling those conventions. [Anshelevich–Postl, Theorem 13](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/16/Papers/014.pdf)

2. Exact classification for two alternatives

Let \(W\) be the winner of the unique tournament edge and \(L\) the other alternative. As above, the rule necessarily selects \(W\) with probability one. Set

\(\alpha=\mu\{r:W\succ_r L\}.\)

By the definition of the edge, \(\tfrac12\le\alpha\le1\).

Proposition. Over populations with positive optimal median, the distortion is \(+\infty\) when \(\alpha=\tfrac12\), is \(3\) when \(\tfrac12<\alpha<1\), and is \(1\) when \(\alpha=1\).

The tie case was proved above. The remaining cases follow below.

2.1 Strict-majority upper bound

Consider any admissible population with \(\alpha>\tfrac12\). Write \(b=\operatorname{med}_\lambda(L)\).

First, \(b\) is finite and the closed ball \(C=\{x:d(x,L)\le b\}\) has mass at least \(\tfrac12\). Finiteness follows because balls of integer radius increase to the entire metric space. For attainment, the definition of the infimum implies that every ball of radius \(b+1/n\) has mass at least \(\tfrac12\); continuity of a probability measure from above gives \(\lambda(C)\ge\tfrac12\).

Let \(H\) be the set of points whose induced ranking places \(W\) above \(L\). Then \(\lambda(H)=\alpha>\tfrac12\), and

\(\lambda(H\cap C)\ge\lambda(H)+\lambda(C)-1>0.\)

Choose \(x\in H\cap C\). Ranking consistency implies \(d(x,W)\le d(x,L)\), including when the ranking is determined by a distance tie. The triangle inequality yields

\(d(W,L)\le d(W,x)+d(x,L)\le2d(x,L)\le2b.\)

For every \(y\in C\), another application of the triangle inequality gives

\(d(y,W)\le d(y,L)+d(L,W)\le b+2b=3b.\)

Since \(C\) has mass at least \(\tfrac12\), it follows that \(\operatorname{med}_\lambda(W)\le3b\).

If \(W\) has the smaller median, the distortion is \(1\). Otherwise its distortion is \(\operatorname{med}_\lambda(W)/b\le3\). This proves the upper bound.

2.2 Matching lower bound for every nonunanimous strict majority

Fix \(\tfrac12<\alpha<1\) and put \(\beta=1-\alpha\), so \(0<\beta<\tfrac12\).

On the real line, place \(L=0\) and \(W=2\). For \(0<\eta<1\), use the population

\(\lambda_\eta=\beta\delta_{-1}+(\tfrac12-\beta)\delta_{1+\eta}+\tfrac12\delta_6.\)

The distances and rankings are:

| Population point | Mass | Distance to \(W\) | Distance to \(L\) | Ranking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(-1\) | \(\beta\) | \(3\) | \(1\) | \(L\succ W\) |
| \(1+\eta\) | \(\tfrac12-\beta\) | \(1-\eta\) | \(1+\eta\) | \(W\succ L\) |
| \(6\) | \(\tfrac12\) | \(4\) | \(6\) | \(W\succ L\) |

The mass preferring \(W\) is \(1-\beta=\alpha\), so this realizes exactly the prescribed two-ranking distribution.

For \(L\), cumulative mass is \(\beta<\tfrac12\) at distance \(1\), and first reaches \(\tfrac12\) at distance \(1+\eta\). For \(W\), cumulative mass is \(\tfrac12-\beta<\tfrac12\) at distance \(1-\eta\), and first reaches \(\tfrac12\) at distance \(3\). Therefore

\(\operatorname{med}_{\lambda_\eta}(L)=1+\eta,\qquad \operatorname{med}_{\lambda_\eta}(W)=3.\)

The ratio is \(3/(1+\eta)\), which tends to \(3\) as \(\eta\) tends to zero. Together with the upper bound, this proves that the supremum is exactly \(3\).

2.3 Unanimity

If \(\alpha=1\), ranking consistency gives \(d(x,W)\le d(x,L)\) almost surely. Consequently, for every radius \(t\), the mass within distance \(t\) of \(W\) is at least the mass within distance \(t\) of \(L\). Taking the defining infima gives

\(\operatorname{med}_\lambda(W)\le\operatorname{med}_\lambda(L).\)

Thus selecting \(W\) has ratio exactly \(1\) whenever the denominator is positive. Such admissible populations exist—for example, a single voter strictly closer to \(W\), at positive distance from both alternatives. This completes the classification.

3. What remains open

The classification supplies a polynomial-time threshold test when there are exactly two alternatives: compute \(\alpha\), distinguish the three cases, and compare the corresponding value with the rational threshold.

It does not establish a polynomial-time algorithm or NP-hardness for an unrestricted number of alternatives. Unbounded instances alone establish neither complexity conclusion.
