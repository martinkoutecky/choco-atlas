The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_\theta\), item-class supplies \(\sigma_g\), additive valuations \(v_\theta\), and a common top pool \(H\) with \(\sum_{g\in H}\sigma_g=1\), compute a finite-support assignment \(x_{\theta,b}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{G}\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,b} b_gx_{\theta,b}=\sigma_g\), and \(v_\theta(b)\ge\tfrac{2}{3}v_\theta(b'-e_g)\) whenever \(x_{\theta,b},x_{\theta',b'}>0\) and \(b'_g>0\), with every type ranking \(H\) above \(G\setminus H\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), normalized by \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|G|\) item classes with positive binary-rational supplies \(\sigma_g\), where \(\sum_g\sigma_g>1\). Each type has nonnegative binary-rational additive values \(v_{\theta,g}\). The input includes \(H\subseteq G\) with \(\sum_{g\in H}\sigma_g=1\) and \(v_{\theta,h}\ge v_{\theta,g}\) for every \(\theta\), \(h\in H\), and \(g\notin H\); ties are allowed.
Bundles are integral vectors \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\), permitting multiple copies of a class; empty bundles are allowed. An assignment has finitely many positive rational masses \(x_{\theta,b}\), satisfying \(\sum_bx_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,b}b_gx_{\theta,b}=\sigma_g\) for every class.
The assignment is \(2/3\)-EFX if \(v_\theta(b)\ge\frac23v_\theta(b'-e_g)\) for every pair of positive-mass entries \((\theta,b),(\theta',b')\) and every \(g\) with \(b'_g>0\), including zero-valued items; \(e_g\) is the unit vector for class \(g\). Comparisons include different bundles assigned to the same type.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; bundle coordinates and assignment masses use binary encoding.

Is computing a \(2/3\)-EFX assignment, or reporting infeasibility, solvable in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), or is this fixed approximation task NP-hard?

For finite societies with nonnegative additive valuations and a common set of top \(n\) items, a polynomial-time algorithm computes a complete \(2/3\)-EFX allocation, allowing ties across the top-set boundary. [Verified result](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.04860).
No result settling this exact mass-assignment problem was located in the literature search.

1. Universal feasibility and polynomial-size witnesses

Write
$$ v_\theta(b)=\sum_{g\in G}v_{\theta,g}b_g. $$

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of all masses and supplies. Thus $D\mu_\theta$ and $D\sigma_g$ are integers. Define
$$ M=\sum_{g\in G}D\sigma_g. $$
Both $\log D$ and $\log M$ are $O(L)$, although their numerical values can be exponential in $L$.

1.1. Every promised instance is feasible

Use the finite theorem supplied in the question: nonnegative additive valuations with a common top set whose cardinality equals the number of agents admit a complete $\frac{2}{3}$-EFX allocation. The theorem allows boundary ties, and its EFX definition includes removal of zero-valued items. See [Definition 4, Section 3.2, and Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.04860).

Construct an explicit finite instance containing:

  • $D\mu_\theta$ agents of type $\theta$;
  • $D\sigma_g$ individually distinguishable copies of class $g$.

There are $D$ agents and $M>D$ items. The copies belonging to $H$ form a common top set of cardinality
$$ \sum_{h\in H}D\sigma_h=D. $$
The common-top-set hypothesis follows directly from the input inequalities.

Apply the finite theorem. If $c_{\theta,b}$ agents of type $\theta$ receive class-count bundle $b$, set
$$ x_{\theta,b}=\frac{c_{\theta,b}}{D}, $$
retaining only positive counts. Counting agents and copies proves the required conservation equations.

For distinct positive entries, choose agents realizing those entries. Removing one copy of a class $g$ from the target gives precisely
$$ v_\theta(b)\ge \frac{2}{3}v_\theta(b'-e_g). $$
Different bundles of the same type are covered because they are assigned to different finite agents. Self-comparisons follow from nonnegativity.

Thus infeasibility never occurs on promised inputs. The finite construction is an exponential-time construction in the binary encoding, rather than a polynomial-time algorithm.

1.2. A witness with at most $\tau+m$ entries exists

Put $d=\tau+m$. Associate with each positive entry the integer column
$$ a_{\theta,b}=(e_\theta,b)\in\mathbb Z^d. $$
Let $A$ consist of these columns and let
$$ r=((\mu_\theta)_\theta,(\sigma_g)_g). $$
The conservation equations are $Ax=r$.

If the columns are dependent, choose a nonzero rational vector $\lambda$ satisfying $A\lambda=0$. Summing its type-coordinate equations gives
$$ \sum_j\lambda_j=0. $$
Consequently, $\lambda$ has both positive and negative coordinates. Set
$$ t=\min_{\lambda_j>0}\frac{x_j}{\lambda_j}, \qquad x'_j=x_j-t\lambda_j. $$
Then $x'\ge0$, $Ax'=r$, and at least one positive entry disappears. No new bundle is introduced, so every surviving fairness comparison remains valid.

Repeating this operation leaves $k\le d$ independent columns.

Every retained bundle originated in the finite allocation, hence $b_g\le M$. Choose $k$ rows giving a nonsingular integer submatrix $B$. The corresponding right-hand side is $z/D$, where the entries of $z$ are integers bounded by $M$. Cramer’s rule gives
$$ x_j=\frac{\det B_j}{D\det B}, $$
where $B_j$ replaces column $j$ by $z$. The determinant expansion yields
$$ |\det B|,\ |\det B_j|\le k!M^k. $$
Thus every retained mass has polynomial bit length, and so does every bundle coordinate.

Conclusion. Every promised instance has a polynomial-size rational witness with at most $\tau+m$ positive entries.

Such witnesses are polynomially verifiable: conservation is checked by rational arithmetic, and fairness requires at most $k^2m$ checks of
$$ 3v_\theta(b)\ge2v_\theta(b'-e_g). $$

1.3. Consequence for the meaning of NP-hardness

The bounded-witness search relation is total and polynomially verifiable. Extending it to malformed inputs by an “invalid input” witness places it in $\mathrm{TFNP}$, because the promise conditions themselves are polynomially checkable.

Suppose a deterministic polynomial-time SAT decision procedure used this search problem as an oracle and was correct for every valid bounded oracle-answer transcript. Then $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$.

Indeed, an NP verifier for unsatisfiability could guess and verify the polynomial-size answer to every adaptive oracle query, simulate the procedure, and accept exactly when it rejects the formula. Totality supplies an accepting transcript for every unsatisfiable formula. Correctness for every valid transcript excludes such a transcript for satisfiable formulas. Hence unsatisfiability would belong to $\mathrm{NP}$.

This does not classify the search problem. It identifies a restriction that an applicable hardness reduction must address.

2. Polynomial-time removal of envy cycles

A partial assignment satisfies the type-mass equations and allocates no more than the supply of each class. Fairness is defined on its positive entries exactly as in the question.

For a given partial assignment, define each type’s minimum utility by
$$ \ell_\theta=\min_{b:x_{\theta,b}>0}v_\theta(b). $$
The minimum exists because every type has positive mass.

Proposition 1

Given an explicitly encoded partial assignment, one can compute, in polynomial time in its encoding length, another assignment which:

  • preserves every type’s mass and the aggregate mass of every distinct bundle;
  • does not decrease any type’s minimum utility;
  • preserves its $\alpha$-EFX property for any $\alpha\in[0,1]$;
  • has an acyclic strict-envy graph.

If there are $k$ distinct bundles, an output with at most $\tau+k-1$ positive entries can be chosen.

Proof

List the distinct bundles as $c^1,\ldots,c^k$, and put
$$ s_j=\sum_\theta x_{\theta,c^j}. $$
Allow a type–bundle pair $(\theta,j)$ precisely when
$$ v_\theta(c^j)\ge\ell_\theta. $$

Solve the transportation linear program
$$ \max\sum_{\theta,j}v_\theta(c^j)y_{\theta,j} $$
subject to
$$ \sum_jy_{\theta,j}=\mu_\theta,\qquad \sum_\theta y_{\theta,j}=s_j,\qquad y_{\theta,j}\ge0, $$
using only allowed pairs.

The original assignment is feasible. The feasible region is bounded, since every variable is at most $1$. All coefficients are rational and have polynomial encoding length. Polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore applies, and we may choose an optimal extreme point.

Every positive output entry has utility at least $\ell_\theta$. If the original assignment was $\alpha$-EFX, then for every original target bundle and permissible removal,
$$ \ell_\theta\ge\alpha v_\theta(c^j-e_g). $$
The target-bundle multiset is unchanged, so these inequalities prove fairness for the output.

Define the strict-envy graph on positive entries. There is an edge from $(\theta,j)$ to $(\theta',j')$ when
$$ v_\theta(c^{j'})>v_\theta(c^j). $$

Suppose a simple directed cycle consists of entries
$$ (\theta_1,j_1),\ldots,(\theta_r,j_r), $$
with indices read cyclically. Let
$$ \delta=\min_i y_{\theta_i,j_i}>0. $$
Remove mass $\delta$ from each cycle entry and give it the next bundle on the cycle.

The inserted pairs are allowed: each recipient strictly prefers its new bundle to its old bundle, whose value was already at least its utility floor. Type masses and bundle masses are preserved. The objective increases by
$$ \delta\sum_{i=1}^r \left(v_{\theta_i}(c^{j_{i+1}}) -v_{\theta_i}(c^{j_i})\right)>0, $$
contradicting optimality. Thus the graph is acyclic.

Finally, there are $\tau+k$ equality rows and one redundancy, obtained by summing either family of conservation equations. An extreme point therefore has at most $\tau+k-1$ positive variables. $\square$

A denominator property

If the type masses and bundle masses are integer multiples of $1/D$, an extreme transportation solution also has all masses in this grid.

To see this, form the bipartite graph of its positive variables, with types on one side and bundles on the other. An undirected cycle would give a nonzero alternating perturbation preserving every equality, contradicting extremality. Thus this graph is a forest.

At a leaf, its unique incident variable equals the remaining marginal mass. Solve that variable, subtract it from the neighboring marginal, and remove the leaf. Induction shows that every variable is an integer multiple of $1/D$.

Thus this rematching operation need not introduce finer denominators.

Sources and support compression

An acyclic finite directed graph has a source. For a source entry with bundle $c$, the absence of incoming edges says
$$ v_\theta(b)\ge v_\theta(c) $$
for every positive observing entry $(\theta,b)$. Equivalently,
$$ \ell_\theta\ge v_\theta(c) $$
for every type $\theta$.

One may subsequently reduce support to at most $\tau+m$ entries using the conservation-column argument in Section 1.2. This introduces no new entry, so fairness, utility floors, and acyclicity survive. Such compression need not preserve the denominator grid.

3. A rigorously safe bulk augmentation

The preceding proposition supplies a source without expanding the society. The following lemma specifies how many copies can safely be added there.

Proposition 2

Suppose a partial assignment is $\frac{2}{3}$-EFX. Let $g$ have positive residual supply, and assume
$$ \ell_\theta\ge2v_{\theta,g} $$
for every type. Let $c$ be a source bundle.

For each type define
$$ r_\theta(c,g)= \min_{h\in\operatorname{supp}(c)\cup\{g\}}v_{\theta,h}. $$
If some type values $g$ positively, set
$$ Q= \min_{\theta:v_{\theta,g}>0} \left\lfloor \frac{\frac32\ell_\theta-v_\theta(c)+r_\theta(c,g)} {v_{\theta,g}} \right\rfloor. $$
If every type values $g$ at zero, set $Q=+\infty$.

Then $Q\ge1$. For any integer $q$ with $1\le q\le Q$, adding $q$ copies of $g$ to a positive rational submass of a source entry preserves $\frac{2}{3}$-EFX, provided the required supply is available.

Proof

Because $c$ is a source,
$$ v_\theta(c)\le\ell_\theta. $$
For any type with $v_{\theta,g}>0$,
$$ \frac32\ell_\theta-v_\theta(c)+r_\theta(c,g) \ge\frac12\ell_\theta \ge v_{\theta,g}. $$
Hence each finite bound defining $Q$ is at least $1$.

For $q\ge1$, the largest value obtainable by removing one item from $c+qe_g$ is
$$ v_\theta(c)+qv_{\theta,g}-r_\theta(c,g). $$
The definition of $Q$ makes this at most $\frac32\ell_\theta$ whenever $v_{\theta,g}>0$.

When $v_{\theta,g}=0$, the minimum $r_\theta(c,g)$ is zero, and the same bound follows from
$$ v_\theta(c)\le\ell_\theta. $$

Every observing entry retains utility at least its old utility floor: unchanged entries retain their utility, and changed entries gain nonnegative value. Therefore every comparison against the new target satisfies
$$ v_\theta(b)\ge\ell_\theta \ge\frac{2}{3}v_\theta(c+qe_g-e_h). $$
Comparisons against unchanged targets remain valid for the same reason. $\square$

The quantities in this lemma are polynomially computable from the current assignment. It proves a safe augmentation, but does not prove a polynomial bound on the number of augmentations required for completion.

4. Exact EFX when all outside classes are universally zero-valued

Here the additional hypothesis is
$$ v_{\theta,g}=0 \qquad \text{for every }\theta\text{ and every }g\notin H. $$
Values inside $H$ may differ arbitrarily across types.

Theorem 3

Under this hypothesis, a complete exact EFX assignment with at most $\tau+m-1$ positive entries can be computed in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ time.

Step 1: allocate one top item to every agent mass

Process the types in any fixed order. For the current type, repeatedly choose a favorite class among the classes in $H$ with positive remaining supply. Assign one copy to mass
$$ a=\min\{\text{remaining mass of the type}, \text{remaining supply of the chosen class}\}. $$

The procedure is well-defined: remaining top supply equals remaining unassigned agent mass. Each operation finishes a type or exhausts a class. The final operation does both, so there are at most
$$ \tau+|H|-1 $$
positive entries.

Let $h^*$ be the class used in the final operation. This class had positive remaining supply when every earlier operation chose its favorite class. Consequently, every positive entry $(\theta,e_h)$ satisfies
$$ v_{\theta,h}\ge v_{\theta,h^*}. $$

Thus the final entry is universally unenvied. At this stage every bundle is a singleton, so the assignment is exact EFX.

Step 2: attach all outside supplies to descendants of the final entry

Choose a positive entry descended from the final entry, with current mass $x$ and bundle $b$. Its top item is $h^*$, and all its other items, if any, are universally zero-valued.

For an unallocated outside class $g$ with supply $s$, set
$$ q=\left\lfloor\frac{s}{x}\right\rfloor, \qquad \delta=s-qx. $$
Then $0\le\delta<x$.

Replace this entry by:

  • mass $x-\delta$ receiving $b+qe_g$;
  • if $\delta>0$, mass $\delta$ receiving $b+(q+1)e_g$.

The aggregate number of newly allocated copies is
$$ q(x-\delta)+(q+1)\delta=qx+\delta=s. $$
At most one additional positive entry is created. Repeat for every outside class, always using a positive descendant of the final entry.

Fairness proof

All utilities remain equal to the values of the top items assigned in Step 1.

A target which remains a singleton has value zero after its only item is removed. Every other target consists of $h^*$ and universally zero-valued items. Removing any item therefore leaves value at most $v_{\theta,h^*}$ to observer type $\theta$.

Every observing entry has utility at least $v_{\theta,h^*}$, by the favorite-choice argument in Step 1. Hence all comparisons satisfy exact EFX, including removals of zero-valued items and comparisons between different bundles of the same type.

Bit complexity and output size

Let $D$ clear all input mass and supply denominators. Step 1 uses minima and differences of grid masses, so all its masses are integer multiples of $1/D$.

Step 2 preserves this invariant because
$$ \delta=s-qx $$
is also an integer multiple of $1/D$. Every positive entry therefore has mass at least $1/D$. Supply conservation implies that every bundle coordinate is at most $D\sigma_g$, which has polynomial bit length.

There are at most $m-|H|$ splits, giving total support at most
$$ (\tau+|H|-1)+(m-|H|)=\tau+m-1. $$
The number of arithmetic operations and the bit length of every intermediate quantity are polynomially bounded. This completes the proof.

5. Exact EFX for two classes and arbitrary supplies

Theorem 4

Every promised instance with $m=2$ has a polynomial-time exact EFX construction, regardless of the number of types or total supply.

Proof

Because supplies are positive, $H$ is nonempty. Since total supply exceeds $1$, $H\ne G$. Thus write
$$ G=\{h,g\},\qquad H=\{h\},\qquad \sigma_h=1. $$
The common-top assumption gives
$$ v_{\theta,h}\ge v_{\theta,g} $$
for every type.

Write
$$ \sigma_g=q+\rho, \qquad q=\lfloor\sigma_g\rfloor,\qquad 0\le\rho<1. $$
For every type assign
$$ x_{\theta,e_h+qe_g}=\mu_\theta(1-\rho), $$
and, when $\rho>0$,
$$ x_{\theta,e_h+(q+1)e_g}=\mu_\theta\rho. $$
The type masses and both supplies are correct.

For observer type $\theta$, abbreviate
$$ a=v_{\theta,h},\qquad c=v_{\theta,g}. $$
Every observing bundle has value at least $a+qc$. Removing an item from the larger possible target leaves either
$$ a+qc \qquad\text{or}\qquad (q+1)c. $$
Since $a\ge c$,
$$ (q+1)c\le a+qc. $$
The smaller possible target has no larger removal value. Thus every permitted comparison satisfies exact EFX.

There are at most $2\tau$ entries, and all coordinates and masses have polynomial bit length. $\square$

6. What remains open

The transportation proposition resolves the problem of finding an unenvied bundle in a compressed assignment while preserving fairness and utility floors. It does not bound the work needed to allocate all residual positive-valued supplies.

A simple example also shows why distributing nearly identical class counts to every type is insufficient.

Take two types of mass $\frac12$ each and three classes $h,a,b$, with
$$ H=\{h\},\qquad \sigma_h=1,\quad \sigma_a=\frac12,\quad \sigma_b=K, $$
where $K\ge1$ is an integer. Values are
$$ (v_{1,h},v_{1,a},v_{1,b})=(1,1,0), $$
$$ (v_{2,h},v_{2,a},v_{2,b})=(1,0,1). $$
These satisfy the promise.

Suppose each type receives both bundles
$$ e_h+Ke_b,\qquad e_h+e_a+Ke_b $$
with mass $\frac14$ each. Conservation holds. But type $1$, observing from the first bundle, has utility $1$. Removing a zero-valued $b$ from the second leaves utility $2$, so the required inequality would be
$$ 1\ge\frac{4}{3}, $$
which fails.

The instance itself even has an exact EFX assignment: give type $1$ mass $\frac12$ of $e_h+e_a$, and type $2$ mass $\frac12$ of $e_h+2Ke_b$. For type $1$, every removal from the other bundle leaves value at most $1$, below its own value $2$. For type $2$, every removal from the other bundle leaves value at most $1$, below its own value $1+2K$. Self-comparisons follow from nonnegativity.

Thus this example refutes a uniform count-rounding shortcut, without suggesting infeasibility or proving hardness.

For arbitrary distinct valuations with positive-valued outside classes, I have neither a polynomial-time completion algorithm nor an applicable hardness reduction.
