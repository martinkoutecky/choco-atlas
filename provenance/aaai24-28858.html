The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many complete agent valuation/budget types with rational masses, finitely encoded recurring item-value vectors, epsilon, and K, consider N-clone realizations with one indivisible item assigned to one clone per micro-round and item arrival rate proportional to N. Under the greedy ratio rule and a specified population-invariant measurable tie-breaking rule, decide whether every admissible adversarial stream in the N-to-infinity, per-capita-horizon limit has limiting essential-supremum multiplicative envy at most K.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: an ordered list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(p_a\), summing to \(1\). The support size \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Type \(a\) has positive rational budget \(B_a\) and additive valuations \(v_{aj}\) for \(m\geq1\) reusable item categories. All input numbers are binary rationals, with total encoding length \(L\).
Given \(\varepsilon\in(0,1]\) and \(K\geq0\), require \(v_{aj}\in\{0\}\cup[\varepsilon,1]\), every category to be valued positively by some type, and every type to value some category positively.
For each \(N\geq2\) making every \(Np_a\) integral, create \(Np_a\) clones of type \(a\), labelled \((a,c/(Np_a))\) for \(1\leq c\leq Np_a\). Each clone retains its type's budget and valuations. One unit indivisible item arrives per micro-round; per-capita time \(h\) contains \(\lfloor Nh\rfloor\) rounds.
An adversary chooses an infinite category sequence \(s_N\) for each such \(N\), possibly using previous allocations. Admissibility requires every type's monopolistic utility to diverge for each fixed \(N\), and \(\lim_{h\to\infty}\liminf_{N\to\infty}N^{-1}\sum_{t\leq\lfloor Nh\rfloor}v_{a,s_N(t)}=\infty\) for every \(a\).
Initialize clone utilities at zero. Allocate each arriving category \(j\) irrevocably to a clone maximizing \(B_a v_{aj}/U\), then add \(v_{aj}\) to that clone's utility. A positive numerator over zero scores \(+\infty\); a zero numerator scores zero. Break ties by the lexicographically smallest clone label, using the same ordering at every population size.
At time \(h\), let \(A_i\) be clone \(i\)'s bundle and define \(E_{ij}^{N,h}=B_iU_i(A_j)/(B_jU_i(A_i))\), with positive-over-zero equal to \(+\infty\) and zero-over-zero equal to \(1\). Let \(Q_{1-\delta}^{N,h}\) be its \((1-\delta)\)-quantile over uniformly sampled ordered distinct clone pairs.
Define the limiting essential-supremum envy of a stream family by \(\mathcal E=\limsup_{h\to\infty}\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}Q_{1-\delta}^{N,h}\); population limits run through the integral realizations above.

Is deciding whether \(\mathcal E\leq K\) for every admissible adversarial stream family solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many agents, greedy allocation has asymptotic budget-adjusted multiplicative envy at most \(1+2\log(1/\varepsilon)\) under non-extreme values and divergent monopolistic utilities, with a matching unrestricted worst-case lower bound. [Yang–Liao–Kroer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.09277).
A later finite-agent treatment gives the bound \(1+2\log(1/\varepsilon)+O(n/W_i)\), where \(W_i\) is agent \(i\)'s monopolistic utility; this is a performance guarantee, not a complexity classification of the universal threshold problem. [Yang–Liao–Gao–Kroer](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02403v1#S7).
No result settling this specific population-limit threshold problem was located.

  • Interpreted finitely encoded recurring vectors as a finite rational category alphabet permitting unrestricted repeated arrivals, without prescribed frequencies.
  • Required strictly positive budgets, as in the later finite-agent formulation; imposed the non-extreme valuation domain and excluded unvalued categories and types.
  • Specified zero-utility score conventions and fixed lexicographic tie-breaking on type and normalized clone labels, following the discrete rule's fixed priority ordering.
  • Retained divergence of monopolistic utilities and required its per-capita analogue uniformly in the population limit, rather than allowing recurrence to be postponed indefinitely as population size grows.
  • Allowed adaptive stream families depending on population size; imposed no stochastic or frequency restrictions.
  • Resolved unspecified limit existence and order with population limsup, vanishing-tail quantiles, and then horizon limsup, retaining essential-supremum rather than exceptional-clone maximum envy.
  • Specified the zero-over-zero envy convention as one and the threshold domain as nonnegative binary rationals.

Result

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, its exact threshold has a finite rational formula.

For each ordered pair of types $a,b$, define
$$ R_{ab}=\left\{\frac{v_{aj}}{v_{bj}}:v_{aj}>0,\ v_{bj}>0\right\}. $$
If $R_{ab}$ is empty, set $C_{ab}=0$. Otherwise, list its distinct elements in decreasing order:
$$ \rho_1>\rho_2>\cdots>\rho_q>0, $$
and set
$$ C_{ab}=1+\sum_{\ell=2}^{q} \left(1-\frac{\rho_\ell}{\rho_{\ell-1}}\right). $$
Define
$$ C_*=\max_{a,b}C_{ab}. $$

I prove that
$$ \sup_{\text{admissible stream families}}\mathcal E=C_*, $$
and that the supremum is attained by an admissible stream family. Consequently, the answer to the universal threshold question is yes exactly when
$$ K\geq C_*. $$

The positive masses and budgets affect the dynamics, but they do not affect this exact worst-case threshold.

1. Two elementary allocation properties

Write $U_i(t)$ for clone $i$’s utility after $t$ rounds, and put
$$ M_a=\max_j v_{aj}\leq 1. $$

Every allocated item goes to a clone that values it positively: some clone has a positive score, whereas all clones assigning value zero have score zero.

Moreover, whenever an item is allocated to a clone of type $a$, that clone has minimum utility among clones of type $a$. Indeed, all those clones have the same budget and valuation for the arriving category, so maximizing their scores is equivalent to minimizing their utilities.

It follows inductively that, at every time,
$$ \max_{i:\,\operatorname{type}(i)=a}U_i - \min_{i:\,\operatorname{type}(i)=a}U_i \leq M_a\leq1. $$
To check the induction, allocating to a minimum-utility clone raises its utility by at most $M_a$; all other utilities remain unchanged.

Both properties hold under the specified tie-breaking rule.

2. A finite-catalogue pairwise upper bound

Fix a finite population, a finite prefix, and distinct clones $i,k$ of types $a,b$. Let $U_i$ denote clone $i$’s final utility in that prefix.

If $R_{ab}$ is empty, then
$$ U_i(A_k)=0, $$
because every item allocated to $k$ has positive value for type $b$ and hence zero value for type $a$.

Suppose instead that $R_{ab}$ is nonempty, and write
$$ r_{ab}=\max R_{ab}=\rho_1. $$
I will prove
$$ U_i(A_k) \leq \frac{B_b}{B_a}C_{ab}U_i+M_b r_{ab}. $$

Set
$$ X=\frac{U_i}{B_a}. $$
Along clone $k$’s allocations, let $y$ be its utility immediately before an allocation, divided by $B_b$. An allocation of category $j$ increases $y$ by
$$ \Delta y=\frac{v_{bj}}{B_b}\leq d_b, \qquad d_b=\frac{M_b}{B_b}. $$
If $v_{aj}>0$, write $\rho=v_{aj}/v_{bj}$. The greedy comparison against clone $i$ gives
$$ \rho y\leq \frac{U_i(t-1)}{B_a}\leq X. $$
This remains valid when a utility is zero: if clone $i$ has zero utility and positive value for the category, clone $k$ can win only if its utility is also zero.

Define the nonincreasing function
$$ f_X(y)= \max\left(\{\,\rho\in R_{ab}:\rho y\leq X\,\}\cup\{0\}\right). $$
For every allocation to $k$, its contribution to $U_i(A_k)/B_b$ is at most
$$ f_X(y)\Delta y. $$
This also covers categories with $v_{aj}=0$.

Let $y_0=0<y_1<\cdots<y_s$ be clone $k$’s successive normalized utilities. Since $f_X$ is nonincreasing and every increment is at most $d_b$,
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{\nu=1}^{s}f_X(y_{\nu-1})(y_\nu-y_{\nu-1}) -\int_0^{y_s}f_X(y)\,dy &\leq \sum_{\nu=1}^{s}(y_\nu-y_{\nu-1}) \bigl(f_X(y_{\nu-1})-f_X(y_\nu)\bigr)\\ &\leq d_b\bigl(f_X(0)-f_X(y_s)\bigr)\\ &\leq d_b r_{ab}. \end{aligned} $$

For $X>0$, direct integration gives
$$ \begin{aligned} \int_0^\infty f_X(y)\,dy &= \rho_1\frac{X}{\rho_1} +\sum_{\ell=2}^{q} \rho_\ell\left(\frac{X}{\rho_\ell} -\frac{X}{\rho_{\ell-1}}\right)\\ &=XC_{ab}. \end{aligned} $$
For $X=0$, the integral is zero, so the same identity holds.

Combining these facts proves the claimed inequality. In particular, whenever $U_i>0$,
$$ E_{ik} \leq C_{ab}+ \frac{B_aM_b r_{ab}}{B_bU_i}. $$

Thus $C_{ab}$ is a catalogue-specific asymptotic upper bound, with an error depending on the envious clone’s utility.

3. Admissibility makes the error vanish in the required order of limits

For empty $R_{ab}$, also set $r_{ab}=0$. Define
$$ D_a=\sum_b p_b\frac{B_b}{B_a}C_{ab}, \qquad G_a=\sum_b p_bM_b r_{ab}. $$
Since $R_{aa}=\{1\}$, we have $C_{aa}=1$, and therefore
$$ D_a\geq p_a>0. $$

Let $W_a^{N,h}$ be type $a$’s monopolistic utility through time $h$. For any clone $i$ of type $a$, decomposition over the allocated bundles gives
$$ W_a^{N,h} = U_i+\sum_{k\ne i}U_i(A_k). $$
Applying the preceding pairwise inequality and using the $Np_b$ clones of each type yields
$$ W_a^{N,h} \leq ND_aU_i+NG_a. $$
Here the coefficient of $U_i$ is exactly $ND_a$: the own-bundle coefficient $1$ replaces the omitted same-type coefficient $C_{aa}=1$.

Consequently, every clone of type $a$ satisfies
$$ U_i\geq \frac{W_a^{N,h}/N-G_a}{D_a}. $$

The population admissibility hypothesis now implies
$$ \lim_{h\to\infty}\liminf_{N\to\infty} \min_i U_i^{N,h}=\infty. $$
Indeed, the displayed lower bound applies to every clone, and there are only finitely many types.

Let
$$ A_*=\max_{a,b}\frac{B_aM_b r_{ab}}{B_b}. $$
At times and populations where all utilities are positive, every distinct pair therefore satisfies
$$ E_{ik}^{N,h}\leq C_*+\frac{A_*}{\min_iU_i^{N,h}}. $$
For every positive error tolerance, the preceding utility-divergence statement bounds this error eventually in $N$, for every sufficiently large $h$. The same bound then applies to every quantile, and hence to the specified iterated limits.

Thus, for every admissible family,
$$ \mathcal E\leq C_*. $$

This argument respects the given order of limits; it does not interchange population and time limits.

4. A uniform rounding lemma for homogeneous blocks

To prove attainability, we need a precise description of a block consisting of one repeated category.

Fix category $j$. Its participating clones are those whose types satisfy $v_{cj}>0$. Define their loads by
$$ z_i=\frac{U_i}{B_cv_{cj}}. $$
During this block, greedy allocation always chooses a minimum-load participating clone. Receiving one item increases that clone’s load by $1/B_c$.

For a prescribed level $z\geq0$, the exact number of allocations needed to make every participating load at least $z$ is
$$ T(z)= \sum_{i:\,v_{\operatorname{type}(i),j}>0} \left\lceil \left(B_{\operatorname{type}(i)}z -\frac{U_i}{v_{\operatorname{type}(i),j}}\right)_+ \right\rceil, $$
where $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$.

To justify this identity, while any participating clone is below $z$, a minimum-load clone is below $z$. Once a clone reaches $z$, it receives no further item until all participating clones reach $z$. Thus each clone receives exactly the number of items appearing in its summand.

At that hitting time, a participating clone’s utility differs from
$$ \max\{U_i,B_cv_{cj}z\} $$
by less than or equal to $v_{cj}\leq1$.

The following consequence controls scheduled block lengths uniformly in population size. Put
$$ p_{\min}=\min_c p_c>0. $$
Suppose the initial utilities satisfy, for every clone of type $c$,
$$ |U_i-Ru_c|\leq D, $$
where $R>0$ and $u_c\geq0$. For a fixed $z_0\geq0$, define
$$ u'_c= \begin{cases} \max\{u_c,B_cv_{cj}z_0\},&v_{cj}>0,\\ u_c,&v_{cj}=0, \end{cases} $$
and
$$ S=\sum_{c:\,v_{cj}>0} p_c\frac{u'_c-u_c}{v_{cj}}. $$

Run a block of $n$ items with
$$ |n-NRS|\leq1. $$
Then every resulting clone utility satisfies
$$ |U_i^{\mathrm{end}}-Ru'_c| \leq D+3+\frac{D/\varepsilon+2}{p_{\min}}. $$

Here is the error calculation. Apply the hitting-time identity at level $Rz_0$. Each clone’s required item count differs from its ideal count by at most
$$ D/v_{cj}+1\leq D/\varepsilon+1. $$
Therefore
$$ |T(Rz_0)-NRS|\leq N(D/\varepsilon+1), $$
and
$$ |n-T(Rz_0)|\leq N(D/\varepsilon+1)+1. $$
At the hitting time, the utility error is at most $D+1$.

Between that hitting time and the scheduled endpoint, the average utility change within type $c$ is at most
$$ \frac{|n-T(Rz_0)|}{Np_c}. $$
At both endpoints, utilities within that type have spread at most $1$, so an individual utility change exceeds the average change by at most $2$. These inequalities give the stated bound. Types not participating in the category retain their utilities.

In particular, a fixed finite sequence of such blocks transforms an initial error $D$ into an error at most
$$ A(D+1), $$
for some constant $A$ depending only on the fixed society and the block sequence, uniformly in $N$ and $R$.

5. A finite template attaining a pair value

Choose types $a,b$ with $R_{ab}$ nonempty. Order its ratios as before, and choose categories $j_\ell$ realizing them:
$$ \frac{v_{a,j_\ell}}{v_{b,j_\ell}}=\rho_\ell. $$

Define ideal per-clone utilities recursively, starting from $u_c^0=0$:
$$ u_c^\ell= \begin{cases} \displaystyle \max\left\{u_c^{\ell-1}, \frac{B_cv_{c,j_\ell}}{v_{a,j_\ell}}\right\}, &v_{c,j_\ell}>0,\\ u_c^{\ell-1},&v_{c,j_\ell}=0. \end{cases} $$
Define the per-capita length of phase $\ell$ by
$$ S_\ell= \sum_{c:\,v_{c,j_\ell}>0} p_c\frac{u_c^\ell-u_c^{\ell-1}}{v_{c,j_\ell}}. $$
All these quantities are rational. Furthermore,
$$ u_a^\ell=B_a, \qquad u_b^\ell=\frac{B_b}{\rho_\ell} \qquad (1\leq\ell\leq q). $$
The first identity holds because every phase’s threshold for type $a$ is $B_a$. The second holds because the ratios decrease, so the thresholds for type $b$ increase.

In particular, every $S_\ell$ is positive: type $b$ gains utility in every phase.

At scale $R$, run category $j_\ell$ for approximately $NRS_\ell$ rounds. The rounding lemma applies with threshold
$$ z_0=\frac{1}{v_{a,j_\ell}}. $$
It shows that actual utilities track $Ru_c^\ell$, up to a uniformly bounded multiple of the initial error plus one.

The ideal value that type $a$ assigns to a type $b$ clone’s template bundle is
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{\ell=1}^{q} \rho_\ell(u_b^\ell-u_b^{\ell-1}) &= B_b+ \sum_{\ell=2}^{q} \rho_\ell \left(\frac{B_b}{\rho_\ell} -\frac{B_b}{\rho_{\ell-1}}\right)\\ &=B_bC_{ab}. \end{aligned} $$
Its ideal budget-adjusted envy is therefore
$$ \frac{B_a(B_bC_{ab})}{B_bB_a}=C_{ab}. $$

The other types have been included in every phase length $S_\ell$. Their allocations are therefore fully accounted for.

6. Repeating the template gives an admissible attaining family

Choose a pair $a,b$ attaining $C_*$. Such a pair has nonempty $R_{ab}$ because $C_{aa}=1$.

Let
$$ S=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}S_\ell>0. $$
We construct a stream for every integral population realization using the same per-capita schedule.

Set $h_0=0$. Stage $r$ consists of:

  • A cycle through all $m$ categories, with per-capita length $1$ for each category.
  • The template from Section 5, at an integer scale $R_r$ chosen so that

$$ R_r\geq r(h_{r-1}+m+1)^2. $$

Thus
$$ h_r=h_{r-1}+m+R_rS. $$
A scheduled block between per-capita times $x$ and $y$ occupies exactly the rounds after $\lfloor Nx\rfloor$ through $\lfloor Ny\rfloor$. Its length differs from $N(y-x)$ by less than $1$, as required by the rounding lemma. Checkpoint $h_r$ therefore coincides exactly with the specified observation time.

Before stage $r$’s template, every clone of type $c$ has utility at most
$$ \frac{h_{r-1}+m}{p_c}+1. $$
Indeed, total allocated utility is at most the number of elapsed rounds, and the within-type utility spread is at most $1$.

Applying the rounding lemma through the fixed template gives, uniformly over all integral populations and all clones of the relevant types,
$$ U_i^{N,h_r} = R_rB_a+O(h_{r-1}+m+1) \qquad (\operatorname{type}(i)=a), $$
and, at the end of template phase $\ell$,
$$ U_k^\ell = R_r\frac{B_b}{\rho_\ell} +O(h_{r-1}+m+1) \qquad (\operatorname{type}(k)=b). $$
The constants here depend only on the society and template.

Every item previously allocated to a type $b$ clone has type $b$ value at least $\varepsilon$. Its previous bundle therefore has type $a$ value at most its previous own utility divided by $\varepsilon$, which is also $O(h_{r-1}+m+1)$.

During phase $\ell$, the type $a$ value of clone $k$’s new items is exactly $\rho_\ell$ times its utility increase. The calculation in Section 5 consequently gives
$$ U_i(A_k) = R_rB_bC_*+O(h_{r-1}+m+1). $$
It follows that, for all distinct pairs of the chosen types,
$$ E_{ik}^{N,h_r} = C_*+ O\left(\frac{h_{r-1}+m+1}{R_r}\right). $$
The error tends to zero with $r$, uniformly in $N$.

These pairs have limiting positive probability under uniform sampling of ordered distinct clones. Their probability tends to $p_ap_b$ if $a\ne b$, and to $p_a^2$ if $a=b$. Therefore, for every sufficiently small fixed $\delta>0$,
$$ \limsup_{N\to\infty}Q_{1-\delta}^{N,h_r} \geq C_*-o_r(1). $$
Taking the remaining limits proves
$$ \mathcal E\geq C_*. $$

Finally, the family is admissible. Each stage’s initial cycle contains exactly $N$ copies of every category. Thus every completed cycle contributes
$$ N\sum_jv_{cj}>0 $$
to type $c$’s monopolistic utility. Infinitely many cycles occur, giving divergence for every fixed $N$. After any prescribed number of cycles, their per-capita contribution is also uniform in $N$, proving the required population-limit divergence.

Combined with Section 3, this establishes
$$ \mathcal E=C_* $$
for the constructed admissible family.

7. Polynomial-time computation

For each pair of types, form at most $m$ positive rational ratios, sort them, remove duplicates, and calculate $C_{ab}$. Then compare their maximum with $K$.

These are polynomially many rational arithmetic operations and comparisons. Each ratio has bit length polynomial in $L$; summing at most $m$ rational terms still produces a rational of bit length polynomial in $m$ and $L$. Thus the entire computation takes polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$.

As a consistency check, for nonempty $R_{ab}$,
$$ \begin{aligned} C_{ab} &\leq 1+\sum_{\ell=2}^{q} \log\frac{\rho_{\ell-1}}{\rho_\ell}\\ &= 1+\log\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_q}\\ &\leq 1+2\log\frac1\varepsilon. \end{aligned} $$
The first inequality uses $1-x\leq-\log x$, and the last uses
$$ \varepsilon\leq\rho_\ell\leq1/\varepsilon. $$
This agrees with the unrestricted logarithmic performance bound in [Yang–Liao–Gao–Kroer, Theorem 7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02403v1#S7). The finite rational formula above supplies the exact threshold for the fixed reusable catalogue.
