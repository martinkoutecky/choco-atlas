The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite role-type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), a symmetric acceptable relation on distinct types, and weak incomplete rankings \(\succeq_t\) satisfying the paper's narcissistic, single-peaked, and tie-sensitive single-crossing promises under a common order \(\rhd\), decide whether there exists a symmetric mass matching \(x_{tu}=x_{ut}\ge 0\) with \(x_{tt}=0\) and \(\sum_u x_{tu}\le \mu_t\), such that for every mutually acceptable pair \(t,u\), it is not the case that both \(D_t(u)>0\) and \(D_u(t)>0\), where \(r_t=\mu_t-\sum_v x_{tv}\) and \(D_t(u)=r_t+\sum_{v:u\succ_t v}x_{tv}\); output \(x\) when one exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(T\), the list of \(\tau\) role types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is support size, not the number of possible types; the alternative set is also \(T\), so \(m=\tau\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Input includes a symmetric acceptability graph \(G=(T,E)\) without loops or isolated vertices, weak orders \(\succeq_t\) on \(A_t=\{t\}\cup N_G(t)\), and a common linear order \(\rhd\). Ties and incompleteness are allowed; every acceptable partner is strictly better than remaining unmatched.
Narcissism requires \(t\succ_t u\) for every \(u\in N_G(t)\). Single-peakedness requires, for distinct \(a,b,c\in A_t\), that \(a\rhd b\rhd c\) and \(a\succ_t b\) imply \(b\succeq_t c\).
Tie-sensitive single-crossing means: for every distinct \(a,b\in T\), restrict \(\rhd\) to types ranking both; their comparisons form three consecutive blocks, preferring \(a\) strictly, tying, and preferring \(b\) strictly, or the reverse. Empty blocks are permitted and omitted.
A mass matching has real entries \(x_{tu}=x_{ut}\geq0\), with \(x_{tt}=0\), \(x_{tu}=0\) when \(\{t,u\}\notin E\), and \(\sum_u x_{tu}\leq\mu_t\). Define \(r_t=\mu_t-\sum_v x_{tv}\) and \(D_t(u)=r_t+\sum_{v\in N_G(t):\,u\succ_t v}x_{tv}\).
It is stable if every \(\{t,u\}\in E\) satisfies \(D_t(u)=0\) or \(D_u(t)=0\); ties do not create strict improvements. Partial matchings are permitted.

Is deciding whether such a stable mass matching exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? In the polynomial-time case, can one also produce a rational stable matching in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?

Discrete weakly stable roommates existence is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even with all three structural restrictions ([Bredereck et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-020-09470-x)).
For equal capacities, ordinally stable fractional roommates matchings always exist and can be found in quadratic time, including ties ([Chen–Roy–Sorge](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0013.pdf)).
For arbitrary capacities with strict rankings, non-bipartite stable allocation has an algorithm using \(O(|E|\log\tau)\) operations with high probability; it also includes edge capacities ([Dean–Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf)). Neither cited formulation states the arbitrary-mass problem with ties and these promises.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Removed zero-mass types so that the supplied type set is exactly the positive-mass support, retaining total mass one.
  • Required at least one acceptable distinct partner per supported type, carrying over the exclusion of isolated vertices.
  • Included each type itself as its uniquely best ranked alternative for narcissism, while retaining the prescribed prohibition on within-type matching.
  • Required zero matching mass on unacceptable pairs, as discrete matchings use only acceptability edges.
  • Restricted single-peaked comparisons to ranked alternatives and crossing comparisons to types ranking both alternatives.
  • Interpreted tie-sensitive single-crossing as ordered comparison blocks even when the tie block is empty, consistent with its intended equivalence to ordinary single-crossing without ties.
  • Took the common structural order as supplied input and the structural restrictions as promises.
  • Allowed arbitrary real matching masses and requested rational output in the algorithmic case; retained partial matching and strict improvement over being unmatched.
  • Identified the number of alternatives with support size and imposed no parity condition on support size, since types represent divisible masses rather than individual agents.

Result

The existence problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). In fact, every valid instance has a stable mass matching, and a rational one can be constructed in deterministic time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The conclusion holds for arbitrary symmetric acceptability graphs and weak preference orders. Narcissism, single-peakedness, and single-crossing are unnecessary.

1. Refining ties preserves the required stability

For each type \(t\), restrict its preference order to \(N_G(t)\), and break ties arbitrarily to obtain a strict linear order \(\succ'_t\). This preserves every original strict comparison:

\(u\succ_t v\Longrightarrow u\succ'_t v.\)

The alternative \(t\) itself can be omitted because self-matching is forbidden and its position never enters \(D_t(u)\).

For any feasible mass matching \(x\), define

\(D'_t(u)=r_t+\sum_{v\in N_G(t):\,u\succ'_t v}x_{tv}.\)

All summands are nonnegative, and every summand occurring in \(D_t(u)\) also occurs in \(D'_t(u)\). Consequently,

\(0\leq D_t(u)\leq D'_t(u).\)

Thus, if \(x\) is stable for the refined strict orders, then for every edge \(\{t,u\}\), some endpoint has \(D'=0\), and the same endpoint has \(D=0\). Therefore \(x\) is stable for the original weak orders.

The refined orders need not retain the three structural restrictions: the theorem used next permits arbitrary strict preferences.

2. The strict stable-allocation theorem

We use the following established result, stated with unused capacity explicit.

Stable-allocation theorem. Let \(H=(V,F)\) be a finite undirected graph, with positive vertex capacities \(b_v\), positive edge capacities \(c_{vw}\), and strict preference orders over neighbors. There exists a symmetric allocation \(y\) satisfying

\(0\leq y_{vw}\leq c_{vw},\qquad \sum_w y_{vw}\leq b_v,\)

such that, writing

\(\rho_v=b_v-\sum_w y_{vw},\qquad B_v(w)=\rho_v+\sum_{z:\,w\succ'_v z}y_{vz},\)

every edge satisfies

\(y_{vw}<c_{vw}\Longrightarrow B_v(w)=0\ \text{or}\ B_w(v)=0.\)

A deterministic algorithm uses polynomially many arithmetic operations.

This is the non-bipartite result in [Dean–Munshi, Section 5, Lemmas 13–15](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf). Their least-preferred auxiliary loops represent unused capacity. The paragraph following Lemma 15 describes deterministic explicit rotation comparisons and states that the resulting running time remains strongly polynomial; hashing supplies the faster randomized bound. Numerical updates use addition, subtraction, minima, and halving.

Here \(B_v(w)=0\) means precisely that \(v\) has no unused capacity and assigns no positive amount to a neighbor worse than \(w\): this follows because all terms defining \(B_v(w)\) are nonnegative. Auxiliary loops are outside options, not the narcissistically preferred self-alternatives in our input.

3. Reduction and proof of existence

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=p_t/q_t\), with positive integers \(p_t,q_t\). Set

\(Q=\prod_{t\in T}q_t,\qquad b_t=Q\mu_t.\)

Every \(b_t\) is a positive integer, and

\(\sum_{t\in T}b_t=Q.\)

Apply the theorem to \(G\), the refined strict orders, vertex capacities \(b_t\), and edge capacities

\(c_{tu}=\min\{b_t,b_u\}.\)

All hypotheses hold: the graph is finite and undirected, the capacities are positive, and the refined rankings are strict neighbor orders. Obtain a stable allocation \(y\).

We show that every original edge has \(B_t(u)=0\) or \(B_u(t)=0\), including edges saturated at their artificial capacity.

Fix \(\{t,u\}\in E\).

  • If \(y_{tu}<c_{tu}\), this follows from the theorem.
  • If \(y_{tu}=c_{tu}\), choose an endpoint \(t\) with \(b_t=c_{tu}\), interchanging the endpoint names if necessary. Feasibility gives

\(b_t=y_{tu}\leq\sum_v y_{tv}\leq b_t.\)

Hence \(\rho_t=0\). Moreover,

\(\sum_{v\neq u}y_{tv}=\sum_v y_{tv}-y_{tu}=0.\)

Nonnegativity implies \(y_{tv}=0\) for every \(v\neq u\). Since \(u\not\succ'_t u\), every term in \(B_t(u)\) is zero. Thus \(B_t(u)=0\).

Now define the required matching by

\(x_{tu}=y_{tu}/Q\quad\text{for }\{t,u\}\in E,\)

and set all other entries, including diagonal entries, to zero. Symmetry and nonnegativity are inherited from \(y\), and

\(\sum_u x_{tu}\leq b_t/Q=\mu_t.\)

Its residual mass is \(r_t=\rho_t/Q\). Therefore, using the original weak preferences,

\(D_t(u)=\frac{1}{Q}\left(\rho_t+\sum_{v:\,u\succ_t v}y_{tv}\right)\leq \frac{B_t(u)}{Q}.\)

For every edge, at least one endpoint has \(B=0\), so that endpoint also has \(D=0\). This proves the required stability.

4. Polynomial bit complexity and rational output

It remains to justify exact computation, rather than merely a polynomial arithmetic-operation count.

The common denominator has polynomial encoding length:

\(\log_2 Q=\sum_t\log_2 q_t\leq L.\)

Thus \(Q\), the integers \(b_t\), and the integers \(c_{tu}\) can all be computed in polynomial time. Their values are at most \(Q\).

Use the deterministic stable-allocation algorithm from Section 2, with explicit rotation comparisons. Let \(K\) be its number of elementary numerical operations. The theorem gives

\(K\leq \operatorname{poly}(\tau,|E|).\)

After integer initialization, addition, subtraction, minima, and halving produce only dyadic rationals. After at most \(K\) operations, every denominator divides \(2^K\): addition and subtraction take the larger denominator exponent, and halving increases it by one.

For completeness, intermediate numerators also have polynomial bit length. Let \(M_0\geq1\) bound the absolute values at initialization. Auxiliary capacities are sums of input capacities, so

\(\log_2 M_0=O(L+\log\tau).\)

Each addition or subtraction increases the largest absolute value by at most a factor of two; minima and halving do not increase it. Therefore every intermediate value has absolute value at most \(2^K M_0\). Written over denominator \(2^K\), its numerator has absolute value at most \(2^{2K}M_0\), and hence bit length

\(O(L+\log\tau+K).\)

All arithmetic operations and comparisons can consequently be performed exactly in polynomial bit time. Finally, division by \(Q\) increases the encoding length by only \(O(L)\).

Because \(|E|\leq\tau(\tau-1)/2\) and \(m=\tau\), the entire construction, including writing the rational matching matrix, takes time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

5. Conclusion

Every promised instance is a YES-instance, and a rational witness is deterministically computable in polynomial time. Discrete roommates hardness does not transfer: it concerns indivisible matchings, whereas the allocation theorem permits fractional mass.
