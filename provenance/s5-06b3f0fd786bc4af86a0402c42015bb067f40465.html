Theorem 1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(t\) equally weighted binary issues and a finite support \(S\subseteq\{-1,+1\}^t\) of complete ballot types with positive rational masses \(\mu_v\) summing to \(1\), where every issue has a strict population majority, decide whether there exists a Condorcet-winning proposal \(p\in\{-1,+1\}^t\): whether, for every challenger \(q\in\{-1,+1\}^t\), \(\sum_{v\in S}\mu_v\,\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^t v_j(p_j-q_j)\right)\geq 0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=t\geq1\) be the number of equally weighted binary issues. A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(S\subseteq\{-1,+1\}^{m}\) of \(\tau=|S|\) distinct complete ballot types, each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_v\), with \(\sum_{v\in S}\mu_v=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. Require \(\sum_{v\in S}\mu_v v_j\neq0\) for every issue \(j\).
A type prefers whichever proposal agrees with its ballot on more issues and abstains when the agreement counts tie. Define \(M(p,q)=\sum_{v\in S}\mu_v\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_j(p_j-q_j)\right)\), with \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0\). A Condorcet winner is a proposal \(p\in\{-1,+1\}^{m}\) satisfying \(M(p,q)\geq0\) for every \(q\in\{-1,+1\}^{m}\); collective ties count as nonlosses.
Is deciding whether such a Condorcet winner exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions?
For explicit finite electorates, existence is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hard even with equally weighted issues and an odd number of voters. [Verified source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2025/pdfs/p179.pdf)
For the rational-mass problem stated here, \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-completeness already follows from the discrete result and the strict-majority condition; this is an encoding consequence, rather than a separately verified literature claim.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Require at least one issue and nonempty support, so the equal issue weights and probability distribution are defined.
  • Use m=t to express the required running-time convention, and define L as the total input bit length.
  • Specify polynomial-time many-one reductions to distinguish NP-hardness from the anchor's coNP-hardness.

1. Strict issue majorities force a unique candidate

For every issue \(\jmath\), define its signed majority mass by

\(\displaystyle a_\jmath=\sum_{v\in S}\mu_vv_\jmath.\)

By hypothesis, \(a_\jmath\neq0\). Define the issue-wise majority proposal \(b\) by \(b_\jmath=\operatorname{sgn}(a_\jmath)\).

Lemma. Every Condorcet winner equals \(b\).

Proof. Suppose \(p_\jmath\neq b_\jmath\) for some issue \(\jmath\), and obtain \(q\) by flipping only coordinate \(\jmath\) of \(p\). For every ballot \(v\),

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\frac1m\sum_{k=1}^{m}v_k(p_k-q_k)\right)=\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\frac{2}{m}v_\jmath p_\jmath\right)=v_\jmath p_\jmath.\)

Therefore,

\(\displaystyle M(p,q)=p_\jmath a_\jmath=-|a_\jmath|<0.\)

Thus \(p\) is not a Condorcet winner. Every proposal different from \(b\) differs on some coordinate, proving the lemma.

In particular, a Condorcet winner exists exactly when \(b\) is one. The lemma establishes uniqueness of a possible winner, not its existence.

2. Nonexistence has a polynomially verifiable certificate

For a proposal \(q\), let \(D(q)=\{\jmath:q_\jmath\neq b_\jmath\}\). Since differing binary coordinates satisfy \(q_\jmath=-b_\jmath\),

\(\displaystyle M(b,q)=\sum_{v\in S}\mu_v\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\sum_{\jmath\in D(q)}v_\jmath b_\jmath\right).\)

Hence nonexistence is equivalent to the existence of an \(m\)-bit proposal \(q\) satisfying \(M(b,q)<0\).

This certificate can be checked in polynomial time:

  • Compute the issue majorities and \(b\).
  • For each supported ballot, compute the integer sum inside the sign.
  • Compute the resulting rational margin exactly and check whether it is negative.

To justify the bit complexity, write each mass as \(\mu_v=r_v/d_v\), with integers encoded in binary, and take \(D=\prod_{v\in S}d_v\). The bit length of \(D\) is at most the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence is bounded by \(L\). The sign of the margin equals the sign of the integer

\(\displaystyle \sum_{v\in S}r_v(D/d_v)\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\sum_{\jmath\in D(q)}v_\jmath b_\jmath\right),\)

which can be computed using polynomially many operations on integers of polynomial bit length.

Thus \(\mathcal E\in\mathrm{coNP}\). This verification uses only the listed support; it does not expand rational masses into individual voters.

For a formal language rather than a promise problem, declare malformed inputs to be no-instances. Distinctness, positivity, normalization, and strict issue majorities are all checkable in polynomial time, so this convention preserves membership in \(\mathrm{coNP}\).

3. Hardness transfers to distinct support and dyadic masses

Theorem 1 of Baharav, Constantinescu, and Wattenhofer states that Condorcet-winner existence is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hard for equally weighted binary issues and an odd number of explicit voters. Their definition permits collective ties and voter abstentions, matching this task. [Verified source, Section 3](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2025/pdfs/p179.pdf).

Here is an exact reduction to the present encoding.

Take such an electorate with odd size \(n\), ballots \(v^{(1)},\ldots,v^{(n)}\), and \(m\) issues. Let \(c_v\) be the multiplicity of ballot \(v\). Choose the smallest power of two \(N\) satisfying \(N\geq n\), and fix any ballot \(u\in\{-1,+1\}^{m}\).

Assign masses by

\(\displaystyle \mu_v=\frac{c_v}{N}+\frac{N-n}{2N}\left(\mathbf1_{\{v=u\}}+\mathbf1_{\{v=-u\}}\right),\)

where an absent original ballot has \(c_v=0\). Merge coincident types and omit zero-mass types.

These masses are dyadic and nonnegative. Every listed type has positive mass, and their total is

\(\displaystyle \frac nN+2\frac{N-n}{2N}=1.\)

The two added antipodal ballots cancel in every issue majority:

\(\displaystyle \sum_v\mu_vv_\jmath=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{n}v^{(i)}_\jmath.\)

The sum on the right is nonzero because it is a sum of an odd number of signs. Thus the required strict-majority condition holds.

They also cancel in every pairwise margin: the preference expression for \(-u\) is the negative of that for \(u\), and the sign function is odd, including at zero. Consequently, for every \(p,q\),

\(\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{new}}(p,q)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\frac1m\sum_{\jmath=1}^{m}v^{(i)}_\jmath(p_\jmath-q_\jmath)\right).\)

This is a positive scalar multiple of the original normalized margin. Every loss, win, and collective tie is therefore preserved exactly, so Condorcet-winner existence is preserved.

The output has at most \(n+2\) distinct types. Its masses have denominators \(2N\) and bit lengths \(O(\log n)\); its total encoding length is polynomial in the explicit electorate’s input length. This establishes the required polynomial-time many-one reduction.

Together with Section 2, it proves \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-completeness.
