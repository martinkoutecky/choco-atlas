The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given hospitals \(H\), rational per-capita quotas \(q_h\), and a finite support \(T\) of single-applicant and couple types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), complete application preferences, and hospital priority classes, compute a stable mass assignment or determine that none exists. Assignment variables \(x_{t,e}\) give the mass of type \(t\) assigned to application \(e\); each type's assigned and unmatched mass totals \(\mu_t\), and hospital loads respect \(q_h\). Stability follows the paper's quota-responsive blocking rule: no positive mass can move from an unmatched or less-preferred assignment to a preferred acceptable application that the involved hospital or hospitals can admit, including the contribution-two capacity use of a combined application. What is the complexity as a function of \(|H|\), \(|T|\), and input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) single-applicant or ordered-couple types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(m=|H|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length. Hospital quotas \(q_h\) are nonnegative binary rationals, measured in applicant positions per unit society mass; one unit of couple mass represents one pair.
Each single type strictly ranks every hospital; each couple type strictly ranks every ordered pair in \(H^2\), including repeated hospitals. All these applications are mutually acceptable and preferred to being wholly unmatched; partial matching of a couple is excluded.
Each hospital ranks individual member-type labels by priority classes, refined by an input strict order within each class. It ranks applications by their worst participating member; applications sharing that member are ordered by their type's application preference. Copies of a type share these rankings.
Let \(E\) be the resulting contracts \(a=(t,e)\), and \(V=T\cup H\). A contract has contribution \(r_a(t)=1\), and \(r_a(h)\) equals the number of its members assigned to \(h\), hence is two for a combined application; all other contributions are zero. Write \(b_t=\mu_t\) and \(b_h=q_h\).
An assignment consists of nonnegative rational contract masses \(x_a\) and unmatched masses \(u_t\), satisfying \(\sum_{a:\,a=(t,e)}x_a+u_t=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_a r_a(h)x_a\le q_h\).
Fractional quota-responsive stability means that every contract \(a\) has a participant \(v\) with \(\sum_{f:\,r_f(v)>0,\ f\succeq_v a}r_f(v)x_f=b_v\), where \(\succeq_v\) includes equality. Contract masses are divisible, including combined applications.

Can a stable mass assignment be computed, or nonexistence reported, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this complete-preference mass problem \(\mathrm{PPAD}\)-hard?

For discrete couples matching without combined applications, stable matchings correspond bijectively to integral unit-contribution stable hypergraph allocations ([Biró–Fleiner–Irving](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10472-015-9491-5)).
Stable fractional allocations with contributions always exist ([Biró–Fleiner](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disopt.2015.02.002)); computing fractional HRC solutions allowing incomplete acceptable lists is \(\mathrm{PPAD}\)-complete ([Csáji](https://egres.elte.hu/tr/egres-21-11.pdf)).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted complete application preferences literally: every hospital and every ordered hospital pair is acceptable and ranked above being unmatched.
  • Hospital priority-class ties are resolved by an input strict refinement over member-type labels; application ties sharing the same weakest member follow the applicant type's preferences.
  • Specified the divisible quota-responsive blocking rule through weighted saturation by weakly better contracts, including contribution-two combined applications.
  • Mass normalization counts singles and couples as society units; hospital quotas count individual positions.
  • Allowed nonnegative rational quotas and excluded partially matched couples, since no outside-option hospital was supplied.
  • Retained combined applications as explicitly requested by the continuous question, although the anchor's bijection excludes them.
  • Used polynomial-time computation versus PPAD-hardness as the two-sided target for this total fractional search problem.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{PPAD}\)-complete. Every valid instance has a rational stable mass assignment, so reporting nonexistence is never necessary.

The key point is that incomplete preference lists can be completed using private fallback hospitals. The construction below preserves solutions under restriction, handles all added combined applications, and uses only dyadic masses and quotas.

1. Existence and membership in \(\mathrm{PPAD}\)

Let \(A\) be the contribution matrix, with \(A_{va}=r_a(v)\). Feasibility of contract masses is precisely

\(x\geq 0,\qquad Ax\leq b.\)

Unmatched masses are then determined by \(u_t=\mu_t-\sum_{a:\,a=(t,e)}x_a\).

First remove every contract incident with a zero-quota hospital. Feasibility forces its mass to zero, and that hospital witnesses its stability: its required prefix sum and quota both equal zero. Remove the zero-quota rows as well. Thus all remaining entries of \(b\) are positive. If no contracts remain, the wholly unmatched assignment is stable.

For the remaining instance, introduce slack variables \(s\) and write

\(s+Ax=b,\qquad s,x\geq 0.\)

This feasible set is nonempty and bounded. Indeed, \(s_v\leq b_v\), and every contract column contains its type coefficient \(1\), so \(x_a\leq\mu_t\).

We apply the following standard formulation of Scarf’s lemma. Suppose \(B=[I\mid A]\), the set \(\{z\geq0:Bz=b\}\) is bounded, and an ordinal matrix \(C\) satisfies

\(C_{ii}\leq C_{ik}\leq C_{ij}\)

whenever \(i,j\) index identity columns, \(i\neq j\), and \(k\) indexes another column. Then there is a feasible set of columns \(J\) such that every column \(k\) has a row \(i\) with \(C_{ik}\leq C_{ij}\) for every \(j\in J\). Computing such a set belongs to \(\mathrm{PPAD}\). These are Theorems 5.1–5.2 of [Kintali et al., *Reducibility Among Fractional Stability Problems*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.1435).

Here is an explicit ordinal matrix satisfying those hypotheses. In row \(v\):

  • Give its own slack column value \(0\).
  • Give incident contract columns distinct positive values increasing with \(v\)’s preference.
  • Give nonincident contract columns values above all incident contract values.
  • Give other slack columns values above all contract values.

All values can be integers of polynomial bit length.

Take a feasible solution supported on the set \(J\) supplied by Scarf’s lemma. Consider any contract \(a\), and a row \(v\) witnessing its ordinal domination.

That row must be incident with \(a\). Otherwise, if \(s_v>0\), the own-slack column contradicts domination. If \(s_v=0\), positivity of \(b_v\) forces some incident contract \(f\) to have \(x_f>0\); its ordinal value is below that of the nonincident column \(a\), again contradicting domination.

Because \(a\) is incident with \(v\), domination also forces \(s_v=0\). Every positively allocated incident contract \(f\) satisfies \(f\succeq_v a\). Consequently,

\(\sum_{f:\,r_f(v)>0,\ f\succeq_v a}r_f(v)x_f=b_v.\)

This is exactly the stipulated stability condition.

The matrices have polynomial size because

\(|E|\leq \tau m^2+\tau m.\)

Given \(J\), a supported rational feasible solution can be recovered by rational linear programming in polynomial time. Polynomial output length also follows from standard determinant bounds: the constraint coefficients belong to \(\{0,1,2\}\), and the right-hand sides have total bit length bounded by the input length. Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{PPAD}\), and rational stable assignments always exist.

2. The hard source problem

We use the incomplete-list instances produced in Csáji’s Lemma 4.6 and Theorem 4.7. They contain only couples, each with bound \(1\), and hospitals with integer quotas \(k_h\). Hospitals are partitioned into two sets; first and second members apply only to their respective sets. Moreover, each couple–hospital incidence occurs in at most one acceptable contract. Computing a stable fractional allocation in this subclass is \(\mathrm{PPAD}\)-hard. [Csáji, *On the complexity of Stable Hypergraph Matching, Stable Multicommodity Flow and related problems*](https://egres.elte.hu/tr/egres-21-11.pdf).

Write \(C\) for the couples and \(E_0\) for the acceptable contracts. Source feasibility is

\(\sum_{a\in E_0:\,a=(c,e)}y_a\leq1,\qquad \sum_{a\in E_0:\,h\text{ participates in }a}y_a\leq k_h.\)

Its stability requires every acceptable contract to have a participant whose weakly preferred prefix fills its bound.

The source’s unit edge bounds are redundant because each contract belongs to a unit-bound couple. The unique-incidence property makes hospital contract rankings agree with individual member priorities, without ties between acceptable applications carrying the same member. No acceptable contract uses a repeated hospital. Thus this source subclass has exactly the contribution and ranking interpretation needed below.

3. Completing preferences and normalizing masses

Let \(n=|C|\geq1\), and let \(N\) be the smallest power of two with \(N\geq n\). Add \(N-n\) dummy couple types with no original acceptable contracts. Denote the resulting type set by \(T\).

Give every type mass

\(\mu_t=\frac1N.\)

For each \(t\in T\), introduce two distinct private hospitals \(p_t\) and \(s_t\), each with quota \(1/N\). Let

\(d_t=(t,(p_t,s_t))\)

be its fallback contract. Every original hospital receives scaled quota \(q_h=k_h/N\).

Each type’s complete preference list has three consecutive parts:

  • Its original acceptable ordered pairs, in their original order.
  • Its fallback pair \((p_t,s_t)\).
  • Every remaining ordered pair, in any strict order.

For a dummy type, the first part is empty. The third part includes every repeated-hospital pair.

At an original hospital, retain the source priorities among originally acceptable member labels, then append every remaining label in any strict order.

At \(p_t\), put the first-member label of \(t\) first. At \(s_t\), put the second-member label of \(t\) first. Complete both individual priority orders arbitrarily. These strict orders can be represented within one priority class, satisfying the required input format.

All applications are declared mutually acceptable.

The fallback is the top application at each private hospital

Consider \(p_t\). The fallback sends only \(t\)’s first member there.

An application sending another label without that first member has a worse participating member. An application sending both members of \(t\) there also has a worse worst member. The remaining competing applications send only \(t\)’s first member to \(p_t\); they share the fallback’s participating label and are ranked below the fallback by \(t\)’s application preference, since no original acceptable pair uses a private hospital.

Therefore \(d_t\) is the unique top application at \(p_t\). The same argument applies at \(s_t\), with the members interchanged.

4. Every added contract below the fallback has zero mass

Let \(x\) be any stable assignment in the completed instance. Apply stability to \(d_t\).

If its witness is the type \(t\), then

\(\sum_{f:\,f=(t,e),\ f\succeq_t d_t}x_f=\frac1N.\)

If its witness is \(p_t\), uniqueness of the top application gives

\(\sum_{f:\,r_f(p_t)>0,\ f\succeq_{p_t}d_t}r_f(p_t)x_f=x_{d_t}=\frac1N.\)

Since the type’s entire mass is \(1/N\), this also implies the preceding type-prefix equality. A witness at \(s_t\) gives the same conclusion.

Hence, in every case,

\(\sum_{f:\,f=(t,e),\ f\succeq_t d_t}x_f=\mu_t.\)

Together with the type mass equation and nonnegativity, this proves

\(u_t=0,\qquad x_f=0\quad\text{whenever }f\prec_t d_t.\)

Thus every positively allocated contract is either originally acceptable or a fallback. In particular, every combined application has zero mass, because all such applications lie below the fallback.

This conclusion holds for every stable solution returned by a solver.

5. Recovering a source solution

For each original acceptable contract, define

\(y_a=Nx_a,\qquad a\in E_0.\)

Type feasibility follows immediately:

\(\sum_{a\in E_0:\,a=(t,e)}y_a=N\sum_{a\in E_0:\,a=(t,e)}x_a\leq1.\)

Only original acceptable contracts can carry positive mass at an original hospital: fallbacks use private hospitals, and all other contracts have zero mass. Therefore,

\(\sum_{a\in E_0:\,h\text{ participates in }a}y_a\leq Nq_h=k_h.\)

Now fix \(a\in E_0\), and take its stability witness in the completed instance.

If the witness is its type \(t\), the fallback is below \(a\). All added contracts below the fallback have zero mass. Multiplying the type-prefix equality by \(N\) therefore gives

\(\sum_{f\in E_0:\,f=(t,e),\ f\succeq_t a}y_f=1.\)

If the witness is an original hospital \(h\), all its positive contract masses come from \(E_0\), and its ordering on those contracts is unchanged. Multiplying its prefix equality by \(N\) gives the corresponding source equality with bound \(k_h\).

An original contract has no private-hospital participant. These cases exhaust its possible witnesses, proving that \(y\) is a stable source allocation.

For completeness, the construction also works in the other direction. Given a source stable allocation \(y\), set

\(x_a=\frac{y_a}{N}\quad(a\in E_0),\qquad x_{d_t}=\frac{1-\sum_{a\in E_0:\,a=(t,e)}y_a}{N},\)

and set all other masses and unmatched masses to zero. Original contracts retain their scaled witnesses. Each fallback and each lower-ranked added contract has its type as a witness, because that type’s entire mass is assigned at or above the fallback. Private quotas hold because each fallback mass is at most \(1/N\).

6. Size and computational consequence

The constructed instance has

\(\tau=N,\qquad m=|H_0|+2N,\qquad N<2n.\)

Writing every complete ordered-pair list takes polynomial space and time. Every mass is \(1/N\), every private quota is \(1/N\), and every original quota is \(k_h/N\). Since \(N\) is a power of two, these are dyadic rationals with polynomial bit length. Decoding multiplies selected output coordinates by \(N\), also in polynomial time.

We therefore have a polynomial search reduction from the hard incomplete-list subclass to the exact complete-preference mass problem. Combined with Section 1, this proves \(\mathrm{PPAD}\)-completeness.

A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would consequently make every \(\mathrm{PPAD}\) search problem polynomial-time solvable. Completeness of preferences, normalization of society mass, and divisibility of combined applications do not remove the hardness.
