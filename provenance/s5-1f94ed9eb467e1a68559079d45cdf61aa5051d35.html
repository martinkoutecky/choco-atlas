The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\), finite-support demand scenarios for each of several blocks of types, the known block marginal distributions defining a coupling set \(Q\), rational \(0<c<p\), a feasible coalition composition \(x\) with \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\), and a grand-coalition order \(y\) satisfying \(v_q(y,\mu)>0\) for every \(q\in Q\), compute \(\rho_y(x)=\max_{\gamma\ge0,\ q\in Q} v_q(\gamma,x)/v_q(y,\mu)\) and return a maximizing order and coupling. Here \(v_q(\gamma,x)=\mathbb{E}_q[p\min\{\sum_t x_tD_t,\gamma\}-c\gamma]\), with \(D_t\) the per-capita demand exposure of type \(t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; total mass need not equal \(1\).
Partition the types into \(R\) nonempty blocks \(B_r\). For each block, explicitly list \(K_r\) distinct demand vectors \(d_r^k\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{B_r}\) and positive rational probabilities \(a_r^k\) summing to \(1\). Set \(m=\sum_r K_r\), and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Let \(\Omega=\prod_r\{1,\ldots,K_r\}\) and \(Q=\{q\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{\Omega}:\sum_{\omega:\omega_r=k}q_\omega=a_r^k\text{ for all }r,k\}\). Only the block lists are input; the product list is implicit. In outcome \(\omega\), every unit of type \(t\in B_r\) has per-capita demand \(D_t(\omega)=(d_r^{\omega_r})_t\).
Assume \(\min_k\sum_{t\in B_r}\mu_t(d_r^k)_t>0\) for at least one block. Input rational \(0<c<p\), a rational coalition composition \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) with \(0<\sum_t x_t<\sum_t\mu_t\), and rational \(y\ge0\).
For any composition \(z\) and order \(h\ge0\), define \(v_q(h,z)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}q_\omega[p\min\{\sum_t z_tD_t(\omega),h\}-ch]\). Promise \(v_q(y,\mu)>0\) for every \(q\in Q\).
The computational problem is to compute exactly \(\rho_y(x)=\max_{\gamma\ge0,\ q\in Q}v_q(\gamma,x)/v_q(y,\mu)\) and return a maximizing order and coupling, representing the coupling by its nonzero outcome probabilities.

Is this exact optimization problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,R,L\), or is it NP-hard? For the decision formulation, use a binary rational threshold \(b\) and ask whether \(\rho_y(x)\ge b\), including its encoding in \(L\).

For finite retailers, the same ratio can be computed using at most \(K=\prod_r K_r\) linear programs, each of size polynomial in the explicitly expanded product support; this does not establish polynomial time in the block-list encoding. [Doan–Nguyen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.5906v3)
No classification of this exact mass problem with implicit product support was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Mass normalization was unspecified; allow arbitrary positive rational total mass, consistent with the unnormalized finite-retailer model.
  • Blocks were unspecified; require a partition into nonempty blocks, with explicitly listed nonnegative rational demand vectors and positive rational probabilities.
  • The coupling-set encoding was unspecified; prescribe all couplings of the block marginals over their implicit Cartesian product.
  • Interpret type demand as a common per-capita exposure, so coalition mass scales that exposure linearly.
  • Require a nonempty proper coalition, matching the domain of the anchored computation.
  • Carry over the assumption that at least one block has strictly positive minimum aggregate demand.
  • Require the supplied order and coalition composition to be rational, and retain strict positivity of grand-coalition profit as an input promise.
  • Define m as the total number of listed block scenarios and L as total binary input length; represent an output coupling by its nonzero entries.
  • Introduce a rational threshold decision formulation to make the NP-hardness alternative precise.

1. Classification

The exact optimization problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. This holds even when every block has two types and two equiprobable demand vectors, and total society demand is deterministic.

Moreover, under the stated promise, the threshold decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete: hardness follows from the reduction below, and every yes-instance has a polynomial-size certificate consisting of an order and a coupling.

2. A promise-preserving reduction from PARTITION

Use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem PARTITION: given positive integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\), encoded in binary, decide whether some subset has sum half their total. Its classical complexity classification appears in [Karp’s paper](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Write \(W=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\).

Construction

For each integer \(w_i\), create a block \(B_i=\{u_i,v_i\}\) with masses and coalition composition

\(\mu_{u_i}=\mu_{v_i}=1,\qquad x_{u_i}=1,\qquad x_{v_i}=0.\)

In coordinate order \((u_i,v_i)\), list the two demand vectors

\(d_i^1=(0,w_i),\qquad d_i^2=(w_i,0),\qquad a_i^1=a_i^2=\frac12.\)

Finally, set

\(p=2,\qquad c=1,\qquad y=W,\qquad b=\frac12.\)

All input requirements hold:

  • Both masses are positive, and \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\).
  • The coalition has mass \(n\), strictly between zero and the society’s mass \(2n\).
  • The two demand vectors in each block are distinct because \(w_i>0\).
  • Each block’s total society demand equals \(w_i\) in both states. Thus every block satisfies the required positive minimum demand condition.
  • The construction has \(R=n\), \(\tau=2n\), and \(m=2n\). Its binary encoding length is polynomial in the PARTITION input length.

The denominator is constant and positive

For every outcome \(\omega\), total society demand is

\(\sum_t\mu_tD_t(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i=W.\)

Every feasible coupling has total probability one: summing the marginal constraints for any one block gives \(\sum_\omega q_\omega=1\). Consequently,

\(v_q(y,\mu)=2\min\{W,W\}-W=W>0.\)

Thus the reduction satisfies the positivity promise for every feasible coupling.

The numerator has an exact upper bound

Define the coalition’s outcome demand by

\(S(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\mathbf{1}_{\{\omega_i=2\}}.\)

The prescribed marginals imply, for every feasible coupling,

\(\mathbb{E}_q S=\sum_{i=1}^n w_iq(\omega_i=2)=\frac W2.\)

For nonnegative real numbers \(s,\gamma\), the identity

\(2\min\{s,\gamma\}-\gamma=s-|s-\gamma|\)

therefore gives

\(v_q(\gamma,x)=\frac W2-\mathbb{E}_q|S-\gamma|.\)

It follows that

\(\frac{v_q(\gamma,x)}{v_q(y,\mu)}=\frac12-\frac{\mathbb{E}_q|S-\gamma|}{W}\le\frac12.\)

Equality holds precisely when \(S=\gamma\) on every outcome with positive coupling probability. Taking expectations then forces \(\gamma=W/2\).

Equivalence with PARTITION

Suppose a subset \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}w_i=W/2\).

Let \(\omega^I\) select state two exactly in the blocks indexed by \(I\), and let \(\omega^{I^c}\) select state two exactly in its complement. Assign probability one half to each of these two outcomes.

The outcomes are distinct, and each block selects each state with probability one half. Hence this coupling belongs to \(Q\). Both outcomes have coalition demand \(W/2\), so the order \(\gamma=W/2\) attains ratio \(1/2\).

Conversely, suppose the maximum ratio is at least \(1/2\). The maximum is attained, as proved in Section 3. The upper bound just established implies that an optimizing pair satisfies \(S=W/2\) on its positive-probability outcomes. At least one such outcome exists. Its state-two blocks consequently give a subset whose weights sum to \(W/2\).

We have proved the exact equivalence

\(\rho_y(x)\ge\frac12\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{the PARTITION instance has an equal-sum partition}.\)

This is a polynomial-time, promise-preserving many-one reduction. It establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness of the threshold problem and of exact optimization.

3. Polynomial-size optimal solutions and decision certificates

The following arguments apply to every instance satisfying the stated promise.

Attainment and the order’s encoding length

For each outcome, define

\(S_\omega=\sum_t x_tD_t(\omega),\qquad T_\omega=\sum_t\mu_tD_t(\omega).\)

An upper bound on every coalition demand, attained by some outcome, is

\(H=\sum_{r=1}^R\max_{1\le k\le K_r}\sum_{t\in B_r}x_t(d_r^k)_t.\)

This quantity can be computed from the block lists. If \(\gamma>H\), then

\(v_q(\gamma,x)=p\sum_\omega q_\omega S_\omega-c\gamma.\)

Replacing \(\gamma\) by \(H\) strictly increases the numerator. Because the denominator is positive, it also increases the ratio. It therefore suffices to optimize over \([0,H]\).

The set \(Q\) is nonempty: the product coupling \(q_\omega=\prod_r a_r^{\omega_r}\) satisfies all marginal constraints. It is a closed, bounded polytope, since its probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one.

The denominator is continuous and positive on this compact set, so it has a strictly positive minimum. The ratio is consequently continuous on the compact set \([0,H]\times Q\), and its maximum is attained.

For any fixed coupling, the numerator is piecewise linear in the order, with possible breakpoints among

\(\{0\}\cup\{S_\omega:\omega\in\Omega\}.\)

A linear function on a closed interval attains its maximum at an endpoint; if it is constant, either endpoint suffices. Applying this to the finitely many linear pieces shows that an optimal order can be chosen from this breakpoint set.

Each \(S_\omega\) is a sum of \(\tau\) products of input rationals. Thus such an order has polynomial binary encoding length, even though there may be exponentially many candidate outcomes.

An optimal coupling can be chosen at a vertex

Fix an optimal breakpoint order \(\gamma_*\), and write

\(N(q)=v_q(\gamma_*,x),\qquad D(q)=v_q(y,\mu),\qquad \rho_*=\rho_y(x).\)

Both \(N\) and \(D\) are linear functions of the outcome probabilities, using \(\sum_\omega q_\omega=1\) to incorporate the constant order costs.

Since \(D(q)>0\), global optimality implies

\(N(q)-\rho_*D(q)\le0\qquad\text{for every }q\in Q.\)

Equality holds for an optimal coupling. Thus this linear functional has maximum zero on \(Q\).

A linear functional on a nonempty compact polytope has a maximizing vertex. All hypotheses have been checked above. Selecting such a vertex \(q_*\) gives \(N(q_*)=\rho_*D(q_*)\), so \(q_*\) is an optimal coupling for \(\gamma_*\).

Support size and probability encoding lengths

Let \(A\) be the marginal-constraint matrix, with entries

\(A_{(r,k),\omega}=\mathbf{1}_{\{\omega_r=k\}}.\)

It has \(m\) rows. For every block, the sum of its rows is the all-ones row. Comparing each block with the first gives \(R-1\) independent row relations. Therefore,

\(\operatorname{rank}(A)\le m-R+1.\)

At a vertex of \(Q\), the columns indexed by positive probabilities must be linearly independent. Indeed, dependence would give a nonzero vector \(h\), supported on those columns, satisfying \(Ah=0\). For sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), both \(q_*+\varepsilon h\) and \(q_*-\varepsilon h\) would be feasible and distinct. Their midpoint would be \(q_*\), contradicting that it is a vertex.

Hence an optimal coupling can have support size

\(s\le m-R+1.\)

Its probabilities also have polynomial encoding length. Choose \(s\) rows making the supported columns into a nonsingular \(s\times s\) matrix \(C\). The positive probabilities satisfy \(Cq_{\mathrm{supp}}=a'\), where \(a'\) consists of input marginal probabilities.

Let \(B\) be the product of their encoded denominators. Its bit length is at most \(L\), and \(z=Ba'\) is an integer vector with entries between zero and \(B\). Cramer’s rule gives

\((q_{\mathrm{supp}})_j=\frac{\det C_j(z)}{B\det C},\)

where \(C_j(z)\) replaces column \(j\) of \(C\) by \(z\). Because \(C\) has zero-one entries, the determinant expansion yields

\(|\det C|\le s!,\qquad |\det C_j(z)|\le Bs!.\)

Thus every supported probability has bit length \(O(L+s\log(s+1))\).

Verification

A certificate lists an order \(\gamma_*\) and at most \(m-R+1\) outcomes with their positive rational probabilities. Each outcome is represented by its \(R\) block-state indices.

A verifier can, in polynomial time:

  • Check the indices, probability signs, and all marginal constraints.
  • Compute coalition and society demands on the listed outcomes.
  • Compute \(N=v_q(\gamma_*,x)\) and \(D=v_q(y,\mu)\) exactly.
  • Check \(D>0\) and \(N-bD\ge0\).

The optimal solutions established above have polynomial encoding length, so every promised yes-instance has such a certificate. Conversely, any accepted certificate supplies a feasible pair attaining a ratio at least \(b\).

This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) on the promised domain. Combined with the reduction, it establishes the claimed promise-respecting \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

4. Consequence for exact optimization

An algorithm computing the exact ratio in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,R,L\) would decide the constructed PARTITION instances by comparing its output with \(1/2\). Therefore such an algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The required output representation is not the obstacle: a maximizing order and coupling always admit polynomial-size rational representations. The difficulty is finding them from the implicit product support.
