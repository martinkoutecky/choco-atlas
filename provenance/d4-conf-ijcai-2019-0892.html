The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), \(p\ge1\), and a rational distribution \(\mu=(\mu_A)_{A\subseteq C}\) over approval types, choose \(W\in\binom{C}{k}\) maximizing \(G_{p,\mu}(W)=\sum_{A\subseteq C}\mu_A\sum_{j=1}^{|A\cap W|}p^{-j}\), where \(\operatorname{AV}_\mu(W)=\sum_A\mu_A|Acap W|\) and \(\operatorname{CC}_\mu(W)=\sum_A\mu_A\mathbf{1}[A\cap W\ne\varnothing]\); compute the resulting AV and CC ratios and characterize their worst cases over \(\mu\) and tied winners.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a finite candidate set, \(m=|C|\), \(1\le k\le m\), and \(p\ge1\) a binary rational. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) approval types \(A\subseteq C\) with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_A\) a binary rational; \(\sum_A\mu_A=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval sets. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L,k\).
Empty approval sets are allowed, but require \(\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}\mu_A>0\). Committees are ordinary subsets of exactly \(k\) candidates.
Define \(G_{p,\mu}(W)=\sum_A\mu_A\sum_{j=1}^{|A\cap W|}p^{-j}\), with an empty inner sum equal to zero, and let \(R_p(\mu,k)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{W\in\binom{C}{k}}G_{p,\mu}(W)\); retain every tied winner.
Define \(\operatorname{AV}_\mu(W)=\sum_A\mu_A|A\cap W|\) and \(\operatorname{CC}_\mu(W)=\sum_A\mu_A\mathbf1[A\cap W\ne\varnothing]\).
For \(X\in\{\operatorname{AV},\operatorname{CC}\}\), the instance ratio is \(r_X(\mu,k,p)=\frac{\min_{W\in R_p(\mu,k)}X_\mu(W)}{\max_{V\in\binom{C}{k}}X_\mu(V)}\), and its worst-case guarantee is \(\kappa_X(k,p)=\inf_{C,\mu}r_X(\mu,k,p)\), over all finite candidate sets and admissible rational societies.

What are the sharp worst-case AV and CC approximation guarantees \(\kappa_{\operatorname{AV}}(k,p)\) and \(\kappa_{\operatorname{CC}}(k,p)\), and which societies and tied winners attain or approach them?

For finite electorates, known bounds for \(k\ge2\) and \(p>1\) are \(\frac{\mathcal W(k\log p)}{k\log p+\mathcal W(k\log p)}\le\kappa_{\operatorname{AV}}\le\frac{2\mathcal W(k\log p)}{k\log p}+\frac1k\) and \(\frac{p-1}{p}\le\kappa_{\operatorname{CC}}\le\frac{p}{p+k/(k+2)}\), where \(\mathcal W\) is the principal Lambert W function and \(\log\) is natural. [Lackner–Skowron](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0058.pdf)
The later full version retains these bounds; no exact characterization for rational-mass societies was located in the search. [Full version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.01527)

  • Allow empty approval types but exclude societies supported entirely on the empty type, so both ratio denominators are positive.
  • Define instance ratios using the minimum over all tied winning committees, with separate AV and CC denominators.

1. Exact guarantees

Both guarantees admit exact formulas, and every value below is attained by a rational society and a worst tied winner.

For every \(k\ge1\) and rational \(p\ge1\),

\( \displaystyle \boxed{\kappa_{\operatorname{AV}}(k,p) =\frac1k\min_{0\le h\le k}\left(h+(k-h)p^{-h}\right).} \)

For CC, the boundary cases are

\( \displaystyle \boxed{\kappa_{\operatorname{CC}}(1,p)=1, \qquad \kappa_{\operatorname{CC}}(2,p)=\frac{p}{p+1}.} \)

For \(k\ge3\), define

\( \displaystyle a=p-\frac{p-1}{k}, \qquad d_h=(ah-1)p^{-h}, \qquad Z=2(k-1)p-k+3. \)

Then

\( \displaystyle \boxed{ \kappa_{\operatorname{CC}}(k,p)= \begin{cases} \left(1+\max_{3\le h\le k}d_h\right)^{-1}, & (2p-3)a\le p-1,\\[2mm] \displaystyle\frac{pZ}{pZ+2(k-1)p+1}, & (2p-3)a>p-1. \end{cases}} \)

The two expressions agree at the regime boundary. In particular,

\( \displaystyle \kappa_{\operatorname{AV}}(k,1)=1, \qquad \kappa_{\operatorname{CC}}(k,1)=\frac1k. \)

For example,

\( \displaystyle \kappa_{\operatorname{CC}}(3,p)= \begin{cases} \displaystyle\frac{p^2}{p^2+2},&1\le p\le\frac74,\\[2mm] \displaystyle\frac{4p^2}{(2p+1)^2},&p\ge\frac74. \end{cases} \)

The bounds quoted in the question appear in Theorem 5 of the [full version of Lackner–Skowron](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.01527). The proofs of the exact formulas follow.

2. Preliminary facts and an exchange inequality

Write

\( \displaystyle g_p(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{t}p^{-j}, \qquad g_p(0)=0, \)

and let \(\mathbb E\) denote expectation over approval types with masses \(\mu_A\).

The denominators in both instance ratios are positive: some candidate has positive approval mass, and some size-\(k\) committee contains that candidate.

Fix a winning committee \(W\) and any other size-\(k\) committee \(V\). Define

\( \displaystyle I=W\cap V,\qquad B=W\setminus V,\qquad D=V\setminus W. \)

Suppose \(s=|B|=|D|>0\). For an approval type \(A\), put

\( \displaystyle i=|A\cap I|,\quad b=|A\cap B|,\quad d=|A\cap D|, \quad w=i+b,\quad v=i+d. \)

For \(x\in B\) and \(y\in D\), direct examination of the four approval possibilities gives

\( \displaystyle g_p\!\left(|A\cap(W-\{x\}+\{y\})|\right)-g_p(w) = \mathbf1[y\in A]p^{-(w+1)} -\mathbf1[x\in A]p^{-w} +(p-1)\mathbf1[x,y\in A]p^{-(w+1)}. \)

Every such exchange has nonpositive expected gain because \(W\) is a winner. Summing over all \(s^2\) exchanges and multiplying by \(p/s\) yields

\[ \displaystyle \mathbb E[\delta]\ge0, \qquad \delta= b p^{1-w} -d\left(1+\frac{(p-1)b}{s}\right)p^{-w}. \tag{1} \]

We also have the global comparison

\[ \displaystyle \mathbb E[g_p(w)-g_p(v)]\ge0. \tag{2} \]

These inequalities allow \(W\) and \(V\) to overlap.

3. AV: lower bound and attaining society

Lower bound

Set

\( \displaystyle \rho=\frac1k\min_{0\le h\le k}\left(h+(k-h)p^{-h}\right). \)

Thus \(0<\rho\le1\). We prove the pointwise inequality

\[ \displaystyle w-\rho v\ge\delta. \tag{3} \]

For fixed \(i,b\), the difference between the two sides is affine in \(d\). Since \(0\le d\le s\), checking its endpoints suffices.

At \(d=0\), this difference is

\( \displaystyle (1-\rho)i+b\left(1-p^{1-w}\right). \)

It is zero when \(w=0\), and nonnegative when \(w\ge1\).

At \(d=s\), the difference is

\( \displaystyle w-\rho(i+s)+(s-b)p^{-w}. \)

Let \(n=i+s\). Then \(w\le n\le k\), and this expression equals

\( \displaystyle w+(n-w)p^{-w}-\rho n. \)

For \(n>0\),

\( \displaystyle \frac{w+(n-w)p^{-w}}{n} =p^{-w}+\frac{w}{n}(1-p^{-w}) \ge p^{-w}+\frac{w}{k}(1-p^{-w}) \ge\rho. \)

This proves (3). Taking expectations and using (1) gives

\( \displaystyle \operatorname{AV}_\mu(W)\ge\rho\,\operatorname{AV}_\mu(V). \)

If \(W=V\), the same conclusion is immediate. Choosing an AV-optimal \(V\) proves the lower bound for every winning \(W\).

Attainment

Choose a minimizing integer \(h\). Take a party block \(P\) of \(k\) candidates and \(k-h\) additional candidates \(D\). Assign masses

\( \displaystyle \mu_P=t,\qquad \mu_{\{d\}}=t p^{-h}\quad(d\in D), \qquad t=\frac{1}{1+(k-h)p^{-h}}. \)

Let \(W\) contain \(h\) candidates of \(P\) and every candidate of \(D\).

Any size-\(k\) committee contains some number \(b\ge h\) of party candidates. Its score is

\( \displaystyle t g_p(b)+t(k-b)p^{-(h+1)}. \)

Increasing \(b\) by one changes this score by

\( \displaystyle t\left(p^{-(b+1)}-p^{-(h+1)}\right)\le0. \)

Thus \(W\) is a winner. Whenever \(h<k\), committees containing \(h+1\) party candidates also tie with \(W\).

Each party candidate has approval mass \(t\), while each additional candidate has approval mass \(tp^{-h}\le t\). Hence \(P\) is AV-optimal, and

\( \displaystyle \frac{\operatorname{AV}_\mu(W)} {\operatorname{AV}_\mu(P)} = \frac{h+(k-h)p^{-h}}{k} =\rho. \)

The universal lower bound ensures that \(W\) is a worst tied winner. This society has \(m=2k-h\) and \(\tau=1+k-h\).

4. CC: a universal lower-bound family

Assume \(p>1\) and \(k\ge3\). Put \(q=p^{-1}\), and define

\( \displaystyle T_h=\sum_{j=1}^{h-1}q^j, \qquad d_h=(ah-1)q^h, \qquad a=p-\frac{p-1}{k}. \)

For \(0\le\lambda\le1\), let

\( \displaystyle F_h(\lambda)=\lambda d_h+(1-\lambda)T_h, \quad z_\lambda=\max_{1\le h\le k}F_h(\lambda), \quad \rho_\lambda=\frac{1}{1+z_\lambda}. \)

We will prove

\[ \displaystyle \operatorname{CC}_\mu(W) \ge\rho_\lambda\,\operatorname{CC}_\mu(V) \tag{4} \]

for every winning \(W\), every size-\(k\) committee \(V\), and every \(\lambda\).

When \(W\ne V\), use the notation of Section 2 and set

\( \displaystyle J=p\bigl(g_p(w)-g_p(v)\bigr). \)

By (1) and (2), both \(\mathbb E[\delta]\) and \(\mathbb E[J]\) are nonnegative. It therefore suffices to prove

\[ \displaystyle \mathbf1[w>0]-\rho_\lambda\mathbf1[v>0] \ge \rho_\lambda\bigl(\lambda\delta+(1-\lambda)J\bigr) \tag{5} \]

pointwise.

Both committees cover the type. If \(w>0\) and \(v>0\), then \(J\le T_w\). Also \(\delta\le d_w\):

  • If \(d\ge1\), then

\( \displaystyle \delta \le \left[\left(p-\frac{p-1}{s}\right)b-1\right]q^w \le(aw-1)q^w=d_w. \)

  • If \(d=0\), then \(i\ge1\), so

\( \displaystyle \delta\le(w-1)p^{1-w}\le d_w. \)

The last inequality follows from

\( \displaystyle d_w-(w-1)p^{1-w} =(p-1)\left(1-\frac wk\right)q^w\ge0. \)

Consequently, the right side of (5) is at most
\(\rho_\lambda z_\lambda=1-\rho_\lambda\).

Only \(W\) covers the type. Here \(i=d=0\), so

\( \displaystyle \delta=w p^{1-w},\qquad J=1+T_w. \)

Bernoulli’s inequality gives

\( \displaystyle p^w\ge1+w(p-1)\ge1+\frac{w(p-1)}{k}, \)

and therefore \(\delta\le1+d_w\). The right side of (5) is at most
\(\rho_\lambda(1+z_\lambda)=1\).

Only \(V\) covers the type. Here \(w=0\), \(d\ge1\), and

\( \displaystyle \delta=-d\le-1,\qquad J=-p g_p(v)\le-1. \)

Thus the right side of (5) is at most \(-\rho_\lambda\).

Neither committee covers the type. Both sides vanish.

Taking expectations proves (4). The case \(W=V\) is immediate.

Evaluating the lower bound

First observe that, for \(h\ge3\),

\[ \displaystyle p^hT_h =\sum_{r=1}^{h-1}p^r \ge(h-1)+\frac{h(h-1)}2(p-1) \ge ah-1=p^h d_h. \tag{6} \]

The first inequality is Bernoulli’s inequality applied separately to each power.

The successive differences are

\[ \displaystyle d_{h+1}-d_h =q^h\left[aq-(ah-1)(1-q)\right]. \tag{7} \]

The bracket strictly decreases with \(h\).

If \((2p-3)a\le p-1\), equivalently \(d_3\ge d_2\), a maximizer of \(d_h\) can be chosen with \(h\ge3\). Taking \(\lambda=1\) in (4) gives

\[ \displaystyle \kappa_{\operatorname{CC}}(k,p) \ge\left(1+\max_{3\le h\le k}d_h\right)^{-1}. \tag{8} \]

If \((2p-3)a>p-1\), choose

\( \displaystyle \lambda_*=\frac{p}{a(2p-3)+1}. \)

The regime condition implies \(0<\lambda_*<1\). Furthermore,

\[ \displaystyle F_{h+1}(\lambda)-F_h(\lambda) =q^h\left[1+\lambda q(a-1)-\lambda ah(1-q)\right]. \tag{9} \]

For \(\lambda=\lambda_*\), the bracket is zero at \(h=2\), positive below it, and negative above it. Hence the maximum is attained at \(h=2,3\).

To evaluate its value, define

\( \displaystyle \theta_2=\frac{k(p-1)+3}{Z}, \qquad \theta_3=\frac{(k-2)p}{Z}, \qquad Z=2(k-1)p-k+3. \)

These are positive and sum to one. Direct calculation gives

\( \displaystyle d_2-T_2=\frac{(p-1)(k-2)}{kp^2}, \qquad T_3-d_3=\frac{(p-1)(p-1+3/k)}{p^3}. \)

Thus

\[ \displaystyle \theta_2d_2+\theta_3d_3 =\theta_2T_2+\theta_3T_3 =\frac{2(k-1)p+1}{pZ} =:z. \tag{10} \]

Since \(F_2(\lambda_*)=F_3(\lambda_*)\), averaging them with these weights shows that their common value is \(z\). Therefore (4) gives

\[ \displaystyle \kappa_{\operatorname{CC}}(k,p) \ge\frac1{1+z} =\frac{pZ}{pZ+2(k-1)p+1}. \tag{11} \]

5. A sampling lemma that verifies all competing committees

The following lemma supplies the sharpness check beyond single exchanges.

Lemma. Let \(B,D\) be disjoint candidate blocks of size \(k\), and suppose a covered voter approves a uniformly chosen \(h\)-subset of \(B\) and one uniformly chosen candidate of \(D\), independently. Allow either a fixed \(h\), or a mixture of \(h=2\) and \(h=3\).

For a committee containing \(k-l\) candidates of \(B\) and \(l\) candidates of \(D\), define

\( \displaystyle A_l=\frac{kp}{l}\left( \text{expected covered-voter score of }B -\text{expected covered-voter score of that committee} \right). \)

For every \(1\le l\le k\),

\[ \displaystyle A_l\ge\min(A_1,A_k). \tag{12} \]

If \(\theta_h\) denotes the distribution of approval counts, then

\[ \displaystyle A_1=\sum_h\theta_h d_h, \qquad A_k=\sum_h\theta_h T_h. \tag{13} \]

Proof. Put \(q=p^{-1}\) and \(\gamma=1-q\). Let \(M_l\) be the expected value of \(q\) raised to the number of approved candidates among the \(k-l\) selected candidates of \(B\). Sampling the \(l\) omitted candidates gives

\[ \displaystyle M_l= \frac{[t^l]Q(t)}{\binom{k}{l}}, \qquad Q(t)=\sum_h\theta_h q^h(1+pt)^h(1+t)^{k-h}. \tag{14} \]

Here \([t^l]Q(t)\) means the coefficient of \(t^l\).

The approved candidate in \(D\) belongs to the committee with probability \(l/k\), independently. Hence, with

\( \displaystyle P_l=\left(1-\frac{\gamma l}{k}\right)M_l, \)

the expected covered-voter score is \((1-P_l)/(p-1)\). Consequently,

\[ \displaystyle A_l=\frac{kp}{p-1}\frac{P_l-P_0}{l}. \tag{15} \]

We verify that the secant slopes in (15) have no strict interior minimum.

For a pure approval count \(h\),

\( \displaystyle Q(t)=q^h(1+pt)^h(1+t)^{k-h}. \)

For a mixture of counts \(2,3\),

\( \displaystyle Q(t)=c(1+pt)^2(1+t)^{k-3}(1+rt), \quad c=q^2(\theta_2+q\theta_3), \quad r=\frac1{\theta_2+q\theta_3}. \)

In the latter case \(1\le r\le p\). Thus in both cases

\( \displaystyle Q(t)=c\prod_{j=1}^{k}(1+w_jt) \)

with all \(w_j\ge1\).

Let \(e_l\) denote the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(l\), and put \(u_l=M_{l+1}-M_l\). A counting identity gives

\[ \displaystyle u_l= \frac{c}{k\binom{k-1}{l}}[t^l]H(t), \quad H(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(w_j-1)\prod_{i\ne j}(1+w_it). \tag{16} \]

For a pure count,

\( \displaystyle H(t)=h(p-1)(1+pt)^{h-1}(1+t)^{k-h}. \)

For the mixture,

\( \displaystyle H(t) =(1+t)^{k-3}(1+pt) \left[2(p-1)(1+rt)+(r-1)(1+pt)\right]. \)

The bracket is a positive constant times \(1+st\), where

\( \displaystyle s=\frac{2(p-1)r+(r-1)p}{2(p-1)+(r-1)}\ge1. \)

Therefore, in either case, \(u_l\) is a positive constant times
\(e_l(v_1,\ldots,v_{k-1})/\binom{k-1}{l}\), with all \(v_j\ge1\).

We use Newton’s inequalities in their precise normalized form: for positive \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\), the sequence
\(e_l(v_1,\ldots,v_n)/\binom nl\) is log-concave. Thus

\( \displaystyle u_l^2\ge u_{l-1}u_{l+1}. \)

This sequence is also nondecreasing. Indeed, couple a uniformly chosen subset of size \(l\) with one of size \(l+1\) by adding a uniformly chosen remaining element; the product cannot decrease because every \(v_j\ge1\).

It follows that \(u_{l+1}/u_l\) is nonincreasing and at least one. Direct subtraction now yields

\[ \displaystyle P_{l+2}-2P_{l+1}+P_l = u_l\left[ \left(1-\frac{\gamma(l+2)}k\right) \left(\frac{u_{l+1}}{u_l}-1\right) -\frac{2\gamma}{k} \right]. \tag{17} \]

The bracket is nonincreasing in \(l\): its two factors before the subtraction are nonnegative and nonincreasing. Hence the second differences of \(P_l\) can change sign only from nonnegative to nonpositive. Its first differences therefore increase and then decrease.

The averages of initial segments of an increasing-then-decreasing sequence also increase and then decrease. To see this explicitly, once a new term falls below the preceding average, the new average remains above that term, and every subsequent term is no larger. Thus the averages cannot start increasing again.

The quantities \((P_l-P_0)/l\) are precisely these initial-segment averages. Their minimum is therefore at \(l=1\) or \(l=k\), proving (12).

Finally,

\( \displaystyle M_0=\sum_h\theta_hq^h,\quad M_1=\sum_h\theta_hq^h\left(1+\frac{h(p-1)}k\right), \quad M_k=1. \)

Substitution into (15) gives (13). This completes the lemma. ∎

6. CC societies attaining the bounds

The two regimes for \(k\ge3\) and \(p>1\)

Take disjoint blocks \(B,D\), each of size \(k\). Choose a number \(z\) and a distribution \(\theta_h\) as follows:

  • In the first regime, choose \(H\in\{3,\ldots,k\}\) maximizing \(d_H\), set \(z=d_H\), and put \(\theta_H=1\).
  • In the second regime, use the weights \(\theta_2,\theta_3\) of (10), and set \(z=[2(k-1)p+1]/(pZ)\).

Put \(\rho=1/(1+z)\). For every \(d\in D\), assign singleton mass

\( \displaystyle \mu_{\{d\}}=\frac{1-\rho}{k}. \)

For every \(S\subseteq B\) of size \(h\) and every \(d\in D\), assign

\[ \displaystyle \mu_{S\cup\{d\}} =\frac{\rho\theta_h}{k\binom{k}{h}}. \tag{18} \]

Omit types whose indicated weight is zero. These masses sum to one.

Every voter approves exactly one candidate of \(D\). Therefore

\( \displaystyle \operatorname{CC}_\mu(D)=1, \qquad \operatorname{CC}_\mu(B)=\rho. \)

Consider any competing committee containing \(l\) candidates of \(D\). Symmetry makes its score depend only on \(l\). For \(l\ge1\), the sampling lemma gives

\[ \displaystyle G_{p,\mu}(B)-G_{p,\mu}(T_l) =\frac{\rho l}{kp}(A_l-z). \tag{19} \]

In the first regime, \(A_1=d_H=z\), while \(A_k=T_H\ge d_H\) by (6). In the second regime, (10) gives \(A_1=A_k=z\). Thus (12) proves that (19) is nonnegative for every competing committee.

Consequently, \(B\) is a winning committee with ratio \(\rho\), matching the universal lower bound. It is therefore a worst tied winner.

There are explicit ties:

  • In both regimes, every committee obtained from \(B\) by one replacement with a candidate of \(D\) ties with \(B\).
  • In the second regime, \(D\) itself also ties with \(B\).

All masses are rational. The first construction has

\( \displaystyle m=2k,\qquad \tau=k\left(1+\binom{k}{H}\right), \)

and the second has

\( \displaystyle m=2k,\qquad \tau=k\left(1+\binom{k}{2}+\binom{k}{3}\right). \)

Boundary cases

For \(k=1\), both objectives equal the approval mass of the selected candidate, so both guarantees are one.

For \(p=1\), the rule is AV. If \(W\) is an AV winner and \(V\) is CC-optimal, then

\( \displaystyle \operatorname{CC}_\mu(W) \ge\frac{\operatorname{AV}_\mu(W)}k \ge\frac{\operatorname{AV}_\mu(V)}k \ge\frac{\operatorname{CC}_\mu(V)}k. \)

To attain equality, take a block \(B\) of \(k\) candidates and \(k-1\) additional singleton candidates. Give approval type \(B\) mass \(1/k\), and each singleton type mass \(1/k\). Every candidate has the same approval mass, so every size-\(k\) committee wins. The committee \(B\) covers mass \(1/k\), whereas one member of \(B\) together with all singleton candidates covers everyone.

For \(k=2\), every committee \(T\) satisfies

\( \displaystyle p^{-1}\operatorname{CC}_\mu(T) \le G_{p,\mu}(T) \le(p^{-1}+p^{-2})\operatorname{CC}_\mu(T). \)

Comparing a winning \(W\) with a CC-optimal \(V\) therefore gives the lower bound \(p/(p+1)\).

For attainment, use three candidates \(a,b,d\), with

\( \displaystyle \mu_{\{a,b\}}=\frac{p}{p+1}, \qquad \mu_{\{d\}}=\frac1{p+1}. \)

All three size-two committees have score \(1/p\). The committee \(\{a,b\}\) covers mass \(p/(p+1)\), while either mixed committee covers everyone.

7. Rationality, finite electorates, and running time

The constructions prove exact attainment; no limiting sequence is required. Clearing a common denominator of the rational masses produces a finite electorate with identical scores up to scaling, identical tied winners, and identical ratios.

The values require only a scan of \(k+1\) AV expressions and, in the first CC regime, at most \(k-2\) CC expressions. All comparisons can be performed exactly over rationals.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, a conservative bound for computing both guarantees is

\( \displaystyle O\!\left(L+k^3\bigl(L+\log(k+1)\bigr)^2\right) \)

bit operations. The calculation is independent of \(m,\tau\), apart from reading the input: the guarantees depend only on \(k,p\).

If an attaining society is output as an explicit support list, its construction takes \(O(\tau m\,\operatorname{poly}(k,L))\) bit operations, with \(m,\tau\) as stated above. In particular, the first CC witness can have exponentially many support types; evaluating its guarantee does not require listing them.
