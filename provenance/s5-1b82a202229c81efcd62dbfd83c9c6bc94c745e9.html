The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a fixed λ ∈ [0,1), let μ be a finite-support probability distribution over resident locations \(x \in [0,1]\), and let the predicted site be \(p ∈ {0,1}\). Does the endpoint rule that chooses the predicted endpoint when its side's mass plus λ is at least the other side's mass, and otherwise chooses the opposite endpoint, remain group-strategyproof against every coalition of positive mass and satisfy robustness \((3+λ)/(1-λ)\) and consistency \((3-λ)/(1+λ)\), where \(W_μ(y)=∫ |y-x|\,dμ(x)\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}a_i\delta_{x_i}\), with distinct binary-rational locations \(x_i\in[0,1]\), positive binary-rational masses \(a_i\), and \(\sum_i a_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. State any running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and other parameters, with \(m=2\) possible mechanism outputs.
Fix \(\lambda\in[0,1)\) and a public prediction \(p\in\{0,1\}\), held fixed during deviations. Residents maximize \(u_x(y)=|y-x|\); define \(W_\mu(y)=\int|y-x|\,d\mu(x)\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)=\max_{y\in[0,1]}W_\mu(y)\).
For any reported probability measure \(\sigma\), put \(v_1(\sigma)=\sigma([0,1/2])\) and \(v_0(\sigma)=\sigma((1/2,1])\). The rule \(f_\lambda(\sigma,p)\) returns \(p\) if \(v_p(\sigma)+\lambda\ge v_{1-p}(\sigma)\), and returns \(1-p\) otherwise.
A coalition is any submeasure \(0\le\nu\le\mu\) with \(\nu([0,1])>0\); it may split and reassign its mass through arbitrary measurable location reports, inducing a report measure \(\rho\) of the same total mass and profile \(\mu'=\mu-\nu+\rho\). Group strategyproofness means every such deviation leaves a positive \(\nu\)-mass of residents with \(u_x(f_\lambda(\mu',p))\le u_x(f_\lambda(\mu,p))\).
Robustness \(\gamma\) means \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\le\gamma W_\mu(f_\lambda(\mu,p))\) for every prediction; consistency \(\beta\) requires this inequality with \(\beta\) whenever \(W_\mu(p)=\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\), including tied optima.

Does this rule remain group-strategyproof against every positive-mass coalition and satisfy robustness \(\frac{3+\lambda}{1-\lambda}\) and consistency \(\frac{3-\lambda}{1+\lambda}\) for every \(\lambda\in[0,1)\)?

For finite lists of equally weighted residents, the same rule is group-strategyproof and satisfies these robustness and consistency bounds ([Istrate–Bonchiş](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09521)). No result settling this exact mass-and-coalition formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified binary-rational resident locations so that the support has a finite input encoding; masses retain the stated probability normalization.
  • Interpreted an endpoint's supporting mass as residents voting for that endpoint because it is farther away: locations at or below the midpoint vote for endpoint 1, including midpoint residents.
  • Resolved equal vote totals in favor of the prediction, as prescribed by the discrete rule.
  • Allowed coalitions to contain arbitrary fractions of each type's mass and to split that mass across measurable reports; extended the voting formula to the resulting report measures.
  • Used the discrete definition's prohibition on unanimous strict improvement, expressed through a positive coalition mass of residents who do not improve.
  • Kept the prediction and parameter fixed during manipulation and treated any welfare-maximizing predicted endpoint as a correct prediction, including ties.
  • Defined the welfare benchmark over the entire segment and expressed approximation guarantees as inequalities to avoid undefined ratios at zero welfare.

Result

Yes. For every fixed \(\lambda\in[0,1)\), the rule is group-strategyproof under the stated positive-mass coalition definition and satisfies robustness \(\frac{3+\lambda}{1-\lambda}\) and consistency \(\frac{3-\lambda}{1+\lambda}\), including tied optima. Both constants are sharp even for societies with support size \(\tau=2\).

The proof below establishes the mass formulation directly, without reducing it to the finite-list result of [Istrate–Bonchiş](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09521).

1. Vote thresholds and endpoint preferences

Define the measurable partition \(A_1=[0,1/2]\) and \(A_0=(1/2,1]\). Thus \(v_j(\sigma)=\sigma(A_j)\) and \(v_0(\sigma)+v_1(\sigma)=1\).

The rule selects the prediction precisely when

\[ v_p(\sigma)\ge \frac{1-\lambda}{2}, \]

and selects the other endpoint precisely when

\[ v_{1-p}(\sigma)>\frac{1+\lambda}{2}. \]

Endpoint utilities are \(u_x(0)=x\) and \(u_x(1)=1-x\). Consequently, whenever \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\) are distinct, every resident in \(A_a\) satisfies \(u_x(b)\le u_x(a)\). This includes indifference at \(x=1/2\) when \(a=1\).

2. Group strategyproofness

Fix a coalition \(0\le\nu\le\mu\) of total mass \(t>0\), and any report measure \(\rho\) of total mass \(t\). Let \(\mu'=\mu-\nu+\rho\), keeping \(p\) fixed.

Write \(a=f_\lambda(\mu,p)\) and \(b=f_\lambda(\mu',p)\). If \(a=b\), every coalition member has unchanged utility, so the required nonimproving mass is \(t>0\).

Suppose therefore that \(a\ne b\). We first show that the reported vote mass for \(b\) strictly increases.

  • If \(b=p\), the original selection of \(a\) gives \(v_b(\mu)<(1-\lambda)/2\), while the new selection gives \(v_b(\mu')\ge(1-\lambda)/2\).
  • If \(b=1-p\), the original selection of \(a=p\) gives \(v_b(\mu)\le(1+\lambda)/2\), while the new selection gives \(v_b(\mu')>(1+\lambda)/2\).

In either case, \(\Delta:=v_b(\mu')-v_b(\mu)>0\). Mass conservation now gives

\[ \Delta=\rho(A_b)-\nu(A_b) \le t-\nu(A_b)=\nu(A_a). \]

Hence \(\nu(A_a)>0\). Every resident in \(A_a\) weakly prefers the original endpoint \(a\) to the new endpoint \(b\), by Section 1. Therefore

\[ \nu\bigl(\{x:u_x(b)\le u_x(a)\}\bigr) \ge\nu(A_a)>0. \]

This proves exactly the required group-strategyproofness statement. The argument allows coalition masses and reports to be arbitrary measures; neither indivisibility nor rationality is used.

3. Welfare and the approximation bounds

Put \(w_j=W_\mu(j)\) for \(j\in\{0,1\}\). Because \(\mu\) is a probability measure,

\[ w_0+w_1=\int(x+1-x)\,d\mu(x)=1. \]

For every \(x,y\in[0,1]\), the triangle inequality gives

\[ |y-x|=|(1-y)(-x)+y(1-x)| \le(1-y)x+y(1-x). \]

Integrating shows that \(W_\mu(y)\le(1-y)w_0+yw_1\le\max\{w_0,w_1\}\). Since both endpoints are available, it follows that

\[ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu)=\max\{w_0,w_1\}. \]

Let \(q=f_\lambda(\mu,p)\), and let \(s=v_q(\mu)\). Every resident in \(A_q\) has distance at least \(1/2\) from \(q\), so \(w_q\ge s/2\). The selection thresholds ensure \(s>0\), because \(\lambda<1\). Using \(w_{1-q}=1-w_q\), we obtain

\[ \frac{\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)}{w_q} =\max\left\{1,\frac{1-w_q}{w_q}\right\} \le\max\left\{1,\frac{2}{s}-1\right\} =\frac{2}{s}-1, \]

where the last equality uses \(s\le1\).

Robustness

If \(q=p\), then \(s\ge(1-\lambda)/2\), giving

\[ \frac{\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)}{W_\mu(q)} \le\frac{4}{1-\lambda}-1 =\frac{3+\lambda}{1-\lambda}. \]

If \(q\ne p\), then \(s>(1+\lambda)/2\), giving

\[ \frac{\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)}{W_\mu(q)} <\frac{4}{1+\lambda}-1 =\frac{3-\lambda}{1+\lambda} \le\frac{3+\lambda}{1-\lambda}. \]

Thus the claimed robustness holds for every prediction.

Consistency, including tied optima

Assume \(W_\mu(p)=\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\).

If \(q=p\), the welfare ratio is \(1\). If \(q\ne p\), the preceding bound is strictly below \((3-\lambda)/(1+\lambda)\). Since this constant is at least \(1\), consistency follows.

This argument includes tied optima. In that case \(w_0=w_1=1/2\), and either output has ratio \(1\).

4. Sharpness within the permitted input class

For any dyadic rational \(s\in(0,1)\), consider

\[ \mu_s=s\delta_{1/2}+(1-s)\delta_1. \]

This is a permitted society with two distinct binary-rational locations and positive binary-rational masses. Its votes and welfare are

\[ v_1(\mu_s)=s,\qquad v_0(\mu_s)=1-s,\qquad W_{\mu_s}(1)=\frac{s}{2},\qquad W_{\mu_s}(0)=1-\frac{s}{2}. \]

Endpoint \(0\) is uniquely optimal because \(s<1\). Whenever the rule selects \(1\), its welfare ratio is \(2/s-1\).

For robustness, use prediction \(p=1\) and choose dyadic values \(s\) approaching \((1-\lambda)/2\) from above. The rule selects \(1\), and the ratios approach \((3+\lambda)/(1-\lambda)\).

For consistency, use the optimal prediction \(p=0\) and choose dyadic values \(s\) approaching \((1+\lambda)/2\) from above. The rule again selects \(1\), and the ratios approach \((3-\lambda)/(1+\lambda)\).

Both sequences exist by density of dyadic rationals, since their limiting thresholds lie strictly between \(0\) and \(1\). Hence neither guarantee can be improved uniformly over the permitted inputs.

5. Evaluation complexity

There are \(m=2\) mechanism outputs. Evaluating the votes, the selected endpoint, and both endpoint welfare values requires \(O(\tau+m)\) rational arithmetic operations, without expanding masses into equally weighted residents.

If \(\lambda\) is supplied as a rational with encoding length \(B\), direct exact arithmetic gives a conservative bit complexity of \(O((\tau+m)(L+B)^2)\): intermediate numerators and denominators have \(O(L+B)\) bits, and schoolbook arithmetic suffices.
