Theorem 5.15 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) items, a finite valuation-type support \(T\), population proportions \(\mu_t\), total mass \(N\), and a bound \(K\), an adversary reveals at most \(K\) ordered cohorts of total mass \(N\), with type totals \(N\mu_t\). Each infinitesimal buyer chooses a utility-maximizing integral bundle at the causal posted prices; the seller's marginal costs are \(c_i(q)=q^d\), with cumulative costs \(C_i(x)=\int_0^x q^d\,dq\). For \(0<\epsilon<1\), \(Z\in(U_{\max},U_{\max}/\epsilon]\), ℓ_i=\min\{Z^{1/d},N\}, and \(B_i=\lceil12\log(4n\ell_i/\epsilon)\rceil\), under the fluid analogues of Theorem 5.15's threshold assumptions, does a causal pricing policy achieve \(W_{\mathrm{on}}\ge\frac{W^*-18\sum_i B_i c_i(B_i)}{18(d+1)(27/16)^{d+1}}\) for every admissible cohort order? Here \(W^*\) is the offline welfare optimum over fractional cohort-to-bundle assignments.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), where \(m=n\) is the number of items. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(a_t=N\mu_t\), where \(N>0\), \(\mu_t>0\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has an explicitly tabulated valuation \(v_t:2^I\to\mathbb Q\), with \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\); values may be negative and need not be monotone. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; running times are measured in \(m,\tau,L,K,d\).
For an integer \(K\ge1\), an adversary reveals at most \(K\) ordered positive-mass cohorts, specified by nonnegative rational type masses \(b_{jt}\), with \(\sum_j b_{jt}=a_t\). Within cohort \(j\), types flow at constant proportions \(b_{jt}/\sum_t b_{jt}\).
The seller knows the society and numerical parameters but not future cohorts. A deterministic causal policy posts nonnegative item prices and may update them continuously using past purchases. Almost every infinitesimal buyer chooses an integral bundle maximizing \(v_t(S)-\sum_{i\in S}p_i\); ties are arbitrary, and purchases are irrevocable without rationing.
For an integer \(d\ge1\), marginal and cumulative production costs are \(c_i(q)=q^d\) and \(C_i(x)=x^{d+1}/(d+1)\), for \(q,x\ge0\). Allocating bundle \(S\) to mass \(h\) contributes value \(hv_t(S)\) and consumes mass \(h\) of each item in \(S\).
Welfare is total allocated value minus \(\sum_i C_i(x_i)\). Let \(W^*\) maximize this welfare over nonnegative cohort-to-bundle masses \(z_{jtS}\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq I}z_{jtS}=b_{jt}\); let \(W_{\mathrm{on}}\) denote realized online welfare.
Set \(U_{\max}=\max_{t,S}v_t(S)>0\). Given rational \(0<\epsilon<1\) and \(U_{\max}<Z\le U_{\max}/\epsilon\), define \(\ell_i=\min\{Z^{1/d},N\}\) and \(B_i=\lceil12\log_2(4m\ell_i/\epsilon)\rceil\). Assume \(N\ge Z^{1/d}\), \(B_i\ge18(2d+1)\), and \(\ell_i\ge2(B_i+1)\) for every item.

Does a deterministic causal posted-pricing policy guarantee \(W_{\mathrm{on}}\ge\frac{W^*-18\sum_i B_i c_i(B_i)}{18(d+1)(27/16)^{d+1}}\) for every admissible cohort sequence and every utility-maximizing tie choice, or is this guarantee impossible?

For discrete buyers with marginal costs \((k-1)^d\), smoothing prices achieve the displayed approximation with the corresponding discrete thresholds ([Blum et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.4992)).
For infinitesimal buyers with continuously updated prices and cumulative power costs, an existing posted-pricing result achieves competitive factor \((d+1)^{(d+1)/d}\); it already implies the requested guarantee ([Huang–Kim](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.4384)).

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result and policy

The guarantee is achievable. In fact, a deterministic causal policy guarantees the stronger inequality
\[ W_{\mathrm{on}}\ge \frac{W^*}{(d+1)^{(d+1)/d}}, \]
with no additive loss.

Post the price \(p_i(q_i)=(d+1)q_i^d\), where \(q_i\) is the mass of item \(i\) already purchased. This policy is nonnegative, continuous, and depends only on past purchases. It is the power-cost specialization of the continuous posted-pricing method of [Huang–Kim, Sections 3.1 and 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.4384). The proof below establishes the bound directly for the stated model.

Accounting for the cohort flow

Fix any admissible cohort sequence and any realized utility-maximizing tie choices. Parameterize arrivals by total buyer mass \(s\in[0,N]\). Within cohort \(j\), whose total mass is \(H_j=\sum_t b_{jt}>0\), let the type-\(t\) arrival rate be \(\theta_t(s)=b_{jt}/H_j\). Consequently,
\[ \int_0^N\theta_t(s)\,ds=a_t. \]

Let \(w_{tS}(s)\) denote the rate of type-\(t\) buyers choosing bundle \(S\). These rates describe integral choices by nonatomic buyers; they do not assign fractional bundles to individual buyers. Almost everywhere,
\[ w_{tS}(s)\ge0,\qquad \sum_Sw_{tS}(s)=\theta_t(s),\qquad q_i'(s)=\sum_t\sum_{S\ni i}w_{tS}(s). \]
In particular, each \(q_i\) is nondecreasing and absolutely continuous. Write \(x_i=q_i(N)\) and \(P_i=(d+1)x_i^d\) for the final quantity and price.

For any price vector \(p\), define
\[ u_t(p)=\max_{S\subseteq I}\left(v_t(S)-\sum_{i\in S}p_i\right). \]
The empty bundle implies \(u_t(p)\ge0\). Moreover, \(u_t\) is nonincreasing in each price coordinate.

Let
\[ A=\int_0^N\sum_t\theta_t(s)u_t(p(s))\,ds \]
be total realized buyer utility. Utility maximization means that every bundle chosen with positive rate has utility \(u_t(p(s))\), regardless of ties.

Prices only increase, so \(p_i(s)\le P_i\). Therefore,
\[ A\ge\sum_ta_tu_t(P)\ge0. \tag{1} \]

Exact online welfare identity

Utility maximization gives the following decomposition of total allocated value:
\[ \int_0^N\sum_{t,S}w_{tS}(s)v_t(S)\,ds = A+\sum_i\int_0^Np_i(q_i(s))q_i'(s)\,ds. \]
By the chain rule for absolutely continuous functions,
\[ \int_0^N(d+1)q_i(s)^dq_i'(s)\,ds=x_i^{d+1}. \]
Subtracting the cumulative production costs yields
\[ W_{\mathrm{on}} = A+\frac{d}{d+1}\sum_i x_i^{d+1}. \]
Define \(M=\frac{d}{d+1}\sum_i x_i^{d+1}\). Thus,
\[ W_{\mathrm{on}}=A+M,\qquad A\ge0,\quad M\ge0. \tag{2} \]

An upper bound on the offline optimum

Consider any feasible offline allocation \(z_{jtS}\), and let
\[ y_i=\sum_{j,t}\sum_{S\ni i}z_{jtS} \]
be its total production of item \(i\).

By the definition of \(u_t(P)\), every bundle satisfies
\[ v_t(S)\le u_t(P)+\sum_{i\in S}P_i. \]
Multiplying by \(z_{jtS}\), summing, and using
\(\sum_{j,S}z_{jtS}=\sum_jb_{jt}=a_t\), its welfare is at most
\[ \sum_ta_tu_t(P)+\sum_i\left(P_iy_i-\frac{y_i^{d+1}}{d+1}\right). \]

For \(p\ge0\), direct maximization over \(y\ge0\) gives
\[ \sup_{y\ge0}\left(py-\frac{y^{d+1}}{d+1}\right) = \frac{d}{d+1}p^{(d+1)/d}. \]
Indeed, for \(p>0\) the derivative is \(p-y^d\), which changes sign at \(y=p^{1/d}\); for \(p=0\), the maximum is attained at \(y=0\).

Set \(\alpha=(d+1)^{(d+1)/d}\). Since \(P_i=(d+1)x_i^d\),
\[ \frac{d}{d+1}P_i^{(d+1)/d} = \alpha\frac{d}{d+1}x_i^{d+1}. \]
The preceding bound applies to every feasible offline allocation, hence
\[ W^* \le \sum_ta_tu_t(P)+\alpha M \le A+\alpha M \le\alpha(A+M) =\alpha W_{\mathrm{on}}. \]
The second inequality uses (1), and the third uses \(A\ge0\) and \(\alpha\ge1\). This proves the stronger guarantee.

Negative valuations and nonmonotonicity cause no difficulty: the argument uses only utility maximization and the availability of the zero-valued empty bundle.

Comparison with the requested bound

Let
\[ F=18(d+1)(27/16)^{d+1}, \qquad E=18\sum_iB_ic_i(B_i)=18\sum_iB_i^{d+1}. \]
The assumptions imply \(E\ge0\). Allocating the empty bundle to everyone gives \(W^*\ge0\).

For every integer \(d\ge1\), the binomial theorem gives \(d+1\le2^d\). Thus
\[ \alpha=(d+1)(d+1)^{1/d}\le2(d+1)\le F. \]
Consequently,
\[ W_{\mathrm{on}} \ge\frac{W^*}{\alpha} \ge\frac{W^*}{F} \ge\frac{W^*-E}{F}, \]
which is exactly the requested guarantee. The proof holds for every admissible cohort sequence and every realized utility-maximizing tie choice.
