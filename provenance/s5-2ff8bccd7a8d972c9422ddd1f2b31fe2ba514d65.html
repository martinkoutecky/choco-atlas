Theorem 15 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given goods with per-capita supplies \(q_j\), a support \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), and each type's cap and explicit nonincreasing marginal utilities, let \(z\) range over integral bundles and let \(U_t(z)=\min(c_t,\sum_j\sum_{\ell=1}^{z_j}u_{t,j,\ell})\). Choose masses \(x_{t,z}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_z x_{t,z}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}z_j\le q_j\); can one compute an allocation whose \(\exp(\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}\log U_t(z))\) is within factor \(e^{1/e}+\varepsilon\) of the maximum, in time polynomial in the encoded instance and the support rather than an expanded population?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) goods with nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\). For each good, an integer \(K_j\ge1\) specifies the explicit marginal-list length; each type has a positive binary-encoded integer cap \(c_t\) and integer marginals \(c_t\ge u_{t,j,1}\ge\cdots\ge u_{t,j,K_j}\ge0\).
Bundles belong to \(Z=\prod_{j=1}^m\{0,\ldots,K_j\}\), and \(U_t(z)=\min\{c_t,\sum_{j=1}^m\sum_{\ell=1}^{z_j}u_{t,j,\ell}\}\).
An allocation consists of masses \(x_{t,z}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{z\in Z}x_{t,z}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T,z\in Z}x_{t,z}z_j\le q_j\). Its welfare is \(W(x)=\exp(\sum_{t,z:x_{t,z}>0}x_{t,z}\log U_t(z))\), with \(W(x)=0\) if any positive allocated mass receives zero utility. Let \(W^*\) be the maximum feasible welfare.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including all explicit marginal lists; allocations are represented by their nonzero rational masses and bundles.
For every rational \(\varepsilon>0\), can one compute a feasible allocation satisfying \(W(x)\ge W^*/(e^{1/e}+\varepsilon)\) in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\)?
For finitely listed agents with capped, nonincreasing marginal utilities, a polynomial-time \((e^{1/e}+\varepsilon)\)-approximation is known. [Chaudhury et al.](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol122-fsttcs2018/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2018.25/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2018.25.pdf)
For finite agents with uncapped additive valuations and single-copy items, the logarithmic bundle-allocation relaxation can be optimized within additive error \(\log(1+\varepsilon)\) in polynomial time; this result does not cover the full model above. [Feng and Li](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol297-icalp2024/LIPIcs.ICALP.2024.63/LIPIcs.ICALP.2024.63.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total society mass to one so that the stated exponential objective is a geometric mean and supplies are per capita.
  • Specify separate explicit marginal-list lengths K_j and restrict each integral bundle coordinate to that length; fractional aggregate supply q_j does not bound an individual's bundle by its integer part.
  • Retain integer utilities and caps, require nonnegative marginals and positive caps, and impose the harmless marginal truncation at the cap.
  • Allow nonnegative rational supplies and unused supply, as in the graded feasibility constraints.
  • Define welfare as zero whenever positive mass receives zero utility, retaining instances with zero optimum.
  • Represent computed allocations by nonzero rational masses and bundles, and make the approximation-parameter dependence polynomial in 1/epsilon.

Conclusion

Yes. There is a deterministic algorithm, polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), that returns a feasible rational allocation satisfying

\(W(x)\ge e^{-1/4}W^*.\)

Since \(1/4<1/e\), this is stronger than the requested guarantee for every positive rational \(\varepsilon\).

The algorithm uses a polynomial-size flow formulation with geometrically rounded cumulative utilities. It operates directly on the listed types and their masses.

1. Positive-utility allocations

Write

\(A_{t,j}(k)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}u_{t,j,\ell},\qquad A_{t,j}(0)=0.\)

These prefix sums are nonnegative integers and have polynomial binary encoding length.

Call an allocation *positive* if every bundle receiving positive mass has positive utility for its assigned type. For a positive allocation, define its logarithmic welfare by

\(F(x)=\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}\log U_t(z).\)

Thus \(W(x)=\exp(F(x))\).

If no positive feasible allocation exists, every feasible allocation has welfare zero. In that case, assigning the zero bundle to each type is a valid answer.

Otherwise, restricting attention to positive-utility bundles gives a nonempty compact polytope: there are finitely many bundles, all masses lie between zero and one, and the constraints are closed. On this polytope \(F\) is a finite linear objective, so it attains a maximum \(F^*\), with \(W^*=\exp(F^*)\).

The exponential number of bundles prevents directly writing this linear program. We replace it with a polynomial-size approximation.

2. A rounded utility state

Set

\(r=1+\frac{1}{8m}.\)

For each type, construct the rational grid

\(G_t=\{0,c_t\}\cup\{r^h:h\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\ r^h<c_t\}.\)

For \(v\in[0,c_t]\), let \(R_t(v)\) be the largest grid element at most \(v\).

For every \(v\in[1,c_t]\),

\(\frac{v}{r}\le R_t(v)\le v.\)

Indeed, consecutive positive grid elements have ratio at most \(r\), including the final interval ending at \(c_t\).

Given a bundle \(z\), define its rounded states recursively by

\(s_0=0,\qquad s_j=R_t\!\left(\min\{c_t,s_{j-1}+A_{t,j}(z_j)\}\right).\)

Also define its exact capped prefix utilities by

\(V_0=0,\qquad V_j=\min\{c_t,V_{j-1}+A_{t,j}(z_j)\}.\)

Consequently, \(V_m=U_t(z)\).

Rounded-state lemma

For every bundle and every stage \(j\),

\(\frac{V_j}{r^j}\le s_j\le V_j,\qquad s_j=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ V_j=0.\)

Proof. The statement holds at stage zero. Suppose it holds at stage \(j-1\), and put \(a=A_{t,j}(z_j)\) and \(b=r^{j-1}\).

The induction hypothesis gives

\(s_{j-1}+a\ge V_{j-1}/b+a\ge (V_{j-1}+a)/b.\)

Since \(b\ge1\), taking the cap preserves this lower bound in the following form:

\(\min\{c_t,s_{j-1}+a\}\ge \frac{\min\{c_t,V_{j-1}+a\}}{b}=\frac{V_j}{b}.\)

If \(V_j>0\), then either \(s_{j-1}>0\), in which case \(s_{j-1}\ge1\), or \(a>0\), in which case the integrality of \(a\) gives \(a\ge1\). Therefore the argument of \(R_t\) lies in \([1,c_t]\). Applying the grid inequality yields

\(s_j\ge V_j/r^j>0.\)

The upper bound follows from \(s_{j-1}\le V_{j-1}\), monotonicity of addition and capping, and downward rounding. If \(V_j=0\), this upper bound forces \(s_j=0\). This proves the induction. \(\square\)

In particular, a bundle has positive utility exactly when its terminal rounded state is positive. For such a bundle,

\(\log U_t(z)-m\log r\le\log s_m\le\log U_t(z).\)

3. A polynomial-size flow network

For each type \(t\), construct a directed acyclic network.

  • Its source is the state \((0,0)\).
  • At every layer \(j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), there is a node \((j,s)\) for each \(s\in G_t\).
  • For every state \(s\) at layer \(j-1\) and every \(k\in\{0,\ldots,K_j\}\), add an arc to

\((j,R_t(\min\{c_t,s+A_{t,j}(k)\}))\).
Label this arc with the quantity \(k\) of good \(j\).

  • Add an arc from every positive terminal state \((m,s)\) to a sink. There is no sink arc from terminal state zero.

Parallel arcs with different quantity labels are retained.

A source-to-sink path chooses exactly one quantity for each good, hence determines a bundle. Its states follow the recursion in Section 2, and its terminal state is positive. Conversely, every positive-utility bundle determines such a path.

Let \(f_{t,a}\) be the flow on arc \(a\). Require source outflow and sink inflow to equal \(\mu_t\), and impose flow conservation at every other node. We may also impose the bounds \(0\le f_{t,a}\le\mu_t\).

For each good \(j\), impose the resource constraint

\(\sum_t\sum_{\substack{a\text{ chooses a quantity}\\\text{of good }j}}k(a)f_{t,a}\le q_j.\)

These constraints preserve supplies exactly.

Equivalence of feasibility

A positive feasible allocation induces feasible flows by sending its bundle masses along their corresponding paths.

Conversely, every feasible flow decomposes into source-to-sink paths. Giving each path’s mass to its associated bundle produces a positive feasible allocation. The resource usage is unchanged because path decomposition preserves every arc’s flow.

Consequently, this flow system is feasible if and only if a positive feasible allocation exists.

Network size

For \(0<a\le1\), the identity

\(\log(1+a)=\int_0^a\frac{1}{1+v}\,dv\)

implies \(\log(1+a)\ge a/2\). Hence

\(\log r\ge\frac{1}{16m}.\)

Since the binary encoding length of every cap is at most \(L\),

\(|G_t|=O(1+mL).\)

Each grid element is a rational power of \(r\) with exponent \(O(mL)\), so its binary encoding length is polynomial in \(m,L\).

The number of arcs per type is

\(O\!\left((1+mL)\left(1+\sum_j(K_j+1)\right)\right).\)

The marginal lists are explicit, so \(\sum_jK_j\le L\). Thus the complete network, including its rational coefficients, has polynomial encoding length.

4. Rational objective coefficients

To obtain an ordinary rational linear program, compute for every positive grid state \(s\) a rational number \(b_t(s)\) satisfying

\(\log s-\frac18\le b_t(s)\le\log s.\)

Here is an explicit polynomial-time construction.

Write \(s=2^k y\), where \(k\) is a nonnegative integer and \(1\le y<2\). Because \(s\le c_t\), we have \(k\le L\).

For \(x\in[1,2]\), put

\(B_N(x)=2\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{2n+1}\left(\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right)^{2n+1}.\)

The logarithm series gives \(B_N(x)\le\log x\). Since \((x-1)/(x+1)\le1/3\), its tail satisfies

\(0\le\log x-B_N(x)\le2\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}(1/3)^{2n+1}=\frac34\,9^{-N}.\)

Choose an integer \(N\) with \(9^N\ge6(L+1)\), and set

\(b_t(s)=kB_N(2)+B_N(y).\)

Then

\(0\le\log s-b_t(s)\le(k+1)\frac34\,9^{-N}\le\frac18.\)

Here \(N=O(\log(L+1))\). All quantities in these finite sums are rational, and their encoding lengths and computation times are polynomial in the input length.

Assign objective coefficient \(b_t(s)\) to the sink arc from terminal state \(s\), and coefficient zero to every other arc. Let \(Q(f)\) denote the resulting linear objective.

5. Optimization and approximation guarantee

Use the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: a rational linear program of polynomial encoding length can be tested for feasibility and, when feasible and bounded, optimized exactly with a rational optimal solution of polynomial encoding length.

Its hypotheses hold here: the network and coefficients have polynomial encoding length, and every variable has an explicit finite bound.

If the linear program is infeasible, Section 3 shows that \(W^*=0\); return the zero-bundle allocation.

Otherwise, let \(\widehat f\) be an optimal flow.

Embed a welfare-optimal positive allocation \(x^*\) into the network, obtaining \(f^*\). For each of its paths, Sections 2 and 4 give

\(b_t(s_m)\ge\log U_t(z)-m\log r-\frac18.\)

Because the allocated masses sum to \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\),

\(Q(f^*)\ge F^*-m\log r-\frac18.\)

Optimality of \(\widehat f\) therefore gives

\(Q(\widehat f)\ge F^*-m\log r-\frac18.\)

Decompose \(\widehat f\) into paths and let \(\widehat x\) be the resulting allocation. On every path,

\(\log U_t(z)\ge\log s_m\ge b_t(s_m).\)

Summing over path masses yields

\(F(\widehat x)\ge Q(\widehat f).\)

Finally, \(\log(1+a)\le a\) gives

\(m\log r\le\frac18.\)

Combining the inequalities,

\(F(\widehat x)\ge F^*-\frac14,\)

and therefore

\(W(\widehat x)\ge e^{-1/4}W^*.\)

6. Rational output and running time

Path decomposition can be performed by repeatedly choosing a positive-flow source-to-sink path and subtracting the minimum flow on that path. Conservation ensures that such a path exists whenever source flow remains.

Each subtraction eliminates at least one positive-flow arc, so the number of extracted paths is at most the number of network arcs. Thus the output has polynomial support size.

All extracted masses are rational with polynomial encoding length. To see this explicitly, take the product of the denominators of the polynomially many rational arc flows as a common denominator. Its binary length is polynomial, and every subtraction preserves that denominator. Duplicate bundles can be merged without changing welfare or feasibility.

The resulting algorithm runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), independently of the precision or denominators of the type masses beyond their binary input lengths.

7. Comparison with the requested bound

The elementary series estimate \(e<3<4\) implies \(1/e>1/4\). Hence, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),

\(e^{1/4}<e^{1/e}+\varepsilon.\)

The allocation proved above consequently satisfies the requested guarantee.

The construction handles capped utilities and all explicitly listed quantities directly. The cited [Chaudhury et al. result](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol122-fsttcs2018/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2018.25/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2018.25.pdf) concerns finite-agent allocations, while [Feng and Li’s configuration formulation](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol297-icalp2024/LIPIcs.ICALP.2024.63/LIPIcs.ICALP.2024.63.pdf) treats additive valuations. Neither theorem is needed for the extension proved here.
