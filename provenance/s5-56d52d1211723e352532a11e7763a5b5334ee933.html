The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite \(V\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\) and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(V\) with \(\LOT(\mu)>0\), consider populations of \(N\) agents with \(N\mu_v\) agents of value \(v\), arriving in uniformly random order. What is the largest universal \(c\) for which one family of deterministic, truthful, individually rational online posted-price policies, not given \(\mu\), guarantees \(\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[U_N]/\LOT(\mu)\ge c\) for every fixed \(\mu\), where \(N\) ranges over sizes making all \(N\mu_v\) integral and \(\LOT(\mu)=\max_{p\ge0}\frac{\sum_{v\in V}\mu_v(v-p)^+}{\sum_{v\in V:v>p}\mu_v}\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a finite list \(V\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_v\), where \(\sum_{v\in V}\mu_v=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; running-time parameters are the number of items \(m=1\), \(\tau\), total support-encoding bit length \(L\), and population size \(N\).
For each \(N\) with all \(N\mu_v\) integral, there are \(N\mu_v\) distinct agents of value \(v\), arriving in uniformly random order; equal values are allowed.
A deterministic online posted-price policy knows \(N\), but receives no support, distribution, or predictions. It observes each arriving agent's reported value, including reports from agents it declines to serve.
While its single item remains available, the policy may decline to serve the current agent or offer a nonnegative price determined solely by \(N\), the arrival position, and earlier reports. Allocation is immediate and irrevocable when the current report strictly exceeds the offered price; equality means rejection.
Only the recipient pays, obtaining utility \(v-p\); all other agents pay zero. Require dominant-strategy truthfulness and individual rationality, and let \(U_N\) be the recipient's utility, or zero if no allocation occurs.
Define \(\operatorname{LOT}(\mu)=\max_{0\le p<\max V}\frac{\sum_{v\in V:v>p}\mu_v(v-p)}{\sum_{v\in V:v>p}\mu_v}\), and restrict to \(\operatorname{LOT}(\mu)>0\).

What is the supremum of universal constants \(c\ge0\) for which one family of such policies guarantees \(\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[U_N]/\operatorname{LOT}(\mu)\ge c\) for every fixed \(\mu\), with the limit taken over integral population sizes?

For finite populations with distinct values, a deterministic truthful online mechanism guarantees a \(1/(625e)\) approximation to the optimal lottery under random arrival order; its constants are not claimed tight. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00175v1)

Result

The supremum is \(\boxed{1}\).

A policy can learn an asymptotically optimal lottery price from an initial sample of agents it declines to serve. Because the society is fixed while its population grows, this sample learns the relevant prices and masses while consuming a vanishing fraction of the population.

1. Reducing the benchmark to finitely many prices

Fix a society satisfying the hypotheses. Write \(b=\max V\). The assumption \(\operatorname{LOT}(\mu)>0\) implies \(b>0\). Define
\[ P=\{0\}\cup\{v\in V:v<b\}. \]
For \(q\in P\), define
\[ A_\mu(q)=\sum_{v>q}\mu_v,\qquad B_\mu(q)=\sum_{v>q}\mu_v(v-q),\qquad R_\mu(q)=\frac{B_\mu(q)}{A_\mu(q)}. \]
These denominators are positive: \(b>q\), so \(A_\mu(q)\ge\mu_b>0\).

For any admissible price \(p\in[0,b)\), let \(q\) be the largest member of \(\{0\}\cup V\) not exceeding \(p\). There is no support value in \((q,p]\), and therefore the sets of values strictly exceeding \(q\) and \(p\) coincide. Consequently,
\[ R_\mu(p)=R_\mu(q)-(p-q)\le R_\mu(q). \]
Also \(q\in P\). Thus
\[ \operatorname{LOT}(\mu)=\max_{q\in P}R_\mu(q). \]
In particular, the maximum in the benchmark definition exists.

2. A universal deterministic policy

For \(N\ge2\), let \(s_N=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor\); let \(s_1=0\).

The policy declines to serve the first \(s_N\) agents and records their reports. When \(s_N>0\), it computes the empirical distribution \(\widehat\mu\) of those reports and considers the candidate set
\[ \widehat P = \left\{ q\in\{0\}\cup\{\text{sampled values}\}: \sum_{v>q}\widehat\mu_v>0 \right\}. \]
If this set is nonempty, it chooses a price \(q_N\) maximizing
\[ R_{\widehat\mu}(q) = \frac{\sum_{v>q}\widehat\mu_v(v-q)} {\sum_{v>q}\widehat\mu_v}, \]
breaking ties by choosing the smallest price. If the set is empty, or \(s_N=0\), it sets \(q_N=0\).

It then offers the fixed price \(q_N\) to every remaining agent until allocation occurs.

This is one deterministic family of policies, using only \(N\) and earlier reports.

Truthfulness and individual rationality. A sampled agent receives no allocation and pays zero regardless of its report, so truthful reporting is weakly dominant. For any subsequent agent, the offered price is independent of its own report. If its true value strictly exceeds that price, truthful reporting obtains positive utility; otherwise rejection yields zero, and inducing allocation cannot yield positive utility. Thus truthful reporting is weakly dominant, including at equality. Every truthful recipient has nonnegative utility.

3. Consistency of the learned price

All limits below are taken over integral population sizes.

Let \(C_v\) be the number of sampled agents of type \(v\), so that \(\widehat\mu_v=C_v/s_N\). Sampling a uniformly random prefix is sampling without replacement. For \(N\ge2\),
\[ \mathbb E[\widehat\mu_v]=\mu_v,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\widehat\mu_v) = \frac{\mu_v(1-\mu_v)}{s_N} \frac{N-s_N}{N-1}. \]
Indeed, indicators for two distinct sampled positions have covariance
\[ \frac{(N\mu_v)(N\mu_v-1)}{N(N-1)}-\mu_v^2 = -\frac{\mu_v(1-\mu_v)}{N-1}, \]
which gives the variance formula.

Since \(s_N\to\infty\), Chebyshev’s inequality and a union bound over the fixed finite support imply
\[ d_N:=\max_{v\in V}|\widehat\mu_v-\mu_v| \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0. \]
Because every \(\mu_v>0\), the sample contains every support value with probability tending to one. On that event, \(\widehat P=P\).

For completeness, the empirical objectives also converge uniformly over \(P\). If a probability vector \(\eta\) on \(V\) satisfies
\[ d=\max_{v\in V}|\eta_v-\mu_v|, \qquad \tau d\le\frac{\mu_b}{2}, \]
then, for every \(q\in P\),
\[ |A_\eta(q)-A_\mu(q)|\le\tau d,\qquad |B_\eta(q)-B_\mu(q)|\le b\tau d. \]
Moreover, \(A_\eta(q)\ge\mu_b/2\) and \(B_\mu(q)\le bA_\mu(q)\). Hence
\[ |R_\eta(q)-R_\mu(q)| \le \frac{|B_\eta(q)-B_\mu(q)|}{A_\eta(q)} + \frac{B_\mu(q)|A_\eta(q)-A_\mu(q)|} {A_\mu(q)A_\eta(q)} \le \frac{4b\tau d}{\mu_b}. \]

Let \(q^\star\in P\) maximize \(R_\mu\). On the event \(\widehat P=P\), empirical optimality gives
\[ R_\mu(q_N) \ge R_{\widehat\mu}(q_N)-\varepsilon_N \ge R_{\widehat\mu}(q^\star)-\varepsilon_N \ge \operatorname{LOT}(\mu)-2\varepsilon_N, \]
where
\[ \varepsilon_N=\max_{q\in P}|R_{\widehat\mu}(q)-R_\mu(q)| \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0. \]
Under truthful reports, the selected price always belongs to \(P\), including the fallback price. Thus \(0\le R_\mu(q_N)\le\operatorname{LOT}(\mu)\le b\), and we obtain
\[ R_\mu(q_N)\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\operatorname{LOT}(\mu), \qquad \mathbb E[R_\mu(q_N)]\longrightarrow\operatorname{LOT}(\mu). \]
The expectation convergence follows from boundedness: for every \(\epsilon>0\), the expected absolute error is at most
\[ \epsilon+b\,\mathbb P\!\left( |R_\mu(q_N)-\operatorname{LOT}(\mu)|>\epsilon \right). \]

4. Expected utility of the policy

Condition on the entire sampled prefix. The remaining masses are
\[ \widetilde\mu_v=\frac{N\mu_v-C_v}{N-s_N}. \]
For every possible prefix,
\[ |\widetilde\mu_v-\mu_v| = \frac{|s_N\mu_v-C_v|}{N-s_N} \le \frac{s_N}{N-s_N}. \]
Since \(s_N/N\to0\), the ratio estimate from the previous section therefore gives, uniformly over all prefixes and all \(q\in P\),
\[ |R_{\widetilde\mu}(q)-R_\mu(q)| \le \frac{4b\tau}{\mu_b}\frac{s_N}{N-s_N} \longrightarrow0 \]
for all sufficiently large \(N\).

Also, \(N\mu_b>s_N\) for all sufficiently large \(N\). Thus every remaining population contains a type-\(b\) agent, and allocation at \(q_N<b\) is certain.

Conditional on the prefix, the remaining order is uniformly random. Its first agent whose value strictly exceeds \(q_N\) is consequently uniform among all remaining eligible agents: their relative order is a uniform permutation. Therefore
\[ \mathbb E[U_N\mid\text{sampled prefix}] = R_{\widetilde\mu}(q_N). \]
Taking expectations and using the uniform bound yields
\[ \left|\mathbb E[U_N]-\mathbb E[R_\mu(q_N)]\right| \longrightarrow0. \]
Combining this with the learned-price consistency proves
\[ \lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{\mathbb E[U_N]}{\operatorname{LOT}(\mu)} =1 \]
for every fixed society in the question.

5. Matching upper bound

Consider the permitted society \(V=\{a\}\), \(\mu_a=1\), where \(a>0\) is rational. Its benchmark is
\[ \operatorname{LOT}(\mu)=\max_{0\le p<a}(a-p)=a. \]
For every policy and every arrival order, a recipient’s utility is at most \(a\), because prices are nonnegative. If there is no allocation, utility is zero. Hence
\[ \frac{\mathbb E[U_N]}{\operatorname{LOT}(\mu)}\le1 \]
for every \(N\).

No universal constant exceeding \(1\) is possible, and the constructed family achieves \(1\). This settles the requested supremum.
