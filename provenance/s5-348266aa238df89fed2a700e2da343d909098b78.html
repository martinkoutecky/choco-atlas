The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(\mathcal{A}\), rational population masses \(\mu_a\), and a coalition structure with blocks \(q^k\) summing to \(\mu\), let each block have an explicitly listed rational polynomial worth function \(F_k(r)\), where \(0\le r\le q^k\), \(F_k(0)=0\), and aggregate worth at population scale \(n\) is \(nF_k(r)\). For integer replications \(n\) making all masses integral, compute the limit, as \(n\to\infty\), of each type's per-player Shapley payoff when the remainder of its block is treated as singleton agents and the other blocks are held fixed. Is the limit \(u_a^k=\int_0^1 \frac{\partial F_k(tq^k)}{\partial r_a}\,dt\), and can these exact payoffs be computed from the explicit polynomial input?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal A\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_a\), normalized by \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
A coalition structure consists of \(K\) nonzero vectors \(q^k\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{\mathcal A}\) with \(\sum_kq^k=\mu\). Types may be split between blocks.
For each block, the input lists a polynomial \(F_k\in\mathbb Q[r_a:a\in\mathcal A]\), with signed binary rational coefficients, nonnegative unary integer exponents, and \(F_k(0)=0\). No monotonicity, nonnegativity of worth, or superadditivity is assumed.
Let \(m\) be the total number of listed monomials, \(d\) the maximum total degree, and \(L\) the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,K,d,L\).
For every positive integer \(n\) making all \(nq_a^k\) integral, block \(S_k^{(n)}\) contains \(nq_a^k\) players of type \(a\). For \(T\subseteq S_k^{(n)}\), let \(c_a(T)\) count its type-\(a\) players and define \(w_k^{(n)}(T)=nF_k(c(T)/n)\).
This worth is evaluated with every member of \(S_k^{(n)}\setminus T\) remaining a singleton and every other block held fixed. Let \(\phi_{a,k}^{(n)}\) be the Shapley payoff of one type-\(a\) player in \(w_k^{(n)}\), defined only when \(q_a^k>0\).

Do the limits \(u_a^k=\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{a,k}^{(n)}\), along all admissible integers, exist and equal \(\int_0^1\frac{\partial F_k}{\partial r_a}(tq^k)\,dt\)? Is computing all these limits exactly as binary rationals polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,K,d,L\), or NP-hard?

Result

Both questions have affirmative answers: every specified limit exists and equals the gradient integral, and all limits can be computed exactly in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,d,L\).

The gradient integral is the Aumann–Shapley expression discussed by [Hart–Monderer](https://math.huji.ac.il/hart/papers/pot-w.pdf). Below is a direct proof for the finite games in the question, followed by an explicit binary bit-complexity bound.

1. Existence and identification of the limits

Fix a block \(k\) and a type \(a\) with \(q_a^k>0\). Write \(q=q^k\), \(F=F_k\), and \(\phi_a^{(n)}=\phi_{a,k}^{(n)}\).

Because \(q\) is rational, its coordinate denominators have a common multiple. Thus there are arbitrarily large admissible integers \(n\). For each such \(n\), fix a type-\(a\) player \(i\).

Assign every player an independent uniform priority in \([0,1]\), and order players by increasing priority. Ties have probability zero, and every permutation is equally likely. Consequently, the Shapley payoff is the expected marginal contribution of \(i\).

Let \(T\) be the priority of \(i\). Conditional on \(T=t\), let \(X_b\) count the type-\(b\) players preceding \(i\). Each other player precedes \(i\) independently with probability \(t\), so

\[ X_b\mid T=t\ \sim\ \operatorname{Bin}\!\left(nq_b-\mathbf 1_{\{b=a\}},t\right). \]

Here \(e_a\) denotes the coordinate unit vector. The random-order definition gives

\[ \phi_a^{(n)} = \int_0^1 \mathbb E\!\left[ n\left( F\left(\frac{X+e_a}{n}\right) - F\left(\frac Xn\right) \right) \,\middle|\,T=t \right]dt. \]

By the fundamental theorem of calculus, for every realization of \(X\),

\[ n\left( F\left(\frac{X+e_a}{n}\right) - F\left(\frac Xn\right) \right) = \int_0^1 \partial_aF\left(\frac Xn+\frac{s}{n}e_a\right)ds. \]

We now bound the difference from the proposed limit.

The hypotheses \(\sum_kq^k=\mu\) and \(\sum_b\mu_b=1\) imply \(0\le q_b\le1\) and \(\sum_bq_b\le1\). Both \(tq\) and \(X/n+se_a/n\) belong to \([0,1]^\tau\). Since \(F\) is a polynomial, the quantity

\[ H_a= \max_b\sup_{r\in[0,1]^\tau} \left|\partial_b\partial_aF(r)\right| \]

is finite. Integration along the segment joining two points of this cube yields

\[ |\partial_aF(x)-\partial_aF(y)| \le H_a\sum_b|x_b-y_b|. \]

For the conditional binomial variables,

\[ \mathbb E\!\left[ \left|\frac{X_b}{n}-tq_b\right| \,\middle|\,T=t \right] \le \frac{\sqrt{q_b}}{2\sqrt n} +\frac{\mathbf 1_{\{b=a\}}}{n}. \]

Indeed, the conditional mean differs from \(tq_b\) by at most \(\mathbf 1_{\{b=a\}}/n\), the conditional variance of \(X_b/n\) is at most \(q_b/(4n)\), and Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the expected absolute centered deviation by its standard deviation.

Summing over types and using \(\sum_b\sqrt{q_b}\le\sqrt{\tau\sum_bq_b}\le\sqrt\tau\), we obtain, uniformly in \(t,s\in[0,1]\),

\[ \mathbb E\!\left[ \left\|\frac Xn+\frac{s}{n}e_a-tq\right\|_1 \,\middle|\,T=t \right] \le \frac{\sqrt\tau}{2\sqrt n}+\frac2n. \]

Substitution into the exact marginal-contribution formula proves

\[ \left| \phi_a^{(n)}-\int_0^1\partial_aF(tq)\,dt \right| \le H_a\left(\frac{\sqrt\tau}{2\sqrt n}+\frac2n\right). \]

The right-hand side tends to zero along every admissible sequence. Therefore

\[ u_a^k=\int_0^1\frac{\partial F_k}{\partial r_a}(tq^k)\,dt. \]

This proof uses no monotonicity, sign, or superadditivity assumption.

2. Explicit rational formula

Write the listed monomials of block \(k\) as

\[ F_k(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{m_k}c_{kj}r^{\alpha_{kj}}, \qquad r^\alpha=\prod_{b\in\mathcal A}r_b^{\alpha_b}, \qquad h_{kj}=\sum_b\alpha_{kj,b}. \]

Constant monomials contribute nothing to a derivative and may be discarded. For a monomial with \(\alpha_{kj,a}>0\), its degree satisfies \(h_{kj}\ge1\), and

\[ \partial_a(c_{kj}r^{\alpha_{kj}})(tq^k) = c_{kj}\alpha_{kj,a}\, t^{h_{kj}-1} \prod_b(q_b^k)^{\alpha_{kj,b}-\mathbf 1_{\{b=a\}}}. \]

Integrating the power of \(t\) gives the exact formula

\[ u_a^k = \sum_{j:\,\alpha_{kj,a}>0} \frac{c_{kj}\alpha_{kj,a}}{h_{kj}} \prod_b(q_b^k)^{\alpha_{kj,b}-\mathbf 1_{\{b=a\}}}. \]

All exponents in this formula are nonnegative. A zero exponent contributes the factor \(1\), including at a zero coordinate. Every summand is rational, hence every required output is rational.

The normalization also checks correctly: by the chain rule and \(F_k(0)=0\),

\[ \sum_{a:q_a^k>0}q_a^k u_a^k = \int_0^1\frac{d}{dt}F_k(tq^k)\,dt = F_k(q^k). \]

3. Algorithm and binary running time

Here \(L\) is the total input length, including the rational coordinates of the supplied coalition structure.

Initialize an accumulator for each required pair \((a,k)\). For each nonconstant monomial, compute

\[ P_{kj}=\prod_b(q_b^k)^{\alpha_{kj,b}}. \]

For every \(a\) with \(q_a^k>0\) and \(\alpha_{kj,a}>0\), add

\[ \frac{c_{kj}\alpha_{kj,a}P_{kj}}{h_{kj}q_a^k} \]

to its accumulator. Division is permitted because only positive \(q_a^k\) are used. This expression equals the preceding formula, even when another coordinate makes \(P_{kj}=0\).

Repeated multiplication computes each product using at most \(d\) multiplications. Scanning coordinates and updating accumulators therefore uses

\[ O\!\left(K\tau+m(d+\tau+1)\right) \]

rational arithmetic operations. Crucially, this algorithm visits only the listed monomials and listed types.

It remains to bound the sizes of those rational operations.

Each input numerator and denominator has at most \(L\) bits. A product of at most \(d\) input coordinates has numerator and denominator lengths \(O(dL+1)\). Including its coefficient, the exponent and degree factors, and division by one positive coordinate, each individual contribution has numerator and denominator lengths

\[ O\!\left((d+2)(L+1)\right). \]

A sum of at most \(m\) such contributions can be expressed over the product of their denominators. That denominator has \(O(m(d+2)(L+1))\) bits.

For the numerator, observe that every coordinate belongs to \([0,1]\) and \(\alpha_{kj,a}/h_{kj}\le1\). Thus each contribution has absolute value at most \(|c_{kj}|\), and any running sum has absolute value at most \(m2^L\). Combining this magnitude bound with the denominator bound shows that every reduced accumulator, and every temporary operand up to a constant factor, has \(O(B)\) bits, where

\[ B=1+(m+1)(d+2)(L+1). \]

Schoolbook integer multiplication and division take \(O(B^2)\) bit operations. The Euclidean algorithm uses \(O(B)\) divisions, so reducing a rational fraction takes at most \(O(B^3)\) bit operations. Applying these conservative bounds gives the explicit total running-time bound

\[ O\!\left( L+ \left[K\tau+m(d+\tau+1)\right] \left[1+(m+1)(d+2)(L+1)\right]^3 \right). \]

There are at most \(K\tau\) output fractions, each with \(O(B)\) bits. Thus output length is polynomial as well. Unary exponent encoding ensures that the dependence on \(d\) is polynomial in the stated input representation.

The exact computation is therefore polynomial-time solvable in the binary model; an arithmetic-operation count alone is not needed for this conclusion.
