The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite candidate set \(X\), a listed set of ranking types \(S\subseteq L(X)\), and a formula \(\varphi\) with outcome atoms, rational mass-threshold atoms, Boolean connectives, and modalities \(\Diamond_\alpha\), determine whether \(\varphi\) holds for every distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(S)\) and every resolute rule \(F:\Delta(S)\to X\). At \((F,\mu)\), \(\mathsf{Mass}_{t\ge q}\) is true exactly when \(\mu_t\ge q\), and \(\Diamond_\alpha\psi\) holds when some transfer \(\mu'=\mu-\nu+\nu'\), with \(0\le\nu\le\mu\) and \(\sum_t\nu_t=\sum_t\nu'_t=\alpha\), makes \(\psi\) true at \((F,\mu')\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Input: a nonempty finite candidate set \(X\), with \(m=|X|\), a nonempty explicitly listed set \(S\) of strict ranking types on \(X\), with \(k=|S|\), and a finite formula \(\varphi\).
A society is represented by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the actual support size, not the number of possible rankings. Here the validity input specifies \(S\), rather than an individual society.
Semantic worlds are all real distributions \(\Delta(S)=\{\mu\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{S}:\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t=1\}\), including boundary distributions; explicitly represented rational societies follow the support convention above.
A rule is any total resolute function \(F:\Delta(S)\to X\), with no continuity, measurability, or computability restriction. At \((F,\mu)\), outcome atom \(x\) holds exactly when \(F(\mu)=x\).
Formulas use outcome atoms, atoms \(\mathsf{Mass}_{t\ge q}\) for \(t\in S\) and binary rational \(q\in[0,1]\), Boolean connectives, and arbitrarily nested modalities \(\Diamond_\alpha\) with binary rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). The mass atom holds exactly when \(\mu_t\ge q\).
The formula \(\Diamond_\alpha\psi\) holds at \((F,\mu)\) exactly when there exist \(\nu,\nu'\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{S}\) with \(\nu\le\mu\) coordinatewise and \(\sum_t\nu_t=\sum_t\nu'_t=\alpha\), such that \(\psi\) holds at \((F,\mu-\nu+\nu')\). Removed mass may be reassigned to its original type; the same \(F\) is used throughout.
Validity means truth for every \(\mu\in\Delta(S)\) and every such \(F\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,k,L,|\varphi|\), and \(\tau\) whenever an explicitly represented society is involved.

Is this continuous validity problem decidable, or is it undecidable?

Discrete validity with fixed finite agents and candidates, preference and outcome atoms, and modalities allowing a specified coalition to change its ballots is decidable by exhaustive enumeration. [Cinà–Endriss](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/42894993/p1009_cina.pdf)
The richer discrete logic including truthful-preference operators is also decidable. [Troquard–van der Hoek–Wooldridge](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.3341) No result settling exactly the continuous validity problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required nonempty candidate and ranking-type sets so that distributions and total resolute rules exist; no three-candidate restriction applies to the decidability anchor.
  • Used strict rankings without indifference, equivalent to the discrete definition's antisymmetric linear orders.
  • Retained the real probability simplex, including zero coordinates, for semantic quantification; the binary-rational support convention governs explicitly represented societies, which are not validity inputs.
  • Distinguished the listed domain size k from the actual positive-mass support size tau.
  • Normalized total mass to one and restricted thresholds and modality parameters to binary rationals in the closed unit interval.
  • Required nonnegative incoming transfers supported on S and allowed removed mass to return to its original type, preserving the discrete permission for coalition members to leave their ballots unchanged.
  • Specified arbitrary total resolute rules without regularity restrictions and held the rule fixed across modal transitions.
  • Made candidates, the listed ranking domain, and the formula variable inputs, with explicit encoding-size parameters.

1. Accessibility and the one-dimensional representation

Write $D=\Delta(S)$. For $\mu,\eta\in D$, define
$$ d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu,\eta) =\frac12\sum_{t\in S}|\mu_t-\eta_t| =\sum_{t\in S}(\mu_t-\eta_t)_+. $$
The second equality follows from $\sum_t(\mu_t-\eta_t)=0$.

Lemma 1. The distribution $\eta$ is accessible from $\mu$ through $\Diamond_\alpha$ if and only if
$$ d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu,\eta)\le\alpha. $$

Proof. If $\eta=\mu-\nu+\nu'$, then
$$ (\mu_t-\eta_t)_+=(\nu_t-\nu'_t)_+\le\nu_t. $$
Summation gives the required inequality.

Conversely, put $d=d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu,\eta)\le\alpha$ and define
$$ r_t=(\mu_t-\eta_t)_+,\qquad s_t=(\eta_t-\mu_t)_+,\qquad h_t=\min\{\mu_t,\eta_t\}. $$
Then
$$ \sum_t r_t=\sum_t s_t=d,\qquad \sum_t h_t=1-d,\qquad \mu-r+s=\eta. $$
If $d<1$, let
$$ a_t=\frac{\alpha-d}{1-d}h_t,\qquad \nu=r+a,\qquad \nu'=s+a. $$
Because $0\le\alpha-d\le1-d$, we have $0\le a\le h$. Hence
$$ 0\le\nu\le r+h=\mu,\qquad \sum_t\nu_t=\sum_t\nu'_t=\alpha, $$
and $\mu-\nu+\nu'=\eta$.

If $d=1$, then $\alpha=1$, and $\nu=r,\nu'=s$ suffice. $\square$

Consequently, $\Diamond_0$ is the identity modality. We may eliminate every occurrence of it before proceeding.

If $k=1$, there is only one world. Every mass atom is true and every modality is the identity. Checking the resulting Boolean formula for each of the $m$ possible outcomes takes
$$ O(L+m|\varphi|) $$
time.

Now suppose $S=\{t_1,t_2\}$. Identify a distribution with its first coordinate:
$$ p\longleftrightarrow(p,1-p),\qquad 0\le p\le1. $$
Then
$$ d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl((p,1-p),(q,1-q)\bigr)=|p-q|. $$
Thus the accessible worlds from $p$ are precisely
$$ B_\alpha(p)=[0,1]\cap[p-\alpha,p+\alpha]. $$
Mass atoms become rational linear inequalities:
$$ \mathsf{Mass}_{t_1\ge q}\iff p\ge q,\qquad \mathsf{Mass}_{t_2\ge q}\iff p\le1-q. $$

2. Positive-radius modalities produce finitely many intervals

For an arbitrary set $A\subseteq[0,1]$, define
$$ N_\alpha(A)=\{p\in[0,1]:B_\alpha(p)\cap A\ne\varnothing\}. $$

Lemma 2. If $0<\alpha\le1$, then $N_\alpha(A)$ is a union of at most
$$ \left\lfloor\frac1\alpha\right\rfloor $$
intervals. Each interval may independently include or exclude either endpoint.

Proof. By symmetry of the distance,
$$ N_\alpha(A) =\bigcup_{a\in A} [\max\{0,a-\alpha\},\min\{1,a+\alpha\}]. $$
Every interval in this union has length at least $\alpha$.

Every connected component of the union contains one of these intervals: choose any point in the component and a generating interval containing that point. The generating interval is connected, so it lies in the same component.

Connected subsets of the real line are intervals. Distinct components therefore contain disjoint generating intervals of length at least $\alpha$. If there were $r$ distinct components, choosing one generating interval from each would give
$$ r\alpha\le1. $$
This also rules out infinitely many components. Each component is a bounded interval, with whichever endpoint inclusion properties its union has. $\square$

This applies without any regularity assumption on $A$. In particular, under an arbitrary rule, the truth set of every positive-radius diamond is a finite union of intervals.

A second elementary observation will control where outcomes can safely be changed.

Lemma 3. Suppose $T\subseteq[0,1]$ is a finite union of intervals. Define
$$ H_\alpha(T)=\{y\in[0,1]:B_\alpha(y)\subseteq T\}. $$
Every boundary point of $H_\alpha(T)$ belongs to the finite list consisting of $0,1$ and the numbers
$$ e-\alpha,\qquad e+\alpha, $$
where $e$ ranges over the endpoints of $T$ and over $0,1$.

Proof. We have
$$ H_\alpha(T) =[0,1]\setminus N_\alpha([0,1]\setminus T). $$
The complement of $T$ is a finite union of intervals and singleton points whose endpoints come from the endpoints of $T$ and $0,1$.

For a nonempty interval with endpoints $a,b$, its $\alpha$-neighborhood is an interval whose possible boundary points are
$$ \max\{0,a-\alpha\},\qquad \min\{1,b+\alpha\}. $$
This follows directly by taking the union of the balls centered in that interval. Endpoint inclusion depends on attainment and clipping, but creates no additional boundary location. A singleton $e$ gives the closed interval $B_\alpha(e)$.

Taking finite unions and then complements creates no boundary points outside the displayed finite list. $\square$

3. A finite partition supports a canonical realizing rule

Let $\theta$ be any formula after eliminating zero-radius modalities. Enumerate its modal occurrences as
$$ D_j=\Diamond_{\alpha_j}\psi_j,\qquad 1\le j\le s, $$
where every $\alpha_j>0$.

Suppose an arbitrary rule $F$ satisfies $\theta$ at $u\in[0,1]$. Let
$$ T_j=\{p\in[0,1]:(F,p)\models D_j\}. $$
By Lemma 2, each $T_j$ is a finite union of intervals.

Construct a finite set $E\subseteq[0,1]$ containing:

  • $0$ and $1$;
  • every threshold location of a mass atom in $\theta$;
  • every endpoint of every $T_j$;
  • every number $e-\alpha_j$ or $e+\alpha_j$ lying in $[0,1]$, where $e$ is an endpoint of $T_j$ or is $0$ or $1$.

Sort its distinct elements:
$$ 0=e_0<e_1<\cdots<e_h=1. $$
The resulting cells are the singleton points $\{e_i\}$ and the open intervals
$$ I_i=(e_i,e_{i+1}). $$

For a candidate $c$, define $\Psi_j^c(y)$ as follows. In $\psi_j$, replace outcome atoms according to outcome $c$, and replace each maximal modal subformula $D_\ell$ by the condition $y\in T_\ell$. This is a quantifier-free numerical formula.

Under the original rule,
$$ (F,y)\models\psi_j \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Psi_j^{F(y)}(y). $$
For fixed $j,c$, the truth value of $\Psi_j^c$ is constant on each open cell: all mass-atom and modal-set boundaries belong to $E$. By Lemma 3, membership in $H_{\alpha_j}(T_j)$ is also constant on each open cell.

For each open cell, let
$$ C_i=\{F(y):y\in I_i\}. $$
This is a nonempty subset of $X$.

Lemma 4. There is a rule $G$ such that:

  • $G(e_i)=F(e_i)$ at every singleton cell;
  • $G$ uses only outcomes in $C_i$ on $I_i$;
  • every outcome in $C_i$ occurs densely in $I_i$;
  • $G(u)=F(u)$;
  • every modal occurrence $D_j$ has truth set $T_j$ under $G$.

Consequently, $(G,u)\models\theta$.

Proof. First construct a coloring satisfying conditions 1–4. An open interval can be partitioned into finitely many sets, each dense in it: enumerate its rational subintervals and, for every required color and every such subinterval, select a fresh rational point. Assign the selected points their colors and assign the remaining points any permitted color. If necessary, change the outcome at $u$ to $F(u)$. Changing one point preserves density.

We prove condition 5 by induction on modal depth. Assume the proper modal descendants of $D_j$ already have their original truth sets. Its operand under $G$ is then evaluated by $\Psi_j^{G(y)}(y)$.

First, suppose $p\in T_j$. There is an original witness $y\in B_{\alpha_j}(p)$ with
$$ (F,y)\models\psi_j. $$
If $y$ is a singleton cell, its outcome is unchanged, so it remains a witness.

Otherwise, $y\in I_i$ and $c=F(y)\in C_i$. The formula $\Psi_j^c$ is true throughout $I_i$. Moreover,
$$ B_{\alpha_j}(p)\cap I_i $$
contains a nonempty open subinterval. Indeed, if the witness lies exactly at a ball endpoint, moving it slightly toward $p$ stays inside both the ball and $I_i$, because $\alpha_j>0$. Density supplies a point in this intersection colored $c$ by $G$. That point is a new witness.

Conversely, suppose $G$ supplies a witness $y$ for $D_j$ at $p$. A singleton witness is also an original witness.

If $y\in I_i$ and $G(y)=c$, then $c\in C_i$. Choose $z\in I_i$ with $F(z)=c$. Constancy of $\Psi_j^c$ gives
$$ (F,z)\models\psi_j. $$
By symmetry of accessibility, every center within distance $\alpha_j$ of $z$ has this witness. Hence
$$ B_{\alpha_j}(z)\subseteq T_j, $$
so $z\in H_{\alpha_j}(T_j)$. Membership in this set is constant on $I_i$, giving $y\in H_{\alpha_j}(T_j)$. Because $p\in B_{\alpha_j}(y)$, we obtain $p\in T_j$.

Thus the truth set of $D_j$ is exactly $T_j$. The induction proves condition 5. The preserved root outcome, mass atoms, and modal truth values establish $\theta$ at $u$. $\square$

The constructed rule can be chosen Borel: on each open cell, all but one of the outcome classes can be countable dense sets, with the remaining class their complement. No Borel restriction is assumed in the semantics; this is a consequence specific to this construction.

4. Eliminating the arbitrary rule by linear arithmetic

We now give an effective satisfiability procedure. Applying it to $\theta=\neg\varphi$ decides validity.

For each modal occurrence, define
$$ B_j=\left\lfloor\frac1{\alpha_j}\right\rfloor. $$
Make the following finite guesses:

  • A number of intervals between $0$ and $B_j$ presenting $T_j$.
  • The inclusion or exclusion of each interval endpoint.
  • The weak ordering of all endpoint expressions used to construct $E$, including their positions relative to $0$ and $1$.
  • An outcome $d_i\in X$ for every singleton cell.
  • A nonempty set $C_i\subseteq X$ for every open cell.
  • A root outcome $c_0\in X$.

The interval endpoints themselves are real variables. Every fixed guess describes membership in $T_j$ by a Boolean combination of rational linear inequalities.

Form $\Psi_j^c$ using these guessed modal sets, exactly as above. Define $\Theta^c$ similarly from the entire formula $\theta$.

For each $j$, impose the following equation for every $x\in[0,1]$:
$$ T_j(x)\ \longleftrightarrow\ \left( \bigvee_i \left( |x-e_i|\le\alpha_j \land \Psi_j^{d_i}(e_i) \right) \right) \lor \left( \bigvee_i\ \bigvee_{c\in C_i} \exists y\, \left( e_i<y<e_{i+1} \land |x-y|\le\alpha_j \land \Psi_j^c(y) \right) \right). $$
Absolute-value inequalities here abbreviate two linear inequalities.

Finally, require a root $u\in[0,1]$ such that $\Theta^{c_0}(u)$ holds and its prescribed outcome is available:
$$ \left( \bigvee_{\{i:d_i=c_0\}}u=e_i \right) \lor \left( \bigvee_{\{i:c_0\in C_i\}}e_i<u<e_{i+1} \right). $$
Existentially quantify all interval endpoints and $u$, and include the linear constraints describing the guessed intervals and endpoint ordering.

The result is a first-order sentence of rational linear real arithmetic.

Necessity

If $\theta$ is satisfiable, take its actual sets $T_j$ and apply Lemma 4. Their interval counts meet the bounds $B_j$. Guess the resulting partition, singleton outcomes, available color sets, and root outcome.

Under the canonical rule, an open cell contributes a witness exactly when the corresponding existential numerical condition holds. Constancy of $\Psi_j^c$ and density of color $c$ justify this assertion, including witnesses at distance exactly $\alpha_j$. Therefore every imposed modal equation and the root requirement hold.

Sufficiency

Suppose one of the numerical sentences holds. On every open cell, assign every color in $C_i$ densely, and assign $d_i$ at its singleton endpoints. Give the root outcome $c_0$; this preserves density.

For fixed $j,c$, $\Psi_j^c$ is constant on every open cell. Whenever its numerical existential condition holds, the ball intersects that cell in a nonempty open subinterval, so density supplies an actual witness of color $c$. Conversely, any actual witness in that cell satisfies the numerical existential condition. The singleton terms are exact as well.

Induction on modal depth now proves that every $D_j$ has precisely its guessed truth set $T_j$. The root requirement then proves $\theta$.

Thus the finite procedure is sound and complete.

5. Effective running-time bound

Here is a deliberately loose bound with a self-contained elimination argument.

Put $n=|\varphi|$. Positive binary rational radii satisfy
$$ \frac1{\alpha_j}\le 2^{O(L)}. $$
Hence the total number of guessed interval endpoints and partition cells is at most
$$ O(n2^{O(L)}). $$
After expanding the finite disjunctions, the numerical sentence has binary length at most
$$ N=2^{p(m+k+L+n)} $$
for a fixed polynomial $p$, with $k=2$. The number of finite guesses is at most $2^{N^{O(1)}}$: endpoint orderings, endpoint flags, singleton outcomes, and cell color subsets all fit this bound.

For completeness, rational linear real arithmetic admits the following direct elimination procedure. Put a quantifier-free formula into disjunctive normal form. To eliminate an existentially quantified variable from a conjunction, express each inequality involving it as a lower or upper bound. Every lower bound must be compatible with every upper bound. The resulting comparison is strict precisely when at least one of those two bounds is strict. Constraints not involving the variable are retained. Equalities can be represented by two weak inequalities. Universal quantifiers are eliminated using
$$ \forall v\,\chi\iff\neg\exists v\,\neg\chi. $$
All resulting coefficients remain rational. This proves effective elimination without quantification over outcome sets.

Define
$$ T_0(z)=z,\qquad T_{h+1}(z)=2^{T_h(z)}. $$
If the current formula has binary length $H$, one elimination step, including Boolean expansion and rational arithmetic, can conservatively be bounded by $2^{H^{O(1)}}$. There are at most $O(N)$ quantified variables after renaming and prenex conversion. Consequently, for a fixed constant $C$, the entire decision procedure has a bound of the form
$$ T_{\lceil CN\rceil}(N^C), \qquad N=2^{p(m+k+L+n)}. $$
This is an explicit computable upper bound, not a tight classification.

Validity itself has no individual-society input. If instead one asks for truth at a supplied rational society universally over rules, the same procedure fixes $u$ to its first mass coordinate. If that society has encoding length $\ell$ and actual support size $\tau\le2$, use $L+\ell$ in the bound; reading and converting its support representation takes polynomial time in $m,k,\tau,\ell$.

More generally, Lemma 1 lets one check accessibility between two explicitly represented rational societies in polynomial time in $m,k$, their actual support sizes, and their binary encoding lengths: only coordinates in the union of their supports contribute to the distance.

6. Semialgebraic outcome sets do not suffice

The preceding procedure uses dense colorings. The following example shows that replacing arbitrary rules by semialgebraic rules would lose satisfiable formulas.

Take three candidates $a,b,c$ and two ranking types. Write $p=\mu_{t_1}$ and set
$$ \varepsilon=\frac14. $$
The numerical conditions below are expressible by the allowed atoms:
$$ p>\varepsilon \iff \neg\mathsf{Mass}_{t_2\ge3/4}, $$
and
$$ 0<p<\varepsilon \iff \neg\mathsf{Mass}_{t_2\ge1} \land \neg\mathsf{Mass}_{t_1\ge1/4}. $$
Let $\Box_1\chi$ abbreviate $\neg\Diamond_1\neg\chi$. Consider
$$ \Gamma= \Box_1 \left( \bigl((a\lor b)\rightarrow p>\varepsilon\bigr) \land \bigl( (0<p<\varepsilon) \rightarrow (\Diamond_\varepsilon a\land\Diamond_\varepsilon b) \bigr) \right). $$

Proposition. The formula $\Gamma$ is satisfiable, but no rule satisfying it has both outcome sets for $a$ and $b$ semialgebraic.

Proof. Partition $(\varepsilon,1]$ into two dense sets, assign them outcomes $a,b$, and assign $c$ on $[0,\varepsilon]$. For every $p\in(0,\varepsilon)$,
$$ B_\varepsilon(p)\cap(\varepsilon,1] =(\varepsilon,\varepsilon+p]. $$
This contains a nonempty open interval, so it contains both outcomes. The first conjunct also holds. Since $\Diamond_1$ is global, this proves $\Gamma$.

Conversely, suppose a rule satisfies $\Gamma$. Its $a$- and $b$-outcome sets are disjoint and lie strictly above $\varepsilon$. For every $p\in(0,\varepsilon)$, each set has a point in
$$ (\varepsilon,\varepsilon+p]. $$
Thus both sets accumulate at $\varepsilon$ from the right.

A semialgebraic subset of the real line is a finite union of intervals and points. Indeed, in a finite polynomial description, the finitely many roots of its nonzero polynomials partition the line into intervals on which every polynomial sign, and hence membership, is constant.

A semialgebraic set lying above $\varepsilon$ and accumulating there must therefore contain $(\varepsilon,\varepsilon+\delta)$ for some $\delta>0$. If both outcome sets were semialgebraic, they would overlap on such an interval, contradicting resoluteness. $\square$

This obstruction already occurs in the decidable case $k=2$.

7. What remains unresolved

The proof settles the full language for $k\le2$. Its essential additional fact is one-dimensional: every positive-radius diamond has a uniformly bounded finite interval presentation. That permits a finite partition on which dense recoloring preserves all modal truth sets.

No corresponding representation has been established here for higher-dimensional simplices. Accordingly, this argument proves neither decidability nor undecidability of the full input problem.
