The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an atomless population \(\Omega\) partitioned into \(\tau\) homogeneous types \(A_a\) with masses \(\mu_a\), a horizon \(T\), and each round an active measurable subpopulation \(S^t\) of mass \(M_t\), the learner observes \(S^t\), chooses a measurable partition \(P^t_1,\ldots,P^t_k\) of \(S^t\) with each coalition having mass \(M_t/k\), and then receives a type-level disutility matrix \(D^t=(d^t_{ab})\). With \(x^t_{a\ell}=\lambda(S^t\cap P^t_\ell\cap A_a)\), \(y^t_{a\ell}(Q)=\lambda(S^t\cap Q_\ell\cap A_a)\) for one fixed whole-population \(k\)-partition \(Q\), and \(L_t(z,D^t)=\sum_{\ell,a,b}d^t_{ab}z_{a\ell}z_{b\ell}\), determine whether a learner running in time polynomial in the encoded input length can guarantee \(\sum_t L_t(x^t,D^t)\le c\min_Q\sum_t L_t(y^t(Q),D^t)+\operatorname{poly}(\tau,k,L)T^\delta\) for fixed \(c>0\) and some \(\delta<1\).

Complexity of Static Regret in the Atomless Type-Mass Model

Result

Under the standard rational-input model and with \(M=1\), the problem is already NP-hard for \(c=1\), even under all of the following restrictions:

  • \(k=2\);
  • all rounds have identical arrivals and disutilities;
  • every type has mass \(1\);
  • all disutilities belong to \(\{0,-1/N,-1\}\), where \(N=|\Theta|\), so \(W=1\).

More precisely, a deterministic polynomial-time \(1\)-static-regret learner with the stated polynomial sublinear additive term would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Moreover, because the model permits signed costs, no guarantee with any constant \(c\neq 1\) can hold at all, even with unlimited computation.

1. A hard one-round optimization problem

Start from an instance \(G=(V,E)\) of MAX-CUT, with \(n=|V|\).

Form the graph \(H\) by adding \(n\) isolated vertices to \(G\). Thus \(H\) has \(N=2n\) vertices. Let \(J\) be the simple complement of \(H\).

For a graph \(Q\) on \(N=2n\) vertices, write \(\operatorname{MaxBis}(Q)\) and \(\operatorname{MinBis}(Q)\) for its maximum and minimum cut sizes among sets of exactly \(n\) vertices.

Claim 1

\(\operatorname{MaxBis}(H)=\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\).

Proof

Let \(S\subseteq V(G)\) be any cut side in \(G\). Add exactly \(n-|S|\) of the isolated vertices to \(S\). The resulting set has size \(n\), hence defines a bisection of \(H\), and its cut size is exactly the cut size of \(S\) in \(G\).

Conversely, restricting any bisection of \(H\) to \(V(G)\) gives a cut of \(G\) with the same size, because the added vertices are isolated. Therefore the two optima are equal. \(\square\)

For every bisection \(S\) of \(H\), there are exactly \(n^2\) unordered vertex pairs crossing the bisection. Each such pair is an edge of exactly one of \(H\) and \(J\). Consequently,

\(\operatorname{MinBis}(J)=n^2-\operatorname{MaxBis}(H)=n^2-\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\).

2. Construction of the type-mass instance

Create one type for each vertex of \(J\). Every type has mass and arrival mass equal to \(1\), and \(k=2\). Thus every label must receive total mass \(n=N/2\).

Let \(B\) be the adjacency matrix of \(J\), and define the symmetric disutility matrix

\(D=-I_N-\frac{1}{N}B\).

Hence \(d(i,i)=-1\), \(d(i,j)=-1/N\) when \(ij\in E(J)\), and \(d(i,j)=0\) otherwise. In particular, all disutilities are nonpositive and \(W=1\).

A feasible allocation is determined by \(y_i=x_{i1}\), because \(x_{i2}=1-y_i\). Its feasible region is

\(P_N=\{y\in[0,1]^N:\sum_i y_i=n\}\).

Every such point is realizable by measurably splitting each atomless type block. It also defines a feasible static partition by taking \(p_{i1}=y_i\) and \(p_{i2}=1-y_i\).

With \(M=1\), the one-round cost is

\(F(y)=y^{\mathsf T}Dy+(\mathbf 1-y)^{\mathsf T}D(\mathbf 1-y)\).

Claim 2

The minimum of \(F\) over \(P_N\) is attained at a vector in \(\{0,1\}^N\).

Proof

Every eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix \(B\) has absolute value at most \(N-1\), by the Gershgorin bound. Therefore every eigenvalue of \(I_N+B/N\) is at least \(1/N>0\). Thus \(D=-(I_N+B/N)\) is negative definite.

It follows that \(F\) is concave on \(P_N\). A concave function on a compact polytope has a minimum at an extreme point: if \(y\) is expressed as a convex combination of extreme points, concavity implies that at least one of those extreme points has value no greater than \(F(y)\).

It remains to identify the extreme points of \(P_N\). If a feasible point had at least two fractional coordinates, one could increase one and decrease the other by a sufficiently small common amount, expressing the point as the midpoint of two distinct feasible points. A feasible point cannot have exactly one fractional coordinate because \(\sum_i y_i=n\) is an integer. Hence every extreme point lies in \(\{0,1\}^N\). \(\square\)

Claim 3

The optimal one-round value determines \(\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\) exactly.

Proof

Let \(y=\mathbf 1_S\) be an extreme point, so \(|S|=n\). Then

\(\|\mathbf 1_S\|_2^2+\|\mathbf 1_{\bar S}\|_2^2=N\),

and

\(\mathbf 1_S^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf 1_S+\mathbf 1_{\bar S}^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf 1_{\bar S}=2\bigl(|E(J)|-|\delta_J(S)|\bigr)\).

Therefore

\(F(\mathbf 1_S)=-N-\frac{2}{N}\bigl(|E(J)|-|\delta_J(S)|\bigr)\).

Minimizing this expression is equivalent to minimizing \(|\delta_J(S)|\). By Claim 2,

\(\operatorname{OPT}=-N-\frac{2}{N}\bigl(|E(J)|-\operatorname{MinBis}(J)\bigr)\).

Using the identity from Section 1 gives

\(\operatorname{OPT}=\kappa-\frac{2}{N}\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\),

where the explicitly computable constant is

\(\kappa=-N-\frac{2}{N}\bigl(|E(J)|-n^2\bigr)\).

Thus consecutive possible optimal values differ by exactly \(2/N\), and the optimal value determines \(\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\). \(\square\)

3. From a \(1\)-static-regret learner to MAX-CUT

Suppose a deterministic polynomial-time learner satisfies

\(\sum_{r=1}^T C^r(x^r)\leq\min_p\sum_{r=1}^T C^r(p\rho^r)+A(\tau,W)T^\delta\),

where \(\delta<1\) and \(A\) is a fixed polynomial.

Repeat the instance constructed above for \(T\) rounds. Because the arrival vector and disutility matrix are identical in every round, the feasible static comparators are exactly the one-round feasible allocations \(P_N\). Hence the benchmark equals \(T\operatorname{OPT}\).

Every online action is also in \(P_N\), so \(C^r(x^r)\geq\operatorname{OPT}\). Writing

\(\overline C=\frac1T\sum_{r=1}^T C^r(x^r)\),

we obtain

\(\operatorname{OPT}\leq\overline C\leq\operatorname{OPT}+A(N,1)T^{\delta-1}\).

Because \(A(N,1)\) is polynomial in \(N\) and \(1-\delta>0\), one can choose a polynomially bounded horizon \(T\) such that

\(A(N,1)T^{\delta-1}<1/N\).

Indeed, if \(A(N,1)\leq C N^a\), it suffices to choose \(T=K N^b\), where \(b(1-\delta)>a+1\) and the fixed constant \(K\) is sufficiently large.

Let \(m=\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\). Since \(\operatorname{OPT}=\kappa-2m/N\),

\(m-\frac12<\frac{N}{2}(\kappa-\overline C)\leq m\).

Thus rounding \(\frac{N}{2}(\kappa-\overline C)\) to its nearest integer recovers \(m\) exactly. The simulation uses only polynomially many rounds and rational arithmetic of polynomial encoding length.

Therefore a polynomial-time \(1\)-static-regret learner would solve MAX-CUT in polynomial time. Consequently, such a learner does not exist unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

4. Constants \(c\neq 1\) are information-theoretically impossible

The obstruction here is the multiplicative comparison applied to signed costs.

Take two types of mass \(1\), two labels, and identical arrivals in every round. Balance forces each label to have mass \(1\).

If every entry of \(d\) equals \(-1\), every feasible allocation has cost

\(C=-\sum_{\ell=1}^2\left(\sum_\theta x_{\theta\ell}\right)^2=-2\).

For \(c>1\), the claimed guarantee would imply

\(-2T\leq -2cT+A(2,1)T^\delta\),

or equivalently

\(2(c-1)T\leq A(2,1)T^\delta\),

which is false for sufficiently large \(T\).

Similarly, if every entry of \(d\) equals \(1\), every feasible allocation has cost \(2\). For \(c<1\), the guarantee would require

\(2(1-c)T\leq A(2,1)T^\delta\),

again impossible for sufficiently large \(T\).

Thus \(c=1\) is the only multiplicative factor that can even be coherent on all rational signed-disutility sequences.

5. Randomized learners and scope

The NP-hardness conclusion above applies directly to deterministic learners and to randomized learners whose guarantee holds pathwise.

If the guarantee is required only in expectation, the same construction gives a one-sided randomized polynomial-time algorithm for MAX-CUT: choose the horizon so that the expected excess is below \(1/(3N)\), and apply Markov’s inequality to the nonnegative excess \(\overline C-\operatorname{OPT}\). Thus an expected-regret learner would imply \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\).

The reduction uses nonzero same-type disutilities \(d(\theta,\theta)\), which are permitted by the stated definition. A modified model imposing \(d(\theta,\theta)=0\), or restricting all costs to be nonnegative, is a different problem and is not settled by this reduction.

Conclusion

For the model exactly as stated:

  • attaining \(1\)-static regret in polynomial time is NP-hard, already for \(k=2\), \(W=1\), and a fixed repeated instance;
  • every factor \(c\neq1\) is impossible independently of computational complexity.

Verification report

Overall assessment

The deterministic \(c=1\) hardness reduction is mathematically sound. The concavity argument, reduction from MAX-CUT, regret amplification, and recovery of the exact optimum all check out. The impossibility argument for \(c\neq 1\) is also correct for signed disutilities.

The write-up has minor modeling and exposition gaps, principally concerning the representation of continuous allocations and the undefined parameter \(M\). These do not undermine the mathematical reduction under the prover’s stated rational-output interpretation.

Step-by-step audit

1. Reduction to balanced cuts

Claim 1 is correct. Adding \(n\) isolated vertices permits any cut side \(S\subseteq V(G)\) to be extended to a side of cardinality \(n\), without changing the cut value. Conversely, restricting a bisection of \(H\) to \(V(G)\) preserves the cut value.

For a bisection of \(2n\) vertices, exactly \(n^2\) unordered pairs cross. Since \(J\) is the simple complement of \(H\), every crossing pair belongs to exactly one of \(H\) and \(J\). Therefore

\[ \operatorname{MinBis}(J)=n^2-\operatorname{MaxBis}(H) \]

is correct.

The proof implicitly uses NP-hardness of unweighted MAX-CUT on simple graphs. This standard starting restriction should be stated explicitly, but it is not a substantive gap.

2. Feasible region and concavity

With two labels and unit mass per type, the representation

\[ y_i=x_{i1},\qquad x_{i2}=1-y_i \]

and feasible region

\[ P_N=\left\{y\in[0,1]^N:\sum_i y_i=n\right\} \]

are correct. Both label-balance constraints follow because \(N=2n\).

The spectral argument is correct. Every eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(B\) satisfies \(\lambda\geq -(N-1)\), hence every eigenvalue of \(I_N+B/N\) is at least \(1/N\). Thus \(D\) is negative definite.

Since

\[ F(y)=y^{\mathsf T}Dy+(\mathbf 1-y)^{\mathsf T}D(\mathbf 1-y), \]

its Hessian is \(4D\), so it is strictly concave. The conclusion that its minimum over a compact polytope is attained at an extreme point is valid.

The characterization of the extreme points of \(P_N\) is also correct. Two fractional coordinates permit a nontrivial feasible perturbation, while exactly one fractional coordinate is incompatible with the integral sum \(n\). Thus every extreme point is integral.

3. Evaluation of the objective

For \(y=\mathbf 1_S\), the diagonal contribution is correctly evaluated as \(-N\).

Because the symmetric adjacency matrix counts each internal edge twice,

\[ \mathbf 1_S^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf 1_S+ \mathbf 1_{\bar S}^{\mathsf T}B\mathbf 1_{\bar S} = 2\bigl(|E(J)|-|\delta_J(S)|\bigr). \]

Consequently,

\[ F(\mathbf 1_S) = -N-\frac{2}{N}\bigl(|E(J)|-|\delta_J(S)|\bigr). \]

The sign is handled correctly: minimizing \(F\) is equivalent to minimizing the bisection cut size in \(J\). Substituting the complement identity yields

\[ \operatorname{OPT} = \kappa-\frac{2}{N}\operatorname{MaxCut}(G), \]

with the stated computable \(\kappa\).

4. Regret-to-optimization conversion

For identical arrivals and costs in every round, the feasible static comparators are indeed exactly \(P_N\), so the benchmark is \(T\operatorname{OPT}\).

Every online action is itself in \(P_N\), giving the essential pathwise lower bound

\[ C^r(x^r)\geq\operatorname{OPT}. \]

Together with the assumed regret bound, this yields

\[ \operatorname{OPT}\leq\overline C \leq \operatorname{OPT}+A(N,1)T^{\delta-1}. \]

Because \(A(N,1)\) is polynomial and \(\delta<1\), a polynomial horizon can make the last term less than \(1/N\). The exponent and multiplicative constant may be hardcoded for the fixed learner and its fixed guarantee.

Writing \(m=\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\), the displayed interval

\[ m-\frac12< \frac N2(\kappa-\overline C) \leq m \]

is correct. Nearest-integer rounding therefore returns \(m\) uniquely.

5. Constants \(c\neq 1\)

When every disutility is \(-1\), balance makes every round cost exactly \(-2\). For \(c>1\), the claimed regret inequality requires a positive linear term to be bounded by a sublinear term, which is impossible.

When every disutility is \(1\), every round cost is exactly \(2\). The analogous contradiction for \(c<1\) is also correct. Thus no constant \(c\neq1\) works over all signed rational disutilities.

6. Randomized extension

For an expected-regret learner, the excess \(\overline C-\operatorname{OPT}\) is nonnegative on every outcome. Markov’s inequality therefore gives constant success probability once its expectation is below \(1/(3N)\).

The claimed one-sided character should be explained explicitly: the recovered rounded value can never exceed the true maximum because \(\overline C\geq\operatorname{OPT}\) pathwise. This is a routine omitted justification, not a flaw in the main deterministic result.

Issues requiring clarification

  • Minor — allocation representation. The assertion that the simulation uses rational arithmetic of polynomial encoding length assumes that every learner action is represented by polynomial-bit rational masses, or by another representation from which its quadratic cost can be computed to sufficient precision in polynomial time. “Standard rational-input model” specifies the inputs but does not explicitly specify continuous action outputs.
  • Minor — role of \(M\). The original statement contains the factor \(M^2\) without defining whether \(M\) is fixed, normalized, or part of the input. The prover sets \(M=1\) without explaining why that is an admissible restriction. The conclusion should be stated under an explicit normalization \(M=1\), or the permitted range and encoding of \(M\) should be specified.
  • Minor — randomized classification. The Markov argument does not explicitly state why erroneous outputs remain one-sided. The needed clarification is that the pathwise inequality \(\overline C\geq\operatorname{OPT}\) prevents the rounded estimate from exceeding \(\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\).
