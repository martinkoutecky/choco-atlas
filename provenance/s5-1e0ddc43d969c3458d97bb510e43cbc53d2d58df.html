The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given worker types \(A\) and position types \(B\) with rational masses \(\alpha_a,\beta_b\), sincere rankings over counterpart types and outside options, and a sincerely stable target flow \(x^\star\), find a report profile or certify that none exists. A flow \(x_{ab}\ge 0\) must satisfy \(\sum_b x_{ab}\le\alpha_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}\le\beta_b\); it records type-level assignment mass while each individual receives one partner or remains unmatched. The instance specifies a computable stable-flow mechanism \(R\), type utilities consistent with the rankings, and cohorts as the strategic actors, each submitting one ranking for its type. Ask whether there is a cohort Nash equilibrium in which no cohort can submit a closer ranking under Kendall distance without lowering its per-capita expected utility, and \(R\)’s expected flow is \(x^\star\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be disjoint nonempty sets of worker and position types. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\alpha_a,\beta_b\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible rankings. Masses need not sum to one or have equal totals across sides. Set \(m=\max\{|A|,|B|\}\).
Each type \(i\) has a sincere strict total ranking \(\pi_i\) of counterpart types and its outside option \(\bot_i\), and rational utilities strictly representing that ranking, normalized by \(u_i(\bot_i)=0\). Both sides are strategic; each cohort submits one arbitrary strict total ranking on the same alternatives, with complete information.
A feasible flow satisfies \(x_{ab}\geq0\), \(\sum_bx_{ab}\leq\alpha_a\), and \(\sum_ax_{ab}\leq\beta_b\). Residual mass is unmatched; each individual has one partner or is unmatched. A flow is stable under a ranking profile if every positive assignment is mutually acceptable and no pair \((a,b)\) has, on both sides, positive mass assigned to alternatives strictly worse than the other type, counting unmatched mass at the outside option.
The input includes a binary-rational sincerely stable target \(x^\star\) and a Boolean circuit of size \(S\) computing the exact rational expected flow \(F(p)\) of a mechanism \(R\) from each report profile \(p\). It is promised that \(F(p)\) is the expectation of a lottery supported on flows stable under \(p\); the circuit's output bits encode rational numerators and positive denominators.
Per-capita expected utility is \(U_a(p)=\alpha_a^{-1}\sum_bF_{ab}(p)u_a(b)\), and symmetrically \(U_b(p)=\beta_b^{-1}\sum_aF_{ab}(p)u_b(a)\).
A qualifying profile \(p\) satisfies \(F(p)=x^\star\) and, for every cohort \(i\) and alternative report \(q_i\), \(U_i(p)\geq U_i(p_{-i},q_i)\); additionally, \(K(q_i,\pi_i)<K(p_i,\pi_i)\) implies \(U_i(p_{-i},q_i)<U_i(p)\). Here \(K\) is Kendall distance on the full rankings, including the outside option.

Is deciding whether a qualifying report profile exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,S\), where \(L\) is the total bit length of the numerical input.

For finite societies with \(n_A\) workers and \(n_B\) positions, an equilibrium-refinement procedure terminates after \(O(n_An_B^2+n_A^2n_B)\) updates; this bounds updates rather than their computational cost. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.04381v1).
In the discrete Gale–Shapley game, every minimally dishonest equilibrium yields the sincere woman-optimal matching. [Source](https://ifaamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2025/pdfs/p197.pdf). No result settling the exact mass/cohort problem above was identified.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when every mass equals \(1\), the target is integral, and the mechanism is deterministic. Thus a polynomial-time algorithm for the stated problem would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof below gives a polynomial-time reduction from Boolean satisfiability. It uses the freedom to supply an arbitrary stable-mechanism circuit; it does not establish hardness for a fixed Gale–Shapley mechanism.

For the general promised input, exhaustive search takes time

\(O\!\left(\tau((m+1)!)^{\tau+1}\left[S+\tau m^2\log(m+1)+\tau m(mS+L)^2\right]\right)\).

The problem also has a \(\Sigma_2^p\) upper bound. Neither statement asserts membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

1. A matching lemma

All instances in the reduction have unit masses. The constructed mechanism always returns an integral matching, so the stability definition in the question becomes ordinary one-to-one stability, with unacceptable partners excluded.

We first prove a fact needed to control arbitrary reports.

Lemma. In a finite, strict, one-to-one matching market, if a perfect stable matching exists, every stable matching is perfect.

Proof. Let \(M\) be a perfect stable matching, and suppose a stable matching \(N\) leaves a worker \(a_0\) unmatched.

The component of \(M\cup N\) containing \(a_0\) is an alternating path ending at a position unmatched in \(N\). Write its vertices so that

\(M(a_j)=b_j\) for \(0\leq j\leq r\), and \(N(b_j)=a_{j+1}\) for \(0\leq j<r\).

Thus \(N(a_0)=\bot_{a_0}\), \(N(a_j)=b_{j-1}\) for \(j\geq1\), and \(b_r\) is unmatched in \(N\). This path cannot enter a cycle: its initial vertex has only an \(M\)-edge, and every vertex has at most one edge from each matching.

Because \(M\) is individually rational, \(a_0\) prefers \(b_0\) to being unmatched. If \(r=0\), the pair \((a_0,b_0)\) immediately blocks \(N\).

Otherwise, stability of \(N\) implies that \(b_0\) prefers \(a_1\) to \(a_0\). Stability of \(M\), applied to \((a_1,b_0)\), then implies that \(a_1\) prefers \(b_1\) to \(b_0\). Repeating these two implications along the path shows that \(a_r\) prefers \(b_r\) to its partner in \(N\). Position \(b_r\), unmatched in \(N\), finds \(a_r\) acceptable because they are matched in \(M\). Hence \((a_r,b_r)\) blocks \(N\), a contradiction. \(\square\)

We also use deferred acceptance with unacceptable partners omitted. Its stability follows directly: a proposer who prefers a potential blocking partner to its final outcome must have proposed there and been rejected; the receiver’s final partner is then preferred to that proposer. Every accepted assignment is mutually acceptable.

2. The reduction

Let \(\varphi\) be a Boolean formula with variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_k\), where \(k\geq1\).

Construct gadgets indexed by \(j=0,\ldots,k\). Gadget \(j\) contains workers \(a_{j1},a_{j2}\) and positions \(b_{j1},b_{j2}\). Gadget \(0\) will check the formula; the remaining gadgets encode variables. Every mass is \(1\).

For each type \(i\), fix an ordered list \(T_i\) of all counterpart types outside its own gadget. Its sincere ranking consists of the displayed prefix followed by that list:

| Type | Sincere ranking |
|---|---|
| \(a_{j1}\) | \(b_{j1}\succ b_{j2}\succ\bot_{a_{j1}}\succ T_{a_{j1}}\) |
| \(a_{j2}\) | \(b_{j2}\succ b_{j1}\succ\bot_{a_{j2}}\succ T_{a_{j2}}\) |
| \(b_{j1}\) | \(a_{j2}\succ a_{j1}\succ\bot_{b_{j1}}\succ T_{b_{j1}}\) |
| \(b_{j2}\) | \(a_{j1}\succ a_{j2}\succ\bot_{b_{j2}}\succ T_{b_{j2}}\) |

Give the first two alternatives utilities \(2\) and \(1\), respectively, the outside option utility \(0\), and the successive tail alternatives utilities \(-1,-2,\ldots\). These integer utilities strictly represent the rankings.

Define the two perfect matchings inside gadget \(j\):

\(X_j=\{(a_{j1},b_{j1}),(a_{j2},b_{j2})\}\),

\(Y_j=\{(a_{j1},b_{j2}),(a_{j2},b_{j1})\}\).

Workers sincerely prefer \(X_j\), while positions sincerely prefer \(Y_j\).

The target \(x^\star\) is the incidence flow of the perfect matching

\(Y=\bigcup_{j=0}^k Y_j\).

This target is sincerely stable: every position receives its sincere first choice, and every assigned pair is mutually acceptable. At the target, each worker has utility \(1\), and each position has utility \(2\).

For position \(b\), let \(v_b\) be its partner in \(Y\), and let \(w_b\) be the other worker in its gadget. Its sincere ranking begins \(v_b\succ w_b\succ\bot_b\). Define its cutoff report

\(c_b:\quad v_b\succ\bot_b\succ w_b\succ T_b\).

Thus \(K(c_b,\pi_b)=1\), and \(c_b\) accepts only \(v_b\).

For any report of \(b\), define the cutoff indicator \(C_b(p)=1\) exactly when \(b\) ranks \(\bot_b\) above \(w_b\).

The mechanism

The circuit implements the following deterministic algorithm.

  • If \(Y\) is not stable under the reports, or some position \(b\) has \(K(p_b,\pi_b)>1\), run worker-proposing deferred acceptance on the entire reported market.
  • Otherwise, solve each gadget separately, restricting reports to its two counterpart types and the outside option. Use worker-proposing deferred acceptance in every gadget except for this possible exception:

Use position-proposing deferred acceptance in gadget \(0\) precisely when:

  • neither position in gadget \(0\) has cutoff indicator \(1\);
  • every variable gadget has exactly one position with cutoff indicator \(1\); and
  • the resulting assignment fails \(\varphi\).

Decode the assignment by setting \(z_j=0\) when \(b_{j1}\) has cutoff indicator \(1\), and \(z_j=1\) when \(b_{j2}\) does.

Return the union of the gadget matchings in the second branch.

The circuit contains the constructed sincere rankings as constants. This is allowed: the input mechanism is arbitrary.

Verification of the stability promise

The first branch returns a stable matching by deferred acceptance.

Consider the second branch. Because \(Y\) is stable under the full reports, its restriction \(Y_j\) is a perfect stable matching in each restricted gadget. By the lemma, either orientation of deferred acceptance returns a perfect matching there.

Moreover, \(K(p_b,\pi_b)\leq1\) implies that \(b\) ranks both workers from its own gadget above every worker outside that gadget. Indeed, a permutation at Kendall distance \(1\) is obtained by one adjacent transposition, and the first two entries of \(\pi_b\) are its two local workers.

Consequently, every position in the returned matching receives a local worker whom it ranks above every nonlocal worker. No pair joining different gadgets can block. Within each gadget the matching is stable, and all assignments are mutually acceptable. The union is therefore stable under the full reports.

Thus the mechanism satisfies the required promise on every report profile. Its lottery is simply a point mass on its deterministic output.

3. Every qualifying profile has a prescribed form

Suppose \(p\) is qualifying and \(F(p)=x^\star\). Because the mechanism is deterministic, its output is exactly \(Y\), which must be stable under \(p\).

3.1. Every position reports within Kendall distance \(1\)

Fix a position \(b\), and replace its report by \(c_b\).

The matching \(Y\) remains stable: its assignment to \(b\) remains acceptable, \(b\) ranks that assignment first, and all pairs not involving \(b\) are unaffected.

Therefore a perfect stable matching exists at the modified profile. The mechanism’s output is stable and hence perfect by the lemma. Since \(b\) accepts only \(v_b\), it must receive \(v_b\), giving sincere utility \(2\).

If \(K(p_b,\pi_b)>1\), this deviation has strictly smaller Kendall distance and preserves \(b\)’s target utility. That violates the honesty refinement. Hence

\(K(p_b,\pi_b)\leq1\) for every position \(b\).

3.2. No position reverses its two local workers

For completeness, Kendall distance \(1\) means one adjacent transposition: if the unique reversed pair were nonadjacent in the sincere ranking, an alternative between them would create an additional reversed pair.

Suppose a position \(b\) exchanges the first two entries of its sincere ranking. It then ranks \(w_b\) first.

Worker \(w_b\) sincerely ranks \(b\) first. If that worker deviates to its sincere report, the two types rank one another first. Every stable integral matching must match them: otherwise they form a blocking pair.

The worker consequently obtains utility \(2\), exceeding its target utility \(1\). This contradicts Nash equilibrium.

Thus every position ranks its target partner first.

3.3. Every worker reports sincerely

Fix a worker \(a\), and replace its report by \(\pi_a\).

The target remains individually rational, and every position still ranks its target partner first. Hence \(Y\) remains stable. All position reports have Kendall distance at most \(1\), so the mechanism uses its second branch.

That branch returns a perfect local matching in every gadget. Worker \(a\), now reporting sincerely, therefore receives either of its two local positions, giving sincere utility at least \(1\).

If its original report were dishonest, this deviation would have smaller Kendall distance. Utility strictly above \(1\) would violate Nash equilibrium; utility equal to \(1\) would violate the honesty refinement. Both are impossible.

Every worker therefore reports sincerely.

3.4. Every position is sincere or uses its cutoff report

A position’s distance-\(1\) report can:

  • exchange its first two entries, which has already been excluded;
  • exchange its second entry and its outside option, producing \(c_b\); or
  • exchange entries at or below the outside option.

In the last case, replacing the report by sincerity leaves its restricted local ranking and cutoff indicator unchanged.

All workers are now sincere, and every position ranks its target partner first. Both before and after the replacement, \(Y\) is stable and the mechanism uses its second branch. Its local computations and formula test are unchanged. The position keeps utility \(2\) with a more sincere report, contradicting the refinement.

Thus every position reports either \(\pi_b\) or \(c_b\).

3.5. A gadget cannot contain two cutoff reports

If both positions in one gadget use cutoff reports, restoring either position to sincerity leaves the other cutoff in place.

With sincere workers and at least one cutoff, worker-proposing deferred acceptance returns \(Y_j\): a worker rejected by its first choice proposes to its target position, displacing the other worker if necessary, who then reaches its target position. Position-proposing deferred acceptance also returns \(Y_j\), since both positions propose to their target partners.

The correcting position therefore retains utility \(2\), contradicting the refinement.

Every gadget has at most one cutoff report.

4. Qualifying profiles imply satisfying assignments

At a profile of the form just established, the local outcomes are explicit:

  • Worker-proposing deferred acceptance returns \(X_j\) if there is no cutoff.
  • It returns \(Y_j\) if there is one cutoff.
  • Position-proposing deferred acceptance returns \(Y_j\).

The first statement follows because both workers’ first proposals, their \(X_j\)-partners, are accepted. The other two follow from the proposal sequences described above.

Every variable gadget uses worker-proposing deferred acceptance. Since the target requires \(Y_j\), each variable gadget must therefore have exactly one cutoff. These cutoffs define a Boolean assignment \(z\).

Now consider gadget \(0\).

Suppose it has one cutoff and \(\varphi(z)\) is false. The cutoff position can restore sincerity. Gadget \(0\) then has no cutoff, the variable assignment is unchanged, and the mechanism selects position-proposing deferred acceptance there. The correcting position still receives its target partner and utility \(2\), violating the refinement.

Suppose it has no cutoff and \(\varphi(z)\) is true. The mechanism selects worker-proposing deferred acceptance there, returning \(X_0\). This contradicts \(F(p)=x^\star\).

Suppose it has no cutoff and \(\varphi(z)\) is false. Both positions in gadget \(0\) are sincere. A worker there can move its outside option above its target partner while keeping its sincere first-choice position above the outside option. The target becomes individually irrational, so the mechanism uses global worker-proposing deferred acceptance.

At this modified profile all positions reject nonlocal workers, so gadgets do not interact. In gadget \(0\), both workers propose to their sincere first choices, and both proposals are accepted. The deviator receives utility \(2\), violating Nash equilibrium.

Two cutoffs have already been excluded. Therefore a qualifying profile requires one cutoff in gadget \(0\) and a satisfying variable assignment.

5. Satisfying assignments produce qualifying profiles

Conversely, let \(z\) satisfy \(\varphi\).

Have every worker report sincerely. In each variable gadget, choose exactly one cutoff position according to \(z\). In gadget \(0\), choose either position to use its cutoff report. Every other position reports sincerely.

The target is stable under these reports, and the mechanism returns \(Y\): every gadget has one cutoff and its worker-proposing outcome is \(Y_j\).

We verify all deviations.

Positions

Every position receives its sincere first choice, with utility \(2\), its maximum possible utility. No report can improve its utility.

A sincere position has Kendall distance \(0\), so it has no strictly more sincere alternative report.

A cutoff position has Kendall distance \(1\), so its only strictly more sincere report is sincerity. Correcting a cutoff in a variable gadget leaves that gadget without a cutoff; worker-proposing deferred acceptance returns \(X_j\), giving the correcting position utility \(1\).

Correcting the cutoff in gadget \(0\) likewise produces \(X_0\), because the encoded assignment satisfies \(\varphi\). Its utility also falls to \(1\).

Thus every position satisfies both requirements.

Workers

Every worker reports sincerely, so the honesty condition imposes no additional deviations to check. It remains to prove the Nash inequalities against arbitrary reports.

All positions’ fixed reports reject nonlocal workers. In any gadget, let \(d\) be its cutoff position and \(e\) its other position. Let \(r\) be the target worker of \(d\), and \(s\) the target worker of \(e\). Sincerely, \(r\) prefers \(e\) and \(s\) prefers \(d\).

Worker \(s\) cannot obtain its preferred position \(d\), because \(d\) rejects \(s\). Hence \(s\) cannot improve on target utility \(1\).

If \(r\) deviates, worker \(s\) remains sincere. Position \(d\) rejects \(s\), and every nonlocal position rejects \(s\). Thus \(e\) is \(s\)’s only mutually acceptable position. Position \(e\) ranks \(s\) first. Any stable matching must consequently match \(s\) to \(e\): otherwise \(s\) is unmatched and \((s,e)\) blocks.

Therefore \(r\) cannot obtain its preferred position \(e\). It too cannot improve on utility \(1\).

These arguments apply to every worker and every alternative report. The constructed profile is qualifying.

We have proved

\(\varphi\text{ is satisfiable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{a qualifying profile exists}.\)

6. Reduction size and computational bounds

Polynomial size of the reduction

The construction has

\(m=2(k+1)\), and \(\tau=4(k+1)\).

All numerical data are integers of magnitude \(O(m)\), so their total bit length is \(L=O(\tau m\log(m+1))\).

The mechanism tests rankings, Kendall distances and stability of a specified matching, evaluates \(\varphi\), and runs deferred acceptance. Each operation takes polynomial time in \(\tau\) and the formula length. Unrolling these computations produces a Boolean circuit of polynomial size. Its flow entries are encoded as \(0/1\) or \(1/1\).

The reduction is therefore polynomial-time, and every produced instance satisfies both promises: sincere stability of the target and reported stability of the mechanism’s output.

Exhaustive-search running time

Write \(a=|A|\), \(b=|B|\). The number of report profiles is

\(P=((b+1)!)^a((a+1)!)^b\leq((m+1)!)^\tau\).

For each profile, the number of unilateral reports to inspect, including unchanged reports, is

\(D=a(b+1)!+b(a+1)!\leq\tau(m+1)!\).

Using ordinary binary integer arithmetic, a sufficient bound for one circuit evaluation, all utility calculations, target comparisons and Kendall-distance calculations is

\(C=O\!\left(S+\tau m^2\log(m+1)+\tau m(mS+L)^2\right)\).

Here circuit output lengths are counted in its size. Each utility sums at most \(m\) rational terms, whose exact intermediate numerator and denominator lengths are \(O(mS+L)\). Schoolbook arithmetic gives the displayed bound.

Enumerating profiles and deviations therefore decides the problem in time \(O(P(1+D)C)\), yielding the bound stated at the beginning. In particular, for fixed \(\tau\), the problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(L+S\).

Quantified upper bound

A report profile has encoding length \(O(\tau m\log(m+1))\). For fixed \(p,i,q_i\), circuit evaluation and exact rational comparisons decide in polynomial time whether the deviation violates either condition:

\(U_i(p_{-i},q_i)>U_i(p)\),

or

\(K(q_i,\pi_i)<K(p_i,\pi_i)\quad\text{and}\quad U_i(p_{-i},q_i)\geq U_i(p)\).

Existence of a qualifying profile is consequently an existential quantification over \(p\), followed by a universal quantification over polynomial-length deviation descriptions, with a polynomial-time predicate. This gives the \(\Sigma_2^p\) upper bound on promised inputs.

The reduction varies the supplied mechanism circuit, so the [cited theorem concerning the fixed Gale–Shapley game](https://ifaamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2025/pdfs/p197.pdf) does not settle this circuit-input problem.
