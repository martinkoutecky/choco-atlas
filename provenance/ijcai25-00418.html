The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational ranking-type masses \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\) for \(T\subseteq S_m\), per-capita class supplies \(\sigma\in[0,1]^m\), a scoring vector \(s\), and a block bound \(R\), choose a feasible rational \(R\)-block quota schedule \(q:[0,1]\to\{0,\ldots,m\}\). Each type receives at most one copy of each class by greedy picking; if \(b_{t,k}(a)\) is the \(k\) highest-ranked available classes for type \(t\) at inventory \(a\), the induced inventory satisfies \(a_q'(z)=-\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t b_{t,q(z)}(a_q(z))\), and the expected utility is \(u_q(z)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\sum_{g_j\in b_{t,q(z)}(a_q(z))}s_{\operatorname{rank}_t(g_j)}\). Maximize \(\mathrm{USW}_\infty(q)=\int_0^1u_q(z)\,dz\), subject to feasibility and \(\int_0^1q(z)\,dz=\sum_j\sigma_j\).

For inventory \(a\), let \(B_{t,k}(a)\) be type \(t\)'s \(k\) highest-ranked distinct classes among those with \(a_j>0\), and let \(b_{t,k}(a)\) be its indicator vector. In particular, \(B_{t,0}(a)=\varnothing\); each participant receives at most one copy of each class.

Is deciding whether a feasible rational schedule with \(\mathrm{USW}_\infty(q)\ge W\) exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
For discrete quota serial dictatorship, utilitarian, egalitarian and Nash optimization take \(O(nm^2K)\) time when each picker's expected utility depends only on her quota and the total number of preceding picks, with utility-oracle time \(K\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.06086v2)

The general $\mathrm P$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$-hard classification remains unresolved. I prove a stronger structural bound than the one in the notes:

Every feasible rational schedule can be replaced by a feasible rational schedule with at least as much welfare and at most $m+1$ blocks.

Consequently, the decision problem is unchanged if $R$ is replaced by
$$ B=\min\{R,m+1\}. $$

I also prove membership in $\mathrm{NP}$, an exact algorithm with running time
$$ 2^{O(m\log(m+1))}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L), $$
and a complete polynomial-time solution for $m=2$, where two blocks suffice for optimal welfare.

The task does not explicitly specify the scores’ encoding. Throughout, I interpret the scores as binary-encoded rationals and include their encoding lengths in $L$.

1. Rates, exhaustion, and exact simulation

For an available set $A\subseteq G$ and an integer $0\le k\le |A|$, define
$$ v_j(A,k) = \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t \mathbf 1_{\{g_j\in B_{t,k}(A)\}} $$
and
$$ u(A,k) = \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t \sum_{g_j\in B_{t,k}(A)} s_{\operatorname{rank}_t(g_j)}. $$

Here $B_{t,k}(A)$ is the set of type $t$’s $k$ highest-ranked classes in $A$. Set $v_j(A,k)=0$ when $g_j\notin A$.

While the available set and quota are constant, inventory decreases at rate $v(A,k)$, and welfare accumulates at rate $u(A,k)$. The definitions give
$$ 0\le v_j(A,k)\le 1, \qquad \sum_jv_j(A,k)=k. $$

Also, because the selected sets are nested as $k$ increases,
$$ v_j(A,k)\le v_j(A,\ell) \quad\text{whenever }k\le\ell\le |A|. $$

Every feasible schedule exhausts every class by time $1$. Indeed, absolute continuity and quota feasibility imply
$$ \sum_j a_j(1) = \sum_j\sigma_j-\int_0^1q(z)\,dz = 0. $$
All final inventories are nonnegative, so each is zero.

For completeness, a rational schedule can be simulated exactly. Given current positive inventories, available set $A$, and quota $k$, the next exhaustion delay is
$$ \delta = \min_{j:v_j(A,k)>0} \frac{a_j}{v_j(A,k)}, $$
unless the quota block ends first. All classes attaining zero simultaneously are removed together. If every consumption rate is zero, no exhaustion occurs, and simulation advances to the block endpoint.

There are at most $m$ exhaustion events. For a schedule with $r$ blocks, computing rates by scanning the listed rankings takes
$$ O((r+m)\tau m) $$
rational arithmetic operations.

These operations have polynomial bit complexity in the input and schedule encoding lengths. To see this, all consumption rates have a common denominator whose bit length is polynomial in $L$. At an exhaustion update, division by a positive rate introduces a factor of polynomial bit length into a common denominator for the state. There are at most $m$ such updates; block-end updates introduce only the given rational endpoints and rate denominators.

2. A linear formulation using nested available sets

The following reconstruction lemma is the main new result.

2.1 The phase polytope

Let
$$ G_+=\{g_j:\sigma_j>0\}. $$
Assume temporarily that $G_+$ is nonempty.

Fix any nested sequence of nonempty sets
$$ A_1=G_+, \qquad A_1\supseteq A_2\supseteq\cdots\supseteq A_P. $$

For each phase $p$ and quota $1\le k\le |A_p|$, introduce a nonnegative duration $x_{p,k}$. Introduce a further nonnegative variable $x_0$ for idle time.

Consider the constraints
$$ x_0+\sum_{p=1}^P\sum_{k=1}^{|A_p|}x_{p,k}=1, $$
$$ \sum_{p=1}^P\sum_{k=1}^{|A_p|} v_j(A_p,k)x_{p,k} = \sigma_j \qquad\text{for every }j, $$
$$ x_0\ge0, \qquad x_{p,k}\ge0. $$

Define the linear welfare functional
$$ U(x) = \sum_{p=1}^P\sum_{k=1}^{|A_p|} u(A_p,k)x_{p,k}. $$

There are deliberately no strict inventory constraints in this formulation. Their absence is justified by the next lemma.

2.2 Reconstruction lemma

Lemma. Every rational point satisfying the displayed constraints yields a feasible rational schedule with welfare $U(x)$ and at most as many blocks as there are positive coordinates among $x_0$ and the $x_{p,k}$.

Proof.

Process phases in increasing order of $p$. Within phase $p$, use each quota with positive $x_{p,k}$ once, in increasing order of $k$, for duration $x_{p,k}$. Skip phases with total duration zero. Place the idle interval of duration $x_0$ last.

We verify that the rates used to define the polytope agree with the actual choices throughout this schedule.

At the start of nominal phase $p$, define
$$ a_j^p = \sigma_j- \sum_{h<p}\sum_k v_j(A_h,k)x_{h,k}. $$
The supply equations give the equivalent expression
$$ a_j^p = \sum_{h\ge p}\sum_k v_j(A_h,k)x_{h,k}. $$
In particular, $a_j^p\ge0$.

If $g_j\notin A_p$, nesting implies $g_j\notin A_h$ for every $h\ge p$. Thus
$$ a_j^p=0. $$

Now suppose $g_j\in A_p$ but $a_j^p=0$. Since the expression for $a_j^p$ is a sum of nonnegative terms,
$$ v_j(A_p,k)=0 \quad\text{for every }k\text{ with }x_{p,k}>0. $$
Every type has strictly positive mass. Therefore, at each such quota, no type selects $g_j$.

Let
$$ C_p=\{g_j:a_j^p>0\}. $$
We have $C_p\subseteq A_p$. Every class in $A_p\setminus C_p$ is unselected by every type at every quota used in phase $p$. Removing these classes consequently leaves each selected set unchanged:
$$ B_{t,k}(C_p)=B_{t,k}(A_p) \quad\text{whenever }x_{p,k}>0. $$
This also proves that each used quota satisfies $k\le |C_p|$.

It remains to show that no class in $C_p$ exhausts strictly before the phase endpoint. Its nominal ending inventory is
$$ a_j^{p+1} = a_j^p-\sum_k v_j(A_p,k)x_{p,k} = \sum_{h>p}\sum_k v_j(A_h,k)x_{h,k} \ge0. $$

A class with $a_j^{p+1}>0$ remains positive throughout the phase, because consumption is nonnegative.

Consider instead a class with
$$ a_j^p>0, \qquad a_j^{p+1}=0. $$
Its total consumption during the phase is positive. Let $K$ be the largest quota used in the phase, and put $h=x_{p,K}>0$. Monotonicity of the rates in the quota gives
$$ v_j(A_p,K)>0. $$

The quota-$K$ run is last. Before it begins, the class retains at least its reserved final consumption
$$ h\,v_j(A_p,K)>0. $$
During that last run, its inventory decreases linearly to zero exactly at the phase endpoint. Thus it is positive throughout the phase interior.

These arguments verify the prescribed linear trajectories and actual selections on every positive-length run. At phase endpoints, newly exhausted classes disappear immediately. Any additional exhausted class still present in a later nominal set is harmless: the supply equations force it to remain unselected by every later positive-duration action.

After the last phase, all inventories are zero by the supply equations. The final idle interval is feasible. Moreover,
$$ \int_0^1q(z)\,dz = \sum_{p,k}k x_{p,k} = \sum_j\sum_{p,k}v_j(A_p,k)x_{p,k} = \sum_j\sigma_j. $$

The actual selections agree with those used in $U(x)$, so the welfare is $U(x)$. Rational durations give rational block endpoints. Merging adjacent equal quotas can only reduce the block count. $\square$

2.3 Every feasible schedule supplies such a point

Take any feasible rational schedule. Partition its trajectory at exhaustion events, recording the nonempty available sets. They form a strictly decreasing chain with at most $|G_+|$ phases.

For each phase and positive quota, let $x_{p,k}$ be the total time spent at that quota in that phase. Let $x_0$ be the total time spent at quota zero, including any empty-inventory tail.

The durations sum to $1$. Since every class is exhausted, they satisfy the supply equations. Their linear welfare is exactly the original welfare.

Thus the phase polytope contains a point representing every feasible schedule with that availability chain.

3. At most $m+1$ blocks suffice

Theorem. Every feasible rational schedule has a feasible rational replacement with at least as much welfare and at most $m+1$ blocks.

Proof.

If all supplies are zero, quota zero for the whole interval proves the assertion.

Otherwise, form the phase polytope associated with the given schedule as in Section 2.3, and maximize $U(x)$ over that polytope.

The polytope is nonempty and compact: all coordinates are nonnegative and sum to $1$. Therefore a maximum exists.

There are $m$ supply equations and one time equation. Let $M x=b$ denote this system. Choose an optimal point with the smallest possible number of positive coordinates.

Its positive-coordinate columns of $M$ are linearly independent. Otherwise there would be a nonzero vector $h$, supported on those coordinates, with
$$ Mh=0. $$
For sufficiently small positive $\eta$, both $x+\eta h$ and $x-\eta h$ would remain feasible. Optimality would then force
$$ U(h)=0, $$
since otherwise one of the two directions would increase welfare.

The time row gives $\sum_i h_i=0$, so $h$ has both positive and negative coordinates. Moving in one direction until a coordinate first reaches zero would preserve feasibility and welfare while reducing the positive support, a contradiction.

Thus the positive support has size at most
$$ \operatorname{rank}(M)\le m+1. $$

This optimal point is rational. Indeed, its positive-coordinate columns are linearly independent, so its positive coordinates are uniquely determined by a full-column-rank rational linear system; selecting independent rows and applying Cramer’s rule proves rationality.

The reconstruction lemma now supplies a feasible rational schedule with at most $m+1$ blocks. Its welfare is the polytope maximum, hence at least that of the original schedule. $\square$

Corollary. Replacing $R$ by
$$ B=\min\{R,m+1\} $$
does not change the decision problem.

For $R\le m+1$, this is immediate. For $R>m+1$, the theorem replaces every qualifying schedule by one satisfying the smaller bound.

The theorem does not assert that the replacement preserves the original block count when that count is smaller than $m+1$.

4. Membership in $\mathrm{NP}$ and an exact algorithm

4.1 Linear programming for a prescribed event pattern

A schedule with at most $B$ blocks, split at every exhaustion event, has at most
$$ N\le B+m\le2m+1 $$
positive-length segments.

A candidate pattern specifies the available set $A_i$ and quota $k_i$ on each segment, with
$$ 0\le k_i\le |A_i|. $$
The sequence must have at most $B$ quota runs:
$$ 1+\bigl|\{i<N:k_i\ne k_{i+1}\}\bigr|\le B. $$

For segment durations $x_i$, define
$$ F_{ij}(x) = \sigma_j-\sum_{h<i}v_j(A_h,k_h)x_h. $$

Consider the rational linear program maximizing $\varepsilon$ subject to
$$ \sum_i x_i=1, \qquad x_i\ge\varepsilon, \qquad 0\le\varepsilon\le1, $$
$$ F_{ij}(x)\ge\varepsilon \quad\text{if }g_j\in A_i, $$
$$ F_{ij}(x)=0 \quad\text{if }g_j\notin A_i, $$
$$ \sigma_j-\sum_i v_j(A_i,k_i)x_i=0 \quad\text{for every }j, $$
$$ \sum_i u(A_i,k_i)x_i\ge W. $$

Claim. The pattern is realizable by a qualifying rational schedule if and only if the linear program is feasible and its optimum is positive.

A realized pattern has finitely many positive durations and positive starting inventories for declared available classes. Their minimum, capped at $1$, supplies a positive feasible $\varepsilon$.

Conversely, suppose the constraints hold with $\varepsilon>0$. On segment $i$, use quota $k_i$ for duration $x_i$. Each declared available class starts positive. Its ending inventory is nonnegative: at an internal endpoint it equals $F_{i+1,j}(x)$, and at the final endpoint it is zero. Hence its linear inventory remains positive throughout the segment interior. Declared unavailable classes have zero inventory and zero rate.

The resulting trajectory therefore satisfies the prescribed dynamics almost everywhere. Summing the final supply equations establishes quota fulfillment, and the last inequality establishes the welfare threshold.

If the optimum is positive, a rational optimal vertex supplies the required rational durations.

4.2 Polynomial-size certificates

The linear program has $N+1=O(m)$ variables and $O(mN+N)$ constraints. Its rational coefficients have encoding length polynomial in $m,\tau,L$: they are sums of input masses or products of input masses and scores.

The feasible region is bounded. An optimum with positive $\varepsilon$ can therefore be chosen at a rational vertex.

To make the coordinate bound explicit, let $d=N+1$. Choose $d$ linearly independent tight constraints defining a vertex and clear denominators. If the resulting integer coefficients have at most $H$ bits, Cramer’s rule expresses coordinates as ratios of determinants bounded in absolute value by
$$ d!\,2^{dH}. $$
Their bit lengths are consequently
$$ O(dH+d\log d), $$
which is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

A certificate consists of the event pattern and these rational coordinates. Checking the quota-run bound and all displayed constraints takes polynomial bit time.

Therefore the general decision problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$, including when $R$ is binary encoded.

4.3 Enumeration and running time

For each $N\le B+m$, assign every initially positive class an exhaustion-segment index
$$ e_j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}. $$
This determines
$$ A_i=\{g_j:\sigma_j>0,\ e_j\ge i\}. $$

Every realized schedule is covered: a class remains available on its exhaustion segment and disappears afterward.

There are at most $N^m$ exhaustion assignments. Enumerate also the quota sequences, of which there are at most $(m+1)^N$, retaining those satisfying quota feasibility and the bound of $B$ runs. Solve the displayed linear program for each candidate.

I use the standard theorem that rational linear programming is solvable in time polynomial in the binary encoding length, including feasibility detection and exact optimization. All coefficients here are rational, and the relevant feasible regions are bounded. This is the setting of [Khachiyan’s polynomial-time linear-programming theorem](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

The resulting running time is
$$ 2^{O((B+m)\log(m+1)+m\log(B+m))} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$
Since $B\le m+1$, this becomes
$$ 2^{O(m\log(m+1))} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

The dependence on the numerical value of $R$ disappears after capping it; its binary encoding remains included in $L$.

4.4 A simpler algorithm when $R\ge m+1$

When the block bound is unrestricted beyond $m+1$, it suffices to enumerate permutations of $G_+$.

For a permutation $\pi$ of its $d=|G_+|$ classes, take the chain
$$ A_p=\{g_{\pi(p)},\ldots,g_{\pi(d)}\}, \qquad 1\le p\le d, $$
and maximize the phase welfare functional from Section 2.

Every actual exhaustion chain can be refined to such a chain: order simultaneous exhaustions arbitrarily and give the inserted phases zero duration. Conversely, every feasible point of every such polytope reconstructs a feasible schedule.

Thus unrestricted optimal welfare equals the maximum of these $d!$ linear-programming optima. In particular, it is attained by a rational schedule with at most $m+1$ blocks. This gives another exact algorithm in
$$ d!\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
time for $R\ge m+1$.

5. A complete solution for two classes

Assume $m=2$, and write the classes as $a,b$, with supplies
$$ \alpha=\sigma_a, \qquad \beta=\sigma_b, \qquad S=\alpha+\beta. $$
Let
$$ p=\sum_{t:a\succ_t b}\mu_t. $$

Define
$$ x_*=\max\{0,S-1\}. $$

Theorem. If $R\ge2$, optimal welfare is
$$ s_2S+(s_1-s_2) \min\left\{ \alpha+(1-p)(S-2x_*), \ \beta+p(S-2x_*) \right\}. $$
It is achieved by a schedule with at most two blocks.

5.1 The amount of quota-two time

For any feasible schedule, let $x$ be its total quota-two duration. Quota two is possible only while both classes are available and consumes each class at rate one. Therefore
$$ x\le\min\{\alpha,\beta\}. $$

Let $y$ and $h$ be its total quota-one and quota-zero durations. Time and supply fulfillment give
$$ x+y+h=1, \qquad 2x+y=S. $$
Consequently,
$$ h=1-S+x\ge0, $$
so
$$ x\ge x_*. $$

Conversely, every
$$ x_*\le x\le\min\{\alpha,\beta\} $$
is feasible with the schedule:

  • Quota two for duration $x$.
  • Quota one for duration $S-2x$.
  • Quota zero for duration $1-S+x$.

The first interval cannot overconsume either class. After it, quota one consumes total remaining supply at aggregate rate one until that supply is exhausted.

The interval of allowed $x$ is nonempty. Indeed, supplies lie in $[0,1]$, so
$$ S-1\le\min\{\alpha,\beta\}. $$

5.2 Welfare depends only on $x$

Let $H$ be the total mass of allocations of an original first-ranked class. Since every allocated copy contributes at least $s_2$, welfare is
$$ \mathrm{USW}_\infty=s_2S+(s_1-s_2)H. $$

First suppose $0<p<1$. Let $y_{\mathrm{pre}}$ be total quota-one time while both classes are available.

All quota-two time occurs before the first exhaustion. If $a$ exhausts first, including exhaustion at time zero when $\alpha=0$, then
$$ p\,y_{\mathrm{pre}}+x=\alpha. $$
The other class’s inventory at that point is nonnegative, giving
$$ y_{\mathrm{pre}} = \frac{\alpha-x}{p} \le \frac{\beta-x}{1-p}. $$

Quota two contributes $x$ to $H$, since every type receives its original favorite during those intervals. Quota one before the first exhaustion contributes $y_{\mathrm{pre}}$. After $a$ exhausts, quota one contributes first-ranked allocations at rate $1-p$. Thus
$$ H = x+y_{\mathrm{pre}} +(1-p)(S-2x-y_{\mathrm{pre}}) = \alpha+(1-p)(S-2x). $$

Similarly, if $b$ exhausts first,
$$ H=\beta+p(S-2x). $$

The condition for $a$ to exhaust first is exactly the condition that the first expression is smaller:
$$ (1-p)(\alpha-x)\le p(\beta-x) $$
if and only if
$$ \alpha+(1-p)(S-2x) \le \beta+p(S-2x). $$
At simultaneous exhaustion the expressions agree. Therefore
$$ H(x)= \min\left\{ \alpha+(1-p)(S-2x), \ \beta+p(S-2x) \right\}. $$

For $p=0$, all types rank $b$ first, so $H=\beta$. The same formula yields $\beta$, because $x\le\alpha$. For $p=1$, it similarly yields $H=\alpha$. Hence the formula holds for every $p\in[0,1]$.

Both affine expressions defining $H(x)$ are nonincreasing in $x$. Since $s_1-s_2\ge0$, welfare is maximized at the smallest feasible value, namely $x_*$.

5.3 Two blocks achieve the optimum

If $S\le1$, then $x_*=0$. Use quota one for duration $S$ and quota zero for duration $1-S$.

If $S\ge1$, use quota two for duration $S-1$, followed by quota one for duration $2-S$. These durations sum to one.

Both schedules are feasible by Section 5.1 and achieve the optimal value. Zero-length intervals are omitted.

The value can be computed using $O(\tau)$ rational arithmetic operations after reading the rankings, with polynomial bit complexity in $\tau,L$.

For $R=1$, feasibility requires $S$ to be an integer. With two classes, the possible values are $0,1,2$, and the corresponding constant quotas are feasible. The threshold can then be checked directly.

6. Polynomial-time cases for arbitrary $m$

The following cases also follow directly from exact simulation.

For $R=1$, write $S=\sum_j\sigma_j$. A constant quota must equal $S$. Reject if $S$ is not an integer; otherwise simulate that unique candidate and check feasibility and welfare. This takes
$$ O(\tau m^2) $$
rational arithmetic operations and polynomial bit time.

For $R=2$, two distinct quotas $k,\ell$ with first-block duration $x$ must satisfy
$$ kx+\ell(1-x)=S, $$
hence
$$ x=\frac{S-\ell}{k-\ell}. $$
Enumerate all ordered distinct quota pairs, retain those with $0<x<1$, and simulate them. Check one-block schedules separately. There are $O(m^2)$ candidates, giving
$$ O(\tau m^4) $$
rational arithmetic operations and polynomial bit time in $m,\tau,L$.

Finally, feasibility alone is guaranteed when $R\ge m+1$. The schedule
$$ q(z)=|\{j:\sigma_j>z\}| $$
gives every type every currently available class, inducing
$$ a_j(z)=\max\{\sigma_j-z,0\}. $$
It has rational endpoints and at most $m+1$ blocks. Its welfare is
$$ \sum_j\sigma_j \sum_t\mu_t s_{\operatorname{rank}_t(g_j)}. $$
This is a feasible benchmark, rather than a general optimality formula.

7. What remains unresolved

The new reconstruction lemma removes the need for strict inventory constraints in the phase formulation and improves the necessary block bound from quadratic to linear. It also gives an exact finite description of unrestricted optimal welfare as a maximum over nested-chain linear programs.

The remaining obstacle is selecting a suitable chain, or a suitable pattern under a smaller block bound, in polynomial time. The arguments above provide neither such an algorithm nor a hardness reduction. Thus they do not settle the requested general classification.
