Classification

For each fixed feature family—product, minimum, and maximum—the threshold problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions.

Hardness holds even when all questionnaire coordinates are Boolean, every response is strict, \(\delta=1\), and \(\tau\le |Q|+1\). The proof below uses the stated freedom to choose signed coefficients and leaves singleton coefficients unpenalized.

Write \(r=|Q|\). If \(r=0\), the answer is immediately yes: a model with one singleton coefficient equal to \(1\) has objective \(0\). We henceforth consider \(r\ge1\).

1. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

For each question and response symbol, define its marginal mass by
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(w_{q,s}=\sum_{\rho:\rho_q=s}\mu_\rho,\qquad s\in\{\succ,\prec,\sim\}.\)

Because the loss is summed question by question, the objective depends only on these marginals:
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(J(a)=\sum_{q\in Q}\bigl(w_{q,\succ}\max\{0,\delta-d_q(a)\}+w_{q,\prec}\max\{0,\delta+d_q(a)\}+w_{q,\sim}|d_q(a)|\bigr)+\lambda\sum_{|S|\ge2}|a_S|.\)

These masses can be computed using \(O(\tau r)\) rational additions. Their encoding lengths are polynomial in \(L\).

An equivalent rational linear program

For every nonempty subset \(S\), introduce nonnegative variables \(u_S,v_S\) and set \(a_S=u_S-v_S\). Introduce nonnegative variables \(t_{q,\succ},t_{q,\prec},t_{q,\sim}\). Impose:

  • \(\sum_{S\ne\emptyset}(u_S-v_S)=1\);
  • \(t_{q,\succ}\ge\delta-d_q(u-v)\);
  • \(t_{q,\prec}\ge\delta+d_q(u-v)\);
  • \(t_{q,\sim}\ge d_q(u-v)\) and \(t_{q,\sim}\ge-d_q(u-v)\);
  • \(\sum_{q,s}w_{q,s}t_{q,s}+\lambda\sum_{|S|\ge2}(u_S+v_S)\le B\).

A feasible model supplies a feasible LP solution by taking the positive and negative parts of its coefficients and setting each \(t\) to its actual loss. Conversely, an LP solution supplies a feasible model satisfying \(J(a)\le B\), because its actual losses are bounded by the corresponding \(t\)-variables and \(|u_S-v_S|\le u_S+v_S\).

There are \(4r+1\) inequalities and one normalization equality. Adding slack or surplus variables therefore gives a system \(Az=b,\ z\ge0\) with \(4r+2\) rows.

A polynomial-size certificate

If this system is feasible, choose a feasible vector having the smallest number of positive coordinates. Its supported columns of \(A\) are linearly independent.

Indeed, suppose they admit a nonzero dependence vector \(h\), supported on those coordinates, with \(Ah=0\). Reverse its sign if necessary so that some \(h_j>0\), and set
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(\varepsilon=\min_{h_j>0}\frac{z_j}{h_j}.\)

Then \(z-\varepsilon h\) remains nonnegative and feasible, and has smaller support—a contradiction. Consequently, the chosen vector has at most \(4r+2\) positive coordinates.

All matrix entries have polynomial encoding length. In particular, a product feature is obtained by multiplying at most \(m\) input rationals; minimum and maximum features select an input rational. Independent supported columns contain an invertible square submatrix. Cramer’s rule, after clearing denominators, shows that their solution coordinates are rational and have polynomial encoding length: for a square system of order \(s\) whose entries have at most \(H\) bits, the resulting coordinates need \(O(s^2H+s\log s)\) bits.

Thus every yes-instance has a rational model with at most \(4r+2\) nonzero coefficients and polynomial total encoding length. A verifier can evaluate its features, normalization, and objective exactly in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,r,L)\) time. All three problems therefore belong to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

2. A Boolean feature construction encoding maximum cut

We reduce from unweighted maximum cut: given a simple undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\) and an integer \(k\), decide whether some cut has at least \(k\) edges. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer, *Some simplified NP-complete graph problems*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1).

We may assume that \(G\) has no isolated vertices and \(1\le k\le |E|\). Delete isolated vertices; the omitted trivial cases can be mapped to fixed yes- or no-instances satisfying these restrictions.

Introduce two additional criteria \(g,b\), and identify the criteria, after relabeling, with
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(N=V\cup\{g,b\},\qquad m=|V|+2.\)

The criterion \(g\) will gate the cut expression. The criterion \(b\) will provide a singleton invisible to all questions.

Questionnaire and signed weights

Let
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(\mathcal U=\{\{i\},\{g,i\}:i\in V\}\cup\{\{i,j\},\{g,i,j\}:\{i,j\}\in E\}.\)

For each \(U\in\mathcal U\), create the question \((x_U,y)\), where:

  • \(y_i=1\) for \(i\in V\cup\{g\}\), and \(y_b=0\);
  • \(x_U\) agrees with \(y\), except that its coordinates in \(U\) equal \(0\).

These pairs are distinct, and
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(r=2|V|+2|E|.\)

Assign integer weights
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(c_{\{i\}}=\deg_G(i),\quad c_{\{g,i\}}=-\deg_G(i),\quad c_{\{i,j\}}=-2,\quad c_{\{g,i,j\}}=2.\)

They satisfy \(\sum_{U\in\mathcal U}c_U=0\).

For the product or minimum family, write
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(D_{U,S}=\phi_S(x_U)-\phi_S(y),\qquad F(S)=\sum_{U\in\mathcal U}c_U D_{U,S}.\)

The two feature families coincide on Boolean points.

The column identity

If \(b\in S\), both feature values vanish, so \(F(S)=0\).

Suppose \(b\notin S\). Since \(S\ne\emptyset\),
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(D_{U,S}=\mathbf1_{\{S\cap U=\emptyset\}}-1.\)

The constant terms cancel because \(\sum_Uc_U=0\). Put \(z_i=\mathbf1_{\{i\in S\}}\) and \(E_U=\prod_{i\in U}(1-z_i)\). Each edge \(\{i,j\}\) contributes
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(E_{\{i\}}+E_{\{j\}}-E_{\{g,i\}}-E_{\{g,j\}}-2E_{\{i,j\}}+2E_{\{g,i,j\}}\)

\(\displaystyle =z_g\bigl((1-z_i)+(1-z_j)-2(1-z_i)(1-z_j)\bigr)\)

\(\displaystyle =z_g(z_i+z_j-2z_iz_j).\)

The last parenthesis is \(1\) exactly when the edge crosses the cut determined by \(S\cap V\). Hence
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(F(S)=\mathbf1_{\{g\in S\}}\operatorname{cut}_G(S\cap V)\qquad\text{when }b\notin S.\)

Let \(K\) denote the maximum cut size. We have proved:

  • \(0\le F(S)\le K\) for every nonempty \(S\);
  • \(F(\{i\})=0\) for every singleton, including \(g\) and \(b\);
  • a subset \(S=\{g\}\cup T\), where \(T\) defines a maximum cut, satisfies \(F(S)=K\).

The vanishing singleton scores are what prevents the unpenalized singleton coefficients from defeating the reduction.

3. Turning the signed weights into a valid society

Choose a power of two \(C\) strictly larger than \(\max_U|c_U|\), and set
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(p_U=\frac{1+c_U/C}{2}.\)

We require marginal probability \(p_U\) of response \(\succ\), marginal probability \(1-p_U\) of response \(\prec\), and no indifference.

Here is an explicit society with these marginals. Sort the distinct values in \(\{0,1\}\cup\{p_U:U\in\mathcal U\}\):
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_\tau=1.\)

For each interval, define a complete response type of mass \(t_j-t_{j-1}\), responding \(\succ\) to question \(U\) exactly when \(t_j\le p_U\), and otherwise responding \(\prec\).

All masses are positive binary rationals and sum to \(1\). The total mass responding \(\succ\) to question \(U\) is exactly \(p_U\). Adjacent types differ at a question whose probability is their common boundary, so the listed types are distinct. Moreover, \(\tau\le r+1\).

Set
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(\delta=1,\qquad \lambda=\frac{k-\tfrac12}{C},\qquad B=r-\frac1{2C}.\)

These satisfy \(\lambda>0\), \(B\ge0\), and the required rational encoding conditions.

A global lower bound on every model

For any real \(d\), the expected loss on question \(U\) obeys
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(p_U\max\{0,1-d\}+(1-p_U)\max\{0,1+d\}\ge1-\frac{c_U}{C}d.\)

This follows by replacing each maximum by its affine argument. Equality holds whenever \(-1\le d\le1\).

Let \(A(a)=\sum_{|S|\ge2}|a_S|\). Summing the inequalities yields
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(J(a)\ge r-\frac1C\sum_Sa_SF(S)+\lambda A(a).\)

Because singleton scores vanish and \(0\le F(S)\le K\),
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(\sum_Sa_SF(S)=\sum_{|S|\ge2}a_SF(S)\le\sum_{|S|\ge2}|a_S|F(S)\le K A(a).\)

This explicitly covers negative coefficients. Therefore
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(J(a)\ge r+\left(\lambda-\frac KC\right)A(a).\)

No-instances

If \(K\le k-1\), then
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(\lambda-\frac KC\ge\frac1{2C}.\)

Every feasible model consequently has \(J(a)\ge r>B\). Thus the constructed instance is a no-instance.

The normalization does not make the construction infeasible: \(a_{\{b\}}=1\), with all other coefficients zero, gives \(d_U=0\) on every question and objective \(r\).

Yes-instances

Suppose a cut \(T\subseteq V\) has size \(t\ge k\). Set \(S=\{g\}\cup T\) and take \(a_S=1\), with every other coefficient zero.

Since \(t\ge1\), \(T\ne\emptyset\), so \(|S|\ge2\). The model satisfies normalization and has interaction penalty \(\lambda\). Every question difference belongs to \(\{-1,0\}\), so equality holds in the expected-loss formula. Since \(F(S)=t\),
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(J(a)=r-\frac tC+\lambda=r+\frac{k-\tfrac12-t}{C}\le r-\frac1{2C}=B.\)

We have proved the exact equivalence
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(G\text{ has a cut of size at least }k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists a\text{ feasible with }J(a)\le B.\)

The construction has \(m=|V|+2\), \(r=2|V|+2|E|\), \(\tau\le r+1\), and total encoding length polynomial in \(|V|+|E|\). It is computable in polynomial time. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for both product and minimum features.

4. Maximum features

Transform any minimum-family instance by replacing every question \((x_q,y_q)\) with \((1-x_q,1-y_q)\), and exchanging \(\succ\) with \(\prec\) in every response type.

For every nonempty \(S\),
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(\max_{i\in S}(1-x_i)=1-\min_{i\in S}x_i.\)

Consequently, for the same coefficient vector,
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(d_q^{\max}(a;1-x_q,1-y_q)=-d_q^{\min}(a;x_q,y_q).\)

Exchanging strict-response directions preserves their losses; indifference losses are also preserved. Feasibility and the interaction penalty remain identical. Thus the transformation preserves \(J(a)\) for every feasible model.

It takes polynomial time and preserves the support size. Applying it to the reduction above proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for maximum features. Together with Section 1, all three classifications are \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

Marginal aggregation takes \(O(\tau|Q|)\) rational additions. Explicitly constructing all feature columns takes \(O(2^m m|Q|)\) rational operations. The resulting LP has \(O(2^m+|Q|)\) variables, \(O(|Q|)\) constraints, and rational entries of polynomial encoding length.

Rational LP feasibility is solvable in time polynomial in its explicit encoding length. Therefore all three problems admit an exact deterministic algorithm with running time
\[ \text{[replace]} \]
\(2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,|Q|,L).\)

For fixed \(m\), this is polynomial time. For variable \(m\), a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,|Q|,L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
