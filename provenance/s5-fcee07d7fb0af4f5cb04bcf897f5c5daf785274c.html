Result

Recovery admits a polynomial-time algorithm at a sufficient sample rate. The observable-type model introduces no computational obstruction: condition on the sampled types and treat each sampled worker as having reliability \(p_{T_i}\).

Here is an explicit sufficient bound, including its dependence on the promises. Define

\(a=\min\{\delta/64,\gamma/16,1/128\}\), and \(H=\left\lceil\log_2\!\left(16mK(n+m)/\epsilon\right)\right\rceil\).

There is a deterministic polynomial-time estimator with the requested guarantee whenever

\(m\ge n+1,\qquad mc^2\ge2,\qquad n\eta^2\ge8\log(4/\epsilon),\)

and

\(s\ge \dfrac{2^{30}H}{c^2a^2n\eta^2},\qquad s\le1.\)

Consequently, in the stated asymptotic regime, a sufficient rate is

\(s=\Omega\!\left(\dfrac{\log(mK/\epsilon)}{c^2\eta^2n\min\{\delta,\gamma\}^2}\right),\)

or, equivalently,

\(ns=\Omega\!\left(\dfrac{\log(mK/\epsilon)}{c^2\eta^2\min\{\delta,\gamma\}^2}\right).\)

The constants displayed above are deliberately conservative. This is a sufficient bound; optimal dependence on the promise parameters is not asserted.

For arbitrary \(n\), the guarantee need not be statistically possible, even with \(s=1\). Thus “polynomial-time solvable” here means recovery under sufficient sampling conditions, rather than recovery from every permitted sample size.

1. Concentration of the sampled worker reliabilities

Write \(p_i=p_{T_i}\) and \(P^2=\sum_{i=1}^n p_i^2\). These are analytical quantities; the algorithm does not know them.

The variables \(p_{T_i}^2\) are independent, lie in \([0,1]\), and have common mean

\(\theta=\sum_t\mu_tp_t^2\ge\eta^2.\)

The multiplicative Chernoff inequality for independent variables in \([0,1]\) gives

\(\Pr[P^2<n\eta^2/2]\le\Pr[P^2<n\theta/2]\le e^{-n\theta/8}\le e^{-n\eta^2/8}.\)

The stated condition on \(n\) makes this at most \(\epsilon/4\).

Henceforth condition on any realization of the types satisfying

\(P^2\ge n\eta^2/2.\)

Conditional on these types, all worker–task observations are independent. Repeated observable types do not change this independence: they merely cause some workers to have equal reliability parameters.

2. A rank-one representation

For each \(1\le k<K\), construct the rational matrix

\(X^{(k)}_{ij}=\mathbf1\{A_{ij}\ne0\}\left(\mathbf1\{A_{ij}\le k\}-k/K\right).\)

For an assigned response, the model gives

\(\Pr[A_{ij}\le k\mid T_i,\text{assigned}]=(1-p_i)k/K+p_iq_j\mathbf1\{g_j\le k\}+p_i(1-q_j)\mathbf1\{h_j\le k\}.\)

Subtracting \(k/K\), and multiplying by the assignment probability, therefore yields

\(\mathbb E[X^{(k)}_{ij}\mid T]=-sp_i r_j^{(k)}.\)

Thus

\(\mathbb E[X^{(k)}\mid T]=-sp(r^{(k)})^\top.\)

This is the rank-one structure underlying the cited spectral approach. [Jeong and Chung, 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/jeong23a/jeong23a.pdf).

We use a construction that leaves out the task being estimated, so that the independence needed below holds exactly.

3. Uniform control of the matrix noise

Let

\(N^{(k)}=X^{(k)}-\mathbb E[X^{(k)}\mid T].\)

Its entries are independent and centered. Each satisfies

\(|N^{(k)}_{ij}|\le2,\qquad \mathbb E[(N^{(k)}_{ij})^2\mid T]\le s,\)

because \(|X^{(k)}_{ij}|\le1\), and it vanishes when the pair is unassigned.

Applying rectangular matrix Bernstein to the entrywise matrix summands is legitimate: their norms are at most \(2\), and the two variance matrices are diagonal with norms at most \(ms\) and \(ns\). Since \(n\le m\), the variance parameter is at most \(ms\). The inequality gives

\(\Pr[\|N^{(k)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}>D\mid T]\le(n+m)e^{-H},\)

where

\(D=\sqrt{2smH}+4H/3.\)

The concentration theorem used here is [Tropp’s rectangular matrix Bernstein inequality, Theorem 1.6](https://tropp.caltech.edu/papers/Tro11-User-Friendly-preprint.pdf).

A union bound over \(k\) shows that, except with conditional probability at most \((K-1)(n+m)e^{-H}\),

\(\|N^{(k)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le D\quad\text{for every }k.\)

On the conditioned type realization, the sampling assumption implies

\(sP^2\ge \dfrac{2^{29}H}{c^2a^2}.\)

Using \(P\le\sqrt n\le\sqrt m\), we obtain the explicit bound

\(\dfrac{D}{scP\sqrt m}\le \sqrt{\dfrac{2H}{sc^2P^2}}+\dfrac{4H}{3scP\sqrt m} \le \dfrac{a}{2^{14}}+\dfrac{4ca^2}{3\cdot2^{29}} <\dfrac{a}{2^{13}}.\)

Here \(c\le1\), which follows from the promise and \(|r_j^{(k)}|\le1\).

4. Learning a worker direction independently of each target task

Fix \(j,k\), and remove column \(j\) from \(X^{(k)}\). Denote the resulting matrix by \(M_{j,k}\).

Its mean has left singular direction \(p^*=p/P\) and nonzero singular value

\(\sigma_{j,k}=sP\|r_{-j}^{(k)}\|_2.\)

Since removing one coordinate reduces the squared norm by at most \(1\),

\(\|r_{-j}^{(k)}\|_2^2\ge c^2m-1\ge c^2m/2.\)

Therefore

\(\sigma_{j,k}\ge scP\sqrt{m/2}.\)

Removing a column cannot increase the noise operator norm. Let \(\lambda_{j,k}\) be the largest singular value of \(M_{j,k}\), and let \(u_{j,k}\) be a corresponding unit left singular vector. Singular-value perturbation gives

\(\sigma_{j,k}-D\le\lambda_{j,k}\le\sigma_{j,k}+D.\)

For completeness, its directional error also follows directly. If \(v\) is the corresponding right singular vector, then \(M_{j,k}v=\lambda_{j,k}u_{j,k}\). Projecting orthogonally to \(p^*\) eliminates the mean matrix, so

\(\|(I-p^*(p^*)^\top)u_{j,k}\|_2\le D/\lambda_{j,k}\le D/(\sigma_{j,k}-D).\)

Choosing the closer sign consequently gives

\(\|u_{j,k}-p^*\|_2\le \sqrt2\,D/(\sigma_{j,k}-D)<a/128.\)

The sign can be selected without knowing \(p^*\): choose the sign having the larger squared norm of its positive coordinates. Indeed, for a vector within \(e<1/\sqrt2\) of the nonnegative unit vector \(p^*\), the negative coordinates have squared norm at most \(e^2\), and the positive coordinates have squared norm at least \(1-e^2\).

Now clip each coordinate into the interval

\([0,b_{j,k}],\qquad b_{j,k}=8s\sqrt m/\lambda_{j,k}.\)

The preceding bounds imply

\(4/P\le b_{j,k}\le24/(cP).\)

Every coordinate of \(p^*\) lies in this interval because \(p_i/P\le1/P\). Coordinatewise clipping therefore cannot increase distance from \(p^*\), and cannot increase the vector norm.

The finite-precision implementation described below produces a rational clipped vector \(w_{j,k}\) satisfying

\(\|w_{j,k}\|_2\le1,\qquad \|w_{j,k}-p^*\|_2\le a/16,\qquad 0\le(w_{j,k})_i\le32/(cP).\)

Crucially, \(w_{j,k}\) uses only columns other than \(j\). It is independent of column \(j\), conditional on the types.

5. Estimating the scaled task profiles

Define

\(v_j^{(k)}=-s^{-1}\sum_i(w_{j,k})_iX^{(k)}_{ij}.\)

Conditional on the training columns,

\(\mathbb E[v_j^{(k)}\mid T,\text{training}] =\langle w_{j,k},p\rangle r_j^{(k)}.\)

Its bias relative to \(Pr_j^{(k)}\) is at most \(aP/16\), since

\(|\langle w_{j,k},p\rangle-P|\le P\|w_{j,k}-p^*\|_2\le aP/16.\)

For the centered weighted sum before division by \(s\), the total variance is at most \(s\), and every summand has absolute value at most \(64/(cP)\). Scalar Bernstein therefore gives

\(\Pr\!\left[ |v_j^{(k)}-\mathbb E[v_j^{(k)}\mid T,\text{training}]|>aP/2 \mid T,\text{training} \right] \le 2\exp\!\left(-\dfrac{sa^2P^2}{8+256a/(3c)}\right).\)

Because \(a,c\le1\),

\(c^2(8+256a/(3c))<94,\)

and the sampling bound makes the exponent at least \(H\).

This estimate applies on each training realization satisfying the weight bounds above. The uniform matrix-noise event implies those bounds for every \(j,k\). Combining the matrix-noise failure probability with a union bound over these scalar deviations proves that, except with conditional probability at most

\((K-1)(n+m)e^{-H}+2m(K-1)e^{-H},\)

all estimates satisfy

\(|v_j^{(k)}-Pr_j^{(k)}|\le aP.\)

This inequality also allows rational rounding error of \(aP/64\), because the bias and deviation bounds leave more than that amount of slack.

6. Recovering the ordered answers and calibrating the scale

Set \(v_j^{(0)}=v_j^{(K)}=0\), and define

\(d_j^{(b)}=v_j^{(b)}-v_j^{(b-1)}.\)

The corresponding true differences are

\(\Delta r_j^{(b)} =1/K-q_j\mathbf1\{b=g_j\}-(1-q_j)\mathbf1\{b=h_j\}.\)

Consequently,

\(|d_j^{(b)}-P\Delta r_j^{(b)}|\le2aP.\)

The smallest true difference occurs at \(g_j\), and the second smallest occurs at \(h_j\). Their separation is \(P(2q_j-1)\ge P\gamma\); the separation between the second smallest and every remaining difference is \(P(1-q_j)\ge P\gamma\).

Since \(4a<\gamma\), choosing the two smallest estimated differences, in increasing order, recovers every ordered pair exactly.

It remains to remove the unknown scale \(P\). Define

\(\ell_j=-\dfrac{K}{K-2}\left(d_j^{(\hat g_j)}+d_j^{(\hat h_j)}\right),\qquad \ell=m^{-1}\sum_j\ell_j.\)

Once the pairs are correct,

\(\Delta r_j^{(g_j)}+\Delta r_j^{(h_j)}=2/K-1.\)

Thus the true value of each scale estimate is \(P\). Because \(K/(K-2)\le3\),

\(|\ell-P|\le12aP.\)

Output

\(\hat q_j=1/K-d_j^{(\hat g_j)}/\ell,\)

clipped into \([1/2,1]\). On the successful event \(\ell>0\), and

\(|\hat q_j-q_j| \le\dfrac{2a+12a}{1-12a} \le16a \le\delta.\)

Clipping preserves this bound because the true \(q_j\) belongs to the clipping interval.

Finally, the definition of \(H\) makes the combined conditional matrix and scalar failure probability at most \(\epsilon/4\). Adding the type-draw failure probability gives total failure probability at most \(\epsilon/2\), hence certainly at most \(\epsilon\). This proves the requested guarantee.

7. Polynomial running time and rational implementation

Here is a conservative bit-complexity bound for the construction above. Put

\(t=\left\lceil\log_2(4096n/a)\right\rceil,\qquad b=O\!\left(t(L+\log(nmK))\right).\)

An implementation using ordinary integer arithmetic has bit running time

\(\widetilde O\!\left( L+\tau+Km\bigl(n^2m+n^3t+b\bigr)b^2 \right).\)

In particular, it is polynomial in \(K,m,\tau,n,L\), with accuracy dependence through \(\log(1/a)\). Small promise parameters affect the required sampling rate explicitly; their binary representations are included in \(L\).

To justify finite-precision spectral computation, for each \(M_{j,k}\) form its rational Gram matrix \(G=M_{j,k}M_{j,k}^\top\). On the successful matrix-noise event, its second-to-first eigenvalue ratio is less than \(1/4\): the second singular value is at most \(D\), whereas the first is at least \(\sigma_{j,k}-D\).

Compute \(G^t e_i\) for every coordinate vector \(e_i\), discard zero vectors, and select the vector with the largest exact rational Rayleigh quotient. Some starting vector has top-eigenvector projection at least \(1/\sqrt n\). Its normalized power iterate has orthogonal-to-leading component at most \(\sqrt n\,4^{-t}\). Selection by Rayleigh quotient preserves the resulting accuracy: if a candidate has squared directional error at most \(e^2\), the selected vector has squared directional error at most \(e^2/(1-1/4)\).

Thus the chosen vector approximates the leading singular direction within \(a/128\). Rational square-root approximations give its normalization and singular value. Together with the perturbation bound, these approximations give the weight bounds used above.

The Gram construction costs \(O(n^2m)\) arithmetic operations per \(j,k\); powering all coordinate vectors costs \(O(n^3t)\). Intermediate rational numbers have \(O(b)\) bits. Rational square roots can be approximated by bisection in polynomial time. Normalize conservatively so that the resulting vector norm is at most \(1\), and round each projected profile with absolute error at most \(a\eta\sqrt n/128\); on the conditioned type event this is less than \(aP/64\).

If a required normalization is zero or a computed scale is nonpositive, output arbitrary estimates. These branches do not occur on the successful event and ensure that the algorithm terminates on every input.

No step enumerates possible unobserved types, estimates every \(p_t\), or requires a lower bound on the individual masses \(\mu_t\).

8. Why a sufficient worker count is necessary

The original assumptions alone do not guarantee recovery for arbitrary \(n\).

For a concrete example, take \(K=3\), two types with masses \(1/4\) and \(3/4\), reliabilities \(1\) and \(0\), and promises

\(\eta=1/2,\qquad \gamma=1/4,\qquad c=1/12.\)

Consider two parameter instances. In both, every task has \(q_j=3/4\). In the first, every ordered pair is \((1,2)\); in the second, every ordered pair is \((2,1)\).

The cumulative profiles for these instances are respectively

\((r_j^{(1)},r_j^{(2)})=(-5/12,-1/3)\)

and

\((r_j^{(1)},r_j^{(2)})=(1/12,-1/3).\)

Both satisfy all three promises.

With probability \((3/4)^n\), every sampled worker has reliability zero. Conditional on this event, the observed types, assignments, and responses have exactly the same distribution under the two instances, even when \(s=1\). Since successful answer outputs for the two instances are disjoint, at least one instance has failure probability at least

\(\tfrac12(3/4)^n.\)

Therefore, a guarantee for arbitrary \(n\) would be false for information-theoretic reasons. Under the sufficient conditions proved above, recovery is polynomial-time.
