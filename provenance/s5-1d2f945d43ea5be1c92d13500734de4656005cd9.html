The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(E\) of contract types, rational available masses \(u_e\), complete transitive preference orders \(\succ_S\) for \(S\in\{D,H\}\), and rational polymatroid capacity regions \(\mathcal P_S\) (given by explicit partition or laminar constraints, or polynomial-time separation oracles), decide whether there is and output an allocation \(x\in\mathcal P_D\cap\mathcal P_H\) with \(0\le x_e\le u_e\) such that, for every \(e\) with \(x_e<u_e\), at least one side \(S\) has no \(y\in\mathcal P_S\) satisfying \(0\le y_f\le u_f\), \(y_e>x_e\), and \(y_f\ge x_f\) for all \(f\succ_S e\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) contract types \(E\) with positive masses \(u_e\), each a binary rational. Here \(m=|E|=\tau\); support size counts listed types, not all possible types. Masses need not sum to one.
For each side \(S\in\{D,H\}\), the input gives a complete transitive weak preference order \(\succeq_S\), allowing ties; \(\succ_S\) denotes its strict part, and ties are not broken.
Each side has a rational polymatroid independence region \(\mathcal P_S\), given by explicit partition or laminar upper-capacity constraints with nonnegative rational capacities, or an exact polynomial-time separation oracle. Every coordinate can receive positive mass in each region.
Let \(L\) be the total finite input bit length, including a supplied bound on rational facet encoding lengths for oracle inputs. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), including oracle calls and their bit complexity.
An allocation is a real vector \(x\in\mathcal P_D\cap\mathcal P_H\) satisfying \(0\le x_e\le u_e\) for every \(e\in E\).
Call it super-stable if, for every \(e\in E\) with \(x_e<u_e\), some side \(S\) admits no real vector \(y\in\mathcal P_S\) satisfying \(0\le y_f\le u_f\) for all \(f\in E\), \(y_e>x_e\), and \(y_f\ge x_f\) for every \(f\succ_S e\). Coordinates tied with or worse than \(e\) may decrease.

Is deciding whether a super-stable allocation exists, and outputting an exact rational allocation when one exists, solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is the existence problem NP-hard?

[Kamiyama](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03820) gives a polynomial-time existence and construction algorithm for super-stable common independent sets of two loopless matroids with ties and polynomial-time independence oracles.
[Iwata and Yokoi](https://www.keisu.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/data/2017/METR17-02.pdf) give a strongly polynomial algorithm for real stable allocations in polymatroid intersection under strict orders, using saturation and exchange capacity oracles.   • Specified polymatroid independence regions rather than base regions, with nonnegative capacities.

The following results are rigorously established:

  • For explicit partition or laminar inputs, existence and exact construction take $2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ time, improving the bound in the notes.
  • A polynomial-time algorithm applies whenever a graph of preference dependencies through capacity constraints is acyclic. This includes, and extends, the two special cases proved in the notes.
  • Super-stability is equivalent to simultaneous stability under a specified collection of only $m$ strict preference refinements.
  • For oracle inputs, there is an exact exponential algorithm whose number of separation calls is $2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\Phi)$, where $\Phi$ is the value of the supplied facet encoding bound.

If only the binary encoding of $\Phi$ counts toward $L$, polynomial-time exact output in $L$ alone is impossible in the literal oracle model. This encoding issue does not classify the intended existence problem; the explicit-input classification remains open.

Throughout, $m=\tau$ counts the listed support.

1. Definitions and complexity parameters

For $A\subseteq E$, write
$$ x(A)=\sum_{e\in A}x_e. $$

Define
$$ Q_S=\mathcal P_S\cap\{x:0\le x\le u\}, \qquad Q=Q_D\cap Q_H, $$
and
$$ B_S(e)=\{f\in E:f\succ_S e\}. $$

The sets $Q_S$ and $Q$ are nonempty compact rational polytopes. They contain zero, and compactness follows from the coordinate bounds. They are downward closed: if $z$ is feasible and $0\le w\le z$, then $w$ is feasible.

For oracle inputs, let $\Phi$ denote the numerical value of the supplied bound on rational facet encoding lengths. Bounds below distinguish $\Phi$ from its binary encoding length.

I use the standard exact rational optimization–separation theorem: for a bounded rational polytope with known facet encoding bound, exact linear optimization is possible with polynomially many strong separation calls, polynomial additional bit operations, and polynomial-length rational queries. The polynomial depends on the dimension, the value of the facet encoding bound, and the encoding lengths of the objective and additional constraints. This includes lower-dimensional feasible sets. See [Grötschel, Lovász and Schrijver, Chapter 6](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1988.pdf), and the discussion of the rational oracle model in [Dadush, Végh and Zambelli](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35149/35149.pdf).

Every application below has a known bounding box $[0,u]$. Additional coordinate constraints have direct exact separation procedures.

2. Tight-set witnesses and super-stable vertices

This section supplies the structural result needed for vertex enumeration.

2.1 A finite subset-inequality description

For each side, define
$$ g_S(A)=\max_{z\in Q_S}z(A) \qquad(A\subseteq E). $$

These maxima exist by compactness. Moreover, $g_S$ is nondecreasing: if $A\subseteq T$, nonnegativity gives $z(A)\le z(T)$ for every $z\in Q_S$, hence
$$ g_S(A)\le g_S(T). $$

We have the exact description
$$ Q_S= \{z\ge0:z(A)\le g_S(A)\text{ for every }A\subseteq E\}. $$

To prove the nontrivial inclusion, let $r_S$ be a polymatroid rank function describing $\mathcal P_S$. Feasibility in $Q_S$ gives
$$ g_S(A)\le r_S(A), \qquad g_S(\{e\})\le u_e. $$
Consequently, a nonnegative vector satisfying all the displayed $g_S$ inequalities satisfies all the rank inequalities of $\mathcal P_S$ and every coordinate cap. It therefore belongs to $Q_S$. The reverse inclusion follows directly from the definition of $g_S$.

No computation of the uncapped rank function is required: each $g_S(A)$ is a bounded linear optimization problem over $Q_S$.

2.2 Exact rejection characterization

Lemma 1. Let $x\in Q$ and $x_e<u_e$. Side $S$ rejects an increase of $e$ in the sense of the task if and only if there exists
$$ e\in A\subseteq B_S(e)\cup\{e\} $$
such that
$$ x(A)=g_S(A). $$

Proof. Put $U=B_S(e)\cup\{e\}$ and define
$$ z_f= \begin{cases} x_f,&f\in U,\\ 0,&f\notin U. \end{cases} $$
Downward closure gives $z\in Q_S$.

An admissible improvement exists exactly when
$$ z+\delta\mathbf 1_e\in Q_S $$
for some $\delta>0$. Such a vector is itself an admissible improvement. Conversely, from any admissible improvement $y$, decrease its strictly better coordinates to their amounts in $x$, retain $y_e$, and set the other coordinates to zero. Downward closure then gives $z+(y_e-x_e)\mathbf1_e\in Q_S$.

In the finite subset-inequality description of $Q_S$, only inequalities indexed by sets containing $e$ change under this increase. Thus no positive increase is possible exactly when some $T\ni e$ is tight at $z$. Indeed, if all those inequalities have positive slack, half their minimum slack gives a permissible positive increase.

Suppose such a $T$ is tight, and let $A=T\cap U$. Then
$$ z(A)=z(T)=g_S(T). $$
Monotonicity of $g_S$ and feasibility of $z$ imply
$$ g_S(A)\le g_S(T)=z(A)\le g_S(A). $$
Therefore $x(A)=z(A)=g_S(A)$, as required.

Conversely, if the stated $A$ is tight, any admissible improvement satisfies
$$ y(A)>x(A)=g_S(A), $$
because it increases $e$ and cannot decrease any other coordinate of $A$. This contradicts feasibility in $Q_S$. $\square$

Full coordinates also have witnesses. If $x_e=u_e$, then
$$ u_e=x_e\le g_S(\{e\})\le u_e, $$
so the singleton is tight on either side. Consequently,
$$ x\text{ is super-stable} \iff \forall e\in E,\ \exists S,\ \exists A: \quad e\in A\subseteq B_S(e)\cup\{e\}, \quad x(A)=g_S(A). $$

2.3 Faces and vertices

Theorem 2. The super-stable set is a union of faces of $Q$. If a super-stable allocation exists, a super-stable vertex of $Q$ exists.

Proof. Choose one permitted witness $(S_e,A_e)$ for each coordinate. The associated set is
$$ F= \{x\in Q:x(A_e)=g_{S_e}(A_e)\text{ for every }e\in E\}. $$

Each equality makes an inequality valid throughout $Q$ tight. Hence $F$ is a face, possibly empty. Explicitly, if a point of $F$ is a strict convex combination of two points of $Q$, equality in every selected inequality forces both points to satisfy those same equalities.

Every point of a nonempty $F$ is super-stable by Lemma 1 and the full-coordinate observation. Conversely, every super-stable allocation belongs to a face constructed this way.

A nonempty compact face contains a vertex: successively maximizing its coordinates produces nested nonempty compact faces, ending in a singleton. A vertex of $F$ is a vertex of $Q$, because any convex decomposition in $Q$ would, by the face property, be a decomposition in $F$. $\square$

2.4 Rational certificate size and verification

A vertex of a polytope in $\mathbb R^m$ has $m$ linearly independent active defining inequalities. Otherwise a nonzero direction orthogonal to all active normals would permit sufficiently small perturbations in both directions, preserving the inactive inequalities by their positive slacks. This would contradict extremality.

Let $\beta$ bound the encoding length of each row in a rational description of $Q$. Clearing denominators in the selected $m$ rows produces integer entries of $O(\beta)$ bits. Each determinant has
$$ O\bigl(m(\beta+\log(m+1))\bigr) $$
bits: its expansion has at most $m!$ terms, each a product of $m$ entries. Cramer’s rule therefore bounds the total vertex encoding length by
$$ O\bigl(m^2(\beta+\log(m+1))\bigr). $$

For explicit inputs, $\beta=O(L)$. For oracle inputs, one may use $\beta=O(L+\Phi)$, by describing $Q$ with the original facet inequalities and the coordinate bounds.

A rational candidate $x$ can be verified without enumerating subsets. After checking feasibility, for each non-full coordinate solve
$$ M_{S,e}(x)= \max\left\{ y_e: y\in Q_S,\ y_f\ge x_f\text{ for every }f\in B_S(e) \right\}. $$
The feasible set contains $x$ and is compact. Thus side $S$ rejects precisely when $M_{S,e}(x)=x_e$.

This requires at most $2m$ exact LPs. Therefore the explicit existence problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$. Oracle verification has polynomial complexity in $m,\tau,L,\Phi$ and the candidate encoding length, including polynomially many oracle calls.

3. An explicit saturation formula

Suppose side $S$ is described by a family $\mathcal C_S$ of upper-capacity constraints
$$ z(C)\le c_{S,C} \qquad(C\in\mathcal C_S). $$

For a feasible $x$, define
$$ \lambda_{S,e}(x)= \min\left\{ u_e,\ \min_{\substack{C\in\mathcal C_S\\e\in C}} \left(c_{S,C}-x(C\cap B_S(e))\right) \right\}. $$
An empty inner minimum is $+\infty$.

Lemma 3. The largest achievable amount of $e$, while preserving at least the amounts of all strictly better coordinates, is exactly $\lambda_{S,e}(x)$.

Proof. Every admissible vector $y$ satisfies, for each $C\ni e$,
$$ y_e+x(C\cap B_S(e))\le y(C)\le c_{S,C}. $$
It also satisfies $y_e\le u_e$. Hence $y_e\le\lambda_{S,e}(x)$.

For attainment, retain $x$ on $B_S(e)$, assign $\lambda_{S,e}(x)$ to $e$, and assign zero elsewhere. A constraint containing $e$ holds by the defining minimum. A constraint not containing $e$ holds because the retained vector is bounded coordinatewise by $x$ there. Coordinate bounds also hold. $\square$

In particular, feasibility gives
$$ x_e\le\lambda_{D,e}(x), \qquad x_e\le\lambda_{H,e}(x). $$
Thus the complete super-stability condition becomes
$$ x\in Q, \qquad x_e=\min\{\lambda_{D,e}(x),\lambda_{H,e}(x)\} \quad\text{for every }e\in E. $$

For a non-full coordinate, equality means that at least one side rejects. For a full coordinate, both thresholds equal $u_e$, so the equation also holds.

These equations make the dependency structure explicit.

4. Polynomial time for acyclic capacity dependencies

Define a directed graph $G$ on $E$. Insert an arc $f\to e$ whenever, for some side $S$ and listed constraint $C$,
$$ f\succ_S e, \qquad f,e\in C. $$

An arc records that $x_f$ can occur in the formula for $\lambda_{S,e}(x)$.

Theorem 4. If $G$ is acyclic, existence and exact construction are polynomial-time solvable. There is at most one super-stable allocation.

This theorem requires only explicit upper-capacity constraints; its proof does not use laminarity.

Algorithm.

  • Choose a topological ordering of $G$, and initialize $p=0$.
  • When processing $e$, compute

$$ g_e=\min\{\lambda_{D,e}(p),\lambda_{H,e}(p)\}. $$

  • Set $p_e=g_e$. If the resulting prefix is infeasible in $Q$, report nonexistence.
  • If all coordinates are processed successfully, output $p$.

Proof. Inductively, suppose every super-stable allocation agrees with $p$ on all processed coordinates.

For the current $e$, every coordinate appearing in any sum
$$ x(C\cap B_S(e)), \qquad e\in C, $$
is a predecessor of $e$ in $G$. It has therefore already been processed. Any hypothetical super-stable allocation $x$ has
$$ \lambda_{S,e}(x)=\lambda_{S,e}(p). $$
The fixed-point characterization in Section 3 forces $x_e=g_e$.

If the updated prefix is infeasible, no feasible completion can exist: every super-stable completion would contain that prefix coordinatewise, contrary to downward closure.

If the algorithm finishes, later assignments have not changed any previously evaluated threshold, because every threshold depends only on predecessors. Hence the final feasible vector satisfies every fixed-point equation from Section 3 and is super-stable. The forced-coordinate induction proves uniqueness. $\square$

Let $n=|\mathcal C_D|+|\mathcal C_H|$. Direct graph construction, threshold evaluation, and prefix checking take $O(nm^2+m^2)$ elementary set operations and rational arithmetic operations.

These operations have polynomial bit complexity. Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of all input capacities and masses. Its bit length is at most $L$. Every assigned amount is an input constant minus a sum of earlier amounts, so its denominator divides $D$. Prefix amounts remain between zero and their input caps. Intermediate sums therefore have $O(L+\log(m+1))$ bits.

The graph is acyclic in both previously established special cases:

  • If the sides have identical weak orders, every arc goes from an earlier indifference class to a later class.
  • If one side ties all types, only the other side contributes arcs, and these follow its strict order.

The theorem also permits conflicting global orders when the conflicting comparisons do not occur inside shared capacity constraints.

5. A singly exponential algorithm for explicit laminar inputs

Theorem 5. For explicit partition or laminar inputs, existence and exact rational construction take
$$ 2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
bit time.

5.1 Counting the inequalities

Remove empty capacity sets and merge duplicate sets by retaining their smallest capacity.

A laminar family of distinct nonempty subsets of an $m$-element set has at most $2m-1$ members. To prove this, append the singleton sets and $E$, preserving laminarity, and form the inclusion tree. Its leaves are the $m$ singletons. Every internal node has at least two children: its maximal proper subsets cover it, and one proper subset alone cannot cover it.

If there are $i$ internal nodes, the tree has $m+i-1$ edges and at least $2i$ edges. Hence $i\le m-1$, proving the bound.

Both sides together consequently contribute at most $4m-2$ capacity inequalities. Including the $m$ upper and $m$ lower coordinate bounds, $Q$ has a description with at most
$$ N\le6m-2 $$
rows. Partition families are laminar, so the same bound applies.

5.2 Enumeration and correctness

Enumerate all choices of $m$ defining rows. For each choice:

  • Discard it if the row normals are linearly dependent.
  • Solve their equality system exactly.
  • Check the resulting vector against all defining inequalities.
  • If feasible, check the saturation equations from Section 3.

Output the first accepted vector. If no vector is accepted, report nonexistence.

Every vertex of $Q$ occurs in this enumeration, by the active-normal argument in Section 2. If a super-stable allocation exists, Theorem 2 supplies a super-stable vertex. Thus the enumeration cannot miss existence. Every accepted vector is feasible and super-stable by the explicit saturation characterization.

The number of row choices is at most
$$ \binom{6m-2}{m}\le2^{6m-2}. $$
Each choice requires polynomial bit time for exact linear algebra, feasibility testing, and saturation testing. The determinant bounds in Section 2 control all intermediate rational encoding lengths.

This proves the stated running time. The algorithm uses no supplied separation-oracle calls in the explicit model.

6. Only $m$ strict refinements are needed

For each $e\in E$, choose a strict refinement $\succ_S^{(e)}$ of each side’s weak order that puts $e$ first within its own indifference class. Order the other tied types arbitrarily. Let $I^{(e)}$ denote the resulting pair of strict preference orders, with the same regions and masses.

Here stability in a strict instance means precisely the allocation condition in the task, using that instance’s strict orders.

Theorem 6. A feasible allocation $x$ is super-stable in the original instance if and only if it is stable in every one of the $m$ instances $I^{(e)}$.

Proof. Under any strict refinement, the strictly better set of every coordinate contains its original strictly better set. Any improvement admissible under the refinement is therefore admissible under the original order. Thus original super-stability implies stability under every refinement.

Conversely, suppose $x$ is not super-stable. There is a non-full $e$ for which both sides admit an improvement preserving all original strictly better coordinates. In $I^{(e)}$, placing $e$ first within its tie gives exactly
$$ B_S^{(e)}(e)=B_S(e). $$
The same two improvements are admissible in $I^{(e)}$, so $x$ is not stable there. $\square$

This is a reduction to simultaneous stability, not a polynomial search algorithm.

For example, take two types $a,b$ of mass $1$, both sides’ regions given by $x_a+x_b\le1$, and both sides tying $a$ with $b$. The refinement placing $a$ first has the unique stable allocation $(1,0)$; the refinement placing $b$ first has the unique stable allocation $(0,1)$. Each strict instance has a solution, but they have no common solution. The original instance accordingly has no super-stable allocation.

7. General oracle search and its bit complexity

The subset description from Section 2 gives an exact oracle algorithm.

First compute $g_S(A)$ for both sides and every nonempty $A\subseteq E$. This uses
$$ 2(2^m-1)\le2^{m+1} $$
exact optimization problems.

The resulting explicit description of $Q$, including coordinate bounds, has at most
$$ N_{\mathrm{or}}=2(2^m-1)+2m $$
rows. Enumerate independent $m$-row systems as in Section 5.

Feasibility can now be checked against the computed subset inequalities. Super-stability can likewise be checked by enumerating the permitted witnesses
$$ e\in A\subseteq B_S(e)\cup\{e\}, \qquad x(A)=g_S(A). $$
Thus no further supplied oracle calls are needed after rank precomputation.

There are at most
$$ N_{\mathrm{or}}^m=2^{O(m^2)} $$
row choices. Theorem 2 proves completeness.

For quantitative bounds, put
$$ \Lambda=m+\tau+L+\Phi+1. $$
Choose fixed polynomials $p,q$ large enough to bound the additional bit work, rational encoding lengths, and separation calls of each precomputation LP and the subsequent exact linear algebra. Such bounds exist by the rational optimization theorem and the determinant bounds already proved.

The algorithm uses at most
$$ 2^{m+1}q(\Lambda) $$
supplied separation calls, on queries of at most $p(\Lambda)$ bits, and
$$ 2^{O(m^2)}p(\Lambda) $$
additional bit operations.

If $T_S(m,b)$ bounds side $S$’s oracle bit cost on a query of encoding length at most $b$, a conservative total bound is
$$ 2^{O(m^2)}p(\Lambda) + 2^{m+1}q(\Lambda) \left( T_D(m,p(\Lambda))+T_H(m,p(\Lambda)) \right). $$

These bounds are polynomial in $\Phi$ per LP. They are polynomial in $L$ per LP only when $\Phi$ is guaranteed polynomial in $L$, or its value is included in the size parameter.

7.1 Why the oracle encoding qualification is necessary

Consider one type of mass $1$, with both sides’ regions
$$ 0\le x_e\le2^{-N}, $$
where the positive integer $N$ is stored in binary. The unique super-stable allocation is
$$ x_e=2^{-N}. $$
Every coordinate can receive positive mass.

This region has an exact separation oracle running in time polynomial in $\log(N+1)$ and the query encoding length. To see this, write a positive query as $a/d$ with positive integers $a,d$, and let $b$ be the binary length of $d$.

  • If $N>b$, then

$$ \frac ad\ge\frac1d>2^{-b}>2^{-N}. $$
The oracle may return the valid separating inequality $x_e\le2^{-b}$, whose encoding length is $O(b)$.

  • If $N\le b$, the oracle constructs $2^N$ using $O(b)$ bits, compares exactly, and either accepts or returns the endpoint inequality.

Negative queries are separated by $x_e\ge0$, and zero is accepted.

The oracle program and a binary description of a facet encoding bound $\Phi=O(N+1)$ have total length $O(\log(N+1))$. Nevertheless, an ordinary exact rational encoding of $2^{-N}$ requires $\Omega(N)$ bits.

Therefore the literal binary-bound oracle convention cannot support polynomial-time exact output in $L$ alone. This is an output-length obstruction, not a hardness proof for existence.
