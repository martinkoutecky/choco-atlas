The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a compact rational price polytope \(Y\), a rational market-side ratio \(\rho\), rational type distributions \(\mu^S\) and \(\mu^B\), and each type’s convex piecewise-linear contribution listed by rational affine pieces, compute a rational minimizer \(y^\star \in \arg\min_{y \in Y} \left( \rho \sum_{t \in T_S} \mu^S_t f_t(y) + (1-\rho) \sum_{t \in T_B} \mu^B_t g_t(y) \right)\). When the economy has zero duality gap, return the corresponding Walrasian price.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\geq 1\) be the number of goods. The input includes a nonempty compact price polytope \(Y=\{y\in\mathbb R_+^m:Ay\leq b\}\), given by \(H\) rational inequalities, and a rational seller share \(0<\rho<1\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses. Here \(T_S,T_B\) are nonempty, \(\tau=|T_S|+|T_B|\), and the conditional distributions satisfy \(\sum_{t\in T_S}\mu_t^S=\sum_{t\in T_B}\mu_t^B=1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
Each contribution \(f_t,g_t:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R\) is specified as the maximum of a nonempty explicit list of rational affine functions; coefficients and function values may have either sign. Let \(K\) count all listed pieces and \(L\) be the total binary input length.
The computational problem is to return a rational \(y^\star\in\arg\min_{y\in Y}F(y)\), where \(F(y)=\rho\sum_{t\in T_S}\mu_t^Sf_t(y)+(1-\rho)\sum_{t\in T_B}\mu_t^Bg_t(y)\).
For the equilibrium interpretation, promise additionally that \(F\) is the price dual of an underlying quasilinear economy, its primal welfare optimum equals \(\min_{y\in\mathbb R_+^m}F(y)\), and \(Y\) contains a global dual minimizer; the returned vector must then be a Walrasian price.

Is this exact minimizer problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,H,K,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies with equally many sellers and buyers, cyclic double-auction prices converge to a minimizer under positive, nonsummable, square-summable step sizes satisfying \(\sum_k|b_k-\lambda a_k|<\infty\) and weight \(\alpha=\lambda/(1+\lambda)\), for \(\lambda>0\). This is an asymptotic convergence result, with no polynomial-time exact-computation guarantee. [Ma–Li](https://www.mechanism-design.org/arch/v001-1/p_01.pdf)
The literature search identified no result specifically treating the distribution-input problem stated here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Used m for the price dimension, rather than the finite model's number of sellers.
  • Specified Y by an explicit rational inequality description and retained nonemptiness and nonnegative prices.
  • Interpreted rho as the seller share of total mass, restricted it to (0,1), and normalized each side's conditional distribution to mass one.
  • Interpreted listed affine pieces as a maximum representation, with nonempty piece lists and unrestricted rational coefficients, consistent with real-valued convex contributions.
  • Introduced H, K, and total binary input length L to specify the computational representation.
  • Made the Walrasian interpretation conditional on genuine economic dual contributions, zero primal–dual welfare gap, and inclusion of a global dual minimizer in Y; zero gap alone cannot justify an arbitrarily constrained price minimizer.

Result

The exact minimizer problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,H,K,L\). An explicit epigraph formulation reduces it to rational linear programming. The same algorithm returns a Walrasian price under the equilibrium promise.

1. Constructing the linear program

Let \(T=T_S\sqcup T_B\), treating seller and buyer types as distinct indices. Define \(h_t=f_t\) for seller types and \(h_t=g_t\) for buyer types, and set
\[ w_t=\begin{cases} \rho\mu_t^S,&t\in T_S,\\ (1-\rho)\mu_t^B,&t\in T_B. \end{cases} \]
Thus every \(w_t\) is a positive rational number, \(\sum_{t\in T}w_t=1\), and \(F(y)=\sum_{t\in T}w_th_t(y)\).

Write each explicitly listed contribution as
\[ h_t(y)=\max_{1\leq j\leq k_t}\{a_{tj}^{\mathsf T}y+c_{tj}\}, \qquad \sum_{t\in T}k_t=K. \]

Introduce one unrestricted real variable \(z_t\) per type. Consider the linear program
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad &\sum_{t\in T}w_tz_t\\ \text{subject to}\quad &Ay\leq b,\qquad y\geq0,\\ &z_t\geq a_{tj}^{\mathsf T}y+c_{tj} &&\text{for every }t\in T,\ 1\leq j\leq k_t. \end{aligned} \]

It has \(m+\tau\) variables and \(H+m+K\) inequalities. The variables \(z_t\) must be unrestricted: imposing their nonnegativity could change the problem because contribution values may be negative.

2. Exact equivalence and attainment

For every feasible pair \((y,z)\), the piece inequalities imply \(z_t\geq h_t(y)\). Positivity of the weights therefore gives
\[ \sum_{t\in T}w_tz_t\geq\sum_{t\in T}w_th_t(y)=F(y). \]
Conversely, for every \(y\in Y\), choosing \(z_t=h_t(y)\) produces a feasible pair whose linear objective equals \(F(y)\).

Each \(h_t\) is continuous, being the maximum of finitely many affine functions. Consequently, \(F\) is continuous. Since \(Y\) is nonempty and compact, \(F\) attains a finite minimum, say \(v\). The corresponding epigraph pair attains linear-program value \(v\). Hence
\[ \min_{\text{LP feasible }(y,z)}\sum_{t\in T}w_tz_t =\min_{y\in Y}F(y)=v. \]

In particular, if \((y^\star,z^\star)\) is any LP optimum, then
\[ v\leq F(y^\star)\leq\sum_{t\in T}w_tz_t^\star=v. \]
Thus \(y^\star\in\arg\min_{y\in Y}F(y)\). Moreover, every optimum has \(z_t^\star=h_t(y^\star)\): otherwise decreasing a strictly slack \(z_t^\star\) to \(h_t(y^\star)\) would strictly decrease the objective.

3. Polynomial-time exact computation

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly encoded rational linear program can be solved in polynomial bit complexity, returning an exact rational optimum whenever it is feasible and has a finite optimum. This theorem includes lower-dimensional feasible regions and unrestricted variables; unrestricted variables can also be replaced by differences of nonnegative variables. Polynomial-time LP algorithms and recovery of exact optima from rational size bounds are discussed in [Goemans’s linear-programming notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-854j-advanced-algorithms-fall-2008/908fe54d659623efadda2d033b895caf_notes_lp.pdf).

All its hypotheses have been checked here:

  • The LP has rational coefficients.
  • Its numbers of variables and inequalities are polynomial in the stated parameters.
  • It is feasible and attains a finite optimum.
  • Its binary encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,H,K,L\).

For the last point, the affine coefficients and polytope inequalities are copied from the input. The only new nonconstant coefficients are the weights \(w_t\). Multiplying binary-encoded rational numbers adds their numerator and denominator bit lengths, up to a constant overhead. Computing \(1-\rho\) also incurs only polynomial overhead. Thus every new coefficient has \(O(L)\) bits, and writing the entire LP requires polynomially many bits.

The algorithm is therefore:

  • Construct this rational LP.
  • Solve it with an exact polynomial-time rational LP algorithm.
  • Return the \(y\)-coordinates of its solution.

No expansion of rational masses into a finite population is required.

A polynomial-size rational minimizer exists

For completeness, the output-size issue can also be verified directly.

Let \(P\) be the epigraph feasible polyhedron and let \(Q\) be its optimal face. At every point of \(Q\), we proved \(z_t=h_t(y)\). Since \(Y\) is compact and the contributions are continuous, all coordinates of \(Q\) are bounded. The set \(Q\) is also closed and nonempty, so it is a nonempty compact convex set and has an extreme point \(x^\star=(y^\star,z^\star)\).

This point is a vertex of \(P\). Indeed, if it were a nontrivial convex combination of two points of \(P\), linearity of the objective and optimality would force both points to belong to \(Q\), contradicting extremality in \(Q\).

Put \(N=m+\tau\), and let \(B\) be the total LP encoding length. At a vertex, the active constraint normals span \(\mathbb R^N\): otherwise a nonzero vector orthogonal to them would permit sufficiently small feasible perturbations in both directions, contradicting the vertex property. Therefore \(x^\star\) is the unique solution of \(N\) linearly independent active rational equations.

Clear denominators separately in these equations. Every resulting integer coefficient, including right-hand sides, has \(O(B)\) bits. Cramer’s rule expresses each coordinate as a ratio of integer determinants. By the determinant expansion, their absolute values are at most \(N!\,2^{O(NB)}\). Each coordinate consequently has \(O(NB+N\log N)\) bits.

Thus an exact rational minimizer has polynomial output length, even when \(Y\) has empty interior.

4. The equilibrium promise

Suppose the additional promise holds. Because \(Y\subseteq\mathbb R_+^m\) and contains a global dual minimizer,
\[ \min_{y\in Y}F(y)=\min_{y\in\mathbb R_+^m}F(y). \]
The returned vector is therefore a global price-dual optimum.

The usual quasilinear price-duality argument explains the Walrasian conclusion. Let \(x^\star\) be an allocation attaining the promised primal welfare optimum \(W^\star\), and let \(\mathcal L(x,y)\) be the price Lagrangian. Feasibility and nonnegative prices give
\[ W^\star\leq\mathcal L(x^\star,y^\star) \leq\sup_x\mathcal L(x,y^\star) =F(y^\star)=W^\star. \]
Every inequality is consequently an equality. Equality with the supremum means that the allocation maximizes the price Lagrangian. Its separation into individual quasilinear objectives implies that every positive-mass type chooses optimally at \(y^\star\). Equality in the first inequality gives resource complementarity, including zero prices for any excess supply when free disposal is allowed. These are precisely the supporting optimality and market-clearing conditions for a Walrasian price.

The cyclic-auction convergence theorem concerns an asymptotic price process; it is not needed for this exact algorithm. See [Ma–Li, Theorem 4.4](https://www.mechanism-design.org/arch/v001-1/p_01.pdf). The decisive computational assumptions here are the explicit affine-piece lists and their rational encoding.
