The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) candidates and a target \(q\), decide whether there is a finite labeled binary voting tree \(\Theta\) such that, for every rational distribution \(\mu\) over strict rankings of \([n]\) with nonzero pairwise margins, \(d^+_{T_\mu}(\Theta(T_\mu)) \ge q\), and construct such a tree when one exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society \(\mu\) is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) strict ranking types with positive binary-rational masses summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not \(n!\).
Require \(\mu(i\succ j)-\mu(j\succ i)\ne 0\) for distinct candidates; orient \(i\to j\) in \(T_\mu\) exactly when this margin is positive. Let \(d^+_{T_\mu}(i)\) denote its out-degree.
A voting tree is a finite rooted tree whose nodes have zero or two children, with leaves labeled by candidates; repetitions are allowed, and no balance or size bound is imposed. Each internal node returns the pairwise winner of its children's labels; equal labels return that label. Its root label is \(\Theta(T_\mu)\).
The decision problem asks whether a single tree \(\Theta\), independent of \(\mu\), satisfies \(d^+_{T_\mu}(\Theta(T_\mu))\ge q\) for every such society. The associated construction problem returns an explicit tree when one exists.
Let \(L\) be the bit length of the relevant finite input. State decision times in \(m,L\), construction times also in the output size, and any society-processing times in \(m,\tau,L\); societies are universally quantified, not supplied as decision inputs.

1. Rational societies realize every tournament

Let $\mathcal T_m$ be the set of tournaments on $[m]$. An admissible society induces a member of $\mathcal T_m$ because each pairwise margin is nonzero.

The converse also holds, including when “binary-rational” means dyadic rational.

Lemma 1. Every $T\in\mathcal T_m$ has an admissible realization with support size $O(m^2)$ and encoding length $O(m^3\log m)$.

Proof. Put $e=\binom m2$. For each edge $u\to v$ of $T$, enumerate the other candidates as $r_1,\ldots,r_{m-2}$ and introduce the rankings
$$ u\succ v\succ r_1\succ\cdots\succ r_{m-2}, $$
$$ r_{m-2}\succ\cdots\succ r_1\succ u\succ v. $$
This pair contributes unnormalized margin $2$ to $u$ against $v$. Every other margin cancels: remaining candidates occur in opposite orders, and each remaining candidate is below both $u,v$ in one ranking and above both in the other.

Consequently, the $2e$ ranking occurrences give margin $2$ in every prescribed direction.

Let $p$ be the smallest power of two with $p\ge e$. Add $p-e$ ranking–reverse pairs, each contributing zero margins. Assign every occurrence mass $1/(2p)$. All prescribed margins now equal $1/p>0$.

Merge repeated rankings. The resulting support masses are positive dyadic rationals summing to $1$. There are at most $2p\le4e$ support rankings. Each ranking uses $O(m\log m)$ bits, and each mass uses $O(\log m)$ bits, proving the encoding bound. $\square$

Thus the original quantifiers are exactly
$$ \exists\Theta\ \forall T\in\mathcal T_m: \quad d_T^+(\Theta(T))\ge q. $$
In particular, the realizing society may depend on $T$, but the witnessing tree remains the same for every $T$.

For a supplied society with support size $\tau$ and encoding length $L$, its tournament can be computed in
$$ O(m^2\tau L^2) $$
bit operations. Indeed, the product of all denominators has $O(L)$ bits. Converting to integer weights over this common denominator and summing the signed weights for every pair gives every margin exactly. This is a society-processing bound, not a decision bound.

2. Threshold bounds and constructive sufficient conditions

Define
$$ K(m)=\max_\Theta\min_{T\in\mathcal T_m}d_T^+(\Theta(T)). $$
The maximum exists because the achievable guarantees form a nonempty subset of $\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$. Decision asks whether $q\le K(m)$.

Elementary cases

For every $m\ge2$,
$$ \lfloor\log_2m\rfloor\le K(m)\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor. $$

For the lower bound, take a balanced tree of height $h=\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor$ with distinct leaf labels. Its winner defeats one opponent at each level. These opponents originate in disjoint sibling subtrees and therefore have distinct labels. Its out-degree is at least $h$.

For the upper bound, when $m$ is odd, the cyclic tournament has every out-degree equal to $(m-1)/2$. When $m$ is even, delete one vertex from a regular tournament on $m+1$ vertices. Every remaining out-degree is at most $m/2$. Evaluating any tree on the relevant tournament proves the upper bound.

Hence
$$ K(2)=K(3)=1,\qquad K(4)=K(5)=2. $$
For $q\le\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor$, the balanced construction has $2^{q+1}-1$ nodes and can be written in $O(L+S)$ time, using the explicit encoding specified below. For $q=0$, a single leaf suffices.

A stronger constructive bound

The following boosting construction is the one used by [Iglesias, Ince, and Loh, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1211.0515).

Lemma 2. If a tree on $n$ candidates guarantees $k\ge1$, there is a tree on $n+k+1$ candidates guaranteeing $k+1$.

Proof. Take a set $U$ of size $n+k$ and a new candidate $0$. For every $n$-subset $A\subseteq U$, match $0$ against a relabeled copy $\Theta_A$ of the given tree. Combine these matches using any full binary tree.

Fix a tournament, and let
$$ W=\{\Theta_A(T):A\subseteq U,\ |A|=n\}. $$
If $|W|\le k$, an $n$-subset $A\subseteq U\setminus W$ exists. Its winner would belong to $A\cap W$, a contradiction. Thus $|W|\ge k+1$.

If $0$ defeats all of $W$, it wins with at least $k+1$ victories. Otherwise, after the initial matches, all labels lie in
$$ R\cup\{0\},\qquad R=\{w\in W:w\to0\}, $$
and at least one label lies in $R$. Since every member of $R$ defeats $0$, a subtree containing an $R$-label returns an $R$-label, by induction. The root therefore lies in $R$. It has $k$ victories in a subset that produced it and the additional victory against $0$. $\square$

Put
$$ a_k=\frac{k(k+1)}2+1. $$
Starting with one match on $a_1=2$ candidates, Lemma 2 applies successively because
$$ a_k=a_{k-1}+k. $$
It follows that, for $q\ge1$,
$$ m\ge a_q\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q\le K(m). $$
Embedding the constructed tree into $[m]$ preserves its guarantee: its evaluation depends only on the used candidates, and its full out-degree cannot decrease.

For later use, let $B_k$ be the leaf count of this construction. Then
$$ B_1=2, $$
$$ B_k=\binom{a_k-1}{k-1}(B_{k-1}+1)\qquad(k\ge2). $$
Indeed, the boosting step uses one old tree and one anchor leaf for every subset.

Since
$$ \binom{a_k-1}{k-1}\le a_k^{k-1}, \qquad B_{k-1}+1\le2B_{k-1}, $$
we obtain
$$ \log_2 B_q \le 1+\sum_{k=2}^{q}\bigl(1+(k-1)\log_2a_k\bigr) = O(q^2\log(q+2)). $$
Thus
$$ B_q=2^{O(q^2\log(q+2))}. $$
This bounds a particular constructive witness, not the minimum witness size.

3. A finite decision algorithm

Put
$$ M=|\mathcal T_m|=2^{\binom m2}, \qquad N=m^M. $$
Enumerate $\mathcal T_m$. For a tree $\Theta$, define its behavior vector
$$ v_\Theta=(\Theta(T))_{T\in\mathcal T_m}\in[m]^M. $$
There are $N$ possible vectors.

For vectors $a,b$, define $a*b$ coordinatewise by the tournament match:
$$ (a*b)(T)=\text{the winner of }a(T)\text{ against }b(T)\text{ in }T. $$
Equal labels return that label.

Let $c_i$ be the constant vector with value $i$. Starting with
$$ C_0=\{c_1,\ldots,c_m\}, $$
compute
$$ C_{t+1}=C_t\cup\{a*b:a,b\in C_t\}. $$

Lemma 3. The set $C_t$ consists exactly of the behavior vectors of trees of height at most $t$.

Proof. At height zero, trees are labeled leaves. Joining two trees under a new root applies $*$ to their vectors. Conversely, each child of a tree of height at most $t+1$ has height at most $t$. Induction proves both inclusions. $\square$

Every strict expansion adds a vector. There are at most $N$ vectors, so the sequence stabilizes. Its stable set $C$ contains exactly the behavior vectors of finite voting trees: every such tree has finite height, and stability means closure under all further products.

Define
$$ G_q=\{a\in[m]^M:d_T^+(a(T))\ge q\text{ for every }T\in\mathcal T_m\}. $$
Accept precisely when
$$ C\cap G_q\ne\varnothing. $$
An accepted vector is realized by one tree satisfying all tournament conditions simultaneously, so this respects the required quantifier order.

Decision time

A vector uses $O(M\log m)$ bits. At most $N$ closure rounds are needed, each examining at most $N^2$ products. Duplicate detection by scanning at most $N$ stored vectors suffices. Products, vector comparisons, tournament enumeration, and degree checks all take time polynomial in $M,m,L$.

Consequently, decision takes
$$ 2^{2^{O(m^2)}}\operatorname{poly}(L). $$
The explicit candidate list implies $m\le L$, so this also proves membership in $\mathrm{2EXPTIME}$.

4. Minimum-size witness construction

The closure algorithm can be strengthened to optimize witness size exactly.

A height bound for minimum-leaf trees

Let
$$ R=|C|\le N. $$

Lemma 4. For every $a\in C$, a tree realizing $a$ with the minimum number of leaves has height at most $R-1$.

Proof. Choose a minimum-leaf realization. Suppose an ancestor node $u$ and a proper descendant $v$ have identical subtree behavior vectors.

Replace the subtree at $u$ by the subtree at $v$. Their outputs agree in every tournament. Induction along the ancestors of $u$ shows that the entire tree’s behavior is unchanged.

The replacement strictly reduces the leaf count: a proper descendant subtree omits at least one sibling subtree, and every sibling subtree contains a leaf. This contradicts minimality.

Thus the subtree behavior vectors along every root-to-leaf path are distinct. They all lie in $C$, so there are at most $R$ nodes on that path, proving the height bound. $\square$

Dynamic programming

For $a\in C$, let $w_t(a)$ be the minimum leaf count of a realization of height at most $t$, with value $\infty$ when no such realization exists.

Initialize
$$ w_0(c_i)=1, \qquad w_0(a)=\infty\quad\text{for nonconstant }a. $$
Use
$$ w_{t+1}(a)= \min\left( w_t(a), \min_{\substack{b,c\in C\\b*c=a}} \bigl(w_t(b)+w_t(c)\bigr) \right). $$
The root decomposition of a full binary tree proves this recurrence in both directions.

By Lemma 4, after $R-1$ rounds,
$$ w(a):=w_{R-1}(a) $$
is the unrestricted minimum leaf count for every $a\in C$.

If $C\cap G_q$ is empty, reject. Otherwise choose an $a$ minimizing $w(a)$ over $C\cap G_q$. This yields the minimum leaf count among all witnessing trees.

For reconstruction, a vector of weight $1$ is represented by a leaf. For any other vector $a$, choose $b,c$ with
$$ b*c=a,\qquad w(a)=w(b)+w(c). $$
Such a pair exists by decomposing a minimum realization at its root: each child must itself be a minimum realization, since replacing a child by a smaller realization would reduce the parent’s leaf count.

Each child has strictly smaller weight. Hence the selected pointers form an acyclic derivation graph, and unfolding it produces a finite minimum-leaf witness.

Encoding and time bounds

Use prefix encoding: an internal node is a single $0$ bit, and a leaf is a $1$ bit followed by its label in
$$ b=\lceil\log_2m\rceil $$
bits. A full binary tree with $\ell$ leaves has $\ell-1$ internal nodes, so its output length is exactly
$$ S=\ell(b+2)-1. $$
Thus minimizing leaves also minimizes bit length under this encoding.

During the dynamic program, finite weights have at most $R+1$ bits, because height at most $R-1$ permits at most $2^{R-1}$ leaves. The closure, product table, dynamic programming, and reconstruction-pointer selection therefore all take time polynomial in $N,M,m,L$. Their combined time remains
$$ 2^{2^{O(m^2)}}\operatorname{poly}(L). $$

Retain only derivation records reachable from the chosen root and reindex them. Their number $D$ is at most the number of nodes in the unfolded tree, since each reachable record occurs there. The compact table has length
$$ O(D(\log(D+2)+b)). $$
A depth-first traversal can emit the explicit prefix string. Even using sequential table scans for every visited node gives
$$ O\!\left(S^2\log(S+2)\right) $$
additional bit operations.

Therefore a minimum-size witness can be constructed in
$$ 2^{2^{O(m^2)}}\operatorname{poly}(L) + O\!\left(S^2\log(S+2)\right). $$

Finally, Lemma 4 gives the coarse bound
$$ S\le(b+2)2^{R-1}-1 \le 2^{2^{2^{O(m^2)}}}. $$
This is an upper bound, not a witness-size lower bound.

5. Parameterized consequences

Decision is fixed-parameter tractable in the threshold

For $q=0$, accept immediately. For $q\ge1$, compare $m$ with $a_q$.

If $m\ge a_q$, Section 2 proves that the answer is yes. Otherwise,
$$ m<a_q=O((q+1)^2). $$
Applying the finite decision algorithm gives time
$$ 2^{2^{O((q+1)^4)}}\operatorname{poly}(L). $$
This establishes fixed-parameter tractability with parameter $q$. It does not establish polynomial time when $q$ varies with the input.

Minimum-size search can also be restricted by $q$

Lemma 5. Suppose a tree uses exactly the label set $U\subseteq[m]$. It guarantees $q$ on $[m]$ if and only if it guarantees $q$ on tournaments on $U$.

Proof. In one direction, restrict any tournament on $[m]$ to $U$. Evaluation is unchanged, and the winner’s full out-degree is at least its out-degree within $U$.

Conversely, extend any tournament on $U$ by making every omitted candidate defeat every member of $U$. The tree still returns a member of $U$, and that member has no victories outside $U$. Its full out-degree therefore equals its out-degree within $U$. A universal guarantee on $[m]$ implies the guarantee on $U$. $\square$

For $q\ge1$, the construction defining $B_q$ uses all $a_q$ labels, so $B_q\ge a_q$.

If $m>B_q$, a witness with $B_q$ leaves exists. Hence a minimum-leaf witness uses at most $B_q$ distinct labels. Lemma 5 allows those labels to be renamed into $[B_q]$. Conversely, any witness on $[B_q]$ embeds into $[m]$.

Therefore the minimum leaf count is unchanged when $m>B_q$ is replaced by $B_q$. Exact minimum-size search may use
$$ m'=\min(m,B_q) $$
candidates.

Since $B_q=2^{O(q^2\log(q+2))}$, this gives a second minimum-witness construction bound:
$$ 2^{2^{2^{O(q^2\log(q+2))}}}\operatorname{poly}(L) + O\!\left(S^2\log(S+2)\right). $$
The output labels are then encoded using the original $m$.

6. Why many-one hardness would imply a collapse

Let $A$ be the language of canonical encodings of yes-instances $(m,q)$, including the explicit list $[m]$.

For strings of length at most $h$, we have $m\le h$. There are only $m$ permitted thresholds for each $m$. Consequently,
$$ |A\cap\{0,1\}^{\le h}| \le\sum_{m=2}^{h}m =O(h^2). $$
Thus $A$ is sparse.

Mahaney’s theorem states that a sparse language which is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions forces $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$. Membership of that language in $\mathrm{NP}$ is unnecessary. Both hypotheses apply here. See [Grochow, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.05825).

Ordinary alternative syntactic encodings can be normalized in polynomial time, so their hardness would transfer to $A$. Therefore
$$ \text{universal voting-tree existence is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}. $$

There is also a nonuniform upper bound:
$$ A\in\mathrm{P}/\mathrm{poly}. $$
For input length $h$, advice can contain $K(2),\ldots,K(h)$ in $O(h\log h)$ bits. Parsing $m,q$ and comparing $q$ with the advised $K(m)$ takes polynomial time. This does not compute the advice uniformly and does not prove membership in $\mathrm{P}$.

7. What output-polynomial construction means here

Repetitions create a distinction between construction on promised yes-instances and a total construction algorithm.

Replacing any leaf labeled $i$ by a full binary tree whose leaves are all labeled $i$ preserves the behavior in every tournament. Thus witnesses can be enlarged arbitrarily.

Promised yes-instances

There is an unconditional construction algorithm polynomial in the size of its own returned output, if input existence is promised.

To see this, run the finite witnessing algorithm, buffering its explicit tree and counting its $R$ computation steps. Counter maintenance costs at most $O(R^2)$ time. Replace one leaf by a monochromatic tree with $R^2$ leaves.

The padded output has size $S\ge R^2$. Its generation, including ordinary binary-counter operations, takes $O(S^2)$ time. Consequently the complete computation takes $\operatorname{poly}(L+S)$ time.

This bound is obtained by inflating the output. It provides no efficient construction of small witnesses. Section 4 instead optimizes witness size exactly.

Total construction: an exact equivalence

Define a total constructor to return either an explicit witness or a distinguished “no” response. Set $S=0$ on no-instances.

Proposition 6. A total constructor running in $\operatorname{poly}(L+S)$ time exists if and only if decision belongs to $\mathrm{P}$.

Proof. Suppose such a constructor has time bound $p(L+S)$ for a polynomial $p$. On a no-instance it must finish within $p(L)$ steps. Simulate it for that many steps. Reject if it returns “no”; otherwise accept. Every no-instance is rejected, and a yes-instance can never return “no”. This is a polynomial-time decider.

Conversely, suppose a polynomial-time decider exists. Reject no-instances using that decider. On a yes-instance, run the finite witnessing algorithm and perform the padding construction above. Its time is polynomial in $L+S$. Thus the resulting total constructor is output-polynomial. $\square$

The forward implication requires no padding. The reverse implication expressly uses the permitted repetitions. Neither direction establishes a polynomial algorithm for constructing minimum-size witnesses.

8. Remaining unresolved questions

The proved results give uniform decidability, exact witness-size optimization with large preprocessing, and fixed-parameter tractability in $q$.

They do not determine whether arbitrary-threshold decision belongs to $\mathrm{P}$. The sparsity argument makes standard $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness conditional on $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$, but does not supply a uniform polynomial-time algorithm.

For construction, total output-polynomial complexity is equivalent to this unresolved decision question. Efficient construction of minimum-size witnesses remains a stronger unresolved question.
