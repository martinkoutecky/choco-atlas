The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\), where each type has either a rating \(a_t\in[0,1]\) or is an abstainer, let \(v\in(0,1)\) be the voter mass and \(A\) the total rating mass, with per-capita budget \(B_0=A/v\). Choose feasible mass-level bribes, including possible bribes to abstainers, with per-capita expenditure \(e\le B_0\). Compute a strategy maximizing post-bribery per-capita utility \(A'/v'-e\), where \(A'\) and \(v'\) are the resulting rating and voter masses.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There is one rated service, so \(m=1\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Each type is either a voter with a binary-rational rating \(a_t\in[0,1]\), or an abstainer. Write \(T_V\) and \(T_\ast\) for these two sets, and define \(v=\sum_{t\in T_V}\mu_t\in(0,1)\), \(A=\sum_{t\in T_V}\mu_ta_t\), and the per-capita budget \(B_0=A/v\).
A strategy may split each type into finitely many rational submasses \(q_{tj}\geq0\), with \(\sum_jq_{tj}=\mu_t\), and assign each submass a rational per-person bribe \(b_{tj}\in[0,1]\). Its expenditure is \(e=\sum_{t,j}q_{tj}b_{tj}\leq B_0\); the zero strategy is allowed.
A voter receiving \(b\) remains a voter and acquires rating \(\min\{1,a_t+b\}\). An abstainer receiving \(b=0\) remains an abstainer; receiving \(b>0\) makes it a voter with rating \(b\). Payments exceeding a voter's remaining rating capacity are still charged.
Thus \(v'=v+\sum_{t\in T_\ast,j:b_{tj}>0}q_{tj}\) and \(A'=\sum_{t\in T_V,j}q_{tj}\min\{1,a_t+b_{tj}\}+\sum_{t\in T_\ast,j}q_{tj}b_{tj}\). Post-bribery per-capita utility is \(A'/v'-e\).

Is computing a feasible strategy maximizing \(A'/v'-e\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies with abstainers, optimal objective-rating strategies are characterized, and an accompanying polynomial-time algorithm computes one. [Grandi–Turrini](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/16/Papers/050.pdf)
No result settling this exact divisible-mass problem was identified in the literature search.

  • Normalized total society mass to 1, matching the per-capita formulation.
  • Required initial ratings, as well as masses, to be binary rationals; L includes all input data.
  • Made mass divisible within types and represented strategies by finite rational submass allocations and rational per-person bribes.
  • Used the full interval [0,1] for allowed bribe amounts and retained the capped additive update, including payment for any excess above rating capacity.
  • Specified that only a strictly positive bribe activates an abstainer, and allowed the zero strategy and a zero initial budget.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Computing an optimal feasible strategy is polynomial-time solvable in \(\tau,L\), with \(m=1\). An optimum requires only one submass per type.

The proof below establishes this directly for divisible masses. The related finite-society result appears in Section 3.2 of [Grandi–Turrini](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/16/Papers/050.pdf).

1. Aggregate bounds

Define the original voters’ total remaining rating capacity by \(\displaystyle C=\sum_{t\in T_V}\mu_t(1-a_t)=v-A\), and put \(\displaystyle E=\min\{B_0,C\}=\min\{A/v,v-A\}\).

Consider any feasible strategy, with expenditure \(e\), and let \(x\) denote the abstaining mass it converts into voters. Thus \(v'=v+x\).

For every original voter, \(\min\{1,a_t+b_{tj}\}\leq a_t+b_{tj}\). Each converted abstainer contributes exactly its payment to the rating numerator. Summing these inequalities gives \(A'\leq A+e\).

Furthermore, every post-bribery rating lies in \([0,1]\), so \(A'\leq v'\). Consequently, its utility \(U\) satisfies both
\(\displaystyle U\leq\frac{A+e}{v+x}-e\)
and \(U\leq1-e\).

These bounds apply even when payments exceed voters’ remaining rating capacities.

2. A universal upper bound

We prove that every feasible strategy has utility at most
\(\displaystyle U^\star=\frac{A+E}{v}-E\).

If \(e\leq C\), feasibility also gives \(e\leq B_0\), hence \(e\leq E\). Since \(A+e\geq0\) and \(x\geq0\),
\(\displaystyle U\leq\frac{A+e}{v+x}-e\leq\frac{A+e}{v}-e=\frac Av+\left(\frac1v-1\right)e\).
The coefficient \(\frac1v-1\) is positive because \(0<v<1\). Therefore \(U\leq U^\star\).

If \(e>C\), feasibility implies \(B_0>C\), so \(E=C\). Using \(A'\leq v'\), we obtain
\(\displaystyle U\leq1-e<1-C=\frac{A+C}{v}-C=U^\star\),
where the equality uses \(A+C=v\).

Thus the upper bound holds for every strategy, regardless of its splitting pattern or payments to abstainers.

3. A rational strategy attaining the bound

If \(C=0\), then \(A=v\) and \(E=0\). The zero strategy has utility \(1=U^\star\), and is optimal by the preceding bound.

Suppose \(C>0\). Define the rational number \(\lambda=E/C\), which lies in \([0,1]\). Use one submass \(q_t=\mu_t\) for each type, and assign
\(\displaystyle b_t=\lambda(1-a_t)\) to each voter and \(b_t=0\) to each abstainer.

These bribes are rational and lie in \([0,1]\). Since \(b_t\leq1-a_t\), no payment is wasted through saturation. The expenditure is
\(\displaystyle e=\sum_{t\in T_V}\mu_t\lambda(1-a_t)=\lambda C=E\leq B_0\).

No abstainer is converted, so \(v'=v\). The rating numerator becomes
\(\displaystyle A'=\sum_{t\in T_V}\mu_t\bigl(a_t+\lambda(1-a_t)\bigr)=A+E\).

Hence the strategy attains \(\displaystyle A'/v'-e=(A+E)/v-E=U^\star\). This proves existence and optimality, including when \(A=0\), in which case the construction gives the zero strategy.

4. Exact polynomial-time computation

Compute \(v,A,C,B_0,E\) by rational arithmetic. If \(C>0\), compute \(\lambda=E/C\) and output the bribes above; otherwise output the zero strategy.

To verify the bit complexity, let \(D\) be the product of all denominators appearing in the input masses and ratings. Its binary length is at most a constant multiple of \(L\). Each denominator of a mass or a product \(\mu_ta_t\) divides \(D\). Therefore \(v\) and \(A\) can be represented over \(D\) with numerators of \(O(L)\) bits: both quantities belong to \([0,1]\).

Subtraction, comparison, and the constant number of multiplications and divisions defining \(C,B_0,E,\lambda\) preserve polynomial bit length. Each output bribe also has polynomial bit length. The algorithm uses \(O(\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations on polynomial-length integers and outputs exactly \(\tau\) submasses. Standard exact integer arithmetic therefore gives polynomial running time in \(\tau,L\).
