The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses of buyer types \(t\) and seller types \(s\), with complete bundle-value functions \(v_t\) and bundle-cost functions \(c_s\) on \(X=\{0,1\}^m\), find fractions \(q_{t,x}\) and \(r_{s,x}\) of each type assigned integral bundles, together with a price function \(p\). Require each type's fractions to sum to one, aggregate item demand to equal supply, and every bundle assigned positive mass to be within \(\varepsilon\) of that type's utility or profit maximum at \(p\); maximize welfare to within \(\varepsilon\) of the optimum. The permitted price class includes item-linear tariffs.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\geq1\) and \(X=\{0,1\}^m\). A society is given by its support: nonempty lists \(T,S\) of buyer and seller types with positive binary-rational masses \(a_t,b_s\), respectively, each side totaling \(1\). The support size is \(\tau=|T|+|S|\), not the number of possible types.
Each type has a complete explicit table \(v_t:X\to\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\) or \(c_s:X\to\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\), monotone under bundle inclusion and zero at the empty bundle. Input also includes binary-rational \(\varepsilon>0\); \(L\) is the total input bit length, including these tables.
An allocation consists of rational fractions \(q_{t,x},r_{s,x}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_xq_{t,x}=\sum_xr_{s,x}=1\) for every respective type and \(\sum_{t,x}a_tq_{t,x}x=\sum_{s,x}b_sr_{s,x}x\).
Prices are a common unrestricted rational table \(p:X\to\mathbb Q\), including item-linear tariffs. Positive fractions must satisfy \(v_t(x)-p(x)\geq\max_{z\in X}(v_t(z)-p(z))-\varepsilon\) and, respectively, \(p(x)-c_s(x)\geq\max_{z\in X}(p(z)-c_s(z))-\varepsilon\); any splitting among qualifying bundles is permitted.
Define \(W(q,r)=\sum_{t,x}a_tq_{t,x}v_t(x)-\sum_{s,x}b_sr_{s,x}c_s(x)\), and let \(W^*\) be its maximum over all allocations satisfying the normalization and item-balance conditions. Find an allocation and prices satisfying the preceding conditions and \(W(q,r)\geq W^*-\varepsilon\).

Is this computational problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For one discrete buyer and seller, a kernel-based elicitation procedure terminates after finitely many rounds; successful termination gives a bundle whose welfare loss is at most the sum of its utility and profit errors. This is a finite-termination guarantee, not a polynomial-time bound. [Source](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/cs15-892F11/kernelMethodForMarketClearing.ijcai09.pdf)
No result settling this exact explicitly tabulated mass-allocation problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted complete bundle functions as explicit binary-rational tables and included their full encoding in L.
  • Restored nonnegative, monotone values and costs, normalized to zero at the empty bundle.
  • Normalized buyer and seller masses separately to one, matching the representative buyer and seller normalization.
  • Resolved the unspecified price class as unrestricted common rational bundle prices, consistent with explicit bundle pricing; imposed no sign restriction.
  • Defined the welfare benchmark over all normalized, item-balanced fractional allocations, independently of prices.
  • Took epsilon to be a positive binary rational, with additive errors, and allowed arbitrary splitting among qualifying bundles.

Conclusion

The problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, one can compute a rational allocation with welfare exactly \(W^*\), together with common rational item-linear prices under which every positively allocated bundle is an exact utility or profit maximizer.

The proof uses the stated permission to split each type’s mass among bundles and the stated item-balance constraints.

1. The welfare linear program

Write \(n=|X|=2^m\). Introduce allocated-mass variables
\(y_{t,x}=a_tq_{t,x}\) and \(z_{s,x}=b_sr_{s,x}\).

Because all masses are positive, this change of variables is invertible:
\(q_{t,x}=y_{t,x}/a_t\) and \(r_{s,x}=z_{s,x}/b_s\).

Consider the linear program with objective

\( \operatorname{maximize}\quad \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{x\in X}v_t(x)y_{t,x} -\sum_{s\in S}\sum_{x\in X}c_s(x)z_{s,x}, \)

subject to

  • \(\sum_{x\in X}y_{t,x}=a_t\) for every \(t\in T\);
  • \(\sum_{x\in X}z_{s,x}=b_s\) for every \(s\in S\);
  • \(\sum_{t,x}y_{t,x}x_j-\sum_{s,x}z_{s,x}x_j=0\) for every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\);
  • \(y_{t,x},z_{s,x}\geq0\) for every applicable index.

Call this program \((P)\). Its constraints are precisely the allocation constraints after the change of variables, and its objective is precisely welfare.

The feasible set is nonempty: assign all mass to the empty bundle. It is bounded, since normalization and nonnegativity imply
\(0\leq y_{t,x}\leq a_t\) and \(0\leq z_{s,x}\leq b_s\).
It is also closed. Thus the objective attains a finite maximum.

All coefficients are rational. A linear objective on a nonempty bounded rational polytope has a rational optimal vertex: an optimal face contains a vertex, and a vertex is determined by a full-rank system of active rational constraints. Consequently, allowing real variables in \((P)\) does not increase the maximum over rational allocations. Its optimal value is exactly the \(W^*\) in the question.

2. The dual supplies common prices

Associate unrestricted dual variables \(\alpha_t\), \(\beta_s\), and \(\pi_j\) with the buyer normalizations, seller normalizations, and item balances, respectively. Write
\(\pi\cdot x=\sum_{j=1}^m\pi_jx_j\).

The dual program \((D)\) is

\( \operatorname{minimize}\quad \sum_{t\in T}a_t\alpha_t+\sum_{s\in S}b_s\beta_s, \)

subject to

\( \alpha_t+\pi\cdot x\geq v_t(x) \quad(t\in T,\ x\in X), \)

and

\( \beta_s-\pi\cdot x\geq-c_s(x) \quad(s\in S,\ x\in X). \)

The signs follow directly from the item-balance constraints: a buyer variable has balance column \(x\), whereas a seller variable has balance column \(-x\).

The dual is feasible. Take \(\pi=0\),
\(\alpha_t=\max_{x\in X}v_t(x)\), and \(\beta_s=0\); the seller constraints then hold because \(c_s(x)\geq0\).

By linear-programming strong duality, since \((P)\) is feasible with finite optimum, \((D)\) has an attained optimum equal to \(W^*\). Both programs have rational optimal solutions.

Choose any optimal primal and dual solutions. Define the common tariff by

\( p(x)=\pi\cdot x \qquad(x\in X). \)

This is a permitted rational price table.

3. Every used bundle is an exact best response

For completeness, the complementary-slackness argument can be checked directly.

Define the nonnegative dual slacks

\( d_{t,x}=\alpha_t+\pi\cdot x-v_t(x), \qquad e_{s,x}=\beta_s-\pi\cdot x+c_s(x). \)

For any primal-feasible and dual-feasible solutions, normalization and item balance give

\( \sum_ta_t\alpha_t+\sum_sb_s\beta_s-W(y,z) = \sum_{t,x}y_{t,x}d_{t,x} +\sum_{s,x}z_{s,x}e_{s,x}. \)

Indeed, expanding the right side produces the dual objective minus welfare, together with
\(\pi\cdot(\sum_{t,x}y_{t,x}x-\sum_{s,x}z_{s,x}x)\), which vanishes by item balance.

At optimality the left side is zero. Every summand on the right is nonnegative, so

\( y_{t,x}>0\Longrightarrow d_{t,x}=0, \qquad z_{s,x}>0\Longrightarrow e_{s,x}=0. \)

For a buyer, dual feasibility says

\( v_t(u)-p(u)\leq\alpha_t \qquad\text{for every }u\in X. \)

If \(q_{t,x}>0\), positivity of \(a_t\) implies \(y_{t,x}>0\), and the zero-slack condition gives
\(v_t(x)-p(x)=\alpha_t\). Therefore

\( v_t(x)-p(x)=\max_{u\in X}\bigl(v_t(u)-p(u)\bigr). \)

Similarly, seller dual feasibility says

\( p(u)-c_s(u)\leq\beta_s \qquad\text{for every }u\in X. \)

If \(r_{s,x}>0\), then \(z_{s,x}>0\), so
\(p(x)-c_s(x)=\beta_s\). Hence

\( p(x)-c_s(x)=\max_{u\in X}\bigl(p(u)-c_s(u)\bigr). \)

Thus all positive fractions satisfy the requested inequalities with zero error. The allocation also has welfare \(W^*\), and therefore satisfies the welfare requirement for every supplied \(\varepsilon>0\).

4. Polynomial running time and output length

The relevant size is the explicit input length.

There are \(\tau n\) table entries. Each explicitly encoded rational entry occupies at least one bit, so \(\tau n=O(L)\). Enumerating all bundles therefore takes polynomial time in the stipulated input size.

Program \((P)\) has \(\tau n\) variables and \(\tau+m\) equality constraints, besides nonnegativity. Its coefficients are table entries, masses, and elements of \(\{0,1,-1\}\). Its full binary encoding has length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The same holds for \((D)\).

The exact rational linear-programming theorem states that a rational LP can be solved, with infeasibility or unboundedness detected when applicable, in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. In the feasible finite-optimum case, it returns a rational optimal solution. See [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239). The hypotheses hold here, as verified above.

Solve both programs exactly, divide \(y_{t,x}\) by \(a_t\) and \(z_{s,x}\) by \(b_s\), and output \(p(x)=\pi\cdot x\) for every bundle. The LP solutions have polynomial encoding length; rational division and the sums defining prices preserve polynomial bit length. Thus construction of the entire allocation and price table also takes polynomial time.

The kernel procedure’s finite-termination bound is unnecessary for this model: explicit tables permit polynomial-size LPs, and the allowed fractional allocations make LP duality sufficient.
