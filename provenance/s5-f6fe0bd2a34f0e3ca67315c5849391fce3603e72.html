The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many types \(j\in[\tau]\) with rational masses \(\mu_j>0\), \(\sum_j\mu_j=1\), per-capita entitlements \(e_j>0\) with \(\sum_j\mu_je_j=1\), known bounded loads \(w_{jt}\), thresholds \(\alpha_j\), and unknown nondecreasing payoff functions \(f_j\) satisfying the paper's Lipschitz and positive near-threshold-gradient assumptions, each type has unit demand \(\eta_j^\star=f_j^{-1}(\alpha_j)\). In each round, a monitor chooses per-capita allocations \(x_{jt}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_j\mu_jx_{jt}\le 1\), then receives one type-level feedback observation with conditional mean \(f_j(x_{jt}/w_{jt})\) and the paper's sub-Gaussian noise bounds. Does there exist an online policy, using weighted MMF allocations, with high-probability sublinear population-weighted inefficiency \(L_T^\mu=\sum_{t=1}^T\min\{1-\sum_j\mu_jx_{jt}+\sum_j\mu_j(x_{jt}-w_{jt}\eta_j^\star)_+,\sum_j\mu_j(w_{jt}\eta_j^\star-x_{jt})_+\}\), sublinear per-type fairness gaps \(\sum_{t=1}^T[u_j(e_j/w_{jt})-u_j(x_{jt}/w_{jt})]\), and a per-round computation bound in \(\tau\), numeric input bit lengths, and maintained confidence-tree sizes?

Conclusion

A policy with polynomial per-round bit complexity exists. In fact, one can obtain, simultaneously with probability at least \(1-\delta\), \(L_T^\mu=o(T)\) and \(F_{jT}=0\) for every type and every horizon.

Under the stated normalization, there is an immediate solution: set \(\widehat\eta_{jt}=\eta_{\max}\). Then \(\sum_j\mu_jw_{jt}\eta_{\max}\le1\), so weighted MMF satisfies every true demand and both losses are identically zero.

To address the substantive learning question, the remainder proves the stronger result obtained by dropping the assumption \(\overline w\eta_{\max}\le1\). Congestion is then possible. All other hypotheses remain in force. The proof is self-contained and does not require transferring the algorithm or arithmetic assumptions of the [cited individual-agent paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.08648).

1. Two properties of optimistic weighted MMF

Write \(d_{jt}=w_{jt}\eta_j^\star\). Suppose the recommendations satisfy \(q_{jt}\ge d_{jt}\) for every type.

First, weighted MMF guarantees
\(x_{jt}\ge\min\{e_j,d_{jt}\}\).

Indeed, if \(\sum_j\mu_jq_{jt}\le1\), then \(x_{jt}=q_{jt}\ge d_{jt}\). Otherwise, define \(g(\lambda)=\sum_j\mu_j\min\{q_{jt},\lambda e_j\}\). Since \(g(1)\le\sum_j\mu_je_j=1\), the solution of \(g(\lambda)=1\) has \(\lambda\ge1\). Consequently,
\(x_{jt}=\min\{q_{jt},\lambda e_j\}\ge\min\{d_{jt},e_j\}\).

After division by \(w_{jt}>0\), monotonicity and saturation of utility give
\(u_j(x_{jt}/w_{jt})\ge u_j(e_j/w_{jt})\).
Thus optimistic recommendations imply \(F_{jT}=0\) for every horizon.

Second, let \(\ell_t\) denote the summand defining \(L_T^\mu\). If MMF allocates all recommended demands, every true demand is satisfied, so \(\ell_t=0\). Otherwise it uses the entire resource, and therefore
\(\ell_t\le\sum_j\mu_j(x_{jt}-d_{jt})_+\).

It consequently suffices to maintain valid upper bounds on the thresholds and prove that actual overallocation vanishes.

2. A rational confidence-tree policy

For each type, initialize a rational upper bound \(U_j=\eta_{\max}\). At the beginning of round \(t\), recommend \(\widehat\eta_{jt}=U_j\) and apply weighted MMF to \(q_{jt}=w_{jt}U_j\).

The observed normalized allocation is \(z_{jt}=x_{jt}/w_{jt}\). MMF never exceeds a recommended demand, so
\(0\le z_{jt}\le U_j\le\eta_{\max}\).
All payoff evaluations therefore lie in the interval where the assumed Lipschitz bound holds.

Dyadic intervals and statistics

At depth \(h\ge0\), partition \([0,\eta_{\max}]\) into \(2^h\) intervals of width \(\eta_{\max}2^{-h}\). Use intervals closed on the left and open on the right, except that the last interval includes \(\eta_{\max}\).

Each type maintains nodes only for intervals that receive observations. A node at depth \(h\) receives observations beginning at round \(2^h\): after round \(t\), update the node containing \(z_{jt}\) at every depth \(h\le\lfloor\log_2t\rfloor\).

For a node with \(n\ge1\) observations, maintain the rational statistics
\(S=\sum_{s\text{ assigned to the node}}1/\sigma_{js}\) and
\(Q=\sum_{s\text{ assigned to the node}}Y_{js}/\sigma_{js}\).

Define the integer
\(c_{h,n}=\max\{2,\lceil\log_2(16\tau\,2^h(h+1)^2n^2/\delta)\rceil\}\),
and the rational radius
\(r_{h,n}=c_{h,n}\lceil\sqrt n\rceil/S\).

If the node’s interval has left endpoint \(a\), compute
\(B=Q/S-r_{h,n}-K2^{-h}\).

Whenever \(B>\alpha_j\), update
\(U_j\leftarrow\min\{U_j,\max\{0,a-(\eta_{\max}/K)(B-\alpha_j)\}\}\).

These updates occur after receiving feedback and affect subsequent allocations. No exploration schedule, utility observations, or knowledge of \(G,\epsilon\) is required.

3. Simultaneous validity of the confidence bounds

We prove that all confidence bounds used by the policy are valid on a single event of probability at least \(1-\delta\).

Fix a type and a particular node. Whether round \(s\) is assigned to this node is determined before observing \(Y_{js}\): the allocation, the interval membership, and the depth-activation condition are already known.

For an assigned observation, define
\(\xi_s=(Y_{js}-f_j(z_{js}))/\sigma_{js}\).
The conditional sub-Gaussian assumption gives, for every real \(\theta\),
\(\mathbb E[\exp(\theta\xi_s)\mid\text{history and allocation}]\le\exp(\theta^2/2)\).

Let \(A_n\) be the sum of these standardized noises through the node’s \(n\)-th observation. Although that observation occurs at a random time, the exponential process with compensator one-half \(\theta^2\) times the number of assigned observations is a nonnegative supermartingale. Stopping at the \(n\)-th assigned observation, or at a finite horizon if it has not occurred, and then applying Fatou’s lemma gives
\(\Pr(\text{the $n\)-th observation occurs and }|A_n|>c_{h,n}\sqrt n)\le2\exp(-c_{h,n}^2/2)\).

This argument permits adaptive interval membership and does not assume independence across types.

For integers \(c\ge2\), \(\exp(-c^2/2)\le2^{-c}\). Hence the preceding failure probability is at most
\(\delta/[8\tau\,2^h(h+1)^2n^2]\).

There are \(\tau2^h\) type-node pairs at depth \(h\). A union bound over every depth and every positive sample count therefore bounds the total failure probability by
\((\delta/8)(\sum_{h\ge0}(h+1)^{-2})(\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2})\le\delta/2\le\delta\),
where each series is at most \(2\).

Let \(\mathcal E\) denote the resulting simultaneous confidence event.

For a node, define its weighted mean payoff
\(\bar f=(\sum_s f_j(z_{js})/\sigma_{js})/S\).
On \(\mathcal E\),
\(|Q/S-\bar f|\le r_{h,n}\).

Every assigned point lies within \(\eta_{\max}2^{-h}\) of the interval’s left endpoint \(a\). The known Lipschitz bound implies
\(f_j(z_{js})\le f_j(a)+K2^{-h}\).
Taking the weighted average yields \(f_j(a)\ge\bar f-K2^{-h}\), and consequently
\(B\le f_j(a)\).

Upper-bound updates are valid

Suppose \(B>\alpha_j\). Then \(f_j(a)>\alpha_j\), so monotonicity implies \(a>\eta_j^\star\). Applying the Lipschitz bound between \(a\) and the threshold gives
\(B-\alpha_j\le f_j(a)-f_j(\eta_j^\star)\le(K/\eta_{\max})(a-\eta_j^\star)\).

Rearranging,
\(\eta_j^\star\le a-(\eta_{\max}/K)(B-\alpha_j)\).

Thus every update preserves \(U_j\ge\eta_j^\star\) on \(\mathcal E\). Since this holds initially, it holds in every round. Section 1 now proves
\(F_{jT}=0\) for every type and every horizon on \(\mathcal E\).

4. Persistent overallocation is impossible

Fix a type \(j\), and work throughout on \(\mathcal E\). We prove
\((z_{jt}-\eta_j^\star)_+\longrightarrow0\).

Fix any \(\varepsilon>0\) with \(\eta_j^\star+\varepsilon\le\eta_{\max}\). Uniqueness of the threshold solution and monotonicity imply
\(\gamma=f_j(\eta_j^\star+\varepsilon/2)-\alpha_j>0\).

Choose a fixed depth \(h\) sufficiently large that
\(\eta_{\max}2^{-h}<\varepsilon/2\) and \(K2^{-h}<\gamma/4\).

Any interval at this depth containing a point \(z\ge\eta_j^\star+\varepsilon\) has left endpoint \(a>\eta_j^\star+\varepsilon/2\). Every observation assigned to such an interval therefore has payoff mean at least \(\alpha_j+\gamma\).

Suppose one such node receives infinitely many observations. For its sample count \(n\),
\(S\ge n/\overline\sigma\), so
\(r_{h,n}\le\overline\sigma c_{h,n}\lceil\sqrt n\rceil/n\longrightarrow0\).
Here \(h\) is fixed and \(c_{h,n}\) grows logarithmically in \(n\).

On \(\mathcal E\), its lower bound consequently satisfies
\(B\ge\bar f-2r_{h,n}-K2^{-h}\ge\alpha_j+\gamma-2r_{h,n}-K2^{-h}>\alpha_j\)
once \(n\) is sufficiently large.

The resulting update sets
\(U_j\le a-(\eta_{\max}/K)(B-\alpha_j)<a\).

Upper bounds never increase. Every subsequent normalized allocation satisfies \(z_{jt}\le U_j<a\), so this node can receive no further observations. This contradicts the assumption that it receives infinitely many.

There are only finitely many intervals at the chosen depth. Each interval that can contain \(z_{jt}\ge\eta_j^\star+\varepsilon\) therefore receives finitely many observations. The finitely many rounds before depth \(h\) becomes active cause no difficulty. Thus
\(z_{jt}<\eta_j^\star+\varepsilon\) eventually.

Values of \(\varepsilon\) larger than \(\eta_{\max}-\eta_j^\star\) are automatic. Since the argument applies to every positive \(\varepsilon\), the claimed convergence follows, including thresholds at either endpoint.

Using \(w_{jt}\le\overline w\),
\((x_{jt}-d_{jt})_+=w_{jt}(z_{jt}-\eta_j^\star)_+\longrightarrow0\).

The support is finite, so Section 1 gives \(\ell_t\longrightarrow0\). Taking Cesàro averages proves
\(L_T^\mu/T=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t\longrightarrow0\).

Both guarantees hold on the same event \(\mathcal E\), whose probability is at least \(1-\delta\).

5. Per-round bit complexity

All stored quantities and allocations are rational.

For weighted MMF, sort the rational breakpoints \(q_{jt}/e_j\). Between consecutive breakpoints, its capacity equation has the form
\(\sum_{j\in S}\mu_jq_{jt}+\lambda\sum_{j\in A}\mu_je_j=1\),
where \(S\) contains saturated types and \(A\) contains unsaturated types. In the congested case, the interval containing the solution has a positive denominator, and
\(\lambda=(1-\sum_{j\in S}\mu_jq_{jt})/(\sum_{j\in A}\mu_je_j)\).
Sorting, locating this interval, and computing allocations require polynomially many rational operations with polynomial intermediate bit lengths.

Each type updates at most \(1+\lfloor\log_2t\rfloor\) confidence nodes per round. The remaining operations are rational arithmetic and comparisons, integer interval-index calculations, and:

  • Computing \(\lceil\sqrt n\rceil\) by integer square root.
  • Computing \(c_{h,n}\) by comparisons with powers of two.
  • Creating or locating nodes in the maintained confidence trees.

Neither operation requires evaluating an irrational square root or logarithm. Moreover, \(n\le t\) and \(h\le\log_2t\), so
\(c_{h,n}=O(\log\tau+\log t+\log(1/\delta))\).

Exact rational sums can accumulate long encodings, but their lengths are included in the stipulated maintained-state parameter \(L\). The number of operations and their operand lengths are polynomial in \(\tau,L,\log t,\log(1/\delta),N_t\). Since \(m=1\), this is the requested bound.

The policy works directly with support types and masses; it never expands a mass into individual agents. The proof establishes sublinear loss, without asserting the sharper rates of the cited individual-agent algorithm.
