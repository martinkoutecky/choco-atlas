The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given sample access to a finitely supported rational distribution \(\mu=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\delta_{a_t}\) over locations \(a_t\in\mathbb{Q}^d\), can a randomized algorithm using \(q=O(1/\epsilon^2)\) independent draws, with no dependence on \(d\) or \(\tau\), return \(x\in\mathbb{R}^d\) such that \(\mathbb{E}[\sum_t\mu_t\|a_t-x\|_1]\leq(1+\epsilon)\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^d}\sum_t\mu_t\|a_t-y\|_1\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is represented by its support: distinct locations \(a_1,\ldots,a_\tau\in\mathbb{Q}^d\) with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; set \(m=d\), and let \(L\) be the total binary encoding length, including the rational accuracy parameter \(0<\epsilon<1\).
The algorithm receives \(d\) and \(\epsilon\), but accesses the society only through an oracle returning independent locations distributed according to \(\mu\); each draw reveals all coordinates, but neither its mass nor the support list. Count each oracle call as one draw and account separately for bit computation.
One facility may be placed anywhere in \(\mathbb{R}^d\). Define \(F_\mu(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\lVert a_t-x\rVert_1\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu=\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^d}F_\mu(y)\). Include societies with \(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu=0\); expectation is over both samples and internal randomness.

Does there exist a randomized algorithm, with bit running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\), using at most \(\lceil C/\epsilon^2\rceil\) independent draws for a universal constant \(C\), that returns \(x\in\mathbb{R}^d\) satisfying \(\mathbb{E}[F_\mu(x)]\leq(1+\epsilon)\operatorname{OPT}_\mu\) for every such society, or is this sample bound impossible?

For finite equally weighted populations, the coordinatewise sample median achieves expected approximation ratio \(1+\epsilon\) using \(\Theta(1/\epsilon^2)\) sampled agents, independently of \(d\), asymptotically as the population size tends to infinity. [Discrete sampling guarantee](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00224-026-10264-z).
The literature search located no verified result explicitly establishing or excluding the exact sample-only distributional guarantee stated here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Reconciled the required support representation with sample access: the support list specifies the instance but is withheld from the algorithm, which observes complete sampled locations without masses.
  • Normalized the positive masses to sum to one, as required for a probability distribution.
  • Restricted the accuracy parameter to binary rational epsilon strictly between zero and one and included its encoding in L.
  • Used m=d for the required running-time convention and required polynomial bit computation separately from the dimension-independent draw bound.
  • Made the draw-bound constant universal and the guarantee uniform over all finite supports, without a population-size limit.
  • Retained zero-optimum societies and specified that expectation includes sampling and internal randomness.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Such an algorithm exists. The coordinatewise sample median satisfies the required guarantee with the universal constant \(C=5\).

The [cited paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00224-026-10264-z) states an asymptotic guarantee for finite populations. The proof below directly establishes a finite-sample guarantee for every distribution in the question.

Algorithm

Set \(k=\lceil \epsilon^{-2}\rceil\) and \(n=2k+1\). Draw independent locations \(A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)}\) from the oracle. For each coordinate \(j\), return the ordinary median of \(A_j^{(1)},\ldots,A_j^{(n)}\).

This uses only revealed coordinates. Since \(0<\epsilon<1\),

\( \displaystyle n=2\lceil\epsilon^{-2}\rceil+1\leq 2\epsilon^{-2}+3<5\epsilon^{-2}\leq\lceil5\epsilon^{-2}\rceil. \)

We prove the following stronger statement: for every integer \(k\geq1\), the coordinatewise median \(X\) of \(2k+1\) independent draws satisfies

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E[F_\mu(X)]\leq\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{2}{ek}}\right)\operatorname{OPT}_\mu. \)

A binomial majority bound

Fix \(0<p<1/2\). Let \(B\) have the binomial distribution with parameters \(2k+1,p\), and define

\( \displaystyle q_k(p)=\Pr(B\geq k+1),\qquad \delta=\frac12-p. \)

We claim that

\( \displaystyle (1-2p)q_k(p)\leq p\sqrt{\frac{2}{ek}}. \)

Write \(B=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2k+1}I_\ell\), where the \(I_\ell\) are independent Bernoulli variables with success probability \(p\). If \(T\) has the binomial distribution with parameters \(2k,p\), independence gives

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E[B\mathbf1_{\{B\geq k+1\}}]=(2k+1)p\,\Pr(T\geq k). \)

Indeed, conditional on \(I_\ell=1\), the event \(B\geq k+1\) means that the remaining \(2k\) variables contain at least \(k\) successes. Since \(B\geq k+1\) on the indicated event,

\( \displaystyle q_k(p)\leq\frac{2k+1}{k+1}\,p\,\Pr(T\geq k)\leq2p\,\Pr(T\geq k). \)

To bound the last probability, take \(\lambda=\log((1-p)/p)>0\). Markov’s inequality and independence yield

\( \displaystyle \Pr(T\geq k)\leq e^{-\lambda k}\mathbb E[e^{\lambda T}]=e^{-\lambda k}(1-p+pe^\lambda)^{2k}=[4p(1-p)]^k. \)

Because \(4p(1-p)=1-4\delta^2\) and \(1-u\leq e^{-u}\),

\( \displaystyle q_k(p)\leq2p\,e^{-4k\delta^2}. \)

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \frac{(1-2p)q_k(p)}p\leq4\delta e^{-4k\delta^2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{ek}}. \)

For the final inequality, the derivative of \(4\delta e^{-4k\delta^2}\) is \(4e^{-4k\delta^2}(1-8k\delta^2)\). Its maximum over \(\delta\geq0\) occurs at \(\delta=1/\sqrt{8k}\) and equals \(\sqrt{2/(ek)}\). This proves the claim.

The factor \(p\) is essential: it allows the bound to control relative cost even when one side of a gap has arbitrarily small mass.

One-dimensional expected cost

Consider any finitely supported real random variable \(Z\), with distinct support values \(b_1<\cdots<b_r\) and positive masses \(w_1,\ldots,w_r\). Define

\( \displaystyle f(y)=\mathbb E[|Z-y|],\qquad p_i=\sum_{t=1}^{i}w_t,\qquad \Delta_i=b_{i+1}-b_i. \)

If \(r=1\), every sample median equals \(b_1\), and its cost is zero. Assume henceforth that \(r\geq2\).

For a support value \(b_\ell\), decomposing distances into gaps gives

\( \displaystyle f(b_\ell)=\sum_{i<\ell}\Delta_i p_i+\sum_{i\geq\ell}\Delta_i(1-p_i). \)

Thus each gap contributes \(\Delta_i p_i\) when the facility is to its right, and \(\Delta_i(1-p_i)\) when the facility is to its left.

Let \(\ell_*\) be the smallest index for which the cumulative mass is at least \(1/2\). Then \(p_i<1/2\) for \(i<\ell_*\), and \(p_i\geq1/2\) for \(i\geq\ell_*\). Hence

\( \displaystyle \min_{y\in\mathbb R}f(y)=f(b_{\ell_*})=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\Delta_i\min\{p_i,1-p_i\}. \)

To justify the minimization over all real locations, \(f\) is affine between consecutive support values, so an interior point cannot improve on both endpoints. Outside the support interval, moving toward the nearest endpoint strictly decreases every distance. Therefore a minimum is attained at a support value.

Now let \(M\) be the median of \(2k+1\) independent copies of \(Z\). Fix a gap \(i\).

If \(p_i<1/2\), the optimal contribution is \(\Delta_i p_i\). The sample median lies on the more costly left side precisely when at least \(k+1\) draws fall among \(b_1,\ldots,b_i\). Therefore its expected contribution is

\( \displaystyle \Delta_i\bigl[p_i+(1-2p_i)q_k(p_i)\bigr]\leq\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{2}{ek}}\right)\Delta_i p_i. \)

If \(p_i>1/2\), interchange left and right. The minority mass is \(1-p_i<1/2\), giving the same bound with \(\Delta_i(1-p_i)\) as the optimal contribution.

If \(p_i=1/2\), both sides contribute exactly \(\Delta_i/2\), so there is no excess cost.

Summing these bounds over all gaps proves

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E[f(M)]\leq\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{2}{ek}}\right)\min_{y\in\mathbb R}f(y). \)

Dimension and zero optimum

For coordinate \(j\), define \(f_j(y)=\mathbb E[|A_j-y|]\), where \(A\) is one oracle draw. Separability of the \(\ell_1\) norm gives

\( \displaystyle F_\mu(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}f_j(x_j),\qquad \operatorname{OPT}_\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\min_{y\in\mathbb R}f_j(y). \)

The equality for the optimum holds because each coordinate has a minimizing median, and these coordinate choices can be combined into an allowed facility.

For each fixed coordinate, the sampled coordinates are independent draws from its marginal distribution. Applying the one-dimensional result and then linearity of expectation gives

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E[F_\mu(X)]\leq\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{2}{ek}}\right)\operatorname{OPT}_\mu. \)

Independence between coordinates is unnecessary. With \(k=\lceil\epsilon^{-2}\rceil\),

\( \displaystyle \sqrt{\frac{2}{ek}}\leq\sqrt{\frac2e}\,\epsilon<\epsilon. \)

This establishes the requested approximation.

No step divides by \(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu\). If that optimum is zero, positivity of the masses forces every support location to equal the minimizing facility. Every draw, and hence the returned coordinatewise median, equals that location.

Bit computation

The algorithm performs exact rational comparisons and copies sampled coordinates into its output. Each sampled coordinate has encoding length at most \(L\). Comparing two such rationals by cross multiplication takes \(O(L^2)\) bit operations using elementary arithmetic.

Sorting each coordinate therefore costs at most

\( \displaystyle O(d\,n\log n\,L^2) \)

bit operations, in addition to reading the samples and computing \(k\). Computing \(k\) from rational \(\epsilon\) requires integer squaring and division on \(O(L)\)-bit integers. Each output coordinate is a sampled rational, so its encoding length is at most \(L\).

Since \(n=O(\epsilon^{-2})\) and \(m=d\), the bit running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\). Neither the support list nor the masses are needed.
