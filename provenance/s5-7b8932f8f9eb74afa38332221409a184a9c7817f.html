The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of customer types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), and nonnegative rational connection costs \(\gamma\) on the complete graph over \(T\cup\{s\}\), let \(q\in[0,\mu]\) be a coalition mass vector and let \(C(q)\) be the minimum spanning-tree cost connecting \(s\) to \(\operatorname{supp}(q)=\{t:q_t>0\}\). Set \(\rho(q)=\min\{\sum_t q_t,1-\sum_t q_t\}\). Classify the complexity, as a function of \(|T|\) and the binary input length, of computing charges \(p_t\) and the maximum feasible \(\epsilon^\star\) subject to \(\sum_t\mu_t p_t=C(\mu)\) and \(\sum_t q_t p_t\le C(q)-\epsilon^\star\rho(q)\) for every \(q\in[0,\mu]\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) customer types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also gives a supplier \(s\notin T\) and nonnegative binary-rational edge costs \(\gamma\) on the undirected complete graph on \(T\cup\{s\}\). Let \(m=\tau+1\) and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
A coalition is any real vector \(q\) with \(0\le q_t\le\mu_t\). Define \(C(q)\) as the MST cost in the subgraph induced by \(\{s\}\cup\{t:q_t>0\}\), with \(C(0)=0\); types outside this set cannot serve as intermediate vertices.
Set \(\rho(q)=\min\{\sum_tq_t,1-\sum_tq_t\}\). Charges \(p_t\in\mathbb R\) are per unit mass and may be negative; \(\epsilon\in\mathbb R\).
The optimization problem computes the maximum feasible \(\epsilon^\star\) and an attaining charge vector, subject to \(\sum_t\mu_tp_t=C(\mu)\) and \(\sum_tq_tp_t\le C(q)-\epsilon\rho(q)\) for every coalition \(q\).

Is this exact optimization problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite-agent MST cost games, computing a weighted least-core allocation is NP-hard for a class of priority functions including \(f(S)=1\), \(f(S)=|S|\), and \(f(S)=c(S)\). [Faigle, Kern and Paulusma](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/5117879/1483.pdf)
No result settling the exact continuous problem defined above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Resolved the coalition-cost ambiguity using the induced subgraph on the participating types and supplier; outside types cannot be intermediate vertices.
  • Set the empty-coalition cost to zero and required a nonempty support.
  • Specified unrestricted real per-unit charges and an unrestricted real optimization parameter, following the allocation domain of the discrete definition.
  • Defined m as the number of graph vertices and L as the total binary input length; there are no additional parameters.

Result

The exact continuous optimization problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\). This holds even when all edge costs are positive integers, all masses are dyadic rationals, and one type has mass at least \(\frac34\).

We prove this by constructing an instance whose optimum determines the minimum number of triples covering a given universe. The construction gives both a universal upper bound and an allocation attaining it.

1. Reducing continuous constraints to full-mass coalitions

For a set \(S\subseteq T\), write \(\mu(S)=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\), and let \(C(S)\) denote the MST cost induced by \(S\cup\{s\}\). Thus \(C(S)\) is the cost of every coalition whose support is exactly \(S\).

Lemma. For \(\epsilon\ge 0\), a budget-balanced charge vector \(p\) satisfies every continuous coalition constraint if and only if both of the following hold:

  • \(p_t\ge\epsilon\) for every \(t\in T\);
  • \(\sum_{t\in S}\mu_tp_t+\epsilon\min\{\mu(S),1-\mu(S)\}\le C(S)\) for every \(S\subseteq T\).

Proof. Suppose first that all continuous constraints hold. Fix \(t\), and choose \(0<\delta<\min\{\mu_t,\frac12\}\). The coalition \(q=\mu-\delta e_t\) has full support, so \(C(q)=C(T)\), while \(\rho(q)=\delta\). Budget balance therefore gives

\(\displaystyle C(T)-\delta p_t\le C(T)-\epsilon\delta.\)

Consequently \(p_t\ge\epsilon\). The set inequalities follow by taking the full-mass coalition on each \(S\).

Conversely, define

\(\displaystyle F(q)=\sum_tq_tp_t+\epsilon\rho(q).\)

Increasing one coordinate by \(\delta\ge0\) changes \(\rho\) by at least \(-\delta\): the function \(z\mapsto\min\{z,1-z\}\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz. Hence this increase changes \(F\) by at least \(\delta(p_t-\epsilon)\ge0\). Thus \(F\) is coordinatewise nondecreasing.

Given a coalition with support \(S\), increase each participating coordinate to its full mass. This preserves the support and therefore preserves its MST cost. The resulting full-mass inequality bounds \(F(q)\) by \(C(S)=C(q)\). The empty coalition satisfies its constraint directly. This proves the lemma.

2. The cover instance and the society

Start with an instance of Exact Cover by 3-Sets: a universe \(W\) of size \(3Q\), and a family \(U\) of \(k\) triples. Selecting a member of \(U\) means selecting its three neighbors in \(W\).

We may assume:

  • \(Q\ge3\);
  • every element belongs to at least two triples.

The first restriction can be enforced by adding three disjoint forced triples. Instances with an uncovered element are immediately negative; otherwise, duplicating every triple enforces the second restriction without changing the minimum cover size.

Let \(d\) be the minimum number of triples covering \(W\). Then \(d\ge Q\), and the instance has an exact cover precisely when \(d=Q\). This is the standard NP-hard cover problem used in the [cover-based MST reduction of Faigle, Kern and Paulusma, Section 2](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/5117879/1483.pdf). The argument below verifies our continuous variant directly.

The customer types are

\(\displaystyle N=U\cup W\cup\{g,h\},\)

where \(g\) and \(h\) are two additional types. The supplier is \(s\). Put

\(\displaystyle n=k+3Q+1,\qquad |N|=n+1.\)

First define auxiliary integer edge costs \(\ell\) on the complete graph. Choose \(M=10Q+10\), and assign the following costs:

| Edge | Auxiliary cost |
|---|---:|
| \(\{s,g\}\) | \(2Q-1\) |
| \(\{s,h\}\) | \(3Q\) |
| \(\{g,h\}\) | \(Q+1\) |
| \(\{h,u\}\), \(u\in U\) | \(Q\) |
| \(\{g,u\}\), \(u\in U\) | \(Q+1\) |
| \(\{s,u\}\), \(u\in U\) | \(3Q\) |
| \(\{u,w\}\), \(w\) belongs to triple \(u\) | \(Q+1\) |
| \(\{h,w\}\), \(w\in W\) | \(2Q+1\) |
| \(\{g,w\}\), \(w\in W\) | \(2Q+2\) |
| \(\{s,w\}\), \(w\in W\) | \(4Q+1\) |
| Every remaining edge | \(M\) |

These are edge costs specified directly on the complete graph. Coalition MSTs always use only their induced vertices.

Choose \(D\) to be the smallest power of two satisfying \(D\ge4n\). Assign masses

\(\displaystyle \mu_i=\frac1D\quad(i\ne h),\qquad \mu_h=1-\frac nD.\)

These masses are positive, sum to \(1\), and satisfy \(\mu_h\ge\frac34\). Finally, define the actual input costs by

\(\displaystyle \gamma_e=\ell_e+D.\)

Let \(c(S)\) be the MST cost under \(\ell\), and \(C(S)\) its cost under \(\gamma\). Every spanning tree on \(S\cup\{s\}\) has exactly \(|S|\) edges, so

\(\displaystyle C(S)=c(S)+D|S|.\)

3. Exact MST cost identities

For \(S\subseteq N\), put \(A=S\cap U\), \(Z=S\cap W\), and let \(z\) be the number of elements of \(Z\) uncovered by \(A\).

The following identities hold:

\(\displaystyle h\in S\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c(S)=3Q+Q|A|+(Q+1)|Z|+Qz.\)

\(\displaystyle g\in S,\ h\notin S\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c(S)=2Q-1+(Q+1)(|A|+|Z|+z).\)

Here is a direct verification by Kruskal’s algorithm.

If \(h\in S\), the edges of cost \(Q\) join all selected triples to \(h\). At cost \(Q+1\), the covered elements join this component, and \(g\) joins if present. Each uncovered element subsequently joins through its edge to \(h\), of cost \(2Q+1\). The connection to the supplier costs \(3Q\): either a direct edge of that cost when \(g\) is absent, or the two edges through \(g\), totaling \((Q+1)+(2Q-1)=3Q\). This gives the first identity.

If \(g\in S\) and \(h\notin S\), the edges of cost \(Q+1\) connect \(g\), every selected triple, and every covered element. The supplier joins at cost \(2Q-1\), and each uncovered element joins at cost \(2Q+2\). This gives the second identity. All remaining available edges are too expensive to improve these choices.

Define baseline values

\(\displaystyle b_u=Q,\qquad b_w=Q+1,\qquad b_g=0,\qquad b_h=3Q.\)

Since all elements are covered by \(U\), the first identity gives

\(\displaystyle c(N)=b(N)=3Q+kQ+3Q(Q+1).\)

Moreover, every element has at least two neighboring triples. Removing one triple therefore leaves all elements covered. Consequently, for every light type \(i\ne h\),

\(\displaystyle c(N\setminus\{i\})=c(N)-b_i,\)

and hence

\(\displaystyle C(N\setminus\{i\})=C(N)-(D+b_i).\)

4. A universal upper bound on the optimum

For any feasible solution, write \(x_i=\mu_ip_i\).

For each light type \(i\ne h\), the full-mass coalition \(N\setminus\{i\}\) has priority \(\frac1D\). Its constraint and budget balance imply

\(\displaystyle x_i\ge D+b_i+\frac{\epsilon}{D}.\)

Now take a minimum cover \(A\subseteq U\), with \(|A|=d\), and consider

\(\displaystyle S=A\cup W\cup\{g\},\qquad t=|S|=d+3Q+1.\)

This set excludes \(h\), so its mass is \(t/D\le n/D\le\frac14\), and its priority is \(t/D\). Summing the preceding lower bounds yields

\(\displaystyle x(S)\ge Dt+Qd+(Q+1)3Q+\frac{\epsilon t}{D}.\)

Since \(A\) covers \(W\), the second MST identity gives

\(\displaystyle C(S)=Dt+2Q-1+(Q+1)(d+3Q).\)

Applying the coalition constraint and cancelling terms gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{2\epsilon t}{D}\le d+2Q-1.\)

Thus every feasible solution satisfies

\(\displaystyle \epsilon\le \frac{D(d+2Q-1)}{2(d+3Q+1)}.\)

5. An allocation attaining the upper bound

Set

\(\displaystyle a=\frac{d+2Q-1}{d+3Q+1},\qquad r=\frac a2,\qquad \epsilon_0=Dr.\)

Because \(Q\ge3\) and \(d\ge Q\),

\(\displaystyle \frac12<a<1.\)

Define total charges by

\(\displaystyle x_i=D+b_i+r\quad(i\ne h),\qquad x_h=D+3Q-rn.\)

Their sum is \(D|N|+b(N)=C(N)\), so budget balance holds. Define per-unit charges by \(p_i=x_i/\mu_i\).

We first check the charge lower bounds required by the lemma. For light types, \(\epsilon_0\mu_i=r\), while \(x_i=D+b_i+r\ge r\). For the heavy type,

\(\displaystyle x_h-\epsilon_0\mu_h=D+3Q-rD>0,\)

because \(r<\frac12\). Therefore \(p_i\ge\epsilon_0\) for every type.

It remains to verify all full-mass coalition inequalities.

5.1. Coalitions containing the heavy type

Let \(h\in S\), and put \(\lambda=|S\setminus\{h\}|\). Since the complement consists entirely of light types,

\(\displaystyle \min\{\mu(S),1-\mu(S)\}=\frac{n-\lambda}{D}.\)

The allocation therefore satisfies

\(\displaystyle x(S)+\epsilon_0\min\{\mu(S),1-\mu(S)\}=D|S|+b(S).\)

By the first MST identity,

\(\displaystyle c(S)=b(S)+Qz\ge b(S).\)

Consequently the required inequality holds for every coalition containing \(h\).

5.2. Coalitions excluding the heavy type

Every such coalition has mass at most \(\frac14\). Its constraint is therefore equivalent to

\(\displaystyle b(S)+a|S|\le c(S).\)

Define its violation expression by

\(\displaystyle V(S)=b(S)+a|S|-c(S).\)

We prove \(V(S)\le0\).

First suppose \(S\ne\varnothing\) and \(g\notin S\). Take an MST on \(S\cup\{s\}\), and choose any edge \(\{s,v\}\) incident with the supplier. For both possible kinds of \(v\),

\(\displaystyle \ell_{sv}=\ell_{sg}+\ell_{gv}.\)

Replacing that edge by \(\{s,g\}\) and \(\{g,v\}\) produces a spanning tree on \(S\cup\{g,s\}\) of the same cost. Thus \(c(S\cup\{g\})\le c(S)\). Since \(b_g=0\),

\(\displaystyle V(S\cup\{g\})\ge V(S)+a.\)

It therefore suffices to prove the assertion for coalitions containing \(g\).

For such a coalition, write \(u=|A|\), \(w=|Z|\), and retain the uncovered-element count \(z\). The second MST identity gives

\(\displaystyle V(S)=(a-1)u+aw+a-(2Q-1)-(Q+1)z.\)

We transform \(S\) into a coalition containing a cover and all elements, without decreasing \(V\).

Eliminate uncovered selected elements. If a selected element \(w_0\) is uncovered, choose a triple \(u_0\notin A\) covering it. Replace \(w_0\) by \(u_0\). The triple count increases by one, the element count decreases by one, and the uncovered count decreases by at least one. Hence

\(\displaystyle V(S')-V(S)\ge(a-1)-a+(Q+1)=Q>0.\)

Repeating this step leaves no selected element uncovered.

Add every missing element. If a missing element is already covered, adding it increases \(V\) by \(a>0\). Otherwise, add it together with a triple covering it. All selected elements remain covered, and this increases \(V\) by \(2a-1>0\). Repetition yields a coalition of the form \(A'\cup W\cup\{g\}\), where \(A'\) covers \(W\).

Let \(d'=|A'|\). Necessarily \(d'\ge d\), and for this final coalition,

\(\displaystyle V=a(d'+3Q+1)-(d'+2Q-1).\)

The definition of \(a\) makes this zero when \(d'=d\). More generally,

\(\displaystyle V=(a-1)(d'-d)\le0.\)

Since the transformations never decreased \(V\), the original coalition also satisfies \(V(S)\le0\). The empty coalition has \(V(\varnothing)=0\).

This verifies every full-mass coalition inequality. The lemma now verifies every real coalition vector. Thus the proposed allocation attains \(\epsilon_0\), and together with the upper bound proves

\(\displaystyle \epsilon^\star=\frac{D(d+2Q-1)}{2(d+3Q+1)}.\)

6. Polynomial reduction and hardness

The construction has support size \(\tau=k+3Q+2\) and \(m=\tau+1\). The power of two \(D\) satisfies \(4n\le D<8n\), so its encoding length is logarithmic in \(n\). All masses are dyadic rationals with polynomial encoding length, and every edge cost is a positive integer of polynomial encoding length. The complete input can therefore be constructed in polynomial time.

The expression determining the optimum is strictly increasing in \(d\). Indeed,

\(\displaystyle \frac{d+1+2Q-1}{d+1+3Q+1}-\frac{d+2Q-1}{d+3Q+1}=\frac{Q+2}{(d+3Q+1)(d+3Q+2)}>0.\)

Therefore the original instance has an exact cover if and only if

\(\displaystyle \epsilon^\star=\frac{D(3Q-1)}{2(4Q+1)}.\)

If it has no exact cover, its optimum is strictly larger.

An exact polynomial-time algorithm for the requested optimization problem would thus decide Exact Cover by 3-Sets in polynomial time. Computing the optimum alone is already \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\), and requiring an attaining charge vector does not weaken that conclusion.
