The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given balanced applicant and position populations with finite type supports \(A\) and \(P\), binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\) and \(\nu_p\) each summing to \(1\), and strict rankings by each type over types on the other side, run synchronous type-mass DA and ADA. Each position type has capacity \(\nu_p\), holds its highest-ranked applicant mass up to capacity, and may split a marginal type; when full, ADA also pre-rejects unmatched mass below its worst-held type. Compute the exact per-applicant proposal masses \(\Pi_{\mathrm{DA}}\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{ADA}}\), and determine whether \(\Pi_{\mathrm{ADA}} \leq \Pi_{\mathrm{DA}}\) holds for every such market, with complexity measured in \(|A|\), \(|P|\), and the input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive binary-rational masses. Let the nonempty applicant and position supports be \(A\) and \(P\), with \(\sum_{a\in A}\mu_a=\sum_{p\in P}\nu_p=1\). Every type has a complete strict ranking of opposite-side types, all acceptable. Set \(m=|P|\), \(\tau=|A|+|P|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Represent applicant type \(a\) by the interval \([0,\mu_a)\) with Lebesgue mass. Each portion retains its own rejection history. Initially all mass is unmatched and no position has rejected anyone.
In each synchronous round, every unmatched portion proposes to its highest-ranked position type that has not rejected it. Each position \(p\) pools new proposals with previously held mass, retains its highest-ranked mass up to capacity \(\nu_p\), and rejects the remainder permanently. Within a marginal applicant type, retain incumbents before newcomers, and within either group retain smaller interval labels first. Held mass makes no new proposal.
ADA additionally does the following after each round: every full position permanently rejects all applicant portions of types strictly below its worst positively held type, including portions currently held elsewhere. These rejections only forbid future proposals to that position; they do not dissolve holdings elsewhere. DA has no such additional step. All rejection information affects proposals starting in the next round.
Stop when no unmatched portion can propose. For \(X\in\{\mathrm{DA},\mathrm{ADA}\}\), let \(q^X_{r,a,p}\) be the mass making a new proposal from \(a\) to \(p\) in round \(r\), and define \(\Pi_X=\sum_{r,a,p}q^X_{r,a,p}\). Since total applicant mass is \(1\), this is the mean number of proposals per applicant; continued holdings and pre-emptive rejections do not count as proposals.

Can \((\Pi_{\mathrm{DA}},\Pi_{\mathrm{ADA}})\) be computed exactly in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computation NP-hard?
Does \(\Pi_{\mathrm{ADA}}\leq\Pi_{\mathrm{DA}}\) hold for every such market, or is there a counterexample?

For finite balanced one-to-one markets with complete strict preferences, ADA returns the DA matching and never makes more proposals ([Gutin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06865v1)).
Fractional DA with indifferences has been studied under different within-class fairness rules ([Cookson and Shah](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~nisarg/papers/dfda.pdf)); no result settling the specified mass proposal comparison was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made rankings complete and all opposite-side types acceptable, preserving the discrete domain.
  • Specified persistent rejection histories for individual portions rather than treating rejection of some mass as rejection of its entire type.
  • Resolved marginal-type retention by prioritizing incumbents, then smaller fixed interval labels; the supplied definitions provide no within-type convention.
  • Restored pre-emptive rejection of portions currently held elsewhere, as the discrete algorithm rejects all lower-ranked applicants, including matched applicants.
  • Specified that synchronous rejection information takes effect in the next round and that position responses include previous holdings.
  • Defined proposal masses as aggregate new proposal mass divided by total applicant mass, which is already normalized to one.
  • Specified stopping when unmatched mass has no eligible proposal, avoiding ambiguity about repeated pre-emptive rejection notifications.
  • Required nonempty supports without translating the discrete lower bound on individual population size into a lower bound on the number of types.

Result

Both quantities can be computed exactly in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Moreover, every market in the question satisfies
\(\Pi_{\mathrm{ADA}}\leq\Pi_{\mathrm{DA}}\).

In fact, DA and ADA produce the same final holdings by applicant and position type. The comparison follows by counting ADA’s skipped choices as virtual proposals. Polynomial-time computation requires a separate argument: long stretches of synchronous rounds can be evaluated through deterministic flow paths.

1. Finite discretization and sufficient state

Write \(n=|A|\). Choose a common denominator \(D\) for all masses, for example the product of their input denominators. Under binary rational encoding, \(\log D=O(L)\). Set
\(M_a=D\mu_a\) and \(K_p=D\nu_p\), so both sides have total integer mass \(D\).

Partition each applicant interval into cells of mass \(1/D\). The specified process never splits a cell. Indeed, initially all boundaries lie on this grid; in each subsequent round, capacities and the masses preceding any within-type cutoff are integer multiples of \(1/D\). The incumbent and label priorities therefore cut only at grid boundaries.

Consequently, for proofs we may regard the market as having \(D\) applicant atoms and integer position capacities \(K_p\). This discretization is conceptual; the algorithm below does not enumerate the atoms.

Every atom proposes to any position at most once. Furthermore, a position rejecting an atom is full at that time, whether the rejection is ordinary or pre-emptive. A full position remains full, since it always pools its existing holdings with new proposals.

If an unmatched atom had been rejected by every position, every position would consequently be full. Their total holdings would then be \(D\), contradicting the existence of that unmatched atom. Thus termination leaves every atom matched.

Each nonterminal round contains at least one actual proposal, and there are at most \(Dm\) actual proposals. Both processes therefore terminate within \(Dm\) proposal rounds.

For each type \(a\), write its preference list as
\(p_{a,1},\ldots,p_{a,m}\).
An atom’s choices descend this list. Once it is held at \(p_{a,i}\), every earlier choice has permanently rejected it. Among later choices, its only possible advance rejections are ADA’s bans, which are common to all atoms of its type.

It follows that the following aggregate state is sufficient:

  • held mass \(h_{a,p}\);
  • unmatched mass classified by type and next available preference-list index;
  • ADA’s current bans.

The histories of different portions are respected by this representation. In particular, all rejected portions of type \(a\) from a given position \(p\), whether incumbents or newcomers, have the same subsequent available choices.

2. Cumulative proposals and the DA fixed point

Use normalized masses in this section, and write \(x_+=\max\{x,0\}\).

For a cumulative proposal matrix \(z\), define
\(H_{a,p}(z)=\min\{z_{a,p},(\nu_p-\sum_{b\succ_p a}z_{b,p})_+\}\)
and
\(R_{a,p}(z)=z_{a,p}-H_{a,p}(z)\).

Here \(b\succ_p a\) means that position \(p\) ranks type \(b\) above type \(a\).

2.1 Why these formulas describe holdings and rejections

For ordinary proposals, a position’s holdings by type are its highest-ranked mass among all proposals ever received.

To verify this without making assumptions about within-type identities, fix a prefix \(T\) of a position’s type ranking. Suppose the cumulative proposal mass from \(T\) is \(z_T\), and its currently held mass is \(\min\{\nu_p,z_T\}\). If the next proposal batch contributes \(q_T\), choosing the highest-ranked pool gives new held prefix mass
\(\min\{\nu_p,\min\{\nu_p,z_T\}+q_T\}=\min\{\nu_p,z_T+q_T\}\).

Induction establishes this identity for every prefix. Taking differences between successive prefixes gives exactly \(H_{a,p}(z)\). Since every portion proposes at most once to a position, \(R_{a,p}(z)\) is the cumulative mass rejected there.

These rejection functions are coordinatewise nondecreasing. Indeed,
\(R_{a,p}(z)=\bigl(z_{a,p}-(\nu_p-\sum_{b\succ_p a}z_{b,p})_+\bigr)_+\).
Increasing its own proposal mass increases this expression; increasing higher-ranked proposal mass decreases the residual capacity and hence also increases it. Other coordinates have no effect.

2.2 DA computes a least fixed point

Define an operator \(F\) on the box \(0\leq z_{a,p}\leq\mu_a\) by
\((F(z))_{a,p_{a,1}}=\mu_a\)
and, for \(i>1\),
\((F(z))_{a,p_{a,i}}=R_{a,p_{a,i-1}}(z)\).

The operator preserves this box and is coordinatewise nondecreasing.

Let \(C^{(t)}\) be DA’s cumulative actual proposal matrix after round \(t\), with \(C^{(0)}=0\). Synchronous timing gives
\(C^{(t+1)}=F(C^{(t)})\).

For the first choice, every atom proposes in the first round. For each later choice, the atoms that have proposed there by round \(t+1\) are exactly those rejected by the preceding choice by round \(t\). This proves the recurrence.

By finite termination, the sequence reaches a fixed point \(C^*\). It is the least fixed point in the box: if \(W=F(W)\), then \(C^{(0)}\leq W\), and monotonicity inductively gives \(C^{(t)}\leq W\) for every \(t\). Thus \(C^*\leq W\).

DA’s total proposal mass is therefore
\(\Pi_{\mathrm{DA}}=\sum_{a,p}C^*_{a,p}\).

3. ADA’s virtual proposals prove the comparison

Whenever an ADA atom skips a pre-emptively rejected position while descending its list, insert a virtual proposal to that position immediately before the atom continues.

Only skipped positions actually traversed in this descent receive virtual proposals. A pre-emptive rejection of an atom still held elsewhere contributes no virtual proposal unless that atom later reaches the rejected choice.

Let \(V\) count actual and virtual proposals together.

3.1 Virtual proposals preserve the holdings formula

At the moment an atom skips position \(p\), that position is full and holds only types strictly above the atom’s type. Position cutoffs never worsen: after adding proposals to a full position, its retained mass is at least as highly ranked as its previous holdings.

Thus a virtual proposal is certainly rejected, and adding it cannot alter holdings. The formulas \(H(V)\) and \(R(V)\) consequently remain valid.

For a precise sequential interpretation, refine each ADA round as follows:

  • Process the virtual visits needed to prepare that round’s actual proposals.
  • Process the actual proposals individually, retaining the best pool after each insertion.
  • Make ordinary rejections from this round available for further actual proposals only in the next round.

For each position, fix its within-type ordering during the round to put original incumbents first, followed by newcomers, with labels ordered as specified. Incremental retention under this fixed ordering gives exactly the prescribed final pool decision. Every virtual arrival is strictly below the existing full cutoff and is rejected throughout this refinement.

3.2 Every virtual cumulative matrix is bounded by \(C^*\)

We prove \(V\leq C^*\) throughout the refinement, by induction over atomic visits.

For a visit to a type’s first choice, its cumulative visited mass cannot exceed \(\mu_a=C^*_{a,p_{a,1}}\).

Now consider a visit to \(p_{a,i}\), where \(i>1\). Before this visit, the arriving atom has already visited and been rejected by \(p_{a,i-1}\). Every atom previously counted at \(p_{a,i}\) has also been rejected by that preceding position. These are distinct atoms.

Consequently, if \(V'\) is obtained by adding the current visit, then
\(V'_{a,p_{a,i}}\leq R_{a,p_{a,i-1}}(V)\).

Assuming \(V\leq C^*\), monotonicity gives
\(V'_{a,p_{a,i}}\leq R_{a,p_{a,i-1}}(C^*)=C^*_{a,p_{a,i}}\).

All other coordinates are unchanged. This proves the invariant.

3.3 The terminal virtual matrix equals \(C^*\)

At termination every atom is matched. With virtual visits included, each atom has visited every preference-list position through its final position and has been rejected by every preceding one.

Hence the terminal virtual matrix satisfies
\(V_{a,p_{a,1}}=\mu_a\)
and
\(V_{a,p_{a,i}}=R_{a,p_{a,i-1}}(V)\)
for \(i>1\). Thus \(V=F(V)\).

The least-fixed-point property gives \(C^*\leq V\), while the preceding invariant gives \(V\leq C^*\). Therefore \(V=C^*\).

In particular, both processes have final type-level holdings \(H(C^*)\). More directly,
\(\Pi_{\mathrm{DA}}-\Pi_{\mathrm{ADA}}\)
is exactly the total mass of ADA’s virtual proposals, which is nonnegative. This proves
\(\Pi_{\mathrm{ADA}}\leq\Pi_{\mathrm{DA}}\).

4. An exact polynomial-time algorithm

The bound of \(Dm\) rounds is potentially exponential in the input length. We now compress the rounds.

Return to integer masses. At a full position \(p\), let \(c_p\) be its worst positively held applicant type. At a nonfull position, leave \(c_p\) undefined.

A full position’s cutoff can only improve. ADA’s current banned types at a full position are exactly those strictly below its current cutoff: earlier bans are contained in this set because cutoffs improve. Nonfull positions have never issued pre-emptive bans.

We divide the run into phases during which the full/nonfull status and the cutoff type of every full position remain unchanged.

4.1 Exact dynamics before a phase boundary

At the beginning of a phase define a positive resource for each position:
\(\rho_p=K_p-\sum_a h_{a,p}\)
if it is nonfull, and
\(\rho_p=h_{c_p,p}\)
if it is full.

These resources measure, respectively, remaining vacancy and remaining marginal-type holdings.

Let the active unmatched states be \(s=(a,i)\), where \(p_{a,i}\) is the next actual proposal destination. There are at most \(d=nm\) such states. During the phase, ADA’s bans are fixed.

Write \(N(a,p)\) for the first available state of type \(a\) strictly below \(p\) on its list, or \(\bot\) if none exists. For DA this is simply the next list index; for ADA it skips the fixed bans.

Define a deterministic transition \(f\) and a holdings increment for a unit in state \(s=(a,i)\), with destination \(p=p_{a,i}\):

  • If \(p\) is nonfull: accept the unit, add one to \(h_{a,p}\), consume one unit of \(\rho_p\), and send the flow to \(\bot\).
  • If \(p\) is full and \(a\succ_p c_p\): accept the unit, displace one unit of type \(c_p\), add one to \(h_{a,p}\), subtract one from \(h_{c_p,p}\), consume one unit of \(\rho_p\), and send the displaced flow to \(N(c_p,p)\).
  • Otherwise: reject the unit, leave holdings unchanged, consume no resource, and send the flow to \(N(a,p)\).

Each active unit makes exactly one actual proposal.

These rules are exact as long as cumulative resource consumption stays strictly below every \(\rho_p\). For a full position, incoming mass strictly above its cutoff then fits entirely within its remaining marginal holdings. Such arrivals displace only that marginal type. Equal-type newcomers are rejected because incumbents have priority; lower types are rejected as well. A nonfull position accepts everything until its vacancy is exhausted.

Thus, before a phase boundary, unmatched flow follows a deterministic map without branching.

4.2 Evaluating arbitrarily many hypothetical rounds

Extend these fixed rules hypothetically even beyond resource exhaustion.

For a starting state \(s\), its orbit
\(s,f(s),f^2(s),\ldots\)
either reaches \(\bot\), or consists of a transient path followed by a cycle. Both the transient length and cycle length are at most \(d\).

For any binary-encoded integer \(k\), we can calculate the number of visits to every state during the first \(k\) hypothetical rounds:

  • a transient state is visited at most once by a starting unit;
  • after reaching a cycle of length \(\ell\), divide the remaining number of visits by \(\ell\);
  • each cycle state receives the quotient number of visits, with one extra visit for the appropriate initial segment.

We can also determine the unmatched state after \(k\) rounds. Multiply these quantities by the initial mass at each starting state and sum. Merging flows are handled by addition.

Let \(w_s(k)\) denote total visits to state \(s\), and let \(\Gamma_p(k)\) be their total resource consumption at position \(p\). These calculations require polynomially many integer operations in \(d\) and \(\log k\). Holdings increments and proposal counts are linear sums of the same visit counts.

4.3 Locating and executing the next boundary round

Define
\(T=\min\{k\geq1:\Gamma_p(k)\geq\rho_p\text{ for some }p\}\).

For every earlier round, all resources remain positive, so induction using the rules above shows that the hypothetical dynamics agree exactly with the true process. Hence we can evaluate the first \(T-1\) rounds in bulk.

The predicate defining \(T\) is nondecreasing in \(k\), because all resource consumption is nonnegative.

If there is unmatched mass at the phase’s start, then \(T\leq Dm\). To see this, suppose there were no resource exhaustion within that bound. The fixed dynamics would then agree with the actual process throughout those rounds. But the actual process terminates within \(Dm\) rounds, and at termination every position is full. At least one position was nonfull at the phase’s start, because unmatched mass existed in a balanced market. Its becoming full would exhaust its vacancy resource, a contradiction.

We can therefore find \(T\) by binary search over \(1,\ldots,Dm\).

After evaluating the first \(T-1\) rounds, execute round \(T\) directly:

  • Form its proposal masses from the resulting unmatched state.
  • At each position, pool proposals with holdings and retain mass in its input type-ranking order.
  • Send rejected mass to its subsequent choices.
  • Apply ADA’s updated bans when preparing the next round’s unmatched states.

This directly simulated round handles simultaneous resource exhaustion and overshooting. In particular, it does not use the fixed-cutoff rules beyond their validity.

5. Number of phases and bit complexity

Give each position a numerical cutoff rank: use \(n+1\) while nonfull, and otherwise use the rank of its worst positively held type, with rank one best.

Each boundary round strictly decreases at least one position’s numerical cutoff:

  • exhausting a vacancy makes a nonfull position full;
  • exhausting marginal holdings means that incoming higher-ranked mass, together with existing higher-ranked holdings, fills the entire capacity. The old marginal type disappears, so the cutoff strictly improves.

Numerical cutoffs never increase. Starting from \(n+1\), each position can undergo at most \(n\) strict decreases. There are therefore at most \(nm\) boundary rounds and phases.

For each phase:

  • the deterministic state map has at most \(nm\) states;
  • its paths and cycles are found in polynomial time;
  • binary search uses \(O(\log(Dm))\) evaluations;
  • each evaluation uses polynomially many integer operations.

The integer sizes are also polynomially bounded. For \(k\leq Dm\), every hypothetical visit count is at most \(kD\leq mD^2\), so its bit length is \(O(L+\log m)\). Resources, holdings updates, and search bounds have the same polynomial bit bound. The true total proposal count is at most \(Dm\).

Run this algorithm separately for DA and ADA. If their integer proposal totals are \(S_{\mathrm{DA}}\) and \(S_{\mathrm{ADA}}\), return
\((S_{\mathrm{DA}}/D,S_{\mathrm{ADA}}/D)\).

Since \(nm\leq m\tau\), the running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). This proves exact polynomial-time computability of the requested pair.
