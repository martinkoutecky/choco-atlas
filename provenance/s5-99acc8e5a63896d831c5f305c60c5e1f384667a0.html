The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational dual pseudometrics \(d_m,d_c\), a finite center set \(M\), and \(k\), compute centers \(x_j\in M\) and rational allocations \(q_{tj}\geq 0\) with \(\sum_j q_{tj}=\mu_t\). For each positive allocation in cluster \(j\), define its loss as \(d_c(t,x_j)+\max_{u:q_{uj}>0}d_m(t,u)\). Does there always exist, and can one compute in time polynomial in \(\tau, |M|, k\), and input bit length \(L\), an allocation with no deviation consisting of \(z_{tj}\leq q_{tj}\), total selected mass at least \(1/k\), and a center \(y\in M\), such that every selected cohort \((t,j)\) has new loss \(d_c(t,y)+\max_{u:\sum_j z_{uj}>0}d_m(t,u)\) strictly less than its current loss divided by \(3+2\sqrt{3}\)?

Let \(M\ne\varnothing\) be a finite set of feasible centers, \(m=|M|\), and \(k\ge1\) an integer. Input distances are finite nonnegative binary rationals: \(d_m\) is a pseudometric on \(T\), and \(d_c\) is a pseudometric on \(T\cup M\); they need not coincide. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An allocation has centers \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\in M\), which may repeat, and rational masses \(q_{tj}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{j=1}^k q_{tj}=\mu_t\). Empty cluster slots are allowed; cluster masses need not be equal.
For each \(q_{tj}>0\), its cohort's current loss is \(\ell_{tj}=d_c(t,x_j)+\max_{u:q_{uj}>0}d_m(t,u)\).

For \(\alpha\ge1\), an allocation is in the \(\alpha\)-core if no such deviation satisfies \(\alpha\bigl(d_c(t,y)+\max_{u\in S_z}d_m(t,u)\bigr)<\ell_{tj}\) for every \(z_{tj}>0\).

Does every instance admit a rational allocation in the \((3+2\sqrt{3})\)-core, and can such an allocation always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\)?

For finite indivisible agents with dual metric loss, a \(3\)-core clustering exists, and a \((3+2\sqrt{3})\)-core clustering can be computed in polynomial time ([Cookson, Shah and Yu](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~nisarg/papers/fair-semicentroid-clustering.pdf)).

Result

Yes to both questions. Every instance admits a rational allocation in the \((3+2\sqrt{3})\)-core, and such an allocation can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

The proof uses a weighted capture routine and a compressed implementation of the finite-agent algorithm. The exponentially large finite society introduced below is used only to prove correctness.

1. Rational allocations and real deviations

We first establish why arbitrary real-valued deviations cause no additional difficulty once an allocation lies on a rational grid.

Lemma 1. Suppose an allocation has \(Nq_{tj}\) integral for every \(t,j\), where \(N\) is a positive integer. Replace each cohort \((t,j)\) by \(Nq_{tj}\) identical agents of type \(t\), assigned to slot \(j\). Give these agents the distances of their types. Then the allocation belongs to the divisible \(\alpha\)-core if and only if the resulting finite clustering belongs to the finite-agent \(\alpha\)-core, with coalition threshold \(N/k\).

Proof. A finite blocking coalition gives a divisible deviation by setting \(z_{tj}\) equal to its number of selected agents from cohort \((t,j)\), divided by \(N\). Its type support, old losses, and new losses agree with those of the finite coalition.

Conversely, suppose \(z\) is a blocking real-valued deviation, with center \(y\). Let

\(I=\{(t,j):z_{tj}>0\}\).

Select all virtual agents belonging to cohorts in \(I\). Their number is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{(t,j)\in I}Nq_{tj}\ge N\sum_{t,j}z_{tj}\ge N/k.\)

Their type support is exactly \(S_z\): enlarging selected masses within already selected cohorts introduces no new type. Every selected virtual agent therefore has the same old and new losses as its selected cohort in the original deviation. All strict improvement inequalities remain valid. Thus these agents form a finite blocking coalition. \(\square\)

The pullback distances on virtual agents are pseudometrics: nonnegativity, symmetry, and the triangle inequalities follow directly from the corresponding properties on types. Distinct virtual agents may have zero distance, which is permitted.

2. A weighted capture lemma

Write \(b=1/k\). At a capture step, let \(w_t\) be the remaining mass of type \(t\), and assume \(\sum_t w_t\ge b\). Let \(U=\{t:w_t>0\}\).

For a nonzero mass vector \(v\) with support \(S=\{t:v_t>0\}\), define its maximum loss at center \(y\) by

\(\displaystyle R(v,y)=\max_{t\in S}\left(d_c(t,y)+\max_{u\in S}d_m(t,u)\right).\)

Lemma 2. In polynomial time, one can find a rational vector \(a\) and center \(x\) such that

\(0\le a_t\le w_t,\qquad \sum_t a_t=b,\)

and, for every vector \(v\) satisfying \(0\le v_t\le w_t\) and \(\sum_t v_t\ge b\), and every \(y\in M\),

\(R(a,x)\le 4R(v,y).\)

If all \(w_t\) and \(b\) are integer multiples of \(1/N\), the returned masses have the same property.

Proof. For \(y\in M\) and \(p,t\in U\), define

\(h_y(p,t)=d_m(p,t)+d_c(p,y)+d_c(t,y).\)

This definition includes \(p=t\), so \(h_y(p,p)=2d_c(p,y)\). We do not require \(h_y\) to be a pseudometric.

For each pair \((y,p)\), sort the values \(h_y(p,t)\), and find the smallest radius \(\rho_{y,p}\) for which

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in U:\,h_y(p,t)\le\rho_{y,p}}w_t\ge b.\)

Such a radius exists because the total remaining mass is at least \(b\). Choose a pair \((x,p)\) minimizing \(\rho_{y,p}\), and denote its radius by \(\rho\). From its ball, take exactly mass \(b\), using a fixed ordering of types and trimming the last contribution if necessary. Call the resulting mass vector \(a\), with support \(C\).

For \(t,u\in C\), the triangle inequality for \(d_m\) gives

\(\displaystyle d_c(t,x)+d_m(t,u) \le d_c(t,x)+d_m(t,p)+d_m(p,u) \le h_x(p,t)+h_x(p,u) \le 2\rho.\)

The second inequality uses only the nonnegativity of the additional terms \(2d_c(p,x)+d_c(u,x)\). Consequently,

\(R(a,x)\le 2\rho.\)

Now fix any comparator \((v,y)\) from the statement. Let \(S\) be its support and \(R=R(v,y)\). Choose \(p'\in S\). For every \(t\in S\),

\(\displaystyle h_y(p',t) =d_m(p',t)+d_c(p',y)+d_c(t,y) \le R+R=2R.\)

Here the first two terms are bounded by the loss of \(p'\), and \(d_c(t,y)\) is bounded by the loss of \(t\). Thus the ball of radius \(2R\) around \(p'\) contains all of \(S\), whose available mass is at least \(\sum_t v_t\ge b\). Therefore

\(\rho\le\rho_{y,p'}\le 2R,\)

and hence \(R(a,x)\le 4R\).

The sorting and mass accumulation involve only the supplied support. Finally, greedy trimming uses minima and subtraction of grid masses, so it preserves the grid \(1/N\). \(\square\)

3. The finite-agent theorem used

We use the following established result as a black box.

Finite-agent lemma. For dual pseudometric losses, Algorithm 1 of Cookson, Shah and Yu may use any subroutine returning a \(4\)-approximate most cohesive cluster: among remaining agents, its returned cluster has at least \(n/k\) members and maximum loss at most four times that of every eligible comparator. With growth parameter

\(\displaystyle c=\frac{12+\sqrt{48}}{16}=\frac{3+\sqrt{3}}4,\)

the algorithm returns a clustering in the \(\beta\)-core, where \(\beta=3+2\sqrt{3}\).

This is their [Lemma 1, specialized to approximation factor \(4\)](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~nisarg/papers/fair-semicentroid-clustering.pdf). We verify its applicability below.

4. Construction on the supplied support

Write each input mass with an integer denominator, and let \(D\) be the product of those denominators. Then \(D\mu_t\) is integral for every type, and the bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). Set

\(N=kD.\)

Thus every input mass and the quota \(b=1/k\) are integer multiples of \(1/N\).

Tentative clusters

Start with remaining masses \(w_t=\mu_t\). For each \(i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), apply Lemma 2 to obtain masses \(a_{ti}\) totaling \(b\), with center \(x_i\), and subtract those masses from \(w\).

Before round \(i\), the remaining total mass is \(1-(i-1)/k\), which is at least \(b\). Therefore all \(k\) rounds are valid, and afterward no mass remains. Define

\(A_i=\{t:a_{ti}>0\},\)

and

\(\displaystyle r_i=\max_{t\in A_i}\left(d_c(t,x_i)+\max_{u\in A_i}d_m(t,u)\right).\)

Each \(A_i\) is nonempty. All \(a_{ti}\) remain on the grid \(1/N\).

Compressed reassignment

Here is the reassignment rule of Algorithm 1, expressed for types and captured pieces. Keep \(A_i,x_i,r_i\) fixed. For each type \(t\), define

\(\displaystyle V_t=\left\{j:\ d_c(u,x_j)+d_m(u,t)\le cr_j\text{ for every }u\in A_j\right\},\)

and, for \(j\in V_t\),

\(\displaystyle \Phi(t,j)=d_c(t,x_j)+cr_j+ \min_{u\in A_j}\bigl(d_m(t,u)-d_c(u,x_j)\bigr).\)

Choose \(j^*(t)\) minimizing \(\Phi(t,j)\), with fixed tie-breaking. Route a positive piece \(a_{ti}\) to \(j^*(t)\) when

\(\Phi(t,j^*(t))<cr_i;\)

otherwise retain it in slot \(i\). Sum the routed pieces to obtain \(q_{tj}\).

The required minimizer exists whenever type \(t\) has a piece in slot \(i\), because \(i\in V_t\): for every \(u\in A_i\),

\(d_c(u,x_i)+d_m(u,t)\le r_i\le cr_i.\)

Routing preserves every type’s total mass. The output therefore satisfies

\(q_{tj}\ge0,\qquad \sum_jq_{tj}=\mu_t,\)

and every \(q_{tj}\) is an integer multiple of \(1/N\). Some final slots may be empty, as permitted.

5. Correctness

Consider, solely for this proof, a finite society consisting of \(N\mu_t\) agents of each type \(t\).

Its feasible centers are exactly \(M\). Define its two distances by pulling back the supplied distances through the agents’ type map, keeping feasible centers fixed. These are finite nonnegative pseudometrics. A finite cluster’s loss depends only on its type support, so it agrees with the corresponding weighted cluster’s loss.

The finite coalition threshold is

\(N/k=D.\)

Each tentative weighted cluster corresponds to exactly \(Na_{ti}\) agents of each type, totaling \(D\) agents.

To check the oracle hypothesis, take any subset of remaining virtual agents with at least \(D\) members, and any feasible center \(y\). Divide its type counts by \(N\), obtaining a vector \(v\) with

\(0\le v_t\le w_t,\qquad \sum_t v_t\ge b.\)

Its maximum finite-agent loss is exactly \(R(v,y)\). Lemma 2 therefore proves that our returned cluster is a \(4\)-approximate most cohesive cluster for this finite instance.

Consequently, the tentative-cluster phase is a permissible execution of the finite algorithm. It captures exactly \(D\) agents per round, so its special case for fewer than \(D\) remaining agents never occurs.

In the reassignment phase, all virtual agents with the same type \(t\) and tentative slot \(i\) have identical eligibility sets, scores, and comparison threshold \(cr_i\). These quantities use the fixed tentative clusters. With the specified tie-breaking, all such agents receive the same destination. Thus routing the whole piece \(a_{ti}\) reproduces the finite algorithm’s reassignment exactly.

All hypotheses of the finite-agent lemma are now verified. Its output is in the finite \((3+2\sqrt{3})\)-core. By Lemma 1, the rational allocation \(q\) is in the divisible \((3+2\sqrt{3})\)-core, including against real-valued deviations.

6. Polynomial running time and exact arithmetic

The algorithm never enumerates the \(N\) virtual agents.

A capture round examines at most \(m\tau\) pairs \((y,p)\), sorting at most \(\tau\) distances for each pair. Over \(k\) rounds, this takes

\(O\!\left(km\tau^2\log(\tau+1)\right)\)

comparisons, plus polynomially many mass and distance operations. Computing all reassignment eligibility sets and scores takes \(O(k\tau^2)\) operations. There are at most \(k\tau\) captured pieces to route.

Mass arithmetic can use integer counts with denominator \(N=kD\). All counts are at most \(N\), whose bit length is \(O(L+\log k)\). Hence every output mass has polynomial bit length.

The irrational constant creates no numerical approximation issue. All reassignment expressions belong to \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})\), with polynomial-bit rational coefficients. Comparing two such expressions reduces to deciding the sign of \(A+B\sqrt{3}\). If the summands have opposing signs, compare \(A^2\) with \(3B^2\); the other sign cases are immediate. These are exact rational operations of polynomial bit complexity.

Therefore the complete construction, including its rational output, runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\).
