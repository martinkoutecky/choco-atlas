The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), a partition into communities \(C_j\), baseline block contact rates \(\lambda^0_{tu}\), and budgets \(\kappa,\beta\), choose nonnegative type-pair link rates \(z_{tu}\) with \(\sum_{t,u}\mu_t\mu_u z_{tu}\le\beta\). Seed masses satisfy \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_t\le\kappa\). Let \(r_t^0=x_t/\mu_t\), \(d_t^\ell=r_t^\ell-r_t^{\ell-1}\), and evolve the block-kernel independent-cascade limit by \(r_u^{\ell+1}=r_u^\ell+(1-r_u^\ell)(1-\exp[-\sum_t\mu_t(\lambda^0_{tu}+z_{tu})d_t^\ell])\); write \(R_t(x,z)=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}r_t^\ell\). Define \(X^*(z)\) as the seed-mass vectors maximizing \(\sum_t\mu_tR_t(x,z)\). For fixed additive gap \(\varepsilon>0\), approximate \(\max_z\min_{x\in X^*(z)}\min_j\frac{\sum_{t\in C_j}\mu_tR_t(x,z)}{\sum_{t\in C_j}\mu_t}\), or decide whether this value is at least \(\theta\) using a corresponding rational-input gap formulation.

Result

The specified gap problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for every fixed rational \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\).

The reduction below uses only two communities, equal dyadic masses, and a strictly positive link budget. It verifies the existence promise for every feasible link addition and handles all exact spread maximizers.

The central observation is that sufficiently small contact rates can force every spread-maximizing continuous seed allocation to be integral. Their first-order spread then encodes a weighted directed cut.

1. A small-contact lemma

Let \(N\ge2\), and let \(W=(W_{tu})\) satisfy:

  • \(W_{tt}=0\);
  • \(W_{tu}\ge1\) for \(t\ne u\);
  • every \(W_{tu}\) is a nonnegative half-integer.

Choose an integer \(Q\ge1\) such that \(\max_u\sum_tW_{tu}\le Q\), and a real number

\(\displaystyle 0<\delta\le\frac{1}{1000NQ^2}.\)

Let \(U\) be any nonnegative real matrix with

\(\displaystyle \sum_{u,t}U_{ut}\le\frac{\delta}{1000},\)

and define the matrix acting on column vectors by

\(\displaystyle A_{ut}=\delta W_{tu}+U_{ut}.\)

Fix an integer \(k\) with \(1\le k<N\). For seed fractions \(s\in[0,1]^N\) satisfying \(\sum_us_u\le k\), consider the cascade with contact matrix \(A\). Write its limiting vector as \(r(s)\), and its unweighted total spread as \(F(s)=\sum_ur_u(s)\).

Lemma. The following statements hold.

  • The cascade has a unique limiting fixed point, depending continuously on \(s\).
  • Every exact maximizer of \(F\) has \(s\in\{0,1\}^N\) and \(\sum_us_u=k\).
  • If \(S=\{u:s_u=1\}\) is any exact spread-maximizing seed set, then \(S\) maximizes

\(\displaystyle C_W(S)=\sum_{t\in S,\ u\notin S}W_{tu}\)
over all sets of cardinality \(k\).

  • An unseeded coordinate satisfies \(r_u(s)\le2\delta Q\).

1.1. Fixed-point equation and continuity

Put \(\rho=\|A\|_\infty\), where the matrix norm is the maximum absolute row sum. Then

\(\displaystyle \rho\le\delta Q+\frac{\delta}{1000}\le2\delta Q\le\frac{2}{1000NQ}<\frac12.\)

Let \(r^0=s\). Multiplying the susceptible-fraction updates through cascade round \(\ell\) gives

\(\displaystyle 1-r_u^{\ell+1}=(1-s_u)\exp\!\left[-(Ar^\ell)_u\right].\)

Indeed, the cumulative increments through that round sum to \(r^\ell\). Thus the cascade iterates the map

\(\displaystyle \Phi_s(r)=1-(1-s)\odot\exp(-Ar),\)

where \(\odot\) and the exponential act coordinatewise.

This map sends \([0,1]^N\) into itself. Its derivative with respect to \(r\) is

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{diag}\!\left((1-s)\odot e^{-Ar}\right)A,\)

whose infinity norm is at most \(\rho<1\). The contraction theorem therefore gives a unique fixed point, and the cascade converges to it.

At this fixed point, define

\(\displaystyle E=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-Ar}),\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}((1-s)\odot e^{-Ar}),\qquad J=DA.\)

Since \(\|J\|_\infty\le\rho<1\), the matrix \(I-J\) is invertible. The implicit function theorem shows that \(r(s)\) is twice continuously differentiable locally, including by local extension at boundary points of the cube. In particular, it is continuous.

The feasible seed polytope is compact, so spread maximizers exist.

1.2. Every maximizer uses the full budget

Implicit differentiation gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{\partial r}{\partial s}=(I-J)^{-1}E.\)

The inverse equals the entrywise nonnegative series \(\sum_{h\ge0}J^h\), and every diagonal entry of \(E\) is positive. Consequently, \(F\) is strictly increasing in each seed coordinate.

If \(\sum_us_u<k<N\), some coordinate can be increased while remaining feasible, strictly increasing spread. Hence every maximizer satisfies \(\sum_us_u=k\).

1.3. Every maximizer is integral

We prove that \(F\) is strictly convex along every feasible direction \(v=e_i-e_j\), with \(i\ne j\).

Differentiate along \(s+tv\). Using primes for derivatives with respect to \(t\),

\(\displaystyle r'=(I-J)^{-1}Ev.\)

The bounds \(\|(I-J)^{-1}\|_\infty\le2\) and \(\|E-I\|_\infty\le\rho\) imply

\(\displaystyle \|r'\|_\infty\le2,\qquad \|r'-v\|_\infty\le4\rho.\)

For the second inequality, use

\(\displaystyle r'-v=(I-J)^{-1}\bigl((E-I)v+Jv\bigr).\)

A second differentiation gives

\(\displaystyle r''=(I-J)^{-1}h,\qquad h=-D(Ar')^{\odot2}-2E(v\odot Ar').\)

Set \(h_0=-2v\odot Av\). Then \(\|h_0\|_\infty\le2\rho\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \|h-h_0\|_\infty\le4\rho^2+4\rho^2+8\rho^2=16\rho^2.\)

Here the three terms bound, respectively, \(D(Ar')^{\odot2}\), \(2(E-I)(v\odot Ar')\), and \(2v\odot A(r'-v)\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle \|r''-h_0\|_\infty\le2(16\rho^2+2\rho^2)=36\rho^2.\)

For \(v=e_i-e_j\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_u(h_0)_u=2(A_{ij}+A_{ji}-A_{ii}-A_{jj}).\)

The off-diagonal baseline weights are at least \(1\), while the baseline diagonal vanishes. Thus

\(\displaystyle \sum_u(h_0)_u\ge4\delta-2(U_{ii}+U_{jj})\ge4\delta-\frac{2\delta}{1000}.\)

Combining the bounds yields

\(\displaystyle F''\ge4\delta-\frac{2\delta}{1000}-36N\rho^2 \ge4\delta-\frac{2\delta}{1000}-\frac{144\delta}{1000}>0.\)

Now suppose a maximizer were nonintegral. Since its coordinate sum is the integer \(k\), it would have at least two fractional coordinates. A sufficiently small transfer between those coordinates is feasible in both directions. Strict convexity along that transfer implies that one of the two perturbed allocations has greater spread, a contradiction.

Thus every exact maximizer is integral.

1.4. Exact maximizers also maximize the baseline cut

The fixed-point equation gives

\(\displaystyle 0\le r_u-s_u=(1-s_u)\bigl(1-e^{-(Ar)_u}\bigr)\le\rho.\)

This already proves the lemma’s bound on an unseeded coordinate.

For \(0\le a\le\rho\), the exponential remainder satisfies \(|1-e^{-a}-a|\le a^2/2\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \left|r_u-s_u-(1-s_u)(As)_u\right|\le\frac32\rho^2.\)

To verify this bound, replacing \(Ar\) by \(As\) costs at most \(\|A(r-s)\|_\infty\le\rho^2\), and the exponential remainder costs at most \(\rho^2/2\).

For an integral seed set \(S\) of size \(k\), it follows that

\(\displaystyle F(\mathbf1_S)=k+\delta C_W(S)+C_U(S)+e_S,\)

where

\(\displaystyle C_U(S)=\sum_{t\in S,\ u\notin S}U_{ut},\qquad 0\le C_U(S)\le\frac{\delta}{1000},\)

and

\(\displaystyle |e_S|\le\frac32N\rho^2\le6N\delta^2Q^2\le\frac{6\delta}{1000}.\)

Every \(C_W(S)\) is a half-integer. Therefore, if \(S\) does not maximize \(C_W\), some set \(S^*\) of size \(k\) satisfies \(C_W(S^*)-C_W(S)\ge1/2\). For these sets,

\(\displaystyle F(\mathbf1_{S^*})-F(\mathbf1_S) \ge\frac{\delta}{2}-\frac{\delta}{1000}-\frac{12\delta}{1000}>0.\)

Every exact spread maximizer therefore maximizes the baseline cut. This completes the lemma.

2. Encoding Independent Set in a distinguished seed

We reduce from Independent Set: given a simple undirected graph \(G\) on \(n\ge1\) vertices and an integer \(1\le K\le n\), decide whether its independence number \(\alpha(G)\) satisfies \(\alpha(G)\ge K\).

This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. For example, it follows from the classical completeness of Node Cover, since the complement of an independent set is a vertex cover. [Karp’s original completeness results](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Add two vertices \(s\) and \(a\). Let \(H\) retain all edges of \(G\), connect \(a\) to every vertex of \(G\) and to \(s\), and give \(s\) no neighbors in \(G\). Put \(d=n+2\).

Assign vertex rewards

\(\displaystyle \omega_i=1\quad(i\in V(G)\cup\{s\}),\qquad \omega_a=K+\frac12.\)

Set \(M=4(n+1)\). For a binary vector \(b\) on \(V(H)\), define

\(\displaystyle \Psi(b)=\sum_i\omega_i b_i-M\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(H)}b_ib_j.\)

The total vertex reward is at most \(2n+3/2<M\). Any selected set containing an edge therefore has negative objective, while the empty set has objective zero. Every maximizer of \(\Psi\) is consequently an independent set.

An independent set containing \(a\) can contain no other vertex of \(H\), so its best reward is \(K+1/2\). An independent set excluding \(a\) obtains its best reward by selecting \(s\) and a maximum independent set of \(G\), giving reward \(1+\alpha(G)\).

These rewards differ because one is a half-integer and the other is an integer. Thus:

  • if \(\alpha(G)\ge K\), every maximizer of \(\Psi\) selects \(s\);
  • if \(\alpha(G)\le K-1\), every maximizer of \(\Psi\) excludes \(s\).

We next realize this objective through a fixed-cardinality directed cut.

3. A half-integral directed-cut construction

Fix the prescribed constant \(\varepsilon\), and write

\(\displaystyle \gamma=\frac12-\varepsilon>0.\)

Choose the smallest power of two \(N\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle N-1\ge\frac{d+1}{\gamma}.\)

Add \(h=N-d-2\) filler vertices, and two further vertices \(p,q\). The resulting set \(T\) has cardinality \(N\). Let

\(\displaystyle O=V(H)\cup\{\text{filler vertices}\},\qquad k=h+1.\)

The choice of \(N\) implies \(h\ge d\). Give every filler reward zero and extend \(\Psi\) to \(O\), keeping penalties only for edges of \(H\).

Every subset of \(V(H)\) can be extended to exactly \(h\) selected ordinary vertices using fillers: if it selects \(j\le d\) vertices, choose \(h-j\) fillers. Therefore, maximizing \(\Psi\) over exactly \(h\) selected vertices of \(O\) has precisely the distinguished-vertex behavior established above.

3.1. Directed weights

For \(i\in O\), let

\(\displaystyle g_i=\frac M2\deg_H(i),\)

taking the degree to be zero for fillers. Put \(D_0=n+2\), and define

\(\displaystyle c_i=g_i-\omega_i+D_0\ge0.\)

Construct a nonnegative directed matrix \(P\) as follows:

  • for each \(\{i,j\}\in E(H)\), set \(P_{ij}=P_{ji}=M/2\);
  • for each \(i\in O\), set \(P_{pi}=c_i\).

Let

\(\displaystyle B_0=1+M|E(H)|+\sum_{i\in O}c_i,\)

and additionally set \(P_{pq}=B_0\). All other entries of \(P\) are zero.

Finally, define

\(\displaystyle W_{tt}=0,\qquad W_{tu}=1+P_{tu}\quad(t\ne u).\)

These weights satisfy all the structural hypotheses of the small-contact lemma.

3.2. Every maximum cut selects \(p\) and excludes \(q\)

For any seed set \(S\) of cardinality \(k\), the uniform off-diagonal part contributes exactly \(k(N-k)\). Thus maximizing \(C_W\) is equivalent to maximizing the cut contribution \(C_P\).

If \(p\notin S\) or \(q\in S\), the edge \(p\to q\) contributes nothing. All remaining entries of \(P\) have total weight \(B_0-1\), so \(C_P(S)\le B_0-1\).

There exists a set of cardinality \(k\) containing \(p\) and excluding \(q\), for example \(p\) together with all \(h\) fillers. Every such set earns at least \(B_0\) from \(p\to q\).

Therefore, every maximum cut has \(p\in S\) and \(q\notin S\).

3.3. The remaining objective is exactly \(\Psi\)

Under those forced choices, exactly \(h\) ordinary vertices are selected. Let their indicators be \(b_i\). Expanding their cut contribution gives

\(\displaystyle C_P(S)=B_0+\sum_{i\in O}c_i(1-b_i) +\frac M2\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(H)} \bigl(b_i(1-b_j)+b_j(1-b_i)\bigr). \)

Collecting linear and quadratic terms,

\(\displaystyle C_P(S)=B_0+\sum_{i\in O}c_i +\sum_{i\in O}(g_i-c_i)b_i -M\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(H)}b_ib_j. \)

Since \(g_i-c_i=\omega_i-D_0\) and \(\sum_i b_i=h\),

\(\displaystyle C_P(S)=B_0+\sum_{i\in O}c_i-D_0h+\Psi(b).\)

The terms preceding \(\Psi(b)\) are constant. Hence every maximum directed cut selects \(s\) exactly in the Independent Set YES case:

\(\displaystyle \alpha(G)\ge K\ \Longrightarrow\ s\in S \quad\text{for every maximum cut}, \)

\(\displaystyle \alpha(G)\le K-1\ \Longrightarrow\ s\notin S \quad\text{for every maximum cut}. \)

All entries of \(W\) are half-integers, as required by the lemma.

4. The continuous society instance

Give every type equal mass \(\mu_t=1/N\). Since \(N\) is a power of two, these masses are dyadic.

Choose

\(\displaystyle Q=\left\lceil\max_u\sum_tW_{tu}\right\rceil,\qquad \delta=\frac{\gamma}{1000NQ^2}.\)

Set the baseline rates, budgets, and threshold to

\(\displaystyle \lambda^0_{tu}=N\delta W_{tu},\qquad \kappa=\frac{k}{N},\qquad \beta=\frac{\delta}{1000N},\qquad \theta=\frac12. \)

In particular, \(\beta>0\). The communities are

\(\displaystyle C_1=\{s\},\qquad C_2=T\setminus\{s\}.\)

4.1. Every feasible addition satisfies the lemma

For any feasible real link addition \(z\),

\(\displaystyle \frac1{N^2}\sum_{t,u}z_{tu}\le\beta.\)

Define \(U_{ut}=z_{tu}/N\). Then

\(\displaystyle \sum_{u,t}U_{ut}\le N\beta=\frac{\delta}{1000}.\)

The cascade’s contact matrix is exactly

\(\displaystyle A_{ut}=\mu_t(\lambda^0_{tu}+z_{tu}) =\delta W_{tu}+U_{ut}. \)

Also, writing \(s_t=x_t/\mu_t=Nx_t\), seed feasibility becomes

\(\displaystyle 0\le s_t\le1,\qquad \sum_t s_t\le k.\)

Maximizing weighted spread is equivalent to maximizing \(F(s)\), because weighted spread equals \(F(s)/N\).

The small-contact lemma applies for every feasible real \(z\). Consequently:

  • \(X^*(z)\) is nonempty, verifying the existence promise;
  • every exact spread maximizer fully seeds exactly \(k\) types;
  • its seed set maximizes the same baseline cut \(C_W\).

Thus every exact spread maximizer has the distinguished seed status established in Section 3, independently of the chosen feasible link addition.

4.2. Independent Set YES instances satisfy the upper promise

Suppose \(\alpha(G)\ge K\). Every exact spread maximizer seeds \(s\), so \(R_s(x,z)=1\).

It seeds \(k-1=h\) other types. Since cascade adoption never decreases below seeding, the coverage of the second community is at least

\(\displaystyle \frac{h}{N-1} =1-\frac{d+1}{N-1} \ge1-\gamma =\frac12+\varepsilon. \)

Therefore, for every feasible \(z\) and every \(x\in X^*(z)\), minimum community coverage is at least \(\theta+\varepsilon\). Taking the prescribed infimum and supremum gives

\(\displaystyle V\ge\theta+\varepsilon.\)

4.3. Independent Set NO instances satisfy the lower promise

Suppose \(\alpha(G)\le K-1\). Every exact spread maximizer leaves \(s\) unseeded.

The lemma bounds its eventual coverage by

\(\displaystyle R_s(x,z)\le2\delta Q =\frac{2\gamma}{1000NQ} \le\gamma =\frac12-\varepsilon. \)

Minimum community coverage is at most the coverage of \(C_1=\{s\}\). Hence, for every feasible \(z\) and every exact spread maximizer,

\(\displaystyle \min_j\operatorname{coverage}(C_j)\le\theta-\varepsilon.\)

It follows that

\(\displaystyle V\le\theta-\varepsilon.\)

Both types of output instance therefore satisfy the required gap promise.

5. Polynomial encoding and conclusion

For fixed \(\varepsilon\), the quantity \(\gamma\) is a positive constant, and the chosen power of two satisfies

\(\displaystyle N<2\left(1+\frac{d+1}{\gamma}\right).\)

Thus \(N\) is linear in \(n\), with a constant depending only on \(\varepsilon\). The rewards, penalties, directed weights, and \(Q\) have magnitudes bounded by polynomials in \(n\). All constructed rational numbers have polynomial binary encoding length, and the full list of \(N^2\) contact rates can be produced in polynomial time.

The construction has two nonempty communities, finite nonnegative rational baseline rates, positive dyadic masses summing to one, a valid positive seed budget, a strictly positive rational link budget, and an admissible threshold \(\theta=1/2\). The existence promise holds for every feasible link addition.

The reduction maps Independent Set YES instances to \(V\ge\theta+\varepsilon\), and NO instances to \(V\le\theta-\varepsilon\). Therefore the specified continuous gap decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. A running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), for fixed \(\varepsilon\), would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
