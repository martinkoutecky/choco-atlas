Theorem 7 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two disjoint stars \(G=(V,E)\), finitely many complete additive valuation types \(\Theta\) with \(u_\theta(v)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(s_v\), and threshold \(t\), decide whether there are nonnegative masses \(x_{\theta,B}\) over nonempty connected bundles \(B\subseteq V\) such that \(\sum_B x_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) for every \(\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,B:v\in B}x_{\theta,B}=s_v\) for every \(v\), and \(x_{\theta,B}>0\) only when \(\sum_{v\in B}u_\theta(v)\ge t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a simple undirected graph consisting of exactly two nonempty star components, and put \(m=|V|\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type specifies complete additive valuations \(u_\theta(v)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\), without utility normalization. Supplies \(s_v\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) and a threshold \(t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) are given in binary; supplies need not sum to \(1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Let \(\mathcal B=\{B\subseteq V:B\ne\varnothing,\ G[B]\text{ is connected}\}\). An allocation assigns real masses \(x_{\theta,B}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}x_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) for every type and \(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{B\in\mathcal B:v\in B}x_{\theta,B}=s_v\) for every vertex.
Require \(x_{\theta,B}>0\Rightarrow\sum_{v\in B}u_\theta(v)\ge t\): every assigned bundle meets the threshold, and all supplies are exhausted.

Is deciding whether such an allocation exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard?

For finitely many agents, deciding whether a partition of all vertices into nonempty connected bundles gives every agent utility at least the threshold is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard on two disjoint stars and polynomial-time solvable on one star, with nonnegative integer valuations ([Bandopadhyay et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18703v1)).
No result settling exactly this mass-allocation problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The threshold's domain was unspecified; use a nonnegative binary rational, extending the discrete nonnegative integer threshold.
  • The supplies' signs were unspecified; use nonnegative binary rationals with no sum normalization, since supplies measure vertex consumption rather than population shares.
  • The allocation masses' domain was unspecified; allow nonnegative real masses.
  • Remove zero-mass types from the input support, following the required society encoding.
  • Interpret two disjoint stars as exactly two nonempty components; singleton stars are permitted because no minimum leaf count is imposed.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete, even when \(\tau=1\), all valuations and the threshold are integers, and every supply belongs to \(\{1/4,1/2\}\). In particular, both star components have positive supplies.

Reduction from \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\)

We use the standard \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete problem \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\): given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), encoded in binary, decide whether some \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i\in S}a_i=\frac{A}{2},\qquad A:=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i.\)

The source problem is one of the classical complete problems in [Karp’s *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Construct two disjoint stars:

  • The first has center \(c\) and leaves \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\).
  • The second is the edge \(\{d,e\}\), viewed as a star with center \(d\).

There is one type, of mass \(\mu=1\). Set

\(\displaystyle u(c)=u(e)=0,\qquad u(v_i)=2a_i,\qquad u(d)=A,\qquad t=A.\)

Set the supplies to

\(\displaystyle s_c=s_d=s_e=\frac12,\qquad s_{v_i}=\frac14\quad(1\le i\le n).\)

These specify all valuations and supplies. The graph has \(m=n+3\) vertices and exactly two nonempty star components. The construction has polynomial bit length: computing \(A\) and doubling the input integers require polynomial time.

Write \(x_B\) for the mass assigned to bundle \(B\), suppressing the sole type index.

Correctness of the reduction

A partition yields a feasible allocation

Suppose \(S\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i=A/2\). Assign mass \(1/4\) to each of

\(\displaystyle B_S=\{c\}\cup\{v_i:i\in S\},\qquad B_{\overline S}=\{c\}\cup\{v_i:i\notin S\},\)

and mass \(1/2\) to \(\{d,e\}\). Assign zero mass to all other bundles.

Each bundle is nonempty and connected. Their utilities are

\(\displaystyle u(B_S)=u(B_{\overline S})=A,\qquad u(\{d,e\})=A.\)

The assigned masses sum to \(1\). The center \(c\) receives total mass \(1/2\), each \(v_i\) receives mass \(1/4\), and \(d,e\) each receive mass \(1/2\). Thus every supply is exhausted and every assigned bundle meets the threshold.

A feasible allocation yields a partition

Suppose a feasible allocation exists. Because the two stars are distinct connected components, a connected bundle contains at most one of \(c,d\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle q(B):=|B\cap\{c,d\}|\in\{0,1\}.\)

The total assigned mass and the center supply equations give

\(\displaystyle \sum_B x_B=1,\qquad \sum_B x_Bq(B)=s_c+s_d=1.\)

Subtracting,

\(\displaystyle \sum_B x_B(1-q(B))=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore every positively assigned bundle contains exactly one of \(c,d\).

Let \(\mathcal C\) denote the connected bundles containing \(c\). Every such bundle lies in the first star. The center supply equation gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{B\in\mathcal C}x_B=\frac12.\)

Moreover, every positively assigned bundle containing \(v_i\) must contain \(c\): it lies in the first component and must contain one of the two designated centers. Hence

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\substack{B\in\mathcal C\\v_i\in B}}x_B=\frac14.\)

Using additivity and these marginal equations,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{B\in\mathcal C}x_Bu(B) =\sum_{i=1}^n 2a_i \sum_{\substack{B\in\mathcal C\\v_i\in B}}x_B =\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{2a_i}{4} =\frac A2. \)

Every positively assigned bundle meets threshold \(A\). Thus

\(\displaystyle \sum_{B\in\mathcal C}x_B\bigl(u(B)-A\bigr) =\frac A2-A\cdot\frac12 =0. \)

Again, every summand with positive mass is nonnegative. It follows that every positively assigned bundle in \(\mathcal C\) has utility exactly \(A\).

Such a bundle exists because its total mass is \(1/2\). Choose one and write it as \(\{c\}\cup\{v_i:i\in S\}\). Then

\(\displaystyle 2\sum_{i\in S}a_i=u(B)=A,\)

which gives the required partition.

This proves a polynomial-time many-one reduction and establishes \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness.

Membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\)

Real allocation masses do not prevent polynomial-size certificates.

For a general instance, keep only pairs \((\theta,B)\) for which \(B\) is nonempty, connected, and satisfies the threshold for type \(\theta\). The feasibility equations become

\(\displaystyle Mx=b,\qquad x\ge0,\)

where \(b\) consists of the \(\tau\) type masses and the \(m\) supplies. Each column of \(M\) has one \(1\) in its type row and the bundle’s incidence vector in its vertex rows. Thus \(M\) is a finite matrix with entries in \(\{0,1\}\) and \(r:=m+\tau\) rows.

Assume the system is feasible, and choose a feasible solution with the fewest positive coordinates. Its supported columns are linearly independent. Indeed, otherwise there is a nonzero vector \(z\), supported on those coordinates, with \(Mz=0\). Summing the type rows yields \(\sum_j z_j=0\), so \(z\) has both positive and negative coordinates. With

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=\min_{j:z_j>0}\frac{x_j}{z_j},\)

the vector \(x-\varepsilon z\) remains nonnegative and feasible and has fewer positive coordinates, a contradiction.

The support therefore has size \(k\le r\). Select \(k\) rows on which those columns form an invertible \(k\times k\) matrix \(N\). The positive coordinates satisfy \(Nx_{\mathrm{supp}}=b'\).

To bound their encoding length, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators appearing in \(b\), and set \(H=\max(1,\max_i|Db_i|)\). Both \(D\) and \(H\) have bit length \(O(L)\). Cramer’s rule expresses each coordinate as an integer numerator divided by \(D\det N\). The determinant expansion gives

\(\displaystyle |\det N|\le k!,\qquad |\text{numerator}|\le k!H.\)

Thus each coordinate is rational with bit length \(O(L+k\log k)\).

A certificate lists these at most \(m+\tau\) type-bundle pairs and their rational masses. Each bundle takes \(m\) incidence bits. A verifier checks connectivity, the threshold inequalities, nonnegativity, and all allocation equations using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

Therefore the problem belongs to \(\mathsf{NP}\), completing the proof of \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness.
