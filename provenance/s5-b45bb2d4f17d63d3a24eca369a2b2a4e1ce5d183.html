The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a known population scale \(N\), an online stream of cohorts over finitely many availability and entry-position types \(t\), and rational cohort fractions \(\mu_t\), can a causal mechanism choose one available vehicle to lead and settle transfers on each segment \(J\) of constant availability so every vehicle’s normalized net chore burden is \(|J|/A_J\), where \(A_J=\sum_{t:\,J\subseteq[a_t,d_t)}\mu_t\), while attaining the Payment Transfer mechanism’s zero-rotation efficiency? On each such segment, followers pay \(u|J|/(N A_J)\), and the leader receives the total; the leader’s net burden and each follower’s payment, multiplied by \(N/u\), should give each vehicle accumulated burden \(\int_{a_t}^{d_t} ds/A(s)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Type \(t\) specifies rational availability \([a_t,d_t)\), with \(0\leq a_t<d_t\), and entry at the front, middle, or rear.
Each cohort is an atomless population of labeled vehicles of measure \(\mu_t\), with a specified internal order and foremost vehicle. The positive integer population scale \(N\) is known; aggregate vehicle quantity is measured by \(N\,d\mu\), without integrality restrictions on \(N\mu_t\). Cohort data are revealed truthfully at arrival; future arrivals remain unknown. Simultaneous events have a specified order.
Exactly one available vehicle leads whenever availability is nonempty. Every follower earns the same positive rational utility \(u\) per unit time; the leader earns zero before transfers. A rotation moves the current leader to the rear while it remains available and incurs positive cost. Front entry and leader departure allow costless leader replacement; the required schedule makes no rotations and otherwise retains the leader.
Let \(J\) be a maximal segment of constant availability, and let \(A_J=\sum_{t:J\subseteq[a_t,d_t)}\mu_t\). At its end, each follower pays \(p_J=u|J|/(NA_J)\); payments are aggregated using \(N\,d\mu\) over the followers and transferred entirely to the single leader, with no external subsidy.
A follower's monetary burden is its payment; the leader's is its forgone utility \(u|J|\) minus its receipts. Fairness requires every vehicle, including the selected leader, to have monetary burden multiplied by \(N/u\) equal to \(|J|/A_J\) on each segment, hence accumulated normalized burden \(\int_{a_t}^{d_t}ds/A(s)\), where \(A(s)=\sum_{t:a_t\leq s<d_t}\mu_t\). The requirement is pointwise, not merely almost everywhere or in expectation.
Does a causal mechanism guarantee these transfers, pointwise fairness, and zero rotations for every admissible stream, or is this guarantee impossible? If such a mechanism exists, can it be implemented in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), where \(m\) is the number of distinct arrival/departure times and \(L\) is the total binary input length, including \(N\) and \(u\)?
For finite vehicles, centralized Payment Transfer achieves equitability and optimal efficiency without rotations, using follower payments \(u|J|/n_J\). [Primary source](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~pstone/Papers/bib2html-links/Convoy-Yedidsion.pdf). No result settling the atomless, pointwise formulation above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized cohort fractions to sum to one; took N to be a known positive integer scale and imposed no integrality restriction on cohort quantities.
  • Interpreted the mass society as atomless within each type and the single leader as a labeled individual; aggregate payments use N times population measure, excluding that individual from the follower set.
  • Required fairness pointwise, including the leader, because the question requires every vehicle's burden rather than an almost-everywhere or expected guarantee.
  • Made availability endpoints rational and half-open, with positive durations, homogeneous positive utility, zero direct leading utility, and centralized budget-balanced transfers.
  • Retained the permitted costless leader changes at front entry and leader departure; otherwise the incumbent remains leader, and rotations are forbidden.
  • Allowed simultaneous cohort events with a specified order and supplied within-cohort ordering, replacing the discrete distinct-arrival assumption that cohorts cannot satisfy.
  • Specified truthful revelation at arrival and settlement at segment end, so unknown future arrivals do not become offline information.
  • Defined m as the number of distinct event times and L as the complete binary input length.

Conclusion

The guarantee is impossible. On every positive-length segment with nonempty availability, the prescribed transfers give the single leader zero monetary burden, whereas pointwise fairness requires a strictly positive burden. This obstruction holds even with complete advance knowledge of the stream.

Segment-level impossibility proof

Let the labeled population carry the atomless measure \(\mu\) specified in the question. Each cohort has its prescribed mass, and aggregate vehicle quantity is measured by \(N\,d\mu\). In particular, every individual vehicle \(\ell\) satisfies \(\mu(\{\ell\})=0\): a measurable singleton of positive measure would be an atom.

Fix a maximal segment \(J\) of constant, nonempty availability and positive length. Write \(h=|J|>0\), and let \(V_J\) be its available population. Then \(\mu(V_J)=A_J>0\).

Under the required schedule, the leader is constant throughout the interior of \(J\). Indeed, availability changes only at segment boundaries, and the schedule makes no rotations and otherwise retains the leader. Denote this leader by \(\ell\), and let \(F_J=V_J\setminus\{\ell\}\) be the followers.

Step 1: Removing the leader does not change follower mass.

By atomlessness,

\[ \]
\(\mu(F_J)=\mu(V_J)-\mu(\{\ell\})=A_J.\)

Consequently, the aggregate follower quantity is \(NA_J\). This remains true for every finite positive integer \(N\).

Step 2: The prescribed payments determine the leader’s receipts.

Every follower pays \(p_J=uh/(NA_J)\). Aggregating exactly as required gives total receipts

\(R_J=\int_{F_J}p_J\,N\,d\mu =\frac{uh}{NA_J}\,N\mu(F_J) =uh.\)

The requirement that all payments be transferred to the single leader therefore fixes its receipts at \(uh\).

Step 3: The leader’s burden contradicts pointwise fairness.

The leader’s monetary burden is its forgone utility minus its receipts:

\(B_J(\ell)=uh-R_J=0.\)

Its normalized burden is consequently \((N/u)B_J(\ell)=0\). Pointwise fairness instead requires

\(\frac{N}{u}B_J(\ell)=\frac{h}{A_J}>0.\)

These equalities are incompatible.

For comparison, every follower has the required normalized burden:

\(\frac{N}{u}p_J=\frac{h}{A_J}.\)

Thus the failure occurs precisely at the selected leader, whose zero population measure does not exempt it from the explicitly pointwise requirement.

Every admissible society has a positive-length segment with nonempty availability: choose any type’s positive-length availability interval and partition it at the finitely many arrival and departure times. Hence the contradiction applies to every admissible stream under the required schedule.

A concrete instance with overlapping cohorts

The obstruction already holds with two distinct types. Take \(\tau=2\), \(\mu_1=\mu_2=1/2\), \(N=2\), and \(u=1\). Let the first cohort be available on \([0,2)\), entering at the rear, and the second on \([1,3)\), entering at the front. Give each cohort any admissible internal order and foremost vehicle.

On \(J=[1,2)\), both cohorts are available, so \(A_J=1\). The foremost vehicle of the second cohort leads throughout this segment under the required schedule.

Each follower pays \(p_J=1/2\). Removing the leader leaves aggregate follower quantity \(NA_J=2\), so total receipts are \(1\). The leader forgoes utility \(1\), giving burden zero; its required normalized burden is \(|J|/A_J=1\).

This is a failure on an overlapping segment with multiple types, independent of simultaneous-event conventions.

Why the finite-vehicle result does not extend

For a finite population of \(n\) available vehicles, there are exactly \(n-1\) followers. With payment \(uh/n\), the leader receives \((n-1)uh/n\), leaving burden \(uh/n\), equal to each follower’s payment. This is the calculation underlying the finite Payment Transfer theorem. [Yedidsion, Alkoby, and Stone, Section 6.1 and Appendix B](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~pstone/Papers/bib2html-links/Convoy-Yedidsion.pdf).

In the present model, excluding one leader removes zero quantity under \(N\,d\mu\), so the follower quantity remains \(NA_J\). Replacing it by \(NA_J-1\) would change the stipulated aggregation rule.

The impossibility is therefore algebraic, preceding any issue of causality or computational complexity. No mechanism with any running time can provide the requested guarantee, so the conditional polynomial-time implementation question does not arise.
