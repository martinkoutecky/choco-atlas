The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) alternatives, \(k\), a panel of \(M\) top-\(k\) ballots encoded by its empirical distribution \(\mu\) over supported ballot types \(t\), and the promise that the ballots were independently drawn from a two-component concentric Mallows mixture with shared center \(\sigma_0\), ask whether sorting \(B_\mu(c)=\sum_t\mu_t b_t(c)\) recovers \(\sigma_0\) with the probability guaranteed by Corollary 8 when the non-expert draw count meets its stated sample-size condition.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=n>3\) alternatives and \(1\leq k<m\) be given. A ballot type is a strict ordered list of \(k\) distinct alternatives; define \(b_t(c)=k+1-j\) if \(c\) occupies position \(j\), and \(b_t(c)=0\) if it is absent.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; masses sum to \(1\), and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
The input also contains a binary integer \(M\geq1\), with \(M\mu_t\) integral for every supported type. It represents the empirical distribution of \(M\) independent ballots.
Each ballot is the top-\(k\) prefix of a complete Kendall Mallows ranking from \(rM(\sigma_0,\theta_g)+(1-r)M(\sigma_0,\theta_b)\), where \(0<r<1\), \(0<\theta_b<\theta_g<\infty\), and the common strict center \(\sigma_0\) is unknown.
For the non-expert component, let \(\Delta=\Pr[\operatorname{rank}(a_1)\leq k]-\Pr[\operatorname{rank}(a_2)\leq k]\), where \(a_1,a_2\) are the first two alternatives in \(\sigma_0\), and put \(D=k^2(1-e^{-\theta_b})^2/(1-e^{-m\theta_b})-m\Delta\).
For \(0<\epsilon<1\) and \(D\neq0\), the stated sampling condition is that each component contributes at least \(2m^2\log(\epsilon^{-1})D^{-2}\) ballots. Recovery probability concerns repeated sampling conditional on the component labels, not a property certified by one realized society.
Define \(\widehat\sigma\) by sorting \(B_\mu(c)=\sum_t\mu_t b_t(c)\) in decreasing order, breaking ties by increasing alternative label.

Is computing \(\widehat\sigma\) with the stated recovery guarantee polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L,k,\log M\), or is it NP-hard?

The discrete result claims high-probability recovery by Borda under the stated sampling threshold; the published version requires sufficiently many ballots in both components. [Published result and supplement](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/collas21a.html)
The empirical-distribution formulation retains exactly the sampled panel and its scores, so this existing recovery claim already concerns the same statistical experiment; it does not supply a genuinely open continuous problem.

Conclusion

Computing \(\widehat{\sigma}\) is polynomial-time solvable in the specified input parameters. However, the literal sampling threshold in the question does not guarantee recovery of the complete center under all the stated hypotheses.

These are separate conclusions: the empirical representation preserves Borda scores exactly, but it also preserves any failure of the proposed statistical guarantee.

1. Exact polynomial-time computation

Write each supported mass as \(\mu_t=u_t/v_t\), with integers encoded in binary. Define its multiplicity by

\[ w_t=\frac{Mu_t}{v_t}. \]

By hypothesis, each \(w_t\) is an integer. Positivity and normalization imply \(0<w_t\leq M\) and \(\sum_t w_t=M\).

For each alternative, initialize an integer score \(S(c)=0\). For every supported ballot \(t=(c_{t,1},\ldots,c_{t,k})\), perform the updates

\[ S(c_{t,j})\leftarrow S(c_{t,j})+w_t(k+1-j), \qquad 1\leq j\leq k. \]

Then

\[ S(c)=\sum_t w_t b_t(c)=M B_\mu(c). \]

Because \(M>0\), sorting by decreasing \(S(c)\), with increasing labels breaking ties, produces exactly \(\widehat{\sigma}\).

To verify the bit complexity, put \(h=\lfloor\log_2 M\rfloor+1\). Computing each \(w_t\) requires multiplication and exact division on integers of at most \(O(L+h)\) bits. Moreover,

\[ 0\leq S(c)\leq kM, \]

so every accumulated score has \(O(h+\log(k+1))\) bits. There are \(\tau k\) updates and \(O(m\log m)\) sorting comparisons. Ordinary binary integer arithmetic therefore gives a polynomial running time in \(m,\tau,L,k,\log M\).

In particular, the algorithm never expands the support into \(M\) individual ballots.

2. What the empirical representation preserves

Let \(T_1,\ldots,T_M\) denote the sampled ballot types. Their empirical masses satisfy, for every realized panel,

\[ M B_\mu(c)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}b_{T_\ell}(c). \]

Thus the ranking computed from the empirical society equals the ranking computed from the sampled panel, including the prescribed tie-breaking. Consequently, their recovery events have identical probabilities, also conditional on the component labels.

This proves that any valid discrete recovery theorem transfers unchanged to this empirical representation. It does not independently establish the sampling bound. The published paper states a pairwise Borda bound in Theorem 7 and a full-center recovery claim requiring sufficiently many samples in both components in Corollary 8. [Published paper, Section 4.2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/collas21a/collas21a.pdf)

The exact threshold posed here can nevertheless be refuted directly.

3. Counterexample to the stated recovery guarantee

Choose

\[ m=n=4,\qquad k=2,\qquad \sigma_0=(1,2,4,3), \]

and parameters

\[ r=\frac12,\qquad \theta_b=\log 1000,\qquad \theta_g=\log 2000,\qquad \epsilon=\frac1{10}. \]

Let \(M=12\), and condition on any component-label sequence containing six ballots from each component. This conditioning event has positive probability, and the ballots remain independent conditional on their labels.

Bounding the threshold

Write \(q=e^{-\theta}\). For a Kendall Mallows model on four alternatives, the normalizing constant is

\[ Z_4(q)=(1+q)(1+q+q^2)(1+q+q^2+q^3). \]

Indeed, inserting the alternative last in the center order into a ranking of the preceding alternatives contributes each possible number of new inversions once; iterating this insertion proves the product formula.

Each factor is at most \((1-q)^{-1}\). Hence the probability of the complete center satisfies

\[ \Pr[\text{complete ranking}=\sigma_0] =\frac1{Z_4(q)} \geq(1-q)^3 \geq1-3q. \]

For the non-expert component, \(q_b=1/1000\). Since its second center alternative is present in the center’s prefix,

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta &=\Pr[\operatorname{rank}(a_1)\leq2] -\Pr[\operatorname{rank}(a_2)\leq2]\\ &\leq\Pr[\operatorname{rank}(a_2)>2]\\ &\leq\Pr[\text{complete ranking}\neq\sigma_0]\\ &\leq3q_b. \end{aligned} \]

Therefore,

\[ \begin{aligned} D &=\frac{4(1-q_b)^2}{1-q_b^4}-4\Delta\\ &\geq4(1-q_b)^2-12q_b\\ &=3.980004 >3.9. \end{aligned} \]

In particular, \(D\neq0\). Using \(\log 10<5/2\), the stated component threshold satisfies

\[ 2m^2\log(\epsilon^{-1})D^{-2} =\frac{32\log 10}{D^2} <\frac{80}{(3.9)^2} <6. \]

Thus six ballots from each component satisfy the stated condition.

Bounding the recovery probability

In both components, \(q\leq1/1000\), so each complete ranking equals \(\sigma_0\) with probability at least \(1-3/1000\).

Let \(F\) be the event that all twelve complete rankings equal \(\sigma_0\). A union bound, conditional on the fixed component labels, gives

\[ \Pr[F\mid\text{component labels}] \geq1-\frac{36}{1000} =0.964. \]

On \(F\), every observed ballot is \((1,2)\). The empirical society therefore has support size one, mass one, and scores

\[ B_\mu(1)=2,\qquad B_\mu(2)=1,\qquad B_\mu(3)=B_\mu(4)=0. \]

The stipulated tie-break yields

\[ \widehat{\sigma}=(1,2,3,4)\neq(1,2,4,3)=\sigma_0. \]

Consequently,

\[ \Pr[\widehat{\sigma}=\sigma_0\mid\text{component labels}] \leq0.036, \]

although the proposed guarantee would require probability at least \(1-\epsilon=0.9\).

This is a failure in the repeated-sampling experiment with both component counts above the threshold.

4. Interpretation of the question

The computational problem is settled: exact computation of \(\widehat{\sigma}\) is polynomial-time solvable.

If the discrete recovery guarantee is supplied as an additional assumption, Section 2 proves its transfer to the empirical representation. Under the literal model and threshold, however, Section 3 disproves that guarantee. Failure of the statistical bound does not imply computational hardness.
