The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many advertiser types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), values \(v_t\), and outside-utility thresholds \(a_t\), a rational slot supply per unit mass \(\kappa\), a nonincreasing piecewise-rational click-rate curve \(\lambda\), and a finite rational lottery \(\nu\) over external bid-mass profiles, compute a finite-description randomized bidding policy \(\Gamma\) and transfers \(q_t\geq 0\). A policy assigns each type's mass across bids in \([0,1]\); GSP ranks combined bid mass and allocates ranks up to \(\kappa\), with symmetric assignment within each type so participation is measured per advertiser. Maximize expected total colluder value minus agency payments, subject to expected per-advertiser utility for each type being at least \(a_t-\varepsilon\), aggregate transfers covering expected payments, and an objective value at least \(\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon\). Is there an algorithm polynomial in \(|T|\), the external-lottery and click-curve descriptions, input bit length, and \(1/\varepsilon\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) advertiser types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has binary-rational value \(v_t\in[0,1]\) and outside-utility threshold \(a_t\in[0,1]\).
The input includes a finite rational lottery \(\nu\) over external profiles, each listing rational bids in \([0,1]\) and positive rational masses of common total \(E\); rational slot supply \(0<\kappa\leq1+E\); and a nonincreasing curve \(\lambda:[0,\kappa]\to[0,1]\). Its \(m\) pieces have rational breakpoints and densely encoded rational-function formulas with no poles on their intervals. Let \(D\) be the external-lottery description length and \(L\) the total input bit length.
A bidding profile measurably assigns each type's mass to bids in \([0,1]\); a randomized policy \(\Gamma\) selects a joint profile independently of the external draw. Combined mass is ranked by decreasing bid; colluders precede external mass at equal bids, and the agency specifies the order of tied colluder mass. Assignments are symmetric within each type.
For each realization, let \(b(x)\) be the ranked bid function on \([0,1+E)\), and \(A_t\) the ranks occupied by type \(t\). Mass at rank \(x<\kappa\) receives click rate \(\lambda(x)\) and pays \(\lambda(x)b(x+)\); unallocated mass receives and pays zero. Here \(b(x+)\) is the right-hand limit, with zero beyond total mass.
Define per-advertiser expected revenue and auction payment by \(r_t(\Gamma)=\frac{v_t}{\mu_t}\mathbb E\int_{A_t\cap[0,\kappa]}\lambda(x)\,dx\) and \(p_t(\Gamma)=\frac{1}{\mu_t}\mathbb E\int_{A_t\cap[0,\kappa]}\lambda(x)b(x+)\,dx\). Expectations include both lotteries.
Transfers \(q_t\in[0,1]\) are fixed per advertiser and paid to the agency. Exact feasibility means \(r_t(\Gamma)-q_t\geq a_t\) for every type and \(\sum_t\mu_tq_t\geq\sum_t\mu_tp_t(\Gamma)\). Promise that some deterministic profile satisfies \(r_t-p_t\geq a_t\) for every type.
Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the supremum of \(U(\Gamma)=\sum_t\mu_t(r_t(\Gamma)-p_t(\Gamma))\) over exactly feasible measurable policies and transfers. A finitely encoded output policy is a finite rational lottery over profiles listing rational constant-bid mass blocks, including their tie order.
For every rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), can such a policy and rational transfers be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,D,L,1/\varepsilon\), with \(r_t(\Gamma)-q_t\geq a_t-\varepsilon\) for every type, exact transfer coverage, and \(U(\Gamma)\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon\)?
For finite advertisers, an additive bicriteria FPTAS achieves these participation and objective errors with nonnegative transfers and exact agency payment coverage, under the analogous deterministic-feasibility promise ([Romano et al., 2022](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/0066.pdf)). Result

Yes, for the payment rule stated in the question. There is an algorithm with the required polynomial running time, finite rational output, participation error, exact transfer coverage, and objective guarantee.

The crucial feature of this continuum model is that almost every advertiser pays its own bid. This differs from finite-advertiser GSP, where the payment uses the next advertiser’s bid; see [Romano et al., 2022](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/0066.pdf). The proof below uses the specified continuum rule throughout.

1. A representation by colluder ranks

Extend the click curve to nonnegative ranks by setting \(\bar\lambda(x)=\lambda(x)\) for \(x<\kappa\), and \(\bar\lambda(x)=0\) for \(x\geq\kappa\). This extension is nonincreasing and takes values in \([0,1]\). Its value at \(\kappa\) does not affect any integral.

Write the external lottery as profiles indexed by \(s\), with rational probabilities \(\pi_s\). For a bid \(b\), let \(C_s(b)\) denote the external mass in profile \(s\) bidding strictly more than \(b\).

Parameterize colluder mass, in its specified ranking order, by \(y\in[0,1)\). A deterministic profile is then represented by:

  • a nonincreasing measurable bid function \(\beta(y)\);
  • a measurable type assignment \(t(y)\), with each type occupying mass \(\mu_t\).

In external realization \(s\), colluder mass at coordinate \(y\) occupies rank \(y+C_s(\beta(y))\), almost everywhere. External mass with the same bid comes after all colluder mass at that bid, which explains the strict inequality in the definition of \(C_s\).

Define its expected click rate by

\(\displaystyle h_\beta(y)=\sum_s\pi_s\bar\lambda\bigl(y+C_s(\beta(y))\bigr).\)

Since \(\beta\) is nonincreasing, \(C_s(\beta(y))\) is nondecreasing. Consequently, \(h_\beta\) is nonincreasing.

A bounded monotone ranked bid function has only countably many discontinuities. Therefore \(b(x+)=b(x)\) almost everywhere. Thus, writing \(P\) for aggregate auction payment,

\(\displaystyle r_t=\frac{v_t}{\mu_t}\int_{\{y:t(y)=t\}}h_\beta(y)\,dy,\qquad P=\sum_t\mu_tp_t=\int_0^1\beta(y)h_\beta(y)\,dy.\)

These identities also hold after taking expectations over policies.

In particular, \(0\leq r_t,p_t\leq1\), and \(0\leq P\leq1\). The deterministic-feasibility promise implies exact feasibility: choose \(q_t=p_t\) for its promised profile.

Every exactly feasible policy satisfies

\(\displaystyle 0\leq\sum_t\mu_ta_t\leq U\leq1.\)

The lower bound follows by summing participation inequalities and using transfer coverage. The upper bound follows because aggregate revenue is at most \(1\) and payments are nonnegative. Hence \(0\leq\mathrm{OPT}\leq1\).

2. Restricting bids without loss

Let \(\mathcal B\) consist of \(0\) and every bid appearing in any external profile. Its cardinality is polynomial in \(D\).

For \(z\in[0,1]\), define

\(\displaystyle F(z)=\max\{b\in\mathcal B:b\leq z\}.\)

For every external bid \(e\),

\(\displaystyle e>F(z)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e>z.\)

Indeed, if \(e\leq z\), then \(e\in\mathcal B\) implies \(F(z)\geq e\); the other direction follows from \(F(z)\leq z\).

Replace \(\beta\) by \(F\circ\beta\), retaining the original colluder order when newly created ties occur. Every colluder’s rank in every external realization is unchanged. Consequently, all type revenues remain unchanged, while aggregate payment weakly decreases.

Thus, for every weighted objective with nonnegative revenue coefficients and a nonnegative payment penalty, bids can be restricted to \(\mathcal B\) without loss.

The all-zero-bid profile, denoted by \(\Gamma_0\), has \(P(\Gamma_0)=0\) and \(r_t(\Gamma_0)\geq0\).

3. A polynomial approximate weighted oracle

We construct an oracle for

\(\displaystyle J(\Gamma)=\sum_t\alpha_t r_t(\Gamma)-\beta P(\Gamma),\)

where \(\alpha_t\geq0\), \(\beta\geq0\), \(\sum_t\alpha_t\leq1\), and \(\beta\leq1\).

3.1 Sorting types

For fixed bid function, the weighted revenue density of type \(t\) is

\(\displaystyle c_t=\frac{\alpha_tv_t}{\mu_t}.\)

Order types so that \(c_1\geq\cdots\geq c_\tau\), and place each type contiguously in that order.

This maximizes weighted revenue for the fixed bid function. To verify this, let \(M_i=\sum_{t\leq i}\mu_t\). For any measurable set \(A\) of measure \(M_i\), monotonicity of \(h_\beta\) gives

\(\displaystyle \int_Ah_\beta(y)\,dy\leq\int_0^{M_i}h_\beta(y)\,dy.\)

For example, this follows by comparing the equally sized sets \([0,M_i)\setminus A\) and \(A\setminus[0,M_i)\): every point of the former precedes every point of the latter.

Now express weighted revenue as

\(\displaystyle c_\tau\int_0^1h_\beta(y)\,dy+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}(c_i-c_{i+1})\int_{\{t(y)\leq i\}}h_\beta(y)\,dy.\)

All coefficients in the sum are nonnegative, and each integral is maximized by the contiguous ordering. Aggregate payment does not depend on type labels.

3.2 Equal subdivisions within each type

Fix a positive integer \(K\). Subdivide each contiguous type interval into \(K\) equal blocks of mass \(\mu_t/K\).

First restrict bids to \(\mathcal B\) as above. On each subdivision interval \(I=(l,r)\), replace the bid function by its essential minimum on \(I\). The resulting bids remain nonincreasing and belong to \(\mathcal B\).

Let \(h\) and \(h'\) denote the click functions before and after this replacement. Pointwise almost everywhere, the replacement lowers bids, so

\(\displaystyle h'(y)\leq h(y),\qquad \beta'(y)h'(y)\leq\beta(y)h(y).\)

Therefore payment cannot increase.

Because the original bid function uses finitely many levels, its essential minimum on \(I\) is its bid level immediately before \(r\). Monotonicity of \(\bar\lambda\) then gives \(h'(y)\geq h(r-)\) for almost every \(y\in I\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \int_I(h-h')\,dy\leq |I|\bigl(h(l+)-h(r-)\bigr).\)

Within a type interval, all block lengths equal \(\mu_t/K\). The sum of their variations is at most \(1\), because \(h\) is nonincreasing and bounded in \([0,1]\). Therefore the revenue loss for each type is at most

\(\displaystyle \frac{v_t}{\mu_t}\frac{\mu_t}{K}=\frac{v_t}{K}\leq\frac1K.\)

After sorting, subdivision therefore loses at most \(1/K\) in the weighted objective.

This bound depends on \(K\), not on \(1/\min_t\mu_t\).

3.3 Rational integral bounds

Fix another positive integer \(H\), and write \(h_0=1/H\).

For an interval \([u,u+\ell]\), define the rational lower Riemann sum

\(\displaystyle I^-(u,\ell)=\frac{\ell}{H}\sum_{j=1}^{H}\bar\lambda\left(u+\frac{j\ell}{H}\right).\)

Monotonicity gives

\(\displaystyle 0\leq\int_u^{u+\ell}\bar\lambda(x)\,dx-I^-(u,\ell)\leq\frac{\ell}{H}.\)

The bound follows by subtracting lower sums from upper sums; their difference is

\(\displaystyle \frac{\ell}{H}\bigl(\bar\lambda(u)-\bar\lambda(u+\ell)\bigr)\leq\frac{\ell}{H}.\)

For a constant-bid block starting at colluder coordinate \(y\), of mass \(\ell\) and bid \(b\), use

\(\displaystyle \sum_s\pi_s I^-(y+C_s(b),\ell)\)

as its expected click integral.

Summing these estimates produces rational numbers \(\widehat r_t\) and \(P^-\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle 0\leq r_t-\widehat r_t\leq h_0,\qquad 0\leq P-P^-\leq h_0.\)

The first bound uses the fact that all blocks of type \(t\) have total mass \(\mu_t\). Define the conservative payment estimate

\(\displaystyle \widehat P=P^-+h_0.\)

Then

\(\displaystyle P\leq\widehat P\leq P+h_0.\)

All these quantities are rational. Evaluation of the densely encoded rational functions at rational arguments takes polynomial time and produces polynomial-length rational numbers. Breakpoints can be located directly from their rational list. No symbolic integration is required.

3.4 Dynamic programming

For the sorted type order, there are \(N=\tau K\) blocks. Their starting colluder coordinates are fixed by the ordered masses, independently of their bids.

For each block \(j\) of type \(t\), and each \(b\in\mathcal B\), compute the edge weight

\(\displaystyle \left(\frac{\alpha_tv_t}{\mu_t}-\beta b\right)\sum_s\pi_s I^-(y_j+C_s(b),\mu_t/K).\)

A dynamic program over block index and previous bid maximizes their sum subject to nonincreasing bids. This exactly maximizes \(\sum_t\alpha_t\widehat r_t-\beta\widehat P\) over these profiles: the additional term \(-\beta h_0\) is constant.

For any such profile,

\(\displaystyle 0\leq J-\left(\sum_t\alpha_t\widehat r_t-\beta\widehat P\right)\leq h_0\left(\sum_t\alpha_t+\beta\right)\leq2h_0.\)

Combining bid restriction, sorting, subdivision, and this numerical bound proves the following oracle guarantee:

For every measurable policy \(\Gamma\), the returned rational-block profile \(\sigma\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\alpha_t\widehat r_t(\sigma)-\beta\widehat P(\sigma)\geq J(\Gamma)-\rho,\qquad \rho=\frac1K+\frac2H.\)

For randomized \(\Gamma\), this follows by taking expectations of the deterministic comparison.

4. Constructing a policy by multiplicative weights

Set

\(\displaystyle d=\frac{\varepsilon}{32},\qquad K=\left\lceil\frac{128}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil,\qquad H=\left\lceil\frac{256}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil.\)

Then \(\rho\leq\varepsilon/64=d/2\).

For a rational objective target \(z\in[0,1]\), consider these estimated constraint margins for a profile and transfer vector:

\(\displaystyle f_t=\widehat r_t-q_t-a_t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\quad\text{for each type }t,\)

\(\displaystyle f_C=\sum_t\mu_tq_t-\widehat P-\frac{\varepsilon}{16},\)

\(\displaystyle f_O=\sum_t\mu_t\widehat r_t-\widehat P-z.\)

There are \(n=\tau+2\) margins, and all belong to \([-3,3]\).

Let nonnegative weights \(w_t,w_C,w_O\) sum to \(1\). Maximizing their weighted margin uses revenue and payment coefficients

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t=w_t+w_O\mu_t,\qquad \beta=w_C+w_O.\)

These satisfy the oracle’s hypotheses. The coefficient of transfer \(q_t\) is \(w_C\mu_t-w_t\), so maximizing over \(q_t\in[0,1]\) simply chooses \(q_t=1\) when this coefficient is positive and \(q_t=0\) otherwise.

Consequently, the oracle and this transfer choice approximately maximize the weighted constraint margin over all measurable policies and transfers.

Explicit regret bound

Start with unnormalized weights \(A_i^1=1\). At iteration \(k\), normalize them to \(w_i^k\), obtain a profile and transfers as above, and compute their estimated margins \(f_i^k\). Put \(g_i^k=f_i^k/3\), and update

\(\displaystyle A_i^{k+1}=A_i^k(1-\eta g_i^k),\qquad \eta=\frac d{12}.\)

All weights stay positive because \(|g_i^k|\leq1\) and \(\eta<1/2\).

Run

\(\displaystyle T=\left\lceil\frac{12n}{\eta d}\right\rceil\)

iterations.

Using \(\log(1-x)\leq-x\) and \(\log(1-x)\geq-x-x^2\) for \(|x|\leq1/2\), comparison of the total weight with each individual weight gives

\(\displaystyle \frac1T\sum_{k=1}^{T}f_i^k\geq\frac1T\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_jw_j^kf_j^k-\frac{3\log n}{\eta T}-3\eta.\)

Here \(3\log n/(\eta T)\leq d/4\) and \(3\eta=d/4\).

Suppose there exists a measurable comparator policy and transfers for which the true versions of all three kinds of margin are nonnegative. At every iteration, their weighted true margin is nonnegative, so the oracle guarantee implies \(\sum_jw_j^kf_j^k\geq-\rho\). Thus every averaged estimated margin is at least \(-d\).

Output the uniform lottery over the \(T\) profiles, with fixed transfers

\(\displaystyle \bar q_t=\frac1T\sum_{k=1}^{T}q_t^k.\)

These transfers are rational and belong to \([0,1]\). They are fixed across policy realizations; averaging the margins is valid because revenue, payment, and transfer constraints are linear in these quantities.

5. A comparator with payment-coverage slack

Choose an exactly feasible pair \((\Gamma^*,q^*)\) with

\(\displaystyle U(\Gamma^*)\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\frac{\varepsilon}{16}.\)

Such a pair exists by the definition of supremum and the established nonemptiness.

Let \(\theta=\varepsilon/8\), and define

\(\displaystyle \Gamma'=(1-\theta)\Gamma^*+\theta\Gamma_0,\qquad q_t'=(1-\theta)q_t^*+\frac{\theta}{2}.\)

The transfers remain in \([0,1]\). Since the zero-bid policy has nonnegative revenues and zero payment,

\(\displaystyle r_t(\Gamma')-q_t'\geq(1-\theta)a_t-\frac{\theta}{2}\geq a_t-\frac{3\varepsilon}{16},\)

and

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_tq_t'-P(\Gamma')\geq\frac{\theta}{2}=\frac{\varepsilon}{16}.\)

Also, since \(0\leq U(\Gamma^*)\leq1\) and \(U(\Gamma_0)\geq0\),

\(\displaystyle U(\Gamma')\geq U(\Gamma^*)-\theta\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\frac{3\varepsilon}{16}.\)

Thus the true participation and coverage margins from Section 4 are nonnegative for this comparator. Its objective margin is nonnegative whenever \(z\leq U(\Gamma')\).

Choosing the objective target

Run the procedure for every target

\(\displaystyle z\in\left\{j\frac{\varepsilon}{8}:0\leq j\leq\left\lfloor\frac8\varepsilon\right\rfloor\right\}.\)

At least one target satisfies both \(z\leq U(\Gamma')\) and

\(\displaystyle z\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}.\)

Indeed:

  • If \(\mathrm{OPT}<\varepsilon/4\), use \(z=0\).
  • Otherwise, round \(\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon/4\) down to this grid. The result is at least \(\mathrm{OPT}-3\varepsilon/8\) and at most \(\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon/4<U(\Gamma')\).

For this target, the averaged estimated margins are at least \(-d\). Therefore its output satisfies

\(\displaystyle \widehat r_t-\bar q_t\geq a_t-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-d\geq a_t-\varepsilon,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_t\bar q_t-\widehat P\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{16}-d=\frac{\varepsilon}{32}>0,\)

and

\(\displaystyle \widehat U:=\sum_t\mu_t\widehat r_t-\widehat P\geq z-d\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\frac{13\varepsilon}{32}.\)

The algorithm need not know which target has this property. For each output, check the rational conditions

\(\displaystyle \widehat r_t-\bar q_t\geq a_t-\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }t,\qquad \sum_t\mu_t\bar q_t\geq\widehat P.\)

Among outputs passing these checks, choose one maximizing the rational number \(\widehat U\). The good target’s output passes, so this selection is defined.

Because \(\widehat r_t\leq r_t\), \(\widehat P\geq P\), and \(\widehat U\leq U\), the selected output has

\(\displaystyle r_t-\bar q_t\geq a_t-\varepsilon,\qquad \sum_t\mu_t\bar q_t\geq P,\qquad U\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\frac{13\varepsilon}{32}\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon.\)

In particular, transfer coverage is exact.

6. Encoding and running time

Each oracle output lists at most \(\tau K\) constant-bid blocks. Their masses are rational numbers \(\mu_t/K\), their bids belong to the rational alphabet \(\mathcal B\), and their sequence specifies the colluder tie order.

The selected policy is a rational uniform lottery over \(T\) such profiles, drawn independently of the external realization. Its transfers are rational averages of vectors with coordinates in \(\{0,1\}\).

There are polynomially many objective targets and iterations:

  • \(K,H\) are of order \(1/\varepsilon\).
  • \(T\) is of order \((\tau+2)/\varepsilon^2\).
  • Each dynamic program has polynomially many states and transitions in \(\tau K\) and \(|\mathcal B|\).
  • Each transition uses at most \(H\) rational evaluations per external profile.

All sampling coordinates have polynomial bit length, even when a type mass is very small or \(E\) is large. Rational-function evaluations, sums, dynamic-program comparisons, and weight updates likewise have polynomial bit complexity. Each weight update adds only polynomially many bits, and there are polynomially many updates.

Thus the running time and output length are polynomial in \(m,\tau,D,L,1/\varepsilon\), with the accuracy input’s encoding included in the total input length.
