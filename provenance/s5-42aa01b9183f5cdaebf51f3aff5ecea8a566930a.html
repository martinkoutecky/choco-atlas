The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite rider types \(r\) and driver types \(d\), rational masses \(\alpha_r\) and supplies \(\beta_d\), and pairwise scores \(w_{rd}\) and acceptance probabilities \(p_{rd}\), each rider assigned a configuration \(\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{\mathcal D}\) receives the paper's BA expected score \(V^{BA}_r(\mathbf{k})\). Choose masses \(x_{r,\mathbf{k}}\ge 0\) to maximize \(\sum_{r,\mathbf{k}}x_{r,\mathbf{k}}V^{BA}_r(\mathbf{k})\), subject to \(\sum_{\mathbf{k}}x_{r,\mathbf{k}}=\alpha_r\) and \(\sum_{r,\mathbf{k}}k_d x_{r,\mathbf{k}}\le\beta_d\) for each type. Can an additive-\(\varepsilon N_R\) approximation be found in time polynomial in the type support, input bit length, and \(1/\varepsilon\), where \(N_R=\sum_r\alpha_r\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(\mathcal R,\mathcal D\) be nonempty rider and driver type sets, with \(m=|\mathcal R|\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|\mathcal R|+|\mathcal D|\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Rider masses \(\alpha_r\) and driver supplies \(\beta_d\) are not normalized; let \(N_R=\sum_r\alpha_r\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Pairwise scores \(w_{rd}\) and acceptance probabilities \(p_{rd}\) are binary rationals in \([0,1]\). A configuration \(\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathcal D}\) notifies \(k_d\) distinct copies of driver type \(d\); all copies accept independently with probability \(p_{rd}\). Best Acceptance selects a highest-scoring acceptor, with arbitrary tie-breaking, and awards zero if none accepts. Denote its expected score by \(V_r^{BA}(\mathbf k)\); the empty configuration is allowed.
An allocation consists of nonnegative masses \(x_{r,\mathbf k}\) satisfying \(\sum_{\mathbf k}x_{r,\mathbf k}=\alpha_r\) and \(\sum_{r,\mathbf k}k_dx_{r,\mathbf k}\le\beta_d\) for every \(d\). Supplies constrain aggregate notification use; no separate bound is imposed on a configuration. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the supremum of \(\sum_{r,\mathbf k}x_{r,\mathbf k}V_r^{BA}(\mathbf k)\) over feasible allocations. Computational allocations use finite lists of nonzero rational masses and binary-encoded configurations.

Does finding a feasible allocation of value at least \(\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon N_R\), for rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), admit an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)\) time, or is achieving this guarantee NP-hard?

Discrete BA welfare maximization is strongly NP-hard even with identical riders, unit scores, and probabilities \(p_{rd}=1-2^{-a_d}\), where \(a_d\ge1\) are integers. For explicitly listed drivers, an additive approximate BA demand oracle is polynomial-time, and common acceptance probabilities permit exact polynomial-time optimization. [Ekbatani et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21533v2)
No result settling the support-encoded mass problem with unbounded integer configurations was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored the score normalization and probability domains: all pairwise scores and probabilities are binary rationals in [0,1].
  • Masses and supplies are positive binary rationals on nonempty supports and need not sum to one.
  • Interpreted repeated driver types in a configuration as distinct copies with independent acceptance decisions; the empty configuration yields zero and score ties are immaterial.
  • Retained the graded question's unbounded integer configurations and aggregate supply constraints, without imposing a per-configuration supply cap.
  • Used a supremum for the potentially infinite configuration space and finite rational allocations with binary-encoded configurations as the computational representation.
  • Restricted the accuracy parameter to rational 0<epsilon<1 and retained the supplied additive approximation guarantee in the two-sided complexity target.

Result

The requested guarantee admits a deterministic algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)\) time. It outputs a finite rational allocation satisfying every supply constraint exactly.

The proof uses a polynomial-size flow linear program. Binary grouping handles large notification counts, and rounded welfare states handle the nonlinear objective.

1. Truncating unbounded configurations

Write \(n=|\mathcal D|\), and set \(\delta=\varepsilon/3\). Choose an integer \(H\) such that \(2^{-H}\leq\delta/n\), for example \(H=\lceil\log_2(n/\delta)\rceil\).

For each pair \((r,d)\) with \(p_{rd}>0\), let \(b_{rd}\geq1\) be the smallest integer satisfying

\(p_{rd}(2^{b_{rd}}-1)\geq H,\)

and define \(K_{rd}=2^{b_{rd}}-1\). If \(p_{rd}=0\), define \(b_{rd}=K_{rd}=0\).

Although \(K_{rd}\) may be exponentially large, its binary representation is short. Indeed, every positive input probability satisfies \(p_{rd}\geq2^{-L}\), so

\(b_{rd}=O(L+\log(H+1)).\)

Truncation lemma. For every rider type \(r\) and configuration \(\mathbf k\), define \(k'_d=\min\{k_d,K_{rd}\}\). Then

\(0\leq V_r^{BA}(\mathbf k)-V_r^{BA}(\mathbf k')\leq\delta.\)

Proof. Couple the two configurations by retaining the first \(k'_d\) notified copies of each type.

Removing copies with acceptance probability zero has no effect. For any other type with \(k_d>K_{rd}\), the probability that none of its retained copies accepts is

\((1-p_{rd})^{K_{rd}}\leq e^{-p_{rd}K_{rd}}\leq e^{-H}\leq2^{-H}\leq\delta/n.\)

If this event fails for every truncated type, every removed accepting copy has a retained accepting copy of the same score. Thus the maximum accepted score is unchanged. A union bound gives probability at most \(\delta\) that the scores differ. Their difference is nonnegative and at most one, proving the lemma. \(\square\)

Truncating every configuration in an allocation decreases notification use and preserves rider masses. Its welfare decreases by at most \(\delta N_R\). Aggregating equal truncated configurations produces an allocation supported on finitely many configurations.

Let \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{cap}}\) be the optimum restricted to \(0\leq k_d\leq K_{rd}\). This finite configuration problem has a compact feasible polytope: every allocation variable lies between zero and its rider mass. Consequently its optimum exists, and the truncation lemma implies

\(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{cap}}\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\delta N_R.\)

This argument does not assume that the original supremum is attained.

2. Representing counts by binary groups

For rider type \(r\), introduce one group for each pair \((d,j)\) with \(0\leq j<b_{rd}\). This group contains \(2^j\) copies of driver type \(d\). Its score and acceptance probability are

\(w_{rd}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{rdj}=1-(1-p_{rd})^{2^j},\)

respectively.

Group acceptance means that at least one copy in the group accepts. Distinct groups use disjoint copies, so their acceptance events are independent.

Every integer \(k_d\) between zero and \(K_{rd}=2^{b_{rd}}-1\) has a unique binary expansion. Therefore subsets of these groups represent exactly all capped configurations. The maximum score among accepting groups equals the maximum score among accepting individual copies.

Let

\(B_r=\sum_d b_{rd},\qquad B=\max_r B_r.\)

Then \(B=O(n(L+\log(H+1)))\). If \(B=0\), all acceptance probabilities are zero, and assigning every rider to the empty configuration solves the problem exactly. Henceforth assume \(B\geq1\).

The group probabilities can have enormous exact denominators. We next approximate them without constructing those denominators.

3. Computing group probabilities with controlled error

Set \(\eta=\delta/B\), and let \(M=\max_{r,d:p_{rd}>0}(b_{rd}-1)\). Choose an integer \(T\) satisfying

\(2^{M+1-T}\leq\eta.\)

For a fixed pair with positive acceptance probability, write \(q=1-p_{rd}\). Compute upward-rounded powers on the dyadic grid with spacing \(2^{-T}\):

\(c_0=2^{-T}\lceil2^Tq\rceil,\qquad c_{j+1}=2^{-T}\lceil2^Tc_j^2\rceil.\)

All these numbers belong to \([0,1]\). Define the approximated group probability by \(\widehat a_{rdj}=1-c_j\).

We claim

\(0\leq a_{rdj}-\widehat a_{rdj}\leq\eta.\)

To prove this, let \(e_j=c_j-q^{2^j}\). Upward rounding gives \(e_j\geq0\) and \(e_0\leq2^{-T}\). Because both numbers being squared belong to \([0,1]\),

\(e_{j+1}\leq(c_j+q^{2^j})e_j+2^{-T}\leq2e_j+2^{-T}.\)

Induction yields

\(e_j\leq(2^{j+1}-1)2^{-T}\leq2^{M+1-T}\leq\eta.\)

This proves the claim. The computation uses only polynomially many operations on numbers with polynomially many bits, since \(T=O(M+\log(B/\delta))\).

For a group subset \(S\), let \(F_r(S)\) be its true BA value and \(\widehat F_r(S)\) its value under the approximated probabilities. Couple each true and approximated group acceptance using an independent uniform random variable. The approximated accepting groups are a subset of the true accepting groups, and the probability that any acceptance differs is at most \(B_r\eta\). Hence

\(0\leq F_r(S)-\widehat F_r(S)\leq B_r\eta\leq\delta.\)

4. A polynomial graph of rounded welfare states

Choose \(Q=\lceil B/\delta\rceil\).

For each rider type, order its \(B_r\) groups by nondecreasing score. Write their scores, approximated probabilities, driver types, and sizes as \(w_i,\widehat a_i,d_i,s_i\), for \(1\leq i\leq B_r\).

Consider adding group \(i\) to a chosen subset of earlier groups. All earlier scores are at most \(w_i\). If group \(i\) accepts, the selected score is \(w_i\); if it rejects, the outcome comes from the earlier subset. Independence therefore gives the exact recurrence

\(\widehat F_i=\widehat a_iw_i+(1-\widehat a_i)\widehat F_{i-1}\)

when group \(i\) is included. Skipping it leaves the value unchanged. Equal scores cause no difficulty.

Construct a layered directed graph. Its source represents state zero before any groups have been considered. After group \(i\), states are integers \(v\in\{0,\ldots,Q\}\), representing rounded welfare \(v/Q\). From a preceding state \(v\), provide two labeled arcs:

  • A skip arc to state \(v\), using no notifications.
  • An include arc to state \(\lfloor Q\widehat a_iw_i+(1-\widehat a_i)v\rfloor\), using \(s_i\) notifications of type \(d_i\).

Keep these as distinct arcs even when they have the same endpoint. Connect every final state \(v\) to a sink by an arc with reward \(v/Q\).

Every group subset determines one source-to-sink path, and every such path determines a group subset. Denote its terminal reward by \(h_r(S)\).

Rounded welfare lemma. Every subset satisfies

\(0\leq\widehat F_r(S)-h_r(S)\leq B_r/Q\leq\delta.\)

Proof. Initially both values are zero. Skipping preserves their difference. Including a group multiplies the preceding difference by \(1-\widehat a_i\), and rounding downward adds an error less than \(1/Q\). Thus the difference remains nonnegative and increases by at most \(1/Q\) per layer. Induction proves the bound. \(\square\)

Combining the two approximation bounds gives, uniformly over all capped configurations,

\(F_r(S)-2\delta\leq h_r(S)\leq F_r(S).\)

Each graph has \(O((B_r+1)(Q+1))\) nodes and arcs. Its destinations can be computed exactly using rational arithmetic.

5. Optimizing mixtures by a flow linear program

Introduce a nonnegative variable \(f_{r,e}\) for each arc of rider graph \(r\). Require a source-to-sink flow of total mass \(\alpha_r\), with flow conservation at every other node.

For each driver type \(d\), impose the exact supply constraint

\(\sum_r\sum_{\substack{e\text{ is an include arc}\\d(e)=d}}s(e)f_{r,e}\leq\beta_d.\)

Maximize the total terminal reward:

\(\sum_r\sum_{v=0}^{Q}(v/Q)f_{r,e^{\mathrm{term}}_v}.\)

This is an ordinary rational linear program.

It is feasible: route all rider mass along the all-skip paths. It is bounded: the graphs are acyclic, and every arc flow is at most its rider mass. Its size is polynomial because \(Q=O(B/\delta)\), giving \(O(mB^2/\delta)\) arcs overall. All coefficients have polynomial bit length; in particular, a group size \(2^j\) requires only \(j+1\) bits.

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore supplies an exact rational optimum in polynomial time. See [Schrijver, *A Course in Combinatorial Optimization*, Section 2.4](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~lex/files/dict.pdf).

Let its objective value be \(Z^*\). Any capped allocation can be routed along the corresponding paths. This preserves notification use and rider masses, while reducing welfare by at most \(2\delta N_R\). Applying this to an optimum capped allocation gives

\(Z^*\geq\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{cap}}-2\delta N_R\geq\mathrm{OPT}-3\delta N_R.\)

6. Extracting an exactly feasible rational allocation

Decompose each optimal graph flow into source-to-sink paths. Explicitly, follow positive-flow arcs from the source to the sink, subtract the minimum flow on that path, and repeat.

A positive path always exists while source flow remains: conservation prevents a positive incoming flow from terminating at an internal node, and acyclicity forces the path to reach the sink. Each subtraction eliminates at least one positive-flow arc. Consequently the decomposition uses at most the number of graph arcs.

For a path \(P\) of mass \(\lambda_P\), output the configuration

\(k_d(P)=\sum_{\substack{i\text{ included by }P\\d_i=d}}s_i\)

with allocation mass \(\lambda_P\). Combine identical configurations if necessary.

Path decomposition preserves total rider mass and every aggregate notification count. Thus the output satisfies precisely the required feasibility constraints. All masses are rational, and there are polynomially many output entries. Their bit lengths remain polynomial: the decomposition performs polynomially many subtractions on the polynomial-bit rational LP solution. Each configuration coordinate has at most \(b_{rd}\) bits.

Finally, every path’s true BA value is at least its terminal reward. The output welfare is therefore at least

\(Z^*\geq\mathrm{OPT}-3\delta N_R=\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon N_R.\)

This proves the claimed algorithm and guarantee.

7. Relation to discrete hardness

The discrete hardness result concerns assigning explicitly listed, indivisible drivers to indivisible riders. The present problem allows fractional masses of configurations and constrains aggregate notification use. The flow decomposition outputs exactly such fractional masses; it requires no rounding to an indivisible assignment. Thus this result does not conflict with the cited discrete hardness theorem. See [Ekbatani et al., Sections 2.3 and 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21533v2).
