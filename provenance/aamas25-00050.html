The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), a finite catalogue \(R\) with rational per-capita supplies \(q_r\) of indivisible copies, binary liked sets \(L_\theta\), and \(t\in\mathbb{N}\), decide whether there exist \(x_{\theta r}\ge0\) such that \(x_{\theta r}=0\) for \(r\notin L_\theta\), \(\sum_{r\in L_\theta}x_{\theta r}=t\mu_\theta\) for every \(\theta\), and \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta r}\le q_r\) for every \(r\); equivalently, does an envy-free mass allocation of whole bundles exist in which every agent receives utility at least \(t\)?

Complexity of Feasible Equal-Utility Allocation

Theorem

Under the standard binary encoding of utilities and rational supplies, deciding whether a feasible equal-utility allocation exists is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness already holds when every utility is strictly positive and every supply is exactly \(1/2\).

Thus the problem is not polynomial-time solvable unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Source problem

Use Equal-Subset-Sum:

Given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), decide whether there are disjoint nonempty index sets \(P,Q\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) such that

\[ \sum_{i\in P}a_i=\sum_{i\in Q}a_i. \]

This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; the positive-integer restriction is standard [Mucha, Nederlof, Pawlewicz, and Węgrzycki](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2019.73).

Reduction

Given an Equal-Subset-Sum instance \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), introduce one good \(r_i\) for every index \(i\), and set

\[ u(r_i)=a_i,\qquad \rho_{r_i}=\frac12. \]

This construction is polynomial-time and uses only strictly positive utilities.

We prove that the constructed allocation instance is feasible if and only if the Equal-Subset-Sum instance is positive.

Forward implication

Suppose there are disjoint nonempty sets \(P,Q\) with

\[ \sum_{i\in P}a_i=\sum_{i\in Q}a_i=:U. \]

Because all \(a_i\) are positive, \(U\geq 1\). Define

\[ x_{\{r_i:i\in P\}}=\frac12,\qquad x_{\{r_i:i\in Q\}}=\frac12, \]

and set all other masses to zero.

Both supported bundles have utility \(U\), and the total mass is one. Since \(P\cap Q=\varnothing\), every good occurs in at most one supported bundle. Its total consumption is therefore either zero or \(1/2=\rho_{r_i}\). Hence the allocation is feasible.

Reverse implication

Suppose a feasible equal-utility allocation \(x\) exists, with common utility \(U\geq1\). Let

\[ \mathcal S=\{B\subseteq R:x_B>0\} \]

be its support.

Every \(B\in\mathcal S\) is nonempty because all utilities are positive and \(u(B)=U\geq1\).

The support cannot consist of a single bundle. Indeed, if \(\mathcal S=\{B\}\), then \(x_B=1\). For every \(r_i\in B\), the consumption of \(r_i\) would be one, contradicting \(\rho_{r_i}=1/2\).

Therefore there are distinct bundles \(B,C\in\mathcal S\). They satisfy

\[ u(B)=u(C)=U. \]

Neither can contain the other. For example, if \(B\subsetneq C\), strict positivity gives

\[ u(C)=u(B)+u(C\setminus B)>u(B), \]

a contradiction.

Consequently \(B\setminus C\) and \(C\setminus B\) are both nonempty. They are disjoint, and cancellation of the common intersection gives

\[ u(B\setminus C) =u(B)-u(B\cap C) =u(C)-u(B\cap C) =u(C\setminus B). \]

Thus

\[ P=\{i:r_i\in B\setminus C\},\qquad Q=\{i:r_i\in C\setminus B\} \]

are disjoint nonempty equal-sum subsets of the original instance.

This proves the correctness of the reduction and hence \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Let \(m=|R|\), and suppose a feasible allocation exists for some common utility \(U\). For each supported bundle \(B\), let \(\chi_B\in\{0,1\}^m\) be its incidence vector.

There is always a feasible allocation supported on at most \(m+1\) bundles. Indeed, if more than \(m+1\) bundles have positive mass, the vectors

\[ (1,\chi_B)\in\mathbb R^{m+1} \]

are linearly dependent. Hence there are coefficients \(d_B\), not all zero, satisfying

\[ \sum_B d_B=0,\qquad \sum_B d_B\chi_B=0. \]

Perturbing the masses in the direction \(d\) preserves both total mass and every resource consumption. Choosing the perturbation magnitude maximally while retaining nonnegativity makes at least one supported mass zero. Repetition reduces the support to at most \(m+1\) bundles.

For any fixed collection of at most \(m+1\) bundles, the feasible weights form a rational polytope whose matrix has entries in \(\{0,1\}\) and whose right-hand side consists of the rational supplies and one. If this polytope is nonempty, it has a rational basic feasible point of polynomial encoding length, by Cramer’s rule and the Hadamard determinant bound.

A certificate therefore consists of at most \(m+1\) bundles and their rational masses. In polynomial time one can verify:

  • nonnegativity and total mass one;
  • that all listed bundles have the same utility \(U\geq1\);
  • every supply inequality.

Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Conclusion

The distribution-over-bundles formulation does not remove the discrete subset-sum obstruction. Fractional masses suffice to mix two equal-sum bundles, while the capacity \(1/2\) ensures that any feasible allocation exposes two distinct equal-utility bundles. The decision problem is therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

Thus the reverse implication is established.

The proposed certificate therefore has polynomial length. All required properties—bundle utilities, rational masses, total mass, and supply inequalities—are verifiable using polynomial-time integer and rational arithmetic.
