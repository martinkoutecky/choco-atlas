The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational type distribution \(\mu\) with \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\), integer-valued decreasing-marginal schedules, and supply \(K=N\kappa\), represent the main economy and each type-deletion economy \(E^{-t}\), formed by reducing \(\mu_t\) by \(1/N\). From a feasible lower-envelope UCE price state, when one such economy has aggregate minimum demand above \(K\) or maximum demand below \(K\), compute a feasible negative-objective witness for the type-aggregated restricted dual. Under Lemma 5’s assumptions, does applying the update with \(\Delta=K\) preserve feasibility and strictly decrease the multiplicity-weighted UCE dual objective?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Assume \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and input binary integers \(N\ge2\), \(K=N\kappa>0\), with \(n_t=N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\); \(L\) is the total input bit length.
There is one homogeneous good. Type \(t\) has an explicitly listed, nonincreasing sequence of positive integer marginal values of length \(\gamma_t\le K\), consumption set \(C_t=\{0,\ldots,\gamma_t\}\), and cumulative valuation \(v_t\), with \(v_t(0)=0\); put \(m=\max_t\gamma_t\). Mass may be split among quantities in \(C_t\).
Index economies by \(e\in\{0,1,\ldots,\tau\}\): \(0\) is the main economy and \(e=t\) deletes mass \(1/N\) of type \(t\), without renormalization. Write \(a_{et}=n_t-\mathbf1\{e=t\}\), \(w_0=1\), and \(w_t=n_t\). Supply remains \(K\) in count units, equivalently \(\kappa\) in mass units; assume \(\sum_ta_{et}\gamma_t\ge K\) for every economy.
Use variables shared by surviving members of each type and by deletion economies of the same type. Variables indexed by \((e,t)\) exist only when \(a_{et}>0\). The UCE dual minimizes \(F=\sum_e w_e[Kp_e+\sum_ta_{et}(\pi_{et}+\alpha_{et})]\), subject to \(\pi_{et},\alpha_{et}\ge0\), \(p_e,\rho_t(k)\) free, \(\pi_{et}\ge v_t(k)-\rho_t(k)\), and \(\rho_t(k)\le kp_e+\alpha_{et}\) for every applicable \((e,t,k)\).
The input is a feasible integer price state with \(\rho_t(k)=\min_{e:a_{et}>0}(kp_e+\alpha_{et})\), \(\min_{e:a_{et}>0}\alpha_{et}=0\), and \(\pi_{et}=\max_{k\in C_t}[v_t(k)-\rho_t(k)]\). Define \(D_t=\arg\max_{k\in C_t}[v_t(k)-\rho_t(k)]\), retaining all ties, and \(B_t(k)=\arg\min_{e:a_{et}>0}(kp_e+\alpha_{et})\).
An economy is over-demanded if \(\sum_ta_{et}\min D_t>K\), and under-demanded if \(\sum_ta_{et}\max D_t<K\); at least one such economy is promised.
The aggregated restricted dual minimizes \(G=\sum_e w_e[Kq_e+\sum_ta_{et}(\lambda_{et}+\nu_{et})]\), subject to \(\lambda_{et}\ge-r_t(k)\) for \(k\in D_t\), \(\nu_{et}\ge r_t(k)-kq_e\) for \(e\in B_t(k)\), and \(\lambda_{et},\nu_{et},q_e,r_t(k)\ge-1\); all variables are rational and otherwise unrestricted.
A witness is a feasible restricted-dual solution with \(G<0\). Its update is \((p_e,\alpha_{et},\pi_{et},\rho_t(k))\leftarrow(p_e,\alpha_{et},\pi_{et},\rho_t(k))+K(q_e,\nu_{et},\lambda_{et},r_t(k))\). Afterwards permit zero-bundle normalization: with \(c_t=\rho_t(0)\), replace \((\alpha_{et},\pi_{et},\rho_t(k))\) by \((\alpha_{et}-c_t,\pi_{et}+c_t,\rho_t(k)-c_t)\).
Is computing a negative-objective restricted-dual witness polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
Under the stated assumptions, can such a witness always be chosen so that its \(\Delta=K\) update, with the permitted normalization, preserves UCE-dual feasibility and strictly decreases \(F\)?
For finitely listed agents, an explicit negative-objective witness is known whenever an economy is over-demanded or under-demanded; a fixed-step feasibility and strict-descent result is also claimed for integer values with decreasing marginals ([Lahaie and Lubin](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03252v2)).
No result settling this exact mass-based, multiplicity-weighted problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the main distribution to total mass one and treated deletion as removal of mass 1/N without renormalization; supply is unchanged.
  • Retained discrete per-member quantities while allowing mass to split among them; used count units N times mass units in demands and objectives.
  • Specified the single-good setting, explicitly listed positive nonincreasing integer marginals, and zero-normalized cumulative valuations; m denotes maximum schedule length.
  • Bounded individual consumption by supply and required enough aggregate consumption capacity to exhaust supply in every economy, matching the full-supply formulation intended by the restricted dual.
  • Specified type-symmetric price variables, omitted absent types, retained a type's own deletion-economy branch when members survive, and weighted each representative deletion economy by its multiplicity Nμ_t.

  • Specified every restricted-dual constraint, variable domain, objective coefficient, and update, including the utility-variable adjustment accompanying the permitted price normalization.
  • Asked whether an appropriate witness permits the fixed step, rather than assuming that every negative-objective direction permits it.

Result

Computing a negative-objective witness is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Moreover, a witness can always be chosen whose \(\Delta=K\) update, followed by the permitted normalization, preserves UCE-dual feasibility and strictly decreases \(F\).

The permission to use rational witnesses matters: we scale an explicit witness by \(1/m\). The corresponding unscaled witness need not preserve feasibility, even with integer prices and nonincreasing integer marginal values.

1. Notation and construction

Let \(E_t=\{e:a_{et}>0\}\), and define \(d_t=\min D_t\) and \(h_t=\max D_t\). Every \(D_t\) is nonempty because \(C_t\) is finite and nonempty. The capacity assumption and \(K>0\) imply \(m\ge1\). Also, \(0\le d_t\le h_t\le\gamma_t\le K\).

Choose an economy \(j\) satisfying one of the promised demand imbalances.

First construct an unscaled direction \(\widehat z=(\widehat q,\widehat\nu,\widehat\lambda,\widehat r)\). Assign pair-indexed variables only for applicable pairs.

If \(j\) is over-demanded, set

\( \displaystyle \widehat q_e=\frac{\mathbf1\{e=j\}}K,\qquad \widehat\lambda_{et}=-\frac{d_t}K,\qquad \widehat\nu_{et}=\frac{d_t\mathbf1\{e\ne j\}}K,\qquad \widehat r_t(k)=\frac{\min\{k,d_t\}}K. \)

If \(j\) is under-demanded, set

\( \displaystyle \widehat q_e=-\frac{\mathbf1\{e=j\}}K,\qquad \widehat\lambda_{et}=\frac{h_t}K,\qquad \widehat\nu_{et}=-\frac{h_t\mathbf1\{e\ne j\}}K,\qquad \widehat r_t(k)=-\frac{\max\{k,h_t\}}K. \)

These are the constructions underlying Theorem \(4\) of [Lahaie and Lubin](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03252v2#S4.SS1). We verify them directly for the stated aggregated problem.

The witness we ultimately return is \(z=\widehat z/m\).

2. Restricted-dual feasibility and negativity

Over-demand

For every \(k\in D_t\), we have \(k\ge d_t\). Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \widehat\lambda_{et}=-d_t/K=-\widehat r_t(k). \)

For \(e=j\),

\( \displaystyle \widehat r_t(k)-k\widehat q_j =\frac{\min\{k,d_t\}-k}K\le0=\widehat\nu_{jt}. \)

For \(e\ne j\),

\( \displaystyle \widehat r_t(k)-k\widehat q_e =\frac{\min\{k,d_t\}}K\le\frac{d_t}K=\widehat\nu_{et}. \)

Thus the supply-direction inequalities hold for every applicable economy and quantity, including all the required binding pairs.

Under-demand

For every \(k\in D_t\), we have \(k\le h_t\). Therefore,

\( \displaystyle \widehat\lambda_{et}=h_t/K=-\widehat r_t(k). \)

For \(e=j\),

\( \displaystyle \widehat r_t(k)-k\widehat q_j =\frac{k-\max\{k,h_t\}}K\le0=\widehat\nu_{jt}. \)

For \(e\ne j\),

\( \displaystyle \widehat r_t(k)-k\widehat q_e =-\frac{\max\{k,h_t\}}K\le-\frac{h_t}K=\widehat\nu_{et}. \)

Again, these inequalities hold for every applicable pair.

In both cases all coordinates of \(\widehat z\) lie in \([-1,1]\). Hence \(\widehat z\) satisfies all lower bounds. Multiplication by \(1/m\in(0,1]\) preserves those bounds and all homogeneous restricted-dual inequalities.

For every economy \(e\ne j\), the objective contribution vanishes: \(\widehat q_e=0\) and \(\widehat\lambda_{et}+\widehat\nu_{et}=0\). Thus, in the over-demand case,

\( \displaystyle G(\widehat z) =\frac{w_j}{K}\left(K-\sum_ta_{jt}d_t\right)<0, \)

and, in the under-demand case,

\( \displaystyle G(\widehat z) =\frac{w_j}{K}\left(\sum_ta_{jt}h_t-K\right)<0. \)

Since the objective is linear, \(G(z)=G(\widehat z)/m<0\). This proves existence without expanding the binary multiplicities into individual agents.

3. Feasibility of the scaled fixed-step update

Write \(\eta=1/m\). Applying the returned witness with \(\Delta=K\) means adding \(K\eta\widehat z\).

By the input assumptions, all applicable \(\pi_{et}\) for a given type equal a common integer \(\Pi_t\). Define the initial demand and supply slacks by

\( \displaystyle H_t(k)=\Pi_t+\rho_t(k)-v_t(k),\qquad S_{et}(k)=kp_e+\alpha_{et}-\rho_t(k). \)

Both are nonnegative integers. Furthermore, \(H_t(k)=0\) exactly when \(k\in D_t\); otherwise \(H_t(k)\ge1\).

Demand inequalities

In the over-demand case, the updated demand slack is

\( \displaystyle H'_t(k)=H_t(k)+\eta\bigl(\min\{k,d_t\}-d_t\bigr). \)

If \(k\ge d_t\), its change is zero. If \(k<d_t\), then \(k\notin D_t\), so

\( \displaystyle H'_t(k)=H_t(k)-\eta(d_t-k) \ge1-\frac{d_t-k}{m}\ge0. \)

In the under-demand case,

\( \displaystyle H'_t(k)=H_t(k)+\eta\bigl(h_t-\max\{k,h_t\}\bigr). \)

If \(k\le h_t\), its change is zero. If \(k>h_t\), then \(k\notin D_t\), and

\( \displaystyle H'_t(k)=H_t(k)-\eta(k-h_t) \ge1-\frac{k-h_t}{m}\ge0. \)

Thus every demand inequality survives.

Supply inequalities

In the over-demand case,

\( \displaystyle S'_{et}(k) =S_{et}(k)+\eta\left( k\mathbf1\{e=j\} +d_t\mathbf1\{e\ne j\} -\min\{k,d_t\}\right). \)

The parenthesized expression is nonnegative: it is \(k-\min\{k,d_t\}\) when \(e=j\), and \(d_t-\min\{k,d_t\}\) otherwise.

In the under-demand case,

\( \displaystyle S'_{et}(k) =S_{et}(k)+\eta\left( \max\{k,h_t\} -k\mathbf1\{e=j\} -h_t\mathbf1\{e\ne j\}\right). \)

Here too the parenthesized expression is nonnegative in both cases. Therefore every supply inequality survives.

Normalization and nonnegativity

Initially, \(\rho_t(0)=\min_{e\in E_t}\alpha_{et}=0\).

For over-demand, the updated zero-bundle price remains zero, so \(c_t=0\). Every offset weakly increases. The already-proved demand inequality at \(k=0\) gives \(\pi'_{et}\ge0\).

For under-demand, the updated zero-bundle price is \(c_t=-\eta h_t\). After normalization,

\( \displaystyle \pi''_{et}=\Pi_t+\eta h_t+c_t=\Pi_t\ge0, \)

and

\( \displaystyle \alpha''_{et} =\alpha_{et}-\eta h_t\mathbf1\{e\ne j\}-c_t =\alpha_{et}+\eta h_t\mathbf1\{e=j\}\ge0. \)

Normalization preserves the demand slack because the changes to \(\pi_{et}\) and \(\rho_t(k)\) cancel. It preserves the supply slack because the same constant is subtracted from \(\alpha_{et}\) and \(\rho_t(k)\).

Hence the final state is UCE-dual feasible.

4. Strict descent and polynomial complexity

Before normalization, linearity gives \(F_{\mathrm{raw}}-F=KG(z)\). Normalization leaves every sum \(\pi_{et}+\alpha_{et}\) unchanged, so it leaves \(F\) unchanged. Consequently,

\( \displaystyle F_{\mathrm{final}}-F = \begin{cases} \displaystyle\frac{w_j}{m}\left(K-\sum_ta_{jt}d_t\right),&\text{over-demand},\\[6pt] \displaystyle\frac{w_j}{m}\left(\sum_ta_{jt}h_t-K\right),&\text{under-demand}. \end{cases} \)

The relevant expression is strictly negative by the promise.

To compute the witness, scan the explicitly listed quantities to compute valuations and demand sets, find an imbalanced economy, and write the formulas above. Even recomputing every envelope value takes at most \(O(\tau^2(m+1))\) arithmetic operations.

The witness has \(O(\tau^2+\tau(m+1))\) coordinates. Their denominators can all be taken as \(Km\), and their numerators have absolute value at most \(m\). Multiplicities and weights remain binary integers throughout. All arithmetic and output bit lengths are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Therefore the entire construction runs in polynomial time.

5. Why the scaling is substantive

The following example satisfies every stated assumption but defeats the unscaled over-demand construction.

Take \(\tau=1\), \(\mu_1=1\), \(N=2\), \(K=3\), and marginal values \((5,5,5)\), so \(v_1(k)=5k\) for \(k\in\{0,1,2,3\}\). Set

\( \displaystyle p_0=5,\quad\alpha_{01}=0,\qquad p_1=3,\quad\alpha_{11}=5. \)

Then

\( \displaystyle (\rho_1(0),\rho_1(1),\rho_1(2),\rho_1(3))=(0,5,10,14), \)

so the utility vector is \((0,0,0,1)\). Thus \(D_1=\{3\}\) and \(\pi_{01}=\pi_{11}=1\). Both economies have sufficient capacity, and the main economy is over-demanded because \(2\cdot3>3\).

The unscaled construction has \(\widehat\lambda_{01}=\widehat\lambda_{11}=-1\). Its \(\Delta=K\) update makes both profits equal to \(1-3=-2\). Its updated zero-bundle price is zero, so normalization cannot repair this violation.

This does not contradict the existence result proved above: scaling by \(1/m=1/3\) produces a feasible update and decreases \(F\) from \(47\) to \(46\). It does show why the stronger unscaled-step claim stated in [Lemma \(5\) of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03252v2#S4.SS1) cannot simply be applied to every input state allowed here.
