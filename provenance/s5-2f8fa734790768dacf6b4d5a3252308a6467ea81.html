The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), direct and cross-channel data \(d_t,g_{u,t}\), noise levels \(\nu_t\), overheads \(\epsilon_t\), and an initial finite partition \(\mathcal C=(x^1,\ldots,x^r)\) with \(\sum_j x_t^j=\mu_t\), let all links of type \(u\) in block \(j\) share a zero-forcing beam \(w_u^j\) against the receiver types represented there. Their type-level utility is \(U_{t,j}(\mathcal C)=\log_2\!\left(1+\frac{|d_t^H w_t^j|^2}{\nu_t+\sum_u\sum_{\ell\ne j}x_u^\ell|g_{u,t}^H w_u^\ell|^2}\right)\). For fixed \(q\), find a sequence of mergers of between two and \(q\) blocks, ending when no acceptable merger remains; a merger of blocks \(J\) is acceptable if, in the merged partition \(\mathcal C'\), \(U_{t,j}(\mathcal C)-\epsilon_t\le U_{t,J}(\mathcal C')\) for every \(j\in J\) and \(t\) with \(x_t^j>0\), strictly for at least one such pair. This uses the paper's comparison convention; the proponent's displayed inequality places the overhead on the opposite utility.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\) encoded as binary rationals, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Type \(u\) has \(N_u\ge2\) antennas, a nonzero direct channel \(d_u\in\mathbb C^{N_u}\), and cross-channels \(g_{u,t}\in\mathbb C^{N_u}\). Channel coordinates have binary-rational real and imaginary parts; noise powers \(\nu_t>0\) and overheads \(\epsilon_t\ge0\) are binary rationals. Write \(m=\max_u N_u\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The input includes an initial partition \(\mathcal C=(x^1,\ldots,x^r)\), where each block is a nonzero nonnegative binary-rational vector and \(\sum_jx_t^j=\mu_t\). A type may occur in several blocks.
For a block \(x\), let \(P_{u,x}\) be the orthogonal projector onto \(\{w:g_{u,t}^Hw=0\text{ whenever }x_t>0\}\). For \(x_u>0\), prescribe \(w_u(x)=P_{u,x}d_u/\|P_{u,x}d_u\|\) when the projection is nonzero, and \(w_u(x)=0\) otherwise; set \(w_u(x)=0\) when \(x_u=0\). The constraint for \(t=u\) concerns other links of the same type in the nonatomic block. Transmit power is at most one per link.
For \(x_t^j>0\), define \(U_{t,j}(\mathcal C)=\log_2\!\left(1+\frac{|d_t^Hw_t(x^j)|^2}{\nu_t+\sum_u\sum_{\ell\ne j}x_u^\ell|g_{u,t}^Hw_u(x^\ell)|^2}\right)\).
Fix an integer \(q\ge2\). A merger chooses \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,|\mathcal C|\}\) with \(2\le|J|\le q\), replaces its blocks by \(x^J=\sum_{j\in J}x^j\), and recomputes beams and utilities. It is acceptable exactly when \(U_{t,j}(\mathcal C)-\epsilon_t\le U_{t,J}(\mathcal C')\) for every \(j\in J\) and \(t\) with \(x_t^j>0\), with strict inequality for at least one such pair. Outsiders need not approve; comparisons are exact, including equality.
Target: For every fixed \(q\), can a sequence of acceptable mergers ending at a partition with no acceptable merger be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,r,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

Result

For every fixed \(q\ge 2\), the search problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,r,L\), with exact comparisons, including equality.

The key points are that all squared channel gains are rational with polynomial bit length, and each approval comparison reduces to comparing products containing at most three integer-power terms. Exact comparison of such products is unconditionally polynomial-time.

1. Block masses remain polynomially encoded

Write the initial blocks as \(x^1,\ldots,x^r\). Every block obtainable by mergers has the form

\(x^I=\sum_{j\in I}x^j\)

for a nonempty subset \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}\). At every stage, these subsets form a partition of the initial block indices. This follows inductively because a merger replaces several disjoint subsets by their union.

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all coordinates of the initial blocks. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Every obtainable coordinate is an integer multiple of \(1/D\). Moreover,

\(0\le x_t^I\le\mu_t\le1.\)

Consequently, every coordinate can be represented with denominator \(D\) and numerator between zero and \(D\), using \(O(L)\) bits. This bound also applies to every candidate merged block.

Because coordinates are nonnegative, the support of a merged block is exactly the union of the supports of its constituent blocks. Thus support computation is exact and polynomial-time, even when a type occurs in several blocks.

2. Squared channel gains are exactly computable rationals

Fix a block \(x\), and put \(S(x)=\{t:x_t>0\}\). For each transmitting type \(u\), select a maximal linearly independent collection from

\(\{g_{u,t}:t\in S(x)\},\)

and let \(A\) be the matrix with these selected vectors as columns. This collection includes the constraint indexed by \(t=u\) whenever \(u\in S(x)\).

All entries belong to \(\mathbb Q(i)\). Matrix rank and a column basis can therefore be computed exactly over this field. The rank agrees with the rank over \(\mathbb C\), since both are characterized by the same nonzero minors.

If \(A\) has at least one column, its Gram matrix \(A^HA\) is invertible: for every nonzero column vector \(v\),

\(v^HA^HAv=\|Av\|^2>0.\)

The prescribed projector is consequently

\(P_{u,x}=I-A(A^HA)^{-1}A^H.\)

Indeed, the subtracted matrix is the orthogonal projector onto the span of the constraint vectors. For an empty collection, \(P_{u,x}=I\).

Define

\(z_{u,x}=P_{u,x}d_u,\qquad s_{u,x}=z_{u,x}^Hz_{u,x}.\)

The vector \(z_{u,x}\) lies in \(\mathbb Q(i)^{N_u}\), and \(s_{u,x}\) is a nonnegative rational. Since an orthogonal projector is Hermitian and idempotent,

\(d_u^Hz_{u,x}=d_u^HP_{u,x}d_u=z_{u,x}^Hz_{u,x}=s_{u,x}.\)

If \(x_u>0\) and \(s_{u,x}>0\), the prescribed beam is \(w_u(x)=z_{u,x}/\sqrt{s_{u,x}}\). Hence

\(|d_u^Hw_u(x)|^2=s_{u,x}\)

and, for every receiving type \(t\),

\(|g_{u,t}^Hw_u(x)|^2=\dfrac{|g_{u,t}^Hz_{u,x}|^2}{s_{u,x}}.\)

Both expressions are rational. If \(x_u=0\) or \(s_{u,x}=0\), all gains from this beam are zero, exactly as prescribed. In particular, no square root needs to be computed.

Bit complexity

These rational computations have polynomial bit complexity, uniformly over all possible block supports.

To see this, multiply the channel coordinates by a common denominator whose binary length is \(O(L)\). The resulting matrices have Gaussian-integer entries of \(O(L)\) bits. Their Gram entries have \(O(L+\log m)\) bits.

For an \(h\times h\) matrix with entries of \(B\) bits, the determinant expansion has \(h!\) terms, each a product of \(h\) entries. Its determinant and cofactors therefore have \(O(h(B+\log h))\) bits. Here \(h\le m\), so these bounds are polynomial. The inverse formula using cofactors then gives polynomial-size representations of the projector entries.

Exact fraction-free elimination computes ranks, determinants, and inverses in polynomial time; its intermediate entries are controlled by minors with the same polynomial bit bounds. Applying the projector to \(d_u\), taking inner products, and forming the gain ratios preserves polynomial bit length. Division occurs only after verifying that \(s_{u,x}>0\).

Thus every required squared gain can be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

3. Each utility is the logarithm of an explicit rational

For a current partition \(\mathcal C\), define the interference-plus-noise quantity

\(I_{t,j}(\mathcal C)=\nu_t+\sum_u\sum_{\ell\ne j}x_u^\ell|g_{u,t}^Hw_u(x^\ell)|^2.\)

Every summand is a nonnegative rational, and \(\nu_t>0\), so \(I_{t,j}(\mathcal C)>0\). Define the rational utility factor

\(F_{t,j}(\mathcal C)=1+\dfrac{|d_t^Hw_t(x^j)|^2}{I_{t,j}(\mathcal C)}.\)

Then \(F_{t,j}(\mathcal C)\ge1\) and

\(U_{t,j}(\mathcal C)=\log_2 F_{t,j}(\mathcal C).\)

There are at most \(r\tau\) summands in an interference quantity. A sum of \(h\) rationals, each with numerator and denominator of at most \(B\) bits, has an explicit representation with \(O(hB+\log h)\) bits: use the product of their denominators as a common denominator. Therefore every utility factor has polynomial bit length and is computable in polynomial time.

The same argument applies to the partition after any candidate merger. It uses exactly the interference expression in the question, without requiring approval from outsiders.

4. Exact approval comparisons take polynomial time

We use the following established theorem.

Fixed-factor power-product comparison theorem. For fixed constants \(k,\ell\), given positive integers \(a_i,b_i,c_j,d_j\), all encoded in binary, deciding

\(\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_i^{b_i}\ge\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}c_j^{d_j}\)

takes deterministic polynomial time in their total encoding length. This is unconditional; see Etessami, Stewart, and Yannakakis, [Proposition \(3.7(2)\)](https://arxiv.org/html/1304.5429). Applying the comparison in both directions also distinguishes equality and strict inequality.

Now fix a candidate merger \(J\) and an approval pair \((t,j)\), where \(j\in J\) and \(x_t^j>0\). Set

\(F_{\mathrm{old}}=F_{t,j}(\mathcal C),\qquad F_{\mathrm{new}}=F_{t,J}(\mathcal C').\)

Write

\(\epsilon_t=a/b,\qquad F_{\mathrm{old}}/F_{\mathrm{new}}=A/B,\)

where \(a\ge0\), \(b\ge1\), and \(A,B\ge1\) are integers. Their binary lengths are polynomial by the preceding sections.

The approval inequality is equivalent, step by step, to

\(\log_2 F_{\mathrm{old}}-a/b\le\log_2 F_{\mathrm{new}},\)

\(\log_2(A/B)\le a/b,\)

\((A/B)^b\le2^a,\)

and finally

\(A^b\le B^b2^a.\)

The equivalences hold because the logarithm and exponentiation with base two are strictly increasing, and \(b>0\).

If \(a=0\), this comparison is simply \(A\le B\). If \(a>0\), the power-product theorem applies with one factor on the left and two on the right. All bases and exponents are positive binary-encoded integers, and the factor counts are fixed constants. Bases equal to one are harmless and may be omitted.

Likewise, strict approval holds exactly when

\(A^b<B^b2^a.\)

Thus weak approval, strict approval, and equality can all be determined exactly in polynomial time. The potentially enormous integer powers need not be expanded.

A candidate merger has at most \(q\tau\) approval pairs. It is acceptable precisely when every comparison gives weak approval and at least one gives strict approval. Consequently, acceptability of a specified candidate is polynomial-time decidable.

5. Algorithm and proof of correctness

Starting from the supplied partition, perform the following steps:

  • Enumerate every subset \(J\) of current block indices satisfying \(2\le|J|\le q\).
  • Test its acceptability using the exact procedure above.
  • If an acceptable subset is found, record the merger, replace its blocks by their sum, and restart the enumeration for the new partition.
  • If the enumeration finishes without finding an acceptable subset, output the recorded sequence and the current partition.

Every recorded merger is acceptable by construction.

If there are currently \(n\) blocks, a merger of \(J\) leaves \(n-|J|+1\le n-1\) blocks. Hence there can be at most \(r-1\) successful mergers. This termination argument requires no monotonicity of utilities.

When the algorithm stops, it has examined every permitted subset in the final partition and rejected each one. Therefore that partition has no acceptable merger.

For fixed \(q\), the number of candidates in any enumeration is bounded by

\(\sum_{k=2}^{\min(q,n)}\binom nk=O_q(r^q).\)

There are at most \(r\) enumerations, including the final unsuccessful one. Thus the total number of candidate tests is \(O_q(r^{q+1})\). Each test has polynomial bit complexity, and all intermediate block masses retain the bounds proved in Section 1. Recording the sequence and final partition also requires only polynomial space and output length.

This proves the claimed deterministic polynomial-time bound for the full mass-based search problem, with support size \(\tau\) and without expanding masses into individual links.
