The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a bipartite support \(\mathcal T=R\mathbin{\dot\cup}S\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and mutually approved type-pair sets \(E_i\subseteq R\times S\) for each layer \(i\in[\ell]\), does there exist a mass matching \(x_{tu}\ge0\) with incident mass at each type at most \(\mu_t\), such that for every \((t,u)\in E_i\) and every layer \(i\), \(h_t^{(i)}h_u^{(i)}=0\), where \(h_t^{(i)}=\mu_t-\sum_{u:(t,u)\in E_i}x_{tu}\) (and symmetrically for \(u\))?

A society is given by its support \(\mathcal T=R\mathbin{\dot\cup}S\): a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; partner alternatives are types, so \(m=\tau\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
There are \(\ell\ge2\) layers, each given explicitly by a mutually approved pair set \(E_i\subseteq R\times S\). Within a layer, approved partners are tied and strictly preferred to disapproved partners, which are tied with being unmatched; empty approval sets are allowed.
A mass matching is a single array \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{R\times S}\), shared across all layers, with \(\sum_{u\in S}x_{tu}\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\in R\) and \(\sum_{t\in R}x_{tu}\le\mu_u\) for every \(u\in S\). Disapproved pairs may receive mass, and mass may remain unmatched.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even for every fixed \(\ell\ge 2\), with equal masses and at most three approved partners per type per layer.

The key fact is that, for equal masses, fractional feasibility implies discrete feasibility. Splitting mass therefore does not evade the cited hardness result.

1. A rounding lemma for equal masses

For a bipartite vertex set \(R\mathbin{\dot\cup}S\), define the fractional matching polytope

\(P=\left\{y\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^{R\times S}:\sum_{u\in S}y_{tu}\le 1\ \text{for every }t\in R,\ \sum_{t\in R}y_{tu}\le 1\ \text{for every }u\in S\right\}.\)

Lemma. Suppose every type has the same mass \(c>0\). An all-layers weakly stable mass matching exists if and only if a discrete matching exists in which every approved edge, in every layer, has an endpoint matched to an approved partner in that layer.

Proof. A discrete matching with the stated property immediately gives a stable mass matching by assigning mass \(c\) to each matched pair.

For the converse, let \(x\) be a stable mass matching and put \(y=x/c\). Then \(y\in P\).

We first justify that every point of \(P\) is a convex combination of incidence matrices of discrete matchings. The polytope is bounded, so it suffices to show that every extreme point is integral.

Suppose \(y\in P\) has a fractional coordinate. Form the bipartite graph consisting of edges with \(0<y_{tu}<1\).

  • If this graph contains a cycle, the cycle has even length. Alternately increasing and decreasing its coordinates by a sufficiently small positive amount gives two distinct feasible points with midpoint \(y\): every vertex sum remains unchanged.
  • Otherwise, a component containing an edge is a tree. Choose a path between two leaves. Each leaf has strictly positive capacity slack: its only positive incident coordinate is its fractional edge, since an incident coordinate equal to \(1\) would violate its capacity bound. Alternating perturbations along the path preserve all internal vertex sums. For a sufficiently small amount, both perturbations also satisfy the endpoint capacity bounds and nonnegativity.

Thus a fractional point cannot be extreme. Every extreme point is consequently a \(0\)-\(1\) incidence matrix of a matching. By the finite-dimensional polytope decomposition theorem, we may write

\(y=\sum_{k=1}^{q}\lambda_k\chi^{M_k},\qquad \lambda_k>0,\qquad \sum_{k=1}^{q}\lambda_k=1,\)

where each \(M_k\) is a discrete matching.

For a vertex \(v\) and layer \(i\), define its normalized unhappy mass at \(z\in P\) by

\(d_v^{(i)}(z)=1-\sum_{e\in E_i:\,v\in e}z_e.\)

Capacity feasibility implies \(d_v^{(i)}(z)\ge 0\). Moreover,

\(d_v^{(i)}(y)=\sum_{k=1}^{q}\lambda_k d_v^{(i)}(\chi^{M_k}).\)

Consequently, if \(d_v^{(i)}(y)=0\), then \(d_v^{(i)}(\chi^{M_k})=0\) for every \(k\): all summands are nonnegative and every coefficient is positive.

Now fix any layer \(i\) and edge \((t,u)\in E_i\). Stability of \(x\), together with \(h_v^{(i)}(x)=c\,d_v^{(i)}(y)\), implies that one endpoint has normalized unhappy mass zero. That same endpoint has unhappy mass zero in every \(M_k\). In a discrete matching, this means that it is matched to an approved partner in layer \(i\).

This holds simultaneously for every layer and approved edge. Therefore every \(M_k\) has the required discrete stability property. \(\square\)

2. Polynomial reduction proving hardness

Theorem 1 of Bentert, Boehmer, Heeger, and Koana establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness of discrete all-layers weak stability for two layers, with symmetric bipartite approvals and at most three approved partners per agent per layer. Their preferences tie disapproved partners with being unmatched, exactly as here. [Source: Theorem 1 and the preference definitions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.07550).

Take an instance of this discrete problem with \(n\) agents. Choose a bipartition \(R\mathbin{\dot\cup}S\) of the union of its approval graphs.

If its matching model allows pairs within one side, deleting those pairs preserves every agent’s happiness in every layer: bipartiteness makes such pairs disapproved in all layers. Thus restricting matchings to \(R\times S\) does not change existence.

Construct the mass instance as follows:

  • Create one type for each original agent.
  • Let \(N=2^{\lceil\log_2 n\rceil}\), and add \(N-n\) dummy types to \(R\).
  • Give every type mass \(\mu_v=1/N\).
  • Copy the two original approved pair sets. Dummy types have no approved partners.

The padding makes the masses dyadic, so the construction works even under the more restrictive interpretation that “binary rational” means a terminating binary fraction. The masses are positive and sum to \(1\).

If the discrete instance has a stable matching, leave dummy types unmatched and assign mass \(1/N\) to every matched pair. Each approved edge has a happy endpoint, so the resulting mass matching is stable.

Conversely, suppose the constructed mass instance has a stable matching. By the lemma, its padded discrete instance has a stable matching. Delete all pairs incident to dummy types. These pairs are disapproved in every layer, so their deletion preserves the happiness of every original agent. The remaining matching solves the original discrete instance.

The construction has \(N<2n\) types and mass encodings of length \(O(\log n)\) per type. Its total encoding length is polynomial in the source input length. In particular, it respects \(m=\tau=N\); it introduces no implicitly represented partner alternatives.

This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for \(\ell=2\). For any fixed \(\ell>2\), add \(\ell-2\) empty layers. They impose no stability conditions, so feasibility is unchanged. Approval degrees remain at most three.

3. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) for arbitrary rational masses

We now consider the full problem, allowing unequal positive rational masses and an explicitly given number of layers.

For each layer \(i\), guess a set \(H_i\subseteq R\mathbin{\dot\cup}S\) of types required to have zero unhappy mass. This certificate contains \(\tau\ell\) bits.

First verify that \(H_i\) covers every approved edge:

\((t,u)\in E_i\ \Longrightarrow\ t\in H_i\ \text{or}\ u\in H_i.\)

Next test feasibility of the following rational linear program:

  • \(x_{tu}\ge 0\) for every \((t,u)\in R\times S\);
  • \(\sum_{u\in S}x_{tu}\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\in R\);
  • \(\sum_{t\in R}x_{tu}\le\mu_u\) for every \(u\in S\);
  • \(\sum_{u:(t,u)\in E_i}x_{tu}=\mu_t\) whenever \(t\in H_i\cap R\);
  • \(\sum_{t:(t,u)\in E_i}x_{tu}=\mu_u\) whenever \(u\in H_i\cap S\).

There are at most \(\tau^2\) variables and polynomially many constraints. Coefficients are \(0\) or \(1\), and the right-hand sides are input rationals. Hence the encoding length is polynomial in \(\tau,\ell,L\), and rational linear-programming feasibility can be decided in polynomial time.

The verifier is sound: an accepted feasible point has nonnegative unhappy masses, and every approved edge has an endpoint whose unhappy mass is zero.

It is also complete: given any stable mass matching, take \(H_i=\{v:h_v^{(i)}=0\}\). Nonnegativity and the stability products imply that these sets cover all approved edges, and the given matching satisfies the linear program.

Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Conclusion

The equal-mass rounding lemma transfers discrete hardness to the exact mass feasibility problem. Combined with the verifier above, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for every fixed \(\ell\ge 2\), and also when \(\ell\) is part of the input. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,\ell,L\) would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
