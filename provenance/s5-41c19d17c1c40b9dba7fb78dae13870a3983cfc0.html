The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\), a positive rational valuation vector \(w\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^m\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^m\), compute a finite-support distribution \(\lambda\) over integral bundles \(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^m\) such that \(\sum_k\lambda_k=1\), \(\sum_k\lambda_k k=q\), and, for every supported \(k,\ell\), \(w\cdot k\ge w\cdot\ell-w_j\) for every \(j\) with \(\ell_j>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here the society has total mass \(1\), a single valuation type, and \(\tau=1\).
There are \(m\ge1\) good types, with common additive values \(w\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^{m}\) and per-capita supplies \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{m}\), all binary encoded. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An integral bundle \(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{m}\) records the number of copies of each good type; empty bundles and repeated copies are allowed.
Compute an explicitly listed finite distribution with distinct bundles \(k\) and positive binary-rational masses \(\lambda_k\), satisfying \(\sum_k\lambda_k=1\) and \(\sum_k\lambda_k k=q\).
Require EFX across its entire support: for every supported \(k,\ell\) and every \(j\) with \(\ell_j>0\), require \(w\cdot k\ge w\cdot\ell-w_j\). Removal means removing one copy; equality is permitted.

Is computing such a distribution for every admissible input possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

Discrete: with identical strictly positive additive valuations, every maximal strict-descent sequence for the variance of bundle values under single-item transfers terminates at an EFX allocation. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05429v1)
Discrete EFX allocations are also polynomial-time computable for additive valuations with identical rankings, hence for identical valuations, when agents and items are explicitly represented. [Source](https://www-cs.stanford.edu/people/bplaut/BenPlautThesisFinal.pdf)
No result settling this exact mass-distribution search problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted m as the number of good types, with bundle coordinates counting copies, as required by the graded question's unrestricted integral bundles.
  • Normalized society mass to 1 and represented its identical valuation by one supported type of mass 1; the output bundle support is separate from the society's support size.
  • Required an explicit finite output with binary-rational masses and binary-encoded integral bundle coordinates.
  • Allowed empty bundles and zero supplies, consistent with complete discrete allocations that may leave agents empty; imposed m at least 1.

A polynomial-time construction

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, one can compute a distribution with at most \(m+1\) distinct supported bundles, and every bundle coordinate and every rational mass has polynomial encoding length.

Throughout, a binary-encoded rational means a rational whose integer numerator and positive denominator are encoded in binary.

1. Normalization and the invariant

Multiply all values by the product of their denominators. This produces positive integer values \(W_1,\ldots,W_m\), with polynomial encoding length, and preserves every EFX inequality. Relabel the good types so that

\(W_1\geq W_2\geq\cdots\geq W_m>0.\)

Write \(V(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}W_jk_j\).

For a nonempty finite support \(S\), let \(h(S)=\min_{k\in S}V(k)\). The support is EFX precisely when

\(V(\ell)-W_j\leq h(S)\quad\text{for every }\ell\in S\text{ and every }j\text{ with }\ell_j>0.\)

Indeed, these inequalities imply the required comparison with every supported bundle, and the required comparisons include one with a minimum-value bundle.

We process the good types in their sorted order. After processing type \(i\), we maintain a rational distribution whose support is EFX, whose mean is

\((q_1,\ldots,q_i,0,\ldots,0),\)

and whose support size is at most \(i+1\). Initially the empty bundle has mass \(1\).

The following construction supplies the inductive step.

2. Allocating one good type in a single batch

Suppose the current distribution has distinct bundles \(b_1,\ldots,b_s\), with positive rational masses \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s\). Its support is EFX, and only types preceding \(i\) occur in these bundles.

Set \(a=W_i\), \(Q=q_i\), and \(v_h=V(b_h)\). If \(Q=0\), leave the distribution unchanged. Assume henceforth that \(Q>0\).

For each integer \(t\), define

\(C(t)=\sum_{h=1}^{s}\lambda_h\max\left\{0,\left\lfloor\frac{t-v_h}{a}\right\rfloor+1\right\}.\)

For cohort \(h\), the expression being weighted counts the levels

\(v_h,\ v_h+a,\ v_h+2a,\ldots\)

that are at most \(t\). These are the bundle values immediately before successive additions of a type-\(i\) item.

The function \(C\) is nondecreasing, \(C(-1)=0\), and \(C(t)\) tends to infinity. Let \(T\) be the smallest nonnegative integer satisfying \(C(T)\geq Q\). Then

\(C(T-1)<Q\leq C(T).\)

Define

\(r_h=\max\left\{0,\left\lfloor\frac{T-1-v_h}{a}\right\rfloor+1\right\},\)

and let \(I\) be the set of indices \(h\) satisfying \(T\geq v_h\) and \(T-v_h\in a\mathbb Z\). Thus \(r_h\) counts cohort \(h\)'s additions at levels strictly below \(T\), and \(h\in I\) means that its next addition occurs exactly at \(T\).

Consequently,

\(C(T-1)=\sum_h\lambda_hr_h,\qquad C(T)-C(T-1)=\sum_{h\in I}\lambda_h.\)

Put \(M=\sum_{h\in I}\lambda_h\), \(\delta=Q-C(T-1)\), and \(p=\delta/M\). The threshold inequalities imply \(M>0\) and \(0<p\leq1\).

Let \(e_i\) be the \(i\)-th unit vector. Replace each cohort as follows:

  • If \(h\notin I\), give mass \(\lambda_h\) to \(b_h+r_he_i\).
  • If \(h\in I\), give mass \(\lambda_h(1-p)\) to \(b_h+r_he_i\), and mass \(\lambda_hp\) to \(b_h+(r_h+1)e_i\).

Discard zero masses. This produces at most \(2s\) bundles with rational masses.

Exact supply preservation

Each cohort's total mass remains \(\lambda_h\), and all coordinates other than \(i\) remain unchanged. The mean number of type-\(i\) copies added is

\(\sum_h\lambda_hr_h+pM=C(T-1)+\delta=Q.\)

Thus the new distribution has exactly the desired updated mean.

EFX preservation

First, \(T\geq\min_hv_h\), since \(C(t)=0\) whenever \(t<\min_hv_h\).

Every resulting bundle has value at least \(T\). To check this, if \(r_h=0\), its base value \(v_h\) is at least \(T\). If \(r_h>0\), the definition of \(r_h\) gives

\(v_h+(r_h-1)a<T\leq v_h+r_ha.\)

For \(h\in I\), the base bundle has value exactly \(T\), and its additional successor has value \(T+a\).

Now consider any resulting bundle \(\ell\).

If \(\ell\) receives a type-\(i\) item, then

\(V(\ell)-a\leq T.\)

For a base bundle this follows from the preceding strict inequality; for an additional successor it follows from \(V(\ell)=T+a\). Every item in \(\ell\) has value at least \(a\), because types are processed in decreasing value. Hence, for every \(j\) with \(\ell_j>0\),

\(V(\ell)-W_j\leq V(\ell)-a\leq T.\)

If \(\ell\) receives no type-\(i\) item, it is an unchanged old bundle. Old EFX therefore gives, for every item type occurring in it,

\(V(\ell)-W_j\leq\min_hv_h\leq T.\)

Since every new supported bundle has value at least \(T\), both cases establish EFX across the entire new support.

This proves the batch step without enumerating individual copies or agents.

3. Reducing the support without losing EFX

Suppose a finite EFX distribution has mean \(x\), and all its bundles use only the first \(i\) coordinates. Associate to each supported bundle \(k^h\) the integer column

\(c_h=(1,k^h_1,\ldots,k^h_i)^{\mathsf T}.\)

The masses satisfy

\(\sum_h\lambda_hc_h=(1,x_1,\ldots,x_i)^{\mathsf T}.\)

If the columns are linearly dependent, compute a nonzero integer vector \(d\) satisfying \(\sum_hd_hc_h=0\). The first row gives \(\sum_hd_h=0\), so \(d\) has both positive and negative entries.

Set

\(\theta=\min_{h:d_h>0}\frac{\lambda_h}{d_h},\qquad \lambda'_h=\lambda_h-\theta d_h.\)

All new masses are nonnegative, and at least one becomes zero. Moreover,

\(\sum_h\lambda'_hc_h=\sum_h\lambda_hc_h.\)

Thus total mass and all coordinate means are preserved. After discarding zero masses, the support is a subset of the previous support, so every EFX inequality remains valid.

Repeat until the columns are independent. There are then at most \(i+1\) supported bundles.

This is a constructive support reduction using only rational linear algebra.

4. The complete algorithm and its correctness

Start with the empty bundle of mass \(1\). For \(i=1,\ldots,m\):

  • Apply the batch construction with \(a=W_i\) and \(Q=q_i\).
  • Apply support reduction to the resulting distribution.

The batch construction preserves EFX and sets the mean of coordinate \(i\) to \(q_i\), while preserving all earlier means. Support reduction preserves those properties and leaves at most \(i+1\) bundles.

Induction therefore proves that the final distribution:

  • has positive rational masses summing to \(1\);
  • has mean exactly \(q\);
  • satisfies EFX across its entire support;
  • contains at most \(m+1\) distinct bundles.

It remains to prove polynomial bit complexity. Sparse support alone would not suffice.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

Uniform bounds on bundle coordinates

Let

\(A=\sum_{j=1}^{m}W_jq_j,\qquad W_{\max}=\max_jW_j,\qquad W_{\min}=\min_jW_j,\)

and define

\(B=\left\lceil\frac{A+W_{\max}}{W_{\min}}\right\rceil.\)

Every intermediate distribution is EFX and has mean value at most \(A\). If a supported bundle \(k\) is nonempty, choose any \(j\) with \(k_j>0\). EFX gives

\(V(k)\leq h(S)+W_j\leq A+W_{\max}.\)

The second inequality uses \(h(S)\leq\sum_{\ell\in S}\lambda_\ell V(\ell)\leq A\). Consequently,

\(0\leq k_j\leq B\quad\text{for every supported bundle and every coordinate }j.\)

The same bound holds for the empty bundle. Since the input numbers are binary encoded, \(B\) has polynomial bit length. This bound applies both before and after each support reduction.

Computing the threshold

At a batch step, let \(v_{\max}=\max_hv_h\). An upper bound for the threshold is

\(U=v_{\max}+a\lceil Q\rceil.\)

For every \(h\), the number of cohort-\(h\) levels at most \(U\) is at least \(\lceil Q\rceil+1\). Since the masses sum to \(1\),

\(C(U)\geq\lceil Q\rceil+1>Q.\)

Therefore binary search over the integers from \(0\) to \(U\) finds \(T\). Both \(U\) and the number of search iterations have polynomial encoding length. Each evaluation of \(C(t)\) involves at most \(m+1\) rational terms.

We next justify that the masses used in these evaluations also have polynomial encoding length.

Bounds on masses after support reduction

Let \(D_q\) be the product of the denominators of all supplies. It has polynomial bit length.

After support reduction, suppose there are \(s\leq i+1\) independent columns. Choose \(s\) rows giving a nonsingular integer square matrix \(H\). Every entry of \(H\) has absolute value at most \(B\), since its rows are drawn from the first row of ones and the bundle coordinates.

The masses uniquely solve

\(H\lambda=y,\)

where every entry of \(y\) is either \(1\) or an already processed supply. Hence \(D_qy\) is an integer vector of polynomial bit length.

By Cramer's rule, each mass can be represented as

\(\lambda_h=\frac{\det H_h}{D_q\det H},\)

where \(H_h\) replaces column \(h\) of \(H\) by \(D_qy\).

For an integer matrix of size \(s\) with entries bounded by \(K\) in absolute value, the determinant has absolute value at most \(s!K^s\), directly from the determinant expansion. Applying this bound to \(H\) and \(H_h\) proves that the reduced numerator and denominator of every mass have polynomial bit length.

Crucially, this bound depends on the input and the bundle-coordinate bound, rather than on arithmetic accumulated in previous stages. Thus every batch begins with polynomial-size masses. Its threshold evaluations, splitting parameter \(p\), and candidate masses consequently have polynomial encoding length.

Bit complexity within support reduction

The support-reduction procedure itself also has polynomial bit complexity.

For a dependent integer matrix whose entries are bounded by \(B\), an integer dependence vector \(d\) with polynomial-size entries can be computed as follows. Select a basis of \(r\) columns, one additional column, and \(r\) rows on which the basis columns form a nonsingular matrix. Cramer's rule expresses the additional column as a rational combination of the basis. Multiplying by the basis determinant gives an integer dependence on the full columns, with coefficients bounded by \(r!B^r\). Here \(r\leq i+1\). Gaussian elimination and determinant computation take polynomial time.

To control mass arithmetic, write all current masses as \(\lambda_h=n_h/E\) with one positive common denominator. Taking the product of their denominators supplies such an \(E\) of polynomial bit length. If index \(p\) achieves the minimum defining \(\theta\), then

\(\lambda'_h=\frac{n_hd_p-n_pd_h}{Ed_p}.\)

Thus one elimination increases the common denominator's bit length by at most the bit length of \(d_p\), which is polynomial. The numerators lie between \(0\) and the new denominator because the updated masses are nonnegative and sum to \(1\).

There are at most \(2i\) candidate bundles in stage \(i\), and each elimination removes at least one. Therefore the entire support reduction uses polynomially many operations on polynomial-size integers. Reducing the final fractions gives the uniform mass bounds proved above.

Combining these bounds over all \(m\) stages proves polynomial running time and polynomial total output length.
