The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), stages \(1,\ldots,\tau\), integers \(k,\ell\), a rational threshold \(\rho\), and a finite rational-support distribution \(\mu\) over temporal SNTV types \(\theta\in(C\cup\{\emptyset\})^\tau\), decide whether committees \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_\tau\) exist such that \(|Q_t|\le k\), \(\sum_{\theta:\theta_t\in Q_t}\mu_\theta\ge\rho\) for every \(t\), and \(|Q_t\triangle Q_{t+1}|\le\ell\) for every consecutive pair. Equivalently, the input may be represented by \(a_{t,c}=\sum_{\theta:\theta_t=c}\mu_\theta\).

Definition

An instance has a candidate set \(C\), \(\tau\) stages, integers \(k,\ell\), a rational \(\rho\), and a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) on temporal SNTV types \(\theta\in(C\cup\{\emptyset\})^\tau\). All rationals are binary encoded. Let \(a_{t,c}=\sum_{\theta:\theta_t=c}\mu_\theta\). Decide whether there are \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_\tau\subseteq C\) with \(|Q_t|\le k\), \(\sum_{c\in Q_t}a_{t,c}\ge\rho\) for every \(t\), and \(|Q_t\triangle Q_{t+1}|\le\ell\) for every consecutive pair.

Is Rational-Mass Conservative Multistage SNTV solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Prover output (gpt-5.6-sol / xhigh, Bolzano prover prompt)

Classification

The problem is \( \mathrm{NP\text{-}complete} \). Hardness already holds when \( \ell=0 \), \( \rho=1/2 \), and the distribution has only two temporal types, each of mass \( 1/2 \).

Membership in NP

A certificate consists of the committees \( Q_1,\ldots,Q_\tau \), represented by characteristic vectors over \( C \). Its length is \( O(\tau |C|) \), hence polynomial in the input size because every explicitly represented temporal type has \( \tau \) coordinates.

For each stage \( t \), the verifier checks:

  • \( |Q_t|\leq k \);
  • \( |Q_t\triangle Q_{t+1}|\leq\ell \), where applicable;
  • \( \sum_{\theta:\theta_t\in Q_t}\mu_\theta\geq\rho \).

The last expression equals \( \sum_{c\in Q_t}a_{t,c} \). Exact addition and comparison of binary-encoded rationals take polynomial time. Indeed, a common denominator may be chosen as the product of the input denominators; its binary length is at most the sum of their binary lengths and is therefore polynomial in the input length. Thus the problem belongs to \( \mathrm{NP} \).

NP-hardness reduction

We reduce from \( \mathrm{VERTEX\ COVER} \).

An instance consists of a simple undirected graph \( G=(V,E) \) and an integer \( K \). The question is whether there exists \( S\subseteq V \) such that \( |S|\leq K \) and \( S\cap e\neq\varnothing \) for every \( e\in E \). This problem is \( \mathrm{NP\text{-}complete} \).

We may restrict to instances with \( E\neq\varnothing \), since instances with \( E=\varnothing \) are trivially affirmative. Enumerate the edges as \( E=\{e_1,\ldots,e_\tau\} \), and choose an arbitrary orientation \( e_t=\{u_t,v_t\} \).

Construct the following rational-mass instance:

  • \( C=V \);
  • there are \( \tau=|E| \) stages;
  • \( k=K \);
  • \( \ell=0 \);
  • \( \rho=1/2 \);
  • there are two temporal types

\( \theta^{(1)}=(u_1,\ldots,u_\tau) \) and
\( \theta^{(2)}=(v_1,\ldots,v_\tau) \);

  • \( \mu_{\theta^{(1)}}=\mu_{\theta^{(2)}}=1/2 \).

This is a valid probability distribution with support size two.

For every stage \( t \) and candidate \( c \),

\[ a_{t,c}= \begin{cases} 1/2,&c\in\{u_t,v_t\},\\ 0,&c\notin\{u_t,v_t\}. \end{cases} \]

Consequently, for every \( Q_t\subseteq C \),

\[ \sum_{c\in Q_t}a_{t,c} =\frac{1}{2}\left|Q_t\cap\{u_t,v_t\}\right|. \]

Because \( u_t\neq v_t \), it follows that

\[ \sum_{c\in Q_t}a_{t,c}\geq\frac12 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Q_t\cap\{u_t,v_t\}\neq\varnothing. \]

Moreover, \( \ell=0 \) gives

\[ |Q_t\triangle Q_{t+1}|\leq0, \]

so \( Q_t=Q_{t+1} \) for every \( t \). Hence every feasible solution has a single common committee \( Q=Q_1=\cdots=Q_\tau \).

Correctness of the reduction

Suppose first that \( G \) has a vertex cover \( S \) with \( |S|\leq K \). Set \( Q_t=S \) for every stage \( t \). The size and transition constraints hold. Since \( S \) meets every edge \( \{u_t,v_t\} \), the preceding equivalence gives

\[ \sum_{c\in Q_t}a_{t,c}\geq\frac12=\rho \]

at every stage. Thus the rational-mass instance is affirmative.

Conversely, suppose the constructed instance has feasible committees \( Q_1,\ldots,Q_\tau \). Since \( \ell=0 \), they are all equal to some \( Q\). The cardinality constraint gives \( |Q|\leq K \). At each stage \( t \), the mass constraint implies \( Q\cap\{u_t,v_t\}\neq\varnothing \). Therefore \( Q \) meets every edge of \( G \), so \( Q \) is a vertex cover of size at most \( K \).

The construction has size \( O(|V|+|E|) \) apart from ordinary candidate identifiers and uses only the fixed rationals \( 0 \), \( 1/2 \), and \( 1 \). It is therefore a polynomial-time many-one reduction.

Relation to the stated discrete hardness result

The stated stronger restriction also transfers exactly. Given a discrete two-agent temporal SNTV instance, assign mass \( 1/2 \) to each agent’s temporal type, aggregating the masses if the two types coincide. If \( s_{t,c}\in\{0,1,2\} \) is the discrete SNTV tally, then

\[ a_{t,c}=\frac{s_{t,c}}{2}. \]

Thus

\[ \sum_{c\in Q_t}a_{t,c}\geq\frac12 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_{c\in Q_t}s_{t,c}\geq1. \]

Hence the supplied hardness theorem yields hardness even with \( \ell=0 \), \( \rho=1/2 \), support size at most two, and \( k=|C|/2 \).

Conclusion

Rational-Mass Conservative Multistage SNTV is \( \mathrm{NP\text{-}complete} \). In particular, it is not polynomial-time solvable unless \( \mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP} \).

Verification Report

Summary

The prover correctly establishes NP-membership and gives a valid polynomial-time reduction from Vertex Cover. The reduction’s central equivalence is correct: with two types of mass \(1/2\), the mass threshold at stage \(t\) is met exactly when the common committee contains an endpoint of edge \(e_t\). Setting \(\ell=0\) forces one committee across all stages, which is precisely a vertex cover.

The conclusion that the problem is NP-complete is therefore sound. Two minor presentational gaps remain, neither affecting the result.

Step-by-step audit

1. Membership in NP

The proposed certificate has \(\tau\) characteristic vectors of length \(|C|\), hence size \(O(\tau |C|)\). Since both the candidates and stage coordinates occur explicitly in the input, this is polynomial in the input length.

All feasibility conditions are polynomial-time checkable:

  • Committee cardinalities require ordinary counting.
  • Symmetric differences require comparing two characteristic vectors.
  • Stage masses require summing finitely many binary-encoded rationals.

The rational-arithmetic justification is correct. Multiplying all denominators produces a common denominator whose bit length is the sum of their bit lengths, so its representation remains polynomial. Exact arithmetic on the resulting polynomial-bit integers is polynomial-time.

No substantive gap occurs in NP-membership.

2. Construction of the reduction

For a graph \(G=(V,E)\) with enumerated edges \(e_t=\{u_t,v_t\}\), the construction sets:

  • \(C=V\),
  • one stage per edge,
  • \(k=K\),
  • \(\ell=0\),
  • \(\rho=1/2\),
  • two temporal types selecting the two oriented endpoints at each stage.

Because the graph is simple, \(u_t\neq v_t\). Thus each endpoint receives mass exactly \(1/2\) at stage \(t\), while every other candidate receives mass \(0\). The displayed formula

\[ \sum_{c\in Q_t}a_{t,c} =\frac12\left|Q_t\cap\{u_t,v_t\}\right| \]

is correct.

It follows correctly that the threshold is met exactly when \(Q_t\) contains at least one endpoint of \(e_t\).

3. Effect of the transition bound

With \(\ell=0\),

\[ |Q_t\triangle Q_{t+1}|\leq 0 \]

implies \(Q_t=Q_{t+1}\). Transitivity therefore gives a common committee at all stages. The argument also works when there is only one stage, where the unique committee is trivially common.

4. Forward correctness

Given a vertex cover \(S\) of size at most \(K\), taking \(Q_t=S\) at every stage satisfies:

  • \(|Q_t|\leq k\),
  • all transition constraints,
  • every mass threshold, because \(S\) meets every corresponding edge.

This direction is complete.

5. Reverse correctness

Given feasible committees, \(\ell=0\) makes them all equal to some \(Q\). The size condition gives \(|Q|\leq K\), and the mass condition at every stage forces \(Q\) to meet every edge. Therefore \(Q\) is a vertex cover of the required size.

This direction is complete.

6. Polynomial size

The construction uses two sequences of \(|E|\) candidate identifiers and fixed rational masses. Its precise bit size is generally \(O(|V|\log |V|+|E|\log |V|)\), depending on the candidate encoding, rather than literally \(O(|V|+|E|)\). The prover’s qualification “apart from ordinary candidate identifiers” makes the intended claim clear, and polynomiality is unaffected.

7. Transfer from the stated discrete result

The scaling identity \(a_{t,c}=s_{t,c}/2\) is correct, including when the two complete temporal types coincide and their masses are aggregated.

However, the assertion that the discrete restriction “transfers exactly” silently assumes that the discrete theorem uses the same at-most-\(k\) committee convention. If the discrete formulation requires committees of exactly \(k\) candidates, a padding argument or an explicit statement of monotonicity is needed. This does not affect the independent Vertex Cover reduction.

Issues

  • The sentence restricting Vertex Cover to \(E\neq\varnothing\) does not explicitly provide the standard reduction proving that this restriction remains NP-hard. Since every excluded instance is affirmative, a fixed affirmative nonempty-edge instance can handle that case, but this routine mapping should be stated.
  • The claimed exact transfer of the supplied discrete theorem should clarify whether committee size means exactly \(k\) or at most \(k\). This is ancillary and does not undermine the main reduction.
