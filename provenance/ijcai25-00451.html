The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational candidate positions \(p_1,\ldots,p_m\in\mathbb{Q}^d\), a positional scoring vector, target \(c^\star\), and finitely many types \(t\) with rational boxes \(B_t\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^d\) and masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), let \(A_t\) be the score vectors realizable by points in \(B_t\) under nearest-candidate ranking and fixed tie-breaking. Decide whether there are variables \(x_{t,z}\ge0\) such that \(\sum_{z\in A_t}x_{t,z}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\), and \(\sum_t\sum_{z\in A_t}x_{t,z}(z(c^\star)-z(c))\ge0\) for every \(c\ne c^\star\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

There are four candidates at rational positions \(q_1<q_2<q_3<q_4\). A type \(t\) has interval \(I_t=[\ell_t,u_t]\), per-unit weight \(w_t\), and mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). A position in \(I_t\) induces a ranking by increasing distance to the candidate positions, using a fixed tie-breaking rule; let \(\Pi_t\) be the induced rankings. Under four-candidate Borda, let \(b_\pi(c)\) denote candidate \(c\)'s score in ranking \(\pi\).

Target

Is this continuous weighted possible-winner problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results

For finite weighted voters in one dimension, possible winner under Borda is NP-complete already with \(m=4\) candidates.

Prover output (gpt-5.6-sol / xhigh, Bolzano prover prompt)

Complexity Classification

Formal mass-assignment model

Fix a designated candidate \(p\). Interpret the continuous formulation as follows: for every type \(t\), its mass \(\mu_t\) may be divided arbitrarily among positions in \(I_t\). Equivalently, type \(t\) chooses a finite nonnegative measure \(\nu_t\) supported on \(I_t\), with \(\nu_t(I_t)=\mu_t\).

A unit of type-\(t\) mass has voting weight \(w_t\). Thus candidate \(c\)'s total Borda score is

\[ S_c=\sum_t w_t\int_{I_t}b_{r(x)}(c)\,d\nu_t(x), \]

where \(r(x)\) is the ranking induced at position \(x\), including the fixed tie-breaking rule.

The possible-winner question asks whether there are such measures for which \(S_p\geq S_c\) for every \(c\neq p\).

Lemma 1: Each interval induces only constantly many ranking cells

For candidates \(i\neq j\), their distances from \(x\) are equal precisely when

\[ x=\frac{q_i+q_j}{2}. \]

There are at most \(\binom{4}{2}=6\) such midpoints. Between two consecutive distinct midpoints, every comparison between candidate distances has a fixed sign. Consequently, the induced ranking is constant on each such open interval. At a midpoint, the fixed tie-breaking rule determines one additional ranking.

After intersecting these cells with \(I_t\), there are at most seven positive-length cells and six singleton cells. Hence

\[ |\Pi_t|\leq 13. \]

These cells and their rankings can be enumerated using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

Lemma 2: Feasible score vectors are exactly fractional ranking assignments

For an allocation \(\nu_t\), define

\[ x_{t,\pi} = \nu_t\bigl(\{x\in I_t:r(x)=\pi\}\bigr) \qquad (\pi\in\Pi_t). \]

Then

\[ x_{t,\pi}\geq 0, \qquad \sum_{\pi\in\Pi_t}x_{t,\pi}=\mu_t. \]

Because \(b_{r(x)}(c)\) is constant on every ranking cell,

\[ S_c = \sum_t w_t\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_t}b_\pi(c)x_{t,\pi}. \]

Conversely, suppose numbers \(x_{t,\pi}\) satisfy the displayed simplex constraints. For every \(\pi\in\Pi_t\), choose a representative position \(z_{t,\pi}\in I_t\) inducing \(\pi\), and set

\[ \nu_t=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_t}x_{t,\pi}\delta_{z_{t,\pi}}, \]

where \(\delta_z\) is the unit point mass at \(z\). This is a feasible type-\(t\) mass assignment and realizes exactly the prescribed variables.

Thus no information relevant to Borda scores is lost by replacing continuous position assignments with the finitely many variables \(x_{t,\pi}\).

The linear program

Candidate \(p\) is a possible co-winner if and only if the following rational linear system is feasible:

\[ x_{t,\pi}\geq 0 \qquad (t,\pi\in\Pi_t), \]

\[ \sum_{\pi\in\Pi_t}x_{t,\pi}=\mu_t \qquad (t), \]

and

\[ \sum_t w_t\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_t} \bigl(b_\pi(p)-b_\pi(c)\bigr)x_{t,\pi} \geq 0 \qquad (c\neq p). \]

The equivalence follows directly from Lemma 2.

For \(T\) types, this system has at most \(13T\) variables, \(T\) mass equalities, and three winner inequalities. All coefficients are rational and have polynomial encoding length: the Borda differences are integers between \(-3\) and \(3\), while \(w_t\) and \(\mu_t\) are input rationals. Rational linear-program feasibility is decidable in polynomial time.

The unique-winner convention is also polynomial-time solvable: maximize a variable \(\varepsilon\) subject to replacing the three winner inequalities by \(S_p-S_c\geq\varepsilon\), and accept exactly when the optimum satisfies \(\varepsilon>0\).

Boundary conventions

If arbitrary measures are allowed, singleton midpoint cells are included as above.

If allocations are required to be absolutely continuous, a singleton cell cannot receive positive mass. One simply omits rankings realized only at isolated points. Every remaining fractional assignment is realizable by placing the prescribed mass on a sufficiently small positive-length subinterval of the corresponding cell. Hence this convention also yields a polynomial-size linear program.

Why the finite weighted hardness does not transfer

An indivisible finite voter of weight \(W\) must select one ranking and therefore contributes one of the discrete score vectors \(Wb_\pi\). In the continuous formulation, a type's mass can be divided among several rankings, so its feasible contribution is the convex hull of those vectors.

The integrality constraint responsible for the finite-voter formulation is therefore absent. The known finite weighted-voter NP-hardness does not transfer to the divisible mass-assignment problem.

Conclusion

Under the stated distribution-over-types interpretation, in which each type's mass is divisible, the continuous weighted possible-winner problem is polynomial-time solvable by rational linear programming. This holds in particular for four-candidate Borda and for either the co-winner or unique-winner convention.

If instead the entire mass of each type were required to choose one common position or ranking, that would be an indivisible-type problem and would not be the continuous mass-assignment formulation analyzed here.

Verification report

Summary

The prover gives a sound polynomial-time reduction to rational linear-program feasibility for a specific formalization: finitely many rationally encoded types whose masses may be divided arbitrarily among positions in their intervals.

However, the USER_TASK does not explicitly specify either this divisibility semantics or the encoding of type data. Consequently, the output establishes a useful conditional result but does not unconditionally classify the problem exactly as posed.

Step-by-step audit

1. Formalization of mass assignment

The prover introduces the requirement that each type may choose an arbitrary finite nonnegative measure of total mass \(\mu_t\). This is internally coherent, and it correctly permits atomic allocations.

It is not, however, stated in the USER_TASK that a type's mass is divisible in this manner. Other plausible interpretations include requiring each type to choose one position or one ranking, or requiring a distribution of a prescribed form. The prover acknowledges the indivisible interpretation only after proving its result.

Thus the sentence “Interpret the continuous formulation as follows” introduces a substantive hypothesis rather than deriving it from the task. The conclusion is valid under that hypothesis, but the hypothesis must be confirmed before it resolves the target problem.

2. Ranking-cell decomposition

For distinct candidates \(i,j\), equality of distances occurs exactly at \(x=(q_i+q_j)/2\). With four candidates, there are at most six distinct such points.

On every connected component outside those points, all pairwise distance comparisons have fixed signs, so the ranking is constant. A fixed tie-breaking rule gives a definite ranking at each midpoint. Therefore the bound \(|\Pi_t|\leq 13\) is correct, though generally loose.

Coincident midpoints cause no problem: they reduce the number of cells, and the tie-breaking rule still produces a single ranking at the coincident point.

The claim of polynomial-time exact enumeration requires the interval endpoints and the tie-breaking rule to have effective finite representations. Rationality is stated only for the candidate positions, not for \(\ell_t,u_t\). Without an input-encoding assumption, the complexity claim is not formally meaningful.

Degenerate intervals can create an isolated intersection point that is not a midpoint. This does not invalidate the bound or the LP, but the later wording “singleton midpoint cells” is too narrow.

3. Equivalence with fractional ranking assignments

Given a measure \(\nu_t\), defining \(x_{t,\pi}\) as the mass of the set inducing \(\pi\) is valid. These variables are nonnegative, sum to \(\mu_t\), and reproduce the Borda scores because the score is constant on each ranking class.

Conversely, choosing one representative point for every realizable ranking and placing an atom of mass \(x_{t,\pi}\) there correctly realizes any point in the corresponding simplex. This proves the claimed equivalence under the arbitrary-measure interpretation.

There is no hidden integrality requirement in this construction. Multiple disconnected cells inducing the same ranking also cause no problem because only their common score vector matters.

4. Linear-program formulation

The winner inequalities are exactly \(S_p-S_c\geq 0\) for each opponent \(c\), so LP feasibility is equivalent to possible co-winner status under the adopted model.

For \(T\) finitely represented types, the variable and constraint counts are linear in \(T\). If \(w_t,\mu_t,\ell_t,u_t\) are binary-encoded rationals, all relevant coefficients and comparisons have polynomial encoding length, and rational LP feasibility is polynomial-time decidable.

The prover explicitly assumes that \(w_t\) and \(\mu_t\) are input rationals, although the USER_TASK does not state this. It also implicitly needs finitely many types and effectively represented interval endpoints. These are necessary model hypotheses, not merely cosmetic details.

5. Unique-winner extension

Maximizing \(\varepsilon\) subject to \(S_p-S_c\geq\varepsilon\) is a correct test for strict victory. The write-up omits the routine justification that the feasible assignment region is compact and hence, whenever feasible, the maximum margin is attained. Because each type's variables lie in a fixed simplex, that justification is immediate and does not require new mathematics.

6. Absolutely continuous allocations

Omitting rankings realized only on isolated sets is correct for measures absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure.

For a ranking realized on a positive-length cell, prescribed mass can be realized by an absolutely continuous density supported inside that cell. The prover's phrase “placing the prescribed mass on” a subinterval should explicitly mean choosing an appropriate density; literal point placement would not be absolutely continuous.

If a positive-mass type has only isolated feasible positions, the resulting simplex is infeasible, as it should be. Degenerate intervals should be mentioned explicitly because their isolated point need not be a candidate midpoint.

7. Relation to finite-voter hardness

The distinction between indivisible weighted voters and divisible type masses is correct. The finite-voter hardness result cannot be transferred merely by treating a divisible type as an indivisible voter.

This explains why the cited hardness does not contradict the LP result. It does not establish anything about the complexity of the alternative indivisible-type interpretation, and the prover appropriately does not claim such a classification.

Required clarification

To make the result fully verifiable as an answer to the target, the problem statement must confirm that:

  • There are finitely many input types.
  • Each type's mass may be divided arbitrarily among positions in its interval.
  • The interval endpoints, masses, and weights are rational numbers given in a standard finite encoding.
  • The fixed tie-breaking rule is effectively evaluable.
  • Either arbitrary measures or absolutely continuous measures are intended; the prover has conditional treatments for both.

With those assumptions, the polynomial-time LP classification is supported by the written argument.
