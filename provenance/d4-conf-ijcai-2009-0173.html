The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite goods set \(G\), bidder types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), integral packages \(I_t\), payments \(p_t\), and rational per-capita inventory \(b_g\), choose accepted masses \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) to maximize \(\sum_{t\in T}p_tx_t\), subject to \(\sum_{t\in T}I_t(g)x_t\le b_g\) for every \(g\in G\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a finite goods set with \(m=|G|\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type submits one atomic bid with a package \(I_t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\) and payment \(p_t\in\mathbb Q\); negative payments mean the auctioneer pays the bidder. Inventory is \(b\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{G}\). Packages consume goods and produce none; unused inventory may be discarded, and there is no required final inventory.
Accepted masses \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) may be split arbitrarily and yield revenue \(\sum_{t\in T}p_tx_t\), subject to \(\sum_{t\in T}I_t(g)x_t\le b_g\) for every \(g\in G\). Integers and rationals are binary encoded; let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including a rational revenue threshold \(q\).

Is deciding whether there exist feasible accepted masses with revenue at least \(q\) solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

Discrete winner determination is NP-hard even with atomic bids and no goods produced, and also with atomic bids and no goods consumed. These hardness results concern accepting whole bid occurrences; they do not classify divisible accepted masses.

  • Payments are binary rationals of either sign, preserving the discrete model's allowance of negative payments.
  • Package coordinates are nonnegative integers, and per-capita inventory coordinates are nonnegative binary rationals.
  • The consumption-only question selects the anchor's no-production restriction; required final inventory is zero and free disposal is retained.
  • Each listed type submits one atomic bid, with no additional OR or XOR constraints.
  • A binary rational revenue threshold is added to express the optimization question as a decision problem.

Result

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable. An exact algorithm runs in \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations, and hence in \(L^{O(1)}\) for the explicitly listed input. Divisibility makes the problem a rational linear feasibility problem.

Exact formulation and proof

Enumerate the support as \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), and define \(A_{g,t}=I_t(g)\). Introduce one real variable \(x_t\) for each listed type. Consider the following system:

  • \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\in T\).
  • \(\sum_{t\in T}A_{g,t}x_t\le b_g\) for every \(g\in G\).
  • \(-\sum_{t\in T}p_tx_t\le-q\).

This system has \(\tau\) variables and \(m+2\tau+1\) inequalities.

Equivalence. If accepted masses meet the requested revenue threshold, they satisfy the first group by their permitted ranges, the second group by inventory feasibility, and the last inequality by the revenue requirement. Conversely, any solution to this system specifies permissible accepted masses, respects every inventory constraint, and has revenue at least \(q\). Arbitrary splitting is expressly permitted, so there is no additional integrality condition.

Thus the answer to the auction decision problem is exactly the feasibility answer for this rational linear system.

Polynomial running time and exactness

We use the standard polynomial-time rational linear feasibility theorem: given a rational matrix \(C\) and rational vector \(d\), feasibility of \(Cz\le d\) over the reals can be decided exactly in time polynomial in the number of variables, the number of inequalities, and the total binary encoding length. This is the linear programming result established by [Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

Its hypotheses hold here: all package entries are integers, and all masses, payments, inventories, and the threshold are rational and binary encoded. Forming the system only copies input coefficients, changes signs, and inserts coefficients from \(\{-1,0,1\}\).

Even using a dense representation, its encoding length \(B\) satisfies

\(B=O\!\left(L+m\tau+\tau^2\right).\)

The additional terms account for explicitly storing the inventory matrix and the bound rows, including zero entries. Construction takes \(O(L+m\tau+\tau^2)\) bit operations. Applying rational linear feasibility then takes

\(\operatorname{poly}\!\left(\tau,m+2\tau+1,B\right)=(m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\)

bit operations. Since the goods and support are explicitly listed, \(m\) and \(\tau\) are bounded by the input length, giving \(L^{O(1)}\) overall.

The theorem decides feasibility exactly, including equality at the revenue threshold and feasible sets of lower dimension. No numerical tolerance assumption is needed. This is a polynomial bit-complexity bound; it does not assert strongly polynomial running time.

Negative payments and discrete hardness

Negative payments cause no difficulty. Indeed, given any feasible acceptance vector, replacing \(x_t\) by zero whenever \(p_t<0\) weakly decreases every inventory use because \(I_t(g)\ge0\), and weakly increases revenue because \(p_tx_t\le0\). Hence these types may also be discarded before solving.

The cited discrete hardness results impose whole-occurrence acceptance. That restriction is absent here: each \(x_t\) ranges over an interval. Only the \(\tau\) listed types require variables; neither possible types nor bid occurrences need to be enumerated. The present problem belongs to \(\mathsf{P}\), so NP-hardness under polynomial-time reductions would imply \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).
