The continuous mirror question, as graded

Let \(T\) be a finite set of agent types, each with side, weight \(w_t\), compatible opposite-side types, and a strict ranking \(\succ_t\) over those types; let \(X_t\) be an atomless cohort of rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Copies of the same partner type are tied. A feasible matching \(\pi\) is a measurable one-to-one pairing of compatible agents, with unmatched agents allowed; write \(r_t^\pi(z)\) for the type of \(z\)’s partner, or \(\bot\) if unmatched, with \(\bot\) ranked last. Define \(\Delta(\pi,\pi')=\sum_{t\in T}\int_{X_t}w_t\bigl(\mathbf{1}[r_t^\pi(z)\succ_t r_t^{\pi'}(z)]-\mathbf{1}[r_t^{\pi'}(z)\succ_t r_t^\pi(z)]\bigr)\,d\lambda(z)\). Given the type graph and masses, does there exist a feasible \(\pi\) such that \(\Delta(\pi,\pi')\ge0\) for every feasible matching \(\pi'\)?

Result

For every fixed rational \(1<c\leq 2\), the problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard, even when all positive masses are equal and dyadic.

The key is an equivalence: an equal-mass atomless society admits a popular matching if and only if its finite type graph admits a popular matching. The reverse implication requires allowing the atomless incumbent to split each type among several partner types.

1. Definitions and two preliminary facts

For partner types \(p,q\), including \(\bot\), define the weighted vote
\[ \text{} \]
\( \displaystyle v_t(p,q)=w_t\bigl(\mathbf{1}[p\succ_t q]-\mathbf{1}[q\succ_t p]\bigr). \)

For finite matchings \(M,N\) on the type graph, let
\( \displaystyle D(N,M)=\sum_t v_t(N(t),M(t)). \)
Thus \(M\) is popular precisely when \(D(N,M)\leq 0\) for every finite matching \(N\). Likewise, by antisymmetry of the given comparison, an atomless matching \(\pi\) is popular precisely when \(\Delta(\pi',\pi)\leq 0\) for every feasible \(\pi'\).

We use the following facts.

Bipartite matching integrality. For a finite bipartite graph, the polytope
\( \displaystyle P(G)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{E}: \sum_{e\ni t}z_e\leq 1\ \text{for every }t\right\} \)
is the convex hull of the incidence vectors of finite matchings.

For completeness, orient every edge from the first side to the second. The oriented vertex-edge incidence matrix is totally unimodular: in any square submatrix, a column with at most one nonzero entry permits induction by determinant expansion; otherwise every column has two opposite entries, so the row sum is zero and the determinant vanishes. Changing the signs of the first-side rows gives the unsigned incidence matrix. Appending unit rows preserves total unimodularity. Consequently, every vertex of \(P(G)\), whose constraint right-hand sides are integral, is integral. Its integral points are exactly matching incidence vectors. Since \(P(G)\) is bounded, the stated convex-hull conclusion follows.

Atomless measure-space facts. A measurable subset of a finite interval has measurable subsets of every measure between zero and its own measure. Also, two such sets of equal positive Lebesgue measure admit a measurable, measure-preserving bijection modulo null sets.

The first assertion follows from continuity of the cumulative measure function. The second is the standard isomorphism theorem for atomless standard finite measure spaces: measurable subsets of intervals, after replacing them by Borel representatives modulo null sets, satisfy its hypotheses. These facts apply to every cohort subset used below. Working modulo null sets agrees with the problem’s convention.

2. Equal-mass equivalence: finite to atomless

Suppose every type has mass \(\delta=1/\tau\), and let \(M\) be a popular finite matching.

Construct \(\pi_M\) by matching entire cohorts along the edges of \(M\), using the identity between their interval coordinates. Leave the other cohorts unmatched. Every agent of type \(t\) then has incumbent partner type \(M(t)\).

Consider any feasible atomless challenger \(\pi'\). For each edge \(\{a,b\}\), let \(y_{ab}\) be the mass matched between its two endpoint types under \(\pi'\). Measure preservation makes this mass the same at both endpoints. Therefore
\( \displaystyle z_{ab}=\frac{y_{ab}}{\delta} \)
belongs to \(P(G)\).

Set \(h_t(p)=v_t(p,M(t))\), and define the affine function
\( \displaystyle F_M(z)= \sum_{\{a,b\}\in E}z_{ab}\bigl(h_a(b)+h_b(a)\bigr) + \sum_t\left(1-\sum_{e\ni t}z_e\right)h_t(\bot). \)

Because the incumbent partner type is constant throughout each cohort,
\( \displaystyle \Delta(\pi',\pi_M)=\delta F_M(z). \)

For every finite matching \(N\),
\( \displaystyle F_M(\mathbf{1}_N)=D(N,M)\leq 0. \)
By bipartite matching integrality, \(z\) is a convex combination of matching incidence vectors. Affineness therefore gives \(F_M(z)\leq 0\). Hence \(\pi_M\) is popular.

3. Equal-mass equivalence: atomless to finite

Now let \(\pi\) be any popular atomless matching with cohort masses \(\delta\). No homogeneity assumption is imposed on \(\pi\).

Write \(x_{ab}\) for its mass on edge \(\{a,b\}\), and write
\( \displaystyle u_t=\delta-\sum_{e\ni t}x_e \)
for the unmatched mass of type \(t\). Again, measure preservation gives consistent edge masses at both endpoints.

The vector \(z=x/\delta\) lies in \(P(G)\). Consequently,
\( \displaystyle z=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\theta_i\mathbf{1}_{M_i}, \qquad \theta_i>0,\qquad \sum_i\theta_i=1, \)
for finite matchings \(M_i\).

Two consequences of this decomposition are essential:

  • If \(x_e=0\), then no \(M_i\) contains \(e\), because every summand is nonnegative.
  • If \(u_t=0\), then every \(M_i\) matches \(t\). Indeed,

\( \displaystyle 1=\sum_i\theta_i\mathbf{1}[t\text{ is matched in }M_i], \)
and every indicator on the right is at most one.

Choose any one of these matchings, denoted \(M\). Thus every edge of \(M\) carries positive incumbent mass, and every type unmatched in \(M\) has positive incumbent unmatched mass.

We prove that \(M\) is popular by embedding each finite challenge into the same agents of the atomless incumbent.

Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) no larger than all the numbers
\( \displaystyle \{x_e:e\in M\}\ \cup\ \{u_t:t\text{ is unmatched in }M\}. \)
This is a nonempty finite collection of positive numbers, since \(\tau\geq 1\).

For each edge \(\{a,b\}\in M\), select a measurable subset of mass \(\varepsilon\) from the type-\(a\) agents whom \(\pi\) matches to type \(b\), and select their corresponding partners under \(\pi\). These partners also have mass \(\varepsilon\). For each type unmatched in \(M\), select mass \(\varepsilon\) from its incumbent unmatched agents.

Denote the resulting selected subset of cohort \(t\) by \(U_t\). The construction has three properties:

  • Every \(U_t\) has measure \(\varepsilon\).
  • Every selected agent has incumbent partner type \(M(t)\).
  • Every incumbent pair containing a selected agent has both endpoints selected.

Let \(N\) be an arbitrary finite matching. Construct an atomless challenger \(\pi_N\) as follows. For each edge \(\{a,b\}\in N\), pair \(U_a\) and \(U_b\) by a measurable, measure-preserving bijection. Leave the other selected sets unmatched, and retain \(\pi\) on all unselected agents.

Equal selected masses provide the required bijections. Property 3 ensures that retaining the incumbent outside the selected sets creates no conflict. Thus \(\pi_N\) is feasible.

Only selected agents change partner types, and their votes give exactly
\( \displaystyle \Delta(\pi_N,\pi) = \varepsilon\sum_t v_t(N(t),M(t)) = \varepsilon D(N,M). \)

Popularity of \(\pi\) implies that this quantity is nonpositive. Since \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(N\) was arbitrary, \(M\) is a popular finite matching.

We have therefore proved
\( \displaystyle G\text{ admits a popular finite matching} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{its equal-mass atomless society admits a popular matching}. \)

The embedded challenge compares the same selected agents across the two matchings, exactly as required by the question.

4. Hardness reduction and input size

For every fixed \(1<c\leq 2\), Heeger–Cseh’s Theorem 9 establishes that finite popular-matching existence is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete for strict bipartite preferences with weight \(c\) on one side and weight \(1\) on the other. [Heeger–Cseh, Theorem 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.05901v2)

Take such a finite instance with \(n\geq 1\) vertices. Add isolated vertices until the number of vertices is
\( \displaystyle \tau=2^{\lceil\log_2 n\rceil}. \)
Isolated vertices are unmatched in every finite matching and contribute zero to every comparison, so this padding preserves popular-matching existence.

Make each vertex a positive-mass type, retain all edges, rankings, and side weights, and assign
\( \displaystyle \mu_t=\frac1\tau \)
to every type. These masses are dyadic and sum to one. By the equivalence proved above, the resulting society admits a popular matching exactly when the original finite instance does.

The construction keeps \(m\) unchanged and has \(\tau<2n\) unless \(n\) already is a power of two. If the finite source has bit length \(L_{\mathrm{fin}}\), the resulting society has
\( \displaystyle L=O(L_{\mathrm{fin}}+\tau\log\tau). \)
The reduction uses \(O(m+\tau)\) graph operations and \(O(L_{\mathrm{fin}}+\tau\log\tau)\) bit operations, hence polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

This proves the requested \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness. Moreover, the equal-mass restriction is itself \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete: its equivalence with the finite problem also supplies membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\).
