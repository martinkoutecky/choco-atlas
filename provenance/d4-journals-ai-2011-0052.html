The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{\tau}\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\) and a symmetric rational type-interaction matrix \(W=(w_{tu})\) with \(w_{tt}=0\), define \(V_W(a)=\sum_{t<u}w_{tu}a_ta_u\) for \(a\in[0,\mu]\); decide whether there exists \(x\in\mathbb{R}^{\tau}\) such that \(\sum_t\mu_tx_t=V_W(\mu)\) and \(\sum_ta_tx_t\ge V_W(a)\) for every coalition-mass vector \(a\in[0,\mu]\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq 1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also contains a symmetric matrix \(W\in\mathbb{Q}^{\tau\times\tau}\) with \(w_{tt}=0\); off-diagonal entries may have either sign and are encoded in binary. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; running times are measured in \(\tau\) and \(L\), with no separate \(m\) parameter.
A coalition is any real mass vector \(a\) satisfying \(0\leq a_t\leq\mu_t\), and its worth is \(V_W(a)=\sum_{t<u}w_{tu}a_ta_u\). A payoff \(x_t\) is a real payoff per unit mass, constant within type.
Call the mass core nonempty if there exists \(x\in\mathbb{R}^{\tau}\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_tx_t=V_W(\mu)\) and \(\sum_ta_tx_t\geq V_W(a)\) for every coalition \(a\). These inequalities include coalitions supported on one type and enforce individual rationality.

Is deciding whether this mass core is nonempty solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete core nonemptiness is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete for graph games, whose coalition worth is the sum of signed edge weights induced by the coalition, and for nondeterministic polynomial-time compact representations admitting polynomial-time translations of graph games.
No complexity classification for the exact mass problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Removed zero-mass coordinates so that the listed types are exactly the positive-mass support.
  • Made explicit that interaction weights may be positive, zero, or negative, as in discrete graph games.
  • Specified binary encoding of rational interaction weights and defined L as total input bit length; this model has no separate m parameter.
  • Interpreted x_t as a payoff per unit mass, constant within type, and retained all real coalition-mass vectors, including coalitions supported on one type.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Classification

The mass-core nonemptiness problem is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions, even when all masses are equal positive dyadic rationals.

Consequently, it is polynomial-time solvable if and only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). It is also \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. Under many-one reductions, calling it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard would be equivalent to asserting \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\).

The key is an exact equivalence between the mass core and a discrete graph-game core.

2. Fractional coalitions introduce no additional constraints

Let \(N=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), and define symmetric edge weights and total type payoffs by

\[ b_{tu}=w_{tu}\mu_t\mu_u, \qquad y_t=\mu_tx_t. \]

For each \(S\subseteq N\), define

\[ v(S)=\sum_{\substack{t<u\\t,u\in S}}b_{tu}, \qquad y(S)=\sum_{t\in S}y_t. \]

Because every \(\mu_t\) is positive, the transformation between \(x\) and \(y\) is invertible.

Lemma. A payoff \(x\) belongs to the mass core if and only if \(y\) satisfies the discrete graph-game core constraints

\[ y(N)=v(N), \qquad y(S)\geq v(S)\quad\text{for every }S\subseteq N. \]

Proof. Necessity follows by considering the coalition that takes all of each type in \(S\) and none of the other types. Efficiency also transforms exactly.

For sufficiency, suppose the discrete constraints hold. Take any real coalition \(a\), and put \(p_t=a_t/\mu_t\). Thus \(0\leq p_t\leq1\). For every \(S\subseteq N\), define

\[ \lambda_S= \prod_{t\in S}p_t \prod_{t\notin S}(1-p_t). \]

These coefficients are nonnegative, and expanding the products gives

\[ \sum_{S\subseteq N}\lambda_S=1, \qquad \sum_{S\ni t}\lambda_S=p_t, \qquad \sum_{S\supseteq\{t,u\}}\lambda_S=p_tp_u \quad(t\ne u). \]

Consequently,

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_ta_tx_t-V_W(a) &=\sum_tp_ty_t-\sum_{t<u}b_{tu}p_tp_u\\ &=\sum_{S\subseteq N}\lambda_S\bigl(y(S)-v(S)\bigr)\\ &\geq0. \end{aligned} \]

This verifies every fractional coalition constraint. The zero diagonal is essential to this calculation: every interaction involves two distinct coordinates. \(\square\)

In particular, single-type coalitions are retained: the discrete singleton constraints give \(y_t\geq0\), equivalently \(x_t\geq0\).

3. An exact cut characterization and a canonical payoff

For \(S\subseteq N\), define its signed cut weight by

\[ c(S)=\sum_{\substack{t\in S\\u\in N\setminus S}}b_{tu}. \]

Each crossing edge occurs once in this sum.

Proposition. The mass core is nonempty if and only if \(c(S)\geq0\) for every \(S\subseteq N\). Whenever it is nonempty, a core payoff is

\[ x_t^*=\frac12\sum_{u\ne t}w_{tu}\mu_u. \]

Proof. Suppose first that the discrete core contains \(y\). Adding the constraints for \(S\) and its complement gives

\[ v(N)=y(N)\geq v(S)+v(N\setminus S). \]

Every edge is either internal to one side or crosses the cut, so

\[ v(N)=v(S)+v(N\setminus S)+c(S). \]

Hence \(c(S)\geq0\).

Conversely, suppose every cut has nonnegative weight. Define

\[ y_t^*=\frac12\sum_{u\ne t}b_{tu}. \]

Summing over all vertices counts each edge twice before multiplication by one half, so \(y^*(N)=v(N)\). Summing over \(S\) counts internal edges twice and crossing edges once, giving

\[ y^*(S)=v(S)+\frac12c(S)\geq v(S). \]

Thus \(y^*\) belongs to the discrete core. The lemma places \(x_t^*=y_t^*/\mu_t\) in the mass core, yielding the stated formula. \(\square\)

This also shows why the existence of an explicit candidate payoff does not yield a polynomial-time decision algorithm: verifying that candidate requires deciding whether every signed cut is nonnegative.

4. Membership in \(\mathrm{coNP}\) and hardness

Membership

If the mass core is empty, the proposition supplies a subset \(S\subseteq N\) with \(c(S)<0\).

Its characteristic vector has \(\tau\) bits. A verifier computes

\[ c(S)=\sum_{\substack{t\in S\\u\notin S}}w_{tu}\mu_t\mu_u \]

using exact rational arithmetic and checks its sign. There are at most \(\tau^2\) summands, and their products and sums have polynomial bit length in \(\tau\) and \(L\). Verification therefore takes polynomial time.

Mass-core emptiness belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), so nonemptiness belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\).

Hardness, including the mass encoding

Use the graph-game core nonemptiness theorem supplied in the question: this problem is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete for signed graph games. The graph representation and classification are also stated in [Greco, Malizia, Palopoli, and Scarcello, *On the Complexity of Core, Kernel, and Bargaining Set*, Section 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.3136).

Take a graph-game instance with \(n\geq1\) explicitly represented vertices and rational edge weights \(q_{tu}\). Missing edges have weight zero.

Let \(k\) be the least power of two with \(k\geq n\). Add \(k-n\) isolated vertices, extending all incident weights by zero. This preserves core nonemptiness: cuts in the padded graph have exactly the weights of their intersections with the original vertices, so the cut characterization applies.

Construct a mass instance with support size \(\tau=k\) and

\[ \mu_t=\frac1k, \qquad w_{tu}=k^2q_{tu}\quad(t\ne u), \qquad w_{tt}=0. \]

All masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one. The matrix is symmetric and satisfies the required diagonal condition. Moreover,

\[ w_{tu}\mu_t\mu_u=q_{tu}. \]

Thus the discrete game associated with the constructed mass instance is exactly the padded source game. Section 2 proves that their cores are simultaneously nonempty.

Finally, \(k<2n\), and multiplying a rational weight by \(k^2\) increases its encoding length by at most a constant times \(\log k\). The dense output matrix and all masses therefore have polynomial total bit length. This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction, proving \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness.

Together with membership, this proves the classification.

5. Interpretation of the requested alternatives

The exact classification is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete. Its complement, mass-core emptiness, is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

Therefore an oracle for nonemptiness decides any \(\mathrm{NP}\) problem by reducing to emptiness, querying the oracle once, and reversing its answer. This establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under Turing reductions.

For many-one reductions, an \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard language belonging to \(\mathrm{coNP}\) would imply \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{coNP}\), hence \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\). Conversely, that equality would make this \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete problem \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Thus the distinction between reduction notions is necessary.
