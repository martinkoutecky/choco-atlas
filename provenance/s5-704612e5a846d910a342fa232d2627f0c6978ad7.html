The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational valuation types \(v_\ell\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{2}\) with masses \(\mu_\ell\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), \(\sum_\ell\mu_\ell=1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_1,q_2\ge0\), find finite-support \(y_{\ell,b}\ge0\), \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{2}\), satisfying \(\sum_b y_{\ell,b}=\mu_\ell\) and \(\sum_{\ell,b}b_a y_{\ell,b}=q_a\), such that \(y_{\ell,b}y_{\ell',b'}>0\) and \(b'_a>0\) imply \(v_\ell\cdot b\ge v_\ell\cdot(b'-e_a)\); or report infeasibility.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) distinct valuation types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=2\) item types. Type \(\ell\) has additive valuation \(v_\ell\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\), mass \(\mu_\ell>0\), and \(\sum_\ell\mu_\ell=1\). Supplies per unit population are \(q_1,q_2\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\). All input rationals are binary encoded; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
An allocation assigns mass \(y_{\ell,b}\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) of type \(\ell\) to integer bundle \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{2}\), with only finitely many positive entries. Types may split across bundles; bundle coordinates are binary encoded and have no imposed upper bound.
Completeness requires \(\sum_b y_{\ell,b}=\mu_\ell\) for every \(\ell\) and \(\sum_{\ell,b}b_a y_{\ell,b}=q_a\) for \(a\in\{1,2\}\).
EFX requires, for every pair of positive entries \(y_{\ell,b},y_{\ell',b'}\) and every \(a\in\{1,2\}\) with \(b'_a>0\), that \(v_\ell\cdot b\ge v_\ell\cdot(b'-e_a)\), where \(e_a\) is the standard unit vector. This includes comparisons within a valuation type and removal of items valued at zero; equality is allowed.
The computational problem is to find such an allocation or report infeasibility.

Is this complete EFX mass-allocation problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with \(m=2\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many agents with nonzero nonnegative additive valuations and at most two item types, a complete EFX allocation always exists and can be found in time polynomial in the number of agents and the total number of individual items ([Gorantla–Marwaha–Velusamy](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.05186)).
No result settling the exact finite-support mass-allocation problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Excluded zero valuation vectors, carrying over the discrete setup's explicit assumption that every agent positively values at least one item type.
  • Removed zero-mass entries and used distinct valuation vectors as the listed support types.
  • Specified rational allocation masses and binary-encoded bundle coordinates to make exact computation well-defined.
  • Used m=2 for the number of item types; there is no finite total number of individual items in the normalized mass model.
  • Retained comparisons within each valuation type, including identical positive entries, because positive mass represents multiple agents; retained removal of zero-valued items as required by the discrete EFX definition.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Proved result. The problem is polynomial-time solvable for \(m=2\), for arbitrary support size \(\tau\). Every valid instance admits a complete EFX allocation. The algorithm below produces at most \(\tau+2\) positive allocation entries.

The key is to simulate the finite-agent algorithm with binary counts, skipping entire sequences of identical rounds.

1. The finite theorem used.

We use Theorem 3.1 of Gorantla–Marwaha–Velusamy: their Algorithm 1 returns a complete EFX allocation for finitely many agents with nonzero, nonnegative additive valuations and two item types. Their EFX definition permits removal of any item from the compared bundle, including an item valued at zero. [Paper, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.05186).

We prove that this particular algorithm can be simulated in polynomial time on the compressed input. Its stated running-time bound alone would not establish this.

2. Exact conversion to integer counts.

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all masses and supplies. Define

\(N=D,\qquad n_\ell=D\mu_\ell,\qquad M_a=Dq_a.\)

These are integers, \(n_\ell>0\), and \(\sum_\ell n_\ell=N\). Conceptually, create \(n_\ell\) agents of valuation \(v_\ell\), with \(M_a\) items of type \(a\).

We never expand these agents or items. Since the denominators are part of the input, \(\log D\le L\); consequently, \(N,n_\ell,M_a\) have \(O(L)\) bits.

A cohort is a triple \((\ell,c,B)\): it represents \(c>0\) agents of valuation type \(\ell\), all receiving bundle \(B\). Cohort counts and bundle coordinates are stored in binary.

3. Two compressed operations.

Fix an item type \(a\).

A complete round through a list of cohorts gives one type-\(a\) item to every represented agent. Performing \(r\) complete rounds changes each cohort bundle from \(B\) to \(B+r e_a\), leaving its count unchanged. This takes one update per cohort.

For a partial round giving items to the first \(s\) represented agents, scan the cohort counts. Entire cohorts before the stopping point receive one item; the cohort containing the stopping point splits into recipients and nonrecipients. Thus a partial round increases the number of cohorts by at most one.

These operations exactly reproduce individual assignments in the corresponding order.

4. Computing the compressed allocation.

Choose the virtual agent order so that copies of each valuation type form a consecutive block. Within any sorting tie, keep whole type blocks consecutive.

Define each type’s preferred item by

\(f(\ell)=1\) if \(v_{\ell,1}>v_{\ell,2}\), and \(f(\ell)=2\) otherwise.

This agrees with Algorithm 1’s tie convention.

Stage I: initial complete rounds.

Let

\(s_a=\sum_{\ell:f(\ell)=a}n_\ell,\qquad p=\min_{a:s_a>0}\left\lfloor\frac{M_a}{s_a}\right\rfloor.\)

The minimum is over a nonempty set because \(N>0\).

Give type \(\ell\) the bundle \(B_\ell=p e_{f(\ell)}\), and set the remaining item counts to

\(u_a=M_a-p s_a.\)

This exactly performs the initial complete rounds. If both remaining counts vanish, return the cohorts.

Stage II: allocating the scarce item.

Choose \(a=1\) if \(u_1<s_1\); otherwise choose \(a=2\). Let \(b\) be the other item type and \(h=u_a\).

The choice is valid: maximality of \(p\) ensures that some nonempty preference group cannot receive another complete round. Hence the chosen group satisfies

\(0\le h<s_a.\)

Sort valuation types by decreasing \(v_{\ell,a}/v_{\ell,b}\), interpreting a zero denominator as \(+\infty\). Give one type-\(a\) item to the first \(h\) represented agents in this sorted order.

All selected agents prefer \(a\), weakly: their ratios precede every ratio belonging to agents preferring \(b\). Thus every selected agent now has bundle

\(B_*=(p+1)e_a.\)

Call the selected cohorts \(\mathcal A\) and the remaining cohorts \(\mathcal C\). Selection cuts at most one type block, so there are at most \(\tau+1\) cohorts.

All remaining items have type \(b\). Write their count as \(U=u_b\). The number of agents represented by \(\mathcal C\) is

\(n_C=N-h>0.\)

If \(U=0\), return.

Stage III: computing the first envy-producing round.

During this stage, only agents in \(\mathcal C\) receive items. After \(r\) complete rounds, their bundles are

\(B_j+r e_b,\qquad j\in I_C,\)

where \(I_C\) is the set of valuation types represented in \(\mathcal C\). The bundles in \(\mathcal A\) remain \(B_*\). Let \(I_A\) denote the types represented there.

For \(i\in I_A\) and \(j\in I_C\), define

\(d_{ij}=v_i\cdot B_*-v_i\cdot B_j.\)

Here \(d_{ij}\ge0\). Indeed, a selected agent values item \(a\) at least as much as item \(b\); \(B_j\) contains \(p\) items, whereas \(B_*\) contains \(p+1\) items of type \(a\).

After \(r\) complete rounds, an agent of type \(i\) in \(\mathcal A\) strictly envies a type-\(j\) agent in \(\mathcal C\) exactly when

\(r v_{i,b}>d_{ij}.\)

If \(v_{i,b}=0\), this never occurs. Otherwise, the first such integer round is

\(t_{ij}=\left\lfloor\frac{d_{ij}}{v_{i,b}}\right\rfloor+1.\)

Set

\(t=\min_{\substack{i\in I_A,\ j\in I_C\\v_{i,b}>0}}t_{ij},\)

with \(t=+\infty\) if the indexing set is empty. Also set

\(R=\left\lfloor\frac{U}{n_C}\right\rfloor.\)

There are two cases.

  • If \(t>R\), perform \(R\) complete rounds through \(\mathcal C\), followed by a partial round of length \(U-Rn_C\), and return.
  • If \(t\le R\), perform exactly \(t\) complete rounds through \(\mathcal C\), replace \(U\) by \(U-tn_C\), and proceed unless \(U=0\).

This reproduces the stage exactly. Its stopping test occurs at complete-round boundaries; the floor formula finds the first boundary with strict envy. If supply runs out during a round, the partial-round operation reproduces that termination.

Stage IV: allocating the remaining items.

Order the cohorts with \(\mathcal A\) first and \(\mathcal C\) second, maintaining the virtual agent order within each part.

Compute

\(k=\left\lfloor\frac{U}{N}\right\rfloor,\qquad \rho=U-kN.\)

Perform \(k\) complete type-\(b\) rounds through this list, followed by a partial round of length \(\rho\), and return.

5. Correctness of the simulation.

The constructed cohorts describe exactly one execution of Algorithm 1:

  • Stage I batches its initial preferred-item rounds.
  • Stage II implements its ratio-sorted selection, with an admissible tie order.
  • Stage III computes its first strict-envy stopping boundary exactly, or reproduces supply exhaustion.
  • Stage IV batches its final rounds in the prescribed selected-agents-first order.

No intermediate allocation or stopping boundary relevant to that execution is changed.

The finite instance satisfies every hypothesis of Theorem 3.1: it has finitely many agents, two item types, and the original nonzero, nonnegative additive valuations. Supplies are nonnegative integers. Therefore, its resulting allocation is complete and EFX.

Convert the final cohorts into masses by setting

\(y_{\ell,B}=\frac{1}{N} \sum_{(\ell,c,B)\text{ final cohort}}c,\)

and omit zero entries. Completeness follows from

\(\sum_B y_{\ell,B}=\frac{n_\ell}{N}=\mu_\ell,\qquad \sum_{\ell,B}B_a y_{\ell,B}=\frac{M_a}{N}=q_a.\)

To verify mass-model EFX, consider two positive entries. If their bundles differ, they correspond to distinct agents in the finite allocation, even when their valuation types coincide. The finite EFX inequality therefore applies. If their bundles coincide, the required inequality follows directly from

\(v_\ell\cdot B-v_\ell\cdot(B-e_a)=v_{\ell,a}\ge0.\)

This also covers deletion of zero-valued items and the self-comparisons required in the question.

6. Support size and bit complexity.

Initially there are \(\tau\) cohorts. Stage II introduces at most one additional cohort.

Complete rounds never split cohorts. There is at most one nonempty partial round in the entire execution: either Stage III terminates with supply exhaustion, or Stage IV performs the final partial round. It introduces at most one further cohort. Consequently, the output has at most \(\tau+2\) positive entries.

Every final bundle coordinate is bounded by the corresponding finite item count \(M_a\), and every cohort count is at most \(N\). They therefore have \(O(L)\) bits. Output masses also have \(O(L)\) bits.

The envy thresholds have polynomial encoding length as well. Since every positive input valuation coordinate lies between \(2^{-L}\) and \(2^L\),

\(0\le d_{ij}\le(p+1)v_{i,a},\qquad \frac{d_{ij}}{v_{i,b}}\le(p+1)2^{2L}\)

whenever \(v_{i,b}>0\). Moreover, \(p\le M_1+M_2\), so each finite threshold has \(O(L)\) bits.

There is one sort of \(\tau\) ratios, at most \(\tau^2\) threshold calculations, and a constant number of cohort scans. All comparisons, divisions, and updates use exact arithmetic on integers of \(O(L)\) bits. Thus the total running time is polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), with \(m=2\).

The formula for \(p\) correctly excludes preference groups of size zero. Such a group imposes no restriction on complete rounds. Since \(N>0\), at least one group is nonempty, so the minimum is defined and finite.

The resulting bundles and remaining counts agree with the finite algorithm after its initial rounds. This also handles \(p=0\) and zero supplies. Returning when both remaining counts vanish is correct.

  • Stage II: scarce-item selection

The choice of \(a\) is valid. If \(u_1<s_1\), then \(s_1>0\). Otherwise, maximality of \(p\) forces \(u_2<s_2\), hence \(s_2>0\). Therefore \(0\le h<s_a\).

The ratio ordering places the chosen preference group before the other group:

  • For \(a=1\), that group has ratios strictly greater than \(1\), whereas the other group has ratios at most \(1\).
  • For \(a=2\), that group has ratios at least \(1\), whereas the other group has ratios strictly less than \(1\).

Zero denominators cause no undefined \(0/0\), because valuation vectors are nonzero. Interpreting those ratios as \(+\infty\) is consistent with the required ordering.

Thus every selected agent previously received \(p\) items of type \(a\), and its new bundle is indeed \(B_*=(p+1)e_a\). Sorting whole type blocks and selecting a prefix splits at most one block.

The stated inequality \(d_{ij}\ge0\) is correct. A selected agent weakly prefers \(a\), while every remaining agent initially has exactly \(p\) items of one type.

\[ r v_{i,b}>d_{ij}. \]
For \(v_{i,b}>0\), the least integer satisfying this strict inequality is the stated floor expression. In particular, the additional \(1\) correctly handles equality. For \(v_{i,b}=0\), envy never arises in these comparisons.

All selected agents have the same bundle throughout this stage, so they cannot strictly envy one another. Testing selected-to-remaining comparisons therefore captures the finite algorithm’s entire stopping condition.

The cited algorithm tests envy at complete-round boundaries and returns immediately if supply is exhausted during a round. Both branches of the compressed version match this behavior. The empty selected-set case gives \(t=+\infty\) and correctly continues until exhaustion.

Although a valuation type can occur in both parts after selection, \(B_j\) is unambiguous for its remaining cohort: all its unselected copies still have their Stage I bundle.

The final order puts selected agents first and remaining agents second, as in Algorithm 1. There are no allocation-dependent stopping tests in this stage. Batching complete rounds and then assigning the final prefix therefore preserves its output.

  • A zero-valued removed item gives equality in the latter calculation.
