Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) publicly known cohort types with positive binary-rational masses \(w_j\), normalized by \(\sum_j w_j=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each cohort is atomless. Its members independently draw private service values from an explicitly listed rational prior \(F_j\) with nonempty finite support \(V_j\subseteq[0,1]\). Put \(m=\sum_j|V_j|\), and let \(L\) be the input bit length.
Use the exact aggregation convention: truthful report masses are \(\mu^*_{jv}=w_jF_j(v)\), and a single tagged agent's deviation leaves these masses unchanged. Reports in cohort \(j\) belong to \(V_j\).
An allocation at report masses \(\mu\) is represented by rational served masses \(0\leq s_{jv}\leq\mu_{jv}\), realized by measurable subsets receiving binary service. Cohort symmetry means a tagged agent reporting \(v\) receives service with conditional probability \(s_{jv}/\mu_{jv}\) when \(\mu_{jv}>0\); zero-mass report cells have separately specified tagged service probabilities.
The report-independent per-capita cost is \(c(t)\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\cup\{\infty\}\), where \(t_j=\sum_v s_{jv}\); infinite cost denotes infeasibility, and \(c(0)=0\). No downward closure or cost monotonicity is assumed. Welfare under truthful reports is \(W=\mathbb E[\sum_{j,v}v s_{jv}-c(t)]\).
You have query access to a possibly randomized allocation oracle \(A\), accepting any rational aggregate report measures and a tagged cohort/report and returning a jointly realizable feasible allocation as above, together with tagged service. Cost evaluation is also available by oracle. Replies have polynomial encoding length; count oracle calls and all other bit operations. Priors can be sampled conditionally on any nonempty subset of their supports.
A mechanism must provide such allocations and tagged payments, with risk-neutral utility \(vx-p\). Payments may have either sign and must have finite expectations. Require exact BIC for every tagged agent against every allowed report when others report truthfully; expectations include all mechanism randomness, including randomized preprocessing.
Is there a uniform black-box transformation that, for every such \(A\) and rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), constructs and implements a BIC mechanism \(M\) satisfying \(W(M)\geq W(A)-\varepsilon\), with expected preprocessing and per-invocation time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\) and the invocation's encoding length?
For finite independent single-parameter agents with values in \([0,1]\), an additive-loss black-box BIC transformation runs in time polynomial in \(n,1/\varepsilon\); with exact functional access, welfare can be preserved without loss. [Hartline–Lucier](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.4756v2).

Result and benchmark interpretation

Yes. A uniform transformation achieves exact BIC with no welfare loss. It uses at most $m$ allocation-oracle calls and $m$ cost-oracle calls per invocation, with polynomial expected bit complexity.

The repair to the previous argument is to query every allowed tag, independently of the actual tagged report, and select the best realized candidate. This avoids assuming that the allocation distribution returned by $A$ is independent of its tag input.

I use the allocation-level welfare specified in the task: welfare is computed from the returned society-wide served-mass vector. The construction dominates every fixed-tag truthful evaluation of $A$, and every probability mixture of those evaluations. Thus it covers both a tag-independent allocation oracle and a tag-dependent oracle evaluated using a fixed or randomly sampled truthful tag.

1. Explicit treatment of tag dependence

Let
$$ \mathcal T=\{(j,v):1\leq j\leq\tau,\ v\in V_j\}, \qquad |\mathcal T|=m. $$

For each $a\in\mathcal T$, let $S^a$ denote the random served-mass vector returned by a query $A(\mu^*,a)$. Define its allocation welfare by
$$ Z_a= \sum_{j,v}vS^a_{jv} - c\!\left(\left(\sum_v S^a_{jv}\right)_{j=1}^{\tau}\right), \qquad W_a(A)=\mathbb E[Z_a]. $$

No equality between these distributions or expectations is assumed.

If the truthful benchmark supplies tag $a$ with probability $\rho_a$, the law of total expectation gives
$$ W(A)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal T}\rho_a W_a(A). $$

This includes a fixed tag, and the natural protocol that samples a truthful member, for which $\rho_{(j,v)}=w_jF_j(v)$.

We will prove the stronger guarantee
$$ W(M)\geq \max\left\{0,\max_{a\in\mathcal T}W_a(A)\right\}. $$

Consequently, the transformation need not know which tag-selection protocol defines the benchmark.

2. Rearranging one feasible allocation

Fix any valid rational report masses $\mu$ and any feasible served-mass vector $s$ returned by the oracle. Write
$$ t_j=\sum_{v\in V_j}s_{jv}. $$

For each cohort, construct a vector $R_\mu(s)$ that preserves $t_j$ and serves higher reports first.

If $t_j=0$, set every served mass in that cohort to zero.

Otherwise, enumerate its support as
$$ v_{j1}>v_{j2}>\cdots>v_{jk_j}. $$

Feasibility gives
$$ 0<t_j\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_j}\mu_{jv_{j\ell}}. $$

Hence there is a smallest index $h_j$ such that
$$ \sum_{\ell=1}^{h_j}\mu_{jv_{j\ell}}\geq t_j. $$

Put
$$ b_j=v_{jh_j}, \qquad r_j=t_j-\sum_{\ell<h_j}\mu_{jv_{j\ell}}. $$

By minimality,
$$ 0<r_j\leq\mu_{jb_j}. $$

In particular, the boundary cell has positive mass. Define
$$ s'_{jv}= \begin{cases} \mu_{jv},&v>b_j,\\ r_j,&v=b_j,\\ 0,&v<b_j. \end{cases} $$

Then
$$ 0\leq s'_{jv}\leq\mu_{jv}, \qquad \sum_v s'_{jv}=t_j. $$

Thus $s'=R_\mu(s)$ preserves the entire vector of cohort totals. Because cost and infeasibility depend only on that vector, $s'$ has exactly the same finite cost as $s$ and is feasible.

Welfare comparison

For a cohort with $t_j>0$, preservation of its total implies
$$ \sum_v v(s'_{jv}-s_{jv}) = \sum_v(v-b_j)(s'_{jv}-s_{jv}). $$

Each summand on the right is nonnegative:

  • For $v>b_j$, both $v-b_j>0$ and $s'_{jv}-s_{jv}=\mu_{jv}-s_{jv}\geq0$.
  • For $v<b_j$, both $v-b_j<0$ and $s'_{jv}-s_{jv}=-s_{jv}\leq0$.
  • For $v=b_j$, the summand is zero.

When $t_j=0$, the cell bounds force both allocations to serve zero mass in that cohort. Therefore
$$ \sum_{j,v}v\,R_\mu(s)_{jv}-c(t) \geq \sum_{j,v}v s_{jv}-c(t). $$

This is a pointwise comparison, requiring neither downward closure nor cost monotonicity.

3. The transformation

Preprocessing sorts each cohort’s support in decreasing order and fixes an enumeration of $\mathcal T$.

On an invocation with report masses $\mu$ and actual tagged cohort/report $(j,r)$:

  • For every $a\in\mathcal T$, query $A(\mu,a)$. Retain its served-mass vector $S^a$ and discard its tagged-service output.
  • Construct $R_\mu(S^a)$ for every reply. Evaluate its cost and compute its report-welfare score

$$ H_a(\mu)= \sum_{j,v}v\,R_\mu(S^a)_{jv} - c\!\left(\left(\sum_v S^a_{jv}\right)_{j=1}^{\tau}\right). $$

  • Include the all-zero allocation as an additional candidate, with score zero. It is feasible because $c(0)=0$.
  • Choose a candidate with maximum score, using a fixed rule to break ties.
  • Return that candidate’s served masses, together with tagged service and payment as defined below.

All query tags, their order, the scores, and the selection rule are independent of the actual tagged cohort/report. The actual report is used only to determine tagged service and payment after a candidate has been selected.

Every selected allocation is feasible: it is either the zero allocation or a rearrangement preserving the totals of a feasible oracle reply.

4. Tagged service, payments, and realizability

For a selected rearranged candidate, define tagged service probabilities separately in each cohort.

If $t_j=0$, set
$$ q_j(v)=0 \quad\text{for every }v\in V_j. $$

Otherwise, using its boundary $b_j$ and remainder $r_j$, set
$$ q_j(v)= \begin{cases} 1,&v>b_j,\\ r_j/\mu_{jb_j},&v=b_j,\\ 0,&v<b_j. \end{cases} $$

These definitions also specify probabilities for zero-mass report cells. For every positive-mass cell,
$$ q_j(v)=\frac{s'_{jv}}{\mu_{jv}}. $$

For the actual tagged report $r$, return binary service $X$ with probability $q_j(r)$ and payment
$$ P= \begin{cases} b_jX,&t_j>0,\\ 0,&t_j=0. \end{cases} $$

For the zero candidate, return $X=P=0$.

Joint realizability

Only a boundary cell can be partially served. Its service fraction is rational; write it as $a/d$, where $0<a\leq d$.

Use the standard divisibility theorem for finite atomless measures: a measurable set of finite mass admits a measurable subset of any prescribed mass between zero and its total mass. The measure restricted to a positive-mass report cell is finite and atomless, so repeated application partitions that cell into $d$ measurable pieces of equal mass.

Choose uniformly among $d$ cyclic shifts and serve $a$ consecutive pieces. Every realization serves exactly the required boundary mass, and every member of that cell has service probability $a/d$. Serve cells above the boundary fully and cells below it not at all.

Applying this construction in every cohort realizes the returned masses and tagged-service probabilities jointly. Tagged service can be coupled to the corresponding cyclic shift. Under the task’s stipulated representation, the algorithm outputs the rational masses and tagged outcome; it does not enumerate the measurable partition.

Finally,
$$ 0\leq P\leq1, $$
so payments have finite expectations.

5. Exact incentive compatibility

Fix any tagged agent in cohort $j$, with true value $v\in V_j$, while all others report truthfully.

By the exact aggregation convention, every allowed report leaves the aggregate masses equal to $\mu^*$. Every oracle query therefore has the same input under every deviation: the masses are unchanged, and the query tags are the fixed enumeration of $\mathcal T$.

Consequently, the distribution of the complete candidate collection and the selected allocation is independent of this agent’s report. We may couple those random choices identically across all reports.

Condition on a selected candidate. If $t_j=0$, every report gives utility zero. Otherwise, conditional expected utility from report $r$ is
$$ U_j(v,r)=(v-b_j)q_j(r). $$

There are three cases.

  • If $v>b_j$, truthful reporting gives $q_j(v)=1$. Since $q_j(r)\leq1$,

$$ U_j(v,v)=v-b_j\geq(v-b_j)q_j(r)=U_j(v,r). $$

  • If $v<b_j$, truthful reporting gives $q_j(v)=0$. Since $q_j(r)\geq0$,

$$ U_j(v,v)=0\geq(v-b_j)q_j(r)=U_j(v,r). $$

  • If $v=b_j$, every report gives expected utility zero.

Thus every incentive inequality holds conditional on every selected candidate. Averaging over oracle randomness, selection randomness, and service randomness preserves the inequalities exactly. Preprocessing is deterministic.

The mechanism is therefore exactly BIC for every tagged agent against every allowed report.

6. Welfare guarantee without tag consistency

Evaluate the mechanism at truthful masses $\mu^*$, and let $H$ be its selected allocation’s welfare.

For each queried tag $a$, the rearrangement inequality gives
$$ H_a(\mu^*)\geq Z_a. $$

Selection then gives, simultaneously for every $a$,
$$ H=\max\left\{0,\max_{a\in\mathcal T}H_a(\mu^*)\right\} \geq Z_a. $$

Taking expectations yields
$$ W(M)=\mathbb E[H]\geq W_a(A) \quad\text{for every }a\in\mathcal T. $$

This argument does not require independence between the candidate replies; it requires only that each query have its prescribed oracle law.

For any truthful benchmark tag distribution $\rho$,
$$ W(M) \geq \sum_{a\in\mathcal T}\rho_aW_a(A) = W(A). $$

The zero candidate also ensures $H\geq0$. At truthful masses, gross value is at most one, and costs are nonnegative, so
$$ 0\leq H\leq1. $$

Thus the transformed welfare expectation is finite. In particular,
$$ W(M)\geq W(A)-\varepsilon $$
for every permitted $\varepsilon$.

7. Oracle calls and bit complexity

Let $B$ be the invocation’s encoding length.

Sorting the explicitly listed rational supports takes polynomial preprocessing time in $m,\tau,L$.

Each invocation uses:

  • exactly $m$ allocation-oracle calls;
  • at most $m$ cost-oracle calls;
  • polynomially many exact rational arithmetic operations to compute totals, rearrangements, scores, and the maximum.

Each rearrangement processes $m$ cells, so all candidates require polynomially many operations. By the polynomial reply-length promise, the arithmetic operands have polynomial encoding length in the stated parameters and $B$. Sums, differences, products, divisions by positive rationals, and exact comparisons retain polynomial bit complexity.

For tagged service, an exact rational Bernoulli draw with probability $a/d$ can be implemented by sampling a uniform integer below $d$. Draw $\lceil\log_2d\rceil$ fair bits and reject integers outside that range. Each trial succeeds with probability greater than one half, except cases handled directly. The expected number of trials is therefore less than two, and expected sampling time is polynomial in the encoding length of $d$.

The construction is independent of $\varepsilon$, hence satisfies the requested polynomial dependence on $1/\varepsilon$.
