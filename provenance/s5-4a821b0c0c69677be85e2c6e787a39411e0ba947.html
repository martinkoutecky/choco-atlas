The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given horizon \(H\), contexts \(\mathcal C\) of size \(g\), and supplier types \(t\) in a finite support \(\mathcal T\) of size \(\tau\), each with mass \(\lambda_t\), known per-unit cost \(k_t\), and unknown context-dependent mean value \(\mu_{t,c}\), choose each round a procurement vector \(x_t\) with \(0\le x_t\le\lambda_t\) and total mass at most \(q\). Observe one bounded reward from a sampled procured unit. Determine the achievable expected aggregate welfare regret against the per-context full-information linear allocation optimum, and whether a policy with per-round computation polynomial in \(\tau\), \(g\), and the input bit length achieves \(\widetilde O(q\sqrt{\tau gH})\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\lambda_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\lambda_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. There are \(m:=g\ge1\) contexts, a known horizon \(H\ge1\), known binary-rational costs \(k_t\in[0,1]\), and a binary-rational procurement cap \(q\in(0,1]\).
Each round \(h\), the context \(c_h\) is revealed before allocation; its exogenous conditional law may depend on previous contexts but is unaffected by allocations, rewards, or policy randomness. Capacities renew each round, and the policy chooses \(x^h\in\mathcal X:=\{x:0\le x_t\le\lambda_t,\ \sum_t x_t\le q\}\), including the zero allocation.
If \(s_h:=\sum_t x_t^h>0\), a type \(J_h\) is sampled with probability \(x_t^h/s_h\); the policy observes only \(J_h\) and a reward \(Y_h\in[0,1]\), satisfying \(\mathbb E[Y_h\mid\mathcal F_{h-1},c_h,x^h,J_h=t]=\mu_{t,c_h}\) for fixed unknown means \(\mu_{t,c}\in[0,1]\). Zero procurement gives no observation.
Expected aggregate welfare is \(W_c(x):=\sum_t x_t(\mu_{t,c}-k_t)\), and expected regret is \(R_H:=\mathbb E\sum_{h=1}^H[\max_{x\in\mathcal X}W_{c_h}(x)-W_{c_h}(x^h)]\). Rewards are supplied as binary rationals; \(L\) denotes the input bit length plus the maximum reward encoding length. State computation bounds in \(m,g,\tau,L,H\).

What is the minimax expected aggregate welfare regret for this model, and can a policy with per-round computation polynomial in \(m,g,\tau,L,\log H\) guarantee \(R_H=\widetilde O(q\sqrt{\tau gH})\) uniformly over admissible instances and processes?

Continuous-budget resource allocation has also been studied with Lipschitz rewards and semi-bandit feedback, a different observation model. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.04373)

Result and interpretation

The worst-case minimax expected regret is
\(\displaystyle \Theta\!\left(q\min\{H,\sqrt{\tau gH}\}\right)\).

Moreover, yes: a policy using rational arithmetic and per-round computation polynomial in \(\displaystyle m,g,\tau,L,\log(H+1)\) guarantees
\(\displaystyle R_H=O\!\left(q\sqrt{\tau gH\log(\tau gH+2)}\right)\)
uniformly over all admissible instances and processes.

Here “minimax” means that the policy knows the society, costs, cap, and horizon, while the worst case ranges over admissible societies, unknown means, and context and reward processes. For an individual fixed society, the minimax regret can be smaller: if every cost equals \(\displaystyle 1\), zero procurement is always optimal.

The supplied [single-unit paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05813v3) establishes a related UCB guarantee, while the supplied [resource-allocation paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.04373) uses semi-bandit feedback. The following proofs address the stated single-sample model directly.

1. A polynomial-time UCB policy

Write \(\displaystyle D=\tau g\). Set
\(\displaystyle A=\left\lceil\log_2(2DH^2)\right\rceil\),
\(\displaystyle B=\left\lceil\log_2(2H)\right\rceil\), and
\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=2^{-B}\).
Thus \(\displaystyle \varepsilon\leq 1/(2H)\).

Maintain a count \(\displaystyle N_{t,c}\) and an integer sum for every supported type–context pair. When observing reward \(\displaystyle Y\), store
\(\displaystyle Z=2^{-B}\lfloor 2^B Y\rfloor\).
Consequently,
\(\displaystyle 0\leq Y-Z<\varepsilon\).

For a pair with count \(\displaystyle n\geq1\), let \(\displaystyle \bar Z_{t,c,n}\) be its stored empirical mean. Define the rational radius
\(\displaystyle r_n=\min\!\left\{1,\varepsilon\left\lceil \varepsilon^{-1}\sqrt{\frac{A}{2n}}\right\rceil\right\}\)
and the index
\(\displaystyle U_{t,c}=\min\{1,\bar Z_{t,c,n}+\varepsilon+r_n\}\).
For an unobserved pair, set \(\displaystyle U_{t,c}=1\).

In context \(\displaystyle c\), choose an allocation maximizing
\(\displaystyle \sum_t x_t(U_{t,c}-k_t)\)
over \(\displaystyle \mathcal X\). This is solved by sorting the positive coefficients \(\displaystyle U_{t,c}-k_t\), filling capacities in that order, and stopping when the cap is reached or no positive coefficient remains. Ties are resolved by a fixed ordering.

Adaptive concentration

For a fixed pair, let \(\displaystyle \bar Y_{t,c,n}\) denote the empirical mean of its first \(\displaystyle n\) actual observations, when those observations occur.

The conditional-mean assumption and boundedness imply Hoeffding’s conditional moment bound:
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[e^{\theta(Y-\mu_{t,c})}\mid\text{history immediately before that reward}]\leq e^{\theta^2/8}\).

In calendar time, let \(\displaystyle S_h\) be the sum of centered rewards observed for this pair and \(\displaystyle N_h\) its observation count. Then
\(\displaystyle \exp(\theta S_h-\theta^2N_h/8)\)
is a nonnegative supermartingale. Indeed, conditional on the chosen allocation and sampled type, either its increment is zero or Hoeffding’s bound applies.

Applying the supermartingale maximal inequality at the time of the \(\displaystyle n\)-th observation, with \(\displaystyle \theta=4a\), gives
\(\displaystyle \Pr\!\left(N_H\geq n,\ \bar Y_{t,c,n}-\mu_{t,c}\geq a\right)\leq e^{-2na^2}\).
Applying the same argument to negative deviations gives the two-sided bound.

A union bound over the \(\displaystyle D\) pairs and \(\displaystyle n\in\{1,\ldots,H\}\) therefore shows that, with probability at least \(\displaystyle 1-1/H\), the event
\(\displaystyle \mathcal E=\left\{|\bar Y_{t,c,n}-\mu_{t,c}|\leq \sqrt{A/(2n)}\text{ for every observed pair and count}\right\}\)
holds. Here we used
\(\displaystyle 2DH e^{-A}\leq 1/H\).

On this event,
\(\displaystyle \mu_{t,c}\leq U_{t,c}\).
To check this, put \(\displaystyle b_n=\sqrt{A/(2n)}\). If \(\displaystyle b_n<1\), rounding gives
\(\displaystyle \mu_{t,c}\leq\bar Y_{t,c,n}+b_n\leq\bar Z_{t,c,n}+\varepsilon+r_n\).
If \(\displaystyle b_n\geq1\), the index equals \(\displaystyle 1\).

Also, on \(\displaystyle \mathcal E\),
\(\displaystyle 0\leq U_{t,c}-\mu_{t,c}\leq d(n)\),
where
\(\displaystyle d(n)=\frac{2\sqrt A}{\sqrt{\max\{1,n\}}}+2\varepsilon\).
For \(\displaystyle n\geq1\), this follows from
\(\displaystyle \bar Z_{t,c,n}\leq\bar Y_{t,c,n}\)
and
\(\displaystyle r_n\leq b_n+\varepsilon\).
For \(\displaystyle n=0\), the index error is at most \(\displaystyle 1\), which the displayed bound covers.

Regret with one sampled observation

Let \(\displaystyle x_c^\star\) maximize the true welfare. On \(\displaystyle \mathcal E\), optimism and optimality of the chosen allocation imply
\(\displaystyle W_c(x_c^\star)-W_c(x^h)\leq\sum_t x_t^h(U_{t,c}-\mu_{t,c})\leq\sum_t x_t^h d(N_{t,c})\).

The essential sampling identity is the following. Conditional on the history, current context, and allocation, if \(\displaystyle s_h>0\),
\(\displaystyle \sum_t x_t^h d(N_{t,c_h})=s_h\,\mathbb E[d(N_{J_h,c_h})\mid\text{history},c_h,x^h]\).
Since \(\displaystyle s_h\leq q\), this is at most \(\displaystyle q\) times the conditional expected width of the sampled pair. For zero procurement, both contributions vanish.

If a pair receives \(\displaystyle N\geq1\) observations, its successive pre-observation counts are \(\displaystyle 0,\ldots,N-1\), and
\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac1{\sqrt{\max\{1,j\}}}\leq2\sqrt N\).
Thus, pathwise, the sum of widths over all observations is at most
\(\displaystyle 4\sqrt A\sum_{t,c}\sqrt{N_{t,c}(H)}+2\varepsilon H\leq4\sqrt{ADH}+1\).
The last inequality uses Cauchy–Schwarz and the fact that there are at most \(\displaystyle H\) observations.

Outside \(\displaystyle \mathcal E\), regret per round is at most \(\displaystyle 2q\), because every feasible welfare lies in \(\displaystyle [-q,q]\). Consequently,
\(\displaystyle R_H\leq4q\sqrt{ADH}+3q\).

This proves the requested uniform guarantee. No independence assumption on rewards was used beyond the stated conditional means.

Bit complexity

All stored reward sums are integers with \(\displaystyle O(\log(H+1))\) bits: each summand is between \(\displaystyle 0\) and \(\displaystyle 2^B\), and there are at most \(\displaystyle H\) summands.

The radius \(\displaystyle r_n\) can be computed without evaluating an irrational square root. Binary-search for the smallest integer \(\displaystyle j\in\{0,\ldots,2^B\}\) satisfying
\(\displaystyle 2n j^2\geq A2^{2B}\);
if no such integer exists, use radius \(\displaystyle 1\).

Indices, comparisons with costs, and the greedy allocation therefore use rationals of polynomial bit length. Exact capacity arithmetic also has polynomial bit length, since the capacities and cap are part of the input.

For example, with schoolbook arithmetic, a sufficient per-round bit-operation bound is
\(\displaystyle O\!\left((\tau\log(\tau+1)+\tau\log(H+1)+1)\,[L+\log(\tau g+1)+\log(H+1)]^2\right)\).

Initialization and storage are polynomial in \(\displaystyle m,g,\tau,L,\log(H+1)\), with \(\displaystyle m=g\). Total computation over the horizon is \(\displaystyle H\) times a polynomial in these parameters.

2. A logarithm-free minimax upper bound

For the exact minimax order, we give an information-theoretic policy. The rational UCB policy above already supplies the requested computational guarantee.

Introduce an outside coordinate and write
\(\displaystyle p_t=x_t/q\quad(t=1,\ldots,\tau),\qquad p_0=1-\sum_t p_t\).
The feasible set becomes
\(\displaystyle P=\left\{p\geq0:\sum_{i=0}^{\tau}p_i=1,\ p_t\leq\lambda_t/q\right\}\).
Let \(\displaystyle d=\tau+1\).

Define mean losses by
\(\displaystyle \ell_{t,c}=(1-\mu_{t,c}+k_t)/2,\qquad \ell_{0,c}=1/2\).
They lie in \(\displaystyle [0,1]\), and
\(\displaystyle \langle\ell_c,p\rangle=\frac12-\frac{W_c(qp_{1:\tau})}{2q}\).
Thus loss regret multiplied by \(\displaystyle 2q\) equals welfare regret.

Simulating ordinary bandit feedback

After allocating \(\displaystyle x=qp_{1:\tau}\), retain the observed sample independently with probability \(\displaystyle s/q=1-p_0\). On retention, let \(\displaystyle I=J\); otherwise let \(\displaystyle I=0\). For zero procurement, set \(\displaystyle I=0\).

Then, conditional on the allocation,
\(\displaystyle \Pr(I=i)=p_i\).
For a retained type observation, use loss \(\displaystyle (1-Y+k_I)/2\); for the outside coordinate, use loss \(\displaystyle 1/2\). Denote this observed loss by \(\displaystyle L_I\).

When all coordinates of \(\displaystyle p\) are positive, the estimator
\(\displaystyle \widehat\ell_i=\frac{\mathbf1\{I=i\}L_I}{p_i}\)
satisfies
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[\widehat\ell_i\mid\text{history},c,p]=\ell_{i,c}\).

Mirror-descent calculation

Use the convex regularizer
\(\displaystyle \psi(p)=-2\sum_{i=0}^{\tau}\sqrt{p_i}\)
and its Bregman divergence
\(\displaystyle D_\psi(u,p)=\psi(u)-\psi(p)-\langle\nabla\psi(p),u-p\rangle\).

Maintain a separate state for each context, initialized at
\(\displaystyle p^{(1)}\in\arg\min_{p\in P}\psi(p)\).
Given an estimate \(\displaystyle \widehat\ell\), define
\(\displaystyle z_i=\frac{p_i}{(1+\eta\widehat\ell_i\sqrt{p_i})^2}\)
and update to
\(\displaystyle p^+\in\arg\min_{v\in P}D_\psi(v,z)\).

These states have positive coordinates. Indeed, \(\displaystyle P\) contains the positive point with \(\displaystyle p_t=\lambda_t/2\) and \(\displaystyle p_0=1/2\). At a minimizer with a zero coordinate, movement toward this point decreases the square-root part by order \(\displaystyle \sqrt\epsilon\), while changes in the other coordinates and linear terms are only order \(\displaystyle \epsilon\). Such a boundary point cannot minimize either objective.

For any comparator \(\displaystyle u\in P\), the update formula gives
\(\displaystyle \nabla\psi(z)=\nabla\psi(p)-\eta\widehat\ell\).
The Bregman identity and the projection optimality condition imply
\(\displaystyle \eta\langle\widehat\ell,p-u\rangle\leq D_\psi(u,p)-D_\psi(u,p^+)+D_\psi(p,z)\).

For completeness, the projection condition gives
\(\displaystyle D_\psi(u,z)\geq D_\psi(u,p^+)+D_\psi(p^+,z)\);
substituting this into the Bregman identity proves the displayed inequality.

The remaining term has a direct coordinatewise bound. Setting \(\displaystyle r_i=\eta\widehat\ell_i\sqrt{p_i}\), one obtains
\(\displaystyle D_\psi(p,z)=\sum_i\sqrt{p_i}\frac{r_i^2}{1+r_i}\leq\eta^2\sum_i p_i^{3/2}\widehat\ell_i^2\).
Because observed losses lie in \(\displaystyle [0,1]\),
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_i p_i^{3/2}\widehat\ell_i^2\mid\text{history},c,p\right]\leq\sum_i\sqrt{p_i}\leq\sqrt d\).

At initialization, first-order optimality yields
\(\displaystyle \langle\nabla\psi(p^{(1)}),u-p^{(1)}\rangle\geq0\),
so
\(\displaystyle D_\psi(u,p^{(1)})\leq\psi(u)-\psi(p^{(1)})\leq2\sqrt d\).

Let \(\displaystyle K_0=\min\{g,H\}\). Summing the mirror-descent inequality separately within each visited context, taking expectations, and using a welfare-optimal comparator for each context gives
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E\sum_{h=1}^H\langle\ell_{c_h},p^h-u_{c_h}\rangle\leq\frac{2K_0\sqrt d}{\eta}+\eta H\sqrt d\).

Taking \(\displaystyle \eta=\sqrt{2K_0/H}\) and multiplying by \(\displaystyle 2q\) proves
\(\displaystyle R_H\leq4q\sqrt{2dK_0H}\leq8q\sqrt{\tau gH}\).

Alternatively, always choosing zero gives \(\displaystyle R_H\leq qH\). Choosing between these policies using the known parameters proves
\(\displaystyle R_H=O\!\left(q\min\{H,\sqrt{\tau gH}\}\right)\).

3. Matching minimax lower bound

We construct admissible instances on which every policy has the claimed order of regret.

Hard types and contexts

Suppose first that \(\displaystyle \tau\geq2\), and put \(\displaystyle N=\tau-1\). Choose a positive dyadic number \(\displaystyle r\) satisfying
\(\displaystyle \frac{q}{4N}<r\leq\frac{q}{2N}\).
Such a number exists by taking a suitable power of two.

Give each of the \(\displaystyle N\) hard types mass \(\displaystyle r\), and write \(\displaystyle b=Nr\). Thus
\(\displaystyle q/4<b\leq q/2\).
Give the remaining type mass \(\displaystyle 1-b\), cost \(\displaystyle 1\), and deterministic reward zero. All hard types have cost \(\displaystyle 1/2\).

Let \(\displaystyle K=\min\{g,H\}\), and use a deterministic context sequence with balanced visit counts \(\displaystyle T_c\) for \(\displaystyle c=1,\ldots,K\). Hence
\(\displaystyle \sum_cT_c=H,\qquad T_c\leq\lceil H/K\rceil,\qquad \sum_cT_c^2\leq2H^2/K\).

For every hard type–context pair, independently choose a sign
\(\displaystyle \theta_{i,c}\in\{-1,+1\}\).
Its rewards are independent Bernoulli variables with mean
\(\displaystyle \mu_{i,c}=\frac12+\delta\theta_{i,c}\),
where
\(\displaystyle \delta=\frac1{16}\min\{1,\sqrt{NK/H}\}\).

These processes satisfy every required conditional-mean condition. Rewards have the binary-rational values \(\displaystyle 0\) and \(\displaystyle 1\), and the masses and costs are also binary rational.

Because all hard masses together total at most \(\displaystyle q\), the oracle fully procures every positive-sign hard type and procures no negative-sign type.

Writing \(\displaystyle z_{i,h}=x_i^h/r\in[0,1]\), the hard-type regret contribution is
\(\displaystyle \delta r\left[\mathbf1\{\theta_{i,c_h}=+1\}(1-z_{i,h})+\mathbf1\{\theta_{i,c_h}=-1\}z_{i,h}\right]\).
Procurement from the remaining type adds nonnegative regret.

Distinguishing a single sign

Fix all signs except \(\displaystyle \theta_{i,c}\), and denote the complete transcript laws for its two values by \(\displaystyle P_+\) and \(\displaystyle P_-\). Let \(\displaystyle M_{i,c}\) count observations of this pair.

The policy’s action kernel and the type-sampling kernel are identical conditional on any common history. Only rewards observed from this pair distinguish the two environments. The KL chain rule therefore gives
\(\displaystyle \operatorname{KL}(P_-\Vert P_+)\leq16\delta^2\,\mathbb E_-M_{i,c}\).

Indeed, the KL divergence of the two Bernoulli distributions is
\(\displaystyle 2\delta\log\frac{1+2\delta}{1-2\delta}\leq16\delta^2\)
for \(\displaystyle \delta\leq1/4\). Their probabilities are strictly positive, so the KL comparison is well-defined.

Pinsker’s inequality implies
\(\displaystyle \operatorname{TV}(P_+,P_-)\leq\sqrt8\,\delta\sqrt{\mathbb E_-M_{i,c}}\).

For any transcript-measurable \(\displaystyle z\in[0,1]\),
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_+(1-z)+\mathbb E_-z\geq1-\operatorname{TV}(P_+,P_-)\).
This applies to each normalized allocation \(\displaystyle z_{i,h}\).

Averaging over all signs

Use the uniform prior on the sign vectors, and define
\(\displaystyle a_{i,c}=\mathbb E[M_{i,c}\mid\theta_{i,c}=-1]\).

There is at most one observation per round, so
\(\displaystyle \sum_{i,c}a_{i,c}=2\sum_{i,c}\mathbb E[M_{i,c}\mathbf1\{\theta_{i,c}=-1\}]\leq2H\).

Averaging the preceding testing bound over the other signs and applying Jensen’s inequality shows that the average total variation for pair \(\displaystyle (i,c)\) is at most
\(\displaystyle \sqrt8\,\delta\sqrt{a_{i,c}}\).

Consequently, the policy’s Bayes regret is at least
\(\displaystyle \frac{\delta b}{2N}\left[NH-\sqrt8\,\delta\sum_{i,c}T_c\sqrt{a_{i,c}}\right]\).

By Cauchy–Schwarz,
\(\displaystyle \sum_{i,c}T_c\sqrt{a_{i,c}}\leq\sqrt{\left(N\sum_cT_c^2\right)\left(\sum_{i,c}a_{i,c}\right)}\leq2H\sqrt{NH/K}\).

The subtracted term divided by \(\displaystyle NH\) is therefore at most
\(\displaystyle 4\sqrt2\,\delta\sqrt{H/(NK)}\leq\sqrt2/4<1/2\).
Thus
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_\theta R_H\geq\frac{\delta bH}{4}\geq\frac{q}{256}\min\{H,\sqrt{NKH}\}\).

Since \(\displaystyle N=\tau-1\geq\tau/2\), this is at least a universal positive constant times
\(\displaystyle q\min\{H,\sqrt{\tau gH}\}\).
When \(\displaystyle g>H\), both minima equal \(\displaystyle H\); when \(\displaystyle g\leq H\), use \(\displaystyle K=g\) and \(\displaystyle N\geq\tau/2\).

A worst-case sign vector has regret at least this prior average, so this is a minimax lower bound.

The case of one supported type

For \(\displaystyle \tau=1\), give the sole type mass \(\displaystyle 1\) and cost \(\displaystyle 1/2\). Use the same balanced contexts and independent signs, with \(\displaystyle N=1\).

Normalize allocation by \(\displaystyle z_h=x^h/q\). The oracle allocates \(\displaystyle q\) for a positive sign and zero for a negative sign. Its regret contribution is exactly the previous expression with \(\displaystyle b=q\) and \(\displaystyle N=1\).

The same testing proof therefore gives
\(\displaystyle R_H=\Omega\!\left(q\min\{H,\sqrt{gH}\}\right)\),
completing the lower bound for every supported-type count.

4. Established conclusions

The logarithm-free upper bound and matching lower bound establish
\(\displaystyle \mathfrak R_{\tau,g,H,q}=\Theta\!\left(q\min\{H,\sqrt{\tau gH}\}\right)\)
for the worst-case minimax problem.

The explicitly described rational UCB policy establishes the requested polynomial per-round computation and uniform guarantee
\(\displaystyle R_H=\widetilde O(q\sqrt{\tau gH})\).
Its proof uses the actual single-sampled-unit feedback, including zero procurement, and counts only supported types.
