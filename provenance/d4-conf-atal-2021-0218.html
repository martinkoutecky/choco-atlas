The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an agenda \(\Phi\), its admissible judgment set \(\mathcal J(\Phi)\), and a rational finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over \(\mathcal J(\Phi)\), define \(\rho_\mu(K)=\max_{J\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}H(J,K)-\min_{J\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}H(J,K)\). Decide whether \(\min_{K\in\mathcal J(\Phi)}\rho_\mu(K)\) is divisible by \(4\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An agenda is an explicitly encoded list \(\Phi=(\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_m)\) of propositional formulas, with \(m\geq1\). Its admissible judgments are \(\mathcal J(\Phi)=\{(\alpha(\varphi_1),\ldots,\alpha(\varphi_m)): \alpha\text{ is a truth assignment}\}\); this set is represented implicitly by the formulas.
A society is given by its support: a nonempty list of \(\tau\) distinct admissible judgment types with positive binary rational masses summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Let \(H\) denote Hamming distance. Collective judgments remain in \(\mathcal J(\Phi)\). Define \(\rho_\mu(K)=\max_{J\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}H(J,K)-\min_{J\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}H(J,K)\) and \(r(\Phi,\mu)=\min_{K\in\mathcal J(\Phi)}\rho_\mu(K)\).
Is deciding whether \(r(\Phi,\mu)\in4\mathbb Z\) solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?
For finite profiles, the corresponding divisibility decision problem is \(\Theta_2^p\)-complete, as also stated in the [2023 journal version](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-023-09598-6). No separately verified result for the distribution formulation is available here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Resolved the representation of the admissible judgment set: it is implicit in the explicitly encoded propositional agenda, rather than supplied as an exhaustive list.
  • Made admissibility precise as joint realizability of the issue values by a truth assignment.
  • Normalized the positive rational masses to sum to one, as required by the stated distribution.
  • Allowed singleton support: the discrete requirement of at least two agents permits repeated identical judgments and therefore does not require two distinct types.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length, including formulas, support judgments, and masses.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(m=\tau=2\). Using the stated finite-profile theorem, it is in fact \(\Theta_2^p\)-complete under polynomial-time many-to-one reductions.

  • The objective depends only on the support.

For any nonempty finite collection of real numbers, the largest absolute difference equals its maximum minus its minimum. Consequently, for every admissible \(K\),

\(\rho_\mu(K)=\max_{J,J'\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}|H(J,K)-H(J',K)|.\)

Thus positive masses and repeated occurrences of a judgment do not affect the objective. In particular, \(\tau=1\) always gives \(r(\Phi,\mu)=0\). The hardness below concerns the first nontrivial support size.

  • A direct, self-contained proof of \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

Let \(F\) be an arbitrary propositional satisfiability instance. Introduce fresh variables \(a,b\), and construct the agenda

\(\Phi_F=(a,\ a\lor(b\land F)).\)

Give each of the two support types \(J^0=(0,0)\) and \(J^1=(1,1)\) mass \(\frac12\).

This is a valid society for every \(F\): setting \(a=b=0\) realizes \(J^0\), while setting \(a=1\) realizes \(J^1\). The support types are distinct, their masses are positive, and their masses sum to \(1\).

We now characterize every admissible collective judgment:

  • If \(a=1\), the judgment is \((1,1)\).
  • If \(a=0\), the judgment is \((0,b\land F)\).
  • Therefore \((1,0)\) is impossible, and \((0,1)\) is admissible exactly when \(F\) is satisfiable.

It follows that \(\mathcal J(\Phi_F)=\{(0,0),(1,1)\}\) when \(F\) is unsatisfiable, and \(\mathcal J(\Phi_F)=\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}\) when \(F\) is satisfiable.

For \(K=(0,0)\), the distances to the two support types are \(0\) and \(2\), so \(\rho_\mu(K)=2\). For \(K=(1,1)\), the distances are \(2\) and \(0\), again giving \(\rho_\mu(K)=2\). For \(K=(0,1)\), both distances are \(1\), giving \(\rho_\mu(K)=0\).

Hence \(r(\Phi_F,\mu)=0\) if \(F\) is satisfiable, and \(r(\Phi_F,\mu)=2\) otherwise. Since \(0\in4\mathbb Z\) and \(2\notin4\mathbb Z\),

\(F\text{ is satisfiable}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ r(\Phi_F,\mu)\in4\mathbb Z.\)

The construction has \(m=\tau=2\), output length \(L=O(|F|+1)\), and polynomial construction time in the encoding length \(|F|\). This proves the requested hardness independently of the finite-profile result.

  • Exact transfer of the finite-profile completeness theorem.

The known theorem states that, given an explicitly listed finite profile \(P=(J_1,\ldots,J_n)\), deciding whether

\(\min_{K\in\mathcal J(\Phi)}\max_{1\leq i,j\leq n}|H(J_i,K)-H(J_j,K)|\)

belongs to \(4\mathbb Z\) is \(\Theta_2^p\)-complete. This is Proposition 8 of the [2023 journal article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-023-09598-6).

To reduce that problem to the present one, retain the agenda, delete duplicate profile types, and assign mass \(\frac1\tau\) to each remaining type. These types are admissible because they came from a valid profile. The masses are positive binary rationals summing to \(1\), each requiring \(O(\log(\tau+1))\) bits. By the identity in the first item, this transformation preserves the objective for every \(K\), and therefore preserves its minimum and the divisibility answer.

For an explicitly listed source profile, deduplication by sorting takes \(O(nm\log(n+1))\) word operations. The output length is \(O(|\Phi|+\tau m+\tau\log(\tau+1))\), hence polynomial in the source length. This proves \(\Theta_2^p\)-hardness for the distribution formulation.

Conversely, replace a distribution by a profile containing each of its \(\tau\) support types once. The same identity preserves the optimum. If the finite-profile convention requires at least two agents and \(\tau=1\), repeat its sole type twice. This transformation takes \(O(L+\tau m)\) time and produces a profile of polynomial length.

Both transformations use the explicitly listed support. Neither expands a rational mass into a corresponding number of voters.

  • Upper bound and running times.

Membership in \(\Theta_2^p\) also follows directly. For an integer threshold \(c\), consider the question whether there exists a truth assignment \(\alpha\) whose induced judgment \(K_\alpha\) satisfies \(\rho_\mu(K_\alpha)\leq c\).

This threshold question is in \(\mathrm{NP}\). A certificate is the assignment \(\alpha\) to all variables occurring in the agenda. It has at most \(L\) bits. Evaluate all agenda formulas, compute the \(\tau\) Hamming distances, and take their maximum minus minimum. On a standard word RAM, verification takes \(O(L+\tau m)\) operations; ordinary bit implementations also take polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

Every Hamming distance lies between \(0\) and \(m\), so \(0\leq r(\Phi,\mu)\leq m\). The threshold predicate is monotone in \(c\), and a truth assignment always induces an admissible judgment. Binary search therefore finds the exact optimum using at most \(\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil\) queries to this \(\mathrm{NP}\) predicate, with query encodings of length \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) and polynomial deterministic overhead. Since \(m\leq L\), the number of queries is \(O(\log L)\).

Testing the resulting integer for divisibility by \(4\) proves membership in \(\Theta_2^p=\mathrm P^{\mathrm{NP}[O(\log L)]}\). Together with the preceding reduction, this establishes completeness. A polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
