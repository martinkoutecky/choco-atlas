The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational population distribution \(\mu\) over a finite value set \(V\) and per-capita supply \(\kappa\), find an algorithm that outputs, for every reported population measure \(\nu\), an anonymous treatment schedule \(x_\nu(v),p_\nu(v)\) that is feasible and truthful for every infinitesimal agent, and maximizes revenue \(\sum_{v\in V}\mu_v p_\mu(v)\) relative to \(EFO_\kappa(\mu)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(V\subseteq\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\) be a nonempty value domain of size \(m\), and let \(\kappa\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1]\) be per-capita supply of a unit-demand service in an atomless population of total mass one.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including values and supply.
A single anonymous mechanism takes any rational reported probability measure \(\nu\) on \(V\) and returns treatments \((x_\nu(v),p_\nu(v))\) for every \(v\in V\), including absent types; these are service probabilities and expected payments.
For every \(\nu\), require \(0\leq x_\nu(v)\leq1\), \(p_\nu(v)\geq0\), \(\sum_v\nu_vx_\nu(v)\leq\kappa\), and individual rationality \(vx_\nu(v)-p_\nu(v)\geq0\).
An individual report does not change \(\nu\); truthfulness means \(vx_\nu(v)-p_\nu(v)\geq vx_\nu(w)-p_\nu(w)\) for every \(\nu\) and \(v,w\in V\). Indifference permits truthful reporting.
Define \(EFO_\kappa(\mu)\) as the maximum of \(\sum_v\mu_vp(v)\) over anonymous feasible, nonnegative-payment, individually rational treatments satisfying \(vx(v)-p(v)\geq vx(w)-p(w)\) for all positive-mass types \(v,w\).

Does a single mechanism, evaluable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), guarantee \(\sum_v\mu_vp_\mu(v)\geq EFO_\kappa(\mu)/12.5\) for every rational society \(\mu\) and supply \(\kappa\), or is this approximation guarantee unattainable in polynomial time?

For finite multi-unit populations, random sampling empirical Myerson is truthful and achieves expected revenue at least \(EFO^{(2)}(\mathbf v)/12.5\), where \(EFO^{(2)}\) replaces the highest value by the second-highest before optimizing envy-free revenue ([Devanur–Hartline–Yan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.3741)).
In large economies, envy freedom and type measurability characterize robust approximate implementation under private values, excludability, and a convergent allocation space; this is an asymptotic implementation result, not the stated computational approximation guarantee ([Jackson–Kremer](https://web.stanford.edu/~jacksonm/envy.pdf)).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative rational values, total population mass one, and rational supply between zero exclusive and one inclusive; supply above one is equivalent to one.
  • Interpreted the output for every reported measure as one algorithm evaluated on that measure, with a uniform guarantee over true societies rather than a mechanism tailored to a known true distribution.
  • Specified service probabilities, expected nonnegative payments, individual rationality, and capacity feasibility; ties in utility permit truthful reporting.
  • Restricted individual reports to the given finite value domain, including types absent from the reported support, and made explicit that an infinitesimal deviation leaves the aggregate measure unchanged.
  • Defined the envy-free benchmark using positive-mass types and retained the unmodified benchmark from the continuous question: removing or changing one highest-valued infinitesimal agent changes no population mass.
  • Replaced the underspecified instruction to maximize revenue relative to the benchmark with the anchor's 12.5 approximation question, and interpreted output as polynomial-time evaluation rather than enumeration of infinitely many reported measures.

Result

Yes. Under the stated definition of truthfulness, a single polynomial-time mechanism can attain the benchmark exactly: for every rational society \(\mu\),

\( \sum_{v\in V}\mu_vp_\mu(v)=EFO_\kappa(\mu). \)

Thus the requested approximation factor \(12.5\) is attainable. The essential premise is that an individual report leaves the reported measure unchanged.

We prove this by reducing the benchmark to mixtures of posted-price treatments and giving an explicit polynomial-time optimization algorithm.

1. An upper bound on every envy-free treatment

Fix a reported probability measure \(\nu\). Write its positive-mass support in increasing order as

\( a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_t, \)

with masses \(b_i=\nu_{a_i}>0\), where \(\sum_{i=1}^t b_i=1\). Define the tail masses

\( Q_j=\sum_{i=j}^t b_i. \)

Consider any treatment feasible for the envy-free benchmark. On the support, denote its allocations and payments by \(x_i,p_i\), and its utilities by \(u_i=a_ix_i-p_i\).

For \(i<j\), the two envy-free inequalities imply

\( a_ix_i-p_i\geq a_ix_j-p_j,\qquad a_jx_j-p_j\geq a_jx_i-p_i. \)

Adding them gives \((a_j-a_i)(x_j-x_i)\geq0\). Because \(a_j>a_i\), allocations are nondecreasing:

\( 0\leq x_1\leq\cdots\leq x_t\leq1. \)

Set \(x_0=0\) and \(d_j=x_j-x_{j-1}\). Then

\( d_j\geq0,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^t d_j=x_t\leq1. \)

Individual rationality gives \(u_1\geq0\). For each \(i\geq2\), type \(a_i\)'s constraint against reporting \(a_{i-1}\) gives

\( u_i\geq u_{i-1}+(a_i-a_{i-1})x_{i-1}. \)

Iterating,

\( u_i\geq\sum_{h=1}^{i-1}(a_{h+1}-a_h)x_h. \)

Consequently, telescoping yields

\( p_i\leq a_ix_i-\sum_{h=1}^{i-1}(a_{h+1}-a_h)x_h =\sum_{j=1}^i a_jd_j. \)

Multiplying by masses and exchanging the finite sums establishes the revenue bound

\( \sum_{i=1}^t b_ip_i \leq\sum_{j=1}^t a_jQ_jd_j. \)

Likewise, the supply constraint becomes

\( \sum_{i=1}^t b_ix_i =\sum_{j=1}^t Q_jd_j \leq\kappa. \)

Therefore every benchmark treatment has revenue at most the optimum of the following linear program:

\( \text{maximize }\sum_{j=1}^t a_jQ_jd_j \quad\text{subject to}\quad d_j\geq0,\quad \sum_{j=1}^t d_j\leq1,\quad \sum_{j=1}^t Q_jd_j\leq\kappa. \)

2. Every feasible vector extends truthfully to all types

For any feasible vector \(d\) in this program, define treatments for every \(v\in V\), including absent types, by

\( x_\nu(v)=\sum_{j=1}^t d_j\mathbf1_{\{v\geq a_j\}}, \qquad p_\nu(v)=\sum_{j=1}^t a_jd_j\mathbf1_{\{v\geq a_j\}}. \)

These are weighted mixtures of posted-price treatments.

Since all \(d_j,a_j\) are nonnegative and \(\sum_jd_j\leq1\), allocations lie in \([0,1]\) and payments are nonnegative. Truthful utility is

\( vx_\nu(v)-p_\nu(v) =\sum_{j=1}^t d_j\max\{v-a_j,0\}\geq0, \)

so individual rationality holds for all types.

For any true value \(v\) and report \(w\), each threshold satisfies

\( (v-a_j)\mathbf1_{\{v\geq a_j\}} \geq (v-a_j)\mathbf1_{\{w\geq a_j\}}. \)

Indeed, when \(v-a_j\) is positive, truthful reporting selects the positive contribution; when it is negative, truthful reporting avoids it; when it is zero, either choice gives zero. Multiplication by \(d_j\geq0\) and summation prove

\( vx_\nu(v)-p_\nu(v) \geq vx_\nu(w)-p_\nu(w). \)

This establishes truthfulness on the entire domain, including absent types.

Supply and revenue under \(\nu\) are respectively

\( \sum_{v\in V}\nu_vx_\nu(v)=\sum_{j=1}^t Q_jd_j\leq\kappa, \)

and

\( \sum_{v\in V}\nu_vp_\nu(v)=\sum_{j=1}^t a_jQ_jd_j. \)

Thus every feasible vector attains its linear-program objective through an admissible globally truthful treatment. Combined with the upper bound, this proves that the program's optimum is exactly \(EFO_\kappa(\nu)\).

3. An optimal treatment uses at most two options

We next solve the program explicitly.

Introduce a no-service option indexed by \(0\), with \(Q_0=R_0=0\). For \(j\geq1\), let \(R_j=a_jQ_j\). Set

\( \alpha_j=d_j\quad(j\geq1),\qquad \alpha_0=1-\sum_{j=1}^t d_j. \)

The optimization becomes

\( \text{maximize }\sum_{j=0}^t R_j\alpha_j \quad\text{subject to}\quad \alpha_j\geq0,\quad \sum_{j=0}^t\alpha_j=1,\quad \sum_{j=0}^tQ_j\alpha_j\leq\kappa. \)

Its feasible set is nonempty and compact, so an optimum exists. Choose an optimum having the smallest number of positive coordinates.

It has at most two positive coordinates. Otherwise, there is a nonzero vector \(h\), supported on its positive coordinates, satisfying

\( \sum_jh_j=0,\qquad \sum_jQ_jh_j=0, \)

because these are only two homogeneous linear equations in at least three unknowns. For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both \(\alpha+\varepsilon h\) and \(\alpha-\varepsilon h\) remain feasible. Optimality therefore forces \(\sum_jR_jh_j=0\). Since \(h\) has both positive and negative coordinates, moving in its direction until the first coordinate reaches zero preserves feasibility and optimality while reducing support—a contradiction.

If it has two positive coordinates, its supply constraint is tight. If supply were slack, transferring a sufficiently small amount of weight in either direction between those two coordinates would remain feasible. Optimality would force their revenues to be equal. The option with smaller supply has supply no larger than their feasible weighted average, so using that option alone would be feasible and equally profitable, again contradicting minimal support.

The supply quantities are distinct: \(Q_1=1>Q_2>\cdots>Q_t>Q_0=0\), because every support mass is positive. Hence a two-option optimum has supplies strictly straddling \(\kappa\).

It follows that an optimum occurs among these explicit candidates:

  • A single option: any \(j\) with \(Q_j\leq\kappa\), giving revenue \(R_j\).
  • Two options: any pair \(i,j\) with \(Q_i<\kappa<Q_j\), using weights

\( \alpha_i=\frac{Q_j-\kappa}{Q_j-Q_i}, \qquad \alpha_j=\frac{\kappa-Q_i}{Q_j-Q_i}, \)

and giving revenue \(\alpha_iR_i+\alpha_jR_j\).

The no-service option is included, so this enumeration covers mixtures whose total weight on actual posted prices is less than one. It also covers singleton supports and zero-valued supports.

4. Polynomial-time evaluation

The mechanism uses the following single rule on every input \(\nu\):

  • Sort its positive-mass support and compute \(Q_j,R_j\).
  • Enumerate all feasible candidates above.
  • Choose a revenue-maximizing candidate, breaking ties by a fixed ordering.
  • Return the threshold treatments from Section 2 for every \(v\in V\).

There are at most \((t+1)^2\) candidates. Sorting requires a polynomial number of rational comparisons, and each candidate requires a constant number of rational arithmetic operations. The selected candidate has at most two active options, so returning all treatments requires only a constant number of operations per domain value.

For completeness, these are polynomial-time bit computations. The product of the input mass denominators has bit length at most their total encoded length. Using it as a common denominator computes every tail mass with polynomially many bits. Each candidate's weights and revenue then use a constant number of operations on these quantities, the input values, and \(\kappa\), so their bit lengths remain polynomial in \(L\). Exact comparisons and the output treatments consequently require polynomial bit complexity.

The algorithm therefore runs in time polynomial in \(m,t,L\), with \(t\) the actual support size. It never expands rational masses into individual agents.

5. Why optimizing separately gives one truthful mechanism

The construction is one deterministic algorithm defined for every rational reported measure. For each fixed \(\nu\), it satisfies all incentive inequalities on \(V\). By the problem's premise, an individual's deviation changes only their report, leaving \(\nu\) fixed. These inequalities are therefore exactly the required truthfulness conditions, even though the selected prices depend on \(\nu\).

This differs from finite-agent mechanisms, where an individual's report changes the profile and incentive constraints connect different profiles, as discussed by [Devanur–Hartline–Yan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.3741). The [Jackson–Kremer result](https://web.stanford.edu/~jacksonm/envy.pdf) concerns asymptotic robust approximate implementation; the explicit construction above establishes the computational guarantee directly.

At truthful input \(\mu\), the returned treatment achieves \(EFO_\kappa(\mu)\). Since this benchmark is nonnegative, it also achieves at least \(EFO_\kappa(\mu)/12.5\).
