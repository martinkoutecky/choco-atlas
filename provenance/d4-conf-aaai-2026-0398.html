The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a fixed finite set of municipality types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_j\) summing to \(1\), resident counts \(p_j\), invitation caps \(u_j\), a letter budget \(B\) per unit of municipality mass, and a contact limit \(\theta\), does there exist a distribution over assignments \(a_j:[0,\mu_j]\to\{0,\ldots,u_j\}\) such that in every draw \(\sum_{j\in T}\int_0^{\mu_j}a_j(z)\,dz=B\), contacted mass \(\sum_{j\in T}|\{z:a_j(z)>0\}|\le\theta\), and \(\mathbb{E}[a_j(z)]=Bp_j/P\) for almost every \(z\), where \(P=\sum_{j\in T}\mu_jp_j\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) of types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_j\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types, and is part of the input.
Each type has a positive rational population weight \(p_j\) and a positive integer invitation cap \(u_j\), encoded in binary. Types admit a common ordering with \(p_1\le\cdots\le p_\tau\) and \(u_1\le\cdots\le u_\tau\).
The budget per unit municipality mass is \(B\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\), and the contact limit is \(\theta\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1]\). Set \(P=\sum_j\mu_jp_j\), and assume \(Bp_j/P\le u_j\) for every type.
Represent type \(j\) by the Lebesgue interval \([0,\mu_j)\). A randomized assignment consists of jointly measurable maps \(a_j:\Omega\times[0,\mu_j)\to\{0,\ldots,u_j\}\) on a probability space \(\Omega\).
Every draw must satisfy \(\sum_j\int_0^{\mu_j}a_j(\omega,z)\,dz=B\) and \(\sum_j\lambda(\{z:a_j(\omega,z)>0\})\le\theta\), where \(\lambda\) denotes Lebesgue measure.
Fairness requires \(\mathbb{E}_\omega[a_j(\omega,z)]=Bp_j/P\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(z\), for every type. The decision problem asks whether such a randomized assignment exists.
Let \(m=\max_j u_j\) and \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and the encoded parameters \(B,\theta\), distinguishing numerical from bit-length dependence.

Is this continuous fair municipality-assignment decision problem solvable in polynomial time in its binary input length, or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many municipalities, the corresponding fair-distribution feasibility problem is NP-hard and admits a pseudopolynomial-time algorithm; minimizing the maximum contacted count admits a polynomial-time additive \(1\)-approximation. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07557v1)
No result settling exactly the continuous problem stated here was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted the finite support as supplied with the input, rather than as a globally fixed constant.
  • Used positive rational population weights, matching the discrete model's real-valued normalized populations while making binary input complexity well-defined.
  • Retained positive integer invitation caps and required the common nondecreasing ordering of populations and caps assumed in the discrete model.
  • Made the per-unit mass budget a positive binary rational and the contact limit a binary rational in (0,1]; retained the discrete necessary condition that each fair expected allocation is at most its cap.
  • Specified Lebesgue measure and joint measurability in the draw and municipality coordinate; interpreted every draw as an ex-post constraint on the assignments being randomized.
  • Did not impose u_j <= B: the discrete bound compares an individual cap with the total letter budget, whereas B here is a budget per unit municipality mass, so it does not provide the same truncation bound.
  • Defined m as the maximum invitation cap and L as total binary input length because this problem has no candidate dimension.

Exact characterization

The continuous problem is solvable in polynomial time in its binary input length.

Define the required mean and contact fraction for type $j$ by
$$ q_j=\frac{Bp_j}{P}, \qquad r_j=\frac{q_j}{u_j}. $$
The hypotheses imply $0<r_j\le 1$. Define
$$ W=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_jr_j =\frac{B}{P}\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\frac{\mu_jp_j}{u_j}. $$

Theorem. A randomized assignment satisfying all the stated requirements exists if and only if
$$ W\le\theta. $$

The construction below uses only the integer invitation counts $0$ and $u_j$. The common ordering of the types is unnecessary for this characterization.

Necessity

Suppose a feasible randomized assignment exists. For every type $j$, draw $\omega$, and point $z$,
$$ a_j(\omega,z) \le u_j\,\mathbf{1}_{\{a_j(\omega,z)>0\}}. $$
Consequently, the contacted mass in a draw satisfies
$$ \sum_{j=1}^{\tau} \lambda\bigl(\{z:a_j(\omega,z)>0\}\bigr) \ge \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\frac{1}{u_j} \int_0^{\mu_j}a_j(\omega,z)\,dz. $$

All integrands are nonnegative and jointly measurable. Tonelli’s theorem therefore permits exchanging expectation and integration. Taking expectations, using the contact constraint, and then using fairness almost everywhere gives
$$ \begin{aligned} \theta &\ge \mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left[ \sum_{j=1}^{\tau} \lambda\bigl(\{z:a_j(\omega,z)>0\}\bigr) \right]\\ &\ge \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\frac{1}{u_j} \int_0^{\mu_j}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}[a_j(\omega,z)]\,dz\\ &= \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\frac{\mu_jq_j}{u_j} =W. \end{aligned} $$
Thus $W\le\theta$ is necessary.

Sufficiency: an explicit measurable construction

Assume $W\le\theta$. Take the probability space $\Omega=[0,1)$ with its Borel sigma algebra and uniform probability measure. For a real number $x$, write its fractional part as
$$ \operatorname{frac}(x)=x-\lfloor x\rfloor. $$
Set
$$ a_j(\omega,z) = u_j\, \mathbf{1}_{\left\{ \operatorname{frac}\left(\frac{z}{\mu_j}+\omega\right)<r_j \right\}}. $$
These maps are jointly measurable because the fractional-part map is Borel measurable. Their values belong to $\{0,u_j\}$, hence satisfy the integer invitation restrictions.

For any fixed $s\in[0,1)$, the map
$$ t\longmapsto \operatorname{frac}(t+s) $$
preserves Lebesgue measure on $[0,1)$. Indeed, splitting the domain at $1-s$ makes it a translation on each of two intervals, whose images partition $[0,1)$.

Fix an arbitrary draw $\omega$. Applying this fact and substituting $t=z/\mu_j$ shows that
$$ \lambda\bigl(\{z:a_j(\omega,z)>0\}\bigr)=\mu_jr_j $$
and
$$ \int_0^{\mu_j}a_j(\omega,z)\,dz =u_j\mu_jr_j =\mu_jq_j. $$
Therefore every draw satisfies the exact budget:
$$ \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\int_0^{\mu_j}a_j(\omega,z)\,dz = \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_jq_j = \frac{B}{P}\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_jp_j =B. $$
Its contacted mass is exactly
$$ \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_jr_j=W\le\theta. $$

Finally, fix any $z\in[0,\mu_j)$. Rotation also preserves uniform probability as $\omega$ varies, so
$$ \Pr_{\omega}\left[ \operatorname{frac}\left(\frac{z}{\mu_j}+\omega\right)<r_j \right]=r_j. $$
It follows that
$$ \mathbb{E}_{\omega}[a_j(\omega,z)] =u_jr_j=q_j=\frac{Bp_j}{P}. $$
Thus fairness holds at every point, which is stronger than the required almost-everywhere condition. This completes the proof.

In particular, $W$ is the minimum achievable bound on contacted mass per draw.

Binary running time

The decision algorithm computes
$$ P=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_jp_j, \qquad S=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\frac{\mu_jp_j}{u_j}, $$
and tests the exact rational inequality
$$ BS\le\theta P. $$

This requires $O(\tau)$ rational arithmetic operations. Even without reducing fractions, all intermediate numerators and denominators have $O(L)$ bits: denominators can be represented as products of input denominators and caps, whose total binary length is $O(L)$; summing the numerators adds at most $O(\log\tau)$ further bits.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic therefore gives the conservative bound
$$ O(\tau L^2) $$
bit operations. The explicit assignment can also be described using $O(\tau L)$ bits by listing the rational thresholds $r_j$ and the formula above.

There is no numerical dependence on $m$, $B$, or $1/\theta$. Their dependence is through their binary encodings included in $L$; in particular, the algorithm does not enumerate invitation counts up to $m$.

Why the finite hardness does not transfer

The cited paper establishes hardness for finitely many individually constrained municipalities. [Gölz et al., *City Sampling for Citizens’ Assemblies*](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07557v1).

Here, each type is a nonatomic interval. In every draw, an arbitrary fraction $r_j$ of that interval can receive its cap, and rotating that subset ensures pointwise fairness while preserving its measure. The proof above establishes that this divisibility makes the contact lower bound exactly attainable.
