The continuous mirror question, as graded

Fix \(k\ge3\). Given candidates \(C\), complete-ranking voter types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), source prices \(\kappa_t\in\{1,\infty\}\), target \(p\), a fixed tie-breaking order, and a rational per-capita budget \(B\), let \(w\) be the current \(k\)-approval winner and \(V=\{t:p\succ_{\rho_t}w\}\). Decide whether there exist transfers \(x_{t,\rho}\ge0\), for \(t\in V\) and \(\rho\in L(C)\), satisfying \(\sum_{\rho}x_{t,\rho}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,\rho}\kappa_t x_{t,\rho}\le B\), such that replacing the transferred mass from ranking \(\rho_t\) by ranking \(\rho\) makes \(p\) win under \(k\)-approval.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix an integer \(k\ge3\). Let \(C\) contain \(m>k\) candidates. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Each type specifies a strict complete ranking \(\rho_t\) and a source price \(\kappa_t\in\{1,\infty\}\); types may share a ranking but have different prices. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Use mass-weighted \(k\)-approval with a supplied fixed priority order to select one winner among tied maximum-score candidates. Let \(w\) be the initial winner, and let the target \(p\in C\) satisfy \(p\ne w\). The budget \(B\ge0\) is a finite binary rational.
Define the eligible sources once, from the initial rankings: \(E=\{t\in T:p\succ_{\rho_t}w,\ \kappa_t=1\}\). Infinite-priced and non-vulnerable mass cannot move. Write \(\mathcal L(C)\) for all strict complete rankings of \(C\).
A transfer chooses real masses \(x_{t,\rho}\ge0\) for \(t\in E\) and \(\rho\in\mathcal L(C)\setminus\{\rho_t\}\), with \(\sum_\rho x_{t,\rho}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,\rho}x_{t,\rho}\le B\). It replaces each transferred mass from \(\rho_t\) by \(\rho\); all remaining mass retains its initial ranking. A source may split its mass among arbitrary destination rankings.

Is deciding whether such transfers make \(p\) the selected winner in \(\mathrm P\), or is it NP-hard, for each fixed \(k\ge3\)? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), with dependence on \(k\) explicit.

Discrete frugal priced bribery under \(k\)-approval is NP-complete for every fixed \(k\ge3\), even with vulnerable-voter prices in \(\{1,\infty\}\). [Dey–Misra–Narahari](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.08248)
Continuous priced bribery for scoring protocols with finite rational prices and a co-winner goal is polynomial-time solvable; this result does not explicitly treat the vulnerability restriction and supplied winner priority used here. [Koutecký–Melissinos–Vu–Sabater](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25444)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Require m>k, following the nonconstant scoring-vector convention, and p different from the initial winner, following the intended bribery setting.
  • Make the budget finite and nonnegative, retaining the discrete budget domain while allowing rational per-capita values.
  • Interpret infinity as prohibiting transfers, avoiding undefined products of infinity and zero.
  • Treat source price as part of a type, so identical rankings with different prices remain distinct support entries.
  • Allow real divisible transfers and arbitrary destination rankings; exclude transfers to the unchanged source ranking because they have no effect and can always be omitted.
  • Use the supplied priority order for both initial and final winner selection, and determine vulnerability only from the initial society.

Result

The problem is in \(\mathrm P\) for every fixed \(k\ge3\). In fact, the same algorithm is polynomial-time when \(k\) is part of the input, subject to \(1\le k<m\).

We give an exact linear program with at most \(\tau+m+1\) variables and \(O(\tau+m)\) constraints. It handles the fixed eligibility restriction, infinite prices, forbidden transfers to the source ranking, and the supplied winner priority.

1. Initial scores and eligible sources

For each type \(t\), let \(A_t\subseteq C\) be its set of top-\(k\) candidates. Define \(a_{t,c}=1\) when \(c\in A_t\), and \(a_{t,c}=0\) otherwise. The initial score of candidate \(c\) is

\(\displaystyle s_c=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_{t,c}.\)

Compute the initial selected winner \(w\), using the supplied priority order, and then compute

\(\displaystyle E=\{t\in T:p\succ_{\rho_t}w,\ \kappa_t=1\}.\)

Throughout the construction, only these original source types may move. In particular, eligibility is never recomputed after transfers.

Let \(H\subseteq C\setminus\{p\}\) consist of the candidates preceding \(p\) in the priority order. For any final score vector \(S\), candidate \(p\) is selected exactly when

\(\displaystyle S_p>S_c\quad(c\in H),\qquad S_p\ge S_c\quad(c\in C\setminus(H\cup\{p\})).\)

2. A characterization of destination approvals

Lemma. Let \(M\ge0\). A vector \(z\in\mathbb R^C\) is the aggregate approval vector of mass \(M\) distributed among strict complete rankings if and only if

\(\displaystyle 0\le z_c\le M\quad(c\in C),\qquad \sum_{c\in C}z_c=kM.\)

Proof. Necessity follows because each unit of mass approves exactly \(k\) distinct candidates.

If \(M=0\), the stated inequalities force \(z=0\), which is realizable by zero mass. Suppose \(M>0\), and set \(q=z/M\). Consider the bounded polytope

\(\displaystyle Q=\left\{q\in[0,1]^C:\sum_{c\in C}q_c=k\right\}.\)

Every vertex of \(Q\) is integral. Indeed, a point with a fractional coordinate must have at least two fractional coordinates: exactly one would contradict the integrality of its coordinate sum. If \(q_i,q_j\in(0,1)\), choose

\(\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<\min\{q_i,1-q_i,q_j,1-q_j\}.\)

Both \(q+\varepsilon(e_i-e_j)\) and \(q-\varepsilon(e_i-e_j)\) belong to \(Q\). They are distinct and have midpoint \(q\), so \(q\) is not a vertex.

By the convex-hull theorem for bounded polytopes, every point of \(Q\) is a convex combination of its vertices. Each vertex is the characteristic vector of a \(k\)-element subset of \(C\). Consequently, there are such subsets \(U_1,\ldots,U_r\) and nonnegative coefficients \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_j=1,\qquad q=\sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_j\mathbf1_{U_j}.\)

Choose a strict complete ranking \(\sigma_j\) whose top-\(k\) set is \(U_j\), and assign it mass \(M\lambda_j\). Its aggregate approval vector is \(z\). This proves sufficiency. \(\square\)

3. The linear program

Introduce the following variables:

  • \(y_t\), for each \(t\in E\), representing mass removed from that original source;
  • \(z_c\), for each \(c\in C\), representing aggregate approvals contributed by reassigned mass;
  • \(\delta\), representing a common margin against candidates in \(H\).

Use \(M=\sum_{t\in E}y_t\) as an abbreviation, not an additional variable. Impose

\(\displaystyle 0\le y_t\le\mu_t\quad(t\in E),\qquad M\le B,\)

\(\displaystyle 0\le z_c\le M\quad(c\in C),\qquad \sum_{c\in C}z_c=kM,\)

and \(\displaystyle 0\le\delta\le1.\)

Define the following affine expressions for final scores:

\(\displaystyle S_c=s_c-\sum_{t\in E}a_{t,c}y_t+z_c\quad(c\in C).\)

Finally, impose

\(\displaystyle S_p-S_c\ge\delta\quad(c\in H),\)

\(\displaystyle S_p-S_c\ge0\quad(c\in C\setminus(H\cup\{p\})).\)

Maximize \(\delta\). Reject if the program is infeasible; otherwise accept exactly when its optimum \(\delta^\star\) is positive.

This is an ordinary rational linear program after substituting the abbreviations \(M\) and \(S_c\).

4. Exact correspondence with permitted transfers

Successful transfers yield a positive objective

Suppose a permitted transfer makes \(p\) the selected winner. Set

\(\displaystyle y_t=\sum_{\rho\ne\rho_t}x_{t,\rho},\)

and

\(\displaystyle z_c=\sum_{t\in E}\sum_{\rho\ne\rho_t}x_{t,\rho}\mathbf1_{\{c\text{ is top-}k\text{ in }\rho\}}.\)

Source capacities and the budget give the constraints on \(y\). The lemma’s necessary conditions give the constraints on \(z\). Removing source approvals and adding destination approvals gives precisely the displayed formula for \(S_c\).

For every \(c\in H\), the margin \(S_p-S_c\) is strictly positive. If \(H\ne\varnothing\), choose

\(\displaystyle \delta=\min\left\{1,\min_{c\in H}(S_p-S_c)\right\}>0.\)

If \(H=\varnothing\), choose \(\delta=1\). All remaining winner constraints hold because \(p\) is selected. Thus \(\delta^\star>0\).

A positive objective yields permitted transfers

Conversely, suppose the program has a feasible solution with \(\delta>0\).

If \(M=0\), all \(y_t\) and \(z_c\) are zero. The unchanged society then has scores \(S_c=s_c\), and the winner constraints select \(p\). Under the premise \(p\ne w\), this case cannot occur, but it needs no transfers.

Now suppose \(M>0\). By the lemma, choose rankings \(\sigma_j\) and coefficients \(\lambda_j\) such that

\(\displaystyle z_c=M\sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_j\mathbf1_{\{c\text{ is top-}k\text{ in }\sigma_j\}},\qquad \sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_j=1.\)

For every \(t\in E\), provisionally reassign mass \(y_t\lambda_j\) to ranking \(\sigma_j\). The total provisional destination mass at \(\sigma_j\) is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in E}y_t\lambda_j=M\lambda_j,\)

so the resulting scores are exactly \(S_c\).

The only possible violation is a provisional reassignment to the source’s own ranking. Cancel every such reassignment: whenever \(\sigma_j=\rho_t\), leave that mass at its original source instead. Each cancellation leaves the resulting society unchanged, since the mass has the same ranking before and after. It also decreases the charged transfer mass.

After cancellation, every destination differs from its source ranking, each source transfers at most \(y_t\le\mu_t\), and total cost is at most \(M\le B\). All sources belong to \(E\). Hence these are permitted transfers with final scores \(S_c\).

The constraints give strict superiority over every candidate preceding \(p\) in priority and weak superiority over every other candidate. Therefore \(p\) is selected.

This proves the acceptance criterion.

5. Running time and exact treatment of strict inequalities

Write \(e=|E|\le\tau\). The program has \(e+m+1\) variables, \(2e+3m+2\) inequality constraints, and one equality constraint. Its matrix coefficients belong to \(\{-1,0,1,-k\}\).

All initial scores have \(O(L)\)-bit rational representations. To see this, write the masses with their encoded denominators and take their product as a common denominator. Its bit length is \(O(L)\); because every initial score lies in \([0,1]\), its numerator has the same bit bound. Score differences likewise have \(O(L)\)-bit representations.

Consequently, a dense encoding of the program has bit length

\(\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{LP}}=O\!\left((m+\tau)^2+(m+\tau)L+\tau\log(k+1)\right).\)

Preprocessing and constructing this encoding take polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Standard exact rational linear programming then solves the program in \(S_{\mathrm{LP}}^{O(1)}\) bit operations, with an absolute exponent independent of \(k\). Overall, the running time is

\(\displaystyle (m+\tau+L+\log(k+1))^{O(1)}.\)

The feasible region, when nonempty, is compact: \(y_t\le\mu_t\) implies \(M\le1\), hence \(z_c\le1\), and \(\delta\in[0,1]\). Thus its optimum is attained. Exact rational optimization permits an exact comparison of \(\delta^\star\) with zero; no predetermined positive tolerance is used.

This agrees with the polynomial-time scoring-protocol result in [Koutecký–Melissinos–Vu–Sabater, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25444). The direct formulation above establishes the required eligibility and priority variants.
