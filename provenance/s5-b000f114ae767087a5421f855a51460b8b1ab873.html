The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two atomless populations partitioned into finitely many types, with rational type masses, integer quotas, and strict rankings over opposite-side types, compute a stable matching. Each individual must receive an integral bundle of distinct partners within quota; the aggregate description may assign mass to different integral bundles. Stability requires that no pair of types has positive mass on both sides who would prefer to match with one another over a current partner or vacancy. Can such a matching always be computed in time polynomial in the number of types \(\tau\), maximum quota \(Q\), and input bit length \(L\), with a polynomial-size aggregate description?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be disjoint, nonempty finite type sets for two atomless populations. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) types with positive mass, each mass \(a_i\) a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Total masses are arbitrary and need not agree.
Each type \(i\) has a nonnegative integer quota \(q_i\) and a strict ranking \(\succ_i\) of opposite-side types and a vacancy option \(\bot\). Individuals of the same type are indifferent partners; only types ranked above \(\bot\) are acceptable, and slots are independent.
An individual receives at most \(q_i\) distinct partners. Several partners may have the same type, but an individual pair may occur only once.
An aggregate description lists positive binary-rational masses \(x_{i,b}\) assigned to integral bundle vectors \(b\), where \(b_j\) counts partners of type \(j\), \(\sum_j b_j\le q_i\), and every represented partner type is mutually acceptable.
Require \(\sum_b x_{i,b}=a_i\) and, for every \(i\in A,j\in B\), \(\sum_b x_{i,b}b_j=\sum_c x_{j,c}c_i\). The description must admit a realization, up to null sets, by reciprocal measurable pairings that preserve mass and never repeat an individual pair.
Pad every bundle to quota with vacancies. An individual of type \(i\) is willing toward type \(j\) precisely when \(j\succ_i k\) for some entry \(k\) of the padded bundle. Stability means that no \(i\in A,j\in B\) have positive masses of willing individuals on both sides.
Write running times in \(m=\max\{|A|,|B|\}\), support size \(\tau\), maximum quota \(Q=\max_i q_i\), and total input bit length \(L\).

Can a stable matching always be computed with an aggregate description of total bit length polynomial in \(m,\tau,Q,L\), in time polynomial in these parameters, or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite many-to-many markets with independent slots and no repeated individual pair, PolyGS terminates and returns a valid stable matching. [Mordig et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.12080)
Greinecker and Vocke establish pairwise-stable outcome existence in a distributional continuum many-to-many model with integral contract multisets. [Source](https://www2.uibk.ac.at/downloads/c9821000/wpaper/2026-02.pdf)
Baïou and Balinski give a strongly polynomial algorithm for divisible stable allocations; this concerns real-valued quantities rather than the individual integral bundles specified here. Neither cited continuous result states the requested aggregate computation bound. [Corrected source](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Unspecified normalization: allow arbitrary positive rational type masses and unequal population totals, retaining the discrete model's freedom to have different population sizes.
  • Unspecified quota domain: use nonnegative integer upper quotas, constant within each type; zero-quota individuals have no slots and cannot be willing toward any partner.
  • Missing outside option and individual rationality: include a strictly ranked vacancy option and permit only mutually acceptable partners.
  • Ambiguous distinctness: require distinct individual partners, while allowing several distinct partners of the same type; strict type rankings induce indifference within a type.
  • Unspecified aggregate representation: use binary-rational masses on integral partner-count vectors, with population conservation, reciprocal incidence balance, and measurable realization without repeated individual pairs.
  • Unspecified blocking treatment of existing partners: willingness concerns a new individual partner and remains possible when the bundle already contains that partner's type; unused slots are padded with vacancies.
  • Unspecified size parameter: define m as the larger side's supported type count and L as the complete input bit length; polynomial output size means total encoded bit length.

Result

A stable matching can always be computed with a polynomial-size aggregate description in polynomial time. In fact, the bounds are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(Q+1)\), which is stronger than the requested dependence on \(Q\).

The proof has three parts: stability depends only on aggregate slot quantities; those quantities admit a small integral-bundle decomposition; and every balanced rational bundle description admits a realization without repeated individual pairs.

1. Stability depends only on aggregate slot quantities

Let \(E\subseteq A\times B\) consist of the mutually acceptable type pairs. For an aggregate description, define its reciprocal slot quantities by

\(y_{ij}=\sum_b x_{i,b}b_j=\sum_c x_{j,c}c_i\qquad ((i,j)\in E).\)

Write \(y_{ij}=0\) outside \(E\). Define the total slot capacity and vacancy mass of type \(i\) by

\(c_i=q_i a_i,\qquad v_i=c_i-\sum_j y_{ij}.\)

Here and below, sums for a type run over opposite-side types.

Fix a mutually acceptable pair \(i,j\). For a padded bundle \(b\), let \(N_i(b,j)\) be the number of its entries strictly worse than \(j\). Because \(j\succ_i\bot\),

\(N_i(b,j)=q_i-\sum_k b_k+\sum_{k:\,j\succ_i k}b_k.\)

Consequently, the total mass of slots worse than \(j\) is

\(D_i(j):=\sum_b x_{i,b}N_i(b,j)=v_i+\sum_{k:\,j\succ_i k}y_{ik}.\)

Let \(W_i(j)\) denote the mass of individuals of type \(i\) willing toward \(j\). Willingness means exactly that \(N_i(b,j)\) is positive. Since this number is integral and at most \(q_i\),

\(W_i(j)\le D_i(j)\le q_iW_i(j).\)

Thus

\(W_i(j)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D_i(j)>0.\)

This also holds when \(q_i=0\), since both quantities then vanish. By the definition of \(v_i\),

\(D_i(j)=c_i-\sum_{k:\,k\succeq_i j}y_{ik},\)

where \(k\succeq_i j\) means \(k=j\) or \(k\succ_i j\).

It follows that stability is exactly the following condition on the slot quantities: for every mutually acceptable pair \(i,j\),

\[\sum_{k:\,k\succeq_i j}y_{ik}=c_i\quad\text{or}\quad\sum_{h:\,h\succeq_j i}y_{hj}=c_j.\tag{1}\]

Pairs that are not mutually acceptable cannot violate stability. On at least one side, the proposed partner type lies below \(\bot\), whereas every represented entry is either acceptable or a vacancy.

In particular, any two valid bundle descriptions with the same slot quantities have the same stability status.

2. Computing stable slot quantities

We use the following known result.

Stable-allocation theorem. Given finite bipartite sets with strict preferences, positive vertex capacities, and nonnegative edge upper bounds, a feasible stable allocation can be computed by a strongly polynomial algorithm. Stability requires that every edge below its upper bound have at least one endpoint whose capacity is filled entirely by allocations to that edge or better edges. Baïou and Balinski’s corrected inductive algorithm establishes this result; its arithmetic uses additions, subtractions, and comparisons, so integer input produces integer output. See their [corrected paper, Section 2 and Theorem 2](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

We now verify its applicability.

Temporarily remove types with quota zero. Their output bundle will be empty, and they are never willing toward any type. If no mutually acceptable pair remains, assigning everyone an empty bundle is stable.

Otherwise, retain the remaining types and their strict preference orders, and give type \(i\) capacity \(c_i=q_i a_i>0\). Assign every mutually acceptable edge \(i,j\) upper bound

\(u_{ij}=c_i+c_j,\)

and every other edge upper bound zero.

Every feasible allocation satisfies

\(y_{ij}\le\min\{c_i,c_j\}<u_{ij}\qquad ((i,j)\in E).\)

Hence no mutually acceptable edge can reach its upper bound. The theorem’s stability condition therefore becomes precisely (1).

All hypotheses hold: the graph is finite, preferences are strict, vertex capacities are positive, and edge bounds are nonnegative. Equality of the two sides’ total capacities is not required.

For exact computation, write each input mass as a rational with positive denominator and let \(D\) be the least common multiple of those denominators. Its binary length is at most the sum of their binary lengths, hence at most \(L\). Multiply all capacities and edge bounds by \(D\), run the algorithm on the resulting integer instance, and divide its output by \(D\). We obtain stable quantities \(y_{ij}\) with

\(Dy_{ij}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}.\)

The scaled capacities, edge bounds, and resulting allocations have binary length \(O(L+\log(Q+1))\). Strong polynomiality therefore gives polynomial bit complexity.

What remains is to convert these quantities into the individual integral bundles required by the question.

3. A small integral-bundle decomposition

We prove a constructive lemma.

Lemma. Suppose a type has mass \(a>0\), quota \(q\), and prescribed rational slot quantities \(z_1,\ldots,z_d\ge 0\) satisfying

\(\sum_{\ell=1}^d z_\ell\le qa.\)

There is a bundle description with at most \(d+1\) positive records that realizes exactly these quantities. It can be computed in polynomial time in the binary lengths of the data and \(\log(q+1)\).

Proof. The case \(q=0\) uses the empty bundle with mass \(a\). Assume \(q>0\).

Partition \([0,qa)\) into consecutive intervals of lengths \(z_1,\ldots,z_d\), labelled by their partner types, followed by a vacancy interval of length

\(z_{\bot}=qa-\sum_{\ell=1}^d z_\ell.\)

Intervals of length zero may be omitted. Let \(\lambda(s)\) denote the label at \(s\).

For each \(t\in[0,a)\), assign the individual indexed by \(t\) the padded bundle consisting of the labels

\(\lambda(t),\lambda(t+a),\ldots,\lambda(t+(q-1)a).\)

This gives exactly \(q\) entries, including vacancies, so its partner counts form an integral bundle of size at most \(q\).

Let \(b_\ell(t)\) be its count of partner type \(\ell\). The intervals \([ra,(r+1)a)\), for \(r=0,\ldots,q-1\), partition \([0,qa)\). Therefore

\(\int_0^a b_\ell(t)\,dt =\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}\int_0^a \mathbf 1_{\{\lambda(t+ra)=\ell\}}\,dt =z_\ell.\)

This proves the required slot quantities.

To obtain a finite description, let

\(s_\ell=\sum_{h=1}^{\ell}z_h\qquad (\ell=1,\ldots,d).\)

A bundle can change only when some \(t+ra\) crosses one of these boundaries. Each boundary gives at most one breakpoint in \([0,a)\), namely its residue

\(\rho_\ell=s_\ell-a\left\lfloor s_\ell/a\right\rfloor.\)

There are at most \(d\) distinct internal breakpoints. They partition \([0,a)\) into at most \(d+1\) positive-length intervals, on each of which the bundle is constant away from endpoints. Output that bundle with mass equal to the interval length. Merge identical bundles if necessary.

The construction does not need to enumerate the \(q\) slots. For a sample point \(t\) inside an interval, put \(s_0=0\). Its counts are

\(b_\ell(t)= \left\lceil\frac{s_\ell-t}{a}\right\rceil- \left\lceil\frac{s_{\ell-1}-t}{a}\right\rceil.\)

Indeed, this counts the integers \(r\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\) satisfying \(s_{\ell-1}\le t+ra<s_\ell\). Both ceilings lie between \(0\) and \(q\). Thus all counts can be computed using polynomially many exact integer arithmetic operations.

If \(Da\) and all \(Dz_\ell\) are integers, every breakpoint and output mass is also an integer multiple of \(1/D\). This completes the proof.

Apply the lemma independently to every type, using its incident quantities \(y_{ij}\). Every represented partner type is mutually acceptable. Each type has at most \(m+1\) records, and reciprocity follows from

\(\sum_b x_{i,b}b_j=y_{ij}=\sum_c x_{j,c}c_i.\)

By Section 1, the resulting description is stable.

4. Realization without repeated individual pairs

The preceding balance equations suffice for realization.

Lemma. Every finite rational bundle description satisfying the quota, acceptability, mass, and reciprocity conditions admits reciprocal measurable mass-preserving pairings without repeated individual pairs.

Proof. Choose a positive integer \(H\) such that every \(Hx_{i,b}\) is integral.

For every record \(i,b\), create \(Hx_{i,b}\) abstract vertices of type \(i\), each carrying bundle \(b\). Give each vertex \(b_j\) stubs requesting a partner of type \(j\).

For each \(i\in A,j\in B\), the two corresponding stub totals agree, because

\(\sum_b Hx_{i,b}b_j=\sum_c Hx_{j,c}c_i.\)

Pair these stubs arbitrarily. This produces a finite bipartite multigraph whose vertices have exactly their prescribed bundles. Parallel edges may occur at this intermediate stage.

Replace each vertex by a separate circle of circumference \(h=1/H\), equipped with Lebesgue measure. Its individuals are indexed by \(t\in[0,h)\).

For every pair of abstract vertices joined by \(k\) parallel edges, select distinct shifts modulo \(h\), for example

\(\sigma_r=\frac{rh}{k+1}\qquad (r=1,\ldots,k).\)

Realize edge \(r\) by pairing the individual \(t\) in its \(A\)-side cell with the individual

\(t+\sigma_r\pmod h\)

in its \(B\)-side cell. The reciprocal pairing is the inverse translation.

Every map is measurable and mass-preserving. Distinct shifts give distinct partners within the same target cell. Edges to different target vertices give partners in disjoint cells. Therefore no individual pair occurs twice. The inverse translations likewise give distinct partners on the \(B\) side.

Every individual has exactly the bundle attached to its abstract vertex. The cells for record \(i,b\) have total mass

\(Hx_{i,b}\cdot h=x_{i,b}.\)

Thus the construction realizes the description, with all required properties.

This establishes admission of a realization. The algorithm does not expand this potentially large multigraph; the requested output is the aggregate description.

5. Output size and running time

Let

\(K=O\bigl(L+\log(Q+1)+\log(\tau+1)\bigr).\)

The construction has at most \(\tau(m+1)\) records. Each mass needs \(O(K)\) bits, and each bundle has at most \(m\) coordinates, each between \(0\) and \(Q\). Consequently, total output length is

\(O\!\left(\tau(m+1)\left[K+m\log(Q+1)\right]\right).\)

The stable-allocation stage takes polynomial bit time in \(m,\tau,L,\log(Q+1)\). The bundle stage uses \(O(\tau(m+1)^2)\) exact arithmetic operations on numbers of polynomially bounded binary length, including the integer divisions for residues and ceilings. Hence the entire computation takes

\(\operatorname{poly}\bigl(m,\tau,L,\log(Q+1)\bigr)\)

bit time.

If “binary rational” specifically means dyadic rational, the proof still applies: choose \(D\) as a power of two, and all output masses remain integer multiples of \(1/D\).

Thus the requested polynomial-time computation and polynomial aggregate output bound both hold.
