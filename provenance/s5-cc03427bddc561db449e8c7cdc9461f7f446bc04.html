The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite project set \(C\), rational costs \(q_c=\mathrm{cost}(c)/B\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over exact additive utility vectors \(u_t\), can one compute in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) a feasible \(P\subseteq C\) such that every feasible completion \(W\supseteq P\) has no fractional FJR1+ witness? A witness would consist of \(\beta>0\) and nonnegative \(z_t,y_c,x_{tc}\) with \(0<z_t\le\mu_t\) whenever \(z_t>0\), \(\sum_t z_t\ge\sum_c q_cy_c\), and, for each \(t\) with \(z_t>0\), \(u_t(W)<\beta\) and \(\sum_c u_t(c)x_{tc}=\beta z_t\). Require \(x_{tc}\le\mu_t y_c\), \(x_{tc}\le z_t\) for \(c\in W\), \(x_{tc}=0\) when \(u_t(c)=0\), and \(x_{tc}=0\) when \(c\notin W\) and \(u_t(W)+u_t(c)>\beta\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a project set with \(m\ge1\), normalized costs \(q_c\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), and nonnegative additive utility vectors \(u_t\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{m}\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\ge1\) distinct types with positive masses \(\mu_t\) given as binary rationals, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
An outcome \(W\subseteq C\) is feasible when \(\sum_{c\in W}q_c\le1\); a feasible completion of \(P\) is any feasible \(W\supseteq P\), without an exhaustiveness requirement.
A fractional FJR1+ witness for \(W\) consists of real \(\beta>0\) and nonnegative real \(z_t,y_c,x_{tc}\) satisfying \(0\le z_t\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_tz_t>0\), and \(\sum_tz_t\ge\sum_cq_cy_c\).
Require \(u_t(W)<\beta\) whenever \(z_t>0\), and \(\sum_cu_t(c)x_{tc}=\beta z_t\) for every type \(t\). For every \(t,c\), require \(x_{tc}\le\mu_ty_c\), additionally \(x_{tc}\le z_t\) when \(c\in W\), and \(x_{tc}=0\) when \(u_t(c)=0\) or when \(c\notin W\) and \(u_t(W)+u_t(c)>\beta\). Project weights have no additional upper bound; assignment bounds apply separately to each type, without summing assignments across types.

Can a feasible \(P\subseteq C\) whose every feasible completion has no fractional FJR1+ witness be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite lists of equally weighted voters with binary rational nonnegative additive utilities and costs, a polynomial-time algorithm computes a feasible partial outcome whose every feasible completion satisfies FJR1+; checking FJR1+ is also polynomial-time ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08888v1)). No published result settling exactly the mass formulation above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one and the available budget to one, so feasibility is the normalized cost sum being at most one.
  • Specified nonempty project and type sets, nonnegative binary rational costs and utilities, and total input bit length L; zero costs and zero utilities remain allowed.
  • Required strictly positive total participating mass, excluding the all-zero witness omitted from the graded question.
  • Made witness variables real, left project weights without an additional upper bound, and imposed utility equations for every type, following the discrete assignment formulation.
  • Specified that completions need not be exhaustive and that assignment bounds apply separately to each type because projects are public goods.

Result

The search problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\).

The residual-budget algorithm from [Section 4 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08888v1#S4) admits a mass-weighted implementation. Below is a direct proof for the formulation in the question, including its binary-arithmetic running time. No expansion into individual voters is needed.

1. Integer utilities and finitely many witness levels

Multiply all utilities by a positive integer \(D\) divisible by their denominators, and write \(v_t(c)=D u_t(c)\). The integers \(v_t(c)\) have polynomial bit length: the product of the input denominators is a permissible choice of \(D\).

This scaling preserves witnesses, by replacing \(\beta\) with \(D\beta\) and leaving \(z,y,x\) unchanged. Henceforth use the integer utilities \(v_t\), and put \(U=\max_t v_t(C)\). If \(U=0\), the utility equations preclude every witness, so returning the zero-cost projects suffices.

Assume \(U>0\).

Lemma 1. If an outcome \(W\) has a witness, it has one at an integer level in
\(\{v_t(W)+v_t(c):c\notin W,\ v_t(c)>0\}\), and therefore at a level between \(1\) and \(U\).

Proof. Fix a witness at level \(\beta\). If \(z_t=0\), its utility equation and the zero-utility assignment restriction imply \(x_{tc}=0\) for every \(c\).

Every type with \(z_t>0\) has a positive assignment to an unselected project. Otherwise,

\( \displaystyle \beta z_t=\sum_{c\in W}v_t(c)x_{tc} \leq v_t(W)z_t<\beta z_t, \)

a contradiction. Define

\( \displaystyle h=\max\{v_t(W)+v_t(c):c\notin W,\ x_{tc}>0\}. \)

The maximum is well defined. Its entries have \(v_t(c)>0\), and the witness restrictions give \(h\leq\beta\). Each participating type has such an assignment, so \(v_t(W)<h\).

Replace every assignment by \(x'_{tc}=(h/\beta)x_{tc}\), leaving \(z,y\) unchanged. The utility equations now hold at \(h\), and all assignment upper bounds remain valid. Every positive unselected assignment has \(v_t(W)+v_t(c)\leq h\), as required. The mass and cost conditions are unchanged.

Finally, \(h\) is a positive integer, and each defining entry is at most \(v_t(C)\leq U\). This proves the lemma.

2. The weighted residual-budget algorithm

Maintain a selected set \(P\) and an aggregate remaining budget \(B_t\) for each type. Initialize

\( \displaystyle P=\{c:q_c=0\},\qquad B_t=\mu_t. \)

Conceptually process the integer levels \(h=U,U-1,\ldots,1\). At the current state, type \(t\)'s aggregate offer for \(c\notin P\) is

\( \displaystyle O_{tc}(h)= \begin{cases} B_t\min\left\{1,\dfrac{v_t(c)}{h-v_t(P)}\right\}, &v_t(P)<h,\\ 0,&v_t(P)\geq h. \end{cases} \)

A project is affordable if \(\sum_t O_{tc}(h)\geq q_c\). Repeatedly purchase an affordable project at the current level until none remains.

Fix project and type orders. Choose the first affordable project \(d\), and obtain payments \(\pi_t\) by starting with \(r=q_d\) and, in type order, setting

\( \displaystyle \pi_t=\min\{O_{td}(h),r\},\qquad r\leftarrow r-\pi_t. \)

Affordability ensures the final residual is zero. Update \(B_t\leftarrow B_t-\pi_t\) and \(P\leftarrow P\cup\{d\}\).

All payments satisfy \(0\leq\pi_t\leq B_t\), and their sum is \(q_d\). Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{c\in P}q_c+\sum_t B_t=1. \)

Thus every intermediate selected set, and the returned set \(P_{\mathrm{fin}}\), is feasible.

Section 6 below implements this conceptual algorithm without visiting all \(U\) levels.

3. The budget invariant

Fix any completion \(W\supseteq P_{\mathrm{fin}}\), and suppose it has a witness at level \(\beta\). For the moment, consider any intermediate state with selected set \(P\subseteq W\).

For each participating type \(t\), meaning \(z_t>0\), define

\( \displaystyle a_t=\beta-v_t(P),\qquad T_t=\sum_{c\notin P}v_t(c)x_{tc},\qquad b_t=\frac{B_t}{\mu_t}. \)

These are well defined because \(\mu_t>0\) and \(v_t(P)\leq v_t(W)<\beta\). Moreover,

\( \displaystyle T_t =\beta z_t-\sum_{c\in P}v_t(c)x_{tc} \geq\bigl(\beta-v_t(P)\bigr)z_t =a_tz_t. \)

Here the inequality uses \(x_{tc}\leq z_t\) on \(P\subseteq W\).

Consider the potential

\( \displaystyle \Phi(P,B)= \sum_{t:z_t>0}\frac{b_tT_t}{a_t} -\sum_{c\notin P}q_cy_c. \)

Lemma 2. Initially \(\Phi\geq0\). Every purchase at a level \(h\geq\beta\) preserves \(\Phi\geq0\).

Proof. Initially \(b_t=1\), and all projects in \(P\) have zero cost. Therefore,

\( \displaystyle \Phi \geq\sum_tz_t-\sum_cq_cy_c \geq0. \)

Now suppose \(d\) is purchased at level \(h\geq\beta\). Since the entire execution selects only projects in \(W\), we have \(d\in W\setminus P\).

For a participating type, abbreviate
\(a=a_t\), \(T=T_t\), \(b=b_t\), \(v=v_t(d)\), \(x=x_{td}\), and \(p=\pi_t/\mu_t\).
Then

\( \displaystyle v<a,\qquad x\leq z_t,\qquad p\leq\frac{b v}{h-v_t(P)}\leq\frac{b v}{a}. \)

The first inequality follows from \(v_t(P)+v_t(d)\leq v_t(W)<\beta\). The payment inequalities follow from the offer definition.

After the purchase, the three quantities \(a,T,b\) become \(a-v,T-vx,b-p\). Direct algebra gives

\( \displaystyle \frac{(b-p)(T-vx)}{a-v} -\frac{bT}{a}+px = \frac{(bv-ap)(T-ax)}{a(a-v)}. \)

The denominator is positive. Both numerator factors are nonnegative: \(bv-ap\geq0\), and
\(T-ax\geq a(z_t-x)\geq0\).
Thus the contribution of this type to the first sum in \(\Phi\) decreases by at most \(p x_{td}\).

Using the mass-specific assignment bound,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t:z_t>0}\frac{\pi_t}{\mu_t}x_{td} \leq \sum_t\pi_t y_d =q_dy_d. \)

This is precisely where \(x_{td}\leq\mu_t y_d\) is needed. Removing \(d\) from the remaining-project cost sum increases \(\Phi\) by \(q_dy_d\). Hence the total change in \(\Phi\) is nonnegative.

4. A witness forces affordability at its target level

Lemma 3. Suppose \(P\subseteq W\) contains all zero-cost projects. If no project outside \(P\) is affordable at level \(\beta\), then \(\Phi(P,B)<0\).

Proof. First,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{c\notin P}q_cy_c>0. \)

Indeed, every remaining project has positive cost. If this sum were zero, all remaining \(y_c\) would be zero, hence all remaining assignments would be zero by \(x_{tc}\leq\mu_t y_c\). This would contradict \(T_t\geq a_tz_t>0\) for a participating type.

Consider a participating type and a positive assignment \(x_{tc}>0\) to a remaining project.

If \(c\in W\setminus P\), additivity gives

\( \displaystyle v_t(c)\leq v_t(W)-v_t(P)<a_t. \)

If \(c\notin W\), the witness restriction gives

\( \displaystyle v_t(c)\leq\beta-v_t(W)\leq a_t. \)

Thus, in either case, the offer cap does not reduce the offer at level \(\beta\):

\( \displaystyle O_{tc}(\beta)=\mu_t b_t\,\frac{v_t(c)}{a_t}. \)

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t:z_t>0}\frac{b_tT_t}{a_t} = \sum_{\substack{c\notin P\\y_c>0}} y_c\sum_{t:z_t>0} \frac{x_{tc}}{\mu_t y_c} \left(\mu_t b_t\frac{v_t(c)}{a_t}\right) \leq \sum_{c\notin P}y_c\sum_t O_{tc}(\beta). \)

For terms with \(x_{tc}=0\), the contribution on the left is zero. For positive assignments, the displayed offer identity applies, and \(x_{tc}/(\mu_t y_c)\leq1\).

By the assumed absence of affordable projects, \(\sum_tO_{tc}(\beta)<q_c\) for every remaining \(c\). Some remaining \(y_c\) is positive, as established above. Therefore,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t:z_t>0}\frac{b_tT_t}{a_t} < \sum_{c\notin P}q_cy_c, \)

which proves \(\Phi<0\).

5. Every completion is witness-free

Fix a feasible completion \(W\supseteq P_{\mathrm{fin}}\). Suppose it has a witness. By Lemma 1, choose such a witness with an integer level \(\beta\) between \(1\) and \(U\).

Throughout the execution, the current selected set is a subset of \(P_{\mathrm{fin}}\), hence a subset of \(W\). Lemma 2 therefore applies to every purchase at levels at least \(\beta\). It implies \(\Phi\geq0\) at the end of level \(\beta\).

At that point all zero-cost projects are selected, and no remaining project is affordable at level \(\beta\). Lemma 3 gives \(\Phi<0\), a contradiction.

Thus every feasible completion of \(P_{\mathrm{fin}}\) has no witness. The argument places no exhaustiveness condition on the completion.

6. Polynomial implementation and bit complexity

It remains to avoid the possibly exponential number \(U\) of conceptual levels and to bound exact-arithmetic sizes.

Skipping empty levels

Fix a state \(P,B\), and let \(H\) be the largest level still to be processed. Partition the integers from \(1\) through \(H\) into intervals on which the active set

\( \displaystyle \{t:v_t(P)<h\} \)

is constant. There are at most \(\tau+1\) such intervals.

Within one interval, each offer \(O_{tc}(h)\) is nonincreasing as \(h\) increases: its active denominator increases, while \(P,B\) remain fixed. Thus the predicate “some remaining project is affordable” is monotone within that interval.

Examine intervals from highest to lowest. An interval whose smallest level has no affordable project can be skipped entirely. In the first interval containing affordability, binary search locates the largest affordable integer level. Purchase the first affordable project there, and repeat the search with that level as the new upper bound.

If no interval contains an affordable level, terminate. This reproduces the conceptual algorithm: between purchases, skipped levels do not change the state.

There are at most \(m\) purchases and one terminal search. Each search examines at most \(\tau+1\) intervals and uses a polynomial number of affordability tests, bounded by \(O((\tau+1)\log(U+1))\). Each test computes at most \(m\tau\) offers. Since \(\log(U+1)\) is polynomial in the input length, the operation count is polynomial.

Exact rational arithmetic

Let \(R_0\) be the product of the input denominators of the masses and costs. Its bit length is \(O(L)\).

At a purchase, write each offer as \(O_{td}(h)=k_tB_t\), where \(k_t\) is either zero, one, or \(v_t(d)/(h-v_t(P))\). Every denominator of \(k_t\) divides an integer between \(1\) and \(U\).

Suppose the current budgets have a common denominator \(R\), divisible by \(R_0\). If \(g_t\) is the reduced denominator of \(k_t\), then all offers, residuals, payments, and updated budgets at this purchase have denominators dividing

\( \displaystyle R'=R\prod_t g_t. \)

To see this, offers are products \(k_tB_t\); residuals are obtained from the cost by successive subtractions; each payment is one of an offer and a residual; and updated budgets are differences. All these numbers therefore share denominator \(R'\).

After at most \(m\) purchases, a common denominator consequently has bit length at most

\( \displaystyle O\!\left(L+m\tau\log(U+1)\right). \)

Budgets and offers lie between zero and one, so their numerators have polynomial bit length as well. During searches, multiplying a budget by a candidate offer coefficient adds only \(O(\log(U+1))\) denominator bits. Utilities, levels, costs, comparisons, and sums likewise have polynomial bit length.

Thus every arithmetic operation and the complete algorithm run in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
