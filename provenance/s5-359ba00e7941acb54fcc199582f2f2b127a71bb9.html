The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(M\), \(R\), \(\mathrm{attSpan}\), the Steemit parameters \(a,b,\mathrm{regen}\), and a finite rational stake distribution \(\nu\), decide whether every finite-support rational refinement \(\mu\) of \(\nu\) into complete types \(\theta=(s_\theta,\ell_\theta)\), with \(\ell_\theta\in[0,1]^M\), and every initial ordering of the \(M\) posts yields a fully ideal final order under the paper’s honest process. A no instance is witnessed by such a refinement and an initial ordering whose final order is not ideal.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Inputs are \(m=M\ge2\) labelled posts, integers \(R\ge1\) and \(h=\mathrm{attSpan}\ge1\), rational parameters \(a>0\), \(b\ge0\), \(a+b<1\), \(0<\mathrm{regen}\le1\), and \(\lceil(a+b)/\mathrm{regen}\rceil>1\), and a rational probability distribution \(\nu\) on positive rational stakes.
A society is given by its support: a list of positive-mass types, each mass a binary rational. Support size counts listed types, not possible types. Write \(\tau=|\operatorname{supp}\nu|\); a refinement can have any finite support size. Integers are binary encoded, posts explicitly listed, and \(L\) is total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\), including dependence on \(R,h\).
A rational refinement is \(\mu=\sum_\theta x_\theta\delta_{(s_\theta,\ell_\theta)}\), where \(x_\theta>0\), \(\ell_\theta\in([0,1]\cap\mathbb Q)^M\), and \(\sum_{\theta:s_\theta=s}x_\theta=\nu(s)\) for every stake \(s\).
Initially all post scores are zero; each type has voting power \(v_\theta=1\) and an empty set of previously voted post labels. Each round begins by replacing \(v_\theta\) with \(\min\{1,v_\theta+\mathrm{regen}\}\) for every type.
If \(R<M\), every round is a voting round; otherwise voting rounds are \(\{1+\lfloor(i-1)(R-1)/(M-1)\rfloor:1\le i\le M\}\). Other rounds contain no votes.
On a voting round, each type examines the first \(\min\{h,\text{number of unvoted posts}\}\) posts in the current order after deleting its previously voted labels. It votes for a post maximizing its likability, breaking ties by current order, even when its likability is zero.
For its chosen post \(p\), set \(c_\theta=\min\{v_\theta,a v_\theta\ell_{\theta p}+b\}\), add \(x_\theta s_\theta c_\theta\) to that post's score, replace \(v_\theta\) by \(v_\theta-c_\theta\), and mark \(p\) voted. All choices use the round's starting order; after aggregating the round's votes, sort by decreasing cumulative score, preserving the previous order on ties.
The ideal score is \(q_p=\sum_\theta x_\theta\ell_{\theta p}\). A final order \(\pi\) is fully ideal exactly when \(q_{\pi(1)}\ge\cdots\ge q_{\pi(M)}\). The decision problem accepts exactly when every rational refinement and every initial post order produces a fully ideal final order.

Is this universal convergence decision problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies with at least two voters, the verified discrete result gives full convergence for equal positive stakes when \(R-1\ge(M-1)\lceil(a+b)/\mathrm{regen}\rceil\), and asserts counterexamples with a nonoptimal top post for unequal stakes or below that bound. [Source](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/129969711/A_Puff_of_Steem_KIAYIAS_DOA13012020_VOR_CC_BY.pdf)
No result settling this exact rational-mass decision problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the stake distribution and its refinements to total population mass one; refinement preserves the stake marginal, and ideal scores weight population mass rather than stake.
  • Required rational likabilities and positive rational stakes, with no bound on refinement support size or rational denominators.
  • Required at least two posts, positive a, and positive regeneration: one post makes the voting schedule undefined, zero a invalidates the intended convergence claim, and zero regeneration makes its bound undefined.
  • Used capped regeneration with min, correcting the max operation that conflicts with the stated voting-power domain and remains in the published version.
  • Restored the prescribed voting schedule, exclusion of previously voted posts from attention, votes of weight zero, and truncation of vote cost at available voting power.
  • Aggregated mass votes and reordered only at round boundaries, matching the discrete procedures; within-round activation order therefore requires no additional quantifier.
  • Resolved unspecified ties by current order for vote selection and stable sorting for score ties.
  • Treated posts as distinct labels even when their likability vectors coincide, so each post can receive one vote from each type.
  • Made posts explicit input objects and encoded numerical parameters in binary; tau denotes input stake-support size, not the unbounded support size of quantified refinements.

A polynomial-time characterization

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). More precisely, it accepts exactly when

\(\displaystyle \tau=1\quad\text{and}\quad R-1\ge (m-1)\left\lceil\frac{a+b}{\mathrm{regen}}\right\rceil.\)

This criterion holds for every attention span \(h\ge1\). Every rejecting instance has a rational counterexample with at most \(2\tau\) listed types.

The threshold agrees with the [discrete convergence theorem cited in the question](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/129969711/A_Puff_of_Steem_KIAYIAS_DOA13012020_VOR_CC_BY.pdf). The proof below establishes the characterization directly for the specified simultaneous-round dynamics and rational masses.

1. Notation and a refinement construction

Write \(g=\mathrm{regen}\), \(F=a+b\), and \(K=\lceil F/g\rceil\). The hypotheses imply

\(\displaystyle 0<a\le F<1,\qquad K\ge2,\qquad 0<g<F.\)

Let \(C_p\) denote the final cumulative score of post \(p\). A pair satisfying \(C_A>C_B\) and \(q_A<q_B\) certifies failure: sorting by cumulative score puts \(A\) before \(B\), which is incompatible with nonincreasing ideal scores.

We will frequently use the following construction.

Lemma. Let \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_t\) be positive rational masses summing to one, and let \(\ell^1,\ldots,\ell^t\) be rational likability vectors. Define

\(\displaystyle \mu=\sum_{s\in\operatorname{supp}\nu}\sum_{j=1}^t \nu(s)\lambda_j\,\delta_{(s,\ell^j)},\qquad S=\sum_s\nu(s)s>0.\)

This is a valid rational refinement. Its orders and voting histories agree with those of the society having unit stake, profile masses \(\lambda_j\), and likabilities \(\ell^j\). Every cumulative score is multiplied by \(S\), while

\(\displaystyle q_p=\sum_j\lambda_j\ell^j_p.\)

Proof. For each stake \(s\), the masses sum to \(\nu(s)\). Types sharing a likability vector start with identical voting power and voted-label sets. Given the same current order, they therefore make identical choices and incur identical power costs.

Inductively, a vote of profile \(j\), with power cost \(c_j\), contributes

\(\displaystyle \sum_s\nu(s)\lambda_j s c_j=S\lambda_jc_j.\)

Thus the scores are a common positive multiple of the corresponding unit-stake scores. This preserves all score comparisons and ties, so stable sorting gives the same next order. The ideal-score identity follows from \(\sum_s\nu(s)=1\). This completes the induction. \(\square\)

2. Sufficiency: equal stakes and enough regeneration

Assume \(\tau=1\), with stake \(s_0>0\), and \(R-1\ge(m-1)K\).

Since \(K\ge2\), this implies \(R\ge m\), so there are exactly \(m\) voting rounds. Set \(T=R-1\) and \(n=m-1\). Write \(T=kn+t\), where \(0\le t<n\). The voting-round formula gives, for \(1\le i\le n\),

\(\displaystyle r_{i+1}-r_i=k+\left\lfloor\frac{it}{n}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{(i-1)t}{n}\right\rfloor\ge k\ge K.\)

Every type votes with full power on its first vote. Suppose it votes with full power on some voting round. Its cost is

\(\displaystyle c=a\ell_p+b\le F<1.\)

If the next voting round is \(d\) rounds later, then \(d\ge K\), so \(dg\ge Kg\ge F\ge c\). There are no intervening votes. Consequently, its power before the next vote is

\(\displaystyle \min\{1,1-c+dg\}=1.\)

Induction proves that every vote is cast with full power.

Each type chooses one unvoted label on each of the \(m\) voting rounds. Hence it votes for every post exactly once, irrespective of \(h\). For any rational refinement,

\(\displaystyle C_p=\sum_\theta x_\theta s_0(a\ell_{\theta p}+b)=s_0(aq_p+b).\)

Because \(as_0>0\), sorting by \(C_p\) sorts by \(q_p\). Thus every refinement and every initial order produces a fully ideal final order.

3. Necessity when there is insufficient time

Assume \(R-1<(m-1)K\). We construct counterexamples for every stake distribution.

Choose distinct labels \(A,B\), and start with order \(A,B,\ldots\). All other posts will have likability zero.

3.1. A single voting round

Suppose \(R=1\).

If \(h=1\), give every type likabilities \(\ell_A=1/2\) and \(\ell_B=1\). Every type votes for \(A\), so

\(\displaystyle C_A=S(a/2+b)>0=C_B,\qquad q_A=1/2<1=q_B.\)

If \(h\ge2\), use the lemma with two profiles:

| Profile mass | Likability of \(A\) | Likability of \(B\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(1/3\) | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| \(2/3\) | \(3/4\) | \(1\) |

Both posts are initially visible. The first profile votes for \(A\), and the second votes for \(B\), each at cost \(F\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle C_A=SF/3<2SF/3=C_B,\qquad q_A=5/6>2/3=q_B.\)

Both cases give a strict reversal.

3.2. The first inter-vote gap is insufficient

Now suppose \(R\ge2\). Let \(d\) be the gap between the first two voting rounds.

If \(R<m\), then \(d=1\), and \(dg=g<F\).

If \(R\ge m\), then

\(\displaystyle d=\left\lfloor\frac{R-1}{m-1}\right\rfloor<K.\)

Since \(K-1<F/g\), this again implies \(dg<F\).

Define

\(\displaystyle \rho=dg,\qquad D=F-\rho.\)

Thus \(0<D<F<1\). A first vote with cost \(F\) leaves power

\(\displaystyle w=1-F+\rho=1-D\in(0,1)\)

before the second vote.

3.3. Attention span \(h=1\)

Use one common likability profile at every stake. Set

\(\displaystyle \delta=\frac{D}{2(1+a)},\qquad \ell_A=1-\delta,\qquad \ell_B=1.\)

Here \(0<\delta<1\), so the profile is admissible.

Every type is forced to vote first for \(A\), incurring cost \(c_A=F-a\delta\). After that round, \(A\) has positive score, while all other scores remain zero. Stable sorting preserves \(B\) as the first unvoted post, so every type votes for \(B\) next.

Since \(a\delta<D\), the power before this second vote is

\(\displaystyle v=1-c_A+\rho=1-D+a\delta<1.\)

Its cost satisfies

\(\displaystyle c_B=\min\{v,av+b\}\le av+b=F-aD+a^2\delta.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle c_A-c_B\ge aD-a(1+a)\delta=\frac{aD}{2}>0.\)

Every type has now voted for both targets, so their scores never change again. Hence

\(\displaystyle C_A=Sc_A>Sc_B=C_B,\qquad q_A=1-\delta<1=q_B.\)

This proves failure for \(h=1\).

3.4. Attention span \(h\ge2\)

Set

\(\displaystyle \delta=\frac{aD}{8F},\qquad \lambda=\frac{2\delta}{1+2\delta}.\)

Because \(0<D<F\) and \(a<1\), we have \(0<\delta<1/8\). Use the lemma with these two profiles:

| Profile mass | Likability of \(A\) | Likability of \(B\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(\lambda\) | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| \(1-\lambda\) | \(1-\delta\) | \(1\) |

Initially both targets are visible. The first profile votes for \(A\), and the second for \(B\), each with cost \(F\). Both targets therefore have positive scores after the first round, while all other posts have zero scores.

After deleting its previously voted target, each profile sees the other target first. Thus the profiles vote for their remaining targets on the second voting round. Both have power \(w=1-D\). Define their second-vote costs by

\(\displaystyle z=\min\{w,b\},\qquad H=\min\{w,aw(1-\delta)+b\}.\)

Their final target scores are

\(\displaystyle C_A=S\bigl(\lambda F+(1-\lambda)H\bigr),\qquad C_B=S\bigl((1-\lambda)F+\lambda z\bigr).\)

These scores remain fixed after the second vote. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle H\le aw+b=F-aD,\qquad z\ge0.\)

It follows that

\(\displaystyle C_B-C_A=\frac{S}{1+2\delta}\bigl(F-H-2\delta(F-z)\bigr)\ge\frac{S}{1+2\delta}(aD-2\delta F)=\frac{3SaD}{4(1+2\delta)}>0.\)

However, the ideal-score difference has the opposite sign:

\(\displaystyle q_A-q_B=\lambda+(1-\lambda)(1-\delta)-(1-\lambda)=\frac{\delta}{1+2\delta}>0.\)

Thus \(B\) finishes before the ideally better \(A\). This proves failure for \(h\ge2\).

Together, these constructions reject every instance below the time bound, for every stake distribution. They use at most \(2\tau\) positive-mass types.

4. Necessity of equal stakes above the time bound

It remains to consider \(R-1\ge(m-1)K\) with \(\tau>1\).

The regeneration argument from Section 2 applies regardless of stakes. Every type votes once for every post, always with full power. Therefore,

\(\displaystyle C_p=a\sum_\theta x_\theta s_\theta\ell_{\theta p}+bS.\)

Choose two supported stakes \(s_H>s_L\), and define

\(\displaystyle t=\frac12\min\{\nu(s_H),\nu(s_L)\}>0,\qquad u=\frac{1+s_L/s_H}{2}.\)

These are rational, and \(s_L/s_H<u<1\).

At stake \(s_H\), assign mass \(t\) the likability \(\ell_A=u\), with all other likabilities zero. At stake \(s_L\), assign mass \(t\) the likability \(\ell_B=1\), with all other likabilities zero. Assign every remaining mass an all-zero vector.

This is a valid refinement: the residual masses at both selected stakes are strictly positive. It has \(\tau+2\le2\tau\) listed types.

Its ideal scores satisfy

\(\displaystyle q_A=tu<t=q_B.\)

Its final cumulative scores satisfy

\(\displaystyle C_A-C_B=at(s_Hu-s_L)=\frac{at(s_H-s_L)}2>0.\)

Thus \(A\) finishes before the ideally better \(B\). Unequal stakes therefore cause rejection even above the time bound.

5. Decision algorithm and bit complexity

The characterization gives the following algorithm:

  • Compare the supported stake values. Reject if there is more than one distinct value.
  • Compute \(K=\lceil(a+b)/g\rceil\) using exact rational arithmetic.
  • Accept exactly when \(R-1\ge(m-1)K\).

The proof establishes both directions of this test.

All integers used in the test have \(O(L)\) bits. There are \(O(\tau+1)\) arithmetic operations on such integers, plus the input scan. With elementary integer arithmetic, a conservative bound is

\(\displaystyle O\bigl(L+(\tau+1)L^2\bigr)\)

bit operations and \(O(L)\) space; in particular, it is bounded by \(O((m+\tau+1)L^2)\).

The dependence on \(R\) and \(h\) is through their binary encoding lengths in \(L\). The attention span does not enter the acceptance criterion, and a numerically large \(R\) is handled by integer arithmetic.
