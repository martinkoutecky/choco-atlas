Theorem 3 (\texttt{TwoAgentsEFoneInterval}) — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many worker types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), monotone non-positive valuations \(v_\theta\), interval chore kinds \(J\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), let \(\mathcal I\) be the integral interval-feasible bundles and choose masses \(x_{\theta,B}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_Bx_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B\ni j}x_{\theta,B}\le q_j\). Decide or compute an allocation whose positive-support bundles satisfy pairwise \(\mathrm{EF1}\), and in which every chore with residual supply cannot be added to any positive-mass bundle without an interval conflict.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) worker types \(\theta\in\Theta\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) chore kinds \(J\), each with a half-open interval \([s_j,f_j)\), binary integers \(0\leq s_j<f_j\), and binary-rational per-capita supply \(q_j\geq0\). Let \(\mathcal I\) contain all subsets of \(J\) whose intervals are pairwise disjoint, including the empty set; a worker receives at most one copy of each kind.
Each type has a Boolean circuit evaluating \(v_\theta:\mathcal I\to\mathbb Z_{\leq0}\) on bundle incidence vectors, with binary integer output, promised to satisfy \(v_\theta(B)\geq v_\theta(C)\) whenever \(B\subseteq C\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including circuits.
An allocation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(x_{\theta,B}\) satisfying \(\sum_{B\in\mathcal I}x_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(u_j:=\sum_{\theta,B\ni j}x_{\theta,B}\leq q_j\).
Pairwise \(\mathrm{EF1}\) means that for every ordered pair \((\theta,B),(\phi,C)\) with positive allocation mass and \(B\neq\emptyset\), some \(j\in B\) satisfies \(v_\theta(B\setminus\{j\})\geq v_\theta(C)\); comparisons include bundles received by the same type.
Maximality means that whenever \(u_j<q_j\), every positive-mass bundle \(B\) contains some \(k\) whose interval overlaps \([s_j,f_j)\), allowing \(k=j\): an additional copy also conflicts with an existing copy of that kind.

Is deciding whether an allocation satisfying pairwise \(\mathrm{EF1}\) and maximality exists in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For two discrete agents with monotone valuations and interval conflicts, a maximal \(\mathrm{EF1}\) allocation is computable in polynomial time using value queries. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04353v2)
For general conflict graphs, the two-agent guarantee extends to monotone valuations with a pseudopolynomial algorithm; existence is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for each fixed number of discrete agents at least three. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14149v1)
No result settling this exact distribution-over-types problem was identified in the search.

  • Normalize total worker mass to one because supplies are per capita; allow arbitrary support size as specified in the graded question.
  • Use half-open positive-length intervals with nonnegative integer endpoints, following the discrete timing convention.
  • Allow nonnegative rational supplies without an additional upper bound, since the graded question specifies none.
  • Retain nonpositive integer valuation outputs and monotonicity on feasible bundles; impose no empty-bundle normalization, which the discrete definition does not specify.
  • Represent valuations by Boolean evaluation circuits with promised monotonicity to make P versus NP-hardness a finite-input question; include circuit descriptions in L.
  • Require EF1 for all ordered positive-support pairs, including different bundles assigned to the same type, and retain the discrete exemption for empty own bundles.
  • Interpret adding residual supply as adding another copy: an existing copy of the same kind blocks addition through its overlapping interval.
  • Select the existence decision problem from the graded question's decision-or-computation alternatives and use rational allocation masses.

The unrestricted classification remains unresolved. I prove two new hardness results that isolate an obstacle to completing it:

1. Support conditions and polynomial certificates

For an allocation $x$, define
$$ K(x)=\{(\theta,B):x_{\theta,B}>0\}, \qquad \mathcal G(x)=\{B:(\theta,B)\in K(x)\text{ for some }\theta\}. $$
For a nonempty feasible bundle $B$, define
$$ h_\theta(B)=\max_{j\in B}v_\theta(B\setminus\{j\}). $$

Pairwise EF1 is equivalent to
$$ h_\theta(B)\geq v_\theta(C) $$
for every supported pair $(\theta,B)$ with $B\neq\emptyset$ and every $C\in\mathcal G(x)$. Indeed, the maximum defining $h_\theta(B)$ is attained, so this inequality is exactly the existence of a permissible deletion for the comparison with $C$.

Consequently, removing support pairs cannot introduce an EF1 violation.

Proposition 1: sparse rational certificates

Every yes-instance has a valid allocation with at most $\tau+m$ positive type–bundle pairs. Each positive mass has binary encoding length
$$ O\bigl(L+(\tau+m)\log(\tau+m+1)\bigr). $$

Proof. Start with any valid allocation $x$, and let
$$ K=K(x), \qquad S=\{j:u_j(x)=q_j\}. $$
Consider the real polytope $P$ with coordinates indexed by $K$ and constraints
$$ y_{\theta,B}\geq0, \qquad \sum_{B:(\theta,B)\in K}y_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta, $$
together with
$$ u_j(y)=q_j\quad(j\in S), \qquad u_j(y)\leq q_j\quad(j\notin S). $$

This polytope contains $x$. Every coordinate is bounded above by its type mass, so $P$ is bounded and has a vertex $y$.

Every real point of $P$ satisfies the mass and supply constraints. Its support is contained in $K$, so it satisfies the EF1 inequalities. Moreover, if $u_j(y)<q_j$, then $j\notin S$, and therefore $u_j(x)<q_j$. Maximality of $x$ says that every bundle occurring in $K$ blocks $j$. Every retained bundle also blocks $j$.

These observations concern real points satisfying the constraints. We next establish that the selected vertex is rational, as required for an allocation.

Let $k$ be the number of positive coordinates of $y$. The active type-mass and supply rows, restricted to those coordinates, have rank $k$. Otherwise, a nonzero direction preserving these rows would exist. Sufficiently small perturbations in both directions would preserve positivity and all inactive inequalities; zero coordinates would remain zero. This would contradict that $y$ is a vertex.

There are at most $\tau+m$ such rows. Hence
$$ k\leq\tau+m. $$
Selecting $k$ independent active rows gives
$$ Ay^+=b, $$
where $A$ is a nonsingular $k$-by-$k$ matrix with entries in $\{0,1\}$. Every entry of $b$ is an input type mass or a tight supply. A tight supply is at most $1$, because total worker mass is $1$.

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of all input masses and supplies. Then $\log_2D=O(L)$, and $Db$ is an integer vector with entries between $0$ and $D$. Cramer’s rule gives
$$ y_i^+=\frac{\det A_i}{D\det A}, $$
where $A_i$ replaces column $i$ of $A$ by $Db$. Hadamard’s inequality yields
$$ |\det A|\leq k^{k/2}, \qquad |\det A_i|\leq Dk^{k/2}. $$
Thus the vertex is rational and has the asserted encoding bound. The preceding EF1 and maximality arguments now show that it is a valid rational allocation. $\square$

A verifier checks bundle feasibility, exact mass and supply constraints, every ordered EF1 comparison, and every maximality obligation. The support and rational encoding bounds make these checks polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

Therefore, the original decision problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$ on the promised inputs.

The same certificate argument applies if we additionally restrict which support pairs are allowed. It also applies if designated chores must be saturated: their saturation equalities are preserved in $P$.

2. An exact dichotomy for valuations in $\{0,-1\}$

For a valuation $v$ taking only the values $0$ and $-1$, call a bundle safe if it is empty or has a deletion of value $0$:
$$ B=\emptyset \quad\text{or}\quad v(B\setminus\{j\})=0\text{ for some }j\in B. $$

Proposition 2: two possible EF1 support regimes

For one worker type with such a valuation, an EF1 and maximal allocation exists if and only if a maximal allocation exists whose supported bundles are either:

  • all of value $-1$; or
  • all safe.

Proof. Suppose an EF1 allocation uses some bundle $C$ with $v(C)=0$. Every supported nonempty $B$ must satisfy EF1 against $C$, so some deletion has value at least $0$. Since valuations are nonpositive, that deletion has value exactly $0$. Thus every supported bundle is safe.

If no supported bundle has value $0$, all supported bundles have value $-1$.

Conversely, safe bundles satisfy EF1 against every comparison bundle because their specified deletion has value $0$. If all supported bundles have value $-1$, monotonicity gives
$$ v(B\setminus\{j\})\geq v(B)=-1=v(C) $$
for every supported nonempty $B$, every $j\in B$, and every supported $C$. Thus EF1 holds in that regime as well. $\square$

This dichotomy explains why a hardness proof for the safe regime alone need not establish hardness of the original problem.

3. A SAT construction compatible with interval conflicts

Let $F$ be a $3$-CNF formula on variables $z_1,\ldots,z_n$, where $n\geq1$ and every clause is nonempty. Satisfiability remains $\mathrm{NP}$-complete under these standard restrictions.

Construct one worker type of mass $1$.

For each copy index $r\in\{1,2\}$, variable index $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and truth value $b\in\{0,1\}$, introduce a chore
$$ j_{r,i,b}. $$
The two chores belonging to $(r,i)$ have the identical interval
$$ [2t,2t+1), \qquad t=(r-1)n+i-1. $$
Different slots have disjoint intervals. Give every such chore supply $1$.

Introduce one additional chore $a$, with interval
$$ [0,4n) $$
and supply
$$ q_a=\frac12. $$
Its interval overlaps every literal-chore interval.

There are $4n+1$ chore kinds. All endpoints and supplies have polynomial binary encoding length.

3.1 The valuation circuit

For each clause $D$ and copy $r$, its falsifying pattern consists of:

  • $j_{r,i,0}$ for a positive literal $z_i$ in $D$;
  • $j_{r,i,1}$ for a negative literal $\neg z_i$ in $D$.

Define
$$ P_F(B)=1 $$
if $B$ contains the entire falsifying pattern of at least one clause in at least one copy. Otherwise define $P_F(B)=0$. Set
$$ v(B)=-P_F(B). $$

An OR of the clause-pattern AND gates computes $P_F$, so the circuit has polynomial size. Each pattern is a conjunction of positive incidence bits. Consequently,
$$ B\subseteq C \quad\Longrightarrow\quad P_F(B)\leq P_F(C), $$
and hence $v(B)\geq v(C)$. The monotonicity promise is therefore satisfied.

Because every clause is nonempty,
$$ v(\emptyset)=v(\{a\})=0. $$

3.2 Maximality forces two bundle forms

Call a bundle full if it contains exactly one literal chore from every one of the $2n$ slots. Such a bundle specifies two complete truth assignments.

Lemma 3. Every positive bundle in a maximal allocation is either $\{a\}$ or a full bundle. Conversely, every feasible allocation supported on these bundle forms is maximal.

Proof. A feasible bundle containing $a$ contains no other chore, because $a$ overlaps every other interval.

Suppose a supported bundle $B$ contains no $a$ and omits both chores of some slot $(r,i)$. Write $\varepsilon=x_B>0$. Since workers in this bundle receive neither chore of that slot,
$$ u_{j_{r,i,b}}\leq1-\varepsilon<1=q_{j_{r,i,b}} $$
for each $b\in\{0,1\}$.

Maximality therefore requires $B$ to block these chores. But, without $a$, only chores in that same slot overlap them. None is present, a contradiction. Thus $B$ contains a chore from every slot, and interval feasibility permits exactly one per slot.

For the converse, each full bundle blocks both literal chores in every slot and also blocks $a$. The bundle $\{a\}$ blocks every chore, including itself under the stated same-kind convention. Hence every supported bundle blocks every undersupplied chore. $\square$

3.3 Safe full bundles encode satisfiability

Lemma 4. A safe full bundle exists if and only if $F$ is satisfiable.

Proof. Suppose a full bundle $B$ is safe. There is a chore $j$ with
$$ v(B\setminus\{j\})=0. $$
The deleted chore belongs to one copy. The other copy remains a complete assignment. Value $0$ means that this remaining copy contains no falsifying clause pattern. Its assignment therefore satisfies $F$.

Conversely, let $\alpha$ satisfy $F$. Choose $\alpha$ in both copies to obtain a full bundle $B_\alpha$. Neither copy falsifies a clause, so
$$ v(B_\alpha)=0. $$
Monotonicity and nonpositivity imply that every deletion also has value $0$. Thus $B_\alpha$ is safe. $\square$

The second copy is essential to soundness: after one deletion, a complete assignment still remains.

4. Two strengthened problems are NP-complete

Theorem 5: requiring safe support

Consider the original problem with the additional requirement that every positive bundle be safe, for valuations restricted to $\{0,-1\}$. This strengthened problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete, even with one type and supplies in $\{1/2,1\}$.

Proof. Membership in $\mathrm{NP}$ follows from Proposition 1: restricting support to safe bundles is preserved by taking a subset of the support, and safeness is verified by circuit evaluations on one-chore deletions.

Use the construction above. Lemma 3 restricts every maximal allocation to $\{a\}$ and full bundles. The supply bound $u_a\leq1/2$ forces positive total mass on full bundles. If every supported bundle is safe, Lemma 4 implies that $F$ is satisfiable.

If $F$ is satisfiable, assign
$$ x_{\{a\}}=\frac12, \qquad x_{B_\alpha}=\frac12. $$
Both bundles are safe, supplies are respected, and Lemma 3 gives maximality. This is a polynomial reduction from $3$-SAT. $\square$

Theorem 6: requiring one designated chore to be saturated

Consider the original problem with the additional requirement
$$ u_a=q_a $$
for one designated chore $a$. This strengthened problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete under the same restrictions.

Proof. Membership in $\mathrm{NP}$ follows from Proposition 1, because its vertex construction preserves every initially saturated chore.

In the constructed instance, saturation of $a$ means that $\{a\}$ receives mass $1/2$. Therefore a value-$0$ bundle is used. EF1 against $\{a\}$ forces every supported full bundle to be safe. Full bundles have total mass $1/2$, so Lemma 4 implies satisfiability.

For a satisfying formula, the allocation in Theorem 5 is EF1: every supported nonempty bundle has a zero-valued deletion, and every comparison bundle has value $0$. It also saturates $a$ and is maximal. $\square$

These proofs explicitly permit arbitrary rational splitting. Soundness follows from the existence of any positive safe full bundle, rather than from an assumption that the type receives a single bundle.

5. Why the original problem remains open

The same SAT instances are always yes-instances of the original problem.

Choose any full bundle $B$, without regard to $F$, and allocate all worker mass to it:
$$ x_B=1. $$
Its literal-chore usages are at most $1$, and its anchor usage is $0$. Lemma 3 proves maximality. There is only one globally used bundle, so the only EF1 comparison is against itself. Monotonicity gives, for every $j\in B$,
$$ v(B\setminus\{j\})\geq v(B). $$
Thus EF1 holds.

In particular, if $F$ is unsatisfiable, this allocation uses only value-$-1$ bundles and exploits the first regime of Proposition 2.

The construction also establishes a precise limitation of the threshold-based approach in the notes. Fixing the comparison threshold at $0$ and requiring the saturated set to contain $a$ produces an implicitly specified feasibility problem that is already $\mathrm{NP}$-hard. Explicit rational linear programming remains polynomial-time solvable; the difficulty here is determining which circuit-described bundles may serve as variables.

What remains unproved is a way to exclude the alternative support regime within the original requirements, or a polynomial-time algorithm that handles both regimes and general valuations. The auxiliary hardness results cannot supply that missing step.
