The continuous mirror question, as graded

Determine the least \(\alpha\) for which a deterministic mechanism family \(M_n\), individually truthful against edge hiding at every scale, guarantees \(W(M_n(I_n))\geq \mathrm{OPT}(I_n)/\alpha-o(n)\) across fixed-support templates; does the paper's \(2\)-barrier persist?

A society is specified by its support: \(\tau\) distinct worker types \(\theta=(N_\theta,b_\theta)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), where \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=|J|\geq1\) task classes, with \(\varnothing\neq N_\theta\subseteq J\), public capacities \(b_\theta\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and public binary-rational supplies \(s_j>0\) and common values \(q_j>0\). Let \(L\) be the template's total encoding length; express running times in \(m,\tau,L,n\) and the expanded input/output size.

For every positive integer \(n\) making all \(n\mu_\theta\) and \(ns_j\) integral, \(I_n\) has \(n\mu_\theta\) labeled workers of type \(\theta\) and \(ns_j\) distinct tasks of class \(j\). Each such worker is eligible for every copy of every class in \(N_\theta\). An allocation assigns each task at most once and at most \(b_\theta\) tasks to each worker. Worker utility is the sum of assigned task values; welfare \(W\) is total utility, and \(\mathrm{OPT}(I_n)\) is maximum feasible welfare.

The family and constant are universal; the remainder may depend on the template.

A deterministic asymptotic factor below $2$

The second alternative holds. There is a universal deterministic mechanism family, truthful on every finite report profile, that achieves
$$ \alpha=\frac{e}{e-1}<2 $$
with a template-dependent constant additive loss at all sufficiently large admissible scales. Consequently,
$$ \boxed{\alpha_*\leq\frac{e}{e-1}<2.} $$

This settles the proposed dichotomy. The exact value of $\alpha_*$ remains undetermined.

The construction has very large running time. Its key feature is that each worker receives a menu determined entirely by the other workers’ reports. We select these menus jointly so that their outputs are feasible on every report profile.

1. A randomized benchmark

Fix public data consisting of $n$ labeled workers, capacities $b_i$, and task classes $J$, with $T_j$ task copies of class $j$ and value $q_j>0$.

Fix a public task order that sorts tasks by decreasing value and resolves ties deterministically. For a worker permutation $\pi$, let $\mathrm{SD}_\pi$ process workers in that order, giving each worker its highest-valued available reported tasks, up to its capacity.

We use the following established theorem.

Ranking theorem. In online bipartite matching with arbitrary positive integer capacities on the offline vertices, choose a uniformly random permutation of those vertices. Match each arriving online vertex to its first eligible vertex in that permutation with remaining capacity. For every fixed graph and arrival order, the expected matching cardinality is at least $1-1/e$ times the maximum feasible cardinality. This is [Theorem 3 of Albers and Schubert, *Optimal Algorithms for Online b-Matching with Variable Vertex Capacities*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-024-01282-9).

Here the offline vertices are workers and the arriving vertices are tasks. Thus all hypotheses, including variable worker capacities, hold.

Write
$$ c=1-\frac1e. $$

Lemma 1. For every finite report profile $P$,
$$ \mathbb E_\pi W(\mathrm{SD}_\pi(P))\geq c\,\mathrm{OPT}(P). $$

Proof. Processing tasks in the fixed task order and assigning each to its first available eligible worker in $\pi$ produces the same allocation as $\mathrm{SD}_\pi$.

To verify this, the first worker in $\pi$ receives exactly its first $b_i$ eligible tasks in the task order, or all its eligible tasks if fewer exist. These are precisely its serial-dictatorship choices. Remove that worker and those tasks; induction proves the assertion for the remaining workers.

Let the distinct task values be
$$ v_1>\cdots>v_d>0, \qquad v_{d+1}=0. $$
For an allocation $A$, let $C_h(A)$ count its assigned tasks having value at least $v_h$. Then
$$ W(A)=\sum_{h=1}^d(v_h-v_{h+1})C_h(A). $$

Let $M_h$ be the maximum feasible cardinality using only tasks of value at least $v_h$. The task-order algorithm’s decisions on this prefix do not depend on later tasks. Applying the Ranking theorem to each prefix gives
$$ \mathbb E_\pi C_h(\mathrm{SD}_\pi(P))\geq cM_h. $$
If $O$ is a welfare-optimal allocation, then $C_h(O)\leq M_h$. Therefore
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E_\pi W(\mathrm{SD}_\pi(P)) &\geq c\sum_{h=1}^d(v_h-v_{h+1})M_h\\ &\geq c\sum_{h=1}^d(v_h-v_{h+1})C_h(O)\\ &=c\,\mathrm{OPT}(P). \end{aligned} $$
This proves the lemma. $\square$

The randomized benchmark is used only to construct a rational fractional solution. The eventual mechanism makes no random choices.

2. A deterministic rounding lemma

We need to round choices while controlling many constraints simultaneously.

Lemma 2. Partition finitely many variables into groups. Suppose $x_k\in[0,1]$ and
$$ \sum_{k\in G}x_k=1 $$
for every group $G$. Let $A=(a_{\ell k})$ be a finite rational matrix satisfying
$$ \sum_\ell |a_{\ell k}|\leq\Delta $$
for every column $k$, where $\Delta>0$.

There is a deterministically computable vector $y_k\in\{0,1\}$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{k\in G}y_k=1 $$
for every group and
$$ \left|\sum_k a_{\ell k}(y_k-x_k)\right|\leq2\Delta $$
for every row $\ell$.

Proof. Keep variables already equal to $0$ or $1$ fixed. Let $F$ be the remaining fractional variables.

Preserve every group-sum equation. Retain a numerical row equation only while
$$ \sum_{k\in F}|a_{\ell k}|>2\Delta; $$
otherwise discard that row equation permanently.

Every group containing fractional variables contains at least two of them: its fractional variables sum to $1$, because any fixed variables in that group are $0$. Hence there are at most $|F|/2$ relevant group equations.

The number of retained numerical equations is strictly less than $|F|/2$, since
$$ \sum_{\ell\text{ retained}}\sum_{k\in F}|a_{\ell k}| \leq |F|\Delta. $$
Thus fewer than $|F|$ equations constrain the $|F|$ fractional variables. A nonzero nullspace direction exists. Move along a deterministically selected rational direction until a variable reaches $0$ or $1$.

Repeat. At least one variable becomes integral per iteration, so the process terminates. Group sums remain exactly $1$.

A retained row has zero error. When a row is discarded, its current value still equals its initial value, and its remaining coefficient sum is at most $2\Delta$. Subsequent changes in each remaining variable have absolute value at most $1$. Its final error is therefore at most $2\Delta$. $\square$

This proof also gives a finite rational algorithm.

3. Profiles with small denominators

First work on the finite domain
$$ \mathcal X=(2^J)^n. $$
A coordinate $P_i\subseteq J$ means that worker $i$ reports every copy of exactly those classes. We allow $P_i=\varnothing$ internally.

Public capacities remain fixed throughout this domain.

For $n\geq16$, put
$$ h(n)=\lfloor n^{1/4}\rfloor. $$
For $P\in\mathcal X$, define its report-type counts
$$ a_{B,b}(P) = |\{i:P_i=B,\ b_i=b\}|. $$
Define their common reduced denominator by
$$ d(P)= \frac{n}{\gcd\{a_{B,b}(P):(B,b)\}}, $$
where zero counts can be omitted from the gcd.

Call $P$ a centre if
$$ d(P)\leq h(n). $$
For $n<16$, take the centre set to be empty.

This definition uses only hypothetical report profiles and public capacities. It does not use true eligibility sets or masses.

Lemma 3. For a fixed worker $i$ and fixed reports $R$ of all other workers, at most one report $B$ completes $(B,R)$ to a centre.

Proof. Each coordinate of a centre’s normalized count vector is a rational number whose denominator is at most $h(n)$. Two distinct such rationals differ by at least $1/h(n)^2$.

Consequently, distinct centre count vectors differ in some unnormalized coordinate by at least
$$ \frac{n}{h(n)^2}\geq\sqrt n\geq4. $$
Changing one worker’s report changes every count coordinate by at most $1$. Thus two centres agreeing on all other workers must have the same count vector. That vector, together with the other workers’ reports and worker $i$’s public capacity, determines $i$’s report uniquely. $\square$

A context is a pair
$$ a=(i,R) $$
for which some centre $P$ satisfies $P_{-i}=R$. By Lemma 3, that centre is unique; denote it by $P^a$.

Crucially, whether worker $i$ has a context depends only on the other workers’ reports.

4. Menus for each context

For each context $a=(i,R)$ and worker permutation $\pi$, construct a menu
$$ F_a^\pi:2^J\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{J}. $$

Run serial dictatorship on the workers preceding $i$ in $\pi$, using their reports from $R$. Let $t_j$ be the number of class-$j$ copies remaining before $i$ is processed.

For a report $B\subseteq J$, the vector $F_a^\pi(B)$ specifies the highest-valued bundle available from these remaining copies in classes $B$, up to capacity $b_i$, using the fixed task order.

Also include the zero menu
$$ F_a^0(B)=0. $$

Every menu respects the reporting worker’s classes and capacity. Its utility is monotone under inclusion:
$$ B'\subseteq B \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sum_jq_jF_a^\pi(B')_j \leq \sum_jq_jF_a^\pi(B)_j. $$
Indeed, the remaining supply vector is fixed independently of $B$, and passing from $B$ to $B'$ restricts the available bundles.

The following identity provides the joint feasibility needed below.

Lemma 4. If $Q_{-i}=R$, then
$$ F_a^\pi(Q_i) = \text{the class-count vector assigned to }i \text{ by }\mathrm{SD}_\pi(Q). $$

Proof. Every worker preceding $i$ has the same report in the menu calculation and in $Q$. Their decisions are independent of $i$’s report. Thus the remaining supply before $i$ is identical, and the two calculations make the same choice for $i$. $\square$

5. Selecting all menus jointly

Let
$$ T=\sum_jT_j, \qquad B_{\max}=\max_i\min\{b_i,T\}, \qquad q_{\max}=\max_jq_j. $$
Set
$$ \Delta=(2^m+1)B_{\max}, \qquad T_{\min}=\min_jT_j, \qquad \varepsilon=\min\left\{1,\frac{2\Delta}{T_{\min}}\right\}. $$

If there are no centres, or if $\varepsilon=1$, select the zero menu in every context. Otherwise proceed as follows.

For every context $a$, create one group of choice variables indexed by the zero menu and all $n!$ permutations. Initially set
$$ x_{a,0}=\varepsilon, \qquad x_{a,\pi}=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{n!}. $$

Define a rational matrix with the following rows.

Task-supply rows

For every $Q\in\mathcal X$ and $j\in J$, include the expression
$$ D_{Q,j}(x) = \sum_{\substack{a=(i,R)\\R=Q_{-i}}} \sum_\pi x_{a,\pi}F_a^\pi(Q_i)_j. $$
Zero-menu contributions vanish. By Lemma 3, at most one context contributes for each worker.

For a fixed $\pi$, Lemma 4 identifies these menu outputs with a subset of the workers’ outputs in $\mathrm{SD}_\pi(Q)$. Hence
$$ D_{Q,j}(x)\leq(1-\varepsilon)T_j. $$

Welfare rows

For every centre $P$, include
$$ U_P(x) = \sum_i\sum_\pi x_{(i,P_{-i}),\pi} \frac{\sum_jq_jF_{(i,P_{-i})}^\pi(P_i)_j}{q_{\max}}. $$
Every worker has a context at a centre. Lemma 4 therefore gives
$$ U_P(x) = \frac{1-\varepsilon}{q_{\max}} \mathbb E_\pi W(\mathrm{SD}_\pi(P)). $$

Column bound and rounding

Fix a column corresponding to one menu in context $a=(i,R)$.

It contributes to task-supply rows only for profiles with $Q_{-i}=R$. There are exactly $2^m$ such profiles. At each, its total contribution over all classes is at most $B_{\max}$.

It contributes to exactly one welfare row, that of $P^a$, with coefficient at most $B_{\max}$.

Thus its total absolute coefficient sum is at most
$$ 2^mB_{\max}+B_{\max}=\Delta. $$

Apply Lemma 2, fixing all choices deterministically using public label orders. This selects one menu $F_a$ per context.

For every profile $Q$ and class $j$, the selected menus demand at most
$$ (1-\varepsilon)T_j+2\Delta\leq T_j, $$
because $\varepsilon T_j\geq2\Delta$.

For every centre $P$, their welfare satisfies
$$ W(P)\geq (1-\varepsilon)\mathbb E_\pi W(\mathrm{SD}_\pi(P)) -2\Delta q_{\max}. $$

When $\varepsilon=1$, selecting all zero menus satisfies this last inequality as well. We have therefore proved:

Lemma 5. The selected menus are jointly feasible on every profile in $\mathcal X$, and every centre satisfies
$$ W(P)\geq(1-\varepsilon)c\,\mathrm{OPT}(P)-2\Delta q_{\max}. $$

All menu selections depend only on public data. They are computed before evaluating the actual reports.

6. The mechanism and exact truthfulness

We now define the mechanism on arbitrary individual-edge reports.

For a worker report $S_i$ of task copies, let
$$ B_i(S_i) = \{j:\text{every copy of class }j\text{ belongs to }S_i\}. $$
This projection may be empty even when $S_i$ is nonempty. It satisfies
$$ S_i'\subseteq S_i \quad\Longrightarrow\quad B_i(S_i')\subseteq B_i(S_i). $$

Given actual reports, form their projected profile $Q\in\mathcal X$.

For each worker $i$:

  • If $(i,Q_{-i})$ is a context, request the class-count bundle $F_{(i,Q_{-i})}(Q_i)$.
  • Otherwise request the zero bundle.

Lemma 5 ensures that the total requested number of class-$j$ copies is at most $T_j$. Assign distinct copies to fulfill all requested counts, using fixed public worker and task orders.

Every assigned copy belongs to a class whose entire set of copies was reported by its recipient. Thus assignments respect reported edges. Menus respect individual capacities.

Proposition 6. This mechanism is truthful on every finite report profile, including reports that split classes.

Proof. Fix worker $i$ and all other individual-edge reports. Their projected reports, and therefore $Q_{-i}$, are fixed.

If $(i,Q_{-i})$ is not a context, worker $i$ receives zero utility under every report.

If it is a context, worker $i$ faces one fixed selected menu $F_{(i,Q_{-i})}$. Hiding edges can only shrink its projected class set. Every selected menu has utility monotone under this inclusion.

Assigning particular copies does not change utility, because values are common within each class. Hence hiding edges cannot increase worker $i$’s utility. $\square$

The zero mechanisms used at small scales are also truthful. This defines a single universal deterministic family.

7. Guarantee for every fixed-support template

Fix any template $H$ from the question. Write
$$ b_H=\max_\theta b_\theta, \qquad s_{\min}=\min_js_j, \qquad S_q=\sum_js_jq_j, \qquad \Delta_H=(2^m+1)b_H. $$

Choose a positive integer $D$ such that every $D\mu_\theta$ is integral.

At any admissible scale, the truthful normalized report-type count vector is exactly the template mass vector, regardless of labeling. Its reduced denominator is at most $D$. Therefore every truthful labeling is a centre once
$$ n\geq N_H:=\max\{16,D^4\}. $$

At such a scale,
$$ T_{\min}=ns_{\min}, \qquad \Delta\leq\Delta_H, \qquad \varepsilon\leq\frac{2\Delta_H}{ns_{\min}}. $$
Also,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}(I_n)\leq nS_q, $$
because $nS_q$ is the total value of all supplied tasks.

Lemma 5 gives, for every labeling,
$$ \begin{aligned} W(M_n(I_n)) &\geq c\,\mathrm{OPT}(I_n) -\varepsilon c\,\mathrm{OPT}(I_n) -2\Delta q_{\max}\\ &\geq c\,\mathrm{OPT}(I_n)-C_H, \end{aligned} $$
where the finite template-dependent constant is
$$ C_H= \frac{2c\Delta_HS_q}{s_{\min}} +2\Delta_Hq_{\max}. $$

For example, define the remainder on admissible scales by
$$ r_H(n) = C_H+c\,nS_q\,\mathbf 1_{\{n<N_H\}}. $$
For small scales, nonnegative welfare and $\mathrm{OPT}(I_n)\leq nS_q$ establish the required guarantee. For all larger scales, the preceding constant-loss bound establishes it.

Finally,
$$ \frac{r_H(n)}n\longrightarrow0, $$
and $c=1/\alpha$ for $\alpha=e/(e-1)$.

This proves the claimed universal asymptotic factor below $2$.
