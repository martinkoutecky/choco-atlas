The continuous mirror question, as graded

For each rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), determine the sharp supremum and infimum of \(C_\mu(a)/\min_{b\in F}C_\mu(b)\) over all finite-support line societies \(\mu\) with strategic mass \(\alpha\) and positive optimal cost, where \(a\) ranges over outcomes of strong equilibria under mass-weighted \(GM^*\). Decide whether the sharp upper envelope is \((1+\alpha)/(1-\alpha)\) for \(\alpha\le 1/2\) and \(3\) for \(\alpha>1/2\), and give matching witness societies or witness families.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Type \(j\) specifies a true location \(z_j\in\mathbb Q\) and whether it is truthful or strategic. Masses \(w_j>0\) sum to \(1\), with strategic mass \(\alpha\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\).
The facility set \(F\subset\mathbb Q\) is finite and nonempty, with \(m=|F|\). Distances are absolute differences, and individual cost at facility \(b\) is \(|z_j-b|\). Write \(C_\mu(b)=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}w_j|z_j-b|\), and require \(\min_{b\in F}C_\mu(b)>0\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and any additional parameters.
Each type is divisible: represent its members by an interval \([0,w_j]\) with Lebesgue measure. A pure report profile consists of measurable maps \(r_j:[0,w_j]\to\mathbb R\) with finite first moments; truthful members report \(r_j(u)=z_j\), while strategic members may report any real location.
Mass-weighted \(GM^*\) chooses the leftmost minimizer in \(F\) of \(\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\int_0^{w_j}|r_j(u)-b|\,du\). A strong equilibrium admits no measurable coalition of strategic members of positive total mass whose joint change of reports strictly reduces individual cost for almost every coalition member; members outside the coalition keep their reports.
For each \(\alpha\), let \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) contain \(C_\mu(a)/\min_{b\in F}C_\mu(b)\) over all such societies and facility sets, where \(a\) is an outcome of a strong equilibrium.
What are the sharp values of \(\sup\mathcal R_\alpha\) and \(\inf\mathcal R_\alpha\) for every rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), with matching witness societies or limiting families?
Is \(\sup\mathcal R_\alpha=(1+\alpha)/(1-\alpha)\) for \(\alpha\leq 1/2\), and \(\sup\mathcal R_\alpha=3\) for \(\alpha>1/2\)?
For finite societies of \(n\) clients with \(k\) strategic clients on a line, strong equilibria exist, and the strong prices of anarchy and stability have asymptotically tight upper bounds \((n+k)/(n-k)\) for \(k\leq n/2\) and \(3\) otherwise. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16829v1)
The literature search located no result settling these extrema for the divisible-mass model defined here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total mass to one, so strategic mass α corresponds to the discrete fraction k/n; types include both true location and strategic status.
  • Chose binary rational true locations and facility coordinates to make the input encoding and bit length explicit; retained arbitrary real reports, as the discrete definition permits any location on the line.
  • Interpreted masses as divisible populations, allowing measurable coalitions containing arbitrary portions of types and allowing different members of a type to report differently.
  • Required report profiles to have finite first moments so perceived costs are finite; coalitional improvement is strict almost everywhere on a coalition of positive mass.
  • Retained a finite nonempty facility set and leftmost tie-breaking from the discrete line model.
  • Interpreted the graded infimum literally as the infimum over all societies and all strong-equilibrium outcomes, rather than the worst-case-over-societies strong price of stability.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Sharp extrema.

For every rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\),
\[ \inf\mathcal R_\alpha=1,\qquad \sup\mathcal R_\alpha= \begin{cases} \dfrac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha},&0\leq\alpha\leq \tfrac12,\\[4pt] 3,&\tfrac12<\alpha\leq1. \end{cases} \]
Both extrema are attained. In particular, the proposed supremum is correct.

Here and below, “binary rational” means a rational represented by a binary numerator and denominator, consistently with the question’s quantification over all rational \(\alpha\).

The bounds coincide with the finite-client bounds in the [supplied paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16829v1). We prove them directly for measurable coalitions; no approximation by finite populations is needed.

1. Notation and an outcome-movement lemma.

Represent the population by the disjoint union \(\Omega\) of the type intervals, with total measure \(1\). Write \(S\subseteq\Omega\) for the strategic population, so its measure is \(\alpha\). For a report profile \(r\), define its reported objective by
\[ H(c)=\int_\Omega |r(u)-c|\,du. \]
The finite-first-moment assumption makes \(H\) finite everywhere. It is convex because each integrand is convex.

Suppose the current winner is \(a\). Consequently,
\[ H(c)\geq H(a)\quad(c\in F), \qquad H(c)>H(a)\quad(c\in F,\ c<a). \]
The second inequality follows from leftmost tie-breaking.

Lemma. If a measurable subset of the population changes all its reports to a facility \(b\), the new winner belongs to the closed interval with endpoints \(a,b\).

Proof. Let \(\widetilde H\) be the new objective. The triangle inequality gives
\[ |x-c|-|x-d|\leq |c-d|. \]
Therefore, whenever
\[ |b-c|-|b-d|=|c-d|, \]
replacing reports by \(b\) yields
\[ \widetilde H(c)-\widetilde H(d)\geq H(c)-H(d). \tag{1} \]
Indeed, each changed member’s contribution to this difference becomes its largest possible value.

First suppose \(a<b\).

  • If \(c<a\), apply (1) with \(d=a\). Then \(\widetilde H(c)-\widetilde H(a)\geq H(c)-H(a)>0\), excluding \(c\).
  • If \(c>b\), convexity gives

\[ H(c)-H(b)\geq \frac{c-b}{b-a}\bigl(H(b)-H(a)\bigr)\geq0. \]
Apply (1) with \(d=b\). Thus \(\widetilde H(c)\geq\widetilde H(b)\); since \(b<c\), tie-breaking also excludes \(c\).

Now suppose \(b<a\).

  • If \(c>a\), (1) with \(d=a\) gives \(\widetilde H(c)\geq\widetilde H(a)\), and tie-breaking excludes \(c\).
  • If \(c<b\), we have \(H(b)>H(a)\). Convexity gives

\[ H(c)-H(b)\geq \frac{b-c}{a-b}\bigl(H(b)-H(a)\bigr)>0. \]
Applying (1) with \(d=b\) excludes \(c\).

These arguments exclude every facility outside the stated interval. \(\square\)

Corollary. Suppose every member of a positive-mass coalition strictly prefers \(b\) to \(a\). If that coalition reports \(b\), either the winner remains \(a\), or almost every coalition member strictly improves.

To verify this, suppose \(a<b\). Strict preference means \(z(u)>(a+b)/2\). Every \(c\in(a,b]\) satisfies \(z(u)>(a+c)/2\), hence \(|z(u)-c|<|z(u)-a|\). For \(b<a\), the corresponding inequalities are \(z(u)<(a+b)/2\leq(a+c)/2\) for \(c\in[b,a)\). The lemma now proves the corollary.

2. Universal bounds for strong equilibria.

Fix any strong-equilibrium profile with winner \(a\), and choose a true-cost minimizer \(b\in F\). Put \(A=C_\mu(a)\), \(B=C_\mu(b)>0\), and \(d=|a-b|\). If \(a=b\), the ratio is \(1\), so assume otherwise.

Partition the strategic population into
\[ P=\{u\in S:|z(u)-b|<|z(u)-a|\}, \qquad Q=S\setminus P, \]
with masses \(p\) and \(q=\alpha-p\), respectively.

Change every report in \(P\) to \(b\). If \(p>0\), the corollary and strong equilibrium imply that the winner remains \(a\). If \(p=0\), the profile is unchanged. Thus, in either case, the modified reported objective satisfies
\[ \widetilde H(a)\leq\widetilde H(b). \]

Let \(T(c)\) denote the truthful population’s contribution to the true cost. Expanding this comparison gives
\[ T(a)+pd+\int_Q|r(u)-a|\,du \leq T(b)+\int_Q|r(u)-b|\,du. \]
Since \(|r(u)-b|-|r(u)-a|\leq d\),
\[ T(a)-T(b)\leq(q-p)d. \tag{2} \]

Members of \(P\) have true-cost difference at most \(d\); members of \(Q\) have true-cost difference at most \(0\). Adding their contributions to (2) proves
\[ A-B\leq(q-p)d+pd=qd. \tag{3} \]

There are two consequences.

First, integrating the triangle inequality \(d\leq|z(u)-a|+|z(u)-b|\) over the entire unit-mass population gives \(d\leq A+B\). Hence (3) implies
\[ (1-q)A\leq(1+q)B. \]
For \(\alpha<1\), we have \(q\leq\alpha<1\), so
\[ \frac AB\leq\frac{1+q}{1-q}\leq\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}. \tag{4} \]

Second, for every member of \(Q\), the definition of \(Q\) gives \(|z(u)-a|\leq|z(u)-b|\). Therefore,
\[ d\leq|z(u)-a|+|z(u)-b|\leq2|z(u)-b|. \]
Integrating over \(Q\) yields \(qd\leq2\int_Q|z(u)-b|\,du\leq2B\). Equation (3) consequently gives
\[ A\leq3B. \tag{5} \]
This includes \(\alpha=1\).

Combining (4) and (5) proves the claimed upper bounds, since \((1+\alpha)/(1-\alpha)\leq3\) exactly when \(\alpha\leq1/2\).

3. Exact witnesses for the supremum.

All witnesses use the two facilities \(F=\{0,1\}\). Zero-mass types in the descriptions are omitted.

For \(0\leq\alpha\leq1/2\), take:

  • truthful mass \(1-\alpha\) at \(t=1/[2(1-\alpha)]\);
  • strategic mass \(\alpha\) at \(1/2\), all reporting \(0\).

Because \(t\in[1/2,1]\), the reported costs are
\[ H(0)=(1-\alpha)t=\frac12, \qquad H(1)=(1-\alpha)(1-t)+\alpha=\frac12. \]
The winner is \(0\).

Every strategic member has true cost \(1/2\) at either facility. Thus no positive-mass strategic coalition can strictly improve, and this is a strong equilibrium. Its true costs are
\[ C_\mu(0)=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}, \qquad C_\mu(1)=\frac{1-\alpha}{2}>0. \]
The ratio is exactly \((1+\alpha)/(1-\alpha)\).

For \(1/2<\alpha\leq1\), take:

  • strategic mass \(1/2\) at \(1/2\), reporting \(0\);
  • strategic mass \(\alpha-1/2\) at \(1\), reporting \(1\);
  • truthful mass \(1-\alpha\) at \(1\).

The reported distribution has mass \(1/2\) at each facility, so the winner is again \(0\).

A strictly improving coalition must consist, up to a null set, entirely of strategic members at \(1\): members at \(1/2\) are indifferent. But members at \(1\) already report \(1\), which maximizes their contribution to
\[ H(0)-H(1), \]
because \(|x|-|x-1|\leq1\), with equality at \(x=1\). Their deviations therefore cannot make this difference positive. Facility \(1\) cannot win, since equality selects \(0\). This proves strong equilibrium.

The true distribution has mass \(1/2\) at \(1/2\) and mass \(1/2\) at \(1\), giving
\[ C_\mu(0)=\frac34,\qquad C_\mu(1)=\frac14. \]
Its ratio is exactly \(3\).

The first witness already attains \(3\) at \(\alpha=1/2\).

4. The infimum, with a two-facility witness.

Every admissible ratio is at least \(1\).

A singleton facility set would attain \(1\) trivially; it is also attained with two facilities and strict preferences. Take \(F=\{0,1\}\), place the entire population at \(-1\), and assign strategic mass \(\alpha\) and truthful mass \(1-\alpha\). Let everyone report \(-1\).

Facility \(0\) wins and is strictly preferred by every member to facility \(1\). Consequently the profile is a strong equilibrium. Since \(C_\mu(0)=1\) and \(C_\mu(1)=2\), its ratio is \(1\).

Thus \(\inf\mathcal R_\alpha=1\) for every \(\alpha\).

5. Strong-equilibrium existence and running time.

For completeness, existence also extends to the divisible model.

Let each strategic type report a nearest facility \(f_j\in F\), while truthful types report their true locations. These reports are constant on each type interval. Let \(H\) be their objective and \(a\) their winner.

Suppose a positive-mass coalition could obtain a strictly preferred outcome \(b>a\). Every participating type has \(z_j>(a+b)/2>a\), and therefore \(f_j>a\): a facility at or left of \(a\) would be farther from \(z_j\) than \(b\).

If every participating type initially reports \(f_j\geq b\), each already minimizes its contribution \(|x-b|-|x-a|\). The deviation cannot decrease \(H(b)-H(a)\), so \(b\) cannot win.

Otherwise, let \(c<b\) be the smallest initial report among participating types of positive coalition mass. Then \(a<c<b\), and no coalition member initially reports below \(c\). Write \(s\) for the total initial reported mass strictly below \(c\). Pointwise distance bounds give
\[ 0\leq H(c)-H(a)\leq(2s-1)(c-a), \]
so \(s\geq1/2\). This mass is outside the coalition and remains below \(c\). For the new objective \(\widetilde H\), it follows that
\[ \widetilde H(b)-\widetilde H(c)\geq(2s-1)(b-c)\geq0. \]
Since \(c<b\), facility \(b\) cannot win even in a tie.

For a proposed improving outcome \(b<a\), every participating type initially reports \(f_j<a\). If all these reports satisfy \(f_j\leq b\), the comparison \(H(b)-H(a)>0\) cannot decrease.

Otherwise, let \(c>b\) be their largest initial report, so \(b<c<a\). Write \(s\) for the initial reported mass strictly above \(c\). Because \(c<a\), leftmost tie-breaking gives
\[ 0<H(c)-H(a)\leq(2s-1)(a-c). \]
Thus \(s>1/2\). This mass is outside the coalition and stays above \(c\), implying
\[ \widetilde H(b)-\widetilde H(c)\geq(2s-1)(c-b)>0. \]
Again \(b\) cannot win. This excludes every profitable measurable coalition and proves existence.

Nearest facilities and the resulting winner can be found using \(O(m\tau)\) exact arithmetic operations. For an explicit bit bound, let \(D\) be the product of all input denominators. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). The scaled coordinates \(Dz_j,Db\) and weights \(Dw_j\) are integers of \(O(L)\) bits, and every cost comparison can use
\[ D^2C_\mu(b)=\sum_j(Dw_j)\,|Dz_j-Db|. \]
The same representation applies to the constructed reports. Elementary integer arithmetic therefore gives a running time of \(O(m\tau L^2)\) bit operations, including construction and evaluation. No denominator-dependent expansion into individual clients is required.

The extremal witnesses use at most three positive-mass types, and their rational encodings have length linear in the encoding length of \(\alpha\).
