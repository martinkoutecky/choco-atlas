Classification

For inputs satisfying the stated hypotheses, deciding whether the optimum is at most \(q\) is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions, already for \(m=2\) and \(\tau=1\).

Consequently:

  • Computing the exact optimum, or returning any minimizing recommendation, is NP-hard and has no polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
  • The threshold problem is NP-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions.
  • Under many-one reductions, the threshold problem is NP-hard if and only if \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\). Thus the direction “at most \(q\)” requires distinguishing NP-hardness from coNP-hardness.

For every fixed \(p\), there is an exact algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Explicit running-time bounds appear below.

The degenerate cases \(m=1\) and \(p=1\) have optimum zero. The hardness result concerns variable \(p\), while keeping both \(m\) and \(\tau\) constant.

1. Rescaling formula and separation across types

Write \(P_t=W^1_{\Lambda_t}\), and define the robust regret of alternative \(a\) for type \(t\) by

\( \displaystyle g_t(a)=\max_{\sigma\in R_t}\mathrm{MR}\bigl(H_\sigma(a),H_\sigma(A);W^1_{H_\sigma(\Lambda_t)}\bigr). \)

For any strictly positive \(\sigma\), the map

\( \displaystyle v\longmapsto u,\qquad u_i=\frac{\sigma_i v_i}{\sigma\cdot v} \)

is a bijection from \(P_t\) onto \(W^1_{H_\sigma(\Lambda_t)}\). Indeed, \(u\) is nonnegative, sums to one, and satisfies

\( \displaystyle u\cdot H_\sigma(\lambda)=\frac{v\cdot\lambda}{\sigma\cdot v}\geq0. \)

Conversely, normalize the vector with coordinates \(u_i/\sigma_i\) to recover \(v\in P_t\). The utility difference becomes

\( \displaystyle u\cdot\bigl(H_\sigma(b)-H_\sigma(a)\bigr)=\frac{v\cdot(b-a)}{\sigma\cdot v}. \)

Therefore,

\[ \displaystyle g_t(a)=\max_{\sigma\in R_t}\max_{v\in P_t}\max_{b\in A}\frac{v\cdot(b-a)}{\sigma\cdot v}. \tag{1} \]

These maxima exist. The set \(P_t\) is nonempty and compact, and compactness of \(R_t\subset\mathbb R^p_{>0}\) gives a positive lower bound on every denominator in (1).

Let \(E_t\) denote the vertices of \(P_t\), and put

\( \displaystyle r_a(v)=\max_{b\in A}v\cdot(b-a),\qquad h_t(v)=\min_{\sigma\in R_t}\sigma\cdot v. \)

Here \(r_a(v)\geq0\), because \(b=a\) is allowed, and \(h_t(v)>0\).

To reduce (1) to vertices, fix \(\sigma\) and write \(v=\sum_j\alpha_jv^j\), where \(v^j\in E_t\), \(\alpha_j\geq0\), and \(\sum_j\alpha_j=1\). For each \(b\),

\( \displaystyle \frac{v\cdot(b-a)}{\sigma\cdot v} =\sum_j\frac{\alpha_j\,\sigma\cdot v^j}{\sigma\cdot v}\, \frac{v^j\cdot(b-a)}{\sigma\cdot v^j}. \)

The coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one. Thus this ratio cannot exceed its largest value at a vertex. Since \(E_t\) is finite, maximizing over \(\sigma\) now gives

\[ \displaystyle \boxed{g_t(a)=\max_{v\in E_t}\frac{r_a(v)}{h_t(v)}.} \tag{2} \]

This independently derives the extreme-point formula in Theorem 3 of [Wilson’s cited paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.3233/FAIA230571). Its hypotheses hold here: the preference polytope is nonempty, the rescalings are strictly positive, and their range is compact.

Finally, there is no constraint coupling recommendations for different types. Hence

\[ \displaystyle \boxed{\operatorname{OPT}=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\min_{a\in A}g_t(a).} \tag{3} \]

To verify equality, every recommendation tuple has objective at least the right-hand side; choosing a minimizing alternative separately for each type attains it.

2. Exact algorithm and running times

Let \(\ell_t=|\Lambda_t|\) and \(N_t=p+\ell_t\). These count the inequalities describing \(P_t\), including nonnegativity.

Every vertex of \(P_t\) is the unique solution of the normalization equation together with \(p-1\) linearly independent active inequalities. This remains true when \(P_t\) has lower affine dimension: at a vertex, the normalization equation and active inequality normals must jointly have rank \(p\). Otherwise a nonzero direction preserving all those equalities would permit a sufficiently small feasible displacement in both directions, contradicting extremality.

Thus all vertices can be found by inspecting at most

\( \displaystyle K_t=\binom{N_t}{p-1} \)

subsets, solving the corresponding rational systems, and checking feasibility.

For each vertex \(v\):

  • Solve one rational linear program to compute \(h_t(v)\).
  • Compute \(s_a=v\cdot a\) for all \(a\in A\), and \(s_{\max}=\max_{a\in A}s_a\).
  • Update every regret using \(r_a(v)=s_{\max}-s_a\) in (2).

The scores require \(O(mp)\) rational arithmetic operations per vertex, rather than a separate comparison of every ordered pair of alternatives.

All vertex coordinates and LP optima have polynomial binary length in \(p,L\). For example, after clearing denominators, Cramer’s rule expresses vertex coordinates as ratios of determinants; the bound \(p!H^p\) for a determinant with entries of magnitude at most \(H\) gives polynomial bit length. Exact rational LP and the arithmetic used above therefore take polynomial time per vertex.

If the vertices are already explicitly supplied, the running time is

\( \displaystyle O\!\left((1+mp)\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}|E_t|\,\operatorname{poly}(p,L)\right). \)

From the specified inequality input, a valid bound is

\[ \displaystyle O\!\left((1+mp)\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\binom{p+\ell_t}{p-1}\operatorname{poly}(p,L)\right). \tag{4} \]

In particular, a coarser bound in the requested parameters is

\( \displaystyle O\!\left(\tau(1+mp)(L+p)^{p-1}\operatorname{poly}(p,L)\right). \)

This is polynomial for every fixed \(p\), but not a polynomial bound when \(p\) varies.

3. The threshold problem belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\)

We give a polynomial-size certificate for the complementary condition \(\operatorname{OPT}>q\).

For every type \(t\) and every alternative \(a\), provide rational vectors \(v_{t,a}\in P_t\), \(\sigma_{t,a}\in R_t\), and an alternative \(b_{t,a}\in A\). Define

\( \displaystyle d_{t,a}=\frac{v_{t,a}\cdot(b_{t,a}-a)}{\sigma_{t,a}\cdot v_{t,a}}. \)

The verifier checks feasibility and checks

\[ \displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\min_{a\in A}d_{t,a}>q. \tag{5} \]

By (1), \(d_{t,a}\leq g_t(a)\). Thus (5) implies \(\operatorname{OPT}>q\).

Conversely, suppose \(\operatorname{OPT}>q\). Formula (2) allows each \(g_t(a)\) to be attained at a vertex \(v\) of \(P_t\), an alternative attaining \(r_a(v)\), and a vertex of \(R_t\) minimizing \(\sigma\cdot v\). If the regret is zero, simply choose \(b=a\).

Vertices of both rational polytopes have polynomial binary length by the determinant argument above. Consequently, these \(m\tau\) witnesses form a polynomial-size certificate with \(d_{t,a}=g_t(a)\), so (5) holds.

Verification uses rational arithmetic and the explicitly listed inequalities, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,p,L\). Therefore the threshold problem belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\).

4. A MAX-CUT construction

We now prove hardness with one type and two alternatives. We use the NP-completeness of unweighted simple MAX-CUT, established by [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1).

Let \(G\) have vertices \(1,\ldots,n\), edge set \(E\), and \(s=|E|\). Write \(C^*(G)\) for its maximum cut size. Orient each edge from its smaller to its larger endpoint, and set

\( \displaystyle M=s+1,\qquad B=2s+2. \)

4.1 A bilinear identity

For \(x,y\in[0,1]^n\), define

\( \displaystyle C_{\times}(x,y)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E,\ i<j}(x_i+y_j-2x_i y_j) \)

and

\( \displaystyle f(x,y)=-C_{\times}(x,y)+M\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i+y_i-2x_i y_i). \)

We claim

\[ \displaystyle \boxed{\min_{x,y\in[0,1]^n}f(x,y)=-C^*(G).} \tag{6} \]

First, the minimum is attained at binary \(x,y\). For fixed \(y\), the function is affine in \(x\), so an endpoint can be chosen for each coordinate without increasing its value. After that, for fixed binary \(x\), the same argument makes \(y\) binary without increasing the value.

For binary \(x,y\), each summand in \(C_{\times}\) is either zero or one, so \(0\leq C_{\times}(x,y)\leq s\). Also,

\( \displaystyle \sum_i(x_i+y_i-2x_i y_i)=|\{i:x_i\neq y_i\}|. \)

If \(x\neq y\), then \(f(x,y)\geq M-s=1\). If \(x=y\), the penalty vanishes and \(C_{\times}(x,x)\) is exactly the cut size represented by \(x\). Thus the best diagonal value is \(-C^*(G)\), while every off-diagonal binary value is positive. This proves (6).

For later use, write

\( \displaystyle f(x,y)=c(y)+\sum_i x_i\alpha_i(y), \)

where

\( \displaystyle c(y)=M\sum_i y_i-\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E,\ i<j}y_j \)

and

\[ \displaystyle \alpha_i(y)=M(1-2y_i)-\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E,\ i<j}(1-2y_j). \tag{7} \]

Expanding these expressions gives precisely the preceding definition of \(f\).

4.2 The preference polytope

Use \(p=3n+1\) coordinates, labeled \(i^+,i^-\), \(z\), and \(r_i\), for \(1\leq i\leq n\).

The preference polytope consists of nonnegative normalized vectors satisfying

\[ \displaystyle v_{r_i}=0,\qquad n(v_{i^+}+v_{i^-})+v_z=1\quad(1\leq i\leq n). \tag{8} \]

These equations have the required homogeneous preference-constraint representation. Include \(-e_{r_i}\) in \(\Lambda\), and include both signs of

\( \displaystyle \lambda_i=n(e_{i^+}+e_{i^-})+e_z-\mathbf1. \)

Nonnegativity forces \(v_{r_i}=0\); normalization converts \(v\cdot\lambda_i=0\) into (8).

Define \(v(x)\), for \(x\in[0,1]^n\), by

\( \displaystyle v(x)_{i^+}=\frac{x_i}{n},\qquad v(x)_{i^-}=\frac{1-x_i}{n},\qquad v(x)_z=v(x)_{r_i}=0. \)

The resulting polytope is

\[ \displaystyle P=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\{v(x):x\in[0,1]^n\}\cup\{e_z\}\bigr). \tag{9} \]

Indeed, if \(v_z<1\), put \(x_i=nv_{i^+}/(1-v_z)\). Equation (8) ensures \(x_i\in[0,1]\) and \(v=(1-v_z)v(x)+v_z e_z\). If \(v_z=1\), then \(v=e_z\). In particular, the preference polytope is nonempty.

4.3 The rescaling polytope

Specify \(R\) through the following linear constraints. Define \(y_i=\sigma_{r_i}-1\), and require

\( \displaystyle 1\leq\sigma_{r_i}\leq2,\qquad \sigma_z=1, \)

\[ \displaystyle \sigma_{i^-}=B+\frac{c(y)}{n},\qquad \sigma_{i^+}=B+\frac{c(y)}{n}+\alpha_i(y). \tag{10} \]

All equalities are encoded by two inequalities. Since \(c\) and \(\alpha_i\) are affine, (10) is an explicit rational linear description.

The coordinates \(y\) range over exactly \([0,1]^n\), and all other coordinates are specified affine functions of \(y\). Hence \(R\) is nonempty and compact.

It is also strictly positive. The coefficient of \(y_i\) in \(c(y)\) is \(M\) minus the number of incoming edges at \(i\), so \(c(y)\geq0\). Moreover,

\( \displaystyle \alpha_i(y)\geq-M-s. \)

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \sigma_{i^-}\geq B>0,\qquad \sigma_{i^+}\geq B-M-s=1, \)

and the remaining coordinates are at least one.

By (7) and (10),

\( \displaystyle v(x)\cdot\sigma(y) =\frac1n\sum_i\bigl(x_i\sigma_{i^+}+(1-x_i)\sigma_{i^-}\bigr) =B+\frac{f(x,y)}n. \)

Together with (6), this proves

\[ \displaystyle \boxed{\min_{x,y\in[0,1]^n}v(x)\cdot\sigma(y) =B-\frac{C^*(G)}n.} \tag{11} \]

4.4 Two alternatives

For a positive rational \(K\), take \(A=\{a^0,a^1\}\), where \(a^0=0\) and

\( \displaystyle a^1_{i^+}=a^1_{i^-}=B,\qquad a^1_z=-K,\qquad a^1_{r_i}=0. \)

Use one type, with mass one and the polytopes just constructed.

On the cube face, \(v(x)\cdot a^1=B\); at the apex, \(e_z\cdot a^1=-K\). A fractional linear function on the convex hull (9) cannot exceed its maximum on the generating sets, by the weighted-average argument in Section 1. Thus (1) and (11) give

\[ \displaystyle g(a^0)=\frac{B}{B-C^*(G)/n}=:h_G,\qquad g(a^1)=K. \tag{12} \]

For the second equality, the regret numerator against \(a^0\) is negative on the cube face and equals \(K\) at the apex; the apex denominator is \(\sigma_z=1\).

Since \(0\leq C^*(G)\leq s\) and \(B=2s+2\),

\[ \displaystyle 1\leq h_G\leq\frac{B}{B-s/n}<2. \tag{13} \]

The construction has \(p=3n+1\), polynomially many explicitly listed rational coefficients, and polynomial binary length.

5. Hardness of the threshold and recommendation problems

5.1 The threshold problem is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete

Take a nontrivial MAX-CUT threshold \(k\), with \(1\leq k\leq s\), and set \(K=2\). By (12)–(13), the optimum is \(h_G\).

Choose the rational threshold

\( \displaystyle q_k=\frac{B}{B-(k-\tfrac12)/n}. \)

Its denominator is positive, and its binary length is polynomial. The function \(c\mapsto B/(B-c/n)\) is strictly increasing throughout the relevant interval. Because cut sizes are integers,

\( \displaystyle \operatorname{OPT}\leq q_k \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C^*(G)\leq k-1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{there is no cut of size at least }k. \)

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from the complement of MAX-CUT. The restrictions \(1\leq k\leq s\) discard only trivially decidable instances.

Therefore the threshold problem is coNP-hard. Section 3 proves membership, so it is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete, already for \(m=2,\tau=1\).

A polynomial-time threshold algorithm would decide MAX-CUT by negating its answer. Hence such an algorithm exists only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

5.2 Returning any minimizing recommendation is NP-hard

The preceding value reduction always recommends \(a^0\), so it alone would not establish hardness of returning a recommendation. To establish that separate claim, use the same construction with \(K=q_k\).

Equation (12) now gives \(g(a^0)=h_G\) and \(g(a^1)=q_k\). Integer cut sizes and the half-integer threshold exclude ties:

  • If \(C^*(G)\leq k-1\), then \(h_G<q_k\), and \(a^0\) is the unique minimizer.
  • If \(C^*(G)\geq k\), then \(h_G>q_k\), and \(a^1\) is the unique minimizer.

Thus one call to an algorithm returning any minimizing recommendation decides MAX-CUT. The allowance for arbitrary tie-breaking does not affect this reduction.

5.3 Why the many-one distinction matters

Since the threshold problem belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\), NP-hardness under many-one reductions would imply \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{coNP}\), and hence \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\). Conversely, if these classes coincide, its coNP-completeness makes it NP-hard.

The fully proved classification is therefore: coNP-complete threshold decision; NP-hard exact value computation and exact recommendation; polynomial-time solvable for every fixed \(p\), with running time (4).
