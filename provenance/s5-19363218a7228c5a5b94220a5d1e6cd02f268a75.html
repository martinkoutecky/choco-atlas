The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(p\ge1\), a finite ordered type set \(T\subset\mathbb{Q}\), and rational masses \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), compute the replication-consistent lower-median facility \(F_{\mathrm{med}}(\mu)=\inf\{y:\mu((-\infty,y])\ge\tfrac12\}\) and certify \(\Phi_p(\mu,F_{\mathrm{med}}(\mu))\le 2^{1-\frac1p}\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\Phi_p(\mu,y)\), where \(\Phi_p(\mu,y)=\left(\sum_{x\in T}\mu_x|x-y|^p\right)^{1/p}\), with admissibility inherited from strategyproof finite replicas.

Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length, and \(m=1\) is the number of facilities.
A facility may be placed at any \(y\in\mathbb R\); individual costs are \(|x-y|\), with no payments. Define \(\Phi_p(\mu,y)=(\sum_{x\in T}\mu_x|x-y|^p)^{1/p}\) and \(F_{\mathrm{med}}(\mu)=\min\{x\in T:\sum_{z\in T:z\leq x}\mu_z\geq\tfrac12\}\).
Use the same formula on finite real-supported distributions to define the mechanism on arbitrary real reports. A deterministic distribution rule \(F\) is admissible if every induced rule \(f_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=F(n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{x_i})\), for \(n\geq2\), is strategyproof, and repeating every report equally many times leaves its outcome unchanged.

Conclusion

The approximation claim holds. For every fixed finite real exponent \(p\geq1\), the rule \(F_{\mathrm{med}}\) is admissible, is computable in time polynomial in \(\tau,L\) with \(m=1\), and satisfies the stated guarantee. Its approximation factor \(2^{1-1/p}\) is attained even on a two-point rational support.

The proof below treats the masses directly and requires no expansion into an agent list.

1. Well-definedness and admissibility

Let \(\mu\) be any probability distribution with nonempty finite real support, and put \(c=F_{\mathrm{med}}(\mu)\). The cumulative mass reaches \(1\), so \(c\) exists and is unique under the prescribed tie-breaking rule. By its definition,

\[ \mu((-\infty,c))<\tfrac12, \qquad \mu((-\infty,c])\geq\tfrac12. \]

For an agent list of length \(n\), write its reports in nondecreasing order as \(x_{(1)},\ldots,x_{(n)}\), including repetitions. Its induced outcome is

\[ f_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=x_{(k)}, \qquad k=\lceil n/2\rceil. \]

Indeed, the first support location whose cumulative count reaches \(n/2\) is exactly this order statistic.

To prove strategyproofness, fix the other reports, let agent \(i\)'s true location be \(a\), and let the truthful outcome be \(c\).

  • If \(a<c\), the truthful list has at most \(k-1\) reports strictly below \(c\). Agent \(i\) already contributes one such report, so replacing that report cannot increase this count. Every resulting outcome \(c'\) therefore satisfies \(c'\geq c\), giving \(|a-c'|\geq|a-c|\).
  • If \(a>c\), at least \(k\) truthful reports are at most \(c\), and none of these belongs to agent \(i\). They remain after any deviation, so \(c'\leq c\). Again, \(|a-c'|\geq|a-c|\).
  • If \(a=c\), the truthful cost is zero and cannot decrease.

Thus every induced rule is strategyproof, including for arbitrary real reports.

Repeating every report \(r\) times, for any positive integer \(r\), preserves the empirical distribution:

\[ \frac1{rn}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\delta_{x_i} = \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{x_i}. \]

Consequently, it preserves the outcome. Hence \(F_{\mathrm{med}}\) is admissible.

2. Polynomial-time computation from the support

Write the input masses as \(\mu_{x_i}=a_i/b_i\), with positive integers \(a_i,b_i\) represented in binary. Let

\[ D=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}b_i, \qquad A_i=a_i(D/b_i). \]

The bit length of \(D\) is at most the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence is \(O(L)\). Moreover, \(\sum_i A_i=D\), so all cumulative sums also have \(O(L)\) bits.

Scan the support in increasing order and return the first \(x_j\) satisfying

\[ 2\sum_{i=1}^{j}A_i\geq D. \]

All products, exact divisions, additions, and comparisons involve polynomially many bits and polynomially many operations. If sorting is necessary, exact comparisons of rational locations also take polynomial time.

The output is an input location, so its encoding length is at most \(L\). The algorithm never evaluates a \(p\)-th power or computes an optimal facility location; it is independent of \(p\). Thus its running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with \(m=1\).

3. Approximation bound for arbitrary finite masses

We prove the stronger statement that, for every finite real-supported probability distribution \(\mu\) and every \(y\in\mathbb R\),

\[ \sum_x\mu_x|x-c|^p \leq 2^{p-1}\sum_x\mu_x|x-y|^p, \qquad c=F_{\mathrm{med}}(\mu). \]

A pairwise inequality

For nonnegative \(u,v\),

\[ u^p+v^p\leq(u+v)^p. \]

If \(u+v>0\), divide by \((u+v)^p\) and use \(t^p\leq t\) for \(t\in[0,1]\); the zero case is immediate.

Also, convexity of \(t\mapsto t^p\) on the nonnegative reals gives

\[ (u+v)^p\leq2^{p-1}(u^p+v^p). \]

Therefore, whenever \(a\leq c\leq b\), the triangle inequality yields

\[ |a-c|^p+|b-c|^p \leq(b-a)^p \leq(|a-y|+|b-y|)^p \leq2^{p-1}\bigl(|a-y|^p+|b-y|^p\bigr). \]

Splitting and pairing the mass

Let \(\ell=\sum_{x<c}\mu_x\). Define masses \(\alpha_x\) by assigning all mass strictly left of \(c\) to \(\alpha\), assigning mass \(1/2-\ell\) at \(c\), and assigning zero mass strictly right of \(c\). Set \(\beta_x=\mu_x-\alpha_x\).

The median inequalities imply

\[ 0\leq\tfrac12-\ell\leq\mu_c. \]

Thus both mass assignments are nonnegative, and

\[ \sum_x\alpha_x=\sum_x\beta_x=\tfrac12. \]

The support of \(\alpha\) lies at or left of \(c\), while that of \(\beta\) lies at or right of \(c\).

For support locations \(a,b\), put \(\gamma_{ab}=2\alpha_a\beta_b\). Its row and column sums are

\[ \sum_b\gamma_{ab}=\alpha_a, \qquad \sum_a\gamma_{ab}=\beta_b. \]

Every pair with positive \(\gamma_{ab}\) satisfies \(a\leq c\leq b\). Multiply the pairwise inequality by \(\gamma_{ab}\) and sum. Using these row and column sums gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_x\mu_x|x-c|^p &=\sum_{a,b}\gamma_{ab}\bigl(|a-c|^p+|b-c|^p\bigr)\\ &\leq2^{p-1}\sum_{a,b}\gamma_{ab}\bigl(|a-y|^p+|b-y|^p\bigr)\\ &=2^{p-1}\sum_x\mu_x|x-y|^p. \end{aligned} \]

This establishes the stronger statement.

The objective is continuous, and projecting any facility location onto the interval between the smallest and largest support locations weakly decreases every individual distance. It therefore attains its minimum on that compact interval. Taking \(p\)-th roots and choosing a minimizer proves

\[ \Phi_p(\mu,F_{\mathrm{med}}(\mu)) \leq 2^{1-1/p}\min_{y\in\mathbb R}\Phi_p(\mu,y). \]

The argument includes \(p=1\) and singleton supports.

4. Sharpness

Take \(\mu=\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_1\). The lower median is \(0\), so its cost is \(2^{-1/p}\).

For every real \(y\), convexity and the triangle inequality imply

\[ \frac{|y|^p+|1-y|^p}{2} \geq \left(\frac{|y|+|1-y|}{2}\right)^p \geq2^{-p}. \]

Equality holds at \(y=1/2\), giving optimal cost \(1/2\). The ratio is therefore exactly \(2^{1-1/p}\).

This agrees with the finite-agent result in [Feigenbaum, Sethuraman, and Ye, Theorem 1](https://www.columbia.edu/~js1353/pubs/fsy.pdf); the direct mass argument above establishes the requested support-encoded formulation.
