The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an attack graph \(G=(A,R)\), a finite support \(T\) of ballot types \(t\in\{0,1\}^{2|A|}\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a positive rational smoothing mass \(\delta\), and tolerance \(\eta>0\), compute a rational vector \(\hat{x}\in[0,1]^A\) with maximum residual at most \(\eta\) for \(x_a=r_a\prod_{b\in\operatorname{Att}(a)}(1-x_b)\), where \(r_a=p_a/(p_a+q_a+\delta)\), \(p_a=\sum_t\mu_t b_t^+(a)\), and \(q_a=\sum_t\mu_t b_t^-(a)\).

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\subseteq\{0,1\}^{2m}\) of \(\tau\) distinct types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The coordinates \(b_t^+(a),b_t^-(a)\) record positive and negative votes independently; both may equal \(1\), and both may equal \(0\).
Given binary rationals \(\delta>0\) and \(\eta>0\), define \(p_a=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t b_t^+(a)\), \(q_a=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t b_t^-(a)\), and \(r_a=p_a/(p_a+q_a+\delta)\). Zero positive-vote mass is allowed; an empty product equals \(1\).

2. Totality, short witnesses, and membership in $\mathrm{PPAD}$

Define
$$ F_a(x)=r_a\prod_{b\in\operatorname{Att}(a)}(1-x_b), \qquad F:[0,1]^A\longrightarrow[0,1]^A. $$

2.1. Exact coefficient computation

The explicitly listed masses allow exact polynomial-time computation of $p_a$ and $q_a$. To see the bit-length bound, use the product of the listed denominators as a common denominator. Its binary length is at most the sum of their binary lengths. Adding the corresponding numerators increases length by at most a polynomial amount.

Computing
$$ r_a=\frac{p_a}{p_a+q_a+\delta} $$
therefore also takes polynomial time and produces coefficients of polynomial binary length.

Because $p_a,q_a\geq0$ and $\delta>0$,
$$ 0\leq r_a<1. $$
In particular, $p_a=0$ gives $r_a=0$.

2.2. Existence and polynomial-length residual witnesses

Each coordinate of $F$ is continuous. Every complementary factor lies in $[0,1]$, and an empty product is $1$. Hence $F$ maps the nonempty compact convex cube into itself. Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem supplies $x^*\in[0,1]^A$ with
$$ F(x^*)=x^*. $$

For $u_i,v_i\in[0,1]$, the telescoping identity
$$ \prod_{i=1}^d u_i-\prod_{i=1}^d v_i = \sum_{i=1}^d (u_i-v_i) \left(\prod_{j<i}u_j\right) \left(\prod_{j>i}v_j\right) $$
implies
$$ \left|\prod_{i=1}^d u_i-\prod_{i=1}^d v_i\right| \leq\sum_{i=1}^d|u_i-v_i|. $$

Since every argument has at most $m$ distinct attackers,
$$ \|F(x)-F(y)\|_\infty\leq m\|x-y\|_\infty. $$
Consequently,
$$ \|(x-F(x))-(y-F(y))\|_\infty \leq(m+1)\|x-y\|_\infty. $$

Choose a nonnegative integer $k$ such that
$$ 2^{-k}\leq\frac{\min\{\eta,1\}}{m+1}. $$
Round each coordinate of $x^*$ down to a multiple of $2^{-k}$. The resulting vector $\hat{x}$ remains in the cube and satisfies
$$ \|\hat{x}-F(\hat{x})\|_\infty \leq(m+1)2^{-k} \leq\eta. $$

The binary encoding of $\eta$ ensures that $k=O(L+\log m)$ suffices. Thus every instance has a polynomial-length rational witness. Given such a witness, exact rational evaluation of all products and comparison with $\eta$ take polynomial time.

These facts establish totality and short, efficiently verifiable witnesses; they do not provide an algorithm for finding a witness.

2.3. Membership theorem and hypothesis checks

We use the following established result: weak fixed-point approximation belongs to $\mathrm{PPAD}$ for a family of continuous self-maps that is polynomially computable on rational inputs and polynomially continuous. Here polynomial continuity means that each requested accuracy has a sufficient continuity radius of polynomial binary length. This is [Etessami and Yannakakis, Proposition 1(2)](https://www.inf.ed.ac.uk/publications/online/1164.pdf).

Both hypotheses hold:

  • Rational evaluation of $F$ uses polynomially many additions, multiplications, and divisions. Each coordinate is a product of at most $m$ rational factors and one precomputed coefficient, so intermediate binary lengths are polynomial in the instance and evaluation-point lengths.
  • For any rational accuracy $c>0$, the radius

$$ \frac{c}{m+1} $$
has polynomial binary length and satisfies
$$ \|x-y\|_\infty<\frac{c}{m+1} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \|F(x)-F(y)\|_\infty<c. $$

The theorem returns a rational vector with residual strictly less than $\eta$, which satisfies the required non-strict inequality. Therefore the problem belongs to $\mathrm{PPAD}$.

3. The source problem for hardness

Its variables receive labels in $\{0,1,\bot\}$. Every variable is the output of exactly one gate; the variables appearing within each gate are distinct. There are two gate types:

  • NOR: inputs $u,v$, output $w$. If both inputs are $0$, the output must be $1$. If either input is $1$, the output must be $0$. Otherwise the output is unrestricted.
  • PURIFY: input $u$, outputs $v,w$. If the input is $0$ or $1$, both outputs must equal it. For every input label, at least one output must be in $\{0,1\}$.

Decode target coordinates as follows:

  • Values in $[0,1/10]$ receive label $0$.

  • All other values receive label $\bot$.

The reduction must work for every vector satisfying the target residual inequalities.

$$ |U(s)-U(s')|=|s-s'| $$

7. Further result: a polynomial-time algorithm for cycle components

A strongly connected component is a maximal vertex set in which every vertex can reach every other by a directed path.

Proposition. Suppose every strongly connected component is either:

  • a singleton, possibly with a self-attack; or
  • a directed simple cycle whose internal edges are exactly its cycle edges.

Then the residual search problem is solvable in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$. Arbitrarily many attacks between different components are allowed.

7.1. Processing components in dependency order

Contract each strongly connected component to one vertex. The resulting graph is acyclic: a directed cycle of distinct components would make their union strongly connected, contradicting maximality.

Process this acyclic graph in topological order. Thus, when processing a component $C$, all coordinates of its external attackers have already been selected.

For $a\in C$, compute exactly
$$ c_a=r_a \prod_{b\in\operatorname{Att}(a)\setminus C}(1-\hat{x}_b). $$
These coefficients satisfy
$$ 0\leq c_a\leq r_a<1. $$

If $C=\{a\}$ has no self-attack, set the temporary exact value to
$$ y_a=c_a. $$

Otherwise, order the component cyclically as $a_1,\ldots,a_s$, including $s=1$ for a self-attacking singleton. Its equations are
$$ y_i=c_i(1-y_{i-1}), \qquad y_0=y_s. $$

7.2. Solving the cycle exactly

Define the affine maps
$$ f_i(z)=c_i(1-z). $$
Each maps $[0,1]$ into itself. Substitution gives
$$ y_1=g(y_1), \qquad g=f_1\circ f_s\circ\cdots\circ f_2. $$
For $s=1$, take $g=f_1$.

The composition has the form
$$ g(z)=\alpha+\beta z, \qquad \beta=(-1)^s\prod_{i=1}^s c_i. $$
Since $|\beta|<1$, its unique possible fixed point is
$$ y_1=\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}. $$

It lies in $[0,1]$: the continuous function $g(z)-z$ is nonnegative at $0$ and nonpositive at $1$, because $g$ maps the interval into itself. The intermediate value theorem supplies a fixed point in the interval, and $1-\beta\neq0$ ensures uniqueness.

Compute the remaining coordinates by
$$ y_i=f_i(y_{i-1}) \qquad(2\leq i\leq s). $$
The fixed-point equation for $g$ verifies the closing equation for $y_1$.

All these values are rational and computed using exact rational arithmetic.

7.3. Rounding preserves the required residual

Choose $k$ such that
$$ h=2^{-k}\leq\frac{\min\{\eta,1\}}2. $$
After solving a component, round every temporary coordinate down to the dyadic grid of spacing $h$. Thus
$$ |\hat{x}_a-y_a|\leq h. $$

For a component without an internal attack,
$$ |\hat{x}_a-F_a(\hat{x})|\leq h. $$

For a cycle vertex with internal predecessor $b$, the previously selected external coordinates remain fixed, so
$$ F_a(\hat{x})=c_a(1-\hat{x}_b). $$
Therefore
$$ \begin{aligned} |\hat{x}_a-F_a(\hat{x})| &=|\hat{x}_a-c_a(1-\hat{x}_b)|\\ &\leq|\hat{x}_a-y_a| +c_a|\hat{x}_b-y_b|\\ &\leq(1+c_a)h \leq2h \leq\eta. \end{aligned} $$
This calculation also applies to a self-attacking singleton.

Later components cannot alter the residual of an earlier component: every attacker outside that earlier component precedes it in the component order. Hence all final residual inequalities hold.

7.4. Polynomial bit complexity

Let $B$ bound the binary lengths of the precomputed $r_a$; Section 2 gives $B$ polynomial in $L$.

Every external coordinate is a dyadic with denominator $2^k$. Thus a coefficient $c_a$, containing at most $m$ such factors, has binary length
$$ O(B+mk). $$

Composing $s$ affine maps increases rational length by at most the sum of the input coefficient lengths, up to polynomial overhead. More explicitly, if $c_i=u_i/v_i$, the affine constant and slope can both be represented with a denominator dividing $\prod_i v_i$. The denominator of the cycle fixed point is obtained by subtracting the corresponding slope numerator from that common denominator. Its binary length is therefore
$$ O\bigl(s(B+mk)\bigr). $$

Computing the remaining cycle values and rounding them uses polynomial-time integer arithmetic. Rounding after each component prevents unbounded growth across components. Since
$$ k=O(L+1), $$
the complete algorithm has polynomial running time.

This proves the proposition.

Corollary. Graphs with at most one attacker per argument admit polynomial-time computation of an exact rational fixed point.

Indeed, their components have the required form. Cycle coefficients are simply the original $r_a$. Outside cycles, each equation is either constant or unary affine. Along any outward path, at most $m$ original coefficients are introduced, so exact rational binary lengths remain polynomial. No rounding is needed in this special case.

8. Further result: exponentially slow successive substitution

The convergence statement in the question cannot by itself provide a polynomial bound in binary input length.

Proposition. There are valid instances with $m=\tau=1$ and input length $O(n)$ for which successive substitution from a fixed interior starting value requires more than $2^{n-2}$ iterations to satisfy the residual tolerance.

Proof. Take one self-attacking argument and one ballot of mass $1$ with positive vote $1$ and negative vote $0$. For an integer $n\geq3$, set
$$ \delta=\eta=2^{-n}, \qquad r=\frac1{1+2^{-n}}. $$
The equation is
$$ x=r(1-x). $$

With one argument, both simultaneous and successive substitution use
$$ x^{(k+1)}=r(1-x^{(k)}). $$
Choose the interior starting value
$$ x^{(0)}=1/4. $$

Define its signed residual by
$$ d_k=(1+r)x^{(k)}-r. $$
Direct substitution gives
$$ d_{k+1}=-rd_k. $$
Since $r\geq2/3$,
$$ |d_0|=\frac{3r-1}{4}\geq1/4. $$
Consequently,
$$ |d_k|=r^k|d_0|\geq\frac14r^k. $$

Put $s=1-r$. Then
$$ 0<s<2^{-n}. $$
Bernoulli’s inequality, obtained by induction for integer $k\geq0$, gives
$$ r^k=(1-s)^k\geq1-ks. $$
For $k\leq2^{n-2}$,
$$ r^k\geq3/4. $$
Hence
$$ |x^{(k)}-r(1-x^{(k)})| =|d_k| \geq3/16 >2^{-n} =\eta. $$

The instance has binary length $O(n)$, but none of these iterations meets the tolerance. This proves the proposition.

The exact fixed point is nevertheless
$$ x^*=\frac{r}{1+r}=\frac1{2+2^{-n}}, $$
which is computable in polynomial time. Thus this is a lower bound for the substitution procedure, not for the search problem itself.

9. What remains unresolved

The classification establishes the equivalence
$$ \begin{gathered} \text{This residual search problem has a polynomial-time algorithm}\\ \Longleftrightarrow\\ \text{Every $\mathrm{PPAD}$ search problem has a polynomial-time algorithm}. \end{gathered} $$
It does not prove either side.

For the $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness alternative, specify the reduction convention. Suppose there were polynomial-time procedures mapping each Boolean formula $\psi$ to a valid target instance and decoding every valid target solution into a correct satisfiability decision.

Then unsatisfiability would have polynomially verifiable certificates:

  • Guess a polynomial-length residual solution for the target instance.
  • Verify its residual exactly.
  • Verify that decoding reports unsatisfiability.

For an unsatisfiable formula, such a certificate exists by totality and the short-witness bound. For a satisfiable formula, none exists by correctness of decoding. Thus unsatisfiability would belong to $\mathrm{NP}$, implying
$$ \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}. $$

The same implication holds if decoding must return a satisfying assignment whenever one exists: accept the certificate precisely when the decoded output fails the direct satisfying-assignment check.

This is a conditional consequence, not a proof that $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness is impossible. Neither an unconditional polynomial-time algorithm for general graphs nor the requested $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness result is derived here.
