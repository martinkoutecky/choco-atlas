The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(d\), rational \(\eta>0\), a rational unit target \(v^\star\in S^{d-1}\), a finite list of rational unit opinion types \(u_i\in S^{d-1}\) with binary-encoded rational masses \(\mu_i\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), and a rational threshold \(q\), does there exist a common infinite intervention sequence \(v^{(t)}\in S^{d-1}\) such that types of total mass at least \(q\) converge to \(v^\star\) under the paper's update rule? Equivalently, does some \(a\in S^{d-1}\) satisfy \(\sum_{i:\langle u_i,a\rangle>0}\mu_i\ge q\); determine the complexity in the input bit length and \(d,\tau\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given an integer \(d\ge2\), rational \(\eta>0\), and rational unit target \(v^\star\in S^{d-1}\), a society is specified by its support: distinct rational unit types \(u_1,\ldots,u_\tau\) with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_i\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; write running times in \(d\), \(\tau\), and total input bit length \(L\).
A common intervention sequence consists of arbitrary real unit vectors \(v^{(t)}\in S^{d-1}\). Starting from \(u_i^{(1)}=u_i\), each type updates by \(u_i^{(t+1)}=(u_i^{(t)}+\eta\langle u_i^{(t)},v^{(t)}\rangle v^{(t)})/\|u_i^{(t)}+\eta\langle u_i^{(t)},v^{(t)}\rangle v^{(t)}\|_2\). Its entire mass converges to the target exactly when \(\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u_i^{(t)}-v^\star\|_2=0\).
The maximum attainable converging mass equals \(M=\max_{a\in S^{d-1}}\sum_{i:\langle u_i,a\rangle>0}\mu_i\); boundary types are excluded. The threshold decision question, for rational \(q\in[0,1]\), is whether \(M\ge q\).

For each fixed rational \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\), is distinguishing the promised cases \(M>1-\varepsilon\) and \(M<1/2+\varepsilon\) solvable in polynomial time in \(d,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite unweighted societies, the same hemisphere-density gap is reported to admit no polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\) ([Hązła et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.05274)).
Maximum agreement with labeled Boolean examples has a verified NP-hard gap between agreement \(1-\varepsilon\) and \(1/2+\varepsilon\) ([Guruswami–Raghavendra](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~venkatg/pubs/papers/halfspace_Full.pdf)). Neither statement explicitly establishes the gap for binary rational unit types with rational masses.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted dimension to d≥2: unrestricted movement toward an arbitrary target, and the stated hemisphere equivalence, fail in dimension one.
  • Removed zero-mass entries and merged duplicate types to implement the required positive-mass support convention.
  • Specified that interventions may have arbitrary real coordinates, as in the discrete model, although input vectors are rational.
  • Restricted the threshold q to [0,1], the domain of attainable mass.
  • Restricted the fixed approximation parameter to 0<ε<1/4 so the two promise cases are disjoint.

Result

For every fixed rational \(\varepsilon\) with \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\), the promised distinction is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. This holds even when all coordinates and masses are dyadic rationals, the intervention parameter is fixed to \(\eta=1\), and the target is \(v^\star=e_1\).

The key is an exact embedding of Boolean examples into a rational unit sphere. No approximation of their directions is necessary.

1. The source hardness theorem and strict inequalities

For a nonempty multiset of labeled examples \((x_j,y_j)\), where \(x_j\in\{-1,1\}^n\) and \(y_j\in\{-1,1\}\), let \(A\) be the maximum agreement fraction of an affine classifier that predicts positive when \(w\cdot x\ge\theta\).

Guruswami–Raghavendra’s Theorem 8.1, with Definitions 2.2–2.3, states that for every fixed positive \(\alpha,\beta\), distinguishing \(A\ge1-\alpha\) from \(A<1/2+\beta\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The examples are explicitly represented Boolean vectors, with repetitions allowed. [Source](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~venkatg/pubs/papers/halfspace_Full.pdf)

We first verify that excluding boundary examples does not change this optimum. Define strict agreement by the condition \(y_j(w\cdot x_j-\theta)>0\), and denote its maximum fraction by \(A_{\mathrm{str}}\).

Every strictly agreeing example agrees under the ordinary classifier, so \(A_{\mathrm{str}}\le A\). Conversely, fix any ordinary classifier. Let \(J_-\) be its correctly classified negative examples. If \(J_-\) is nonempty, choose

\[ 0<\delta<\min_{j\in J_-}(\theta-w\cdot x_j). \]

The minimum is positive because these finitely many examples satisfy strict negative inequalities. If \(J_-\) is empty, choose any \(\delta>0\). Replace the threshold by \(\theta'=\theta-\delta\).

Every previously correct positive example now satisfies \(w\cdot x_j-\theta'\ge\delta>0\). Every previously correct negative example remains strictly negative by the choice of \(\delta\). Thus strict agreement is at least the original agreement, proving \(A_{\mathrm{str}}=A\).

Apply the source theorem with \(\alpha=\beta=\varepsilon/2\). Its hard instances therefore satisfy either \(A_{\mathrm{str}}\ge1-\varepsilon/2\) or \(A_{\mathrm{str}}<1/2+\varepsilon/2\).

2. Exact embedding into rational unit vectors

Let the source instance contain \(N\) labeled examples. We may assume \(n\ge1\), appending an unused Boolean coordinate if necessary.

Choose the least power of two \(k\) such that \(k^2\ge n+1\), and put \(d=k^2\) and \(r=d-n\). Then \(r\ge1\) and \(d<4(n+1)\).

For each example, construct

\[ z_j=\frac{y_j}{k}(x_j,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{r\text{ coordinates}}). \]

Every coordinate is a dyadic rational, and \(z_j\) is exactly unit length: it has \(d=k^2\) coordinates, each of squared magnitude \(1/k^2\).

We prove that the maximum open-hemisphere fraction of these vectors is exactly \(A_{\mathrm{str}}\).

For any \(a\in S^{d-1}\), define \(w=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) and \(\theta=-\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{d}a_\ell\). Direct calculation gives

\[ \langle z_j,a\rangle=\frac{y_j}{k}(w\cdot x_j-\theta). \]

Consequently, every hemisphere selects precisely the strictly agreeing examples of an affine classifier.

Conversely, given affine parameters \((w,\theta)\ne(0,0)\), set \(b=(w,-\theta,0,\ldots,0)\) and \(a=b/\|b\|_2\). The available padding coordinate makes this construction possible, and

\[ \langle z_j,a\rangle=\frac{y_j(w\cdot x_j-\theta)}{k\|b\|_2}. \]

The denominator is positive, so every strict sign is preserved. The excluded parameters \((0,0)\) agree strictly with no examples and cannot improve the optimum: a constant classifier agrees with at least half the examples.

Hence the embedding preserves the optimum exactly, including its boundary convention.

3. Distinct support and binary rational masses

Merge identical vectors among the \(z_j\). Write the resulting distinct unit types as \(u_1,\ldots,u_\tau\), with multiplicities \(c_1,\ldots,c_\tau\). Thus \(\tau\le N\), each \(c_i\) is positive, and \(\sum_i c_i=N\).

Giving type \(u_i\) mass \(\mu_i^0=c_i/N\) preserves every hemisphere fraction exactly. In particular, the resulting optimum is \(M_0=A_{\mathrm{str}}\). These masses are rationals with binary encodings of polynomial length.

To cover also the stricter interpretation that “binary rational” means dyadic, we now produce positive dyadic masses while preserving the promised gap.

Choose the least power of two \(Q\) satisfying \(Q\ge\max\{2N,4N/\varepsilon\}\). Put \(h_i=\lfloor Qc_i/N\rfloor\) and \(R=Q-\sum_i h_i\). Since the unrounded quantities sum to the integer \(Q\), we have \(0\le R<\tau\).

Add one to any \(R\) distinct values \(h_i\), obtaining integers \(p_i\), and define \(\mu_i=p_i/Q\). These masses sum to one. They are positive because \(Qc_i/N\ge2\). Moreover,

\[ \left|\mu_i-\mu_i^0\right|\le\frac1Q, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\left|\mu_i-\mu_i^0\right| \le\frac{\tau}{Q}\le\frac{\varepsilon}{4}. \]

Thus the mass of every hemisphere changes by at most \(\varepsilon/4\). Taking maxima on both sides yields \(|M-M_0|\le\varepsilon/4\).

All construction steps use integer arithmetic of polynomial bit complexity. For fixed \(\varepsilon\), the output has \(d=O(n)\), \(\tau\le N\), and total input bit length

\[ L=O\!\left(\tau d\log(d+1)+\tau\log(N+1)+d\right). \]

In particular, merging repetitions does not require expanding binary-encoded masses into individual agents.

4. Preservation of the promised gap

In the source completeness case,

\[ M\ge A_{\mathrm{str}}-\varepsilon/4 \ge1-3\varepsilon/4>1-\varepsilon. \]

In the source soundness case,

\[ M\le A_{\mathrm{str}}+\varepsilon/4 <1/2+3\varepsilon/4<1/2+\varepsilon. \]

Finally, set \(\eta=1\) and \(v^\star=e_1\), both valid rational inputs. By the hemisphere characterization supplied in the question, the constructed value \(M\) is exactly the maximum attainable converging mass.

This is a polynomial-time reduction into the two required promised cases. Therefore the distinction is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and a polynomial-time algorithm in \(d,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The ordinary threshold question is also \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard already at \(q=3/4\): because \(\varepsilon<1/4\), every constructed completeness instance satisfies \(M>3/4\), whereas every soundness instance satisfies \(M<3/4\).
