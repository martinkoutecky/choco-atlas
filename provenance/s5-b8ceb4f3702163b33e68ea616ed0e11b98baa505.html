The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a fixed solved exchange network and a finite set of complete contract types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), scale each type’s capacities by its mass and define coalition value \(v(z)\) from the resulting network-flow problem for \(0\le z\le\mu\). Can one compute the continuous egalitarian allocation of per-person payments \(p_t\), subject to \(\sum_t z_t p_t\ge v(z)\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t p_t\) equal to available profit, by a maximum-flow characterization minimizing deviation in per-person payments, in time polynomial in network size, \(|T|\), and input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) complete contract types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; masses need not sum to one.
The input is a fixed solved, directed acyclic exchange network \(G=(V,E)\), with source \(a\), sink \(b\), and \(m=|E|\). Each type controls a disjoint bundle \(E_t\) of contract arcs with nonnegative rational per-person capacities \(u_e\); all remaining arcs have fixed capacities equal to their flows in the supplied winning solution \(f^*\). Unused arcs are deleted. Nonnegative rational profits \(c_e\) occur on trading arcs; contract arcs have zero profit.
For a coalition \(z\in\prod_{t\in T}[0,\mu_t]\), contract arc \(e\in E_t\) has capacity \(z_tu_e\), while trading capacities remain fixed. Define \(v(z)\) as the maximum of \(\sum_e c_ef_e\) over feasible \(a\)-\(b\) flows of unrestricted total value. All lower bounds are zero: the winning schedule has already been selected. Assume \(f^*\) is optimal at \(z=\mu\), and set available profit \(P=v(\mu)\).
The continuous core consists of per-person payments \(p_t\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_t z_tp_t\geq v(z)\) for every real coalition \(0\leq z\leq\mu\), and \(\sum_t\mu_tp_t=P\).
The continuous egalitarian allocation is the core allocation with lexicographically maximal increasing payment quantile: type \(t\) occupies mass \(\mu_t\) at payment \(p_t\), and quantiles are compared on their first interval of disagreement. Deviation means \(\Delta(p)=\max_t p_t-\min_t p_t\), rather than the range of total type payments. Computation includes determining whether the core is empty.

Is exact computation of this continuous egalitarian allocation polynomial in \(|V|,m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is total input bit length, or is it NP-hard?
Does it admit a polynomial-size maximum-flow characterization in which terminal type flows are \(\mu_tp_t\) and an egalitarian allocation minimizes \(\Delta(p)\)?

For finite contracts in a fixed winning exchange, a minimum-deviation almost-equal maximum flow characterizes the egalitarian allocation, and a polynomial-time computation is given. [Heller–Krumke](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.15389).
A fuller treatment also fixes the winning flow and deletes unused arcs; it does not establish the rational-mass version above. [Heller’s thesis](https://publica-rest.fraunhofer.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/d4f6391c-b1a9-4712-bbac-1e5618ae8e61/content). No result settling this exact continuous problem was identified in the search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Mass normalization was unspecified; positive rational masses have arbitrary total mass.
  • Complete types were undefined; each type includes its entire bundle of contract arcs and their per-person capacities, with one common participation mass scaling the bundle.
  • The fractional-coalition flow problem was unspecified; it uses variable-throughput flows on the fixed winning network, type capacities scaled by coalition mass, and trading arcs capped by the supplied winning flow.
  • The winning-schedule restriction is made explicit by deleting unused arcs and retaining fixed trading capacities, rather than permitting new winning trades.
  • Lower bounds and balances for fractional coalitions were unspecified; winning-schedule requirements are treated as already resolved, and the coalition problem uses zero lower bounds and ordinary source-to-sink flow conservation.
  • Available profit is identified with the full-society optimal flow profit; all numerical data are binary rationals and profits and capacities are nonnegative.
  • The continuous egalitarian objective is defined by the mass-weighted increasing payment quantile, preserving per-person equality; minimum range is a separate characterization question.
  • Existence was not specified for fractional coalitions; computation includes detecting an empty continuous core, without assuming that discrete existence transfers.

Result

Exact computation is polynomial-time solvable in \(|V|,m,\tau,L\), including deciding whether the core is empty.

The proposed minimum-deviation characterization fails: the continuous egalitarian allocation need not minimize \(\Delta(p)\) over the core. Moreover, under the usual meaning of a maximum-flow characterization—its maximum-flow terminal vectors are exactly the core allocations—an ordinary flow representation need not exist, regardless of its size.

The proofs below use the rational masses directly, without expanding them into individual players.

1. A polynomial-size linear description of the continuous core

Let \(C=\bigcup_{t\in T}E_t\) be the contract arcs and \(R=E\setminus C\) the remaining arcs. Write \(k_e=f_e^*\) for \(e\in R\). Since unused arcs were deleted, \(k_e>0\).

Define the incidence matrix \(B\) by putting \(+1\) at an arc’s tail and \(-1\) at its head, and let \(d=\mathbf e_a-\mathbf e_b\). A feasible exchange flow satisfies \(Bf=Fd\), where \(F\geq0\) is its unrestricted total value.

We use the standard rational linear programming theorem: feasibility and optimization, including obtaining an exact rational optimum when it is finite and attained, are computable in time polynomial in the dimensions and coefficient bit length.

Coalition separation is a linear program

For a proposed payment vector \(p\), define its maximum coalition violation by

\(\displaystyle \Phi(p)=\max_{0\leq z\leq\mu}\bigl(v(z)-p\cdot z\bigr).\)

The coalition and exchange-flow optimizations can be combined into the single LP

\(\displaystyle \max_{z,f,F}\ \sum_{e\in E}c_ef_e-\sum_{t\in T}p_tz_t\)

subject to

  • \(Bf=Fd\);
  • \(f_e\leq u_ez_t\) for \(e\in E_t\);
  • \(f_e\leq k_e\) for \(e\in R\);
  • \(z_t\leq\mu_t\);
  • \(f,z,F\geq0\).

For each fixed \(z\), its inner optimization is precisely the definition of \(v(z)\), so its optimum is \(\Phi(p)\).

This LP is feasible at zero. Every flow coordinate is bounded by its capacity at \(\mu\), every coalition coordinate is bounded, and \(F\) is determined by \(Bf=Fd\). Thus its feasible set is bounded and its optimum is attained. The zero solution also shows \(\Phi(p)\geq0\). Consequently, coalitional rationality is equivalent to \(\Phi(p)=0\).

Dualizing gives a compact description

Introduce unrestricted vertex variables \(y_v\), nonnegative contract multipliers \(\lambda_e\), nonnegative fixed-capacity multipliers \(\gamma_e\), and nonnegative coalition-bound multipliers \(\eta_t\).

The dual minimizes

\(\displaystyle \sum_{e\in R}k_e\gamma_e+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\eta_t\)

subject to

  • \(y_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}-y_{\operatorname{head}(e)}+\lambda_e\geq0\), for \(e\in C\);
  • \(y_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}-y_{\operatorname{head}(e)}+\gamma_e\geq c_e\), for \(e\in R\);
  • \(y_a-y_b\leq0\);
  • \(p_t+\eta_t\geq\sum_{e\in E_t}u_e\lambda_e\), for \(t\in T\);
  • \(\lambda,\gamma,\eta\geq0\).

For verification, the Lagrangian is

\(\displaystyle c\cdot f-p\cdot z+y^\top(Fd-Bf)+\sum_{e\in E_t}\lambda_e(u_ez_t-f_e)+\sum_{e\in R}\gamma_e(k_e-f_e)+\sum_t\eta_t(\mu_t-z_t).\)

Requiring every coefficient of the nonnegative primal variables to be nonpositive gives exactly the displayed dual constraints.

By strong LP duality, \(\Phi(p)=0\) precisely when this dual has an optimum of zero. All coefficients in its objective are strictly positive. Therefore, at a zero optimum, \(\gamma_e=\eta_t=0\).

It follows that the continuous core is exactly the projection onto \(p\) of the following rational polyhedron:

  • \(p_t\geq0\), for every \(t\);
  • \(\sum_t\mu_tp_t=P\);
  • \(y_a\leq y_b\);
  • \(y_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}-y_{\operatorname{head}(e)}\geq c_e\), for \(e\in R\);
  • \(y_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}-y_{\operatorname{head}(e)}+\lambda_e\geq0\), for \(e\in C\);
  • \(\lambda_e\geq0\), for \(e\in C\);
  • \(p_t\geq\sum_{e\in E_t}u_e\lambda_e\), for every \(t\).

There are \(O(|V|+m+\tau)\) variables and constraints, with polynomial input bit length. Here \(P=\sum_ec_ef_e^*\) is known from the supplied optimal solution. Thus core emptiness is decidable by one polynomial-size LP.

For an additional direct check of sufficiency, these inequalities imply, for every feasible coalition flow,

\(\displaystyle c\cdot f\leq y^\top Bf+\sum_{e\in C}\lambda_ef_e=F(y_a-y_b)+\sum_{e\in C}\lambda_ef_e\leq\sum_tz_t\sum_{e\in E_t}u_e\lambda_e\leq p\cdot z.\)

Taking the maximum over feasible flows establishes every coalition inequality.

2. Exact computation of the weighted quantile optimum

Suppose the core \(\mathcal C\) is nonempty. It is a closed convex polyhedron, since the preceding formulation is a polyhedral projection. Efficiency and positivity of the masses give

\(\displaystyle 0\leq p_t\leq P/\mu_t.\)

Hence \(\mathcal C\) is compact.

For \(p\in\mathcal C\), its increasing payment quantile is the step function on total mass \(\sum_t\mu_t\) obtained by ordering the payments, with payment \(p_t\) occupying mass \(\mu_t\).

The following successive LP procedure computes its unique lexicographic maximum.

Procedure

Maintain a nonempty feasible set \(K\subseteq\mathcal C\) and a set \(U\) of unfrozen types. Initially, \(K=\mathcal C\) and \(U=T\).

At each stage:

  • Solve

\(\displaystyle \alpha=\max\{r:p\in K,\ p_t\geq r\text{ for all }t\in U\}.\)

  • Set

\(\displaystyle K'=\{p\in K:p_t\geq\alpha\text{ for all }t\in U\}.\)

  • For every \(t\in U\), maximize \(p_t\) over \(K'\). Freeze exactly the types in

\(\displaystyle I=\{t\in U:\max_{p\in K'}p_t=\alpha\}.\)

  • Replace \(K\) by \(K'\) and \(U\) by \(U\setminus I\).

A frozen coordinate equals \(\alpha\) throughout \(K'\).

Every stage freezes a type

Suppose \(I\) were empty. For each \(t\in U\), choose \(p^{(t)}\in K'\) with \(p^{(t)}_t>\alpha\). Every coordinate indexed by \(U\) is at least \(\alpha\) in every chosen vector.

Their average therefore belongs to \(K'\) and has every coordinate indexed by \(U\) strictly greater than \(\alpha\). This contradicts the definition of \(\alpha\).

Thus at least one type freezes per stage, and there are at most \(\tau\) stages.

If unfrozen types remain, the same averaging argument, applied only to those types, gives a vector in \(K'\) whose remaining coordinates all exceed \(\alpha\). Consequently, successive freezing levels are strictly increasing.

At termination every coordinate is frozen, so the final feasible set consists of one payment vector \(p^*\).

Why this maximizes the weighted quantile

Let \(q\in\mathcal C\) differ from \(p^*\). There is a first stage at which \(q\) fails to belong to the newly restricted feasible set. At that stage:

  • all previously frozen payments of \(q\) agree with those of \(p^*\);
  • those common payments are below the current level \(\alpha\);
  • some remaining payment of \(q\) is below \(\alpha\);
  • all remaining payments of \(p^*\) are at least \(\alpha\).

After their common frozen mass, the quantile of \(q\) therefore takes a smaller value on a positive-length interval before the remaining quantile of \(p^*\) does. Positivity of every \(\mu_t\) ensures that this interval has positive mass.

Thus \(q\) is lexicographically worse than \(p^*\). This proves both optimality and uniqueness, for arbitrary positive rational masses.

Bit complexity

The procedure uses \(O(\tau^2)\) LP optimizations. Their matrices contain only the original coefficients and additional coefficients from \(\{0,1,-1\}\); previously computed levels occur only in right-hand sides.

To check that these right-hand sides retain polynomial bit length, clear denominators in the original coefficient matrix. Each newly fixed level has a denominator dividing the previous common right-hand-side denominator multiplied by an integer basis determinant. Hadamard’s determinant bound gives polynomial bit length for each such factor. There are at most \(\tau\) stages, and every fixed level lies between zero and \(\max_t P/\mu_t\). Hence both denominators and numerators have polynomial bit length.

Therefore the entire procedure runs in polynomial time and returns the exact rational allocation.

3. A counterexample to minimum deviation

Take three types with

\(\displaystyle \mu_1=\mu_2=\mu_3=1.\)

Use vertices \(a,r_1,r_3,x,y,q,b\) and the following arcs. In this table, the capacity at \(\mu\) and the winning flow are equal in every row.

| Arc | Ownership | Capacity at \(\mu\) and winning flow | Profit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| \((a,r_1)\) | Type \(1\) | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| \((r_1,x)\) | Fixed | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| \((a,r_3)\) | Type \(3\) | \(2\) | \(0\) |
| \((r_3,x)\) | Fixed | \(2\) | \(0\) |
| \((x,y)\) | Type \(2\) | \(3\) | \(0\) |
| \((y,b)\) | Fixed | \(3\) | \(2/3\) |
| \((a,q)\) | Type \(3\) | \(3\) | \(0\) |
| \((q,b)\) | Fixed | \(3\) | \(1\) |

For contract arcs, the table entries specify their per-person capacities, since every mass is one.

This is an acyclic network. The supplied positive flows satisfy conservation, and no arc is unused. The branch through \(x,y\) contributes profit \(2\), and the branch through \(q\) contributes profit \(3\). These exhaust both profitable fixed arcs, so the winning flow is optimal and \(P=5\).

For every \(z\in[0,1]^3\),

\(\displaystyle v(z)=\frac23\min\{z_1+2z_3,\,3z_2\}+3z_3.\)

Indeed, the first branch can carry exactly any amount up to the displayed minimum: its incoming contract capacities sum to \(z_1+2z_3\), and its outgoing contract capacity is \(3z_2\). Its fixed arcs impose no smaller bound on this box. The second branch independently carries \(3z_3\).

Its entire continuous core

We claim that the core is exactly

\(\displaystyle \mathcal C=\{p(s)=(s,\,2-3s,\,3+2s):0\leq s\leq2/3\}.\)

Necessity. Efficiency gives \(p_1+p_2+p_3=5\).

The coalition \(z=(1,1/3,0)\) has value \(2/3\), so

\(\displaystyle 3p_1+p_2\geq2.\)

The coalition \(z=(0,2/3,1)\) has value \(13/3\), so

\(\displaystyle 2p_2+3p_3\geq13.\)

Substituting efficiency into these inequalities gives, respectively,

\(\displaystyle p_3\leq3+2p_1,\qquad p_3\geq3+2p_1.\)

Thus \(p_3=3+2p_1\) and \(p_2=2-3p_1\). Nonnegativity gives \(0\leq p_1\leq2/3\).

Sufficiency. Let \(s\in[0,2/3]\) and put \(\theta=3s/2\in[0,1]\). Then

\(\displaystyle p(s)\cdot z=3z_3+\theta\,\frac23(z_1+2z_3)+(1-\theta)\,2z_2.\)

A convex combination of two numbers is at least their minimum. Therefore,

\(\displaystyle p(s)\cdot z\geq3z_3+\min\left\{\frac23(z_1+2z_3),\,2z_2\right\}=v(z).\)

Efficiency and nonnegativity also hold, proving the claim.

Egalitarian allocation versus minimum range

Throughout this segment, \(p_3\) is the largest payment. The smallest payment is

\(\displaystyle \min_t p_t(s)=\min\{s,\,2-3s\}.\)

This has its unique maximum at \(s=1/2\). Because the first quantile interval records the minimum payment, the unique egalitarian allocation is consequently

\(\displaystyle p^{\mathrm{EA}}=(1/2,\,1/2,\,4),\qquad \Delta(p^{\mathrm{EA}})=7/2.\)

However, its range along the entire core is

  • \(\Delta(p(s))=3+s\), for \(0\leq s\leq1/2\);
  • \(\Delta(p(s))=1+5s\), for \(1/2\leq s\leq2/3\).

Both expressions increase with \(s\). The unique minimum-range allocation is therefore

\(\displaystyle p(0)=(0,\,2,\,3),\qquad \Delta(p(0))=3<7/2.\)

This disproves minimum-deviation characterization under the stated assumptions. The counterexample already has unit masses; its essential feature is the real coalition constraints with the permitted contract bundles. The finite-contract characterizations in [Heller–Krumke](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.15389) and [Heller’s thesis](https://publica-rest.fraunhofer.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/d4f6391c-b1a9-4712-bbac-1e5618ae8e61/content) therefore cannot be transferred to this coalition value function from the fixed-winning-schedule assumption alone.

4. Why an ordinary maximum-flow representation also fails

Here “ordinary maximum-flow representation” means a capacitated network whose sink has one terminal arc per type, and whose maximum-flow terminal vectors are exactly \((\mu_tp_t)_{t\in T}\) for the core allocations. It permits no additional coupling constraints beyond capacities and flow conservation.

Such terminal vectors satisfy a pairwise exchange property.

To prove this, let \(F,G\) be two maximum flows with terminal vectors \(x,y\), and suppose \(y_i>x_i\). Orient each positive difference \(G_e-F_e\) forward and each negative difference backward, with magnitude \(|G_e-F_e|\).

Because the two maximum flows have equal total value, these differences form a nonnegative circulation in the residual network of \(F\). A nonnegative circulation decomposes into directed simple cycles. A forward difference on terminal arc \(i\) therefore belongs to such a cycle.

That cycle enters the sink through terminal arc \(i\) and leaves through the reverse of some terminal arc \(j\). Simplicity prevents another visit to the sink, so augmenting a sufficiently small positive amount around this cycle changes only those two terminal coordinates:

\(\displaystyle x\longmapsto x+\varepsilon\mathbf e_i-\varepsilon\mathbf e_j.\)

It preserves feasibility and total flow value, and hence remains a maximum flow.

Apply this to the counterexample, whose masses are all one. Choose

\(\displaystyle x=p(0)=(0,2,3),\qquad y=p(s)=(s,2-3s,3+2s)\)

with \(0<s<2/3\). Terminal coordinates \(1\) and \(3\) increase, while only coordinate \(2\) decreases. The cycle containing the forward difference on terminal arc \(1\) must therefore leave the sink through the reverse of terminal arc \(2\). It produces another maximum-flow terminal vector

\(\displaystyle (\varepsilon,\,2-\varepsilon,\,3).\)

But this vector is outside the core: every core vector satisfies \(p_3=3+2p_1\).

This contradiction excludes any ordinary maximum-flow network whose terminal vectors are exactly this core. In particular, the requested flow characterization with minimum \(\Delta(p)\) fails, while exact computation remains polynomial through the LP formulation.
