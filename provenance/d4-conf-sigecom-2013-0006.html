The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_{g,v}\) over finitely many cohort-value types, a monotone service-cost oracle \(C\) on served-mass vectors, and a black-box baseline allocation rule \(\mathcal A\) for the aggregate population, can one compute in time polynomial in \(1/\epsilon\), support size \(\tau\), input bit length \(L\), and oracle runtime a BIC, individually rational mechanism that recovers expected cost and has expected social cost at most \(O(\log h)\,\mathbb E[SC(\mathcal A)]+\epsilon\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) cohort-value types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_{g,v}\) a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\) public cohorts, total mass \(\sum_{g,v}\mu_{g,v}=1\), and rational private values \(v\in[0,1]\), with at least one positive value; set \(h=v_{\max}/v_{\min}\), using the largest and smallest positive supported values.
For cohort \(g\), permitted reports are its supported values and zero. Individuals are nonatomic: a unilateral report changes neither the aggregate society nor the public cohort. Mechanisms treat individuals with the same cohort and report identically in distribution.
A randomized allocation specifies served fractions \(x_{g,v}\in[0,1]\); its served-mass vector is \(z_g=\sum_v\mu_{g,v}x_{g,v}\). An exact oracle returns nonnegative rational \(C(z)\), coordinatewise monotone on \(\prod_g[0,\sum_v\mu_{g,v}]\), with \(C(0)=0\); no continuity or convexity is assumed.
The arbitrary, possibly randomized baseline oracle \(\mathcal A\) accepts rational aggregate report distributions with the same cohort totals and permitted reports, and returns feasible rational served fractions. Both oracles have finite binary outputs; let \(T\) denote their runtime bounds and \(L\) the input bit length.
Let \(p_{g,v}\) be payments per unit mass. BIC requires \(v\bar x_{g,v}-\bar p_{g,v}\geq v\bar x_{g,w}-\bar p_{g,w}\) for every permitted \(v,w\) within a cohort, where bars average all mechanism randomness at the truthful aggregate society. Require individual rationality in every realization: only served individuals pay, with nonnegative charges at most their truthful value.
Define \(SC=C(z)+\sum_{g,v}\mu_{g,v}v(1-x_{g,v})\); expected cost recovery means \(\mathbb E[\sum_{g,v}\mu_{g,v}p_{g,v}]\geq\mathbb E[C(z)]\). Costs are evaluated on realized served masses before taking expectations.

Does a black-box transformation, with construction and execution time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon,T\), always produce such a BIC, individually rational, expected-cost-recovering mechanism satisfying \(\mathbb E[SC(\mathcal M)]\leq K(1+\log h)\mathbb E[SC(\mathcal A)]+\epsilon\) for a universal constant \(K\), or is this approximation guarantee unattainable with polynomial oracle access?

For finite independent agents, a polynomial-time black-box transformation achieves BIC, individual rationality, expected cost recovery, and social-cost inflation \(O(1+\min\{\log h,\log n\})\), plus arbitrary additive error ([source](https://www.fuhuthu.com/cost-recovery.pdf)). No result settling this exact nonatomic oracle model was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one and values to the interval [0,1]; allowed zero values and required a nonempty positive-value support to define h.
  • Interpreted cohorts as public identities and private information as value only; deviations are by nonatomic individuals, not entire cohorts.
  • Restricted reports to each cohort's finite supported values together with zero.
  • Defined service costs on cohort totals of realized served mass, with nonnegative costs and zero cost for serving nobody.
  • Specified symmetric treatment within cohort-report types, fractional service, per-unit-mass payments, and expectations over mechanism randomness rather than random aggregate populations.
  • Carried over individual rationality and nonnegative payments through charges only to served individuals, bounded by their truthful values.
  • Specified exact rational cost queries, rational allocation outputs, and baseline queries on aggregate report distributions preserving cohort totals.
  • Used 1+log h to include h=1 without a zero approximation factor.
  • Required polynomial construction and execution time, including oracle runtimes and binary input length.

Result

The guarantee is attainable with polynomial oracle access. There is a transformation using one call to \(\mathcal A\) and \(O(L)\) calls to \(C\) that satisfies BIC, individual rationality in every realization, and cost recovery in every realization. It achieves

\(\mathbb E[SC(\mathcal M)]\leq 3(1+\ln h)\mathbb E[SC(\mathcal A)]\).

Thus no additive error is needed.

The key nonatomic feature is that a unilateral report leaves the aggregate distribution unchanged. Consequently, allocation schedules and prices computed from that aggregate remain fixed when checking an individual’s incentive constraints.

1. Reassigning service within each cohort

Fix one realization of the baseline at the truthful aggregate society. Write its allocations as \(x_{g,v}\), its served masses as \(z_g\), and its cost as \(c=C(z)\).

For each cohort, define

\(b_g=\sum_{v>0}\mu_{g,v},\qquad q_g=\min\{z_g,b_g\}\).

Construct an allocation \(a\) by assigning exactly mass \(q_g\) to the highest positive values in cohort \(g\): serve higher values completely, at most one boundary value fractionally, and lower values not at all. Set \(a_{g,0}=0\), including when zero is a permitted but unsupported report. If \(q_g=0\), set the entire cohort’s schedule to zero.

This gives a nondecreasing allocation schedule on every cohort’s permitted reports. Its served-mass vector is \(q\), so coordinatewise monotonicity of the cost gives

\(C(q)\leq C(z)=c\).

We next verify that this reassignment does not decrease served value.

Fix a cohort with positive supported values \(u_1<\cdots<u_r\). Let \(s_i=\mu_{g,u_i}x_{g,u_i}\), let \(r_g=\sum_i s_i\), and define the tail masses

\(B_i=\sum_{\ell=i}^r\mu_{g,u_\ell},\qquad S_i=\sum_{\ell=i}^r s_\ell\).

Since \(r_g\leq q_g\), we have \(S_i\leq\min\{q_g,B_i\}\). The highest-value-first allocation has served tail mass exactly \(\min\{q_g,B_i\}\). The telescoping identity

\(\sum_{i=1}^r u_i s_i=u_1r_g+\sum_{i=2}^r(u_i-u_{i-1})S_i\)

therefore implies

\(\sum_v\mu_{g,v}v a_{g,v}\geq\sum_v\mu_{g,v}v x_{g,v}\).

The claim is immediate for cohorts with no positive supported values. Summing over cohorts gives

\(U(a)\leq U(x)\),

where \(U(y)=\sum_{g,v}\mu_{g,v}v(1-y_{g,v})\) denotes unserved value.

2. Exact incentive-compatible payments

Consider any nondecreasing allocation schedule \(y\) in a cohort. List its permitted reports as

\(0=u_0<u_1<\cdots<u_r\),

and suppose \(y_0=0\). Define payments per unit mass by

\(p_0=0,\qquad p_i=\sum_{\ell=1}^i u_\ell(y_\ell-y_{\ell-1})\).

These payments satisfy all incentive constraints.

Indeed, if \(i>j\), the truthful utility advantage of type \(u_i\) over report \(u_j\) is

\(u_i(y_i-y_j)-(p_i-p_j)=\sum_{\ell=j+1}^i(u_i-u_\ell)(y_\ell-y_{\ell-1})\geq0\).

If \(i<j\), that advantage is

\((p_j-p_i)-u_i(y_j-y_i)=\sum_{\ell=i+1}^j(u_\ell-u_i)(y_\ell-y_{\ell-1})\geq0\).

Both inequalities follow from monotonicity of \(y\).

Furthermore,

\(0\leq p_i\leq u_i\sum_{\ell=1}^i(y_\ell-y_{\ell-1})=u_i y_i\).

Implement the payment by charging each served individual \(p_i/y_i\) when \(y_i>0\), and charging nothing when unserved. Every truthful served individual then pays a nonnegative amount at most their value. This proves individual rationality in every realization. In the nonatomic fractional-allocation model, the class’s total payment is exactly \(\mu_{g,u_i}p_i\).

We will also use the following consequence: if \(y_i=0\) whenever \(u_i<t\), then

\(p_i\geq t y_i\).

To see this, every nonzero increment \(y_\ell-y_{\ell-1}\) occurs at a report \(u_\ell\geq t\), so the payment formula gives the inequality directly.

3. The transformation

Let

\(v_{\min}=\min\{v>0:\mu_{g,v}>0\text{ for some }g\},\qquad J=1+\lfloor\log_2 h\rfloor\).

Define thresholds

\(t_j=2^jv_{\min},\qquad j=0,\ldots,J\).

In particular, \(t_J>v_{\max}\).

After obtaining the baseline realization and constructing \(a\) as above, form, for each threshold,

\(y^j_{g,v}=a_{g,v}\mathbf 1_{\{v\geq t_j\}},\qquad z^j_g=\sum_v\mu_{g,v}y^j_{g,v}\).

Every \(y^j\) is nondecreasing within each cohort and is zero at report zero. Compute its payments \(p^j\) using Section 2, and its revenue

\(R_j=\sum_{g,v}\mu_{g,v}p^j_{g,v}\).

Choose the smallest index \(k\) satisfying

\(R_k\geq C(z^k)\).

Such an index always exists: at \(j=J\), allocation, revenue, and cost are all zero.

Output allocation \(y^k\), charging served individuals \(p^k_{g,v}/y^k_{g,v}\) and unserved individuals zero.

All these computations use exact rational arithmetic.

4. Incentives, individual rationality, and cost recovery

Fix the truthful aggregate society and condition on a realization of the baseline oracle’s randomness.

A unilateral report changes neither that aggregate nor the individual’s public cohort. Hence it changes none of the computed objects \(a\), \(k\), or the cohort’s allocation and payment schedules. An individual reporting \(w\) receives service probability \(y^k_{g,w}\) and expected payment \(p^k_{g,w}\).

Section 2 proves the incentive inequalities conditional on this baseline realization. Averaging them over the baseline randomness proves precisely the required BIC inequalities.

Section 2 also proves individual rationality in every realization: unserved individuals pay zero, and truthful served individuals pay between zero and their value.

Finally, the selected threshold satisfies

\(\sum_{g,v}\mu_{g,v}p^k_{g,v}=R_k\geq C(z^k)\).

Thus costs are recovered in each realization of the baseline. Taking expectations gives expected cost recovery. The cost here is evaluated at the realized served-mass vector \(z^k\), before averaging.

5. Social-cost bound

Continue to fix one baseline realization.

Because \(k\) is the first successful threshold, every \(j<k\) satisfies

\(R_j<C(z^j)\).

Also \(z^j\leq q\leq z\) coordinatewise, so

\(C(z^j)\leq c\).

Let the served value discarded between consecutive thresholds be

\(D_j=\sum_{g,v:\,t_j\leq v<t_{j+1}}\mu_{g,v}v a_{g,v}\).

Since \(t_{j+1}=2t_j\), and Section 2 gives \(p^j_{g,v}\geq t_jy^j_{g,v}\),

\(D_j\leq 2t_j\sum_{g,v:\,t_j\leq v<t_{j+1}}\mu_{g,v}a_{g,v}\leq 2t_j\sum_{g,v}\mu_{g,v}y^j_{g,v}\leq 2R_j\).

For each failed threshold, therefore,

\(D_j\leq2c\).

All positive supported values are at least \(t_0\). Consequently, the service discarded by the chosen threshold is partitioned by these bands:

\(U(y^k)=U(a)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}D_j\leq U(x)+2kc\).

Combining this with \(C(z^k)\leq c\) yields

\(SC(\mathcal M)\leq U(x)+(1+2k)c\leq(1+2J)SC(\mathcal A)\).

Finally,

\(1+2J\leq3+2\log_2h=3+\frac{2}{\ln2}\ln h\leq3(1+\ln h)\).

This bound holds separately for every baseline realization. Averaging proves

\(\mathbb E[SC(\mathcal M)]\leq3(1+\ln h)\mathbb E[SC(\mathcal A)]\).

No continuity or convexity assumption was used.

6. Oracle access and running time

The transformation makes one baseline query and at most \(J+1\) cost queries.

Because the positive rational values are encoded in the input and lie in \([0,1]\), their denominators have at most \(L\) bits. Hence \(v_{\min}\geq2^{-L}\), giving \(h\leq2^L\) and \(J=O(L)\).

Sorting the supported values, computing the allocations and payments, and comparing revenues with costs require polynomially many rational arithmetic operations. Every constructed rational has bit length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,T\): it is obtained through polynomially many sums, products, and quotients of input rationals and oracle outputs. The resulting construction and execution time is therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,T\), and consequently also in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon,T\).

The support is handled explicitly; the algorithm never enumerates unsupported cohort-value types or computes expectations of the randomized baseline.
