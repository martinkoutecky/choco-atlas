The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite graph \(H=(V,E)\) and positive rational type masses \(\mu_e\) summing to \(1\), let \(x_e\in[0,\mu_e]\) describe a subpopulation and let its cost be \(\tau(H[\operatorname{supp}(x)])\). Decide whether there are nonnegative per-unit densities \(p_e(x)\), for every nonzero \(x\) and each \(e\in\operatorname{supp}(x)\), such that \(\sum_{e\in\operatorname{supp}(x)}x_ep_e(x)=\tau(H[\operatorname{supp}(x)])\) and \(p_e(x)\ge p_e(y)\) whenever \(x\le y\) and \(e\in\operatorname{supp}(x)\); in the yes case, give a concise evaluator for the densities.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(H=(V,E)\) be a finite undirected simple graph with \(E\ne\varnothing\), and put \(m=|V|\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|E|\) edge types with positive masses \(\mu_e\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_{e\in E}\mu_e=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For \(S\subseteq E\), let \(H[S]\) denote the edge-induced subgraph and \(\operatorname{vc}(H[S])\) its unweighted vertex cover number. A subpopulation is any real vector \(x\in\prod_{e\in E}[0,\mu_e]\), with \(\operatorname{supp}(x)=\{e:x_e>0\}\); its cost is \(c(x)=\operatorname{vc}(H[\operatorname{supp}(x)])\), with \(c(0)=0\).
A density scheme assigns finite nonnegative real numbers \(p_e(x)\) for every nonzero \(x\) and every \(e\in\operatorname{supp}(x)\), satisfying \(\sum_{e\in\operatorname{supp}(x)}x_ep_e(x)=c(x)\) and \(p_e(x)\ge p_e(y)\) whenever \(x\le y\) coordinatewise and \(e\in\operatorname{supp}(x)\). No continuity or uniform boundedness is required.

Is deciding whether such a density scheme exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? In yes instances, give a concise evaluator for the densities. State decision and construction running times in \(m,\tau,L\), and evaluation running times also in the bit length \(B\) of a rational query \(x\).

For discrete edge players, a population monotonic allocation scheme consists of shares \(a_{S,e}\) satisfying \(\sum_{e\in S}a_{S,e}=\operatorname{vc}(H[S])\) and \(a_{S,e}\ge a_{T,e}\) for \(S\subseteq T\) and \(e\in S\). Its existence is decidable in polynomial time, with a constructive scheme when one exists ([Xiao, Fang and Du](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02824)).
No result settling this exact continuous density problem was located in the literature search.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). More precisely, a density scheme exists if and only if every connected component of \(H\) containing an edge is a star or a double star, where a double star consists of two stars joined by an edge between their centers.

The criterion is independent of the positive masses \(\mu_e\). Below is a complete proof and an exact rational evaluator.

1. Necessity: restriction to full-mass subpopulations

Suppose a density scheme exists. For each nonempty \(S\subseteq E\), define \(x^S\) by \(x^S_e=\mu_e\) for \(e\in S\), and \(x^S_e=0\) otherwise. Define discrete shares by
\(a_{S,e}=\mu_e p_e(x^S)\) for \(e\in S\).

Because every \(\mu_e\) is positive, \(\operatorname{supp}(x^S)=S\). Budget balance therefore gives
\(\sum_{e\in S}a_{S,e}=\operatorname{vc}(H[S])\).

If \(S\subseteq T\), then \(x^S\le x^T\). Density monotonicity, multiplied by the same positive number \(\mu_e\), gives
\(a_{S,e}\ge a_{T,e}\) for every \(e\in S\).
Thus these shares constitute a discrete population monotonic allocation scheme.

Theorem 1 and Lemma 7 of Xiao, Fang and Du state that, for a finite undirected simple graph, its unweighted edge-player vertex-cover game admits such a scheme exactly when every connected component is a tree of diameter at most three. These hypotheses apply directly to \(H\) and the shares just constructed. Components containing edges are consequently stars or double stars. [Xiao, Fang and Du](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.02824v3)

2. Construction of the continuous densities

Assume every component containing an edge is a star or a double star. Define densities separately within each component.

Stars

For a star component \(C\), put \(X_C(x)=\sum_{f\in E(C)}x_f\). For every supported edge \(e\in E(C)\), define
\(p_e(x)=1/X_C(x)\).

The denominator is positive because \(x_e>0\).

Double stars

Let the centers be \(u,v\), let \(b=uv\) be their connecting edge, and let \(A_u,A_v\) be the respective sets of pendant edges. Put
\(X_u(x)=\sum_{f\in A_u}x_f\) and
\(X_v(x)=\sum_{f\in A_v}x_f\).

For each supported pendant edge, define:

  • \(p_e(x)=1/X_u(x)\) when \(e\in A_u\).
  • \(p_e(x)=1/X_v(x)\) when \(e\in A_v\).

If \(x_b>0\), define its density by:

  • \(p_b(x)=1/x_b\) when \(X_u(x)=X_v(x)=0\).
  • \(p_b(x)=0\) when \(X_u(x)+X_v(x)>0\).

Every denominator used is positive. Hence all assigned densities are finite and nonnegative.

3. Verification of the construction

Budget balance

For a star, a nonempty supported subgraph has vertex-cover number one: the center covers every edge, and at least one vertex is necessary. Its total payment is
\(\sum_{e\in E(C):\,x_e>0}x_ep_e(x)=X_C(x)/X_C(x)=1\).
An inactive star contributes neither cost nor payment.

For a double star, there are three cases.

  • Both pendant groups are active.

Choose one supported edge from each group. These two edges have disjoint endpoints, so every vertex cover has size at least two. The centers \(u,v\) cover all supported edges, so the cover number is exactly two. Each pendant group pays one, and the connecting edge pays zero.

  • Exactly one pendant group is active.

All supported edges, including the connecting edge if present, meet that group's center. Thus the cover number is one. The active pendant group pays one, and the connecting edge pays zero.

  • Neither pendant group is active.

The supported subgraph is either empty or consists solely of \(b\). Its cover number is respectively zero or one, exactly matching the prescribed payment.

Finally, vertex-cover numbers add across connected components: a cover restricts to a cover of each component, and the union of minimum component covers covers the whole graph. Payments also add across components. Therefore
\(\sum_{e\in\operatorname{supp}(x)}x_ep_e(x)=c(x)\).

Monotonicity

Let \(x\le y\), and let \(e\in\operatorname{supp}(x)\).

For a star edge or a pendant edge, its density is the reciprocal of its group's total mass. That total is positive at \(x\) and cannot decrease at \(y\). Consequently \(p_e(x)\ge p_e(y)\).

For a connecting edge \(b\), consider the pendant groups.

  • If a pendant group is active at \(x\), it remains active at \(y\), so both densities are zero.
  • If neither is active at \(x\), but one becomes active at \(y\), the density decreases from \(1/x_b\) to zero.
  • If neither is active at either vector, then \(0<x_b\le y_b\), giving \(1/x_b\ge1/y_b\).

These cases exhaust all possibilities. The construction is therefore a density scheme.

Its discontinuity when a pendant coordinate first becomes positive is permitted by the problem's hypotheses.

4. Decision, construction, and evaluation times

Recognition and construction

Build adjacency lists and traverse the connected components. Accept precisely when every component is a tree with at most two vertices of degree at least two.

To justify this test, in any tree the vertices of degree at least two induce a connected subgraph, if nonempty: every internal vertex on the unique path between two such vertices also has degree at least two. Thus:

  • A nontrivial tree with no such vertices is a single edge.
  • With one such vertex, it is a star.
  • With two, those vertices are adjacent and every other vertex is a leaf, so it is a double star.

Conversely, stars and double stars satisfy the test.

On acceptance, store each edge's group, and mark the connecting edges. This is the complete evaluator description; no coalition table is required.

Decision and construction each take \(O(m+\tau+L)\) time on a standard graph RAM, including reading the input. Charging binary operations explicitly gives the conservative bound
\(O\!\left(L+(m+\tau)\log(m+\tau+L+2)\right)\).
The evaluator description uses \(O(\tau)\) records.

Exact rational evaluation

For a valid rational query \(x\) of total bit length \(B\), scan the coordinates to compute all group sums, then return the reciprocals and connecting-edge densities prescribed above. This takes \(O(\tau)\) rational arithmetic and record operations.

An explicit bit bound is
\(O\!\left(\tau\bigl(B+\log(m+\tau+2)\bigr)^2\right)\).

Indeed, accumulate each group sum using unreduced fractions. Its denominator is a product of input denominators and has \(O(B)\) bits. Since \(\sum_e x_e\le\sum_e\mu_e=1\), its nonnegative numerator is at most its denominator and also has \(O(B)\) bits. Each addition therefore costs \(O(B^2)\) bit operations using schoolbook arithmetic. Taking a reciprocal exchanges numerator and denominator. All returned densities are exact rationals of \(O(B)\) bits each.

This evaluation bound assumes the query belongs to the stipulated domain; evaluation does not otherwise need the masses.
