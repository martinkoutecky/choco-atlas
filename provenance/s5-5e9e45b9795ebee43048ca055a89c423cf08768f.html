The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu\), good classes \(G\) with rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), additive values \(v_t\), and normalized influence weights \(a_t,k_{s,t}\), choose an integral bundle \(S_0\) for a tagged type-\(t\) agent and a distribution \(\pi\) over integral bundles for the remaining population. Require \(\sum_S\pi_S=1\), \(\sum_{S\ni g}\pi_S=q_g\), and \(S_0\subseteq\{g:q_g>0\}\); for couplings \(\lambda\) with row sums \(\mu_s\) and column sums \(\pi_S\), compute and return a maximizing pair \((S_0,\pi)\) for \(a_t v_t(S_0)+\min_{\lambda}\sum_{s,S} k_{s,t}\lambda_{s,S}v_t(S)\), with a minimizing coupling certificate.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_s\), where \(\sum_s\mu_s=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(G\) contain \(m\geq1\) good classes, with binary-rational supplies \(q_g\in[0,1]\). An integral bundle is a subset of \(G\), including the empty subset; each recipient can receive at most one unit of each class. Types have nonnegative binary-rational additive values \(v_s\).
Fix a tagged type \(t\in T\). Its individual agent has zero population mass, so its bundle does not reduce per-capita supplies. Nonnegative binary-rational influence coefficients satisfy \(a_t+\sum_s\mu_s k_{s,t}=1\); \(k_{s,t}\) is influence per unit mass of type \(s\).
Choose \(S_0\subseteq\{g:q_g>0\}\) and a bundle distribution \(\pi\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq G}\pi_S=1\) and \(\sum_{S\ni g}\pi_S=q_g\) for every \(g\).
Define \(\Lambda(\mu,\pi)=\{\lambda\geq0:\sum_S\lambda_{s,S}=\mu_s,\ \sum_s\lambda_{s,S}=\pi_S\}\) and \(F_t(S_0,\pi)=a_t v_t(S_0)+\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\mu,\pi)}\sum_{s,S}k_{s,t}\lambda_{s,S}v_t(S)\).
The computational problem is to return an exact maximizing pair \((S_0,\pi)\) and a minimizing coupling \(\lambda\), representing distributions and couplings by lists of their nonzero rational entries. Any optimizer is acceptable; \(L\) denotes total input bit length.

Is this exact optimization problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents, maximizing worst-assignment utility over partitions is NP-hard; finding a worst assignment for a fixed partition is polynomial-time, and network externalities admit a polynomial-time \(1/2\)-approximation. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.06191)
No result settling exactly the mass-and-coupling problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize population mass to one, as required by the specified distribution and coupling marginals.
  • Interpret integral bundles as subsets of good classes, allowing empty bundles and at most one unit of each class; restrict per-capita supplies to [0,1].
  • Use nonnegative binary-rational additive values and influence coefficients, consistent with goods and positive externalities.
  • Interpret influence coefficients as densities and normalize them by a_t + sum_s mu_s k_{s,t} = 1.
  • Treat the tagged individual as having zero population mass; its chosen bundle does not reduce per-capita supplies.
  • Represent returned distributions and minimizing couplings by their nonzero rational entries, avoiding mandatory enumeration of every bundle.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The exact optimization problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with only two types, every supply equal to \(\tfrac12\), and strictly positive self-influence and influence coefficients. The proof gives an explicit formula for the optimum on the constructed instances, each of which admits an optimizer using only two bundles.

1. Reduction and hypothesis checks

We reduce from \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\): given positive binary-encoded integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\), decide whether some subset sums to half their total. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; see [these KTH lecture notes, page 9](https://www.csc.kth.se/utbildning/kth/kurser/DD2352/algokomp12/Forelasningar/F10.pdf).

Write \(W=\sum_{g=1}^{m}w_g\), and, for a bundle \(S\), write \(w(S)=\sum_{g\in S}w_g\).

Construct the following society:

  • \(G=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(q_g=\tfrac12\) for every good.
  • \(T=\{t,u\}\), with \(\mu_t=\mu_u=\tfrac12\).
  • The tagged type has additive values \(v_t(\{g\})=w_g\); the other type has \(v_u(\{g\})=0\).
  • \(a_t=\tfrac12\), \(k_{t,t}=\tfrac34\), and \(k_{u,t}=\tfrac14\).

These are valid binary-rational data. The two types are distinct, their masses are positive, and the required normalization holds:

\[ \text{ } \]

\(a_t+\mu_tk_{t,t}+\mu_uk_{u,t}=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac34+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac14=1.\)

The construction has encoding length polynomial in the \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) input length.

Since every supply is positive, \(S_0=G\) is permitted. Since the tagged individual has zero population mass, this choice does not affect the supply constraints. Its direct utility is \(W/2\), the largest possible direct utility.

2. Rewrite the minimizing coupling

Fix any feasible distribution \(\pi\). Its expected bundle value is fixed by the supplies:

\(\sum_S\pi_Sw(S)=\sum_gw_g\sum_{S\ni g}\pi_S=\tfrac12\sum_gw_g=W/2.\)

Put \(c=W/2\).

Because there are exactly two types, a coupling is determined by \(z_S=\lambda_{t,S}\). Its constraints are precisely

\(0\le z_S\le\pi_S,\qquad \sum_Sz_S=\tfrac12,\)

and then \(\lambda_{u,S}=\pi_S-z_S\). Indeed, these equations give both column marginals and both row marginals.

Define

\(H(\pi)=\min\bigl\{\sum_Sz_Sw(S):0\le z_S\le\pi_S,\ \sum_Sz_S=\tfrac12\bigr\}.\)

The feasible set is nonempty, since \(z_S=\pi_S/2\) is feasible, and compact, so the minimum exists. Substitution into the externality term gives

\(\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\mu,\pi)}\sum_{s,S}k_{s,t}\lambda_{s,S}w(S)=\tfrac14\sum_S\pi_Sw(S)+\tfrac12H(\pi)=W/8+\tfrac12H(\pi).\)

Consequently,

\(F_t(S_0,\pi)=\tfrac12w(S_0)+W/8+\tfrac12H(\pi).\)

3. Exact upper bound

Define the minimum partition imbalance

\(D=\min_{S\subseteq G}|2w(S)-W|,\)

and put \(d=D/2\). Every bundle satisfies \(|w(S)-c|\ge d\).

We prove that every feasible distribution satisfies

\(H(\pi)\le(c-d)/2=(W-D)/4.\)

If \(d=0\), use the feasible choice \(z_S=\pi_S/2\). Its objective value is \(c/2\), proving the bound.

Now suppose \(d>0\). Let \(\mathcal L=\{S:w(S)<c\}\) and \(\mathcal U=\{S:w(S)>c\}\). These sets contain all bundles, since none has value \(c\). Moreover,

\(w(S)\le c-d\) for \(S\in\mathcal L\), and \(w(S)\ge c+d\) for \(S\in\mathcal U\).

There are two cases.

  • The total \(\pi\)-mass of \(\mathcal L\) is at least \(\tfrac12\).

Choose \(z\) supported on \(\mathcal L\), with \(0\le z_S\le\pi_S\) and total mass \(\tfrac12\). Such a choice exists by taking mass from these finitely many bundles until reaching \(\tfrac12\), splitting the last entry if necessary. Then
\(\sum_Sz_Sw(S)\le(c-d)/2\).

  • The total \(\pi\)-mass of \(\mathcal L\) is less than \(\tfrac12\).

The mass of \(\mathcal U\) exceeds \(\tfrac12\). Choose \(r\) supported there, with \(0\le r_S\le\pi_S\) and total mass \(\tfrac12\). Set \(z=\pi-r\). This is feasible, and
\(\sum_Sz_Sw(S)=c-\sum_Sr_Sw(S)\le c-(c+d)/2=(c-d)/2\).

This proves the bound in every case. Since \(w(S_0)\le W\), it follows that

\(F_t(S_0,\pi)\le W/2+W/8+(W-D)/8=(6W-D)/8.\)

4. Attainment with two bundles

Choose a subset attaining the minimum imbalance. Replacing it by its complement if necessary gives a subset \(A\) with

\(w(A)=(W-D)/2,\qquad w(G\setminus A)=(W+D)/2.\)

Write \(B=G\setminus A\), and choose

\(S_0=G,\qquad \pi_A=\pi_B=\tfrac12,\)

with all other distribution entries zero. Each good belongs to exactly one of \(A,B\), so its marginal supply is exactly \(\tfrac12\).

A minimizing coupling is

\(\lambda_{t,A}=\tfrac12,\qquad \lambda_{u,B}=\tfrac12,\)

with all other entries zero. To verify minimality, write \(z_A=x\) and \(z_B=\tfrac12-x\), where \(0\le x\le\tfrac12\). The objective defining \(H\) becomes

\(\tfrac12w(B)+x\bigl(w(A)-w(B)\bigr).\)

Because \(w(A)\le w(B)\), this is minimized at \(x=\tfrac12\). Therefore

\(H(\pi)=\tfrac12w(A)=(W-D)/4.\)

This distribution and coupling attain the preceding upper bound. Thus the constructed instance has exact optimum

\(\mathrm{OPT}=(6W-D)/8.\)

5. Complexity consequence

The \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) instance is a yes-instance exactly when \(D=0\), equivalently when

\(\mathrm{OPT}=3W/4.\)

Suppose an algorithm returned an exact maximizing pair and a minimizing coupling in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Apply it to the constructed instance and evaluate the objective from its returned rational entries. Polynomial running time bounds the output length, so this evaluation and comparison with \(3W/4\) take polynomial time using exact rational arithmetic.

The equivalence above would then decide \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) in polynomial time. Hence the requested exact optimization problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and has no polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The hardness already holds with \(\tau=2\) and fixed influence coefficients. Moreover, every constructed instance has an optimum represented by two distribution entries and two coupling entries, so the reduction does not depend on large output supports.
