The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given schools \(S\), rational per-capita capacities \(q_s\), and finitely many types \(t\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), each type has a path-independent choice function \(C_t\), an entering or continuing status, a previous bundle \(B_t^-\) if continuing, and a priority tier at each school. Priority tiers are strictly ordered, with mass within a tier divisible at a capacity cutoff. Find \(x_{t,B}\geq 0\), the mass of type \(t\) assigned bundle \(B\subseteq S\), such that \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), school capacities are respected, every positively used bundle is individually and dynamically rational, and the allocation is non-wasteful and has no justified claims. A justified claim occurs when positive mass assigned bundle \(B\) would choose to add school \(s\), yet positive mass assigned to \(s\) from a lower-priority type had no tenure at \(s\) in the previous period.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) schools with nonnegative binary-rational capacities \(q_s\). A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has an entering or continuing status, a previous bundle \(B_t^-\subseteq S\), a path-independent choice function \(C_t:2^S\to2^S\) with \(C_t(A)\subseteq A\), supplied by its full table, and a priority tier at each school. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including these tables.
Entrants have \(B_t^-=\varnothing\). Previous bundles satisfy \(C_t(B_t^-)=B_t^-\) and \(\sum_{t:s\in B_t^-}\mu_t\leq q_s\). Tenure at \(s\) means precisely \(s\in B_t^-\).
School priority tiers are strictly ordered; write \(t>_s u\) when \(t\)'s tier is higher. Mass is divisible, and equal-tier comparisons create no claims; no additional tie-breaking or proportional splitting is imposed.
An allocation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,B}\), indexed by \(t\) and \(B\subseteq S\), with \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and school loads \(a_s=\sum_t\sum_{B:s\in B}x_{t,B}\leq q_s\).
Every \(x_{t,B}>0\) must satisfy individual rationality \(C_t(B)=B\), and, for continuing types, dynamic rationality \(C_t(B\cup B_t^-)=B\).
A positively assigned bundle \(B\) demands \(s\) if \(s\notin B\) and \(s\in C_t(B\cup\{s\})\). Non-wastefulness requires \(a_s=q_s\) whenever such demand exists.
A justified claim exists if some \(x_{t,B}>0\) demands \(s\), and some \(x_{u,D}>0\) satisfies \(s\in D\), \(t>_s u\), and \(s\notin B_u^-\). An allocation is dynamically stable when all preceding requirements hold and no justified claim exists.

Can a dynamically stable allocation be computed, or its nonexistence reported, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite teachers with strict school priorities and path-independent choices, tenure-respecting deferred acceptance always computes a dynamically stable matching ([Amieva–Bonifacio–Neme](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.07851v2)).
Continuum many-to-many models have existence results for pairwise and tree stability ([Greinecker–Vocke](https://www2.uibk.ac.at/downloads/c9821000/wpaper/2026-02.pdf)); these concern different models and stability notions. No result settling the exact computational problem above was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The representation of choice functions was unspecified; use full choice tables and include their size in the total input bit length.
  • Capacities are nonnegative binary rationals, without an upper bound; the school set is nonempty.
  • Require the previous assignment restricted to present types to respect current capacities and individual rationality, as a previous feasible matching does in the discrete model.
  • Entrants have empty previous bundles; continuing types may have empty previous bundles, in which case dynamic rationality reduces to individual rationality.
  • Interpret dynamic rationality using the exact Blair comparison C_t(B union B_t^-) = B, rather than bundle containment or expected welfare.
  • Require claims to concern a school absent from the claimant's assigned bundle, and tenure to be specific to the displaced type's previous bundle at that school.
  • Interpret divisible priority tiers as allowing arbitrary splitting within ties, with no claims between equal-tier types.
  • Use exact rational allocation masses and permit reporting nonexistence, without assuming that discrete existence carries over.

A polynomial-time algorithm

The search problem is polynomial-time solvable. Every valid instance has a rational dynamically stable allocation. The algorithm solves at most $1+m\tau$ rational linear programs of polynomial encoding length.

The key is to minimize the total mass of demands within a threshold-constrained allocation polytope. Path independence ensures that satisfying one demand creates no new demands. This makes it possible to lower thresholds while preserving feasibility.

1. A demand-reduction lemma

Write $P_t=B_t^-$. Call a bundle $B$ admissible for type $t$ if
$$ C_t(B)=B,\qquad C_t(B\cup P_t)=B. $$
For entrants, the second equality follows from the first. Define
$$ D_t(B)=\{s\in S\setminus B:s\in C_t(B\cup\{s\})\}. $$

We use path independence in the form
$$ C_t(A\cup E)=C_t(C_t(A)\cup E). $$

First, path independence implies idempotence:
$$ C_t(C_t(A))=C_t(A), $$
by taking $E=\varnothing$.

It also implies consistency. If $C_t(A)\subseteq E\subseteq A$, then
$$ C_t(A) =C_t(A\cup E) =C_t(C_t(A)\cup E) =C_t(E). $$

Consequently, for any individually rational bundle $B$ and any $w\notin D_t(B)$,
$$ C_t(B\cup\{w\})=B. $$
Indeed, this is immediate if $w\in B$. Otherwise, absence of demand means that $w$ is not chosen, so
$$ C_t(B\cup\{w\})\subseteq B\subseteq B\cup\{w\}. $$
Consistency and $C_t(B)=B$ give the equality.

Lemma. Suppose $B$ is admissible and $s\in D_t(B)$. Set
$$ B'=C_t(B\cup\{s\}). $$
Then:

  • $B'$ is admissible.
  • $B'\setminus B=\{s\}$.
  • $D_t(B')\subseteq D_t(B)\setminus\{s\}$.

Proof. Choice containment gives $B'\subseteq B\cup\{s\}$, and the definition of demand gives $s\in B'$. Since $s\notin B$, this proves the second assertion.

Idempotence gives $C_t(B')=B'$. For dynamic rationality, path independence gives
$$ \begin{aligned} C_t(B'\cup P_t) &=C_t(B\cup\{s\}\cup P_t)\\ &=C_t(C_t(B\cup P_t)\cup\{s\})\\ &=C_t(B\cup\{s\})\\ &=B'. \end{aligned} $$
Thus $B'$ is admissible.

Now take $w\notin D_t(B)$. The equality established above and path independence imply
$$ \begin{aligned} C_t(B'\cup\{w\}) &=C_t(B\cup\{s,w\})\\ &=C_t(C_t(B\cup\{w\})\cup\{s\})\\ &=C_t(B\cup\{s\})\\ &=B'. \end{aligned} $$
Hence $w\notin D_t(B')$. Also $s\notin D_t(B')$, because $s\in B'$. Therefore
$$ D_t(B')\subseteq D_t(B)\setminus\{s\}. $$
In particular,
$$ |D_t(B')|\le |D_t(B)|-1. $$
This proves the lemma. $\square$

Thus satisfying a demand preserves dynamic rationality, adds only the demanded school, and strictly reduces the number of demands.

2. Threshold-constrained allocation polytopes

At each school $s$, compress the priority tiers occurring among the supported types and number them from lowest to highest:
$$ r_s(t)\in\{1,\ldots,k_s\},\qquad k_s\le\tau. $$
Thus
$$ t>_s u\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r_s(t)>r_s(u). $$

For a threshold vector
$$ d\in\prod_{s\in S}\{0,1,\ldots,k_s\}, $$
retain precisely the admissible pairs $(t,B)$ satisfying
$$ r_s(t)\le d_s\qquad\text{for every }s\in D_t(B), $$
and
$$ r_s(t)\ge d_s\qquad\text{for every }s\in B\setminus P_t. $$

Let $\mathcal P(d)$ be the polytope of allocations supported on these retained pairs and satisfying
$$ x_{t,B}\ge0,\qquad \sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t, $$
and
$$ a_s=\sum_t\sum_{B:s\in B}x_{t,B}\le q_s. $$
There are no additional fullness constraints.

Every point of $\mathcal P(d)$ satisfies all the stability requirements except possibly non-wastefulness.

Feasibility and individual and dynamic rationality hold by construction. To exclude justified claims, suppose a positive bundle of type $t$ demands $s$, and a positive bundle of type $u$ occupies $s$ without tenure there. The threshold restrictions give
$$ r_s(t)\le d_s\le r_s(u). $$
Thus $t>_s u$ is impossible. This argument permits equal tiers exactly as required by the question.

The initial threshold vector
$$ d_s=k_s\qquad\text{for every }s $$
has a nonempty polytope. Indeed, assign every type its previous bundle:
$$ x_{t,P_t}=\mu_t. $$
Each $P_t$ is admissible by the premises; entrants have $P_t=\varnothing$. Previous feasibility supplies the capacity inequalities. Every demand has rank at most $k_s$, and these bundles have no non-tenured assignments. Hence this allocation belongs to $\mathcal P(d)$.

3. Optimizing demand mass permits threshold reduction

For each nonempty $\mathcal P(d)$, solve the linear program
$$ \text{minimize }\quad \Phi(x)=\sum_t\sum_B |D_t(B)|\,x_{t,B} \quad\text{over }x\in\mathcal P(d). $$
The objective counts total demand mass, counting separately the schools demanded by each bundle.

The polytope is bounded: every coordinate satisfies $0\le x_{t,B}\le\mu_t$. Consequently, an optimum exists.

Boundary-demand lemma. Let $x$ be an optimal solution. If $a_s<q_s$, no positive bundle demanding $s$ has priority rank $d_s$.

Proof. Suppose instead that $x_{t,B}>0$, $s\in D_t(B)$, and $r_s(t)=d_s$. Set
$$ B'=C_t(B\cup\{s\}). $$
The demand-reduction lemma proves that $B'$ is admissible and that its demands form a subset of the old demands. Therefore it satisfies all the demand restrictions for $\mathcal P(d)$.

Its only newly assigned school is $s$. If this assignment is non-tenured, its rank equals $d_s$, so it satisfies the recipient restriction as well. Every other retained assignment already satisfied its restriction. Thus $(t,B')$ is an allowed coordinate.

Choose the positive rational number
$$ \varepsilon=\frac12\min\{x_{t,B},q_s-a_s\}. $$
Move mass $\varepsilon$ from $B$ to $B'$. Type masses remain unchanged. The load at $s$ increases by $\varepsilon$, within its capacity; every other school’s load stays unchanged or decreases because
$$ B'\subseteq B\cup\{s\}. $$
The modified allocation therefore remains in $\mathcal P(d)$.

Its objective change is
$$ \varepsilon\bigl(|D_t(B')|-|D_t(B)|\bigr)\le-\varepsilon<0, $$
contradicting optimality. $\square$

Now suppose an optimal allocation has an underfilled school $s$ with positive demand. Demand restrictions imply $d_s\ge1$. The boundary-demand lemma and the integrality of priority ranks imply that every positive demander at $s$ satisfies
$$ r_s(t)\le d_s-1. $$

Lower just that threshold:
$$ d'_s=d_s-1,\qquad d'_w=d_w\quad\text{for }w\ne s. $$
The current allocation remains feasible in $\mathcal P(d')$:

  • Its positive demands at $s$ satisfy the strengthened demand restriction.
  • Its non-tenured assignments at $s$ satisfy the weakened recipient restriction.
  • All other restrictions and allocation constraints are unchanged.

Thus each such threshold reduction preserves nonemptiness.

4. The algorithm and its correctness

The algorithm is:

  • Initialize $d_s=k_s$ for every school.
  • Compute an exact rational minimizer of $\Phi$ over $\mathcal P(d)$.
  • If every school with positive demand is full, return this allocation.
  • Otherwise, choose an underfilled school with positive demand, decrease its threshold by one, and return to step 2.

The initial polytope is nonempty, and Section 3 proves that every reduction preserves nonemptiness. Every linear program is therefore feasible.

Each reduction decreases the nonnegative integer
$$ \sum_{s\in S}d_s $$
by one. Hence there are at most
$$ \sum_{s\in S}k_s\le m\tau $$
reductions.

An unsuccessful iteration always permits a reduction. In particular, if every threshold reaches zero, positive demand is forbidden everywhere, so the algorithm must return. The number of linear programs is at most
$$ 1+\sum_{s\in S}k_s\le1+m\tau. $$

At termination, the returned allocation belongs to $\mathcal P(d)$, so it satisfies feasibility, individual rationality, dynamic rationality, and absence of justified claims by Section 2. The termination test supplies non-wastefulness. It is therefore dynamically stable.

This also proves existence directly, without assuming existence in order to initialize the algorithm.

5. Polynomial running time and rational output

There are at most
$$ N=\tau2^m $$
type–bundle pairs. Because each choice function is supplied by its full table, $N=O(L)$ under the stated explicit encoding.

Admissibility and demand sets can be computed by table lookups for every pair, using polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$. Each threshold polytope has at most $N$ variables, $\tau$ type-mass equations, and $m$ school-capacity inequalities, together with nonnegativity. Its matrix coefficients are in $\{0,1\}$; its right-hand sides are input rationals. Objective coefficients are integers between $0$ and $m$. Thus every linear program has polynomial encoding length.

We use the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: an explicitly encoded nonempty bounded rational polyhedron, with a rational linear objective, has an optimal rational point computable in time polynomial in the encoding length, with polynomial output bit length. Here nonemptiness follows from the invariant above, boundedness follows from the type-mass equations, and all data have polynomial encoding length.

Loads and positive demands can then be checked by exact rational arithmetic. Combining these checks with the bound on the number of iterations gives total running time
$$ \left(1+\sum_{s\in S}k_s\right)\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) =\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

The final rational allocation has polynomial encoding length. Under the stated valid-instance premises, the nonexistence outcome never occurs.
