The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a society \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) valuation types, with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\) and each type’s cumulative valuation \(F_t\) given by an arithmetic circuit, decide whether the resource \([0,1]\) can be divided using at most \(\tau-1\) ordered cuts into alternately labeled intervals \(A_+\) and \(A_-\), so that \(F_t(A_+) = F_t(A_-)\) for every type \(t\) with \(\mu_t > 0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\geq1\) valuation types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type supplies an acyclic arithmetic circuit \(F_t\) with binary rational constants and gates \(+, -, \times, \min, \max\), promised to satisfy \(F_t(x)=\int_0^x f_t(y)\,dy\) for a bounded, integrable, nonnegative density \(f_t\). Valuation totals need not equal \(1\).
For \(0\leq k\leq\tau-1\), choose real cuts \(0=c_0\leq c_1\leq\cdots\leq c_k\leq c_{k+1}=1\). Label successive intervals alternately, starting with either sign; coincident cuts and endpoint cuts are allowed.
For either sign, define \(F_t(A_\pm)=\sum_{j:\,[c_j,c_{j+1}]\text{ has sign }\pm}(F_t(c_{j+1})-F_t(c_j))\). Feasibility requires \(F_t(A_+)=F_t(A_-)\) for every supported type.
Use the discrete Turing model; state running times in \(\tau\), the total circuit gate count \(m\), and the total binary input length \(L\).

Is this exact feasibility problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For a finite list of \(n\) agents, exact consensus halving with at most \(n-1\) cuts is \(\exists\mathbb{R}\)-complete, including for nonnegative densities ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.03101)).
The stated mass formulation has unchanged feasibility constraints and cut budget; consequently, the same classification applies. This is an inference from the verified result, so the target is already settled.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified the arithmetic circuit basis and binary encoding of rational constants.
  • Interpreted cumulative valuations as integrals of bounded, integrable, nonnegative densities, consistent with the hardness construction; imposed no normalization on valuation totals.
  • Allowed coincident cuts and endpoint cuts, and either initial label, as in the discrete definition.
  • Defined interval-union valuations additively and required exact equality separately for every supported type.
  • Defined m as total circuit gate count and L as total binary input length because there is no candidate-count parameter.

Classification

The problem is \(\exists\mathbb{R}\)-complete, and therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, under polynomial-time many-one reductions in the discrete Turing model. Hardness already holds for the budget \(\tau-1\). Thus a polynomial-time algorithm in \(\tau,m,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The classification concerns inputs satisfying the stated density promise; the algorithm need not verify that promise.

1. Cuts, labels, and masses

Fix a budget \(k\leq\tau-1\). We may assume that the first interval has positive sign: reversing every sign negates every balance equation and therefore preserves feasibility.

With \(c_0=0\) and \(c_{k+1}=1\), the equations are precisely

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j\bigl(F_t(c_{j+1})-F_t(c_j)\bigr)=0,\qquad t=1,\ldots,\tau.\)

The masses do not occur in these equations. Consequently, changing positive masses while preserving their sum preserves feasibility.

A solution using fewer than \(k\) cuts can be represented using exactly \(k\) listed cuts by appending cuts at \(1\). All added intervals have zero valuation, so this padding preserves every equation. Thus the formulation also captures an “at most” cut budget.

2. Membership in \(\exists\mathbb{R}\)

Here \(\exists\mathbb{R}\) denotes the class of decision problems polynomial-time reducible to truth of existential sentences over real polynomial equalities and inequalities with binary-encoded integer coefficients.

Introduce cut variables satisfying

\(0\leq c_1\leq\cdots\leq c_k\leq1.\)

For each type and each endpoint, make a copy of its circuit and introduce variables for its gate outputs. Encode the gates as follows:

| Gate | Constraint |
|---|---|
| Constant \(a/b\), with \(b>0\) | \(bz=a\) |
| Addition | \(z=u+v\) |
| Subtraction | \(z=u-v\) |
| Multiplication | \(z=uv\) |
| Minimum | \((u\leq v\land z=u)\lor(v\leq u\land z=v)\) |
| Maximum | \((u\geq v\land z=u)\lor(v\geq u\land z=v)\) |

Let \(y_{t,j}\) denote the output of the copy evaluating \(F_t(c_j)\). Add

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j(y_{t,j+1}-y_{t,j})=0\)

for every type, and existentially quantify all cut and gate variables.

Each gate constraint determines its correct output. Induction through the acyclic circuit therefore shows that any satisfying assignment has \(y_{t,j}=F_t(c_j)\). Its balance constraints then give a feasible partition. Conversely, evaluating the circuits at any feasible cuts supplies a satisfying assignment. This proves equivalence in both directions.

Define

\(Q=(k+2)(m+\tau)+k+1.\)

The formula has \(O(Q)\) variables and polynomial atoms, each of degree at most \(2\). Its binary length is bounded by

\(B=O\bigl((k+2)L+Q\log(Q+2)\bigr).\)

It can be constructed in time polynomial in \(B\), without expanding the polynomials computed by the original circuits. Since \(k\leq\tau-1\), this construction takes polynomial time in \(\tau,m,L\). Hence the problem belongs to \(\exists\mathbb{R}\).

3. Hardness with support size preserved

Known theorem. Deligkas, Fearnley, Melissourgos, and Spirakis prove that exact consensus halving for \(n\) agents with at most \(n-1\) cuts is \(\exists\mathbb{R}\)-complete (Theorem 16). Their inputs are cumulative valuation circuits. Their hardness construction uses nonzero, bounded, nonnegative piecewise affine densities with rational descriptions; Section 6.2.3 constructs the cumulative circuits using the permitted gates. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.03101)

The gate conventions match: a rational scalar-multiplication gate can be replaced by a rational constant gate followed by multiplication. Nonnegativity, boundedness, and integrability satisfy the task’s promise.

There is one additional issue: the task counts supported valuation types, whereas the theorem counts agents. We ensure that all resulting types are distinct.

Take an instance from the theorem’s hardness construction, with cumulative valuations \(F_1,\ldots,F_n\), and put

\(T_i=F_i(1)>0,\qquad \widehat F_i(x)=\dfrac{i}{T_i}F_i(x).\)

The numbers \(T_i\) can be computed in polynomial time from the explicit piecewise affine descriptions produced by that construction. Indeed, the integral of a piece \(\alpha x+\beta\) on \([u,v]\) is

\(\displaystyle \frac{\alpha}{2}(v^2-u^2)+\beta(v-u).\)

Polynomially many such rational calculations produce totals and scaling factors of polynomial binary length.

Each \(\widehat F_i\) has density \((i/T_i)f_i\), which remains bounded, integrable, and nonnegative. Moreover,

\(\widehat F_i(1)=i.\)

Thus the cumulative valuations are pairwise distinct, giving exactly \(\tau=n\) supported types.

Assign each type mass \(\mu_i=1/n\), and set the budget to \(k=n-1=\tau-1\). These masses are positive, sum to \(1\), and require \(O(n\log(n+1))\) bits altogether.

For every partition,

\(\displaystyle \widehat F_i(A_+)-\widehat F_i(A_-)=\frac{i}{T_i}\bigl(F_i(A_+)-F_i(A_-)\bigr).\)

The multiplier is strictly positive. Therefore the new balance equation holds exactly when the original one holds. Cuts and labels are unchanged, so feasibility is preserved in both directions.

Adding the scaling requires only \(O(n)\) gates and polynomially many bits. Composing this transformation with the cited hardness reduction gives a polynomial-time reduction to the stated problem, with \(\tau,m,L\) all polynomially bounded in the source input length. This proves \(\exists\mathbb{R}\)-hardness.

4. Running-time consequence

The existential formula above also gives an exact decision algorithm. For an existential formula with \(O(Q)\) variables and atoms, degree at most \(2\), and binary length \(B\), singly exponential real-algebraic decision algorithms give the conservative bit-time bound

\(\mathrm{poly}(B)(Q+2)^{O(Q)}.\)

The hypotheses follow directly from the gate encoding. The arithmetic-operation and intermediate integer-size bounds needed for this bit-time estimate are given in Theorem 2.27 of [Basu’s survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.1534).

Using \(k\leq\tau-1\), a corresponding bound in the requested parameters is

\(\displaystyle \bigl(L+\tau(m+\tau)+2\bigr)^{O(\tau(m+\tau))}.\)

This establishes decidability while the hardness result settles the requested classification.
