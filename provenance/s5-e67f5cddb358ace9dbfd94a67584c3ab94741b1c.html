The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite object set \(A_0\), an outside option \(a_0\), per-capita capacities \(\kappa_a\), and a regular probability measure \(\mu\) over complete types \((S,v)\), find a measurable direct mechanism for a unit mass of atomless agents that maximizes expected per-capita revenue while satisfying individual DSIC and IR, aggregate capacity constraints, and integral assignments to each individual. The mechanism may assign fractional shares of each type mass across objects, with every individual receiving an acceptable object or \(a_0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A_0\) contain \(m\geq1\) objects, with an unlimited outside option \(a_0\) and binary rational capacities \(\kappa_a\in[0,1]\). The report domain is \(D=\{(S,v):\varnothing\neq S\subseteq A_0,\ v\in(0,\infty)\}\); utility from object \(a\) and payment \(p\in\mathbb R\) is \(v\mathbf1_{\{a\in S\}}-p\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; support values are positive binary rationals, masses sum to \(1\), and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Assume \(\mu=h\otimes q\): value and acceptable set are independent. Write the value support as \(v_1<\cdots<v_k\), with probabilities \(q_j>0\). Require nondecreasing discrete hazards \(q_j/\sum_{\ell=j}^kq_\ell\) and regularity: the sequence \(\phi_j=v_j-(v_{j+1}-v_j)\sum_{\ell=j+1}^kq_\ell/q_j\), for \(j<k\), followed by \(\phi_k=v_k\), is nondecreasing.
Represent the atomless population by \(D\times[0,1]\) with measure \(\mu\otimes\lambda\), where \(\lambda\) is Lebesgue measure and the second coordinate \(z\) is a fixed, publicly observable identity that cannot be misreported. A mechanism consists of measurable maps \(f(z,t)\in S\cup\{a_0\}\) and \(p(z,t)\in\mathbb R\), with integrable truthful payments. Each individual receives one integral assignment; different identities of the same type may receive different objects.
For every \(z\) and \(t=(S,v),t'\in D\), require individual DSIC and IR: \(v\mathbf1_{\{f(z,t)\in S\}}-p(z,t)\geq v\mathbf1_{\{f(z,t')\in S\}}-p(z,t')\) and \(v\mathbf1_{\{f(z,t)\in S\}}-p(z,t)\geq0\). Indifference may be resolved by the designer subject to these inequalities.
Require truthful aggregate feasibility \(\int\mathbf1_{\{f(z,t)=a\}}\,d(\mu\otimes\lambda)\leq\kappa_a\) for every \(a\in A_0\). Given a nonnegative binary rational threshold \(R\), the revenue-threshold problem asks whether such a mechanism exists with \(\int p(z,t)\,d(\mu\otimes\lambda)\geq R\).

Is this revenue-threshold problem NP-hard or solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For finitely many agents, independent values and acceptable sets, and continuous value distributions with nondecreasing hazard rates, an optimal deterministic DSIC, IR mechanism maximizes positive virtual surplus at each profile and charges critical values ([Mishra–Roy](https://doi.org/10.3982/TE1239)).
No result settling the exact atomless, individual-DSIC revenue-threshold problem specified here was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Reconciled the measure formulation with the mandatory support encoding by using finitely many complete types with positive rational values and rational masses summing to one; the population itself remains atomless.
  • Restored independence of value and acceptable set, which the anchor assumes.
  • Replaced the anchor's density-based nondecreasing-hazard assumption by nondecreasing discrete hazards, and defined the graded question's regularity through nondecreasing discrete virtual values.
  • Separated distributional support from the report domain: DSIC and IR hold on all nonempty acceptable sets and all positive real values, preserving the anchor's rich domain.
  • Made within-type splitting deterministic by using fixed, nonreportable identities distributed independently of types; DSIC holds for each identity rather than only in expectation.
  • Specified nonempty acceptable sets, a worthless and unlimited outside option, real payments, positive values, rational capacities between zero and one, and designer-controlled resolution of indifference.
  • Interpreted aggregate capacity constraints at the truthful society and formulated exact revenue optimization through a rational revenue threshold.

Result

The revenue-threshold problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

More precisely, optimal revenue equals the value of a rational linear program with at most \(m\tau\) variables and \(\tau+m\) constraints. Regularity is sufficient for this conclusion; the additional hazard-rate assumption is not needed.

1. A virtual-surplus linear program

Let \(S_1,\ldots,S_n\) be the distinct acceptable sets occurring in the support, with probabilities \(h_i>0\). Independence implies that every pair \((S_i,v_j)\) occurs with mass \(h_iq_j\), so \(\tau=nk\).

Write \(Q_j=\sum_{\ell=j}^k q_\ell\). The virtual values are

\( \phi_j=v_j-(v_{j+1}-v_j)Q_{j+1}/q_j \)

for \(j<k\), and \(\phi_k=v_k\). By assumption, they are nondecreasing.

For each \(i,j\) and \(a\in S_i\), let \(x_{ija}\) denote the population mass of type \((S_i,v_j)\) assigned object \(a\). Define \(B\) as the optimal value of the following linear program:

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} B=\max\quad& \sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{a\in S_i}\phi_jx_{ija}\\ \text{subject to}\quad& \sum_{a\in S_i}x_{ija}\leq h_iq_j &&\text{for every }i,j,\\ & \sum_{i:\,a\in S_i}\sum_{j=1}^k x_{ija}\leq\kappa_a &&\text{for every }a\in A_0,\\ &x_{ija}\geq0. \end{aligned} \)

The zero vector is feasible, and all variables are bounded, so this program has a finite attained optimum.

We will prove that optimal mechanism revenue is exactly \(B\).

2. Every admissible mechanism has revenue at most \(B\)

Fix an identity \(z\) and an acceptable set \(S_i\). At report \((S_i,v_j)\), write

\( y_j=\mathbf1_{\{f(z,(S_i,v_j))\in S_i\}}, \qquad p_j=p(z,(S_i,v_j)), \qquad u_j=v_jy_j-p_j. \)

IR gives \(u_1\geq0\). DSIC for true type \((S_i,v_{j+1})\) against report \((S_i,v_j)\) gives

\( u_{j+1}\geq u_j+(v_{j+1}-v_j)y_j. \)

Consequently,

\( u_j\geq\sum_{\ell<j}(v_{\ell+1}-v_\ell)y_\ell, \)

and therefore

\( p_j\leq v_jy_j-\sum_{\ell<j}(v_{\ell+1}-v_\ell)y_\ell. \)

Multiplying by \(q_j\), summing, and exchanging the finite sums yields

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} \sum_{j=1}^kq_jp_j &\leq \sum_{j=1}^kq_jv_jy_j -\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(v_{\ell+1}-v_\ell)Q_{\ell+1}y_\ell\\ &=\sum_{j=1}^kq_j\phi_jy_j. \end{aligned} \)

This argument allows negative payments and uses only the stated DSIC and IR inequalities.

Now define

\( x_{ija} = h_iq_j\int_0^1 \mathbf1_{\{f(z,(S_i,v_j))=a\}}\,dz. \)

Integral assignments and aggregate feasibility make these variables feasible for the linear program. Because every supported type has positive mass, integrability of truthful payments implies integrability of each supported payment function. We can therefore integrate the preceding inequality over identities and sum over acceptable sets, obtaining

\( \displaystyle \operatorname{Rev}(f,p) \leq \sum_{i,j}\sum_{a\in S_i}\phi_jx_{ija} \leq B. \)

3. A finite family of mechanisms satisfying DSIC on the full domain

Consider the following finite family \(\mathcal M\) of posted-price mechanisms.

Choose, for each object \(a\), either a price

\( P_a\in\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\} \)

or mark it unavailable. Also choose a total ordering of the objects.

At report \((S,v)\), select an available object in \(S\) having minimum price, breaking price ties by the chosen ordering. Assign it and charge its price if \(v\) is at least that price. Otherwise assign \(a_0\) and charge zero. If \(S\) contains no available object, also assign \(a_0\) for zero.

These mechanisms are measurable. They satisfy DSIC and IR for every report in \(D\), including reports outside the support. Indeed, truthful utility is

\( \max\{0,v-\min_{a\in S,\ a\text{ available}}P_a\}, \)

with utility zero when there is no available acceptable object. A misreport that obtains an acceptable object cannot obtain one below this minimum price. A misreport that obtains an unacceptable object gives utility \(-P_a\leq0\), and the outside option gives zero.

For \(M\in\mathcal M\), let \(r(M)\) be its expected revenue and \(d_a(M)\) its expected consumption of object \(a\).

Any probability vector \((\lambda_M)_{M\in\mathcal M}\) can be implemented using the public identity coordinate: partition \([0,1]\) into intervals of lengths \(\lambda_M\), and use mechanism \(M\) on its interval. Each identity then faces a deterministic DSIC, IR mechanism. Aggregate revenue and consumption are respectively

\( \sum_M\lambda_Mr(M) \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_M\lambda_Md_a(M). \)

Thus these mixtures are admissible mechanisms in the question’s integral-assignment model.

4. Posted prices attain every capacity-penalized virtual-surplus bound

Fix arbitrary nonnegative object costs \(c=(c_a)_{a\in A_0}\). Define

\( \displaystyle F(c)= \sum_{i=1}^nh_i \sum_{j=1}^kq_j \max\left\{0,\phi_j-\min_{a\in S_i}c_a\right\}. \)

We claim that

\( \displaystyle \max_{M\in\mathcal M} \left(r(M)-\sum_a c_ad_a(M)\right)=F(c). \)

Upper bound

The payment bound from Section 2 applies to each posted-price mechanism without requiring capacity feasibility. Subtracting its object costs gives

\( \displaystyle r(M)-\sum_a c_ad_a(M) \leq \sum_{i,j}h_iq_j \sum_{a\in S_i} (\phi_j-c_a) \mathbf1_{\{M(S_i,v_j)=a\}}. \)

At each supported type, the mechanism assigns at most one object. Its contribution is therefore at most

\( \max\left\{0,\phi_j-\min_{a\in S_i}c_a\right\}. \)

Summing proves the upper bound \(F(c)\).

Attainment

For each object \(a\), let

\( \ell_a=\min\{j:\phi_j>c_a\} \)

if this set is nonempty. Offer \(a\) at price \(v_{\ell_a}\); otherwise mark it unavailable. Break price ties by increasing \(c_a\), with any fixed rule for equal costs.

Regularity ensures that \(\{j:\phi_j>c_a\}\) is a tail of the value support. It also ensures that \(c_a\leq c_b\) implies that \(a\)'s price is no higher than \(b\)'s price, treating unavailable objects as having an infinite price for this comparison only.

For a fixed acceptable set \(S_i\), choose \(a_i\in S_i\) minimizing \(c_a\), consistently with the tie rule, and put \(c_i=c_{a_i}\). If

\( \ell_i=\min\{j:\phi_j>c_i\} \)

exists, the posted-price mechanism assigns \(a_i\) exactly at support values \(v_j\) with \(j\geq\ell_i\), charging \(v_{\ell_i}\). If no such index exists, it assigns the outside option throughout that set’s supported values.

The virtual-value definition gives the telescoping identity

\( q_j\phi_j=v_jQ_j-v_{j+1}Q_{j+1} \)

for \(j<k\), together with \(q_k\phi_k=v_kQ_k\). Hence, for every \(\ell\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_{j=\ell}^kq_j\phi_j=v_\ell Q_\ell. \)

When \(\ell_i\) exists, the conditional revenue minus object cost is consequently

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} (v_{\ell_i}-c_i)Q_{\ell_i} &=\sum_{j=\ell_i}^kq_j(\phi_j-c_i)\\ &=\sum_{j=1}^kq_j\max\{0,\phi_j-c_i\}. \end{aligned} \)

When \(\ell_i\) does not exist, both sides are zero. Multiplying by \(h_i\) and summing proves attainment of \(F(c)\).

5. Linear-programming duality proves exact attainability under capacities

Let \(W\) be the optimal revenue among mixtures of mechanisms in \(\mathcal M\). It is the value of the finite linear program

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} W=\max\quad&\sum_{M\in\mathcal M}\lambda_Mr(M)\\ \text{subject to}\quad& \sum_{M\in\mathcal M}\lambda_Md_a(M)\leq\kappa_a &&\text{for every }a,\\ &\sum_{M\in\mathcal M}\lambda_M=1,\qquad \lambda_M\geq0. \end{aligned} \)

The mechanism offering no objects makes this program feasible. Its feasible region is a closed subset of a finite simplex, so its optimum is attained.

Its dual has nonnegative capacity multipliers \(c_a\) and a free variable \(\eta\), with constraints

\( \eta\geq r(M)-\sum_a c_ad_a(M) \)

for every \(M\). By Section 4 and finite-dimensional linear-programming strong duality,

\( \displaystyle W=\min_{c\geq0}\left\{\sum_a\kappa_ac_a+F(c)\right\}. \)

Now take the dual of the virtual-surplus program defining \(B\). Its variables are \(c_a\geq0\) and \(\alpha_{ij}\geq0\), with constraints

\( \alpha_{ij}+c_a\geq\phi_j \qquad(a\in S_i), \)

and objective

\( \sum_a\kappa_ac_a+\sum_{i,j}h_iq_j\alpha_{ij}. \)

For fixed \(c\), the smallest feasible \(\alpha_{ij}\) is

\( \alpha_{ij}= \max\left\{0,\phi_j-\min_{a\in S_i}c_a\right\}. \)

The virtual-surplus program is feasible and bounded, so strong duality applies and gives

\( \displaystyle B=\min_{c\geq0}\left\{\sum_a\kappa_ac_a+F(c)\right\}=W. \)

An optimal mixture therefore implements revenue \(B\) with integral assignments and DSIC and IR at every identity and every report. Combined with Section 2, this proves that \(B\) is exactly the maximum admissible revenue.

6. Polynomial-time decision procedure

The algorithm constructs and solves the virtual-surplus linear program from Section 1 and answers yes exactly when \(B\geq R\).

The marginals \(h_i,q_j\), the sorted values, and the virtual values can all be computed from the support list by rational arithmetic. Their bit lengths are polynomial in \(L\). The program has at most \(m\tau\) variables and \(\tau+m\) constraints, with rational coefficients of polynomial total bit length.

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore permits exact optimization and comparison with the rational threshold \(R\) in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The finite posted-price family is used only to prove attainability; the algorithm does not enumerate it.
