The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite horizon \(H\), resource capacities per capita \(a_r\), and a deterministic known stream of cohorts with rational masses \(\mu_{t,k}\) and task attributes \((v_k,d_k,q_k)\), find a welfare-maximizing feasible schedule. Choose accepted mass \(x_{t,k}\) with \(0 \le x_{t,k} \le \mu_{t,k}\), set \(x_{t,k}=0\) when the task cannot finish by \(H+1\), and require \(\sum_{(t,k):\,t \le u < t+d_k} q_{k,r}x_{t,k} \le a_r\) for every resource \(r\) and time \(u\). Maximize \(\sum_{t,k} v_k x_{t,k}\), with each individual task receiving its full requested bundle or being rejected.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) arrival-tagged types \((t,k)\) with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_{t,k}\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge 1\) resources with positive rational per-capita capacities \(a_r\), and a finite discrete horizon \(H\ge 1\). Each listed arrival time satisfies \(t\in\{1,\ldots,H\}\); task attributes are a nonnegative rational value \(v_k\), a positive integer duration \(d_k\), and a resource bundle \(q_k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{m}\). All numerical inputs are binary encoded; \(L\) is their total bit length.
The entire stream is known in advance. Choose accepted masses \(x_{t,k}\in[0,\mu_{t,k}]\). Every accepted individual starts immediately at \(t\), receives its full bundle throughout \([t,t+d_k)\), and completes at \(t+d_k\); other individuals are rejected. Mass within a cohort may be split between acceptance and rejection.
Require \(x_{t,k}=0\) if \(t+d_k>H+1\), and \(\sum_{(t,k):\,t\le u<t+d_k}q_{k,r}x_{t,k}\le a_r\) for every \(r\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\) and \(u\in\{1,\ldots,H\}\). Welfare is \(\sum_{t,k}v_kx_{t,k}\).

Is finding a welfare-maximizing feasible schedule solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), \(L\), and \(H\), accounting for the binary encoding of \(H\).

The discrete problem with indivisible tasks is NP-hard even with one resource and a deterministic stream known in advance. That result does not establish the complexity of the mass formulation above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Treat arrival time together with task attributes as a society type, so tau counts positive-mass cohorts.
  • Normalize total cohort mass to one, making capacities and welfare per capita.
  • Use positive integer durations and nonnegative integer bundle entries; zero entries are permitted, as in the supplied examples.
  • Use nonnegative rational values, consistent with the supplied discussion of nonnegative valuations, and positive rational per-capita capacities.
  • Binary encode the horizon, arrival times, durations, and all other numerical data.
  • Permit splitting each cohort's mass while preserving immediate start, uninterrupted service, and full-bundle allocation for every accepted individual.

Result

The mass formulation is solvable exactly in polynomial time, even when \(H\) is binary encoded. A valid bit-complexity bound is

\[ \bigl(m\tau^2(L+1)\bigr)^{O(1)}. \]

The key is to retain capacity constraints only at distinct arrival times, rather than at all \(H\) time steps.

1. Remove cohorts that cannot finish

Index the listed support by \(i\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), writing its data as \(t_i,d_i,q_{i,r},v_i,\mu_i\). Define the completion time \(e_i=t_i+d_i\), and let

\[ I=\{i:e_i\le H+1\},\qquad n=|I|. \]

Every cohort outside \(I\) must have \(x_i=0\), so it can be removed from the optimization. If \(n=0\), rejecting every cohort is optimal. Henceforth assume \(n\ge1\).

Let \(S=\{t_i:i\in I\}\) be the set of distinct eligible arrival times. Its size satisfies \(|S|\le n\le\tau\).

2. Capacity checks at arrivals suffice

For a nonnegative vector \(x=(x_i)_{i\in I}\), define the load on resource \(r\) at time \(u\) by

\[ \ell_r(u)=\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\t_i\le u<e_i}}q_{i,r}x_i. \]

Lemma. The inequalities \(\ell_r(u)\le a_r\) for every \(u\in\{1,\ldots,H\}\) and every resource \(r\) are equivalent to the inequalities \(\ell_r(s)\le a_r\) for every \(s\in S\) and every resource \(r\).

Proof. Necessity follows because every \(s\in S\) lies in \(\{1,\ldots,H\}\).

For sufficiency, fix a time \(u\). If no eligible arrival occurs by \(u\), its load is zero. Otherwise, let \(s=\max\{t_i:i\in I,\ t_i\le u\}\).

Consider any cohort active at \(u\). Its arrival time satisfies \(t_i\le u\), so the definition of \(s\) gives \(t_i\le s\). Moreover, \(s\le u<e_i\). Thus this cohort was also active at \(s\). Consequently, the set of cohorts active at \(u\) is contained in the set active at \(s\).

Since \(q_{i,r}x_i\ge0\), this containment implies \(\ell_r(u)\le\ell_r(s)\le a_r\). This holds for every resource and every time. \(\square\)

The strict inequality \(s<e_i\) correctly accounts for the half-open service interval: cohorts completing at \(s\) consume no capacity at \(s\).

3. An exact rational linear program

For \(s\in S\), \(r\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), and \(i\in I\), define

\[ A_{(s,r),i}=q_{i,r}\mathbf{1}_{\{t_i\le s<e_i\}}. \]

Solve the linear program with objective

\[ \max\sum_{i\in I}v_i x_i, \]

subject to the cohort bounds

\[ 0\le x_i\le\mu_i\qquad(i\in I) \]

and the capacity constraints

\[ \sum_{i\in I}A_{(s,r),i}x_i\le a_r \qquad(s\in S,\ r\in\{1,\ldots,m\}). \]

There are \(n\le\tau\) variables and at most \(m|S|+2n\le(m+2)\tau\) inequalities.

By the lemma, this program describes exactly the feasible accepted-mass vectors after removing the compulsory rejections. Its objective is exactly their welfare. Splitting cohort mass is permitted by the problem, so every feasible vector of this program is a valid schedule.

All coefficients are rational. The zero vector is feasible, and the bounds \(0\le x_i\le\mu_i\) make the feasible set compact. Therefore an optimum exists.

We now apply the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly represented rational linear program can be solved in polynomial bit time, returning an exact rational optimum when it is feasible and bounded. These hypotheses have all been verified above. Polynomial-time LP algorithms and the polynomial encoding size of optimal vertices are covered in [Goemans’s linear-programming notes, Sections 11–13](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-854j-advanced-algorithms-fall-2008/908fe54d659623efadda2d033b895caf_notes_lp.pdf).

Append \(x_i=0\) for the removed cohorts to obtain an optimal schedule for the original instance.

4. Running time and binary encoding of the horizon

Computing each \(e_i=t_i+d_i\) and comparing it with \(H+1\) uses integers of \(O(L+1)\) bits. Sorting eligible arrival times takes \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1)(L+1))\) bit operations.

The dense capacity matrix has at most \(m\tau^2\) entries. Each entry is either zero or an input bundle coordinate. Including the bounds, right-hand sides, and objective, the LP has total encoding length

\[ B=O\!\left(m\tau^2(L+1)\right). \]

A direct construction also takes \(O(m\tau^2(L+1))\) bit operations. Applying polynomial-time rational LP therefore yields the overall bound

\[ T(m,\tau,L,H)=\bigl(m\tau^2(L+1)\bigr)^{O(1)}. \]

There is no dependence on the numerical value of \(H\) beyond its contribution to \(L\), which includes its binary encoding length \(\Theta(\log(H+1))\). Since the support and capacities are explicitly listed, \(\tau,m=O(L)\), so this is polynomial in the full input length.

For comparison, explicitly constructing constraints for all time steps gives a dense LP of encoding length \(O((mH+\tau)\tau(L+1))\), and hence a bound of \(((mH+\tau)\tau(L+1))^{O(1)}\). That bound is polynomial in the numerical horizon and can be exponential in its binary encoding length. The arrival-time reduction eliminates this issue.

The indivisible-task hardness result does not transfer: indivisibility imposes discrete acceptance choices, whereas the formulation here permits precisely the continuous variables used by the LP.
