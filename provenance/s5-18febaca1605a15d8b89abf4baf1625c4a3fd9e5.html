The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite PU and SU type sets \(I,J\), rational supplies \(p_i,q_j\), a finite aspiration grid \(\mathcal R=\{0,\delta,\ldots,R_{\max}\}\), and \(\epsilon=h\delta\) for integer \(h>1\), find rational mass flows \(x_{ijrs}\) and aspiration masses \(y_{ir},z_{js}\). Types include complete utility and interaction profiles. The input explicitly encodes, for each type pair and grid-threshold pair, whether a feasible time-power allocation meets both thresholds, with thresholds through \(R_{\max}+\epsilon\). Require \(\sum_r y_{ir}=p_i\), \(\sum_s z_{js}=q_j\), and flows bounded by their aspiration-class supplies; all positive flow must have a feasible resource witness. Every positive aspiration class with level above zero must be fully matched, so any unmatched mass has aspiration zero. For every \(i,j,r,s\) with \(y_{ir}>0\) and \(z_{js}>0\), require that no feasible allocation meets \(r\delta+\epsilon\) and \(s\delta+\epsilon\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I,J\) be nonempty finite sets of primary-user and secondary-user types. A society is given by its support: its \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) types with positive masses \(p_i,q_j\), each a binary rational; these masses need not sum to one or balance across sides. Support size counts listed types, not possible types.
Let \(\delta>0\) be rational, \(N\geq0\) and \(h>1\) integers, \(\mathcal R=\{r\delta:0\leq r\leq N\}\), \(R_{\max}=N\delta\), and \(\epsilon=h\delta\). Write \(m=N+1\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Types specify complete interaction profiles. For each pair, feasible allocations have time \(t\in[0,1]\) and power \(0<P\leq\overline P_{ij}(t)\), with a finite pair-specific power limit. Utilities are nonnegative and bounded below \((N+1)\delta\); primary utility is quasi-convex, nonincreasing in time and nondecreasing in power, while secondary utility is quasi-concave with opposite trends.
The computational input explicitly lists exact Boolean tables \(A_{ij}(a,b)\) for \(a,b\in\{k\delta:0\leq k\leq N+h\}\): an entry is one exactly when some feasible allocation gives both utilities at least \(a,b\). Tables are promised to arise from the specified profiles, hence are downward closed. Resource witnesses may be real and are existentially certified by these entries; distinct matches share no resource constraint.
Find nonnegative rational aspiration masses \(y_{ir},z_{js}\) and matching flows \(x_{ijrs}\), indexed by \(0\leq r,s\leq N\), satisfying \(\sum_r y_{ir}=p_i\), \(\sum_s z_{js}=q_j\), \(\sum_{j,s}x_{ijrs}\leq y_{ir}\), and \(\sum_{i,r}x_{ijrs}\leq z_{js}\).
Require equality in the first matching bound whenever \(r>0\), and in the second whenever \(s>0\); thus unmatched mass has aspiration zero. Require \(x_{ijrs}>0\Rightarrow A_{ij}(r\delta,s\delta)=1\).
Require \(y_{ir}>0\) and \(z_{js}>0\Rightarrow A_{ij}(r\delta+\epsilon,s\delta+\epsilon)=0\), including classes carrying flow to one another. If no such outcome exists, report infeasibility.

Is finding such an outcome, or determining infeasibility, solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,h\).

For finite individual agents under these utility and grid assumptions, randomized bilateral matching converges almost surely to an \(\epsilon\)-pairwise stable outcome; this is not a polynomial-time bound. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.00306).
Welfare optimization for fractional matching with stability defined through agents’ average cardinal utilities is NP-hard, but uses a different stability notion and objective. [Source](https://cs.au.dk/~iannis/publ/C96-ec19b.pdf). No result settling this exact mass/table problem was located.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. Every promised instance is feasible.

The proof reduces the problem to bipartite stable allocation with strict preferences over contracts. It uses the listed types directly, without expanding rational masses into individual agents.

Write \(d=|I||J|\). A conservative deterministic bit-complexity bound is

\(O\!\left(L+(\tau+dm)^2L^2\right)\subseteq O\!\left(L+\tau^4m^2L^2\right).\)

The dependence on \(h\) is through the explicitly supplied tables, which contain \(d(m+h)^2\) entries. The allocation computation itself has no additional dependence on \(h\).

1. The stable-allocation theorem

We use the following established theorem.

Let \(G=(U\cup V,E)\) be a finite bipartite multigraph. Each vertex \(v\) has a nonnegative quota \(b_v\) and a strict preference order over its incident edges. Each edge \(e\) has capacity \(c_e\). A feasible allocation satisfies

\(0\leq f_e\leq c_e,\qquad \sum_{e\ni v}f_e\leq b_v.\)

Call an edge \(e\) dominated at \(v\) if

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\substack{g\ni v\\g\succeq_v e}}f_g=b_v.\)

An allocation is stable if every edge \(e\) with \(f_e<c_e\) is dominated at at least one endpoint.

Bipartite stable-allocation theorem. Such a stable allocation always exists. With rational quotas and capacities, it can be computed exactly using \(O((|U|+|V|+|E|)^2)\) arithmetic and comparison operations. Integral data yield an integral allocation.

This is the Baïou–Balinski inductive algorithm; the edge-preference formulation allowing parallel edges and the quadratic bound are stated in [Biró and Fleiner, Sections 1 and 3.1](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e265a098dc8b1a0c77f40bd8784e4da0794205ff&repid=rep1&type=pdf). The original result appears in the [corrected Baïou–Balinski paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

Domination means that the endpoint has filled its quota entirely with contracts ranked at least as highly as \(e\). Thus an unsaturated edge cannot offer an improvement to both endpoints.

2. Constructing a small contract graph

For convenience, put

\(B_{ij}(r,s)=A_{ij}(r\delta,s\delta).\)

For each \(i,j\) and \(r\in\{0,\ldots,N\}\) whose row contains a feasible entry, define

\(S_{ij}(r)=\max\{s\in\{0,\ldots,N\}:B_{ij}(r,s)=1\}.\)

Retain the contract

\(e=(i,j,r,S_{ij}(r)).\)

Let \(E\) be the set of retained contracts. There are at most

\(|E|\leq dm\)

contracts.

The essential coverage property is:

If \(B_{ij}(a,b)=1\), with \(0\leq a,b\leq N\), then a retained contract gives primary aspiration \(a\) and secondary aspiration at least \(b\).

Indeed, that row is nonempty and \(S_{ij}(a)\geq b\).

Construct a bipartite multigraph with vertex classes \(I,J\), one edge for each retained contract, and quotas

\(b_i=p_i,\qquad b_j=q_j.\)

Give each contract between \(i,j\) capacity

\(c_e=\min\{p_i,q_j\}.\)

This capacity introduces no restriction beyond the endpoint quotas.

At each primary vertex, rank contracts by decreasing primary aspiration index \(r\). At each secondary vertex, rank them by decreasing secondary aspiration index \(s\). Break equal-index ties arbitrarily but consistently, obtaining strict orders. All listed contracts are acceptable, including those giving aspiration zero.

The graph is bipartite, its quotas and capacities are rational, and its edge orders are strict. Therefore every hypothesis of the stable-allocation theorem holds. Compute a stable allocation \(f\).

3. Recovering the requested masses and flows

For each retained contract \(e=(i,j,r,s)\), set

\(x_{ijrs}=f_e.\)

Set all other matching flows to zero. Define matched aspiration masses by

\(Y_{ir}=\sum_{j,s}x_{ijrs},\qquad Z_{js}=\sum_{i,r}x_{ijrs}.\)

The unmatched masses are

\(u_i=p_i-\sum_rY_{ir}\geq0,\qquad v_j=q_j-\sum_sZ_{js}\geq0.\)

Assign these masses aspiration zero:

\(y_{ir}=Y_{ir}+\mathbf 1_{\{r=0\}}u_i,\qquad z_{js}=Z_{js}+\mathbf 1_{\{s=0\}}v_j.\)

Consequently,

\(\sum_r y_{ir}=p_i,\qquad \sum_s z_{js}=q_j.\)

Also,

\(\sum_{j,s}x_{ijrs}\leq y_{ir},\qquad \sum_{i,r}x_{ijrs}\leq z_{js},\)

with equality whenever the corresponding aspiration index is positive.

Finally, every retained contract is feasible by construction. Hence

\(x_{ijrs}>0\Longrightarrow A_{ij}(r\delta,s\delta)=1.\)

Thus all requirements except the no-blocking condition have been verified.

4. Proof of the no-blocking condition

Suppose, towards a contradiction, that for some \(i,j,r,s\),

\(y_{ir}>0,\qquad z_{js}>0,\qquad A_{ij}((r+h)\delta,(s+h)\delta)=1.\)

Because both utilities are strictly below \((N+1)\delta\), this feasible table entry implies

\(r+h\leq N,\qquad s+h\leq N.\)

By the coverage property, the graph contains the retained contract

\(e=(i,j,r+h,S_{ij}(r+h)),\)

where

\(S_{ij}(r+h)\geq s+h>s.\)

This contract gives each endpoint a strictly higher aspiration than the respective positive-mass class under consideration.

At primary vertex \(i\), every flow contributing to \(Y_{ir}\) is on a contract strictly worse than \(e\), since its primary index is \(r<r+h\). If \(r=0\), the remaining contribution to \(y_{ir}\) is unmatched mass. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\substack{g\ni i\\g\succeq_i e}}f_g\leq p_i-y_{ir}<p_i.\)

So \(e\) is not dominated at \(i\).

The same argument at secondary vertex \(j\), using \(S_{ij}(r+h)>s\), gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\substack{g\ni j\\g\succeq_j e}}f_g\leq q_j-z_{js}<q_j.\)

So \(e\) is not dominated at \(j\).

Moreover, \(e\) itself occurs in both sums. Hence

\(f_e<p_i,\qquad f_e<q_j,\)

and consequently

\(f_e<\min\{p_i,q_j\}=c_e.\)

Thus \(e\) is an unsaturated edge dominated at neither endpoint, contradicting stability of \(f\).

We have proved

\(y_{ir}>0\ \text{and}\ z_{js}>0\Longrightarrow A_{ij}(r\delta+\epsilon,s\delta+\epsilon)=0.\)

The argument also covers classes carrying flow to each other: their lower-aspiration contract is strictly worse than \(e\) at both endpoints.

5. Exact arithmetic and running time

Scanning the input and constructing the retained contracts takes \(O(L)\) time under the ordinary explicit-table representation. Let \(K=|E|\leq dm\). Sorting the incident preference lists requires \(O(K\log(K+1))\) comparisons. Stable allocation then requires \(O((\tau+K)^2)\) arithmetic and comparison operations.

For an explicit bit bound, write each input mass as a binary-encoded numerator divided by a positive denominator. Let \(D\) be the product of all mass denominators. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Multiplying all quotas and capacities by \(D\) gives integer data with \(O(L)\)-bit values.

The bipartite inductive algorithm uses additions, subtractions, comparisons, and minima of residual quantities; it preserves integrality. Its quantities remain bounded by the total scaled quota, which also has \(O(L)\) bits. Dividing the resulting allocation by \(D\) therefore gives rational flows with \(O(L)\)-bit numerators and denominators. The recovered aspiration masses have the same bound.

Using a conservative \(O(L^2)\) cost per arithmetic operation, including preprocessing and output, yields

\(T_{\mathrm{bit}}=O\!\left(L+(\tau+dm)^2L^2\right).\)

Since \(d\leq\tau^2/4\), this is bounded by

\(O\!\left(L+\tau^4m^2L^2\right).\)

There is no factor proportional to the numerical magnitudes of the masses. The parameter \(h\) affects the input length through the \(d(m+h)^2\) supplied Boolean entries.

6. Conclusion

Every promised instance admits the required rational outcome, and the construction above finds one in polynomial time. Therefore infeasibility never occurs within the promised input class.

The profile assumptions enter through the exact feasibility tables and the stated utility bound. No computation of real resource witnesses is needed.
