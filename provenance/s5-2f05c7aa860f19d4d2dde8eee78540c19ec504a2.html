The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational per-capita capacities \(c_{jr}\) and a finite support \(T\) of service types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), budgets \(B_t\), demand limits \(D_t\), and base-demand vectors \(a_{tjr}\), compute an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal primal-dual solution in time polynomial in \(M,R,|T|,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\). The primal variables \(z_{tj}\geq 0\) define per-type allocations \(x_{tjr}=a_{tjr}z_{tj}\); maximize \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t B_t\log(\sum_j z_{tj})\), subject to \(\sum_j z_{tj}\leq D_t\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tjr}\leq c_{jr}\). The dual resource prices should support the typewise budget-optimal bundles to \(\varepsilon\) accuracy, yielding the continuous analogue of a non-wasteful, frugal market equilibrium.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(M\geq1\) nodes and \(R\geq1\) resource kinds; write \(m=MR\). Capacities \(c_{jr}\), per-person budgets \(B_t\), finite demand limits \(D_t\), and base demands \(a_{tjr}\) are strictly positive binary rationals. Every type can use every node. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A person's utility for a nonnegative bundle \(y\) is \(U_t(y)=\min\{D_t,\sum_j\min_r y_{jr}/a_{tjr}\}\). At nonnegative prices \(p\), individual purchases maximize this utility subject only to \(\sum_{j,r}p_{jr}y_{jr}\leq B_t\), without capacity constraints.
Consider the primal program \(\max F(z)=\sum_t\mu_t B_t\log u_t\), where \(u_t=\sum_j z_{tj}>0\), \(z_{tj}\geq0\), \(u_t\leq D_t\), \(x_{tjr}=a_{tjr}z_{tj}\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tjr}\leq c_{jr}\). Let \(p\) be the capacity multipliers in its Lagrange dual and \(G\) its dual objective; demand-limit multipliers are also included.
An exact non-wasteful, frugal equilibrium uses these proportional bundles, permits no utility improvement using remaining capacity, clears every positively priced resource, and has \(z_{tj}>0\) only at nodes minimizing \(q_{tj}=\sum_r p_{jr}a_{tjr}\). Arbitrary splitting among tied cheapest nodes is allowed.
For binary rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), an approximate solution consists of a primal feasible allocation and dual feasible multipliers with \(G-F(z)\leq\varepsilon\). Additionally, for every type require \(\sum_jq_{tj}z_{tj}\leq B_t+\varepsilon\), \(u_t\geq\min\{D_t,B_t/q_t\}-\varepsilon\), and \(\sum_jz_{tj}(q_{tj}-q_t)\leq\varepsilon\), where \(q_t=\min_jq_{tj}\) and the utility benchmark is \(D_t\) when \(q_t=0\). Require \(p_{jr}(c_{jr}-\sum_t\mu_tx_{tjr})\leq\varepsilon\) for every resource.

Can this approximate primal-dual equilibrium problem be solved in time polynomial in \(M,R,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\), equivalently polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many services, non-wasteful, frugal equilibria are exactly the primal-dual optima of the corresponding capped logarithmic program; they exist and have a unique utility vector. A barrier-method discussion bounds centering steps but supplies no full bit-complexity bound for the approximation requirements above. [Nguyen, Le, and Bhargava](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.09756v4)
The search located no result explicitly treating the support-distribution problem with these simultaneous approximation requirements.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the masses to sum to one, interpreting budgets, demand limits, allocations, and capacities per person.
  • Required strictly positive capacities, budgets, finite demand limits, and base demands, preserving the stated positive-demand setting and the nondegeneracy needed for logarithmic utility and interior feasibility.
  • Retained access to every node, as in the simplified discrete model; defined m as the number MR of node-resource goods.
  • Defined epsilon accuracy by an additive primal-dual gap together with additive individual budget, utility, frugality, and market-clearing errors; primal and dual feasibility remain exact.
  • Included demand-limit dual multipliers and used capacity multipliers of the mass-weighted program as per-unit resource prices.
  • Allowed arbitrary allocation across tied cheapest nodes and defined the zero-price utility benchmark to be the demand limit.

Polynomial-time solvability

The approximate equilibrium problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(M,R,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\). The proof requires a gap substantially smaller than \(\varepsilon\), but the additional precision has polynomial bit length.

The algorithm below returns rational allocations and multipliers with exact primal and dual feasibility and all the requested approximation guarantees.

1. Dual formulation and a nonnegative gap identity

Write

\(W=\sum_t\mu_tB_t\).

Normalize the demand-limit multiplier as \(\mu_t\lambda_t\), where \(\lambda_t\geq0\). The capacity multipliers remain \(p_{jr}\geq0\). Define

\(q_{tj}=\sum_rp_{jr}a_{tjr}\), \(\qquad q_t=\min_jq_{tj}\).

For fixed multipliers, maximizing the Lagrangian over nonnegative allocations gives the dual objective

\(G(p,\lambda)=\sum_{j,r}p_{jr}c_{jr}+\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_tD_t+\sum_t\mu_tB_t\left(\log\frac{B_t}{q_t+\lambda_t}-1\right)\),

provided \(q_t+\lambda_t>0\) for every type. Otherwise its value is infinite. Indeed, for a fixed total utility, the Lagrangian assigns that utility to a cheapest node, and its remaining maximization is

\(\sup_{u>0}\{B_t\log u-(q_t+\lambda_t)u\}\),

attained at \(u=B_t/(q_t+\lambda_t)\).

Introduce auxiliary variables \(s_t>0\) with the linear constraints

\(s_t\leq q_{tj}+\lambda_t\qquad\text{for every }t,j\).

Define

\(\widetilde G(p,\lambda,s)=\sum_{j,r}p_{jr}c_{jr}+\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_tD_t+\sum_t\mu_tB_t\left(\log\frac{B_t}{s_t}-1\right)\).

These constraints imply \(G(p,\lambda)\leq\widetilde G(p,\lambda,s)\). Consequently, it suffices to make

\(\Phi(z,p,\lambda,s)=\widetilde G(p,\lambda,s)-F(z)\)

small.

For a primal feasible allocation, let

\(C_{jr}(z)=\sum_t\mu_ta_{tjr}z_{tj}\),

\(A_t=\sum_jz_{tj}(q_{tj}+\lambda_t-s_t)\),

and

\(H_t=B_t\left(\frac{s_tu_t}{B_t}-1-\log\frac{s_tu_t}{B_t}\right)\).

Direct expansion gives the identity

\(\Phi=\sum_{j,r}p_{jr}(c_{jr}-C_{jr}(z))+\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_t(D_t-u_t)+\sum_t\mu_tA_t+\sum_t\mu_tH_t.\)

Every term on the right is nonnegative. For \(H_t\), this follows from \(\log v\leq v-1\) for \(v>0\).

Also, if

\(J_t=\sum_jz_{tj}(q_{tj}-q_t)\),

then

\(A_t=J_t+(q_t+\lambda_t-s_t)u_t\geq J_t\).

Thus a bound \(\Phi\leq\delta\) immediately implies

\(p_{jr}(c_{jr}-C_{jr}(z))\leq\delta\),

\(\lambda_t(D_t-u_t)\leq\delta/\mu_t\),

and

\(J_t\leq A_t\leq\delta/\mu_t\).

The remaining work is to control expenditure and individual utility, and to obtain \(\Phi\leq\delta\) with polynomial bit complexity.

2. Polynomial-size bounds containing an optimal pair

Use the positive rational constants

\(\mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t,\quad B_{\min}=\min_tB_t,\quad B_{\max}=\max_tB_t,\)

\(D_{\min}=\min_tD_t,\quad D_{\max}=\max_tD_t,\quad c_{\min}=\min_{j,r}c_{jr},\quad a_{\max}=\max_{t,j,r}a_{tjr}.\)

Set

\(\kappa=\min\left\{\frac{D_{\min}}{4M},\frac{c_{\min}}{4a_{\max}}\right\},\qquad z^0_{tj}=\kappa,\qquad u^0=M\kappa.\)

This allocation is strictly feasible. Its utilities satisfy \(u^0\leq D_{\min}/4\), and, using \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\),

\(C_{jr}(z^0)=\kappa\sum_t\mu_ta_{tjr}\leq\kappa a_{\max}\leq c_{\min}/4.\)

The primal feasible set, with zero utilities allowed temporarily, is compact because \(0\leq z_{tj}\leq D_t\). There is an optimum with every utility positive: the objective is finite at \(z^0\), whereas it tends to negative infinity if any utility tends to zero; the other utilities remain bounded.

Let \(z^*\) be an optimum. The segment from \(z^*\) to \(z^0\) is feasible. Its one-sided objective derivative at \(z^*\) is therefore nonpositive:

\(\sum_t\mu_tB_t\left(\frac{u^0}{u_t^*}-1\right)\leq0.\)

It follows that

\(\sum_t\frac{\mu_tB_tu^0}{u_t^*}\leq W.\)

Each summand is positive, so every optimal utility satisfies

\(u_t^*\geq\frac{\mu_tB_tu^0}{W}\geq\alpha,\qquad \alpha=\frac{\mu_{\min}B_{\min}u^0}{W}>0.\)

Strict feasibility verifies Slater’s condition. The finite-dimensional concave-programming KKT theorem therefore supplies optimal multipliers satisfying

\(q_{tj}^*+\lambda_t^*\geq B_t/u_t^*\),

with equality whenever \(z_{tj}^*>0\), together with

\(p_{jr}^*(c_{jr}-C_{jr}(z^*))=0,\qquad \lambda_t^*(D_t-u_t^*)=0.\)

Multiplying the stationarity equalities by \(\mu_tz_{tj}^*\) and summing gives

\(W=\sum_{j,r}p_{jr}^*c_{jr}+\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_t^*D_t.\)

Hence

\(p_{jr}^*\leq W/c_{\min},\qquad \lambda_t^*\leq W/(\mu_{\min}D_{\min}).\)

Taking \(s_t^*=B_t/u_t^*\) gives

\(B_{\min}/D_{\max}\leq s_t^*\leq B_{\max}/\alpha.\)

Because \(u_t^*>0\), at least one node has \(z_{tj}^*>0\). Stationarity then implies \(s_t^*=q_t^*+\lambda_t^*\). Thus this lifted optimal pair has \(\Phi=0\).

All the constants just constructed are rational numbers of polynomial encoding length.

3. An explicit bounded domain with an interior ball

Define

\(\ell=\alpha/2,\qquad \sigma=B_{\min}/(2D_{\max})\),

and the rational upper bound

\(H=4\left(1+D_{\max}+\frac{W}{c_{\min}}+\frac{W}{\mu_{\min}D_{\min}}+\frac{B_{\max}}{\alpha}+\frac{B_{\min}}{D_{\max}}\right).\)

Let \(K\) be the rational polytope consisting of the variables \((z,p,\lambda,s)\) subject to:

  • all original primal feasibility constraints and \(u_t\geq\ell\);
  • \(0\leq p_{jr}\leq H\) and \(0\leq\lambda_t\leq H\);
  • \(\sigma\leq s_t\leq H\);
  • \(s_t\leq q_{tj}+\lambda_t\) for every \(t,j\).

The preceding section proves that \(K\) contains a point \(y^*\) with \(\Phi(y^*)=0\).

It also has an explicit strict interior point \(y^0\): use the allocation \(z^0\), and set

\(p_{jr}^0=H/4,\qquad \lambda_t^0=H/2,\qquad s_t^0=H/4.\)

For the primal lower utility constraint, \(\alpha\leq u^0\), so \(u^0>\ell\). All other primal inequalities were already strict. The dual node inequalities have slack at least \(H/4\), and the choice of \(H\) makes every box inequality strict.

Write the defining inequalities as \(a_i\cdot y\leq b_i\). A computable rational interior radius is

\(r_0=\min_i\frac{b_i-a_i\cdot y^0}{2(1+\lVert a_i\rVert_1)}>0.\)

Indeed, if \(\lVert h\rVert_2\leq r_0\), then

\(|a_i\cdot h|\leq\lVert a_i\rVert_1r_0<(b_i-a_i\cdot y^0)/2.\)

Thus the Euclidean ball of radius \(r_0\) centered at \(y^0\) lies in \(K\). The radius has polynomial encoding length.

The dimension is

\(n=\tau M+m+2\tau.\)

Every coordinate in \(K\) lies between zero and \(H\), since \(z_{tj}\leq D_{\max}<H\). Therefore \(K\) lies in the origin-centered ball of rational radius

\(R_0=nH.\)

4. Polynomial-time computation of a small gap

The function \(\Phi\) is convex and differentiable on \(K\). Its gradient is

\(\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial z_{tj}}=-\frac{\mu_tB_t}{u_t},\qquad \frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial p_{jr}}=c_{jr},\qquad \frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\lambda_t}=\mu_tD_t,\qquad \frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial s_t}=-\frac{\mu_tB_t}{s_t}.\)

In particular, the gradient is exactly rational at rational query points.

A rational Lipschitz bound on \(K\) is

\(\Lambda=1+\frac{MW}{\ell}+\sum_{j,r}c_{jr}+\sum_t\mu_tD_t+\frac{W}{\sigma}.\)

The sum of the absolute gradient coordinates is smaller than \(\Lambda\), so \(\Phi\) is \(\Lambda\)-Lipschitz in Euclidean distance.

Fix a positive rational \(\delta<1\). We now justify an algorithm returning a rational point in \(K\) with \(\Phi\leq\delta\).

A sublevel set with a known interior radius

Set

\(C=1+2\Lambda R_0,\qquad \theta=\delta/(4C).\)

Since \(\Phi(y^*)=0\) and both \(y^0,y^*\) belong to \(K\), Lipschitz continuity gives \(\Phi(y^0)<C\). By convexity, the point

\(y^\theta=(1-\theta)y^*+\theta y^0\)

satisfies \(\Phi(y^\theta)\leq\delta/4\).

Convexity of \(K\) also implies that the ball of radius \(\theta r_0\) centered at \(y^\theta\) lies in \(K\): each of its points is a convex combination of \(y^*\) and a point in the radius-\(r_0\) ball around \(y^0\).

Consequently,

\(S=\{y\in K:\Phi(y)\leq\delta/2\}\)

contains a ball of rational radius

\(r=\min\{\theta r_0,\delta/(4\Lambda)\}.\)

This follows because moving by distance at most \(r\) changes \(\Phi\) by at most \(\delta/4\). The center need not be known; the radius is explicitly computable.

A rational cutting procedure

At a rational query point \(x\):

  • Check every defining inequality of \(K\) exactly. If one is violated, its normal gives a rational central cut containing \(S\).
  • Otherwise, compute a rational \(\widehat\Phi(x)\) with error at most \(\delta/8\).
  • If \(\widehat\Phi(x)\leq7\delta/8\), return \(x\). It belongs to \(K\) and satisfies \(\Phi(x)\leq\delta\).
  • Otherwise, \(\Phi(x)>3\delta/4\). Return the exact rational gradient cut

\(\nabla\Phi(x)\cdot(y-x)\leq0.\)

The last cut contains \(S\). For \(y\in S\), convexity gives

\(\nabla\Phi(x)\cdot(y-x)\leq\Phi(y)-\Phi(x)<-\delta/4.\)

The gradient is nonzero, since its capacity-price coordinates are the strictly positive capacities.

The rational, rounded ellipsoid cutting-plane theorem now applies: an outer radius and a positive inner radius are known, and every unsuccessful query returns a nonzero rational central cut containing \(S\). Its bit-complexity guarantee gives termination with an accepted point in time polynomial in the dimension, geometric encoding lengths, and oracle cost. The standard rounded implementation preserves containment and decreases volume until it cannot contain the promised radius-\(r\) ball. See Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver, [*Geometric Algorithms and Combinatorial Optimization*, Chapter 5](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4).

Here, \(\log(R_0/r)\) is polynomially bounded in \(M,R,\tau,L,\log(1/\delta)\).

For completeness, the logarithm evaluations also have polynomial bit complexity. Scale any positive rational argument as \(2^kv\), with \(1\leq v<2\), and use

\(\log v=2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{2i+1}}{2i+1},\qquad w=\frac{v-1}{v+1}\in[0,1/3).\)

The tail decreases geometrically. The same expansion computes \(\log2\). Polynomially many rational operations therefore provide the required absolute accuracy; the weights in \(\Phi\) merely add their polynomial encoding lengths to the precision requirement.

Thus we can obtain exact feasibility and \(\Phi\leq\delta\) in time polynomial in \(M,R,\tau,L,\log(1/\delta)\).

5. Choosing a gap that guarantees every individual condition

For \(v>0\),

\(v-1-\log v\geq\frac{(v-1)^2}{2\max\{1,v\}}.\)

To verify this, for \(v\geq1\) integrate \((x-1)/x\) from \(1\) to \(v\), bounding \(1/x\) below by \(1/v\). For \(v\leq1\), integrate \((1-x)/x\) from \(v\) to \(1\), bounding \(1/x\) below by \(1\).

Applying this inequality to \(H_t\) yields

\(H_t\geq\frac{(s_tu_t-B_t)^2}{2\max\{B_t,s_tu_t\}}.\)

On \(K\), define

\(K_{\mathrm{money}}=B_{\max}+HD_{\max}.\)

Then \(\max\{B_t,s_tu_t\}\leq K_{\mathrm{money}}\). The gap identity therefore implies

\(|s_tu_t-B_t|\leq\sqrt{\frac{2K_{\mathrm{money}}\delta}{\mu_t}}.\)

Choose the rational constants

\(b=\min\{1/2,B_{\min}/(4D_{\max})\}\)

and

\(\delta=\min\left\{\varepsilon,\frac{\mu_{\min}\varepsilon}{2},\frac{\mu_{\min}b^2\varepsilon^2}{2K_{\mathrm{money}}},\frac{\mu_{\min}B_{\min}\varepsilon^2}{4D_{\max}^2}\right\}.\)

For every type, this guarantees

\(|s_tu_t-B_t|\leq b\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon/2\),

\(|s_tu_t-B_t|\leq B_{\min}\varepsilon/(4D_{\max})\),

and

\(\delta/\mu_t\leq\varepsilon/2,\qquad \delta/\mu_t\leq B_{\min}\varepsilon^2/(4D_{\max}^2).\)

Expenditure

By the definition of \(A_t\),

\(\sum_jq_{tj}z_{tj}=s_tu_t-\lambda_tu_t+A_t.\)

Hence

\(\sum_jq_{tj}z_{tj}\leq B_t+|s_tu_t-B_t|+\delta/\mu_t\leq B_t+\varepsilon.\)

Frugality and resource complementarity

Section 1 gives

\(\sum_jz_{tj}(q_{tj}-q_t)=J_t\leq\delta/\mu_t\leq\varepsilon\)

and

\(p_{jr}(c_{jr}-C_{jr}(z))\leq\delta\leq\varepsilon.\)

Individual utility, including zero prices

Let \(w_t\) denote the required benchmark:

\(w_t=\min\{D_t,B_t/q_t\}\) if \(q_t>0\), and \(w_t=D_t\) if \(q_t=0\).

In both cases,

\(0<w_t\leq D_t\leq D_{\max},\qquad q_tw_t\leq B_t.\)

Suppose, for contradiction, that \(u_t<w_t-\varepsilon\), and put \(d=w_t-u_t>\varepsilon\). Then

\(B_t-q_tu_t\geq B_t-\frac{B_tu_t}{w_t}=\frac{B_td}{w_t}\geq\frac{B_{\min}d}{D_{\max}}>\frac{B_{\min}\varepsilon}{D_{\max}}.\)

On the other hand, \(s_t\leq q_t+\lambda_t\) implies

\(B_t-q_tu_t=B_t-s_tu_t+(s_t-q_t)u_t\leq |B_t-s_tu_t|+\lambda_tu_t.\)

Since \(D_t-u_t\geq d\), the demand-limit slack bound gives

\(\lambda_tu_t\leq\frac{\delta D_{\max}}{\mu_td}.\)

Our choice of \(\delta\) consequently yields

\(B_t-q_tu_t\leq\frac{B_{\min}\varepsilon}{4D_{\max}}+\frac{B_{\min}\varepsilon^2}{4D_{\max}d}<\frac{B_{\min}\varepsilon}{2D_{\max}},\)

a contradiction. Therefore \(u_t\geq w_t-\varepsilon\). This argument never divides by \(q_t\), so it also proves the required zero-price case.

6. Output and complexity

Run the algorithm of Section 4 with the \(\delta\) chosen in Section 5. Output \(z\), the proportional bundles \(x_{tjr}=a_{tjr}z_{tj}\), capacity multipliers \(p_{jr}\), and demand-limit multipliers \(\mu_t\lambda_t\).

Primal feasibility is exact. Dual feasibility and finiteness follow from

\(q_t+\lambda_t\geq s_t\geq\sigma>0.\)

The true duality gap satisfies

\(0\leq G(p,\lambda)-F(z)\leq\widetilde G(p,\lambda,s)-F(z)=\Phi\leq\delta\leq\varepsilon.\)

Section 5 proves all the additional requirements.

Every domain bound and precision parameter has polynomial encoding length, and

\(\log(1/\delta)\leq\operatorname{poly}(M,R,\tau,L)+2\log(1/\varepsilon).\)

The algorithm therefore has the requested polynomial running time. It depends on the explicitly listed support types only. Since \(M,R\leq m=MR\) and \(n=\tau M+m+2\tau\), the same guarantee is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\).
