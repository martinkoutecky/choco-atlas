The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\tau\) types with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\), integer weight vectors \(a_t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^k\), and a feasible rational aggregate \(z\), let \(N_M(z)\) count labelled coalitions with aggregate weight \(Mz\) in replications having \(M\mu_t\) members of each type. Compute \(\Phi(z)=\limsup_{M\to\infty,\,N_M(z)>0} M^{-1}\log N_M(z)\) and an approximately maximizing coalition-mass profile.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(m=k\geq1\) be the number of weight coordinates. Each type has a binary-encoded vector \(a_t\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\setminus\{0\}\). Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the masses, weights, and rational aggregate \(z\).
A coalition-mass profile is \(x\in P(z):=\{x\in\mathbb R^{\tau}:0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t,\ \sum_t x_ta_t=z\}\); the input promises \(P(z)\neq\varnothing\).
For positive integers \(M\) with every \(M\mu_t\) integral, form a society of distinguishable members, with \(M\mu_t\) members of type \(t\). Let \(N_M(z)\) count all subsets whose aggregate weight is exactly \(Mz\), including the empty subset when applicable.
Using natural logarithms, define \(\Phi(z)=\limsup_{M\to\infty,\,N_M(z)>0}M^{-1}\log N_M(z)\), with replication sizes restricted as above.
For rational \(x\in P(z)\), let \(C_M(x)\) count subsets selecting exactly \(Mx_t\) members of each type, and define \(R(x)=\limsup_{M\to\infty}M^{-1}\log C_M(x)\), restricting to sizes for which all \(M\mu_t\) and \(Mx_t\) are integral.
Given a positive integer precision parameter \(p\), the computational problem is to compute a rational \(v\) with \(|v-\Phi(z)|\leq2^{-p}\) and a rational \(x\in P(z)\) with \(R(x)\geq\Phi(z)-2^{-p}\).
Is this problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,p\), or is it NP-hard?
For finite labelled societies, exact aggregate-coalition counts admit a dynamic program; the stated bound is \(O(n\prod_{j=1}^{m}\omega_j)\), where \(\omega_j\) is the total weight in coordinate \(j\). This is a numerical-weight bound, not a polynomial bound in binary input length. [Source](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/115407/1/MPRA_paper_115407.pdf)

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,p\), in the ordinary Turing bit model. The proof must address exact feasibility, lower dimensional feasible sets, and the singularity of entropy derivatives at the boundary.

1. The replication limit is an entropy maximum

Define binary entropy by
\(h(q)=-q\log q-(1-q)\log(1-q)\) for \(q\in[0,1]\), with \(0\log 0=0\). Define
\(F(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t h(x_t/\mu_t)\) on \(P(z)\).

The function \(F\) is continuous and concave. Since the positive masses sum to one,
\(0\leq F(x)\leq\log 2\).
The promised nonempty set \(P(z)\) is compact, so the maximum
\(F^*=\max_{x\in P(z)}F(x)\)
exists.

We first prove
\(\Phi(z)=F^*\)
and, for every rational feasible profile \(x\),
\(R(x)=F(x)\).

For integers \(n\geq1\) and \(0\leq j\leq n\), the binomial entropy bounds are
\[ (n+1)^{-1}\exp(nh(j/n))\leq {n\choose j}\leq\exp(nh(j/n)). \]
Here and below, this displayed formula is read as
\((n+1)^{-1}\exp(nh(j/n))\leq {n\choose j}\leq\exp(nh(j/n))\).

For completeness, when \(0<j<n\), consider a binomial random variable with success probability \(q=j/n\). Its probability at \(j\) is
\({n\choose j}\exp(-nh(j/n))\).
This probability is at most one. It is also at least \(1/(n+1)\): the ratio of the probabilities at successive indices \(\ell+1\) and \(\ell\) is
\((n-\ell)q/((\ell+1)(1-q))\), which shows that \(j\) is a mode. The endpoint cases follow directly.

For a rational profile \(x\), at every replication size permitted in the definition of \(R(x)\),
\(C_M(x)=\prod_t {M\mu_t\choose Mx_t}\).
Consequently,
\(F(x)-\tau\log(M+1)/M\leq M^{-1}\log C_M(x)\leq F(x)\).
There are arbitrarily large permitted sizes, namely multiples of a common denominator of the coordinates of \(\mu\) and \(x\). Thus the restricted limit exists and equals \(F(x)\).

Now fix an admissible size \(M\). A coalition is specified, as far as its aggregate is concerned, by its type counts \(u_t\). Hence
\(N_M(z)=\sum_u\prod_t {M\mu_t\choose u_t}\),
where the sum ranges over integral vectors satisfying
\(0\leq u_t\leq M\mu_t\) and \(\sum_tu_ta_t=Mz\).

Each summand corresponds to the feasible profile \(u/M\), and is at most \(\exp(MF^*)\). There are at most \((M+1)^\tau\) summands. Therefore, whenever \(N_M(z)>0\),
\(M^{-1}\log N_M(z)\leq F^*+\tau\log(M+1)/M\).
This proves \(\Phi(z)\leq F^*\).

Conversely, for any rational \(x\in P(z)\), its permitted replication sizes satisfy
\(N_M(z)\geq C_M(x)>0\).
Thus \(\Phi(z)\geq F(x)\).

Finally, a nonempty rational polytope has rational vertices, and rational convex combinations of its vertices are dense in it. By continuity of \(F\),
\(\sup_{x\in P(z)\cap\mathbb Q^\tau}F(x)=F^*\).
This proves both asserted identities. It also establishes that the sizes with positive aggregate counts are unbounded.

2. Finding the affine hull and a rational interior point

Write \(A\) for the \(m\)-by-\(\tau\) matrix whose columns are the vectors \(a_t\), and abbreviate \(P(z)\) to \(P\).

We use the standard bit-model theorem that rational linear programming is polynomial-time solvable and can return an optimal rational vertex of polynomial encoding length. This theorem applies to bounded feasible rational polyhedra, including those of lower dimension. [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1981a.pdf)

For each coordinate \(t\), solve the two linear programs minimizing and maximizing \(x_t\) over \(P\). Let \(x_0\) be the average of their \(2\tau\) returned optimizers. Define
\(I_0=\{t:\max_{x\in P}x_t=0\}\)
and
\(I_1=\{t:\min_{x\in P}x_t=\mu_t\}\).

For every \(t\notin I_0\cup I_1\), we have
\(0<x_{0,t}<\mu_t\).
Indeed, a maximizing optimizer contributes a positive coordinate to the average, and a minimizing optimizer contributes a coordinate strictly below \(\mu_t\).

Consider the rational affine space
\(H=\{x:Ax=z,\ x_t=0\text{ for }t\in I_0,\ x_t=\mu_t\text{ for }t\in I_1\}\).
It is exactly the affine hull of \(P\). To verify this, observe that \(P\subseteq H\), while every remaining box inequality is strict at \(x_0\). Therefore a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(x_0\) within \(H\) lies in \(P\).

Let \(d=\dim H\). If \(d=0\), then \(P=\{x_0\}\). Returning \(x_0\) and a sufficiently accurate rational evaluation of \(F(x_0)\) solves the problem. Henceforth assume \(d\geq1\).

Rational Gaussian elimination gives a parametrization
\(x=x_0+By\), with \(y\in\mathbb R^d\), in which some \(d\) rows of \(B\) form the identity matrix. These rows correspond to free coordinates of \(x\). Define
\(K_0=\{y:x_0+By\in P\}\).

Because those free coordinates belong to \([0,\mu_t]\subseteq[0,1]\), every coordinate of \(y\in K_0\) belongs to \([-1,1]\). In particular,
\(K_0\subseteq\mathbb B(0,R)\), where \(R=d+1\) and \(\mathbb B\) denotes a Euclidean ball.

Set
\(s=\min_{t\notin I_0\cup I_1}\min\{x_{0,t},\mu_t-x_{0,t}\}>0\),
and
\(\beta=\max\{1,\max_t\sum_{i=1}^d|B_{ti}|\}\).

Then
\(\mathbb B(0,r_0)\subseteq K_0\), where \(r_0=s/(2\beta)\).
Indeed, for \(\|y\|_2\leq r_0\), each coordinate changes from \(x_0\) by at most
\(\beta\|y\|_2\leq s/2\); the forced coordinates do not change.

All these quantities have polynomial encoding length. Optimal vertices have polynomial encoding length by determinant bounds; averaging polynomially many such vertices preserves polynomial encoding length. Gaussian elimination, and the subsequent computations of \(s,\beta,r_0\), do likewise. Thus an exponentially small interior radius is allowed, but its logarithm has polynomial magnitude.

3. Removing the boundary singularities at controlled cost

Put
\(\varepsilon=2^{-p}\), \(\eta=\varepsilon/4\),
and define the contracted polytope
\(P_\eta=(1-\eta)P+\eta x_0\).

Concavity and nonnegativity of \(F\) give, for an optimizer \(x^*\in P\),
\(F((1-\eta)x^*+\eta x_0)\geq(1-\eta)F^*+\eta F(x_0)\geq F^*-\eta\log 2\).
Consequently,
\(\max_{x\in P_\eta}F(x)\geq F^*-\varepsilon/4\).

In the preceding coordinates,
\(K=(1-\eta)K_0\)
parametrizes \(P_\eta\) through \(x=x_0+By\). It has an explicit rational inequality description:
\(\eta x_0\leq x_0+By\leq(1-\eta)\mu+\eta x_0\).
It also satisfies
\(\mathbb B(0,r)\subseteq K\subseteq\mathbb B(0,R)\),
where \(r=(1-\eta)r_0\).

For every unforced coordinate and every \(x\in P_\eta\),
\(x_t\geq\eta s\) and \(\mu_t-x_t\geq\eta s\).
The forced coordinates contribute zero entropy and have zero rows in \(B\).

Define \(f(y)=F(x_0+By)\). This function is concave and differentiable on a neighborhood of \(K\), with gradient
\(\nabla f(y)=\sum_{t\notin I_0\cup I_1}B_{t,\cdot}^{\,T}\log((\mu_t-x_t)/x_t)\),
where \(x=x_0+By\).

Choose a positive integer \(q\) such that \(\eta s\geq2^{-q}\). Such a \(q\) can be computed with magnitude polynomial in the input size and \(p\). Since both \(x_t\) and \(\mu_t-x_t\) lie between \(\eta s\) and one,
\(\left|\log((\mu_t-x_t)/x_t)\right|\leq q\log2\leq q\).
It follows that
\(\|\nabla f(y)\|_2\leq G\), where \(G=\tau\beta q\).

By integrating the gradient along a segment in the convex set \(K\), we obtain
\(|f(y)-f(w)|\leq G\|y-w\|_2\)
for all \(y,w\in K\). Thus \(f\) has an explicit Lipschitz bound whose encoding length is polynomial.

4. Polynomial-time rational evaluation and optimization

First, logarithms of positive rationals can be approximated in polynomial bit time. Here is an explicit construction.

Given a positive rational \(u\), write \(u=2^kv\) with \(1\leq v<2\). Put \(w=(v-1)/(v+1)\), so \(0\leq w<1/3\). Then
\(\log v=2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}w^{2j+1}/(2j+1)\).
After \(N\) terms, the tail is bounded by
\(2|w|^{2N+1}/(1-w^2)\).
The same series with \(w=1/3\) computes \(\log2\).

If \(u\) has encoding length \(b\), then \(|k|\) is bounded by a linear function of \(b\). Therefore absolute error \(2^{-a}\) requires only \(O(a+\log(b+1))\) terms. Rational arithmetic on these truncated series has polynomial bit complexity.

This gives polynomial-time rational approximations to both \(f(y)\) and \(\nabla f(y)\) at rational feasible points. For values, one may use
\(\mu_t h(x_t/\mu_t)=\mu_t\log\mu_t-x_t\log x_t-(\mu_t-x_t)\log(\mu_t-x_t)\),
with zero terms omitted.

To make the optimization guarantee explicit, set \(\delta=\varepsilon/4\). At each rational feasible point \(c\in K\), compute a rational vector \(\widehat g\) satisfying
\(\|\widehat g-\nabla f(c)\|_2\leq\delta/(8R)\).

This can be achieved by approximating every logarithm in the gradient formula to absolute error at most
\(\delta/(8R\tau\beta)\).
The triangle inequality and the definition of \(\beta\) prove the stated vector error bound.

We use the standard rational central-cut ellipsoid procedure: it maintains rational enclosing ellipsoids for the portions retained by successive central cuts. After \(O(d^2(1+\log(R/\rho)))\) cuts, their volume is smaller than that of a ball of radius \(\rho\). Rounding preserves containment and polynomial bit complexity when the supplied cut normals have polynomial encoding length. In dimension one, interval bisection provides the same property. [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1981a.pdf)

Start with the ellipsoid \(\mathbb B(0,R)\). At each center \(c\):

  • If \(c\notin K\), use an exactly violated rational linear inequality to make a central cut retaining all of \(K\).
  • If \(c\in K\), record \(c\) and compute \(\widehat g\). If \(\widehat g\neq0\), retain the halfspace \(\widehat g^{\,T}(y-c)\geq0\).
  • If \(\widehat g=0\), stop and retain \(c\) as a candidate.

We now prove that polynomially many iterations produce a candidate whose value is within \(\delta\) of \(\max_K f\).

Let \(y^*\) maximize \(f\) on \(K\). Define
\(\alpha=\delta/(8R(G+1))\), \(\rho=\alpha r\),
and the ball
\(D=\mathbb B((1-\alpha)y^*,\rho)\).

Convexity of \(K\) and \(\mathbb B(0,r)\subseteq K\) imply \(D\subseteq K\): every point of \(D\) is a convex combination of \(y^*\) and a point of \(\mathbb B(0,r)\).

Moreover, every \(y\in D\) satisfies
\(\|y-y^*\|_2\leq\alpha(R+r)\leq2\alpha R\).
The Lipschitz bound therefore gives
\(f(y)\geq f(y^*)-2\alpha RG\geq f(y^*)-\delta/4\).

Suppose a feasible center \(c\) has
\(f(c)<f(y^*)-\delta\).
Concavity implies, for every \(y\in D\),
\(\nabla f(c)^T(y-c)\geq f(y)-f(c)>3\delta/4\).
Since \(y,c\in K\), their distance is at most \(2R\). Hence
\(\widehat g^{\,T}(y-c)>3\delta/4-(\delta/(8R))\,2R=\delta/2\).
Thus the objective cut retains the entire ball \(D\).

Every constraint cut also retains \(D\). Consequently, if every recorded feasible center had gap greater than \(\delta\), all ellipsoids would continue to contain \(D\). After enough iterations their volume would be smaller than the volume of \(D\), a contradiction.

The stopping case \(\widehat g=0\) is also valid. Concavity gives
\(f(y^*)-f(c)\leq\nabla f(c)^T(y^*-c)\leq\delta/4\).

Therefore a recorded candidate has value at least
\(\max_K f-\delta\).
The number of iterations is polynomial because
\(\rho=\delta r/(8R(G+1))\)
has polynomial encoding length. Exact constraint checks and all gradient computations are polynomial-time operations at the polynomial-size rational ellipsoid centers.

This argument returns points exactly in \(K\), rather than points that merely approximate its constraints.

5. Constructing the requested outputs

Evaluate \(f\) at every recorded candidate with absolute error at most
\(\gamma=\varepsilon/16\).
Select a candidate \(\widehat y\) with the largest approximate value, and set
\(\widehat x=x_0+B\widehat y\).

Comparison of approximate values loses at most \(2\gamma\), so
\(F(\widehat x)\geq\max_{x\in P_\eta}F(x)-\delta-2\gamma\).
Combining this with the contraction bound yields
\(F(\widehat x)\geq F^*-\varepsilon/4-\varepsilon/4-\varepsilon/8=F^*-5\varepsilon/8\).

The profile \(\widehat x\) is rational, has polynomial encoding length, and belongs exactly to \(P_\eta\subseteq P(z)\). Section 1 therefore gives
\(R(\widehat x)=F(\widehat x)\geq\Phi(z)-\varepsilon\).

Let \(v\) be its computed rational entropy value. Then
\(|v-F(\widehat x)|\leq\varepsilon/16\).
Since \(F(\widehat x)\leq F^*=\Phi(z)\), we obtain
\(|v-\Phi(z)|\leq5\varepsilon/8+\varepsilon/16=11\varepsilon/16<\varepsilon\).

All preprocessing, optimization, and evaluation steps take time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,p\). Thus both outputs required by the task are obtained with the stipulated guarantees. The numerical dynamic program for finite exact counts is unnecessary for this replication-limit problem.
