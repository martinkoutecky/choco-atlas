The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite sets of firm types \(F\) and worker types \(W\), rational masses \(p_f,q_w\), strict rankings over counterpart types, and two explicitly listed rational lotteries over stable flows, compute their least upper and greatest lower bounds under each side's stochastic-dominance order, or report when a requested bound does not exist. A stable flow \(x\) satisfies \(x_{fw}\ge 0\), \(\sum_w x_{fw}\le p_f\), \(\sum_f x_{fw}\le q_w\), and, for each \(f,w\), \(\left(p_f-\sum_{w':\,w'\succeq_f w}x_{fw'}\right)\left(q_w-\sum_{f':\,f'\succeq_w f}x_{f'w}\right)=0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(F,W\) be nonempty disjoint sets of firm and worker types. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|F|+|W|\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Masses \(p_f,q_w\) need not sum to one or have equal totals. Each type strictly ranks all counterpart types above being unmatched; \(\succeq_a\) includes equality.
A stable flow is a real matrix satisfying \(x_{fw}\ge0\), \(\sum_w x_{fw}\le p_f\), \(\sum_f x_{fw}\le q_w\), and \(\bigl(p_f-\sum_{w':\,w'\succeq_f w}x_{fw'}\bigr)\bigl(q_w-\sum_{f':\,f'\succeq_w f}x_{f'w}\bigr)=0\) for every \((f,w)\). Let \(S\) be the stable-flow set and \(R=\operatorname{conv}(S)\) the set of expected flows of finite lotteries over stable flows.
The input gives two explicit lotteries, each with positive rational weights summing to one and rational stable-flow matrices. Identify lotteries having the same expected flow; write their expectations as \(u,v\).
For \(z,y\in R\), define \(z\succeq_F y\) by \(\sum_{w':\,w'\succeq_f w}z_{fw'}\ge\sum_{w':\,w'\succeq_f w}y_{fw'}\) for every \(f,w\); define \(\succeq_W\) analogously using column prefixes in workers’ rankings. Unmatched mass is the residual row or column mass.
Compute the least upper and greatest lower bounds of \(u,v\) within \(R\) under each order, returning their exact expected-flow matrices, or report separately that a requested bound does not exist. Let \(m=\max\{|F|,|W|\}\), let \(k\) be the total number of listed lottery terms, and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

Is this continuous bound-computation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite many-to-many markets with substitutable preferences satisfying the law of aggregate demand, rational random stable matchings have both bounds for each side, with explicit formulas after common-denominator refinement ([Juárez–Neme–Oviedo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.08156v5)).
Real-valued stable allocations and network stable flows have known lattice structures ([Fleiner](https://real.mtak.hu/20949/1/OnStableMatchings.pdf)); this concerns deterministic allocations and flows. No result settling the exact lottery problem above was located.

1. Identification with stable allocations

Put \(n=|F|\), \(s=|W|\), and \(d=ns\). Thus \(d\le m^2\). Construct the complete bipartite graph on the \(\tau=n+s\) supported types, with vertex quotas \(p_f,q_w\) and edge capacities
\(b_{fw}=\min\{p_f,q_w\}\).

These capacities impose no additional feasibility restriction: every feasible flow already satisfies \(x_{fw}\le b_{fw}\).

For a feasible flow \(x\), define
\(a_{fw}(x)=p_f-\sum_{w'\succeq_f w}x_{fw'}\) and
\(c_{fw}(x)=q_w-\sum_{f'\succeq_w f}x_{f'w}\).
Both are nonnegative. Moreover,

\(a_{fw}(x)=\bigl(p_f-\sum_{w'}x_{fw'}\bigr)+\sum_{w'\prec_f w}x_{fw'}.\)

Consequently, \(a_{fw}(x)>0\) holds exactly when firm \(f\) can increase its allocation on \((f,w)\), using either unmatched mass or mass assigned to strictly worse workers. The analogous statement holds for \(c_{fw}(x)\).

In the standard stable-allocation model, an edge blocks precisely when its allocation is below capacity and both endpoints can make such an improvement. This is the definition used in [Fleiner, Section 2](https://real.mtak.hu/20949/1/OnStableMatchings.pdf).

If both \(a_{fw}(x)\) and \(c_{fw}(x)\) are positive, then \(x_{fw}<p_f\) and \(x_{fw}<q_w\), hence \(x_{fw}<b_{fw}\). Therefore,

\( (f,w)\text{ blocks }x\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_{fw}(x)c_{fw}(x)>0.\)

Thus the stable allocations of this constructed instance are exactly the set \(S\) in the question. Its hypotheses are satisfied: the graph is finite and bipartite, quotas and capacities are positive, and preferences are strict. Equal total masses are unnecessary.

2. A polynomial-size extended formulation of \(R\)

We use the following known rotation representation. For a bipartite stable-allocation instance, one can compute a stable allocation \(x^0\), a poset on \(h=O(d)\) rotations, rotation vectors \(\rho_i\), and positive maximal weights \(r_i\), such that

\(S=\left\{x^0+\sum_{i=1}^{h}\lambda_i\rho_i:\lambda\in C\right\},\)

where

\(C=\left\{\lambda:0\le\lambda_i\le r_i,\quad i\prec j\text{ and }\lambda_j>0\Longrightarrow\lambda_i=r_i\right\}.\)

The representation and poset are computable in polynomial time. This is the strict-preference special case of [Karzanov, Sections 5–7, particularly Proposition 6.3 and equation (7.1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11556).

Here is the convexification step explicitly. Normalize by \(t_i=\lambda_i/r_i\), and define

\(P=\left\{t\in[0,1]^h:t_i\ge t_j\text{ whenever }i\prec j\right\},\)

\( \Phi(t)=x^0+\sum_{i=1}^{h}r_it_i\rho_i.\)

We claim that \(R=\Phi(P)\).

First, every normalized vector from \(C\) belongs to \(P\): if \(t_j>0\), each predecessor \(t_i\) equals \(1\); if \(t_j=0\), the required inequality follows from nonnegativity.

Conversely, take \(t\in P\). List its distinct coordinate values together with the endpoints as

\(0=\beta_0<\beta_1<\cdots<\beta_\ell=1.\)

For each \(a\), define the binary vector \(e^a\) by
\(e^a_i=1\) exactly when \(t_i\ge\beta_a\).
Because \(t_i\ge t_j\) whenever \(i\prec j\), the support of \(e^a\) is a down-set. Hence \((r_ie^a_i)_i\in C\). Also,

\(t=\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}(\beta_a-\beta_{a-1})e^a.\)

The coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one. Applying the affine map \(\Phi\) shows that \(\Phi(t)\) is an expectation of a finite lottery over stable flows. This proves the claim.

Thus \(R\) has an explicit extended formulation with \(O(d)\) variables and \(O(d^2)\) inequalities. It is compact.

3. Prefix coordinates and lottery identification

For \(X\in\{F,W\}\), let \(A_Xz\in\mathbb R^d\) be the vector of all preference-prefix sums on side \(X\). Then the order in the question is exactly

\(z\succeq_X y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A_Xz\ge A_Xy\)

coordinatewise.

Both maps \(A_F\) and \(A_W\) are invertible. For example, if firm \(f\) ranks workers as \(w_1,\ldots,w_s\), and its prefix coordinates are \(b_{f,1},\ldots,b_{f,s}\), then

\(z_{f,w_j}=b_{f,j}-b_{f,j-1},\qquad b_{f,0}=0.\)

The column version proves invertibility of \(A_W\).

Compute each lottery’s expectation by rational arithmetic. Two lotteries are identified exactly when their expected matrices coincide. Everything below depends only on these matrices \(u,v\).

4. Computing or rejecting a least upper bound

Fix \(X\in\{F,W\}\). Define the coordinatewise threshold

\(a^+=\max\{A_Xu,A_Xv\}\)

and the polytope

\(P_X^+=\{t\in P:A_X\Phi(t)\ge a^+\}.\)

Its image under \(\Phi\) is exactly the set of common upper bounds of \(u,v\) within \(R\).

If \(P_X^+\) is empty, no least upper bound exists. Otherwise, solve one linear program for every prefix coordinate:

\(\ell_j=\min_{t\in P_X^+}(A_X\Phi(t))_j,\qquad j=1,\ldots,d.\)

Each minimum exists because \(P_X^+\) is nonempty and compact. Finally, test feasibility of

\(t\in P_X^+,\qquad A_X\Phi(t)=\ell.\)

If feasible, return \(z=\Phi(t)\). This is a common upper bound. Every common upper bound \(y\) satisfies \(A_Xy\ge\ell=A_Xz\), so \(y\succeq_Xz\). Therefore \(z\) is the least upper bound.

If the final system is infeasible, no least upper bound exists. Indeed, any least upper bound \(z\) would satisfy \(A_Xz\le A_Xy\) for every common upper bound \(y\). Since \(z\) itself is a common upper bound, each coordinate would necessarily equal its corresponding minimum \(\ell_j\), contradicting infeasibility.

5. Computing or rejecting a greatest lower bound

Define

\(a^-=\min\{A_Xu,A_Xv\},\qquad P_X^-=\{t\in P:A_X\Phi(t)\le a^-\}.\)

If this polytope is empty, no greatest lower bound exists. Otherwise, compute

\(g_j=\max_{t\in P_X^-}(A_X\Phi(t))_j,\qquad j=1,\ldots,d,\)

and test

\(t\in P_X^-,\qquad A_X\Phi(t)=g.\)

A feasible solution yields the greatest lower bound \(z=\Phi(t)\): every common lower bound \(y\) satisfies \(A_Xy\le g=A_Xz\).

Conversely, a greatest lower bound would attain every coordinate maximum \(g_j\). Therefore infeasibility proves its nonexistence.

Run Sections 4 and 5 separately for both sides. This produces all four requested answers.

6. Exactness and running time

The rotation construction has polynomially bounded intermediate encoding lengths for integral data. Rational quotas and capacities can be scaled to integers using their common denominator, whose binary length is polynomial in the input length; scaling back preserves the representation. Thus all extended-formulation coefficients have polynomial bit length. These arithmetic guarantees are stated in [Karzanov’s computational-complexity discussion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11556).

Computing expectations takes \(O(kd)\) rational arithmetic operations. After polynomial-time rotation preprocessing, the four bound computations require at most \(4d+8\) exact rational linear programs. Each has \(O(d)\) variables, \(O(d^2)\) constraints, and coefficients of polynomial bit length. All are bounded feasibility or optimization problems.

Exact rational linear programming therefore gives polynomial bit running time and polynomial-size rational output matrices. In the requested parameters, the algorithm uses:

  • \(O(km^2)\) rational operations for the expectations;
  • preprocessing polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\);
  • \(O(m^2)\) exact linear programs, each with \(O(m^2)\) variables and \(O(m^4)\) constraints.

The total deterministic bit running time is \((m+\tau+k+L)^{O(1)}\). No enumeration proportional to masses or denominators occurs.

This settles the computational question without requiring that all four bounds always exist.
