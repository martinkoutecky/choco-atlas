1. Universal subsequential results

Let $P$ contain zero, all genuine buyer values, and all seller reserves. Put

$$ w_s=\nu_s\ell_s, \qquad W=\sum_{s\in S}w_s. $$

For an admissible $N$, the number of unit sellers is $NW$.

1.1. Every execution produces exactly $NW$ events

Initially, the private proxies provide a matching covering every unit. This remains true when a reserve is zero, because the construction includes its proxy.

Suppose a covering matching exists immediately before a sale. The chosen maximum matching then covers every residual unit. Removing the sold unit and decrementing the demand of its matched buyer preserves a matching covering all other units.

A departing set is removed only when the remaining buyers can cover the remaining units. Thus coverage is an invariant. After every buyer has departed, coverage forces the remaining unit set to be empty. Consequently,

$$ |E_N|=NW. $$

Fix any complete execution. Assign each unit seller to the chronological rank at which its unit was sold. These assignments form a perfect matching between unit sellers and event ranks, and all its edges belong to the union graph.

1.2. Finite eating guarantees

The following argument applies to any finite bipartite graph of unit sellers and unit-capacity events with a perfect matching and nonnegative event prices. It independently establishes the finite properties stated in the supplied [paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol055-icalp2016/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.142/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.142.pdf).

The eating rates are constant between exhaustion times. There are finitely many events, so accumulated shares and revenue are absolutely continuous.

Fix sellers $i,j$. At almost every time $t$ when $i$ has an available incident event, let $M_i(t)$ be the highest available incident price. Seller $i$ earns at rate $M_i(t)$. Every event incident to $i$ that $j$ currently eats has price at most $M_i(t)$. Since $j$ eats at total rate at most one,

$$ \sum_{e\in E_i}p_e r_j[e](t) \leq M_i(t) = \sum_{e\in E_i}p_e r_i[e](t). $$

If $i$ has no available incident event, both displayed revenue rates are zero almost everywhere. Integration proves finite envy-freeness.

For revenue, write $F(t)$ for the total revenue eaten by time $t$ and $T=\sum_e p_e$. Fix a perfect matching. For every unexhausted event $e$, its matched seller can access an event of price $p_e$, and therefore earns at rate at least $p_e$. Those matched sellers are distinct, so

$$ F'(t)\geq\sum_{e\text{ unexhausted at }t}p_e. $$

Every exhausted event has contributed its entire price. Nonnegative prices therefore give

$$ F'(t) \geq T-\sum_{e\text{ exhausted at }t}p_e \geq T-F(t). $$

Multiplying by $e^t$ and integrating from zero to one yields

$$ F(1)\geq(1-e^{-1})T. $$

1.3. Compactness and passage to the limit

For $q=(p,A)\in P\times 2^S$, event capacities and seller budgets give

$$ \eta_N(q)\geq0, \qquad \sum_q\eta_N(q)=W, $$

$$ z_{s,N}(q)\geq0, \qquad \sum_s z_{s,N}(q)\leq\eta_N(q), \qquad \sum_q z_{s,N}(q)\leq w_s. $$

Also, $z_{s,N}(p,A)=0$ when $s\notin A$.

The coordinate domain is finite for a fixed input, and all coordinates are bounded. Hence every sequence of admissible parameters tending to infinity has a subsequence along which $\eta_N$ and every $z_{s,N}$ converge simultaneously.

Let $(\eta,z)$ be such a limit. All preceding inequalities pass to it. In particular, $\eta(q)=0$ implies $z_s(q)=0$. Define

$$ x_s(q)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{z_s(q)}{w_s\eta(q)},&\eta(q)>0,\\ 0,&\eta(q)=0. \end{cases} $$

Then

$$ \int x_s\,d\eta = \frac{1}{w_s}\sum_q z_s(q) \leq1, $$

and, on every positive atom,

$$ \sum_s w_sx_s(q) = \frac{\sum_s z_s(q)}{\eta(q)} \leq1. $$

Thus every joint subsequential limit is feasible.

Finally, the finite revenue inequality gives

$$ \sum_s\sum_{(p,A)}p\,z_{s,N}(p,A) \geq (1-e^{-1}) \sum_{(p,A)}p\,\eta_N(p,A). $$

Both sides are finite linear combinations of convergent coordinates. Therefore every joint subsequential limit satisfies

$$ R\geq(1-e^{-1})\int p\,d\eta. $$

This proves neither uniqueness of the limit nor type-level envy-freeness.

2. An auction-realizable obstruction to uniform incidence

Consider one seller type $s$ and one genuine buyer type, with

$$ \nu_s=\mu_b=1, \qquad \ell_s=d_b=1, \qquad \rho_s=2, \qquad v_b=1, $$

and complete eligibility.

There are $N$ genuine buyers, $N$ unit sellers, and $N$ private proxies.

At price zero, no buyer is compulsory:

  • All units can be covered by their proxies, avoiding any prescribed genuine buyer.
  • Any prescribed proxy can be avoided by assigning its unit to a genuine buyer and all other units to their proxies.

Thus no sale occurs at zero.

At price one, the genuine buyers depart without purchasing: the proxies alone cover all units. Every remaining proxy must then purchase its own unit. Fixed lexicographic buyer ties determine their order, independently of seller priority, because each proxy has exactly one eligible unit.

Consequently, every event has price one and exactly one incident unit seller. For $N>1$, no event is incident to all unit sellers of its type.

Nevertheless,

$$ \eta_N(1,\{s\})=1, \qquad z_{s,N}(1,\{s\})=1, \qquad x_s(1,\{s\})=1. $$

The measures converge, and the kernel is feasible and envy-free.

This example also shows why averaging finite envy requires care. Define

$$ f_{s,N}(e) = \frac{|\{i:\operatorname{type}(i)=s,\ e\in E_i\}|}{Nw_s}, \qquad h_{s,N}(e) = \sum_{i:\operatorname{type}(i)=s}a_i[e]. $$

Averaging finite envy over sellers of types $s,s'$ gives

$$ \frac{1}{Nw_s}\sum_e p_eh_{s,N}(e) \geq \frac{1}{Nw_{s'}}\sum_e p_e f_{s,N}(e)h_{s',N}(e). $$

The desired type-level comparison replaces $f_{s,N}(e)$ by $\mathbf 1_{\{s\in A_e\}}$. The resulting discrepancy is

$$ \Delta_{s,s',N} = \frac{1}{Nw_{s'}} \sum_e p_e\bigl(\mathbf 1_{\{s\in A_e\}}-f_{s,N}(e)\bigr) h_{s',N}(e). $$

In this example, $f_{s,N}(e)=1/N$ and $h_{s,N}(e)=1$, so

$$ \Delta_{s,s,N}=1-\frac1N. $$

Thus neither uniform incidence nor vanishing of all these discrepancies holds universally. The self-envy inequality remains tautological, so this calculation does not demonstrate an envy violation.

3. A fully resolved class with arbitrary reserves and supplies

Assume henceforth that there is exactly one genuine buyer type, eligible for every seller type. Its mass is $\mu>0$, demand is the positive integer $d$, and value is $v>0$. Seller masses, supplies, and reserves are otherwise arbitrary as in the question.

Define the genuine demand per unit of replication by

$$ q=\mu d. $$

Let

$$ r_*= \min\left\{ r\in\{\rho_s:s\in S\}: \sum_{\rho_s\leq r}w_s\geq q \right\}, $$

with $r_*=\infty$ if this set is empty, and put

$$ p_*=\min(v,r_*). $$

I prove that, for all sufficiently large admissible $N$, the event and sharing tables are exactly independent of $N$.

3.1. No sale occurs before $p_*$

At a clock value $p<p_*$, define

$$ U(p)=\sum_{\rho_s\leq p}w_s. $$

By the definition of $p_*$,

$$ U(p)<q. $$

There are finitely many such clock values. Hence we can choose $N$ sufficiently large that, at every one of them,

$$ N(q-U(p)) \geq \max\left(d,\max_s\ell_s\right). $$

Proceed inductively, assuming no earlier sale.

After removing proxies whose reserves are at most $p$, the $NU(p)$ units without active proxies can all be covered by genuine buyers. All other units can be covered by their own proxies. Thus departing proxies need purchase nothing.

No genuine buyer copy is compulsory: removing one leaves genuine demand $Nq-d$, which still covers all $NU(p)$ units without active proxies.

No remaining proxy is compulsory either. Removing a proxy of supply $\ell_s$ leaves at most $NU(p)+\ell_s$ units to be covered by genuine buyers, within their demand $Nq$.

Therefore there is no sale at $p$. The induction proves the assertion.

4. Exact tables when $p_*=v$

Let

$$ C=\{s:\rho_s\leq v\}, \qquad H=\sum_{s\in C}w_s. $$

At price $v$, the departing set contains all genuine buyers and all proxies of reserve $v$.

Every unit of a type in $C$ must be assigned to this departing set: after its removal, no buyer eligible for that unit remains. Units outside $C$ can all be assigned to their own surviving proxies. Thus a minimizing covering matching assigns exactly $NH$ units to the departing set, precisely the units of types in $C$.

These units may be sold in any order by choosing seller priority. Indeed, in every residual covering matching minimizing departing assignments, every remaining unit of a type in $C$ is assigned to the departing set. Hence each can be selected next by the stipulated seller tie rule.

The union graph on these units and these $NH$ events is therefore complete.

Afterward, every surviving proxy buys all units of its original seller copy. Its events are incident only to units of that copy. Varying priority within the copy gives a complete graph between its $\ell_s$ units and its $\ell_s$ events.

All events have price $v$. The atom table is

| Atom | Event mass | Nonzero kernel entries |
|---|---:|---|
| $(v,C)$, if $H>0$ | $H$ | $x_s=1/H$ for $s\in C$ |
| $(v,\{s\})$, for $s\notin C$ | $w_s$ | $x_s=1/w_s$ |

Each complete component has equally many sellers and events, all at the same price. Every unit seller eats exactly one unit by time one.

The table is therefore exact for all sufficiently large admissible $N$. Every type earns $v$ per unit seller. Types in $C$ compare equally with one another; comparisons between distinct components have right-hand side zero. Thus the kernel is feasible and type-level envy-free, and

$$ R=vW=\int p\,d\eta. $$

This includes $C=\varnothing$, when only the singleton atoms occur.

5. Exact tables when $p_*=r_*<v$

Write $r=p_*$ and define

$$ L=\{s:\rho_s<r\}, \qquad T=\{s:\rho_s=r\}, \qquad C=L\cup T. $$

Put

$$ u=\sum_{s\in L}w_s, \qquad a=\sum_{s\in T}w_s, \qquad H=u+a, \qquad h=H-q. $$

The threshold definition gives

$$ u<q\leq H, \qquad a>0, \qquad h\geq0. $$

5.1. Departure events

Only proxies of types in $T$ depart at price $r$.

The $NH$ units of types in $C$ must be covered by genuine buyers and these departing proxies. Genuine buyers have total demand $Nq$, so every covering matching assigns at least $Nh$ units to departing proxies.

This lower bound is achievable: assign any $Nh$ units of types in $T$ to their own proxies, all other units of types in $C$ to genuine buyers, and all units outside $C$ to their own proxies. The selection is possible because

$$ h=H-q<a, $$

using $q>u$.

Thus exactly $Nh$ departure sales occur, all from types in $T$. Any prescribed ordering of any such selected units can be enforced by seller priority. Every departure event consequently has incidence set consisting of all unit sellers of types in $T$.

5.2. The remaining sales

After those departures, exactly $Nq$ units of types in $C$ remain. Their proxies have all departed, so genuine buyers must cover all of them. Their total demand is also $Nq$. Every genuine buyer copy is therefore saturated in every covering matching.

Every unit outside $C$ must be assigned to its own proxy: genuine demand is entirely needed for the remaining units of types in $C$. Those proxies are also saturated.

All active buyers are consequently compulsory until their demands are exhausted. The fixed buyer tie orders determine the sequence of genuine and proxy sales independently of seller priority.

For any specified genuine-sale rank and any unit of a type in $C$, choose the departure units to avoid that unit, then choose its position in the priority order of the remaining units of types in $C$. This is possible even when the specified unit has type in $T$, since the remaining mass of types in $T$ is $q-u>0$.

Thus each of the $Nq$ genuine-sale events is incident to all unit sellers of types in $C$.

Each proxy above reserve $r$ again generates a private complete component for its original seller copy. No event in such a component is incident to another seller type.

All events have price $r$. The normalized event table is therefore

$$ \eta(r,T)=h, \qquad \eta(r,C)=q, \qquad \eta(r,\{s\})=w_s \quad(s\notin C). $$

Zero-mass atoms are omitted, and coincident atoms are merged. In particular, when $u=0$, the first two atoms merge into $(r,C)$ of mass $H$.

5.3. Exact eating calculation

Suppose first that $u>0$ and $h>0$, so the atoms $(r,T)$ and $(r,C)$ are distinct.

Before either exhausts:

  • Sellers of types in $L$ eat only the $C$-events.
  • Sellers of types in $T$ divide their rate among the $NH$ tied events, sending fractions $q/H$ and $h/H$ to the $C$- and $T$-events, respectively.

Hence the normalized capacity-loss rates are

$$ u+\frac{aq}{H} \quad\text{for the $C$-atom}, \qquad \frac{ah}{H} \quad\text{for the $T$-atom}. $$

Define

$$ \theta = \frac{q}{u+aq/H} = \frac{qH}{uH+aq}. $$

Since

$$ uH+aq-qH=u(H-q)=uh\geq0, $$

we have $\theta\leq1$. The initial exhaustion time of the $T$-atom is $H/a\geq1$, so the $C$-atom exhausts first, at time $\theta$.

Sellers in $L$ then stop. Sellers in $T$ continue on the $T$-atom. Its residual capacity at time one is

$$ h-a\left(1-\frac{q\theta}{H}\right) = \frac{u^2h}{uH+aq} \geq0. $$

Thus this continuation is valid throughout the remaining interval.

The resulting sharing table is

$$ z_s(r,C)= \begin{cases} w_s\theta,&s\in L,\\ w_s q\theta/H,&s\in T, \end{cases} $$

$$ z_s(r,T) = w_s\left(1-\frac{q\theta}{H}\right) \quad(s\in T), $$

and

$$ z_s(r,\{s\})=w_s \quad(s\notin C). $$

All other entries vanish.

These formulas also cover the boundary cases after omitting or merging atoms:

  • If $h=0$, then $q=H$ and $\theta=1$.
  • If $u=0$, then $\theta=1$, $C=T$, and the merged atom has mass $H$ and sharing entry $z_s=w_s$.

The kernel is obtained by dividing each entry by $w_s\eta$.

5.4. Type-level envy-freeness

Feasibility follows directly from the eating construction. For envy, it suffices to compute per-unit revenues on the incident atoms.

If $s\in L$, its per-unit revenue is $r\theta$. Its only incident atom is the $C$-atom. Another type in $L$ receives the same amount there, while a type in $T$ receives

$$ r\frac{q\theta}{H}\leq r\theta. $$

A type outside $C$ receives nothing on that atom.

If $s\in T$, its total per-unit revenue is $r$. Its incident atoms are the $C$- and $T$-atoms. Another type in $T$ receives $r$ on these atoms, a type in $L$ receives $r\theta\leq r$, and a type outside $C$ receives zero.

Finally, a type outside $C$ is incident only to its own singleton atom, on which every other type receives zero.

These are all ordered pairs of seller types. Thus the kernel is type-level envy-free.

6. A stronger, sharp revenue bound in this class

In the case $r_*<v$, the sharing calculation gives

$$ R = r\left(W-u(1-\theta)\right). $$

$$ \alpha=\frac{u}{H}, \qquad \beta=\frac{q}{H}. $$

$$ \frac{u(1-\theta)}{H} = \frac{\alpha^2(1-\beta)} {\alpha+(1-\alpha)\beta}. $$

For $\alpha>0$, differentiation with respect to $\beta$ gives

$$ \frac{d}{d\beta} \left( \frac{\alpha^2(1-\beta)} {\alpha+(1-\alpha)\beta} \right) = -\frac{\alpha^2} {\bigl(\alpha+(1-\alpha)\beta\bigr)^2}. $$

Thus, also including $\alpha=0$,

$$ \frac{u(1-\theta)}{H} \leq \frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{2-\alpha}. $$

The following identity supplies the exact maximum:

$$ 3-2\sqrt2-\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{2-\alpha} = \frac{\bigl(\alpha-(2-\sqrt2)\bigr)^2}{2-\alpha} \geq0. $$

Since $H\leq W$,

$$ R \geq 2(\sqrt2-1)\,rW = 2(\sqrt2-1)\int p\,d\eta. $$

The case $p_*=v$ distributes all revenue, so the same bound holds throughout the one-buyer-type class.

The constant is sharp as a uniform bound. Take two unit-supply seller types with reserves zero and one, total mass $W=1$, and masses $\alpha$ and $1-\alpha$. $$ \alpha=2-\sqrt2, \qquad \beta=\alpha $$

from the permitted region. The threshold price is one, and the exact revenue fraction above approaches $2(\sqrt2-1)$.

As a concrete rational example, take seller masses one and two, reserves zero and one, unit supplies, and genuine demand mass two at value two. Then

$$ u=1,\quad a=2,\quad q=2,\quad H=3,\quad h=1, \qquad \theta=\frac67. $$

The two atoms have masses one and two, and

Thus even this tractable replicated class can leave event revenue undistributed.

For envy-freeness, whenever seller $i$ has an available incident event, every event currently eaten by another seller and incident to $i$ has price at most $M_i(t)$. Nonnegative prices and the other seller’s total rate being at most one justify the displayed rate inequality.

When seller $i$ has no available incident event, all its incident events are exhausted, so neither seller can obtain further revenue on those events. Integrating the almost-everywhere inequality gives the specified finite envy condition.

For revenue, the perfect matching assigns distinct sellers to distinct events. Each seller matched to an unexhausted event can still access that event and therefore earns at least its price per unit time. Thus

$$ F'(t)\geq \sum_{e\text{ unexhausted at }t}p_e. $$

Exhausted events have contributed their full prices, while contributions from unexhausted events are nonnegative. Hence

$$ F'(t)\geq T-F(t). $$

Together with $F(0)=0$, the integrating-factor calculation gives

$$ F(1)\geq (1-e^{-1})T. $$
