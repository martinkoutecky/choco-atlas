The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given stages \(T\) and tolerance \(r\), a finite rational set of arrival-location types \((a,z)\) with rational masses summing to one, choose facility locations \(y_s\) for \(s=1,\ldots,T+r-1\) and service masses \(x_{a,z,s}\) summing to each type's mass, with \(a<a'\) implying every positive service stage for \((a,z)\) is no later than every positive service stage for \((a',z')\), to minimize the total weighted distance sum of \(x_{a,z,s}|y_s-z|\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(T,r\ge 1\) be integers encoded in unary, and let \(m=T+r-1\) be the number of service stages. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) distinct types \((a,z)\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\times\mathbb Q\), each with positive mass \(w_{a,z}\) as a binary rational, with \(\sum_{a,z}w_{a,z}=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; arrival stages may have no mass. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Choose facility locations \(y_s\in\mathbb R\) and divisible service masses \(x_{a,z,s}\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) for \(s\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(\sum_s x_{a,z,s}=w_{a,z}\) and \(x_{a,z,s}=0\) unless \(a\le s\le a+r-1\).
Impose FCFS: for every pair of support types with \(a<a'\), if \(x_{a,z,s}>0\) and \(x_{a',z',s'}>0\), then \(s\le s'\). Equal arrival stages impose no order, and different arrival stages may share a service stage.
There is one uncapacitated facility per stage, no movement cost, and no restriction on its location. The total cost is \(C=\sum_{a,z}\sum_{s=1}^{m}x_{a,z,s}|y_s-z|\).

Given a binary rational threshold \(B\ge0\), is deciding whether a feasible solution with \(C\le B\) exists in \(\mathrm P\), or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,T,r\).

For indivisible unit-weight agents, exact minimum total distance under the same service windows and FCFS condition is computable in time polynomial in the number of agents, \(T\), and \(r\). [Wang et al. (2023)](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/25725/25497)
No result settling this exact divisible-mass formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored the omitted service windows: a type arriving at stage a may receive service only at stages a through a+r-1.
  • Specified positive integer T and r, integer arrival stages from 1 through T, and unrestricted rational spatial locations, following the discrete domains.
  • Defined m as the service horizon T+r-1 and chose unary encoding of T and r so that polynomial time in the explicit stage horizon has its ordinary input-length meaning.
  • Specified nonnegative real service masses and real facility locations; mass is divisible, and there are no capacity constraints or movement costs.
  • Made FCFS weak across different arrival stages, permitting simultaneous service, with no ordering constraint within an arrival stage.
  • Allowed empty arrival stages while retaining positive mass for every listed support type.
  • Converted minimum-cost optimization to its threshold decision problem using a nonnegative binary rational threshold B.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). An exact algorithm uses

\(O\!\left((r\tau^4+Tm^2\tau^2)L^2\right)\)

bit operations, where \(m=T+r-1\). In particular, binary rational masses need not be expanded into unit-weight agents.

The proof below gives a dynamic program directly for the stated formulation. The [cited algorithm of Wang et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/25725/25497) provides context; its unit-weight result is not needed as a black box.

1. Divisibility does not improve the optimum

Write \(\mathcal S\) for the support and \(Q=\{z:(a,z)\in\mathcal S\}\) for the set of distinct spatial coordinates.

Lemma 1. Every feasible divisible solution can be replaced, without increasing cost, by a feasible solution serving each support type entirely at one stage.

Proof. Fix a feasible solution \((x,y)\). For each type \((a,z)\), choose a stage \(s_{a,z}\) minimizing \(|y_s-z|\) among stages with \(x_{a,z,s}>0\). Such a stage exists because its positive mass must be served.

Assign its entire mass to \(s_{a,z}\). Its new cost satisfies

\(w_{a,z}|y_{s_{a,z}}-z|\le\sum_s x_{a,z,s}|y_s-z|\).

The chosen stage belongs to the type’s service window. Moreover, whenever \(a<a'\), both chosen stages had positive mass in the original solution, so the original FCFS condition gives \(s_{a,z}\le s_{a',z'}\). Thus feasibility is preserved. Summing the displayed inequality proves the claim. \(\square\)

Lemma 2. For every fixed assignment of whole support types to stages, optimal facility locations can be chosen from \(Q\).

Proof. At a nonempty stage, aggregate coincident coordinates and write its coordinates as \(z_1<\cdots<z_n\), with positive weights \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\). Let \(V=\sum_jv_j\), and choose the first index \(t\) satisfying \(\sum_{j\le t}v_j\ge V/2\).

For \(F(y)=\sum_jv_j|y-z_j|\), the slope immediately to the left of \(z_t\) is

\(2\sum_{j<t}v_j-V\le0\),

and the slope immediately to its right is

\(2\sum_{j\le t}v_j-V\ge0\).

The slopes are nondecreasing as \(y\) increases, so \(z_t\) minimizes \(F\). It belongs to \(Q\). An empty stage may also be placed anywhere in \(Q\). \(\square\)

There is at least one feasible whole-type assignment: serve each type at its arrival stage. There are finitely many whole-type assignments and facility vectors in \(Q^m\). Lemmas 1–2 therefore show that the original problem has an attained optimum equal to the minimum over this finite family.

Henceforth we optimize whole-type assignments with facility locations in \(Q\).

2. A clustering subproblem with fixed centers

Consider one arrival cohort, whose support coordinates are \(z_1<\cdots<z_n\), with weights \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\).

For \(1\le i\le j\le n\), define the optimal cost of serving that block with one free facility:

\(g(i,j)=\min_y\sum_{t=i}^jw_t|z_t-y|\).

For a prescribed facility position \(f\), define

\(g_f(i,j)=\sum_{t=i}^jw_t|z_t-f|\).

By Lemma 2, \(g(i,j)\) is attained at a weighted median of the block.

Let \(F=(f_1,\ldots,f_d)\), where \(d\le2\), be a list of fixed centers. Define \(H(k,F)\) as the minimum cost of serving the cohort using these fixed centers and at most \(k\) additional free centers. Centers may be unused.

Lemma 3. All values \(H(k,F)\), for \(0\le k\le r-1\), can be computed in \(O(rn^2)\) arithmetic operations once the block costs are available.

Proof. For any finite collection of centers, assign each coordinate to a nearest center, resolving ties toward the smaller center coordinate. Coincident centers can be represented by one label, because using every center is not required.

The resulting nonempty assignment blocks are consecutive in spatial order. Indeed, for two centers \(u<v\),

\(|z-u|\le|z-v|\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z\le(u+v)/2\).

Thus a nearest-center choice cannot move from a larger center to a smaller center as \(z\) increases.

We can consequently optimize over partitions into consecutive blocks. Let \(E(j,h,M)\) be the minimum cost of partitioning the first \(j\) coordinates into nonempty consecutive blocks, using exactly \(h\) free-center blocks and exactly the fixed-center labels in \(M\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\), each once.

Initialize \(E(0,0,\varnothing)=0\), and set all other states with \(j=0\) to infinity. For \(j>0\), enumerate the start \(i+1\) of the final block, where \(0\le i<j\). Its possible contributions are:

  • \(E(i,h-1,M)+g(i+1,j)\), when \(h\ge1\);
  • \(E(i,h,M\setminus\{\ell\})+g_{f_\ell}(i+1,j)\), for \(\ell\in M\).

Take the minimum of these candidates. Then

\(H(k,F)=\min_{0\le h\le k,\ M\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}}E(n,h,M)\).

To verify equality, first observe that every DP partition gives a valid assignment to at most \(k\) free centers and the prescribed fixed centers: place each free center at its block’s weighted median. Therefore \(H(k,F)\) is at most the DP value.

Conversely, an optimum for \(H(k,F)\) exists: for each assignment to center labels, every nonempty free center can be placed at a weighted median, leaving finitely many relevant possibilities. Assigning to nearest centers produces consecutive blocks. These blocks occur among the DP candidates, and replacing each free center by its block’s weighted median cannot increase cost. Therefore the DP value is at most \(H(k,F)\).

There are \(O(rn)\) states because \(d\le2\), and each state considers \(O(n)\) candidates. \(\square\)

3. What FCFS allows us to remember

List only the nonempty arrival stages:

\(a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_K\),

and let \(n_i\) be the support size of cohort \(i\). Thus

\(K\le T,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^K n_i=\tau\).

Their actual arrival stages remain \(a_i\); empty arrival stages are not compressed out of service windows.

For any feasible whole-type schedule, let \(b_i\) be the latest stage serving cohort \(i\), and set \(b_0=0\). FCFS gives

\(b_{i-1}\le s\le b_i\)

for every stage \(s\) serving cohort \(i\). Also \(a_i\le b_i\le a_i+r-1\).

This yields the central observation: when processing the next cohort, the only previously chosen facility that it can use is the one at the previous boundary stage \(b_{i-1}\). Earlier facilities cannot be used. Facilities strictly between consecutive boundaries serve only the current cohort.

The algorithm also permits a boundary stage to be unused by the current cohort. This only reserves that stage and restricts subsequent cohorts to stages at least as large. Such reservations remain feasible.

4. The outer dynamic program

A state \((b,p)\) records a reserved boundary stage \(b\) and its facility position \(p\in Q\). All processed cohorts have been served by stage \(b\), and subsequent cohorts will be assigned only to stages at least \(b\).

Start with the sentinel state \(D_0(0,\bot)=0\).

When processing cohort \(i\), write \(a=a_i\). From a previous state \((b,p)\), choose its new boundary

\(c\in\{a,\ldots,a+r-1\},\qquad c\ge b\),

and a boundary facility position \(q\in Q\).

There are two transition cases.

Keeping the same boundary

If \(c=b\), all of the current cohort must be served at that stage. Its facility remains \(p\), so \(q=p\), and the added cost is

\(\sum_{t=1}^{n_i}w_t|z_t-p|\).

Advancing the boundary

If \(c>b\), stage \(c\) receives the prescribed facility \(q\).

The available intermediate stages are precisely

\(\{s:\max\{a,b+1\}\le s<c\}\).

Their number is

\(k=c-\max\{a,b+1\}\),

which satisfies \(0\le k\le r-1\).

If \(b\ge a\), the previous boundary facility \(p\) is also inside the current cohort’s window. Otherwise it cannot be used. Consequently the added cost is

\(H_i(k,(p,q))\quad\text{if }b\ge a\),

and

\(H_i(k,(q))\quad\text{if }b<a\).

Here \(H_i\) is the clustering subproblem for cohort \(i\). Its free centers can be assigned injectively to the \(k\) intermediate stages. This is valid because equal arrival stages impose no service order.

For each transition, update \(D_i(c,q)\) with the previous value plus the indicated added cost, taking the minimum over all predecessors.

The output is

\(C_{\mathrm{DP}}=\min_{c,q}D_K(c,q)\).

5. Correctness

Proposition. \(C_{\mathrm{DP}}\) equals the optimum of the original divisible problem.

Proof: every DP path is feasible. Proceed inductively. Before a transition from \((b,p)\), all previously processed mass is served at stages at most \(b\).

If \(c=b\), the current cohort is served at \(b\), within its window.

If \(c>b\), the clustering solution uses only the eligible old boundary stage, eligible intermediate stages, and stage \(c\). Each is within the current cohort’s window. Previously used facilities are preserved, and the intermediate stages and stage \(c\) contain no previously served mass.

In both cases, every current service stage is at least \(b\). Every earlier service stage is at most \(b\), so FCFS holds between the current cohort and every earlier cohort. Stage sharing causes no difficulty because equality is allowed.

The block DP supplies actual whole-type assignments realizing each added cost. Thus every finite outer DP path gives a feasible solution of that cost, and

\(C_{\mathrm{DP}}\ge C^\star\),

where \(C^\star\) is the original optimum.

Proof: an optimal schedule is represented. By Section 1, choose an optimal whole-type schedule with facility positions in \(Q\). For each cohort, use its actual latest service stage as \(b_i\), and its facility position there as the corresponding boundary position.

FCFS gives \(b_{i-1}\le b_i\) and places every service stage of cohort \(i\) between these boundaries.

If the boundaries coincide, the same-boundary transition reproduces its cost.

If they differ, all facilities used by this cohort belong to exactly the set allowed by the advancing-boundary transition: the previous boundary if eligible, the new boundary, and eligible intermediate stages. Hence its assignment is a feasible candidate for that transition’s clustering subproblem. The transition cost is at most the cohort’s cost in the optimal schedule.

Following these boundaries through the DP gives

\(C_{\mathrm{DP}}\le C^\star\).

Together, the two inequalities prove equality. \(\square\)

6. Running time and exact arithmetic

Put \(q=|Q|\le\tau\).

For each cohort, all free block costs \(g(i,j)\) can be computed in \(O(n_i^3)\) arithmetic operations: scan each block to find a weighted median and evaluate its cost. Fixed-center block costs can be obtained from prefix sums for each \(f\in Q\).

There are \(O(q^2)\) relevant fixed-center lists of length one or two. Lemma 3 computes their tables for all \(0\le k\le r-1\) in

\(O(rq^2n_i^2)\)

arithmetic operations per cohort.

The outer DP has \(O(mq)\) states per cohort and at most \(O(mq)\) transitions per state. Therefore the total number of arithmetic operations is

\(O\!\left(\tau^3+rq^2\sum_i n_i^2+Km^2q^2\right)\).

Using \(\sum_i n_i^2\le\tau^2\), \(q\le\tau\), and \(K\le T\), this is at most

\(O(r\tau^4+Tm^2\tau^2)\).

It remains to bound the arithmetic bit lengths. Let \(W\) be the product of the input mass denominators, and \(Z\) the product of the input coordinate denominators. Write

\(w_{a,z}=u_{a,z}/W,\qquad z=v_z/Z\),

with integer \(u_{a,z}>0\) and integer \(v_z\). Both products and all these integers have \(O(L)\) bits. Moreover, \(\sum_{a,z}u_{a,z}=W\).

Measure cost in units of \(1/(WZ)\). Every contribution becomes the integer

\(u_{a,z}|v_y-v_z|\).

Let \(R=\max_{z\in Q}v_z-\min_{z\in Q}v_z\). Since all chosen centers lie in \(Q\), every finite cost stored by either DP is at most \(WR\), and therefore has \(O(L)\) bits. Intermediate prefix sums also have \(O(L)\) bits.

Using elementary integer arithmetic, each required operation costs at most \(O(L^2)\) bit operations. Finally, compare the optimum \(A/(WZ)\) with the binary rational threshold by cross multiplication.

The resulting bound is

\(O\!\left((r\tau^4+Tm^2\tau^2)L^2\right) =O\!\left((r\tau^4+T(T+r-1)^2\tau^2)L^2\right)\).

Because \(T\) and \(r\) are unary encoded, this is polynomial in the total input length.
