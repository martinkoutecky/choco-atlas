The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a fixed number of DAOs and true member values supported on at most \(\tau\) values in \(\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), with rational masses \(\mu_j(v)\) satisfying \(\sum_{j,v}\mu_j(v)=1\), determine the best worst-case welfare approximation factor \(\alpha^*(\tau)\) among deterministic two-level mechanisms. Each mechanism awards one indivisible item to at most one DAO, assigns each value class access \(x_{jv}\in\{0,1\}\) and per-member payment \(p_{jv}\), and is truthful, budget-balanced, individually rational, and equal-treatment on every finite refinement of the masses, where one member's report changes mass by \(1/N\). Welfare is \(\sum_{j,v}\mu_j(v)v x_{jv}\), compared with the optimum \(\max_j\sum_v\mu_j(v)v\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix \(k\ge 2\) nonempty DAOs and one indivisible item, so \(m=1\). Members belong to exactly one DAO, have values and reports in \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), and obtain utility \(vx-p\).
A society is given by its support: at most \(\tau\) distinct value types \(v\), each with positive binary-rational total mass and binary-rational DAO masses \(\mu_j(v)\), where \(\sum_{j,v}\mu_j(v)=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the supported-value count, not the number of possible values; DAO membership is recorded by the mass vector of each type. For computational statements on rational-valued inputs, let \(L\) be their total bit length and state running times in \(m,\tau,L,k\).
A deterministic two-level mechanism has a local aggregation rule for each DAO producing one nonnegative scalar bid. The upper rule sees only these bids, awards the item to at most one DAO, and specifies nonnegative DAO charges \(P_j\). Each lower rule sees only its DAO's reported masses, allocation, and charge, and assigns access \(x_{jv}\in\{0,1\}\) and nonnegative per-member payments \(p_{jv}\); access requires that DAO to win. Any tie-breaking is part of the deterministic mechanism.
Mechanism rules are defined on all finite-support reported societies, including reports outside the support bound. Outputs depend on reported masses, not on the choice of finite refinement.
For every integer \(N\) with \(N\mu_j(v)\in\mathbb Z\), interpret the society as \(N\mu_j(v)\) members of each class. Aggregate bids and DAO charges are in mass units: their finite-population amounts are multiplied by \(N\), while per-member payments remain \(p_{jv}\). Singleton identity aggregation requires \(a_j(N^{-1}\delta_b)=b/N\), where \(\delta_b\) is unit mass at report \(b\).
On every such refinement and every report profile, require DSIC against any unilateral report change, which moves mass \(1/N\) within the same DAO; individual rationality; exact budget balance \(\sum_v\mu_j(v)p_{jv}=P_j\); and equal treatment, meaning identical reports within a DAO receive identical access and payments.
At truthful reports define \(\mathrm{SW}(M,\mu)=\sum_{j,v}\mu_j(v)v x_{jv}\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)=\max_j\sum_v\mu_j(v)v\). Let \(\alpha^*(\tau;k)=\inf_M\sup_{\mu:\,|\operatorname{supp}(\mu)|\le\tau,\,\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)>0}\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)/\mathrm{SW}(M,\mu)\), with ratio \(+\infty\) when welfare is zero; the infimum ranges over the mechanisms just defined, without computational restrictions.

What is the optimal worst-case welfare approximation factor \(\alpha^*(\tau;k)\) for each fixed \(k\ge2\)?

For finite societies, the optimal worst-case factor under truthfulness, budget balance, individual rationality, and equal treatment is \(H_\ell=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}1/i\), where \(\ell\) is the largest DAO size: matching achievability and impossibility results are known ([source](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol282-aft2023/LIPIcs.AFT.2023.21/LIPIcs.AFT.2023.21.pdf)).

Results

For every fixed $k\ge2$, the following bounds hold:
$$ \alpha^*(1;k)=1,\qquad \alpha^*(2;k)=2, $$
and
$$ \tau-1\le \alpha^*(\tau;k)\le\tau \qquad(\tau\ge3). $$

The lower bound $\tau-1$ improves the bound in the notes when $\tau\ge4$. Its proof handles repeated reports and the refinement requirements directly. The optimal factor remains unresolved for $\tau\ge3$.

Throughout, a mechanism’s worst-case ratio means the supremum appearing in the question. All lower-bound witnesses use rational values and rational masses.

1. Consequences of individual truthfulness

Fix a finite refinement, one member, and all other reports. Let $x(b)$ and $p(b)$ be that member’s access and payment when reporting $b$.

Individual rationality and nonnegative payments imply
$$ x(b)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p(b)=0. $$

DSIC has the following consequences.

  • Access is nondecreasing in the report. Indeed, adding the DSIC inequalities for true values $b<b'$ gives

$$ (b'-b)\bigl(x(b')-x(b)\bigr)\ge0. $$

  • All reports that receive access pay the same amount. If both $b$ and $b'$ receive access, their two DSIC inequalities give

$$ p(b)\le p(b'),\qquad p(b')\le p(b). $$

  • If access is available at some report and its payment is $t$, every report greater than $t$ receives access, and every report less than $t$ is excluded. For an excluded report $b$, DSIC against a served report gives $b\le t$. For a served report, IR gives $b\ge t$. Either allocation is possible at $b=t$.

These statements apply separately on every permitted refinement.

2. Upper bound $\alpha^*(\tau;k)\le\tau$

Fix a rational constant $\rho>1$. We construct an admissible mechanism with factor at most $\rho\tau$.

2.1 Aggregation and upper allocation

In DAO $j$, write its distinct reports in decreasing order:
$$ b_{j1}>\cdots>b_{jr_j}, $$
with positive masses $w_{j1},\ldots,w_{jr_j}$. Define
$$ Q_{ji}=\sum_{h=1}^{i}w_{jh}, \qquad A_j=\max_{1\le i\le r_j}b_{ji}Q_{ji}. $$

This depends only on reported masses. For a singleton of mass $1/N$ reporting $b$,
$$ A_j=\frac bN, $$
so singleton identity aggregation holds.

For $A>0$, let $z(A)$ be the unique integer satisfying
$$ \rho^{z(A)}\le A<\rho^{z(A)+1}. $$
Rank positive bids lexicographically by $(z(A_j),j)$, with larger indices having higher priority. Positive bids outrank zero bids.

Award the item to the highest-ranked DAO. If all bids are zero, award it to a fixed DAO and charge zero.

Suppose DAO $j$ wins and the highest-ranked positive rival has rank $(z,h)$. Charge the winner
$$ C_j= \begin{cases} \rho^z,&j>h,\\ \rho^{z+1},&j<h. \end{cases} $$
All losers pay zero. If every rival bid is zero, the winner pays zero.

For fixed rivals with a positive bid, DAO $j$ wins exactly when
$$ A_j\ge C_j. $$
Thus its winning threshold is attained, and its winning charge is independent of its own bid.

2.2 Lower allocation and payments

A losing DAO excludes everyone and charges zero.

A winner charged zero serves everyone for free. A winner charged $C>0$ chooses the largest index $i$ such that
$$ b_{ji}Q_{ji}\ge C. $$
It serves all reports at least $b_{ji}$ and charges every served member
$$ \frac{C}{Q_{ji}}. $$

The index exists because the upper rule ensures $C\le A_j$.

Exact budget balance follows from
$$ Q_{ji}\frac{C}{Q_{ji}}=C. $$
Every served report is at least $b_{ji}\ge C/Q_{ji}$, proving IR. Payments are nonnegative, identical reports receive identical outcomes, and access is confined to the winner.

The lower maps can be extended arbitrarily to allocation-charge signals that never occur. For example, on any remaining signal, charge every member $C/s_j$, where $s_j$ is its DAO’s mass, and give access according to the allocation signal. The required properties above concern actual mechanism outcomes.

2.3 DSIC on every refinement

Fix any permitted refinement $N$, a member of DAO $j$, and all other reports.

Suppose some rival aggregate bid is positive. The fixed finite-population acquisition cost is
$$ q=NC_j>0. $$
Let $n$ be this DAO’s member count, and define
$$ s^*=\max\left\{ s\in\{1,\ldots,n\}: \text{at least }s-1\text{ other members report at least }q/s \right\}. $$
The set is nonempty because it contains $s=1$. Put $t=q/s^*$.

If the member reports $b\ge t$, it and $s^*-1$ qualifying others form a feasible coalition of size $s^*$. Hence the DAO wins.

No feasible coalition can have size greater than $s^*$. A larger coalition containing this member supplies the required qualifying partners. A larger coalition excluding this member also supplies at least the required number of partners. Either possibility contradicts the definition of $s^*$.

Feasible coalitions are closed under union: enlarging their size lowers the required equal share. Consequently, their unique largest coalition contains this member and has size $s^*$. Its payment is $t$.

Conversely, if this member is served in a feasible coalition of size $s$, then $s$ is a candidate defining $s^*$, and
$$ b\ge\frac qs\ge\frac q{s^*}=t. $$
Thus access occurs exactly at reports $b\ge t$, with payment $t$.

The mass-based lower rule implements this largest coalition. A largest feasible coalition cannot omit a member whose report is at least its equal share, since adding that member preserves feasibility. It is therefore a complete prefix of report classes, precisely the prefix selected above.

This threshold allocation is DSIC.

If every rival bid is zero, any positive report makes this DAO win, and access is free. A positive-valued member receives its maximum possible utility truthfully; a zero-valued member obtains utility zero under every report. This proves DSIC in the remaining case.

The argument applies to every permitted $N$, while the rules themselves depend only on masses.

2.4 Welfare and computation

At truthful reports, let
$$ W_j=\sum_i w_{ji}b_{ji}. $$
Each summand satisfies
$$ w_{ji}b_{ji}\le Q_{ji}b_{ji}\le A_j. $$
Hence, on a society with at most $\tau$ supported values,
$$ W_j\le r_jA_j\le\tau A_j. $$

The winning DAO’s served welfare is at least $A_j$. A prefix attaining $A_j$ is feasible at its actual charge, and the lower rule selects an equally large or larger prefix. The zero-charge case also satisfies this inequality.

The winner has maximal grid level, so
$$ A_j\ge\frac1\rho\max_h A_h. $$
Consequently,
$$ \mathrm{SW}(M_\rho,\mu) \ge\frac{\max_hA_h}{\rho} \ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)}{\rho\tau}. $$

Taking the infimum over rational $\rho>1$ gives
$$ \alpha^*(\tau;k)\le\tau. $$
This does not assert attainment by a single mechanism.

For fixed rational $\rho>1$, the construction is computable on rational inputs in the conservative bound
$$ O_\rho(k\tau L^4) $$
bit operations, with $m=1$. Rational cumulative masses, bids, relevant grid powers, and charges have $O_\rho(L)$ bits; grid exponents have magnitude $O_\rho(L)$. No refinement is constructed. For reported profiles outside the support bound, $\tau$ in this running-time statement is replaced by the actual supported-value count.

3. Calibrating a positive acquisition cost

Fix an admissible mechanism $M$ with finite worst-case ratio $F$ on a support bound at least two. Mechanisms with infinite ratio already satisfy every lower bound proved below.

Fix $q>0$. Consider societies having one member in each DAO, each of mass $1/k$. Reports
$$ kb_1,\ldots,kb_k $$
produce upper bids $b_1,\ldots,b_k$ by singleton identity.

Fix every rival bid at $q$ and vary DAO $j$’s bid $b$. Every society in this family has at most two supported values.

Finite approximation forces positive welfare. Therefore the upper winner must serve its singleton; it cannot award the item to an unserved singleton. In particular, DAO $j$ cannot win at $b=0$.

Its singleton payment is $kP_j(b)$ by budget balance. Dividing its DSIC inequalities by $k$ shows that the upper allocation and charge in this family satisfy scalar DSIC with value $b$. Section 1 therefore supplies a threshold $t_j$: winning bids pay $t_j$, bids above it win, and bids below it lose.

The threshold is finite. Otherwise, letting $b$ grow would leave welfare at most $q$ while optimal welfare grows without bound.

At the all-$q$ bid vector, choose an unallocated DAO $j$, which exists because $k\ge2$. Then
$$ t_j\ge q. $$

Because the upper rule sees only bids, the following conclusion holds regardless of DAO $j$’s internal composition:

Against rival bids all equal to $q$, whenever DAO $j$ wins, its mass-unit charge is $t_j$.

In a refinement of size $N$, its finite-population charge is therefore the fixed positive number
$$ C=Nt_j. $$
Within this DAO, unserved members pay zero, and any nonempty served set has total payment exactly $C$.

4. Universal lower bound of two

Choose an integer $n\ge2$, let
$$ N=n+k-1,\qquad q=\frac1N, $$
and calibrate a DAO $j$ as in Section 3. Its finite-population acquisition cost satisfies $C=Nt_j\ge1$.

Choose rational $a$ with $0<a<1/n$. Give DAO $j$ one member of value $1$ and $n-1$ members of value $a$. Every rival DAO has one member of value $1$. All members have mass $1/N$, and the global support is $\{a,1\}$.

Suppose a value-$a$ member is served, paying $p\le a$ by IR. Raise its report to $1$. Section 1 shows that it remains served and pays $p$, because $1>p$.

Equal treatment now makes both report-$1$ members served at payment $p\le a$. Every remaining member reports $a$ and pays at most $a$. Total finite-population payments are therefore at most
$$ na<1. $$
But this DAO is still serving a member, hence wins and must collect $C\ge1$. This contradicts budget balance.

Thus no value-$a$ member is served. Whichever DAO wins,
$$ \mathrm{SW}(M,\mu)\le\frac1N, $$
whereas
$$ \mathrm{OPT}(\mu)=\frac{1+(n-1)a}{N}. $$
The ratio is at least $1+(n-1)a$. Letting rational $a$ approach $1/n$ from below and then letting $n$ grow proves
$$ \alpha^*(\tau;k)\ge2 \qquad(\tau\ge2). $$

5. A fixed-cost lemma with one boundary report

The cost-sharing characterization in [Theorem 3.4 of *Is Shapley Cost Sharing Optimal?*](https://theory.stanford.edu/~tim/papers/IsShapley.pdf) assumes consumer sovereignty and a boundary convention. We do not invoke that characterization. The following lemma explicitly handles one report that may lie on a payment boundary.

Lemma

Consider $n$ members with deterministic DSIC, IR, nonnegative payments, and equal treatment. Assume every nonempty served set has total payment $C$, where $C\ge1$.

Fix baseline reports consisting of:

  • at most one report equal to $1$;
  • reports $d$ satisfying $0\le d<1$ and

$$ d\ne\frac Cs \qquad(s=1,\ldots,n). $$

Choose $B>C$. Assume that whenever a nonempty subset of baseline reports is replaced by $B$, all report-$B$ members are served.

For such a profile, let $S$ be its report-$B$ members, and let $R$ be the largest coalition satisfying
$$ b_i\ge\frac C{|R|} \qquad(i\in R). $$
Write $h=|R|$.

Then:

  • if $h>C$, the mechanism serves exactly $R$, charging each served member $C/h$;
  • if $h\le C$, every report strictly below $1$ is excluded, and every report-$B$ member pays at least $1$.

Proof

A coalition $T$ is *feasible* if every member reports at least $C/|T|$. Feasible coalitions are closed under union, so their largest coalition $R$ exists uniquely. It contains every report-$B$ member: adding such a member preserves feasibility.

Induct downward on $|S|$. When all reports equal $B$, everyone is served by hypothesis and pays $C/n$ by equal treatment and budget balance. Both conclusions follow.

Now consider a profile with fewer report-$B$ members. For any member $i\notin S$, replace its report by $B$. This profile has one more report-$B$ member, so the induction hypothesis applies. Let its largest feasible coalition be $R_i$, with size $h_i$.

The member is served at that modified profile. Its payment there is therefore its threshold for the original fixed reports of the others, by Section 1.

Case $h>C$

If $i\in R$, then $R_i=R$. Indeed, $R$ remains feasible after increasing $i$’s report. If $R_i$ were larger, the original report
$$ b_i\ge C/h\ge C/h_i $$
would also make $R_i$ feasible in the original profile, contradicting maximality of $R$.

Thus $h_i=h>C$, and induction gives threshold $C/h$. The original report is strictly above this threshold: a report below $1$ avoids every $C/s$ by hypothesis, while the possible report $1$ exceeds $C/h$. Hence $i$ is served and pays $C/h$.

If $i\notin R$, then $R\cup\{i\}$ is feasible after replacing its report by $B$. Thus
$$ h_i\ge h+1>C. $$
Induction gives threshold $C/h_i$. Necessarily
$$ b_i<C/h_i; $$
otherwise $R_i$ would be feasible originally. Thus $i$ is excluded.

All report-$B$ members are served by hypothesis and pay a common amount $p$. Budget balance gives
$$ |S|p+(h-|S|)\frac Ch=C, $$
so $p=C/h$. This proves the first conclusion.

Case $h\le C$

Consider a member with original report $d<1$.

If $h_i\le C$, induction gives its threshold at least $1$, so it is excluded. If $h_i>C$, induction gives threshold $C/h_i$. Its original report must be strictly below that threshold; otherwise $R_i$ would be feasible originally, contradicting $h\le C$. It is again excluded.

Only the report-$B$ members and the possible report-$1$ member remain.

Let $s=|S|$. If there is no served report-$1$ member, budget balance gives report-$B$ payment
$$ p=\frac Cs\ge1, $$
because $s\le h\le C$.

Suppose the report-$1$ member $u$ is served. Replacing its report by $B$ cannot yield $h_u>C$: in that case $C/h_u<1$, so the same coalition would be feasible before replacement, contradicting $h\le C$.

Thus $h_u\le C$, and induction gives $u$’s threshold at least $1$. IR and service at report $1$ force its payment to equal $1$.

Furthermore, $R_u$ contains the original $s$ report-$B$ members and $u$, so
$$ s+1\le h_u\le C. $$
Budget balance now gives
$$ p=\frac{C-1}{s}\ge1. $$
This completes the induction and the proof. $\square$

6. Stronger lower bound $\alpha^*(\tau;k)\ge\tau-1$

Fix $\tau\ge3$ and put
$$ r=\tau-1. $$
Let $M$ have finite worst-case ratio $F$ on societies with at most $\tau$ supported values.

Choose an integer $R\ge2$ and define
$$ T_h=R^{h-1}\qquad(h=1,\ldots,r), \qquad n=T_r, \qquad N=n+k-1. $$

Set $q=1/N$. Section 3 supplies a DAO $j$ whose acquisition cost against singleton rivals reporting $1$ is
$$ C=Nt_j\ge1. $$

6.1 Baseline society

DAO $j$ has:

  • one member of value $1$;
  • for each $h=2,\ldots,r$, exactly $T_h-T_{h-1}$ members of value $d_h$.

Every rival DAO has one member of value $1$. All masses are $1/N$.

Choose the rational values strictly decreasing and satisfying
$$ 0<d_h<\frac1{T_h+1}, \qquad d_h\ne\frac Cs \quad(s=1,\ldots,n). $$
They can be chosen arbitrarily close to their displayed upper bounds: only finitely many values are forbidden, and the upper bounds are strictly decreasing.

The baseline society has exactly $r$ supported values.

6.2 Verifying the lemma’s service hypothesis

Choose a rational sentinel value
$$ B>\max\{C,1,Fn\}. $$

Replace any nonempty subset of DAO $j$’s baseline reports by $B$. Such a society has at most
$$ r+1=\tau $$
supported values. The report $1$ remains globally present in the rival DAOs, so there are at most the original $r$ values plus $B$.

If no report-$B$ member received access, welfare would be at most $n/N$, because every other report is at most $1$. But optimal welfare is at least $B/N$, giving ratio at least
$$ \frac Bn>F. $$
This contradicts the definition of $F$.

Thus some report-$B$ member is served, and equal treatment serves them all. Whenever DAO $j$ serves anyone, Section 3 and budget balance require total finite-population payment $C$. All hypotheses of the lemma are therefore satisfied.

6.3 Excluding every baseline value below $1$

Fix a baseline member reporting $d_g$, and replace its report by $B$.

For each $h\ge2$, the number of reports at least $d_h$ after replacement is at most $T_h+1$. Consequently,
$$ d_h\,\#\{i:b_i\ge d_h\} \le d_h(T_h+1)<1\le C. $$

No feasible coalition can contain a report below $1$. To see this, let $d_h$ be its smallest report. Its size is at most the number of reports at least $d_h$, while feasibility would require
$$ C\le |T|d_h<1, $$
a contradiction.

Hence the largest feasible coalition consists only of the sentinel member and, possibly, the original report-$1$ member. Its size is
$$ h= \begin{cases} 2,&C\le2,\\ 1,&C>2. \end{cases} $$

If $h>C$, then $1\le C<2$, and the lemma gives the sentinel member payment
$$ \frac C2\ge\frac12. $$
If $h\le C$, the lemma gives payment at least $1$.

This payment is the chosen member’s threshold with its other reports fixed. But
$$ d_g<\frac1{T_g+1}\le\frac13<\frac12. $$
The member is therefore excluded at its baseline report.

This applies to every baseline member of value below $1$.

6.4 Welfare ratio

Only DAO $j$’s single value-$1$ member can be served there. Each rival also consists of one value-$1$ member. Therefore
$$ \mathrm{SW}(M,\mu)\le\frac1N. $$

DAO $j$’s total value is
$$ \frac1N\left( 1+\sum_{h=2}^{r}(T_h-T_{h-1})d_h \right), $$
which exceeds every rival’s total value. The ratio is consequently at least
$$ 1+\sum_{h=2}^{r}(T_h-T_{h-1})d_h. $$

For fixed $R$, take permitted rational $d_h$ arbitrarily close to $1/(T_h+1)$ from below. This gives a supremum at least
$$ 1+\sum_{h=2}^{r} \frac{T_h-T_{h-1}}{T_h+1}. $$
For each fixed $h\ge2$,
$$ \frac{T_h-T_{h-1}}{T_h+1} = \frac{R^{h-1}-R^{h-2}}{R^{h-1}+1} \longrightarrow1 \qquad(R\longrightarrow\infty). $$
Thus $F\ge r=\tau-1$. Since $M$ was arbitrary,
$$ \alpha^*(\tau;k)\ge\tau-1 \qquad(\tau\ge3). $$

7. Exact cases and the remaining gap

Any feasible outcome has welfare at most $\mathrm{OPT}$, giving the universal lower bound $1$. Section 2 supplies factors arbitrarily close to $1$ for one supported value. Hence
$$ \alpha^*(1;k)=1. $$

For two supported values, Sections 2 and 4 give
$$ \alpha^*(2;k)=2. $$

For all remaining support counts, the proved bounds are
$$ \tau-1\le\alpha^*(\tau;k)\le\tau \qquad(\tau\ge3). $$

The stronger lower-bound proof uses $r$ baseline values and one additional sentinel value in intermediate profiles. Taking $r=\tau$ would require applying finite approximation to profiles with $\tau+1$ supported values, which is not justified. Thus this argument does not establish a lower bound of $\tau$.
