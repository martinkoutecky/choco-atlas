The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite item set \(\Omega\), a finite set of valuation types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational-valued ultra valuations \(v_t:2^\Omega\to\mathbb{Q}\), and per-capita supplies \(q_a\), find a mass assignment \(\lambda_{t,S}\ge 0\) maximizing \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\subseteq\Omega}\lambda_{t,S}v_t(S)\), subject to \(\sum_{S\subseteq\Omega}\lambda_{t,S}=\mu_t\) for each type and \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\ni a}\lambda_{t,S}\le q_a\) for each item \(a\). Each unit of mass receives an integral bundle.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=|\Omega|\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible valuations.
Each type has a rational-valued valuation \(v_t:2^\Omega\to\mathbb Q\), encoded by a Boolean circuit returning a signed binary numerator and positive binary denominator on a bundle's incidence vector. Valuations need not be monotone, nonnegative, or normalized.
The valuations are promised ultra: whenever \(A,B\subseteq\Omega\), \(|A|\le |B|\), and \(x\in A\setminus B\), some \(y\in B\setminus A\) satisfies \(v_t(A)+v_t(B)\le v_t((A\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\})+v_t((B\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\})\).
Supplies \(q_a\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) are per capita. An assignment splits each type's mass among integral bundles, including the empty bundle: \(\lambda_{t,S}\ge0\), \(\sum_{S\subseteq\Omega}\lambda_{t,S}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni a}\lambda_{t,S}\le q_a\). Unused supply is allowed.
Maximize \(\sum_t\sum_{S\subseteq\Omega}\lambda_{t,S}v_t(S)\), representing an assignment by its nonzero rational entries. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including circuit descriptions, masses, supplies, and any rational welfare threshold.

Is exact welfare maximization for this mass-assignment problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is its associated welfare-threshold decision problem NP-hard?

For a single ultra valuation, adding any item of maximum marginal value to a cardinality-optimal bundle preserves cardinality optimality; an optimal bundle can consequently be found with \(O(m^2)\) value queries. [Lehmann](https://arxiv.org/html/1712.04236)
For a related unit-capacity fractional allocation program, polynomial demand-query communication computes discretized welfare values. [Nisan–Segal](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/www/cs15-892F13/NisanSegal.pdf)
No result addressing precisely the mass-input problem specified here was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The valuation representation was unspecified; use exact Boolean circuits with binary rational outputs and promise the ultra property.
  • The supplies' domain was unspecified; allow arbitrary nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies.
  • Following the valuation definition, allow negative values, nonmonotonicity, and arbitrary empty-bundle values.
  • Represent the requested assignment by its nonzero rational entries, with omitted entries zero.
  • Define the associated decision problem using a binary-rational welfare threshold and include its encoding in L.

Result

Exact welfare maximization is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The algorithm returns an optimal assignment with at most \(m+\tau\) nonzero rational entries. Consequently, the welfare-threshold decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\).

The proof combines an exact demand algorithm for ultra valuations with rational linear programming through a separation oracle. We explicitly address recovery of the assignment and its encoding length.

1. Ultra valuations admit exact polynomial-time demand queries

For a rational price vector \(p\), write \(p(S)=\sum_{a\in S}p_a\) and define \(u(S)=v(S)-p(S)\).

Lemma 1. If \(v\) is ultra, then \(u\) is ultra.

Proof. An exchange preserves the combined incidence vector of the two bundles. Thus, for \(A'=A-x+y\) and \(B'=B-y+x\),

\(p(A)+p(B)=p(A')+p(B').\)

Subtracting this equality from the exchange inequality for \(v\) proves the same inequality for \(u\). \(\square\)

For completeness, the greedy demand algorithm and its correctness follow directly from exchange. This is also the algorithm described in [Lehmann, Section 15](https://arxiv.org/html/1712.04236).

Lemma 2. Let \(u\) be ultra, and suppose \(A\) maximizes \(u\) among bundles of cardinality \(k<m\). If \(x\notin A\) maximizes \(u(A+x)\), then \(A+x\) maximizes \(u\) among bundles of cardinality \(k+1\).

Proof. Among all optimal bundles \(B\) of cardinality \(k+1\), choose one maximizing \(|A\cap B|\). Suppose \(A\not\subseteq B\), and choose \(z\in A\setminus B\). Exchange gives some \(y\in B\setminus A\) such that

\(u(A)+u(B)\le u(A-z+y)+u(B-y+z).\)

Cardinality optimality gives

\(u(A-z+y)\le u(A),\qquad u(B-y+z)\le u(B).\)

Therefore both inequalities are equalities. In particular, \(B-y+z\) is another optimal bundle of cardinality \(k+1\), and its intersection with \(A\) has one more element. This contradicts the choice of \(B\).

Hence \(B=A+y\) for some \(y\notin A\). By the choice of \(x\),

\(u(A+x)\ge u(A+y)=u(B).\)

Thus \(A+x\) is optimal at cardinality \(k+1\). \(\square\)

Starting from \(A_0=\emptyset\), successively choose

\(A_{k+1}=A_k+x_k,\qquad x_k\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x\notin A_k}u(A_k+x).\)

Lemma 2 proves that \(A_k\) is optimal at cardinality \(k\). Returning the highest-valued bundle among \(A_0,\ldots,A_m\) therefore computes

\(\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{S\subseteq\Omega}\bigl(v(S)-p(S)\bigr).\)

This takes \(O(m^2)\) circuit evaluations. All cardinalities must be examined, since complementarities can make later additions profitable after earlier negative marginal values.

The circuit output widths are bounded by the circuit encoding length. Thus every returned numerator and denominator has polynomially many bits. At rational prices of polynomial encoding length, all arithmetic and comparisons in this algorithm take polynomial time. We have an exact polynomial-time demand oracle, including for unnormalized or negative valuations.

2. The allocation LP and its dual

First remove every item with zero supply. Indeed, if \(q_a=0\), nonnegativity forces every assignment entry for a bundle containing \(a\) to be zero. Restricting each valuation to the remaining items preserves exchange, because the original exchange property applies to bundles within that restricted ground set.

Let the remaining ground set be \(E\), with \(n=|E|\le m\). If \(n=0\), assigning every type to the empty bundle solves the problem directly. Henceforth every remaining supply is positive.

The specified optimization problem is exactly the finite linear program

\(\displaystyle \max_{\lambda\ge0}\ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{S\subseteq E}v_t(S)\lambda_{t,S},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\subseteq E}\lambda_{t,S}=\mu_t\quad(t=1,\ldots,\tau),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq E\\a\in S}}\lambda_{t,S}\le q_a\quad(a\in E).\)

Call this program \(P\). It is feasible: assign each type entirely to \(\emptyset\). Its feasible region is bounded, because \(0\le\lambda_{t,S}\le\mu_t\). Therefore its optimum exists.

Its dual is

\(\displaystyle \min_{\alpha,p}\ \sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t+\sum_{a\in E}q_ap_a,\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t+\sum_{a\in S}p_a\ge v_t(S)\quad(t=1,\ldots,\tau,\ S\subseteq E),\)

\(p_a\ge0\quad(a\in E),\)

where each \(\alpha_t\) is unrestricted in sign. Call this program \(D\).

Finite-dimensional LP strong duality applies: \(P\) is feasible and bounded, and \(D\) is feasible by taking zero prices and sufficiently large \(\alpha_t\). Both programs attain the same optimal value.

The dual has only \(d=\tau+n\) variables.

3. Exact separation for the dual

Given rational \((\alpha,p)\), first check the nonnegativity constraints on \(p\). Otherwise, for every type compute a demanded bundle

\(\displaystyle S_t\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{S\subseteq E}\left(v_t(S)-\sum_{a\in S}p_a\right).\)

All bundle inequalities for type \(t\) hold exactly when

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t\ge v_t(S_t)-\sum_{a\in S_t}p_a.\)

If this fails, the oracle returns the explicitly indexed violated inequality

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t+\sum_{a\in S_t}p_a\ge v_t(S_t).\)

Thus separation takes \(O(\tau n^2)\) valuation evaluations and polynomial-time rational arithmetic. Crucially, the oracle returns an actual defining inequality, together with its type and bundle.

4. Explicit bounds for exact oracle linear programming

We verify the size and boundedness hypotheses needed for exact rational optimization.

Choose a computable integer \(B\), polynomially bounded in \(L\), bounding the numerator and denominator bit lengths of all input rationals and all circuit outputs. Set \(M=2^B\). Then

\(|v_t(S)|\le M\)

for every type and bundle. Since the total mass is one, every feasible welfare lies in \([-M,M]\).

There exists an optimal dual solution satisfying

\(-M\le\alpha_t\le M,\qquad 0\le p_a\le 2M/q_a.\)

To prove this, start with any optimal dual solution and decrease each \(\alpha_t\) to its demand value

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t=\max_{S\subseteq E}\bigl(v_t(S)-p(S)\bigr).\)

This preserves feasibility and cannot increase the objective. Because every \(\mu_t>0\), optimality forces the resulting objective to equal the optimum. The empty bundle and nonnegative prices give

\(-M\le v_t(\emptyset)\le\alpha_t\le M.\)

If the optimal welfare is \(W^\star\), then

\(\displaystyle \sum_aq_ap_a=W^\star-\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t\le M-(-M)=2M.\)

Every summand is nonnegative, proving the individual price bounds.

Let \(q_{\min}=\min_{a\in E}q_a\), and put

\(P_{\max}=1+2M/q_{\min}.\)

We may therefore add the box constraints

\(-M-1\le\alpha_t\le M+1,\qquad 0\le p_a\le P_{\max}\)

without changing the optimum. All these bounds have polynomial encoding length.

The resulting polytope is full-dimensional. An explicit interior point is

\(\alpha_t=M+\tfrac12,\qquad p_a=\tfrac12.\)

Every bundle inequality has slack at least \(\tfrac12\), and every box inequality has slack at least \(\tfrac12\). Since each bundle inequality has at most \(d\) unit coefficients, a Euclidean ball of radius \(1/(4(d+1))\) around this point lies inside the polytope.

Finally, every defining inequality has polynomial encoding length. Clearing denominators row by row and applying Cramer’s rule to any nonsingular system of \(d\) tight inequalities bounds every vertex’s encoding length by a polynomial in \(d,L\).

5. Recovering an optimal assignment

We use the standard rational oracle-LP theorem: for a bounded rational polytope with known polynomial bounds on coefficient and vertex encoding lengths, an exact separation oracle permits exact linear optimization in polynomial time. When the oracle returns defining inequalities, its constructive dual-recovery version also returns optimal nonnegative multipliers on polynomially many such inequalities. See [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Theorems 3.8–3.10](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/10046/10046D.pdf).

Section 4 verifies these hypotheses, including an explicit interior point and radius. Section 3 supplies the required exact separation oracle.

Apply this theorem to the boxed dual \(D\). Write every constraint with the inequality directed as \(\ge\). The recovered multipliers satisfy coefficient balance against the minimization objective vector \((\mu,q)\), and their weighted right-hand sides equal \(W^\star\).

The added box constraints have zero multipliers. To see this, choose the optimal dual solution bounded by \([-M,M]\) and \(2M/q_a\) established in Section 4. At this solution all added lower and upper bounds are strictly slack; the original constraints \(p_a\ge0\) are retained separately. Complementary slackness with any optimal multiplier solution therefore forces every added-box multiplier to vanish.

Let \(\lambda_{t,S}\) denote the multiplier on the bundle inequality indexed by \((t,S)\), and let \(s_a\) denote the multiplier on \(p_a\ge0\). Coefficient balance gives exactly

\(\displaystyle \sum_S\lambda_{t,S}=\mu_t,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\sum_{S\ni a}\lambda_{t,S}+s_a=q_a.\)

All multipliers are nonnegative. Hence \(\lambda\) is a feasible mass assignment, with unused supply \(s_a\). Its welfare is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,S}\lambda_{t,S}v_t(S)=W^\star,\)

because the constraints \(p_a\ge0\) have zero right-hand sides. Thus the recovered assignment is exactly optimal.

6. Support size, output length, and decision complexity

The oracle procedure identifies only polynomially many bundle inequalities. Restrict \(P\) to the corresponding assignment variables; the recovered multipliers show that this restricted LP already attains \(W^\star\).

Add one nonnegative supply-slack variable for every remaining item. The resulting standard-form LP has \(d=\tau+n\) equality rows. Its matrix has full row rank: the empty-bundle columns make the type rows independent, and the slack columns make the supply rows independent of those rows. Consequently, an optimal basic feasible solution has at most \(d\) positive variables in total, and therefore at most

\(\tau+n\le\tau+m\)

nonzero assignment entries.

These entries have polynomial bit length. Indeed, the basis matrix has only zero-one entries. Clear the denominators of the \(d\) masses and supplies using their product, whose bit length is polynomial in \(d,L\). Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound \(|\det A|\le d^{d/2}\) then give polynomial encoding length for every basic coordinate. Computing welfare uses at most \(d\) circuit values, so its exact rational encoding length is polynomial as well.

The algorithm therefore outputs the requested list of nonzero rational entries in polynomial time. Comparing its exact welfare with any input rational threshold also takes polynomial time.

This settles the specified mass-assignment problem. Its threshold decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{P}\); NP-hardness under polynomial-time reductions would consequently imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
