The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(S\) with rational masses \(\mu_s \ge 0\), \(\sum_{s\in S}\mu_s=1\), integral per-unit resource endowments \(b_{sk}\), and tasks \(i=1,\ldots,m\) with rational demands \(d_{ik}\), positive rewards \(\rho_i\), and a threshold \(Q\), decide whether there are task decisions \(y_i\in\{0,1\}\) and nonnegative aggregate flows \(x_{isk}\) such that \(\sum_{s\in S}x_{isk}=d_{ik}y_i\), \(\sum_{i=1}^m x_{isk}\le\mu_s b_{sk}\), and \(\sum_{i=1}^m\rho_i y_i\ge Q\). Each completed task receives its full required resources; coordination costs are zero.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Classification

The decision problem is NP-complete, even when \( \tau=r=1 \), the sole mass is \( \mu_1=1 \), and all demands, endowments, rewards, and the threshold are integers. Consequently, it has no polynomial-time algorithm in \( m,\tau,r,L \) unless \( \mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP} \).

Divisible flows make resource allocation straightforward once the completed tasks are chosen. Choosing those tasks remains NP-hard.

2. Exact characterization of feasible task selections

For each resource kind \( k \), define its total available amount by
\( C_k=\sum_{s\in S}\mu_s b_{sk} \).

Lemma. A vector \( y\in\{0,1\}^m \) admits feasible aggregate flows if and only if
\( \sum_{i=1}^m d_{ik}y_i\le C_k \)
for every \( k \).

*Proof.* Suppose feasible flows exist. Summing their task equalities and then using their supply inequalities gives
\( \sum_i d_{ik}y_i=\sum_s\sum_i x_{isk}\le\sum_s\mu_s b_{sk}=C_k \).
This proves necessity.

Conversely, suppose all the stated inequalities hold. If \( C_k>0 \), set
\( x_{isk}=d_{ik}y_i\,\mu_s b_{sk}/C_k \).
These numbers are nonnegative, and
\( \sum_s x_{isk}=d_{ik}y_i \),
because the supply fractions sum to one. For every type \( s \),
\( \sum_i x_{isk}=(\mu_s b_{sk}/C_k)\sum_i d_{ik}y_i\le\mu_s b_{sk} \).

If \( C_k=0 \), nonnegativity and the assumed inequality imply
\( \sum_i d_{ik}y_i=0 \).
Every summand therefore vanishes, so setting all \( x_{isk}=0 \) satisfies the task equalities and supply inequalities for this resource. The constructions apply independently to each resource kind. This proves sufficiency. \( \square \)

Thus the original decision problem is exactly the following selection problem:
choose \( y\in\{0,1\}^m \) satisfying
\( \sum_i d_{ik}y_i\le C_k \) for every \( k \), and
\( \sum_i\rho_i y_i\ge Q \).

The lemma also handles tasks with all demands zero: selecting such a task consumes no resources and earns its full reward.

3. Membership in \( \mathrm{NP} \)

The certificate is simply the \( m \)-bit vector \( y \). By the lemma, verification requires checking the resource inequalities and the reward threshold; no real-valued flow certificate is needed.

Here is an explicit bit-complexity justification. Write the rational inputs as integer numerators over positive integer denominators. Let \( D \) be the product of all those denominators, including those of the masses, demands, rewards, and threshold. Since their encodings together occupy at most \( L \) bits, \( D \) has \( O(L) \) bits.

Define the integers
\( a_{ik}=Dd_{ik} \),
\( w_i=D\rho_i \),
\( q=DQ \), and
\( B_k=\sum_s(D\mu_s)b_{sk} \).
The factors \( D\mu_s \) are integers. Each resulting number, and each sum used in verification, has \( O(L) \) bits: products add bit lengths, and summing at most \( m \) or \( \tau \) terms adds at most \( O(\log(m+\tau)) \) bits.

Verification now checks the integer inequalities
\( \sum_i a_{ik}y_i\le B_k \) and
\( \sum_i w_i y_i\ge q \).
Using ordinary quadratic-time integer multiplication and division, preprocessing and verification take
\( O((L+\tau r+mr+m)L^2) \)
bit operations, hence polynomial time. For the explicitly listed input, this is also bounded by \( O(L^3) \).

Therefore the problem belongs to \( \mathrm{NP} \).

4. NP-hardness with one type and one resource

Use the standard NP-complete SUBSET SUM problem: given positive integers \( a_1,\ldots,a_m \) and a positive integer target \( B \), decide whether a subset sums exactly to \( B \).

Construct a society instance as follows:

  • Set \( \tau=r=1 \) and \( \mu_1=1 \).
  • Give its sole type endowment \( b_{11}=B \).
  • For each task \( i \), set \( d_{i1}=a_i \) and \( \rho_i=a_i \).
  • Set \( Q=B \).

Every parameter satisfies the required hypotheses. The construction copies binary-encoded integers and has polynomial size.

By the lemma, a task selection is resource-feasible precisely when
\( \sum_i a_i y_i\le B \).
Its reward reaches the threshold precisely when
\( \sum_i a_i y_i\ge B \).
Both conditions hold exactly when
\( \sum_i a_i y_i=B \).

Thus the constructed instance is a yes-instance if and only if the SUBSET SUM instance is a yes-instance. This proves NP-hardness under the stated restrictions. Together with membership in \( \mathrm{NP} \), it proves NP-completeness.

This reduction applies directly to divisible flows and does not use the finite-agent theorem concerning integer contributions.

5. Running times and their interpretation

An exact algorithm computes the scaled integer data above and enumerates all \( 2^m \) task selections. Enumerate in Gray-code order, so consecutive selections differ in one task. Maintain the \( r \) resource totals and the reward total; each step uses \( O(r+1) \) additions, subtractions, and comparisons on \( O(L) \)-bit integers.

Including preprocessing, its running time is
\( O((L+\tau r+mr+m)L^2+2^m(r+1)L) \)
bit operations, or
\( O(L^3+2^m(r+1)L) \)
for the explicitly listed encoding. In particular, fixed \( m \) gives polynomial time in \( \tau,r,L \). Fixing \( \tau \) and \( r \) alone does not remove NP-hardness.

For comparison, when \( r=1 \), the scaled integer formulation also admits the usual capacity dynamic program in
\( O(m(B_1+1)L) \)
bit operations after preprocessing. Each layer records the maximum reward attainable at each exact resource consumption, using the previous layer to enforce that each task is selected at most once. This also correctly processes zero-demand tasks. The bound depends on the *numerical value* of \( B_1 \), which can be exponential in \( L \); it therefore does not yield polynomial time for binary-encoded inputs.
