The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational \(M>0\), \(c_0,c>0\), and \(\delta\in(0,1)\), let \(X=[0,M]\) be an atomless population of one complete type. For a finite or countable family \(\mathcal H\) of measurable positive-mass associations, give each agent the paper's unit baseline value, direct benefit equal to the mass directly connected, discounted indirect benefits by association-path distance, and per-capita association cost \(\sum_{A\ni x}(c+c_0/\nu(A))\). Characterize or compute a family \(\mathcal H\) maximizing aggregate net benefit over \(X\), including the optimal structures at the threshold.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) denotes support size, not the number of possible types. Here \(\tau=1\): all agents have the same type, and \(X=[0,M]\) carries Lebesgue measure \(\nu\), with total mass \(M\), without probability normalization.
The input consists of binary rationals \(M,c_0,c>0\), \(\delta\in(0,1)\), and a welfare threshold \(q\in\mathbb Q\); let \(L\) be their total bit length. State running times in \(\tau,L\); there is no additional combinatorial size parameter \(m\).
An admissible family \(\mathcal H\), possibly empty, is a finite or countable set of distinct Lebesgue-measurable associations \(A\subseteq X\) with \(\nu(A)>0\). Membership is nonexclusive.
Define \(t_{\mathcal H}(x,y)\) as the minimum \(k\geq0\) for which associations \(A_0,\ldots,A_k\) satisfy \(x\in A_0\), \(y\in A_k\), and \(A_{j-1}\cap A_j\neq\varnothing\) for every \(1\leq j\leq k\); set it to \(\infty\) if no such chain exists, and set \(\delta^\infty=0\). Nonempty intersections of zero mass also transmit connections.
Each agent has unit baseline benefit and benefit \(B_{\mathcal H}(x)=1+\int_{X\setminus\{x\}}\delta^{t_{\mathcal H}(x,y)}\,d\nu(y)\); directly connected mass thus receives weight \(1\), and each other agent's value is counted once.
Each member of \(A\) pays \(c+c_0/\nu(A)\). Aggregate welfare is \(V(\mathcal H)=\int_X B_{\mathcal H}(x)\,d\nu(x)-\sum_{A\in\mathcal H}(c_0+c\nu(A))\), with \(V(\mathcal H)=-\infty\) when the cost sum diverges. Welfare ties are retained.

Is deciding whether some admissible \(\mathcal H\) satisfies \(V(\mathcal H)\geq q\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it NP-hard?
What are all welfare-maximizing families as functions of \(M,c_0,c,\delta\), including at thresholds where optimal structures tie?

For \(n\geq2\) discrete agents, the unique welfare maximizer is the grand association when \(c+c_0/n<n-1\), and the empty family when \(c+c_0/n>n-1\); both tie at equality, as the displayed welfare comparison shows. [Jun–Kim](https://hermes-ir.lib.hit-u.ac.jp/hermes/ir/re/18044/HJeco0500200010.pdf)

  • Use Lebesgue measure of total mass M, without normalizing masses to sum to one.
  • Require delta, as well as the costs and total mass, to be a binary rational, and introduce a binary-rational welfare threshold q to define the complexity question.

  • Preserve the discrete path convention: direct distance is zero, consecutive associations need only intersect nontrivially, and zero-mass intermediaries can transmit connections.

Complete result

Define \(g=M^2-cM-c_0\). The maximum welfare is
\[ V^*=M+\max\{0,g\}. \]

All maximizing families are as follows:

  • If \(g<0\), the empty family is the unique maximizer.
  • If \(g>0\), the maximizers are exactly the singleton families \(\{A\}\), where \(A\subseteq X\) is Lebesgue-measurable and \(\nu(A)=M\).
  • If \(g=0\), the empty family and all those full-measure singleton families maximize welfare.

Thus the decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). These conclusions hold for every permitted \(\delta\).

1. Measurability and the benefit bound

For an admissible family \(\mathcal H\), let \(U=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal H}A\) and \(u=\nu(U)\). Define the connection kernel \(K(x,y)=\delta^{t_{\mathcal H}(x,y)}\).

This kernel is measurable. Indeed, for each nonnegative integer \(k\), the set of pairs satisfying \(t_{\mathcal H}(x,y)\leq k\) is the union of rectangles \(A_0\times A_r\) over all valid association chains of lengths \(r\leq k\). There are countably many such chains, and every rectangle is measurable. Whether consecutive associations intersect, including at a null set, determines which chains enter this union.

If either endpoint lies outside \(U\), no chain connects them. Consequently,
\[ 0\leq K(x,y)\leq \mathbf 1_U(x)\mathbf 1_U(y). \]

Removing the singleton \(\{x\}\) from the inner integral does not change it, since Lebesgue measure is atomless. Tonelli’s theorem therefore gives total benefit \(M+F\), where
\[ F=\int_X\int_X K(x,y)\,d\nu(y)\,d\nu(x), \qquad 0\leq F\leq u^2. \]

This bound includes all connections transmitted through null intersections.

2. A welfare bound with explicit equality conditions

A countably infinite family has divergent costs because each association contributes at least \(c_0>0\). Its welfare is therefore \(-\infty\), so it cannot maximize welfare or satisfy a finite threshold.

Consider a nonempty finite family with \(N\) associations. Write
\[ S=\sum_{A\in\mathcal H}\nu(A). \]
Since membership may overlap, \(S\geq u\). Also \(0<u\leq M\).

Introduce the nonnegative quantities \(R=S-u\) and \(D=u^2-F\), and put \(\lambda=u/M\). Then \(0<\lambda\leq1\), and
\[ V(\mathcal H)-M =u^2-cu-Nc_0-cR-D. \]

Substituting \(g=M^2-cM-c_0\) gives the exact identity
\[ V(\mathcal H)-M =\lambda g-u(M-u)-(N-\lambda)c_0-cR-D. \]

Every subtracted term is nonnegative: \(u\leq M\), \(N\geq1\geq\lambda\), and \(R,D\geq0\). In particular,
\[ V(\mathcal H)-M\leq\lambda g. \]

For the empty family, welfare is \(M\). For a singleton family \(\{A\}\) with \(\nu(A)=M\), almost every pair lies in \(A\times A\), hence is directly connected. Thus \(F=M^2\), and its welfare is \(M+g\).

3. Classification of every maximizer

Case \(g<0\). Every nonempty finite family satisfies \(V(\mathcal H)-M\leq\lambda g<0\). Infinite families have welfare \(-\infty\). Therefore the empty family is the unique maximizer.

Case \(g>0\). The bound gives
\[ V(\mathcal H)-M\leq\lambda g\leq g, \]
and full-measure singleton families attain \(M+g\).

For equality, \(\lambda=1\) is necessary because \(g>0\). The exact identity then requires \((N-1)c_0=0\), so \(N=1\). Its sole association consequently has measure \(u=M\). Conversely, every such singleton family attains the bound.

Case \(g=0\). The exact identity gives \(V(\mathcal H)\leq M\). If a nonempty family attains equality, then \((N-\lambda)c_0=0\). Since \(c_0>0\), \(N\) is a positive integer, and \(\lambda\leq1\), this forces \(N=1\) and \(\lambda=1\). Hence its sole association has full measure. Every such family, together with the empty family, attains \(M\).

This proves the stated classification. As actual measurable sets, maximizing associations need not equal \(X\): arbitrary null-set omissions preserve welfare.

The threshold can equivalently be written
\[ c+\frac{c_0}{M}=M. \]
The right-hand side is \(M\) because removing an agent’s singleton does not remove any Lebesgue mass.

4. Decision algorithm and running time

Compute \(g=M^2-cM-c_0\), and answer yes exactly when
\[ q\leq M+\max\{0,g\}. \]

This performs a constant number of exact rational arithmetic operations and comparisons. Intermediate numerators and denominators have \(O(L)\) bits. With schoolbook arithmetic, the running time is \(O(L^2)\) bit operations and the space requirement is \(O(L)\).

These bounds apply to the promised support size \(\tau=1\); there is no additional combinatorial size parameter.
