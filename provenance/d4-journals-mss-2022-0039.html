Result. Computing the exact rational minimizing weight vector is polynomial-time solvable. The minimizer is unique and affine in the constituency shares. This agrees with the characterization in [Kirsch–Toth, Theorem 21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12704#page=13). We derive the formula directly for the stated objective and then prove its exact computational complexity.

1. Conditional moments. Write \(p_z=\mu(\{z\})\) and \(p_{z,t}=\rho^z(\{t\})\). Define

\(\displaystyle h_z=\sum_{t\in\operatorname{supp}\rho^z}p_{z,t}t,\qquad a_z=\sum_{t\in\operatorname{supp}\rho^z}p_{z,t}|t|,\qquad d_z=\sum_{t>0}p_{z,t}-\sum_{t<0}p_{z,t}.\)

The three scalar quantities needed for the optimization are

\(\displaystyle q=\sum_zp_zd_z^2,\qquad A=\sum_zp_za_z,\qquad B=\sum_zp_zd_zh_z.\)

They are rational finite sums. Since \(|d_z|\leq1\), the non-tight correlation hypothesis gives \(0\leq q<1\).

The fair sign at zero has conditional expectation zero. Consequently, for every constituency \(\lambda\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[\chi_\lambda\mid Z=z]=d_z,\qquad \mathbb E[T_\lambda\mid Z=z]=h_z.\)

Moreover, \(T_\lambda\chi_\lambda=|T_\lambda|\), including when \(T_\lambda=0\). Thus

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[T_\lambda\chi_\lambda\mid Z=z]=a_z.\)

For distinct constituencies \(\lambda,\nu\), conditional independence of the bias draws and independence of the fair signs imply

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[\chi_\lambda\chi_\nu\mid Z=z]=d_z^2,\qquad \mathbb E[\chi_\lambda T_\nu\mid Z=z]=d_zh_z.\)

Averaging over \(Z\), and using \(\chi_\lambda^2=1\), yields

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[\chi_\lambda\chi_\nu]= \begin{cases} 1,&\lambda=\nu,\\ q,&\lambda\ne\nu. \end{cases}\)

Also, since \(\sum_\nu\alpha_\nu=1\),

\(\displaystyle \beta_\lambda:=\mathbb E[R\chi_\lambda] =\alpha_\lambda A+\sum_{\nu\ne\lambda}\alpha_\nu B =(A-B)\alpha_\lambda+B.\)

2. Unique global minimizer. Let \(\mathbf1\in\mathbb R^M\) denote the vector whose entries are all one, and define the second-moment matrix

\(\displaystyle G=\mathbb E[\chi\chi^{\mathsf T}]=(1-q)I+q\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}.\)

Expanding the objective gives

\(\displaystyle F(w):=\mathbb E[(R-w^{\mathsf T}\chi)^2] =\mathbb E[R^2]-2\beta^{\mathsf T}w+w^{\mathsf T}Gw.\)

All expectations exist because the random variables are bounded. For every nonzero \(x\in\mathbb R^M\),

\(\displaystyle x^{\mathsf T}Gx =(1-q)\sum_{\lambda=1}^M x_\lambda^2 +q\left(\sum_{\lambda=1}^M x_\lambda\right)^2>0.\)

Here the first term is strictly positive because \(q<1\), and the second is nonnegative. Hence \(G\) is positive definite and invertible.

Let \(w^\star\) be the unique solution of \(Gw^\star=\beta\). Expanding around this solution gives

\(\displaystyle F(w)-F(w^\star) =(w-w^\star)^{\mathsf T}G(w-w^\star).\)

Positive definiteness proves that \(w^\star\) is the unique global minimizer.

To compute it explicitly, put \(S=\sum_\lambda w^\star_\lambda\). The normal equations are

\(\displaystyle (1-q)w^\star_\lambda+qS=(A-B)\alpha_\lambda+B.\)

Summing them gives

\(\displaystyle \bigl(1+(M-1)q\bigr)S=A+(M-1)B.\)

Both \(1-q\) and \(1+(M-1)q\) are positive. Substituting the resulting value of \(S\) into each normal equation yields

\(\displaystyle \boxed{ w^\star_\lambda =\frac{A-B}{1-q}\,\alpha_\lambda +\frac{B-qA}{(1-q)\bigl(1+(M-1)q\bigr)} \qquad(\lambda=1,\ldots,M). }\)

Indeed, the constant numerator simplifies using

\(\displaystyle B\bigl(1+(M-1)q\bigr)-q\bigl(A+(M-1)B\bigr)=B-qA.\)

This formula also covers \(M=1\): since \(\alpha_1=1\), it gives \(w^\star_1=A\).

3. Exact computation and bit complexity. The algorithm scans the listed distributions to compute \(d_z,h_z,a_z\), accumulates \(q,A,B\), and evaluates the boxed expression for each constituency. It uses \(O(K+M)\) rational arithmetic operations.

To establish polynomial time in the binary input model, we must also bound the sizes of the intermediate and output rationals.

Let \(Q\) be the product of the positive denominators of all input rationals. Every input denominator divides \(Q\), and the binary length of \(Q\) is \(O(L)\), because the sum of the lengths of its factors is bounded by the input length.

The reduced denominators of the conditional moments satisfy:

  • \(d_z\) has denominator dividing \(Q\);
  • \(h_z\) and \(a_z\) have denominators dividing \(Q^2\);
  • \(q\) and \(A\) have denominators dividing \(Q^3\);
  • \(B\) has denominator dividing \(Q^4\).

For example, each summand \(p_zd_zh_z\) has denominator dividing \(Q^4\), so its finite sum does too. The other assertions follow in the same way. These bounds also hold for partial sums during accumulation.

Furthermore, \(0\leq q<1\), \(0\leq A\leq1\), and \(|B|\leq1\). Thus their numerators, when expressed over the common denominator \(D=Q^4\), have binary length \(O(L)\). Write

\(\displaystyle q=\frac{u}{D},\qquad A=\frac{v}{D},\qquad B=\frac{s}{D}.\)

If \(\alpha_\lambda=a_\lambda/b_\lambda\), the minimizing weight can be represented explicitly as

\(\displaystyle w^\star_\lambda= \frac{ (v-s)a_\lambda\bigl(D+(M-1)u\bigr) +b_\lambda(sD-uv) }{ b_\lambda(D-u)\bigl(D+(M-1)u\bigr) }. \)

Every integer in this expression has binary length \(O(L+\log M)\). Its denominator is nonzero because \(q<1\). Reduction to lowest terms uses a greatest-common-divisor computation, also polynomial in these bit lengths.

Therefore every arithmetic operation takes polynomial time, and the complete output has polynomial length. The algorithm is polynomial-time in \(K,M,L\), hence in all the requested parameters, with \(m=2\) and \(\tau=M\).

The derivation applies to every promised input. Symmetry, sufficient randomness, and nontriviality require no additional computational step in the proof; positive definiteness follows directly from the stipulated non-tight correlation.
