Result

Both regimes admit an exactly feasible \((1-\varepsilon)\)-approximation in polynomial time. In fact, the algorithm below has logarithmic dependence on reciprocal accuracy and works without any angular restriction.

More precisely:

  • For every rational \(0<\alpha<1\), an \((\alpha,1)\)-approximation is computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and the encoding length of \(\alpha\).
  • For every rational \(\beta>1\), an \((1,\beta)\)-approximation is computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and the encoding length of \(\beta\).

Consequently, the stated discrete approximation barriers disappear for mass allocations. The endpoint \((1,1)\) requires a qualification: some instances have a unique irrational optimum, so rational outputs cannot always attain it.

The proof uses an explicit rational polyhedral relaxation of the demand disk. Rational lifted cone approximations are also studied in [Kocuk’s paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.00256); all approximation estimates needed here are proved below.

1. Formulation

Identify each demand with the vector \(d_t=(a_t,b_t)\). Define

\(D\lambda=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\lambda_t d_t,\qquad V(\lambda)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\lambda_t v_t.\)

The original feasible set is

\(K=\{\lambda\in\mathbb R^\tau:0\leq\lambda_t\leq\mu_t,\ \|D\lambda\|_2\leq C\}.\)

It is nonempty because it contains zero, and compact because it is a closed subset of the bounded mass box. Thus its continuous objective attains its maximum, denoted by \(\mathrm{OPT}\).

Our construction uses exactly \(\tau\) mass variables. It does not expand masses into individual customers.

2. A rational outer approximation of the disk

Fix an integer \(k\geq1\). For \(j=1,\ldots,k\), define

\(c_j=\dfrac{4^j-1}{4^j+1},\qquad s_j=\dfrac{2^{j+1}}{4^j+1}.\)

These numbers are positive rationals satisfying

\(c_j^2+s_j^2=1.\)

For a vector \((x,y)\), introduce auxiliary variables \(\xi_0,\eta_0,\ldots,\xi_k,\eta_k\), subject to

\(\xi_0\geq |x|,\qquad \eta_0\geq |y|,\)

\(\xi_j=c_j\xi_{j-1}+s_j\eta_{j-1}\quad (j=1,\ldots,k),\)

\(\eta_j\geq |-s_j\xi_{j-1}+c_j\eta_{j-1}|\quad (j=1,\ldots,k),\)

\(\xi_k\leq C,\qquad \eta_k\leq \dfrac{s_k}{c_k}\xi_k.\)

Each absolute-value inequality represents two linear inequalities. Consequently, this is a rational linear system.

Let \(P_k(C)\) be its projection onto the coordinates \((x,y)\), and put

\(\gamma_k=\dfrac{1}{c_k}=\dfrac{4^k+1}{4^k-1}=1+\dfrac{2}{4^k-1}.\)

We will prove the inclusions

\(\{(x,y):\|(x,y)\|_2\leq C\}\subseteq P_k(C)\subseteq\{(x,y):\|(x,y)\|_2\leq\gamma_k C\}.\)

Every vector in the disk admits a lift

Define angles, used only for this proof, by

\(\theta_0=\pi/2,\qquad \theta_j=2\arctan(2^{-j})\quad (j\geq1).\)

The half-angle identities give \(c_j=\cos\theta_j\) and \(s_j=\sin\theta_j\).

Furthermore,

\(\theta_{j-1}/2\leq\theta_j\leq\theta_{j-1}.\)

Indeed, \(\arctan u\) is increasing and concave on the nonnegative real axis and vanishes at zero. Hence \(\arctan(u/2)\geq\frac12\arctan u\); apply this with \(u=2^{-(j-1)}\), including \(j=1\).

For a vector in the disk, choose

\(\xi_0=|x|,\qquad \eta_0=|y|,\)

and recursively choose equality in the absolute-value inequalities:

\(\xi_j=c_j\xi_{j-1}+s_j\eta_{j-1},\qquad \eta_j=|-s_j\xi_{j-1}+c_j\eta_{j-1}|.\)

Each step rotates the preceding vector and reflects its second coordinate if necessary. It therefore preserves its Euclidean norm.

The initial vector has angle in \([0,\theta_0]\). If the preceding vector has angle \(u\in[0,\theta_{j-1}]\), the new angle is \(|u-\theta_j|\). Since \(\theta_{j-1}\leq2\theta_j\), this angle belongs to \([0,\theta_j]\). Induction proves that the terminal vector has angle at most \(\theta_k\).

Thus

\(\xi_k\leq\|(\xi_k,\eta_k)\|_2=\|(x,y)\|_2\leq C,\)

and

\(\eta_k\leq\tan(\theta_k)\xi_k=\dfrac{s_k}{c_k}\xi_k.\)

The zero vector satisfies the same constraints by taking every auxiliary variable equal to zero. This proves the first inclusion.

Every lifted vector lies in the expanded disk

Consider any solution of the linear system. All auxiliary variables are nonnegative. For every \(j\), rotation invariance and the absolute-value inequality imply

\(\xi_j^2+\eta_j^2\geq(c_j\xi_{j-1}+s_j\eta_{j-1})^2+(-s_j\xi_{j-1}+c_j\eta_{j-1})^2=\xi_{j-1}^2+\eta_{j-1}^2.\)

The initial inequalities therefore imply

\(\|(x,y)\|_2\leq\|(\xi_k,\eta_k)\|_2.\)

Using the terminal inequalities gives

\(\|(\xi_k,\eta_k)\|_2\leq\xi_k\sqrt{1+(s_k/c_k)^2}=\dfrac{\xi_k}{c_k}\leq\gamma_k C.\)

This proves the second inclusion.

3. Solving the relaxed problem

Substitute

\(x=\sum_t a_t\lambda_t,\qquad y=\sum_t b_t\lambda_t\)

into the linear system above. Retain the exact mass bounds and maximize \(V(\lambda)\). This produces a rational linear program.

It is feasible because the zero allocation has a zero lift. Its objective is bounded above by \(\sum_t\mu_t v_t\). The polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore returns an exact rational optimum with polynomial encoding length; see, for example, [MIT’s linear-programming notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html).

Write \(\lambda^{(k)}\) for its mass coordinates. The two disk inclusions prove

\(0\leq\lambda_t^{(k)}\leq\mu_t,\qquad V(\lambda^{(k)})\geq\mathrm{OPT},\qquad \|D\lambda^{(k)}\|_2\leq\gamma_k C.\)

The objective inequality follows because every allocation feasible for the original problem admits a feasible lift in this linear program.

For \(m=2\), the program has \(\tau+2k+2\) variables and \(O(\tau+mk)\) constraints. Every newly introduced coefficient has encoding length \(O(k)\). Construction and exact solution consequently take

\(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,k)\)

bit operations. Here \(m=2\), as stipulated in the question.

4. Exact feasibility and multiplicative approximation

Let \(0<\varepsilon<1\). Choose \(k\geq1\) so that

\(\dfrac{2}{4^k+1}\leq\varepsilon.\)

Such a choice satisfies \(k=O(1+\log(1/\varepsilon))\), and can be found using rational comparisons.

Return

\(\widehat\lambda=c_k\lambda^{(k)}.\)

Because \(0<c_k<1\), all mass bounds remain satisfied. Homogeneity of total demand gives

\(\|D\widehat\lambda\|_2=c_k\|D\lambda^{(k)}\|_2\leq c_k\gamma_k C=C.\)

Likewise,

\(V(\widehat\lambda)=c_kV(\lambda^{(k)})\geq c_k\mathrm{OPT}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}.\)

This proves the requested exactly feasible approximation.

Writing \(\ell(z)\) for the binary encoding length of a rational parameter \(z\), its running time is

\(\operatorname{poly}\bigl(m,\tau,L+\ell(\varepsilon),1+\log(1/\varepsilon)\bigr).\)

In particular, it is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\) when the supplied numerical parameters are included in \(L\).

For every \(\beta>1\), choosing \(k\) so that

\(\dfrac{2}{4^k-1}\leq\beta-1\)

and returning \(\lambda^{(k)}\) directly gives an \((1,\beta)\)-approximation. Its running time is

\(\operatorname{poly}\bigl(m,\tau,L+\ell(\beta),1+\log^+(1/(\beta-1))\bigr),\)

where \(\log^+u=\max\{0,\log u\}\).

5. If “binary-rational” requires dyadic outputs

The preceding algorithms already return rationals with polynomial-length binary encodings. If the requirement is specifically that outputs be dyadic, the following modification meets that requirement, using the stated dyadic input mass bounds.

Define the rational quantity

\(H=\sum_t(|a_t|+b_t).\)

If \(H=0\), allocating every mass achieves the optimum with zero total demand. Assume henceforth \(H>0\).

For any integer \(p\geq0\), round the relaxed allocation upward, capped at its mass bound:

\(u_t=\min\{\mu_t,\ 2^{-p}\lceil2^p\lambda_t^{(k)}\rceil\}.\)

Then \(u_t\) is dyadic and

\(0\leq u_t-\lambda_t^{(k)}\leq2^{-p}.\)

Since every value is nonnegative,

\(V(u)\geq V(\lambda^{(k)})\geq\mathrm{OPT}.\)

The triangle inequality gives, irrespective of demand cancellation,

\(\|Du\|_2\leq\|D\lambda^{(k)}\|_2+\sum_t(u_t-\lambda_t^{(k)})\|d_t\|_2\leq\gamma_k C+2^{-p}H.\)

Dyadic exactly feasible approximation

Choose \(k,p\) satisfying

\(\gamma_k\leq1+\varepsilon/8,\qquad 2^{-p}H\leq\varepsilon C/8.\)

Then \(\|Du\|_2\leq(1+\varepsilon/4)C\).

Choose \(r\geq0\) with \(2^{-r}\leq\varepsilon/4\), and define the dyadic multiplier

\(h=2^{-r}\lfloor2^r(1-\varepsilon/2)\rfloor.\)

It satisfies

\(1-3\varepsilon/4\leq h\leq1-\varepsilon/2.\)

Return \(\widehat\lambda=hu\). This allocation is dyadic and respects every mass bound. Moreover,

\(\|D\widehat\lambda\|_2\leq(1-\varepsilon/2)(1+\varepsilon/4)C\leq C,\)

and

\(V(\widehat\lambda)\geq h\mathrm{OPT}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}.\)

The exponents satisfy \(k,r=O(1+\log(1/\varepsilon))\) and \(p=O(L+1+\log(1/\varepsilon))\), so the polynomial running-time guarantee remains valid.

Dyadic \((1,\beta)\)-approximation

For \(\beta>1\), instead choose

\(\gamma_k\leq1+(\beta-1)/2,\qquad 2^{-p}H\leq(\beta-1)C/2.\)

Returning \(u\) gives \(V(u)\geq\mathrm{OPT}\) and \(\|Du\|_2\leq\beta C\). Again, all computations and output lengths are polynomial in the input and parameter encoding lengths.

Thus both conclusions hold under either interpretation of binary-rational outputs.

6. Application to the angular regimes and endpoint qualification

No step uses \(\phi\), \(\delta\), or \(\delta'\). Negative real demand components, cancellation, and individual demands exceeding \(C\) are all allowed.

Therefore the exactly feasible scheme applies both to regime (i) and to regime (ii), with no dependence on the reciprocal angular gap or its cotangent.

For polynomial-bit factors, the classification is:

| Factors | Guarantee for mass allocations |
|---|---|
| \(0<\alpha<1,\ \beta\geq1\) | Polynomial-time algorithm; exact feasibility is available |
| \(\alpha=1,\ \beta>1\) | Polynomial-time algorithm |
| \(\alpha=1,\ \beta=1\) | Rational-output attainment can be impossible |

For the last row, take one type with

\(\mu_1=v_1=C=1,\qquad d_1=-N+i,\)

where \(N\geq1\) is an integer. Feasibility is precisely

\(0\leq\lambda_1\leq1/\sqrt{N^2+1}.\)

Since the objective equals \(\lambda_1\), the unique optimum is \(1/\sqrt{N^2+1}\). It is irrational: the integer \(N^2+1\) lies strictly between the consecutive squares \(N^2\) and \((N+1)^2\).

The demand argument is \(\pi-\arctan(1/N)\). For every allowed \(\delta\), sufficiently large \(N\) places this instance in regime (i). For regime (ii), with \(\tau=1\), choosing \(N=2^{q(1)+1}\) gives

\(\arctan(1/N)<1/N<2^{-q(1)}=\delta'.\)

Thus the exact endpoint obstruction occurs in both regimes. It is an output-representation obstruction, independent of computational complexity; it does not affect any positive approximation tolerance.
