The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite applicant type set \(T\), binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), strict rankings over \(o_1,o_2,\bot\), and each organization’s rational piecewise-polyhedral choice map \(C_j(q)\leq q\) satisfying \(q\leq q'\Rightarrow C_j(q)\leq C_j(q')\), find an exactly stable allocation \(x_{t,a}\geq 0\) with \(\sum_a x_{t,a}=\mu_t\), or determine that none exists. Require applicant and organization individual rationality, and rule out every nonzero blocking cohort \(z\) whose members prefer \(o_j\) to their current assignment and satisfy \(z\leq C_j(x_{\cdot,o_j}+z)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are exactly \(m=2\) organizations, \(o_1,o_2\), and an outside option \(\bot\). A society is given by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) applicant types \(T\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and a strict ranking \(\succ_t\) over \(\{o_1,o_2,\bot\}\). Types may also encode characteristics relevant to organizations; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each organization has a single-valued choice map \(C_j:[0,\mu]\to[0,\mu]\), where inequalities are componentwise, satisfying \(0\leq C_j(q)\leq q\), \(q\leq q'\Rightarrow C_j(q)\leq C_j(q')\), and contraction consistency: \(C_j(q)\leq r\leq q\Rightarrow C_j(r)=C_j(q)\).
Each map is explicitly encoded by a finite partition of \([0,\mu]\) into regions specified by rational strict or weak linear inequalities, with a rational affine formula on each region. Boundary assignments are included; continuity is not required. Let \(K\) be the total number of regions and \(L\) the total binary input length.
An allocation has real masses \(x_{t,a}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{a\in\{o_1,o_2,\bot\}}x_{t,a}=\mu_t\). Individual rationality means \(x_{t,o_j}=0\) whenever \(\bot\succ_t o_j\), and \(C_j(x_{\cdot,o_j})=x_{\cdot,o_j}\) for each organization.
A blocking cohort for \(o_j\) is a real vector \(z\geq0\), \(z\neq0\), satisfying \(z_t\leq\sum_{a:o_j\succ_t a}x_{t,a}\) for every type and \(z\leq C_j(x_{\cdot,o_j}+z)\). Stability means individual rationality and absence of such a cohort for either organization; an organization may drop incumbent mass when evaluating a cohort.

Can an exactly stable allocation be found, or its nonexistence determined, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finite applicants with complementary organizational choices, alternate deferred acceptance finds a stable outcome; its number of choice evaluations is polynomial in the number of contracts. [Discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10942v1)
Continuum matching has an existence theorem for continuous, preference-induced organizational choices, including complementarities; this does not settle exact computation for the encoded maps above. [Continuum result](https://www.columbia.edu/~yc2271/files/papers/Continuum-submission.pdf)

Result

The problem is $\mathsf{NP}$-hard already for $m=2$. Stable existence is also in $\mathsf{NP}$ on the promised input class.

The reduction below uses dyadic masses, $2n+2$ types, and only $4n+8$ globally specified affine regions. Every constructed choice map satisfies contractiveness, monotonicity, and contraction consistency.

1. Two consequences of the blocking definition

Write $x^j=x_{\cdot,o_j}$.

First, contraction consistency implies idempotence. Indeed, applying it with $r=C_j(q)$ gives
$$ C_j(C_j(q))=C_j(q). $$

Lemma 1: Stable allocations are unsplit

In every stable allocation, each type assigns its entire mass to one alternative.

Proof. Suppose type $t$ has positive mass at two or more alternatives. Let $o_j$ be its most preferred alternative carrying positive mass. This alternative must be an organization: if it were $\bot$, positive mass at any other occupied alternative would violate applicant individual rationality.

Consequently,
$$ x_{t,o_j}>0, \qquad \sum_{a:o_j\succ_t a}x_{t,a} =\mu_t-x_{t,o_j}>0. $$
Choose
$$ 0<\varepsilon\leq \min\{x_{t,o_j},\mu_t-x_{t,o_j}\}, $$
and set $z_t=\varepsilon$, with all other coordinates of $z$ zero. This cohort satisfies availability, and $x^j+z$ belongs to the choice map’s domain. Organizational individual rationality and monotonicity give
$$ C_j(x^j+z)\geq C_j(x^j)=x^j\geq z. $$
Thus $z$ blocks, contradicting stability. $\square$

An unsplit allocation can therefore be represented by an assignment
$$ p:T\longrightarrow\{o_1,o_2,\bot\}. $$

For such an assignment, define the available menu
$$ q^j_t= \begin{cases} \mu_t,&p(t)=o_j\text{ or }o_j\succ_t p(t),\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$

Lemma 2: Exact stability criterion

An unsplit assignment satisfying applicant individual rationality is stable if and only if
$$ C_j(q^j)=x^j \qquad(j=1,2). $$

Proof. Suppose the assignment is stable. Fix $j$ and let $y=C_j(q^j)$. Organizational individual rationality and monotonicity imply
$$ x^j=C_j(x^j)\leq y. $$
If $y\neq x^j$, define $z=y-x^j$. This vector is nonnegative and nonzero.

At every incumbent coordinate, $x^j_t=q^j_t=\mu_t$, so contractiveness forces $y_t=\mu_t$ and hence $z_t=0$. At every nonincumbent coordinate, contractiveness gives $z_t=y_t\leq q^j_t$. Thus positive cohort mass occurs only at types preferring $o_j$ to their assignment, and availability holds.

Idempotence gives
$$ C_j(x^j+z)=C_j(y)=y\geq z. $$
This is a block, a contradiction. Hence $C_j(q^j)=x^j$.

Conversely, suppose these equalities hold. Contraction consistency, applied between $q^j$ and $x^j$, gives
$$ C_j(x^j)=x^j, $$
so organizational individual rationality holds.

Any blocking cohort would satisfy $x^j+z\leq q^j$, and therefore
$$ z\leq C_j(x^j+z)\leq C_j(q^j)=x^j. $$
Availability forces $z_t=0$ at incumbent coordinates. At nonincumbent coordinates, $x^j_t=0$, so the displayed inequality also forces $z_t=0$. This contradicts $z\neq0$. $\square$

2. Membership in $\mathsf{NP}$

A certificate specifies one alternative per type. Check applicant individual rationality, construct the two menus, and apply Lemma 2.

Each menu coordinate is either zero or an input rational mass. Scanning the explicitly encoded regions, testing their rational strict or weak inequalities, evaluating the selected affine formula, and comparing the result with $x^j$ all take polynomial time using exact rational arithmetic.

Thus stable existence belongs to $\mathsf{NP}$ on inputs promised to satisfy the choice-map axioms and partition requirements. This does not require checking those promises.

3. Source problem and applicant types

We reduce from positive-integer SUBSET SUM: given binary-encoded positive integers
$$ w_1,\ldots,w_n,\ B, $$
decide whether some $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{i\in S}w_i=B. $$
This is the classical $\mathsf{NP}$-complete problem called KNAPSACK in [Karp’s original paper, problem 18](https://studylib.net/doc/27985824/karp1972).

Put $W=\sum_i w_i$. We may restrict to
$$ 0<B<W. $$
This restriction preserves hardness: instances with $B=W$ are yes-instances, and those with $B>W$ are no-instances. These can respectively be mapped to the fixed restricted instances with weights $(1,1)$ and target $1$, and weights $(2,2)$ and target $1$.

Create types
$$ a_1,\ldots,a_n,\ b_1,\ldots,b_n,\ d_1,d_2. $$
Their rankings are
$$ \begin{aligned} a_i &: o_1\succ o_2\succ\bot,\\ b_i &: o_2\succ o_1\succ\bot,\\ d_1 &: o_1\succ\bot\succ o_2,\\ d_2 &: o_2\succ\bot\succ o_1. \end{aligned} $$

Let $N$ be the smallest power of two satisfying $N\geq2n+2$, and set $\delta=1/N$. Define
$$ \mu_{a_i}=\mu_{b_i}=\delta,\qquad \mu_{d_1}=\delta,\qquad \mu_{d_2}=1-(2n+1)\delta. $$
All masses are positive dyadic rationals, and their sum is one.

4. A choice map with a polynomial global partition

We first define and verify a generic map used for both organizations.

Order the paired coordinates as $(r_i,s_i)$, designate one marker type $d$, and ignore the other marker $\bar d$. Let $\eta=\mu_d$, choose a threshold $H$ with $0<H<W$, and define
$$ \sigma(q)=\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_{i=1}^n w_i q_{s_i}. $$

The ignored coordinate always has output zero. The other outputs are as follows.

Above the threshold: If $\sigma(q)>H$, retain every active coordinate:
$$ C(q)_{r_i}=q_{r_i},\qquad C(q)_{s_i}=q_{s_i},\qquad C(q)_d=q_d. $$

At the threshold with sufficient paired mass: If
$$ \sigma(q)=H \quad\text{and}\quad q_{r_i}\geq q_{s_i}\ \text{for every }i, $$
choose
$$ C(q)_{r_i}=C(q)_{s_i}=q_{s_i}, \qquad C(q)_d=q_d. $$

Fallback: In every other case, choose
$$ C(q)_{r_i}=C(q)_{s_i}=0, \qquad C(q)_d=\min\{q_d,\eta/2\}. $$

Explicit region encoding

The preceding definition has an explicit partition into $2n+4$ rational polyhedral regions:

  • One region with $\sigma(q)>H$.
  • One region with $\sigma(q)=H$ and all $q_{r_i}\geq q_{s_i}$.
  • Two regions with $\sigma(q)<H$, distinguished by $q_d\leq\eta/2$ or $q_d>\eta/2$.
  • For each $i$, two regions specified by

$$ \sigma(q)=H,\qquad q_{r_\ell}\geq q_{s_\ell}\quad(\ell<i),\qquad q_{r_i}<q_{s_i}, $$
again distinguished by $q_d\leq\eta/2$ or $q_d>\eta/2$.

The fourth family assigns a failed equality condition to its first violated pair. Hence these regions are disjoint and exhaustive, including all boundaries.

Every formula is rational affine. In fallback regions the marker formula is either $q_d$ or the constant $\eta/2$. Equalities are encoded by two weak linear inequalities.

Verification of contractiveness

Nonnegativity is immediate. Above the threshold, active coordinates are retained. At a successful equality, copied mass satisfies $q_{s_i}\leq q_{r_i}$. In fallback, only marker mass is chosen, and it does not exceed $q_d$. Thus
$$ 0\leq C(q)\leq q. $$

Verification of monotonicity

Let $q\leq q'$.

If $q$ uses fallback, its only potentially positive output is the marker cap. Every branch at $q'$ satisfies
$$ C(q')_d\geq\min\{q'_d,\eta/2\} \geq\min\{q_d,\eta/2\}. $$
All other comparisons follow from nonnegativity.

If $\sigma(q)>H$, then $\sigma(q')>H$, and both outputs retain their active inputs.

Finally, suppose $q$ uses the successful equality branch. If $\sigma(q')=H$, positivity of all weights and $q'_{s_i}\geq q_{s_i}$ imply
$$ q'_{s_i}=q_{s_i}\quad\text{for every }i. $$
Also,
$$ q'_{r_i}\geq q_{r_i}\geq q_{s_i}=q'_{s_i}, $$
so $q'$ uses the same branch and its output dominates that at $q$.

If instead $\sigma(q')>H$, its output retains its active inputs; in particular,
$$ q'_{r_i}\geq q_{r_i}\geq q_{s_i}=C(q)_{r_i}. $$
The supporter and marker comparisons also hold. This proves monotonicity.

Verification of contraction consistency

Suppose
$$ C(q)\leq p\leq q. $$

If $q$ is above the threshold, every active coordinate is retained, so those coordinates of $p$ equal those of $q$. The ignored coordinate has no effect. Consequently $C(p)=C(q)$.

If $q$ uses successful equality, then
$$ p_{s_i}=q_{s_i},\qquad p_d=q_d,\qquad p_{r_i}\geq q_{s_i}=p_{s_i}. $$
Therefore $\sigma(p)=H$, $p$ also uses successful equality, and $C(p)=C(q)$.

If $q$ uses fallback, then
$$ \sigma(p)\leq\sigma(q)\leq H. $$
When $\sigma(p)<H$, $p$ uses fallback. If $\sigma(p)=H$, then $\sigma(q)=H$, and positivity of the weights forces $p_{s_i}=q_{s_i}$ for all $i$. Some pair failed at $q$, so for that pair
$$ p_{r_i}\leq q_{r_i}<q_{s_i}=p_{s_i}. $$
Thus $p$ still uses fallback.

In either fallback case,
$$ \min\{q_d,\eta/2\}\leq p_d\leq q_d $$
implies
$$ \min\{p_d,\eta/2\}=\min\{q_d,\eta/2\}. $$
Hence $C(p)=C(q)$. All three required axioms hold globally.

5. The two organizations and encoding size

Use the generic map as follows:
$$ \begin{aligned} C_1 &: r_i=a_i,\quad s_i=b_i,\quad d=d_1,\quad \bar d=d_2,\quad H=B,\\ C_2 &: r_i=b_i,\quad s_i=a_i,\quad d=d_2,\quad \bar d=d_1,\quad H=W-B. \end{aligned} $$

Section 4 verifies every choice-map promise for both organizations.

There are
$$ m=2,\qquad \tau=2n+2,\qquad K=4n+8. $$
Each partition has only polynomially many inequalities and formula coefficients. The weighted-sum coefficients are
$$ \frac{w_i}{\delta}=Nw_i, $$
whose binary lengths are polynomial in the source input length. The thresholds, masses, and marker caps also have polynomial binary length. Thus the entire global piecewise-affine encoding is generated in polynomial time and has polynomial length $L$.

6. Correctness of the reduction

A stable allocation yields a subset-sum solution

Suppose a stable allocation exists. By Lemma 1 it is unsplit, and by Lemma 2,
$$ C_1(q^1)=x^1,\qquad C_2(q^2)=x^2. $$

The rankings imply
$$ \begin{aligned} q^1_{a_i}&=\delta, & q^1_{b_i}&=\delta-x^2_{b_i}, & q^1_{d_1}&=\mu_{d_1}, & q^1_{d_2}&=0,\\ q^2_{b_i}&=\delta, & q^2_{a_i}&=\delta-x^1_{a_i}, & q^2_{d_2}&=\mu_{d_2}, & q^2_{d_1}&=0. \end{aligned} $$
In particular, the paired inequalities required for successful equality hold at both menus.

Neither organization can use fallback: it would choose exactly half of its marker’s full mass, whereas $x^j_{d_j}$ must be either zero or that full mass. Consequently,
$$ \sigma_1(q^1)\geq B, \qquad \sigma_2(q^2)\geq W-B. $$

Suppose $\sigma_1(q^1)>B$. Then $C_1$ retains every $a_i$ coordinate, so
$$ x^1_{a_i}=\delta \quad\text{for every }i. $$
It follows that $q^2_{a_i}=0$ for every $i$, giving
$$ \sigma_2(q^2)=0<W-B, $$
a contradiction.

Likewise, if $\sigma_2(q^2)>W-B$, then every $b_i$ is assigned to $o_2$, implying $\sigma_1(q^1)=0<B$. Therefore
$$ \sigma_1(q^1)=B,\qquad \sigma_2(q^2)=W-B. $$

The equality formula for $C_1$ gives
$$ x^1_{a_i}=x^1_{b_i}=q^1_{b_i}. $$
Define
$$ S=\{i:x^1_{b_i}=\delta\}. $$
Because the allocation is unsplit, $q^1_{b_i}$ is $\delta$ for $i\in S$ and zero otherwise. Hence
$$ B=\sigma_1(q^1) =\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_i w_iq^1_{b_i} =\sum_{i\in S}w_i. $$
Thus $S$ solves SUBSET SUM.

A subset-sum solution yields a stable allocation

Conversely, suppose
$$ \sum_{i\in S}w_i=B. $$
Assign both $a_i$ and $b_i$ to $o_1$ when $i\in S$, and assign both to $o_2$ when $i\notin S$. Assign $d_j$ to $o_j$.

Every assigned organization is acceptable. The available menus satisfy
$$ q^1_{a_i}=\delta,\qquad q^1_{b_i}= \begin{cases} \delta,&i\in S,\\ 0,&i\notin S, \end{cases} $$
and
$$ q^2_{b_i}=\delta,\qquad q^2_{a_i}= \begin{cases} 0,&i\in S,\\ \delta,&i\notin S. \end{cases} $$
Each marker is fully available only to its own organization.

Consequently,
$$ \sigma_1(q^1)=B,\qquad \sigma_2(q^2)=W-B. $$
Both menus satisfy their paired inequalities, so both maps use successful equality. Their outputs are exactly the constructed incumbent vectors:
$$ C_1(q^1)=x^1,\qquad C_2(q^2)=x^2. $$
Lemma 2 proves stability.

We have therefore proved the equivalence
$$ \text{an exactly stable allocation exists} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}: \sum_{i\in S}w_i=B. $$

7. Complexity conclusion

The reduction is polynomial, uses exactly two organizations, and always produces maps satisfying all promises. Stable existence is therefore $\mathsf{NP}$-hard and, by Section 2, $\mathsf{NP}$-complete on the promised input class.

An algorithm that finds an exactly stable allocation or correctly reports nonexistence in time polynomial in $m,\tau,K,L$ would decide SUBSET SUM in polynomial time. Thus such an algorithm would imply $\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}$.
