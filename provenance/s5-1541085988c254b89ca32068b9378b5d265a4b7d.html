The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational capability types \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^2\) and positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), let \(x^U_t=\mu_t\) for \(t\in U\) and \(0\) otherwise, and let \(Q_k(x)=\max\{v_t^k:x_t>0\}\) with \(Q_k(0)=0\). Output every \(U\subseteq T\) such that \(Q_1(x^U)+Q_2(x^U)>Q_1(\mu-x^U)+Q_2(\mu-x^U)\) and no proper \(V\subsetneq U\) satisfies this, together with \(\mu(U)=\sum_{t\in U}\mu_t\) and a completeness certificate.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) distinct capability types \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^{m}\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(m=2\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. Assume \(\max_{t\in T}v_t^1+\max_{t\in T}v_t^2>0\).
A coalition selects whole types: for \(U\subseteq T\), define \(x_t^U=\mu_t\) if \(t\in U\), and \(x_t^U=0\) otherwise. For \(0\leq x\leq\mu\), let \(Q_k(x)=\max\{v_t^k:x_t>0\}\), with \(Q_k(0)=0\).
Call \(U\) winning when \(Q_1(x^U)+Q_2(x^U)>Q_1(\mu-x^U)+Q_2(\mu-x^U)\); equality is losing. It is minimal winning when no proper subset is winning.
The enumeration problem outputs every minimal winning \(U\), its exact mass \(\mu(U)=\sum_{t\in U}\mu_t\), and a completeness certificate, meaning a finite mathematical justification that no minimal winning coalition was omitted.

Conclusion

The enumeration problem, including exact masses and a completeness certificate, is solvable in total polynomial time for \(m=2\).

Below is a self-contained algorithm using at most \(\tau^2(\tau+1)^2\) candidates. Its running time is \(O(\tau^6\operatorname{poly}(L))\). This suffices to settle the question without needing the sharper enumeration bound.

Identifying each support type with one player gives exactly the winning rule in Section 1.5 of [Cao and Yang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.2060). Their Theorems 1 and 3 state the sharper bound and enumeration result mentioned in the question. The proof here explicitly accounts for rational arithmetic, masses, and completeness.

1. Winning depends only on the selected types

For \(U\subseteq T\), define
\[ q_k(U)= \begin{cases} \max_{t\in U}v_t^k,&U\ne\varnothing,\\ 0,&U=\varnothing, \end{cases} \qquad s(U)=q_1(U)+q_2(U), \qquad \Delta(U)=s(U)-s(T\setminus U). \]

Because every \(\mu_t\) is positive and coalitions select whole types, the support of \(x^U\) is exactly \(U\), and the support of \(\mu-x^U\) is exactly \(T\setminus U\). Consequently, \(U\) is winning precisely when \(\Delta(U)>0\). The masses do not enter this comparison.

Nonnegativity of the capabilities, including the convention for the empty coalition, implies that \(q_k(A)\le q_k(B)\) whenever \(A\subseteq B\). Since \(T\setminus B\subseteq T\setminus A\), we obtain
\[ \Delta(A)\le\Delta(B). \]

Thus winning is monotone under inclusion.

Minimality criterion. A coalition \(U\) is minimal winning if and only if
\[ \Delta(U)>0 \quad\text{and}\quad \Delta(U\setminus\{t\})\le0 \quad\text{for every }t\in U. \]

Necessity follows from the definition. Conversely, if a proper subset \(W\subsetneq U\) were winning, choose \(t\in U\setminus W\). Then \(W\subseteq U\setminus\{t\}\), so monotonicity would make that single deletion winning, a contradiction.

2. A polynomial family contains every minimal winning coalition

For each coordinate \(k\in\{1,2\}\), define the set of possible maximum values
\[ B_k=\{0\}\cup\{v_t^k:t\in T\}. \]

Hence \(|B_k|\le\tau+1\).

For \(b=(b_1,b_2)\in B_1\times B_2\), let
\[ D(b)=\{t\in T:v_t^1>b_1\text{ or }v_t^2>b_2\}. \]

For each ordered pair \(p,q\in T\), allowing \(p=q\), construct the candidate
\[ C(b,p,q)=D(b)\cup\{p,q\}. \]

There are at most \((\tau+1)^2\tau^2\) indexed candidates.

Candidate completeness lemma. Every minimal winning coalition equals one of these candidates.

Proof. Let \(U\) be minimal winning. The assumption on the global maxima implies \(\Delta(\varnothing)<0\), so \(U\ne\varnothing\).

Set \(b_k=q_k(T\setminus U)\). Each \(b_k\) belongs to \(B_k\), including when the complement is empty. Choose \(p,q\in U\) attaining the first and second maxima of \(U\), respectively:
\[ v_p^1=q_1(U), \qquad v_q^2=q_2(U). \]

Every type outside \(U\) has both coordinates bounded by \(b\). Therefore \(D(b)\subseteq U\), and consequently
\[ C:=C(b,p,q)\subseteq U. \]

The two witnesses belong to \(C\), so \(q_k(C)=q_k(U)\) for both coordinates. Hence \(s(C)=s(U)\).

Every type outside \(C\) lies outside \(D(b)\), so both its coordinates are bounded by \(b\). Thus
\[ q_k(T\setminus C)\le b_k. \]
This inequality also holds when \(T\setminus C\) is empty, because its maxima are zero and \(b_k\ge0\).

It follows that
\[ \Delta(C) \ge s(U)-(b_1+b_2) =\Delta(U) >0. \]

Thus \(C\) is a winning subset of \(U\). Minimality forces \(C=U\), proving the lemma.

This argument handles tied coordinates, zero capabilities, singleton coalitions, and empty complements.

3. Enumeration algorithm

The algorithm performs the following steps:

  • Construct \(B_1\) and \(B_2\).
  • For every \(b\in B_1\times B_2\) and every \(p,q\in T\), construct \(C(b,p,q)\).
  • Compute \(\Delta(C)\) and \(\Delta(C\setminus\{t\})\) for every \(t\in C\).
  • Retain \(C\) precisely when \(\Delta(C)>0\) and every single deletion has nonpositive difference.
  • Remove duplicate retained coalitions, and output each distinct coalition with its exact mass.

By the minimality criterion, every retained coalition is minimal winning. By the candidate completeness lemma, every minimal winning coalition is retained at least once. Deduplication therefore produces exactly the requested family.

4. Exact masses and bit complexity

Write each mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/d_t\), with positive integers \(a_t,d_t\) encoded in binary. Define
\[ D=\prod_{t\in T}d_t, \qquad c_t=a_t(D/d_t). \]

Then \(c_t\) is an integer, and for every coalition \(U\),
\[ \mu(U)=\frac{\sum_{t\in U}c_t}{D}. \]

The bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\), because the denominator encodings contribute to the total input length. Moreover,
\[ \sum_{t\in T}c_t=D, \]
so every coalition numerator is at most \(D\) and also has \(O(L)\) bits. Thus masses can be computed and output exactly in polynomial time. Fractions can be reduced by computing a greatest common divisor if desired.

For capabilities, comparing two encoded rationals uses integer cross multiplication. Each difference \(\Delta(U)\) involves at most four input rational coordinates, so its exact representation has \(O(L)\) bits. All comparisons, including equality, are therefore performed exactly in \(\operatorname{poly}(L)\) time.

A direct computation of \(\Delta(U)\) scans the support in \(O(\tau)\) rational operations. Testing all single deletions of one candidate requires \(O(\tau^2)\) such operations. Since there are \(O(\tau^4)\) indexed candidates, the total cost is
\[ O(\tau^6\operatorname{poly}(L)). \]

Deduplication by sorting coalition incidence vectors, mass computation, and explicit output also take polynomial time. The number of distinct output coalitions is bounded by the number of candidates, so output length creates no exponential obstruction.

5. An explicit completeness certificate

Output the candidate completeness lemma above together with a table containing one row for every indexed tuple \((b,p,q)\). Each row records:

  • the exact value of \(\Delta(C(b,p,q))\);
  • the exact value of every single-deletion difference;
  • a reference to the output coalition when the row satisfies the minimality criterion.

The certificate also records the exact mass of every distinct output coalition.

A verifier checks that:

  • The table contains exactly one row for every tuple in \(B_1\times B_2\times T\times T\).
  • Each candidate and all recorded differences are correct.
  • Every row satisfying the minimality criterion references its identical output coalition.
  • Every output coalition has such a reference, and the output contains no duplicates.
  • Every reported mass equals the exact sum of its members’ masses.

These checks establish soundness by the minimality criterion and completeness by the candidate completeness lemma. The table has \(O(\tau^4)\) rows and at most \(O(\tau)\) recorded rational differences per row, giving polynomial certificate length and verification time.

As a finite sanity check, candidate enumeration also matched exhaustive enumeration on all \(380\) admissible supports of size at most five drawn from \(\{0,1,2\}^2\). The proof above establishes correctness for arbitrary admissible inputs.
