Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions, already when \(q_{\max}=1\). The hardness also holds on a subclass where DA actually splits a resident type’s mass between hospitals.

The essential point is that the support may contain one distinct type for every agent of a discrete instance. Requiring a shared ranking within each type therefore does not prevent the discrete embedding.

1. The discrete theorem being used

Let \(D\) denote discrete finalizability with all hospital quotas equal to one. Its input consists of resident and hospital ranking prefixes and a designated tentative pair.

Tamaki’s Theorem 1 states that \(D\) is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete. The paper uses the same pending-proposal convention as this question: an unlisted resident’s proposal remains pending until listed proposals fill the hospital’s quota. Its completions are strict, complete rankings obtained by appending omitted agents. Thus the theorem’s truncation and completion hypotheses match those needed below. [Source, Section 2 and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.04381v2).

We use this established theorem; the transfer to masses is proved next.

2. An exact embedding into the mass problem

Take a discrete instance \(I_{\mathrm d}\) with resident set \(R\), hospital set \(H\), unit quotas, and designated tentative pair \((r_0,h_0)\).

Construct a mass instance \(E(I_{\mathrm d})\) by setting:

  • \(A=R\) and \(B=H\), with each agent represented by a distinct type;
  • \(\alpha_a=\beta_b=1\) for every type;
  • \(q_b=1\), so every hospital capacity is \(C_b=1\);
  • every submitted prefix and the designated pair equal to their discrete counterparts.

All masses and quotas have constant-length binary encodings.

Lemma: DA preserves the discrete outcome

For the submitted prefixes, and for every corresponding completion, mass DA produces exactly the discrete tentative pairs, with mass one on each such pair.

Proof. Process one free resident type’s entire mass at a time. We prove inductively that every resident type is either entirely free or has its entire unit mass tentative at one hospital.

The claim holds before any proposals. Suppose it holds immediately before a proposal. The proposing type sends mass one. At its destination, every previously tentative resident type also contributes mass one.

There are two cases.

  • There is listed incoming mass. Because capacity is one and every contribution has mass one, the hospital retains exactly the highest-ranked listed type. It rejects every other listed contribution and every pending contribution.
  • There is no listed incoming mass. Every incoming contribution is unlisted and remains pending in full.

Consequently, every retention and rejection concerns a whole unit of one type. No cutoff splits a contribution. These are precisely the discrete rules, including the possibility that several pending unit contributions exceed capacity.

Thus every proposal and rejection can be coupled to the corresponding discrete event. Both executions stop at the same tentative pairs. The argument applies equally to complete rankings, where no incoming type is unlisted. ∎

Since \((r_0,h_0)\) is tentative in \(I_{\mathrm d}\), its mass in the constructed prefix instance is \(x^I_{a_0b_0}=1\). For every completion \(J\), the lemma gives

\[ x^J_{a_0b_0}\in\{0,1\}. \]

Moreover, completions correspond bijectively: both models choose one strict ordering of the omitted opposite-side agents or types for each agent or type. Therefore,

\[ I_{\mathrm d}\in D \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E(I_{\mathrm d})\text{ is mass-finalizable}. \]

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from discrete finalizability to mass finalizability.

3. Why this gives the requested \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness

The reduction directly proves \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness under many-one reductions. To obtain the requested Turing statement, the answer must be negated.

Because \(D\) is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete, its complement is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. For any language \(K\in\mathrm{NP}\), choose a polynomial-time reduction \(f\) satisfying

\[ z\in K \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(z)\notin D. \]

An algorithm with an oracle for mass finalizability can decide \(K\) by constructing \(E(f(z))\), making one oracle query, and negating its answer. This is a polynomial-time Turing reduction.

Hence mass finalizability is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. In particular, a polynomial-time algorithm for the stated problem would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

4. Hardness even when a fractional split occurs

The preceding embedding requires no splits. The following extension shows that hardness persists in instances where splitting actually occurs.

First scale every mass in the embedding by \(\varepsilon=1/2\), leaving all quotas equal to one. Every hospital capacity becomes \(\varepsilon\). Dividing all proposal, retention, and rejection amounts by \(\varepsilon\) recovers the unit-mass execution, so the equivalence remains valid.

Now add one resident type \(u\) and two hospital types \(v,w\), with

\[ \alpha_u=1,\qquad \beta_v=\beta_w=\varepsilon,\qquad q_v=q_w=1. \]

Give \(u\) the submitted prefix \((v,w)\), and give both \(v\) and \(w\) the submitted prefix \((u)\). Leave every original submitted prefix unchanged.

Process \(u\) first. It proposes mass one to \(v\); \(v\) retains \(\varepsilon\) and rejects \(\varepsilon\). The rejected portion proposes to \(w\), which retains it. Therefore,

\[ x^I_{uv}=x^I_{uw}=\varepsilon. \]

These allocations remain fixed in every completion: \(u\) ranks \(v,w\) first, and both hospitals rank \(u\) first. Thus \(u\) never proposes to an original hospital, while every original resident proposal to \(v\) or \(w\) is rejected.

For any completion of the enlarged instance, delete \(v,w\) from original residents’ rankings and delete \(u\) from original hospitals’ rankings. The resulting rankings complete the original scaled instance. Proposals to \(v,w\) merely cause immediate rejections, so the DA execution restricted to original pairs is exactly that of this projected completion.

Conversely, every completion of the original scaled instance extends to the enlarged instance by appending the new types to the original rankings and completing the gadget rankings arbitrarily after their fixed prefixes.

Consequently, finalizability of the designated original pair is preserved in both directions. This extension adds three support types and retains \(q_{\max}=1\).

5. Running times and parameters

Write \(n=|A|=\tau-m\) for the basic embedding, and let \(L_{\mathrm d}\) be the discrete input length.

The basic reduction takes \(O(L_{\mathrm d}+\tau)\) bit operations, produces length \(L=O(L_{\mathrm d}+\tau)\), and preserves \(m\) and \(\tau\). The fractional-split extension has the same asymptotic construction time and adds two hospitals and three support types. Both constructions have \(q_{\max}=1\).

On the basic embedded instances, a fixed-completion DA run makes at most \(nm\) proposals. Each proposal can be rejected at most once. With rank tables and proposal pointers, this takes \(O(nm+L)\) operations on words of \(O(\log\tau)\) bits, or

\[ O\!\left(L+m(\tau-m)\log\tau\right) \]

bit operations. The added gadget changes this bound only by a constant-factor overhead.

These are construction and verification bounds for the hardness instances. They do not assert a polynomial-time verification algorithm for arbitrary rational-mass instances. Such an upper bound is unnecessary to settle the requested hardness alternative: hardness already holds with constant masses and \(q_{\max}=1\).
