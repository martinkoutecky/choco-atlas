The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of doctor types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), preferences, eligibility, and hospital and regional priority tiers, together with hospitals partitioned into regions, rational per-capita capacities \(q_h\), regional quotas \(\delta_r\), and regional weights \(w(h)\), can one compute a direct mechanism returning assignment masses \(x_{t,h}\) and unmatched masses \(x_{t,\emptyset}\)? Require \(\sum_{h\in H}x_{t,h}+x_{t,\emptyset}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t\in T}x_{t,h}\le q_h\), and \(\sum_{t\in T,\,h\in r}x_{t,h}\le\delta_r\). The induced lottery for a member of type \(t\) assigns hospital \(h\) with probability \(x_{t,h}/\mu_t\); require truthful preference reporting to be weakly preferred in stochastic dominance to any misreport by an infinitesimal member, and require mass-adapted versions of the paper's fairness and non-wastefulness conditions under regional weights.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) doctor types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(m=|H|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Hospitals are partitioned into nonempty regions \(R\). Hospital capacities \(q_h\) and regional upper quotas \(\delta_r\) are nonnegative binary rationals; weights \(w(h)\) are binary rationals. Each type has fixed eligibility and hospital and regional priority tiers, and a strict preference order \(\succ_t\) over eligible hospitals and \(\emptyset\). Eligible contracts are acceptable to both their hospital and region; no consistency between hospital and regional priorities is assumed. Strict priority comparisons \(\succ_h,\succ_r\) compare tiers, leaving equal tiers tied.
An assignment consists of nonnegative masses satisfying \(\sum_{h\in H}x_{t,h}+x_{t,\emptyset}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_t x_{t,h}\le q_h\), and \(\sum_{t,h\in r}x_{t,h}\le\delta_r\), with \(x_{t,h}=0\) whenever \(h\) is ineligible or \(\emptyset\succ_t h\).
For any mass array \(y\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), define \(A_\varepsilon(y,h)=w(h)\) if \(\sum_t y_{t,h}+\varepsilon\le q_h\), and \(A_\varepsilon(y,h)=-\infty\) otherwise. Let \(e_{t,b}\) denote the unit array at coordinate \((t,b)\), including \(b=\emptyset\).
Non-wastefulness forbids any eligible \(h\in r\), source \(b\), and \(0<\varepsilon\le x_{t,b}\) with \(h\succ_t b\) such that \(y=x-\varepsilon e_{t,b}\) and \(y+\varepsilon e_{t,h}\) is feasible, and either \(b=\emptyset\), \(b\notin r\), or \(b\in r\) and \(A_\varepsilon(y,h)>A_\varepsilon(y,b)\), or these two values are equal and \((t,h)\succ_r(t,b)\).
Fairness forbids disjoint positive portions of equal mass \(\varepsilon\) at \((t,b)\) and \((s,j)\), with an eligible \(h\succ_t b\) and \(h,j\in r\), such that, for \(y=x-\varepsilon e_{s,j}\), either \(h=j\) and both \((t,h)\succ_h(s,j)\) and \((t,h)\succ_r(s,j)\), or \(h\ne j\) and \(A_\varepsilon(y,h)>A_\varepsilon(y,j)\), or \(h\ne j\), these values are equal, and \((t,h)\succ_r(s,j)\). The portions may belong to the same type.
A direct mechanism must specify a lottery for every strict preference report by a member with fixed eligibility and priority tiers, including reports absent from the support. On supported reports it assigns probability \(x_{t,h}/\mu_t\). An infinitesimal member's report leaves the aggregate society unchanged; truthful reporting must weakly stochastic-dominate every misreport under that member's true preferences. Feasibility, individual rationality, fairness, and non-wastefulness must hold at every reported society.
Does such a direct mechanism exist on this entire domain, and, if it exists, is exact computation of its assignment masses and individual report lotteries polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,|R|,L\), or NP-hard?
For finite doctors with strict priorities, GDA-RO is strategy-proof and returns an individually rational, feasible, fair and non-wasteful assignment under regional weights ([Sun–Todo–Yokoo](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0058.pdf)).
Continuum matching has conditional stability-existence results for convex, continuous firm choices ([Che–Kim–Kojima](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.3982/ecta13547)); these concern a different formulation. No result settling the exact mechanism question above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total doctor mass to one, making capacities and quotas per capita; allowed nonnegative rational capacities and quotas and arbitrary rational weights, whose ordering alone matters.
  • Specified strict doctor preferences with an unmatched option, fixed eligibility, mutual institutional acceptability of eligible contracts, and individual rationality.
  • Interpreted priority tiers as weak institutional orders with strict comparisons only between distinct tiers; imposed no hospital–region priority consistency.
  • Restored the requirement that a doctor strictly prefer the proposed hospital in non-wastefulness. Although absent from the displayed discrete definition, it is required by its explanatory text and intended result.
  • Defined the mass versions through positive portions of equal mass, testing room for the entire transferred portion after the prescribed removal. Non-wastefulness removes the claimant's source portion; fairness removes only the envied portion when comparing hospital weights.
  • Allowed claimant and envied portions to have the same type while requiring them to be disjoint, representing distinct members of an atomless type population.
  • Specified individual report lotteries even for reports outside the support, holding eligibility, priority tiers and the aggregate society fixed under an infinitesimal deviation.
  • Included existence in the target because the anchor guarantees mechanism properties rather than asserting a complexity classification, and the continuous formulation does not independently guarantee existence.

Status and proved results

A direct mechanism exists on the entire stated domain. Its outputs can be chosen rational with polynomial bit length, and an exact finite construction is available.

The general complexity classification remains unresolved. The results below additionally establish:

  • polynomial-time verification of fairness and non-wastefulness;
  • polynomial-time exact construction when $m$ is fixed;
  • polynomial-time exact construction, with $m$ variable, when weights are constant within regions and regional priorities follow a common ordering of doctor tiers.

All arguments are self-contained. In particular, the polynomial bit-length bound is quantified.

Computing an individual lottery means evaluating it for one supplied report. Explicitly listing lotteries for every strict report could itself require factorial output size.

1. Eliminating the positive-portion quantifiers

For a feasible assignment $x$, define its hospital and regional loads by

$$ \ell_h=\sum_t x_{t,h}, \qquad \ell_r=\sum_{h\in r}\ell_h. $$

For two eligible contracts in the same region, define

$$ (t,h)\triangleright_r(s,j) $$

to mean

$$ w(h)>w(j), $$

or

$$ w(h)=w(j) \quad\text{and}\quad (t,h)\succ_r(s,j). $$

This auxiliary comparison incorporates weights and regional priority, but does not incorporate hospital priority.

Lemma 1: finite witness characterizations

For a feasible, individually rational assignment, non-wastefulness fails exactly when there exist an eligible hospital $h\in r$ and a source $b$ such that

$$ x_{t,b}>0, \qquad h\succ_t b, \qquad \ell_h<q_h, $$

and one of the following holds:

  • $b=\emptyset$ or $b\notin r$, and $\ell_r<\delta_r$;
  • $b\in r$, and $(t,h)\triangleright_r(t,b)$.

Fairness fails exactly when there exist a source $(t,b)$, an occupant $(s,j)$, and an eligible target $h\succ_t b$, with $h,j\in r$, such that

$$ x_{t,b}>0, \qquad x_{s,j}>0, $$

and either

$$ h=j, \qquad (t,h)\succ_h(s,h), \qquad (t,h)\succ_r(s,h), $$

or

$$ h\ne j, \qquad \ell_h<q_h, \qquad (t,h)\triangleright_r(s,j). $$

Proof

For non-wastefulness, strict preference implies $h\ne b$. Moving a positive portion from $b$ to $h$ requires hospital slack at $h$. If the source lies outside $r$, it also requires regional slack. If the source lies inside $r$, regional load is unchanged.

Whenever the proposed move is feasible,

$$ A_\varepsilon(x-\varepsilon e_{t,b},h)=w(h). $$

If $b\in r$, restoring the removed source portion restores its original feasible hospital load, so

$$ A_\varepsilon(x-\varepsilon e_{t,b},b)=w(b). $$

The comparison in the definition therefore becomes precisely $\triangleright_r$.

Conversely, the stated strict slack conditions permit a sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon\le x_{t,b}$. All constraints other than the target hospital and, when applicable, the target region either remain unchanged or become weaker. Individual rationality of the move follows from $h\succ_t b$ and positive, individually rational source mass.

For fairness with $h\ne j$, restoring the occupant portion gives

$$ A_\varepsilon(x-\varepsilon e_{s,j},j)=w(j). $$

Because the removal occurred at another hospital,

$$ A_\varepsilon(x-\varepsilon e_{s,j},h)=w(h) $$

is possible for positive $\varepsilon$ exactly when $\ell_h<q_h$. The prohibited comparison is consequently $\triangleright_r$.

For $h=j$, the definition directly requires the two strict priority comparisons displayed above.

Conversely, choose $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small to fit within both positive masses and, in the different-hospital case, within target-hospital slack. Choosing

$$ \varepsilon\le \frac12\min\{x_{t,b},x_{s,j}\} $$

also permits disjoint portions if the source and occupant coordinates coincide. Thus the finite conditions supply the required witness. ∎

There are polynomially many candidate witnesses: at most

$$ O\!\left(\tau^2(m+1)m^2\right). $$

Hence feasibility, individual rationality, fairness, and non-wastefulness of a supplied rational assignment can all be checked in polynomial time in its encoding length.

2. Report-independent cutoff menus

Write $a$ for a member’s fixed eligibility and priority attributes. A supported type $t$ consists of attributes $a_t$ and a strict preference order.

For each region, write $\rho_r(a,h)$ for the ordered regional tier label of an eligible contract; smaller labels denote higher priority. These are the actual ordered tier labels, so an absent report’s attributes can also be compared with a supported contract’s tier.

Introduce a lottery variable $u$ uniformly distributed on $(0,1)$. For $h\in r$, define the key

$$ K(a,h,u)=\bigl(-w(h),\rho_r(a,h),u\bigr), $$

ordered lexicographically, with smaller keys ranked higher.

In particular,

$$ (t,h)\triangleright_r(s,j) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad K(a_t,h,u)<K(a_s,j,v) $$

for every $u,v\in(0,1)$.

Hospital priorities do not enter this key. This is sufficient because same-hospital fairness in the question requires a strict regional comparison as well as a strict hospital comparison.

Choose cutoffs $c_h$ and $c_r$. For a positive quota, a cutoff is either $+\infty$ or a pair

$$ c=\bigl((-w,\rho),z\bigr), \qquad z\in[0,1]. $$

For a zero quota, its cutoff is $-\infty$.

Define the menu

$$ \mathcal M_a(u) = \{\emptyset\} \cup \left\{ h: h\text{ is eligible for }a,\ K(a,h,u)<c_h,\ K(a,h,u)<c_{r(h)} \right\}. $$

For any strict report, select its most-preferred member of this menu. For a supported type, let $p_{t,b}$ denote the resulting probability and put

$$ x_{t,b}=\mu_t p_{t,b}. $$

A cutoff vector is a certificate if these masses are feasible and

$$ c_h\ne+\infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ell_h=q_h, $$

and

$$ c_r\ne+\infty \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ell_r=\delta_r. $$

Thus every restrictive cutoff certifies fullness. In particular,

$$ \ell_h<q_h\Longrightarrow c_h=+\infty, \qquad \ell_r<\delta_r\Longrightarrow c_r=+\infty. $$

Explicit probability formulas

For any attributes $a$ and hospital $h$, availability has the form

$$ h\in\mathcal M_a(u) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u<z_{a,h} $$

for a threshold $z_{a,h}\in[0,1]$.

To obtain this threshold from one cutoff:

  • a strictly better weight-tier pair gives threshold $1$;
  • a strictly worse pair gives threshold $0$;
  • an equal pair gives the cutoff coordinate;
  • $+\infty$ gives $1$, and $-\infty$ gives $0$.

The hospital’s final threshold is the minimum of its hospital and regional thresholds.

For a supported type, let

$$ B_t=\{h:h\text{ is eligible and }h\succ_t\emptyset\}, $$

and define

$$ v_{t,h} = \max\left( \{z_{a_t,g}:g\in B_t,\ g\succ_t h\}\cup\{0\} \right). $$

Then

$$ p_{t,h} = \max\{0,z_{a_t,h}-v_{t,h}\}, \qquad h\in B_t, $$

and

$$ p_{t,\emptyset} = 1-\max\left( \{z_{a_t,h}:h\in B_t\}\cup\{0\} \right). $$

All other hospital probabilities are zero. Indeed, selecting $h$ means that $u$ is below its threshold and above every threshold of an acceptable hospital preferred to it.

3. Every certificate supplies the required mechanism properties

Strategy-proofness, including absent reports

Fix the aggregate society, a member’s attributes $a$, its true preference order $P$, and another report $P'$.

For every $u$, both reports face the same menu $\mathcal M_a(u)$. Truthful reporting selects its $P$-best element. Consequently, the truthful outcome is weakly preferred under $P$ to the outcome selected by $P'$ at that same $u$.

For every initial segment of $P$, membership of the misreported outcome in that segment implies membership of the truthful outcome. Taking probabilities proves weak stochastic dominance.

This argument applies whether or not either report occurs in the support. It uses exactly the stated infinitesimal interpretation: the member’s report leaves the aggregate society and its chosen certificate unchanged.

Individual rationality follows because $\emptyset$ is always available. Feasibility is part of the certificate definition.

Non-wastefulness

Suppose a witness from Lemma 1 exists. Its target hospital has slack, hence $c_h=+\infty$.

If the source lies outside the target region, that region also has slack, hence $c_r=+\infty$. The preferred target is therefore available whenever type $t$ receives $b$, contradicting menu selection.

If $b\in r$, the witness requires

$$ (t,h)\triangleright_r(t,b). $$

Thus, at every $u$,

$$ K(a_t,h,u)<K(a_t,b,u). $$

Whenever the menu selects $b$, its key is below $c_r$, so the target’s key is also below $c_r$. Together with $c_h=+\infty$, this makes the preferred target available. Again menu selection gives a contradiction.

Fairness

Suppose a witness from Lemma 1 exists. Positive source and occupant mass supply realizations $u,v$ at which type $t$ receives $b$ and type $s$ receives $j$.

If $h\ne j$, target-hospital slack gives $c_h=+\infty$, while

$$ (t,h)\triangleright_r(s,j) $$

gives

$$ K(a_t,h,u)<K(a_s,j,v)<c_r. $$

The target is available to the source member, contradicting $h\succ_t b$.

If $h=j$, strict regional priority gives

$$ K(a_t,h,u)<K(a_s,h,v). $$

The occupant’s key lies below both $c_h$ and $c_r$, so the source member’s target key does too. This is the same contradiction.

No argument requires distinct source and occupant types. Thus every certificate satisfies precisely the fairness and non-wastefulness conditions in the question.

4. Certificate existence on the full domain

The construction uses finite replicas only to prove existence; it does not claim an efficient algorithm in binary input length.

4.1 A finite greedy rejection lemma

Give hospitals integer capacities $Q_h$, regions integer capacities $D_r$, and contracts a strict order within each region.

For a proposal set $S_r$, process its contracts in that order. Accept a contract whenever both its hospital and regional quotas permit it.

Fix a proposed contract $e$ at hospital $h\in r$. Let $n_g(e,S_r)$ be the number of proposals at hospital $g$ preceding $e$. The number accepted before reaching $e$ is

$$ \min\left\{ D_r,\ \sum_{g\in r}\min\{Q_g,n_g(e,S_r)\} \right\}. $$

Without the regional quota, the rule accepts the first $Q_g$ proposals at each hospital. The regional quota truncates that sequence after its first $D_r$ acceptances, proving the formula.

Therefore $e$ is rejected exactly when

$$ n_h(e,S_r)\ge Q_h $$

or

$$ \sum_{g\in r}\min\{Q_g,n_g(e,S_r)\}\ge D_r. $$

Both conditions persist when proposals are added. Rejection is permanent.

Moreover, in the final greedy choice, every rejected contract is blocked by either:

  • its hospital being full of preceding contracts; or
  • its region being full of preceding contracts.

If the regional prefix has filled its quota, the second conclusion holds. Otherwise the rejection formula forces the first.

4.2 Finite proposals and weak cutoff representation

Initially no contracts have been proposed. An unheld doctor proposes its best unproposed acceptable contract. Regions recompute their greedy choices from all proposals received so far.

Permanent rejection implies that a doctor has at most one held contract: it proposes again only after its previous proposal is rejected, and earlier rejected proposals cannot return.

The process terminates because each contract is proposed at most once. Every acceptable contract preferred to a doctor’s final assignment was proposed and rejected.

Order contracts first by their keys $K$, then break remaining ties by fixed doctor and hospital identifiers. For each full hospital or region with positive quota, set its cutoff to the raw key of its worst assigned contract. Use $+\infty$ for a quota with slack and $-\infty$ for a zero quota.

The rejection lemma gives:

  • every assigned contract has raw key weakly below both relevant cutoffs;
  • every preferred rejected contract has raw key weakly above at least one relevant cutoff.

Tie-breaking does not invalidate these statements: preceding contracts have raw keys weakly smaller even when their raw keys coincide.

Thus the finite assignment agrees with strict-cutoff menu selection except possibly at lottery coordinates equal to cutoff coordinates.

4.3 Rational replicas and a limiting certificate

Choose a common denominator $D$ for all masses and quotas. For

$$ N=kD, \qquad k=1,2,\ldots, $$

create

$$ n_t=N\mu_t $$

doctors of each supported type. Give hospitals capacities $Nq_h$ and regions quotas $N\delta_r$.

Assign the doctors of type $t$ the values

$$ u_{t,i}=\frac{i-\frac12}{n_t}, \qquad i=1,\ldots,n_t. $$

Run the finite proposal process.

Put

$$ d=m+|R|. $$

Each cutoff is either an infinity or has a weight-tier pair drawn from the fixed finite collection of supported eligible contracts and a coordinate in $[0,1]$. Extract a subsequence along which:

  • every cutoff’s status and finite weight-tier pair are fixed;
  • all finite coordinates converge.

The second extraction is possible by compactness of a finite-dimensional cube. Let $c$ be the limiting cutoff vector.

Once the statuses and pairs are fixed, every availability threshold is a minimum of constants and cutoff coordinates. The probability formulas in Section 2 are therefore continuous in those coordinates.

For each supported type, at most $d$ grid doctors have a lottery coordinate equal to a cutoff coordinate. Away from these doctors, the finite assignment agrees with its strict menu.

A midpoint grid of $n_t$ points estimates the length of any interval with error at most $2/n_t$. If $x^{(N)}_{t,b}$ denotes the finite assigned count divided by $N$, and $p^{(N)}_{t,b}$ its strict-menu probability, then

$$ \left| x^{(N)}_{t,b}-\mu_t p^{(N)}_{t,b} \right| \le \frac{d+2}{N}. $$

Here each exceptional doctor has mass $1/N$, and

$$ \mu_t\frac{2}{n_t}=\frac2N. $$

Consequently,

$$ x^{(N)}_{t,b}\longrightarrow \mu_t p_{t,b}. $$

Feasibility passes to the limit. Every restrictive cutoff had its quota exactly full throughout the chosen subsequence, so its limiting quota is also full. Zero quotas retain cutoff $-\infty$.

Thus $c$ is a certificate. Combined with Section 3, this proves mechanism existence on the entire domain.

5. Exact computation and explicit bit-length bounds

5.1 A finite collection of rational linear programs

For every positive-quota hospital and region, enumerate either $+\infty$ or a finite cutoff pair drawn from supported eligible contracts in that container.

For each such choice, enumerate a weak ordering of the finite cutoff coordinates. Impose coordinate bounds

$$ 0\le z_i\le1. $$

Within one enumerated case, pair comparisons and the coordinate ordering determine every minimum, maximum, and positive-part branch in Section 2. Every probability is consequently a constant or a difference of two members of

$$ \{0,1,z_1,\ldots,z_f\}, $$

where $f\le d$ is the number of finite cutoffs.

Feasibility and restrictive-cutoff fullness become rational linear constraints. There are at most $6d$ inequalities after converting equalities to pairs of inequalities:

  • $d$ quota inequalities;
  • at most $2d$ fullness inequalities;
  • $2f$ coordinate bounds;
  • at most $f-1$ ordering inequalities.

Equal coordinates cause no difficulty: alternative branches then have equal values.

The limiting certificate belongs to some enumerated case. Its linear program is therefore feasible. Its feasible set is bounded by the coordinate bounds.

Use the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem: feasibility and optimization are solvable in time polynomial in the number of variables, number of constraints, and coefficient encoding length. A rational vertex can be selected deterministically by sequentially maximizing the coordinates and fixing their optimal values. The resulting unique point is a vertex.

Selecting the first feasible case and this vertex defines a deterministic certificate choice at every reported society.

5.2 Quantitative denominator and determinant bounds

Take $D$ to be the product of the denominators appearing in the masses and quotas. Since their encodings are included in $L$,

$$ D\le 2^L. $$

An affine probability has coefficients in $\{-1,0,1\}$. A hospital or regional load is a sum of at most $\tau m$ such expressions weighted by masses.

For any one coefficient, its absolute value is at most

$$ m\sum_t\mu_t=m. $$

The same bound applies to the absolute constant term. Input quotas have absolute value at most $2^L$.

After clearing denominators using $D$, all matrix entries and right-hand sides therefore have absolute value at most $2^B$, where it is sufficient to take

$$ B=2L+\left\lceil\log_2(m+2)\right\rceil+3. $$

Suppose $f>0$. At a vertex, the active constraint normals span $\mathbb R^f$. Otherwise, a nonzero direction orthogonal to all active normals would permit sufficiently small perturbations in both directions, contradicting extremality.

Choose $f$ independent active constraints. The vertex solves

$$ Az=b, $$

where $A$ and $b$ are integer-valued and their entries have absolute value at most $2^B$.

Hadamard’s inequality bounds both $\det A$ and every Cramer numerator by

$$ f^{f/2}2^{fB}. $$

Hence every coordinate has numerator and denominator bit length at most

$$ 2+fB+\left\lceil\frac f2\log_2 f\right\rceil. $$

All coordinates can be represented over the common denominator $|\det A|$. Every probability is a constant or a coordinate difference, so its denominator divides $|\det A|$. Every assignment mass can be represented over

$$ D|\det A|. $$

Because assignment masses lie in $[0,1]$, their numerators obey the same bound as their denominators. Their bit lengths are polynomial in $m$ and $L$.

If $f=0$, all probabilities are constants and the conclusion follows directly from the input masses.

This proves the polynomial bit-length claim without leaving denominator clearing implicit.

5.3 Computational consequences

A supplied certificate can be checked in polynomial time: compute its thresholds and probabilities, then check quotas and fullness exactly.

The construction is thus a total search problem on valid society inputs with polynomial-size rational witnesses and polynomial-time verification.

There are at most

$$ (\tau m+1)^d d! $$

enumerated cases. Since regions partition $m$ hospitals into nonempty sets,

$$ d=m+|R|\le2m. $$

Therefore, for every fixed $m$, this construction runs in polynomial time in the remaining input parameters. It is not a polynomial-time bound when $m$ varies.

Once a certificate is available, an individual report requires only:

  • comparison of its contracts with the cutoffs;
  • computation of availability thresholds;
  • evaluation of the probability formulas in its reported preference order.

This is polynomial-time exact computation in the society and query encoding lengths, including for reports absent from the support.

6. A polynomial-time mechanism for a variable-size subclass

This section gives an additional tractable case that permits strict regional priorities and arbitrary hospital priorities.

Assume:

  • Constant weights within regions. For each $r$, there is a weight $W_r$ such that

$$ w(h)=W_r \qquad\text{for every }h\in r. $$

  • Common doctor tiers. Each fixed attribute profile $a$ has a tier $\gamma(a)$, with smaller values denoting higher priority. For all eligible contracts $h,j\in r$,

$$ (a,h)\succ_r(a',j) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \gamma(a)<\gamma(a'). $$
Equal doctor tiers give tied regional priorities.

Eligibility and hospital priorities remain unrestricted. Strict preferences also remain unrestricted.

6.1 The algorithm

Process the supported doctor tiers in increasing order.

During one tier, use a local time interval $[0,1]$. Each supported type $t$ in that tier consumes mass at rate $\mu_t$. At each time, it chooses its most-preferred option from:

  • eligible hospitals with positive residual hospital and regional capacities;
  • $\emptyset$, which has unlimited capacity.

Let $\bar q_h$ and $\bar\delta_r$ denote residual capacities. If the currently selected hospitals induce rates

$$ \lambda_h = \sum_{\substack{t\text{ in the current tier}\\t\text{ currently chooses }h}} \mu_t, \qquad \lambda_r=\sum_{h\in r}\lambda_h, $$

and the current local time is $v$, advance by

$$ \Delta = \min\left( \{1-v\} \cup \left\{\frac{\bar q_h}{\lambda_h}:\lambda_h>0\right\} \cup \left\{\frac{\bar\delta_r}{\lambda_r}:\lambda_r>0\right\} \right). $$

Assign $\mu_t\Delta$ to each type’s current option and update residual capacities.

An exhausted hospital closes permanently. An exhausted region permanently closes all its hospitals. At local time $1$, proceed to the next tier.

Every stage except a tier endpoint exhausts at least one previously unexhausted hospital or region. If there are $k$ supported tiers, the total number of stages is at most

$$ m+|R|+k\le m+|R|+\tau. $$

6.2 Individual report lotteries and strategy-proofness

For a member in a supported tier, draw $u$ uniformly from that tier’s local interval $(0,1)$ and give it the most-preferred eligible open hospital or $\emptyset$ at time $u$, according to its report.

This agrees with the supported assignment masses: consuming at rate $\mu_t$ over an interval of length $\Delta$ assigns mass $\mu_t\Delta$.

An absent preference report with the same fixed attributes uses the same tier timeline. If an attribute profile has a tier absent from the support, use the available hospitals after all higher supported tiers have finished and before lower supported tiers begin; this menu is constant because that absent tier consumes no aggregate mass.

The aggregate timeline is unchanged by an infinitesimal member’s report. At every $u$, truthful reporting selects its most-preferred member of the same report-independent menu. The pointwise argument from Section 3 proves weak stochastic dominance over every misreport.

6.3 Feasibility and individual rationality

The event rule stops consumption when a quota is reached, so quotas are never exceeded. Each supported type consumes at rate $\mu_t$ for one unit of local time, including consumption of $\emptyset$, hence

$$ \sum_{h\in H}x_{t,h}+x_{t,\emptyset}=\mu_t. $$

Eligibility is imposed in each menu. Because $\emptyset$ is available, no type consumes an unacceptable hospital.

6.4 Non-wastefulness

Consider the characterization in Lemma 1.

If the source lies outside the target region, a prohibited move requires final slack at both the target hospital and target region. These resources never exhausted, so the target was open throughout type $t$’s tier. A type could not consume a less-preferred source while that eligible target was open.

If the source lies inside the target region, the two hospital weights are equal. The two contracts also have the same regional priority because they belong to the same type. Thus

$$ (t,h)\triangleright_r(t,b) $$

is false, and no prohibited within-region move exists.

6.5 Fairness

First suppose $h\ne j$. Equal regional weights mean that a fairness witness must have

$$ \gamma(a_t)<\gamma(a_s). $$

The source tier was therefore processed before the occupant tier.

Positive mass at $(s,j)$ implies that its region had not exhausted during the earlier source tier. Target-hospital slack implies that the target hospital never exhausted. Thus $h$ was open throughout the source tier, contradicting positive consumption of $b$ with $h\succ_t b$.

Now suppose $h=j$. A witness again requires

$$ \gamma(a_t)<\gamma(a_s). $$

Positive later-tier consumption at $h$ implies that neither $h$ nor its region exhausted during the earlier source tier. Hence $h$ was open throughout that tier, giving the same contradiction.

The proof excludes these witnesses using regional priority alone, so arbitrary hospital priorities cause no problem.

6.6 Exact polynomial-time arithmetic

Use a common input denominator $D\le2^L$. Every positive consumption rate has the form

$$ \lambda=\frac{M}{D}, \qquad 1\le M\le D, $$

because the total society mass is $1$.

Suppose current quantities have a common denominator $E$ divisible by $D$. A quota event has duration

$$ \frac{a/E}{M/D}=\frac{aD}{EM}, $$

and a tier endpoint has duration with denominator dividing $E$.

After the updates, a valid common denominator is

$$ E'=EDM, $$

taking $M=1$ for a tier endpoint. Consequently, after $s$ stages,

$$ \log_2 E \le \log_2 D+2s\log_2 D = O\!\left((m+|R|+\tau)L\right). $$

Residual capacities are bounded by their input values, assignment masses by $1$, and local times by $1$. Their numerator lengths are therefore polynomial as well.

Each stage needs polynomially many comparisons and rational operations, and there are polynomially many stages. Individual report lotteries are obtained by summing the lengths of the intervals on which each reported option is best.

Thus the entire mechanism is exactly computable in polynomial time on this subclass, with no bound on $m$.
