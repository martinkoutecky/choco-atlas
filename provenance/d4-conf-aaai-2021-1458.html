The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(T\), two valuation-stream types \(A,B\) with masses \(\mu_A=\mu_B=1/2\), normalized streams \(v_{\theta t}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_t v_{\theta t}=1\), and only prefix revelation, determine the best online policy choosing irrevocable \(x_{At},x_{Bt}\ge0\) with \(x_{At}+x_{Bt}\le1\), subject to \(u_\theta=\sum_t v_{\theta t}x_{\theta t}\ge1/2\), maximizing the worst-case ratio \(\frac{u_A+u_B}{\sum_t\max\{v_{At},v_{Bt}\}}\); in particular, determine whether guarded \(p=2.7\) achieves \(0.916\) and whether any fair-share policy can exceed \(0.933\).

| $3$ | $161/512$ | $227/512$ | $0$ | $0$ |

All entries are nonnegative dyadic rationals. Normalization follows from
$$ 214+137+161=214+298=512, \qquad 285+227=512. $$

The following inequalities allow fractions to remain unallocated: whenever $A$ receives fraction $w$, feasibility implies that $B$ receives at most $1-w$.

$$ 214x+137y+161z\ge256. $$

$$ W_{\mathrm I} \le 1-\frac{71}{512}x +\frac{137}{512}y -\frac{66}{512}z. $$

2. An exact criterion for fair continuation

Consider a revealed prefix with at least one item remaining. Write
$$ U_\theta=\text{utility already allocated to type }\theta, $$
$$ R_\theta=1-\sum_{\text{revealed }t}v_{\theta t}, \qquad d_\theta=\max\left\{0,\frac12-U_\theta\right\}. $$
Thus $R_\theta$ is the total value still to arrive, and $d_\theta$ is the remaining utility deficit.

Proposition. There exists an online continuation that satisfies fairness for every admissible suffix if and only if:

  • $R_\theta=0$ implies $d_\theta=0$;
  • defining $q_\theta=d_\theta/R_\theta$ when $R_\theta>0$, and $q_\theta=0$ otherwise,

$$ q_A+q_B\le1. $$

Necessity. If $R_\theta=0$ and $d_\theta>0$, no future allocation can supply the missing utility.

Otherwise, an admissible suffix places all residual values $(R_A,R_B)$ in the next item and gives every subsequent item zero value. The residual values are binary rationals because the revealed prefix is rational. On this item, each type with positive residual value must receive fraction at least $d_\theta/R_\theta$. Item feasibility therefore requires $q_A+q_B\le1$.

Sufficiency. Allocate fraction $q_\theta$ of every remaining item to type $\theta$. This uses only information available at the prefix and is feasible because the fractions sum to at most $1$. The additional utility is exactly $q_\theta R_\theta=d_\theta$ when $R_\theta>0$. Types with zero residual value already have their fair share. Thus every type finishes with utility at least $1/2$. $\square$

At the terminal prefix, the condition is simply $d_A=d_B=0$.

Consequently, every fair-share policy must satisfy this criterion after each nonterminal reachable prefix. Checking it takes $O(\tau)$ exact real operations. If the state quantities are rational with total encoding length $B$, elementary rational arithmetic gives an $O(\tau B^2)$ bit-time bound.

3. Direct fairness proof for the guarded policy

The following argument applies to every fixed $p>0$, including $p=27/10$.

Before a trigger, an item with values $(a,b)$ is allocated in fractions
$$ q_A=\frac{a^p}{a^p+b^p}, \qquad q_B=\frac{b^p}{a^p+b^p}. $$
For $(a,b)=(0,0)$, use equal division as stipulated.

For a processed prefix, possibly ending partway through an item, let $H_\theta$ be the total value of all processed item fractions to type $\theta$. Let $U_\theta$ be its allocated utility and set
$$ \ell_\theta=H_\theta-U_\theta. $$
Since every processed fraction is completely allocated, $\ell_\theta$ is the value to $\theta$ of the fractions allocated to the other type. Normalization gives
$$ U_\theta+(1-H_\theta)=1-\ell_\theta. $$
Thus the trigger condition is precisely $\ell_\theta=1/2$.

A pointwise inequality

For all $a,b\ge0$,
$$ bq_B \ge \left(1+\frac2p\right)aq_B-\frac ap. $$
When either value is zero, the inequality follows directly from the allocation rule.

For $a,b>0$, put $r=b/a$. Multiplying the claimed inequality by $p(1+r^p)/a$ reduces it to
$$ h(r):=pr^{p+1}-(p+1)r^p+1\ge0. $$
Its derivative is
$$ h'(r)=p(p+1)r^{p-1}(r-1). $$
Therefore $h$ decreases on $(0,1)$ and increases on $(1,\infty)$. Since $h(1)=0$, the inequality holds.

Utility at a trigger

Suppose $A$ triggers first. Apply the pointwise inequality to every processed item fraction, multiplying by the processed fraction where necessary, and sum. Before the trigger,
$$ U_B\ge \left(1+\frac2p\right)\ell_A-\frac{H_A}{p}. $$
At the trigger, $\ell_A=1/2$, giving the stronger bound
$$ \boxed{ U_B\ge\frac12+\frac{1-H_A}{p}. } $$
In particular, $B$ already has its fair share.

Moreover,
$$ U_A+(1-H_A)=\frac12. $$
Giving $A$ the rest of the current item and every subsequent item therefore makes its final utility exactly $1/2$. The case where $B$ triggers first follows by exchanging the type labels.

If neither type triggers, neither loss can pass $1/2$: during each item the losses vary continuously, and crossing that threshold would cause a trigger. At the end $H_\theta=1$, so
$$ u_\theta=1-\ell_\theta\ge\frac12. $$

Finally, simultaneous triggers cause no problem. At an $A$ trigger, the displayed margin implies $U_B\ge1/2+(1-H_A)/p$. A simultaneous $B$ trigger requires $U_B+(1-H_B)=1/2$. Nonnegativity forces
$$ 1-H_A=1-H_B=0, \qquad U_B=\frac12. $$
Both types then already have their fair shares and all remaining values are zero. The stipulated tie rule is harmless.

This proves fairness without importing the paper’s fairness lemma.

Exact real arithmetic

Maintain $\ell_A,\ell_B$ until a trigger. For the current item, compute the proportional fractions and the candidate processed fractions
$$ f_\theta= \frac{1/2-\ell_\theta}{v_{\theta t}(1-q_\theta)} $$
when the denominator is positive; otherwise set $f_\theta=+\infty$. Process proportionally until the earliest candidate trigger, or until the item ends.

For $p=27/10$, each power can be computed with a fixed number of multiplications and a nonnegative tenth-root extraction. In a model where these operations and exact comparisons have unit cost, the policy uses $O(m\tau)$ operations and $O(\tau)$ working real registers, with $\tau=2$. Reading the binary input additionally requires $O(L)$ decoding work.

This is a real-arithmetic bound, not a polynomial bit-time claim.

Exact symbolic bit computation

An exact implementation can return symbolic algebraic representations of its real allocations.

For a positive-valued item $(a_i,b_i)$, its fraction $q_i$ allocated to $A$ is the unique solution in $(0,1)$ of
$$ b_i^{27}q_i^{10}=a_i^{27}(1-q_i)^{10}. $$
Uniqueness follows because $q_i/(1-q_i)$ is strictly increasing there. Items with a zero value have rational proportional fractions.

Before the first trigger,
$$ \ell_A=\sum_{i<t}a_i(1-q_i), \qquad \ell_B=\sum_{i<t}b_iq_i. $$
Testing whether a candidate trigger occurs within the current item uses linear polynomials in the $q_i$. Comparing the two candidate trigger fractions, after multiplying by positive denominators, uses polynomials of degree at most $2$.

Each exact comparison can therefore be decided by an existential real formula containing at most $m$ variables and $O(m)$ integer polynomials of degree at most $10$. Clearing rational denominators gives coefficient bit lengths $O(L+\log(m+1))$.

A standard single-block quantifier-elimination theorem uses $s^{k+1}d^{O(k)}$ integer arithmetic operations for $k$ variables, $s$ polynomials, and degree bound $d$, with intermediate integer bit lengths at most $Hd^{O(k)}$ when input coefficient lengths are at most $H$. [Theorem 2.27 in Basu’s survey](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011_final-sep4-2014.pdf).

Applying that theorem with $k\le m$, $s=O(m)$, and $d=10$, and using elementary integer arithmetic, gives the conservative total bit-time bound
$$ (L+\log(m+1))^2(m+1)^{O(m)} $$
for $\tau=2$. The allocation expressions themselves use the specified algebraic roots and rational arithmetic. This establishes exact implementability, while leaving polynomial bit-time implementation unproved.
