The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given advertiser types \(t=(q_t,v_t,c_t)\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), \(K\) ordered slots per page, rational position factors \(\lambda_s\), and an inventory ratio \(\rho\in(0,1]\), consider a continuum of pages of total mass \(\rho/K\), each assigned a full integral slate. For each ordered slate \(\sigma\in T^K\), choose its page mass \(y_\sigma\geq 0\), subject to \(\sum_\sigma y_\sigma=\rho/K\) and \(\sum_\sigma n_t(\sigma)y_\sigma\leq\mu_t\) for every type \(t\), where \(n_t(\sigma)\) counts that type in the slate. Maximize \(\sum_\sigma y_\sigma\sum_{s=1}^K q_{t_s}v_{t_s}\Lambda_s\prod_{r<s}c_{t_r}\), with \(\Lambda_s=\prod_{j<s}\lambda_j\), and find an optimal rational slate-mass distribution.

Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. All type parameters are rational, with \(q_t,c_t\in[0,1]\) and \(v_t\geq0\).
There are \(m=K\geq1\) ordered slots per page, rational position factors \(\lambda_s\in[0,1]\) with \(\lambda_K=0\), and a rational inventory ratio \(\rho\in(0,1]\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Result

Exact welfare maximization is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\), including finding an optimal rational slate-mass distribution. The associated decision problem with a binary rational welfare threshold is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-complete}\).

Hardness holds even under the restrictions

\(\displaystyle \rho=1,\qquad \tau=2m,\qquad \mu_t=\frac{1}{2m},\qquad \lambda_s=1\ \text{for }s<m,\qquad q_tv_t=1-c_t.\)

We can additionally require that \(m\) is a power of two, so all masses are dyadic. Here \(\tau\) counts distinct types in the supplied support.

1. Source problem and construction

We reduce from monotone exact-two-in-four satisfiability: every clause contains four positive variable occurrences, and every clause must have exactly two true occurrences. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-complete}\), as established in [Lemma 2 of *On the complexity of the balanced vertex ordering problem*](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/383/pdf).

Let the source formula have variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_{n_0}\) and clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_h\). Add unconstrained variables until the number \(n\) of variables is a power of two. This preserves satisfiability and increases the variable count by less than a factor of two.

Let \(a_{ji}\) be the number of occurrences of \(x_i\) in clause \(C_j\). Thus

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ji}=4\quad\text{for every }j.\)

Choose pairwise distinct primes

\(\displaystyle p_1,\ldots,p_n,r_1,\ldots,r_h.\)

For each variable \(x_i\), create two types \(t_{i,0}\) and \(t_{i,1}\), with

\(\displaystyle c_{i,0}=\frac1{p_i},\qquad c_{i,1}=\frac1{p_i\prod_{j=1}^{h}r_j^{a_{ji}}}.\)

Set

\(\displaystyle q_{i,b}=\frac{b+1}{2},\qquad v_{i,b}=\frac{1-c_{i,b}}{q_{i,b}}\quad\text{for }b\in\{0,1\}.\)

Consequently, \(q_{i,b}v_{i,b}=1-c_{i,b}\). All parameters satisfy the required ranges; indeed, \(0<c_{i,b}<1\) and \(0<v_{i,b}<2\).

These are \(2n\) distinct types. Types belonging to different variables have different continuation probabilities because their denominators contain different private primes \(p_i\). The two types belonging to one variable have different qualities, even when that variable is unconstrained.

Finally, set

\(\displaystyle m=K=n,\qquad \rho=1,\qquad \mu_{i,b}=\frac1{2n},\)

and set \(\lambda_s=1\) for \(s<n\), with \(\lambda_n=0\). Therefore \(\Lambda_s=1\) for every slot \(s\leq n\).

Define the integer and rational threshold

\(\displaystyle D=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_i\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{h}r_j^2\right),\qquad B=\frac{1-D^{-1}}{n}.\)

We will prove that the optimum welfare equals \(B\) exactly when the source formula is satisfiable.

2. A universal welfare bound for the constructed instance

For a slate \(\sigma=(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\), write

\(\displaystyle P_\sigma=\prod_{s=1}^{n}c_{t_s}.\)

Its welfare telescopes:

\(\displaystyle F_\sigma=\sum_{s=1}^{n}(1-c_{t_s})\prod_{r<s}c_{t_r}=1-P_\sigma.\)

Indeed, each summand is the difference between the prefix products ending immediately before and immediately after slot \(s\).

Now let \(y\) be any feasible distribution. Since every slate contains exactly \(n\) entries,

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\sum_\sigma n_t(\sigma)y_\sigma=n\sum_\sigma y_\sigma=1.\)

The capacities also sum to one. Because every capacity constraint is an upper bound, all of them must therefore be tight:

\(\displaystyle \sum_\sigma n_t(\sigma)y_\sigma=\mu_t=\frac1{2n}\quad\text{for every }t.\)

Normalize the slate masses to probabilities by setting \(\pi_\sigma=ny_\sigma\). Then \(\sum_\sigma\pi_\sigma=1\), and

\(\displaystyle \sum_\sigma\pi_\sigma n_t(\sigma)=\frac12\quad\text{for every }t.\)

The product of all continuation probabilities is

\(\displaystyle \prod_{i=1}^{n}c_{i,0}c_{i,1}=\left(\prod_i p_i^{-2}\right)\left(\prod_j r_j^{-4}\right)=D^{-2}.\)

It follows that

\(\displaystyle \sum_\sigma\pi_\sigma\log P_\sigma=\sum_t\left(\sum_\sigma\pi_\sigma n_t(\sigma)\right)\log c_t=\frac12\sum_t\log c_t=-\log D.\)

Apply Jensen’s inequality to the strictly convex exponential function:

\(\displaystyle \sum_\sigma\pi_\sigma P_\sigma\geq \exp\!\left(\sum_\sigma\pi_\sigma\log P_\sigma\right)=D^{-1}.\)

All products are positive, so the logarithms are defined. Strict convexity also gives the exact equality condition: equality holds if and only if

\(\displaystyle P_\sigma=D^{-1}\quad\text{for every slate with }y_\sigma>0.\)

Therefore

\(\displaystyle W(y)=\frac1n\left(1-\sum_\sigma\pi_\sigma P_\sigma\right)\leq \frac{1-D^{-1}}n=B.\)

3. Equality slates encode satisfying assignments

Suppose a slate satisfies \(P_\sigma=D^{-1}\).

The exponent of the private prime \(p_i\) in the denominator of \(P_\sigma\) is

\(\displaystyle n_{t_{i,0}}(\sigma)+n_{t_{i,1}}(\sigma).\)

Its exponent in \(D\) is one. Unique prime factorization therefore implies

\(\displaystyle n_{t_{i,0}}(\sigma)+n_{t_{i,1}}(\sigma)=1\quad\text{for every }i.\)

Thus the slate chooses exactly one Boolean value for each variable. In particular, the equality condition excludes repetitions, although repetitions are permitted by the formulation.

Let \(b_i\) be the selected value. The exponent of the clause prime \(r_j\) in the denominator of \(P_\sigma\) is then

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ji}b_i.\)

Its exponent in \(D\) is two. Hence

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ji}b_i=2\quad\text{for every }j.\)

The assignment therefore satisfies every exact-two-in-four clause. Conversely, any satisfying assignment yields an equality slate by choosing its corresponding types.

It remains to show that a satisfying assignment yields a *feasible distribution*, rather than merely one useful slate.

Let \(\sigma\) contain the types selected by a satisfying assignment, and let \(\bar\sigma\) contain the complementary choices. Complementation preserves exact-two-in-four satisfaction because every clause has four occurrences. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle P_\sigma=P_{\bar\sigma}=D^{-1}.\)

Assign masses

\(\displaystyle y_\sigma=y_{\bar\sigma}=\frac1{2n},\)

with all other masses zero. Their total mass is \(1/n\). Every type appears exactly once across these two slates, so its consumption is exactly \(1/(2n)=\mu_t\). This distribution is feasible and has welfare \(B\).

Conversely, a feasible distribution of welfare \(B\) must, by the equality condition from Section 2, contain an equality slate. That slate gives a satisfying assignment.

The feasible region is nonempty and compact: homogeneous slates with masses \(\mu_t/n\) give a feasible distribution, and every coordinate lies between zero and \(1/n\). Thus an optimum is attained. We have proved

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{OPT}=B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{the source formula is satisfiable}.\)

If the source formula is unsatisfiable, then \(\operatorname{OPT}<B\). Therefore testing whether \(\operatorname{OPT}\geq B\) decides the source problem.

4. Polynomial binary encoding

Take the first \(n+h\) primes. The standard bound on the \(k\)-th prime, \(p_k=O(k\log(k+1))\), allows their deterministic construction by a sieve in polynomial time. Each prime has \(O(\log(n+h))\) bits.

Across all true-choice denominators, the total number of clause-prime factors, counted with multiplicity, is

\(\displaystyle \sum_i\sum_j a_{ji}=4h.\)

Thus the total encoding length of the constructed parameters, including their numerators, masses and position factors, is

\(\displaystyle L=O\!\left((n+h)\log(n+h)\right).\)

The threshold \(B=(D-1)/(nD)\) has the same polynomial bit-length bound. All products and rational parameters can be constructed in polynomial time.

Hence this is a polynomial-time many-one reduction. Exact optimization is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\); a polynomial-time algorithm returning an optimal rational distribution would also decide the source formula by evaluating that distribution’s welfare.

5. Rational output size and running times

The hardness is not an output-size obstruction. Every instance has an optimal rational distribution using at most \(\tau+1\) positive slates, with polynomial-size masses.

To prove this, first note that the homogeneous slate consisting of \(m\) copies of type \(t\), assigned mass \(\rho\mu_t/m\), gives a feasible distribution. The feasible region is finite-dimensional and compact, so an optimum exists.

Add nonnegative slack variables to the capacity inequalities. The resulting system has \(\tau+1\) equality rows. Its coefficient matrix is integral, with entries of absolute value at most \(m\).

Choose an optimal solution with the fewest positive variables, counting both slate masses and slacks. Its positive-variable columns must be linearly independent. Otherwise a nonzero dependence would permit sufficiently small feasible perturbations in both directions. Optimality forces the objective to be constant along that dependence, and moving until a positive variable becomes zero contradicts minimality. Therefore there are at most \(\tau+1\) positive variables, and hence at most \(\tau+1\) positive slate masses.

For their bit lengths, select an invertible square subsystem on those columns and apply Cramer’s rule. A common denominator for the right-hand side has \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits. Hadamard’s determinant bound then gives each positive mass bit length

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(L+\log(m+1)+(\tau+1)\log((m+1)(\tau+1))\right).\)

A certificate listing these slates and masses can therefore be verified using exact rational arithmetic in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and the threshold’s bit length. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), and the reduction proves that the rational-threshold decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-complete}\).

For an unconditional exact algorithm, enumerate all \(\tau^m\) slates and solve the resulting rational linear program. Its coefficients have polynomial bit length, so this takes

\(\displaystyle \tau^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\)

deterministic time and returns an optimal rational distribution. For fixed \(m\), this is polynomial; a bound polynomial simultaneously in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
