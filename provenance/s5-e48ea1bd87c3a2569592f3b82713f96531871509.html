The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite item set \(M\), finite valuation-type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu\), subadditive valuation oracles \(v_\theta:2^M\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), and \(\psi\ge\max_{\theta\in T}v_\theta(M)\), where one unit of population carries one unit of each item supply, compute a universally truthful randomized posted-price mechanism using polynomially many type-level demand queries. The mechanism must produce masses \(x_{\theta,S}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_S x_{\theta,S}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,S:j\in S}x_{\theta,S}\le 1\), while achieving \(\mathbb{E}[W(x)]\ge\frac{1}{2\alpha(\beta+1)^2}\operatorname{OPT}_\infty-\frac{\psi}{m}\), with \(\alpha=O(\log\!\log m)\) for subadditive valuations and \(\beta=O(\log\!\log m)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) contain \(m\ge2\) items. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), where \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible valuations. Each item has supply one.
Each type comprises nonatomic individuals with a normalized, monotone, nonnegative subadditive valuation \(v_\theta:2^M\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\). A public binary-rational bound \(\psi\ge\max_\theta v_\theta(M)\) is supplied independently of reports. Let \(L\) include the input bit length and a bound on valuation-answer bit lengths.
A type-level demand query specifies available items and nonnegative rational item prices and returns a utility-maximizing bundle; resolve ties by a fixed public ordering. Individuals have quasilinear utility and may decline participation.
A posted-price mechanism offers individuals additive item-price menus over available bundles, with admission, prices, and availability independent of that individual's report; population portions may receive different menus. Truthfulness concerns deviations by single individuals, and universal truthfulness holds for every fixed random seed.
An allocation records bundle masses \(x_{\theta,S}\ge0\), including the empty bundle, with \(\sum_Sx_{\theta,S}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,S:j\in S}x_{\theta,S}\le1\) for every \(j\in M\), in every realization. Define \(W(x)=\sum_{\theta,S}x_{\theta,S}v_\theta(S)\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}_\infty=\max_x W(x)\) over these constraints.

Can a universally truthful randomized posted-price mechanism, using \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time and type-level demand queries, attain \(\mathbb E[W(x)]\ge\operatorname{OPT}_\infty/[2\alpha(m)(\beta(m)+1)^2]-\psi/m\) on every instance, for universal functions \(\alpha,\beta\ge1\) satisfying \(\alpha(m),\beta(m)=O(1+\log\log m)\), or is this approximation guarantee impossible under those resource bounds?

For finite bidders, a universally truthful posted-price mechanism achieves the corresponding welfare bound in polynomial time using at most one demand query per bidder. [Assadi–Kesselheim–Singla](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01420)
No result settling this precise nonatomic support-oracle model was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted the distribution and per-unit-population supply convention as total population mass one and supply one of each item.
  • Restored normalized and monotone valuations, as required by the discrete definition.
  • Defined the continuous benchmark as maximum mass-weighted welfare over the stated bundle-mass feasibility constraints, including empty-bundle allocations.
  • Interpreted types as nonatomic populations and truthfulness as individual dominant-strategy truthfulness, rather than coordinated deviations by an entire type.
  • Specified additive posted prices, voluntary participation, report-independent individual menus and admission, and feasibility in every random realization.
  • Made the valuation bound public and independent of reports, matching the discrete mechanism's exogenous-bound assumption.
  • Restricted valuation answers to binary rationals with bounded bit length and defined L to make polynomial running time meaningful.
  • Fixed public demand tie-breaking and allowed demand queries restricted to available items.
  • Used m at least two and O(1+log log m) to make the asymptotic guarantee meaningful for small item sets; alpha and beta are universal functions rather than instance-selected parameters.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Proven conclusion

The guarantee is attainable: a deterministic zero-price mechanism achieves exactly \(\operatorname{OPT}_\infty\) using \(\tau\) demand queries.

The decisive feature is that total population mass and every item’s supply both equal \(1\). Consequently, even giving every individual all items is feasible. This removes scarcity throughout the stated instance class.

Computing the optimum

For every type \(\theta\) and bundle \(S\subseteq M\), monotonicity gives
\[ v_\theta(S)\le v_\theta(M). \]
Thus every feasible allocation satisfies
\[ W(x) =\sum_{\theta,S}x_{\theta,S}v_\theta(S) \le \sum_\theta v_\theta(M)\sum_Sx_{\theta,S} =\sum_\theta\mu_\theta v_\theta(M). \]

Conversely, allocating \(M\) to the entire population gives \(x_{\theta,M}=\mu_\theta\). Each item’s consumption is
\[ \sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1, \]
so this allocation is feasible. Therefore
\[ \operatorname{OPT}_\infty=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta v_\theta(M). \]

Mechanism and implementation

Offer every individual the full set of available items \(M\), with every item priced at zero.

For each supported type \(\theta\), make one demand query with available set \(M\) and zero prices. Let its answer be \(D_\theta\), using the stipulated public tie-breaking order. Output
\[ x_{\theta,D_\theta}=\mu_\theta, \qquad x_{\theta,S}=0\quad\text{for }S\ne D_\theta. \]

The mechanism is feasible because, for every item \(j\),
\[ \sum_{\theta:j\in D_\theta}\mu_\theta \le\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1. \]
Moreover, zero-price utility maximization and monotonicity imply
\[ v_\theta(D_\theta) =\max_{S\subseteq M}v_\theta(S) =v_\theta(M). \]
Hence its welfare is exactly \(\operatorname{OPT}_\infty\), irrespective of how ties are resolved.

The implementation uses \(\tau\) demand queries and polynomial time in \((m,\tau,L)\). Its output has at most one nonzero bundle mass per type, and copies the supplied masses without numerical approximation.

Universal truthfulness and the requested bound

Admission, prices, and availability are constant and independent of every individual’s report. Reporting truthfully yields a utility-maximizing bundle at zero payment. Any deviation can yield only another bundle from the same menu or nonparticipation, neither of which increases utility. Thus the mechanism is truthful. Being deterministic, it is also universally truthful.

Choose the universal functions \(\alpha(m)=\beta(m)=1\). They satisfy the requested growth bounds, and
\[ \mathbb E[W(x)] =\operatorname{OPT}_\infty \ge \frac{\operatorname{OPT}_\infty}{8}-\frac{\psi}{m}. \]

Scope of the conclusion

This settles every instance under the stated premises. A version with actual competition for supply would require changing those premises—for example, allowing total population mass greater than \(1\), or item supplies smaller than \(1\). The argument above does not settle that modified question.

Verdict

The prover’s conclusion is correct and rigorous under the stated model. The mechanism achieves exactly \(\operatorname{OPT}_\infty\), is universally truthful, and uses polynomial time and \(\tau\) type-level demand queries. No major or minor issues were found.

The absence of scarcity follows from explicit premises: total population mass is \(1\), and each item has supply \(1\). This is not an unintended interpretation of an unspecified parameter. The finite-bidder comparison does not override those premises.

Step-by-step verification

  • Upper bound on welfare.

For every supported type \(\theta\) and every \(S\subseteq M\), monotonicity gives \(v_\theta(S)\le v_\theta(M)\). Multiplying by the nonnegative allocation masses and summing gives
\[ W(x)\le\sum_\theta v_\theta(M)\sum_Sx_{\theta,S} =\sum_\theta\mu_\theta v_\theta(M). \]
The final equality uses exactly the required type-mass constraints. This step is valid.

  • Attainment of the upper bound.

The allocation \(x_{\theta,M}=\mu_\theta\), with all other masses zero, satisfies every type-mass constraint. Each item has total consumption \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\), equal to its supply. Its welfare equals the upper bound. Thus the asserted formula
\[ \operatorname{OPT}_\infty=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta v_\theta(M) \]
is established.

  • Validity of the demand queries.

The available set \(M\) and zero item prices are permitted query inputs. A returned bundle \(D_\theta\) maximizes \(v_\theta(S)\), because payments are zero. Monotonicity makes \(M\) a maximizer, so every demand answer has \(v_\theta(D_\theta)=v_\theta(M)\). The fixed public tie-breaking rule therefore cannot affect welfare.

  • Feasibility of the implemented allocation.

Assigning mass \(\mu_\theta\) to \(D_\theta\) preserves every type’s total mass, including when \(D_\theta=\varnothing\). For each item \(j\), consumption is a sum over a subset of the supported types and is at most \(1\). This proves feasibility in the mechanism’s sole deterministic realization.

  • Welfare of the implemented allocation.

Substituting the verified demand values yields
\[ W(x)=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta v_\theta(D_\theta) =\sum_\theta\mu_\theta v_\theta(M) =\operatorname{OPT}_\infty. \]
This equality is exact.

  • Resource bounds.

There is one demand query per supported type. Each returned bundle can be recorded with \(m\) membership bits, and the allocation uses at most \(\tau\) nonzero entries. Copying the supplied binary-rational masses requires polynomial time and introduces no precision issue. The claimed implementation bounds follow without enumerating all bundles or evaluating their values.

  • Truthfulness and participation.

Every individual receives the same report-independent menu, admission, and availability. Truthful demand selection maximizes that individual’s utility over the menu. A deviation can select only another offered bundle, while declining participation gives utility zero. Nonnegative valuations and zero payments ensure that declining cannot improve upon truthful selection. Consequently, truthfulness holds for single-individual deviations. Determinism implies universal truthfulness.

  • Requested approximation guarantee.

The choices \(\alpha(m)=\beta(m)=1\) satisfy both the lower bounds and the required asymptotic growth. The denominator is \(2\cdot1\cdot(1+1)^2=8\). Since \(\operatorname{OPT}_\infty\ge0\) and \(\psi\ge0\),
\[ \operatorname{OPT}_\infty\ge\operatorname{OPT}_\infty/8-\psi/m. \]
The requested guarantee follows for every \(m\ge2\), including instances with zero valuations.
