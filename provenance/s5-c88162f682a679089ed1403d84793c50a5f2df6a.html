The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_A,\mu_B\) summing to one, endowments \(e_A=(1,0)\) and \(e_B=(0,1)\), positive proposed bundles \(x_A,x_B\), a queried type \(i\), and a finite union \(K_i\) of rational polyhedral regions of coalition mass profiles, decide whether there exist \(q\in K_i\) and bundles \(y_A,y_B\in\mathbb R_+^2\) such that \(q_i>0\), \(q_Ay_A+q_By_B\le q_Ae_A+q_Be_B\), every type \(t\) with \(q_t>0\) weakly prefers \(y_t\) to \(x_t\), and type \(i\) strictly prefers \(y_i\) to \(x_i\), for \(u(z)=\log z_1+\log z_2\). Such a profile and allocation are a known feasible blocking deviation, so their existence means reject; otherwise accept.

Result

The existence of a known blocking deviation is polynomial-time solvable for \(m=\tau=2\). A deterministic algorithm uses

\[ O\!\left(\left[\sum_{j=1}^{r}(h_j+4)^3+1\right](L+1)^2\right) \]

bit operations, where \(h_j\) is the number of inequalities defining the \(j\)-th polyhedron and \(\sum_j h_j=h\). In particular, this is \(O((h+r+1)^3(L+1)^2)\).

The proof below gives an exact algorithm using rational arithmetic, including exact treatment of strict improvement.

1. Eliminate the deviation bundles

Define the positive rational numbers \(c_A=x_{A1}x_{A2}\) and \(c_B=x_{B1}x_{B2}\), and write \(\alpha=\sqrt{c_A}\), \(\beta=\sqrt{c_B}\).

Because the proposed bundles have positive coordinates, their utilities are finite. For any participating type \(t\), weak improvement therefore requires a positive bundle and is equivalent to \(y_{t1}y_{t2}\ge c_t\). Strict improvement is equivalent to a strict product inequality.

Write a coalition profile as \(q=(a,b)\).

Lemma 1. A profile permits a blocking deviation for either queried type if and only if

\[ a>0,\qquad b>0,\qquad a\alpha+b\beta<\sqrt{ab}. \]

Proof. First, both masses must be positive. If \(a=0\), the coalition has no first commodity. Feasibility forces the first coordinate of every participating type’s bundle to be zero, so no participating type can weakly improve. The case \(b=0\) is identical. The empty profile cannot satisfy \(q_i>0\).

Now suppose \(a,b>0\). Let \(v_A=ay_A\) and \(v_B=by_B\) denote aggregate allocations. For any nonnegative vectors \(v_A,v_B\),

\[ \sqrt{(v_{A1}+v_{B1})(v_{A2}+v_{B2})} \ge \sqrt{v_{A1}v_{A2}}+\sqrt{v_{B1}v_{B2}}. \]

Indeed, the difference between the squares of the two sides is

\[ \left(\sqrt{v_{A1}v_{B2}}-\sqrt{v_{B1}v_{A2}}\right)^2\ge0. \]

Feasibility bounds the total allocation coordinatewise by \((a,b)\). Weak improvement for both types, together with strict improvement for the queried type, consequently gives

\[ \sqrt{ab} \ge \sqrt{(v_{A1}+v_{B1})(v_{A2}+v_{B2})} \ge a\sqrt{y_{A1}y_{A2}}+b\sqrt{y_{B1}y_{B2}} > a\alpha+b\beta. \]

This proves necessity.

Conversely, suppose \(S=a\alpha+b\beta<\sqrt{ab}\). Choose

\[ y_A=\frac{\alpha}{S}(a,b), \qquad y_B=\frac{\beta}{S}(a,b). \]

Both bundles are positive, and they exhaust exactly the coalition’s endowment:

\[ ay_A+by_B=\frac{a\alpha+b\beta}{S}(a,b)=(a,b). \]

Moreover,

\[ y_{A1}y_{A2}=c_A\frac{ab}{S^2}>c_A, \qquad y_{B1}y_{B2}=c_B\frac{ab}{S^2}>c_B. \]

Thus both types strictly improve, including the queried type. This proves sufficiency. \(\square\)

In particular, strict improvement for just the queried type is possible exactly when strict improvement for both types is possible. The queried type still determines which knowledge set \(K_i\) is used.

2. Blocking depends only on the coalition ratio

For \(a,b>0\), put \(t=a/b\). Dividing the inequality in Lemma 1 by \(b\) gives the equivalent condition

\[ \alpha t+\beta<\sqrt t. \]

Define

\[ g(t)=\frac{\alpha t+\beta}{\sqrt t} =\alpha\sqrt t+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt t}, \qquad t>0. \]

Blocking is therefore equivalent to \(g(t)<1\).

Its derivative is

\[ g'(t)=\frac{\alpha t-\beta}{2t^{3/2}}. \]

Hence \(g\) strictly decreases before, and strictly increases after, its unique minimizer

\[ t_*=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{c_B}{c_A}}. \]

At that minimizer,

\[ g(t_*)=2(c_Ac_B)^{1/4}. \]

Thus some unrestricted positive profile can block if and only if \(16c_Ac_B<1\). Knowledge restrictions require determining which ratios are available.

3. Obtain the available ratio interval from each polyhedron

Write the explicitly listed union as \(K_i=\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}Q_j\). For each component, form

\[ P_j=Q_j\cap\bigl([0,\mu_A]\times[0,\mu_B]\bigr). \]

This adds four inequalities. Each \(P_j\) is a bounded, closed rational polyhedron, possibly empty or of lower dimension.

We first justify how to recover its vertices.

Lemma 2. Every vertex of a nonempty \(P_j\) is the intersection of two defining boundary lines with linearly independent normals. Moreover, \(P_j\) is the convex hull of its vertices.

Proof. At a vertex, the normals of the active inequalities must span the plane. Otherwise there is a nonzero direction orthogonal to all active normals. A sufficiently small displacement in either direction preserves the active equalities and every inactive inequality, contradicting extremality. Thus two independent active normals exist.

For the convex-hull statement, a bounded polyhedron of dimension zero or one is a point or compact segment, generated by its vertices.

For a two-dimensional bounded polyhedron, intersect a line through an interior point with the polyhedron. The point lies between two boundary points. Every boundary point satisfies a defining equality with nonzero normal: otherwise all effective inequalities are strict, giving a feasible neighborhood. Its intersection with that boundary line is a compact segment or point. The endpoints are vertices of the polyhedron, because any convex decomposition of such an endpoint must stay in the same supporting face and then coincide with that endpoint. Thus boundary points, and consequently interior points, are convex combinations of vertices. \(\square\)

We can therefore enumerate all vertices by solving every pair of independent boundary equations and retaining exactly those intersections satisfying all inequalities. An empty retained list means \(P_j\) is empty.

Let \(V_j\) be this list. There is a profile in \(P_j\) with both coordinates positive exactly when:

  • some vertex has positive first coordinate; and
  • some vertex has positive second coordinate.

Necessity follows from Lemma 2 and nonnegativity. For sufficiency, the midpoint of two such vertices has both coordinates positive.

Discard the component if either condition fails. Otherwise define

\[ \ell_j=\min_{\substack{v\in V_j\\v_B>0}}\frac{v_A}{v_B}. \]

If a vertex has \(v_A>0\) and \(v_B=0\), set \(u_j=\infty\). Otherwise define

\[ u_j=\max_{\substack{v\in V_j\\v_B>0}}\frac{v_A}{v_B}. \]

Here \(\ell_j\) is finite and nonnegative, and \(u_j>0\).

Lemma 3. The ratios attained by profiles with both coordinates positive are exactly

\[ D_j= \begin{cases} [\ell_j,u_j]\cap(0,\infty),&u_j<\infty,\\ [\ell_j,\infty)\cap(0,\infty),&u_j=\infty. \end{cases} \]

Proof. Every vertex satisfies \(v_A\ge\ell_jv_B\). If \(u_j\) is finite, every vertex also satisfies \(v_A\le u_jv_B\). Convex combinations preserve these inequalities, proving that every attainable ratio belongs to the claimed interval.

For finite \(u_j\), take vertices attaining \(\ell_j\) and \(u_j\). Their second coordinates are positive. Along their segment, the ratio is continuous and has those endpoint values, so it attains every intermediate value. A zero lower endpoint is excluded precisely because it has zero first coordinate.

If \(u_j=\infty\), take a vertex \(v\) attaining \(\ell_j\) and a vertex \(w=(w_A,0)\) with \(w_A>0\). Along their segment, parametrized by \(0\le s<1\), the ratio equals

\[ \ell_j+\frac{s\,w_A}{(1-s)v_B}. \]

It ranges from \(\ell_j\) to arbitrarily large finite values. Again, only a possible zero endpoint is excluded. \(\square\)

4. Exact rational tests

For each remaining component, we must decide whether \(g(t)<1\) for some \(t\in D_j\).

The monotonicity proved in Section 2 reduces this to three cases.

Case 1: The available interval contains \(t_*\).

This occurs exactly when

\[ c_A\ell_j^2\le c_B \]

and, if \(u_j<\infty\),

\[ c_B\le c_Au_j^2. \]

These comparisons are valid because all quantities being squared are nonnegative. Since \(t_*>0\), the excluded zero endpoint causes no difficulty.

The component permits blocking exactly when

\[ 16c_Ac_B<1. \]

Case 2: The whole interval lies above \(t_*\).

This occurs when \(c_A\ell_j^2>c_B\). Then \(\ell_j>0\), and \(g\) attains its minimum on the available interval at the attained endpoint \(t_0=\ell_j\).

Case 3: The whole interval lies below \(t_*\).

This occurs when \(u_j<\infty\) and \(c_Au_j^2<c_B\). Then \(u_j>0\), and the minimum occurs at the attained endpoint \(t_0=u_j\).

Cases 2 and 3 require evaluating \(g(t_0)<1\) for a positive rational \(t_0\). This also has a purely rational test. Put \(p=c_Ac_B\) and

\[ R=t_0-c_At_0^2-c_B. \]

Since \(\alpha t_0+\beta>0\), squaring its comparison with \(\sqrt{t_0}\) is valid and gives

\[ g(t_0)<1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R>2t_0\sqrt p. \]

Because the right-hand side is positive, this is equivalent to

\[ R>0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad R^2>4t_0^2p. \]

Thus every decision uses exact rational comparisons. In particular, equality is correctly rejected as a blocking witness.

The algorithm returns “blocking exists” if any component passes its test, and “no blocking exists” otherwise. Lemmas 1–3 and the monotonicity of \(g\) prove both directions of correctness.

5. Running time and strictness check

For component \(j\), let \(H_j=h_j+4\). There are \(O(H_j^2)\) boundary pairs. Solving a pair and checking its intersection against every inequality takes \(O(H_j)\) rational arithmetic operations. The remaining vertex scans and ratio tests take at most \(O(H_j^2)\) operations.

Every rational constructed uses only a constant number of arithmetic operations on input coefficients or on a vertex’s coordinates. Its numerator and denominator therefore have \(O(L+1)\) bits. Exact operations and comparisons take \(O((L+1)^2)\) bit operations using elementary integer arithmetic. This proves

\[ T(2,2,r,h,L) = O\!\left(\left[\sum_{j=1}^{r}(h_j+4)^3+1\right](L+1)^2\right) \subseteq O((h+r+1)^3(L+1)^2). \]

An empty union is handled immediately.

Checking blocking only at vertices would be incorrect. For example, take \(\mu_A=\mu_B=1/2\), proposed bundles \(x_A=x_B=(1/4,1/4)\), and the known component \(a+b=1/2\). After clipping, its vertices are \((1/2,0)\) and \((0,1/2)\), neither of which blocks. Its interior profile \((1/4,1/4)\) does block, using \(y_A=y_B=(1/2,1/2)\). The algorithm handles this by testing the ratio interval.

The cited paper restricts its finite TU formulation to bounded integer payoff vectors ([Liu, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.04595v10)). The polynomial-time conclusion here follows independently from the continuous blocking criterion and the rational algorithm proved above.
