The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of additive value types \(T\), rational population masses \(\mu_t\), and per-capita supplies \(\rho_j\) for indivisible items \(j\), compute a direct mechanism maximizing truthful per-capita revenue. Each type-\(t\) agent receives an integral bundle and pays a transfer; the mechanism must be individually rational and Bayesian incentive compatible, and its aggregate allocation must respect supply for every reported population composition. At the truthful composition, if \(q_{tj}\) is the expected allocation of item \(j\) to type \(t\) and \(p_t\) its expected payment, the objective is \(\sum_t\mu_t p_t\), subject to \(\sum_t\mu_t q_{tj}\le\rho_j\) and \(\langle v_t,q_t\rangle-p_t\ge\langle v_t,q_s\rangle-p_s\) for all \(t,s\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless population of total mass \(1\), given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) types \(t\in T\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) item categories; type \(t\) has additive values \(v_t\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m\), and category \(j\) has binary rational per-capita supply \(\rho_j\ge0\). Each agent may receive at most one unit of each category, with no further demand constraints. Agents are risk-neutral and quasi-linear; transfers may have either sign, and nonparticipation gives utility \(0\).
A randomized direct mechanism uses reports in \(T\) and may depend on the reported composition \(r\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\), where \(\sum_t r_t=1\). Agents making the same report receive the same marginal bundle lottery and expected payment. Each realized allocation assigns integral bundles measurably across the population and allocates mass at most \(\rho_j\) of category \(j\), for every reported composition; unused supply is allowed.
At truthful composition \(r=\mu\), let \(q_{tj}\in[0,1]\) be the probability that report \(t\) receives category \(j\), and let \(p_t\in\mathbb R\) be its expected payment. An individual deviation does not change the composition. Require interim individual rationality \(\langle v_t,q_t\rangle-p_t\ge0\) and Bayesian incentive compatibility \(\langle v_t,q_t\rangle-p_t\ge\langle v_t,q_s\rangle-p_s\) for every \(t,s\in T\); indifference permits truthful reporting.
The optimization problem is to compute a succinct description of a mechanism maximizing exact truthful per-capita revenue \(\sum_t\mu_t p_t\). Let \(L\) be the total binary encoding length of the masses, values, and supplies.

Is this continuous mechanism-design problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many independent additive bidders with explicitly listed finite type distributions, exact revenue-optimal randomized BIC, interim-IR mechanisms can be computed in time polynomial in the numbers of items, bidders, and listed types, with polynomial dependence on numerical bit lengths. [Cai–Daskalakis–Weinberg](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.4572).
No result settling this exact atomless-population problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the population to total mass one and interpreted masses as an atomless society, so an individual deviation leaves the reported composition unchanged.
  • Interpreted each item as a category with a mass of indivisible copies; each agent receives at most one copy per category and has no demand constraint.
  • Specified nonnegative binary rational values and supplies, unrestricted signed transfers, risk-neutral quasi-linear utility, and zero utility from nonparticipation.
  • Used interim individual rationality and incentive compatibility at the truthful composition, matching the discrete optimization formulation; required supply feasibility at every reported composition in every realized allocation.
  • Allowed composition-dependent mechanisms, equal marginal outcomes for equal reports, random integral bundles, disposal of unused supply, and truthful reporting under indifference.
  • Required a succinct mechanism description rather than an explicit enumeration of all population compositions.

Conclusion

The problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). A polynomial-size rational linear program gives the exact optimum, and every feasible solution admits a succinct implementation satisfying supply constraints in every realization.

I use the standard measurable interpretation of randomized mechanisms: allocation indicators are jointly measurable in agents and mechanism randomness, so Tonelli’s theorem applies.

1. Necessary linear constraints

Write \(q_{tj}\) for report \(t\)’s allocation probability for category \(j\), and \(p_t\) for its expected payment, at truthful composition.

Every admissible mechanism satisfies:

  • \(0\le q_{tj}\le1\), for every \(t,j\).
  • \(\sum_t\mu_tq_{tj}\le\rho_j\), for every \(j\).
  • \(\sum_jv_{tj}q_{tj}-p_t\ge0\), for every \(t\).
  • \(\sum_jv_{tj}q_{tj}-p_t\ge\sum_jv_{tj}q_{sj}-p_s\), for every \(t,s\).

For completeness, the supply inequality follows from realized feasibility. Let \(X_t\) be the true-type-\(t\) population, with measure \(\mu_t\), and let \(a_j(x,\omega)\in\{0,1\}\) denote the realized allocation of category \(j\). Tonelli’s theorem gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_tq_{tj} =\mathbb E_\omega\!\left[\int_Xa_j(x,\omega)\,d\nu(x)\right] \le\rho_j. \)

The other inequalities are exactly the allocation bounds, interim IR, and BIC from the question. Additivity and risk neutrality ensure that allocation probabilities and expected payments determine every relevant expected utility.

2. Payments can be restricted to be nonnegative

This restriction preserves the optimal value, despite the permission to use transfers of either sign.

Take any feasible table \((q,p)\), and define truthful utilities \(u_t=\sum_jv_{tj}q_{tj}-p_t\). Let \(\delta=\min_tu_t\), which is nonnegative by IR, and increase every payment by \(\delta\):

\(\displaystyle p'_t=p_t+\delta.\)

All utility differences between reports remain unchanged, so BIC remains valid. Truthful utilities become \(u_t-\delta\), so IR also remains valid.

Choose \(t_0\) attaining the minimum. Its new truthful utility is zero. Its incentive constraint against every report \(s\) therefore implies

\(\displaystyle 0\ge\sum_jv_{t_0j}q_{sj}-p'_s, \qquad p'_s\ge\sum_jv_{t_0j}q_{sj}\ge0. \)

The last inequality uses nonnegative values and allocation probabilities. Revenue increases by \(\delta\), since the population has total mass one.

Consequently, every feasible mechanism is weakly dominated in revenue by a feasible table with nonnegative payments.

3. The finite optimization problem

Consider the linear program maximizing \(\sum_t\mu_tp_t\), subject to the four families of constraints in Section 1 and the additional constraints \(p_t\ge0\).

It has \(\tau(m+1)\) variables and \(O(\tau m+\tau^2+m)\) inequalities. All coefficients are rational, and explicitly writing them requires only polynomial encoding length in \(m,\tau,L\).

The feasible region is nonempty: \(q=0,p=0\) is feasible. It is also bounded. Indeed, IR and nonnegative payments give

\(\displaystyle 0\le p_t\le\sum_jv_{tj}q_{tj}\le\sum_jv_{tj}, \)

and allocation probabilities lie in \([0,1]\). Thus the feasible region is a compact rational polytope, and the objective attains its maximum.

The standard exact rational linear-programming theorem computes an optimal rational solution in polynomial time in the dimensions and binary encoding length; see, for example, Theorem 4 in the supplied [Cai–Daskalakis–Weinberg paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.4572). Its hypotheses hold here because the constraints are explicit, rational, and define a nonempty bounded polytope.

The solution has polynomial encoding length. One can also see this directly: an optimum exists at a vertex, whose coordinates solve a square rational linear system; clearing denominators and applying Cramer’s rule gives polynomial bounds on their bit lengths.

Let \((q^*,p^*)\) be such an optimum. Sections 1–2 show that its objective value upper-bounds the revenue of every admissible mechanism. It remains to implement it.

4. Exact implementation by cyclic allocation

We first describe the implementation for an arbitrary reported composition \(r\) and arbitrary target probabilities \(Q_{tj}\in[0,1]\) satisfying \(\sum_tr_tQ_{tj}\le\rho_j\).

Since the input specifies the population through its support, a canonical realization is the unit interval with Lebesgue measure. True types initially occupy intervals of lengths \(\mu_t\). A measurable report profile partitions the population into measurable report sets \(S_t\), of measures \(r_t\).

For \(r_t>0\), define the normalized rank within report group \(t\) by

\(\displaystyle h_t(x)=\frac{\operatorname{Leb}(S_t\cap[0,x])}{r_t}. \)

Under normalized Lebesgue measure on \(S_t\), this rank is uniformly distributed on \([0,1]\). To verify this, let \(H_t(a)=\operatorname{Leb}(S_t\cap[0,a])/r_t\). It is continuous and nondecreasing, with endpoints zero and one. For \(b\in(0,1)\), put \(a_b=\sup\{a:H_t(a)\le b\}\). Continuity gives \(H_t(a_b)=b\), and therefore

\(\displaystyle \frac{\operatorname{Leb}\{x\in S_t:h_t(x)\le b\}}{r_t} =H_t(a_b)=b. \)

For report groups of measure zero, set \(h_t(x)=0\).

Draw one common random variable \(U\), uniformly distributed on \([0,1)\). Give an agent \(x\) reporting \(t\) category \(j\) precisely when

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{frac}(h_t(x)+U)<Q_{tj}, \)

where \(\operatorname{frac}(z)=z-\lfloor z\rfloor\).

This rule has all the required properties:

  • Integral measurable allocations. Each category is assigned by a measurable indicator taking values in \(\{0,1\}\).
  • Identical bundle lotteries within each report. For each fixed agent, \(\operatorname{frac}(h_t(x)+U)\) is uniform. Thus the entire bundle lottery depends only on the vector \(Q_t\), and category \(j\) is received with probability \(Q_{tj}\).
  • Supply feasibility in every realization. For every fixed \(U\), rotation modulo one preserves the uniform distribution of ranks within \(S_t\). Hence the allocated mass of category \(j\) within that group is exactly \(r_tQ_{tj}\). Its total allocated mass is exactly \(\sum_tr_tQ_{tj}\le\rho_j\).

There is no demand constraint coupling categories, so the bundles produced by this common-seed construction are permitted.

The construction also applies to any atomless probability space. The elementary divisibility theorem for atomless finite measures permits repeated equal-mass binary partitions, producing a fixed measurable uniform coordinate \(z:X\to[0,1]\). Replace the interval rank above by

\(\displaystyle h_t(x)=\frac{\nu\{y\in S_t:z(y)\le z(x)\}}{r_t}. \)

The distribution of \(z\) restricted to \(S_t\) has no atoms: it is dominated by the unconditional uniform distribution. Its cumulative distribution function is therefore continuous, and the preceding rank argument applies unchanged.

5. Extension to every reported composition

To turn the optimal table into a mechanism defined at every composition, set

\(\displaystyle D_j(r)=\sum_tr_tq^*_{tj}, \qquad c(r)=\min\!\left(\{1\}\cup \left\{\frac{\rho_j}{D_j(r)}:D_j(r)>0\right\}\right). \)

Define report \(t\)’s probabilities and deterministic payment by

\(\displaystyle Q_{tj}(r)=c(r)q^*_{tj}, \qquad P_t(r)=c(r)p^*_t. \)

These quantities satisfy \(0\le c(r)\le1\) and

\(\displaystyle \sum_tr_tQ_{tj}(r)=c(r)D_j(r)\le\rho_j. \)

Implement these probabilities using Section 4 and charge \(P_t(r)\).

At truthful composition, the LP supply inequalities imply \(c(\mu)=1\). Consequently, the mechanism implements exactly \((q^*,p^*)\) and earns its optimal objective value.

An individual deviation changes neither composition nor group measures. The lottery received after any report is the corresponding report lottery, independently of the agent’s rank. Therefore its expected deviation utility is exactly the expression in the LP’s incentive constraint.

In fact, at every fixed composition, all IR expressions and all BIC utility differences are multiplied by the common nonnegative factor \(c(r)\). Thus the extension satisfies those inequalities there as well.

Finally, the output is succinct: it consists of the polynomial-length rational table \((q^*,p^*)\), the supplies, and the fixed scaling and cyclic-allocation rules. It does not enumerate agents, report profiles, bundles, or random realizations.

This supplies both directions: every admissible mechanism is upper-bounded by the LP optimum, and the LP optimum is attained by an admissible succinct mechanism.
