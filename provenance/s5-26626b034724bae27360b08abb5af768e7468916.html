The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) binary issues, a feasible set \(C\) represented by implications and promised \(k\)-restrictive for fixed \(k\), and ballot types \(b^1,\ldots,b^\tau\in\{0,1\}^m\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), compute \(w\in C\) such that no nonempty issue set \(S\) has both \(w^S\in C\) and positive mass of types agreeing with \(1-w\) on every issue in \(S\), with \(F_\mu(w^S)-F_\mu(w)\ge 1/m^2\). Here \(w^S\) flips \(w\) on \(S\), and \(F_\mu(w)=\sum_t\mu_t H_{u_t(w)}\), where \(u_t(w)\) counts the issues on which type \(t\) agrees with \(w\) and \(H_r=\sum_{q=1}^r1/q\), with \(H_0=0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq 1\) binary issues. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) distinct types \(b^t\in\{0,1\}^m\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input includes a list \(\mathrm{Imp}\) of implications \((x,d)\to(y,e)\), with \(x,y\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\) and \(d,e\in\{0,1\}\); the literal \((x,d)\) means that issue \(x\) has value \(d\). Let \(C\neq\varnothing\) be the assignments satisfying every implication. Ballots need not belong to \(C\).
For each \(w\in C\), construct a directed graph \(G_w\) on the vertices \((x,d)\). Each implication \((x,d)\to(y,e)\) contributes the edge \((x,d)\to(y,e)\) if \(w_y\neq e\), and the edge \((y,1-e)\to(x,1-d)\) if \(w_x=d\).
Let \(R_w(x,d)\) be the vertices reachable from \((x,d)\), excluding the source itself. For a fixed integer \(k\geq2\), the input is promised to be \(k\)-restrictive: \(\max_{x,d}|R_w(x,d)|=k-1\) for every \(w\in C\), relative to the supplied implication list.
Write \(w^S\) for the assignment obtained by flipping exactly the issues in \(S\). Define \(u_t(w)=|\{j:b^t_j=w_j\}|\), \(H_0=0\), \(H_r=\sum_{q=1}^r1/q\), and \(F_\mu(w)=\sum_t\mu_tH_{u_t(w)}\).
The search problem is to output any \(w\in C\) for which there is no nonempty \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\) satisfying simultaneously \(w^S\in C\), \(\sum_{t:\,b^t_j=1-w_j\ \forall j\in S}\mu_t>0\), and \(F_\mu(w^S)-F_\mu(w)\geq1/m^2\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\), with dependence on \(k\) explicit.

Classification

The search problem is NP-hard for every fixed $k\geq 2$. This holds even when all movable issues are unconstrained and every other issue is fixed to $0$.

The reduction below has the following property: if an exact-cover instance has no solution, the constructed society has a unique terminal outcome, namely the all-zero outcome; if it has a solution, every terminal outcome encodes an exact cover. Thus the reduction establishes hardness of finding any terminal outcome.

1. Encoding exact cover by balance equations

We reduce from the NP-complete Exact Cover problem: given a finite universe $U=\{1,\ldots,h\}$ and subsets $A_1,\ldots,A_p\subseteq U$, decide whether some subcollection covers every element exactly once. Its NP-completeness is a classical result of [Karp](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

We may assume $h,p\geq 1$ and that every $A_i$ is nonempty. Empty sets can be removed, and the excluded trivial cases can be mapped to fixed nontrivial yes or no instances.

Introduce binary variables $c_0,c_1,\ldots,c_p$ and the homogeneous equations

$$ \sum_{i:\,e\in A_i}c_i-c_0=0 \qquad(e\in U). $$

If $c_0=1$, these equations say precisely that the sets with $c_i=1$ form an exact cover. If $c_0=0$, nonnegativity and the nonemptiness of every $A_i$ force every $c_i=0$.

We next encode these equations using rows whose entries are exclusively $1$ and $-1$.

Put

$$ N=2(p+1), $$

and index a binary vector $z\in\{0,1\}^N$ by coordinates $z_{i,+},z_{i,-}$ for $0\leq i\leq p$. Construct the following signed rows:

  • A baseline row with pair $(1,-1)$ at every index $i$.
  • For every index $i$, a row obtained from the baseline by replacing its pair at $i$ with $(-1,1)$.
  • For every element $e\in U$, a row with pair $(-1,-1)$ at index $0$, pair $(1,1)$ at every index $i$ with $e\in A_i$, and pair $(1,-1)$ at every other index.

There are

$$ r_0=p+h+2 $$

rows. Let $r$ be the smallest power of two satisfying $r\geq r_0$, and repeat the baseline row until there are exactly $r$ rows. Denote them by $a^1,\ldots,a^r$, and define

$$ \delta_j(z)=a^j\cdot z. $$

Call $z$ balanced if $\delta_j(z)=0$ for every $j$.

Balance lemma. The nonzero balanced binary vectors encode exactly the exact covers of the original instance.

Proof. Subtracting the baseline equation from the equation with pair $i$ reversed gives

$$ -2z_{i,+}+2z_{i,-}=0. $$

Thus every balanced vector satisfies

$$ z_{i,+}=z_{i,-}=c_i $$

for some binary $c_i$. Each element row then has dot product

$$ 2\left(\sum_{i:\,e\in A_i}c_i-c_0\right). $$

Consequently, balance implies the homogeneous equations above. If $c_0=0$, all $c_i$ vanish, so $z=0$. Otherwise $c_0=1$, and the selected sets form an exact cover.

Conversely, an exact cover gives a balanced vector by setting both coordinates of pair $0$ to $1$, setting both coordinates of every selected set to $1$, and setting all other coordinates to $0$. $\square$

2. Issues, parameters, and the exact reachability promise

Fix the requested integer $k\geq 2$. Let $m$ be the smallest power of two satisfying

$$ m\geq \max\{2N+k,\;4rN(N+1)\}. $$

The first $N$ issues are free. Every remaining issue $x$ is forced to $0$ by supplying the implication

$$ (x,1)\to(x,0). $$

Choose $k$ issues $z_1,\ldots,z_k$ among issues $2N+1,\ldots,m$. In addition, supply the cycle implications

$$ (z_i,1)\to(z_{i+1},1) \qquad(1\leq i\leq k), $$

where $z_{k+1}=z_1$.

The feasible assignments are exactly

$$ C=\{0,1\}^N\times\{0\}^{m-N}. $$

In particular, $C$ is nonempty.

We verify the graph promise relative to this supplied implication list. At every feasible outcome, a forcing implication $(x,1)\to(x,0)$ contributes neither edge: its consequent agrees with the outcome, and its antecedent does not. Each cycle implication contributes precisely its forward edge between value-$1$ literals. Thus $G_w$ consists of a directed cycle on the $k$ vertices $(z_i,1)$ and isolated vertices elsewhere.

Every cycle vertex reaches exactly the other $k-1$ cycle vertices, excluding itself. Therefore

$$ \max_{x,d}|R_w(x,d)|=k-1 \qquad\text{for every }w\in C. $$

The instance satisfies the exact $k$-restrictiveness promise.

Write

$$ w(z)=(z,0^{m-N}), \qquad w^0=w(0). $$

3. Ballots and positive dyadic masses

Use issues $N+1,\ldots,2N$ as auxiliary fixed issues.

For a signed vector $v\in\{-1,1\}^N$, construct a ballot $b(v)$ as follows:

  • On free issue $i$, its value is $(1+v_i)/2$.
  • Among the $N$ auxiliary issues, set exactly $|\{i:v_i=1\}|$ ballot values to $0$, using the first such number of auxiliary issues.
  • Set every other fixed issue’s ballot value to $1$.

At $w^0$, this ballot has exactly $N$ agreements: the free agreements count the negative entries of $v$, while the auxiliary agreements count its positive entries. Consequently,

$$ u_{b(v)}(w(z))=N+v\cdot z. $$

For each row $a^j$, include the two ballots $b(a^j)$ and $b(-a^j)$.

Include two additional ballots:

  • $b^\uparrow$ has value $1$ on every issue.
  • $b^\downarrow$ has value $0$ on every free issue and value $1$ on every fixed issue.

For $s=\sum_i z_i$, their utilities are

$$ u_\uparrow(w(z))=s, \qquad u_\downarrow(w(z))=N-s. $$

Assign masses

$$ \alpha=\frac{2}{m^2}, \qquad \beta=\frac{1}{4m^3}, \qquad \gamma=\frac{1-\alpha-\beta}{2r}. $$

Give $b^\uparrow$ mass $\alpha$, give $b^\downarrow$ mass $\beta$, and give each of the $2r$ row ballots mass $\gamma$.

Because $m\geq 4$,

$$ \alpha+\beta\leq\frac12, \qquad \gamma\geq\frac{1}{4r}>0. $$

All masses are positive and sum to $1$. Since $m$ and $r$ are powers of two, they are dyadic rationals.

If some constructed ballots coincide, merge them and add their masses. This produces the required distinct support, with

$$ \tau\leq 2r+2. $$

Merging preserves the objective and all positive-support conditions. In particular, the support still contains the upward and downward ballots with positive mass.

4. Potential estimates

Put

$$ \varepsilon=\frac{1}{m^2}, \qquad D(z)=F_\mu(w(z))-F_\mu(w^0). $$

The utilities calculated above give the exact identity

$$ D(z)= \alpha H_s +\beta(H_{N-s}-H_N) +\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{r} \left(H_{N+\delta_j(z)}+H_{N-\delta_j(z)}-2H_N\right). $$

Here $|\delta_j(z)|\leq N$, so every harmonic index is valid.

Harmonic penalty for an imbalance

For an integer $d$ with $1\leq d\leq N$,

$$ 2H_N-H_{N+d}-H_{N-d} = \sum_{q=0}^{d-1} \left(\frac{1}{N-q}-\frac{1}{N+q+1}\right). $$

Every summand is positive, and the first equals $1/(N(N+1))$. Hence

$$ H_{N+d}+H_{N-d}-2H_N \leq-\frac{1}{N(N+1)}. $$

The expression is symmetric in $d$ and equals zero when $d=0$.

Every nonzero balanced vector improves on zero

If $z$ is balanced and nonzero, then $s\geq 1$ and the row-ballot terms vanish. Since $H_s\geq 1$, $H_N\leq N$, and $N\leq m$,

$$ D(z) \geq \frac{2}{m^2}-\frac{H_N}{4m^3} \geq \frac{2}{m^2}-\frac{N}{4m^3} \geq \frac{7}{4m^2} > \varepsilon. $$

Every unbalanced vector is worse than zero by more than the threshold

If $z$ is unbalanced, at least one row contributes the harmonic penalty above, and all other row contributions are nonpositive. Also $H_{N-s}-H_N\leq 0$. Therefore

$$ D(z) \leq \frac{2H_s}{m^2} -\frac{\gamma}{N(N+1)} \leq \frac{2N}{m^2} -\frac{1}{4rN(N+1)}. $$

To check the threshold margin explicitly, put

$$ K=4rN(N+1). $$

Our choice ensures $m\geq K$, and $K>2N+1$. Thus

$$ m^2\geq K^2>K(2N+1), $$

which implies

$$ \frac{2N}{m^2}-\frac1K<-\frac1{m^2}. $$

Consequently,

$$ D(z)<-\varepsilon \qquad\text{for every unbalanced }z. $$

5. Every terminal output decides exact cover

First suppose $z$ is unbalanced. Let $S$ consist of the free issues on which $z_i=1$. This set is nonempty, and flipping it sends $w(z)$ to $w^0$.

The move is feasible and supported by $b^\downarrow$, which has value $0$ on every issue in $S$. Its improvement is

$$ F_\mu(w^0)-F_\mu(w(z)) =-D(z)>\varepsilon. $$

Therefore every terminal outcome must be balanced.

Now suppose the exact-cover instance is satisfiable. By the balance lemma there is a nonzero balanced vector $z$. From $w^0$, flip precisely the free issues with $z_i=1$. This feasible move is supported by $b^\uparrow$, and its improvement satisfies

$$ F_\mu(w(z))-F_\mu(w^0)=D(z)>\varepsilon. $$

Thus $w^0$ is not terminal. Every terminal outcome is consequently nonzero and balanced, and the balance lemma extracts an exact cover from it.

If the exact-cover instance is unsatisfiable, the balance lemma says that zero is the only balanced vector. Every other feasible outcome is nonterminal by the downward move established above. Moreover, every nonempty feasible move from $w^0$ leads to an unbalanced vector and decreases the potential. Hence $w^0$ is terminal and is the unique terminal outcome.

Terminal outcomes always exist: a global maximizer of $F_\mu$ over the finite nonempty set $C$ has no positive-gain feasible move.

We can therefore decide Exact Cover by constructing this instance, calling the search solver once, and accepting exactly when its returned free vector is nonzero. In the accepting case, the returned vector also yields an exact-cover witness.

This proves the claimed NP-hardness.

6. Size and running-time consequences

The reduction satisfies

$$ N=2(p+1), \qquad r<2(p+h+2), $$

and

$$ m=O\bigl((p+h)p^2+k\bigr), \qquad \tau\leq 2r+2. $$

Each mass has a common denominator $8rm^3$, so its encoding uses $O(\log(rm))$ bits. The implication list has $m-N+k$ entries. After merging duplicate ballots, the total input length satisfies

$$ L=O(rm+m\log(m+1)). $$

The construction and decoding take polynomial time in the original input length and $k$. If a search solver runs in time $T_k(m,\tau,L)$, the reduction decides Exact Cover in time

$$ \operatorname{poly}(\ell+k)+T_k(m,\tau,L), $$

where $\ell$ is the exact-cover input length. For every fixed $k$, all constructed parameters are polynomial in $\ell$. Thus a polynomial-time solver in $m,\tau,L$ would imply $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$. In particular, no bound of the form $f(k)\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ is possible unless $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$.

For completeness, an unconditional exact algorithm is to enumerate all feasible assignments and return a global maximizer. Define

$$ B=L+m\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil +\lceil\log_2(\tau+1)\rceil+10. $$

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, this gives the conservative bound

$$ O\left( (m+\tau)B^2 + 2^m\left( L+\tau m\log(m+1)+\tau B^2 \right) \right), $$

with no additional dependence on $k$.

Indeed, write $\mu_t=p_t/q_t$, put $Q=\prod_t q_t$ and $D=m!$, and precompute the integers

$$ A_t=Q\mu_t, \qquad E_s=DH_s. $$

Then

$$ F_\mu(w)=\frac{\sum_t A_tE_{u_t(w)}}{QD}. $$

The integers have $O(B)$ bits. Precomputation costs $O((m+\tau)B^2)$; each assignment can be checked for feasibility and evaluated within the per-assignment bound displayed above.

7. Consequence for explicitly listed voters

The hardness construction also converts directly to polynomially many discrete voters. Take

$$ n=8rm^3. $$

Before merging duplicate ballots, the corresponding voter multiplicities are

$$ n_\uparrow=16rm, \qquad n_\downarrow=2r, $$

and, for each of the $2r$ row ballots,

$$ n_{\mathrm{row}}=4m^3-8m-1. $$

These are positive integers and sum to $n$. Explicitly listing them requires $O(rm^4)$ ballot bits, still polynomial in the exact-cover input size for fixed $k$.

Their unnormalized PAV objective is exactly $nF_\mu$, so the improvement threshold becomes $n/m^2$. Hence the same hardness applies to the exact discrete stopping condition with the compact implication-list representation.

The move-search inference in the cited [Proposition 16](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02609v2#S5) fails on this construction. At $w^0$, changing a single free issue gives a nonzero imbalance and decreases the potential; fixed issues cannot be changed feasibly. Nevertheless, whenever an exact cover exists, a larger supported move improves by more than the threshold. The construction uses ballots inconsistent with $C$, as expressly permitted in the task.
