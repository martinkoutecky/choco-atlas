The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of job types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\geq 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), and rational work requirements \(k_t>0\), choose a measurable service rate \(a_t(u,s)\geq 0\) for each \(u\in[0,\mu_t]\) and time \(s\geq 0\). Require \(\sum_{t\in T}\int_0^{\mu_t}a_t(u,s)\,du\leq 1\) at every time and \(\int_0^{T_t(u)}a_t(u,s)\,ds=k_t\), where \(T_t(u)\) is that job's completion time. Minimize \(\sum_{t\in T}\int_0^{\mu_t}\log T_t(u)\,du\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Each type has binary-rational work requirement \(k_t>0\). There is one resource, so \(m=1\), with unit capacity; all jobs are available at time zero.
A schedule consists of jointly measurable rates \(a_t:[0,\mu_t]\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\), locally integrable in time for almost every job, satisfying \(\sum_t\int_0^{\mu_t}a_t(u,s)\,du\leq1\) at every time \(s\). There is no additional pointwise rate bound.
For almost every job, its completion time is the first time \(T_t(u)\in(0,\infty)\) at which \(\int_0^{T_t(u)}a_t(u,s)\,ds=k_t\); service stops thereafter. Integrals over jobs use Lebesgue measure, and schedules may distinguish jobs of the same type.
Define \(V=\inf_a\sum_t\int_0^{\mu_t}\log T_t(u)\,du\), using the natural logarithm and schedules with a well-defined objective. The input also includes a binary-rational threshold \(b\in\mathbb{Q}\); \(L\) is the total input bit length.

Is deciding whether \(V\leq b\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finitely many jobs on one resource, minimizing the product of completion times gives Shortest Job First and is envy-free in expectation under uniform random tie-breaking; with two jobs, product-minimizing allocations are also envy-free in expectation for multiple resources. All complexity statements use the ordinary bit model of computation.

2. Exact evaluation of the scheduling infimum

Sort the supported types so that
$$ k_1\leq k_2\leq\cdots\leq k_\tau. $$
Define
$$ P_0=C_0=0,\qquad P_i=\sum_{j=1}^i\mu_j,\qquad C_i=\sum_{j=1}^i\mu_jk_j. $$

Represent the population by $(0,1)$, placing type $i$ on $(P_{i-1},P_i)$. Write $k(x)=k_i$ on that interval, and define
$$ W(q)=\int_0^q k(x)\,dx. $$
Thus
$$ W(q)=C_{i-1}+k_i(q-P_{i-1}) \quad\text{for }P_{i-1}\leq q\leq P_i. $$
Because every $k_i$ is positive, $W$ is continuous and strictly increasing.

2.1. Minimum work needed to complete a given mass

If $E$ is a measurable set of jobs of mass $q$, then
$$ \int_E k(x)\,dx\geq W(q). $$

For $0<q<1$, put $I=(0,q)$. Since $k$ is nondecreasing, there is a number $\kappa$ such that $k\leq\kappa$ almost everywhere on $I$ and $k\geq\kappa$ almost everywhere outside $I$. Consequently,
$$ (k-\kappa)(\mathbf 1_E-\mathbf 1_I)\geq0 $$
almost everywhere: on $I$ both factors are nonpositive, and outside $I$ both are nonnegative. Integrating and using $\lambda(E)=\lambda(I)$ gives
$$ \int_E k-\int_I k = \int_0^1(k-\kappa)(\mathbf 1_E-\mathbf 1_I)\,dx \geq0. $$
The cases $q=0,1$ follow directly.

2.2. A lower bound for every admissible schedule

For an admissible schedule, write
$$ A(x,s)=\int_0^s a(x,r)\,dr,\qquad F(s)=\lambda\{x:T(x)\leq s\}. $$
We disregard the null set on which the jobwise assumptions fail. Joint measurability makes $A$ measurable; its jobwise continuity and monotonicity make the completion time $T$ measurable.

Every job completed by time $s$ has received its required work. Tonelli’s theorem and the capacity constraint therefore give
$$ \begin{aligned} \int_{\{T\leq s\}}k(x)\,dx &\leq \int_0^1 A(x,s)\,dx\\ &=\int_0^s\int_0^1a(x,r)\,dx\,dr\\ &\leq s. \end{aligned} $$
The minimum-work inequality implies
$$ W(F(s))\leq s. $$

Let
$$ Q(q)=\inf\{s\geq0:F(s)\geq q\}, \qquad 0<q<1. $$
Whenever $F(s)\geq q$, we have
$$ s\geq W(F(s))\geq W(q). $$
Taking the infimum yields
$$ Q(q)\geq W(q). $$

The right continuity of $F$ gives
$$ \lambda\{q:Q(q)\leq s\}=F(s), $$
so $Q$, evaluated at a uniform point of $(0,1)$, has the same distribution as $T$.

Put $k_{\min}=\min_i k_i$. Since
$$ Q(q)\geq W(q)\geq k_{\min}q, $$
the negative parts of $\log Q$ and $\log W$ are integrable. Also, $W(q)\leq C_\tau$, so the positive part of $\log W$ is bounded. Thus, including schedules whose positive objective is infinite,
$$ \int_0^1\log T(x)\,dx = \int_0^1\log Q(q)\,dq \geq \int_0^1\log W(q)\,dq. $$
In particular, no admissible schedule has objective $-\infty$.

2.3. Feasible schedules approach the bound

Fix a positive integer $N$. Divide each type into $N$ batches of mass $\mu_i/N$. Process these batches consecutively in increasing type order.

A type-$i$ batch is processed for
$$ d_i=\frac{\mu_i k_i}{N} $$
units of time. During its processing interval, every job in that batch receives rate $N/\mu_i$, and every other job receives rate zero. Its aggregate rate is exactly one, and each job receives
$$ \frac{N}{\mu_i}d_i=k_i. $$
Half-open processing intervals ensure capacity feasibility at every time. These rates are jointly measurable, locally integrable, and stop after completion.

Batch $j$ of type $i$ completes at
$$ C_{i-1}+\frac{j\mu_i k_i}{N}. $$
Its completion-time quantile $Q_N$ therefore satisfies, almost everywhere,
$$ W(q)\leq Q_N(q)\leq C_\tau,\qquad Q_N(q)-W(q)\leq \frac{\max_i\mu_i k_i}{N}. $$
Hence $Q_N(q)\to W(q)$ for every $q>0$, apart from immaterial interval-endpoint conventions. Moreover,
$$ |\log Q_N(q)| \leq |\log W(q)|+|\log C_\tau|. $$
The right side is integrable. Dominated convergence proves
$$ \lim_{N\to\infty}\int_0^1\log Q_N(q)\,dq = \int_0^1\log W(q)\,dq. $$

Combining the lower bound and this construction gives the exact infimum:
$$ \boxed{V=\int_0^1\log W(q)\,dq.} $$

3. A polynomial-size expression for the value

On the interval for type $i$, substitute $z=W(q)$, so $dz=k_i\,dq$. Using the continuous extension $z\log z=0$ at $z=0$,
$$ V= \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\frac{ C_i\log C_i-C_i-C_{i-1}\log C_{i-1}+C_{i-1} }{k_i}. $$
The nonlogarithmic terms sum to
$$ -\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\frac{C_i-C_{i-1}}{k_i} =-\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i=-1. $$

Define
$$ c_i=C_i\left(\frac1{k_i}-\frac1{k_{i+1}}\right) \quad (i<\tau), \qquad c_\tau=\frac{C_\tau}{k_\tau}. $$
Collecting the logarithmic terms gives
$$ \boxed{V=-1+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}c_i\log C_i.} $$
The sorted requirements imply $c_i\geq0$, and telescoping gives
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}c_i = \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\frac{C_i-C_{i-1}}{k_i} =1. $$

Sorting and computing these rationals takes polynomial time in $L$. Their bit lengths are polynomially bounded: the denominators in each rational sum can be bounded by products of the participating denominators, whose bit lengths add. Subsequent products, differences, and reciprocals preserve polynomial bit length.

This computes an exact expression for $V$. Deciding its comparison with $b$ requires further work.

4. Equality with the threshold is decidable in polynomial time

4.1. Equality is impossible unless $b=-1$

Choose a positive integer $D$ clearing the denominators of all $c_i$ and of $b+1$. If $V=b$, then
$$ \prod_i C_i^{Dc_i}=\exp(D(b+1)). $$
The left side is rational. If $b+1\neq0$, then $D(b+1)$ is a nonzero integer.

The Hermite–Lindemann theorem states that $\exp(\alpha)$ is transcendental whenever $\alpha$ is a nonzero algebraic number. Its hypotheses apply to this integer, contradicting rationality of the left side. Thus
$$ V=b\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b=-1. $$
This standard theorem follows from the Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem; a self-contained proof of the latter is given by [Popescu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14352).

4.2. A self-contained equality algorithm for products of powers

At $b=-1$, write $C_i=p_i/q_i$ in positive integers and choose $D$ clearing the coefficient denominators. Put $n_i=Dc_i$. Then
$$ V=-1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \prod_i p_i^{n_i}=\prod_i q_i^{n_i}. $$
The bases and exponents have polynomial-size binary encodings, although expanding the products could take exponential space.

Here is a polynomial-time equality algorithm that avoids expansion.

Represent the two products by lists of pairs $(a,e)$, with integer base $a>1$ and positive integer exponent $e$. Discard unit bases and zero exponents. Whenever a left pair $(a,e)$ and a right pair $(b,f)$ have
$$ g=\gcd(a,b)>1, $$
replace their contribution using
$$ \frac{a^e}{b^f} = \frac{(a/g)^e}{(b/g)^f}\,g^{e-f}. $$
Place $g^{e-f}$ on the appropriate side, and discard any new unit bases or zero exponents.

To bound the number of steps, let
$$ \Phi=\sum_{\text{both lists}}\log_2 a, $$
counting every listed base separately. If $e\neq f$, a replacement decreases $\Phi$ by $\log_2 g\geq1$. If $e=f$, it decreases $\Phi$ by $2\log_2 g\geq2$. Therefore there are at most the initial $\Phi$ steps, which is bounded by the total input length of the bases.

Each step increases the number of pairs by at most one. No base exceeds the largest original base. If $E$ denotes the sum of all exponents in both lists, then a step replaces $e+f$ by at most
$$ e+f+|e-f|\leq2(e+f). $$
Thus $E$ increases by at most a factor of two per step. Since there are polynomially many steps, all exponent bit lengths remain polynomial. Euclidean gcd computations and the other integer operations consequently take polynomial time.

At termination, every left base is coprime to every right base. The two products are then coprime integers, so they are equal precisely when both lists are empty.

This proves a polynomial-time equality test for every scheduling instance, with unrestricted $\tau$.

5. Certified approximation in polynomial time

For completeness, the precision needed later can be computed efficiently.

For a positive rational $r$ of bit length $h$, write
$$ r=2^z y,\qquad 1\leq y<2, $$
where $|z|=O(h)$. Put
$$ v=\frac{y-1}{y+1}, $$
so $0\leq v<1/3$. Integrating the geometric series for $2/(1-v^2)$ gives
$$ \log y= 2\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}\frac{v^{2\ell+1}}{2\ell+1}+E_M, $$
with
$$ 0\leq E_M\leq \frac{2v^{2M+1}}{(2M+1)(1-v^2)}. $$
The same formula computes $\log2$ using $v=1/3$.

Because
$$ \log r=z\log2+\log y, $$
choosing
$$ M=O(p+\log(h+1)) $$
suffices to obtain error at most $2^{-p}$. The finite rational sums have polynomial bit length in $h$ and $M$, so their exact evaluation takes polynomial time.

Since $c_i\geq0$ and $\sum_i c_i=1$, approximating every $\log C_i$ to error $2^{-p}$ approximates $V$ to the same error. Hence:

An additive approximation to $V$ with error at most $2^{-p}$ is computable in time polynomial in $L$ and $p$.

This alone does not bound the precision needed for exact threshold decision.

6. New result: arbitrary thresholds are tractable for fixed support size

6.1. The quantitative theorem used

For a rational $r=p/q$ in lowest terms, define its height as
$$ H(r)=\max\{|p|,q\}. $$

Theorem 1 of [Cijsouw and Waldschmidt, *Linear forms and simultaneous approximations*](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1977__34_2_173_0.pdf) has the following rational specialization.

For each $n\geq1$, there is an effectively computable constant $K_n>0$. Let $\alpha_j>0$ and $\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_n$ be rational. Choose $A_j\geq6$ bounding both $H(\alpha_j)$ and $\exp(|\log\alpha_j|)$, and $B\geq6$ bounding all $H(\beta_j)$. Put
$$ \Lambda=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^n\beta_j\log\alpha_j, \qquad \Omega=\prod_{j=1}^n\log A_j. $$
If $\Lambda\neq0$, then
$$ |\Lambda|> \exp\!\left( -K_n\Omega(\log\Omega)(\log B+\log\Omega) \right). $$
Here the algebraic-degree parameter in their theorem is $1$.

6.2. Application to the scheduling expression

Apply the theorem with
$$ n=\tau,\qquad \alpha_i=C_i,\qquad \beta_i=c_i,\qquad \beta_0=-(b+1). $$
Then
$$ \Lambda=V-b. $$

All $\alpha_i$ are positive rationals, and all coefficients are rational, so the degree and positivity hypotheses hold. Zero coefficients and bases equal to one are allowed in the stated theorem.

Let $h\geq3$ be an integer bounding the numerator and denominator bit lengths of every $C_i$, $c_i$, and $b+1$. Section 3 proves that $h$ is polynomially bounded in $L$. In particular,
$$ H(C_i),H(c_i),H(b+1)\leq2^h $$
and
$$ \exp(|\log C_i|)=\max\{C_i,C_i^{-1}\}\leq2^h. $$
We may therefore take
$$ A_i=B=2^{h+3}. $$

Set $u=(h+3)\log2$. Then $1<u<h+3$, and
$$ \Omega=u^\tau,\qquad \log\Omega=\tau\log u. $$
Using $\log u<h+3$ gives
$$ \Omega(\log\Omega)(\log B+\log\Omega) < \tau(\tau+1)(h+3)^{\tau+2}. $$

Choose an effectively computable integer $M_\tau\geq K_\tau$, and define
$$ R=M_\tau\tau(\tau+1)(h+3)^{\tau+2}. $$
Whenever $V\neq b$, the theorem yields
$$ |V-b|>\exp(-R). $$
Since $\log2>1/2$, the integer
$$ p=2R+3 $$
satisfies
$$ 2^{-p}<\exp(-R)<|V-b|. $$

6.3. The decision algorithm and its running time

First use Section 4 to test whether $V=b$. If equality holds, answer yes.

Otherwise compute $\widehat V$ with
$$ |\widehat V-V|\leq2^{-(p+2)}. $$
The proved separation implies that $\widehat V-b$ has the same sign as $V-b$. Its rational sign therefore decides the instance.

For fixed $\tau$, $M_\tau$ is a constant and $p$ is polynomial in $h$, hence polynomial in $L$. Section 5 computes the required approximation in polynomial time. This proves
$$ \boxed{\text{For every fixed }\tau,\text{ arbitrary rational thresholds are decidable in }\mathrm{P}.} $$

More explicitly, the proof gives a uniform running-time bound of the form
$$ f(\tau)L^{O(\tau)} $$
for an effectively computable function $f$. This is not a polynomial bound jointly in $\tau$ and $L$.

6.4. An unbounded-support special case

There is also a polynomial-time subclass with unrestricted $\tau$: suppose every cumulative work value is a power of two,
$$ C_i=2^{z_i},\qquad z_i\in\mathbb Z. $$
This property and the exponents $z_i$ can be computed from the rational encodings in polynomial time. Then
$$ V=-1+\gamma\log2,\qquad \gamma=\sum_i c_i z_i\in\mathbb Q. $$
The rational $\gamma$ has polynomial bit length. If $\gamma=0$, comparison is rational. Otherwise, applying the preceding quantitative theorem with only one logarithm gives polynomial-time decision, regardless of the number of supported types.

7. What the unrestricted problem encodes

This section assumes general binary numerator-and-denominator rational inputs.

Define rational logarithmic comparison to be the following decision problem: given rational coefficients $\alpha_j$, positive rational bases $r_j$, and a rational $d$, decide whether
$$ \sum_j\alpha_j\log r_j\leq d. $$

The value formula gives a polynomial-time reduction from scheduling threshold decision to this problem:
$$ V\leq b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_i c_i\log C_i\leq b+1. $$

The converse follows from an exact realization.

7.1. Realizing positive logarithmic weights

Let
$$ 0<R_1<\cdots<R_n $$
be positive rationals, and let positive rational weights $w_i$ sum to one. Define
$$ R_0=0,\qquad h_i=\sum_{j=i}^n\frac{w_j}{R_j},\qquad k_i=\frac1{h_i},\qquad \mu_i=(R_i-R_{i-1})h_i. $$
All masses and requirements are positive. Also,
$$ h_i-h_{i+1}=\frac{w_i}{R_i}>0, $$
so $k_1<\cdots<k_n$.

The total mass is
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_i\mu_i &=\sum_i(R_i-R_{i-1})\sum_{j=i}^n\frac{w_j}{R_j}\\ &=\sum_j\frac{w_j}{R_j}\sum_{i=1}^j(R_i-R_{i-1})\\ &=\sum_j w_j=1. \end{aligned} $$
Its cumulative work satisfies
$$ C_i=\sum_{\ell=1}^i\mu_\ell k_\ell =\sum_{\ell=1}^i(R_\ell-R_{\ell-1}) =R_i. $$
Therefore its value coefficients are
$$ c_i=R_i(h_i-h_{i+1})=w_i\quad(i<n), \qquad c_n=R_nh_n=w_n. $$
Its infimum is exactly
$$ V=-1+\sum_iw_i\log R_i. $$

The construction has polynomial output bit length: each $h_i$ is a sum of at most $n$ explicitly represented rationals, and the subsequent reciprocals and products have polynomial-size numerator and denominator representations.

7.2. Signed coefficients

For a general expression
$$ S=\sum_j\alpha_j\log r_j, $$
discard zero terms. Replace each term with negative coefficient by
$$ \alpha_j\log r_j=|\alpha_j|\log(1/r_j). $$
Combine equal bases, discard bases equal to one, and sort. All remaining coefficients are positive.

If their sum is $H>0$, normalize them by $H$ and apply the realization construction. It produces
$$ V=-1+\frac{S}{H}. $$
Choosing
$$ b=-1+\frac dH $$
gives
$$ V\leq b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\leq d. $$
If no terms remain, $S=0$ and rational comparison decides the source instance; it can be mapped to a fixed yes-instance or no-instance.

Thus
$$ \boxed{ \text{Scheduling threshold decision is polynomial-time equivalent to rational logarithmic comparison.} } $$

7.3. The fixed threshold already captures products of powers

For positive integers and binary-encoded nonnegative integer exponents,
$$ X=\prod_i A_i^{e_i},\qquad Y=\prod_j B_j^{f_j}, $$
we have
$$ X\leq Y \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_i e_i\log A_i-\sum_j f_j\log B_j\leq0. $$
The preceding reduction produces a scheduling instance with $b=-1$.

Conversely, at $b=-1$, clearing coefficient denominators gives
$$ V\leq-1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \prod_i p_i^{Dc_i}\leq\prod_i q_i^{Dc_i}, $$
where $C_i=p_i/q_i$. All bases and exponents have polynomial-size binary encodings.

Therefore the scheduling problem restricted to $b=-1$ is polynomial-time equivalent to comparison of products of powers.

7.4. Why the dyadic qualification matters

The realization does not always produce dyadic requirements. For example,
$$ R_1=1,\quad R_2=2,\quad w_1=w_2=\frac12 $$
gives
$$ h_1=\frac34,\qquad k_1=\frac43. $$
Consequently, the reverse-equivalence conclusions above have not been proved for dyadic-only inputs. The earlier analytic and algorithmic results do apply to those inputs.

8. Remaining open point

The exact optimization value and polynomial-time equality test are established. The new quantitative argument settles arbitrary rational thresholds for every fixed support size.

For growing $\tau$, however, the proved precision bound contains both the parameter-dependent constant $M_\tau$ and a power of $h$ whose exponent grows with $\tau$. It does not establish the requested uniform polynomial-time bound.

The reductions identify an exact arithmetic comparison problem; they do not establish $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness. Accordingly, neither alternative in the unrestricted target question is proved here.
