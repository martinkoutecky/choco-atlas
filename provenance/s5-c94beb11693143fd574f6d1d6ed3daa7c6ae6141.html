The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support rational income distribution \(\mu\) with a unique mass median \(q\), mean income \(\bar y\), \(q<\bar y\), and required per-capita revenue \(\rho\le\bar y\), compute a TAL parameter \(\theta^*\in[0,1]\) such that no other \(\theta\) gives strictly higher post-tax income to mass greater than \(1/2\). For each \(\theta\), define the TAL tax schedule by the paper’s capped or leveled form, choosing its balancing constant so that \(\int R^\theta(y)\,d\mu(y)=\rho\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\delta_{y_i}\), with distinct nonnegative binary-rational incomes \(y_i\), positive binary-rational masses \(w_i\), and \(\sum_iw_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Assume \(\bar y=\sum_iw_iy_i>0\) and a unique mass median \(q<\bar y\), meaning \(\mu([0,q))<1/2\) and \(\mu((q,\infty))<1/2\). The required per-capita revenue is a binary rational \(\rho\in[0,\bar y]\).
For \(\theta\in[0,1]\), the TAL tax schedule is \(R^\theta(y)=\min\{\theta y,\lambda\}\) when \(\rho<\theta\bar y\), and \(R^\theta(y)=\max\{\theta y,y-c\}\) when \(\rho>\theta\bar y\). Choose a nonnegative balancing constant so that \(\sum_iw_iR^\theta(y_i)=\rho\); when \(\rho=\theta\bar y\), set \(R^\theta(y)=\theta y\). Any nonuniqueness of the constant gives the same taxes on the support.
Voters prefer greater post-tax income. An equilibrium parameter \(\theta^*\) satisfies \(\mu(\{y:y-R^\theta(y)>y-R^{\theta^*}(y)\})\le1/2\) for every \(\theta\in[0,1]\); indifferent voters do not count toward a strict majority.

Can an exact rational equilibrium parameter \(\theta^*\) always be computed in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is this computation NP-hard?

For finite societies with an odd number of at least three taxpayers and median income below mean income, an explicit TAL majority equilibrium is known ([discrete result](https://theeconomics.uma.es/malagawpseries/Papers/METCwp2010-1.pdf)).
The published treatment also establishes equilibrium existence for a finite taxpayer model ([published result](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304406810001369)); no result settling the stated rational-mass computational problem was located.

Result

An exact rational equilibrium can always be computed in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\). In fact, there is an explicit formula involving only the median and two weighted sums.

The formula is the weighted analogue of the finite-taxpayer formula in Theorem 3 of the [working paper](https://theeconomics.uma.es/malagawpseries/Papers/METCwp2010-1.pdf). The proof below establishes its validity directly for rational masses, including indifference.

1. Formula and preliminary identities

Define

\[ H=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\max\{y_i-q,0\}, \qquad A=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\min\{y_i,q\}. \]

For every nonnegative income \(y\), we have \(y=\min\{y,q\}+\max\{y-q,0\}\). Consequently, \(A+H=\bar y\).

Return

\[ \theta^*= \begin{cases} 0,&\rho\le H,\\[2mm] \dfrac{\rho-H}{A},&\rho>H. \end{cases} \]

The second branch never divides by zero: \(\rho>H\), together with \(\rho\le\bar y=A+H\), implies \(A>0\). Moreover, that branch gives \(0<\theta^*\le1\).

Because \(q<\bar y\), some support income exceeds \(q\); hence \(H>0\).

We will prove that every challenger’s strict supporters lie entirely below \(q\) or entirely above \(q\). Each such set has mass strictly less than \(1/2\) by hypothesis.

2. Endpoint revenues

If \(\rho=0\), every support tax is zero: taxes are nonnegative, masses are positive, and their weighted sum is zero.

If \(\rho=\bar y\), every support tax equals income: each tax is at most income, and the weighted sum of the nonnegative differences \(y_i-R^\theta(y_i)\) is zero.

Thus every parameter is an equilibrium at either endpoint. The stated formula therefore works there. For the remainder, assume \(0<\rho<\bar y\).

3. Equilibrium when \(\rho\le H\)

At \(\theta=0\), post-tax income is \(\min\{y,c_0\}\), where the balancing constant satisfies

\[ \sum_iw_i\min\{y_i,c_0\}=\bar y-\rho. \]

Let \(F(c)=\sum_iw_i\min\{y_i,c\}\). This function is continuous and nondecreasing. If \(y_{\max}=\max_i y_i\), then

\[ F(q)=A=\bar y-H\le\bar y-\rho, \qquad F(y_{\max})=\bar y. \]

The intermediate value theorem therefore supplies a balancing constant \(c_0\in[q,y_{\max}]\). The problem’s convention permits this choice of constant.

Every income \(y_i\le q\) consequently pays zero under \(\theta^*=0\). No challenger can strictly improve the post-tax income of such a voter, because TAL taxes are nonnegative. Hence all strict supporters of any challenger belong to \((q,\infty)\), whose mass is less than \(1/2\).

This proves equilibrium in the first branch.

4. Equilibrium when \(\rho>H\)

Write \(a=(\rho-H)/A\). Since \(\rho<\bar y=A+H\), we have \(0<a<1\). Furthermore,

\[ \rho-a\bar y =\frac{H(\bar y-\rho)}{A}>0. \]

Thus parameter \(a\) uses the leveling branch.

Set \(c^*=(1-a)q\). Its resulting taxes are

\[ \max\{ay,y-c^*\} = \begin{cases} ay,&y\le q,\\ y-(1-a)q,&y>q. \end{cases} \]

Equivalently, this tax is \(a\min\{y,q\}+\max\{y-q,0\}\). Its weighted revenue is \(aA+H=\rho\), so \(c^*\) is a valid balancing constant. The candidate’s post-tax income is therefore \(\min\{(1-a)y,c^*\}\).

Challengers with \(\theta<a\)

Because \(\theta\bar y<a\bar y<\rho\), such a challenger also uses the leveling branch. Let \(c_\theta\) be its balancing constant. Its post-tax income is \(\min\{(1-\theta)y,c_\theta\}\).

We claim that \(c_\theta\le c^*\). Suppose instead that \(c_\theta>c^*\). Since \(1-\theta>1-a\), for every support income,

\[ \min\{(1-\theta)y_i,c_\theta\} \ge \min\{(1-a)y_i,c^*\}. \]

For every \(y_i>q\), both arguments of the minimum on the left exceed \(c^*\), whereas the minimum on the right equals \(c^*\). The inequality is therefore strict at every such income. There is positive mass above \(q\), so the challenger’s aggregate post-tax income would exceed the candidate’s.

This contradicts balancedness: both aggregate post-tax incomes equal \(\bar y-\rho\). Hence \(c_\theta\le c^*\).

For every \(y_i\ge q\), the candidate’s post-tax income equals \(c^*\), while the challenger’s is at most \(c_\theta\le c^*\). Thus all strict supporters of this challenger lie below \(q\), with mass less than \(1/2\).

Challengers with \(\theta>a\)

For \(y_i\le q\), the candidate’s tax is \(ay_i\). We show that no such voter strictly prefers the challenger.

If \(\rho>\theta\bar y\), the challenger’s tax satisfies \(R^\theta(y_i)\ge\theta y_i\ge ay_i\). The same inequality holds at \(\rho=\theta\bar y\), when taxes are proportional.

If \(\rho<\theta\bar y\), let \(\lambda_\theta\) be the challenger’s balancing cap. Since every support tax is at most \(\lambda_\theta\) and the masses sum to one,

\[ \rho=\sum_iw_i\min\{\theta y_i,\lambda_\theta\} \le\lambda_\theta. \]

Also, \(\rho>a\bar y>aq\). Thus, for \(y_i\le q\), both \(\theta y_i\) and \(\lambda_\theta\) are at least \(ay_i\). Consequently,

\[ R^\theta(y_i)=\min\{\theta y_i,\lambda_\theta\}\ge ay_i. \]

All strict supporters therefore lie above \(q\), again with mass less than \(1/2\).

The remaining challenger, \(\theta=a\), produces the same taxes. This completes the equilibrium proof.

5. Exact computation and bit complexity

Sort the distinct incomes and accumulate their masses. The median \(q\) is the first income at which cumulative mass exceeds \(1/2\). The strict median hypotheses guarantee that cumulative mass before this income is less than \(1/2\).

Then compute \(H\), \(A\), and the stated formula using exact rational arithmetic.

Sorting requires \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) rational comparisons, and the remaining computation requires \(O(\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations. All intermediate numerators and denominators have bit lengths polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\): the calculation uses finite sums and products of input rationals followed by a single division. Standard exact integer arithmetic therefore gives polynomial total running time and polynomial output length.

The output parameter is rational; it need not be a binary fraction with a power-of-two denominator.

Thus the stated computation is polynomial-time solvable, with no need to expand masses into individual taxpayers.
