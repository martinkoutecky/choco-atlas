The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), initial opinions \(q_t\in[0,1]\), and a rational matrix \(A\) with \(A_{tu}\ge 0\), \(\sum_u A_{tu}=1-\beta_t\) for \(\beta_t>0\), and \(\mu_t A_{tu}=\mu_u A_{ut}\), consider a nonatomic population in which every type-\(t\) member uses these shared parameters. In round \(r\), a member samples a current opinion from type \(u\) with probability \(A_{tu}\), or uses \(q_t\) with probability \(\beta_t\), then updates using \(\lambda(r)=r/(r+1)\). Given \(\epsilon>0\), find an explicit round bound \(R(\epsilon)\) guaranteeing that the vector of expected typewise opinion means is within \(\epsilon\) in \(\ell_\infty\) distance of the unique type-level FJ fixed point \(\mathbf{x}^*=A\mathbf{x}^*+\boldsymbol{\beta}\odot\mathbf{q}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Opinions are scalar, so \(m=1\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Each type has a rational initial opinion \(q_t\in[0,1]\). The rational matrix \(A\) satisfies \(A_{tu}\ge0\), \(\sum_u A_{tu}=1-\beta_t\), \(\beta_t\in(0,1]\), and \(\mu_tA_{tu}=\mu_uA_{ut}\). Same-type sampling is permitted.
Initially every type-\(t\) member has opinion \(q_t\). In each synchronous round \(r\ge1\), a member samples type \(u\) with probability \(A_{tu}\) and then samples an opinion from that type's preceding-round distribution, independently of her own history; with probability \(\beta_t\), the sampled value is \(q_t\).
Writing this sampled value as \(S\), her update is \(X(r)=\frac{r}{r+1}X(r-1)+\frac{1}{r+1}S\). Let \(y_t(r)\) be the expected opinion of a uniformly sampled type-\(t\) member, and let \(\mathbf{x}^*\) be the unique FJ fixed point satisfying \(x_t^*=\sum_u A_{tu}x_u^*+\beta_tq_t\).
Given rational \(\epsilon>0\), the computational problem is to produce an explicit integer bound \(R(\epsilon)\ge0\) such that \(\|\mathbf{y}(r)-\mathbf{x}^*\|_\infty\le\epsilon\) for every integer \(r\ge R(\epsilon)\). Bounds may be represented succinctly by finite expressions using binary rationals, arithmetic, rational powers, and ceilings; minimality is not required.

Is computing such an explicit round bound polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finite societies with symmetric interaction weights and positive anchoring weights, a quantitative convergence-in-expectation bound is known; the specified averaging schedule gives an upper round bound exponential in the inverse minimum anchoring weight. [Fotakis, Palyvos-Giannas, and Skoulakis](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/16/Papers/047.pdf).
Later work analyzes different limited-information update rules and the expectation of the opinion error norm, rather than the norm of expected opinions. No result explicitly treating the nonatomic problem specified here was identified. [Opinion Dynamics with Limited Information](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-023-01157-5).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Initial opinions and the tolerance are binary rationals, so the computational input has a finite bit length.
  • Every member initially holds her type's initial opinion; updates are synchronous and use preceding-round opinions.
  • Sampling within a selected type uses its normalized opinion distribution and fresh randomness independent of the updating member's history.
  • Positive diagonal entries of the type interaction matrix are permitted: another member of the same nonatomic type is distinct from the updating individual.
  • The guarantee holds for every round at or after the bound, consistent with the discrete definition of convergence.
  • The bound need not be minimal and may be a succinct expression, separating computation of the bound from expansion of an enormous integer.
  • The otherwise inapplicable parameter m is fixed to 1, the opinion dimension.

Conclusion

Computing a valid round bound is polynomial-time under the permitted succinct representation. Let \(b=\min_{t\in T}\beta_t\). One valid output is

\(R(\epsilon)=\begin{cases}0,&\epsilon\ge 1,\\[2pt]\left\lceil 2\epsilon^{-1/b}\right\rceil-2,&0<\epsilon<1.\end{cases}\)

The numerical value of this bound can be enormous, but its expression has polynomial encoding length. The proof below does not require detailed balance or computation of the fixed point.

1. Bounded opinions and the fixed point

Write \(\mathbf q=(q_t)_{t\in T}\), \(B=\operatorname{diag}(\beta_t)\), and define \(\|\mathbf v\|_\infty=\max_{t\in T}|v_t|\). Because the support is finite and every anchoring weight is positive, \(0<b\le 1\).

Every individual opinion remains in \([0,1]\): initially this holds by assumption, and each subsequent sampled value and update is a convex combination of values in this interval. Thus all expectations are well-defined.

For every vector \(\mathbf v\), nonnegativity and the row-sum assumptions give

\(\displaystyle |(A\mathbf v)_t|\le\sum_u A_{tu}|v_u|\le(1-\beta_t)\|\mathbf v\|_\infty\le(1-b)\|\mathbf v\|_\infty.\)

Consequently, the induced matrix norm satisfies \(\|A\|_\infty\le 1-b<1\). The series

\(\displaystyle \mathbf x^*=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A^kB\mathbf q\)

therefore converges and satisfies \(\mathbf x^*=A\mathbf x^*+B\mathbf q\). If two vectors satisfy this equation, their difference \(\mathbf d\) satisfies \(\|\mathbf d\|_\infty\le(1-b)\|\mathbf d\|_\infty\), forcing \(\mathbf d=\mathbf 0\). Hence the fixed point is unique.

Moreover, \(F(\mathbf z)=A\mathbf z+B\mathbf q\) maps \([0,1]^\tau\) into itself: each coordinate is nonnegative and at most \((1-\beta_t)+\beta_t=1\). Its iterates from \(\mathbf 0\) are the partial sums of the displayed series. Their limit therefore belongs to \([0,1]^\tau\), giving

\(\|\mathbf q-\mathbf x^*\|_\infty\le 1.\)

2. Exact recurrence for expected opinions

For a type-\(t\) member, let \(S_t(r)\) denote her sampled value in round \(r\). By the specified sampling rule,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[S_t(r)]=\sum_u A_{tu}y_u(r-1)+\beta_tq_t.\)

This includes the term with \(u=t\); same-type sampling causes no exception. Taking expectations in the update yields

\(\displaystyle \mathbf y(r)=\frac{rI+A}{r+1}\mathbf y(r-1)+\frac{B\mathbf q}{r+1},\qquad \mathbf y(0)=\mathbf q.\)

Define \(\mathbf e(r)=\mathbf y(r)-\mathbf x^*\). Subtracting the fixed-point equation gives

\(\displaystyle \mathbf e(r)=\frac{rI+A}{r+1}\mathbf e(r-1).\)

The matrix \((rI+A)/(r+1)\) is nonnegative, and its type-\(t\) row sum is

\(\displaystyle \frac{r+1-\beta_t}{r+1}=1-\frac{\beta_t}{r+1}\le 1-\frac{b}{r+1}.\)

Applying the same row-sum norm inequality as above and iterating, we obtain

\(\displaystyle \|\mathbf e(r)\|_\infty\le\prod_{k=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{k+1}\right)\|\mathbf e(0)\|_\infty\le\prod_{k=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{k+1}\right).\)

The corresponding finite-agent expectation recurrence appears in Section 3 of [Fotakis, Palyvos-Giannas, and Skoulakis](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/16/Papers/047.pdf). Here it follows directly from the specified type-distribution sampling rule.

3. Explicit decay estimate

For \(0\le z<1\), the elementary inequality \(\log(1-z)\le-z\) follows from \(\log(1-z)=-\int_0^z(1-u)^{-1}\,du\). Therefore, for \(r\ge1\),

\(\displaystyle \prod_{k=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{k+1}\right)\le\exp\left(-b\sum_{k=1}^{r}\frac1{k+1}\right).\)

Since the function \(x\mapsto 1/x\) decreases on the positive real numbers,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{r}\frac1{k+1}=\sum_{j=2}^{r+1}\frac1j\ge\int_2^{r+2}\frac{dx}{x}=\log\frac{r+2}{2}.\)

Combining these inequalities proves

\(\displaystyle \|\mathbf y(r)-\mathbf x^*\|_\infty\le\left(\frac{2}{r+2}\right)^b.\)

This also holds at \(r=0\), because its right-hand side is \(1\).

If \(\epsilon\ge1\), boundedness immediately makes \(R(\epsilon)=0\) valid. Otherwise, \(2\epsilon^{-1/b}>2\), so the proposed \(R(\epsilon)\) is a nonnegative integer. For every integer \(r\ge R(\epsilon)\),

\(\displaystyle r+2\ge\left\lceil2\epsilon^{-1/b}\right\rceil\ge2\epsilon^{-1/b},\)

and hence

\(\displaystyle \|\mathbf y(r)-\mathbf x^*\|_\infty\le\left(\frac2{r+2}\right)^b\le\epsilon.\)

This proves the required guarantee for every subsequent round.

4. Computational complexity

The algorithm performs only the following operations:

  • Compute \(\beta_t=1-\sum_u A_{tu}\) if these weights are not separately supplied.
  • Compute the positive rational \(b=\min_t\beta_t\).
  • Compare \(\epsilon\) with \(1\) and output the appropriate expression for \(R(\epsilon)\).

Exact rational sums, comparisons, and reciprocals take polynomial time in the total binary input length \(L\). Their intermediate encoding lengths are polynomial: a common denominator for any row sum can be chosen as the product of that row’s input denominators, whose binary length is bounded by their total encoding length.

The output contains only polynomial-length rational constants, one rational-power operation, one ceiling, and arithmetic. The algorithm outputs this expression without evaluating or expanding the resulting integer. That is precisely the succinct representation permitted in the question.

Thus the stated computational problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(\tau\) and \(L\), with \(m=1\). A large numerical round bound does not imply hardness of producing its succinct expression.
