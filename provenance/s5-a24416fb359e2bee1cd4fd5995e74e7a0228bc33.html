The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many types \(T\) with class labels and compatible item categories, let the society be a rational mass distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\). Items arrive online as a finite sequence of categories with per-capita supplies \(w_o\); at each arrival the policy learns that arrival and its compatible types but not future arrivals, and irrevocably assigns masses \(x_{t,o}\geq 0\), subject to \(\sum_t x_{t,o}\leq w_o\) and \(\sum_o x_{t,o}\leq\mu_t\). Define USW as \(\sum_{t,o}x_{t,o}\), compare it with the offline maximum flow, and define \(V_i^*(Y_j)\) by optimally rematching the item mass assigned to class \(j\) to class \(i\)'s types. For every \(\alpha\in(0,1]\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), does there exist such an instance on which every non-wasteful online policy satisfying \(E[V_i]\geq\alpha E[V_i^*(Y_j)]\) for all class pairs has expected USW at most \((1/(1+\alpha)+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), partitioned into \(k\geq2\) nonempty classes \(T_i\). Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
There are \(m\) arrivals. Arrival \(o\) reveals its category, positive binary-rational supply \(w_o\), and compatible types \(C_o\); future arrivals and the stopping time are unknown. Compatibility gives value one per unit assigned, and incompatibility gives value zero.
A possibly randomized online policy irrevocably chooses real masses \(x_{t,o}\geq0\), with \(x_{t,o}=0\) for \(t\notin C_o\), \(\sum_t x_{t,o}\leq w_o\), and \(\sum_o x_{t,o}\leq\mu_t\). Almost surely, after each arrival, any unassigned supply has no compatible type with residual capacity; this is non-wastefulness.
Write \(V_i(X)=\sum_{t\in T_i,o}x_{t,o}\) and \(Y_{j,o}(X)=\sum_{t\in T_j}x_{t,o}\). Define \(V_i^*(y)\) as the maximum compatible flow to types in \(T_i\), using their full capacities \(\mu_t\) and arrival capacities \(y_o\).
An \(\alpha\)-CEF policy guarantees \(\mathbb E[V_i(X)]\geq\alpha\mathbb E[V_i^*(Y_j(X))]\) for every class pair on every finite input, including every possible stopping prefix. Expectations concern only policy randomness.
Let \(\mathrm{USW}(X)=\sum_iV_i(X)\), and let \(\mathrm{OPT}(I)>0\) be the offline maximum compatible flow. Inputs are fixed independently of realized random choices. Let \(L\) be total input bit length; state any running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\) and other relevant parameters, although policies here have no running-time restriction.

For every \(\alpha\in(0,1]\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), does there exist an input \(I\) such that every non-wasteful online \(\alpha\)-CEF policy satisfies \(\mathbb E[\mathrm{USW}(X)]\leq(1/(1+\alpha)+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}(I)\)?

For finite indivisible matching, the corresponding competitive-ratio upper bound is \(1/(1+\alpha)+\varepsilon\). [Hajiaghayi et al.](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/20ceffa1fcec0f882868e5b891e3e7fa-Paper-Conference.pdf)
For finite unit-capacity divisible matching, a later result bounds randomized non-wasteful \(\alpha\)-CEF policies by \((1+\alpha-e^{\alpha-1})/(1+\alpha)\); it does not formulate the support-encoded rational-mass problem above. [Borst and Springer](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23408v1#S4.SS2)

Partial resolution and a globally valid lottery obstruction

The requested statement is proved here for randomized policies when $\alpha=1$, and for deterministic policies for every $\alpha\in(0,1]$.

For $0<\alpha<1$, the main randomized question remains open. The new result is a sufficient condition for extending a randomized allocation prefix to a globally valid $\alpha$-CEF policy. Applying it shows that the earlier lottery obstruction persists even under the requirement that the policy be fair on every input.

1. A common rational-mass construction

Choose an integer $r\geq1$ and put
$$ n=2^r-1,\qquad q=2^{-r-1}. $$
There are $n$ small classes, each containing one type of mass $q$, and one large class containing one type of mass $(n+2)q$. Thus
$$ k=\tau=n+1,\qquad nq+(n+2)q=1. $$
These are supported types with positive dyadic masses.

For a dyadic supply $S$ satisfying
$$ q\leq S\leq(n+1)q, $$
the input consists of:

  • One arrival of supply $S$, compatible with every type.
  • One arrival of supply $q$ for each small type, compatible only with that type.

Categories may all be identical. Write $A_i$ for the first arrival’s allocation to small class $i$, and $Z$ for its allocation to the large class.

Exact identities

Non-wastefulness forces the first arrival to be fully assigned. Indeed, if some supply remained, the allocated mass would be less than $S<1$, so some compatible type would retain capacity. Consequently,
$$ Z+\sum_{i=1}^n A_i=S,\qquad 0\leq A_i\leq q $$
almost surely.

At the stopping prefix containing only this arrival, small class $i$ has optimistic valuation
$$ V_i^*(Y_{\mathrm{large}})=\min\{q,Z\}. $$
Therefore every randomized $\alpha$-CEF policy satisfies
$$ \mathbb E[A_i]\geq\alpha\,\mathbb E[\min\{q,Z\}]. $$

The private arrival for small type $i$ must allocate exactly its remaining capacity $q-A_i$. Thus
$$ \mathrm{USW}(X)=nq+Z $$
almost surely.

Offline, all $S$ units of the first arrival can be assigned to the large type, whose capacity exceeds $S$. Every private arrival can then be fully assigned. Hence
$$ \mathrm{OPT}(I)=nq+S>0. $$

All these identities hold regardless of allocation precision or running time.

2. Proven common-input upper bounds

Randomized policies: a weaker bound, and the requested endpoint

Set
$$ S=(n+1)q=\frac12. $$
The small types can receive at most $nq$ from the first arrival, so $Z\geq q$ almost surely. The fairness inequalities consequently become
$$ \mathbb E[A_i]\geq\alpha q. $$
Summing gives
$$ \mathbb E[Z]\leq(n+1)q-\alpha nq. $$
Using the exact welfare and optimum identities,
$$ \frac{\mathbb E[\mathrm{USW}(X)]}{\mathrm{OPT}(I)} \leq \frac{(2-\alpha)n+1}{2n+1} = 1-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2(2n+1)}. $$
Choosing $n$ of the prescribed form with $n\geq1/(4\varepsilon)$ proves the common-input bound
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{USW}(X)] \leq \left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}+\varepsilon\right)\mathrm{OPT}(I). $$

At $\alpha=1$, this is precisely the requested conclusion:
$$ \frac{\mathbb E[\mathrm{USW}(X)]}{\mathrm{OPT}(I)} \leq \frac12+\frac{1}{4n+2}. $$

For $0<\alpha<1$, however,
$$ 1-\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{1}{1+\alpha} = \frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{2(1+\alpha)}>0. $$
Thus this argument does not settle the main question.

Deterministic policies: the requested bound for every $\alpha$

Choose a dyadic $\beta$ with $0\leq\beta\leq\alpha$ and set
$$ S=(\beta n+1)q. $$
For a deterministic policy, first-prefix fairness gives
$$ A_i\geq\alpha\min\{q,Z\}. $$
If $Z>q$, these inequalities imply
$$ Z = S-\sum_i A_i \leq (\beta n+1)q-\alpha nq \leq q, $$
a contradiction. Hence $Z\leq q$, and therefore
$$ \frac{\mathrm{USW}(X)}{\mathrm{OPT}(I)} \leq \frac{n+1}{(1+\beta)n+1}. $$
The difference from the target is
$$ \frac{n+1}{(1+\beta)n+1}-\frac{1}{1+\alpha} = \frac{(\alpha-\beta)n+\alpha} {(1+\alpha)((1+\beta)n+1)} \leq (\alpha-\beta)+\frac1n. $$
Choose $\alpha-\beta\leq\varepsilon/2$ and $n\geq2/\varepsilon$. This establishes the requested common-input bound for deterministic policies.

3. A sufficient condition for globally fair continuation

This section concerns societies in which each class contains exactly one type. Capacities may differ.

For such a class $i$, with capacity $\mu_i$, the optimistic valuation of a bundle is
$$ V_i^*(Y_j) = \min\left\{ \mu_i,\, \sum_{o:\,i\in C_o}Y_{j,o} \right\}. $$
To justify this formula, the displayed sum is the total compatible supply in the bundle. A flow to one type is bounded by both this sum and $\mu_i$, and the smaller bound is achievable by distributing flow among the compatible arrivals.

Definition: absolute-load water-filling

The load of a type is its cumulative allocated mass, without normalization by its capacity.

For each arrival, continuously allocate its supply among compatible, unsaturated types having minimum current load. Divide equally among tied minimum-load types. Continue until the arrival is fully assigned or all compatible types are saturated.

This rule is non-wasteful.

Lemma: continuation from a randomized prefix

Suppose a fixed arrival prefix has produced a random feasible allocation. Let
$$ A_i=V_i(X_{\mathrm{prefix}}), \qquad E_{ij}=V_i^*(Y_j(X_{\mathrm{prefix}})). $$
Assume, for every ordered class pair,
$$ \mathbb E[A_i] \geq \alpha\,\mathbb E[\max\{A_i,E_{ij}\}]. $$
Then continuing with absolute-load water-filling is $\alpha$-CEF at every subsequent stopping prefix, for every fixed continuation.

Proof

Fix an outcome of the prefix randomness and a subsequent stopping prefix. Let $F_i$ be class $i$’s load at that stopping prefix.

We first prove
$$ V_i^*(Y_j(X_{\mathrm{final}})) \leq \max\{E_{ij},F_i\}. $$

If $i$ is saturated, its optimistic valuation is at most $\mu_i=F_i$, so the inequality holds.

Otherwise, $i$ remains unsaturated throughout the continuation. Whenever $j$ receives mass from a subsequent arrival compatible with $i$, water-filling gives
$$ \text{current load of }j \leq \text{current load of }i \leq F_i. $$
Let $B_{ij}$ be the total such mass received by $j$. The load of $j$ increases monotonically from $A_j$. All increments counted in $B_{ij}$ occur while its load is at most $F_i$. Their total therefore satisfies
$$ B_{ij}\leq\max\{0,F_i-A_j\}. $$

The initial compatible supply in $j$’s bundle is at most $A_j$, so $E_{ij}\leq A_j$. Adding subsequent compatible supply can increase its capped valuation by at most $B_{ij}$. Thus
$$ V_i^*(Y_j(X_{\mathrm{final}})) \leq E_{ij}+B_{ij} \leq E_{ij}+\max\{0,F_i-A_j\}. $$
If $F_i\leq A_j$, the last expression equals $E_{ij}$. If $F_i>A_j$, it is at most $F_i$, because $E_{ij}\leq A_j$. This proves the claimed bound.

Since $F_i\geq A_i$,
$$ \max\{E_{ij},F_i\} \leq \max\{E_{ij},A_i\}+(F_i-A_i). $$
Taking expectations and applying the hypothesis,
$$ \begin{aligned} \alpha\,\mathbb E[V_i^*(Y_j(X_{\mathrm{final}}))] &\leq \alpha\,\mathbb E[\max\{E_{ij},F_i\}]\\ &\leq \mathbb E[A_i]+\alpha\,\mathbb E[F_i-A_i]\\ &\leq \mathbb E[F_i]. \end{aligned} $$
The last inequality uses $\alpha\leq1$. This proves fairness at every subsequent stopping prefix. $\square$

Nonvacuity

From an empty prefix, $A_i=E_{ij}=0$, so the lemma applies with $\alpha=1$. Consequently, absolute-load water-filling is a non-wasteful $1$-CEF policy on every input for every singleton-class society.

Thus the societies used in Section 1 admit globally valid policies, including at $\alpha=1$.

4. Globally valid lotteries defeat the deterministic construction

Fix $0<\alpha<1$. Choose dyadic parameters satisfying
$$ \frac{\alpha}{2}\leq\beta<\alpha, \qquad \frac{1-\alpha}{8}\leq p\leq\frac{1-\alpha}{4}. $$
Such choices exist by density of dyadic rationals.

Choose $n=2^r-1$ sufficiently large that
$$ \frac1n\leq\alpha-\beta, \qquad n(1-\alpha)p\geq\alpha(1-p). $$
Use the society from Section 1 and put
$$ S=(\beta n+1)q,\qquad s=\frac{S}{nq}=\beta+\frac1n,\qquad a=\frac Sn=sq. $$
Then
$$ \frac{\alpha}{2}\leq s\leq\alpha<1, $$
so $a\leq q$.

Define an online policy as follows.

  • If the first arrival has supply $S$ and is compatible with every type, assign all of it to the large type with probability $p$. With probability $1-p$, assign $a$ to every small type.
  • After that arrival, use absolute-load water-filling.
  • If the first arrival does not meet the specified condition, use absolute-load water-filling from the beginning.

This policy uses only revealed information. Both lottery outcomes are feasible and assign the entire first supply.

Verification of the continuation condition

It remains to check the lemma’s hypothesis after the special first arrival.

For a small class $i$ and the large class:

  • In the large-allocation outcome, $A_i=0$ and $E_{i,\mathrm{large}}=q$.
  • In the small-allocation outcome, $A_i=a$ and $E_{i,\mathrm{large}}=0$.

Hence
$$ \mathbb E[A_i]=(1-p)a, $$
and
$$ \mathbb E[\max\{A_i,E_{i,\mathrm{large}}\}] =pq+(1-p)a. $$
The required inequality is equivalent to
$$ (1-\alpha)(1-p)s\geq\alpha p. $$
Because $s\geq\alpha/2$, it suffices that
$$ (1-\alpha)(1-p)\geq2p. $$
This is equivalent to
$$ p(3-\alpha)\leq1-\alpha, $$
which follows from $p\leq(1-\alpha)/4$ and $3-\alpha<4$.

For the large class and a small class, the corresponding quantities are
$$ \mathbb E[A_{\mathrm{large}}]=pS, $$
and
$$ \mathbb E[\max\{A_{\mathrm{large}},E_{\mathrm{large},i}\}] =pS+(1-p)a. $$
The required inequality is equivalent to
$$ (1-\alpha)pS\geq\alpha(1-p)a. $$
Since $S=na$, this is exactly the imposed condition
$$ n(1-\alpha)p\geq\alpha(1-p). $$

For two small classes, their first allocations coincide in each outcome, and each values the other’s first bundle at that same amount. Their continuation inequalities therefore hold. Self-pairs also hold.

The lemma now proves that this policy is non-wasteful and $\alpha$-CEF on every finite input on this society, including every stopping prefix.

Welfare on the deterministic upper-bound input

On the input consisting of the special first arrival followed by the private arrivals, every small type finishes full, while the large type retains its first allocation. Therefore
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{USW}(X)]=nq+pS, $$
and
$$ \frac{\mathbb E[\mathrm{USW}(X)]}{\mathrm{OPT}(I)} = \frac{1+ps}{1+s}. $$
Since $s\leq\alpha$,
$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{1+ps}{1+s}-\frac1{1+\alpha} &= \left(\frac1{1+s}-\frac1{1+\alpha}\right) +\frac{ps}{1+s}\\ &\geq \frac{p\beta}{1+\alpha}\\ &\geq \frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{16(1+\alpha)}>0. \end{aligned} $$

Thus, for every $0<\alpha<1$, this version of the deterministic two-phase construction admits a globally valid randomized policy whose ratio exceeds the target by a positive amount independent of $n$. Moreover, $\beta$ may be chosen arbitrarily close to $\alpha$.

This closes the scope gap in the earlier lottery example: global validity does not restore the deterministic argument.

Pathwise comparison
