The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(B,\delta\), a finite family \(\mathcal K\) of \(\delta\)-approximate candidate sets, rational masses \(\mu_{K,\theta}\) on types \((K,\theta)\) with \(\theta\in K\), and a target \(\alpha\), compute whether the \(N\to\infty\) limit of \(M_{\mathrm{opt}}^{(\delta)}\)—with each bidder's allocation probability and expected payment multiplied by \(N\)—has welfare ratio at least \(\alpha\) for every undominated bid assignment; characterize the limiting allocation and payment intensities and undominated bids as part of the computation.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Input: an integer \(B\geq1\), binary rationals \(\delta\in(0,1)\) and \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), and explicitly listed nonempty sets \(K\subseteq\{0,\ldots,B\}\), each contained in \([(1-\delta)c,(1+\delta)c]\) for some \(c\geq0\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \(t=(K,\theta)\), with \(\theta\in K\), having positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\). Require \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\) and \(H=\max_t\theta>0\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(m=B+1\) and \(L\) be the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
For \(N\geq2\) bidders, pure bids are \(\{0,\ldots,B\}\). Put \(D=((1+\delta)/(1-\delta))^2-1\). For any nonzero real bid vector \(z\in[0,B]^N\), let \(T\) be the unique nonnegative solution of \(\sum_i(z_i-T)_+=DT\), where \((a)_+=\max\{a,0\}\).
The allocation probability is \(f_{N,i}(z)=\frac{N+D}{ND}(1-T/z_i)\) when \(z_i>T\), and zero otherwise. At the all-zero vector set \(f_{N,i}=1/N\). Expected payment is \(p_{N,i}(v)=v_i f_{N,i}(v)-\int_0^{v_i}f_{N,i}(s,v_{-i})\,ds\); any remaining allocation probability leaves the good unallocated.
A bid \(b\) is undominated for \(K\) if no probability distribution over alternative integer bids gives utility \(\vartheta f_{N,i}-p_{N,i}\) at least as large for every \(\vartheta\in K\) and every opponents' integer bid vector, with strict improvement for at least one such pair. Write this set as \(U_N(K)\), and define \(U_\infty(K)=\{b:\exists N_0\ \forall N\geq N_0,\ b\in U_N(K)\}\).
An admissible mass assignment consists of real numbers \(x_{t,b}\geq0\) with \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\), supported on \(b\in U_\infty(K)\). Replicate it using integers \(n_{t,b}^{(N)}\) with \(\sum_b n_{t,b}^{(N)}=N\mu_t\), \(n_{t,b}^{(N)}/N\to x_{t,b}\), and \(n_{t,b}^{(N)}=0\) whenever \(x_{t,b}=0\), along population sizes clearing the mass denominators.
For a bidder in a positive-mass cell, define allocation and payment intensities by \(A_{t,b}(x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}Nf_{N,i}\) and \(P_{t,b}(x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}Np_{N,i}\). The welfare ratio is \(R(x)=H^{-1}\sum_{t,b}\theta x_{t,b}A_{t,b}(x)\).
What are \(U_\infty(K)\) and the limiting intensities, including whether the limits exist independently of the replication sequence? Is deciding whether \(R(x)\geq\alpha\) for every admissible assignment polynomial-time solvable or NP-hard?
For finite populations, this mechanism guarantees welfare ratio at least \(\frac{(1-\delta)^2+4\delta/N}{(1+\delta)^2}\); its allocation probabilities and expected payments admit efficient evaluation, the latter using symbolic integration. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.1147).
No result settling this precise mass-assignment threshold problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Retained integer valuations and pure bids from zero through B; only population masses become continuous. Candidate sets are explicitly listed, nonempty, and delta-approximate.
  • Normalized total population mass to one, kept B and delta fixed as N grows, and made delta and alpha binary-rational inputs.
  • Excluded societies with zero maximum true valuation so that the welfare ratio has a positive denominator.
  • Completed the undefined all-zero allocation profile by assigning uniformly at zero payment, consistent with the stated behavior at equal bids.
  • Defined limiting undominated bids by eventual finite-population undominance, retaining mixed dominators and unrestricted opponents' bids rather than recomputing dominance after taking limits.
  • Allowed types to split their mass among bids. Defined replication by convergent cell frequencies with no bidders in zero-mass cells, excluding vanishing exceptional bid groups that the mass assignment does not record.
  • Defined the welfare benchmark as the maximum true valuation in the positive-mass support and the limiting welfare through allocation intensities.
  • Set m=B+1 because this auction has one good and no candidate-count parameter; L includes the explicitly encoded candidate sets and numerical inputs.

Result

The threshold problem is polynomial-time solvable. The undominated bids eventually form an integer interval, and the limiting welfare depends only on the highest bid receiving positive mass.

Write \(a_t=\min K_t\), \(h_t=\max K_t\), and \(M=\max_t a_t\). Then

\(U_\infty(K)=\{\min K,\min K+1,\ldots,\max K\}\).

For an admissible assignment, define \(q=\max\{b:x_{t,b}>0\text{ for some }t\}\) and \(s=\sum_t x_{t,q}\). Every positive-mass cell has sequence-independent limits

\(A_{t,b}(x)=\mathbf 1_{\{b=q\}}/s,\qquad P_{t,b}(x)=q\,\mathbf 1_{\{b=q\}}/s.\)

Consequently,

\(R(x)=\dfrac{\sum_t\theta_t x_{t,q}}{Hs}.\)

Here is an explicit decision criterion. Set \(c=\alpha H\), \(F=\{t:a_t=M\}\), \(O=\{t:a_t<M\le h_t\}\), and

\(\Gamma(c)=\sum_{t\in F}\mu_t(\theta_t-c)+\sum_{t\in O}\mu_t\min\{\theta_t-c,0\}.\)

Then \(R(x)\ge\alpha\) for every admissible assignment if and only if

  • \(\Gamma(c)\ge0\);
  • \(\theta_t\ge c\) for every type satisfying \(h_t>M\).

These conditions require only \(O(\tau)\) exact arithmetic operations after reading the input. All results below are proved directly from the definitions; no additional theorem from the referenced [source paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.1147) is needed.

1. Monotonicity and separating opponent profiles

Fix \(N\), a bidder, and opponents’ bids \(w_1,\ldots,w_{N-1}\). Write \(g(v)=f_{N,i}(v,w)\).

For \(v>0\), put \(y=T/v\). Its defining equation becomes

\((1-y)_++\sum_j(w_j/v-y)_+=Dy.\)

The left side minus the right side is continuous and strictly decreasing in \(y\), so its root is unique. Increasing \(v\) decreases every \(w_j/v\); therefore the root \(y\) cannot increase. Since

\(g(v)=\dfrac{N+D}{ND}(1-y)_+,\)

\(g\) is nondecreasing on positive bids.

This remains true at zero. If some opponent bids positively, \(g(0)=0\). If all opponents bid zero, then

\(g(0)=1/N,\qquad g(v)=\dfrac{N+D}{N(D+1)}\quad(v>0),\)

and the latter quantity is at least \(1/N\).

The utility at valuation \(\vartheta\) is

\(u_\vartheta(v)=(\vartheta-v)g(v)+\int_0^v g(z)\,dz.\)

Monotonicity gives the following two identities and inequalities:

  • For \(b<a\) and \(\vartheta\ge a\),

\(u_\vartheta(a)-u_\vartheta(b)=(\vartheta-a)(g(a)-g(b))+\int_b^a(g(z)-g(b))\,dz\ge0.\)

  • For \(b>h\) and \(\vartheta\le h\),

\(u_\vartheta(h)-u_\vartheta(b)=(h-\vartheta)(g(b)-g(h))+\int_h^b(g(b)-g(z))\,dz\ge0.\)

We also need profiles that distinguish adjacent integer bids. Let all \(n=N-1\) opponents bid an integer \(q\ge1\), and define

\(r_q=\dfrac{nq}{n+D}.\)

For an own bid \(v\le r_q\), the threshold is exactly \(r_q\), so \(g(v)=0\). For \(v>r_q\), evaluating the threshold equation at \(T=v\) gives a negative residual, so its root is below \(v\); hence \(g(v)>0\).

Because \(g\) vanishes below \(r_q\) and is nondecreasing,

\(p(v)=vg(v)-\int_0^v g(z)\,dz\ge r_qg(v)\qquad(v>r_q).\)

Thus \(u_\vartheta(v)<0\) whenever \(\vartheta<r_q<v\).

Finally, if \(n>DB\), then for every integer \(q\in\{1,\ldots,B\}\),

\(r_q-(q-1)=\dfrac{n-D(q-1)}{n+D}>0.\)

This one population bound supplies all the separating profiles used next.

2. Exact eventual undominated bids

Let \(a=\min K\) and \(h=\max K\). We prove that whenever \(N-1>DB\),

\(U_N(K)=\{a,a+1,\ldots,h\}.\)

Bids outside the interval are dominated

If \(b<a\), the first utility inequality shows that bid \(a\) weakly improves utility for every valuation in \(K\) and every opponent profile.

For strict improvement, let all opponents bid \(a\), and take valuation \(a\). Here \(r_a>a-1\ge b\), so bid \(b\) gives zero utility. Bid \(a\) gives

\(u_a(a)=\int_0^a g(z)\,dz>0,\)

because \(r_a<a\) and \(g\) is positive above \(r_a\). Thus \(a\) dominates \(b\).

If \(b>h\), the second utility inequality shows that \(h\) weakly improves utility everywhere. Let all opponents bid \(b\), and take valuation \(h\). Since \(r_b>b-1\ge h\), bid \(h\) gives zero utility, whereas

\(u_h(b)\le(h-r_b)g(b)<0.\)

Thus \(h\) dominates \(b\).

Bids inside the interval resist mixed domination

Take \(b\in\{a,\ldots,h\}\), and suppose a distribution \(\sigma\) weakly dominates \(b\).

First suppose \(b\ge1\). If \(b<B\), let all opponents bid \(b+1\), and take valuation \(a\). The cutoff satisfies \(r_{b+1}>b\ge a\). Every bid at most \(b\) gives zero utility, while every bid above \(b\) gives strictly negative utility. Therefore domination requires

\(\sigma(\{b+1,\ldots,B\})=0.\)

For \(b=B\), this support restriction holds automatically.

Now let all opponents bid \(b\), and take valuation \(h\). Since \(r_b>b-1\), every integer bid below \(b\) gives zero utility. Bid \(b\) gives strictly positive utility:

\(u_h(b)=(h-b)g(b)+\int_0^b g(z)\,dz>0.\)

A distribution supported on bids at most \(b\) can match this utility only if \(\sigma(b)=1\). Such a distribution cannot give strict improvement anywhere.

If \(b=0\), then \(a=0\). Let all opponents bid \(1\), and take valuation \(0\). Bid zero gives zero utility, while every positive integer bid gives strictly negative utility. Again, domination forces probability one on \(b\).

Thus every bid in the interval is undominated. In particular, the equality holds for all \(N\ge\lfloor DB\rfloor+2\), proving

\(U_\infty(K)=\{\min K,\ldots,\max K\}.\)

3. Replication and sequence-independent intensities

First, every admissible real assignment has a replication of the required form. In each type, choose one positive-mass cell. Along population sizes clearing the mass denominators, round down \(Nx_{t,b}\) in the other positive cells and assign the remaining integer count to the chosen cell. Keep zero-mass cells empty. Each rounding error is bounded independently of \(N\), so the normalized counts converge to \(x\).

The multiplicative containment assumption implies that a set containing zero must be \(\{0\}\): the inequality \(0\ge(1-\delta)c\) forces \(c=0\). Since \(H>0\), some type consequently has \(a_t\ge1\). Hence \(M\ge1\), and every admissible assignment has a positive highest occupied bid \(q\).

Fix any permitted replication sequence, and let \(C_N\) be the total number bidding \(q\). Then

\(C_N/N\longrightarrow s>0.\)

The support-preservation requirement ensures that nobody bids above \(q\). All lower integer bids are at most \(q-1\). For sufficiently large \(N\),

\(T_N=\dfrac{C_Nq}{C_N+D}>q-1.\)

This value solves the threshold equation with exactly the bidders at \(q\) active. Therefore, for each such bidder,

\(f_{N,i}=\dfrac{N+D}{N(C_N+D)},\qquad Nf_{N,i}\longrightarrow1/s.\)

Every lower bidder has allocation zero eventually.

For a bidder at \(q\), remove that bidder when examining the payment integral. The opponents alone have cutoff

\(\rho_N=\dfrac{(C_N-1)q}{C_N-1+D}>q-1\)

for all sufficiently large \(N\). Own bids at most \(\rho_N\) receive zero allocation. Monotonicity therefore gives

\(0\le N\int_0^q f_{N,i}(v,v_{-i})\,dv\le(q-\rho_N)Nf_{N,i}(q,v_{-i}).\)

Here \(q-\rho_N=Dq/(C_N-1+D)\to0\), while \(Nf_{N,i}(q,v_{-i})\to1/s\). The integral contribution vanishes, proving

\(Np_{N,i}\longrightarrow q/s.\)

For a lower bidder, every own bid in the payment integral is also below the eventual threshold. Both allocation and payment are therefore exactly zero eventually.

These arguments use only \(C_N/N\to s\) and the absence of bids above \(q\). Thus the limits are independent of the replication sequence, and

\(R(x)=\dfrac{\sum_t\theta_t x_{t,q}}{H\sum_t x_{t,q}}.\)

4. Proof of the decision criterion

Every admissible assignment has \(q\ge M\), because a type attaining lower endpoint \(M\) has positive mass and can use only bids at least \(M\).

Highest occupied bid equal to \(M\)

If \(q=M\), every type in \(F\) must place its entire mass at \(M\). A type can additionally place mass at \(M\) precisely when it belongs to \(O\).

Write \(y_t=x_{t,M}\) for \(t\in O\). Each \(y_t\) can independently range over \([0,\mu_t]\): its remaining mass can bid \(a_t<M\). All types with \(h_t<M\) also bid below \(M\).

Since \(F\) is nonempty and has positive total mass, the denominator is positive. Thus \(R(x)\ge\alpha\) is equivalent to

\(\sum_{t\in F}\mu_t(\theta_t-c)+\sum_{t\in O}y_t(\theta_t-c)\ge0.\)

Minimizing this expression independently over \(0\le y_t\le\mu_t\) gives exactly \(\Gamma(c)\). Therefore the universal threshold condition among assignments with \(q=M\) is precisely \(\Gamma(c)\ge0\).

If this condition fails, a violating assignment places all types in \(F\), and all types in \(O\) with \(\theta_t<c\), at bid \(M\). Every other type bids its lower endpoint. This assignment attains the negative expression \(\Gamma(c)\).

Highest occupied bid above \(M\)

If \(q>M\), every type bidding \(q\) satisfies \(h_t\ge q>M\). Hence, if all such types satisfy \(\theta_t\ge c\), their weighted average valuation is at least \(c\), giving \(R(x)\ge\alpha\).

Conversely, suppose some type has \(h_t>M\) and \(\theta_t<c\). Place its entire mass at \(M+1\), and let every other type bid its lower endpoint. This is admissible because

\(a_t\le M<M+1\le h_t.\)

The selected type is the only type at the highest bid, so the resulting welfare ratio is \(\theta_t/H<\alpha\).

Together, these two cases prove both directions of the stated criterion.

5. Running time

The extrema of the explicitly listed sets, \(H\), and \(M\) can be found by scanning the input. The criterion then requires \(O(\tau)\) arithmetic operations.

For an explicit bit bound, write \(\alpha=A/d\), let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of the masses, and set \(w_t=Q\mu_t\). The sign of \(\Gamma(c)\) is the sign of the integer

\(G=\sum_{t\in F}w_t(d\theta_t-AH)+\sum_{t\in O}w_t\min\{d\theta_t-AH,0\}.\)

The other condition is checked by comparing \(d\theta_t\) with \(AH\). All integers involved have \(O(L)\) bits. Elementary integer arithmetic therefore gives a bound of \(O(L+\tau L^2)\) bit operations.

The undominated sets can be represented by their endpoints in \(O(L)\) time. Explicitly expanding them adds \(O(\tau m\log m)\) output time. In particular, the threshold decision is polynomial in \(L\), and therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
