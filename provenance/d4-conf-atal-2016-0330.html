The continuous mirror question, as graded

Input schools \(C\), types \(T\) with binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), strict rankings \(\succ_t\), initial schools \(\omega(t)\), and ordered priority blocks with each type contiguous in the induced individual master list, plus rational quotas \(p_c,q_c\) satisfying initial feasibility. Can the paper’s representative, dummy-student, school-state, and cycle rules be batched to compute the exact allocation matrix \(x_{t,c}\) in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and does that allocation equal the aggregate TTCR-SS outcome on every integral replication of the instance?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq1\) schools. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a strict ranking \(\succ_t\) over \(C\), an initial school \(\omega(t)\), and a position in a strict ordering of contiguous type blocks. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Quotas are binary rationals satisfying \(0\leq p_c\leq\sum_{t:\omega(t)=c}\mu_t\leq q_c\). An allocation has \(x_{t,c}\geq0\), \(\sum_c x_{t,c}=\mu_t\), \(p_c\leq\sum_t x_{t,c}\leq q_c\), and \(x_{t,c}=0\) whenever \(\omega(t)\succ_t c\).
An integral replication at scale \(K\) uses \(K\mu_t\) identical students of type \(t\) and quotas \(Kp_c,Kq_c\), all integers; its strict master list concatenates the ordered type blocks.
Define TTCR-SS on a replication as follows. Initially all students remain and no assignments are finalized. Write \(r_c\) for remaining students initially endowed with \(c\), and \(z_c\) for finalized assignments to \(c\).
A school is minimum if \(r_c>0\) and \(r_c+z_c=Kp_c\), decrementable if \(r_c>0\) and \(r_c+z_c>Kp_c\), maximum if \(r_c=0\) and \(z_c=Kq_c\), and incrementable if \(r_c=0\) and \(z_c<Kq_c\).
Each school with \(r_c>0\) supplies its highest-priority remaining endowed student as representative; each incrementable school supplies a dummy representative only when some school is decrementable. Maximum schools supply none.
Each real representative points to the representative of her favorite represented school. Every dummy points to the highest-priority real representative whose initial school is decrementable.
Execute all directed cycles simultaneously, including self-loops: assign each real student in a cycle to the school of her outgoing neighbor and remove her from the remaining students; dummies receive no assignment. Recompute and repeat until no students remain.
Let \(D\) be the least positive integer clearing all mass and quota denominators, and define \(x_{t,c}\) as the number of type-\(t\) students assigned to \(c\) at scale \(D\), divided by \(D\).
Is exact computation of \(x\) polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard? Does the normalized aggregate TTCR-SS outcome equal \(x\) for every integral replication scale \(K\)?
Discrete TTCR-SS has an \(O(nm)\) algorithm for \(n\) explicitly listed students, also stated in the [2017 full version](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/316567382_Strategy-proof_School_Choice_Mechanisms_with_Minimum_Quotas_and_Initial_Endowments). No result settling this exact rational-mass computation or replication agreement was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to 1; quotas use the same mass units.
  • Interpreted ordered contiguous priority blocks as a strict total ordering of types, with identical copies contiguous in every replication.
  • Defined integral replication by scaling both masses and quotas by the same positive integer that clears their denominators; aggregates are divided by that scale.
  • Defined the computational output using the least denominator-clearing replication, leaving agreement across all replication scales as a separate open question.
  • Made quotas nonnegative, allowed zero quotas and empty initial schools, and required nonempty school and type sets.
  • Restored individual rationality, conditional dummy introduction, dummy eligibility restricted to decrementable schools, and simultaneous execution of all cycles including self-loops.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result. Exact computation is polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L\), and the normalized aggregate outcome equals \(x\) at every integral replication scale \(K\).

The proof gives an exact simulation using at most \(\tau+2m\) batches, regardless of the number of replicated students. It analyzes the rule in the question directly; the corresponding discrete cycle construction appears in the [2017 full version](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/316567382_Strategy-proof_School_Choice_Mechanisms_with_Minimum_Quotas_and_Initial_Endowments).

2. Compressed states and the effect of one round. Fix an integral replication scale \(K\). Write \(P_c=Kp_c\), \(Q_c=Kq_c\), and let \(u_t\) be the number of remaining students of type \(t\). Relabel types so their blocks occur in the order \(1,\ldots,\tau\).

For every school with \(r_c>0\), its representative’s type is

\(\displaystyle h(c)=\min\{t:\omega(t)=c,\ u_t>0\}.\)

Consequently, the representative’s preferences are determined by \(h(c)\). Representatives at different schools have different types, and their relative priorities are determined by their block order. The identities and priorities of individual students within a block therefore do not affect the graph after its vertices are labeled by schools.

Let \(s_c=r_c+z_c\). We first prove the invariant

\(\displaystyle P_c\leq s_c\leq Q_c\quad\text{for every school }c.\)

It holds initially by the input assumptions.

When some school is decrementable, let \(a\) be the school whose real representative has highest priority among representatives of decrementable schools. Every dummy points to \(a\). This has two consequences:

  • Every directed cycle contains a real representative, since a dummy’s successor is real.
  • At most one directed cycle contains a dummy, and that cycle contains exactly one dummy. Indeed, any dummy-containing cycle contains \(a\); two distinct cycles cannot share \(a\). Two dummies cannot belong to the same cycle because both would have the same successor \(a\).

Consider the simultaneous execution of all cycles.

In a cycle containing only real representatives, each participating school loses one remaining endowed student and receives one finalized assignment. Thus its \(s_c\) is unchanged. Incoming edges from outside the cycle contribute no assignments, because their originating students are not executed.

If there is a dummy-containing cycle, let \(b\) denote its dummy’s school. Its structure is

\(\displaystyle b\longrightarrow a\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow b.\)

The school \(a\) loses one remaining endowed student and receives no assignment from its predecessor, which is the dummy. Hence \(s_a\) decreases by one. The empty school \(b\) receives one assignment, so \(s_b=z_b\) increases by one. Every intermediate real school loses one remaining student and receives one assignment. All other cycles preserve their schools’ \(s_c\).

Therefore the only changes to \(s\) are

\(\displaystyle s_a\leftarrow s_a-1,\qquad s_b\leftarrow s_b+1\)

when a dummy-containing cycle exists. Before execution, \(a\) is decrementable and \(b\) is incrementable. Since all quantities are integers,

\(\displaystyle s_a-P_a\geq1,\qquad Q_b-z_b\geq1.\)

These inequalities prove preservation of the invariant.

Whenever students remain, the graph is nonempty and every vertex has one outgoing edge: each real representative can at least point to her own represented school, and every supplied dummy has its specified real successor. A finite graph with one outgoing edge per vertex contains a cycle. Every such cycle contains a real representative, so each round removes at least one student. The discrete procedure consequently terminates.

At termination, \(r_c=0\), and the invariant gives \(P_c\leq z_c\leq Q_c\). Moreover, every assigned student receives a school weakly preferred to her initial school, since that initial school was represented when she chose her outgoing edge. Thus the resulting allocation satisfies all constraints in the question.

3. An exact batching lemma. At any nonterminal compressed state, construct the graph on school labels and identify all its cycles.

Let \(H\) be the set of schools whose real representatives lie on cycles. For \(c\in H\), let \(f(c)\) be its outgoing neighbor’s school. The preceding argument shows \(H\neq\varnothing\).

Define a positive integer batch length \(B\) as follows.

If no cycle contains a dummy, set

\(\displaystyle B=\min_{c\in H}u_{h(c)}.\)

If a cycle contains the dummy of school \(b\), whose successor is the real representative of decrementable school \(a\), set

\(\displaystyle B=\min\left(\{u_{h(c)}:c\in H\}\cup\{s_a-P_a,\ Q_b-z_b\}\right).\)

Claim. The next \(B\) discrete rounds have the same graph on school labels, with the same representative types, immediately before each round. Their aggregate effect is to assign \(B\) students of type \(h(c)\) to school \(f(c)\), for every \(c\in H\).

To prove the claim, consider an integer \(j\) with \(0\leq j<B\). Provided the first \(j\) rounds use the stated cycles, every participating type still has

\(\displaystyle u_{h(c)}-j>0\)

students remaining. Nonparticipating types have not lost any students. Hence all representative types remain unchanged. In particular, no previously nonempty school becomes empty: its representative type remains present.

When there is no dummy-containing cycle, all \(s_c\) remain unchanged. When there is such a cycle, only \(s_a\) and \(z_b\) change, and

\(\displaystyle s_a-j>P_a,\qquad z_b+j<Q_b.\)

Thus \(a\) remains decrementable and \(b\) remains incrementable. All other school categories remain unchanged as well.

It follows that the represented schools, real representatives’ preferences, and the highest-priority decrementable representative all remain unchanged. Therefore every outgoing edge remains unchanged.

Starting with \(j=0\), this proves the claim by induction. At the end of the \(B\)-th round, at least one defining minimum is attained: a participating type is exhausted, a decrementable school reaches its lower quota, or an incrementable school reaches its upper quota. The graph can then be recomputed.

Accordingly, one batch performs the simultaneous updates

\(\displaystyle u_{h(c)}\leftarrow u_{h(c)}-B,\qquad r_c\leftarrow r_c-B\quad(c\in H),\)

and

\(\displaystyle z_{f(c)}\leftarrow z_{f(c)}+B,\qquad A_{h(c),f(c)}\leftarrow A_{h(c),f(c)}+B\quad(c\in H),\)

where \(A_{t,c}\) records cumulative assignments. If several updates address the same variable, their increments are summed. By the batching claim, these updates exactly reproduce the discrete execution.

4. A polynomial bound on the number of batches. Every batch ends with at least one of the following events:

  • A type is exhausted.
  • A decrementable school reaches its lower quota.
  • An incrementable school reaches its upper quota.

There are at most \(\tau\) events of the first kind.

For the second kind, observe that while \(r_c>0\), the quantity \(s_c\) can only stay constant or decrease. An increase is possible only when the school supplies a dummy, which requires \(r_c=0\). Thus, after a nonempty school reaches \(s_c=P_c\), it cannot become decrementable again while nonempty. Once empty, it stays empty and cannot become decrementable either. Each school therefore has at most one event of the second kind.

For the third kind, an empty school remains empty, and its finalized count \(z_c\) never decreases. Once \(z_c=Q_c\), it cannot become incrementable again. Each school has at most one event of the third kind.

Hence the total number of batches is at most

\(\displaystyle \tau+2m.\)

Simultaneous events only reduce this bound. There is no need to count school exhaustion separately: a school becomes empty only when its last remaining type is exhausted.

5. Exact computation and bit complexity. Apply the batched algorithm at scale \(D\), with initial state

\(\displaystyle u_t=D\mu_t,\qquad r_c=\sum_{\omega(t)=c}u_t,\qquad z_c=0,\qquad A_{t,c}=0.\)

The batching lemma proves that its final \(A\) is exactly the assignment-count matrix of discrete TTCR-SS at scale \(D\). Return

\(\displaystyle x_{t,c}=\frac{A_{t,c}}{D}.\)

This is a polynomial-time computation in the binary input model.

To verify that statement, reduce the input rationals and compute \(D\) as the least common multiple of their denominators. If those denominators are \(d_1,\ldots,d_N\), then

\(\displaystyle D\leq\prod_{i=1}^{N}d_i,\qquad \log_2D\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log_2d_i=O(L).\)

Thus \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits, even when its numerical value is exponentially large. Computing it by successive greatest-common-divisor and least-common-multiple operations takes polynomial bit time.

All remaining and finalized student counts are at most \(D\). The integers \(Dp_c\) and \(Dq_c\) also have \(O(L)\) bits; this remains true when an upper quota exceeds one, because its numerator is part of the input. Batch lengths, quota slacks, and assignment counts consequently have \(O(L)\) bits.

For implementation, maintain at each school a queue of its types in block order. A queue advances whenever its current type is exhausted, so there are at most \(\tau\) advances in total.

In a batch, there are at most \(m\) represented schools. Scanning each real representative’s ranking finds all outgoing edges using \(O(m^2)\) elementary operations. Finding the cycles of this graph takes \(O(m)\) operations, and computing and applying the batch requires \(O(m)\) arithmetic operations. Together with initialization and denominator computation, at most \(\tau+2m\) batches on integers of \(O(L)\) bits give polynomial bit complexity.

The output itself has polynomial size: it consists of \(\tau m\) rational entries with numerators at most \(D\) and common denominator \(D\).

6. Replication agreement. Let \(K\) be any integral replication scale allowed by the question. For a reduced rational with denominator \(d\), integrality after multiplication by \(K\) requires \(d\) to divide \(K\). Hence every denominator entering \(D\) divides \(K\), so

\(\displaystyle K=\lambda D\)

for a positive integer \(\lambda\).

Compare the batched executions at scales \(D\) and \(K\). Initially, every count and quota at scale \(K\) is \(\lambda\) times its counterpart at scale \(D\).

Suppose this proportionality holds at corresponding batch boundaries. Positivity, zero tests, and quota equalities and inequalities agree between the two states. The representative types therefore agree, as do their priorities, the represented schools, and every edge of the graph.

Each candidate in the formula for the batch length at scale \(K\) is \(\lambda\) times its counterpart at scale \(D\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle B_K=\lambda B_D.\)

The batch updates preserve proportionality of all remaining counts, finalized counts, and cumulative assignment counts. Induction proves that the executions have the same sequence of type-level graphs and terminate at corresponding batch boundaries. Their final matrices satisfy

\(\displaystyle A^{(K)}_{t,c}=\lambda A^{(D)}_{t,c}.\)

Since the batched executions exactly reproduce their respective discrete executions,

\(\displaystyle \frac{A^{(K)}_{t,c}}{K}=\frac{\lambda A^{(D)}_{t,c}}{\lambda D}=\frac{A^{(D)}_{t,c}}{D}=x_{t,c}.\)

This settles both questions: exact computation is polynomial-time, and normalized aggregate outcomes agree at every integral replication scale.
