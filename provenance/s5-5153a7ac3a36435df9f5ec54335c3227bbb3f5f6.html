The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(N\), \(k\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) with \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb{N}\), and Robertson–Webb oracles for \(\tau\) normalized valuation types \(v_t\), determine the minimum worst-case expected number of type-oracle queries for a \(k\)-round protocol with success probability at least \(2/3\) to partition \([0,1]\) into \(N\) contiguous intervals and assign one to each agent so that every type-\(t\) agent values its interval at least \(1/N\). Each query to a type oracle is reusable for all agents of that type; characterize the complexity in \(N\), \(k\), \(\tau\), and \(\mu\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), \(N,k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\), and \(n_t=N\mu_t\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\) agents have type \(t\).
Each type has a private valuation \(V_t\) induced by a nonnegative integrable density on \([0,1]\), normalized by \(V_t([0,1])=1\); densities may vanish.
The available Robertson–Webb queries are \(\operatorname{Cut}_t(\alpha)\), returning the leftmost \(y\) with \(V_t([0,y])=\alpha\) for \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), and \(\operatorname{Eval}_t(y)\), returning \(V_t([0,y])\) at a cut point obtained in an earlier round. Endpoints \(0,1\) are available initially; each type query costs one and its answer is reusable for every agent of that type.
A randomized protocol uses at most \(k\) simultaneous query batches, each depending only on earlier answers and its random coins. It outputs a partition into \(N\) contiguous intervals whose internal boundaries are returned cut points, labels exactly \(n_t\) intervals by type \(t\), and succeeds when every interval labeled \(t\) has value at least \(1/N\). Shared endpoints have zero value.
For fixed \(N,k,\mu\), let \(Q(N,k,\mu)=\inf_A\sup_{(V_t)}\mathbb{E}[q_A(V)]\), where the infimum ranges over such protocols succeeding with probability at least \(2/3\) on every admissible valuation profile, and \(q_A\) counts all queries, including failed executions. Oracle answers are exact reals and local computation is uncharged.

What is the asymptotically tight value of \(Q(N,k,\mu)\), uniformly in \(N,k,\tau\) and the mass vector \(\mu\)?
Express query bounds in these parameters; any running-time claims must also specify dependence on the binary input length \(L\) and the exact-real computation model. There is no separate parameter \(m\) in this problem.

For an explicit population with separate agent oracles, proportional contiguous allocation has an expected-query lower bound \(\Omega(kN^{1+1/k})\) in the range \(1\leq k\leq\log N\), and a deterministic upper bound \(O(kN^{1+1/k})\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.00738v5#S0.SS3)
No result settling the dependence on prescribed type multiplicities with reusable type queries was identified in the literature search.

The general characterization remains open. However, the one-batch gap identified in NOTES can be closed for every admissible equal-multiplicity population.

If $N\geq2$ and
$$ n_1=\cdots=n_\tau=g,\qquad N=\tau g, $$
then
$$ \boxed{\frac13\tau(N-1)\leq Q(N,1,\mu)\leq\tau(N-1).} $$
Consequently,
$$ Q(N,1,\mu)=\Theta(N\tau) $$
with absolute constants. In particular, the previously unresolved example $N=M^2$, $\tau=M$, $n_t=M$, $k=1$ has complexity $\Theta(M^3)$.

I also prove a combinatorial lower bound for one batch that depends explicitly on the prescribed multiplicities. For multiple batches, I give a self-contained proof of the verified support-aware upper bound. No matching characterization for arbitrary multiplicities and arbitrary $k$ is established.

1. Definitions and universal bounds

Let
$$ \mathcal T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\},\qquad n_t=N\mu_t. $$
The assumptions imply $n_t\geq1$, $\sum_t n_t=N$, and $\tau\leq N$.

For each type, write
$$ F_t(x)=V_t([0,x]),\qquad C_t(\alpha)=\operatorname{Cut}_t(\alpha). $$
An integrable nonnegative density makes $F_t$ continuous and nondecreasing. Therefore every requested cut exists, and
$$ F_t(C_t(\alpha))=\alpha. $$
Furthermore,
$$ \alpha<\beta\implies C_t(\alpha)<C_t(\beta). $$
Indeed, the opposite ordering would contradict monotonicity and the displayed cumulative values. This remains true when densities vanish.

If $N=1$, then $\tau=1$ and allocation of the entire cake needs no queries:
$$ Q(1,k,\mu)=0. $$
Henceforth assume $N\geq2$.

On a successful execution, all $N$ allocated intervals have positive length: a zero-length interval has value zero under an integrable density. There are thus $N-1$ distinct internal boundaries. Each must have been returned by a cut query, so success requires at least $N-1$ queries. Consequently,
$$ \boxed{Q(N,k,\mu)\geq\frac23(N-1).} $$
This argument includes failed executions in the expected cost.

2. A new one-batch lower bound depending on the multiplicities

The following finite combinatorial quantity is defined entirely by $(n_t)_{t\in\mathcal T}$.

For each integer $i$ with $1\leq i<N$, define
$$ I_i= \left\{ t\in\mathcal T: \text{there exists }S\subseteq\mathcal T\setminus\{t\} \text{ such that } \sum_{s\in S}n_s<i<\sum_{s\in S}n_s+n_t \right\}. $$
Thus $t\in I_i$ exactly when position $i$ can lie strictly inside type $t$’s block in some ordering of the type blocks.

Define an undirected graph $G_i$ on $\mathcal T$. Distinct types $a,b$ form an edge if, for some $S\subseteq\mathcal T\setminus\{a,b\}$, either
$$ i=\sum_{s\in S}n_s+n_a $$
or
$$ i=\sum_{s\in S}n_s+n_b. $$
Equivalently, an ordering of the type blocks can have a boundary at position $i$ between consecutive blocks of types $a$ and $b$.

Let $\lambda_i(n)$ be the minimum of
$$ \sum_{t\in\mathcal T}x_t $$
subject to
$$ 0\leq x_t\leq1,\qquad x_t=1\quad(t\in I_i),\qquad x_a+x_b\geq1\quad(\{a,b\}\in E(G_i)). $$
The feasible set is nonempty because $x_t=1$ for every type is feasible.

Proved lower bound.
$$ \boxed{ Q(N,1,\mu)\geq \frac23\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\lambda_i(n). } $$

2.1. Construction of the valuation profiles

Choose $N+1$ disjoint closed intervals
$$ B_0<B_1<\cdots<B_N $$
strictly inside $(0,1)$, where $B_i<B_j$ means that every point of $B_i$ precedes every point of $B_j$.

Inside each $B_i$, choose $\tau$ disjoint positive-length intervals
$$ J_{1,i}<J_{2,i}<\cdots<J_{\tau,i}. $$
Write their endpoints as $a_{r,i}<b_{r,i}$.

For a permutation $\pi:\mathcal T\to\{1,\ldots,\tau\}$, give type $t$ a uniform spike on each $J_{\pi(t),i}$, of total value $1/(N+1)$. Thus its density is
$$ f_t^\pi(x)= \sum_{i=0}^{N} \frac{\mathbf 1_{J_{\pi(t),i}}(x)} {(N+1)\lvert J_{\pi(t),i}\rvert}. $$
These densities are nonnegative and integrable, and each has total integral $1$. They are therefore admissible.

For every type, the cumulative value before its spike in $B_i$ is $i/(N+1)$, and after that spike it is $(i+1)/(N+1)$. Consequently, for $\alpha>0$,
$$ C_t(\alpha)\in B_i \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{i}{N+1}<\alpha\leq\frac{i+1}{N+1}. $$
At the upper equality, the leftmost cut is the end of that spike. At the lower equality, it is the end of the preceding spike.

In particular, the band containing a cut is determined by its query argument, independently of $\pi$.

2.2. Every successful allocation follows the hidden type order

Consider a successful allocation on one of these profiles. Write its boundaries as
$$ 0=y_0<y_1<\cdots<y_N=1 $$
and let $w_j$ label interval $[y_{j-1},y_j]$.

First, every internal boundary $y_i$ lies strictly inside $B_i$.

To prove the lower endpoint inequality, proceed inductively from the left. The portion before the beginning of $B_1$ contains only the first spike of each type, worth $1/(N+1)<1/N$. Hence $y_1$ lies after the beginning of $B_1$. If $y_{i-1}$ lies after the beginning of $B_{i-1}$, the interval from $y_{i-1}$ to the beginning of $B_i$ meets at most the spike in $B_{i-1}$, again worth at most $1/(N+1)$. Thus $y_i$ lies after the beginning of $B_i$.

The symmetric induction from the right shows that $y_i$ lies before the end of $B_i$. This proves the assertion.

Next, the piece labeled $w_i$ can meet only spikes in $B_{i-1}$ and $B_i$. If
$$ y_i\leq a_{\pi(w_i),i}, $$
it obtains value only from $B_{i-1}$, at most $1/(N+1)$. Therefore success requires
$$ y_i>a_{\pi(w_i),i}. $$
Likewise, if
$$ y_i\geq b_{\pi(w_{i+1}),i}, $$
the next piece obtains value only from $B_{i+1}$, again at most $1/(N+1)$. Hence
$$ y_i<b_{\pi(w_{i+1}),i}. $$
Together,
$$ a_{\pi(w_i),i}<y_i<b_{\pi(w_{i+1}),i}. $$

If $\pi(w_i)>\pi(w_{i+1})$, the two spikes are ordered in the opposite direction:
$$ a_{\pi(w_i),i}>b_{\pi(w_{i+1}),i}, $$
a contradiction. Thus
$$ \pi(w_1)\leq\pi(w_2)\leq\cdots\leq\pi(w_N). $$
Because exactly $n_t$ intervals must receive label $t$, the successful label sequence consists of the type blocks, with their prescribed lengths, in precisely the order $\pi$.

Two further consequences will be used:

  • If $w_i=w_{i+1}=t$, then $y_i$ lies strictly inside type $t$’s spike in $B_i$. Since spikes of different types are disjoint, this boundary must come from a cut query to type $t$ in band $i$.
  • If $w_i=a$ and $w_{i+1}=b$ are distinct, they are consecutive types in the order $\pi$. The allowed interval for $y_i$ lies between the beginning of $a$’s spike and the end of $b$’s spike. Only these two types have spikes there. Hence this boundary must come from a cut query to $a$ or $b$ in band $i$.

2.3. Applying the success requirement to a simultaneous batch

Fix any randomized one-batch protocol satisfying the required success probability.

Let $p_{t,i}$ be the probability that its batch contains at least one query
$$ \operatorname{Cut}_t(\alpha), \qquad \frac{i}{N+1}<\alpha\leq\frac{i+1}{N+1}. $$
These probabilities do not depend on $\pi$: the sole batch is chosen before receiving any answers.

For $t\in I_i$, choose a permutation in which position $i$ is internal to type $t$’s block. On that profile, success requires the query event defining $p_{t,i}$. Therefore
$$ p_{t,i}\geq\frac23. $$
For an edge $\{a,b\}\in E(G_i)$, choose a permutation with those blocks adjacent at position $i$. Success requires a cut in band $i$ from at least one of these two types. The union bound gives
$$ p_{a,i}+p_{b,i}\geq\frac23. $$

Set
$$ x_{t,i}=\min\left\{1,\frac32p_{t,i}\right\}. $$
The preceding inequalities make $(x_{t,i})_t$ feasible for the definition of $\lambda_i(n)$. Clipping a coordinate at $1$ preserves every edge constraint. Hence
$$ \sum_t p_{t,i} \geq\frac23\sum_t x_{t,i} \geq\frac23\lambda_i(n). $$

Each cut query contributes to at most one event indexed by $(t,i)$. Thus, execution by execution, the query count is at least the number of these events that occur. Taking expectations,
$$ \mathbb E[q_A]\geq \sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\sum_t p_{t,i} \geq \frac23\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\lambda_i(n). $$
Taking the infimum over eligible protocols proves the lower bound.

3. Complete resolution for equal multiplicities in one batch

Suppose $n_t=g$ for every type, so $N=\tau g$.

If $i$ is not divisible by $g$, write
$$ i=rg+j,\qquad 1\leq j<g. $$
Every type can occupy block position $r+1$, so $I_i=\mathcal T$ and
$$ \lambda_i(n)=\tau. $$
There are $N-\tau$ such indices.

If $i=rg$ with $1\leq r<\tau$, then $I_i$ is empty. Any two distinct types can be consecutive at this position: put $r-1$ other types before them. Therefore $G_i$ is the complete graph.

For $\tau\geq2$, its fractional vertex-cover minimum is $\tau/2$. Indeed, summing all edge constraints yields
$$ (\tau-1)\sum_t x_t\geq\frac{\tau(\tau-1)}2, $$
while $x_t=1/2$ attains equality. There are $\tau-1$ such indices. For $\tau=1$, there are no such indices.

Consequently, in all cases,
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\lambda_i(n) = \tau(N-\tau)+\frac{\tau(\tau-1)}2. $$
The new lower bound becomes
$$ Q(N,1,\mu) \geq \frac23\tau(N-\tau)+\frac13\tau(\tau-1). $$
Subtracting $\tau(N-1)/3$ from its right-hand side gives
$$ \frac13\tau(N-\tau)\geq0. $$
Hence
$$ Q(N,1,\mu)\geq\frac13\tau(N-1). $$

For the matching upper bound, query every type at all levels $j/N$, $1\leq j<N$, in one batch. The subdivision construction proved below, with $N$ singleton children, produces a proportional allocation. Its cost is $\tau(N-1)$.

This proves the claimed uniform characterization.

4. Support-aware upper bounds for arbitrary multiplicities and batches

Let
$$ D=\lceil\log_2N\rceil,\qquad K=\min\{k,D\}. $$
For arbitrary admissible multiplicities,
$$ \boxed{ Q(N,k,\mu)\leq \min\left\{ \tau(N-1),\ N-1+ 2(\tau-1) \min_{1\leq r\leq K} r\bigl(\lceil N^{1/r}\rceil-1\bigr) \right\}. } $$
The proof is included to make the result independent of NOTES.

4.1. Subproblem invariant and completion of a single type

A subproblem has a physical interval $[x,y]$, integers $a,s$ with $0\leq a<a+s\leq N$, and remaining counts $c_t$ summing to $s$. For every participating type, maintain
$$ x\leq C_t(a/N), \qquad C_t((a+s)/N)\leq y. $$
Initially, $[x,y]=[0,1]$, $a=0$, $s=N$, and $c_t=n_t$, so the invariant holds.

If $s=1$, the interval has value at least $1/N$ for its sole type.

If only type $t$ participates, query
$$ C_t((a+j)/N),\qquad 1\leq j<s, $$
in one batch. The invariant and strict ordering of quantiles show that these cuts partition $[x,y]$ into $s$ positive-length pieces, each worth at least $1/N$ to $t$.

4.2. Subdivision of a mixed subproblem

Choose positive child populations $s_1,\ldots,s_h$ summing to $s$, and put
$$ p_0=a,\qquad p_j=a+\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}s_\ell. $$
Query every participating type at levels $p_j/N$, $1\leq j<h$.

For each $j<h$, order the remaining type groups by $C_t(p_j/N)$ and take the first $s_j$ agents, consuming whole groups except possibly the last selected group. Let $S_j$ be this selected multiset and set
$$ z_j=\max_{t\in\operatorname{supp}(S_j)}C_t(p_j/N). $$
Put all remaining agents in $S_h$, and set $z_0=x$, $z_h=y$.

Child $j$ receives interval $[z_{j-1},z_j]$ and integer range $[p_{j-1},p_j]$.

For a type selected into a nonfinal child, its upper quantile is at most $z_j$. Every type remaining after the previous selection has its preceding quantile at least $z_{j-1}$. The parent invariant supplies the first lower endpoint and final upper endpoint. Thus every child satisfies the invariant.

The boundaries are strictly increasing. A type selected into the next child remained after the preceding selection, so its preceding quantile is at least the preceding boundary; its next quantile is strictly larger. The parent invariant similarly gives strict separation from $x$ and $y$. All new boundaries are returned cut points.

4.3. Bounding participating type occurrences

For a subproblem with $T$ participating types, define its excess as $T-1$.

If it produces children with support sizes $T_1,\ldots,T_h$, then
$$ \sum_{j=1}^{h}(T_j-1)\leq T-1. $$
To prove this, let $r_t$ count how many children contain type $t$. Each of the first $h-1$ selections splits at most one remaining type group. Therefore
$$ \sum_t(r_t-1)\leq h-1. $$
Since $\sum_jT_j=\sum_tr_t$,
$$ \sum_j(T_j-1) =\sum_tr_t-h \leq T-1. $$

Thus total excess among active subproblems never exceeds $\tau-1$. At any depth, if the mixed subproblems have support sizes $T_1,\ldots,T_M$, then
$$ M\leq\tau-1,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{M}T_j\leq2(\tau-1). $$

4.4. Round and query counts

Fix $1\leq r\leq k$ and let
$$ b=\lceil N^{1/r}\rceil. $$
At each mixed subproblem of population $s$, create $\min\{b,s\}$ children with populations differing by at most one. Complete single-type subproblems in the next batch.

After depth $d$, every unresolved population is at most
$$ \left\lceil\frac{N}{b^d}\right\rceil. $$
This follows inductively from balanced subdivision and the identity
$$ \left\lceil\frac{\lceil N/b^d\rceil}{b}\right\rceil = \left\lceil\frac{N}{b^{d+1}}\right\rceil. $$
Because $b^r\geq N$, after $r$ subdivisions all unresolved populations are singletons. Single-type subproblems created earlier finish by batch $r$.

At each depth, mixed subproblems use at most
$$ (b-1)\sum_jT_j\leq2(\tau-1)(b-1) $$
queries. Completed single-type populations are disjoint portions of the original population; completing a population of size $s$ costs $s-1$ queries. Their total cost is at most $N-1$.

The total is therefore at most
$$ N-1+2r(\tau-1)(b-1). $$
Taking $r=1$ with $N$ singleton children separately gives the upper bound $\tau(N-1)$. For $r>D$, the branching factor remains $2$, so this accounting cannot improve on choosing $r=D$. This proves the displayed bound.

In particular,
$$ Q(N,k,\mu)=O\bigl(N+k\tau N^{1/k}\bigr) \qquad(1\leq k\leq D). $$
For $k\geq D$,
$$ Q(N,k,\mu)\leq N-1+2(\tau-1)D =O(N+\tau\log_2\tau). $$
For the last equality, put $x=N/\tau\geq1$ and use
$$ \tau\log_2N =\tau\log_2\tau+\tau\log_2x, \qquad \tau\log_2x =N\frac{\ln x}{x\ln2} \leq\frac{N}{e\ln2}. $$

5. What remains unresolved

The new lower bound settles all equal-multiplicity populations for one batch, but it does not settle arbitrary unequal multiplicities.

It also does not give a strong lower bound for multiple batches. In fact, the spike family above is easy in two batches. In the first batch, query every type at
$$ \alpha=\frac{1}{2(N+1)}. $$
The returned points lie in the first band and reveal the entire type order by sorting their coordinates.

Let $w_1,\ldots,w_N$ be the prescribed block sequence in that order. In the second batch, query
$$ C_{w_i}(i/N),\qquad 1\leq i<N, $$
and use these points as boundaries. At every level, the quantiles are ordered by the same type order. Hence
$$ C_{w_{i-1}}((i-1)/N) \leq C_{w_i}((i-1)/N), $$
so interval $i$ has value at least $1/N$ for $w_i$. The first and final intervals satisfy the same conclusion using endpoints $0,1$. The total cost is $\tau+N-1=O(N)$.

Thus a different hard family or additional argument is required for a multibatch lower bound.

When $\tau=N$, integrality forces $n_t=1$. Type reuse then offers no sharing, and the separate-agent lower bound supplied in the task, together with the upper bound proved here, gives
$$ Q(N,k,\mu)=\Theta\bigl(kN^{1+1/k}\bigr) \qquad(1\leq k\leq\log N). $$
The source’s oracle model and constant-success expected-query statement are given in [Appendix C, including Corollary 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.00738v5#A3.SS2).

All results here concern query complexity. The upper-bound protocols use exact comparisons of oracle coordinates and exact integer/rational bookkeeping. No running-time bound polynomial in the binary input length $L$ is asserted. In particular, the explicit output contains $N$ intervals, and the definition of $\lambda_i(n)$ involves subsets of the support.
