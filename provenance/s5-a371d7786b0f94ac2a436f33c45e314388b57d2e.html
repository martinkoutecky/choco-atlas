The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(T\) of worker types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and task roles \(J\) with rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), rewards \(r_j\), and type-specific parameters \((p_{tj},c_{tj})\), compute an optimal EFS contract. Each role's copies share a contract share \(\alpha_j\), and each worker receives an integral bundle \(B\subseteq J\). Let \(x_{t,B}\) be the mass of type-\(t\) workers receiving \(B\), and \(s_{t,B}\geq 0\) their subsidy. Require \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B:j\in B}x_{t,B}=b_j\), and effort feasibility \(\alpha_jp_{tj}r_j-c_{tj}\geq 0\) for every assigned task in every occupied configuration. For every pair of occupied configurations \((t,B)\) and \((t',B')\), require \(\sum_{j\in B}(\alpha_jp_{tj}r_j-c_{tj})+s_{t,B}\geq\sum_{j\in B'}\max\{\alpha_jp_{tj}r_j-c_{tj},0\}+s_{t',B'}\). Maximize per-capita revenue \(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}\bigl(\sum_{j\in B}(1-\alpha_j)p_{tj}r_j-s_{t,B}\bigr)\), or decide whether this optimum is at least a given rational \(R\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau=|T|\geq1\) worker types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. There are \(m=|J|\geq1\) task roles, with binary-rational supplies \(0<b_j\leq1\), rewards \(r_j\in[0,1]\), success probabilities \(p_{tj}\in[0,1]\), and effort costs \(c_{tj}\geq0\). Assume each role has some type satisfying \(p_{tj}r_j-c_{tj}\geq0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including a rational threshold \(R\).
Each worker receives a bundle \(B\subseteq J\), possibly empty, containing at most one copy of each role. Type masses may be split among bundles. All copies of role \(j\) share a real contract share \(\alpha_j\in[0,1]\): success pays \(\alpha_jr_j\), failure pays zero; shirking has zero cost and zero success probability, and effort is chosen when indifferent. Subsidies are unconditional payments per worker.
Choose real masses \(x_{t,B}\geq0\) and real subsidies \(s_{t,B}\geq0\), with \(\sum_{B\subseteq J}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:j\in B}x_{t,B}=b_j\). A configuration \((t,B)\) is occupied when \(x_{t,B}>0\); its workers all receive subsidy \(s_{t,B}\).
Write \(u_{tj}=\alpha_jp_{tj}r_j-c_{tj}\). An envy-free contract with subsidies (EFS) requires \(u_{tj}\geq0\) for every occupied \((t,B)\) and every \(j\in B\), and, for every ordered pair of occupied configurations, \(\sum_{j\in B}u_{tj}+s_{t,B}\geq\sum_{j\in B'}\max\{u_{tj},0\}+s_{t',B'}\).
Its per-capita revenue is \(\operatorname{Rev}=\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}\bigl(\sum_{j\in B}(1-\alpha_j)p_{tj}r_j-s_{t,B}\bigr)\). The decision problem asks whether an EFS contract with \(\operatorname{Rev}\geq R\) exists; infeasible instances have answer no.

Is this continuous EFS revenue-threshold problem in \(\mathsf{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard?

For finitely many individually listed workers and distinct indivisible tasks, optimal EFS contract design is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard, and is polynomial-time solvable when the number of tasks is constant. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25431v2#S5).
No complexity result for this exact mass-and-role formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified rational input domains: success probabilities and rewards lie in [0,1], effort costs are nonnegative, and the threshold may be any rational.
  • Restricted contract shares to [0,1], made masses and contract variables real, and specified unconditional subsidies per worker.
  • Restricted positive role supplies to at most one unit of worker mass, consistent with bundles containing at most one copy of each role; zero-supply roles are omitted.
  • Carried over the assumption that every role is useful for at least one positive-mass type at full transfer.
  • Included empty bundles and specified effort at utility ties, as required by weak effort feasibility and the revenue formula.
  • Used the existence of a feasible contract meeting the revenue threshold as the decision formulation, with infeasible instances answered no.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result. The continuous EFS revenue-threshold problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard. The following polynomial-time reduction uses only dyadic rational inputs and applies directly to the stated mass-and-role model.

2. Source problem and construction. We reduce from PARTITION: given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), decide whether some \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j\in S}a_j=\frac{A}{2},\qquad A:=\sum_{j=1}^n a_j.\)

PARTITION is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete. [Karp, *Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems*](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Let \(D\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(D\geq 2\max_j a_j\), and define \(v_j=a_j/D\). Thus \(0<v_j\leq 1/2\).

Choose the smallest integer \(h\) with \(2^h\geq n+2\), and put \(q=2^{-h}\). Construct \(n\) roles and \(n+2\) listed types: a distinguished type \(0\), auxiliary types \(1,\ldots,n\), and a neutral type \(*\). Their masses are

\(\displaystyle \mu_0=\mu_1=\cdots=\mu_n=q,\qquad \mu_*=1-(n+1)q.\)

These masses sum to one and are positive: \(\mu_*\geq q>0\).

Every role has reward \(r_j=1\) and supply \(b_j=3q/2\). Define probabilities and costs as follows:

  • For type \(0\), let \(p_{0j}=v_j\) and \(c_{0j}=0\).
  • For auxiliary type \(j\) on its own role, let \(p_{jj}=1\) and \(c_{jj}=1/2\).
  • For every remaining type-role pair, let \(p_{tj}=0\) and \(c_{tj}=1\).

In particular, auxiliary type \(j\) can exert effort only on role \(j\), and the neutral type can receive only the empty bundle. Every role satisfies the task’s feasibility assumption because \(p_{jj}r_j-c_{jj}=1/2\).

Set the revenue threshold to

\(\displaystyle R=\sum_{j=1}^n M_j,\qquad M_j:=\frac12\left(q+\frac q2v_j\right).\)

All constructed data are dyadic rationals. Their bit lengths are polynomial in the PARTITION input length, including

\(\displaystyle R=\frac{nq}{2}+\frac{qA}{4D}.\)

The probability and cost tables have polynomial size. Hence this construction is a polynomial-time reduction.

3. A revenue upper bound and its equality conditions. Consider any EFS contract for the constructed instance. Define the assignment marginals

\(\displaystyle y_{tj}:=\sum_{B:j\in B}x_{t,B},\qquad z_j:=y_{0j}.\)

The effort constraints exclude all types other than \(0\) and \(j\) from role \(j\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle z_j+y_{jj}=\frac{3q}{2}.\)

Each of these types has mass \(q\), and a bundle contains at most one copy of a role. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \frac q2\leq z_j\leq q,\qquad \frac q2\leq y_{jj}\leq q.\)

In particular, a positive mass of auxiliary type \(j\) receives role \(j\). Its effort constraint forces \(\alpha_j\geq 1/2\).

The expected success mass on role \(j\) is

\(\displaystyle H_j=v_jz_j+y_{jj}=\frac{3q}{2}-(1-v_j)z_j.\)

Its gross revenue, before subsidies, is \(g_j=(1-\alpha_j)H_j\). Direct subtraction gives

\(\displaystyle M_j-g_j =\left(\alpha_j-\frac12\right)H_j +\frac12(1-v_j)\left(z_j-\frac q2\right).\)

Both terms are nonnegative. Moreover, \(H_j\geq y_{jj}\geq q/2>0\) and \(1-v_j>0\). Writing the total subsidy expenditure as

\(\displaystyle C:=\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}s_{t,B}\geq0,\)

we obtain the exact identity

\(\displaystyle R-\operatorname{Rev} =\sum_{j=1}^n\left[ \left(\alpha_j-\frac12\right)H_j +\frac12(1-v_j)\left(z_j-\frac q2\right) \right]+C.\)

Thus every EFS contract has \(\operatorname{Rev}\leq R\). If it satisfies \(\operatorname{Rev}\geq R\), every nonnegative term in this identity must vanish. It follows that

\(\displaystyle \alpha_j=\frac12,\qquad z_j=\frac q2\quad\text{for every }j,\qquad C=0.\)

Since \(x_{t,B}s_{t,B}\geq0\), the last equality implies \(s_{t,B}=0\) for every occupied configuration.

4. Attaining the threshold implies a partition. Suppose an EFS contract satisfies \(\operatorname{Rev}\geq R\). By the preceding equality conditions, an occupied type-\(0\) bundle \(B\) has utility

\(\displaystyle U(B)=\frac12\sum_{j\in B}v_j.\)

Any two occupied configurations of type \(0\) have zero subsidies. Envy-freeness in both directions therefore gives equality of their utilities. Denote their common utility by \(U\).

Type \(0\) has total mass \(q\), so averaging its utility over its occupied configurations yields

\(\displaystyle qU =\sum_Bx_{0,B}\frac12\sum_{j\in B}v_j =\frac12\sum_{j=1}^n v_jz_j =\frac q4\sum_{j=1}^n v_j.\)

Hence \(U=\frac14\sum_jv_j\). Every occupied type-\(0\) bundle therefore satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j\in B}v_j=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^n v_j,\)

or, equivalently,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j\in B}a_j=\frac A2.\)

At least one such bundle is occupied because type \(0\) has positive mass. It supplies a solution to PARTITION. This argument permits arbitrary real mass splitting.

5. A partition attains the threshold. Conversely, suppose \(S\) solves PARTITION. Set every share to \(\alpha_j=1/2\) and every subsidy to zero. Assign:

  • Mass \(q/2\) of type \(0\) to \(S\), and mass \(q/2\) to its complement.
  • All mass \(q\) of auxiliary type \(j\) to the singleton bundle \(\{j\}\).
  • All neutral mass to the empty bundle.

Each role receives mass \(q+q/2=b_j\). Type \(0\) has positive utility on every assigned role, and every auxiliary type has utility zero on its assigned role, so all effort constraints hold.

We verify every kind of envy comparison. Both type-\(0\) bundles have utility

\(\displaystyle \frac12\sum_{j\in S}v_j=\frac{A}{4D}.\)

They therefore do not envy each other. A singleton \(\{j\}\) has value \(a_j/(2D)\) to type \(0\). Since \(j\) belongs to one of the two partition parts, positivity of the integers implies \(a_j\leq A/2\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \frac{a_j}{2D}\leq\frac{A}{4D},\)

so type \(0\) does not envy any auxiliary configuration. It also does not envy the empty bundle.

For each auxiliary type, its own role has utility zero and every other role has utility \(-1\). Its shirking-adjusted value of every bundle is therefore zero, equal to its own utility. The neutral type likewise values every bundle at zero after allowing shirking. Thus all EFS inequalities hold.

Finally, role \(j\) has expected success mass \(q+(q/2)v_j\). With share \(1/2\) and zero subsidies, its revenue is exactly \(M_j\). Total revenue is \(R\).

6. Conclusion. The constructed instance admits an EFS contract with revenue at least \(R\) if and only if the original PARTITION instance is a yes-instance. This proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness of the exact continuous formulation. In particular, a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).
