The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) alternatives with rational feature vectors \(z_i\), a common rational parameter matrix \(B\), and \(\tau\) agent-feature types \(x_t\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), where rankings for type \(t\) follow the Plackett–Luce model, compute the probabilistic Borda winner distribution \(\psi\) to additive error \(\epsilon\), where \(\psi_i = \frac{2}{m(m-1)}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\exp(x_t^\top Bz_i)}{\exp(x_t^\top Bz_i)+\exp(x_t^\top Bz_j)}\).

Given \(m\ge 2\) alternatives, let \(z_i\in\mathbb Q^k\), \(x_t\in\mathbb Q^d\), and \(B\in\mathbb Q^{d\times k}\); all entries may have either sign, and \(B\) is supplied as input.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\ge 1\) distinct agent-feature types \(x_t\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings or types.
Each type has a Plackett–Luce distribution over strict rankings with utilities \(u_{ti}=x_t^\top Bz_i\); equal utilities are allowed.
Probabilistic Borda assigns winning probabilities proportional to mass-weighted expected Borda scores. Its winner distribution is defined by
\(\displaystyle \psi_i=\frac{2}{m(m-1)}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\exp(u_{ti})}{\exp(u_{ti})+\exp(u_{tj})}.\)
Given binary-rational \(0<\epsilon<1\), compute a rational probability vector \(\widehat\psi\) with \(\max_i|\widehat\psi_i-\psi_i|\le\epsilon\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; use the Turing bit model and state running times in \(m,\tau,d,k,L\), and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\).

Can this approximation problem be solved in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,d,k,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite societies, probabilistic Borda winning probabilities are proportional to summed pairwise preference probabilities; under Plackett–Luce these pairwise probabilities have the displayed exponential form and are described as easy to compute. This establishes an identity, rather than a bit-complexity bound ([Zhao et al.](https://www.auai.org/uai2018/proceedings/papers/179.pdf)).
No result settling this precise rational-support, bit-complexity approximation problem was located in the literature search.

\(p_{tij}+p_{tji}=1\).

\(r=\min\{h\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 1}:2^{-h}\leq\epsilon\}\)

\(1-\sigma(s)=1/(1+\exp(s))\leq\exp(-s)\leq\exp(-q)<2^{-q}\),

\(S_N(s)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}s^\ell/\ell!\).
Return
\(v(s)=2^{-q}\left\lfloor 2^q S_N(s)/(1+S_N(s))\right\rfloor\).

We now justify its accuracy explicitly.

Taylor’s theorem applies because the exponential function has continuous derivatives of every order. It gives
\(0\leq\exp(s)-S_N(s)\leq e^q q^n/n!\).

For completeness, the factorial bound needed here follows from monotonicity of the natural logarithm:
\(\ln(n!)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ln j\geq\int_1^n\ln x\,dx=n\ln n-n+1\geq n\ln n-n\).
Consequently, \(n!\geq(n/e)^n\). Since \(e<3<4\) and \(n=8q\), we obtain
\(e^q q^n/n!\leq e^q(eq/n)^n<2^{2q}2^{-n}=2^{-6q}\).

\(0\leq E/(1+E)-S/(1+S)=(E-S)/((1+E)(1+S))\leq E-S<2^{-6q}\).

\(|v(s)-\sigma(s)|\leq 2^{1-q}\).

\(U_{ti}=\widetilde x_t^\top\widetilde y_i\).

\(H=L+\lceil\log_2(dk+2)\rceil+1\)

\(R\leftarrow 1+(s/j)R\)

Expanding this nested expression gives exactly \(S_N(s)\): its coefficient of \(s^\ell\) is \(1/\ell!\).

\((C,F)\leftarrow(bjF+aC,\;bjF)\).

\(O(N(H+\log_2(N+2)))=O(q(H+\log_2(q+2)))\)

\(O\!\left(L^3+(mdk+\tau md)H^2+\tau m^2q^3(H+\log_2(q+2))^2+\tau m^2K^2+mK\right)\).

Here \(O(L^3)\) covers forming the common denominators and converting input rationals. The remaining terms cover exact utilities, pairwise Taylor evaluations, integer aggregation, and output writing, respectively.
