Theorem E.6 (Limiting density frontier) — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a continuum of recipient mass with finitely many complete types, unit budgets, common valuations and sizes, and per-capita sequential arrival streams of indivisible goods with positive-density spread at most \(\gamma\), determine the optimal prefix-wise \(\alpha\)-FEF\(1\) guarantee \(F_\infty(\sigma)\) when every good has size at most \(\sigma\), and determine \(\lim_{\sigma\to0}F_\infty(\sigma)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless probability space given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) complete recipient types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here every recipient has budget \(1\) and the same additive values and sizes, so identical complete types merge and \(\tau=1\).
There are \(m\) realized good types, with binary-rational values \(v_j\ge0\) and sizes \(0<s_j\le\sigma\), where \(0<\sigma\le1\). Fix a supplied bound \(\gamma>1\); the ratio of largest to smallest positive \(v_j/s_j\) is at most \(\gamma\), with spread defined as \(1\) if all values are zero.
A finite per-capita arrival stream consists of \(T\) rounds. Round \(t\) reveals a good type \(j_t\) and a positive binary-rational mass \(a_t\) of identical copies. A deterministic online policy immediately and irrevocably distributes these copies measurably among recipients and unrestricted charity, conserving copy mass; every individual receives whole copies and always has total size at most \(1\). The policy knows no future arrivals.
Write \(A_x^t\) for individual \(x\)'s integer multiset bundle and \(c_j^t\) for charity's per-capita copy mass of type \(j\). A charity comparison is any finite integer multiset using only types with \(c_j^t>0\), with unrestricted multiplicities: positive per-capita mass supplies arbitrarily many copies to the pooled charity.
An allocation is \(\alpha\)-FEF\(1\), for \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), if there is a full-measure set of individuals such that, for every comparing individual \(x\) in it and every feasible submultiset \(S\) of another individual's bundle in it or every charity comparison of size at most \(1\), some submultiset \(X\subseteq S\) with \(|X|\le1\) satisfies \(v(A_x^t)\ge\alpha v(S\setminus X)\). Require this after every round.
For fixed \(\gamma\), let \(F_\infty(\sigma)\) be the supremum of factors guaranteed by some deterministic online policy on every such finite stream. Partial solution: sharp regimes and improved bounds

The fixed-size frontier can be determined in a nontrivial range of parameters. In both horizon models,
$$ \boxed{ F_\infty(\sigma)=\frac1\gamma \quad\text{whenever there is an integer }q\ge2 \text{ with }\sigma\ge\frac1q \text{ and }\gamma\le\frac{q}{q-1}. } $$
In particular, this holds whenever $1<\gamma\le2$ and $\sigma\ge1/2$.

For the remaining regimes, I prove improved bounds, but do not determine the sharp frontier. The small-good limit is determined for every fixed $\gamma>1$:
$$ \boxed{ \lim_{\sigma\downarrow0}F_\infty^{\mathrm{unk}}(\sigma) = \lim_{\sigma\downarrow0}F_\infty^{\mathrm{ann}}(\sigma) = \frac1{1+\ln\gamma}. } $$

All arguments below apply directly to the specified atomless, pooled-charity model.

1. Statements of the bounds

Write
$$ D=1+\ln\gamma, $$
and define
$$ B_\gamma(\sigma)= \begin{cases} \displaystyle\frac{1-(D+1)\sigma}{D(1-\sigma)}, &0<\sigma<\frac1{D+1},\\[6pt] 0,&\frac1{D+1}\le\sigma\le1. \end{cases} $$
Here $F_\infty^{\mathrm{ann}}$ denotes the announced-horizon frontier, and $F_\infty^{\mathrm{unk}}$ the unknown-horizon frontier.

For every $0<\sigma\le1$,
$$ \boxed{ \max\left\{\frac1\gamma,B_\gamma(\sigma)\right\} \le F_\infty^{\mathrm{unk}}(\sigma) \le F_\infty^{\mathrm{ann}}(\sigma) \le\frac1D. } $$
The lower bound $B_\gamma$ improves the small-good lower bound in the notes.

There is also a family of packing upper bounds. For any integer $q\ge2$ satisfying
$$ \sigma>\frac1{q+1}, $$
put
$$ b_q=\min\left\{\sigma,\frac1q\right\}. $$
Then
$$ \boxed{ F_\infty^{\mathrm{ann}}(\sigma) \le \max\left\{ \frac{q-1}{q}, \frac{1-b_q}{\gamma(q-1)b_q} \right\}. } $$
This upper bound also applies to the unknown-horizon model.

If $\sigma\ge1/q$, it simplifies to
$$ F_\infty^{\mathrm{ann}}(\sigma) \le \max\left\{\frac{q-1}{q},\frac1\gamma\right\}. $$
Consequently, when $\gamma\le q/(q-1)$, the upper bound matches the constructive $1/\gamma$ guarantee.

For example, when $\gamma=3/2$ and $\sigma=3/8$, taking $q=3$ gives the exact frontier $2/3$ in both horizon models.

2. Measurable implementation of the policies

Let the society be $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mu)$, where $\mu(\Omega)=1$ and $\mu$ is atomless.

We use the standard divisibility theorem for atomless finite measures: for every measurable $C$ and every $r\in[0,\mu(C)]$, there is a measurable subset of $C$ with measure $r$. Its hypotheses hold here.

Repeatedly bisecting each cell into equal-measure measurable pieces produces a measurable uniform label
$$ U:\Omega\longrightarrow[0,1]. $$
Indeed, the binary labels from the first $k$ bisections assign measure $2^{-k}$ to every dyadic interval of length $2^{-k}$. Thus the distribution of $U$ agrees with Lebesgue measure on dyadic intervals, and hence on all Borel sets.

Our policies maintain a finite partition of $[0,1]$ into intervals, with a common integer bundle on each interval. An interval of length $p$ represents recipient mass $p$.

Giving one copy to every recipient in that interval consumes copy mass $p$. If the remaining supply is $a<p$, split off a portion of length $a$, give one copy to that portion, and finish the round. Fixing interval order makes these operations deterministic.

Call each such assignment a batch. Each recipient receives at most one copy in a batch. Endpoint preimages have measure zero. Since the stream and the number of batches are finite, all comparisons can use one common full-measure set.

Batch termination and the finite representation are justified in Section 7.

3. A prefix-wise $1/\gamma$ guarantee for arbitrary sizes

Policy. Send zero-value goods to charity. For each positive-value arrival, repeatedly choose a minimum-value interval among those to which one copy feasibly fits. Allocate one copy throughout that interval, or throughout a portion if less supply remains. If no interval is feasible, send the remainder to charity.

This policy uses no horizon information.

Fix a finite stream containing positive values, and let
$$ d_{\min}=\min_{j:v_j>0}\frac{v_j}{s_j}, \qquad d_{\max}=\max_{j:v_j>0}\frac{v_j}{s_j}. $$
The density-spread assumption gives
$$ d_{\max}\le\gamma d_{\min}. $$
Every good assigned to a recipient has positive value.

Fix a completed round and a comparing recipient $x$ in the common full-measure set.

Comparison with another recipient

Let $S\subseteq A_y$ be feasible. If $v(S)=0$, the fairness inequality is immediate. Otherwise, choose a positive-value copy $f\in S$ assigned latest among the positive-value copies of $S$.

If $x$ could receive $f$ immediately before its batch, the minimum-value rule gives
$$ v(A_y^{\mathrm{before}}) \le v(A_x^{\mathrm{before}}). $$
Every positive-value copy of $S\setminus\{f\}$ was already assigned to $y$ before this batch. Therefore
$$ v(S\setminus\{f\}) \le v(A_y^{\mathrm{before}}) \le v(A_x^{\mathrm{before}}) \le v(A_x). $$

If $x$ could not receive $f$, then
$$ s(A_x^{\mathrm{before}})>1-s_f. $$
Hence
$$ v(A_x)\ge v(A_x^{\mathrm{before}}) >d_{\min}(1-s_f). $$
Since $S$ is feasible,
$$ v(S\setminus\{f\}) \le d_{\max}s(S\setminus\{f\}) \le d_{\max}(1-s_f). $$
Together these inequalities imply
$$ v(A_x)\ge\frac1\gamma v(S\setminus\{f\}). $$
If $s_f=1$, the residual comparison has value zero, so the same conclusion holds.

Comparison with charity

Let $S$ be a feasible charity comparison of positive value, and choose any positive-value copy $f\in S$.

Positive charity mass of its type was created only when no interval could receive another copy. At that time, every recipient in the common full-measure set satisfied
$$ s(A_x^{\mathrm{before}})>1-s_f. $$
The preceding density calculation again gives
$$ v(A_x)\ge\frac1\gamma v(S\setminus\{f\}). $$

This proof uses only the densities and feasibility of $S$. It therefore permits unrestricted charity multiplicities and does not require its other types to have arrived before $f$ was rejected.

All-zero streams are trivial. Thus the policy guarantees prefix-wise $(1/\gamma)$-FEF$1$.

4. An improved small-good lower bound

Fix a rational parameter $0<\theta\le1$.

Policy. Send zero-value goods to charity. For an arriving positive-value type of density $\rho=v/s$, declare an interval eligible if one copy fits and its current bundle value $w$ satisfies
$$ w<\theta\rho(1-s). $$
Repeatedly allocate to a minimum-value eligible interval. Send the remaining supply to charity when no interval is eligible.

Proposition. This policy guarantees prefix-wise $\theta$-FEF$1$ whenever
$$ \bigl(\theta+(1-\theta)\sigma\bigr)D+\sigma\le1. $$
It guarantees exact FEF$1$ between recipients.

Occupied-size bound

Normalize all positive values by $d_{\min}$. This is only an analysis device: scaling values and densities equally preserves the policy’s threshold tests and value ordering. Normalized positive densities lie in $[1,\gamma]$.

Put
$$ \beta=\theta+(1-\theta)\sigma. $$
Fix one recipient’s accepted copies. For an accepted copy of normalized density $r$ and size $s$, let $w_0$ be the value immediately before acceptance. Then
$$ w_0<\theta r(1-s). $$
Interpolate through this copy: after an interpolated size $\ell\in[0,s]$, the cumulative value is $w=w_0+r\ell$. Therefore
$$ w<r\bigl(\theta(1-s)+\ell\bigr) \le r\bigl(\theta+(1-\theta)s\bigr) \le r\beta. $$
Since $r\ge1$, along this interpolation,
$$ \frac{d\ell}{dw}=\frac1r \le\min\left\{1,\frac{\beta}{w}\right\}, $$
where the right side is interpreted as $1$ at $w=0$.

The last accepted copy gives a final normalized value smaller than $\gamma\beta$. Summing the interpolated sizes and integrating yields
$$ \begin{aligned} s(A_x) &\le \int_0^{\gamma\beta} \min\left\{1,\frac{\beta}{w}\right\}\,dw\\ &=\beta+\beta\ln\gamma\\ &=\beta D. \end{aligned} $$
The empty bundle satisfies the same bound.

This estimate applies after every intermediate batch. Under the proposition’s hypothesis,
$$ s(A_x)\le1-\sigma. $$
Consequently, every arriving good fits every recipient. Eligibility can fail only because of the value threshold.

Charity comparisons

Suppose positive mass of type $j$ is sent to charity. Since all recipients could receive it, every recipient then satisfies
$$ v(A_x)\ge\theta\rho_j(1-s_j). $$
Values only increase, so this inequality persists.

Let $S$ be any feasible charity comparison of positive value. Choose a copy $f\in S$ having maximum positive density $\rho_f$. Its type has positive charity mass, so
$$ v(A_x)\ge\theta\rho_f(1-s_f). $$
Every remaining positive-value copy has density at most $\rho_f$. Thus
$$ v(S\setminus\{f\}) \le\rho_f s(S\setminus\{f\}) \le\rho_f(1-s_f). $$
It follows that
$$ v(A_x)\ge\theta v(S\setminus\{f\}). $$

Recipient comparisons, including empty bundles

If $A_y$ is empty, every comparison with it has value zero.

Otherwise, let $f$ be the last copy assigned to $y$. Immediately before its batch, every recipient could receive $f$.

If $x$ was eligible, minimum-value selection gives
$$ v(A_x^{\mathrm{before}}) \ge v(A_y^{\mathrm{before}}). $$
If $x$ was ineligible, then
$$ v(A_x^{\mathrm{before}}) \ge\theta\rho_f(1-s_f) >v(A_y^{\mathrm{before}}). $$
Therefore, at the completed round,
$$ v(A_x)\ge v(A_y\setminus\{f\}). $$
For $S\subseteq A_y$, remove $f$ when $f\in S$. Otherwise, $S\subseteq A_y\setminus\{f\}$ and no removal is needed. This proves exact recipient-to-recipient FEF$1$.

Finally, the sufficient condition rearranges to
$$ \theta\le \frac{1-(D+1)\sigma}{D(1-\sigma)}. $$
When this expression is positive, rational choices of $\theta$ approach it from below. Taking the supremum over policies proves the lower bound $B_\gamma(\sigma)$.

5. The logarithmic upper bound in both horizon models

Fix
$$ \alpha>\frac1{1+\ln\gamma}. $$
Choose a dyadic rational $g$ with
$$ 1<g<\gamma, \qquad \alpha(1+\ln g)>1. $$
Continuity of the logarithm and density of dyadic rationals justify this choice.

For a power of two $K$, define dyadic densities
$$ h_k=1+\frac{k(g-1)}K, \qquad 0\le k\le K, $$
and put
$$ R_K=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\left(1-\frac{h_k}{h_{k+1}}\right). $$
Writing $\Delta=(g-1)/K$, this is the right Riemann sum
$$ R_K=\Delta\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac1{h_{k+1}}. $$
Monotonicity of $1/h$ gives
$$ 0\le\ln g-R_K \le \Delta\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \left(\frac1{h_k}-\frac1{h_{k+1}}\right) = \Delta(1-1/g). $$
Thus $R_K\to\ln g$. Choose $K$ large enough that
$$ \alpha(1+R_K)>1. $$
Next choose a dyadic size $\delta=2^{-r}\le\sigma$ sufficiently small that
$$ \alpha(1-\delta)(1+R_K)>1. $$

There are $K+1$ rounds. Round $k$ supplies mass
$$ \delta^{-1}+1 $$
of a type with size $\delta$ and value $h_k\delta$. All inputs are binary rationals, and the density spread is $g<\gamma$.

Forced prefix benchmarks

Every recipient holds at most $\delta^{-1}$ copies in total. Consequently, at least mass $1$ from each round goes to charity.

After round $k$, charity permits a comparison of $\delta^{-1}$ copies of that round’s type. This comparison has size $1$ and, after any permitted deletion, value at least
$$ h_k(1-\delta). $$
Prefix-wise $\alpha$-FEF$1$ therefore requires almost every recipient to have value at least
$$ \alpha h_k(1-\delta). $$

Let $z_k$ be the per-capita size allocated to recipients from round $k$, and $W_k$ their average value after that round. These averages exist: the measurable bundles have bounded size and bounded value. Additivity gives
$$ W_k=\sum_{i=0}^k h_i z_i, \qquad W_k\ge b h_k, \qquad b=\alpha(1-\delta). $$

Capacity contradiction

Set $W_{-1}=0$. Summation by parts gives
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{k=0}^K z_k &=\sum_{k=0}^K\frac{W_k-W_{k-1}}{h_k}\\ &=\frac{W_K}{h_K} +\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} W_k\left(\frac1{h_k}-\frac1{h_{k+1}}\right)\\ &\ge b+b\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \left(1-\frac{h_k}{h_{k+1}}\right)\\ &=\alpha(1-\delta)(1+R_K)>1. \end{aligned} $$
The inequality uses only nonnegative coefficients, since the densities increase.

Individual feasibility, however, implies
$$ \sum_{k=0}^K z_k\le1. $$
This contradiction proves the upper bound $1/D$.

The stream is fixed and finite. Announcing its length does not change the contradiction, so the bound holds in both horizon models.

6. Forced equalization and the packing upper bounds

The next lemma is the key additional result.

Lemma: an integer supply can force identical bundles

Suppose the society initially has empty bundles. One round supplies mass $k$ of a single type, where $k\ge1$ is an integer. Its size $s$ and value $v$ satisfy
$$ 0<s\le\frac1{k+1}, \qquad v>0. $$
If the resulting allocation is $\alpha$-FEF$1$ with
$$ \alpha>\frac{k-1}{k}, $$
then almost every recipient receives exactly $k$ copies and charity receives zero mass.

Proof. Let $n(x)$ be the number of copies received by $x$, and let $c$ be the charity mass. Conservation gives
$$ \int_\Omega n(x)\,d\mu(x)+c=k. $$
The count function is measurable and bounded by $\lfloor1/s\rfloor$.

If $c>0$, charity permits a comparison of $k+1$ copies, because $(k+1)s\le1$. After deleting at most one copy, its value is at least $kv$. Fairness therefore implies
$$ n(x)v\ge\alpha kv>(k-1)v $$
for almost every $x$. Since $n(x)$ is an integer, $n(x)\ge k$ almost everywhere. This contradicts
$$ \int_\Omega n(x)\,d\mu(x)=k-c<k. $$
Thus $c=0$ and the average count is $k$.

Suppose that the set of recipients with at least $k+1$ copies has positive measure. Choose such a recipient $y$ in the full-measure fairness set. A submultiset of $k+1$ copies in $y$’s bundle is feasible. Comparing against it again forces $n(x)\ge k$ almost everywhere.

The positive-measure set with $n(x)\ge k+1$ would then make the average count strictly greater than $k$, a contradiction. Hence $n(x)\le k$ almost everywhere. Its average is $k$, so $n(x)=k$ almost everywhere. This proves the lemma.

The charity step uses precisely the stipulated unrestricted multiplicities: even a small positive charity mass makes the $k+1$-copy comparison available.

Proposition: a two-round packing obstruction

Fix an integer $q\ge2$ and suppose
$$ \sigma>\frac1{q+1}. $$
Let
$$ b=\min\left\{\sigma,\frac1q\right\}. $$
I prove that no policy can guarantee a factor
$$ \alpha> \max\left\{ \frac{q-1}{q}, \frac{1-b}{\gamma(q-1)b} \right\}. $$

Choose a dyadic density $g$ with $1<g<\gamma$ and a dyadic size $h\le b$, sufficiently close to $\gamma$ and $b$, respectively, that
$$ h>\frac1{q+1}, \qquad \alpha g(q-1)h>1-h, \qquad \frac{1-h}{q}<\sigma. $$
These choices are possible by the strict assumed inequality and continuity. At $h=b$, the last inequality holds: if $b=\sigma$, it follows from $\sigma>1/(q+1)$; if $b=1/q$, it follows from $\sigma\ge1/q$.

Choose a dyadic $s$ satisfying
$$ \frac{1-h}{q} < s < \min\left\{ \frac1{q+1}, \sigma, \frac{\alpha g(q-1)h}{q} \right\}. $$
Each upper endpoint is strictly larger than the lower endpoint, so dyadic density guarantees such an $s$.

Use the following two rounds:

  • Mass $q$ of a low type with size $s$ and value $s$.
  • Mass $1$ of a high type with size $h$ and value $gh$.

All inputs are binary rationals. The two positive densities are $1$ and $g<\gamma$.

If the policy fails fairness after round one, the counterexample is complete. Otherwise, the lemma applies because
$$ s<\frac1{q+1}, \qquad \alpha>\frac{q-1}{q}. $$
Almost every recipient therefore has exactly $q$ low copies.

Their occupied size satisfies
$$ qs>1-h. $$
Thus no high copy fits almost any recipient. The second-round supply goes to charity, apart from assignments on a null set, which consume zero copy mass.

Charity now permits $q$ high copies, since
$$ qh\le1. $$
After deleting at most one, the comparison retains value at least
$$ (q-1)gh. $$
Every recipient in a full-measure set still has value $qs$, whereas our choice of $s$ gives
$$ qs<\alpha(q-1)gh. $$
Fairness fails after round two.

This proves the packing upper bound. The proof works when the horizon $T=2$ is announced.

Exact fixed-size regimes

If $\sigma\ge1/q$, then $b=1/q$, and the packing upper bound becomes
$$ F_\infty^{\mathrm{ann}}(\sigma) \le \max\left\{\frac{q-1}{q},\frac1\gamma\right\}. $$
If also $\gamma\le q/(q-1)$, then
$$ \frac1\gamma\ge\frac{q-1}{q}. $$
Combining with Section 3 yields
$$ F_\infty^{\mathrm{unk}}(\sigma) = F_\infty^{\mathrm{ann}}(\sigma) = \frac1\gamma. $$
The greedy policy attains this factor.

Equivalently, put
$$ p(\sigma)=\max\left\{2,\left\lceil\frac1\sigma\right\rceil\right\}. $$
The frontier is determined whenever
$$ \gamma\le\frac{p(\sigma)}{p(\sigma)-1}. $$

These regimes also show that the logarithmic constant is not the fixed-size frontier universally. Indeed,
$$ \ln\gamma<\gamma-1 $$
for $\gamma>1$, by integrating $1/t<1$ over $(1,\gamma)$. Hence
$$ \frac1\gamma<\frac1{1+\ln\gamma}. $$

7. Running time and representation size

Here $\tau=1$: generated intervals with different bundles are allocation states, not different complete recipient types.

Let $L$ be the total binary input length, and define
$$ s_{\min}=\min_j s_j, \qquad Q=\left\lfloor\frac1{s_{\min}}\right\rfloor. $$
Every recipient receives at most $Q$ copies over the entire stream.

For the threshold policy, let $b_\theta$ be the encoding length of its rational parameter; for greedy, set $b_\theta=0$.

A full-interval batch adds no interval. A partial batch introduces at most one cut and finishes its round. Therefore:

  • Before round $t$, there are at most $t$ intervals.
  • During that round, there are at most $tQ$ full batches and one partial batch.
  • After any prefix, there are at most $T+1$ intervals.

Selecting each eligible minimum by scanning the intervals gives, including bundle-vector bookkeeping, the sufficient bit running-time bound
$$ O\left( \bigl(m\tau+mT+T^3(Q+1)\bigr) \operatorname{poly}\bigl( L+b_\theta+\log(m(Q+1)) \bigr) \right), \qquad \tau=1. $$
All interval endpoints remain dyadic: updates use additions and subtractions of masses and endpoints. Bundle counts are integers bounded by $Q$; bundle sizes and values are exact sums with polynomial bit length in the displayed parameters. Eligibility tests use exact rational arithmetic.

The allocation after a prefix is represented by at most $T+1$ interval records and their integer bundle vectors. The full transition record contains
$$ O\bigl(T^2(Q+1)\bigr) $$
batches.

These are running times for the finite symbolic allocation representation interpreted through the fixed measurable label $U$. No polynomial-time bound in $L$ alone is asserted: $Q$ can be exponentially large in binary input length. Both constructive policies work without knowing $T$.

8. The limit and the remaining open regimes

Since
$$ \lim_{\sigma\downarrow0}B_\gamma(\sigma)=\frac1D, $$
the bounds from Sections 1, 4, and 5 imply, by squeezing,
$$ \lim_{\sigma\downarrow0}F_\infty^{\mathrm{unk}}(\sigma) = \lim_{\sigma\downarrow0}F_\infty^{\mathrm{ann}}(\sigma) = \frac1{1+\ln\gamma}. $$

The fixed-size frontier is also completely determined in the regimes of Section 6. Outside those regimes, the proved upper and lower bounds need not match. The arguments therefore leave the general fixed-positive-size frontier and equality of the two horizon frontiers unresolved.
