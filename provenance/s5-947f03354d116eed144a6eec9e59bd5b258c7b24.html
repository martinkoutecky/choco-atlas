The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite complete valuation types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), good kinds \(\mathcal{K}\) with rational per-household supplies \(q_g\), a conflict graph \(H\) on \(\mathcal{K}\), and rational additive values \(v_t(g)\), can one compute in time polynomial in the explicit input size and bit lengths a finite-support allocation \(x_{t,b}\)? Each bundle \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{\mathcal{K}}\) must have independent positive support in \(H\); copies of the same kind do not conflict. Require \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}b_gx_{t,b}=q_g\), and pairwise EF1 across every positively used type-bundle and every positively used target bundle, where EF1 is evaluated on the integral bundles. For the Theorem 5 mirror, restrict to \(\rho=\sum_g q_g\le 2\) and \(\delta=\max_g\sum_{h:gh\in E(H)}q_h\le 2/3\).

The general complexity classification remains unresolved. I prove a new structural result that advances the construction problem:

Any singleton assignment of household mass $1$ can be extended, in polynomial time, to use all supplies with bundles of at most three copies, while preserving every household’s initial copy.

The construction uses two exact rational maximum-flow computations. It yields an EF1 allocation under an explicitly checkable condition on valuations. I also prove that its bound on unmatched supply is sharp, and that three-copy bundles are necessary on some promised instances throughout the range $\frac12<\delta\le\frac23$.

For completeness, I re-establish existence, polynomial-size certificates, and polynomial construction for fixed $\tau$. These conclusions do not resolve polynomial construction for unrestricted $\tau$.

1. Definitions and algorithmic tools

For a feasible bundle $b$, define its size and its EF1 residual value by
$$ |b|=\sum_g b_g, \qquad R_t(b)=v_t(b)-\max\bigl(\{v_t(g):b_g>0\}\cup\{0\}\bigr). $$
Thus an allocation is EF1 precisely when
$$ v_t(b)\ge R_t(c) $$
for every two positive entries $(t,b)$ and $(s,c)$.

Write $e_g$ for one copy of kind $g$. Compatibility includes equality of kinds: because $H$ has no loops, two copies of the same kind are compatible.

I use the following standard algorithmic facts, with their exact-arithmetic guarantees made explicit.

  • A directed network with nonnegative rational capacities has an exactly computable rational maximum flow and a minimum cut, in time polynomial in its number of vertices, number of edges, and capacity encoding length. Indeed, multiply capacities by the product of their denominators and run the Edmonds–Karp algorithm. It uses $O(VE^2)$ augmentations independently of capacity magnitudes. Integer arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity because the scaling factor has polynomial bit length.
  • Feasibility of a finite system of rational linear equalities and inequalities can be decided in polynomial bit complexity. When feasible, an exact rational feasible point of polynomial encoding length can be returned.

Neither theorem requires expanding a rational capacity into individual units.

2. Existence and polynomial-size certificates

Proposition 1: every promised input admits an allocation

Every promised input has a finite rational EF1 allocation using bundles of at most three copies.

Proof. Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of all masses and supplies. Construct a finite society with $D$ agents, of whom $D\mu_t$ have type $t$, and with $Dq_g$ individual copies of kind $g$. Join individual copies exactly when their kinds conflict.

The number of individual goods is
$$ D\rho\le 2D. $$
Every existing copy of kind $g$ has degree
$$ D\sum_{h:gh\in E(H)}q_h\le \frac{2D}{3}. $$
Valuations are nonnegative and additive, so the finite-agent theorem supplied in the question applies.

The cited algorithm also gives a three-copy bound: its intermediate assigned bundles have one or two goods, swaps permute such bundles, and its terminal matching adds at most two goods to a singleton. If there are at most $D$ goods, singleton and empty bundles suffice directly. These properties are visible in [Theorem 5, Algorithm 4, and Definition 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13083v1#S4).

For each type–bundle pair, let $k_{t,b}$ count the agents receiving that kind-count bundle, and set
$$ x_{t,b}=\frac{k_{t,b}}{D}. $$
Counting agents and copies proves both required equality constraints. Feasibility follows from the construction of the expanded conflict graph.

For two distinct positive entries, select representative agents and apply finite-agent EF1. Removing one individual good removes exactly one copy of its kind. Comparison of an entry with itself follows from $R_t(b)\le v_t(b)$. Therefore the aggregated allocation satisfies the required EF1 condition. $\square$

This is an existence proof, not a polynomial construction: although $\log D\le L$, the numerical value of $D$ can be exponential in $L$.

Proposition 2: short exact certificates

Every promised input has such an allocation with at most $m+\tau$ positive entries and polynomial total encoding length.

Proof. Starting from Proposition 1, represent entry $(t,b)$ by the integer column
$$ a_{t,b}=(e_t,b)\in\mathbb Z^{\tau+m}. $$
The mass and supply equations become
$$ Ax=d, \qquad d=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau,q_1,\ldots,q_m). $$

If the used columns are dependent, choose a nonzero rational vector $z$ with $Az=0$. Summing its type-coordinate equations gives $\sum_i z_i=0$, so $z$ has both signs. Set
$$ \alpha=\min_{i:z_i>0}\frac{x_i}{z_i}, \qquad x'=x-\alpha z. $$
Then $Ax'=d$, every coordinate remains nonnegative, and at least one becomes zero. Deleting zero entries preserves EF1 because it only removes comparisons. Repeating leaves $k\le m+\tau$ independent columns.

Choose $k$ rows giving an invertible square matrix $C$. Its entries are integers between $0$ and $3$. Write $a=Dd_I$ for the selected right-hand side after clearing denominators. Each coordinate of $a$ lies between $0$ and $2D$. Cramer’s rule gives
$$ x_j=\frac{\det C^{(j)}(a)}{D\det C}. $$
Hadamard’s inequality gives
$$ |\det C|\le (3\sqrt{k})^k, \qquad |\det C^{(j)}(a)| \le 2D\sqrt{k}(3\sqrt{k})^{k-1}. $$
Thus each mass has encoding length
$$ O\bigl(L+k\log(k+1)\bigr). $$
The bundles have at most three copies, so their encodings are also polynomial. $\square$

Consequently, a proposed bounded certificate can be verified in polynomial time by checking the rational equalities, conflict feasibility, and every required EF1 comparison.

3. A polynomial extension theorem for singleton assignments

This section concerns feasibility first; fairness is addressed afterward.

Suppose $1<\rho\le2$, and suppose singleton masses $y_{t,g}\ge0$ satisfy
$$ \sum_g y_{t,g}=\mu_t, \qquad z_g:=\sum_t y_{t,g}\le q_g. $$
Thus every household initially receives one copy. Let
$$ r_g=q_g-z_g, \qquad \sum_g r_g=\rho-1. $$

An initial class is a positive entry $a=(t,g)$, with mass $y_a=y_{t,g}$ and anchor kind $g_a=g$.

Proposition 3: extension preserving all anchors

Under $\delta\le\frac23$, these singleton assignments can be extended in polynomial time to a complete feasible allocation with at most three copies per household. Every household retains its anchor.

Proof.

Step 1: balance the two sides

Introduce an auxiliary kind $\ast$, with no conflicts, whose residual supply is
$$ r_\ast=2-\rho. $$
It has no initial assigned copies. The total residual supply, including this auxiliary kind, is now $1$.

The augmented total supply is $2$. Its weighted conflict-degree bound remains $\delta$, because the auxiliary kind has no incident edges.

At the end, delete auxiliary copies from all bundles.

Step 2: compute a maximum matching by rational flow

Construct a network with:

  • residual nodes $h$ of positive supply $r_h$, with source capacity $r_h$;
  • initial-class nodes $a$, with sink capacity $y_a$;
  • capacity $2$ from $h$ to $a$ whenever $h$ and $g_a$ are compatible.

Let $f_{a,h}$ be a maximum flow, and put
$$ u_h=r_h-\sum_a f_{a,h}, \qquad d=\sum_h u_h. $$
Both sides have total capacity $1$, so the total unused class capacity is also $d$.

If $d=0$, assign the matched residual copy to each corresponding anchor mass. This already proves the proposition, with at most two copies before deleting auxiliary copies.

Assume $d>0$.

Step 3: identify the obstructing residual set

Form a directed bipartite graph with:

  • an arc $h\to a$ for every compatibility edge;
  • an arc $a\to h$ whenever $f_{a,h}>0$.

Let $U$ be the class nodes having unused capacity. Let $W$ consist of all nodes from which a node of $U$ is reachable. Define
$$ S=\{h:h\notin W\}, \qquad N(S)=\{a:a\text{ is compatible with some }h\in S\}. $$

Every unmatched residual node belongs to $S$. Otherwise an unmatched residual node could reach an unused class, giving an augmenting path. The forward compatibility edges always have positive residual capacity, since their capacity is $2$ and the total flow is at most $1$.

No class in $N(S)$ belongs to $W$, since an arc from $S$ to that class would make its residual predecessor reach $U$. Consequently every class in $N(S)$ is saturated.

Moreover, positive flow into such a class comes only from $S$. Indeed, if $f_{a,h}>0$ with $h\in W$, the reverse arc $a\to h$ would put $a$ in $W$.

Set
$$ s=\sum_{h\in S}r_h, \qquad n=\sum_{a\in N(S)}y_a. $$
All unmatched residual supply lies in $S$, and precisely $n$ units flow out of $S$. Hence
$$ d=s-n>0. $$

Let $Q$ be the initial classes outside $N(S)$. Their total mass is $1-n>0$. Every anchor in $Q$ conflicts with every residual kind in $S$.

For any $h\in S$, the conflicting anchor mass $1-n$ is bounded by its weighted conflict degree. Therefore
$$ n\ge1-\delta. $$
Conversely, choose any positive class in $Q$. Its anchor conflicts with all residual kinds in $S$, so
$$ s\le\delta. $$
Define
$$ \alpha=\delta+n-1\ge0. $$
Then
$$ d=s-n\le 2\delta-1-\alpha. $$

The auxiliary kind is not in $S$: it is compatible with every initial class, including every unused class.

Step 4: find a sufficiently large block of matched flow

The directed subgraph on $S\cup N(S)$ has no arcs leaving it. Choose a sink strongly connected component $B$ of this subgraph.

Every class in the subgraph is saturated and has positive mass, so it has an outgoing positive-flow arc. Every residual kind is compatible with initial mass at least
$$ 1-\sum_{g:gh\in E(H)}z_g\ge1-\delta>0, $$
so it also has an outgoing arc. Thus $B$ contains both residual and class nodes.

For a residual node in $B$, all its compatible initial classes belong to $B$, because $B$ is a sink component. Hence the total initial-class mass in $B$, denoted $K$, satisfies
$$ K\ge1-\delta. $$

Regard each positive edge $f_{a,h}$ inside $B$ as a token of capacity $f_{a,h}$, representing an anchor together with its already matched residual copy. All classes in $B$ are saturated, and their outgoing positive-flow arcs stay inside $B$. Therefore the total token capacity is exactly $K$.

Step 5: match the unmatched residual supply to these tokens

Connect an unmatched residual kind $h'\in S$ to token $(a,h)$ precisely when $h'$ is compatible with both $g_a$ and $h$.

For a fixed $h'\in S$, the capacity of forbidden tokens is at most
$$ \sum_{\substack{a\in B\\g_a h'\in E(H)}}y_a + \sum_{\substack{h\in B\\hh'\in E(H)}}r_h. $$
The first term counts part of the anchor inventory; the second counts part of the residual inventory. Together these inventories are bounded by the original supplies.

In addition, $h'$ conflicts with all anchor mass in $Q$, namely $1-n$. That inventory is disjoint from the token inventories just counted. Thus
$$ \text{forbidden token capacity} \le\delta-(1-n)=\alpha. $$
Every unmatched residual kind therefore has compatible token capacity at least
$$ K-\alpha\ge1-\delta-\alpha. $$
Since $\delta\le\frac23$,
$$ 2\delta-1\le1-\delta. $$
Combining this with the bound on $d$ gives
$$ K-\alpha\ge1-\delta-\alpha\ge d. $$

The total unmatched demand is $d$. Consequently every nonempty subset of unmatched residual kinds has neighborhood capacity at least $d$, hence at least its own demand. A second rational maximum-flow computation therefore saturates all unmatched residual demands.

Explicitly, use source capacities $u_{h'}$, token-to-sink capacities $f_{a,h}$, and compatibility-edge capacities $2$. A cut of capacity less than $d$ cannot cross a compatibility edge. If its source-side residual set is $X$, it must include all neighboring tokens, so its capacity is at least
$$ d-\sum_{h'\in X}u_{h'} +\text{capacity of the token neighborhood of }X \ge d. $$
This proves saturation by max-flow/min-cut.

Step 6: form the final bundles

Let $p_{h',a,h}$ be the second flow. For each initial class $a$, assign:

  • singleton $e_{g_a}$ to mass $y_a-\sum_h f_{a,h}$;
  • pair $e_{g_a}+e_h$ to mass $f_{a,h}-\sum_{h'}p_{h',a,h}$;
  • triple $e_{g_a}+e_h+e_{h'}$ to mass $p_{h',a,h}$.

All these masses are nonnegative. The first flow accounts for matched residual supply; the second accounts for exactly its unmatched remainder. Type masses and all supplies are therefore correct.

Every triple is feasible because its three pairwise compatibilities were checked. Repeated kinds cause no problem. All households retain their anchors.

Finally delete auxiliary copies, combine duplicate entries, and omit zero masses.

The first network has polynomial size. It has polynomially many positive-flow edges, so the token network also has polynomial size. Both exact flow computations and the final aggregation have polynomial bit complexity. $\square$

4. A sharp bound on the initial matching deficit

The proof above gives more than triple extendibility.

Proposition 4: quantitative deficit bound

For any singleton assignment of mass $1$, the unmatched original supply after a maximum one-copy extension satisfies
$$ d\le \max\left\{ 0,\, \min\{\rho+\delta-2,\;2\delta-1\} \right\}. $$
In particular, under the promises,
$$ d\le\frac13. $$

Proof. If $d=0$, the assertion holds. Otherwise use the set $S$ from Proposition 3. The auxiliary kind is outside $S$, so
$$ s\le\rho-1. $$
Together with $s\le\delta$ and $n\ge1-\delta$, this gives
$$ d=s-n \le \min\{\rho-1,\delta\}-(1-\delta) =\min\{\rho+\delta-2,\;2\delta-1\}. $$
The latter expression is positive whenever $d>0$.

The auxiliary residual supply is fully matched, since any unmatched auxiliary supply could be sent directly to an unused class. Therefore $d$ is also exactly the unmatched original supply.

Finally, $2\delta-1\le\frac13$ under $\delta\le\frac23$. $\square$

This explains the two polynomial regimes from the notes: when $\delta\le\frac12$ or $\rho+\delta\le2$, the deficit is zero.

5. When the extension is EF1

We now choose the initial singletons using valuations.

Process types in any fixed order. For the current type, repeatedly choose a most valuable kind with positive remaining supply and assign it to the largest possible mass of that type.

There is sufficient supply to fill household mass $1$ because $\rho>1$. Each batch exhausts either a remaining type mass or a remaining kind supply, so there are at most $m+\tau$ batches.

Let $y_{t,g}$ be the resulting singleton masses. For each type define
$$ a_t=\min\{v_t(g):y_{t,g}>0\}. $$
The minimum exists because $\mu_t>0$.

The greedy construction gives
$$ r_h>0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_t(h)\le a_t \quad\text{for every type }t. $$
Indeed, a kind with positive final residual supply was available during every initial choice.

Proposition 5: a checkable sufficient condition for exact EF1 construction

Run the first flow of Proposition 3 on these greedy singletons.

If its deficit is zero, its extension is EF1. If the deficit is positive, let $S$ be the residual set constructed there. Suppose
$$ v_t(h)+v_t(k)\le a_t $$
for every type $t$ and every compatible pair $h,k\in S$, allowing $h=k$.

Then the complete extension of Proposition 3 is EF1. This sufficient condition and the allocation can be computed in polynomial time for unrestricted $\tau$.

Proof. Every final household of type $t$ retains an initial kind $g$, so nonnegativity gives
$$ v_t(b)\ge v_t(g)\ge a_t. $$

Consider a target bundle $c$.

If it is a singleton, then $R_t(c)=0$.

If it is an anchor plus one original residual copy $h$, removing its anchor leaves value $v_t(h)\le a_t$. Since removal of a maximum-valued copy minimizes the remaining value,
$$ R_t(c)\le v_t(h)\le a_t. $$

If it is a triple, its two residual copies $h,k$ both belong to $S$. The unmatched copy belongs to $S$, and the token’s matched copy belongs to the sink component $B\subseteq S$. Removing the anchor therefore leaves value
$$ v_t(h)+v_t(k)\le a_t. $$
Again, $R_t(c)$ is no larger than the value left by that particular removal.

Deleting an auxiliary copy only produces an anchor singleton or an anchor–residual pair, already covered above.

Thus for every positive observer entry and every positive target entry,
$$ v_t(b)\ge a_t\ge R_t(c). $$
This is EF1.

Checking the sufficient condition requires at most $O(\tau m^2)$ rational comparisons. All construction steps have polynomial complexity by Proposition 3. $\square$

The condition can be weakened computationally: after constructing the extension, it suffices to check only the residual pairs actually used in positive triple entries.

If the condition fails, this procedure has not established nonexistence. Proposition 1 guarantees existence; the procedure has merely failed to certify fairness of its chosen extension.

Corollary: the previously established supply regimes

For unrestricted $\tau$, exact construction is polynomial whenever
$$ \delta\le\frac12 \qquad\text{or}\qquad \rho+\delta\le2. $$

For $\rho>1$, Proposition 4 gives zero deficit, and Proposition 5 gives EF1. For $\rho\le1$, use
$$ x_{t,e_g}=\mu_tq_g, \qquad x_{t,0}=\mu_t(1-\rho). $$
These masses satisfy all equalities, and every target residual is zero.

6. Sharpness and necessity of three-copy bundles

The following family lies in the parameter range left open by the previous round.

Choose a binary rational
$$ \frac12<\theta\le\frac23. $$
There is one type, with mass $1$, and three kinds $A,B,C$. Set
$$ q_A=q_B=\theta, \qquad q_C=2-2\theta. $$
The only conflict edge is $AB$. Values are
$$ v(A)=100, \qquad v(B)=1, \qquad v(C)=3. $$
Then
$$ \rho=2, \qquad \delta=\theta. $$

Proposition 6: the deficit bound is attained

The greedy singleton construction has maximum-flow deficit exactly
$$ 2\theta-1. $$

Proof. Greedy singletons assign $A$ to mass $\theta$ and $C$ to mass $1-\theta$. Residual supplies are
$$ r_B=\theta, \qquad r_C=1-\theta. $$
Residual $B$ is compatible only with the initial $C$ classes, of total mass $1-\theta$. Thus at least $2\theta-1$ units remain unmatched.

This lower bound is achieved by matching $1-\theta$ units of $B$ to the $C$ classes and $1-\theta$ units of $C$ to the $A$ classes. The latter is possible because $1-\theta<\theta$. $\square$

Proposition 7: no EF1 allocation uses only bundles of size at most two

Proof. Suppose such an allocation existed. Since $\rho=2$ and household mass is $1$,
$$ 0 = 2-\rho = \sum_b x_b(2-|b|). $$
Every summand is nonnegative, and every used mass is positive. Therefore every used bundle has exactly two copies.

The total mass of households containing $A$ is at most $q_A=\theta$. Hence some positive mass receives an $A$-free bundle, whose value is at most $6$.

If $2e_A$ were used, its residual value would be $100$, violating EF1 for that $A$-free household. Thus $2e_A$ is absent.

Because $AB$ is a conflict, every copy of $A$ must consequently belong to an $AC$ bundle. Such bundles have total mass $\theta$ and consume $\theta$ units of $C$.

If $2e_B$ were also absent, every copy of $B$ would have to belong to a $BC$ bundle, requiring another $\theta$ units of $C$. But after allocating $AC$ bundles, the remaining $C$ supply is
$$ 2-3\theta<\theta, $$
because $\theta>\frac12$. This is impossible. Therefore $2e_B$ has positive mass.

Its value is $2$, whereas an $AC$ target has residual value $3$. EF1 fails. $\square$

A complete EF1 allocation nevertheless exists explicitly:
$$ x_{e_A}=2\theta-1, \qquad x_{e_A+e_C}=1-\theta, $$
$$ x_{e_B+e_C}=2-3\theta, \qquad x_{e_C+2e_B}=2\theta-1, $$
with zero entries omitted.

All masses are nonnegative and sum to $1$. The kind totals are
$$ (2\theta-1)+(1-\theta)=\theta $$
for $A$,
$$ (2-3\theta)+2(2\theta-1)=\theta $$
for $B$, and
$$ (1-\theta)+(2-3\theta)+(2\theta-1)=2-2\theta $$
for $C$.

The used bundle values are $100,103,4,5$, and their residual values are $0,3,1,2$, respectively. Thus every observer value is at least every target residual.

Moreover, this family satisfies Proposition 5: its obstructing set is $S=\{B\}$, its least initial singleton value is $3$, and
$$ v(B)+v(B)=2\le3. $$
Hence the new algorithm succeeds on instances where a two-copy algorithm necessarily fails.

7. Polynomial construction for fixed support size

To retain the previous round’s algorithmic conclusion in a self-contained form, let $\mathcal B_3$ be all feasible bundles of size at most three, including the empty bundle. Its size satisfies
$$ B:=|\mathcal B_3|\le\binom{m+3}{3}. $$

For each type, form
$$ C_t=\{v_t(b):b\in\mathcal B_3\}. $$
Enumerate threshold vectors $a\in\prod_t C_t$. For a fixed vector, permit entry $(t,b)$ exactly when
$$ v_t(b)\ge a_t \qquad\text{and}\qquad R_s(b)\le a_s\quad\text{for every }s. $$
Solve the rational linear system consisting of nonnegative permitted masses and the required type and supply equalities.

Any feasible solution is EF1, since every positive observer and target satisfy
$$ v_t(b)\ge a_t\ge R_t(c). $$

Conversely, Proposition 1 supplies an allocation using $\mathcal B_3$. Set
$$ a_t=\min\{v_t(b):x_{t,b}>0\}. $$
For every used bundle $c$, comparison with a type-$t$ bundle attaining this minimum gives $R_t(c)\le a_t$. Therefore every used entry is permitted for this enumerated vector, so its linear system is feasible.

There are at most $B^\tau$ vectors. Each system has at most $\tau B$ variables and polynomial-length rational data. Exact rational linear feasibility therefore gives running time
$$ (m+1)^{O(\tau)}\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L). $$
Support reduction as in Proposition 2 yields at most $m+\tau$ positive entries. This is polynomial for every fixed $\tau$, but not for unrestricted $\tau$.

8. What remains unresolved

The new extension theorem removes one potential obstacle: extending singleton bundles to a complete feasible allocation with three-copy bundles is polynomial even for succinct rational masses.

The remaining obstacle is fairness. When the residual-pair domination condition fails, preserving the initial anchors may produce triples that some low-valued household envies after removal of one copy. The finite-agent algorithm can change bundles through envy swaps, but the arguments here do not give a polynomial bound for an analogous rational-mass procedure.

Thus the general construction question remains open for unrestricted $\tau$ in
$$ \frac12<\delta\le\frac23, \qquad 2-\delta<\rho\le2, $$
outside the additional sufficient condition proved above.

Feasibility itself is always affirmative on promised inputs. Any proposed hardness classification must therefore concern finding allocations, rather than deciding their existence, and must specify its search-reduction notion.
