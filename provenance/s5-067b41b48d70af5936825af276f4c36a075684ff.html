The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses μ, linear valuations v_t, per-agent budgets B_t, per-capita supplies q, and ε > 0, compute a feasible type-level allocation x and prices p forming an approximate Fisher equilibrium, with |v_t · x_t - u_t★| ≤ ε for every type t with μ_t > 0.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. There are \(m\geq1\) divisible goods with per-capita supplies \(q_j>0\). Each type has a per-agent budget \(B_t>0\) and linear valuation vector \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^m\); the valuation matrix has no zero row or column. All numerical inputs, including \(0<\varepsilon<1\), are binary rationals; let \(L\) be their total encoding length.
A type-level bundle \(x_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^m\) is allocated to every agent of type \(t\). Feasibility means \(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}\leq q_j\). At prices \(p\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^m\), define \(U_t(p)=\sup\{v_t\cdot y:y\geq0,\ p\cdot y\leq B_t\}\). An exact equilibrium has \(p\cdot x_t\leq B_t\), \(v_t\cdot x_t=U_t(p)\), and \(p_j(q_j-\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj})=0\); write \(u_t^\star\) for its unique utility for type \(t\).
Compute rational feasible \((x,p)\) satisfying \(p\cdot x_t\leq B_t\), \(U_t(p)-v_t\cdot x_t\leq\varepsilon\), exact market complementarity, and \(|v_t\cdot x_t-u_t^\star|\leq\varepsilon\) for every supported type.

Is this computation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite buyers, projected gradient computes feasible allocations with additive utility error \(\varepsilon\) in \(O(s\log s\log(1/\varepsilon))\) time, where \(s\) counts nonzero valuations and the hidden constant depends on valuations and budgets. Exact rational linear Fisher equilibria also admit a polynomial-time algorithm ([Devanur et al.](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/market.pdf)); no separate theorem for the stated mass formulation was verified.

Result

The computation problem is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, an exact rational equilibrium can be computed in polynomial time. The mass formulation reduces to a finite linear Fisher market with exactly \(\tau\) buyers.

1. The finite-market theorem

We use the following known result: a linear Fisher market with unit supplies, positive rational buyer budgets, and nonnegative rational valuations with no zero row or column admits an exact rational equilibrium computable in polynomial time in its binary encoding length. The algorithm produces prices and allocations of polynomial encoding length. This is established in Devanur, Papadimitriou, Saberi, and Vazirani, Section 2, Lemma 7.1, and Theorem 8.12. [Source](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/market.pdf)

Here an exact equilibrium consists of feasible allocations that maximize each buyer’s utility subject to its budget, together with prices satisfying market complementarity.

2. Constructing the finite market

Create one buyer for each supported type \(t\). Give buyer \(t\) budget

\(\displaystyle M_t=\mu_t B_t\)

and valuation coefficients

\(\displaystyle w_{tj}=q_jv_{tj}.\)

Each good in the constructed market has supply \(1\). Write \(z_{tj}\) for its allocation to buyer \(t\), and \(\pi_j\) for its price.

All hypotheses of the finite-market theorem hold:

  • Each budget \(M_t\) is positive because \(\mu_t>0\) and \(B_t>0\).
  • Every \(w_{tj}\) is a nonnegative rational.
  • Multiplication by \(q_j>0\) preserves the zero pattern of the valuation matrix, so there is no zero row or column.
  • There are exactly \(\tau\) buyers and \(m\) goods.

Apply the theorem to obtain an exact rational equilibrium \((z,\pi)\).

3. Transferring individual optimality

Define the output by

\(\displaystyle x_{tj}=\frac{q_jz_{tj}}{\mu_t},\qquad p_j=\frac{\pi_j}{q_j}.\)

To verify optimality, define the constructed buyer’s optimal utility as

\(\displaystyle W_t(\pi)=\sup\left\{\sum_jw_{tj}a_j:a\geq0,\ \sum_j\pi_ja_j\leq M_t\right\}.\)

For each fixed type \(t\), the transformation

\(\displaystyle a_j=\frac{\mu_ty_j}{q_j}\)

is a bijection between nonnegative bundles \(y\) and nonnegative bundles \(a\). Under this transformation,

\(\displaystyle \sum_j\pi_ja_j=\mu_t\sum_jp_jy_j,\qquad \sum_jw_{tj}a_j=\mu_t\sum_jv_{tj}y_j.\)

Consequently, the original budget constraint is equivalent to the constructed budget constraint:

\(\displaystyle p\cdot y\leq B_t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \pi\cdot a\leq M_t.\)

Taking suprema over these corresponding feasible bundles gives

\(\displaystyle W_t(\pi)=\mu_tU_t(p).\)

Because \((z,\pi)\) is an exact equilibrium,

\(\displaystyle \pi\cdot z_t\leq M_t,\qquad w_t\cdot z_t=W_t(\pi).\)

Therefore,

\(\displaystyle p\cdot x_t=\frac{\pi\cdot z_t}{\mu_t}\leq B_t\)

and

\(\displaystyle v_t\cdot x_t=\frac{w_t\cdot z_t}{\mu_t}=\frac{W_t(\pi)}{\mu_t}=U_t(p).\)

Thus every supported type receives an exactly optimal affordable bundle.

4. Feasibility, complementarity, and utility accuracy

For each good \(j\), feasibility of \(z\) gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}=q_j\sum_tz_{tj}\leq q_j.\)

Moreover,

\(\displaystyle p_j\left(q_j-\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}\right)=\pi_j\left(1-\sum_tz_{tj}\right)=0,\)

where the last equality is market complementarity in the constructed equilibrium.

Hence \((x,p)\) is an exact equilibrium of the original society. By the stipulated uniqueness of equilibrium utility for each supported type,

\(\displaystyle v_t\cdot x_t=u_t^\star.\)

Both requested error quantities are therefore zero:

\(\displaystyle U_t(p)-v_t\cdot x_t=0,\qquad \left|v_t\cdot x_t-u_t^\star\right|=0.\)

They satisfy the required bounds for every positive \(\varepsilon\).

5. Binary complexity

Multiplication of two binary rationals produces a rational whose numerator and denominator have bit lengths bounded by the sums of the corresponding input bit lengths, up to constant overhead. Thus each \(M_t\) and \(w_{tj}\) has encoding length \(O(L)\), and the complete constructed market has encoding length \(O((m\tau+\tau)L)\).

The finite-market algorithm consequently runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and returns rational \(z,\pi\) of polynomial encoding length. The output transformations multiply or divide these rationals by input rationals. They therefore preserve polynomial encoding length and take polynomial time.

No buyer replication is involved, even when a mass is exponentially small. The algorithm computes an exact equilibrium independently of the accuracy parameter, so its running time is also polynomial in the requested parameters \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\).
