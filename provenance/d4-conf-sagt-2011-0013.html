The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses and a symmetric binary affinity matrix, compute the worst welfare ratio between any feasible allocation and a two-sided exchange-stable mass allocation, or decide for rational \(R\) whether that ratio exceeds \(R\). In particular, determine whether the ratio is at most \(1+2(m-1)\gamma_m^*\), the analogue of Theorem 5’s bound.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(p_a\), each a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Mass is nonatomic and need not sum to one. State any running times in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length.
There are \(m\geq2\) houses with rational quotas \(q_h\geq2\), satisfying \(\sum_hq_h=\sum_ap_a\), and common desirabilities \(D_h\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\). Assume either all quotas are equal or all desirabilities are equal. Houses have zero utility.
The affinity matrix \(A\in\{0,1\}^{\tau\times\tau}\) is symmetric; diagonal entries describe affinity between distinct individuals of the same type. A feasible allocation is a real matrix \(x_{ah}\geq0\) with \(\sum_hx_{ah}=p_a\) and \(\sum_ax_{ah}=q_h\); there are no vacancies.
A type \(a\) individual's utility in house \(h\) is \(u_{ah}(x)=D_h+\sum_bA_{ab}x_{bh}\). Define two-sided exchange stability by infinitesimal equal-mass exchanges: no \(a,b,h\neq g\) with \(x_{ah}>0\) and \(x_{bg}>0\) satisfy \(u_{ag}(x)\geq u_{ah}(x)\) and \(u_{bh}(x)\geq u_{bg}(x)\), with at least one strict inequality. Houses always consent; indifferent exchanges do not block.
Define \(E=\frac12\sum_{a,b}A_{ab}p_ap_b>0\), \(E_{\mathrm{in}}(x)=\frac12\sum_h\sum_{a,b}A_{ab}x_{ah}x_{bh}\), \(W(x)=\sum_hq_hD_h+2E_{\mathrm{in}}(x)\), and \(\gamma_m^*=\max_{x\text{ feasible}}E_{\mathrm{in}}(x)/E\).
Let \(\operatorname{PoA}=\max_{x\text{ feasible}}W(x)/\inf_{x\text{ stable}}W(x)\), interpreting a zero denominator as \(+\infty\).
Does \(\operatorname{PoA}\leq1+2(m-1)\gamma_m^*\) hold for every such mass instance, or can this bound be violated?
For discrete individuals, binary affinities, integer quotas at least two, and nonnegative integer common desirabilities, the same bound holds when quotas or desirabilities are equal; a family asymptotically attains it. [Bodine-Baron et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1104.0052)

Result

The bound holds for every instance in the question:

\(\operatorname{PoA}\leq 1+2(m-1)\gamma_m^*.\)

Here is a direct proof for the specified nonatomic exchange rule. The analogous discrete statement is [Theorem 5 of Bodine-Baron et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1104.0052).

1. Affinity decomposition and existence of stable allocations

Write \(x_h=(x_{ah})_{a=1}^{\tau}\), and define

\(B=\sum_hq_hD_h,\qquad I_h(x)=\frac12x_h^{\mathsf T}Ax_h,\qquad I(x)=\sum_hI_h(x)=E_{\mathrm{in}}(x).\)

For distinct houses, define \(C_{hg}(x)=x_h^{\mathsf T}Ax_g\). Symmetry of \(A\) gives \(C_{hg}=C_{gh}\). Since \(\sum_hx_h=p\), expanding the quadratic form gives

\(E=I(x)+\sum_{h<g}C_{hg}(x).\)

All these affinity quantities are nonnegative. Consequently, \(I(x)\leq E\).

Let \(P=\sum_ap_a=\sum_hq_h>0\). The allocation \(x_{ah}=p_aq_h/P\) is feasible. The feasible set is closed and bounded in \(\mathbb R^{\tau m}\), hence compact. Therefore the continuous function \(I\) attains its maximum. Moreover, at the proportional allocation,

\(\displaystyle I(x)=E\sum_h\left(\frac{q_h}{P}\right)^2>0.\)

Thus, writing \(\gamma=\gamma_m^*\), we have \(0<\gamma\leq1\), and optimal welfare is

\(W_{\max}=B+2\gamma E.\)

We next prove that every maximizer of \(I\) is stable. Suppose an allocation \(x\) admits a blocking exchange between type \(a\) in house \(h\) and type \(b\) in house \(g\). Set

\(s=\bigl(u_{ag}(x)-u_{ah}(x)\bigr)+\bigl(u_{bh}(x)-u_{bg}(x)\bigr)>0.\)

The two types must differ: if \(a=b\), the two gains are negatives of each other and cannot both be nonnegative with one strict.

Let \(t=e_a-e_b\), where \(e_a,e_b\) are standard basis vectors. For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), the exchange

\(x_h(\varepsilon)=x_h-\varepsilon t,\qquad x_g(\varepsilon)=x_g+\varepsilon t\)

is feasible because \(x_{ah}>0\) and \(x_{bg}>0\). All other columns remain fixed. The desirability terms cancel in the sum of gains, so

\(s=t^{\mathsf T}A(x_g-x_h).\)

Using symmetry of \(A\), exact expansion gives

\(I(x(\varepsilon))-I(x)=\varepsilon s+\varepsilon^2t^{\mathsf T}At.\)

Since \(s>0\), this expression is positive for sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\). Hence a maximizer cannot admit a blocking exchange. In particular, stable allocations exist.

2. A bound for each pair of houses

Fix a stable allocation \(x\) and distinct houses \(h,g\). We claim that

\(C_{hg}(x)\leq 2\bigl(I_h(x)+I_g(x)\bigr)+R_{hg},\)

where

\(R_{hg}=\max\{q_h(D_h-D_g),\,q_g(D_g-D_h)\}.\)

To prove this, consider two exhaustive cases.

Case 1. Every type present in house \(h\) weakly prefers staying there:

\(u_{ag}(x)\leq u_{ah}(x)\quad\text{whenever }x_{ah}>0.\)

Multiply each inequality by \(x_{ah}\) and sum over types. This yields

\(q_h(D_g-D_h)+C_{hg}(x)-2I_h(x)\leq0,\)

and therefore

\(C_{hg}(x)\leq2I_h(x)+q_h(D_h-D_g).\)

Case 2. Some type \(a\) present in house \(h\) strictly prefers house \(g\):

\(u_{ag}(x)>u_{ah}(x).\)

For every type \(b\) with \(x_{bg}>0\), stability forces

\(u_{bh}(x)<u_{bg}(x).\)

Indeed, a weak preference in the opposite direction would combine with type \(a\)'s strict gain to form a blocking exchange. Multiplying these inequalities by \(x_{bg}\) and summing gives

\(q_g(D_h-D_g)+C_{hg}(x)-2I_g(x)<0.\)

In particular,

\(C_{hg}(x)\leq2I_g(x)+q_g(D_g-D_h).\)

In either case, nonnegativity of \(I_h,I_g\) proves the claimed pairwise bound.

3. Summing under either hypothesis

Each \(I_h\) occurs in exactly \(m-1\) unordered house pairs. Thus the decomposition of \(E\) and the pairwise bound imply

\(\displaystyle E\leq(2m-1)I(x)+\sum_{h<g}R_{hg}.\)

We now check the two allowed hypotheses.

Equal desirabilities. If \(D_h=D\) for all houses, then \(R_{hg}=0\). Since \(B\geq0\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{h<g}R_{hg}=0\leq(m-1)B.\)

Equal quotas. If \(q_h=q\) for all houses, then \(R_{hg}=q|D_h-D_g|\). Nonnegativity of the desirabilities gives \(|D_h-D_g|\leq D_h+D_g\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{h<g}R_{hg}\leq q\sum_{h<g}(D_h+D_g)=(m-1)q\sum_hD_h=(m-1)B.\)

Therefore, under either hypothesis, every stable allocation satisfies

\(E\leq(2m-1)I(x)+(m-1)B.\)

4. Concluding the price-of-anarchy bound

Set \(K=1+2(m-1)\gamma\). For any stable allocation, the preceding inequality gives

\(\displaystyle W_{\max}=B+2\gamma E\leq K B+2(2m-1)\gamma I(x).\)

Because \(\gamma\leq1\),

\((2m-1)\gamma\leq1+2(m-1)\gamma=K.\)

Using \(I(x)\geq0\), we obtain

\(W_{\max}\leq K\bigl(B+2I(x)\bigr)=K W(x).\)

This holds for every stable allocation. Taking the infimum therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \inf_{x\text{ stable}}W(x)\geq\frac{W_{\max}}{K}>0.\)

The strict positivity follows from \(E>0\) and \(\gamma>0\). Thus the zero-denominator convention never arises under the stated hypotheses, and division proves

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{PoA}\leq K=1+2(m-1)\gamma_m^*.\)

No attainment of the infimum over stable allocations is needed.

Although the quadratic coefficient can be negative, the positive linear coefficient guarantees an increase for sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\). Hence every maximizer is stable, and the set of stable allocations is nonempty.

This reasoning uses precisely the task’s infinitesimal exchange definition. It also accommodates diagonal affinities without modifying the utility formula.

3. Pairwise house inequality
