The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational finite-support mass tables for seller and buyer valuation types across independently matched bilateral-trade venues, compute an optimal Bayesian incentive-compatible, interim individually rational, strongly budget-balanced mechanism for each venue type pair.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq1\) explicitly listed public venue type pairs. Each venue independently matches one seller owning one indivisible good with one buyer; their private valuations are independent draws from the specified seller and buyer distributions.
A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Support size counts listed types, not possible types; let \(\tau\) be the total support size across all input societies and \(L\) the total input bit length. Each distribution has total mass \(1\), and valuations are binary rationals in \([0,1]\).
At each venue, a direct randomized mechanism specifies trade probabilities \(x(s,b)\in[0,1]\) and expected transfers \(p_B(s,b),p_S(s,b)\in\mathbb R\). Buyer and seller utilities relative to no trade are \(bx-p_B\) and \(p_S-sx\), respectively.
Require Bayesian incentive compatibility and interim individual rationality, with expectations over the independently matched partner. True types and reports range over \([0,1]\); mechanisms must satisfy these requirements throughout that domain.
Strong budget balance means \(p_B(s,b)=p_S(s,b)\) at every report profile. Transfers need not vanish when trade does not occur; there are no transfers or feasibility constraints connecting venues.
The computational problem is to compute an optimal mechanism for every listed venue, maximizing \(\mathbb E[(b-s)x(s,b)]\); return its allocation and transfer tables on the positive-mass supports, admitting an extension satisfying the stated requirements.

Is this exact mechanism-computation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Known results: finite-support bilateral trade admits an exact linear-program characterization of optimal gains from trade under these incentive, participation, and budget constraints. The characterization also covers arbitrary Borel probability distributions, including finite-support mass societies; these are established results in [Liu, Qin, Ren and Wang](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03849v2).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted venue type pairs as an explicitly listed collection of public distribution pairs, with independent bilateral matching and no constraints or transfers connecting venues.
  • Normalized each seller and buyer mass table separately to total mass one and restricted rational valuations to [0,1], following the valuation domain.
  • Used the full [0,1] true-type and report domains from the mechanism definition; finite output tables must admit a feasible extension.
  • Interpreted optimality as maximizing expected gains from trade, allowing randomized trade and unrestricted signed expected transfers, including transfers without trade.
  • Defined m as the number of listed venue pairs, tau as total positive-mass support size, and L as total input bit length.
  • Allowed unequal and singleton supports because the general distribution characterization covers them; the anchor's equal support sizes are indexing conventions.

Result

The exact mechanism-computation problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

I prove this by giving a polynomial-size rational linear program for each venue and an explicit construction of strongly balanced transfers. The construction also extends the mechanism to every true type and report in \([0,1]\).

The characterization below agrees with Proposition 3.1 and Corollary 3.2 of [Liu, Qin, Ren and Wang](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03849v2). To verify the extension and computational requirements directly, I give a self-contained finite-support argument.

1. A linear program for one venue

Fix a venue. Sort its distinct seller and buyer support values as

\(0\leq s_1<\cdots<s_n\leq1,\qquad 0\leq b_1<\cdots<b_k\leq1.\)

Write their positive probability masses as \(a_i\) and \(c_j\), respectively. Thus \(\sum_i a_i=\sum_j c_j=1\). Repeated listings of the same valuation can first be merged by adding their masses.

Introduce allocation variables \(x_{ij}\in[0,1]\). Define the interim trade probabilities

\(q_j=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i x_{ij},\qquad r_i=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_j x_{ij}.\)

Define the following linear functions of the allocation:

\(\displaystyle G(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_i c_j(b_j-s_i)x_{ij},\)

\(\displaystyle \alpha_j(x)=\sum_{h=1}^{j-1}(b_{h+1}-b_h)q_h,\qquad \beta_i(x)=\sum_{h=i+1}^{n}(s_h-s_{h-1})r_h,\)

\(\displaystyle \Delta(x)=G(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_j\alpha_j(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\beta_i(x).\)

Empty sums are zero. Consider the linear program

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad &G(x)\\ \text{subject to}\quad &0\leq x_{ij}\leq1 &&\text{for every }i,j,\\ &q_j\leq q_{j+1} &&\text{for }1\leq j<k,\\ &r_i\geq r_{i+1} &&\text{for }1\leq i<n,\\ &\Delta(x)\geq0. \end{aligned}\)

I will prove that its feasible allocations are exactly the support allocations admitting the required extension.

2. Necessity of the constraints

Take any mechanism satisfying the stated requirements, and restrict it to the positive-mass supports. Let \(U_j\) and \(V_i\) be its truthful interim buyer and seller utilities.

Adding the two buyer incentive inequalities between adjacent support types gives

\((b_{j+1}-b_j)(q_{j+1}-q_j)\geq0.\)

Since the support values are strictly increasing, \(q_{j+1}\geq q_j\). The corresponding seller inequalities give

\((s_{i+1}-s_i)(r_i-r_{i+1})\geq0,\)

so \(r_i\geq r_{i+1}\).

A buyer of type \(b_{j+1}\) can report \(b_j\). Therefore,

\(U_{j+1}\geq U_j+(b_{j+1}-b_j)q_j.\)

Starting with \(U_1\geq0\), which follows from interim individual rationality, repeated application yields

\(U_j\geq\alpha_j(x).\)

Likewise, a seller of type \(s_i\) can report \(s_{i+1}\), giving

\(V_i\geq V_{i+1}+(s_{i+1}-s_i)r_{i+1}.\)

Starting with \(V_n\geq0\) yields

\(V_i\geq\beta_i(x).\)

Strong budget balance cancels the transfers when buyer and seller utilities are added and averaged. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sum_j c_jU_j+\sum_i a_iV_i=G(x).\)

Combining this identity with the utility lower bounds proves

\(G(x)\geq\sum_j c_j\alpha_j(x)+\sum_i a_i\beta_i(x),\)

which is precisely \(\Delta(x)\geq0\). Thus every admissible mechanism restricts to a feasible LP allocation.

3. Extension of every feasible allocation

Now fix any feasible LP allocation. Abbreviate \(\Delta=\Delta(x)\geq0\).

For any seller report \(s\), let \(i(s)\) be the smallest index with \(s_i\geq s\), when such an index exists. For any buyer report \(b\), let \(j(b)\) be the largest index with \(b_j\leq b\), when such an index exists. Set

\(\displaystyle \widetilde x(s,b)= \begin{cases} x_{i(s),j(b)},&\text{if both indices exist},\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases}\)

This preserves every support allocation.

Its interim buyer allocation, denoted \(Q(b)\), equals zero below \(b_1\), equals \(q_j\) on \([b_j,b_{j+1})\), and equals \(q_k\) on \([b_k,1]\). Hence \(Q\) is nondecreasing.

Its interim seller allocation, denoted \(R(s)\), equals \(r_1\) on \([0,s_1]\), equals \(r_i\) on \((s_{i-1},s_i]\) for \(i>1\), and equals zero above \(s_n\). Hence \(R\) is nonincreasing.

Define desired truthful interim utilities throughout the domain by

\(\displaystyle U(b)=\Delta+\int_0^b Q(t)\,dt,\qquad V(s)=\int_s^1 R(t)\,dt.\)

Both are nonnegative. The step-function definitions give, at support values,

\(U(b_j)=\Delta+\alpha_j(x),\qquad V(s_i)=\beta_i(x).\)

Define desired interim payments by

\(B(b)=bQ(b)-U(b),\qquad S(s)=sR(s)+V(s).\)

Their expectations agree. Indeed,

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} \sum_j c_jB(b_j)-\sum_i a_iS(s_i) &=G(x)-\Delta-\sum_jc_j\alpha_j(x)-\sum_i a_i\beta_i(x)\\ &=0. \end{aligned}\)

Let \(T\) denote their common expectation, and define the transfer at every report profile by

\(\widetilde p_B(s,b)=\widetilde p_S(s,b)=B(b)+S(s)-T.\)

This is strongly budget balanced at every profile. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_{s\sim F_S}[\widetilde p_B(s,b)]=B(b),\qquad \mathbb E_{b\sim F_B}[\widetilde p_S(s,b)]=S(s). \)

Thus the constructed mechanism induces exactly the desired interim allocations and payments for every report.

To verify buyer incentive compatibility, a buyer with true value \(b\) reporting \(z\) receives utility

\(bQ(z)-B(z)=U(z)+(b-z)Q(z).\)

Truthful utility minus this deviation utility is

\(\displaystyle U(b)-U(z)-(b-z)Q(z)=\int_z^b[Q(t)-Q(z)]\,dt.\)

If \(b\geq z\), the integrand is nonnegative. If \(b<z\), reversing the integration limits and using \(Q(t)\leq Q(z)\) again gives a nonnegative result.

For a seller with true value \(s\) reporting \(z\), deviation utility is

\(S(z)-sR(z)=V(z)+(z-s)R(z).\)

Truthful utility minus deviation utility is

\(\displaystyle V(s)-V(z)+(s-z)R(z)=\int_z^s[R(z)-R(t)]\,dt.\)

Because \(R\) is nonincreasing, this expression is nonnegative for both \(s\geq z\) and \(s<z\).

Therefore Bayesian incentive compatibility holds for every true type and report in \([0,1]\). Interim individual rationality follows from \(U,V\geq0\).

The functions constructed above are Borel measurable: the allocation is a finite step function, and the payments use integrals of finite step functions. A randomized trade with probability \(\widetilde x(s,b)\), together with the deterministic transfer \(\widetilde p_B(s,b)\), realizes the specified allocation and expected transfers. This uses the expressly permitted ability to transfer money when trade does not occur.

In particular, the output transfer table can be computed directly as

\(\displaystyle p_{B,ij}=p_{S,ij} =b_jq_j-\Delta-\alpha_j(x)+s_ir_i+\beta_i(x)-T, \)

where

\(\displaystyle T=\sum_i a_i\bigl(s_ir_i+\beta_i(x)\bigr).\)

This proves sufficiency and completes the characterization.

4. Exact polynomial-time computation

For one venue, the LP has \(nk\) variables and

\(2nk+(k-1)+(n-1)+1\)

inequalities. Every coefficient is rational and computable from the input using polynomially many rational additions and multiplications.

These computations produce coefficients of polynomial bit length: denominators of sums can be bounded by products of the input denominators, whose bit lengths add, and each coefficient uses only polynomially many such operations.

The feasible set is nonempty because \(x=0\) is feasible. It is closed and contained in \([0,1]^{nk}\), so it is compact and the objective attains its maximum.

Apply the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a rational LP can be solved in time polynomial in its total binary encoding length, returning an exact rational optimum when its feasible set is nonempty and its objective is bounded. Both hypotheses have just been verified.

There is also no obstruction from output length. A bounded rational polytope has an optimal vertex. After clearing denominators in its defining constraints, Cramer’s rule and determinant bounds show that every coordinate of a vertex has polynomial bit length in the LP encoding length. The transfer table then requires only polynomially many rational operations on those coordinates, so it also has polynomial bit length.

For each listed venue, \(n,k\leq\tau\). Thus, even when societies are reused across venues,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\ell=1}^{m}n_\ell k_\ell\leq m\tau^2.\)

The total LP encoding length, solution time, and table output length are therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Finally, the venues have no shared transfers or feasibility constraints. Each venue can consequently be optimized independently. By necessity, every admissible mechanism has objective at most its venue’s LP optimum; by sufficiency, the computed optimal allocation and transfer tables attain that optimum and admit the required full-domain extension.
