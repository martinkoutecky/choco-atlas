The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\), a population scale \(N\), a finite legal type set \(\mathcal X\subseteq\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m\), an approximation target \(\rho>0\), and an audit budget \(B\), consider societies \(\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal X)\) with \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). Does there exist a mechanism truthful for each individual and each legal report profile that achieves welfare at least \(\rho\) times optimum on every legal society while using at most \(B\) expected individual audits on truthful societies? For the Theorem 6.1 witness, take \(\mathcal X=\{\mathbf e_j,\delta\mathbf e_j:j\in[m]\}\).

A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types and their binary rational masses. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; masses sum to \(1\).
Such a society represents \(N\) labeled individuals, with \(N\mu_t\) individuals of type \(t\). Each individual may report any type in \(\mathcal X\).
A randomized mechanism receives the reports and may adaptively audit individuals. An audit reveals only whether that individual's report equals their true type; the mechanism has no other access to true types. Count distinct audited individuals.
The mechanism uses no payments and outputs an outcome in \([m]\), possibly a null outcome valued at zero by every type. An individual's utility is their true valuation of the output, including when caught lying. Mechanisms are defined also after individuals are removed.

A new hardness result

I prove that the mechanism-existence problem is $\mathsf{coNP}$-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions, already when
$$ \rho=1,\qquad B=0,\qquad N=\frac{|\mathcal X|}{2}. $$
Consequently, an algorithm polynomial in $m,|\mathcal X|,\log N,L$ would imply $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$. This hardness also holds when $N$ is encoded in unary.

This substantially advances the classification, but it does not establish $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness under the usual many-one reduction convention. Under polynomial-time Turing reductions, it does establish $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness. I keep that distinction explicit.

1. Two preliminary lemmas

Write a labeled report profile as $r=(r_1,\ldots,r_N)\in\mathcal X^N$, and define its reported welfare by
$$ W_o(r)=\sum_{i=1}^N r_{i,o}. $$
Every such profile is a permissible truthful labeled realization: its society assigns mass equal to the multiplicity of each type divided by $N$.

Lemma 1: A zero audit budget eliminates verification on all report profiles

Suppose a mechanism has expected audits zero on every truthful labeled profile. Then, for every fixed report profile, its outcome distribution is independent of the true types.

Proof. Fix reports $r$. On the truthful realization with true profile $r$, the number of audited individuals is a nonnegative integer with expectation zero. Therefore, the probability of performing any audit is zero.

Couple this execution with an execution having the same reports and arbitrary legal true types, using identical mechanism randomness. Before the first audit, both executions have identical observations: the reports and internal randomness. Thus the event of initiating an audit is identical in both executions. It has probability zero. Outside this zero-probability event, the executions produce identical outputs. $\square$

Lemma 2: A sufficient condition for truthful welfare maximization

Fix a total priority order on outcomes. Consider the deterministic rule that maximizes reported welfare and breaks ties using this order.

Let $t$ be an individual's true type and $a$ a possible alternative report. Set
$$ d_o=a_o-t_o. $$
If
$$ t_v>t_u\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_v\le d_u $$
for every pair of outcomes $u,v$, then reporting $a$ cannot improve this individual's utility, for any fixed reports of the other individuals.

Proof. Let $Q_o$ be the welfare score when the individual reports $t$. Reporting $a$ changes this score to $Q_o+d_o$.

Suppose truthful reporting selects $u$, while reporting $a$ selects $v$ with $t_v>t_u$. Optimality and the hypothesis give
$$ Q_u\ge Q_v,\qquad d_u\ge d_v. $$
Consequently,
$$ Q_u+d_u\ge Q_v+d_v. $$
Since the alternative report selects $v$, equality must hold. The two inequalities above must therefore also be equalities. Thus $u$ and $v$ were tied under truthful reporting, and $u$ precedes $v$ in the fixed priority order. They remain tied after the alternative report, so the rule cannot select $v$ while $u$ is available with the same maximal score. This is a contradiction. $\square$

These lemmas impose neither anonymity nor restrictions on the other individuals' reports.

2. The reduction

The source problem is unsatisfiability of Boolean formulas in conjunctive normal form with clauses of at most three literals. Satisfiability for these formulas is $\mathsf{NP}$-complete; hence unsatisfiability is $\mathsf{coNP}$-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. This is the standard satisfiability result recorded in [Karp's paper](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Let $\varphi$ have variables $x_1,\ldots,x_K$ and clauses $F_1,\ldots,F_R$. We may assume $K\ge2$, by adding unused variables if necessary. This preserves satisfiability.

Set
$$ N=K+1,\qquad c=K-1,\qquad \beta=\frac{K-1}{K},\qquad \eta=\frac14. $$

There are the following outcomes:

  • a distinguished outcome $\star$;
  • a baseline outcome $z$;
  • variable outcomes $A_1,\ldots,A_K$;
  • clause outcomes $C_1,\ldots,C_R$;
  • private outcomes $D_1,\ldots,D_K$.

Thus
$$ m=2K+R+2. $$
Call the outcomes $A_v,C_r,D_v$ the guard outcomes.

Selector types

For every $i\in[K]$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$, introduce a type $t^{i,b}$, representing the assignment choice $x_i=b$. Its valuations are
$$ t^{i,b}_\star=0,\qquad t^{i,b}_z=\beta, $$
and
$$ t^{i,b}_{A_v}= \begin{cases} 0,&v=i,\\ 1,&v\ne i. \end{cases} $$
For a clause outcome, set
$$ t^{i,b}_{C_r}= \begin{cases} 0,&F_r\text{ contains a literal involving }x_i \text{ made true by }b,\\ 1,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
Finally,
$$ t^{i,b}_{D_v}= \begin{cases} 1,&v=i\text{ and }b=1,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$

Each selector type therefore has utility levels $0,\beta,1$, with $0<\beta<1$.

Two distinguished types

Introduce
$$ s^-=(c-\eta)\mathbf e_\star,\qquad s^+=(c+\eta)\mathbf e_\star. $$
Both value only $\star$, and both have strictly positive valuation there because $K\ge2$.

The legal domain is
$$ \mathcal X= \{t^{i,b}:i\in[K],\ b\in\{0,1\}\} \cup\{s^-,s^+\}. $$
The variable coordinates distinguish selectors belonging to different variables, and the private coordinates distinguish the two selectors belonging to the same variable. The distinguished types are also different from every selector and from each other. Hence
$$ |\mathcal X|=2K+2=2N. $$

Set the decision parameters to
$$ \rho=1,\qquad B=0. $$
All valuations and parameters are nonnegative binary rationals, as required.

3. What the guard outcomes encode

Lemma 3: The guard inequalities encode satisfiability

There exists a multiset of exactly $K$ selector types whose welfare at every guard outcome is at most $c=K-1$ if and only if $\varphi$ is satisfiable.

Proof. Let $a_{i,b}$ be the multiplicity of selector $t^{i,b}$, so
$$ a_{i,b}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^K\sum_{b=0}^1 a_{i,b}=K. $$
Write $a_i=a_{i,0}+a_{i,1}$.

At variable outcome $A_i$, welfare is
$$ W_{A_i}=K-a_i. $$
The inequality $W_{A_i}\le K-1$ implies $a_i\ge1$. Since there are $K$ variables and the total multiplicity is $K$, all these inequalities together force
$$ a_i=1\qquad\text{for every }i. $$
Thus exactly one assignment choice is selected for each variable.

Let $S_r$ be the set of pairs $(i,b)$ whose assignment choice makes some literal of clause $F_r$ true. Then
$$ W_{C_r}=K-\sum_{(i,b)\in S_r}a_{i,b}. $$
The clause guard inequality $W_{C_r}\le K-1$ is equivalent to
$$ \sum_{(i,b)\in S_r}a_{i,b}\ge1. $$
Under the one-choice-per-variable condition, this says precisely that the resulting assignment satisfies $F_r$.

The private guard inequalities introduce no additional restriction under that condition, because
$$ W_{D_i}=a_{i,1}\le1\le K-1. $$
This proves that any multiset satisfying all guard inequalities gives a satisfying assignment.

Conversely, given a satisfying assignment, select its one corresponding selector for each variable. Every variable guard has welfare $K-1$, every clause guard has welfare at most $K-1$, and every private guard has welfare at most $1\le K-1$. $\square$

For any multiset of $K$ selectors, each guard welfare is an integer between $0$ and $K$. Consequently, Lemma 3 also proves:

If $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable, every multiset of $K$ selectors has some guard outcome with welfare exactly $K$.

The baseline welfare for any such multiset is
$$ W_z=K\beta=K-1=c. $$

4. A satisfying formula forces nonexistence

Assume $\varphi$ is satisfiable. Choose its $K$ assignment selectors, one per variable.

Consider a truthful labeled profile consisting of those selectors and one individual of type $s^-$. All guard welfares are at most $c$, baseline welfare is $c$, and
$$ W_\star=c-\eta<c. $$
Thus $\star$ is strictly suboptimal.

With $\rho=1$, expected welfare must equal maximal welfare. Indeed, every available outcome has welfare at most that maximum, so any positive probability on a strictly suboptimal outcome would make expected welfare strictly smaller. Therefore, the mechanism must output $\star$ with probability zero on this profile.

Now replace the distinguished individual's true type and report by $s^+$. All other reports and labels remain unchanged. Then
$$ W_\star=c+\eta>c, $$
while every other real outcome has welfare at most $c$. Hence $\star$ is the unique welfare-maximizing outcome and must be output with probability one.

Return to the first true profile, but let its $s^-$ individual falsely report $s^+$. By Lemma 1, the outcome distribution is identical to that of the truthful $s^+$ profile. The individual therefore obtains utility $c-\eta>0$, whereas truthful reporting gave utility zero.

This violates dominant-strategy truthfulness. Consequently,
$$ \varphi\text{ satisfiable} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed mechanism-existence instance is infeasible}. $$

Both truthful profiles used here have support size $\tau=N=K+1$, with every mass equal to $1/N$. They are among the societies covered by the required guarantees.

5. An unsatisfiable formula admits a truthful mechanism

Assume now that $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable.

Use the deterministic mechanism that performs no audits and selects an outcome maximizing reported welfare, with a fixed priority order for ties. Its welfare is exact on every truthful profile. It remains to prove truthfulness on every report profile.

5.1 When does the mechanism select $\star$?

For an arbitrary report profile of size $N$, let $k$ be the number of reports in $\{s^-,s^+\}$. The other $n=N-k$ reports are selectors.

If $k=0$, then $W_\star=0$, while
$$ W_z=\beta N>0. $$
Thus $\star$ is not selected.

If $k=1$, there are exactly $K$ selectors. Unsatisfiability and Lemma 3 imply that some guard has welfare $K$. Meanwhile,
$$ W_\star\le c+\eta=K-\frac34<K. $$
Thus $\star$ is again not selected.

If $k\ge2$, every outcome other than $\star$ has welfare at most $n$, since each selector contributes at most one and each distinguished type contributes zero. But
$$ W_\star \ge 2(c-\eta) =2K-\frac52 >K-1 \ge n, $$
where the strict inequality uses $K\ge2$. Thus $\star$ is the unique maximizing outcome.

We have proved, for every legal report profile,
$$ \text{the mechanism selects }\star \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k\ge2. $$
In particular, replacing an $s^-$ report by $s^+$, or conversely, never changes whether $\star$ is selected.

5.2 Truthfulness of selector types

Fix a true selector type $t$.

First consider an alternative selector report $a$. At outcomes with true value one, the changes $d_o=a_o-t_o$ belong to $\{-1,0\}$. At the baseline outcome, the change is zero. At outcomes with true value zero, the changes belong to $\{0,1\}$.

Therefore,
$$ t_v>t_u\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_v\le d_u. $$
Lemma 2 proves that this deviation cannot improve utility.

Next consider a distinguished report $a\in\{s^-,s^+\}$. At guard outcomes with true value one, the change is $-1$. At the baseline, the change is $-\beta$. At outcomes with true value zero, the change is nonnegative: it is positive at $\star$ and zero at the other such outcomes. Since
$$ -1<-\beta<0, $$
the same condition from Lemma 2 holds. This deviation also cannot improve utility.

Thus every selector type is truthful against every legal alternative report.

5.3 Truthfulness of the distinguished types

For a true distinguished type $t=s^-$ or $t=s^+$, a selector report decreases its reported value at $\star$ and weakly increases every other reported coordinate. Because $\star$ is the only outcome with positive true utility, this satisfies Lemma 2's condition. Such a deviation cannot help.

A true $s^+$ type reporting $s^-$ likewise decreases the $\star$ coordinate and leaves all others unchanged, so Lemma 2 applies.

Finally, a true $s^-$ type reporting $s^+$ leaves the number $k$ of distinguished reports unchanged. Section 5.1 shows that both reports give exactly the same probability of $\star$: either zero or one. Since this individual values only $\star$, the deviation leaves utility unchanged.

These cases exhaust all types and reports. The mechanism observes no true types, so the argument holds for arbitrary reports of the other individuals, including lies.

5.4 Definition after removals

For every smaller population size, define a separate mechanism that audits everyone, outputs a welfare-maximizing outcome if all reports pass, and otherwise outputs null.

This mechanism is truthful. If another individual lies, both truthful reporting and any deviation by the individual under consideration yield null. If all others report truthfully, a false report yields null, while truthful reporting gives nonnegative utility.

The task imposes no consistency requirement linking these mechanisms across population sizes. The budget and welfare guarantees for the specified population size $N$ are met by the zero-audit mechanism above.

We conclude that
$$ \varphi\text{ unsatisfiable} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed instance is feasible}. $$

6. Complexity consequences and remaining scope

Combining the two directions gives the exact equivalence
$$ \varphi\text{ unsatisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(\varphi)\text{ admits a mechanism satisfying the task}. $$

The construction writes
$$ m=2K+R+2,\qquad |\mathcal X|=2K+2,\qquad N=K+1. $$
Every valuation has binary length $O(\log K)$. Constructing the explicit domain takes polynomial time in the formula's input length, and the resulting total binary length $L$ is polynomial in that length. Encoding $N$ in unary would still give a polynomial-size reduction.

Thus the mechanism-existence problem is $\mathsf{coNP}$-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. A polynomial-time algorithm in $m,|\mathcal X|,\log N,L$ would decide satisfiability by constructing $f(\varphi)$ and negating its answer, implying $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$.

There is no single support parameter $\tau$ in the decision input. The existence proof covers every feasible support size, and the nonexistence witness uses $\tau=N$. When the constructed instance is feasible, evaluating its welfare-maximizing rule on a compressed society takes $O(m\tau)$ rational arithmetic operations, with polynomial bit complexity in the domain and society encoding lengths and $\log N$.

The reduction also makes the problem $\mathsf{NP}$-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions: one oracle query, followed by negating the response, decides satisfiability. However, negating an oracle answer is not a many-one reduction to the existence problem. The requested $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness under the usual many-one convention remains unproved, and no membership in $\mathsf{coNP}$ is asserted.
