The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(k\) items, rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), and a finite directed block network whose blocks have rational masses \(\mu_t\), values \(v_t\), desired bundles \(S_t\), and referral roles, does a polynomial-time mass extension of Net-\(\sqrt{k}\)-APM achieve welfare at least \(1/\sqrt{k}\) of the feasible mass-allocation optimum while remaining individually rational and dominant-strategy truthful in each bidder’s value and invitation choices? Each individual can invite an adjacent block, activating its positive mass; each served individual receives a whole desired bundle, and the mechanism may serve a fraction of each block. No budget-balance guarantee is required.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=k\geq1\) be the number of item kinds, with binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\geq0\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each with binary-rational mass \(\mu_t>0\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible bundles or referral roles.
Each type occupies an atomless block and has a public nonempty desired bundle \(S_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), a private binary-rational value \(v_t\geq0\), and an outgoing-neighbor set in a finite directed block network with seller \(s\). An individual values exactly \(S_t\) at \(v_t\), and every other bundle at zero; bundles and block memberships cannot be misreported.
The seller activates its outgoing-neighbor blocks. Each active individual can invite any subset of their block's outgoing neighbors; one invitation activates the entire target block, recursively. Activation is the least closure of these invitations, including invitations from single individuals of zero mass. Inactive individuals receive nothing and pay zero, and their reports cannot affect the outcome.
Give individuals fixed public labels within their blocks. A deterministic mechanism selects measurable subsets of individuals, giving each selected individual their whole desired bundle, and specifies signed per-individual payments. If \(x_t\) is the served mass of type \(t\), feasibility means \(0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t\), \(x_t=0\) for inactive types, and \(\sum_{t:j\in S_t}x_t\leq q_j\) for every item kind \(j\).
Define \(\mathrm{OPT}\) as the maximum of \(\sum_t v_t x_t\) over these feasible mass allocations for the currently active population. Individual utility is \(v_i a_i-p_i\), where \(a_i\in\{0,1\}\) indicates service. Individual rationality requires nonnegative utility at a truthful value for every invitation subset; dominant-strategy truthfulness requires truthful value and inviting every outgoing neighbor to weakly dominate every joint value-and-invitation deviation, fixing everyone else's reports.
For computation, allow finite report partitions into \(r\) public-label intervals with rational endpoints and finitely many explicitly recorded singleton exceptions \(e\); singleton reports and invitation events remain observable despite having zero mass. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. Require a finite allocation/payment representation whose construction and pointwise evaluation take polynomial time in \(m,\tau,|E|,L,r,e\); any tie-breaking must use a fixed public order.
Target. Does a polynomial-time mass analogue achieve welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/\sqrt{k}\) on every truthful-value profile, for every invitation profile, while satisfying individual rationality and dominant-strategy truthfulness in individual values and invitations, with no budget-balance requirement?
Nearest known results. For finite bidders and one copy of each item, Net-\(\sqrt{k}\)-APM achieves this welfare ratio, individual rationality, and invitation-and-value strategyproofness, while potentially running a deficit. [Discrete approximation result](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14472v1).
No result settling this exact atomless, individual-to-block activation formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total potential population mass to one, so supplies are measured per capita of the potential society rather than renormalized after activation.
  • Required at least one item kind and nonempty desired bundles, since the discrete ranking divides by the square root of bundle size; retained nonnegative values and supplies.
  • Made desired bundles and block membership public and fixed, with only values and invitation subsets strategically reportable.
  • Specified activation as the least closure starting from the seller's neighbors; a single individual's invitation activates an entire adjacent block even when that individual has zero mass.
  • Preserved deterministic whole-bundle service by using public individual labels and measurable served subsets, rather than interpreting fractional service as individual lotteries.
  • Specified pointwise individual incentives, retaining singleton deviations explicitly instead of treating a block as one strategic agent.
  • Added finite rational report partitions and singleton exceptions to make computation on deviating profiles meaningful; included their sizes in the running-time parameters.
  • Compared welfare against the optimum for the currently active population and required the ratio under any invitation profile with truthful values.
  • Interpreted mass analogue as a mechanism retaining the stated approximation and incentive guarantees, since no particular interpolation of the discrete algorithm was specified.
  • Allowed signed payments, set inactive allocations and payments to zero, and required fixed public tie-breaking.

Resolution

Yes. In fact, the stated mass model admits a deterministic polynomial-time mechanism achieving welfare exactly \(\mathrm{OPT}\).

The construction uses optimal mass allocations, individual posted prices, and a rebate for inviting every outgoing neighbor. The proof treats each individual as the strategic agent; it does not assume that a whole positive-mass block reports jointly.

1. Public labels and activation

Identify block \(t\) with the public-label interval \([0,1)\), carrying measure \(\mu_t\) times Lebesgue measure. Fix public orders on blocks and item kinds, and use increasing label order within each block.

Write \(\widehat v_i\) for individual \(i\)'s reported value and \(R_i\subseteq N_t\) for her reported invitation set, where \(N_t\) is her block’s public outgoing-neighbor set.

Construct the reported block graph by including an edge from \(t\) to \(u\) whenever some individual in block \(t\) invites \(u\). In particular, singleton invitation events contribute edges. Include the seller’s outgoing edges. The active blocks are precisely those reachable from the seller in this graph: reachability implements the required least activation closure.

We will use the following observation.

Activation observation. Fix everyone else’s reports. An individual’s activity status is independent of her own value and invitation reports.

Indeed, her invitations can change only edges leaving her block \(t\). Reachability of \(t\) does not depend on edges leaving \(t\): any path reaching \(t\) can be shortened to a simple path, which reaches \(t\) before using any outgoing edge of \(t\). If \(t\) is unreachable, changing its outgoing edges cannot change any reachable block.

Consequently, an inactive individual’s reports have no effect on the active population. All subsequent calculations use only active blocks’ reports.

2. An optimal mass allocation and canonical prices

For mass calculations, replace each active block’s bid function by its canonical interval step function equal to it almost everywhere. Merge adjacent intervals with equal bids and use a fixed half-open endpoint convention. Singleton bid exceptions do not enter this step function; their actual bids remain available for pointwise allocation.

This canonical function is unchanged by changing one individual’s value report.

Index its positive-length intervals across active blocks by \(h\). Let:

  • \(t(h)\) be interval \(h\)’s block;
  • \(b_h\) be its bid;
  • \(\nu_h>0\) be its mass;
  • \(S_h=S_{t(h)}\) be its desired bundle.

Solve the rational linear program

\( \displaystyle W=\max_x\left\{\sum_h b_hx_h: 0\leq x_h\leq\nu_h,\quad \sum_{h:j\in S_h}x_h\leq q_j\ \text{for every }j \right\}. \)

Its feasible set is nonempty because \(x=0\) is feasible, and compact because every coordinate has a finite upper bound. Hence an optimum exists. Select an optimum canonically by successively maximizing its coordinates in public block-and-label order, while preserving the optimal objective and previously selected coordinates.

The dual is

\( \displaystyle \min_{y,z}\left\{ \sum_jq_jy_j+\sum_h\nu_hz_h: y_j\geq0,\ z_h\geq0,\quad \sum_{j\in S_h}y_j+z_h\geq b_h\ \text{for every }h \right\}. \)

It is feasible, for example with \(y=0\) and \(z_h=b_h\). Strong linear-programming duality therefore supplies a dual optimum of value \(W\).

Define \(B=\max_h b_h\), taking \(B=0\) when there are no active intervals. There is a dual optimum satisfying

\( 0\leq y_j\leq B,\qquad 0\leq z_h\leq B. \)

To verify this, start with any dual optimum and cap every coordinate at \(B\). Every constraint has right-hand side at most \(B\). If a coordinate appearing in a constraint was capped, its new contribution alone is \(B\), so that constraint remains satisfied. Otherwise its left-hand side is unchanged. All objective coefficients are nonnegative, so capping cannot increase the objective. By weak duality, the capped solution remains optimal.

Among bounded dual optima, select \(y\) by successive lexicographic minimization in the fixed public item order. This uniquely determines the item-price vector. Define block \(t\)’s posted bundle price by

\( \displaystyle c_t=\sum_{j\in S_t}y_j. \)

Thus \(0\leq c_t\leq kB\).

Price independence. Fix an individual’s invitations and everyone else’s reports. Changing only that individual’s value report leaves the active blocks, canonical interval step functions, linear programs, and canonical prices unchanged.

This is exact independence, since one individual has zero mass.

3. Realizing the optimal allocation pointwise

We first establish the relation between bids and prices.

For primal and dual optima, their objective difference is

\( \displaystyle 0= \sum_j y_j\left(q_j-\sum_{h:j\in S_h}x_h\right) +\sum_hz_h(\nu_h-x_h) +\sum_hx_h(c_{t(h)}+z_h-b_h). \)

Every summand is nonnegative by primal and dual feasibility. Therefore:

  • If \(b_h<c_{t(h)}\), then \(c_{t(h)}+z_h-b_h>0\), implying \(x_h=0\).
  • If \(b_h>c_{t(h)}\), dual feasibility gives \(z_h\geq b_h-c_{t(h)}>0\), implying \(x_h=\nu_h\).
  • If \(b_h=c_{t(h)}\), a partial allocation is permitted.

For each canonical interval \(h=[\ell_h,u_h)\), select its initial label segment of mass \(x_h\), namely

\( \displaystyle D_h=\left[\ell_h,\ \ell_h+\frac{x_h}{\mu_{t(h)}}\right). \)

Let \(D\) be the union of these segments. Their endpoints are rational.

The actual service rule for every active individual \(i\) in block \(t\), including every singleton exception, is

\( \displaystyle a_i= \begin{cases} 1,&\widehat v_i>c_t,\\ 0,&\widehat v_i<c_t,\\ \mathbf 1_{\{i\in D\}},&\widehat v_i=c_t. \end{cases} \)

An inactive individual receives nothing.

On each canonical interval, this rule agrees almost everywhere with membership in \(D_h\), by the three conclusions above. Hence its served mass on that interval is exactly \(x_h\). Exceptional point allocations do not change any mass constraint.

The allocation is consequently measurable and feasible on every admissible report profile. Each served individual receives her whole desired bundle.

4. Payments

For an active individual \(i\) in block \(t\), define

\( \displaystyle p_i=a_ic_t-c_t\,\mathbf 1_{\{R_i=N_t\}}. \)

Thus inviting every outgoing neighbor earns a rebate equal to the posted bundle price:

  • A fully inviting served individual pays zero.
  • A fully inviting unserved individual receives \(c_t\).
  • An individual withholding an invitation pays \(c_t\) if served and zero otherwise.

Inactive individuals pay zero.

If \(N_t\) is empty, its only invitation subset is the full set, so the rebate applies.

5. Individual rationality

Fix arbitrary reports of everyone else and any invitation subset for individual \(i\). Suppose she reports her true value \(v_i\).

If active, her utility is

\( \displaystyle u_i=a_i(v_i-c_t)+c_t\,\mathbf 1_{\{R_i=N_t\}}. \)

Under the truthful service rule,

\( \displaystyle a_i(v_i-c_t)=\max\{v_i-c_t,0\}. \)

This identity holds also at equality, regardless of label tie-breaking. Both terms in the utility expression are nonnegative. Therefore individual rationality holds at a truthful value for every invitation subset.

An inactive individual has utility zero.

6. Dominant-strategy truthfulness for joint deviations

Fix all reports of everyone except individual \(i\), whose true value is \(v_i\).

By the activation observation, either she is inactive under every invitation action or active under every invitation action. In the inactive case, every action gives utility zero. Consider the active case.

Truthful value with full invitations

Let \(c_t^{F}\) be her bundle price when she invites every outgoing neighbor. At a truthful value, her utility is

\( \displaystyle u_i^{F} =c_t^{F}+\max\{v_i-c_t^{F},0\} =\max\{v_i,c_t^{F}\} \geq v_i. \)

A deviation that retains full invitations

Changing only her value report leaves \(c_t^{F}\) unchanged, by price independence. Any such deviation produces a service indicator \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and utility

\( \displaystyle a(v_i-c_t^{F})+c_t^{F} \leq \max\{v_i,c_t^{F}\} =u_i^{F}. \)

Thus no value deviation with full invitations is profitable.

A deviation that withholds an invitation

The deviation may change the active population, allocation, and price. Nevertheless, it receives no rebate. If its price is \(c_t^{R}\geq0\) and its service indicator is \(a\), its utility is

\( \displaystyle u_i^{R}=a(v_i-c_t^{R})\leq v_i. \)

Indeed, an unserved individual gets zero, while a served individual gets at most \(v_i\). This inequality holds for every deviating value report.

Combining the inequalities gives

\( \displaystyle u_i^{F}\geq v_i\geq u_i^{R}. \)

Therefore truthful value reporting together with full invitations weakly dominates every joint value-and-invitation deviation.

7. Exact welfare

Consider any truthful-value profile and any invitation profile. Within each active type \(t\), the bid is \(v_t\) everywhere. The mass linear program is therefore precisely the program defining \(\mathrm{OPT}\), possibly with redundant interval subdivisions.

The pointwise implementation has exactly the optimal served masses, since its finite exceptional set has zero mass. Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \sum_t v_tx_t=\mathrm{OPT}. \)

Because \(k\geq1\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}\geq0\), this establishes the requested bound

\( \displaystyle \sum_t v_tx_t\geq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{\sqrt{k}}. \)

8. Polynomial construction and evaluation

The finite report representation gives polynomially many canonical intervals and explicitly recorded singleton events.

Activation is graph reachability, including singleton invitation edges. Canonical bid intervals are constructed by scanning and merging the reported intervals. Their masses are rational with polynomial encoding length.

The primal and bounded dual have polynomially many variables and constraints and rational coefficients of polynomial encoding length. We use the standard theorem that rational linear programs admit exact polynomial-time optimization; see [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239).

The canonical choices require only polynomially many additional coordinate optimizations. These successively expose faces of the original rational polytopes. Each coordinate optimum is attained at an original vertex, whose encoding length is polynomial by the determinant bounds for rational linear systems. Thus the added equality constraints retain polynomial encoding length.

The prices and allocation cutoffs consequently have polynomial encoding length. Overlaying the input report intervals with the allocation cutoffs produces a polynomial-size allocation/payment representation; singleton exceptions are evaluated separately. Under the stipulated public-label representation, pointwise evaluation requires interval comparisons, bid-price comparison, checking whether the invitation set is full, and evaluating the displayed payment formula.

Hence construction and evaluation meet the requested polynomial bound in \(m,\tau,|E|,L,r,e\).

No budget-balance assertion is needed: the rebates are permitted signed payments. The proof retains singleton reports pointwise and singleton invitations in activation, while using their zero mass only in the supply and welfare calculations.
