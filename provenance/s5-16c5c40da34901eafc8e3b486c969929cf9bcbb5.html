The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational \(d>0\), a finite set \(X\subseteq[0,d]\), and a unit-mass society \(\mu\) over types \((x,t)\in X\times\{L,H\}\) with rational masses and public location marginal \(\nu_x=\mu_{x,L}+\mu_{x,H}\), does there exist a universally truthful mechanism that maps compatible reported type-mass profiles to a distribution over facility locations \(y\in[0,d]\) and guarantees expected post-exchange welfare at least half the maximum over all \(y\) and feasible mass assignments of the houses? Define post-exchange assignment by finite replication of the rational masses, running TTC, and aggregating its outcome; use \(u(y,z,L)=d-|z-y|\) and \(u(y,z,H)=|z-y|\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(d>0\) be rational and \(X\subseteq[0,d]\cap\mathbb Q\) nonempty and finite, with \(m=|X|\). A unit-mass society is given by its support: the \(\tau\) types \((x,t)\in X\times\{L,H\}\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_{x,t}\). Missing masses are zero; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. The public house marginal is \(\nu_x=\mu_{x,L}+\mu_{x,H}>0\). Running times are measured in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Utilities are \(u(y,z,L)=d-|z-y|\) and \(u(y,z,H)=|z-y|\). A feasible house assignment consists of real masses \(a_{x,t,z}\ge0\), for \(x,z\in X\), satisfying \(\sum_z a_{x,t,z}=\mu_{x,t}\) and \(\sum_{x,t}a_{x,t,z}=\nu_z\). Define \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)=\max_{y\in[0,d],\,a}\sum_{x,t,z}a_{x,t,z}u(y,z,t)\).
For post-exchange assignment, let \(N\) be the least common denominator of the masses and create \(N\mu_{x,t}\) agents of each type, each owning a house at \(x\) and carrying mass \(1/N\). Index agents and their houses by increasing location, then \(L\) before \(H\), then copy number. Run TTC using true utilities, breaking utility ties by increasing house index, and aggregate the resulting assignments to obtain \(a^{\mathrm{TTC}}(\mu,y)\). Set \(W(\mu,y)=\sum_{x,t,z}a^{\mathrm{TTC}}_{x,t,z}(\mu,y)u(y,z,t)\).
A mechanism receives only the public data and a rational reported profile \(\rho\ge0\) satisfying \(\rho_{x,L}+\rho_{x,H}=\nu_x\), and chooses a facility location in \([0,d]\), without payments or initial house reassignment. Reports affect only the facility; exchanges always use true types.
A deterministic rule is truthful if, in every finite equal-mass replication with fixed indexed endowments, no single replica improves its true post-TTC utility by changing its facility-stage report, regardless of the other reports. Keep that replication and its house indices fixed in each comparison. A universally truthful mechanism is a lottery, independent of reports, over such deterministic rules.

Result and mechanism

Yes. Choose the facility location independently of reports, placing it at \(0\) or \(d\), each with probability \(\tfrac12\).

This is the endpoint lottery in [Zhang (2016), Section 4, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.04782). The following argument proves its guarantee directly for the specified rational-mass model, including its fixed-index truthfulness requirement and fractional assignment benchmark.

1. Universal truthfulness

Define two deterministic rules on every admissible public input and reported profile:

\(M_0(\rho)=0,\qquad M_d(\rho)=d.\)

Fix any finite equal-mass replication, its indexed endowments, its true types, and arbitrary reports by the other replicas. Under either rule, changing one replica’s facility-stage report leaves the facility location unchanged.

The subsequent TTC procedure uses true types and the same fixed house indices. Thus every preference ordering, including tie-breaking, remains unchanged. Consequently, the entire TTC assignment and the deviating replica’s true utility remain unchanged.

Both deterministic rules are therefore truthful in every replication required by the question. Choosing between them with a fair coin independent of reports is universally truthful.

2. TTC weakly improves every replica’s utility

Fix a facility location \(y\) and the canonical replication with \(N\) agents.

In the agent-house representation of TTC, each remaining agent points to its most preferred remaining house, and each remaining house points to its original owner. Utility ties are resolved by the prescribed house indices.

At every round, an agent’s original house remains available whenever that agent remains. To verify this invariant, observe that a house lies in a removed directed cycle if and only if its original owner lies in that cycle: the house points to its owner, and the owner’s only incoming house edge is from that original house. Hence each round removes original houses together with their owners.

When an agent is removed, it receives its most preferred remaining house. Its original house is still among those houses. Because the tie-breaking ordering refines true utility, the received house gives utility at least that of its original house.

The procedure terminates: every nonempty finite directed graph with one outgoing edge per vertex contains a directed cycle, so each round removes at least one agent.

Define the welfare of the initial endowments by

\(B(\mu,y)=\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{t\in\{L,H\}}\mu_{x,t}u(y,x,t).\)

Multiplying each replica’s utility inequality by its mass \(\tfrac1N\) and summing proves

\(W(\mu,y)\ge B(\mu,y).\)

This conclusion applies to the exact canonical replication and tie-breaking specified in the question; no invariance under alternative replications is needed.

3. Expected welfare of the endpoint lottery

For every \(x\in[0,d]\), the endpoint utilities satisfy

\(u(0,x,L)+u(d,x,L)=(d-x)+x=d,\)

and

\(u(0,x,H)+u(d,x,H)=x+(d-x)=d.\)

Since the society has unit total mass, it follows that

\(B(\mu,0)+B(\mu,d)=\sum_{x,t}\mu_{x,t}d=d.\)

Therefore the proposed lottery satisfies

\(\mathbb E[W(\mu,Y)]=\tfrac12W(\mu,0)+\tfrac12W(\mu,d)\ge\tfrac12B(\mu,0)+\tfrac12B(\mu,d)=\tfrac d2.\)

4. Comparison with the fractional optimum

For every feasible facility location, house location, and type,

\(0\le u(y,z,t)\le d,\)

because \(y,z\in[0,d]\). Every feasible assignment has total mass

\(\sum_{x,t,z}a_{x,t,z}=\sum_{x,t}\mu_{x,t}=1.\)

Consequently, every feasible assignment and facility location satisfy

\(\sum_{x,t,z}a_{x,t,z}u(y,z,t)\le d,\)

so \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\le d\). Combining this with the preceding bound gives

\(\mathbb E[W(\mu,Y)]\ge\tfrac d2\ge\tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}(\mu).\)

This comparison covers arbitrary feasible real assignment masses directly; it requires no reduction of the fractional optimum to a house permutation.

5. Implementation

The mechanism only samples one fair bit and outputs one of the two public endpoints. It does not compute the least common denominator, expand the society into replicas, or run TTC itself. Its facility-choice computation therefore has polynomial bit complexity in the input length \(L\), even when the canonical replication is exponentially large.
