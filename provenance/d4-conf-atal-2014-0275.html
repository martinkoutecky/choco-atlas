The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of contributor types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), a differentiable aggregate value function \(V(x)\) on \(0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t\), cross-type curvature bounds \(\kappa_{tu}\), a maximum issue size \(q\), and tolerance \(\varepsilon\), determine whether \(T\) has a partition \(\mathcal C\) into issues of size at most \(q\) with \(F(\mathcal C)=\sum_{t<u:\,C(t)\ne C(u)}\mu_t\mu_u\kappa_{tu}\leq\varepsilon\). Return such a partition and the issue-based type allocations \(\widehat a_t=\mu_t\int_0^1\partial_tV(s\mu^{C(t)})\,ds\), with the stated aggregate allocation-error certificate.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) contributor types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The real-valued function \(V\), normalized by \(V(0)=0\), is represented by a rational arithmetic circuit with \(m\) gates, defined and twice continuously differentiable on a neighborhood of \(B=\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\). All function assumptions below are promises.
Assume \(V\geq0\) on \(B\), and, for nonnegative vectors \(x,y,z\) with pairwise disjoint supports and \(x+y+z\in B\), assume \(V(x+y)\leq V(x)+V(y)\) and \(V(x+y+z)\geq V(x+y)+V(x+z)+V(y+z)-V(x)-V(y)-V(z)\).
Input also includes symmetric binary-rational bounds \(\kappa_{tu}\geq0\), promised to satisfy \(|\partial_t\partial_uV(x)|\leq\kappa_{tu}\) throughout \(B\) for distinct types, an integer \(1\leq q\leq\tau\), and binary-rational \(\varepsilon\geq0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An issue partition \(\mathcal C\) consists of nonempty, disjoint subsets covering \(T\), each containing at most \(q\) types; a type's mass cannot be split. Write \(C(t)\) for its issue and \(\mu^C_u=\mu_u\) for \(u\in C\), and \(0\) otherwise. Define \(F(\mathcal C)=\sum_{t<u:\,C(t)\ne C(u)}\mu_t\mu_u\kappa_{tu}\), counting each unordered pair once.
The approximation problem asks whether such a partition has \(F(\mathcal C)\leq\varepsilon\), and, when one exists, asks for its issue allocations \(\widehat a_t=\mu_t\int_0^1\partial_tV(s\mu^{C(t)})\,ds\), with aggregate certificate \(\sum_t|a_t-\widehat a_t|\leq2F(\mathcal C)\leq2\varepsilon\), where \(a_t=\mu_t\int_0^1\partial_tV(s\mu)\,ds\). Allocations are exact definite-integral expressions, so numerical integration precision is not an additional input.

Is this tolerance-constrained issue approximation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,q,L\), or is deciding the existence of a qualifying partition NP-hard?

For finite strongly subadditive games, a two-group decomposition with pairwise cut weight \(\zeta\) gives per-agent Shapley error at most \(2\zeta\), with stated running time \(O(2^{\max(|C_1|,|C_2|)}+n^3)\); this does not establish feasibility under a prescribed issue-size bound. [Soufiani et al.](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2014/aamas/p1209.pdf)
The continuous diagonal allocation is the Aumann–Shapley attribution; its attribution theory does not settle this bounded-issue approximation problem, for which no exact matching complexity result was located. [Sun and Sundararajan](https://arxiv.org/html/1102.0989)

Result

Deciding whether a qualifying partition exists is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard already for \(q=3\). This holds with equal dyadic masses, a quadratic polynomial \(V\), and exact mixed-derivative bounds. Thus a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,q,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

On promised inputs, feasibility also has a polynomial-time verifier. Moreover, every partition satisfies the stronger allocation certificate

\(\displaystyle \sum_t|a_t-\widehat a_t|\leq F(\mathcal C).\)

The cases \(q=1\) and \(q=\tau\) are immediate: the former forces singleton issues; the latter permits one issue with zero cut weight. The hardness concerns the prescribed intermediate size bound.

1. The allocation certificate

Fix any issue partition \(\mathcal C\). For a type \(t\), put \(C=C(t)\). For \(s,r\in[0,1]\), define

\(\displaystyle z_s(r)=s\mu^C+rs(\mu-\mu^C).\)

Every coordinate of this vector lies between zero and its corresponding mass, so \(z_s(r)\in B\). Its endpoints are \(s\mu^C\) and \(s\mu\).

Because \(V\) is twice continuously differentiable on a neighborhood of \(B\), the chain rule and the fundamental theorem of calculus give

\(\displaystyle \partial_tV(s\mu)-\partial_tV(s\mu^C) =\int_0^1s\sum_{u\notin C}\mu_u\,\partial_u\partial_tV(z_s(r))\,dr. \)

Here \(u\notin C\) implies \(u\ne t\), so the promised mixed-derivative bounds apply. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle |\partial_tV(s\mu)-\partial_tV(s\mu^C)| \leq s\sum_{u\notin C}\mu_u\kappa_{tu}. \)

Integrating and multiplying by \(\mu_t\) yields

\(\displaystyle |a_t-\widehat a_t| \leq \frac{\mu_t}{2}\sum_{u\notin C(t)}\mu_u\kappa_{tu}. \)

Upon summing over \(t\), every unordered pair separated by the partition appears twice. Symmetry of \(\kappa\) therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_t|a_t-\widehat a_t| \leq \frac12\sum_t\sum_{u\notin C(t)}\mu_t\mu_u\kappa_{tu} =F(\mathcal C). \)

Thus a qualifying partition automatically satisfies the requested certificate, since

\(\displaystyle \sum_t|a_t-\widehat a_t| \leq F(\mathcal C)\leq\varepsilon\leq2\varepsilon. \)

This argument uses only smoothness and the derivative bounds.

2. Source problem and reduction

We use Partition Into Triangles: given a simple graph \(G=(U,E)\), decide whether its vertices can be partitioned into triples, each inducing a triangle. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even for graphs of maximum degree four, by Theorem 10 of [van Rooij, van Kooten Niekerk, and Bodlaender](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00224-012-9412-5).

Take a nonempty such graph. Write \(n=|U|\) and \(M=|E|\).

If \(3\nmid n\) or \(M<n\), a triangle partition is impossible: a triangle partition has \(n/3\) triangles containing altogether \(n\) distinct edges. These cases are mapped to a fixed promised no-instance described below. Otherwise, \(n\geq3\) and \(M\geq n\).

Let \(D\) be the least power of two satisfying \(D\geq n\). Construct the society as follows:

  • The support contains the \(n\) graph vertices and \(D-n\) additional types, so \(\tau=D\).
  • Every type has mass \(\mu_t=1/D\).
  • Set \(q=3\) and \(\varepsilon=M-n\).
  • Set \(\kappa_{tu}=D^2\mathbf1_{\{\{t,u\}\in E\}}\), where additional types have no incident edges.
  • With \(A=4D+1\), use the polynomial

\(\displaystyle V(x)=A\sum_{t\in T}x_t-D^2\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}x_ux_v.\)

All masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one. All bounds are nonnegative integers, symmetric in their indices, and \(\varepsilon\geq0\).

3. Verification of every function promise

The constructed \(V\) is a polynomial, hence is defined and twice continuously differentiable everywhere. Also \(V(0)=0\).

For every type \(t\),

\(\displaystyle \partial_tV(x)=A-D^2\sum_{u:\{t,u\}\in E}x_u. \)

The graph has maximum degree four, and every coordinate in \(B\) is at most \(1/D\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \partial_tV(x)\geq A-D^2\frac4D=1. \)

The same bound holds for additional types, whose neighbor sums are empty. For \(x\in B\), the segment from zero to \(x\) lies in \(B\), and hence

\(\displaystyle V(x)=\int_0^1\sum_t x_t\partial_tV(sx)\,ds \geq\sum_t x_t\geq0. \)

For distinct types,

\(\displaystyle \partial_t\partial_uV(x) =-D^2\mathbf1_{\{\{t,u\}\in E\}} =-\kappa_{tu}. \)

Thus every supplied mixed-derivative bound is exact.

To check the remaining inequalities, define

\(\displaystyle \beta(x,y)=D^2\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}(x_uy_v+x_vy_u). \)

Expansion of the quadratic polynomial gives

\(\displaystyle V(x+y)=V(x)+V(y)-\beta(x,y). \)

For nonnegative vectors, \(\beta(x,y)\geq0\), proving the required subadditivity.

A second expansion gives

\(\displaystyle V(x+y+z) =V(x)+V(y)+V(z)-\beta(x,y)-\beta(x,z)-\beta(y,z). \)

Using the two-vector identity, the right-hand side is exactly

\(\displaystyle V(x+y)+V(x+z)+V(y+z)-V(x)-V(y)-V(z). \)

The required three-vector inequality therefore holds with equality. These identities hold in particular for every disjoint-support triple specified in the promises.

For completeness, the fixed no-instance uses four types of mass \(1/4\), \(q=3\), \(\varepsilon=0\), and the construction above with the complete graph on four vertices: \(D=4\), \(A=17\), and \(\kappa_{tu}=16\) for every distinct pair. The preceding promise checks apply. Every admissible partition separates some pair because no issue can contain all four types; that pair contributes one to \(F\). Thus this is a promised no-instance.

4. Equivalence of the partition questions

For each graph edge, its cut weight is

\(\displaystyle \mu_u\mu_v\kappa_{uv} =\frac1D\frac1D D^2=1. \)

Every other pair has weight zero. Therefore \(F(\mathcal C)\) equals the number of graph edges whose endpoints belong to different issues.

Let

\(\displaystyle I(\mathcal C)=\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}|E(G[C\cap U])| \)

be the number of graph edges internal to issues. Every edge is either internal or cut, so

\(\displaystyle F(\mathcal C)=M-I(\mathcal C).\)

Consider an issue \(C\), and put \(k=|C\cap U|\). Since \(|C|\leq3\), we have \(k\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), and

\(\displaystyle |E(G[C\cap U])|\leq\binom{k}{2}\leq k. \)

Equality between the edge count and \(k\) holds precisely when either \(k=0\), or \(k=3\) and the three vertices form a triangle. Indeed, for \(k=1\) and \(k=2\), the maximum edge counts are respectively zero and one, strictly below \(k\).

Because the issues partition all graph vertices,

\(\displaystyle I(\mathcal C)\leq\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}|C\cap U|=n.\)

Now suppose \(\mathcal C\) qualifies. Then

\(\displaystyle M-I(\mathcal C)=F(\mathcal C)\leq\varepsilon=M-n, \)

so \(I(\mathcal C)\geq n\). Combining the two bounds gives \(I(\mathcal C)=n\). Each issue’s nonnegative deficit

\(\displaystyle |C\cap U|-|E(G[C\cap U])|\)

must consequently vanish. Thus every issue containing graph vertices contains exactly three of them, forming a triangle. Such an issue cannot contain an additional type because its size is already three. These issues give a triangle partition of \(G\).

Conversely, if \(G\) has a triangle partition, take those triangles as issues and place every additional type in a singleton issue. Exactly \(n\) graph edges are internal, so

\(\displaystyle F(\mathcal C)=M-n=\varepsilon.\)

We have proved the equivalence

\(\displaystyle G\text{ has a triangle partition} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed society has a qualifying partition}. \)

5. Encoding size and verification

Since \(D<2n\), the support has size \(\tau=O(n)\). Maximum degree four implies \(M\leq2n\), so the polynomial \(V\) has an arithmetic circuit with \(m=O(n)\) gates.

The constants \(D,D^2,A\), the masses, and the tolerance have \(O(\log n)\) bits each. Even listing the entire matrix of bounds takes only \(O(n^2\log n)\) bits. The reduction is therefore polynomial-time and produces only promised inputs.

On promised inputs, a feasibility witness assigns each type an issue label. It requires \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) bits. A verifier counts issue sizes and computes \(F(\mathcal C)\) exactly using polynomially many rational operations of polynomial bit length. No evaluation of \(V\), integration, or verification of the analytic promises is needed.

Thus the combinatorial feasibility predicate is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, with hardness witnessed entirely within the promised class.

6. Exact allocations do not remove the hardness

Given a partition, its requested integral expressions can be produced in polynomial time: differentiate the arithmetic circuit, substitute \(s\mu^{C(t)}\), and retain the definite-integral notation. Circuit sharing keeps the expression representation polynomial in size.

In the reduction, even integration is elementary. Let \(d_G(t)\) be the number of graph neighbors of \(t\), and let \(d_C(t)\) count those neighbors in the same issue. Both are zero for additional types. Direct integration gives

\(\displaystyle a_t=\frac AD-\frac{d_G(t)}2, \qquad \widehat a_t=\frac AD-\frac{d_C(t)}2. \)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sum_t|a_t-\widehat a_t| =\frac12\sum_t(d_G(t)-d_C(t)) =F(\mathcal C), \)

because every cut edge contributes at both endpoints. The stronger certificate from Section 1 is therefore sharp.

The existence question is already \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under these elementary allocation formulas. Hence the requested approximation problem has no polynomial-time algorithm in the stated parameters unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
