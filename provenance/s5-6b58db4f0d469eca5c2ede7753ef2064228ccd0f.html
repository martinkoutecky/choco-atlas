The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of contact classes \(J\), rational class masses \(\nu_j\), a row-stochastic contact matrix \(K\), initial red, blue, and agnostic masses \(R_j,B_j,A_j\) summing to \(\nu_j\), a horizon \(\tau\), and a seed budget \(\beta\), choose \(0\le x_j^B\le B_j\) and \(0\le x_j^A\le A_j\) with \(\sum_j(x_j^B+x_j^A)\le\beta\). Set \(R_j^0=R_j+x_j^B+x_j^A\) and \(A_j^0=A_j-x_j^A\). At each round define \(r_j^t=R_j^t/\nu_j\), \(a_j^t=A_j^t/\nu_j\), \(q_j^{R,t}=\sum_\ell K_{j\ell}r_\ell^t\), and \(q_j^{A,t}=\sum_\ell K_{j\ell}a_\ell^t\), then update \(R_j^{t+1}=\nu_j(q_j^{R,t}+r_j^tq_j^{A,t})\) and \(A_j^{t+1}=A_j^tq_j^{A,t}\). What is \(\max_x\sum_jR_j^\tau\), and what approximation guarantees can be achieved in time polynomial in \(|J|\), \(\tau\), input bit length \(L\), and \(1/\epsilon\)?

Proved results and remaining question

The first question has an affirmative answer: a deterministic algorithm returns feasible rational seeds with value at least
$$ (1-1/e-\epsilon)\mathrm{OPT} $$
in time polynomial in $m,\tau,T,L,1/\epsilon$.

I also prove a new hardness result for the exact mass model: exact optimization is NP-hard already for $T=1$, and a fully polynomial approximation scheme would imply $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$ in the deterministic case, or $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}$ in the randomized case.

These results do not settle whether some fixed approximation factor $1-1/e+\delta$ is achievable. That part remains open.

1. Valid states and the optimization domain

Write
$$ b_j^t=\frac{B_j^t}{\nu_j}, \qquad q_j^{B,t}=\sum_\ell K_{j\ell}b_\ell^t. $$
The normalized updates are
$$ r_j^{t+1}=q_j^{R,t}+r_j^tq_j^{A,t}, \qquad a_j^{t+1}=a_j^tq_j^{A,t}. $$
Using the identities $r_j^t+a_j^t+b_j^t=1$ and $q_j^{R,t}+q_j^{A,t}+q_j^{B,t}=1$, we obtain
$$ \begin{aligned} b_j^{t+1} &=1-q_j^{R,t}-(r_j^t+a_j^t)q_j^{A,t}\\ &=q_j^{B,t}+b_j^tq_j^{A,t}. \end{aligned} $$
Thus all updated fractions are nonnegative and sum to one. Induction proves state validity at every time.

Index the positive seed capacities by
$$ I=\{(j,c):c\in\{B,A\},\ C_{j,c}>0\}, \qquad C_{j,B}=B_j,\quad C_{j,A}=A_j. $$
Let $n=|I|\le\tau$, and write $x_i,C_i$ for these coordinates and capacities.

Define
$$ f(x)=F_T(x) $$
on the entire box $0\le x_i\le C_i$. Even when the budget is exceeded, such points still specify valid initial states. The recurrence makes $f$ a polynomial, because every denominator $\nu_j$ is a fixed positive input constant.

The budget-feasible region is compact and nonempty, so an optimal real vector $x^\star$ exists.

If $n=0$, all mass is initially red, remains red, and the zero seed vector is optimal with value one. Henceforth assume $n>0$.

2. An exact representation and diminishing returns

2.1 Independent computation trees

For a root of class $j$ and depth $t+1$, construct two independent depth-$t$ subtrees:

  • an own-state subtree of class $j$;
  • a contacted subtree whose class $\ell$ is sampled according to row $j$ of $K$.

The root takes the contacted subtree’s state when that state is red or blue; otherwise it takes the own-state subtree’s state.

At a depth-zero leaf of class $j$, sample its original state with probabilities
$$ R_j/\nu_j,\quad B_j/\nu_j,\quad A_j/\nu_j. $$
Conditional on an original state corresponding to coordinate $i$, recolour the leaf red independently with probability $x_i/C_i$.

The resulting initial state probabilities are exactly the seeded fractions. Independence of the two subtrees gives red and agnostic probabilities
$$ q_j^{R,t}+r_j^tq_j^{A,t}, \qquad a_j^tq_j^{A,t}, $$
respectively. Induction therefore identifies these tree probabilities with the deterministic mass recurrence.

Finally sample the root class with probabilities $\nu_j$. Its probability of being red at depth $T$ is $f(x)$.

This representation uses independent copies. It does not identify the mass recurrence with a stochastic process on a fixed finite graph.

2.2 Conditional coverage formula

Order the leaves recursively by putting the contacted subtree before the own-state subtree. Induction on depth shows that the output is the first nonagnostic leaf in this order, or agnostic if every leaf is agnostic.

Condition on all tree class labels and all original leaf states.

If the first originally nonagnostic leaf is red, the output stays red under every seed choice: any newly nonagnostic leaf preceding it is also red.

If the first originally nonagnostic leaf is blue, the output becomes red precisely when at least one leaf in the prefix ending at that blue leaf is seeded. If every original leaf is agnostic, the output becomes red precisely when at least one leaf is seeded.

Let $W$ indicate that the original output is not red. When $W=1$, let $N_i$ count occurrences of seed type $i$ in the relevant prefix. There are $N=2^T$ leaves, so
$$ \sum_iN_i\le N. $$
Conditional independence of seeding decisions yields
$$ f(x)=f(0)+ \mathbb E\left[ W\left(1-\prod_i(1-x_i/C_i)^{N_i}\right) \right]. $$
The finite probability space in this expectation is independent of $x$.

For a fixed count vector, put
$$ g(x)=1-\prod_i(1-x_i/C_i)^{N_i}. $$
When $N_i\ge1$,
$$ \partial_i g(x)= \frac{N_i}{C_i}(1-x_i/C_i)^{N_i-1} \prod_{k\ne i}(1-x_k/C_k)^{N_k}\ge0. $$
When $i\ne k$ and both counts are positive,
$$ \partial_{ik}g(x)= -\frac{N_iN_k}{C_iC_k} (1-x_i/C_i)^{N_i-1} (1-x_k/C_k)^{N_k-1} \prod_{\ell\ne i,k}(1-x_\ell/C_\ell)^{N_\ell}\le0. $$
For $N_i\ge2$,
$$ \partial_{ii}g(x)= -\frac{N_i(N_i-1)}{C_i^2} (1-x_i/C_i)^{N_i-2} \prod_{k\ne i}(1-x_k/C_k)^{N_k}\le0. $$
If the required counts are absent, the corresponding derivative is zero. These polynomial formulas also hold at the boundary.

Differentiating the finite expectation proves
$$ \partial_i f(x)\ge0, \qquad \partial_{ik}f(x)\le0 \quad\text{for every }i,k. $$
Consequently, $f$ is monotone, and each marginal derivative is nonincreasing whenever any coordinate increases.

With
$$ C_{\min}=\min_iC_i, \qquad U=\frac{2^T}{C_{\min}}, \qquad H=\frac{4^T}{C_{\min}^2}, $$
the same formulas give
$$ 0\le\partial_i f(x)\le U, \qquad |\partial_{ii}f(x)|\le H. $$
Although these bounds can be exponentially large, their binary descriptions have length polynomial in $T,L$.

3. A positive lower bound on the optimum

Choose any coordinate $i=(j,c)$ and seed only that coordinate by
$$ s=\min\{\beta,C_i\}>0. $$
Nonnegativity implies
$$ r^{t+1}\ge Kr^t $$
componentwise, and therefore
$$ r^T\ge K^Tr^0\ge \frac{s}{\nu_j}K^Te_j. $$
Here $K^T$ denotes the $T$-th matrix power.

Every class has a positive incoming transition. For $m>1$, failure of this property would contradict irreducibility; for $m=1$, row-stochasticity gives $K_{11}=1$.

Starting at $j$ and repeatedly choosing a predecessor produces a positive walk of length exactly $T$ ending at $j$. Its transition product is at least $p^T$. Hence some entry in column $j$ of $K^T$ is at least $p^T$.

Writing $\nu_{\min}=\min_j\nu_j$, we obtain the explicit rational bound
$$ 0<\mu:= \nu_{\min}p^T\frac{s}{\nu_j} \le\mathrm{OPT}. $$
Also $\mu\le s\le\beta$.

The rational $\mu$ has polynomial bit length. Under the transition promise,
$$ \log\mu^{-1}=O(L+T\log m), $$
where the implicit constant may depend on $d$.

In fact, the approximation algorithm below does not need that promise: rational input entries satisfy $p\ge2^{-L}$, giving the still-polynomial bound
$$ \log\mu^{-1}=O(TL+L). $$

4. A polynomial-time gradient oracle

For a rational query point in the capacity box and rational accuracy $\rho>0$, every coordinate of $\nabla f(x)$ can be approximated within absolute error $\rho$ in time polynomial in
$$ m,n,T,L,\ \text{the query bit length},\ \log(1/\rho). $$

To prove this, differentiate the recurrence:
$$ \begin{aligned} D_i r_j^{t+1} ={}&\sum_\ell K_{j\ell}D_i r_\ell^t +(D_i r_j^t)q_j^{A,t} +r_j^t\sum_\ell K_{j\ell}D_i a_\ell^t,\\ D_i a_j^{t+1} ={}&(D_i a_j^t)q_j^{A,t} +a_j^t\sum_\ell K_{j\ell}D_i a_\ell^t. \end{aligned} $$
For a seed coordinate belonging to class $j$, initially
$$ D_i r_j^0=1/\nu_j. $$
The initial agnostic derivative is $-1/\nu_j$ for an agnostic seed coordinate and zero for a blue seed coordinate. All derivatives at other classes are initially zero. Finally,
$$ D_i f(x)=\sum_j\nu_jD_i r_j^T. $$

4.1 Treatment of fixed divisions

Compute each reciprocal $1/\nu_j$ exactly as a rational constant before evaluating the circuit. Initial normalized fractions are then formed by multiplication by this constant.

Thus the evaluated circuit has only addition, subtraction and multiplication gates. It does not divide by rounded denominators. The exact reciprocal constants have polynomial bit length.

The circuit has
$$ S=O(nTm^2+nm) $$
gates.

4.2 Bounds and rounding precision

Exact state fractions lie in $[0,1]$. Exact derivatives of any state probability have absolute value at most $U$: differentiate the tree representation one leaf occurrence at a time. A single occurrence contributes at most $1/C_i$ in absolute value, because its contribution is a difference of two conditional event probabilities divided by $C_i$. There are at most $2^T$ occurrences.

The weighted sums in the recurrence and their partial sums are bounded by the corresponding sums of absolute values. Consequently, every exact circuit intermediate is bounded by, for example, the integer
$$ M=\left\lceil10m(1+U+\nu_{\min}^{-1})\right\rceil. $$
Its logarithm is polynomially bounded in $T,L,\log m$.

Round all rational inputs, including reciprocal constants, and every gate output to a fixed-point grid of spacing
$$ \zeta=2^{-b}. $$
Each rounding contributes error at most $\zeta$.

If both input errors are at most $E\le1$, an addition or subtraction has error at most $2E$ before rounding. A multiplication has error at most
$$ 2ME+E^2\le(2M+1)E. $$
With $A=2M+3$, induction over the circuit gives final error at most
$$ A^{S+1}\zeta, $$
provided this quantity is at most one. Choose $b$ so that
$$ A^{S+1}2^{-b}\le\min\{\rho,1\}. $$
This requires only
$$ O\bigl(S\log A+\log\max\{1,\rho^{-1}\}\bigr) $$
fractional bits. The same induction ensures that all preceding errors are at most one, justifying the multiplication bound.

All fixed-point operands therefore have polynomial bit length, proving the oracle claim.

5. A finite greedy algorithm

Set
$$ \eta=\epsilon/4, \qquad \gamma=\frac{\epsilon\mu}{4\beta}, \qquad \rho=\frac{\eta\gamma}{10}, \qquad h=\frac{\rho}{H}. $$
Start at $x=0$.

At each iteration:

  • Return if the budget is exhausted or all coordinates are saturated.
  • For every unsaturated coordinate, compute an approximation $g_i$ satisfying

$$ |g_i-\partial_i f(x)|\le\rho. $$
Put $G=\max_i g_i$. If $G\le\gamma$, return.

  • Choose an unsaturated coordinate $i$ attaining $G$, and put

$$ q=(1-\eta)G, \qquad s_{\max}=\min\left\{C_i-x_i,\ \beta-\sum_kx_k\right\}. $$

  • Approximate $\partial_i f(x+s_{\max}e_i)$ within error $\rho$. If this approximation is at least $q$, take increment $s_{\max}$.
  • Otherwise bisect $[0,s_{\max}]$, retaining a left endpoint whose derivative approximation is at least $q$ and a right endpoint whose approximation is below $q$. At the initial left endpoint, use the stored value $G>q$. Stop when the interval length is at most $h$, and take its right endpoint as the increment.

All endpoint arithmetic is exact rational arithmetic. Every increment is positive, respects the capacity, and respects the remaining budget.

5.1 Quality throughout an increment

For a bisection increment with final endpoints $l,u$, the true endpoint derivatives satisfy
$$ \partial_i f(x+le_i)\ge q-\rho, \qquad \partial_i f(x+ue_i)\le q+\rho. $$
The curvature bound and $u-l\le\rho/H$ imply
$$ \partial_i f(x+ue_i)\ge q-2\rho. $$
Since this derivative is nonincreasing along the increment, it is at least $q-2\rho$ throughout the increment.

A full increment has endpoint derivative at least $q-\rho$, so the same weaker lower bound holds.

At the start of the increment, every eligible true marginal is at most $G+\rho$, and all marginals subsequently decrease. Because $G>\gamma$,
$$ \rho\le\eta G/10. $$
Moreover,
$$ \begin{aligned} q-2\rho-(1-2\eta)(G+\rho) &=\eta G-(3-2\eta)\rho\\ &\ge0. \end{aligned} $$
Thus throughout each increment the selected marginal is at least
$$ c=1-2\eta=1-\epsilon/2 $$
times the largest eligible true marginal.

5.2 Polynomially many increments

An increment ending strictly before both the capacity and budget limits must be a bisection increment. Its endpoint satisfies
$$ \begin{aligned} \partial_i f(x_{\mathrm{new}}) &\le(1-\eta)G+\rho\\ &\le(1-\eta/2)(G-\rho)\\ &\le(1-\eta/2)\partial_i f(x_{\mathrm{old}}). \end{aligned} $$
The middle inequality follows from
$$ \frac{\eta G}{2}-(2-\eta/2)\rho\ge0, $$
using $\rho\le\eta G/10$.

At the start of each such increment, the selected true derivative exceeds
$$ \gamma-\rho\ge\gamma/2. $$
Every derivative is initially at most $U$ and never increases. Therefore a coordinate can undergo at most
$$ 1+\frac{\log(2U/\gamma)}{-\log(1-\eta/2)} $$
interior increments. Since
$$ -\log(1-\eta/2)\ge\eta/2, $$
this number is polynomial in $T,L,1/\epsilon$.

There are additionally at most $n$ capacity-saturating increments and one budget-exhausting increment.

Each bisection requires at most
$$ O(1+\log(H/\rho)) $$
oracle evaluations, because $s_{\max}\le1$.

For output bit complexity, take a common denominator $D$ for the input capacities and budget. Capacity and budget endpoints introduce no new denominator factors. Bisection introduces only powers of two. The total number of bisection rounds is polynomial, so all iterates have denominator dividing
$$ D\,2^Q $$
for a polynomially bounded integer $Q$.

Together with the gradient oracle, these bounds prove polynomial running time and polynomial output length.

6. Approximation guarantee

Let $x^\star$ be an optimal feasible vector. For any current feasible point $x$, define $y=x\vee x^\star$ coordinatewise. This vector belongs to the capacity box, although it may exceed the budget.

Monotonicity and diminishing returns give
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{OPT}-f(x) &\le f(y)-f(x)\\ &=\int_0^1\nabla f(x+t(y-x))\cdot(y-x)\,dt\\ &\le\nabla f(x)\cdot(y-x). \end{aligned} $$
A saturated coordinate contributes zero, and
$$ \sum_i(y_i-x_i) =\sum_i(x_i^\star-x_i)^+ \le\sum_i x_i^\star \le\beta. $$
Hence, whenever an unsaturated coordinate exists,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}-f(x) \le\beta\max_{i:x_i<C_i}\partial_i f(x). $$

There are three termination cases.

6.1 Exhausted budget

Parametrize the algorithm’s piecewise linear path by spent mass $b$. The marginal-quality result implies, almost everywhere along the path,
$$ \frac{d}{db}f(x(b)) \ge\frac{c}{\beta}\bigl(\mathrm{OPT}-f(x(b))\bigr). $$
Multiplying the corresponding inequality for the gap by $e^{cb/\beta}$ and integrating yields
$$ \mathrm{OPT}-f(x(b)) \le e^{-cb/\beta}\bigl(\mathrm{OPT}-f(0)\bigr). $$
At $b=\beta$, using $f(0)\ge0$,
$$ f(x)\ge(1-e^{-c})\mathrm{OPT}. $$
Since $c=1-\epsilon/2$,
$$ e^{-c}-e^{-1} =\int_c^1e^{-u}\,du \le1-c=\epsilon/2. $$
Therefore
$$ f(x)\ge(1-1/e-\epsilon/2)\mathrm{OPT}, $$
which implies the requested guarantee.

6.2 Small marginals

If the algorithm stops at $G\le\gamma$, every eligible true marginal is at most $\gamma+\rho$. Thus
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{OPT}-f(x) &\le\beta(\gamma+\rho)\\ &=\frac{\epsilon\mu}{4}(1+\eta/10)\\ &\le\epsilon\mu/3\\ &\le\epsilon\mathrm{OPT}/3. \end{aligned} $$
This is stronger than the requested approximation.

6.3 All coordinates saturated

The returned vector dominates every feasible vector coordinatewise. Monotonicity makes it optimal.

All termination cases establish a deterministic polynomial-time $(1-1/e-\epsilon)$-approximation.

7. New hardness result for the exact fractional model

The following reduction concerns the stated deterministic recurrence, including fractional seeds and all contact-matrix promises.

7.1 Construction from Independent Set

Start with a simple undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ having $n\ge2$ vertices and an integer $1\le k<n$. Deciding whether $G$ has an independent set of size at least $k$ is NP-complete: the standard Independent Set problem follows from the NP-completeness of Clique by complementing the graph. The cases outside this range are trivial and can be handled separately. See [Karp’s NP-completeness results](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Use one class for each vertex and an additional class $0$, so
$$ m=n+1. $$
Let $M$ be the smallest power of two with $M\ge m$. Assign masses
$$ A_v=\nu_v=1/M\quad(v\in V), \qquad R_0=\nu_0=(M-n)/M. $$
All other masses are zero. Thus every mass is a dyadic rational and $\tau=m$.

For a vertex class $v$, set
$$ K_{vu}= \begin{cases} 1/m,&\{v,u\}\in E,\\ 0,&u\in V\text{ and }\{v,u\}\notin E, \end{cases} \qquad K_{v0}=1-\deg(v)/m. $$
For class $0$, set
$$ K_{0j}=1/m \quad\text{for every class }j. $$

Every row sums to one. Since $\deg(v)\le n-1=m-2$,
$$ K_{v0}\ge2/m>0. $$
Every vertex class contacts $0$, and class $0$ contacts every class. Therefore $K$ is irreducible. The positive self-loop at $0$ makes it aperiodic. Finally,
$$ p=1/m, \qquad p^{-1}=m\le m^d. $$

Choose
$$ T=1, \qquad \beta=(n-k)/M. $$
The budget is positive and less than one. The whole construction has polynomial encoding length.

7.2 Exact objective

Only the vertex classes can be seeded. Write their initial agnostic fractions after seeding as
$$ a_v=1-Mx_v^A. $$
Then $0\le a_v\le1$, and budget feasibility is equivalent to
$$ \sum_v a_v\ge k. $$

There is no blue mass. The blue update in Section 1 shows that blue remains absent. Class $0$ has initial agnostic fraction zero, so its agnostic fraction at time one is also zero.

Consequently,
$$ \begin{aligned} F_1(x) &=1-\sum_j\nu_j a_j^1\\ &=1-\frac{1}{Mm}\sum_v\sum_{u:\{v,u\}\in E}a_va_u\\ &=1-\frac{2}{Mm}\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}a_ua_v. \end{aligned} $$

Thus maximizing $F_1$ is equivalent to minimizing
$$ E_G(a)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}a_ua_v $$
subject to
$$ 0\le a_v\le1, \qquad \sum_va_v\ge k. $$

7.3 Fractional solutions admit lossless rounding

Lemma. Every feasible $a$ can be replaced by an integral vector $\widehat a\in\{0,1\}^n$ with
$$ \sum_v\widehat a_v=k, \qquad E_G(\widehat a)\le E_G(a). $$

Proof. If $\sum_va_v>k$, replace $a$ by
$$ a'=\frac{k}{\sum_va_v}a. $$
This preserves the box constraints and makes the sum exactly $k$. Since the multiplier is at most one,
$$ E_G(a')= \left(\frac{k}{\sum_va_v}\right)^2E_G(a) \le E_G(a). $$

Now suppose the sum is $k$ and two coordinates $a_i,a_j$ are strictly between zero and one. Consider
$$ a(s)=a+se_i-se_j $$
on the interval
$$ -\min\{a_i,1-a_j\} \le s\le \min\{1-a_i,a_j\}. $$
This preserves the sum and all box constraints.

Terms of $E_G$ involving only one of $i,j$ are affine in $s$. If $\{i,j\}\in E$, its term is
$$ (a_i+s)(a_j-s) =a_ia_j+s(a_j-a_i)-s^2. $$
Therefore
$$ E_G(a(s)) =E_G(a)+\lambda s-\mathbf 1_{\{\{i,j\}\in E\}}s^2 $$
for some real $\lambda$. This is concave on the interval.

A concave function on a closed interval has an endpoint whose value is no larger than its value at any specified interior point: the interior value is at least the corresponding convex combination of the endpoint values. Choose such an endpoint. At least one of $a_i,a_j$ becomes zero or one, and the energy does not increase.

Repeat. Each step decreases the number of fractional coordinates. A vector with integer sum $k$ cannot have exactly one fractional coordinate. Hence the procedure ends with an integral vector of sum $k$. This proves the lemma. $\square$

For rational input vectors, this rounding can also be performed in polynomial time: after the initial scaling, every step uses rational additions, comparisons and evaluations of a quadratic polynomial, and there are at most $n$ steps.

7.4 Consequences

Let
$$ e_{\min}(k)= \min_{\substack{U\subseteq V\\|U|=k}} |E(G[U])|. $$
The rounding lemma and the correspondence between integral vectors and vertex subsets give the exact identity
$$ \mathrm{OPT} =1-\frac{2e_{\min}(k)}{Mm}. $$
In particular,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G\text{ has an independent set of size at least }k. $$
This proves NP-hardness of exact optimization already at $T=1$.

Furthermore, define
$$ \Delta=\frac{2}{Mm}. $$
The reduction has the gap
$$ \begin{cases} \mathrm{OPT}=1,&\text{yes instance},\\ \mathrm{OPT}\le1-\Delta,&\text{no instance}. \end{cases} $$
Since $m\le M<2m$, the reciprocal $\Delta^{-1}$ is polynomially bounded in the graph size.

Suppose a fully polynomial approximation scheme existed: for every rational accuracy $\zeta>0$, it returned feasible seeds of value at least
$$ (1-\zeta)\mathrm{OPT} $$
in time polynomial in the input length and $1/\zeta$.

Run it with $\zeta=\Delta/4$, and accept precisely when the returned feasible seeds satisfy
$$ F_1(x)>1-\Delta/2. $$
Feasibility and $F_1(x)$ can be checked exactly in polynomial time, because the horizon is one.

On a yes instance, the approximation guarantee gives
$$ F_1(x)\ge1-\Delta/4>1-\Delta/2. $$
On a no instance, every feasible solution has value at most $1-\Delta$, so acceptance is impossible.

A deterministic scheme would therefore imply $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$. A randomized scheme succeeding with probability at least $2/3$ would yield an $\mathrm{RP}$ algorithm for Independent Set: no instances are always rejected, and yes instances are accepted with probability at least $2/3$. Hence it would imply $\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{RP}$, and thus $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}$.

8. What remains open about a fixed improvement

The hardness gap in Section 7 is inverse-polynomial, not a constant gap. It therefore does not rule out an approximation factor $1-1/e+\delta$ for any fixed $\delta$ in the requested range.

The supplied [discrete adoption hardness theorem](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0442.pdf) concerns choosing whole vertices. It cannot be transferred without an additional reduction that controls fractional seeding and reproduces the mass recurrence.

One concrete obstruction is already visible in the all-agnostic case. If $A_j=\nu_j$ for every class, the feasible uniform fractional seeds
$$ x_j^A=\beta\nu_j $$
give initial fractions $a_j^0=1-\beta$, uniformly in $j$. Row-stochasticity and induction then give
$$ a_j^t=(1-\beta)^{2^t}. $$
Blue stays absent, so
$$ F_T(x)=1-(1-\beta)^{2^T}. $$
For one class with $K=(1)$, this is the exact optimum: seeding mass $s$ gives $1-(1-s)^{2^T}$, which is increasing in $s$, so $s=\beta$ is optimal.

By contrast, a single stochastic vertex with self-contact, initially red with probability $\beta$ and otherwise agnostic, retains its initial state. Its red probability remains $\beta$. Thus the two objectives already differ in the smallest nontrivial example.

The unresolved target is therefore specific: either establish a polynomial-time fixed improvement above $1-1/e$ for these mass dynamics, or construct a constant-gap hardness reduction that remains valid under fractional seeding.
