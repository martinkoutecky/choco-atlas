The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\) and a support \(T\) of \(\tau\) strict rankings with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), compute candidate points \(x_c\) and type points \(y_t\) in \(\mathbb{Q}^{\tau}\), with polynomial bit length, such that \(c \succ_t c'\) exactly when \(\|y_t-x_c\|_2 < \|y_t-x_{c'}\|_2\), for every \(t \in T\) and \(c,c' \in C\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set with \(m\geq 1\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq 1\) distinct strict rankings of all candidates, each with a positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Compute unrestricted rational points \(x_c,y_t\in\mathbb{Q}^{\tau}\), for every \(c\in C\) and \(t\in T\), with total encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), such that \(c\succ_t c'\iff\|y_t-x_c\|_2<\|y_t-x_{c'}\|_2\) for every type and every pair of distinct candidates. Distance ties are forbidden.

Is this embedding construction problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a finite list of \(n\) voters with strict rankings, an embedding in dimension \(n\) is computable in polynomial time for every \(p\)-norm with \(p>1\), including Euclidean distance. [Verified embedding result](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09201v3#S5).
No separate result specifically formulated for rational masses was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Require at least one candidate and one supported type, and explicitly interpret each ranking as a strict total order of all candidates.
  • Define L as the total binary input length and polynomial output bit length as the total encoding length of all coordinates.
  • Allow coordinates of either sign without bounding the geometric region; impose no general-position condition beyond the required absence of distance ties.

Result

The construction problem is solvable in polynomial time. In fact, integer coordinates suffice. The masses do not enter the distance constraints, so each support type can be embedded directly, retaining its given mass.

Construction

Enumerate the support as \(T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_\tau\}\). For each candidate \(c\), let \(r_i(c)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\) be its position in the ranking of \(t_i\), with position \(1\) most preferred. Thus
\(c\succ_{t_i}c'\iff r_i(c)<r_i(c')\).

Set \(B=\tau m^2\). Let \(e_i\) denote the \(i\)-th standard basis vector of \(\mathbb{Q}^{\tau}\), and output
\(x_c=(-r_1(c),\ldots,-r_\tau(c))\) and \(y_{t_i}=B e_i\).

These points have exactly the requested dimension and integer coordinates.

Proof of correctness

Define \(S_c=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}r_j(c)^2\). Since every rank lies between \(1\) and \(m\),
\(\tau\leq S_c\leq\tau m^2\).

Expanding the squared Euclidean distance gives
\(\|y_{t_i}-x_c\|_2^2=B^2+2B r_i(c)+S_c\).

Fix a type \(t_i\) and distinct candidates \(c,c'\) with \(c\succ_{t_i}c'\). Their integer ranks satisfy \(r_i(c')-r_i(c)\geq1\). Consequently,
\(\|y_{t_i}-x_{c'}\|_2^2-\|y_{t_i}-x_c\|_2^2 =2B(r_i(c')-r_i(c))+S_{c'}-S_c\).

The rank term is at least \(2B\), while the bounds on \(S_c\) imply
\(S_{c'}-S_c\geq-\tau(m^2-1)\). Therefore,
\(\|y_{t_i}-x_{c'}\|_2^2-\|y_{t_i}-x_c\|_2^2 \geq2\tau m^2-\tau(m^2-1)=\tau(m^2+1)>0\).

Because square root is strictly increasing on nonnegative numbers, this proves
\(\|y_{t_i}-x_c\|_2<\|y_{t_i}-x_{c'}\|_2\).

Conversely, if \(c\) is not preferred to \(c'\), strictness of the ranking implies \(c'\succ_{t_i}c\). Applying the same argument with the candidates exchanged gives the opposite strict distance inequality. Hence the required equivalence holds for every pair, and no distance ties occur.

For \(m=1\), the pairwise requirements are vacuous, and the same construction remains valid.

Encoding length and running time

There are \(m\tau+\tau^2\) output coordinates. Every coordinate has absolute value at most \(B=\tau m^2\), so each requires \(O(\log(2+\tau m^2))\) bits, including a sign and the constant denominator \(1\). The total coordinate encoding length is therefore
\(O((m\tau+\tau^2)\log(2+\tau m^2))\).

The rank table is obtained by scanning the input rankings. Computing \(B\) and writing the coordinate arrays uses polynomially many operations on integers of the stated bit length. Including reading the input, the algorithm thus runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The given positive rational mass \(\mu_{t_i}\) remains attached to \(y_{t_i}\); no expansion into individual voters is needed. This is the Euclidean specialization of the construction pattern in [Theorem 9 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09201v3#S5), with an explicit integer scale and encoding bound proved above.
