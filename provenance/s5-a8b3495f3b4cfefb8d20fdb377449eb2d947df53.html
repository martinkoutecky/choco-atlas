The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many opportunity types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), bidders \(B\), and normalized monotone diminishing-returns utilities \(F_b\), determine the worst-case ratio of the fluid greedy allocation to the offline optimum. Type \(t\) arrives at rate \(\mu_t\), and infinitesimal mass is assigned to a bidder maximizing its current marginal utility; in particular, determine whether the ratio is at least \(0.5096\) and whether a fixed-support instance can attain at most \(19/33\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) opportunity types \(T\) with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq1\) bidders \(B\); let \(L\) be the encoded input bit length.
Each bidder has a nonnegative, continuously differentiable utility \(F_b:\prod_{t\in T}[0,\mu_t]\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}\), with Lipschitz gradient, \(F_b(0)=0\), and \(0\leq\partial_tF_b(y)\leq\partial_tF_b(x)\) whenever \(x\leq y\). Utilities are accessed by value and gradient oracles. State any running times in \(m,\tau,L\), accuracy, and smoothness parameters, counting oracle calls separately.
An allocation is \(x=(x_{bt})\geq0\) with \(\sum_bx_{bt}=\mu_t\). Its welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_bF_b(x_b)\); define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_xW(x)\) over such allocations and restrict to \(\mathrm{OPT}>0\).
Over time \(s\in[0,1]\), every type arrives simultaneously at constant rate \(\mu_t\). A fluid greedy trajectory is absolutely continuous, starts at \(x(0)=0\), and satisfies almost everywhere \(\dot x_{bt}(s)\geq0\), \(\sum_b\dot x_{bt}(s)=\mu_t\), and \(\dot x_{bt}(s)>0\Rightarrow b\in\arg\max_c\partial_tF_c(x_c(s))\). Allocation is irrevocable; ties may split mass among maximizers, and the guarantee must hold for every admissible tie resolution.
Let \(\rho=\inf W(x(1))/\mathrm{OPT}\), taking the infimum over all these instances and greedy trajectories.
What is the sharp worst-case ratio \(\rho\)? In particular, is \(\rho\geq0.5096\), and does a single instance with fixed finite support admit a greedy trajectory with ratio at most \(19/33\)?
Discrete random-order greedy guarantees \(0.5096\) for monotone submodular welfare and, more generally, partition-matroid maximization; a \(19/33\) example is known for the latter class, without establishing that bound for bidder-separable welfare. [Buchbinder et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.05529v2)

Result

The sharp worst-case ratio is \(\boxed{\rho=1-1/e}\).

Consequently, \(\rho\geq0.5096\), and no instance in the stated class admits a greedy trajectory with ratio at most \(19/33\). The lower bound holds for every admissible tie resolution. A sequence of finite-support, bidder-separable examples proves sharpness.

1. A gradient bound for the optimality gap

Write \(\mu=(\mu_t)_{t\in T}\). For any bidder \(b\) and vectors \(u,z\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\), let \(d=(z-u)_+\), where the positive part is taken coordinatewise. Then \(u+d=u\vee z\), and the segment from \(u\) to \(u+d\) remains inside the utility’s domain.

Nonnegative partial derivatives imply coordinatewise monotonicity. The diminishing-returns hypothesis also gives \(\nabla F_b(u+rd)\leq\nabla F_b(u)\) for \(r\in[0,1]\). Therefore,

\[ F_b(z)-F_b(u) \leq F_b(u+d)-F_b(u) =\int_0^1\langle\nabla F_b(u+rd),d\rangle\,dr \leq\langle\nabla F_b(u),d\rangle \leq\langle\nabla F_b(u),z\rangle. \]

The last inequality uses \(0\leq d\leq z\) and \(\nabla F_b(u)\geq0\).

The allocation set is compact and welfare is continuous, so an optimal allocation \(x^*\) exists. Applying the preceding inequality bidder by bidder shows that, for any \(x\) in the product of the bidders’ boxes,

\[ \mathrm{OPT}-W(x) \leq\sum_{b,t}\partial_tF_b(x_b)x^*_{bt} \leq\sum_t\mu_t\max_b\partial_tF_b(x_b). \tag{1} \]

Here the final inequality follows from \(x^*_{bt}\geq0\) and \(\sum_bx^*_{bt}=\mu_t\). In particular, this argument never evaluates a utility outside its stated domain.

2. Every fluid greedy trajectory achieves \(1-1/e\)

Fix any admissible trajectory and set \(w(s)=W(x(s))\). Integrating its rate constraints gives \(\sum_bx_{bt}(s)=s\mu_t\); hence \(0\leq x_{bt}(s)\leq\mu_t\).

Because the utilities are continuously differentiable on compact boxes, their gradients are bounded. Thus \(w\) is absolutely continuous, and the chain rule holds almost everywhere. The greedy condition yields

\[ w'(s) =\sum_{b,t}\partial_tF_b(x_b(s))\dot x_{bt}(s) =\sum_t\mu_t\max_b\partial_tF_b(x_b(s)). \tag{2} \]

This equality remains valid under every admissible tie split: all bidders receiving positive rate for a type have the same maximal partial derivative.

Combining (1) and (2),

\[ w'(s)+w(s)\geq\mathrm{OPT} \quad\text{almost everywhere}. \]

Multiplying by \(e^s\), integrating from \(0\) to \(1\), and using \(w(0)=0\), we obtain

\[ e\,w(1)\geq(e-1)\mathrm{OPT}. \]

Therefore every admissible trajectory satisfies \(W(x(1))\geq(1-1/e)\mathrm{OPT}\).

This is the continuous-greedy differential inequality underlying the general guarantee in [Bian et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v54/bian17a/bian17a.pdf); the proof above checks it directly for the specified domains and trajectory rules.

3. Sharpness within bidder-separable welfare

Let \(n=2^k\), with \(k\geq1\). Take \(m=\tau=n\), with bidders and types indexed by \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and give every type mass \(\mu_t=1/n\). These masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one.

Every bidder has the identical utility

\[ F_b(z)=1-\prod_{t=1}^n(1-nz_t), \qquad z\in[0,1/n]^n. \]

We verify all hypotheses:

  • Each factor belongs to \([0,1]\), so \(0\leq F_b(z)\leq1\), and \(F_b(0)=0\).
  • The utility is polynomial, hence continuously differentiable.
  • Its partial derivatives are

\(\partial_tF_b(z)=n\prod_{j\ne t}(1-nz_j)\).
They are nonnegative and nonincreasing under coordinatewise increases.

  • Its Hessian has zero diagonal and off-diagonal entries

\(\partial_i\partial_jF_b(z)=-n^2\prod_{\ell\notin\{i,j\}}(1-nz_\ell)\).
Every absolute row sum is at most \(n^2(n-1)\). Since the Hessian is symmetric, its Euclidean operator norm has the same upper bound. Integrating the Hessian along a segment proves that the gradient is \(n^2(n-1)\)-Lipschitz.

Allocate type \(t\) entirely to bidder \(t\). Every bidder then has a coordinate equal to \(1/n\), making its utility one. Since each bidder’s utility is at most one, \(\mathrm{OPT}=n\).

Now consider the equal-split trajectory \(x_{bt}(s)=s/n^2\). Its rates satisfy \(\dot x_{bt}(s)=1/n^2\) and \(\sum_b\dot x_{bt}(s)=1/n=\mu_t\). Along this trajectory,

\[ \partial_tF_b(x_b(s)) =n(1-s/n)^{n-1}, \]

independently of \(b\). Every bidder is therefore a maximizer for every type, so the trajectory is admissible.

At its endpoint, the welfare ratio is

\[ \frac{W(x(1))}{\mathrm{OPT}} =1-\left(1-\frac1n\right)^n. \]

As \(n\) tends to infinity through powers of two, this converges to \(1-1/e\). Every member of the sequence has finite support and satisfies all assumptions. Hence \(\rho\leq1-1/e\), completing the proof of equality.

4. Oracle complexity of a finite-step discretization

For completeness, the guarantee also has a direct oracle implementation with explicit accuracy dependence.

Let \(K_b\) be Euclidean gradient-Lipschitz bounds, \(K=\max_bK_b\), and \(R^2=\sum_t\mu_t^2\leq1\). The gradient of \(W\) is \(K\)-Lipschitz because its bidder blocks are independent.

Use \(m\) value queries to compute \(V=\max_bF_b(\mu)\). Monotonicity and the allocation giving everything to one bidder imply \(0<V\leq\mathrm{OPT}\leq mV\).

Starting at \(y^0=0\), perform \(N\) steps. At each step, query every bidder’s gradient, choose a maximizing bidder for each type, let \(v^j\) assign its entire mass to that bidder, and update \(y^{j+1}=y^j+v^j/N\). These points remain in the utility domains, and \(y^N\) is a feasible allocation.

The smoothness inequality, (1), and \(\|v^j\|_2^2=R^2\) give

\[ W(y^{j+1}) \geq W(y^j)+\frac{\mathrm{OPT}-W(y^j)}N -\frac{KR^2}{2N^2}. \]

Iterating this recurrence and using \((1-1/N)^N\leq e^{-1}\) yields

\[ W(y^N)\geq(1-1/e)\mathrm{OPT}-\frac{KR^2}{2N}. \]

Thus \(N\geq\max\{1,KR^2/(2\varepsilon V)\}\) ensures a ratio of at least \(1-1/e-\varepsilon\).

The algorithm uses \(m\) value-oracle calls, \(mN\) gradient-oracle calls, and \(O(Nm\tau)\) arithmetic operations and comparisons. Query coordinates have bit length \(O(L+\log N)\); rational bookkeeping costs \(O(Nm\tau\,\mathrm{poly}(L+\log N))\) bit operations. These counts treat oracle outputs as exactly comparable; their representation and evaluation costs are not specified by the input model.

5. The two numerical questions

Since \(1-1/e\approx0.63212056\), the requested \(0.5096\) guarantee holds with room to spare.

Also, \(e>5/2\), so \(1-1/e>3/5>19/33\). Therefore no finite-support instance—or any other instance satisfying the stated assumptions—can have an admissible trajectory with ratio at most \(19/33\).

The \(19/33\) construction in [Buchbinder et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.05529v2) concerns discrete random-order greedy for general partition-matroid maximization. It does not give that upper bound for this simultaneous-arrival fluid model.
