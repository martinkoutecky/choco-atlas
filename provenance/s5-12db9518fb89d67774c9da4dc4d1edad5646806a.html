Theorem 2 (Individual Regret) — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given τ cohorts with rational masses μθ summing to \(1\), per-member quotas \(qθ\), horizon \(H\), and an unknown distribution \(G\) over \([0,̄x]^τ\), each round observe an independent shock vector \(Z_s ∼ G\). Choose allocation masses \(a_{s,θ} ∈ [0,μθ]\) with total supply ρ = ∑θ μθqθ per round and cumulative cohort quotas \(Hμθqθ\). Does there exist an online algorithm whose high probability per-member regret for every cohort, relative to the offline policy maximizing expected welfare subject to the same expected quotas, is sublinear in \(H\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: an ordered list of \(\tau\ge2\) cohort types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Given binary-rational quotas \(0<q_\theta\le1\), bound \(\bar x>0\), horizon \(H\in\mathbb N\), and confidence parameter \(0<\delta<1\), put \(\rho=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta q_\theta\), with \(0<\rho<1\).
In round \(s\), observe the entire truthful valuation vector \(Z_s\sim G\), independently across rounds, where \(G\) is an unknown joint distribution on \([0,\bar x]^\tau\); coordinates may be dependent. Every member of cohort \(\theta\) has value \(Z_{s,\theta}\).
Choose irrevocable, possibly randomized allocation masses satisfying \(0\le a_{s,\theta}\le\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_\theta a_{s,\theta}=\rho\), and almost surely \(\sum_{s=1}^H a_{s,\theta}=H\mu_\theta q_\theta\). Per-member utility is the cohort's total utility divided by its mass.
The benchmark is a measurable, possibly randomized stationary policy \(b^*(Z)\) maximizing \(\mathbb E_G[\sum_\theta Z_\theta b_\theta(Z)]\), subject to \(0\le b_\theta(Z)\le\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_\theta b_\theta(Z)=\rho\) almost surely, and \(\mathbb E_G[b_\theta(Z)]=\mu_\theta q_\theta\). Among welfare maximizers, select the lexicographically greatest expected cohort-utility vector in the supplied cohort order.
Define \(R_\theta(H)=\mu_\theta^{-1}\bigl(H\mathbb E_G[Z_\theta b^*_\theta(Z)]-\sum_{s=1}^H Z_{s,\theta}a_{s,\theta}\bigr)\). The desired guarantee is \(\Pr\{\max_\theta R_\theta(H)\le r(H)\}\ge1-\delta\), uniformly over \(G\), with \(r(H)=o(H)\) for fixed society and \(\delta\).
For computation, use exact-real arithmetic and comparisons at unit cost, without an optimization oracle; \(L\) is the bit length of the supplied rational data. State running times in \(\tau,L,H,\log(1/\delta)\); there is one resource per round, so \(m=1\).

Is there a polynomial-time online algorithm achieving this simultaneous sublinear per-member regret guarantee, or is achieving this guarantee NP-hard?

For finite agents with independent, identically distributed valuations, a truthful online mechanism has simultaneous individual regret \(O(\bar x\sqrt{nH\log(nH/\delta)})\), treating offline optimization as an efficient oracle. [Yin–Agrawal–Zeevi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.12112).
General stochastic online allocation admits asymptotically optimal aggregate-reward algorithms, but that guarantee does not establish individual regret against this benchmark. [Balseiro–Lu–Mirrokni](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10124). No result settling this exact cohort problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified positive masses summing to one, at least two supported cohorts, positive quotas at most one, a positive bounded valuation domain, and nontrivial supply strictly between zero and one.
  • Interpreted cumulative quotas as exact pathwise equalities, rather than expected online constraints; fractional allocation removes any quota-integrality requirement.
  • Specified truthful full-vector observation before each irrevocable allocation and retained the graded question's arbitrary joint shock distribution.
  • Defined the offline benchmark as a stationary measurable policy with pointwise supply and capacity constraints and exact expected cohort quotas.
  • Resolved possible nonuniqueness of welfare-optimal cohort utilities by lexicographic selection in the supplied cohort order.
  • Defined per-member regret by dividing cohort utility differences by cohort mass, with a simultaneous one-sided high-probability bound uniform over the unknown distribution.
  • Chose an exact-real arithmetic computational model because arbitrary real shocks have no supplied finite encoding; identified the resource count as one and excluded an optimization oracle from the continuous algorithm.

Result

There is a polynomial-time online algorithm achieving the stated guarantee, even with arbitrary dependence between coordinates. For each fixed society and fixed confidence parameter, its simultaneous per-member regret is \(O(H^{2/3}\sqrt{\log H})\).

The proof uses exact lexicographic optimization, rather than an approximation to the welfare objective. Its main ingredient is a stability lemma: a policy defined by lexicographic prices has nearly the benchmark’s expected utility for every cohort whenever its expected quotas are nearly correct.

1. Preliminary reductions and benchmark existence

Write \(d_\theta=\mu_\theta q_\theta\), and let

\(P=\{a:0\leq a_\theta\leq\mu_\theta,\ \sum_\theta a_\theta=\rho\}\).

Randomization in a stationary policy can be replaced by its conditional expected allocation given the valuation vector. This preserves feasibility and all expected utilities. Thus the benchmark can be represented by a measurable function with values in \(P\).

The benchmark exists. Indeed, the feasible allocations form a nonempty weak-star compact subset of \(L^\infty(G)^\tau\): they are bounded, their pointwise inequalities and equality define weak-star closed sets, and their expected-quota constraints are weak-star continuous. Nonemptiness follows from the constant allocation \(a=d\). Welfare and each cohort’s expected utility are weak-star continuous because the valuations are bounded. Successively maximizing welfare and the cohort utilities therefore leaves a nonempty compact set at every stage.

Multiplying a cohort’s expected total utility by the positive constant \(\mu_\theta^{-1}\) does not change a lexicographic comparison. Hence we may use expected total cohort utilities in the lexicographic objectives.

If \(q_\theta=1\), feasibility forces the benchmark allocation to equal \(\mu_\theta\) almost surely. Our algorithm allocates \(\mu_\theta\) to this cohort every round. Remove these forced cohorts, subtract their masses from the resource, and retain the supplied order on the remaining cohorts. Denote their number by \(t\). Since \(\rho<1\), we have \(t\geq1\).

If \(t=1\), every cohort’s allocation is fixed each round, so Hoeffding’s inequality immediately gives simultaneous regret at most

\(\bar x\sqrt{\frac H2\log\frac{2\tau}{\delta}}\)

with probability at least \(1-\delta\). Thus consider \(t\geq2\). All remaining quotas satisfy \(0<q_\theta<1\). Put

\(\eta=\min_{\theta\text{ remaining}}\min(q_\theta,1-q_\theta)>0,\qquad \mu_{\min}=\min_\theta\mu_\theta>0.\)

Below, allocation vectors and transfer graphs concern the remaining cohorts unless otherwise stated.

2. A stability lemma for lexicographic prices

Introduce an auxiliary random vector \(U\), independent of \(Z\), whose coordinates lie in \([0,1]\). Its distribution will be specified later. For each remaining cohort define the vector reward

\(r_\theta(z,u)=(z_\theta,\ z_\theta e_\theta,\ u_\theta)\in\mathbb R^{t+2},\)

where \(e_\theta\) denotes its coordinate vector in the remaining cohort order.

Thus summing \(r_\theta a_\theta\) records, in order, welfare, every remaining cohort’s utility, and an auxiliary final objective.

For vector prices \(\lambda_\theta\in\mathbb R^{t+2}\), define \(\pi_\lambda(z,u)\) by sorting the vectors \(r_\theta(z,u)+\lambda_\theta\) in decreasing lexicographic order and filling cohort capacities in that order until the remaining resource is exhausted. Break equal-score ties in the supplied cohort order. This is a measurable policy.

Lemma. Suppose \(\pi=\pi_\lambda\), let \(d'=\mathbb E[\pi]\), and let \(v^*_\theta=\mathbb E[Z_\theta b^*_\theta(Z)]\). Then

\(\left|\mathbb E[Z_\theta\pi_\theta(Z,U)]-v^*_\theta\right|\leq\frac{\bar x(t-1)}2\|d'-d\|_1\)

for every remaining cohort.

Proof. Lift the benchmark to the product space by setting \(b(z,u)=b^*(z)\). It remains lexicographically optimal for welfare and the cohort utilities on this space: integrating any competing policy over \(U\) gives a feasible policy in the original problem.

At each \((z,u)\), transfer mass from coordinates where \(b_\theta>\pi_\theta\) to coordinates where \(\pi_\phi>b_\phi\). Explicitly, put

\(p_\theta=(b_\theta-\pi_\theta)_+,\qquad n_\phi=(\pi_\phi-b_\phi)_+,\qquad T=\sum_\theta p_\theta=\sum_\phi n_\phi.\)

When \(T>0\), use transfers \(h_{\theta\phi}=p_\theta n_\phi/T\); otherwise use zero transfers. Their row and column sums give exactly the change from \(b\) to \(\pi\).

Whenever \(h_{\theta\phi}>0\), we have \(\pi_\theta<\mu_\theta\) and \(\pi_\phi>0\). The greedy rule therefore implies

\(r_\phi+\lambda_\phi-r_\theta-\lambda_\theta\ \geq_{\mathrm{lex}}\ 0.\)

Let \(F_{\theta\phi}=\mathbb E[h_{\theta\phi}]\). For each positive edge define its average reward change

\(g_{\theta\phi}=\frac{\mathbb E[h_{\theta\phi}(r_\phi-r_\theta)]}{F_{\theta\phi}}.\)

Then \(g_{\theta\phi}+\lambda_\phi-\lambda_\theta\) is lexicographically nonnegative. To justify taking expectations here, the first coordinate of a lexicographically nonnegative random vector is nonnegative. If its expectation is zero, it vanishes almost surely; the same argument then applies successively to later coordinates.

Consider any directed cycle of positive edges. Transfer the same expected amount along every edge, using proportional portions of the corresponding \(h_{\theta\phi}\). This gives a feasible modification of \(b\): pointwise it uses a subset of the original transfers, and its expected quota change is zero.

Prices cancel around the cycle. Consequently its total reward change is lexicographically nonnegative. Its projection onto welfare and the cohort utilities is also lexicographically nonnegative. That projection cannot be strictly positive, by the benchmark’s optimality. It must therefore be zero.

We may consequently remove directed cycles from \(F\) without changing the difference in any expected cohort utility. Remove a cycle by subtracting its smallest edge flow and proportionally reducing the corresponding pointwise transfers. Edge-average rewards remain unchanged, so this argument continues to apply.

The remaining graph is acyclic. Its divergence is \(d-d'\). Decompose it into paths from positive-divergence vertices to negative-divergence vertices. The sum of the path amounts is

\(B=\frac12\|d-d'\|_1.\)

Each path has at most \(t-1\) edges, so the remaining total edge flow is at most \((t-1)B\). A unit of transfer contributes at most \(\bar x\) in absolute value to any specified cohort’s utility change. Since removed cycles contributed zero to that utility, the claimed bound follows. \(\square\)

This lemma does not require prices for the unknown benchmark distribution.

3. Computing an empirical price policy without an oracle

Take \(k\) independent observations \((Z_i,U_i)\). Solve the empirical lexicographic allocation problem

\(\operatorname{lexmax}\ \sum_{i=1}^k\sum_\theta r_\theta(Z_i,U_i)a_{i,\theta}\)

subject to

\(0\leq a_{i,\theta}\leq\mu_\theta,\qquad \sum_\theta a_{i,\theta}=\rho',\qquad \sum_{i=1}^k a_{i,\theta}=kd_\theta,\)

where \(\rho'\) is the remaining resource.

This is a capacitated transportation problem: observation nodes have supply \(\rho'\), cohort nodes have demand \(kd_\theta\), and observation-to-cohort arcs have capacity \(\mu_\theta\). The constant allocation \(a_{i,\theta}=d_\theta\) proves feasibility.

It can be solved with \(t+2\) ordinary minimum-cost flow computations. Optimize the objectives successively. After each optimization, obtain dual potentials and fix every arc with strictly nonzero reduced cost at the bound forced by that reduced cost. Leave zero-reduced-cost arcs free within their current bounds. These restrictions describe exactly the optimal face: the objective difference between feasible flows is a sum of reduced-cost terms, each with its prescribed nonnegative sign. Thus the next problem remains a capacitated flow problem.

For these computations one may use [Orlin’s strongly polynomial minimum-cost flow algorithm](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.41.2.338), which uses \(O(E\log V(E+V\log V))\) arithmetic operations on a capacitated network. Here \(V=k+t\) and \(E=kt\). Its arithmetic-operation bound does not depend on the numerical magnitudes of the observed costs.

It remains to extract lexicographic prices. In the residual graph of an empirical optimum, assign vector gain \(r_\theta(Z_i,U_i)\) to a forward observation-to-cohort arc and its negative to a backward arc. There is no lexicographically positive residual cycle, since augmenting such a cycle would improve the empirical objective.

Let \(P_v\) be the greatest lexicographic gain of a path ending at \(v\), allowing a zero-gain start at every vertex. These labels are finite: removing a nonpositive cycle never reduces a path’s gain, so simple paths suffice. Bellman–Ford-style relaxation computes the labels in polynomial time and gives

\(P_v\geq_{\mathrm{lex}}P_w+c_{wv}\)

for every residual arc. Set \(\lambda_\theta=-P_\theta\). The forward and backward residual inequalities show that each empirical allocation maximizes the pointwise lexicographic priced objective.

Thus the empirical optimum agrees with the canonical greedy policy \(\pi_\lambda\) at every observation where all cohort scores are distinct.

Controlling exceptional observations with finite random bits

Choose \(U_{i,\theta}\) independently and uniformly from

\(\{0,1/M,\ldots,(M-1)/M\},\)

where \(M\) is a power of two satisfying

\(M\geq\frac4\delta\bigl((k+1)\tau^2\bigr)^\tau.\)

With probability at least \(1-\delta/4\), every price vector has score ties at at most \(t-1\) distinct training observations.

To prove this, choose \(t\) distinct observations and a tied cohort pair at each. Equality of the final score coordinates requires \(t\) equations of the form

\(U_{i,\theta}-U_{i,\phi}=\lambda_{\phi,\mathrm{last}}-\lambda_{\theta,\mathrm{last}}.\)

Their coefficient matrix in the \(t\) price coordinates has rank at most \(t-1\). A nonzero dependence among its rows therefore imposes a nontrivial linear equation on the random right-hand sides. Conditioning on all but one auxiliary coordinate with nonzero coefficient, this equation holds with probability at most \(1/M\).

There are at most \(((k+1)\tau^2)^\tau\) choices of observations and pairs. The union bound proves the assertion, including for prices chosen from the data.

Consequently, on this event,

\(\left|\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{\pi_{\lambda,\theta}(Z_i,U_i)}{\mu_\theta}-q_\theta\right|\leq\frac{t-1}{k}.\)

4. Uniform estimation of the policy’s expected quotas

The price-policy class has polynomial sample growth, even though prices are unrestricted real vectors.

Let \(D=\tau(\tau+2)\). On any \(N\) observations, the greedy allocations are determined by signs of the affine expressions appearing in all coordinates of all pairwise score comparisons. There are at most \(N\tau^3\) such expressions, in at most \(D\) price parameters.

An arrangement of \(J\) affine hyperplanes in dimension \(D\), including its lower-dimensional faces, has at most

\(\sum_{j=0}^D2^j\binom Jj\leq(1+2J)^D\)

sign patterns. Once these signs are specified, the tie rule fixes the cohort order and hence the allocation. Thus the normalized functions \(f_{\lambda,\theta}=\pi_{\lambda,\theta}/\mu_\theta\), which lie in \([0,1]\), have sample growth at most

\(g(N)=(1+2N\tau^3)^D.\)

For completeness, symmetrization and a ghost sample give, when \(k\varepsilon^2\geq2\),

\(\Pr\{\sup_f|\mathbb Ef-\widehat{\mathbb E}_k f|>\varepsilon\}\leq4g(2k)\exp(-k\varepsilon^2/8).\)

Indeed, symmetrization introduces a factor two and reduces the threshold to \(\varepsilon/2\). Conditional on the paired samples, random swaps give a sum of independent signed differences, each bounded by one. Hoeffding’s inequality and a union bound over the possible evaluation vectors give the displayed bound.

Choose \(\varepsilon\) at least

\(\sqrt{\frac8k\left[D\log(1+8k\tau^3)+\log\frac{16\tau}{\delta}\right]}.\)

Applying the bound to every remaining cohort yields, with probability at least \(1-\delta/4\),

\(\sup_\lambda\left|\mathbb E\frac{\pi_{\lambda,\theta}}{\mu_\theta}-\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{\pi_{\lambda,\theta}(Z_i,U_i)}{\mu_\theta}\right|\leq\varepsilon\)

simultaneously for all remaining cohorts.

Combining this with the preceding exceptional-observation bound, put

\(e=\varepsilon+\frac{\tau-1}{k}.\)

For the learned policy, with probability at least \(1-\delta/2\),

\(|d'_\theta-d_\theta|\leq\mu_\theta e,\qquad \|d'-d\|_1\leq e.\)

The stability lemma then gives

\(\left|\mathbb E[Z_\theta\pi_{\lambda,\theta}]-v^*_\theta\right|\leq\frac{\bar x(\tau-1)e}{2}.\)

These conclusions hold uniformly over the unknown joint distribution.

5. The online algorithm and exact horizon quotas

Take

\(k=\min\{H,\lceil H^{2/3}\rceil\},\)

and choose \(T\) at least

\(\sqrt{\frac H2\log\frac{8\tau(H+1)}{\delta}}.\)

Define

\(A=He+T,\qquad \ell=\min\{H,\lceil A/\eta\rceil\}.\)

The displayed logarithms and square roots are only convenient notation. Choose arithmetic-computable rational upper bounds for \(\varepsilon\) and \(T\), within a factor four of the displayed quantities, and use those values throughout. Powers of two provide such bounds using only arithmetic and comparisons.

If \(k+\ell\geq H\), allocate \(d_\theta\) every round. Otherwise:

  • For the first \(k\) rounds, allocate \(d_\theta\) and collect the training observations.
  • Compute the empirical prices.
  • For the next \(n=H-k-\ell\) rounds, propose allocations from \(\pi_\lambda\), using fresh independent auxiliary randomness.
  • Before accepting each proposal, check that the resulting remaining quota \(B_\theta\), with \(r\) rounds remaining, satisfies \(0\leq B_\theta\leq r\mu_\theta\) for every cohort. If the check fails, fill the entire remaining horizon at the constant vector given by current remaining quotas divided by current remaining rounds.
  • After the \(n\) proposal rounds, fill the final \(\ell\) rounds at the constant remaining-quota vector divided by \(\ell\).

This algorithm is always feasible. Whenever it switches to constant filling, the invariant \(0\leq B_\theta\leq r\mu_\theta\) gives valid per-round capacities, and \(\sum_\theta B_\theta=r\rho'\) gives the required per-round resource. Constant filling exhausts every quota exactly.

We next show that the early switch does not occur on a high-probability event.

Conditional on training, the tentative future allocations are independent. Hoeffding’s inequality, followed by a union bound over cohorts and prefixes, gives probability at least \(1-\delta/4\) that

\(\left|\sum_{j=1}^h\pi_{\lambda,\theta}(Z_{k+j},U_{k+j})-hd'_\theta\right|\leq\mu_\theta T\)

for every relevant prefix \(h\).

On the quota-estimation event, tentative cumulative allocation therefore differs from the corresponding constant-quota allocation by at most \(\mu_\theta A\). At any proposal prefix, the remaining number of rounds satisfies \(r\geq\ell\), and the remaining quota lies between

\(\mu_\theta(rq_\theta-A)\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_\theta(rq_\theta+A).\)

Since \(r\eta\geq A\), this interval lies inside \([0,r\mu_\theta]\). Every proposal therefore passes the feasibility check.

6. Simultaneous regret bound

Conditional on training, Hoeffding’s inequality also bounds each remaining cohort’s tentative utility over the \(n\) proposal rounds:

\(\sum_{j=1}^n Z_{k+j,\theta}\pi_{\lambda,\theta}(Z_{k+j},U_{k+j})\geq n\,\mathbb E[Z_\theta\pi_{\lambda,\theta}]-\mu_\theta\bar x T.\)

For forced cohorts, apply the same inequality to their full-horizon utilities. A union bound gives these utility inequalities with probability at least \(1-\delta/4\).

The exceptional-observation, uniform-estimation, prefix-allocation, and utility events thus hold together with probability at least \(1-\delta\).

On their intersection, there is no early switch. Utilities in exploration and in the final reserve are nonnegative. For each remaining cohort,

\(R_\theta(H)\leq\bar x(k+\ell)+\frac{n}{\mu_\theta}\left(v^*_\theta-\mathbb E[Z_\theta\pi_{\lambda,\theta}]\right)+\bar x T\)

and hence

\(R_\theta(H)\leq\bar x\left[k+\ell+T+\frac{H(\tau-1)e}{2\mu_{\min}}\right].\)

Forced cohorts have regret at most \(\bar xT\). Also, for every feasible algorithm and every sample path, \(R_\theta(H)\leq H\bar x\), because benchmark per-member utility per round is at most \(\bar x\) and realized utility is nonnegative.

A valid simultaneous bound is therefore

\(r(H)=\bar x\min\left\{H,\ k+\ell+T+\frac{H(\tau-1)e}{2\mu_{\min}}\right\}.\)

This also covers the constant-allocation fallback.

For fixed society and fixed confidence,

\(e=O(H^{-1/3}\sqrt{\log H}),\qquad T=O(\sqrt{H\log H}),\qquad \ell=O(H^{2/3}\sqrt{\log H}).\)

Consequently,

\(r(H)=O_{\mathrm{society},\,\delta}(\bar xH^{2/3}\sqrt{\log H})=o(H).\)

All constants and probability estimates are independent of the joint distribution.

7. Running time and conclusion

Training uses \(t+2\) scalar minimum-cost flow solves on networks with \(k+t\) vertices and \(kt\) arcs, followed by polynomial-time vector shortest-path computations. Each online proposal requires sorting \(t\) vectors of length \(t+2\).

Each auxiliary coordinate uses

\(O(\tau\log((k+1)\tau)+\log(1/\delta))\)

random bits. Quota checks, constant filling, and numerical upper-bound calculations require polynomially many arithmetic operations. Small masses or quota slack affect the regret constants but do not cause loops proportional to their reciprocals.

A conservative overall bound, including random-bit generation and reading the rational input, is

\(O\!\left((L+H+\tau+\log(1/\delta))^6\right)\)

in the stipulated exact-real arithmetic model. No optimization oracle is used.

Thus the target guarantee is achievable in polynomial time. The argument uses only independence across rounds; coordinates within a round may have arbitrary dependence.
