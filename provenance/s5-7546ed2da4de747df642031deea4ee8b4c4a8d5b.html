The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 3.3 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given α ∈ (1,2] and ε > 0, can any deterministic anonymous mechanism F map each finite-support rational distribution μ over location types t ∈ [0,1] and prediction ŷ to a facility location y ∈ [0,1], be strategyproof under every finite refinement of μ, and satisfy both improved bounds? Define u_y(t) = 1 - |y - t|, ER(y,μ) = max_{t ∈ supp(μ)} u_y(t) / min_{t ∈ supp(μ)} u_y(t), OPT(μ) = min_{z ∈ [0,1]} ER(z,μ), and mid(μ) = (min supp(μ) + max supp(μ))/2. The bounds are ER(F(μ, mid(μ)), μ) ≤ (α - ε)OPT(μ) and ER(F(μ, ŷ), μ) ≤ (α/(α - 1) - ε)OPT(μ) for every ŷ.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is a probability measure \(\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{\tau}p_k\delta_{t_k}\), with distinct locations \(t_k\in[0,1]\), positive binary rational masses \(p_k\), and \(\sum_k p_k=1\). It is given by its support list; \(\tau\) counts positive-mass types, not possible types.
A deterministic anonymous mechanism \(F(\mu,\hat y)\in[0,1]\) depends only on the measure and an external prediction \(\hat y\in[0,1]\). Utilities are \(u_y(t)=1-|y-t|\).
A finite refinement is any representation \(\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_j\delta_{x_j}\), with \(n\ge2\) and positive rational weights. Strategyproofness under every finite refinement means that, for every such representation, every \(j\), every report \(s\in[0,1]\), and every fixed prediction, \(u_{F(\mu,\hat y)}(x_j)\ge u_{F(\mu-w_j\delta_{x_j}+w_j\delta_s,\hat y)}(x_j)\).
Define \(\mathrm{ER}(y,\mu)=\max_{t\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}u_y(t)/\min_{t\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}u_y(t)\), \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)=\min_{z\in[0,1]}\mathrm{ER}(z,\mu)\), and \(\mathrm{mid}(\mu)=(\min\operatorname{supp}\mu+\max\operatorname{supp}\mu)/2\). A positive numerator over zero gives \(+\infty\); when all utilities are zero, set the ratio to \(1\). Singleton supports are allowed.
For computational claims on rational locations and predictions, state running times in \(\tau\), total binary input length \(L\), and encoded parameters; there is one facility and no candidate-count parameter \(m\).

For fixed \(\alpha\in(1,2]\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), does a deterministic anonymous mechanism, strategyproof under every finite refinement, exist satisfying both \(\mathrm{ER}(F(\mu,\mathrm{mid}(\mu)),\mu)\le(\alpha-\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\) and \(\mathrm{ER}(F(\mu,\hat y),\mu)\le(\alpha/(\alpha-1)-\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\) for every society and prediction, or are these simultaneous improvements impossible?

For finite unweighted agent profiles, deterministic anonymous strategyproof mechanisms attain consistency \(\alpha\) and robustness \(\alpha/(\alpha-1)\), and cannot improve both by any \(\varepsilon>0\). [Verified finite-agent bounds](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11193v2).
No published result addressing exactly the mass model with the refinement requirement above was located.

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to sum to one, as required by a distribution.
  • Defined every finite refinement as an arbitrary splitting into positive rational pieces, each of which can unilaterally move its entire mass through a location misreport.
  • Kept the prediction fixed during deviations and allowed all reports and predictions in [0,1].
  • Allowed singleton supports, assigned envy ratio 1 when all utilities are zero, and assigned infinity when only the denominator is zero.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Answer and finite-agent premise

The simultaneous improvements are impossible. The refinement requirement allows a direct reduction to the two-agent case of the finite-agent lower bound supplied in the question.

We use that bound in the following form: for every \(\alpha\in(1,2]\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), no deterministic anonymous two-agent mechanism, strategyproof for each fixed prediction, satisfies both approximation bounds \(\alpha-\varepsilon\) at the midpoint prediction and \(\alpha/(\alpha-1)-\varepsilon\) at every prediction. This is the two-agent specialization of [Theorem 3.3 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11193v2#Thmtheorem3.3). Its obstruction uses distinct agent locations, so the convention for all-zero utilities on singleton supports does not affect its application.

Reduction from societies to two agents

Suppose, for contradiction, that a mechanism \(F\) satisfying all the requested properties exists. Define
\[ \mu_{a,b}=\tfrac12\delta_a+\tfrac12\delta_b, \qquad f(a,b,\hat y)=F(\mu_{a,b},\hat y) \]
for \(a,b,\hat y\in[0,1]\).

Every \(\mu_{a,b}\) is an admissible society. If \(a=b\), its support-list representation is simply \(\delta_a\); otherwise its two positive masses are both \(1/2\). Thus this reduction uses only binary rational masses and societies with \(\tau\leq2\).

The mechanism \(f\) is deterministic because \(F\) is deterministic. It is anonymous because \(\mu_{a,b}=\mu_{b,a}\).

To check strategyproofness, fix \(a,b,\hat y\) and a possible report \(s\). The expression
\[ \mu_{a,b}=\tfrac12\delta_a+\tfrac12\delta_b \]
is a permitted finite refinement, including when \(a=b\): refinements need not have distinct locations. Applying refinement strategyproofness to its first component gives
\[ u_{F(\mu_{a,b},\hat y)}(a) \geq u_{F(\mu_{a,b}-\tfrac12\delta_a+\tfrac12\delta_s,\hat y)}(a) = u_{F(\mu_{s,b},\hat y)}(a). \]
Equivalently,
\[ u_{f(a,b,\hat y)}(a)\geq u_{f(s,b,\hat y)}(a). \]
Applying the same requirement to the second component proves the corresponding inequality for the agent at \(b\). Hence \(f\) is strategyproof for every fixed prediction.

Transfer of the guarantees and contradiction

For every facility location \(y\), the two-agent profile \((a,b)\) and the society \(\mu_{a,b}\) have exactly the same maximum and minimum utilities. Consequently, their envy ratios agree pointwise. Duplicate locations do not change either extremum.

Their midpoint predictions also agree:
\[ \mathrm{mid}(\mu_{a,b})=\frac{a+b}{2}. \]
At this location, both agent utilities equal
\[ 1-\frac{|a-b|}{2}\geq\frac12, \]
so the envy ratio is \(1\). Since every defined envy ratio is at least \(1\), it follows that
\[ \mathrm{OPT}(\mu_{a,b})=1. \]

The assumed guarantees for \(F\), restricted to these societies, therefore imply
\[ \mathrm{ER}\!\left(f\!\left(a,b,\frac{a+b}{2}\right),(a,b)\right) \leq\alpha-\varepsilon \]
and, for every prediction,
\[ \mathrm{ER}(f(a,b,\hat y),(a,b)) \leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}-\varepsilon. \]
Thus \(f\) satisfies exactly the two simultaneous improvements prohibited by the supplied finite-agent lower bound. All its hypotheses—determinism, anonymity, strategyproofness with prediction fixed, and the two performance guarantees—have been verified. This is a contradiction.

For completeness, when \(\alpha-\varepsilon<1\), a singleton society already contradicts the consistency inequality because its envy ratio and optimum are both \(1\). The reduction above settles the remaining parameter range.

The argument is purely a restriction to societies with \(\tau\leq2\); it requires no expansion of binary-encoded masses into a population and makes no computational assumption.
