The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given employers \(E\), complete applicant types \(u\in\mathcal U\) of capacity \(q_u=1\), rational masses \(\mu_u\), affiliate map \(\alpha(u)\), binary applicant and employer approval data, affiliate-specific approval vectors, parameter λ∈[0,1], and rational employer capacities \(\kappa_e\), find a rational assignment \(x_{u,e}≥0\) for \(e∈E∪\{\gamma\}\) satisfying ∑_{e∈E∪\{\gamma\}}x_{u,e}=μ_u and ∑_u x_{u,e}≤κ_e, with no type-level blocking tuple admitting any δ>0 mass deviation satisfying the paper's six strict preference tests.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) complete applicant types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_u\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Masses need not sum to \(1\). There are \(m\geq1\) employers, nonnegative rational capacities \(\kappa_e\), and a common rational \(\lambda\in[0,1]\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type has individual capacity \(1\), exactly one affiliate employer \(\alpha(u)\), applicant approvals \(p_u(e)\), employer approvals \(d_e(u)\), and its affiliate employer’s placement approvals \(b_u(e)\), all binary. Types specify all these data; equal utilities give indifference, without tie-breaking.
An assignment has rational \(x_{u,e}\geq0\) for \(e\in E\cup\{\gamma\}\), with \(\sum_e x_{u,e}=\mu_u\) and \(\sum_u x_{u,e}\leq\kappa_e\) for real employers. Here \(\gamma\) means unmatched mass, has no capacity constraint, and has zero approval value.
Employer utility is \(V_f(x)=\sum_u d_f(u)x_{u,f}+\lambda\sum_{u:\alpha(u)=f}\sum_{g\in E}b_u(g)x_{u,g}\). Define \(h_f(u,g)=\mathbf1_{g=f}d_f(u)+\lambda\mathbf1_{\alpha(u)=f}b_u(g)\), with \(h_f(\gamma,g)=h_f(u,\gamma)=0\).
A deviation selects a tuple \((a,a',a'',e,e',e'')\) and mass \(\delta>0\). Real applicant roles denote homogeneous cohorts of mass \(\delta\), with type \(t(a)\); distinct cohorts may have the same type. They are disjoint except for the explicitly permitted reuse \(a''=a'\).
Initially \(a\) is assigned to \(e'\neq e\), possibly \(\gamma\). Either \(a'\) is assigned to \(e\), or \(a'=\gamma\) and \(e\) has at least \(\delta\) unused capacity. Either \(a''\) is initially unmatched, is \(\gamma\), or reuses \(a'\); either \(e''\) has at least \(\delta\) initially unused capacity, is \(\gamma\), or reuses \(e'\).
Require \(e'=\gamma\Rightarrow a''=\gamma\), \(a'=\gamma\Rightarrow e''=\gamma\), and \(a''=a'\neq\gamma\iff e''=e'\neq\gamma\). A real \(e''\) cannot equal \(e\). Move \(a\) from \(e'\) to \(e\), \(a'\) from \(e\) to \(e''\), and \(a''\) from unmatched status to \(e'\); in the reuse case move \(a'\) only once, from \(e\) to \(e'\). Missing roles make no move; all cohort supplies and final capacities must be feasible. Denote the resulting assignment by \(y\).
The six strict tests are \(p_{t(a)}(e)>p_{t(a)}(e')\), \(V_e(y)>V_e(x)\), and, whenever the indicated role is real: \(p_{t(a')}(e'')>0\), \(h_{e'}(t(a''),e')>0\), \(p_{t(a'')}(e')>0\), \(h_{e''}(t(a'),e'')>0\), respectively for \(a',e',a'',e''\). Interpret missing applicant arguments and unmatched destinations as zero. An assignment is stable if no such deviation satisfies all six tests.

Result

The search problem is polynomial-time solvable. A rational stable assignment always exists. In fact, the algorithm below produces one assignment that is stable simultaneously for every \(\lambda\in[0,1]\).

The algorithm uses reservation quotas on type masses. This is motivated by the reservation approach in [the cited finite-applicant paper](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/0051.pdf), but the correctness proof below works directly in the stated mass model and does not expand masses into individual applicants.

1. Definitions for the construction

Write \(U\) for the supported types. Extend applicant approvals by \(p_u(\gamma)=0\).

We use four disjoint classes of type-employer edges:

  • \(G_0\): \((u,e)\) with \(\alpha(u)=e\) and \(p_u(e)=d_e(u)=b_u(e)=1\).
  • \(G_1\): \((u,e)\) with \(\alpha(u)\ne e\) and \(p_u(e)=d_e(u)=1\).
  • \(G_2\): \((u,e)\) with \(\alpha(u)=e\), \(p_u(e)=d_e(u)=1\), and \(b_u(e)=0\).
  • \(G_3\): \((u,e)\) with \(\alpha(u)=e\), \(p_u(e)=b_u(e)=1\), and \(d_e(u)=0\).

For each employer \(f\), define its \(G_0\) group and its total mass by

\(S_f=\{u\in U:(u,f)\in G_0\},\qquad W_f=\sum_{u\in S_f}\mu_u.\)

The sets \(S_f\) are pairwise disjoint, because each type has exactly one affiliate employer.

Reserve the amount

\(r_f=\min\{\kappa_f,W_f\}\)

of employer \(f\)'s capacity for its own \(G_0\) group. During the first stage, also reserve that much of \(S_f\)'s mass by imposing the export quota \(W_f-r_f\).

All assignments constructed below use only edges on which the applicant approval is \(1\).

2. The algorithm

Initially set all real-employer assignments to zero, and let \(R_u=\mu_u\) be the remaining mass of type \(u\).

Stage 1: Assign on \(G_1\), subject to reservations

Initialize receiving budgets and export quotas as

\(B_f=\kappa_f-r_f,\qquad Q_f=W_f-r_f.\)

Process every \(G_1\) edge once, in any fixed order.

For an edge \((u,e)\), assign the amount

\(z=\min\{R_u,B_e,Q_f\}\quad\text{if }u\in S_f,\)

and assign

\(z=\min\{R_u,B_e\}\quad\text{if }u\notin\bigcup_f S_f.\)

Increase \(x_{u,e}\) by \(z\), decrease \(R_u\) and \(B_e\) by \(z\), and, when \(u\in S_f\), decrease \(Q_f\) by \(z\).

The resulting assignment is maximal subject to these budgets: for every \(G_1\) edge, at least one applicable resource is zero at the end of Stage 1. Indeed, processing the edge exhausts at least one resource, and all resources subsequently only decrease.

Stage 2: Fill the \(G_0\) reservations

For each employer \(f\), assign exactly \(r_f\) of the remaining mass in \(S_f\) to \(f\), using any order of its types.

This is feasible. If Stage 1 exported total mass \(Z_f\) from \(S_f\), its export quota gives

\(Z_f\le W_f-r_f.\)

Thus at least \(r_f\) of that group's mass remains. Meanwhile, Stage 1 assigned at most \(\kappa_f-r_f\) to employer \(f\), leaving capacity at least \(r_f\).

Stage 3: Assign on \(G_2\)

Process every \(G_2\) edge once. Assign the minimum of the type's remaining mass and the employer's remaining capacity.

Stage 4: Assign on \(G_3\)

Process every \(G_3\) edge once, using the same rule.

Finally, set \(x_{u,\gamma}=R_u\).

Every stage respects supplies and employer capacities, so the output is feasible.

3. Two consequences of the reservations

In this section, \(B_f\) and \(Q_f\) denote their values at the end of Stage 1, and \(R_u^{(1)}\) denotes type \(u\)'s remaining mass at that time.

Lemma 1: Unmatched group mass means a slack export quota

If some type in \(S_f\) has positive unmatched mass in the final assignment, then \(Q_f>0\).

Proof. Types in \(S_f\) can be assigned by this algorithm only on \(G_1\) and \(G_0\): their own-employer edge belongs to neither \(G_2\) nor \(G_3\).

Stage 1 exports \(Z_f\) from the group, and Stage 2 assigns exactly \(r_f\) to \(f\). Consequently, the group's final unmatched mass is

\(W_f-Z_f-r_f=Q_f.\)

Positive unmatched mass therefore implies \(Q_f>0\). \(\square\)

Lemma 2: A finally vacant employer had a slack receiving budget

If employer \(g\) has positive unused capacity in the final assignment, then \(B_g>0\).

Proof. Let \(I_g\) be the mass assigned to \(g\) in Stage 1. Then

\(B_g=\kappa_g-r_g-I_g.\)

Stage 2 assigns exactly \(r_g\) to \(g\). If later stages assign total mass \(T_g\), the final unused capacity is

\(\kappa_g-I_g-r_g-T_g=B_g-T_g.\)

Its positivity implies \(B_g>0\). \(\square\)

We will use the following two consequences of Stage 1 maximality.

Consequence A. Suppose \(u\) is finally unmatched and \((u,e)\in G_1\). Then \(B_e=0\).

Indeed, \(R_u^{(1)}>0\). If \(u\in S_f\), Lemma 1 also gives \(Q_f>0\). Thus the receiving budget is the only applicable resource that can be zero on this edge.

It follows that employer \(e\) is filled completely by Stages 1 and 2, because

\(I_e+r_e=\kappa_e.\)

Therefore every final incumbent at \(e\) is on \(G_0\) or \(G_1\).

Consequence B. Suppose type \(v\) receives positive mass at its own affiliate employer \(f=\alpha(v)\). If either \(v\notin\bigcup_j S_j\), or \(Q_f>0\), then no finally vacant employer \(g\ne f\) satisfies \(p_v(g)=d_g(v)=1\).

To prove this, suppose such \(g\) existed. The edge \((v,g)\) belongs to \(G_1\). Positive assignment of \(v\) at its own employer, which occurs after Stage 1, implies \(R_v^{(1)}>0\). Lemma 2 gives \(B_g>0\). The group quota is either inapplicable or positive by hypothesis. Hence all resources on \((v,g)\) would be positive at the end of Stage 1, contradicting maximality.

4. Reduction of possible deviations

Every real-employer assignment produced by the algorithm satisfies \(p_u(e)=1\).

The first strict test of a deviation is

\(p_{t(a)}(e)>p_{t(a)}(e').\)

Because approvals are binary, the entering cohort must approve \(e\) and have approval \(0\) for its initial destination. In our assignment, this forces \(e'=\gamma\): the entering cohort is initially unmatched.

The requirement \(e'=\gamma\Rightarrow a''=\gamma\) eliminates the third applicant role. The permitted reuse case is also impossible because it requires \(e'\ne\gamma\).

Thus every potential blocking deviation has one of two forms:

  • An unmatched cohort enters an employer with initially unused capacity.
  • An unmatched cohort enters employer \(e\), while an incumbent cohort moves from \(e\) to a distinct employer \(g\) with initially unused capacity.

For the second form, the incumbent's strict approval test rules out \(g=\gamma\). Its destination tests require

\(p_v(g)=1,\qquad h_g(v,g)>0,\)

where \(v\) is the incumbent's type.

Let \(u\) be the entering type and define its contribution at the receiving employer by

\(H=h_e(u,e)=d_e(u)+\lambda\mathbf 1_{\alpha(u)=e}b_u(e).\)

For an insertion without an incumbent, the utility gain per unit mass is \(H\).

For an insertion that relocates an incumbent of type \(v\) to \(g\), the utility gain per unit mass is

\(\Delta=H-h_e(v,e)+\lambda\mathbf 1_{\alpha(v)=e}b_v(g).\)

This follows directly from the utility formula: the unmatched entrant adds \(H\), the incumbent loses its contribution at \(e\), and the incumbent's new placement contributes to \(e\)'s affiliate utility precisely when \(\alpha(v)=e\).

We now show that no allowed deviation has positive gain.

5. Proof of stability

Fix an initially unmatched entering cohort of type \(u\), and an employer \(e\) with \(p_u(e)=1\).

Case 1: The edge belongs to none of \(G_0,G_1,G_2,G_3\)

If \(u\) is not an affiliate of \(e\), this means \(d_e(u)=0\). If it is an affiliate, this means \(d_e(u)=b_u(e)=0\). In either situation, \(H=0\), so an insertion alone cannot strictly improve employer utility.

For a relocation, every possible incumbent lies on one of the four edge classes:

  • A \(G_1\) incumbent has \(h_e(v,e)=1\) and \(\alpha(v)\ne e\), giving \(\Delta=-1\).
  • A \(G_0\) incumbent gives \(\Delta=-1-\lambda+\lambda b_v(g)\le -1\).
  • A \(G_2\) incumbent gives \(\Delta=-1+\lambda b_v(g)\le0\), because \(\lambda\le1\).
  • A \(G_3\) incumbent gives \(\Delta=-\lambda+\lambda b_v(g)\le0\).

There is no strict utility increase.

Case 2: \((u,e)\in G_1\)

Here \(H=1\). Consequence A says that \(e\) is full and has only \(G_0\) and \(G_1\) incumbents. Hence an insertion without relocation is infeasible.

For a \(G_1\) incumbent, \(\Delta=1-1=0\).

For a \(G_0\) incumbent,

\(\Delta=1-(1+\lambda)+\lambda b_v(g)=\lambda(b_v(g)-1)\le0.\)

Neither relocation gives a strict gain.

Case 3: \((u,e)\in G_0\)

Because a member of \(S_e\) is finally unmatched, Lemma 1 gives \(Q_e>0\).

Also, \(r_e<W_e\). Otherwise \(r_e=W_e\), the export quota would be zero, and Stage 2 would assign the entire group. Since \(r_e=\min\{\kappa_e,W_e\}\), it follows that

\(r_e=\kappa_e.\)

Thus employer \(e\) is filled entirely by \(G_0\) assignments, and an insertion alone is infeasible.

Every incumbent \(v\) at \(e\) satisfies \(v\in S_e\) and \(\alpha(v)=e\). A valid relocation destination \(g\ne e\) must satisfy

\(h_g(v,g)=d_g(v)>0.\)

Together with the applicant test, this gives \(p_v(g)=d_g(v)=1\). But \(Q_e>0\), so Consequence B rules out any such finally vacant destination. Therefore no allowed relocation exists.

Case 4: \((u,e)\in G_2\)

Here \(H=1\).

When this edge was processed in Stage 3, it assigned as much mass as supply and capacity allowed. Since \(u\) remains unmatched at the end, its mass was not exhausted then. Employer \(e\)'s capacity must therefore have been exhausted.

Consequently, \(e\) is full, and its incumbents belong only to \(G_0,G_1,G_2\).

A \(G_1\) incumbent gives \(\Delta=0\), while a \(G_0\) incumbent gives

\(\Delta=1-(1+\lambda)+\lambda b_v(g)\le0.\)

A \(G_2\) incumbent has \(\alpha(v)=e\) and \(v\notin\bigcup_f S_f\). Any valid distinct relocation destination must satisfy \(p_v(g)=d_g(v)=1\), as in Case 3. Consequence B rules it out.

Thus no blocking deviation exists in this case.

Case 5: \((u,e)\in G_3\)

Here \(H=\lambda\). The same greedy argument, now applied to Stage 4, shows that \(e\) is full.

For a \(G_0\) incumbent,

\(\Delta=\lambda-(1+\lambda)+\lambda b_v(g)=-1+\lambda b_v(g)\le0.\)

For a \(G_1\) incumbent,

\(\Delta=\lambda-1\le0.\)

A \(G_2\) or \(G_3\) incumbent is an affiliate of \(e\) outside all \(S_f\). Consequence B again rules out every valid distinct vacant relocation destination.

This case includes \(\lambda=0\); none of the arguments requires \(\lambda>0\).

These five cases exhaust all destinations approved by an unmatched entrant. Every potential blocking deviation either is infeasible, lacks a permitted relocation destination, or fails the strict employer utility test. The output is therefore stable. \(\square\)

6. Rationality and running time

The algorithm performs \(O(\tau m)\) arithmetic operations: each \(G_1\) edge is processed once, and each subsequent stage processes at most one own-employer edge per type.

To bound arithmetic bit lengths, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses and capacities. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Every amount computed by the algorithm is an integer multiple of \(1/D\), because the algorithm uses only sums, differences, and minima of such numbers.

All these quantities are nonnegative and bounded by input capacities or the total input mass. Their numerators therefore have bit length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Exact arithmetic and the rational output consequently require polynomial bit time and space.

The construction never uses \(\lambda\). The proof applies to every \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), so the same output is stable throughout that interval. There are no instances requiring a report of nonexistence.
