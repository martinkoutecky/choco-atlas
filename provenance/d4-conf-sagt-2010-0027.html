The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 3.4 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of complete valuation types \(T\) with masses \(\mu_t\), finite indivisible good classes \(G\), per-capita supplies \(s_g\), and additive values \(v_t(g)\in\{1,\ldots,M\}\), let \(\pi_r\) be a lottery over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\) for report \(r\in T\). Maximize \(\alpha\), or decide whether \(\alpha\ge\alpha_0\), subject to \(\sum_{r\in T}\mu_r\sum_b\pi_r(b)b_g=s_g\), \(\sum_b\pi_t(b)v_t(b)\ge\sum_b\pi_r(b)v_t(b)\) for all \(t,r\), and \(v_t(b)\ge\alpha V_t\) whenever \(\pi_r(b)>0\), where \(V_t=\sum_{g\in G}s_gv_t(g)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) complete valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|G|\ge1\) indivisible good classes, binary-rational per-capita supplies \(s_g\ge0\) with \(\sum_gs_g>0\), and additive values \(v_t(g)\in\{1,\ldots,M\}\), where \(M\ge1\) is an input integer. Write \(v_t(b)=\sum_gb_gv_t(g)\) and \(V_t=\sum_gs_gv_t(g)\).
For each allowed report \(r\in T\), choose a probability distribution \(\pi_r\) on \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\) with finite first moments; bundles have no additional size cap. Require exact aggregate feasibility \(\sum_r\mu_r\sum_b\pi_r(b)b_g=s_g\) for every \(g\).
Truthfulness in expectation means \(\sum_b\pi_t(b)v_t(b)\ge\sum_b\pi_r(b)v_t(b)\) for all \(t,r\in T\). The guaranteed fairness factor \(\alpha\) requires \(v_t(b)\ge\alpha V_t\) for every \(t,r\in T\) and every \(b\) with \(\pi_r(b)>0\).
Given a binary-rational threshold \(\alpha_0\in[0,1]\), is deciding whether such lotteries exist with \(\alpha\ge\alpha_0\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and \(M\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length.
For discrete societies, an efficient truthful randomized allocation guarantees every recipient at least \((1-\epsilon)/k\) of every truthful agent’s total value, for \(k\) agents and \(n\) goods when \(0<\epsilon<1\) and \(MkM^k/n<\epsilon\). [Mossel–Tamuz](https://www.tamuz.caltech.edu/papers/1003.5480.pdf)
For finitely many agents and non-atomic resources, truthful randomized equal division exists; this concerns divisible resources, rather than the mass society with integral bundles above. Neither verified result classifies the decision problem above. [Mossel–Tamuz](https://www.tamuz.caltech.edu/papers/1003.5480.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one, so the supplied quantities are per-capita supplies and V_t is the corresponding per-capita benchmark.
  • Specified nonempty support and good classes, nonnegative binary-rational supplies with positive total supply, and binary encoding of M and the threshold.
  • Restricted allowed reports to T, as in the graded constraints; complete valuation means the entire additive valuation vector is specified.
  • Made the fairness quantifiers explicit: every supported bundle under every report must meet every supported type’s value guarantee, retaining the anchor’s guarantee for all recipients.
  • Required each lottery to be a probability distribution with finite first moments, without imposing finite support, rational probabilities, or an additional bundle cap.
  • Used the decision formulation with threshold in [0,1].
  • Retained the finite-agent large-goods condition in the discrete benchmark; did not substitute support size for the number of agents in that condition, since a mass society has no finite agent count.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even when

\(\displaystyle M=2,\qquad \alpha_0=1,\qquad s_g\in\left\{\frac14,\frac12\right\},\)

and every type mass is a positive dyadic rational.

The proof first eliminates the truthfulness constraints, then encodes graph coloring through the integral bundles. A finite-certificate argument establishes membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

2. Pooling eliminates the truthfulness constraints

Because every \(V_t\) is positive, existence with some \(\alpha\ge\alpha_0\) is equivalent to existence at \(\alpha=\alpha_0\).

Define

\(\displaystyle h_t=\left\lceil\alpha_0V_t\right\rceil,\qquad B=\left\{b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}:v_t(b)\ge h_t\text{ for every }t\in T\right\}.\)

The rounding is exact because every bundle value is integral.

Lemma. The instance is feasible if and only if there is one probability distribution \(p\) on \(B\), with finite first moments, whose mean is \(s\).

Proof. Given feasible report lotteries, define their pooled distribution by

\(\displaystyle p(b)=\sum_{r\in T}\mu_r\pi_r(b).\)

It is a probability distribution, its support lies in \(B\), and aggregate feasibility gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_b p(b)b_g=s_g\qquad\text{for every }g.\)

Its first moments are finite because there are finitely many report lotteries with finite first moments.

Conversely, given such a distribution \(p\), set \(\pi_r=p\) for every report \(r\). Aggregate feasibility holds because the masses sum to one. Fairness holds because \(p\) is supported on \(B\). For every \(t,r\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_b\pi_t(b)v_t(b)=\sum_b\pi_r(b)v_t(b),\)

so every truthfulness inequality holds with equality. \(\square\)

Thus the positive masses affect neither the existence criterion nor the hardness mechanism.

3. At threshold one, fairness inequalities become equalities

Suppose \(\alpha_0=1\), and let \(p\) be the pooled distribution. For every type \(t\),

\(\displaystyle v_t(b)\ge V_t\quad\text{on the support of }p,\qquad \mathbb E_p[v_t(b)]=v_t(s)=V_t.\)

Consequently,

\[\displaystyle v_t(b)=V_t\qquad\text{for every }t\text{ and every }b\text{ with }p(b)>0.\tag{1}\]

Indeed, the nonnegative random variable \(v_t(b)-V_t\) has expectation zero. Any positive-probability bundle with strictly positive excess would contradict that expectation.

4. Hardness with values one and two

We reduce from proper graph four-colorability. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard: graph three-colorability is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, and adjoining one vertex adjacent to every original vertex transforms three-colorability into four-colorability. A four-coloring of the enlarged graph leaves at most three colors available to the original graph; conversely, a three-coloring extends by assigning the new vertex a fourth color. The starting hardness theorem is established in [Garey–Johnson–Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(76)90059-1).

Let \(H=(U,E)\) be a finite simple graph, with \(n=|U|\ge1\) and \(e=|E|\). Use colors indexed by \(\{0,1,2,3\}\).

Goods and supplies. Introduce goods

\(\displaystyle x_{u,c}\quad(u\in U,\ c\in\{0,1,2,3\}),\)

and slack goods

\(\displaystyle z_{a,c}\quad(a\in E,\ c\in\{0,1,2,3\}).\)

Set

\(\displaystyle s_{x_{u,c}}=\frac14,\qquad s_{z_{a,c}}=\frac12.\)

There are \(m=4n+4e\) goods, and their total supply is

\(\displaystyle S=\sum_gs_g=n+2e.\)

Valuation types. Every unspecified value below is one.

  • A baseline type \(t_0\) values every good at one.
  • For each vertex \(u\), a type \(t_u\) assigns value two to its four goods \(x_{u,c}\).
  • For each edge \(a=\{u,v\}\) and color \(c\), a type \(t_{a,c}\) assigns value two to exactly \(x_{u,c},x_{v,c},z_{a,c}\).

These types are distinct, complete, and have values in \(\{1,2\}\). Their number is

\(\displaystyle \tau=1+n+4e.\)

Their total supply values are

\[\displaystyle V_{t_0}=S,\qquad V_{t_u}=S+1,\qquad V_{t_{a,c}}=S+1.\tag{2}\]

For the vertex types, the extra value is \(4\cdot\frac14=1\). For the edge-color types, it is \(\frac14+\frac14+\frac12=1\).

Choose positive dyadic masses as follows. Let \(q=\lceil\log_2\tau\rceil\), give each nonbaseline type mass \(2^{-q}\), and give the baseline type mass

\(\displaystyle \mu_{t_0}=1-(\tau-1)2^{-q}.\)

The baseline mass is positive because \(2^q\ge\tau\). Finally, set \(\alpha_0=1\).

Feasibility implies a proper coloring. Suppose the constructed instance is feasible. Take any positive-probability bundle \(b\) from its pooled distribution.

Write \(|b|=\sum_gb_g\). By (1) and the baseline equality,

\(\displaystyle |b|=S.\)

Subtracting this equality from each vertex-type equality in (2) gives

\[\displaystyle \sum_{c=0}^{3}b_{x_{u,c}}=1\qquad\text{for every }u.\tag{3}\]

The coordinates are nonnegative integers. Therefore exactly one of the four coordinates in (3) is one, and the others are zero. Assign that color to \(u\).

Likewise, subtracting the baseline equality from each edge-color equality gives

\[\displaystyle b_{x_{u,c}}+b_{x_{v,c}}+b_{z_{a,c}}=1.\tag{4}\]

The endpoints of an edge cannot both receive color \(c\), because then the left side of (4) would be at least two. Thus the extracted coloring is proper.

A proper coloring implies feasibility. Conversely, suppose \(\kappa:U\to\{0,1,2,3\}\) is a proper coloring.

For each \(j\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), shift every color by \(j\) modulo four. Define an integral bundle \(b^{(j)}\) by putting one unit in \(x_{u,c}\) precisely when \(u\)'s shifted color is \(c\), and setting, for each edge \(a=\{u,v\}\),

\(\displaystyle b^{(j)}_{z_{a,c}}=1-b^{(j)}_{x_{u,c}}-b^{(j)}_{x_{v,c}}.\)

Properness ensures that these slack coordinates belong to \(\{0,1\}\). Each vertex contributes one unit, and each edge contributes two slack units. Hence

\(\displaystyle |b^{(j)}|=n+2e=S.\)

Equations (3) and (4) hold, so every bundle has exactly the total supply value \(V_t\) under every type \(t\). Every bundle therefore satisfies fairness at threshold one.

Choose the four bundles with probability \(\frac14\) each. Every vertex visits every color once, giving

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[b_{x_{u,c}}]=\frac14.\)

For an edge, its two differently colored endpoints occupy any specified color in exactly two of the four shifts. Thus

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[b_{z_{a,c}}]=\frac12.\)

The mean is exactly \(s\). The pooling lemma supplies truthful report lotteries.

This proves the required equivalence.

Reduction size. The construction has

\(\displaystyle m=4(n+e),\qquad \tau=1+n+4e,\qquad M=2.\)

Its explicit valuation matrix and numerical data have binary length \(O((n+e)^2)\), and can be constructed in that many bit operations. The reduction is therefore polynomial.

5. Finite certificates and membership in NP

For a general instance, define

\(\displaystyle H_*=\max_{t\in T}h_t,\qquad C=\left\{c\in B:\sum_gc_g\le H_*\right\}.\)

Every fair bundle \(b\) contains a fair bundle \(c\le b\) belonging to \(C\). To prove this, choose a coordinatewise minimal fair sub-bundle of \(b\). Such a choice exists because \(b\) has finitely many sub-bundles.

If its total size exceeded \(H_*\), removing any one present unit would leave a bundle \(c'\) with

\(\displaystyle v_t(c')\ge\sum_gc'_g\ge H_*\ge h_t\qquad\text{for every }t,\)

using the assumption that every individual value is at least one. This contradicts minimality.

It follows that the instance is feasible exactly when there exist numbers \(\lambda_c\) satisfying

\[\displaystyle \lambda_c\ge0,\qquad \sum_{c\in C}\lambda_c=1,\qquad \sum_{c\in C}\lambda_cc_g\le s_g\quad\text{for every }g.\tag{5}\]

For necessity, replace each bundle in a feasible pooled lottery by a fixed fair sub-bundle in \(C\). This gives a distribution on the finite set \(C\), whose mean is coordinatewise at most \(s\).

For sufficiency, let

\(\displaystyle d=s-\sum_{c\in C}\lambda_cc\ge0.\)

Choose \(c\) with probabilities \(\lambda_c\). Independently choose a nonnegative integral random vector \(D\) with mean \(d\): each coordinate is its floor plus a Bernoulli variable whose mean is its fractional part. Then \(c+D\) is fair, has mean \(s\), and has finite support. Apply the pooling lemma.

It remains to bound the certificate length. The feasible region in (5) is a compact rational polytope. At an extreme point, at most \(m+1\) variables \(\lambda_c\) are positive. Indeed, if more were positive, their augmented columns \((c,1)\in\mathbb R^{m+1}\) would be linearly dependent. A sufficiently small perturbation in both directions along that dependence would preserve normalization, every coordinate mean, and nonnegativity, contradicting extremality.

For these at most \(m+1\) selected bundles, the corresponding rational feasible polytope has an extreme point. Its defining coefficients have absolute value at most \(H_*+1\). Clearing the supply denominators and applying Cramer's rule bounds each probability's encoding length by

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(L+m\log\bigl((m+1)(H_*+1)\bigr)\right).\)

Also,

\(\displaystyle H_*\le\left\lceil M\sum_gs_g\right\rceil,\)

so \(\log(H_*+1)=O(L+\log(m+1))\). The selected bundles and probabilities consequently have polynomial total encoding length.

A verifier checks their integrality, fairness, normalization, and the inequalities (5) using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), and completes the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness proof.

6. Running times and easy boundary cases

A deterministic algorithm enumerates \(C\) and solves the rational linear feasibility problem (5). Since

\(\displaystyle |C|\le(H_*+1)^m,\)

polynomial-time rational linear programming gives the bit running-time bound

\(\displaystyle (H_*+1)^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(M+1)).\)

Using \(H_*\le\lceil Mm2^L\rceil\), a bound expressed entirely in the requested parameters is

\(\displaystyle 2^{O(m(L+\log(M+1)+\log(m+1)))}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(M+1)).\)

In particular, this is \(2^{O(mL)}\operatorname{poly}(L)\) under the explicit input encoding. The certificate verification runs in polynomial time.

For \(M=1\), all bundle values equal their total size. Writing \(S=\sum_gs_g\), feasibility is equivalent to

\(\displaystyle S\ge\lceil\alpha_0S\rceil.\)

Necessity follows by taking expectations. For sufficiency, with \(h=\lceil\alpha_0S\rceil>0\), the vector \((h/S)s\le s\) lies in the convex hull of the fair bundles \(h e_g\); apply (5). The case \(h=0\) is immediate. This criterion is computable in polynomial time.

For \(m=1\), every positive valuation coefficient cancels from the fairness inequality. Feasibility is likewise equivalent to

\(\displaystyle s_g\ge\lceil\alpha_0s_g\rceil,\)

and is polynomial-time decidable for arbitrary \(M\).

The hardness result therefore already begins at \(M=2\), with unbounded \(m\) and \(\tau\). Unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\), no algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,M\) solves the general problem.
