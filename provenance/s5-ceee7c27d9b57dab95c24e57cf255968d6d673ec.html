The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given integers \(s,\Delta s>0\), a finite type graph \(H=(T,F)\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and an initial count \(\alpha_t\in\{0,\ldots,s\}\) of A-supporters per district type \(t\), treat each type as an atomless mass of interchangeable districts, with every pair of districts in compatible types adjacent. Each dissolved district must transfer all \(s\) voters to compatible surviving districts; each survivor must receive exactly \(\Delta s\) voters, with integral transfers for each district but fractional type-level averages allowed. What is the maximum mass of surviving districts that can have a strict A-majority, and is that mass at least a given rational threshold \(\rho\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Input also consists of a simple undirected compatibility graph \(H=(T,F)\), \(m=|F|\), binary positive integers \(s,\Delta s\), integers \(\alpha_t\in\{0,\ldots,s\}\), and a binary rational threshold \(\rho\in[0,1]\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Type \(t\) represents an atomless mass of districts, each initially containing \(\alpha_t\) A-voters and \(s-\alpha_t\) B-voters. Districts are adjacent exactly when their distinct types are adjacent in \(H\).
A redistribution splits each type's mass arbitrarily into dissolved and surviving districts and into integer transfer patterns. A dissolved district of type \(t\) has nonnegative integer outgoing counts \((a_u,b_u)_{u\in N_H(t)}\) satisfying \(\sum_u a_u=\alpha_t\) and \(\sum_u b_u=s-\alpha_t\).
A surviving district of type \(u\) retains its original voters and has nonnegative integer incoming counts \((c_t,d_t)_{t\in N_H(u)}\) satisfying \(\sum_t(c_t+d_t)=\Delta s\). For each ordered compatible pair \((t,u)\), the mass-weighted outgoing A-count equals the mass-weighted incoming A-count, and likewise for B. These matching conditions define fractional type averages while preserving integral district patterns.
Consequently the dissolved mass is \(\Delta s/(s+\Delta s)\), and the surviving mass is \(s/(s+\Delta s)\). A surviving type-\(u\) district wins exactly when \(\alpha_u+\sum_t c_t>(s+\Delta s)/2\); ties lose. The objective is the total winning mass measured against the original mass \(1\).

Is deciding whether a feasible redistribution has winning mass at least \(\rho\) solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,s,\Delta s\), distinguishing polynomial dependence on numerical sizes from dependence on their bit lengths.

For finite districts, Biased Dissolution is polynomial-time solvable when \(s=\Delta s=1\), and NP-complete for every other fixed positive pair \((s,\Delta s)\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.2664)

  • Specified the compatibility graph as simple and undirected, following the discrete graph convention; districts of the same type are therefore incompatible.

Result

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable, including when \(s\) and \(\Delta s\) are binary encoded.

An exact linear program uses \(3\tau+8m\) variables and \(O(\tau+m)\) constraints. Its bit complexity is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\); it does not require polynomial dependence on the numerical values of \(s\) or \(\Delta s\).

The essential point is that the relevant averages can always be realized by mixtures of integer district patterns. We prove this before constructing the linear program.

1. A convex-hull lemma for integer patterns

Let \(r\geq 1\), let \(M\) be a positive integer, and let \(\ell,h\) be integers satisfying \(0\leq\ell\leq h\leq M\). Define

\(P_r(M;\ell,h)=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{2r}:\sum_{i=1}^r(a_i+b_i)=M,\ \ell\leq\sum_{i=1}^r a_i\leq h\}\).

Lemma. Every point of \(P_r(M;\ell,h)\) is a convex combination of nonnegative integer points of the same polytope.

Proof. Fix \((a,b)\in P_r(M;\ell,h)\), and put \(q=\sum_i a_i\). Choose an integer-valued random variable \(J\), supported on \(\{\ell,h\}\), with expectation \(q\). Explicitly, when \(\ell<h\), use

\(\Pr(J=\ell)=(h-q)/(h-\ell)\), and \(\Pr(J=h)=(q-\ell)/(h-\ell)\).

When \(\ell=h\), take \(J=\ell\).

If \(q>0\), choose an index \(I_A\) with probabilities \(\Pr(I_A=i)=a_i/q\). If \(q=0\), choose any index instead. Similarly, if \(M-q>0\), choose \(I_B\) with probabilities \(\Pr(I_B=i)=b_i/(M-q)\); otherwise choose any index. Make these choices independently of \(J\).

Construct an integer pattern by assigning all \(J\) A-voters to index \(I_A\) and all \(M-J\) B-voters to index \(I_B\). Every resulting pattern has total \(M\) and A-count between \(\ell\) and \(h\).

For \(q>0\), the expected A-count at index \(i\) is

\(\mathbb E[J\,\mathbf 1_{\{I_A=i\}}]=\mathbb E[J]\Pr(I_A=i)=a_i\).

If \(q=0\), nonnegativity implies \(a_i=0\) for every \(i\), and \(J=0\) almost surely, so the same conclusion holds. The corresponding calculation for B gives expected count \(b_i\).

Thus the distribution is the required finite convex combination. It uses at most \(2r^2\) patterns. Conversely, every convex combination of integer points satisfies the defining linear constraints. This proves the lemma.

In particular:

  • Taking \(M=s\) and \(\ell=h=\alpha_t\) describes exactly the possible average outgoing patterns of a dissolved type-\(t\) district.
  • Taking \(M=\Delta s\) and integer bounds on the total incoming A-count describes exactly the possible average incoming patterns of a specified outcome class.

This lemma preserves integral counts in every district; only the masses assigned to patterns are fractional.

2. The compact linear program

Write \(D=\Delta s\). For each type \(u\), define the integer threshold

\(k_u=\left\lfloor (s+D)/2\right\rfloor+1-\alpha_u\).

Because the incoming A-count is integral, a surviving type-\(u\) district wins exactly when its incoming A-count is at least \(k_u\). It loses exactly when that count is at most \(k_u-1\).

Let \(\vec F=\{(t,u):\{t,u\}\in F\}\); thus \(|\vec F|=2m\).

Introduce nonnegative variables:

  • \(d_t,w_t,\ell_t\): dissolved, winning-survivor, and losing-survivor masses of type \(t\);
  • \(x_{tu}^{A,W},x_{tu}^{B,W},x_{tu}^{A,L},x_{tu}^{B,L}\), for each \((t,u)\in\vec F\): mass-weighted incoming counts from type \(t\) to the winning or losing survivors of type \(u\).

Impose the following constraints.

Partition each type’s mass:

\(d_t+w_t+\ell_t=\mu_t\qquad(t\in T).\)

Account for all voters of dissolved districts:

\(\sum_{u\in N_H(t)}(x_{tu}^{A,W}+x_{tu}^{A,L})=\alpha_t d_t\qquad(t\in T),\)

\(\sum_{u\in N_H(t)}(x_{tu}^{B,W}+x_{tu}^{B,L})=(s-\alpha_t)d_t\qquad(t\in T).\)

Supply exactly \(D\) incoming voters per surviving district:

\(\sum_{t\in N_H(u)}(x_{tu}^{A,W}+x_{tu}^{B,W})=D w_u\qquad(u\in T),\)

\(\sum_{t\in N_H(u)}(x_{tu}^{A,L}+x_{tu}^{B,L})=D\ell_u\qquad(u\in T).\)

Enforce the two outcome classes:

\(\sum_{t\in N_H(u)}x_{tu}^{A,W}\geq k_u w_u\qquad(u\in T),\)

\(\sum_{t\in N_H(u)}x_{tu}^{A,L}\leq(k_u-1)\ell_u\qquad(u\in T).\)

Require the requested winning mass:

\(\sum_{u\in T}w_u\geq\rho.\)

There are \(3\tau+8m\) variables, \(5\tau\) equalities, and \(2\tau+1\) inequalities besides nonnegativity.

The extreme threshold cases require no special treatment. If \(k_u>D\), nonnegativity and the incoming-total equation give

\(k_u w_u\leq\sum_t x_{tu}^{A,W}\leq D w_u,\)

forcing \(w_u=0\). If \(k_u\leq0\), the losing constraint gives

\(0\leq\sum_t x_{tu}^{A,L}\leq(k_u-1)\ell_u,\)

forcing \(\ell_u=0\).

3. Proof that the formulation is exact

Every redistribution yields an LP solution

Given a feasible redistribution, partition the surviving districts according to their actual outcomes. Let their masses be \(w_t,\ell_t\), and let the dissolved mass be \(d_t\).

Define each \(x\)-variable by integrating the corresponding incoming integer count over its survivor class. The incoming-total equations follow because every survivor receives exactly \(D\) voters. The outcome inequalities follow because every winning pattern has incoming A-count at least \(k_u\), and every losing pattern has incoming A-count at most \(k_u-1\).

For each ordered compatible pair, the stated matching conditions identify the combined incoming counts of the two survivor classes with the outgoing counts of dissolved districts. Summing over recipients therefore gives the two outgoing equations.

Finally, the actual winning mass is exactly \(\sum_u w_u\). Thus any redistribution meeting the threshold gives a feasible LP solution.

Every LP solution yields a redistribution

Fix a feasible LP solution. We construct all district patterns.

Dissolved districts. For any type \(t\) with \(d_t>0\), set

\(a_u=(x_{tu}^{A,W}+x_{tu}^{A,L})/d_t,\qquad b_u=(x_{tu}^{B,W}+x_{tu}^{B,L})/d_t.\)

The outgoing equations give

\(\sum_u a_u=\alpha_t,\qquad\sum_u b_u=s-\alpha_t.\)

Apply the lemma with \(M=s\) and \(\ell=h=\alpha_t\). It expresses these averages as a finite mixture of valid integer outgoing patterns.

Winning survivors. For any type \(u\) with \(w_u>0\), normalize its winning incoming counts:

\(a_t=x_{tu}^{A,W}/w_u,\qquad b_t=x_{tu}^{B,W}/w_u.\)

Their total is \(D\), and their A-total is at least \(k_u\). Positive \(w_u\) implies \(k_u\leq D\), as established above. The lemma with bounds \(\max\{0,k_u\}\) and \(D\) expresses these averages as a mixture of integer incoming patterns, each of which wins.

Losing survivors. For any type \(u\) with \(\ell_u>0\), normalize

\(a_t=x_{tu}^{A,L}/\ell_u,\qquad b_t=x_{tu}^{B,L}/\ell_u.\)

Their total is \(D\), and their A-total is at most \(k_u-1\). Positive \(\ell_u\) implies \(k_u\geq1\). Apply the lemma with bounds \(0\) and \(\min\{D,k_u-1\}\). Every resulting integer pattern loses.

If a class has zero mass, its associated counts are zero by the corresponding total equations and nonnegativity, so no patterns are needed for it. If a type has no neighbors, the outgoing and incoming equations force \(d_t=w_t=\ell_t=0\), contradicting \(\mu_t>0\); consequently, the LP correctly rejects such an instance.

Now split each type’s atomless mass into its dissolved, winning, and losing classes, and then into the finitely many patterns just constructed. These splits are allowed by the problem’s assumptions.

For each \((t,u)\in\vec F\), the constructed outgoing A-count is

\(x_{tu}^{A,W}+x_{tu}^{A,L},\)

which equals the combined constructed incoming A-count. The same equality holds for B. These are exactly the required matching conditions; the stated model imposes no additional coupling between individual districts.

Hence this construction is a feasible redistribution, and its winning mass is exactly \(\sum_u w_u\), which is at least \(\rho\).

This proves equivalence in both directions. It also proves that maximizing \(\sum_u w_u\) in the LP computes the exact maximum winning mass.

Conservation of district mass

Summing the outgoing and incoming total equations gives

\(s\sum_t d_t=D\sum_t(w_t+\ell_t).\)

Together with \(\sum_t(d_t+w_t+\ell_t)=1\), this yields

\(\sum_t d_t=D/(s+D),\qquad\sum_t(w_t+\ell_t)=s/(s+D).\)

Thus the formulation automatically enforces the prescribed dissolved and surviving masses. Its objective measures winning mass against the original mass \(1\).

4. Running time and bit complexity

Let \(q=m+\tau\) and \(b=\lceil\log_2(s+D+2)\rceil\).

The LP has \(O(q)\) variables and constraints. Its integer coefficients have \(O(b)\) bits, and its rational right-hand sides are the supplied masses and threshold. A dense binary encoding of the LP has length

\(B=O(q^2+L+\tau b).\)

Indeed, the dense matrix contributes \(O(q^2)\) constant-size entries; only \(O(\tau)\) coefficients involve district sizes or majority thresholds.

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming decides feasibility in time polynomial in the dimensions and binary encoding length. Its hypotheses hold here: all coefficients and right-hand sides are rational, and the dimensions and encoding length have the bounds just given. Consequently, the decision algorithm takes

\(\bigl((m+\tau)^2+L+\tau\log_2(s+\Delta s+2)\bigr)^{O(1)}\)

bit operations. Since \(\log_2(s+\Delta s+2)=O(L)\), this is also

\((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}.\)

The explicitly supplied support and graph have size polynomially bounded by \(L\), so this is polynomial in total input length. The dependence on district sizes is through their bit lengths, not through \(s\) or \(\Delta s\) themselves. No integer-pattern enumeration is required.

5. Why the finite-district hardness does not transfer

The [cited finite-district classification](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.2664) concerns individual districts whose dissolution and survival choices are discrete. Here, each type’s mass can be divided among roles and patterns. The lemma proves that these divisions admit an exact convex description while retaining integer voter counts in every district.

Therefore, the finite-district hardness does not obstruct the polynomial-time algorithm for this atomless model.
