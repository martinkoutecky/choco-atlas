The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), innate opinions \(s_t\), baseline resistances \(\alpha_t\), a rational column-stochastic influence kernel \(P\), and a quota \(\kappa\), choose real masses \(z_{t,0},z_{t,1}\geq 0\) with \(z_{t,0}+z_{t,1}\leq\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t(z_{t,0}+z_{t,1})\leq\kappa\). For each allocation, let \(r_t=\mu_t-z_{t,0}-z_{t,1}\), \(a_{t,*}=\alpha_t\), \(a_{t,0}=0\), \(a_{t,1}=1\), and let the unique equilibrium satisfy \(x_{t,h}=a_{t,h}s_t+(1-a_{t,h})\sum_uP_{ut}y_u\) and \(y_t=(r_tx_{t,*}+z_{t,0}x_{t,0}+z_{t,1}x_{t,1})/\mu_t\). Determine the global minimum and maximum of \(F_{\mathrm{MSE}}=\sum_t[r_t(x_{t,*}-\bar{s})^2+z_{t,0}(x_{t,0}-\bar{s})^2+z_{t,1}(x_{t,1}-\bar{s})^2]\), where \(\bar{s}=\sum_t\mu_ts_t\); equivalently, for rational \(q\), decide whether a feasible allocation achieves \(F_{\mathrm{MSE}}\geq q\) or \(F_{\mathrm{MSE}}\leq q\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Input also includes binary rational innate opinions \(s_t\in[0,1]\), baseline resistances \(\alpha_t\in[0,1]\), a nonnegative rational column-stochastic matrix \(P\), a quota \(\kappa\in[0,1]\), and a threshold \(q\in\mathbb Q\). Here \(P_{ut}\) is the aggregate influence weight of type \(u\) on type \(t\).
Choose real masses \(z_{t,0},z_{t,1}\geq0\) satisfying \(z_{t,0}+z_{t,1}\leq\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t(z_{t,0}+z_{t,1})\leq\kappa\); these masses receive resistance \(0\) and \(1\), respectively, without changing their innate opinions or influence weights.
Set \(r_t=\mu_t-z_{t,0}-z_{t,1}\) and \(a_{t,*}=\alpha_t\), \(a_{t,0}=0\), \(a_{t,1}=1\). For \(h\in\{*,0,1\}\), equilibrium is defined by \(x_{t,h}=a_{t,h}s_t+(1-a_{t,h})\sum_uP_{ut}y_u\) and \(y_t=(r_tx_{t,*}+z_{t,0}x_{t,0}+z_{t,1}x_{t,1})/\mu_t\).
Restrict to inputs for which these equations have a unique equilibrium for every allocation satisfying the budget constraints, including the zero allocation.
Define \(\bar s=\sum_t\mu_ts_t\) and \(F_{\mathrm{MSE}}=\sum_t[r_t(x_{t,*}-\bar s)^2+z_{t,0}(x_{t,0}-\bar s)^2+z_{t,1}(x_{t,1}-\bar s)^2]\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; running times are measured in \(\tau\) and \(L\), with \(\kappa\) and \(q\) included in the input. There is no separate candidate parameter \(m\).

For each of the two decision problems—whether some feasible allocation satisfies \(F_{\mathrm{MSE}}\geq q\), and whether some feasible allocation satisfies \(F_{\mathrm{MSE}}\leq q\)—is it in \(\mathrm P\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete MSE maximization and minimization, allowing resistance changes at at most \(k\) agents, are NP-hard ([discrete hardness result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07805)); the formal hardness formulation allows arbitrary selected resistance values in \([0,1]\).
No result settling either decision problem for this precise mass-splitting formulation was located.

Result

Both decision problems are \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions.

The reductions below apply directly to mass splitting; they do not require transferring the [cited discrete hardness result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07805). They use only baseline resistances in \(\{0,1\}\), and every constructed instance satisfies the required uniqueness promise.

We reduce from Subset Sum: given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) and an integer \(B\), decide whether some subset sums to \(B\). We use its standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete restriction \(1\leq B\leq S\), where \(S=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\).

All rational numbers below are represented by binary-encoded numerators and denominators.

1. Common equilibrium gadgets

A fixed anchor

Introduce an anchor type \(A\), with \(s_A=0\), \(\alpha_A=1\), and \(P_{AA}=1\). Choose its mass so that \(\mu_A>\kappa\).

For any feasible allocation, its aggregate equilibrium equation is

\( y_A=\dfrac{z_{A,0}}{\mu_A}y_A. \)

Indeed, its baseline and resistance-one groups both express \(0\), while its resistance-zero group expresses \(y_A\). Because \(z_{A,0}\leq\kappa<\mu_A\), the coefficient is strictly less than \(1\). Consequently, \(y_A=0\) uniquely, and all its subgroup opinions equal \(0\).

Writing \(c=\bar s\), the anchor therefore contributes exactly \(\mu_Ac^2\) to the objective, regardless of interventions on it.

A variable type

A variable type \(V\) has \(s_V=1\), \(\alpha_V=1\), and influence weights

\( P_{VV}=\dfrac12,\qquad P_{AV}=\dfrac12. \)

Let \(v=z_{V,0}/\mu_V\), so \(0\leq v\leq1\). Its baseline and resistance-one groups both express \(1\). Its resistance-zero group expresses \(b=y_V/2\). Hence

\( y_V=1-v+\dfrac v2y_V, \qquad y_V=y(v):=\dfrac{1-v}{1-v/2}, \qquad b=b(v):=\dfrac{y(v)}2. \)

The denominator is at least \(1/2\), so this equilibrium is unique. Also, \(0\leq y(v)\leq1\).

The variable type’s contribution to the objective is exactly

\( \mu_V\big[(1-v)(1-c)^2+v(b(v)-c)^2\big]. \)

Allocating resistance one to this type changes neither its equilibrium nor its objective contribution, but consumes budget.

A follower type

For the minimization reduction, a variable type also has a follower \(H\), with \(s_H=1\), \(\alpha_H=0\), and \(P_{VH}=1\). No other type receives influence from \(H\).

Let \(w=z_{H,1}/\mu_H\). The follower’s baseline and resistance-zero groups express \(y(v)\); its resistance-one group expresses \(1\). Thus its aggregate equilibrium is uniquely determined, and its objective contribution is

\( \mu_H\big[(1-w)(y(v)-c)^2+w(1-c)^2\big]. \)

When \(c=1/10\), interventions on the follower cannot decrease this contribution. To see this, use \(0\leq y(v)\leq1\) and calculate

\( (1-c)^2-(y(v)-c)^2 =(1-y(v))(1+y(v)-2c)\geq0. \)

Its contribution is therefore at least \(\mu_H(y(v)-c)^2\), with equality when no follower intervention is made.

2. Maximization is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard

Construction

Given a Subset Sum instance, append the dummy weight \(a_{n+1}=4S\). Define

\( W=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}a_i=5S. \)

The dummy weight exceeds \(B\), so a subset of the extended list sums to \(B\) precisely when a subset of the original list does.

Construct one variable type \(V_i\) for each extended weight, with

\( \mu_{V_i}=\dfrac{3a_i}{4W}. \)

Add the anchor with mass \(\mu_A=1/4\). These masses sum to \(1\). Since all variable innate opinions equal \(1\), the innate mean is \(c=3/4\).

Set

\( \kappa=\dfrac{3B}{4W}, \qquad q=\dfrac{3}{16}+\dfrac{\kappa}{2}. \)

Because \(B\leq S\) and \(W=5S\), we have \(\kappa\leq3/20<1/4=\mu_A\). Thus the anchor is fixed for every feasible allocation.

An upper bound with exact equality conditions

For a variable type, compare its contribution with the line joining its values at \(v=0\) and \(v=1\). Because \(c=3/4\),

\( (1-v)(1-c)^2+v(b-c)^2 =(1-c)^2+(2c-1)v-vb(2c-b). \)

Here \(0\leq b\leq1/2\), so \(vb(2c-b)\geq0\). Therefore,

\( \mu_{V_i}\big[(1-v_i)(1-c)^2+v_i(b(v_i)-c)^2\big] \leq \dfrac{\mu_{V_i}}{16}+\dfrac{z_{V_i,0}}2. \)

Equality holds exactly when \(v_i\in\{0,1\}\). Indeed, the correction vanishes at both endpoints, whereas for \(0<v_i<1\), all three factors \(v_i\), \(b(v_i)\), and \(2c-b(v_i)\) are strictly positive.

The anchor’s contribution and the constant parts of the variable contributions sum to

\( \dfrac14\left(\dfrac34\right)^2+ \dfrac34\left(\dfrac14\right)^2 =\dfrac{3}{16}. \)

Consequently, every feasible allocation satisfies

\( F_{\mathrm{MSE}} \leq \dfrac{3}{16}+\dfrac12\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}z_{V_i,0} \leq \dfrac{3}{16}+\dfrac{\kappa}{2} =q. \)

Equivalence with Subset Sum

Suppose a feasible allocation satisfies \(F_{\mathrm{MSE}}\geq q\). Both inequalities above must be equalities. Hence

\( \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}z_{V_i,0}=\kappa, \qquad v_i\in\{0,1\}\ \text{for every }i. \)

Let \(I=\{i:v_i=1\}\). Then

\( \sum_{i\in I}\dfrac{3a_i}{4W} =\kappa =\dfrac{3B}{4W}, \)

so \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=B\). The dummy weight cannot belong to \(I\), because it exceeds \(B\). The original Subset Sum instance is therefore a yes-instance.

Conversely, if a subset \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) sums to \(B\), allocate \(z_{V_i,0}=\mu_{V_i}\) for \(i\in I\), and make no other interventions. Its total cost is exactly \(\kappa\), and every variable attains equality in its upper bound. Thus \(F_{\mathrm{MSE}}=q\).

This proves the required equivalence for maximization.

3. Minimization is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard

Construction

Use the original Subset Sum weights, without a dummy weight.

For each \(i\), construct a variable type \(V_i\) and its follower \(H_i\), with masses

\( \mu_{V_i}=\dfrac{a_i}{15S}, \qquad \mu_{H_i}=\dfrac{\mu_{V_i}}2=\dfrac{a_i}{30S}. \)

Add the anchor with mass \(\mu_A=9/10\). The total mass is

\( \dfrac1{15}+\dfrac1{30}+\dfrac9{10}=1. \)

All variable and follower innate opinions equal \(1\), so now \(c=1/10\).

Set

\( \kappa=\dfrac{B}{20S}, \qquad q=\dfrac9{100}-\dfrac{32}{25}\kappa. \)

We have \(\kappa\leq1/20<9/10=\mu_A\), so the anchor remains fixed for every feasible allocation.

At zero allocation, every variable and follower expresses \(1\). Thus the objective at zero allocation is

\( F_0=\dfrac1{10}\left(\dfrac9{10}\right)^2+ \dfrac9{10}\left(\dfrac1{10}\right)^2 =\dfrac9{100}. \)

An exact lower-bound identity

For one variable–follower pair, divide its contribution without follower interventions by the variable mass. The resulting function is

\( f(v)= (1-v)\dfrac{81}{100} +v\left(\dfrac{y(v)}2-\dfrac1{10}\right)^2 +\dfrac12\left(y(v)-\dfrac1{10}\right)^2. \)

It satisfies the exact identity

\( f(v)= \dfrac{243}{200}-\dfrac{32}{25}v +\dfrac{v(4v-3)^2}{50(1-v/2)^2}. \)

Here is an explicit algebraic verification. Put \(D=1-v/2\). Then

\( \dfrac{y(v)}2-\dfrac1{10}=\dfrac{8-9v}{20D}, \qquad y(v)-\dfrac1{10}=\dfrac{18-19v}{20D}. \)

Multiplying the claimed identity by \(800D^2\) reduces it to

\( 648(1-v)D^2+2v(8-9v)^2+(18-19v)^2 =(972-1024v)D^2+16v(4v-3)^2. \)

Expanding either side gives \(972-1996v+1411v^2\), verifying the identity.

Since \(v\geq0\) and \(D\geq1/2\), it follows that

\( f(v)\geq\dfrac{243}{200}-\dfrac{32}{25}v, \)

with equality exactly when \(v\in\{0,3/4\}\).

Follower interventions cannot lower their contributions, as proved in Section 1. Therefore, for every feasible allocation,

\( F_{\mathrm{MSE}} \geq \mu_Ac^2+\sum_{i=1}^n\mu_{V_i}f(v_i). \)

Using the identity, \(\sum_i\mu_{V_i}=1/15\), and \(\mu_{V_i}v_i=z_{V_i,0}\), we obtain

\( F_{\mathrm{MSE}} \geq \dfrac9{100} -\dfrac{32}{25}\sum_{i=1}^n z_{V_i,0} +\sum_{i=1}^n \dfrac{\mu_{V_i}v_i(4v_i-3)^2}{50(1-v_i/2)^2} \geq \dfrac9{100}-\dfrac{32}{25}\kappa =q. \)

Equivalence with Subset Sum

Suppose a feasible allocation satisfies \(F_{\mathrm{MSE}}\leq q\). Equality must hold throughout the preceding lower bound. In particular,

\( \sum_{i=1}^n z_{V_i,0}=\kappa, \qquad v_i\in\{0,3/4\}\ \text{for every }i. \)

Let \(I=\{i:v_i=3/4\}\). Its intervention cost is

\( \sum_{i\in I}\dfrac34\mu_{V_i} =\sum_{i\in I}\dfrac{a_i}{20S} =\kappa =\dfrac{B}{20S}. \)

Thus \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=B\), and the Subset Sum instance is a yes-instance.

Conversely, given a subset \(I\) summing to \(B\), allocate

\( z_{V_i,0}=\dfrac34\mu_{V_i}\quad\text{for }i\in I, \)

and make no other interventions. Its total cost is exactly \(\kappa\). Every variable–follower pair attains equality in the identity, so \(F_{\mathrm{MSE}}=q\).

This proves the required equivalence for minimization.

4. Promise and encoding checks

For every feasible allocation in either construction:

  • The anchor has the unique equilibrium \(y_A=0\), because \(\mu_A>\kappa\).
  • Each variable has the unique equilibrium \(y(v)=(1-v)/(1-v/2)\).
  • Each follower, when present, depends only on its variable and has a uniquely determined aggregate opinion.
  • Once the aggregate opinions are fixed, every subgroup opinion is fixed by its defining equation.

These statements establish uniqueness of the full equilibrium, including at zero allocation.

Every column of \(P\) has nonnegative entries summing to \(1\): the anchor column contains its self-weight \(1\); each variable column contains two weights \(1/2\); and each follower column contains its parent’s weight \(1\).

The maximization construction has support size \(\tau=n+2\), and the minimization construction has support size \(\tau=2n+1\). All masses are positive, and all rational parameters and thresholds have binary encoding length polynomial in the Subset Sum input length. Thus both constructions are polynomial-time many-one reductions, with no separate candidate parameter.

Accordingly, both requested decision problems are \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. No membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) is asserted; it is unnecessary for this conclusion.
