The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) candidates, \(z\) equal-mass districts, fixed tie-breaking, and a polynomial-time sampler \(M\) that outputs rational mass vectors \(\mu\) over types \((d,\pi)\) with total mass \(1/z\) in each district, estimate \(\mathbb{E}_{\mu\sim M}[\mathrm{MR}_\infty(\mu)]\) within additive error \(\varepsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\). For plurality, \(\mathrm{MR}_\infty(\mu)\le m^2\), so Hoeffding sampling uses at most \(\left\lceil (m^2-1)^2\ln(2/\delta)/(2\varepsilon^2)\right\rceil\) samples; each sample’s ratio is computable from its supported types.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge 2\) candidates and let \(z\ge 1\) be the number of districts. A type is \((d,\pi)\), where \(d\in\{1,\ldots,z\}\) and \(\pi\) is a strict ranking of \(C\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Require \(\sum_\pi\mu(d,\pi)=1/z\) for every district, so total mass is \(1\).
Each district selects its plurality winner using mass-weighted scores; the overall winner \(w(\mu)\) is the candidate winning the most districts. Fixed input priority orders resolve ties at both levels. Let \(S_c(\mu)\) be candidate \(c\)'s global mass-weighted plurality score and define \(\mathrm{MR}_\infty(\mu)=\max_{c\in C}S_c(\mu)/S_{w(\mu)}(\mu)\).
Access to \(M\) consists of independent sampling calls, each returning such a society in time at most \(T_M\). Let \(\tau\) bound every output's support size and \(L\) bound the binary encoding lengths of the input and each output. Accuracy parameters \(\varepsilon,\delta\) are binary rationals with \(0<\varepsilon,\delta<1\).

Can \(r=\mathbb{E}_{\mu\sim M}[\mathrm{MR}_\infty(\mu)]\) be estimated by a randomized algorithm returning \(\widehat r\) with \(\Pr(|\widehat r-r|\le\varepsilon)\ge1-\delta\), in time polynomial in \(m,z,\tau,L,T_M,\varepsilon^{-1},\log(1/\delta)\), or is achieving this approximation guarantee NP-hard?

For finite equal-size districts, Bachrach, Lev, Lewenberg and Zick established additive estimation with confidence \(1-\delta\), polynomial in the score-ratio range, \(\varepsilon^{-1}\), and \(\log(1/\delta)\), assuming efficient sampling and winner computation. [Source](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/main.pdf)
No result explicitly addressing this supported rational-mass sampling problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted the voting rule to plurality, the rule for which the graded question specifies its approximation guarantee.
  • Specified strict rankings, at least two candidates, a positive integer number of districts, and positive supported masses normalized to total mass one.
  • Made fixed tie-breaking explicit as input priority orders at both district and overall levels; did not impose the strict-victory restrictions used only for worst-case constructions.
  • Defined the ratio using the winner of the most districts, consistent with the intended misrepresentation definition; the algorithm listing's global-score winner would instead make every sampled ratio one, and the longer COMSOC version retains that slip.
  • Specified independent black-box sampling, uniform output support and encoding bounds, sampling cost, and binary rational accuracy parameters in the open unit interval.

Result

The requested guarantee is achievable in randomized polynomial time. An empirical mean of independently sampled ratios suffices. The essential facts are a uniform bound \(\mathrm{MR}_\infty(\mu)\le m^2\) and polynomial-time exact evaluation of each ratio from its supported rational representation.

1. A uniform bound on the ratio

For each district \(d\) and candidate \(c\), define the district plurality score
\[ \]
\(s_{d,c}=\sum_{\pi:\,\pi\text{ ranks }c\text{ first}}\mu(d,\pi).\)

Every ranking has exactly one first-ranked candidate. Consequently, the prescribed district normalization gives
\[ \]
\(\sum_{c\in C}s_{d,c}=1/z.\)

Let \(h_d\) be the selected winner of district \(d\). Since tie-breaking selects a candidate with maximum score,
\[ \]
\(s_{d,h_d}=\max_{c\in C}s_{d,c}\ge 1/(mz).\)

Define \(K_c=|\{d:h_d=c\}|\). Each district selects exactly one winner, so \(\sum_{c\in C}K_c=z\). The overall winner \(w=w(\mu)\) maximizes \(K_c\), even when selected by tie-breaking. Thus
\[ \]
\(K_w\ge\lceil z/m\rceil.\)

The global score of \(w\) includes its scores in all districts it wins. Nonnegativity therefore implies
\[ \]
\(S_w(\mu)=\sum_{d=1}^{z}s_{d,w}\ge \frac{K_w}{mz}\ge\frac{\lceil z/m\rceil}{mz}\ge\frac1{m^2}.\)

Also, the global candidate scores sum to the total mass:
\[ \]
\(\sum_{c\in C}S_c(\mu)=1.\)

Hence \(\max_c S_c(\mu)\le1\). The denominator is strictly positive, and including \(w\) among the candidates in the numerator gives
\[ \]
\(1\le\mathrm{MR}_\infty(\mu)=\frac{\max_c S_c(\mu)}{S_w(\mu)}\le m^2.\)

This bound holds for every permitted society, independently of the smallest positive mass and of either priority order. In particular, the expectation \(r\) exists and is finite.

2. Exact evaluation from the support

Consider a sampled society with support size \(t\le\tau\), and write its masses as \(a_j/b_j\), with positive integer denominators. Set
\[ \]
\(Q=\prod_{j=1}^{t}b_j.\)

Each mass can be represented exactly as the integer weight
\[ \]
\(u_j=a_j(Q/b_j).\)

Because the masses sum to one, \(\sum_j u_j=Q\). Accumulate these integer weights according to district and first-ranked candidate. All district scores then have common denominator \(Q\), so their comparisons, including exact ties, reduce to integer comparisons. Apply the input district priorities, count district wins, and apply the overall priority.

Similarly, let \(A_c=QS_c(\mu)\), obtained by summing the appropriate integer weights. The exact sampled ratio is
\[ \]
\(\mathrm{MR}_\infty(\mu)=\frac{\max_c A_c}{A_w},\)

where \(A_w>0\) by Section 1.

Conservatively, each denominator has at most \(L\) bits, so \(Q\) has at most \(O(\tau L)\) bits. Every accumulated score lies between zero and \(Q\). Integer multiplication, exact division, addition, and comparison on these lengths take polynomial time. Reading the supported rankings and processing the candidate–district scores likewise takes polynomial time in \(m,z,\tau,L\).

Thus there is a deterministic exact evaluation procedure whose running time is polynomial in \(m,z,\tau,L\). It never enumerates unsupported rankings or expands rational masses into individual voters.

3. Estimator and accuracy proof

Put \(B=m^2-1\). Let \(k\) be the smallest nonnegative integer satisfying
\[ \]
\(2^k\delta\ge2,\)

and choose
\[ \]
\(N=\left\lceil\frac{B^2k}{2\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil.\)

The integer \(k\) is computable by exact rational comparisons. Moreover,
\[ \]
\(k=\lceil\log_2(2/\delta)\rceil\ge\ln(2/\delta).\)

Make \(N\) independent calls to \(M\), compute the exact values \(X_i=\mathrm{MR}_\infty(\mu_i)\), and return
\[ \]
\(\widehat r=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_i.\)

The variables \(X_i\) are independent, each has expectation \(r\), and each belongs to \([1,m^2]\).

Hoeffding’s inequality states that independent variables in a common interval of length \(B\) satisfy
\[ \]
\(\Pr\left(\left|\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_i-\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb E[X_i]\right|\ge\varepsilon\right)\le2\exp\left(-\frac{2N\varepsilon^2}{B^2}\right).\)

All its hypotheses have just been verified. Substituting the chosen sample count yields
\[ \]
\(\Pr(|\widehat r-r|\ge\varepsilon)\le2e^{-k}\le\delta.\)

Therefore
\[ \]
\(\Pr(|\widehat r-r|\le\varepsilon)\ge1-\delta.\)

This is the same bounded-range sampling principle used in Theorem 5.1 of [Bachrach, Lev, Lewenberg and Zick](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/main.pdf). The preceding sections establish the range and exact evaluation requirements directly for the supported rational-mass model.

4. Bit complexity and conclusion

The sample count satisfies
\[ \]
\(N=O\!\left(m^4\varepsilon^{-2}\bigl(1+\log(1/\delta)\bigr)\right).\)

Sampling costs at most \(NT_M\). Each sampled ratio has numerator and denominator of \(O(\tau L)\) bits. To form the exact empirical mean, one may use the product of these denominators as a common denominator and then divide by \(N\). The resulting numerator and denominator have at most
\[ \]
\(O(N\tau L+\log N+\log m)\)

bits: the denominator product has \(O(N\tau L)\) bits, and the sum is at most \(Nm^2\) times that product. Consequently, exact accumulation and output also take polynomial time in the requested parameters.

The first alternative in the question is therefore proved: efficient independent sampling permits the stated additive approximation in randomized polynomial time.
