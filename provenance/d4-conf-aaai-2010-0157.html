The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution μ over bidder types, per-capita item supplies, and a type \(t\) with a losing bid on bundle \(S\), designate mass \(q>0\) of type \(t\) as a cohort. What is the exact threshold \(p^\star\) such that changing that cohort’s bid on \(S\) to \(p\) leaves the welfare-maximizing allocation and fixed-tie-broken bidder-optimal core outcome unchanged for \(b_t(S) ≤ p ≤ p^\star\), while a bid above \(p^\star\) changes the selected outcome?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses sum to \(1\). There are \(m\geq1\) item kinds with positive binary-rational per-capita supplies \(s_j\).
Each type has an explicit list of admissible nonempty bundles \(B\subseteq[m]\) and nonnegative binary-rational bids \(b_r(B)\); bids are XOR. The empty bundle is always admissible with bid zero. Utilities are quasilinear; the core uses reported bids.
Given a listed bundle \(S\) of type \(t\) and binary-rational \(0<q\leq\mu_t\), label a cohort \(c\) of mass \(q\) separately from the remaining type-\(t\) mass, retaining identical bids initially and omitting zero-mass classes.
An allocation specifies masses \(x_{rB}\geq0\), with \(\sum_Bx_{rB}=\mu_r\) and \(\sum_{r,B:j\in B}x_{rB}\leq s_j\). Each member receives one whole bundle; splitting a type's mass across bundles is permitted.
An outcome also specifies total payments \(h_{rB}\), with \(0\leq h_{rB}\leq x_{rB}b_r(B)\) and \(h_{r\varnothing}=0\). Members of a positive-mass class \((r,B)\) each pay \(h_{rB}/x_{rB}\). Use the transferable-utility core with the seller: coalitions may contain arbitrary submasses of these classes, and the seller contributes its entire supply; bidder-only coalitions have no items.
Select an allocation maximizing \(\sum_{r,B}x_{rB}b_r(B)\), breaking ties by lexicographically minimizing \(x\). For that allocation, select core payments minimizing \(\sum_{r,B}h_{rB}\), breaking ties by lexicographically minimizing \(h\). Coordinate orders are fixed by the input labels and bundle lists and do not depend on bid amounts. Promise that initially \(x_{cS}=0\); the cohort may win other bundles.
Let \(O(p)\) be this selected allocation-payment pair when only \(b_c(S)\) is replaced by the real number \(p\geq b_t(S)\). Define \(p^\star=\sup\{p\geq b_t(S):O(p)=O(b_t(S))\}\), and include whether \(O(p^\star)=O(b_t(S))\) in the threshold problem.
Is computing this exact threshold, including its endpoint status, possible in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), the total number \(N\) of listed bids, and the total input bit length \(L\).
Discrete: an optimal mixed-integer method for bidder-optimal envy quotes is established, without a polynomial-time guarantee ([Othman–Sandholm](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/www/envy%20quotes%20for%20CAs.aaai10.pdf)). No result settling this precise mass-cohort threshold problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one and interpreted supplies relative to that normalization; required positive rational supplies.
  • Specified explicit XOR bundle-bid lists, nonnegative rational bids, and the zero-valued empty bundle; unlisted nonempty bundles are inadmissible.
  • Interpreted continuous allocation as splitting bidder mass among whole bundles, preserving at most one bundle per bidder.
  • Made the cohort a persistent labeled class before baseline selection and promised that none of it receives S; it may receive other bundles.
  • Specified nonnegative, individually rational payments, zero payments for the empty bundle, and constant per-person payments within each labeled allocation class.
  • Used the reported-bid transferable-utility core, allowing arbitrary submass coalitions with the seller's entire supply.
  • Specified bid-independent lexicographic allocation and payment tie-breaking, and interpreted bidder-optimal as minimizing total core payments.
  • Replaced the potentially nonexistent largest unchanged bid by its supremum and recorded endpoint inclusion, since fixed tie-breaking need not preserve the outcome at equality.
  • Allowed unrestricted real bid increases for threshold definition; true valuations and a no-overbidding cap are not inputs to this reported-bid computation.

Result

The exact threshold and its endpoint status are computable in polynomial time. The algorithm uses \(O(N+\tau)\) rational linear programs, each with \(O(m+N+\tau)\) variables and constraints and polynomial encoding length. Its total bit running time is
\[ (m+\tau+N+L)^{O(1)}. \]

The key is that arbitrary submasses may form coalitions. This makes the blocking-coalition calculation a linear program and gives a compact characterization of minimum-revenue core payments.

1. Welfare and its dual

After labeling the cohort, let \(R\leq\tau+1\) be the number of positive-mass classes. Write \(\mathcal A_r\) for class \(r\)'s admissible bundles, including the empty bundle, and let
\[ K=\sum_r|\mathcal A_r|\leq 2N+\tau+1. \]
The increase comes from duplicating type \(t\)'s list when its remaining mass is positive.

For item prices \(\lambda\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m\), write \(\lambda(B)=\sum_{j\in B}\lambda_j\). For a fixed bid vector \(b\), the welfare LP maximizes \(\sum_{r,B}x_{rB}b_r(B)\), subject to the allocation constraints in the question. Its dual is
\[ \min_{\lambda,w}\left\{s\cdot\lambda+\sum_r\mu_rw_r: \lambda\geq0,\quad w_r+\lambda(B)\geq b_r(B)\ \text{for every }r,B\right\}. \]
The empty-bundle constraints imply \(w_r\geq0\).

The primal is feasible by allocating everyone the empty bundle. It is bounded because \(0\leq x_{rB}\leq\mu_r\). Thus strong LP duality applies. Let \(W(b)\) be its optimum and define the optimal dual polytope
\[ D(b)=\left\{(\lambda,w): \lambda\geq0,\ w\geq0,\ w_r+\lambda(B)\geq b_r(B),\ s\cdot\lambda+\sum_r\mu_rw_r=W(b)\right\}. \]
This polytope is nonempty and compact: positivity of every supply and retained mass gives \(\lambda_j\leq W(b)/s_j\) and \(w_r\leq W(b)/\mu_r\).

For any welfare-optimal \(x\) and any \((\lambda,w)\in D(b)\), the duality gap is
\[ 0=\sum_j\lambda_j\left(s_j-\sum_{r,B:j\in B}x_{rB}\right) +\sum_{r,B}x_{rB}\bigl(w_r+\lambda(B)-b_r(B)\bigr). \]
Every term is nonnegative. Consequently,
\[ x_{rB}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ w_r+\lambda(B)=b_r(B), \qquad s\cdot\lambda=\sum_{r,B}x_{rB}\lambda(B). \]

2. Exact characterization of core payments

Lemma. Fix a welfare-optimal allocation \(x\) and payments satisfying the stipulated bounds. The outcome is in the core if and only if some \((\lambda,w)\in D(b)\) satisfies
\[ h_{rB}\geq x_{rB}\lambda(B)\qquad\text{for every }r,B. \]

Proof. Index the positive-mass allocated classes by \(i\). Write their masses as \(x_i\), their types as \(r(i)\), and their current bundles as \(B_i\). Their per-member utilities and the seller’s utility are
\[ u_i=b_{r(i)}(B_i)-h_i/x_i\geq0, \qquad H=\sum_i h_i. \]
Bidder-only coalitions cannot block: they have no items, their worth is zero, and their current utilities are nonnegative.

For coalitions containing the seller, consider
\[ \Phi(u)=\max_z\sum_i\sum_{C\in\mathcal A_{r(i)}}z_{iC}\bigl(b_{r(i)}(C)-u_i\bigr), \]
subject to
\[ z_{iC}\geq0,\qquad \sum_Cz_{iC}\leq x_i,\qquad \sum_i\sum_{C:j\in C}z_{iC}\leq s_j. \]

This is exactly the maximum coalition worth minus the included bidders’ current utility. Indeed, a feasible \(z\) includes mass \(y_i=\sum_Cz_{iC}\) from class \(i\) and assigns those members whole bundles. Conversely, an optimal allocation for any seller-containing coalition produces such a \(z\). Arbitrary submasses and further splitting by assigned bundle are permitted by the hypotheses.

Hence the core condition is \(\Phi(u)\leq H\). Assigning every class its current bundle is feasible and yields objective \(H\), so the condition is equivalent to \(\Phi(u)=H\).

The dual of this blocking LP is
\[ \min_{\lambda,\alpha}\left\{s\cdot\lambda+\sum_i x_i\alpha_i: \lambda,\alpha\geq0,\ \alpha_i+\lambda(C)\geq b_{r(i)}(C)-u_i\right\}. \]
The primal is feasible and bounded, so strong duality applies.

Suppose the outcome is in the core. Choose an optimal blocking dual, whose objective is \(H\). Its current-bundle constraints and allocation feasibility give
\[ H\leq\sum_i x_i\bigl(\alpha_i+\lambda(B_i)\bigr) \leq s\cdot\lambda+\sum_i x_i\alpha_i=H. \]
Thus equality holds throughout. Since every \(x_i>0\),
\[ \alpha_i+\lambda(B_i)=h_i/x_i. \]
In particular, \(h_i\geq x_i\lambda(B_i)\).

Set \(w_i=u_i+\alpha_i\). The dual constraints and the preceding equality imply
\[ w_i\geq b_{r(i)}(C)-\lambda(C)\quad\text{for every }C, \qquad w_i=b_{r(i)}(B_i)-\lambda(B_i). \]
Therefore \(w_i=\max_C\{b_{r(i)}(C)-\lambda(C)\}\), which depends only on the type. Denote it by \(w_r\). It is nonnegative because the empty bundle is admissible. Moreover,
\[ s\cdot\lambda+\sum_r\mu_rw_r =H+\sum_i x_i u_i =\sum_i x_i b_{r(i)}(B_i) =W(b). \]
Thus \((\lambda,w)\in D(b)\).

Conversely, suppose \((\lambda,w)\in D(b)\) and \(h_i\geq x_i\lambda(B_i)\). Define \(\alpha_i=h_i/x_i-\lambda(B_i)\geq0\). The welfare complementary equalities give \(u_i+\alpha_i=w_{r(i)}\). Hence the welfare dual constraints imply
\[ \alpha_i+\lambda(C)\geq b_{r(i)}(C)-u_i. \]
This is a feasible blocking dual, and its objective equals
\[ s\cdot\lambda+\sum_i x_i\alpha_i =s\cdot\lambda+H-\sum_i x_i\lambda(B_i)=H. \]
Thus \(\Phi(u)\leq H\), proving core membership. ∎

3. Minimum-revenue core payments are item-price payments

Define the payment vector induced by a welfare dual as
\[ T_x(\lambda)_{rB}=x_{rB}\lambda(B). \]

For every \((\lambda,w)\in D(b)\), this vector satisfies the payment bounds: on positive allocation coordinates, \(\lambda(B)\leq b_r(B)\), and its empty-bundle payment is zero. The lemma shows that it is in the core.

Conversely, every core payment vector \(h\) has a supporting welfare dual with \(T_x(\lambda)\leq h\) coordinatewise. Replacing \(h\) by \(T_x(\lambda)\) preserves core membership and weakly lowers every payment. Therefore, if \(h\) minimizes total revenue, equality must hold in every coordinate.

It follows that the selected payments are computed by:

  • Minimizing \(s\cdot\lambda=\sum_{r,B}T_x(\lambda)_{rB}\) over \(D(b)\).
  • On that minimizing face, successively minimizing the coordinates of \(T_x(\lambda)\) in the specified payment order.

These are linear objectives. Thus the full payment selection uses at most \(K+1\) LPs.

4. A single LP gives the threshold

Let \(p_0=b_t(S)\), and let \((x^0,h^0)=O(p_0)\). Write \(W_0=W(b^0)\) and \(D_0=D(b^0)\), where \(b^0\) denotes the initial bids after cohort labeling. By the promise, \(x^0_{cS}=0\).

Define
\[ F_0=\left\{(\lambda,w)\in D_0: x^0_{rB}\lambda(B)=h^0_{rB}\ \text{for every }r,B\right\}. \]
Section 3 shows that \(F_0\) is nonempty. It is compact because it is a closed subset of \(D_0\).

Compute
\[ P=\max_{(\lambda,w)\in F_0}\bigl(w_c+\lambda(S)\bigr). \]
This is one rational LP. Its maximum is attained and finite, and its initial cohort constraint ensures \(P\geq p_0\).

Claim. The requested threshold is \(p^\star=P\).

Choose a maximizer \((\lambda^\dagger,w^\dagger)\in F_0\). For every \(p\in[p_0,P]\), it satisfies the changed cohort constraint \(w_c+\lambda(S)\geq p\). All other dual constraints are unchanged, and its dual objective is \(W_0\). Meanwhile, \(x^0\) retains primal objective \(W_0\), because \(x^0_{cS}=0\). Therefore \(x^0\) remains welfare-optimal throughout this interval.

If \(p<P\), the changed constraint is strictly slack at this dual solution. The nonnegative duality-gap formula then forces every optimal allocation at \(p\) to have \(x_{cS}=0\). Such an allocation has the same welfare under the initial bids, so it was initially optimal. Since \(x^0\) was the lexicographically smallest initial optimum, it remains the selected allocation.

On this interval, the optimal welfare dual set is exactly
\[ D(p)=D_0\cap\{(\lambda,w):w_c+\lambda(S)\geq p\}. \]
It contains \((\lambda^\dagger,w^\dagger)\), which reproduces \(h^0\). The original payment vector minimizes total payment and then lexicographic order over the price-payment vectors obtained from \(D_0\). It therefore remains selected over the subset obtained from \(D(p)\).

Thus \(O(p)=O(p_0)\) for every \(p_0\leq p<P\).

Now suppose \(p>P\) and nevertheless \(O(p)=O(p_0)\). Then \(x^0\) is selected and \(W(b^p)=W_0\). By Section 3, its selected minimum-revenue payments \(h^0\) must equal \(T_{x^0}(\lambda)\) for some optimal welfare dual at \(p\). That dual belongs to \(F_0\) and satisfies \(w_c+\lambda(S)\geq p>P\), contradicting the definition of \(P\).

Hence no bid above \(P\) preserves the outcome. Together with the preceding interval result and the fact that \(p_0\) itself preserves the outcome, this proves \(p^\star=P\).

5. Endpoint status

At \(p=P\), the original allocation remains welfare-optimal and the original payments remain feasible. Additional optimal allocations can, however, appear.

Compute the lexicographically selected welfare allocation \(x^P\) at the rational bid \(P\). Then
\[ O(p^\star)=O(p_0)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^P=x^0. \]
The forward implication is immediate. For the reverse implication, if the selected allocation remains \(x^0\), the payment argument in Section 4 still applies at \(P\): the smaller dual set contains a dual reproducing \(h^0\), so the same payments remain selected.

Both endpoint statuses can occur. For example, take one type of mass one, label the entire type as the cohort, and give each of two items supply one. List \(T=\{1\}\) before \(S=\{2\}\), with initial bids \(b(T)=1\) and \(b(S)=0\). Initially everyone receives \(T\), and minimum core revenue is zero. The threshold is \(1\). At that bid, lexicographically minimizing the \(T\)-coordinate assigns everyone \(S\), so the endpoint is excluded. Reversing the order of the two listed bundles includes the endpoint.

6. Exact arithmetic and running time

The complete algorithm performs:

  • At most \(K+1\) LPs to select the initial allocation.
  • At most \(K+1\) LPs to select the initial payments.
  • One LP to compute \(P\).
  • At most \(K+1\) LPs to select and compare the endpoint allocation.

Thus it uses at most \(3K+4=O(N+\tau)\) LP calls. Each has \(O(m+N+\tau)\) variables and constraints.

Exact rational LP optimization runs in polynomial bit time, and rational vertices have polynomial encoding length; see [Goemans’s linear programming notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-854j-advanced-algorithms-fall-2008/908fe54d659623efadda2d033b895caf_notes_lp.pdf). These facts apply here because all coefficients are rational and the relevant polytopes are nonempty and bounded.

More specifically, successive lexicographic minimizations restrict an original polytope to faces. Their optimum values are attained at vertices of that original polytope, so repeated tie-breaking does not cause uncontrolled growth of rational bit lengths. The initial allocation, welfare, and payments consequently have polynomial encoding length. The threshold LP then has polynomial encoding length, yielding a rational \(P\) of polynomial encoding length; the same argument applies to endpoint selection.

The total running time is therefore \((m+\tau+N+L)^{O(1)}\), and polynomial in the explicit input length \(L\). No enumeration of possible types or of unlisted bundles is required.
