Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite agent types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), item classes \(K\) with rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_k\), and additive utilities \(v_{\theta k}\), find nonnegative masses \(x_{\theta,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\subseteq K\) satisfying \(\sum_B x_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B:k\in B}x_{\theta,B}=\sigma_k\), such that every pair of positive-mass assignments \((\theta,B)\) and \((\theta',B')\) satisfies \(u_\theta(B)\ge u_\theta(B')\) or \(u_\theta(B\setminus\{k\})\ge u_\theta(B'\setminus\{k\})\) for some \(k\in B\cup B'\); output such an \(x\), with exact or approximate complexity guarantees.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \(\Theta\) with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_\theta\) a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Normalize \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\).
There are \(m=|K|\) item classes with binary rational per-capita supplies \(0\leq\sigma_k\leq1\). Each individual receives an integral bundle \(B\subseteq K\), possibly empty, containing at most one copy per class. Utilities are additive, with signed binary rational values \(v_{\theta k}\) and \(u_\theta(B)=\sum_{k\in B}v_{\theta k}\); zero values are allowed.
An allocation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(x_{\theta,B}\), represented by their nonzero entries, satisfying \(\sum_{B\subseteq K}x_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B:k\in B}x_{\theta,B}=\sigma_k\).
It is EF1 if every ordered pair \((\theta,B),(\theta',B')\) with positive assigned masses satisfies \(u_\theta(B)\geq u_\theta(B')\), or \(u_\theta(B\setminus\{k\})\geq u_\theta(B')\) for some \(k\in B\), or \(u_\theta(B)\geq u_\theta(B'\setminus\{k\})\) for some \(k\in B'\). Copies in different individuals' bundles are distinct, even when their classes coincide. Comparisons include assignments to the same type and use weak inequalities.

Is deciding whether such an EF1 allocation exists, and constructing one when it does, solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is feasibility NP-hard, where \(L\) is the total input bit length?

For finite agents and individually listed items with signed additive utilities, an EF1 allocation always exists and double round robin computes one in polynomial time. [Verified full version](https://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~haziz/fair_mixed_journal.pdf).
No result settling this precise support-mass problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the society's total mass to one so supplies are per-capita quantities.
  • Restricted supplies to the interval [0,1], as bundles contain at most one copy of each class; allowed zero supplies and empty bundles.
  • Specified signed binary rational utilities, including zero, and defined L as the total input bit length.
  • Interpreted EF1 as removing one copy from one individual's bundle. The graded formula removes from both bundles when a class occurs in both; distinct physical items in the discrete definition dictate the one-sided interpretation.
  • Selected exact feasibility and construction, including reporting infeasibility, because the discrete algorithmic anchor supplies no approximation target or existence guarantee for this mass model.
  • Specified a sparse rational representation of allocations to avoid requiring an exponentially indexed output table.

Partial results

I do not settle the general heterogeneous signed case. I prove a new polynomial-time construction for every valuation matrix of rank at most one, including types with opposite preferences. I also establish polynomial certificates, give two exact algorithms with explicit running-time bounds, and exhibit a feasible instance where rotating a strict envy cycle destroys EF1.

1. An exact criterion for EF1

For a type $\theta$ and bundles $B,C\subseteq K$, define
$$ a_\theta(B)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{-v_{\theta k}:k\in B\}\bigr), $$
$$ b_\theta(C)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{v_{\theta k}:k\in C\}\bigr). $$

The ordered comparison from an assignment $(\theta,B)$ to an assignment with bundle $C$ satisfies EF1 if and only if
$$ u_\theta(C)-u_\theta(B) \leq \max\{a_\theta(B),b_\theta(C)\}. $$

Indeed, deleting an item from $B$ can increase its utility by at most $a_\theta(B)$, whereas deleting an item from $C$ can decrease its evaluated utility by at most $b_\theta(C)$. If the utility difference is nonpositive, the direct comparison suffices. If it is positive and satisfies the displayed inequality, the positive maximum is attained by an actual item, giving a permitted deletion.

Common classes are retained in this calculation: deleting a physical copy from either bundle is a separate permissible operation.

2. Polynomial certificates

Proposition. Feasibility belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$. Whenever an EF1 allocation exists, one exists with at most $m+\tau$ nonzero entries, whose rational masses have polynomial bit length.

Proof. Put $n=m+\tau$. Let $S$ be the support of an EF1 allocation. Associate to each $(\theta,B)\in S$ the column
$$ c_{\theta,B}=(e_\theta,\mathbf 1_B)\in\{0,1\}^{n}, $$
where the first coordinates encode population totals and the remaining coordinates encode supplies.

Consider
$$ P_S= \left\{ y\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{S}: \sum_{(\theta,B)\in S}y_{\theta,B}c_{\theta,B} =(\mu,\sigma) \right\}. $$

This polytope is nonempty. Summing its population equations gives
$$ \sum_{(\theta,B)\in S}y_{\theta,B}=1, $$
so it is bounded and therefore has a vertex.

The columns corresponding to positive coordinates of a vertex are linearly independent. Otherwise, a nonzero linear dependence supported on those coordinates would permit sufficiently small perturbations in both directions, preserving nonnegativity and every equation. That contradicts extremality. Consequently, at most $n$ coordinates are positive.

To bound their encoding lengths, suppose there are $r\leq n$ positive coordinates. Select $r$ rows on which their columns form an invertible matrix $A$. Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the input masses and supplies. Its bit length is at most $L$.

Cramer’s rule expresses each positive coordinate as
$$ \frac{\det A_i}{D\det A}, $$
where $A_i$ replaces one column of $A$ by the corresponding entries of $D(\mu,\sigma)$. Since $A$ has binary entries,
$$ |\det A|\leq r^{r/2}. $$
Every entry of the replaced column lies between $0$ and $D$. Expansion along that column gives
$$ |\det A_i|\leq rD(r-1)!. $$
Thus every coordinate has bit length $O(L+r\log r)$.

Taking a subset of the original support preserves EF1 because it removes comparisons. The vertex therefore supplies the claimed certificate.

A verifier checks the population and supply equations and all ordered comparisons using the criterion in Section 1. There are at most $n^2$ comparisons, each involving polynomial-length exact rational arithmetic. Hence verification is polynomial. $\square$

3. Two exact algorithms

I use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: feasibility and optimization for an explicitly represented rational LP are polynomial in its encoding length; a nonempty bounded feasible polytope permits a basic feasible solution to be returned in polynomial time.

3.1. Enumerating sparse supports

Proposition. Decision and construction admit an exact algorithm with running time
$$ \binom{m+\tau}{m}\, 2^{m(m+\tau)} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

Proof. By Section 2, a feasible instance has a certificate whose support size is at most $n=m+\tau$.

Let $r_\theta$ denote the number of listed bundles for type $\theta$. Every type has positive mass, so it suffices to enumerate vectors satisfying
$$ r_\theta\geq1, \qquad \sum_\theta r_\theta\leq\tau+m. $$
Writing $s_\theta=r_\theta-1$, their number is
$$ \#\left\{s\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{\tau}: \sum_\theta s_\theta\leq m\right\} = \binom{\tau+m}{m}. $$

For each vector, enumerate an ordered list of $r_\theta$ bundles for each type. There are at most
$$ (2^m)^{\sum_\theta r_\theta} \leq 2^{m(m+\tau)} $$
such choices.

For each choice:

  • Check EF1 for every ordered pair of listed assignments.
  • If every comparison passes, solve the population and supply equations with nonnegative variables only on the listed assignments.

A feasible LP solution is EF1 because its positive support is a subset of a family whose comparisons all passed. Conversely, the support of a sparse EF1 allocation appears in the enumeration and witnesses feasibility of its LP.

Each candidate has at most $n$ variables and $n$ equality constraints. Its coefficients are binary and its right-hand side comes from the input. Thus its processing takes polynomial time. $\square$

For fixed $\tau$, this gives a running time of
$$ 2^{O(m^2)}\operatorname{poly}(m,L). $$

3.2. Enumerating global bundle families

There is also an exact algorithm polynomial in $\tau,L$ for every fixed $m$.

For a nonempty family $H\subseteq2^K$, define
$$ E_\theta(H)= \left\{ B\in H: u_\theta(C)-u_\theta(B) \leq \max\{a_\theta(B),b_\theta(C)\} \text{ for every }C\in H \right\}. $$

Solve the population and supply equations with nonnegative variables only for pairs $(\theta,B)$ with $B\in E_\theta(H)$.

Every feasible solution is EF1: its own bundles pass comparisons against every bundle in $H$. Conversely, if an EF1 allocation exists, choosing $H$ to be its actual family of used bundles makes every positive assignment an allowed variable.

There are fewer than $2^{2^m}$ families. Computing the sets $E_\theta(H)$ and solving each explicit LP takes time polynomial in $2^m,m,\tau,L$. The resulting bound is therefore
$$ 2^{2^m}\operatorname{poly}(2^m,m,\tau,L). $$
This retains the per-family dependence on $2^m$ that was omitted in the previous write-up. A basic feasible solution has at most $m+\tau$ positive entries.

4. A polynomial construction for rank-one valuations

Theorem. Suppose there are rational numbers $w_k$ and $\alpha_\theta$ such that
$$ v_{\theta k}=\alpha_\theta w_k \qquad \text{for every }\theta,k. $$
The numbers $\alpha_\theta$ and $w_k$ may have either sign or be zero. Every such instance admits an EF1 allocation, constructible in polynomial time using at most $\tau+m$ nonzero entries.

Furthermore, comparisons between types whose coefficients have different signs, including zero, satisfy exact envy-freeness.

This hypothesis is precisely that the valuation matrix has rank at most one.

4.1. Construction

Write
$$ W(B)=\sum_{k\in B}w_k. $$
Partition the types into
$$ P=\{\theta:\alpha_\theta>0\},\qquad Z=\{\theta:\alpha_\theta=0\},\qquad N=\{\theta:\alpha_\theta<0\}. $$

Maintain groups consisting of a type, a bundle, and a positive rational mass. Initially, each type has one group receiving the empty bundle.

Process each class $k$ once.

  • If $w_k>0$, order groups first from $P$, then $Z$, then $N$. Within each part, order them by increasing $W(B)$.
  • If $w_k<0$, order groups first from $N$, then $Z$, then $P$. Within each part, order them by decreasing $W(B)$.
  • If $w_k=0$, use any order.

Give class $k$ to a prefix of this ordering having total mass exactly $\sigma_k$. Take whole groups until the boundary, and split at most one boundary group.

4.2. Separation between the three parts

The construction maintains the following property: every assigned bundle of a type in $P$ has scalar utility at least that of every assigned bundle of a type in $Z$, and every assigned bundle of a type in $Z$ has scalar utility at least that of every assigned bundle of a type in $N$. Also, every assigned bundle in $P$ has scalar utility at least that of every assigned bundle in $N$.

Equivalently, with the order
$$ P\succ Z\succ N, $$
whenever $\theta$ is in a higher part than $\theta'$, their assigned bundles satisfy
$$ W(B)\geq W(C). $$

Initially all scalar utilities are zero.

Consider one update and two groups in different parts, the first in the higher part. Let $r_B,r_C\in\{0,1\}$ indicate receipt of the new class, treating the two portions of a split group separately.

If $w_k>0$, the prefix order implies $r_B\geq r_C$. Hence
$$ W(B\cup\text{new items})-W(C\cup\text{new items}) = W(B)-W(C)+w_k(r_B-r_C) \geq0. $$

If $w_k<0$, the reversed part order implies $r_B\leq r_C$. The same expression is again nonnegative. If $w_k=0$, it is unchanged.

Thus the separation property holds after every update.

It immediately gives exact envy-freeness between different parts:

  • A type in $P$ prefers weakly larger scalar utility, and its assigned bundles dominate those in $Z$ and $N$.
  • A type in $N$ prefers weakly smaller scalar utility, and its assigned bundles are dominated in scalar utility by those in $Z$ and $P$.
  • A type in $Z$ evaluates every bundle at zero.

4.3. EF1 within each part

For types in $Z$, every comparison is direct. It remains to consider comparisons within $P$ or within $N$.

Fix an evaluator $\theta$ in one of these two parts. The value of the new class to this evaluator is
$$ d=\alpha_\theta w_k. $$

Within its part, the ordering has the following properties:

  • If $d>0$, every recipient has weakly lower pre-update utility, as evaluated by $\theta$, than every nonrecipient.
  • If $d<0$, every recipient has weakly higher pre-update utility, as evaluated by $\theta$, than every nonrecipient.

To check this explicitly, types in $P$ evaluate scalar utilities in their original order, whereas types in $N$ evaluate them in reverse order. The algorithm orders scalar utilities increasingly when $w_k>0$ and decreasingly when $w_k<0$, giving exactly the two properties above.

Assume inductively that the pre-update allocation is EF1. Consider an ordered comparison within the part.

If both assignments receive the new class, both evaluated utilities increase by $d$. Every previous direct comparison or deletion witness remains valid. If neither receives it, the comparison is unchanged.

Suppose exactly one receives it.

If $d>0$:

  • A recipient’s comparison to a nonrecipient remains valid because its own utility increases.
  • For a nonrecipient comparing to a recipient, the selection order gives

$$ u_\theta(B_{\mathrm{nonrecipient}}) \geq u_\theta(B_{\mathrm{recipient}}) $$
before the update. Deleting the recipient’s new copy establishes EF1 afterward.

If $d<0$:

  • A nonrecipient’s comparison to a recipient remains valid because the evaluated utility of the other bundle decreases.
  • For a recipient comparing to a nonrecipient, the selection order gives the reverse pre-update utility ordering. Deleting the recipient’s own new copy restores a bundle whose utility is at least the nonrecipient’s utility.

These arguments also cover the two portions of a split group, whose pre-update bundles are identical. If $d=0$, no evaluated utility changes.

The initial allocation is envy-free. Induction proves EF1 within both parts, and Section 4.2 proves every remaining comparison.

4.4. Feasibility, representation, and complexity

Each update preserves every type’s total mass and all previously assigned supplies. It assigns class $k$ to exactly mass $\sigma_k$. Processing each class once ensures at most one copy of that class per individual.

Initially there are $\tau$ groups, and each class splits at most one group. Thus the final allocation has at most $\tau+m$ entries after merging duplicates.

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the input masses and supplies. All group masses remain integer multiples of $1/D$, obtained through additions and subtractions. Since each lies in $[0,1]$, its bit length is polynomial in $L$. Scalar utilities are sums of input rational values and also have polynomial bit length. Each of the $m$ rounds sorts at most $\tau+m$ groups.

Finally, the rank-one hypothesis can be checked and its factorization found in polynomial time. If the matrix is nonzero, take a nonzero row as $w$. For each type, determine $\alpha_\theta$ from one nonzero coordinate of $w$ and verify every remaining coordinate. If the matrix is zero, every feasible allocation is envy-free, and the same prefix-splitting construction applies.

This completes the polynomial construction. $\square$

5. A strict envy cycle whose rotation destroys EF1

The following example places the failure of utility-improving reassignment inside an actual feasible allocation.

There are five classes and three types, with valuations

| Type | $b_1$ | $b_2$ | $d$ | $c_1$ | $c_2$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $\theta_1$ | $-10$ | $5$ | $-4$ | $2$ | $2$ |
| $\theta_2$ | $1$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| $\theta_3$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ |

Take masses
$$ \mu_{\theta_1}=\mu_{\theta_2}=\frac14, \qquad \mu_{\theta_3}=\frac12, $$
and supplies
$$ \sigma_{b_1}=\sigma_{b_2}=\sigma_d=\frac14, \qquad \sigma_{c_1}=\sigma_{c_2}=\frac12. $$

Define
$$ B=\{b_1,b_2\},\qquad D=\{d\},\qquad C=\{c_1,c_2\}. $$
Assign all of $\theta_1$ to $B$, all of $\theta_2$ to $D$, and all of $\theta_3$ to $C$. This satisfies every population and supply equation.

It is EF1:

  • Type $\theta_1$ evaluates $B,D,C$ at $-5,-4,4$. Deleting $b_1$ from its own bundle leaves utility $5$, which suffices against both other bundles.
  • Type $\theta_2$ has own utility zero. Its only strict envy is toward $B$, and deleting $b_1$ from $B$ leaves evaluated utility zero.
  • Type $\theta_3$ evaluates everything at zero.
  • Comparisons within a type are direct because each type receives only one bundle.

There is a strict envy cycle:
$$ (\theta_1,B)\longrightarrow(\theta_2,D) \longrightarrow(\theta_1,B), $$
because
$$ -5<-4 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 0<1. $$

Swap the entire masses of these two assignments. Type totals and supplies remain unchanged, and both participating types strictly improve their utility.

Nevertheless, $\theta_1$ now receives $D$ and fails EF1 against $C$:
$$ u_{\theta_1}(D)=-4,\qquad u_{\theta_1}(C)=4. $$
Deleting its own item leaves utility zero, still below $4$. Deleting either item from $C$ leaves utility $2$, still above $-4$. These exhaust the permissible deletions.

Consequently, arbitrary strict envy-cycle rotation is not an EF1-preserving operation in this model. A general construction based on such rotations needs an additional preservation argument.
