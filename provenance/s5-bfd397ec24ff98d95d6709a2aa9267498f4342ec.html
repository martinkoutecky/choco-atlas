The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational \(\mu\in\Delta(\Theta)\), per-capita supplies \(\rho_g\) of finitely many good types, integral bundle configurations \(B\in\mathcal{B}\), monotone valuations \(v_\theta\), and \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), compute \(x_{\theta,B}\ge0\) and residual masses \(z_g\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_B x_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B}a_g(B)x_{\theta,B}+z_g=\rho_g\), minimizing \(\sum_g z_g\), subject to supported bundles satisfying \(v_\theta(B)\ge(1-\varepsilon)v_\theta(B'\setminus\{g\})\) for every supported pair and copy \(g\in B'\), no envy of the residual multiset, and \(\mathrm{NW}_\infty(x)\ge\frac{1}{2e^{1/e}}\mathrm{NW}_\infty^\star\); determine whether the optimum residual mass is zero and compute such an assignment.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) good types, integer configuration bounds \(K_g\geq1\), rational supplies \(0\leq\rho_g\leq K_g\), and rational \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\). Configurations are \(\mathcal B=\prod_g\{0,\ldots,K_g\}\); \(B_g\) counts copies of good type \(g\).
Each type has an explicitly tabulated monotone valuation \(v_\theta:\mathcal B\to\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\), with \(v_\theta(0)=0\) and distinct values on distinct configurations. Extend it to the box \(\prod_g[0,K_g]\) by multilinear interpolation on each unit cell, denoted \(\bar v_\theta\).
An assignment consists of real masses \(x_{\theta,B}\geq0\) and residuals \(z_g\geq0\), satisfying \(\sum_Bx_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B}B_gx_{\theta,B}+z_g=\rho_g\).
For every \(x_{\theta,B}>0\), \(x_{\theta',B'}>0\), and \(g\) with \(B'_g>0\), require \(v_\theta(B)\geq(1-\varepsilon)v_\theta(B'-e_g)\), where \(e_g\) removes one copy; also require \(v_\theta(B)\geq\bar v_\theta(z)\).
Define \(\mathrm{NW}_\infty(x)=\exp(\sum_{\theta,B:x_{\theta,B}>0}x_{\theta,B}\log v_\theta(B))\), taking it as zero if a supported bundle has zero value. Let \(\mathrm{NW}_\infty^\star\) be its maximum over assignments satisfying the mass and supply equations with \(z=0\), without fairness constraints.
Require \(\mathrm{NW}_\infty(x)\geq\mathrm{NW}_\infty^\star/(2e^{1/e})\), and minimize \(\sum_gz_g\); an infeasible instance has optimum \(+\infty\).
Is deciding whether the minimum residual mass is zero in \(\mathrm P\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and can a minimizing assignment, or infeasibility, be computed in polynomial time? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), \(|\mathcal B|\), the total input bit length \(L\), and \(1/\varepsilon\).
For finitely many agents, a polynomial-time algorithm produces partial \((1-\varepsilon)\)-EFX allocations with no envy of charity, \(\widetilde O(\sqrt{n/\varepsilon})\) unallocated goods, and the same Nash welfare factor; it does not assert minimum charity ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.07638)). No result settling exactly the continuous formulation above was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified the configuration domain as the full bounded integer box, including the empty bundle and every single-copy deletion, rather than an unspecified family.
  • Used explicit binary-rational valuation tables and included their entire encoding in L.
  • Normalized empty-bundle values to zero and carried over nondegeneracy as distinct values on distinct configurations.
  • Defined valuation of the rational residual vector by multilinear interpolation, which agrees with the discrete valuation at integral residuals; the discrete definition supplies no fractional extension.
  • Defined continuum Nash welfare as the mass-weighted geometric mean of realized bundle values, with zero welfare whenever positive mass receives zero value.
  • Defined the Nash welfare benchmark over complete assignments without fairness constraints, matching the unrestricted discrete benchmark.
  • Applied fairness and residual non-envy to every positively supported bundle, including comparisons between bundles assigned to the same type.
  • Allowed real assignment masses and treated infeasibility as infinite optimum.

Zero residual and exact minimization are NP-hard

1. Result

Theorem. Deciding whether the minimum residual mass is zero is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard, even when

$$ m=3,\qquad \varepsilon=\frac14, $$

all masses and valuation entries are dyadic rationals, and the instance is guaranteed to have a qualifying assignment.

Consequently, unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, no algorithm running in

$$ \operatorname{poly}\!\left(m,\tau,|\mathcal B|,L,1/\varepsilon\right) $$

can always compute an exact minimizing assignment, or report infeasibility.

The proof below is a polynomial-time reduction from Set Cover. It uses a strict welfare margin, so it does not require exact comparisons between logarithmic objectives.

2. Starting problem and numerical parameters

We use the classical $\mathrm{NP}$-complete Set Cover decision problem: given a universe $U=\{1,\ldots,r\}$, sets $S_1,\ldots,S_n\subseteq U$, and an integer $k$, decide whether at most $k$ sets cover $U$. Its hardness is established in [Karp’s original paper](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

We restrict to

$$ n,r\geq1,\qquad 1\leq k\leq n,\qquad \bigcup_{j=1}^nS_j=U. $$

These restrictions preserve hardness: excluded cases can be decided directly and mapped to fixed yes or no instances satisfying the restrictions. We may also delete duplicate element incidence patterns, since two elements contained in exactly the same sets impose the same covering requirement.

There are three good types. Write a configuration as $B=(a,b,c)$ and set

$$ K_0=2,\qquad K_1=K_2=H. $$

Define

$$ q=100(n+1)^2,\qquad s_j=q+3j,\qquad p=q+3(n+1),\qquad H=p+3. $$

The distinguished configurations are

$$ A=(2,0,0),\qquad E=(1,0,0), $$

and

$$ L=(0,0,H),\qquad B_j=(0,s_j,H-s_j),\qquad F=(0,p,H-p). $$

Their external coordinates, meaning their last two coordinates, form the set

$$ \mathcal C= \{(0,H)\} \cup\{(s_j,H-s_j):1\leq j\leq n\} \cup\{(p,H-p)\}. $$

Every member of $\mathcal C$ has coordinate sum $H$. Their first coordinates are separated by at least $3$. Let their downward closure be

$$ D=\{(b,c): (b,c)\leq C\text{ coordinatewise for some }C\in\mathcal C\}. $$

Choose powers of two

$$ \beta=2^{-\lceil\log_2(100nH)\rceil}, \qquad \sigma=2^{-\lceil\log_2(100rH)\rceil}, \qquad \alpha=\beta\sigma. $$

Set

$$ \eta=2n\beta+r\alpha,\qquad \mu_G=1-\eta. $$

The supported types are:

  • a guardian type $G$, of mass $\mu_G$;
  • a cost type $C_j$, of mass $\beta$, for each set $S_j$;
  • an element type $T_i$, of mass $\alpha$, for each element $i$;
  • a filler type $Q$, of mass $n\beta$.

Thus

$$ \tau=n+r+2, \qquad \mu_G+\sum_j\beta+\sum_i\alpha+n\beta=1. $$

The following bounds will be used repeatedly:

$$ r\alpha H\leq\frac{\beta}{100}, $$

and

$$ H\eta \leq \frac{2}{100}+\frac{1}{10000nH} <\frac3{100}, \qquad \eta<\frac1{100}. $$

In particular, $\mu_G>H\eta$ and $\mu_G-\eta>0$.

Define the supplies by

$$ T=k\beta(q+3n)+r\alpha H, $$

$$ \rho_0=2\mu_G,\qquad \rho_1=T,\qquad \rho_2=H\eta-T. $$

They satisfy the required bounds. Indeed,

$$ \frac{T}{\beta} \leq k(q+3n)+\frac1{100} <(k+1)q, $$

because $k\leq n$ and $3n^2+1/100<q$. Also,

$$ \frac{T}{\beta} \leq n(q+3n)+\frac1{100} <np. $$

Consequently,

$$ 0<T<n\beta p<H\eta, $$

and therefore $0<\rho_1,\rho_2<H$, while $0<\rho_0<2$.

3. Explicit monotone valuations with distinct values

We first define integer-valued monotone functions $f_\theta$, then add a small perturbation that makes every table injective.

Let

$$ N=|\mathcal B|=3(H+1)^2, $$

and let $M$ be the smallest power of two with $M\geq N$. Define

$$ d(a,b,c)= \frac{a(H+1)^2+b(H+1)+c}{128M}. $$

Then

$$ d(0)=0,\qquad 0\leq d(B)<\frac1{128}<\frac1{100}. $$

The function $d$ is injective and strictly increases when any coordinate increases.

For every type, put

$$ v_\theta(B)=f_\theta(B)+d(B). $$

Here are the base functions.

Guardian valuation

For $C=(b,c)$, define

$$ f_G(a,b,c)= \begin{cases} 0,&(a,b,c)=(0,0,0),\\ 1,&a=0,\ C\in D,\ C\ne(0,0),\\ 4,&(a,b,c)=E,\\ 5,&(a,b,c)=A,\\ 8,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$

This is monotone. The set $D$ is downward closed, so an external configuration outside $D$ stays outside $D$ when increased. Increasing a pure anchor configuration to a mixed configuration raises its base value to $8$. All remaining comparisons respect the displayed order of values.

Element valuations

For each $i\in U$, define

$$ f_{T_i}(a,b,c)= \max\left\{ \mathbf1_{\{B\ne0\}}, \ 2\mathbf1_{\{a\geq1\}}, \ 4\mathbf1_{\{\exists j:\ i\in S_j,\ (b,c)\geq(s_j,H-s_j)\}} \right\}. $$

Each condition in this maximum describes an upward closed set, so $f_{T_i}$ is monotone.

Cost valuations

Let

$$ P_j=(s_j-1,H-s_j). $$

Define

$$ f_{C_j}(a,b,c)= \max\left\{ \mathbf1_{\{B\ne0\}}, \ 2\mathbf1_{\{(b,c)\geq P_j\}}, \ 4\mathbf1_{\{(b,c)\geq(s_j,H-s_j)\}} \right\}. $$

Again, all defining conditions are upward closed.

Filler valuation

Define

$$ f_Q(a,b,c)= \max\left\{ \mathbf1_{\{B\ne0\}}, \ 2\mathbf1_{\{a\geq1\}}, \ 4\mathbf1_{\{b+c\geq H\}} \right\}. $$

This is monotone as well.

Verification of the valuation assumptions

All $f_\theta$ are integer valued, monotone, and zero at $0$. Adding $d$ preserves monotonicity and leaves $v_\theta(0)=0$.

For different configurations with the same base value, injectivity follows from injectivity of $d$. For different base values, the integer difference has magnitude at least $1$, whereas the perturbation difference has magnitude less than $1/100$. Thus every valuation has distinct values on distinct configurations.

Every nonzero configuration has positive value, and uniformly,

$$ v_\theta(B)<9<16. $$

All table entries are dyadic rationals. After deletion of duplicate element incidence patterns, the supported valuation types are also pairwise distinct.

4. The benchmark is positive

There is always an unconstrained positive-welfare assignment with zero residual.

Give the guardian $A$. Give every other type the same distribution on $L$ and $F$, with probability

$$ \lambda=\frac{T}{\eta p} $$

on $F$. Since $0<T<n\beta p<\eta p$, we have $0<\lambda<1$.

The total mass of non-guardian types is $\eta$, so their external supplies are

$$ \eta\lambda p=T $$

and

$$ H\eta-T. $$

The guardian consumes $2\mu_G$ anchor goods. Thus all supply equations hold.

Every allocated configuration is nonzero and has positive value. Hence

$$ \mathrm{NW}_\infty^\star>0. $$

In particular, every qualifying assignment must exclude configuration $0$ from its support.

5. Every qualifying zero-residual assignment has a restricted form

Assume that $x$ is a qualifying assignment with $z=0$.

5.1. A guardian receiving bundle bounds all removals

The total external supply is $H\eta<\mu_G$. If every supported guardian configuration had at least one external good, guardian consumption of external goods would be at least $\mu_G$, a contradiction.

Therefore some supported guardian configuration has no external goods. Because zero configurations are excluded, it is $E$ or $A$. Its guardian value is at most

$$ 5+\frac1{100}<6. $$

A configuration whose base guardian value is $8$ has guardian value at least $8$. Thus fairness against this supported guardian receiving bundle forbids any supported donor configuration having a one-good removal with base guardian value $8$: that comparison would require a receiving value at least

$$ \frac34\cdot8=6. $$

Consequently, every one-good removal from every supported donor has guardian base value less than $8$.

5.2. Consequences for supported configurations

Consider first a pure external configuration $B=(0,b,c)$.

If $b+c\geq H+2$, each one-good removal has external coordinate sum greater than $H$, and hence lies outside $D$. Such a configuration is forbidden.

If $b+c=H+1$, both $b,c$ are positive because each is at most $H$. Its two removals have coordinate sum $H$, so membership in $D$ would require both removals to belong to $\mathcal C$. Their first coordinates differ by $1$, contradicting the separation of $\mathcal C$.

It follows that every supported pure external configuration has

$$ b+c\leq H. $$

Moreover, if $b+c=H$ and $b,c>0$, membership of both removals in $D$ requires a distinguished first coordinate in each of

$$ \{b-1,b\},\qquad \{b,b+1\}. $$

If $b$ itself is not distinguished, this would require both $b-1$ and $b+1$ to be distinguished, contradicting separation by at least $3$. At the endpoints, $(0,H)$ is distinguished, whereas $(H,0)$ is forbidden because the largest distinguished first coordinate is $p=H-3$.

Therefore the only supported pure external configurations of coordinate sum $H$ are

$$ L,\ B_1,\ldots,B_n,\ F. $$

Next consider a mixed configuration with $a>0$ and $b+c>0$.

  • If $a=2$, removing one anchor good leaves a mixed configuration with guardian base value $8$.
  • If $a=1$ and $b+c\geq2$, removing an external good can leave a mixed configuration with guardian base value $8$.

Hence a supported mixed configuration can only have

$$ a=1,\qquad b+c=1. $$

5.3. The supply equations force equality in a linear bound

Define

$$ w(a,b,c)=\frac a2+\frac{b+c}{H}. $$

The preceding restrictions imply $w(B)\leq1$ for every supported configuration. For mixed configurations the inequality is strict, since

$$ \frac12+\frac1H<1. $$

On the other hand, the zero-residual supply equations give

$$ \sum_{\theta,B}x_{\theta,B}w(B) = \frac{\rho_0}{2}+\frac{\rho_1+\rho_2}{H} = \mu_G+\eta = 1. $$

The total assignment mass is also $1$. Therefore every supported configuration must have $w(B)=1$. The support is consequently contained in

$$ \{A,L,B_1,\ldots,B_n,F\}. $$

The positive anchor supply implies that $A$ is supported. Removing an anchor good from $A$ produces $E$, whose guardian value is at least $4$. Thus every supported guardian receiving bundle must have value at least $3$.

Every distinguished external configuration has guardian value less than $1+1/100$. The guardian therefore receives only $A$, with its full mass $\mu_G$. This consumes the entire anchor supply. All other types receive only

$$ L,B_1,\ldots,B_n,F. $$

6. Zero residual implies a cover of size at most $k$

Let

$$ J=\{j:B_j\text{ is supported by some type}\}. $$

Element types force coverage

Fairness against donor $A$ requires every element type’s receiving value to be at least

$$ \frac34v_{T_i}(E)\geq\frac32. $$

Among the distinguished external configurations, type $T_i$ has base value $4$ exactly at those $B_j$ with $i\in S_j$. It has base value $1$ at all the others, and their values are less than $1+1/100$.

Thus every element type receives only configurations $B_j$ corresponding to sets containing that element. Since every element type has positive mass, $J$ covers $U$.

Supporting a set configuration incurs its entire cost mass

Fix $j\in J$. The supported donor $B_j$ has the removal

$$ B_j-e_1=(0,P_j), $$

whose value to cost type $C_j$ is at least $2$. Consequently, every receiving configuration supported by $C_j$ must have value at least $3/2$.

A distinguished external configuration $(0,t,H-t)$ dominates $P_j$ precisely when

$$ s_j-1\leq t\leq s_j. $$

By separation of distinguished first coordinates, only $B_j$ satisfies this condition. Hence all distinguished receiving configurations other than $B_j$ have cost-type value less than $1+1/100$.

It follows that the entire mass $\beta$ of type $C_j$ receives $B_j$.

Therefore,

$$ T=\rho_1 \geq \beta\sum_{j\in J}s_j \geq |J|\beta q. $$

But $T<(k+1)\beta q$. Since $|J|$ is an integer,

$$ |J|\leq k. $$

We have proved that a qualifying zero-residual assignment yields a Set Cover solution of size at most $k$. Fractional splitting does not avoid this implication: any positive support on $B_j$ forces the whole cost type $C_j$ onto $B_j$.

7. A cover yields a qualifying zero-residual assignment

Suppose $J$ covers $U$ and $|J|\leq k$. For each element $i$, choose $j(i)\in J$ such that $i\in S_{j(i)}$.

Assign:

  • the guardian its full mass on $A$;
  • cost type $C_j$ its full mass on $B_j$ if $j\in J$, and on $L$ otherwise;
  • element type $T_i$ its full mass on $B_{j(i)}$.

The first external-good consumption of these types is

$$ T_0 = \beta\sum_{j\in J}s_j + \alpha\sum_{i=1}^r s_{j(i)}. $$

It satisfies

$$ T_0 \leq k\beta(q+3n)+r\alpha H =T. $$

Give the filler mass

$$ u=\frac{T-T_0}{p} $$

on $F$, and its remaining mass $n\beta-u$ on $L$. Since $0\leq T-T_0\leq T<n\beta p$, this is valid.

The first external supply is now exactly $T$. Every non-guardian configuration has external cardinality $H$, so the second external supply is automatically $H\eta-T$. The anchor supply is also exact. Set $z=0$.

Fairness verification

For the guardian, receiving $A$ has value at least $5$. Removals from external donors have guardian base value $1$, and the removal from $A$ is $E$, with value less than $4+1/100$. All guardian comparisons hold.

For element types and the filler, receiving configurations have base value $4$. External removals have base value $1$ to these types, and $E$ has base value $2$. Their comparisons hold.

For an active cost type $C_j$ with $j\in J$, its receiving value has base $4$. Every relevant removal has base at most $2$, so its comparisons hold.

For an inactive cost type $C_\ell$, its receiving configuration is $L$, of value at least $1$. Every supported external donor is $L$, $F$, or some $B_j$ with $j\ne\ell$. None of their removals dominates $P_\ell$: such a removal has external cardinality $H-1$, so domination would require equality with $P_\ell$, which separation excludes. Their removal values, and the value of $E$, are less than $1+1/100$. Multiplication by $3/4$ gives a value less than $1$.

Thus all fairness inequalities hold. Charity inequalities hold because $z=0$.

Welfare verification

Write

$$ V_A=v_G(A),\qquad U^\star=\log\mathrm{NW}_\infty^\star. $$

We first bound the benchmark uniformly.

In any positive-welfare benchmark assignment, let $t$ be the guardian mass receiving at least one external good. The external supply gives $t\leq H\eta$. Guardian configurations without external goods have value at most $V_A$, and every valuation is less than $16$. Therefore

$$ U^\star \leq \mu_G\log V_A + H\eta\log(16/V_A) + \eta\log16. $$

The constructed assignment has log welfare at least $\mu_G\log V_A$, because all other receiving values are at least $1$. Since $V_A\geq5$,

$$ U^\star-\log\mathrm{NW}_\infty(x) < H\eta\log4+\eta\log16 < \frac1{10}. $$

Here we used $H\eta<3/100$, $\eta<1/100$, $\log4<2$, and $\log16<4$.

Also, $\log2>1/2$, as follows from integrating $1/t$ on $[1,2]$. Hence

$$ \mathrm{NW}_\infty(x)> \frac{\mathrm{NW}_\infty^\star}{2} > \frac{\mathrm{NW}_\infty^\star}{2e^{1/e}}. $$

The constructed zero-residual assignment qualifies.

8. Every constructed instance is feasible

This strengthens the reduction: the hard question concerns exact minimization even under a feasibility promise.

Give the guardian mass $\mu_G-\eta$ on $A$ and mass $\eta$ on $E$. Give every other type its full mass on $E$. Set

$$ z=(0,T,H\eta-T). $$

The anchor consumption is

$$ 2(\mu_G-\eta)+\eta+\eta=2\mu_G, $$

and no external goods are allocated. Thus all mass and supply equations hold.

All supported configurations are $E$ and $A$. Removing one good from them produces $0$ or $E$. Monotonicity therefore proves all fairness comparisons.

For charity, both external residual coordinates are less than $H\eta<3/100$, so $z$ lies in the external unit cell at anchor coordinate zero. For every type, the three nonzero corners of that cell have base value $1$. For the guardian this follows because $(1,1)\leq(p,3)\in\mathcal C$; for the other types their higher-value external thresholds have cardinality at least $H-1>2$.

Consequently, multilinear interpolation gives

$$ \bar v_\theta(z) \leq \left(1+\frac1{100}\right) \left(z_1+z_2-z_1z_2\right) \leq \left(1+\frac1{100}\right)H\eta <1. $$

Every receiving value is at least $1$, so charity inequalities hold.

Finally, put $V_E=v_G(E)$. Since $V_A<6$ and $V_E\geq4$, we have $V_A/V_E<2$. This assignment’s log welfare is at least

$$ (\mu_G-\eta)\log V_A+\eta\log V_E \geq \mu_G\log V_A-\eta\log2. $$

Using the benchmark bound from Section 7,

$$ U^\star-\log\mathrm{NW}_\infty(x) < H\eta\log4+\eta\log16+\eta\log2 < \frac{11}{100} < \log2. $$

It therefore satisfies even the stronger welfare factor $2$.

9. The minimum is attained

For completeness, we verify that zero residual cannot arise merely as an unattained infimum.

The mass and supply equations bound every variable:

$$ 0\leq x_{\theta,B}\leq\mu_\theta,\qquad 0\leq z_g\leq\rho_g. $$

Each numerically violated fairness comparison imposes the closed restriction

$$ x_{\theta,B}x_{\theta',B'}=0. $$

The charity condition is equivalently

$$ x_{\theta,B} \max\{\bar v_\theta(z)-v_\theta(B),0\}=0. $$

This is closed because multilinear interpolation is continuous; adjacent cell interpolants agree on their common face.

The benchmark is positive. Hence the welfare condition is equivalently the closed restrictions

$$ x_{\theta,0}=0 $$

for every type, and

$$ \sum_{\theta,B\ne0}x_{\theta,B}\log v_\theta(B) \geq U^\star-\log(2e^{1/e}). $$

Thus the qualifying set is closed and bounded. Section 8 proves it is nonempty. The continuous objective $\sum_gz_g$ therefore attains its minimum.

Since residuals are nonnegative, that minimum is zero exactly when a qualifying zero-residual assignment exists.

Combining Sections 6 and 7 proves

$$ \text{minimum residual is zero} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the Set Cover instance has a cover of size at most }k. $$

10. Bit complexity and optimization consequence

The construction uses

$$ m=3,\qquad \tau=n+r+2,\qquad 1/\varepsilon=4, $$

and

$$ H=O(n^2),\qquad |\mathcal B|=3(H+1)^2=O(n^4). $$

Every rational entry has bit length $O(\log(n+r+2))$. Thus the total output bit length satisfies

$$ L=O\!\left((n+r)|\mathcal B|\log(n+r+2)\right). $$

A direct tabulation checks at most $n$ distinguished configurations or incidence conditions per entry. It constructs the instance in

$$ O\!\left( \tau n|\mathcal B|\, \operatorname{poly}(\log(n+r+2)) \right) $$

bit operations. This is polynomial in the Set Cover input size.

Therefore zero-residual decision is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard with fixed $m$ and fixed $1/\varepsilon$. The hardness does not arise from an exponentially represented configuration space or from exact welfare comparisons.

Under the usual exact-output convention, an algorithm returning an exact minimizing assignment would reveal whether its residual sum is zero. Such an algorithm would decide Set Cover in polynomial time. Hence, unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, no algorithm with running time polynomial in $m,\tau,|\mathcal B|,L,1/\varepsilon$ can always return an exact minimizer or report infeasibility. This obstruction already holds on the constructed instances, all of which are feasible.
