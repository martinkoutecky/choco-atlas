The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(F\) and \(W\), compatible pairs \(E\subseteq F\times W\), rational type masses \(\alpha_f,\beta_w\) totaling \(1\) on each side, strict rankings of compatible types, and rational pair costs \(c_{fw}\), compute a minimum-cost stable assignment \(x_{fw}\geq 0\). Require \(\sum_w x_{fw}\leq\alpha_f\), \(\sum_f x_{fw}\leq\beta_w\), and, for every \(fw\in E\), require \(p_f(w;x)=0\) or \(p_w(f;x)=0\), where \(p_f(w;x)=\alpha_f-\sum_{w':\,w'\succeq_f w}x_{fw'}\) and \(p_w(f;x)=\beta_w-\sum_{f':\,f'\succeq_w f}x_{f'w}\). Minimize \(\sum_{fw\in E}c_{fw}x_{fw}\); characterize its complexity in the number of types, compatible pairs, and input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(F\) and \(W\) be disjoint finite type sets, with compatible pairs \(E\subseteq F\times W\); put \(m=|E|\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|F|+|W|\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Masses satisfy \(\sum_f\alpha_f=\sum_w\beta_w=1\). Each type strictly ranks all its compatible types, all of which are preferable to remaining unmatched. Pair costs \(c_{fw}\in\mathbb Q\) may have either sign.
An assignment has real coordinates \(x_{fw}\geq0\), with \(\sum_{w:fw\in E}x_{fw}\leq\alpha_f\) and \(\sum_{f:fw\in E}x_{fw}\leq\beta_w\); there are no additional pair capacities.
Define \(p_f(w;x)=\alpha_f-\sum_{w':fw'\in E,\,w'\succeq_f w}x_{fw'}\) and \(p_w(f;x)=\beta_w-\sum_{f':f'w\in E,\,f'\succeq_w f}x_{f'w}\), where \(\succeq\) includes equality.
An assignment is stable when every \(fw\in E\) satisfies \(p_f(w;x)=0\) or \(p_w(f;x)=0\). Its cost is \(\sum_{fw\in E}c_{fw}x_{fw}\).

Is computing a minimum-cost stable assignment polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Discrete minimum-cost stable edge sets with consistent, substitutable, cardinally monotone firm choices and quota-based strict worker preferences admit a strongly polynomial algorithm counting oracle calls ([Karzanov](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17067v3)).
The continuous question is already settled: [Dean and Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf) give a polynomial-time algorithm for minimum-cost bipartite stable allocation with strict preferences, including exactly the assignment model above.

Conclusion and the theorem used

Computing a minimum-cost stable assignment is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). This follows from Dean and Munshi’s continuous stable-allocation result, with a reduction that preserves both stability and cost.

We use the following structural result from [Dean and Munshi, Section 4, especially Lemmas 11–12](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf). For a bipartite stable-allocation instance with strict preferences, vertex quotas, and edge capacities, one can construct in polynomial time:

  • A stable allocation \(z^0\).
  • Polynomially many rotation vectors \(h^i\in\{-1,0,1\}^{E'}\), with multiplicities \(r_i>0\).
  • A directed acyclic graph of precedence relations.

Every stable allocation has the form \(z^0+\sum_i y_i h^i\), where \(0\leq y_i\leq r_i\), and every precedence arc \(i\to j\) imposes \(y_j>0\Longrightarrow y_i=r_i\). Conversely, every vector satisfying these conditions produces a stable allocation. With integer quotas and capacities, the construction gives integer \(z^0\) and \(r_i\); its allocation updates use additions, subtractions, and minima.

Below we verify the model correspondence and explain why this yields exact polynomial-time optimization for binary rational input.

Exact correspondence with stable allocation

For a feasible assignment \(x\), define its unmatched masses by

\(a_f=\alpha_f-\sum_{w:fw\in E}x_{fw}\), and \(b_w=\beta_w-\sum_{f:fw\in E}x_{fw}\).

Both are nonnegative. Strict preferences give the identities

\(p_f(w;x)=a_f+\sum_{w':\,w\succ_f w'}x_{fw'}\),

\(p_w(f;x)=b_w+\sum_{f':\,f\succ_w f'}x_{f'w}\).

Thus \(p_f(w;x)>0\) means that \(f\) has positive unmatched mass or positive allocation to a partner strictly worse than \(w\). The analogous statement holds for \(w\).

To represent unmatched mass within a model requiring exact vertex quotas, introduce a dummy firm \(f_0\) and a dummy worker \(w_0\), each with quota \(1\). Retain the original vertices and quotas, and add the edges \(fw_0\), \(f_0w\), and \(f_0w_0\). Set capacities to

\(u_{fw}=\min\{\alpha_f,\beta_w\}\), \(u_{fw_0}=\alpha_f\), \(u_{f_0w}=\beta_w\), and \(u_{f_0w_0}=1\).

These capacities add no restrictions on original coordinates: feasibility already implies \(x_{fw}\leq\min\{\alpha_f,\beta_w\}\).

Each real vertex ranks its dummy partner last. Each dummy ranks all real neighbors, in any strict order, ahead of the other dummy. Original edges retain their costs; dummy edges have cost zero.

Let \(T=\sum_{fw\in E}x_{fw}\). Extend \(x\) by

\(z_{fw}=x_{fw}\), \(z_{fw_0}=a_f\), \(z_{f_0w}=b_w\), and \(z_{f_0w_0}=T\).

Every real vertex has exactly its quota. Since both original sides have total mass \(1\),

\(\sum_f a_f=\sum_w b_w=1-T\),

so both dummy vertices also have exactly their quota. All edge capacities are respected.

Conversely, every allocation satisfying these exact quotas restricts to a feasible original assignment. Its real-to-dummy coordinates are necessarily \(a_f\) and \(b_w\), and the dummy-to-dummy coordinate is necessarily \(T\). Hence extension and restriction are inverse maps.

In the capacitated stable-allocation model, an edge blocks precisely when it is unsaturated and each endpoint has positive allocation to a strictly worse partner. For an original edge \(fw\), the amounts allocated to worse partners in the extended instance are exactly \(p_f(w;x)\) and \(p_w(f;x)\).

Moreover, if both quantities are positive, then \(x_{fw}<u_{fw}\). Indeed, equality \(x_{fw}=\min\{\alpha_f,\beta_w\}\) exhausts the quota of at least one endpoint, making that endpoint’s corresponding \(p\)-value zero.

Consequently, an original edge blocks in the extended instance exactly when both original \(p\)-values are positive. A dummy edge cannot block: a real endpoint ranks its dummy last, while on \(f_0w_0\) neither endpoint has a worse partner.

We have therefore proved a cost-preserving bijection between the stable assignments in the question and the stable allocations of the extended instance. The extended graph has \(N=\tau+2\) vertices and \(M=m+\tau+1\) edges.

Optimization over rotations, including signed costs

Apply the stated structural result to the extended instance. Write its edge-cost vector as \(\gamma\), including zero costs on dummy edges, and define

\(d_i=\sum_{e\in E'}\gamma_e h^i_e\).

The cost associated with a permissible rotation vector \(y\) is

\(\sum_{e\in E'}\gamma_e z^0_e+\sum_i d_i y_i\).

We first show that restricting each coordinate to \(y_i\in\{0,r_i\}\) does not worsen the optimum.

Suppose \(0<y_i<r_i\). Every immediate predecessor of \(i\) is fully applied, because \(y_i>0\). Every immediate successor has coordinate zero, because a positive successor would force \(y_i=r_i\). Therefore replacing \(y_i\) by either endpoint preserves all precedence conditions. Choose zero when \(d_i\geq0\), and choose \(r_i\) when \(d_i<0\). This does not increase cost. Repeating for all partial coordinates proves the assertion.

Thus optimization reduces to choosing a predecessor-closed subset of rotations, with rotation weights \(k_i=r_i d_i\). This finite problem handles arbitrary signs.

For completeness, it reduces to a minimum cut as follows. Put \(H=1+\sum_i|k_i|\). For every rotation with \(k_i<0\), add an arc from the source to \(i\) of capacity \(-k_i\); for every rotation with \(k_i>0\), add an arc from \(i\) to the sink of capacity \(k_i\). For every precedence arc \(i\to j\), add an arc \(j\to i\) of capacity \(H\).

Selecting no rotations gives a cut of capacity \(\sum_{k_i<0}(-k_i)<H\). Hence a minimum cut crosses no precedence arc of capacity \(H\). Its source-side rotations form a predecessor-closed subset \(S\), and its capacity is

\(\sum_{k_i<0}(-k_i)+\sum_{i\in S}k_i\).

Minimizing this capacity therefore minimizes the assignment cost.

Binary encoding and exact output

It remains to check that the algorithm is polynomial in input bit length, rather than in a potentially enormous population obtained by expanding masses.

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses. Its binary length is at most \(L+1\). Multiply every extended quota and capacity by \(D\). They become integers bounded by \(D\). Scaling allocations by \(D\) preserves feasibility and stability, and multiplies every cost by the same positive factor.

Run the rotation construction on this integer instance. Allocation coordinates and rotation multiplicities are integers bounded by \(D\). Capacity differences and augmentation updates therefore have polynomial binary length. The construction uses polynomially many operations and produces a polynomial-size precedence graph.

Let \(C\) be the product of the cost denominators, also of polynomial binary length. After multiplying rotation weights by \(C\), each is an integer of the form

\(Ck_i=r_i\sum_{e\in E'}h^i_e(C\gamma_e)\).

This expression has polynomial binary length: \(r_i\leq D\), and the sum contains at most \(M\) signed input-cost terms. The finite precedence capacity can now be chosen as \(1+\sum_i|Ck_i|\), which likewise has polynomial binary length. An exact polynomial-time minimum-cut algorithm therefore solves the closure problem in polynomial bit time.

Apply the selected rotations, divide the resulting allocation by \(D\), and restrict to original edges. The resulting rational assignment has polynomial-size coordinates. By the stability correspondence and the rotation optimization argument, it minimizes cost over all real stable assignments.

This proves the requested polynomial-time classification without expanding any supported type into individual agents.
