The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given commodities \(N\), a finite set of types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), endowments \(e_t\in\{0,1\}^N\), and integer valued utilities \(u_t:\{0,1\}^N\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty\}\) sharing a unimodular demand type \(D\), find a competitive equilibrium: a price vector \(p\) and assignment masses \(\lambda_{t,S}\ge 0\) such that \(\sum_S\lambda_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,S}\lambda_{t,S}\mathbf{1}_S=q=\sum_t\mu_t e_t\), and \(\lambda_{t,S}>0\) only when \(S\in\arg\max_R(u_t(R)-p\cdot\mathbf{1}_R)\). Each unit of mass receives an integral bundle.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(N\) be a set of \(m\geq1\) commodities. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
Each type has an endowment \(e_t\in\{0,1\}^N\) and a utility \(u_t:\{0,1\}^N\to\mathbb Z\cup\{-\infty\}\), encoded by an explicit list of acceptable bundles and their binary integer values; unlisted bundles have value \(-\infty\). Finite values may have either sign. Utilities are quasilinear, with no budget constraints.
Write \(A_t=\{S\subseteq N:u_t(\mathbf1_S)>-\infty\}\) and \(q=\sum_t\mu_te_t\). Promise that an assignment of acceptable bundles clearing \(q\) exists.
An explicitly supplied finite set \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^N\) consists of nonzero primitive vectors, is closed under negation, and spans \(\mathbb R^N\). Every linearly independent subset of \(D\) extends to an integer basis with determinant \(\pm1\).
For every type and every \(p\in\mathbb R^N\), each edge of the convex hull of \(\arg\max_{S\in A_t}\{u_t(\mathbf1_S)-p\cdot\mathbf1_S\}\) is parallel to a vector in \(D\); this is the common unimodular demand-type promise.
A competitive equilibrium consists of unrestricted rational prices \(p\in\mathbb Q^N\) and rational masses \(\lambda_{t,S}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\in A_t}\lambda_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\in A_t}\lambda_{t,S}\mathbf1_S=q\), with positive mass assigned only to utility-maximizing bundles at \(p\). Ties permit arbitrary splitting of mass; each individual receives an integral bundle.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including utilities and \(D\); report running times in \(m,\tau,|D|,L\).

Is finding an exact competitive equilibrium for this promised continuous problem possible in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,|D|,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite societies with integer utilities and a common unimodular demand type, the universally convergent dynamic auction reaches integer equilibrium prices from any integer starting prices in finitely many rounds; this is a finite-convergence guarantee, without a polynomial-time bound. Result

Finding an exact competitive equilibrium is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,|D|,L\). In fact, the algorithm below works without the common unimodular demand-type promise.

The essential hypotheses are the explicit lists of acceptable bundles, unrestricted quasilinear prices, feasibility, and permission to split each type’s mass among bundles.

1. The welfare linear program

Write \(x_S=\mathbf1_S\), \(v_{t,S}=u_t(x_S)\), and \(K=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}|A_t|\). Throughout, \(\sum_{t,S}\) sums over all types and their acceptable bundles. Feasibility and positivity of every \(\mu_t\) imply that every \(A_t\) is nonempty.

Consider the linear program with one variable \(\lambda_{t,S}\) for each listed acceptable bundle:

\(\displaystyle (\mathrm P)\qquad \max_{\lambda}\ \sum_{t,S}v_{t,S}\lambda_{t,S}\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\in A_t}\lambda_{t,S}=\mu_t\qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,S}\lambda_{t,S}x_S=q,\)

\(\displaystyle \lambda_{t,S}\geq0\qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau,\ S\in A_t).\)

These are precisely the mass and commodity-clearing constraints in the question. Each variable represents the mass of individuals receiving the integral bundle \(x_S\).

The feasibility promise makes this LP nonempty. Moreover, every feasible variable satisfies \(0\leq\lambda_{t,S}\leq\mu_t\leq1\). Thus its feasible region is compact. All objective coefficients are finite integers, so the LP has a finite, attained optimum. Negative utility values cause no difficulty.

2. The dual variables provide prices

Associate an unrestricted variable \(\alpha_t\) with each type-mass equality and an unrestricted vector \(p\in\mathbb R^m\) with commodity clearing.

The Lagrangian is

\(\displaystyle \mathcal L(\lambda,\alpha,p)=\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t+p\cdot q+\sum_{t,S}\lambda_{t,S}(v_{t,S}-\alpha_t-p\cdot x_S).\)

Its supremum over nonnegative \(\lambda\) is finite exactly when every coefficient in the last sum is nonpositive. Consequently, the dual is

\(\displaystyle (\mathrm D)\qquad \min_{\alpha,p}\ \sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t+p\cdot q\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t+p\cdot x_S\geq v_{t,S}\qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau,\ S\in A_t).\)

Both \(\alpha\) and \(p\) are unrestricted in sign. The dual is feasible: take \(p=0\) and \(\alpha_t=\max_{S\in A_t}v_{t,S}\).

By strong LP duality, since the primal is feasible and has a finite optimum, the dual also attains an optimum, and the two optimal values coincide. This theorem does not require independent equality constraints or an interior feasible point.

3. Every optimal primal-dual pair is an equilibrium

Let \(\lambda^*\) and \((\alpha^*,p^*)\) be optimal solutions. Define the nonnegative dual slacks

\(\displaystyle s_{t,S}=\alpha_t^*+p^*\cdot x_S-v_{t,S}\geq0.\)

Using the primal equalities gives the exact identity

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,S}\lambda_{t,S}^*s_{t,S}=\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t^*+p^*\cdot q-\sum_{t,S}v_{t,S}\lambda_{t,S}^*.\)

The right-hand side is zero by equality of the optimal values. Every summand on the left is nonnegative. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \lambda_{t,S}^*>0\ \Longrightarrow\ s_{t,S}=0.\)

Fix a bundle \(S\) receiving positive mass from type \(t\). Its zero slack implies

\(\displaystyle v_{t,S}-p^*\cdot x_S=\alpha_t^*.\)

For every other acceptable bundle \(R\in A_t\), dual feasibility implies

\(\displaystyle v_{t,R}-p^*\cdot x_R\leq\alpha_t^*.\)

Thus \(S\) maximizes the required utility at prices \(p^*\). Unlisted bundles have utility \(-\infty\), so none can improve on \(S\).

The primal equalities ensure the correct type masses and exact commodity clearing. Hence \((p^*,\lambda^*)\) satisfies every equilibrium condition.

This respects the requirement that each individual receive an integral bundle: for each type, partition its mass into portions of sizes \(\lambda_{t,S}^*\), and give everyone in the corresponding portion bundle \(x_S\).

4. Exactness and running time

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly encoded rational LP can be solved in polynomial time in its total binary encoding length, with an exact rational optimal solution when its optimum is finite. The running time is measured in bit operations, and the output has polynomial encoding length. This is the classical polynomial-time LP result originating with [Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239).

Here its hypotheses can be checked directly.

First, computing \(q\) does not require expansion into a finite population. Write each mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/d_t\), and set \(Q=\prod_t d_t\). The binary length of \(Q\) is at most the sum of the denominator lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Each coordinate of \(q\) can be written as

\(\displaystyle q_j=\frac{\sum_t a_t(Q/d_t)e_{t,j}}{Q}.\)

Because the masses sum to one and endowment coordinates are binary, \(0\leq q_j\leq1\). Its numerator and denominator therefore have \(O(L)\) bits. Straightforward exact arithmetic computes all coordinates in \(O(m\tau L^2)\) bit operations.

Next, put \(r=m+\tau\). The primal has \(K\) variables and \(r\) equalities, with constraint-matrix entries in \(\{0,1\}\). The dual has \(r\) unrestricted variables and \(K\) inequalities, again with matrix entries in \(\{0,1\}\). All remaining coefficients are the supplied utilities, masses, and computed coordinates of \(q\).

Under a dense binary encoding, each LP consequently has length at most

\(\displaystyle H=O\!\left(rK+rL+L\right).\)

Since every acceptable bundle is explicitly listed, \(K\leq L\), so

\(\displaystyle H=O\!\left((m+\tau+1)L\right).\)

Let \(P\) be a fixed polynomial bounding the chosen exact rational LP algorithm. Reading the input, computing \(q\), and solving the two LPs takes

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(L+m\tau L^2+P\!\left((m+\tau+1)L\right)\right)\)

bit operations. This is polynomial in \(m,\tau,|D|,L\). The algorithm does not use \(D\); its encoding contributes only to reading the input and to \(L\).

Both LPs have rational data and finite attained optima, as established above. Their exact rational outputs therefore give the rational prices and rational masses required by the question.

5. Scope of the conclusion

The proof uses the continuous allocation rule exactly as specified. Splitting a type’s mass among bundles makes the welfare LP’s feasible points admissible allocations. There is no additional integer allocation constraint to enforce.

Thus the finite-convergence auction theorem is unnecessary for this computational question. The polynomial-time conclusion follows directly from an explicitly sized rational LP and its dual.
