The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given balanced rational supplies \(a_i,b_j\) over finite employer and worker types, rational productivities \(\alpha_{ij}\), and dummy types for reservation options, find a \(P\)-optimal stable mass matching: choose \(x_{ij}\ge 0\) and type payoffs \(u_i,v_j\ge 0\) such that \(\sum_j x_{ij}=a_i\), \(\sum_i x_{ij}=b_j\), \(u_i+v_j\ge\alpha_{ij}\), and \(x_{ij}>0\Rightarrow u_i+v_j=\alpha_{ij}\). Under the promise that the maximum-productivity type-flow matrix is unique, return a compatible flow and payoffs with employer payoffs componentwise maximal and worker payoffs componentwise minimal among stable solutions.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) employer and worker types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Let \(m\) be the number of employer types and \(L\) the total input bit length.
Real employer and worker supplies are positive and balanced, with common total \(S>0\); masses need not sum to one. Productivities \(\alpha_{ij}\) and reservation payoffs \(r_i,s_j\) are nonnegative binary rationals. Transfers between real matched types are unrestricted.
Add one dummy employer and one dummy worker, each of mass \(S\), with fixed zero payoffs; set \(\alpha_{i0}=r_i\), \(\alpha_{0j}=s_j\), and \(\alpha_{00}=0\). Include these types in \(m\) and \(\tau\).
A stable mass outcome consists of a flow \(x_{ij}\geq0\) and per-unit type payoffs \(u_i,v_j\geq0\), satisfying \(\sum_jx_{ij}=a_i\), \(\sum_ix_{ij}=b_j\), \(u_i+v_j\geq\alpha_{ij}\), and \(x_{ij}>0\Rightarrow u_i+v_j=\alpha_{ij}\), with \(u_0=v_0=0\).
Promise that the augmented feasible flow maximizing \(\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}x_{ij}\) is unique. A \(P\)-optimal outcome has every real employer payoff componentwise maximal and every real worker payoff componentwise minimal among stable outcomes.

Is computing a \(P\)-optimal stable mass outcome under this promise possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For discrete nondegenerate RiFle markets, the proposal algorithm returns a \(P\)-optimal stable outcome; in the fully flexible assignment model, uniqueness of the optimal assignment supplies the relevant condition. [Eriksson–Karlander](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9801096)
For a related model with integer multi-unit supplies and demands, competitive equilibria exist and restricted equilibrium prices form a complete lattice. [Jaume–Massó–Neme](https://bse.eu/sites/default/files/working_paper_pdfs/389revised.pdf)
The checked sources do not state a complexity classification for this exact rational-mass problem.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted productivities to nonnegative binary rationals, matching the discrete assignment model.
  • Used positive balanced real supplies with arbitrary common total, without probability normalization.
  • Made reservation payoffs explicit nonnegative rational inputs and represented outside options by aggregate dummy types with sufficient mass and fixed zero payoffs.
  • Applied the uniqueness promise to the entire augmented flow matrix, including dummy entries.
  • Interpreted type payoffs as uniform per-unit payoffs and employer/worker optimality as simultaneous componentwise extrema over real types.
  • Defined m as the employer support size and L as the total binary input length; both support counts include dummies.

Result

Computing a \(P\)-optimal stable mass outcome is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, the uniqueness promise is unnecessary.

The proof uses two rational linear programs: one computes an optimal flow, and the other selects the simultaneous employer maxima and worker minima.

1. Eliminate the dummy flows

Let \(I\) and \(J\) denote the real employer and worker types. Thus \(|I|=m-1\) and \(|J|=\tau-m-1\). Throughout, sums indexed by \(i\) and \(j\) range over \(I\) and \(J\).

Define the net surplus and the reservation-payoff baseline by

\[ c_{ij}=\alpha_{ij}-r_i-s_j, \qquad B=\sum_i a_i r_i+\sum_j b_j s_j. \]

The numbers \(c_{ij}\) may be negative.

For a nonnegative real-to-real flow \(q=(q_{ij})\), impose

\[ \sum_j q_{ij}\leq a_i, \qquad \sum_i q_{ij}\leq b_j. \]

Write \(T=\sum_{i,j}q_{ij}\). Its unique extension to an augmented feasible flow is

\[ x_{ij}=q_{ij},\qquad x_{i0}=a_i-\sum_jq_{ij},\qquad x_{0j}=b_j-\sum_iq_{ij},\qquad x_{00}=T. \]

Indeed, the real row and column equations hold by construction. Since both real sides have total mass \(S\), the dummy row has total \(S-T+T=S\), and so does the dummy column. All entries are nonnegative.

Conversely, any augmented feasible flow yields such a \(q\). The real row and column equations force \(x_{i0}\) and \(x_{0j}\); the dummy row then forces \(x_{00}=T\).

Its objective value is

\[ \sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}x_{ij} = \sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}q_{ij} +\sum_i r_i\left(a_i-\sum_jq_{ij}\right) +\sum_j s_j\left(b_j-\sum_iq_{ij}\right) = B+\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}q_{ij}. \]

Consequently, augmented optimal flows correspond exactly to solutions of the following linear program:

\[ \text{maximize }\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}q_{ij} \quad\text{subject to}\quad q_{ij}\geq0,\quad \sum_jq_{ij}\leq a_i,\quad \sum_iq_{ij}\leq b_j. \tag{P} \]

Let \(C\) be its optimal value. Its feasible set is nonempty because it contains the zero flow. It is closed and bounded because \(0\leq q_{ij}\leq a_i\), so the optimum is attained. Also, \(C\geq0\).

2. Stable payoffs are exactly optimal dual solutions

The dual of \((P)\) is

\[ \text{minimize }D(p,t):=\sum_i a_i p_i+\sum_j b_j t_j \quad\text{subject to}\quad p_i\geq0,\quad t_j\geq0,\quad p_i+t_j\geq c_{ij}. \tag{D} \]

The dual is feasible: take \(t_j=0\) and \(p_i=M\), where \(M=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{c_{ij}:i\in I,j\in J\}\bigr)\). Since \((P)\) is feasible and has a finite optimum, linear-programming strong duality gives an attained dual optimum equal to \(C\).

Associate real payoffs to dual variables by

\[ u_i=r_i+p_i,\qquad v_j=s_j+t_j, \]

and fix \(u_0=v_0=0\). Nonnegativity follows from the hypotheses on reservations and from \(p,t\geq0\). The real-pair inequalities follow because

\[ u_i+v_j-\alpha_{ij}=p_i+t_j-c_{ij}\geq0. \]

The dummy-pair inequalities are precisely \(p_i\geq0\) and \(t_j\geq0\).

To establish the required equalities on positive flows, use the exact duality-gap identity

\[ D(p,t)-\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}q_{ij} = \sum_i p_i\left(a_i-\sum_jq_{ij}\right) +\sum_j t_j\left(b_j-\sum_iq_{ij}\right) +\sum_{i,j}q_{ij}(p_i+t_j-c_{ij}). \tag{1} \]

Every summand is nonnegative for primal and dual feasible points.

If \(q\) and \((p,t)\) are optimal, the gap is zero. Thus every summand vanishes:

  • If \(q_{ij}>0\), then \(p_i+t_j=c_{ij}\), hence \(u_i+v_j=\alpha_{ij}\).
  • If \(x_{i0}>0\), then \(p_i=0\), hence \(u_i=r_i=\alpha_{i0}\).
  • If \(x_{0j}>0\), then \(t_j=0\), hence \(v_j=s_j=\alpha_{0j}\).
  • On the dummy–dummy pair, equality always holds.

The resulting outcome is therefore stable.

Conversely, suppose an augmented outcome is stable. Its dummy inequalities imply \(u_i\geq r_i\) and \(v_j\geq s_j\). Hence \(p_i=u_i-r_i\) and \(t_j=v_j-s_j\) are dual feasible. Stability makes every summand in (1) vanish. Therefore

\[ \sum_{i,j}c_{ij}q_{ij}=D(p,t). \]

Weak duality gives \(\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}q_{ij}\leq C\leq D(p,t)\), so both sides equal \(C\).

We have proved that stable outcomes correspond exactly to arbitrary pairs of optimal primal and dual solutions, extended as above. In particular, every optimal dual payoff vector works with every optimal flow.

3. The optimal dual face has the required lattice closure

Define

\[ \mathcal D^* = \left\{(p,t): p_i,t_j\geq0,\; p_i+t_j\geq c_{ij},\; D(p,t)=C \right\}. \]

This set is nonempty by strong duality. It is compact: it is closed, and positive supplies imply

\[ 0\leq p_i\leq \frac{C}{a_i}, \qquad 0\leq t_j\leq \frac{C}{b_j}. \]

Take \((p,t),(\widehat p,\widehat t)\in\mathcal D^*\). Define coordinatewise

\[ p_i^+=\max(p_i,\widehat p_i),\qquad t_j^-=\min(t_j,\widehat t_j), \]

and

\[ p_i^-=\min(p_i,\widehat p_i),\qquad t_j^+=\max(t_j,\widehat t_j). \]

Both \((p^+,t^-)\) and \((p^-,t^+)\) are dual feasible. For the first, choose whichever original vector supplies the smaller worker coordinate \(t_j^-\). The employer coordinate \(p_i^+\) is at least that vector’s employer coordinate, so their sum is at least \(c_{ij}\). For the second, choose the vector supplying \(p_i^-\); its worker coordinate is at most \(t_j^+\), giving the same conclusion.

Moreover, coordinatewise minimum plus maximum equals the sum of the two original coordinates. Consequently,

\[ D(p^+,t^-)+D(p^-,t^+) = D(p,t)+D(\widehat p,\widehat t) = 2C. \]

Each feasible dual objective is at least \(C\), by weak duality. Therefore both new objectives equal \(C\). In particular,

\[ (p^+,t^-)\in\mathcal D^*. \tag{2} \]

This proves the needed employer-maximizing, worker-minimizing closure directly for rational masses.

4. One additional LP selects all extrema simultaneously

Solve

\[ \text{maximize }H(p,t):=\sum_i p_i-\sum_j t_j \quad\text{subject to}\quad (p,t)\in\mathcal D^*. \tag{E} \]

Its feasible set is nonempty and compact, so an optimizer \((p^P,t^P)\) exists.

We claim that, for every \((p,t)\in\mathcal D^*\),

\[ p_i^P\geq p_i\quad\text{for all }i, \qquad t_j^P\leq t_j\quad\text{for all }j. \tag{3} \]

If any inequality failed, form the join from (2):

\[ \widetilde p_i=\max(p_i^P,p_i), \qquad \widetilde t_j=\min(t_j^P,t_j). \]

It belongs to \(\mathcal D^*\), and

\[ H(\widetilde p,\widetilde t)-H(p^P,t^P) = \sum_i(\widetilde p_i-p_i^P) +\sum_j(t_j^P-\widetilde t_j). \]

Every term is nonnegative. A failed inequality in (3) makes at least one term strictly positive, contradicting optimality in \((E)\). This proves (3).

Adding the fixed reservation payoffs preserves these comparisons. Thus \(u_i^P=r_i+p_i^P\) is simultaneously maximal for every real employer, and \(v_j^P=s_j+t_j^P\) is simultaneously minimal for every real worker among stable outcomes. Pairing these payoffs with an optimal flow gives a \(P\)-optimal stable outcome.

5. Exact polynomial-time complexity

Use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly specified LP with rational coefficients can be solved exactly in time polynomial in its dimensions and total binary encoding length. This is the bit-complexity formulation of polynomial-time LP solvability; see [Theorem 17.1 in these University of Washington lecture notes](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse521/22au/521-lecture-16.pdf).

The algorithm is:

  • Solve \((P)\) exactly, obtaining \(q^*\) and \(C\).
  • Solve \((E)\) exactly, obtaining \((p^P,t^P)\).
  • Extend \(q^*\) to \(x^*\), and output the payoffs defined above.

Here \((P)\) has \((m-1)(\tau-m-1)\) variables, at most \(m\tau\), and polynomially many constraints. Program \((E)\) has \(\tau-2\) variables and at most \(m\tau\) pair inequalities, together with nonnegativity and the equality \(D(p,t)=C\).

All constructed coefficients have polynomial encoding length. Each \(c_{ij}\) requires only a fixed number of rational arithmetic operations on input numbers. The value \(C\) also has polynomial encoding length: an optimal vertex of the bounded rational polytope \((P)\) is determined by independent tight constraints, and Cramer’s rule, after clearing denominators, bounds its coordinate bit lengths polynomially. Evaluating the rational objective at that vertex preserves a polynomial bound.

Thus the second LP also has polynomial encoding length, and both LP calls and the final rational arithmetic take time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). There is no expansion into individual units of mass.

Under the promise, the computed augmented optimal flow is the unique one. The proof and algorithm also work when several optimal flows exist.
