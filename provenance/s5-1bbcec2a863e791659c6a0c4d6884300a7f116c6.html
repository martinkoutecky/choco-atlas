The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite supported resident and hospital types with binary-rational masses, a resident-to-hospital population ratio, strict rankings over counterpart types, and integral per-unit quotas, consider atomless populations of those types. A feasible assignment must assign each resident unit at most once, assign every resident unit with lower quota one, and give each hospital unit an integral bundle meeting its quotas; type-level bundle frequencies may be fractional. Residents vote by comparing assigned hospital types, and each hospital unit compares its padded bundles using the paper's slot-correspondence rule. Does every feasible instance admit a popular assignment, and can one be computed in time polynomial in the supported types and input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(m\) be the number of hospital types and \(L\) the total input bit length. Normalize hospital mass to \(1\) and resident mass to the given positive binary-rational population ratio \(\rho\).
Each type specifies a strict ranking of mutually acceptable counterpart types. Resident type \(i\) has lower quota \(\ell_i\in\{0,1\}\) and upper quota \(1\); hospital type \(j\) has binary-encoded integer quotas \(0\leq\ell_j\leq u_j\). Distinct units of the same counterpart type are indifferent, and every acceptable type is preferred to \(\bot\).
Realize each type as a Lebesgue interval of its prescribed mass. An assignment measurably partitions hospital units into classes with integral bundles of distinct resident units, using measure-preserving bijections between occupied slot classes and disjoint resident subsets. Only acceptable pairs may occur; almost every hospital unit receives between \(\ell_j\) and \(u_j\) residents; each resident is assigned at most once, and almost every resident with \(\ell_i=1\) is assigned. Multiple residents of the same type may occupy one bundle.
Assignments may have real bundle frequencies. A computed assignment is represented by a finite list of nonzero binary-rational hospital-type/bundle frequencies, with bundles encoded as integer type-multiplicity vectors; these specify a realization by partitions and slot bijections.
Compare assignments on the same individual populations. Each resident votes \(+1\), \(-1\), or \(0\) according to its assigned hospital types. At each hospital unit, pad both bundles to \(u_j\) with \(\bot\), remove common resident identities and common dummy entries, and choose any bijection between the remaining entries; sum the hospital's type-comparison votes over that bijection. Equal types contribute \(0\).
Let \(\Delta(N,M,\mathrm{corr})\) be the resident vote integral plus the hospital slot-vote integral in favor of \(N\). A feasible assignment \(M\) is popular if \(\Delta(N,M,\mathrm{corr})\leq0\) for every feasible measurable assignment \(N\) and every measurable choice of slot correspondences.

Does every feasible instance admit a popular assignment? On inputs promised feasible, is deciding whether a popular assignment exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? Can a popular assignment be computed within the same polynomial bound whenever one exists?

For finite individual populations with strict preferences, feasibility guarantees a popular feasible matching computable in polynomial time ([Nasre et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2021.30)). A later discrete many-to-many extension computes a maximum-size popular critical matching in polynomial time ([Nasre et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2023.114281)).
No result settling this precise atomless problem was identified in the literature search.

  • Normalized total hospital mass to one and total resident mass to the positive binary-rational resident-to-hospital ratio.
  • Included mutual acceptability, resident lower quotas in {0,1}, and nonnegative integral hospital quotas with lower quota at most upper quota; quotas are binary encoded.
  • Made same-type units indifferent and ranked every acceptable counterpart above being unmatched.
  • Used fixed labelled Lebesgue populations and measure-preserving slot bijections to define individual assignments and prevent duplicate assignment of a resident.
  • Allowed repeated resident types within a hospital bundle, while requiring distinct resident identities.
  • Preserved cancellation of common resident identities rather than cancelling distinct residents merely because their types coincide; universally quantified all measurable slot correspondences.
  • Defined computational output through sparse rational bundle frequencies with binary-encoded multiplicities, while allowing real frequencies for existence and competing assignments.

1. Bookkeeping and measurable realization

Let $n=\tau-m$ be the number of supported resident types. Write $r_i$ for resident-type masses and $h_j$ for hospital-type masses. Thus
$$ \sum_i r_i=\rho, \qquad \sum_j h_j=1. $$

A bundle at a type $j$ hospital is a vector $b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}^n$. Its cardinality is
$$ s(b)=\sum_i b_i. $$
Only acceptable resident types may have positive coordinates, and feasibility requires
$$ \ell_j\leq s(b)\leq u_j. $$

For any measurable assignment, let $q_{jb}$ be the mass of type $j$ hospital units receiving bundle $b$. There are only finitely many possible bundles: the number may be enormous, but every coordinate is bounded by the finite integer $u_j$.

The assigned mass of resident type $i$ is
$$ R_i=\sum_{j,b}b_iq_{jb}. $$
Consequently, feasible bundle frequencies satisfy
$$ \sum_bq_{jb}=h_j, \qquad R_i\leq r_i, \qquad R_i=r_i\ \text{if }\ell_i=1. $$

Conversely, any nonnegative frequencies satisfying these conditions have a measurable realization. Partition each hospital population according to its bundle frequencies. For each bundle class, create its finitely many occupied slot classes. Partition each resident-type population into disjoint subsets of the required slot masses, leaving the remaining mass unassigned, and use measure-preserving bijections to fill the slots. Disjoint resident subsets ensure that no resident is used twice.

Here and below, sets and maps are considered modulo null sets. A positive-measure Lebesgue subset is measure-isomorphic, modulo null sets, to an interval of the same measure. One obtains such an identification through the cumulative-measure parametrization of the subset. This justifies the partitions and bijections used below.

2. A feasible instance without a popular assignment

There is one hospital type $h$, of mass $1$, with
$$ \ell_h=u_h=1. $$
There are three resident types $a,b,c$, with masses
$$ r_a=\frac14, \qquad r_b=\frac14, \qquad r_c=\frac12. $$
Every resident is mandatory and accepts $h$. The hospital ranking is
$$ a\succ_h b\succ_h c\succ_h\bot. $$

The instance is feasible by the realization construction above.

Fix an arbitrary feasible measurable assignment $M$. Let $H_a,H_b,H_c$ be its hospital populations receiving the respective resident types. Mandatory assignment and capacity one imply
$$ \mu(H_a)=\frac14, \qquad \mu(H_b)=\frac14, \qquad \mu(H_c)=\frac12. $$

Choose subsets $A\subseteq H_a$, $B\subseteq H_b$, and $C\subseteq H_c$, each of measure $\delta=\frac14$. Construct $N$ by moving the old residents of $A$ to $B$, those of $B$ to $C$, and those of $C$ to $A$, through measure-preserving bijections. Keep every other assignment unchanged.

Every hospital still receives one resident and every resident remains assigned. Every resident retains hospital type $h$, so resident votes vanish.

The hospital changes on $A,B,C$ are respectively
$$ a\longrightarrow c, \qquad b\longrightarrow a, \qquad c\longrightarrow b. $$
Their total vote is
$$ -\delta+\delta+\delta=\delta>0. $$
Capacity one makes the correspondence unique at every changed hospital.

Thus $M$ is not popular. Since $M$ was arbitrary, this feasible instance has no popular assignment, even with real bundle frequencies.

3. Polynomial-time popularity verification for arbitrary capacities

3.1. Resident classes relative to the candidate

Fix a feasible assignment $M$, represented by rational frequencies $q_{jb}$.

For each resident type $i$, let $G_i$ consist of its acceptable hospital types together with $\bot$. Partition its population by its old partner state $g\in G_i$. The class masses are
$$ a_{ij}=\sum_b b_iq_{jb}, \qquad a_{i\bot}=r_i-\sum_j a_{ij}. $$

Let
$$ D=\sum_i|G_i|\leq n(m+1). $$

Write $v_i(p,g)$ for the vote of resident type $i$ comparing new state $p$ against old state $g$, and $v_j(i,k)$ for the corresponding hospital vote comparing resident types. These votes belong to $\{-1,0,1\}$, with $\bot$ ranked last.

The challenger will assign residents from the old classes $(i,g)$ to new hospitals. Its resident vote is therefore determined by these class labels.

3.2. Two forms of hospital comparison

Consider an old type $j$ hospital bundle $b$, of cardinality $s=s(b)$, and a new bundle of cardinality $t$.

After common dummy entries are removed:

  • If $t\leq s$, there are $s$ old real entries, compared against $t$ new real entries and $s-t$ new dummy entries.
  • If $t\geq s$, there are $s$ old real entries and $t-s$ old dummy entries, compared against $t$ new real entries.

Common resident identities cause no difficulty for an upper bound: restore each canceled common identity as a comparison with itself. This adds zero hospital vote. That resident also has zero resident vote.

Thus every challenger and correspondence can be represented using one of these two forms.

3.3. The linear program

For each supplied group $(j,b)$, introduce nonnegative masses $\theta^-,\theta^+$ satisfying
$$ \theta^-+\theta^+=q_{jb}. $$
They represent the portions using respectively $t\leq s$ and $t\geq s$.

Within this group, suppress the indices $j,b$. Let $k$ range over resident types with $b_k>0$. Let $(i,g)$ range over old resident classes whose resident type $i$ accepts hospital type $j$.

For the first portion, introduce:

  • $z^-_{ig,k}$: new real entries from class $(i,g)$ compared with old type $k$ entries;
  • $d^-_k$: new dummy entries compared with old type $k$ entries.

Impose
$$ \sum_{i,g}z^-_{ig,k}+d^-_k=b_k\theta^-, $$
and
$$ \ell_j\theta^- \leq \sum_{k,i,g}z^-_{ig,k} \leq s\theta^-. $$

For the second portion, introduce:

  • $z^+_{ig,k}$: new real entries compared with old type $k$ entries;
  • $e^+_{ig}$: new real entries compared with old dummy entries.

Impose
$$ \sum_{i,g}z^+_{ig,k}=b_k\theta^+, $$
and
$$ 0\leq\sum_{i,g}e^+_{ig}\leq(u_j-s)\theta^+. $$
The new cardinality here is at least $s\geq\ell_j$, so the lower quota is automatic.

Finally, introduce newly unmatched resident masses $w_{ig}$ and impose
$$ w_{ig} + \sum_{j,b} \left( \sum_k \bigl(z^{jb,-}_{ig,k}+z^{jb,+}_{ig,k}\bigr) + e^{jb,+}_{ig} \right) =a_{ig}. $$
Absent variables in this sum are interpreted as zero. Set $w_{ig}=0$ whenever $\ell_i=1$.

For the group $(j,b)$, its vote contribution is
$$ \begin{aligned} W_{jb}={}& \sum_{k,i,g} \bigl(z^-_{ig,k}+z^+_{ig,k}\bigr) \bigl(v_i(j,g)+v_j(i,k)\bigr) \\ &-\sum_kd^-_k +\sum_{i,g}e^+_{ig}\bigl(v_i(j,g)+1\bigr). \end{aligned} $$
Indeed, replacing an old acceptable resident by a dummy contributes $-1$ at the hospital, and replacing an old dummy by an acceptable resident contributes $+1$.

Maximize
$$ \sum_{j,b}W_{jb} + \sum_{i,g}w_{ig}v_i(\bot,g). $$

3.4. Why integral bundles do not invalidate this LP

The variables above are aggregate slot masses. We must prove that they can be decomposed into integral bundles at individual hospitals.

For a positive $\theta^-$, divide its local variables by $\theta^-$. The resulting polytope is
$$ \sum_{i,g}z_{ig,k}+d_k=b_k, \qquad \ell_j\leq\sum_{k,i,g}z_{ig,k}\leq s, \qquad z,d\geq0. $$
This is an integral network-flow polytope. To see the network explicitly, supply $b_k$ units at each old-type node $k$. Send its flow either to an occupied sink, along parallel arcs labeled $(i,g)$, or to a dummy sink. Bound the total flow into the occupied sink between the integers $\ell_j$ and $s$.

All supplies and bounds are integral, so every vertex is integral. The polytope is bounded; hence every point is a finite convex combination of integral vertices.

For the positive $\theta^+$ portion, the normalized polytope is
$$ \sum_{i,g}z_{ig,k}=b_k, \qquad e\geq0, \qquad \sum_{i,g}e_{ig}\leq u_j-s. $$
It is a product of simplices with integral vertices.

An integral point in either polytope describes:

  • an integral new bundle satisfying the hospital quotas;
  • the old-state class of every new resident slot;
  • an allowed comparison of old and new entries after dummy cancellation.

Therefore every feasible LP vector decomposes into finitely many integral local configurations.

3.5. Avoiding accidental common resident identities

To realize such configurations, partition each old hospital group into its configuration classes. Partition each old resident class $(i,g)$ into the resident subsets demanded by the new slots.

We can choose every new slot bijection so that, almost everywhere, its resident was not an old resident of that same individual hospital.

Here is the required argument. Let a slot domain $S$ and its resident subset $R$ have equal positive measure $v$. Identify both with $[0,v)$ modulo null sets. Consider the family of bijections obtained by translation modulo $v$ on the resident interval.

For a fixed hospital unit, only finitely many residents are forbidden: its at most $u_j$ old residents. Each forbidden identity excludes at most one translation parameter. Thus the forbidden translation parameters have measure zero for that hospital. Fubini's theorem implies that almost every translation avoids all forbidden identities at almost every hospital unit.

There are finitely many new slot classes, so suitable translations can be chosen for all of them. Resident subsets are disjoint, ensuring distinct residents in every bundle and preventing repeated assignment.

Consequently, the prescribed hospital correspondences are allowed: no common resident identities need to be removed, and the dummy cancellation is exactly the one modeled above.

3.6. Exactness and running time

Every measurable challenger and allowed correspondence yields a feasible LP vector with the same vote, by recording its local comparisons and restoring canceled common identities as zero comparisons.

Conversely, every feasible LP vector is realizable by the integral decomposition and identity-avoidance construction above, with precisely its objective value.

Therefore the LP optimum is exactly
$$ \max_{N,\mathrm{corr}}\Delta(N,M,\mathrm{corr}). $$

The feasible region is nonempty because $N=M$ is permitted. It is bounded: all local slot masses are bounded by a capacity times an old group mass, and unmatched masses are bounded by resident class masses. The optimum is therefore attained and is at least zero. Hence $M$ is popular exactly when the optimum is zero.

If the supplied list has $Q$ groups, the LP has $O(QnD+QD+D)$ variables and polynomially many constraints. Capacities and multiplicities enter only as binary-encoded coefficients; no individual slots are expanded. Every rational coefficient has encoding length polynomial in the input and supplied-assignment lengths.

Exact rational linear programming therefore proves:

Theorem 1. Popularity of a supplied rational assignment is decidable in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$ and the assignment's encoding length, even when hospital capacities are binary encoded.

The proof also shows that the maximum opposing vote depends only on the bundle frequencies, not on their particular measurable realization.

4. Polynomial-size rational certificates

I now prove that allowing real frequencies does not create popular assignments that require superpolynomial rational representations.

4.1. A finite configuration dual

For this section, fix a popular assignment $M$, possibly with real frequencies.

For each positive old group $(j,b)$, let $\mathcal C_{jb}$ be its finite set of integral local configurations described in Section 3. A configuration $c$ has:

  • counts $d_{ig}(c)$ of new residents from old class $(i,g)$;
  • a total vote $C(c)$, including the assigned residents and hospital comparisons.

These satisfy
$$ d_{ig}(c)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}, \qquad \sum_{i,g}d_{ig}(c)\leq u_j, \qquad |C(c)|\leq2u_j. $$

The equivalent configuration LP has a variable $y_{jb,c}$ for the mass using configuration $c$. Its constraints are
$$ \sum_c y_{jb,c}=q_{jb} $$
and
$$ \sum_{j,b,c}d_{ig}(c)y_{jb,c}+w_{ig}=a_{ig}, $$
with $w_{ig}$ prohibited for mandatory residents.

Give the resident-class constraints dual variables $\alpha_{ig}$ and the hospital-group constraints dual variables $\lambda_{jb}$. The dual inequalities are
$$ \lambda_{jb} + \sum_{i,g}d_{ig}(c)\alpha_{ig} \geq C(c) $$
for every configuration, together with
$$ \alpha_{ig}\geq v_i(\bot,g) \quad\text{when }\ell_i=0. $$

Define
$$ F_{jb}(\alpha) = \max_{c\in\mathcal C_{jb}} \left( C(c)-\sum_{i,g}d_{ig}(c)\alpha_{ig} \right). $$
Eliminating $\lambda$ leaves the dual objective
$$ \Phi_M(\alpha) = \sum_{i,g}a_{ig}\alpha_{ig} + \sum_{j,b}q_{jb}F_{jb}(\alpha), $$
subject to the optional-resident inequalities.

The finite primal is feasible and bounded. Linear-programming strong duality and dual attainment apply, even though its right-hand sides may be real. Since $M$ is popular, its optimum is zero. Thus some $\alpha^*$ satisfies
$$ \Phi_M(\alpha^*)=0. $$

The key point is that $\alpha$ has only $D\leq n(m+1)$ coordinates.

4.2. A bounded-encoding lemma

Lemma. Suppose a nonempty polyhedron in $\mathbb R^D$ is defined by finitely many integer inequalities whose coefficients and right-hand sides have absolute value at most $B$. It contains a rational point of encoding length polynomial in $D$ and $\log(B+1)$, independently of the number of inequalities.

Proof. Choose its point $p$ of minimum Euclidean norm. Such a point exists because the polyhedron is closed and one may restrict to a sufficiently large compact ball.

Let $V$ be the span of the normals of constraints active at $p$. If $p$ had a nonzero component $h$ in $V^\perp$, then $p-\varepsilon h$ would satisfy the active constraints with equality. For sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, it would also satisfy every inactive constraint, since their number is finite and each is strict at $p$.

But
$$ \|p-\varepsilon h\|^2 = \|p\|^2-(2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2)\|h\|^2 < \|p\|^2 $$
for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, a contradiction. Thus $p\in V$.

Choose $r\leq D$ independent active normals spanning $V$, and let their matrix and right-hand side be $A,b$. Then
$$ p=A^{\mathsf T}(AA^{\mathsf T})^{-1}b. $$
This is rational. The integer entries of $AA^{\mathsf T}$ have absolute value at most $DB^2$, and
$$ |\det(AA^{\mathsf T})| \leq r!(DB^2)^r. $$
Cramer's rule gives polynomial bit bounds for the inverse and for $p$. If there are no active normals, the preceding argument gives $p=0$. $\square$

4.3. A small rational dual witness

For each positive group $(j,b)$, choose a configuration attaining $F_{jb}(\alpha^*)$. Impose all inequalities saying that this chosen configuration attains the maximum, together with the optional-resident inequalities. They define a polyhedron $P$ containing $\alpha^*$.

On $P$, every relevant $F_{jb}$ is affine, so $\Phi_M$ is affine.

Let $P_0$ be obtained by imposing equality on every defining inequality of $P$ active at $\alpha^*$. I claim that $\Phi_M$ is identically zero on $P_0$.

Indeed, for any $y\in P_0$, the point
$$ \alpha^*-\varepsilon(y-\alpha^*) $$
belongs to $P$ for sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$. Active inequalities remain equalities, while inactive inequalities remain satisfied by their strict slack at $\alpha^*$.

Since $\alpha^*$ minimizes the affine function $\Phi_M$ on $P$, both this point and $y$ have objective at least $\Phi_M(\alpha^*)$. Affinity then forces
$$ \Phi_M(y)=\Phi_M(\alpha^*)=0. $$

Set
$$ U=\max\{1,u_1,\ldots,u_m\}. $$
The defining inequalities of $P_0$ have integer coefficients and constants bounded in absolute value by $4U$: they compare affine functions with configuration counts bounded by $U$ and votes bounded by $2U$.

The lemma therefore supplies a rational $\widehat\alpha\in P_0$ of polynomial encoding length. In particular,
$$ \Phi_M(\widehat\alpha)=0. $$

This argument does not assume rational frequencies for $M$.

4.4. Replacing the real frequencies

Fix this rational $\widehat\alpha$. For every positive old group define
$$ G_{jb} = F_{jb}(\widehat\alpha) + \sum_i b_i \bigl(\widehat\alpha_{ij}-\widehat\alpha_{i\bot}\bigr), $$
where only acceptable pairs contribute.

Each $G_{jb}$ has polynomial rational encoding length. Indeed, $F_{jb}(\widehat\alpha)$ is one of finitely many values
$$ C(c)-\sum_{i,g}d_{ig}(c)\widehat\alpha_{ig}, $$
whose integer coefficients have polynomial bit length. Taking their maximum does not increase that bound.

The resident-class formulas give
$$ \Phi_M(\widehat\alpha) = \sum_i r_i\widehat\alpha_{i\bot} + \sum_{j,b}q_{jb}G_{jb}. $$

Now introduce frequency variables $p_{jb}$ using only bundles that occur positively in $M$, and impose
$$ \sum_b p_{jb}=h_j, $$
$$ \sum_{j,b}b_i p_{jb}\leq r_i, $$
with equality when $\ell_i=1$, and
$$ \sum_{j,b}G_{jb}p_{jb} = -\sum_i r_i\widehat\alpha_{i\bot}. $$
All variables are nonnegative.

This is a nonempty rational polytope: the original real frequencies $q_{jb}$ satisfy it. It is bounded because each hospital-type sum is fixed.

Choose a vertex $\widehat p$. It has at most
$$ m+n+1=\tau+1 $$
positive coordinates. To justify this bound, there are $m$ hospital-sum rows, $n$ resident-total rows, and one last equality. If more than $m+n+1$ columns corresponding to positive coordinates existed, they would be linearly dependent. A sufficiently small perturbation in both directions along that dependence would preserve every row value and nonnegativity, contradicting that $\widehat p$ is a vertex.

The positive coordinates of $\widehat p$ have polynomial encoding length. They are determined by a nonsingular system of at most $\tau+1$ active rows. Its coefficients and right-hand sides have polynomial rational encoding length, so Cramer's rule gives the required bound.

Realize $\widehat p$ as an assignment $\widehat M$. Its feasibility follows from Section 1. For its resident-class masses,
$$ \Phi_{\widehat M}(\widehat\alpha)=0. $$
The vector $\widehat\alpha$, together with
$$ \lambda_{jb}=F_{jb}(\widehat\alpha), $$
is a feasible configuration dual for $\widehat M$. Weak duality therefore bounds every challenger vote by zero. Hence $\widehat M$ is popular.

We have proved:

Theorem 2. If a popular measurable assignment exists, then a popular rational assignment exists with at most $\tau+1$ nonzero hospital-type/bundle frequencies and total encoding length polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

The replacement can use only bundles already occurring positively in the original assignment.

5. Complexity consequence and remaining questions

Corollary. Popular-assignment existence belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$, including when existence is quantified over measurable assignments with real frequencies.

A certificate is the polynomial-size rational frequency list supplied by Theorem 2. Its feasibility is checked through the bundle and marginal conditions in Section 1, and its popularity is checked by Theorem 1. Both checks take polynomial time. This conclusion also holds without the feasibility promise.

Thus the universal-existence question is answered negatively, and general verification and certificate size are resolved. The requested $\mathrm{P}$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$-hard classification and polynomial-time construction remain open in this argument.
