The continuous mirror question, as graded

There are \(m\ge1\) goods, each with one divisible unit of supply. Each type has a positive binary-rational budget per unit mass \(b_\theta\) and binary-rational linear utilities \(u_{\theta j}\ge0\). Every type values some good positively, and every good is valued positively by some type.
Compute exact rational prices \(p_j>0\) and aggregate spendings \(f_{\theta j}\ge0\), with \(f_{\theta j}=0\) whenever \(u_{\theta j}=0\), satisfying \(\sum_j f_{\theta j}=\mu_\theta b_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta f_{\theta j}=p_j\).
Optimality requires \(f_{\theta j}>0\Longrightarrow u_{\theta j}/p_j=\max_{k\in G}u_{\theta k}/p_k\); spending may be split arbitrarily among tied maximizers. The price normalization is \(\sum_jp_j=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta b_\theta\), with no additional normalization of budgets.

Conclusion

Computing the specified exact normalized Fisher equilibrium is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, it admits a strongly polynomial algorithm in the arithmetic model. The essential reduction replaces each support type by one buyer whose budget is its total mass-weighted budget.

1. Reduction to a finite Fisher market

Let \(\Theta\) denote the listed support, with \(|\Theta|=\tau\). For each \(\theta\in\Theta\), define
\(B_\theta=\mu_\theta b_\theta\).

Construct a Fisher market with:

  • one buyer for each \(\theta\in\Theta\), having budget \(B_\theta\);
  • the original \(m\) goods, each with one divisible unit of supply;
  • buyer utilities \(U_{\theta j}=u_{\theta j}\).

Every \(B_\theta\) is positive and rational, because both \(\mu_\theta\) and \(b_\theta\) are positive rationals. Every buyer values some good positively, and every good is valued positively by some buyer, exactly as required by the task.

This construction has polynomial encoding length. Indeed, if \(\mu_\theta=a/c\) and \(b_\theta=d/h\), their product can be represented as \(ad/(ch)\). Its numerator and denominator have bit lengths bounded by the sums of the corresponding input bit lengths. Summing over types, the constructed input has encoding length \(O(L)\). No expansion into individual agents is needed.

2. Applicable exact algorithm

Végh’s Theorem 6.4 computes an exact equilibrium for this finite linear Fisher market using
\(O\!\left(n^4+n^2(e+n\log n)\log n\right)\)
arithmetic operations, where \(n\) counts buyers and goods and \(e\) counts positive utility entries. Section 6.2 assumes a positive utility entry incident to every buyer and good, which we verified above. The accompanying encoding analysis establishes rational intermediate values of polynomially bounded encoding length. [Végh, Section 6.2 and Theorem 6.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.4882v5).

Apply this algorithm with
\(n=\tau+m\)
and
\(e=\left|\{(\theta,j):u_{\theta j}>0\}\right|\leq\tau m\).

Let \(p_j\) be its equilibrium prices and \(x_{\theta j}\) its allocations.

Every price is strictly positive. To verify this, suppose \(p_j=0\). Some buyer \(\theta\) has \(u_{\theta j}>0\). Adding any quantity of good \(j\) to a bundle costs nothing and increases that buyer’s utility. Consequently, the buyer’s utility maximization problem has no finite optimal bundle. This contradicts equilibrium optimality, since an equilibrium allocation supplies at most one unit of that good.

3. Conversion to the required spendings

Define
\(f_{\theta j}=p_jx_{\theta j}\).

The finite equilibrium spends every buyer’s budget, so
\(\sum_j f_{\theta j}=B_\theta=\mu_\theta b_\theta\).

Every good is fully allocated, hence
\(\sum_\theta f_{\theta j}=p_j\sum_\theta x_{\theta j}=p_j\).

It remains to verify the required optimality condition. For a buyer \(\theta\), suppose \(x_{\theta j}>0\) but
\(u_{\theta k}/p_k>u_{\theta j}/p_j\)
for some good \(k\). Choose spending \(0<\varepsilon\leq p_jx_{\theta j}\). Replacing \(\varepsilon/p_j\) units of good \(j\) by \(\varepsilon/p_k\) units of good \(k\) preserves expenditure and increases utility by
\(\varepsilon(u_{\theta k}/p_k-u_{\theta j}/p_j)>0\).
This contradicts optimality of the buyer’s bundle. Therefore
\(f_{\theta j}>0\Longrightarrow u_{\theta j}/p_j=\max_k u_{\theta k}/p_k\).

Because every buyer values some good positively and all prices are positive, this maximum is positive. Thus \(u_{\theta j}=0\) implies \(f_{\theta j}=0\). The argument permits arbitrary splitting among tied maximizers.

Finally, summing the spending identities gives the requested normalization:
\(\sum_jp_j=\sum_\theta\sum_j f_{\theta j}=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta b_\theta\).
No rescaling of budgets or prices is required.

4. Running time and exactness

Substituting the instance sizes into the theorem gives
\(O\!\left((m+\tau)^4+(m+\tau)^2(\tau m+(m+\tau)\log(m+\tau))\log(m+\tau)\right)\)
arithmetic operations.

Constructing the weighted budgets, converting allocations to spendings, and writing all entries require at most an additional \(O(\tau m+\tau)\) arithmetic operations.

The transformed input has length \(O(L)\), and the algorithm maintains rational numbers of encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Exact rational arithmetic therefore yields bit running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), including production of the exact rational output. This completely settles the stated complexity target.
