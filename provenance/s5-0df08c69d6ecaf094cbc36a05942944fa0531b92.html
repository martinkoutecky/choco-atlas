The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational mass distribution \(\mu\) over urgency types \(U=\{0,U_0\}\), choose \(q_{uv}\in[0,1]\) for each ordered type pair, where \(q_{uv}\) is the fraction of such encounters in which the first agent gets right of way and \(q_{uv}+q_{vu}=1\). Minimize \(J_\mu(q)=\sum_{u,v\in U}\mu_u\mu_v\bigl(q_{uv}v+(1-q_{uv})u\bigr)\), with each individual encounter still assigning the resource to one agent.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(U\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\) of \(\tau\) distinct urgency types, each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_u\), with \(\sum_{u\in U}\mu_u=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Two agents are sampled independently by type. Each interaction has \(m=2\) outcomes: exactly one agent incurs a unit delay costing its urgency, and the other incurs zero cost.
A centralized policy specifies \(q_{u,v}\in[0,1]\), the probability of delaying the first agent in ordered pair \((u,v)\), subject to \(q_{u,v}+q_{v,u}=1\) and \(q_{u,u}=1/2\). There are no bidding or karma constraints.
The objective is exact minimization of \(W_1^\mu(q)=\sum_{u,v\in U}\mu_u\mu_v\bigl(q_{u,v}u+(1-q_{u,v})v\bigr)\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is exact minimization of \(W_1^\mu\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite populations with independent identically distributed binary urgencies and symmetric random encounters, an explicit centralized policy minimizes expected aggregate delay cost, giving a polynomial-time algorithm ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.09198)).
Continuous-population karma models have a stationary Nash equilibrium existence theorem ([source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13235-023-00503-0)); this concerns constrained strategic policies rather than the optimization defined here. No exact complexity classification for this binary rational support formulation was located.

Result

Exact minimization is polynomial-time solvable for the specified \(m=2\) model, for arbitrary support size \(\tau\) and binary rational inputs. The optimal policy delays the agent with smaller urgency and uses probability \(1/2\) when the urgencies coincide.

Both the optimal policy and its exact rational objective value can be computed in polynomial time.

Optimality proof

Order the distinct support values as \(u_1<\cdots<u_\tau\), and write \(\mu_i=\mu_{u_i}>0\) and \(q_{ij}=q_{u_i,u_j}\).

For a diagonal pair, the objective contribution is fixed:

\(\mu_i^2\bigl(q_{ii}u_i+(1-q_{ii})u_i\bigr)=\mu_i^2u_i.\)

For \(i<j\), combine the contributions from the two ordered pairs. Using the required constraint \(q_{ji}=1-q_{ij}\), their sum is

\(\mu_i\mu_j\bigl(q_{ij}u_i+(1-q_{ij})u_j+q_{ji}u_j+(1-q_{ji})u_i\bigr)=2\mu_i\mu_j\bigl(q_{ij}u_i+(1-q_{ij})u_j\bigr).\)

Consequently,

\(W_1^\mu(q)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i^2u_i+2\sum_{i<j}\mu_i\mu_j\bigl(q_{ij}u_i+(1-q_{ij})u_j\bigr).\)

Define \(q^\star_{ij}=1\) for \(i<j\), \(q^\star_{ij}=0\) for \(i>j\), and \(q^\star_{ii}=1/2\). This policy is feasible: all entries belong to \([0,1]\), each reversed pair sums to \(1\), and the diagonal entries have the prescribed value.

Subtracting its objective value from that of any feasible policy gives the exact identity

\(W_1^\mu(q)-W_1^\mu(q^\star)=2\sum_{i<j}\mu_i\mu_j(u_j-u_i)(1-q_{ij}).\)

Every summand is nonnegative, because \(\mu_i,\mu_j>0\), \(u_j-u_i>0\), and \(q_{ij}\leq 1\). Thus \(q^\star\) is globally optimal.

Moreover, equality forces \(q_{ij}=1\) for every \(i<j\). The reversal and diagonal constraints then determine every other entry. Hence the optimal policy is unique, including when \(\tau=1\).

Its exact value is

\(\min_q W_1^\mu(q)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i^2u_i+2\sum_{i<j}\mu_i\mu_j u_i=\sum_{u,v\in U}\mu_u\mu_v\min\{u,v\}.\)

Exact bit complexity

Write the input rationals as \(u_i=a_i/b_i\) and \(\mu_i=c_i/d_i\), where the numerators are nonnegative integers and the denominators are positive integers. Reduced fractions are not required.

Policy computation. Comparing two urgencies requires comparing the integers \(a_i b_j\) and \(a_j b_i\). These products have \(O(L)\) bits. Sorting therefore takes polynomial time, and writing the entire policy takes \(O(\tau^2)\) constant-size rational entries.

Objective computation. Use the common denominator

\(D=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}b_i d_i^2.\)

Its binary length is \(O(L)\), since the sum of the input denominators’ binary lengths is at most \(L\), and each appears with exponent at most \(2\).

Each diagonal term has denominator \(b_i d_i^2\), which divides \(D\). Each off-diagonal term \(2\mu_i\mu_j u_i\), with \(i<j\), has denominator \(b_i d_i d_j\), which also divides \(D\). Thus the displayed optimal-value formula can be evaluated exactly as \(N/D\) by integer multiplication, exact division, and addition.

The intermediate integer sizes remain polynomially bounded. Indeed, all contributions are nonnegative, and normalization gives

\(0\leq W_1^\mu(q^\star)\leq \max_i u_i\sum_{i,j}\mu_i\mu_j=\max_i u_i.\)

Therefore every accumulated numerator is at most \(D\max_i u_i\), whose binary length is \(O(L)\). There are \(O(\tau^2)\) terms, and standard integer arithmetic consequently gives polynomial running time; schoolbook arithmetic suffices for an \(O(\tau^2L^2)\) bound. Reducing the final fraction by a greatest-common-divisor computation also takes polynomial time.

Because \(m=2\) is fixed in the stated problem, this establishes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).
