The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), an axis \(\triangleleft\), a finite set of single-peaked voter types \(t=(\succ_t,\ell_t)\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and target \(p\), choose \(x_{t,k}\ge0\) with \(\sum_k x_{t,k}=\mu_t\) to minimize \(\sum_{t,k}|k-\ell_t|x_{t,k}\), subject to \(p\) being an SP-AV winner after the mass transfer; equivalently, decide whether a transfer of cost at most \(B\) exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given a nonempty candidate set \(C\) of size \(m\), a linear axis \(\triangleleft\), and a target \(p\in C\), consider types \(t=(\succ_t,\ell_t)\), where \(\succ_t\) is a strict total ranking single-peaked on the supplied axis and \(\ell_t\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass \(\mu_t\) as a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A transfer chooses real masses \(x_{t,k}\geq0\), for \(k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\), with \(\sum_{k=0}^m x_{t,k}=\mu_t\). This mass retains ranking \(\succ_t\) and approves its top \(k\) candidates; both increases and decreases of approval counts are allowed.
Its cost is \(\sum_t\sum_{k=0}^m |k-\ell_t|x_{t,k}\). Under SP-AV, candidates with maximum total approving mass are winners, including all ties; empty and full approval sets are permitted without adjustment.
Given a nonnegative binary rational budget \(B\), decide whether a transfer of cost at most \(B\) makes \(p\) a winner.

Is this continuous SP-AV support-bribery decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For indivisible voters, support bribery with these unit costs is NP-hard even with single-peaked rankings ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.00387)). No result settling exactly this divisible-mass problem was located in the literature search.

Result

The problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, the same algorithm works for arbitrary strict rankings: single-peakedness is unnecessary.

An exact linear formulation

Let \(T\) be the supplied support, so \(|T|=\tau\). For each \(t\in T\), let \(r_t(c)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\) be the position of candidate \(c\) in \(\succ_t\). Define \(a_{t,k,c}\) to equal \(1\) when \(r_t(c)\leq k\), and \(0\) otherwise.

Thus \(a_{t,k,c}\) records whether mass of type \(t\) transferred to approval count \(k\) approves \(c\). In particular, \(a_{t,0,c}=0\) and \(a_{t,m,c}=1\), as required by the stipulated treatment of empty and full approval sets.

Introduce a real variable \(x_{t,k}\) for every \(t\in T\) and \(k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\). Consider the following linear feasibility problem:

  • Nonnegativity: \(x_{t,k}\geq0\) for every \(t,k\).
  • Conservation of each original type’s mass: \(\sum_{k=0}^{m}x_{t,k}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\in T\).
  • Winner conditions: \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(a_{t,k,p}-a_{t,k,c})x_{t,k}\geq0\) for every \(c\in C\setminus\{p\}\).
  • Budget: \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{k=0}^{m}|k-\ell_t|x_{t,k}\leq B\).

The quantities \(|k-\ell_t|\) are fixed integer coefficients, so the budget constraint is linear.

Proof of equivalence

Suppose first that a successful transfer of cost at most \(B\) exists. Use its transferred masses as the variables \(x_{t,k}\). Nonnegativity and conservation hold by the definition of a transfer.

The resulting approving mass of candidate \(c\) is exactly

\(\displaystyle S_c(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_{t,k,c}x_{t,k}.\)

Because all candidates attaining the maximum score are winners, \(p\) is a winner precisely when \(S_p(x)\geq S_c(x)\) for every \(c\neq p\). These are exactly the winner constraints. The transfer’s stated cost is exactly the left-hand side of the budget constraint. Consequently, the linear system is feasible.

Conversely, suppose the linear system has a real solution. For every original type \(t\), assign mass \(x_{t,k}\) to approval count \(k\), retaining ranking \(\succ_t\). Nonnegativity and conservation make this an allowed transfer. Its candidate scores are the quantities \(S_c(x)\) above, so the winner constraints make \(p\) a winner. The budget constraint ensures cost at most \(B\).

Therefore the instance is a yes-instance if and only if the linear system is feasible.

Polynomial running time and exactness

There are \(N=\tau(m+1)\) variables. Besides nonnegativity, there are \(\tau\) conservation equalities, \(m-1\) winner inequalities, and one budget inequality. Replacing each equality by two inequalities gives \(2\tau+m\) inequalities besides nonnegativity.

Every left-hand coefficient is an integer of absolute value at most \(m\): conservation coefficients are \(0\) or \(1\), winner coefficients belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and cost coefficients lie in \(\{0,\ldots,m\}\). The right-hand sides are the supplied rational masses, the rational budget, and zeros. Hence the entire system has binary encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and its coefficients can be constructed in polynomial time from the supplied rankings.

The polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming decides feasibility of an explicitly given rational linear system over the reals, in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. Thus it applies directly here, including when the feasible region has lower dimension or success requires exact ties or exact budget equality. See [Gács and Lovász, *Khachiyan’s linear programming algorithm*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0196677480900024).

This proves the claimed polynomial running time. The formulation is exact for divisible mass; it imposes no integrality restrictions corresponding to indivisible voters.
