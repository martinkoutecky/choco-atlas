The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and a convex rational worth function \(F\) on \([0,\mu]\) with \(F(0)=0\), let \(\sigma_t=\int_0^1 \partial_t F(\lambda\mu)\,d\lambda\). Find masses \(h_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) receiving payoff \(\ell_t+1\), where \(\ell_t=\lfloor\sigma_t\rfloor\), and assign the remaining mass of each type payoff \(\ell_t\). Require \(\sum_t(\ell_t\mu_t+h_t)=F(\mu)\) and, for every \(z\in[0,\mu]\), require \(\sum_t\bigl(\ell_t z_t+\max\{0,z_t-(\mu_t-h_t)\}\bigr)\ge F(z)\). Among feasible \(h\), minimize \(\sum_t\bigl((\mu_t-h_t)(\ell_t-\sigma_t)^2+h_t(\ell_t+1-\sigma_t)^2\bigr)\).

Result

The decision problem is NP-hard, even under all of the following additional restrictions:

  • The worth function has degree \(d=2\) and nonnegative coefficients.
  • It satisfies the stated increasing-marginal convexity condition.
  • The grand-coalition worth is \(F(\mu)=2\).
  • All masses and the threshold are dyadic rationals.
  • All permitted payoffs are positive.

The proof is a polynomial-time reduction from Independent Set. The crucial point is that feasibility will be equivalent to the low-paid masses having independent support.

1. Construction

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a simple undirected graph with \(V=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and let \(K\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\) be the requested independent-set size.

There are \(n\) vertex types and one additional type, called the reservoir. Choose

\[ k=\lceil\log_2(8n)\rceil,\qquad p=2^{-k},\qquad M=np,\qquad q=1-M. \]

Each vertex type has mass \(p\), and the reservoir has mass \(q\). Thus the masses sum to one, and

\[ 0<M\le \frac18,\qquad q\ge\frac78. \]

Write \(z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\) for the vertex masses in a coalition, and \(x\) for its reservoir mass. Define

\[ b=\frac{1}{4M},\qquad Q(z)=b\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}z_i z_j,\qquad W=Q(p,\ldots,p)=bp^2|E|. \]

For each vertex, put

\[ r_i=bp\,\deg(i)=\frac{\deg(i)}{4n},\qquad a_i^{*}=\frac{3/2-r_i}{q}. \]

The superscript distinguishes these fixed coefficients from the low-paid masses used below. In particular,

\[ 0\le r_i<\frac14,\qquad qa_i^{*}\ge\frac54. \]

Finally, define

\[ C=\frac{1}{q-M},\qquad S=p\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i^{*},\qquad c=-Cq-S-\frac{W}{q}. \]

The worth function is

\[ F(z,x)= 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_i+(2+c)x +Cx^2+x\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i^{*}z_i+Q(z). \]

These definitions give

\[ qS=\frac32M-2W. \]

Consequently,

\[ 2+c =2-\frac{q}{q-M}-\frac{3M/2-W}{q} \ge 2-\frac76-\frac{3}{14} =\frac{13}{21}>0. \]

Every nonzero coefficient of \(F\) is therefore positive.

The marginal worths are

\[ \partial_{z_i}F =2+a_i^{*}x+b\sum_{j:\{i,j\}\in E}z_j, \]

and

\[ \partial_xF =2+c+2Cx+\sum_i a_i^{*}z_i. \]

Each expression is nondecreasing in every coordinate, so the required convexity condition holds.

Clearly \(F(0)=0\). By the definition of \(c\),

\[ F(p,\ldots,p,q)=2. \]

Thus the construction satisfies every promise in the question.

2. Shapley densities and efficiency

For each vertex type,

\[ \sigma_i =2+\frac{qa_i^{*}+bp\,\deg(i)}2 =2+\frac34 =\frac{11}{4}. \]

Hence \(\ell_i=2\).

For the reservoir,

\[ \sigma_0 =2+c+Cq+\frac S2 =2-\frac S2-\frac Wq =2-\frac{3M}{4q}. \]

Set

\[ u=\frac{3M}{4q}. \]

Because \(0<u<1\), the reservoir has \(\ell_0=1\). No Shapley density is integral, so none of the high-paid masses is forced to vanish.

Let

\[ a_i=p-h_i,\qquad A=\sum_i a_i,\qquad H=\sum_i h_i=M-A. \]

Thus \(a_i\) is the low-paid mass of vertex type \(i\). Efficiency requires

\[ 2M+H+q+h_0=2, \]

which is equivalent to

\[ h_0=q-H. \]

The reservoir’s low-paid mass is consequently \(H\). Since \(0\le H\le M<q\), these reservoir masses always satisfy their bounds.

For a coalition \((z,x)\), define

\[ R(z)=\sum_i\max\{0,z_i-a_i\}. \]

Its payoff is

\[ P(z,x)=2\sum_i z_i+2x+R(z)-\min\{x,H\}. \]

Thus efficiency determines the reservoir allocation uniquely once the vertex allocations are chosen.

3. Exact characterization of feasibility

Lemma. An efficient allocation is feasible if and only if

\[ a_i a_j=0\qquad\text{for every }\{i,j\}\in E. \]

Equivalently, \(\{i:a_i>0\}\) must be an independent set.

Necessity

Consider the coalition with vertex masses \(z_i=a_i\) and reservoir mass \(x=0\). It contains only low-paid vertex mass, so \(R(a)=0\). Therefore

\[ P(a,0)-F(a,0)=-Q(a). \]

Feasibility requires \(Q(a)\le0\). But \(Q(a)\) is a sum of nonnegative terms with positive coefficient \(b\). Hence every edge satisfies \(a_i a_j=0\).

Sufficiency

Suppose \(a_i a_j=0\) on every edge. We prove the core inequality for every real coalition.

First, write \(R_i(z)=\max\{0,z_i-a_i\}\). For every edge, at least one endpoint has zero low-paid mass. Since \(z_i,z_j\le p\), this implies

\[ z_i z_j\le p\bigl(R_i(z)+R_j(z)\bigr). \]

Summing over edges gives

\[ Q(z) \le bp\sum_i\deg(i)R_i(z) \le\frac14R(z). \]

In particular, \(R(z)-Q(z)\ge0\).

The definition of \(c\) also gives the identity

\[ F(z,x)-2\sum_i z_i-2x = Q(z)-\frac{Wx}{q} -x\sum_i a_i^{*}(p-z_i)-Cx(q-x). \]

Let

\[ T(z)=\sum_i a_i^{*}(p-z_i)\ge0. \]

The core slack is therefore

\[ \Delta(z,x)=P(z,x)-F(z,x) = R(z)-Q(z)+Cx(q-x)+xT(z)+\frac{Wx}{q}-\min\{x,H\}. \]

Fix any \(z\). We check this slack at \(x=0,H,q\).

At \(x=0\),

\[ \Delta(z,0)=R(z)-Q(z)\ge0. \]

At \(x=H\),

\[ \Delta(z,H) = R(z)-Q(z)+CH(q-H)+HT(z)+\frac{WH}{q}-H. \]

Because \(H\le M\),

\[ C(q-H)\ge C(q-M)=1. \]

Every remaining term is nonnegative, so \(\Delta(z,H)\ge0\).

For \(x=q\), put \(\delta_i=p-z_i\). Since \(h_i=p-a_i\),

\[ R(z)-H=-\sum_i\min\{h_i,\delta_i\}. \]

Also, \(Q(z)\le W\), and

\[ qT(z)=\sum_i qa_i^{*}\delta_i \ge\sum_i\delta_i \ge\sum_i\min\{h_i,\delta_i\}. \]

It follows that

\[ \Delta(z,q)=R(z)-H+W-Q(z)+qT(z)\ge0. \]

Finally, on each interval \([0,H]\) and \([H,q]\), the function \(\Delta(z,x)\) is concave in \(x\): its quadratic term is \(-Cx^2\), and all other terms are affine there. A concave function is bounded below by the smaller of its endpoint values. The three endpoint checks therefore prove

\[ \Delta(z,x)\ge0 \qquad \text{for every }z\in[0,p]^n,\ x\in[0,q]. \]

This proves sufficiency and the lemma.

4. The objective measures independent-set size

For vertex types, the distances from the Shapley density are \(3/4\) and \(1/4\). Their total contribution is

\[ D_{\mathrm{vertices}} =\frac{9M}{16}-\frac H2. \]

The reservoir’s low-paid mass is \(H\), and its high-paid mass is \(q-H\). Its contribution is

\[ D_{\mathrm{reservoir}} =H(1-u)^2+(q-H)u^2 =qu^2+H(1-2u). \]

Combining these expressions yields

\[ D(h)=\frac{9M}{16q}+\kappa H, \qquad \kappa=\frac{1-4M}{2q}>0. \]

In fact, \(M\le1/8\) implies \(\kappa\ge1/4\).

Let \(\alpha(G)\) denote the maximum independent-set size. By the feasibility lemma,

\[ A=\sum_i a_i\le p\,\alpha(G). \]

Equality is attainable: choose a maximum independent set \(I\), set \(a_i=p\) for \(i\in I\), and set \(a_i=0\) otherwise. The lemma guarantees feasibility.

Therefore the minimum possible deviation is exactly

\[ D_{\min} =\frac{9M}{16q}+\kappa p\bigl(n-\alpha(G)\bigr). \]

Define the rational quantity

\[ D_K=\frac{9M}{16q}+\kappa p(n-K). \]

To ensure that the input threshold is dyadic, choose

\[ B= \frac{\left\lceil 2^{k+3}D_K\right\rceil}{2^{k+3}}. \]

The rounding error satisfies

\[ 0\le B-D_K<\frac p8<\kappa p. \]

If \(\alpha(G)\ge K\), then \(D_{\min}\le D_K\le B\).

If \(\alpha(G)\le K-1\), then

\[ D_{\min}\ge D_K+\kappa p>B. \]

Thus

\[ \alpha(G)\ge K \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed instance has a feasible allocation with }D(h)\le B. \]

This completes the reduction.

5. Encoding size and running times

The reduction has

\[ \tau=n+1,\qquad d=2,\qquad m=2n+|E|+2=O(\tau^2). \]

Every rational coefficient has \(O(\log n)\) bits. The masses and threshold also have \(O(\log n)\) bits.

With sparse monomial encoding, the output length is \(O((n+|E|)\log n)\). Even with dense exponent vectors, it is \(O((n+|E|)(n+\log n))\). Construction takes polynomial bit time, including writing those vectors.

Consequently, unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\), there is no algorithm with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,d,L\). This already holds for the degree-two instances above.

For completeness, the general problem admits an exact, though much slower, decision algorithm. Under the stated convexity condition, it runs in

\[ (2+m+\tau+d+L)^{\,2^{O(\tau)}} \]

bit operations. In particular, fixing the support size \(\tau\) gives polynomial-time solvability.

Here is the justification.

First, the Shapley densities can be computed exactly in polynomial bit time. A monomial \(c_\alpha z^\alpha\) of degree \(r=|\alpha|\), with \(\alpha_t>0\), contributes

\[ \frac{c_\alpha\alpha_t}{r}\,\mu^{\alpha-e_t} \]

to \(\sigma_t\).

Next, fix an allocation \(h\), and split each coordinate interval at \(\mu_t-h_t\). On each resulting box, the payoff function is affine. The increasing-marginal condition makes \(F\) convex in each coordinate separately. Hence \(F\) minus the payoff is separately convex, and its maximum on that box occurs at a vertex: replace one coordinate at a time by an endpoint that does not decrease its value.

It is therefore enough to check the core inequality at the at most \(3^\tau\) points satisfying

\[ z_t\in\{0,\mu_t-h_t,\mu_t\}. \]

At these three levels, the payoff contribution is respectively

\[ 0,\qquad \ell_t(\mu_t-h_t),\qquad \ell_t\mu_t+h_t. \]

Substitution gives polynomial inequalities in the \(\tau\) variables \(h_t\), of degree at most \(\max\{d,1\}\). The box constraints, efficiency, integral-density restrictions, and deviation threshold are linear. Thus the decision problem becomes nonemptiness of an explicitly described rational semialgebraic set.

Standard cylindrical algebraic decomposition decides such formulas with a doubly exponential bound in the number of variables; see the [CAD complexity analysis of England and Davenport](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.02494). Expanding the substitutions introduces at most \(m(d+1)^\tau\) terms per core constraint, with polynomial coefficient bit length. These bounds give the displayed running time.

The [discrete object-owner allocation result](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24906v2) therefore does not extend to this continuous polynomial representation.
