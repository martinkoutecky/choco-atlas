The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite doctor and hospital-position type sets, binary-rational masses, and each type’s strict ranking of acceptable opposite-side types plus an outside option, compute a stable mass flow that is doctor-optimal: for every doctor type and every preference-list prefix, the mass assigned within that prefix is at least as large as in any other stable flow. Stability requires that no mutually acceptable type pair has positive mass on both sides assigned to options they like less than each other. Report if no doctor-optimal stable flow exists.

Let \(D\) and \(H\) be finite sets of doctor types and hospital-position types, with positive binary-rational masses \(a_d\) and \(b_h\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|D|+|H|\) types with positive mass and their masses; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Set \(m=|H|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length. Masses need not sum to one, and the two sides may have unequal total mass.
Each type strictly ranks its acceptable opposite-side types above the outside option \(\emptyset\); omitted types are unacceptable. Lists may be empty. Each hospital position accommodates one unit of doctor mass.
A feasible flow consists of real numbers \(x_{dh}\geq 0\), zero on pairs that are not mutually acceptable, satisfying \(\sum_h x_{dh}\leq a_d\) and \(\sum_d x_{dh}\leq b_h\). Define outside masses \(x_{d\emptyset}=a_d-\sum_hx_{dh}\) and \(x_{\emptyset h}=b_h-\sum_dx_{dh}\).
A flow is stable if no mutually acceptable pair \((d,h)\) has both positive doctor mass assigned to options strictly below \(h\) and positive hospital-position mass assigned to options strictly below \(d\), counting the outside option on both sides. This condition also applies when \(x_{dh}>0\).
A stable flow \(x\) is doctor-optimal if, for every stable flow \(y\), every \(d\in D\), and every prefix \(S\) of \(d\)'s list including its final outside option, \(\sum_{h\in S}x_{dh}\geq\sum_{h\in S}y_{dh}\).

Conclusion

An exact doctor-optimal stable flow always exists and can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The corrected Baïou–Balinski results settle this question. The proof below verifies the correspondence, including the prefix definition of optimality and exact bit complexity.

1. The stable-allocation result used

For finite row and column sets, positive capacities, nonnegative entry bounds, and strict preferences, Baïou and Balinski establish existence and polynomial computation of a row-optimal stable allocation. Their stability condition at an entry below its bound requires either the row’s weakly better entries or the column’s weakly better entries to exhaust its capacity. Their row preference relation satisfies

\(z\succeq_d w\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z_{dh}<w_{dh}\ \Longrightarrow\ z_{dg}=0\text{ for every }g\text{ strictly worse than }h.\)

They also prove that every stable allocation assigns the same total mass to each row. These are the corrected paper’s Theorem 2, Lemma 8, Theorem 10, and Corollary 4. The algorithms use additions, subtractions, comparisons, and minima. [Corrected paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

We apply these results without any nondegeneracy assumption.

2. Exact reduction to stable allocation

Let \(E\subseteq D\times H\) consist of the mutually acceptable pairs. Restrict each preference list to these pairs, retaining its order. A removed option always receives zero mass, so deleting it changes neither stability nor any substantive prefix comparison.

If \(E=\varnothing\), the zero flow is the unique feasible flow and is doctor-optimal. Hence assume \(E\neq\varnothing\).

Write the input masses as binary-encoded fractions. Let \(Q\) be the product of their positive denominators, and define the positive integers

\(A_d=Qa_d,\qquad B_h=Qb_h,\qquad M=1+\sum_{d\in D}A_d+\sum_{h\in H}B_h.\)

Construct a stable-allocation instance with doctors as rows, hospitals as columns, capacities \(A_d,B_h\), and entry bound \(M\) on every pair in \(E\). Other pairs have bound zero and can be omitted.

A feasible original flow \(x\) corresponds exactly to an allocation \(z\) through \(z_{dh}=Qx_{dh}\). Indeed, the row and column constraints are precisely the scaled original constraints, and the additional entry bound is redundant: every feasible entry satisfies

\(z_{dh}\leq\min\{A_d,B_h\}<M.\)

No equality between total supply and total demand is required.

Stability is identical

For \((d,h)\in E\), define

\(R_{dh}=A_d-\sum_{g:\,g\succeq_d h}z_{dg},\qquad C_{dh}=B_h-\sum_{e:\,e\succeq_h d}z_{eh}.\)

Here the sums include the candidate partner itself and all strictly better partners.

By feasibility and the definitions of outside mass, \(R_{dh}\) is exactly the scaled doctor mass assigned below \(h\), including outside mass. Likewise, \(C_{dh}\) is exactly the scaled hospital mass assigned below \(d\), including outside mass. Both quantities are nonnegative.

Thus the question’s stability condition is equivalent to requiring, for every \((d,h)\in E\),

\(R_{dh}=0\quad\text{or}\quad C_{dh}=0.\)

Because every feasible entry is strictly below \(M\), this is precisely the stable-allocation condition stated above. This equivalence applies equally when \(z_{dh}>0\). Consequently, scaling gives a bijection between the two sets of stable solutions.

3. Row-optimality implies every required prefix inequality

Compute a row-optimal stable allocation \(z^\star\). Fix any other stable allocation \(w\) and any doctor \(d\). Define

\(\Delta_h=z^\star_{dh}-w_{dh}.\)

The equal-row-total property gives

\(\sum_{h\in H}\Delta_h=0.\)

Suppose \(\Delta_k<0\) and \(\Delta_j>0\). Then \(z^\star_{dk}<w_{dk}\) and \(z^\star_{dj}>0\). By row-optimality and the preference relation in Section 1, \(j\) cannot be strictly worse than \(k\). The entries are distinct, and preferences are strict, so every positive difference occurs strictly above every negative difference.

Now let \(S\) be a prefix of the doctor’s restricted acceptable list.

If \(S\) contains no negative difference, then \(\sum_{h\in S}\Delta_h\geq0\) directly. Otherwise, \(S\) contains some negative difference, so it contains every positive difference. All differences outside \(S\) are therefore nonpositive, and

\(\sum_{h\in S}\Delta_h=-\sum_{h\notin S}\Delta_h\geq0.\)

This proves every acceptable-partner prefix inequality. Reintroducing deleted zero-mass options preserves those inequalities. The prefix including the final outside option has total mass \(A_d\) under both allocations, so its inequality holds with equality.

Dividing by \(Q\), the flow \(x^\star_{dh}=z^\star_{dh}/Q\), with the prescribed outside masses, is doctor-optimal exactly as defined in the question.

4. Polynomial bit complexity

Set \(n=|D|=\tau-m\). The constructed instance has \(n\) rows, \(m\) columns, and at most \(nm\) permitted entries. Its construction requires only polynomial time.

The binary length of \(Q\) is at most the sum of the input denominator lengths, hence is \(O(L)\). Each scaled capacity and \(M\) consequently has \(O(L+\log\tau)\) bits.

Apply the inductive stable-allocation algorithm, followed by the row-optimal algorithm. Their number of arithmetic operations is polynomial in the constructed instance size, independently of capacity magnitudes.

Since their numerical operations are additions, subtractions, and minima, starting with integer data preserves integrality. Stored allocation entries are bounded by \(M\); capacities, residuals, and sums of entries have magnitude bounded by a polynomial in \(\tau\) times \(M\). Thus all numerical operands have polynomial bit length, and every arithmetic operation can be performed exactly in polynomial bit time.

Finally, division by \(Q\) yields rational output of polynomial encoding length. Computing outside masses also takes polynomial time.

Therefore the entire computation runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Under the stated hypotheses, the nonexistence outcome never occurs.
