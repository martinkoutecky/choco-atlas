The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\tau\) recipient types with masses \(\rho_\theta > 0\) satisfying \(\sum_\theta \rho_\theta = 1\), \(m\) uniformly arriving item types, and unknown sub-Gaussian reward laws with normalized means \(v_{\theta k} \in [a,b]\) satisfying \(\sum_k v_{\theta k} = 1\), choose before each arrival a nonnegative matrix \(x_t\) with \(\sum_\theta x_{t,\theta k} = 1\); assign an arriving type-\(k\) item to type \(\theta\) with probability \(x_{t,\theta k}\), then to a uniformly sampled member of that type, observing only its reward. Require with probability at least \(1 - 1/T\) that every type satisfies \(\sum_k x_{t,\theta k} v_{\theta k} / \rho_\theta \ge 1\) at every \(t < T\), and determine whether an algorithm can achieve regret \(T W^*(v) - \sum_t (1/m) \sum_{\theta,k} x_{t,\theta k} v_{\theta k}\) of \(\tilde{O}(\text{poly}(\tau,m,\rho_{\min}^{-1}) \sqrt{T})\) against the clairvoyant proportional optimum while computing each \(x_t\) in polynomial type-level time.

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) recipient types with positive binary-rational masses \(\rho_\theta\), where \(\sum_\theta\rho_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type is an atomless population with its normalized probability measure. Write \(\rho_{\min}=\min_\theta\rho_\theta\).

Result

Yes. A polynomial-time algorithm exists. In fact, it achieves the stronger bound
\(R_T=O_{a,b,\sigma}\!\left(\rho_{\min}^{-1}\sqrt{\tau mT\log(\tau mT)}\right)\)
jointly with proportionality at every round, with probability at least \(1-1/T\).

The algorithm uses a mass-proportional initial exploration phase, followed by one rational linear program per round. Its confidence sets explicitly impose normalization. A sufficiently large linear exploration bonus then compensates for the cost of enforcing proportionality robustly.

This proof does not use the exponential maximization in the cited [Schiffer–Zhang algorithm](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07022v1#S3.SS1).

1. Notation and normalized confidence sets

Write \(d=\tau m\), and define the unnormalized welfare
\(F_v(x)=\sum_{\theta,k}x_{\theta k}v_{\theta k}\).
Thus the optimal unnormalized welfare is \(V^*(v)=mW^*(v)\).

Let \(B_{\theta k}=\rho_\theta\). This allocation is feasible for every normalized mean matrix, because
\(\sum_\theta B_{\theta k}=1\) and
\(\sum_kB_{\theta k}v_{\theta k}=\rho_\theta\).
Moreover, \(F_v(B)=1\).

Consider estimates \(\widehat v_{\theta k}\) and positive rational radii \(r_{\theta k}\). Define
\(\ell_{\theta k}=\max\{a,\widehat v_{\theta k}-r_{\theta k}\}\) and
\(u_{\theta k}=\min\{b,\widehat v_{\theta k}+r_{\theta k}\}\).

For each type, use the confidence polytope
\(C_\theta=\{w\in\mathbb R^m:\ell_{\theta k}\leq w_k\leq u_{\theta k}\ \text{for all }k,\ \sum_kw_k=1\}\).

Assume for now that every \(C_\theta\) is nonempty and contains \(v_\theta\). Define
\(g_\theta(x_\theta)=\min_{w\in C_\theta}\sum_kx_{\theta k}w_k\)
and
\(f(x)=\sum_{\theta,k}x_{\theta k}r_{\theta k}\).

An allocation is robustly proportional if
\(g_\theta(x_\theta)\geq\rho_\theta\) for every type. Such an allocation is proportional for the true means. Also, \(B\) is robustly proportional: normalization gives \(B_\theta\cdot w=\rho_\theta\) for every \(w\in C_\theta\).

For every nonnegative row \(x_\theta\),
\(0\leq x_\theta\cdot v_\theta-g_\theta(x_\theta)\leq2\sum_kx_{\theta k}r_{\theta k}\).
Indeed, any two vectors in \(C_\theta\) differ in coordinate \(k\) by at most \(2r_{\theta k}\), and the minimum is attained because \(C_\theta\) is compact.

2. A robust proportionality repair lemma

Lemma. Suppose \(\max_{\theta,k}r_{\theta k}\leq a/4\). Let \(x\) be an allocation proportional for the true means. Then at least one of the following holds:

  • \(F_v(x)\leq1+(2b/a)f(x)\).
  • There exists a robustly proportional allocation \(z\) satisfying

\(F_v(z)\geq F_v(x)-(2b/a)f(x)\) and
\(f(z)\geq f(x)/2\).

Proof. Put
\(c_\theta=g_\theta(x_\theta)\),
\(d_\theta=(\rho_\theta-c_\theta)_+\),
\(s_\theta=(c_\theta-\rho_\theta)_+\),
\(D=\sum_\theta d_\theta\), and \(S=\sum_\theta s_\theta\).

Since \(x_\theta\cdot v_\theta\geq\rho_\theta\), the preceding confidence bound gives
\(d_\theta\leq2\sum_kx_{\theta k}r_{\theta k}\).
Consequently, \(D\leq2f(x)\).

If \(D=0\), \(x\) itself is robustly proportional, so conclusion 2 holds with \(z=x\). Hence suppose \(D>0\).

Case 1: \(S<(b/a)D\). We have
\(F_v(x)\leq\sum_\theta c_\theta+2f(x)=1+S-D+2f(x)\).
Using \(b/a\geq1\) and \(D\leq2f(x)\), this implies
\(F_v(x)\leq1+(b/a-1)D+2f(x)\leq1+(2b/a)f(x)\).
This proves conclusion 1.

Case 2: \(S\geq(b/a)D\). We transfer allocation from types with positive robust surplus to types with a robust deficit.

For a donor \(\theta\), let \(M_\theta=\sum_kx_{\theta k}\), and remove total allocation mass
\(q_\theta=(D/a)(s_\theta/S)\),
distributed proportionally across its row:
\(\delta_{\theta k}=(q_\theta/M_\theta)x_{\theta k}\).

These expressions are well-defined because a donor has \(c_\theta>\rho_\theta>0\), hence \(M_\theta>0\). They do not remove more than the donor’s allocation: since \(s_\theta\leq c_\theta\leq bM_\theta\),
\(q_\theta/M_\theta\leq bD/(aS)\leq1\).

Let \(A_k=\sum_{\theta:s_\theta>0}\delta_{\theta k}\). Give each deficit type \(\eta\) the added allocation \((d_\eta/D)A_k\) of item type \(k\). Leave other types unchanged. Call the resulting allocation \(z\).

The total removed mass is
\(\sum_kA_k=\sum_{\theta:s_\theta>0}q_\theta=D/a\).
The deficit weights sum to one, so every column of \(z\) still sums to one.

For a donor, homogeneity of \(g_\theta\) gives
\(g_\theta(z_\theta)=(1-q_\theta/M_\theta)c_\theta\geq c_\theta-bq_\theta\).
The case assumption implies \(bq_\theta\leq s_\theta\), so this is at least \(\rho_\theta\).

For a deficit type \(\eta\), every candidate mean coordinate is at least \(a\). Therefore,
\(g_\eta(z_\eta)\geq c_\eta+a(d_\eta/D)\sum_kA_k=c_\eta+d_\eta=\rho_\eta\).
Thus \(z\) is robustly proportional.

Only donor removals can decrease true welfare, and their total mass is \(D/a\). Hence
\(F_v(z)\geq F_v(x)-bD/a\geq F_v(x)-(2b/a)f(x)\).

Similarly, additions cannot decrease \(f\), so
\(f(z)\geq f(x)-(a/4)(D/a)=f(x)-D/4\geq f(x)/2\).
This proves conclusion 2. \(\square\)

3. A single linear bonus makes robust optimization optimistic

Set
\(K=4b/(a\rho_{\min})\)
and \(U_{\theta k}=\widehat v_{\theta k}+r_{\theta k}\).

Consider the optimization problem
\(\max_x\{\langle x,U\rangle+Kf(x):x\geq0,\ \sum_\theta x_{\theta k}=1,\ g_\theta(x_\theta)\geq\rho_\theta\ \text{for all }\theta\}\).

Proposition. Under the assumptions of the repair lemma, the optimum of this problem is at least \(V^*(v)\).

Proof. Let \(x^*\) attain \(V^*(v)\). Such an allocation exists because the true feasible set is nonempty and compact.

If conclusion 2 of the lemma holds, its allocation \(z\) satisfies, using \(U\geq v\),
\(\langle z,U\rangle+Kf(z)\geq V^*(v)-(2b/a)f(x^*)+(K/2)f(x^*)\geq V^*(v)\).
The last inequality follows from \(K/2\geq2b/a\).

Otherwise, conclusion 1 gives
\(V^*(v)\leq1+(2b/a)f(x^*)\).
Every allocation entry is at most one, so
\(f(B)=\sum_{\theta,k}\rho_\theta r_{\theta k}\geq\rho_{\min}f(x^*)\).
Consequently,
\(\langle B,U\rangle+Kf(B)\geq1+K\rho_{\min}f(x^*)\geq V^*(v)\).
The baseline is robustly proportional, so it is an admissible witness. \(\square\)

Let \(x\) maximize the robust bonus objective. Since
\(0\leq U_{\theta k}-v_{\theta k}\leq2r_{\theta k}\),
the proposition implies
\(V^*(v)-F_v(x)\leq(K+2)f(x)\).

After dividing by \(m\), this is the required one-round regret bound in terms of uncertainty on the allocation actually used.

4. Polynomial-size rational formulation

The robust optimization above is an ordinary rational LP after introducing dual variables.

For each type, the minimization defining \(g_\theta\) has dual
\(g_\theta(x_\theta)=\max\{\lambda_\theta+\sum_k\ell_{\theta k}\alpha_{\theta k}-\sum_ku_{\theta k}\beta_{\theta k}:\lambda_\theta+\alpha_{\theta k}-\beta_{\theta k}=x_{\theta k},\ \alpha_{\theta k},\beta_{\theta k}\geq0\}\),
where \(\lambda_\theta\) is unrestricted.

This follows by attaching multipliers \(\lambda_\theta\), \(\alpha_{\theta k}\), and \(\beta_{\theta k}\) to normalization, lower bounds, and upper bounds respectively. Strong LP duality applies because \(C_\theta\) is nonempty and compact, so the primal has a finite attained optimum.

Therefore, robust proportionality is equivalent to the existence of these variables satisfying
\(\lambda_\theta+\sum_k\ell_{\theta k}\alpha_{\theta k}-\sum_ku_{\theta k}\beta_{\theta k}\geq\rho_\theta\).

The resulting LP has \(O(\tau m)\) variables and constraints. It is feasible because it contains \(B\), and its objective is bounded because allocation entries lie in \([0,1]\). Rational linear programming admits exact optimization in polynomial bit complexity; see, for example, the [Princeton presentation of the LP-in-\(\mathrm P\) theorem](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~wayne/kleinberg-tardos/pdf/LinearProgrammingIII.pdf).

It remains to specify rational radii and prove the probability and cumulative regret bounds.

5. The algorithm

Let
\(h=\lceil\log_2(12dT^2)\rceil\),
\(N_0=\max\{1,\lceil128\sigma^2h/a^2\rceil\}\), and
\(H_0=\lceil(m/\rho_{\min})(2N_0+8h)\rceil\).

These integers are computable using rational arithmetic and integer comparisons.

For a positive sample count \(n\), let \(j_n\) be the least integer satisfying \(n2^{2j_n}\geq2h\), and define
\(r(n)=\sigma2^{j_n}\).
Then
\(\sigma\sqrt{2h/n}\leq r(n)<2\sigma\sqrt{2h/n}\).
In particular, \(n\geq N_0\) implies \(r(n)\leq a/4\).

The algorithm is:

  • Use \(B\) for the first \(\min\{T,H_0\}\) rounds.
  • If \(H_0<T\) and some pair has fewer than \(N_0\) observations after these rounds, use \(B\) for the remaining rounds.
  • Otherwise, at each remaining round compute pairwise empirical means and radii \(r_{\theta k}=r(N_{\theta k})\), and form the normalized confidence polytopes.
  • If a confidence polytope is empty, use \(B\) for that round. Otherwise solve the robust bonus LP from Section 3 and use its maximizing allocation.

Nonemptiness of a row confidence polytope is checked by
\(\ell_{\theta k}\leq u_{\theta k}\) for every coordinate and
\(\sum_k\ell_{\theta k}\leq1\leq\sum_ku_{\theta k}\).

Every step uses only the permitted history.

6. Simultaneous confidence and sufficient initial samples

For each pair, imagine generating its independent reward sequence in advance and revealing successive elements when that pair is selected. This represents the stipulated fresh-draw model. Its first \(n\) observations are independent draws from that pair’s stationary law, regardless of when they are revealed.

The sub-Gaussian assumption gives
\(\Pr(|\widehat v_{\theta k,n}-v_{\theta k}|>r(n))\leq2e^{-h}\).
A union bound over all \(d\) pairs and \(1\leq n\leq T\) yields a simultaneous confidence event \(E_C\) with
\(\Pr(E_C^c)\leq2dTe^{-h}\leq1/(6T)\).

On \(E_C\), every confidence polytope formed by the algorithm contains its true normalized mean row.

Now suppose \(H_0<T\). During the initial phase, a pair \((\theta,k)\) is selected independently each round with probability \(\rho_\theta/m\). Its count is therefore binomial, with expectation
\(\mu_{\theta k}=H_0\rho_\theta/m\geq2N_0+8h\).

For a binomial variable of mean \(\mu\), the multiplicative Chernoff bound states
\(\Pr(N\leq\mu/2)\leq e^{-\mu/8}\).
Since \(N_0\leq\mu_{\theta k}/2\), it follows that
\(\Pr(N_{\theta k}<N_0)\leq e^{-h}\).

Thus the event \(E_N\) that every pair has at least \(N_0\) initial observations satisfies
\(\Pr(E_N^c)\leq de^{-h}\leq1/(12T^2)\).
When \(H_0\geq T\), define \(E_N\) to hold automatically.

On \(E_C\cap E_N\), all initial allocations are proportional, and every subsequent allocation is robustly proportional for a confidence set containing the truth. Proportionality therefore holds at every round on this same event.

7. Cumulative uncertainty and regret

Let \(I_t\) indicate that round \(t\) uses the robust LP. Define \(Y_t\) to equal the radius of the selected pair when \(I_t=1\), and zero otherwise.

Before the item arrives, the choice of \(I_t\), the allocation, and all radii are determined by the history. Uniform item arrivals therefore give
\(\mathbb E[Y_t\mid\mathcal H_t]=I_tf(x_t)/m\).

Pathwise, \(0\leq Y_t\leq a/4\): the algorithm uses the LP only after all counts reach \(N_0\). Hence the martingale differences
\(\mathbb E[Y_t\mid\mathcal H_t]-Y_t\)
have absolute value at most \(a/4\). Azuma–Hoeffding gives an event \(E_M\), of probability at least \(1-1/(3T)\), on which
\(\sum_tI_tf(x_t)/m\leq\sum_tY_t+(a/4)\sqrt{2T\ln(3T)}\).

The realized sum has a deterministic bound. Whenever a pair is selected, its count increases by one. Thus, writing \(N_{\theta k}^T\) for final counts,
\(\sum_tY_t\leq2\sigma\sqrt{2h}\sum_{\theta,k}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\theta k}^T-1}n^{-1/2}\leq4\sigma\sqrt{2h}\sum_{\theta,k}\sqrt{N_{\theta k}^T}\).

Here \(\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}n^{-1/2}\leq2\sqrt N\), as follows by comparison with the integral of \(s^{-1/2}\). Since \(\sum_{\theta,k}N_{\theta k}^T=T\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\(\sum_tY_t\leq4\sigma\sqrt{2hdT}\).

On \(E_C\cap E_N\cap E_M\), every round after the initial phase uses the LP. Section 3 bounds its regret by \((K+2)f(x_t)/m\). Each initial round has regret at most \(b\), because \(W^*(v)\leq b\) and baseline welfare is nonnegative. Consequently,
\(R_T\leq b\min\{T,H_0\}+(K+2)\left(4\sigma\sqrt{2hdT}+(a/4)\sqrt{2T\ln(3T)}\right)\).

The joint event has probability at least
\(1-1/(6T)-1/(12T^2)-1/(3T)\geq1-1/T\).

Finally,
\(H_0=O_{a,\sigma}((m/\rho_{\min})h)\)
and
\(\min\{T,H_0\}\leq\sqrt{TH_0}\).
Together with \(K=4b/(a\rho_{\min})\) and \(h=O(\log(dT))\), this proves
\(R_T=O_{a,b,\sigma}\!\left(\rho_{\min}^{-1}\sqrt{\tau mT\log(\tau mT)}\right)\).

This covers also the case \(H_0\geq T\), where the algorithm uses the baseline throughout.

8. Bit complexity and the population representation

Empirical means are rational with encoding length polynomial in \(L+\log T\). The radii, confidence endpoints, bonus coefficient, and initial-phase thresholds also have polynomial encoding length. The exponent \(j_n\) is found by integer comparisons over \(O(\log T+\log h)\) possible exponents.

Thus each round requires polynomial-time rational arithmetic and at most one LP of polynomial size, giving computation time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log T)\).

The algorithm outputs probabilities for the explicitly supported types. Recipient selection and normalized member sampling are precisely the operations stipulated in the question; no population members or unsupported types are enumerated.

The guarantee is therefore resolved affirmatively.
