The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given goods \(J\) with rational per-capita supplies \(s_j\), and supported buyer types \(t\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, linear utilities \(u_{tj}\), per-capita budgets \(m_t\), and utility caps \(d_t\), find equilibrium prices \(p_j\) and bundles \(x_{tj}\) per unit of type mass. Each type chooses a utility-maximizing bundle at those prices up to its cap, spending the minimum needed if it reaches the cap; aggregate allocations satisfy \(\sum_t \mu_t x_{tj} \le s_j\), with equality whenever \(p_j>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(J\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) divisible goods with positive rational per-capita supplies \(s_j\). A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) buyer types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has positive rational linear utility coefficients \(u_{tj}\), per-capita budget \(m_t\), and per-capita utility cap \(d_t\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
At prices \(p\in\mathbb R_{≥0}^{m}\), a type chooses a bundle \(z\in\mathbb R_{≥0}^{m}\) maximizing \(\sum_j u_{tj}z_j\) subject to \(\sum_jp_jz_j\le m_t\) and \(\sum_ju_{tj}z_j\le d_t\). If its maximum utility equals \(d_t\), it must minimize expenditure among bundles attaining that cap; remaining ties are unrestricted. Individual demand has no supply constraint.
An equilibrium consists of such bundles \(x_t\), interpreted per unit of type mass, and prices satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}\le s_j\), with equality whenever \(p_j>0\). The computational problem is to find an exact rational equilibrium.

Is this equilibrium computation problem polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Known results: A strong separation oracle and a guaranteed point of bounded binary encoding length permit polynomial-time point finding in a convex set; finite-buyer linear markets with utility caps admit exact polynomial-time equilibrium computation using this framework. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.01257)
A subsequent combinatorial polynomial-time algorithm computes finite-buyer equilibria respecting utility caps and minimum expenditure. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.07210) No result explicitly addressing the stated distributional input model was verified.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). A support-listed society reduces exactly to a finite market with \(\tau\) buyers. Both budgets and utility caps must be scaled by type mass.

I use the intended interpretation that budgets and caps are positive rational market parameters.

1. Finite-market theorem used

For a finite Fisher market with rational linear utilities, positive rational budgets and utility caps, an exact rational equilibrium satisfying the minimum-expenditure rule can be computed in polynomial time in its binary input length. This is the utility-capped model \(M_2\) in [Devanur et al., Sections 2 and 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.01257). The subsequent algorithm computes a modest MBB equilibrium, which respects the same rule; its polynomial running time and bounds on intermediate encoding lengths appear in [Bei et al., Section 2, Lemmas 10–11, and Theorem 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.07210).

We apply this theorem after normalizing supplies to one unit per good.

2. Constructing the finite market

Create one buyer for each listed type \(t\), and one unit of each good \(j\). Define its budget, utility cap, and utility coefficients by

\(B_t=\mu_t m_t,\qquad D_t=\mu_t d_t,\qquad a_{tj}=s_j u_{tj}.\)

For original prices \(p\), define normalized prices

\(P_j=s_jp_j.\)

For a per-capita bundle \(z\) of type \(t\), define the corresponding finite-buyer bundle by

\(q_j=\dfrac{\mu_t z_j}{s_j}.\)

Because \(\mu_t\) and every \(s_j\) are positive, this is a bijection between nonnegative bundle spaces, with inverse \(z_j=s_jq_j/\mu_t\).

The two essential identities are

\(\displaystyle \sum_j a_{tj}q_j=\mu_t\sum_j u_{tj}z_j,\qquad \sum_jP_jq_j=\mu_t\sum_jp_jz_j.\)

3. Exact preservation of individual demand

The expenditure identity gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_jp_jz_j\le m_t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_jP_jq_j\le B_t.\)

Likewise, the utility identity gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_ju_{tj}z_j\le d_t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_ja_{tj}q_j\le D_t.\)

Thus the bundle bijection preserves feasibility. Since utilities are multiplied by the positive constant \(\mu_t\), it also preserves utility maximization in both directions.

Moreover, maximum utility equals \(d_t\) in the original problem exactly when it equals \(D_t\) in the finite problem. Among bundles attaining these respective caps, expenditures are multiplied by the same positive constant \(\mu_t\). Consequently, expenditure minimizers correspond exactly, including all remaining ties.

This argument holds even when some prices are zero. It also uses the stipulated absence of supply constraints in individual demand: the correspondence is between the entire individual feasible sets.

4. Exact preservation of equilibrium

Let \(q_t\) be the finite buyer’s allocation, and recover the original allocation and prices by

\(\displaystyle x_{tj}=\frac{s_jq_{tj}}{\mu_t},\qquad p_j=\frac{P_j}{s_j}.\)

Section 3 proves that every recovered bundle satisfies the original demand rule. For each good,

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}=s_j\sum_tq_{tj}.\)

Therefore,

\(\displaystyle \sum_tq_{tj}\le1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}\le s_j.\)

Since \(s_j>0\), we also have \(P_j>0\) exactly when \(p_j>0\). Full allocation of every positively priced normalized good therefore corresponds exactly to full allocation of every positively priced original good.

The inverse transformation proves the converse as well. Hence the two markets’ equilibrium sets are in bijection.

5. Binary complexity and algorithm

All constructed parameters are positive rationals. Multiplying two binary-encoded rationals increases numerator and denominator encoding lengths by at most the sum of their input lengths, up to a constant. Thus each constructed parameter has encoding length \(O(L)\), and the transformed market’s total input length is \(O((m\tau+\tau)L)\).

The algorithm is therefore:

  • Construct the unit-supply finite market with parameters \(B_t,D_t,a_{tj}\).
  • Compute its exact rational equilibrium using the finite-market theorem.
  • Recover \(x_{tj}=s_jq_{tj}/\mu_t\) and \(p_j=P_j/s_j\).

The finite-market theorem applies because there are exactly \(\tau\) buyers, \(m\) unit-supply goods, and positive rational parameters of polynomial total encoding length. Recovery uses polynomial-time rational arithmetic and preserves polynomial output encoding length.

In particular, a small mass may produce a large per-capita allocation, but division by \(\mu_t\) adds only its binary encoding length. No expansion into a population whose size depends on mass denominators is required.

This proves polynomial-time exact equilibrium computation for the stated support input model.
