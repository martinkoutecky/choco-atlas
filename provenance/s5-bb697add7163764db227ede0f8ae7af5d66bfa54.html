Theorem 4.4 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete cost types \(t\), with \(v_t(e)\in\{0,-c_e\}\), and rational per-capita supplies \(b_e\) for \(r\) task classes, construct one succinct randomized direct mechanism that works at every admissible scale \(q\), where \(q\mu_t\) workers have type \(t\) and \(qb_e\) copies of task class \(e\) are allocated. Does it produce a lottery over integral allocations satisfying Theorem 4.4's strategyproofness, fairness, and efficiency guarantees at each scale?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) complete types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=r\geq1\) task classes, publicly known binary rational costs \(c_e>0\), and binary rational per-capita supplies \(b_e\geq0\) with \(\sum_e b_e>0\). Masses satisfy \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); utilities are additive with \(v_t(e)\in\{0,-c_e\}\), without payments or externalities. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An admissible scale is a positive integer \(q\) for which every \(q\mu_t\) and \(qb_e\) is integral. At this scale there are \(n=q\) workers and \(qb_e\) indivisible copies of class \(e\); every copy must be allocated.
Seek one uniform anonymous randomized direct mechanism, defined on every reported profile over the full domain \(\prod_e\{0,-c_e\}\), including reports absent from the truthful support. At each scale it must be group-strategyproof in expectation: for arbitrary outsiders’ reports, no coalition can jointly misreport so that all members weakly increase their expected true utilities and one strictly increases.
A succinct lottery is represented by an exact randomized sampler returning binary-encoded counts \(h_{t,a}\) of workers reporting type \(t\) who receive bundle vector \(a\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\). Conditional on these counts, bundles are assigned uniformly among workers of each reported type; copies within each class are interchangeable. The counts satisfy \(\sum_a h_{t,a}=q\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,a}h_{t,a}a_e=qb_e\), with \(\mu\) here denoting the reported distribution.
Require ex-ante envy-freeness, proportionality, equitability, Pareto optimality, utilitarian-welfare maximization, and egalitarian-welfare maximization; require every realized allocation to be envy-free, proportional, and equitable up to one item, Pareto optimal, and utilitarian-welfare maximizing. Equitability means equal own utilities; equitability up to one item means that, for each ordered pair \(i,j\), either \(v_i(A_i)\geq v_j(A_j)\), or removing one copy from \(A_i\cup A_j\) makes this inequality hold.
Ex-ante properties apply to the expected fractional allocation, with efficiency compared against all fractional allocations. Also require ex-post \(2\)-approximation of egalitarian welfare: \(\min_i v_i(A_i)\geq2\operatorname{OPT}_q\), where \(\operatorname{OPT}_q=\max_B\min_i v_i(B_i)\) over complete integral allocations. Fairness and efficiency are evaluated under truthful reports.

Is constructing such a uniform succinct mechanism polynomial-time solvable, or NP-hard, when construction time and expected exact-sampling time are measured in \(m\), the current support size \(\tau\), \(L\), and \(\log q\)?

For explicitly listed finite populations and chores, a polynomial-time randomized mechanism achieves all these guarantees, with stated running time \(O(Mn)\) for \(M\) copies and \(n\) workers. [Sun–Chen](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/117770/1/1-s2.0-S037722172500150X-main.pdf)
No result settling this precise uniform, succinct-output problem was identified in the search.

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one and defined admissible scales as positive integers making all worker counts and task-copy counts integral.
  • Made inherent costs publicly known positive binary rationals; allowed zero supplies but required positive total supply, and used m=r for the number of task classes.
  • Interpreted succinctness as exact sampling of bundle multiplicity tables with implicit uniform assignment within reported types; imposed anonymity to make the support-only input determine individual allocation lotteries.
  • Required a uniform mechanism over all reported profiles and the full restricted-additive type domain, rather than a lottery defined only at the given truthful distribution.
  • Measured construction and expected sampling time in the compressed input and log q, with tau referring to the support of the profile currently processed.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, with expected exact-sampling time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log q\).

The construction uses the separation into universally disliked classes and classes having zero reporters found in [Sun–Chen](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/117770/1/1-s2.0-S037722172500150X-main.pdf). The essential compression comes from changing the randomization: use a random cyclic shift for universally disliked chores, and assign every copy of each remaining class to one randomly selected zero reporter. All required properties are proved below.

1. Definition of the mechanism

Fix an admissible scale \(q\), and write \(n=q\). At the current reported profile, let \(N_t\) be the number of workers reporting type \(t\), and let \(M_e=qb_e\) be the number of copies of class \(e\). These are integers.

Define \(U\) to be the set of classes that every worker reports as having value \(-c_e\). For \(e\notin U\), let \(Z_e\) be the number of workers reporting value zero on class \(e\). Thus \(Z_e>0\).

Order the reported types lexicographically by their zero/dislike vectors. Introduce internal slots \(0,\ldots,q-1\), and give each type a consecutive block containing exactly \(N_t\) slots. These slots are computational devices, not worker identities.

The sampler performs the following steps.

  • Choose \(s\) uniformly from \(0,\ldots,q-1\).
  • List all copies of classes in \(U\) in nondecreasing order of cost, breaking ties by class index. Assign the copy with zero-based list index \(j\) to slot \((s+j)\bmod q\).
  • For every class \(e\notin U\) with \(M_e>0\), choose one slot uniformly among the \(Z_e\) slots whose type reports zero on \(e\), and assign all \(M_e\) copies to that slot.
  • Compute the resulting bundle vector at each slot and output its histogram \(h_{t,a}\).
  • Conditional on this histogram, assign its bundles uniformly among workers of each reported type, as required in the question.

Steps 2–4 admit a compressed implementation proved in Section 6; no list of copies or workers is actually constructed.

Every copy is allocated exactly once. The same rule applies to every reported profile over the full domain and to every admissible scale.

2. Anonymity and expected class counts

The histogram sampler depends only on the reported type counts. Conditional assignment is uniform within each reported type. Consequently, permuting worker identities permutes the output distribution in the same way: the mechanism is anonymous.

We now establish its individual expected counts.

For \(e\in U\), fix any internal slot \(p\). Each particular copy of class \(e\) reaches \(p\) for exactly one of the \(q\) possible shifts. Therefore its expected count at \(p\) is \(M_e/q\).

For a worker reporting type \(t\), uniform conditional assignment gives expected class count equal to the expected total count in type \(t\)'s slots divided by \(N_t\). Hence every worker receives expected count \(M_e/q\) from every class \(e\in U\).

For \(e\notin U\), a zero-reporting type \(t\) contains the selected slot with probability \(N_t/Z_e\). Its expected total count is therefore \(M_eN_t/Z_e\). Uniform conditional assignment implies that each worker of this type receives expected count \(M_e/Z_e\). Workers reporting dislike receive none.

Thus, for every worker \(i\),

\( \mathbb E[a_{i,e}]=M_e/q \) if \(e\in U\);

\( \mathbb E[a_{i,e}]=M_e/Z_e \) if \(e\notin U\) and \(i\) reports zero;

\( \mathbb E[a_{i,e}]=0 \) otherwise.

These statements hold at every reported profile, including profiles outside the truthful support.

3. Group-strategyproofness in expectation

Use positive disutilities \(d_i=-v_i\). Fix an arbitrary coalition \(S\), arbitrary fixed outsiders’ reports, and arbitrary true types for coalition members.

Compare the profile where members of \(S\) report truthfully with any joint deviation. We prove that the coalition’s total expected true disutility cannot decrease, class by class.

A class having a zero reporter before the deviation

Every copy is initially assigned to a zero reporter. Any recipient belonging to \(S\) truly values that class at zero, because coalition members initially report truthfully. Therefore the coalition’s initial total true disutility from this class is zero.

After deviation its true disutility is nonnegative, so it cannot decrease.

A class universally reported as disliked before the deviation

Every coalition member truly dislikes this class. Its initial total expected coalition disutility is

\( |S|M_ec_e/q \).

There are two possibilities after deviation.

  • If the class remains universally reported as disliked, every worker still receives expected count \(M_e/q\). The coalition’s expected disutility is unchanged.
  • If the class acquires a zero reporter, every such reporter belongs to \(S\), since outsiders’ reports are unchanged and originally all reported dislike. All copies are therefore assigned within \(S\), and their true coalition disutility is \(M_ec_e\). This is at least \( |S|M_ec_e/q \), because \( |S|\leq q \).

Summing over classes proves that total expected coalition disutility cannot decrease.

If every coalition member weakly increased expected true utility and one strictly increased it, total expected coalition disutility would strictly decrease. This contradicts the established inequality.

Thus the mechanism is group-strategyproof in expectation for arbitrary outsiders’ reports.

4. Ex-ante fairness and efficiency

Throughout this section, reports are truthful. Define

\( C=\sum_{e\in U}M_ec_e \).

Every worker truly dislikes every class in \(U\). Every other class is assigned only to workers who truly value it at zero. By Section 2, every worker’s expected own utility is therefore

\( \mathbb E[v_i(A_i)]=-C/q \).

Envy-freeness, proportionality, and equitability

Equitability follows immediately from equality of expected own utilities.

For workers \(i,j\), worker \(i\)'s expected valuation of the universal chores assigned to \(j\) is \(-C/q\). Its valuation of \(j\)'s remaining chores is nonpositive. Consequently,

\( \mathbb E[v_i(A_j)]\leq -C/q=\mathbb E[v_i(A_i)] \),

which proves ex-ante envy-freeness.

Let \(E_q\) denote the complete multiset of available copies. Worker \(i\)'s disutility for this multiset is at least \(C\). Hence

\( \mathbb E[v_i(A_i)]=-C/q\geq v_i(E_q)/q \),

which proves proportionality.

Utilitarian-welfare maximization and Pareto optimality

For a universally disliked copy of class \(e\), every recipient incurs cost \(c_e\). For every other copy, the greatest attainable utility is zero.

Thus every complete fractional allocation has total utility at most \(-C\). Every realization of our mechanism attains exactly \(-C\). Its expected fractional allocation therefore maximizes utilitarian welfare.

A fractional Pareto improvement would weakly increase all utilities and strictly increase one, thereby strictly increasing their sum above \(-C\). This is impossible. Hence the expected fractional allocation is Pareto optimal.

The same argument proves utilitarian-welfare maximization and Pareto optimality for every realized integral allocation.

Egalitarian-welfare maximization

For any complete fractional allocation \(B\),

\( \min_i v_i(B_i)\leq q^{-1}\sum_i v_i(B_i)\leq -C/q \).

Our expected allocation gives utility \(-C/q\) to every worker, attaining this upper bound. It therefore maximizes egalitarian welfare among all fractional allocations.

5. Ex-post fairness and the egalitarian approximation

We first prove the relevant round-robin lemma independently.

Lemma: deleting a bundle’s last chore makes its load no greater than any other load

Let the universally disliked copies have costs

\( x_0\leq x_1\leq\cdots\leq x_{K-1} \).

Before shifting slots, position \(p\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\) receives list indices \(p,p+q,p+2q,\ldots\). Write \(\ell_p\) for its number of copies and \(L_p\) for their total cost.

If \(\ell_p>0\), deleting its last copy leaves load at most \(L_r\), for every position \(r\).

Proof. Each position receives either \(\lfloor K/q\rfloor\) or \(\lceil K/q\rceil\) copies.

If \(p\leq r\), then \(\ell_r\geq\ell_p-1\), and for every \(k\in\{0,\ldots,\ell_p-2\}\),

\( x_{p+kq}\leq x_{r+kq} \).

Summing these inequalities bounds position \(p\)'s load after deleting its last copy by a prefix of position \(r\)'s load.

If \(p>r\), then \(\ell_r\geq\ell_p\). For every such \(k\),

\( p+kq<r+(k+1)q \),

so

\( x_{p+kq}\leq x_{r+(k+1)q} \).

Again, summing bounds the remaining load by a subset of position \(r\)'s load. Empty sums are zero. Since costs are positive, either subset has cost at most \(L_r\). This proves the lemma.

A cyclic shift only permutes positions, so the lemma remains valid after shifting. Uniform assignment within reported types also only permutes bundles.

Envy-freeness up to one item

In a truthful realization, worker \(i\)'s own disutility equals its universal load \(L_i\), because its other chores have value zero.

Worker \(i\)'s disutility for worker \(j\)'s bundle is at least \(L_j\): every universal chore is disliked by \(i\), and other chores contribute nonnegative disutility.

If \(i\) has a universal chore, delete its last such chore. The lemma gives remaining own disutility at most \(L_j\), and therefore at most \(i\)'s disutility for \(j\)'s bundle.

If \(i\) has no universal chore, its own utility is zero, so it already envies nobody. This proves ex-post envy-freeness up to one item.

Equitability up to one item

Own utilities are exactly \(-L_i\). For any ordered pair \(i,j\), if \(L_i\leq L_j\), the required inequality already holds.

Otherwise \(i\) has a universal chore, and deleting its last such chore leaves load at most \(L_j\). The deleted copy belongs to \(A_i\subseteq A_i\cup A_j\), so this proves the precise equitability-up-to-one-item condition in the question.

Proportionality up to one item

After the same deletion, the lemma gives remaining own disutility at most every worker’s universal load. In particular, it is at most

\( \min_j L_j\leq q^{-1}\sum_jL_j=C/q \).

Since \(d_i(E_q)\geq C\), the remaining utility is at least \(-d_i(E_q)/q=v_i(E_q)/q\).

A worker with no universal chore already satisfies proportionality. Hence every realization is proportional up to one item.

A \(2\)-approximation of egalitarian welfare

Define the optimal integral maximum disutility by

\( T_q=\min_B\max_i d_i(B_i) \),

where \(B\) ranges over complete integral allocations. Then \(\operatorname{OPT}_q=-T_q\).

If \(C=0\), every worker’s realized own disutility is zero. Since all utilities are nonpositive, \(T_q=0\), and the desired inequality holds.

Suppose \(C>0\), and let \(c_{\max}\) be the largest cost of a universally disliked class having at least one copy. Every complete integral allocation satisfies

\( T_q\geq C/q \)

because its total disutility is at least \(C\), and

\( T_q\geq c_{\max} \)

because some worker must receive a universally disliked copy of cost \(c_{\max}\).

Let \(H=\max_i L_i\) in our realization. Apply the lemma to a worker attaining \(H\). Deleting its last universal chore, of cost at most \(c_{\max}\), leaves load at most the minimum universal load. Therefore

\( H\leq\min_jL_j+c_{\max}\leq C/q+c_{\max}\leq 2T_q \).

Consequently,

\( \min_i v_i(A_i)=-H\geq-2T_q=2\operatorname{OPT}_q \),

as required. Computing \(T_q\) is unnecessary.

6. Polynomial-time compressed exact sampling

It remains to implement the mechanism without enumerating workers or copies.

Compressing the universal bundles

Process universal classes in the cost order specified above. For each such class, compute

\( M_e=qd_e+R_e,\qquad 0\leq R_e<q \),

and let \(P_e\) be the total number of universal copies in preceding classes.

Every slot receives \(d_e\) copies of class \(e\), plus one additional copy precisely on the cyclic interval of length \(R_e\) starting at

\( (s+P_e)\bmod q \).

Indeed, the consecutive list indices belonging to class \(e\) contain \(d_e\) complete cycles through the slots, followed by this residual interval.

Each residual interval has at most two boundary points on the slot circle. Hence the universal bundle vector changes at only \(O(m)\) boundaries.

Selecting recipients of the remaining classes

For \(e\notin U\), compute \(Z_e\) by summing the lengths of the zero-reporting type blocks. Draw a uniform integer in \(0,\ldots,Z_e-1\), and locate its slot by scanning those blocks cumulatively.

This chooses a slot uniformly among all eligible slots without listing them. If its index is \(z_e\), add the boundaries \(z_e,z_e+1\), isolating this singleton slot.

There are at most \(m\) such selected slots.

Producing the histogram

Collect and sort:

  • the type-block boundaries;
  • the universal residual-interval boundaries;
  • the selected-slot singleton boundaries;
  • the endpoints \(0,q\).

There are \(O(m+\tau)\) boundaries.

Between consecutive boundaries, both the reported type and the entire bundle vector are constant. For each nonempty interval, compute that vector and output its interval length as the corresponding count. Equal pairs \((t,a)\) can be merged.

Thus each sampled histogram has \(O(m+\tau)\) nonzero entries. It satisfies exactly

\( \sum_a h_{t,a}=N_t=q\mu_t \)

and

\( \sum_{t,a}h_{t,a}a_e=M_e=qb_e \).

Bit complexity and exact randomness

All multiplicities, slot indices, interval lengths, and bundle coordinates have bit length polynomial in \(L+\log q+\log m\). Rational cost comparisons also take polynomial time.

Sorting boundaries, locating recipients, and evaluating bundle vectors require polynomially many arithmetic operations in \(m,\tau,L,\log q\).

A uniform integer in \(0,\ldots,K-1\), where \(1\leq K\leq q\), can be sampled exactly by rejection from \(\lceil\log_2K\rceil\) unbiased bits. For \(K>1\), acceptance probability exceeds \(1/2\); for \(K=1\), no random bits are needed. Each draw therefore has expected time polynomial in \(\log q\).

The sampler makes at most \(m+1\) such draws. Consequently, preparation time and expected exact-sampling time are polynomial in the required parameters. Conditional uniform assignment is precisely the interpretation of the histogram prescribed by the question; the sampler need not enumerate that assignment.

Conclusion

The construction is uniform over all reported profiles and all admissible scales, anonymous, and group-strategyproof in expectation. It satisfies every specified ex-ante and ex-post guarantee, while producing polynomial-size histograms with polynomial expected exact-sampling time.
