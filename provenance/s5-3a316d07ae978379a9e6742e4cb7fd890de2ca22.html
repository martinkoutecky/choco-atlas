The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates, seats, rational masses μ on supported ballot types, an idealized STV count, a winner w, a specified round i, and a rational safety margin ε>0, find the minimum mass m of ballots with first preference w to retarget among the other continuing candidates. Require that the prior elimination path remains as in the paper and that t_i(w)-m+ε≤t_i(c)+δ_c for every continuing c≠w, with ∑_{c≠w}δ_c=m; each source type's transferred mass is bounded by its μ-mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge2\) candidates and let \(1\le s<m\) be the number of seats. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Types are nonempty strict partial rankings; masses need not sum to one. Write \(N=\sum_b\mu_b\).
Use exact fractional STV with fixed quota \(Q=\lfloor N/(s+1)\rfloor+1\) and an input total order resolving all ties. Initially each ballot mass goes to its first preference. Elect a candidate of largest tally reaching \(Q\); otherwise eliminate a candidate of smallest tally. When the continuing candidates equal the remaining seats, elect them all and stop.
Elimination transfers each parcel at its current value to its next continuing preference. An elected candidate with tally \(T\) transfers eligible parcels with multiplier \(\min\{1,(T-Q)/V\}\), where \(V\) is their total current value; eligible destinations are continuing candidates below quota. If \(V=0\), nothing transfers. Parcels with no eligible preference exhaust; there is no rounding or multiple elimination.
In the original count, let \(w\) be a winner elected by survival without reaching quota, and let \(i\) be an elimination round at which \(w\) continues. Let \(C_i\) and \(t_i(c)\) denote the continuing set and tallies immediately before that round. Input also includes binary rational \(\varepsilon>0\) and budget \(B\ge0\).
For each supported type \(b\) beginning with \(w\), choose real masses \(x_{b,c}\ge0\) for \(c\in C_i\setminus\{w\}\), with \(\sum_c x_{b,c}\le\mu_b\). Retarget each selected portion by replacing its initial \(w\) with \(c\) and deleting any later occurrence of \(c\), preserving the remaining order; all other mass stays unchanged.
Set \(\delta_c=\sum_bx_{b,c}\) and \(a=\sum_c\delta_c\). Require \(t_i(w)-a+\varepsilon\le t_i(c)+\delta_c\) for every \(c\in C_i\setminus\{w\}\). Rerunning the modified count must either eliminate \(w\) before round \(i\), or preserve the original election and elimination sequence through round \(i-1\) and eliminate \(w\) at round \(i\).
Is deciding whether such a retargeting exists with \(a\le B\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,s,i\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length.
The discrete sufficient-condition lemma guarantees a valid winner-elimination manipulation when enough first-preference ballots are available; it gives no complexity classification for this constrained problem. Later discrete work develops winner-elimination upper bounds and algorithms for general STV margins ([Blom et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.03511)). No result settling this exact continuous problem was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonempty strict partial rankings, exhaustion, positive unnormalized masses, and the literal Droop-quota extension Q=floor(N/(s+1))+1; normalization and the continuous quota were unspecified.
  • Specified exact fractional surplus transfers, eligibility, election priority, stopping rules, and deterministic tie-breaking because idealized STV was not fully defined.
  • Restricted w to a winner elected by survival without reaching quota and i to an original elimination round at which w continues, as required by the intended discrete setting.
  • Defined retargeting as replacing the initial w by the recipient and deleting any subsequent occurrence of that recipient, preserving other preferences.
  • Restored the lemma's allowance for earlier elimination of w; otherwise required the complete prior election and elimination sequence to remain unchanged.
  • Required actual elimination in the rerun, since the safety inequality alone does not exclude an intervening quota election.
  • Used a rational-budget existence question to classify the minimum-mass problem without assuming that a minimum is attained under the count's strict inequalities.

Partial resolution

I do not settle whether the unrestricted problem is in $\mathrm{P}$ or is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard.

I prove two additional results:

  • At every original elimination round except the final one, the mass and tally inequalities alone are sufficient. They reduce to one explicit scalar test. Including computation of the original count, this gives a polynomial-time algorithm for fixed $s$ or fixed $i$.
  • If every supported ranking is a singleton, the full problem is polynomial-time solvable, including manipulations that change the prefix.

I also give a fully specified existential-real encoding, including stationary parcels, exhaustion, removal, and stopping.

1. Definitions and aggregate feasibility

A partial ranking is interpreted as an ordered list of distinct candidates, possibly omitting candidates. A round is one election or elimination action; the terminal election by survival is a stopping action.

Put
$$ D=C_i\setminus\{w\},\qquad F=\sum_{b:\,b\text{ begins with }w}\mu_b,\qquad M=\min\{B,F\}. $$
Write
$$ A=t_i(w),\qquad U_c=t_i(c)\quad(c\in D). $$
For $0\le a\le M$, define
$$ K(a)=A-a+\varepsilon,\qquad \ell_c(a)=\max\{0,K(a)-U_c\},\qquad S(a)=\sum_{c\in D}\ell_c(a). $$

Lemma 1: aggregation and realization

The possible aggregate allocations are exactly
$$ \delta_c\ge0,\qquad a=\sum_{c\in D}\delta_c,\qquad a\le F. $$

Proof. These constraints follow by summing the per-type capacities.

Conversely, suppose $F>0$. Set
$$ x_{b,c}=\frac{\mu_b\delta_c}{F} $$
for every supported type beginning with $w$. Then
$$ \sum_{c\in D}x_{b,c} =\frac{\mu_ba}{F}\le\mu_b, \qquad \sum_{b:\,b\text{ begins with }w}x_{b,c}=\delta_c. $$
Thus the aggregate vector is realizable. If $F=0$, only the zero vector is possible. $\square$

This lemma concerns realizability of the aggregates. It does not assert that aggregates determine an arbitrary modified count.

Lemma 2: one scalar test for the mass and tally constraints

There is a retargeting satisfying the capacities, budget, and required tally inequalities, without imposing the count condition, if and only if
$$ S(M)\le M. $$

Proof. At a given total amount $a$, the required inequalities are exactly
$$ \delta_c\ge\ell_c(a)\quad(c\in D). $$
An aggregate vector with sum $a$ therefore exists exactly when $S(a)\le a$: assign the lower bounds and distribute the remainder to any member of $D$.

Each $\ell_c(a)$ is nonincreasing in $a$. Hence, if $S(a)\le a$ for some $a\le M$, then
$$ S(M)\le S(a)\le a\le M. $$
Conversely, $S(M)\le M$ supplies an aggregate vector with total amount $M$, which Lemma 1 realizes. $\square$

If $F=0$, the instance is necessarily a no-instance. Indeed, the candidate $d$ originally eliminated at round $i$ satisfies $t_i(d)\le A$, whereas the required inequality with no retargeting would give $A+\varepsilon\le t_i(d)$.

2. Conservation and a sufficient allocation below quota

Let $e$ be the number of original quota elections before round $i$, and let
$$ \rho=s-e,\qquad k=|C_i|. $$
Thus $\rho$ is the number of remaining seats. Because round $i$ is an elimination round,
$$ k>\rho,\qquad t_i(c)<Q\quad(c\in C_i). $$

Define the total headroom of the destinations by
$$ g_c=Q-U_c>0,\qquad G=\sum_{c\in D}g_c. $$

Lemma 3: conservation bound

The original count satisfies
$$ \sum_{c\in C_i}t_i(c)\le N-eQ<(\rho+1)Q. $$
Consequently,
$$ G>(k-\rho-2)Q+A. $$
Also, $A\ge F$.

Proof. At a quota election, the elected candidate’s tally is $T$. The total transferred value is
$$ \lambda V,\qquad \lambda=\min\left\{1,\frac{T-Q}{V}\right\} $$
when $V>0$, and is zero when $V=0$. In either case it is at most $T-Q$. Thus the total value held by continuing candidates decreases by at least $Q$. An elimination cannot increase that total.

After $e$ quota elections, the continuing value is therefore at most $N-eQ$. The quota definition gives
$$ N<(s+1)Q, $$
so
$$ N-eQ<(\rho+1)Q. $$
Now
$$ \begin{aligned} G &=(k-1)Q-\sum_{c\in D}U_c\\ &=(k-1)Q-\sum_{c\in C_i}t_i(c)+A\\ &>(k-\rho-2)Q+A. \end{aligned} $$
Finally, $w$ still holds all its original first-preference mass: it has not been removed, and transfers away from a candidate occur only when that candidate is removed. Therefore $A\ge F$. $\square$

Lemma 4: an allocation below quota guarantees success

Suppose an aggregate allocation satisfies
$$ a\le M,\qquad \delta_c\ge\ell_c(a),\qquad U_c+\delta_c<Q\quad(c\in D). $$
Every realization from Lemma 1 is a valid manipulation.

Proof. Every member of $D\cup\{w\}$ continues throughout the original prefix before round $i$. The original tallies of these candidates are nondecreasing while they continue. In particular,
$$ t_q(c)+\delta_c\le U_c+\delta_c<Q $$
at each original round $q\le i$.

We induct along the original actions until the modified count eliminates $w$. While the actions match, the tally offsets are
$$ \widehat t_q(w)=t_q(w)-a,\qquad \widehat t_q(c)=t_q(c)+\delta_c\quad(c\in D), $$
and all other continuing tallies are unchanged.

Initially these identities hold directly. The retargeted parcels remain at their new first preferences because their destinations have not been removed.

Consider an original action at an outside candidate.

  • If it is an elimination, its parcels and their current values are unchanged. The continuing sets agree, so the transfers agree.
  • If it is an election, its parcels and tally are unchanged. The candidates in $D\cup\{w\}$ are below quota in both counts; all other eligibility comparisons are unchanged. Thus the eligible parcels, their total value, and the multiplier agree.

These facts preserve the offsets.

At an original election, all affected candidates remain below quota, so the same outside candidate is elected.

At an original elimination before $i$, the originally selected candidate is outside $D\cup\{w\}$. Relative to that candidate, outside tallies and tie priorities are unchanged, and destination tallies have increased. Only $w$ has decreased. Consequently, the modified elimination selects either the original candidate or $w$.

The survival condition cannot intervene while the prefix matches: the continuing set and number of filled seats agree with the original count.

If $w$ is not eliminated earlier, then immediately before round $i$ the offsets hold. Every continuing candidate is below quota, and
$$ \widehat t_i(w)+\varepsilon =A-a+\varepsilon \le U_c+\delta_c =\widehat t_i(c) \quad(c\in D). $$
Since $\varepsilon>0$, $w$ is the unique minimum and is eliminated at round $i$. $\square$

Lemma 5: constructing an allocation below quota

If
$$ S(M)\le M<G, $$
there is an allocation satisfying Lemma 4, with $a=M$.

Proof. First, $K(M)<Q$. Otherwise $\ell_c(M)\ge g_c$ for every destination, which would imply
$$ S(M)\ge G>M, $$
a contradiction.

Thus $0\le\ell_c(M)<g_c$ for every $c$. Put
$$ \theta=\frac{M-S(M)}{G-S(M)}. $$
The hypotheses imply $0\le\theta<1$. Define
$$ \delta_c=\ell_c(M)+\theta\bigl(g_c-\ell_c(M)\bigr). $$
Then
$$ \sum_{c\in D}\delta_c =S(M)+\theta\bigl(G-S(M)\bigr)=M, $$
and
$$ \ell_c(M)\le\delta_c<g_c. $$
These are exactly the required lower bounds and strict quota bounds. $\square$

3. Exact characterization at nonfinal original eliminations

Theorem 6

If
$$ |C_i|\ge s-e+2, $$
then the full problem is a yes-instance if and only if
$$ S(M)\le M. $$

Proof. Necessity is Lemma 2.

For sufficiency, Lemma 3 gives
$$ G>(k-\rho-2)Q+A\ge A\ge F\ge M. $$
Hence $S(M)\le M<G$. Lemma 5 constructs an allocation below quota, and Lemma 4 proves that it is valid. $\square$

An election decreases both the number of continuing candidates and the number of remaining seats by one. An elimination decreases their difference by one. Because the promised original count ends by survival, its final elimination occurs when this difference is one.

Therefore Theorem 6 applies precisely to every original elimination except the final one.

This proves a structural conclusion without assuming that intermediate rational values have polynomial bit length: at a nonfinal original elimination, the count condition adds no further feasibility obstruction.

Bit-time bound

Put
$$ h=L+\log(m\tau+2),\qquad H=O\!\left(2^{\min\{s,i-1\}}h\right). $$
The original prefix can be simulated with parcel and tally bit lengths bounded by $H$.

To verify this, initially use a common denominator $D_0$ whose bit length is $O(L)$. Suppose the current common denominator is $D$. At an election with a multiplier strictly between zero and one, write
$$ T=\frac JD,\qquad V=\frac ED. $$
Then
$$ \lambda=\frac{J-QD}{E}, $$
and $DE$ is a valid common denominator after the transfer. Because $V\le N$,
$$ E\le ND. $$
Thus each such election at most doubles the denominator bit length, apart from an additive $O(L)$ term. Eliminations and multipliers equal to zero or one require no enlargement. There are at most $\min\{s,i-1\}$ preceding quota elections.

Using elementary integer arithmetic, simulation, the scalar test, and construction of a witness take
$$ O\!\left(L^2+(i+1)(\tau+1)mH^2\right) $$
bit operations.

Consequently, the full problem at nonfinal original eliminations is polynomial-time solvable for fixed $s$, and also for fixed $i$. This bound does not establish polynomial time when both are unrestricted.

4. The full singleton-ranking problem is in $\mathrm{P}$

Assume throughout this section that every supported ranking has length one. Let $u_c$ be candidate $c$’s initial tally.

All parcels exhaust when their holder is removed. Tallies of continuing candidates therefore never change, in either count. In particular,
$$ F=u_w,\qquad A=F,\qquad U_c=u_c\quad(c\in D). $$

Let
$$ H_0=\{c:u_c\ge Q\},\qquad h_0=|H_0|. $$
The original count elects these candidates first, in decreasing tally order with the prescribed tie rule. The promise that $w$ wins by survival implies
$$ h_0<s,\qquad F<Q. $$

Let
$$ E=C\setminus(H_0\cup C_i),\qquad k_i=|E|. $$
These are exactly the candidates eliminated before round $i$. Hence
$$ i=h_0+k_i+1,\qquad k_i\le m-s-1. $$

Write $d\prec w$ when the elimination tie rule selects $d$ ahead of $w$. For an amount $a$, put
$$ W(a)=F-a $$
and define
$$ k(a)= \left| \left\{ d\in E: u_d<W(a) \ \text{or}\ \bigl(u_d=W(a)\ \text{and}\ d\prec w\bigr) \right\} \right|. $$
Thus $k(a)$ counts the unchanged outside candidates that precede $w$ in elimination order. It is nonincreasing in $a$, and $k(a)\le k_i$.

Lemma 7: exact timing criterion

Suppose an aggregate allocation satisfies the budget and required inequalities. Let
$$ h=\bigl|\{c\in D:u_c+\delta_c\ge Q\}\bigr|. $$
It is valid if and only if
$$ h_0+h<s $$
and
$$ h=0 \quad\text{or}\quad \bigl(h\ge1\ \text{and}\ h+k(a)<k_i\bigr). $$

Proof. The inequalities ensure
$$ u_c+\delta_c\ge W(a)+\varepsilon>W(a)\quad(c\in D). $$
Thus no destination precedes $w$ in elimination order.

All quota candidates are elected before any elimination. If $h_0+h=s$, those elections fill every seat and $w$ is never eliminated.

Otherwise, exactly the $k(a)$ preceding members of $E$ are eliminated before $w$. Survival cannot occur before its elimination: the number of eliminations needed to reach survival is $m-s$, whereas
$$ k(a)\le k_i\le m-s-1. $$
The elimination round of $w$ is consequently
$$ r=h_0+h+k(a)+1. $$

If $h=0$, the original quota elections are unchanged. The preceding members of $E$ occur in their original order. Since $k(a)\le k_i$, either $r<i$, or $r=i$ with a matching prefix.

If $h\ge1$, a destination is elected before $w$ is eliminated. That destination continues at original round $i$, so the prefixes cannot match. Validity therefore requires $r<i$, equivalently $h+k(a)<k_i$. $\square$

Lemma 8: at most one additional quota election is enough

Every successful singleton manipulation can be replaced, without changing $a$, by a successful manipulation with at most one member of $D$ reaching quota.

Proof. For a successful allocation,
$$ K(a)=F-a+\varepsilon<Q. $$
Indeed, if $K(a)\ge Q$, every destination would reach quota. Because round $i$ is an elimination,
$$ |D|\ge s-h_0. $$
Those destinations, together with the original quota candidates, would fill every seat before an elimination.

Thus
$$ 0\le\ell_c(a)<Q-u_c $$
for every destination. Choose any $c_*\in D$ and define
$$ \delta'_c=\ell_c(a)\quad(c\ne c_*),\qquad \delta'_{c_*}=a-\sum_{c\ne c_*}\ell_c(a). $$
Since $S(a)\le a$, these values satisfy every lower bound and sum to $a$. Only $c_*$ can reach quota.

If no destination reaches quota, Lemma 7 gives validity. Otherwise the new quota count is one. If the previous count was $h\ge1$, then
$$ h_0+1\le h_0+h<s,\qquad 1+k(a)\le h+k(a)<k_i. $$
Lemma 7 again gives validity. $\square$

The polynomial algorithm

Accept if either of the following branches succeeds.

Branch A. Test feasibility of the rational linear system
$$ \begin{gathered} \delta_c\ge0,\qquad a=\sum_{c\in D}\delta_c,\qquad 0\le a\le M,\\ F-a+\varepsilon\le u_c+\delta_c<Q \quad(c\in D). \end{gathered} $$
This exactly describes allocations with no destination reaching quota; Lemma 7 makes every such allocation valid.

Branch B. Check the four conditions
$$ h_0+1<s,\qquad k(M)\le k_i-2,\qquad S(M)\le M,\qquad F-M+\varepsilon<Q. $$

If they hold, construct the allocation in Lemma 8 with $a=M$. It has at most one destination reaching quota. With none, it is valid. With one, the first two conditions and Lemma 7 prove validity.

Conversely, suppose a successful allocation has $h\ge1$ at amount $a$. Lemma 7 gives
$$ h_0+1<s,\qquad k(a)\le k_i-2. $$
Moreover, success implies $K(a)<Q$ and $S(a)\le a$. Since $a\le M$ and $k$, $K$, and $S$ are nonincreasing,
$$ k(M)\le k_i-2,\qquad K(M)<Q,\qquad S(M)\le M. $$
Thus Branch B succeeds. A successful allocation with $h=0$ satisfies Branch A. This proves completeness of the algorithm.

Strict inequalities in Branch A are handled by introducing $0\le\eta\le1$, replacing each strict inequality $f<g$ by
$$ f+\eta\le g, $$
and maximizing $\eta$. Feasibility of the original strict system is equivalent to a positive optimum. All allocation variables are bounded by $M$, so the auxiliary feasible region is compact when nonempty.

Let $\mathrm{LP}(n,p,b)$ denote a polynomial bit-time bound for rational linear programming with $n$ variables, $p$ constraints, and coefficient bit length at most $b$. With
$$ h=O\!\left(L+\log(m\tau+2)\right), $$
the singleton algorithm takes
$$ O\!\left(L^2+(\tau+m\log m)h^2\right) +\mathrm{LP}\!\left(m+1,O(m),O(h)\right). $$
This is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$, with no restriction on $s$ or $i$.

5. Why the final-elimination case remains substantive

Consider singleton rankings with two seats and masses

| Type | Mass |
|---|---:|
| $[d]$ | $2$ |
| $[e]$ | $3$ |
| $[c]$ | $7/2$ |
| $[w]$ | $4$ |
| $[z]$ | $5$ |

Here
$$ N=\frac{35}{2},\qquad Q=6. $$
The original count eliminates $d,e,c$, then elects $w,z$ by survival. Choose $i=3$ and $B=4$, so
$$ D=\{c,z\},\qquad F=M=A=4,\qquad G=\frac72. $$

A successful manipulation requiring a changed prefix

Take $\varepsilon=59/10$ and
$$ \delta_c=\frac{12}{5},\qquad \delta_z=\frac85,\qquad a=4. $$
The modified tallies are
$$ t'(d)=2,\quad t'(e)=3,\quad t'(c)=\frac{59}{10},\quad t'(w)=0,\quad t'(z)=\frac{33}{5}. $$
Candidate $z$ is elected first and exhausts its parcels. Candidate $w$ is eliminated second, before $i$. The required inequalities hold because
$$ A-a+\varepsilon=\frac{59}{10} =t'(c)<t'(z). $$

No successful manipulation can preserve the original prefix until eliminating $w$. At such an elimination, both $c$ and $z$ would still continue and would have to be below quota. Hence
$$ \delta_c<\frac52,\qquad \delta_z<1,\qquad a<\frac72. $$
But the required inequality for $c$ gives
$$ a+\delta_c\ge\frac{32}{5}, $$
whereas the quota bounds give
$$ a+\delta_c<6<\frac{32}{5}. $$

Aggregate feasibility need not imply success at the final elimination

Instead take $\varepsilon=6$. Then
$$ S(M)=\left(6-\frac72\right)+(6-5)=\frac72\le4, $$
so the aggregate constraints are feasible.

Nevertheless, every allocation satisfying them has
$$ A-a+\varepsilon=10-a\ge6. $$
Both $c$ and $z$ therefore reach quota initially. They fill the two seats before any elimination, so $w$ cannot be eliminated.

Thus the scalar characterization from Theorem 6 cannot be extended to the final elimination without additional reasoning.

6. A complete existential-real encoding

The unrestricted problem belongs to $\exists\mathbb R$. Here this means that it reduces in polynomial time to an existential formula over the reals whose atoms are polynomial equalities and inequalities with integer coefficients.

The construction below avoids expanding the original count’s intermediate rationals.

6.1 Ranking portions and state variables

For each original type, create its unchanged portion. For each type beginning with $w$, also create a retargeted portion for every $c\ne w$. Its ranking is the prescribed replacement of $w$ by $c$, with any later occurrence of $c$ deleted.

There are at most
$$ P=\tau m $$
portions. Their initial values are $\mu_b$ for unmodifiable types,
$$ \mu_b-\sum_{c\ne w}x_{b,c} $$
for unchanged portions of modifiable types, and $x_{b,c}$ for retargeted portions.

Encode the original and modified counts separately through round $i$, including a state after that round. For each count and state, use:

  • Boolean continuing flags $u_c$;
  • the number $e$ of seats already filled;
  • a Boolean stopped flag $H$;
  • for each portion $p$, a nonnegative current value $v_p$ and Boolean position flags $l_{p,j}$.

If portion $p$ has ranking
$$ (c_{p,1},\ldots,c_{p,\ell_p}), $$
position zero denotes exhaustion. Impose
$$ \sum_{j=0}^{\ell_p}l_{p,j}=1,\qquad l_{p,j}\le u_{c_{p,j}}\quad(j>0), $$
and
$$ l_{p,0}=1\implies v_p=0. $$
Every Boolean variable $b$ is constrained by $b(b-1)=0$.

Tallies are defined by
$$ t_c=\sum_{p,j:\,c_{p,j}=c}l_{p,j}v_p. $$
Introduce a product variable for each term, so all defining equations have degree at most two.

Initially every candidate continues, $e=0$, $H=0$, and every portion is at position one with its specified initial value.

6.2 Action selection and stopping

At each round, introduce Boolean flags
$$ I,\quad S,\quad \mathsf E_c,\quad \mathsf D_c $$
for idle, survival, election of $c$, and elimination of $c$. Require exactly one action:
$$ I+S+\sum_c(\mathsf E_c+\mathsf D_c)=1. $$
Put $M_C=\sum_cu_c$. Impose $0\le e\le s$ and
$$ H=1\iff e=s,\qquad I=H. $$

When $H=0$, survival is selected exactly when
$$ M_C=s-e. $$
A single election or elimination is allowed only when
$$ M_C>s-e. $$

For an election flag $\mathsf E_c=1$, require $u_c=1$, $t_c\ge Q$, and comparison against every continuing candidate. The comparison is
$$ t_c\ge t_d $$
when $c$ wins their election tie, and $t_c>t_d$ otherwise.

For an elimination flag $\mathsf D_c=1$, require $u_c=1$, every continuing tally to be below $Q$, and
$$ t_c\le t_d $$
when $c$ wins their elimination tie, with $t_c<t_d$ otherwise.

These constraints select exactly the stipulated action.

State updates are:

  • Idle: freeze $u_c,e$ and all parcel states.
  • Survival: set $e'=s$, every $u'_c=0$, and every parcel to exhausted value zero.
  • Election or elimination:

$$ u'_c=u_c-\mathsf E_c-\mathsf D_c. $$
An election sets $e'=e+1$; an elimination sets $e'=e$.

The relation between $H'$ and $e'$ supplies stopping after the last seat is filled.

6.3 Eligible destinations and transfer multipliers

For a normal action, let $u'_c$ be the continuing flags after removing the selected candidate. Introduce Boolean eligibility flags $g_c$.

For elimination,
$$ g_c=u'_c. $$
For election,
$$ g_c=1\iff(u'_c=1\ \text{and}\ t_c<Q). $$
Thus election eligibility uses the state before that action’s transfers.

For each portion and possible current position $j>0$, the first eligible later position is computed by a scan. Set
$$ z_{p,j,j}=1. $$
For $k=j+1,\ldots,\ell_p$, impose
$$ n_{p,j,k}=z_{p,j,k-1}g_{c_{p,k}}, \qquad z_{p,j,k}=z_{p,j,k-1}(1-g_{c_{p,k}}). $$
Finally put
$$ n_{p,j,0}=z_{p,j,\ell_p}. $$
Here $n_{p,j,k}=1$ identifies the first eligible position after $j$, and $n_{p,j,0}=1$ means that no eligible later preference exists. This also prevents a parcel from moving backwards to a previously skipped preference.

For an election, define $V$ as the total current value of parcels held by the elected candidate whose scan finds an eligible destination. Holding and eligibility indicators, and their products with $v_p$, are represented by auxiliary product variables.

Let $T$ be the elected candidate’s tally. Constrain $0\le\lambda\le1$ by the disjunction
$$ \begin{aligned} &(V=0\ \land\ \lambda=0)\\ {}\lor{}&(V>0\ \land\ T-Q\ge V\ \land\ \lambda=1)\\ {}\lor{}&(V>0\ \land\ 0\le T-Q<V\ \land\ \lambda V=T-Q). \end{aligned} $$
Outside election rounds set $\lambda=0$.

6.4 Parcel updates

These guarded constraints specify every parcel transition.

During a normal action:

  • If its holder is not selected, retain its position and value.
  • If its holder is selected and $n_{p,j,0}=1$, set its new position to zero and its new value to zero.
  • If its holder is eliminated and $n_{p,j,k}=1$ for $k>j$, set its new position to $k$ and impose

$$ v'_p=v_p. $$

  • If its holder is elected and $n_{p,j,k}=1$ for $k>j$, set its new position to $k$ and impose

$$ v'_p=\lambda v_p. $$

Already exhausted parcels remain exhausted. Idle and survival use the updates stated above.

For elections, these equations represent only the parcel value that continues. The retained quota and other discarded value leave the continuing state.

All guards are Boolean formulas. For example, a guard $b=1$ requiring an equation $f=0$ is encoded as
$$ b=0\ \lor\ f=0. $$

$$ O(R) $$

$$ R=i(\tau+1)m^3. $$
