The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given τ resident types and τ hospital types, each with mass \(1/\tau\), strict rankings over the opposite-side types, a predicted bijection π, and an error bound η, does there exist a stable flow \(x_{ij}\geq 0\) with every row and column summing to \(1/\tau\), such that every edge with \(x_{ij}>0\) is within rank displacement η of the predicted partner on both sides? Stability means no type pair has positive mass on both sides assigned to types each ranks below the other. In the rank-query version, rankings are exposed by rank queries and instances may promise that at most \(6/\tau\) mass leaves the predicted flow.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Each side of a society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq2\) types with positive binary-rational masses; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here every mass is \(1/\tau\), each side totals \(1\), and the number of possible partners is \(m=\tau\).
Each resident \(r_i\) and hospital \(h_j\) has a complete strict ranking of the opposite support; write \(p_i(j)\) and \(q_j(i)\) for ranks, with rank \(1\) best. Input also includes a predicted bijection \(\pi\) and an integer \(0\leq\eta<\tau/2\).
A flow \(x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{\tau\times\tau}\) is perfect if every row and column sums to \(1/\tau\). It is stable if no pair \((i,j)\) satisfies both \(\sum_{k:p_i(k)>p_i(j)}x_{ik}>0\) and \(\sum_{\ell:q_j(\ell)>q_j(i)}x_{\ell j}>0\); all pairs are tested, including edges outside the permitted windows.
Decide whether a perfect stable flow exists with \(x_{ij}>0\) only when \(\lvert p_i(j)-p_i(\pi(i))\rvert\leq\eta\) and \(\lvert q_j(i)-q_j(\pi^{-1}(j))\rvert\leq\eta\).
Rankings are accessible only through unit-cost queries returning either a specified partner's rank or the partner at a specified rank. Restrict to instances promising some perfect stable flow \(y\), without window restrictions, with \(2\sum_i(1/\tau-y_{i,\pi(i)})\leq6/\tau\), counting displaced mass on both sides.
Let \(L\) be the binary encoding length of the support, prediction, bound, and underlying rankings; express running times in \(m,\tau,L,\eta\), separately from query counts.

What is the worst-case deterministic exact query complexity of this decision problem: is \(\Omega((\tau-2\eta)^2)\) necessary even under the displaced-mass promise, or can \(o((\tau-2\eta)^2)\) queries suffice as \(\tau-2\eta\to\infty\)?

Discrete prediction-error testing requires \(\Omega((n-2\eta)^2)\) preference queries even when at most six agents must change partners to reach stability ([McCauley et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02254v1)).
For fractional matching, polynomial-time results cover unpruned ordinal stability with strict bipartite preferences, but do not address this prediction-window query problem ([Chen, Roy, and Sorge](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0013.pdf)).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made rankings complete and strict, used rank 1 as best, and required stability against every original pair.
  • Restricted the integer error bound to the nonvacuous lower-bound regime 0 <= eta < tau/2.
  • Specified both partner-to-rank and rank-to-partner oracle queries, since the anchor leaves preference-query access unspecified.
  • Used deterministic exact algorithms to make the query-complexity target precise.
  • Interpreted the six-agent promise as displaced endpoint mass summed over both sides, bounded by 6/tau; its witnessing stable flow need not satisfy the windows.
  • Defined m as the opposite-side support size and L as the underlying binary instance length.

Result

The lower bound is necessary in the precise two-query model of the question. Writing $k=\tau-2\eta$, every deterministic exact algorithm has worst-case query complexity

$$ \Omega(k^2)=\Omega((\tau-2\eta)^2). $$

This holds even under the stronger promise that an unrestricted stable bijection can be obtained from the prediction by changing only four agents’ partners.

An exact algorithm uses $O(m\tau)$ queries and $O(L+m\tau)$ word-RAM time, where $m=\tau$. Consequently, the query complexity is $\Theta(\tau^2)$ when $\tau-2\eta=\Theta(\tau)$. No uniformly tight upper bound in terms of $\tau-2\eta$ alone is asserted.

The lower bound below is proved directly for both permitted query operations. Thus it repairs the oracle-model gap identified in NOTES without requiring an interpretation of the preference queries in [McCauley et al., Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02254v1#S5).

1. Stable flows and stable bijections

For a bijection $\mu$, let its associated flow be

$$ x^\mu_{ij}= \begin{cases} 1/\tau,&j=\mu(i),\\ 0,&j\neq\mu(i). \end{cases} $$

A pair $(i,j)$ blocks $\mu$ precisely when

$$ p_i(j)<p_i(\mu(i)) \quad\text{and}\quad q_j(i)<q_j(\mu^{-1}(j)). $$

For $x^\mu$, these inequalities are equivalent to positivity of the two sums in the question’s definition of instability. Therefore $x^\mu$ is stable if and only if $\mu$ is stable.

Lemma 1. Every perfect bijection contained in the positive support of a stable flow is stable.

Proof. Suppose $x_{i,\mu(i)}>0$ for every $i$. If $(i,j)$ blocked $\mu$, then

$$ \sum_{u:p_i(u)>p_i(j)}x_{iu} \geq x_{i,\mu(i)}>0 $$

and

$$ \sum_{v:q_j(v)>q_j(i)}x_{vj} \geq x_{\mu^{-1}(j),j}>0. $$

This contradicts stability of $x$. The argument tests every original pair, including pairs outside the windows. $\square$

We also need the following decomposition, including its support property.

Lemma 2. If $z$ is a nonnegative square matrix with every row and column sum equal to $1$, then

$$ z=\sum_{a=1}^{t}\lambda_a P^{\mu_a}, \qquad \lambda_a>0, \qquad \sum_{a=1}^{t}\lambda_a=1, $$

where every $\mu_a$ uses only positive entries of $z$.

Proof. More generally, consider a nonnegative residual matrix whose row and column sums all equal some $c>0$. In its positive-support bipartite graph, let $S$ be any set of rows and $N(S)$ its neighboring columns. The mass leaving $S$ is $c|S|$, and it enters columns with total capacity $c|N(S)|$. Hence

$$ |S|\leq |N(S)|. $$

Hall’s theorem states that this condition, for every row subset in a finite bipartite graph with equally sized sides, guarantees a perfect matching. Thus the residual matrix has a supported permutation $\mu$.

Subtract $\delta P^\mu$, where $\delta$ is the minimum of the residual entries used by $\mu$. Nonnegativity is preserved, all row and column sums become $c-\delta$, and at least one positive entry disappears. Repeat if $c-\delta>0$.

No positive entry is introduced, so there are at most $\tau^2$ iterations. The process finishes with the zero matrix. The subtracted coefficients are positive and sum to $1$, by considering any row sum of the original matrix. Every selected permutation uses positive entries of the original matrix. $\square$

For a perfect flow $x$, the matrix $z=\tau x$ satisfies Lemma 2. If $x$ is stable, Lemma 1 makes every component bijection stable. Any restriction on positive-support edges is inherited by every component. Consequently,

$$ \boxed{ \text{A window-supported perfect stable flow exists} \iff \text{a window-supported stable bijection exists}. } $$

2. Exact interpretation of the displaced-mass promise

Let $y$ be an unrestricted perfect stable flow. Apply Lemma 2 to $\tau y$, and write

$$ t_a=|\{i:\mu_a(i)\neq\pi(i)\}|. $$

Every component $\mu_a$ is stable, and the decomposition gives

$$ 2\sum_i\left(\frac1\tau-y_{i,\pi(i)}\right) = \frac2\tau\sum_a\lambda_a t_a. $$

The promise therefore implies

$$ \sum_a\lambda_a t_a\leq3. $$

At least one $t_a$ is at most $3$: otherwise every term in this finite weighted average would exceed $3$.

For any two bijections $\mu,\pi$, exactly as many hospitals as residents change partners. Indeed, if

$$ S=\{i:\mu(i)\neq\pi(i)\}, $$

then agreement outside $S$ and bijectivity imply $\mu(S)=\pi(S)$. Precisely those hospitals have changed partners. Thus $t_a\leq3$ means at most six agents change partners.

Conversely, a stable bijection changing at most three resident partners satisfies

$$ 2\sum_i\left(\frac1\tau-x^\mu_{i,\pi(i)}\right) = \frac{2|\{i:\mu(i)\neq\pi(i)\}|}{\tau} \leq\frac6\tau. $$

Hence the fractional promise is equivalent to the existence of an unrestricted stable bijection changing at most six agents.

3. A direct lower-bound construction

Put $n=\tau=m$ and $k=n-2\eta$. We construct hard instances whenever $k\geq4$, which suffices for the requested asymptotic regime. Define

$$ s=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor, \qquad e=k-2s\in\{0,1\}. $$

Thus $s\geq2$ and

$$ n=2s+2\eta+e. $$

3.1 Agents, prediction, and baseline rankings

There are core residents and hospitals

$$ R_A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_s\}, \qquad R_B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_s\}, $$

$$ H_A=\{A_1,\ldots,A_s\}, \qquad H_B=\{B_1,\ldots,B_s\}. $$

There are also $\eta$ upper dummy pairs, $\eta$ lower dummy pairs, and $e$ extra dummy pairs. Write their hospital sets as $H_U,H_D,H_X$, and their resident sets as $R_U,R_D,R_X$, respectively.

The prediction pairs $a_u$ with $A_u$, $b_v$ with $B_v$, and every dummy with its corresponding dummy.

Every dummy pair ranks each other first; all its remaining rankings are fixed arbitrarily. All unspecified orders below are also fixed in advance.

For each $u$, resident $a_u$ has the baseline list

$$ [\,H_A\setminus\{A_u\}\mid H_U\mid A_u\mid H_D\mid H_B\mid H_X\,]. $$

For each $v$, hospital $B_v$ has the baseline list

$$ [\,R_B\setminus\{b_v\}\mid R_U\mid b_v\mid R_D\mid R_A\mid R_X\,]. $$

Hospital $A_u$ ranks $a_u$ first, then all of $R_B$, then every remaining resident. Resident $b_v$ ranks $B_v$ first, then all of $H_A$, then every remaining hospital.

These lists are complete and strict.

Baseline stability. Every dummy has its first choice under $\pi$. Every hospital in $H_A$ and every resident in $R_B$ also has its first choice. A blocking pair could therefore only involve a resident in $R_A$ and a hospital in $H_B$. But every resident in $R_A$ ranks its predicted hospital above every hospital in $H_B$. Thus $\pi$ is stable, so the baseline instance is a YES instance satisfying the promise with zero displaced mass.

3.2 One modified instance for every hidden pair

For each $u$, choose a map

$$ g_u:\{1,\ldots,s\}\longrightarrow H_A\setminus\{A_u\} $$

whose fibers have size at most two. Such a map exists: list the $s-1$ target hospitals and assign the $s$ arguments cyclically to this list. Only its first target is used twice.

Similarly, for each $v$, choose

$$ f_v:\{1,\ldots,s\}\longrightarrow R_B\setminus\{b_v\} $$

with fibers of size at most two.

For every candidate $(u,v)\in\{1,\ldots,s\}^2$, modify exactly two lists:

  • In $a_u$’s list, swap $B_v$ with $g_u(v)$.
  • In $B_v$’s list, swap $a_u$ with $f_v(u)$.

All other lists remain at baseline.

Both swapped-in partners now occupy ranks between $1$ and $s-1$. Their predicted partners remain at rank $s+\eta$. Hence

$$ p_{a_u}(A_u)-p_{a_u}(B_v)\geq\eta+1, $$

$$ q_{B_v}(b_v)-q_{B_v}(a_u)\geq\eta+1. $$

In particular, $(a_u,B_v)$ blocks $\pi$, and its edge lies outside both windows.

3.3 Every modified instance is a NO instance

In every baseline or modified list of a resident in $R_A$, the predicted hospital has rank $s+\eta$. All other core hospitals have ranks in

$$ \{1,\ldots,s-1\} \quad\text{or}\quad \{s+2\eta+1,\ldots,2s+2\eta\}. $$

Every such rank differs from $s+\eta$ by at least $\eta+1$. The same assertion holds for the predicted resident and all other core residents on each hospital list in $H_B$. The swaps preserve this property.

In every stable bijection, each dummy must be paired with its corresponding dummy: otherwise that mutually first-choice pair would block.

Now suppose a stable bijection satisfies the windows. Since dummy hospitals are occupied by their dummy partners, each resident in $R_A$ must receive a core hospital. Its window permits only its predicted core hospital. Likewise, each hospital in $H_B$ must receive its predicted resident. Together these assignments determine the entire prediction $\pi$.

But $\pi$ is blocked by $(a_u,B_v)$ in the modified instance. Therefore no stable bijection satisfies the windows. Section 1 implies that no window-supported perfect stable flow exists either.

3.4 Every modified instance satisfies the promise

For the modification indexed by $(u,v)$, define a bijection $\sigma$ by

$$ \sigma(a_u)=B_v, \qquad \sigma(b_v)=A_u, $$

with all other residents retaining their predicted partners.

We prove that $\sigma$ is stable.

Dummy endpoints cannot participate in a blocking pair because they retain their first choices. Hospitals $A_c$ with $c\neq u$ and residents $b_d$ with $d\neq v$ also retain their first choices. After excluding those endpoints, all remaining pairs have one of four forms.

  • $(a_c,B_d)$. If $c\neq u$, resident $a_c$ ranks its predicted hospital above every hospital in $H_B$. If $c=u$ and $d\neq v$, resident $a_u$ ranks its new partner $B_v$, now in the upper core block, above every other hospital in $H_B$. The remaining pair $(a_u,B_v)$ is assigned.
  • $(a_c,A_u)$. If $c=u$, resident $a_u$ prefers its new partner $B_v$ to $A_u$. If $c\neq u$, hospital $A_u$ ranks its assigned resident $b_v$ above $a_c$, because it ranks all of $R_B$ above $R_A\setminus\{a_u\}$.
  • $(b_v,B_d)$. If $d=v$, hospital $B_v$ prefers its new partner $a_u$ to $b_v$. If $d\neq v$, resident $b_v$ ranks its assigned hospital $A_u$ above $B_d$, because it ranks all of $H_A$ above $H_B\setminus\{B_v\}$.
  • $(b_v,A_u)$. This pair is assigned.

Thus no pair blocks $\sigma$.

Exactly two residents and two hospitals change partners. Its associated stable flow therefore satisfies

$$ 2\sum_i\left(\frac1n-x^\sigma_{i,\pi(i)}\right) = \frac4n \leq\frac6n. $$

Every modified instance satisfies the required promise.

4. The adversary works for both query operations

There are $s^2$ modified instances, all with the same public support, masses, prediction, and bound.

Fix a query and compare its baseline answer with its answers in all modified instances.

For a query to resident $a_u$’s list:

  • A specified-partner rank query can change only if the specified partner is $B_v$ or $g_u(v)$. A hospital in $H_B$ identifies at most one candidate; a hospital in $H_A$ belongs to at most two fibers of $g_u$.
  • A specified-rank partner query can change only at the baseline position of $B_v$ or $g_u(v)$. A lower core position identifies at most one candidate; an upper core position identifies at most two.

Therefore any query to this list distinguishes the baseline from at most two candidates.

The same argument applies to hospital $B_v$’s list, using the fiber bound for $f_v$. Every other list is unchanged in every modification. Hence:

$$ \boxed{\text{Every permitted query distinguishes at most two candidates.}} $$

Let a deterministic exact algorithm make $Q$ queries on the baseline instance. If

$$ 2Q<s^2, $$

some candidate modification agrees with every answer along that baseline transcript. Inductively, determinism forces the algorithm to make the same next query after every identical prefix. It therefore also stops with the same answer on that modification.

The baseline is YES, whereas the modification is NO, and both satisfy the promise. This contradicts correctness. Consequently,

$$ Q\geq \left\lceil\frac{s^2}{2}\right\rceil = \left\lceil \frac12\left\lfloor\frac{\tau-2\eta}{2}\right\rfloor^2 \right\rceil. $$

For $k\geq4$, $\lfloor k/2\rfloor\geq k/3$, so in particular

$$ Q\geq\frac{k^2}{18}. $$

This proves the required $\Omega((\tau-2\eta)^2)$ lower bound uniformly as $\tau-2\eta\to\infty$, including when both query operations are available.

5. An exact quadratic-query algorithm

The following upper bound does not require the displaced-mass promise.

Query every list using partner-at-rank queries, and invert the lists to obtain all ranks. Define the original window endpoints

$$ a_i=\max\{1,p_i(\pi(i))-\eta\}, \qquad b_i=\min\{\tau,p_i(\pi(i))+\eta\}, $$

$$ c_j=\max\{1,q_j(\pi^{-1}(j))-\eta\}, \qquad d_j=\min\{\tau,q_j(\pi^{-1}(j))+\eta\}. $$

Tighten the upper endpoints to

$$ B_i= \min\left( \{b_i\}\cup \{p_i(j)-1:q_j(i)<c_j\} \right), $$

$$ D_j= \min\left( \{d_j\}\cup \{q_j(i)-1:p_i(j)<a_i\} \right). $$

Keep precisely the edges

$$ E= \{(i,j): a_i\leq p_i(j)\leq B_i,\; c_j\leq q_j(i)\leq D_j \}. $$

An upper endpoint below its lower endpoint produces an empty list.

5.1 Every original-window stable bijection uses only edges of $E$

Let $\mu$ be stable and satisfy the original windows.

If $q_j(i)<c_j$, hospital $j$ prefers resident $i$ to its assignment, whose rank is at least $c_j$. Moreover, $i$ cannot be assigned to $j$, because that would violate $j$’s window. Stability and strict rankings therefore imply

$$ p_i(\mu(i))<p_i(j), $$

and hence $p_i(\mu(i))\leq p_i(j)-1$. Taking all such hospitals gives

$$ p_i(\mu(i))\leq B_i. $$

Symmetrically, if $p_i(j)<a_i$, resident $i$ prefers $j$ to its assignment, and stability implies

$$ q_j(\mu^{-1}(j))\leq q_j(i)-1. $$

Taking all such residents gives $q_j(\mu^{-1}(j))\leq D_j$. The original lower endpoints already hold, so $\mu$ uses only edges of $E$.

It is also stable on $E$: a blocking pair in $E$ would be an original blocking pair.

5.2 Every perfect matching stable on $E$ is originally stable

Let $\mu$ be perfect and stable on $E$. Pairs in $E$ cannot block. For an excluded pair $(i,j)$, at least one defining inequality of $E$ fails:

  • If $p_i(j)<a_i$, then $D_j\leq q_j(i)-1$, so hospital $j$ prefers its assignment to $i$.
  • If $q_j(i)<c_j$, then $B_i\leq p_i(j)-1$, so resident $i$ prefers its assignment to $j$.
  • If $p_i(j)>B_i$, resident $i$ prefers its assignment, whose rank is at most $B_i$.
  • If $q_j(i)>D_j$, hospital $j$ prefers its assignment, whose rank is at most $D_j$.

Thus excluded pairs cannot block either. Since $E$ is contained in the original windows, $\mu$ is an original-window stable bijection.

5.3 Deferred acceptance decides whether such a matching exists

Run sequential resident-proposing deferred acceptance on $E$, with inherited strict preferences. Accept exactly when the output is perfect.

For completeness, its correctness follows from two rejection arguments.

First, the output is stable on $E$. A resident who prefers an acceptable hospital to its final assignment must have proposed there and been rejected. Once a hospital rejects a resident, its held partner only improves, so that pair cannot block at termination. Unmatched agents regard every acceptable partner as preferable to being unmatched.

Second, suppose $E$ admits a perfect stable matching $\mu$. Consider the earliest rejection of any edge $(i,\mu(i))$. Hospital $j=\mu(i)$ rejects $i$ in favor of some $i'$ whom it strictly prefers. Since $\mu$ is a bijection, $j\neq\mu(i')$. Resident $i'$ has proposed to $j$. If $i'$ preferred $\mu(i')$ to $j$, it would already have been rejected by $\mu(i')$, contradicting earliest rejection. Otherwise $(i',j)$ blocks $\mu$, also a contradiction.

Thus no edge of $\mu$ is ever rejected. No resident can exhaust its list while unmatched, because its partner in $\mu$ is on that list. The deferred-acceptance output is therefore perfect whenever a perfect stable matching exists on $E$.

Sections 5.1–5.2 and the flow–bijection equivalence now prove the algorithm’s correctness.

6. Queries, running time, and conclusion

There are $2\tau$ lists, each of length $m=\tau$. Recovering them requires at most $2m\tau$ queries.

List inversion, endpoint computation, filtering in the recovered preference order, and deferred acceptance each use $O(m\tau)$ word operations. Deferred acceptance makes at most one proposal per retained edge. Thus the bounds are:

| Resource | Bound |
|---|---|
| Preference queries | $O(m\tau)$ |
| Word-RAM running time, including unit-cost queries | $O(L+m\tau)$ |
| Auxiliary storage | $O(m\tau)$ words |

Charging each rank/index word operation $O(\log(m+\tau))$ bit operations gives the corresponding accounting bound

$$ O\bigl(L+m\tau\log(m+\tau)\bigr). $$

No fractional-flow computation is needed.

Together, the direct adversary and exact algorithm establish

$$ \Omega((\tau-2\eta)^2) \leq Q_{\mathrm{det}}(\tau,\eta) \leq O(m\tau)=O(\tau^2). $$

In particular, $o((\tau-2\eta)^2)$ queries cannot suffice as $\tau-2\eta\to\infty$, even under the displaced-mass promise.

4. Both query operations and the adversary

A specified-partner rank query changes only when it names one of the swapped entries. A specified-rank partner query changes only when it names one of their original positions.

For a fixed resident list, a lower core partner or position selects at most one candidate. An upper core partner or position selects at most two candidates by the stipulated fiber-size bound. The hospital-list argument is symmetric. All other lists are unchanged.

The only wording defect is:
