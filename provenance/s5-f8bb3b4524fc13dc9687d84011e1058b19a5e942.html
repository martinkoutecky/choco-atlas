Theorem 7 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a population distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) retailer types, where each type \(t\) has a rational nonnegative demand \(d_t(\omega)\) in each finite scenario \(\omega\) with rational probability \(q_\omega\), and a rational allocation rate \(a_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t \mu_t a_t=C(\mu)\), decide whether there is a coalition mass vector \(x\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\) such that \(\sum_t x_ta_t>C(x)\), where \(C(x)=\min_{y\ge0}2\sum_\omega q_\omega\left|\sum_t x_td_t(\omega)-y\right|\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) retailer types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) explicitly listed joint demand scenarios \(\Omega\), with binary rational probabilities \(q_\omega>0\) summing to \(1\). Each type has binary rational demand \(d_t(\omega)\geq0\) in each scenario; no independence is assumed.
Mass is divisible: a coalition is any real vector \(x\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\). Ordering is free, transportation costs are zero, and holding and shortage costs are both \(2\) per unit; its cost is \(C(x)=\min_{y\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}}2\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}q_\omega\left|\sum_t x_td_t(\omega)-y\right|\).
The input includes signed binary rational allocation rates \(a_t\), promised to satisfy \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=C(\mu)\). A coalition blocks exactly when \(\sum_t x_ta_t>C(x)\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is deciding whether a blocking coalition exists in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For indivisible retailers, detecting a blocking coalition is NP-hard even with two equiprobable scenarios and these fixed costs ([Chen–Zhang](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=14b7e1dd7883296924c559049b44a0b8c4aed9ba&repid=rep1&type=pdf)).
Bounded measure newsvendor games, including nonatomic societies, have nonempty cores ([Montrucchio–Scarsini](https://www.carloalberto.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/no.15.pdf)); this is an existence result. No complexity classification for the decision problem above was located.

Result

Deciding whether a blocking coalition exists is in $\mathrm P$. It reduces to feasibility of a rational linear program with $\tau+m+1$ variables and $O(\tau+m)$ constraints.

The reduction uses divisibility to replace the strict blocking inequality by a non-strict inequality with gain at least $1$.

1. Attainment of the coalition cost

For any nonnegative vector $v\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{\tau}$, define its scenario demands and expected loss by
$$ D_\omega(v)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}v_td_t(\omega), \qquad H(v,y)=2\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}q_\omega|D_\omega(v)-y|. $$

When $v$ is a feasible coalition, its cost is $\min_{y\geq0}H(v,y)$.

This minimum is attained. Indeed, let
$$ M(v)=\max_{\omega\in\Omega}D_\omega(v)\geq0. $$
For $y>M(v)$, all scenario demands are at most $M(v)$, so
$$ H(v,y)-H(v,M(v)) =2\sum_\omega q_\omega\bigl(y-M(v)\bigr) =2\bigl(y-M(v)\bigr)>0. $$
Consequently, minimizing over $y\geq0$ is equivalent to minimizing the continuous function $H(v,\cdot)$ over the compact interval $[0,M(v)]$.

Also, for every $\lambda>0$,
$$ D_\omega(\lambda v)=\lambda D_\omega(v), \qquad H(\lambda v,\lambda y)=\lambda H(v,y). $$
These identities will justify both scaling steps below.

2. The rational linear feasibility problem

Introduce real variables
$$ v_t\geq0\quad(t=1,\ldots,\tau),\qquad y\geq0,\qquad u_\omega\geq0\quad(\omega\in\Omega). $$

Test feasibility of the following system:
$$ \begin{aligned} u_\omega&\geq \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}v_td_t(\omega)-y &&(\omega\in\Omega),\\ u_\omega&\geq y-\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}v_td_t(\omega) &&(\omega\in\Omega),\\ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}a_tv_t-2\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}q_\omega u_\omega &\geq1. \end{aligned} $$

Here $v$ is an auxiliary nonnegative vector without the coalition upper bounds. The first two inequalities imply
$$ u_\omega\geq|D_\omega(v)-y|. $$

We prove that this system is feasible if and only if a blocking coalition exists.

3. Proof of equivalence

A blocking coalition yields a feasible point

Suppose $x\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]$ blocks. By attainment, choose $y^*\geq0$ such that
$$ C(x)=H(x,y^*). $$
Its blocking gain is
$$ \delta=\sum_ta_tx_t-C(x)>0. $$

Set
$$ v_t=\frac{x_t}{\delta}, \qquad y=\frac{y^*}{\delta}, \qquad u_\omega=\frac{|D_\omega(x)-y^*|}{\delta}. $$
These variables are nonnegative. Linearity of scenario demand gives
$$ u_\omega=|D_\omega(v)-y|, $$
so both inequalities for each scenario hold. Moreover,
$$ \sum_ta_tv_t-2\sum_\omega q_\omega u_\omega =\frac{\sum_ta_tx_t-H(x,y^*)}{\delta} =1. $$
Thus the linear system is feasible.

A feasible point yields a blocking coalition

Conversely, suppose $(v,y,u)$ satisfies the system. Since all $u_\omega$ are nonnegative,
$$ \sum_ta_tv_t\geq1+2\sum_\omega q_\omega u_\omega\geq1. $$
In particular, $v\neq0$.

Every listed mass $\mu_t$ is strictly positive. Hence
$$ \lambda= \min\left\{ 1,\ \min_{t:v_t>0}\frac{\mu_t}{v_t} \right\} $$
is well-defined and strictly positive. Set $x=\lambda v$. For coordinates with $v_t>0$, the definition of $\lambda$ gives $x_t\leq\mu_t$; coordinates with $v_t=0$ have $x_t=0$. Therefore $x$ is a feasible coalition.

Using the admissible order quantity $\lambda y$, the scenario inequalities and positivity of the probabilities give
$$ \begin{aligned} C(x) &\leq H(x,\lambda y)\\ &=\lambda H(v,y)\\ &\leq2\lambda\sum_\omega q_\omega u_\omega. \end{aligned} $$
Consequently,
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_ta_tx_t-C(x) &\geq \lambda\left(\sum_ta_tv_t-2\sum_\omega q_\omega u_\omega\right)\\ &\geq\lambda>0. \end{aligned} $$
Thus $x$ blocks. This completes the equivalence proof.

4. Polynomial running time

The standard rational linear-programming theorem states that feasibility of a finite system of linear inequalities with binary rational coefficients can be decided exactly in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. It applies to unbounded feasible regions and does not require strict inequalities. See [Khachiyan’s polynomial-time linear-programming theorem](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

Our system has:

  • $\tau+m+1$ variables;
  • $\tau+3m+2$ inequalities, counting nonnegativity;
  • coefficients consisting only of input numbers $d_t(\omega)$, $a_t$, $q_\omega$, their negatives, $2q_\omega$, and constants.

Its encoding length is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$: demand coefficients are repeated only a constant number of times, and multiplying a binary rational probability by $2$ increases its encoding length by at most a constant. The system can therefore be constructed and tested in polynomial time.

No approximation of a strict inequality is involved: strict blocking is exactly equivalent to feasibility with the fixed non-strict margin $1$.

The efficiency promise $\sum_t\mu_ta_t=C(\mu)$ is not needed by the reduction, so the algorithm applies in particular to all promised inputs. No independence assumption is used. The scaling into the coalition box relies on divisible mass and strictly positive support masses; this step is unavailable for indivisible participation.
