The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) candidates and a finite support of τ complete ranking types with binary-rational masses μ_t summing to \(1\), orient each pair by weighted majority with a fixed tie rule. Compute any candidate of maximum out-degree, with complexity measured in \(m\), τ, mass bit length \(L\), and ℓ, the number of pairwise losses of a Copeland winner; determine whether the \(O(\ell m)\) tally bound can be improved or matched by a lower bound for explicit profiles.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(m\geq2\). A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) distinct strict complete ranking types \(\succ_t\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total bit length of the masses.
Orient \(a\to b\) when \(\sum_{t:a\succ_t b}\mu_t>1/2\); at equality, orient toward the smaller candidate label. A Copeland winner has maximum out-degree in this tournament; output any such candidate.
Let \(\ell\) be the minimum in-degree, unknown to the algorithm. The explicit support is freely accessible. A pairwise tally evaluates the majority mass for one unordered pair; count tallies separately from all profile access and arithmetic, which contribute to bit running time. Express running times in \(m,\tau,L,\ell\).

Is finding a Copeland winner for this rational-mass society solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?
For explicit profiles, can the \(O((\ell+1)m)\) pairwise-tally bound be improved, or does a matching worst-case lower bound hold?

For arbitrary tournaments accessed through an arc oracle, a deterministic algorithm finds every Copeland winner without knowing \(\ell\), using linear auxiliary space and \(O(\ell m)\) time and lookups when \(\ell\geq1\); a matching randomized oracle lower bound is established. [Beretta et al.](https://pages.di.unipi.it/rossano/assets/pdf/papers/TKDE23b.pdf)
These oracle results do not establish a tally lower bound for algorithms with access to explicit ranking supports; no such result was verified in the sources checked.

The unrestricted worst-case tally question remains unresolved here. Beyond the verified notes, I prove:

These are structural algorithms: they count candidates satisfying rank inequalities, without aggregating the supporting masses of individual candidate pairs.

1. Exact arithmetic and polynomial-time solvability

Write the masses as $\mu_t=p_t/q_t$, with positive binary-encoded integers. This representation also covers dyadic masses. Define

$$ D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}q_t, \qquad w_t=p_t(D/q_t). $$

Then each $w_t$ is a positive integer, and normalization gives

$$ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}w_t=D. $$

All these integers have $O(L)$ bits. Elementary multiplication and division construct them in $O(\tau L^2)$ bit operations.

For each ranking, construct the inverse rank array $r_t$, where $r_t(a)$ is the position of candidate $a$. Smaller positions mean higher preference. This takes $O(\tau m\log m)$ bit operations.

For an ordered pair of distinct candidates, define

$$ S(a,b)=\sum_{t:r_t(a)<r_t(b)}w_t. $$

Comparing $2S(a,b)$ with $D$ determines the majority direction; equality is resolved toward the smaller label. One tally costs

$$ O\bigl(\tau(L+\log m)\bigr) $$

bit operations.

Strict completeness gives $S(a,b)+S(b,a)=D$. Thus the majority rule and label tie-breaking define exactly one arc for each unordered pair.

Computing all unordered pairs, accumulating out-degrees, and selecting a maximum takes

$$ O\bigl( \tau L^2+\tau m\log m+ m^2\tau(L+\log m) \bigr) $$

bit operations. This is polynomial in the explicit input length $O(\tau m\log m+L)$. Consequently, winner finding belongs to $\mathrm{FP}$; an NP-hardness result under standard polynomial-time reductions would imply $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$.

2. A self-contained parameterized tally algorithm

Here is a proof of the $O((\ell+1)m)$ bound that handles $\ell=0$ and requires no advance knowledge of $\ell$.

Filtering at a threshold

Fix a nonnegative integer $k$. Initially, every candidate is active and has a loss counter equal to zero.

Process candidate labels in increasing order. When candidate $a$ is active, compare it with each currently active candidate of larger label. Increment the loser's counter. Delete a candidate immediately when its counter reaches $k+1$. If $a$ is deleted, stop its comparisons and continue with the next label.

Each unordered pair is compared at most once during filtering. Hence every recorded loss is against a distinct opponent.

The number of filtering comparisons is at most $m(k+1)$: each comparison increments one counter, and no counter is incremented after reaching $k+1$.

Let $A$ be the final active set. Every pair of candidates in $A$ was compared: its smaller endpoint remained active during its processing, as did the larger endpoint. Each candidate in $A$ therefore has in-degree at most $k$ in the induced tournament on $A$. Writing $s=|A|$, we obtain

$$ \frac{s(s-1)}2 = \sum_{a\in A}d^-_A(a) \leq sk. $$

Thus, when $A$ is nonempty,

$$ |A|\leq2k+1. $$

In fact, $A$ cannot become empty: a deletion removes only the loser of a comparison between two active candidates.

Every deleted candidate has at least $k+1$ actual losses. In particular, no candidate of actual in-degree at most $k$ is deleted.

Verification and stopping

For every candidate in $A$, determine its complete in-degree by comparing it with all other candidates. This uses at most $(2k+1)(m-1)$ additional lookups.

If the smallest verified in-degree is at most $k$, output a candidate attaining it. This candidate is globally optimal: every deleted candidate has in-degree greater than $k$.

If every verified in-degree exceeds $k$, then every candidate has in-degree greater than $k$, so $\ell>k$.

Run phases with thresholds

$$ 0,1,2,4,8,\ldots $$

until verification succeeds. It necessarily succeeds once $k\geq\ell$, because every minimum-in-degree candidate then survives filtering. If $\ell\geq1$, the successful threshold is at most $2\ell$. Summing phase costs gives

$$ P=O((\ell+1)m) $$

arc lookups.

An ordered doubly linked list of active candidates supports the filtering traversal and deletions in constant word operations per comparison. The loss counters, activity information, and list use $O(m)$ words. Each word has $O(\log m)$ bits.

Implementing each lookup by the exact tally from Section 1 gives bit running time

$$ B= O\bigl( \tau L^2+\tau m\log m+ (\ell+1)m\,\tau(L+\log m) \bigr). $$

3. Reusing supporting-type sets

The support-sensitive improvement from the notes also follows directly.

For each requested unordered pair, name its endpoints $u,v$ so that $u\succ_1 v$, and form

$$ U(u,v)=\{t:u\succ_t v\}. $$

Every such set contains type $1$, so at most $2^{\tau-1}$ different sets can occur.

For the first requested pair with a given set $U$, tally the pair and store

$$ \sigma(U)= \operatorname{sgn}\left(2\sum_{t\in U}w_t-D\right). $$

Subsequent pairs with that set reuse the stored sign. A positive sign gives $u\to v$, a negative sign gives $v\to u$, and a zero sign invokes the current pair's label tie-break. Storing the sign rather than an endpoint is necessary for tied pairs.

Every new set corresponds to an actual request and causes one tally. Consequently,

$$ Q\leq\min\{P,2^{\tau-1}\}, $$

and hence

$$ Q= O\left(\min\{(\ell+1)m,2^{\tau-1}\}\right). $$

A binary trie on the membership bits implements this cache on demand. It creates $O(\tau Q)$ nodes. Since $Q=O(m^2)$ and the positive-mass encoding has $L\geq\tau$ up to encoding constants, node addresses have $O(L+\log m)$ bits. Constructing and searching keys therefore preserves the bit-time bound $B$ above.

This improvement depends on $\tau$ and does not improve the unrestricted worst case by itself.

4. A rank-dominance counting lemma

The zero-tally algorithms use the following elementary geometric counting result.

Lemma. Fix $d\in\{2,3,4\}$. Suppose each candidate $a$ is assigned a point

$$ p(a)=(p_1(a),\ldots,p_d(a))\in\{1,\ldots,m\}^d, $$

and the values in each coordinate are distinct. All counts

$$ N(a)= \left| \{b:p_i(b)>p_i(a)\text{ for every }i\} \right| $$

can be computed in $O(m\log^d m)$ bit operations.

Proof. Put $h=\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$. Sweep points in decreasing first coordinate. Before inserting $p(a)$, the inserted points are exactly those with first coordinate greater than $p_1(a)$. Query an upper orthant in the remaining $s=d-1$ coordinates, then insert $p(a)$.

For completeness, construct the required dynamic counting structure on the known points as follows.

For $s=1$, sort and compress the coordinate values and use a Fenwick tree of insertion counts. Updates and suffix queries take $O(h)$ word operations.

For $s>1$, construct a balanced segment tree on the first coordinate. At every node, construct an $(s-1)$-coordinate structure on the points belonging to that node's interval. Each point belongs to $O(h)$ nodes.

An insertion updates the auxiliary structures on one root-to-leaf path. An upper-orthant query decomposes the first-coordinate suffix into $O(h)$ disjoint node intervals and queries their auxiliary structures. Induction gives $O(h^s)$ word operations per insertion or query.

The structures use $O(mh^{s-1})$ words. Their coordinate lists and initially zero counters can be constructed in $O(mh^s)$ word operations: at each recursion level, the total number of point occurrences increases by at most an $O(h)$ factor, and sorting a list of $p$ points costs $O(ph)$ word operations.

Here $s\leq3$. Coordinates, counters, and addresses therefore have $O(h)$ bits. Preprocessing and the $m$ queries and insertions take

$$ O(mh^s h)=O(mh^d) $$

bit operations. The structures start with empty insertion counters. This proves the lemma. $\square$

5. Zero tallies for every society with at most three types

There are three exhaustive cases.

5.1 A mass greater than one half

If $\mu_h>1/2$, every pair follows ranking $h$: its preferred endpoint already has supporting mass greater than $1/2$.

The top candidate of that ranking is the unique Copeland winner, and $\ell=0$. No pairwise tally is needed.

This observation applies for arbitrary $\tau$.

5.2 A mass equal to one half

Suppose $\mu_h=1/2$. All remaining types have total mass $1/2$, and their masses are positive.

For any pair, ranking $h$ determines the majority direction unless every remaining type disagrees with it. In that exceptional case, the majority is tied and the smaller label wins. Indeed, agreement by even one remaining type increases the supporting mass of ranking $h$'s preferred endpoint strictly above $1/2$.

Start with the out-degree in ranking $h$,

$$ d_h(a)=m-r_h(a). $$

Define the sets of losses and gains relative to this ranking by

$$ A_a= \left\{ b<a: r_h(b)>r_h(a),\ r_t(b)<r_t(a)\text{ for every }t\ne h \right\}, $$

and

$$ B_a= \left\{ b>a: r_h(b)<r_h(a),\ r_t(b)>r_t(a)\text{ for every }t\ne h \right\}. $$

The preceding characterization gives exactly

$$ d^+(a)=m-r_h(a)-|A_a|+|B_a|. $$

To verify the two corrections:

  • A base win becomes a loss precisely when all remaining types prefer the opponent and that opponent has the smaller label. These are the opponents in $A_a$.
  • A base loss becomes a win precisely when all remaining types prefer $a$ and $a$ has the smaller label. These are the opponents in $B_a$.

Both counts are upper-orthant counts in $\tau+1$ coordinates. For $A_a$, use the point

$$ \left( m+1-a,\ r_h(a),\ (m+1-r_t(a))_{t\ne h} \right). $$

Every coordinate of $b$'s point exceeds the corresponding coordinate of $a$'s point exactly when $b\in A_a$.

For $B_a$, use

$$ \left( a,\ m+1-r_h(a),\ (r_t(a))_{t\ne h} \right). $$

Again, coordinatewise strict domination is exactly the required membership condition.

When $\tau\leq3$, Section 4 computes both families of counts in

$$ O(m\log^{\tau+1}m) $$

bit operations. Each counting run starts with empty counters. No supporting mass of a candidate pair is aggregated.

For arbitrary $\tau$, one may instead check the displayed rank conditions directly for every ordered pair. This still uses zero tallies and takes

$$ O\bigl(\tau L^2+\tau m^2\log m\bigr) $$

bit operations, including preprocessing.

5.3 Three masses strictly below one half

Suppose $\tau=3$ and $\mu_t<1/2$ for every $t$.

A singleton coalition has mass below $1/2$. Every two-type coalition has mass

$$ 1-\mu_t>1/2, $$

where $t$ is the omitted type. Thus a candidate beats an opponent exactly when at least two rankings prefer it. There are no tied pairwise majorities.

For a nonempty set of types $S$, define

$$ D_S(a)= \left| \{b:r_t(b)>r_t(a)\text{ for every }t\in S\} \right|. $$

Then

$$ d^+(a)= D_{\{1,2\}}(a)+ D_{\{1,3\}}(a)+ D_{\{2,3\}}(a)- 2D_{\{1,2,3\}}(a). $$

To prove the identity, fix an opponent $b$ and let $q$ be the number of rankings preferring $a$ to $b$. Its contribution to the right-hand side is zero for $q=0,1$, one for $q=2$, and $3-2=1$ for $q=3$. These are exactly its contributions to the out-degree.

The three two-type counts are two-dimensional dominance counts. The three-type count is a three-dimensional dominance count. Section 4 therefore computes every score in $O(m\log^3 m)$ bit operations, without any pairwise mass aggregation.

Combining the exhaustive cases proves:

Theorem. Every society with $\tau\leq3$ has a zero-tally Copeland-winner algorithm with bit running time

$$ O(L^2+m\log^4 m). $$

If $\tau=3$ and all masses are below $1/2$, the bound improves to

$$ O(L^2+m\log^3 m). $$

For $\tau=2$, the bound is $O(L^2+m\log^3 m)$.

6. Zero tallies can coexist with large minimum in-degree

Three types, with no tied majorities

Let $m=3k$, and partition the labels into consecutive blocks

$$ X=\{1,\ldots,k\},\quad Y=\{k+1,\ldots,2k\},\quad Z=\{2k+1,\ldots,3k\}. $$

Use rankings

$$ X\succ_1Y\succ_1Z,\qquad Y\succ_2Z\succ_2X,\qquad Z\succ_3X\succ_3Y, $$

with increasing labels within every block, and masses

$$ \mu_1=\frac14,\qquad \mu_2=\mu_3=\frac38. $$

These are positive dyadic masses, all below $1/2$. Between blocks, the supporting masses are respectively $5/8$, $5/8$, and $3/4$, giving

$$ X\to Y,\qquad Y\to Z,\qquad Z\to X. $$

Within each block, all rankings follow increasing labels.

A candidate in position $j$ within a block loses to the $k$ candidates in the block beating its own, and to the $j-1$ earlier candidates in its own block. Hence

$$ d^-(a)=k+j-1, \qquad \ell=k=\frac m3. $$

The zero-tally algorithm computes a winner in $O(L^2+m\log^3 m)$ bit time. Thus the zero-tally result for three-type supports includes profiles with $\ell$ linear in $m$, even without majority ties.

Unbounded support size in the one-half-mass class

The same parameters can occur with growing $\tau$.

Give mass $1/2$ to the ranking

$$ Y\succ Z\succ X, $$

with increasing labels within blocks. Choose $s$ distinct rankings having block order

$$ Z\succ X\succ Y, $$

allowing arbitrary orders inside blocks, and give each mass $1/(2s)$. There are $(k!)^3$ possible rankings of this form. Taking $s$ to be a power of two makes all masses dyadic.

Between blocks, the first ranking disagrees unanimously with the remaining support on $X,Y$ and on $Y,Z$, so label tie-breaking gives $X\to Y$ and $Y\to Z$. All types give $Z\to X$.

Within a block, the mass-$1/2$ ranking prefers the smaller label. That endpoint either wins strictly or wins the tie. Thus the same calculation yields $\ell=k$.

The support size is $\tau=s+1$ and can grow with $m$. Section 5.2 nevertheless supplies a polynomial-time zero-tally algorithm throughout this class.

7. What remains open

The polynomial-time classification is settled. The generic parameterized bound, its support-sensitive refinement, and the zero-tally results above are proved.

They do not settle the unrestricted worst-case tally question. Easy classes with growing support do not exclude a lower bound witnessed by other profiles. Likewise, the randomized lower bound of Beretta et al. concerns arc-oracle access, where rankings and masses are unavailable; it does not directly constrain algorithms using the explicit support. [Beretta et al., Theorem 3.2](https://pages.di.unipi.it/rossano/assets/pdf/papers/TKDE23b.pdf)

The remaining task is to prove either an improvement applicable to every explicit profile, or a matching worst-case lower bound that accounts for unrestricted profile access and arithmetic.
