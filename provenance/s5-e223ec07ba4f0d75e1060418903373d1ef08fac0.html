The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given institutions with rational per-capita capacities and a finite support of applicant types with rational masses, where each type has a complete unit-demand choice function and a priority tier at each institution, characterize when a stable mass assignment exists for every priority profile over tiers, and determine whether such an assignment can be computed in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the input bit length \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S\) contain \(m\geq1\) institutions with positive binary-rational per-capita capacities \(q_s\). A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(w_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t w_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type has an explicitly tabulated choice function \(C_t:2^S\to2^S\), with \(C_t(A)\subseteq A\) and \(|C_t(A)|\leq1\), and a tier label \(r_{ts}\) at each institution. Empty choices from nonempty menus are permitted. Institution \(s\) is acceptable to \(t\) exactly when \(C_t(\{s\})=\{s\}\).
A priority profile specifies, independently at each institution, a strict order \(\succ_s\) of its supplied tier labels and an outside-option label \(\bot_s\). Type \(t\) is institutionally acceptable exactly when \(r_{ts}\succ_s\bot_s\). Members of the same tier are tied; ties are not broken. Universal quantification ranges over all such orders, keeping tier membership fixed.
A mass assignment has real entries \(x_{ta}\geq0\) for \(a\in S\cup\{0\}\), where \(0\) means unmatched, with \(\sum_a x_{ta}=w_t\) and loads \(\ell_s=\sum_t x_{ts}\leq q_s\). Types may split their mass. Individual rationality requires \(x_{ts}=0\) unless both sides find the assignment acceptable.
Write \(B_0=\varnothing\) and \(B_a=\{a\}\) for \(a\in S\). A blocking move exists if some \(t,a,s\), with \(a\neq s\), satisfy \(x_{ta}>0\), \(C_t(B_a\cup\{s\})=\{s\}\), and \(r_{ts}\succ_s\bot_s\), and either \(\ell_s<q_s\) or some \(u\) has \(x_{us}>0\) and \(r_{ts}\succ_s r_{us}\). Stability means individual rationality and no blocking move; equal-tier displacement does not block.

Which capacity, mass, choice, and tier-membership data admit a stable mass assignment for every priority profile? Is deciding this property in \(\mathrm{P}\) or NP-hard; on data satisfying it, is computing an exact rational stable assignment for a supplied profile polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or NP-hard?

For finite applicants with positive capacities and strict institutional priorities, universal stable existence is equivalent to weak acyclicity (no directed cycle among acceptable institutions, with \(a\to b\) when \(C_t(\{a,b\})=\{b\}\)) and acceptable-consistency (no unacceptable institution chosen over an acceptable one from a binary menu); a constructive algorithm is known. [Caspari–Khanna](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.06815v2).
For a continuum with strict applicant preferences and atomless institutional scores, stable matchings exist and admit a cutoff characterization. This establishes a neighboring result under different choice and priority assumptions; no result for the exact tiered problem above was identified. [Azevedo–Leshno](https://eduardomazevedo.github.io/papers/Azevedo-Leshno-Supply-and-Demand-Matching.pdf).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total applicant mass to one so capacities are per capita; required nonempty support, nonempty institutions, and strictly positive capacities, consistent with the discrete necessity arguments.
  • Interpreted complete choice functions as fully specified functions, permitting empty choices from nonempty menus; chose explicit tables and included their encoding in L.
  • Kept supplied tier membership fixed and interpreted every priority profile as every independent strict ordering of tier labels together with an institutional outside option.
  • Used strict priority improvement for displacement, with no tie-breaking or blocking through equal-tier displacement.
  • Defined assignments as divisible real masses, including unmatched mass, and blocking through positive source mass and positive vacancy or lower-priority assigned mass.
  • Interpreted computation as producing an exact rational stable assignment for a supplied profile on data having universal stable existence.

2. Definitions and a sufficient condition

For each type $t$, define its individually acceptable set
$$ A_t=\{s\in S:C_t(\{s\})=\{s\}\}. $$
Let $G_t$ be the directed graph on $A_t$ having an edge $a\to b$ precisely when
$$ a\ne b,\qquad C_t(\{a,b\})=\{b\}. $$

Call $C_t$ acceptable-consistent if
$$ a\in A_t,\quad b\notin A_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C_t(\{a,b\})\ne\{b\}. $$

Only singleton and binary-menu entries affect stability: every menu in a blocking move is either $\{s\}$ or $\{a,s\}$. Larger-menu entries still contribute to the explicit input length.

Proposition 1. If every $G_t$ is acyclic and every $C_t$ is acceptable-consistent, then every priority profile admits a stable mass assignment. An exact rational assignment can be computed in polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$.

Proof. Since $G_t$ is acyclic, choose a strict ordering of $A_t$ that places $b$ above $a$ whenever $a\to b$. Such an ordering exists by topological sorting. Place being unmatched below every element of $A_t$.

Fix the supplied priority profile. Retain only pairs $(t,s)$ acceptable to both sides. At each institution, refine its tier ordering to a strict ordering of the retained types, preserving every comparison between distinct tiers.

We use the following standard bipartite stable-allocation theorem. Given strict preference lists, positive vertex quotas, and nonnegative edge capacities, a stable allocation exists and is computable in polynomial time. An edge blocks when it has residual capacity and each endpoint either has unused quota or has positive allocation on a worse edge. Unused quota can be represented by dummy partners ranked last. Polynomial algorithms for this formulation are described by [Dean–Munshi, Sections 2–3](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

Apply this theorem with type quota $w_t$, institution quota $q_s$, and edge capacity
$$ u_{ts}=\min(w_t,q_s). $$
These capacities satisfy the required endpoint bounds. The graph has at most $m\tau$ edges. Let $x_{ts}$ be the resulting allocation and set
$$ x_{t0}=w_t-\sum_{s\in S}x_{ts}. $$
This is a feasible, individually rational mass assignment.

Suppose it has an original blocking move $(t,a,s)$. The target $s$ belongs to $A_t$: when $a=0$, this follows from the singleton choice; when $a\in S$, individual rationality gives $a\in A_t$, and acceptable-consistency excludes choosing an individually unacceptable target. Thus $(t,s)$ is a retained edge.

The constructed type ordering ranks $s$ above $a$, or above being unmatched when $a=0$. The institutional blocking comparison also survives the refinement of tiers.

The positive source mass gives
$$ x_{ts}<w_t. $$
A target vacancy gives $x_{ts}\leq\ell_s<q_s$. Alternatively, strict displacement involves another type $u$ with $x_{us}>0$, giving
$$ x_{ts}<\ell_s\leq q_s. $$
Consequently,
$$ x_{ts}<\min(w_t,q_s)=u_{ts}. $$
The retained edge therefore has residual capacity and satisfies both endpoint blocking conditions, contradicting stability of the auxiliary allocation.

For exact arithmetic, scale by the product $D$ of the input denominators. Its bit length is $O(L)$. The quotas and edge capacities become integers. The cited algorithms use augmentations obtained through addition, subtraction, and minima of residual quantities, so integer scaled data remain integer. The polynomial number of operations involves numbers of polynomial bit length. Scaling back gives exact rational output. $\square$

3. New local mass obstructions

For a type $t$ and institution $s$, define its individually acceptable tier cohort
$$ T_s(t)=\{u:r_{us}=r_{ts},\ s\in A_u\}, \qquad M_s(t)=\sum_{u\in T_s(t)}w_u. $$
When $s\in A_t$, this cohort includes $t$.

The profiles used below accept only $t$’s tier at selected institutions and reject every tier at the remaining institutions. These are legitimate profiles: put the selected tier above the outside option and every other tier below it.

3.1. An obstruction from acceptable-inconsistency

Lemma 2. Suppose
$$ a\in A_t,\qquad b\notin A_t,\qquad C_t(\{a,b\})=\{b\}, $$
and
$$ q_a>M_a(t)-w_t, \qquad q_b>M_b(t). $$
Then universal stable existence fails.

Proof. Choose a profile accepting only $t$’s tier at $a$ and $b$, and rejecting all types at every other institution.

Individual rationality permits type $t$ to be assigned only to $a$ or to be unmatched. Suppose a stable assignment exists.

If $x_{ta}=0$, then $x_{t0}=w_t>0$. Every possible occupant of $a$ belongs to $T_a(t)\setminus\{t\}$, so
$$ \ell_a\leq M_a(t)-w_t<q_a. $$
The unmatched mass of $t$ blocks the vacancy at $a$. Thus $x_{ta}>0$ is necessary.

Every possible occupant of $b$ belongs to $T_b(t)$, so
$$ \ell_b\leq M_b(t)<q_b. $$
Type $t$ is institutionally acceptable at $b$. Its positive mass at $a$, together with the specified binary choice, therefore blocks the vacancy at $b$. This is a contradiction. $\square$

3.2. An obstruction from strongly connected choice sets

For $A\subseteq A_t$, define
$$ U_t(A)=\bigcup_{s\in A}T_s(t), \qquad W_t(A)=\sum_{u\in U_t(A)}w_u, \qquad Q(A)=\sum_{s\in A}q_s. $$

Lemma 3. Suppose $A\subseteq A_t$, $|A|\geq2$, and the induced graph $G_t[A]$ is strongly connected. If
$$ q_s>M_s(t)-w_t\quad\text{for every }s\in A, $$
and
$$ Q(A)>W_t(A), $$
then universal stable existence fails.

Proof. Accept only $t$’s tier at every institution in $A$ and reject every tier elsewhere. Suppose a stable assignment exists.

Every occupant of $s\in A$ belongs to $T_s(t)$. If $s$ is full, then
$$ x_{ts} =\ell_s-\sum_{u\ne t}x_{us} \geq q_s-\bigl(M_s(t)-w_t\bigr)>0. $$
Thus every full institution in $A$ contains positive mass of $t$.

If $x_{t0}>0$, singleton choices force every institution in $A$ to be full.

Otherwise all of $t$’s mass is assigned within $A$, so $x_{ta}>0$ for some $a\in A$. For any edge $a\to b$, a vacancy at $b$ would be blocked by this positive source mass. Hence $b$ must be full, and the preceding inequality gives $x_{tb}>0$.

This argument propagates along directed paths. Strong connectivity and $|A|\geq2$ ensure that every vertex, including the initial vertex, is reachable from $a$ by a positive-length directed walk. Therefore every institution in $A$ is full.

But all institutional mass in this profile comes from types in $U_t(A)$. Feasibility would consequently require
$$ Q(A)=\sum_{s\in A}\ell_s \leq \sum_{u\in U_t(A)}w_u=W_t(A), $$
contrary to the hypothesis. $\square$

These are sufficient obstructions, not converses.

Both families can be checked in polynomial time under the stated encoding. Lemma 2 requires checking type–institution pairs. For Lemma 3, enumerate at most $\tau 2^m$ choices of $(t,A)$ and test strong connectivity and the rational inequalities. Since a complete choice table is supplied, $2^m=O(L)$; therefore this enumeration is polynomial in $L$.

4. A complete characterization when eligible tier masses fit

For each institution $s$ and supplied tier label $R$, define
$$ M_s(R)=\sum_{\substack{t:r_{ts}=R\\s\in A_t}}w_t. $$
This counts only the tier’s individually acceptable types.

Consider the following two hypotheses:

  • Every tier’s individually acceptable mass fits:

$$ q_s\geq M_s(R) \quad\text{for every }s,R. $$

  • If a tier contains any individually unacceptable type, the inequality is strict:

$$ \bigl(\exists t:\ r_{ts}=R,\ s\notin A_t\bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q_s>M_s(R). $$

Theorem 4. Under these two hypotheses, universal stable existence holds if and only if every $G_t$ is acyclic and every $C_t$ is acceptable-consistent. Recognition and exact rational construction for a supplied profile are polynomial-time solvable.

Proof.

Necessity of acceptable-consistency. Suppose type $t$ violates acceptable-consistency at $(a,b)$. The first hypothesis gives
$$ q_a\geq M_a(t)>M_a(t)-w_t, $$
where the strict inequality uses $w_t>0$. Since $t$ is individually unacceptable at $b$, the second hypothesis gives
$$ q_b>M_b(t). $$
Lemma 2 supplies a profile without a stable assignment.

Necessity of acyclicity. Suppose $G_t$ contains a directed cycle. Let $A$ be the vertices of a simple directed cycle. Its induced graph is strongly connected, and $|A|\geq2$.

For every $s\in A$, the first hypothesis gives
$$ q_s\geq M_s(t)>M_s(t)-w_t. $$
For each $u\in U_t(A)$, let
$$ k_u=|\{s\in A:u\in T_s(t)\}|. $$
Every such $k_u$ is at least $1$, and $k_t=|A|$, because $A\subseteq A_t$. Therefore
$$ \sum_{s\in A}M_s(t) =\sum_{u\in U_t(A)}k_uw_u \geq W_t(A)+(|A|-1)w_t >W_t(A). $$
Consequently,
$$ Q(A)\geq\sum_{s\in A}M_s(t)>W_t(A). $$
Lemma 3 again supplies a profile without a stable assignment.

Sufficiency. Proposition 1 applies without any capacity hypothesis.

Algorithms. The tier masses and the two hypotheses can be checked by exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time. Acyclicity is checked by graph search, and acceptable-consistency by inspecting singleton and binary choices. Proposition 1 provides polynomial exact construction whenever these checks succeed. $\square$

The acyclicity conclusion actually needs only the first capacity hypothesis. The second hypothesis is used solely to force acceptable-consistency.

A convenient stronger capacity condition implies both hypotheses. Define the total mass of a tier, including individually unacceptable members, by
$$ \widehat M_s(R)=\sum_{t:r_{ts}=R}w_t. $$
If
$$ q_s\geq\widehat M_s(R)\quad\text{for every }s,R, $$
then the first hypothesis holds. If the tier contains an individually unacceptable type $t$, positivity of $w_t$ gives
$$ q_s\geq\widehat M_s(R)\geq M_s(R)+w_t>M_s(R), $$
so the second hypothesis also holds.

Thus Theorem 4 includes:

  • arbitrary tier memberships when every $q_s\geq1$;
  • distinct type labels at each institution whenever $q_s\geq w_t$ for every individually acceptable pair $(t,s)$.

Neither the support size nor the number of tiers needs to be bounded.

5. Why the capacity hypotheses cannot simply be dropped

Cycles can be universally stable below the tier-mass threshold

Take one type of mass $1$ and three acceptable institutions $a,b,c$, each of capacity $\tfrac14$. Set
$$ C(\{a,b\})=\{b\},\qquad C(\{b,c\})=\{c\},\qquad C(\{c,a\})=\{a\}. $$
Set the choice from the three-element menu to empty.

For any profile, fill every institution accepting the type and leave the remaining mass unmatched. The total required institutional mass is at most $\tfrac34$. Every institutionally acceptable target is full, and the sole type cannot strictly displace itself. Hence the assignment is stable under every profile.

The acceptable-choice graph contains a cycle, while its tier mass at each institution is $1>\tfrac14$.

Equality at a mixed tier can protect acceptable-inconsistency

Take two institutions and two types with
$$ w_t=w_u=q_a=q_b=\tfrac12. $$
Both types share a tier at each institution. Specify
$$ C_t(\{a\})=\{a\},\quad C_t(\{b\})=\varnothing,\quad C_t(\{a,b\})=\{b\}, $$
and
$$ C_u(\{a\})=\varnothing,\quad C_u(\{b\})=\{b\},\quad C_u(\{a,b\})=\{b\}. $$

For every profile, assign $t$ entirely to $a$ when their shared tier is accepted there, and otherwise leave $t$ unmatched. Assign $u$ entirely to $b$ when the shared tier is accepted there, and otherwise leave $u$ unmatched.

Unmatched mass either faces institutional rejection or its acceptable institution is full. The only additional possible move is $t$ from $a$ to $b$. If its tier is accepted at $b$, that institution is full with the equal-tier mass of $u$, so displacement does not block.

Thus these data are universally stable despite $t$’s acceptable-inconsistency. Here
$$ M_a(R)=M_b(R)=\tfrac12=q_a=q_b. $$
The first hypothesis of Theorem 4 holds, but its strict inequality for mixed tiers fails.

6. General characterization and complexity bounds

The preceding results do not characterize all instances. For completeness, the following gives an exact finite characterization without the capacity hypotheses.

Fix a priority profile. Let $\succeq_s$ denote the reflexive closure of $\succ_s$, and define
$$ H_s=\{\bot_s\}\cup\{r_{ts}:r_{ts}\succ_s\bot_s\}. $$
Choose $h\in\prod_s H_s$.

A position $(t,a)$, with $a\in S\cup\{0\}$, is allowed when:

  • if $a\in S$, then $a\in A_t$, $r_{ta}\succ_a\bot_a$, and $r_{ta}\succeq_a h_a$;
  • for every $s\in S\setminus\{a\}$ satisfying $r_{ts}\succ_s h_s$,

$$ C_t(B_a\cup\{s\})\ne\{s\}. $$

The second condition also tests individually unacceptable targets, as the task’s blocking definition requires.

Let $F(h)$ be the feasibility system
$$ y_{ta}\geq0,\qquad \sum_{a\in S\cup\{0\}}y_{ta}=w_t, $$
$$ y_{ta}=0\quad\text{at disallowed positions}, $$
$$ \sum_t y_{ts}\leq q_s,\qquad \sum_t y_{ts}=q_s\quad\text{if }h_s\ne\bot_s. $$

Theorem 5. The supplied profile admits a stable assignment if and only if $F(h)$ is feasible for some $h$.

Proof. Given a stable assignment $x$, set $h_s=\bot_s$ when $s$ has a vacancy. Otherwise set $h_s$ to the lowest occupied tier. This tier exists because $q_s>0$, fullness implies positive load, and the support is finite.

Individual rationality verifies the first allowed-position condition at every positive position. If a positive position violated the second condition, its proposed target would either have a vacancy or contain positive mass in the lower cutoff tier. The target is institutionally acceptable because its tier lies strictly above $h_s\succeq_s\bot_s$. The resulting move would block. Thus $x$ satisfies $F(h)$.

Conversely, suppose $y$ satisfies $F(h)$. Its positive positions are individually rational. A blocking target with a vacancy must have $h_s=\bot_s$, because any other cutoff imposes fullness. Institutional acceptability then gives $r_{ts}\succ_s h_s$. For a blocking displacement of $u$, the first allowed-position condition gives
$$ r_{ts}\succ_s r_{us}\succeq_s h_s. $$
In either case, the second allowed-position condition prohibits the required choice of $s$. Therefore $y$ is stable. $\square$

Flow feasibility and exact output

Each $F(h)$ is a feasible-circulation problem. Use vertices $p,z$, one vertex for each type, and one for each destination. Add arcs:

  • $p\to t$ with lower and upper bounds $w_t$;
  • $t\to a$ for allowed positions, with bounds $[0,1]$;
  • $s\to z$ with bounds $[q_s,q_s]$ when $h_s\ne\bot_s$, and $[0,q_s]$ otherwise;
  • $0\to z$ with bounds $[0,1]$;
  • $z\to p$ with bounds $[1,1]$.

Conservation enforces precisely the displayed feasibility constraints. The assignment-arc upper bound $1$ is sufficient because total mass is $1$. Rational feasible circulation is decidable in polynomial time.

Let $D$ be the product of all input denominators. Scaling produces integer bounds. Integral circulation feasibility then shows that every feasible real system $F(h)$ has a solution whose entries are integer multiples of $1/D$. Assignment entries lie in $[0,1]$, so their bit lengths are polynomial in $L$.

Hence real stable existence always implies the existence of an exact rational stable assignment of polynomial encoding length.

Supplied-profile bounds

There are at most $(\tau+1)^m$ cutoff vectors. Exact existence and construction therefore take
$$ (\tau+1)^m\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
time.

The explicit-table encoding gives $m=O(\log L)$ and $\tau=O(L)$, so this is at most
$$ 2^{O((\log L)^2)}. $$
Supplied-profile existence also belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$: a cutoff vector is a polynomial-length certificate, verified by the flow test.

Polynomial algorithms with bounded numbers of tiers

Let $d_s$ be the number of supplied tier labels occurring among types at institution $s$. Labels occurring in no type can be discarded because they affect no relevant comparison.

There are $(d_s+1)!$ relevant orders at $s$, including the outside option, and at most $d_s+1$ cutoffs for each order. Thus universal recognition takes at most
$$ \left(\prod_{s\in S}(d_s+1)!(d_s+1)\right) \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
time.

If $d_s\leq d$ for a fixed constant $d$, the enumeration factor is at most
$$ \bigl((d+1)!(d+1)\bigr)^m. $$
Since $m=O(\log L)$, this is polynomial in $L$. The same argument makes supplied-profile construction polynomial.

This establishes polynomial recognition and exact construction for bounded numbers of tiers, even when $\tau$ is unbounded.

For unrestricted tier counts, universal recognition belongs to $\Pi_2^p$: a profile has polynomial encoding length, and the property has the form “for every profile, there exists a cutoff vector passing a polynomial-time flow test.”

7. What remains unresolved

Theorem 4 settles both requested algorithmic questions on its stated capacity class. The bounded-tier result settles another class with unbounded support. Lemmas 2 and 3 provide polynomially checkable certificates of failure beyond those classes.

For unrestricted data, the cutoff-and-flow criterion characterizes universal existence, but the arguments do not give a polynomial recognition algorithm or establish NP-hardness. The quasipolynomial construction algorithm likewise does not settle polynomial construction on the entire universal-existence promise class.
