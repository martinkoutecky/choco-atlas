The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many goods, a finite support of complete positive-bid SSPMA types \(t\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), and a feasible rational per-capita target \(q\), find an equilibrium price vector \(p\) and distributions \(\lambda_{t,x}\) over each type's integral bundles \(x\). Require \(\sum_x\lambda_{t,x}=1\), \(\lambda_{t,x}>0\) only when \(x\) is demanded by type \(t\) at \(p\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t\sum_x\lambda_{t,x}x=q\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge 1\) goods. A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), encoded in binary and summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Each type \(t\) is a finite multiset \(B_t\) of complete positive SSPMA bids \((b,w_b)\), where \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\) lists a value for every good and \(w_b\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) is an integer capacity; all integers are encoded in binary. Put \(K=\sum_t|B_t|\) and \(W_t=\sum_{b\in B_t}w_b\).
For a bid, let \(\Delta_{w_b}=\{y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\sum_i y_i\le w_b\}\) and \(D_b(p)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{y\in\Delta_{w_b}}\langle b-p,y\rangle\). Type demand is \(D_t(p)=\{\sum_{b\in B_t}y_b:y_b\in D_b(p)\}\); all maximizing bundles are allowed, without fixed tie-breaking.
The target supply is \(q\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m\) with \(\sum_iq_i\le\sum_t\mu_tW_t\). Prices may have either sign. A distribution is represented by its nonzero rational probabilities on integral bundles.
Find an equilibrium price vector \(p\in\mathbb R^m\), represented exactly by rational coordinates, and distributions \(\lambda_{t,x}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_x\lambda_{t,x}=1\), \(\lambda_{t,x}>0\Rightarrow x\in D_t(p)\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t\sum_x\lambda_{t,x}x=q\).
Is this equilibrium computation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length, or is it NP-hard?
For finitely many agents with complete positive SSPMA bids and feasible integral supply, equilibrium prices are optimal supply dual variables of a network linear program and are computable in polynomial time in the numbers of goods and bids. This discrete result does not state the complexity of the normalized mass problem with distributions over integral bundles.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted positive bids as positive integer weights, with nonnegative integer values for every good, following the discrete bid domain; values need not be strictly positive.
  • Made feasibility explicit as nonnegative rational supply whose total quantity does not exceed the mass-weighted total bid capacity.
  • Allowed prices of either sign, following the discrete equilibrium pricing domain; no separate seller or disposal convention is added.
  • Retained every maximizing bid allocation at ties and defined type demand by summing integral bid demands.
  • Specified sparse rational representations of bundle distributions and exact rational price coordinates so the computational output has a finite encoding.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,K,L\). An exact rational equilibrium can be computed with at most \(1+m|B_t|\) bundles in the distribution of each type \(t\), hence at most \(\tau+mK\) bundles altogether.

The proof has two parts: compute rational prices and average allocations by a linear program, then convert those averages into distributions over integral demanded bundles.

1. A rational transportation linear program

Index every bid occurrence separately, so repeated bids in a multiset have separate indices. Write \(J_t\) for the occurrences belonging to type \(t\), and \(J\) for their union. Thus \(|J|=K\). For \(j\in J_t\), write its values and capacity as \(b_{ji}\) and \(w_j\), and put \(r_j=\mu_t w_j\).

Introduce an outside option indexed by \(0\), with \(b_{j0}=0\). Allocating capacity to this option represents leaving it unused.

If \(K=0\), the supply hypothesis implies \(q=0\). Taking \(p=0\) and assigning probability \(1\) to the zero bundle for every type solves the problem. Henceforth assume \(K>0\).

Consider the linear program

\( \displaystyle \max_{z\ge 0}\ \sum_{j\in J}\sum_{i=1}^{m} b_{ji}z_{ji} \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle \sum_{i=0}^{m}z_{ji}=r_j \quad (j\in J), \qquad \sum_{j\in J}z_{ji}=q_i \quad (1\le i\le m). \)

Here \(z_{ji}\) is the contribution of bid occurrence \(j\) to the society’s average allocation of coordinate \(i\).

Feasibility. Let \(C=\sum_j r_j>0\) and \(Q=\sum_iq_i\). The hypothesis gives \(Q\le C\). A feasible solution is

\( \displaystyle z_{ji}=\frac{r_jq_i}{C}\quad (i\ge1), \qquad z_{j0}=\frac{r_j(C-Q)}{C}. \)

The row sums are \(r_j\), and each real-good column sums to \(q_i\). All entries are nonnegative.

Boundedness. Every feasible variable satisfies \(0\le z_{ji}\le r_j\). Thus the feasible region is compact, and the maximum exists and is finite.

The dual linear program is

\( \displaystyle \min_{p,u}\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}p_iq_i+\sum_{j\in J}r_ju_j \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle u_j+p_i\ge b_{ji}\quad (j\in J,\ 1\le i\le m), \qquad u_j\ge0. \)

The goods variables \(p_i\) are unrestricted in sign. The inequalities \(u_j\ge0\) arise from the outside-option variables.

The dual is feasible: take \(u_j=0\) and \(p_i=\max_{j\in J}b_{ji}\). By linear-programming duality, primal and dual optima exist and have equal values.

We use the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem: a rational linear program can be solved in time polynomial in its dimensions and input bit length; whenever a finite optimum exists, a rational optimal solution of polynomial bit length can be returned. Our programs have \(K(m+1)\) primal variables and \(K+m\) equality constraints, with rational coefficients of polynomial total bit length. Consequently, rational optimal solutions \(z,p,u\) can be computed in polynomial time.

2. Positive allocations use only maximizing options

Set \(p_0=0\), and define the net value of coordinate \(i\) for bid \(j\) by

\( \displaystyle d_{j0}=0,\qquad d_{ji}=b_{ji}-p_i\quad (i\ge1),\qquad M_j=\max_{0\le i\le m}d_{ji}. \)

Dual feasibility gives \(u_j\ge M_j\).

For optimal primal and dual solutions, their objective difference is

\( \displaystyle 0=\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{i=0}^{m}z_{ji}\bigl(u_j+p_i-b_{ji}\bigr). \)

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore,

\( \displaystyle z_{ji}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ u_j+p_i-b_{ji}=0. \)

For each \(j\), the row sum \(r_j\) is positive, so at least one coordinate has \(z_{ji}>0\). At that coordinate, \(u_j=d_{ji}\le M_j\). Together with \(u_j\ge M_j\), this proves

\( \displaystyle u_j=M_j,\qquad z_{ji}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ d_{ji}=M_j. \)

For \(j\in J_t\), normalize its row by defining

\( \displaystyle a_{ji}=\frac{z_{ji}}{\mu_t}\quad (0\le i\le m). \)

Then

\( \displaystyle a_{ji}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{ji}=w_j,\qquad a_{ji}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ d_{ji}=M_j. \)

Thus each normalized row is a fractional allocation of the bid’s integer capacity, using only its maximizing options.

3. An exact rounding lemma

We now give a finite distribution realizing such an average while preserving its zero coordinates.

Lemma. Let \(a_0,\ldots,a_m\) be nonnegative rational numbers whose sum is an integer \(w\). There is a rational distribution over nonnegative integral vectors \(y=(y_0,\ldots,y_m)\) such that:

  • \(\sum_i y_i=w\) for every supported vector;
  • \(\mathbb E[y_i]=a_i\) for every coordinate;
  • \(a_i=0\) implies \(y_i=0\) for every supported vector.

Moreover, the distribution can be constructed using at most \(m+1\) vectors and polynomial-time rational arithmetic.

Proof. Put \(S_{-1}=0\) and \(S_i=\sum_{h=0}^{i}a_h\). For \(v\in[0,1)\), define

\( \displaystyle y_i(v)=\lfloor S_i+v\rfloor-\lfloor S_{i-1}+v\rfloor. \)

Since the cumulative sums are nondecreasing, each \(y_i(v)\) is a nonnegative integer. Telescoping gives

\( \displaystyle \sum_{i=0}^{m}y_i(v)=\lfloor w+v\rfloor-\lfloor v\rfloor=w. \)

If \(a_i=0\), then \(S_i=S_{i-1}\), so \(y_i(v)=0\).

For a uniformly distributed parameter \(V\) on \([0,1)\), one has \(\mathbb E[\lfloor s+V\rfloor]=s\). Indeed, writing \(s=n+\rho\), where \(n\) is integral and \(0\le\rho<1\), the floor is \(n\) with probability \(1-\rho\) and \(n+1\) with probability \(\rho\). Hence

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E[y_i(V)]=S_i-S_{i-1}=a_i. \)

To obtain a finite rational distribution, partition \([0,1)\) at the points \(1-\{S_i\}\) for \(0\le i<m\) with \(\{S_i\}\ne0\), where \(\{s\}=s-\lfloor s\rfloor\). There are at most \(m\) distinct interior points. The vector \(y(v)\) is constant on the interior of each resulting interval.

Choose the midpoint of each interval and give its vector probability equal to the interval length. The endpoints have measure zero, so this finite distribution has the expectations proved above. All endpoints, midpoints, and probabilities are rational. The construction uses at most \(m+1\) intervals and polynomial-time rational arithmetic. This proves the lemma.

4. Polynomial-size distributions for entire types

For each type \(t\), apply the lemma’s rounding formula to every row \(a_j\), \(j\in J_t\), using the same parameter \(v\):

\( \displaystyle y_{ji}(v)=\lfloor S_{ji}+v\rfloor-\lfloor S_{j,i-1}+v\rfloor,\qquad S_{ji}=\sum_{h=0}^{i}a_{jh}. \)

Define the type’s real-good bundle by

\( \displaystyle x_t(v)_i=\sum_{j\in J_t}y_{ji}(v)\quad (1\le i\le m). \)

For each bid, its extended allocation has total \(w_j\), and positive coordinates occur only where \(a_{ji}>0\). Section 2 shows that all such coordinates have net value \(M_j\). Therefore its real-good allocation has surplus

\( \displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{m}(b_{ji}-p_i)y_{ji}(v)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}d_{ji}y_{ji}(v)=w_jM_j. \)

For any feasible real-good allocation \(y\in\Delta_{w_j}\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{m}(b_{ji}-p_i)y_i\le M_j\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_i\le M_jw_j, \)

where the last inequality uses \(M_j\ge0\). Thus the rounded real-good allocation belongs to \(D_j(p)\). Summing over the bids proves

\( \displaystyle x_t(v)\in D_t(p). \)

Now partition \([0,1)\) using the union of all rounding breakpoints for bids in \(J_t\). Each bid contributes at most \(m\) breakpoints, so there are at most \(1+m|B_t|\) intervals. On each interval’s interior, the entire bundle \(x_t(v)\) is constant.

Assign that bundle the rational probability equal to the interval length, merging duplicate bundles if necessary. This defines \(\lambda_{t,x}\). If \(B_t\) is empty, use probability \(1\) on the zero bundle.

The probabilities sum to \(1\), and every supported bundle belongs to \(D_t(p)\). Furthermore,

\( \displaystyle \sum_x\lambda_{t,x}x_i=\mathbb E[x_t(V)_i]=\sum_{j\in J_t}a_{ji}=\frac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_{j\in J_t}z_{ji}. \)

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \sum_t\mu_t\sum_x\lambda_{t,x}x_i=\sum_{j\in J}z_{ji}=q_i \)

for every good \(i\). These are precisely the required equilibrium conditions.

5. Output size and complexity

The optimal LP solutions have polynomial bit length. Dividing by the positive rational masses, taking cumulative sums, computing rational breakpoints and midpoints, sorting them, and evaluating floors all require polynomial-time exact arithmetic.

There are at most \(\tau+mK\) supported bundles altogether. Every coordinate of a type’s bundle is at most \(W_t\), whose binary length is polynomial in the input length. Interval probabilities have polynomial bit length; merging polynomially many duplicate bundles also preserves polynomial bit length.

Thus both the computation and its explicit output have polynomial size in \(m,\tau,K,L\). The normalized masses require fractional flows, but the rounding construction converts those flows into exact distributions over integral demanded bundles without an exponential increase in support.
