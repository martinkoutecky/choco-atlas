Proposition 13 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a candidate set \(C\), a committee \(W\subseteq C\) of size \(k\), and the positive-mass approval types \(\mathcal A\) with rational masses \(\mu_A\) summing to \(1\), decide whether for every \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and every \(S\subseteq\mathcal A\), \(\sum_{A\in S}\mu_A\ge \ell/k\) and \(\left|\bigcap_{A\in S} A\right|\ge\ell\) imply \(\left|W\cap\bigcup_{A\in S}A\right|\ge\ell\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set with \(|C|=m\), and let \(W\subseteq C\) have size \(k\), where \(1\leq k\leq m\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal A\) of \(\tau\) distinct approval sets \(A\subseteq C\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_A\), with \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A=1\). Empty and full approval sets are allowed. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval sets; \(L\) is the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\).
Call \(W\) mass-PJR if, for every \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and every nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal A\),
\(\sum_{A\in S}\mu_A\geq\ell/k\quad\text{and}\quad|\bigcap_{A\in S}A|\geq\ell\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|W\cap\bigcup_{A\in S}A|\geq\ell\).
Is deciding whether \(W\) satisfies mass-PJR in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?
For finite approval electorates, recognizing PJR, equivalently generalized Hare-PSC under dichotomous preferences, is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete; see [Aziz and Huang](https://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~haziz/pjr-test.pdf).
[Cevallos and Stewart](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.12990) define the identical condition for positive real vote strengths, with quota equal to total strength divided by committee size, and claim recognition is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete. Thus the supplied target overlaps an existing complexity claim rather than an unclaimed continuous problem.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified the committee-size domain as 1 ≤ k ≤ m, consistent with the discrete model.
  • Allowed empty and full approval sets, consistent with the approval formulation and its treatment of complete indifference.
  • Restricted quantified coalitions to nonempty subsets: the positive threshold already excludes the empty coalition, and this avoids an undefined empty intersection.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length and required explicit support encoding with distinct approval types.
  • Specified polynomial-time Turing reductions for NP-hardness because the anchor concerns coNP-complete recognition, while the requested target uses NP-hardness.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Deciding whether \(W\) satisfies mass-PJR is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions, and hence \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. It belongs to \(\mathrm P\) if and only if \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof below handles distinct approval sets and works even when every mass is dyadic, meaning its denominator is a power of two.

1. Polynomial verification of a violation

A certificate that \(W\) fails mass-PJR consists of an integer \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and a nonempty subset \(S\subseteq\mathcal A\). Its validity requires exactly these three checks:

\(k\sum_{A\in S}\mu_A\geq\ell,\qquad \left|\bigcap_{A\in S}A\right|\geq\ell,\qquad \left|W\cap\bigcup_{A\in S}A\right|<\ell.\)

The subset has a \(\tau\)-bit encoding, and \(\ell\) needs \(O(\log k)\) bits.

For explicit bit-complexity bounds, write \(\mu_A=p_A/d_A\) and take \(D=\prod_{A\in\mathcal A}d_A\). The bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). Precompute the integer weights \(w_A=p_A(D/d_A)\), using \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations with ordinary integer arithmetic. Then the mass check becomes

\(k\sum_{A\in S}w_A\geq\ell D.\)

Computing the intersection and union takes \(O(m\tau)\) time with approval bit vectors. After preprocessing, certificate verification takes

\(O(m\tau+\tau L+L^2).\)

Thus failure of mass-PJR belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), and recognition belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\). This argument uses exact arithmetic, including at quota equalities.

2. Hardness with dyadic masses

We reduce Balanced Biclique to failure of mass-PJR. This is the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete source problem used in [Aziz and Huang, Theorem 1](https://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~haziz/pjr-test.pdf). Its input is a bipartite graph \(G=(U,V,E)\) and a positive integer \(t\); the question is whether there exist \(t\) vertices on each side with all cross edges present.

Write \(a=|U|\) and \(s=|V|\). We may restrict to

\(2\leq t\leq\min(a,s).\)

Indeed, the excluded cases are decidable directly and can be replaced by fixed yes- or no-instances satisfying this restriction.

Construction

Let \(q\) be the smallest power of two strictly greater than \(s\), and let \(k\) be the smallest power of two at least \(t+1\). Set

\(N=kq,\qquad b=t(q-1),\qquad R=N-s-b=(k-t)q+t-s.\)

These integers satisfy \(q>s\), \(k\geq t+1\), and

\(R\geq q+t-s>0.\)

Create pairwise disjoint candidate sets \(U,H,Z\), where

\(|H|=t-1,\qquad |Z|=k-t+1.\)

Let \(C=U\cup H\cup Z\) and \(W=H\cup Z\). Consequently, \(|W|=k\) and \(m=a+k\).

We construct approval types by initially assigning integer weights:

  • For each \(v\in V\), assign weight \(1\) to its neighborhood \(A_v=\{u\in U:(u,v)\in E\}\).
  • Assign weight \(b\) to \(B=U\cup H\).
  • Assign weight \(R\) to the empty approval set.

Merge identical approval sets by adding their weights. Finally, divide every weight by \(N\).

The resulting support has \(\tau\leq s+2\) distinct approval sets. All masses are positive and sum to one because

\(s+b+R=N.\)

Both \(k\) and \(q\) are powers of two, so \(N\) is a power of two and every mass is dyadic. Also, \(B\) is distinct from every graph neighborhood because \(H\neq\emptyset\).

A biclique produces a violation

Suppose \(U'\subseteq U\) and \(V'\subseteq V\) form a biclique with \(|U'|=|V'|=t\).

Let \(S\) contain \(B\) and every distinct neighborhood type occurring among vertices of \(V'\). Because each selected type includes the weights of *all* vertices having that neighborhood, the combined graph weight in \(S\) is at least \(t\). Hence

\(\sum_{A\in S}\mu_A\geq\frac{b+t}{N}=\frac{tq}{kq}=\frac tk.\)

Every approval set in \(S\) contains \(U'\), so their intersection has size at least \(t\). Their union contains exactly the candidates of \(H\) from \(W\), giving

\(\left|W\cap\bigcup_{A\in S}A\right|=t-1.\)

Thus \(S\) violates mass-PJR at \(\ell=t\).

A violation produces a biclique

Conversely, suppose a nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal A\) violates mass-PJR at an integer \(j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\).

The empty approval set cannot belong to \(S\), because then the intersection would be empty.

Furthermore, \(B\) must belong to \(S\). Otherwise \(S\) consists solely of graph neighborhood types, whose combined integer weight is at most \(s<q\). Its mass would therefore be strictly below

\(\frac qN=\frac1k\leq\frac jk,\)

contradicting the quota condition.

Since \(B\in S\), and no approval set contains a candidate of \(Z\), the represented committee candidates are exactly \(H\). Therefore the violation implies

\(t-1<j,\qquad\text{hence }j\geq t.\)

Let \(g\) be the number of vertices of \(V\) whose neighborhood types belong to \(S\). After merging, the total integer weight of \(S\) is exactly \(b+g\). Its quota condition yields

\(b+g\geq jq,\)

and consequently

\(g\geq jq-t(q-1)=(j-t)q+t\geq t.\)

In particular, \(S\) contains a graph neighborhood type. Its common intersection is therefore contained in \(U\). The cohesion condition says that this intersection contains at least \(j\geq t\) candidates.

Choose any \(t\) such common candidates and any \(t\) of the \(g\) graph vertices represented by the selected types. Every chosen vertex approves every chosen candidate, so they form a balanced biclique.

This proves the exact equivalence:

\(G\text{ has a balanced biclique of size }t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W\text{ fails mass-PJR}.\)

Size and construction time

The chosen powers satisfy \(q\leq2s\) and \(k\leq2t\). Thus

\(m=a+k\leq a+2t,\qquad \tau\leq s+2,\qquad N\leq4st.\)

Every integer weight needs \(O(\log(st))\) bits. Using approval bit vectors, the output length is bounded by

\(L=O(m\tau+\tau\log(st)+m\log m).\)

Even merging neighborhoods by pairwise comparisons takes only \(O(s^2m)\) time; the numerical construction also takes polynomial time. Hence this is a polynomial-time many-one reduction to failure of mass-PJR.

Together with Section 1, failure is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete and recognition is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete.

3. Turing hardness and a deterministic running time

A recognition oracle decides Balanced Biclique with one query: construct the instance above and reverse the oracle’s answer. Thus recognition is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions.

For completeness, there is a direct exhaustive algorithm expressed in the requested parameters. Enumerate every nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal A\), and compute

\(r=\left|W\cap\bigcup_{A\in S}A\right|,\qquad h=\left|\bigcap_{A\in S}A\right|.\)

For this particular \(S\), a violation exists exactly when

\(r+1\leq k,\qquad h\geq r+1,\qquad k\sum_{A\in S}w_A\geq(r+1)D.\)

Indeed, any violating \(\ell\) satisfies \(\ell\geq r+1\), so its cohesion and mass conditions also hold at \(r+1\). Conversely, these checks make \(r+1\) a violating value.

Including preprocessing, this gives the deterministic bound

\(O\!\left(\tau L^2+2^\tau(m\tau+\tau L+L^2)\right).\)

No dependence on the number of all possible approval sets is needed. The complexity classification is consistent with the weighted condition and recognition claim in [Cevallos and Stewart](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.12990); the proof above establishes it for the precise finite encoding in the question.
