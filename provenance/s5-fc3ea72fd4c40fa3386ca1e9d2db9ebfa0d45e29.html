The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a candidate set \(C\) partitioned into \(\rho\) parties, a designated party \(P^\dagger\), and \(\tau\) voter types, each a complete ranking \(\theta\) of \(C\) with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\), is there a nomination of one candidate from each party such that the nominee of \(P^\dagger\) strictly outscores every other nominee under Borda? For a nomination \(x\), the score of candidate \(c\) is \(B_\mu^x(c)=\sum_{\theta}\mu_\theta\bigl(\rho-\operatorname{pos}_{\theta,x}(c)\bigr)\), where rankings are restricted to the nominees.

Result

The mass-based Borda nomination problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even when every party has size at most two and the support size satisfies \(\tau\le 3\), if rational numerators and denominators are encoded in binary.

If “binary-rational” means specifically a dyadic rational, the problem is still \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete, already with \(\tau\le 6\) and constant-size mass encodings.

1. Scores and the source theorem

For a nomination \(x\), let \(N(x)\) be its set of \(\rho\) nominees. For \(c\in N(x)\), define

\[ b_\theta(c,x)= \bigl|\{d\in N(x)\setminus\{c\}:c\text{ precedes }d\text{ in }\theta\}\bigr|. \]

Thus \(b_\theta(c,x)\) is the Borda score from ranking \(\theta\) restricted to the nominees. The mass-based score is

\[ S_\mu(c,x)=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta b_\theta(c,x). \]

Writing \(p=x(P^\dagger)\), the nomination succeeds exactly when \(S_\mu(p,x)>S_\mu(c,x)\) for every \(c\in N(x)\setminus\{p\}\).

We use the following established theorem: explicit-electorate Borda nomination is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard with exactly three voters and every party of size at most two. The paper explicitly defines the problem using a designated party and a unique winner, with strict complete rankings restricted to the nominated candidates. Consequently, its success condition matches the present problem. Its hardness construction has multiple nonempty parties. See [Schlotter, Cechlárová, and Trellová, Theorem 11 and Section 2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-024-09658-5).

2. Exact normalization gives a polynomial reduction

Take an instance of that restricted explicit-electorate problem. Denote its three rankings by \(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3\); they need not be distinct.

Keep the candidates, parties, and designated party unchanged. For each distinct ranking \(\theta\) appearing among the three ballots, let

\[ k_\theta=\bigl|\{i\in\{1,2,3\}:\theta_i=\theta\}\bigr|, \qquad \mu_\theta=\frac{k_\theta}{3}. \]

These masses are positive, sum to one, and have constant-size binary numerator–denominator encodings. The support contains only distinct rankings and has size \(\tau\le 3\).

For every nomination \(x\) and nominee \(c\), its explicit-electorate score is \(S_V(c,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}b_{\theta_i}(c,x)\). Grouping identical rankings gives

\[ S_\mu(c,x) =\sum_\theta\frac{k_\theta}{3}b_\theta(c,x) =\frac{1}{3}S_V(c,x). \]

Hence, for every pair of nominees \(c,d\),

\[ S_\mu(c,x)-S_\mu(d,x) =\frac{1}{3}\bigl(S_V(c,x)-S_V(d,x)\bigr). \]

The positive factor preserves every strict comparison and every tie. Since the feasible nominations are unchanged, the source instance is affirmative if and only if the mass-based instance is affirmative.

After indexing candidates by integers, comparing and merging three rankings and copying the partition takes \(O(m\log m)\) bit operations. The output has \(L=O(m\log m)\), preserves \(\rho\), and preserves the maximum party size. This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction, proving \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness.

3. Hardness also holds for dyadic masses

If masses must have finite binary expansions, the preceding use of \(1/3\) is unavailable. The following exact construction resolves that interpretation too.

Let \(\overline{\theta_i}\) denote the reversal of \(\theta_i\). Initially assign masses to six possibly repeated rankings as follows:

| Ranking | Mass |
|---|---:|
| \(\theta_1\) | \(5/16\) |
| \(\overline{\theta_1}\) | \(3/16\) |
| \(\theta_2\) | \(3/16\) |
| \(\overline{\theta_2}\) | \(1/16\) |
| \(\theta_3\) | \(3/16\) |
| \(\overline{\theta_3}\) | \(1/16\) |

All masses are positive dyadic rationals, and their sum is one. Merge any identical rankings by adding their masses. This yields a valid support of size \(\tau\le 6\), still with constant-size dyadic encodings.

Reversal commutes with restriction to \(N(x)\). Each other nominee lies below \(c\) in exactly one of a ranking and its reversal, so

\[ b_{\overline{\theta_i}}(c,x)=\rho-1-b_{\theta_i}(c,x). \]

In each row pair, the forward mass exceeds the reverse mass by \(2/16\). The reverse masses sum to \(5/16\). Therefore the resulting score is

\[ S_{\mathrm{dyad}}(c,x) =\frac{2}{16}\sum_{i=1}^{3}b_{\theta_i}(c,x) +\frac{5}{16}(\rho-1) =\frac18 S_V(c,x)+\frac{5}{16}(\rho-1). \]

The added term is identical for all nominees, and the multiplicative factor is positive. Thus this construction also preserves every strict comparison and tie, for every nomination.

It takes \(O(m\log m)\) bit operations and preserves the candidates, parties, designated party, and party-size bound. This proves hardness even under the dyadic interpretation.

4. Polynomial verification and running times

A certificate specifies one candidate from each party, using \(O(\rho\log m)\) bits. Its validity can be checked directly.

For exact score verification, write each input mass as \(\mu_\theta=a_\theta/q_\theta\), with binary-encoded positive integers. Set

\[ D=\prod_\theta q_\theta, \qquad w_\theta=a_\theta(D/q_\theta). \]

Every \(w_\theta\) is an integer, and normalization gives \(\sum_\theta w_\theta=D\). Since the total input length is \(L\), the product \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits.

For each ranking, scan from bottom to top, counting nominated candidates encountered. This computes every nominee’s restricted Borda score. Then compute the integer scores

\[ T(c,x)=\sum_\theta w_\theta b_\theta(c,x)=D\,S_\mu(c,x). \]

They satisfy \(0\le T(c,x)\le(\rho-1)D\), so their bit lengths are \(O(L+\log\rho)=O(L)\). Since \(D>0\), comparing these integers verifies the unique-winner condition exactly.

Using ordinary quadratic-time integer arithmetic, a conservative verification bound is \(O\!\left(\tau(m+\rho)L^2\right)\) bit operations. Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathsf{NP}\).

For completeness, exhaustive enumeration gives a deterministic running-time bound of

\[ O\!\left(m^\rho\,\tau(m+\rho)L^2\right), \]

because there are \(\prod_P|P|\le m^\rho\) nominations. This is polynomial for fixed \(\rho\). For unrestricted \(\rho\), the proved classification is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete, so a polynomial-time algorithm exists if and only if \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).
