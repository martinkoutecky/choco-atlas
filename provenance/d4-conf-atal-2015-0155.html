Otherwise, if a maker commitment opposes \(R\), Assert a nonempty set of its negations; otherwise Question a nonempty subset of \(R\setminus A\) if \(R\) is unchanged, and Assert such a subset if it changed or this is the first iteration. \( K=m(m-1) \),

\( U=h(ma+K+b) \).

Here \( K \) counts all possible global comparison atoms, and \( U \) counts all possible preference-information atoms.

If \( m=1 \), the initial recommendation is empty for either goal. Every dialogue immediately succeeds with one terminal move, so the computation is immediate. The argument below also covers this case.

Model-selection lemma. For every base encountered during a dialogue, its compatible model with inclusion-smallest property set can be found by scanning the supplied models.

For a base \( B \), define

\( \mathcal C(B)=\{\pi\in\Pi:B\subseteq\mathcal P(\pi)\} \).

First, \( B\subseteq P_t \) throughout the dialogue for type \( t \). This holds initially. Subsequently, the only preference-information atoms added to \( B \) come from maker commitments, and all such information belongs to \( P_t \). Consequently, the compatibility promise makes \( \mathcal C(B) \) nonempty.

Suppose \( \pi_1,\pi_2\in\mathcal C(B) \). The model \( \pi_{12} \) associated with their property-set intersection satisfies

\( Q_{\pi_{12}}=Q_{\pi_1}\cap Q_{\pi_2} \),

\( \mathcal P(\pi_{12})=\mathcal P(\pi_1)\cap\mathcal P(\pi_2) \).

Because both allowed-atom sets contain \( B \), their intersection contains \( B \). Thus \( \pi_{12}\in\mathcal C(B) \).

Repeatedly applying this argument to the finite nonempty family \( \mathcal C(B) \) produces a compatible model \( \pi_* \) satisfying

\( Q_{\pi_*}=\bigcap_{\pi\in\mathcal C(B)}Q_\pi \).

Therefore \( Q_{\pi_*}\subseteq Q_\pi \) for every compatible model \( \pi \). Property sets identify models uniquely, so \( \pi_* \) is the unique inclusion-smallest compatible model.

It follows that an algorithm can test compatibility for every supplied model and retain one whose property set has minimum cardinality. Every other compatible property set contains \( Q_{\pi_*} \), and a proper superset has larger cardinality. Hence this procedure returns precisely \( \pi[B] \).

With bit vectors for allowed atoms and properties, this scan takes \( O(r(U+q+1)) \) elementary bit-vector operations. No enumeration of subsets of \( Q \) is needed.

Simulation and history-size bounds. Let \( p_t=|P_t| \). Consistency gives the useful bound

\( p_t\leq C:=h\left(m+\binom m2+1\right) \).

Indeed, each criterion permits at most one evaluation assignment per option and one importance assignment. Its strict comparisons contain at most one orientation of each unordered option pair: both orientations would constitute a directed cycle.

\( T_t:=7p_t+2K+1 \).

  • Update and consistency. Set containment, union, intersection, and comparison reversal operate on finite atom sets. Single-valued assignments are checked by scanning assignments. Acyclicity is checked by a topological sort for each criterion. Using adjacency matrices, consistency testing costs \( O(U+h(m+K)) \) elementary operations.

Every utterance contains only a comparison set, a preference-information set where applicable, and a fixed locution label. The respective atom universes have sizes \( K \) and \( U \). Encoding atoms using identifiers derived from the input gives polynomial-length utterances. Consequently, a history containing at most \( T_t \) utterances has polynomial length. Commitment stores and other recorded state also contain only atoms from these finite universes.

The fixed polynomial bound on policy and evaluator computation therefore applies to polynomial-length inputs. It follows that the entire dialogue is computable in polynomial time.

Correctness follows by induction on transitions: initialization matches the specified initial state; each update performs exactly the specified tests and additions; the lemma returns the prescribed model; and deterministic policies select the prescribed utterances and revisions. Thus the simulation returns the actual \( z_t \) and \( \ell_t \).

Explicit running-time bound. The following conservative bound makes all requested parameter dependencies explicit. Let

\( T=\max_t T_t=7\max_t p_t+2K+1 \),

\( N=1+U+K+q+m+h+r+a+b \).

In particular,

\( T\leq 7h\left(m+\binom m2+1\right)+2m(m-1)+1 \).

Choose a fixed integer \( d\geq 1 \) such that policy and evaluator calls on inputs of length \( s \) cost \( O(s^d) \). Such a \( d \) exists by the uniform fixed polynomial-bound promise.

Histories and call inputs can be encoded in \( O(L(T+1)N) \) bits. After indexing atoms, preprocessing costs at most \( O(L^2+LrN) \) bit operations. Per update or utterance, model scanning, set operations, consistency checks, and a direct history scan cost at most

\( O\!\left(L(r+T+1)N\right) \).

For the consistency term, observe that \( h(m+K)\leq 2hK+h\leq 2U+h \), so it is covered by this bound.

Including aggregation proved below, one valid total bit-operation bound is

\( O\!\left( L^2+LrN+ \tau(T+1)\left[(L(T+1)N)^d+L(r+T+1)N\right] +\tau^2LN +\tau\left(L+\log(T+1)\right)^3 \right) \).

The unspecified exponent \( d \) reflects the supplied policies and evaluators; their hypothesis does not support a universal numerical exponent independent of that bound.

Exact rational aggregation. Write each positive input mass as \( \mu_t=u_t/v_t \), with positive integers \( u_t,v_t \), and form the common denominator

\( V=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}v_t \).

Its binary length is at most a constant plus the sum of the input denominator lengths, hence \( O(L) \). Define the integer weights

\( w_t=u_t(V/v_t) \).

Then \( \mu_t=w_t/V \), and the normalization promise gives

\( \sum_t w_t=V \).

For each terminal state actually encountered, compute

\( W_z=\sum_{t:z_t=z}w_t \),

and compute

\( W_\ell=\sum_t w_t\ell_t \).

The required outputs are exactly

\( M_z=W_z/V \),

\( \bar\ell=W_\ell/V \).

Because the weights are positive and \( \ell_t\leq T \),

\( 0<W_z\leq V \),

\( 0<W_\ell\leq VT \).

Thus every intermediate numerator and denominator has \( O(L+\log(T+1)) \) bits. Integer multiplication, exact division, addition, and reduction by greatest common divisors all take polynomial time. A conservative bound using elementary arithmetic is \( O(\tau(L+\log(T+1))^3) \).

Represent terminal recommendations canonically and group equal states. Even direct pairwise grouping costs only \( O(\tau^2LN) \) bit operations. At most \( \tau \) groups are nonempty, and positivity guarantees that precisely those groups have nonzero mass. This avoids enumerating the potentially much larger universe of possible terminal rankings.

Therefore every individual terminal state, every nonzero terminal-state mass, and the exact average move count are computable in polynomial time. Under standard polynomial-time reductions that produce promised instances, \( \mathrm{NP} \)-hardness would consequently imply \( \mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP} \).
