The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_t\) over municipality population types \(p_t\), and a rational average house supply \(H\), form finite instances with \(N\mu_t\) municipalities of type \(t\) and \(NH\) seats, for admissible \(N\). Do the type-level seat masses \(x^{(N)}_{t,k}\) produced by \(Q\) converge as \(N\) grows, and can their limit be computed from \((\mu_t,p_t,H)\)? In particular, is the limit the quota-respecting allocation that assigns each type only \(\lfloor q_t\rfloor\) or \(\lceil q_t\rceil\) seats, where \(q_t=Hp_t/\sum_u\mu_up_u\), filling upper-quota seats in descending priority \(p_t/(\lfloor q_t\rfloor+1)\) and splitting mass at ties?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and positive binary rational populations \(p_t\). The support size \(\tau\) is not the number of possible types. Let \(H\geq0\) be binary rational and \(P=\sum_t\mu_tp_t\).
For each positive integer \(N\) with every \(N\mu_t\) and \(NH\) integral, create municipalities \((t,j)\), \(1\leq j\leq N\mu_t\), of population \(p_t\). Starting with zero seats, at house size \(h+1\) a municipality of type \(t\) currently holding \(a\) seats is eligible exactly when \(a<p_t(h+1)/(NP)\); give the next seat to an eligible municipality maximizing \(p_t/(a+1)\). Break ties by lexicographic order of \((t,j)\), and stop at \(NH\). There is no minimum seat requirement.
Define \(x^{(N)}_{t,k}=N^{-1}\lvert\{j:(t,j)\text{ receives exactly }k\text{ seats}\}\rvert\), for nonnegative integers \(k\).
Define a candidate allocation by \(q_t=Hp_t/P\): give every type its lower quota, then distribute residual seat mass \(H-\sum_t\mu_t\lfloor q_t\rfloor\) among types with nonintegral quotas, each with capacity \(\mu_t\), in descending order of \(p_t/(\lfloor q_t\rfloor+1)\), breaking ties by type index and splitting a type's mass when its capacity is only partly filled.
Do all coordinates \(x^{(N)}_{t,k}\) converge along admissible \(N\), and is their limit the candidate allocation?
On inputs promised to have these limits, is deciding \(\lim_N x^{(N)}_{t,k}\geq b\), for an input type \(t\), binary integer \(k\geq0\), and binary rational \(b\in[0,1]\), in \(\mathrm{P}\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(\tau\) and total input bit length \(L\); there is no candidate-count parameter \(m\).
Discrete \(Q\) satisfies quota and house monotonicity ([Balinski–Young](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~jwrobbin/141dir/balinski-young.pdf)); its original axiomatic characterization has a defect identified by [Mayberry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16592547/).
Polynomial-time computation of discrete \(Q\) is stated under unary house-size encoding ([Brill et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-022-01852-1)). No result settling this replication-limit problem was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized municipality masses to sum to one, so N is the number of municipalities and H is seats per municipality.
  • Specified positive rational populations, nonempty support, and nonnegative rational house supply.
  • Used the zero-minimum version of Q, as in the anchor, rather than its later extension with minimum requirements.
  • Defined admissible N and defined seat masses as municipality counts divided by N.
  • Resolved Q's multivalued tie behavior by a fixed lexicographic order on type and municipality indices; used the corresponding type order in the candidate allocation.
  • Made the proposed upper-quota filling allocation explicit, excluding integral-quota types from additional seats.
  • Formalized limit computation through rational threshold queries on inputs promised to converge, without assuming rational limits or an exact output representation.
  • Used support size and total binary input length as complexity parameters; this apportionment problem has no candidate-count parameter m.

Result

All coordinates converge along admissible replication sizes. The proposed candidate allocation is not always their limit.

The binary-encoded limit-coordinate decision problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard, under polynomial-time many-one reductions, even when $H$ is an integer and the queried seat count is $k=1$. The existence promise is redundant.

An exact deterministic algorithm runs in
$$ 2^{O(L)}\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L) $$
time. The decision problem also belongs to $\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{NP}}$.

The proofs below use the specified sequential rule directly.

1. Convergence and an exact limit formula

Write
$$ r_t=\frac{p_t}{P}, \qquad \sum_t\mu_t r_t=1. $$
If $H=0$, then $x^{(N)}_{t,0}=\mu_t$ and all other coordinates vanish. Assume henceforth that $H>0$, and put
$$ M_t=\lceil Hr_t\rceil. $$

1.1. Seats as tasks

Represent the $k$-th seat of municipality $(t,j)$ by a task $(t,j,k)$. Its priority is $p_t/k$. At assignment step $h$, this task is available exactly when
$$ k-1<\frac{r_t h}{N}. $$
Thus its release threshold in normalized time is $(k-1)/r_t$.

Only tasks with $1\leq k\leq M_t$ can be available by the final step. Indeed,
$$ k-1<Hr_t \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k\leq\lceil Hr_t\rceil. $$

The rule is equivalent to choosing the highest-priority available unprocessed task. To verify the predecessor condition, an available task whose predecessor remains unprocessed has an available predecessor with strictly greater priority. It therefore cannot be chosen before that predecessor.

There is always an eligible municipality. Before step $h$, the current seat counts sum to $h-1$, whereas the eligibility bounds sum to
$$ \sum_t N\mu_t\frac{r_t h}{N}=h. $$
If every municipality were ineligible, summing its current count against its bound would contradict these totals.

A layer consists of the tasks with fixed $(t,k)$ and all municipality indices $j$. It has $N\mu_t$ tasks. Complete layers are ordered by decreasing $p_t/k$, with equal priorities ordered by type index.

1.2. Service of a priority prefix

A prefix of complete layers contains layers $1,\ldots,K_t$ for each type, where $0\leq K_t\leq M_t$. Define
$$ A_K(s)=\sum_t\mu_t\min\{K_t,\lceil r_t s\rceil\}, \qquad 0\leq s\leq H. $$
In particular, $A_K(0)=0$.

Let $D_K^{(N)}(h)$ be the number of prefix tasks processed through step $h$. The number of available prefix tasks at that step is $NA_K(h/N)$. Whenever an available prefix task remains, the selected task belongs to the prefix. Consequently,
$$ D_K^{(N)}(h) = \min\{D_K^{(N)}(h-1)+1,\;NA_K(h/N)\}. $$
Iterating this recurrence gives
$$ \frac{D_K^{(N)}(NH)}{N} = \min_{0\leq h\leq NH} \left\{H-\frac hN+A_K(h/N)\right\}. $$

Define
$$ D_K=\min_{0\leq s\leq H}\{H-s+A_K(s)\}. $$
This minimum is attained at an element of
$$ E_K=\{0,H\}\cup \left\{\frac{\ell}{r_u}: 0\leq\ell<K_u,\ \frac{\ell}{r_u}\leq H \right\}. $$
Indeed, the jumps of $A_K$ occur immediately after release thresholds. Between consecutive distinct elements of $E_K$, it is constant on the interval excluding the left endpoint and including the right endpoint. The objective decreases there with slope $-1$, so its minimum occurs at the right endpoint. Hence
$$ D_K=\min_{s\in E_K}\{H-s+A_K(s)\}. $$

For every admissible $N$,
$$ 0\leq \frac{D_K^{(N)}(NH)}N-D_K\leq\frac1N. $$
The lower bound follows because the discrete grid is a subset of $[0,H]$. For the upper bound, choose a minimizer $s_*$ and set $s_N=\lfloor Ns_*\rfloor/N$. Monotonicity of $A_K$ gives
$$ H-s_N+A_K(s_N) \leq H-s_*+A_K(s_*)+(s_*-s_N) \leq D_K+\frac1N. $$

1.3. Coordinates

For a layer $(t,k)$, let $D^-_{t,k}$ and $D^+_{t,k}$ denote the continuous prefix minima immediately before and immediately after that layer. Put
$$ d_{t,k}=D^+_{t,k}-D^-_{t,k} \quad (1\leq k\leq M_t), $$
and $d_{t,k}=0$ for $k>M_t$.

Processing a municipality’s $k$-th task is equivalent to giving it at least $k$ seats. Thus the normalized number of municipalities with at least $k$ seats converges to $d_{t,k}$, with error at most $1/N$. Therefore
$$ x_{t,0}=\mu_t-d_{t,1}, \qquad x_{t,k}=d_{t,k}-d_{t,k+1}\quad(k\geq1) $$
are the limits, and
$$ |x^{(N)}_{t,0}-x_{t,0}|\leq\frac1N, \qquad |x^{(N)}_{t,k}-x_{t,k}|\leq\frac2N\quad(k\geq1). $$

Admissible $N$ are unbounded: any common multiple of the denominators of the masses and $H$, and all its positive multiples, is admissible. This proves convergence along all admissible replication sizes. The event formula also proves that every limit is rational.

2. The candidate allocation can fail

Take
$$ \mu_1=\mu_2=\frac12,\qquad p_1=3,\quad p_2=1,\qquad H=\frac34. $$
Then $P=2$, and
$$ r_1=\frac32,\quad r_2=\frac12, \qquad q_1=\frac98,\quad q_2=\frac38. $$
Admissible $N$ are precisely the positive multiples of $4$.

The first $N/2$ seats go to distinct type-$1$ municipalities: their first-seat priority is $3$, exceeding every competing priority.

Type-$1$ second seats become eligible exactly when
$$ h>\frac{2N}{3}. $$
Until then, subsequent seats go to previously unseated type-$2$ municipalities. After that threshold, type-$1$ second seats have priority $3/2$, exceeding the type-$2$ first-seat priority $1$, and receive all remaining seats.

Thus the exact relevant counts are
$$ \begin{aligned} \text{type-$2$ first seats} &=\left\lfloor\frac{2N}{3}\right\rfloor-\frac N2,\\ \text{type-$1$ second seats} &=\frac{3N}{4}-\left\lfloor\frac{2N}{3}\right\rfloor. \end{aligned} $$
Both fit their municipality capacities. Type-$1$ third seats and type-$2$ second seats are unavailable throughout.

The limits are consequently
$$ x_{1,1}=\frac5{12},\quad x_{1,2}=\frac1{12}, \qquad x_{2,0}=\frac13,\quad x_{2,1}=\frac16. $$

The candidate first assigns one seat to every type-$1$ municipality, consuming seat mass $1/2$. Its residual mass $1/4$ goes entirely to type-$1$ second seats because $3/2>1$. It therefore gives
$$ x^{\mathrm{cand}}_{1,1}=\frac14,\quad x^{\mathrm{cand}}_{1,2}=\frac14,\qquad x^{\mathrm{cand}}_{2,0}=\frac12. $$
These differ from the actual limits.

3. A coordinate that measures a rounding minimum

The following observation will convert a satisfiability construction into a coordinate query.

Lemma. Suppose $H\geq2$ is an integer, $P=1$, and there is a distinguished type $*$ with
$$ r_*=\frac1{2H}. $$
Suppose every other type $i$ satisfies $Hr_i>1$. Define
$$ F(s)=\sum_{i\ne *}\mu_i\lceil r_i s\rceil-s, \qquad 0\leq s\leq H. $$
Then
$$ x_{*,1}=-\min_{0\leq s\leq H}F(s). $$

Proof. Type $*$ has final quota $1/2$, so only its first layer is available. For every other type,
$$ \frac{r_i}{\lceil Hr_i\rceil} > \frac1{2H}=r_*, $$
because $\lceil Hr_i\rceil<Hr_i+1<2Hr_i$. Thus every available layer of every other type precedes the distinguished first layer in priority order.

The prefix before that layer contains all available tasks of the other types. Its minimum is
$$ D^-=H+\min_sF(s). $$
The prefix after it contains all available tasks, and processes exactly $NH$ tasks, so its limit is $D^+=H$. Their difference is the distinguished first-layer service. Since type $*$ cannot receive a second seat, this service equals $x_{*,1}$. $\square$

Write
$$ e(y)=\lceil y\rceil-y. $$
If $\sum_i\mu_i r_i=1$ and $c=\mu_*r_*$, then
$$ F(s)=-cs+\sum_{i\ne *}\mu_i e(r_i s). $$
We will encode Boolean constraints in the nonnegative rounding terms.

4. A rounding-pair identity

Let $x+y$ have fractional part $\Delta$, where $0<\Delta<1$, and put $a=\{x\}$. Then
$$ e(x)+e(y)=1-\Delta+I, $$
where
$$ I= \begin{cases} 1,&0<a<\Delta,\\ 0,&a=0,\ a=\Delta,\text{ or }\Delta<a<1. \end{cases} $$

To verify this identity, if $0<a<\Delta$, both fractional parts are positive and sum to $\Delta$, giving $2-\Delta$. If $a>\Delta$, they sum to $1+\Delta$, giving $1-\Delta$. At either endpoint $a=0$ or $a=\Delta$, one argument is integral and the same lower value results.

Thus a pair of positive-population types can impose a unit penalty when one fractional part lies in a specified interval.

5. Polynomial-size encoding of Boolean constraints

We reduce from satisfiability of a $3$-CNF formula with $n\geq1$ variables and $c_{\mathrm{cl}}\geq1$ nonempty clauses, each involving at most three distinct variables. This version is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete: repeated literals can be removed, tautological clauses discarded, and a fresh forced variable can ensure the stated nonemptiness conditions.

Put
$$ g=n+c_{\mathrm{cl}}, \qquad \Omega=(n+2)!, \qquad M_i=1+i\Omega\quad(1\leq i\leq n). $$
These moduli are at least $7$ and pairwise coprime. Indeed, a common divisor of $M_i$ and $M_j$ is coprime to $\Omega$ and divides $(i-j)\Omega$, hence divides $|i-j|$. But $|i-j|$ divides $\Omega$, forcing that common divisor to be $1$.

Let
$$ Q=\prod_{i=1}^n M_i. $$
Boolean values will be represented by residues $0$ and $1$ modulo the corresponding $M_i$.

For each variable and clause, we specify three rational parameters $(M,\theta,\delta)$.

5.1. Variable constraints

For variable $i$, use
$$ M=M_i,\qquad \delta=1-\frac3{2M},\qquad \theta=1-\frac5{4M}. $$
For an integer residue $z$ modulo $M$, consider
$$ a_0=\left\{\frac zM+\theta\right\}. $$
Its values are
$$ a_0= \begin{cases} 1-\dfrac5{4M},&z=0,\\[3pt] 1-\dfrac1{4M},&z=1,\\[3pt] \dfrac{z-5/4}{M},&2\leq z<M. \end{cases} $$
Therefore
$$ a_0>\delta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z\in\{0,1\}. $$
All other residues lie strictly between $0$ and $\delta$.

Each displayed value has distance at least $1/(4M)$ from the boundaries $0$, $\delta$, and $1$.

5.2. Clause constraints

For a clause involving variable set $J$, let
$$ M=\prod_{i\in J}M_i. $$
There is a unique assignment to these variables falsifying the clause. Let $R\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$ be its Chinese-remainder encoding.

Use
$$ \delta=\frac1{2M}, \qquad \theta=\left\{\frac1{4M}-\frac RM\right\}. $$
Then
$$ \left\{\frac zM+\theta\right\}\in(0,\delta) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z=R. $$
For $z=R$, the fractional part is $1/(4M)$. For every other residue, it has the form
$$ \frac{d+1/4}{M}, \qquad 1\leq d<M, $$
and lies outside that interval.

Again, every value has distance at least $1/(4M)$ from the boundaries $0$, $\delta$, and $1$.

5.3. An additional pair

Index the preceding $g$ constraints by $j=1,\ldots,g$, writing their parameters as $(M_j,\theta_j,\delta_j)$.

Add a pair indexed by $j=0$ with
$$ M_0=1,\qquad \theta_0=\frac34,\qquad \delta_0=\frac12. $$
Its ideal fractional part is $3/4$, outside the penalized interval, with boundary margin $1/4$.

Let
$$ V=\max_{0\leq j\leq g}M_j. $$
For every pair,
$$ 0<\theta_j<1, \qquad \frac1{2V}\leq\delta_j\leq1-\frac1{2V}. $$
Every $M_j$ divides $Q$.

6. The constructed society and query

Define
$$ A=1000V, \qquad w=\frac1{10000V(A+g)}. $$
Give pair $0$ weight $w_0=Aw$ and every other pair weight $w_j=w$. Set
$$ W=\sum_{j=0}^g w_j=(A+g)w=\frac1{10000V}, \qquad Z=\sum_{j=0}^g w_j\delta_j. $$
Thus $0<Z<W$.

Choose the integer times
$$ T_0=100000V(A+g)Q, \qquad H=T_0+Q-1. $$
In particular, $T_0$ is divisible by every constraint modulus, and
$$ T_0\leq H<2T_0. $$

For pair $j$, create two types, each of mass $w_j$, with populations
$$ r_{j,1}=\frac1{M_j}+\frac{\theta_j}{T_0}, \qquad r_{j,2}=6-\frac1{M_j}+\frac{\delta_j-\theta_j}{T_0}. $$
Both populations are positive, and
$$ r_{j,1}+r_{j,2}=6+\frac{\delta_j}{T_0}. $$

Create the distinguished type with
$$ r_*=\frac1{2H}, \qquad \mu_*=\frac{4W+Z/T_0}{1-r_*}. $$
Finally, create an anchor type with
$$ r_a=1, \qquad \mu_a=1-2W-\mu_*. $$

The total number of types is
$$ \tau=2g+4. $$

6.1. Validity and normalization

Since $T_0,H\geq2$,
$$ 4W\leq\mu_*\leq6W, \qquad \mu_a\geq1-8W>\frac34. $$
Indeed, the numerator defining $\mu_*$ lies between $4W$ and $9W/2$, and its denominator is at least $3/4$.

All masses are positive and sum to $1$. Taking the displayed populations as the input $p_t$, their weighted population sum is
$$ \begin{aligned} P &=\mu_a+\sum_jw_j(r_{j,1}+r_{j,2})+\mu_*r_*\\ &=1-2W-\mu_*+6W+\frac Z{T_0}+\mu_*r_*\\ &=1. \end{aligned} $$
Thus these populations are also the normalized rates $r_t$.

Every nondistinguished type satisfies $Hr_t>1$. For the first member of a pair,
$$ Hr_{j,1}\geq\frac H{M_j}\geq\frac{T_0}{V}>1. $$
For the second member, using $M_j\geq1$, $\delta_j-\theta_j>-1$, and $T_0\geq2$ gives $r_{j,2}>4$. The anchor also satisfies the condition.

The lemma from Section 3 therefore applies.

6.2. The threshold

Put
$$ c_*=\mu_*r_*=\frac{\mu_*}{2H}, \qquad \alpha=c_*+\frac Z{T_0}, $$
and define
$$ C=W-\alpha H+\frac w2, \qquad b=-C. $$
The query asks whether
$$ x_{*,1}\geq b. $$
By Section 3, it is equivalent to
$$ \min_{0\leq s\leq H}F(s)\leq C. $$

The threshold is valid. The preceding bounds imply
$$ 2W\leq c_*H\leq3W, \qquad 2W\leq\alpha H\leq5W. $$
Consequently,
$$ \frac W2\leq b\leq4W<1. $$
We will also use
$$ \frac{2W}{H}\leq\alpha\leq\frac{4W}{T_0}. $$

7. Soundness: an accepting query yields a satisfying assignment

Suppose some $s\in[0,H]$ satisfies
$$ F(s)\leq C. $$
Since $C\leq-W/2<0$ and $F(0)=0$, we have $s>0$.

Set
$$ n_s=\lceil s\rceil, \qquad v=n_s-s. $$
The anchor rounding error is exactly $v$.

7.1. Every accepting time is close to an integer

All rounding errors are nonnegative, so
$$ F(s)\geq-c_*s. $$
Together with $-C\geq W/2$ and $c_*\leq3W/H$, this yields
$$ s\geq\frac H6\geq\frac{T_0}{6}. $$

Also,
$$ F(s)\geq-c_*H+\mu_a v. $$
Hence
$$ \mu_a v \leq C+c_*H =W-\frac{ZH}{T_0}+\frac w2 \leq\frac{3W}{2}. $$
Since $\mu_a>3/4$,
$$ v\leq2W. $$

7.2. Exact decomposition into penalties

For pair $j$, define
$$ \Delta_j=\delta_j\frac s{T_0}-6v. $$
This lies strictly between $0$ and $1$. For the lower bound,
$$ \Delta_j \geq\frac1{2V}\cdot\frac16-12W =\frac1{12V}-\frac{12}{10000V}>0. $$
For the upper bound, note that
$$ \frac Q{T_0} =\frac1{100000V(A+g)} \leq\frac1{8V}. $$
Thus
$$ \Delta_j \leq\delta_j\frac H{T_0} \leq1-\frac1{2V}+\frac Q{T_0} <1. $$

The sum of the pair’s two arguments is
$$ (r_{j,1}+r_{j,2})s =6n_s+\Delta_j. $$
The rounding-pair identity therefore gives
$$ e(r_{j,1}s)+e(r_{j,2}s) = 1-\delta_j\frac s{T_0}+6v+I_j(s), $$
where $I_j(s)$ is either $0$ or $1$.

Substituting into $F$ yields the exact identity
$$ F(s) = W-\alpha s+(\mu_a+6W)v +\sum_{j=0}^g w_j I_j(s). $$
In particular,
$$ F(s)\geq W-\alpha s. $$

7.3. Every accepting time is close to $T_0$

Comparing the last lower bound with $F(s)\leq C$ gives
$$ \alpha(H-s)\leq\frac w2. $$
Using $\alpha\geq2W/H$,
$$ H-s\leq\frac{wH}{4W}. $$
Since $H/T_0<2$,
$$ 1-\frac s{T_0} \leq\frac{w}{2W} =\frac1{2(A+g)} \leq\frac1{2000V}. $$
The upper deviation is bounded by $Q/T_0$. Therefore
$$ \left|\frac s{T_0}-1\right|<\frac1{100V}. $$

7.4. The penalties retain their intended meanings

Let
$$ a_j(s)=\{r_{j,1}s\}, \qquad \bar a_j=\left\{\frac{n_s}{M_j}+\theta_j\right\}. $$
Before taking fractional parts, their arguments differ by
$$ -\frac v{M_j}+\theta_j\left(\frac s{T_0}-1\right). $$
Its absolute value is at most
$$ 2W+\frac1{100V}<\frac1{80V}. $$
Also,
$$ |\Delta_j-\delta_j| \leq\frac1{100V}+12W <\frac1{80V}. $$

Every ideal value $\bar a_j$ has distance at least
$$ \frac1{4M_j}\geq\frac1{4V} $$
from the boundaries $0$, $\delta_j$, and $1$, by Section 5. The perturbations are smaller than this margin, including their combined effect on comparison with $\delta_j$. Thus they cannot change whether the fractional part lies inside the penalized interval.

It follows that:

  • A variable pair has $I_j(s)=0$ exactly when $n_s\bmod M_i$ is $0$ or $1$.
  • A clause pair has $I_j(s)=1$ exactly when $n_s$ has its falsifying residue modulo that clause’s modulus.
  • The additional pair has $I_0(s)=0$.

If the formula were unsatisfiable, either some variable residue would be non-Boolean, giving a variable penalty, or all variable residues would define a Boolean assignment and some clause would be false, giving a clause penalty. In either case,
$$ \sum_{j=0}^g w_j I_j(s)\geq w. $$
The exact decomposition would then imply
$$ F(s)\geq W-\alpha s+w \geq W-\alpha H+w >C, $$
a contradiction.

Therefore an accepting query implies satisfiability.

8. Completeness: a satisfying assignment yields an accepting query

Suppose the formula is satisfiable. By the Chinese remainder theorem, there is a unique $z\in\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}$ whose residue modulo every $M_i$ is the corresponding satisfying Boolean value.

Set
$$ s=T_0+z. $$
This is an integer in $[T_0,H]$. Because $T_0$ is divisible by $Q$, it has the intended variable and clause residues. Moreover,
$$ v=0, \qquad \left|\frac s{T_0}-1\right| \leq\frac Q{T_0}<\frac1{100V}. $$
The same perturbation estimates from Section 7.4 therefore apply. Every variable and clause penalty is zero, as is the additional pair’s penalty. Hence
$$ F(s)=W-\alpha s. $$

Finally,
$$ \alpha(H-s)\leq\alpha Q \leq\frac{4WQ}{T_0} =\frac{w}{25000V} <\frac w2. $$
Thus
$$ F(s)<W-\alpha H+\frac w2=C, $$
and the coordinate query accepts.

We have proved the equivalence
$$ \text{formula satisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_{*,1}\geq b. $$

8.1. Size and running time of the reduction

Each modulus $M_i=1+i(n+2)!$ has $O(n\log(n+2))$ bits. Each clause modulus is a product of at most three such moduli. The integer $Q$ has $O(n^2\log(n+2))$ bits.

All subsequent integers and rational numbers have polynomial bit length in the formula size. Clause residues are computed using the constructive Chinese remainder theorem, with the pairwise-coprime hypotheses already checked. Factorials, products, rational arithmetic, and extended Euclidean computations all take polynomial time in their bit lengths.

The reduction produces exactly $2(n+c_{\mathrm{cl}})+4$ types and has polynomial output length. It is therefore a polynomial-time many-one reduction to the original coordinate query, rather than merely to an auxiliary optimization problem.

This proves $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness even for integer $H$ and $k=1$.

9. Exact computation and upper bounds in $\tau,L$

The convergence formula supplies upper bounds independent of any separately encoded candidate count.

9.1. Succinctly constructing the needed prefixes

A coordinate query needs at most four prefix minima: two surrounding layer $(t,k)$ and two surrounding layer $(t,k+1)$. For $k=0$, only the two surrounding $(t,1)$ are needed. Unavailable layers contribute zero.

For an available layer $(t,k)$, let
$$ z_u=\frac{kp_u}{p_t}. $$
The prefix including the layer has counts
$$ K_u^+= \begin{cases} \min\{M_u,\lfloor z_u\rfloor\},&u\leq t,\\ \min\{M_u,\lceil z_u\rceil-1\},&u>t, \end{cases} $$
while the prefix excluding it has
$$ K_u^-= \begin{cases} \min\{M_u,\lfloor z_u\rfloor\},&u<t,\\ \min\{M_u,\lceil z_u\rceil-1\},&u\geq t. \end{cases} $$
These formulas count strictly higher priorities and then apply type-index tie-breaking. Their computation takes $\tau\operatorname{poly}(L)$ time.

9.2. Deterministic exponential time

For each needed prefix, enumerate $E_K$ and evaluate the objective exactly. Put
$$ J=\sum_tM_t. $$
This is a derived event bound, not an additional input parameter.

Since $P\geq\mu_tp_t$,
$$ r_t\leq\frac1{\mu_t}, \qquad M_t\leq1+\frac H{\mu_t}. $$
Binary encoding gives
$$ J\leq\tau\,2^{O(L)}. $$
Each event evaluation requires $\tau\operatorname{poly}(L)$ time, so exact coordinate comparison takes
$$ 2^{O(L)}\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L) $$
deterministic time.

9.3. Membership in $\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{NP}}$
