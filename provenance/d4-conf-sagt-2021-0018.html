The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), utilities \(u_{ts}\in[0,1]\), and \(\epsilon\in[3/4,1]\), compute a type-symmetric policy \(q\). Each type-\(t\) agent votes herself with probability \(q_{t,\bot}\); otherwise she selects type \(s\) with probability \(q_{ts}\), and a proxy is sampled from that type’s mass. Delegation continues until a self-voting guru is reached, with utility \(u_{ts}\) for a type-\(t\) agent whose guru has type \(s\), and zero if no guru is reached. Require every agent’s expected utility to be at least \(1-\epsilon\) times her best unilateral-deviation utility, and normalized welfare \(\sum_t\mu_t U_t(q)\) to be at least \(1-\epsilon\) times the supremum welfare over measurable delegation policies.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless probability space partitioned into a finite nonempty type set \(T\). It is given by its support: the \(\tau=|T|\) positive masses \(\mu_t\), encoded as binary rationals with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here \(m=\tau\) is the number of available proxy types, and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Utilities \(u_{ts}\in[0,1]\) and \(\epsilon\in[3/4,1]\) are binary rationals. A type-symmetric policy has nonnegative rational entries satisfying \(q_{t,\bot}+\sum_{s\in T}q_{ts}=1\): \(\bot\) means voting oneself, while action \(s\) means delegating to an independently sampled agent from the normalized mass of type \(s\), including when \(s=t\).
Each agent independently samples her action and proxy once; delegation follows these choices until a self-voting guru is reached. Repeated types do not terminate delegation; a cycle or infinite chain without a guru gives zero utility. A type-\(t\) agent represented by a type-\(s\) guru receives \(u_{ts}\), including \(u_{tt}\) when voting herself.
Let \(U_t(q)\) be expected utility and \(B_t(q)\) the supremum utility obtainable by changing only one type-\(t\) agent's mixed action, holding everybody else's policy fixed. Such a deviation has zero mass and does not change the population policy; abstention is also allowed and gives zero.
Let \(\operatorname{OPT}\) be the supremum normalized expected welfare over all measurable agent-dependent mixed policies using the same type-selection and proxy-sampling mechanism; these policies need not be type-symmetric and may abstain.
The required policy satisfies \(U_t(q)\geq(1-\epsilon)B_t(q)\) for every \(t\), and \(\sum_t\mu_tU_t(q)\geq(1-\epsilon)\operatorname{OPT}\).

Can these simultaneous incentive and welfare approximation guarantees be obtained in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is computing a policy with these guarantees NP-hard?

For finite-agent liquid democracy, a linear-time algorithm provides these same bicriteria guarantees for \(\epsilon\in[3/4,1]\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05142v2)
No result settling this precise atomless, type-symmetric approximation problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the society atomless, with each supported type represented by a measurable population of its stated mass.
  • Specified binary-rational utilities and epsilon, total input bit length L, and rational output probabilities to give the computational question a finite encoding.
  • There is no separate candidate set; defined m as the number of available proxy types, so m equals tau.
  • Interpreted q_ts as an unconditional delegation probability, with q_t,bot plus all delegation probabilities summing to one.
  • Specified normalized sampling within the selected type, permitting delegation to a distinct agent of one's own type.
  • Carried over independent, once-sampled agent choices; repeated types are not agent cycles, and infinite chains without a guru receive zero.
  • Defined unilateral deviation as an individual deviation of zero mass, rather than a change to the entire type's policy; included abstention as a zero-utility deviation.
  • Defined the measurable welfare benchmark using agent-dependent policies with the same type-selection and proxy-sampling actions, without imposing symmetry or incentive constraints, and allowed abstention.

Polynomial-time construction

Both guarantees are achievable in polynomial time. In fact, a single rational policy gives both guarantees with factor \(1/4\), and therefore works for every \(\epsilon\in[3/4,1]\).

The construction specializes the self-voting and greedy-delegation scheme in [Theorem 14 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.05142v2#S4.SS2). The argument below verifies it directly for the stated atomless model and its agent-dependent welfare benchmark.

1. Universal upper bounds

For each supported type \(t\), define its largest possible guru utility by

\(M_t:=\max_{s\in T}u_{ts}\).

This maximum exists because \(T\) is finite and nonempty.

Under any admissible policy or unilateral deviation, a type-\(t\) agent either reaches a guru of some type \(s\), receiving \(u_{ts}\leq M_t\), or receives zero through abstention, a cycle, or an infinite chain. Because utilities are nonnegative, her realized utility always lies in \([0,M_t]\).

Consequently, for every population policy \(q\),

\(B_t(q)\leq M_t\).

The same pointwise bound applies to every measurable agent-dependent policy allowed in the definition of \(\operatorname{OPT}\). Integrating over agents, whose type masses are \(\mu_t\), gives normalized expected welfare at most \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tM_t\). Taking the supremum over all such policies yields

\(\operatorname{OPT}\leq\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tM_t\).

Neither bound requires the supremum to be attained.

2. The algorithm

Fix an ordering of the supported types. For every \(t\), choose the first maximizer

\(s_t\in\operatorname{argmax}_{s\in T}u_{ts}\).

Output the type-symmetric policy specified by

\(q_{t,\bot}=\tfrac12,\qquad q_{t,s_t}=\tfrac12,\qquad q_{ts}=0\quad\text{for }s\neq s_t.\)

Thus every agent independently votes herself with probability \(1/2\); otherwise she delegates to an independently sampled agent of type \(s_t\).

All entries are nonnegative rationals and each row sums to one. When \(s_t=t\), the two actions remain distinct: voting oneself and sampling a proxy of one’s own type.

3. Utility lower bound in the atomless model

Fix a type-\(t\) agent \(i\). Let \(E_t\) be the event that:

  • \(i\) chooses delegation to type \(s_t\);
  • her sampled proxy chooses to vote herself.

Since \(\mu_{s_t}>0\), the normalized sampling distribution on type \(s_t\) is atomless. Hence the sampled proxy is distinct from \(i\) with probability one, including when \(s_t=t\).

Conditional on delegation and the sampled proxy’s identity, that proxy votes herself with probability \(1/2\), independently of \(i\)’s action. This uses exactly the stipulated independent sampling of agents’ actions; no action is resampled during delegation. Therefore

\(\Pr(E_t)=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac14.\)

On \(E_t\), the sampled proxy is a self-voting guru of type \(s_t\). Agent \(i\) therefore receives

\(u_{t,s_t}=M_t.\)

On the complementary event, her utility is nonnegative. It follows that

\(U_t(q)\geq\Pr(E_t)M_t=\tfrac14M_t.\)

This bound counts only chains terminating at the first proxy. It neither treats repeated types as termination nor assumes anything about longer chains.

4. Both required guarantees

The assumed range of \(\epsilon\) gives

\(0\leq1-\epsilon\leq\tfrac14.\)

For every type \(t\), the utility lower bound and deviation upper bound imply

\(U_t(q)\geq\tfrac14M_t\geq(1-\epsilon)M_t\geq(1-\epsilon)B_t(q).\)

For welfare, multiplying the utility lower bounds by the positive type masses and summing gives

\(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tU_t(q)\geq\tfrac14\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tM_t\geq\tfrac14\operatorname{OPT}\geq(1-\epsilon)\operatorname{OPT}.\)

Thus the output satisfies both guarantees simultaneously, including the endpoints of the specified interval.

5. Bit complexity

Computing all maximizers requires \(O(\tau m)\) exact rational comparisons. Comparing two binary rationals can be done by cross-multiplying their numerators and positive denominators; the resulting integer lengths and arithmetic costs are polynomial in \(L\).

Here \(m=\tau\), so the algorithm uses \(O(\tau^2)\) comparisons. Writing the full policy requires \(O(\tau(m+1))\) entries, each equal to zero or \(1/2\). Its output length is polynomial, and its total running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Hence the specified policy-construction problem is polynomial-time solvable.
