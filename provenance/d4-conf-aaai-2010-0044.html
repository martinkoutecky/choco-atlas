定理4 (Theorem 4) — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), finite action sets \(D_t\), rational unary rewards \(r_t(d)\), symmetric rational role-pair rewards \(\psi_{tu}(d,e)\), and rational \(\epsilon>0\), find a finite rational representation of coalition action masses \(z^j_{td}\) and residual singleton masses \(z^0_{td}\), with \(\sum_{j\ge 0,d} z^j_{td}=\mu_t\), whose welfare is within \(\epsilon\) of the supremum over feasible partitions and pure action assignments of a unit-mass population. The welfare is the integrated unary reward plus half the integrated pair reward for pairs in the same coalition, with singleton interactions zero.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. The population is atomless, and each type's mass may be split freely.
Each type has an explicitly listed nonempty finite action set \(D_t\), with \(m=\max_t|D_t|\). Unary rewards \(r_t(d)\) and pair rewards \(\psi_{tu}(d,e)=\psi_{ut}(e,d)\) are explicitly given finite rationals, possibly negative; each type has some action with nonnegative unary reward. Pair rewards include interactions between distinct individuals of the same type.
A feasible assignment consists of finitely or countably many measurable positive-mass coalitions and residual individuals forming singleton coalitions, with each individual choosing one pure action. Write \(z^j_{td}\geq0\) for type-action mass in coalition \(j\geq1\), and \(z^0_{td}\geq0\) for residual singleton mass; require \(\sum_{j\geq0}\sum_{d\in D_t}z^j_{td}=\mu_t\). Benchmark masses may be real. Zero-mass coalitions contribute no pair reward and may be replaced by singletons.
The welfare is \(W(z)=\sum_{t,d}r_t(d)\sum_{j\geq0}z^j_{td}+\frac12\sum_{j\geq1}\sum_{t,u}\sum_{d\in D_t,e\in D_u}\psi_{tu}(d,e)z^j_{td}z^j_{ue}\); singleton interactions are zero. Let \(W^\star\) be its supremum over feasible assignments.
Given also a binary rational \(\epsilon>0\), output finitely many rational coalition masses and rational residual singleton masses satisfying feasibility and \(W(z)\geq W^\star-\epsilon\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including \(\epsilon\).

Is this additive-accuracy search problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents, coalition structure generation with unary and binary DCOP rewards has an NP-complete welfare-threshold decision problem, even when all rewards are nonnegative. This discrete result does not classify the atomless search problem stated above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required nonempty action sets and at least one nonnegative unary-reward action per type, carrying over the discrete model's domain assumption; other unary rewards and pair rewards may be negative.
  • Interpreted the mass population as atomless, allowing measurable splitting of a type among coalitions and pure actions.
  • Allowed countably many positive-mass coalitions in the supremum and represented all remaining individuals as singletons; zero-mass coalitions have zero integrated pair reward.
  • Included same-type pair rewards between distinct individuals; the individual self-pair diagonal has product measure zero.
  • Defined m as the maximum action-set size and L as the total explicit binary input length, including epsilon, so polynomial time means polynomial in binary accuracy length.
  • Made the pair integral an unnormalized product-mass integral with the stated factor of one half.

Result

The search problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(\tau=1\), every unary reward is zero, and every pair reward belongs to \(\{0,1\}\). A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof gives a polynomial-time reduction from the clique decision problem to one call to the additive-accuracy search problem.

1. Constructing an atomless instance from a graph

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a simple undirected graph with \(V=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and let \(\omega(G)\) denote its maximum clique size.

Construct a society with one type of mass \(\mu_1=1\), action set \(D_1=V\), and unary rewards \(r_1(i)=0\). Define the symmetric pair rewards by
\(\psi_{11}(i,j)=1\) when \(\{i,j\}\in E\), and \(\psi_{11}(i,j)=0\) otherwise.

These data satisfy every input requirement. In particular, rewards between individuals of the same type are permitted, and all unary rewards are nonnegative. Here \(\tau=1\) and \(m=n\).

Write \(z_i^j=z_{1i}^j\). Because each edge is counted twice in the ordered pair sum, the welfare is
\(W(z)=\sum_{j\geq1}\sum_{\{i,\ell\}\in E}z_i^jz_\ell^j\).

2. Merging coalitions bounds every feasible assignment

For any feasible assignment, including one with countably many positive-mass coalitions, define the total action masses
\(x_i=\sum_{j\geq0}z_i^j\).

Feasibility gives \(x_i\geq0\) and \(\sum_i x_i=1\). For each edge, nonnegativity implies
\(\sum_{j\geq1}z_i^jz_\ell^j\leq\bigl(\sum_{j\geq0}z_i^j\bigr)\bigl(\sum_{j\geq0}z_\ell^j\bigr)=x_ix_\ell\).

For countably many coalitions, this inequality follows first for finite partial sums and then by monotone convergence. Consequently,
\(W(z)\leq F_G(x)\), where \(F_G(x)=\sum_{\{i,\ell\}\in E}x_ix_\ell\).

Conversely, any nonnegative vector \(x\) with \(\sum_i x_i=1\) is realizable by one coalition containing the entire population, with action masses \(x_i\). This uses the stipulated freedom to split the atomless type mass. Its welfare equals \(F_G(x)\). Thus
\(W^\star=\max\{F_G(x):x_i\geq0,\ \sum_i x_i=1\}\).

The maximum exists because the simplex is compact and \(F_G\) is continuous.

3. Computing the optimum in terms of the clique number

We prove the identity
\(\max\{F_G(x):x_i\geq0,\ \sum_i x_i=1\}=\frac12\bigl(1-\frac1{\omega(G)}\bigr)\).

This is the [Motzkin–Straus identity](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S0008414X00039493); the following argument proves the needed form directly.

Suppose two positive coordinates \(x_i,x_\ell\) correspond to nonadjacent vertices. Holding all other coordinates fixed, write
\(F_G(x)=C+a x_i+b x_\ell\),
where \(a=\sum_{v:\{i,v\}\in E}x_v\) and \(b=\sum_{v:\{\ell,v\}\in E}x_v\). Neither coefficient depends on \(x_i,x_\ell\), since the graph has no loops and these vertices are nonadjacent.

Move their combined mass to \(i\) if \(a\geq b\), and to \(\ell\) otherwise. Feasibility is preserved, welfare does not decrease, and the number of positive coordinates decreases. Repeating produces a vector \(y\) supported on a clique of size \(s\leq\omega(G)\), with \(F_G(y)\geq F_G(x)\).

On that clique,
\(F_G(y)=\frac12(1-\sum_v y_v^2)\).
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\sum_v y_v^2\geq1/s\), hence
\(F_G(x)\leq F_G(y)\leq\frac12(1-1/s)\leq\frac12(1-1/\omega(G))\).

Uniform mass on a maximum clique attains equality. Therefore
\(W^\star=f(\omega(G))\), where \(f(q)=\frac12(1-1/q)\).

In particular, this optimum is attained by one coalition with rational action masses.

4. An additive-accuracy output decides clique

The clique decision problem asks whether \(\omega(G)\geq k\) and is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. [Karp’s original paper](https://www2.seas.gwu.edu/~simhaweb/champalg/tsp/papers/KarpOriginal.pdf) establishes this result. We may restrict to \(n\geq2\) and \(2\leq k\leq n\), since the remaining cases are directly decidable.

Let \(h=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\), and supply the constructed society with the dyadic accuracy
\(\epsilon=2^{-(2h+2)}\leq1/(4n^2)\).

Suppose a polynomial-time algorithm returns a feasible rational assignment of welfare \(V\) satisfying
\(W^\star-\epsilon\leq V\leq W^\star\).

The gap between consecutive relevant optimum values is
\(f(k)-f(k-1)=1/(2k(k-1))\geq1/(2n(n-1))>\epsilon\).

It follows that:

  • If \(\omega(G)\leq k-1\), then \(V\leq W^\star\leq f(k-1)\).
  • If \(\omega(G)\geq k\), then \(V\geq W^\star-\epsilon\geq f(k)-\epsilon>f(k-1)\).

Thus the exact rational comparison \(V>f(k-1)\) decides whether \(G\) contains a clique of size at least \(k\).

The society’s explicitly listed reward table has polynomial size, and \(\epsilon\) has \(O(\log n)\) bits. A polynomial-time search algorithm necessarily produces an output of polynomial length. Its welfare can therefore be evaluated and compared with \(f(k-1)\) using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

This establishes the claimed \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness directly for the atomless search problem, including its real-valued benchmark and countably many allowed coalitions.
