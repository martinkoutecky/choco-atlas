The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many valuation types \(T\), type masses \(\mu_t\), good categories \(G\), per-capita supplies \(\sigma_g\), and additive values \(v_t(g)\), compute a feasible bundle-mass plan \(\pi_{t,B}\) assigning integral bundles \(B\) to population mass while satisfying \(\sum_B\pi_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}B_g\pi_{t,B}=\sigma_g\), maximizing or guaranteeing \(\alpha\) such that \(v_t(B)\ge\alpha R_t(B')\) whenever \(\pi_{t,B}>0\) and \(\pi_{u,B'}>0\), where \(R_t(B')\) is the expected value after uniformly deleting one good from \(B'\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) good categories \(G\), with nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_g\) and nonnegative binary-rational additive values \(v_t(g)\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An integral bundle is \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\), allowing repeated copies of a category and the empty bundle; write \(|B|=\sum_g B_g\) and \(v_t(B)=\sum_g B_gv_t(g)\).
A bundle-mass plan has finitely many positive binary-rational entries \(\pi_{t,B}\), with \(\sum_B\pi_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}B_g\pi_{t,B}=\sigma_g\) for every category. Bundle coordinates are encoded in binary.
Set \(R_t(0)=0\); for \(|B|>0\), set \(R_t(B)=\frac{|B|-1}{|B|}v_t(B)\), corresponding to uniformly deleting one individual copy, including copies of zero value.
For \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), a plan is \(\alpha\)-EFR if \(v_t(B)\geq\alpha R_t(B')\) whenever \(\pi_{t,B}>0\) and \(\pi_{u,B'}>0\), including comparisons within the same type.

Can a complete \(0.73\)-EFR bundle-mass plan always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or does this approximation guarantee fail through nonexistence or computational intractability?

For finitely many agents and individually listed indivisible goods with nonnegative additive valuations, a complete \(0.73\)-EFR allocation can be computed in polynomial time ([Farhadi et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/16675/16482)).
No result settling this precise bundle-mass approximation question was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one so that supplies are per capita.
  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational values and supplies, following the nonnegative additive valuation domain and making input bit complexity explicit.
  • Interpreted good categories as repeatable individual copies and integral bundles as arbitrary nonnegative integer vectors; no individual bundle capacity was supplied.
  • Allowed empty bundles and assigned their deletion value zero, consistent with the published definition's exclusion of empty envied bundles.
  • Specified uniform deletion over individual copies, including zero-valued copies, rather than over distinct categories.
  • Specified finite rational plan support and binary encoding of bundle counts.
  • Restricted the fairness factor to the interval from zero to one and selected the anchor's 0.73 guarantee rather than exact maximization.

Partial resolution

Nonexistence is ruled out: every valid instance admits a complete $0.73$-EFR plan with polynomially many entries and polynomial-length encodings.

I do not resolve polynomial-time computability for arbitrary supported valuation types, or establish computational hardness. The new algorithmic result proved below covers arbitrary masses and supplies whenever, for each type, its smallest positive category value is at least $0.73$ times its largest positive category value. In particular, valuations taking values in $\{0,\lambda_t\}$ admit a polynomial-time complete $1$-EFR plan.

1. Denominators and existence for every instance

Write the input masses and supplies as
$$ \mu_t=\frac{p_t}{b_t}, \qquad \sigma_g=\frac{q_g}{d_g}, $$
where all denominators are positive integers. Choose explicitly
$$ N=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t\prod_{g=1}^{m}d_g. $$
Then
$$ n_t=N\mu_t, \qquad Q_g=N\sigma_g $$
are integers, $n_t\geq1$, and
$$ \sum_t n_t=N. $$
Moreover, $\log_2N\leq L$. If “binary-rational” means dyadic rational, this choice of $N$ is a power of two.

Let
$$ M=\sum_g Q_g. $$
Construct a finite instance with $n_t$ agents of type $t$ and $Q_g$ individually listed copies of category $g$. There are $N$ agents and $M$ goods. Each copy has the corresponding nonnegative input value, and valuations are additive.

For $M>0$, the supplied finite-agent theorem gives a complete $0.73$-EFR allocation. Its deletion experiment chooses uniformly among individual copies. These are exactly the hypotheses and deletion convention of [Farhadi et al., Theorem 1 and Definition 3](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/16675/16482). For $M=0$, allocate empty bundles.

For a multiplicity vector $B$ of cardinality $k>0$, uniform deletion leaves expected value
$$ v_t(B)-\frac{1}{k}\sum_g B_gv_t(g) = \frac{k-1}{k}v_t(B) = R_t(B). $$
This includes repeated copies and zero-valued copies.

Let $c_{t,B}$ count the type-$t$ agents receiving bundle $B$. Define
$$ \pi_{t,B}=\frac{c_{t,B}}{N}, $$
omitting zero counts. Completeness gives
$$ \sum_B\pi_{t,B}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_{t,B}B_g\pi_{t,B}=\sigma_g. $$
Two distinct positive type-bundle entries have witnessing agents, so their required EFR comparison follows from the finite allocation. This includes distinct bundles assigned to the same type. An entry compared with itself satisfies
$$ v_t(B)\geq R_t(B)\geq0.73R_t(B). $$
Comparisons against the empty bundle are immediate.

Thus every instance has a complete $0.73$-EFR plan. The construction above does not give the requested running time: the numerical values of $N$ and $M$ can be exponential in $L$.

2. Polynomial-size witnesses for the general problem

The preceding existence proof can be sparsified while preserving the denominator $N$.

Set
$$ d=\tau+m, \qquad H=\max\{1,M\}, $$
and
$$ K=4d^2+2d\left\lceil\log_2(H+1)\right\rceil+1. $$

Proposition. Every instance has a complete $0.73$-EFR plan with fewer than $K$ positive entries, each of the form $c_{t,B}/N$, where
$$ 1\leq c_{t,B}\leq N, \qquad 0\leq B_g\leq M. $$

Proof. Start from the aggregated finite allocation. Index its positive entries by $i$, with type $t_i$, bundle $B_i$, and positive integer count $c_i$. Define
$$ a_i=(e_{t_i},B_i)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{d}. $$
The required equations become
$$ \sum_i c_i a_i = (n_1,\ldots,n_\tau,Q_1,\ldots,Q_m). $$
Every coordinate of $a_i$ is at most $H$.

Suppose the current support has $k\geq K$ entries. Its $2^k$ subsets have sums taking at most $(kH+1)^d$ different values. We verify that
$$ 2^k>(kH+1)^d. $$
For $k\geq4d^2$,
$$ \log_2k\leq\frac{k}{2d}. $$
Indeed, $(\log_2x)/x$ decreases for $x\geq4$, and
$$ \log_2(4d^2)=2+2\log_2d\leq2d. $$
Here the final inequality follows from $d\leq2^{d-1}$ for positive integer $d$. Consequently,
$$ d\log_2(kH+1) \leq d\log_2k+d\log_2(H+1) \leq\frac{k}{2}+d\log_2(H+1) <k. $$
The strict inequality follows from $k\geq K$.

Two distinct subsets therefore have equal sums. Subtracting their indicator vectors gives a nonzero vector
$$ y\in\{-1,0,1\}^{k}, \qquad \sum_i y_i a_i=0. $$
Each column’s type coordinates sum to one, so $\sum_i y_i=0$. Hence $y$ has both positive and negative entries.

Put
$$ \lambda=\min_{i:y_i=1}c_i $$
and replace each count by $c_i-\lambda y_i$. The counts remain nonnegative integers, all equations are preserved, and at least one positive entry disappears.

Fairness persists because the new positive support is a subset of the old support. Repeat until fewer than $K$ entries remain. The type equations still imply $\sum_i c_i=N$, so every remaining count is at most $N$. The bundles are unchanged and retain their coordinate bound $M$. ∎

Since
$$ \log_2(M+1)=O(L+\log m), $$
the proposition gives polynomial-length certificates. Such a certificate can be verified in polynomial time by checking the exact mass and supply equations and every ordered support-pair EFR inequality using rational arithmetic.

Thus the bounded search relation is total and polynomially verifiable: it belongs to $\mathrm{TFNP}$. Formally, malformed inputs can be assigned a distinguished “invalid input” witness, since validity is polynomial-time checkable.

This excludes an output-size obstruction. It supplies neither a polynomial-time construction nor a hardness result.

3. A new polynomial-time regime

For a nonzero valuation type, define
$$ a_t=\min_{g:v_t(g)>0}v_t(g), \qquad b_t=\max_g v_t(g). $$

Theorem. If
$$ a_t\geq\frac{73}{100}b_t $$
for every nonzero type $t$, a complete $0.73$-EFR plan can be computed in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

More generally, the algorithm guarantees $\rho$-EFR, where
$$ \rho=\min_{t:v_t\not\equiv0}\frac{a_t}{b_t}. $$
When all valuations are zero, take $\rho=1$. The output has at most
$$ \tau(2m+1)+1 $$
positive entries, all with denominator $N$.

The main step is an exact result for the binary masks
$$ w_t(g)=\mathbf1_{\{v_t(g)>0\}}. $$
We construct a complete plan satisfying, for every positive support pair and every individual copy $c$ in the compared bundle $C$,
$$ w_t(B)\geq w_t(C-c). $$
Thus the construction removes envy after deleting any individual copy, including one valued at zero.

3.1 Computing balanced fractional loads

For now, ignore categories that nobody values. Also omit zero-supply categories. For each remaining category, let
$$ A_g=\{t:w_t(g)=1\}, $$
which is nonempty.

We use rational linear programming to compute nonnegative loads $\ell_t$ and a fractional assignment $x$ satisfying
$$ \sum_t x_{t,g}=Q_g, \qquad \sum_g x_{t,g}=n_t\ell_t, $$
together with
$$ x_{t,g}>0,\ s\in A_g \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ell_t\leq\ell_s. $$
In words, a category is assigned only to types having the smallest load among the types that value it.

Here is an explicit polynomial-time construction and proof.

Maintain an active type set $S$ and an active category set $H$. Every active category has at least one active type that values it. At each stage solve the linear program
$$ \min z $$
subject to
$$ \sum_{t\in A_g\cap S}x_{t,g}=Q_g \quad(g\in H), $$
$$ \sum_{g\in H:t\in A_g}x_{t,g}\leq n_tz \quad(t\in S), $$
$$ x_{t,g}\geq0, \qquad z\geq0. $$
It is feasible: assign each category to any active type that values it, and choose a sufficiently large $z$. Its optimum $z^*$ is attained.

For each $t\in S$, minimize its load over the feasible solutions with $z=z^*$. Let $T$ be the types whose minimum load equals $n_tz^*$. Therefore every type in $T$ is saturated in every optimal solution.

First, $T$ is nonempty. Otherwise, for each active type there would be an optimal solution in which its load is strictly below its cap. Averaging these solutions would make every active type strictly below its cap. Their maximum normalized load would then be strictly below $z^*$, contradicting optimality.

Choose an optimal solution $x^0$ in which every type outside $T$ is strictly below its cap. Such a solution exists by averaging one suitable optimal solution for each type outside $T$.

Suppose $x^0_{t,g}>0$ for $t\in T$, and category $g$ is also valued by some $s\in S\setminus T$. A sufficiently small transfer of category $g$ from $t$ to $s$ preserves every supply equation and cap, but makes $t$ unsaturated. This contradicts the definition of $T$.

Consequently, $T$ receives goods only from
$$ H_T=\{g\in H:A_g\cap S\subseteq T\}. $$
Every category in $H_T$ must be assigned within $T$. Since every member of $T$ is saturated,
$$ \sum_{g\in H_T}Q_g=z^*\sum_{t\in T}n_t. $$

Assign $\ell_t=z^*$ to all $t\in T$, record the restriction of $x^0$ to $T$ and $H_T$, and remove those types and categories. Every remaining category still has an active type that values it. The residual assignment gives every remaining type a normalized load strictly below $z^*$, so the next optimum is strictly smaller.

At least one type disappears at each stage. Thus there are at most $\tau$ stages, using at most $O(\tau^2)$ linear programs.

The recorded assignments cover every category and give each type total load $n_t\ell_t$. If $x_{t,g}>0$, category $g$ was removed at the same stage as $t$. Any other original type valuing $g$ was removed either at that stage or earlier. Since stage loads strictly decrease, its load is at least $\ell_t$. This proves the desired load property.

All linear programs have polynomially many variables and constraints. Their coefficients have polynomial bit length. Moreover, each stage load has the explicit form
$$ z^*= \frac{\sum_{g\in H_T}Q_g}{\sum_{t\in T}n_t}, $$
so its encoding has polynomial length. The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore applies with all hypotheses satisfied.

3.2 Converting the assignment into integer category counts

Retain the category-type edges
$$ E=\{(g,t):t\in A_g,\ \ell_t=\min_{s\in A_g}\ell_s\}. $$
The fractional assignment just constructed uses only these edges.

Set
$$ \underline{k}_t=\lfloor\ell_t\rfloor, \qquad \overline{k}_t=\lceil\ell_t\rceil. $$
Consider a network with:

  • an exact flow $Q_g$ from the source to category $g$;
  • category-to-type edges in $E$, each of capacity $M$;
  • a type-to-sink edge with lower bound $n_t\underline{k}_t$ and upper bound $n_t\overline{k}_t$.

The fractional assignment is feasible in this network because
$$ n_t\underline{k}_t \leq n_t\ell_t \leq n_t\overline{k}_t. $$
All capacities and lower bounds are integers. The integral-flow theorem, applied through the usual lower-bound reduction to maximum flow, gives an integral feasible flow. A polynomial-time maximum-flow algorithm finds it without expanding capacities into individual copies.

Let $X_{t,g}$ denote its category-to-type flows. Then
$$ \sum_tX_{t,g}=Q_g, $$
and
$$ n_t\underline{k}_t \leq \sum_gX_{t,g} \leq n_t\overline{k}_t. $$
Additionally,
$$ X_{t,g}>0,\ s\in A_g \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ell_t\leq\ell_s. $$

3.3 Distributing copies within a type without listing its agents

Fix a type $t$. Regard its $n_t$ hypothetical agents as cyclically indexed by
$$ 0,1,\ldots,n_t-1. $$
Process categories in a fixed order, assigning their $X_{t,g}$ copies cyclically and retaining the pointer between categories.

This produces balanced cardinalities: every type-$t$ agent receives either
$$ \left\lfloor\frac{\sum_gX_{t,g}}{n_t}\right\rfloor \quad\text{or}\quad \left\lceil\frac{\sum_gX_{t,g}}{n_t}\right\rceil $$
goods. Therefore every resulting bundle $B$ satisfies
$$ \underline{k}_t\leq |B|\leq\overline{k}_t. $$
Every assigned category is valued by its recipient type, so
$$ w_t(B)=|B|. $$

The cyclic assignment has a polynomial-size implementation. Write
$$ X_{t,g}=n_th_{t,g}+r_{t,g}, \qquad 0\leq r_{t,g}<n_t. $$
Every agent receives $h_{t,g}$ copies of category $g$, and agents in one cyclic interval receive an additional copy. That interval has at most two endpoints. The endpoints across all categories partition the indices into at most $2m+1$ intervals on each of which the entire bundle is constant.

Thus each type yields at most $2m+1$ bundle atoms. Their counts and coordinates are computed with binary integer arithmetic.

3.4 Proof of the deletion inequalities

Consider a type-$t$ own bundle $B$ and a bundle $C$ assigned to type $u$, before adding globally unvalued categories.

If $w_t(C)=0$, every deletion comparison is immediate.

Otherwise, some category $g$ in $C$ is valued by $t$. Since $X_{u,g}>0$, the retained-edge property gives
$$ \ell_u\leq\ell_t. $$
Hence
$$ |C| \leq\lceil\ell_u\rceil \leq\lfloor\ell_t\rfloor+1 \leq |B|+1. $$
For any individual copy $c$ in $C$, the remaining bundle contains $|C|-1$ copies, each having mask value at most one. Therefore
$$ w_t(C-c)\leq |C|-1\leq |B|=w_t(B). $$
This proof includes deletion of a copy that $t$ values at zero.

3.5 Adding globally unvalued categories

Choose an agent whose current bundle has globally minimum cardinality $k_{\min}$. Give that agent every copy of every category valued at zero by all types.

This is implemented by selecting a minimum-cardinality atom and, if necessary, splitting one agent from it. It increases the number of atoms by at most one.

Let $C^*$ be the padded bundle and $C^0$ its previous bundle. For every observer type $t$ and every own bundle $B$,
$$ w_t(C^*)=w_t(C^0) \leq |C^0| =k_{\min} \leq w_t(B). $$
The last inequality holds because own mask utilities equal the previous bundle cardinalities, and padding changes no utility.

Thus every observer is envy-free toward $C^*$ even before deletion. All other deletion comparisons persist because utilities are unchanged.

Aggregating atom counts and dividing by $N$ gives the exact prescribed masses and supplies, with at most $\tau(2m+1)+1$ entries. This completes the binary-mask construction.

3.6 Transferring the guarantee to the original valuations

For any nonzero type $t$ and any bundle $D$,
$$ a_tw_t(D)\leq v_t(D)\leq b_tw_t(D). $$
Therefore, for every compared nonempty bundle $C$ and every individual copy $c$ in it,
$$ v_t(B) \geq a_tw_t(B) \geq a_tw_t(C-c) \geq \frac{a_t}{b_t}v_t(C-c). $$
Averaging over uniform deletion gives
$$ v_t(B)\geq\frac{a_t}{b_t}R_t(C)\geq\rho R_t(C). $$
Zero valuation types and empty compared bundles satisfy the inequality directly.

This proves the theorem. In particular:

  • If $v_t(g)\in\{0,\lambda_t\}$ for every $t,g$, the guarantee is $1$-EFR.
  • If $a_t\geq0.73b_t$ for every nonzero type, the guarantee is $0.73$-EFR.

The algorithm uses polynomially many rational linear programs, one integral network-flow computation, and polynomially many binary arithmetic operations. It never lists the $N$ agents or the $M$ copies.

4. Exact EFR for proportional types, with both encoding gaps repaired

The proportional-types result from the notes also admits a fully specified compressed implementation.

Proposition. Suppose
$$ v_t(g)=\lambda_tw(g) $$
for some nonnegative reference valuation $w$ and nonnegative scalars $\lambda_t$. A complete $1$-EFR plan can be computed in polynomial time, with at most $m+\tau$ entries and denominator $N$.

4.1 Obtaining the reference valuation

If some input row is nonzero, choose that row as $w$. Choose a category $g_0$ with $w(g_0)>0$ and compute
$$ \lambda_t=\frac{v_t(g_0)}{w(g_0)}. $$
Under the proportionality hypothesis, these scalars satisfy every coordinate equality. They and $w$ have polynomial-length rational encodings. The hypothesis can also be checked by exact rational comparisons.

If all input rows are zero, use $w=0$. The zero-valued batching procedure below applies directly, and all fairness comparisons are automatic.

4.2 A finite greedy invariant

For $N$ hypothetical agents with common valuation $w$, process categories in nonincreasing order of $w(g)$. Give each copy to a minimum-utility agent.

This procedure maintains
$$ w(B)\geq w(C-c) $$
for every pair of agents and every individual copy $c$ in the compared bundle $C$.

To prove the invariant, suppose a copy of value $c_0$ is given to a minimum-load agent whose previous load is $U$. Every other agent has load at least $U$. Every previously assigned copy has value at least $c_0$. Deleting any copy from the updated bundle therefore leaves value at most
$$ U+c_0-c_0=U. $$
All comparisons toward that updated bundle hold. Comparisons toward unchanged bundles persist because the recipient’s utility increases and other utilities remain unchanged. The initial empty allocation satisfies the invariant.

This argument also covers $c_0=0$.

4.3 Batching a positive-valued category

Represent the population by atoms $(c_i,B_i)$, where $c_i$ agents have bundle $B_i$. Initially there is one atom $(N,0)$.

For a category of value $c>0$ with $Q$ copies, write
$$ \frac{w(B_i)}{c}=a_i+r_i, \qquad a_i=\left\lfloor\frac{w(B_i)}{c}\right\rfloor, \qquad 0\leq r_i<1. $$
Each agent in atom $i$ has successive assignment events at normalized loads
$$ a_i+r_i,\ a_i+r_i+1,\ a_i+r_i+2,\ldots. $$
The greedy procedure processes these events in nondecreasing order.

For an integer $q\geq0$, the number of events strictly below $q$ is
$$ C(q)=\sum_i c_i\max\{0,q-a_i\}. $$
For $Q>0$, find the smallest integer $q\geq0$ satisfying
$$ C(q+1)\geq Q. $$
Binary search suffices, using the upper bound $\max_i a_i+Q$. Minimality gives
$$ C(q)\leq Q\leq C(q+1). $$

Give every agent in atom $i$ the first
$$ h_i=\max\{0,q-a_i\} $$
copies. The remaining $Q-C(q)$ events occur in $[q,q+1)$: each active atom, with $a_i\leq q$, contributes one event per agent at $q+r_i$.

Sort active atoms by $r_i$ and process these events, breaking ties arbitrarily. At most one atom is processed partially and splits. This realizes a permissible greedy execution and increases the number of atoms by at most one.

4.4 Batching zero-valued categories

Choose a minimum-load atom of count $c_i$. For a zero-valued category, write
$$ Q=c_ih+r, \qquad 0\leq r<c_i. $$
Give $h$ copies to every agent in that atom and one extra copy to $r$ of them. Every assignment is permitted by the greedy rule because utilities remain unchanged. Again, at most one atom splits.

Consequently there are at most $m+1$ atoms throughout. Counts are at most $N$, and coordinates are at most $M$. Loads, quotients, and search endpoints have polynomial bit length, so every category is processed in polynomial time.

4.5 Assigning the final atoms to supported types

Let the final atoms have counts $c_i$. Partition the integer interval $[0,N)$ into consecutive atom intervals of lengths $c_i$. Independently partition it into consecutive type intervals of lengths $n_t$.

For each nonempty intersection, assign its atom’s bundle to its type, with mass equal to the intersection length divided by $N$. This preserves all atom counts and all type counts, hence every mass and supply equation.

If there are $r\leq m+1$ atoms, the two interval partitions have at most $r+\tau-1$ nonempty intersections. Thus the output has at most $m+\tau$ entries, all with denominator $N$.

The common-reference invariant gives
$$ w(B)\geq w(C-c). $$
Multiplication by $\lambda_t\geq0$ gives
$$ v_t(B)\geq v_t(C-c). $$
Averaging over deletion proves $1$-EFR. This completes the proposition.

5. What remains unresolved

The results establish universal existence and polynomial-size verifiable witnesses. They also give polynomial-time constructions for two substantive regimes: proportional valuations, and valuations whose positive values have sufficiently small spread within each type.

They do not settle the general computational question. In particular, neither exponential finite replication nor the sparsification argument establishes computational intractability. A general polynomial-time construction or a justified search-complexity hardness result is still needed.
