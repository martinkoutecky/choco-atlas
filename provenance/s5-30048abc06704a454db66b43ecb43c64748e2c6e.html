The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses μ_t summing to \(1\), complete rankings π_t, a party partition, a non-increasing scoring rule \(f\), contact rates β_{st}, and a seed-mass budget \(b\), choose \(x_t\) with \(0 ≤ x_t ≤ μ_t\) and ∑_t x_t ≤ b. For a population of size \(N\), type-\(s\) agents have independent edge probability \(β_{st}/N\) to each type-\(t\) agent. Define \(a_t^0=h_t^0=x_t\), \(h_t^{r+1}=(μ_t-a_t^r)(1-\exp[-∑_s β_{st}h_s^r])\), and \(a_t^{r+1}=a_t^r+h_t^{r+1}\), with limit \(a_t^∞\). Apply the paper's constructive ranking update to each activated type, and score candidate \(c\) by \(q_c(x)=∑_t[(μ_t-a_t^∞)f(π_t(c))+a_t^∞ f(π̃_t^+(c))]\). Rank candidates by these scores with the paper's tie-breaking rule. If \(H_i(x)\) counts party \(C_i\)'s candidates among the \(k\) winners, maximize either \(H_1(x)-H_1(0)\) (CDW) or \(H_1(x)-\max_{i\ne1}H_i(x)-(H_1(0)-\max_{i\ne1}H_i(0))\) (CMEC).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible rankings. Each type has a strict complete ranking \(\pi_t\).
There are \(p\geq2\) parties \(C_i=\{c_1^i,\ldots,c_k^i\}\), each with \(k\geq2\) candidates; \(m=pk\), and \(C_1\) is the target. Input includes a nonconstant scoring vector \(f(1)\geq\cdots\geq f(m)\geq0\), finite contact rates \(\beta_{st}\geq0\), and budget \(0<b\leq1\), all binary rational; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Choose real seed masses \(0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t x_t\leq b\). Define diffusion deterministically by \(a_t^0=h_t^0=x_t\), \(h_t^{r+1}=(\mu_t-a_t^r)(1-\exp(-\sum_s\beta_{st}h_s^r))\), \(a_t^{r+1}=a_t^r+h_t^{r+1}\), and \(a_t^\infty=\lim_{r\to\infty}a_t^r\).
Construct \(\widetilde\pi_t^+\) by simultaneously replacing every original segment consisting of one opponent followed by a nonempty maximal block of target candidates with that block followed by the opponent; preserve internal orders and leave an initial target block unchanged. Each activated mass undergoes this update once.
Set \(q_c(x)=\sum_t[(\mu_t-a_t^\infty)f(\pi_t(c))+a_t^\infty f(\widetilde\pi_t^+(c))]\). Elect the \(k\) highest-scoring candidates, resolving exact ties by smaller party index and then smaller candidate index. Let \(H_i(x)\) count elected candidates from \(C_i\).
The two objectives are \(D(x)=H_1(x)-H_1(0)\) (CDW) and \(M(x)=H_1(x)-\max_{i\ne1}H_i(x)-[H_1(0)-\max_{i\ne1}H_i(0)]\) (CMEC).

For each objective separately, does maximization admit a polynomial-time multiplicative approximation with some fixed factor \(\alpha\in(0,1]\), or is approximation NP-hard for every such \(\alpha\)? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,k,p\).

For finite voters with independent-cascade diffusion, CDW and CMEC are reported NP-hard to approximate within every fixed positive factor, even with deterministic edges and three parties of two candidates each. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.04037).
No result settling approximation for the exact mass recurrence and election objectives defined above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The unspecified party partition is restricted to equally sized parties, each with exactly k candidates, following the discrete model; at least two parties and two winners are retained.
  • The scoring rule is a nonconstant, nonnegative binary-rational vector supplied as input; the discrete model excludes constant scoring rules.
  • Contact rates are finite nonnegative binary rationals, with no upper bound of one: they are rates rather than finite-network edge probabilities.
  • The normalized budget is binary rational with 0<b<=1, and seed masses may be real and divisible.
  • The constructive update is expressed as simultaneous original-segment block swaps, preserving the discrete update for consecutive target candidates.
  • Exact score ties use the discrete priority order: party index first, then candidate index.
  • The supplied recurrence defines the continuous diffusion directly; finite population size N is not an input, and no unverified stochastic-limit equivalence is assumed.

1. Result

Both objectives are \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard to approximate within every fixed factor \(\alpha\in(0,1]\), unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

This holds even with:

  • two parties, \(p=2\);
  • zero contact rates, \(\beta_{st}=0\);
  • Borda scoring, \(f(r)=m-r\);
  • dyadic rational masses and budget.

The construction uses variable \(k\). It does not establish hardness for fixed \(k=2\).

The [cited finite-voter result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.04037) concerns discrete seed choices and independent-cascade diffusion. We prove hardness directly for the fractional mass model in the question.

2. Reduction parameters

We reduce from CLIQUE. Let \(G=(V,F)\) be a simple graph with \(n\) vertices and \(E\) edges, and let \(K\) be the requested clique size.

We may restrict to instances satisfying \(3\leq K<n\), having no isolated vertices, and satisfying \(E\geq Q:=\binom{K}{2}\). The excluded cases are polynomial-time decidable: remove isolated vertices, then check the cases \(K\leq2\), \(K\geq n\), and \(E<Q\). Thus this restriction remains \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. In particular, \(n\leq2E\).

Set

\[ \text{[intentionally omitted]} \]

\(k:=1000(E+1)^3,\quad p:=2,\quad m:=2k.\)

We use Borda scoring \(f(r)=2k-r\).

Choose a dyadic rational \(\delta>0\) such that, writing \(\eta:=n\delta\) and \(\varepsilon:=1-\eta\),

\( \frac{1}{24E}\leq\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{12E}. \)

Such a \(\delta\) is computable with \(O(\log(nE))\) bits. Explicitly, take \(s:=\lceil\log_2(96En)\rceil\) and

\( \delta:=2^{-s}\left\lceil \frac{(1-1/(12E))2^s}{n}\right\rceil. \)

Then \(\varepsilon\leq1/(12E)\), while

\( \varepsilon>\frac{1}{12E}-n2^{-s}\geq\frac{7}{96E}>\frac{1}{24E}. \)

Choose a dyadic \(\gamma\) with \(0<\gamma<1/(K+1)\), for example

\( \gamma:=2^{-\lceil\log_2(2(K+1))\rceil}. \)

Finally, define the budget and the initial score gap by

\( b:=K\delta,\qquad g:=(K+2-\gamma)\delta. \)

These satisfy \(0<b<1\), \(g>0\), and

\( g< (K+2)\delta\leq(n+1)\delta<2\eta, \)

using \(K<n\) and \(n\geq3\).

All contact rates will be zero. Consequently, for every feasible seed vector, \(h_t^1=0\) and \(a_t^\infty=x_t\).

3. Candidates and active rankings

Partition the target party into:

  • \(h:=k-2E+Q-1\) candidates called fixed target winners;
  • one distinguished target candidate \(A\);
  • \(\ell:=2E-Q\) remaining target candidates.

Indeed, \(h+1+\ell=k\). Among the \(h\) fixed target winners, designate one helper \(w_j\) for each edge \(e_j\), and four spacer candidates. The remaining \(h-E-4\) fixed target winners form an initial block.

The opponent party contains:

  • an edge candidate \(e_j\) for each edge;
  • a separator \(z_j\) for each edge;
  • two candidates \(u,v\);
  • \(k-2E-2\) other opponents.

For each graph vertex \(a\), create an active type of mass \(\delta\). Its ranking is the following concatenation:

  • the initial block of \(h-E-4\) fixed target winners;
  • one triplet for each edge \(e_j\), in a fixed edge order:
  • \(e_j,w_j,z_j\) if \(a\) is incident to that edge;
  • \(e_j,z_j,w_j\) otherwise;
  • \(u\);
  • the four spacer target candidates;
  • \(v\);
  • \(A\);
  • the \(\ell\) remaining target candidates;
  • all other opponents.

Every ranking is strict and complete.

The stipulated update has the following exact effects:

  • \(A\) moves upward by one position, gaining one Borda point;
  • \(e_j\) loses one Borda point precisely when \(a\) is incident to edge \(j\);
  • the initial target block is unchanged.

The helper following an incident edge forms a singleton target block, because its successor is \(z_j\). A nonincident edge is followed by \(z_j\), so it is unchanged.

The original rank of \(A\) is the same in every active type:

\( r_A^{\mathrm{act}}=h+2E+3=k+Q+2. \)

The original rank of edge candidate \(e_j\) is also independent of the vertex:

\( r_j^{\mathrm{act}}=h-E-4+3j-2. \)

Thus its original Borda advantage over \(A\) within an active type is

\( d_j:=r_A^{\mathrm{act}}-r_j^{\mathrm{act}}=3(E-j)+9. \)

In particular, \(9\leq d_j\leq3E+6\).

If types must have distinct rankings, use distinct cyclic orders of \(n\) candidates within the initial target block. This is possible because that block has more than \(n\) candidates, and it changes none of the calculations above.

4. Neutral rankings and exact calibration

We now construct types of total mass \(\varepsilon\) whose rankings are unchanged by activation.

Every neutral ranking places all target candidates first, in this order:

  • the \(h\) fixed target winners;
  • \(A\);
  • the \(\ell\) remaining targets.

Thus \(A\)'s neutral rank is

\( r_A^{\mathrm{neu}}=h+1=k-2E+Q. \)

All targets form an initial block, so the entire ranking is unchanged by the update.

Place \(z_j\) at rank \(k+j\), and place \(u,v\) at ranks \(2k-1,2k\), respectively.

For each edge candidate, prescribe the mean neutral rank

\( r_j:=r_A^{\mathrm{neu}}+\frac{\eta d_j-g}{\varepsilon}. \)

We next verify that these means can be realized by a polynomial-size support of complete rankings.

Since \(g<2\eta\) and \(12E-1\leq\eta/\varepsilon\leq24E-1\),

\( r_j>k-2E+1+7(12E-1)=k+82E-6>k+E+2. \)

Also,

\( r_j<k+(24E-1)(3E+6)\leq k+72E^2+144E<2k-3. \)

Consecutive prescribed means differ by \(3\eta/\varepsilon\geq33\). Therefore the sets

\( \{\lfloor r_j\rfloor,\lceil r_j\rceil\} \)

are pairwise disjoint, avoid the separator positions, and avoid the positions reserved for \(u,v\).

Write \(r_j=t_j+\theta_j\), where \(t_j:=\lfloor r_j\rfloor\) and \(0\leq\theta_j<1\). Partition \([0,1]\) at the distinct numbers \(\theta_j\). On each resulting interval, place edge candidate \(e_j\) at:

  • rank \(t_j+1\) when the interval lies below \(\theta_j\);
  • rank \(t_j\) otherwise.

Fill all unoccupied opponent positions with the remaining opponents in a fixed order. Give this ranking mass \(\varepsilon\) times the interval length. There are at most \(E+1\) positive-mass neutral types, and the mean rank of \(e_j\) is exactly \(r_j\).

These masses are dyadic. Indeed,

\( \varepsilon\theta_j=\varepsilon r_A^{\mathrm{neu}}+\eta d_j-g-\varepsilon t_j \)

is dyadic. Each neutral mass is a difference of such quantities, possibly using endpoints \(0\) or \(\varepsilon\). Its bit length is \(O(\log(nE))\).

At zero seeds, the score difference between an edge candidate and \(A\) is consequently

\( q_{e_j}(0)-q_A(0)=\eta d_j-\varepsilon(r_j-r_A^{\mathrm{neu}})=g. \)

Thus every edge candidate initially strictly beats \(A\).

5. Which candidates can affect the election?

We prove that, under every feasible seed vector, exactly the edge candidates determine whether \(A\) gains the target party an additional seat.

All comparisons below hold for arbitrary fractional activation of every type.

Fixed target winners

Every fixed target winner precedes \(A\) by at least two positions in every active ranking, both before and after updating. For the last spacer, the separation is exactly two; the initial-block candidates and helpers have larger separations.

In every neutral ranking, each fixed target winner precedes \(A\) by at least one position. Hence its overall score exceeds \(A\)'s by at least

\( 2\eta+\varepsilon>g. \)

Edge scores can only decrease, while \(A\)'s score can only increase. Since their initial advantage is \(g\), every fixed target winner strictly beats every edge candidate.

Separators

Every separator precedes \(A\) by at least six positions in every active ranking, before and after updating. To check the closest case, consider the last triplet. An incident separator remains at its rank while \(A\) moves upward once; a nonincident separator moves downward once while \(A\) moves upward once. The resulting separation is six in either case.

In neutral rankings, a separator lies below \(A\) by at most \(3E\) positions. Therefore

\( q_{z_j}(x)-q_A(x)\geq6\eta-3E\varepsilon\geq6\eta-\frac14>g. \)

The last strict inequality follows from \(g<2\eta\) and \(\eta\geq11/12\). Thus every separator also strictly beats every edge candidate.

There are

\( h+E=k-E+Q-1\leq k-1 \)

fixed target winners and separators altogether. Since all of them beat every remaining candidate, they are always elected.

All other candidates lie below \(A\)

Every remaining target candidate follows \(A\) in every original and updated ranking. The update preserves the relative order of target candidates. Their scores therefore remain strictly below \(A\)'s.

Every opponent other than edge candidates, separators, \(u,v\) follows \(A\) in every original and updated ranking, so its score is strictly lower as well.

For \(u,v\), their Borda advantage over \(A\) in an active ranking is at most six, before or after updating. In neutral rankings, they lie below \(A\) by at least \(2k-1-r_A^{\mathrm{neu}}\geq k\) positions. Consequently,

\( q_u(x)-q_A(x),\ q_v(x)-q_A(x)\leq6\eta-\varepsilon k<0, \)

because \(\varepsilon\geq1/(24E)\) and \(k/(24E)>6\).

This completes the required classification of all candidates.

6. The exact edge inequalities

Let \(x_a\in[0,\delta]\) denote the seed mass assigned to vertex type \(a\), and put

\( S:=\sum_{a\in V}x_a\leq b=K\delta. \)

Seeds placed in neutral types have no electoral effect.

For an edge \(e_j=\{a,c\}\), the update calculation gives the exact identity

\( q_{e_j}(x)-q_A(x)=g-S-x_a-x_c. \)

Call an edge *passed* if its candidate has score at most \(A\)'s. Exact equality counts as passed because the target party wins ties against the opponent party.

If \(P(x)\) is the number of passed edges, the candidates strictly above \(A\) are precisely:

  • the \(h+E\) always-elected candidates;
  • the \(E-P(x)\) unpassed edge candidates.

Thus \(A\)'s election rank is

\( h+E+E-P(x)+1=k+Q-P(x). \)

It follows that

\( A\text{ is elected}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P(x)\geq Q. \)

We now connect this condition to CLIQUE.

A passed edge requires both endpoints to be nearly fully seeded

If edge \(\{a,c\}\) is passed, then

\( S+x_a+x_c\geq g=(K+2-\gamma)\delta. \)

Using \(S\leq K\delta\), we obtain

\( x_a+x_c\geq(2-\gamma)\delta. \)

Since each endpoint seed mass is at most \(\delta\), both satisfy

\( x_a,x_c\geq(1-\gamma)\delta. \)

Define

\( U:=\{a\in V:x_a\geq(1-\gamma)\delta\}. \)

The budget implies

\( |U|(1-\gamma)\delta\leq K\delta. \)

Because \(\gamma<1/(K+1)\), we have \(K/(1-\gamma)<K+1\), and hence \(|U|\leq K\).

Every passed edge belongs to the graph induced by \(U\). Therefore

\( P(x)\leq\binom{|U|}{2}\leq\binom{K}{2}=Q. \)

If \(A\) is elected, then \(P(x)\geq Q\), so all these inequalities are equalities. Hence \(|U|=K\), and every pair of vertices in \(U\) is an edge. Thus \(U\) is a \(K\)-clique.

A clique makes \(A\) elected

Conversely, suppose \(U\) is a \(K\)-clique. Seed each of its vertex types fully, using \(x_a=\delta\), and assign zero seeds elsewhere.

Then \(S=K\delta\). For every clique edge,

\( q_{e_j}(x)-q_A(x)=g-K\delta-2\delta=-\gamma\delta<0. \)

Thus at least \(Q\) edges are passed, and \(A\) is elected.

We have proved

\( \exists x\text{ feasible with }A\text{ elected}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G\text{ has a }K\text{-clique}. \)

7. The objective gap and approximation hardness

At zero seeds, no edge is passed, so \(A\) is unelected. The target party elects exactly its \(h\) fixed winners: \(H_1(0)=h\).

For every feasible seed vector, \(P(x)\leq Q\). Therefore:

  • if \(P(x)<Q\), \(A\) and all lower-scoring target candidates are unelected, giving \(H_1(x)=h\);
  • if \(P(x)=Q\), \(A\) occupies the last elected position, giving \(H_1(x)=h+1\).

Consequently,

\( \operatorname{OPT}_D=\begin{cases}1,&G\text{ has a }K\text{-clique},\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases} \)

For two parties, \(H_2(x)=k-H_1(x)\). Hence the CMEC objective satisfies the exact identity

\( M(x)=[2H_1(x)-k]-[2H_1(0)-k]=2D(x). \)

Thus

\( \operatorname{OPT}_M=\begin{cases}2,&G\text{ has a }K\text{-clique},\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases} \)

For any fixed \(\alpha>0\), a multiplicative approximation must return a positive objective value whenever the optimum is positive. Its output would therefore distinguish the two cases and solve CLIQUE.

This argument applies separately to CDW and CMEC. Moreover, every constructed instance has \(\beta=0\), so evaluating a rational output seed vector involves only rational linear scores and exact comparisons; no exponential-function evaluation is required.

8. Size and running times

The reduction has

\( p=2,\quad k=1000(E+1)^3,\quad m=2k,\quad \tau\leq n+E+1. \)

All numerical entries have \(O(\log(nE))\) bits. With explicitly listed rankings and a dense contact-rate matrix,

\( L=O\bigl((m\tau+\tau^2)\log(m+\tau)\bigr). \)

The construction takes \(\widetilde O(m\tau+\tau^2)\) bit operations, where the notation suppresses logarithmic factors.

On these zero-contact instances, objective evaluation uses \(O(m\tau+m\log m)\) rational arithmetic operations, with bit complexity polynomial in \(L\) and the seed vector’s encoding length.

Therefore, unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\), neither objective admits a fixed-positive-factor approximation with running time \((m+\tau+L+k+p)^{O(1)}\).
