The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given colleges \(C\), a finite set of student types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\nu_\theta\), type preference orders, rational per-capita capacities \(\kappa_c\), continuous utilities \(U_c\) over assigned type masses, and a feasible rational initial assignment, does the fixed-order blocking-mass refinement reach a state with no blocking move or repeat an exact state after finitely many updates? For a finite computational encoding, take the \(U_c\) to be rational polynomials; at each step choose the first blocking type, source, and destination, then a feasible mass transfer maximizing the receiving college’s utility gain, with fixed lexicographic tie-breaking.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\nu_\theta\), normalized by \(\sum_\theta\nu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq1\) colleges, nonnegative rational capacities \(\kappa_c\), and strict type rankings over \(C\cup\{\bot\}\), where \(\bot\) means unmatched.
An assignment has real coordinates \(x_{\theta j}\geq0\), with \(\sum_{j\in C\cup\{\bot\}}x_{\theta j}=\nu_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta c}\leq\kappa_c\). The initial assignment is rational. Each college evaluates its assigned vector \(x_c\) using an arbitrary real-valued rational polynomial \(U_c\), encoded as a list of monomials with binary coefficients and exponents. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; measure running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
For an incoming type \(\theta\), source \(j\in C\cup\{\bot\}\), and destination \(c\in C\) ranked strictly above \(j\), let \(h=\kappa_c-\sum_\eta x_{\eta c}\). A feasible move transfers \(a\in[0,x_{\theta j}]\) from \(j\) to \(c\), and expels \(b=\max\{0,a-h\}\) of one incumbent type \(\eta\) from \(c\) to \(\bot\), subject to \(b\leq x_{\eta c}\). All other coordinates stay unchanged; the source and expelled mass need not consent.
The triple \((\theta,j,c)\) is blocking if some feasible move has \(a>0\) and strictly increases \(U_c\). At each update, select the first blocking triple in fixed index order, maximize \(U_c(x'_c)-U_c(x_c)\) over its feasible moves, and break ties by the lexicographically smallest \((\eta,a)\). Include \(a=0\) in this maximization. Amounts and subsequent states are exact real numbers, without rounding.
Stop when no blocking triple exists or the entire assignment equals an earlier assignment exactly; impose no iteration cutoff.

Is deciding whether this refinement stops after finitely many updates, from the supplied initial assignment, solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite students, deterministic refinement stops at a matching without blocking pairs or a repeated matching after at most \(|\mathcal M|+1\) updates, where \(\mathcal M\) is the set of feasible matchings; this bound can be exponential in the student count. I prove $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness by a polynomial-time many-one reduction from $3$-SAT. The construction uses only two colleges and utilities of degree at most three.

I interpret “fixed index order” as lexicographic order of the input indices. The construction works for any fixed ordering of the three index fields.

1. A known Boolean computation construction

Let $G=(V,E)$ be an undirected graph with positive rational edge weights $w_{ij}$. For a Boolean vector $q\in\{0,1\}^{V}$, define its cut value by

$$ F(q)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}(q_i+q_j-2q_iq_j). $$

An improving flip changes one bit and strictly increases $F$. A maximal improving-flip sequence ends when no improving flip exists. Such sequences are finite, because $F$ strictly increases on the finite set of Boolean vectors.

We use the following known construction:

Forced-simulation fact. Given a $3$-CNF formula $\varphi$, one can construct, in polynomial time, a positively rational-weighted graph $G$, an initial Boolean vector $q^0$, and a designated vertex $v_*$ such that every maximal improving-flip sequence starting from $q^0$ ends at a vector $q^*$ satisfying

$$ q^*_{v_*}=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \varphi\text{ is satisfiable}. $$

This follows from the forced simulation in [Tscheuschner, *The Complexity of Local Max-Cut*, Theorem 3.5.22 and the proof of Theorem 3.7.1, pp. 85–91](https://digital.ub.uni-paderborn.de/hsx/download/pdf/442308). The construction forces every maximal flip sequence to have the prescribed simulation as its projection; designated terminal colors encode the simulated machine’s terminal configuration.

To check applicability, choose a fixed deterministic machine that enumerates all truth assignments, checks $\varphi$, and halts with an output bit indicating satisfiability. It uses linear space after constant-factor input padding. Include that output bit in its configuration encoding. The published simulation runs for more steps than there are machine configurations, treating halting configurations as absorbing. Because this machine always halts, the designated terminal bit is its correct output. The resulting graph and binary encodings of its rational weights have polynomial size.

The universal quantifier over improving-flip sequences will accommodate the refinement’s prescribed priority rule.

2. Society, rankings, and initial assignment

Write $V=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, where $n\geq1$. Let

$$ k=\left\lceil\log_2(4n+8)\right\rceil, \qquad \delta=2^{-k}. $$

There are two colleges, $A$ and $B$, with capacities

$$ \kappa_A=n\delta, \qquad \kappa_B=1. $$

For every vertex $i$, introduce two types $\theta_{i,0}$ and $\theta_{i,1}$, each of mass $\delta$. Introduce two additional types $\alpha,\beta$, also of mass $\delta$, and a filler type of mass

$$ 1-(2n+2)\delta. $$

The filler mass is positive, since

$$ (2n+2)\delta \leq \frac{2n+2}{4n+8} < \frac12. $$

Thus the support size is $\tau=2n+3$, and all masses are positive dyadic rationals summing to one.

Designate

$$ \sigma=\theta_{v_*,1}. $$

The strict rankings are:

| Types | Ranking |
|---|---|
| Boolean types other than $\sigma$ | $A\succ\bot\succ B$ |
| $\sigma$ | $A\succ B\succ\bot$ |
| $\alpha,\beta$ | $B\succ\bot\succ A$ |
| Filler | $\bot\succ A\succ B$ |

Initially, for every vertex $i$, assign the entire mass of $\theta_{i,q_i^0}$ to $A$ and leave $\theta_{i,1-q_i^0}$ unmatched. All other types are unmatched. College $B$ is empty.

Call an assignment Boolean at $A$ if, for every $i$, exactly one of $\theta_{i,0},\theta_{i,1}$ has its entire mass $\delta$ at $A$. Such an assignment encodes a Boolean vector $q$. College $A$ is then full.

3. A quadratic utility that implements exact improving flips

For the Boolean-type coordinates at $A$, write

$$ r_{i,b}=\frac{x_{\theta_{i,b},A}}{\delta}. $$

Extend $F$ to real arguments using the same polynomial, and put

$$ W=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}, \qquad M=W+1. $$

Define

$$ U_A(x_A) = F(r_{1,1},\ldots,r_{n,1}) - M\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r_{i,0}+r_{i,1}-1)^2 + 3M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{b=0}^{1}(r_{i,b}^2-r_{i,b}). $$

This utility ignores the remaining type coordinates.

At a Boolean assignment, both added sums vanish, so

$$ U_A(x_A)=F(q). $$

3.1 Strict convexity along every relevant move

Denote the displayed polynomial in $r$ by $V(r)$. For any direction $v\in\mathbb R^{2n}$,

$$ \begin{aligned} D^2V(r)[v,v] ={}& -4\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}v_{i,1}v_{j,1}\\ &-2M\sum_i(v_{i,0}+v_{i,1})^2 +6M\sum_{i,b}v_{i,b}^2. \end{aligned} $$

For each edge,

$$ -4w_{ij}v_{i,1}v_{j,1} \geq -2w_{ij}(v_{i,1}^2+v_{j,1}^2). $$

Each weighted vertex degree is at most $W$. Consequently, the edge contribution is at least $-2W\|v\|^2$. Also,

$$ -2M\sum_i(v_{i,0}+v_{i,1})^2 \geq -4M\|v\|^2. $$

Therefore

$$ D^2V(r)[v,v] \geq (6M-4M-2W)\|v\|^2 = 2\|v\|^2. $$

Thus $V$ is strictly convex.

At a Boolean assignment, any positive admission into $A$ must expel an occupied Boolean type: $A$ is full, and all other types have zero enrollment there. The incoming missing type has available mass $\delta$, as does every possible occupied incumbent.

For each such incumbent, the feasible interval is exactly $[0,\delta]$. Writing $t=a/\delta$, the move follows the affine path

$$ r(t)=r+t e_{\mathrm{in}}-t e_{\mathrm{out}}, \qquad 0\leq t\leq1. $$

Its utility gain is strictly convex in $t$. Hence its maximum occurs at an endpoint, and no interior amount maximizes it.

3.2 Only a correct Boolean swap can block

Suppose the incoming type is $\theta_{i,1-q_i}$.

If the expelled type is its occupied counterpart $\theta_{i,q_i}$, the endpoint $t=1$ is precisely the assignment obtained by flipping bit $i$. Its gain is

$$ F(q^{(i)})-F(q), $$

where $q^{(i)}$ denotes $q$ with bit $i$ flipped.

If instead an occupied type from another pair is expelled, the endpoint has one pair sum equal to two and another equal to zero. Thus the pair penalty contributes $-2M$. Every coordinate is still zero or one, so the last sum remains zero. Both the original and new vectors of coordinates $r_{i,1}$ are Boolean, whose cut values lie in $[0,W]$. The endpoint gain is therefore at most

$$ W-2M<0. $$

Strict convexity makes every positive interior amount on this incorrect path have negative gain as well: its gain is strictly below the interpolation between gain zero at $t=0$ and a negative gain at $t=1$.

For the correct counterpart, strict convexity similarly gives:

  • If the Boolean flip gain is positive, the unique maximizing amount is $a=\delta$.
  • If that gain is nonpositive, no positive amount gives a positive gain.

It follows that every blocking admission into $A$ corresponds exactly to an improving Boolean flip. Its uniquely selected incumbent is the counterpart type, and its uniquely maximizing amount is $\delta$. Thus the refinement’s incumbent and amount tie-breaks are respected.

Every such update preserves the Boolean form of the assignment at $A$.

4. A cubic utility that activates an infinite trajectory

At $B$, write

$$ t=x_{\sigma B}, \qquad z_1=x_{\alpha B}, \qquad z_2=x_{\beta B}, \qquad p=\frac{\delta}{4}. $$

Define

$$ U_B(x_B) = t\left[ 1-z_1^2-z_2^2+z_1z_2+p(z_1+z_2) \right]. $$

This is a rational polynomial of degree three.

When $t=0$, the utility is identically zero as $\alpha$ or $\beta$ moves. Hence neither oscillator type blocks.

When $B$ is empty and $\sigma$ is unmatched, admitting amount $a$ of $\sigma$ gives utility gain $a$. There is no capacity restriction or expulsion. Exact maximization uniquely selects

$$ a=\delta. $$

After this admission, $t=\delta$, and admissions of the two oscillator types maximize

$$ -z_1^2-z_2^2+z_1z_2+p(z_1+z_2). $$

Only $\sigma,\alpha,\beta$ can reach $B$ under the rankings. Their total mass is $3\delta<1$, so all these admissions fit without expulsion.

5. Priority prevents premature activation

Index $A$ before $B$. Put all Boolean types before $\alpha,\beta$, put $\sigma$ last among the Boolean types, and put $\alpha$ before $\beta$. Place the filler last.

Before activation, every potentially blocking Boolean move has source $\bot$ and destination $A$. The activation triple is

$$ (\sigma,\bot,B). $$

Every Boolean admission precedes this activation triple: its type index is at most that of $\sigma$, its source is the same, and its destination index is smaller. This also holds for any fixed lexicographic ordering of the three fields.

Before activation:

  • Neither oscillator type blocks, because $t=0$.
  • The filler has no eligible move from its current location.
  • No Boolean type other than $\sigma$ can enter $B$.
  • The type $\sigma$ cannot move from $A$ to $B$, because it prefers $A$.

Consequently, whenever an improving Boolean flip exists, the refinement performs one before any activation.

The updates at $A$ therefore form a maximal improving-flip sequence starting at $q^0$. This phase is finite, and its assignment never repeats, because $F(q)$ strictly increases.

Let $q^*$ be its terminal Boolean vector. The forced-simulation fact gives

$$ q^*_{v_*}=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \varphi\text{ is satisfiable}. $$

6. Satisfiable formulas produce finite stopping

Suppose $\varphi$ is satisfiable. Then $q^*_{v_*}=1$, so the entire mass of $\sigma$ is assigned to $A$.

No admission into $A$ blocks, by terminality of the Boolean flip sequence and Section 3. The activation triple has zero source mass. The type $\sigma$ cannot move from $A$ to $B$. Finally, $t=0$ makes all oscillator gains zero.

Thus no blocking triple remains. The refinement stops after finitely many updates.

7. Unsatisfiable formulas produce exact nontermination

Suppose $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable. Then $q^*_{v_*}=0$, so $\sigma$ is entirely unmatched. The activation triple blocks and moves its entire mass to $B$.

This transfer leaves $x_A$ unchanged. It also cannot create a blocking admission into $A$: the only changed possible source is $\sigma$, whose mass is now at $B$ rather than $\bot$. Transferring an amount from either source to $A$ has the same feasible destination changes and utility gains. These were already nonpositive at the terminal Boolean assignment.

Therefore $A$ remains fixed. The subsequent updates involve only $\alpha,\beta$, with $t=\delta$ fixed.

7.1 Exact oscillator maximization

If coordinate $z_i$ increases by $a$, with the other coordinate denoted $z_\ell$, its gain is

$$ \delta\left[a(p-2z_i+z_\ell)-a^2\right]. $$

Set

$$ g_i=p-2z_i+z_\ell. $$

When $g_i>0$ and $g_i/2$ is feasible, the unique maximizing amount is

$$ a=\frac{g_i}{2}, $$

with positive gain $\delta g_i^2/4$. When $g_i\leq0$, no positive amount gives positive gain.

Initially $z_1=z_2=0$. Both gradients equal $p$, so the priority rule updates $\alpha$ first, giving $z_1=p/2$.

7.2 An invariant proving infinitely many updates

After oscillator update $r\geq1$, let $i$ be the type just updated and $\ell$ the other type. I claim

$$ p-z_i=p\,2^{-r}, \qquad p-z_\ell=p\,2^{-(r-1)}. $$

The first update establishes the claim.

Assuming it after update $r$, substitution gives

$$ g_i=0, \qquad g_\ell=3p\,2^{-r}>0. $$

Thus only the other oscillator type blocks, and its unique maximizing amount is

$$ a=3p\,2^{-(r+1)}. $$

Its new deficit is

$$ p\,2^{-(r-1)}-3p\,2^{-(r+1)} = p\,2^{-(r+1)}. $$

This proves the invariant at the next update.

Every resulting coordinate is strictly below $p<\delta$, so the amount is available at its unmatched source. It also fits into $B$ without expulsion, as established in Section 4.

Hence a blocking triple exists after every finite oscillator update. Each update strictly increases one oscillator coordinate and leaves the other unchanged, so no assignment repeats during this phase. No assignment in this phase equals an earlier preactivation assignment, because its $\sigma$ enrollment at $B$ is $\delta$, whereas that enrollment was previously zero.

Neither stopping condition ever occurs.

8. Polynomial size and conclusion

The society has

$$ m=2, \qquad \tau=2n+3. $$

The number $\delta$ and all masses and capacities have $O(\log n)$ bits. The quadratic utility at $A$ expands into $O(n+|E|)$ monomials; the cubic utility at $B$ has constantly many monomials.

Summing the rational edge weights and scaling by $\delta^{-1}$ or $\delta^{-2}$ require only polynomially many bits and polynomial-time rational arithmetic. All exponents are at most three. Thus the complete refinement instance has size polynomial in $|\varphi|$ and is constructible in polynomial time.

We have proved the exact equivalence

$$ \varphi\text{ is satisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed refinement stops after finitely many updates}. $$

This establishes $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness. In particular, a polynomial-time algorithm in $m,\tau,L$ would imply $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$.
