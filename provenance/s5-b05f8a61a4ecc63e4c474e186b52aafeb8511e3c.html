6. A self-contained upper bound for the budget problem

The preceding polynomial-time test concerns existence at some cost. It does not imply polynomial-time budget feasibility.

For clarity, the general budget problem still has a polynomial-size existential real formulation. Here is the formulation and the required realization argument.

6.1. Variables and constraints

Use normalized quota $1$. Include explicitly
$$ 0<\lambda\leq k+1, \qquad \lambda\geq\frac{k+1}{1+B}. $$

For each baseline type $r$, let $b_{r,t}\geq0$ be its current weight, initialized by
$$ b_{r,1}=\lambda\mu_r. $$
At round $t<m$, impose
$$ b_{r,t+1}= \begin{cases} \alpha_t b_{r,t},&h_{r,t}=t,\\ b_{r,t},&h_{r,t}\neq t. \end{cases} $$
Exhausted weights may remain as bookkeeping variables but are excluded from tallies.

For additions introduce nonnegative variables:

  • $u_j$: initial mass first choosing candidate $j$;
  • $e$: initial empty mass;
  • $A_{t,j}$: current added weight at candidate $j\geq t$;
  • $z_{t,j}$ and $z_{t,\bot}$: outgoing added weight transferred to $j>t$ or exhausted.

Impose
$$ \lambda+e+\sum_j u_j=k+1, \qquad A_{1,j}=u_j, $$
$$ \sum_{j>t}z_{t,j}+z_{t,\bot}=\alpha_t A_{t,t}, $$
and
$$ A_{t+1,j}=A_{t,j}+z_{t,j}. $$
The combined tally is
$$ V_{t,j} = A_{t,j} + \sum_{r:h_{r,t}=j}b_{r,t}. $$

At a prescribed win require
$$ V_{t,t}\geq1, \qquad 0\leq\alpha_t\leq1, \qquad (1-\alpha_t)V_{t,t}=1. $$
Require $V_{t,t}\geq V_{t,j}$ for every competitor, making the comparison strict precisely when $j$ has earlier priority than $t$.

At a prescribed loss require
$$ \alpha_t=1, \qquad V_{t,j}<1 \quad(j\geq t). $$
Require $V_{t,t}\leq V_{t,j}$, making the comparison strict precisely when $j$ has earlier priority than $t$.

There is no constraint on the final label.

These are $O(\tau m+m^2)$ variables and constraints of degree at most $2$, constructible in polynomial time.

6.2. Why arbitrary added flows are realizable

Fix any feasible solution of these constraints. For each candidate $t$, its incoming added weight is
$$ I_t=u_t+\sum_{i<t}z_{i,t}=A_{t,t}. $$
Its outgoing weight is $\alpha_t I_t$.

Consider the directed graph with source arcs carrying $u_t$, candidate arcs carrying $z_{t,j}$, and exhaustion arcs carrying $z_{t,\bot}$. Candidate arcs strictly increase removal index.

Starting from a positive source arc, follow positive outgoing arcs. Stop at exhaustion, at the final candidate, or at a candidate whose multiplier is zero. A positive incoming weight and positive multiplier guarantee a positive outgoing arc whenever one is required.

A ballot following this path contributes its initial mass multiplied by the product of preceding multipliers to each traversed arc. Choose its initial mass as the minimum of the available arc weight divided by the corresponding multiplier product. All these denominators are positive.

Subtracting the path contribution preserves nonnegativity and conservation. At least one positive arc becomes zero. Repetition terminates after at most the number of arcs, and each extracted path is a strict ballot because its indices increase.

Including a possible empty ballot, at most
$$ \frac{m(m-1)}2+2m $$
positive-mass added types suffice. The extracted campaign realizes the prescribed flow and therefore the structure.

Conversely, an actual campaign directly supplies all the formulation’s variables. Thus the formulation is exact.

It follows that budget decision belongs to $\exists\mathbb{R}$. This establishes an upper bound, not $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness or polynomial-time solvability.

7. The remaining optimization question

The unresolved part is feasibility at a specified small budget and optimization of the finite cost. The quadratic equations above couple surplus multipliers, baseline weights, and added flows. The new feasibility characterization does not remove that coupling.

Nor can budget feasibility be replaced by comparing $B$ with the infimum. For example, take two candidates, $k=1$, baseline mass $1$ on $[A]$, and priority $A$ before $B$. Require $A$ to lose first.

Its tally is at least $1$, so a loss requires
$$ 1<\frac{1+a}{2}, $$
hence $a>1$. Conversely, adding mass $1$ on $[B]$ and any positive mass $e$ on the empty ballot gives tied tallies $1$ below quota $1+e/2$, and priority selects $A$ as the loser. The infimum is therefore $1$, but budget $1$ is infeasible.

What has been settled here is the exact distinction between infinite and finite infimum, with a polynomial-time rational witness in every finite case. A general polynomial-time algorithm for the finite optimum, attainment, and campaigns within rational $\varepsilon>0$—with running time polynomial in $\log(1+1/\varepsilon)$—or a valid hardness reduction remains to be proved.
