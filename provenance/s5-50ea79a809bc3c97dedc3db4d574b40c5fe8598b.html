Theorem 4.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a finite-support society \(\mu\) over locations \(x\in\mathbb{R}\) on a road, with binary-rational masses, consider deterministic rules \(M\) that place a facility in \(\mathbb{R}^2\). Require that for every type \(x\), every movable mass \(\eta\leq \mu_x\), and every finite-support report distribution \(\rho\) of mass \(\eta\), \(\|M(\mu-\eta\delta_x+\rho)-(x,0)\| \geq \|M(\mu)-(x,0)\|\). What is the infimum, over such cohort-strategyproof rules, of the worst-case ratio between maximum distance to the support and the smallest enclosing-circle radius of the support?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is a finite-support probability measure \(\mu=\sum_{x\in S}\mu_x\delta_x\) on \(\mathbb R\), given by the list of its \(\tau=|S|\) types with positive binary-rational masses summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) denotes the total mass-encoding bit length. Locations may be arbitrary real numbers, and the road has fixed dimension \(1\).
A deterministic rule \(M\) maps every such society to a facility in \(\mathbb R^2\); type \(x\) incurs Euclidean distance to \((x,0)\). Require unanimity: \(M(\delta_x)=(x,0)\).
Call \(M\) cohort-strategyproof if, for every society, every \(x\in S\), every rational \(0\leq\eta\leq\mu_x\), and every nonnegative finite-support report measure \(\rho\) on \(\mathbb R\) with rational masses and total mass \(\eta\), \(\|M(\mu-\eta\delta_x+\rho)-(x,0)\|\geq\|M(\mu)-(x,0)\|\). Reports may split the moved mass among several locations.
For \(|S|\geq2\), set \(R(\mu)=\min_{y\in\mathbb R^2}\max_{x\in S}\|y-(x,0)\|\) and \(A(M)=\sup_{\mu:\,|S|\geq2}\max_{x\in S}\|M(\mu)-(x,0)\|/R(\mu)\).

What is \(\inf_M A(M)\), where the infimum ranges over deterministic, unanimous, cohort-strategyproof rules on this domain?

For finite lists of \(n\geq2\) agents on a line with planar facility outputs, a deterministic strategyproof mechanism achieves approximation ratio \(\sqrt2\), and a matching lower bound holds for every deterministic strategyproof mechanism ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20196v1)). No prior result settling the exact rational-mass, split-report problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized society masses to sum to one, interpreting the society as a probability distribution.
  • Restricted moved masses and report-measure masses to rationals so that every deviation remains within the specified binary-rational society domain; report locations remain arbitrary real numbers.
  • Included singleton societies in the rule's domain and required unanimity there, reflecting the discrete approximation inequality when the optimum is zero; evaluated ratios only on supports containing at least two distinct locations.
  • Defined L as the total mass-encoding bit length because locations are arbitrary reals and the target asks for an approximation guarantee, not a running time.

Result

The infimum is attained and equals \(\boxed{\sqrt{2}}\).

I interpret the masses as rational numbers encoded in binary, consistent with the rational-report condition. The construction below is defined on all finite-support probability measures, so it also covers every rational split report.

1. Construction and approximation ratio

For a society \(\mu\), let \(a=\min\operatorname{supp}\mu\) and \(b=\max\operatorname{supp}\mu\). Define

\(\displaystyle M_*(\mu)=\left(\frac{a+b}{2},\frac{b-a}{2}\right).\)

This is the support-based version of the augmented midpoint mechanism in [Definition 4.1 of the supplied source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20196v1#S4.SS1.SSS1). We prove its stronger split-report property directly.

The rule is deterministic. If \(\mu=\delta_x\), then \(a=b=x\), giving \(M_*(\delta_x)=(x,0)\); hence it is unanimous.

Suppose \(a<b\), and write \(m=(a+b)/2\) and \(r=(b-a)/2\). For any facility \(y\), the triangle inequality gives

\(\displaystyle b-a\leq\|y-(a,0)\|+\|y-(b,0)\|\leq2\max_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}\|y-(x,0)\|.\)

Thus \(R(\mu)\geq r\). The facility \((m,0)\) has distance at most \(r\) from every support point, so \(R(\mu)=r\).

Our rule outputs \((m,r)\). Since every support point satisfies \(|x-m|\leq r\),

\(\displaystyle \|M_*(\mu)-(x,0)\|^2=(m-x)^2+r^2\leq2r^2.\)

Equality holds at both endpoints. Consequently, \(A(M_*)=\sqrt{2}\).

2. Cohort-strategyproofness, including split reports

Fix a society \(\mu\), a type \(x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\), and any permitted deviation

\(\displaystyle \nu=\mu-\eta\delta_x+\rho.\)

The measure \(\nu\) is nonnegative, has total mass \(1\), and has finite, nonempty support. Let \(a',b'\) be its support endpoints.

For any endpoints \(c\leq d\), expansion yields the identity

\(\displaystyle \left\|\left(\frac{c+d}{2},\frac{d-c}{2}\right)-(x,0)\right\|^2 =\frac{(c-x)^2+(d-x)^2}{2}.\)

We show that each endpoint term cannot decrease.

  • If \(a<x\), then the positive mass at \(a\) is untouched by subtracting mass at \(x\). Thus \(a\in\operatorname{supp}\nu\), implying \(a'\leq a<x\), and therefore \((a'-x)^2\geq(a-x)^2\). If \(a=x\), the same inequality holds because its right-hand side is zero.
  • If \(x<b\), the positive mass at \(b\) likewise remains. Thus \(b'\geq b>x\), giving \((b'-x)^2\geq(b-x)^2\). If \(b=x\), the right-hand side is zero.

Adding these inequalities and applying the identity gives

\(\displaystyle \|M_*(\nu)-(x,0)\|^2\geq\|M_*(\mu)-(x,0)\|^2.\)

Taking nonnegative square roots proves the required cohort-strategyproofness inequality. This argument covers partial or complete removal of the mass at \(x\), with that mass redistributed among any finite number of locations.

3. Matching lower bound

We use the supplied finite-agent result: every deterministic strategyproof mechanism for two agents on the line, with facility outputs in the plane, has approximation ratio at least \(\sqrt{2}\). This is the two-agent case of [Theorem 4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20196v1#S4.SS1.SSS1).

Let \(M\) be any deterministic, unanimous, cohort-strategyproof society rule. Define a two-agent mechanism by

\(\displaystyle F(u,v)=M\left(\frac12\delta_u+\frac12\delta_v\right).\)

These societies have admissible rational masses, including when the locations coincide.

For the first agent, a unilateral report change from \(u\) to \(z\) is exactly the permitted cohort deviation with \(\eta=1/2\) and \(\rho=(1/2)\delta_z\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \|F(z,v)-(u,0)\|\geq\|F(u,v)-(u,0)\|.\)

The identical argument applies to the second agent. Thus \(F\) is deterministic and strategyproof. Moreover, \(F(u,u)=(u,0)\) by unanimity.

For \(u\neq v\), the society and the two-agent profile have the same maximum-distance objective and the same optimum \(|u-v|/2\). Therefore, if \(A(M)\) is finite, \(F\) is \(A(M)\)-approximate; coincident profiles also satisfy this guarantee because their cost is zero. The finite-agent lower bound implies \(A(M)\geq\sqrt{2}\). If \(A(M)\) is infinite, that inequality is immediate.

Together with the constructed rule, this proves \(\inf_M A(M)=\sqrt{2}\), with the infimum attained.
