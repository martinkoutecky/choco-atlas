The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two alternatives \(s,t\), a finite set of role types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_u>0\) summing to \(1\), and an allowed option set \(A_u\subseteq T\cup\{s,t\}\) for each type \(u\), choose a revised mass profile \(\eta_{u,B}\geq 0\) with \(\sum_B\eta_{u,B}=\mu_u\). Its cost is \(\sum_{u\in T}\sum_{B\subseteq T\cup\{s,t\}}\eta_{u,B}|A_u\triangle B|\). Each option targeting a role permits delegation to any member of that role. Is there a revised profile of cost at most a given rational budget \(\beta\), leaving a well-formed election, such that \(s\) wins in every valid delegation, where each agent chooses one allowed option and almost every delegation path ends at an alternative? With two alternatives, majority and plurality coincide.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list of \(\tau=|T|\) role types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_u\), summing to \(1\), and option sets \(A_u\subseteq T\cup\{s,t\}\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible option sets; there are \(m=2\) distinct alternatives, and the budget is a binary rational \(\beta\geq0\).
Realize each role as an interval of length \(\mu_u\) with Lebesgue measure. An alternative option permits a direct vote; a role option permits delegation to any other individual of that role, with no capacity bound. Option sets may mix roles and alternatives.
Choose real masses \(\eta_{u,B}\geq0\), indexed by \(u\in T\) and \(B\subseteq T\cup\{s,t\}\), with \(\sum_B\eta_{u,B}=\mu_u\). Partition each role interval into subintervals of these lengths, omitting zero-length cells; individuals in a cell have options \(B\), while retaining their role.
The cost is \(\sum_{u\in T}\sum_{B\subseteq T\cup\{s,t\}}\eta_{u,B}|A_u\triangle B|\); inserting and deleting options are both permitted.
A profile is well formed if every individual has a finite allowed path to an alternative and at least one valid delegation exists; the input profile must also be well formed. A valid delegation is a measurable choice of one permitted individual or alternative for each individual, whose iterates reach an alternative after finitely many steps almost everywhere.
Alternatives are absorbing and have no voting mass. An alternative's score is the mass of individuals whose paths end there; \(s\) wins when its score is at least \(1/2\), so ties count as wins.
The decision problem asks whether some well-formed revised profile of cost at most \(\beta\) makes \(s\) win in every valid delegation. Measure running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the total input bit length \(L\), including the budget encoding.

Is this continuous All Election Bribery decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete All Election Bribery is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and \(\mathrm{W}[1]\)-hard parameterized by the arc-edit budget \(k\), even with two alternatives on directed acyclic graphs of maximum outdegree and depth two; discrete All Winner Determination with two alternatives is solvable in linear time. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.05482)
No result settling this exact continuous mass model was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the individual population and delegation semantics precise using intervals with Lebesgue measure, measurable delegation choices, and finite termination almost everywhere.
  • Specified real revised masses and omitted zero-mass cells, so an absent option subtype supplies no individuals as delegation targets.
  • Defined well-formedness by finite allowed reachability for every individual and existence of a valid delegation; required it for both the input and revised profiles.
  • Specified that roles remain fixed after option changes, role targets include all revised subtypes of that role, and delegation has no capacity bound.
  • Excluded self-delegation while allowing delegation to another individual of the same role.
  • Fixed ties as wins, alternatives as absorbing objects with zero voting mass, and the budget domain as nonnegative binary rationals.
  • Retained arbitrary mixtures of role and alternative options and both insertion and deletion moves, following the general election-graph definition rather than the narrower informal voting scenario.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The continuous All Election Bribery problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The reduction below permits arbitrary real cell masses and arbitrary insertions and deletions. Thus, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Reachability lemma (proved). Consider any well-formed revised profile. Call a cell *safe* if its individuals have no finite allowed path to \(t\), and let \(F\) be the total mass of safe cells. Then the minimum score of \(s\) over valid delegations is exactly \(F\). Consequently, \(s\) wins in every valid delegation if and only if \(F\geq 1/2\).

To prove this, construct a finite directed graph whose vertices are the positive-mass cells and the alternatives. A cell with options \(B\) has an arc to each alternative in \(B\), and to every cell whose role belongs to \(B\). Self-loops can be retained; they never occur on shortest paths.

Individual reachability of an alternative is equivalent to cell-graph reachability. An individual path projects to a graph walk. Conversely, a shortest graph path visits distinct cells, so it can be realized by choosing an individual in each successive cell. Every delegation step then goes to a different individual, as required.

Every valid delegation sends almost every individual in a safe cell to \(s\): termination is required almost everywhere, and termination at \(t\) is impossible. Hence every valid delegation gives \(s\) score at least \(F\).

For attainability, choose one representative in each cell. In every cell that reaches \(t\), choose an outgoing arc that decreases shortest-path distance to \(t\), delegating to the representative of its target cell or directly to \(t\). All individuals in these cells reach \(t\).

Every remaining cell reaches \(s\), by well-formedness. Moreover, no outgoing cell arc from such a cell can lead to a cell reaching \(t\), since that would give the source a path to \(t\). Choose outgoing arcs decreasing shortest-path distance to \(s\) within these remaining cells.

The resulting choice is measurable because it is constant on each of finitely many measurable cells. Each individual reaches an alternative after finitely many steps because the relevant distance strictly decreases. Representatives always lie in different cells from their delegators. This is therefore a valid delegation, and its score for \(s\) is exactly \(F\). This proves the lemma.

Reduction construction. We reduce from CLIQUE: given an explicitly represented simple undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\) and an integer \(k\), decide whether \(G\) contains a clique of size \(k\). This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete by [Karp’s theorem](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Write \(n=|V|\), \(q=|E|\), and assume \(2\leq k\leq n\). This restriction preserves hardness: excluded cases are immediately decidable and can be mapped to fixed yes or no instances satisfying the restriction.

Let \(C=\binom{k}{2}\). Choose powers of two \(D,H\) satisfying

\(D\geq 8(q+C+1),\qquad H\geq 4(n+1),\)

and set

\(\delta=D^{-1},\qquad \varepsilon=(DH)^{-1},\qquad \beta=2k\varepsilon.\)

In particular,

\(k\varepsilon\leq n\varepsilon<\delta/4.\)

Create one role \(x_v\) for each vertex, one role \(y_e\) for each edge, and two additional roles \(a,b\). Define their masses and original options as follows.

| Role | Mass | Original options |
|---|---|---|
| \(x_v\), for \(v\in V\) | \(\varepsilon\) | \(\{t\}\) |
| \(y_e\), for \(e=\{u,v\}\in E\) | \(\delta\) | \(\{x_u,x_v\}\) |
| \(a\) | \(b_s=1/2-C\delta-k\varepsilon\) | \(\{s\}\) |
| \(b\) | \(b_t=1-b_s-n\varepsilon-q\delta\) | \(\{t\}\) |

These masses sum to \(1\). They are all positive. Indeed, \(C\delta<1/8\), \(q\delta<1/8\), and \(n\varepsilon<\delta/4\leq 1/32\). Therefore,

\(b_s>1/2-1/8-1/32>0,\)

and, using the definition of \(b_t\),

\(b_t=1/2+C\delta-q\delta+(k-n)\varepsilon>1/2-1/8-1/32>0.\)

All masses and the budget are dyadic rationals.

The input profile is well formed. Vertex roles and padding roles vote directly; edge-role individuals can delegate to either endpoint role and then reach \(t\). Choosing fixed representatives gives a measurable valid delegation. Every original option set has size at most two, and every original allowed path reaches an alternative within two steps.

Forward implication (proved). Suppose \(K\subseteq V\) is a clique of size \(k\). Change the entire role \(x_v\) from options \(\{t\}\) to options \(\{s\}\) for each \(v\in K\), leaving all other roles unchanged.

Each changed individual incurs two edits, so the total cost is \(2k\varepsilon=\beta\). The revised profile remains well formed.

Its safe cells are precisely:

  • role \(a\);
  • the \(k\) selected vertex roles;
  • the \(C\) edge roles corresponding to edges inside \(K\).

Their total mass is

\(F=b_s+k\varepsilon+C\delta=1/2.\)

By the reachability lemma and the specified tie rule, \(s\) wins in every valid delegation.

Reverse implication (proved). Suppose an arbitrary well-formed revision of cost at most \(\beta\) makes \(s\) win in every valid delegation. Its cell masses may be arbitrary real numbers.

Let

\(S=\{v\in V:\text{every positive-mass cell of role }x_v\text{ is safe}\},\qquad r=|S|.\)

A safe cell in a vertex role cannot contain option \(t\), since that option gives a direct path to \(t\). Its option set \(B\) must also be nonempty, because every individual must have a finite allowed path to an alternative. Since its original options were \(\{t\}\), its cost per unit mass is therefore

\(|\{t\}\triangle B|=1+|B|\geq 2.\)

Each vertex role indexed by \(S\) consequently costs at least \(2\varepsilon\). Thus,

\(2r\varepsilon\leq\beta=2k\varepsilon,\qquad\text{so }r\leq k.\)

Let \(e(S)\) denote the number of graph edges with both endpoints in \(S\).

We now bound the safe mass by separating cells whose options were changed from cells whose options were retained.

Every changed cell has cost per unit mass at least \(1\). Hence the total mass of changed safe cells is at most the total editing cost, and therefore at most \(\beta\).

For unchanged safe cells:

  • role \(a\) contributes at most \(b_s\);
  • vertex roles and role \(b\) contribute nothing, because unchanged cells have options \(\{t\}\);
  • an unchanged cell in edge role \(y_{\{u,v\}}\) is safe exactly when \(u,v\in S\).

For the last assertion, if either endpoint role contains a cell reaching \(t\), an unchanged edge-cell individual can delegate into that cell and reach \(t\). Conversely, if all cells of both endpoint roles are safe, neither allowed first step can lead to \(t\). The lack of delegation capacity bounds is essential here.

It follows that

\(F\leq b_s+e(S)\delta+\beta.\)

If \(e(S)\leq C-1\), then

\(F\leq (1/2-C\delta-k\varepsilon)+(C-1)\delta+2k\varepsilon=1/2-\delta+k\varepsilon<1/2.\)

This contradicts the reachability lemma, because the revision makes \(s\) win universally. Consequently \(e(S)\geq C\).

Since \(G\) is simple and \(r\leq k\),

\(e(S)\leq\binom{r}{2}\leq\binom{k}{2}=C.\)

Thus equality holds throughout. Because \(k\geq 2\), equality forces \(r=k\), and \(e(S)=\binom{k}{2}\) means that every pair of vertices in \(S\) is adjacent. Therefore \(S\) is a clique of size \(k\).

This argument covers all real-valued splits and all allowed revisions. Fractional edits cannot compensate for a missing clique edge: their total safe mass is bounded by the budget, while the missing edge creates the strict deficit above.

Encoding and conclusion (proved). Choose \(D,H\) as the smallest powers of two satisfying their bounds. Since \(q,C\leq\binom{n}{2}\), their binary encodings have length \(O(\log n)\). The masses, padding masses, and budget also have \(O(\log n)\)-bit encodings. There are \(\tau=n+q+2\) roles, each with at most two original options, so the complete construction has polynomial size and is computable in polynomial time.

We have proved that the constructed continuous instance admits a successful revision of cost at most \(\beta\) if and only if \(G\) contains a clique of size \(k\). This settles the requested classification as \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, already with two alternatives and the stated restricted initial profiles.
