Proposition A.5 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Input candidates \(C\), a committee \(W\), a finite support \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), approval sets \(A_t\subseteq C\), and rational payments \(p_{t,d}\geq 0\) and residuals \(r_t\geq 0\), where \(p_{t,d}=0\) if \(d\notin A_t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p_{t,d}=1\) for each \(d\in W\). Decide whether for every \(c\in C\setminus W\), \(S\subseteq W\), and nonnegative mass portions \(x_t,y_t\) satisfying \(x_t+y_t\leq\mu_t\), \(x_t=y_t=0\) when \(c\notin A_t\), \(x_t=0\) when \(|A_t\cap S|>1\), and \(y_t=0\) when \(A_t\cap S\neq\varnothing\), the deviation value \(\sum_{t\in T}(x_t\max_{d\in S}p_{t,d}+y_t r_t)\) is at most \(1\), with \(\max\varnothing=0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a finite candidate set, \(m=|C|\), and \(\varnothing\ne W\subseteq C\), with \(k=|W|\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type specifies a nonempty approval set \(A_t\subseteq C\), binary rational payment densities \(p_{t,d}\ge0\) for \(d\in W\), and a binary rational residual density \(r_t\ge0\). Every candidate has positive approving mass. Types include their payment and residual data, so identical approval sets may have different types.
Require \(p_{t,d}=0\) whenever \(d\notin A_t\), and \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p_{t,d}=1\) for every \(d\in W\). Budgets are unrestricted and derived as \(b_t=r_t+\sum_{d\in W}p_{t,d}\).
An admissible deviation chooses \(c\in C\setminus W\), \(S\subseteq W\), and real mass portions \(x_t,y_t\ge0\) with \(x_t+y_t\le\mu_t\). Require \(x_t=y_t=0\) if \(c\notin A_t\), \(x_t=0\) if \(|A_t\cap S|>1\), and \(y_t=0\) if \(A_t\cap S\ne\varnothing\).
Its value is \(\sum_{t\in T}(x_t\max_{d\in S}p_{t,d}+y_t r_t)\), with \(\max\varnothing=0\). The input is weakly stable precisely when every admissible deviation has value at most \(1\).
State running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length.

Is continuous weak-stability recognition solvable in polynomial time, or is detecting an admissible deviation of value greater than \(1\) \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard (equivalently, recognition \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hard)?

For finite lists of voters, weak-stability recognition is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete, even for Pareto-optimal committees; detecting a violation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. [Discrete complexity result](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24307v1#A1)

Result

Detecting an admissible deviation of value greater than \(1\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Continuous weak-stability recognition is therefore \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete.

Hardness holds even when there is exactly one candidate outside the committee, all residuals are zero, all types have equal masses, every approval set has size at most three, and the committee is Pareto optimal.

1. Optimizing the continuous mass portions

Fix \(c\in C\setminus W\) and \(S\subseteq W\). Let \(F_c(S)\) denote the maximum value of an admissible deviation with these fixed choices.

The optimization separates across types: each constraint involving \(x_t,y_t\) concerns only that type. Types not approving \(c\) must contribute zero. For a type approving \(c\), there are three cases.

  • If \(A_t\cap S=\varnothing\), every payment \(p_{t,d}\) with \(d\in S\) is zero. Thus the contribution is \(y_t r_t\), whose maximum is \(\mu_t r_t\), attained by \(x_t=0,y_t=\mu_t\).
  • If \(A_t\cap S=\{d\}\), then \(y_t=0\). All payments to other members of \(S\) are zero, so the contribution is \(x_t p_{t,d}\). Its maximum is \(\mu_t p_{t,d}\), attained by \(x_t=\mu_t,y_t=0\).
  • If \(|A_t\cap S|\geq 2\), both \(x_t\) and \(y_t\) must be zero.

Consequently, writing \(d_t(S)\) for the unique member of \(A_t\cap S\) when that intersection is a singleton,

\(F_c(S)=\sum_{t:\ c\in A_t,\ A_t\cap S=\varnothing}\mu_t r_t+\sum_{t:\ c\in A_t,\ |A_t\cap S|=1}\mu_t p_{t,d_t(S)}.\)

The maximizing choices above can be made simultaneously, because there are no constraints coupling different types. Hence this formula gives an attained maximum, not merely an upper bound.

It follows that a violation exists exactly when some pair \((c,S)\) satisfies \(F_c(S)>1\). Allowing fractional mass portions does not increase the optimum beyond these choices using entire type masses.

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) and running times

A certificate for a violation consists only of \(c\) and the \(k\)-bit incidence vector of \(S\). The verifier evaluates the formula above using exact rational arithmetic.

Each contributing term is a product of two input rationals. The sum contains at most \(\tau\) terms, and its numerator and denominator have \(O(L)\) bits: their lengths are bounded by the total lengths of the participating input numerators and denominators, plus \(O(\log\tau)\). Thus comparison with \(1\) is polynomial-time.

With approval-incidence preprocessing and ordinary integer arithmetic, a conservative verification bound is

\(O\bigl((m\tau+\tau k)L^2\bigr)\)

bit operations. Therefore violation detection belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), and recognition belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\).

Enumerating all outside candidates and all removed sets gives an exact recognition algorithm with the conservative bound

\(O\bigl(m\,2^k\,\tau k\,L^2\bigr).\)

If \(C=W\), stability holds vacuously.

3. A normalized Max-Cut reduction

We use the following known result: given a finite simple unweighted graph \(G=(V,E)\) and an integer \(K\), deciding whether some cut contains at least \(K\) edges is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. This is the Simple Max Cut result of [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(76)90059-1).

We may restrict to \(1\leq K\leq |E|\): thresholds outside this range are trivial and can be mapped to fixed yes- or no-instances within the range.

Ensuring that the threshold exceeds every degree

Let \(\Delta_0\) be the maximum degree of the source graph. Add \(q=\Delta_0+1\) disjoint edges, on new vertices, and replace \(K\) by \(K+q\).

The maximum cut size increases by exactly \(q\). Indeed, any cut crosses at most these \(q\) added edges, and any cut of the original graph can be extended to cross all of them. Thus the transformation preserves the answer.

The resulting maximum degree is \(\max(\Delta_0,1)\), whereas the new threshold is strictly larger. The transformation has polynomial size.

Rename the resulting instance \((G,K)\), and write

\(n=|V|,\qquad h=|E|,\qquad \Delta=\max_{v\in V}\deg_G(v).\)

We now have \(1\leq K\leq h\) and \(K>\Delta\).

Candidates, types, and densities

Create one winner \(d_v\) for every vertex \(v\), and one outside candidate \(c\):

\(W=\{d_v:v\in V\},\qquad C=W\cup\{c\}.\)

There are \(h\) edge types and \(n\) filler types. Set

\(\tau=h+n,\qquad \mu_t=1/\tau\ \text{for every type},\qquad \alpha=\frac{2}{2K-1}.\)

All residual densities are zero.

For each edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), create a type \(t_e\) with

\(A_{t_e}=\{c,d_u,d_v\},\qquad p_{t_e,d_u}=p_{t_e,d_v}=\tau\alpha.\)

Its other payment densities are zero.

For each vertex \(v\), create a filler type \(f_v\) with

\(A_{f_v}=\{d_v\},\qquad p_{f_v,d_v}=\tau\bigl(1-\alpha\deg_G(v)\bigr).\)

Its other payment densities are zero.

Checking every input requirement

Since \(K>\Delta\) and both are integers,

\(\alpha\deg_G(v)\leq\frac{2(K-1)}{2K-1}<1.\)

Thus every filler payment is strictly positive. All densities are nonnegative rationals, and payments occur only to approved candidates.

The \(\tau\) masses are positive and sum to \(1\). For each winner \(d_v\), the incoming payment is exactly

\(\sum_t\mu_t p_{t,d_v}=\deg_G(v)\alpha+\bigl(1-\alpha\deg_G(v)\bigr)=1.\)

Every winner has positive approving mass because of its filler type. Candidate \(c\) has positive approving mass because \(h\geq 1\). All approval sets are nonempty. Budgets are obtained by the prescribed formula, with no further restriction required.

The construction therefore satisfies every premise of the mass problem.

4. Correctness of the reduction

For \(S\subseteq W\), define the corresponding vertex subset

\(U_S=\{v\in V:d_v\in S\}.\)

Filler types do not approve \(c\), so they cannot contribute to a deviation toward \(c\). An edge type contributes positively exactly when precisely one of its two approved winners belongs to \(S\), equivalently when its edge crosses the cut \((U_S,V\setminus U_S)\).

In that case its optimal contribution is

\(\mu_{t_e}p_{t_e,d}=(1/\tau)(\tau\alpha)=\alpha.\)

If neither endpoint is selected, its residual and relevant payments are zero. If both endpoints are selected, admissibility forces its mass portions to zero.

Writing \(\delta_G(U)\) for the edges with exactly one endpoint in \(U\), we obtain the exact identity

\(F_c(S)=\alpha|\delta_G(U_S)|=\frac{2|\delta_G(U_S)|}{2K-1}.\)

Because the cut size is an integer,

\(F_c(S)>1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2|\delta_G(U_S)|>2K-1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |\delta_G(U_S)|\geq K.\)

Every vertex subset corresponds to a removed set \(S\), and \(c\) is the only outside candidate. Therefore the constructed mass instance admits a violation if and only if the Max-Cut instance is a yes-instance.

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction, proving \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

5. Size, Pareto optimality, and conclusion

The constructed parameters are

\(m=n+1,\qquad k=n,\qquad \tau=h+n.\)

Every rational has \(O(\log(m+\tau))\) bits: \(K\leq h\), and the payment numerators are bounded by a polynomial in \(h+n\). Even with a dense payment table,

\(L=O\bigl(\tau k\log(m+\tau)\bigr).\)

A conservative construction-time bound is \(O\bigl(\tau k\log^2(m+\tau)\bigr)\) bit operations. Thus both the padding and the society construction have polynomial size and running time.

For completeness, define Pareto optimality here by approval counts: no committee of size \(k\) gives every type at least its current number of approved winners and gives some type strictly more. Any different committee of size \(k\) must omit some \(d_v\). Its positive-mass filler type \(f_v\), approving only \(d_v\), then loses its sole approved winner. Hence \(W\) is Pareto optimal.

Combining the reduction with membership proves that violation detection is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete and recognition is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete. A polynomial-time recognition algorithm would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
