The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given criteria \(N\), ordered domains \(X_i\), categories \(C_1 \prec \cdots \prec C_p\), and a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) with positive rational masses over labeled types \((x,k)\), decide whether there are nondecreasing frontier profiles \(b^1,\ldots,b^{p-1}\) and an upset \(\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^N\) such that the resulting U-NCS model assigns every \(x\) in \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\) to its observed category \(k\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(N=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(m\geq1\), and let \(C_1\prec\cdots\prec C_p\), with \(p\geq2\). Each criterion has domain \(X_i=\mathbb R\), ordered so that larger values are better; observed coordinates are binary rationals.
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) labeled types \((x,k)\), with \(x\in\mathbb Q^m\) and \(k\in\{1,\ldots,p\}\), each with positive binary-rational mass, summing to \(1\). Each observed profile has a unique label. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A U-NCS model consists of real frontier profiles \(b^1,\ldots,b^{p-1}\) satisfying \(b_i^h\leq b_i^{h+1}\), and one upset \(\mathcal F\subseteq2^N\), shared by all frontiers. No additive representation of \(\mathcal F\) is required.
Set \(S_h(x)=\{i\in N:x_i\geq b_i^h\}\). The model assigns \(x\) to \(C_{1+|\{h\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}:S_h(x)\in\mathcal F\}|}\); equality with a frontier therefore counts toward the higher category. Empty and full upsets are allowed.
Exact consistency means assigning every support type \((x,k)\) to \(C_k\).

Is exact U-NCS consistency for this rational-mass society solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,p,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite labeled examples, U-NCS representability has a faithful SAT encoding. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.10098)
The related two-category inverse NCS problem, with criterion-wise complete preorders and unknown approval upsets and sufficient coalitions, is NP-hard. [Source](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2018/0010.pdf)
No result explicitly stated for rational-mass societies was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The ordered domains lacked an effective input representation. Chose real domains with binary-rational observed coordinates and real frontiers; larger coordinates mean better performance.
  • Normalized the positive rational masses to sum to one because the input is a distribution.
  • Required a unique observed label per profile, following the discrete definition of reference assignments as a function.
  • Resolved the inconsistent frontier indexing by placing frontier h between categories h and h+1 and giving an explicit assignment formula with automatic treatment of the extreme categories.
  • Preserved weak frontier comparisons, coordinate-wise nondecreasing frontiers, and an unrestricted shared upset, including empty and full upsets.

Result

Exact U-NCS consistency is \( \mathrm{NP}\text{-complete} \), already for \( p=2 \). Thus a polynomial-time algorithm in \( m,\tau,p,L \) would imply \( \mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP} \).

The proof transfers the cited inverse-NCS hardness result to numerical profiles, explicitly handles unique profiles and positive masses, and supplies a polynomial certificate without enumerating the upset.

1. Transferring inverse-NCS hardness

The known result we use is Proposition 2 of [*Accountable Approval Sorting*, Section 3.3](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2018/0010.pdf):

Given finitely many alternatives, a fixed complete preorder on each criterion, and a GOOD/BAD label for each alternative, deciding whether there exist criterion-wise approval upsets and an upset of sufficient coalitions realizing all labels is \( \mathrm{NP}\text{-hard} \).

The preorders are input data; only the approval sets and sufficient coalitions are unknown. This matches the required separation between fixed numerical orders and unknown thresholds.

Let an instance have a nonempty alternative set \( A \) and \( m\geq1 \) criteria. For criterion \( i \), list its indifference classes from worst to best as \( E_{i,0},\ldots,E_{i,r_i-1} \). Define the integer profile of alternative \( a \) by
\( x_i(a)=j \) whenever \( a\in E_{i,j} \).
Consequently, higher numerical values correspond exactly to better positions in the given preorder.

Approval sets become thresholds. An upset of a complete preorder contains either every member of an indifference class or none: if two alternatives are indifferent, each is weakly preferred to the other. Moreover, the accepted classes form a suffix of the ordered classes. Hence every approval upset has the form
\( A_i=\{a:x_i(a)\geq t_i\} \)
for some integer \( t_i\in\{0,\ldots,r_i\} \). The choices \( t_i=0 \) and \( t_i=r_i \) represent approval of every alternative and of no alternative, respectively.

Conversely, every real threshold defines an approval upset of the original preorder.

Set \( p=2 \), identify BAD with \( C_1 \) and GOOD with \( C_2 \), and use \( b_i^1=t_i \). Then the original approval coalition of each alternative is exactly \( S_1(x(a)) \). Keeping the same sufficient-coalition upset therefore preserves every assignment in both directions. With one frontier, the frontier-order condition is vacuous.

Ensuring unique observed profiles

Different alternatives can receive identical rank profiles.

If identical profiles have different labels, the inverse-NCS instance is infeasible: every admissible approval set treats those alternatives identically on every criterion, so their approval coalitions coincide. In this case, output the fixed infeasible society with \( m=1 \), \( p=2 \), profiles \( 0 \) and \( 1 \), and respective labels \( C_2 \) and \( C_1 \), each of mass \( 1/2 \). It is infeasible because \( S_1(0)\subseteq S_1(1) \), and an upset cannot accept the former while rejecting the latter.

Otherwise, merge identical profiles. Their labels agree, and deleting duplicate constraints preserves feasibility in both directions.

Thus the resulting numerical instance satisfies the unique-label requirement.

2. Adding positive binary-rational masses

Suppose the resulting support contains \( T\geq1 \) distinct profiles. Let \( d=\lceil\log_2 T\rceil \). Give each of the first \( T-1 \) profiles mass \( 2^{-d} \), and give the last profile mass
\( 1-(T-1)2^{-d} \).

These masses sum to \( 1 \). The last mass is positive because \( T\leq2^d \), so
\( 1-(T-1)2^{-d}\geq2^{-d}>0 \).
Every mass is dyadic and requires only \( O(\log(T+1)) \) bits.

Exact consistency depends only on the labeled support: every mass is positive, and the definition requires every support type to receive its specified category. Adding these masses therefore preserves feasibility.

Rank coordinates are integers bounded by the number of original alternatives. Ranking the preorders, detecting duplicate profiles, and constructing the masses all take polynomial time and produce polynomial-length output. This completes a polynomial many-one reduction and proves \( \mathrm{NP}\text{-hardness} \) for \( p=2 \).

3. A polynomial certificate for arbitrary numbers of categories

We next prove membership in \( \mathrm{NP} \), including when \( p \) is encoded in binary.

Eliminating the explicit upset

For ordered frontiers, \( S_{h+1}(x)\subseteq S_h(x) \). Since \( \mathcal F \) is an upset, the successful frontier indices form an initial segment. Thus a type \( (x,k) \) receives exactly label \( C_k \) if and only if:

  • when \( k>1 \), \( S_{k-1}(x)\in\mathcal F \);
  • when \( k<p \), \( S_k(x)\notin\mathcal F \).

For chosen frontiers, collect these required-positive and required-negative coalitions into families \( \mathcal P \) and \( \mathcal Q \), respectively.

Lemma. A suitable upset exists exactly when no \( P\in\mathcal P \) and \( Q\in\mathcal Q \) satisfy \( P\subseteq Q \).

Proof. Such a containment is impossible if \( P \) must belong to an upset and \( Q \) must not. Conversely, if no such containment exists, define
\( \mathcal F=\{B\subseteq N:\text{ some }P\in\mathcal P\text{ satisfies }P\subseteq B\} \).
This family is an upset, contains every required-positive coalition, and contains no required-negative coalition. The construction also permits \( \mathcal P \) to be empty. This proves the lemma.

There are at most \( \tau \) coalitions in each family, so the condition is checked in \( O(m\tau^2) \) time.

Discretizing and compressing the frontiers

For criterion \( i \), let its distinct observed values be
\( v_{i,1}<\cdots<v_{i,r_i} \), where \( r_i\leq\tau \).
Every real threshold has the same comparisons with all observed values as one member of
\( B_i=\{v_{i,1},\ldots,v_{i,r_i},v_{i,r_i}+1\} \).

Indeed, replace a threshold by the smallest observed value at least as large as it, if one exists, and otherwise by \( v_{i,r_i}+1 \). No observed value changes approval status. This replacement is nondecreasing as a function of the threshold, so it also preserves frontier order. All candidate values have polynomial bit length.

Only the frontier indices in
\( H=\{k-1:(x,k)\text{ is observed},\ k>1\}\cup\{k:(x,k)\text{ is observed},\ k<p\} \)
are needed for the preceding membership conditions. There are at most \( 2\tau \) such indices.

A certificate specifies, for each \( h\in H \) and each criterion \( i \), a threshold from \( B_i \). The verifier checks that these profiles are ordered and then checks the lemma’s containment condition.

Ordered profiles at the selected indices always extend to all frontier indices: before the first selected index, use its profile; afterward, use the profile at the largest selected index not exceeding the current index. This extension is nondecreasing and preserves every selected profile. The lemma supplies an upset, and the initial-segment argument then guarantees every requested label.

Conversely, any consistent model yields such a certificate by discretizing its frontiers and retaining only indices in \( H \).

The certificate uses \( O(m\tau\log(\tau+1)) \) bits for candidate indices, and verification is polynomial in the input length. No exponential representation of \( \mathcal F \), or enumeration of all \( p-1 \) frontiers, is required.

Conclusion

The reduction proves \( \mathrm{NP}\text{-hardness} \) under all the stated numerical, mass, and unique-profile requirements, already with \( p=2 \). The certificate argument proves membership in \( \mathrm{NP} \). Therefore the target problem is \( \mathrm{NP}\text{-complete} \).
