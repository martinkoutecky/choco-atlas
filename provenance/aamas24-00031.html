The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite worker types \(P\) and firm types \(Q\) with rational masses \(\alpha_p\) and \(\beta_q\), strict rankings of partner types with ties among individuals of the same type, a complete scenario \(I_A\) with \(E_A=P\times Q\), and a restricted scenario \(I_B\) with \(E_B\subseteq P\times Q\) and unchanged rankings on common types, does there exist a deterministic measurable measure-preserving partial matching \(M\) of the atomless type intervals, supported on \(E_B\), such that for every feasible mass matching \(N\) in each scenario \(s\in\{A,B\}\), the total worker-and-firm mass preferring its assignment in \(M\) to its assignment in \(N\), \(\Delta_s(M,N)\), satisfies \(\Delta_s(M,N)\ge 0\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions. Let \(E\subseteq\Theta_W imes\Theta_F\) be a compatibility graph. Each type class is an atomless set of agents of the stated rational mass; agents of a type share a strict ranking of adjacent types and are indifferent among agents of the same type. Every acceptable partner is preferred to being unmatched.

A feasible matching is a measurable partial measure-preserving bijection between workers and firms whose matched type pairs lie in \(E\); thus every individual receives one individual partner or is unmatched. For profiles \(I_A,I_B\), define \(\operatorname{vote}_I(a;M,N)\in\{-1,0,1\}\) by \(a\)'s preference between its partners in \(M\) and \(N\), and let \(\Delta_I(M,N)=\int\operatorname{vote}_I(a;M,N)\,d\lambda(a)\).

The input consists of \(\Theta_W,\Theta_F,E\), rational masses \(\mu^W,\mu^F\), and \(I_A,I_B\), where \(I_B\) is obtained from \(I_A\) by downshifting one adjacent type for each of at most four positive-mass types, preserving all other comparisons.

Target. Is deciding whether there exists a feasible measurable matching \(M\) with \(\Delta_I(M,N)\ge 0\) for every feasible \(N\) and each \(I\in\{I_A,I_B\}\) solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results. The finite-agent version is NP-complete even when the two profiles differ only by a downshift performed by four agents.

Complexity classification

The atomless rational-mass problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard, even when every type has mass \(1\) and exactly four types perform one downshift. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).

The proof requires care because atomlessness permits a type to split among several partner types. The key observation is that any such fractional matching decomposes into finite matching “cohorts,” and an unpopular cohort can be challenged independently on a positive-measure subset.

Source problem

Use the following known restricted version of finite RobustPopularMatching:

  • The input is a finite bipartite compatibility graph \(G=(W\cup F,E)\) with two strict preference profiles \(I_A,I_B\).
  • Profile \(I_B\) is obtained from \(I_A\) by one downshift performed by each of four agents.
  • The question is whether some finite matching is popular under both profiles.

This problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete by [Bullinger, Gangam, and Shahkar, Theorem 4.9](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p225.pdf).

Construction of the atomless instance

For every \(w\in W\), create one worker type \(\theta_w\) of mass \(1\). For every \(f\in F\), create one firm type \(\theta_f\) of mass \(1\). Realize every type class as a copy of the Lebesgue interval \([0,1)\).

Put \((\theta_w,\theta_f)\) in the type compatibility graph exactly when \(wf\in E\). Type \(\theta_v\) ranks adjacent partner types in exactly the order in which finite agent \(v\) ranks the corresponding finite agents.

Thus the four finite agents whose preferences change become exactly four positive-mass types whose preferences change. Each original downshift remains a downshift of the corresponding adjacent type.

The construction has polynomial size and uses only the rational mass \(1\).

The bipartite matching polytope

For a measurable matching \(M\), define its type-pair masses by

\[ x_{wf}=\lambda\bigl(\{a\in\theta_w:M(a)\in\theta_f\}\bigr). \]

Measure preservation gives

\[ x_{wf}\geq 0,\qquad \sum_{f:wf\in E}x_{wf}\leq 1,\qquad \sum_{w:wf\in E}x_{wf}\leq 1. \]

Hence \(x\) lies in the fractional bipartite matching polytope

\[ \mathcal P(G)= \left\{ x\in\mathbb R_{\geq 0}^{E}: \sum_{e\ni v}x_e\leq 1 \text{ for every }v\in W\cup F \right\}. \]

The incidence matrix of a bipartite graph is totally unimodular. Since the right-hand side is integral, every vertex of \(\mathcal P(G)\) is the incidence vector of a finite matching. Therefore every \(x\in\mathcal P(G)\) has a decomposition

\[ x=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_k\chi^{P_k}, \qquad \lambda_k>0,\qquad \sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_k=1, \]

where each \(P_k\) is a finite matching of \(G\).

Lemma 1: finite popularity implies atomless popularity

Let \(P\) be a finite matching popular under a profile \(I\). Construct the block matching \(M_P\) by matching the whole type \(\theta_w\) bijectively to the whole type \(\theta_f\) whenever \(wf\in P\), and leaving the whole type unmatched whenever the corresponding finite vertex is unmatched.

Consider any measurable challenger \(N\), and let \(y_{wf}\) be its type-pair masses. Then \(y\in\mathcal P(G)\), so write

\[ y=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\alpha_j\chi^{Q_j} \]

for finite matchings \(Q_j\), where \(\alpha_j\geq 0\) and \(\sum_j\alpha_j=1\).

Because every agent of type \(\theta_v\) has the same partner type under \(M_P\), the vote of such an agent against \(N\) depends only on its partner type under \(N\). Consequently the popularity margin is linear in \(y\), including the unmatched mass. Explicitly, the unmatched mass of type \(v\) is

\[ 1-\sum_{e\ni v}y_e = \sum_j\alpha_j\mathbf 1_{\{v\text{ is unmatched in }Q_j\}}. \]

It follows that

\[ \Delta_I(M_P,N) = \sum_j\alpha_j\Delta_I(P,Q_j). \]

Since \(P\) is popular, every term \(\Delta_I(P,Q_j)\) is nonnegative. Hence \(\Delta_I(M_P,N)\geq 0\). As \(N\) was arbitrary, \(M_P\) is popular in the atomless instance.

Applying this argument to both profiles shows that every finite robust popular matching induces an atomless robust popular matching.

Lemma 2: atomless popularity yields a finite popular cohort

Let \(M\) be any measurable matching popular under profile \(I\), and decompose its type-pair matrix as

\[ x=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_k\chi^{P_k}. \]

We claim that every \(P_k\) with \(\lambda_k>0\) is a finite popular matching under \(I\).

For each edge \(wf\), the \(M\)-matched subset of \(\theta_w\) assigned to \(\theta_f\) has mass

\[ x_{wf}=\sum_{k:wf\in P_k}\lambda_k. \]

By atomlessness, partition this subset into measurable pieces of mass \(\lambda_k\), one for each \(k\) satisfying \(wf\in P_k\), and partition its image under \(M\) correspondingly. Likewise, the \(M\)-unmatched subset of type \(v\) has mass

\[ 1-\sum_{e\ni v}x_e = \sum_{k:v\text{ unmatched in }P_k}\lambda_k, \]

so it can be partitioned into pieces of mass \(\lambda_k\).

This produces, for every \(k\), a cohort \(C_v^k\subseteq\theta_v\) of mass \(\lambda_k\) such that:

  • if \(vf\in P_k\), then \(M\) matches \(C_v^k\) to \(C_f^k\);
  • if \(v\) is unmatched in \(P_k\), then every agent in \(C_v^k\) is unmatched under \(M\).

Suppose \(P_k\) were not popular. Then there would be a finite matching \(Q\) satisfying

\[ \Delta_I(P_k,Q)<0. \]

Define a measurable challenger \(N\) as follows:

  • on every cohort other than the \(k\)-th cohort, retain \(M\);
  • on the \(k\)-th cohort, implement \(Q\), matching the equal-mass sets \(C_w^k\) and \(C_f^k\) whenever \(wf\in Q\).

Such measure-preserving bijections exist because these are atomless Lebesgue sets of equal mass. Only agents in the \(k\)-th cohort change partners. Their old and new partner types are exactly those in \(P_k\) and \(Q\), respectively. Therefore

\[ \Delta_I(M,N)=\lambda_k\Delta_I(P_k,Q)<0, \]

contradicting the popularity of \(M\). Thus every \(P_k\) is popular.

If \(M\) is popular under both \(I_A\) and \(I_B\), the same argument applies separately to each profile. Hence every \(P_k\) occurring with positive coefficient is popular under both profiles.

Correctness of the reduction

The constructed atomless instance has a robust popular measurable matching if and only if the source finite instance has a robust popular matching:

  • The forward implication from the finite instance to the atomless instance follows from Lemma \(1\).
  • The reverse implication follows from Lemma \(2\) by choosing any finite matching \(P_k\) with \(\lambda_k>0\).

Thus the construction is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from the restricted finite RobustPopularMatching problem.

Conclusion

The target problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard even under the additional restrictions that all type masses equal \(1\) and only four types change their preferences. Atomless divisibility does not remove the finite hardness: every fractional candidate contains positive-measure finite matching cohorts, and any unpopular cohort can be defeated independently.

This argument proves hardness but does not establish that the general rational-mass problem belongs to \(\mathsf{NP}\).

Step-by-step audit

1. Source problem

The cited [Theorem 4.9](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p225.pdf) does state that RobustPopularMatching is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete when the second profile differs by downshifts performed by four agents. The paper’s definition of a downshift preserves all comparisons not involving the shifted alternative, matching the restriction required by the target.

Thus the source problem supplies the required \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard family.

2. Construction

Replacing each finite worker or firm by a unit-mass type preserves:

  • the bipartite compatibility graph;
  • every strict preference comparison;
  • the convention that every acceptable partner is preferred to being unmatched;
  • the four downshifts; and
  • polynomial input size.

Because every type has mass \(1\), finite vote counts correspond exactly to continuum vote masses in block matchings.

3. Fractional matching representation

For any measurable matching, the type-pair masses satisfy nonnegativity and the degree constraints of the bipartite fractional matching polytope. The column constraints follow from measure preservation.

The invocation of total unimodularity is correct: the vertex-edge incidence matrix of a bipartite graph is totally unimodular, and the integral right-hand side implies that all vertices of the polytope are integral. Integral feasible points are precisely incidence vectors of finite matchings, including partial matchings.

Consequently every type-pair matrix has a finite convex decomposition into finite matching incidence vectors.

4. Lemma 1

When \(M_P\) is a block matching, every member of a type receives the same partner type. Its vote against a challenger therefore depends linearly on the challenger’s partner-type masses.

The prover also accounts correctly for unmatched mass. Hence the identity

\[ \Delta_I(M_P,N)=\sum_j\alpha_j\Delta_I(P,Q_j) \]

is valid. Popularity of \(P\) makes every summand nonnegative, proving popularity of \(M_P\). Applying the same calculation under both profiles proves the robust version.

5. Lemma 2

The cohort construction is consistent:

  • every edge-specific matched set has precisely the total mass assigned to matchings containing that edge;
  • unmatched mass has precisely the total coefficient of matchings leaving the vertex unmatched;
  • atomlessness permits the required measurable partitions;
  • corresponding images under \(M\) ensure that each cohort is closed under the original matching; and
  • every type contributes mass \(\lambda_k\) to cohort \(k\).

If \(P_k\) loses to a finite matching \(Q\), implementing \(Q\) solely inside cohort \(k\) produces a feasible measurable challenger. Agents outside the cohort retain their partners and cast zero votes, while each finite vote inside the cohort is scaled by \(\lambda_k\). Therefore

\[ \Delta_I(M,N)=\lambda_k\Delta_I(P_k,Q)<0, \]

contradicting popularity.

The decomposition and cohorts depend only on \(M\), not on the profile. Thus applying the argument to \(I_A\) and \(I_B\) shows that every positive-weight \(P_k\) is popular under both profiles.

6. Reduction equivalence and complexity conclusion

Both implications are established:

  • a finite robust popular matching induces a measurable robust popular block matching;
  • a measurable robust popular matching yields a positive-weight finite matching popular under both profiles.

The construction is polynomial and remains within the target’s restricted instances. Therefore the claimed \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness follows.

The prover appropriately does not claim membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\), which is unnecessary for answering the stated polynomial-time-versus-hardness question.
