The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a current parameter \(r\), a rational bound \(0 \leq \sigma < 1/3\), and a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over rational ideal points, choose an update \(y\) maximizing \(|y-r|\). Require \(y\) to lie between \(r\) and the reality-aware weighted median \(q_r(\eta)\) for every honest society \(\eta=\mu-\nu\), where the hidden sybil subdistribution satisfies \(0 \leq \nu \leq \mu\) and \(\|\nu\|_1 \leq \sigma\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Input consists of \(r\in\mathbb Q\), a rational bound \(0\leq\sigma<1/3\), and a society \(\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\delta_{x_i}\), with distinct rational ideal points \(x_i\), positive binary-rational masses \(w_i\), and \(\sum_iw_i=1\). Preferences are single-peaked on \(\mathbb R\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, the parameter dimension is \(m=1\), and \(L\) is the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
For any nonzero finite-support measure \(\eta\), let \(M(\eta)\) be its closed interval of weighted medians, computed relative to its own total mass. Define the reality-aware weighted median \(q_r(\eta)\) as the unique point of \(M(\eta)\) closest to \(r\).
A hidden sybil subdistribution is \(\nu=\sum_i s_i\delta_{x_i}\), where \(s_i\in\mathbb R\), \(0\leq s_i\leq w_i\), and \(\sum_i s_i\leq\sigma\); its honest society is \(\eta=\mu-\nu\).
An update \(y\in\mathbb R\) is safe if \(y\in[\min\{r,q_r(\eta)\},\max\{r,q_r(\eta)\}]\) for every such \(\nu\). The optimization problem chooses a safe update maximizing \(|y-r|\); its threshold decision version additionally receives a binary-rational \(d\geq0\) and asks whether a safe update satisfies \(|y-r|\geq d\).

Is an optimal safe update computable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is the threshold decision problem \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite electorates, outer-\(\sigma\) suppression is sybil-safe and sybil-live below \(1/3\) penetration; least conservatism is also claimed. [Verified discrete result](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0081.pdf)
A later full treatment establishes safety–liveness bounds for median-based rules with partial participation. [Verified related result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.05271) No result settling this exact rational-support optimization problem was located.

  • Defined the reality-aware weighted median by selecting the point of the weighted-median interval closest to r, extending the discrete status-quo tie convention.
  • Computed honest weighted medians relative to the remaining honest mass and allowed arbitrary real fractional removals within each type.
  • Used the full closed interval between r and the honest median as the safe update domain, following the stated continuous question and the intended directional safety interpretation rather than the discrete base rule's literal restriction to reported votes.
  • Introduced a rational distance threshold to give the optimization problem a precise associated decision problem; set the parameter dimension m to one.

Result

An optimal safe update is computable in polynomial time. For the specified dimension \(m=1\), an exact algorithm uses \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2)\) bit operations. The threshold decision problem is therefore in \(\mathrm P\).

The key is that two explicit admissible deletions attain the smallest and largest possible reality-aware medians.

1. Weighted medians and cumulative order

For a nonzero finite-support measure \(\eta\) of total mass \(H\), define its normalized cumulative distribution by

\[ F_\eta(z)=\frac{\eta((-\infty,z])}{H}. \]

Write its median interval as \(M(\eta)=[\ell_\eta,u_\eta]\). Its endpoints satisfy

\[ \ell_\eta=\inf\{z:F_\eta(z)\geq\tfrac12\}, \qquad u_\eta=\inf\{z:F_\eta(z)>\tfrac12\}. \]

To justify these formulas, list the positive-mass support points in increasing order. Let \(\ell_\eta\) be the first point where cumulative mass reaches half the total, and \(u_\eta\) the first point where it strictly exceeds half. Every point between them has mass at most \(H/2\) strictly on either side, so is a weighted median. Below \(\ell_\eta\), the mass strictly to the right exceeds \(H/2\); above \(u_\eta\), the mass strictly to the left exceeds \(H/2\). Thus the median set is exactly this closed interval, including the possible gap between two support points.

Projection onto that interval gives

\[ q_r(\eta)=\max\{\ell_\eta,\min\{r,u_\eta\}\}. \]

Consequently, if two measures satisfy \(F_{\eta_1}(z)\geq F_{\eta_2}(z)\) for every real \(z\), then

\[ \ell_{\eta_1}\leq\ell_{\eta_2}, \qquad u_{\eta_1}\leq u_{\eta_2}, \qquad q_r(\eta_1)\leq q_r(\eta_2). \]

Indeed, the sets defining each infimum for \(\eta_2\) are contained in the corresponding sets for \(\eta_1\). The projection formula is nondecreasing in both endpoints.

2. Two deletions attain the extreme medians

Sort the original support so that \(x_1<\cdots<x_\tau\). Put \(T=1-\sigma>0\), and define two honest measures:

  • \(\eta_{\mathrm{left}}\): delete exactly mass \(\sigma\), starting at the largest support point and proceeding leftward.
  • \(\eta_{\mathrm{right}}\): delete exactly mass \(\sigma\), starting at the smallest support point and proceeding rightward.

Fractional deletion from the final affected atom is allowed by the hypotheses. Both measures are therefore admissible, and both have total mass \(T\).

Let \(C(z)=\mu((-\infty,z])\). Their cumulative masses are

\[ \eta_{\mathrm{left}}((-\infty,z])=\min\{C(z),T\}, \qquad \eta_{\mathrm{right}}((-\infty,z])=\max\{C(z)-\sigma,0\}. \]

Now take any admissible deletion \(\nu\). Write \(t=\nu(\mathbb R)\leq\sigma\), \(\eta=\mu-\nu\), and \(H=1-t\geq T\). If \(A(z)=\eta((-\infty,z])\), then

\[ \max\{C(z)-t,0\}\leq A(z)\leq\min\{C(z),H\}. \]

We claim that, for every real \(z\),

\[ F_{\eta_{\mathrm{left}}}(z)\geq F_\eta(z) \geq F_{\eta_{\mathrm{right}}}(z). \]

For the first inequality, if \(C(z)\geq T\), the leftmost distribution function equals \(1\). Otherwise,

\[ F_\eta(z)=\frac{A(z)}H \leq\frac{C(z)}H \leq\frac{C(z)}T =F_{\eta_{\mathrm{left}}}(z). \]

For the second inequality, if \(C(z)\leq\sigma\), the rightmost distribution function equals \(0\). Otherwise \(C(z)>\sigma\geq t\), and

\[ F_\eta(z)\geq\frac{C(z)-t}{1-t} \geq\frac{C(z)-\sigma}{1-\sigma} =F_{\eta_{\mathrm{right}}}(z). \]

The middle inequality follows from the explicit identity

\[ \frac{C(z)-t}{1-t}-\frac{C(z)-\sigma}{1-\sigma} = \frac{(\sigma-t)(1-C(z))}{(1-t)(1-\sigma)} \geq0. \]

Applying Section 1 proves

\[ q_r(\eta_{\mathrm{left}}) \leq q_r(\mu-\nu) \leq q_r(\eta_{\mathrm{right}}) \]

for every admissible \(\nu\). Since both bounding measures are admissible, the bounds are attained.

Define \(q_-=q_r(\eta_{\mathrm{left}})\) and \(q_+=q_r(\eta_{\mathrm{right}})\). Thus \(q_-\leq q_+\) are exactly the minimum and maximum possible reality-aware honest medians.

3. The complete safe set and its optimum

For any possible honest median \(q\), the permitted interval is

\[ I(q)=[\min\{r,q\},\max\{r,q\}]. \]

Both endpoint functions are nondecreasing in \(q\). Because \(q_-\) and \(q_+\) bound all possible medians and are themselves attained, the intersection of all permitted intervals is exactly

\[ S=[\min\{r,q_+\},\max\{r,q_-\}]. \]

For completeness, every permitted interval has lower endpoint at most \(\min\{r,q_+\}\) and upper endpoint at least \(\max\{r,q_-\}\), so it contains the displayed interval. Conversely, the intervals corresponding to the attained medians \(q_+\) and \(q_-\) impose those two endpoint bounds.

It follows that an optimal safe update is

\[ y^*= \begin{cases} q_-,&q_->r,\\ q_+,&q_+<r,\\ r,&q_-\leq r\leq q_+. \end{cases} \]

The first two cases cannot occur simultaneously. In each, the safe set extends from \(r\) in only one direction, and its far endpoint maximizes distance. In the third case, the safe set is the singleton \(\{r\}\).

4. Exact algorithm and bit complexity

Let \(C_j=\sum_{i=1}^j w_i\). The median endpoints of the two trimmed measures can be found directly from these original prefix sums:

\[ \ell_{\mathrm{left}} =x_{\min\{j:C_j\geq(1-\sigma)/2\}}, \qquad u_{\mathrm{left}} =x_{\min\{j:C_j>(1-\sigma)/2\}}, \]

\[ \ell_{\mathrm{right}} =x_{\min\{j:C_j\geq(1+\sigma)/2\}}, \qquad u_{\mathrm{right}} =x_{\min\{j:C_j>(1+\sigma)/2\}}. \]

These formulas follow by substituting the trimmed cumulative masses from Section 2 into the half-mass conditions. Both thresholds lie strictly between \(0\) and \(1\), so every required index exists.

The algorithm therefore consists of sorting the support, scanning the prefix sums for these four indices, projecting \(r\) onto the resulting two median intervals, and applying the formula for \(y^*\). The distinction between strict and nonstrict inequalities handles all median ties, including \(\sigma=0\).

Here is an explicit bit bound. Take a common denominator \(D\) equal to the product of the input denominators of the masses and \(\sigma\). Its bit length is \(O(L)\), because the sum of those denominators’ encoded lengths is at most \(L\). Write \(w_i=W_i/D\) and \(\sigma=B/D\). Then all prefix numerators have \(O(L)\) bits, and the four comparisons become integer comparisons of \(2\sum_{i=1}^jW_i\) with \(D-B\) or \(D+B\).

Sorting uses \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) exact rational comparisons. Each takes \(O(L^2)\) bit operations using ordinary integer multiplication. Common-denominator conversion and the scan take \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations. The remaining projections and comparisons use \(O(L)\)-bit rational operands. Thus the total bound is

\[ O(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2). \]

The output \(y^*\) is either \(r\) or an original support point, so its encoding also has polynomial size. For the threshold problem, compute \(y^*\) and test \(|y^*-r|\geq d\) exactly, taking \(L\) to include the encoding of \(d\).

Hence both problems are polynomial-time solvable on the specified domain \(m=1\). The proof actually uses only \(\sigma<1\); the stronger promised bound \(\sigma<1/3\) is unnecessary for this optimization.
