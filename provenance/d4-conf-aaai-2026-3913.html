The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite candidate-answer set \(\mathcal C\), question bank \(\mathcal O\), and finite worker-type set \(\mathcal T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), where each type specifies its response behavior, form the NO2 likelihood and pairwise question-value objective by weighting each type’s response-history contributions by \(\mu_t\). Under what conditions does the resulting gradient-based bilevel optimization retain Theorem 1’s convergence guarantee for the maximizing beliefs and question values?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge2\) questions and \(K\ge1\) independent binary candidate variables have a given bipartite support graph, with every question having a nonempty parent set. A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type supplies a deterministic binary response vector and an observed partial history \(\Gamma_t\), leaving at least one question unanswered. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state any running times in \(m,K,\tau,L\) and the additional parameters.
The belief vector \(\theta\) consists of independent candidate probabilities \(p_k\) and edge failure probabilities \(q_{ik}\), all in \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), for a given rational \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\); put \(q_{ik}=1\) on nonedges. Conditional on candidate configuration \(c\in\{0,1\}^K\), questions are independent and \(P_\theta(O_i=0\mid c)=\prod_{k:c_k=1}q_{ik}\). Write \(P_\theta(\Gamma)\) for the resulting marginal history probability; candidate probabilities need not sum to one.
Set \(Y^t_{ij}=1\) exactly when the observed history of type \(t\) contains a positive response to \(i\) and a negative response to \(j\), and otherwise set it to zero. Define \(\beta_i=\sum_k(1-q_{ik})p_k\), \(D_{ij}=(\beta_i+\beta_j)(s_i-s_j)\), and, for a given rational \(\alpha>0\), \(G_\mu(S,\theta)=\sum_t\mu_t\sum_{i\ne j}Y^t_{ij}\bigl(D_{ij}-\log\sum_{a\ne b}e^{D_{ab}}\bigr)-\alpha\|S\|_2^2/2\), with \(S\in\mathbb R^m\).
Let \(S^*(\theta)\in\operatorname{argmax}_S G_\mu(S,\theta)\). Define \(S^{(0)}_i=1/m\) and \(S^{(r+1)}=S^{(r)}+\eta_r\nabla_S G_\mu(S^{(r)},\theta)\), where \(\eta_r>0\). For each type, append its response to the unanswered question with largest score in \(S\), breaking ties by a fixed question order, to obtain \(\Gamma_t(S)\).
Define \(F(\theta)=\sum_t\mu_t\log P_\theta(\Gamma_t(S^*(\theta)))\) and \(F_r(\theta)=\sum_t\mu_t\log P_\theta(\Gamma_t(S^{(r)}(\theta)))\).
Under what conditions on the instance and step sizes do the inner iterates converge uniformly to maximizing question values, while \(\max_\theta F_r(\theta)\to\max_\theta F(\theta)\) and every sequence \(\theta_r\in\operatorname{argmax}_\theta F_r(\theta)\) has a convergent subsequence whose every accumulation point maximizes \(F\)?
For finite workers, a conditional convergence result asserts convergence of approximate optimal values and accumulation points of maximizers under uniform inner convergence and a Lipschitz outer objective; it does not establish global convergence of outer gradient ascent. [Source](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38820).
A general bilevel approximation theorem establishes analogous conclusions under compactness, joint continuity, unique bounded inner solutions, uniform Lipschitz dependence on inner variables, and uniform inner convergence; it does not specifically analyze history selection by deterministic question ranking. [Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/franceschi18a/franceschi18a.pdf).

1. Conditions that suffice

The inner problem has a unique maximizer for every admissible belief vector, and suitable gradient steps converge to it uniformly. The additional issue is deterministic ranking: convergence of scores does not, by itself, imply convergence of the selected-history likelihoods.

Here are two sufficient conditions for the outer conclusions:

  • Unique highest unanswered score: for every type and every admissible belief vector, exactly one unanswered question has the highest optimal score. Compactness then supplies a uniform positive gap, and eventually \(F_r=F\) everywhere.
  • Equal likelihoods at exact top ties: whenever several unanswered questions tie for the highest optimal score, appending any of them gives the same history probability. This implies uniform convergence \(F_r\to F\). To write the approximate optimal values as maxima, rather than suprema, one must additionally ensure that the \(F_r\) attain their maxima. An explicit sufficient condition for attainment is given below.

These are sufficient conditions, not a characterization of all instances for which the conclusions happen to hold.

2. Uniform convergence of the inner problem

Let \(E\) be the number of support edges, and let

\( \Theta=[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]^{K+E}. \)

Define the aggregated comparison weights and their total by

\( a_{ij}=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tY^t_{ij},\qquad A=\sum_{i\ne j}a_{ij}. \)

For each ordered pair, put

\( v_{ij}(\theta)=(\beta_i+\beta_j)(e_i-e_j),\qquad h(\theta)=\sum_{i\ne j}a_{ij}v_{ij}(\theta). \)

Then the inner objective is

\( G_\mu(S,\theta)=h(\theta)^\top S-A\log Z(S,\theta)-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|S\|_2^2,\qquad Z(S,\theta)=\sum_{i\ne j}\exp(v_{ij}(\theta)^\top S). \)

If a type has \(u\) observed positive answers and \(v\) observed negative answers, it contributes \(uv\) comparisons. Since at least one question is unanswered, \(u+v\le m-1\). Consequently,

\( 0\le A\le \left\lfloor\frac{(m-1)^2}{4}\right\rfloor. \)

Set

\( B=2K(1-\varepsilon)^2,\qquad V=\sqrt{2}\,B,\qquad M=\alpha+AV^2. \)

Indeed, each summand in \(\beta_i\) is at most \((1-\varepsilon)^2\), so \(\beta_i+\beta_j\le B\) and \(\|v_{ij}\|_2\le V\).

Proposition 1: uniqueness, boundedness, and continuity

For every \(\theta\in\Theta\), the maximizer \(S^*(\theta)\) exists and is unique. Moreover,

\( \|S^*(\theta)\|_2\le \frac{AV}{\alpha}, \)

and \(\theta\mapsto S^*(\theta)\) is continuous.

Proof. Every comparison contribution is a log softmax probability and therefore is nonpositive. Thus

\( G_\mu(S,\theta)\le-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|S\|_2^2. \)

Continuity and this coercive upper bound imply existence of a maximizer.

Let \(\pi_{ij}=\exp(v_{ij}^\top S)/Z\). Differentiation gives

\( -\nabla_S^2G_\mu(S,\theta)=\alpha I+A\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi}(v_{ij}). \)

For any vector \(x\),

\( 0\le x^\top\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi}(v_{ij})x\le \mathbb E_\pi[(x^\top v_{ij})^2]\le V^2\|x\|_2^2. \)

Hence, uniformly in \(S,\theta\),

\( \alpha I\preceq-\nabla_S^2G_\mu(S,\theta)\preceq MI. \)

In particular, \(G_\mu\) is \(\alpha\)-strongly concave, proving uniqueness.

At \(S=0\), the softmax weights are uniform. Since \(v_{ji}=-v_{ij}\), their mean is zero, and \(\nabla_SG_\mu(0,\theta)=h(\theta)\). Strong concavity and stationarity at \(S^*\) imply

\( \alpha\|S^*\|_2^2\le h(\theta)^\top S^*\le AV\|S^*\|_2, \)

which proves the bound.

Finally, suppose \(\theta_n\to\theta\). Boundedness gives convergent subsequences of \(S^*(\theta_n)\). If such a subsequence converges to \(\bar S\), the maximizing inequalities, together with joint continuity of \(G_\mu\), give

\( G_\mu(\bar S,\theta)\ge G_\mu(S,\theta)\quad\text{for every }S. \)

Uniqueness implies \(\bar S=S^*(\theta)\). All subsequential limits are therefore the same, proving continuity. \(\square\)

Proposition 2: explicit step-size conditions

Assume the prescribed step sizes satisfy

\( 0<\eta_r\le\frac1M,\qquad \sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\eta_r=\infty. \)

Then the iterates converge uniformly:

\( e_r:=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|S^{(r)}(\theta)-S^*(\theta)\|_2\longrightarrow0. \)

More precisely, with

\( R=\frac1{\sqrt m}+\frac{AV}{\alpha}, \)

one has

\( e_r\le R\prod_{\ell=0}^{r-1}(1-\alpha\eta_\ell)\le R\exp\!\left(-\alpha\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-1}\eta_\ell\right). \)

Proof. Write \(f_\theta=-G_\mu(\cdot,\theta)\). For any \(S,T\), integration of the Hessian along the segment between them gives

\( \nabla f_\theta(S)-\nabla f_\theta(T)=H(S-T),\qquad \alpha I\preceq H\preceq MI. \)

For \(0<\eta\le1/M\), every eigenvalue of \(I-\eta H\) belongs to \([0,1-\alpha\eta]\). Thus the gradient update is a contraction with factor \(1-\alpha\eta\). Applying this with \(T=S^*(\theta)\), and using the uniform initial-distance bound \(R\), proves the product estimate. The exponential estimate follows from \(1-x\le e^{-x}\); the divergent sum implies convergence. \(\square\)

For example, \(\eta_r=1/M\) gives

\( e_r\le R e^{-\alpha r/M}. \)

No summability condition on \(\sum_r\eta_r^2\) is needed for these deterministic, exact-gradient updates.

Positive step sizes alone do not suffice. If \(A=0\), the update is \(S^{(r+1)}=(1-\alpha\eta_r)S^{(r)}\); the constant choice \(\eta_r=2/\alpha\) makes the nonzero initialization oscillate.

Uniform convergence of selected question values

Let \(U_t\) be the unanswered questions of type \(t\), and let \(j_{t,r}(\theta)\) be the question selected using \(S^{(r)}(\theta)\). Without any gap or tie assumption,

\( 0\le\max_{j\in U_t}S_j^*(\theta)-S_{j_{t,r}(\theta)}^*(\theta)\le2e_r. \)

To verify this, let \(i\) maximize \(S^*\) on \(U_t\). Since \(j_{t,r}\) maximizes \(S^{(r)}\),

\( S_i^*-S_{j_{t,r}}^*\le(S_i^*-S_i^{(r)})+(S_{j_{t,r}}^{(r)}-S_{j_{t,r}}^*)\le2e_r. \)

Thus selected questions become uniformly optimal in score value, even when their identities do not stabilize.

3. Fixed-history likelihoods are well behaved

For \(j\in U_t\), define

\( \Gamma_{t,j}=\Gamma_t\text{ with type }t\text{’s response to }j\text{ appended},\qquad \ell_{t,j}(\theta)=\log P_\theta(\Gamma_{t,j}). \)

These are fixed-history functions; no ranking is involved in their definition.

For every partial binary history \(\Gamma\),

\( P_\theta(\Gamma)\ge\varepsilon^{K+E+m}>0. \)

Indeed, consider only the configuration in which all candidates are active. Its probability is at least \(\varepsilon^K\). Conditional on that configuration, a negative response to question \(i\) has probability at least \(\varepsilon^{d_i}\), where \(d_i\) is its degree. A positive response has probability at least \(\varepsilon\), because its parent set is nonempty. Conditional independence supplies the stated bound.

Consequently, each \(\ell_{t,j}\) is continuous and smooth on the parameter box.

One also has the useful uniform bound

\( |\ell_{t,j}(\theta)-\ell_{t,j}(\theta')|\le\frac1\varepsilon\|\theta-\theta'\|_1. \)

To prove it, represent the model by independent candidate Bernoulli variables and independent edge-failure Bernoulli variables. A question is positive precisely when some active parent has a nonfailed edge. This representation has the required conditional distribution.

For any parameter coordinate \(x\), differentiation of an event probability gives

\( \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\log P_\theta(\Gamma)=\mathbb E_\theta\!\left[\frac{B_x}{x}-\frac{1-B_x}{1-x}\,\middle|\,\Gamma\right], \)

where \(B_x\) is the corresponding Bernoulli variable. The integrand has absolute value at most \(1/\varepsilon\). Integrating these coordinate bounds along the segment joining \(\theta\) and \(\theta'\) proves the inequality.

This regularity applies to each fixed-history branch. Ranking can still make their selected combination discontinuous.

4. A uniform score gap gives eventual exact agreement

Assume:

Gap condition. For every \(\theta\in\Theta\) and every type \(t\) with \(|U_t|\ge2\), the highest score in \(S^*(\theta)\) on \(U_t\) is unique.

For each such type, the difference between its largest and second-largest score is a positive continuous function of \(\theta\). Continuity follows because order statistics of finitely many continuous functions are continuous. Compactness of \(\Theta\), and finiteness of the support, therefore give a number \(\Delta>0\) such that

\( S^*_{j_t^*(\theta)}(\theta)-S_j^*(\theta)\ge\Delta \)

for every type with multiple unanswered questions and every competing \(j\in U_t\).

Whenever \(2e_r<\Delta\), every approximate selection equals its exact selection. Hence there is \(r_0\) such that

\( F_r(\theta)=F(\theta)\quad\text{for every }\theta\in\Theta,\ r\ge r_0. \)

The unique selection is locally constant in \(\theta\), so \(F\) is continuous. It therefore attains a maximum on \(\Theta\). For all \(r\ge r_0\),

\( \max_{\theta\in\Theta}F_r(\theta)=\max_{\theta\in\Theta}F(\theta),\qquad \operatorname{argmax}F_r=\operatorname{argmax}F. \)

Thus any sequence of approximate maximizers has a convergent subsequence, and every accumulation point maximizes \(F\): compactness gives subsequences, and continuity makes \(\operatorname{argmax}F\) closed.

For \(\eta_r=1/M\), it suffices to take

\( r_0=\max\!\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac M\alpha\log\!\left(\max\{1,4R/\Delta\}\right)\right\rceil\right\}. \)

This ensures \(e_r\le\Delta/4\).

If every type has only one unanswered question, selection is independent of scores and \(F_r=F\) for every \(r\). Otherwise, the gap condition is strong: because \(\Theta\) is connected, each type’s unique winning question must remain the same throughout the box.

The argument guarantees attainment for all sufficiently large \(r\). If maximizers are required at every earlier index as well, attainment at those finitely many indices must also be assumed or verified.

5. A more flexible theorem allowing ties and changing selections

Define the exact top set

\( T_t(\theta)=\operatorname{argmax}_{j\in U_t}S_j^*(\theta). \)

Assume:

Exact-tie compatibility. For every \(\theta,t\) and every \(i,j\in T_t(\theta)\),

\( \ell_{t,i}(\theta)=\ell_{t,j}(\theta). \)

This condition allows the selected question to change with \(\theta\).

Proposition 3: continuity and uniform outer approximation

Under exact-tie compatibility, \(F\) is continuous and

\( \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|F_r(\theta)-F(\theta)|\longrightarrow0. \)

Proof of continuity. Suppose \(\theta_n\to\theta\). Along any subsequence, finiteness of the support and question sets permits a further subsequence on which all selected question indices are constant, say \(j_t\). Continuity of \(S^*\) implies \(j_t\in T_t(\theta)\). Exact-tie compatibility and continuity of the likelihood branches then give

\( F(\theta_n)\longrightarrow\sum_t\mu_t\ell_{t,j_t}(\theta)=F(\theta) \)

along that further subsequence. This implies continuity of \(F\).

For uniform approximation, define the near-top set

\( T_t^\delta(\theta)=\left\{j\in U_t:\max_{i\in U_t}S_i^*(\theta)-S_j^*(\theta)\le\delta\right\} \)

and the modulus

\( \omega(\delta)=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_t\mu_t\max_{i,j\in T_t^\delta(\theta)}|\ell_{t,i}(\theta)-\ell_{t,j}(\theta)|. \)

We claim \(\omega(\delta)\to0\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\). Otherwise, choose \(\delta_n\downarrow0\) and parameters witnessing a discrepancy bounded away from zero. Compactness gives a convergent parameter subsequence, and finiteness permits all witnessing indices to be fixed along a further subsequence. At the limit, each witnessing pair belongs to the corresponding exact top set. Continuity and exact-tie compatibility force every discrepancy to tend to zero, a contradiction.

The score-regret bound places each approximate selected question in \(T_t^{2e_r}(\theta)\); the exact selected question is in \(T_t^0(\theta)\). Therefore,

\( \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|F_r(\theta)-F(\theta)|\le\omega(2e_r)\longrightarrow0. \)

This proves the proposition. \(\square\)

Attainment of the approximate maxima

Uniform convergence does not itself prove that a discontinuous \(F_r\) attains its supremum. The following explicit condition suffices.

Let

\( T_{t,r}(\theta)=\operatorname{argmax}_{j\in U_t}S_j^{(r)}(\theta), \)

and let \(j_{t,r}(\theta)\) be its first member in the prescribed question order. Require, for every sufficiently large \(r\),

\( \ell_{t,j_{t,r}(\theta)}(\theta)\ge\ell_{t,j}(\theta)\quad\text{for every }j\in T_{t,r}(\theta),\ \theta,t. \)

Under this condition, \(F_r\) is upper semicontinuous. Indeed, the finite iterates are continuous in \(\theta\). Along a convergent parameter sequence, pass to a subsequence with constant selected indices. Those indices belong to the top sets at the limit, and the displayed inequality makes their limiting likelihoods no greater than the likelihoods selected at the limit. Summing with positive masses proves upper semicontinuity.

An upper semicontinuous function on the compact box attains its maximum. Thus exact-tie compatibility together with this finite-iterate tie condition yields precisely

\( \max_\theta F_r(\theta)\longrightarrow\max_\theta F(\theta). \)

If \(\theta_r\in\operatorname{argmax}F_r\), write \(d_r=\sup_\theta|F_r-F|\). Then

\( F(\theta_r)\ge\max_\theta F(\theta)-2d_r. \)

Compactness supplies convergent subsequences; continuity of \(F\) shows that every accumulation point maximizes \(F\).

Even without approximate attainment, the proved statement remains valid with suprema:

\( \sup_\theta F_r(\theta)\longrightarrow\max_\theta F(\theta). \)

Moreover, every sequence satisfying

\( F_r(\theta_r)\ge\sup_\theta F_r(\theta)-\zeta_r,\qquad \zeta_r\to0, \)

has only \(F\)-maximizing accumulation points, by the same argument with error \(2d_r+\zeta_r\).

6. Ranking can genuinely destroy continuity in this model

Here is an admissible instance in which \(F\) is not even upper semicontinuous.

Take \(m=4\), \(K=1\), all four support edges, one type of mass \(1\), and \(\varepsilon=1/8\). Its observed history is \(O_1=1,O_2=0\); its unanswered responses are \(O_3=0,O_4=1\). Put question \(3\) before question \(4\) in the fixed order.

Consider the parameter point

\( p=\frac12,\qquad q_1=\frac14,\qquad q_2=\frac34,\qquad q_3=q_4=\frac14. \)

Only \(Y_{12}\) is nonzero. Writing \(b_i=\beta_i\), one has

\( b_1=\frac38,\qquad b_2=\frac18,\qquad b_3=b_4=\frac38. \)

Choose a sufficiently large positive integer \(\alpha\). We verify that slightly increasing \(q_3\) makes question \(4\) strictly outrank question \(3\).

Set \(h=(b_1+b_2)(e_1-e_2)\). At \(S=0\), the Hessian of \(\log Z\) is

\( H_0=\frac16\sum_{a<b}(b_a+b_b)^2(e_a-e_b)(e_a-e_b)^\top. \)

Put \(x=1/\alpha\). Stationarity is

\( S=x\bigl(h-\nabla\log Z(S)\bigr). \)

At \(x=0,S=0\), the derivative of this equation with respect to \(S\), after bringing all terms to the left, is \(I\). The analytic implicit-function theorem therefore gives an analytic solution near \(x=0\), with expansion

\( S^*=xh-x^2H_0h+O(x^3). \)

For positive small \(x\), uniqueness identifies this solution with the inner maximizer. Direct calculation gives

\( (H_0h)_3-(H_0h)_4=\frac{(b_1+b_2)(b_2-b_1)(b_3-b_4)}3. \)

Consequently,

\( S_3^*-S_4^*=\frac{(b_1+b_2)(b_1-b_2)(b_3-b_4)}{3\alpha^2}+O(\alpha^{-3}). \)

Analyticity also permits differentiation of this expansion with respect to \(b_3\). At the specified point,

\( \frac{\partial}{\partial b_3}(S_3^*-S_4^*)=\frac1{24\alpha^2}+O(\alpha^{-3})>0 \)

for all sufficiently large \(\alpha\).

At \(b_3=b_4\), exchange symmetry and uniqueness give \(S_3^*=S_4^*\) exactly. Increasing \(q_3\) slightly decreases \(b_3\), so the positive derivative implies \(S_3^*<S_4^*\).

At the tie, the rule selects question \(3\), whose appended response is negative:

\( P_\theta(O_1=1,O_2=0,O_3=0)=p(1-q_1)q_2q_3=\frac9{128}. \)

After the perturbation, the rule selects question \(4\), whose response is positive:

\( P_\theta(O_1=1,O_2=0,O_4=1)=p(1-q_1)q_2(1-q_4)=\frac{27}{128}. \)

Hence \(F\) takes the value \(\log(9/128)\) at the tie, while approaching along these perturbations gives \(\log(27/128)\). It is not upper semicontinuous there.

This example establishes failure of the regularity hypothesis, not failure of every possible outer convergence conclusion. In particular, a fixed-history smoothness argument cannot justify a general ranking-dependent Lipschitz claim.

7. Computational costs and relation to the cited results

The following counts use real arithmetic, counting an exponential or logarithm evaluation as one operation.

The coefficients \(a_{ij}\) can be precomputed in \(O(\tau m^2)\) arithmetic operations, in addition to reading the input of length \(L\). For a fixed \(\theta\), computing the \(\beta_i\) costs \(O(E)\); each inner gradient costs \(O(m^2)\), since every feature has only two nonzero coordinates. Thus computing \(r\) iterates costs

\( O(L+\tau m^2+E+rm^2). \)

With \(\eta_r=1/M\), uniform score error at most \(\delta>0\) is guaranteed after at most

\( r_\delta=\max\!\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac M\alpha\log\!\left(\max\{1,R/\delta\}\right)\right\rceil\right\} \)

iterations.

After selecting the appended questions in \(O(\tau m)\) operations, direct enumeration of candidate configurations evaluates all selected-history probabilities in

\( O\!\left(2^K(K+E+\tau m)\right) \)

operations. This enumeration is over candidate configurations, not over possible society types; the society dependence is on its actual support size \(\tau\).

Accordingly, one direct evaluation of \(F_r\), including preprocessing, has arithmetic cost

\( O\!\left(L+\tau m^2+E+rm^2+\tau m+2^K(K+E+\tau m)\right). \)

These are arithmetic counts, not polynomial bit-time claims. The inner iteration bound depends on the numerical condition ratio \(M/\alpha\), which can be exponential in its binary encoding length. Numerical implementation also requires a specified precision for exponentials and logarithms. No efficient global outer-optimization algorithm follows from the convergence proof.

The finite-worker statement in the cited [BiO-HMC paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38820/42782) is conditional on uniform inner convergence and outer Lipschitz regularity. The [Franceschi et al. theorem](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/franceschi18a/franceschi18a.pdf), Theorem 3.2, likewise assumes joint continuity, unique bounded inner solutions, uniform Lipschitz dependence on the inner variable, and uniform inner convergence.

Here the inner assumptions have explicit proofs and constants. Deterministic history selection requires additional analysis, supplied by the gap or tie-compatibility conditions above. The resulting statements concern globally optimal outer values and accumulation points of global approximate maximizers; they do not establish global convergence of outer gradient ascent.
