The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), positive rational values \(v_a(t),v_b(t)\), and budgets \(B_a,B_b\), choose allocation densities \(q_i:T\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_tq_i(t)=B_i\). The leader chooses \(q_a\), the follower best responds with \(q_b\), and the task is to compute an optimal commitment and its value for \(U_a=\sum_t\mu_tv_a(t)\frac{q_a(t)}{q_a(t)+q_b(t)}\), using the paper's zero-allocation convention; an \(\varepsilon\)-approximation version is also natural.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
There are two players, leader \(a\) and follower \(b\), with positive binary-rational budgets \(B_a,B_b\) and values \(v_i(t)\). Pure allocations are densities \(q_i:T\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_tq_i(t)=B_i\).
Define shares \(s_a(t)=q_a(t)/(q_a(t)+q_b(t))\) and \(s_b(t)=1-s_a(t)\) when the denominator is positive; when both allocations vanish, set \(s_a(t)=0\) and \(s_b(t)=1\). Utilities are \(U_i(q_a,q_b)=\sum_t\mu_tv_i(t)s_i(t)\).
After observing the leader's commitment, the follower chooses its unique utility-maximizing feasible allocation \(R(q_a)\). Let \(V=\max_{q_a}U_a(q_a,R(q_a))\).
The exact threshold problem additionally receives a binary-rational threshold \(c\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length. The player count is fixed, and there is no additional size parameter \(m\).

Is deciding whether \(V\ge c\) solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many battlefields with real allocations, positive rational values, and positive budgets, [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07690v3) characterize optimal commitment through a linear-time univariate formulation for each of linearly many follower supports and claim polynomially many iterations.
No separate bit-complexity classification of the support-mass threshold problem was found in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required both budgets to be positive binary rationals, carrying over positivity and specifying their finite input encoding.
  • Specified the omitted follower objective using its own values and the same mass-weighted proportional utility.
  • Resolved the zero-allocation convention by awarding the entire type to the follower when both densities vanish.
  • Kept allocations pure and constant within each listed type, as prescribed by the density formulation.
  • Used the exact rational-threshold decision formulation of optimal value to make the P-versus-NP-hard target precise without imposing a representation for irrational optimal allocations; did not select the optional approximation variant.
  • Defined L as total input bit length, including the threshold; this model has no additional m parameter.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result

The exact threshold problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(\tau,L\) for the stated positive rational values.

We prove this by expressing the leader’s problem in terms of its shares. The resulting optimization has a single quadratic constraint, and its optimum can be computed using rational arithmetic and one square root. This supplies an exact bit-complexity argument for the model studied by [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07690v3).

2. Removing the support masses

Write \(A=B_a\), \(B=B_b\), and define, for each \(t\in T\),

\(x_t=\mu_tq_a(t),\qquad y_t=\mu_tq_b(t),\qquad u_t=\mu_tv_a(t),\qquad w_t=\mu_tv_b(t).\)

All \(u_t,w_t\) are positive rationals. Since each \(\mu_t>0\), this is a bijective change of allocation variables. The budgets become

\(\sum_t x_t=A,\qquad \sum_t y_t=B.\)

The shares are unchanged: whenever the denominator is positive, the leader’s share is \(x_t/(x_t+y_t)\). At \(x_t=y_t=0\), its share remains zero. Utilities become the corresponding sums weighted by \(u_t,w_t\).

This transformation uses exactly \(\tau\) coordinates and increases encoding length only polynomially. In particular, no expansion according to mass denominators is needed.

3. The follower’s response and a necessary share inequality

Fix a feasible leader allocation \(x\), and put \(P=\{t:x_t>0\}\). This set is nonempty because \(A>0\).

On a coordinate in \(P\), the follower’s utility contribution is

\(f_t(y_t)=w_t\frac{y_t}{x_t+y_t},\)

with derivatives

\(f_t'(y_t)=\frac{w_tx_t}{(x_t+y_t)^2}>0,\qquad f_t''(y_t)=-\frac{2w_tx_t}{(x_t+y_t)^3}<0.\)

On a coordinate outside \(P\), its contribution is the constant \(w_t\), including at \(y_t=0\). Consequently, an optimal response allocates nothing outside \(P\): transferring any positive allocation from such a coordinate to a coordinate in \(P\) strictly increases utility.

There is a unique \(\lambda>0\) satisfying

\(\sum_{t\in P}\max\left\{\sqrt{\frac{w_tx_t}{\lambda}}-x_t,0\right\}=B.\)

Indeed, the left-hand side is continuous, tends to infinity as \(\lambda\) tends to zero, and becomes zero for sufficiently large \(\lambda\). It is strictly decreasing wherever it is positive.

The follower’s response is

\(y_t=\max\left\{\sqrt{\frac{w_tx_t}{\lambda}}-x_t,0\right\}\quad(t\in P),\qquad y_t=0\quad(t\notin P).\)

To verify optimality, its marginal utility equals \(\lambda\) on every coordinate with \(y_t>0\), and is at most \(\lambda\) on every coordinate with \(y_t=0\). The supporting-line inequality for each concave \(f_t\), together with the budget equality, therefore shows that no feasible response has greater utility. Strict concavity on \(P\), and the absence of allocations outside \(P\), prove uniqueness.

Let \(z_t\) be the leader’s share under this response. The formula above gives

\(z_t=\min\left\{1,\sqrt{\frac{\lambda x_t}{w_t}}\right\}.\)

This also holds when \(x_t=0\), since then \(z_t=0\).

For every coordinate,

\(\lambda y_t=w_tz_t(1-z_t),\qquad \lambda x_t\ge w_tz_t^2.\)

For \(z_t<1\), both relations are equalities. For \(z_t=1\), the second follows from \(\lambda x_t\ge w_t\).

Define

\(E(z)=\sum_t w_tz_t^2,\qquad D(z)=\sum_t w_tz_t(1-z_t).\)

Summing the preceding relations yields \(D(z)=\lambda B\) and \(E(z)\le\lambda A\). Hence every attainable share vector satisfies

\(G(z):=BE(z)-AD(z)=\sum_t w_t\bigl((A+B)z_t^2-Az_t\bigr)\le0.\)

The leader’s utility is \(\sum_tu_tz_t\). Thus the following optimization gives an upper bound on \(V\):

\(\max\left\{\sum_tu_tz_t:\ 0\le z_t\le1,\ G(z)\le0\right\}.\)

We next solve this optimization and prove that its optimum is attainable.

4. Solving the share optimization

Set

\(\alpha=\frac{A}{2(A+B)},\qquad \rho_t=\frac{u_t}{w_t}.\)

Thus \(0<\alpha<1/2\) and every \(\rho_t>0\). For \(\beta\ge0\), define

\(z_t(\beta)=\min\{1,\alpha+\beta\rho_t\}.\)

There is a unique \(\beta_*>0\) for which \(G(z(\beta_*))=0\).

To prove this, observe first that

\(G(z(0))=-\frac{A^2}{4(A+B)}\sum_t w_t<0.\)

For sufficiently large \(\beta\), all coordinates equal one, and then

\(G(z(\beta))=B\sum_t w_t>0.\)

For each \(t\), the function \(w_t((A+B)z^2-Az)\) is strictly increasing for \(z>\alpha\). Therefore \(G(z(\beta))\) is continuous and strictly increasing until all coordinates have saturated at one. The asserted unique root follows. At that root, at least one coordinate is strictly below one.

We now prove that \(z^*=z(\beta_*)\) maximizes the share optimization. Define

\(\kappa=\frac{1}{2(A+B)\beta_*}>0.\)

Consider the function

\(\mathcal L(z)=\sum_tu_tz_t-\kappa G(z).\)

It is a sum of strictly concave coordinate functions. The unconstrained maximizer of its \(t\)-th coordinate satisfies

\(u_t-\kappa w_t\bigl(2(A+B)z_t-A\bigr)=0,\)

and therefore equals

\(\alpha+\frac{u_t}{2\kappa(A+B)w_t}=\alpha+\beta_*\rho_t.\)

This number is positive. Consequently, its maximizer over \(0\le z_t\le1\) is precisely \(z_t^*\). Hence \(z^*\) maximizes \(\mathcal L\) on the entire box.

For any feasible \(z\), the inequalities \(G(z)\le0\) and \(\kappa>0\) give

\(\sum_tu_tz_t\le\mathcal L(z)\le\mathcal L(z^*)=\sum_tu_tz_t^*,\)

where the last equality uses \(G(z^*)=0\). This proves optimality.

5. Attaining the optimum with a leader commitment

Every coordinate of \(z^*\) is positive, and at least one lies strictly below one. Thus \(D(z^*)>0\). Set

\(\lambda_*=\frac{D(z^*)}{B},\qquad x_t^*=\frac{w_t(z_t^*)^2}{\lambda_*},\qquad y_t^*=\frac{w_tz_t^*(1-z_t^*)}{\lambda_*}.\)

These allocations are nonnegative, with every \(x_t^*>0\). By construction,

\(\sum_t y_t^*=B.\)

Because \(G(z^*)=BE(z^*)-AD(z^*)=0\), we also have

\(\sum_t x_t^*=\frac{E(z^*)}{\lambda_*}=A.\)

Their leader shares are exactly \(z_t^*\), since

\(x_t^*+y_t^*=\frac{w_tz_t^*}{\lambda_*}.\)

Moreover, the follower’s marginal utility at this allocation is, on every coordinate,

\(\frac{w_tx_t^*}{(x_t^*+y_t^*)^2}=\lambda_*.\)

The supporting-line inequality for its concave utility therefore proves that \(y^*\) is optimal under budget \(B\). Since all \(x_t^*>0\), that utility is strictly concave, so this response is unique. Coordinates with \(z_t^*=1\) have \(y_t^*=0\), and the same marginal calculation remains valid there.

Thus the upper bound from Section 3 is attained. We have proved

\(V=\sum_tu_t\min\{1,\alpha+\beta_*\rho_t\}.\)

The original optimal commitment is recovered as \(q_a^*(t)=x_t^*/\mu_t\).

6. Exact computation with polynomial bit complexity

Sort the types so that

\(\rho_1\le\rho_2\le\cdots\le\rho_\tau.\)

Since \(z_t(\beta)=\min\{1,\alpha+\beta\rho_t\}\), the coordinates strictly below one form a nonempty prefix. We enumerate prefix lengths \(k=1,\ldots,\tau\).

For a candidate prefix \(K=\{1,\ldots,k\}\), let \(S=T\setminus K\), and define the rational numbers

\(W_K=\sum_{t\in K}w_t,\qquad W_S=\sum_{t\in S}w_t,\qquad H_K=\sum_{t\in K}\frac{u_t^2}{w_t}.\)

In particular, \(H_K>0\). Substituting \(z_t=\alpha+\beta\rho_t\) on \(K\) and \(z_t=1\) on \(S\) gives

\(G(z)=(A+B)H_K\beta^2-\frac{A^2}{4(A+B)}W_K+BW_S.\)

Here the terms linear in \(\beta\) vanish because \(2(A+B)\alpha=A\). Thus the only possible positive parameter for this candidate is \(\beta=\sqrt{Q_K}\), where

\(Q_K=\frac{\frac{A^2}{4(A+B)}W_K-BW_S}{(A+B)H_K}.\)

Discard the candidate if \(Q_K\le0\). Otherwise, check

\(\alpha+\sqrt{Q_K}\rho_t\le1\quad(t\in K),\qquad \alpha+\sqrt{Q_K}\rho_t\ge1\quad(t\in S).\)

These checks require only rational arithmetic. Since \(\rho_t>0\) and \(1-\alpha>0\), they are equivalent to

\(Q_K\rho_t^2\le(1-\alpha)^2\quad(t\in K),\qquad Q_K\rho_t^2\ge(1-\alpha)^2\quad(t\in S).\)

The actual strictly unsaturated prefix yields a valid candidate, so at least one passes. Every passing candidate gives \(z(\sqrt{Q_K})\) with \(G=0\), and therefore gives the unique parameter \(\beta_*\). Allowing equality in the checks handles support-boundary ties.

For a passing candidate, define

\(C_K=\sum_{t\in S}u_t+\alpha\sum_{t\in K}u_t.\)

The exact optimal value is

\(V=C_K+H_K\sqrt{Q_K}.\)

To compare it with the input threshold \(c\), put \(d=c-C_K\). If \(d\le0\), answer yes. If \(d>0\), both sides of \(H_K\sqrt{Q_K}\ge d\) are positive, so squaring is equivalent and gives the rational comparison

\(H_K^2Q_K\ge d^2.\)

Equality is therefore decided exactly.

There are \(\tau\) candidates, and a direct implementation uses \(O(\tau^2)\) rational arithmetic operations and comparisons, in addition to sorting. Every rational appearing above is formed from input rationals by a bounded number of arithmetic operations per summand and sums of at most \(\tau\) terms. Their numerator and denominator bit lengths are polynomial in \(\tau,L\). Exact rational arithmetic and comparison consequently take polynomial time.

This proves the claimed classification. It also shows that \(V\) is an algebraic number of degree at most two over the rationals.
