Theorem 3.4 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite ordered sequence of cohorts with masses \(\alpha_j>0\) summing to \(1\) and profiles \(t^j\in\{0,1\}^d\), positive coefficients \(c_k\), capacity fraction \(\kappa\in(0,1]\), and exact marginal advice \(a_k=\sum_j\alpha_j t^j_k\), what is the optimal worst-case competitive ratio, uniformly over the number of cohorts? On arrival of cohort \(j\), a causal policy observes \((\alpha_j,t^j)\) and admits mass \(x_j\in[0,\alpha_j]\), subject almost surely to \(\sum_jx_j\le\kappa\); its dimension utility is \(U_k=c_k\,\mathbb{E}[\sum_jx_jt^j_k]\), and its objective is \(\min_k U_k\). Compare it with the offline optimum that knows the full ordered sequence and has the same capacity.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is encoded by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) distinct binary types with positive total mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not \(2^d\). An instance additionally specifies an ordered sequence of \(N\) cohorts \((\alpha_j,t^j)\), with \(\alpha_j>0\), \(\sum_j\alpha_j=1\), and \(t^j\in\{0,1\}^d\); types may recur.
The coefficients \(c_k>0\) and capacity \(\kappa\in(0,1]\) are binary rationals, normalized by \(\min_k c_k=1\). An oblivious adversary fixes the entire sequence before the policy's random choices.
Before arrivals, the policy knows only \(d,c,\kappa\) and exact advice \(a_k=\sum_j\alpha_jt^j_k\), not the support, cohort count, or future sequence. Upon observing \((\alpha_j,t^j)\), it irrevocably chooses \(x_j\in[0,\alpha_j]\), with \(\sum_jx_j\le\kappa\) almost surely.
Its value is \(A_\pi(\mathcal I)=\min_k c_k\mathbb E_\pi[\sum_jx_jt^j_k]\). The clairvoyant benchmark is \(O(\mathcal I)=\max\{\min_k c_k\sum_jy_jt^j_k:0\le y_j\le\alpha_j,\ \sum_jy_j\le\kappa\}\).
Define \(R^*(d)=\sup_\pi\inf_{\mathcal I:O(\mathcal I)>0}A_\pi(\mathcal I)/O(\mathcal I)\), where the infimum includes every finite cohort count, admissible capacity, coefficient vector, and sequence with \(d\) dimensions. Policies are causal and may randomize. State running times in \(m=d\), \(\tau\), \(N\), and total binary input length \(L\).

What is the exact optimal worst-case competitive ratio \(R^*(d)\) for this mass-selection problem, uniformly over the number of cohorts?

For discrete selections with exact marginal advice, the attainable ratio is at least \(1/[4(2+\sqrt2)\sqrt d]\), while every policy has worst-case ratio at most \(O(d^{-1/2})\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10389v3)
The same source establishes guarantees for fractional decisions with unit arrival caps; it does not determine the exact optimal ratio for arbitrary cohort masses. No exact characterization for the stated cohort model was located.

Partial resolution: exact in two dimensions and sharper uniform bounds

The exact ratio for general $d$ remains unresolved. The following results improve the bounds in the notes and completely resolve the two-dimensional case.

Define, for $d\ge2$,
$$ s_d=\left\lfloor\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4d}}2\right\rfloor, \qquad C_d=2s_d-4+\frac{2d}{s_d}, $$
and
$$ B_d= \min\left\{ \frac2{s_d}, \min_{\substack{2\le q\le d\\1\le h<q}} \frac{h(q-1)}{q(2h-1)-h^2} \right\}. $$

Proven results.
$$ R^*(1)=R^*(2)=1. $$
For every $d\ge3$,
$$ \boxed{ \max\left\{\frac2d,\frac1{C_d}\right\} \le R^*(d)\le B_d. } $$
Moreover,
$$ C_d<4\sqrt d-3. $$
Thus the lower bound is attained by a finite, polynomial-time policy and implies
$$ R^*(d)\ge \max\left\{\frac2d,\frac1{4\sqrt d-3}\right\}. $$

In particular, the smallest unresolved dimension satisfies
$$ \frac23\le R^*(3)\le\frac45. $$

All guarantees below hold uniformly over arbitrary finite cohort counts, arbitrary admissible cohort masses, recurring types, and the coefficient vectors specified in the task.

1. Preliminary facts and an advice-derived benchmark

Write
$$ a_k=\sum_j\alpha_jt^j_k. $$

1.1 Randomization does not improve the optimum

For a randomized policy, fix its initial information and an observed cohort prefix. Define a deterministic policy’s allocation at that prefix to be the expected allocation of the randomized policy when supplied that same prefix.

This expectation depends only on the prefix, by causality. On each complete instance, its allocations are
$$ \bar x_j=\mathbb E[x_j]. $$
They satisfy
$$ 0\le\bar x_j\le\alpha_j, \qquad \sum_j\bar x_j\le\kappa. $$
Furthermore,
$$ \min_k c_k\sum_j\bar x_jt^j_k = \min_k c_k\mathbb E\left[\sum_jx_jt^j_k\right]. $$
Thus this deterministic policy has exactly the randomized policy’s value on every instance.

This is an existence argument; efficiently computing the expectations of an arbitrary randomized policy is not asserted.

Also, the expected allocation of any policy is a feasible offline allocation. Consequently,
$$ A_\pi(\mathcal I)\le O(\mathcal I), $$
and therefore $R^*(d)\le1$.

1.2 A feasible allocation computed from advice

Assume every $a_k>0$, and define
$$ H=\sum_{k=1}^d\frac1{c_k}, \qquad b=\min_k c_ka_k, \qquad \ell=\min\left\{b,\frac{\kappa}{H}\right\}, \qquad U=\min\{b,\kappa\}. $$
The normalization gives
$$ 1\le H\le d. $$

Consider the causal allocation
$$ x_j^{(0)} = \alpha_j \max_{k:t^j_k=1}\frac{\ell}{c_ka_k}, $$
where the maximum is zero for the zero type.

Each multiplier is at most one, so the cohort cap holds. Its expenditure satisfies
$$ \sum_jx_j^{(0)} \le \sum_k\frac{\ell}{c_ka_k} \sum_j\alpha_jt^j_k = \ell H \le\kappa. $$
For every coordinate,
$$ \sum_jx_j^{(0)}t^j_k \ge \frac{\ell}{c_ka_k} \sum_j\alpha_jt^j_k = \frac{\ell}{c_k}. $$
Thus this policy achieves value at least $\ell$.

Conversely, available coordinate mass bounds the benchmark by $b$. A coordinate whose coefficient is one bounds it by $\kappa$. Hence
$$ \ell\le O(\mathcal I)\le U. $$
Also,
$$ \frac U\ell\le H\le d: $$
if $\ell=b$, then $U=\ell$; otherwise $\ell=\kappa/H$ and $U\le\kappa$.

These observations also prove
$$ O(\mathcal I)>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_k>0\text{ for every }k. $$
Accordingly, all subsequent divisions by marginals occur only on instances with positive benchmark.

For $d=1$, accepting positive-type mass until capacity is exhausted attains $\min\{a_1,\kappa\}$. Thus $R^*(1)=1$.

2. Exact optimality in two dimensions

We now prove $R^*(2)=1$.

Set
$$ r_k=\frac1{c_k}, \qquad H=r_1+r_2, \qquad b=\min\{c_1a_1,c_2a_2\}. $$
Because the coefficients are normalized,
$$ \max\{r_1,r_2\}=1, \qquad 1<H\le2. $$

Let $w$ be the total mass of type $(1,1)$.

2.1 Exact offline formula

Define
$$ V(w)= \min\left\{ b,\kappa,\frac{\kappa+w}{H} \right\}. $$
Then
$$ O(\mathcal I)=V(w). $$

To prove the upper bound, suppose an allocation achieves value $v$. Its two unweighted coverages are at least $r_1v$ and $r_2v$. Their sum is at most total selected mass plus selected joint-type mass, and therefore at most $\kappa+w$. Hence
$$ Hv\le\kappa+w. $$
The bounds $v\le b$ and $v\le\kappa$ follow as in Section 1.

For achievability, first suppose $w\le\kappa$ and put $v=V(w)$. Select all joint-type mass and additionally select singleton mass
$$ f_k(w)=(r_kv-w)_+ $$
in coordinate $k$, where $(u)_+=\max\{u,0\}$.

There is enough singleton mass because
$$ f_k(w)\le a_k-w. $$
The expenditure is
$$ w+(r_1v-w)_++(r_2v-w)_+ = \max\{w,r_1v,r_2v,Hv-w\}. $$
This identity follows by considering whether $w$ lies below, between, or above the two numbers $r_1v,r_2v$. Every term on the right is at most $\kappa$, by the definition of $v$ and $r_k\le1$.

The resulting coordinate coverages are at least $r_kv$. The aggregate selections can be distributed among the corresponding cohorts within their caps.

If $w\ge\kappa$, select joint-type mass $\kappa$. This achieves value $\kappa$. Moreover, $a_k\ge w$, so $b\ge\kappa$, and
$$ \frac{\kappa+w}{H}\ge\kappa. $$
Thus $V(w)=\kappa$, completing the offline formula.

2.2 A monotonicity property

For fixed initial advice, regard $V(u)$ as a function of $u\ge0$ and define
$$ f_k(u)=(r_kV(u)-u)_+. $$
Each $f_k$ is nonincreasing.

Indeed, for $u_2\ge u_1$,
$$ 0\le V(u_2)-V(u_1)\le\frac{u_2-u_1}{H}. $$
Therefore,
$$ r_kV(u_2)-u_2 - \bigl(r_kV(u_1)-u_1\bigr) \le \left(\frac{r_k}{H}-1\right)(u_2-u_1) \le0. $$
Taking positive parts preserves monotonicity.

2.3 The online policy

Maintain:

  • $R_k$: remaining mass possessing coordinate $k$;
  • $q$: total observed joint-type mass;
  • $S_k$: selected singleton mass in coordinate $k$;
  • $z$: selected joint-type mass.

Initially,
$$ R_k=a_k,\qquad q=S_1=S_2=z=0. $$

Upon observing a cohort, first subtract its coordinate masses from $R_1,R_2$. If it is joint-type, also increase $q$ by its mass. Define
$$ W=q+\min\{R_1,R_2\}. $$
This is an upper bound on the instance’s total joint-type mass.

The allocation rule is:

  • For type $(1,0)$, select

$$ x_j= \bigl(r_1V(W)-q-R_1-S_1\bigr)_+, $$
and increase $S_1$ by $x_j$.

  • For type $(0,1)$, use the analogous rule for $S_2$.
  • For type $(1,1)$, select

$$ x_j=\min\{\alpha_j,\kappa-S_1-S_2-z\}, $$
and increase $z$ by $x_j$.

  • For type $(0,0)$, select zero.

The following proof establishes that the displayed singleton allocations respect their caps and that the remaining capacity is always nonnegative.

2.4 Singleton caps and feasibility

After each cohort, define
$$ D_k=r_kV(W)-q-R_k. $$
We first prove the invariant
$$ S_k\ge(D_k)_+. $$

Initially, $D_k\le0$, because $V(W)\le c_ka_k$.

A joint cohort leaves $W$ unchanged: it increases $q$ and decreases both remaining marginals by the same mass. It also leaves $q+R_k$ unchanged. Thus it leaves both $D_k$ unchanged.

A singleton cohort in coordinate other than $k$ cannot increase $W$, and hence cannot increase $D_k$.

For a singleton cohort of mass $\alpha$ in coordinate $k$, $q$ stays fixed, $R_k$ decreases by $\alpha$, and $V(W)$ cannot increase. Consequently,
$$ D_k^{\mathrm{new}} \le D_k^{\mathrm{old}}+\alpha. $$
The rule updates
$$ S_k^{\mathrm{new}}=\max\{S_k^{\mathrm{old}},D_k^{\mathrm{new}}\}. $$
Using the previous invariant, its increment is at most $\alpha$. This proves the cohort cap and preserves the invariant.

Now fix the complete instance and its actual joint mass $w$. At every prefix,
$$ w\le W, \qquad q+R_k\ge W. $$
It follows that
$$ (D_k)_+ \le (r_kV(W)-W)_+ = f_k(W) \le f_k(w). $$
Since $S_k$ starts at zero and is updated by taking a maximum with $D_k$,
$$ 0\le S_k\le f_k(w) $$
at every prefix.

If $w\le\kappa$, the offline calculation gives
$$ w+f_1(w)+f_2(w)\le\kappa. $$
Thus all observed joint mass can be selected: inductively, after any current joint cohort,
$$ q+S_1+S_2 \le w+f_1(w)+f_2(w) \le\kappa. $$
Its capacity cap never truncates the cohort. Singleton decisions also remain within total capacity.

If $w\ge\kappa$, then $V(w)=\kappa$ and $f_k(w)=0$. Hence $S_1=S_2=0$ throughout, and the policy simply selects joint mass up to $\kappa$.

This proves feasibility in every case.

2.5 Final value

When $w\le\kappa$, termination gives
$$ R_1=R_2=0,\qquad q=w. $$
The invariant and upper bounds imply
$$ S_k=f_k(w), \qquad z=w. $$
Hence each coordinate’s coverage is at least $r_kV(w)$.

When $w\ge\kappa$, the policy selects joint mass $\kappa$, attaining $V(w)=\kappa$.

Thus the policy always attains the offline benchmark. Together with $R^*(2)\le1$,
$$ \boxed{R^*(2)=1.} $$

3. A uniform $2/d$ guarantee

For $d\ge3$, consider the $d$ cyclic coordinate pairs
$$ \{1,2\},\{2,3\},\ldots,\{d-1,d\},\{d,1\}. $$
Run the exact two-dimensional policy on each pair, with capacity $\kappa$ and the corresponding marginal advice.

For each pair, divide its two coefficients by their minimum before applying Section 2. This common rescaling changes neither its optimal allocations nor the statement that the policy matches its pairwise offline benchmark.

Let $x_j^{(i)}$ denote the allocation of pair policy $i$. Use
$$ x_j=\frac1d\sum_{i=1}^d x_j^{(i)}. $$
This is a convex combination of feasible allocations, so it respects every cohort cap and total capacity.

Every globally feasible allocation is feasible for each pairwise problem. Therefore each pairwise offline value, measured using the original coefficients, is at least $O(\mathcal I)$.

Each coordinate belongs to two cyclic pairs. Both corresponding policies provide weighted coverage at least $O(\mathcal I)$ in that coordinate. Consequently,
$$ c_k\sum_jx_jt^j_k \ge\frac2dO(\mathcal I) $$
for every $k$.

Thus
$$ R^*(d)\ge\frac2d. $$

4. An integer-threshold greedy/protection lemma

The controlled-greedy and protection construction is related to the approach in [Hu–Li–Wu](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10389v3). The proof here explicitly accommodates arbitrary cohort masses.

Lemma. Let $g\ge2$ be an integer and $\theta>0$. Suppose
$$ \theta H\le g\kappa, \qquad (g-1)\theta\le O(\mathcal I). $$
There is a causal feasible policy with value at least $\theta/2$.

The policy uses $g,\theta$ and the advice; it does not use the benchmark.

4.1 Construction

Maintain separate greedy and protection budgets, each equal to $\kappa$.

For greedy selection, write
$$ G_{j-1,k}=\sum_{i<j}y_it^i_k. $$
Increase $y_j$ from zero while:

  • $y_j<\alpha_j$;
  • greedy capacity remains;
  • at least $g$ coordinates satisfy

$$ t^j_k=1, \qquad G_{j-1,k}+y_j<\frac{\theta}{c_k}. $$

Then put
$$ G_{j,k}=G_{j-1,k}+y_jt^j_k. $$

The remaining coordinate mass after the current observation is
$$ R_{j,k}=a_k-\sum_{i\le j}\alpha_it^i_k. $$
Let
$$ Z_{j-1,k}=\sum_{i<j}z_{i,k}. $$
Scan coordinates in a fixed order. For a coordinate present in the current cohort, set $z_{j,k}$ to the minimum of
$$ \alpha_j,\qquad \left( \frac{\theta}{c_k} -G_{j,k}-Z_{j-1,k}-R_{j,k} \right)_+, \qquad \text{remaining protection budget}. $$
For an absent coordinate, set $z_{j,k}=0$. Protection spending is charged as $\sum_{j,k}z_{j,k}$.

Output
$$ x_j=\frac{y_j+\max_k z_{j,k}}2. $$
Both numerator terms lie in $[0,\alpha_j]$. Also,
$$ \sum_jx_j \le \frac12\left(\sum_jy_j+\sum_{j,k}z_{j,k}\right) \le\kappa. $$
Thus the policy is feasible even when the lemma’s benchmark hypothesis fails.

4.2 Exhausted greedy budget

Suppose greedy spends $\kappa$. Its total truncated coverage is
$$ \sum_k\min\left\{G_{N,k},\frac{\theta}{c_k}\right\}. $$
During every positive interval of greedy expenditure, at least $g$ currently covered coordinates are below target. Hence this sum is at least $g\kappa$.

It is also at most
$$ \sum_k\frac{\theta}{c_k} =\theta H \le g\kappa. $$
Equality follows, forcing every coordinate to reach its target. Since $x_j\ge y_j/2$, the policy’s value is at least $\theta/2$.

4.3 Slack greedy budget: bounding final deficits

Suppose greedy spends less than $\kappa$. Define
$$ s_k=\left(\frac{\theta}{c_k}-G_{N,k}\right)_+, \qquad Q=\{k:s_k>0\}. $$
Let $y^*$ be an optimal offline allocation, which exists by compactness of the finite feasible region. Put
$$ \lambda=\frac1{g-1}, \qquad q_j=\sum_{k\in Q}t^j_k. $$
The hypothesis gives $\theta\le\lambda O(\mathcal I)$, so
$$ \sum_ks_k \le \sum_j(\lambda y_j^*-y_j)q_j. $$

If $q_j\ge g$, all these $q_j$ coordinates remain below target throughout cohort $j$, because their final greedy coverages remain below target. Greedy budget slack rules out a budget stop. Thus $y_j=\alpha_j$, and
$$ \lambda y_j^*-y_j\le0. $$

If $q_j<g$, integrality gives $q_j\le g-1$, and therefore
$$ (\lambda y_j^*-y_j)q_j \le \lambda(g-1)y_j^* =y_j^*. $$
Summing,
$$ \sum_ks_k\le\sum_jy_j^*\le\kappa. $$

4.4 Protection fills the deficits

Define the coordinate potential
$$ P_{j,k}=G_{j,k}+Z_{j,k}+R_{j,k}. $$
Before adding current protection, its change is
$$ (y_j-\alpha_j)t^j_k\le0. $$
Whenever protection is positive, it raises the potential to at most $\theta/c_k$. Consequently, from the first positive protection allocation onward, that coordinate’s potential remains at most target.

At termination, remaining marginal mass is zero. Thus
$$ Z_{N,k}\le s_k $$
for every coordinate; this also holds when that coordinate receives no protection.

If protection exhausts its budget,
$$ \kappa=\sum_kZ_{N,k}\le\sum_ks_k\le\kappa. $$
Equality forces $Z_{N,k}=s_k$ coordinatewise.

If protection remains slack, no request was truncated by its budget. Initially,
$$ P_{0,k}=a_k \ge\frac{O(\mathcal I)}{c_k} \ge\frac{\theta}{c_k}. $$
Assuming the previous potential is at least target, its decline during the current cohort is at most $\alpha_jt^j_k$. The cohort cap therefore permits restoration to target. Induction gives
$$ P_{N,k}=G_{N,k}+Z_{N,k}\ge\frac{\theta}{c_k}. $$

In either case,
$$ c_k\sum_jx_jt^j_k \ge \frac{c_k}{2}(G_{N,k}+Z_{N,k}) \ge\frac{\theta}{2}. $$
This proves the lemma.

5. A finite policy achieving $1/C_d$

Use $\ell,U,H$ from Section 1 and put
$$ \zeta=\frac U\ell. $$
Then
$$ 1\le\zeta\le H\le d. $$

If $\zeta<2$, the advice-only policy achieves ratio at least $1/\zeta>1/2$. Assume henceforth that $\zeta\ge2$.

Let
$$ G=\max\{g\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}:g(g-1)\le\zeta\}, $$
and define
$$ C(\zeta)=2G-4+\frac{2\zeta}{G}. $$

For each $g=2,\ldots,G$, run the lemma’s policy with
$$ \theta_g=g\ell. $$
Its first hypothesis always holds:
$$ \theta_gH=g\ell H\le g\kappa. $$
Whenever
$$ g(g-1)\ell\le O(\mathcal I), $$
its second hypothesis holds, and its value is at least $g\ell/2$.

Include the advice-only policy, denoted policy zero, and assign weights
$$ w_0=\frac2{C(\zeta)}, $$
$$ w_g=\frac4{C(\zeta)} \quad(2\le g<G), $$
$$ w_G= \frac2{C(\zeta)} \left(\frac{\zeta}{G}-(G-1)\right). $$
These weights are nonnegative. Their numerators sum to
$$ 2+4(G-2)+2\left(\frac{\zeta}{G}-(G-1)\right) = C(\zeta), $$
so they sum to one.

Use the convex combination of their cohort allocations. It is feasible. Moreover, if individual policies have guaranteed values $v_i$, every coordinate’s weighted coverage in the mixture is at least $\sum_iw_iv_i$.

Let $j$ be the largest satisfied threshold among $2,\ldots,G$, or let $j=1$ if none is satisfied.

If $j<G$, the guaranteed contribution of policy zero and policies through $j$ is
$$ \frac{2\ell}{C(\zeta)} + \sum_{g=2}^j\frac4{C(\zeta)}\frac{g\ell}{2} = \frac{\ell j(j+1)}{C(\zeta)}. $$
The next threshold fails, so
$$ O(\mathcal I)<j(j+1)\ell. $$
Hence the competitive ratio is at least $1/C(\zeta)$.

If $j=G$, the policies below $G$ contribute
$$ \frac{\ell G(G-1)}{C(\zeta)}, $$
and policy $G$ contributes
$$ \frac{\ell}{C(\zeta)} \bigl(\zeta-G(G-1)\bigr). $$
Their sum is
$$ \frac{\zeta\ell}{C(\zeta)} = \frac U{C(\zeta)} \ge \frac{O(\mathcal I)}{C(\zeta)}. $$

Thus the finite mixture achieves $1/C(\zeta)$.

5.1 Uniformizing the guarantee

On each interval
$$ g(g-1)\le\zeta<g(g+1), $$
the function $C(\zeta)$ equals
$$ 2g-4+\frac{2\zeta}{g}, $$
and is increasing. At the boundary $\zeta=g(g+1)$, the formulas for $g$ and $g+1$ both equal $4g-2$. Thus $C(\zeta)$ is continuous and increasing on $[2,\infty)$.

Since $\zeta\le d$,
$$ C(\zeta)\le C(d)=C_d. $$
Also $C_d\ge2$, so the case $\zeta<2$ provides at least the same uniform guarantee. Therefore
$$ R^*(d)\ge\frac1{C_d}. $$

Finally, the definition of $s_d$ gives
$$ s_d(s_d-1)\le d<s_d(s_d+1), $$
and hence
$$ s_d-1<\sqrt d<s_d+1. $$
Using
$$ C_d = 4\sqrt d-4+ \frac{2(s_d-\sqrt d)^2}{s_d}, $$
together with $s_d\ge2$, yields
$$ C_d<4\sqrt d-4+\frac2{s_d}\le4\sqrt d-3. $$

6. A new upper-bound family with linear support size

Fix integers $q\ge2$ and $1\le h<q$. All coefficients are one. Choose a dyadic capacity satisfying
$$ 2q\kappa<1. $$

On the $q$ coordinates arranged cyclically, let the common prefix contain $q$ cohorts of mass $\kappa$, whose types are the $q$ cyclic blocks of $h$ consecutive coordinates. Every coordinate belongs to exactly $h$ prefix types.

There are two possible suffixes.

  • Joint suffix: one all-coordinate cohort of mass $\kappa$, followed by zero-type mass $1-(q+1)\kappa$.
  • Singleton suffix: one singleton cohort of mass $\kappa$ for each coordinate, followed by zero-type mass $1-2q\kappa$.

Both instances have advice
$$ a_k=(h+1)\kappa $$
for every coordinate. Their initial information and entire prefixes are identical.

6.1 Offline values

For the joint suffix, selecting its joint cohort gives
$$ O_{\mathrm{joint}}=\kappa. $$

For the singleton suffix, every nonzero type has at most $h$ coordinates. Thus total coordinate coverage is at most $h\kappa$, giving
$$ O_{\mathrm{single}}\le\frac{h\kappa}{q}. $$
Selecting mass $\kappa/q$ from every prefix cohort attains equality:
$$ O_{\mathrm{single}}=\frac{h\kappa}{q}. $$

6.2 Policy tradeoff

Let $P$ be the policy’s expected total prefix selection, and write $p=P/\kappa\in[0,1]$.

The sum of expected prefix coordinate coverages is $hP$. In the joint instance, expected suffix selection is at most $\kappa-P$. Therefore its minimum coordinate coverage is at most
$$ \frac{hP}{q}+\kappa-P. $$
Its ratio is consequently at most
$$ 1-\left(1-\frac hq\right)p. $$

In the singleton instance, the sum of final expected coordinate coverages is at most
$$ hP+\kappa-P. $$
Its ratio is at most
$$ \frac{1+(h-1)p}{h}. $$

Thus every policy has ratio on at least one instance at most
$$ \max_{0\le p\le1} \min\left\{ 1-\left(1-\frac hq\right)p, \frac{1+(h-1)p}{h} \right\}. $$

For $h>1$, the decreasing and increasing affine functions meet at
$$ p= \frac{h-1}{2h-1-h^2/q}, $$
which lies in $[0,1]$. Substitution gives
$$ \frac{h(q-1)}{q(2h-1)-h^2}. $$
For $h=1$, the same expression equals one and the bound holds directly.

The worse instance is selected using expected prefix behavior, independently of realized random choices. The adversary is therefore oblivious.

Taking $q=3,h=2$ proves
$$ R^*(3)\le\frac45. $$
Taking $q=4,h=2$ gives $R^*(4)\le3/4$.

For any $d\ge q$, duplicate an existing coordinate until the instance has $d$ coordinates. The duplicated coordinate has the same coefficient and exactly the same coverage, so both objectives remain unchanged. This proves all the cyclic-family bounds appearing in $B_d$.

Both instances use at most $2q+1$ cohorts and support types.

7. The grouped-prefix upper bound

For completeness, here is a self-contained proof of the square-root upper bound retained from the notes.

Fix $s\ge2$ with $s(s-1)\le d$, and initially use $s(s-1)$ coordinates. Partition them into $s$ groups $G_1,\ldots,G_s$, each of size
$$ h=s-1. $$
All coefficients are one. Choose dyadic $\kappa$ with $2s\kappa<1$.

The common prefix contains, for each group, one cohort of mass $\kappa$ whose type is that group’s indicator.

For each hidden group $m$, its suffix consists of:

  • singleton cohorts of mass $\kappa$ for the $h$ coordinates in $G_m$;
  • one cohort of mass $\kappa$ possessing all coordinates outside $G_m$;
  • zero-type mass $1-2s\kappa$.

Every coordinate has advice $2\kappa$, independently of $m$.
