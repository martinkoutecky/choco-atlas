The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a population of \(N\) agents represented by a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over reported locations in \([0,1]\), and two predicted facility locations, characterize the achievable consistency–robustness trade-off for anonymous, Pareto-efficient, strategy-proof mechanisms. Each individual deviation moves mass \(1/N\); evaluate minimum utility as \(1-\sup_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\min_{j\in\{1,2\}}|x-y_j|\) for chosen facilities \(y_1,y_2\), with consistency measured when the predictions are optimal and robustness over arbitrary predictions.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is a probability measure \(\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\delta_{x_i}\), with distinct rational locations \(x_i\in[0,1]\), positive binary-rational masses \(w_i\), and \(\sum_iw_i=1\). Its input is its support list; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. A positive integer \(N\), encoded in binary, specifies deviation granularity; masses need not be multiples of \(1/N\). Running times are measured in \(m=2\), \(\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), and \(\log N\).
Predictions are rational locations \(0\leq\pi_1\leq\pi_2\leq1\). A deterministic anonymous mechanism \(f_N(\mu,\pi)\) returns rational facilities \(0\leq y_1\leq y_2\leq1\), possibly coincident, and depends only on the reported measure and predictions. A type at \(x\) has utility \(u_x(y)=1-\min_{j\in\{1,2\}}|x-y_j|\).
For every fixed prediction, require Pareto efficiency with respect to all positive-mass types and strategy-proofness under every feasible move \(\mu'=\mu-N^{-1}\delta_x+N^{-1}\delta_z\): for every \(x\) with \(\mu(\{x\})\geq1/N\) and every rational report \(z\in[0,1]\), require \(u_x(f_N(\mu,\pi))\geq u_x(f_N(\mu',\pi))\). Predictions remain fixed during deviations; zero-mass types are removed.

Partial classification with sharper lower bounds

I interpret binary-rational masses as rational numbers represented in binary. The full achievable region remains unresolved. The results below sharpen the necessary bounds and determine the exact infimum of robustness for $N=2$.

Define
$$ b_0=\frac{3+\sqrt{15}}6. $$

The principal conclusions are:

| Granularity | Proved necessary conditions | Proved achievability |
|---|---|---|
| $N=1$ | Only the stipulated $\alpha,\beta\geq1$ | Every such pair |
| $N=2$ | $\beta\geq b_0$ and $\beta\geq1+\frac1{6\alpha}$ | Every $(b,b)$ with $b>b_0$; also the parameterized families below |
| $N\geq3$ | $\beta\geq\frac76$ | $(\frac32,\frac32)$ |

In particular,
$$ \inf\{\beta:\text{an admissible mechanism has robustness }\beta\} =b_0 \qquad\text{for }N=2. $$

Whether this infimum is attained with rational outputs remains open. All necessary bounds below apply without using anonymity.

1. Distance notation and elementary geometry

For an ordered facility pair $y=(y_1,y_2)$, write
$$ d_x(y)=\min\{|x-y_1|,|x-y_2|\}, $$
$$ R(\mu,y)=\max_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}d_x(y), \qquad R^*(\mu)=\min_y R(\mu,y). $$
Thus
$$ U(\mu,y)=1-R(\mu,y), \qquad U^*(\mu)=1-R^*(\mu). $$

Pareto efficiency means that no facility pair weakly decreases every positive-mass type’s distance and strictly decreases at least one such distance.

The pair $(1/4,3/4)$ covers $[0,1]$ within radius $1/4$. Consequently,
$$ 0\leq R^*(\mu)\leq\frac14. $$

For three distinct support points $a<b<c$,
$$ R^*(\{a,b,c\})=\frac12\min\{b-a,c-b\}. $$
Indeed, two of the three points must share a nearest facility. Their separation is at least the smaller adjacent gap, yielding the lower bound. Placing one facility at the midpoint of a closest adjacent pair and the other at the remaining point attains it.

We shall use two Pareto-efficiency observations.

Observation 1. If a Pareto-efficient pair for $\{a,b,c\}$ contains the middle point $b$, its other facility is $a$ or $c$.

If the other facility lies below $b$, moving it to $a$ gives type $a$ distance zero, preserves type $b$’s zero distance, and preserves type $c$’s service by $b$. This is a strict improvement unless the other facility was already $a$. The case above $b$ is symmetric. Coincident facilities at $b$ are also dominated.

Observation 2. A Pareto-efficient pair for $\{0,b,1\}$ contains $0$ or $1$.

Suppose both facilities are strictly interior. If the middle type is served by the left facility, move the right facility to $1$. The left facility preserves service for $0$ and $b$, while type $1$ strictly improves. If the middle type is served by the right facility, move the left facility to $0$. A nearest-facility tie permits either argument.

2. A range lemma forced by strategy-proofness and Pareto efficiency

Fix a prediction, a rational $b\in(0,1)$, and positive rational numbers $A,B,q$ satisfying
$$ A+B+q=1. $$
Consider the family
$$ \mu_x=A\delta_0+B\delta_b+q\delta_x, \qquad x\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1], $$
where the allowed deviation size is $q=1/N$.

Every change from $\mu_x$ to $\mu_z$ is a feasible move of one $q$-chunk. Let
$$ F(x)=f_N(\mu_x,\pi). $$

Lemma 2.1: the right facility has a truncated identity form

There is a rational $C\in[b,1]$ such that, for every rational $x\in[b,1]$,
$$ F(x)_2=\min\{x,C\}. $$

Proof. First, Pareto efficiency gives
$$ 0\leq F(x)_1\leq b\leq F(x)_2\leq x \qquad (x\geq b). $$

At $x=b$, the support is $\{0,b\}$, so Pareto efficiency forces $(0,b)$.

For $x>b$, the upper bound on the right facility follows as follows. If the left facility exceeds $x$, moving it to $x$ improves every type. If the left facility is below $x$ and the right facility exceeds $x$, moving the latter to $x$ weakly improves every type and strictly improves type $x$. If the left facility equals $x$, the other facility is unused; moving it to $0$ strictly improves type $0$.

If the left facility exceeds $b$, moving it to $b$ improves types $0,b$ and preserves type $x$’s service by the right facility. If the right facility is below $b$, moving it to $b$ improves types $b,x$ and preserves type $0$’s service by the left facility. This proves the displayed bounds.

Put
$$ Y(x)=F(x)_2, \qquad C=Y(1). $$
For $x\geq b$, the true movable type’s distance is $x-Y(x)$.

Strategy-proofness for type $1$ implies
$$ 1-C\leq1-Y(z) \qquad(z\in\mathbb Q\cap[b,1]), $$
so $C$ is the maximum value of $Y$.

We next prove $Y(x)=x$ for every rational $b<x<C$. Suppose instead that
$$ v=Y(x)<x. $$
Let
$$ \mathcal R=\{Y(z):z\in\mathbb Q\cap[b,1]\}. $$
Strategy-proofness at $x$ forbids
$$ \mathcal R\cap(v,2x-v)\neq\varnothing, $$
because any facility coordinate in that interval would be strictly closer to $x$ than its truthful distance $x-v$.

Since $C>x$, the set $\mathcal R\cap(v,1]$ is nonempty. Define
$$ c=\inf\bigl(\mathcal R\cap(v,1]\bigr). $$
Then
$$ c\geq2x-v>v. $$
Choose rational $t$ with
$$ \frac{v+c}{2}<t<c. $$
Because $Y(t)\leq t$ and $\mathcal R$ contains no value in $(v,c)$, we have $Y(t)\leq v$.

By the definition of $c$, some $r\in\mathcal R$ satisfies
$$ c\leq r<2t-v. $$
A report producing that facility coordinate gives true type $t$ distance at most
$$ r-t<t-v\leq t-Y(t), $$
contradicting strategy-proofness. Therefore $Y(x)=x$ for rational $b<x<C$.

Because $C$ is rational, reporting $1$ at $\mu_C$ gives distance zero, so strategy-proofness forces $Y(C)=C$. Finally, for $x>C$, reporting $C$ gives distance $x-C$. Hence
$$ x-Y(x)\leq x-C. $$
Together with $Y(x)\leq C$, this gives $Y(x)=C$. ∎

This lemma is the key additional restriction: Pareto efficiency prevents gaps in the facility range outside the fixed support.

3. Robustness restricts that range

Define
$$ Q(t)=\frac{1-t/2}{1-t} \qquad(0\leq t<1). $$
This function is strictly increasing, since
$$ Q'(t)=\frac1{2(1-t)^2}>0. $$

For $\beta\geq1$, put
$$ h_\beta=\frac{\beta-1}{\beta-1/2}. $$
Then
$$ Q(t)\leq\beta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\leq h_\beta. $$

Continue with the family from Section 2, and assume $\beta$-robustness. Let $\mathcal D$ be the set of all facility coordinates appearing in $F(z)$ for rational $z\in[0,1]$.

If $a\in\mathcal D\cap(0,b)$, a report produces a facility at $a$. Strategy-proofness at $\mu_a$ therefore forces a truthful facility at $a$. Observation 1 then forces the other facility to be $0$ or $b$.

Writing
$$ h=\min\{a,b-a\}, $$
we obtain
$$ R(\mu_a,F(a))\geq h, \qquad R^*(\mu_a)=h/2. $$
Thus robustness requires $Q(h)\leq\beta$, or $h\leq h_\beta$. Consequently, whenever $b>2h_\beta$,
$$ \mathcal D\cap(h_\beta,b-h_\beta)=\varnothing. $$

Lemma 3.1: an upper bound on the external range

Suppose
$$ 2h_\beta<b\leq\frac12. $$
For the constant $C$ in Lemma 2.1,
$$ C\leq b+h_\beta. $$

Proof. Suppose $C>b+h_\beta$. Choose rational $\varepsilon$ satisfying
$$ h_\beta<\varepsilon< \min\{C-b,b-h_\beta\}. $$
This interval is nonempty because both upper bounds exceed $h_\beta$.

At report $x=b+\varepsilon$, Lemma 2.1 gives a facility exactly at $x$. Write the other facility as $a\in[0,b]$. The forbidden-coordinate interval above implies
$$ a\leq h_\beta \quad\text{or}\quad a\geq b-h_\beta. $$

In the first case, type $b$ has distance exactly $\varepsilon$, since
$$ b-a\geq b-h_\beta>\varepsilon. $$
In the second case, type $0$ has distance at least $b-h_\beta>\varepsilon$. Hence
$$ R(\mu_{b+\varepsilon},F(b+\varepsilon))\geq\varepsilon. $$
The adjacent support gaps are $b$ and $\varepsilon<b$, so
$$ R^*(\mu_{b+\varepsilon})=\varepsilon/2. $$
The utility approximation ratio is therefore at least
$$ Q(\varepsilon)>Q(h_\beta)=\beta, $$
a contradiction. ∎

4. A consistency-dependent lower bound for every $N\geq2$

Theorem 4.1

Every admissible $\alpha$-consistent, $\beta$-robust mechanism with $N\geq2$ satisfies
$$ \boxed{\beta\geq1+\frac1{6\alpha}.} $$

Proof. If $\beta\geq7/6$, the conclusion follows from $\alpha\geq1$. Assume therefore that $\beta<7/6$. Then
$$ h_\beta<\frac14. $$

Put $q=1/N$ and, for rational $b$ satisfying
$$ 2h_\beta<b<\frac12, $$
consider
$$ \mu_x=\frac{1-q}{2}\delta_0+ \frac{1-q}{2}\delta_b+ q\delta_x. $$
The two fixed masses are positive because $N\geq2$.

Fix prediction
$$ \pi=(b/2,1). $$
At $\mu_1$, the smaller adjacent support gap is $b$, so
$$ R^*(\mu_1)=b/2. $$
The prediction attains this radius and is correct.

Let $C=F(1)_2$. Since type $1$ has distance $1-C$,
$$ U(\mu_1,F(1))\leq C. $$
Consistency and Lemma 3.1 give
$$ \frac{1-b/2}{\alpha} \leq U(\mu_1,F(1)) \leq C \leq b+h_\beta. $$
Thus
$$ \alpha(b+h_\beta)\geq1-b/2. $$

Let rational $b$ decrease to $2h_\beta$. This yields
$$ 3\alpha h_\beta\geq1-h_\beta. $$
Substituting the definition of $h_\beta$ and multiplying by the positive denominator $\beta-1/2$ gives
$$ 3\alpha(\beta-1)\geq\frac12. $$
This is precisely the asserted inequality. ∎

Corollary 4.2: a stronger universal robustness bound

Every admissible mechanism with $N\geq2$ satisfies
$$ \boxed{\beta\geq b_0=\frac{3+\sqrt{15}}6.} $$

Proof. A $\beta$-robust mechanism is automatically $\beta$-consistent. Apply Theorem 4.1 with consistency factor $\alpha=\beta$:
$$ \beta\geq1+\frac1{6\beta}. $$
Equivalently,
$$ 6\beta^2-6\beta-1\geq0. $$
The positive root is $b_0$, and the other root is negative. ∎

This improves the earlier lower bound $(4+\sqrt7)/6$.

5. A stronger robustness obstruction for $N\geq3$

Theorem 5.1

For every $N\geq3$, every admissible mechanism satisfies
$$ \boxed{\beta\geq\frac76.} $$

Proof. Suppose $\beta<7/6$, so $h_\beta<1/4$. Put $q=1/N$ and consider
$$ \mu_\star=q\delta_0+(1-2q)\delta_{1/2}+q\delta_1. $$
All three masses are positive.

Fix any prediction. First move the chunk initially at $1$, keeping the other masses fixed. This is the family
$$ q\delta_0+(1-2q)\delta_{1/2}+q\delta_x. $$
Lemma 3.1, with $b=1/2$, gives
$$ f_N(\mu_\star,\pi)_2\leq\frac12+h_\beta. $$

Next move the chunk initially at $0$, keeping the other masses fixed. Reflecting locations through $x\mapsto1-x$ and applying the same lemma gives
$$ f_N(\mu_\star,\pi)_1\geq\frac12-h_\beta. $$
Reflection preserves distances, Pareto efficiency, feasible moves, and robustness.

Since $h_\beta<1/4$, both facilities are strictly inside $(0,1)$. Observation 2 says that no such pair is Pareto efficient for support $\{0,1/2,1\}$. This contradiction proves the theorem. ∎

The obstruction uses two independently movable endpoint chunks. It is unavailable for $N=2$, because their combined mass would already exhaust the probability measure.

6. Residual masses and basic constructions

Put $q=1/N$. At each support location, write
$$ \mu(\{x\})=qk_x+r_x, \qquad k_x=\lfloor N\mu(\{x\})\rfloor, \qquad 0\leq r_x<q. $$
Define
$$ \rho=\sum_x r_x\delta_x, \qquad K=\sum_x k_x. $$
Then
$$ \mu=\rho+q\sum_x k_x\delta_x, \qquad \rho([0,1])=1-Kq, \qquad 0\leq K\leq N. $$

Moving a $q$-chunk decreases one integer count and increases another; all remainders remain unchanged. Thus $\rho$ and $K$ are invariant under every feasible deviation.

Pareto-efficient optimal selection

A deterministic optimal selector can lexicographically minimize
$$ \left( R(\mu,y), \sum_x\mu(\{x\})d_x(y), y_1,y_2 \right). $$
The successive minimizations attain their minima on compact sets. Any Pareto improvement weakly decreases the first objective and strictly decreases the second, because all support masses are positive. Hence this selector is Pareto efficient.

Rationality and polynomial-time implementation are justified in Section 8.

Complete resolution for $N=1$

If a society has at least two support points, every mass is strictly below $1$, so no deviation is feasible. A singleton society receives utility $1$ under the optimal selector and cannot improve.

The optimal Pareto-efficient selector is therefore strategy-proof for $N=1$ and achieves $(1,1)$. Consequently every allowed pair is achievable.

A universal $(3/2,3/2)$ mechanism

For every $N$, return the support endpoints:
$$ f_N(\mu,\pi)= \left( \min\operatorname{supp}(\mu), \max\operatorname{supp}(\mu) \right). $$

Both endpoint types receive utility $1$, proving Pareto efficiency. An endpoint type cannot improve. When an interior type deviates, the original endpoints remain present; they either remain unchanged or move outward. Its distance to the endpoint pair cannot decrease. This proves strategy-proofness.

Let the endpoint radius be $r$. Then $r\leq1/2$. If $r>0$, choose a support point attaining it. Together with the endpoints, these are three points whose pairwise separations are at least $r$. Two share a facility in any optimal pair, so
$$ R^*(\mu)\geq r/2. $$
Therefore
$$ \frac{U^*(\mu)}{U(\mu,f_N(\mu,\pi))} \leq\frac{1-r/2}{1-r} \leq\frac32. $$
The support $\{0,1/2,1\}$ attains this factor. Because the mechanism ignores predictions, the bound applies to consistency and robustness.

7. Menu mechanisms and sharp robustness infimum for $N=2$

For $N=2$, the invariant $K$ is $0$, $1$, or $2$.

  • If $K=0$, there are no feasible deviations; use the Pareto-efficient optimum.
  • If $K=2$, the residual measure is zero, and there are at most two support locations; return the endpoints, giving every type utility $1$.
  • If $K=1$, there is a unique representation

$$ \mu=\rho+\frac12\delta_x. $$

Only the last case requires a construction.

7.1. A fixed menu

Let $S=\operatorname{supp}(\rho)$ and define
$$ c=\min_y\max_{s\in S}d_s(y). $$
For a rational threshold satisfying
$$ 0<t\leq\frac14, \qquad c\leq t, $$
use the menu
$$ \mathcal M_t(S)= \{y:0\leq y_1\leq y_2\leq1,\ d_s(y)\leq t\text{ for all }s\in S\}. $$
Select the lexicographic minimum of
$$ \left( d_x(y), \max_{s\in S}d_s(y), \sum_{s\in S}\rho(\{s\})d_s(y), y_1,y_2 \right). $$

The menu is nonempty and compact. It remains fixed during deviations. The truthful outcome minimizes the movable type’s distance over that menu, so strategy-proofness follows.

For Pareto efficiency, any dominating outcome remains in the menu. A strict improvement for the movable type contradicts the first objective. Otherwise, the residual maximum weakly decreases. If it strictly decreases, the second objective is contradicted; if it does not, any strict improvement for a residual type strictly decreases the positively weighted third objective. Thus the selected outcome is Pareto efficient.

7.2. Two radius bounds

Write $r=R^*(\mu)$.

Anchoring bound. There is a pair containing $x$ with overall radius at most $2r$.

Take an optimal pair and replace a facility nearest to $x$ by $x$. Its displacement is at most $r$. The triangle inequality gives radius at most $2r$ for the points previously served by it; all other points retain radius at most $r$.

Consequently, if $2r\leq t$, the menu selector has movable distance zero, and its second objective gives
$$ R(\mu,f_N(\mu,\pi))\leq2r. $$

Uniform menu bound. For every $x\in[0,1]$, the menu contains a pair with
$$ d_x(y)\leq1-3t. $$

To prove this, let $a=\min S$ and $b=\max S$. If $b-a\leq2t$, one facility covers $S$ and the other can be placed at $x$.

Suppose $b-a>2t$. If $a>1-4t$, the pair $(1-3t,1-t)$ covers $S$ within radius $t$.

Otherwise, set $y_1=a+t$. Let $s$ be the smallest support point strictly above $a+2t$. Every support point from $s$ onward must lie in the other interval of any feasible two-interval cover: an interval containing $a$ ends no later than $a+2t$. Hence their diameter is at most $2t$. Set
$$ y_2=\min\{s+t,1-t\}. $$
This covers the remaining support.

In either construction,
$$ t\leq y_1\leq1-3t, \qquad 3t\leq y_2\leq1-t. $$
Outside $[y_1,y_2]$, distance to the pair is at most $1-3t$. Inside it, distance is at most
$$ \frac{y_2-y_1}{2} \leq\frac12-t \leq1-3t, $$
where the last inequality uses $t\leq1/4$.

Since the selector minimizes $d_x$ and keeps residual distances at most $t$,
$$ R(\mu,f_N(\mu,\pi))\leq1-3t, \qquad U(\mu,f_N(\mu,\pi))\geq3t. $$

The restriction $t>0$ explicitly repairs the zero-threshold issue identified in the notes.

7.3. Approximation analysis

Define
$$ A(t)=\frac{1-t/2}{1-t}, \qquad C(t)=\frac{1-t}{3t}. $$

If $r\leq t/2$, the anchoring bound yields
$$ \frac{U^*(\mu)}{U(\mu,f_N(\mu,\pi))} \leq\frac{1-r}{1-2r} \leq A(t). $$

If $t/2<r\leq t$, an optimal pair belongs to the menu. The selected movable distance is at most $r$, and its residual radius is at most $t$. Thus the overall radius is at most $t$, giving
$$ \frac{U^*(\mu)}{U(\mu,f_N(\mu,\pi))} \leq\frac{1-r}{1-t} \leq A(t). $$

If $r>t$, the uniform bound gives
$$ \frac{U^*(\mu)}{U(\mu,f_N(\mu,\pi))} \leq\frac{1-r}{3t} \leq C(t). $$

The resulting approximation factor is at most
$$ \max\{A(t),C(t)\}. $$

7.4. Prediction-independent family

Fix rational $0<\gamma\leq1/4$ and choose
$$ t=\max\{\gamma,c\}. $$
This achieves
$$ (\alpha,\beta)=(B(\gamma),B(\gamma)), $$
where
$$ B(\gamma)= \max\left\{ \frac{1-\gamma/2}{1-\gamma}, \frac{1-\gamma}{3\gamma} \right\}. $$

If $c\leq\gamma$, this follows directly from the preceding analysis. If $c>\gamma$, then $t=c$ and $r\geq c$. At $r=c$, an optimal pair belongs to the menu and the selected outcome is optimal. At $r>c$, its ratio is at most $C(c)\leq C(\gamma)$.

The function $A$ is increasing and $C$ is decreasing. Their intersection is
$$ \gamma_0=1-\sqrt{\frac35}, \qquad B(\gamma_0)=b_0. $$
Rational thresholds approach $\gamma_0$, and continuity shows that every $(b,b)$ with $b>b_0$ is achievable.

Combined with Corollary 4.2, this proves the claimed exact robustness infimum for $N=2$. It does not prove attainment at $b_0$.

7.5. Prediction-sensitive family

Fix rational $0<\gamma\leq1/4$. Set
$$ p=\max_{s\in S}d_s(\pi), \qquad t=\max\{\gamma,\min\{p,1/4\}\}. $$
Because $p\geq c$ and $c\leq1/4$, the menu is feasible.

This achieves
$$ \boxed{ \alpha=A(\gamma), \qquad \beta=\max\left\{\frac76,C(\gamma)\right\}. } $$

For robustness, $t\in[\gamma,1/4]$. Monotonicity gives
$$ A(t)\leq A(1/4)=\frac76, \qquad C(t)\leq C(\gamma). $$

For consistency, let the prediction be correct, and put $r=R^*(\mu)$. Then
$$ p\leq r\leq1/4. $$
If $r\geq\gamma$, we have $t\leq r$, and the prediction belongs to the menu. The selected movable distance and residual radius are both at most $r$, so the outcome is optimal.

If $r<\gamma$, then $t=\gamma$. For $r\leq\gamma/2$, the anchoring bound gives factor at most $A(\gamma)$. For $\gamma/2<r<\gamma$, the correct prediction belongs to the menu, so the overall radius is at most $\gamma$ and the same factor follows.

The argument uses only the prediction’s optimal radius, and therefore covers every correct prediction, including nonunique optima.

For example, $\gamma=2/9$ gives
$$ (\alpha,\beta)=\left(\frac87,\frac76\right). $$

Because $K$ and $\rho$ are invariant, combining these menu constructions with the $K=0$ and $K=2$ rules preserves strategy-proofness on the entire $N=2$ mass domain.

8. Rational outputs and computation

All constructions above are deterministic and anonymous. They use only the measure, the fixed prediction, and specified tie-breaking objectives.

For completeness, their optimization rules admit rational outputs and polynomial-time implementation without expanding the society into $N$ chunks.

For each support point $s$, the distance function is obtained by taking maxima and minima of the four affine functions
$$ s-y_1,\quad y_1-s,\quad s-y_2,\quad y_2-s. $$
Partition the ordered facility triangle by the equality lines between all these affine functions. There are polynomially many lines. On each resulting cell, every support distance, its maximum, and each weighted distance sum is affine.

For a menu, also include the lines where any of these affine functions equals $t$. Its feasible region is then a union of closed cells. Successive minimization of affine objectives on a compact polygon selects faces; the final coordinate objectives select a vertex. Hence it suffices to enumerate intersections of the arrangement lines and domain boundaries, retain feasible vertices, and compare the actual objective tuples.

There are polynomially many candidate intersections. With rational locations, masses, and thresholds, their coordinates and objective values are rational with polynomial encoding length. This proves rationality and polynomial-time implementability of the optimal selectors, residual optimum $c$, and menu selectors.

The arithmetic for
$$ k_x=\lfloor N\mu(\{x\})\rfloor $$
and $r_x$ uses binary rational operations. Thus running times are polynomial in $\tau$, $L$, and $\log N$, with a fixed rational mechanism parameter $\gamma$.

9. What remains open

These results do not give a necessary-and-sufficient classification.

For $N=2$, the former robustness gap is closed at the level of the infimum. Boundary attainment remains unresolved, as does achievability throughout the consistency-dependent region allowed by
