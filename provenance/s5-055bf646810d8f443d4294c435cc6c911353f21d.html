The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite complete type set \(\Theta\), distribution \(\mu\), type-level disutilities \(v_\theta(q)\), and an online per-capita arrival stream of chore classes, determine whether there is a nonanticipating family of irrevocable policies for \(N\mu_\theta\) agents and \(N\)-scaled arrivals such that, at every normalized prefix, \(\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{\sigma_N}\max_a v_{\theta(a)}(B_a)/\operatorname{MMS}^{(N)}_{\theta(a)}\le\alpha\); minimize \(\alpha\), with the \(k\)-value and \(D\) restrictions for Theorems 1 and 6 and without them for Theorem 5.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_\theta\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) chore classes \(Q\), with a given table of additive disutilities \(v_\theta(q)\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\); a bundle's disutility is the sum over its chore occurrences. The support and table remain fixed as population size grows. Let \(L\) be their total binary encoding length.
For each integer \(N\geq2\) with all \(N\mu_\theta\) integral, there are exactly \(N\mu_\theta\) agents of type \(\theta\). Each arrival is one indivisible chore, retaining its table disutilities without rescaling.
A deterministic policy \(\pi_N\) knows the support and table, but no future arrivals or stopping time; it immediately and irrevocably assigns every arriving chore to one agent, using only the observed history. The adversary may choose each next class after observing previous assignments.
At normalized prefix \(t>0\), let \(\sigma_N\in Q^{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}\) range over all class histories of that length, and let \(B_a^{\pi_N}(\sigma_N)\) be agent \(a\)'s assigned bundle. Thus arrival counts scale with \(N\), with no prescribed class frequencies.
Let \(\operatorname{MMS}^{(N)}_\theta(\sigma_N)\) be the standard chore minmax share of the arrived chore occurrences, using \(N\) bundles, including empty bundles, and type \(\theta\)'s disutilities. Set \(0/0=0\).
A single family \((\pi_N)_N\) attains \(\alpha\geq1\) if, for every \(t>0\), \(\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{\sigma_N\in Q^{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}}\max_a \frac{v_{\theta(a)}(B_a^{\pi_N}(\sigma_N))}{\operatorname{MMS}^{(N)}_{\theta(a)}(\sigma_N)}\leq\alpha\), where the limit uses only admissible population sizes. There is no imposed bound on distinct disutilities or their positive-value ratios across inputs.
State algorithmic running times in \(m,\tau,L,N\), and the number \(j\) of arrivals processed.

What is the infimum attainable asymptotic MMS ratio \(\alpha^*(\Theta,\mu,v)\) in this model, and its sharp worst-case value over the permitted inputs?

For unrestricted finite populations with disutilities revealed on arrival, the optimal deterministic online MMS ratio is \(n\): every allocation attains \(n\), and every guarantee \(n-\varepsilon\) is impossible ([Song et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14039v2#S4)). An independent result establishes the same lower bound ([Seddighin and Seddighin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12984)).
No result settling this fixed-support, known-table replication limit was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational support masses to sum to one; population sizes range over integers making every type count integral.
  • Made the chore-class domain a finite explicit table of nonnegative binary rational additive disutilities, fixed independently of population size; the table is known initially, as suggested by the given complete type-level data.
  • Interpreted the unspecified arrival supremum as all adaptive class histories of length floor(Nt), with no prescribed class frequencies or advance knowledge of the stopping time.
  • Retained indivisible chore occurrences and their unscaled disutilities; N-scaled arrivals refer to their counts.
  • Used deterministic policies and required one policy family to satisfy the guarantee at every positive normalized prefix.
  • Computed each prefix MMS using all N agents as the number of bundles, allowing empty bundles; assigned zero to the zero-over-zero ratio, which is the only possible zero-denominator case under nonnegative additive disutilities.
  • Applied the unrestricted anchor, imposing no uniform k-value or disutility-ratio restriction across inputs.

1. Benchmark bounds and admissible populations

Because all masses are positive rationals, their common denominator divides infinitely many population sizes. Thus the set of admissible populations is unbounded.

Fix an admissible population size $N$ and a prefix with class counts $c_q$. Write
$$ W_\theta=\sum_q c_qv_\theta(q), \qquad M_\theta=\operatorname{MMS}^{(N)}_\theta, \qquad P_\theta=\max_{q:c_q>0}v_\theta(q). $$
For an empty prefix, set $P_\theta=0$.

Every partition into $N$ bundles has maximum disutility at least its average. Moreover, every occurrence belongs to a bundle, whose disutility is at least that occurrence’s disutility. Hence
$$ M_\theta\geq \frac{W_\theta}{N}, \qquad M_\theta\geq P_\theta. $$

If $M_\theta=0$, then $W_\theta=0$. Nonnegativity implies that every arrived occurrence has zero disutility for type $\theta$, so every bundle assigned to that type also has zero disutility. The prescribed convention therefore handles all zero denominators.

Indivisibility persists in the replication limit. For one class of disutility $1$, at $t=3/2$,
$$ \operatorname{MMS}^{(N)} =\left\lceil\frac{\lfloor3N/2\rfloor}{N}\right\rceil =2, $$
whereas average disutility converges to $3/2$.

2. A deterministic family attaining ratio $2$

2.1 Policy and routing discrepancy

Fix orderings of types and agent labels. Route each class independently.

For the $k$-th occurrence of a class, let $a_\theta(k-1)$ be the number of its previous occurrences routed to type $\theta$. Select the first type satisfying
$$ \mu_\theta k-a_\theta(k-1)>0. $$
Such a type exists because
$$ \sum_\theta\bigl(\mu_\theta k-a_\theta(k-1)\bigr)=1. $$
Within the selected type, assign the chore to a least-loaded agent, using that type’s disutilities and breaking ties by labels.

For every type and every $k$,
$$ a_\theta(k)-\mu_\theta k<1. $$
Indeed, the claim holds at $k=0$. For the selected type,
$$ a_\theta(k)-\mu_\theta k =1-\bigl(\mu_\theta k-a_\theta(k-1)\bigr)<1. $$
For an unselected type, its discrepancy decreases by $\mu_\theta>0$, so the induction hypothesis remains valid.

The policy is deterministic, irrevocable, and uses only the observed history.

2.2 Load guarantee

Type $\theta$ has
$$ K_\theta=N\mu_\theta $$
agents. Define
$$ C_\theta=\sum_qv_\theta(q), $$
and let $T_\theta$ be the total disutility of chores routed to that type, measured by its own row. The routing bound gives
$$ T_\theta =\sum_q a_{\theta q}v_\theta(q) \leq \mu_\theta W_\theta+C_\theta. $$

Consider an agent of type $\theta$ with positive final load. Let $p$ be its last positive-disutility chore, and let $S$ be the total load within that type immediately before this assignment. Its previous load was at most $S/K_\theta$, because it was least-loaded. Subsequent zero-disutility assignments do not change its load. Therefore
$$ v_\theta(B_a) \leq \frac{T_\theta}{K_\theta}+p \leq \frac{W_\theta}{N} +\frac{C_\theta}{N\mu_\theta} +P_\theta. $$
The same inequality holds for zero-load agents.

For each row containing a positive entry, put
$$ d_\theta=\min\{v_\theta(q):v_\theta(q)>0\}. $$
Whenever $M_\theta>0$, at least one positive occurrence has arrived, so $M_\theta\geq d_\theta$. Consequently,
$$ \frac{v_\theta(B_a)}{M_\theta} \leq 2+\frac{C_\theta}{N\mu_\theta d_\theta}. $$
Define
$$ H=\max_{\theta:\,d_\theta\text{ exists}} \frac{C_\theta}{\mu_\theta d_\theta}, $$
with $H=0$ when every row is zero. We obtain the uniform finite-population bound
$$ \max_a\frac{v_{\theta(a)}(B_a)} {\operatorname{MMS}^{(N)}_{\theta(a)}} \leq 2+\frac{H}{N}. $$

For each fixed input, $H$ is finite and independent of the history and prefix length. Taking the required supremum and limsup proves attainment of $2$ for every fixed $t>0$.

2.3 Running time

Use a common denominator $D$ for the masses, writing $D\mu_\theta=b_\theta$. Maintain an integer deficit for each class and type. On an occurrence of that class, add $b_\theta$ to every corresponding deficit, select the first positive deficit, and subtract $D$ from it.

Scale the disutility table to integers using another common denominator, and maintain a minimum heap for each type. Products of input denominators and scaled entries have $O(L)$ bits.

Put
$$ B=L+\left\lceil\log_2(N+j+2)\right\rceil. $$
Through $j$ arrivals, all counters and loads have $O(B)$ bits. Each arrival uses
$$ O\bigl(\tau+\log(N+1)\bigr) $$
integer additions and comparisons. A conservative total bit-operation bound is
$$ O\!\left( (m\tau+1)B^3+ \bigl(N+j(\tau+\log(N+1))\bigr)B \right). $$
Space usage is
$$ O\bigl((m\tau+N)B\bigr) $$
bits. The policy never computes MMS.

3. The bound $m$ and exact ratio-$1$ cases

Assign occurrences of each class cyclically among all $N$ agents, using an independent cyclic counter for each class. An agent receives at most $\lceil c_q/N\rceil$ occurrences of class $q$.

In every partition into $N$ bundles, some bundle contains at least $\lceil c_q/N\rceil$ occurrences of that class. Thus, for every type,
$$ M_\theta\geq v_\theta(q)\left\lceil\frac{c_q}{N}\right\rceil. $$
Summing these separate inequalities yields
$$ v_\theta(B_a) \leq \sum_qv_\theta(q)\left\lceil\frac{c_q}{N}\right\rceil \leq mM_\theta. $$
Together with Section 2, this proves
$$ \alpha^*(\Theta,\mu,v)\leq\min\{m,2\}. $$

The cyclic policy uses $O(m\log(N+1))$ additional bits and processes each arrival in $O(\log(N+1))$ bit operations after input processing.

For $m=1$, the benchmark is exactly
$$ M_\theta=v_\theta(q)\left\lceil\frac{j}{N}\right\rceil. $$
Cyclic assignment therefore attains $1$, proving $\alpha^*=1$.

Also, if every class has zero disutility for at least one supported type, assign that class to such a type. Every assigned agent’s own load is zero, and again $\alpha^*=1$.

Here the lower bound $\alpha^*\geq1$ follows from the definition’s restriction to guarantees $\alpha\geq1$.

4.4 Verification of the asymptotic quantifiers

Let $(\pi_N)_N$ be any policy family. For every population $N=200k$, its allocation on the displayed history has ratio at least $R$ at one of the nine fixed normalized endpoints.

By the infinite pigeonhole principle, some fixed endpoint $t_i$ witnesses this ratio for infinitely many such populations. For those populations, its history has exactly $\lfloor Nt_i\rfloor$ arrivals. Therefore
$$ \limsup_{N\to\infty} \sup_{\sigma_N\in Q^{\lfloor Nt_i\rfloor}} \max_a \frac{v(B_a^{\pi_N}(\sigma_N))} {\operatorname{MMS}^{(N)}(\sigma_N)} \geq R. $$

Thus no policy family attains any $\alpha<R$ on this fixed input. In particular,
$$ \alpha^*(\{1\},1,v)\geq\frac{3707}{2000}, \qquad A\geq\frac{3707}{2000}. $$
The proof applies regardless of the policy’s computational power.

5. An exact nontrivial instance

For one type and two classes with disutilities $1$ and $2$,
$$ \alpha^*=\frac32. $$

For the lower bound, first present $N$ unit chores. Their MMS is $1$. Either the policy creates load at least $2$, or every agent receives exactly one chore.

In the latter case, append $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ double chores. The endpoint is $t=3/2$. Its MMS is exactly $2$: pair the unit chores, retaining one singleton when $N$ is odd, and put each double chore alone. This uses exactly $N$ bundles. Every assignment of a double chore creates load at least $3$.

Thus, for every population, one of the fixed endpoints $1$ and $3/2$ has ratio at least $3/2$. The same finite-endpoint argument as above proves the asymptotic lower bound.

For the upper bound, assign every chore to a least-loaded agent. Let $C$ be the prefix MMS. It is an integer.

If $C=1$, there are only unit chores and at most $N$ of them, so maximum load is at most $1$. If $C\geq2$, choose a maximum-load agent and let $p\in\{1,2\}$ be its last chore. If its previous load was $\ell$, least-load assignment and the final total disutility $W$ give
$$ N\ell+p\leq W\leq NC. $$
Hence $\ell<C$, and integrality implies $\ell\leq C-1$. Its final load is at most
$$ \ell+p\leq C+1\leq\frac32C. $$

A minimum heap implements this policy through $j$ arrivals in
$$ O\bigl((N+j\log(N+1))\log(N+j+2)\bigr) $$
bit operations.
