The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite rational value set \(V=\{v_1>\cdots>v_\tau\ge 0\}\), a unit-mass society with distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(V)\), per-capita supply \(\rho\in(0,1)\), a finite menu of symmetric strategyproof and individually rational unit-demand rules with rational per-capita rank functionals \(U_j(q)\) and \(P_j(q)\), and a finite rational basis of report-independent rebate functionals \(B_k(q)\), choose a lottery \(\lambda\in\Delta_t\), a nonnegative rebate \(R(q)=\sum_k\theta_k B_k(q)\), and the largest \(\alpha\). Require, for every \(\mu\in\Delta(V)\), that \(R(q_\mu)\ge 0\), \(\sum_j\lambda_jP_j(q_\mu)\ge R(q_\mu)\), and \(\sum_j\lambda_j(U_j(q_\mu)-P_j(q_\mu))+R(q_\mu)\ge\alpha W_\rho(\mu)\), where \(q_\mu\) is the nonincreasing value quantile and \(W_\rho(\mu)=\int_0^\rho q_\mu(s)\,ds\). Return the optimal lottery, rebate, and guaranteed ratio.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix a rational value alphabet \(V=\{v_1>\cdots>v_m\ge0\}\) with \(v_1>0\), and rational supply \(0<\rho<1\). Agents are nonatomic, have unit demand and quasilinear utilities, and total mass is one.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types and their binary rational masses, summing to one; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. All rational societies supported in \(V\), including proper subsets, are admissible.
Let \(q_\mu\) be the right-continuous nonincreasing value quantile on \([0,1)\), with \(q_\mu(1)\) equal to the smallest positive-mass type, and let \(W_\rho(\mu)=\int_0^\rho q_\mu(s)\,ds\).
The input contains \(t\ge1\) promised symmetric, strategyproof, individually rational allocation rules, feasible for supply \(\rho\), with zero payment for a zero report; ties are treated symmetrically. Their expected per-capita allocated value and revenue are \(U_j(q)\) and \(P_j(q)\).
Each input functional \(F\), including the \(b\) rebate basis functionals \(B_k\), is explicitly encoded as \(F(q)=\int_0^1 f(s)q(s)\,ds+\sum_\ell c_\ell q(r_\ell)\), where \(f\) is a step function with rational breakpoints and signed rational heights, and \(c_\ell,r_\ell\) are rational with \(r_\ell\in[0,1]\).
Choose \(\lambda\in\Delta_t\), unrestricted coefficients \(\theta\in\mathbb R^b\), and \(R(q)=\sum_{k=1}^b\theta_kB_k(q)\). Every agent receives the same rebate \(R(q_\mu)\); changing one agent’s report leaves the population distribution unchanged, so this rebate is report-independent.
Maximize \(\alpha\in\mathbb R\), subject, for every admissible \(\mu\), to \(R(q_\mu)\ge0\), \(\sum_j\lambda_jP_j(q_\mu)\ge R(q_\mu)\), and \(\sum_j\lambda_j(U_j(q_\mu)-P_j(q_\mu))+R(q_\mu)\ge\alpha W_\rho(\mu)\). Constraints use expectations over mechanism randomness. Return an optimal lottery, rebate coefficients, and ratio.

Is this optimization problem solvable in polynomial time, or is its decision version asking whether a feasible ratio \(\alpha\ge a\) exists, for a binary rational threshold \(a\), NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,t,b\) and total input bit length \(L\), uniformly over admissible supports \(\tau\le m\).

Result

The optimization problem is solvable in polynomial time by an exact rational linear program. Its decision version belongs to \(\mathrm P\).

The key fact is that every admissible quantile is a convex combination of quantiles supported on the two extreme values, \(\{v_1,v_m\}\). For these two-value societies, the encoded functionals are piecewise affine in a single mass parameter. Point evaluations require one-sided limiting constraints, but only polynomially many.

The argument below treats the stated nonatomic model directly. For the related finite-agent LP characterizations, see [Guo–Conitzer, expanded treatment](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~conitzer/betterAIJ14.pdf).

1. Reduction to societies with at most two types

First assume \(m\ge2\), and write \(h=v_1\), \(\ell=v_m\), and \(\delta=h-\ell>0\).

For rational \(x\in[0,1]\), let \(q_x\) be the quantile of the society with mass \(x\) at \(h\) and mass \(1-x\) at \(\ell\), omitting zero-mass types. Thus, for \(s<1\),

\(q_x(s)=\ell+\delta\,\mathbf 1_{\{s<x\}},\)

and the stipulated endpoint convention gives

\(q_x(1)=\ell+\delta\,\mathbf 1_{\{x=1\}}.\)

In particular, \(q_0\) and \(q_1\) are the constant-low and constant-high quantiles.

Lemma. Every admissible \(q_\mu\) is a convex combination of admissible quantiles of this form, with equality at every point of \([0,1]\).

Proof. Write \(p_i=\mu(\{v_i\})\), allowing \(p_i=0\), and put

\(S_i=\sum_{k=1}^{i}p_k,\qquad \Delta_i=v_i-v_{i+1}\quad(1\le i<m).\)

For \(s<1\), right continuity gives the identity

\(q_\mu(s)=v_m+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\Delta_i\mathbf 1_{\{s<S_i\}}.\)

Indeed, if \(q_\mu(s)=v_j\), then the indicators are zero for \(i<j\) and one for \(i\ge j\); the sum telescopes to \(v_j-v_m\).

This identity also respects the separately defined value at \(s=1\). If \(v_j\) is the smallest positive-mass type, then \(S_i=1\) exactly for \(i\ge j\). Consequently,

\(q_\mu(1)=v_m+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\Delta_i\mathbf 1_{\{S_i=1\}}.\)

Since \(\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\Delta_i=h-\ell=\delta\), it follows pointwise, including at \(1\), that

\(q_\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{\Delta_i}{\delta}\,q_{S_i}.\)

The coefficients are positive and sum to one. Each \(S_i\) is rational, so every \(q_{S_i}\) is admissible. This proves the lemma.

For fixed \((\lambda,\theta,\alpha)\), each required inequality is of the form \(H(q)\ge0\), where \(H\) is a linear functional:

  • \(H(q)=\sum_k\theta_kB_k(q)\);
  • \(H(q)=\sum_j\lambda_jP_j(q)-\sum_k\theta_kB_k(q)\);
  • \(H(q)=\sum_j\lambda_j(U_j(q)-P_j(q))+\sum_k\theta_kB_k(q)-\alpha W_\rho(q)\).

The lemma implies

\(H(q_\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{\Delta_i}{\delta}H(q_{S_i}).\)

Therefore, an inequality holds for every admissible society if and only if it holds for every rational two-value society \(q_x\).

This also proves that any violation has an admissible witness with support size at most two. The reduction uses the premise that proper subsets of the alphabet are admissible.

If \(m=1\), there is only the constant quantile \(q(s)=v_1\), and the universal constraints already reduce to three inequalities.

2. Exact finite reduction, including point evaluations

Consider an encoded functional

\(F(q)=\int_0^1 f(s)q(s)\,ds+\sum_\nu c_\nu q(r_\nu).\)

Define

\(A_F=\int_0^1 f(s)\,ds+\sum_\nu c_\nu,\qquad I_F(x)=\int_0^x f(s)\,ds.\)

Also define

\(C_F^{<}(x)=\sum_{\nu:r_\nu<x}c_\nu,\qquad C_F^{\le}(x)=\sum_{\nu:r_\nu\le x}c_\nu.\)

For \(0\le x<1\), direct substitution gives

\(F(q_x)=\ell A_F+\delta\bigl(I_F(x)+C_F^{<}(x)\bigr).\)

At \(x=1\), the endpoint convention instead gives

\(F(q_1)=hA_F.\)

These formulas allow signed heights and signed point coefficients.

Let

\(0=z_0<z_1<\cdots<z_{K-1}=1\)

be the common grid containing:

  • every step-function breakpoint of every \(U_j,P_j,B_k\);
  • every point-evaluation location in those functionals;
  • \(\rho\), together with \(0\) and \(1\).

Including \(\rho\) accounts for \(W_\rho\), whose kernel is \(\mathbf 1_{[0,\rho)}\).

For every functional \(F\), construct the following coefficient rows:

| Row label | Coefficient \(F_e\) |
|---|---|
| Actual value at \(z<1\) | \(\ell A_F+\delta\bigl(I_F(z)+C_F^{<}(z)\bigr)\) |
| Actual value at \(1\) | \(hA_F\) |
| Right limit at \(z<1\) | \(\ell A_F+\delta\bigl(I_F(z)+C_F^{\le}(z)\bigr)\) |
| Left limit at \(1\) | \(\ell A_F+\delta\bigl(I_F(1)+C_F^{<}(1)\bigr)\) |

There are \(K\) actual rows, \(K-1\) right-limit rows, and one left-limit row: \(2K\) rows in total.

For welfare,

\(W_\rho(q_x)=\ell\rho+\delta\min\{x,\rho\}.\)

Its one-sided limits agree with this continuous expression.

Lemma. For any fixed linear combination \(H\) of the encoded functionals and \(W_\rho\), the condition \(H(q_x)\ge0\) for every rational \(x\in[0,1]\) is equivalent to nonnegativity of all \(2K\) constructed rows.

Proof.

Suppose first that \(H(q_x)\ge0\) for every rational \(x\). Every grid point is rational, so all actual rows are nonnegative. Each one-sided row is the limit along a rational sequence of nonnegative values, and is therefore nonnegative as well.

Conversely, suppose all constructed rows are nonnegative. On each open interval \((z_i,z_{i+1})\), every integral \(I_F(x)\) is affine in \(x\), and every point-evaluation contribution is constant. Thus \(H(q_x)\) is affine there.

Its lower-end limit is the right-limit row at \(z_i\). Its upper-end limit is the actual row at \(z_{i+1}\) when \(z_{i+1}<1\), because the strict inequality \(r_\nu<x\) excludes terms at that endpoint both in the actual value and in the left limit. When \(z_{i+1}=1\), its upper-end limit is the separately included left-limit row at \(1\).

An affine function between two nonnegative endpoint limits is nonnegative throughout the interval. The actual rows cover the grid points themselves. Hence \(H(q_x)\ge0\) for every rational \(x\).

The limiting rows need not represent admissible societies themselves. Their necessity follows from rational admissible societies approaching them; their sufficiency follows from the affine interpolation just proved.

3. The exact linear program

Let \(E\) be the set of these \(2K\) row labels. For each \(e\in E\), write the constructed coefficients as \(U_{j,e},P_{j,e},B_{k,e},W_e\).

Use variables \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_t,\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_b,\alpha\), and solve:

Maximize \(\alpha\), subject to

\(\lambda_j\ge0\quad(1\le j\le t),\qquad \sum_{j=1}^t\lambda_j=1,\)

and, for every \(e\in E\),

\(\sum_{k=1}^b\theta_kB_{k,e}\ge0,\)

\(\sum_{j=1}^t\lambda_jP_{j,e}-\sum_{k=1}^b\theta_kB_{k,e}\ge0,\)

\(\sum_{j=1}^t\lambda_j(U_{j,e}-P_{j,e})+\sum_{k=1}^b\theta_kB_{k,e}-\alpha W_e\ge0.\)

The variables \(\theta_k\) are unrestricted, as required.

The first lemma reduces the universal constraints to rational two-value societies. The second lemma reduces those constraints to these finite rows. Thus this LP has exactly the feasible set of the original optimization problem.

For the decision problem, add \(\alpha\ge a\) and test feasibility.

4. Feasibility, boundedness, and attainment

Under the standard strategyproofness promise on the nonnegative report domain, the zero-payment normalization implies nonnegative expected payments. To see this, fix a population distribution and consider a zero-valued agent. Reporting zero gives utility zero; reporting any value with expected payment \(p\) gives utility \(-p\). Strategyproofness therefore implies \(p\ge0\).

Individual rationality also gives \(U_j(q)-P_j(q)\ge0\). Consequently, choosing any one menu rule, setting \(\theta=0\), and taking \(\alpha=0\) is feasible.

Every feasible ratio is bounded above by one. Indeed, consider the admissible constant-high society \(q_1\). Supply feasibility gives

\(U_j(q_1)\le\rho v_1=W_\rho(q_1).\)

The budget constraint gives \(R(q_1)\le\sum_j\lambda_jP_j(q_1)\). Combining this with the ratio constraint yields

\(\alpha\rho v_1\le\sum_j\lambda_j(U_j(q_1)-P_j(q_1))+R(q_1)\le\sum_j\lambda_jU_j(q_1)\le\rho v_1.\)

Because \(\rho v_1>0\), this proves \(\alpha\le1\).

All LP coefficients are rational. The standard exact rational linear-programming theorem states that a feasible rational LP with objective bounded above attains a rational optimum, and that an optimal solution can be found in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. Its hypotheses hold here. Unrestricted, possibly unbounded rebate coefficients do not prevent attainment of the bounded objective.

The resulting \((\lambda^*,\theta^*,\alpha^*)\) therefore supplies the requested optimal lottery, rebate coefficients, and ratio.

5. Running time and dependence on support size

Let \(N\) be the total number of encoded step-function records and point-evaluation terms, and put \(D=2t+b+1\), counting \(U_j,P_j,B_k,W_\rho\). Then \(K\le N+3\).

For \(m\ge2\), the LP has:

  • \(t+b+1\) variables;
  • \(6K+t\) inequalities and one equality;
  • one additional inequality for the decision threshold.

Sorting the grid and constructing all coefficients by prefix integration and summation takes

\(O\bigl(m+N\log N+DK\bigr)\)

rational arithmetic operations.

Let \(L\) include the binary encodings of the alphabet, supply, functionals, and decision threshold when applicable. Each constructed coefficient has polynomial bit length; a conservative bound is \(O(L^2)\). Hence the LP encoding length is \(O\bigl(K(t+b+1)L^2\bigr)\), up to standard indexing overhead.

The complete algorithm therefore runs in

\(\operatorname{poly}(m,t,b,L)\)

bit operations. Its running time is independent of any particular society’s support size \(\tau\), and is uniform over all admissible \(\tau\le m\). It enumerates neither supports nor mass vectors. For \(m=1\), a single coefficient row suffices.
