The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite house categories \(H\), a finite set \(T\) of strict rankings closed under adjacent swaps, and rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma\in\Delta(H)\), let \(\rho_{\mu,t,h}\) be the allocation marginal for a tagged type-\(t\) student under the replica limit of random priority with background distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\). Decide whether \(\rho\) is the unique Borel-measurable rule \(q_{\mu,t,h}\) such that, for every \(\mu\), \(q_{\mu,t,\cdot}\in\Delta(H)\), \(\sum_t\mu_tq_{\mu,t,h}=\sigma_h\), \(q_{\mu,t,h}=0\) whenever \(\rho_{\mu,t,h}=0\), and adjacent-swap reports \(t,t'\) satisfy \(q_{\mu,t,h}=q_{\mu,t',h}\) for every category \(h\) outside the swapped pair.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(H\) contain \(m\geq1\) house categories, let \(T\) be a nonempty, explicitly listed set of strict rankings of \(H\), closed under every adjacent swap, and let \(\sigma\in\Delta(H)\) have strictly positive binary-rational coordinates. Total student mass and total supply are both \(1\).
A rational society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass and their binary-rational masses. Here \(\tau\) is support size, not the number of possible rankings. Write \(d=|T|\) and \(L\) for input bit length; measure running times in \(m,d,L\), and additionally \(\tau\) for operations taking a society as input.
For any real \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\) and tagged report \(t\in T\), define \(\rho_{\mu,t,h}\) as follows. Take finite economies with \(k\) students, including one tagged student reporting \(t\), background type proportions tending to \(\mu\), and exactly \(k\sigma_h\) copies of category \(h\), along integers \(k\) making these supplies integral. Uniformly permute the students; each successively chooses her highest-ranked available category. Then \(\rho_{\mu,t,h}\) is the limiting tagged allocation probability as \(k\to\infty\).
An admissible rule is a Borel-measurable map \(q:\Delta(T)\times T\to\Delta(H)\), using the usual topology on the full real simplex, such that for every \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), \(t\in T\), and \(h\in H\),
\(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tq_{\mu,t,h}=\sigma_h\), and \(\rho_{\mu,t,h}=0\Rightarrow q_{\mu,t,h}=0\).
If \(t,t'\) differ by swapping adjacent categories \(a,b\), require \(q_{\mu,t,h}=q_{\mu,t',h}\) for every \(h\notin\{a,b\}\), holding the background \(\mu\) fixed. All report constraints apply even when \(\mu_t=0\); equality of rules is pointwise, including such reports.

Is deciding whether \(\rho\) is the unique admissible rule on \(\Delta(T)\), given \(H,T,\sigma\), in \(\mathrm{P}\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

In the finite model with \(n\) students and \(n\) distinct houses, uniqueness under equal treatment of equals, support efficiency, and localizedness is equivalent to full column rank of the matrix encoding their linear equality constraints; uniqueness is established for \(n\leq5\). [Brandt, Greger, and Romen](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11976v2)
Random priority converges to continuum probabilistic serial, including proportionately growing unequal category supplies; this identifies the limit but does not settle uniqueness under the constraints above. [Che and Kojima](https://www.columbia.edu/~yc2271/files/publications/psrp-Final.pdf)

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). More strongly, every instance satisfying the stated hypotheses has \(\rho\) as its unique admissible rule.

The proof establishes uniqueness separately at every real society, including all reports having zero mass.

1. The report domain and the continuum allocation

Adjacent transpositions connect all strict rankings of \(H\): one can transform any ranking into any other by successively moving each category to its desired position. Since \(T\) is nonempty and closed under every adjacent swap, \(T\) contains every strict ranking. Thus \(d=m!\).

Fix an arbitrary \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\). For a nonempty available set \(R\subseteq H\), let \(\operatorname{top}_t(R)\) denote the highest-ranked category in \(R\) under report \(t\), and define

\(p_R(h)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\,\mathbf 1\{\operatorname{top}_t(R)=h\}\)

for \(h\in R\), with \(p_R(h)=0\) otherwise.

Consider continuum probabilistic serial: every type consumes its favorite available category at unit speed. Consequently, category \(h\) is consumed at aggregate rate \(p_R(h)\) while the available set is \(R\).

This identifies the tagged limit by Che–Kojima’s Theorem 2 and its proportional-quota extension in Section 7.3. To check the hypotheses, choose a common denominator \(D\) of the supplies and write \(k=Dq\). Category \(h\) then has \((D\sigma_h)q\) copies, with \(D\sigma_h\) a positive integer, and type counts divided by \(q\) converge to \(D\mu_t\). There are finitely many categories and rankings. Their convergence is uniform over type–category coordinates and permits limiting type masses of zero; the tagged student changes normalized counts by a vanishing amount. Thus it applies also when \(\mu_t=0\). All categories are acceptable, so their outside option can be placed last. [Che and Kojima](https://www.columbia.edu/~yc2271/files/publications/psrp-Final.pdf)

We now derive the properties of this continuum allocation needed for uniqueness.

Exhaustion stages

Initially, every category has positive remaining supply. At a stage with available set \(R\) and remaining supplies \(s_h>0\), its duration is

\(\ell=\min_{h\in R:\,p_R(h)>0}\frac{s_h}{p_R(h)}.\)

This minimum exists because \(\sum_{h\in R}p_R(h)=1\). Its value is positive. During the stage, replace \(s_h\) by \(s_h-\ell p_R(h)\), and remove every category whose remaining supply becomes zero. At least one category is removed, so there are at most \(m\) stages.

Consumption has total rate \(1\) whenever any category remains. All supply is exhausted at termination; therefore, total duration is \(1\).

Write the distinct exhaustion times as

\(0=u_0<u_1<\cdots<u_r=1.\)

Let \(e_h\) be the exhaustion time of category \(h\), and put

\(G_i=\{h:e_h=u_i\},\qquad R_i=\{h:e_h\geq u_i\},\qquad \ell_i=u_i-u_{i-1}.\)

Thus \(R_i\) is the available set during stage \(i\). Write \(p_i(h)=p_{R_i}(h)\). Supply exhaustion gives

\[\sigma_h=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\ell_i p_i(h).\tag{1}\]

We will also need the strict positivity property

\[h\in G_i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_i(h)>0.\tag{2}\]

Indeed, such a category has strictly positive supply at the start of stage \(i\) and zero supply at its end. Its constant consumption rate during that positive-length stage must therefore be positive. This argument includes simultaneous exhaustions.

Tagged probabilities and support

For a report \(t\), define its set of categories above \(h\) by

\(S_t(h)=\{g\in H:g\succ_t h\}.\)

Use the convention \(\max\varnothing=0\), and write \(x_+=\max\{x,0\}\).

The tagged report chooses \(h\) precisely during the interval when \(h\) remains available and every category ranked above it has exhausted. Hence

\[\rho_{\mu,t,h}=\left(e_h-\max_{g\in S_t(h)}e_g\right)_+.\tag{3}\]

In particular,

\[\rho_{\mu,t,h}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e_h\leq\max_{g\in S_t(h)}e_g.\tag{4}\]

Formula (3) holds for every report, irrespective of its mass in \(\mu\).

2. Localizedness and normalization give a subset potential

Let \(q\) be any admissible rule. Continue holding \(\mu\) fixed, and abbreviate \(q_{\mu,t,h}\) to \(q_{t,h}\).

For \(S\subseteq H\setminus\{h\}\), define \(x_{S,h}=q_{t,h}\), where \(t\) is any report satisfying \(S_t(h)=S\).

This is well-defined. Any two such reports can be connected by adjacent swaps entirely within the block \(S\) above \(h\), and entirely within the block below \(h\). None of these swaps moves \(h\), so localizedness preserves its allocation coordinate.

For distinct \(a,b\notin S\), compare two reports with the same ordered prefix \(S\), followed respectively by \(a,b\) and \(b,a\), and then the same suffix. Their row sums both equal \(1\), while localizedness makes every coordinate outside \(\{a,b\}\) equal. Therefore,

\[x_{S,a}+x_{S\cup\{a\},b}=x_{S,b}+x_{S\cup\{b\},a}.\tag{5}\]

Define \(v(\varnothing)=0\). For a set \(S=\{h_1,\ldots,h_k\}\), define

\(v(S)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{\{h_1,\ldots,h_{j-1}\},h_j}.\)

This value does not depend on the chosen ordering of \(S\). An adjacent exchange in that ordering preserves the two affected summands by (5), and preserves all other summands because their predecessor sets are unchanged. Adjacent exchanges connect all orderings.

Appending \(h\) to an ordering of \(S\) gives

\[x_{S,h}=v(S\cup\{h\})-v(S).\tag{6}\]

An ordering of all of \(H\) is a report, so row normalization also gives

\[v(H)=1.\tag{7}\]

Thus localizedness and normalization express every allocation coordinate as an increment of one potential on subsets.

3. Support reduces the potential to exhaustion levels

For \(i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\), define

\(D_i=\{h:e_h\leq u_i\},\qquad w_i=v(D_i),\)

and set \(D_0=\varnothing\), \(w_0=0\). Since \(D_r=H\), equation (7) gives \(w_r=1\).

Let \(S\neq\varnothing\), and suppose

\(\max_{g\in S}e_g=u_i.\)

Every \(h\in D_i\setminus S\) satisfies \(e_h\leq\max_{g\in S}e_g\). By (4), support efficiency, and (6),

\(v(S\cup\{h\})-v(S)=x_{S,h}=0.\)

Add the elements of \(D_i\setminus S\) one at a time. Throughout, the maximum exhaustion time remains \(u_i\), so every addition has zero increment. Consequently,

\[v(S)=v(D_i)=w_i.\tag{8}\]

This also handles ties: any nonempty subset whose maximum exhaustion time is \(u_i\) has the same potential.

Let \(j(h)\) be the index satisfying \(e_h=u_{j(h)}\). For a subset \(S\), let \(j(S)\) satisfy \(\max_{g\in S}e_g=u_{j(S)}\), with \(j(\varnothing)=0\). Equations (6) and (8) yield

\[q_{t,h}=w_{\max\{j(S_t(h)),j(h)\}}-w_{j(S_t(h))}.\tag{9}\]

Define the successive increments

\(\delta_i=w_i-w_{i-1},\qquad i=1,\ldots,r.\)

We claim that (9) is equivalently

\[q_{t,h}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\delta_i\,\mathbf 1\{\operatorname{top}_t(R_i)=h\}.\tag{10}\]

To verify this, put \(j=j(h)\) and \(k=j(S_t(h))\).

If \(k\geq j\), some category above \(h\) remains available throughout every stage in which \(h\) is available. Thus \(h\) is never top in \(R_i\), and both sides of (10) are zero.

If \(k<j\), category \(h\) is available exactly for \(i\leq j\), and every category above it has exhausted exactly when \(i>k\). Therefore, it is top in \(R_i\) precisely for \(k<i\leq j\). The right-hand side of (10) is then

\(\sum_{i=k+1}^{j}\delta_i=w_j-w_k,\)

as required by (9).

4. A triangular supply system forces the increments

Taking the \(\mu\)-weighted sum of (10), admissibility gives

\[\sigma_h=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\delta_i p_i(h)\qquad\text{for every }h\in H.\tag{11}\]

Compare this with the probabilistic-serial supply equations (1):

\[\sum_{i=1}^{r}p_i(h)(\delta_i-\ell_i)=0\qquad\text{for every }h\in H.\tag{12}\]

Choose one category \(h_j\in G_j\) for each exhaustion level \(j\). Consider the square matrix

\(B_{j,i}=p_i(h_j),\qquad 1\leq j,i\leq r.\)

If \(i>j\), category \(h_j\) has already exhausted, so \(B_{j,i}=0\). By (2), \(B_{j,j}=p_j(h_j)>0\). Hence \(B\) is lower triangular with

\(\det B=\prod_{j=1}^{r}p_j(h_j)>0.\)

Restricting (12) to these selected categories gives \(B(\delta-\ell)=0\). Invertibility therefore implies

\(\delta_i=\ell_i=u_i-u_{i-1}\qquad\text{for every }i.\)

Since \(w_0=u_0=0\), summation gives \(w_i=u_i\). Substituting into (9),

\(q_{\mu,t,h}=\left(e_h-\max_{g\in S_t(h)}e_g\right)_+=\rho_{\mu,t,h}.\)

The society \(\mu\) was arbitrary. Thus equality holds pointwise on the entire real simplex and for every report, including reports of zero mass.

5. Admissibility and complexity

For completeness, \(\rho\) itself is admissible.

Its coordinates are nonnegative, and each report chooses exactly one available category throughout a time interval of total length \(1\), so its coordinates sum to \(1\). Equation (1) proves supply feasibility. Support efficiency holds by definition. If two reports swap adjacent categories \(a,b\), then \(S_t(h)\) is unchanged for every \(h\notin\{a,b\}\); formula (3) therefore proves localizedness.

It is also Borel-measurable on the full real simplex. In the stage construction, the rates are finite linear functions of \(\mu\) for each fixed available set. Durations and remaining supplies are obtained through arithmetic operations, division on sets where the denominator is positive, finite minima, and comparisons deciding which categories exhaust. These operations and branches are Borel. There are at most \(m\) stages, so every exhaustion time \(e_h\), and consequently every coordinate in (3), is Borel.

Therefore, every valid input has answer “unique.” Under the stated promise, the decision algorithm simply returns that answer; reading the input takes \(O(L)\) time. If input validation is required, checking rankings, adjacent-swap closure, and the rational supply conditions takes polynomial time in \(m,d,L\).

For operations taking a rational society as input, the exhaustion schedule can be computed using \(O(m^2\tau)\) arithmetic operations by scanning its \(\tau\) supported rankings at each stage. All \(d\) tagged allocation vectors then require \(O(dm)\) further operations using prefix maxima in (3). Exact bit complexity is polynomial: for any realized sequence of exhaustion groups, the stage durations solve the triangular rational system above, whose coefficients have polynomial bit length; determinant bounds give polynomial bit length for its solution.

The uniqueness proof uses the support of the continuum allocation at a fixed background society. It therefore settles the stated continuum question without requiring a characterization of finite random serial dictatorship.
