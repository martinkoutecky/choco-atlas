The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite firm and worker type sets \(B\) and \(S\), rational masses \(\mu_b,\mu_s\), type values and reservation wages \(v_b,c_s\), and an allowed-link relation \(E\subseteq B\times S\), compute a mass matching \(x_{bs}\geq 0\) satisfying \(\sum_{s:(b,s)\in E}x_{bs}\leq\mu_b\) and \(\sum_{b:(b,s)\in E}x_{bs}\leq\mu_s\), together with supporting wages for its matched mass, so that no linked firm and worker tranche can mutually benefit by rematching. For the returned matching, compute the minimum and maximum supporting wage for each active type pair.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(B,S\) be nonempty, disjoint finite sets of firm and worker types, with values \(v_b,c_s\in\mathbb Q\) and allowed links \(E\subseteq B\times S\). Values and wages may have either sign.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|B|+|S|\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_b,\mu_s\). Masses need not sum to one or balance between sides; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Set \(m=|E|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A mass matching has \(x_{bs}\geq0\) on \(E\), with \(\sum_s x_{bs}\leq\mu_b\) and \(\sum_b x_{bs}\leq\mu_s\). Each active pair \(x_{bs}>0\) receives one wage \(w_{bs}\in[c_s,v_b]\), paid uniformly on that pair's matched mass.
A matched firm tranche earns \(v_b-w_{bs}\); a matched worker tranche receives \(w_{bs}\). Unmatched firms earn zero and unmatched workers receive \(c_s\). Tranches may leave existing partners to rematch.
Every tranche of type \(b\) can match every tranche of type \(s\) when \((b,s)\in E\). Stability excludes two currently unpaired, positive-mass linked tranches that can both strictly improve at some new wage; indifference does not block.
Equivalently, assign each firm tranche its current wage, or \(v_b\) if unmatched, and each worker tranche its current wage, or \(c_s\) if unmatched: for every linked pair of tranche statuses with positive mass, the firm's assigned number must not exceed the worker's.
Compute a stable matching and supporting wages, and, keeping that matching fixed, the minimum and maximum of each active pair's wage over all its supporting wage profiles.

Is this exact computational problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents with a common reservation value and grid-valued firm values, a terminating deferred-acceptance procedure returns a stable allocation and its pointwise minimum and maximum supporting prices; the stated result does not establish a polynomial bound in binary input length.   • Supporting wages were unspecified within an active type pair; use one uniform wage per active pair.

1. Result

The exact problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Three rational linear programs suffice: one computes the matching, and two compute supporting wage profiles that simultaneously attain every active pair’s minimum and maximum wage.

The key is that the stated tranche-level stability condition forces a common assigned number for every type. This identifies stable outcomes with primal–dual optimal solutions of a maximum-surplus matching LP.

2. Stability forces a common assigned number within each type

Fix a feasible matching \(x\). Define its active links and unmatched masses by

\(A(x)=\{(b,s)\in E:x_{bs}>0\}\),

\(r_b=\mu_b-\sum_{s:(b,s)\in E}x_{bs}\), and \(r_s=\mu_s-\sum_{b:(b,s)\in E}x_{bs}\).

Suppose wages support stability.

If \((b,s)\) and \((b,t)\) are active, compare the firm tranche matched to \(t\) with the worker tranche of type \(s\). The stated stability inequality gives \(w_{bt}\leq w_{bs}\). Reversing the roles gives \(w_{bs}\leq w_{bt}\). Thus all active wages incident to a firm type coincide.

Similarly, if \((b,s)\) and \((d,s)\) are active, comparing the firm tranche of type \(b\) with the worker tranche matched to \(d\) gives \(w_{bs}\leq w_{ds}\), and the reverse comparison gives equality. All active wages incident to a worker type therefore coincide.

If \(r_b>0\) and \((b,s)\) is active, comparing an unmatched firm tranche with a matched worker tranche gives \(v_b\leq w_{bs}\). Individual rationality gives \(w_{bs}\leq v_b\), so \(w_{bs}=v_b\).

Likewise, if \(r_s>0\) and \((b,s)\) is active, stability gives \(w_{bs}\leq c_s\), while individual rationality gives the reverse inequality. Hence \(w_{bs}=c_s\).

Consequently, every positive-mass status of type \(t\) has the same assigned number, denoted \(p_t\). A completely unmatched firm has \(p_b=v_b\), and a completely unmatched worker has \(p_s=c_s\).

It follows that supporting wage profiles correspond exactly to vectors in the following polyhedron \(Q(x)\):

  • \(p_b\leq v_b\) for every \(b\in B\), and \(p_s\geq c_s\) for every \(s\in S\).
  • \(p_b\leq p_s\) for every \((b,s)\in E\).
  • \(p_b=p_s\) for every \((b,s)\in A(x)\).
  • \(p_b=v_b\) whenever \(r_b>0\).
  • \(p_s=c_s\) whenever \(r_s>0\).

The necessity of these constraints follows from the preceding comparisons and the stated stability inequality.

For sufficiency, take any \(p\in Q(x)\) and assign \(w_{bs}=p_b=p_s\) on every active link. The first constraints guarantee \(c_s\leq w_{bs}\leq v_b\). The unmatched-mass equalities ensure that every positive-mass tranche of type \(t\) has assigned number \(p_t\). Finally, \(p_b\leq p_s\) on every allowed link is precisely the stability condition supplied in the question.

Thus \(Q(x)\) describes all supporting wage profiles, without restricting their possible variation beyond what stability itself requires.

3. Computing a supportable matching by linear programming

Put \(a_{bs}=v_b-c_s\). Consider the primal LP \(P\):

\( \displaystyle \text{maximize } W(x)=\sum_{(b,s)\in E}a_{bs}x_{bs} \),

subject to

  • \(x_{bs}\geq0\) for every \((b,s)\in E\);
  • \(\sum_{s:(b,s)\in E}x_{bs}\leq\mu_b\) for every \(b\in B\);
  • \(\sum_{b:(b,s)\in E}x_{bs}\leq\mu_s\) for every \(s\in S\).

Its dual \(D\) is

\( \displaystyle \text{minimize } F(u)=\sum_{t\in B\cup S}\mu_tu_t \),

subject to \(u_t\geq0\) for every type and

\(u_b+u_s\geq a_{bs}\qquad((b,s)\in E)\).

The primal is feasible because \(x=0\) is feasible. Its feasible region is compact: each variable lies between zero and \(\min\{\mu_b,\mu_s\}\). The dual is also feasible: take all worker variables to be zero and all firm variables to equal

\(K=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{a_{bs}:(b,s)\in E\}\bigr)\).

Therefore strong LP duality applies: optimal solutions exist and their objective values coincide. This is the standard finite-dimensional [LP duality theorem](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/18/Notes/n12-duality.html).

For completeness, the relevant complementary-slackness conditions follow directly from the gap identity. For any primal-feasible \(x\) and dual-feasible \(u\),

\( \displaystyle F(u)-W(x)=\sum_{t\in B\cup S}r_tu_t+\sum_{(b,s)\in E}x_{bs}(u_b+u_s-a_{bs}). \)

To verify this identity, substitute \(r_b=\mu_b-\sum_sx_{bs}\) and \(r_s=\mu_s-\sum_bx_{bs}\), and collect the coefficient of each \(x_{bs}\).

Every term on the right is nonnegative. Hence, for any optimal \(x\) and optimal \(u\),

\(r_tu_t=0\) for every type, and \(u_b+u_s=a_{bs}\) on every active link.

Now define

\(p_b=v_b-u_b\), and \(p_s=c_s+u_s\).

Nonnegativity of \(u\) gives \(p_b\leq v_b\) and \(p_s\geq c_s\). Dual feasibility gives

\(p_b\leq p_s\qquad((b,s)\in E)\).

On active links, complementary slackness gives \(p_b=p_s\). If \(r_b>0\), it gives \(u_b=0\), hence \(p_b=v_b\); if \(r_s>0\), it gives \(p_s=c_s\).

Thus \(p\in Q(x)\). By Section 2, every optimal solution of \(P\) admits supporting stable wages.

Conversely, a stable outcome yields \(p\in Q(x)\). Set \(u_b=v_b-p_b\) and \(u_s=p_s-c_s\). These variables are dual-feasible, and all terms in the gap identity vanish. Weak duality then proves that \(x\) and \(u\) are optimal.

We have therefore proved the exact characterization: the supportable matchings are precisely the optimal solutions of \(P\).

4. Exact wage extrema for the fixed matching

Fix the optimal matching \(x\) computed above. We have proved that \(Q(x)\) is nonempty.

It is also compact. It is closed because its defining constraints are non-strict linear equalities and inequalities. To establish boundedness, consider any type:

  • If a firm \(b\) has an active incident link \((b,s)\), then \(c_s\leq p_b=p_s\leq v_b\). If it has no active link, then \(r_b=\mu_b>0\), so \(p_b=v_b\).
  • If a worker \(s\) has an active incident link \((b,s)\), then \(c_s\leq p_s=p_b\leq v_b\). If it has no active link, then \(r_s=\mu_s>0\), so \(p_s=c_s\).

Every coordinate therefore has finite bounds.

Moreover, \(Q(x)\) is closed under coordinatewise minimum and maximum. Indeed, for \(p,q\in Q(x)\), define

\((p\wedge q)_t=\min\{p_t,q_t\}\), and \((p\vee q)_t=\max\{p_t,q_t\}\).

Constant bounds, fixed-coordinate equalities, and active-link equalities are preserved by both operations. For an allowed-link inequality, \(p_b\leq p_s\) and \(q_b\leq q_s\) imply

\(\min\{p_b,q_b\}\leq\min\{p_s,q_s\}\)

and

\(\max\{p_b,q_b\}\leq\max\{p_s,q_s\}\).

Thus both new vectors belong to \(Q(x)\).

Compute \(p^{-}\) by minimizing \(\sum_t p_t\) over \(Q(x)\). Compactness guarantees an optimizer. This optimizer is componentwise least: if some \(q\in Q(x)\) had \(q_j<p^{-}_j\), then \(p^{-}\wedge q\) would be feasible, would not increase any coordinate, and would strictly decrease coordinate \(j\). Its coordinate sum would be smaller, contradicting optimality.

Similarly, maximizing \(\sum_t p_t\) over \(Q(x)\) produces a componentwise greatest vector \(p^{+}\).

Hence every active pair has the exact wage bounds

\(\min w_{bs}=p^{-}_b=p^{-}_s\), and \(\max w_{bs}=p^{+}_b=p^{+}_s\).

Both endpoint wage profiles support the same fixed matching \(x\). In particular, the minima are simultaneously attainable, as are the maxima.

5. Algorithm and binary-input complexity

The algorithm is:

  • Solve \(P\) exactly, obtaining a rational optimal matching \(x\).
  • Compute \(A(x)\) and the unmatched masses \(r_t\), and construct \(Q(x)\).
  • Minimize and maximize \(\sum_t p_t\) over \(Q(x)\), obtaining \(p^{-}\) and \(p^{+}\).
  • Return \(x\), supporting wages \(w_{bs}=p^{-}_b\) on active links, and the bounds \(p^{-}_b,p^{+}_b\) for each such link.

The standard rational-LP complexity theorem provides exact rational optimizers in time polynomial in the number of variables, constraints, and total binary encoding length; it also bounds the output encoding length polynomially. See the discussion of polynomial algorithms and exact optimum recovery in [MIT’s LP algorithm notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/18/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html).

Here \(P\) has \(m\) variables and \(O(m+\tau)\) constraints. Its coefficients are input masses, differences \(v_b-c_s\), and zeros or ones, so its encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The returned \(x\) has polynomial encoding length. Exact rational arithmetic therefore computes \(A(x)\), the residual masses, and their zero tests in polynomial time. Each wage LP has \(\tau\) variables and \(O(m+\tau)\) constraints, with coefficients drawn from \(v_b,c_s\) and \(\{-1,0,1\}\). Their encoding lengths are likewise polynomial.

All three LPs are feasible and bounded, as proved above. Consequently, the algorithm and its exact output have polynomial bit complexity.

The proof permits either sign for values and wages and requires no normalization or balance of masses. If \(m=0\), the empty matching is stable and there are no active wage bounds to report.
