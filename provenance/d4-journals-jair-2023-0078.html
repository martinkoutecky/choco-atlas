The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(N\), \(V\), \(D\), rational masses \(\mu_{q,c}\) over provider service classes \(q\) and costs \(c\), known cost priors, and rationally represented duration CDFs \(G_q\), compute an \(\epsilon\)-optimal direct procurement auction. The auction chooses finite-stage invocation schedules of real-valued provider mass, conditional on prior noncompletion, and payments to maximize expected consumer utility subject to BIC and interim IR; ask whether the stronger DSIC and ex-post IR guarantees also hold.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \((q,c)\) with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_{q,c}\) a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(m\) is the number of publicly observable service classes. The population is nonatomic, with total mass \(N=\sum_{q,c}\mu_{q,c}>0\), not necessarily one.
Costs are private and nonnegative. For class \(q\), let its possible costs be \(c_{q,1}<\cdots<c_{q,h_q}\), its mass be \(M_q\), and its common cost prior be \(p_{q,j}=\mu_{q,c_{q,j}}/M_q\). Assume regularity: \(\phi_{q,1}=c_{q,1}\) and \(\phi_{q,j}=c_{q,j}+(c_{q,j}-c_{q,j-1})\sum_{\ell<j}p_{q,\ell}/p_{q,j}\) are nondecreasing in \(j\).
The consumer has binary-rational value \(V>0\), deadline \(D>0\), and tolerance \(0<\epsilon<1\). Each commonly known duration CDF \(G_q\) is continuous piecewise linear on \([0,D]\), specified by rational breakpoints and values, with \(G_q(0)=0\). Completion by \(D\) earns \(V\), otherwise zero; additional completions earn nothing.
A direct auction accepts only cost reports from the reporting provider’s class cost set. At each reported mass histogram \(r\), with \(\sum_b r_{q,b}=M_q\), it chooses finitely many times \(0\le t_1\le\cdots\le t_K\le D\), recruitment fractions \(a_{q,b,k}\ge0\) with \(\sum_k a_{q,b,k}\le1\), and nonnegative per-unit payments \(\pi_{q,b,k}\). Recruitment within each reported type is anonymous; no provider is recruited twice, and the empty schedule is permitted.
Stage \(k\) recruits mass \(x_{q,b,k}=r_{q,b}a_{q,b,k}\) only if no completion has occurred. Invoked providers incur their entire cost immediately, receive payment upon invocation, and continue working without cancellation. Define the mass completion law by \(S_r(t)=\prod_{k:t_k\le t}\prod_{q,b}(1-G_q(t-t_k))^{x_{q,b,k}}\), with \(0^0=1\); \(S_r(t)\) is the probability of no completion by \(t\).
An individual deviation leaves the histogram unchanged. A class-\(q\) provider of true cost \(c\), reporting \(b\), has expected utility \(u_q(c,b;r)=\sum_k a_{q,b,k}S_r(t_k)(\pi_{q,b,k}-c)\). BIC and interim IR require \(u_q(c,c;\mu)\ge u_q(c,b;\mu)\) and \(u_q(c,c;\mu)\ge0\) for every possible \(q,c,b\). DSIC requires the same incentive inequality at every histogram \(r\); ex-post IR requires \(\pi_{q,c,k}\ge c\) whenever a truthful provider can actually be invoked.
Rules may randomize, with incentive inequalities and objectives evaluated in expectation over that randomization. The truthful consumer objective is \(U=V(1-S_\mu(D))-\sum_{q,c,k}\mu_{q,c}a_{q,c,k}S_\mu(t_k)\pi_{q,c,k}\). An \(\epsilon\)-optimal auction has utility within additive \(\epsilon\) of the supremum over these BIC, interim-IR auctions. Auction rules must have finite descriptions permitting evaluation to requested numerical precision; \(L\) denotes total input bit length.
Is computing an \(\epsilon\)-optimal auction polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\), or NP-hard?
Can the same BIC, interim-IR utility supremum be approached arbitrarily closely by DSIC, ex-post-IR auctions?
For finitely many providers with independent durations and independent regular cost priors, an optimal contingent procurement auction is claimed to maximize consumer utility among all BIC, interim-IR auctions and also satisfy DSIC and ex-post IR; no polynomial-time guarantee is established. Status and new progress

The DSIC, ex-post-IR approximation question has an affirmative answer.

The general computational classification remains unresolved here. I prove two additional results about it:

  • When every populated class satisfies $G_q(D)<1$, an explicit uniform discretization reduces additive approximation to a finite search.
  • Even with $m=\tau=1$ and $G_1(D)=1/2$, fixed additive accuracy can require exponentially many recruitment stages in the input bit length. This remains true in expectation for randomized auctions.

The second result rules out a polynomial-time algorithm that always outputs an explicit list of polynomially many stages. It does not establish NP-hardness under the task’s more general finite-description requirement, which permits compact descriptions of large schedules.

1. The payment bound

For a class $q$, suppress its subscript and write its supported costs as $c_1<\cdots<c_h$, with masses $\mu_j>0$.

At the truthful histogram, define the expected invocation probability and expected payment per provider by
$$ A_j=\mathbb E\left[\sum_k a_{j,k}S_\mu(t_k)\right], \qquad T_j=\mathbb E\left[\sum_k a_{j,k}S_\mu(t_k)\pi_{j,k}\right]. $$
Expectations include auction randomization. Since an individual deviation leaves the histogram unchanged, a cost-$c_i$ provider reporting $c_j$ has utility $T_j-c_iA_j$.

It suffices to consider auctions with finite expected payments: nonnegative payments with infinite total expectation give consumer utility $-\infty$.

For $i<j$, adding the two incentive inequalities gives
$$ (c_j-c_i)(A_i-A_j)\ge0. $$
Thus BIC requires
$$ A_1\ge\cdots\ge A_h\ge0. $$

Put $u_j=T_j-c_jA_j$. The incentive constraint against reporting $c_{j+1}$ gives
$$ u_j\ge u_{j+1}+(c_{j+1}-c_j)A_{j+1}. $$
Together with $u_h\ge0$, this implies
$$ T_j\ge c_jA_j+ \sum_{\ell=j+1}^{h}(c_\ell-c_{\ell-1})A_\ell. $$
Multiplying by masses and summing yields
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_j\mu_jT_j &\ge \sum_j\mu_jc_jA_j+ \sum_{\ell=2}^{h}(c_\ell-c_{\ell-1})A_\ell \sum_{j<\ell}\mu_j\\ &=\sum_j\mu_j\phi_jA_j. \end{aligned} $$
The last equality uses exactly the virtual-cost formula in the question; it requires no normalization of population mass.

Conversely, for any nonincreasing allocation vector, define
$$ T_j=c_jA_j+ \sum_{\ell=j+1}^{h}(c_\ell-c_{\ell-1})A_\ell. $$
These payments satisfy every incentive inequality and interim IR. Indeed, for $i<j$,
$$ u_i-u_j= \sum_{\ell=i+1}^{j}(c_\ell-c_{\ell-1})A_\ell, $$
and monotonicity gives
$$ (c_j-c_i)A_j \le u_i-u_j \le(c_j-c_i)A_i. $$
The lower bound is the incentive constraint for type $i$ against report $j$; the upper bound is the reverse constraint. Also, every $u_j$ is nonnegative.

2. Sorting a schedule and implementing it by DSIC payments

A deterministic schedule $\sigma$ consists of times $t_k$ and recruitment masses $x_{q,j,k}$ satisfying
$$ x_{q,j,k}\ge0, \qquad \sum_kx_{q,j,k}\le\mu_{q,j}. $$
Define its virtual surplus by
$$ \mathcal F(\sigma) = V\bigl(1-S_\mu(D)\bigr) - \sum_{q,j,k}\phi_{q,j}x_{q,j,k}S_\mu(t_k). $$

2.1. Cheapest-first sorting

Let
$$ z_{q,k}=\sum_jx_{q,j,k}, \qquad R_{q,k}=\sum_{\ell\le k}z_{q,\ell}, \qquad R_{q,0}=0, $$
and let
$$ B_{q,j}=\sum_{i<j}\mu_{q,i}. $$
Replace the assignment within class $q$ by
$$ \widehat x_{q,j,k} = \left| [B_{q,j},B_{q,j}+\mu_{q,j}) \cap [R_{q,k-1},R_{q,k}) \right|, $$
where the bars denote interval length.

This preserves every class’s recruitment mass at every stage. It therefore preserves the survival law. It is feasible because the recruited intervals are disjoint and have total length at most $M_q$.

Write $s_k=S_\mu(t_k)$. Since each CDF is nondecreasing,
$$ 1\ge s_1\ge\cdots\ge s_K\ge0. $$
Tied invocation times cause no difficulty because $G_q(0)=0$.

For a class $q$, let $C_q(R)$ be the minimum virtual cost of selecting mass $R$. Regularity means that $\phi_{q,j}$ is nondecreasing in $j$. Consequently, filling the cost intervals in increasing order attains $C_q(R)$: replacing selected mass of a higher virtual cost by available mass of a lower virtual cost weakly reduces cost, and such exchanges terminate at the cheapest-first assignment.

For the original assignment, put
$$ b_{q,k}=\sum_j\phi_{q,j}x_{q,j,k}. $$
Every recruitment prefix satisfies
$$ \sum_{\ell\le k}b_{q,\ell}\ge C_q(R_{q,k}). $$
Setting $s_{K+1}=0$, summation by parts gives
$$ \sum_{k=1}^{K}s_kb_{q,k} = \sum_{k=1}^{K}(s_k-s_{k+1}) \sum_{\ell\le k}b_{q,\ell}. $$
All coefficients are nonnegative. The sorted assignment attains $C_q(R_{q,k})$ for every prefix simultaneously, so it weakly reduces expected virtual costs. Hence
$$ \mathcal F(\widehat\sigma)\ge\mathcal F(\sigma). $$

2.2. An extension to every histogram

For the sorted schedule, define fractions
$$ \widehat a_{q,j,k}=\frac{\widehat x_{q,j,k}}{\mu_{q,j}}, \qquad F_{q,j,k}=\sum_{\ell\le k}\widehat a_{q,j,\ell}. $$
At each recruitment prefix, earlier cost intervals are full, at most one interval is partial, and later intervals are empty. Thus
$$ F_{q,1,k}\ge\cdots\ge F_{q,h_q,k}. $$

Use these same times and fractions at every reported histogram $r$. They remain feasible because each type’s fractions sum to at most one. Define
$$ A_{q,j}(r)=\sum_k\widehat a_{q,j,k}S_r(t_k). $$
With $s_k(r)=S_r(t_k)$ and $s_{K+1}(r)=0$,
$$ A_{q,j}(r) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \bigl(s_k(r)-s_{k+1}(r)\bigr)F_{q,j,k}. $$
The coefficients are nonnegative, so
$$ A_{q,1}(r)\ge\cdots\ge A_{q,h_q}(r) $$
at every histogram.

Set
$$ T_{q,j}(r) = c_{q,j}A_{q,j}(r) + \sum_{\ell=j+1}^{h_q} (c_{q,\ell}-c_{q,\ell-1})A_{q,\ell}(r). $$
Pay the same amount at every invocation of reported type $j$:
$$ \pi_{q,j,k}(r)= \begin{cases} T_{q,j}(r)/A_{q,j}(r),&A_{q,j}(r)>0,\\ c_{q,j},&A_{q,j}(r)=0. \end{cases} $$

If $A_{q,j}(r)=0$, monotonicity forces all higher-cost allocation probabilities to be zero, so $T_{q,j}(r)=0$. Thus these invocation payments implement the specified expected payments in both branches.

The pairwise verification in Section 1 applies at every fixed $r$, proving DSIC. This uses the stipulated assumption that a deviation leaves $r$ unchanged.

Moreover, whenever $A_{q,j}(r)>0$,
$$ c_{q,j} \le\pi_{q,j,k}(r) = c_{q,j}+ \sum_{\ell>j} (c_{q,\ell}-c_{q,\ell-1}) \frac{A_{q,\ell}(r)}{A_{q,j}(r)} \le c_{q,h_q}. $$
Thus truthful invocation payments satisfy ex-post IR.

At truth, these payments attain the payment bound exactly, so the constructed auction’s consumer utility is $\mathcal F(\widehat\sigma)$.

For rational schedules and rational numerical histograms, the rule has a finite description permitting arbitrary-precision evaluation. Survival factors have rational bases and rational exponents; zero factors can be identified from the encoded data. Positive factors, sums, and the displayed payment ratios are computable to any requested precision. This does not assert a polynomial evaluation bound.

3. A uniform finite discretization

Assume in this section that
$$ G_q(D)<1 $$
for every populated class. Define
$$ H_q(u)=-\log(1-G_q(u)), \qquad 0\le u\le D. $$
Then
$$ S_\mu(t)= \exp\left(-\sum_{q,j,k:t_k\le t} x_{q,j,k}H_q(t-t_k)\right). $$

Let $b_q$ be the maximum slope of a linear segment of $G_q$, and put
$$ B=\max_q\frac{b_q}{1-G_q(D)}. $$
Each $H_q$ is $B$-Lipschitz. Indeed, on its linear segments,
$$ H_q'(u)=\frac{G_q'(u)}{1-G_q(u)} \le\frac{b_q}{1-G_q(D)}, $$
and integrating across the finitely many segments gives the claimed bound.

Also define
$$ \overline H= \max_q\frac{G_q(D)}{1-G_q(D)}, \qquad W=\sum_{q,j}\mu_{q,j}\phi_{q,j}, \qquad \Phi=\max_{q,j}\phi_{q,j}. $$
The inequality
$$ -\log(1-g)=\int_0^g\frac{du}{1-u} \le\frac{g}{1-g} $$
shows that $H_q(u)\le\overline H$.

If $B=0$, all CDFs vanish on $[0,D]$, and the empty schedule is optimal. Hence suppose $B>0$.

Fix an accuracy $\eta>0$.

3.1. Rounding invocation times

Choose
$$ J= \max\left\{ 1,\left\lceil \frac{4D(V+W)NB}{\eta} \right\rceil \right\}, \qquad \Delta=\frac DJ. $$
Round every invocation time downward to the grid
$$ 0,\Delta,\ldots,J\Delta. $$

For any pair of stages, rounding changes their time difference by at most $\Delta$. Extend $H_q(u)$ to be zero for $u<0$; this extension is still $B$-Lipschitz because $H_q(0)=0$.

At corresponding evaluation times, the survival exponents therefore differ by at most
$$ NB\Delta. $$
This statement includes stages that become tied after rounding: their newly coincident recruitment contributes $H_q(0)=0$.

Since $z\mapsto e^{-z}$ is $1$-Lipschitz on the nonnegative real line,
$$ |S_\mu(t_k)-S_\mu^{\mathrm{round}}(t_k^{\mathrm{round}})| \le NB\Delta. $$
The same bound holds at the deadline. Consequently,
$$ |\mathcal F(\sigma)-\mathcal F(\sigma^{\mathrm{round}})| \le(V+W)NB\Delta \le\frac{\eta}{4}. $$
All recruitment at a common grid time can now be merged without changing the objective.

3.2. Rounding recruitment masses

There are at most $Q=J+1$ grid times. Put
$$ C=\Phi+(V+W)\overline H. $$
Here $C>0$ because $B>0$ implies that some terminal completion probability is positive.

Choose a positive rational $h$ such that
$$ h\le\frac{\eta}{4\tau Q C}. $$
At every type and grid time, round recruitment mass downward to a multiple of $h$.

Let $\xi$ be the total removed mass. Then
$$ \xi\le\tau Qh. $$
At any time, removing this mass changes the survival exponent by at most $\overline H\xi$, hence changes survival by at most $\overline H\xi$.

The virtual-cost difference is bounded by the removed virtual cost plus the effect on survival:
$$ \left| \sum_{q,j,k}\phi_{q,j}x_{q,j,k}S(t_k) - \sum_{q,j,k}\phi_{q,j}x'_{q,j,k}S'(t_k) \right| \le\Phi\xi+W\overline H\xi. $$
Together with the completion-value term, this gives
$$ |\mathcal F(\sigma^{\mathrm{round}}) -\mathcal F(\sigma^{\mathrm{grid}})| \le C\xi \le\frac{\eta}{4}. $$

Thus every finite real schedule has a feasible grid schedule satisfying
$$ \mathcal F(\sigma^{\mathrm{grid}}) \ge\mathcal F(\sigma)-\frac{\eta}{2}. $$
The bound is uniform over the original number of stages.

3.3. Consequence: an effective finite search

The grid family is finite: every recruitment entry is a nonnegative integer multiple of $h$, and each type’s total is at most its available mass.

Evaluate each grid objective within $\eta/8$, and choose a schedule maximizing the approximate values. Its exact objective is within $\eta/4$ of the best grid objective. Therefore it satisfies
$$ \mathcal F(\sigma_{\mathrm{chosen}}) \ge\sup_\sigma\mathcal F(\sigma)-\frac{3\eta}{4}. $$
Sorting and applying Section 2 produce a DSIC, ex-post-IR auction with at least this utility.

For rationally encoded numerical input, including supported costs, all grid parameters can be chosen effectively and the finite search terminates. Its running time is not asserted to be polynomial. In particular, the number $J$ depends on numerical magnitudes, rather than only their bit lengths.

4. Equality of the incentive-constrained suprema

Let $U_{\mathrm{BIC}}^*$ and $U_{\mathrm{DSIC}}^*$ denote the utility suprema under the respective incentive and IR requirements.

For any BIC, interim-IR auction, Section 1 gives
$$ U\le\mathbb E[\mathcal F(\sigma)] \le\sup_\sigma\mathcal F(\sigma). $$
Also, DSIC and ex-post IR imply BIC and interim IR. Hence
$$ U_{\mathrm{DSIC}}^* \le U_{\mathrm{BIC}}^* \le\sup_\sigma\mathcal F(\sigma). $$

If all $G_q(D)<1$, Section 3 approximates the last supremum arbitrarily closely using finitely described rational schedules. Section 2 implements their sorted versions by DSIC, ex-post-IR auctions. Thus all three quantities are equal.

The remaining case has a direct construction. If some populated class satisfies $G_q(D)=1$, recruit mass $\delta>0$ of its cheapest type at time zero and pay $c_{q,1}$ upon invocation. Use the same recruitment fraction at every histogram and recruit no other report.

This rule is DSIC: the cheapest type has zero truthful utility, and every higher-cost type receives negative utility from claiming that report. It is ex-post IR. At truth,
$$ S_\mu(D)=(1-G_q(D))^\delta=0, \qquad U=V-c_{q,1}\delta. $$
Taking $\delta$ arbitrarily small proves that its utility approaches $V$. Nonnegative payments imply $U\le V$, and nonnegative virtual costs imply $\mathcal F(\sigma)\le V$.

Therefore, in all cases,
$$ \boxed{ U_{\mathrm{BIC}}^* = U_{\mathrm{DSIC}}^* = \sup_\sigma\mathcal F(\sigma). } $$
In particular, for every $\eta>0$, a finitely described DSIC, ex-post-IR auction has utility at least $U_{\mathrm{BIC}}^*-\eta$. Randomization does not increase this supremum.

5. Exponentially many stages can be necessary

This section supplies the new obstruction to a polynomial bound on the number of stages.

5.1. The rational input family

For an integer $n\ge4$, take one class, one supported cost, and total mass one:
$$ m=\tau=1, \qquad \mu=1, \qquad c=2^n, \qquad V=4c, \qquad D=1. $$
Regularity is automatic because there is one cost type.

Let
$$ d_n=2^{-2n}, $$
and specify the continuous piecewise-linear CDF
$$ G(u)= \begin{cases} u/(2d_n),&0\le u\le d_n,\\ 1/2,&d_n\le u\le1. \end{cases} $$
All input numbers are binary rational. The total input bit length is $\Theta(n)$, and $G(D)=1/2$, so this family avoids the zero-survival degeneracy.

Write
$$ \lambda=\log2, \qquad \mathcal U_n=\frac12\left(V-\frac c\lambda\right). $$
The integral representation of $\log2$ gives
$$ \frac12<\lambda<1. $$
The number $\mathcal U_n$ will be an upper benchmark; I do not claim it is the exact supremum for a fixed $n$.

5.2. An upper bound for every schedule with finitely many stages

Consider a deterministic schedule with $K\ge1$ positive recruitment stages. Write its masses as $x_k$, and let
$$ X=\sum_kx_k\le1, \qquad y_k=\lambda x_k, \qquad z_k=\sum_{\ell<k}y_\ell. $$

Because $G(u)\le1/2$ throughout the horizon,
$$ S(t_k)\ge e^{-z_k}, \qquad S(D)\ge e^{-\lambda X}. $$
The first inequality remains valid for tied invocation times.

Set
$$ P=1-e^{-\lambda X}. $$
Then $0\le P\le1/2$. Expected virtual costs satisfy
$$ c\sum_kx_kS(t_k) \ge\frac c\lambda\sum_ky_ke^{-z_k}. $$

For every $y\ge0$,
$$ y-1+e^{-y}\ge\frac12(1-e^{-y})^2. $$
To verify this, subtract the right side from the left. The resulting function is zero at $y=0$ and has derivative $(1-e^{-y})^2\ge0$.

Consequently,
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_ky_ke^{-z_k} &\ge \sum_ke^{-z_k}(1-e^{-y_k}) +\frac12\sum_ke^{-z_k}(1-e^{-y_k})^2. \end{aligned} $$
Define
$$ \Delta_k=e^{-z_k}(1-e^{-y_k}). $$
These increments telescope:
$$ \sum_k\Delta_k=P. $$
Since $e^{-z_k}\le1$,
$$ e^{-z_k}(1-e^{-y_k})^2\ge\Delta_k^2. $$
Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives
$$ \sum_ky_ke^{-z_k} \ge P+\frac12\sum_k\Delta_k^2 \ge P+\frac{P^2}{2K}. $$

Combining the completion-value and cost bounds,
$$ \mathcal F(\sigma) \le \left(V-\frac c\lambda\right)P -\frac{cP^2}{2\lambda K}. $$
The right side is increasing for $0\le P\le1/2$. Its derivative is at least
$$ c\left(4-\frac1\lambda-\frac1{2\lambda K}\right) \ge c\left(4-\frac3{2\lambda}\right)>0. $$
It follows that
$$ \boxed{ \mathcal F(\sigma) \le\mathcal U_n-\frac{c}{8\lambda K} \le\mathcal U_n-\frac{c}{8K}. } $$

5.3. A schedule close to the upper benchmark

Let
$$ K_n=2^{2n}=\frac1{d_n}. $$
Recruit mass $1/K_n$ at each time
$$ t_k=(k-1)d_n, \qquad 1\le k\le K_n, $$
and pay $c$ upon invocation. With one possible report, this auction is DSIC and ex-post IR.

At stage $k$, every earlier batch has reached the CDF plateau. Thus
$$ S(t_k)=e^{-\lambda(k-1)/K_n}, \qquad S(D)=e^{-\lambda}=\frac12. $$
Its expected payment is the geometric sum
$$ P_n^{\mathrm{pay}} = \frac c{K_n} \sum_{i=0}^{K_n-1}e^{-\lambda i/K_n} = \frac{c}{2K_n(1-e^{-\lambda/K_n})}. $$

For $y>0$,
$$ 0\le\frac1{1-e^{-y}}-\frac1y\le1. $$
The lower bound follows from $1-e^{-y}\le y$. The upper bound is equivalent to $e^y\ge1+y$.

Applying this with $y=\lambda/K_n$ gives
$$ 0\le P_n^{\mathrm{pay}}-\frac c{2\lambda} \le\frac c{2K_n}. $$
Hence this auction has utility at least
$$ \mathcal U_n-\delta_n, \qquad \delta_n=\frac c{2K_n}=2^{-n-1}. $$
Together with the preceding upper bound,
$$ \mathcal U_n-\delta_n \le U_{\mathrm{BIC}}^* \le\mathcal U_n. $$

5.4. Randomization does not avoid the stage lower bound

Let an arbitrary randomized BIC, interim-IR auction operate on this instance, and let $K$ be its random number of positive recruitment stages.

With one cost type, interim IR implies that expected payment is at least $c$ times expected invocation mass. Therefore
$$ U\le\mathbb E[\mathcal F(\sigma)]. $$

For nonempty schedules, Section 5.2 implies
$$ \mathcal F(\sigma) \le\mathcal U_n-\frac{c}{8(K+1)}. $$
This inequality also holds for the empty schedule: its virtual surplus is zero, whereas $\lambda>1/2$ implies $\mathcal U_n>c$.

Thus
$$ U\le\mathcal U_n-\frac c8 \mathbb E\left[\frac1{K+1}\right]. $$
If the auction is $\epsilon$-optimal, then
$$ U\ge U_{\mathrm{BIC}}^*-\epsilon \ge\mathcal U_n-\delta_n-\epsilon. $$
Consequently,
$$ \mathbb E\left[\frac1{K+1}\right] \le\frac{8(\epsilon+\delta_n)}c. $$

If $\mathbb E[K]$ is finite, Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$ 1 \le \mathbb E[K+1]\, \mathbb E\left[\frac1{K+1}\right]. $$
Hence
$$ \boxed{ \mathbb E[K]\ge \frac{c}{8(\epsilon+\delta_n)}-1. } $$
If $\mathbb E[K]$ is infinite, the claimed lower bound holds automatically.

Take the fixed tolerance $\epsilon=1/16$. For $n\ge4$,
$$ 8(\epsilon+\delta_n)\le\frac34, $$
so
$$ \mathbb E[K]\ge\frac43\,2^n-1\ge2^n. $$

Proved conclusion: for this family of input length $\Theta(n)$, every $1/16$-optimal BIC, interim-IR auction uses exponentially many positive recruitment stages in expectation. In particular, every deterministic such auction requires exponentially many stages.

6. What this establishes about computation

The unrestricted optimization is effectively approximable by finite search on rational inputs, and its incentive constraints can be implemented without losing utility.

However, polynomially many explicitly listed stages cannot suffice in general. The preceding family proves this even with one class and one supported cost.

The task permits more general finite descriptions. Indeed, the large schedule in Section 5.3 itself has a compact description:
$$ K_n=2^{2n}, \qquad t_k=(k-1)2^{-2n}, \qquad a_k=2^{-2n}. $$
Therefore the stage lower bound alone neither rules out a polynomial-time algorithm producing suitable compact rules nor supplies an NP-hardness reduction.

The remaining target is to classify optimization under that permitted representation. No polynomial-time algorithm or NP-hardness proof for it is established here.
