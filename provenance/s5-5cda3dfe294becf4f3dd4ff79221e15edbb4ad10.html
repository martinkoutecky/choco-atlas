The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given chore classes \(E\), rational per-capita supplies \(s_e\), and an adversarial measurable arrival stream \(\sigma:[0,1]\to\{0,1\}^{E}\) of binary-additive cost types, with \(\mu_t=\lambda(\{u:\sigma(u)=t\})\), what is the least worst-case \(\alpha\) achievable by a deterministic causal policy? The policy sees each type only as its mass arrives and assigns each mass point \(u\) an integral bundle \(B(u)\), depending only on the stream prefix through \(u\); it must satisfy \(\int_0^1 a_e(B(u))\,du=s_e\) for every \(e\) and \(c_{\sigma(u)}(B(u))\le\alpha\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_{\sigma(u)}(s)\) almost everywhere, where \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(s)=\min_{\pi:\,\mathbb{E}_{B\sim\pi}[a_e(B)]=s_e\ \forall e}\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B\sim\pi}c_t(B)\), and \(\pi\) ranges over distributions on integral bundles.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(E\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) chore classes, with per-capita supplies \(s_e\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\). An integral bundle is a vector \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}^{E}\), including the empty bundle; write \(a_e(B)=B_e\). A binary-additive type \(t\in\{0,1\}^{E}\) has cost \(c_t(B)=\sum_{e\in E}t_eB_e\).
A society is specified by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) distinct positive-mass types and their binary-rational masses \(\mu_t>0\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the supplies and society encoding; finite-data running times are measured in \(m,\tau,L\) and any additional algorithmic parameters.
The arrival order is any Lebesgue-measurable stream \(\sigma:[0,1]\to\{0,1\}^{E}\) realizing that society, meaning \(\lambda(\{u:\sigma(u)=t\})=\mu_t\). A policy initially knows only \(E,s\) and the total arriving mass \(1\); the support, masses and \(\tau\) are withheld.
A deterministic causal policy produces a measurable bundle assignment \(B_\sigma(u)\), irrevocably at arrival. Streams agreeing through \(u\) must receive the same assignment at \(u\). For every stream, feasibility requires \(\int_0^1a_e(B_\sigma(u))\,du=s_e\) for every \(e\); cumulative assignments may never exceed the supplies.
Define \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(s)=\min_{\pi}\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B\sim\pi}c_t(B)\), where \(\pi\) ranges over probability distributions on \(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}^{E}\) satisfying \(\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[a_e(B)]=s_e\) for every \(e\). An \(\alpha\)-MMS guarantee, with \(\alpha\geq1\), requires \(c_{\sigma(u)}(B_\sigma(u))\leq\alpha\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_{\sigma(u)}(s)\) almost everywhere; a zero benchmark requires zero assigned cost.

What is the optimal worst-case MMS approximation guarantee achievable by a deterministic causal policy in this model, as a function of \(m,\tau,s\), when neither the support nor its masses are known in advance?

For finite societies with \(n\) agents, \(m\) individual chores and \(k\) realized unknown types, deterministic online allocation admits no \(o(\min\{n,k\log k,\log m\})\) guarantee even for binary-additive costs; an \(O(\min\{n,k\log^{1+\epsilon}k,\log m\})\) guarantee exists for every fixed \(\epsilon>0\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10960v1)
No result settling this precise continuous causal model was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted chore classes as allowing multiple identical copies: integral bundles are arbitrary nonnegative integer count vectors, with empty bundles allowed and no additional per-person caps.
  • Removed zero-supply classes and used a nonempty class set with positive rational supplies; total agent mass is normalized to one.
  • Kept the anchor's uninformed information model: the society has a finite support encoding, but its support, masses and support size are withheld from the policy.
  • Required measurable assignments, irrevocability and causal agreement on identical prefixes, together with cumulative inventory feasibility.
  • Retained all binary types, including the all-zero type, and interpreted a zero MMS benchmark as requiring zero cost.

Status and new results

The general supply- and support-dependent optimum remains unresolved. The following results give a sharper upper bound, resolve additional cases, and characterize one support-dependent trade-off exactly.

  • Exact MMS is achievable whenever at most three supplies are nonintegral, regardless of the unknown support size.
  • A supply-dependent upper bound can be obtained by examining nested integral subset sums of the fractional supplies. This improves the bound in the notes.
  • For four supplies

$$ s_i=h_i+\frac12,\qquad h_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}, $$
the optimal guarantee on societies with exactly two types is
$$ 1+\frac{1}{h_1+h_2+h_3+h_4+2}. $$
One policy achieves this guarantee for every support size.

  • For arbitrary supplies, one policy achieves exact MMS on homogeneous societies and a factor of two on every society. Together with a lower bound, this completely characterizes the attainable simultaneous one-type and two-type guarantees on four half-unit supplies.

All policies below know only the supplies and arriving prefixes. They use countably many scheduling intervals; no finite-event or polynomial-time implementation is claimed.

1. The benchmark

Identify a binary type with its disliked set $S\subseteq E$, and write
$$ s(S)=\sum_{e\in S}s_e. $$

Proposition. For every type $S$,
$$ \mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_S(s)=\lceil s(S)\rceil. $$

Proof. Every admissible distribution satisfies
$$ \mathbb E[c_S(B)]=s(S). $$
Since bundle costs are integers, their essential supremum is at least $\lceil s(S)\rceil$.

For the reverse inequality, suppose $S\ne\varnothing$ and let $k=\lceil s(S)\rceil$. On the disliked coordinates, choose the bundle having $k$ copies of class $e$ with probability $s_e/k$, for each $e\in S$, and choose the zero vector with the remaining probability
$$ 1-\frac{s(S)}k. $$
These probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one. Every disliked coordinate has expectation $s_e$, and the cost is either zero or $k$.

Independently round each indifferent coordinate between its floor and ceiling, with expectation $s_e$. This produces a finite-support distribution of the required mean without changing the cost.

For $S=\varnothing$, every bundle has zero cost. $\square$

Thus an approximation factor $\alpha$ imposes the integer cap
$$ K_S=\left\lfloor\alpha\lceil s(S)\rceil\right\rfloor $$
on every nonempty type.

2. Deterministic scheduling under measurable interleavings

We first establish the scheduling facts used by the policies.

Scheduling lemma. Suppose a finite distribution of integral bundles has mean vector $w$, and every supported coordinate is at most an integer $M\ge1$. Given $H>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$, there is a bounded Borel schedule
$$ f:(0,H]\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E} $$
using supported bundles such that, for every coordinate and every $0<x\le H$,
$$ \left|\int_0^x f_e(z)\,dz-xw_e\right|\le\varepsilon x. $$
If the supported costs on a specified set $S$ are either zero or a fixed positive integer $K$, the schedule can additionally satisfy
$$ \int_0^x c_S(f(z))\,dz\ge xw(S). $$

Proof. Choose $r\in(0,1)$ such that
$$ \frac{M(1-r)}r\le\varepsilon. $$
Partition $(0,H]$ into
$$ (Hr^{i+1},Hr^i],\qquad i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}. $$
Within each interval, schedule every supported bundle for its distributional proportion of that interval. Fix a deterministic ordering of the supported bundles.

Every complete interval has mean $w$. If $x$ lies in one interval, only its incomplete portion contributes an error. Because supported coordinates and their means belong to $[0,M]$, this error is at most $M$ times the interval length. That length is at most
$$ \frac{1-r}{r}x, $$
giving the asserted estimate. The infinitely many complete intervals below $x$ cause no problem because the schedule is bounded.

For the additional assertion, put all cost-$K$ bundles before all cost-zero bundles in each interval, in increasing schedule time. Let $p=w(S)/K$ be their total probability. On an interval of length $\ell$, the cumulative cost during its first $y$ units is
$$ K\min\{y,p\ell\}\ge Kpy=w(S)y. $$
Adding the exact contributions of earlier complete intervals proves the assertion. $\square$

To apply a schedule to a type during a phase starting at time $a$, define its cumulative clock
$$ x_S(u)=\int_a^u\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma(v)=S\}}\,dv. $$
Set $f(0)=0$. Then
$$ \int_a^u\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma(v)=S\}}f_e(x_S(v))\,dv = \int_0^{x_S(u)}f_e(z)\,dz. $$

Here $x_S$ is nondecreasing and absolutely continuous, with derivative $\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma=S\}}$ almost everywhere. The identity is the absolutely continuous change-of-variable formula. It can also be checked first for interval indicators, using the primitive of an interval indicator, and then extended to bounded Borel functions by simple-function approximation. Consequently, arbitrary measurable interleavings do not change a type’s cumulative scheduled allocation.

3. A stronger sufficient condition: antichains of equality constraints

The key improvement is that equality constraints need not have supply sum one.

Antichain cap theorem. Let $x\in\mathbb R_{>0}^{E}$ be an initial supply vector. For each nonempty $S\subseteq E$, choose a positive integer cap $K_S$ satisfying
$$ K_S\ge x(S). $$
Suppose the equality family
$$ \mathcal U=\{S\ne\varnothing:K_S=x(S)\} $$
is an antichain under inclusion.

Then a deterministic causal policy allocates exactly $x$, never exceeds inventory, and assigns every arriving nonempty type $S$ cost at most $K_S$. The empty type always has zero cost.

The policy uses neither the support nor the masses.

Proof. Put $d=|E|$. For every set outside $\mathcal U$, its cap has strictly positive slack. Define
$$ \delta=\min_{\substack{S\ne\varnothing\\S\notin\mathcal U}} \bigl(K_S-x(S)\bigr), $$
with $\delta=1$ if the indexing family is empty. Choose
$$ 0<\rho< \min\left\{\frac{\min_e x_e}{4},\frac{\delta}{4d}\right\}. $$

Phase $j$ starts at $1-h_j$, where $h_j=2^{-j}$, and has length $h_j/2$. Let $R_j$ be its starting residual inventory and define
$$ v_j=\frac{R_j}{h_j}. $$
We maintain
$$ \|v_j-x\|_\infty<\rho, \qquad v_j(T)\le K_T\quad(T\in\mathcal U). $$
Initially these statements hold with $v_0=x$.

Set
$$ \eta_j=\rho\,2^{-j-3}, \qquad \varepsilon_j=\frac{\eta_j}{2d}. $$

For each possible arriving type $S$, choose a mean vector $w^{j,S}$ as follows:

  • If $S\in\mathcal U$, put $w^{j,S}_e=v_{j,e}$ on $S$ and $w^{j,S}_e=v_{j,e}+\eta_j$ outside $S$.
  • Otherwise, including $S=\varnothing$, put $w^{j,S}_e=v_{j,e}+\eta_j$ everywhere.

These means admit finite distributions respecting the current type’s cap. For $S\in\mathcal U$,
$$ w^{j,S}(S)=v_j(S)\le K_S. $$
For nonempty $S\notin\mathcal U$,
$$ w^{j,S}(S) \le x(S)+d(\rho+\eta_j) <x(S)+2d\rho <K_S. $$
Use the distribution from Section 1, with mean $w^{j,S}$ and integer cap $K_S$. Its disliked cost is either zero or $K_S$. Round indifferent coordinates independently. For the empty type, simply round every coordinate.

All supported coordinates are bounded by the fixed integer
$$ M=\max\left\{ \max_{S\ne\varnothing}K_S,\ \left\lceil\max_e x_e+2\rho\right\rceil \right\}. $$

Run the scheduling lemma on each type’s cumulative clock, with horizon $h_j/2$ and error $\varepsilon_j$. For types in $\mathcal U$, use its additional cumulative-cost property.

Let $D_j(a)$ be the allocation during the first $a$ units of the phase. The type clocks sum to $a$. Since every mean differs coordinatewise from $v_j$ by a quantity in $[0,\eta_j]$,
$$ \left|D_{j,e}(a)-av_{j,e}\right| \le(\eta_j+\varepsilon_j)a. $$

We also claim that
$$ D_j(a)(T)\ge av_j(T) \qquad(T\in\mathcal U). $$
Consider the contribution of a current type $S$, with clock value $z$.

If $S=T$, the additional scheduling property gives the required lower bound directly.

If $S\in\mathcal U$ and $S\ne T$, the antichain hypothesis gives $T\setminus S\ne\varnothing$. Therefore
$$ w^{j,S}(T)\ge v_j(T)+\eta_j. $$
The scheduling error on $T$ is at most
$$ d\varepsilon_jz=\frac{\eta_jz}{2}. $$
Thus this type contributes at least $zv_j(T)$.

If $S\notin\mathcal U$, every coordinate in $T$ receives the extra mean $\eta_j$, so the same argument applies. Summing the type contributions proves the claim.

At phase end,
$$ v_{j+1} = v_j-\frac{2}{h_j} \left(D_j(h_j/2)-\frac{h_j}{2}v_j\right). $$
Consequently,
$$ \|v_{j+1}-v_j\|_\infty\le\eta_j+\varepsilon_j. $$
The total possible displacement satisfies
$$ \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\eta_j+\varepsilon_j) = \frac{\rho}{4}\left(1+\frac1{2d}\right) \le\frac{3\rho}{8}<\rho. $$
This preserves the first invariant. The lower bound on allocation to each $T\in\mathcal U$ gives
$$ v_{j+1}(T)\le v_j(T)\le K_T, $$
preserving the second.

Every residual coordinate at a phase endpoint is positive, because $v_j$ remains within $\rho$ of the positive vector $x$. Since allocations are nonnegative, inventory cannot have been exceeded earlier in that phase. Finally,
$$ R_j=h_jv_j\longrightarrow0, $$
so all supplies are allocated exactly.

The assignment is measurable by the cumulative-clock construction. Phase-start residuals, the current type, and all clocks depend only on the observed prefix, proving causality.

For a complete definition on the arrival domain, assign the empty bundle at time $1$ and at every phase boundary. These countably many assignments affect neither integrals nor almost-everywhere guarantees. All other scheduling boundaries use a fixed deterministic convention. $\square$

The proof works for positive real supplies. This will also allow us to apply it to residual inventories arising at arbitrary stopping times.

4. An improved supply-dependent upper bound

Remove the integer part of each supply:
$$ h_e=\lfloor s_e\rfloor, \qquad f_e=s_e-h_e, \qquad J=\{e:f_e>0\}. $$
Thus $0<f_e<1$ on $J$.

Define the family of integral fractional subset sums
$$ \mathcal I= \{A\subseteq J:A\ne\varnothing,\ f(A)\in\mathbb Z\}. $$
Let
$$ \mathcal N= \{A\in\mathcal I: \text{there exists }D\in\mathcal I\text{ with }D\subsetneq A\}. $$
When $\mathcal N\ne\varnothing$, define
$$ Q(s)=\min_{A\in\mathcal N}s(A). $$

Theorem. One deterministic causal policy, independent of the society, achieves
$$ \overline\alpha(s)= \begin{cases} 1,&\mathcal N=\varnothing,\\[2mm] 1+\dfrac1{Q(s)},&\mathcal N\ne\varnothing. \end{cases} $$

More precisely, an arriving type $S$ receives cost at most its MMS, except possibly when $S\cap J\in\mathcal N$; in that case its cost is at most its MMS plus one.

Proof. Give every arriving agent the constant integral bundle $h$. This allocates the integer supplies exactly and causally.

If $J=\varnothing$, this already gives exact MMS. Otherwise, allocate the residual supplies $f$ on $J$, using the caps
$$ K_A= \begin{cases} f(A)+1,&A\in\mathcal N,\\ \lceil f(A)\rceil,&A\notin\mathcal N. \end{cases} $$
Their equality family consists precisely of the inclusion-minimal members of $\mathcal I$. This is an antichain. The antichain cap theorem therefore applies.

For a type $S\subseteq E$, put $A=S\cap J$. Its original benchmark is
$$ \lceil s(S)\rceil=h(S)+\lceil f(A)\rceil. $$
If $A=\varnothing$, or if $A\notin\mathcal N$, its assigned cost is at most this benchmark.

If $A\in\mathcal N$, the cost is at most
$$ h(S)+f(A)+1=\lceil s(S)\rceil+1. $$
Moreover,
$$ \lceil s(S)\rceil =h(S)+f(A) \ge h(A)+f(A) =s(A)\ge Q(s). $$
Hence its cost-to-benchmark ratio is at most $1+1/Q(s)$.

The baseline allocation and residual allocation separately respect their inventories, so their sum does also. $\square$

For $A\in\mathcal N$, choose a proper $D\in\mathcal I$ contained in $A$. Both
$$ f(D)\quad\text{and}\quad f(A\setminus D)=f(A)-f(D) $$
are positive integers. Therefore $f(A)\ge2$, and $Q(s)\ge2$. In particular,
$$ \overline\alpha(s)\le\frac32. $$

This bound can improve strictly on the earlier bound: an integral subset sum of fractional supplies need not be relaxed when it contains no smaller integral subset sum.

Exact MMS with at most three nonintegral supplies

Corollary. If $|J|\le3$, exact MMS is achievable simultaneously for every support size.

Proof. If $A\in\mathcal N$, both $D$ and $A\setminus D$ above have positive integral fractional sums. Since every fractional supply is strictly below one, each of these two disjoint sets must contain at least two classes. Thus $|A|\ge4$, a contradiction. Hence $\mathcal N=\varnothing$. $\square$

This includes every instance with $m\le3$, and also instances with arbitrarily many classes but at most three nonintegral supplies.

5. A general lower-bound obstruction from balanced subset covers

The following criterion explains why some nested equalities obstruct integral causal allocation.

Balanced-cover obstruction. Suppose $S\subseteq E$ satisfies
$$ q=s(S)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}. $$
Let $T_1,\ldots,T_\ell$ be nonempty proper subsets of $S$, with
$$ p_i=s(T_i)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}. $$
Suppose there are positive weights $\lambda_i$ such that
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda_i\mathbf 1_{T_i} =\mathbf 1_S. $$
Define the finite set
$$ \mathcal H= \left\{ b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{S}: b(S)=q,\ b(T_i)=p_i\text{ for all }i \right\}. $$
If
$$ s|_S\notin\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal H), $$
then no feasible deterministic causal policy guarantees a factor below
$$ 1+\frac1q $$
on every society with exactly two types.

We use $\operatorname{conv}(\varnothing)=\varnothing$.

Proof. Suppose such a policy has guarantee $\alpha<1+1/q$. Run it on the auxiliary homogeneous stream of type $S$, and let $A_e(u)$ denote its cumulative allocation.

For every dyadic $u\in(0,1)$ and every $i$, consider the stream of type $S$ through time $u$ and type $T_i$ afterward. Its two masses are positive binary rationals.

Because $\alpha q<q+1$, the prefix type has integer cost cap $q$. Causality therefore gives
$$ A(S)(u)\le uq. $$
Since $p_i<q$,
$$ \alpha p_i<p_i+\frac{p_i}{q}<p_i+1, $$
so the continuation type has cap $p_i$. Its remaining inventory must consequently satisfy
$$ p_i-A(T_i)(u)\le(1-u)p_i. $$
Thus
$$ A(T_i)(u)\ge up_i. $$

The balanced-cover identity gives
$$ A(S)(u)=\sum_i\lambda_iA(T_i)(u), \qquad q=\sum_i\lambda_ip_i. $$
Combining these identities with the inequalities forces
$$ A(S)(u)=uq, \qquad A(T_i)(u)=up_i $$
for every $i$ and every dyadic $u$.

Cumulative allocations are absolutely continuous: their coordinates are nonnegative and have finite integrals by feasibility. The identities therefore extend to all times. Differentiating yields
$$ B_\sigma(u)|_S\in\mathcal H $$
almost everywhere on the auxiliary stream.

The set $\mathcal H$ is finite because every coordinate is at most $q$. Feasibility of the auxiliary stream now implies
$$ s|_S=\int_0^1B_\sigma(u)|_S\,du \in\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal H), $$
a contradiction. $\square$

The argument requires fairness only on the tested two-type societies. It does not assume a fairness guarantee on the auxiliary homogeneous society.

6. A sharp family: four half-unit supplies with integer parts

Take four classes with
$$ s_i=h_i+\frac12, \qquad h_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}, $$
and put
$$ P=\sum_{i=1}^{4}h_i, \qquad q=P+2. $$

Theorem. The optimal worst-case guarantee on societies with exactly two types is
$$ 1+\frac1{P+2}. $$
The same factor is achievable by one policy for every society on these supplies.

Upper bound. Every fractional supply is $1/2$. The integral fractional subset sums are precisely the six pairs, of sum one, and the full set, of sum two. Only the full set belongs to $\mathcal N$. Hence
$$ Q(s)=s(E)=P+2, $$
and Section 4 supplies the claimed universal policy.

Lower bound. Apply the balanced-cover obstruction with $S=E$ and all six pairs $T$. Give each pair weight $1/3$; every coordinate belongs to three pairs, so this is a balanced cover.

A vector $b\in\mathcal H$ would satisfy
$$ b_i+b_j=h_i+h_j+1 $$
for every pair. Setting $d_i=b_i-h_i$ gives
$$ d_i+d_j=1 $$
for every pair. Subtracting equations shows all four $d_i$ are equal, and then each equals $1/2$. This contradicts integrality. Thus $\mathcal H=\varnothing$, proving the matching lower bound. $\square$

In particular, the optimal universal constant remains exactly $3/2$, attained when all $h_i=0$.

7. Exact homogeneous guarantees can coexist with a universal factor of two

We next give a different policy, useful for understanding the unknown-support issue.

Theorem. For every supply vector, one deterministic causal policy is feasible on every stream, gives exact MMS on every society with $\tau=1$, and gives a factor of two on every society.

A schedule with small error relative to remaining time

For each possible type $T$, choose the benchmark-attaining finite distribution from Section 1, of mean $s$ and cost at most $\lceil s(T)\rceil$. Their coordinates have a common finite bound $M$.

Given $\varepsilon>0$, construct a schedule $F^T:(0,1)\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}$ with these properties:
$$ \int_0^1F^T_e(u)\,du=s_e, $$
and
$$ \left|\int_0^uF^T_e(v)\,dv-us_e\right| \le\varepsilon\min\{u,1-u\}. $$

To do this, partition the two halves of the domain into the intervals
$$ (2^{-(j+2)},2^{-(j+1)}], \qquad [1-2^{-(j+1)},1-2^{-(j+2)}), \qquad j\ge0. $$
Subdivide every interval into $N$ equal pieces, where $N\ge M/\varepsilon$, and schedule the distribution exactly on every piece.

For a point $u$ inside a piece, complete pieces contribute their exact mean. The error is at most $M/N$ times the parent interval’s length. That length is at most $\min\{u,1-u\}$, proving the estimate. Countably many boundary values may be fixed arbitrarily.

Write
$$ P^T(u)=\int_0^uF^T(v)\,dv. $$
For $u<1$, the normalized residual then satisfies
$$ \left\|\frac{s-P^T(u)}{1-u}-s\right\|_\infty\le\varepsilon. $$

Detecting a mixed prefix and completing the allocation

Let
$$ \Delta= \min_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq E} \left(2\lceil s(S)\rceil-s(S)\right)>0. $$
Choose
$$ 0<\varepsilon< \min\left\{\frac{\min_e s_e}{2},\frac{\Delta}{2m}\right\}. $$

At time $u>0$, let
$$ x_T(u)=\int_0^u\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma(v)=T\}}\,dv, \qquad N(u)=|\{T:x_T(u)>0\}|. $$

While $N(u)=1$, use the schedule $F^T(u)$ of the unique positive-mass prefix type $T$. If the current type differs from $T$, give the empty bundle. Such discrepancies occur on a null set during a pure prefix.

If the entire stream is homogeneous, this rule allocates exactly $s$ and gives exact MMS.

Otherwise define
$$ a=\inf\{u>0:N(u)\ge2\}. $$
Then $a<1$, and $N(u)\ge2$ for every $u>a$. Once a mixed prefix is observed, its history determines $a$.

If $a>0$, there is a unique type $T_0$ appearing almost everywhere before $a$, and the residual at $a$ is
$$ R=s-P^{T_0}(a). $$
If $a=0$, put $R=s$. In either case,
$$ v=\frac{R}{1-a} $$
is positive and satisfies $\|v-s\|_\infty\le\varepsilon$.

For every nonempty $S$,
$$ v(S)\le s(S)+m\varepsilon <s(S)+\Delta \le2\lceil s(S)\rceil. $$
Apply the antichain cap theorem to the normalized remaining interval, with initial supplies $v$ and caps
$$ K_S=2\lceil s(S)\rceil. $$
All inequalities are strict, so its equality family is empty. Rescaling arrival time from $(a,1)$ to $(0,1)$ allocates exactly $R$ in physical time, with the stated caps.

Before $a$, assigned costs satisfy the exact benchmark cap. After $a$, they satisfy twice that cap. Every component policy respects inventory.

This rule is causal: a pure-prefix type is determined by the prefix, and after mixing occurs the prefix determines $a$, $R$, and the normalized continuation history. For each fixed stream its assignments are measurable. Assign the empty bundle at times zero and one, and use the boundary conventions from Section 3. $\square$

Thus the optimum when performance is required only on $\tau=1$ societies is one, even though the policy must remain feasible on every society.

8. An exact one-type versus two-type trade-off

Now specialize to four supplies $s_i=1/2$.

Call a pair $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)$ attainable if one deterministic causal policy is feasible on all streams and guarantees factor $\alpha_1$ on every one-type society and factor $\alpha_2$ on every two-type society.

Theorem. For $\alpha_1,\alpha_2\ge1$, the pair is attainable exactly when
$$ \alpha_2\ge\frac32 \quad\text{and}\quad \left(\alpha_1\ge\frac32\ \text{or}\ \alpha_2\ge2\right). $$

Necessity. Section 6 gives $\alpha_2\ge3/2$.

Suppose also that $\alpha_1<3/2$ and $\alpha_2<2$. On the homogeneous all-four stream, the benchmark is two, so the homogeneous guarantee forces cost at most two almost everywhere.

For every dyadic prefix endpoint and every pair continuation, the pair benchmark is one. The two-type guarantee therefore forces continuation cost at most one. Causality and feasibility give exactly the six cumulative pair inequalities used in Section 5. Their balanced sum, together with the homogeneous total-cost bound, forces all pair equalities. Differentiation would give four coordinate assignments equal to $1/2$, contradicting integrality.

Therefore $\alpha_1<3/2$ requires $\alpha_2\ge2$.

Sufficiency. If $\alpha_1\ge3/2$ and $\alpha_2\ge3/2$, use the universal $3/2$ policy.

If $\alpha_2\ge2$, use Section 7’s policy, which gives factor one on homogeneous societies and factor two on all societies. $\square$

Consequently, even on this fixed supply vector, there is no policy that simultaneously achieves the separately optimal factors for support sizes one and two: those separate optima are one and $3/2$, respectively.

9. What remains open

The antichain theorem is a sufficient condition, not a characterization. Likewise, the balanced-cover obstruction provides lower bounds but does not establish that every failure of exact MMS must arise in this way.

For example, consider
$$ s=\left(\frac15,\frac45,\frac25,\frac35\right). $$
The full fractional sum is two, and there are complementary pairs of sum one. Section 4 gives a $3/2$ upper bound. However, the corresponding pair equalities admit integral bundles whose convex combinations have mean $s$, so the lower-bound obstruction used for four half-unit supplies does not settle this instance.
