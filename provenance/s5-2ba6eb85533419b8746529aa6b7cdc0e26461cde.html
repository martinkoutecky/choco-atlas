The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite hospitals \(H\) partitioned into regions, hospital orders, doctor types \(T\), rational mass distribution \(\mu\), type rankings, hospital priorities, capacities \(q_h\), regional caps \(q_r\), and target capacities \(\bar q_h\), compute the canonical doctor-proposing continuous FDA mass matching \(x^{\mathrm{CFDA}}\) and output a certificate of feasibility, continuous stability, and constrained efficiency.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions. Let \(H=\bigsqcup_{r\in R}H_r\), with a fixed cyclic order on each \(H_r\). Types \(t\in T\) have rational masses \(\mu_t\), strict rankings over \(H\cup\{\varnothing\}\), and hospitals have strict priorities over \(T\cup\{\varnothing\}\); unacceptable options rank below \(\varnothing\). A mass matching has \(x_{th}\ge0\), \(\sum_hx_{th}\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_tx_{th}\le q_h\), and \(\sum_{h\in H_r,t}x_{th}\le q_r\).

Define \(x^{\mathrm{CFDA}}\) by continuous doctor-proposing FDA: rejected mass proposes, in type order, to its next hospital; after a proposal to region \(r\), each hospital first retains priority-best applicant mass up to \(\bar q_h\), then hospitals cyclically retain priority-best remaining applicant mass until hospital capacities or \(q_r\) bind, rejecting all other applicant mass. Continue until no rejected mass can propose.

A fractional block is mass of type \(t\) currently at a worse \(h_0\) (or \(\varnothing\)) that prefers \(h\), where \(h\) has spare capacity or lower-priority assigned mass. It is tolerated only if \(r(h)\) is full, all mass at \(h\) has higher priority than \(t\), and, when \(h_0\in H_{r(h)}\), an infinitesimal move satisfies \(x_h-\bar q_h>x_{h_0}-\bar q_{h_0}\). Continuous stability means every fractional block is tolerated. Constrained efficiency means no feasible matching weakly first-order-stochastically improves every type and every hospital's priority-responsive mass allocation, with one strict improvement.

Target. Is the rational-input problem of computing \(x^{\mathrm{CFDA}}\) and a certificate of feasibility, continuous stability, and constrained efficiency in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results. For finite individual doctors, FDA terminates and returns a stable, constrained-efficient matching. Continuum matching models establish stable-matching existence under other continuous choice-function assumptions, but do not analyze this mass-FDA computation problem.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

Conclusion

As stated, the requested certificate need not exist: mass-FDA can return a matching that is not continuously stable under the given definition. Thus the proposed polynomial-time-versus-NP-hard dichotomy does not apply to the requested total computation problem.

The following counterexample uses admissible reserves and does not depend on how the cyclic step divides the remaining capacity.

1. Instance

There is one region containing hospitals \(a\) and \(b\), with cyclic order \(a,b\). Set
\[ q_a=q_b=q_r=1,\qquad \bar q_a=\tfrac12,\qquad \bar q_b=0. \]

There are two types, processed in order \(u,t\), with masses
\[ \mu_u=\mu_t=1. \]

Their strict preferences are
\[ u:\ a\succ\varnothing\succ b, \qquad t:\ b\succ a\succ\varnothing. \]

Both hospitals have strict priorities
\[ t\succ u\succ\varnothing. \]

All data are rational. In particular, each reserve is at most its hospital capacity, and their sum is at most the regional quota.

2. An invariant of mass-FDA

Type \(u\) first proposes its unit mass to \(a\). Hospital \(a\) initially retains \(\tfrac12\) in the reserve stage, and the cyclic stage retains the remaining \(\tfrac12\): there are no other applicants, and neither hospital nor regional capacity prevents this. Thus \(a\) initially holds one unit.

Claim. After every subsequent regional choice, hospital \(a\) holds at least \(\tfrac12\).

To prove this, suppose \(a\) holds at least \(\tfrac12\) before the next proposal. Its retained mass remains available to the regional choice, together with any new applicants. The first stage therefore retains exactly \(\tfrac12\) at \(a\), selecting the highest-priority available mass. The subsequent stage retains additional mass; it does not remove the mass retained in the first stage. Consequently, \(a\) still holds at least \(\tfrac12\).

This proves the claim inductively. It uses the usual doctor-proposing rule in the question: accepted mass stays until rejected, and rejected mass proceeds to its next hospital.

Because the regional quota is one, any feasible terminal outcome consequently satisfies
\[ x_b\le\tfrac12. \]

Type \(u\) never proposes to its unacceptable hospital \(b\). Hence every feasible terminal outcome has the form
\[ x_{ua}=v,\qquad x_{ta}=z,\qquad x_{tb}=w,\qquad x_{ub}=0, \]
where
\[ v,z,w\ge0,\qquad v+z+w\le1,\qquad w\le\tfrac12. \]

Write
\[ m=1-z-w \]
for the unmatched mass of type \(t\).

3. Every such terminal outcome is unstable

We exhaust all possibilities.

Case 1: \(v>0\)

Regional feasibility gives
\[ m=1-z-w\ge v>0. \]

Thus some type-\(t\) mass is unmatched, and hospital \(a\) holds positive mass of the lower-priority type \(u\). A positive amount of unmatched type-\(t\) mass therefore forms a fractional block at \(a\).

This block is not tolerated: the requirement that all mass at \(a\) have higher priority than \(t\) fails, because \(a\) holds type \(u\).

Case 2: \(v=0\) and \(m>0\)

Total regional occupancy is
\[ z+w=1-m<1. \]

Hospital \(b\) has spare capacity because \(w\le\tfrac12<1\). Unmatched type-\(t\) mass prefers \(b\), so it forms a fractional block there.

The block is not tolerated because the region is not full.

Case 3: \(v=0\) and \(m=0\)

Here
\[ z+w=1,\qquad z=1-w\ge\tfrac12. \]

There is positive type-\(t\) mass at \(a\), which prefers \(b\). Hospital \(b\) has spare capacity, so this mass forms a fractional block at \(b\).

If \(w>0\), the block is not tolerated: the mass already at \(b\) is also type \(t\), and therefore does not have strictly higher priority than \(t\).

If \(w=0\), then \(z=1\). The condition concerning higher-priority mass at the empty hospital is vacuously satisfied, but the required within-region inequality fails:
\[ x_b-\bar q_b=0 \quad\text{whereas}\quad x_a-\bar q_a=\tfrac12. \]

The conclusion also holds if the inequality is evaluated after the proposed infinitesimal move. Moving mass \(\varepsilon\) from \(a\) to \(b\) gives
\[ x_b'-\bar q_b=\varepsilon,\qquad x_a'-\bar q_a=\tfrac12-\varepsilon. \]
For every \(0<\varepsilon<\tfrac14\), the required strict inequality is false.

These cases exhaust all possible terminal allocations of the stated form. Every one contains an untolerated fractional block.

4. The instance itself admits a stable, constrained-efficient matching

For comparison, assign all type-\(t\) mass to \(b\), leaving type \(u\) unmatched:
\[ y_{tb}=1, \]
with every other entry zero.

This matching is feasible. Type \(t\) receives its first choice. The only fractional block is unmatched type \(u\) seeking \(a\). It is tolerated: the region is full, \(a\) is empty so the higher-priority condition is vacuous, and the source is unmatched so the within-region inequality does not apply.

The matching is also constrained-efficient. Hospital \(b\) receives one full unit of its highest-priority type. Any allocation weakly improving its priority distribution must retain at least one unit of type \(t\) at \(b\). Hospital capacity forces that amount to be exactly one, and the regional quota then forces all other assignments to be zero. Thus no distinct feasible matching can weakly improve all participants with one strict improvement.

5. Consequence for the target

The obstruction is specifically the claimed stability of the prescribed output, rather than nonexistence of suitable matchings.

On this rational instance, mass-FDA cannot produce an outcome satisfying the requested stability certificate. Consequently, no algorithm can always compute its outcome and a valid certificate of all three properties on the stated input domain.

A revised complexity question requires changing the procedure or the stability definition. The finite-doctor result cannot supply the missing guarantee for the definitions given here.
