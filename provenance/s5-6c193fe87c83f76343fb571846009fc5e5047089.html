The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of applicant and position states with rational masses, preferences, priors, and learning histories, together with a feasible mass matching and consistent beliefs, does there exist a finite sequence of positive-mass transfers between mutually blocking states that updates participating agents’ beliefs by Bayes’ rule and ends at a stable mass matching?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are two nonempty sides and \(m\) intrinsic types, disjoint between sides. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) states with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts listed states, not all possible states. Total mass is \(1\); side totals may differ. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
There are \(r\) publicly distinguishable population labels, each with a fixed actual intrinsic type. A state specifies its label, actual type, strict ranking of opposite-side types and its own type (the single option), individual prior, and private learning history. Rankings and priors may differ within an intrinsic type; labels reveal no intrinsic type initially.
An input flag selects assignment with or without replacement. Each prior is a product of strictly positive binary-rational probabilities over opposite-side labels’ types, conditioned on injectivity in the without-replacement case; the actual label-type assignment satisfies that restriction. A history records labels encountered and their actual types. Beliefs are the prior conditioned on that history; agents know their own and current partners’ types and never forget observations.
A mass matching is a nonnegative binary-rational matrix between states, with row and column sums bounded by their masses; residual mass is single. Initially matched portions have their partner labels in their histories. Portions with different histories or current partner types are treated as separate states, and new states may arise during a path.
For portions in states \(a,b\) on opposite sides, let \(c_a,c_b\) be their current partner types, using their own types when single. They mutually block precisely when \(\alpha_a(\ell_b,t)>0\) for some \(t\succ_a c_a\), and \(\alpha_b(\ell_a,u)>0\) for some \(u\succ_b c_b\), where \(\ell_a,\ell_b\) are their labels. Only positive available portions can block.
A bilateral move selects equal positive rational masses from mutually blocking portions and matches them together, leaving their former partners single. Only the selected portions learn their new partners’ actual types and update by Bayes’ rule; everyone else retains their history and beliefs. A unilateral move lets positive matched mass become single when its own type is strictly preferred to its partner’s type, without learning. All moves preserve population masses and all other matches.
An outcome is stable when every positive matched portion is individually rational and no positive-mass mutually blocking portions remain. Given the initial matching and histories above, finite reachability asks whether some finite sequence of these moves ends at a stable outcome; a sequence of length zero is allowed.
Is this finite-reachability decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,r,L\).
For finite named individuals, every self-consistent initial outcome admits a finite path to a stable outcome under either assignment modality, with unilateral departures used when necessary ([Lazarova–Dimitrov](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/57651/1/Paths_to_stability_IJGT_R1.pdf)). No result settling the mass-state problem defined above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The graded question omitted unilateral departures. Included them because the existence result and subsequent proofs explicitly use them, including after a disappointing learning match.
  • Normalized combined population mass to one, retained unequal side totals and unmatched mass, and required both sides to be nonempty.
  • Resolved the missing continuous analogue of agent names by introducing persistent population labels with homogeneous actual intrinsic types. Learning a label's type is private to the participating portion; splitting mass does not create a fresh label.
  • Specified individual priors as positive rational product distributions, conditioned on injectivity without replacement. This supplies the joint probabilities needed for Bayesian updates, which marginal priors alone do not determine.
  • Interpreted without replacement at the population-label level and required distinct actual types across labels on each side in that modality.
  • Allowed histories and matching status to split states during a path, rather than requiring updated portions to remain in the initial support.
  • Required initial histories to contain current partner labels and correct observations, with beliefs obtained by conditioning; treated the supplied histories as initial private information rather than requiring certification of an earlier blocking path.
  • Specified rational positive transfer amounts, local availability constraints, displacement of former partners, no forgetting, and stability as both individual rationality and absence of bilateral blocking.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). More strongly, every valid instance admits a finite path to a stable outcome, under either assignment modality.

The proof uses rational masses to obtain finitely many equal atoms. It then alternates between stability for the actual types and learning moves. Shared population labels cause no difficulty because the argument does not require independent uncertainty across atoms.

1. The actual assignment always remains possible

Write \(\Lambda\) for the set of population labels, with \(|\Lambda|=r\), and write \(\theta(\ell)\) for label \(\ell\)’s actual intrinsic type.

Fix any portion and its individual prior. Let \(\omega^*\) denote the actual assignment of types to its opposite-side labels.

With replacement, the prior probability of \(\omega^*\) is positive: it is a finite product of strictly positive probabilities. Without replacement, the same product is positive, and \(\omega^*\) satisfies injectivity. Consequently, its probability after conditioning on injectivity is also positive.

Every history consists of truthful observations. If \(E_H\) is the event specified by history \(H\), then \(\omega^*\in E_H\), so the conditioning event has positive probability. Moreover, for every opposite-side label \(\ell\),

\(\omega^*\in E_H\cap\{T_\ell=\theta(\ell)\}\).

It follows that, at every reachable outcome,

\(\alpha(\ell,\theta(\ell))>0\).

Thus every pair that mutually strictly improves its actual partner types is a permitted bilateral blocking pair.

Also, once a history records label \(\ell\), its posterior type distribution is concentrated on \(\theta(\ell)\). Repeated encounters and the order of truthful observations do not affect this conclusion.

2. Rational masses admit a finite atomic realization

Let \(q_a\) be the mass of initial state \(a\), and let \(x_{ab}\) be the initial matching matrix.

Choose a positive integer \(D\) that is a common multiple of the denominators of all \(q_a\) and \(x_{ab}\). Replace state \(a\) by \(Dq_a\) bookkeeping atoms, each of mass \(1/D\). Each atom inherits the state’s:

  • public label and actual type;
  • strict ranking and individual prior;
  • initial learning history.

The total number of atoms is

\(N=\sum_a Dq_a=D\),

because total population mass is \(1\).

For every positive entry \(x_{ab}\), match \(Dx_{ab}\) distinct atoms from state \(a\) to distinct atoms from state \(b\). The row and column bounds guarantee enough atoms for all these matches. Leave the remaining atoms single.

These atoms are auxiliary bookkeeping objects; they introduce no new public labels. An atom learns the original population label of its partner.

Every permitted atomic move has a permitted mass counterpart:

  • a bilateral move selects mass \(1/D\) from each of two blocking portions;
  • a unilateral move separates a matched portion of mass \(1/D\);
  • former partners become single, and only the selected bilateral participants update their histories.

All resulting masses and matching entries remain integer multiples of \(1/D\). Portions can be represented separately whenever their histories or current partner types differ, as the task explicitly permits.

It therefore suffices to construct a finite stable path for these atoms.

3. Complete-information stabilization is available

We use the following complete-information theorem.

Roth–Vande Vate path theorem. In a finite two-sided one-to-one matching market, from any individually rational matching there is a finite sequence of moves satisfying mutually strictly improving blocking pairs that ends at an individually rational matching with no such blocking pair. The result permits unequal side sizes and indifferences between partners; blocking requires strict improvement by both participants. See the theorem and proof, including the treatment of ties, on pages \(1476\)–\(1478\) of [Roth and Vande Vate, *Random paths to stability in two-sided matching*](https://web.stanford.edu/~alroth/papers/1990_E_Random_Paths.pdf).

Here are the hypothesis checks for its application.

Give each atom complete-information preferences over opposite-side atoms according to their actual intrinsic types, and rank being single according to its own type. These preferences are complete and transitive. Atoms of the same opposite-side intrinsic type are tied. No partner is tied with being single, because opposite-side types and the atom’s own type are disjoint and the original ranking is strict.

Both sets of atoms are finite. Their sizes need not agree.

Before applying the theorem, remove each match that either participant finds worse than being single. Each removal is an authorized unilateral move. After finitely many removals, the matching is individually rational.

Every bilateral move supplied by the complete-information theorem is legal under the uncertainty rule, by Section 1: both actual improving types have positive posterior probability. The selected atoms update their histories as required.

Individual rationality persists throughout this stabilization path. Each selected participant strictly improves from an individually rational assignment, former partners become single, and all other matches remain unchanged.

Call a matching actually stable when it is individually rational and has no mutually strictly improving pair for the actual types. We have proved:

From every reachable outcome, a finite permitted path reaches an actually stable matching.

4. Any remaining blocker produces new learning

Consider an actually stable matching, together with its current histories.

Suppose atoms \(i\) and \(j\) nevertheless mutually block under uncertainty. They cannot both have the other’s public label in their histories.

Indeed, if both labels had been observed, their beliefs about those labels would be concentrated on the actual types. Their uncertainty-blocking conditions would then say exactly that

\(\theta(\ell_j)\succ_i c_i\quad\text{and}\quad\theta(\ell_i)\succ_j c_j\).

That would be an actual blocking pair, contradicting actual stability.

Consequently, satisfying any uncertainty blocker at an actually stable matching adds a previously unobserved label to at least one selected atom’s history.

To count this progress, let \(H_i\) be the set of opposite-side labels recorded in atom \(i\)’s history, and define

\(K=\sum_{i=1}^{N}|H_i|\).

Histories never lose observations, so every move weakly increases \(K\). The uncertainty-blocking move just described strictly increases it. Moreover,

\(0\leq K\leq Nr\).

This bound counts learned labels, independently of the number of possible histories or subsequently created mass states.

5. A finite path reaches stability

Perform the following procedure.

  • Remove individually irrational matches by unilateral moves, and follow a finite complete-information stabilization path from Section 3.
  • If no uncertainty-blocking pair remains, stop.
  • Otherwise, satisfy one uncertainty-blocking pair and return to step 1.

Every move is permitted. Every passage through step 1 takes finitely many moves.

By Section 4, each execution of step 3 strictly increases \(K\). Other moves cannot decrease \(K\), and \(K\) is bounded by \(Nr\). Hence step 3 occurs at most \(Nr-K_{\mathrm{initial}}\) times. The procedure therefore terminates after finitely many moves.

At termination, the atomic outcome is individually rational and has no uncertainty-blocking pair.

Finally, aggregate atoms into their corresponding mass states and matching cells. Individual rationality is preserved. If two positive mass portions mutually blocked, atoms representing those portions would satisfy the same blocking inequalities. Such atoms could not already be partners: current partners know each other’s label types, precluding strict improvement toward that same partner. They would therefore constitute an atomic blocking pair, a contradiction.

The aggregated outcome is consequently stable under the task’s definition. All moves and final matching entries are rational, and population masses are preserved exactly.

6. Decision complexity

On the stipulated domain of valid inputs, the decision algorithm returns YES.

Its running time is \(\mathrm{O}(1)\) under a validity promise, or \(\mathrm{O}(L)\) if it reads the supplied input. These bounds hold for all \(m,\tau,r\) represented by the input.

If malformed encodings must additionally be rejected, the stated validity conditions can be checked in polynomial time. In particular, exact rational arithmetic checks masses, prior factors and matching bounds; label tables check truthful histories and actual injectivity. Conditioning probabilities need not be computed, because Section 1 guarantees positive conditioning events. A conservative implementation using schoolbook arithmetic takes \(\mathrm{O}(L^3)\) bit operations.

The atomic construction establishes existence of a path, rather than a polynomial bound on producing one. A common denominator can have value as large as \(2^{\mathrm{O}(L)}\), so the number of bookkeeping atoms can be exponential in the input length.
