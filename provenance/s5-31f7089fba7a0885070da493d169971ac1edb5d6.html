The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) resource classes, XOR types \(t\in T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_r\), and explicit rational bids \((B_{tj},p_{tj})\), choose feasible masses \(x_{t,S}\) assigned to whole bundles \(S\subseteq R\), with \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:r\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_r\). For rational \(\varepsilon>0\), compute a feasible plan within \(\varepsilon\) of the maximum normalized log Nash welfare \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}\log u_t(S)\), when positive welfare is feasible; otherwise report that no positive-welfare plan exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(R\) be a set of \(m\ge 1\) resource classes. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge 1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Thus \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has an explicit finite list of XOR bids \((B_{tj},p_{tj})\), with \(B_{tj}\subseteq R\) and positive rational \(p_{tj}\); empty bundles are allowed. Define \(u_t(S)=\max(\{p_{tj}:B_{tj}\subseteq S\}\cup\{0\})\). Let \(b\) be the total number of bids.
Supplies \(q_r\) are nonnegative binary rationals. A plan assigns nonnegative masses \(x_{t,S}\) to whole bundles \(S\subseteq R\), with \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:r\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_r\). Each recipient receives at most one unit of each class; unused supply is allowed.
A positive-welfare plan has \(u_t(S)>0\) whenever \(x_{t,S}>0\). Its normalized log Nash welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_{t,S:x_{t,S}>0}x_{t,S}\log u_t(S)\), using natural logarithms.
Given a positive binary-rational tolerance \(\varepsilon\), compute a feasible positive-welfare plan with \(W(x)\ge W^*-\varepsilon\), where \(W^*\) is the maximum, or report that no positive-welfare plan exists. Plans are represented by their nonzero rational masses; \(L\) is the total binary input length, including \(\varepsilon\).

Is this computational problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,b,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents, the XOR Nash-welfare threshold problem is reported NP-complete in [Ramezani–Endriss](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/16965/16965D.pdf). Its stated single-bid strengthening is problematic when unlisted bundles have zero utility. No result settling this precise mass problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive type masses to sum to one, giving the stated per-capita supplies and normalized objective a fixed meaning.
  • Specified XOR utility as the maximum price of a contained bid, with zero when no bid is contained; prices are positive binary rationals and empty bid bundles remain allowed.
  • Specified nonnegative supplies, at most one unit of each resource class per recipient, and unused supply, following the graded question's subset bundles and capacity inequalities.
  • Defined positive welfare as positive utility for every bundle receiving positive mass, with zero-mass terms omitted and natural logarithms used.
  • Specified binary encoding of the tolerance, total bid count, total input bit length, and finite rational representation of plans to make polynomial time unambiguous.

Result

The problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,b,L\). This includes exact detection of whether a positive-welfare plan exists and, when one exists, computation of an exactly feasible rational plan whose welfare is within \(\varepsilon\) of the optimum.

The proof has two ingredients: reduction to the explicitly listed bid bundles, and rational approximation of the logarithmic objective coefficients.

1. Listed bid bundles suffice

Let \(J_t\) be the index set of bids of type \(t\). If \(J_t\) is empty for any type, then that type has zero utility for every bundle. Since \(\mu_t>0\), no positive-welfare plan exists. Henceforth assume every \(J_t\) is nonempty.

For each listed bid, define its bundle’s actual utility by

\(v_{tj}:=u_t(B_{tj})=\max\{p_{tk}:k\in J_t,\ B_{tk}\subseteq B_{tj}\}.\)

This is positive because the maximum includes \(p_{tj}\). All the values \(v_{tj}\) can be computed by checking bundle inclusions and comparing rational prices, using at most \(O(mb^2)\) inclusion operations and polynomial-time rational arithmetic.

Lemma. Every feasible positive-welfare plan can be replaced by one supported on listed bid bundles, with the same welfare and no greater consumption of any resource.

Proof. Consider a bundle \(S\) assigned positive mass to type \(t\). Since \(u_t(S)>0\), some bid \(j\in J_t\) attains the maximum defining its utility:

\(B_{tj}\subseteq S,\qquad p_{tj}=u_t(S).\)

Monotonicity gives \(u_t(B_{tj})\le u_t(S)\), while bid \(j\) gives \(u_t(B_{tj})\ge p_{tj}=u_t(S)\). Thus

\(u_t(B_{tj})=u_t(S).\)

Replace the mass receiving \(S\) by the same mass receiving \(B_{tj}\). This preserves the type’s total assigned mass and its welfare contribution. Because \(B_{tj}\subseteq S\), resource consumption cannot increase.

Perform this replacement for every positive entry and aggregate masses assigned to the same bundle. There are only finitely many type-bundle pairs, so this produces the claimed plan. \(\square\)

The permission to leave supply unused is what makes the resource inequalities preserved by this replacement.

2. An equivalent finite linear program

Introduce one variable \(y_{tj}\) for each listed bid. Define the rational polytope

\(P:=\left\{y\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{b}:\ \sum_{j\in J_t}y_{tj}=\mu_t\ \text{for every }t,\quad \sum_t\sum_{\substack{j\in J_t\\r\in B_{tj}}}y_{tj}\le q_r\ \text{for every }r\in R\right\}.\)

For \(y\in P\), construct a plan by aggregating duplicate bundles:

\(x_{t,S}:=\sum_{\substack{j\in J_t\\B_{tj}=S}}y_{tj},\)

and set all other masses to zero. Its type totals and resource consumption are exactly those specified by the constraints of \(P\). Every assigned bundle has positive utility.

Writing \(c_{tj}:=\log v_{tj}\), its welfare is

\(W(x)=F(y):=\sum_t\sum_{j\in J_t}c_{tj}y_{tj}.\)

This equality also holds when duplicate bundles occur: their actual utilities \(v_{tj}\) are identical.

Conversely, the lemma converts every feasible positive-welfare plan into such a vector \(y\), preserving welfare. Consequently:

  • A positive-welfare plan exists if and only if \(P\) is nonempty.
  • When \(P\) is nonempty, \(W^*=\max_{y\in P}F(y)\).

Indeed, \(P\) is closed and every coordinate satisfies \(0\le y_{tj}\le\mu_t\le1\), so \(P\) is compact. Since \(F\) is continuous, this maximum is attained.

There are \(b\) variables, \(\tau\) equality constraints, and \(m\) resource inequalities, besides nonnegativity. The constraint coefficients are zero or one, and the right-hand sides are input rationals. Thus \(P\) has a description of polynomial binary length.

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: feasibility can be decided in polynomial time, and a rational objective over a nonempty bounded rational polytope can be optimized exactly in polynomial time, returning a rational optimizer of polynomial encoding length. This theorem permits equality constraints and feasible sets with empty interior. See [Khachiyan’s polynomial linear-programming result](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

The only remaining issue is that the coefficients \(c_{tj}\) need not be rational.

3. Polynomial-time rational approximation of logarithms

We give an explicit construction to ensure that the running time depends on the binary length of \(\varepsilon\), rather than on \(1/\varepsilon\).

Set \(\delta:=\varepsilon/4\). For each positive input rational \(v=v_{tj}\), we will compute a rational \(\widehat c\) such that

\(\lvert\widehat c-\log v\rvert\le\delta.\)

Write

\(v=2^k a,\qquad 1\le a<2,\)

where \(k\) is an integer. Binary lengths and exact comparisons suffice to find \(k\). Since \(v\) is one of the listed rational prices, its numerator and denominator each have at most \(L\) bits. Therefore \(\lvert k\rvert\le L+1\), and \(a\) has \(O(L)\) bits.

For \(1\le w\le2\), put

\(z(w):=\frac{w-1}{w+1},\qquad H_N(w):=2\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{z(w)^{2i+1}}{2i+1}.\)

Here \(0\le z(w)\le1/3\). Integrating the uniformly convergent geometric series for \(2/(1-z^2)\) on this interval gives

\(\log w=2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{z(w)^{2i+1}}{2i+1}.\)

The omitted tail satisfies, for \(N\ge1\),

\(0\le\log w-H_N(w)\le \frac{2z(w)^{2N+1}}{(2N+1)(1-z(w)^2)}\le \frac{9}{4(2N+1)}\,3^{-(2N+1)}\le3^{-2N}.\)

Define the rational approximation

\(\widehat c:=kH_N(2)+H_N(a).\)

Because \(\log v=k\log2+\log a\), the triangle inequality yields

\(\lvert\widehat c-\log v\rvert\le(\lvert k\rvert+1)3^{-2N}\le(L+2)3^{-2N}.\)

Choose any \(N\ge1\) satisfying

\((L+2)3^{-2N}\le\delta.\)

The smallest such integer can be found by repeated exact rational comparisons. It satisfies

\(N=O\!\left(1+\log(L+2)+\max\{0,\log(1/\varepsilon)\}\right).\)

Since \(\varepsilon\) is a positive rational encoded within \(L\) bits, this bound is polynomial in \(L\).

Finally, these truncated sums are computable using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time. More explicitly, if \(z=A/D\) has \(O(L)\) bits, the denominator of its \(i\)-th summand has \(O((i+1)L+\log(i+1))\) bits. Even using the product of all summand denominators gives only \(O(N^2L+N\log N)\) bits. Intermediate powers, sums, and multiplication by \(k\) therefore have polynomial bit length.

We have thus constructed all rational coefficients \(\widehat c_{tj}\) in polynomial time, with

\(\lvert\widehat c_{tj}-c_{tj}\rvert\le\delta.\)

4. Algorithm and approximation guarantee

Run exact rational linear programming on

\(\max_{y\in P}\widehat F(y),\qquad \widehat F(y):=\sum_t\sum_{j\in J_t}\widehat c_{tj}y_{tj}.\)

If \(P\) is empty, report that no positive-welfare plan exists. Section 2 proves this report correct.

Otherwise, let \(\widehat y\) be an exact rational optimizer of this rational linear program. For every \(y\in P\), normalization gives

\(\sum_t\sum_{j\in J_t}y_{tj}=\sum_t\mu_t=1.\)

Hence coefficient errors cannot accumulate beyond \(\delta\):

\(\lvert F(y)-\widehat F(y)\rvert\le\sum_t\sum_jy_{tj}\lvert c_{tj}-\widehat c_{tj}\rvert\le\delta.\)

Let \(y^*\) maximize the true objective \(F\) over \(P\). Using the coefficient bound and the optimality of \(\widehat y\), we obtain

\(F(\widehat y)\ge\widehat F(\widehat y)-\delta\ge\widehat F(y^*)-\delta\ge F(y^*)-2\delta=W^*-\varepsilon/2.\)

Convert \(\widehat y\) into a plan \(\widehat x\) by aggregating duplicate bundles as in Section 2. Then

\(W(\widehat x)=F(\widehat y)\ge W^*-\varepsilon.\)

The plan is exactly feasible: only the objective coefficients were approximated; all constraints were imposed with their exact rational values. Its assigned bundles all have positive utility.

The rational LP has polynomial encoding length, so its optimizer has polynomial encoding length and is computable in polynomial time. Aggregation preserves polynomial encoding length. The returned plan has at most \(b\) nonzero type-bundle entries.

5. Relation to finite-agent hardness

The [Ramezani–Endriss paper](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/16965/16965D.pdf) states an XOR Nash-welfare hardness result for finite-agent allocations. Such a result does not establish hardness for this mass model.

In a finite-agent allocation, each individual must receive one indivisible bundle. Here a type’s mass may be split among several whole bundles, and its welfare contribution is linear in the masses receiving those bundles. The preceding proof uses precisely that permitted splitting.

The argument covers empty bids, zero supplies, prices below one, and duplicate or dominated bids. No additional assumptions are needed.
