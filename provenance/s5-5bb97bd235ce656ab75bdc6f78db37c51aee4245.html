The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many agent types with rational population masses, finite contract templates specifying eligible roles and coalition sizes, and strict type-specific preferences over feasible integral bundles satisfying scale economies, find a measurable allocation to feasible multilateral contracts such that each participant receives an integral bundle, the outcome is individually rational, and no positive-mass cohort can weakly setwise block it by coordinating over new contracts and bundle changes.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Type \(t\) occupies an atomless interval of length \(\mu_t\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\) explicitly listed contract templates. Each template specifies finitely many roles, each assigned a type, and requires at least two distinct participants. Any eligible participants may instantiate a template; there are no transfers or externalities.
An integral bundle records nonnegative integer counts of participation in each eligible template-role. For each type, the input explicitly lists a finite, downward-closed family of feasible bundles containing the empty bundle, together with a strict total ranking. Let \(D\) be the maximum bundle size and \(L\) the total input bit length.
For an available multiset \(A\), let \(C_t(A)\) be the uniquely highest-ranked feasible submultiset. Let \(P(A)\) be the union of signatory types in its templates. Promise scale economies: whenever \(A\subsetneq A'\), \(P(C_t(A)\setminus C_t(A'))\subseteq P(C_t(A')\setminus C_t(A))\), with multiset differences.
An allocation \(Y\) is a measurable family of concrete contract copies with finite integral incident bundles \(Y_a\). Contract families are parameterized by measure spaces whose role maps preserve participation mass; every copy has all its required signatories. Write \(b(Y_a)\) for the template-role multiset received by participant \(a\).
Individual rationality means \(b(Y_a)=C_t(b(Y_a))\) for almost every type-\(t\) participant. Copies producing the same bundle are preference-equivalent.
A positive-mass weak setwise block consists of a measurable family \(Z\) of new contract copies, a cohort \(S=N(Z)\) of positive mass, and a measurable allocation \(Y^*\subseteq Y\cup Z\), such that almost every \(a\in S\) strictly improves and satisfies \(Z_a\subseteq Y_a^*\) and \(b(Y_a^*)=C_t(b(Y_a\cup Z_a))\). Thus retained contracts must be jointly consistent; contracts involving outsiders may be canceled, and contracts disjoint from \(S\) remain unchanged. An allocation is weakly setwise stable if it is individually rational and has no such block.

Is deciding whether a weakly setwise stable measurable allocation exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,D,L\).

For finite agents with strict preferences over concrete contract sets and scale economies defined using actual signatories, constrained serial dictatorship produces a weakly setwise stable outcome; no polynomial running-time bound is stated. [Huang](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19479v2)
For anonymous continuum markets with bilateral contracts, stability and weak setwise stability coincide; this does not classify the complexity of the multilateral problem above. [Vocke](https://www2.uibk.ac.at/downloads/c9821000/wpaper/2026-01.pdf)

Partial results and scope

I do not resolve the general $\mathrm{P}$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness question. I establish three results:

  • When $D\leq 1$, a weakly setwise stable allocation always exists. Thus the existence decision problem in this case takes $O(L)$ time, including reading the input.
  • The finite systems in the notes give a sound sufficient condition for existence, without needing a measurable selection from an arbitrary existing allocation.
  • Under an explicit standard Borel convention for contract families, the characterization becomes necessary as well. In that framework, existence belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$, and a sparse-support argument improves the exhaustive-search bound.

I also give a promised instance with $m=3$, $\tau=2$, and $D=2$ having no stable allocation under that standard Borel convention.

The measurability distinction matters. For the conditional results, I assume that each template’s concrete copies form a standard Borel space, its role maps are Borel, and participation-mass preservation applies to every measurable subfamily. I also interpret roles as distinct signatory positions. These conventions describe the usual measurable matching framework, but they are stronger than merely specifying arbitrary parameter measure spaces. I do not silently extend the conditional conclusions to arbitrary measure spaces.

1. Finite notation and a sound existence certificate

Let $\mathcal F_t$ be type $t$’s explicitly listed feasible family. Define its individually rational bundles by

$$ \mathcal I_t=\{b\in\mathcal F_t:C_t(b)=b\}. $$

Let

$$ n=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}|\mathcal F_t|, $$

and let $R$ be the total number of listed template-roles. Represent bundles by vectors in $\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}^{R}$, assigning zero to ineligible coordinates.

For every template $j$, choose a reference role $r_j^0$. Define

$$ h:\mathbb R^R\longrightarrow\mathbb R^{R-m}, \qquad h(v)_{j,r}=v_{j,r}-v_{j,r_j^0} \quad(r\ne r_j^0). $$

Consequently, $h(v)=0$ means that the aggregate participation masses at every role of each template agree.

For $b\in\mathcal I_t$ and nonempty $z\in\mathcal F_t$, set

$$ c=C_t(b+z). $$

Call $(t,b,z)$ an improving transition if

$$ c\succ_t b \qquad\text{and}\qquad z\leq c. $$

Because $c\leq b+z$, such a transition satisfies

$$ 0\leq c-z\leq b. $$

Thus its desired retained counts are available, although this inequality alone does not establish measurable retention of concrete copies.

Choose bundle supports $S_t\subseteq\mathcal I_t$, and let $\mathcal E(S)$ be the improving transitions starting from bundles in $S_t$.

Consider the allocation system

$$ x_{t,b}>0 \quad(b\in S_t), $$

$$ \sum_{b\in S_t}x_{t,b}=\mu_t \quad(t=1,\ldots,\tau), $$

$$ \sum_{t,b\in S_t}x_{t,b}h(b)=0, $$

and the blocking system

$$ \lambda_e\geq 0 \quad(e\in\mathcal E(S)), $$

$$ \sum_{e\in\mathcal E(S)}\lambda_e=1, $$

$$ \sum_{e=(t,b,z)\in\mathcal E(S)}\lambda_e h(z)=0. $$

1.1 Realization of balanced prescribed bundles

Lemma. Suppose participants are measurably partitioned into finitely many classes with prescribed finite integral bundles. If aggregate participation masses balance at every template’s roles, the prescribed bundles can be realized by measurable contract families with distinct role occupants.

Proof. For each role, form a slot space containing one slot for every prescribed occurrence at each participant. Its measure counts occurrences and integrates over participants. It is a finite disjoint union of measurable subsets of atomless intervals.

For a template $j$, balance gives the same total slot mass $q_j$ at each role. If $q_j>0$, each role-slot space is measure-isomorphic, modulo null sets, to $[0,q_j)$. Write $f_r(s)$ for the participant attached to the role-$r$ slot at parameter $s$.

Independently rotate each role parametrization by a uniform shift $\theta_r$ on the circle of length $q_j$. For fixed $s$, the participants

$$ f_r(s+\theta_r\bmod q_j) $$

are independent across roles. Their marginal distributions are atomless: each is obtained by integrating a bounded integer multiplicity against participant Lebesgue measure. Therefore any particular pair of role occupants coincides with probability zero.

There are finitely many role pairs. Fubini’s theorem implies that some fixed collection of shifts makes coincident role occupants occur only on a null set of copy parameters. Remove those parameters.

The slot parametrizations preserve measure, so the resulting families give the prescribed incident bundles almost everywhere. Their copy spaces are labelled intervals; hence this construction also satisfies the standard Borel convention stated above. $\square$

1.2 Every block yields a balanced transition mixture

Lemma. If an individually rational allocation with positive-mass bundle support $S$ has a positive-mass weak setwise block, its blocking system is feasible.

Proof. Let $Z$ be the new contracts and let $T=N(Z)$ be the blocking cohort. For almost every $a\in T$, define

$$ b=b(Y_a),\qquad z=b(Z_a),\qquad c=b(Y_a^*). $$

Individual rationality gives $b\in\mathcal I_t$. The block conditions give

$$ c=C_t(b+z),\qquad c\succ_t b,\qquad z\leq c. $$

Downward closure implies $z\in\mathcal F_t$, and membership in $N(Z)$ gives $z\ne 0$.

There are finitely many transition classes. Let $\widehat\lambda_{t,b,z}$ be their participant masses. Current-bundle classes of mass zero can be omitted.

Every new copy has all its required roles. Participation-mass preservation therefore gives

$$ \sum_{t,b,z}\widehat\lambda_{t,b,z}h(z)=0. $$

Also,

$$ \sum_{t,b,z}\widehat\lambda_{t,b,z}=\mu(T)>0. $$

Dividing by $\mu(T)$ produces a feasible solution of the blocking system. $\square$

Proposition: unconditional sufficient condition. If the allocation system is feasible and the blocking system is infeasible, a weakly setwise stable measurable allocation exists.

Proof. Realize the allocation masses using the first lemma. It is individually rational because every prescribed bundle belongs to $\mathcal I_t$. A block would make the blocking system feasible by the second lemma, contradicting its infeasibility. $\square$

This proposition avoids the verifier’s retention-selection objection: it constructs the allocation explicitly and excludes blocks by a necessary numerical condition.

1.3 Equivalent separator certificate

The blocking system is feasible exactly when the origin belongs to

$$ \operatorname{conv} \{h(z):(t,b,z)\in\mathcal E(S)\}. $$

For a nonempty transition set, strict separation therefore makes blocking infeasibility equivalent to the existence of $p\in\mathbb R^{R-m}$ such that

$$ p\cdot h(z)\geq 1 \qquad ((t,b,z)\in\mathcal E(S)). $$

Indeed, a strictly positive separator can be rescaled because there are finitely many vectors. Conversely, multiplying these inequalities by a feasible $\lambda$ and summing would give

$$ 0 = p\cdot\sum_e\lambda_eh(z) \geq \sum_e\lambda_e = 1. $$

If there are no transitions, the separator conditions are vacuous and the normalization equation makes the blocking system infeasible.

Thus positive balanced bundle masses together with such a separator form a sound finite certificate of stable existence.

2. Repair of the characterization under a standard Borel convention

Here I impose the additional measurable-copy convention stated at the beginning.

More precisely, let $X_j$ be template $j$’s standard Borel copy space, with copy measure $\nu_j$ and Borel role maps $f_{j,r}$. For a Borel subfamily $E\subseteq X_j$, define

$$ n_{j,r,E}(a) = \#\{x\in E:f_{j,r}(x)=a\}. $$

Participation-mass preservation means that, for every measurable participant set $A$,

$$ \nu_j\bigl(E\cap f_{j,r}^{-1}(A)\bigr) = \int_A n_{j,r,E}(a)\,d\mu(a). $$

Null exceptional families can be discarded. Individual rationality then lets us work with incident degrees bounded by $D$.

2.1 Measurable incidence and retention selection

The required descriptive-set-theoretic fact is the Lusin–Novikov theorem: a Borel relation between standard Borel spaces with countable sections is a countable union of graphs of partial Borel functions, and its projection is Borel. A statement appears in [Lecomte’s descriptive-set-theory paper, Theorem 1.4](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~dominique.lecomte/articles/treeable-1.pdf).

Apply it to the role-incidence relation

$$ \Gamma_{j,r} = \{(a,x)\in N\times X_j:f_{j,r}(x)=a\}. $$

This relation is Borel, and its sections over participants have size at most $D$. Hence the incident copies at a role admit a measurable enumeration without repetition: choose the first applicable partial function in the countable enumeration, remove that copy, and repeat. At most $D$ selections are needed.

The same argument applies after restricting to any Borel copy subfamily. It follows that restricted incidence counts are measurable.

It also permits measurable selection of a specified number of incident copies at each role. The selection relation is Borel. Its sections over copies are finite, since each copy has finitely many signatories, so its projection onto the copy space is Borel.

This resolves the particular problem of repeated copies having identical signatory tuples: the copies are distinct elements of $X_j$, and the measurable enumeration distinguishes those elements.

2.2 Small cohorts sharing no existing copy

Let $d$ be the maximum template arity. For a participant set $A$, let $N_Y(A)$ be the other participants sharing an existing copy with somebody in $A$.

The preceding incidence results make $N_Y(A)$ measurable. Counting ordered pairs of distinct signatories gives

$$ \mu(N_Y(A)) \leq D(d-1)\mu(A). $$

To justify the estimate, every participation by a member of $A$ reaches at most $d-1$ other signatories. Integrating over copy families counts at most

$$ (d-1)\int_A |b(Y_a)|\,d\mu(a) \leq D(d-1)\mu(A) $$

such reached incidences, and the measure of their participant union is at most their total incidence mass.

Lemma. For finitely many disjoint positive-mass Borel participant sets $H_1,\ldots,H_k$, there are positive-mass Borel subsets $G_i\subseteq H_i$ such that no existing copy contains two members of $\bigcup_iG_i$, after discarding null exceptions.

Proof. First consider one positive-mass set $H$.

Partition the population by type and successively finer dyadic interval partitions. Let $E_\ell$ be the existing copies with two signatories in the same cell at level $\ell$. Distinct signatories are eventually separated, so $E_\ell$ decreases to the empty set.

The total copy measure is finite because

$$ \sum_j k_j\nu_j(X_j) = \int_N |b(Y_a)|\,d\mu(a) \leq D, $$

where $k_j$ is template $j$’s arity. Consequently,

$$ \sum_j\nu_j(E_\ell\cap X_j)\longrightarrow 0. $$

Let $B_\ell$ be the participants incident to these exceptional copies. It is measurable by the incidence results above, and

$$ \mu(B_\ell) \leq d\sum_j\nu_j(E_\ell\cap X_j) \longrightarrow 0. $$

Choose $\ell$ with $\mu(H\setminus B_\ell)>0$, and choose a partition cell meeting that set in positive mass. Their intersection contains at most one signatory of every existing copy. Atomlessness permits reducing its mass to any sufficiently small positive value.

Now choose the $G_i$ successively. Put $K=D(d-1)$. Choose $\delta>0$ so small that

$$ k(K+1)\delta < \frac12\min_i\mu(H_i). $$

At step $i$, the previously selected sets and their existing neighbors remove less than half the mass of $H_i$, provided every earlier $G_j$ has mass at most $\delta$. Apply the preceding single-set construction to the remaining part of $H_i$, and choose a positive-mass subset of mass at most $\delta$.

Each $G_i$ is internally independent and shares no existing copy with earlier selected sets. $\square$

2.3 A balanced transition mixture gives a consistent block

Suppose the blocking system has a solution $\lambda$. For each current bundle used by a positive transition, put

$$ \alpha_{t,b} = \sum_z\lambda_{t,b,z}>0. $$

Its current-bundle class

$$ H_{t,b} = \{a:\text{$a$ has type $t$ and }b(Y_a)=b\} $$

has positive mass. Apply the preceding lemma to obtain independent positive-mass subsets $G_{t,b}$.

Choose $\varepsilon>0$ satisfying

$$ \varepsilon\alpha_{t,b} < \mu(G_{t,b}) $$

for every relevant class. Split selected subsets into transition cohorts of masses $\varepsilon\lambda_{t,b,z}$.

Prescribe new bundle $z$ to the corresponding transition cohort. The blocking equalities balance these new role masses, so the realization lemma constructs new copies $Z$.

For a participant taking transition $(t,b,z)$, let

$$ c=C_t(b+z). $$

Measurably select exactly $c-z$ of their old participations; this is possible by the incidence-enumeration result and

$$ 0\leq c-z\leq b. $$

No old copy contains two selected participants. Thus different cohort members cannot make conflicting retention decisions. Keep an old copy meeting the cohort precisely when its unique cohort member selected it. Keep all old copies disjoint from the cohort, and keep every new copy.

The resulting measurable allocation $Y^*$ gives every cohort member exactly $c$. Each strictly improves, keeps every new participation, and chooses from their old and new availability. The cohort mass is

$$ \varepsilon\sum_e\lambda_e=\varepsilon>0. $$

It is therefore a weak setwise block.

Conditional characterization. Under the standard Borel convention, an individually rational allocation with positive-mass bundle support $S$ is weakly setwise stable exactly when its blocking system is infeasible. Consequently, stable existence is equivalent to finding a support satisfying the allocation system and a transition separator.

No scale-economies assumption was used in this characterization.

3. Complexity and sparse supports

3.1 Polynomial checking of a fixed support

All improving transitions can be enumerated by checking pairs $b,z\in\mathcal F_t$. For each pair, scan $\mathcal F_t$ to compute $C_t(b+z)$.

There are at most $n^2$ candidate pairs. A direct implementation uses

$$ O\bigl(Rn^3\log(D+2)\bigr) $$

bit operations for the bundle-containment comparisons, with ordinary polynomial indexing overhead.

For a proposed support, replace strict positivity by

$$ x_{t,b}\geq\eta,\qquad 0\leq\eta\leq 1, $$

and maximize $\eta$ subject to the allocation equalities. Exactly supported feasibility holds precisely when the optimum is positive.

The blocking system and separator system are ordinary rational linear programs. Hence fixed-support checking takes

$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,D,L) $$

time.

3.2 Polynomial-size rational certificates

The positive-mass certificate can be chosen rational with polynomial bit length. The auxiliary mass polytope is bounded: every mass coordinate lies between zero and its type mass, and $\eta\in[0,1]$. If a positive optimum exists, a rational optimal vertex has $\eta>0$.

For the separator, write

$$ p=p^+-p^-, \qquad p^+,p^-\geq 0, $$

and introduce nonnegative slack variables. The separator inequalities become a rational system of equalities with nonnegative variables.

The elementary rational-point bound needed here is this: a feasible system

$$ Au=b,\qquad u\geq 0 $$

with rational data has a feasible solution whose positive-coordinate columns of $A$ are linearly independent. Indeed, if those columns are dependent, a nonzero dependence permits moving along the corresponding null direction until at least one positive coordinate becomes zero, preserving feasibility. Repeating proves the assertion. The remaining coordinates are determined by a nonsingular rational submatrix.

Cramer’s rule and determinant bounds then give polynomial encoding length in the matrix dimensions and input coefficient bit lengths. This argument handles unrestricted separator coordinates through the split above; it does not assume that the original separator polyhedron has a vertex.

Thus, under the standard Borel convention, a support, positive rational masses, and a rational separator constitute an $\mathrm{NP}$ certificate.

For arbitrary parameter spaces, this remains a polynomial-size sufficient certificate, but necessity has not been established here.

3.3 Sparse-support improvement

Define

$$ k=\tau+R-m. $$

Proposition. If a support has feasible allocation masses and a separator, some such support uses at most $k$ bundles in total.

Proof. Restrict the nonnegative allocation-mass polytope to the given support. It is nonempty and compact. Choose a vertex.

The equality matrix has columns

$$ g_{t,b}=(e_t,h(b)) $$

and at most $k$ rows. At a vertex, the columns corresponding to positive coordinates must be linearly independent: otherwise a small motion in both directions along a dependence would preserve nonnegativity and contradict extremality.

Hence at most $k$ coordinates are positive. Their actual support is a subset of the original support. Removing current bundles only removes improving transitions, so the original separator remains valid. $\square$

It follows that the certificate search needs only supports of size at most $k$. Its running time is

$$ \left( \sum_{i=0}^{\min(n,k)}\binom ni \right) \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,D,L). $$

In particular, it is bounded by either of

$$ 2^n\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,D,L) $$

and

$$ (n+1)^k\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,D,L). $$

Because the feasible families and roles are explicit,

$$ n=O(L),\qquad R=O(L). $$

Downward closure also gives $D+1\leq n$ by taking a deletion chain from a maximum-size bundle.

Under the standard Borel convention these are decision algorithms, including the bound

$$ 2^{O(L)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,D,L). $$

For arbitrary parameter spaces, acceptance is sound; rejection has not been proved conclusive.

4. Complete resolution when $D\leq 1$

Theorem. If $D\leq 1$, a weakly setwise stable measurable allocation always exists, without the scale-economies promise.

For $D=0$, the empty allocation proves the claim: a block would require a nonempty new bundle contained in a feasible chosen bundle of size zero.

Assume henceforth that $D=1$.

4.1 Reduction to template intensities

Call a role acceptable to its type if its singleton bundle is feasible and strictly preferred to the empty bundle.

Discard any template having an unacceptable role. Such a template cannot occur on positive mass in an individually rational allocation. Nor can it occur on positive mass in a block: a cohort member keeping its new participation must choose that singleton and strictly improve over an individually rational current bundle, which is at least as good as empty.

Let $J$ be the remaining templates. If $J$ is empty, the empty allocation is stable.

For $j\in J$, let

$$ M_{t,j} = \#\{\text{roles of template $j$ assigned to type $t$}\}. $$

A template intensity $q_j\geq 0$ uses $M_{t,j}q_j$ participants of type $t$. Introduce unassigned masses $u_t\geq 0$. Feasibility is

$$ u_t+\sum_{j\in J}M_{t,j}q_j=\mu_t \qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau). $$

For a type incident to a template, rank that template by its least preferred role singleton among its roles of that type. Strict singleton rankings imply strict rankings of these least preferred roles across different templates.

4.2 Scarf’s lemma and its hypotheses

Use the following form of Scarf’s lemma.

Let $B$ be an $\ell\times s$ matrix whose first $\ell$ columns form the identity. Suppose $b>0$ and

$$ \{x\geq 0:Bx=b\} $$

is bounded. Let $C$ satisfy

$$ C_{i,i}<C_{i,k}<C_{i,j} $$

whenever $i\ne j\leq\ell$ and $k>\ell$. Then there is a feasible $x$ such that, for every column $a$, some row $i$ satisfies

$$ C_{i,a}\leq C_{i,j} \qquad \text{for every }j\in\operatorname{supp}(x). $$

This is the support-domination form used for fractional stable hypergraph matching by [Aharoni and Fleiner, *On a lemma of Scarf*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0095-8956%2802%2900028-X).

Here take

$$ B=[I_\tau\mid M], \qquad b=(\mu_t)_t. $$

All $\mu_t$ are positive. The feasible set is nonempty because $u_t=\mu_t$, $q_j=0$ is feasible. It is bounded: $u_t\leq\mu_t$, and each $q_j$ is bounded by $\mu_t/M_{t,j}$ for any incident type.

Construct $C$ as follows:

  • The own slack entry is $C_{t,t}=0$.
  • Incident real-template entries are positive integers reflecting the ranking of their least preferred role, with better roles receiving larger values.
  • Nonincident real-template entries have a common value $H$ larger than all incident-template entries.
  • Other types’ slack entries have value $H+1$.

Then every real-template entry lies strictly between the own slack entry and another type’s slack entry, as required.

Apply Scarf’s lemma and obtain feasible masses $(u,q)$ with dominating support.

4.3 Domination prevents every block

Fix a retained template $j$. Scarf supplies a row $t$ dominating its column.

This row must be incident to $j$. If it were nonincident, then $C_{t,j}=H$. Since $\mu_t>0$, the row equation forces a positive own slack or a positive incident-template coordinate. That supported column has row value strictly below $H$, contradicting domination.

Moreover, $u_t=0$, because otherwise the supported own slack entry would have value zero, below the positive value $C_{t,j}$.

Every supported template $k$ incident to $t$ therefore satisfies

$$ C_{t,k}\geq C_{t,j}. $$

Its least preferred type-$t$ role is consequently at least as good as the least preferred type-$t$ role of $j$. Every role of $k$ is at least as good as its own least preferred role.

Realize the masses by assigning disjoint participant sets of mass $q_j$ to each role of each template. The capacity equations leave exactly $u_t$ participants unassigned. Because $D=1$, these role sets are disjoint even when a template repeats a type; matching their interval parametrizations gives distinct signatories.

Now almost every participant of the dominating type $t$ is assigned, and their singleton is at least as good as the least preferred type-$t$ role of $j$. Nobody of that type can strictly improve by taking that particular role of $j$.

Thus a positive-mass block cannot contain template $j$: its copies would require a positive participation mass at that role, all belonging to strictly improving cohort members. Discarded templates were already shown unable to occur in a block. Hence no positive-mass block exists. $\square$

4.4 Running times for this case

For the existence decision problem with $D\leq 1$, the answer is always yes. Reading the explicitly encoded input takes $O(L)$ time.

The proof does not imply a polynomial-time construction algorithm. One explicit construction procedure is available by enumerating dominating supports of $[I_\tau\mid M]$.

A dominating feasible support can be reduced to a vertex support of size at most $\tau$: take a vertex of its feasible face, and observe that domination is preserved when support shrinks. Therefore construction can be performed in

$$ \left( \sum_{i=0}^{\tau}\binom{m+\tau}{i} \right) \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$

time, interpreting terms with $i>m+\tau$ as zero.

5. A promised nonexistence instance with $D=2$

This section uses the standard Borel convention. All templates are bilateral, so distinctness of signatories is automatic.

Take types $A,B$, each of mass $1/2$, and three templates $a,b,c$, each with one role of each type. Suppress role subscripts in bundles.

Let

$$ \mathcal F_A=\{0,a,b,c,a+b,a+c\}, $$

with ranking

$$ a+c\succ_A a+b\succ_A0\succ_A a\succ_A b\succ_A c, $$

and let

$$ \mathcal F_B=\{0,a,b,c,a+b\}, $$

with ranking

$$ c\succ_B a+b\succ_B0\succ_B a\succ_B b. $$

Both families are downward closed.

5.1 Verification of scale economies

Every nonempty multiset of template-roles has signatory-type union $\{A,B\}$.

Suppose availability expands and the chosen bundle loses participation. It must also gain participation. Otherwise the new choice would be a proper submultiset of the old choice. Both bundles would have been available before and after the expansion, contradicting the strict optimality of the two different choices.

Consequently, whenever the lost multiset is nonempty, both the lost and gained multisets have type union $\{A,B\}$. The promised inclusion holds. When the lost multiset is empty, the inclusion is immediate.

5.2 Role balance restricts every individually rational allocation

The individually rational bundles are exactly

$$ \mathcal I_A=\{0,a+b,a+c\}, \qquad \mathcal I_B=\{0,a+b,c\}. $$

Let $v,w$ be the type-$A$ masses receiving $a+b,a+c$, respectively. Let $y,z$ be the type-$B$ masses receiving $a+b,c$.

Balance for $b$, $a$, and $c$, respectively, gives

$$ v=y, \qquad v+w=y, \qquad w=z. $$

Thus

$$ w=z=0,\qquad v=y. $$

Every individually rational allocation therefore assigns only $0$ and $a+b$, with the same mass $v$ receiving $a+b$ on each side.

5.3 Positive empty mass gives a block

If $v<1/2$, both types have positive empty mass. Choose equally sized positive-mass subsets of those empty classes, and give paired participants one new $a$-copy and one new $b$-copy.

Both types choose $a+b$ from this availability and strictly improve. No old contract involves these participants, so retaining all old contracts is consistent.

Hence stability would require

$$ v=\frac12. $$

5.4 The all-$a+b$ allocation is also blocked

Suppose everybody receives $a+b$ almost everywhere.

The old $a$-copies form a measurable perfect matching between the two type intervals: each participant has exactly one old $a$-participation. Under the standard Borel convention, the role maps on this family are injective after null exceptions are removed. Their images and inverse maps are Borel by the injective case of Lusin–Novikov.

Choose a type-$A$ set $G$ of mass $\varepsilon$, where

$$ 0<\varepsilon<\frac14. $$
