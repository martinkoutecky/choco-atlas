The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(T\) of complete assignment types, rational society masses \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}^{T}\), per-capita capacities \(q\), and a specified anonymous continuum lift \(\mathcal F\), compute the largest \(r\in[0,1]\) such that for every positive-mass type \(t\), every alternative report \(\rho\), and every truthful-ranking prefix \(j_1\succ\cdots\succ j_K\), \(\sum_{k=1}^{K}r^k x_{\mu,t}(t)_{j_k}\ge\sum_{k=1}^{K}r^k x_{\mu,t}(\rho)_{j_k}\); equivalently, decide whether this degree is at least \(r_0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) contain \(m\ge2\) objects, and let \(\mathcal P\) be all strict complete rankings of \(M\). A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\subseteq\mathcal P\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Thus \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Per-capita capacities satisfy \(q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^{M}\) and \(\sum_jq_j\ge1\).

The specified anonymous continuum rule at this society is represented by a supplied Boolean circuit \(C\): on a report \(\rho\in\mathcal P\), encoded as a permutation, it outputs a lottery \(C(\rho)\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{M}\), with coordinates encoded as binary numerators and positive denominators. These lottery properties and truthful feasibility \(\sum_t\mu_tC(t)_j\le q_j\) are promises. A unilateral report by a zero-mass individual leaves \(\mu\) unchanged; define \(x_{\mu,t}(\rho)=C(\rho)\). Reports may be outside \(T\).

Define \(d\) as the largest \(r\in[0,1]\) for which, for every \(t\in T\) with truthful ranking \(j_1\succ\cdots\succ j_m\), every \(\rho\in\mathcal P\), and every \(K\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), \(\sum_{k=1}^{K}r^k C(t)_{j_k}\ge\sum_{k=1}^{K}r^k C(\rho)_{j_k}\). The threshold \(r_0\in[0,1]\) is binary rational. Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the numerical inputs and \(s\) the circuit encoding length.

Is deciding whether \(d\ge r_0\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,s\), or is it NP-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?

For finite societies, discounted-dominance inequalities characterize partial strategyproofness ([Mennle–Seuken](https://www.ifi.uzh.ch/ce/publications/PSP.pdf)); an enumeration algorithm computes the degree for rational-valued computable mechanisms, but examines exponentially many profiles and reports ([algorithmic treatment](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.3675)). No result settling this precisely encoded continuum decision problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Complete assignment types are strict complete rankings of individual objects; all such rankings are admissible reports, including rankings outside the society's support.
  • Society mass is normalized to one, and capacities are positive binary rationals with total capacity at least one; at least two objects are required.
  • A unilateral deviation is by a zero-mass individual, rather than an entire positive-mass type, and therefore does not change the society distribution.
  • The unspecified representation of the continuum lift is fixed as a Boolean circuit evaluating its report-to-lottery map at the supplied society; outputs are exact binary rationals, with lottery validity and truthful capacity feasibility promised.
  • Anonymity and ordinal reporting make the individual's lottery depend on the report and fixed society, rather than an unobservable truthful type.
  • The threshold decision formulation is used, avoiding an unspecified representation of a potentially irrational exact degree.
  • NP-hardness uses polynomial-time Turing reductions.

Result

The promised decision problem is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions, and therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions.

Hardness holds with a single supported type, \(\tau=1\), the fixed threshold \(r_0=3/4\), and a circuit whose range contains at most two lotteries. The constructed degree is either \(1/2\) or \(1\).

Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L,s\) exists only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Feasible discounts form an interval

For a supported type \(t\), write its ranking as \(j_1\succ\cdots\succ j_m\). For a report \(\rho\), define

\(a_k=C(t)_{j_k}-C(\rho)_{j_k}\)

and, for each prefix length \(K\),

\(F_K(r)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}r^k a_k.\)

Call \(r\in[0,1]\) *feasible* if all these prefix polynomials are nonnegative, for every supported type and every report.

Lemma. If \(R>0\) is feasible, then every \(r\in[0,R]\) is feasible.

Proof. Fix a type and report, and put \(\lambda=r/R\in[0,1]\). For every \(K\),

\(F_K(r)=\lambda^K F_K(R)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{K-1}(\lambda^\ell-\lambda^{\ell+1})F_\ell(R).\)

To verify this identity, expand the right-hand side. The coefficient multiplying \(R^k a_k\) is

\(\lambda^K+\sum_{\ell=k}^{K-1}(\lambda^\ell-\lambda^{\ell+1})=\lambda^k,\)

including the case \(k=K\), where the sum is empty. Thus that contribution is \(r^k a_k\), as required.

Every coefficient in the displayed identity is nonnegative. Since feasibility of \(R\) gives \(F_\ell(R)\ge0\) for every prefix, it follows that \(F_K(r)\ge0\). This applies to every type and report, proving the lemma.

The feasible set contains \(0\). It is closed because it is the intersection of finitely many sets defined by non-strict polynomial inequalities. Being a closed subset of \([0,1]\), it has a maximum \(d\). The lemma then shows that the feasible set is exactly \([0,d]\).

In particular,

\(d\ge r_0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F_K(r_0)\ge0\text{ for every }t,\rho,K.\)

This equivalence justifies checking the inequalities at the supplied threshold.

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{coNP}\)

On promised inputs, a certificate for a NO answer consists of a supported type \(t\), a permutation \(\rho\), and a prefix length \(K\) such that

\(\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_0^k\bigl(C(t)_{j_k}-C(\rho)_{j_k}\bigr)<0.\)

By the preceding equivalence, such a certificate exists exactly when \(d<r_0\).

The certificate has polynomial length: a ranking requires \(O(m\log m)\) bits, while the type index and prefix length require \(O(\log\tau+\log m)\) bits. A verifier checks that the report is a permutation, evaluates the circuit on \(t\) and \(\rho\), and computes the displayed rational expression exactly.

The arithmetic also takes polynomial time. Circuit outputs have polynomially bounded bit length in \(s\). If \(r_0=u/v\), a common denominator can be obtained by multiplying \(v^m\) by the denominators of the relevant coordinates of both output lotteries. Its bit length is \(O(m(L+s))\), up to the encoding constants. Clearing denominators therefore reduces the sign test to polynomial-size integer arithmetic.

Thus the complement has a polynomial-time verifier, establishing membership in \(\mathrm{coNP}\) on the promised domain. No verification of the promises is required.

3. A promise-preserving hardness construction

We reduce from Boolean circuit unsatisfiability. Boolean circuit satisfiability is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, so its complement is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete. [Circuit-SAT theorem](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~15451-s20/lectures/lec16-np-beamer.pdf)

Let \(B\) be a Boolean circuit on \(n\ge1\) input bits. Restricting to \(n\ge1\) loses no generality: an unused input can be added.

Objects, society, and capacities

Take

\(M=\{a,b,c\}\cup\{u_i,v_i:1\le i\le n\},\qquad m=2n+3.\)

There is one supported type,

\(t:\ a\succ b\succ c\succ u_1\succ v_1\succ\cdots\succ u_n\succ v_n,\)

with mass \(\mu_t=1\). Set \(q_j=1\) for every object and \(r_0=3/4\).

Define two lotteries \(x,y\) by

\(x_a=1/4,\quad x_b=0,\quad x_c=3/4,\)

\(y_a=0,\quad y_b=1/2,\quad y_c=1/2,\)

with all other coordinates zero.

Both lotteries have nonnegative rational coordinates summing to one.

Encoding assignments in reports

For every permutation \(\rho\), define \(z(\rho)\in\{0,1\}^n\) by

\(z_i(\rho)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u_i\text{ precedes }v_i\text{ in }\rho.\)

Define the supplied circuit to output \(y\) precisely when both conditions hold:

  • The first object in \(\rho\) is \(b\).
  • \(B(z(\rho))=1\).

Otherwise, it outputs \(x\).

This function has a polynomial-size Boolean circuit. For example, each pair-order bit can be computed by comparing the object identifiers at each ordered pair of positions and taking an OR over the matching positions. This uses polynomially many gates in \(m\). The circuit then evaluates \(B\), tests the first object, and selects between two constant rational output vectors.

Every assignment \(z\in\{0,1\}^n\) is realized by a report starting with \(b\): begin with \(b,a,c\), and then list each pair as \(u_i,v_i\) when \(z_i=1\), or as \(v_i,u_i\) when \(z_i=0\).

Verification of every promise

The truthful report starts with \(a\), so \(C(t)=x\), independently of \(B\).

Every valid report receives either \(x\) or \(y\); hence every output is a lottery with correctly encoded nonnegative rational coordinates and positive denominators.

Truthful feasibility holds because, for every object \(j\),

\(\sum_{t'\in T}\mu_{t'}C(t')_j=x_j\le1=q_j.\)

The mass is positive and normalized, every capacity is positive, and \(\sum_jq_j=m\ge1\).

Finally, \(m\), the numerical encoding length, and the supplied circuit length are polynomial in the source instance size. The reduction therefore runs in polynomial time and always produces a promised instance.

4. Exact degrees of the constructed instances

If \(B\) is unsatisfiable

The circuit outputs \(x\) on every report. Thus every truthful-minus-report difference is zero, and every discount in \([0,1]\) is feasible. Therefore \(d=1\).

If \(B\) is satisfiable

A satisfying assignment yields a report receiving \(y\). Every report receives either \(x\) or \(y\).

Reports receiving \(x\) impose only equality constraints. For reports receiving \(y\), the differences along the truthful ranking are

\((x_a-y_a,x_b-y_b,x_c-y_c,0,\ldots,0)=(1/4,-1/2,1/4,0,\ldots,0).\)

Consequently, the prefix polynomials are

\(F_1(r)=r/4,\)

\(F_2(r)=r/4-r^2/2=(r/4)(1-2r),\)

and, for every \(K\ge3\),

\(F_K(r)=r/4-r^2/2+r^3/4=(r/4)(1-r)^2.\)

For \(r\in[0,1]\), the first and all later-prefix polynomials are nonnegative. The second-prefix polynomial is nonnegative exactly when \(r\in[0,1/2]\).

Since a report receiving \(y\) exists, the feasible set is exactly \([0,1/2]\). Therefore \(d=1/2\).

We have proved

\(B\text{ is unsatisfiable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d\ge3/4.\)

This establishes \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness under promise-preserving polynomial-time many-one reductions.

5. The requested Turing hardness

Together with membership, the reduction establishes \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-completeness on the promised domain.

To solve circuit satisfiability using an oracle for the target problem, construct the instance above, ask whether \(d\ge3/4\), and negate the oracle’s answer. This is a polynomial-time Turing reduction using one oracle query. Since circuit satisfiability is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, the target problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under the requested reductions.

The endpoint \(r_0=0\) always gives YES, but the hardness construction uses a fixed interior threshold and strictly positive degrees. It applies directly to the specified zero-mass deviation model: the population masses remain fixed while the individual’s lottery changes with the report.
