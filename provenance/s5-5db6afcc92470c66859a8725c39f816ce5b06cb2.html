The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite directed delegation template with rational-mass voter cohorts \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), each with accuracy \(p_t\) and specified delegate options, plus finitely many individually stochastic delegate roles with zero initial mass, choose an acyclic permitted routing of the cohort mass and delegate roles to maximize the probability that the correct voting mass exceeds \(1/2\). What is the approximation complexity of this optimization problem, and does the set-cover inapproximability of Theorem 4.1 persist in this model?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Consider a binary election with correct alternative \(T\). A society is given by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) atomless voter types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
A directed template on these types and \(k\) individually stochastic delegate roles specifies permitted delegations; roles have zero initial mass. Let \(m\) count template arcs and \(L\) be the total input bit length. For fixed \(r\in(0,1/2)\), all type accuracies \(p_t\) and role accuracies \(q_j\) are binary rationals in \((r,1]\).
Choose routing before votes are realized. Each type chooses nonnegative rational fractions summing to \(1\), allocating its own plus received mass between direct voting and permitted outgoing arcs; the same fractions apply to all received mass. Each role either votes with all received mass or forwards all of it to one permitted neighbor.
The arcs used for delegation must be acyclic; all mass must eventually vote. Direct voting is always permitted, including at vertices without outgoing arcs.
If \(a_t\) is mass voting directly at type \(t\), its correct mass is deterministically \(p_ta_t\). A voting role \(j\) casts one ballot for its entire terminal weight \(w_j\), with independent correctness \(X_j\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q_j)\).
Maximize \(P=\Pr[\sum_t p_ta_t+\sum_{j\text{ voting}}w_jX_j>1/2]\); a tie does not elect \(T\). Write \(\mathrm{OPT}\) for the maximum and \(N=2+\tau+k\); an approximation ratio \(\alpha\) means \(P\geq\alpha\mathrm{OPT}\).

For each fixed \(r\in(0,1/2)\), is there a polynomial-time approximation with ratio \((\ln N)^{-C}\) for some constant \(C>0\), or is achieving this ratio NP-hard for every constant \(C>0\)? State running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

For finite independent voters with indivisible transitive delegations and accuracies exceeding \(r\), approximating optimal correct-majority probability within \((\ln n)^{-C}\) is impossible unless \(P=NP\), for every fixed \(r\in(0,1/2)\) and \(C>0\), by a set-cover reduction ([Becker et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.01828)).
[Zhang and Grossi](https://comsoc-community.org/assets/proceedings/comsoc-2021/ZhangGrossi.pdf) give an optimal weighted-delegation construction on complete finite networks with accuracies in \([1/2,1]\), using expected weights from mixed delegations. No result settling the specified atomless-cohort model was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Cohort randomness was unspecified: atomless direct voting contributes exactly its accuracy times its retained voting mass; only individual delegate roles contribute Bernoulli randomness.
  • Cohort splitting and incoming-mass handling were unspecified: types may partition mass among direct voting and permitted delegates, applying the same rational fractions to their own and received mass.
  • Individual role routing was unspecified: each role votes with all received mass or forwards all of it to one permitted neighbor, preserving indivisible individual delegation.
  • The graded question omitted the accuracy restriction of the anchor: all accuracies are binary rationals strictly exceeding a fixed r in (0,1/2), with 1 allowed.
  • Direct voting, timing, and ties were made explicit: every vertex may vote directly, routing precedes vote realization, and a tie fails to elect the correct alternative.
  • The finite voter count has no continuous counterpart: the approximation scale uses N=2+tau+k, counting supported types and individually specified roles, with a shift to handle small instances.
  • For this binary-election template, m denotes the number of permitted delegation arcs and L the total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

For every fixed \(r\in(0,1/2)\) and every fixed \(C>0\), achieving approximation ratio \((\ln N)^{-C}\) is NP-hard. Consequently, an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L)\) with this guarantee would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof uses a direct reduction. It already applies when the template is acyclic, every delegation path has length at most two, and every type has at most one outgoing arc.

1. Set-cover instance and dyadic parameters

Consider a Set Cover instance with a nonempty universe \(U\), a family \(\mathcal S\) whose union is \(U\), and minimum cover size \(s\). Write \(u=|U|\) and \(v=|\mathcal S|\). Finding a minimum cover is NP-hard; the corresponding decision problem is among [Karp’s NP-complete problems](https://studylib.net/doc/27985824/karp1972).

Fix a dyadic rational \(c\) satisfying \(r<c<1/2\). It is a constant throughout the reduction.

Let \(H\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(H\geq u\). Pad the universe to size \(H\) by adding copies of one existing element. Each added copy belongs to exactly the sets containing that element. This preserves the minimum cover size: a cover of the original universe covers the copies, and a cover of the padded universe covers the original universe.

Define

\(A_0=\dfrac{1/2-c}{1-c}.\)

Since \(0<c<1/2\), we have \(0<A_0<1/2\). Choose an integer \(b\) with

\(2^{-b}\leq\dfrac{A_0}{8H},\)

and set

\(A=2^{-b}\bigl(\lfloor 2^bA_0\rfloor+1\bigr),\qquad \mu=\dfrac{A}{H},\qquad B=1-A.\)

These are positive dyadic rationals: \(H\) is a power of two, and

\(A_0<A\leq A_0+2^{-b}\leq A_0\bigl(1+\frac{1}{8H}\bigr)<1.\)

Their bit lengths are \(O(\log H)\), with constants depending on \(r\).

The strict-majority margin when all element mass votes correctly will be

\(\delta=cB+A-\frac12=(1-c)(A-A_0).\)

In particular,

\(0<\delta\leq\dfrac{(1-c)A_0}{8H}<\dfrac{(1-c)\mu}{8}.\)

2. Election construction

For every padded element \(i\), introduce:

  • a supported type \(t_i\) of mass \(\mu\) and accuracy \(c\);
  • a zero-initial-mass gateway role \(g_i\) of accuracy \(c\);
  • the single arc \(t_i\to g_i\).

Introduce one additional supported background type of mass \(B\), accuracy \(c\), and no outgoing arcs.

For every set \(S\in\mathcal S\), introduce a zero-initial-mass set role \(z_S\) with accuracy \(1/2\). Add the arc \(g_i\to z_S\) precisely when \(i\in S\). Set roles have no outgoing arcs.

Thus

\(\tau=H+1,\qquad k=H+v,\qquad N=3+2H+v.\)

Every supported type has positive mass, and all accuracies exceed \(r\). The template is a directed acyclic graph. A gateway either votes with all received mass or forwards all of it to one set role. Every routing therefore terminates after at most two delegation arcs.

The background type must vote directly and contributes deterministic correct mass \(cB\).

3. Exact characterization of every positive-probability routing

Fix any feasible routing. Let \(d_i\in[0,\mu]\) be the mass voting directly at element type \(t_i\), and define

\(D=\sum_{i=1}^{H}d_i,\qquad b_i=\mu-d_i.\)

The gateway \(g_i\) receives mass \(b_i\). Because roles cannot split, this entire mass reaches one terminal role: either \(g_i\) itself or a set role.

Let \(h\) count terminal gateways carrying positive mass, and let \(z\) count set roles carrying positive mass.

Positive probability forces almost all mass through every gateway

If every terminal role votes correctly, the correct mass is

\(M_{\mathrm{all}}=cB+cD+(A-D)=\frac12+\delta-(1-c)D.\)

This is the largest possible correct mass for the fixed routing. Hence positive success probability implies

\(D<\dfrac{\delta}{1-c}<\dfrac{\mu}{8}.\)

Consequently, for every element,

\(b_i=\mu-d_i\geq\mu-D>\frac{7\mu}{8}>\delta.\)

In particular, every gateway receives positive mass.

Any terminal error prevents a correct majority

Each terminal role carrying positive mass receives at least one entire gateway mass \(b_i\). If that terminal votes incorrectly, the correct mass is at most

\(M_{\mathrm{all}}-b_i\leq\frac12+\delta-b_i<\frac12.\)

Conversely, positive success probability already implies \(M_{\mathrm{all}}>1/2\). Thus, for every routing with positive success probability, the election succeeds if and only if every terminal role carrying mass votes correctly.

Independence now gives the exact formula

\(P=c^h\,2^{-z}.\)

This argument covers arbitrary rational splitting by the types. No restriction to integral type delegations has been imposed.

Extracting a cover

Select every set whose set role carries positive mass. For each terminal gateway \(g_i\), additionally select any set containing \(i\); such a set exists because the family covers the universe.

Every element is covered:

  • if its gateway forwards, its destination set is selected;
  • if its gateway votes, its selected replacement set contains the element.

After removing repeated selections, this produces a cover of size at most \(h+z\). Therefore \(h+z\geq s\). Since \(c<1/2\),

\(P=c^h2^{-z}\leq 2^{-(h+z)}\leq 2^{-s}.\)

Routings with zero success probability also satisfy this upper bound.

Attaining the upper bound

Choose a minimum cover of size \(s\). Route every element type’s entire mass to its gateway, and have that gateway forward to a containing set in the chosen cover.

Every chosen set can receive positive mass. Indeed, each set in a minimum-cardinality cover has an element not covered by the other chosen sets; otherwise that set could be removed.

There is no direct element mass, and exactly \(s\) set roles carry mass. The all-correct outcome has correct mass \(1/2+\delta\), and any terminal error prevents victory by the preceding argument. Therefore this routing has probability \(2^{-s}\).

We have proved the exact identity

\(\boxed{\mathrm{OPT}=2^{-s}.}\)

Moreover, every positive-probability routing yields a cover of size at most \(h+z\), with \(P\leq 2^{-(h+z)}\).

4. Amplification rules out every inverse-polylogarithmic ratio

Suppose an algorithm outputs a feasible routing satisfying

\(P\geq(\ln N)^{-C}\mathrm{OPT}\)

in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L)\).

Start with an arbitrary coverable Set Cover instance having \(u\) elements, \(v\) sets, and optimum \(s\). Set

\(S=u+v,\qquad R=S^2.\)

Take \(R\) disjoint copies of this instance: each copied set contains elements only from its own copy. Their combined minimum cover size is exactly \(Rs\).

Apply the election construction to this combined instance, including its padding to a power-of-two universe size. Padding does not change the cover optimum. Consequently,

\(\mathrm{OPT}=2^{-Rs}.\)

The approximation algorithm’s output has positive success probability. Extract a cover of the combined instance as above, and let its size be \(t\). Then

\(2^{-t}\geq P\geq(\ln N)^{-C}2^{-Rs},\)

so

\(t\leq Rs+C\log_2(\ln N).\)

The padded universe size satisfies \(H<2Ru\). Hence

\(N=3+2H+Rv\leq 3+4Ru+Rv\leq 5S^3.\)

For fixed \(C\), all sufficiently large \(S\) therefore satisfy

\(C\log_2(\ln N)\leq C\log_2(\ln(5S^3))<S^2=R.\)

It follows that

\(t<R(s+1).\)

Partition the extracted sets among the \(R\) copies, and let \(t_\ell\) be the number selected from copy \(\ell\). Each copy is covered, so \(t_\ell\geq s\), and

\(\sum_{\ell=1}^{R}t_\ell=t<R(s+1).\)

If every \(t_\ell\) were at least \(s+1\), the sum would be at least \(R(s+1)\). Thus some copy has \(t_\ell=s\). Taking the smallest extracted cover among the copies solves the original Set Cover instance exactly.

The finitely many smaller values of \(S\), below the threshold depending on \(C\), can be handled by enumerating all set subfamilies. This costs a constant depending on \(C\), and hence preserves polynomial running time.

Therefore the assumed approximation algorithm would solve Set Cover exactly in polynomial time, implying \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). Notice that the reduction never needs to evaluate the returned routing’s probability.

5. Input sizes and running times

For a construction with padded universe size \(H\) and \(v\) sets,

\(\tau=H+1,\qquad k=H+v,\)

and \(m\) is \(H\) plus the number of padded element–set incidences. All numerical parameters have \(O(\log N)\) bits, so an explicit encoding satisfies

\(L=O\bigl((m+\tau+k)\log N\bigr),\)

with constants depending on fixed \(r\). Construction takes

\(O\bigl((m+\tau+k)\operatorname{poly}(\log N)\bigr)\)

bit operations, including writing the instance. Cover extraction uses \(O(m+\tau+k)\) graph operations, plus the time needed to read the algorithm’s output.

For the amplified reduction with \(S=u+v\) and \(R=S^2\), the bounds are

\(\tau,k=O(S^3),\qquad m=O(S^4),\qquad L=O(S^4\log S).\)

Thus the entire reduction and postprocessing are polynomial. An approximation algorithm with running time \((m+\tau+k+L)^{O(1)}\) would consequently give a polynomial-time exact Set Cover algorithm.

The source of hardness is the indivisibility of the gateway roles. The deterministic background makes the winning event an exact conjunction of terminal correctness events, while the majority margin prevents fractional direct voting from bypassing those gateways.

Step-by-step verification

1. Source problem and padding

The proof uses minimum Set Cover on an explicitly represented finite, nonempty universe covered by the supplied family.

The cited NP-completeness result applies: Karp’s Set Covering problem asks whether a subfamily of bounded cardinality covers the union of the supplied sets. Thus coverability is already built into that formulation. Excluding the empty-universe case removes only a trivial case. Computing an exact minimum cover would decide this NP-complete problem. [Karp’s original paper, reproduced at the cited link](https://studylib.net/doc/27985824/karp1972).

Padding by copies of an existing element preserves the optimum exactly. Any original cover contains a set covering that element and therefore covers its copies; any padded cover also covers every original element.

Finding: Correct.

2. Numerical parameters and majority margin

The choice of a fixed dyadic \(c\) with \(r<c<1/2\) is admissible. All constructed accuracies consequently lie in the required interval.

The inequalities
\[ 0<A_0<1/2,\qquad A_0<A\leq A_0+2^{-b} \]
follow from the definition. In particular, the strict inequality \(A>A_0\) holds even when \(2^bA_0\) is integral, because the construction adds \(1\) after taking the floor.

The definitions give positive dyadic masses and
\[ H\mu+B=A+(1-A)=1. \]
The margin computation is correct:
\[ \delta=c(1-A)+A-\frac12=(1-c)(A-A_0). \]
The bound
\[ 0<\delta\leq\frac{(1-c)A_0}{8H} <\frac{(1-c)\mu}{8} \]
uses \(A>A_0\) and \(\mu=A/H\), as required.

Minor issue: The condition
\[ 2^{-b}\leq\frac{A_0}{8H} \]
does not imply the asserted \(O(\log H)\) bit lengths. It allows arbitrarily large \(b\).

For a concrete failure of that implication, take \(c=1/4\) and \(H=1\). Then \(A_0=1/3\), and arbitrarily large odd \(b\) satisfy the condition. For those choices,
\[ A=\frac{2^b+1}{3\cdot2^b}, \]
whose reduced dyadic denominator is \(2^b\). Its encoding length therefore grows with \(b\), although \(H\) remains fixed.

Minimum clarification needed: Specify that \(b\) is the smallest integer satisfying the displayed condition, or otherwise explicitly require \(b=O(\log H)\), with constants depending on \(r\). This is a parameter-selection clarification, not a missing mathematical argument.

The construction satisfies every relevant model restriction:

  • There are exactly \(H+1\) supported types, all with positive mass.
  • Roles have zero initial mass.
  • Each element type has one outgoing arc; the background type has none.
  • The template is acyclic, and delegation paths have length at most two.
  • A gateway’s forwarding decision is indivisible, as required for roles.
  • Direct voting remains available.
  • The counts \(\tau=H+1\), \(k=H+v\), and \(N=3+2H+v\) are correct.

The shared-fractions restriction on types causes no additional complication because element types receive no incoming mass.

Finding: Correct.

4. Characterization of positive-probability routings

For a fixed routing, the maximum possible correct mass is correctly computed as
\[ M_{\mathrm{all}} =cB+cD+(A-D) =\frac12+\delta-(1-c)D. \]

Positive success probability requires \(M_{\mathrm{all}}>1/2\). Therefore
\[ D<\frac{\delta}{1-c}<\frac{\mu}{8}. \]
Since each \(d_i\leq D\),
\[ b_i=\mu-d_i>\frac{7\mu}{8}. \]
The earlier bound on \(\delta\) implies \(b_i>\delta\). Thus every gateway receives positive mass.

Because gateways cannot split, each positive terminal weight contains an entire gateway mass. If such a terminal role errs, its weight is removed from the all-correct total, giving
\[ M_{\mathrm{all}}-b_i \leq\frac12+\delta-b_i <\frac12. \]
Additional errors cannot increase correct mass.

Conversely, for a positive-probability routing the all-correct outcome exceeds \(1/2\). Hence success is exactly the conjunction of correctness at every terminal role carrying positive mass.

The independence hypothesis applies to these distinct voting roles, so
\[ P=c^h2^{-z} \]
is valid. Zero-weight roles are appropriately excluded.

Finding: Correct, including arbitrary rational type splitting and the strict-majority convention.

5. Cover extraction and exact optimum

Every forwarded gateway identifies a selected containing set. Every terminal gateway can be replaced in the extracted cover by a containing set because the family covers the universe.

After deduplication, the extracted cover has cardinality at most \(h+z\). Consequently,
\[ h+z\geq s, \qquad P=c^h2^{-z}\leq2^{-(h+z)}\leq2^{-s}. \]
Zero-probability routings also satisfy the upper bound.

For attainment, every set in a minimum-cardinality cover has a private element relative to the other chosen sets. Otherwise that set could be removed, contradicting minimality. Such private elements ensure that the described assignment can give every chosen set positive weight.

With no direct element voting and exactly \(s\) positive set-role weights, the previous characterization gives probability \(2^{-s}\).

Finding: The identity \(\mathrm{OPT}=2^{-s}\) is established, and the maximum is attained on the constructed instances.

6. Amplification

For \(R\) disjoint copies, the cover optimum is exactly \(Rs\), since sets cannot cover elements from another copy. Subsequent padding preserves that optimum.

The extracted cover size \(t\) satisfies
\[ t\leq h+z, \qquad P\leq2^{-(h+z)}\leq2^{-t}. \]
Thus the approximation guarantee indeed implies
\[ t\leq Rs+C\log_2(\ln N). \]

The size estimates are valid:
\[ H<2Ru, \qquad N\leq3+4Ru+Rv\leq5S^3. \]
The last inequality holds for \(S=u+v\geq2\).

For fixed \(C\), the logarithmic additive term is eventually smaller than \(R=S^2\). Therefore
\[ t<R(s+1). \]
Each copy’s extracted cover has size at least \(s\). The averaging argument then forces some copy’s cover to have exactly \(s\) sets.

Padding does not invalidate the partition: each padded element inherits memberships from an element in one original copy, and the selected sets still partition by their original copy labels. Restricting coverage to the unpadded elements covers each original copy.

Taking the smallest extracted cover requires no knowledge of \(s\) and no evaluation of the routing probability. Handling bounded \(S\) by enumeration costs a constant depending on fixed \(C\).

Finding: Correct. The amplification converts the approximation algorithm into an exact polynomial-time Set Cover algorithm.
