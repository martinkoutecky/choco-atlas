The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two sides with type masses \(\mu_t\), capacities at most \(3\), acceptable counterpart types, and a strict ranking of counterpart types for each type, is a proposed feasible population matching \(p\) popular? Each individual receives an integral multiset \(B\) of counterpart types with \(|B|\le b_t\); compare bundles by the highest-ranked type whose multiplicity differs. A rival \(q\) is feasible when the aggregate mass of each type-pair contract agrees on both sides. Allow any coupling \(z_{t,B,B'}\) of the candidate and rival bundles within each type, and ask whether the maximum mass-weighted lexicographic vote for a rival is at most zero.

Conclusion

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Capacities at most \(3\) give polynomially many bundles, and the permitted rival matchings and couplings are represented exactly by a rational linear program of polynomial size.

1. Polynomially many bundles

For each supported type \(t\), let \(d_t\) be its number of acceptable counterpart types. A bundle corresponds to a vector of nonnegative integer multiplicities whose sum is at most \(b_t\). Introducing one slack coordinate gives

\( K_t:=|\mathcal B_t|=\binom{d_t+b_t}{b_t}. \)

This also holds when \(d_t=0\), in which case the empty bundle is the sole bundle. Since \(d_t\leq m\) and \(b_t\leq3\),

\( K_t\leq D:=\binom{m+3}{3}. \)

All these bundles can be enumerated in polynomial time by listing nondecreasing sequences of at most \(b_t\) acceptable counterpart types. This includes repeated types and bundles below capacity, as required.

Let

\( S_t:=\{B\in\mathcal B_t:p_{t,B}>0\}, \qquad N:=\sum_{t\in T}|S_t|. \)

The input explicitly lists these \(N\) positive entries, so \(N\leq L\). For any coupling, a first marginal \(p_{t,B}=0\) forces every corresponding coupling entry to be zero. We therefore need variables only for first-coordinate bundles in \(S_t\).

2. The linear program

Introduce a nonnegative real variable \(z_{t,B,B'}\) for every \(t\in T\), \(B\in S_t\), and \(B'\in\mathcal B_t\).

Maximize

\( F(z):=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{B\in S_t}\sum_{B'\in\mathcal B_t} v_t(B',B)\,z_{t,B,B'}. \)

Impose the first-marginal constraints

\( \sum_{B'\in\mathcal B_t}z_{t,B,B'}=p_{t,B} \qquad(t\in T,\ B\in S_t). \)

For each acceptable pair \((u,w)\in E\), impose

\( \sum_{B\in S_u}\sum_{B'\in\mathcal B_u} n_w(B')z_{u,B,B'} - \sum_{C\in S_w}\sum_{C'\in\mathcal B_w} n_u(C')z_{w,C,C'} =0. \)

These are all the constraints, apart from nonnegativity.

Every objective coefficient is computable from the given strict ranking: compare the multiplicities of \(B'\) and \(B\) in ranking order and use the first difference. The resulting coefficient belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\).

3. Exact equivalence with the election problem

We prove that the feasible points of this linear program correspond exactly to the rivals and couplings allowed in the question.

From a rival and coupling to a feasible LP point.
Let \(q\) be feasible and let \(z\) have marginals \(p,q\). If \(B\notin S_t\), nonnegativity and its zero first marginal imply \(z_{t,B,B'}=0\) for every \(B'\). Delete these zero coordinates.

The remaining entries satisfy the LP first-marginal constraints. Moreover,

\( \sum_{B\in S_t}z_{t,B,B'}=q_{t,B'}. \)

Consequently, each LP balance equation is precisely the corresponding balance equation for \(q\). The objective is the prescribed election score.

From a feasible LP point to a rival and coupling.
Given an LP-feasible \(z\), define

\( q_{t,B'}:=\sum_{B\in S_t}z_{t,B,B'}. \)

These quantities are nonnegative, and the first-marginal constraints give

\( \sum_{B'\in\mathcal B_t}q_{t,B'} = \sum_{B\in S_t}p_{t,B} = \mu_t. \)

The last equality follows from the promised feasibility of \(p\). The LP balance equations give, for every \((u,w)\in E\),

\( \sum_{B'\in\mathcal B_u}q_{u,B'}n_w(B') = \sum_{C'\in\mathcal B_w}q_{w,C'}n_u(C'). \)

Thus \(q\) satisfies every feasibility condition in the question. Extending \(z\) by zeros for first-coordinate bundles outside \(S_t\) makes it a coupling with marginals \(p,q\). Its election score is again \(F(z)\).

Therefore, the LP optimum is exactly the maximum election score in the definition of popularity.

The maximum is attained: every variable satisfies \(0\leq z_{t,B,B'}\leq p_{t,B}\), so the feasible region is closed and bounded. It is nonempty because the diagonal coupling

\( z_{t,B,B}=p_{t,B}, \qquad z_{t,B,B'}=0\quad(B'\neq B) \)

is feasible, using feasibility of \(p\). This coupling has score zero. Hence the optimum \(F^*\) satisfies \(F^*\geq0\), and

\( p\text{ is popular}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F^*=0. \)

In particular, a tied election is handled exactly as specified.

4. Polynomial bit complexity

The number of variables is

\( \sum_{t\in T}|S_t|K_t \leq ND \leq L\binom{m+3}{3}, \)

and there are \(N+m\) equality constraints.

All constraint coefficients are integers of absolute value at most \(3\). Objective coefficients have absolute value at most \(1\). The right-hand sides are the explicitly supplied binary rationals \(p_{t,B}\). Thus even a dense encoding of the LP has bit length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and it can be constructed in polynomial time.

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a linear program with binary-encoded rational coefficients can be solved in time polynomial in its encoding length, with an exact rational optimal value when its feasible region is nonempty and its optimum is finite. Here the coefficients are rational, feasibility was established above, and boundedness guarantees a finite optimum. Its hypotheses therefore hold.

Compute \(F^*\) exactly and test whether it equals zero. This yields the claimed polynomial-time decision algorithm; no numerical tolerance is needed.

That does not contradict this result: finite-agent rivals must be discrete matchings, whereas the present model permits arbitrary real bundle masses and couplings subject solely to the stated linear constraints.

The equivalence proved above uses precisely those population feasibility conditions. It requires neither integrality nor any additional realization by discrete agents.
