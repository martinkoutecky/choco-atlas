The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over provider types \(\theta=(p_{\theta j},c_{\theta j},b_\theta)_{j\in J}\), a dependency DAG \(D\), and a rational workload rate \(\lambda\) of workflow copies per provider, determine the exact, approximation, or parameterized complexity of finding a maximum-expected-welfare assignment of task occurrences to provider mass, where each unit of mass receives an integral bundle of size at most \(b_\theta\), and computing the limiting per-provider externality payment from the paper’s rule.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Normalize \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Input also includes \(m\geq1\) tasks \(J\), a dependency DAG \(D\), nonnegative rational completion values \(v_j\), and rational workload \(\lambda>0\) copies per provider. Types specify rational \(p_{\theta j}\in[0,1]\), rational \(c_{\theta j}\geq0\), and integer capacities \(b_\theta\geq1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each workflow copy contains one occurrence of every task. Assignments are fixed before execution; an occurrence has at most one provider and may be left unassigned. Tasks execute topologically, only after all prerequisites succeed; blocked tasks incur no cost. Attempt outcomes are independent Bernoulli trials with the assigned provider's probability, and attempting an occurrence costs \(c_{\theta j}\) regardless of success. Welfare is total completed-task value minus total incurred cost.
Provider mass may be split among integral bundles containing at most \(b_\theta\) occurrences, possibly from different copies; blocked occurrences still consume assigned capacity. Record an allocation by rational masses \(x_a\geq0\) of workflow patterns \(a:J\to\{0\}\cup\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\), where \(0\) means unassigned, with \(\sum_a x_a=\lambda\) and \(\sum_a x_a|a^{-1}(\theta)|\leq\mu_\theta b_\theta\). Let \(F(s)\) denote maximum expected welfare for provider masses \(s\), keeping workload \(\lambda\) fixed even when \(\sum_\theta s_\theta\neq1\).
The discrete outcome payment is \(R_i=V(z)-C_{-i}(z)-W^*_{-i}\), where \(z\) is the realized completion vector, \(C_{-i}(z)\) is the cost actually incurred by other providers, and \(W^*_{-i}\) is optimal expected welfare without provider \(i\). Define its continuous expected payment, averaged over providers of type \(\theta\), as \(r_\theta=C_\theta/\mu_\theta+\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}[F(\mu)-F(\mu-\varepsilon e_\theta)]/\varepsilon\), where \(C_\theta\) is that type's total expected incurred cost in the chosen optimum and \(e_\theta\) is its coordinate vector. Any maximizing allocation is allowed; payments correspond to that allocation.

Is finding an exact maximum-expected-welfare mass assignment and computing its limiting expected payments polynomial-time solvable, or NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), \(L\), and \(b_{\max}=\max_\theta b_\theta\).

For discrete providers without capacity constraints, the outcome-payment mechanism is truthful, individually rational, and welfare maximizing at the truthful equilibrium, and pays unassigned providers zero; this is an equilibrium existence result, not a computational bound. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.0595). No result settling this precise continuous assignment-and-payment problem was located.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(\tau=2\), \(b_{\max}=1\), and every dependency path has at most two edges. The decision problem “is optimal expected welfare at least a given rational threshold?” is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

An exact maximizing mass assignment and all its limiting expected payments can nevertheless be computed in

\((\tau+1)^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}\!\bigl(m,\tau,L,\log(1+b_{\max})\bigr)\)

bit operations. Thus the general problem is not polynomial-time solvable unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). The dependence on capacities is through their binary encoding, rather than enumeration up to \(b_{\max}\).

The proof below establishes hardness for the allocation problem and gives an exact algorithm for the joint allocation-and-payment task.

1. Expected welfare and the allocation linear program

For a task \(j\), let \(A_j\) be its set of distinct strict ancestors in the dependency DAG. Introduce \(p_{0j}=c_{0j}=0\) for an unassigned task.

For a workflow pattern \(a\), define

\(q_j(a)=\prod_{k\in A_j}p_{a(k),k},\qquad n_\theta(a)=|\{j:a(j)=\theta\}|.\)

The empty product is \(1\). Pre-sample an independent Bernoulli outcome for each assigned occurrence. An assigned task \(j\) is attempted exactly when every strict ancestor’s pre-sampled outcome is successful. Consequently, its attempt probability is \(q_j(a)\), and its completion probability is \(q_j(a)p_{a(j),j}\).

Shared ancestors appear once in this product: one ancestor occurrence has one outcome, even when multiple dependency paths pass through it.

The pattern’s expected welfare and type-specific expected costs are therefore

\(w(a)=\sum_{j\in J}q_j(a)\bigl(v_jp_{a(j),j}-c_{a(j),j}\bigr),\)

\(k_\theta(a)=\sum_{j:a(j)=\theta}q_j(a)c_{\theta j}.\)

These rational coefficients are computable in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,L)\) bit operations per pattern.

Using exactly the allocation representation specified in the question, optimal welfare is the following linear program:

\(\displaystyle F(s)=\max_{x\geq0}\left\{\sum_a w(a)x_a:\ \sum_a x_a=\lambda,\quad \sum_a n_\theta(a)x_a\leq s_\theta b_\theta\ \text{for every }\theta\right\}.\)

There are \(N=(\tau+1)^m\) patterns. The all-unassigned pattern makes this program feasible, and the workload equality makes its feasible region bounded.

2. A reduction from maximum independent set

Let \(G=(U,E)\) be a simple undirected graph, with \(n\geq1\) vertices and \(e\) edges. Orient each edge arbitrarily. This orientation will only specify the gadgets; it need not be acyclic.

Write \(d_i^+\) and \(d_i^-\) for the resulting outdegree and indegree of vertex \(i\), and let \(\alpha(G)\) denote the maximum independent-set size.

Independent-set decision is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete: equivalently, the complement of an independent set is a vertex cover, whose decision problem is covered by [Karp’s NP-completeness theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Tasks, types, and dependencies

There are two supported types, denoted \(H\) and \(B\). Put

\(\gamma=\frac{47}{200},\qquad h_i=\frac{632+81d_i^++16d_i^-}{1000}.\)

Construct:

  • A root task \(r_i\) for each vertex \(i\).
  • A gate task \(g_{ij}\) and terminal task \(t_{ij}\) for each oriented edge \(i\to j\).
  • \(K=n+e\) independent auxiliary tasks.

Their parameters are:

| Task | Completion value | Type \(H\): \((p,c)\) | Type \(B\): \((p,c)\) | Immediate prerequisites |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| \(r_i\) | \(2\) | \((1,h_i)\) | \((4/5,0)\) | None |
| \(g_{ij}\) | \(1\) | \((1,4/5)\) | \((1/2,0)\) | \(r_i\) |
| \(t_{ij}\) | \(1/10\) | \((1,0)\) | \((0,0)\) | \(g_{ij},r_j\) |
| Auxiliary task | \(\gamma\) | \((0,0)\) | \((1,0)\) | None |

The dependency graph is a DAG: roots precede gates, which precede terminals. Every path has at most two edges.

The total number of tasks is

\(m=n+2e+K=2n+3e.\)

Choose a power of two \(T\) satisfying \(T\geq m+K\), and set

\(\lambda=\frac1T,\qquad \mu_B=\frac KT,\qquad \mu_H=1-\frac KT,\qquad b_H=b_B=1.\)

Both masses are positive, are dyadic rationals, and sum to \(1\). All input numbers have polynomial encoding length.

Divide pattern masses by \(\lambda\). In these units, patterns have total mass \(1\), type \(B\) has aggregate capacity \(K\), and type \(H\) has aggregate capacity \(T-K\geq m\). Thus type \(H\)’s capacity cannot restrict any allocation.

An individual pattern can contain many assignments to one type: its occurrences are assigned to different providers of that type. This is compatible with capacity one and the allocation model in the question.

3. Auxiliary tasks implement an exact assignment charge

Call the roots, gates, and terminals the *real tasks*. For a real-task pattern \(a\), let \(W_R(a)\) be its expected welfare and \(d_B(a)\) its number of type-\(B\) assignments.

Assignments with probability zero and cost zero can be removed without changing any completion event or incurred cost. We may therefore exclude type-\(B\) terminal assignments and type-\(H\) auxiliary assignments. It follows that

\(0\leq d_B(a)\leq n+e=K.\)

Let \(M=T F(\mu)\), the optimum in the units where workflow mass equals \(1\). Then

\(\displaystyle M=\gamma K+\max_a\bigl(W_R(a)-\gamma d_B(a)\bigr).\)

To prove the upper bound, consider any feasible allocation. If its real tasks consume average type-\(B\) capacity \(\overline d_B\), the auxiliary tasks can consume at most \(K-\overline d_B\). Their welfare is therefore at most \(\gamma(K-\overline d_B)\). Total welfare is at most

\(\gamma K+\mathbb E\bigl[W_R(a)-\gamma d_B(a)\bigr]\leq\gamma K+\max_a\bigl(W_R(a)-\gamma d_B(a)\bigr).\)

For the reverse bound, take any real-task pattern \(a\), and assign exactly \(K-d_B(a)\) auxiliary tasks to type \(B\). This uses exactly \(K\) type-\(B\) occurrences and at most \(m\) type-\(H\) occurrences, so it is feasible and achieves the claimed value.

This identity also proves that fractional mixtures cannot improve upon the best adjusted real-task pattern.

4. The adjusted objective encodes an independent set

Optimize \(W_R(a)-\gamma d_B(a)\). Let \(P_i\) be the success probability chosen for root \(r_i\).

For fixed root choices, assigning a terminal to \(H\) has zero cost and weakly increases welfare. Since \(H\)’s capacity is unrestricted, each edge gadget can be optimized independently.

For an oriented edge \(i\to j\), its optimal adjusted contribution is

\(\displaystyle f(P_i,P_j)=\max\left\{0,\ P_i\left(\frac15+\frac{P_j}{10}\right),\ P_i\left(\frac12+\frac{P_j}{20}\right)-\gamma\right\}.\)

These three alternatives correspond respectively to leaving the gate unassigned, assigning it to \(H\), and assigning it to \(B\).

Every root is assigned

The function \(f\) is nondecreasing in each argument on \([0,1]^2\): each expression inside the maximum has that property.

Replacing an unassigned root by a type-\(B\) assignment raises its own adjusted contribution from zero to

\(2\cdot\frac45-\gamma=\frac{273}{200}>0,\)

and cannot decrease any incident gadget’s optimized contribution. Thus every maximizing adjusted pattern assigns every root.

Define \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\) by \(x_i=1\) when root \(r_i\) is assigned to \(H\), and \(x_i=0\) when it is assigned to \(B\). Then

\(P_i=\frac45+\frac{x_i}{5}.\)

Gate choices and edge contributions

The adjusted type-\(B\) gate contribution minus the type-\(H\) contribution is

\(\displaystyle P_i\left(\frac3{10}-\frac{P_j}{20}\right)-\frac{47}{200}.\)

For \(P_i=4/5\), this difference is either \(-27/1000\) or \(-35/1000\). For \(P_i=1\), it is either \(25/1000\) or \(15/1000\). The type-\(H\) contribution is always positive.

Therefore the maximizing gate uses \(H\) exactly when \(x_i=0\), and \(B\) exactly when \(x_i=1\). Direct substitution gives:

| \(x_i\) | \(x_j\) | \(f(P_i,P_j)\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| \(0\) | \(0\) | \(224/1000\) |
| \(0\) | \(1\) | \(240/1000\) |
| \(1\) | \(0\) | \(305/1000\) |
| \(1\) | \(1\) | \(315/1000\) |

Equivalently,

\(\displaystyle f(P_i,P_j)=\frac{224+81x_i+16x_j-6x_ix_j}{1000}.\)

The root’s adjusted contribution is

\(\displaystyle \frac{273}{200}+\left(\frac{635}{1000}-h_i\right)x_i.\)

By the definition of \(h_i\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{635}{1000}-h_i=\frac{3-81d_i^+-16d_i^-}{1000}.\)

Summing all roots and edge gadgets cancels the degree-dependent linear terms. The adjusted real-task welfare becomes

\(\displaystyle \frac{273n}{200}+\frac{224e}{1000}+\frac1{1000}\left(3\sum_i x_i-6\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}x_ix_j\right).\)

Let \(S=\{i:x_i=1\}\), and let \(e(S)\) count edges with both endpoints in \(S\). The variable part is \(3|S|-6e(S)\).

If \(S\) contains an edge, remove a vertex having \(d\geq1\) neighbors within \(S\). The variable part increases by

\(-3+6d\geq3.\)

Hence every maximizing set is independent. On independent sets the variable part is \(3|S|\), so its maximum is \(3\alpha(G)\).

Restoring the auxiliary-task constant proves the exact identity

\(\displaystyle F(\mu)=\frac{1600n+459e+3\alpha(G)}{1000T}.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \alpha(G)\geq k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(\mu)\geq\frac{1600n+459e+3k}{1000T}.\)

This is a polynomial-time reduction, proving the claimed hardness with \(\tau=2\) and \(b_{\max}=1\).

5. Exact limiting payments

The dual of the allocation linear program is

\(\displaystyle F(s)=\min_{t,y}\left\{\lambda t+\sum_\theta s_\theta b_\theta y_\theta:\ y_\theta\geq0,\quad t+\sum_\theta n_\theta(a)y_\theta\geq w(a)\ \text{for every }a\right\}.\)

The all-unassigned pattern implies \(t\geq0\). Let \(\mathcal D^*(\mu)\) be the optimal dual face at \(\mu\).

Because \(\lambda>0\) and every supported mass \(\mu_\theta>0\), this face is compact: its nonnegative coordinates satisfy

\(\lambda t+\sum_\theta\mu_\theta b_\theta y_\theta=F(\mu).\)

The required one-sided marginal is

\(\displaystyle \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\frac{F(\mu)-F(\mu-\varepsilon e_\theta)}{\varepsilon}=b_\theta\max_{(t,y)\in\mathcal D^*(\mu)}y_\theta.\)

Here is a justification that includes degeneracy. The dual feasible polyhedron has finitely many vertices. For provider masses sufficiently close to \(\mu\), all objective coefficients are positive, so a minimum is attained at a vertex. Thus, locally, \(F\) is the minimum of finitely many affine functions.

For sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), vertices not optimal at \(\mu\) cannot become optimal: their positive objective gaps dominate their finite changes under this perturbation. Among vertices optimal at \(\mu\), decreasing coordinate \(\theta\) selects the largest \(y_\theta\). The same maximum over the whole optimal face is attained at one of its vertices.

For a chosen maximizing allocation \(x\), compute

\(\displaystyle C_\theta=\sum_a x_a k_\theta(a).\)

Its exact limiting payment is therefore

\(\displaystyle r_\theta=\frac{C_\theta}{\mu_\theta}+b_\theta\max_{(t,y)\in\mathcal D^*(\mu)}y_\theta.\)

In particular, an arbitrary optimal dual multiplier need not give the correct one-sided payment. Each coordinate requires maximization over the optimal dual face; different coordinates may attain their maxima at different dual solutions.

6. Running time, output size, and decision membership

Enumerate the \(N=(\tau+1)^m\) patterns and compute their coefficients. Solve the primal allocation program exactly. Then, for each type, maximize \(y_\theta\) subject to dual feasibility and

\(\lambda t+\sum_\eta\mu_\eta b_\eta y_\eta=F(\mu).\)

These are \(\tau\) additional bounded rational linear programs. Rational linear programming is polynomial-time solvable in the explicit encoding length; see [Khachiyan’s polynomial-time linear-programming result](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319). Applied here, it yields total bit complexity

\(\displaystyle (\tau+1)^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}\!\bigl(m,\tau,L,\log(1+b_{\max})\bigr).\)

The capacity logarithm is already included in \(L\).

A maximizing basic feasible allocation has at most \(\tau+1\) positive pattern masses. Indeed, after adding resource slack variables, there are \(\tau+1\) equality rows, so a basic solution has at most that many positive variables. Its rational masses have polynomial encoding length: the basis matrix has integer entries bounded by \(\max(1,m)\), and its right-hand side has polynomial bit length.

Therefore a polynomial-size certificate consists of at most \(\tau+1\) patterns and their masses. Feasibility and expected welfare can be checked exactly in polynomial time. Together with the reduction, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness of the welfare-threshold decision problem.

The discrete equilibrium-existence statement supplies no computational shortcut for this continuous optimization problem.
