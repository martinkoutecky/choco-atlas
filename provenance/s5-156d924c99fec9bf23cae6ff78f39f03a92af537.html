The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item classes, rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), and a rational mass distribution \(\mu\) over a finite support \(T\) of normalized, monotone, submodular valuation types, determine whether there is a truthful-in-expectation direct mechanism achieving expected welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q)/O(\log m/\log\log m)\). Each individual must receive an integral bundle and a payment lottery; the mechanism must specify how a named individual’s report, including an off-support report, selects their lottery while aggregate allocations respect supply. State the communication or query complexity for representing and evaluating this rule in \(m,\tau,L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) contain \(m\geq3\) item classes; an individual receives a bundle \(S\subseteq M\), with at most one unit of each class. Supplies \(q_j\geq0\) are binary rationals.
A society is an atomless population of total mass one, given by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Types and admissible reports are normalized, monotone, submodular valuations \(v:2^M\to\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\), with normalization meaning \(v(\varnothing)=0\). Valuations are private black boxes supporting arbitrary computable queries; local computation is unrestricted, and all communicated bits count. Let \(L\) be the total encoding length of masses and supplies plus a bound on the encoding length of any valuation entry, including off-support reports.
A direct mechanism must, for every finitely supported reported society, specify a lottery \(K_{\mu,q}(w)\) over integral bundles and payments for each individual report \(w\), including reports outside \(T\). Payments are real charges with finite absolute expectation; individuals have risk-neutral quasilinear utility.
A named individual's deviation leaves the aggregate reported distribution unchanged but selects that individual's lottery through their own report. Require \(\mathbb E_{K_{\mu,q}(v)}[v(S)-P]\geq\mathbb E_{K_{\mu,q}(w)}[v(S)-P]\) for every admissible \(v,w\) and every reported society.
The lotteries must admit a specified measurable joint realization whose allocated mass of class \(j\) is at most \(q_j\) almost surely. Define \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q)\) as the maximum expected welfare over all such feasible allocations, without incentive constraints; truthful welfare is \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\mathbb E_{K_{\mu,q}(v_t)}[v_t(S)]\).

Does there exist a truthful-in-expectation direct mechanism guaranteeing welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q)/(C\log m/\log\log m)\), for a universal constant \(C\), with \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) communication to construct a finite representation of its allocation and payment rule and to evaluate and sample any named individual's lottery, or is this guarantee impossible with polynomial communication?

For finite societies, a truthful-in-expectation \(O(\log m/\log\log m)\)-approximation with polynomial communication is known in the unrestricted-query model. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/1011.3232v2)
A later finite-society mechanism is universally truthful, achieves an \(O((\log\log m)^3)\)-approximation, and uses \(O(n)\) demand queries. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02716)
No result settling this exact atomless reporting and implementation problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted the distribution as an atomless population of total mass one, rather than a prior over a finite population.
  • Allowed nonnegative rational per-capita supplies and at most one unit of each class per individual, matching the set-valued bundle domain.
  • Interpreted normalized as zero value for the empty bundle, without imposing unit value on the full bundle.
  • Retained unrestricted valuation queries and unrestricted local computation; polynomial communication does not require polynomial computation or a value-query implementation.
  • Made valuations rational and defined L using aggregate input encoding length and a bound on individual valuation-entry encoding lengths, rather than charging for an exponential valuation table.
  • Required a rule for every finitely supported reported society and every admissible off-support report; a single individual changes their own lottery but not aggregate masses.
  • Required a measurable joint realization satisfying supplies almost surely, rather than only expected supply feasibility, and defined OPT using the same feasibility convention.
  • Specified risk-neutral quasilinear utility and integrable payment charges; imposed no additional payment-sign restriction.
  • Restricted the asymptotic statement to m at least three so the logarithmic ratio is defined; the excluded fixed item counts are separate constant-size cases.

Result

Yes. Under the stated deviation rule, exact optimal welfare is achievable with polynomial communication. The construction uses item prices from the configuration LP, together with a finite rule for selecting among equally desirable bundles. Submodularity is not needed.

The crucial hypothesis is that a named individual’s deviation leaves the aggregate reported society unchanged. Consequently, the prices and the finite selection rule constructed from that society remain fixed during every incentive comparison.

1. The configuration LP equals the atomless welfare optimum

Fix a reported society with valuations \(v_t\), positive masses \(\mu_t\), and supplies \(q_j\). Replace each supply by \(\bar q_j=\min\{q_j,1\}\); this preserves feasibility because each individual receives at most one unit of each class.

For each type \(t\) and bundle \(S\subseteq M\), let \(x_{t,S}\) denote the population mass assigned bundle \(S\). Consider the finite, although exponentially large, LP:

\( \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad & \sum_t\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}v_t(S),\\ \text{subject to}\quad &\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t &&\text{for every }t,\\ &\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\leq \bar q_j &&\text{for every }j,\\ &x_{t,S}\geq0. \end{aligned} \)

Claim. Its optimal value is \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q)\).

For one direction, take any feasible measurable joint allocation. Define \(x_{t,S}\) as the expected allocated mass of type \(t\) receiving \(S\). Tonelli’s theorem applies to these nonnegative measurable indicators. It gives the type-mass equalities and, by almost-sure feasibility, the supply inequalities. Expected welfare equals the LP objective. Thus \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q)\) is at most the LP optimum.

Conversely, let \(x\) be LP-feasible and put \(r_{t,S}=x_{t,S}/\mu_t\). Use the canonical population consisting of a copy of \([0,1)\) for each type \(t\), with measure \(\mu_t\) times Lebesgue measure. Partition \([0,1)\) into consecutive half-open intervals of lengths \(r_{t,S}\). Let \(F_t\) map each interval to its bundle.

Draw one common random variable \(U\), uniform on \([0,1)\), and assign individual \((t,u)\) the bundle

\( F_t((u+U)\bmod 1). \)

Each fixed individual has bundle probabilities \(r_{t,S}\). For every realization of \(U\), rotation preserves Lebesgue measure, so the mass receiving class \(j\) is exactly

\( \sum_t\mu_t\sum_{S\ni j}r_{t,S} =\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S} \leq \bar q_j. \)

This is a measurable joint realization attaining the LP objective. The claim follows.

The same construction works on any atomless population: within each type set, repeated measurable bisection produces a coordinate uniformly distributed on \([0,1)\), along which the construction can be pulled back.

2. Computing optimal allocations and supporting prices

The dual LP is

\( \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad &\sum_t\mu_t a_t+\sum_j\bar q_jp_j,\\ \text{subject to}\quad &a_t+\sum_{j\in S}p_j\geq v_t(S) &&\text{for every }t,S,\\ &p_j\geq0. \end{aligned} \)

The variables \(a_t\) are formally unrestricted, but the empty-bundle constraints imply \(a_t\geq0\). Write \(p(S)=\sum_{j\in S}p_j\).

We use the standard exact rational ellipsoid theorem with primal recovery: a rational LP whose dual has polynomial dimension and a strong separation oracle can be solved exactly with polynomially many oracle calls and polynomial-length rational queries. Primal recovery retains polynomially many original dual constraints, solves the corresponding restricted primal, and obtains an optimal sparse primal solution. This recovery procedure is described, for example, in Section 2.2 and the proof of Theorem 3.5 of [Kearns and Roughgarden, *Marginals-to-Models Reducibility*](https://www.timroughgarden.algorithmsilluminated.org/papers/marg.pdf).

Here are the required hypothesis checks.

  • Separation. Given rational \((a,p)\), first check nonnegativity. For each type, ask the computable demand query

\( D_t\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{S\subseteq M}\{v_t(S)-p(S)\}. \)
Query its value. A type has a violated bundle constraint precisely when \(v_t(D_t)-p(D_t)>a_t\), in which case \(D_t\) identifies one.

  • Encoding bounds. The dual has \(d=\tau+m\) variables. Every constraint has coefficients from \(\{0,1\}\), apart from a valuation entry of length at most \(L\). The objective coefficients are the supplied masses and supplies.
  • Feasibility and finite optimum. The primal is nonempty—assign everyone the empty bundle—and compact. The dual is feasible by taking \(p=0\) and \(a_t=v_t(M)\). Hence both optimal values are finite and attained.
  • Magnitude bounds. Choose a rational \(B=2^{L+1}\), which bounds every valuation entry. Any dual-feasible solution can have every coordinate clipped to \(B\), preserving feasibility and weakly decreasing its objective. Indeed, if clipping changes a coordinate occurring in a bundle constraint, that constraint’s left side remains at least \(B\); otherwise it remains unchanged.
  • Query communication. A demand query communicates a polynomial-length price vector and receives an \(m\)-bit bundle and one valuation entry. Demand maximization is an allowed computable query and can be performed privately by exhaustive enumeration. Its local computational cost causes no additional communication.

Thus we can construct exact optimal solutions \(x\) and \((a,p)\) with \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) communication.

Choose \(x\) to be a basic optimal solution. There are only \(d\) type and supply constraints, so it has at most \(d\) positive bundle variables. Its rational coordinates, and the dual coordinates, have polynomial encoding length by the usual determinant bounds for rational basic solutions.

Complementary slackness gives

\( x_{t,S}>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_t(S)-p(S)=a_t. \)

For completeness, this implication follows directly by expanding the zero primal-dual gap:

\( 0= \sum_jp_j\left(\bar q_j-\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\right) +\sum_t\sum_Sx_{t,S}\bigl(a_t+p(S)-v_t(S)\bigr). \)

Every summand is nonnegative. Since each type has positive mass, some \(x_{t,S}\) is positive. Dual feasibility therefore also yields

\( a_t=\max_{S\subseteq M}\{v_t(S)-p(S)\}. \)

Thus every bundle used for a type is demanded by that type at prices \(p\).

3. A finite selection rule that preserves clearing

An implementation must define a rule for every report without transmitting complete valuation tables or testing equality of black boxes. We now construct such a rule.

Let

\( \mathcal B=\{S:x_{t,S}>0\text{ for some }t\}. \)

Then \(|\mathcal B|\leq \tau+m\). Query each support type’s values on all bundles in \(\mathcal B\), and define its signature

\( b_t=(v_t(S))_{S\in\mathcal B}. \)

Partition the types into groups \(G\) having equal signatures. Let \(b_G(S)\) denote their common value on \(S\in\mathcal B\), and define

\( \mu_G=\sum_{t\in G}\mu_t,\qquad r_{G,S}=\frac{\sum_{t\in G}x_{t,S}}{\mu_G}. \)

These probabilities sum to one. Put

\( h_G=\max_{S\in\mathcal B}\{b_G(S)-p(S)\}. \)

For every \(t\in G\), we have \(h_G=a_t\): the maximum over \(\mathcal B\) is at most \(a_t\), and some positive variable \(x_{t,S}\) supplies equality.

Moreover,

\( r_{G,S}>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_G(S)-p(S)=h_G. \)

Indeed, some \(t\in G\) has \(x_{t,S}>0\), and complementary slackness applies. Consequently, every bundle used by the group lottery is demanded by every member of that group.

Replace \(x\) by

\( x'_{t,S}=\mu_t r_{G,S}\qquad(t\in G). \)

This replacement preserves total mass assigned to every bundle:

\( \sum_{t\in G}x'_{t,S} =\sum_{t\in G}x_{t,S}. \)

It also preserves welfare, because all group members have the same values on every allocated bundle:

\( \sum_{t\in G}\sum_Sx'_{t,S}v_t(S) =\sum_{S\in\mathcal B}b_G(S)\sum_{t\in G}x_{t,S} =\sum_{t\in G}\sum_Sx_{t,S}v_t(S). \)

Thus the group lotteries remain feasible and optimal.

4. The rule for every report and its incentive proof

The stored representation consists of the prices \(p\), the bundle list \(\mathcal B\), and the group data \((b_G,h_G,r_G)\).

For any admissible report \(w\), compute

\( H(w)=\max_{S\subseteq M}\{w(S)-p(S)\} \)

and a maximizing bundle \(D(w)\), using a fixed deterministic tie-breaking order. Also query \(w(S)\) for every \(S\in\mathcal B\).

Define \(K_{\mu,q}(w)\) as follows:

  • If the signature \((w(S))_{S\in\mathcal B}\) equals some stored \(b_G\), and \(H(w)=h_G\), sample \(S\) according to \(r_G\).
  • Otherwise, assign \(D(w)\).

In either case charge the deterministic bundle price \(P=p(S)\).

Every bundle produced for report \(w\) maximizes \(w(S)-p(S)\). This is immediate in the second branch. In the first branch, every positively probable bundle satisfies

\( w(S)-p(S)=b_G(S)-p(S)=h_G=H(w). \)

Now fix any reported society and any admissible true valuation \(v\) and alternative report \(w\). Truthful reporting gives expected utility exactly \(H(v)\). Under report \(w\), every realized bundle and payment satisfy

\( v(S)-P=v(S)-p(S)\leq H(v). \)

Taking expectations proves the required inequality:

\( \mathbb E_{K_{\mu,q}(v)}[v(S)-P] \geq \mathbb E_{K_{\mu,q}(w)}[v(S)-P]. \)

This argument covers all off-support valuations and reports. All payments are bounded by the finite number \(\sum_jp_j\), so their absolute expectations are finite.

For every support type \(v_t\in G\), the signature and utility tests succeed. Its lottery is therefore exactly \(r_G\).

5. Specified joint realization and optimal welfare

Order the bundles in \(\mathcal B\). For each group \(G\), let \(F_G:[0,1)\to\mathcal B\) be the categorical map whose consecutive half-open intervals have lengths \(r_{G,S}\).

On the canonical population from Section 1, draw one common uniform \(U\). Assign individual \((t,u)\), where \(t\in G\), the bundle

\( S(t,u,U)=F_G((u+U)\bmod1), \)

and charge \(p(S(t,u,U))\).

This is measurable. Every named individual of type \(t\in G\) has exactly lottery \(r_G\). For every realization of \(U\), the allocated mass of class \(j\) is

\( \sum_G\mu_G\sum_{S\ni j}r_{G,S} =\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S} \leq\bar q_j\leq q_j. \)

An off-support report by one named individual can be coupled using the same shifted coordinate and its categorical allocation map. Changing that individual’s allocation changes no allocated mass, so feasibility remains valid.

By the welfare-preservation calculation in Section 3 and the LP characterization in Section 1, truthful welfare is exactly

\( \sum_t\mu_t\,\mathbb E_{K_{\mu,q}(v_t)}[v_t(S)] =\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q). \)

6. Communication and the requested guarantee

Construction requires polynomial communication for LP optimization and recovery, followed by at most \(\tau(\tau+m)\) additional valuation-entry queries. All stored bundles, signatures, probabilities, and prices have polynomial total encoding length.

Evaluating any named individual’s lottery requires one demand query and at most \(\tau+m\) value queries. The result is either a stored rational lottery or a single bundle. Exact sampling from the rational lottery is local; communicating the sampled bundle and its rational payment requires polynomially many bits. The joint realization also has a finite description: the stored rational interval endpoints and the common-rotation formula.

All these bounds are \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), without expanding rational masses into individual copies.

With natural logarithms, for \(m\geq3\) we have \(\log m>1\) and \(0<\log\log m<\log m\). Hence exact optimal welfare implies the requested guarantee even with \(C=1\).
