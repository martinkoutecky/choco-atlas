The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite rational distribution \(\mu\) over supported valuation types \(T\), a rational per-capita capacity \(b\), and a rational bundle menu \(q_r\) satisfying \(\sum_{r\in T}\mu_r q_r\le b\), decide whether there are per-capita payments \(p_r\) such that \(v_t(q_t)-p_t\ge v_t(q_r)-p_r\) for every \(t,r\in T\). This asks whether one payment menu makes truthful reporting optimal and prevents envy among present types.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There is one divisible resource, so the resource dimension is \(m=1\). The input specifies a per-capita capacity \(b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) and a deterministic quantity menu \(q_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tq_t\le b\); unused capacity is permitted.
Valuations are specified by the explicit binary rational table \(V_{tr}=v_t(q_r)\), allowing either sign and requiring equal entries whenever the corresponding quantities coincide. No linearity or monotonicity is assumed.
Each type receives its menu quantity and makes a per-capita payment \(p_t\in\mathbb{R}\); negative payments are transfers to that type. Reporting any \(r\in T\) gives the fixed outcome \((q_r,p_r)\), with the society and menu held fixed.
A payment menu is admissible when \(V_{tt}-p_t\ge V_{tr}-p_r\) for every \(t,r\in T\). These weak inequalities express both truthful reporting and absence of envy among supported types. There are no additional payment, participation, or budget-balance constraints.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is deciding whether an admissible payment menu exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For discrete agents, simultaneous incentive-compatible and envy-free implementability is characterized by absence of negative cycles in the graph combining unilateral-reporting and outcome-exchange constraints. [Cohen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.5328)
An extension to divisible allocations is also explicitly stated; this does not explicitly classify the distribution-support problem above. [Cohen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.5328)

  • Interpreted the scalar capacity and quantities as one divisible resource, with nonnegative rational quantities and capacity.
  • Retained the graded capacity inequality and allowed unused capacity.
  • Specified valuation encoding by the explicit rational table of evaluations at offered quantities, with consistency for repeated quantities; retained unrestricted signs and general valuations.
  • Allowed arbitrary real payments of either sign, following the discrete payment domain, without adding participation or budget-balance conditions.

Classification

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. For the stated case \(m=1\), an exact algorithm uses \(O(\tau^3)\) additions and comparisons after integer scaling, with a conservative total bound of \(O(L^3+\tau^3L^2)\) bit operations.

The decisive point is that the society and quantity menu remain fixed under every report. Consequently, there are exactly \(\tau\) payment variables and \(\tau^2\) admissibility inequalities.

1. Reduction to difference constraints

For each ordered pair \(t,r\in T\), admissibility requires

\(V_{tt}-p_t\ge V_{tr}-p_r.\)

Rearranging gives the equivalent inequality

\(p_t\le p_r+V_{tt}-V_{tr}.\)

Define a directed graph \(G\) whose vertex set is \(T\). For every ordered pair \(r,t\), include the edge \(r\to t\) with rational weight

\(w(r,t)=V_{tt}-V_{tr}.\)

Thus a payment menu is admissible precisely when

\(p_t\le p_r+w(r,t)\qquad\text{for every edge }r\to t.\)

The masses and capacity do not appear in these inequalities: the input already supplies a feasible quantity menu, and there are no additional restrictions on payments.

2. Exact feasibility characterization

Proposition. An admissible payment menu exists if and only if \(G\) has no directed cycle of negative total weight.

Necessity. Suppose an admissible menu exists. Consider a directed cycle \(t_0\to t_1\to\cdots\to t_k=t_0\). Summing its edge inequalities yields

\(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(p_{t_{i+1}}-p_{t_i})\le\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}w(t_i,t_{i+1}).\)

The left side telescopes to zero, so the cycle weight is nonnegative.

Sufficiency. Suppose no directed cycle has negative weight. Add a new source \(s\), with an edge of weight zero from \(s\) to every vertex of \(T\), and no edges entering \(s\).

Every vertex is reachable from \(s\). Any repeated-vertex segment of a walk is a closed walk. Such a closed walk decomposes into directed cycles, all of which have nonnegative weight. Removing that segment therefore does not increase the walk’s weight. Repeating this removal produces a simple path.

There are finitely many simple paths, so each vertex \(t\) has a finite minimum path weight \(d_t\) from \(s\). For every edge \(r\to t\), appending that edge to a minimum path to \(r\) gives a walk to \(t\). Hence

\(d_t\le d_r+w(r,t).\)

Setting \(p_t=d_t\) satisfies every admissibility inequality. This proves the proposition.

In particular, whenever real payments exist, rational payments exist.

3. Algorithm and exact running time

Write each table entry as \(V_{tr}=a_{tr}/h_{tr}\), where \(a_{tr}\) is an integer and \(h_{tr}\) is a positive integer. Define

\(D=\prod_{t,r\in T}h_{tr},\qquad A_{tr}=DV_{tr},\qquad W(r,t)=A_{tt}-A_{tr}.\)

All \(A_{tr}\) and \(W(r,t)\) are integers. Since \(D>0\), scaling preserves the sign of every cycle weight.

The following recurrence implements negative-cycle detection using exact integers. Initialize \(d_0(t)=0\) for every \(t\in T\), and compute, for \(k=1,\ldots,\tau\),

\(d_k(t)=\min\left\{d_{k-1}(t),\ \min_{r\in T}\bigl(d_{k-1}(r)+W(r,t)\bigr)\right\}.\)

By induction on \(k\), \(d_k(t)\) is the minimum weight of a walk ending at \(t\), starting at any vertex, and using at most \(k\) edges. The initialization represents the zero-edge walks.

Acceptance rule: accept exactly when \(d_\tau(t)=d_{\tau-1}(t)\) for every \(t\).

To verify this rule:

  • If there is no negative cycle, deleting repeated-vertex segments shows that every walk can be replaced, without increasing its weight, by a path with at most \(\tau-1\) edges. Therefore \(d_\tau=d_{\tau-1}\).
  • If \(d_\tau=d_{\tau-1}\), the recurrence implies \(d_{\tau-1}(t)\le d_{\tau-1}(r)+W(r,t)\) for every pair \(r,t\). Consequently, \(p_t=d_{\tau-1}(t)/D\) is an admissible menu.

Thus the algorithm decides feasibility and constructs payments on acceptance.

For the bit bound, the sum of the denominator bit lengths is at most \(L\), so \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits. The scaled entries and edge weights also have \(O(L)\) bits. Each intermediate minimum is the weight of a walk with at most \(\tau\) edges, so it has \(O(L+\log\tau)\) bits.

There are \(\tau\) rounds, each examining \(\tau^2\) edges. This gives \(O(\tau^3)\) additions and comparisons. Using elementary integer arithmetic, scaling and these rounds fit within the conservative bound

\(O(L^3+\tau^3L^2)\)

bit operations. Here \(\tau\le L\), because the support and valuation table are explicitly encoded. With \(m=1\), this is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

4. Equal quantities and the cited characterization

Coincident quantities create no extra difficulty. If \(q_t=q_r\), the input consistency condition gives \(V_{tt}=V_{tr}\) and \(V_{rr}=V_{rt}\). The two corresponding inequalities imply \(p_t\le p_r\) and \(p_r\le p_t\), hence \(p_t=p_r\). The graph constraints already enforce this.

[Cohen et al., Theorem 3.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.5328) characterize simultaneous incentive-compatible and envy-free implementability through absence of negative cycles in their combined constraint graph. The proof above establishes the characterization directly for this support formulation. Here reporting and envy compare the same fixed menu outcomes, and the graph has only \(\tau\) vertices and \(\tau^2\) edges. Its explicit polynomial size yields the requested complexity classification.
