The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 1 (Alon-Edelman) — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational society distribution \(\mu\), rational target type-power vector \(b\), and threshold \(\alpha\), decide whether there exist nonnegative type weights \(w_t\) and per-capita quota \(q\) such that, for every replication level \(r\) with \(r\mu_t\in\mathbb{Z}\), the weighted rule on \(r\mu_t\) indistinguishable voters of type \(t\) has normalized type-total Banzhaf power \(P_{r,t}\) satisfying \(\limsup_{r\to\infty}\sum_t\lvert P_{r,t}-b_t\rvert\le\alpha\); equivalently, minimize this limsup.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Types are labels imposing identical weights on their members.
The remaining input is \(b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{T}\) with \(\sum_tb_t=1\), and \(\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; there are \(m=2\) collective outcomes.
Choose real weights \(w_t\ge0\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_tw_t=1\), and a real per-capita quota \(0<q\le1\), all fixed independently of replication.
For each \(r\in R_\mu:=\{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}:r\mu_t\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for every }t\}\), the voter set \(N_r\) contains \(r\mu_t\) distinct voters of type \(t\). A coalition \(S\subseteq N_r\) wins exactly when \(\sum_{i\in S}w_{t(i)}\ge rq\); equality is winning.
Let \(B_i(v_r)\) be voter \(i\)'s normalized Penrose–Banzhaf index and \(P_{r,t}:=\sum_{i\in N_r:t(i)=t}B_i(v_r)\). Coalitions range over individual voters, including voters sharing a type.
The decision problem asks whether such fixed \(w,q\) exist with \(\limsup_{r\to\infty,\ r\in R_\mu}\sum_{t\in T}|P_{r,t}-b_t|\le\alpha\).

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Discrete: the [Alon–Edelman stability bound](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/banz3.pdf) states that, for \(1\le k<n\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1/(k+1)\), any simple game whose voters outside a designated set of \(k\) have total normalized Banzhaf power at most \(\varepsilon\) is within \(\ell_1\)-distance \((2k+1)\varepsilon/(1-(k+1)\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\) of a simple game making those outside voters null.
Asymptotic: [Lindner and Machover](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/424/1/pltmss00.pdf) prove conditional convergence of Banzhaf power ratios to weight ratios at a quota of half the total weight. No result deciding exactly the fixed-type replication problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted the supplied type set to positive-mass support and normalized the society masses to sum to one.
  • Required the target type-total power vector to be nonnegative and sum to one, and the rational threshold to be nonnegative.
  • Allowed real nonnegative weights and a real quota, as in weighted voting games; normalized mean weight to one by scale invariance.
  • Interpreted per-capita quota as absolute quota rq at replication r, with 0<q<=1 so the empty coalition loses and the grand coalition wins.
  • Used the formal weighted-game convention that meeting the quota is winning.
  • Made weights and per-capita quota fixed across all replications, and restricted the limsup to positive integer replication levels with integral type counts.
  • Interpreted indistinguishable voters as distinct individual voters with identical type weights, and defined type-total power by summing their normalized Banzhaf indices.
  • Set m=2 for the binary collective outcome domain and L to the total binary input length.

Result

The problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). In fact, every valid input is a YES-instance, even when \(\alpha=0\): the fixed choice
\[ w_t=\frac{b_t}{\mu_t},\qquad q=\frac12 \]
satisfies \(P_{r,t}\to b_t\) for every type.

Below is a self-contained proof of the required convergence. The relevant lattice case is also covered by Theorem 3.6 of [Lindner and Machover](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/424/1/pltmss00.pdf): their hypotheses concern finitely many positive integer weights, with greatest common divisor one among the weights occurring infinitely often. Rational scaling makes those hypotheses applicable here after deleting zero-weight voters.

1. Feasibility and integer scaling

Because \(\mu_t>0\), the proposed weights are well-defined, nonnegative rationals. They satisfy
\[ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_tw_t=\sum_{t\in T}b_t=1. \]
Thus they satisfy the required normalization and are independent of replication.

Let \(D\) be the least common multiple of the denominators of the reduced fractions \(\mu_t\), and put \(d_t=D\mu_t\). Then every \(d_t\) is a positive integer, and
\[ R_\mu=\{Dn:n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\}. \]
Indeed, integrality of \(r\mu_t\) is equivalent to divisibility of \(r\) by the reduced denominator of \(\mu_t\).

Clear the denominators of the positive weights and divide the resulting integers by their greatest common divisor. This gives integers \(a_t\ge0\) and a positive rational \(c\) such that
\[ w_t=\frac{a_t}{c}, \qquad \gcd\{a_t:a_t>0\}=1. \]
Normalization implies
\[ \sum_t\mu_ta_t=c. \]
Define the positive integer
\[ A=\sum_td_ta_t=Dc. \]

At replication \(r=Dn\), multiplying all weights and the quota by \(c\) leaves the game unchanged. In this integer representation, total weight is \(nA\), and the winning threshold is \(nA/2\).

2. A lattice local limit lemma

We use the following elementary statement.

Lemma. Let \(X\) be a finitely supported, nonnegative integer-valued random variable. Suppose \(0\) belongs to its support, the greatest common divisor of its positive support elements is one, and its variance \(\sigma^2\) is positive. Write \(\nu=\mathbb EX\). For independent copies \(X_1,\ldots,X_N\), and every fixed finite \(B\),
\[ \Pr\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^N X_j=k\right) = \frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi N\sigma^2}} \]
uniformly over integers \(k\) satisfying \(|k-N\nu|\le B\).

Proof. Let \(\phi(\theta)=\mathbb E e^{i\theta X}\). Fourier inversion gives
\[ \Pr\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^N X_j=k\right) = \frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi(\theta)^N e^{-ik\theta}\,d\theta. \]

If \(|\phi(\theta)|=1\), equality in the triangle inequality implies that all \(e^{i\theta x}\), for support elements \(x\), coincide. Since \(0\) is in the support, they must all equal one. The greatest-common-divisor hypothesis then implies \(\theta\in2\pi\mathbb Z\). Consequently, for each sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\), there is \(\rho<1\) such that
\[ |\phi(\theta)|\le\rho \quad\text{whenever}\quad \delta\le|\theta|\le\pi. \]
The contribution of this region, multiplied by \(\sqrt N\), therefore tends to zero.

Put \(f(\theta)=e^{-i\nu\theta}\phi(\theta)\). Finite support gives the Taylor expansion
\[ f(\theta)=1-\frac{\sigma^2\theta^2}{2}+O(|\theta|^3). \]
After decreasing \(\delta\), there is \(\gamma>0\) such that
\[ |f(\theta)|\le e^{-\gamma\theta^2} \quad\text{for}\quad |\theta|\le\delta. \]

Writing \(h=k-N\nu\) and substituting \(\theta=u/\sqrt N\), the remaining contribution to \(\sqrt N\) times the probability is
\[ \frac1{2\pi} \int_{|u|\le\delta\sqrt N} f(u/\sqrt N)^N e^{-ihu/\sqrt N}\,du. \]
For each fixed \(u\), the integrand tends to \(e^{-\sigma^2u^2/2}\). Its absolute value is bounded by the integrable function \(e^{-\gamma u^2}\). Moreover, convergence of the phase factor is uniform for \(|h|\le B\), since
\[ |e^{-ihu/\sqrt N}-1|\le \frac{B|u|}{\sqrt N}. \]
Dominated convergence, uniformly in these \(h\), yields the limit
\[ \frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-\sigma^2u^2/2}\,du = \frac1{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}. \]
This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

3. Individual pivotal probabilities

Partition the \(Dn\) voters into \(n\) blocks, each containing \(d_t\) voters of type \(t\). A uniformly random coalition selects each individual voter independently with probability \(1/2\).

Let \(X\) denote the selected integer weight in one complete block. Then
\[ \mathbb EX=\frac A2, \qquad V:=\operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac14\sum_td_ta_t^2>0. \]
Its support contains \(0\) and every positive \(a_t\): the latter occurs by selecting just one voter of type \(t\). Every support element is an integer combination of the \(a_t\). Hence the greatest common divisor of its positive support elements is exactly one. The lemma applies.

Fix a type \(t\) with \(a_t>0\), and designate one voter of that type in the first block. Let \(Y_t\) be the selected weight of the other voters in that block. The selected weight of all voters other than the designated voter has distribution
\[ S_{n,t}=X_1+\cdots+X_{n-1}+Y_t, \]
where all displayed variables are independent and the \(X_j\) have distribution \(X\).

Let \(\psi_{n,t}\) be this voter's absolute Banzhaf power, namely its probability of being pivotal. Because equality at the quota wins,
\[ \psi_{n,t} = \Pr\!\left(\frac{nA}{2}-a_t\le S_{n,t}<\frac{nA}{2}\right). \]
This half-open interval contains exactly \(a_t\) integers. Explicitly, they are
\[ k=\left\lceil\frac{nA}{2}\right\rceil-a_t+j, \qquad j\in\{0,\ldots,a_t-1\}. \]
Thus exact quota ties cause no discrepancy in the number of relevant lattice points.

For every such \(k\) and every support value \(y\) of \(Y_t\),
\[ k-y-\frac{(n-1)A}{2} = \left(k-\frac{nA}{2}\right)+\frac A2-y. \]
The right-hand side is bounded independently of \(n\), because \(a_t\), \(A\), and the finite support of \(Y_t\) are fixed. Applying the lemma with \(N=n-1\) therefore gives
\[ \Pr(X_1+\cdots+X_{n-1}=k-y) = \frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi nV}}, \]
uniformly over the relevant \(k\) and \(y\).

Conditioning on \(Y_t\), and then summing over the \(a_t\) integers in the pivotal interval, yields
\[ \psi_{n,t} = \frac{a_t+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi nV}}. \]
If \(a_t=0\), a voter of type \(t\) never changes coalition weight, so \(\psi_{n,t}=0\) exactly.

4. Aggregate normalized power

The normalized Banzhaf index is well-defined at every replication. The empty coalition loses and the grand coalition wins; adding voters successively must therefore produce at least one pivotal transition.

All \(Dn\mu_t\) voters of type \(t\) have the same absolute power. Consequently,
\[ P_{Dn,t} = \frac{Dn\mu_t\psi_{n,t}} {\sum_sDn\mu_s\psi_{n,s}} = \frac{\mu_t\psi_{n,t}}{\sum_s\mu_s\psi_{n,s}}. \]
The preceding asymptotics imply
\[ \sqrt{2\pi nV}\sum_s\mu_s\psi_{n,s} \longrightarrow \sum_s\mu_sa_s=c>0. \]
Therefore, for every positive-weight type,
\[ P_{Dn,t}\longrightarrow \frac{\mu_ta_t}{c} = \mu_tw_t = b_t. \]
For a zero-weight type, \(P_{Dn,t}=b_t=0\) at every replication.

Since \(T\) is finite and every admissible replication is \(Dn\), this proves
\[ \lim_{\substack{r\to\infty\\r\in R_\mu}} \sum_{t\in T}|P_{r,t}-b_t|=0. \]
In particular, the required limsup is at most every permitted \(\alpha\).

5. Computational conclusion

On valid inputs, the decision algorithm always returns YES. If a witness is requested, it computes \(w_t=b_t/\mu_t\) and \(q=1/2\).

Each quotient has binary encoding length polynomial in \(L\), and the \(\tau\) rational divisions take polynomial time. Neither the block construction nor any replicated voter set needs to be generated: those objects are used only in the convergence proof.

Thus the stated decision problem is polynomial-time solvable, and every rational target distribution is realized exactly in the limit.
