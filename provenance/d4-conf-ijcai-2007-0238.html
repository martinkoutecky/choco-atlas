The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\), and NTU rules whose literals test whether coalition mass \(z_t\) is positive or zero and whose triggered rules add chosen payoff-increment vectors, decide whether \(V(\mu)\) contains an \(x\) such that for every nonzero \(z\leq\mu\) and every \(y\in V(z)\), some \(t\) with \(z_t>0\) satisfies \(y_t\leq x_t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each encoded as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; masses need not sum to \(1\).
The input contains \(m\) NTU rules. Each rule \(r\) has a conjunction of literals testing \(z_t>0\) or \(z_t=0\), and an explicitly listed, finite, nonempty set \(A_r\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{T}\) of payoff-increment vectors with binary rational coordinates of either sign. Every vector vanishes outside the types tested positively by that rule.
A coalition is any real vector \(z\) with \(0\leq z\leq\mu\) and \(z\neq0\). Let \(R(z)\) be the rules whose conjunctions hold, and define \(V(z)=\{\sum_{r\in R(z)}a_r:a_r\in A_r\}\), with \(V(z)=\{0\}\) when no rule applies.
Each coordinate is the common payoff per member of that type; increments are not multiplied or divided by coalition mass. A coalition chooses exactly one vector from every triggered rule, without convexification. Members prefer larger own-coordinate payoffs, with blocking requiring strict improvement for every participating type.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

Is deciding whether there exists \(x\in V(\mu)\) such that, for every nonzero real \(z\leq\mu\) and every \(y\in V(z)\), some \(t\) with \(z_t>0\) satisfies \(y_t\leq x_t\), in \(\mathrm{P}\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Result

The problem is \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions, and therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. This holds even when every mass equals \(1\).

The key fact is that coalition masses affect neither rule activation nor payoff comparison. The continuous model is therefore exactly equivalent to a finite-player model with one player per supported type.

1. Coalitions reduce exactly to supports

For a coalition \(z\), define its support by \(\operatorname{supp}(z)=\{t\in T:z_t>0\}\).

For each subset \(S\subseteq T\), let \(R_S\) contain precisely those rules whose positive literals belong to \(S\) and whose negative literals belong to \(T\setminus S\). Define

\(W(S)=\left\{\sum_{r\in R_S}a_r:a_r\in A_r\right\}\),

with \(W(S)=\{0\}\) when \(R_S\) is empty.

Lemma. For every admissible coalition \(z\), \(V(z)=W(\operatorname{supp}(z))\). Moreover, every nonempty subset of \(T\) occurs as an admissible coalition support.

Proof. A positive literal concerning \(t\) holds exactly when \(t\in\operatorname{supp}(z)\); a zero literal holds exactly when \(t\notin\operatorname{supp}(z)\). Thus \(R(z)=R_{\operatorname{supp}(z)}\). The definitions of the feasible payoff sets then give the claimed equality.

For any nonempty \(S\subseteq T\), define \(z^S_t=\mu_t\) for \(t\in S\), and \(z^S_t=0\) otherwise. Positivity of every \(\mu_t\) implies that \(z^S\neq0\), that \(0\leq z^S\leq\mu\), and that \(\operatorname{supp}(z^S)=S\). ∎

Since \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu)=T\), grand-coalition feasibility is exactly \(x\in W(T)\).

Consequently, the required condition on \(x\) is equivalent to

\(\displaystyle \forall\,\varnothing\neq S\subseteq T\;\forall\,y\in W(S)\;\exists\,t\in S:\ y_t\leq x_t.\)

Both directions follow from the lemma: every real coalition determines such a subset, and every nonempty subset has the representative \(z^S\).

In particular, replacing all positive masses by \(1\) preserves the answer.

2. Polynomial-time transfer of the finite-player hardness result

Use the finite-player theorem supplied in the question: core nonemptiness for NTU marginal-contribution nets is \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\)-complete. The cited paper’s Definition 6.1 uses conjunctions of positive and negative player literals, finite alternatives of signed payoff increments supported on positively tested players, and sums formed by choosing one alternative from every applicable rule. Its Theorem 6.3 gives the completeness result. [Malizia, Palopoli and Scarcello, Definitions 6.1 and Theorem 6.3](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/07/Papers/226.pdf)

Here is the reduction, including the representation checks.

Take a finite-player instance with player set \(N\). Construct a continuous instance as follows:

  • Set \(T=N\), with one distinct supported type for each player.
  • Set \(\mu_t=1\) for every \(t\in T\).
  • Replace each positive player literal by \(z_t>0\), and each negative player literal by \(z_t=0\).
  • Retain every rule’s payoff alternatives, inserting zero coordinates wherever its original representation omitted them.

This construction preserves the number \(m\) of non-default rules and gives \(\tau=|N|\). Every alternative remains supported on the types tested positively by its rule. Singleton alternatives consisting of the zero vector may be omitted or retained, since they do not change any sum.

For every finite coalition \(S\subseteq N\), the rules it triggers are exactly \(R_S\). Its feasible payoff vectors, embedded in the full coordinate space with zeros outside \(S\), are therefore exactly \(W(S)\). Its strict blocking condition is precisely

\(\displaystyle y_t>x_t\quad\text{for every }t\in S.\)

By Section 1, these are also exactly the possible blocking events in the continuous instance. Thus the finite instance has a nonempty core if and only if the constructed continuous instance has a feasible unblocked grand-coalition payoff.

The reduction has polynomial size and running time. More explicitly, if the finite encoding has length \(L_{\mathrm{fin}}\), padding explicitly listed alternatives to \(\tau\) coordinates and copying the rules takes at most \(O\!\left(\tau(L_{\mathrm{fin}}+m+1)\right)\) bit operations under a straightforward encoding. Writing the unit masses adds \(O(\tau)\) bits.

All hypotheses of the finite-player hardness theorem are therefore preserved. The continuous problem is \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\)-hard.

3. Membership in \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\)

A grand-coalition payoff has a polynomial-size description: choose one explicitly listed alternative from every rule triggered by \(T\), and sum the chosen vectors.

For a fixed such payoff \(x\), a blocking certificate consists of:

  • a nonempty support \(S\subseteq T\), encoded by \(\tau\) bits;
  • one alternative index for every rule in \(R_S\).

The verifier evaluates the rule conjunctions, computes \(y\), and checks \(y_t>x_t\) for every \(t\in S\). Section 1 proves that such certificates capture all real blocking coalitions.

To check the arithmetic bound, each coordinate sum contains at most \(m\) input rationals. An unreduced common denominator can be taken as their denominator product. Its binary length is at most the sum of their input lengths, hence \(O(L)\). The corresponding numerator also has length \(O(L+\log(m+1))=O(L)\). Thus exact summation and comparison use polynomially many bit operations.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, constructing and checking one payoff pair takes the conservative bound

\(O\!\left(L+(m+1)\tau L^2\right).\)

The decision condition consequently has a polynomial-time existential–universal formulation: existentially choose valid grand-coalition alternatives, then universally consider support-and-alternative descriptions and require that each valid nonempty description fails to block. Invalid universal descriptions are accepted automatically.

By the quantified-predicate characterization of \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\), the problem belongs to that class. Together with Section 2, this proves completeness.

4. An explicit deterministic running-time bound

For completeness, put \(k_r=|A_r|\) and \(K=\prod_{r=1}^{m}k_r\), taking \(K=1\) when \(m=0\).

There are at most \(K\) grand-coalition choice tuples. For each of the \(2^\tau-1\) nonempty supports, there are at most \(K\) coalition choice tuples, because every \(k_r\) is positive. Exhaustively comparing these tuples therefore decides the problem in

\(O\!\left(2^\tau K^2\bigl[L+(m+1)\tau L^2\bigr]\right)\)

bit operations. Since each \(k_r\leq L\) under the explicit-list encoding, a bound solely in the requested parameters is

\(O\!\left(2^\tau L^{2m}\bigl[L+(m+1)\tau L^2\bigr]\right).\)

This is an exhaustive upper bound, not a polynomial-time algorithm. The proved classification is \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\)-completeness; in particular, polynomial-time solvability would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
