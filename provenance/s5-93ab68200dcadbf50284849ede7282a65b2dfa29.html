The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given platforms \(P\), a finite support \(T\) of complete applicant types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), and rational per-capita group caps \(\beta_{p,h}\), maximize expected assigned mass over lotteries on measurable assignments. Each person may receive at most one eligible platform; in every outcome, mass from group \(h\) assigned to platform \(p\) is at most \(\beta_{p,h}\); and each person of type \(t\) is assigned to one of their top \(k\) platforms with probability in \([L_{t,k},U_{t,k}]\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P\) contain \(m\geq1\) platforms. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type specifies eligible platforms \(E_t\subseteq P\), a strict ranking of \(P\), nonempty group membership \(H_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\chi\}\), and rational bounds \(0\leq L_{t,k}\leq U_{t,k}\leq1\) for \(1\leq k\leq m\). Every group has positive mass; groups may overlap. Write \(R_{t,k}\) for the top \(k\) platforms and \(\Delta=\max_t|H_t|\).
Realize type \(t\) as an atomless interval \([0,1)\) with measure \(\mu_t\,ds\). An outcome is a measurable assignment \(a(t,s)\in E_t\cup\{\bot\}\), where \(\bot\) means unassigned; thus each person receives at most one platform.
Input caps \(\beta_{p,h}\geq0\) are binary rationals. Every outcome must satisfy \(\sum_{t:h\in H_t}\mu_t\int_0^1\mathbf1\{a(t,s)=p\}\,ds\leq\beta_{p,h}\) for every platform and group.
A lottery is jointly measurable in its randomness and person coordinates. Define \(q_{t,k}(s)=\Pr[a(t,s)\in R_{t,k}]\); exact individual fairness requires \(L_{t,k}\leq q_{t,k}(s)\leq U_{t,k}\) for every person and \(k\).
Let \(V\) be expected assigned mass and \(\mathrm{OPT}\) its supremum over exactly fair lotteries satisfying every outcome cap. Consider instances admitting such a lottery and a binary-rational \(0<\epsilon<1\).

Does a polynomial-time bicriteria approximation exist, with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,\chi,L\), that preserves every outcome cap and, for some \(1\leq f_\epsilon=O(\Delta(1+\log(1/\epsilon)))\), attains \(V\geq\max\{0,\mathrm{OPT}-\epsilon\}/f_\epsilon\) and \((L_{t,k}-\epsilon)/f_\epsilon\leq q_{t,k}(s)\leq(U_{t,k}+\epsilon)/f_\epsilon\) for every person and \(k\); or is achieving these guarantees NP-hard?

For discrete edge allocations, allowing multiple platforms per item, a polynomial-time bicriteria approximation is reported with factor \(O(\Delta\log(n/\epsilon))\), exact group caps, and the corresponding scaled individual bounds. [Panda, Louis and Nimbhorkar](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.09951v4).
No result settling this precise atomless approximation problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified an atomless interval for each supported type and normalized total society mass to one, so caps measure fractions of the entire society.
  • Completed applicant types with eligibility, strict rankings over all platforms, group memberships, and individual probability bounds for every prefix. Groups cover the society and have positive mass; eligibility sets may be empty.
  • Used nonnegative rational caps and ordered probability bounds in [0,1]; there are no additional platform capacities or lower group bounds.
  • Retained the graded question's at-most-one-platform restriction, although the discrete source explicitly permits multiple platforms per item.
  • Required individual probability bounds for every person, rather than only for a randomly selected member of a type, and required joint measurability of the lottery.
  • Used exactly feasible instances to define the approximation benchmark, avoiding an undefined optimum when individual constraints are inconsistent.
  • Interpreted epsilon as a normalized mass and probability tolerance. Replaced the discrete total-population quantity n by total mass one, giving the factor O(Delta(1+log(1/epsilon))); support size remains an input-size parameter.
  • Corrected the stated objective term from OPT plus epsilon to OPT minus epsilon, truncated at zero: the proof subtracts residual mass, and a positive additive guarantee is impossible on a zero-assignment instance. Neither the latest arXiv version nor the published version fixes this sign.

Result

The atomless problem is polynomial-time solvable exactly. In particular, the requested guarantees hold with \(f_\epsilon=1\).

The algorithm outputs rational interval endpoints and a jointly measurable assignment rule driven by one uniform random variable. This gives a polynomial-size description of the lottery.

1. A finite linear program

For each supported type \(t\) and platform \(p\), introduce a variable \(x_{t,p}\), representing the probability that a person of type \(t\) receives \(p\).

Consider the linear program with objective

\( \displaystyle \max \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\sum_{p\in P}x_{t,p}, \)

subject to:

  • \(x_{t,p}\geq0\) for every \(t,p\), and \(x_{t,p}=0\) whenever \(p\notin E_t\).
  • \(\sum_{p\in P}x_{t,p}\leq1\) for every \(t\).
  • \(\sum_{t:h\in H_t}\mu_t x_{t,p}\leq\beta_{p,h}\) for every \(p,h\).
  • \(L_{t,k}\leq\sum_{p\in R_{t,k}}x_{t,p}\leq U_{t,k}\) for every \(t,k\).

Write \(W\) for its optimum. We will prove that \(W=\mathrm{OPT}\) and construct a lottery attaining it.

2. Every exactly fair lottery gives a feasible LP solution

Let \(a\) be any admissible exactly fair lottery, with randomness on a probability space \(\Omega\). Define

\( \displaystyle x_{t,p}=\int_0^1\Pr_{\omega\in\Omega}[a(\omega,t,s)=p]\,ds. \)

Joint measurability allows Tonelli’s theorem to be applied to these nonnegative indicator functions.

Eligibility implies \(x_{t,p}=0\) for \(p\notin E_t\). Since each person receives at most one platform,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{p\in P}x_{t,p}=\int_0^1\Pr[a(\omega,t,s)\neq\bot]\,ds\leq1. \)

For every platform and group, Tonelli’s theorem and the cap in every outcome give

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t:h\in H_t}\mu_t x_{t,p} = \mathbb E_\omega\!\left[\sum_{t:h\in H_t}\mu_t\int_0^1 \mathbf1\{a(\omega,t,s)=p\}\,ds\right] \leq\beta_{p,h}. \)

For any \(t,k\), the events of receiving distinct platforms are disjoint. Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{p\in R_{t,k}}x_{t,p} =\int_0^1q_{t,k}(s)\,ds. \)

Integrating the pointwise fairness bounds proves the corresponding LP inequalities.

Finally, the lottery’s expected assigned mass equals

\( \displaystyle V=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\sum_{p\in P}x_{t,p}. \)

Thus every admissible lottery produces a feasible LP solution with the same objective value. In particular, the promised lottery makes the LP feasible, and

\( \mathrm{OPT}\leq W. \)

3. Every feasible LP solution has an exact implementation

Fix any feasible LP solution \(x\). For type \(t\), enumerate the platforms in its ranking order as \(p_{t,1},\ldots,p_{t,m}\), and define cumulative endpoints

\( \displaystyle b_{t,0}=0,\qquad b_{t,j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}x_{t,p_{t,i}}\quad(1\leq j\leq m). \)

The row constraint gives \(b_{t,m}\leq1\). Partition \([0,1)\) into intervals

\( I_{t,p_{t,j}}=[b_{t,j-1},b_{t,j}),\qquad I_{t,\bot}=[b_{t,m},1). \)

Intervals of zero length are empty. In particular, every ineligible platform has an empty interval.

Let \(F_t:[0,1)\to E_t\cup\{\bot\}\) assign the label of the interval containing its argument. Draw a single random variable \(\omega\) uniformly from \([0,1)\), and set

\( \displaystyle a(\omega,t,s)=F_t(\{s+\omega\}), \)

where \(\{u\}=u-\lfloor u\rfloor\) is the fractional part.

The fractional-part map is Borel measurable, as are the interval-label maps. Since there are finitely many types, this defines a jointly measurable lottery.

Every outcome satisfies every cap

For each fixed \(\omega\), the map \(s\mapsto\{s+\omega\}\) preserves Lebesgue measure on \([0,1)\). Indeed, it is translation on the two intervals separated by \(1-\omega\), with wraparound on the second interval. Therefore, for every \(t,p,\omega\),

\( \displaystyle \int_0^1\mathbf1\{a(\omega,t,s)=p\}\,ds =|I_{t,p}|=x_{t,p}. \)

Consequently, the mass assigned to \(p\) from group \(h\), in every outcome, is exactly

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t:h\in H_t}\mu_t x_{t,p}\leq\beta_{p,h}. \)

Overlapping groups introduce no additional difficulty: this equality holds separately for each group.

Every person has the required marginals

For each fixed \(s\in[0,1)\), the map \(\omega\mapsto\{s+\omega\}\) likewise preserves Lebesgue measure. Hence, for every person coordinate \(s\),

\( \displaystyle \Pr[a(\omega,t,s)=p]=|I_{t,p}|=x_{t,p}. \)

It follows that

\( \displaystyle q_{t,k}(s)=\sum_{p\in R_{t,k}}x_{t,p}\in[L_{t,k},U_{t,k}]. \)

This statement holds for every \(s\), including interval endpoints; there is no exceptional null set of persons.

The lottery’s assigned mass in every outcome, and therefore its expected assigned mass, is

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\sum_{p\in P}x_{t,p}. \)

Applying this construction to an optimal LP solution proves \(W\leq\mathrm{OPT}\). Together with Section 2,

\( \boxed{W=\mathrm{OPT}}. \)

In particular, the supremum defining \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is attained.

4. Polynomial-time construction and output size

The LP has \(\tau m\) variables and \(O(\tau m+m\chi+\tau)\) constraints. Its coefficients are input rationals, zero, or one, so its binary encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,\chi,L\).

We use the standard polynomial-time solvability theorem for rational linear programming: a rational LP can be optimized exactly, or classified as infeasible or unbounded, in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. See [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=zvmmf&option_lang=eng&paperid=5239&wshow=paper).

Here the feasible set is nonempty by Section 2, and every variable lies in \([0,1]\). It is therefore compact, so an optimum exists. Rational LP theory supplies an optimal rational solution of polynomial encoding length. Computing its cumulative endpoints preserves polynomial encoding length.

The output consists of these endpoints and the rotation rule from Section 3, and therefore has polynomial size.

A compact finite lottery is also available if desired. Choose a positive integer \(D\) divisible by every denominator of the computed \(x_{t,p}\), and replace the continuous seed by \(\omega=J/D\), where \(J\) is uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,D-1\}\). The product of the denominators is a valid choice, and its binary length is polynomial.

To verify pointwise marginals, fix \(s\) and write \(Ds=r+\theta\), with \(r=\lfloor Ds\rfloor\) and \(0\leq\theta<1\). Then

\( \displaystyle D\{s+J/D\}=((r+J)\bmod D)+\theta. \)

Every interval endpoint multiplied by \(D\) is an integer. As \(J\) varies, the integer part on the right visits each element of \(\{0,\ldots,D-1\}\) once. An interval of length \(x_{t,p}\) therefore contains exactly \(Dx_{t,p}\) of these points, including when \(\theta=0\). Its probability is exactly \(x_{t,p}\).

Every such outcome already satisfies the cap calculation in Section 3. Thus this finite lottery can be described succinctly without enumerating its possibly exponentially many outcomes. Exact sampling of \(J\) by binary rejection sampling takes expected polynomial time.

5. The requested guarantees

Set \(f_\epsilon=1\). The constructed lottery satisfies

\( \displaystyle V=\mathrm{OPT}\geq\max\{0,\mathrm{OPT}-\epsilon\}, \)

and, for every person and prefix,

\( \displaystyle L_{t,k}-\epsilon\leq L_{t,k}\leq q_{t,k}(s)\leq U_{t,k}\leq U_{t,k}+\epsilon. \)

Because every supported type has nonempty group membership, \(\Delta\geq1\). Hence

\( \displaystyle 1=O\!\left(\Delta(1+\log(1/\epsilon))\right). \)

All requested guarantees therefore hold, with exact caps, exact individual fairness, and optimal expected assigned mass.
