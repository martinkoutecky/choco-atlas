The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite outcome set \(K\), a finite support \(S \subseteq L(K)\) of true-ranking types with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, and a formula \(\varphi\), decide whether there exist a feasible state \(X=(x_{t,r})\), where \(x_{t,r}\ge 0\) and \(\sum_r x_{t,r}=\mu_t\), and an anonymous deterministic rule \(F:\Delta(L(K))\to K\) such that \(\varphi\) holds. Take formulas to be generated from outcome atoms, rational linear inequalities in \(X\), Boolean connectives, a modality for reaching a state by changing at most specified mass \(q_t\) of each type, and a type-preference modality for reaching a state with a more preferred outcome.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(K\) be a nonempty finite outcome set, \(m=|K|\), and \(L(K)\) the linear rankings without ties between distinct outcomes. A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(S\subseteq L(K)\) of \(\tau\) true-ranking types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
The state space is \(\mathcal X=\{X\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{S\times L(K)}:\sum_r x_{t,r}=\mu_t\text{ for every }t\}\). Put \(z(X)_r=\sum_t x_{t,r}\). A rule is an arbitrary total deterministic function \(F:\Delta(L(K))\to K\), with no regularity requirement; the outcome at \(X\) is \(o_F(X)=F(z(X))\).
Formulas are finite expressions built from outcome atoms \(a\in K\), rational linear inequalities in the coordinates of \(X\), Boolean connectives, and modalities \(\Diamond_q\) and \(\Diamond_t^{\mathrm{pref}}\). Outcome atom \(a\) holds exactly when \(o_F(X)=a\); inequalities and Boolean connectives have their usual meanings.
Each budget vector has binary rational entries \(0\leq q_t\leq\mu_t\). Define \(F,X\models\Diamond_q\psi\) iff some \(Y\in\mathcal X\) satisfies \(\frac12\sum_r|x_{t,r}-y_{t,r}|\leq q_t\) for every \(t\) and \(F,Y\models\psi\).
Define \(F,X\models\Diamond_t^{\mathrm{pref}}\psi\) iff some \(Y\in\mathcal X\) satisfies \(o_F(Y)\succeq_t o_F(X)\) and \(F,Y\models\psi\), where \(\succeq_t\) is weak preference induced by ranking \(t\), including equality of outcomes. This modality imposes no mass-change restriction; all modalities keep the society and rule fixed.
The input is \(K,S,(\mu_t),\varphi\); rankings are explicit permutations and linear atoms have signed binary rational coefficients listed sparsely by coordinate, with either \(<\) or \(\leq\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and formula length \(|\varphi|\).

Is the problem of deciding whether there exist \(F\) and \(X\in\mathcal X\) with \(F,X\models\varphi\) decidable or undecidable?

For finite discrete societies and outcome sets, social-choice modal satisfiability is decidable ([Troquard, van der Hoek and Wooldridge](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.3341)). A different logic allowing countable outcome sets and modalities over claimed preferences has an undecidability result ([Parmann and Ågotnes](https://academic.oup.com/logcom/article/31/4/1055/6105782)). No result settling the mass-state problem defined here was located.

Result

The problem is undecidable. This holds even when preference modalities are absent, all masses are equal dyadic rationals, every budget entry is either zero or the corresponding type’s entire mass, and linear atoms use only non-strict inequalities with at most two nonzero coefficients.

We prove this by an effective reduction from first-order satisfiability.

1. The source problem

We use the classical theorem that satisfiability of first-order sentences with a single binary relation symbol, no function symbols, and no constants is undecidable. This remains true without equality. A modern proof is given by [Hostert, Dudenhefner, and Kirst, *Undecidability of Dyadic First-Order Logic in Coq*](https://www.ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/HostertEtAl_2022_Undecidability.pdf).

Let \(\theta\) be such a sentence, with binary relation symbol \(R\). Write its variables as \(v_1,\ldots,v_s\), taking \(s\geq 1\). We use the connective basis consisting of negation, conjunction, and existential quantification; the other usual connectives have size-linear expansions into this basis.

We will construct a society and a formula \(\varphi_\theta\) such that

\(\theta\text{ has a nonempty first-order model}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists F\,\exists X\in\mathcal X:\ F,X\models\varphi_\theta.\)

2. A cube of states with injective aggregation

Choose a power of two \(n\) satisfying

\(s+3\leq n<2(s+3).\)

Set \(m=n+1\), and choose an outcome set \(K\) of that size. Choose distinct rankings \(r_0,r_1,\ldots,r_n\). These can be produced explicitly: after indexing the outcomes from zero through \(n\), let \(r_h\) put outcome \(h\) first and list the remaining outcomes in increasing order.

Take the support to consist of the distinct true rankings \(r_1,\ldots,r_n\), each with mass \(1/n\). Index these types by \(h\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\). Thus \(\tau=n\), and all masses are positive dyadic rationals summing to one.

Define the formula

\(C:=\bigwedge_{h=1}^{n}\left(x_{h,r_0}+x_{h,r_h}=1/n\right).\)

Here and below, a linear equality abbreviates two permitted non-strict inequalities. For example, the displayed equality abbreviates

\(\left(x_{h,r_0}+x_{h,r_h}\leq 1/n\right)\wedge\left(-x_{h,r_0}-x_{h,r_h}\leq-1/n\right).\)

Because every row has total mass \(1/n\) and all coordinates are nonnegative, \(C\) forces every other coordinate of row \(h\) to vanish.

Consequently, the states satisfying \(C\) are precisely the states \(X(u)\), for \(u\in[0,1]^n\), defined by

\(x_{h,r_h}=u_h/n,\qquad x_{h,r_0}=(1-u_h)/n,\)

with all other coordinates zero.

Aggregation is injective on this cube: for every \(h\),

\(z(X(u))_{r_h}=u_h/n.\)

Indeed, on \(C\), no other row puts mass on \(r_h\). Therefore \(z(X(u))=z(X(w))\) implies \(u=w\).

This injectivity has an essential consequence: every assignment of outcomes to the states satisfying \(C\) extends to a total rule \(F\). Assign the prescribed outcome at each aggregate \(z(X(u))\), and choose any fixed outcome at aggregates outside this image.

3. Budget modalities implement coordinate quantifiers

For each row \(h\), define the budget vector \(q^h\) by

\(q^h_h=1/n,\qquad q^h_k=0\quad(k\neq h).\)

Also define \(q^*\) by \(q^*_h=1/n\) for every row.

Use the abbreviations

\(\langle h\rangle\psi:=\Diamond_{q^h}\psi,\qquad [h]\psi:=\neg\langle h\rangle\neg\psi,\)

\(\mathsf E\psi:=\Diamond_{q^*}\psi,\qquad \mathsf A\psi:=\neg\mathsf E\neg\psi.\)

For any two distributions of mass \(1/n\) in one row,

\(\frac12\sum_r|x_{h,r}-y_{h,r}|\leq\frac12\sum_r(x_{h,r}+y_{h,r})=1/n.\)

Thus \(q^*\) makes every state accessible from every state. In particular, \(\mathsf E\) and \(\mathsf A\) are existential and universal modalities over the entire state space.

On the cube \(C\), accessibility under \(q^h\) has the exact characterization

\(X(u)\mathrel{\longrightarrow_{q^h}}X(w)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u_k=w_k\text{ for every }k\neq h.\)

To verify this, zero budgets force all other rows to remain unchanged, while row \(h\) has distance \(|u_h-w_h|/n\leq1/n\).

Define a guarded modality

\(M_h\psi:=\langle h\rangle(C\wedge\psi).\)

At a cube state, \(M_h\) therefore existentially chooses any value of coordinate \(u_h\) in \([0,1]\), preserving all other coordinates.

For row indices \(h,k\), let

\(E_{hk}:=\left(x_{h,r_h}=x_{k,r_k}\right).\)

On \(C\), this formula holds exactly when \(u_h=u_k\).

4. Encoding a domain and a binary relation

Rows \(1,\ldots,s\) represent the source variables. Reserve three further, distinct rows

\(d=s+1,\qquad a=s+2,\qquad b=s+3.\)

They will be auxiliary coordinates for a domain predicate and a binary relation. Any remaining rows are unused.

Choose four distinct outcomes \(c_{00},c_{01},c_{10},c_{11}\) in \(K\). Define outcome formulas

\(B_D:=c_{10}\vee c_{11},\qquad B_R:=c_{01}\vee c_{11},\)

and

\(\mathit{Allowed}:=c_{00}\vee c_{01}\vee c_{10}\vee c_{11}.\)

The first bit records domain membership; the second records membership in the encoded binary relation.

Define the consistency formula

\(\Gamma:=\mathsf A(C\rightarrow\mathit{Allowed})\wedge\mathsf E(C\wedge B_D)\)

\(\phantom{\Gamma:=}\wedge\bigwedge_{h\neq d}\mathsf A\left((C\wedge B_D)\rightarrow[h](C\rightarrow B_D)\right)\)

\(\phantom{\Gamma:=}\wedge\bigwedge_{h\notin\{a,b\}}\mathsf A\left((C\wedge B_R)\rightarrow[h](C\rightarrow B_R)\right).\)

All conjunctions range over the \(n\) rows.

Proven consequence: dependence only on designated coordinates

Suppose a rule satisfies \(\Gamma\). If two cube states differ only in coordinate \(h\neq d\), then they agree on \(B_D\).

Indeed, if \(B_D\) holds at the first state, the corresponding universally imposed implication forces it at the second. If it holds only at the second, apply the same implication there: the accessibility relation is symmetric, so this would force it at the first.

Changing finitely many coordinates one at a time shows that the truth of \(B_D\) depends only on \(u_d\). The identical argument shows that the truth of \(B_R\) depends only on \((u_a,u_b)\).

Consequently, there are well-defined sets

\(D\subseteq[0,1],\qquad Q\subseteq[0,1]^2\)

such that, at every cube state,

\(F,X(u)\models B_D\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u_d\in D,\)

\(F,X(u)\models B_R\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(u_a,u_b)\in Q.\)

The conjunct \(\mathsf E(C\wedge B_D)\) ensures \(D\neq\varnothing\).

We therefore obtain a first-order structure \(\mathcal M_F\) with domain \(D\) and relation

\(R^{\mathcal M_F}:=Q\cap D^2.\)

5. Translating first-order formulas

For each source variable index \(i\), define the domain test

\(\mathit{Dom}_i:=M_d(E_{di}\wedge B_D).\)

At every cube state, this has the exact meaning

\(F,X(u)\models\mathit{Dom}_i\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u_i\in D.\)

Indeed, changing row \(d\) can set \(u_d=u_i\), while preserving row \(i\).

Define the translation \(T\) recursively:

\(T(R(v_i,v_j)):=M_aM_b(E_{ai}\wedge E_{bj}\wedge B_R),\)

\(T(\neg\psi):=\neg T(\psi),\)

\(T(\psi\wedge\chi):=T(\psi)\wedge T(\chi),\)

\(T(\exists v_i\,\psi):=M_i(\mathit{Dom}_i\wedge T(\psi)).\)

The auxiliary rows are distinct from all source-variable rows.

Translation lemma

Suppose \(F\) satisfies \(\Gamma\). For every cube state with \(u_i\in D\) for all \(1\leq i\leq s\), and every source formula \(\psi\),

\(F,X(u)\models T(\psi)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal M_F,\rho_u\models\psi,\)

where \(\rho_u(v_i)=u_i\).

Proof. Proceed by structural induction.

For an atomic formula, the modalities \(M_aM_b\) change only the two auxiliary rows. The final equalities force their values to be \(u_i\) and \(u_j\). Such choices always exist. By the established coordinate independence,

\(F,X(u)\models T(R(v_i,v_j))\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(u_i,u_j)\in Q.\)

Since \(u_i,u_j\in D\), this is precisely \(R^{\mathcal M_F}(u_i,u_j)\).

Negation and conjunction follow directly from their semantics and the induction hypotheses.

For existential quantification, \(M_i\) chooses an arbitrary \(w\in[0,1]\) for row \(i\), preserving all other rows. The conjunct \(\mathit{Dom}_i\) restricts this choice exactly to \(w\in D\). Hence all source-variable coordinates still belong to \(D\), so the induction hypothesis applies at the chosen state. The resulting condition is exactly the first-order semantics of \(\exists v_i\,\psi\). This completes the induction.

Define the target formula

\(\varphi_\theta:=C\wedge\Gamma\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^{s}\mathit{Dom}_i\wedge T(\theta).\)

6. Correctness of the reduction

From target satisfiability to a first-order model

Suppose \(F,X\models\varphi_\theta\). The conjunct \(C\) identifies \(X\) with a cube state \(X(u)\). The formula \(\Gamma\) defines the nonempty structure \(\mathcal M_F\), and all domain tests ensure \(u_i\in D\).

By the translation lemma,

\(\mathcal M_F,\rho_u\models\theta.\)

Because \(\theta\) is a sentence, its truth is independent of the assignment. Thus \(\theta\) has a nonempty first-order model.

From a first-order model to target satisfiability

Suppose \(\theta\) has a nonempty model. We first justify that it has a model with at most countably many elements.

Starting with any model \(\mathcal N\models\theta\), choose one element. At each stage, add witnesses for all existential formulas true in \(\mathcal N\) whose finitely many parameters lie in the current set. There are countably many such formulas and parameter tuples at each stage. The union \(A\) of the resulting sets is nonempty and at most countable.

The induced relational structure on \(A\) is elementary in \(\mathcal N\). This follows by induction on formulas: atomic formulas agree by restriction; Boolean connectives preserve agreement; and every existential statement true in \(\mathcal N\) with parameters in \(A\) has its parameters in some stage and a witness in the following stage. Conversely, an existential witness in \(A\) is also a witness in \(\mathcal N\), using the induction hypothesis. Thus the induced structure also satisfies \(\theta\).

Let \(\mathcal M\models\theta\) be such a model, and choose an injection \(\eta:M\rightarrow[0,1]\). Put

\(D:=\eta(M),\)

\(Q:=\{(\eta(x),\eta(y)):(x,y)\in R^{\mathcal M}\}.\)

Assign the following outcome to every cube state \(X(u)\):

\(c_{\varepsilon\delta},\qquad \varepsilon=\mathbf1_D(u_d),\quad\delta=\mathbf1_Q(u_a,u_b).\)

Because aggregation is injective on the cube, these assignments extend to a total deterministic rule \(F\). Assign \(c_{00}\) at aggregates outside the cube’s aggregate image.

This rule satisfies \(\Gamma\): the selected outcomes are allowed, the domain is nonempty, and the two bits have exactly the required coordinate independence.

Choose a cube state whose source-variable coordinates all lie in \(D\). The decoded structure \(\mathcal M_F\) is isomorphic to \(\mathcal M\). Therefore the translation lemma gives \(T(\theta)\) at this state, and all other conjuncts of \(\varphi_\theta\) hold there.

This proves both directions of the reduction.

7. Effectiveness, size, and conclusion

The construction respects every input requirement:

  • The support contains \(\tau=n\) distinct, explicitly represented true rankings.
  • The outcome set has size \(m=\tau+1\).
  • Every mass is the dyadic rational \(1/\tau\).
  • All budgets have entries in \(\{0,1/\tau\}\).
  • Every linear atom uses at most two nonzero coefficients, each in \(\{-1,1\}\).
  • No preference modality is used.

In particular, the construction never enumerates all \(m!\) rankings. The formula \(C\) excludes unused report coordinates through nonnegativity and the row-sum equations.

Let \(N\) be the source sentence’s bit length. Then \(\tau=O(N)\) and \(m=\tau+1\). Expanding all abbreviations, a conservative bound on the output length and construction time is

\(O\!\left((N+\tau)\tau m\log(m+\tau)\right)=O\!\left(N^3\log(N+2)\right).\)

For this bound, each repeated copy of \(C\) includes the explicit ranking names in its coordinate references. The formula contains \(O(N+\tau)\) guarded modal constructions or consistency clauses. Consequently, both \(L\) and \(|\varphi_\theta|\) are polynomially bounded in \(N\).

A decision algorithm for the stated mass-state problem would therefore decide the undecidable source problem. Hence no algorithm decides all inputs, so there is no decision running-time bound in \(m,\tau,L,|\varphi|\).

The continuous coordinates are crucial: despite finite support and finite outcomes, the constrained state cube can represent an arbitrary first-order domain and relation through the unrestricted rule.
