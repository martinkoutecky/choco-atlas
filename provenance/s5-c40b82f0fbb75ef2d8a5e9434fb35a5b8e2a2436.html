The continuous mirror question, as graded

Let \(\mathcal U\) be a finite set of complete user types, each specifying a reward vector \(u\in[0,1]^M\) with \(\sum_a u(a)=1\) and any behavior parameters used by the interaction model. The society is an unknown rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\mathcal U\), with support size at most \(\tau\). A protocol adaptively selects users from this population, observes their identities and integral arm demonstrations but not their rewards, and outputs one arm; users respond according to the paper’s demonstration-plus-deployment utility model. For a query budget \(q\), what is the minimum worst-case distortion achievable by a protocol that makes truthful ordinal preferences a best response, over all such \(\mu\) and user best responses?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) positive-mass types, with masses \(\mu_i\) encoded as binary rationals and \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a rational reward vector \(u_i\in[0,1]^m\), \(\sum_a u_i(a)=1\), and a rational discount factor \(0<\delta_i<1\); assume \(m\ge2\). Let \(L\) be the total encoding length.
The support description specifies the hidden environment, not information supplied to the protocol. Each type has one strategic representative with an opaque persistent identity; its mass weights social welfare, not its influence when queried. A protocol knows \(m,\tau,L,q\), and can query either an independent draw from \(\mu\) or a previously observed representative. Both operations count toward the budget \(q\).
On query \(t\), representative \(H_t\) proposes one integral arm \(a_t\); the protocol observes the identity and proposal, then chooses whether to execute it, indicated by \(e_t\in\{0,1\}\). It may stop after \(K\le q\) queries and deploy one possibly randomized arm \(A\). Rewards and discount factors remain unobserved; there are no monetary transfers.
Representative \(i\) maximizes \(\mathbb E[\sum_{t=1}^{K}\delta_i^{t-1}\mathbf1\{H_t=i\}e_tu_i(a_t)+\delta_i^K u_i(A)]\). Strategies may depend on the public interaction history. Truthful ordinal play proposes a highest-reward arm not previously proposed by that representative, and a highest-reward arm after all arms have been proposed; ties use a fixed arm order. Require this strategy to be weakly dominant for every representative.
Write \(w_\mu(a)=\sum_i\mu_i u_i(a)\) and \(D(P)=\sup_{\mu,u,\delta,\sigma\in\mathrm{NE}(P)}\max_a w_\mu(a)/\mathbb E_{P,\sigma}[w_\mu(A)]\), where the supremum includes every mutually best-response strategy profile and every admissible hidden society of encoding length at most \(L\); a zero denominator gives infinite distortion. Expectations include sampling and protocol randomization. State computational running times in \(m,\tau,L,q\).

What is the optimal achievable worst-case distortion as a function of \(m,\tau,L,q\) for these incentive-compatible protocols? In particular, for which query budgets is distortion \(O(\sqrt{m\log m})\) achievable, and what lower bounds apply?

Partial solution: stronger bounds for intermediate supports

The optimal distortion for all $(m,\tau,L,q)$ remains unresolved. The results below give explicit incentive-compatible protocols, lower bounds witnessed at truthful equilibria, and several matching regimes.

Two additional bounds improve the dependence on intermediate support sizes:

  • A truncated harmonic protocol achieves distortion

$$ O\!\left(\sqrt{m\left(\log(q+1)+\frac{\tau}{q+1}\right)}\right) $$
when $1\le q<m$.

  • A family hiding a shared arm beyond the available query history gives the lower bound

$$ \Omega\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{m\min\{\tau,m/q\}}}{q}\right). $$

All statements below concern the specified supremum over every Nash equilibrium, not just truthful play.

1. Definitions and bounds

Let $D^*(m,\tau,L,q)$ be the infimum of $D(P)$ over protocols satisfying the required weak dominance condition. Assume the admissible class of societies is nonempty. Throughout, $L$ is the common encoding cap appearing in the definition of distortion.

For $q\ge1$, define
$$ k=\min\{q,m\},\qquad H_k=\sum_{r=1}^k\frac1r, $$
and
$$ C_k= \begin{cases} \tau/(k+1),&k<m,\\ 0,&k=m. \end{cases} $$
Set
$$ R_k=H_k+\frac{C_k}{2} +\frac12\sqrt{C_k^2+4C_kH_k}. $$
Also define
$$ B_{m,k}= \begin{cases} \max\left\{4m/(k+1),\sqrt{2mH_k}\right\},&k<m,\\ \sqrt{mH_m},&k=m. \end{cases} $$

The upper bounds proved below hold for every encoding cap:
$$ D^*(m,\tau,L,q) \le \min\left\{ m,\sqrt{m\tau},B_{m,k},\sqrt{mR_k} \right\}. $$
In particular,
$$ R_k\le 2H_k+C_k. $$
If $\tau=1$ and $q\ge1$, the stronger equality $D^*=1$ holds.

Under the standard explicit binary encoding of rational masses, coordinates, and discounts, there is an absolute constant $C$ such that all lower-bound families below fit within
$$ L_0=C\tau m\log_2(m+\tau+2). $$
Consequently, if $\tau\ge2$, $q\ge1$, and $L\ge L_0$, then
$$ D^*(m,\tau,L,q)\ge c\max\left\{ 1,\, \min\{\tau,\sqrt m\},\, \min\left\{\tau,\frac{m}{q+1}\right\},\, \min\left\{\sqrt m,\sqrt{\frac{m\tau}{q}}\right\},\, \frac{\sqrt{m\min\{\tau,m/q\}}}{q} \right\} $$
for an absolute constant $c>0$.

These imply:

  • If $L\ge L_0$, then $D^*(m,\tau,L,0)=m$.
  • If $\tau\ge2$ and $L\ge L_0$, then

$$ D^*(m,\tau,L,1) =\Theta\!\left(\min\{m,\sqrt{m\tau}\}\right). $$

  • Writing $h=1+\ln m$, distortion $O(\sqrt{m\log m})$ is achievable whenever

$$ q\ge \max\left\{ 1,\left\lceil \min\left\{\sqrt{\frac m h},\frac{\tau}{h}\right\} \right\rceil \right\}. $$

  • For $\tau\ge m$ and $L\ge L_0$, achieving this distortion requires

$$ q=\Omega\!\left(\sqrt{\frac m{\log m}}\right). $$
Thus the sufficient budget is sharp up to constants for these supports.

2. An incentive-compatible harmonic protocol

The harmonic lottery is related to the harmonic scoring construction in [Boutilier et al., Section 3](https://procaccia.info/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/optvoting.pdf). Its bounded-query implementation and incentive properties are proved here.

Protocol

Fix $1\le k\le m$.

  • On query $1$, independently sample a representative from $\mu$ and receive its proposal.
  • On queries $2,\ldots,k$, query that same representative.
  • Suppress every demonstration: $e_t=0$.
  • List distinct proposed arms in their order of first appearance, then append missing arms in the fixed arm order. Denote the resulting permutation by $\pi$.
  • With probability $1/2$, deploy a uniform arm. Otherwise deploy $\pi_r$, where

$$ \Pr(r)=\frac1{rH_k},\qquad 1\le r\le k. $$

The sampled query is the first of the $k$ queries. The protocol stops at $K=k$.

Weak dominance and behavior at every equilibrium

Conditional on representative $i$ being selected, its expected payoff is
$$ \delta_i^k\left[ \frac1{2m} +\frac1{2H_k}\sum_{r=1}^k\frac{u_i(\pi_r)}r \right]. $$
The positive factor $\delta_i^k$ does not change its maximizers.

Put $c_r=1/r$ for $r\le k$ and $c_r=0$ otherwise. If positions $r<s$, with $r\le k$, have
$$ u_i(\pi_r)<u_i(\pi_s), $$
then swapping those arms increases the weighted sum by
$$ (c_r-c_s)\bigl(u_i(\pi_s)-u_i(\pi_r)\bigr)>0. $$
Therefore a maximizing permutation places the $k$ largest rewards first, in nonincreasing order. Truthful ordinal proposals attain this maximum. Every ordered list of $k$ distinct arms is attainable by proposing that list, so the maximizing first $k$ positions are feasible.

No other representative acts during this interaction. Conditional on another representative being selected, changing $i$’s strategy has no effect. Thus truthful ordinal play is weakly dominant against arbitrary history-dependent opponent strategies.

Furthermore, $\mu_i>0$. If an equilibrium gave $i$ a conditional payoff below the attainable maximum, replacing its selected behavior by truthful play would strictly improve its total expected payoff. Hence, at every Nash equilibrium, the completed permutation maximizes the weighted sum almost surely.

It follows that every such permutation satisfies
$$ u_i(\pi_r)\le\frac1r,\qquad 1\le r\le k. $$
If $k<m$, every omitted arm satisfies
$$ u_i(a)\le\frac1{k+1}. $$
Indeed, the first $k$ arms and the omitted arm all have reward at least $u_i(a)$, and their rewards sum to at most one. Tied arms may be ordered differently at an equilibrium; these inequalities remain valid.

3. The support-sensitive harmonic bound

Fix any society and any Nash equilibrium of the harmonic protocol. Let
$$ x=\max_a w_\mu(a), $$
and choose an optimal arm $a^*$.

Let $T$ be expected social welfare conditional on using the harmonic branch:
$$ T=\sum_i\mu_i\, \mathbb E\left[ \frac1{H_k}\sum_{r=1}^k\frac{w_\mu(\pi_r)}r \,\middle|\,i\text{ selected} \right]. $$
The protocol’s expected welfare is
$$ W=\frac1{2m}+\frac T2. $$

Split the optimal arm’s welfare into its revealed and omitted contributions:
$$ X=\sum_i\mu_i u_i(a^*)\Pr(a^*\in\{\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_k\}\mid i), $$
$$ Y=\sum_i\mu_i u_i(a^*)\Pr(a^*\notin\{\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_k\}\mid i). $$
Thus $x=X+Y$.

Revealed contribution

Define
$$ s(a)=\sum_i\mu_i\, \mathbb E\left[ \sum_{r=1}^k\frac{\mathbf1\{\pi_r=a\}}r \,\middle|\,i \right]. $$
Whenever $a^*$ appears at rank $r$, normalization gives $u_i(a^*)\le1/r$. Consequently,
$$ X\le s(a^*). $$
The harmonic branch deploys $a^*$ with probability $s(a^*)/H_k$. Therefore
$$ T\ge \frac{x\,s(a^*)}{H_k}\ge\frac{xX}{H_k}. $$

Omitted contribution

For $k<m$, put
$$ z_i=u_i(a^*)\Pr(a^*\notin\{\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_k\}\mid i). $$
On every maximizing permutation omitting $a^*$, all revealed arms have reward at least $u_i(a^*)$. Also $w_\mu(a)\ge\mu_i u_i(a)$. Hence
$$ T\ge\sum_i\mu_i^2z_i. $$
Since an omitted arm has utility at most $1/(k+1)$,
$$ \sum_i z_i\le\frac{\tau}{k+1}=C_k. $$
Cauchy–Schwarz now gives
$$ Y^2 =\left(\sum_i\mu_i z_i\right)^2 \le \left(\sum_i\mu_i^2z_i\right) \left(\sum_i z_i\right) \le C_kT. $$
When $k=m$, there are no omitted contributions, so $Y=0$ and the same inequality holds with $C_k=0$.

Combining the contributions

The two estimates imply
$$ x=X+Y\le\frac{H_kT}{x}+\sqrt{C_kT}. $$
Since $x>0$, set $t=T/x^2$. Then
$$ 1\le H_kt+\sqrt{C_kt}. $$
Solving this quadratic inequality in $\sqrt t$ gives
$$ t\ge \frac4{\left(\sqrt{C_k+4H_k}+\sqrt{C_k}\right)^2} =\frac1{R_k}. $$
Thus $T\ge x^2/R_k$, and
$$ W\ge\frac1{2m}+\frac{x^2}{2R_k} \ge\frac{x}{\sqrt{mR_k}}, $$
where the last step is the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality. This proves
$$ D(P)\le\sqrt{mR_k}. $$

Finally,
$$ \sqrt{C_k^2+4C_kH_k}\le C_k+2H_k, $$
because squaring the nonnegative sides reduces the assertion to $0\le4H_k^2$. Therefore
$$ R_k\le2H_k+C_k. $$

4. Other upper bounds

The support-independent truncation bound

The rank inequalities imply
$$ w_\mu(a)\le s(a)+b,\qquad b= \begin{cases} 1/(k+1),&k<m,\\ 0,&k=m. \end{cases} $$
To verify this, an included contribution satisfies $u_i(a)\le1/r$, while an omitted contribution is at most $b$.

Consequently,
$$ W\ge\frac1{2m}+\frac{x(x-b)_+}{2H_k}. $$
If $k<m$ and $x\le2b$, then $x/W\le4m/(k+1)$. If $x>2b$, then
$$ W\ge\frac1{2m}+\frac{x^2}{4H_k} \ge\frac{x}{\sqrt{2mH_k}}. $$
For $k=m$, the stronger estimate is
$$ W\ge\frac1{2m}+\frac{x^2}{2H_m} \ge\frac{x}{\sqrt{mH_m}}. $$
These prove the bound $B_{m,k}$.

A one-query support bound

Independently sample one representative, receive its proposal, suppress the demonstration, and deploy that proposal with probability $1/2$, otherwise deploying uniformly.

Conditional on selection, every best response proposes a favorite arm. This establishes weak dominance of truthful play and favorite proposals at every equilibrium.

Let $v_i=\max_a u_i(a)$, and let $T_0$ be welfare when deploying the sampled favorite. Every favorite of $i$ has welfare at least $\mu_i v_i$, so
$$ T_0\ge\sum_i\mu_i^2v_i. $$
Also $x\le\sum_i\mu_i v_i$. By Cauchy–Schwarz and $v_i\le1$,
$$ x^2\le \left(\sum_i\mu_i^2v_i\right)\left(\sum_i v_i\right) \le\tau T_0. $$
Therefore
$$ W\ge\frac1{2m}+\frac{x^2}{2\tau} \ge\frac{x}{\sqrt{m\tau}}. $$

If $\tau=1$, deploy the proposal without the uniform branch. Every equilibrium then deploys a welfare-maximizing arm, giving distortion one.

Uniform deployment without any queries has expected welfare $1/m$, and therefore distortion at most $m$. Choosing among these protocols using the known parameters proves the stated minimum of upper bounds.

5. Lower-bound conventions and encodings

Every lower bound below uses truthful play. Because truthful strategies are weakly dominant in every admissible protocol, their joint profile is a Nash equilibrium. It is therefore a valid witness for the distortion definition.

Use the same opaque identities across societies in each indistinguishability family. Assign representative $i$ discount
$$ \delta_i=\frac{i}{\tau+1}. $$
These discounts distinguish all types, even when utility vectors coincide.

Whenever representatives are partitioned into $s$ groups, let group $j$ contain $n_j\ge1$ representatives, with $\sum_j n_j=\tau$. A group of total mass $1/s$ is implemented by assigning each member mass
$$ \frac1{s n_j}. $$
Thus all individual masses are explicitly positive rationals.

In the constructions with $s$ exceptional representatives, each has the specified equal exceptional mass, and the remaining mass is divided equally among the $\tau-s$ other representatives.

All lower-bound constructions requiring a nontrivial range of $q$ use $q\le m$. Their utility, mass, and discount numerators and denominators are bounded by a fixed polynomial in $m+\tau$:

  • group masses have denominators at most $\tau^2$;
  • utility denominators are at most $m^2$ or $q+1$;
  • the additional mass denominators below are $O(m^2\tau)$;
  • discount denominators equal $\tau+1$.

Each rational therefore needs $O(\log_2(m+\tau+2))$ bits. There are $\tau m$ utility coordinates and $O(\tau)$ other rationals. This proves the asserted encoding bound $L_0$.

No queries

With no queries, the deployment distribution is society-independent. Some arm $b$ has deployment probability at most $1/m$.

Give every representative utility $\mathbf e_b$, where $\mathbf e_b$ assigns utility one to $b$ and zero elsewhere. Use equal masses $1/\tau$. Optimal welfare is one, while deployed welfare is at most $1/m$. Thus distortion is at least $m$.

Together with uniform deployment, this proves $D^*=m$ when $L\ge L_0$.

Complete-ordinal indistinguishability

Let
$$ s=\min\{\tau,\lfloor\sqrt m\rfloor\}. $$
Partition the representatives into $s$ groups of total mass $1/s$, and choose distinct arms $a_1,\ldots,a_s$. Let $U$ denote the uniform utility vector and put
$$ v_j=\left(1-\frac1m\right)U+\frac1m\mathbf e_{a_j}. $$

For each $j$, construct a society in which group $j$ has utility $\mathbf e_{a_j}$ and every other group $\ell$ has utility $v_\ell$.

Each representative has the same truthful proposal sequence in every society: its assigned favorite first, the other arms in fixed order, and its favorite after exhaustion. Masses, identities, and discounts also agree. Thus even arbitrarily many adaptive queries have the same truthful transcript distribution.

Let $p$ be the common deployment distribution. Some $j$ has $p(a_j)\le1/s$. In society $j$,
$$ w_\mu(a_j)\ge\frac1s, $$
and
$$ \mathbb E[w_\mu(A)] \le\frac1m+\frac1{s^2}+\frac1{sm} \le\frac3{s^2}, $$
using $s^2\le m$. Distortion is at least $s/3$, proving
$$ D(P)=\Omega(\min\{\tau,\sqrt m\}). $$

6. Lower bounds from unrevealed ranks

A common arm beyond the query budget

Suppose $1\le q\le m/2$, and set
$$ s=\min\left\{\tau,\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2q}\right\rfloor\right\}, \qquad h=m-sq. $$
Partition the first $sq$ arms in fixed order into $s$ blocks of size $q$. Assign one block to each of $s$ representative groups of total mass $1/s$.

For every candidate $b$ among the last $h$ arms, give each representative utility $1/(q+1)$ on its group’s block and on $b$, and zero elsewhere.

Its first $q$ truthful proposals are its block arms. No representative receives more than $q$ queries, so the transcript is independent of $b$.

Some candidate has deployment probability at most $1/h$. Its welfare is $1/(q+1)$, while every block arm has welfare $1/[s(q+1)]$. Therefore
$$ D(P)\ge\frac1{1/s+1/h}\ge\frac s2, $$
because $h\ge m/2\ge s$.

The inequality $\lfloor z\rfloor\ge z/2$ for $z\ge1$ gives the desired order
$$ \Omega\!\left(\min\left\{\tau,\frac{m}{q+1}\right\}\right). $$
For $q>m/2$, the expression inside this last minimum is below two, so the universal lower bound $D(P)\ge1$ suffices.

Rare representatives with an unrevealed shared arm

This strengthens the one-query construction and applies to larger budgets.

Suppose $\tau\ge2$ and $1\le q\le b=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$. Let $B$ be the first $b$ arms in fixed order, let $C$ contain the other $h=m-b$ arms, and set
$$ s=\min\left\{\tau-1,\left\lfloor\frac bq\right\rfloor\right\}. $$
Choose $s$ labeled disjoint blocks of $q$ private arms uniformly from $B$. Independently choose a common arm $c$ uniformly from $C$.

Define
$$ \varepsilon= \frac1{\left\lceil 2\sqrt{\frac{mq}{s(q+1)}} \right\rceil}. $$
Give exceptional representative $j$ mass $\varepsilon/s$ and utility $1/(q+1)$ on its private block and on $c$. Give the other $\tau-s$ representatives uniform utility and equal masses $(1-\varepsilon)/(\tau-s)$.

Because $s\le m/(2q)$,
$$ \frac{mq}{s(q+1)}\ge1. $$
Consequently,
$$ \varepsilon\le\frac12,\qquad \varepsilon^2\le\frac{s(q+1)}{4mq},\qquad \varepsilon\ge\frac13\sqrt{\frac{s(q+1)}{mq}}. $$

Every exceptional representative’s first $q$ truthful proposals are its private arms. Thus $c$ is independent of the entire transcript, even when an exceptional representative is encountered.

Let $S$ be the union of the private blocks. Averaging over this finite family,
$$ \Pr(A=c)\le\frac1h, $$
and
$$ \Pr(A\in S)\le q\varepsilon+\frac{sq}{b}. $$
For the second inequality, encountering any exceptional representative has probability at most $q\varepsilon$: only fresh draws can discover one, and there are at most $q$ such draws. On the no-encounter event, the transcript is independent of the uniform set $S$.

For every arm,
$$ w_\mu(a)=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{m} +\frac{\varepsilon}{s(q+1)}\mathbf1\{a\in S\} +\frac{\varepsilon}{q+1}\mathbf1\{a=c\}. $$
Hence average deployed welfare is at most
$$ \frac1m+ \frac{q\varepsilon^2}{s(q+1)} +\frac{\varepsilon q}{(q+1)b} +\frac{\varepsilon}{(q+1)h} \le\frac4m. $$
Here we used $b\ge m/3$, $h\ge m/2$, $q+1\ge2$, and the displayed bounds on $\varepsilon$.

Every society has optimal welfare at least $\varepsilon/(q+1)$. Some society therefore has distortion at least
$$ \frac{m\varepsilon}{4(q+1)} \ge \frac{\sqrt{ms}}{12\sqrt{q(q+1)}}. $$
Moreover,
$$ s\ge\frac1{12}\min\{\tau,m/q\}. $$
Indeed, $\lfloor b/q\rfloor\ge b/(2q)\ge m/(6q)$, and $\tau-1\ge\tau/2$.

This proves
$$ D(P)=\Omega\!\left( \frac{\sqrt{m\min\{\tau,m/q\}}}{q} \right). $$
For $q>b$, this expression is bounded by an absolute constant, so $D(P)\ge1$ extends the statement to all $q\ge1$.

At $q=1$, this lower bound matches the upper bound $\min\{m,\sqrt{m\tau}\}$ up to constants.

7. A statistical lower bound with identical ordinal reports

We next prove an additional bound that is useful when several sampled identities can be observed but their cardinal utilities remain unknown.

Lemma

Suppose $q\ge1$ and an integer $s$ satisfies
$$ 1\le s\le\tau-1,\qquad s\max\{q,s\}\le m. $$
Then, for $L\ge L_0$,
$$ D(P)\ge c_0\sqrt{\frac{ms}{\max\{q,s\}}} $$
for an absolute constant $c_0>0$.

Construction

Put
$$ r=\max\{q,s\},\qquad \mu_0=\frac1{2r},\qquad \beta=1-s\mu_0, $$
and
$$ \varepsilon=\frac1{\left\lceil2\sqrt{mr/s}\right\rceil}. $$
The assumptions imply
$$ \beta\ge\frac12,\qquad \varepsilon\le\mu_0,\qquad \varepsilon^2\le\frac{s}{4mr},\qquad \varepsilon\ge\frac13\sqrt{\frac{s}{mr}}. $$

Choose distinct private arms $a_1,\ldots,a_s$ uniformly without replacement from all $m$ arms. Define
$$ v_j=\left(1-\frac1m\right)U+\frac1m\mathbf e_{a_j}. $$

In a reference society, representative $j\le s$ has mass $\mu_0$ and utility $v_j$. The remaining representatives have uniform utility and equal masses $\beta/(\tau-s)$.

In society $J$, replace representative $J$’s mass by $\varepsilon$ and its utility by $\mathbf e_{a_J}$. Divide the resulting background mass $\beta+\mu_0-\varepsilon$ equally among the remaining $\tau-s$ background representatives. Other private representatives are unchanged.

For a fixed assignment of private arms, all these societies have exactly the same truthful proposal oracle. The only observable distinction is the distribution of sampled identities.

Likelihood comparison

Pre-generate $q$ independent identity draws. A protocol consumes the next draw whenever it requests a fresh sample; unused draws are ignored. Together with internal randomness, these draws determine the truthful interaction.

Let $N_J$ be the number of appearances of identity $J$ in these $q$ draws, and let $N_{\mathrm{bg}}$ count background appearances. Relative to the reference society, the likelihood ratio for society $J$ is
$$ \Lambda_J= \left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu_0}\right)^{N_J} \left(\frac{\beta+\mu_0-\varepsilon}{\beta}\right)^{N_{\mathrm{bg}}}. $$
On $N_J\ge1$, since $\varepsilon\le\mu_0$,
$$ \Lambda_J \le 2r\varepsilon \left(1+\frac{\mu_0}{\beta}\right)^q \le2e\,r\varepsilon. $$
The last inequality uses
$$ \frac{q\mu_0}{\beta}\le\frac qr\le1. $$

Let $E_J$ be the event that the protocol actually encounters identity $J$. Then $E_J$ implies $N_J\ge1$. Since the truthful output is the same function of draws and randomness across the societies,
$$ \Pr_J(A=a_J,E_J) \le2e\,r\varepsilon\,\Pr_0(A=a_J). $$
Averaging over $J$ and using distinctness of the private arms gives
$$ \frac1s\sum_{J=1}^s\Pr_J(A=a_J,E_J) \le\frac{2e\,r\varepsilon}{s}. $$

Output without encountering the exceptional identity

Condition on $J$, all other private-arm assignments, the identity draws, and protocol randomness. Until identity $J$ is queried, the transcript does not depend on $a_J$. The event of never querying $J$ likewise does not depend on $a_J$.

The arm $a_J$ is uniform among the $m-s+1$ remaining arms. Therefore
$$ \Pr_J(A=a_J,E_J^c)\le\frac1{m-s+1}. $$
The condition $s\max\{q,s\}\le m$ implies $s^2\le m$, and hence $m-s+1\ge m/2$. Thus
$$ \Pr_J(A=a_J,E_J^c)\le\frac2m. $$

Welfare calculation

In society $J$, all nonexceptional representatives together contribute at most $2/m$ to the welfare of any arm. The uniform part contributes at most $1/m$, and the private bonuses contribute at most $1/m$.

Averaging over $J$ and the private-arm assignments, deployed welfare is therefore at most
$$ \frac2m+\varepsilon\left( \frac2m+\frac{2e\,r\varepsilon}{s} \right) \le\frac6m. $$
Optimal welfare is at least $\varepsilon$ in every society. Some society has distortion at least
$$ \frac{m\varepsilon}{6} \ge\frac1{18}\sqrt{\frac{ms}{r}}. $$
This proves the lemma.

Consequence for arbitrary supports and budgets

Combining the lemma with the complete-ordinal lower bound yields
$$ D(P)=\Omega\!\left( \min\left\{\sqrt m,\sqrt{\frac{m\tau}{q}}\right\} \right). $$

For completeness, consider the cases explicitly.

If $\tau>\sqrt m$, the complete-ordinal bound already gives $\Omega(\sqrt m)$.

Otherwise put $t=\tau-1$, so $t\ge\tau/2$.

  • If $q\le t$, choose $s=t$. Then $s\max\{q,s\}=t^2\le m$, and the lemma gives $\Omega(\sqrt m)$.
  • If $t<q\le m/t$, choose $s=t$. The lemma gives $\Omega(\sqrt{m\tau/q})$.
  • If $q>m/t$, then

$$ \sqrt{\frac{m\tau}{q}}<\sqrt{\tau t}\le\tau. $$
The complete-ordinal lower bound $\Omega(\tau)$ suffices.

Together with the preceding constructions, this proves the lower-bound maximum stated in Section 1.

8. Query thresholds and matching regimes

Let $h=1+\ln m$. Since $H_k\le h$, the support-sensitive bound gives
$$ D(P)\le\sqrt{m\left(2h+\frac{\tau}{k+1}\right)} $$
when $k<m$.

Thus $q\ge\lceil\tau/h\rceil$ suffices for distortion at most $\sqrt{3mh}$; if this would require more than $m$ queries, taking $k=m$ instead gives $\sqrt{mh}$.

Independently, the truncation bound $B_{m,k}$ gives $O(\sqrt{mh})$ when
$$ q\ge\left\lceil\sqrt{\frac m h}\right\rceil. $$
Choosing the smaller sufficient budget proves the threshold in Section 1.

The following regimes are settled up to constants, subject to $L\ge L_0$ where lower bounds are used.

One query

For $\tau\ge2$,
$$ D^*(m,\tau,L,1) =\Theta\!\left(\min\{m,\sqrt{m\tau}\}\right). $$
Consequently, in the usual asymptotic interpretation as $m$ grows, one query achieves $O(\sqrt{m\log m})$ precisely when $\tau=O(\log m)$. For $\tau=1$, one query achieves distortion one.

Large supports and small budgets

If
$$ 1\le q\le\sqrt{\frac m h}, \qquad \tau\ge\frac mq, $$
then
$$ D^*(m,\tau,L,q)=\Theta(m/q). $$
The upper bound follows because both terms in $B_{m,q}$ are $O(m/q)$. The hidden-rank construction gives the matching lower bound.

In particular, for $\tau\ge m$, achieving $O(\sqrt{m\log m})$ requires $q=\Omega(\sqrt{m/\log m})$, matching the sufficient budget.

Intermediate supports: a remaining gap

For
$$ 2\le\tau\le\sqrt{mh}, $$
the support-sensitive upper bound supplies a sufficient budget
$$ q=O\!\left(\max\{1,\tau/h\}\right). $$
The rare shared-arm lower bound supplies the necessary condition
$$ q=\Omega\!\left(\sqrt{\tau/h}\right) $$
for achieving $O(\sqrt{mh})$ distortion, with constants allowed to depend on the promised distortion constant.

To check the latter assertion, if $q\le m/\tau$, the lower bound is $\Omega(\sqrt{m\tau}/q)$. If $q>m/\tau$, the budget already exceeds $\sqrt{\tau/h}$, because
$$ \tau\le\sqrt{mh},\qquad h\le m $$
imply $\tau^3\le m^2h$.

These necessary and sufficient budgets do not match in general.

9. Computation and unresolved questions

The exhibited upper-bound protocols run in expected time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,q$, counting sampling and representative queries as oracle operations.

The harmonic protocol uses $O(m+k)$ list operations. Exact sampling of its rational rank distribution is also polynomial: take $M=k!$ and integer weights $M/r$. Their total has $O(k\log(k+1))$ bits. Uniform-bit rejection sampling selects an integer below that total in fewer than two attempts on average. The protocol never processes the hidden support or observes utilities or discounts.

The [finite-principal demonstration mechanism](https://aima.cs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/neurips20ws-mpag) does not directly resolve discovery of opaque representatives through weighted draws. The protocols here use the permitted suppression $e_t=0$ and establish their incentive and equilibrium guarantees directly.

The general optimum remains open. In particular, the bounds do not determine the minimum budget at intermediate supports, close the large-budget distortion gap, or characterize encoding caps below those containing the explicit lower-bound families.
