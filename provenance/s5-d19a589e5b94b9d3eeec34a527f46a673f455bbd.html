Theorem 6 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), symmetric rational affinities \(w_{ts}\), and an interaction graph on distinct types with edges where \(w_{ts}\ne 0\) and vertex-cover number at most \(4\), decide for rational \(r\) whether the mass can be partitioned into finitely or countably many nonzero coalition compositions \(x^j\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^{T}\) satisfying \(\sum_j x^j_t=\mu_t\) and \(\frac{\sum_s w_{ts}x^j_s}{\sum_s x^j_s}\ge r\) whenever \(x^j_t>0\); equivalently, compute the maximum egalitarian welfare.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Affinities satisfy \(w_{ts}=w_{st}\in\mathbb Q\) and \(w_{tt}=0\); positive, zero, and negative values are allowed. The interaction graph \(G=(T,E)\), with \(E=\{\{t,s\}:t\ne s,\ w_{ts}\ne0\}\), has vertex-cover number at most \(4\).
The input also includes \(r\in\mathbb Q\). Let \(m=|E|\) and let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
A coalition structure is a finite or countably infinite family of nonzero compositions \(x^j\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\) satisfying \(\sum_jx^j_t=\mu_t\) for every \(t\); a type's mass may be split among coalitions, including coalitions containing only that type.
For every \(j\) and every \(t\) with \(x^j_t>0\), the utility is \(u_t(x^j)=\frac{\sum_{s\in T}w_{ts}x^j_s}{\sum_{s\in T}x^j_s}\). The decision problem asks whether a coalition structure exists with \(u_t(x^j)\ge r\) for all such pairs.

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, under the promise that the interaction graph has vertex-cover number at most \(4\)?

For indivisible agents, egalitarian welfare maximization in symmetric fractional hedonic games is weakly NP-hard even on graphs with vertex-cover number at most \(4\), allowing signed weights. [Hanaka, Ikeyama, and Ono](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05139)
No complexity result for this precise mass-partition problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Set diagonal affinities to zero, following the loopless graph model; thus portions of the same type have zero mutual affinity.
  • Require nonempty support so that the society and its utility constraints are defined.
  • Define m as the number of interaction-graph edges and L as the total binary input length; there is no separate alternatives universe.
  • Use the stated threshold decision problem directly, avoiding an unstated attainment assumption in the claimed equivalence with maximum welfare.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\) under the stated promise. More generally, the same argument works for every fixed bound on the vertex-cover number.

The proof constructs a polynomial-size rational linear program. It also shows that allowing countably infinite coalition structures does not change feasibility: every feasible instance has a finite coalition structure with rational compositions.

1. Preliminary reductions

If \(r\leq 0\), the answer is yes: place each type in its own coalition. Since \(w_{tt}=0\), every resulting utility is zero.

Henceforth assume \(r>0\). If a type is isolated in the interaction graph, its utility is zero in every coalition containing it. Its mass is positive, so the answer is no.

Find a vertex cover \(C\subseteq T\) with \(k=|C|\leq 4\), and let \(I=T\setminus C\). Such a cover can be found by the usual bounded-depth branching procedure: for an uncovered edge, branch on including either endpoint, stopping after four choices. This takes \(O(m+\tau)\) time after reading the input.

Because \(C\) covers every edge,

\[ \text{[Equation rendered below using the requested delimiters.]} \]

\(w_{ij}=0\quad\text{for all }i,j\in I.\)

Write \(n=|I|\). After rejecting isolated types, every member of \(I\) has an edge to \(C\), so \(n\leq m\). Also,

\(m\leq kn+\binom{k}{2}=O(\tau).\)

2. Coalition eligibility depends on at most four coordinates

For a nonzero composition \(x\), define its total mass and normalized cover masses by

\(D(x)=\sum_{t\in T}x_t,\qquad q_c(x)=x_c/D(x)\quad(c\in C).\)

The vector \(q(x)\) belongs to the simplex

\(\Delta=\{q\in\mathbb R_{\geq 0}^{C}:\sum_{c\in C}q_c\leq 1\}.\)

For each \(i\in I\), define the affine function

\(f_i(q)=\sum_{c\in C}w_{ic}q_c-r.\)

Since there are no affinities between members of \(I\), whenever \(x_i>0\),

\(u_i(x)\geq r\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f_i(q(x))\geq 0.\)

For each \(c\in C\), also define \(h_c(q)=q_c\). Its sign records whether cover type \(c\) is present.

Thus consider the signs of the \(N=n+k=\tau\) affine functions

\(\{f_i:i\in I\}\cup\{h_c:c\in C\}.\)

A sign pattern assigns one of \(\{-,0,+\}\) to each function. Retain precisely those patterns realized by some \(q\in\Delta\). Let their set be \(\mathcal S\).

Zero signs must be retained: a type whose utility equals \(r\) is eligible.

Lemma: There are \(O((N+1)^k)\) retained patterns

First consider sign patterns in all of \(\mathbb R^k\). A nonempty sign cell is relatively open in the affine subspace defined by its zero-sign equations. If that subspace has codimension \(j\), it is defined by some \(j\) independent hyperplanes from the arrangement.

There are at most \(\binom Nj\) choices for those hyperplanes. On their intersection, the remaining hyperplanes define at most

\(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-j}\binom N\ell\)

full-dimensional regions. This region bound follows inductively: adding a hyperplane creates at most as many new regions as the preceding arrangement induces on that hyperplane.

Consequently the number of sign cells, including all dimensions, is at most

\(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom Nj\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-j}\binom N\ell=O((N+1)^k).\)

Constant affine functions have a fixed sign and can be ignored for this count. Restricting to \(\Delta\) cannot increase the number of realized patterns. Therefore,

\(H:=|\mathcal S|=O((\tau+1)^4)=O((m+1)^4).\)

3. The patterns can be enumerated exactly in polynomial time

Process the affine functions one at a time. Extend each retained prefix by each of the three possible signs, keeping an extension precisely when it is realizable in \(\Delta\).

For a candidate prefix, introduce \(q\in\Delta\) and \(0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1\). For each processed affine function \(g\), impose:

  • \(g(q)\geq\varepsilon\) for sign \(+\);
  • \(g(q)=0\) for sign \(0\);
  • \(g(q)\leq-\varepsilon\) for sign \(-\).

Maximize \(\varepsilon\). The candidate is realizable exactly when this rational linear program is feasible and its optimum is positive.

Indeed, a realizing point has a positive minimum margin over its finitely many strict sign conditions. Conversely, positive \(\varepsilon\) certifies all requested signs. If there are no strict conditions, any feasible point permits \(\varepsilon=1\).

Each enumeration LP has at most \(k+1\leq 5\) variables and \(O(N+1)\) constraints. Its feasible region is bounded. It can therefore be solved exactly by enumerating sets of active constraints, solving the associated constant-dimensional linear systems, and checking the resulting vertices.

For completeness, this gives a conservative enumeration bound of

\(O((N+1)^{2k+3}\operatorname{poly}(L))\leq O((\tau+1)^{11}\operatorname{poly}(L)).\)

To obtain this bound, there are \(O((N+1)^{k+1})\) candidate-prefix tests altogether. Each test considers \(O((N+1)^{k+1})\) sets of active constraints and checks each candidate against \(O(N+1)\) constraints. Constant-dimensional rational linear algebra uses \(\operatorname{poly}(L)\) bit operations.

4. A closed polyhedral cone for each pattern

Fix \(\sigma\in\mathcal S\). Define

\(A_\sigma=\{c\in C:\sigma(h_c)=+\},\qquad B_\sigma=\{i\in I:\sigma(f_i)\in\{0,+\}\}.\)

Coordinate functions cannot have negative signs on \(\Delta\).

Construct a cone \(K_\sigma\) of compositions \(x\), with an auxiliary variable \(D\), as follows.

First impose nonnegativity, total mass, and absence conditions:

\(x_t\geq 0,\qquad D=\sum_{t\in T}x_t,\)

\(x_c=0\quad(c\in C\setminus A_\sigma),\qquad x_i=0\quad(i\in I\setminus B_\sigma).\)

For each \(i\in I\), let

\(\ell_i(x,D)=\sum_{c\in C}w_{ic}x_c-rD.\)

Impose

\(\ell_i(x,D)\geq 0\text{ if }\sigma(f_i)=+,\)

\(\ell_i(x,D)=0\text{ if }\sigma(f_i)=0,\)

\(\ell_i(x,D)\leq 0\text{ if }\sigma(f_i)=-.\)

Finally, impose the utility inequalities for the potentially present cover types:

\(\sum_{s\in T}w_{cs}x_s-rD\geq 0\qquad(c\in A_\sigma).\)

All these constraints are homogeneous linear equalities or inequalities with rational coefficients. Thus \(K_\sigma\) is a closed convex polyhedral cone.

Strict signs have deliberately been weakened here. The following two claims establish that this weakening is sound.

Claim 1: Every nonzero member of \(K_\sigma\) is a valid coalition

For nonzero \(x\), nonnegativity gives \(D>0\).

If \(x_i>0\) for \(i\in I\), then \(i\in B_\sigma\), and the imposed sign constraint gives \(\ell_i(x,D)\geq 0\). Dividing by \(D\) yields \(u_i(x)\geq r\).

If \(x_c>0\) for \(c\in C\), then \(c\in A_\sigma\), and its explicitly imposed utility inequality gives \(u_c(x)\geq r\).

These cover every present type.

Claim 2: Every valid coalition belongs to some \(K_\sigma\)

Let \(x\) be valid, and take the exact sign pattern \(\sigma\) of its normalized vector \(q(x)\). This pattern belongs to \(\mathcal S\).

A cover type is present exactly when its coordinate function has positive sign. A present type \(i\in I\) must satisfy \(f_i(q(x))\geq 0\), so it belongs to \(B_\sigma\).

Furthermore,

\(\ell_i(x,D(x))=D(x)f_i(q(x)),\)

which proves all the required sign constraints. Validity supplies the cover-type inequalities.

Therefore \(x\in K_\sigma\).

5. The master linear program

For every \(\sigma\in\mathcal S\), introduce one block \((x^\sigma,D^\sigma)\) satisfying the defining constraints of \(K_\sigma\). Add the mass-balance equations

\(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal S}x^\sigma_t=\mu_t\qquad(t\in T).\)

This is a rational linear feasibility problem.

Claim 3: Master-LP feasibility is equivalent to the requested feasibility

From the LP to a coalition structure.
Discard zero blocks. By Claim 1, every remaining block is a valid nonzero coalition. The balance equations ensure that these finitely many coalitions use exactly the prescribed masses.

From a coalition structure to the LP.
Suppose a finite or countably infinite valid structure \(\{x^j\}\) is given. By Claim 2, assign each coalition to its exact sign pattern. For each \(\sigma\), set

\(y^\sigma=\sum_{j:\,\sigma(q(x^j))=\sigma}x^j.\)

These coordinatewise sums converge because they are sums of nonnegative quantities bounded by the prescribed masses.

Every defining constraint of \(K_\sigma\) is linear and homogeneous. Such constraints remain true under these sums. Explicitly, for any fixed coefficient vector \(a\),

\(\sum_j|a\cdot x^j|\leq \max_t|a_t|\sum_j\sum_t x^j_t=\max_t|a_t|,\)

so all relevant linear expressions can be summed absolutely. Hence \(y^\sigma\in K_\sigma\). The vectors \(y^\sigma\) satisfy the mass-balance equations and give a feasible master-LP solution.

This also proves that countably infinite structures provide no additional feasibility.

6. Running time and conclusion

The master LP has

\(O(\tau H)=O(\tau(m+1)^4)\)

variables and constraints. Its sparse encoding has

\(O(\tau(m+1)^4L)\)

bits: leaf-sign constraints involve at most four cover coordinates, and there are at most four cover-utility constraints per block. All coefficients come from the input, together with \(0\), \(1\), and sign changes.

Using polynomial-time exact rational linear programming, a conservative total bit-complexity bound is

\(O(L+m+\tau)+O((m+1)^{11}\operatorname{poly}(L))+\operatorname{poly}(\tau(m+1)^4L).\)

In particular, this is \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\). The dependence is on the support size \(\tau\), as required.

A feasible rational LP has a rational feasible solution; extracting its nonzero blocks gives a finite rational coalition structure with at most \(O((m+1)^4)\) coalitions.

The cited hardness theorem concerns partitions of indivisible vertices. [Hanaka, Ikeyama, and Ono](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05139) Divisible masses permit the linear-program construction above, including mass contributions to several pattern blocks. Signed affinities cause no difficulty because every inequality is retained with its original rational coefficients.

Therefore the stated mass-partition decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\).
