The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(d>0\) and a rational finite-support probability measure \(\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j\delta_{x_j}\) over ideal locations on \(\mathbb R\), a deterministic mechanism \(F\) chooses a range \((a,b)\) with \(b-a\le d\). Let \(SC_\mu(a,b)=\int c_d(x;a,b)\,d\mu(x)\), and let \(OPT_\mu\) be its minimum over feasible ranges. Call \(F\) mass-SGSP if there are no submeasure \(\eta\le\mu\) and replacement measure \(\nu\) with \(\nu(\mathbb R)=\eta(\mathbb R)\) such that, under \(F(\mu-\eta+\nu)\), the coalition is weakly better off \(\eta\)-almost everywhere and strictly better off on positive \(\eta\)-mass. Prove or refute that every deterministic mass-SGSP \(F\) has unbounded worst-case ratio \(SC_\mu(F(\mu))/OPT_\mu\) over rational finite-support societies.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix \(d\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), with the facility at \(0\). A society is given by its support: \(\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j\delta_{x_j}\), with distinct \(x_j\in\mathbb Q\), positive binary rational masses, and \(\sum_j\mu_j=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. Any running time is stated in \(\tau,L\); there is no candidate-count parameter \(m\).
A feasible outcome is an endpoint pair \((a,b)\in\mathbb R^2\) with \(0\le b-a\le d\), representing the closed accessibility interval \([a,b]\). Define \(c_d(x;a,b)=|x|-\operatorname{length}([\min\{x,0\},\max\{x,0\}]\cap[a,b])\).
Set \(SC_\mu(a,b)=\sum_j\mu_jc_d(x_j;a,b)\) and \(OPT_\mu=\min_{0\le b-a\le d}SC_\mu(a,b)\). A deterministic mechanism \(F\) selects one feasible outcome for every society, with no computational restriction.
Call \(F\) mass-SGSP if there are no nonnegative rational finite-support measures \(\eta\le\mu\) and \(\nu\) on \(\mathbb Q\), with \(\eta(\mathbb R)=\nu(\mathbb R)>0\), such that, writing \(\mu'=\mu-\eta+\nu\), we have \(c_d(x;F(\mu'))\le c_d(x;F(\mu))\) for \(\eta\)-almost every \(x\), strictly on a set of positive \(\eta\)-mass. Coalition members may retain their reports, and each type's mass may be split.
Define \(R_d(F)=\sup_\mu SC_\mu(F(\mu))/OPT_\mu\), with \(0/0=1\) and a positive numerator divided by zero equal to \(+\infty\).

For every fixed \(d\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), must every deterministic mass-SGSP mechanism satisfy \(R_d(F)=+\infty\), or does one exist with \(R_d(F)<+\infty\)?

For discrete societies of \(n\) agents, deterministic SGSP mechanisms have social-cost approximation ratio at least \((n+2)/4\); a deterministic SGSP mechanism achieves \(n-1\) for \(n\ge2\). [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08993v1).

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

For every fixed \(d\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), every deterministic mass-SGSP mechanism satisfies \(R_d(F)=+\infty\). Unboundedness is already witnessed by societies with \(\tau\le 3\).

The proof adapts the three-profile construction underlying the discrete lower bound in [Theorem 3 of the supplied paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08993v1). We verify the mass deviations explicitly, including retained reports and rationality.

1. Zero-cost societies and intervals containing the facility

Fix a deterministic mass-SGSP mechanism \(F\).

The society \(\delta_d\) has optimum zero. If \(F(\delta_d)\) gives its type positive cost, then \(R_d(F)=+\infty\) immediately. Otherwise, writing \(F(\delta_d)=(a,b)\), zero cost means that \([a,b]\) covers the entire segment \([0,d]\). Because \(b-a\le d\), necessarily \(F(\delta_d)=(0,d)\).

The same argument applies to \(\delta_{-d}\). Thus it remains to consider mechanisms satisfying

\(F(\delta_d)=(0,d),\qquad F(\delta_{-d})=(-d,0).\)

We first prove that, for any society supported in \([-d,d]\) with positive mass at both \(-d\) and \(d\), its selected interval contains \(0\).

Indeed, suppose its outcome \((a,b)\) has \(a>0\). Let the entire society report \(d\), using the admissible deviation \(\eta=\mu\), \(\nu=\delta_d\). The resulting outcome is \((0,d)\). Every nonpositive true type has unchanged cost, since both intervals save it zero distance. Every positive true type now has zero cost and therefore weakly benefits. The positive mass at \(d\) strictly benefits: its original saving was less than \(d\) because \(a>0\). This contradicts mass-SGSP.

If \(b<0\), the entire society can instead report \(-d\), with the symmetric contradiction. Consequently \(a\le0\le b\), as claimed.

For any such outcome, define its left and right reach by \(p=-a\) and \(q=b\). Feasibility gives

\(p\ge0,\qquad q\ge0,\qquad p+q\le d.\)

For \(0\le t\le d\), the costs are

\(c_d(-t;a,b)=\max\{t-p,0\},\qquad c_d(t;a,b)=\max\{t-q,0\}.\)

2. Three societies whose outcomes must agree

Choose rational numbers \(0<\alpha<1/2\) and \(0<\varepsilon<d\). Consider

\(\mu_0=\frac12\delta_{-d}+\frac12\delta_d,\)

\(\mu_L=\alpha\delta_{-d}+\left(\frac12-\alpha\right)\delta_{-\varepsilon}+\frac12\delta_d,\)

\(\mu_R=\frac12\delta_{-d}+\left(\frac12-\alpha\right)\delta_{\varepsilon}+\alpha\delta_d.\)

All three outcomes contain \(0\), by the preceding argument. Write their reaches as \((p_0,q_0)\), \((p_L,q_L)\), and \((p_R,q_R)\).

Equality of the left reaches

Suppose \(p_L>p_0\). At \(\mu_0\), the negative half can use

\(\eta=\frac12\delta_{-d},\qquad \nu=\alpha\delta_{-d}+\left(\frac12-\alpha\right)\delta_{-\varepsilon}.\)

This produces \(\mu_L\). Every coalition member has true type \(-d\), so every member strictly benefits when the left reach increases. This is forbidden.

Suppose instead \(p_L<p_0\). At \(\mu_L\), use the reverse deviation

\(\eta=\alpha\delta_{-d}+\left(\frac12-\alpha\right)\delta_{-\varepsilon},\qquad \nu=\frac12\delta_{-d}.\)

This produces \(\mu_0\). The true type \(-\varepsilon\) weakly benefits because its cost is nonincreasing in the left reach. The retained positive mass \(\alpha\) at true type \(-d\) strictly benefits. This is also forbidden.

Therefore \(p_L=p_0\).

Equality of the right reaches

If \(q_L>q_0\), let the entire society \(\mu_0\) report \(\mu_L\): take \(\eta=\mu_0\), \(\nu=\mu_L\). All negative true types have unchanged cost because \(p_L=p_0\), while the positive half at \(d\) strictly benefits.

If \(q_L<q_0\), use the reverse full-society deviation from \(\mu_L\) to \(\mu_0\). Again all negative true types have unchanged cost, and the positive half at \(d\) strictly benefits.

Both possibilities contradict mass-SGSP. Hence \(q_L=q_0\).

For \(\mu_R\), the same comparisons with signs exchanged first force \(q_R=q_0\) through right-half deviations, and then force \(p_R=p_0\) through full-society deviations. Thus all three outcomes have common reaches \(p,q\), satisfying \(p+q\le d\).

Every deviation above uses nonnegative rational measures of equal mass. The reports retained by coalition members are included in both \(\eta\) and \(\nu\), exactly as permitted by the definition.

3. An unbounded approximation ratio

Dropping the nonnegative costs of the near-zero types gives

\(SC_{\mu_L}(F(\mu_L))\ge\alpha(d-p)+\frac12(d-q),\)

\(SC_{\mu_R}(F(\mu_R))\ge\frac12(d-p)+\alpha(d-q).\)

Taking the average of these lower bounds yields

\(\max\{SC_{\mu_L}(F(\mu_L)),SC_{\mu_R}(F(\mu_R))\}\ge\frac{\frac12+\alpha}{2}(2d-p-q)\ge\left(\frac14+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)d.\)

Meanwhile, the feasible interval \([0,d]\) gives

\(OPT_{\mu_L}\le\alpha d+\left(\frac12-\alpha\right)\varepsilon.\)

The interval \([-d,0]\) gives the identical bound for \(OPT_{\mu_R}\). Therefore

\(R_d(F)\ge\frac{\left(\frac14+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)d}{\alpha d+\left(\frac12-\alpha\right)\varepsilon}.\)

This inequality also holds if either optimum is zero, using the stipulated infinite-ratio convention whenever its numerator is positive.

Now choose \(\alpha=2^{-k}\) and \(\varepsilon=\alpha d\), for any integer \(k\ge2\). These locations are rational, and all masses are positive dyadic rationals. Each perturbed society has exactly three distinct support points. The preceding bound becomes

\(R_d(F)\ge\frac{\frac14+\frac{\alpha}{2}}{\alpha\left(\frac32-\alpha\right)}\ge\frac{1}{6\alpha}=\frac{2^k}{6}.\)

Since \(k\) is arbitrarily large, \(R_d(F)=+\infty\). This completes the proof without any computational assumption.

1. Zero-cost societies

Otherwise, zero cost requires the intersection of the selected interval with \([0,d]\) to have length \(d\). Since the selected interval is closed and has length at most \(d\), it must equal \([0,d]\). The argument for \(\delta_{-d}\) is identical.

The resulting case distinction is exhaustive: either unboundedness is already established on a singleton society, or both displayed endpoint outcomes are forced.

2. Containment of the facility

The full-society deviations are admissible: \(\eta=\mu\) satisfies \(\eta\le\mu\), and both \(\eta\) and the replacement singleton measure have total mass one and rational finite support.

If \(a>0\), every nonpositive true type receives zero saving under both the original interval and \([0,d]\). The case \(b<0\) is symmetric. Because feasibility requires \(a\le b\), these cases exhaust intervals failing to contain zero.

For intervals containing zero, the reach definitions, feasibility inequalities, and displayed cost formulas follow directly from the intersection-length definition. In particular, \(p,q\le d\), so endpoint costs are exactly \(d-p\) and \(d-q\).

The proposed comparison intervals give the stated upper bound

\[ \alpha d+\left(\frac12-\alpha\right)\varepsilon \]

The mechanism and \(d\) remain fixed while \(k\) grows. Hence the supremum is infinite. Increasing input bit length is permitted; the task asks for a uniform approximation ratio over all admissible societies.
