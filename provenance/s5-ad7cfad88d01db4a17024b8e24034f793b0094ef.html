The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite latent state set \(\Theta\), prior \(\pi\), supported provider/report types \(t\) with masses \(\mu_t\), supplied posterior summaries \(q_t\) and \(r_t\) for each type and its peers, and valid score bounds \(\ell<u\), compute \(K_t=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}q_t(\theta)r_t(\theta)/\pi(\theta)\), the per-unit log-PMI payment \(\rho_t=b(\log K_t-\ell)/(u-\ell)\), and the total payout \(\sum_t\mu_t\rho_t\), exactly where possible and otherwise to a requested precision.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(\Theta\) be a finite latent state set of size \(m\geq 1\). A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types.
The input supplies a strictly positive rational prior distribution \(\pi\) on \(\Theta\) and rational probability vectors \(q_t,r_t\) on \(\Theta\). These summarize a type's report and its peers' reports, respectively; the report and peer data are conditionally independent given the latent state. The summaries are supplied, rather than inferred from the masses.
Also supplied are a positive rational budget per unit mass \(b\), rational bounds \(\ell<u\), and an integer precision parameter \(p\geq1\). Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the rational input.
Define \(K_t=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}q_t(\theta)r_t(\theta)/\pi(\theta)\). The bounds are promised to satisfy \(\ell\leq\ln K_t\leq u\) whenever \(K_t>0\).
Set \(\rho_t=b(\ln K_t-\ell)/(u-\ell)\) when \(K_t>0\), and \(\rho_t=0\) when \(K_t=0\); the total payout is \(P=\sum_t\mu_t\rho_t\).
The computational problem is to compute every \(K_t\) exactly and every \(\rho_t\) and \(P\) to absolute error at most \(2^{-p}\); exact payments may also be represented by finite expressions using rational arithmetic and natural logarithms.

Is this continuous-society payment computation solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,p\), or is it NP-hard?

For a finite list of providers with supplied posterior summaries, the latent-state overlap score is computable using \(O(m)\) arithmetic operations per provider. This establishes arithmetic-operation complexity, without specifying bit complexity for logarithms or requested payout precision.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive masses to sum to one and interpreted b as the budget per unit mass, replacing the discrete per-provider allocation B/n.
  • Required the prior and supplied posterior vectors to be binary rational probability distributions, with a strictly positive prior; posterior entries may be zero.
  • Restored the zero-overlap branch: types with K_t=0 receive zero payment, and score bounds apply only to positive overlaps.
  • Interpreted log as the natural logarithm and valid bounds as a supplied promise, rather than requiring their computation.
  • Specified absolute numerical error 2^{-p} for each payment and the total, with running time measured in p.
  • Resolved exact real output by allowing finite rational-and-logarithm expressions; exact overlap scores are rational.
  • Treated peer posterior summaries as supplied inputs, without reconstructing them from the society's masses.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,p\). The arithmetic-operation bound extends to a bit-complexity bound, including the requested logarithm and payment precision.

Below, integers are represented in binary, and rational numbers are represented by an integer numerator and a positive integer denominator. Fractions need not be reduced.

1. Exact computation of the overlap scores.

For each supported type \(t\), compute

\[ K_t=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\frac{q_t(\theta)r_t(\theta)}{\pi(\theta)}. \]

The strict positivity of \(\pi(\theta)\) makes every division well-defined. All summands are nonnegative, so \(K_t\) is a nonnegative rational whose equality to zero can be decided exactly.

Every input numerator and denominator has at most \(L\) bits. Consequently, each summand can be represented using a numerator and denominator with at most \(3L+O(1)\) bits. Putting the \(m\) summands over the product of their denominators gives a representation of \(K_t\) with numerator and denominator bit lengths bounded by

\[ B=O(mL+\log(m+1)). \]

Indeed, the product denominator has \(O(mL)\) bits, and adding \(m\) resulting numerators requires only an additional \(O(\log(m+1))\) bits.

Computing all scores therefore takes \(O(m\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations on integers of polynomial bit length. Ordinary binary integer arithmetic makes this a polynomial-time computation.

If \(K_t=0\), its payment is exactly zero. It remains to handle positive scores.

2. A constructive logarithm approximation bound.

We prove the following statement: given a positive rational \(x=a/d\), with numerator and denominator of at most \(B\) bits, and an integer \(h\geq1\), one can compute a rational \(A\) satisfying

\[ |A-\ln x|\leq 2^{-h} \]

in time polynomial in \(B+h\).

Choose the integer \(k\) such that

\[ 2^k\leq x<2^{k+1}, \qquad y=x\,2^{-k}. \]

Then \(1\leq y<2\). Since \(1\leq a,d<2^B\), we have \(2^{-B}<x<2^B\), and hence \(|k|\leq B\). The appropriate \(k\) can be found by comparing integers after binary shifts; even checking all integers between \(-B\) and \(B\) takes polynomial time.

Set

\[ z=\frac{y-1}{y+1}. \]

Thus \(0\leq z<1/3\), and its numerator and denominator have \(O(B)\) bits.

For \(0\leq w\leq1/3\), define the rational finite sum

\[ S_N(w)=2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{w^{2j+1}}{2j+1}. \]

To bound its error, first observe that

\[ \ln\frac{1+w}{1-w}=2\int_0^w\frac{dt}{1-t^2}. \]

This identity follows by differentiation and equality at \(w=0\). Applying the finite geometric-series identity inside the integral yields the exact remainder formula

\[ \ln\frac{1+w}{1-w}-S_N(w) =2\int_0^w\frac{t^{2N}}{1-t^2}\,dt. \]

The remainder is nonnegative and at most

\[ \frac{2w^{2N+1}}{(2N+1)(1-w^2)} \leq \frac34\,9^{-N} \leq 2^{-2N}. \]

The middle inequality uses \(w\leq1/3\) and \(2N+1\geq1\).

Since \(y=(1+z)/(1-z)\), while \(2=(1+1/3)/(1-1/3)\), define

\[ A=kS_N(1/3)+S_N(z). \]

Using \(\ln x=k\ln2+\ln y\) and the triangle inequality gives

\[ |A-\ln x| \leq (|k|+1)2^{-2N} \leq (B+1)2^{-2N}. \]

Choose \(N=h+B+2\). Because \(B+1\leq2^{B+1}\), this error is at most \(2^{-h}\).

It remains to verify bit complexity. Each term of either finite sum has numerator and denominator bit lengths \(O(NB+\log(N+1))\). Even naive fraction addition, multiplying denominators without cancellation, produces intermediate fractions with bit lengths at most

\[ O\!\left(N^2B+N\log(N+1)\right). \]

There are only \(O(N)\) terms. Powers can be computed successively, and every integer operation therefore involves polynomially many bits. The approximation algorithm has running time and output length polynomial in \(B+h\), proving the statement.

3. Precision sufficient for every payment.

Compute the positive rational

\[ C=\frac{b}{u-\ell} \]

exactly. Its numerator and denominator have \(O(L)\) bits: subtraction and division involve only a constant number of input rationals.

Write \(C=c/d\), with positive integers \(c,d\), and let \(s\) be the bit length of \(c\). Then \(s=O(L)\) and \(C<2^s\).

Choose

\[ h=p+s+2. \]

For every positive \(K_t\), apply the logarithm algorithm to obtain \(A_t\) with \(|A_t-\ln K_t|\leq2^{-h}\), and compute the rational approximation

\[ \widehat{\rho}_t=C(A_t-\ell). \]

For zero scores, set \(\widehat{\rho}_t=0\). For every positive score,

\[ |\widehat{\rho}_t-\rho_t| =C|A_t-\ln K_t| \leq 2^s2^{-h} =2^{-(p+2)}. \]

Thus every payment already meets the requested error bound.

Compute the total approximation using exact rational arithmetic:

\[ \widehat P=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\widehat{\rho}_t. \]

Positivity of the masses and their normalization imply

\[ |\widehat P-P| \leq\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t |\widehat{\rho}_t-\rho_t| \leq 2^{-(p+2)}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t =2^{-(p+2)}. \]

This also meets the requested error bound. In particular, summing over types does not require an additional precision factor depending on \(\tau\).

If dyadic numerical outputs are desired, round each \(\widehat{\rho}_t\) and \(\widehat P\) independently to the nearest multiple of \(2^{-p}\). Rounding contributes at most \(2^{-(p+1)}\), so each final error is at most

\[ 2^{-(p+2)}+2^{-(p+1)} =\frac34\,2^{-p} <2^{-p}. \]

4. Overall complexity and exact representations.

The scores have polynomial bit length, and the required logarithm precision is \(h=p+O(L)\). Therefore the logarithm computation, rational payment arithmetic, weighted summation, and optional rounding all run in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,p\). The intermediate denominator growth from summing over \(\tau\) types remains polynomial, since it adds the polynomial bit lengths of those denominators.

The bound promise also ensures \(0\leq\rho_t\leq b\) and \(0\leq P\leq b\). Thus the rounded outputs require only \(O(L+p)\) bits each.

Exact finite expressions are available as well: output \(0\) for zero scores, \(C(\ln K_t-\ell)\) for positive scores, and their rationally weighted sum for \(P\). These expressions have polynomial encoding length.

Only the supplied \(\tau\) supported types are processed; no enumeration of other possible types is needed. Hence the requested computation is polynomial-time solvable. An NP-hardness claim under polynomial-time reductions would consequently imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
