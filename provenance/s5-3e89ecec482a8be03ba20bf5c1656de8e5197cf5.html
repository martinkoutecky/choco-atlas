The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many complete valuation types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), goods \(G\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and nonnegative integer valuation vectors \(v_{t,g}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{\ell}\), does there exist a finitely supported distribution \(x_{t,b}\ge 0\) over type-and-integral-bundle states \( (t,b) \), where \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{G}\), such that \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_gx_{t,b}=q_g\) for every \(g\)? Require that for every ordered pair of occupied states \((t,b),(t',b')\) and every dimension \(k\), some deletion vector \(r\in\{0,1\}^{G}\) satisfies \(r_g\le b'_g\), \(\sum_g r_g\le 1\), and \(\sum_g b_gv_{t,g,k}\ge\sum_g(b'_g-r_g)v_{t,g,k}\). Theorem 12’s reduction uses one valuation type, binary valuations, and \(q_g=1/2\) for every good kind.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a set of \(m\ge 1\) good kinds and let \(\ell\ge 1\) be the number of valuation dimensions. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge 1\) complete valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
For every \(t,g,k\), the input specifies \(v_{t,g,k}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}\); supplies are binary rationals \(q_g\ge 0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length. A bundle is \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{G}\), with empty bundles and repeated copies allowed, and \(V_{t,k}(b)=\sum_g b_gv_{t,g,k}\).
A feasible allocation is a finitely supported family of real masses \(x_{t,b}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_gx_{t,b}=q_g\) for every \(g\).
It is weak simultaneously envy-free up to one good if, for every ordered pair \((t,b),(t',b')\) with \(x_{t,b}>0\) and \(x_{t',b'}>0\), and every \(k\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}\), there exists \(r\in\{0,1\}^{G}\) such that \(r_g\le b'_g\), \(\sum_gr_g\le 1\), and \(V_{t,k}(b)\ge V_{t,k}(b'-r)\). The deletion may depend on the ordered pair and dimension.

Is deciding whether such an allocation exists solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,\ell,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents and individually listed items, deciding existence of a complete weak simultaneously envy-free allocation up to one good is strongly NP-complete even for two identical agents with binary valuations. Result

The decision problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even when \(\tau=1\), the unique type has mass \(1\), all valuations belong to \(\{0,1\}\), and every supply equals \(1/2\).

The proof below accounts for both real allocation masses and repeated copies in bundles.

1. A consequence of binary valuations

Consider a single type of mass \(1\), and let \(S\) be the finite set of bundles receiving positive mass. In a dimension with binary valuations, write

\(W(b)=\sum_g b_gv_g\).

Weak simultaneous envy-freeness up to one good implies

\(|W(b)-W(c)|\le 1\qquad\text{for all }b,c\in S.\)

Indeed, apply the fairness condition in both directions. Each permitted deletion reduces the relevant value by at most \(1\).

We will use the following elementary observation. Suppose integer values on a finite support have range at most \(1\), and their positive-weight average is \(a\). Then every supported value belongs to

\(\{\lfloor a\rfloor,\lceil a\rceil\}.\)

To prove this, let the minimum and maximum be \(u\) and \(w\). If they coincide, their common value equals \(a\). Otherwise, integrality and the range bound give \(w=u+1\). Because both endpoints receive positive weight, \(u<a<u+1\), proving the assertion.

2. Polynomial reduction from the finite-agent problem

We use the established result that deciding existence of a complete weak simultaneously envy-free allocation up to one good is strongly \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete for two identical agents with binary valuations. This is [Theorem 12 of Kawase, Roy, and Sanpui](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol360-fsttcs2025/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2025.41/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2025.41.pdf).

Take an instance of that problem with individually listed items \(G\), dimensions \(1,\ldots,d\), and common valuations \(v_{g,k}\in\{0,1\}\). Construct an instance of the mass formulation as follows:

  • Treat each listed item as a separate good kind.
  • Use one valuation type, with mass \(1\).
  • Set \(q_g=1/2\) for every good kind.
  • Retain the original \(d\) valuation dimensions.
  • For each good kind \(g\), add a dimension that assigns value \(1\) to \(g\) and value \(0\) to every other kind.

Thus the constructed instance has \(\ell=d+m\) dimensions and polynomial encoding length.

A finite-agent solution gives a mass solution

Suppose \(A\) and \(G\setminus A\) form a complete fair allocation to the two finite agents. Let their incidence bundles be \(b\) and \(\bar b\), respectively.

Give mass \(1/2\) to each bundle. Every good kind then has supply \(1/2\).

Fairness in the original dimensions follows from the assumed finite-agent allocation. In each added dimension, the two bundle values are \(0\) and \(1\), so any envy disappears by deleting the corresponding good from the envied bundle. Comparisons of a bundle with itself require no deletion.

Hence the constructed mass instance is a yes-instance.

A mass solution gives a finite-agent solution

Conversely, suppose the constructed instance has a fair allocation, with positive support \(S\).

For each good kind \(g\), its added dimension implies that the supported coordinates \(b_g\) have range at most \(1\). Their average is \(q_g=1/2\). The observation in Section 1 therefore gives

\(b_g\in\{0,1\}\qquad\text{for every }b\in S.\)

Thus the additional dimensions exclude repeated copies from every supported bundle.

Now fix an original dimension \(k\), and define

\(s_k=\sum_g v_{g,k},\qquad W_k(b)=\sum_g b_gv_{g,k}.\)

Its average over the allocation is

\(\sum_{b\in S}x_bW_k(b)=\sum_gq_gv_{g,k}=s_k/2.\)

The supported values \(W_k(b)\) are integers with range at most \(1\). Consequently, every supported bundle satisfies

\(W_k(b)\in\{\lfloor s_k/2\rfloor,\lceil s_k/2\rceil\}.\)

Choose any \(b\in S\), and allocate its indicated items to the first finite agent and all remaining items to the second. The second bundle has value \(s_k-W_k(b)\), so

\(|W_k(b)-(s_k-W_k(b))|\le 1.\)

If the two values coincide, neither agent envies the other in dimension \(k\). If they differ, the difference is exactly \(1\). The higher-valued bundle contains an item valued \(1\), whose deletion eliminates the envy. This argument applies independently in every original dimension, exactly as permitted by weak simultaneous fairness.

Therefore the extracted finite-agent allocation is complete and fair.

The construction is a polynomial many-one reduction, proving \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness under the stated restrictions.

3. Polynomial-size certificates for the general formulation

For completeness, we prove membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\), despite the allowance of arbitrary real masses and unbounded integer bundles.

3.1. Bounding coordinates of valued goods

Assume a fair allocation exists. Define

\(Q=\sum_gq_g,\qquad \mu_*=\min_t\mu_t>0,\qquad M=\max_{t,g,k}v_{t,g,k}.\)

Call a good kind *valued* if at least one of its input valuations is positive.

Fix a type \(t\) and dimension \(k\). Since the support is finite and \(\mu_t>0\), the minimum value among bundles assigned to that type exists:

\(a_{t,k}=\min\{V_{t,k}(b):x_{t,b}>0\}.\)

Using nonnegativity and the supply equalities,

\(a_{t,k}\mu_t\le\sum_bx_{t,b}V_{t,k}(b)\le\sum_{s,b}x_{s,b}V_{t,k}(b)=\sum_gq_gv_{t,g,k}\le MQ.\)

Compare a bundle attaining this minimum with any supported typed bundle \((s,c)\). Fairness and the fact that one deletion removes value at most \(M\) imply

\(V_{t,k}(c)\le a_{t,k}+M\le MQ/\mu_*+M.\)

For any valued kind \(g\), choose \(t,k\) with \(v_{t,g,k}\ge 1\). Nonnegativity then gives, for every supported bundle \(c\),

\(c_g\le V_{t,k}(c)\le MQ/\mu_*+M.\)

This bounds every valued coordinate by a number of polynomial bit length.

3.2. Bounding coordinates of entirely unvalued goods

An entirely unvalued good contributes zero in every type and dimension. Fairness comparisons therefore do not depend on its coordinate: deleting such a good never improves an inequality.

For each entirely unvalued kind \(g\), replace its coordinate by a random integer taking the values \(\lfloor q_g\rfloor\) and \(\lceil q_g\rceil\), with probabilities chosen to give mean \(q_g\). Apply the product of these finite distributions to each original supported typed bundle, splitting its mass accordingly while retaining all valued coordinates.

This operation preserves every type mass and every valued-good supply. It gives each unvalued good its required global supply because the total population mass is \(1\). It also preserves fairness and finite support. Zero-weight outcomes are omitted.

Thus a fair allocation exists in which every bundle coordinate is bounded by

\(H=\left\lceil 1+Q+M(Q/\mu_*+1)\right\rceil.\)

The integer \(H\) has polynomial bit length under the given binary encoding.

3.3. Reducing the support and rationalizing its masses

Take the finite support \(S\) of the bounded allocation just constructed. Associate with each typed bundle \((t,b)\in S\) the integer column

\(a_{t,b}=(e_t,b)\in\mathbb Z^{\tau+m},\)

where \(e_t\) is the unit vector recording its type. Feasibility is precisely

\(\sum_{(t,b)\in S}x_{t,b}a_{t,b}=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau,q_1,\ldots,q_m),\qquad x_{t,b}\ge 0.\)

Among feasible weight vectors on this fixed finite support, choose one with the fewest positive entries. Its positively weighted columns are linearly independent.

To justify this, suppose those columns admitted a nonzero dependence with coefficients \(c_i\). Summing their type coordinates gives \(\sum_i c_i=0\), so the coefficients have both signs. If their current positive weights are \(x_i\), set

\(\theta=\min_{i:c_i>0}x_i/c_i.\)

Replacing \(x_i\) by \(x_i-\theta c_i\) preserves the constraints, keeps all weights nonnegative, and eliminates at least one positive entry. This contradicts minimality.

Consequently, the number \(h\) of positively weighted typed bundles satisfies \(h\le m+\tau\). Removing support elements preserves fairness.

These independent columns contain an invertible square minor of order \(h\). Their weights are the unique solution of the corresponding rational linear system, so Cramer’s rule makes every weight rational.

Their encoding lengths are polynomial as well. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses and supplies. Its bit length is polynomial in \(L\). The minor’s entries are bounded by \(H\), and the right-hand side, multiplied by \(D\), has entries bounded by \(DH\). The determinant expansion therefore bounds the relevant numerators and denominators by \(D\,h!\,H^h\). Their bit lengths are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Thus every yes-instance has a certificate comprising at most \(m+\tau\) typed bundles, with polynomial-bit-length integer coordinates and rational masses.

3.4. Verification

The verifier checks nonnegative integer coordinates, positive rational masses, and all type-mass and supply equalities using exact arithmetic.

For each ordered pair \((t,b),(s,c)\) in the certificate and every dimension \(k\), it checks

\(V_{t,k}(b)\ge V_{t,k}(c)-\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{v_{t,g,k}:c_g>0\}\bigr).\)

The maximum is exactly the greatest value removable by a permitted deletion of at most one good. Hence this inequality is equivalent to the required fairness condition.

There are polynomially many checks, all involving polynomial-bit-length numbers. The general decision problem therefore belongs to \(\mathsf{NP}\).

Together with Section 2, this proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness, and rules out a polynomial-time algorithm in the stated parameters unless \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).
