The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T=H\times V\), rational masses \(\mu_{h,v}\) summing to one, and a public priority order on \(H\), consider a single indivisible item and one-bit reports. In the limit of finite replications of these types, represent the winner lottery by per-unit-mass intensities \(\lambda_{h,b}\), with total winning probability \(\sum_{h,v,b}x_{h,v,b}\lambda_{h,b}=1\), where \(x_{h,v,b}\) is the mass of type \((h,v)\) reporting \(b\). Among mechanisms satisfying the limiting, per-capita-scaled incentive and ex-post individual-rationality constraints, maximize expected winner value \(\sum_{h,v,b}x_{h,v,b}\lambda_{h,b}v\); determine whether the optimum is attained or only approached.

The designer chooses reporting masses \(x_{h,v,b}\ge0\), finite intensities \(\lambda_{h,b}\ge0\), and real conditional winner payments \(c_{h,b}\), with \(\sum_bx_{h,v,b}=\mu_{h,v}\) and \(\sum_{h,v,b}x_{h,v,b}\lambda_{h,b}=1\); losers pay zero. Randomized allocations are permitted.
In a replication of size \(n\), intensity means winning probability \(\lambda_{h,b}/n\) and expected payment \(\lambda_{h,b}c_{h,b}/n\). An infinitesimal unilateral deviation leaves the aggregate report profile and these menus fixed.
The limiting incentive constraints are \(x_{h,v,b}>0\Rightarrow\lambda_{h,b}(v-c_{h,b})\ge\lambda_{h,1-b}(v-c_{h,1-b})\). Ex-post individual rationality requires \(c_{h,b}\le v\) whenever \(x_{h,v,b}\lambda_{h,b}>0\). Indifferent types may split their mass between reports.
Expected welfare is \(W=\sum_{h,v,b}x_{h,v,b}\lambda_{h,b}v\); the full-communication benchmark is \(W_{\mathrm{full}}=\max\{v:\mu_{h,v}>0\}\).

Yes. Exact welfare is attainable with finite rational intensities, and an exact mechanism can be constructed in polynomial time.

I use the stated mass-menu constraints. The supplied priority order imposes no additional displayed restriction on the menus. The construction below handles arbitrary finite support, including multiple classes and distinct valuations.

Construction

Let \(S=\{(h,v):\mu_{h,v}>0\}\), and define

\(M=\max\{v:(h,v)\in S\}=W_{\mathrm{full}}\), \(q=\sum_{(h,M)\in S}\mu_{h,M}\).

The nonempty finite support and positive masses imply \(0<q\leq 1\).

If there is a supported valuation below \(M\), define

\(s=\max\{v:(h,v)\in S,\ v<M\}\), \(p=(s+M)/2\).

Then \(s<p<M\). If all supported valuations equal \(M\), set \(p=M\).

For every \(h\in H\), choose the menus

\(\lambda_{h,0}=0,\quad c_{h,0}=0,\qquad \lambda_{h,1}=1/q,\quad c_{h,1}=p.\)

Assign every supported type with valuation \(M\) entirely to report \(1\), and every other supported type entirely to report \(0\). Thus, for each \((h,v)\in S\),

\(x_{h,v,1}=\mu_{h,v}\) if \(v=M\), and \(x_{h,v,1}=0\) otherwise; set \(x_{h,v,0}=\mu_{h,v}-x_{h,v,1}\).

Unsupported types have zero reporting masses.

Verification of feasibility

Mass conservation and normalization

The reporting masses are nonnegative and satisfy \(x_{h,v,0}+x_{h,v,1}=\mu_{h,v}\).

Report \(0\) has zero intensity. The total mass assigned to report \(1\) is \(q\), so

\(\displaystyle \sum_{h,v,b}x_{h,v,b}\lambda_{h,b}=\frac{q}{q}=1.\)

All intensities are finite because \(q>0\).

Incentive constraints

For a type with valuation \(v\), the two menu utilities are

\(u_0(v)=\lambda_{h,0}(v-c_{h,0})=0,\qquad u_1(v)=\lambda_{h,1}(v-c_{h,1})=(v-p)/q.\)

If \(v=M\), then \(M\geq p\), hence \(u_1(M)\geq u_0(M)\). Its assigned report \(1\) satisfies the incentive constraint.

If \(v<M\), the lower-value case defining \(s\) applies. Consequently, \(v\leq s<p\), and therefore \(u_1(v)<0=u_0(v)\). Its assigned report \(0\) satisfies the incentive constraint strictly.

When at least two distinct valuations are supported, the maximum-value types also strictly prefer their assigned report, since \(p<M\). When all valuations equal \(M\), the types are indifferent, and the stipulated permission to assign indifferent types applies.

Ex-post individual rationality

Whenever \(x_{h,v,b}\lambda_{h,b}>0\), necessarily \(b=1\) and \(v=M\). For those winners,

\(c_{h,1}=p\leq M=v.\)

All other types have zero winning intensity. Thus the stated ex-post individual rationality condition holds.

Welfare and exact attainment

For any feasible mechanism, nonnegativity and normalization give

\(\displaystyle M-W=\sum_{h,v,b}x_{h,v,b}\lambda_{h,b}(M-v)\geq 0.\)

Thus \(M\) is an upper bound on feasible welfare.

In the constructed mechanism, every positive allocation weight belongs to a maximum-value type. Hence

\(\displaystyle W=\sum_{(h,M)\in S}\mu_{h,M}\frac{M}{q}=\frac{qM}{q}=M=W_{\mathrm{full}}.\)

Therefore, for every rational \(\varepsilon>0\), the construction satisfies \(W\geq W_{\mathrm{full}}-\varepsilon\). Zero loss is attained; no limiting sequence of intensities is needed.

The required intensity magnitude can nevertheless be large. Indeed, in any exact-welfare mechanism, the nonnegative terms in the displayed expression for \(M-W\) must all vanish. Consequently, types below \(M\) receive no positive allocation weight. Writing \(\Lambda=\max_{h,b}\lambda_{h,b}\), normalization then implies

\(\displaystyle 1=\sum_{(h,M)\in S,b}x_{h,M,b}\lambda_{h,b}\leq \Lambda\sum_{(h,M)\in S,b}x_{h,M,b}=\Lambda q.\)

Thus every exact mechanism has \(\Lambda\geq 1/q\). Our construction attains this bound.

Polynomial-time construction

Scan the supported types to compute \(M\), the largest supported value below \(M\) when present, and \(q\). These operations require polynomially many exact rational comparisons and additions.

To check encoding size explicitly, write the masses contributing to \(q\) as \(a_i/b_i\), and take \(B=\prod_i b_i\). Its binary length is at most the sum of the input denominator lengths, up to a constant. Then

\(\displaystyle q=A/B,\qquad A=\sum_i a_i(B/b_i).\)

Since \(0<q\leq 1\), we have \(0<A\leq B\). Therefore both \(q\) and \(1/q=B/A\) have binary encodings of length \(O(L)\). The midpoint price \(p\) also has encoding length \(O(L)\).

The output consists of the reporting masses for the \(\tau\) supported types and two menus for each of the \(m\) classes. Its size and construction time are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), independently of \(\varepsilon\), and hence polynomial in the requested parameters.

The supplied [finite-bidder auction comparison](https://kylewoodward.com/blog-data/pdfs/references/blumrosen%2Bnisan%2Bsegal-journal-of-artificial-intelligence-research-2007A.pdf) is not needed for this proof. The decisive properties here are positive mass at the largest supported valuation and permission to use the finite intensity \(1/q\).
