The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_a,\mu_b\) with \(\mu_a+\mu_b=1\), two complete additive valuation types over a finite good catalogue \(G\), and \(\tfrac12\) copies of every good per unit population, compute a polynomial-size mass allocation \(\rho_{t,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\subseteq G\) satisfying \(\sum_B\rho_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{B\ni g}\rho_{t,B}=\mu_t/2\), and \(v_t(B)\ge v_t(B'\setminus\{g\})\) for some \(g\in B'\), for every type \(t\) and supported bundles \(B,B'\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

Let \(G\) be a finite set of goods and let \(t\in\{a,b\}\) have rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\mu_a+\mu_b=1\), and an additive valuation \(v_t(B)=\sum_{g\in B}v_t(g)\), given by rational item values. A mass allocation is a polynomial-support family \(\rho_{t,B}\ge 0\) over \(B\subseteq G\) such that \(\sum_B\rho_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{B\ni g}\rho_{t,B}=\mu_t/2\) for every \(t,g\). Whenever \(\rho_{t,B},\rho_{t,B'}>0\), require some \(g\in B'\) with \(v_t(B)\ge v_t(B'\setminus\{g\})\).

Target

Is this mass-allocation problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results

For two discrete agents with additive valuations, a polynomial-time randomized truthful mechanism produces only EF1 allocations and gives each agent each good with marginal probability \(1/2\).
The mass-allocation problem above asks for a distribution over bundles for each valuation type, rather than a lottery over allocations to two discrete agents.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

The answer depends on whether “valuation” implicitly requires nonnegative item values. For arbitrary rational item values, as explicitly stated, the problem is NP-hard. If all item values are nonnegative, it is polynomial-time solvable. Both statements admit short proofs.

The requirement that a witnessing good exist also matters: as written, an empty bundle cannot have positive mass.

1. Preliminary observations. Fix a type \(t\) with \(\mu_t>0\), and define \(p_{t,B}=\rho_{t,B}/\mu_t\). The constraints become a probability distribution over bundles satisfying
\[ \Pr(g\in B)=\frac12 \]
for every good \(g\), together with the stated condition between every pair of supported bundles. The two types have no constraints coupling their distributions, so they can be treated independently.

A supported bundle cannot be empty: taking \(B'=B=\varnothing\) makes the required existential statement false. Consequently, instances with fewer than two goods are infeasible. Indeed, with no goods the only bundle is empty; with one good, its inclusion probability forces the empty bundle to have probability \(1/2\). At least one type has positive mass because the masses sum to one.

2. NP-hardness for unrestricted rational values. We reduce from PARTITION. Its input is a list of positive integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\), encoded in binary; the question is whether some subset has sum \(W/2\), where \(W=\sum_i w_i\).

Construct goods \(g_1,\ldots,g_n,z_1,z_2\). Give both types mass \(1/2\) and identical item values
\[ v_t(g_i)=-w_i,\qquad v_t(z_1)=v_t(z_2)=0. \]
The construction has polynomial encoding length.

Suppose a feasible mass allocation exists. Consider either type and any two supported bundles \(B,B'\). All item values are nonpositive. Thus the required witness \(g\in B'\) satisfies
\[ v_t(B)\ge v_t(B'\setminus\{g\}) =v_t(B')-v_t(g)\ge v_t(B'). \]
Applying the condition in the reverse direction gives equality. Therefore all supported bundles have the same value, say \(c\).

The marginal constraints determine this common value:
\[ c=\mathbb E[v_t(B)] =\sum_{g\in G}v_t(g)\Pr(g\in B) =-\frac W2. \]
Hence any supported bundle contains a subset of the goods \(g_i\) whose corresponding weights sum to \(W/2\). The PARTITION instance is a yes-instance.

Conversely, suppose \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}w_i=W/2\). Define complementary bundles
\[ A=\{g_i:i\in I\}\cup\{z_1\}, \qquad C=\{g_i:i\notin I\}\cup\{z_2\}. \]
For each type, set \(\rho_{t,A}=\rho_{t,C}=\mu_t/2\), with all other masses zero. Every good has the required marginal mass. Both bundles have value \(-W/2\). For every ordered pair of supported bundles, including a bundle paired with itself, deleting the zero-valued good from the second bundle witnesses the required inequality.

This proves the equivalence and establishes NP-hardness, even with identical types, equal masses, and support of size two in every constructed yes-instance.

3. Polynomial-time construction for nonnegative values. Now assume \(v_t(g)\ge0\) for every type and good, and that there are at least two goods.

Fix a positive-mass type and suppress its subscript. We construct a partition \(G=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}C\) into nonempty bundles satisfying the required condition in both directions.

Initially put one distinct good in each bundle. Process the remaining goods in any order, assigning each to a bundle whose current value is smaller, breaking ties arbitrarily.

For a nonempty bundle \(X\), write
\[ s_X=v(X),\qquad M_X=\max_{g\in X}v(g). \]
We prove that throughout the procedure,
\[ s_A-s_C\le M_A, \qquad s_C-s_A\le M_C. \]
Initially each bundle is a singleton, so these inequalities follow from nonnegativity.

For the induction step, suppose \(s_A\le s_C\), and a good of value \(x\ge0\) is added to \(A\). If \(s_A+x\le s_C\), then
\[ s_C-(s_A+x)\le s_C-s_A\le M_C, \]
while the reverse difference is nonpositive. If \(s_A+x\ge s_C\), then
\[ (s_A+x)-s_C\le x\le\max(M_A,x), \]
while the reverse difference is nonpositive. These are exactly the two required inequalities after the update. The case of adding to \(C\) is symmetric.

At termination, deleting a maximum-value good from \(C\) gives
\[ v(C\setminus\{g\})=s_C-M_C\le s_A=v(A). \]
The reverse condition follows identically. For a bundle paired with itself, deleting any of its goods cannot increase its value, by nonnegativity.

Give each of \(A,C\) probability \(1/2\), or equivalently mass \(\mu_t/2\). Since they partition the goods, every marginal constraint holds. Perform this construction independently for each positive-mass type; zero-mass types require no supported bundles.

The algorithm uses polynomially many exact rational operations, with polynomial bit complexity, and outputs at most two bundles per type. Together with the infeasibility check for fewer than two goods, it completely solves the nonnegative case.

Initialization. With at least two goods, the two initial bundles are nonempty. For singleton values \(a,c\ge0\), the proposed invariants reduce to \(a-c\le a\) and \(c-a\le c\), both valid.

These cases exhaust all possibilities, including equality. The symmetric update is justified by exchanging the bundle names.
