The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational piecewise linear convex \(V:[0,1]\to\mathbb{Q}\) with \(V(0)=0\), choose \(q\in L^1([0,1])\cap L^2([0,1])\) to minimize \(\frac12\int_0^1 x^2q(x)^2\,dx\). Set \(p(x)=V'(x)-xq(x)\) and \(R(u)=p(u)+\int_u^1q(y)\,dy\); require \(R(u)\geq\frac{1}{1-u}\int_u^1R(y)\,dy\) for almost every \(u\in[0,1)\). Characterize the optimizer and determine how it can be represented from the finite input \(V\).

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). For every input satisfying the stated hypotheses, the infimum is attained by a unique admissible transfer, up to almost-everywhere equality:

\[ q_*(x)=\frac{xV'(x)-V(x)}{x^2} =\left(\frac{V(x)}x\right)' \quad\text{almost everywhere on }(0,1). \]

This transfer makes total payment independent of entry time:

\[ R_*(u)=V(1) \quad\text{almost everywhere}. \]

The minimum cost is rational and can be computed exactly in polynomial time. The proof below establishes optimality directly for the continuous problem.

1. Notation and exact representation

Write the breakpoints as

\[ 0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_m=1, \]

and let \(s_i\) be the slope of \(V\) on \((t_{i-1},t_i)\). Convexity means

\[ s_1\leq s_2\leq\cdots\leq s_m. \]

For each internal breakpoint, define the nonnegative slope increment

\[ \Delta_j=s_{j+1}-s_j, \qquad 1\leq j<m. \]

Since \(V(0)=0\),

\[ V(x)=s_1x+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\Delta_j(x-t_j)_+. \]

Define

\[ C_i=\sum_{j<i}t_j\Delta_j, \qquad d_i=\sum_{j=i}^{m-1}(1-t_j)\Delta_j. \]

Empty sums are zero, so \(C_1=0\) and \(d_m=0\). On the \(i\)-th open interval,

\[ V(x)=s_ix-C_i, \]

and consequently

\[ V(1)-V(x)-(1-x)V'(x) =V(1)-s_i+C_i =d_i. \]

All these quantities are rational and determined by the finite input.

2. Rewriting the no-delay constraint

For an admissible \(q\), put

\[ Q(x)=\int_x^1q(y)\,dy, \qquad f(x)=xq(x). \]

Here \(Q\) is bounded because \(q\in L^1\), and \(f\in L^2\) because \(q\in L^2\). Moreover,

\[ R(u)=V'(u)-f(u)+Q(u) \]

is integrable.

Fubini’s theorem applies to the tail-integral term: its absolute double integral is bounded by \(\int_0^1|q(y)|\,dy\). Therefore,

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_u^1R(y)\,dy &=V(1)-V(u)-\int_u^1yq(y)\,dy +\int_u^1(y-u)q(y)\,dy\\ &=V(1)-V(u)-uQ(u). \end{aligned} \]

Multiplying the no-delay inequality by \(1-u\) and rearranging gives the equivalent condition

\[ Q(u)-(1-u)f(u) \geq V(1)-V(u)-(1-u)V'(u). \]

Thus, if \(d(x)=d_i\) on \((t_{i-1},t_i)\), feasibility is exactly

\[ Q(x)-(1-x)f(x)\geq d(x) \quad\text{almost everywhere}. \tag{1} \]

Convexity ensures \(d(x)\geq0\).

3. An admissible candidate with constant total payment

Define

\[ q_*(x)=\frac{C_i}{x^2} \quad\text{for }x\in(t_{i-1},t_i). \tag{2} \]

Endpoint and breakpoint values may be assigned arbitrarily.

When \(m=1\), this is the zero transfer. When \(m\geq2\), it vanishes on the first interval, and every remaining interval has \(x\geq t_1>0\). Hence \(q_*\) is bounded and belongs to \(L^1\cap L^2\).

The function \(V(x)/x\), extended at zero by \(s_1\), is continuous and piecewise continuously differentiable with bounded derivative. It is therefore absolutely continuous. Its derivative is precisely (2), so

\[ Q_*(x)=\int_x^1q_*(y)\,dy =V(1)-\frac{V(x)}x. \]

Also,

\[ p_*(x)=V'(x)-xq_*(x)=\frac{V(x)}x. \]

Consequently,

\[ R_*(x)=p_*(x)+Q_*(x)=V(1) \quad\text{almost everywhere}. \]

In particular, \(q_*\) is feasible, with equality in every no-delay constraint. Equivalently, writing \(f_*(x)=xq_*(x)\),

\[ Q_*(x)-(1-x)f_*(x)=d(x). \tag{3} \]

4. A nonnegative multiplier certificate proves optimality

Define, almost everywhere,

\[ \lambda(x)=\frac{d(x)}{(1-x)^2}, \qquad \Lambda(x)=\int_0^x\lambda(u)\,du. \]

Because \(d_m=0\), the function \(\lambda\) vanishes on the final interval. Thus \(\lambda\) is bounded. Convexity gives \(\lambda\geq0\).

We first prove the identity

\[ \Lambda(x)=C_i+d_i\frac{x}{1-x} \quad\text{on }(t_{i-1},t_i). \tag{4} \]

The derivative of the right-hand side is \(d_i/(1-x)^2=\lambda(x)\). At an internal breakpoint \(t_i\), its jump is

\[ (C_{i+1}-C_i) +(d_{i+1}-d_i)\frac{t_i}{1-t_i} =t_i\Delta_i-(1-t_i)\Delta_i\frac{t_i}{1-t_i} =0. \]

Its limit at zero is zero. These derivative, continuity, and initial-value properties prove (4). It follows that

\[ \frac{\Lambda(x)}x-(1-x)\lambda(x) =\frac{C_i}{x} =f_*(x). \tag{5} \]

For any admissible \(q\), Fubini now yields

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^1\lambda(x)\bigl[Q(x)-(1-x)f(x)\bigr]\,dx &=\int_0^1f(x) \left[\frac{\Lambda(x)}x-(1-x)\lambda(x)\right]\,dx\\ &=\int_0^1f(x)f_*(x)\,dx. \end{aligned} \tag{6} \]

To check absolute integrability in this application, let \(M=\|\lambda\|_\infty\). The absolute double integral is at most

\[ \int_0^1 |q(y)|\int_0^y|\lambda(x)|\,dx\,dy \leq M\int_0^1y|q(y)|\,dy<\infty. \]

For feasible \(q\), subtracting (6) for \(q_*\) and using (1), (3), and \(\lambda\geq0\), gives

\[ \int_0^1 f_*(x)\bigl(f(x)-f_*(x)\bigr)\,dx = \int_0^1\lambda(x) \bigl[Q(x)-(1-x)f(x)-d(x)\bigr]\,dx \geq0. \]

Expanding the square therefore proves

\[ \begin{aligned} J(q)-J(q_*) &=\frac12\int_0^1(f-f_*)^2\,dx +\int_0^1f_*(f-f_*)\,dx\\ &\geq\frac12\int_0^1x^2(q-q_*)^2\,dx. \end{aligned} \tag{7} \]

This establishes global optimality. If another feasible transfer has the same cost, (7) forces \(x(q-q_*)=0\) almost everywhere. Since \(x>0\) almost everywhere, it forces \(q=q_*\) almost everywhere.

Thus the infimum is always attained and its optimizer is unique.

5. Exact minimum and polynomial-time decision

Integrating (2) gives

\[ \boxed{ J_*(V)= \frac12\sum_{i=2}^{m} C_i^2 \left(\frac1{t_{i-1}}-\frac1{t_i}\right), \qquad C_i=\sum_{j<i}t_j(s_{j+1}-s_j). } \tag{8} \]

For \(m=1\), the sum is empty and the minimum is zero.

An exact algorithm is:

  • Compute the rational slopes from the breakpoint and vertex data.
  • Compute the coefficients successively using

\[ C_1=0,\qquad C_{i+1}=C_i+t_i(s_{i+1}-s_i). \]

  • Evaluate the rational expression (8).
  • Compare it with \(B\) by exact rational arithmetic.

This requires \(O(m)\) rational arithmetic operations. Each slope and slope increment has bit length \(O(L)\). Successive coefficient sums have bit length \(O(mL)\), and a conservative bound for intermediate cost sums is \(O(m^2L)\). Standard integer arithmetic therefore makes the total running time polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\), hence in \(m,\tau,L\), with \(\tau=1\).

The optimizer has an exact finite representation consisting of the rational breakpoints and rational coefficients \(C_i\), with the formula \(q_*(x)=C_i/x^2\) on each interval.

As checks, affine \(V\) gives \(q_*=0\). For a single kink,

\[ V(x)=ax+\Delta(x-t)_+, \qquad \Delta\geq0, \]

the optimizer and minimum are

\[ q_*(x)= \begin{cases} 0,&0<x<t,\\ t\Delta/x^2,&t<x<1, \end{cases} \qquad J_*(V)=\frac12\Delta^2t(1-t). \]
