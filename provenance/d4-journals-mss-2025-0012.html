The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) alternatives and two distributions \(\mu,\nu\) over rankings in \(S_n\), each specified by its positive-support rankings and binary rational masses, with each support a Condorcet domain, decide whether there is a relabeling \(\pi\in S_n\) such that \(\nu(\pi\cdot\rho)=\mu(\rho)\) for every ranking \(\rho\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\geq 1\) be the number of alternatives, labelled by \([m]\). Rankings are strict linear orders, represented by elements of \(S_m\).
A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Masses sum to \(1\). Support size counts listed types, not all \(m!\) possible rankings.
Input consists of two such societies \(\mu,\nu\). Write \(\tau_\mu,\tau_\nu\) for their support sizes, \(\tau=\max\{\tau_\mu,\tau_\nu\}\), and \(L\) for the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Both supports are promised to be Condorcet domains: for every triple of alternatives, some alternative never occupies some position among first, middle, and last in the rankings restricted to that triple.
For \(\rho=(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\), define \(\pi\cdot\rho=(\pi(a_1),\ldots,\pi(a_m))\). Unlisted rankings have mass zero. Decide whether some \(\pi\in S_m\) satisfies \(\nu(\pi\cdot\rho)=\mu(\rho)\) for every \(\rho\in S_m\).
Is this mass-preserving relabeling problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
Known discrete result: relabeling isomorphism of two equally sized, unweighted Condorcet domains is decidable in time polynomial in their cardinality and the number of alternatives. This result does not state a complexity classification for distributions with rational masses.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Renamed the number of alternatives from n to m and allowed every positive integer m, since the anchor imposes no lower bound beyond positivity.
  • Made the distribution normalization explicit: each society has total mass one, and unlisted rankings have mass zero.
  • Defined the relabeling action as applying the same permutation to alternative labels while preserving ranking positions.
  • Used separate support sizes for the two societies and their maximum as the shared running-time parameter; L is the total input bit length.
  • Treated the Condorcet-domain requirement as an input promise, without requiring maximality, equal support sizes, or inclusion of the identity ranking.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, even without the Condorcet-domain promise. The crucial fact is that choosing the image of one full ranking uniquely determines the relabeling.

A ranking determines a candidate relabeling

Let \(D_\mu,D_\nu\subseteq S_m\) be the two supports. Fix any ranking \(\rho^*=(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\in D_\mu\); such a ranking exists because the masses sum to \(1\).

For each \(\sigma=(b_1,\ldots,b_m)\in D_\nu\), define \(\pi_\sigma\) by
\(\pi_\sigma(a_i)=b_i\) for every \(i\in[m]\).

Both lists contain every alternative exactly once, so \(\pi_\sigma\) is a permutation. Moreover, it is the unique permutation satisfying \(\pi_\sigma\cdot\rho^*=\sigma\): equality of the ordered lists forces every equation \(\pi_\sigma(a_i)=b_i\).

Consequently, any mass-preserving relabeling must be one of these at most \(\tau_\nu\) candidates. Indeed, positivity of \(\mu(\rho^*)\) forces its image to belong to \(D_\nu\).

Algorithm

First reject if \(\tau_\mu\ne\tau_\nu\). This rejection is valid because a mass-preserving relabeling maps one support bijectively onto the other.

Otherwise:

  • Choose \(\rho^*\in D_\mu\).
  • For each \(\sigma\in D_\nu\), construct \(\pi_\sigma\) as above.
  • For every \(\rho\in D_\mu\), form \(\eta=\pi_\sigma\cdot\rho\). Search the target support for \(\eta\), and reject this candidate if \(\eta\) is absent or its mass differs from \(\mu(\rho)\).
  • Accept if some candidate passes every check; otherwise reject.

All mass comparisons are exact. For positive denominators, the binary rationals \(p/q\) and \(r/s\) are equal precisely when \(ps=rq\), so no floating-point arithmetic or expansion into repeated rankings is needed.

Correctness

Completeness. Suppose a valid relabeling \(\pi\) exists. Then \(\sigma=\pi\cdot\rho^*\) belongs to \(D_\nu\). By uniqueness of the permutation taking \(\rho^*\) to \(\sigma\), the algorithm’s candidate \(\pi_\sigma\) equals \(\pi\). Every support and mass check therefore passes.

Soundness. Suppose a candidate \(\pi_\sigma\) passes. For every \(\rho\in D_\mu\), the algorithm has verified both \(\pi_\sigma\cdot\rho\in D_\nu\) and \(\nu(\pi_\sigma\cdot\rho)=\mu(\rho)\).

The action of a permutation on rankings is bijective, since applying its inverse reverses the action. Thus \(\pi_\sigma\cdot D_\mu\) has cardinality \(\tau_\mu\). It is contained in \(D_\nu\), whose cardinality is also \(\tau_\mu\), so \(\pi_\sigma\cdot D_\mu=D_\nu\).

Now let \(\rho\notin D_\mu\). If \(\pi_\sigma\cdot\rho\) belonged to \(D_\nu=\pi_\sigma\cdot D_\mu\), injectivity would imply \(\rho\in D_\mu\), a contradiction. Hence both \(\mu(\rho)\) and \(\nu(\pi_\sigma\cdot\rho)\) are zero. The required equality therefore holds for every ranking in \(S_m\).

Running time

An elementary implementation searches the target support by scanning its listed rankings. There are at most \(\tau\) candidates, at most \(\tau\) source rankings per candidate, and at most \(\tau\) target rankings per search.

Each ranking has \(m\) entries. Each rational’s encoding has at most \(L\) bits, and schoolbook multiplication compares two rational masses in \(O(L^2)\) bit operations. Including candidate construction, ranking transformations, comparisons, and elementary data access, a conservative bound for this implementation is \(O(\tau^3(m+L)^2)\) bit operations. This is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The argument applies to arbitrary supports of strict full rankings. Thus the rational masses introduce no hardness here, and the stated discrete Condorcet-domain result is unnecessary for this problem.

The prover’s argument is correct and fully resolves the task. It gives a polynomial-time algorithm for arbitrary explicitly listed supports of strict full rankings, so the Condorcet-domain promise is unnecessary.

  • Existence of an anchor ranking. Since the listed masses are positive and sum to \(1\), \(D_\mu\) is nonempty. Choosing \(\rho^*\in D_\mu\) is therefore valid.
  • Candidate construction and exhaustion. For each \(\sigma=(b_1,\ldots,b_m)\in D_\nu\), the equations \(\pi_\sigma(a_i)=b_i\) define a permutation: each alternative appears exactly once in each ranking. They also determine that permutation uniquely. Any valid relabeling must send \(\rho^*\) to a ranking of positive target mass, hence to some member of \(D_\nu\). Consequently, the candidate list contains every possible valid relabeling.
  • Support-size rejection. A permutation acts bijectively on \(S_m\). The required mass equality implies that a ranking has positive source mass exactly when its image has positive target mass. Thus a valid relabeling maps the supports bijectively, and rejecting unequal support sizes is justified.
  • Exact mass comparison. With positive denominators, \(p/q=r/s\) is equivalent to \(ps=rq\). This remains valid for unreduced fractions. Binary integer multiplication supplies an exact polynomial-time comparison; there is no dependence on the numerical magnitude of a denominator beyond its bit length.
  • Completeness. Given a valid relabeling \(\pi\), its image \(\sigma=\pi\cdot\rho^*\) occurs in the target support. Uniqueness gives \(\pi_\sigma=\pi\), so that candidate passes every check. This establishes completeness without additional assumptions.
  • Soundness on listed and unlisted rankings. A passing candidate verifies the required equality on every source-support ranking. Its support image is contained in \(D_\nu\); injectivity and equal finite cardinalities make this containment an equality. The prover then correctly uses injectivity to show that every unlisted source ranking has an unlisted image. Both masses are zero there, establishing the required equality on all of \(S_m\).
  • Running time. The three nested scans involve at most \(\tau^3\) ranking comparisons. Alternative labels require \(O(\log(m+1))\) bits each, so a ranking comparison costs \(O(m\log(m+1))\) bit operations. Rational comparisons cost \(O(L^2)\) using schoolbook multiplication. These costs, candidate construction, and ranking transformations fit the stated conservative bound \(O(\tau^3(m+L)^2)\). Scanning and elementary data access do not invalidate that bound for the explicitly listed input.
  • Edge cases and scope. The reasoning includes \(m=1\) and singleton supports. It requires neither a Condorcet-domain theorem nor any unstated structural property of the supports. The algorithm addresses exactly the mass-preserving relabeling condition in the task.
