The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational group masses \(\lambda_1+\lambda_2=1\), a finite-support rational distribution \(\nu\) over utility-preference pairs \((u,\sigma)\), a bias factor \(\beta\in(0,1]\), and per-capita capacities \(\kappa_\ell\) with \(\kappa=\sum_\ell\kappa_\ell\le1\), compute the stable mass allocation \(x_{g,u,\sigma,\ell}\). Group \(g\)'s type mass is \(\lambda_g\nu_{u,\sigma}\), its observed utility is \(u\) for \(g=1\) and \(\beta u\) for \(g=2\), and its capacity at institution \(\ell\) is \(\lambda_g\kappa_\ell\); process types by observed utility, using a shared tie rule and splitting mass at capacity boundaries. Compute the utility ratio against the maximum true utility obtainable from total mass \(\kappa\), and the ratios of the groups’ per-capita selection rates and first-choice rates.

1. An exact allocation algorithm.

Let \(t_1,\ldots,t_\tau\) be the types sorted by decreasing \(u_t\), breaking ties by their support-list order. Because \(\beta>0\), multiplying utilities by \(\beta\) preserves both strict comparisons and ties. Thus both groups use this same order.

First compute a normalized allocation \(a_{t,\ell}\), using type masses \(\nu_t\) and institution capacities \(\kappa_\ell\):

  • Initialize \(c_\ell=\kappa_\ell\) and every \(a_{t,\ell}=0\).
  • For each type \(t_i\), initialize its remaining mass \(s=\nu_{t_i}\).
  • Visit the institutions in the order \(\sigma_{t_i}(1),\ldots,\sigma_{t_i}(m)\). At institution \(\ell\), set \(d=\min\{s,c_\ell\}\), assign \(a_{t_i,\ell}=d\), and update \(s\leftarrow s-d\) and \(c_\ell\leftarrow c_\ell-d\).
  • Leave any remaining \(s\) unassigned.

Return \(x_{g,t,\ell}=\lambda_g a_{t,\ell}\).

To verify this formula, compare the normalized run with group \(g\)'s prescribed run. Initially, every group capacity is \(\lambda_g\) times its normalized counterpart, and each incoming type mass has the same scaling. At every assignment step,

\(\min\{\lambda_g s,\lambda_g c_\ell\}=\lambda_g\min\{s,c_\ell\}\).

Subtraction preserves this scaling. Induction over types and their ranking positions therefore proves \(x_{g,t,\ell}=\lambda_g a_{t,\ell}\) for every entry. Hence the algorithm computes exactly the allocation specified in the question.

2. Total assigned mass and representation ratio.

Write \(y_i=\sum_\ell a_{t_i,\ell}\), \(N_0=0\), and \(N_i=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\nu_{t_j}\).

When a type starts, let \(C=\sum_\ell c_\ell\) be the total remaining capacity. Its ranking lists every institution, and every institution is acceptable. Consequently, it receives exactly \(\min\{\nu_{t_i},C\}\) mass: if mass remains after its entire ranking is scanned, every institution must have zero remaining capacity.

It follows inductively that the total assigned mass after processing the first \(i\) types is \(\min\{N_i,\kappa\}\). In particular,

\(y_i=\min\{\nu_{t_i},\max\{\kappa-N_{i-1},0\}\}\).

Since \(N_\tau=1\) and \(\kappa\leq1\), we have \(\sum_i y_i=\kappa\). Thus, for each group,

\(\rho_g=\lambda_g^{-1}\sum_{t,\ell}x_{g,t,\ell}=\sum_{t,\ell}a_{t,\ell}=\kappa\).

Because \(\kappa>0\), the representation ratio is well-defined and \(R=1\).

3. First-choice ratio and its positive denominator.

Both quantities inside this minimum are positive by hypothesis. Hence \(p>0\), and \(P=p/p=1\).

4. Utility optimality.

The vector \(y=(y_1,\ldots,y_\tau)\) above is feasible for the optimization defining \(U^\star\): each \(y_i\) lies between \(0\) and \(\nu_{t_i}\), and its total is \(\kappa\).

We prove it is optimal directly. Let \(r\) be the smallest index with \(N_r\geq\kappa\), which exists because \(N_\tau=1\geq\kappa\). Then

\(y_i=\nu_{t_i}\) for \(i<r\), \(y_r=\kappa-N_{r-1}\), and \(y_i=0\) for \(i>r\).

Let \(z\) be any feasible vector for \(U^\star\), indexed in the same type order, and put \(h=u_{t_r}\). Because \(\sum_i(y_i-z_i)=0\),

\(\sum_i u_{t_i}(y_i-z_i)=\sum_i(u_{t_i}-h)(y_i-z_i)\).

Every summand on the right is nonnegative:

  • For \(i<r\), \(u_{t_i}\geq h\) and \(y_i-z_i=\nu_{t_i}-z_i\geq0\).
  • For \(i=r\), the utility coefficient is zero.
  • For \(i>r\), \(u_{t_i}\leq h\) and \(y_i-z_i=-z_i\leq0\).

Therefore \(\sum_i u_{t_i}y_i\geq\sum_i u_{t_i}z_i\), proving \(U^\star=\sum_i u_{t_i}y_i\).

\(\sum_{g,t,\ell}u_t x_{g,t,\ell}=(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)\sum_{t,\ell}u_t a_{t,\ell}=\sum_i u_{t_i}y_i=U^\star\).

Finally, \(\sum_t\nu_tu_t>0\), together with nonnegative utilities and positive masses, implies \(u_{t_1}>0\). Since \(y_1=\min\{\nu_{t_1},\kappa\}>0\), we have \(U^\star>0\). Thus \(U\) is well-defined and \(U=1\).
