Given a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over locations \(z_t\in\mathbb R^2\), with positive binary-rational masses summing to one, define each rotated coordinate median as the lower weighted median: the smallest projected value whose cumulative mass is at least \(1/2\). Let \(m_\theta\) be the facility location formed from the two medians at uniformly random orientation \(\theta\), and let \(C_p(\mu;y)=\left(\sum_t\mu_t\|z_t-y\|_2^p\right)^{1/p}\). Exact value

For every \(p\in(1,2)\),

\(\displaystyle \boxed{\alpha_p^{\mathrm{mass}}=\frac{2^{\,2-1/p}}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}\bigl(\cos^p\theta+\sin^p\theta\bigr)^{1/p}\,\mathrm d\theta.}\)

The upper bound follows by exact replication from the finite-profile theorem given in the question. The lower bound already holds with three distinct locations and dyadic masses, so the conclusion covers both interpretations of “binary-rational”: arbitrary rationals encoded in binary and rationals with finite binary expansions.

1. Upper bound by exact replication

Write the proposed constant as \(A_p\).

We use the stated finite-profile theorem in the following precise form: for every finite list \(X=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\) of planar locations, allowing repetitions, and every measurable coordinate-median selection \(M_\Theta\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_\Theta\!\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^N\|x_i-M_\Theta\|_2^p\right)^{1/p}\right]\le A_p\min_y\left(\sum_{i=1}^N\|x_i-y\|_2^p\right)^{1/p}.\)

These hypotheses, including repeated locations and arbitrary measurable median selections, are explicit in the [supplied finite-profile result](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18152v1).

Fix an admissible society. Since its masses are rational, choose a positive integer \(N\) such that \(n_t=N\mu_t\) is a positive integer for every \(t\). Then \(\sum_t n_t=N\). Form \(X\) by including \(n_t\) copies of \(z_t\).

For every unit vector \(v\) and real number \(a\),

\(\displaystyle \#\{i:\langle x_i,v\rangle\le a\}=N\sum_{t:\langle z_t,v\rangle\le a}\mu_t.\)

Consequently, \(q_\mu(v)\) is exactly the \(\lceil N/2\rceil\)-th order statistic of the replicated projections: their lower median. Thus the replicated mechanism returns precisely \(m_\theta\) for every angle, including projection ties.

We also check measurability. For each pair of distinct support locations, equality of their projections along either rotating axis occurs at finitely many angles. Outside the union of these finite sets, each projection ordering is fixed on each remaining interval. The selected median there is the projection of a fixed support location. Hence \(m_\theta\) is piecewise continuous and measurable.

For every facility \(y\), replication gives

\(\displaystyle \left(\sum_{i=1}^N\|x_i-y\|_2^p\right)^{1/p}=N^{1/p}C_p(\mu;y).\)

Taking minima gives the same scaling for the optimum. Applying the finite-profile theorem and cancelling \(N^{1/p}\) therefore yields

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_\Theta[C_p(\mu;m_\Theta)]\le A_p\operatorname{OPT}_p(\mu).\)

All hypotheses have been checked: the profile is finite and planar, repetitions are permitted, the selected coordinates are medians, and the selection is measurable. It follows that \(\alpha_p^{\mathrm{mass}}\le A_p\).

2. A three-point dyadic family

For each integer \(k\ge1\), define

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon_k=2^{-4k},\qquad R_k=2^k,\qquad w_k=\frac{1-\varepsilon_k}{2}.\)

Place masses \(w_k,w_k,\varepsilon_k\) at the distinct points

\(\displaystyle A=(-1,0),\qquad B=(1,0),\qquad T_k=(0,R_k),\)

respectively. Denote this society by \(\mu^{(k)}\). Every mass is positive and dyadic, and their sum is one.

Every individual mass is strictly below \(1/2\), whereas every pair has total mass strictly above \(1/2\):

\(\displaystyle 2w_k=1-\varepsilon_k>\frac12,\qquad w_k+\varepsilon_k=\frac{1+\varepsilon_k}{2}>\frac12.\)

It follows that, along every axis, the weighted lower median equals the middle value of the three projected locations. This remains true when projections coincide: the mass strictly below the middle value is less than \(1/2\), and the mass at or below it exceeds \(1/2\).

3. The optimal cost tends to one

Define

\(\displaystyle g(y)=\|A-y\|_2^p+\|B-y\|_2^p.\)

The function \(g\) is convex: the Euclidean norm is convex, and the power function is convex and increasing on nonnegative arguments for \(p>1\). Also \(g(-y)=g(y)\), since \(B=-A\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle g(0)\le\frac{g(y)+g(-y)}2=g(y).\)

Since \(g(0)=2\), the two endpoint masses contribute at least \(2w_k=1-\varepsilon_k\) to the cost raised to power \(p\), at every facility. Evaluating the full objective at the origin gives

\(\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon_k\le\operatorname{OPT}_p(\mu^{(k)})^p\le1-\varepsilon_k+\varepsilon_kR_k^p.\)

Here

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon_kR_k^p=2^{-(4-p)k}\longrightarrow0.\)

Both bounds tend to one, so \(\operatorname{OPT}_p(\mu^{(k)})\to1\). In particular, every member of the family has positive optimum.

4. The expected mechanism cost tends to the claimed constant

Let \(m_{k,\theta}\) be the mechanism output for this family. Define

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{clip}(x,\ell,h)=\min\{h,\max\{\ell,x\}\}.\)

The middle value of two interval endpoints and a third value is the third value clipped to that interval. Thus the coordinates of \(m_{k,\theta}\) are

\(\displaystyle a_{k,\theta}=\operatorname{clip}(R_k\sin\theta,-|\cos\theta|,|\cos\theta|),\)

\(\displaystyle b_{k,\theta}=\operatorname{clip}(R_k\cos\theta,-|\sin\theta|,|\sin\theta|).\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \|m_{k,\theta}\|_2^2=a_{k,\theta}^2+b_{k,\theta}^2\le\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1.\)

Fix \(\theta\in(0,\pi/2)\). As \(R_k\to\infty\), both outlier projections eventually exceed their respective upper endpoints. Hence, for all sufficiently large \(k\),

\(\displaystyle m_{k,\theta}=\cos\theta\,e_\theta+\sin\theta\,f_\theta=(\cos2\theta,\sin2\theta).\)

Its distances from \(A\) and \(B\) are respectively \(2\cos\theta\) and \(2\sin\theta\). Meanwhile, uniformly in the angle, the outlier contribution satisfies

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon_k\|T_k-m_{k,\theta}\|_2^p\le\varepsilon_k(R_k+1)^p\le2^p\,2^{-(4-p)k}\longrightarrow0.\)

Since \(w_k\to1/2\), we obtain, for every interior first-quadrant angle,

\(\displaystyle C_p(\mu^{(k)};m_{k,\theta})\longrightarrow2^{\,1-1/p}\bigl(\cos^p\theta+\sin^p\theta\bigr)^{1/p}.\)

To justify angular averaging, first observe that the scalar middle-value operation commutes with negation. Thus, for this family, \(q_{\mu^{(k)}}(-v)=-q_{\mu^{(k)}}(v)\). Using \(e_{\theta+\pi/2}=f_\theta\) and \(f_{\theta+\pi/2}=-e_\theta\) shows that \(m_{k,\theta+\pi/2}=m_{k,\theta}\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_\Theta[C_p(\mu^{(k)};m_{k,\Theta})]=\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}C_p(\mu^{(k)};m_{k,\theta})\,\mathrm d\theta.\)

Finally, both endpoint distances are at most two, so

\(\displaystyle C_p(\mu^{(k)};m_{k,\theta})^p\le(1-\varepsilon_k)2^p+\varepsilon_k(R_k+1)^p\le2^{p+1}.\)

This supplies a constant integrable bound independent of \(k\). Dominated convergence now gives

\(\displaystyle \lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb E_\Theta[C_p(\mu^{(k)};m_{k,\Theta})]=A_p.\)

Together with \(\operatorname{OPT}_p(\mu^{(k)})\to1\), this proves \(\alpha_p^{\mathrm{mass}}\ge A_p\). Combined with Section 1, it establishes the asserted equality.
