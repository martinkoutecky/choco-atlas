Theorem 2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support rational distribution \(\mu_0\) over initial opinions in \(\mathbb{Q}^d\) and a common confidence radius \(\epsilon>0\), define each round by moving all mass at each support point \(x\) to \(g_{\mu_k}(x)=\frac{\sum_{y:\|x-y\|_2\le\epsilon}\mu_k(y)y}{\sum_{y:\|x-y\|_2\le\epsilon}\mu_k(y)}\), combining masses at coincident points. Compute the least \(t_*\) such that \(\mu_{t_*}=\mu_{t_*+1}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=d\geq1\) be the opinion dimension. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) distinct types \(x_i\in\mathbb Q^d\), each with positive binary rational mass \(w_i\), where \(\sum_iw_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also contains a common binary rational confidence radius \(\epsilon>0\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; measure running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Starting from \(\mu_0=\sum_iw_i\delta_{x_i}\), update synchronously at integer rounds. For \(S_k=\operatorname{supp}(\mu_k)\), define
\(g_{\mu_k}(x)=\frac{\sum_{y\in S_k:\|x-y\|_2\leq\epsilon}\mu_k(y)y}{\sum_{y\in S_k:\|x-y\|_2\leq\epsilon}\mu_k(y)}\), and \(\mu_{k+1}=\sum_{x\in S_k}\mu_k(x)\delta_{g_{\mu_k}(x)}\).
Thus each point includes its own mass, the confidence boundary is included, and coincident images have their masses added. All updates and equality comparisons are exact.
Compute, in binary, \(t_* = \min\{k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}:\mu_k=\mu_{k+1}\}\).

Is computing \(t_*\) solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For \(n\) explicitly listed equal-weight agents, synchronous homogeneous dynamics terminate within \(n^8+n\) rounds, independently of dimension ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/1412.6546)).

Status and new partial result

I do not settle the general polynomial-time versus $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness question. I prove a further bound that depends on the smallest mass, rather than on a common denominator.

Let
$$ \alpha=\min_{1\leq i\leq\tau}w_i. $$

Theorem. The dynamics terminate, and
$$ t_*\leq \tau-1+ \left\lfloor\frac{\tau(\tau-1)^2}{16\alpha^4}\right\rfloor. $$
This bound is independent of the opinion dimension, the confidence radius, and the initial coordinates. It holds even for arbitrary positive real masses.

For the rational inputs in the task, this yields an exact algorithm whose running time is polynomial in
$$ m,\tau,L,\left\lceil\frac1\alpha\right\rceil. $$
Consequently, exact computation is polynomial-time whenever the minimum mass is bounded below by the reciprocal of a fixed polynomial in $m,\tau,L$, even if the common mass denominator is exponentially large.

All results used below are stated and justified here.

1. A weighted energy inequality

Consider any current society
$$ \mu=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_i\delta_{z_i}, $$
where the $z_i$ are distinct, the masses $v_i$ are positive, and $\sum_i v_i=1$. Define
$$ N_i=\{j:\|z_i-z_j\|_2\leq\epsilon\}, \qquad H_i=\sum_{j\in N_i}v_j. $$
Its updated positions and displacements are
$$ y_i=\frac{\sum_{j\in N_i}v_jz_j}{H_i}, \qquad \Delta_i=y_i-z_i. $$

Define the energy intrinsically on the measure by
$$ \mathcal E(\mu) =\sum_{i,j=1}^{r}v_iv_j \min\{\|z_i-z_j\|_2^2,\epsilon^2\}. $$
It satisfies
$$ 0\leq\mathcal E(\mu)\leq\epsilon^2. $$

Lemma 1. If $\mu'$ is the updated measure, then
$$ \mathcal E(\mu)-\mathcal E(\mu') \geq 4\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_i^2\|\Delta_i\|_2^2 + 2\sum_{\substack{i<j\\j\in N_i}} v_iv_j\|\Delta_i+\Delta_j\|_2^2. $$
In particular,
$$ \mathcal E(\mu)-\mathcal E(\mu') \geq4\sum_i v_i^2\|\Delta_i\|_2^2. $$

Proof. Freeze the current confidence graph and put
$$ b_{ij}= \begin{cases} v_iv_j,&j\in N_i,\\ 0,&j\notin N_i. \end{cases} $$
The matrix $(b_{ij})$ is symmetric, $b_{ii}=v_i^2$, and
$$ \sum_jb_{ij}=v_iH_i. $$
For a tuple of positions $u=(u_1,\ldots,u_r)$, write
$$ Q(u)=\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}\|u_i-u_j\|_2^2. $$

For a current confidence edge, the contribution to $\mathcal E(\mu)$ is its squared distance. For a current nonedge, it is $\epsilon^2$. At the next round, each truncated contribution is at most the corresponding frozen expression. Therefore
$$ \mathcal E(\mu)-\mathcal E(\mu') \geq Q(z)-Q(y). $$
This remains valid if updated positions coincide: grouping coincident positions multiplies their summed masses and gives exactly the same double sum.

The update rule gives
$$ \sum_jb_{ij}(z_i-z_j)=-v_iH_i\Delta_i. $$
Expanding $Q(y)-Q(z)$ and using symmetry yields
$$ Q(y)-Q(z) = 4\sum_i\Delta_i\cdot \sum_jb_{ij}(z_i-z_j) + \sum_{i,j}b_{ij}\|\Delta_i-\Delta_j\|_2^2. $$
Consequently,
$$ Q(z)-Q(y) = 2\sum_i v_iH_i\|\Delta_i\|_2^2 + 2\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}\Delta_i\cdot\Delta_j. $$
Separating the diagonal terms and grouping each unordered edge gives
$$ Q(z)-Q(y) = 4\sum_i v_i^2\|\Delta_i\|_2^2 + 2\sum_{\substack{i<j\\j\in N_i}} v_iv_j\|\Delta_i+\Delta_j\|_2^2. $$
Every term on the right is nonnegative. This proves the lemma. $\square$

Stationarity, including equality of measures

Corollary 2. A society is stationary if and only if every two distinct support points are at distance strictly greater than $\epsilon$.

Proof. If this separation condition holds, every support point averages only itself.

Conversely, suppose $\mu=\mu'$. Their energies are equal, so Lemma 1 and positivity of all masses imply $\Delta_i=0$ for every $i$. Taking scalar products of the update identities with $z_i$ and summing gives
$$ \sum_i v_iH_i z_i\cdot\Delta_i = -\sum_{\substack{i<j\\j\in N_i}} v_iv_j\|z_i-z_j\|_2^2. $$
The left side is zero. Every summand on the right is nonnegative before its minus sign, so every confidence edge joins coincident positions. Since current support points are distinct, no edge can join two different support points. $\square$

Thus equality of consecutive measures cannot conceal a nontrivial permutation of moving positions.

In particular, for $\tau=1$, we have $t_*=0$. For $\tau=2$, we have $t_*=0$ if the distance exceeds $\epsilon$, and $t_*=1$ otherwise. The remaining argument addresses arbitrary support size.

2. A quantitative decrease for a noncomplete component

Form the undirected confidence graph on the current support, with an edge between distinct points at distance at most $\epsilon$.

Lemma 3. Suppose every current mass is at least $\alpha>0$. If a connected component $C$ has $s$ vertices and diameter greater than $\epsilon$, then
$$ \mathcal E(\mu)-\mathcal E(\mu') \geq \frac{16\alpha^4\epsilon^2}{s(s-1)^2}. $$

Proof. Choose $p,q\in C$ with $\|z_p-z_q\|_2>\epsilon$, and define the unit vector
$$ e=\frac{z_p-z_q}{\|z_p-z_q\|_2}. $$
For $i\in C$, put
$$ u_i=e\cdot z_i, \qquad R=\max_{i\in C}u_i-\min_{i\in C}u_i, \qquad c=\frac{\max_{i\in C}u_i+\min_{i\in C}u_i}{2}. $$
Then
$$ R\geq u_p-u_q=\|z_p-z_q\|_2>\epsilon, \qquad |u_i-c|\leq\frac R2. $$

Define the scalar graph energy
$$ F=\sum_{\substack{i<j\\i,j\in C\\j\in N_i}} v_iv_j(u_i-u_j)^2. $$
Connectivity supplies a simple path between vertices attaining the minimum and maximum of $u_i$. If it has $\ell$ edges, then $\ell\leq s-1$. Cauchy–Schwarz along this path gives
$$ R^2\leq \ell\sum_{\text{path edges }\{i,j\}}(u_i-u_j)^2. $$
Since every edge weight $v_iv_j$ is at least $\alpha^2$,
$$ F\geq\frac{\alpha^2R^2}{s-1}. $$

Write $\delta_i=e\cdot\Delta_i$. No confidence edge leaves $C$, so the projected update equations give
$$ v_iH_i\delta_i =\sum_{j\in N_i}v_iv_j(u_j-u_i). $$
Multiplying by $u_i-c$, summing over $C$, and grouping unordered edges yields
$$ F=-\sum_{i\in C}v_iH_i(u_i-c)\delta_i. $$
Another application of Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$ F\leq \left(\sum_{i\in C}v_i^2\delta_i^2\right)^{1/2} \left(\sum_{i\in C}H_i^2(u_i-c)^2\right)^{1/2}. $$
Because $H_i\leq1$ and $|\delta_i|\leq\|\Delta_i\|_2$, this implies
$$ F\leq \left(\sum_{i\in C}v_i^2\|\Delta_i\|_2^2\right)^{1/2} \frac{\sqrt{s}R}{2}. $$
Combining the lower and upper bounds for $F$ and cancelling $R>0$ gives
$$ \sum_{i\in C}v_i^2\|\Delta_i\|_2^2 \geq \frac{4\alpha^4R^2}{s(s-1)^2}. $$
Lemma 1 now implies
$$ \mathcal E(\mu)-\mathcal E(\mu') \geq \frac{16\alpha^4R^2}{s(s-1)^2} \geq \frac{16\alpha^4\epsilon^2}{s(s-1)^2}. $$
$\square$

3. The termination bound

Theorem 4. Starting from $\tau$ support points with minimum mass $\alpha$, the dynamics terminate and satisfy
$$ t_*\leq \tau-1+ \left\lfloor\frac{\tau(\tau-1)^2}{16\alpha^4}\right\rfloor. $$

Proof. Support size never increases: the next support is the image of the current support under the averaging map. Moreover, every current atom carries the sum of the masses of a nonempty group of original atoms. Hence every current mass remains at least $\alpha$.

Call an update a *merging update* if it strictly reduces support size. There are at most $\tau-1$ such updates.

Consider a nonstationary update that is not a merging update. By Corollary 2, its confidence graph has a component containing at least two vertices. Such a component cannot be complete: in a complete component, every point averages exactly the whole component and all its positions coincide after the update, causing a merger.

Thus this update has a connected component of size $s\leq\tau$ with diameter greater than $\epsilon$. For $\tau\geq2$, Lemma 3 shows that its energy decrease is at least
$$ \eta=\frac{16\alpha^4\epsilon^2}{\tau(\tau-1)^2}. $$
All other updates also have nonnegative energy decrease. Since the initial energy is at most $\epsilon^2$ and energy is always nonnegative, the number of nonstationary updates without a merger is at most
$$ \left\lfloor\frac{\epsilon^2}{\eta}\right\rfloor = \left\lfloor\frac{\tau(\tau-1)^2}{16\alpha^4}\right\rfloor. $$

These bounds apply to every finite prefix of nonstationary updates. An infinite sequence of such updates would contradict their bounded total number. Therefore a stationary round exists.

There are exactly $t_*$ nonstationary updates before the first stationary round. Adding the bounds for the two kinds of updates proves the result. For $\tau=1$, stationarity is immediate and the displayed bound equals zero. $\square$

This proof requires neither rational masses nor replication into equal-weight agents.

4. Exact computation and binary bit complexity

Let
$$ U=\left\lceil\frac1\alpha\right\rceil. $$

Proposition 5. For the inputs in the task, exact round-by-round computation of $t_*$ takes time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,U$.

Proof. Write the masses in reduced form as $w_i=p_i/q_i$, and compute
$$ N=\operatorname{lcm}(q_1,\ldots,q_\tau), \qquad a_i=Nw_i. $$
The $a_i$ are positive integers, $\sum_i a_i=N$, and
$$ \log_2N\leq\sum_i\log_2q_i\leq L. $$
These quantities are computable in polynomial time.

Retain the original $\tau$ labels and their fixed integer masses $a_i$, allowing label positions $z_i(k)$ to coincide. Define
$$ A_i(k)= \sum_{\substack{j\\\|z_i(k)-z_j(k)\|_2\leq\epsilon}}a_j. $$
Then
$$ z_i(k+1)= \frac{ \sum_{\substack{j\\\|z_i(k)-z_j(k)\|_2\leq\epsilon}} a_jz_j(k) }{A_i(k)}. $$
Coincident labels have identical neighborhoods and updates. Therefore these labeled updates implement exactly the required aggregation of masses.

Let $Q_0$ be the product of the denominators of all input coordinates. All initial coordinates have denominator $Q_0$, with $\log_2Q_0\leq L$. Recursively use the common denominator
$$ Q_{k+1}=Q_k\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}A_i(k). $$
Because $1\leq A_i(k)\leq N$, every updated coordinate can be represented with this denominator, and
$$ \log_2Q_k\leq L+k\tau\log_2N. $$

Every position remains in the convex hull of the initial positions. Each coordinate thus has absolute value at most $2^L$, so its numerator with denominator $Q_k$ has bit length
$$ O\bigl(L+k\tau\log_2(N+1)\bigr). $$
Intermediate arithmetic has polynomially bounded bit length in the same parameters.

Confidence tests use the exact rational comparison
$$ \sum_{h=1}^{m} \bigl(z_{i,h}(k)-z_{j,h}(k)\bigr)^2 \leq\epsilon^2. $$
Clearing positive denominators gives an integer comparison, including equality at the confidence boundary. Coordinate equality tests and updates also use exact integer arithmetic.

At each round, halt if every pair of labels at distinct positions is farther apart than $\epsilon$. By Corollary 2, this is precisely the stationarity condition. Testing before updating returns the required first stationary index.

Theorem 4 bounds the number of updates by a polynomial in $\tau,U$. Consequently, both the number of arithmetic operations and their operand bit lengths are polynomial in $m,\tau,L,U$. $\square$

Consequences

For any fixed polynomial $p$, the promise
$$ \alpha\geq\frac1{p(m,\tau,L)} $$
makes the algorithm polynomial-time.

This condition permits exponentially large common denominators. For example, for $b\geq3$, the three masses
$$ w_1=\frac14+2^{-b}, \qquad w_2=\frac14, \qquad w_3=\frac12-2^{-b} $$
have minimum mass $\alpha=1/4$ and least common denominator $2^b$. Nevertheless, Theorem 4 gives
$$ t_*\leq2+\frac{3\cdot2^2}{16(1/4)^4}=194 $$
for every allowed choice of positions and radius.

For arbitrary inputs, $\alpha\geq2^{-L}$, so $U\leq2^L+1$. Proposition 5 therefore also gives a general exact algorithm with running time
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\,2^{O(L)}. $$
Since $\tau\alpha\leq1$, we have $\tau\leq U$. The termination bound then gives
$$ t_*\leq U+\frac{U^7}{16}, $$
so the binary output has $O(L)$ bits.

5. Exponential duration still occurs

For completeness, the slow example from the notes can be proved directly.

Take $m=1$, $\epsilon=1$, and positions $-1,0,1$. For an integer $b\geq2$, put
$$ r=2^{-b}, \qquad w_1=w_3=\frac{1-r}{2}, \qquad w_2=r. $$
The minimum mass is $\alpha=r$, and the input length is $L=\Theta(b)$.

Set
$$ q=\frac{1-r}{1+r}. $$
As long as the outer points are not neighbors, symmetry keeps the middle point at zero. Each outer point averages itself and the midpoint, multiplying its position by $q$. Thus the positions are
$$ -q^k,\quad0,\quad q^k $$
until
$$ K=\min\{k\geq0:q^k\leq1/2\}. $$
The midpoint edges persist because $q^k\leq1$. At round $K$, all three points are mutual neighbors and merge at zero in the next update. Before that update, the outer positions strictly contract. Hence
$$ t_*=K+1. $$

For the lower bound, put
$$ u=\frac{2r}{1+r}, \qquad k_0=2^{b-2}=\frac1{4r}. $$
Here $q=1-u$ and $0<u<1$. Bernoulli’s inequality gives
$$ q^{k_0}\geq1-k_0u =1-\frac1{2(1+r)} >\frac12. $$
Therefore $K>2^{b-2}$.

For the upper bound, take $B=2^b=1/r$. Another application of Bernoulli’s inequality gives
$$ q^{-B} = \left(1+\frac{2r}{1-r}\right)^B \geq1+\frac2{1-r}>3. $$
Thus $q^B<1/2$, so $K\leq2^b$. We obtain
$$ 2^{b-2}+1<t_*\leq2^b+1. $$
In particular, $t_*=2^{\Theta(L)}$ with only three support points.

The slow example itself is computable in polynomial time

The following known theorem is applicable: for fixed numbers of factors, two products of positive integer powers can be compared, including equality, in time polynomial in the binary lengths of their bases and exponents. This is unconditional. [Etessami–Stewart–Yannakakis, Proposition 3.7(2)](https://arxiv.org/html/1304.5429)

For the present example,
$$ q=\frac{2^b-1}{2^b+1}, $$
and, for $k\geq1$,
$$ q^k\leq\frac12 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2(2^b-1)^k\leq(2^b+1)^k. $$
The left side has two factors and the right side has one. All bases have $O(b)$ bits. Binary search for $K$ in the proved interval $[1,2^b]$ uses $O(b)$ comparisons, each with an $O(b)$-bit exponent. The cited theorem therefore gives polynomial total running time.

Thus even a family with exponential duration can have polynomial-time exact computation of its termination round.

The new bound establishes polynomial-time exact computation for all inputs with inverse-polynomial minimum mass, extending the denominator-based sufficient condition.

It does not handle arbitrary exponentially small masses in polynomial time. I have proved neither a general polynomial-time algorithm nor an $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness reduction. Resolving the original question requires one of those additional arguments.
