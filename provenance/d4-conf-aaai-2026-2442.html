Theorem 5.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) candidates and a society \(\mu\) supported on \(\tau\) rankings, with binary rational masses satisfying \(N\mu_\pi\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\), find a randomized mechanism outputting a ranking \(\psi\) that is \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-DP when one person changes type, corresponding to moving mass \(1/N\) between rankings. Can it guarantee \(\mathbb{E}[K_\mu(\psi)]\le (1+\xi)\operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu)+\widetilde O_{\xi,\delta}\!\left(\frac{m^{65/22}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}}{\varepsilon N}\right)\), with runtime polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log N\), where \(K_\mu(\psi)=\sum_\pi\mu_\pi K(\psi,\pi)\)? The pure-DP version asks for additive error \(\widetilde O_\xi\!\left(\frac{m^{27/7}}{\varepsilon N}\right)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m,N\ge 1\) be integers, with \(N\) public and encoded in binary; types and outputs are strict rankings in \(S_m\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) rankings with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_\pi\), satisfying \(\sum_\pi\mu_\pi=1\) and \(N\mu_\pi\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Write \(K\) for Kendall's tau distance, \(K_\mu(\psi)=\sum_\pi\mu_\pi K(\psi,\pi)\), and \(\operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu)=\min_{\psi\in S_m}K_\mu(\psi)\).
Use central differential privacy with public binary rational parameters \(0<\varepsilon\le1\) and \(0<\delta<1/2\). Neighboring societies have the same \(N\) and differ by transferring mass \(1/N\) from one ranking to another; the transfer may change the support. Pure privacy uses \(\delta=0\).
Fix any constant \(\xi>0\). The notation \(\widetilde O_\xi\) suppresses constants depending on \(\xi\) and polylogarithmic factors in \(m,N,1/\varepsilon,1/\delta\), omitting \(1/\delta\) in the pure case.

Can a randomized mechanism running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log N)\) time guarantee, on every such society,
\(\mathbb E[K_\mu(\psi)]\le(1+\xi)\operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu)+\widetilde O_\xi\!\left(\frac{m^{65/22}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}}{\varepsilon N}\right)\) under \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-DP,
and, separately, \(\mathbb E[K_\mu(\psi)]\le(1+\xi)\operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu)+\widetilde O_\xi\!\left(\frac{m^{27/7}}{\varepsilon N}\right)\) under pure \(\varepsilon\)-DP, or is either approximation guarantee computationally unattainable in this runtime?

For an explicit list of \(N\) rankings, [Hillebrand et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11319v1) establish both guarantees in \((Nm)^{O(1)}\) time for every fixed \(\xi>0\).
No result establishing or excluding these guarantees with the stated support-encoded runtime was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize the masses to sum to one, as required by the discrete average-cost objective.
  • Treat N as a public positive integer encoded in binary, fixed across neighboring inputs.
  • Require privacy across all admissible supports, including transfers that introduce or remove a supported ranking.
  • Use strict complete rankings, central privacy, positive epsilon at most one, and approximate delta strictly between zero and one half; pure privacy sets delta to zero.
  • Fix xi as a positive constant, matching the approximation scheme's quantification.
  • Include the binary rational privacy parameters in the total input bit length L and specify the logarithmic factors suppressed by the additive-error notation.

1. Result

Both guarantees are achievable in the stated support-encoded runtime.

The key is to use the explicit-list theorem only when the *effective population* \(\varepsilon N\) is polynomially bounded. In that regime, sampling and privacy amplification reduce the input to a polynomial-size explicit list. For larger \(\varepsilon N\), a mechanism operating directly on pairwise comparison counts suffices.

The proof below also accounts for finite-bit sampling and exact pure privacy.

Fix \(\xi>0\). Write
\(\displaystyle D=\binom m2,\qquad t=\varepsilon N.\)

The case \(m=1\) is immediate, so assume \(m\ge2\). All Kendall costs lie in \([0,D]\).

For distinct candidates \(u,v\), define the integer count
\(\displaystyle c_{uv}=N\sum_\pi\mu_\pi\mathbf1[\pi(u)<\pi(v)]\)
and put \(w_{uv}=c_{uv}/N\). These counts can be computed exactly from the support in polynomial time. They satisfy \(c_{uv}+c_{vu}=N\), and a neighboring transfer changes each count by at most one.

For a weight matrix \(A\), use the convention
\(\displaystyle \operatorname{cost}_A(\psi)=\sum_{\psi(u)<\psi(v)}A_{vu}.\)
Thus \(K_\mu(\psi)=\operatorname{cost}_w(\psi)\).

We use two established results:

  • The explicit-list mechanisms stated in the question, with their expected approximation guarantees. These are [Theorem 5.1 of Hillebrand et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11319v1#S5).
  • A deterministic polynomial-time approximation scheme for nonnegative weighted tournaments whose opposite-edge sums lie between fixed positive constants. In particular, for rational \(A\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle A_{uv}\ge0,\qquad \tfrac12\le A_{uv}+A_{vu}\le2,\)
it returns a ranking of cost at most \((1+\xi)\min_\psi\operatorname{cost}_A(\psi)\), in polynomial time for fixed \(\xi\). Scaling \(A\) by \(1/2\) gives the hypotheses of the [Kenyon-Mathieu–Schudy weighted-tournament PTAS](https://cs.brown.edu/~claire/Publis/kenyonschudy.pdf).

2. Sampling lemmas

Privacy amplification

Let an explicit-list mechanism on \(n\) records be \((\varepsilon_0,\delta_0)\)-DP. Uniformly sample \(n\le N\) distinct labeled individuals, put them in uniformly random order, and apply that mechanism.

The resulting mechanism is
\(\displaystyle \left(\log\!\left(1+\frac nN(e^{\varepsilon_0}-1)\right),\frac nN\delta_0\right)\text{-DP}.\)

Here is a proof under the replacement adjacency required in the question.

Let neighboring populations differ only at one labeled individual, whose record is respectively \(a\) or \(b\). Put \(q=n/N\). When \(n<N\), let \(Q\) be the output law conditional on excluding that individual, and let \(P_a,P_b\) be the laws conditional on including it.

The conditional samples defining \(P_a,P_b\) can be coupled to differ in one coordinate. Likewise, \(P_a\) and \(Q\) can be coupled by replacing the included record \(a\) with a uniformly selected unused unchanged record. Consequently, for every output event, writing \(u=Q(E)\), \(v=P_a(E)\), \(z=P_b(E)\),
\(\displaystyle v\le e^{\varepsilon_0}\min(u,z)+\delta_0.\)

It follows that
\(\displaystyle v-z\le(e^{\varepsilon_0}-1)\min(u,z)+\delta_0.\)
Since \(\min(u,z)\le(1-q)u+qz\), the unconditional laws satisfy
\(\displaystyle (1-q)u+qv \le \bigl(1+q(e^{\varepsilon_0}-1)\bigr)\bigl((1-q)u+qz\bigr)+q\delta_0. \)
Interchanging the populations proves the reverse inequality. If \(n=N\), coupling their uniformly random orderings directly gives the original privacy guarantee.

The labeled population is only a proof device: sampling can be implemented from the integer support multiplicities.

Expected sampling error

Let \(\widehat\mu\) be the empirical society of a uniform sample without replacement of size \(n\), and define
\(\displaystyle \Delta=\max_{\psi\in S_m}|K_{\widehat\mu}(\psi)-K_\mu(\psi)|. \)

For each unordered pair, the empirical comparison frequency has variance
\(\displaystyle \frac{p(1-p)}n\frac{N-n}{N-1}\le\frac1{4n} \)
when \(N>1\). Every ranking selects one comparison cost per unordered pair. Therefore
\(\displaystyle \Delta\le\sum_{u<v}|\widehat w_{uv}-w_{uv}|, \qquad \mathbb E\Delta\le\frac D{2\sqrt n}. \)
When \(n=N\), \(\Delta=0\).

If \(\psi^\ast\) optimizes the original society, then
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[\operatorname{OPT}_K(\widehat\mu)] \le \mathbb E[K_{\widehat\mu}(\psi^\ast)] = \operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu). \)

Hence an explicit-list mechanism with expected guarantee
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[K_{\widehat\mu}(\psi)\mid\widehat\mu] \le(1+\xi)\operatorname{OPT}_K(\widehat\mu)+\beta \)
gives
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[K_\mu(\psi)] \le(1+\xi)\operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu)+\beta+\mathbb E\Delta. \)

This bound allows the output to depend arbitrarily on the sampled society.

3. Polynomially bounded effective population

Let \(H\) be the public integer
\(\displaystyle H= 1+\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil+\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil +\lceil\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\rceil, \)
with \(\lceil\log_2(1/\delta)\rceil\) added in the approximate-private case.

Choose a sufficiently large universal constant \(C\), and put
\(\displaystyle T=Cm^2H^2,\qquad s=\left\lceil m^4(T+1)^2\right\rceil. \)

If \(t<1\), output a fixed ranking. Its cost is at most \(D\), which is absorbed by either requested additive bound.

Suppose now that \(1\le t\le T\). Set
\(\displaystyle n=\min(N,s),\qquad \varepsilon_0=\frac{t}{16n}. \)
Sample \(n\) distinct individuals in uniformly random order and run the appropriate explicit-list mechanism with parameter \(\varepsilon_0\). For approximate privacy, use \(\delta_0=\delta/16\).

The hypotheses of the explicit-list theorem hold: the sample is an explicit list of strict rankings, and \(\varepsilon_0\le1\). Indeed, if \(n=N\), then \(\varepsilon_0=\varepsilon/16\); otherwise \(n=s\ge T\ge t\).

Using \(e^x-1\le2x\) for \(0\le x\le1\), amplification gives privacy parameter at most
\(\displaystyle \frac nN\,2\varepsilon_0=\frac{\varepsilon}{8}. \)
The approximate privacy parameter is at most \(\delta/16\).

Crucially,
\(\displaystyle \varepsilon_0n=\frac{\varepsilon N}{16}.\)
The explicit-list additive bounds therefore become precisely the requested bounds, up to permitted constants and logarithmic factors.

All transformed logarithms are permitted: \(n\le s\), and
\(\displaystyle \varepsilon_0^{-1}\le16s\)
because \(t\ge1\). Moreover, \(\log(1/\delta_0)=O(\log(1/\delta))\).

The extra sampling error is zero if \(N\le s\). Otherwise,
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E\Delta\le\frac D{2\sqrt s}\le1. \)
This constant is absorbed by the requested additive terms: for either exponent \(p\in\{65/22,27/7\}\), the condition \(t\le Cm^2H^2\) implies
\(\displaystyle 1=O\!\left(\frac{m^pH^2}{t}\right). \)

Finally, the sample size and \(\varepsilon_0^{-1}\) are polynomial in \(m,H\). Thus this branch runs in polynomial time in the original input length, including even a polynomial dependence on \(\varepsilon_0^{-1}\) in the explicit-list implementation.

4. Large effective population

Assume \(t>T\). We construct mechanisms using only the counts \(c_{uv}\).

Construction

For every \(u<v\), privately release a noisy estimate \(h_{uv}\) of \(w_{uv}\), using independent noise.

Classify the pair as follows:

  • If \(h_{uv}>7/8\), declare \((u,v)\) strongly oriented.
  • If \(h_{uv}<1/8\), declare \((v,u)\) strongly oriented.
  • Otherwise, declare the pair balanced.

Use fresh independent noise for a second release:

  • For a strongly oriented pair \((u,v)\), release a noisy estimate of \(w_{uv}-w_{vu}\), place it in \(A_{uv}\), and set \(A_{vu}=0\).
  • For a balanced pair with \(u<v\), release a noisy estimate in \(A_{uv}\) of \(w_{uv}\), and set \(A_{vu}=1-A_{uv}\).

If \(A\) satisfies the bounded-tournament conditions from Section 1, run the deterministic PTAS on \(A\). Otherwise output a fixed ranking.

All classifications and the boundedness test are post-processing of private releases.

Pure-private noise and privacy

Put
\(\displaystyle a=\frac{\varepsilon}{32D},\qquad r=1-a.\)
At both stages, add independent integer noise \(Z\) with probability mass function
\(\displaystyle \Pr(Z=k)=\frac{1-r}{1+r}r^{|k|},\qquad k\in\mathbb Z, \)
to the corresponding integer count, then divide by \(N\).

The first vector of integer queries has \(\ell_1\)-sensitivity at most \(D\). Conditional on any first-stage classification, the second vector has \(\ell_1\)-sensitivity at most \(2D\).

A translation by an integer vector \(h\) changes the joint noise likelihood by at most \(r^{-\|h\|_1}\). Since
\(\displaystyle \log(1/r)\le2a,\)
the first release is \(\varepsilon/16\)-DP and the second is \(\varepsilon/8\)-DP. Adaptive composition gives privacy at most \(3\varepsilon/16\).

Approximate-private noise and privacy

For each integer count query, add an independent Gaussian with standard deviation
\(\displaystyle \sigma_c=\frac{16\sqrt D}{\varepsilon} \sqrt{2\log(20/\delta)}, \)
then round the released count to the nearest integer and divide by \(N\).

The first query vector has \(\ell_2\)-sensitivity at most \(\sqrt D\), and the second has sensitivity at most \(2\sqrt D\), uniformly over first-stage classifications.

The Gaussian mechanism therefore makes each stage \((\varepsilon/8,\delta/16)\)-DP. Rounding is post-processing. The composed mechanism is \((\varepsilon/4,\delta/8)\)-DP.

Boundedness with high probability

Let \(\mathcal E\) be the event that every normalized noise at both stages has magnitude at most \(1/32\).

On \(\mathcal E\), a strongly oriented pair satisfies
\(\displaystyle w_{uv}>27/32,\)
so its nonzero noisy weight lies between \(21/32\) and \(33/32\). A balanced pair satisfies
\(\displaystyle 3/32\le w_{uv}\le29/32,\)
so both noisy opposite weights lie between \(1/16\) and \(15/16\), and sum to one.

Thus \(A\) is a bounded nonnegative tournament on \(\mathcal E\).

For geometric noise,
\(\displaystyle \Pr(\mathcal E^c) \le6D\exp\!\left(-\frac{t}{1024D}\right). \)
For rounded Gaussian noise, Gaussian tails give, for a universal positive constant \(c\),
\(\displaystyle \Pr(\mathcal E^c) \le4D\exp\!\left(-\frac{ct^2}{m^2H}\right). \)
Here the rounding error is at most \(1/(2N)\), and \(N\ge t>T\) is sufficiently large.

Because \(H\ge\log_2N\) and \(t>Cm^2H^2\), choosing \(C\) sufficiently large makes both bounds at most \(1/N\).

Expected objective error

Condition on the first-stage classification. Define \(W^0\) by retaining the true weights on balanced pairs and replacing each strongly oriented pair \((u,v)\) by
\(\displaystyle W^0_{uv}=w_{uv}-w_{vu},\qquad W^0_{vu}=0.\)
Also put
\(\displaystyle B=\sum_{\text{strong }(u,v)}w_{vu}\ge0.\)

For every ranking,
\(\displaystyle K_\mu(\psi)=B+\operatorname{cost}_{W^0}(\psi). \)
This is an exact pair-by-pair identity.

Let
\(\displaystyle \Gamma=\max_{\psi\in S_m} |\operatorname{cost}_A(\psi)-\operatorname{cost}_{W^0}(\psi)|. \)
On \(\mathcal E\), the PTAS guarantee and the preceding identity imply
\(\displaystyle K_\mu(\psi) \le(1+\xi)\operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu)+(2+\xi)\Gamma. \)
Indeed, applying the PTAS comparison to an original optimum introduces two objective perturbations; the remaining constant term is \(-\xi B\le0\).

On \(\mathcal E^c\), cost is at most \(D\). Consequently,
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[K_\mu(\psi)] \le(1+\xi)\operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu) +(2+\xi)\mathbb E\Gamma+\frac DN. \)

It remains to bound \(\mathbb E\Gamma\).

For Gaussian noise, each fixed ranking’s unrounded perturbation is a sum of at most \(D\) independent signed Gaussians. A Gaussian maximum bound over the \(2m!\) signed objective perturbations, together with rounding error, gives
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E\Gamma \le\frac{\sigma_c}{N}\sqrt{2D\log(2m!)}+\frac D{2N} = O\!\left( \frac{m^{5/2}\sqrt{\log(m+1)\log(1/\delta)}}{t} +\frac{m^2}{N} \right). \)

For completeness, geometric noise admits an analogous maximum bound. Its moment generating function is
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E e^{zZ} = \frac{a^2}{a^2-2r(\cosh z-1)}. \)
For \(|z|\le a/4\), using \(\cosh z-1\le z^2\) yields
\(\displaystyle \log\mathbb E e^{zZ}\le\frac{4z^2}{a^2}. \)
Conditional on classification, a ranking’s perturbation has coefficients in \(\{0,1,-1\}\). Hence, for \(0<\lambda\le aN/4\),
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E\Gamma \le \frac{\log(2m!)}{\lambda} +\frac{4D\lambda}{a^2N^2}. \)
Optimizing within this interval gives
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E\Gamma = O\!\left( \frac{\sqrt{D\log(2m!)}+\log(2m!)}{aN} \right) = O\!\left(\frac{m^{7/2}\log(m+1)}{t}\right). \)

Thus the large-population branch has additive errors
\(\displaystyle \widetilde O_\xi\!\left( \frac{m^{5/2}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}}{\varepsilon N} \right) \quad\text{and}\quad \widetilde O_\xi\!\left( \frac{m^{7/2}}{\varepsilon N} \right). \)
They are stronger than the requested errors because \(5/2<65/22\) and \(7/2<27/7\).

5. Finite-bit implementation and exact pure privacy

The preceding ideal mechanisms leave privacy slack. We now use that slack to obtain bounded polynomial running time without assuming exact continuous sampling or unbounded rejection sampling.

Sampling from the support

Maintain the remaining integer multiplicities. At each step, select an integer in the interval of remaining population labels and locate its support block.

A uniform integer in an interval of size \(M\le N\) can be approximated by taking a uniform \(b\)-bit integer modulo \(M\). Its total variation error is at most \(M/2^b\).

Thus an entire ordered sample of size at most \(s\) can be approximated within total variation \(\eta\), with
\(\displaystyle b=O(\log N+\log s+\log(1/\eta))\)
bits per step. No expansion of the population occurs.

Sampling the large-branch noise

The geometric and rounded Gaussian distributions can likewise be approximated within any prescribed total variation error \(\eta\) in time polynomial in their parameter bit lengths and \(\log(1/\eta)\).

To see the relevant complexity, truncate their integer support to \([-R,R]\) with omitted probability at most \(\eta/4\). Their tail bounds allow \(\log R\) to be polynomially bounded in the parameter bit lengths and \(\log(1/\eta)\).

Use inverse-CDF sampling on a sufficiently fine dyadic uniform grid, evaluating the CDF to error \(O(\eta/R)\). Binary search takes \(O(\log R)\) evaluations. Errors can affect the coupled inverse samples only when the uniform value lies near one of the \(2R+1\) CDF boundaries; the total probability of those intervals is \(O(\eta)\). Geometric CDFs use rational powers, and Gaussian CDFs admit polynomial-time numerical evaluation to the required precision.

Allocate the error across the \(2D\) draws. This produces a mechanism \(Q_\mu\) whose output law is within total variation \(\eta\) of the ideal law \(P_\mu\), uniformly over societies. Actual released matrices are rational and have polynomial bit length.

Approximate privacy

Choose
\(\displaystyle \eta\le\min(\delta/100,1/N).\)
The ideal mechanism has privacy at most \((\varepsilon/4,\delta/8)\). For every output event,
\(\displaystyle Q_\mu(E) \le e^{\varepsilon/4}Q_{\mu'}(E) +\delta/8+(1+e^{\varepsilon/4})\eta \le e^\varepsilon Q_{\mu'}(E)+\delta. \)
The expected cost increases by at most \(D\eta\le D/N\).

Pure privacy

Total variation approximation alone would not preserve pure privacy. Use the following explicit repair.

Put \(F=m!\). Construct a data-independent distribution \(U\) on rankings satisfying
\(\displaystyle U(\psi)\ge\frac1{2F}\)
for every ranking: take a uniform sufficiently long bit string, reduce its integer value modulo \(F\), and unrank the resulting permutation.

Set
\(\displaystyle \lambda=2^{-\lceil\log_2N\rceil}, \qquad \eta\le\frac{\varepsilon}{100NF}, \qquad R_\mu=(1-\lambda)Q_\mu+\lambda U. \)
The mixture probability \(\lambda\) is dyadic and can be implemented exactly. Also
\(\displaystyle \frac1{2N}\le\lambda\le\frac1N.\)

The ideal mechanism is at most \(\varepsilon/4\)-DP, so
\(\displaystyle Q_\mu(E) \le e^{\varepsilon/4}Q_{\mu'}(E) +(1+e^{\varepsilon/4})\eta. \)

For every nonempty event \(E\), \(U(E)\ge1/(2F)\). Since \(e^\varepsilon-1\ge\varepsilon\),
\(\displaystyle (1+e^{\varepsilon/4})\eta \le(e^\varepsilon-1)\lambda U(E). \)
Substituting into the mixture inequality gives
\(\displaystyle R_\mu(E)\le e^\varepsilon R_{\mu'}(E).\)
The empty event is immediate. Thus \(R\) is exactly pure \(\varepsilon\)-DP.

The repair increases expected cost by at most
\(\displaystyle D\eta+\lambda D=O(D/N).\)
Its precision requirement remains polynomial because
\(\displaystyle \log(1/\eta)=O(\log N+\log(1/\varepsilon)+m\log(m+1)). \)
Only a permutation index is computed; the \(F\) rankings are never enumerated.

The finite-bit implementations and privacy repair also take polynomial time.

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log N)\)
for each fixed \(\xi\).

The sampling error, numerical approximation error, and privacy-repair cost are all absorbed by the permitted logarithmic factors. Therefore, on every valid society, the resulting mechanisms satisfy respectively
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[K_\mu(\psi)] \le (1+\xi)\operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu) +\widetilde O_\xi\!\left( \frac{m^{65/22}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}}{\varepsilon N} \right) \)

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[K_\mu(\psi)] \le (1+\xi)\operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu) +\widetilde O_\xi\!\left( \frac{m^{27/7}}{\varepsilon N} \right) \)
