THEOREM 1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational mass vector \(\mu\), per-capita supply \(s\), capacities \(\lambda_t\), and marginal values \(u_t(1)\ge\cdots\ge u_t(\lambda_t)\), run the alternative ascending auction for each admissible \(N\) with \(N\mu_t\) bidders of type \(t\) and \(Ns\) indivisible units; compute the limit of normalized allocations and clinching payments, and determine whether it equals the mass welfare optimum and the normalized multi-unit Vickrey outcome.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Normalize \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Type \(t\) has capacity \(\lambda_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and explicitly listed private marginal values \(u_t(1)\ge\cdots\ge u_t(\lambda_t)\ge0\), all integers. Let \(m=\max_t\lambda_t\), and let \(U_t\) be the piecewise-linear interpolation of their cumulative sums, with \(U_t(0)=0\). Utilities are quasilinear; there are no budgets or reserve prices. Supply per capita is a positive binary rational \(s\).
For each admissible integer \(N\), create \(N\mu_t\) separate bidders of type \(t\) and supply \(M_N=Ns\) indivisible units. Admissibility requires all these counts to be integers, \(N\ge2\), and \(M_N\ge m\).
The ascending auction uses integer prices \(p=0,1,\ldots\). For bidder \(i\) of type \(t\), write \(Q_i(p)=|\{j:u_t(j)>p\}|\). Sincere bids are \(x_i^0=Q_i(0)\) and \(x_i^p=\min\{x_i^{p-1},\max\{Q_i(p),C_i^{p-1}\}\}\) thereafter.
While aggregate demand exceeds supply, cumulative clinches are \(C_i^p=\max\{0,M_N-\sum_{h\ne i}x_h^p\}\). Close at the first price \(r\) with \(\sum_i x_i^r\le M_N\).
If \(r>0\), give each bidder \(x_i^r\) units and distribute the remaining \(M_N-\sum_i x_i^r\) units by selecting uniformly a subset of that size from the dropped unit slots, of which bidder \(i\) has \(x_i^{r-1}-x_i^r\). Set terminal \(C_i^r=x_i^*\). If \(r=0\), assign \(x_i^*=x_i^0\), leaving excess supply unsold.
With \(C_i^{-1}=0\), payments are \(y_i=\sum_{p=0}^r p(C_i^p-C_i^{p-1})\). Define normalized expected type totals \(A_t^{(N)}=N^{-1}\mathbb E\sum_{i:t(i)=t}x_i^*\) and \(P_t^{(N)}=N^{-1}\mathbb E\sum_{i:t(i)=t}y_i\).
The mass welfare optimum is \(\max\{\sum_t\mu_tU_t(a_t/\mu_t):0\le a_t\le\mu_t\lambda_t,\ \sum_ta_t\le s\}\). For each replica, the truthful multi-unit Vickrey auction uses uniform rationing among tied marginal units and leaves zero-valued units unassigned; normalize its expected type allocation and payment totals in the same way.

Do the allocation and payment limits exist as \(N\to\infty\) through all admissible integers, does the limiting allocation attain the mass welfare optimum, and do both limits equal the normalized Vickrey limits?
Is exact computation of these limits polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Known results: [Ausubel (2004)](https://econ.umd.edu/sites/www.econ.umd.edu/files/pubs/efficient-ascending-auction-aer.pdf) states that sincere bidding yields an efficient Vickrey outcome and an ex post perfect equilibrium in the finite auction.
[Okamoto (2018)](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20160970) disproves the unrestricted ex post perfect equilibrium claim and modifies the rationing procedure; that correction concerns continuation incentives.

Result

All the requested limits exist, the limiting allocation attains the mass welfare optimum, and the allocation and payment limits equal their normalized Vickrey counterparts. Exact computation is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

In fact, the normalized expected outcomes of both auctions are eventually constant along the admissible replicas.

The proof below uses the specified sincere bidding and rationing rules directly. It does not require an equilibrium claim; [Okamoto’s correction](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20160970) concerns the unrestricted ex post perfect equilibrium assertion.

1. Explicit formulas

For every nonnegative integer price \(p\), define

\( q_t(p)=|\{j:u_t(j)>p\}|,\qquad D(p)=\sum_t\mu_tq_t(p). \)

Thus \(ND(p)\) is the aggregate sincere demand in replica \(N\) whenever the clinching constraint does not bind.

Abundant supply. If \(D(0)\le s\), the limits are

\( A_t=\mu_tq_t(0),\qquad P_t=0. \)

Scarce supply. Suppose \(D(0)>s\). Define

\( r=\min\{p\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:D(p)\le s\}. \)

This exists because all marginal values are finite. Put

\( h_t=q_t(r),\qquad e_t=|\{j:u_t(j)=r\}|, \)

and

\( H=\sum_t\mu_th_t=D(r),\qquad T=\sum_t\mu_te_t=D(r-1)-D(r). \)

The last equality uses the integrality of marginal values. By the definition of \(r\),

\( H\le s<H+T, \)

so \(T>0\), and the rational number

\( \alpha=\frac{s-H}{T} \)

satisfies \(0\le\alpha<1\). The limits are

\( \boxed{A_t=\mu_t(h_t+\alpha e_t),\qquad P_t=r\mu_t(h_t+\alpha e_t).} \)

We now prove all these assertions, including the boundary case \(H=s\).

2. Admissible replicas and the ascending-auction limits

Admissible integers are unbounded: every sufficiently large multiple of a common denominator of the masses and supply satisfies the integrality conditions, \(N\ge2\), and \(Ns\ge m\).

If \(D(0)\le s\), every replica closes at price zero. Each bidder of type \(t\) receives \(q_t(0)\) units and pays zero. The abundant-supply formulas therefore hold for every admissible replica.

Now suppose \(D(0)>s\), and define the strictly positive gap

\( \delta=D(r-1)-s. \)

Fix any admissible \(N\) satisfying

\( N\delta\ge m. \)

We prove that the auction closes at \(r\) and that every cumulative clinch before closing is zero.

At price zero, bids are \(q_t(0)\). More generally, suppose that at some price \(p<r\) bids equal \(q_t(p)\). Aggregate demand is \(ND(p)>Ns\), so the auction continues. For bidder \(i\) of type \(t\), its cumulative clinch is

\( C_i^p =\max\{0,Ns-(ND(p)-q_t(p))\} =\max\{0,q_t(p)-N(D(p)-s)\}. \)

Because \(D\) is nonincreasing and \(p\le r-1\),

\( D(p)-s\ge D(r-1)-s=\delta. \)

Consequently,

\( q_t(p)-N(D(p)-s)\le m-N\delta\le0, \)

and hence \(C_i^p=0\).

This proves the zero-clinch assertion at price zero. It also supplies the induction step: if the previous bid is \(q_t(p-1)\) and the previous clinch is zero, the bidding rule gives

\( x_i^p=\min\{q_t(p-1),q_t(p)\}=q_t(p). \)

Thus bids equal \(q_t(p)\) and clinches vanish at every price \(p<r\). At price \(r\), bids equal \(h_t\), and aggregate demand is \(NH\le Ns\). Therefore \(r\) is the actual closing price.

A bidder of type \(t\) drops exactly

\( q_t(r-1)-q_t(r)=e_t \)

slots at closing. Across all bidders, there are \(NT\) dropped slots, and the auction selects exactly \(N(s-H)\) of them.

In a uniformly chosen subset of that size, every slot has inclusion probability

\( \frac{N(s-H)}{NT}=\alpha. \)

Therefore each type-\(t\) bidder receives expected quantity \(h_t+\alpha e_t\), and

\( A_t^{(N)}=\mu_t(h_t+\alpha e_t). \)

All preterminal clinches are zero. The payment rule consequently gives, for every realized allocation,

\( y_i=r x_i^*. \)

It follows that

\( P_t^{(N)}=r\mu_t(h_t+\alpha e_t). \)

These equalities hold for every admissible \(N\) satisfying \(N\delta\ge m\). They prove existence of both limits through all admissible integers, with eventual exact stabilization.

3. Equality with the Vickrey limits

Write \(\widehat A_t^{(N)}\) and \(\widehat P_t^{(N)}\) for the normalized expected Vickrey totals.

3.1. Allocations

Under scarce supply, the replica contains exactly \(NH\) marginal units valued strictly above \(r\), and \(NT\) marginal units valued exactly \(r\). Since

\( NH\le Ns<N(H+T), \)

an efficient allocation assigns all units valued above \(r\), then \(N(s-H)\) units valued exactly \(r\).

To justify this characterization, allocating a lower-valued marginal unit while leaving a higher-valued one unallocated would permit a welfare-improving exchange. Such exchanges respect bidder quantities because each bidder’s marginals are nonincreasing. Ties at \(r\) affect only which bidders receive the remaining units.

The prescribed uniform rationing therefore gives

\( \widehat A_t^{(N)}=\mu_t(h_t+\alpha e_t) \)

for every admissible \(N\) in the scarce-supply case. Although marginal slots within a tied block can be labeled arbitrarily, their selection determines a feasible bidder quantity with the same value.

Under abundant supply, Vickrey assigns every positive-valued marginal unit and leaves zero-valued units unassigned. Hence

\( \widehat A_t^{(N)}=\mu_tq_t(0). \)

3.2. Payments

In the abundant-supply case, every rival receives all its positive-valued units, both with and without bidder \(i\). Removing \(i\) creates no additional rival welfare, so its Vickrey payment is zero.

Consider scarce supply and an admissible \(N\) satisfying \(N\delta\ge m\). Fix a realized Vickrey allocation and a bidder \(i\) of type \(t\). Write

\( q=h_t+k, \)

where \(k\) is the number of its selected marginal units valued \(r\). Let

\( M=Ns,\qquad B=N(s-H). \)

Because only \(B\) tied units are selected altogether, \(k\le B\). The number of rival marginal units valued strictly above \(r\) is \(NH-h_t\), and

\( M-q=NH-h_t+B-k\ge NH-h_t. \)

On the other hand, the number of rival marginal units valued at least \(r\) is

\( N(H+T)-(h_t+e_t) =M+N\delta-(h_t+e_t)\ge M, \)

because \(h_t+e_t\le\lambda_t\le m\).

Order the rivals’ positive marginal values nonincreasingly as \(v_1,v_2,\ldots\). The preceding inequalities imply that every rank from \(M-q+1\) through \(M\) has value exactly \(r\).

For completeness, let \(W_{-i}(z)\) denote the maximum rival welfare with supply at most \(z\). Selecting the highest rival marginals is feasible, after ordering ties consistently with each bidder’s marginal sequence, and gives

\( W_{-i}(z)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{z}v_\ell \)

for the quantities used here.

The rivals’ realized welfare equals \(W_{-i}(M-q)\): otherwise, improving their allocation while holding \(i\)’s quantity fixed would contradict efficiency of the full allocation. The Vickrey externality payment is therefore

\( W_{-i}(M)-W_{-i}(M-q) =\sum_{\ell=M-q+1}^{M}v_\ell =rq. \)

When \(q=0\), this is an empty sum. Taking expectations yields

\( \widehat P_t^{(N)}=r\mu_t(h_t+\alpha e_t). \)

Thus both normalized Vickrey totals equal the ascending-auction totals for every sufficiently large admissible replica. In particular, their limits coincide.

4. Optimality for the mass welfare problem

Under abundant supply, set \(a_t=\mu_tq_t(0)\). This vector is feasible because \(\sum_ta_t=D(0)\le s\). It obtains every positive marginal value of every type. Since all remaining marginals are zero, it maximizes each type’s utility separately and therefore attains the mass welfare optimum.

Now consider scarce supply. We first establish a useful bound. For every type \(t\) and every real \(z\in[0,\lambda_t]\),

\( U_t(z)\le rz+\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_t}\max\{u_t(j)-r,0\}. \)

Indeed, write \(z=\ell+\theta\), where \(\ell\) is an integer and \(0\le\theta<1\), with the endpoint \(z=\lambda_t\) handled by \(\theta=0\). Piecewise-linear interpolation gives

\( U_t(z)-rz =\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(u_t(j)-r)+\theta(u_t(\ell+1)-r). \)

Every coefficient lies between zero and one. Bounding each term by its positive part proves the inequality.

Equality holds whenever \(h_t\le z\le h_t+e_t\): all marginals above \(r\) are included fully, and any additional included marginals equal \(r\).

For any feasible mass allocation \(a\), applying the bound to \(z=a_t/\mu_t\) gives

\( \sum_t\mu_tU_t(a_t/\mu_t) \le r\sum_ta_t+\sum_t\mu_t\sum_j\max\{u_t(j)-r,0\} \le rs+\sum_t\mu_t\sum_j\max\{u_t(j)-r,0\}. \)

The last inequality uses \(r>0\) and \(\sum_ta_t\le s\).

For the limiting allocation,

\( A_t/\mu_t=h_t+\alpha e_t\in[h_t,h_t+e_t], \)

and

\( \sum_tA_t=H+\alpha T=s. \)

Thus both upper bounds are attained with equality. The limiting allocation attains the mass welfare optimum.

5. Exact polynomial-time computation

Let \(K=\sum_t\lambda_t\), so \(K\le m\tau\). These \(K\) marginal values are explicitly present in the input.

First compute \(D(0)\). If \(D(0)\le s\), output the abundant-supply formulas.

Otherwise, sort the distinct positive marginal values in increasing order. For each value \(v\), let

\( w(v)=\sum_t\mu_t|\{j:u_t(j)=v\}|. \)

Starting from \(D(0)\), subtract \(w(v)\) as each value is encountered. The first value for which the remaining demand is at most \(s\) is \(r\). This follows because demand changes only at listed integer marginal values. Then compute \(h_t,e_t,H,T,\alpha\) and the displayed allocation and payment formulas.

Sorting requires at most \(O(K\log K)\) integer comparisons, followed by \(O(K+\tau)\) exact rational operations.

These operations have polynomial bit complexity. A common denominator for the input masses and supply can be chosen as the product of their denominators; its bit length is at most their total input bit length. Weighted sums count at most \(K\) marginal entries, so their numerators have polynomial bit length as well. Forming \(\alpha\), multiplying by a mass, and multiplying by \(r\) preserves polynomial bit length.

The algorithm never enumerates bidders, constructs a replica, or iterates through all integer prices up to the largest value. Hence exact computation is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and indeed polynomial in the explicit input length.
