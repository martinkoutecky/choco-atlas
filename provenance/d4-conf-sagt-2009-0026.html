The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\), \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), rational per-member weights \(a_t\ge 0\), quota \(0\le q\le\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_t\), and subsidy bound \(\Delta\ge 0\), does there exist a nonnegative per-member payoff \(p_t\) for each type such that \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p_t\le 1+\Delta\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}p_t x_t\ge 1\) for every winning mass vector \(x\) with \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}a_t x_t\ge q\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a binary rational per-member weight \(a_t\ge0\); the quota and subsidy bound are binary rationals satisfying \(0<q\le\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_t\) and \(\Delta\ge0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(\tau\) and \(L\) (there is no separate candidate parameter \(m\)).
A coalition is any real mass vector \(x\) satisfying \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\); it has value \(1\) when \(\sum_{t\in T}a_t x_t\ge q\), and value \(0\) otherwise. Thus equality at the quota wins, the empty coalition loses, and the whole society wins.
A payoff assigns a finite real per-member amount \(p_t\ge0\) uniformly within each type. The decision problem asks whether there exists such a payoff with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p_t\le1+\Delta\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}p_t x_t\ge1\) for every winning coalition \(x\).

Is this continuous subsidy-feasibility decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite weighted voting games, deciding whether the cost of stabilizing the grand coalition is at most a given subsidy is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete for binary integer weights and quota, and polynomial-time solvable for unary weights ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/0907.4385)).
No result settling exactly the continuous decision problem above was located in the literature search.

Result

The continuous subsidy-feasibility problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). In fact, its optimal subsidy has an explicit formula.

Define the total weight
\(W=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_t\).
By hypothesis, \(0<q\le W\). The minimum total payoff satisfying all winning-coalition constraints is exactly \(W/q\). Consequently, the instance is feasible if and only if
\[ W\le q(1+\Delta), \]
and the minimum subsidy is \(\Delta_{\min}=W/q-1\).

Proof of the exact optimum

For a payoff vector \(p\), write its total payment as
\(C(p)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p_t\).

Lower bound. Suppose \(p\) satisfies every winning-coalition constraint. Consider the coalition
\(x_t^*=(q/W)\mu_t\) for every \(t\in T\).

Since \(0<q/W\le1\), each coordinate satisfies \(0\le x_t^*\le\mu_t\), so this is an admissible coalition. Its weight is
\[ \sum_{t\in T}a_t x_t^* =\frac qW\sum_{t\in T}a_t\mu_t =q. \]
Equality at the quota wins, so the payoff constraint for this coalition gives
\[ 1\le\sum_{t\in T}p_t x_t^* =\frac qW C(p). \]
Multiplying by the positive number \(W/q\) yields \(C(p)\ge W/q\).

Attainment. Define \(p_t^*=a_t/q\) for every \(t\in T\). These per-member payoffs are finite and nonnegative because \(q>0\) and \(a_t\ge0\). For any winning coalition \(x\),
\[ \sum_{t\in T}p_t^*x_t =\frac1q\sum_{t\in T}a_tx_t \ge1. \]
Thus \(p^*\) satisfies every required constraint, and its total payment is
\[ C(p^*)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\frac{a_t}{q} =\frac Wq. \]

The lower bound is therefore attained. A payoff within the prescribed budget exists exactly when \(W/q\le1+\Delta\), equivalently \(W\le q(1+\Delta)\). Since \(W\ge q\), the stated minimum subsidy is nonnegative.

Exact algorithm and running time

Compute \(W\) using rational arithmetic, then test \(W\le q(1+\Delta)\). If the answer is yes, the formula \(p_t^*=a_t/q\) supplies an explicit feasible payoff.

For completeness, all arithmetic can be performed with polynomially bounded integer lengths. Write each product \(\mu_ta_t\) as \(n_t/d_t\), with a positive denominator, without requiring reduction. The common denominator \(H=\prod_{t\in T}d_t\) has bit length \(O(L)\), because the sum of the input denominator lengths is at most \(L\). The corresponding numerator of \(W\) also has bit length \(O(L)\): indeed, \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\) and nonnegative weights imply \(W\le\max_t a_t\), whose bit length is bounded by the input length. The same bounds hold for intermediate partial sums.

Writing \(W=N/H\), \(q=Q/R\), and \(\Delta=D/S\), with positive denominators, the decision is the integer comparison
\[ NRS\le HQ(S+D). \]
Every integer here has bit length \(O(L)\). Using elementary integer multiplication, sequential rational summation and this comparison take \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{P}\) in the requested parameters.

Why the discrete hardness does not transfer

The cited finite-game hardness theorem concerns coalitions consisting of subsets of indivisible players. [Theorem 4.1 of *The Cost of Stability in Coalitional Games*](https://arxiv.org/html/0907.4385).

The decisive coalition here takes the fraction \(q/W\) of every supported type. Such a coalition is always admissible in the continuous model, whereas a corresponding subset need not exist in a finite game. This continuous coalition forces the lower bound that the proportional payoff attains.
