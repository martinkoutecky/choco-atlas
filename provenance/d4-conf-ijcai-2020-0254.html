The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) candidates, a finite-support society of approval types \(S\subseteq C\) with rational masses \(\mu_S\), and a fractional coalition \(z_S\in[0,\mu_S]\) of mass \(q=\sum_S z_S\), where \(q\ge \ell/m\) and the coalition unanimously agrees on the placement of at least \(\ell\) candidates, what is PRR's exact worst-case ratio of expected average satisfaction to \(\ell\)? In particular, does this ratio equal \(29/32\) over all such societies and coalitions?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge1\) candidates. A society is given by its support \(\mathcal T\subseteq2^C\): the list of its \(\tau\) approval types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_S\), normalized by \(\sum_{S\in\mathcal T}\mu_S=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L,\ell\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length.
PRR independently includes each candidate \(c\) in a committee \(W\subseteq C\) with probability \(p_c=\sum_{S\ni c}\mu_S\); every subset, including the empty and full committees, is allowed.
A fractional coalition has real masses \(0\le z_S\le\mu_S\) and total mass \(q=\sum_S z_S\). For an integer \(1\le\ell\le m\), it is admissible when \(q\ge\ell/m\) and \(\left|\bigcap_{z_S>0}S\right|+\left|\bigcap_{z_S>0}(C\setminus S)\right|\ge\ell\).
Its average satisfaction is \(\operatorname{avs}_W(z)=q^{-1}\sum_S z_S\bigl(|S\cap W|+|(C\setminus S)\cap(C\setminus W)|\bigr)\). Define \(\rho\) as the infimum of \(\mathbb E_{W\sim\mathrm{PRR}}[\operatorname{avs}_W(z)]/\ell\) over all such societies, admissible coalitions, and integers \(m,\ell\).

What is the exact worst-case approximation ratio \(\rho\) of PRR? Does \(\rho=29/32\), or does fractional coalition membership change this ratio?

For finite electorates, PRR guarantees expected average satisfaction at least \((29/32)\ell\) for every qualifying coalition, and this constant is tight. [Source](https://www.rupertfreeman.com/vnw-full.pdf)
No result explicitly treating the fractional-coalition model above was located in the literature search.

Exact ratio

Fractional coalition membership does not change the ratio: \(\rho=29/32\).

We prove the lower bound directly for real coalition masses and give a rational instance attaining equality. The instance also appears in Theorem 6 of the [cited paper](https://www.rupertfreeman.com/vnw-full.pdf).

1. Reduction to coalition mass \(\ell/m\)

Fix a society and an admissible coalition \(z\) of mass \(q\ge\ell/m\). Define
\(z'_S=\frac{\ell}{mq}z_S\).

The scaling factor lies in \((0,1]\), so \(0\le z'_S\le\mu_S\), and the new coalition has mass exactly \(\ell/m\). Its positive support is unchanged, so it remains cohesive. Moreover, for every committee \(W\), scaling both the numerator and denominator in average satisfaction gives
\(\operatorname{avs}_W(z')=\operatorname{avs}_W(z)\).

Consequently, it suffices to consider coalitions with
\(q=\ell/m\in(0,1]\).
This reduction is exact even when the coalition masses are irrational.

2. A bound for each candidate

Fix a candidate \(c\). Choose a stance—approval or disapproval—held by at least half of the coalition, breaking ties arbitrarily. Let \(x\in[1/2,1]\) be the fraction of coalition mass holding this stance.

Let \(s\) be the mass outside the coalition holding the same stance. Here the outside mass of type \(S\) is \(\mu_S-z_S\), so
\(0\le s\le1-q\).

Under PRR, the probability that the decision on \(c\) agrees with the chosen stance is
\(d=qx+s\).
For approval this is the inclusion probability; for disapproval it is the exclusion probability.

Thus the expected contribution of \(c\) to coalition average satisfaction is
\(e_c=xd+(1-x)(1-d)\).
Expanding gives
\(e_c=2qx^2-(1+q)x+1+(2x-1)s\).

Since \(2x-1\ge0\) and \(s\ge0\),
\(e_c\ge F_q(x)\), where
\(F_q(x)=2qx^2-(1+q)x+1\).

We now minimize this quadratic on \([1/2,1]\).

  • If \(0<q\le1/3\), then

\(F'_q(x)=4qx-(1+q)\le3q-1\le0\).
Hence its minimum is \(F_q(1)=q\).

  • If \(1/3\le q\le1\), its minimizer is

\(x_*=(1+q)/(4q)\).
The inequalities \(q\ge1/3\) and \(q\le1\) imply \(x_*\le1\) and \(x_*\ge1/2\), respectively. Therefore its minimum is
\(g(q)=1-\frac{(1+q)^2}{8q}=\frac{6q-q^2-1}{8q}\).

In addition, if the coalition is unanimous on \(c\), then \(x=1\), and the exact expression yields
\(e_c=q+s\ge q\).

These bounds use only the defining inequalities on real masses.

3. The universal lower bound

By linearity of expectation,
\(\mathbb E[\operatorname{avs}_W(z)]=\sum_{c\in C}e_c\).

If \(q\le1/3\), every candidate contributes at least \(q\). Therefore
\(\mathbb E[\operatorname{avs}_W(z)]\ge mq=\ell\).

Now suppose \(q\ge1/3\). Cohesiveness supplies at least \(\ell\) unanimous candidates. Select exactly \(\ell\) of them. Each selected candidate contributes at least \(q\), and each remaining candidate contributes at least \(g(q)\). Thus
\(\mathbb E[\operatorname{avs}_W(z)]\ge\ell q+(m-\ell)g(q)\).

Using \(q=\ell/m\), we obtain
\(\frac{\mathbb E[\operatorname{avs}_W(z)]}{\ell}\ge q+\frac{1-q}{q}g(q)=\frac{9q^3-7q^2+7q-1}{8q^2}\).

The following factorization certifies the desired bound:
\(\frac{9q^3-7q^2+7q-1}{8q^2}-\frac{29}{32}=\frac{(3q-2)^2(4q-1)}{32q^2}\).

For \(q\ge1/3\), the denominator is positive and \(4q-1>0\), so the right-hand side is nonnegative. Consequently, every admissible fractional coalition satisfies
\(\mathbb E[\operatorname{avs}_W(z)]\ge\frac{29}{32}\ell\).
Hence \(\rho\ge29/32\).

4. A rational instance attaining equality

Let \(C=\{c_1,c_2,c_3\}\) and \(\ell=2\). Define the society and coalition by:

| Approval type \(S\) | Society mass \(\mu_S\) | Coalition mass \(z_S\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(\{c_1,c_2,c_3\}\) | \(5/12\) | \(5/12\) |
| \(\{c_1,c_2\}\) | \(1/4\) | \(1/4\) |
| \(\varnothing\) | \(1/3\) | \(0\) |

The society masses are positive rationals summing to one. The coalition has mass \(q=2/3=\ell/m\), unanimously approves \(c_1,c_2\), and disagrees on \(c_3\). It is therefore admissible.

PRR has inclusion probabilities
\(p_{c_1}=p_{c_2}=2/3\) and \(p_{c_3}=5/12\).
Within the coalition, the approval fraction for \(c_3\) is
\((5/12)/(2/3)=5/8\).

Its expected average satisfaction is consequently
\(\mathbb E[\operatorname{avs}_W(z)]=2\cdot\frac23+\frac58\cdot\frac5{12}+\frac38\cdot\frac7{12}=\frac{29}{16}\).

Dividing by \(\ell=2\) gives \(29/32\), proving \(\rho\le29/32\). Together with the universal lower bound, this settles the question.
