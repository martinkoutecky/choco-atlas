The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(τ\) types with positive rational masses \(μ_t\) summing to \(1\) and strictly positive rational valuation vectors \(v_t\) over \(m\) resources, compute per-person bundles \(x_t\in[0,1]^m\) maximizing \(λ\), subject to \(λ\leq\sum_j v_{tj}x_{tj}\) for every type and \(\sum_tμ_t x_{tj}=1\) for every resource. Also compute rational anonymous prices \(p_j\) and per-person type budgets \(b_t=p\cdot x_t\) such that every affordable bundle \(z\in[0,1]^m\) with \(p\cdot z\leq b_t\) satisfies \(v_t\cdot z\leq v_t\cdot x_t\). Is this computable in time polynomial in \(m,τ\), and the input bit length \(L\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq1\) divisible resources, each of supply \(1\), and each type has strictly positive rational additive valuations \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^m\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Compute rational per-person bundles \(x_t\in[0,1]^m\) and the maximum rational \(\lambda\) subject to \(\lambda\leq v_t\cdot x_t\) for every type and \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}=1\) for every resource.
Also compute anonymous rational prices \(p_j>0\) and type-specific per-person token budgets \(b_t=p\cdot x_t\geq0\) supporting the chosen allocation: for every \(t\) and every real bundle \(z\in[0,1]^m\), \(p\cdot z\leq b_t\) implies \(v_t\cdot z\leq v_t\cdot x_t\). Unspent tokens have no utility; any maximizing bundle is acceptable.

Is this exact allocation-and-support computation solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a finite list of agents with strictly positive additive valuations and unit resource supplies, a max-min allocation and supporting anonymous prices and unequal token budgets can be computed in polynomial time. Supporting prices and budgets can also be computed for any given Pareto-optimal allocation in that finite-agent model. These results do not classify the precise mass model stated above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The price domain was unspecified; require strictly positive rational prices, consistent with the finite-agent supporting-price construction, and nonnegative budgets.
  • Require exact rational allocation and objective outputs, matching the rational input and exact computational question.
  • Take the resource set and society support to be nonempty, so m and tau are positive integers.
  • Define L as the total binary input bit length; impose no price normalization because the finite-agent model leaves token scale arbitrary.
  • Allow ties in demand without tie-breaking, since supporting an allocation requires membership in the demand set.

Result

The exact problem as stated is polynomial-time solvable. Its normalization makes the allocation constraints degenerate: every feasible allocation gives every type the full bundle.

The allocation is forced

Fix any resource \(j\). Using the supply equality and the normalization of the masses gives
\[ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t(1-x_{tj}) =\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t-\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t x_{tj} =1-1=0. \]
Each summand is nonnegative because \(\mu_t>0\) and \(x_{tj}\leq1\). A finite sum of nonnegative numbers equals zero only if every summand equals zero. Since every mass is strictly positive, \(x_{tj}=1\) for every type \(t\).

Applying this argument to every resource proves that the unique feasible allocation is \(x_t=(1,\ldots,1)\) for every \(t\). Conversely, this allocation satisfies every supply equality because \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).

Consequently, every feasible objective value satisfies
\[ \lambda\leq\lambda^\star:=\min_{1\leq t\leq\tau}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{tj}. \]
The forced allocation together with \(\lambda=\lambda^\star\) satisfies all utility constraints. Thus \(\lambda^\star\) is the attained maximum and is rational.

Supporting prices and budgets

Choose \(p_j=1\) for every resource and \(b_t=p\cdot x_t=m\) for every type. These prices are anonymous, rational, and strictly positive, and the budgets satisfy the required equality.

For any type \(t\) and any real bundle \(z\in[0,1]^m\),
\[ v_t\cdot x_t-v_t\cdot z =\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{tj}(1-z_j)\geq0, \]
because every valuation is positive and every coordinate of \(z\) is at most \(1\). Therefore the required utility inequality holds for every bundle in the cube, including every bundle satisfying \(p\cdot z\leq b_t\). This proves the supporting property.

Exact computational complexity

The algorithm outputs the all-ones bundles, the all-ones price vector, the budgets \(b_t=m\), and the minimum of the \(\tau\) rational valuation sums.

Computing those sums and comparing them takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). To check bit complexity explicitly, a common denominator for any row sum is the product of its input denominators. Its binary length is at most the sum of their binary lengths, hence is \(O(L)\). The corresponding numerator also has polynomial binary length. Exact integer arithmetic therefore computes and compares all row sums in polynomial time. The remaining outputs have polynomial total length.

This settles every instance under the stated premises. No additional theorem about the finite-agent model is needed.
