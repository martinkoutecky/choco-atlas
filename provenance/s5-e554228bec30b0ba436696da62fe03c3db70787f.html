Lemma 3 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\tau\) ordered outfall types with rational masses \(\mu_i>0\), \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\), rational per-capita bounds and pollution tolerances, attenuation rates, initial pollution, and rational concave quadratic per-firm profits \(\phi_i\), compute an exact optimal per-firm discharge vector \(q\) maximizing normalized total profit subject to the cumulative pollution constraints.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) outfall types ordered upstream to downstream, each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_i\), with \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\). The support size \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types; each type occupies one outfall.
All numerical inputs are binary rationals. Per-firm discharge bounds satisfy \(0\leq a_i\leq u_i<\infty\); initial pollution \(b_0\) and outfall tolerances \(b_i\) are nonnegative; residual pollution factors satisfy \(0<k_i\leq1\).
Per-firm profits are \(\phi_i(z)=\alpha_i z-\beta_i z^2\), with \(\beta_i\geq0\) and \(\alpha_i\geq2\beta_i u_i\), so profits are concave, nondecreasing on \([0,u_i]\), and zero at zero.
For a per-firm vector \(q\), define aggregate pollution by \(p_1=b_0+\mu_1q_1\) and \(p_i=k_{i-1}p_{i-1}+\mu_iq_i\) for \(2\leq i\leq\tau\). Tolerances use the same aggregate units as pollution. Assume \(q=a\) satisfies every tolerance.
Compute an exact maximizer of \(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i\phi_i(q_i)\) over \(q\in\mathbb R^{\tau}\) subject to \(a_i\leq q_i\leq u_i\) and \(p_i\leq b_i\); any maximizer is acceptable. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; there is no independent \(m\) parameter.

Is this exact optimization problem solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many firms with real discharge decisions, an exact layer-by-layer algorithm has a claimed \(\mathcal O(n^3)\) running time under constant-time analytic evaluations; this bound does not account for input bit lengths. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.13300).
No result specifically addressing the mass formulation was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Each supported type occupies a single ordered outfall; its aggregate discharge is its mass times its common per-firm discharge.
  • Pollution tolerances and initial pollution are measured in aggregate units for a society of total mass one.
  • Restored nonnegative finite discharge bounds, nonnegative pollution levels and tolerances, and strictly positive residual factors at most one.
  • Restored zero profit at zero and nondecreasing profits on the physical discharge interval; permitted linear and constant quadratic degeneracies.
  • Made the assumption that minimum production is feasible precise by requiring the lower-bound discharge vector to satisfy all pollution constraints.

Result

The exact optimization problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\). This follows by a polynomial-size reduction to rational convex quadratic programming, for which polynomial-time exact optimization is a classical theorem.

An exact optimizer can be returned as rational coordinates, each represented by a numerator and denominator written in binary.

1. An equivalent convex quadratic program

Introduce pollution variables \(p_1,\ldots,p_\tau\), and let \(x=(q_1,\ldots,q_\tau,p_1,\ldots,p_\tau)\).

Consider minimizing

\(f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i\beta_iq_i^2-\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i\alpha_iq_i\)

subject to

  • \(a_i\leq q_i\leq u_i\) for every \(i\);
  • \(p_i\leq b_i\) for every \(i\);
  • \(p_1-\mu_1q_1=b_0\);
  • \(p_i-k_{i-1}p_{i-1}-\mu_iq_i=0\) for \(2\leq i\leq\tau\).

For every discharge vector \(q\), the last two sets of equations determine exactly the pollution vector prescribed in the question. Consequently, projection onto the discharge coordinates gives a bijection between feasible points of this quadratic program and feasible discharge vectors.

Moreover, \(f(x)\) is the negative of the original profit objective. Thus any minimizer yields an exact maximizer of the required objective.

Writing \(f(x)=\tfrac12x^{\mathsf T}Hx+c^{\mathsf T}x\), its Hessian is

\(H=\operatorname{diag}(2\mu_1\beta_1,\ldots,2\mu_\tau\beta_\tau,0,\ldots,0)\).

Every diagonal entry is nonnegative because \(\mu_i>0\) and \(\beta_i\geq0\). Hence \(H\) is positive semidefinite, and this is a convex quadratic program with rational data.

This argument includes the cases \(\beta_i=0\), where some profit functions are linear.

2. Feasibility, attainment, and encoding size

Feasibility and attainment. The assumed feasible vector \(q=a\), together with its recursively determined pollution vector, supplies a feasible point.

Every feasible discharge coordinate lies in the bounded interval \([a_i,u_i]\). Also, \(p_1=b_0+\mu_1q_1\geq0\). Inductively, \(p_{i-1}\geq0\), \(k_{i-1}>0\), and \(q_i\geq0\) imply \(p_i\geq0\). Therefore every feasible point satisfies \(0\leq p_i\leq b_i\).

The feasible set is consequently bounded. It is closed because its constraints are linear equalities and non-strict inequalities. Being nonempty and compact, it admits a minimizer of the continuous function \(f\).

Size of the reduction. There are \(2\tau\) variables. Replacing each of the \(\tau\) equalities by two inequalities gives \(5\tau\) inequalities in total.

The constraint coefficients are input rationals, their negatives, or constants. The only newly formed objective coefficients are \(2\mu_i\beta_i\) and \(-\mu_i\alpha_i\).

For binary-encoded rational numbers, multiplication adds numerator and denominator bit lengths, up to a constant additive term. Thus these products have bit lengths bounded by the sums of the corresponding input bit lengths. They can be computed by polynomial-time integer arithmetic.

Even with a dense matrix representation, the complete quadratic-program instance has encoding length \(\mathcal O(L+\tau^2)\), and constructing it takes polynomial time. No replication according to masses or denominators is involved.

3. The exact optimization theorem and its application

We use the following classical result.

Exact convex quadratic-programming theorem. Given a rational symmetric positive semidefinite matrix \(H\), a rational vector \(c\), and rational linear constraints \(Ax\leq d\), a deterministic algorithm can compute an exact rational minimizer of \(\tfrac12x^{\mathsf T}Hx+c^{\mathsf T}x\), whenever a minimum exists, in time polynomial in the total binary encoding length.

Kozlov, Tarasov, and Khachiyan prove polynomial-time exact optimization: their definition of exact solution includes both the exact optimal value and a point attaining it. The theorem allows positive semidefinite matrices, including singular ones. See [*The polynomial solvability of convex quadratic programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189), particularly the definition on page 1320 and optimizer recovery on page 1323.

All hypotheses hold here:

  • The constructed objective and constraints have rational coefficients.
  • The Hessian is symmetric positive semidefinite.
  • The feasible set is nonempty.
  • A minimum is attained.
  • The constructed encoding length is polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

Applying the theorem therefore computes an exact optimal vector \(x^\star\) in polynomial time. Returning its first \(\tau\) coordinates gives the required discharge vector \(q^\star\), by the equivalence proved in Section 1.

Neither strict convexity nor strict feasibility is required. Thus zero quadratic coefficients, fixed discharge coordinates, redundant constraints, and tolerances already tight at \(q=a\) cause no exception.
