Status and proved results

The exact value of $R_{\tau_0}$ remains unresolved. I establish the bounds
$$ 1+\sqrt2\le R_{\tau_0}\le3 $$
and a new finite characterization of the optimization over mechanisms.

More precisely, for a fixed population size and a candidate set whose pair sums and pair gaps are all distinct, every anonymous strategyproof mechanism is represented exactly by a finite table of ordinal-type counts. Its optimal bounded-support approximation guarantee is an attained, computable rational number. A limiting argument shows that these candidate sets suffice for the worst-case optimization defining $R_{\tau_0}$.

The finite characterization preserves strategyproofness on profiles of every support size. It does not yet evaluate the resulting limiting optimization.

1. Self-contained bounds

1.1. An admissible family with ratio at most $3$

For a profile of $n$ reports, let $q$ be its $r$th order statistic, where
$$ r=\lceil n/2\rceil. $$
Let $P(t)$ be the unordered pair of candidates closest to $t$, resolving distance ties toward the smaller coordinate. The mechanism outputs $P(q)$, written in increasing order.

This mechanism is anonymous and defined on all real profiles.

To prove strategyproofness, first observe that both sorted coordinates of $P(t)$ are nondecreasing in $t$. Indeed, suppose $s<t$, a candidate $u$ leaves the selected pair, and a candidate $v$ enters it. Selection implies
$$ |s-u|\le |s-v|, \qquad |t-v|\le |t-u|. $$
If $u>v$, these inequalities imply
$$ s\ge\frac{u+v}{2}, \qquad t\le\frac{u+v}{2}, $$
contradicting $s<t$. Thus every departing candidate is smaller than every entering candidate, which proves the assertion.

Write $P(s)=(a_s,b_s)$ and $P(t)=(a_t,b_t)$ in increasing order. The function
$$ \Delta(z)=d_z(P(t))-d_z(P(s)) $$
is nonincreasing: each summand has the form $|z-v|-|z-u|$ with $u\le v$. Since $P(s)$ minimizes individual cost at $s$, $\Delta(s)\ge0$. Therefore every resident at $z\le s$ weakly prefers $P(s)$ to $P(t)$. Similarly, every resident at $z\ge t$ weakly prefers $P(t)$.

Fix the other reports $z_1\le\cdots\le z_{n-1}$ and put $z_0=-\infty$. The selected order statistic as a function of the remaining report is
$$ q(x)=\min\{\max\{x,z_{r-1}\},z_r\}. $$
If a deviation increases $q$, the true position satisfies $x\le q$; if it decreases $q$, the true position satisfies $x\ge q$. The preceding preference implications rule out a profitable deviation in either case. Thus the mechanism is strategyproof on all profiles.

For the approximation bound, define
$$ F(t)=\sum_x\mu_x|x-t|, \qquad D=F(q). $$
At least half the mass lies weakly on each side of $q$. The convex function $F$ consequently has a nonpositive left derivative and a nonnegative right derivative at $q$, so $q$ minimizes $F$.

For an optimal pair $O=(o_1,o_2)$,
$$ 2D\le F(o_1)+F(o_2)=\operatorname{OPT}. $$
The triangle inequality and individual optimality at $q$ give
$$ S_\mu(P(q)) \le2D+d_q(P(q)) \le2D+d_q(O). $$
Also,
$$ d_q(O) \le\sum_x\mu_x\bigl(2|q-x|+d_x(O)\bigr) =2D+\operatorname{OPT}. $$
Combining these inequalities yields
$$ S_\mu(P(q))\le4D+\operatorname{OPT}\le3\operatorname{OPT}. $$
Every denominator is positive, because $d_x(a,b)\ge|a-b|$ and the finite candidate set has positive minimum separation. Hence
$$ R_{\tau_0}\le3. $$

The mechanism can be evaluated from the support in
$$ O\!\left((\tau\log\tau+m)\operatorname{poly}(L+\log n)\right) $$
bit operations, without expanding the population into $n$ reports.

1.2. A two-support lower bound

We first record a consequence of strategyproofness. Suppose reports $u$ and $v$ induce the same strict ordering of outcome classes, with every outcome in one class strictly preferred to every outcome in a lower class. Changing one report from $u$ to $v$ cannot change the outcome class. An improvement would violate strategyproofness at $u$; a deterioration would violate it at $v$ through the reverse deviation.

Take
$$ n=2^s,\quad s\ge2,\qquad \varepsilon=n^{-2},\quad \delta=n^{-1}, $$
and
$$ C=\{-\varepsilon,\varepsilon,2-\varepsilon,2+\varepsilon\}. $$
Classify pairs as $LL$, $LR$, or $RR$ according to their cluster membership. Their costs are:

| Position | $LL$ | $LR$ | $RR$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $0$ | $2\varepsilon$ | $[2,2+2\varepsilon]$ | $4$ |
| $1-\delta$ | $2-2\delta$ | $[2-2\varepsilon,2+2\varepsilon]$ | $2+2\delta$ |
| $1+\delta$ | $2+2\delta$ | $[2-2\varepsilon,2+2\varepsilon]$ | $2-2\delta$ |
| $2$ | $4$ | $[2,2+2\varepsilon]$ | $2\varepsilon$ |

Since $\varepsilon<\delta$ and $\delta+\varepsilon<1$, the strict class ordering is $LL,LR,RR$ at both $0$ and $1-\delta$, and its reverse at both $2$ and $1+\delta$.

Put
$$ \alpha=\sqrt2-1,\qquad k=\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor,\qquad p=k/n, $$
and start with $k$ residents at $0$ and $n-k$ at $2$. Depending on the initial output, perform the indicated sequence of individual report changes:

| Initial class | Changes | Final output cost, or lower bound | Feasible comparison cost |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| $LL$ | All left residents move to $1-\delta$ | $4-2p-2p\delta$ | $2p(1+\delta)+2\varepsilon(1-p)$ |
| $RR$ | All right residents move to $1+\delta$ | $4p+2(1-p)(1-\delta)$ | $2\varepsilon p+2(1-p)(1+\delta)$ |
| $LR$ | All left residents move to $1-\delta$ | $2-2\varepsilon p$ | $2p(1+\delta)+2\varepsilon(1-p)$ |

Class preservation justifies the final output classes. The comparison pairs are respectively $RR$, $LL$, and $RR$. Their costs upper-bound the optimum, so each row gives a valid lower bound on the actual ratio.

Every final society has exactly two support points. Intermediate societies may have three; the stipulated global strategyproofness applies to them.

As $s$ tends to infinity, the three ratio bounds converge respectively to
$$ \frac2\alpha-1=1+2\sqrt2,\qquad \frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}=1+\sqrt2,\qquad \frac1\alpha=1+\sqrt2. $$
Thus, for every admissible family, the supremum in the definition of $R_{\tau_0}$ has limsup at least $1+\sqrt2$. All constructed coordinates and masses are dyadic rationals, and their total encoding length is $O(\log n)$. Consequently,
$$ R_{\tau_0}\ge1+\sqrt2. $$

These numerical bounds agree with the published unrestricted bounds, but the argument above verifies the bounded-support and empirical limiting requirements directly. [Published paper](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/39422/1/1-s2.0-S0304397524005309-main.pdf)

2. Consecutive optimal pairs and an obstruction to an independent construction

2.1. A consecutive optimal pair always exists

Write the candidates as
$$ c_1<\cdots<c_m. $$
The function $F(t)=\sum_x\mu_x|x-t|$ is convex, and the cost of a pair is $F(a)+F(b)$.

Suppose an optimal pair is $(c_i,c_j)$ with $j-i>1$, and choose $i<k<j$. Convexity gives
$$ F(c_k)\le\max\{F(c_i),F(c_j)\}. $$
Replacing the endpoint with the larger $F$ value by $c_k$ does not increase the pair cost and strictly decreases its index separation. Repeating this operation produces an optimal consecutive pair.

Therefore, if candidates are partitioned alternately into
$$ C_{\mathrm{o}}=\{c_1,c_3,\ldots\}, \qquad C_{\mathrm{e}}=\{c_2,c_4,\ldots\}, $$
then
$$ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu,C) = \min_{a\in C_{\mathrm{o}}}F(a) + \min_{b\in C_{\mathrm{e}}}F(b). $$
Indeed, the right side minimizes over a subset of feasible pairs, and that subset contains a consecutive optimal pair.

2.2. Different quantiles in the two sets can still approach ratio $3$

Selecting the nearest site in each fixed candidate set to a fixed order statistic is individually strategyproof. This follows from the clipping argument in Section 1, applied to a single nearest-candidate map. Summing the two strategyproof cost inequalities proves strategyproofness of their independent combination. The disjoint candidate sets ensure distinct facilities.

Nevertheless, this construction does not automatically improve the approximation bound.

For any fixed $\alpha\in(0,1/2)$, consider selecting the nearest odd-indexed candidate to the lower $\alpha$ order statistic and the nearest even-indexed candidate to the upper $1-\alpha$ order statistic. For sufficiently large even $n$, take
$$ C=\{0,1,2,3\}, \qquad \mu=\tfrac12\delta_{1-\eta}+\tfrac12\delta_{2+\eta}, \qquad 0<\eta<\tfrac12. $$
The selected pair is $(0,3)$, with social cost $3$. The optimal pair $(1,2)$ has cost $1+2\eta$. Hence the ratio is
$$ \frac3{1+2\eta}\longrightarrow3. $$
The reverse assignment of the two quantiles has the same obstruction on
$$ C=\{0,1,2,3,4\}, \qquad \mu=\tfrac12\delta_{2-\eta}+\tfrac12\delta_{3+\eta}, $$
where its output is $(1,4)$ and the optimal pair is $(2,3)$.

Thus preserving the optimum through a fixed partition does not by itself yield a matching mechanism.

3. Pairwise preferences have a single crossing

Let $\mathcal A(C)$ be the set of unordered feasible pairs, and put
$$ K=|\mathcal A(C)|=\binom m2. $$
For $A=(a,b)$ with $a<b$, define
$$ u_A=a+b,\qquad w_A=b-a. $$
A direct calculation gives
$$ d_x(A)=\max\{|2x-u_A|,w_A\}. $$

Call $C$ generic if the $K$ pair sums $u_A$ are all distinct and the $K$ pair gaps $w_A$ are all distinct.

Single-crossing lemma

Suppose $u_A<u_B$ and $w_A\ne w_B$. There is exactly one position $t_{AB}$ at which the costs coincide, with
$$ d_x(A)<d_x(B)\quad\text{for }x<t_{AB}, $$
and
$$ d_x(B)<d_x(A)\quad\text{for }x>t_{AB}. $$
Explicitly,
$$ t_{AB}= \begin{cases} \displaystyle \max\left\{\frac{u_A+u_B}{4},\frac{u_A+w_B}{2}\right\}, & w_B>w_A,\\[6pt] \displaystyle \min\left\{\frac{u_A+u_B}{4},\frac{u_B-w_A}{2}\right\}, & w_A>w_B. \end{cases} $$

Proof. Set
$$ h=\frac{u_B-u_A}{2}, \qquad t_0=\frac{u_A+u_B}{4}. $$
First suppose $w_B>w_A$.

For $x<t_0$,
$$ |2x-u_A|<|2x-u_B|. $$
Together with $w_A<w_B$, this implies $d_x(A)<d_x(B)$.

If $w_B\le h$, then at $t_0$ both costs equal $h$. For $x>t_0$,
$$ |2x-u_A|>|2x-u_B|, \qquad |2x-u_A|>h\ge w_B, $$
so $d_x(A)>d_x(B)$. The unique crossing is $t_0$.

If $w_B>h$, put
$$ t=\frac{u_A+w_B}{2}>t_0. $$
At this point,
$$ |2t-u_A|=w_B, \qquad |2t-u_B|=|w_B-2h|\le w_B, $$
so both costs equal $w_B$. For $t_0<x<t$, both $|2x-u_A|$ and $w_A$ are smaller than $w_B$, giving $d_x(A)<d_x(B)$. For $x>t$, $|2x-u_A|$ exceeds both $w_B$ and $|2x-u_B|$, giving the reverse strict inequality.

This proves the first formula. Reflecting positions through the origin and exchanging $A,B$ gives the second formula. $\square$

In particular, on a generic candidate set every pair of outcomes has exactly one indifference position. For rational candidates, all these positions are rational.

4. Exact finite representation of strategyproof mechanisms

Fix $n$ and a generic encoded candidate set $C$.

Form a breakpoint set consisting of all candidate coordinates and all positions $t_{AB}$. Write its distinct elements as
$$ b_1<\cdots<b_B. $$
Partition the real line into its singleton breakpoints and intervening open intervals:
$$ (-\infty,b_1),\{b_1\},(b_1,b_2),\ldots,\{b_B\},(b_B,\infty). $$
Call these sets $I_1,\ldots,I_T$, where
$$ B\le m+\binom K2, \qquad T=2B+1=O(m^4). $$

Every open type has a constant strict ordering of all outcomes, by the single-crossing lemma. Every singleton type has its fixed weak ordering. Including candidate coordinates also ensures that every individual cost function is affine on each open type.

Choose a rational representative $r_t\in I_t$ for every type. For a profile, let $N_t$ be its number of residents in type $I_t$. The possible count vectors form the finite set
$$ \mathcal N_{n,T} = \left\{N\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^T:\sum_{t=1}^T N_t=n\right\}, $$
of size
$$ H=\binom{n+T-1}{T-1}. $$

4.1. Every anonymous strategyproof mechanism induces a count table

We identify an outcome with its unordered pair; sorting the pair changes neither costs nor strategyproofness.

Fix all reports except one, and let $x,y$ belong to the same open type. Their strict outcome rankings coincide. If changing the report from $x$ to $y$ changed the unordered output, one of these two positions would strictly prefer the other output. Either the forward or reverse deviation would then violate strategyproofness.

Thus changing a report within an open type leaves the unordered output unchanged. Singleton types contain only one possible report.

Replace reports, one at a time, by their type representatives. The output remains unchanged throughout. Anonymity then implies that the mechanism is represented by a table
$$ f:\mathcal N_{n,T}\longrightarrow\mathcal A(C). $$

This argument uses strategyproofness on every intermediate profile, without a support restriction.

4.2. The exact finite strategyproofness constraints

A table $f$ defines a strategyproof mechanism on all real profiles if and only if, for every count vector $N$, every $t$ with $N_t>0$, and every type $u$,
$$ d_{r_t}(f(N)) \le d_{r_t}\bigl(f(N-e_t+e_u)\bigr). $$
Here $e_t$ is the $t$th unit vector.

Necessity follows by applying strategyproofness to profiles at the representatives.

For sufficiency, consider a true position $x\in I_t$ and a report $y\in I_u$. Their count vectors differ by $-e_t+e_u$. The weak ordering of outcomes at $x$ is exactly the ordering at $r_t$, so the displayed inequality also holds with $x$ replacing $r_t$. This is the required strategyproofness inequality.

Consequently, full strategyproofness is characterized by a finite collection of constraints over all $H$ count vectors. Restricting these constraints to count vectors with at most $\tau_0$ occupied types would not suffice.

5. Exact computation of a table’s bounded-support guarantee

For a count vector $N$, let
$$ J(N)=\{t:N_t>0\}. $$
Define
$$ W_{\tau_0}(f,C) = \max_{\substack{N\in\mathcal N_{n,T}\\ |J(N)|\le\tau_0}} \ \max_{O\in\mathcal A(C)} \ \sup_{z_t\in I_t,\ t\in J(N)} \frac{\sum_{t\in J(N)}N_t d_{z_t}(f(N))} {\sum_{t\in J(N)}N_t d_{z_t}(O)}. $$
The factor $n^{-1}$ cancels. Taking the maximum over $O$ is equivalent to dividing by the minimum feasible social cost.

5.1. One occupied position per type suffices

Suppose a society has several occupied positions within an open type $I_t$. Let $z_t$ be their multiplicity-weighted average. This average remains in $I_t$. Because every $d_x(A)$ is affine on that interval,
$$ \sum_{x\in I_t}k_xd_x(A)=N_t d_{z_t}(A) $$
for every outcome $A$.

Replacing all these residents by residents at $z_t$ therefore preserves every pair’s social cost and preserves the count vector, hence the mechanism’s output. Singleton types need no replacement.

It follows that every society with support at most $\tau_0$ can be compressed to at most one position per occupied type without changing its ratio. Conversely, every configuration in the definition of $W_{\tau_0}$ has support at most $\tau_0$.

Encoded positions suffice for the same supremum: representatives and finite endpoints are rational, and positions in open intervals can be approximated by encoded rational positions within the same types. Costs are continuous and denominators remain positive.

Thus $W_{\tau_0}(f,C)$ is exactly the worst-case guarantee on the task’s encoded societies for this fixed $n,C$.

5.2. Only endpoint evaluations are required

Fix $N$ and $O$. On the product of the occupied types, numerator and denominator are affine functions of the positions $z_t$. The denominator is positive everywhere, including finite boundary points, since
$$ \sum_tN_t d_{z_t}(O)\ge n\,|o_1-o_2|>0. $$

For a bounded closed box, write any point as a convex combination of vertices. If $U,V$ are the affine numerator and denominator, then
$$ \frac{U(z)}{V(z)} = \sum_v \frac{\lambda_vV(v)}{\sum_{v'}\lambda_{v'}V(v')} \frac{U(v)}{V(v)}. $$
The coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one. Hence the maximum occurs at a vertex.

Using interval closures is legitimate for a supremum: approach each endpoint from within its original type while keeping the table output fixed.

For an unbounded interval, any position tending to infinity makes the ratio tend to $1$. Indeed, for any two outcomes $A,O$,
$$ |d_x(A)-d_x(O)| \le |a_1-o_1|+|a_2-o_2|, $$
so the difference of numerator and denominator is uniformly bounded, whereas the denominator tends to infinity. Truncating the unbounded intervals and applying the bounded-box result therefore reduces the supremum to finite endpoint evaluations and the value $1$.

There are at most $2^{|J(N)|}$ finite endpoint combinations. All associated ratios are rational. Therefore $W_{\tau_0}(f,C)$ is an exactly computable rational number.

5.3. The fixed-candidate optimum is attained

Let $\mathscr S_n(C)$ be the finite set of tables satisfying the constraints of Section 4. It is nonempty because the median mechanism induces such a table.

For generic $C$, the optimal guarantee among all anonymous deterministic strategyproof mechanisms is exactly
$$ \Gamma_{n,\tau_0}(C) = \min_{f\in\mathscr S_n(C)}W_{\tau_0}(f,C). $$
The minimum is attained and rational.

A coarse exhaustive-search bound can be stated entirely in the prescribed input parameters. Put
$$ \ell=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil, \qquad Q=2^{T\ell}. $$
Since $H\le(n+1)^T\le Q$, enumerating all tables, checking strategyproofness, and evaluating their guarantees takes at most
$$ K^Q\,Q\, \bigl(T^2+K\tau_0 2^{\tau_0}\bigr) \operatorname{poly}(L+\ell+m+\tau_0) $$
bit operations, with $K=O(m^2)$ and $T=O(m^4)$.

This bound is deliberately coarse and extremely large. The mechanism class imposes no efficiency restriction.

6. Generic candidate sets suffice for the global optimization

For an arbitrary encoded candidate set $C$, let $\Gamma_{n,\tau_0}(C)$ denote the infimum of the fixed-candidate guarantee over anonymous deterministic mechanisms strategyproof on all real profiles.

Generic rational candidate sets are dense among candidate sets with $m$ distinct ordered sites. The nongeneric conditions are finitely many nontrivial linear equations equating two pair sums or two pair gaps. Their union has empty interior, and rational points are dense in its open complement.

We now prove the inequality
$$ \Gamma_{n,\tau_0}(C) \le \liminf_{j\to\infty}\Gamma_{n,\tau_0}(C_j) $$
whenever generic encoded sets $C_j$ of the same cardinality converge coordinatewise to $C$.

6.1. Constructing a limiting strategyproof mechanism

Pass to a subsequence along which the right-hand guarantees converge to their liminf. For each $C_j$, choose an optimal finite table and its corresponding mechanism $M_j$.

The number of breakpoints is uniformly bounded by $m+\binom K2$. Pass to a subsequence with a constant breakpoint count $B$. Every breakpoint lies in the candidate span: outside that span, two pair costs differ by their distinct pair sums and cannot coincide. Thus the sorted breakpoints are uniformly bounded. Pass to a further subsequence on which each sorted breakpoint converges.

Let $Z$ be the finite set of their limits. At each $z\in Z$, the type index of $z$ has finitely many possibilities. Another subsequence makes all these indices constant. For every $x\notin Z$, its type index is eventually constant because each breakpoint converges to a point different from $x$.

Therefore, along this subsequence, every real position has an eventually constant type index.

Label candidate pairs by their two indices in the ordered candidate set. The population size, type count, and pair-label set are now fixed. There are only finitely many tables, so one further subsequence has a constant table.

For every real profile $P$, the output pair indices of $M_j(P)$ consequently stabilize. Define $M(P)$ to use those indices in $C$. This mechanism is anonymous and deterministic.

Fix any real profile, any resident’s true position $x$, and any real deviation. The relevant two outputs eventually stabilize in pair indices. Taking limits in the strategyproofness inequalities for $M_j$ gives
$$ d_x(M(P))\le d_x(M(P')). $$
Thus $M$ is strategyproof on all real profiles.

For every fixed encoded profile of support at most $\tau_0$, take limits in its approximation inequalities. Pair costs are continuous in candidate coordinates, and the optimum is the minimum of finitely many continuous pair-cost functions, hence is continuous. This proves the claimed inequality for $\Gamma$.

6.2. Removing nongeneric sets from the supremum

For fixed $n$, the preceding inequality gives
$$ \sup_{C\text{ encoded}}\Gamma_{n,\tau_0}(C) = \sup_{C\text{ generic and encoded}}\Gamma_{n,\tau_0}(C). $$
The inequality from right to left is immediate. For the reverse inequality, approximate any nongeneric $C$ by generic encoded sets and apply Section 6.1. This argument applies separately for every candidate-set cardinality.

7. The resulting characterization of $R_{\tau_0}$

Define
$$ \beta_{n,\tau_0} = \inf_{M_n} \sup_{(\mu,C)\in\mathcal E_n} \frac{S_\mu(M_n(\mu,C))} {\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,C)}, $$
where the infimum requires anonymity and strategyproofness on all real profiles and candidate sets.

Because the candidate set is public, mechanisms for different fixed candidate sets can be selected independently. Hence
$$ \beta_{n,\tau_0} = \sup_{C\text{ encoded}}\Gamma_{n,\tau_0}(C). $$
For completeness, every mechanism’s uniform guarantee is at least the right-hand side. Conversely, for any $\epsilon>0$, choose for each encoded $C$ a fixed-candidate mechanism within $\epsilon$ of its infimum. Assemble these into one mechanism; on other candidate sets, use a constant feasible outcome. Strategyproofness is checked separately for each public candidate set, so the assembled mechanism is admissible.

The population sizes can likewise be optimized independently:
$$ R_{\tau_0}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\beta_{n,\tau_0}. $$
One direction follows because every family has its $n$th guarantee at least $\beta_{n,\tau_0}$. For the other, choose an admissible $M_n$ with guarantee at most $\beta_{n,\tau_0}+1/n$ for each $n$.

Combining Sections 4–6 gives the exact characterization
$$ R_{\tau_0} = \limsup_{n\to\infty} \sup_{\substack{m\ge2\\ C\text{ generic and encoded}}} \min_{f\in\mathscr S_n(C)} W_{\tau_0}(f,C). $$

This formula eliminates the unrestricted search over functions on real profiles in favor of finite strategyproof count tables. It also separates the support restriction in the objective from the unrestricted strategyproofness constraints.

8. What remains open

The finite optimization above has not been evaluated uniformly over candidate sets and population sizes. In particular, it does not establish whether its limiting value is $1+\sqrt2$, $3$, or an intermediate number, nor whether that value depends on $\tau_0$.

A complete solution still requires a matching admissible guarantee and lower bound. The consecutive-pair reduction alone does not supply such a guarantee, as the independent-quantile examples demonstrate.
