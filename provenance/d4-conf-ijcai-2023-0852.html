1. Distributional setup and support endpoints

Put $N=n-1$. All focal types face the same rival maximum, denoted by $D$, with CDF $H(z)^N$.

For each supported type $s$, let
$$ a_s=\operatorname{ess\,inf}V_s, \qquad b_s=\operatorname{ess\,sup}V_s. $$
Define
$$ a=\min_s\bar\beta_s a_s, \qquad M=\max_s\bar\beta_s b_s. $$

These endpoints are rational and computable from the input. Indeed, a nonzero polynomial has finitely many zeros. A nonnegative polynomial density is therefore positive almost everywhere on each piece where its polynomial is nonzero. The first and last nonzero pieces determine its essential support endpoints.

In particular, $b_s>0$ and $M>0$. Each rival bid, and hence $D$, is absolutely continuous. Its density almost everywhere is
$$ g_N(z)=N H(z)^{N-1}H'(z). $$
For each fixed input, this density is bounded and supported on $[a,M]$.

The following payment threshold will be useful:
$$ P_t(\beta)>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \beta b_t>a. $$

To prove it, first observe that $D>a$ almost surely. If $\beta b_t\le a$, winning has probability zero. Conversely, if $\beta b_t>a$, choose $z$ strictly between these quantities. Then
$$ \Pr(V_t>z/\beta)>0, \qquad \Pr(D\le z)=H(z)^N>0. $$
The second inequality holds because some rival type has positive probability of bidding below every $z>a$. Independence gives positive winning probability. Since $D>0$ almost surely, payment is positive.

2. Reduction to weak-constraint bisection

Fix a focal type and abbreviate
$$ b=b_t,\qquad c=1+\widehat\gamma_t,\qquad B=\widehat B_t. $$
Thus $c>1$ and $B>0$. Write
$$ A(\beta)=\mathbb E[V_t\mathbf1\{\beta V_t\ge D\}], \qquad h(\beta)=cP(\beta)-A(\beta). $$
The utility is $U=A-P$, and the ROI constraint is $h\le0$.

Feasibility

A feasible parameter exists exactly when
$$ \frac ab<\frac1c. $$

For necessity, if $ca\ge b$, then
$$ cD-V_t>ca-b\ge0 $$
almost surely. Positive payment implies positive winning probability, so the ROI residual is strictly positive.

For sufficiency, any parameter satisfying
$$ \frac ab<\beta<\frac1c $$
has positive payment and satisfies
$$ cD-V_t\le(c\beta-1)V_t<0 $$
on the winning event. Moreover, $P$ is continuous and tends to zero as $\beta$ decreases to $a/b$. Continuity follows by dominated convergence, because ties have probability zero. Parameters sufficiently close to $a/b$ therefore also meet the positive budget bound.

Monotonicity

Define
$$ J(\beta)=\int_0^{\bar v}v^2f_t(v)g_N(\beta v)\,dv. $$
Then
$$ A'(\beta)=J(\beta), \qquad P'(\beta)=\beta J(\beta), $$
and consequently
$$ U'(\beta)=(1-\beta)J(\beta), \qquad h'(\beta)=(c\beta-1)J(\beta). $$

For justification, use
$$ A(\beta)=\int_0^{\bar v}vf_t(v)H(\beta v)^N\,dv $$
and
$$ P(\beta)= \int_0^{\bar v}f_t(v) \left(\int_0^{\beta v}zg_N(z)\,dz\right)dv. $$
Boundedness of $g_N$ supplies integrable bounds for the difference quotients on every compact interval of positive $\beta$. For a fixed $\beta$, its finitely many breakpoints affect only finitely many values of $v$, so differentiation under these integrals is valid.

Since $J\ge0$, payment is nondecreasing, utility is nondecreasing up to $1$, and $h$ decreases up to $1/c$ and increases thereafter.

Extend the functions continuously to zero. It follows that
$$ C=\{\beta\in[0,1]:P(\beta)\le B,\ h(\beta)\le0\} =[0,b^*] $$
for some $b^*\in[0,1]$.

The feasibility promise supplies a feasible parameter strictly above $a/b$ and below $1/c$. Hence $b^*>a/b$, so $P(b^*)>0$. Thus $b^*$ satisfies the original constraints and maximizes utility on $[0,1]$.

If a parameter $\beta>1$ is feasible, monotonicity gives
$$ P(1)\le P(\beta),\qquad h(1)\le h(\beta),\qquad U(1)\ge U(\beta). $$
Also $P(1)>0$, because $a/b<1/c<1$. Therefore $b^*$ is a feasible global maximizer.

It remains to implement membership queries for $C$. Exact membership tests permit ordinary bisection from $[0,1]$, including at query points with zero payment.

3. An endpoint-dominance lemma

The next lemma supplies exact signs without evaluating exponentially small quantities.

Lemma. Let $r>0$, and suppose:

  • $H(r)>0$;
  • $H$ is strictly increasing on some interval immediately to the left of $r$;
  • $G$ is measurable on $[0,r]$, with $|G(z)|\le C_0$;
  • on an interval immediately to the left of $r$, $G$ equals a nonzero rational polynomial $Q$.

Then an integer threshold $T$ can be constructed using rational arithmetic such that, for every $N\ge T$,
$$ \operatorname{sign}\left( \int_0^r G(z)g_N(z)\,dz \right) $$
equals the sign of $Q$ immediately to the left of $r$.

When the rational data and polynomial degrees have polynomial encoding length, the construction takes polynomial time and $T$ has polynomial bit length.

Proof

Choose rational $\eta>0$ such that $H$ is strictly increasing on $[r-\eta,r]$ and $G=Q$ there. Expand
$$ Q(r-x)=\sum_{j=0}^e a_jx^j. $$
Let $k$ be the smallest index with $a_k\ne0$, and put
$$ \sigma=\operatorname{sign}(a_k). $$

Choose a rational $\delta$ with $0<\delta\le\min\{\eta/2,1\}$ such that
$$ \delta\sum_{j>k}|a_j|\le\frac{|a_k|}{2}. $$
If the sum is zero, this last condition imposes no restriction. For $0<x\le\delta$,
$$ \sigma Q(r-x)\ge\frac{|a_k|}{2}x^k. $$
In particular, on
$$ [r-\delta/2,\ r-\delta/4] $$
we have
$$ \sigma G(z)\ge w, \qquad w=\frac{|a_k|}{2}\left(\frac{\delta}{4}\right)^k>0. $$

Set
$$ p_0=H(r-\delta),\qquad p_1=H(r-\delta/2),\qquad p_2=H(r-\delta/4). $$
Strict increase gives
$$ 0\le p_0<p_1<p_2. $$
The integrand has the desired sign throughout $[r-\delta,r]$. Its contribution below that interval is bounded in magnitude by $C_0p_0^N$. Therefore
$$ \sigma\int_0^rG(z)g_N(z)\,dz \ge w(p_2^N-p_1^N)-C_0p_0^N. $$
Writing $s=p_1/p_2$ and $\kappa=1-s>0$, this implies
$$ \sigma\int_0^rG(z)g_N(z)\,dz \ge p_2^N\left[w-(w+C_0)s^N\right]. $$

For $0\le s<1$,
$$ s\le\frac1{1+\kappa}. $$
Indeed, $s(1+\kappa)=s(2-s)\le1$. The binomial theorem then gives
$$ s^N\le\frac1{(1+\kappa)^N} \le\frac1{1+N\kappa}. $$
Consequently, the displayed lower bound is strictly positive whenever
$$ N>\frac{C_0}{w\kappa}. $$
One valid threshold is
$$ T=1+\left\lfloor\frac{C_0}{w\kappa}\right\rfloor. $$

All these operations are rational. Polynomial evaluation and translation require only powers bounded by the polynomial degree. The quantities $\delta,w,p_1,p_2,\kappa$ consequently have polynomial bit length, as does $T$. This proves the lemma.

4. Applying the lemma to ROI

Fix a rational query $q>0$. Define the focal tail probability and tail first moment by
$$ S(y)=\int_y^b f_t(v)\,dv, \qquad W(y)=\int_y^b vf_t(v)\,dv, $$
with both set to zero for $y\ge b$.

Independence gives
$$ P(q)=\int_0^M zS(z/q)g_N(z)\,dz $$
and
$$ h(q)=\int_0^M \left[czS(z/q)-W(z/q)\right]g_N(z)\,dz. $$

If $qb\le a$, both quantities vanish and the weak constraints hold. Assume henceforth that $qb>a$.

Let $r$ be the largest rival-support point at or below $qb$. More explicitly, partition the bid axis by all scaled rival breakpoints. On each open piece, the mixture density $H'$ is a nonnegative polynomial. Select the last piece with a nonzero polynomial whose left endpoint is below $qb$, and truncate its right endpoint at $qb$.

This construction gives rational $r$ satisfying
$$ 0<r\le\min\{qb,M\}, \qquad H(r)=H(qb)>0. $$
There is an interval immediately to the left of $r$ on which $H$ is strictly increasing. Above $r$ and below $qb$, the rival distribution has no mass. Above $qb$, the focal tail functions vanish. Thus
$$ h(q)=\int_0^r G_q(z)g_N(z)\,dz, \qquad G_q(z)=czS(z/q)-W(z/q). $$

A valid global bound is
$$ |G_q(z)|\le cM+b. $$
On a sufficiently short interval immediately to the left of $r$, the function $G_q$ is a rational polynomial of degree at most $d+2$.

The essential issue is whether that polynomial could vanish identically.

Nonvanishing for queries above $1/c$

Suppose $q>1/c$. Write $y=z/q$. On the relevant focal piece, $S$ and $W$ are polynomials satisfying
$$ W'(y)=yS'(y). $$
If the local polynomial $G_q$ were identically zero, then
$$ cq\,yS(y)-W(y)=0. $$
Differentiating yields
$$ cq\,S(y)+(cq-1)yS'(y)=0. $$
Write $S(y)=\sum_j s_jy^j$. The coefficient of $y^j$ in this identity is
$$ \bigl(cq+(cq-1)j\bigr)s_j. $$
Every factor in parentheses is strictly positive because $cq>1$. Therefore every $s_j$ would vanish.

This is impossible: immediately to the left of $r/q\le b$, the tail probability $S$ is positive, by the definition of the essential upper endpoint $b$.

The endpoint-dominance lemma therefore applies. It produces a polynomial-bit-length threshold $T_{\mathrm{ROI}}(q)$ and an exact eventual sign for $h(q)$.

For $q\le1/c$, no threshold is needed:
$$ cD-V_t\le(cq-1)V_t\le0 $$
on the winning event. Hence the ROI constraint holds for every $N$.

This handles support gaps and also cases where the local polynomial vanishes at $r$: its first nonzero Taylor coefficient still determines the eventual sign.

5. Applying the lemma to the budget

Continue with $qb>a$, and put
$$ p=H(r)=H(qb). $$

Case A: $p<1$

Winning requires $D\le qb$, so
$$ P(q)\le M p^N. $$
The same elementary inequality used above gives
$$ p^N\le\frac1{1+N(1-p)}. $$
Thus, for example,
$$ N\ge \left\lceil\frac{M}{B(1-p)}\right\rceil $$
implies $P(q)<B$. This threshold has polynomial bit length and is computable without taking a large power.

Case B: $p=1$

Here $r=M$, and all rival mass is included in the integral. Therefore
$$ P(q)-B = \int_0^M\left[zS(z/q)-B\right]g_N(z)\,dz. $$
The integrand has absolute value at most $M+B$.

Its polynomial expression immediately to the left of $M$ cannot be identically zero. Otherwise, on the corresponding focal piece,
$$ q\,yS(y)=B $$
would be a polynomial identity. Its left-hand side has zero constant coefficient, whereas $B>0$.

The endpoint-dominance lemma consequently gives an exact eventual sign for $P(q)-B$, beyond a polynomial-bit-length threshold $T_{\mathrm{budget}}(q)$.

6. Polynomial bit complexity and the large-$n$ algorithm

Let $L_{\mathrm{soc}}$ denote the encoding length of the society data, excluding $n$ and $\varepsilon$. Define
$$ K=\min\{k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:2^{-k}\le\varepsilon\}. $$
Every midpoint in $K$ steps of bisection from $[0,1]$ has bit length at most $K+1$.

The constructions above use only:

  • rational integration of the original density pieces;
  • rational scaling and sorting of breakpoints;
  • polynomial substitution and translation;
  • polynomial evaluation at rational points;
  • rational comparisons and integer rounding.

They do not expand $H^N$.

For completeness, their bit bounds follow from elementary rational-arithmetic estimates. Integrating a density coefficient introduces a divisor at most $d+2$. Evaluating or scaling a degree-$d+2$ polynomial uses powers with exponent at most $d+2$. Sums of polynomially many rationals can be placed over the product of their denominators, whose bit length is at most the sum of the denominator bit lengths. Translating a polynomial introduces binomial coefficients of bit length at most $O(d)$.

These estimates apply successively to $H,S,W$, the endpoint $r$, the translated local polynomial, $\delta,w,\kappa$, and the threshold. The number of construction stages is fixed, so their composition remains a polynomial bound.

It follows that there is an effectively obtainable polynomial $R$ such that every threshold arising from a query of bit length at most $K+1$ is less than
$$ 2^{R(m,\tau,d,L_{\mathrm{soc}},K)}. $$
The bound is uniform over all supported focal types.

Theorem. Under the rational rival-parameter interpretation, the requested intervals can be computed in time polynomial in
$$ m,\tau,d,L,\log n,\log(1/\varepsilon) $$
whenever
$$ n-1\ge 2^{R(m,\tau,d,L_{\mathrm{soc}},K)}. $$

Proof. For each focal type, initialize $[\ell,u]=[0,1]$. At each midpoint $q$, determine weak feasibility using Sections 4 and 5. Under the displayed hypothesis, every applicable threshold is satisfied, so every comparison is exact.

If $q$ satisfies both weak constraints, replace $\ell$ by $q$; otherwise replace $u$ by $q$. Section 2 shows that the weak feasible set is $[0,b^*]$, so every update preserves
$$ b^*\in[\ell,u]. $$
After $K$ steps,
$$ u-\ell=2^{-K}\le\varepsilon. $$
The interval contains the feasible global maximizer $b^*$.

There are $\tau K$ queries. Each uses polynomial-time rational calculations and a binary comparison of $N$ with the constructed threshold. Hence the claimed running time follows. For $K=0$, the initial interval already suffices.

The algorithm can also operate without first constructing a uniform bound: it computes the thresholds at its actual queries and uses the exact comparison rules whenever $N$ exceeds them.

7. What this resolves—and what remains open

The new result removes the exponentially small residual obstacle for the sufficiently large-$n$ regime of the entire supported density class. It requires neither strict positivity throughout the supports nor nonzero limiting residuals.

It does not resolve the full binary-encoded problem. If $N$ falls below a constructed threshold, the endpoint rule makes no assertion about the finite-$N$ sign. Such a threshold has polynomial bit length but may have exponentially large numerical value.

For comparison, exact dense expansion still gives an algorithm polynomial in numerical $n$: $H^N$ has degree at most $N(d+1)$ on each rival piece, and the expectation integrands have polynomial degree and coefficient bit length in $n$ and the remaining parameters. Rational piecewise integration followed by the bisection of Section 2 therefore terminates in polynomial time in numerical $n$. This remains insufficient when $n$ is binary encoded.

The unresolved range is thus a genuine intermediate range: numerical $n$ can be exponential in the society description, while not yet large enough for the proved endpoint-dominance comparisons. No polynomial-time treatment of that range, and no NP-hardness reduction, is established here.
