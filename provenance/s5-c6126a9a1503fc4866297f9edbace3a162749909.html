The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite resident types \(R\) and hospital types \(H\), rational masses \(\mu_r\) and \(\nu_h\), strict orders over types, per-hospital capacities \(c_h\), and an expansion budget \(\beta\), let \(q_h=\nu_hc_h\). Is there \(\delta_h\ge0\) with \(\sum_h\delta_h\le\beta\) such that canonical resident-proposing deferred acceptance on type mass, with hospital-type capacities \(q_h+\delta_h\) and only boundary types split, returns a stable mass matching \(x\) satisfying \(\sum_{r,h}\operatorname{rank}_r(h)x_{rh}\le\kappa\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: nonempty lists of resident types \(R\) and hospital types \(H\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_r\) or \(\nu_h\). Set \(m=|H|\) and \(\tau=|R|+|H|\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses need not sum to one. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Every type has a strict, complete order over the opposite support; every pair is acceptable and preferred to being unmatched. Hospital preferences are responsive. Input capacities are \(c_h\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), with aggregate capacities \(q_h=\nu_hc_h\) satisfying \(\sum_hq_h\ge\sum_r\mu_r\). The budget \(\beta\) and threshold \(\kappa\) are nonnegative binary rationals.
An expansion is \(\delta\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{H}\) with \(\sum_h\delta_h\le\beta\), measured in resident-mass units; write \(Q_h=q_h+\delta_h\). A mass matching has \(x_{rh}\ge0\), \(\sum_hx_{rh}=\mu_r\), and \(\sum_rx_{rh}\le Q_h\).
Such a matching is stable if no pair \((r,h)\) has both positive resident mass assigned below \(h\) in \(r\)'s order and either unused capacity at \(h\) or positive mass at \(h\) from a resident type ranked below \(r\).
Canonical resident-proposing mass deferred acceptance starts with all mass proposing to its first choice. In synchronous rounds each hospital retains the highest-priority mass among its holdings and new proposals up to \(Q_h\), splitting only the type at the acceptance boundary; rejected mass advances to its next choice, including mass displaced in later rounds. Members of a type are interchangeable, with no additional tie-breaking. Let \(x(\delta)\) be its terminal allocation.
The decision problem asks whether some expansion satisfies \(\sum_{r\in R,h\in H}\operatorname{rank}_r(h)x_{rh}(\delta)\le\kappa\), where ranks are positions among hospital types and the sum is not normalized.

Is this continuous capacity-expansion decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

The discrete decision problem with integer capacity expansions is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete under strict, complete preferences and sufficient initial capacity ([Bobbio et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.01302)).
With fixed capacities, resident-optimal bipartite stable mass allocations are computable in polynomial time ([Dean and Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf)); this result does not classify budgeted capacity expansion.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The normalization was unspecified: masses are arbitrary positive rational totals, without unit-sum normalization, preserving the discrete model's population-count interpretation.
  • The preference domain was incomplete: require nonempty supports, strict complete orders, universal acceptability, and responsive hospital preferences.
  • The capacity domain was unspecified: retain nonnegative integer per-hospital capacities, while aggregate capacities and expansions are measured in resident-mass units.
  • The initial-capacity condition was omitted: require total initial capacity at least total resident mass, so all resident mass is matched and no unmatched-mass penalty is needed.
  • The numeric domains were unspecified: budget and threshold are nonnegative binary rationals; expansion decisions and assignments are nonnegative real quantities.
  • Canonical mass deferred acceptance and boundary splitting were undefined: specify synchronous proposals, retention by type priority, partial retention only at an acceptance boundary, and advancement of all rejected or subsequently displaced mass.
  • Mass stability and ranks were undefined: use positive-mass blocking pairs, rank hospital types rather than individual hospital copies, and retain the unnormalized rank sum.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The following reduction handles arbitrary real expansions directly.

1. Source problem and construction

We reduce from \(\mathrm{SUBSET\ SUM}\): given positive binary integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) and a target \(T\), decide whether some subset sums to \(T\). This is a standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem. [Karp’s paper](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf) calls this equality version “knapsack.”

Write \(W=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_i\). We may restrict to \(1\le T\le W\): cases outside this range are trivial and can be replaced by fixed yes or no instances.

Set \(K=W+1\) and \(M_i=Kw_i\). For each index \(i\), introduce three resident types \(r_i,p_i,s_i\) and four hospital types \(A_i,B_i,C_i,D_i\).

Their masses and initial aggregate capacities are:

| Resident type | Mass | Hospital type | Initial capacity |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| \(r_i\) | \(w_i\) | \(A_i\) | \(0\) |
| \(p_i\) | \(M_i\) | \(B_i\) | \(M_i\) |
| \(s_i\) | \(M_i\) | \(C_i\) | \(M_i\) |
| | | \(D_i\) | \(w_i\) |

Every hospital has mass \(\nu_h=1\), so these aggregate capacities are realized by integer input capacities \(c_h=q_h\).

The resident preference prefixes are:

| Resident | Preference prefix |
|---|---|
| \(r_i\) | \(A_i\succ B_i\succ D_i\) |
| \(p_i\) | \(B_i\succ C_i\succ D_i\) |
| \(s_i\) | \(C_i\succ B_i\succ D_i\) |

The hospital preference prefixes are:

| Hospital | Preference prefix |
|---|---|
| \(A_i\) | \(r_i\succ p_i\succ s_i\) |
| \(B_i\) | \(s_i\succ r_i\succ p_i\) |
| \(C_i\) | \(p_i\succ s_i\succ r_i\) |
| \(D_i\) | \(r_i\succ s_i\succ p_i\) |

Append every unlisted opposite-side type in a fixed order. Thus all preferences are strict and complete, and every pair is acceptable.

Set the expansion budget and rank threshold to

\[ \beta=T,\qquad \kappa=(4K+3)W-(2K+2)T. \]

Since \(T\le W\), we have \(\kappa\ge(2K+1)W>0\).

The initial total capacity equals total resident mass: each gadget contributes \(2M_i+w_i\) to both quantities.

2. Deferred acceptance stays within each gadget

Fix any admissible real expansion. Write

\[ \alpha_i=\delta_{A_i},\qquad b_i=\delta_{B_i},\qquad e_i=\delta_{C_i},\qquad d_i=\delta_{D_i}. \]

During deferred acceptance:

  • Any \(p_i\)-mass rejected by \(B_i\) is permanently retained by \(C_i\): \(C_i\) ranks \(p_i\) first and has capacity at least \(M_i\).
  • Any \(s_i\)-mass rejected by \(C_i\) is permanently retained by \(B_i\): \(B_i\) ranks \(s_i\) first and has capacity at least \(M_i\).
  • Any \(r_i\)-mass rejected by \(B_i\) is permanently retained by \(D_i\): \(D_i\) ranks \(r_i\) first and has capacity at least \(w_i\).

Consequently, no resident mass reaches the appended preference tails. Formally, this follows by induction over rounds: before any proposal outside these routes, each designated receiving hospital has sufficient capacity for its highest-priority resident type, so such a proposal cannot arise.

Thus every terminal allocation has the following support:

\[ r_i:\{A_i,B_i,D_i\},\qquad p_i:\{B_i,C_i\},\qquad s_i:\{C_i,B_i\}. \]

Moreover, \(r_i\)-mass held at \(A_i\) is exactly \(\min\{w_i,\alpha_i\}\).

For completeness, deferred acceptance terminates on these constructed instances, including for irrational expansions. Suppress the index \(i\), and put \(u=(w-\alpha)_+\). Because \(u\le w<M\), the first cumulative amount of \(p\)-mass rejected by \(B\) is \(P_0=(u-b)_+\). After \(C\) receives cumulative \(p\)-mass \(P\), it has rejected cumulative \(s\)-mass \(S=(P-e)_+\).

Until all \(p\)-mass has left \(B\), the whole \(u\) units of \(r\)-mass remain there: rejecting any \(r\)-mass requires first rejecting all lower-priority \(p\)-mass. The next feedback update therefore gives

\[ P'=\min\{M,(u+S-b)_+\}. \]

If \(u\le b+e\), then \(P_0\le e\), so \(S=0\) and no feedback occurs. If \(u>b+e\), then \(P_0>e\), and each feedback update satisfies

\[ P'=\min\{M,P+(u-b-e)\}. \]

The increment is a fixed positive real number. Hence \(P\) reaches \(M\) after finitely many updates, after which only final rejections to permanent destinations remain.

The terminal allocation is stable. Indeed, mass assigned below a hospital must previously have been rejected there. Upon rejecting resident type \(r\), that hospital is full and holds only types ranked at least as high as \(r\). Subsequent proposals cannot worsen its retained priority boundary. Therefore it cannot terminate with either unused capacity or positive mass ranked below \(r\).

3. The threshold lemma

Lemma. If \(\alpha_i+b_i+e_i<w_i\), then the combined rank cost of \(p_i,s_i\) is at least \(4M_i-e_i\). For every expansion, their combined rank cost is at least \(2M_i\).

Proof. Suppress the index \(i\). Under the strict inequality, \(\alpha<w\), so the \(r\)-mass outside \(A\) is \(u=w-\alpha>b+e\).

Let \(z=x_{rB}\). All \(2M\) units of \(p,s\)-mass lie in \(B,C\), and \(z\) additional units of \(r\)-mass lie in \(B\). Their combined capacity is \(2M+b+e\). Therefore

\[ 2M+z\le2M+b+e,\qquad z\le b+e. \]

It follows that \(x_{rD}=u-z>0\).

If \(x_{pB}>0\), then \((r,B)\) blocks: positive \(r\)-mass is assigned to \(D\), which \(r\) ranks below \(B\), while \(B\) holds positive \(p\)-mass and ranks \(r\) above \(p\). Stability therefore forces \(x_{pB}=0\).

All \(M\) units of \(p\)-mass consequently occupy their second choice \(C\). Since \(C\) has capacity \(M+e\), at most \(e\) units of \(s\)-mass can remain there. The rank costs satisfy

\[ \operatorname{cost}(p)=2M,\qquad \operatorname{cost}(s)=2M-x_{sC}\ge2M-e. \]

Their combined cost is at least \(4M-e\). The universal bound \(2M\) follows because both types have mass \(M\) and every rank is at least \(1\). \(\square\)

4. A subset-sum solution gives a feasible expansion

Suppose \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}w_i=T\).

Expand \(A_i\) by \(w_i\) for \(i\in I\), and make every other expansion zero. The budget used is exactly \(T\).

For \(i\in I\), every resident type receives its first choice. The gadget cost is \(2M_i+w_i\).

For \(i\notin I\), the threshold lemma forces \(p_i\) entirely into \(C_i\). Because \(C_i\) has capacity exactly \(M_i\), all \(s_i\)-mass lies in \(B_i\). Hospital \(B_i\) is then full, and all \(r_i\)-mass lies in \(D_i\). The gadget cost is \(4M_i+3w_i\).

The total cost is therefore

\[ \sum_{i\in I}(2M_i+w_i)+ \sum_{i\notin I}(4M_i+3w_i) =(4K+3)W-(2K+2)T =\kappa. \]

Thus the constructed instance is a yes instance.

5. Any feasible real expansion yields a subset-sum solution

Conversely, fix any expansion with total cost at most \(T\), and define

\[ S=\{i:\alpha_i+b_i+e_i\ge w_i\},\qquad W_S=\sum_{i\in S}w_i. \]

The budget implies

\[ W_S\le \sum_{i\in S}(\alpha_i+b_i+e_i) \le T. \]

If the source instance has no subset summing to \(T\), then the integer \(W_S\) satisfies \(W_S\le T-1\).

Every \(r_i\) contributes rank cost at least \(w_i\). Apply the universal probe bound to indices in \(S\), and the stronger threshold bound to indices outside \(S\). The total rank cost is at least

\[ W+\sum_{i\in S}2M_i+ \sum_{i\notin S}(4M_i-e_i) = 4KW-2KW_S+W-\sum_{i\notin S}e_i. \]

Since \(\sum_{i\notin S}e_i\le T\), this is at least

\[ 4KW-2K(T-1)+W-T. \]

Subtracting the decision threshold gives

\[ \bigl(4KW-2K(T-1)+W-T\bigr)-\kappa =2K+T-2W =T+2>0. \]

Hence every admissible real expansion exceeds \(\kappa\) when the subset-sum instance is a no instance. This proves the converse.

6. Encoding size and conclusion

The construction has \(m=4n\) hospital types and support size \(\tau=7n\). Preference lists have polynomial total length.

Every numerical datum is an integer of magnitude at most \(O(W^2)\), so its binary length is \(O(\log W)\). Since \(\log W\) is polynomially bounded by the source input length, the reduction has polynomial binary encoding size.

We have therefore proved \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness even when all masses and initial aggregate capacities are integers and initial total capacity equals total resident mass. Arbitrary fractional expansions are covered by the converse argument. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Source problem and valid construction

The source problem is the binary-encoded positive-integer version of \(\mathrm{SUBSET\ SUM}\). Its hardness is standard; this precise formulation is also described in [“Subset Sum Made Simple”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08248). Restricting to \(1\le T\le W\) excludes only readily decidable cases and does not undermine the reduction.

The constructed instance satisfies the task’s requirements:

  • All resident and hospital masses are positive integers.
  • Every initial capacity is a nonnegative integer, realized with \(\nu_h=1\).
  • Appending unlisted types produces strict, complete preferences.
  • Each gadget has resident mass and initial capacity both equal to \(2M_i+w_i\).
  • The budget is nonnegative, and the stated bound \(\kappa\ge(2K+1)W>0\) is correct.

The local resident prefixes put \(D_i\) in position \(3\), so all subsequently used ranks are correct.

2. Deferred acceptance remains local

The permanence assertions are valid. Each designated receiving hospital ranks the arriving type first among the entire support, and its capacity is at least that type’s total mass:

  • \(C_i\) can permanently retain all arriving \(p_i\)-mass.
  • \(B_i\) can permanently retain all arriving \(s_i\)-mass.
  • \(D_i\) can permanently retain all arriving \(r_i\)-mass.

The induction over rounds rules out a first proposal outside these routes. Consequently, the claimed terminal support is justified. Since all \(r_i\)-mass initially proposes to \(A_i\) and no other mass reaches it, its retained amount is exactly \(\min\{w_i,\alpha_i\}\).

Termination for real expansions

The cumulative-rejection equations are consistent with synchronous deferred acceptance.

Initially, \(u=(w-\alpha)_+\le w<M\), so \(B\) can retain all arriving \(r\)-mass while rejecting \(P_0=(u-b)_+\) units of \(p\)-mass.

After cumulative \(p\)-proposals of amount \(P\), hospital \(C\) rejects cumulative \(s\)-mass \(S=(P-e)_+\). Until \(p\)-mass at \(B\) is exhausted, its priority order requires retaining all \(r\)-mass before any remaining \(p\)-mass. Thus the stated update
\(P'=\min\{M,(u+S-b)_+\}\)
is valid.

The two cases are exhaustive:

  • If \(u\le b+e\), then \(P_0\le e\), and no \(s\)-mass initiates feedback.
  • If \(u>b+e\), then the update increases cumulative rejection by the fixed positive amount \(u-b-e\), capped at \(M\).

Therefore the process terminates after finitely many updates even for irrational expansions. This need not be a polynomial number of rounds; the proof uses termination, not a polynomial running-time bound.

Stability

The rejection argument correctly verifies the task’s blocking-pair definition. After rejecting a type, a hospital is full and retains only types ranked at least as highly. Subsequent proposals cannot worsen this priority boundary.

Splitting the rejected type at the boundary causes no problem: retained mass of the same type is not strictly lower-priority mass. Zero-capacity hospitals also cause no exception. Thus the terminal allocation is stable.

3. Threshold lemma

Every step of the lemma is justified.

Under \(\alpha+b+e<w\), the outside-\(A\) mass is \(u=w-\alpha>b+e\). Locality places all \(2M\) units of \(p,s\)-mass in \(B,C\), yielding
\(2M+z\le2M+b+e\)
for \(z=x_{rB}\). Hence \(z\le b+e<u\), so \(x_{rD}>0\).

If \(x_{pB}>0\), the pair \((r,B)\) satisfies both blocking conditions: resident mass occupies the worse hospital \(D\), and \(B\) holds positive lower-priority \(p\)-mass. Stability therefore forces \(x_{pB}=0\).

It follows that all \(p\)-mass occupies \(C\), leaving room there for at most \(e\) units of \(s\)-mass. The resulting costs \(2M\) and \(2M-x_{sC}\ge2M-e\) are correct. The universal lower bound \(2M\) follows directly from ranks being at least \(1\).

No integrality assumption on expansions enters this lemma.

4. Forward implication

For a subset summing to \(T\), the proposed expansion uses exactly the budget.

In selected gadgets, all first-choice proposals fit immediately, giving cost \(2M_i+w_i\).

In unselected gadgets, the threshold lemma and the original capacities force all \(p_i\)-mass into \(C_i\), all \(s_i\)-mass into \(B_i\), and all \(r_i\)-mass into \(D_i\). Their cost is \(4M_i+3w_i\).

The displayed total simplifies correctly to
\((4K+3)W-(2K+2)T=\kappa\).
The forward implication is established for the specified canonical allocation.

For
\(S=\{i:\alpha_i+b_i+e_i\ge w_i\}\),
nonnegativity and the budget give \(W_S\le T\). The quantity \(W_S\) is an integer even when expansions are irrational. Thus, on a source no instance, \(W_S\le T-1\).
