The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a Boolean integrity constraint \(\varphi\) with feasible type set \(X_\varphi=\operatorname{Mod}(\varphi)\), decide whether there are maps \(g_j:\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]\to\{0,1\}\), one for each issue \(j\), with \(g_j(0)=0\) and \(g_j(1)=1\), such that for every finitely supported distribution \(\mu\) over \(X_\varphi\) with rational masses, the judgment vector \((g_j(\sum_{x\in X_\varphi:x_j=1}\mu_x))_{j=1}^m\) belongs to \(X_\varphi\).

Input is a Boolean formula \(\varphi\) on \(m\geq 3\) issues, with \(X_\varphi=\operatorname{Mod}(\varphi)\subseteq\{0,1\}^m\). Promise that each coordinate takes both values among elements of \(X_\varphi\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses sum to \(1\); types outside the support have mass \(0\).

The same maps must serve all societies; no monotonicity or regularity is imposed, and maps may differ between issues.
Only \(\varphi\) is supplied to the existence decision. Measure its running time in \(m\) and its encoding bit length \(L\); for procedures taking a represented society, use \(m,\tau,L,L_\mu\), where \(L_\mu\) is that society's encoding bit length.

I do not establish that hardness. The following results are proved below:

  • $\mathcal E$ is exactly semantic renamable Horn recognition.
  • $\mathcal E$ is $\mathsf{coNP}$-hard, even for promised CNF inputs of clause width at most $5$.

  • Promised $2$-CNF inputs always admit the required maps.

In particular,
$$ \mathcal E\in\mathsf P \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}. $$
This conditional statement and $\mathsf{coNP}$-hardness do not establish many-one $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness.

1. Reduction to a finite closure condition

Write $X=X_\varphi$. The coordinate promise implies that $X$ is nonempty.

For $b\in\{0,1\}^m$, define $T_b$ by
$$ T_b(x,y)_j= \begin{cases} x_j,&x_j=y_j,\\ b_j,&x_j\ne y_j. \end{cases} $$
Thus coordinate $j$ uses AND if $b_j=0$, and OR if $b_j=1$. Call $b$ valid if
$$ T_b(x,y)\in X \qquad\text{for every }x,y\in X. $$

Theorem 1. The maps required in the task exist if and only if a valid $b$ exists.

Proof. Suppose the maps exist, and set
$$ b_j=g_j(1/2). $$
For distinct $x,y\in X$, consider the society assigning mass $1/2$ to each. Its marginal on coordinate $j$ is their common value when they agree, and $1/2$ otherwise. The endpoint requirements therefore make its output $T_b(x,y)$, which must belong to $X$. For identical inputs, $T_b(x,x)=x$. Hence $b$ is valid.

Conversely, suppose $b$ is valid. For rational $p\in[0,1]$, define
$$ g_j(p)= \begin{cases} 1,&b_j=0\text{ and }p=1,\\ 1,&b_j=1\text{ and }p>0,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
Both endpoint requirements hold.

Let the positive support of a society be $x^1,\ldots,x^\tau$. Because all listed masses are positive and sum to $1$, its output on an AND coordinate is $1$ precisely when every supported type has value $1$. Its output on an OR coordinate is $1$ precisely when some supported type has value $1$.

The resulting vector is consequently the fold of $T_b$ over the support. Coordinatewise AND and OR are associative, and binary closure keeps every intermediate fold in $X$. The final output belongs to $X$. $\square$

The promise also gives a useful consequence:
$$ b\text{ valid}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b\in X. $$
Indeed, fold $T_b$ over all elements of $X$. Each coordinate contains both values, so its folded value is $b_j$. Closure puts this fold in $X$.

For completeness, the connection with Horn functions is explicit. Put
$$ R_b(x)=x\oplus b, $$
where $\oplus$ is coordinatewise addition modulo $2$. Then
$$ R_b(T_b(x,y))=R_b(x)\land R_b(y). $$
Thus $b$ is valid exactly when $R_b(X)$ is closed under AND. This is the oriented-intersection definition of a renamable Horn function used in [*Polynomial Certificates for Propositional Classes*](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/t/tech_reports/reports/2004-8/report.pdf). The proofs below establish the needed recognition properties directly.

2. Exact binary constraints on orientations

A partial assignment $(S,a)$ consists of $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and $a\in\{0,1\}^S$. It is forbidden if no $x\in X$ extends it, and minimally forbidden if deleting any assigned coordinate makes it extendible.

Every forbidden partial assignment contains a minimally forbidden restriction: repeatedly delete coordinates while forbiddenness persists. Under the coordinate promise, every minimally forbidden assignment has at least two coordinates.

Lemma 2. A vector $b$ is valid if and only if every minimally forbidden $(S,a)$ satisfies
$$ \bigl|\{j\in S:b_j=a_j\}\bigr|\leq 1. $$

Proof of necessity. Suppose distinct $i,k\in S$ satisfy $b_i=a_i$ and $b_k=a_k$.

Minimality supplies $x^{(i)}\in X$ agreeing with $a$ on $S\setminus\{i\}$. It must disagree at $i$, since otherwise it would extend the forbidden assignment. Likewise, there is $x^{(k)}\in X$ agreeing with $a$ on $S\setminus\{k\}$ and disagreeing at $k$.

At $i$ and $k$, these inputs disagree, so their $T_b$ output equals $a$. At every other coordinate of $S$, both inputs already equal $a$. Their output therefore extends $(S,a)$ and lies outside $X$, contradicting validity.

Proof of sufficiency. Suppose instead that $x,y\in X$ and $z=T_b(x,y)\notin X$. Choose a minimally forbidden restriction $(S,z|_S)$.

The sets
$$ A=\{j\in S:x_j\ne z_j\}, \qquad B=\{j\in S:y_j\ne z_j\} $$
are nonempty, since neither feasible input extends that restriction. They are disjoint: if both inputs differed from $z_j$, they would both equal $1-z_j$, forcing the output to equal $1-z_j$.

Choose $i\in A$ and $k\in B$. These coordinates are distinct, the inputs disagree at each, and consequently
$$ b_i=z_i,\qquad b_k=z_k. $$
The minimally forbidden restriction agrees with $b$ in at least two coordinates, contrary to the assumed condition. $\square$

Introduce orientation variables $B_1,\ldots,B_m$. A minimally forbidden $(S,a)$ contributes the clauses
$$ (B_i\ne a_i)\lor(B_k\ne a_k), \qquad i,k\in S,\quad i<k. $$
By Lemma 2, their conjunction $H_X$ has exactly the valid orientations as its satisfying assignments.

Although there may be exponentially many minimally forbidden assignments, there are at most
$$ 4\binom m2 $$
distinct clauses in $H_X$.

3. Additional result: prime refinement of CNF inputs

Assume in this section that the supplied formula is already a CNF,
$$ \varphi=D_1\land\cdots\land D_r. $$
Remove tautological clauses and duplicate literals. A remaining clause contains at most one literal of each variable.

A clause $C$ is an implicate of $\varphi$ if $\varphi\models C$. It is a prime implicate if no proper subclause is an implicate.

For a non-tautological clause
$$ C=\bigvee_{j\in S}\ell_j, $$
let $a_j$ be the value falsifying $\ell_j$. Then $C$ is an implicate precisely when $(S,a)$ is forbidden, and is prime precisely when $(S,a)$ is minimally forbidden.

3.1 Any prime refinement gives the exact orientation set

For each supplied clause $D_t$, choose any prime implicate $C_t\subseteq D_t$, and put
$$ F=C_1\land\cdots\land C_r. $$

Theorem 3. The formulas $F$ and $\varphi$ are equivalent. Moreover, if $H_F$ contains the binary orientation clauses contributed by these selected $C_t$, then
$$ \operatorname{Mod}(H_F) = \{b:T_b\text{ preserves }X_\varphi\}. $$

Proof. Every selected $C_t$ is entailed by $\varphi$, so $\varphi\models F$. Conversely, $C_t\subseteq D_t$ implies $C_t\models D_t$. Hence $F\models\varphi$.

If $b$ is valid, Lemma 2 applied to each prime $C_t$ shows that $b$ satisfies $H_F$.

For the converse, suppose $b$ satisfies $H_F$. We show that $T_b$ preserves every clause $C_t$.

Let $(S,a)$ be the falsifying partial assignment of one such clause. Satisfaction of its orientation constraints says that $b$ agrees with $a$ on at most one coordinate of $S$.

Suppose $x,y$ satisfy the clause but $z=T_b(x,y)$ falsifies it. Since $x$ satisfies it, choose $i\in S$ with $x_i\ne a_i$. Since $z_i=a_i$, the other input must have $y_i=a_i$, and $b_i=a_i$. Similarly, satisfaction by $y$ supplies $k\in S$ with $y_k\ne a_k$, $x_k=a_k$, and $b_k=a_k$. The coordinates $i,k$ are distinct because $y_i=a_i$. Thus $b$ agrees with $a$ twice, a contradiction.

Therefore $T_b$ preserves every $C_t$, hence their conjunction $F$, and therefore $X_\varphi$. $\square$

This theorem does not require enumerating all prime implicates. Choosing one prime subclause of each supplied clause suffices. Every such choice produces the same set of valid orientations.

3.2 A linear number of adaptive SAT queries

Let $N$ be the total number of literal occurrences after normalization.

Starting with $C=D_t$, consider each literal $\ell$ currently present. Query satisfiability of
$$ \varphi\land\bigwedge_{\lambda\in C\setminus\{\ell\}}\neg\lambda. $$
If the query is unsatisfiable, then $\varphi\models C\setminus\{\ell\}$, so delete $\ell$. Otherwise retain it.

The resulting clause remains an implicate. It is prime: when a literal was retained, the SAT answer supplied a model of $\varphi$ falsifying every other literal then present. That model still falsifies every other literal remaining at the end. Thus deleting that retained literal from the final clause destroys entailment.

There are at most $N$ queries across all supplied clauses. The promise excludes empty or singleton prime clauses: these would respectively make $\varphi$ unsatisfiable or force a coordinate.

After refinement, construct $H_F$ and solve it by the polynomial-time $2$-SAT algorithm. Theorem 3 proves the correctness of this existence decision and of the resulting orientation.

All query encodings and other work have bit length and running time polynomial in $m,L$.

3.3 Fixed clause width permits nonadaptive queries

Suppose every supplied clause has at most $k$ literals, where $k$ is fixed.

For each clause $D_t$ and each subclause $C\subseteq D_t$, prepare the SAT query
$$ \varphi\land\bigwedge_{\lambda\in C}\neg\lambda. $$
All these queries are determined before any answer is received. Their answers identify every entailed subclause of each $D_t$. Select an inclusion-minimal entailed subclause; it is a prime implicate.

There are at most $r2^k$ queries. For fixed $k$, this is polynomial in the input length. Constructing $H_F$ and solving $2$-SAT then decides existence. Consequently,
$$ \mathcal E\text{ restricted to fixed-width CNF} \in \mathsf P_{\parallel}^{\mathsf{NP}}. $$
Here $\mathsf P_{\parallel}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ means deterministic polynomial-time computation using polynomially many nonadaptive NP-oracle queries.

3.4 Two tractable restrictions

If the supplied CNF clauses are promised to be prime implicates, no SAT queries are needed: construct their orientation constraints directly and solve $2$-SAT.

For a promised $2$-CNF input, existence always holds. Choose any $b\in X_\varphi$. Each non-tautological binary clause forbids one assignment on its two coordinates. Since $b$ satisfies that clause, $b$ agrees with its falsifying assignment on at most one coordinate. The clause-preservation argument in Theorem 3 applies to every clause. Hence $b$ is valid.

The syntactic and semantic questions nevertheless differ, even under the coordinate promise. Consider
$$ \varphi= (\neg x_1\lor x_2) \land(x_1\lor\neg x_2) \land(x_1\lor\neg x_2\lor x_3) \land(x_1\lor\neg x_2\lor\neg x_3). $$
The last two clauses are redundant, so its domain is
$$ X_\varphi=\{x:x_1=x_2\}. $$
Every coordinate takes both values, and this domain is AND-closed.

But the displayed CNF is not syntactically renamable Horn. Under the substitution $x_j=y_j\oplus b_j$, a literal is positive precisely when $b_j$ equals its falsifying value. The first two clauses require $b_1=b_2$. Then exactly one of $x_1,\neg x_2$ becomes positive. The third clause consequently requires $b_3=1$, while the fourth requires $b_3=0$.

Prime refinement removes this syntactic obstruction by replacing both redundant clauses with $x_1\lor\neg x_2$.

4. General formulas: a polynomial-time SAT-oracle algorithm

The CNF refinement argument does not automatically apply to arbitrary formulas: an equivalent CNF over the same issues can have exponential size. We therefore establish the general upper bound separately.

Maintain a $2$-CNF $H$, initially empty, whose clauses are all necessary for validity.

  • Solve $H$. If unsatisfiable, reject. Otherwise select a satisfying orientation $b$.
  • Ask SAT whether there exist $x,y,z$ satisfying

$$ \varphi(x)\land\varphi(y)\land\neg\varphi(z) \land[z=T_b(x,y)]. $$
If unsatisfiable, accept.

  • Otherwise recover a counterexample by SAT self-reduction, using $O(m)$ additional queries.
  • Starting with the full forbidden assignment $z$, greedily delete coordinates while the remaining restriction is still forbidden. Each deletion test asks SAT whether some model of $\varphi$ extends the proposed smaller restriction.
  • Add the binary constraints of the resulting minimally forbidden restriction to $H$, and repeat.

Every query has polynomial bit length in $m,L$; this statement avoids assuming unit-length variable names.

The deletion procedure preserves forbiddenness. It yields a minimally forbidden restriction because an extension witnessing that a retained coordinate could not be deleted remains an extension after other restrictions are removed.

Every added clause is necessary by Lemma 2. Furthermore, its sufficiency proof shows that the current counterexample supplies two coordinates of the final restriction agreeing with $b$. Thus $b$ violates at least one newly added clause. Because $b$ satisfied the previous $H$, that clause was not already present.

At most $4\binom m2$ distinct clauses can be added. There are therefore $O(m^2)$ unsuccessful iterations, each using $O(m)$ SAT queries and polynomial additional work.

Rejection is sound because all accumulated constraints are necessary. Acceptance is sound because it directly verifies closure. Hence
$$ \mathcal E\in\mathsf P^{\mathsf{NP}}. $$

5. Promise-preserving hardness, including width-$5$ CNF

Let
$$ E=\{000,011,101,110\}. $$

Lemma 4. No orientation is valid for $E$.

Proof. The coordinate promise holds for $E$, so validity would imply $b\in E$ by Theorem 1.

Coordinatewise,
$$ T_b(x,y) = b\oplus\bigl((x\oplus b)\land(y\oplus b)\bigr). $$
This identity retains the common input value when the inputs agree and returns $b_j$ otherwise.

Set
$$ x=b\oplus011,\qquad y=b\oplus101. $$
Both have even parity and belong to $E$. But
$$ T_b(x,y) = b\oplus(011\land101) = b\oplus001, $$
which has odd parity. This contradicts closure. $\square$

Take an instance $\psi(u_1,\ldots,u_k)$ of $3$-CNF UNSAT. Introduce $v_1,v_2,v_3,s,t$, and let $\eta(v)$ be the $3$-CNF whose clauses exclude the four odd-parity assignments. Thus $\operatorname{Mod}(\eta)=E$.

Define
$$ \varphi_\psi(u,v,s,t) = (\neg s\lor t)\lor\bigl(\psi(u)\land\eta(v)\bigr). $$
Its encoding length is polynomial in that of $\psi$.

The coordinate promise holds. Setting $s=0$ allows every assignment to $u,v,t$. Setting $s=t=1$ supplies feasible assignments with $s=1$.

If $\psi$ is unsatisfiable, then
$$ \varphi_\psi\equiv\neg s\lor t. $$
This domain is AND-closed: an AND output with $s=1$ comes from two inputs with $s=1$, which must both have $t=1$. The output therefore also has $t=1$. Theorem 1 gives a YES instance.

If $\psi$ is satisfiable, fix a satisfying assignment $u=a$ and fix $s=1,t=0$. The feasible $v$-vectors on this slice are exactly $E$. Every $T_b$ preserves coordinates fixed identically in both inputs. Global closure would therefore imply closure of this slice under the restriction of $T_b$ to its three $v$-coordinates. Lemma 4 rules this out.

Consequently,
$$ \psi\text{ unsatisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \varphi_\psi\in\mathcal E. $$
This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from the standard $\mathsf{coNP}$-complete problem $3$-CNF UNSAT.

It also produces width-$5$ CNF inputs. If
$$ \psi\land\eta=\bigwedge_D D, $$
then distributivity gives
$$ \varphi_\psi\equiv\bigwedge_D(\neg s\lor t\lor D). $$
Every $D$ has at most three literals, so every resulting clause has at most five. Thus the additional nonadaptive upper bound applies to a restriction that is already $\mathsf{coNP}$-hard.

6. Represented societies and finite arity

Once a valid $b$ is available, evaluating the constructed rule on a valid represented society requires only folding AND or OR across its positive support. Marginal arithmetic is unnecessary. Reading the representation and folding its types takes
$$ O(m\tau+L_\mu) $$
time. Here $\tau$ is exactly the listed support size.

The finite-arity existence question in the [cited aggregation paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1711.01574v3) does not imply rational-distribution existence. The relation $E$ demonstrates this directly.

Coordinatewise ternary XOR is anonymous and unanimous:
$$ a\oplus a\oplus a=a. $$
It preserves $E$, because the parity of the output is the sum modulo $2$ of the three input parities. Nevertheless, Lemma 4 excludes the rational-distribution maps for $E$.

7. Proven conclusion and remaining gap

If $\mathcal E$ were in $\mathsf P$, the promise-preserving UNSAT reduction would put UNSAT, and therefore SAT, in $\mathsf P$. Conversely, if $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$, the SAT-oracle algorithm becomes polynomial-time. This proves
$$ \mathcal E\in\mathsf P \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}. $$

The same reduction proves $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions: query existence on $\varphi_\psi$ and negate the answer to decide SAT.

Negating an oracle answer is not a many-one reduction. I therefore leave the requested many-one $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness alternative open. The additional progress is the exact prime-refinement characterization for CNF and the nonadaptive upper bound for every fixed clause width.
