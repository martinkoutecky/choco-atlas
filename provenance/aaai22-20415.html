The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(\mathcal T\), rational masses \(\mu\) with \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\), rational AEG utilities \(v_{ab}\in\{-M,1\}\), and a support bound \(Q\), decide whether there exists a finite-support partition \(\lambda=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}\rho_\ell\delta_{z^\ell}\), with \(q\le Q\) and \(\sum_{\ell}\rho_\ell z^\ell=\mu\), such that no occupied coalition \(z\) admits a type-\(a\) move to an occupied coalition \(y\) satisfying \(z_a>0\), \(U_a(y)>U_a(z)\), and \(\operatorname{in}_a(y)\ge\operatorname{out}_a(y)\), where \(U_a(z)=\sum_bv_{ab}z_b\), \(\operatorname{in}_a(y)=\sum_{b:v_{ba}>0}y_b\), and \(\operatorname{out}_a(y)=\sum_{b:v_{ba}<0}y_b\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(\mathcal T\): the list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), with \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The other inputs are a positive binary-rational \(M\), directed utilities \(v_{ab}\in\{-M,1\}\) for all \(a,b\in\mathcal T\), including equal types, and a positive binary-encoded integer \(Q\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; express running times in \(\tau,Q,L\). No separate candidate parameter \(m\) occurs.
A proposed partition is \(\lambda=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}\rho_\ell\delta_{z^\ell}\), where \(\delta_z\) is a point mass, \(q\le Q\), and \(\sum_\ell\rho_\ell z^\ell=\mu\). The admissible domains and normalization of \(z^\ell\) and \(\rho_\ell\) remain unspecified.
For a coalition vector \(z\), define \(U_a(z)=\sum_bv_{ab}z_b\), \(\operatorname{in}_a(z)=\sum_{b:v_{ba}>0}z_b\), and \(\operatorname{out}_a(z)=\sum_{b:v_{ba}<0}z_b\).
A partition is stable if no occupied source \(z\), occupied destination \(y\), and type \(a\) satisfy \(z_a>0\), \(U_a(y)>U_a(z)\), and \(\operatorname{in}_a(y)\ge\operatorname{out}_a(y)\). Utility improvement is strict, consent ties permit entry, and only occupied destinations are tested.
Is deciding the existence of such a stable partition with support at most \(Q\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
For finite societies of \(n\) agents, existence of a majority-in stable partition is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete when off-diagonal utilities belong to \(\{-n,1\}\), with deviations also permitting singleton formation.
