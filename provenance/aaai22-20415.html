The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(\mathcal T\), rational masses \(\mu\) with \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\), rational AEG utilities \(v_{ab}\in\{-M,1\}\), and a support bound \(Q\), decide whether there exists a finite-support partition \(\lambda=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}\rho_\ell\delta_{z^\ell}\), with \(q\le Q\) and \(\sum_{\ell}\rho_\ell z^\ell=\mu\), such that no occupied coalition \(z\) admits a type-\(a\) move to an occupied coalition \(y\) satisfying \(z_a>0\), \(U_a(y)>U_a(z)\), and \(\operatorname{in}_a(y)\ge\operatorname{out}_a(y)\), where \(U_a(z)=\sum_bv_{ab}z_b\), \(\operatorname{in}_a(y)=\sum_{b:v_{ba}>0}y_b\), and \(\operatorname{out}_a(y)=\sum_{b:v_{ba}<0}y_b\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by its support \(\mathcal T\): the \(\tau\ge1\) types of positive mass, with binary rational masses \(\mu_a>0\) satisfying \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\). Here \(m=\tau\) is the utility-matrix dimension, not a universe of possible types.
The input also supplies a rational coefficient \(M>0\), directed utilities \(v_{ab}\in\{-M,1\}\), and a binary integer \(Q\ge1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length. Diagonal entries describe interactions between distinct individuals of the same type.
Interpret a coalition profile as an actual mass vector \(z\in\prod_a[0,\mu_a]\setminus\{0\}\). Under the positive-measure interpretation, a partition is \(\lambda=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}\rho_\ell\delta_{z^\ell}\), with distinct profiles, real weights \(\rho_\ell>0\), \(q\le Q\), and \(\sum_\ell\rho_\ell z^\ell=\mu\); no probability normalization is imposed on \(\lambda\).
Define \(U_a(z)=\sum_bv_{ab}z_b\), \(\operatorname{in}_a(y)=\sum_{b:v_{ba}>0}y_b\), and \(\operatorname{out}_a(y)=\sum_{b:v_{ba}<0}y_b\).
A partition is majority-in stable if there are no occupied profile \(z\), type \(a\) with \(z_a>0\), and destination \(y\in\operatorname{supp}(\lambda)\cup\{0\}\) such that \(U_a(y)>U_a(z)\) and \(\operatorname{in}_a(y)\ge\operatorname{out}_a(y)\). The zero destination represents forming a singleton; utility ties prevent a move, and voting ties permit it. Moves are evaluated for an infinitesimal individual, without consent from the abandoned coalition.
Is deciding the existence of such a partition in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,Q,L\), including any dependence on \(M\).
For finite agents, existence of a majority-in stable partition is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete when distinct-agent utilities belong to \(\{-n,1\}\), where \(n\) is the agent count; the corresponding majority-out problem is also \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.
