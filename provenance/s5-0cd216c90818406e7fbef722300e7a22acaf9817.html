The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given providers \(N\), supported visitor types \(\mathcal T\subseteq 2^N\), rational masses \(\mu_A>0\) with \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal T}\mu_A=1\), and per-capita-scaled provider costs \(C_i\), compute each type's aggregate charge \(y_A=\mu_A\sum_{i\in A} C_i/d_i\), where \(d_i=\sum_{A\in\mathcal T:i\in A}\mu_A>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(N=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(m\geq1\), be the providers. A visitor type \(A\subseteq N\) specifies exactly the providers used; the empty type is allowed.
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) distinct types \(\mathcal T\) with positive masses \(\mu_A\), each a binary rational, and \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal T}\mu_A=1\). Here \(\tau=|\mathcal T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each provider has a nonnegative binary rational cost \(C_i\), expressed per unit of total population; equivalently, a population of size \(q\) has total provider cost \(qC_i\). Require \(d_i=\sum_{A\in\mathcal T:i\in A}\mu_A>0\) for every provider.
Compute exactly the aggregate charge of every supported type, \(y_A=\mu_A\sum_{i\in A}C_i/d_i\), with an empty sum equal to zero. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including explicitly encoded subsets, masses, and costs.

Is exact computation of \((y_A)_{A\in\mathcal T}\) possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite visitors \(V\), usage sets \(K(j)\subseteq N\), and nonnegative provider costs \(c_i\), the cost game \(c(S)=\sum_{i\in\bigcup_{j\in S}K(j)}c_i\) has Shapley charges \(x_j=\sum_{i\in K(j)}c_i/|\{k\in V:i\in K(k)\}|\), assuming every provider is used ([Wang, 2011](https://doi.org/10.4236/ajor.2011.12008)).
No result settling the exact continuous computation problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Provider costs were unspecified in sign and encoding; require nonnegative binary rationals, preserving the discrete model's allowance of zero costs.
  • Interpret per-capita-scaled costs as total provider costs divided by total population, so normalized aggregate charges use the stated formula.
  • Allow the empty visitor type, as the discrete usage mapping permits it; its charge is zero.
  • Use m for the positive number of providers and L for the total encoded input length; require exact rational output.

Conclusion

Exact computation of the entire vector \((y_A)_{A\in\mathcal T}\) is possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The key point is that both the intermediate integers and the exact outputs have polynomially bounded bit lengths.

We use the standard binary encoding of rational numbers by integer numerators and positive integer denominators. The proof also applies to binary fixed-point encodings, whose conversion to this representation has polynomial cost.

An exact algorithm

Write the input masses and costs as
\[ \mu_A=\frac{a_A}{b_A}, \qquad C_i=\frac{u_i}{v_i}, \]
where \(a_A,b_A,v_i\) are positive integers and \(u_i\) is a nonnegative integer. Reduction to lowest terms is unnecessary.

First form a common denominator for the masses:
\[ D=\prod_{A\in\mathcal T}b_A, \qquad s_A=a_A\frac{D}{b_A}. \]
Every division here is exact, and \(\mu_A=s_A/D\). The normalization hypothesis gives
\[ \sum_{A\in\mathcal T}s_A=D. \]

For each provider, compute the integer
\[ T_i=\sum_{\substack{A\in\mathcal T\\i\in A}}s_A. \]
Then \(d_i=T_i/D\). Since every provider has positive usage mass, and all masses are positive,
\[ 1\leq T_i\leq D. \]

Next form
\[ E=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(v_iT_i). \]
This is a positive integer. For each supported type, compute
\[ Z_A=s_A\sum_{i\in A}u_i\frac{E}{v_iT_i}. \]
Again, every division is exact. Output the rational number \(Z_A/E\), optionally reduced to lowest terms. For the empty type, the sum is empty and the output is zero.

To verify correctness, substitute the definitions:
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{Z_A}{E} &=s_A\sum_{i\in A}\frac{u_i}{v_iT_i}\\ &=\frac{s_A}{D}\sum_{i\in A} \frac{u_i/v_i}{T_i/D}\\ &=\mu_A\sum_{i\in A}\frac{C_i}{d_i}\\ &=y_A. \end{aligned} \]
Thus the algorithm computes exactly the requested charges.

Bit-length bounds

We now bound the arithmetic cost, rather than merely counting rational operations.

Because \(L\) includes the binary encodings of all input integers, their total bit length is at most \(L\). In particular,
\[ D\leq 2^L, \qquad \prod_{i=1}^{m}v_i\leq 2^L, \qquad u_i\leq 2^L. \]
Normalization and positivity imply \(s_A\leq D\), and we already proved \(T_i\leq D\). Consequently,
\[ E=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}v_i\right) \left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}T_i\right) \leq 2^{(m+1)L}. \]

Each quotient \(E/(v_iT_i)\) is at most \(E\). Therefore, for every supported type,
\[ 0\leq Z_A \leq s_A\,m\,2^L E \leq m\,2^{(m+3)L}. \]
It follows that all output numerators and denominators have
\[ O\bigl(mL+\log(m+1)\bigr) \]
bits.

The same bound applies to every intermediate integer in the stated algorithm: partial products are bounded by their final products, and partial sums are bounded by their final sums because all summands are nonnegative.

Set
\[ B=(m+3)L+\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil+2. \]
All arithmetic can therefore be performed on integers with at most \(B\) bits.

Running time and output size

Constructing \(D\) and the \(s_A\), accumulating the \(T_i\), constructing \(E\), and computing every \(Z_A\) uses
\[ O(m\tau+m+\tau) \]
integer additions, multiplications, and exact divisions. Membership in the explicitly encoded subsets can be recorded in an incidence table in polynomial time.

Elementary binary addition, multiplication, and long division on integers of at most \(B\) bits each take polynomial time; multiplication and division have an elementary \(O(B^2)\) bound. Thus the unreduced exact output can be computed in
\[ O\bigl(L+(m\tau+m+\tau)B^2\bigr) \]
time.

If reduced fractions are required, compute \(\gcd(Z_A,E)\) for each type and divide both integers by it. The Euclidean algorithm has polynomial bit complexity: its positive remainders decrease by at least a factor of two every two steps, so there are \(O(B)\) divisions, giving the sufficient bound \(O(B^3)\) per fraction.

Finally, the entire output has at most \(O(\tau B)\) bits. Both computation time and output length are therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), completing the proof.
