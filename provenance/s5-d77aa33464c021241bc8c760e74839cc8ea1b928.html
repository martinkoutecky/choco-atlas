The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(L>0\) and a rational finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over location types \(x\in[0,L]\), define \(\operatorname{ER}(y,\mu)=\max_{x\ne x'\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\frac{L-|y-x|}{L-|y-x'|}\), with value \(1\) for singleton support, and \(\operatorname{ER}^*(\mu)=\min_{y\in[0,L]}\operatorname{ER}(y,\mu)\). Does the rule \(F(\mu)=L/2\) give a deterministic mass-group-strategyproof mechanism with \(\sup_\mu \operatorname{ER}(F(\mu),\mu)/\operatorname{ER}^*(\mu)\le 2\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix a public binary rational \(L>0\). A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) distinct rational location types \(x_i\in[0,L]\), each with positive binary rational mass \(p_i\), where \(\sum_i p_i=1\). Write \(\mu=\sum_i p_i\delta_{x_i}\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(B\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(\tau,B\) and any other parameters (there is no candidate-count parameter \(m\)).
A deterministic mechanism maps each such society to a facility location in \([0,L]\), without payments. A type \(x\) has cost \(|y-x|\) and utility \(u(y,x)=L-|y-x|\).
A coalition selects rational masses \(c_i\in[0,p_i]\) with \(\sum_i c_i>0\), and may split each selected mass among finitely many rational reports in \([0,L]\), preserving its total mass. The resulting society \(\mu'\) retains all unselected mass. A mechanism \(G\) is mass-group-strategyproof if every such deviation has some \(i\) with \(c_i>0\) and \(|G(\mu')-x_i|\ge |G(\mu)-x_i|\); thus a deviation is forbidden only when every participating type strictly benefits.
Define \(\operatorname{ER}(y,\mu)=\max_{x\ne x'\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}u(y,x)/u(y,x')\), with value \(1\) for singleton support and \(a/0=+\infty\) for \(a>0\). Define \(\operatorname{ER}^*(\mu)=\min_{y\in[0,L]}\operatorname{ER}(y,\mu)\).

Does \(F(\mu)=L/2\) give a deterministic mass-group-strategyproof mechanism satisfying \(\sup_{\mu}\operatorname{ER}(F(\mu),\mu)/\operatorname{ER}^*(\mu)\le 2\)?

For finite agent lists with at least two agents, the constant midpoint mechanism is group-strategyproof with approximation ratio exactly \(2\), and every deterministic strategyproof mechanism has approximation ratio at least \(2\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02330v1). No result settling this precise mass-coalition formulation was located in the search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the fixed interval length public and binary rational, and required rational locations as well as rational masses to specify a finite binary input; masses sum to one.
  • Defined mass coalitions by partial selection and finite splitting of rational mass among rational reports, retaining unselected mass and preserving total mass.
  • Used the discrete definition's prohibition on deviations that strictly benefit every participant, rather than the stronger weak-benefit convention.
  • Defined positive utility divided by zero as positive infinity; retained the stipulated singleton-support value of one, including when its utility is zero.

Result

Yes. The constant mechanism \(F(\mu)=L/2\) is deterministic and mass-group-strategyproof, and
\(\sup_{\mu}\operatorname{ER}(F(\mu),\mu)/\operatorname{ER}^*(\mu)=2\).

The proof below directly handles rational mass coalitions, including partial participation and split reports. It agrees with the finite-agent midpoint guarantee in the [cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02330v1).

1. Mass-group-strategyproofness

Fix any admissible society \(\mu\) and any admissible coalition deviation producing \(\mu'\). Since the mechanism is constant, \(F(\mu')=F(\mu)=L/2\).

Consequently, every participating type \(x_i\) satisfies
\(\lvert F(\mu')-x_i\rvert=\lvert F(\mu)-x_i\rvert\).
Because \(\sum_i c_i>0\), at least one participating type exists, and that type satisfies the required weak inequality. Thus \(F\) is mass-group-strategyproof.

This argument applies regardless of how selected masses are split among reports.

2. The optimum is finite and at least one

For singleton support, the definition gives \(\operatorname{ER}(y,\mu)=1\) for every location \(y\), so \(\operatorname{ER}^*(\mu)=1\).

Now suppose \(\tau\ge 2\). Define
\(U_{\max}(y)=\max_i(L-\lvert y-x_i\rvert)\) and
\(U_{\min}(y)=\min_i(L-\lvert y-x_i\rvert)\).

Both functions are continuous. Moreover, \(U_{\max}(y)>0\): a zero utility requires \(\lvert y-x_i\rvert=L\), which, for fixed \(y\in[0,L]\), can hold for at most one distinct location.

If \(U_{\min}(y)=0\), a distinct type has positive utility, so \(\operatorname{ER}(y,\mu)=+\infty\). Otherwise,
\(\operatorname{ER}(y,\mu)=U_{\max}(y)/U_{\min}(y)\).
Indeed, distinct types attain the two extrema when they differ; when they coincide, any two distinct types have ratio one. Therefore \(\operatorname{ER}(y,\mu)\ge 1\) everywhere.

This extended-valued function is lower semicontinuous: it is continuous wherever \(U_{\min}>0\), and tends to \(+\infty\) at any point where \(U_{\min}=0\), because \(U_{\max}\) remains positive there. Hence it attains its minimum on the compact interval \([0,L]\). The next section supplies a finite value, establishing \(1\le\operatorname{ER}^*(\mu)<+\infty\).

3. Approximation guarantee

For every support location \(x_i\in[0,L]\),
\(\lvert L/2-x_i\rvert\le L/2\), and hence
\(L/2\le u(L/2,x_i)\le L\).

Thus every ratio between utilities of distinct types is at most two, giving \(\operatorname{ER}(F(\mu),\mu)\le 2\). Together with \(\operatorname{ER}^*(\mu)\ge 1\), this proves
\(\operatorname{ER}(F(\mu),\mu)/\operatorname{ER}^*(\mu)\le 2\).

Singleton societies have approximation ratio one.

4. Sharpness

Consider the admissible society \(\mu=\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_{L/2}\). Its locations and masses are rational.

At the mechanism’s output \(L/2\), the two utilities are \(L/2\) and \(L\), so \(\operatorname{ER}(F(\mu),\mu)=2\).

At location \(L/4\), both utilities equal \(3L/4\), so \(\operatorname{ER}(L/4,\mu)=1\). The lower bound from Section 2 therefore gives \(\operatorname{ER}^*(\mu)=1\). This society attains approximation ratio two.

5. Running time

Because \(L\) is fixed and public, the mechanism outputs the fixed rational \(L/2\) in constant time, independent of \(\tau\) and \(B\). An implementation that first reads the entire input takes \(O(B)\) time. No enumeration of possible location types is needed.
