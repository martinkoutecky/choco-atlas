1. Certificates for stability

Fix the quotas at their original values, so $b'_j=b_j$. Write
$$ a_t=\sum_c x_{t,c}, \qquad y_j=\sum_u\sum_{d\in C_j}x_{u,d}. $$
Throughout this section, assume $x$ is feasible.

For an acceptable pair $(t,c)$, define two kinds of certificates.

A type certificate is the conjunction
$$ a_t=\mu_t, \qquad x_{t,d}=0\quad\text{for every }d\text{ with }c\succ_t d. $$

A quota certificate from a set $C_j$ containing $c$ is the conjunction
$$ y_j=b_j, \qquad x_{u,d}=0 \quad\text{whenever }d\in C_j\text{ and }t\succ_j u. $$

Lemma. A feasible assignment is stable if and only if every acceptable pair has a type certificate or at least one quota certificate.

Proof. Feasibility gives $a_t\leq\mu_t$. Consequently, the negation of
$$ a_t<\mu_t \quad\text{or}\quad x_{t,d}>0\text{ for some }d\text{ with }c\succ_t d $$
is precisely the type certificate.

Likewise, because $y_j\leq b_j$, the negation of the willingness condition for quota set $C_j$ is precisely its quota certificate. Negating the conjunction defining a blocking pair therefore gives a type certificate or a certificate from some containing quota set. Applying this separately to every acceptable pair proves the assertion. $\square$

All comparisons here are the original strict comparisons. No ties have been discarded.

2. Existence at the original quotas

The external theorem

I use the following form of Scarf’s lemma. Suppose
$$ B=[I_r\ M] $$
has $r+p$ columns, where $p>0$, and $h\geq0$. Assume
$$ \{\alpha\geq0:B\alpha=h\} $$
is bounded. Let an ordinal matrix $R$ satisfy
$$ R_{ii}\leq R_{ik}\leq R_{ij} \quad \text{whenever }i,j\leq r,\ i\neq j,\ k>r. $$
Then there is a set $J$ of $r$ columns such that:

  • some nonnegative solution of $B\alpha=h$ is supported on $J$;
  • for every column $k$, some row $i$ satisfies

$$ R_{ik}\leq R_{iq}\quad\text{for every }q\in J. $$

For rational input, finding such a $J$ belongs to $\mathrm{PPAD}$. The existence statement and computational result are Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 of [Kintali, Poplawski, Rajaraman, Sundaram, and Teng](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.1435). Their formulation allows degeneracy and repeated ordinal entries.

Zero-quota preprocessing

If $b_j=0$, nonnegativity and feasibility force every assignment mass entering $C_j$ to vanish. Moreover, $C_j$ then supplies a quota certificate for every acceptable pair entering it: its load equals its quota, and it has no positive assigned mass.

Delete every acceptable pair whose college belongs to a zero-quota set, fixing its mass at zero, and remove the zero-quota rows. If no acceptable pairs remain, the zero assignment is stable by the preceding observation.

Otherwise, let $p>0$ be the number of remaining pairs. We construct a Scarf instance.

The incidence matrix

Create one row for each type and one row for each remaining quota set. Let $r$ be the number of rows. For an assignment column $e=(u,c)$, put
$$ M_{t,e}=\mathbf 1_{\{t=u\}}, \qquad M_{j,e}=\mathbf 1_{\{c\in C_j\}}. $$
The two formulas refer respectively to type rows and quota rows.

Set
$$ h_t=\mu_t,\qquad h_j=b_j. $$
Every coordinate of $h$ is positive: the support masses are positive, and zero-quota rows have been removed.

The assignment inequalities are exactly $Mx\leq h$. Introducing slack variables $s$ gives
$$ B=[I_r\ M], \qquad \alpha=(s,x), \qquad B\alpha=h. $$
This feasible set is nonempty because $(s,x)=(h,0)$ is feasible. It is bounded: every assignment coordinate is bounded by its type’s mass, and nonnegativity gives
$$ 0\leq s_i\leq h_i. $$

The ordinal matrix

For each row, strictly refine its weak order on incident assignment columns, preserving all original strict comparisons:

  • a type row orders its colleges by that type’s preferences;
  • a quota row orders assignment columns by the master priority of their types.

Columns originally tied may be ordered arbitrarily. Let $\rho_i(e)$ be distinct ranks between $1$ and $p$ for the incident columns in row $i$, with larger ranks meaning more preferred.

Define $R$ as follows:
$$ R_{ii}=0, \qquad R_{ij}=p+2\quad(j\leq r,\ j\neq i), $$
and, for assignment columns,
$$ R_{i,e}= \begin{cases} \rho_i(e),&e\text{ is incident in row }i,\\ p+1,&e\text{ is nonincident in row }i. \end{cases} $$
Every assignment-column entry lies between $1$ and $p+1$, so the ordinal inequalities required by Scarf’s lemma hold.

Apply that lemma to obtain $J$ and a feasible $\alpha=(s,x)$ supported on $J$.

Decoding domination into stability

Fix a remaining acceptable pair $e=(t,c)$. Choose a witnessing row $i$ such that
$$ R_{i,e}\leq R_{i,q}\quad\text{for every }q\in J. $$

Because $h_i>0$ and $B_i\alpha=h_i$, at least one column $q\in J$ contributes positively to row $i$. Such a column is either its own slack column, whose ordinal entry is zero, or an incident assignment column, whose entry is at most $p$.

Thus $e$ cannot be nonincident in row $i$: its ordinal entry would then be $p+1$, contradicting the witnessing inequality.

Since $e$ is incident, $R_{i,e}>0$. The same inequality excludes row $i$’s own slack column from $J$. The solution is supported on $J$, so $s_i=0$: row $i$ is saturated.

Finally, every positive assignment column incident in row $i$ belongs to $J$ and has rank at least $\rho_i(e)$. If such a column were strictly worse than $e$ in the original order, preservation of strict comparisons would give it a smaller rank, a contradiction.

Therefore:

  • if $i$ is a type row, it supplies a type certificate for $e$;
  • if $i$ is a quota row, it supplies a quota certificate for $e$.

Every remaining pair has a certificate. Restoring the deleted coordinates as zero supplies certificates for the deleted pairs through their zero-quota sets. Section 1 now proves stability at the original quotas.

Rationality and polynomial encoding length

The preceding existence argument initially permits real coordinates. Rational coordinates of polynomial encoding length can be obtained without losing the certificates.

Fix $J$ and consider the nonempty bounded polytope
$$ Q_J=\{\alpha\geq0:B\alpha=h,\ \alpha_q=0\text{ for }q\notin J\}. $$
Choose a vertex of this polytope. Its positive support columns are linearly independent. Indeed, a nonzero dependence among them would allow sufficiently small perturbations in both directions while keeping all positive coordinates nonnegative, contradicting extremality.

If that support has size $s\leq r$, select $s$ rows giving a nonsingular square coefficient matrix $A$. Its entries belong to $\{0,1\}$.

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the coordinates of $h$, and write $H=Dh$. Both $D$ and the integer coordinates of $H$ have polynomial bit length in the input. Cramer’s rule expresses each positive coordinate as
$$ \frac{\det A^{(k)}}{D\det A}, $$
where $A^{(k)}$ replaces one column of $A$ by the appropriate coordinates of $H$. Hadamard’s inequality gives
$$ |\det A|\leq s^{s/2}, \qquad |\det A^{(k)}|\leq s^{s/2}\max_i|H_i|. $$
Thus the coordinates have polynomial encoding length.

Moreover, once $J$ is found, rational linear programming finds a supported feasible solution in polynomial time. The certificate argument above applies to every feasible solution supported on $J$.

The construction has
$$ p\leq\tau m,\qquad r\leq\tau+N, $$
and ordinal entries require only $O(\log(p+2))$ bits. Hence it is polynomial in the explicit input size, using the given support size $\tau$.

We have proved both fixed-quota rational existence and membership of the fixed-quota search problem in $\mathrm{PPAD}$.

Consequence for the objective

The objective is nonnegative. The constructed rational stable assignment, with $b'=b$, attains zero. Therefore
$$ \boxed{ \min_{x,b'\text{ feasible and stable}} \max_j|b'_j-b_j|=0. } $$

Every optimizer must have $b'_j=b_j$ for every $j$. Producing an optimizer is consequently equivalent to finding a stable rational assignment at the original quotas.

The threshold decision problem is also trivial: a threshold $\delta$ is attainable exactly when $\delta\geq0$.

3. Hardness, including quota sets of size at most four

The source problem

A hypergraphic preference system consists of a finite hypergraph $H=(V,E)$ with nonempty edges and, for each vertex $v$, a strict total order $\succ_v$ on its incident edges.

A fractional matching is a vector $z\geq0$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{e\ni v}z_e\leq1 \quad\text{for every }v. $$
It is stable when, for every edge $e$, there is a vertex $v\in e$ such that
$$ z_e+\sum_{\substack{f\ni v\\f\succ_v e}}z_f=1. $$

Finding such a matching is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-complete even when each vertex belongs to at most three edges. This is Theorem 2, with the stability definition from Section 2, of [Ishizuka and Kamiyama](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol123-isaac2018/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2018.11/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2018.11.pdf).

We reduce from this restricted problem.

Empty instances and padding

If $E=\varnothing$, map the instance to one college, its singleton quota zero, and one type of mass one accepting no college. Decode any optimizer to the empty matching. This explicitly handles the empty edge list.

For $E\neq\varnothing$, discard isolated vertices. Pad with disjoint singleton edges on new vertices until
$$ K:=|E| $$
is a power of two. Here and below, $E$ denotes the padded edge list. The padding adds fewer than the original number of edges and preserves the maximum vertex degree bound.

Each added singleton edge has weight one in every stable matching: its sole vertex has no other incident edge, so its stability equation requires that weight. Restricting a stable matching to the original edges preserves all their stability equations. Padding therefore preserves the search problem, and makes $1/K$ dyadic.

The college instance

For every edge $e$, introduce a type $t_e$ and a college $c_e$, with
$$ \mu_{t_e}=\frac1K, \qquad A_{t_e}=\{c_e\}. $$
College $c_e$ accepts only $t_e$.

For each vertex $v$, introduce a marker college $d_v$ accepting no type.

Include all singleton quota sets, with
$$ b_{\{c_e\}}=\frac1K, \qquad b_{\{d_v\}}=0. $$
Also include, for each vertex $v$, the quota set
$$ S_v=\{d_v\}\cup\{c_e:v\in e\}, \qquad b_{S_v}=\frac1K. $$

Specify every priority order as follows:

  • College $c_e$ has the one-element order on $t_e$; marker colleges have empty acceptable-type orders.
  • The singleton master order for $\{c_e\}$ has its one acceptable type $t_e$; the marker singleton has an empty order on acceptable types.
  • On the incident types of $S_v$, order $t_e$ above $t_f$ exactly when $e\succ_v f$.

If the input convention requires master orders on all support types, extend each listed order to a strict total order on all types, placing unlisted types afterward in a fixed arbitrary order. For an empty listed order, use that fixed order.

These specifications satisfy priority consistency. A member college accepts at most one type, so its restricted order agrees with every containing master order.

All remaining input conditions also hold:

  • There are $K$ positive masses of size $1/K$, summing to one.
  • Every quota set is nonempty and every singleton is present.
  • Marker colleges make the sets $S_v$ distinct.
  • Since each vertex belongs to at most three edges,

$$ |S_v|\leq4. $$

  • College $c_e$ belongs to its singleton and the sets corresponding to the vertices of $e$; college $d_v$ belongs to its singleton and $S_v$. Thus the incidence bound holds with

$$ \ell=\max\left\{2,\ 1+\max_{e\in E}|e|\right\}. $$

The bound on quota-set size does not assert a constant bound on $\ell$. The latter remains an input parameter, as allowed in the question. The complete construction has polynomial size.

Feasibility correspondence

The only potentially nonzero assignment coordinates are $x_{t_e,c_e}$. Define
$$ z_e=Kx_{t_e,c_e}. $$
The constraints for $S_v$ become exactly
$$ \sum_{e\ni v}z_e\leq1. $$

The type and singleton constraints give $z_e\leq1$. These are redundant in the hypergraph because each edge is nonempty and hence meets some vertex whose matching constraint already implies this bound.

Consequently, scaling establishes a correspondence between feasible hypergraph matchings and feasible assignments at the original quotas.

Stability correspondence

Fix an edge $e$.

If $z_e=1$, type $t_e$ is fully assigned to its sole acceptable college, giving a type certificate. In the hypergraph, feasibility forces every other edge incident to any vertex of $e$ to have zero weight. Thus the hypergraph stability equation holds at every vertex of $e$.

Suppose instead that $z_e<1$. Type $t_e$ has unmatched mass, so it has no type certificate. Its singleton quota is slack, so that singleton supplies no quota certificate.

The remaining containing quota sets are precisely $S_v$ for $v\in e$. Such a set supplies a certificate exactly when
$$ \sum_{f\ni v}z_f=1, \qquad z_f=0\quad\text{for every }f\ni v\text{ with }e\succ_v f. $$
Because the incident-edge order is strict and total, these conditions are equivalent to
$$ z_e+\sum_{\substack{f\ni v\\f\succ_v e}}z_f=1. $$
For the reverse implication, subtract this equation from the feasible total-load inequality. Nonnegativity forces every lower-ranked edge weight to vanish and the total load to equal one.

The certificate lemma therefore proves that assignment stability and fractional hypergraph stability correspond exactly.

Every optimizer has unchanged quotas by Section 2. Scaling its assignment by $K$ and removing padding edges yields a stable matching for the source instance in polynomial time. This proves $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hardness.

Together with membership, we obtain
$$ \boxed{ \text{Finding an optimal rational assignment is }\mathrm{PPAD}\text{-complete}. } $$
The hardness holds under the additional restrictions just proved.

4. A precise barrier to many-one SAT hardness

The previous sections also give a useful limitation on a natural decision-to-search interpretation of $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness.

Let an instance $I$ have a valid optimizer witness $w$ consisting of rational assignment masses and unchanged quotas. There is a polynomial $q$ such that every instance has such a witness of length at most $q(|I|)$, by Section 2.

Validity of a proposed witness is checkable in polynomial time: check nonnegativity, acceptability, the mass and quota inequalities, unchanged quotas, and the stability certificates for every acceptable pair. All calculations use explicitly given rational numbers and orders.

Consider a hypothetical reduction with polynomial-time functions $f$ and $g$ such that:

  • $f$ maps every Boolean formula $\varphi$ to a valid instance;
  • for every valid optimizer $w$ of $f(\varphi)$, $g(\varphi,w)$ returns the correct Boolean answer to whether $\varphi$ is satisfiable.

Proposition. Such a reduction implies $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$.

Proof. A nondeterministic verifier for unsatisfiability guesses an optimizer $w$ of $f(\varphi)$ of length at most $q(|f(\varphi)|)$, verifies it, and accepts exactly when
$$ g(\varphi,w)=0. $$

If $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable, a short optimizer exists by totality, and the decoder returns zero for it. Hence an accepting certificate exists.

If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, the decoder returns one for every valid optimizer. Hence no guessed witness can cause acceptance.

Thus unsatisfiability belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$. Since it is $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete, this implies $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$. $\square$

The proposition concerns the explicitly defined reduction, which must recover SAT’s answer from every optimizer. It does not establish an unconditional prohibition on all possible notions of oracle or search hardness.
