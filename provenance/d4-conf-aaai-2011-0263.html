The continuous mirror question, as graded

Fix a finite set \(J\) of item categories and a finite set \(\Theta\) of gross-substitutes valuations over integral bundles \(B\subseteq J\), with per-capita supplies \(q_j\). For populations of size \(N\), give category \(j\) an integer supply approximating \(Nq_j\), and let the empirical type distribution converge to \(\mu\in\Delta(\Theta)\). Among sequences of anonymous finite-population VCG redistribution mechanisms that are strategy-proof, individually rational, and non-deficit at every \(N\), find a limiting per-type rebate rule \(\beta_\theta(\mu)\) maximizing \(\inf_{\mu:\,p(\mu)>0}\frac{\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta\beta_\theta(\mu)}{p(\mu)}\), where \(p(\mu)\) is limiting VCG revenue per capita and allocations assign integral bundles to each unit of mass subject to the category supplies.

What is the sharp worst-case limiting redistribution guarantee \(\sup_{(r_N)}\inf_{\mu\in\Delta(\Theta)\cap\mathbb{Q}^{|\Theta|}:p(\mu)>0}\frac{\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\beta_\theta(\mu)}{p(\mu)}\) achievable by admissible sequences?

The sharp limiting guarantee is \(1\)

The guarantee is attained by Cavallo’s rebate, provided that the counterfactual auction retains the inventory of the original population.

In fact, a stronger statement holds: for every rational society, sufficiently large exact replications redistribute all VCG revenue. The threshold may depend on the society.

1. Welfare and the gross-substitutes facts used

For an integer inventory \(c=(c_j)_{j\in J}\) and a type-count vector \(h\), let \(W_c(h)\) denote optimal integral welfare. Define its fractional relaxation by

\( F(t,c)=\max\left\{\sum_{\theta,B}v_\theta(B)y_{\theta B}: y_{\theta B}\geq0,\ \sum_B y_{\theta B}=t_\theta,\ \sum_{\theta,B:j\in B}y_{\theta B}\leq c_j \right\}. \)

Linear-programming duality gives

\[ F(t,c)= \min\left\{ \sum_\theta t_\theta u_\theta+\sum_j c_j\pi_j: u_\theta\geq0,\ \pi_j\geq0,\ u_\theta+\sum_{j\in B}\pi_j\geq v_\theta(B) \right\}. \tag{1} \]

We use three standard gross-substitutes facts:

  • A valuation satisfies gross substitutes precisely when it satisfies the exchange condition: for \(A,B\) and \(a\in A\setminus B\), either

\( v(A)+v(B)\leq v(A\setminus\{a\})+v(B\cup\{a\}), \)
or there exists \(b\in B\setminus A\) such that
\( v(A)+v(B)\leq v((A\setminus\{a\})\cup\{b\})+ v((B\setminus\{b\})\cup\{a\}). \)

  • Its indirect utility

\( U(\pi)=\max_{B\subseteq J}\left(v(B)-\sum_{j\in B}\pi_j\right) \)
is submodular in prices:
\( U(\pi)+U(\sigma)\geq U(\pi\vee\sigma)+U(\pi\wedge\sigma). \)

These are the one-sided specializations of Theorems 9 and 6 of [Hatfield et al., *Full Substitutability*](https://web.stanford.edu/~ost/papers/fs2.pdf).

  • For finitely many labeled, indivisible goods and gross-substitutes buyers, the configuration LP has an integral optimal solution. This follows from Walrasian-equilibrium existence and its configuration-LP characterization; see Theorem 9 and Lemma 2 of [Paes Leme–Wong](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-018-1334-9). These statements permit ties. Rational valuations satisfy the same conclusions by positive scaling to integer valuations.

Here is why these facts apply to the category capacities and consumption restriction in the question.

Integral welfare equals the compressed LP value

Replace category \(j\) by \(c_j\) labeled copies. For a set \(A\) of copies, let \(\rho(A)\) be its set of represented categories, and extend a buyer’s valuation to

\( \widetilde v(A)=v(\rho(A)). \)

This extension satisfies the exchange condition. Indeed, fix \(a\in A\setminus B\), of category \(j\).

  • If \(A\setminus\{a\}\) still contains category \(j\), transferring \(a\) cannot decrease the valuation sum, by free disposal.
  • If \(a\) is the only such copy in \(A\), but \(B\) contains category \(j\), exchange \(a\) with a copy of that category in \(B\). Both category sets remain unchanged.
  • Otherwise apply the exchange condition to \(\rho(A),\rho(B)\) and \(j\). A category transfer lifts directly. A category exchange lifts by choosing a corresponding copy in \(B\); if another copy of its category remains in \(B\), free disposal only improves the resulting inequality.

Thus the copy valuations are gross substitutes.

A feasible compressed fractional allocation lifts to the labeled configuration LP: distribute each type’s bundle masses equally among its agents, and choose the copy of each requested category uniformly. Every labeled copy of category \(j\) then has load at most \(c_j/c_j=1\). Categories with zero capacity have zero allocation.

Conversely, project any labeled fractional allocation through \(\rho\). Category usage weakly decreases and welfare is preserved. Hence the two fractional optimum values coincide. An integral optimum in the labeled market projects to a feasible original allocation after redundant copies are discarded. Therefore, for all integer \(h,c\),

\[ W_c(h)=F(h,c). \tag{2} \]

Welfare is submodular in the participating agents

Fix inventory \(c\), and write \(W(S)\) for welfare with participating agent set \(S\). By (1)–(2),

\[ W(S)=\min_{\pi\geq0} \left(c\cdot\pi+\sum_{i\in S}U_i(\pi)\right). \tag{3} \]

Let \(\pi,\sigma\) minimize this expression for \(S,T\), respectively. Such minimizers exist: prices can be capped at the maximum bundle value without increasing the objective.

For agents in \(S\cap T\), use price submodularity. For agents belonging to just one set, use the fact that indirect utility decreases when prices increase. Finally,

\( c\cdot\pi+c\cdot\sigma =c\cdot(\pi\vee\sigma)+c\cdot(\pi\wedge\sigma). \)

Consequently,

\[ W(S)+W(T) \geq \left[c\cdot(\pi\vee\sigma)+\sum_{i\in S\cup T}U_i(\pi\vee\sigma)\right] + \left[c\cdot(\pi\wedge\sigma)+\sum_{i\in S\cap T}U_i(\pi\wedge\sigma)\right] \geq W(S\cup T)+W(S\cap T). \tag{4} \]

This proof requires no uniqueness of allocations.

2. VCG revenue and an admissible rebate sequence

Let \(\mathcal R_c(h)\) denote VCG revenue with inventory \(c\), for any number of participating agents. Summing Clarke-pivot payments over any efficient assignment gives

\[ \mathcal R_c(h) = W_c(h)-\sum_{\theta:h_\theta>0} h_\theta\bigl(W_c(h)-W_c(h-e_\theta)\bigr). \tag{5} \]

Thus total revenue is independent of which efficient assignment is selected. In particular, uniform tie-breaking leaves total revenue unchanged.

Revenue is monotone under adding agents. Indeed, for an agent \(a\in S\), (5) gives

\[ \mathcal R_c(S)-\mathcal R_c(S\setminus\{a\}) = \sum_{i\in S\setminus\{a\}} \left[ W(S\setminus\{i\})+W(S\setminus\{a\}) -W(S)-W(S\setminus\{a,i\}) \right] \geq0, \tag{6} \]

where each summand is nonnegative by (4).

At population size \(N\), set

\[ c_N=(\lfloor Nq_j\rfloor)_{j\in J}, \qquad r_N(s)=\frac{\mathcal R_{c_N}(s)}{N}, \qquad \sum_\theta s_\theta=N-1. \tag{7} \]

The counterfactual revenue in (7) uses inventory \(c_N\), not inventory \(c_{N-1}\).

This defines an admissible finite mechanism:

  • Nonnegative rebates: Clarke-pivot payments are nonnegative, since the remaining agents could retain their assigned bundles when the removed agent is absent.
  • Non-deficit: By (6), for every full profile \(n\),

\( \sum_\theta n_\theta r_N(n-e_\theta) \leq \sum_\theta n_\theta\frac{\mathcal R_{c_N}(n)}N =R_N(n). \)

  • Strategy-proofness: Each rebate depends only on the other reports. With these reports fixed, truthful reporting makes every selected efficient assignment maximize the agent’s true Groves utility. Uniform selection among maximizers preserves this property.
  • Individual rationality: Truthful pivot utility is \(W(S)-W(S\setminus\{i\})\geq0\), and the rebate increases it.
  • Anonymity: Both the rebate and the required uniform allocation lottery are invariant under relabeling agents.

It remains to prove the rebate limits and their performance.

3. Exact welfare stabilization along a replication ray

Fix a rational society \(\mu\), with support \(T\) of size \(\tau\). Throughout this section, take \(N=kD\), so

\( n=N\mu,\qquad c_N=Nq. \)

Restrict the dual variables to types in \(T\), and choose

\( V=\max\left\{1,\max_{\theta\in T}v_\theta(J)\right\}. \)

The dual in (1) can be restricted to the compact polytope

\[ P=\left\{ (u,\pi): 0\leq u_\theta\leq V,\ 0\leq\pi_j\leq V,\ u_\theta+\sum_{j\in B}\pi_j\geq v_\theta(B) \right\}. \tag{8} \]

To justify this restriction, first replace \(u_\theta\) by \(U_\theta(\pi)\). Then cap every price exceeding \(V\) at \(V\). Any bundle containing a capped category still has nonpositive net value, while the empty bundle has net value zero; hence the cap does not change indirect utilities. The objective weakly decreases, and indirect utilities lie in \([0,V]\).

Let \(E\) be the finite vertex set of \(P\), and define

\( w=\min_{(u,\pi)\in P}(\mu\cdot u+q\cdot\pi), \qquad E_0=\{(u,\pi)\in E:\mu\cdot u+q\cdot\pi=w\}. \)

For a nonnegative deletion vector \(d\) supported on \(T\), put

\[ H(d)=\max_{(u,\pi)\in E_0}d\cdot u, \qquad a_\theta=H(e_\theta). \tag{9} \]

Claim. For all sufficiently large \(N=kD\), simultaneously for every integer \(d\geq0\) with \(\sum_\theta d_\theta\leq2\),

\[ W_{Nq}(N\mu-d)=Nw-H(d). \tag{10} \]

Proof. Choose \(N\) large enough that every supported count is at least two. If there are vertices outside \(E_0\), their objective gaps have a positive minimum

\( \delta=\min_{(u,\pi)\in E\setminus E_0} \bigl(\mu\cdot u+q\cdot\pi-w\bigr)>0. \)

At such a vertex, the perturbed objective is at least

\( N(w+\delta)-d\cdot u\geq Nw+N\delta-2V. \)

For \(N\delta>2V\), this exceeds \(Nw\). Every active vertex has perturbed objective at most \(Nw\), because \(d,u\geq0\). Thus only active vertices can minimize the perturbed objective, whose minimum is \(Nw-H(d)\).

If all vertices are active, this conclusion holds directly. Equation (2) converts the fractional optimum into integral welfare. This proves (10). \(\square\)

4. Submodularity makes the deletion losses additive

For supported types \(\alpha,\theta\), choose two distinct agents of those types; if the types coincide, choose two agents of that type. Applying (4) to their single-agent deletions gives

\( W_{Nq}(n-e_\alpha)+W_{Nq}(n-e_\theta) \geq W_{Nq}(n)+W_{Nq}(n-e_\alpha-e_\theta). \)

Substituting (10) yields

\[ H(e_\alpha+e_\theta)\geq a_\alpha+a_\theta. \tag{11} \]

The reverse inequality follows directly from (9):

\( H(e_\alpha+e_\theta) =\max_{E_0}(u_\alpha+u_\theta) \leq\max_{E_0}u_\alpha+\max_{E_0}u_\theta =a_\alpha+a_\theta. \)

Therefore,

\[ H(e_\alpha+e_\theta)=a_\alpha+a_\theta. \tag{12} \]

In particular, for sufficiently large exact replications,

\( W_{Nq}(n)-W_{Nq}(n-e_\theta)=a_\theta, \)

and

\[ W_{Nq}(n-e_\alpha)-W_{Nq}(n-e_\alpha-e_\theta)=a_\theta. \tag{13} \]

Thus deleting one agent leaves every remaining agent’s marginal welfare contribution unchanged.

5. Revenue and rebates eventually stabilize exactly

Define

\( P_\mu=w-\sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta a_\theta. \)

Equations (5), (10), and (13) imply

\[ R_N(N\mu) =Nw-\sum_{\theta\in T}N\mu_\theta a_\theta =NP_\mu. \tag{14} \]

For every supported type \(\alpha\), the same calculation after deletion gives

\[ \mathcal R_{Nq}(N\mu-e_\alpha) = Nw-a_\alpha- \sum_{\theta\in T} \left(N\mu_\theta-\mathbf1_{\theta=\alpha}\right)a_\theta =NP_\mu. \tag{15} \]

These identities hold for every sufficiently large \(N=kD\). They prove both existence of the required revenue limit and

\( p(\mu)=P_\mu. \)

By (7) and (15),

\( r_N(N\mu-e_\alpha)=P_\mu \)

eventually. Hence the rebate limits exist at every rational society, including zero-revenue societies, and satisfy

\[ \beta_\alpha(\mu)=p(\mu) \quad\text{for every supported }\alpha. \tag{16} \]

For every society with \(p(\mu)>0\), the resulting limiting redistribution fraction is therefore

\[ \frac{\sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta\beta_\theta(\mu)}{p(\mu)} = \frac{p(\mu)\sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta}{p(\mu)} =1. \tag{17} \]

6. Matching upper bound

For any admissible sequence, finite-profile non-deficit at \(N\mu\), divided by \(N\), gives

\( \sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta r_N(N\mu-e_\theta) \leq \frac{R_N(N\mu)}N. \)

Taking the prescribed limits is valid because the support is finite and all required limits exist. Consequently,

\( \sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta\beta_\theta(\mu)\leq p(\mu). \)

Every positive-revenue society therefore has limiting fraction at most \(1\). The assumed existence of such a society makes the infimum’s domain nonempty. Combining this bound with (17) proves

\( \boxed{ \sup_{(r_N)} \inf_{\substack{\mu\in\Delta(\Theta)\cap\mathbb Q^{|\Theta|}\\p(\mu)>0}} \frac{\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\beta_\theta(\mu)}{p(\mu)} =1. } \)

7. Exact computation and the boundary issue

The formula for \(p(\mu)\) is computationally explicit. First solve the bounded dual (8) for \(w\). Then, for each supported type \(\theta\), maximize \(u_\theta\) on its optimal face

\( \mu\cdot u+q\cdot\pi=w. \)

This computes \(a_\theta\), and hence \(p(\mu)\) and all supported rebate limits. There are \(\tau+1\) rational LPs, each with \(\tau+m\) variables and \(O(\tau2^m+\tau+m)\) constraints. Exact computation takes

\( (\tau+1)\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau2^m,|\Theta|,L) \)

bit operations. No accuracy parameter is needed.

Similarly, evaluating a finite rebate on an others-profile with support size \(t\) requires at most \(t+1\) compressed welfare LPs, using (5). Its value-computation time is

\( (t+1)\operatorname{poly}(m,t2^m,|\Theta|,L), \)

where here \(L\) includes the binary population size and counts. These are bounds for welfare, revenue, and rebate values.

Exact replication matters. For example, take two categories with \(q_1=q_2=1/4\), and additive types valuing category one at \(2\) and \(1\), respectively, and category two at zero. Let their masses be \(1/4\) and \(3/4\). At every exact replication, VCG revenue is \(N/4\). If instead the high-type count is increased by one, revenue becomes \(N/2\), although the empirical societies converge to the same \(\mu\).

Thus the proof establishes the requested exact-replication guarantee while fully allowing capacity boundaries and ties.
