The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many complete types \(T\) with masses \(\mu_t\ge0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), good classes \(G\) with per-capita supplies \(\sigma_g\), and restricted-additive values \(a_{t,g}\in\{0,w_g\}\), choose masses \(x_{t,\mathbf b}\ge0\) over integral configurations \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\) satisfying \(\sum_{\mathbf b}x_{t,\mathbf b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,\mathbf b}b_gx_{t,\mathbf b}=\sigma_g\). Minimize \(\lambda\), where \(v_t(\mathbf b)=\sum_hb_ha_{t,h}\), subject to \(x_{t,\mathbf b}>0\) and \(x_{u,\mathbf b'}>0\) implying \(v_t(\mathbf b'-\mathbf e_g)\le\lambda v_t(\mathbf b)\) for every \(g\) with \(b'_g>0\); decide whether \(\lambda\le\sqrt{2}\), equivalently whether a \(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)-EFX allocation exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) complete valuation types \(t\in T\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|G|\ge1\) good classes, with nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_g\) and weights \(w_g\). Each type specifies \(a_{t,g}\in\{0,w_g\}\) for every class. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Configurations are \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\), including the empty configuration, with \(v_t(\mathbf b)=\sum_g b_ga_{t,g}\). A complete allocation is a nonnegative real array \(x_{t,\mathbf b}\) satisfying \(\sum_{\mathbf b}x_{t,\mathbf b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,\mathbf b}b_gx_{t,\mathbf b}=\sigma_g\) for every \(g\); its support need not be finite.
It is \(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\)-EFX if, for every two occupied type–configuration pairs \((t,\mathbf b),(u,\mathbf b')\), including pairs with \(t=u\), and every \(g\) with \(b'_g>0\), one has \(v_t(\mathbf b'-\mathbf e_g)\le\sqrt2\,v_t(\mathbf b)\), where \(\mathbf e_g\) is the unit vector. Removal is required even when \(a_{t,g}=0\); equality is allowed.

Is deciding whether a complete \(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\)-EFX allocation exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents and individually listed goods with restricted-additive valuations, an algorithm guarantees a complete \(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\)-EFX allocation; the theorem does not establish a polynomial running time ([Kaviani, Seddighin, and Shahrezaei](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05139v2#S6)).
No result settling this exact mass-and-integral-configuration problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Supplies and common good weights were not encoded explicitly; both are nonnegative binary rationals, consistent with the discrete nonnegative valuation domain.
  • Zero-mass types are omitted from the input support; masses retain the stated normalization to one.
  • Allocation masses are nonnegative reals, and configuration support may be infinite; no unstated finite-support or bundle-size bound is imposed.
  • Empty configurations and zero-valued goods are allowed, and all supplies must be allocated.
  • EFX comparisons include distinct configurations occupied by the same type, allow equality, and require removal of every present good, including goods valued at zero by the comparing type.
  • The target retains the fixed approximation threshold and asks its decision complexity, rather than asking for exact optimization of the approximation factor.

Result

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable. Every valid input admits a complete \(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)-EFX allocation.

The finite-agent existence theorem suffices: rational masses and supplies can be represented exactly by a finite population and finitely many individually labeled goods. This representation may be exponentially large, but it is used only to prove existence.

1. The finite theorem has the required EFX convention

The theorem of Kaviani, Seddighin, and Shahrezaei guarantees that every finite restricted-additive instance admits a complete allocation \((X_i)_{i\in A}\) satisfying, for distinct agents \(i,j\) and every item \(h\in X_j\),

\(\displaystyle V_i(X_j\setminus\{h\})\le \sqrt{2}\,V_i(X_i).\)

Their definition quantifies over all items in the compared bundle, without requiring positive value to the comparing agent. Their restricted-additive valuations are nonnegative. Thus the theorem uses exactly the removal convention required here. See [Definition 2.1 and the EFX definition in Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05139v2#S2), and [Theorem 6.1 and its completeness step](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05139v2#S6).

For \(i=j\), the same inequality follows directly from nonnegativity:

\(\displaystyle V_i(X_i\setminus\{h\})\le V_i(X_i)\le\sqrt{2}\,V_i(X_i).\)

We now verify all hypotheses and transfer the resulting allocation.

2. Clearing denominators gives a finite instance

Choose a positive integer \(Q\) divisible by every denominator appearing in the masses \(\mu_t\) and supplies \(\sigma_g\). Define

\(\displaystyle n_t=Q\mu_t,\qquad k_g=Q\sigma_g.\)

Each \(n_t\) is a positive integer, each \(k_g\) is a nonnegative integer, and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}n_t=Q\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=Q.\)

Construct a finite instance with:

  • \(n_t\) distinct agents of type \(t\), for every \(t\in T\);
  • \(k_g\) distinct labeled items of class \(g\), for every \(g\in G\).

Write \(t(i)\) for agent \(i\)'s type. Assign that agent additive valuation

\(\displaystyle V_i(S)=\sum_{h\in S}a_{t(i),\,g(h)},\)

where \(g(h)\) is item \(h\)'s class. Every item of class \(g\) has value either \(0\) or \(w_g\) to every agent. Consequently these valuations are nonnegative and restricted additive, as required by the finite theorem.

If there are no items, assigning the empty configuration to every type immediately satisfies the task. Otherwise, apply the theorem to obtain a complete \(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)-EFX allocation \((X_i)_{i\in A}\).

3. Aggregation preserves the masses and supplies

For each agent \(i\), define its integral configuration by

\(\displaystyle b(i)_g=\bigl|\{h\in X_i:g(h)=g\}\bigr|.\)

Thus \(V_i(X_i)=v_{t(i)}(\mathbf b(i))\).

Define the allocation array

\(\displaystyle x_{t,\mathbf b}=\frac{1}{Q}\bigl|\{i\in A:t(i)=t,\ \mathbf b(i)=\mathbf b\}\bigr|.\)

This is nonnegative and has finite support. For every type \(t\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\mathbf b}x_{t,\mathbf b}=\frac{n_t}{Q}=\mu_t.\)

For every class \(g\), completeness of the finite allocation gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,\mathbf b}b_gx_{t,\mathbf b}=\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{i\in A}b(i)_g=\frac{k_g}{Q}=\sigma_g.\)

Hence \(x\) is complete and has exactly the prescribed type masses and supplies.

4. Aggregation preserves every required EFX comparison

Take any two occupied pairs \((t,\mathbf b)\) and \((u,\mathbf b')\), and any class \(g\) with \(b'_g>0\).

If the pairs are different, their positive masses ensure that there are distinct agents \(i,j\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle t(i)=t,\quad \mathbf b(i)=\mathbf b,\qquad t(j)=u,\quad \mathbf b(j)=\mathbf b'.\)

Choose a labeled item \(h\in X_j\) of class \(g\), which exists because \(b'_g>0\). By construction and finite-agent EFX,

\(\displaystyle v_t(\mathbf b'-\mathbf e_g)=V_i(X_j\setminus\{h\})\le\sqrt{2}\,V_i(X_i)=\sqrt{2}\,v_t(\mathbf b).\)

This applies also when \(t=u\): distinct agents of the same type remain subject to the finite theorem. It also applies when \(a_{t,g}=0\), because that theorem requires removal of every item.

If the two occupied pairs coincide, nonnegativity gives

\(\displaystyle v_t(\mathbf b-\mathbf e_g)=v_t(\mathbf b)-a_{t,g}\le v_t(\mathbf b)\le\sqrt{2}\,v_t(\mathbf b).\)

These cases exhaust all comparisons in the task. Therefore \(x\) is a complete \(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)-EFX allocation.

5. Decision complexity

On every input satisfying the stated hypotheses, the correct answer is YES. The decision algorithm therefore simply accepts valid inputs. If validity checking is included, checking the rational data, valuation restrictions, and mass normalization takes polynomial time in the input bit length \(L\).

The denominator-cleared instance can be very large, so this proof does not establish polynomial-time construction of an allocation. It completely settles the requested existence decision problem.
