The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite item set \(M\), \(\tau\) bidder types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, rational valuations \(v_t(S)\), bundle-size bound \(C\), and per-capita supplies \(q_j\), does the mass-based CCA achieve either \(R=\Omega(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu/(C^2\log(2+\tau)\log^2 m))\) or \(W=\Omega(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu/\log(2+\tau))\)? If not, what dependence on the smallest positive type mass or its bit length is necessary?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) contain \(m\ge2\) items and let \(1\le C\le m\). A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to one; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Supplies \(q_j>0\) are binary rationals. Each type has an explicit table of nonnegative rational values \(v_t(S)\), with \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\); monotonicity is not assumed. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; express any running times in \(m,\tau,L,C\) and the price-step encoding length.
A feasible mass allocation consists of \(y_{t,S}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_S y_{t,S}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}y_{t,S}\le q_j\). Define \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu=\max_y\sum_{t,S}y_{t,S}v_t(S)\), allowing all bundles.
Define the mass-based combinatorial clock auction as follows. Initially \(p_j^0=0\). In round \(r\), all mass of type \(t\) submits one bundle \(B_t^r\) maximizing strictly positive utility \(v_t(S)-p^r(S)\), where \(p^r(S)=\sum_{j\in S}p_j^r\); it drops out and submits no bid if the maximum is nonpositive, with \(B_t^r=\varnothing\) for bookkeeping. Demand ties are arbitrary; consider runs in which every submitted bundle has size at most \(C\).
Put \(D_j^r=\sum_{t:j\in B_t^r}\mu_t\). If the auction continues, update every price by \(p_j^{r+1}=p_j^r+\varepsilon D_j^r\), for a positive rational step \(\varepsilon\), including items without excess demand.
At each round, choose an arbitrary revenue-maximizing allocation of historical bids: \(x_{t,s}\ge0\), for submitted bids with \(s\le r\), subject to \(\sum_s x_{t,s}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,s:j\in B_t^s}x_{t,s}\le q_j\), maximizing \(\sum_{t,s}x_{t,s}p^s(B_t^s)\). Mass may split among historical bids, but each individual receives at most one bundle.
Stop exactly when \(D_j^r\le q_j\) and the chosen maximizer also satisfies \(D_j^r+\sum_{t,s:j\in B_t^s\setminus B_t^r}x_{t,s}\le q_j\) for every item. This requires feasibility of the union of each individual's current demand and assigned historical bundle. Output that maximizer, with revenue \(R=\sum_{t,s}x_{t,s}p^s(B_t^s)\) and welfare \(W=\sum_{t,s}x_{t,s}v_t(B_t^s)\).

For every instance and all sufficiently small positive rational \(\varepsilon\), does every such run achieve either \(R=\Omega(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu/(C^2\log(2+\tau)\log^2 m))\) or \(W=\Omega(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu/\log(2+\tau))\), with universal constants independent of masses and supplies?
If not, what dependence on \(\mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t\) or the binary encoding length of the masses is necessary in these approximation guarantees?

For finite bidders and unit item supplies, the demand-proportional CCA with the compatibility stopping rule satisfies the analogous revenue-or-welfare guarantee with \(\log n\), provided price increments are sufficiently small relative to the valuation grid. Conclusion

No. No dependence solely on \(\mu_{\min}\) or on the encoding length of the masses can repair these guarantees. A counterexample already exists with one type of mass one. The obstruction comes from heterogeneous supplies.

The literal rule also permits a zero-welfare stop in round zero when current demand is feasible and the empty revenue maximizer is chosen. The counterexample below avoids that issue: it forces excess demand until dropout and ends with strictly positive revenue.

Counterexample

Fix an integer \(k\ge1\), and put \(\delta=2^{-k}\). Take

\[ M=\{a,b\},\qquad C=2,\qquad \tau=1,\qquad \mu_1=1, \]

with supplies \(q_a=1\) and \(q_b=\delta\). The single type has values

\[ v(\varnothing)=0,\qquad v(\{a\})=1,\qquad v(\{b\})=0,\qquad v(\{a,b\})=2. \]

These values are even monotone. All input numbers satisfy the required rationality conditions.

We prove that, for every rational step \(0<\varepsilon<1\), every run outputs

\[ W=2\delta,\qquad R=2\delta\varepsilon\left(\left\lceil\frac1\varepsilon\right\rceil-1\right)<2\delta, \]

whereas \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu=1+\delta\).

The optimal mass allocation

Write \(z=y_{1,\{a,b\}}\) and \(w=y_{1,\{a\}}\). Bundles of value zero can be omitted without decreasing welfare.

Feasibility implies \(z\le\delta\) and \(z+w\le1\). Therefore every feasible allocation has welfare

\[ 2z+w=(z+w)+z\le1+\delta. \]

Equality is attained by assigning mass \(\delta\) to \(\{a,b\}\) and mass \(1-\delta\) to \(\{a\}\). Both the type-mass constraint and the supply constraints hold. Consequently,

\[ \mathrm{OPT}_\mu=1+\delta. \]

The entire auction run

Let \(K=\lceil1/\varepsilon\rceil\).

We establish inductively that, in every round \(0\le r<K\), both prices equal \(r\varepsilon\), and the unique submitted bundle is \(\{a,b\}\).

Indeed, when both prices equal \(p<1\), the utilities are

\[ u(\{a,b\})=2-2p,\qquad u(\{a\})=1-p,\qquad u(\{b\})=-p,\qquad u(\varnothing)=0. \]

Because \(1-p>0\), the bundle \(\{a,b\}\) has strictly greater utility than every alternative. Its demand is mass one on each item. In particular,

\[ D_b^r=1>\delta=q_b. \]

Thus the auction must continue, regardless of the selected revenue maximizer. Both prices increase by \(\varepsilon\), proving the induction.

At round \(K\), both prices equal \(K\varepsilon\ge1\). Every bundle has nonpositive utility, so the type drops out. Current demand is zero. The compatibility inequalities now coincide with the historical allocation’s supply constraints, which every revenue maximizer satisfies. Hence the auction stops exactly at round \(K\).

There are no demand ties affecting this argument.

Every final maximizer has small welfare and revenue

Every submitted historical bundle is \(\{a,b\}\). Its bid price in round \(s<K\) is \(2s\varepsilon\), strictly increasing with \(s\).

Let \(h=\sum_{s<K}x_{1,s}\) be the total allocated mass. Since every historical bid contains item \(b\), feasibility requires \(h\le\delta\). Therefore

\[ \sum_{s<K}2s\varepsilon x_{1,s} \le2(K-1)\varepsilon h \le2(K-1)\varepsilon\delta. \]

The upper bound is attained by assigning mass \(\delta\) to the last submitted bid. This allocation is feasible because \(\delta\le1\).

Since \(\varepsilon<1\), we have \(K\ge2\), so the last bid price is strictly positive. Equality requires total allocated mass \(\delta\), all assigned to that last bid. Thus every final revenue maximizer has

\[ R=2(K-1)\varepsilon\delta,\qquad W=2\delta. \]

Finally, \((K-1)\varepsilon<1\), giving \(R<2\delta\). Consequently,

\[ \frac{R}{\mathrm{OPT}_\mu} < \frac{2\delta}{1+\delta}, \qquad \frac{W}{\mathrm{OPT}_\mu} = \frac{2\delta}{1+\delta}. \]

Both ratios tend to zero as \(k\) increases, uniformly over all rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\).

Consequences for the proposed dependence

Here \(m=2\), \(C=2\), and \(\tau=1\), so both proposed approximation denominators are fixed positive constants. The ratios above therefore contradict the proposed dichotomy for any positive universal constants.

Moreover, \(\mu_{\min}=1\), and the entire mass description has constant encoding length throughout the family. Thus no finite approximation factor depending only on the masses can suffice.

The supply dependence required by this nondegenerate family is stronger than logarithmic. Writing \(q_{\min}=\delta\), its exact welfare loss is

\[ \frac{\mathrm{OPT}_\mu}{W} = \frac{1+\delta}{2\delta} = 2^{k-1}+\frac12. \]

For example, any repaired dichotomy of the form \(R\ge c_R\mathrm{OPT}_\mu/F_R\) or \(W\ge c_W\mathrm{OPT}_\mu/F_W\), with positive universal constants, must have at least one of \(F_R,F_W\) grow as \(\Omega(q_{\min}^{-1})\) on this family. Otherwise both alternatives fail.

With ordinary binary numerator-denominator encoding, this family has total input length \(L=\Theta(k)\). The necessary loss is therefore exponential in the supply encoding length, and \(2^{\Omega(L)}\) on this family.

This does not contradict the cited discrete result: its allocations consist of pairwise disjoint bundles under unit item supplies, whereas the construction above uses unequal capacities. [Bousquet–Cai–Hunkenschröder–Vetta, Section 3 and Theorem 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06495).
