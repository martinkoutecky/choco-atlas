Conclusion

Exact-mass recovery is polynomial-time solvable under the stated promises. In fact, the specified Partial-SP rule reduces to selecting the most frequent vote ranking on each subset. Each such ranking is uniquely the restriction of the hidden ranking.

Writing \(T_F(m,r,k,t)\) for the running time of the specified aggregator, a deterministic implementation takes

\[ O\!\left(L+\tau L^2+\tau k\lceil\log_2 m\rceil+T_F(m,r,k,t)\right) \]

bit operations. The separation inequality is unnecessary for this exact-mass result.

The case \(t=m\) simply returns \(A\); the proof below also settles every nontrivial case \(1\leq t<m\).

1. The prediction kernel is symmetric

Fix a subset \(S=S_j\), and suppress the index \(j\). For a ranking \(v\in\mathcal L(S)\), define its extension set by

\[ E(v)=\{\eta\in\mathcal L(A):\eta|_S=v\}. \]

Every such set has cardinality

\[ C=\binom{m}{k}(m-k)!=\frac{m!}{k!}, \]

because one chooses the positions occupied by \(S\), places its alternatives in the prescribed order, and orders the remaining alternatives arbitrarily.

By the definition of the Mallows normalizers, \(\sum_\eta K(\eta\mid\rho)=1\) for every \(\rho\). Moreover, Kendall–tau distance is symmetric, so

\[ K(\eta\mid\rho)=K(\rho\mid\eta). \]

Consequently, \(\sum_\rho K(\eta\mid\rho)=1\) for every \(\eta\) as well.

We now show that the matrix \(B\), with entries \(B(v\mid h)\), has both column sums and row sums equal to one. First,

\[ \sum_v B(v\mid h) =\frac1C\sum_{\rho\in E(h)}\sum_v Q(v\mid\rho) =\frac1C\sum_{\rho\in E(h)}1 =1. \]

Here \(\sum_v Q(v\mid\rho)=1\), since the extension sets partition \(\mathcal L(A)\).

For the other sums, all sums are finite, so we may interchange them:

\[ \sum_h B(v\mid h) =\frac1C\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal L(A)} \sum_{\eta\in E(v)}K(\eta\mid\rho) =\frac1C\sum_{\eta\in E(v)}1 =1. \]

Thus the denominator in the definition of \(g\) equals one, and

\[ g(u\mid v)=\sum_h B(u\mid h)B(v\mid h)=g(v\mid u). \]

Every entry of \(K\), hence of \(Q\), \(B\), and \(g\), is strictly positive. Therefore all prediction-normalization ratios are defined and equal to one:

\[ \frac{g(u\mid v)}{g(v\mid u)}=1. \]

Also, using the column sums of \(B\),

\[ \sum_u g(u\mid v) =\sum_h B(v\mid h)\sum_u B(u\mid h) =\sum_h B(v\mid h) =1. \]

The society promise now gives

\[ f(v)=\sum_u\mu(v,u) =Q(v\mid\pi^\star)\sum_u g(u\mid v) =Q(v\mid\pi^\star). \]

Hence the exact Partial-SP score is precisely

\[ f(v)\sum_{u\in\mathcal L(S)} \frac{g(u\mid v)}{g(v\mid u)} =k!\,f(v). \]

Its maximizers are exactly the maximizers of the vote marginal.

2. A label-swap lemma for Kendall–tau distance

For a ranking \(\xi\), write \(a\prec_\xi b\) when \(a\) precedes \(b\). Let \(\rho,\eta\in\mathcal L(A)\), and suppose

\[ a\prec_\rho b \qquad\text{and}\qquad b\prec_\eta a. \]

Let \(T_{ab}\eta\) be the ranking obtained by exchanging the positions of the labels \(a\) and \(b\). Define

\[ N= \left|\left\{c\in A\setminus\{a,b\}: a\prec_\rho c\prec_\rho b \ \text{and}\ b\prec_\eta c\prec_\eta a \right\}\right|. \]

Then

\[ d(\eta,\rho)-d(T_{ab}\eta,\rho)=1+2N. \]

To verify this identity, recall that Kendall–tau distance counts unordered pairs whose relative orders disagree.

  • The pair \(\{a,b\}\) changes from disagreement to agreement, decreasing the distance by one.
  • Pairs involving neither label are unchanged.
  • For an alternative \(c\) outside the interval between \(a\) and \(b\) in \(\rho\), the total contribution of \(\{a,c\}\) and \(\{b,c\}\) is unchanged: \(\rho\) places both labels on the same side of \(c\), and exchanging their positions preserves that total.
  • If \(a\prec_\rho c\prec_\rho b\) and \(b\prec_\eta c\prec_\eta a\), both pairs disagree before the exchange and agree afterward, decreasing the distance by two.
  • If \(a\prec_\rho c\prec_\rho b\) but \(c\) lies outside the interval between \(b\) and \(a\) in \(\eta\), exactly one of the two pairs disagrees both before and after the exchange.

These cases exhaust the alternatives and prove the identity. In particular,

\[ d(T_{ab}\eta,\rho)<d(\eta,\rho). \]

Since both dispersions lie strictly between zero and one, and both mixture weights are positive, this implies

\[ K(T_{ab}\eta\mid\rho)>K(\eta\mid\rho). \]

3. The hidden restriction is the unique marginal mode

Fix any full ranking \(\rho\), and put \(h=\rho|_S\). We prove that \(h\) uniquely maximizes \(Q(v\mid\rho)\) over \(v\in\mathcal L(S)\).

Take \(v\neq h\). Read the alternatives in order \(v\), replacing each alternative by its position in \(h\). The resulting sequence is not increasing, so it has an adjacent descent. Thus some adjacent alternatives in \(v\) have order \(b,a\), while \(a\prec_\rho b\).

Let \(v'\) be obtained by exchanging these two labels. The map \(T_{ab}\) is a bijection from \(E(v)\) to \(E(v')\): exchanging labels commutes with restriction to \(S\), and applying the exchange twice restores the original ranking.

For every \(\eta\in E(v)\), we have \(b\prec_\eta a\). The lemma therefore gives a strict inequality for every corresponding pair of extensions. Summing yields

\[ Q(v'\mid\rho) =\sum_{\eta\in E(v)}K(T_{ab}\eta\mid\rho) > \sum_{\eta\in E(v)}K(\eta\mid\rho) =Q(v\mid\rho). \]

Consequently, every \(v\neq h\) fails to maximize \(Q(\,\cdot\mid\rho)\). A maximizer exists because \(\mathcal L(S)\) is finite, so \(h\) is its unique maximizer.

Applying this result with \(\rho=\pi^\star\), Section 1 proves that, for every society,

\[ \widehat\sigma_j=\pi^\star|_{S_j}. \]

There are no maximizing ties.

4. Recovery by the specified aggregator

The promised \(t\)-consistency holds for every full ranking, hence in particular for \(\pi^\star\). Therefore

\[ F(\widehat\sigma_1,\ldots,\widehat\sigma_r) = F(\pi^\star|_{S_1},\ldots,\pi^\star|_{S_r}) = \operatorname{Top}_t(\pi^\star). \]

This proves correctness of the specified exact-mass Partial-SP rule.

The argument uses neither the displayed separation inequality nor a claim that restriction preserves the Mallows family. It directly compares the sums over full-ranking extensions.

5. Algorithm and bit complexity

The implementation is:

  • For every society, group listed types by their vote ranking and sum their masses.
  • Select the vote ranking with largest marginal mass.
  • Apply the specified \(F\) to the selected rankings.

All these operations use the given support representation.

For an explicit bit bound, write the listed masses as \(a_i/b_i\), with positive integer denominators, and let \(D=\prod_i b_i\). The sum of the denominator bit lengths is at most \(L\), so \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits. Compute each scaled mass as \(a_i(D/b_i)\), and accumulate these integers by vote ranking.

Each society has total mass one. Thus every accumulated numerator is at most \(D\) and has \(O(L)\) bits. Using schoolbook multiplication and exact division, constructing \(D\), scaling all masses, accumulating them, and comparing the marginals costs \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations.

Encode each alternative by its input-order index. A vote ranking then has \(k\lceil\log_2 m\rceil\) bits. Separate binary tries for the societies group these keys deterministically in \(O(\tau k\lceil\log_2 m\rceil)\) time. Including input reading and aggregation gives the stated bound

\[ O\!\left(L+\tau L^2+\tau k\lceil\log_2 m\rceil+T_F(m,r,k,t)\right). \]

Because \(F\) is promised polynomial-time evaluable, this is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,r,k,t\).

Finally, positivity proves that every vote–prediction pair belongs to every society’s support. Thus, on promised inputs,

\[ \tau=r(k!)^2. \]

The explicit input can therefore be factorially large in \(k\), but the algorithm is polynomial in its listed-support size and bit length.

The [published result](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/054e9f9a286671ababa3213d6e59c1c2-Paper-Conference.pdf), specifically Theorem 1 and Corollary 1, discusses finite-sample guarantees. The exact computational conclusion above follows independently from the kernels specified in this task.
