The continuous mirror question, as graded

For any finite number \(k\) of constituencies, let each constituency \(g\) be a finite-support measure \(\nu_g\) on resident locations on the line, with rational masses and \(\sum_g \nu_g(\mathbb{R})=1\), and let the two sites be \(0\) and \(1\). Define \(\mathrm{SW}_{\nu}(a)=\sum_g\int |z-a|\,d\nu_g(z)\). Among deterministic local full-information two-step mechanisms that choose one site \(r_g\in\{0,1\}\) using only \(\nu_g\), then choose a winner from the proposed sites, determine the minimum worst-case ratio \(\sup_{k,(\nu_g)}\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}\mathrm{SW}_{\nu}(a)/\mathrm{SW}_{\nu}(w)\). Is the optimum exactly \(3\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(k\geq1\) nonempty constituencies have finite-support measures \(\nu_g\) on \(\mathbb R\), with positive binary-rational masses and \(\sum_g\nu_g(\mathbb R)=1\). The alternatives are \(A=\{0,1\}\), and \(\mathrm{SW}_\nu(a)=\sum_g\int |z-a|\,d\nu_g(z)\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here a type is a constituency-location pair, locations are binary rationals, and \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. State any running times in \(m=2\), \(\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), and \(k\).
A deterministic mechanism uses one common local rule \(f\), setting \(r_g=f(\nu_g)\in A\) independently of other constituencies and their number. Its second stage receives only the multiset \(\{(\nu_g(\mathbb R),r_g):g=1,\ldots,k\}\) and chooses \(w_M\in\{r_g:g=1,\ldots,k\}\). Both rules include deterministic tie-breaking; no unanimity or scale-invariance condition is imposed.
Define \(D(M)=\sup_{k,(\nu_g)}\max_{a\in A}\mathrm{SW}_\nu(a)/\mathrm{SW}_\nu(w_M)\), with a zero denominator giving \(+\infty\).

What is \(D^*=\inf_M D(M)\) over these mechanisms? Is \(D^*=3\)?

For finite unweighted societies, every deterministic local full-information mechanism has worst-case distortion at least \(3\), and a matching upper bound is known for two alternatives on a line ([Voudouris](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/40061/1/1-s2.0-S0020019025000031-main.pdf)). No result settling the normalized rational-mass formulation was located.

Result

Yes: \(D^*=3\), and the infimum is attained.

The lower bound below uses only normalized societies with equal dyadic masses and one location per constituency. It allows arbitrary mass dependence in the local rule and arbitrary deterministic tie-breaking.

1. A mechanism with distortion at most \(3\)

Write \(s_g=\nu_g(\mathbb R)\) and \(C_g(a)=\int |z-a|\,d\nu_g(z)\).

Choose each constituency’s representative to maximize its local welfare:

\(r_g\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{a\in\{0,1\}} C_g(a)\),

breaking ties in favor of \(0\). At the second stage, compute

\(H_a=\sum_{g:r_g=a}s_g\)

and choose the alternative with greatest \(H_a\), again breaking ties in favor of \(0\). Since \(H_0+H_1=1\), the selected alternative is represented by at least one constituency. This is the rational-mass version of [Voudouris’s Max-Weight-of-Optimal mechanism](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/40061/1/1-s2.0-S0020019025000031-main.pdf).

Here is a direct proof of its guarantee.

Let \(w\) be its winner and \(o=1-w\). Then \(H_w\geq 1/2\). For every constituency represented by \(w\), local optimality gives \(C_g(w)\geq C_g(o)\). Also, the triangle inequality gives

\(C_g(w)+C_g(o)=\int (|z-w|+|z-o|)\,d\nu_g(z)\geq s_g\).

Consequently, \(C_g(w)\geq s_g/2\) for these constituencies, and hence

\(\mathrm{SW}_\nu(w)\geq H_w/2\geq 1/4\).

For constituencies represented by \(w\), the difference \(C_g(o)-C_g(w)\) is nonpositive. For every other constituency, the reverse triangle inequality gives \(C_g(o)-C_g(w)\leq s_g\). Summing,

\(\mathrm{SW}_\nu(o)-\mathrm{SW}_\nu(w)\leq 1-H_w\leq H_w\leq 2\mathrm{SW}_\nu(w)\).

Thus \(\mathrm{SW}_\nu(o)\leq 3\mathrm{SW}_\nu(w)\), proving \(D(M)\leq 3\).

For this particular mechanism, equality occurs with two constituencies:

\(\nu_1=\tfrac12\delta_{1/2},\qquad \nu_2=\tfrac12\delta_0\).

Their representatives are respectively \(0\) and \(1\); the second-stage tie selects \(0\). The two welfare values are \(1/4\) and \(3/4\).

2. A lower bound at a fixed normalized mass

Lemma. Fix any admissible mechanism \(M\) and any \(k=2^s\), where \(s\geq 1\). Restrict attention to societies consisting of \(k\) constituencies of the form

\(\nu_g=\tfrac1k\delta_{z_g}\),

with dyadic \(z_g\in[0,1]\). The supremum of distortion over these societies is at least

\(3-\dfrac{4}{k+2}\).

Proof. Denote this restricted supremum by \(R\). If \(R=+\infty\), the claim holds immediately. Otherwise, put \(q=1/(R+1)\).

Let \(\mathcal Q\) be the dyadic numbers in \([0,1]\), and define

\(S_a=\{z\in\mathcal Q:f(\tfrac1k\delta_z)=a\}\), for \(a\in\{0,1\}\).

These sets partition \(\mathcal Q\). Finite distortion forces \(0\in S_1\): otherwise, a society with all constituencies at \(0\) selects \(0\), whose welfare is zero. Similarly, \(1\in S_0\).

Both sets are therefore nonempty. Define

\(\alpha=\inf S_0,\qquad \beta=\sup S_1\).

We have \(\alpha\leq\beta\). Indeed, if \(\beta<\alpha\), density supplies a dyadic \(x\) strictly between them. Membership in either \(S_0\) or \(S_1\) contradicts the corresponding bound.

For these equal-mass constituencies, the second-stage input is determined solely by the number \(j\) represented by \(0\). Write its output as \(h(j)\). Every count is realizable using locations \(1\) and \(0\). The requirement to choose a representative implies

\(h(0)=1,\qquad h(k)=0\).

Take the least index \(t\) with \(h(t)=0\), and put \(j=t-1\). Then

\(0\leq j\leq k-1,\qquad h(j)=1,\qquad h(j+1)=0\).

No monotonicity of \(h\) is assumed.

For any restricted society, set \(T=\frac1k\sum_g z_g\). Because all locations belong to \([0,1]\),

\(\mathrm{SW}_\nu(0)=T,\qquad \mathrm{SW}_\nu(1)=1-T\).

If the winner is \(1\), the distortion bound implies \(T\leq R(1-T)\), equivalently \(T\leq 1-q\). If the winner is \(0\), it implies \(1-T\leq RT\), equivalently \(T\geq q\).

We now apply these inequalities to two families of indistinguishable second-stage inputs.

First, take \(j\) constituencies at \(1\) and \(k-j\) constituencies at any \(y\in S_1\). Their representatives give count \(j\), so the winner is \(1\). Therefore,

\(\dfrac{j+(k-j)y}{k}\leq 1-q\).

This holds for every \(y\in S_1\), so taking the supremum yields

\(\beta\leq 1-\dfrac{kq}{k-j}\).

Second, take \(j+1\) constituencies at any \(x\in S_0\) and the remaining \(k-j-1\) at \(0\). The winner is \(0\), so

\(\dfrac{(j+1)x}{k}\geq q\).

Taking the infimum over \(x\in S_0\) yields

\(\alpha\geq \dfrac{kq}{j+1}\).

Together with \(\alpha\leq\beta\), these imply

\(\dfrac{kq}{j+1}\leq 1-\dfrac{kq}{k-j}\),

and hence

\(q\leq \dfrac{(j+1)(k-j)}{k(k+1)}\).

The positive integers \(j+1\) and \(k-j\) sum to the odd integer \(k+1\). Their product is therefore at most

\(\dfrac{(k+1)^2-1}{4}=\dfrac{k(k+2)}4\).

It follows that

\(\dfrac1{R+1}=q\leq \dfrac{k+2}{4(k+1)}\),

which rearranges to

\(R\geq \dfrac{4(k+1)}{k+2}-1=3-\dfrac4{k+2}\).

This proves the lemma. The use of infima and suprema requires no continuity of either mechanism rule. All societies used in the inequalities have valid dyadic inputs.

3. The universal lower bound

For every mechanism \(M\), its unrestricted distortion dominates each restricted supremum in the lemma. Thus, for every \(s\geq 1\),

\(D(M)\geq 3-\dfrac4{2^s+2}\).

Letting \(s\) grow gives \(D(M)\geq 3\). Combined with Section 1, this proves \(D^*=3\).

Crucially, within each application of the lemma, every local input has the same mass \(1/k\). The proof never equates decisions at different masses or different numbers of constituencies. Every constructed society has support size \(\tau=k\), since constituency-location pairs are distinct types.

4. Running time of the attaining mechanism

The mechanism computes welfare sums directly from the given support. It uses \(O(m\tau+mk)\) exact rational arithmetic operations.

Under the usual explicit binary encoding of rationals, intermediate fractions can be kept at \(O(L)\) bits: their denominators divide products of input denominators, and their numerators have the same asymptotic bit bound. Schoolbook arithmetic therefore gives a conservative bound of

\(O((m\tau+mk)L^2)\)

bit operations. Here \(m=2\) and \(k\leq\tau\), so this is \(O(\tau L^2)\).
