The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given item kinds \(X\), types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), per-capita supplies \(q_x\in[0,1]\), and rational additive pairwise values \(d_{t,u}(x)\), find a mass allocation \(z_{t,B}\ge 0\) to integral bundles \(B\subseteq X\) satisfying \(\sum_B z_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B\ni x}z_{t,B}=q_x\). For every two occupied cohorts \((t,B)\) and \((u,C)\), require subsets \(S_B\subseteq B\) and \(S_C\subseteq C\) with \(|S_B|+|S_C|\le K\) such that \(d_{t,u}(B\setminus S_B)\ge d_{t,u}(C\setminus S_C)\); copies of the same kind in the two bundles count separately. Does a deterministic algorithm, polynomial in \(|X|\), \(\tau=|T|\), and the input bit length, always return a polynomial-size allocation with \(K=\lceil C\sqrt{\tau}\rceil\) for a universal constant \(C\)?

Result

Yes. There is a universal constant \(C>0\) such that every instance admits an allocation fair up to \(\lceil C\sqrt{\tau}\rceil\) removals. Such an allocation, with at most \(\tau(m+1)\) positive entries and polynomial total binary encoding length, can be constructed deterministically in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

The construction actually gives every type the same item marginals \(q_x\). Its supported bundles can nevertheless differ between types.

1. The discrepancy ingredient

We use the following constructive two-color discrepancy theorem:

There is a fixed integer \(a\ge 1\) and a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm which, given a rational matrix \(A\in[0,1]^{r\times s}\), finds \(\chi\in\{-1,1\}^{s}\) satisfying

\(\|A\chi\|_\infty\le a\sqrt r.\)

This follows from Theorem 1 of [Connor, Dupré la Tour, Narayan, and Schierreich](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13287v1#S3): take two colors, with the same \(r\) additive valuation functions attached to both colors. If \(Y\) is the first color class, its discrepancy guarantee bounds \(\left|A_i(Y)-\frac12 A_i([s])\right|\); assigning sign \(1\) on \(Y\) and sign \(-1\) elsewhere doubles this discrepancy. Enlarge the universal constant to an integer \(a\).

We use the rational-input, polynomial-time formulation of this theorem. Every application below has entries in \([0,1]\), including applications to restrictions of a matrix to selected columns.

2. Exact marginals with discrepancy controlled throughout the support

Lemma 1. Let \(A\in[0,1]^{r\times m}\) and \(q\in[0,1]^m\) be rational, with \(m,r\ge 1\). One can deterministically construct, in polynomial time, rational positive weights \(\lambda_j\) and vectors \(b^j\in\{0,1\}^m\), with at most \(m+1\) vectors, such that

\(\sum_j\lambda_j=1,\qquad \sum_j\lambda_j b^j=q,\)

and every supported vector satisfies

\(\|A(b^j-q)\|_\infty\le a\sqrt r+1.\)

The weights have polynomial total binary encoding length.

Proof. We first express \(q\) exactly as a mixture of nearby dyadic points, then round those points while retaining their mean.

Initial dyadic mixture

Set \(b=\lceil\log_2(2m)\rceil\). For each coordinate define

\(\ell_x=2^{-b}\lfloor 2^bq_x\rfloor,\qquad \theta_x=2^b(q_x-\ell_x)\in[0,1).\)

For \(u\in(0,1)\), define

\(p_x(u)=\ell_x+2^{-b}\mathbf 1[u<\theta_x].\)

Partition \((0,1)\) at the distinct thresholds \(\theta_x\). On each resulting interval, \(p(u)\) is constant. Give that point weight equal to the interval length. This yields at most \(m+1\) dyadic points, with rational weights.

For every coordinate,

\(\mathbb E[p_x]=\ell_x+2^{-b}\theta_x=q_x.\)

Moreover, each point belongs to \([0,1]^m\) and satisfies \(|p_x-q_x|\le 2^{-b}\). Thus, for every row \(i\),

\(\left|A_i(p-q)\right|\le m2^{-b}\le 1.\)

Rounding one dyadic level

Suppose the current points have coordinates with denominator \(2^j\), where \(j\ge 1\). For a supported point \(p\), let

\(J=\{x:2^jp_x\text{ is odd}\}.\)

Apply the discrepancy theorem to the columns of \(A\) indexed by \(J\). Extend the resulting signing by zero outside \(J\), obtaining \(\chi\) with

\(\|A\chi\|_\infty\le a\sqrt r.\)

Replace \(p\), of weight \(\lambda\), by

\(p^+=p+2^{-j}\chi,\qquad p^-=p-2^{-j}\chi,\)

each of weight \(\lambda/2\). If \(J\) is empty, leave \(p\) unchanged.

An odd numerator lies strictly between \(0\) and \(2^j\). Hence both successors remain in \([0,1]^m\), and their numerators become even. They therefore have denominator \(2^{j-1}\). Their average is exactly \(p\), and

\(\|A(p^\pm-p)\|_\infty\le 2^{-j}a\sqrt r.\)

After each level, compress the mixture to at most \(m+1\) points while preserving its mean. Here is an explicit compression procedure. If there are more than \(m+1\) points, their augmented vectors \((p,1)\) are linearly dependent. Find a nonzero integer vector \(c\) satisfying

\(\sum_i c_i p^i=0,\qquad \sum_i c_i=0.\)

The coefficients \(c_i\) include both positive and negative entries. Set

\(\varepsilon=\min_{c_i>0}\frac{\lambda_i}{c_i},\qquad \lambda_i'=\lambda_i-\varepsilon c_i.\)

The new weights are nonnegative, preserve both total weight and mean, and at least one becomes zero. Delete zero weights and repeat. Compression changes no retained point, so it preserves every discrepancy bound already established.

Perform the rounding levels \(j=b,b-1,\ldots,1\). At the end, every point is a binary vector, and the mean remains exactly \(q\). For every final point, the initial error and subsequent increments give

\(\|A(b^j-q)\|_\infty\le 1+a\sqrt r\sum_{h=1}^{b}2^{-h}<1+a\sqrt r.\)

Polynomial complexity and encoding

There are \(O(\log(m+1))\) levels. Before compression, each level contains at most \(2(m+1)\) points, so it uses polynomially many discrepancy calls and linear-algebra operations.

For completeness, compression also has controlled bit complexity. All point coordinates have denominators dividing \(2^b=O(m)\). After multiplying augmented vectors by \(2^b\), their entries are integers of size \(O(m)\). Integer dependencies \(c\) can therefore be computed by rational linear algebra with coefficient bit lengths \(O(m\log(m+1))\), using determinant bounds.

Maintain a common denominator \(D\) for the weights. Initially, its bit length is at most the sum of the input denominator bit lengths. Splitting multiplies \(D\) by \(2\). A compression step can use the new common denominator \(D c_k\), where \(k\) attains the minimum defining \(\varepsilon\). There are only \(O(m\log(m+1))\) such steps, and each adds polynomially many bits. Consequently all arithmetic and all final weight encodings have polynomial size. This proves the lemma. ∎

3. Converting weighted discrepancy into signed removals

For an additive function \(f\), write

\(f(q)=\sum_{x\in X}q_xf(x).\)

Lemma 2. Fix an integer \(T\ge 1\), a supply vector \(q\in[0,1]^X\), and a signed additive valuation \(v\). One can construct four rational additive functions \(f^1,f^2,f^3,f^4\), with values in \([0,1]\), such that the following holds:

If bundles \(B,C\subseteq X\) satisfy

\(\left|f^h(B)-f^h(q)\right|\le T,\qquad \left|f^h(C)-f^h(q)\right|\le T\)

for every \(h\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), then there exist removals from the two bundles, of total size at most \(14T\), making

\(v(B\setminus S_B)\ge v(C\setminus S_C).\)

The bundles may overlap. Copies removed from the two bundles count separately.

Proof. Assume \(X\ne\varnothing\). Order the kinds by nonincreasing \(|v(x)|\).

If \(\sum_xq_x\le 6T\), let \(S=X\). Otherwise, let \(S\) be the shortest prefix of this ordering satisfying

\(q(S):=\sum_{x\in S}q_x\ge 6T.\)

Since each \(q_x\le 1\), in either case

\(q(S)<6T+1.\)

Let \(R=X\setminus S\), and define

\(S^+=\{x\in S:v(x)>0\},\qquad S^-=\{x\in S:v(x)<0\},\)

with analogous sets \(R^+,R^-\). Put \(p=\min_{x\in S}|v(x)|\).

Define

\(f^1(x)=\mathbf 1[x\in S^+],\qquad f^2(x)=\mathbf 1[x\in S^-].\)

When \(p>0\), define

\(f^3(x)= \begin{cases} v(x)/p,&x\in R^+,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad f^4(x)= \begin{cases} -v(x)/p,&x\in R^-,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases}\)

When \(p=0\), set \(f^3=f^4=0\). The ordering ensures that \(|v(x)|\le p\) on \(R\), so all four functions have values in \([0,1]\).

Choose the comparison-specific removals

\(S_B=B\cap S^-,\qquad S_C=C\cap S^+.\)

The discrepancy assumptions imply

\(|S_B|\le q(S^-)+T\le q(S)+T<7T+1,\)

and similarly \(|S_C|<7T+1\). These cardinalities are integers, hence each is at most \(7T\).

It remains to prove the desired inequality.

If \(R=\varnothing\), then \(B\setminus S_B\) contains no negatively valued items, whereas \(C\setminus S_C\) contains no positively valued items. The inequality follows.

If \(p=0\), every item in \(R\) has value zero, and the same argument applies.

Finally, suppose \(R\ne\varnothing\) and \(p>0\). Since \(S\ne X\), it was chosen by the prefix rule, giving \(q(S)\ge 6T\). Also every item in \(S\) has nonzero value. Therefore

\(|B\cap S^+|+|C\cap S^-| \ge q(S^+)+q(S^-)-2T =q(S)-2T \ge 4T.\)

The assumptions for \(f^3,f^4\) give

\(|v(B\cap R^+)-v(C\cap R^+)|\le 2pT,\)

\(|v(B\cap R^-)-v(C\cap R^-)|\le 2pT.\)

Consequently,

\(v(B\cap R)-v(C\cap R)\ge -4pT.\)

After the chosen removals,

\(v(B\setminus S_B)-v(C\setminus S_C) =v(B\cap S^+)-v(C\cap S^-) +v(B\cap R)-v(C\cap R).\)

Every positive item in \(S\) has value at least \(p\), and every negative item has value at most \(-p\). Thus the last expression is at least

\(p\bigl(|B\cap S^+|+|C\cap S^-|\bigr)-4pT\ge 0.\)

This proves the lemma. No step required disjoint bundles: each removal concerns its own bundle copy. ∎

4. Constructing the allocation

If \(m=0\), assign mass \(\mu_t\) to the empty bundle for each type. Henceforth assume \(m\ge 1\).

Set

\(r_0=4(2\tau-1)=8\tau-4,\qquad T=\lceil a\sqrt{r_0}\rceil+1.\)

For every ordered pair \((t,u)\), including diagonal pairs, apply Lemma 2 to

\(v=d_{t,u}\)

using the same \(q\) and \(T\). Denote the resulting four functions by \(f_{t,u}^1,\ldots,f_{t,u}^4\).

For each type \(t\), form a matrix \(A_t\) whose rows are:

  • the four functions for every outgoing pair \((t,u)\);
  • the four functions for every incoming pair \((u,t)\), excluding a second copy of the diagonal pair.

There are exactly \(r_0\) rows, counted with multiplicity. All entries lie in \([0,1]\), as required by Lemma 1.

Apply Lemma 1 to \(A_t,q\). This produces a distribution supported on at most \(m+1\) bundles. Write its positive weights as \(\lambda_{t,B}\). Its marginals satisfy

\(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=1,\qquad \sum_{B:\,x\in B}\lambda_{t,B}=q_x.\)

Every supported bundle also satisfies the discrepancy assumptions of Lemma 2 for every valuation whose auxiliary functions occur in \(A_t\), because

\(a\sqrt{r_0}+1\le T.\)

Define the allocation masses by

\(z_{t,B}=\mu_t\lambda_{t,B}.\)

These masses are positive rational numbers on the listed support.

Exact feasibility

For each type,

\(\sum_Bz_{t,B}=\mu_t.\)

For each kind,

\(\sum_{t,B:\,x\in B}z_{t,B} =\sum_t\mu_tq_x =q_x,\)

using \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Thus all type masses and supplies are met exactly.

Fairness, including same-type comparisons

Consider any ordered pair of occupied cohorts \((t,B),(u,C)\).

When \(t\ne u\), the four functions for \(d_{t,u}\) occur in both \(A_t\) and \(A_u\). Hence both bundles satisfy their discrepancy bounds, and Lemma 2 eliminates this comparison’s envy with at most \(14T\) removals.

When \(t=u\), the four diagonal functions for \(d_{t,t}\) occur in \(A_t\). Both supported bundles satisfy their bounds, so the same lemma applies.

Therefore the allocation is fair up to \(14T\) removals under precisely the comparison and copy-counting conventions in the question.

5. Universal constant and polynomial-time conclusion

Since \(\tau\ge 1\),

\(T\le a\sqrt{8\tau-4}+2 \le (3a+2)\sqrt{\tau}.\)

Choose the fixed integer

\(C=14(3a+2).\)

Then

\(14T\le C\sqrt{\tau}\le \lceil C\sqrt{\tau}\rceil.\)

This proves the requested universal removal bound.

The auxiliary functions are computable using rational sorting, summation, and division, with polynomial bit complexity. Lemma 1 is applied once per type to a matrix with \(8\tau-4\) rows and \(m\) columns. Its distributions have polynomially encoded rational weights. Multiplying those weights by the input masses preserves polynomial encoding length.

There are at most \(\tau(m+1)\) positive allocation entries, each containing one type, one \(m\)-bit bundle, and one polynomially encoded rational mass. The entire construction is deterministic and polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L\).

Thus the polynomial-time alternative holds for the exact mass-allocation model, without a feasibility promise: every instance is feasible for this universal bound.

2. Lemma 1: exact marginals with controlled discrepancy

The definitions give \(0\leq\theta_x<1\). Partitioning at the distinct thresholds produces at most \(m+1\) positive-length intervals, and the interval lengths are positive rational weights summing to one. Threshold points themselves do not affect the mixture.

\[ |A_i(p-q)|\leq \sum_x A_{i,x}|p_x-q_x| \leq m2^{-b}\leq 1. \]

  • makes at least one weight zero.

Compression retains existing points rather than introducing new ones. It therefore preserves every discrepancy bound already satisfied by the support.

Final discrepancy

The superscript indexing of the final vectors does not affect the calculation.

  • The dyadic grid denominator satisfies \(2^b=O(m)\).
