The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of agent types \(T\), a rational mass distribution \(\mu\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), binary eligibility profiles over item types, and an adversarially ordered sequence of \(H\) arrival batches with rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), what is the minimum worst-case number \(B\) of advice bits needed by a possibly randomized online mechanism to attain the offline maximum matched mass on every sequence? For the egalitarian variant, what is the minimum \(B\) needed to maximize the minimum expected per-capita utility among supported types? At batch \(j\), the mechanism irrevocably assigns eligible unmatched mass \(x_{tj}\), subject to \(\sum_t x_{tj}\le q_j\) and \(\sum_j x_{tj}\le\mu_t\); the oracle knows the full sequence.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The parameter \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) item types and truthful binary eligibility profiles \(e_{tr}\in\{0,1\}\); every item type is eligible for some supported type. Initially the mechanism knows this catalog, the society, \(H\geq1\), and an upper bound \(L\) on the total binary input length, including the arrival sequence.
An adversary fixes a sequence \(\sigma=((r_j,q_j))_{j=1}^{H}\), with \(r_j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), positive binary-rational supplies \(q_j\), and \(\sum_jq_j=1\). Promise that an offline eligible assignment can match all society mass.
Only at round \(j\) are \(r_j,q_j\) revealed. The mechanism irrevocably chooses rational masses \(x_{tj}\geq0\), with \(x_{tj}=0\) if \(e_{t r_j}=0\), \(\sum_t x_{tj}\leq q_j\), and \(\sum_jx_{tj}\leq\mu_t\); unused supply is discarded. Each individual can receive at most one unit.
For a randomized mechanism, define matched mass \(K=\mathbb E[\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}]\) and egalitarian welfare \(E=\min_{t\in T}\mathbb E[\sum_jx_{tj}]/\mu_t\). Each offline benchmark maximizes the corresponding objective under the same constraints with the full sequence known.
An unlimited oracle knows the full sequence and supplies a binary advice tape before execution, independently of the mechanism's private randomness. The mechanism may read at most \(B\) bits in total; tape length, request timing, and unread contents convey no additional information. No strategic or additional fairness constraints are imposed.
Let \(B_K^*(m,\tau,H,L)\) and \(B_E^*(m,\tau,H,L)\) be the least worst-case budgets over all such inputs for a single online mechanism to equal the respective offline benchmark on every sequence. Computation is unrestricted; state any running times in \(m,\tau,H,L,B\).

What are \(B_K^*(m,\tau,H,L)\) and \(B_E^*(m,\tau,H,L)\)? Can exact offline optimality be attained with less advice than encoding the entire future sequence, or is that much information necessary in the worst case?

For discrete societies with \(n\) agents, \(n\) items, and a perfect eligible matching, randomized mechanisms require at least \(\lceil\log_2(n!)\rceil\) advice bits for either objective with binary utilities; that budget also suffices deterministically. [Aleksandrov–Walsh](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.15909)
Fractional online matching has algorithms and lower bounds for robustness–consistency tradeoffs with suggested matchings, a different advice model; no result settling the budgets defined here was located. [Choo–Jin–Shin](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/0b8705a611ed1ce19cdb759031078705-Abstract-Conference.html)

Exact characterization and worst-case bounds

The two advice budgets are equal. They admit an exact finite characterization by rational linear programs and a finite partition problem. This characterization covers arbitrary joint values of $m,\tau,H,L$, including cases where the initially known input consumes most of $L$.

It also yields the following more explicit conclusions:

  • If $m\le 2$, $\tau=1$, or $H=1$, the budget is zero.
  • For $H=2$, the budget is bounded independently of numerical precision, and its worst-case value for sufficiently large $L$ is

$$ \left\lceil\log_2\min\left\{\max\{1,m-1\}, \binom{\tau}{\lfloor\tau/2\rfloor}\right\}\right\rceil. $$

  • For every fixed $m\ge3$, $\tau\ge2$, and $H\ge3$, under a standard binary encoding,

$$ B_K^*(m,\tau,H,L)=B_E^*(m,\tau,H,L)=\Theta(L) \qquad\text{as }L\to\infty. $$

Thus, encoding the entire future sequence is unnecessary in general, but advice of the same asymptotic order as its binary description can be necessary, already with two supported types and three arrivals.

Exact finite-$L$ numbers depend on the serialization used to measure input length. Throughout the exact characterization below, fix that serialization. For the running-time and asymptotic statements, assume a standard polynomial-time parseable encoding whose length is linear in the bit lengths of rational numerators and denominators, up to framing overhead.

1. Both objectives require perfect matching almost surely

Write
$$ M_t=\sum_{j=1}^{H}x_{tj}. $$
Every execution satisfies
$$ 0\le M_t\le\mu_t. $$

The promise supplies a perfect eligible assignment. Because its constraints have rational coefficients, a rational perfect assignment also exists: the nonempty bounded rational polytope of perfect assignments has a rational vertex. Consequently, both offline benchmarks equal $1$.

If a randomized mechanism attains $K=1$, then
$$ \mathbb E\left[\sum_t(\mu_t-M_t)\right]=0. $$
The random variable inside the expectation is nonnegative, so it is zero almost surely. Each of its finitely many nonnegative summands must therefore be zero almost surely.

If the mechanism attains $E=1$, then every ratio
$$ \frac{\mathbb E[M_t]}{\mu_t} $$
equals $1$: every ratio is at most $1$, and their minimum is $1$. Hence $\mathbb E[\mu_t-M_t]=0$ for every $t$, which again implies that all deficits vanish almost surely.

Conversely, filling every supported type almost surely attains both benchmarks. Therefore, either objective is attained exactly if and only if
$$ M_t=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t,\quad\text{almost surely}. $$

Moreover, a perfect execution uses the entire supply of every arrival. Indeed,
$$ \sum_{j=1}^{H}\left(q_j-\sum_t x_{tj}\right) =1-\sum_tM_t=0, $$
and every summand is nonnegative.

It follows that the mechanisms achieving exact optimality are the same for the two objectives. In particular,
$$ B_K^*(m,\tau,H,L)=B_E^*(m,\tau,H,L). $$
Denote their common value by $B^*(m,\tau,H,L)$.

2. A finite linear-program test for sharing one online policy

Fix initially known data $I$: the catalog, society, horizon, and length bound, with the specified values of $m,\tau,H,L$.

Let $\mathcal F_I$ be the finite set of all arrival sequences consistent with $I$ that satisfy the perfect-assignment promise and the total length bound. Different encodings of the same sequence can be deduplicated.

For a nonempty subfamily $S\subseteq\mathcal F_I$, let $\mathcal P(S)$ be the set of its distinct nonempty arrival prefixes. A prefix records all revealed pairs, including their exact rational supplies.

For $p\in\mathcal P(S)$, let $r(p)$ and $q(p)$ be the item type and supply of its last arrival. For $\sigma\in S$, write $p_j(\sigma)$ for its length-$j$ prefix.

Introduce one variable $z_{t,p}$ for each supported type $t$ and each prefix $p\in\mathcal P(S)$. Consider the following rational linear program:
$$ z_{t,p}\ge0, $$
$$ z_{t,p}=0 \qquad\text{if }e_{t,r(p)}=0, $$
$$ \sum_t z_{t,p}=q(p) \qquad\text{for every }p\in\mathcal P(S), $$
$$ \sum_{j=1}^{H}z_{t,p_j(\sigma)}=\mu_t \qquad\text{for every }\sigma\in S\text{ and every }t. $$

Call this system $\operatorname{LP}_I(S)$, and call $S$ *compatible* when it is feasible. Declare the empty family compatible.

Lemma

A family $S$ is compatible if and only if one deterministic online policy can perfectly match every sequence in $S$, without receiving advice that distinguishes members of $S$.

Proof

Suppose such a policy exists. Its allocation at an observed prefix $p$ is the same for every sequence in $S$ having that prefix. Use that allocation as $z_{t,p}$. Eligibility and nonnegativity hold. Perfect matching uses every arrival’s supply and fills every supported type, so all displayed equations hold.

Conversely, suppose the system is feasible. Its feasible region is bounded: for every variable,
$$ 0\le z_{t,p}\le q(p). $$
A nonempty bounded rational polytope has a rational vertex. Thus we may choose a rational feasible solution.

When the observed prefix is $p$, allocate $z_{t,p}$ to type $t$. Eligibility and supply constraints hold by construction.

The cumulative capacity constraints also hold. Every prefix $p\in\mathcal P(S)$ extends to some $\sigma\in S$. Nonnegativity and the terminal equation for that sequence imply
$$ \sum_{\substack{u\text{ nonempty}\\u\text{ prefix of }p}}z_{t,u} \le \sum_{j=1}^{H}z_{t,p_j(\sigma)} =\mu_t. $$
At the end of every sequence in $S$, equality holds.

To define the policy on all other histories, let it allocate zero forever after the first prefix outside $\mathcal P(S)$. All allocations preceding that departure were feasible, so this extension remains a valid mechanism. This proves the lemma. $\square$

The test contains at most $\tau H|S|$ variables and polynomially many constraints in $\tau,H,|S|$. Its coefficients and right-hand sides are explicitly determined by the finite input family.

3. Exact budgets for all four parameters

Define $c_I$ to be the smallest number of compatible families needed to partition $\mathcal F_I$.

This number is obtained by an explicit finite recurrence. Set
$$ d_I(\varnothing)=0. $$
For every nonempty $S\subseteq\mathcal F_I$, set
$$ d_I(S)= 1+\min_{\substack{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq S\\ \operatorname{LP}_I(A)\text{ feasible}}} d_I(S\setminus A). $$
Then
$$ c_I=d_I(\mathcal F_I). $$

Every singleton is compatible by the promise, so this recurrence is always defined.

Let $\mathfrak I(m,\tau,H,L)$ be the set of initially known inputs having at least one promised completion within the length bound. The exact answer is
$$ \boxed{ B_K^*(m,\tau,H,L) = B_E^*(m,\tau,H,L) = \max_{I\in\mathfrak I(m,\tau,H,L)} \left\lceil\log_2 d_I(\mathcal F_I)\right\rceil. } $$
If there are no promised inputs, take the budget to be zero.

This is a finite characterization using enumeration, rational linear-program feasibility, and the displayed recurrence. It contains no optimization over unspecified online mechanisms.

Upper bound: a partition gives an advice protocol

Fix $I$ and a minimum compatible partition
$$ \mathcal F_I=S_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}\cdots\mathbin{\dot\cup}S_{c_I}. $$
The mechanism computes this partition and a rational feasible solution for each corresponding linear program.

The oracle sends the index of the class containing the actual sequence, using
$$ \left\lceil\log_2 c_I\right\rceil $$
bits. The mechanism follows that class’s policy from Section 2 and perfectly matches the sequence.

All these computations depend only on $I$. Thus a single mechanism can perform them for each initially known input. Its worst-case budget is the maximum in the boxed formula.

Lower bound: randomization and adaptive reading do not improve it

Suppose a randomized mechanism uses at most $B$ bits and is exactly optimal on every sequence in $\mathcal F_I$.

For each $\sigma\in\mathcal F_I$, choose a successful advice tape $a_\sigma$. This choice is independent of private randomness, as required in the question.

Represent the mechanism’s private randomness on a common random-tape probability space. For each advised sequence $(\sigma,a_\sigma)$, perfect matching occurs with probability one by Section 1. Since $\mathcal F_I$ is finite, the intersection of all these success events also has probability one.

Fix a private random tape in that intersection. The mechanism now behaves deterministically and succeeds on every selected advised sequence.

For each sequence, record the bits read, in read order, and pad this transcript with zeros to length $B$. There are at most $2^B$ resulting strings. Partition $\mathcal F_I$ according to these padded transcripts.

Each resulting class is compatible. To see this, take two sequences in the same class and a common observed prefix. Starting from the same known input and fixed private random tape, their executions have the same observations through that prefix. Inductively, their next bit requests, answers, and allocations agree: the answers are the same entries of their common padded transcript. Consequently, the allocation at any shared prefix is the same. These allocations satisfy $\operatorname{LP}_I(S)$ for the class $S$.

This argument allows adaptive reading and early stopping. It uses precisely the assumption that tape length, timing, and unread contents supply no additional information.

Therefore $\mathcal F_I$ has a partition into at most $2^B$ compatible classes:
$$ c_I\le2^B. $$
Hence
$$ B\ge\left\lceil\log_2c_I\right\rceil. $$
Taking the worst case over $I$ proves the boxed formula.

In particular, randomization does not reduce the exact advice budget.

Equivalent hypergraph formulation

Construct a hypergraph whose vertices are the sequences in $\mathcal F_I$ and whose hyperedges are the inclusion-minimal incompatible families.

Compatibility is hereditary: restricting a feasible prefix policy to a smaller family remains feasible. Therefore a color class contains no hyperedge exactly when it is compatible. The hypergraph’s chromatic number is consequently $c_I$.

Thus the budget can equivalently be expressed through the chromatic numbers of explicit finite hypergraphs defined by rational linear programs.

Running time

Write $N=|\mathcal F_I|$. Enumerating all binary strings of length at most $L$, parsing them, and testing the promise takes
$$ 2^{O(L)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,H,L,B) $$
time under the encoding assumption.

Testing every subfamily uses $2^N$ rational linear programs, each of size polynomial in $N,\tau,H,L$. The recurrence examines at most $3^N$ pairs $A\subseteq S$. Thus the exact computation takes
$$ 3^N\operatorname{poly}(N,m,\tau,H,L,B) $$
time after enumeration.

Since $N\le2^{L+1}-1$, this gives the finite bound
$$ 2^{\,2^{O(L)}}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,H,L,B). $$
Rational linear-program feasibility is applicable because all coefficients are rational and explicitly available. Its rational solutions have polynomial bit length in their linear-program descriptions.

No efficiency claim stronger than this is needed for the advice characterization.

4. A smaller sufficient message: aggregate supplies

The exact characterization does not require sending the future sequence. A simpler upper bound can omit its order altogether.

For each catalog item type $r$, define its eligibility neighborhood
$$ C_r=\{t:e_{tr}=1\}. $$
Let $\mathcal C$ be the distinct neighborhoods in the catalog. For a sequence, define
$$ s_C=\sum_{j:C_{r_j}=C}q_j. $$

Knowing the vector $(s_C)_{C\in\mathcal C}$ is sufficient.

Indeed, aggregating a perfect offline assignment by neighborhood gives rational numbers $y_{tC}$ satisfying
$$ y_{tC}\ge0,\qquad y_{tC}=0\text{ if }t\notin C, $$
$$ \sum_t y_{tC}=s_C,\qquad \sum_Cy_{tC}=\mu_t. $$
Such numbers can also be computed by rational maximum flow.

On an arrival with neighborhood $C$ and supply $q_j$, allocate
$$ x_{tj}=\frac{q_j}{s_C}y_{tC}. $$
The denominator is positive because $C$ has arrived. This allocation is eligible and uses exactly $q_j$. Its cumulative allocation to each type never exceeds its terminal allocation, since all terms are nonnegative. At termination,
$$ \sum_jx_{tj} = \sum_{C:s_C>0}\frac{y_{tC}}{s_C} \sum_{j:C_{r_j}=C}q_j = \sum_Cy_{tC} = \mu_t. $$

For fixed known input $I$, let $D_I$ be the number of distinct aggregate vectors among $\mathcal F_I$. Enumerating those vectors permits a fixed-length index, giving
$$ \left\lceil\log_2c_I\right\rceil \le \left\lceil\log_2D_I\right\rceil \le \left\lceil\log_2|\mathcal F_I|\right\rceil \le L+1. $$
Consequently,
$$ B^*(m,\tau,H,L)\le L+1. $$

If the serialization splits into a known block of length $\ell(I)$ and an arrival block, set $R=L-\ell(I)$. The same counting argument gives the more local bound
$$ \left\lceil\log_2c_I\right\rceil\le R+1. $$
The exact recurrence further accounts for required arrival encoding and promise restrictions; it does not treat the known part of the input as unknown information.

Aggregate-vector enumeration takes exponential time in $L$. Once the vector is decoded, flow computation and online allocation take $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,H,L,B)$ time.

5. Zero-advice regimes

One supported type or one arrival

If $\tau=1$, every catalog item type is eligible for the sole supported type, by the catalog assumption. Allocating all supplies to that type succeeds.

If $H=1$, the only supply is $1$. The promise implies that its item type is eligible for every supported type. Allocate $\mu_t$ to each type.

Thus both cases require zero advice.

At most two item types

For two item types, partition the supported types into those eligible only for item type $1$, only for item type $2$, and for both. Let their total masses be $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$, respectively.

The promise excludes types eligible for neither, so
$$ \alpha+\beta+\gamma=1. $$
Let $S_1,S_2$ be the final aggregate supplies. Offline feasibility implies
$$ S_1\ge\alpha,\qquad S_2\ge\beta,\qquad S_1+S_2=1. $$

Use the following rule: each arrival fills its exclusive group first, then the common group. Fill individual types within a group in a fixed order.

For a prefix with observed supplies $a,b$, the supply directed to the common group is
$$ g(a,b)=\max\{0,a-\alpha\}+\max\{0,b-\beta\}. $$
Because $a\le S_1$ and $b\le S_2$,
$$ g(a,b) \le(S_1-\alpha)+(S_2-\beta) =\gamma. $$

This proves inductively that the common group always has capacity for the excess supply: after an arrival, the cumulative exclusive allocations are $\min\{a,\alpha\}$ and $\min\{b,\beta\}$, and the cumulative excess is exactly $g(a,b)\le\gamma$. Nothing is discarded.

At termination, both exclusive groups are filled and $g(S_1,S_2)=\gamma$, so the common group is filled as well. The one-item case follows immediately from the promise.

Hence
$$ B^*(m,\tau,H,L)=0 \qquad\text{if }m\le2. $$
This rule takes $O(H\tau)$ rational arithmetic operations and polynomial bit time in the input length.

6. An explicit exact characterization for two arrivals

Suppose $H=2$. Fix a possible first arrival with neighborhood $A$ and supply $f$. The second supply is necessarily $1-f$.

For a possible second neighborhood $C$, put
$$ D_C=T\setminus C, \qquad \mu(U)=\sum_{t\in U}\mu_t. $$

The two arrivals admit a perfect assignment exactly when
$$ D_C\subseteq A,\qquad \mu(D_C)\le f\le\mu(A). $$

Necessity follows because all types outside $C$ must be filled in round one, which must use its full supply. For sufficiency, fill $D_C$ first and distribute the remaining first-round supply within $A\setminus D_C$. The inequalities guarantee enough capacity. All remaining demand is eligible for round two and totals $1-f$.

Let $\mathcal D_{A,f}$ be the distinct exclusion sets of second arrivals consistent with the promise and length bound after this first arrival.

Define $c_{A,f}$ as the minimum number of subsets $U_1,\ldots,U_k\subseteq A$ satisfying
$$ \mu(U_i)\le f $$
such that every $D\in\mathcal D_{A,f}$ is contained in some $U_i$.

This is an exact finite set-cover problem.

To justify it, a family of second possibilities can share a first allocation if and only if
$$ \mu\left(\bigcup_D D\right)\le f. $$
Necessity holds because their common first allocation must fill their union. For sufficiency, fill the union and distribute the remaining supply within $A$.

Different first arrivals have disjoint prefix branches and hence independent allocation variables. Their local classes can reuse the same class indices. Therefore, for fixed $I$,
$$ c_I=\max_{(A,f)\text{ possible first arrival}}c_{A,f}. $$
Together with Section 3, this determines the exact finite-$L$ two-round budget.

Pairwise compatibility is insufficient

Take three types of masses
$$ \mu_1=\mu_2=\frac14,\qquad \mu_3=\frac12. $$
The common first arrival is universal with supply $3/4$. There are three possible second neighborhoods $T\setminus\{i\}$, each with supply $1/4$.

Every pair of these sequences is compatible: the union of their forced singleton sets has mass at most $3/4$. All three are incompatible because their union has mass $1$.

Thus an ordinary graph of pairwise incompatibilities would miss a necessary advice bit. The hypergraph or linear-program formulation is needed.

A sharp precision-independent bound

The inclusion-maximal members of $\mathcal D_{A,f}$ suffice as the covering sets $U_i$. They form an antichain, so their number is at most
$$ \binom{\tau}{\lfloor\tau/2\rfloor}. $$

For completeness, an antichain $\mathcal A$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{D\in\mathcal A}|D|!(\tau-|D|)!\le\tau!, $$
because a permutation’s initial sets are nested and therefore include at most one antichain member. Dividing by $\tau!$ and using the maximality of the central binomial coefficient proves the bound.

There are also at most $\max\{1,m-1\}$ maximal exclusion sets. If $A\ne T$, the first item type cannot recur as the second item type in a promised sequence. If $A=T$, its own exclusion set is empty, which is maximal only when every possible exclusion set is empty.

Consequently, writing
$$ N(m,\tau)= \min\left\{\max\{1,m-1\}, \binom{\tau}{\lfloor\tau/2\rfloor}\right\}, $$
we obtain
$$ B^*(m,\tau,2,L)\le\left\lceil\log_2N(m,\tau)\right\rceil. $$

This bound is attained for sufficiently large $L$, for each fixed $m,\tau$. Here is a complete construction when $N(m,\tau)>1$.

Put $k=\lfloor\tau/2\rfloor$. Choose a power of two
$$ Q>\tau(2k+1). $$
Assign each type mass either $\lfloor Q/\tau\rfloor/Q$ or $\lceil Q/\tau\rceil/Q$, choosing the counts to make their sum $1$. These positive dyadic masses differ from $1/\tau$ by less than $1/Q$, so
$$ \min_{|U|=k+1}\mu(U) - \max_{|D|=k}\mu(D) > \frac1\tau-\frac{2k+1}{Q} >0. $$

Choose $N(m,\tau)$ distinct $k$-element sets $D_i$. Include a universal item type and item types with neighborhoods $T\setminus D_i$; pad the catalog with universal duplicates if necessary. Let
$$ f=\max_{|D|=k}\mu(D). $$
The common first arrival is universal with supply $f$. Sequence $i$ then has second neighborhood $T\setminus D_i$ and supply $1-f$.

Each sequence is promised feasible. A shared first allocation for two distinct sequences would fill $D_i\cup D_j$, which has at least $k+1$ elements and mass greater than $f$. Therefore every compatible class has at most one sequence, and Section 3 gives the matching lower bound.

The construction has finite encoding length for fixed $m,\tau$. If $N(m,\tau)=1$, the zero upper bound already proves equality.

7. Linear advice is necessary with fixed small structural parameters

Take two supported types of mass $1/2$ each, and three item types:

  • $F$, eligible for both;
  • $A$, eligible only for the first;
  • $B$, eligible only for the second.

For $0<a<1/2$, consider
$$ \sigma_a= \left((F,1/2),(A,a),(B,1/2-a)\right). $$
A perfect assignment exists. Its first-round allocation is forced:
$$ x_{1,1}=\frac12-a,\qquad x_{2,1}=a. $$
Indeed, perfect matching must use the later exclusive supplies in full, leaving exactly these demands for the first arrival.

For an integer $b\ge2$, choose
$$ a=\frac{k}{2^{b+1}}, \qquad 1\le k\le2^b-1. $$
These $2^b-1$ sequences have identical known data and first arrival. No two can share a perfect online policy, because their forced first allocations differ. Thus
$$ B^*(3,2,3,L)\ge \left\lceil\log_2(2^b-1)\right\rceil=b $$
whenever the length bound includes this family.

Each sequence has encoding length at most $Cb+C_0$, for encoding-dependent constants $C,C_0$. Choosing $b$ proportional to $L$ and using the upper bound $L+1$ yields
$$ B^*(3,2,3,L)=\Theta(L). $$

Extension to every fixed nondegenerate parameter triple

Fix $m\ge3$, $\tau\ge2$, and $H\ge3$.

Partition the supported types into two nonempty groups, each of total mass $1/2$, using fixed positive dyadic masses. Use $F$ eligible for both groups, $A$ only for the first, and $B$ only for the second. Additional catalog items may duplicate $F$.

Set $p=0$ if $H=3$. Otherwise prepend $H-3$ fixed positive dyadic $A$-arrivals totaling $p=1/8$. Such a split exists: for $h=H-3$, choose a power of two $Q\ge h$, give the first $h-1$ arrivals supply $p/Q$, and the last supply $p-(h-1)p/Q$.

After this common prefix, use
$$ (F,1/2),\qquad (A,a),\qquad (B,1/2-p-a), $$
where
$$ a=\frac{k}{2^{b+2}}, \qquad 1\le k\le2^b-1. $$
All supplies are positive and sum to $1$, since $0<a<1/4$.

A perfect assignment exists. The prefix fills mass $p$ in the first group. The flexible arrival contributes
$$ \frac12-p-a $$
to the first group and $p+a$ to the second. The final exclusive arrivals complete both groups. These rational group allocations can be distributed among their supported types without exceeding individual capacities.

Through the common prefix ending with the flexible arrival, perfect matching forces cumulative allocation to the first group to be
$$ \frac12-a, $$
because its only remaining supply is $a$. Distinct values of $a$ therefore require incompatible prefix allocations.

There are $2^b-1$ such sequences, so the budget is at least $b$. All masses, catalog padding, and prefix supplies have fixed encoding length when $m,\tau,H$ are fixed; the varying supplies require $O(b)$ bits. Hence
$$ \boxed{ B^*(m,\tau,H,L)=\Theta(L) } $$
for every fixed $m\ge3$, $\tau\ge2$, $H\ge3$.

The constants depend on the fixed parameters and encoding. This asymptotic statement is not a uniform lower bound when those parameters grow with $L$; the exact characterization in Section 3 covers that joint regime.

8. Answer to the information question

The oracle need not describe future order, repeated arrivals, or distinctions between catalog items with identical eligibility neighborhoods. Aggregate supplies suffice, and compatible families can compress the advice further.

Nevertheless, numerical precision alone can force linearly many advice bits. The three-round construction makes a first allocation depend injectively on an unrevealed rational supply. Randomization cannot remove that information requirement, because exact optimality entails perfect matching almost surely.

For arbitrary finite parameters, the partition recurrence gives the exact budget under the chosen serialization. It replaces the unresolved joint asymptotic issue with an explicit, terminating computation.
