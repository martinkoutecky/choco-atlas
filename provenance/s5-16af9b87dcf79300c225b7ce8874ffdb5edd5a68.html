Theorem 1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), fixed existing-cascade seed memberships, candidate seed capacities by type, rational interaction rates \(\lambda_{tu}\), cascade priorities by type, and a rational budget \(\beta\), choose rational seed masses \(x_t\) with \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_t=\beta\). For each admissible population size \(N\), users of type \(t\) have independent directed edges to users of type \(u\) with probability \(\lambda_{tu}/N\). In the \(N\to\infty\) limit, each still-susceptible type-\(u\) user receives independent Poisson arrivals from type-\(t\), cascade-\(c\) users newly activated in the previous round, with mean \(\lambda_{tu}d^r_{t,c}\), and adopts the highest-priority arriving cascade. If \(q^\infty_{t,\mathrm{new}}(x)\) is the final mass of type \(t\) activated by the new cascade, compute a feasible \(x\) with \(J(x)\le\inf_y J(y)+\varepsilon\), where \(J(x)=1-\sum_{t\in T}q^\infty_{t,\mathrm{new}}(x)\) and \(\varepsilon>0\) is a requested additive error.

There are \(m\ge2\) cascades, forming \(C=C_e\cup\{c_{\mathrm{new}}\}\). Each type specifies its fixed existing-seed membership set \(B_t\subseteq C_e\), a strict priority order \(\succ_t\) on \(C\), and a binary rational candidate capacity \(a_t\in[0,\mu_t]\). Interaction rates \(\lambda_{tu}\) are nonnegative binary rationals.
A feasible choice consists of rational masses \(0\le x_t\le a_t\) with \(\sum_t x_t=\beta\), where the binary rational budget satisfies \(0<\beta\le\sum_t a_t\). At round zero, mass \(x_t\) adopts the highest-priority cascade in \(B_t\cup\{c_{\mathrm{new}}\}\); the remaining mass adopts the highest-priority member of \(B_t\), or stays susceptible when \(B_t=\varnothing\). Thus seed overlaps are resolved by the same priority rule as simultaneous arrivals.
Let \(d^r_{t,c}\) be mass newly adopting cascade \(c\) in round \(r\), and let \(s_t^r\) be susceptible mass after that round. Adoption is permanent, and only newly activated mass transmits in the next round. Define \(h^r_{u,c}=\sum_{t\in T}\lambda_{tu}d^r_{t,c}\).

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. In particular, an algorithm with the stated guarantee and running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

This holds even with two cascades, dyadic input masses and capacities, polynomially bounded integer interaction rates, and diffusion terminating after round one. The proof below uses the specified mass recurrence directly.

1. Constructing the society

We reduce from CLIQUE: given a simple undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\) and an integer \(k\), decide whether \(G\) contains a clique of size \(k\). This is a standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem. [Karp, 1972](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9)

Write \(V=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and assume \(n\ge2\) and \(2\le k\le n\). Inputs outside this range are decidable directly, so this restriction preserves hardness. Let \(d_i\) be the degree of vertex \(i\), and define

\(N=n^2-|E|.\)

Let \(Q\) and \(P\) be the smallest powers of two satisfying \(Q\ge n+1\) and \(P\ge N\), respectively. Set

\(b=\dfrac{1}{4Q},\qquad w=\dfrac{1}{4P},\qquad \rho=1-(n+1)b-Nw.\)

Since \((n+1)b\le1/4\) and \(Nw\le1/4\), we have \(\rho\ge1/2\).

There are two cascades: an existing cascade \(c_0\) and the new cascade \(c_1=c_{\mathrm{new}}\). Introduce the following supported types.

| Types | Mass | Candidate capacity | Existing-seed membership | Priority |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| \(v_i\), for each vertex \(i\) | \(b\) | \(b\) | \(\{c_0\}\) | \(c_1\succ c_0\) |
| One auxiliary source \(v_\ast\) | \(b\) | \(0\) | \(\{c_0\}\) | \(c_0\succ c_1\) |
| \(r_{ij}\), for each edge \(\{i,j\}\in E\) | \(w\) | \(0\) | \(\varnothing\) | \(c_0\succ c_1\) |
| \(p_i\), for each vertex \(i\) | \((n-d_i)w\) | \(0\) | \(\varnothing\) | \(c_0\succ c_1\) |
| One inert type \(v_{\mathrm{res}}\) | \(\rho\) | \(0\) | \(\{c_0\}\) | \(c_0\succ c_1\) |

All listed masses are positive: in particular, \(d_i\le n-1\), so \(n-d_i\ge1\). Their sum is one, because

\(|E|+\sum_{i=1}^n(n-d_i)=|E|+n^2-2|E|=N.\)

All masses and capacities are dyadic.

The only nonzero interaction rates are:

  • For every edge \(\{i,j\}\in E\), set \(\lambda_{v_i,r_{ij}}=\lambda_{v_j,r_{ij}}=1/b\).
  • For every vertex \(i\), set \(\lambda_{v_i,p_i}=\lambda_{v_\ast,p_i}=1/b\).

Every unspecified rate is zero. Thus every receiver has zero outgoing rates, and every source has zero incoming rates.

Set the budget to

\(\beta=kb.\)

Only the vertex types have positive capacities. Consequently, a feasible allocation is equivalent to a rational vector \(z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\) satisfying

\(x_{v_i}=bz_i,\qquad 0\le z_i\le1,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^n z_i=k.\)

The budget is admissible because \(0<kb\le nb=\sum_t a_t\).

2. Computing the diffusion exactly

At round zero, the stipulated overlap rule gives

\(d^0_{v_i,c_1}=bz_i,\qquad d^0_{v_i,c_0}=b(1-z_i),\qquad s^0_{v_i}=0.\)

The auxiliary source adopts \(c_0\) with mass \(b\), and the inert type adopts \(c_0\) with mass \(\rho\). Every receiver is initially susceptible.

For an edge receiver \(r_{ij}\), the round-zero arrival intensities are

\(h^0_{r_{ij},c_1}=z_i+z_j,\qquad h^0_{r_{ij},c_0}=2-z_i-z_j.\)

Because \(c_0\succ_{r_{ij}}c_1\), the recurrence gives

\(d^1_{r_{ij},c_1}=w(1-e^{-(z_i+z_j)})e^{-(2-z_i-z_j)}=w\bigl(e^{-2+z_i+z_j}-e^{-2}\bigr).\)

Likewise, at receiver \(p_i\),

\(h^0_{p_i,c_1}=z_i,\qquad h^0_{p_i,c_0}=2-z_i,\)

and therefore

\(d^1_{p_i,c_1}=(n-d_i)w\bigl(e^{-2+z_i}-e^{-2}\bigr).\)

All round-one adopters are receivers with zero outgoing rates. All sources were already fully adopted at round zero. Hence every arrival intensity in round one is zero, and

\(d^r_{t,c}=0\qquad\text{for every }r\ge2.\)

Define the total new-cascade adoption by \(F(z)=1-J(x)\). We have the exact formula

\(F(z)=\beta+w\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}\bigl(e^{-2+z_i+z_j}-e^{-2}\bigr)+w\sum_{i=1}^n(n-d_i)\bigl(e^{-2+z_i}-e^{-2}\bigr).\)

Thus there is no infinite-horizon approximation involved in this construction.

3. Fractional allocations cannot improve the optimum

The displayed expression for \(F\) is convex in \(z\): each nonconstant term is a positive multiple of the exponential of an affine function.

We prove explicitly that every feasible vector is dominated by an integral feasible vector.

Suppose a feasible \(z\) is not integral. Since \(\sum_i z_i=k\) is an integer, at least two coordinates, say \(z_i,z_j\), are strictly between zero and one. Let \(e_i,e_j\) denote the corresponding coordinate vectors, and set

\(\alpha=\min\{z_i,1-z_j\}>0,\qquad \gamma=\min\{1-z_i,z_j\}>0.\)

Both vectors

\(z^-=z-\alpha(e_i-e_j),\qquad z^+=z+\gamma(e_i-e_j)\)

are feasible. Each has fewer fractional coordinates than \(z\), and

\(z=\dfrac{\gamma}{\alpha+\gamma}z^-+\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+\gamma}z^+.\)

Convexity implies

\(F(z)\le\dfrac{\gamma}{\alpha+\gamma}F(z^-)+\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+\gamma}F(z^+)\le\max\{F(z^-),F(z^+)\}.\)

Choose an endpoint with value at least \(F(z)\), and repeat. The number of fractional coordinates decreases each time, so the procedure terminates at an integral feasible vector. If the original vector is rational, every endpoint is rational.

It follows that the maximum over feasible rational allocations is attained at \(z=\mathbf{1}_S\) for some set \(S\subseteq V\) with \(|S|=k\).

4. Integral values encode induced edges

Define the positive constants

\(A=e^{-1}-e^{-2},\qquad \Gamma=(1-e^{-1})^2.\)

For an integral vector \(z=\mathbf{1}_S\), an edge receiver contributes, after removing its factor \(w\):

  • \(0\), if neither endpoint belongs to \(S\);
  • \(A\), if exactly one endpoint belongs to \(S\);
  • \(1-e^{-2}=2A+\Gamma\), if both endpoints belong to \(S\).

Let \(e_G(S)\) be the number of edges with both endpoints in \(S\). The sum of the linear contributions from edge receivers is \(A\sum_{i\in S}d_i\). The balancing receivers contribute \(A\sum_{i\in S}(n-d_i)\). Therefore,

\(F(\mathbf{1}_S)=\beta+w\bigl(nkA+\Gamma e_G(S)\bigr).\)

Combining this identity with the preceding rounding argument gives

\(\max_{z\text{ feasible rational}}F(z)=\beta+w\left(nkA+\Gamma\max_{|S|=k}e_G(S)\right).\)

This identity handles the continuous budget exactly.

5. An inverse-polynomial additive gap

Let

\(K=\binom{k}{2},\qquad V_\ast=\beta+w(nkA+\Gamma K).\)

If \(G\) contains a clique of size \(k\), then \(\max_{|S|=k}e_G(S)=K\), so

\(\max F=V_\ast.\)

If \(G\) contains no such clique, then every \(k\)-vertex set has at most \(K-1\) induced edges. Thus

\(\max F\le V_\ast-w\Gamma.\)

Moreover, \(e>2\), as follows already from the first three terms of its power series. Hence \(e^{-1}<1/2\), and

\(\Gamma=(1-e^{-1})^2>\dfrac14.\)

Consequently, the two cases have a gap greater than \(w/4\).

Supply the approximation algorithm with the dyadic accuracy

\(\varepsilon=\dfrac{w}{32}.\)

Its guarantee is equivalent to

\(F(z)\ge\max F-\varepsilon.\)

Therefore, its returned allocation satisfies:

  • In the clique case, \(F(z)\ge V_\ast-w/32\).
  • In the non-clique case, \(F(z)\le\max F<V_\ast-w/4\).

It remains to justify that these cases can be distinguished using polynomial-time numerical computation.

6. Polynomial-precision evaluation suffices

Every exponential argument in \(F(z)\) belongs to \([-2,0]\). The expression for \(V_\ast\) uses only \(e^{-1}\) and \(e^{-2}\).

For a rational \(q\in[-2,0]\), approximate \(e^q\) by

\(T_M(q)=\sum_{j=0}^M\dfrac{q^j}{j!}.\)

For \(M\ge3\), the absolute tail is bounded by

\(\left|e^q-T_M(q)\right|\le\sum_{j=M+1}^{\infty}\dfrac{2^j}{j!}<16\cdot2^{-M}.\)

Indeed, starting at the fourth term, the ratio of successive terms \(2^j/j!\) is at most \(1/2\). Thus absolute error at most \(\eta\) requires only \(M=O(\log(1/\eta))\) terms. Exact rational evaluation of this finite sum has polynomial bit complexity in the bit length of \(q\) and in \(M\).

Choose

\(\eta=\dfrac{w}{256n^2},\)

and approximate every required exponential to absolute error at most \(\eta\). Let \(\widehat F\) and \(\widehat V_\ast\) be the resulting rational evaluations.

For \(F\), the total receiver mass is \(Nw\le1/4\), and each difference of two exponentials has error at most \(2\eta\). Hence

\(|\widehat F-F|\le2Nw\eta\le\dfrac{\eta}{2}\le\dfrac{w}{64}.\)

For \(V_\ast\), the errors in \(A\) and \(\Gamma\) are at most \(2\eta\) and \(3\eta\), respectively. Since \(k\le n\) and \(K\le n^2/2\),

\(|\widehat V_\ast-V_\ast|\le w(2nk+3K)\eta\le4wn^2\eta=\dfrac{w^2}{64}\le\dfrac{w}{64}.\)

We can now decide CLIQUE by testing whether

\(\widehat F-\widehat V_\ast>-\dfrac{w}{8}.\)

In the clique case,

\(\widehat F-\widehat V_\ast\ge-\dfrac{w}{32}-\dfrac{w}{64}-\dfrac{w}{64}=-\dfrac{w}{16}>-\dfrac{w}{8}.\)

In the non-clique case,

\(\widehat F-\widehat V_\ast<-\dfrac{w}{4}+\dfrac{w}{64}+\dfrac{w}{64}=-\dfrac{7w}{32}<-\dfrac{w}{8}.\)

The test therefore distinguishes the two cases correctly.

7. Complexity and conclusion

The constructed society has

\(m=2,\qquad \tau=|E|+2n+2=O(n^2).\)

Its interaction rates are either zero or \(1/b=4Q<8(n+1)\). Every input number has \(O(\log n)\) bits, and even an explicit full interaction matrix has polynomial encoding length.

Because \(P<2N\),

\(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}=128P<256N\le256n^2.\)

Thus an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\) would run in polynomial time on these instances. Its rational output would have polynomial length, and the numerical comparison proved above would also take polynomial time.

One call to such an algorithm would therefore decide CLIQUE in polynomial time. This proves the requested additive approximation task is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and rules out the proposed running-time guarantee unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). No claim of hardness for a fixed constant accuracy is needed.
