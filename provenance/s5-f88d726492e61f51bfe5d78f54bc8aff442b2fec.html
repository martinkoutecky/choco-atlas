Proposition 4 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses μ_t summing to \(1\), per-person reform advantages \(d_t\), and per-capita lobby budgets \(b_0,b_1\), lobby 1 chooses offers \(p_t\ge0\). Find the minimum spend \(V=\min\sum_tμ_t p_t\) subject to \(\sum_t z_t(p_t+d_t)\ge b_0\) for every blocking mass vector \(0\le z_t\leμ_t\) with \(\sum_t z_t\ge1/2\); return an optimal offer vector and determine whether \(b_1\ge V\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=2\) alternatives, status quo and reform; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Each type has a binary rational per-person reform advantage \(d_t\ge0\). The per-capita lobby budgets are binary rationals satisfying \(0<b_0<b_1\). Offers are divisible money, and lobby 1 chooses one real offer \(p_t\ge0\) per person of type \(t\).
Lobby 1 moves first; lobby 0 observes its offers and moves once. Payments depend on the recipient's vote and are honored irrespective of the outcome. Individual indifference favors reform; reform requires strictly more than half the voting mass.
A blocking mass vector is any real vector \(z\) with \(0\le z_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t z_t\ge\tfrac12\); a type's mass may be split. An offer deters a profitable counteroffer exactly when \(\sum_t z_t(p_t+d_t)\ge b_0\) for every blocking vector, with equality admitted.
Define \(V=\min_{p\ge0}\{\sum_t\mu_t p_t:\sum_t z_t(p_t+d_t)\ge b_0\text{ for every blocking }z\}\). The computational problem is to compute \(V\), return an optimal offer vector, and decide whether \(b_1\ge V\).

Is this exact optimization problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is its budget decision problem NP-hard?

For an odd finite legislature with nonnegative reform advantages, an optimal offer exists that targets a prefix of legislators ordered by increasing advantage and equalizes their offer-plus-advantage; the targeted prefix is characterized explicitly ([Le Breton–Zaporozhets](https://tse-fr.eu/sites/default/files/medias/doc/wp/2007/sequential.pdf)).
Continuum lobbying models already yield structural coalition results ([Dippel](https://doi.org/10.1177/0951629811423092)); no source verified here establishes the computational complexity of the exact rational-support problem above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted the undefined domain of reform advantages to nonnegative binary rationals, following the discrete model's nonnegative preference parameters; zero advantages remain allowed.
  • Specified binary rational budgets with 0 < b_0 < b_1, carrying over the positive opposing stake and the first lobby's assumed budget advantage.
  • Made each positive type mass divisible and allowed real blocking vectors and real nonnegative offers, while retaining one common per-person offer for each type.
  • Specified strict majority for reform, so exactly half the mass can block, and retained reform-favoring individual indifference and weak deterrence inequalities.
  • Set m = 2 and defined L as total input bit length; no finite population size remains after normalization to total mass 1.

Result

The exact optimization problem is solvable in polynomial time. An optimal offer vector can be chosen to raise all advantages below a common threshold to that threshold. Both the optimal value and such an offer vector are rational and have polynomial encoding length.

The proof below uses the stated blocking constraint directly and allows blocking coalitions to split each type’s mass.

1. The cost of the cheapest blocking coalition

Write \(x_+=\max\{x,0\}\). For any nonnegative cost vector \(c=(c_t)_{t=1}^{\tau}\), define

\(H(c)=\min\left\{\sum_t z_t c_t:0\le z_t\le\mu_t,\ \sum_t z_t=\tfrac12\right\}\)

and

\(F_c(a)=\tfrac a2-\sum_t\mu_t(a-c_t)_+,\qquad a\in\mathbb R.\)

Because all \(c_t\) are nonnegative, minimizing over blocking vectors of mass at least \(\tfrac12\) gives the same value as minimizing over vectors of mass exactly \(\tfrac12\). Indeed, a vector of mass \(s>\tfrac12\) can be replaced by \(z/(2s)\), which respects every coordinate bound and weakly decreases its cost.

Threshold identity. For every nonnegative \(c\),

\(H(c)=\max_{a\in\mathbb R}F_c(a).\)

To prove this, consider any vector \(z\) of mass exactly \(\tfrac12\). For every real \(a\),

\(\sum_t z_t c_t=\tfrac a2-\sum_t z_t(a-c_t)_++\sum_t z_t(c_t-a)_+\ge\tfrac a2-\sum_t\mu_t(a-c_t)_+=F_c(a).\)

Thus \(H(c)\ge F_c(a)\) for every \(a\).

For equality, let \(q_c\) be the smallest support value satisfying

\(\sum_{t:c_t\le q_c}\mu_t\ge\tfrac12.\)

Its minimality gives \(\sum_{t:c_t<q_c}\mu_t<\tfrac12\). Choose all mass with \(c_t<q_c\), enough mass with \(c_t=q_c\) to reach exactly \(\tfrac12\), and no mass with \(c_t>q_c\). Type splitting makes this choice feasible. Its cost is

\(\sum_{t:c_t<q_c}\mu_t c_t+\left(\tfrac12-\sum_{t:c_t<q_c}\mu_t\right)q_c=F_c(q_c).\)

This proves the identity and shows that its maximum is attained.

Consequently, an offer vector \(p\) is feasible exactly when \(H(d+p)\ge b_0\).

2. A lower bound on every feasible expenditure

Let \(B(p)=\sum_t\mu_t p_t\). Since \(p_t\ge0\),

\(0\le(a-d_t)_+-(a-d_t-p_t)_+\le p_t.\)

Therefore, for every real \(a\),

\(F_{d+p}(a)\le F_d(a)+B(p).\)

If \(p\) is feasible, the threshold identity supplies an \(a\) with \(F_{d+p}(a)\ge b_0\). Also \(F_{d+p}(a)\le a/2\), so necessarily \(a\ge2b_0\). It follows that

\(B(p)\ge b_0-\max_{a\ge2b_0}F_d(a).\)

Together with nonnegativity of expenditure, this proves

\(V\ge\max\left\{0,\ b_0-\max_{a\ge2b_0}F_d(a)\right\}.\)

We next evaluate the scalar maximum explicitly.

3. Computing the relevant threshold

Let \(q\) be the smallest advantage value satisfying

\(\sum_{t:d_t\le q}\mu_t\ge\tfrac12.\)

Thus \(\sum_{t:d_t<q}\mu_t<\tfrac12\). Define

\(a_*=\max\{2b_0,q\}.\)

Claim. The maximum of \(F_d(a)\) over \(a\ge2b_0\) is attained at \(a_*\).

For \(u<v\le q\), only types with \(d_t<q\) can contribute to the difference of positive parts, and each contribution is at most \(v-u\). Hence

\(F_d(v)-F_d(u)\ge(v-u)\left(\tfrac12-\sum_{t:d_t<q}\mu_t\right)>0.\)

For \(q\le u<v\), every type with \(d_t\le q\) contributes exactly \(v-u\) to that difference. Consequently,

\(F_d(v)-F_d(u)\le(v-u)\left(\tfrac12-\sum_{t:d_t\le q}\mu_t\right)\le0.\)

Thus \(F_d\) increases up to \(q\) and is nonincreasing thereafter. Restricting its domain to \(a\ge2b_0\) gives the claimed maximizer.

Define

\(G(h)=\sum_t\mu_t(h-d_t)_+\)

and

\(R=\max\{0,\ b_0-F_d(a_*)\}=\max\{0,\ b_0-\tfrac{a_*}{2}+G(a_*)\}.\)

The preceding lower bound is \(V\ge R\).

4. An optimal offer vector attaining the bound

If \(R=0\), take \(p_t=0\) for every type. Then \(F_d(a_*)\ge b_0\), so the threshold identity proves feasibility.

Suppose \(R>0\). Since \(a_*\ge2b_0\),

\(R=b_0-\tfrac{a_*}{2}+G(a_*)\le G(a_*).\)

The function \(G\) is continuous, with \(G(0)=0\) because all advantages are nonnegative. Therefore there exists \(h\in[0,a_*]\) satisfying \(G(h)=R\). Set

\(p_t=(h-d_t)_+,\qquad c_t=d_t+p_t=\max\{d_t,h\}.\)

These offers respect the requirement of one per-person offer for each type, and their expenditure is

\(\sum_t\mu_t p_t=G(h)=R.\)

Because \(h\le a_*\), for every type,

\((a_*-d_t)_+-(a_*-c_t)_+=(h-d_t)_+.\)

Summing gives

\(F_c(a_*)=F_d(a_*)+G(h)=F_d(a_*)+R=b_0.\)

The threshold identity now implies \(H(c)\ge b_0\), so every blocking vector satisfies the required inequality. Equality is admissible by the problem’s assumptions.

This feasible vector attains the lower bound. Hence the exact formula is

\(\boxed{V=\max\left\{0,\ b_0-\tfrac{a_*}{2}+\sum_t\mu_t(a_*-d_t)_+\right\},\qquad a_*=\max\{2b_0,q\}.}\)

When expenditure is positive, the construction targets precisely the types with \(d_t<h\), equalizing their offer-plus-advantage at \(h\).

5. Exact algorithm and bit complexity

Sort the types so that \(d_1\le\cdots\le d_\tau\), preserving their corresponding masses.

  • Find the first index whose cumulative mass is at least \(\tfrac12\); its advantage is \(q\).
  • Compute \(a_*\) and the rational value \(R\) above.
  • If \(R=0\), return the zero offer vector.
  • Otherwise, compute the unique solution of \(G(h)=R\) by scanning the sorted breakpoints, then return \(p_t=(h-d_t)_+\).
  • Decide the budget question by the exact rational comparison \(b_1\ge R\).

For completeness, the breakpoint step requires no numerical approximation. Define

\(A_k=\sum_{i=1}^k\mu_i,\qquad D_k=\sum_{i=1}^k\mu_i d_i.\)

Choose the smallest \(k\) such that either \(k=\tau\) or

\(R\le A_k d_{k+1}-D_k.\)

Then set

\(h=\dfrac{R+D_k}{A_k}.\)

If \(k>1\), failure of the preceding comparison implies

\(R>A_{k-1}d_k-D_{k-1}=A_kd_k-D_k,\)

so \(h>d_k\). For \(k=1\), the same strict inequality follows from \(R>0\). If \(k<\tau\), the successful comparison gives \(h\le d_{k+1}\). Therefore exactly the first \(k\) indices can contribute to \(G(h)\), and

\(G(h)=A_kh-D_k=R.\)

Moreover, \(G\) is strictly increasing above \(d_1\): for \(v>u\ge d_1\), the first type alone contributes \(\mu_1(v-u)>0\) to \(G(v)-G(u)\). Since \(G(a_*)\ge R\), the computed solution satisfies \(h\le a_*\), as required in the feasibility proof.

Sorting uses \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) rational comparisons. All subsequent scans use \(O(\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations.

To check encoding lengths, let \(Q\) be the product of all input denominators. Its binary length is at most \(L\), up to encoding conventions. Input rationals and \(a_*\) have denominators dividing \(Q\); products such as \(\mu_t d_t\) have denominators dividing \(Q^2\). Thus all sums defining \(A_k,D_k,F_d(a_*)\), and \(R\) have polynomial bit length. The division defining \(h\), and the subtractions defining the offers, also produce rationals of polynomial bit length. Exact integer arithmetic and comparisons on these quantities therefore take polynomial time in \(\tau,L\).

Since \(m=2\) is fixed, this establishes polynomial time on the stated domain in \(m,\tau,L\). In particular, the budget decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm P\); it can be NP-hard under polynomial-time reductions only if \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
