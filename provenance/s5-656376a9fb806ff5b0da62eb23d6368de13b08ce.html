The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given types \(T\) with masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), housing categories \(H\) with rational per-capita supplies \(q_h\), and a strict preference list of at most two acceptable categories per type plus an outside option, allocate each unit of mass one indivisible dwelling or the outside option. Represent allocations by flows \(x_{th}\ge 0\), with \(\sum_h x_{th}\le \mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{th}\le q_h\). Call an allocation Pareto optimal if no positive submass can be reassigned feasibly so that every member of that submass strictly improves while all other assignments stay fixed. Compute the number of distinct exact supports \(S=\{h\in H:\sum_t x_{th}>0\}\) attained by Pareto-optimal allocations.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(H\) contain \(m\) housing categories with nonnegative binary rational supplies \(q_h\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type strictly ranks at most two distinct acceptable categories, all above an outside option \(\bot\) of unlimited supply; unlisted categories cannot be assigned.
An allocation consists of real flows \(x_{th}\geq 0\), zero for unlisted categories, satisfying \(\sum_{h\in H}x_{th}\leq\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{th}\leq q_h\); set \(x_{t\bot}=\mu_t-\sum_{h\in H}x_{th}\). Flows divide a type into submasses whose members each receive one dwelling or the outside option.
An allocation is Pareto optimal if no positive total submass can be moved to strictly preferred assignments while preserving feasibility and leaving every other member's assignment fixed. Submasses may be selected separately from each type and current assignment.
Define \(S(x)=\{h\in H:\sum_t x_{th}>0\}\) and let \(N\) be the number of distinct sets \(S(x)\) attained by Pareto-optimal allocations, excluding the outside option.

Can \(N\) be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computation \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?

For discrete applicants and unit-capacity houses, counting distinct image sets of Pareto-optimal matchings is \(\#\mathrm{P}\)-complete even with two-entry preference lists and unmatched applicants allowed ([source](https://real.mtak.hu/40588/1/1401.5354v4.pdf)).
No result settling this precise continuous support-counting problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Supplies were described only as rational; require nonnegative binary rational supplies, without imposing a total-supply normalization.
  • Specify that every listed category is strictly preferred to an unlimited outside option, and that unlisted categories cannot be assigned, following the incomplete-list convention.
  • Allow real allocation flows and arbitrary positive submasses selected from individual type-assignment groups; this specifies the continuous coalition semantics.
  • Use m for the number of housing categories and L for total input bit length.
  • For the exact counting target, interpret NP-hardness using polynomial-time Turing reductions.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Theorem. Computing \(N\) exactly is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions, even when all masses and supplies are dyadic rationals and all two-entry lists share the same first choice.

The proof gives a direct reduction from SUBSET SUM; it does not rely on transferring the discrete matching result to continuous allocations.

Construction. Let \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) be positive binary-encoded integers, and write \(W=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\). Choose a power of two \(D>W\). For a parameter \(Q\) satisfying \(0<Q<W/D\), construct the following society.

There are categories \(c,d,h_1,\ldots,h_n\) and supported types \(0,1,\ldots,n\). For each \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), set
\(\mu_i=w_i/D\), give type \(i\) the preference list \(c\succ h_i\succ\bot\), and set \(q_{h_i}=\mu_i\).

Type \(0\) has mass \(\mu_0=(D-W)/D\), accepts only \(d\), and has supply \(q_d=\mu_0\). Finally, set \(q_c=Q\).

Every mass is positive, their sum is \(1\), and every list has at most two acceptable categories. Each private category \(h_i\) can be assigned only to type \(i\), and \(d\) only to type \(0\).

Lemma 1: Pareto optimality in this construction. A feasible allocation is Pareto optimal if and only if every member receives an acceptable category and \(c\) is full.

*Proof.* First suppose an allocation is Pareto optimal.

If \(x_{i\bot}>0\) for some \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), the unused supply at \(h_i\) is
\(q_{h_i}-x_{ih_i}=\mu_i-x_{ih_i}=x_{ic}+x_{i\bot}\geq x_{i\bot}>0\).
Thus a positive submass can move from \(\bot\) to \(h_i\), strictly improving while everyone else stays fixed. This contradicts Pareto optimality. The same argument for type \(0\) shows that \(x_{0\bot}=0\), so \(x_{0d}=\mu_0\).

Consequently, all main-type mass \(W/D\) is assigned to \(c\) or private categories. Let \(z=\sum_{i=1}^n x_{ic}\). If \(z<Q\), then
\(\sum_{i=1}^n x_{ih_i}=W/D-z>0\).
Some private category therefore contains positive mass. Since \(c\) has positive unused supply, a sufficiently small positive submass can move from that private category to \(c\), strictly improving. Hence \(z=Q\).

Conversely, suppose nobody receives \(\bot\) and \(c\) is full. Members assigned to \(c\) or \(d\) already receive their first choice. The only possible strict improvements are moves from some \(h_i\) to \(c\). Any collection of such moves with positive total mass strictly increases occupancy of \(c\), and no member currently assigned to \(c\) can leave as part of a strictly improving move. Since all other members must stay fixed, the proposed improvement violates \(c\)'s supply constraint. Thus the allocation is Pareto optimal. \(\square\)

Lemma 2: Exact correspondence between supports and weighted subsets. Let \(N(Q)\) denote the number of Pareto-optimal supports in the constructed society. Then
\(N(Q)=\left|\left\{A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}:\sum_{i\in A}w_i\leq DQ\right\}\right|\).

*Proof.* For a Pareto-optimal allocation, define
\(A=\{i:x_{ih_i}=0\}\).
Lemma 1 implies that every type in \(A\) is assigned entirely to \(c\). Therefore
\(\sum_{i\in A}\mu_i\leq Q\), or equivalently \(\sum_{i\in A}w_i\leq DQ\).

Moreover, because \(Q>0\) and \(\mu_0>0\), the support is exactly
\(S(x)=\{c,d\}\cup\{h_i:i\notin A\}\).
Thus different subsets \(A\) give different supports.

It remains to prove that every subset satisfying the inequality is attainable. Fix such an \(A\), let \(T=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus A\), and define
\(R=Q-\sum_{i\in A}\mu_i\) and \(M=\sum_{i\in T}\mu_i\).
The assumed inequality gives \(R\geq0\), while
\(M-R=W/D-Q>0\).
Hence \(M>0\) and \(\theta=R/M\) satisfies \(0\leq\theta<1\).

For each \(i\in A\), assign its entire mass to \(c\). For each \(i\in T\), assign
\(x_{ic}=\theta\mu_i\) and \(x_{ih_i}=(1-\theta)\mu_i\).
Assign type \(0\) entirely to \(d\).

Every type is fully assigned. Each private supply constraint holds, and occupancy of \(c\) is
\(\sum_{i\in A}\mu_i+\theta M=\sum_{i\in A}\mu_i+R=Q\).
The allocation is therefore feasible and Pareto optimal by Lemma 1. Since \(\theta<1\), every \(h_i\) with \(i\in T\) receives positive mass, giving exactly the claimed support. \(\square\)

Reduction from SUBSET SUM. We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem: given positive binary integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) and an integer \(B\), decide whether some subset sums exactly to \(B\). This is the classical equality version of knapsack in [Karp’s reducibility theorem](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Targets outside \(1\leq B<W\) can be decided directly: negative targets and targets greater than \(W\) are impossible, while \(0\) and \(W\) are attained by the empty and full subsets. Thus only \(1\leq B<W\) requires oracle calls.

Construct two societies as above, differing only in the shared supply:
\(Q_+=B/D\) and \(Q_-=(2B-1)/(2D)\).
Both satisfy \(0<Q_\pm<W/D\). Lemma 2 gives
\(N(Q_+)=\left|\left\{A:\sum_{i\in A}w_i\leq B\right\}\right|\)
and
\(N(Q_-)=\left|\left\{A:\sum_{i\in A}w_i\leq B-\tfrac12\right\}\right|\).

Since every subset sum is an integer, their difference is exactly
\(N(Q_+)-N(Q_-)=\left|\left\{A:\sum_{i\in A}w_i=B\right\}\right|\).
The SUBSET SUM instance is therefore a yes-instance precisely when this difference is positive.

Encoding and conclusion. Choose \(D\) to be the least power of two strictly greater than \(W\). Its binary length is \(O(\log W)\). All constructed masses and supplies have numerators and denominators of \(O(\log W)\) bits, and all are dyadic rationals. Each instance has \(\tau=n+1\) supported types and \(m=n+2\) categories, so its total encoding length is polynomial in the original input length.

By Lemma 2, each oracle answer is at most \(2^n\), and therefore has at most \(n+1\) bits. Constructing the two instances, subtracting the answers, and testing positivity all take polynomial time.

This proves the requested \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions. In particular, a polynomial-time exact algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

  • The acceptability restrictions ensure that only type \(i\) can occupy \(h_i\), and only type \(0\) can occupy \(d\).

There is no reliance on unsupported types or on assigning unlisted categories.

2. Lemma 1: Necessity

The exclusion of outside-option mass is valid. For a main type, feasibility and the definition of outside-option flow give
\[ q_{h_i}-x_{ih_i} =\mu_i-x_{ih_i} =x_{ic}+x_{i\bot}. \]
Thus positive outside-option mass implies enough unused private supply to move a positive submass to an acceptable category. This improvement leaves all other members fixed.

For type \(0\), the corresponding unused supply is \(\mu_0-x_{0d}=x_{0\bot}\), so the same conclusion holds.

Once outside-option mass is excluded, private occupancy totals \(W/D-z\). If \(z<Q\), this total is positive because \(Q<W/D\). Some private category consequently has positive occupancy, while \(c\) has positive slack. A positive submass smaller than both quantities can strictly improve to \(c\).

These steps correctly prove that every Pareto-optimal allocation fully assigns every type to acceptable categories and fills \(c\).

3. Lemma 1: Sufficiency

The converse accounts for simultaneous improvements, not merely individual moves.

Members at \(c\) or \(d\) have no strictly preferred assignment. With nobody at the outside option, every possible strictly improving move is from a private category to \(c\). Any positive total mass of such moves increases occupancy of \(c\), and no strictly improving move releases capacity there.

Because \(c\) is already full, every proposed improvement violates feasibility. This matches the task’s definition of Pareto optimality.

4. Lemma 2: Supports imply weighted subsets

For a Pareto-optimal allocation, \(x_{ih_i}=0\) forces \(x_{ic}=\mu_i\). Therefore
\[ \sum_{i\in A}w_i\leq DQ. \]

Both \(c\) and \(d\) necessarily belong to the support, since their occupancies are \(Q>0\) and \(\mu_0>0\). Each private category belongs precisely when its index is outside \(A\). Consequently, the support determines \(A\) uniquely.

This establishes the required necessity and injectivity without confusing distinct allocations with distinct supports.

5. Lemma 2: Every qualifying subset is attainable

The stated calculations are correct:
\[ R\geq0,\qquad M-R=W/D-Q>0. \]
Hence \(M>0\) and \(0\leq\theta=R/M<1\). The construction never divides by zero.

The proposed flows are nonnegative, fully assign each type, respect every private supply, and give total occupancy \(Q\) at \(c\). Lemma 1 therefore applies.

Strict positivity of \((1-\theta)\mu_i\) for every \(i\in T\) ensures the exact desired support. The equality case \(R=0\) is included: then \(\theta=0\). The case \(T=\varnothing\) cannot arise under the qualifying inequality because \(Q<W/D\).

Thus the claimed correspondence is a bijection, and the formula for \(N(Q)\) is established.

6. SUBSET SUM reduction

The invoked theorem is the standard NP-completeness of deciding whether a subset of positive binary-encoded integers sums to an integer target. The positivity and encoding hypotheses match the reduction. This formulation is also explicitly recorded in [university lecture notes on NP-completeness](https://www.cs.sjsu.edu/~pollett/255.1.19s/Lec20190508.html).

The boundary handling is complete: targets below zero or above \(W\) are impossible, while zero and \(W\) are attained. Thus oracle calls are needed only when \(1\leq B<W\).

For that range, both supplies satisfy the construction’s strict parameter restrictions:
\[ 0<Q_-<Q_+<W/D. \]
Since subset sums are integers, subtracting the counts at thresholds \(B\) and \(B-\tfrac12\) counts exactly the subsets summing to \(B\). Testing whether this difference is positive correctly decides SUBSET SUM.

Two oracle calls followed by subtraction constitute a polynomial-time Turing reduction, as claimed.

The numbers of supported types and categories are linear in \(n\), and the complete constructed inputs have polynomial length in the source input.
