The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of types \(T\) with modular valuations \(v_t(B)=\sum_r a_{tr}B_r\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_r\), and a finitely supported rational initial distribution \(x^0_{t,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^R\), where \(\sum_B x^0_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}x^0_{t,B}B_r=q_r\), compute a finite rational sequence of one-resource deals. Each deal pairs donor and recipient cohorts and moves mass \(\epsilon>0\) from bundles \(B\) and \(D\) to \(B-e_r\) and \(D+e_r\), respectively, with payment balances updated by GUPF from initial balances \(\pi^0_{t,B}=v_t(B)-W(x^0)\), where \(W(x^0)=\sum_{t,B}x^0_{t,B}v_t(B)\). Require every deal to be individually rational and the final state to be efficient and envy-free across all occupied cohorts.

The input includes a rational initial distribution \(x^0\) with \(s\) occupied type–bundle pairs, satisfying \(\sum_Bx^0_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}x^0_{t,B}B_r=q_r\). All numbers, including bundle coordinates, use binary encoding; \(L\) is total input bit length.
A cohort has a type, bundle, positive mass, and per-person payment balance \(\pi\in\mathbb Q\); positive balances mean payments made, with no borrowing limit. Utility is \(u_t(B,\pi)=v_t(B)-\pi\), and welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}v_t(B)\).
Initially \(\pi^0_{t,B}=v_t(B)-W(x^0)\); this one-off payment need not be individually rational.

The computational problem is to produce an explicit finite rational sequence of these deals ending in an efficient, envy-free state; the empty sequence is allowed.

Proven progress and remaining complexity question

Polynomial-time construction of the required explicit sequence is impossible, unconditionally: some inputs require exponentially many deals.

A stronger constructive result is also available. Every input has a canonical valid sequence such that:

  • its length has polynomial bit length;
  • any specified deal can be computed in polynomial time from its binary index;
  • its terminal allocation and balances have a polynomial-size description.

These results do not establish NP-hardness for the original unbounded-output search problem. Below, I prove them and give a precise conditional obstruction to hardness under reductions that access solutions by deal index.

I interpret the intended sequence as consisting of individually rational deals. Both output-length lower bounds remain valid even if this restriction is dropped.

1. The payment invariant

For a person whose type is $t$, the GUPF update gives
$$ \begin{aligned} v_t(B')-\pi' &=v_t(B')-\bigl(\pi+v_t(B')-v_t(B)-\Delta W\bigr)\\ &=v_t(B)-\pi+\Delta W. \end{aligned} $$
Thus every person's utility increases by exactly $\Delta W$, including unchanged persons.

Initially every person's utility equals $W(x^0)$. Induction, with cohort splitting inheriting the existing balance, therefore proves
$$ \boxed{\pi=v_t(B)-W(x)} $$
for every occupied cohort at every state reached by a finite sequence.

For a deal transferring resource $r$ from type $t$ to type $u$, modularity gives
$$ \Delta W=\epsilon(a_{ur}-a_{tr}). $$
Because $\epsilon>0$, the participants strictly gain utility exactly when
$$ \boxed{a_{ur}>a_{tr}.} $$
When this holds, unchanged persons also strictly gain, satisfying their weaker requirement. Hence this inequality characterizes individually rational deals.

2. A canonical finite rational sequence

Index the initial occupied type–bundle pairs by $i=1,\ldots,s$. Write their types, bundles, and masses as
$$ t_i,\qquad B_i,\qquad x_i>0. $$
For each resource $r$, define
$$ M_r=\max_t a_{tr}, $$
and select a maximizing type $w_r$.

Let $H$ be the least common multiple of the denominators of the $x_i$, and put
$$ n_i=Hx_i. $$
Each $n_i$ is a positive integer, and
$$ \sum_i n_i=H. $$
Conceptually partition initial cohort $i$ into labeled cohorts
$$ c_{i,1},\ldots,c_{i,n_i}, $$
each of mass $1/H$. This partition is permitted by the splitting rule.

For each type $t$, choose one initial pair $i_t$ of that type and distinguish the cohort
$$ h_t=c_{i_t,1}. $$
Such a pair exists because every listed type has positive mass.

Define
$$ e_{ir}= \begin{cases} B_{ir},&a_{t_i r}<M_r,\\ 0,&a_{t_i r}=M_r. \end{cases} $$

Process triples $(i,j,r)$ in lexicographic order. For each triple, perform $e_{ir}$ deals transferring one unit of resource $r$ per person from $c_{i,j}$ to $h_{w_r}$, with mass $1/H$ in every deal.

Legality and individual rationality

Whenever $e_{ir}>0$, the donor's coefficient is strictly smaller than $M_r$. Consequently:

  • The donor's type differs from the recipient's type, so the two cohorts are disjoint.
  • The recipient is a maximizing type for resource $r$, so the deal is individually rational.
  • The donor never receives resource $r$ in this construction: all recipients of $r$ have maximizing types.
  • Before its $\ell$-th donation of $r$, the donor has

$$ B_{ir}-\ell+1\ge1 $$
units of that resource.

Thus every specified deal is legal and individually rational.

The number of deals is exactly
$$ \boxed{ K=\sum_{i=1}^s n_i\sum_{r=1}^m e_{ir}. } $$
This is finite. If $K=0$, the construction outputs the empty sequence.

3. Efficiency and envy-freeness of the terminal state

For any feasible allocation, define its mass-weighted resource quantities by
$$ Q_{tr}=\sum_B x_{t,B}B_r. $$
Then
$$ W(x)=\sum_{t,r}a_{tr}Q_{tr} \le\sum_r M_rq_r, $$
because $Q_{tr}\ge0$ and $\sum_tQ_{tr}=q_r$.

At the end of the canonical sequence, every cohort of a strictly nonmaximizing type has zero units of the corresponding resource. All transferred units go to maximizing types. Therefore the terminal allocation attains the upper bound, proving
$$ \boxed{W^*=\sum_rM_rq_r.} $$

For every feasible allocation,
$$ W^*-W(x) =\sum_{t,r}(M_r-a_{tr})Q_{tr}. $$
Every summand is nonnegative. Hence an allocation is efficient exactly when
$$ Q_{tr}=0\quad\text{whenever }a_{tr}<M_r. $$
Since occupied masses are positive and bundle coordinates are nonnegative, this is equivalent to
$$ B_r>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_{tr}=M_r $$
for every occupied type–bundle pair $(t,B)$.

Now consider terminal cohorts $(t,B,\pi)$ and $(u,D,\rho)$. If $D_r>0$, efficiency implies $a_{ur}=M_r$, and therefore $a_{tr}\le a_{ur}$. Summing coordinatewise gives
$$ v_t(D)\le v_u(D). $$
Using the payment invariant,
$$ \begin{aligned} v_t(D)-\rho &=v_t(D)-v_u(D)+W^*\\ &\le W^*\\ &=v_t(B)-\pi. \end{aligned} $$
This proves every envy inequality.

Thus the canonical sequence always ends in an efficient, envy-free state.

4. Polynomial-time access to any deal

The conceptual partition into $H$ cohorts need not be explicitly materialized. I now give formulas that compute a deal directly from its index.

Define
$$ b_i=\sum_r e_{ir},\qquad P_i=\sum_{h<i}n_hb_h,\qquad E_{ir}=\sum_{\ell\le r}e_{i\ell},\qquad E_{i0}=0. $$
Thus $P_i$ is the number of deals preceding initial pair $i$.

Given $1\le k\le K$, locate the unique $i$ with
$$ P_i<k\le P_i+n_ib_i. $$
Its $b_i$ is positive. Define
$$ j=1+\left\lfloor\frac{k-P_i-1}{b_i}\right\rfloor, \qquad z=1+\bigl((k-P_i-1)\bmod b_i\bigr). $$
Locate the unique resource $r$ satisfying
$$ E_{i,r-1}<z\le E_{ir}. $$
The $k$-th deal has donor $c_{i,j}$, recipient $h_{w_r}$, resource $r$, and mass $1/H$.

To make this a complete deal description, we must compute the two current bundles without executing earlier deals.

Counting the first $p$ deals

Let $0\le p\le K$. For each $i$ with $b_i>0$, put
$$ R_i(p)=\min\{\max\{p-P_i,0\},n_ib_i\}, $$
$$ f_i(p)=\left\lfloor\frac{R_i(p)}{b_i}\right\rfloor, \qquad u_i(p)=R_i(p)-f_i(p)b_i. $$
Here $f_i(p)$ counts fully processed donor cohorts of initial pair $i$, and $u_i(p)$ counts deals in its partially processed donor cohort.

The number of resource-$r$ donations by cohorts from pair $i$ among the first $p$ deals is
$$ A_{ir}(p) = f_i(p)e_{ir} + \min\{e_{ir},\max\{u_i(p)-E_{i,r-1},0\}\}. $$
For $b_i=0$, define $A_{ir}(p)=0$.

Consequently,
$$ C_r(p)=\sum_iA_{ir}(p) $$
is the total number of resource-$r$ deals in that prefix.

The number of resource-$r$ donations already made by a particular cohort $c_{i,j}$ is
$$ d_{i,j,r}(p) = \min\left\{ e_{ir}, \max\{p-P_i-(j-1)b_i-E_{i,r-1},0\} \right\}. $$
Indeed, this cohort's resource-$r$ deals occupy the consecutive positions immediately after
$$ P_i+(j-1)b_i+E_{i,r-1}. $$

Its current bundle coordinate is therefore
$$ \boxed{ B_{i,j,r}(p) = B_{ir}-d_{i,j,r}(p) +\mathbf 1_{\{c_{i,j}=h_{w_r}\}}C_r(p). } $$
The subtraction counts its own donations, and the addition counts all donations it receives as the distinguished recipient.

Finally, current welfare is
$$ W(p) = W(x^0) +\frac1H \sum_{i,r}(M_r-a_{t_i r})A_{ir}(p). $$
Thus current balances can also be computed from
$$ \pi=v_t(B)-W(p). $$

Applying these formulas at $p=k-1$ gives the current donor and recipient bundles, their balances if desired, and the welfare change of the $k$-th deal. This verifies direct access to a complete legal deal, rather than merely to its resource and cohort labels.

Encoding and running time

The binary length of $H$ is polynomially bounded: $H$ is at most the product of the input denominators, so its logarithm is at most the sum of their binary lengths.

Also,
$$ K \le H\sum_i x_i\sum_rB_{ir} =H\sum_rq_r. $$
Both $H$ and the binary-encoded quantities on the right have polynomial bit length. Hence $K$ has polynomial bit length, although its numerical value can be exponential.

During the construction, each bundle coordinate is bounded by its initial coordinate plus $K$. The displayed formulas involve only polynomially many integer and rational arithmetic operations on numbers of polynomial bit length.

It follows that:

  • preprocessing takes polynomial time;
  • any indexed deal takes polynomial time to compute;
  • enumerating the explicit sequence takes polynomial time per deal and polynomial working space.

No earlier deal needs to be simulated to answer an indexed query.

A compact reachable terminal state

Let
$$ \widetilde B_{ir}=B_{ir}-e_{ir}, \qquad K_r=\sum_i n_ie_{ir}. $$
At termination, every nondistinguished cohort from pair $i$ has bundle $\widetilde B_i$. The distinguished cohort $h_t$ has bundle
$$ \widetilde B_{i_t} +\sum_{\{r:w_r=t\}}K_re_r, $$
where here $e_r$ denotes the standard coordinate vector.

For each initial pair, combine all its nondistinguished cohorts into one cohort, omitting it if its mass is zero. There are at most $s$ such cohorts and $\tau$ distinguished cohorts. Thus the terminal state has support at most
$$ \boxed{s+\tau.} $$
Its masses, bundles, and balances are all computable in polynomial time.

5. Two exact exponential output-length lower bounds

5.1 Binary bundle coordinates

Let $N\ge1$. Take one resource, two types, and
$$ \mu_1=\mu_2=\frac12,\qquad a_{11}=0,\qquad a_{21}=1. $$
Initially type $1$ holds bundle $(N)$ and type $2$ holds $(0)$, each with mass $1/2$. Supply is $N/2$, and initial welfare is zero.

Every efficient allocation has welfare $N/2$: all resource quantity must belong to type $2$.

For any deal, its participating mass satisfies $\epsilon\le1/2$. Its welfare change is at most $\epsilon$, even if the deal is not individually rational. Therefore a sequence of $J$ deals reaching efficiency must satisfy
$$ \frac N2 =\sum_{k=1}^J\Delta W_k \le\frac J2. $$
Hence $J\ge N$.

Exactly $N$ individually rational deals suffice: repeatedly transfer one unit per person between the two entire type cohorts. The resulting efficient state is envy-free by Section 3.

Taking $N=2^n$ gives input length $L=\Theta(n)$ and minimum sequence length
$$ 2^{\Omega(L)}. $$

5.2 Small rational masses, with initially unit-size bundles

There is also an exponential obstruction without large initial bundle coordinates.

Let $N\ge2$ and take
$$ \mu_1=1-\frac1N,\qquad \mu_2=\frac1N,\qquad a_{11}=0,\qquad a_{21}=1. $$
Initially type $1$ holds $(1)$ and type $2$ holds $(0)$. Supply and optimal welfare are both
$$ 1-\frac1N, $$
while initial welfare is zero.

A positive welfare change requires transfer from type $1$ to type $2$. Such a deal has mass at most $1/N$, because its recipient belongs to type $2$. All other deals have nonpositive welfare change. Thus every deal satisfies
$$ \Delta W\le\frac1N. $$
For any sequence of $J$ deals ending efficiently,
$$ 1-\frac1N\le\frac JN, $$
so
$$ \boxed{J\ge N-1.} $$

This bound is attained. Partition type $1$ into $N-1$ subcohorts of mass $1/N$, and transfer from each subcohort to the entire type-$2$ cohort once. Each deal is individually rational. The recipient ends with bundle $(N-1)$, and all type-$1$ individuals end with $(0)$.

With $N=2^n$, the input again has length $\Theta(n)$, but its initial bundle coordinates are only zero and one. Its shortest solution has $2^n-1$ deals.

Thus binary rational masses independently create an exponential output obstruction.

6. Finite existence does not imply termination of every path

The finite-agent convergence result cannot be transferred wholesale to arbitrary rational cohort splitting. The [cited paper](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/07/Papers/200.pdf) models finitely many agents and a finite set of goods.

For a direct counterexample to finite termination in the present model, take the first lower-bound instance with $N=1$. At deal $j\ge1$, transfer mass
$$ \epsilon_j=2^{-(j+1)} $$
from remaining type-$1$ individuals with bundle $(1)$ to remaining type-$2$ individuals with bundle $(0)$.

Before deal $j$, both available cohorts have mass $2^{-j}$, so the deal is legal. Its welfare increase is $\epsilon_j>0$, making it individually rational.

After $j$ deals, the remaining inefficiently allocated quantity is
$$ 2^{-(j+1)}>0. $$
Every finite state is therefore inefficient, and the sequence continues indefinitely.

This does not affect the constructive existence result: the canonical procedure deliberately chooses a finite path.

7. What this proves about hardness

The explicit-output lower bounds conclusively rule out polynomial running time in $m,\tau,s,L$. They do not, by themselves, prove NP-hardness.

The random-access construction gives an additional precise complexity statement. Consider the following access model for a solution oracle:

  • on an input, it selects one valid sequence;
  • it supplies its length in binary;
  • it answers requests for a deal at a specified binary index;
  • it can supply the terminal state.

The answers must refer consistently to the selected sequence. A reduction is required to work for every valid choice of oracle solutions.

Proven conditional statement. If a decision problem is solvable by a deterministic polynomial-time reduction using this oracle access, then that decision problem belongs to $\mathrm P$.

Proof. Choose the canonical solution from Sections 2–4 for every oracle input. Its length, indexed deals, and terminal state are computable in polynomial time. A polynomial-time reduction makes polynomially many queries of polynomial length. Replace each oracle query by the corresponding canonical computation. Because the reduction must work for every valid solution choice, it works for these canonical choices. The simulation runs in polynomial time. $\square$

Consequently, NP-hardness under this specified access model would imply
$$ \mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}. $$

This statement does not cover a reduction whose decoder processes an entire exponentially long sequence in time polynomial in that sequence's length. Such a decoder may take exponential time in the original input length. A hardness claim using that convention needs a separate, explicit reduction.

The remaining gap is therefore the requested NP-hardness classification under a precisely specified reduction notion for the unbounded-output search problem. The mathematical constructions above settle the impossibility of polynomial-time explicit output and substantially strengthen the constructive upper bound, but leave that hardness question open.
