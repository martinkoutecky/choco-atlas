The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite outcome set \(Y\), types \(t\in T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) and rational beliefs \(q_t\), and fixed type-level reports and wagers of the other forecasters, compute a designated type’s optimal prediction and per-agent wager under WALLA I or II’s population baseline, and the resulting pooled forecast. The population baseline is the wager-weighted average over types; the single forecaster’s mass is negligible, as in the limit of finite populations with many agents per type.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(Y\) be a finite outcome set with \(m=|Y|\geq2\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
For each type, the input specifies rational beliefs \(q_t\in\Delta(Y)\), fixed rational reports \(r_t\in\Delta(Y)\), and fixed rational per-agent wagers \(v_t\geq0\). Also given are a designated type \(t_0\), a binary-rational constant \(c_3>0\), and a variant \(V\in\{\mathrm I,\mathrm{II}\}\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Use the Brier score normalized as \(s(p,y)=1-\tfrac12\|p-\delta_y\|_2^2\), where \(\delta_y\) is the point mass at \(y\). Require \(W=\sum_t\mu_tv_t>0\).
Define the linear population pool \(\bar r=W^{-1}\sum_t\mu_tv_tr_t\) and baselines \(b^{\mathrm I}(y)=W^{-1}\sum_t\mu_tv_ts(r_t,y)\) and \(b^{\mathrm{II}}(y)=s(\bar r,y)\).
A single risk-neutral forecaster of type \(t_0\) has zero mass and chooses \(p\in\Delta(Y)\) and a per-agent wager \(w\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}\). All other forecasters, including those of type \(t_0\), retain their fixed reports and wagers; the deviation leaves the population baseline and pool unchanged.
Her expected net payout is \(U(p,w)=\sum_{y\in Y}q_{t_0}(y)w\bigl(s(p,y)-b^V(y)-c_3w\bigr)\). The computational problem is to find any jointly maximizing pair \((p,w)\) exactly and compute the resulting linear pooled forecast.

Is this computation polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\) for each of WALLA I and WALLA II, or is it NP-hard?

For finite populations, the best-response wager for a fixed prediction has a closed-form characterization, and truthful prediction maximizes expected payout for every positive wager. [Verified finite-population results](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04389v1).

Result

The specified computation is polynomial-time solvable for both WALLA I and WALLA II. An exact jointly maximizing pair is always rational and has polynomial encoding length.

The zero-mass assumption fixes the population pool, but the optimization remains substantive: the optimal wager can be positive. The argument below covers both positive-wager and zero-wager cases.

The cited paper establishes truthful prediction and a best-response wager formula for a baseline independent of the agent’s choices ([Theorems 3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04389v1)). Here we derive the required formulas directly and prove their exact computational complexity.

1. Expected Brier score

Write \(q=q_{t_0}\), and define the wager weights
\(\alpha_t=\mu_tv_t/W\). Since \(W>0\), these are well-defined, nonnegative, and satisfy \(\sum_t\alpha_t=1\). Thus \(\bar r=\sum_t\alpha_tr_t\).

For every \(p\in\Delta(Y)\) and \(y\in Y\), expanding the specified score gives

\(s(p,y)=\frac12+p_y-\frac12\|p\|_2^2.\)

Consequently, its expectation under \(q\) is

\(S_q(p):=\sum_yq_y s(p,y)=\frac12+q\cdot p-\frac12\|p\|_2^2=\frac12+\frac12\|q\|_2^2-\frac12\|p-q\|_2^2.\)

In particular,

\(S_q(q)-S_q(p)=\frac12\|p-q\|_2^2.\)

Because \(q\in\Delta(Y)\), truthful prediction is feasible and uniquely maximizes the expected score.

2. Exact advantages for the two variants

Let \(B_V=\sum_yq_yb^V(y)\) and \(A_V=S_q(q)-B_V\). These quantities do not depend on the deviating agent’s choices.

For WALLA I, interchange the two finite sums in the baseline expectation:

\(B_{\mathrm I}=\sum_t\alpha_tS_q(r_t).\)

The expected-score identity therefore gives

\(A_{\mathrm I}=\frac12\sum_t\alpha_t\|q-r_t\|_2^2=\frac{1}{2W}\sum_t\mu_tv_t\|q-r_t\|_2^2.\)

For WALLA II, \(B_{\mathrm{II}}=S_q(\bar r)\), so

\(A_{\mathrm{II}}=\frac12\|q-\bar r\|_2^2.\)

Both advantages are nonnegative. Their zero cases differ:

  • \(A_{\mathrm I}=0\) exactly when \(r_t=q\) for every type with \(v_t>0\). This follows because the sum consists of nonnegative terms, and \(\mu_t>0\).
  • \(A_{\mathrm{II}}=0\) exactly when \(\bar r=q\).

For completeness, the two advantages obey

\(A_{\mathrm I}=A_{\mathrm{II}}+\frac12\sum_t\alpha_t\|r_t-\bar r\|_2^2.\)

To verify this, expand \(q-r_t=(q-\bar r)+(\bar r-r_t)\). The weighted cross term vanishes because \(\sum_t\alpha_t(\bar r-r_t)=0\).

3. Complete joint maximization

Since \(\sum_yq_y=1\), the payoff can be written as

\(U(p,w)=w\left(A_V-\frac12\|p-q\|_2^2\right)-c_3w^2.\)

Completing the square yields the identity

\(U(p,w)=\frac{A_V^2}{4c_3}-c_3\left(w-\frac{A_V}{2c_3}\right)^2-\frac w2\|p-q\|_2^2.\)

For every feasible pair, both subtracted terms are nonnegative: \(c_3>0\) and \(w\geq0\). Therefore

\(U(p,w)\leq\frac{A_V^2}{4c_3}.\)

Because \(A_V\geq0\), the pair

\((p^*,w^*)=\left(q,\frac{A_V}{2c_3}\right)\)

is feasible and attains this upper bound. This proves global joint optimality, including existence despite the unbounded wager domain.

Explicitly, the optimal wagers are

\(w_{\mathrm I}^*=\frac{\sum_t\mu_tv_t\|q-r_t\|_2^2}{4c_3W},\qquad w_{\mathrm{II}}^*=\frac{\|q-\bar r\|_2^2}{4c_3}.\)

If \(A_V>0\), equality in the upper bound requires both \(w=A_V/(2c_3)>0\) and \(p=q\); hence the maximizing pair is unique.

If \(A_V=0\), then

\(U(p,w)=-c_3w^2-\frac w2\|p-q\|_2^2.\)

Thus the maximizing pairs are precisely those with \(w=0\) and arbitrary \(p\in\Delta(Y)\). Choosing \(p=q\) still supplies an exact maximizing pair.

4. Resulting pooled forecast

Under the stipulated nonatomic interpretation, the deviating individual has zero mass. Her optimal wager is finite, so her contribution to both aggregate wager and aggregate weighted report is zero.

All positive-mass forecasters retain their reports and wagers, including those of type \(t_0\). The resulting forecast is therefore

\(\widehat p=\bar r=\frac{\sum_t\mu_tv_tr_t}{W}.\)

This conclusion uses the zero-mass premise, rather than replacing the entire designated type’s report or wager.

5. Exact polynomial-time computation

An algorithm computes \(W\), the coordinate sums \(\sum_t\mu_tv_tr_t(y)\), and hence \(\bar r\). It then computes the appropriate advantage formula and returns \((q,A_V/(2c_3))\) together with \(\bar r\). This uses \(O(\tau m)\) rational arithmetic operations.

To establish polynomial bit complexity, let \(D\) be the product of the positive denominators of all input rationals. Its binary encoding length is \(O(L)\), because the sum of the input denominator lengths is bounded by \(L\). Every input rational can be expressed over denominator \(D\), with an integer numerator of \(O(L)\) bits.

Writing \(\mu_t=a_t/D\), \(v_t=b_t/D\), and \(c_3=C/D\), we have

\(W=S/D^2,\qquad S=\sum_ta_tb_t>0,\qquad C>0.\)

Every coordinate of \(\bar r\) can be represented over denominator \(DS\). The formula for \(A_{\mathrm I}\) can be represented over denominator \(2D^2S\), and the formula for \(A_{\mathrm{II}}\) over denominator \(2D^2S^2\). Dividing by \(2c_3\) gives wager denominators \(4DSC\) and \(4DS^2C\), respectively.

All these expressions involve fixed-degree products of input-derived integers and sums of at most \(O(\tau m)\) terms. Their numerators and denominators consequently have \(O(L+\log(\tau m))\) bits. Standard integer arithmetic, exact division, and optional fraction reduction therefore take polynomial time. In particular, the exact output itself has polynomial length.

Thus both variants admit exact polynomial-time algorithms; no approximation or equilibrium computation is needed.
