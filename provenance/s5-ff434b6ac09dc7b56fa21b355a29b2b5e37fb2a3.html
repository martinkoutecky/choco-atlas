The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of complete patient–donor types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), block compatibility \(A\subseteq T\times T\), rational transplant weights \(w_{tu}\), and a fixed cycle cap \(k\), form the LP over compatible type cycles \(C\) of lengths \(2,\ldots,k\), with variables \(y_C\ge0\), supplies \(\sum_C a_{tC}y_C\le\mu_t\), and objective \(\max\sum_C y_C\sum_{(t,u)\in C}w_{tu}\). Compute its optimum and determine whether its cycle-formulation relaxation dominates the corresponding type-level PE and PRE relaxations, as in Lemma 2.

Compatibility is constant within type blocks and is specified by a loopless directed graph \(G=(T,A)\), with positive rational weights \(w_{tu}\). Let \(m=|A|\), let \(L\) be the total input bit length, and fix an integer cycle cap \(k\ge3\).
An exchange pattern \(C\) is a compatible cyclic sequence of \(2\) through \(k\) type occurrences; types may repeat because different pairs can share a type. Let \(a_{tC}\) count occurrences of \(t\), and \(b_{tu,C}\) count traversals of arc \((t,u)\). Define \(P_C=\{y\ge0:\sum_C a_{tC}y_C\le\mu_t\text{ for every }t\}\), with objective \(\max\sum_C y_C\sum_{(t,u)\in A}b_{tu,C}w_{tu}\), and arc projection \(x_{tu}=\sum_C b_{tu,C}y_C\).
Define the type-level PE and PRE relaxations by direct capacity substitution: both require \(x\ge0\), \(\sum_u x_{tu}=\sum_u x_{ut}\le\mu_t\), and zero flow on arcs between distinct strongly connected components. Their objective is \(\max\sum_{(t,u)\in A}w_{tu}x_{tu}\).

These bounds retain their stated right-hand side in the normalized mass units; use the full path family.

2. Exact optimization by a polynomial-size rational LP

Let \(\mathcal C\) denote the set of compatible exchange patterns, and let \(\ell(C)\) be the number of type occurrences in \(C\).

Enumeration. For each \(\ell\in\{2,\ldots,k\}\), enumerate all ordered tuples \((t_1,\ldots,t_\ell)\in T^\ell\). Keep a tuple precisely when all its consecutive arcs and its closing arc \((t_\ell,t_1)\) belong to \(A\). This procedure permits repeated types, as required.

Every exchange pattern has such a representation. We may identify cyclic rotations; alternatively, retaining duplicate representations gives an equivalent LP, because their variables can be summed into the variable for the corresponding pattern. Consequently, the number \(N\) of variables satisfies

\(N\le \sum_{\ell=2}^{k}\tau^\ell\le (k-1)\tau^k.\)

Thus enumeration takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau\) for fixed \(k\).

Coefficient sizes. For each retained pattern, compute \(a_{tC}\), \(b_{tu,C}\), and its objective coefficient

\(c_C=\sum_{(t,u)\in A}b_{tu,C}w_{tu}.\)

The occurrence and traversal counts are integers between \(0\) and \(k\). Moreover, \(c_C\) is a sum of at most \(k\) input weights, counted with multiplicity. Its rational encoding therefore has bit length polynomial in \(L\) for fixed \(k\). The resulting LP has \(N\) variables, \(\tau\) capacity inequalities, and nonnegativity constraints, with total encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Feasibility and boundedness. The zero vector is feasible. Summing all type-capacity constraints gives

\(\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}\ell(C)y_C =\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}a_{tC}y_C \le \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1.\)

Since \(y_C\ge 0\), each coordinate satisfies \(y_C\le 1/\ell(C)\). Hence the feasible region is bounded and closed, so an optimum exists. If there are no patterns, the optimum is simply zero.

We now apply the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: a feasible rational LP with a finite optimum admits an exact rational optimal solution computable in time polynomial in its encoding length. Polynomial-time LP algorithms and the rational bit-size property are discussed in [Trevisan’s linear programming notes](https://lucatrevisan.github.io/cs261/lecture05.pdf).

All hypotheses have been checked, and the constructed LP has polynomial encoding length. Therefore exact optimization over \(P_C\), including production of an optimal rational vector, is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed \(k\).

3. The arc projection satisfies the shared constraints

Define \(Q\) to be the set of nonnegative arc vectors satisfying the flow-balance, type-capacity, and strongly connected component constraints shared by PE and PRE.

Take any \(y\in P_C\), and define its projection by

\(x_{tu}=\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}b_{tu,C}y_C.\)

For every type \(t\) and pattern \(C\),

\(\sum_{u:(t,u)\in A}b_{tu,C} =a_{tC} =\sum_{u:(u,t)\in A}b_{ut,C}.\)

Indeed, every occurrence of \(t\) in the cyclic sequence has exactly one outgoing traversal and one incoming traversal. This remains true when types repeat.

Multiplying by \(y_C\) and summing over patterns yields

\(\sum_{u:(t,u)\in A}x_{tu} =\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}a_{tC}y_C =\sum_{u:(u,t)\in A}x_{ut} \le\mu_t.\)

Nonnegativity follows from \(b_{tu,C}\ge 0\) and \(y_C\ge 0\).

Finally, any arc traversed by a pattern belongs to a closed directed walk. Its tail reaches its head through that arc, and its head reaches its tail through the remaining walk. Thus its endpoints lie in the same strongly connected component. No pattern uses an arc between distinct components, so its projected flow is zero.

Therefore \(x\in Q\).

The objective is also preserved:

\(\sum_{(t,u)\in A}w_{tu}x_{tu} =\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}y_C\sum_{(t,u)\in A}b_{tu,C}w_{tu}.\)

4. Every PE and PRE path inequality is redundant

This conclusion holds for every \(x\in Q\), whether or not it comes from bounded exchange patterns.

Summing the outgoing capacity inequalities gives

\(\sum_{(t,u)\in A}x_{tu} =\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{u:(t,u)\in A}x_{tu} \le\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1.\)

Fix any simple directed path \((t_1,\ldots,t_{k+1})\) from the required full path family.

For PE, the consecutive path arcs are distinct. By nonnegativity,

\(\sum_{p=1}^{k}x_{t_pt_{p+1}} \le\sum_{(t,u)\in A}x_{tu} \le 1\le k-1.\)

For PRE, simplicity means that all \(t_p\) are distinct. Consequently, the ordered pairs \((t_p,t_q)\) with \(p<q\) are distinct, so each present arc contributes at most once. With absent arcs interpreted as zero,

\(\sum_{1\le p<q\le k+1}x_{t_pt_q} \le\sum_{(t,u)\in A}x_{tu} \le 1\le k-1.\)

Thus every required inequality is satisfied. In fact,

\(\mathrm{PE}=\mathrm{PRE}=Q,\qquad \pi(P_C)\subseteq Q,\)

where \(\pi(P_C)\) denotes the arc projection.

The redundancy is a direct consequence of retaining the right-hand side \(k-1\) while normalizing total mass to \(1\).

5. Strict containment for every fixed cycle cap

Fix \(k\ge 3\), and put \(n=k+1\). Let

\(T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_n\},\qquad A=\{(t_i,t_{i+1}):1\le i<n\}\cup\{(t_n,t_1)\}.\)

Give every type mass \(1/n\) and every arc weight \(1\). This graph is a single strongly connected component.

Every vertex has exactly one outgoing arc. Hence a directed walk advances one position around the cycle at each step and returns to its starting type only after a positive multiple of \(n\) steps. There is therefore no compatible exchange pattern of length between \(2\) and \(k=n-1\), even allowing repeated types.

It follows that

\(\pi(P_C)=\{0\},\qquad \operatorname{OPT}(P_C)=0.\)

Now assign flow \(1/n\) to every arc. At each type, incoming and outgoing flow both equal its capacity \(1/n\). Thus this vector lies in \(Q\), and hence in both PE and PRE. Its objective value is \(n(1/n)=1\).

Conversely, any feasible PE or PRE vector has objective at most \(1\), because all weights are \(1\) and total arc flow is at most \(1\). Therefore

\(\operatorname{OPT}(\mathrm{PE})=\operatorname{OPT}(\mathrm{PRE})=1.\)

This proves strict containment and a strict objective gap for every fixed \(k\ge 3\).

6. Relation to integral clearing

The [Abraham–Blum–Sandholm hardness theorem](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/www/kidneyExchange.EC07.withGrantInfo.pdf) concerns selecting vertex-disjoint bounded cycles in an integral exchange. Here the variables \(y_C\) are continuous. For fixed \(k\), their polynomially many columns form an ordinary rational LP.

Thus the integral hardness result is consistent with the polynomial-time algorithm proved above. Both questions in the stated normalized formulation are resolved.
