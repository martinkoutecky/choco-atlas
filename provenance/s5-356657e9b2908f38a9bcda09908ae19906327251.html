The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many complete additive valuation types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), and finitely many indivisible good slots \(G\) with rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), can one compute in time polynomial in \(|G|\), \(\tau=|T|\), \(k\), and input bit length \(L\) a sparse rational allocation \(\lambda_{t,B}\)? Here \(\lambda_{t,B}\) is the mass of type-\(t\) agents receiving the integral bundle \(B\subseteq G\), with \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:g\in B}\lambda_{t,B}=q_g\); for every pair of positive-mass bundle classes \((t,B),(s,C)\) and every \(Y\subseteq C\) with \(|Y|=k\), require \(v_t(B)\geq \frac{k}{k+1}v_t(C\setminus Y)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(G\) contain \(m\geq1\) good slots, with binary-rational supplies \(q_g\in[0,1]\). Each type specifies nonnegative binary-rational additive values for every slot. An agent may receive at most one unit of each slot; empty bundles are allowed.
The input also contains an integer \(1\leq k\leq m\); \(L\) is the total input bit length.
An allocation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(\lambda_{t,B}\), indexed by \(t\in T\) and integral bundles \(B\subseteq G\), satisfying \(\sum_{B\subseteq G}\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:g\in B}\lambda_{t,B}=q_g\).
It is feasible if, whenever \(\lambda_{t,B}>0\) and \(\lambda_{s,C}>0\), every \(Y\subseteq C\) with \(|Y|=k\) satisfies \(v_t(B)\geq\frac{k}{k+1}v_t(C\setminus Y)\). This includes comparisons between different bundles assigned to the same type, removal of zero-valued goods, and a vacuous condition when \(|C|<k\).
A sparse allocation has at most \(\tau+m\) positive entries, encoded by explicitly listing their bundles and rational masses.

Can feasibility be decided and, whenever feasible, a sparse allocation be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finite societies with \(n\) explicitly listed agents and nonnegative additive valuations, a complete \(\frac{k}{k+1}\)-EFkX allocation exists and is computable in polynomial time for \(1\leq k\leq\min\{n,m\}\). [Discrete allocation guarantee](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10371v1#S3.SS1)
No complexity classification for the exact mass-and-slot problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one, making supplies per capita; restricted supplies to [0,1] because bundles contain each slot at most once.
  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational valuation entries, retaining the discrete valuation domain and making bit complexity defined.
  • Used 1 <= k <= m: the lower bound follows the anchor, while its upper bound by the finite agent count has no counterpart in an atomless society.
  • Allowed empty bundles and preserved removal of exactly k goods, including zero-valued goods; comparisons against smaller bundles are vacuous.
  • Defined sparse as at most tau+m positive entries, each explicitly encoded.
  • Included infeasibility reporting because arbitrary rational supplies are not covered by the discrete unconditional existence guarantee.

Result. Feasibility is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, already for \(k=1\), integer valuations, and dyadic masses and supplies. Every feasible instance nevertheless has a sparse allocation with polynomial bit length. Thus the requested polynomial-time decision-and-construction algorithm exists only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Sparse certificates and membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\). Let \(d=\tau+m\). For each type–bundle pair \((t,B)\), define a column \(a_{t,B}\in\{0,1\}^{d}\): its type coordinates indicate \(t\), and its slot coordinates indicate membership in \(B\). The mass and supply equations are

\(A\lambda=b,\qquad b=((\mu_t)_{t\in T},(q_g)_{g\in G}).\)

Suppose a feasible allocation exists, and restrict \(A\) to its positive support. If those columns are linearly dependent, choose a nonzero rational vector \(z\) with \(Az=0\). Summing the type coordinates gives \(\sum_i z_i=0\), so \(z\) has both positive and negative entries. Set

\(\varepsilon=\min_{i:z_i>0}\lambda_i/z_i,\qquad \lambda'=\lambda-\varepsilon z.\)

Then \(\lambda'\) is nonnegative, satisfies the same equations, and has strictly smaller support. Its fairness follows because deleting supported bundles deletes comparisons without introducing any. Repeating this operation produces a feasible allocation whose \(s\) positive columns are linearly independent, with \(s\leq d\).

These masses have polynomial bit length. Choose \(s\) rows giving an invertible matrix \(M\in\{0,1\}^{s\times s}\). Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators appearing in \(b\), so \(\log_2 D=O(L)\). The selected equations are

\(M\lambda=b',\qquad Db'\in\mathbb Z^s.\)

All coordinates of \(b'\) lie in \([0,1]\). By Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s determinant bound, every mass can be written with numerator and denominator of absolute value at most

\(D\,s^{s/2}.\)

Indeed, the columns of \(M\) have Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt{s}\), and the replacement column \(Db'\) has norm at most \(D\sqrt{s}\). Consequently, each mass needs \(O(L+s\log(s+1))\) bits.

A sparse certificate can also be checked in polynomial time. For any supported \((t,B)\) and \((s,C)\), when \(|C|\geq k\), the maximum of \(v_t(C\setminus Y)\) over \(|Y|=k\) is obtained by removing the \(k\) least valuable slots of \(C\), including zero-valued slots. Sorting these values reduces all removal inequalities to one exact rational comparison. When \(|C|<k\), the condition is vacuous.

This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

2. Polynomial reduction from Independent Set. Take a simple graph \(H=(V,E)\), with \(n=|V|\geq1\), \(e=|E|\), and an integer \(1\leq r\leq n\). The source question is whether \(H\) contains an independent set of size at least \(r\). Its \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness follows, for example, from the classical completeness of Clique by complementing the graph. [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Choose the smallest integer \(a\) satisfying \(2^a\geq8(n+e+1)\), and put

\(p=2^{-a}.\)

Set \(k=1\). Create one vertex slot \(g_u\) for every \(u\in V\), together with three additional slots \(b,d,h\). Their supplies are

\(q_{g_u}=p,\qquad q_b=q_d=1,\qquad q_h=p/2.\)

There are \(e+2\) valuation types:

| Type | Mass | Nonzero slot values |
|---|---:|---|
| Target type \(t_\star\) | \(p\) | \(v_{t_\star}(g_u)=1\) for every \(u\in V\), and \(v_{t_\star}(h)=2r\) |
| Edge type \(t_f\), for each \(f=\{u,v\}\in E\) | \(4p\) | \(v_{t_f}(b)=v_{t_f}(g_u)=v_{t_f}(g_v)=1\) |
| Filler type \(t_0\) | \(1-(4e+1)p\) | None |

All unspecified values are zero. In particular, every type values \(d\) at zero.

The masses sum to one and are positive: since \(p\leq1/[8(n+e+1)]\),

\((4e+1)p<1/2.\)

The valuation types are distinct. Different edges give different endpoint coordinates; the target values \(h\) positively; and the filler has zero valuation.

The resulting parameters are \(m=n+3\) and \(\tau=e+2\). Every number has polynomial bit length, so this is a polynomial-time construction.

3. Soundness: feasibility implies an independent set. Suppose the constructed instance has a feasible allocation. Call a bundle *represented* if it occurs with positive mass for some type.

First, every represented bundle contains both \(b\) and \(d\). To see this for either slot \(x\in\{b,d\}\), the total allocation mass is one and \(q_x=1\), so

\(\sum_{t,B:x\notin B}\lambda_{t,B}=1-q_x=0.\)

Nonnegativity forces every positive entry to contain \(x\).

Fix an edge \(f=\{u,v\}\). The mass of type \(t_f\) receiving at least one of \(g_u,g_v\) is at most their combined supply:

\(\sum_{B:B\cap\{g_u,g_v\}\neq\varnothing}\lambda_{t_f,B}\leq q_{g_u}+q_{g_v}=2p.\)

Its total mass is \(4p\). Therefore some positive entry \((t_f,B_f)\) contains neither endpoint slot. Since \(b\in B_f\), its value is exactly

\(v_{t_f}(B_f)=1.\)

Now let \(C\) be any represented bundle. Apply fairness from \((t_f,B_f)\) to \(C\), removing \(Y=\{d\}\). This removal is permitted because \(d\in C\), and \(d\) has zero value. Thus

\(1\geq\frac12v_{t_f}(C\setminus\{d\})=\frac12\bigl(1+|C\cap\{g_u,g_v\}|\bigr).\)

It follows that \(|C\cap\{g_u,g_v\}|\leq1\). Since this holds for every edge, the vertex slots in every represented bundle form an independent set in \(H\).

Next, the target type has mass \(p\), whereas the total supply of \(h\) is only \(p/2\). Consequently,

\(\sum_{B:h\notin B}\lambda_{t_\star,B}\geq p-p/2=p/2>0.\)

Choose a positive target entry \((t_\star,B_\star)\) with \(h\notin B_\star\). Since \(q_h>0\), some represented bundle \(C_h\) contains \(h\). Fairness toward \(C_h\), again removing \(d\), gives

\(v_{t_\star}(B_\star)\geq\frac12v_{t_\star}(C_h\setminus\{d\})\geq\frac12(2r)=r.\)

Because \(h\notin B_\star\) and the target values only vertex slots and \(h\),

\(v_{t_\star}(B_\star)=|\{u\in V:g_u\in B_\star\}|.\)

Thus \(B_\star\) contains at least \(r\) vertex slots, and those vertices form an independent set.

4. Completeness: an independent set gives a sparse feasible allocation. Conversely, suppose \(H\) has an independent set \(S\) of size exactly \(r\); if a larger one exists, take any \(r\) of its vertices. Write \(D_0=\{b,d\}\).

Assign the following positive entries:

  • Target type: bundle \(D_0\cup\{g_u:u\in S\}\), with mass \(p\).
  • Each edge type: bundle \(D_0\), with mass \(4p\).
  • Filler type: bundle \(D_0\cup\{g_u\}\), with mass \(p\), for every \(u\notin S\).
  • Filler type: bundle \(D_0\cup\{h\}\), with mass \(p/2\).
  • Filler type: bundle \(D_0\), with mass

\(R=1-(4e+1)p-(n-r)p-p/2\).

The last mass is positive, because

\(R=1-(4e+n-r+3/2)p\geq1-4(n+e+1)p\geq1/2.\)

The type masses are correct. Each selected vertex slot is supplied entirely to the target; each unselected vertex slot is supplied entirely through its filler entry. The supply of \(h\) is \(p/2\). Every bundle contains \(b,d\), giving supply one for each.

It remains to verify every fairness comparison.

For an edge type \(t_f\), its own bundle has value one. Every represented bundle has value at most two for this type: the target’s vertex set is independent, a filler vertex bundle contains only one vertex, and the other bundles contain no vertex slots. Hence, for every represented \(C\) and every singleton \(Y\subseteq C\),

\(v_{t_f}(D_0)=1\geq\frac12v_{t_f}(C)\geq\frac12v_{t_f}(C\setminus Y).\)

For the target type, its own bundle has value \(r\). Represented bundles have target values among \(r,0,1,2r\), all at most \(2r\), since \(r\geq1\). Therefore,

\(r\geq\frac12v_{t_\star}(C)\geq\frac12v_{t_\star}(C\setminus Y)\)

for every required comparison.

The filler type values every bundle at zero, so all its comparisons hold. These arguments cover comparisons between different bundles assigned to the same type as well.

The allocation has \(e+n-r+3\) positive entries, which is at most \(\tau+m=e+n+5\). It is already sparse.

We have proved the exact equivalence:

\(\text{The constructed instance is feasible}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H\text{ has an independent set of size at least }r.\)

5. Classification and the discrete guarantee. The reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for the fixed value \(k=1\); Section 1 proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\). Thus feasibility is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

The [cited discrete allocation guarantee](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10371v1#S3.SS1) concerns partitioning individual goods among explicitly listed agents. Expanding this problem’s supplies into copies introduces an additional restriction: each agent may receive at most one copy of each original slot. That restriction is not supplied by the cited theorem, so its existence guarantee does not settle this model.

Every feasible instance does admit a polynomial-size sparse allocation, but finding it jointly with deciding feasibility is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.
