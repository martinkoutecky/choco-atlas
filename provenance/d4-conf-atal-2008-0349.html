The continuous mirror question, as graded

Fix \(C=\{p,a,b\}\) and rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). An instance gives rational masses \(\nu_{r,w}\) of fixed voters by ranking \(r\) and weight \(w\), and masses \(\lambda_w\) of controllable voters by weight, with total mass \(1\). Choose \(x_{w,\pi}\ge 0\) for each weight \(w\) and ranking \(\pi\in S_3\), with \(\sum_{\pi\in S_3}x_{w,\pi}=\lambda_w\). Is there an allocation \(x\) for which \(p\) is a Copeland\(_\alpha\) winner, using weighted pairwise margins from the fixed and allocated masses?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix \(C=\{p,a,b\}\), with \(m=3\), and a rational constant \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). Rankings are strict linear orders; \(S_3\) denotes their set. Weights \(w\) are nonnegative integers encoded in binary.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Fixed types \((r,w)\) have masses \(\nu_{r,w}\); controllable types \(w\) have masses \(\lambda_w\). Here \(\tau\) counts these listed types, not possible rankings or weights, and \(\sum_{r,w}\nu_{r,w}+\sum_w\lambda_w=1\).
An allocation consists of real masses \(x_{w,\pi}\geq0\), with \(\sum_{\pi\in S_3}x_{w,\pi}=\lambda_w\) for every controllable weight. Controllable mass may be split freely among rankings, retaining its weight.
For distinct candidates \(c,d\), define \(\sigma_r(c,d)=1\) if \(r\) ranks \(c\) above \(d\), and \(-1\) otherwise. The pairwise margin is \(M(c,d)=\sum_{r,w}w\nu_{r,w}\sigma_r(c,d)+\sum_{w,\pi}wx_{w,\pi}\sigma_\pi(c,d)\).
Under \(\mathrm{Copeland}_\alpha\), a candidate receives one point for each positive margin, \(\alpha\) points for each zero margin, and zero for each negative margin. All candidates of maximum score are winners; no tie-breaking is applied. The decision problem asks whether some allocation makes \(p\) a winner, possibly tied.

For each fixed rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), is this continuous manipulation problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m=3\), \(\tau\), and the total input bit length \(L\).

Discrete weighted manipulation with exactly three candidates and this winner convention is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for every fixed rational \(0\leq\alpha<1\), and in \(\mathrm{P}\) for \(\alpha=1\). [Source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas08/proceedings/pdf/paper/AAMAS08_0293.pdf)
No result settling this exact divisible-mass formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The weight domain was unspecified; retained the discrete definition's nonnegative integer weights, encoded in binary, including zero.
  • Made rankings strict linear orders and interpreted winner as any maximum-score candidate, allowing shared victory without tie-breaking.
  • Interpreted alpha as a fixed rational constant, matching both the graded question and the discrete theorem; retained the alpha=1 endpoint.
  • Specified allocation masses as nonnegative real numbers, with unrestricted splitting within each controllable weight type.
  • Defined support size as the combined number of positive-mass fixed and controllable types, and L as total input bit length.

Result

For every fixed rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), the continuous manipulation problem with \(m=3\) is in \(\mathrm{P}\).

An exact algorithm uses \(O(\tau)\) arithmetic operations and \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations with schoolbook integer arithmetic. Whenever manipulation is possible, there is a rational allocation using only the rankings \(p>a>b\) and \(p>b>a\).

1. Every successful allocation can put \(p\) first

Define the score contribution of a margin by

\(f_\alpha(t)=1\) if \(t>0\), \(f_\alpha(t)=\alpha\) if \(t=0\), and \(f_\alpha(t)=0\) if \(t<0\).

Because \(0\leq\alpha\leq1\), this function is nondecreasing.

Take any allocation. In every controllable ranking, move \(p\) to first place while preserving the relative order of \(a\) and \(b\). Transfer each allocated mass to the resulting ranking, retaining its weight. This preserves the mass constraint for every controllable type.

For \(c\in\{a,b\}\), the transformation changes each controllable contribution to the margin \(M(p,c)\) from either sign to the positive sign. Nonnegative weights therefore imply

\(M'(p,c)\geq M(p,c)\).

Consequently, \(p\)’s score does not decrease. Candidate \(c\)’s contribution from its contest against \(p\), namely \(f_\alpha(-M(p,c))\), does not increase. The margin between \(a\) and \(b\) is unchanged, so neither rival’s total score increases.

Thus, if \(p\) was a winner, it remains a winner after this transformation. Conversely, an allocation putting \(p\) first is an allowed allocation. Hence it suffices to consider the two rankings \(p>a>b\) and \(p>b>a\).

2. The remaining margin ranges over an exact interval

Let the fixed contributions be

\(F_{cd}=\sum_{r,w}w\nu_{r,w}\sigma_r(c,d)\),

and let the total controllable weighted mass be

\(W=\sum_w w\lambda_w\).

Once all controllable mass ranks \(p\) first, the two margins involving \(p\) are fixed:

\(A=M(p,a)=F_{pa}+W,\qquad B=M(p,b)=F_{pb}+W.\)

Write \(D=F_{ab}\). If \(y_w\) is the mass of controllable type \(w\) assigned to \(p>a>b\), then the remaining mass \(\lambda_w-y_w\) is assigned to \(p>b>a\). Therefore

\(q=M(a,b)=D+\sum_w w(2y_w-\lambda_w),\qquad 0\leq y_w\leq\lambda_w.\)

Since \(w\geq0\), every such margin lies in

\(I=[D-W,D+W].\)

Every point of this interval is attainable. If \(W>0\) and \(q\in I\), set

\(\theta=\dfrac{q-D+W}{2W}.\)

The interval condition gives \(0\leq\theta\leq1\). Assign, for every controllable type,

\(x_{w,p>a>b}=\theta\lambda_w,\qquad x_{w,p>b>a}=(1-\theta)\lambda_w.\)

These masses are nonnegative and sum to \(\lambda_w\). Their margin between \(a\) and \(b\) is

\(D+(2\theta-1)W=q.\)

If \(W=0\), the interval is the singleton \(\{D\}\), and assigning every controllable type to \(p>a>b\) realizes it. This also handles controllable types of weight zero.

Thus \(I\) is exactly the attainable set of margins between \(a\) and \(b\).

3. An exact three-case decision rule

Define

\(P=f_\alpha(A)+f_\alpha(B),\qquad u=f_\alpha(-A),\qquad v=f_\alpha(-B).\)

For an attainable margin \(q\), the three scores are

\(\operatorname{score}(p)=P,\qquad \operatorname{score}(a)=u+f_\alpha(q),\qquad \operatorname{score}(b)=v+f_\alpha(-q).\)

Let \(\ell=D-W\) and \(h=D+W\). The algorithm accepts precisely when at least one row of the following table has all its conditions satisfied.

| Sign of the remaining margin | Attainability condition | Conditions for \(p\) to be a winner |
|---|---|---|
| \(q<0\) | \(\ell<0\) | \(P\geq u\) and \(P\geq v+1\) |
| \(q=0\) | \(\ell\leq0\leq h\) | \(P\geq u+\alpha\) and \(P\geq v+\alpha\) |
| \(q>0\) | \(h>0\) | \(P\geq u+1\) and \(P\geq v\) |

The attainability conditions follow directly from the closed interval \(I\). For example, a negative margin exists exactly when its lower endpoint is negative. The score conditions follow by substituting the corresponding values of \(f_\alpha(q)\) and \(f_\alpha(-q)\).

Correctness. If a row passes, choose \(q=\ell\), \(q=0\), or \(q=h\), respectively. Section 2 constructs an allocation realizing that margin, and the row’s score inequalities make \(p\) a winner.

Conversely, any successful allocation can be transformed as in Section 1. Its remaining margin lies in \(I\) and has exactly one of the three signs. Its attainability and winning inequalities therefore satisfy the corresponding row.

This proves both directions of the decision rule, including all pairwise ties and ties for maximum score.

4. Running time and rational witnesses

For \(m=3\), each listed fixed type contributes to three margins, and each listed controllable type contributes to \(W\). Computing \(F_{pa},F_{pb},F_{ab},W\) takes \(O(\tau)\) arithmetic operations. The subsequent three-case test takes \(O(1)\) operations.

To establish a bit bound, write each listed mass as \(n_i/d_i\), with \(d_i>0\), and form the common denominator

\(Q=\prod_i d_i.\)

Its bit length is \(O(L)\), since the sum of the encoded denominator lengths is at most \(L\). Compute all four aggregates as integers scaled by \(Q\), using contributions of the form

\(w_i n_i(Q/d_i).\)

These integers and their partial sums have \(O(L)\) bits. Indeed, the masses are nonnegative and sum to one, so every partial weighted sum has magnitude at most the largest listed weight; after scaling, its magnitude is at most \(Q\) times that weight. Signed fixed-margin sums satisfy the same bound.

There are \(O(\tau)\) integer multiplications, exact divisions, and additions. Schoolbook arithmetic therefore gives the explicit bound

\(T(m=3,\tau,L)=O(\tau L^2)\)

bit operations. Comparisons of scores take constant time because \(\alpha\) is fixed. The algorithm processes only the listed support; it does not enumerate weights up to their numerical values.

For an accepted row, the selected margin \(q\) is rational. When \(W>0\), the construction’s \(\theta\) and each allocated mass have \(O(L)\)-bit rational representations. Thus successful real allocations can always be replaced by rational allocations of polynomial total encoding length.

5. Relation to discrete manipulation

The cited discrete classification appears in [Theorem 4.1 of *Copeland Voting: Ties Matter*](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas08/proceedings/pdf/paper/AAMAS08_0293.pdf). It does not extend to the divisible formulation: here the common fractional split \(\theta\) realizes every margin in \([D-W,D+W]\), including an exact zero whenever the interval contains it.

The continuous problem is therefore polynomial-time solvable for every fixed rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), with no unresolved cases.
