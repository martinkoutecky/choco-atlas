The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given (m), a society (\mu)\in\Delta(S_m)), (\eta>0), (\epsilon>0), and (\alpha)\ge1), design a polynomial-time randomized ranking mechanism (M) minimizing the worst-case (\beta) such that it is (\epsilon)-DP for every valid (\eta)-neighbor (\mu'=\mu+\eta(e_{\pi'}-e_{\pi})) and satisfies (\mathbb{E}[K_\mu(M(\mu))]\le\alpha\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)+\beta) for every (\mu).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

Let \(S_m\) be the rankings of \(m\) items. A society is a rational, finitely supported distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(S_m)\), given by its support and weights. Define \(K_\mu(\sigma)=\sum_{\pi\in S_m}\mu(\pi)K(\sigma,\pi)\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)=\min_{\sigma\in S_m}K_\mu(\sigma)\), where \(K\) is Kendall tau distance.
A valid \(\eta\)-neighbor is \(\mu'=\mu+\eta(e_{\pi'}-e_\pi)\in\Delta(S_m)\). A randomized mechanism \(M\) is \(\epsilon\)-DP if, for every valid neighbor pair and \(T\subseteq S_m\), \(\Pr[M(\mu)\in T]\le e^\epsilon\Pr[M(\mu')\in T]\). Its additive error is the least \(eta\ge0\) such that \(\mathbb{E}[K_\mu(M(\mu))]\le\alpha\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)+eta\) for every \(\mu\).

Target

Is finding a polynomial-time \(\epsilon\)-DP ranking mechanism minimizing its worst-case additive error polynomial-time solvable, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results

For finite profiles, Theorem 13 of the supplied source proves that, for every \(\alpha,\epsilon>0\), no central \(\epsilon\)-DP \((\alpha,0.01m^2)\)-approximation exists when \(n=o(m/\epsilon)\).

Status

The target does not presently define a finite computational problem, so it cannot rigorously be classified as polynomial-time solvable or \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard. The supplied Theorem 13 is an information-theoretic privacy–utility lower bound, not a computational hardness result.

Nevertheless, several relevant claims can be proved:

  • The finite-profile lower bound transfers to the distributional model when \(\eta=1/n\).
  • There is a polynomial-time \(0\)-DP mechanism with exact additive error \(m(m-1)/4\).
  • For \(\eta=1/n\), every \(\epsilon\)-DP mechanism has additive error at least \(\binom m2/(e^{n\epsilon}+1)\); for \(\epsilon=0\), the optimal error is exactly \(m(m-1)/4\).
  • Computing the nonprivate benchmark \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\) is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard even for polynomial-support rational societies.
  • Once the input domain is finite and explicitly enumerated, the optimal unrestricted private mechanism is computable by linear programming. This does not enforce polynomial running time in the succinct parameters \(m\) and \(n\).

1. Why the requested complexity classification is not yet well-defined

A computational problem requires a family of finitely encoded instances. Here \(M\) is itself an algorithm defined on every rational society, and its objective is

\(\sup_\mu\bigl(\mathbb E[K_\mu(M(\mu))]-\alpha\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\bigr)\).

Thus “finding \(M\)” quantifies over algorithms rather than asking for an output associated with a finite input. The following information is missing:

  • whether \(\alpha,\epsilon,\eta\) are fixed constants or part of the input;
  • an exact finite representation of \(e^\epsilon\);
  • a finite input domain, or a succinct representation of the infinitely many rational societies;
  • a representation of randomized mechanisms and their probabilities;
  • a decision threshold \(B\), such as asking whether some mechanism has additive error at most \(B\);
  • a uniform running-time bound and a finite encoding of candidate mechanisms.

In particular, “polynomial-time mechanism” is an asymptotic semantic property of an algorithm. It is not a finite constraint that can simply be included in an optimization problem.

The value of \(\eta\) also changes the adjacency structure substantially. Along every path of valid \(\eta\)-neighbors, each coordinate changes by an integral multiple of \(\eta\). Therefore, if \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) lie in the same connected component, then

\(\nu(\rho)-\mu(\rho)\in\eta\mathbb Z\)

for every \(\rho\in S_m\). Moreover, if every nonzero weight of \(\mu\) is strictly smaller than \(\eta\), then \(\mu\) is isolated: no coordinate can lose mass \(\eta\), and if another society transferred mass into \(\mu\), the receiving coordinate of \(\mu\) would have weight at least \(\eta\). If societies and their neighbors are required to remain rational, an irrational \(\eta\) yields no neighbor edges at all.

Consequently, a relationship such as \(\eta=1/n\) and a restriction to empirical \(n\)-voter societies is essential.

2. Transfer of the supplied lower bound

Fix \(n\in\mathbb N\), put \(\eta=1/n\), and define the empirical grid

\(\mathcal D_{m,n}=\{\mu:\mu(\pi)=h_\pi/n,\ h_\pi\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\ \sum_\pi h_\pi=n\}\).

Given a profile \(\Pi=(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_n)\), let \(\mu_\Pi\) be its empirical distribution.

Suppose \(M\) is \(\epsilon\)-DP under the stated \(1/n\)-transfer adjacency. Define a finite-profile mechanism by

\(A(\Pi)=M(\mu_\Pi)\).

If \(\Pi'\) is obtained from \(\Pi\) by replacing one ranking \(\pi\) with \(\pi'\), then

\(\mu_{\Pi'}=\mu_\Pi+\frac1n(e_{\pi'}-e_\pi)\).

Thus \(\mu_\Pi\) and \(\mu_{\Pi'}\) are valid neighbors, so \(A\) is centrally \(\epsilon\)-DP. Furthermore,

\(K_{\mu_\Pi}(\sigma)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nK(\sigma,\pi_i)\),

and hence the utility guarantees of \(M\) and \(A\) coincide on empirical societies.

It follows directly from [Theorem 13 of Alabi, Ghazi, Kumar, and Manurangsi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.14652) that, asymptotically when \(n=o(m/\epsilon)\), no mechanism on the full rational-distribution domain can have additive error at most \(0.01m^2\), for any fixed \(\alpha,\epsilon>0\).

This conclusion is information-theoretic: it excludes even computationally unbounded mechanisms. It does not establish \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness of finding the optimum mechanism.

3. A universal polynomial-time private mechanism

Let \(U\) ignore its input and output a uniformly random element of \(S_m\). Because its output distribution is input-independent, \(U\) is \(0\)-DP and therefore \(\epsilon\)-DP for every \(\epsilon\ge0\).

Put \(C=\binom m2\). For every fixed \(\pi\in S_m\) and every unordered pair of items, a uniformly random ranking disagrees with \(\pi\) on that pair with probability \(1/2\). By linearity of expectation,

\(\mathbb E_{\sigma\sim U}[K(\sigma,\pi)]=C/2\).

Averaging over \(\pi\sim\mu\) gives

\(\mathbb E[K_\mu(U(\mu))]=C/2=\frac{m(m-1)}4\)

for every society \(\mu\). Since \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\ge0\), the mechanism has additive error at most \(C/2\).

This value is exact: for a point mass \(\mu=e_\pi\), one has \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)=0\), while the expected loss remains \(C/2\). Thus the additive error of \(U\) is exactly

\(\frac12\binom m2=\frac{m(m-1)}4\).

Uniform sampling of a permutation is polynomial-time.

4. A general lower bound for \(\eta=1/n\)

Let \(\pi\) be any ranking and let \(\bar\pi\) be its reverse. Then \(K(\pi,\bar\pi)=C\).

The point masses \(e_\pi\) and \(e_{\bar\pi}\) are joined in \(\mathcal D_{m,n}\) by the path

\(\mu_k=(1-k/n)e_\pi+(k/n)e_{\bar\pi}\),

where \(0\le k\le n\). Consecutive societies are valid \(1/n\)-neighbors. By composing the DP inequalities along this path, the output laws \(P=M(e_\pi)\) and \(Q=M(e_{\bar\pi})\) satisfy

\(P(T)\le e^{n\epsilon}Q(T)\)

and

\(Q(T)\le e^{n\epsilon}P(T)\)

for every \(T\subseteq S_m\).

Set \(R=e^{n\epsilon}\). Pointwise, the two inequalities imply

\(|P(\sigma)-Q(\sigma)|\le\frac{R-1}{R+1}\bigl(P(\sigma)+Q(\sigma)\bigr)\).

After summing over \(\sigma\), this gives

\(\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\le\frac{R-1}{R+1}\).

Take a maximal coupling \(X\sim P\), \(Y\sim Q\), so that

\(\Pr[X\ne Y]=\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\).

The triangle inequality and the diameter bound \(K(X,Y)\le C\mathbf 1_{\{X\ne Y\}}\) imply

\(C\le K(\pi,X)+K(X,Y)+K(Y,\bar\pi)\).

Taking expectations yields

\(\mathbb E[K(\pi,X)]+\mathbb E[K(Y,\bar\pi)]\ge C\bigl(1-\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\bigr)\ge\frac{2C}{R+1}\).

Both point-mass societies have optimum zero. Therefore, if \(M\) has additive error \(\beta\), each expectation on the left is at most \(\beta\). Consequently,

\(\beta\ge\frac{C}{e^{n\epsilon}+1}\).

For \(\epsilon=0\), this lower bound is \(C/2\), matching the uniform mechanism. Hence:

Exact zero-privacy result. For \(\eta=1/n\), every \(\alpha\ge0\), and \(\epsilon=0\), the minimum possible worst-case additive error is exactly

\(\frac12\binom m2=\frac{m(m-1)}4\).

5. The nonprivate benchmark is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard

Computing \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\), or an attaining ranking, is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard even when \(\mu\) has polynomial support.

Use the standard \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard Minimum Feedback Arc Set in Tournaments problem; deterministic \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness is proved by [Charbit, Thomassé, and Yeo](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/stephan.thomasse/liste/feedback.pdf).

Let \(T\) be a tournament on \(m\) vertices and let \(C=\binom m2\). For each arc \(a\to b\), choose an arbitrary ordering \(R_{ab}\) of the other \(m-2\) vertices and create the two rankings

\(a\succ b\succ R_{ab}\)

and

\(\operatorname{rev}(R_{ab})\succ a\succ b\).

These two rankings contribute pairwise margin \(2\) in favor of \(a\) over \(b\). Their contributions cancel for every other pair:

  • for a pair among the remaining vertices, the second ranking reverses the first;
  • for \(a\) or \(b\) against a remaining vertex, one ranking puts \(a,b\) above that vertex and the other puts them below it.

Taking these two rankings for every tournament arc produces \(N=2C\) rankings. Let \(\mu_T\) be their uniform distribution.

For every tournament arc \(a\to b\), exactly \(N/2+1\) profile rankings put \(a\) before \(b\), while \(N/2-1\) put \(b\) before \(a\). Let \(b_T(\sigma)\) be the number of tournament arcs directed backward in the order \(\sigma\). The contribution of a pair to the total Kendall loss is \(N/2-1\) when \(\sigma\) agrees with the tournament and \(N/2+1\) when it disagrees. Therefore,

\(N K_{\mu_T}(\sigma)=C(N/2-1)+2b_T(\sigma)\).

Thus \(\sigma\) minimizes \(K_{\mu_T}\) if and only if it minimizes \(b_T(\sigma)\), which is precisely Minimum Feedback Arc Set in Tournaments. The construction contains \(O(m^2)\) rankings of length \(m\) and has polynomial encoding length.

This proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness of the benchmark computation. It does not prove hardness of optimal private-mechanism synthesis, because that problem optimizes a single mechanism simultaneously over all societies and couples its behavior through DP constraints.

6. An explicitly finite repair is a linear program

Let \(\mathcal X\) be a finite, explicitly listed set of societies, let \(E\) be its explicitly listed symmetric adjacency relation, and let the privacy multiplier \(\lambda\ge1\) be rational. For every \(x\in\mathcal X\) and \(\sigma\in S_m\), introduce a variable \(p_{x,\sigma}\), together with \(\beta\).

The optimal unrestricted mechanism is given by the linear program minimizing \(\beta\), subject to

\(p_{x,\sigma}\ge0\),

\(\sum_{\sigma\in S_m}p_{x,\sigma}=1\),

\(p_{x,\sigma}\le\lambda p_{y,\sigma}\) for every \((x,y)\in E\),

and

\(\sum_{\sigma\in S_m}p_{x,\sigma}K_x(\sigma)\le\alpha\operatorname{OPT}(x)+\beta\).

Singleton DP inequalities are equivalent to inequalities for arbitrary output events: summing the singleton inequalities proves the event inequality, while singleton events give the converse.

Hence this finite explicit problem is solvable in polynomial time in the size of the displayed LP. However, for the complete empirical domain,

\(|\mathcal D_{m,n}|=\binom{n+m!-1}{m!-1}\),

and the LP has \(|\mathcal D_{m,n}|m!\) probability variables. It is therefore not polynomial in the succinct parameters \(m\) and \(\log n\). Moreover, the LP describes an explicit lookup table and does not enforce that the mechanism have a succinct polynomial-time evaluator.

Conclusion

The rigorous conclusion is not that the requested mechanism-design problem is in \(\mathsf P\), nor that it is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard. As stated, it lacks the finite encoding needed for either classification.

The nearest established facts point in different directions: explicit finite mechanism design is a linear program, computing the underlying Kemeny optimum is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard, and Theorem 13 gives an unconditional utility lower bound. A new formulation and a reduction that handles the global DP constraints are required to obtain the requested complexity dichotomy.

3. Input-independent mechanism

The error calculation is correct.

With \(C=\binom m2\), a uniformly random ranking reverses each pair relative to a fixed ranking with probability \(1/2\). Hence its expected Kendall loss is \(C/2\) against every society. A point-mass society has optimum zero, proving that the worst-case additive error is exactly \(C/2=m(m-1)/4\).

Privacy is immediate because the output law is input-independent.

There is, however, a computational-model ambiguity. On a standard probabilistic Turing machine using fair random bits and required to halt in worst-case polynomial time, exact uniform sampling from \(m!\) outcomes is not automatic when \(m!\) is not a power of two. Rejection sampling gives expected polynomial time but unbounded worst-case time. The prover must state the randomness and running-time model under which “uniform sampling of a permutation is polynomial-time.”

4. Lower bound for \(\eta=1/n\)

This proof is correct.

The path from \(e_\pi\) to \(e_{\bar\pi}\) has \(n\) valid neighbor steps. Group privacy along this path gives the two likelihood-ratio inequalities with \(R=e^{n\epsilon}\).

For each output \(\sigma\), those inequalities imply

\[ |P(\sigma)-Q(\sigma)| \le \frac{R-1}{R+1}(P(\sigma)+Q(\sigma)). \]

Summation gives the stated total-variation bound. The maximal-coupling identity, Kendall triangle inequality, and diameter estimate then yield

\[ \mathbb E[K(\pi,X)]+\mathbb E[K(Y,\bar\pi)] \ge \frac{2C}{R+1}. \]

Because each point mass has optimum zero, each expectation is at most \(\beta\), so

\[ \beta\ge\frac{C}{e^{n\epsilon}+1}. \]

At \(\epsilon=0\), this equals \(C/2\), matching the upper bound.

The heading “zero-privacy result” is misleading: \(\epsilon=0\) is perfect privacy, not absence of privacy. The formula and argument themselves are unaffected.

\[ N K_{\mu_T}(\sigma) = C(N/2-1)+2b_T(\sigma). \]

Thus exact computation of \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu_T)\), or of an optimizer, determines a minimum-backward-arc ordering.
