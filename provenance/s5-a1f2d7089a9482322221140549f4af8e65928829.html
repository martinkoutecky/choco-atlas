The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given vendors with rational base prices and bundle-discount schedules whose demand thresholds are population shares; a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over complete valuation types \(\theta\); and an aggregate allocation \(x_{\theta,a}\) of each type’s mass to whole choices \(a\in S^c\), promised to maximize welfare, compute budget-balanced transfers among positive- and negative-surplus cohorts \(g=(\theta,a)\). Let \(m_g=x_{\theta,a}\), and let \(\sigma_g=v_\theta(a)-p_x(a)-\max_{a'\in S^c}(v_\theta(a')-b(a'))\), where \(p_x(a)\) is the market price under the allocation and \(b(a)\) is the sum of base prices for choice \(a\). Require each payer’s total transfer to be at most \(m_g\sigma_g\), each deficit cohort to receive \(m_h(-\sigma_h)\), and transfers to go only from a positive-surplus cohort buying the full bundle from an active-discount vendor \(s\) to a negative-surplus cohort whose choice includes an item from \(s\). Deviators pay base prices and receive no discount, as in the paper’s rule. Return the resulting per-person price adjustments.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S\) contain \(m\geq1\) real vendors and a null vendor \(\bot\), and let \(c\geq1\) be the number of product types. Supplies are unlimited; a whole choice \(a\in S^c\) selects one vendor per product, with \(\bot\) meaning no purchase.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Each type explicitly lists nonnegative rational valuations \(v_\theta(a)\) for every choice, with \(v_\theta(\bot,\ldots,\bot)=0\).
Vendor \(s\) has nonnegative rational item base prices \(b_s^k\) and an explicitly listed schedule \((q_{s,i},d_{s,i})\): threshold vectors \(q_{s,i}\in[0,1]^c\) increase componentwise, strictly in at least one coordinate between consecutive levels, starting with \(q_{s,0}=0\); nonnegative bundle prices strictly decrease from \(d_{s,0}=\sum_k b_s^k\). The null vendor has zero base prices and no discounts. Let \(H\) count all nonzero schedule levels.
The input allocation lists nonnegative binary rational masses \(x_{\theta,a}\) with \(\sum_a x_{\theta,a}=\mu_\theta\). Define demand \(D_s^k=\sum_{\theta,a:a_k=s}x_{\theta,a}\), activated level \(i_s=\max\{i:D_s^k\geq q_{s,i}^k\text{ for every }k\}\), and base choice price \(b(a)=\sum_k b_{a_k}^k\).
The market price is \(p_x(a)=d_{s,i_s}\) when \(a=(s,\ldots,s)\), and \(p_x(a)=b(a)\) otherwise; a vendor is active when \(i_s>0\). The allocation is promised to maximize \(\sum_{\theta,a}x_{\theta,a}(v_\theta(a)-p_x(a))\) over all feasible real mass allocations.
For each positive-mass cohort \(g=(\theta,a)\), set \(m_g=x_{\theta,a}\) and \(\sigma_g=v_\theta(a)-p_x(a)-\max_{a'\in S^c}(v_\theta(a')-b(a'))\). Any deviation, including refusing all or part of a premium while retaining the same choice, forfeits transfers and discounts and pays base prices; equal utility does not constitute a profitable deviation.
Compute nonnegative rational transfers \(t_{gh}\), allowed only when \(\sigma_g>0\), \(\sigma_h<0\), cohort \(g\) buys the full bundle from an active vendor \(s\), and cohort \(h\)'s choice contains \(s\). Require \(\sum_h t_{gh}\leq m_g\sigma_g\) for every positive-surplus cohort and \(\sum_g t_{gh}=m_h(-\sigma_h)\) for every negative-surplus cohort.
Return the per-person adjustments \(\Delta p_g=(\sum_h t_{gh}-\sum_h t_{hg})/m_g\); thus \(\sum_g m_g\Delta p_g=0\). Adjustments may have either sign; no additional fairness condition is imposed. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including valuation tables, schedules, and allocation.

Is this promised continuous transfer-and-price computation solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,c,H,L\).

For finite buyers, rational stabilizing group transfers exist for every welfare-maximizing allocation and can be computed by maximum flow; the stated bound is \(O(Tm^c)\) for integer aggregate surpluses, where \(T\) is total required subsidy ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.00682v5)). No result settling this exact continuous problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized population mass to one so demand thresholds are population shares.
  • Made complete valuation types explicit tables of nonnegative binary rational values, with zero valuation for buying nothing; included the null vendor with zero prices and no discounts.
  • Specified nonnegative base and bundle prices, componentwise nested threshold vectors, strictly decreasing bundle prices, and activation at weak threshold inequalities.
  • Specified binary rational allocation masses, real feasible mass allocations for the welfare promise, and total binary input length.
  • Included refusal of all or part of a premium among deviations that forfeit discounts and transfers, including when the product choice is retained.
  • Specified nonnegative directed transfer amounts and derived signed per-person adjustments by net outflow divided by positive cohort mass.
  • Retained the graded question's absence of fairness: the anchor concerns rational stabilizing group transfers, while proportional fairness belongs to the subsequent individual-price construction.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The promised computation is polynomial-time solvable. Welfare maximality guarantees that the permitted transfer network can cover every deficit. This guarantee holds directly for continuous masses.

Write \(Q=(m+1)^c\) and \(N=\tau Q\). One explicit bound on the number of exact arithmetic operations is
\( O\!\left(c(H+m+N)+(m+N)(m+cN)^2\right). \)
All numbers manipulated can have \(O(L)\) bits. Using elementary integer arithmetic therefore gives the bit-time bound
\( O\!\left(L^2\left[c(H+m+N)+(m+N)(m+cN)^2\right]\right). \)
Because the valuation tables are explicit, \(N\leq O(L)\), so this is polynomial in \(L\).

Definitions and permitted donors. Let \(\mathcal G\) be the positive-mass cohorts. For a cohort \(g=(\theta,a)\), write \(a_g=a\), and define
\( U_\theta=\max_{a'\in S^c}\bigl(v_\theta(a')-b(a')\bigr). \)
Thus
\( \sigma_g=v_\theta(a_g)-p_x(a_g)-U_\theta. \)

Every positive-surplus cohort buys a full bundle from an active real vendor. Indeed, every other choice has \(p_x(a_g)=b(a_g)\), and hence
\( \sigma_g=v_\theta(a_g)-b(a_g)-U_\theta\leq 0. \)

Let \(A\) be the active vendors. For \(s\in A\), define
\( P_s=\{g\in\mathcal G:\sigma_g>0,\ a_g=(s,\ldots,s)\}, \qquad C_s=\sum_{g\in P_s}m_g\sigma_g. \)
Here \(C_s\) is the total permitted donor capacity at vendor \(s\).

Let
\( \mathcal N=\{h\in\mathcal G:\sigma_h<0\}, \qquad r_h=m_h(-\sigma_h)>0. \)
For \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal N\), define its neighboring active vendors by
\( V(\mathcal B)=\{s\in A:\text{some }h\in\mathcal B\text{ has }s\text{ in }a_h\}. \)

Feasibility lemma. For every \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal N\),
\( \boxed{\ \sum_{h\in\mathcal B}r_h\leq\sum_{s\in V(\mathcal B)}C_s.\ } \)

To prove this, suppose some \(\mathcal B\) violates the inequality, and put \(V=V(\mathcal B)\). Form the set of cohorts
\( \mathcal M= \mathcal B\ \cup\ \{g\in\mathcal G:a_g=(s,\ldots,s)\text{ for some }s\in V\}. \)

Choose, for each type \(\theta\), a base-price maximizing choice \(a_\theta^\star\), so that
\( v_\theta(a_\theta^\star)-b(a_\theta^\star)=U_\theta. \)
Construct an allocation \(y\) by moving the entire mass of every cohort \(g=(\theta,a_g)\in\mathcal M\) to \(a_\theta^\star\), and leaving every other cohort at its original choice. This is feasible: each type retains its total mass, and supplies are unlimited.

We next compare utilities under \(y\) and \(x\).

For every choice \(a\), the schedule assumptions imply
\( p_y(a)\leq b(a). \)
Consequently, the mass moved from a cohort \(g=(\theta,a_g)\) obtains utility per person at least \(U_\theta\). Its total utility change is therefore at least
\( m_g\bigl[U_\theta-(v_\theta(a_g)-p_x(a_g))\bigr] =-m_g\sigma_g. \)

Every cohort outside \(\mathcal M\) has a weakly lower market price under \(y\):

  • A choice that is not a full bundle always has its fixed base price.
  • A full bundle from a vendor inactive under \(x\) originally has its base price, which cannot increase.
  • Consider a full bundle from an active vendor \(s\notin V\). No moved cohort originally buys any item from \(s\). This follows from the definition of \(V\) for cohorts in \(\mathcal B\), and from the full-bundle form of the other moved cohorts. Therefore \(D_s^k(y)\geq D_s^k(x)\) for every product \(k\). The original activated level remains available, so the bundle price cannot increase.
  • There are no unmoved positive-mass full-bundle cohorts at vendors in \(V\), by the definition of \(\mathcal M\).

Writing \(W\) for welfare, these observations give
\( W(y)-W(x)\geq-\sum_{g\in\mathcal M}m_g\sigma_g. \)

The positive-surplus cohorts in \(\mathcal M\) are exactly the cohorts in \(\bigcup_{s\in V}P_s\). Moreover, \(\mathcal M\) contains every cohort in \(\mathcal B\). Thus
\( \sum_{g\in\mathcal M}m_g\sigma_g = \sum_{s\in V}C_s -\sum_{\substack{g\in\mathcal M\\ \sigma_g<0}}m_g(-\sigma_g) \leq \sum_{s\in V}C_s-\sum_{h\in\mathcal B}r_h <0. \)
It follows that \(W(y)>W(x)\), contradicting the promised welfare maximality of \(x\). This proves the lemma.

The argument uses feasible real mass reallocations throughout; no discretization or finite-buyer approximation is needed.

Maximum-flow construction. Put \(R=\sum_{h\in\mathcal N}r_h\). If \(R=0\), return zero transfers and zero adjustments. Otherwise construct a directed network with source \(o\), sink \(z\), one vertex for each active vendor, and one vertex for each negative-surplus cohort. Its arcs are:

  • \(o\to s\), with capacity \(C_s\);
  • \(s\to h\), with capacity \(R\), whenever \(a_h\) contains \(s\);
  • \(h\to z\), with capacity \(r_h\).

All capacities are finite nonnegative rationals.

We verify that every source–sink cut has capacity at least \(R\). A cut crossing a vendor-to-cohort arc already has capacity at least \(R\). For a cut crossing no such arc, let \(\mathcal B\) be the negative-cohort vertices on its sink side. Every vendor in \(V(\mathcal B)\) must also be on the sink side. Hence the cut capacity is at least
\( \sum_{s\in V(\mathcal B)}C_s+ \sum_{h\in\mathcal N\setminus\mathcal B}r_h \geq \sum_{h\in\mathcal B}r_h+ \sum_{h\in\mathcal N\setminus\mathcal B}r_h =R, \)
where the inequality is the feasibility lemma.

The total capacity entering \(z\) is \(R\). By the maximum-flow/minimum-cut theorem, the maximum-flow value is therefore exactly \(R\). In particular, every arc \(h\to z\) is saturated.

Let \(f_{s,h}\) be the flow on \(s\to h\). Then
\( \sum_h f_{s,h}\leq C_s, \qquad \sum_s f_{s,h}=r_h. \)

Cohort transfers and stability. For each vendor \(s\), distribute its outgoing amounts \(f_{s,h}\) among the donor cohorts \(g\in P_s\), whose individual capacities are \(m_g\sigma_g\).

A greedy procedure suffices: maintain a donor’s remaining capacity and a recipient’s remaining required amount, transfer their minimum, and advance whichever becomes zero. Since total outgoing flow is at most \(C_s\), all outgoing amounts can be assigned. The resulting rational transfers satisfy
\( \sum_{g\in P_s}t_{gh}=f_{s,h}, \qquad \sum_h t_{gh}\leq m_g\sigma_g. \)
Every assigned transfer has exactly the required eligibility properties. Summing over vendors gives
\( \sum_g t_{gh}=r_h=m_h(-\sigma_h) \)
for every negative-surplus cohort.

Now return
\( \Delta p_g= \frac{\sum_h t_{gh}-\sum_h t_{hg}}{m_g}. \)
Each transfer contributes once positively and once negatively, so
\( \sum_gm_g\Delta p_g=0. \)

These adjustments also establish the intended stability:

  • If \(\sigma_g>0\), then \(0\leq\Delta p_g\leq\sigma_g\).
  • If \(\sigma_g<0\), then \(\Delta p_g=\sigma_g\).
  • If \(\sigma_g=0\), then \(\Delta p_g=0\).

In every case, the adjusted utility satisfies
\( v_\theta(a_g)-p_x(a_g)-\Delta p_g\geq U_\theta. \)
Every deviation specified in the question forfeits transfers and discounts and therefore gives utility at most \(U_\theta\), including retaining the same choice while refusing a premium. Equality is allowed, so no such deviation is profitable.

Running time and binary encoding. Demand computation, schedule activation, base-price maximization, surplus computation, and network construction take
\( O\!\left(c(H+m+N)\right) \)
exact arithmetic operations.

The network has
\( V_{\mathrm{net}}\leq m+N+2, \qquad E_{\mathrm{net}}\leq m+(c+1)N. \)
Edmonds–Karp computes a maximum flow in
\( O(V_{\mathrm{net}}E_{\mathrm{net}}^2) = O\!\left((m+N)(m+cN)^2\right) \)
operations. Its augmentation bound depends on graph size, independently of the numerical value of \(R\). The greedy disaggregation takes \(O(N+E_{\mathrm{net}})\) additional operations and produces that many nonzero transfers; omitted entries are zero.

For completeness, rational arithmetic does not conceal an exponential bit cost. Let \(B\) be the product of all positive denominators appearing in the numerical input. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Every input rational, computed demand, base price, best base-price utility, and surplus has denominator dividing \(B\). Thus every capacity \(m_g\sigma_g\), every deficit, and every network capacity has denominator dividing \(B^2\).

Scale the network by \(B^2\). It now has integer capacities of \(O(L)\) bits: input magnitudes are at most \(2^L\), prices involve at most \(c\) summands, and total cohort mass is one. Starting from zero, Edmonds–Karp maintains integer flows and residual capacities of \(O(L)\) bits. Rescaling and greedy disaggregation preserve this bit bound. Finally, dividing by a positive input mass \(m_g\) produces adjustments of \(O(L)\) bits.

Elementary arithmetic on these integers costs at most \(O(L^2)\) per operation, giving the stated bit-time bound. Explicitly listing zero transfer entries, if desired, adds at most \(O(N^2L)\) bit operations, already covered by that bound. A coarse bound purely in the input length is \(O(L^7)\).

The [cited finite-buyer analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.00682v5) assumes integral capacities for its Ford–Fulkerson running-time bound. Using Edmonds–Karp avoids dependence on the subsidy’s numerical magnitude. The continuous existence guarantee follows from the welfare argument proved above. The algorithm uses the optimality promise without needing to verify it.
