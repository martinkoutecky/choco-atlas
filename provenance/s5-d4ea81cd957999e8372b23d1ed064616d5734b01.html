The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu\), a bounded worth oracle \(V(t,\ell;q)\) on feasible participation-mass vectors \(q\) satisfying \(\sum_\ell q_{t,\ell}=\mu_t\), and sampling access to specified probability measures \(\nu_{t,\ell}\) over those vectors, compute or estimate every \(\Psi_{t,\ell}=\int V(t,\ell;q)\,d\nu_{t,\ell}(q)\). What query bounds suffice for additive error \(\varepsilon\), and what exact-query lower bounds hold when each \(\nu_{t,\ell}\) has full support?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the finite input bit length.
There are \(m\ge2\) participation levels \(\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\), with \(0\) denoting non-participation. The feasible domain is \(Q=\{q\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T\times m}:\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}q_{t,\ell}=\mu_t\text{ for every }t\}\).
An exact point-query oracle supplies \(V(t,\ell;q)\in[-1,1]\), Borel measurable in \(q\), with \(V(t,\ell;q^0)=0\), where \(q^0_{t,0}=\mu_t\) and all other entries vanish. No continuity or monotonicity is assumed.
For each \((t,\ell)\), a specified Borel probability measure \(\nu_{t,\ell}\) on \(Q\) has an independent exact-sampling oracle. Define \(\Psi_{t,\ell}=\int_Q V(t,\ell;q)\,d\nu_{t,\ell}(q)\). Full support means every nonempty relatively open subset of \(Q\) has positive measure; atoms are permitted.
Count worth queries and sampling calls separately, treating exact real oracle operations as unit cost. State query bounds and any additional running times in \(m,\tau,L,\varepsilon,\delta\), where \(0<\varepsilon,\delta<1\).

What worst-case query bounds suffice to estimate all \(\Psi_{t,\ell}\) simultaneously to additive error \(\varepsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\): are polynomial bounds possible, or are superpolynomial bounds necessary?
When every \(\nu_{t,\ell}\) has full support, can deterministic exact evaluation use finitely many worth queries, or what exact-query lower bounds hold?

For finite games on \(n\ge2\) players and \(m\ge2\) levels, all \(N=nm\binom{n+m-2}{m-1}\) normalized readings—averages conditional on one player's level and the histogram of the others—admit simultaneous error \(\varepsilon\) using \(O(\varepsilon^{-2}N\log(N/\delta))\) queries. 1. Answer and oracle model

Let \(K=m\tau\), the number of integrals to be estimated. Polynomial randomized bounds suffice: one can estimate all \(K\) integrals simultaneously using at most \(Kr\) worth queries and \(Kr\) sampling calls, where

\(r=\left\lceil 2\varepsilon^{-2}\log\!\left(\frac{2K}{\delta}\right)\right\rceil.\)

Thus each query count is \(O\!\left(m\tau\,\varepsilon^{-2}\log(m\tau/\delta)\right)\). No regularity of the worth functions, and no full-support hypothesis, is needed for this bound.

For deterministic exact evaluation, full support implies that no finite number of worth queries suffices, even for one output coordinate. The stronger lower bound depends on the measure: a nonzero nonatomic part prevents determination from any countable collection of point readings, whereas a purely atomic full-support measure can have its integral determined by countably many readings.

All complexity statements below use the stipulated exact-real oracle model.

2. Polynomial randomized approximation

Index the coordinates by \(i=(t,\ell)\), and write \(V_i(q)=V(t,\ell;q)\) and \(\nu_i=\nu_{t,\ell}\).

For each \(i\), independently sample \(r\) points \(Q_{i,1},\ldots,Q_{i,r}\) from \(\nu_i\), query their worths, and output

\(\widehat{\Psi}_i=\frac1r\sum_{j=1}^{r}V_i(Q_{i,j}).\)

Borel measurability ensures that these are measurable random variables. Boundedness ensures integrability, and hence

\(\mathbb E[V_i(Q_{i,j})]=\Psi_i,\qquad -1\leq V_i(Q_{i,j})\leq1.\)

Concentration calculation

For completeness, if \(X\) is any random variable in \([-1,1]\), let \(g(\lambda)=\log\mathbb E[e^{\lambda X}]\). Boundedness permits differentiation under the expectation. The second derivative is the variance under the probability measure obtained by exponential tilting, so \(g''(\lambda)\leq1\). Consequently,

\(\log\mathbb E[e^{\lambda(X-\mathbb EX)}]\leq\lambda^2/2.\)

For \(r\) independent copies, multiplication of moment-generating functions and Markov’s inequality give

\(\Pr\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}(X_j-\mathbb EX_j)\geq r\varepsilon\right)\leq\exp(-\lambda r\varepsilon+r\lambda^2/2).\)

Taking \(\lambda=\varepsilon\), and applying the same argument to \(-X_j\), yields

\(\Pr\!\left(\left|\frac1r\sum_{j=1}^{r}X_j-\mathbb EX_1\right|>\varepsilon\right)\leq2e^{-r\varepsilon^2/2}.\)

Applying this separately to each coordinate and taking a union bound,

\(\Pr\!\left(\max_i|\widehat{\Psi}_i-\Psi_i|>\varepsilon\right)\leq2K e^{-r\varepsilon^2/2}\leq\delta.\)

Independence between different coordinates is unnecessary for the union bound; independence of successive samples within each coordinate suffices.

Query counts and additional running time

The algorithm uses at most

  • \(Kr\) worth queries;
  • \(Kr\) sampling calls.

The additional work is \(O(L+Kr)\) exact-real operations when sampled points can be passed directly to the worth oracle. If each point’s \(K\) coordinates must be explicitly copied, a conservative additional bound is \(O(L+K^2r)\). Both bounds are polynomial in \(L,m,\tau,\varepsilon^{-1}\), and \(\log(\delta^{-1})\).

There is no enumeration of possible types beyond the supplied support \(T\). The number of outputs is \(m\tau\); polynomial dependence on that number is the relevant output-size dependence.

3. Exact information left by point queries

The following lemma identifies precisely what point readings determine. It applies even when the algorithm is granted complete knowledge of the measures.

Lemma: the compatible integral interval

Fix one coordinate, with measure \(\nu\). Let \(S\subseteq Q\) be a finite or countable set of queried points, with consistent observed values \(a(q)\in[-1,1]\). Retain the required value \(V(q^0)=0\). Define

\(F=S\cup\{q^0\},\qquad R=\nu(Q\setminus F),\)

and

\(C=\sum_{q\in S\setminus\{q^0\}}\nu(\{q\})a(q).\)

The series defining \(C\) converges absolutely. Among all admissible Borel worth functions consistent with these readings, the possible integrals are exactly the interval \([C-R,C+R]\).

Proof. Since \(F\) is countable, countable additivity gives

\(\int_F V\,d\nu=C.\)

The bound \(|V|\leq1\) therefore implies

\(\left|\int_Q V\,d\nu-C\right|\leq\nu(Q\setminus F)=R.\)

Conversely, prescribe the observed values on \(S\), prescribe zero at \(q^0\), and prescribe any constant \(c\in[-1,1]\) on \(Q\setminus F\). This defines a Borel function: an arbitrary function on a countable subset of the metric space \(Q\), extended constantly on its complement, is Borel measurable. Its integral is \(C+cR\). Varying \(c\) realizes the entire stated interval. ∎

Consequently, the smallest possible worst-case error based on this transcript is exactly \(R\). In particular, the readings determine the integral exactly if and only if

\(\nu(S\cup\{q^0\})=1.\)

This is an information statement. Computing \(C\) may additionally require knowledge of the atom masses; sampling access alone does not automatically supply them exactly.

For simultaneous evaluation, the lemma applies separately to the set \(S_i\) of points queried for each coordinate \(i\). Readings of other coordinates impose no restrictions on \(V_i\), because the problem assumes no relations between the worth functions.

4. Full support rules out finite deterministic exact evaluation

Geometric fact

The domain \(Q\) is a product of scaled simplices. Its affine dimension is \(\tau(m-1)\geq1\).

More directly, fix any type \(t\). Moving mass continuously from its level \(0\) entry to its level \(1\) entry produces a nontrivial line segment in \(Q\), because \(\mu_t>0\). Thus \(Q\) is infinite.

For any finite \(F\subseteq Q\), choose \(x\in Q\setminus F\). The distance from \(x\) to \(F\) is positive, so there is a nonempty relatively open ball in \(Q\) disjoint from \(F\). If \(\nu\) has full support, this ball has positive measure. Therefore,

\(\nu(Q\setminus F)>0.\)

Adaptive lower bound

Suppose a deterministic algorithm is correct for every admissible worth oracle and terminates after finitely many worth queries. Run it against the oracle whose worths are all zero.

Fix a coordinate \(i\), and let \(S_i\) be the finite set queried for that coordinate during this run. Define

\(V_i^+(q)=\mathbf1_{Q\setminus(S_i\cup\{q^0\})}(q),\qquad V_i^-(q)=-\mathbf1_{Q\setminus(S_i\cup\{q^0\})}(q),\)

and leave every other worth function zero.

Both alternative oracles are Borel measurable, bounded by one, and satisfy the anchor condition. Both return zero at every location queried during the original run. Determinism therefore implies, inductively through the queries, that the algorithm follows the same execution and produces the same output under both alternatives.

Their coordinate-\(i\) integrals, however, are

\(\Psi_i^+=R_i,\qquad \Psi_i^-=-R_i,\qquad R_i=\nu_i(Q\setminus(S_i\cup\{q^0\}))>0.\)

The common output cannot be correct for both. This is a contradiction.

Hence, when every measure has full support, there is no finite worst-case deterministic exact-query bound. Indeed, a universally correct deterministic procedure cannot terminate after finitely many queries even on the all-zero worth oracle.

5. Stronger bounds and the role of atoms

Measures with a nonzero nonatomic part

For a probability measure \(\nu\), let

\(A=\{q\in Q:\nu(\{q\})>0\}\)

be its set of point atoms. This set is countable: for every positive integer \(k\), there are at most \(k\) points of mass at least \(1/k\), and every point atom belongs to one of these finite sets.

Let \(\gamma=\nu(Q\setminus A)\). For any countable queried set \(S\), countable additivity gives

\(\nu((S\cup\{q^0\})\setminus A)=0.\)

It follows that

\(\nu(Q\setminus(S\cup\{q^0\}))\geq\gamma.\)

If \(\gamma>0\), the compatible-integral lemma shows that no countable collection of point readings determines the integral exactly. Its worst-case error is at least \(\gamma\).

In particular, for an atomless measure, that error is exactly one after any countable collection of readings.

Atomless full-support measures exist here. Normalize Lebesgue measure on \(Q\) within its affine hull. The domain is compact and has positive, finite relative volume. To check full support, let \(U\) be a nonempty relatively open subset and choose \(x\in U\). The point with entries \(b_{t,\ell}=\mu_t/m\) has every coordinate positive. For sufficiently small positive \(\theta\), the point \((1-\theta)x+\theta b\) lies in \(U\) and has every coordinate positive. A sufficiently small ball in the affine hull around this point lies in \(U\cap Q\), and has positive relative volume.

Thus, even under full support, deterministic finite-query approximation with uniform error \(\varepsilon<1\) is impossible in the worst case. Random sampling permits the polynomial approximation established above.

Purely atomic full-support measures

Full support does not imply that uncountably many readings are necessary.

Choose a sequence of distinct points \((x_j)_{j\geq1}\) dense in \(Q\), excluding \(q^0\), and define

\(\nu=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}\delta_{x_j}.\)

Such a sequence exists because \(Q\) is separable and has no isolated points. Every nonempty relatively open set contains some \(x_j\), so \(\nu\) has full support.

For every admissible worth function,

\(\int_Q V\,d\nu=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}V(x_j).\)

The series converges absolutely. Thus the countable collection of readings at all the \(x_j\) determines the integral.

Every one of these readings is necessary for universal exact determination: if \(x_j\) is unqueried, changing the worth only at \(x_j\) preserves the other readings and changes the integral by a nonzero amount.

This establishes countable sufficiency as an information statement, not termination of a finite exact computation.
