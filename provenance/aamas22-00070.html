The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational population masses \(\mu_t\) over complete additive valuation types \(t\in T\), rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_j\) of \(q\) replicated indivisible chore categories, and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), define \(\theta_{t,2/3}\) as the maximum \(\lambda\) for which a finite-support distribution \(y_b\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{q}\) satisfies \(\sum_b y_b=2/3\), \(\sum_b b_jy_b=\sigma_j\) for every \(j\), and \(v_t\cdot b\ge\lambda\) whenever \(y_b>0\). Compute a finite-support mass assignment \(x_{t,b}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}b_jx_{t,b}=\sigma_j\), and \(v_t\cdot b\ge\theta_{t,2/3}\) whenever \(x_{t,b}>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive mass, each with its mass \(\mu_t\) as a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=q\ge1\) replicated indivisible chore categories, with binary rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_j\ge0\). Each type has a complete additive valuation vector \(v_t\in\mathbb Q_{\le0}^{m}\). Bundles are arbitrary \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\), including the empty bundle, valued at \(v_t\cdot b\).
Define \(\theta_{t,2/3}\) as the maximum \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\) admitting finitely supported nonnegative real weights \(y_b\) with \(\sum_b y_b=2/3\), \(\sum_b b_jy_b=\sigma_j\) for every \(j\), and \(v_t\cdot b\ge\lambda\) whenever \(y_b>0\).
The computational problem is to find finitely supported nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,b}\) satisfying \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}b_jx_{t,b}=\sigma_j\) for every \(j\), and \(v_t\cdot b\ge\theta_{t,2/3}\) whenever \(x_{t,b}>0\), or report infeasibility. Thresholds are defined by the input, not supplied separately; bundle coordinates are encoded in binary. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Is this mass-assignment problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies with \(n\ge2\) agents and additive nonpositive chore valuations, a complete \(1\)-out-of-\(\lfloor2n/3\rfloor\) MMS allocation always exists and can be computed in strongly polynomial time. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.07424).
No result settling this exact distribution-over-types problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Valuation entries are binary rationals and nonpositive, including zero, following the discrete chore domain.
  • Supplies are nonnegative binary rationals; zero supplies are allowed, and supplies have no additional normalization.
  • The benchmark weights are nonnegative real numbers; computational output masses are rational and bundle coordinates are encoded in binary.
  • Empty bundles are allowed, following the discrete allocation definition.
  • Because continuous feasibility was not specified or established, the computational problem permits reporting infeasibility without restricting the input domain.
  • The category count q is denoted m for running-time statements; L is the total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Every valid input is feasible. The construction below avoids computing the thresholds exactly.

Proof, first reducing to integer costs. Fix a type \(t\). Choose a positive integer \(D\) clearing the denominators of its valuations, and put \(a_j=-Dv_{t,j}\). Thus \(a_j\) are nonnegative integers and \(v_t\cdot b=-a\cdot b/D\).

Coordinates with \(\sigma_j=0\) must be zero in every positive-mass bundle, because all bundle coordinates and masses are nonnegative. Coordinates with \(a_j=0\) do not affect the threshold. Temporarily retain only

\[ J=\{j:\sigma_j>0,\ a_j>0\}. \]

Write \(d=|J|\), and regard \(a\) and \(\sigma\) as vectors on these coordinates. Zero-cost coordinates will be restored later.

For an integer \(C\ge0\), define

\[ K(C)=\{b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{d}:a\cdot b\le C\}. \]

Let \(C^*\) be the smallest integer \(C\) for which there are nonnegative weights on \(K(C)\) satisfying

\[ \sum_b y_b=\frac23, \qquad \sum_b b_jy_b=\sigma_j\quad(j\in J). \]

This minimum exists. Indeed, put

\[ H=\sum_{j\in J}a_j\left\lceil\frac32\sigma_j\right\rceil. \]

Round each coordinate of \(\frac32\sigma\) randomly to its floor or ceiling, preserving its expectation. This gives a finitely supported probability distribution whose bundles have cost at most \(H\). Multiplying its probabilities by \(\frac23\) supplies the required witness. Hence \(C^*\le H\).

Moreover,

\[ \theta_{t,2/3}=-\frac{C^*}{D}. \]

To verify this equality, any full threshold witness with value bound \(\lambda\) has integer costs at most \(\lfloor-D\lambda\rfloor\); its projection onto \(J\) therefore implies \(C^*\le\lfloor-D\lambda\rfloor\). Consequently \(\lambda\le-C^*/D\). Conversely, a witness at \(C^*\) can be extended to the zero-cost coordinates by taking its product with a probability distribution having mean \(\frac32\sigma_j\) on those coordinates. The extended weights have mass \(\frac23\), the correct supplies, and cost at most \(C^*\).

If \(J\) is empty, this argument gives \(C^*=0\) and \(\theta_{t,2/3}=0\).

Lemma: a polynomial-size family of configurations suffices with factor \(\frac43\). Suppose \(d\ge1\), \(a\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^{d}\), and \(C\ge1\). A family \(R(C)\subseteq K(C)\) of size \(O((d+1)^4)\) can be constructed in polynomial time such that every \(b\in K(C)\) admits nonnegative rational weights \(z_r\) satisfying

\[ \sum_{r\in R(C)}z_rr=b, \qquad \sum_{r\in R(C)}z_r\le\frac43. \]

Call coordinate \(j\) large if \(a_j>C/4\), and small otherwise. Enumerate every feasible configuration \(g\) supported on large coordinates. Each contains at most three items, since four large items would have total cost greater than \(C\). There are therefore at most \(\binom{d+3}{3}\) such configurations.

Include every such \(g\) in \(R(C)\). For each small coordinate \(j\), also include

\[ g+\left\lfloor\frac{C-a\cdot g}{a_j}\right\rfloor e_j. \]

These configurations are feasible by construction, and their number is at most \((d+1)\binom{d+3}{3}\).

To prove the representation property, fix \(b\in K(C)\). Let \(g\) be its large-coordinate part, and put

\[ r=C-a\cdot g, \qquad s=\sum_{j\text{ small}}a_jb_j\le r. \]

For each small coordinate with \(b_j>0\), define

\[ q_j=\left\lfloor\frac r{a_j}\right\rfloor\ge1, \qquad Q=\sum_{\substack{j\text{ small}\\b_j>0}}\frac{b_j}{q_j}. \]

If \(Q\le1\), assign weight \(b_j/q_j\) to \(g+q_je_j\), and weight \(1-Q\) to \(g\). This reproduces \(b\) exactly with total weight \(1\).

Now suppose \(Q>1\). Assign weight \(b_j/(Qq_j)\) to \(g+q_je_j\). These weights sum to \(1\), reproduce \(g\), and reproduce \(b_j/Q\) of each small coordinate. The uncovered amount is

\[ h_j=b_j\left(1-\frac1Q\right), \]

with total cost

\[ W=\sum_{j\text{ small}}a_jh_j =s\left(1-\frac1Q\right). \]

We claim \(W\le C/4\). If \(r\le C/4\), this follows from \(W\le s\le r\). If \(r>C/4\), then

\[ q_ja_j>r-a_j\ge r-C/4, \]

so

\[ Q\le\frac{s}{r-C/4}. \]

It follows that

\[ W=s-\frac sQ \le s-r+\frac C4 \le\frac C4. \]

For every small coordinate, the homogeneous configuration \(q_j^0e_j\), where \(q_j^0=\lfloor C/a_j\rfloor\), belongs to \(R(C)\), using \(g=0\). Furthermore,

\[ q_j^0a_j>C-a_j\ge\frac{3C}{4}. \]

Cover the remaining amount \(h_j\) with weight \(h_j/q_j^0\) on this homogeneous configuration. The additional total weight is at most

\[ \sum_{j\text{ small}}\frac{h_j}{q_j^0} \le\frac{4W}{3C} \le\frac13. \]

Together with the weight \(1\) already assigned, this proves the bound \(\frac43\). All constructed weights are rational. This completes the lemma.

Using the lemma to test a capacity. For the fixed type, and any integer \(C\ge1\), construct \(R(C)\) and solve the explicit rational linear program

\[ P(C)= \min\left\{ \sum_{r\in R(C)}z_r: \sum_{r\in R(C)}r_jz_r=\sigma_j\ (j\in J),\ z_r\ge0 \right\}. \]

If it is infeasible, set \(P(C)=+\infty\). Call the capacity successful when \(P(C)\le1\). For nonempty \(J\), declare capacity zero unsuccessful.

Every integer \(C\ge C^*\) is successful. Indeed, take a threshold witness of mass \(\frac23\), whose bundles belong to \(K(C)\). Apply the lemma to each bundle and multiply its representation weights by the bundle’s witness mass. The resulting configurations reproduce the supplies exactly and have total mass at most

\[ \frac43\cdot\frac23=\frac89<1. \]

Thus

\[ C\ge C^*\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P(C)\le\frac89. \]

In particular, failure at a capacity certifies \(C<C^*\).

Finding a safe capacity without computing \(C^*\). Initialize integer endpoints \(\ell=-1\) and \(h=H\). Capacity \(H\) is successful, as proved above; save its LP solution.

While \(h-\ell>1\), test \(c=\lfloor(\ell+h)/2\rfloor\).

  • If \(c\) is successful, replace \(h\) by \(c\) and save its LP solution.
  • Otherwise, replace \(\ell\) by \(c\).

The invariants are that \(h\) is successful and \(\ell<C^*\). A failed test preserves the latter invariant by the implication just proved.

At termination, \(h=\ell+1\). Since \(C^*\) is an integer and \(\ell<C^*\),

\[ h=\ell+1\le C^*. \]

This argument does not require the success predicate to be monotone below \(C^*\).

The saved solution at \(h\) has total mass at most \(1\). Add the missing mass on the empty bundle. We obtain a rational probability distribution \(p\) with mean \(\sigma\) on \(J\), every bundle of which has cost at most \(h\le C^*\).

Restoring zero-cost coordinates and assigning types. A rational probability distribution with prescribed nonnegative rational coordinate means can be constructed with at most one more bundle than the number of coordinates.

Explicitly, for a desired mean \(u_j\), write \(u_j=k_j+f_j\), where \(k_j=\lfloor u_j\rfloor\) and \(0\le f_j<1\). For a uniform parameter \(U\) on \([0,1]\), use coordinate

\[ k_j+\mathbf1_{\{U<f_j\}}. \]

The resulting bundle is constant between consecutive distinct fractional parts. Interval lengths give rational probabilities, and its coordinate expectations equal \(u_j\).

Use this construction with \(u_j=\sigma_j\) for the zero-cost coordinates, and take its product with \(p\). Set all zero-supply coordinates to zero. Denote the resulting full probability distribution by \(p_t\). It has mean \(\sigma\), and every positive-probability bundle satisfies

\[ v_t\cdot b =-\frac{a\cdot b}{D} \ge-\frac{C^*}{D} =\theta_{t,2/3}. \]

For a type with \(J\) empty, construct \(p_t\) directly on its zero-cost coordinates.

Do this independently for every type, and output

\[ x_{t,b}=\mu_t p_{t,b}. \]

Then

\[ \sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_{t,b}b_jx_{t,b} =\sum_t\mu_t\sigma_j =\sigma_j, \]

and every positive-mass bundle meets its type’s threshold.

Polynomial running time and output size. Clearing denominators by their product gives \(D\), the integer costs, and \(H\) with bit lengths polynomial in \(L\). The search uses \(O(\log(H+1))\) LP calls.

Each LP has \(O((m+1)^4)\) variables and at most \(m\) equality constraints. Its configuration coordinates are bounded by \(H\), so its explicit rational matrix has polynomial encoding length. The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore applies: feasibility and the finite optimum can be computed exactly in polynomial bit time.

An optimal basic solution has at most \(d\) positive variables. Its coefficients have polynomial bit length by the determinant bounds for basic solutions. Adding the empty bundle gives at most \(d+1\) bundles; restoring zero-cost coordinates by the product construction gives at most \((m+1)^2\) bundles per type. Multiplication by \(\mu_t\) preserves polynomial encoding length.

Consequently both the computation and the complete rational output have size polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). No expansion into a finite society is required.
