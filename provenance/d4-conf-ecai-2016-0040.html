The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given issues \(I\), an integrity constraint \(\Gamma\), a finite support \(S\subseteq R(I,\Gamma)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_r\) summing to \(1\), and partial ballots \(\ell,\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_u\), is there a rational ballot \(r^\star\in R(I,\Gamma)\) minimizing \(D_\mu(r^\star)=\sum_{r\in S}\mu_r d(r^\star,r)\) that agrees with \(\ell\) and does not agree with any \(\ell_j\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I=\{x_1,\ldots,x_m\}\) be Boolean issues and \(\Gamma\) an explicitly represented propositional formula over \(I\), of size \(c\). A rational ballot is a Boolean assignment satisfying \(\Gamma\); write \(R(I,\Gamma)\) for their set.
A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(S\subseteq R(I,\Gamma)\) of \(\tau\) distinct types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_r\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible ballots.
The input also contains partial ballots \(\ell,\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_u\in\{0,1,\star\}^m\), where \(u\geq0\). Agreement means equality at every coordinate other than \(\star\); nonagreement means disagreement at some specified coordinate.
Using Hamming distance \(d\), define \(D_\mu(b)=\sum_{r\in S}\mu_r d(b,r)\). Decide whether some global minimizer \(b\in R(I,\Gamma)\) agrees with \(\ell\) and does not agree with any \(\ell_j\). All tied global minimizers are eligible; the partial ballots do not restrict the domain of minimization.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. State running times in \(m,\tau,c,u,L\).

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite, equally weighted profiles, this decision problem is \(\Theta_2^p\)-hard even with three individuals, hence para-\(\Theta_2^p\)-hard parameterized by the number of individuals. No separate classification for the binary-rational mass encoding is assumed here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified that rational ballots are Boolean assignments satisfying an explicitly represented propositional integrity constraint, rather than ballots with fractional coordinates.
  • Specified partial ballots, including unrestricted coordinates and the existential disagreement required for nonagreement; allowed u = 0.
  • Specified that minimization is global over all feasible ballots and that every tied minimizer is eligible.
  • Used m for the number of issues and L for total binary input length, with c denoting the integrity-constraint size.

Conclusion

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, already when \(\tau=2\), both masses are \(1/2\), and \(u=0\). Thus a polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The reduction below respects the requirement that every supplied support ballot satisfies \(\Gamma\), even when the source formula is unsatisfiable.

1. Direct reduction from SAT

Let \(\varphi(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\) be a SAT instance. Introduce two additional issues \(y,z\), so that \(m=n+2\), and define

\( \displaystyle \Gamma= \left(\neg y\land\neg z\land\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\neg x_i\right) \lor \left(y\land z\land\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}x_i\right) \lor \left(\neg y\land z\land\varphi\right). \)

Supply the two distinct ballots

\( r^{(0)}=(0^n,0,0),\qquad r^{(1)}=(1^n,1,1), \)

each with mass \(1/2\). They satisfy the first and second disjuncts of \(\Gamma\), respectively. Consequently, the support is valid and nonempty independently of whether \(\varphi\) is satisfiable.

Set

\( \ell=(\star^n,0,1),\qquad u=0. \)

Every rational ballot is a global minimizer. Indeed, the two support ballots are complementary at every coordinate. For every Boolean assignment \(b\), each coordinate contributes exactly one to the sum of its distances from them. Hence

\( \displaystyle d(b,r^{(0)})+d(b,r^{(1)})=m, \qquad D_\mu(b)=\frac m2. \)

In particular, this objective is constant on the entire rational domain.

The query is equivalent to satisfiability of \(\varphi\). An assignment agreeing with \(\ell\) has \(y=0,z=1\). These values make the first two disjuncts of \(\Gamma\) false, and the third disjunct holds exactly when its original coordinates satisfy \(\varphi\). Therefore

\( \displaystyle \varphi\text{ is satisfiable} \iff \text{some rational global minimizer agrees with }\ell. \)

This proves both directions of the reduction. Crucially, \(\ell\) is used only to select eligible minimizers; it never changes the minimization domain.

If \(\varphi\) has formula size \(s\), then the constructed instance has \(\tau=2\), \(u=0\), \(m=n+2\), and \(c=O(s+n)\). Its binary encoding and construction time are polynomial in the SAT input length. The masses have constant encoding length.

2. The support-two case is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete

For completeness, membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) also holds for the entire restriction \(\tau=2\), including unequal masses.

Write the support as \(\{r,s\}\), with masses \(\alpha,\beta>0\) and \(\alpha+\beta=1\).

If \(\alpha>\beta\), the triangle inequality gives, for every \(b\),

\( \displaystyle D_\mu(b)-D_\mu(r) = \alpha d(b,r)+\beta\bigl(d(b,s)-d(r,s)\bigr) \geq (\alpha-\beta)d(b,r). \)

Thus \(r\) is the unique global minimizer: it is rational by hypothesis, and the displayed lower bound is strictly positive for \(b\neq r\). The case \(\beta>\alpha\) is symmetric. Eligibility of this unique minimizer can be checked directly.

If \(\alpha=\beta=1/2\), then

\( \displaystyle D_\mu(b)\geq\frac{d(r,s)}2, \)

and this value is attained by the rational ballot \(r\). Equality holds exactly when \(b_i=r_i=s_i\) at every coordinate where \(r_i=s_i\): at such a coordinate, disagreement contributes two instead of zero to the distance sum; at coordinates where \(r_i\neq s_i\), either value contributes one.

Consequently, a certificate in the equal-mass case is a ballot satisfying \(\Gamma\), these shared-coordinate requirements, and all query conditions. Verification takes \(O(c+m(u+1))\) Boolean operations. Comparing the masses takes polynomial time in \(L\).

Together with Section 1, this establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for \(\tau=2\). For \(\tau=1\), the sole support ballot is the unique minimizer, so the problem is polynomial-time solvable.

3. Binary-rational arithmetic and general upper bounds

Suppose the masses are encoded as binary fractions \(\mu_r=p_r/q_r\), with positive integer denominators. Define

\( \displaystyle Q=\prod_{r\in S}q_r,\qquad w_r=p_r(Q/q_r). \)

These are integers satisfying \(\sum_r w_r=Q\). Multiplying the objective by \(Q>0\) preserves all minimizers:

\( \displaystyle F(b)=QD_\mu(b)=\sum_{r\in S}w_r d(b,r). \)

The total denominator encoding length is at most \(L\), so \(Q\) has \(O(L)\) bits. Moreover, \(0\leq F(b)\leq mQ\), so objective values have \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits. All comparisons can therefore be exact, with polynomial bit complexity.

An \(\mathrm{NP}\) oracle can answer whether a rational ballot satisfies \(F(b)\leq t\): guess \(b\), evaluate \(\Gamma\), and compute \(F(b)\). Binary search over the integer interval from \(0\) to \(mQ\) finds the unrestricted optimum \(F^*\) using \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) oracle queries. One final query asks for a rational ballot satisfying \(F(b)=F^*\) and all eligibility conditions.

This proves the general upper bound

\( \displaystyle \text{the problem belongs to }\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{NP}}=\Delta_2^p. \)

The oracle algorithm has polynomial overhead in \(L\); each certificate verification uses polynomial time in \(c+m\tau+m(u+1)\) and \(L\).

A fully deterministic algorithm enumerates all \(2^m\) assignments, retaining the smallest unrestricted objective and whether an eligible ballot attains it. Using elementary exact integer arithmetic, a conservative bit-operation bound is

\( \displaystyle O\!\left( L^3+ 2^m\bigl(c+m(\tau+u+1)\bigr)L^2 \right). \)

4. Transfer of the supplied hardness premise

Under binary numerator–denominator encoding, the stated \(\Theta_2^p\)-hardness for three equally weighted individuals transfers directly. Replace repeated ballots by one support type with mass equal to its multiplicity divided by three. These masses are positive, have constant encoding length, and preserve the objective exactly:

\( \displaystyle D_\mu(b)=\frac13\sum_{i=1}^{3}d(b,r_i). \)

The resulting support has \(\tau\leq3\). Hence the general problem is also \(\Theta_2^p\)-hard, parameterized hardness persists for support size, and no separate mass-encoding classification is needed for this lower bound. The direct SAT reduction remains sufficient for the requested conclusion and uses only the dyadic mass \(1/2\).
