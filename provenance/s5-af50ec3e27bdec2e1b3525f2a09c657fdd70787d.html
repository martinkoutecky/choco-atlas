The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite rational distribution \(\mu\) over commute-interval types \([x_j,y_j]\), does the least point where right-side mass is at most left-side mass maximize \(W_\mu(t)=-\sum_j\mu_j(|t-x_j|+|t-y_j|)\), and does the resulting direct mechanism remain group strategy-proof when a coalition may split type mass among alternate reports?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\ge1\) distinct commute-interval types \(I_j=[x_j,y_j]\), with rational endpoints \(x_j\le y_j\), and positive binary rational masses \(\mu_j\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types; \(L\) is the total encoding length. Facility locations range over \(\mathbb R\), so no finite candidate-count parameter \(m\) applies; state any running times in \(\tau,L\) and any additional input parameters.
The true benefit of type \(j\) at location \(t\) is \(u_j(t)=-(|t-x_j|+|t-y_j|)\), and welfare is \(W_\mu(t)=\sum_j\mu_j u_j(t)\).
Define \(R_\mu(t)=\sum_{j:x_j>t}\mu_j\), \(A_\mu(t)=\sum_{j:y_j<t}\mu_j\), and the direct mechanism \(f(\mu)=\inf\{t\in\mathbb R:R_\mu(t)\le A_\mu(t)\}\).
A coalition selects rational masses \(0\le c_j\le\mu_j\) with \(\sum_jc_j>0\). For each \(j\), it distributes \(c_j\) among finitely many rational reported intervals \(J_{jk}=[a_{jk},b_{jk}]\), where \(a_{jk}\le b_{jk}\), using nonnegative rational masses \(q_{jk}\) satisfying \(\sum_kq_{jk}=c_j\).
The resulting report distribution is \(\nu=\sum_j(\mu_j-c_j)\delta_{I_j}+\sum_{j,k}q_{jk}\delta_{J_{jk}}\), where \(\delta_I\) denotes unit mass at \(I\). Benefits remain evaluated at true types. Group strategy-proofness means that every such deviation has some \(j\) with \(c_j>0\) and \(u_j(f(\nu))\le u_j(f(\mu))\); thus only deviations strictly benefiting every participating type are forbidden.

For every such society, does \(f(\mu)\) maximize \(W_\mu\) over \(\mathbb R\), and is \(f\) group strategy-proof under these mass-splitting deviations, or can either property fail?

For finite lists of agents, the lowest balanced mechanism maximizes total benefit for these benefit functions and is group strategy-proof for interval-peaked benefits. [Source](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ipsjjip/28/0/28_976/_pdf)

Result

Both properties hold for every society in the question: \(f(\mu)\) maximizes \(W_\mu\) over \(\mathbb R\), and \(f\) is group strategy-proof under the specified mass-splitting deviations.

The cited paper establishes the finite-agent statements in Theorems 1 and 2. The proof below treats masses and split reports directly. [Fukui, Shurbevski, and Nagamochi](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ipsjjip/28/0/28_976/_pdf)

1. Characterizing the mechanism

Consider any finitely supported interval distribution \(\eta=\sum_i w_i\delta_{[\ell_i,r_i]}\), where \(w_i>0\), \(\ell_i\le r_i\), and \(\sum_iw_i=1\). Define its cumulative endpoint mass by

\(H_\eta(t)=\sum_iw_i\bigl(\mathbf 1_{\{\ell_i\le t\}}+\mathbf 1_{\{r_i\le t\}}\bigr).\)

Each interval contributes its mass at each endpoint; a degenerate interval contributes twice at its single endpoint. Thus the total endpoint mass is \(2\).

Let \(E_\eta\) be the finite set of endpoints, and define

\(a_\eta=\min\{e\in E_\eta:H_\eta(e)\ge1\}.\)

This minimum exists because \(H_\eta(\max E_\eta)=2\). By its definition,

\(H_\eta(t)<1\quad\text{for every }t<a_\eta,\qquad H_\eta(a_\eta)\ge1.\)

Lemma. \(f(\eta)=a_\eta\).

Proof. Write

\(X_\eta(t)=\sum_{\ell_i\le t}w_i,\qquad Y_\eta^{<}(t)=\sum_{r_i<t}w_i.\)

The balance difference is

\(R_\eta(t)-A_\eta(t)=1-X_\eta(t)-Y_\eta^{<}(t).\)

Since \(Y_\eta^{<}(t)\le\sum_{r_i\le t}w_i\), we obtain

\(R_\eta(t)-A_\eta(t)\ge1-H_\eta(t)>0\qquad(t<a_\eta).\)

Consequently, no point below \(a_\eta\) belongs to the set whose infimum defines \(f(\eta)\).

Because there are finitely many endpoints, there is an \(\varepsilon>0\) such that no endpoint lies in \((a_\eta,a_\eta+\varepsilon)\). For every \(t\) in that interval,

\(X_\eta(t)+Y_\eta^{<}(t)=H_\eta(a_\eta).\)

Therefore,

\(R_\eta(t)-A_\eta(t)=1-H_\eta(a_\eta)\le0.\)

The balance set contains points arbitrarily close to \(a_\eta\) from above and contains no point below it. Its infimum is \(a_\eta\), proving the lemma.

This argument does not assume that the balance inequality holds at the infimum itself. For example, masses \(3/5\) at \([0,0]\) and \(2/5\) at \([1,1]\) give \(f(\eta)=0\), although \(R_\eta(0)=2/5>A_\eta(0)=0\).

Thus \(f\) selects the lower weighted median of the endpoint masses.

2. Welfare maximization

Define the cost

\(C_\mu(t)=-W_\mu(t)=\sum_j\mu_j\bigl(|t-x_j|+|t-y_j|\bigr).\)

This is a continuous, piecewise linear function. At every point that is not a true endpoint, differentiation gives

\(C_\mu'(t)=\sum_j\mu_j\bigl(2\mathbf 1_{\{x_j\le t\}}-1+2\mathbf 1_{\{y_j\le t\}}-1\bigr)=2\bigl(H_\mu(t)-1\bigr).\)

Set \(a=f(\mu)\). The lemma gives \(H_\mu(t)<1\) for every \(t<a\). Monotonicity of \(H_\mu\), together with \(H_\mu(a)\ge1\), gives \(H_\mu(t)\ge1\) for every \(t>a\). Hence, away from endpoints,

\(C_\mu'(t)<0\quad(t<a),\qquad C_\mu'(t)\ge0\quad(t>a).\)

To compare \(C_\mu(a)\) with its value at any other point, partition the segment between those points at all true endpoints. On each resulting open subsegment, \(C_\mu\) is linear with the slope just calculated; continuity joins the subsegments. It follows that \(C_\mu\) strictly decreases up to \(a\) and never decreases after \(a\).

Therefore \(C_\mu(a)\le C_\mu(t)\) for every \(t\in\mathbb R\), or equivalently,

\(W_\mu(f(\mu))\ge W_\mu(t)\qquad\text{for every }t\in\mathbb R.\)

In fact, \(f(\mu)\) is the leftmost welfare maximizer.

3. Group strategy-proofness under mass splitting

Fix an allowed deviation. Let

\(S=\{j:c_j>0\},\qquad a=f(\mu),\qquad b=f(\nu).\)

The condition \(\sum_jc_j>0\) ensures that \(S\) is nonempty. The reported distribution \(\nu\) has finite support, nonnegative masses, and total mass

\(\sum_j(\mu_j-c_j)+\sum_{j,k}q_{jk}=1.\)

After discarding zero masses and merging duplicate intervals, the lemma therefore applies to \(\nu\).

Suppose, for a contradiction, that every participating type strictly benefits:

\(u_j(b)>u_j(a)\qquad(j\in S).\)

For an interval \(I=[x,y]\), let \(d(t,I)=\inf_{z\in I}|t-z|\). Directly considering \(t<x\), \(x\le t\le y\), and \(t>y\) gives

\(|t-x|+|t-y|=(y-x)+2d(t,I).\)

Thus strict benefit means strictly smaller distance to the true interval.

If \(b=a\), no participant strictly benefits. It remains to consider the two directions of movement.

Case 1: \(b>a\)

We first show that \(x_j>a\) for every \(j\in S\). If \(x_j\le a\), then for every \(t\ge a\),

\(d(t,I_j)=\max\{t-y_j,0\},\)

which is nondecreasing in \(t\). Moving from \(a\) to \(b>a\) could not strictly benefit that type. This contradicts the assumption, establishing \(x_j>a\).

Both true endpoints of every participating type therefore exceed \(a\). Removing coalition mass contributes no reduction to \(H_\mu(a)\), while every reported endpoint contributes a nonnegative amount. Consequently,

\(H_\nu(a)=H_\mu(a)+\sum_{j\in S,k}q_{jk}\bigl(\mathbf 1_{\{a_{jk}\le a\}}+\mathbf 1_{\{b_{jk}\le a\}}\bigr)\ge H_\mu(a)\ge1.\)

By the lemma, \(f(\nu)\le a\). This contradicts \(b>a\).

Case 2: \(b<a\)

We similarly show that \(y_j<a\) for every \(j\in S\). If \(y_j\ge a\), then for every \(t\le a\),

\(d(t,I_j)=\max\{x_j-t,0\}.\)

Moving left from \(a\) could not reduce this distance. Hence strict benefit requires \(y_j<a\).

Since \(S\) is finite and nonempty, choose \(t\) satisfying

\(\max\{b,\max_{j\in S}y_j\}<t<a.\)

Both true endpoints of every participating type lie below \(t\). Removing its coalition mass therefore removes exactly \(2c_j\) from cumulative endpoint mass. Its replacement reports contribute at most

\(2\sum_kq_{jk}=2c_j.\)

It follows explicitly that

\(H_\nu(t)=H_\mu(t)-2\sum_{j\in S}c_j+\sum_{j\in S,k}q_{jk}\bigl(\mathbf 1_{\{a_{jk}\le t\}}+\mathbf 1_{\{b_{jk}\le t\}}\bigr)\le H_\mu(t).\)

Because \(t<a=f(\mu)\), the lemma gives \(H_\mu(t)<1\). But \(t>b=f(\nu)\), so the same lemma and monotonicity give \(H_\nu(t)\ge1\). These inequalities contradict \(H_\nu(t)\le H_\mu(t)\).

Both directions are impossible. Therefore every allowed deviation has some participating type \(j\) satisfying

\(u_j(f(\nu))\le u_j(f(\mu)).\)

This is precisely the group strategy-proofness required in the question.

4. Exact computation

The characterization also gives an algorithm using only the supported types:

  • Attach weight \(\mu_j\) to each of \(x_j\) and \(y_j\).
  • Sort the \(2\tau\) endpoint entries.
  • Return the first coordinate at which cumulative weight reaches \(1\).

This uses \(O(\tau\log(2\tau))\) comparisons and arithmetic operations.

For an explicit bit bound, write \(\mu_j=p_j/d_j\) and use the common denominator \(D=\prod_jd_j\). Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Replace each endpoint weight by the integer \(p_j(D/d_j)\), and compare cumulative weight with \(D\). Rational endpoint comparisons use cross multiplication. With schoolbook integer arithmetic, a sufficient total bound is

\(O\bigl(\tau\log(2\tau)L^2\bigr)\)

bit operations. No finite facility-candidate parameter is needed.
