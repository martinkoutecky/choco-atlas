The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), an integer budget \(K\), a finite support \(T\) of nonempty approval types \(A_t\subseteq C\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and a rational fractional committee \(x\in[0,1]^C\) with \(\|x\|_1\le K\), decide whether there is an integral committee \(W\subseteq C\) with \(|W|\le K\) such that \(|W\cap A_t|\ge\left\lfloor\sum_{c\in A_t}x_c\right\rfloor\) for every \(t\in T\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set with \(m=|C|\), and let \(K\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\).
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct nonempty approval types \(A_t\subseteq C\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval types.
The input also contains a binary-rational fractional committee \(x\in[0,1]^C\) satisfying \(\sum_{c\in C}x_c\le K\).
Decide whether an integral committee \(W\subseteq C\) exists with \(|W|\le K\) and \(|W\cap A_t|\ge\left\lfloor\sum_{c\in A_t}x_c\right\rfloor\) for every \(t\). These inequalities apply to every positive-mass type, independently of its mass.
Let \(L\) denote the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\), and \(K\).

Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Finite-voter floor-preserving rounding is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when the supplied fractional committee lies in the fractional core.
The stated distribution formulation is already covered by that hardness result: duplicate approval sets impose identical constraints, and positive masses do not affect feasibility.

Conclusion

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, using the finite-voter hardness theorem stated in the question as a premise. Compressing duplicate approval sets and assigning positive masses gives a polynomial-time reduction to the support representation.

Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

For each support type, define \(d_t=\left\lfloor\sum_{c\in A_t}x_c\right\rfloor\). A certificate is the incidence vector of a committee \(W\subseteq C\), containing \(m\) bits. Verification checks \(|W|\le K\) and \(|W\cap A_t|\ge d_t\) for every \(t\).

These checks use exact arithmetic. Set \(B=L+\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil+2\). For any type, a common denominator for its fractional sum can be obtained by multiplying the denominators of the relevant coordinates. Its binary length is at most \(L+1\). Since each coordinate lies in \([0,1]\), the numerator of the sum has binary length at most \(B\). Thus successive rational additions and the final integer division can be performed in \(O(mB^2)\) bit operations using elementary arithmetic.

All demands can therefore be computed, and a certificate verified, in \(O(L+\tau mB^2)\) bit operations. Comparing the committee size with the binary-encoded \(K\) is included in this bound. Hence the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Polynomial-time hardness reduction

Use the stated theorem that finite-voter floor-preserving rounding is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Write an instance of that problem as an explicitly listed sequence of nonempty approval sets \(A_1,\ldots,A_n\), together with \(C\), \(K\), and the supplied fractional committee \(x\).

Let \(B_1,\ldots,B_\tau\) be the distinct approval sets occurring in that sequence. Keep \(C\), \(K\), and \(x\) unchanged, and use these distinct sets as the support.

To assign masses that are valid even if “binary-rational” requires finite binary expansions, let \(b=\lceil\log_2\tau\rceil\), and define \(\mu_t=2^{-b}\) for \(t<\tau\), and \(\mu_\tau=1-(\tau-1)2^{-b}\). Because \(\tau\le 2^b\), every mass is positive, and their sum is \(1\). Each has an encoding of \(O(1+\log\tau)\) bits. For \(\tau=1\), this construction gives the single mass \(1\).

For every committee \(W\), we have the equivalence

\(\displaystyle \bigl(\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\ |W\cap A_i|\ge\lfloor\sum_{c\in A_i}x_c\rfloor\bigr) \iff \bigl(\forall t\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\},\ |W\cap B_t|\ge\lfloor\sum_{c\in B_t}x_c\rfloor\bigr). \)

Indeed, every original approval set equals one of the \(B_t\), and every \(B_t\) occurs in the original list. Duplicate sets impose exactly the same inequality. The assigned masses do not appear in any feasibility inequality, while the budget constraint is unchanged. Thus the reduction preserves both yes-instances and no-instances.

For completeness, let \(N\) be the source instance’s total binary length. With explicit candidate and voter lists, \(m,n\le N\). Expanding approval sets into incidence vectors and eliminating duplicates by pairwise comparison takes \(O(N^3)\) bit operations; the resulting instance has length \(O(N^2)\). The reduction is therefore polynomial.

The source theorem’s additional fractional-core restriction is unnecessary here: hardness on that restricted source family already implies hardness of the present problem. The reduction does not need to preserve fractional-core membership under the newly assigned masses.

Running-time implications

Combining the reduction with membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in the total input length exists if and only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

An unconditional exact algorithm first computes the demands and then enumerates all committees of size at most \(k=\min\{K,m\}\). Its running time can be bounded by

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(L+\tau mB^2+m(\tau+1)\log_2(m+1)\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{m}{j}\right).\)

The cases \(K=0\) and \(K\ge m\) are always feasible for valid inputs: respectively, choose the empty committee or all candidates. Hardness concerns the unrestricted family with intermediate budgets.
