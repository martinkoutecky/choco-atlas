The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite worker-type and position-type sets \(A\) and \(B\), a compatibility graph \(E\), rational masses \(\alpha_a,\beta_b\) summing to \(1\), rational edge weights \(w_{ab}\), and rational piecewise-linear strictly increasing payoff functions \(u^a_{ab},u^b_{ab}\), find a stable allocation or determine that none exists. An allocation is a finite-support rational measure \(\lambda_{ab}\) on splits \(x\in[0,w_{ab}]\) for each \((a,b)\in E\), with total matched mass incident to each type at most its mass; unmatched mass receives payoff \(0\). For every compatible edge \((a,b)\) and split \(x\), require that it is not the case that positive mass of type \(a\) receives payoff below \(u^a_{ab}(x)\) and positive mass of type \(b\) receives payoff below \(u^b_{ab}(w_{ab}-x)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be disjoint nonempty finite worker-type and position-type sets, with a connected compatibility graph \(E\subseteq A\times B\) and positive rational weights \(w_{ab}\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\alpha_a,\beta_b\), where \(\sum_a\alpha_a=\sum_b\beta_b=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; write \(m=|E|\).
Each edge has continuous strictly increasing payoff functions \(u^a_{ab},u^b_{ab}:[0,w_{ab}]\to\mathbb R\), explicitly encoded by rational breakpoints and affine coefficients. Let \(K\) be the total number of linear pieces and \(L\) the total input bit length. Endpoint payoffs need not be zero.
An allocation consists of nonnegative finite atomic measures \(\lambda_{ab}\) on \([0,w_{ab}]\), with rational atom locations and masses, satisfying \(\sum_b\lambda_{ab}([0,w_{ab}])\le\alpha_a\) and \(\sum_a\lambda_{ab}([0,w_{ab}])\le\beta_b\).
An atom at \(x\) gives its worker mass payoff \(u^a_{ab}(x)\) and its position mass payoff \(u^b_{ab}(w_{ab}-x)\); residual unmatched mass receives \(0\). Require individual rationality: every occupied atom gives both sides nonnegative payoffs.
For each type \(t\), let \(D_t(z)\) be the total mass of that type receiving payoff strictly below \(z\), including unmatched mass. The allocation is stable if, for every \((a,b)\in E\) and every real \(x\in[0,w_{ab}]\), either \(D_a(u^a_{ab}(x))=0\) or \(D_b(u^b_{ab}(w_{ab}-x))=0\). Indifference does not block.

Can a stable allocation be found, or its nonexistence determined, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finite individual agents, existence and finite termination of a constructive algorithm are established, without a polynomial-time bound ([Mani–Ozdaglar–Pentland](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.3062)).
A related discrete assignment model with quasilinear buyers, piecewise-linear transfer frictions, and gross-substitutes valuations has a polynomial-time equilibrium algorithm ([Balkanski–En–Faenza](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13620v2)); this is a different model. No polynomial-time bound or NP-hardness result for the precise mass problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored continuity of the piecewise-linear payoff functions and specified their explicit rational encoding.
  • Required positive edge weights and a connected compatibility graph, following the discrete setup; both type sets are nonempty.
  • Interpreted normalization as unit mass on each side separately, rather than unit mass across both sides combined.
  • Allowed real-valued payoff functions with arbitrary endpoint values, since no zero-endpoint normalization is stated; required nonnegative realized payoffs to retain the intended nonnegative-offer and voluntary-participation convention.
  • Used the intended absence of simultaneous strict improvements, correcting the displayed discrete stability definition's conjunction; ties do not block.
  • Specified that finite-support rational measures have rational split locations and nonnegative rational atom masses, and that blocking is tested at all real feasible splits.
  • Defined m as the number of compatibility edges, K as the total number of payoff pieces, and L as total input bit length.

1. Minimum-payoff certificates

For an edge $e=(a,b)$, write
$$ f_e(x)=u^a_{ab}(x), \qquad v_e(x)=u^b_{ab}(w_{ab}-x). $$
Then $f_e$ is continuous and strictly increasing, while $v_e$ is continuous and strictly decreasing.

For any individually rational finite atomic allocation, define $p_a$ to be the minimum payoff received by worker mass of type $a$, including $0$ if some of that type is unmatched. Define $q_b$ analogously. These minima exist and are attained by positive mass because each type has positive total mass and finitely many realized payoffs. Individual rationality implies
$$ p_a,q_b\geq 0. $$

Consequently,
$$ D_a(z)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z>p_a, $$
and similarly for $b$. Thus stability is equivalent to
$$ \text{there is no }e=(a,b)\text{ and }x\in[0,w_{ab}] \text{ such that }f_e(x)>p_a,\quad v_e(x)>q_b. $$

Lower bounds suffice

We will also use thresholds that need not equal the actual minima.

Suppose nonnegative thresholds $p_a,q_b$, edge masses $y_e\geq 0$, and splits $x_e$ for positive-mass edges satisfy:

  • The capacity inequalities.
  • Every positive-mass edge satisfies

$$ f_e(x_e)\geq p_a,\qquad v_e(x_e)\geq q_b. $$

  • Positive thresholds require saturation:

$$ p_a>0\ \Longrightarrow\ \sum_b y_{ab}=\alpha_a, \qquad q_b>0\ \Longrightarrow\ \sum_a y_{ab}=\beta_b. $$

  • No split strictly improves both thresholds.

Then the allocation $y_e\delta_{x_e}$ is individually rational and stable.

Indeed, every occupied payoff is at least its threshold. An unmatched type has threshold $0$ by condition 3, so its unmatched payoff also meets the threshold. Therefore every actual minimum is at least the corresponding threshold. A blocking split would strictly exceed both actual minima and hence both thresholds, contradicting condition 4.

One split per occupied edge

Every stable allocation has at most one distinct occupied split on each edge.

To prove this, suppose an edge has positive atoms at $x_1<x_2$. For any $x$ strictly between them,
$$ f_e(x)>f_e(x_1), \qquad v_e(x)>v_e(x_2). $$
The worker mass at $x_1$ and position mass at $x_2$ both strictly improve, producing a blocker.

This argument does not require common realized payoffs within a type and remains valid with arbitrary endpoint payoffs.

2. Three linear inequalities characterize each interval

Refine each edge’s partition by combining the breakpoints of $f_e$ with the reflected breakpoints of $u^b_{ab}$. On every resulting interval $[s,t]$, where $s<t$, write
$$ f_e(x)=cx+d, \qquad v_e(x)=\eta-hx. $$
Strict monotonicity implies $c,h>0$.

Let $Q$ be the total number of refined intervals. If an edge’s original functions have $k_e^a$ and $k_e^b$ pieces, its refinement has at most $k_e^a+k_e^b-1$ intervals. Hence
$$ Q\leq K. $$

Fix thresholds $p,q$ and put
$$ \ell=\frac{p-d}{c}, \qquad r=\frac{\eta-q}{h}. $$

Blocking test

A blocker on $[s,t]$ is exactly a point satisfying
$$ x\in[s,t],\qquad \ell<x<r. $$
Such a point exists if and only if
$$ \ell<t,\qquad r>s,\qquad \ell<r. $$
Necessity follows from any such point. Conversely, these inequalities imply
$$ \max\{s,\ell\}<\min\{t,r\}, $$
so a point between these two quantities is a blocker.

Negating the three inequalities shows that this interval contains no blocker if and only if at least one of
$$ p\geq ct+d, $$
$$ q\geq \eta-hs, $$
$$ hp+cq\geq hd+c\eta $$
holds.

Contract feasibility test

There is a split in $[s,t]$ giving both sides at least their thresholds if and only if
$$ p\leq ct+d, $$
$$ q\leq \eta-hs, $$
$$ hp+cq\leq hd+c\eta. $$

Indeed, the required set of splits is
$$ [s,t]\cap[\ell,r]. $$
This intersection is nonempty exactly when
$$ \ell\leq t,\qquad r\geq s,\qquad \ell\leq r. $$
When these conditions hold, one valid split is
$$ x=\max\left\{s,\frac{p-d}{c}\right\}. $$

The blocking test and the contract feasibility test therefore use precisely the same three hyperplanes, with opposite inequality directions. This observation is the basis of the algorithm below.

3. Existence with real splits

For completeness, existence is proved here rather than assumed from the notes. Rational existence will follow from the algorithm in Section 4.

Choose a common denominator $N$ for all masses. Replace each worker type $a$ by $N\alpha_a$ identical agents and each position type $b$ by $N\beta_b$ identical agents. Each side has $N$ agents.

For a positive integer $n$, restrict edge $e$ to splits
$$ G_{e,n}=\left\{\frac{jw_e}{n}:0\leq j\leq n\right\}. $$
For every compatible pair of agents, retain the contracts giving both sides nonnegative payoffs. Each agent ranks these contracts by payoff, breaks ties arbitrarily, and ranks every retained contract above being unmatched.

Run deferred acceptance: a free worker proposes its best unproposed contract; each position retains its best received contract and rejects the others. Rejected workers continue proposing. There are finitely many contracts, each proposed at most once, so the procedure terminates.

No contract strictly improves both agents’ original payoffs over the final outcome. Otherwise, the worker ranks that contract above its final outcome and must have proposed it. Its rejection implies that the position’s final contract ranks at least as high as the rejected contract, contradicting the position’s strict payoff improvement.

Aggregate matched agent pairs into masses $1/N$. Let $p_a^{(n)},q_b^{(n)}$ be the actual type minima. A grid split strictly exceeding both corresponding minima would give agents attaining those minima a strictly improving acceptable contract. Thus no such grid split exists.

Let $y_e^{(n)}$ be the aggregate mass on edge $e$. When it is positive, select any occupied split $x_e^{(n)}$; otherwise set $x_e^{(n)}=0$. For every positive-mass edge,
$$ f_e(x_e^{(n)})\geq p_a^{(n)}, \qquad v_e(x_e^{(n)})\geq q_b^{(n)}. $$

Define
$$ M=\max\left(\{0\}\cup \{f_e(w_e),v_e(0):e\in E\}\right). $$
Every individually rational realized payoff lies in $[0,M]$. The vectors
$$ \left(y^{(n)},x^{(n)},p^{(n)},q^{(n)}\right) $$
therefore lie in a fixed compact box. Pass to a convergent subsequence, retaining its original grid indices $n_j$, and denote the limits by $y,x,p,q$.

Capacity inequalities pass to the limit. If $y_e>0$, then $y_e^{(n_j)}>0$ eventually, and continuity gives
$$ f_e(x_e)\geq p_a,\qquad v_e(x_e)\geq q_b. $$
If $p_a>0$, then $p_a^{(n_j)}>0$ eventually. That worker type is consequently fully matched in those approximations, yielding
$$ \sum_b y_{ab}=\alpha_a. $$
The position statement is identical.

Finally, suppose a split $z$ strictly exceeded both limiting thresholds. Choose grid points $z_j\in G_{e,n_j}$ converging to $z$. Continuity and threshold convergence imply, eventually,
$$ f_e(z_j)>p_a^{(n_j)}, \qquad v_e(z_j)>q_b^{(n_j)}, $$
contradicting grid stability.

The limiting data satisfy all the lower-bound certificate conditions from Section 1. Hence $y_e\delta_{x_e}$ is a stable allocation with real masses and locations.

The agent expansion is used only for existence; its size need not be polynomial in the binary input length.

4. A finite candidate set in threshold space

The key improvement is that only the $\tau$ threshold variables need to be enumerated.

For each refined interval on $e=(a,b)$, include the three hyperplanes
$$ p_a=ct+d, $$
$$ q_b=\eta-hs, $$
$$ hp_a+cq_b=hd+c\eta. $$
For each threshold coordinate $\theta_t$, also include
$$ \theta_t=0,\qquad \theta_t=M. $$
Let $\mathcal H$ be this list. Its size satisfies
$$ H:=|\mathcal H|\leq 3Q+2\tau\leq 3K+2\tau. $$

Candidate lemma

At least one feasible lower-bound certificate has its threshold vector at the intersection of $\tau$ hyperplanes from $\mathcal H$ with linearly independent normals.

Proof. Start with the real stable allocation proved in Section 3. By Section 1 it has at most one split per occupied edge. Let its edge masses be $y_e$, splits be $x_e$, and actual minima be $\theta^0$.

Construct a polyhedron $P$ in the threshold variables alone:

  • Impose $0\leq\theta_t\leq M$.
  • For every refined interval, choose one blocking-test inequality satisfied by $\theta^0$ and impose it.
  • For every occupied edge, choose a refined interval containing $x_e$ and impose all three contract feasibility inequalities for that interval.
  • If a type is not fully matched under $y$, impose $\theta_t=0$.

All these constraints hold at $\theta^0$, so $P$ is nonempty. It is bounded by the box constraints, and every constraint boundary belongs to $\mathcal H$.

A nonempty bounded polyhedron has a vertex. One way to see this is to minimize its coordinates successively: each minimization selects a nonempty face of the preceding compact face, and after all coordinates are fixed the remaining face is a singleton.

At a vertex, the active constraint normals span the ambient space. Otherwise a nonzero direction orthogonal to all active normals would preserve the active constraints; sufficiently small perturbations in both directions would also preserve all inactive constraints. This contradicts extremality.

Thus a vertex $\theta^\ast$ of $P$ is determined by $\tau$ independent hyperplanes from $\mathcal H$.

The chosen blocking inequalities exclude every blocker at $\theta^\ast$. Each originally occupied edge admits a split meeting $\theta^\ast$. Moreover, any type with $\theta_t^\ast>0$ was fully matched under the original masses: every originally nonsaturated type was constrained to threshold $0$.

Keeping the original masses and choosing the newly feasible splits therefore gives a lower-bound certificate at $\theta^\ast$. $\square$

Algorithm

Enumerate every $\tau$-element subset of $\mathcal H$.

For each subset:

  • If its normals are dependent, discard it. Otherwise solve its rational linear system to obtain a candidate $\theta$.
  • Discard $\theta$ unless it lies in $[0,M]^\tau$ and passes every blocking test.
  • Form a graph $F_\theta\subseteq E$ containing precisely the edges that admit a split meeting both thresholds. Use the contract feasibility tests to determine this.
  • Solve the following rational linear feasibility problem:

$$ y_e\geq0\qquad(e\in F_\theta), $$
$$ \sum_{b:(a,b)\in F_\theta}y_{ab}\leq\alpha_a, \qquad \sum_{a:(a,b)\in F_\theta}y_{ab}\leq\beta_b, $$
together with
$$ \sum_b y_{ab}=\alpha_a\quad\text{whenever }p_a>0, $$
$$ \sum_a y_{ab}=\beta_b\quad\text{whenever }q_b>0. $$

  • For a feasible candidate, select a feasible refined interval for every positive-mass edge and use

$$ x_e=\max\left\{s,\frac{p_a-d}{c}\right\}. $$
Output $y_e\delta_{x_e}$.

Every output is stable by the lower-bound certificate lemma. The candidate lemma ensures that at least one candidate succeeds.

If $M=0$, the empty allocation is already stable: all possible worker and position payoffs are nonpositive, so no split strictly improves both unmatched payoffs.

5. A stable allocation with forest support

For a successful threshold candidate, choose a vertex of the mass feasibility polyhedron. This polyhedron is nonempty and bounded: each edge mass is bounded by its endpoint capacities.

Its positive-mass support contains no cycle.

Indeed, every cycle in a bipartite graph has even length. If all its edge masses are positive, alternately add and subtract a sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$ around the cycle. Both resulting vectors remain nonnegative, and every vertex’s total incident mass is unchanged. Thus capacities and required saturation equalities remain satisfied.

The original mass vector is the midpoint of two distinct feasible vectors, contradicting its being a vertex.

Therefore the positive-mass support is a forest on the $\tau$ types, and has at most
$$ \tau-1 $$
edges. Selecting feasible splits on those edges gives a stable allocation with at most $\tau-1$ atoms.

This bound can be necessary. Consider a star with one worker type, positive masses on all position types, and
$$ u^a_{ab}(x)=1+x, \qquad u^b_{ab}(y)=1+y. $$
If any worker mass is unmatched, equal total masses imply that some position type also has unmatched mass. Both minima are then $0$, and their edge admits a strict improvement for both sides, contradicting stability. Hence all mass is matched. Every position type has positive mass, so every star edge is occupied. There are exactly $\tau-1$ such edges.

6. A direct polynomial algorithm for one type on one side

Assume $A=\{a\}$. Then $\alpha_a=1$, and connectedness makes $a$ adjacent to every position type.

For each $b$, define its individually rational split set
$$ I_b=\{x\in[0,w_{ab}]:f_{ab}(x)\geq0,\ v_{ab}(x)\geq0\}. $$
Because one function increases and the other decreases, $I_b$ is either empty or a closed interval, possibly a singleton. Its endpoints are rational and computable by scanning the linear pieces and solving any relevant affine zero equation.

Let
$$ S=\{b:I_b\neq\varnothing\}. $$
For $b\in S$, let $R_b=\max I_b$ and
$$ T_b=f_{ab}(R_b)\geq0. $$
Choose the worker threshold
$$ p= \begin{cases} \min_{b\in B}T_b,&S=B,\\ 0,&S\neq B. \end{cases} $$

For each $b\in S$, assign mass $\beta_b$ on its edge at
$$ x_b=\min\{x\in[0,R_b]:f_{ab}(x)\geq p\}. $$
This set is nonempty because $f_{ab}(R_b)=T_b\geq p$. Explicitly, $x_b=0$ if $f_{ab}(0)\geq p$; otherwise it is the unique solution of $f_{ab}(x_b)=p$.

These splits are individually rational. Their worker payoffs are at least $p$, and $x_b\leq R_b$ implies
$$ v_{ab}(x_b)\geq v_{ab}(R_b)\geq0. $$

Leave every $b\notin S$ unmatched. The assigned worker mass is $\sum_{b\in S}\beta_b\leq1$. If $p>0$, then $S=B$, so the worker type is saturated.

Set
$$ q_b= \begin{cases} v_{ab}(x_b),&b\in S,\\ 0,&b\notin S. \end{cases} $$

There is no blocker:

  • If $b\in S$ and $x_b=0$, then $v_{ab}(x)\leq q_b$ for every available split.
  • If $b\in S$ and $x_b>0$, then $f_{ab}(x_b)=p$. For $x\leq x_b$, the worker payoff is at most $p$; for $x>x_b$, the position payoff is strictly below $q_b$.
  • If $b\notin S$, a split satisfying $f_{ab}(x)>p\geq0$ and $v_{ab}(x)>q_b=0$ would belong to $I_b$, contradicting its emptiness.

The lower-bound certificate lemma proves stability. All computations use polynomially many rational operations on polynomial-size numbers. The symmetric construction handles $|B|=1$.

7. Complexity consequences and the remaining question

The preceding algorithm proves that every valid instance has a rational stable allocation of polynomial encoding length. Stability can be verified in polynomial time: check capacities and individual rationality, compute actual minima, and apply the three-inequality blocking test to every refined interval.

Thus the problem admits a formulation in $\mathrm{TFNP}$: its solution relation is total, polynomially balanced, and polynomial-time verifiable. Invalid input encodings can be recognized in polynomial time and assigned a trivial output.

This also limits a conventional satisfiability reduction. Suppose polynomial-time maps $F,G$ satisfy:

  • $F$ maps every Boolean formula $\varphi$ to a valid instance;
  • from every stable allocation of $F(\varphi)$, $G$ correctly decides whether $\varphi$ is satisfiable.

An unsatisfiable formula would then have an $\mathrm{NP}$ certificate: guess a polynomial-size stable allocation of $F(\varphi)$, verify it, and check that $G$ returns “unsatisfiable.” Such a certificate exists for every unsatisfiable formula and for no satisfiable formula. Hence $\mathrm{UNSAT}\in\mathrm{NP}$, implying
$$ \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}. $$
This is a conditional restriction on that reduction notion, not a general hardness classification.

The unresolved step is to remove the dependence of the exponent on $\tau$, or to establish an appropriate search-hardness result. Forest support reduces the size of a solution, but the proof does not give a polynomial method for finding its thresholds or selecting its edges when $\tau$ varies.
