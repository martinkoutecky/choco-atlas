The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given item types \(J\), per-capita supplies \(\bar c_j\), and a finite support \(T\) of bidder types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), where each type has a complete XOR menu of bids \((a_{tk},b_{tk})\), compute an efficient mass allocation \(y_{tk}\) and nonnegative prices \(p_j\). Each type’s assigned mass must not exceed \(\mu_t\), total item use must not exceed per-capita supply, every used bid must maximize \(b_{tk}-p\cdot a_{tk}\) over that type’s menu and the outside option, and positively priced supply must clear.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of its \(\tau\geq1\) bidder types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There is one seller with zero reserves, \(m\geq1\) item types, and positive binary-rational per-capita supplies \(\bar c_j\). Binary-encoded integers \(q_j\geq1\) bound individual bundle quantities; these bounds are distinct from aggregate per-capita supplies.
Each type has an explicitly listed complete XOR menu \(K_t\), containing every bundle \(a\in\prod_{j=1}^m\{0,\ldots,q_j\}\) once, with truthful nonnegative binary-rational values \(b_t(a)\), normalized at the empty bundle and weakly increasing coordinatewise. Utilities are quasilinear. Write \(K=\sum_t|K_t|\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A mass allocation has \(y_{tk}\geq0\), \(\sum_{k\in K_t}y_{tk}\leq\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,k}a_{tk,j}y_{tk}\leq\bar c_j\). Each individual receives at most one whole menu bundle, but a type's mass may split among bundles; unassigned mass receives the empty bundle. Efficiency means maximizing \(\sum_{t,k}b_{tk}y_{tk}\) over these allocations.
An equilibrium uses common prices \(p_j\geq0\) on the given items: every bundle receiving positive mass maximizes \(b_{tk}-p\cdot a_{tk}\) over the complete menu and the outside option of utility zero. If \(\sum_k y_{tk}<\mu_t\), the outside option must also maximize utility. Ties may split arbitrarily, and \(p_j>0\) requires \(\sum_{t,k}a_{tk,j}y_{tk}=\bar c_j\).

Is exact computation of an efficient mass allocation and equilibrium prices polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite bidders, a valid-cut procedure is asserted to converge to an artificial Walrasian equilibrium, where cuts become additional priced items; this is not a polynomial-time guarantee ([Day–Lubin](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15893v2)).
Equilibrium existence for continuum quasilinear markets with indivisible bundles is known ([Azevedo–Weyl–White](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/records/1btvv-6pd54)); no exact computational complexity classification for the stated rational-support representation was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational bidder masses to sum to one, matching the distribution and per-capita interpretation.
  • Specified positive rational supplies, nonempty bidder support, at least one item type, one seller, and zero reserves.
  • Introduced positive integer individual bundle bounds q_j to make complete menus finite. Individual bounds are separate from per-capita supplies, which constrain aggregate consumption.
  • Interpreted complete menus as explicit tables of all bundles within the individual bounds, with truthful nonnegative rational values satisfying normalization and free disposal.
  • Specified that mass splitting allocates whole bundles to different individuals of the same type, and defined efficiency by total mass-weighted value.
  • Restored the demand condition for unassigned mass: its outside option must maximize utility. Allowed arbitrary splitting among tied utility maximizers.
  • Retained prices on the given items as requested by the graded question, without adding artificial items or imposing a price-selection tie-break.

Claim. Exact computation of an efficient mass allocation and equilibrium prices is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,K,L\). The prices apply to the original items, and the result holds without additional restrictions on valuations.

1. The allocation linear program.

Let \(I=\{(t,k):t\in T,\ k\in K_t\}\), so \(|I|=K\). Write \(a_{tk}\) for the bundle and \(b_{tk}\) for its value. Consider the rational linear program

\(\displaystyle (P)\qquad \max_{y\geq0}\ \sum_{(t,k)\in I}b_{tk}y_{tk}, \)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{k\in K_t}y_{tk}\leq\mu_t \quad(t\in T), \qquad \sum_{(t,k)\in I}a_{tk,j}y_{tk}\leq\bar c_j \quad(1\leq j\leq m). \)

These are exactly the feasibility and efficiency conditions in the question. Thus an optimal solution of \((P)\) is an efficient mass allocation.

The feasible set is nonempty because \(y=0\) is feasible. It is closed and bounded: each coordinate satisfies \(0\leq y_{tk}\leq\mu_t\). Consequently, \((P)\) has an attained, finite optimum.

Splitting a type’s mass does satisfy the requirement that each individual receives one whole bundle. For example, represent type \(t\) by an interval of length \(\mu_t\), partition it into pieces of lengths \(y_{tk}\), and assign bundle \(a_{tk}\) to every individual in its corresponding piece. The remaining piece receives the empty bundle.

2. The dual program.

Introduce one dual variable \(u_t\geq0\) for each type constraint and one \(p_j\geq0\) for each supply constraint. The dual is

\(\displaystyle (D)\qquad \min_{u,p\geq0} \left(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tu_t+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bar c_jp_j\right), \)

subject to

\(\displaystyle u_t+p\cdot a_{tk}\geq b_{tk} \qquad((t,k)\in I). \)

It is feasible: take \(p=0\) and \(u_t=\max_{k\in K_t}b_{tk}\). Strong linear-programming duality applies because the primal is feasible with finite optimum. Hence both programs have optimal solutions, with equal objective values.

Choose any primal optimum \(y\) and any dual optimum \((u,p)\). Define their feasibility slacks by

\(\displaystyle s_t=\mu_t-\sum_{k\in K_t}y_{tk},\qquad r_j=\bar c_j-\sum_{(t,k)\in I}a_{tk,j}y_{tk},\qquad d_{tk}=u_t+p\cdot a_{tk}-b_{tk}. \)

All these quantities are nonnegative. Direct expansion gives the duality-gap identity

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_tu_t+\sum_j\bar c_jp_j-\sum_{t,k}b_{tk}y_{tk} = \sum_tu_ts_t+\sum_jp_jr_j+\sum_{t,k}y_{tk}d_{tk}. \)

For the chosen optima, the left side is zero. Every summand on the right is nonnegative, so each summand is zero. In particular,

\(\displaystyle y_{tk}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ b_{tk}-p\cdot a_{tk}=u_t, \)

\(\displaystyle s_t>0\ \Longrightarrow\ u_t=0, \qquad p_j>0\ \Longrightarrow\ r_j=0. \)

3. Verification of every equilibrium condition.

Dual feasibility gives, for every type and every menu bundle,

\(\displaystyle b_{tk}-p\cdot a_{tk}\leq u_t,\qquad 0\leq u_t.\)

Thus \(u_t\) bounds the utility of every bundle and of the outside option.

If \(y_{tk}>0\), the first complementary-slackness implication shows that its utility equals \(u_t\). Therefore every bundle assigned positive mass maximizes utility over the complete menu and the outside option. Multiple assigned bundles necessarily tie, as permitted.

If some type mass is unassigned, then \(s_t>0\), hence \(u_t=0\). Every menu utility is consequently at most zero, while the outside option has utility zero. Thus the outside option maximizes utility.

Finally, \(p_j>0\) implies \(r_j=0\), which is precisely

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,k}a_{tk,j}y_{tk}=\bar c_j. \)

Prices are nonnegative by dual feasibility. Therefore \((y,p)\) is an equilibrium satisfying all the stated requirements, and \(y\) is efficient.

This argument also proves that every efficient mass allocation can be supported by equilibrium prices: the gap identity holds for any primal optimum paired with any dual optimum.

For completeness, the converse holds as well. Given an equilibrium \((y,p)\), set

\(\displaystyle u_t=\max\left\{0,\ \max_{k\in K_t}(b_{tk}-p\cdot a_{tk})\right\}. \)

Then \((u,p)\) is dual feasible. Utility maximization gives \(y_{tk}d_{tk}=0\); the condition on unassigned mass gives \(u_ts_t=0\); and the condition on positive prices gives \(p_jr_j=0\). The gap identity therefore gives equal primal and dual objectives. Weak duality then proves that \(y\) is efficient.

4. Exact polynomial-time computation.

Use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly represented rational linear program can be solved in polynomial time in its encoding length, returning an exact rational optimum when the optimum is finite and attained. Polynomial-time rational LP optimization follows from the ellipsoid method and its exact optimization consequences. See [Khachiyan’s polynomial algorithms](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239) and [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Section 3](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1981a.pdf).

Here all hypotheses have been checked:

  • \((P)\) has \(K\) variables and \(m+\tau\) constraints, besides nonnegativity.
  • \((D)\) has \(m+\tau\) variables and \(K\) constraints, besides nonnegativity.
  • All coefficients are explicitly given integers or binary-encoded rationals.
  • Both programs are feasible and have attained, finite optima.

Constructing even dense representations of these programs requires only polynomial space and time in \(m,\tau,K,L\). In particular, binary bundle quantities remain binary coefficients; there is no expansion into individual copies of items.

There is also no obstacle from exact output size. To see this directly, clear denominators separately in each constraint row. The resulting integer coefficients have polynomial bit length. If their absolute values are bounded by \(2^B\), a vertex defined by \(r\) independent active constraints has coordinates given by Cramer’s rule. The relevant determinants have absolute value at most \(r!\,2^{rB}\), and therefore have bit length at most

\(\displaystyle rB+\log_2(r!)+O(1).\)

This is polynomial in the program’s encoding length. Optimal vertices exist for both programs: the primal optimal face is nonempty and compact; the dual optimal face is also compact because every coefficient \(\mu_t,\bar c_j\) in its objective is strictly positive and all dual variables are nonnegative. A vertex of an optimal face is a vertex of the corresponding feasible polyhedron.

The algorithm is therefore to solve \((P)\) and \((D)\) exactly and return \(y\) together with the item-price coordinates \(p\). The preceding proof verifies the output.

5. Why finite-bidder hardness does not carry over.

The finite-bidder construction in [Day–Lubin](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15893v2) starts from integer winner determination and introduces cuts priced as artificial items. In the present problem, type masses may split among whole bundles, so the linear program itself describes the permitted allocations. Its dual already supplies prices on the original items.

The representation matters as well: complete menus can be exponentially large in \(m\), but all \(K\) entries are explicitly part of the input. The proved bound is polynomial in that explicit input size.
