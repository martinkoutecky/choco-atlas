The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) goods and \(\tau\) types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), per-member endowments \(e_t\), and WGS demand systems \(D_t\) with elasticity at least \(-f\), compute prices \(p\) and, for each type \(t\), an individual price vector \(\hat p_t\), desired bundle \(z_t\), and allocation \(x_t\). Require \(p \le \hat p_t \le (1+\epsilon)p\), \(z_t \in D_t(\hat p_t,p^\top e_t)\), \(0 \le x_t \le z_t\), \(\sum_t \mu_t x_t \le \bar e := \sum_t \mu_t e_t\), and \(p^\top(\bar e-\sum_t\mu_t x_t) \le \epsilon p^\top\bar e\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\ge1\) divisible goods, rational per-member endowments \(e_t\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m\), and aggregate supply \(\bar e=\sum_t\mu_t e_t\) with \(\bar e_j>0\) for every good.
For positive prices \(q\in\mathbb R_{>0}^m\) and budgets \(b\ge0\), each demand correspondence \(D_t(q,b)\subseteq\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\) is nonempty, satisfies \(D_t(\alpha q,\alpha b)=D_t(q,b)\) for \(\alpha>0\), and satisfies \(q^\top y=b\) for every \(y\in D_t(q,b)\).
WGS means that for every \(y\in D_t(q,b)\), \(q'\ge q\), and \(b'\ge b\), some \(y'\in D_t(q',b')\) satisfies \(y'_j\ge y_j\) whenever \(q'_j=q_j\). Elasticity at least \(-f\), for a given rational \(f>0\), means that increasing only \(q_j\) by a factor \(a\ge1\), at unchanged budget, admits such a demand \(y'\) additionally satisfying \(y'_j\ge y_j/a^f\).
Demand systems are represented by oracles returning an initial demand and the witnesses above, including the elasticity witness for a single-price increase. Use exact real arithmetic and comparisons; one oracle call costs \(T_D\). Let \(L\) be the total binary length of the rational input, and let \(R\ge2\) be a supplied bound on the largest-to-smallest price ratio attainable at a positive-price approximate equilibrium of the requested accuracy.
Given rational \(0<\epsilon<1/4\), compute \(p\in\mathbb R_{>0}^m\), normalized by \(\min_jp_j=1\), and per-member vectors \(\hat p_t,z_t,x_t\) satisfying
\(p\le\hat p_t\le(1+\epsilon)p,\quad z_t\in D_t(\hat p_t,p^\top e_t),\quad 0\le x_t\le z_t,\quad \sum_t\mu_t x_t\le\bar e,\quad p^\top(\bar e-\sum_t\mu_t x_t)\le\epsilon p^\top\bar e\), with all vector inequalities componentwise.

Result

The computation is polynomial-time solvable in the stated oracle model. There is an exact reduction to a finite market with \(\tau\) agents that preserves the approximation conditions, price ratios, WGS, and the elasticity bound.

A running-time bound is

\( \operatorname{poly}(L)+O\!\left(\tau m^2(1+f)(T_D+m)\epsilon^{-2}\log R\right). \)

The reduction does not require any homogeneity of demand with respect to budget alone.

1. Construct one aggregate agent per supported type

For each type \(t\), define its aggregate endowment and demand correspondence by

\( E_t:=\mu_t e_t,\qquad \widetilde D_t(q,B):=\left\{\mu_t y:y\in D_t(q,B/\mu_t)\right\}. \)

Here \(B\) denotes the aggregate agent’s budget. Since \(\mu_t>0\), these definitions are valid for every admissible price vector and budget.

The resulting finite market has exactly \(\tau\) agents and aggregate supply

\( \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}E_t=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t e_t=\bar e. \)

At market prices \(p\), aggregate agent \(t\)’s budget is

\( B_t=p^\top E_t=\mu_t p^\top e_t. \)

Consequently,

\( \widetilde D_t(\hat p_t,B_t)=\left\{\mu_t z:z\in D_t(\hat p_t,p^\top e_t)\right\}. \)

This identity is the essential reduction: aggregate demand is defined through the original per-member budget.

2. Verify the demand hypotheses and oracle access

Fix a type \(t\), and abbreviate its mass by \(\mu\).

Nonemptiness. Since \(D_t(q,B/\mu)\) is nonempty, so is \(\widetilde D_t(q,B)\).

Scale invariance. For every \(\alpha>0\),

\( \widetilde D_t(\alpha q,\alpha B) =\mu D_t(\alpha q,\alpha B/\mu) =\mu D_t(q,B/\mu) =\widetilde D_t(q,B). \)

The middle equality uses precisely the given simultaneous scaling of prices and budget.

Full expenditure. If \(Y\in\widetilde D_t(q,B)\), then \(y:=Y/\mu\) belongs to \(D_t(q,B/\mu)\). Thus

\( q^\top Y=\mu q^\top y=\mu(B/\mu)=B. \)

WGS. Suppose \(Y\in\widetilde D_t(q,B)\), \(q'\ge q\), and \(B'\ge B\). Then \(y:=Y/\mu\in D_t(q,B/\mu)\), and \(B'/\mu\ge B/\mu\). The original WGS oracle supplies

\( y'\in D_t(q',B'/\mu),\qquad y'_j\ge y_j\ \text{whenever }q'_j=q_j. \)

Setting \(Y':=\mu y'\) gives

\( Y'\in\widetilde D_t(q',B'),\qquad Y'_j\ge Y_j\ \text{whenever }q'_j=q_j. \)

Elasticity. Suppose only coordinate \(j\)’s price increases by a factor \(a\ge1\), with aggregate budget unchanged. The per-member budget \(B/\mu\) is also unchanged. Applying the elasticity oracle to \(y=Y/\mu\) gives a WGS witness satisfying \(y'_j\ge y_j/a^f\). Hence its aggregate counterpart satisfies

\( Y'_j=\mu y'_j\ge Y_j/a^f. \)

Thus the same elasticity bound is preserved.

Each aggregate oracle call uses one original oracle call: divide the supplied budget and old demand vector by \(\mu\), query the original oracle, and multiply the returned vector by \(\mu\). Its cost is therefore

\( \widetilde T_D=T_D+O(m). \)

Zero budgets cause no difficulty: positive prices and full expenditure imply that both original and aggregate demand at budget zero consist only of the zero vector.

3. Apply the finite-agent algorithm

The finite-agent theorem supplied in the question applies to scale-invariant, fully spending WGS demand systems with the stated elasticity witnesses. With \(n\) agents, it computes the specified approximate equilibrium in time

\( O\!\left(nm^2fT_D\epsilon^{-2}\log(p_{\max}/p_{\min})\right). \)

Its approximation definition agrees with the three allocation conditions in this problem. See Definition 2.2 and Theorem 3.3 of [Garg–Husić–Végh](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.07948).

All demand hypotheses have just been verified for the aggregate market. Moreover, the correspondence in Section 4 below preserves feasible approximate-equilibrium prices in both directions. Thus the supplied price-ratio bound \(R\) transfers unchanged.

The required dominating demand witnesses can also be retained explicitly. Store the initial demands and subsequent oracle outputs. Removing allocated goods preserves domination. At a market-price increase, an individual price either stays unchanged or increases in a coordinate where that agent’s allocation is zero; budgets only increase. A WGS call therefore restores a stored dominating demand. This costs at most one additional call per agent per market-price increase and is absorbed in the running-time bound.

4. Translate the output and verify every condition

Let the aggregate algorithm produce market prices \(P>0\), individual prices \(Q_t\), allocated bundles \(X_t\), and stored demand witnesses \(Z_t\), satisfying

\( P\le Q_t\le(1+\epsilon)P,\qquad Z_t\in\widetilde D_t(Q_t,P^\top E_t),\qquad 0\le X_t\le Z_t, \)

\( \sum_tX_t\le\bar e,\qquad P^\top\left(\bar e-\sum_tX_t\right)\le\epsilon P^\top\bar e. \)

Define per-member bundles by

\( x_t:=X_t/\mu_t,\qquad z_t:=Z_t/\mu_t. \)

The definition of aggregate demand gives

\( z_t\in D_t\!\left(Q_t,\frac{P^\top E_t}{\mu_t}\right) =D_t(Q_t,P^\top e_t). \)

Since each mass is positive, division preserves componentwise inequalities, so \(0\le x_t\le z_t\). Furthermore,

\( \sum_t\mu_tx_t=\sum_tX_t\le\bar e, \)

and

\( P^\top\left(\bar e-\sum_t\mu_tx_t\right) =P^\top\left(\bar e-\sum_tX_t\right) \le\epsilon P^\top\bar e. \)

For normalization, set

\( s:=\min_jP_j>0,\qquad p:=P/s,\qquad \hat p_t:=Q_t/s. \)

Then \(\min_jp_j=1\) and \(p\le\hat p_t\le(1+\epsilon)p\). Scale invariance gives

\( D_t(Q_t,P^\top e_t)=D_t(Q_t/s,(P^\top e_t)/s) =D_t(\hat p_t,p^\top e_t). \)

Dividing the expenditure inequality by \(s\) establishes the requested inequality at prices \(p\).

Conversely, any requested solution becomes an aggregate solution by setting \(X_t=\mu_tx_t\) and \(Z_t=\mu_tz_t\). Therefore the reduction preserves the approximation accuracy and the feasible price vectors exactly.

5. Arithmetic and input-size accounting

Computing the rational endowments \(E_t=\mu_te_t\) takes polynomial time in the binary input length. Product encodings have polynomial length. The construction introduces neither a common population denominator nor additional agents. Dividing the final bundles by their masses takes \(O(\tau m)\) exact arithmetic operations.

For completeness, fractional powers in the bounded-elasticity price-update implementation can be avoided. Let \(\delta=\epsilon/4\), let \(F=\max\{1,\lceil f\rceil\}\), and use the rational multiplier

\( a:=1+\delta/(2F). \)

Writing \(u=\delta/(2F)\), the binomial theorem and \(\binom{F}{k}\le F^k\) give

\( a^f\le a^F =\sum_{k=0}^{F}\binom{F}{k}u^k \le\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(Fu)^k =\frac{1}{1-\delta/2} \le1+\delta. \)

The final inequality follows from \(\delta\le1\). Thus increasing a coordinate by at most \(a\) reduces its demand by at most the allowed factor \(1+\delta\). A coordinate whose current demand exceeds \((1+\delta)\) times its allocation still dominates that allocation after the update; unchanged coordinates retain domination by WGS.

Clamp updates at the upper individual-price bound. Each coordinate requires \(O(F)\) updates, because

\( \log(1+\delta)\le\delta,\qquad \log a\ge\delta/(4F). \)

Hence this implementation uses only rational multipliers, exact arithmetic, comparisons, and the supplied witness oracles, with the same asymptotic dependence after replacing \(f\) by \(F=O(1+f)\).

Substituting \(n=\tau\) and \(\widetilde T_D=T_D+O(m)\) proves the claimed polynomial bound. In particular, there is no dependence on the numerical value of \(1/\min_t\mu_t\).
