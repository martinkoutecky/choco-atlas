The continuous mirror question, as graded

The guarantee is relative to a hidden joint law \(P\) over latent and observed rankings whose observed marginal is \(\widehat{\mu}\), whose latent marginal is \(\mu\), and which satisfies the paper’s \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\)-smoothness promise and \(\ell=|M\cap G_2|\): require \(F_\mu(S)\geq(\beta-\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}_\mu\), where \(F_\mu(S)=\mathbb{E}_{\succ\sim\mu}[f(S,\succ)]\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu=\max_{|S'|=k}F_\mu(S')\).

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here types are observed rankings, masses sum to \(1\), and \(\tau\) is support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The input includes an exact polynomial-time rational evaluator for \(f(S,r)\geq0\), with \(f(\varnothing,r)=0\). It depends only on the positions occupied by \(S\) in \(r\), and is monotone submodular. If the sorted positions of equal-size committees \(A,B\) satisfy coordinatewise dominance of \(A\) over \(B\), then, for every \(T\subseteq C\setminus(A\cup B)\), require \(f(A,r)-f(B,r)\geq f(A\cup T,r)-f(B\cup T,r)\geq0\).
For a ranking distribution \(\nu\), write \(F_\nu(S)=\mathbb E_\nu[f(S,r)]\), \(a_\nu(S,c)=F_\nu(S\cup\{c\})-F_\nu(S)\), and \(B_f=\max_{r,c}f(\{c\},r)>0\). Fix a public candidate order to break all ties; let \(M\) be the top \(k\) candidates by \(F_\mu(\{c\})\), and \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu=\max_{|A|=k}F_\mu(A)\).
Only the observed distribution \(\widehat\mu\) is supplied. A hidden joint law \(P\) has marginals \(\mu,\widehat\mu\); supplied rational parameters satisfy \(0<\alpha\leq1\), \(0<\varepsilon<\beta\leq1\), \(0\leq\gamma<1\), and \(\ell=|M\cap G_2|\). Require \(a_\mu(C\setminus\{c\},c)>0\) for every \(c\), and \(\min_{c\in M}a_\nu(C\setminus\{c\},c)\geq\alpha B_f\) for both \(\nu\in\{\mu,\widehat\mu\}\).
Latent order preservation means: for all distinct \(c,d\), if \(F_\mu(\{c\})\leq F_\mu(\{d\})\), then \(a_\mu(S,c)\leq a_\mu(S,d)\) for every \(S\subseteq C\setminus\{c,d\}\); for such pairs in the same group, also require \(a_\mu(S,d)-a_\mu(S,c)\leq a_\mu(T,d)-a_\mu(T,c)\) whenever \(T\subseteq S\).
Bias order preservation means: for distinct same-group \(c,d\) and every \(S\subseteq C\setminus\{c,d\}\), \(0<a_\mu(S,c)\leq\beta a_\mu(S,d)\) implies \(a_{\widehat\mu}(S,c)\leq\gamma a_{\widehat\mu}(S,d)\).

Resolution

Yes. A deterministic algorithm using only the supplied data returns an integral committee \(S\) satisfying
\( |S|=k \), \( |S\cap G_2|=\ell \), and the stronger guarantee
\( F_\mu(S)\geq\beta\,\mathrm{OPT}_\mu \)
simultaneously for every compatible hidden law. Its running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k\), under the stipulated polynomial-time rational evaluator.

The algorithm uses the two-phase greedy selection appearing in [Algorithm 1 of the cited paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/boehmer23a/boehmer23a.pdf). Below is a self-contained deterministic proof for the exact-expectation input in this question.

1. Algorithm and notation

Write the supplied observed distribution as rankings \(r_1,\ldots,r_\tau\) with rational masses \(p_1,\ldots,p_\tau\). Compute observed expectations exactly:
\( H(U):=F_{\widehat\mu}(U)=\sum_{h=1}^{\tau}p_h f(U,r_h) \).

Starting with \(U=\varnothing\), perform:

  • For \(\ell\) steps, add a candidate \(c\in G_2\setminus U\) maximizing \(H(U\cup\{c\})-H(U)\).
  • For \(k-\ell\) steps, add a candidate \(c\in G_1\setminus U\) maximizing the same quantity.
  • Return \(S=U\).

Use the public order for every tie. The feasibility assumptions ensure that both phases can be completed. Thus \( |S|=k \) and \( |S\cap G_2|=\ell \).

For the proof, fix an arbitrary compatible hidden law and abbreviate
\( F=F_\mu \), \( a(U,c)=a_\mu(U,c) \), and \( b(U,c)=a_{\widehat\mu}(U,c) \).
Both \(F\) and \(H\) are normalized, monotone, submodular functions, because these properties are preserved by nonnegative weighted averages.

2. The latent singleton optimum is a committee optimum

Lemma 1. \( F(M)=\mathrm{OPT}_\mu \).

Proof. Let \(A\) be any size-\(k\) committee different from \(M\). Choose \(c\in A\setminus M\) and \(d\in M\setminus A\). By the definition of \(M\),
\( F(\{c\})\leq F(\{d\}) \).

Set \(W=A\setminus\{c\}\). The global latent order-preservation assumption applies because \(W\subseteq C\setminus\{c,d\}\), giving
\( a(W,c)\leq a(W,d) \).
Consequently,
\( F(A)\leq F((A\setminus\{c\})\cup\{d\}) \).

This replacement increases the intersection with \(M\) by one. Repeating it reaches \(M\) without decreasing the value. Thus \(F(A)\leq F(M)\) for every size-\(k\) committee \(A\). Since \(M\) itself has size \(k\), the lemma follows. ∎

3. Improving a context decreases another candidate’s marginal

The second latent order-preservation condition has a useful consequence.

Lemma 2. Suppose \(u,v\) are distinct candidates in the same group with \(F(\{u\})\leq F(\{v\})\). Let \(t\) be a third candidate and \(W\subseteq C\setminus\{u,v,t\}\). Then
\( a(W\cup\{u\},t)\geq a(W\cup\{v\},t) \).

Proof. Apply the marginal-gap condition to the pair \(u,v\), with smaller context \(W\) and larger context \(W\cup\{t\}\):
\( a(W\cup\{t\},v)-a(W\cup\{t\},u)\leq a(W,v)-a(W,u) \).

Expanding the marginals gives the identity
\( a(W\cup\{u\},t)-a(W\cup\{v\},t) =[a(W,v)-a(W,u)]-[a(W\cup\{t\},v)-a(W\cup\{t\},u)] \).
The right-hand side is nonnegative by the preceding inequality. ∎

We will use this lemma repeatedly. In particular, suppose a context excluding \(t\) contains some number of candidates from a group. Replace those candidates by the same number of highest-ranked candidates of that group excluding \(t\), where ranking means decreasing latent singleton value, with public tie-breaking. Every candidate entering the context has singleton value at least that of every candidate leaving it. Pair the entering and leaving candidates arbitrarily and apply Lemma 2 to each replacement. The marginal of \(t\) cannot increase.

This reasoning also applies successively to replacements in both groups. Other context candidates remain fixed during each replacement.

4. Observed greedy choices retain a latent factor of \(\beta\)

Lemma 3. At a greedy step with current committee \(U\), let \(s\) be the selected candidate. If \(t\in M\setminus U\) belongs to the group being selected from, then
\( a(U,s)\geq\beta\,a(U,t) \).

Proof. Submodularity and the strict full-context positivity assumption imply, for every \(c\notin U\),
\( a(U,c)\geq a(C\setminus\{c\},c)>0 \).

Similarly, because \(t\in M\), observed submodularity and the supplied lower bound imply
\( b(U,t)\geq b(C\setminus\{t\},t)\geq\alpha B_f>0 \).

If \(s=t\), the conclusion follows from \(\beta\leq1\). Otherwise, suppose for contradiction that
\( a(U,s)\leq\beta\,a(U,t) \).
The candidates are distinct and in the same group, so bias order preservation gives
\( b(U,s)\leq\gamma b(U,t)<b(U,t) \).
The strict inequality uses \(\gamma<1\) and \(b(U,t)>0\). This contradicts the observed greedy choice of \(s\). ∎

5. Comparing the greedy increments with the increments of \(M\)

Order each group by decreasing latent singleton value, breaking ties publicly. Write
\( M\cap G_2=\{y_1,\ldots,y_\ell\} \)
and
\( M\cap G_1=\{x_1,\ldots,x_{k-\ell}\} \)
in those orders. These are the corresponding group prefixes because \(M\) is a prefix of the global order.

Define
\( Y_j=\{y_1,\ldots,y_j\} \),
\( X_i=\{x_1,\ldots,x_i\} \),
and \(Y_0=X_0=\varnothing\).

Let the algorithm’s selections be \(b_1,\ldots,b_\ell\) in the first phase and \(a_1,\ldots,a_{k-\ell}\) in the second phase. Put
\( B_j=\{b_1,\ldots,b_j\} \),
\( A_i=\{a_1,\ldots,a_i\} \),
and \(B_0=A_0=\varnothing\).

5.1. First phase

Fix \(1\leq j\leq\ell\). Let \(t\) be the highest-ranked candidate in \(G_2\setminus B_{j-1}\). Since \(B_{j-1}\) has only \(j-1\) elements, \(t\in Y_j\subseteq M\).

The set \(V=Y_j\setminus\{t\}\) consists of the highest-ranked \(j-1\) candidates of \(G_2\setminus\{t\}\). By the repeated-replacement consequence of Lemma 2,
\( a(B_{j-1},t)\geq a(V,t) \).

We also have \(F(V)\leq F(Y_{j-1})\). If \(t=y_j\), the sets are equal. Otherwise, \(V\) is obtained from \(Y_{j-1}\) by replacing \(t\) with \(y_j\); latent order preservation shows that this replacement cannot increase the value, since \(t\) precedes \(y_j\).

Therefore,
\( a(V,t)=F(Y_j)-F(V)\geq F(Y_j)-F(Y_{j-1})=a(Y_{j-1},y_j) \).

Applying Lemma 3 at this step yields
\( a(B_{j-1},b_j)\geq\beta\,a(B_{j-1},t)\geq\beta\,a(Y_{j-1},y_j) \).

5.2. Second phase

Fix \(1\leq i\leq k-\ell\). The current committee is \(U=B_\ell\cup A_{i-1}\). Let \(t\) be the highest-ranked candidate in \(G_1\setminus A_{i-1}\). Again, \(t\in X_i\subseteq M\).

Define
\( V=Y_\ell\cup(X_i\setminus\{t\}) \)
and
\( R=Y_\ell\cup X_{i-1} \).

Replace \(B_\ell\) by \(Y_\ell\), and then replace \(A_{i-1}\) by \(X_i\setminus\{t\}\). Each replacement occurs within one group, replaces candidates by candidates of at least as high singleton value, and excludes \(t\). Lemma 2 therefore gives
\( a(U,t)\geq a(V,t) \).

Moreover, \(F(V)\leq F(R)\). If \(t=x_i\), the sets coincide. Otherwise, \(V\) is obtained from \(R\) by replacing the better candidate \(t\) with \(x_i\), so latent order preservation gives the inequality.

Since \(V\cup\{t\}=Y_\ell\cup X_i\), it follows that
\( a(V,t)=F(Y_\ell\cup X_i)-F(V)\geq F(Y_\ell\cup X_i)-F(R)=a(R,x_i) \).

Lemma 3 now yields
\( a(B_\ell\cup A_{i-1},a_i)\geq\beta\,a(Y_\ell\cup X_{i-1},x_i) \).

6. Approximation and the simultaneous guarantee

Summing the inequalities from both phases and telescoping gives
\( F(S)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a(B_{j-1},b_j)+\sum_{i=1}^{k-\ell}a(B_\ell\cup A_{i-1},a_i) \)

\( \displaystyle \geq\beta\left[\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a(Y_{j-1},y_j)+\sum_{i=1}^{k-\ell}a(Y_\ell\cup X_{i-1},x_i)\right]=\beta F(M)=\beta\,\mathrm{OPT}_\mu. \)

Empty sums handle either phase having zero steps, including empty groups. Since \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu\geq0\), the requested factor \(\beta-\varepsilon\) follows.

The algorithm accesses only \(H\), the groups, the supplied counts, and the public tie-breaking order. All latent candidates and orderings introduced above are proof devices. The compatible hidden law was arbitrary, whereas the deterministic output depends only on the supplied data. Thus the same returned committee satisfies the guarantee for every compatible hidden law.

7. Running time

There are \(k\) selection steps. Each step requires at most \(m+1\) evaluations of \(H\), including the current committee value, so there are \(O(mk)\) observed-score evaluations.

Each evaluation of \(H\) uses \(\tau\) evaluations of the supplied rational evaluator and an exact weighted sum. Under the stipulated polynomial-time evaluator, its rational outputs have polynomial bit length. Products, sums, and comparisons consequently have polynomial bit complexity in \(m,\tau,L\).

The total running time is therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k\), which is stronger than the requested dependence on \(\alpha^{-1},\varepsilon^{-1},(1-\gamma)^{-1}\). No reconstruction of the hidden law or verification of its promised properties is needed.
