Theorem 4.5 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational role shares \(\mu\), a nonnegative rational piecewise-linear worth function \(V\) on \([0,\mu]\) with \(V(0)=0\) that vanishes near the origin, and a rational payoff-rate vector \(p\), determine whether there is an unbounded sequence \(q_j\) with \(q_j\mu_t\) integral for every role \(t\), and grand-coalition kernel allocations \(x^{(q_j)}\) of the corresponding replicated games, such that \(q_j x_i^{(q_j)}\to p_t\) for every player \(i\) of role \(t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) roles with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also contains \(p\in\mathbb Q^\tau\) and a continuous nonnegative piecewise-linear function \(V:[0,\mu]\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}\), where \([0,\mu]=\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\). Encode \(V\) by an explicit rational polyhedral subdivision with \(M\) cells and rational affine formulas agreeing on overlaps. Promise that \(V(0)=0\) and \(V\) vanishes on a relative neighborhood of the origin. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(\tau,M,L\).
For each positive integer \(q\) with \(q\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\) for every role, let \(N_q\) contain \(q\mu_t\) players of role \(t\), denoted \(N_{q,t}\). Define \(v_q(S)=V((|S\cap N_{q,t}|/q)_{t=1}^{\tau})\). Consider only sufficiently large \(q\) that every singleton has worth zero; no superadditivity is assumed.
A grand-coalition allocation is \(x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{N_q}\) with \(\sum_i x_i=V(\mu)\). Set \(e_q(S,x)=v_q(S)-\sum_{i\in S}x_i\) and \(s_{ik}^{(q)}(x)=\max_{S\subseteq N_q:\ i\in S,\ k\notin S}e_q(S,x)\). It belongs to the kernel exactly when \((s_{ik}^{(q)}(x)-s_{ki}^{(q)}(x))x_k\leq0\) for every ordered pair of distinct players.
Replica-limit membership asks whether there exist admissible \(q_j\to\infty\) and kernel allocations \(x^{(q_j)}\) such that \(\max_{1\leq t\leq\tau}\max_{i\in N_{q_j,t}}|q_jx_i^{(q_j)}-p_t|\to0\).

Is this replica-limit membership problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite nonnegative games with zero singleton worths, the kernel has an exact representation as a finite union of closed convex polyhedra. [Maschler–Peleg](https://msp.org/pjm/1966/18-2/pjm-v18-n2-p10-p.pdf).
For continuous superadditive games on countably many players, the prekernel intersects the least core; this concerns a different infinite-player model. [Einy–Monderer–Moreno](https://economia.uc3m.es/dmoreno/Eny-Monderer-MorenoET1998.pdf). No result settling the replica-limit membership problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive role shares to sum to one, treating them as a distribution over the listed support.
  • Specified the piecewise-linear function as continuous and explicitly encoded by rational polyhedral cells and affine formulas; introduced the cell count M and total input bit length L.
  • Defined replication using integer q, q times each role's mass players, and coalition worth V evaluated at coalition role counts divided by q, without scaling total worth.
  • Restricted to the sufficiently large replica sizes with zero singleton worths, preserving the finite-game normalization; the vanishing-near-origin promise guarantees this restriction removes only finitely many sizes.
  • Interpreted convergence across changing player sets as uniform convergence of scaled payoffs over all players in each replica.
  • Allowed arbitrary rational proposed payoff rates; imposed nonnegativity and efficiency on the kernel allocations rather than adding promises on p.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result

The replica-limit membership problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\) under polynomial-time many-one reductions. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm for this problem would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

We prove this by reducing from \(\mathrm{3SAT}\), whose \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness is the only complexity-theoretic hardness result used. See the [standard theorem and proof](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~fp/courses/flac/lectures/lecture33.html).

2. Exact equal treatment within roles

We first establish a property needed to exclude arbitrary, potentially nonsymmetric, sequences of kernel allocations.

Lemma. In every replica, every kernel allocation pays all players of the same role equally.

Proof. Let \(i,k\) have the same role. Swapping these players maps coalitions containing \(i\) and excluding \(k\) bijectively onto coalitions containing \(k\) and excluding \(i\), without changing coalition worth. For corresponding coalitions \(S,S'\),

\(e_q(S',x)=e_q(S,x)+x_i-x_k.\)

Taking maxima gives

\(s_{ki}^{(q)}(x)=s_{ik}^{(q)}(x)+x_i-x_k.\)

If \(x_i>x_k\), then \(x_i>0\), and the kernel inequality for the ordered pair \((k,i)\) fails:

\((s_{ki}^{(q)}(x)-s_{ik}^{(q)}(x))x_i=(x_i-x_k)x_i>0.\)

Interchanging \(i,k\) excludes the opposite strict inequality. Thus \(x_i=x_k\). \(\square\)

Accordingly, write a kernel allocation as \(x_i=a_t/q\) for players of role \(t\). Its scaled role-payoff vector satisfies \(a\geq0\), and the convergence required in the question becomes exactly \(a^{(q_j)}\to p\).

3. Construction of the society and its worth function

Let \(\Phi\) be a formula with \(n\) variables and \(m\) clauses, each having three literal occurrences. Pad with unused variables until the number of variables is a power of two \(N\geq\max\{8,n\}\). Padding preserves satisfiability, and \(N\) is linear in \(n+1\).

Create roles \(A,B,X_1,\ldots,X_N\), so \(\tau=N+2\). Set

\(\mu_A=\mu_B=\frac14,\qquad \mu_{X_i}=\frac1{2N},\)

and

\(p_A=p_B=1,\qquad p_{X_i}=0.\)

All masses are positive dyadic rationals, and their sum is one. The admissible replica sizes are exactly the positive multiples of \(2N\).

For a coalition profile \(z\in[0,\mu]\), define

\(d=\frac14-z_A,\qquad e=\frac14-z_B,\qquad b_i=z_{X_i}.\)

Thus \(d,e\) measure missing mass from roles \(A,B\), whereas \(b_i\) measures included mass of role \(X_i\). All these coordinates are nonnegative.

For each clause \(C\), define the linear form

\(w_C(d,b)=d-\sum_{X_i\text{ occurs positively in }C}b_i-\sum_{X_i\text{ occurs negatively in }C}(d-b_i).\)

Both sums count literal occurrences. Define

\(W(d,b)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{b_i-d:1\leq i\leq N\}\cup\{w_C(d,b):C\in\Phi\}\bigr),\)

and

\(g(d,e,b)=d+e+W(d,b).\)

In particular, \(W\geq0\), \(g\geq d+e\), and these functions are positively homogeneous.

Set

\(H=W\left(0,\left(\frac1{2N}\right)_{i=1}^N\right).\)

The forms \(b_i-d\) show that \(H\geq1/(2N)\). Each clause form at this point is at most \(3/(2N)\). Hence

\(0<\frac1{2N}\leq H\leq\frac3{2N}\leq\frac3{16}.\)

Finally, define

\(V(z)=\max\{0,\ z_A+z_B+H-g(d,e,b)\}.\)

Verification of the promises

The function \(V\) is continuous, nonnegative, and piecewise linear.

At the grand-coalition profile, \(d=e=0\) and \(b_i=1/(2N)\), so

\(V(\mu)=\frac12+H-H=\frac12=\sum_t\mu_tp_t.\)

Thus the supplied vector \(p\) is nonnegative and efficient.

At the origin, \(d=e=1/4\) and \(b=0\). Therefore the expression inside the outer maximum is

\(H-\frac12-W\left(\frac14,0\right)\leq H-\frac12<0.\)

Continuity makes this expression negative on a relative neighborhood of the origin. Consequently, \(V\) vanishes there, including at the origin. All sufficiently large replicas therefore have zero singleton worths.

Explicit subdivision and encoding size

The definition of \(W\) uses \(K=N+m+1\) linear forms; denote them by \(w_1,\ldots,w_K\), including the zero form. For each \(j\), let

\(R_j=\{z\in[0,\mu]:w_j(d,b)\geq w_k(d,b)\text{ for every }k\},\)

and define the affine formula

\(A_j(z)=z_A+z_B+H-d-e-w_j(d,b).\)

Supply the cells

\(R_j^+=R_j\cap\{A_j\geq0\},\qquad R_j^-=R_j\cap\{A_j\leq0\},\)

with formulas \(A_j\) and \(0\), respectively. Empty pieces and repetitions may be omitted.

These cells cover the box. They meet face to face: on \(R_j\cap R_k\), the supporting equality \(w_j=w_k\) implies \(A_j=A_k\); intersections of pieces with opposite signs additionally satisfy \(A_j=A_k=0\). Thus the supplied formulas agree on every overlap.

There are at most \(M=2K\) listed cells. Each uses at most \(K+2\tau+1\) inequalities, and coefficient bit lengths are \(O(\log\tau)\). A dense encoding therefore has

\(L=O(K^2\tau\log\tau).\)

Construction, and any feasibility checks used to omit empty cells, require polynomial time. Specifically, rational linear-program feasibility is polynomial in the number of variables, constraints, and coefficient bit length; here these are \(\tau\), \(O(K+\tau)\), and \(O(\log\tau)\). The resulting reduction runs in \((\tau+M+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations, and its output length is polynomial in the source formula length.

4. The integer gap encoding satisfiability

For any nonnegative integer vector \(B\),

\(W(1,B)=0\)

holds exactly when \(B\in\{0,1\}^N\) is a satisfying assignment.

Indeed, the inequalities \(B_i-1\leq0\) force every coordinate to be Boolean. At Boolean coordinates, \(w_C(1,B)\) is one minus the number of satisfied literal occurrences in \(C\). Hence every clause inequality is nonpositive exactly when every clause is satisfied.

It follows that:

  • If \(\Phi\) is satisfiable, some \(\beta\in\{0,1\}^N\) satisfies \(W(1,\beta)=0\).
  • If \(\Phi\) is unsatisfiable, then \(W(1,B)\geq1\) for every nonnegative integer vector \(B\), because \(W(1,B)\) is a nonnegative integer.

This integer gap is what distinguishes the two cases.

5. Satisfiable formulas give replica-limit membership

Suppose \(\Phi\) is satisfiable, and choose \(\beta\in\{0,1\}^N\) with \(W(1,\beta)=0\).

For every sufficiently large admissible \(q\), set \(h=1/q\) and allocate

\(x_i=\frac1q\text{ on roles }A,B,\qquad x_i=0\text{ on all roles }X_i.\)

This allocation is nonnegative and efficient. Its scaled payoff vector is exactly \(p\).

For a profile where the expression defining \(V\) is nonnegative, its excess is

\(V(z)-p\cdot z=H-g(d,e,b).\)

Where that expression is nonpositive, its excess is \(-p\cdot z\leq0\). Choose \(q\) large enough that \(h<H\).

The pair of positive-payoff roles

A coalition containing a specified \(A\)-player and excluding a specified \(B\)-player has \(e\geq h\). Since \(g\geq e\), its excess is at most \(H-h\). The profile

\(d=0,\qquad e=h,\qquad b=0\)

attains this bound, because \(W(0,0)=0\). Thus

\(s_{AB}^{(q)}(x)=H-h.\)

In the reverse direction, excluding a specified \(A\)-player requires \(d\geq h\), giving the same upper bound. The profile

\(d=h,\qquad e=0,\qquad b=h\beta\)

attains it, since positive homogeneity gives \(W(h,h\beta)=0\). These coordinates lie on the coalition-count grid and inside the box for sufficiently large \(q\). Hence

\(s_{BA}^{(q)}(x)=H-h.\)

Thus the two positive-payoff roles are balanced.

Pairs involving a zero-payoff role

Let

\(z^\star=\left(\frac14,\frac14,0,\ldots,0\right).\)

Its excess is \(H\), the maximum possible excess. It includes every \(A\)- and \(B\)-player and excludes every \(X_i\)-player. Consequently,

\(s_{A,X_i}^{(q)}(x)=s_{B,X_i}^{(q)}(x)=H.\)

Conversely, a coalition including an \(X_i\)-player and excluding an \(A\)-player has \(d\geq h\), so its excess is at most \(H-h\). Excluding a \(B\)-player instead gives the same bound using \(e\geq h\). Therefore

\(s_{X_i,A}^{(q)}(x)\leq H-h,\qquad s_{X_i,B}^{(q)}(x)\leq H-h.\)

These are precisely the inequalities needed when the other player receives positive payoff. The reverse ordered inequalities have a zero payoff multiplier.

Pairs of zero-payoff players also have zero multipliers. Within each role, the equal allocation makes opposite surpluses equal by swapping the players.

All kernel inequalities therefore hold. Taking any unbounded sequence of sufficiently large admissible replicas proves membership.

6. Unsatisfiable formulas exclude every converging kernel sequence

Suppose now that \(\Phi\) is unsatisfiable. Assume, for contradiction, that there are admissible \(q_j\to\infty\) and kernel allocations whose scaled payoffs converge to \(p\).

By the equal-treatment lemma, write their scaled role payoffs as \(a\), with

\(a_A\to1,\qquad a_B\to1,\qquad a_{X_i}\to0,\qquad a\geq0.\)

For each such replica, define

\(\alpha_A=2-a_A,\qquad\alpha_B=2-a_B,\qquad C_a=H+\frac{2-a_A-a_B}{4}.\)

Eventually,

\(\frac34\leq\alpha_A,\alpha_B\leq\frac54,\qquad C_a\to H>0.\)

Where the expression defining \(V\) is nonnegative, direct subtraction of coalition payoffs gives

\(V(z)-a\cdot z=C_a-\ell_a(d,e,b),\)

where

\(\ell_a(d,e,b)=\alpha_A d+\alpha_B e+\sum_i a_{X_i}b_i+W(d,b).\)

Every term in this loss is nonnegative. Elsewhere, excess is \(-a\cdot z\leq0\).

Surplus from \(A\) against \(B\)

Excluding a specified \(B\)-player requires \(e\geq h\), so active excess is at most \(C_a-\alpha_Bh\). The profile \(d=0,e=h,b=0\) attains this value.

For all sufficiently large \(j\), this value is positive, because \(C_a\to H>0\) and \(h\to0\). Thus inactive profiles cannot improve it, and

\(s_{AB}^{(q)}(x)=C_a-\alpha_Bh.\)

Surplus from \(B\) against \(A\)

Excluding a specified \(A\)-player requires \(d\geq h\). On the coalition-count grid,

\(D=qd,\qquad E=qe,\qquad B_i=qb_i\)

are nonnegative integers, with \(D\geq1\). Positive homogeneity gives

\(q\ell_a(d,e,b)=\alpha_AD+\alpha_BE+\sum_i a_{X_i}B_i+W(D,B).\)

There are two cases.

If \(D=1\), unsatisfiability and the integer-gap result imply

\(q\ell_a(d,e,b)\geq\alpha_A+1\geq\frac74.\)

If \(D\geq2\), nonnegativity of every other term implies

\(q\ell_a(d,e,b)\geq2\alpha_A\geq\frac32.\)

Therefore every active coalition in this direction has excess at most \(C_a-\frac32h\). Including inactive profiles yields

\(s_{BA}^{(q)}(x)\leq\max\left\{0,C_a-\frac32h\right\}.\)

But

\(s_{AB}^{(q)}(x)=C_a-\alpha_Bh\geq C_a-\frac54h.\)

For sufficiently large \(j\), this is positive and strictly exceeds \(C_a-\frac32h\). Consequently,

\(s_{AB}^{(q)}(x)>s_{BA}^{(q)}(x).\)

Also \(a_B\to1\), so every \(B\)-player has positive payoff eventually. The kernel inequality for the ordered pair consisting of an \(A\)-player and a \(B\)-player is therefore violated. This contradiction excludes every proposed converging kernel sequence.

Notice that the proof permits arbitrary small positive payoffs to the variable roles: their contributions to \(\ell_a\) are nonnegative and cannot remove the gap.

7. Complexity conclusion

The polynomial construction satisfies

\(\Phi\text{ is satisfiable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\text{ belongs to the replica-limit kernel}.\)

Both directions hold for the exact finite-replica kernel conditions, and the negative direction rules out all converging sequences of kernel allocations. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for the stated input model.
