The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_\tau\}\), masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), entitlement weights \(w_t>0\), a finite set of indivisible good classes \(G\) with per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and a matroid \(M_t=(G,\mathcal{I}_t)\) for each type, compute masses \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\) of type \(t\) receiving each integral independent bundle \(B\in\mathcal{I}_t\), subject to \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:g\in B}x_{t,B}\le q_g\), maximizing \(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}w_tf(r_t(B))\). When \(f(0)=-\infty\), first maximize positive mass, then apply a fixed type-order refinement; otherwise apply the refinement after weighted-welfare maximization.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: an ordered list \(T=(t_1,\ldots,t_\tau)\) of types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\) good classes \(G\), nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and positive binary-rational entitlement weights \(w_t\). Each type has a matroid \(M_t=(G,\mathcal I_t)\), accessible through a unit-cost rank oracle \(r_t\); a bundle contains at most one good of each class.
The common welfare function is a strictly increasing, discretely concave table \(f(0),\ldots,f(m)\). Its finite entries are binary rationals of either sign; only \(f(0)\) may equal \(-\infty\). Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the numerical input.
An allocation assigns nonnegative masses \(x_{t,B}\) to independent bundles \(B\in\mathcal I_t\), subject to \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:g\in B}x_{t,B}\le q_g\). Supplies may remain unused. Compute an exact allocation represented by its nonzero bundle masses.
Write \(u_t=\sum_Bx_{t,B}r_t(B)\) and \(p_t=\sum_{B\ne\emptyset}x_{t,B}\). If \(f(0)\) is finite, maximize \(W=\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}w_tf(r_t(B))\), then lexicographically maximize \((u_{t_1},\ldots,u_{t_\tau})\).
If \(f(0)=-\infty\), successively maximize \(\sum_tp_t\), lexicographically maximize \((p_{t_1},\ldots,p_{t_\tau})\), maximize \(W_+=\sum_{t,B\ne\emptyset}x_{t,B}w_tf(r_t(B))\), and lexicographically maximize \((u_{t_1},\ldots,u_{t_\tau})\). Every stage preserves all preceding optima; remaining ties are arbitrary.

Is computing this refined mass allocation solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), with polynomially many rank-oracle queries, or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many individual agents, the corresponding concave weighted-welfare rule, including positive-agent priority, utility tie-breaking, and nonredundancy, is computable in polynomial time with valuation-oracle access ([discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.14454v2)).
No result settling this exact mass problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Removed zero-mass types so the supplied type list is exactly the positive-mass support; retained total mass one.
  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational supplies and positive binary-rational weights, with no normalization of weights.
  • Used rank-oracle access to matroids, matching the discrete valuation-oracle model.
  • Restored the anchor's strictly increasing, discretely concave welfare-function domain. Supplied the relevant finite values as a rational table to make exact bit complexity well-defined; finite values may be negative.
  • Interpreted each good class as a matroid ground-set element, allowing at most one unit of that class per individual bundle and allowing unused supply.
  • Interpreted type-order utility refinement as lexicographic maximization of aggregate type utilities; positive-recipient priority becomes lexicographic maximization of positive mass by type.
  • Restored weighted welfare among positive recipients after positive-mass priority and before the final utility refinement when the zero-utility welfare value is negative infinity.
  • Specified an exact allocation through its nonzero bundle masses, avoiding a requirement to enumerate every independent bundle.

Result

The refined mass allocation is computable exactly in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), with polynomially many rank-oracle queries. The algorithm outputs at most \(\tau(2m+2)\) nonzero bundle masses.

The proof uses a polynomial-dimensional polytope that records both good-use marginals and bundle-size distributions. In fact, concavity and monotonicity of the finite welfare entries are unnecessary for this algorithm.

1. A polytope for each type

If \(m=0\), the unique allocation gives every type the empty bundle. Hence assume \(m\geq 1\).

For \(B\subseteq G\), let \(\chi_B\in\{0,1\}^m\) be its incidence vector. For \(k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\), let \(e_k\in\{0,1\}^{m+1}\) be the corresponding unit vector. Define

\(P_t=\operatorname{conv}\{(\chi_B,e_{|B|}):B\in\mathcal I_t\}\subseteq\mathbb R^{2m+1}.\)

Write a point of \(P_t\) as \((y_t,z_t)\). Its intended interpretation is:

  • \(y_{t,g}\) is the fraction of type \(t\) receiving class \(g\);
  • \(z_{t,k}\) is the fraction of type \(t\) receiving a bundle of size \(k\).

This interpretation is exact: membership in \(P_t\) means precisely that these quantities arise from a probability distribution over independent bundles.

Every generating point \((\chi_B,e_{|B|})\) is a vertex. Indeed, if it is a convex combination of generating points, each coordinate of \(\chi_B\), being either zero or one, forces every bundle with positive coefficient to have exactly the same incidence vector. Thus all contributing bundles equal \(B\). Conversely, every vertex of a finite convex hull belongs to its generating set.

2. Exact optimization over \(P_t\) by greedy

We first construct an exact linear-optimization oracle for \(P_t\).

Given arbitrary rational coefficients \(a_g\) and \(b_k\), optimization reduces to finding an independent bundle maximizing

\(\displaystyle \sum_{g\in B}a_g+b_{|B|}.\)

Order the goods as \(g_1,\ldots,g_m\), with \(a_{g_1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{g_m}\). Scan this order, accepting a good whenever adding it preserves independence. Independence is tested by

\(r_t(I\cup\{g\})=|I|+1.\)

Let \(R=r_t(G)\), and let \(I_k\) consist of the first \(k\) accepted goods, for \(0\leq k\leq R\), with \(I_0=\emptyset\).

Claim. For each such \(k\), \(I_k\) maximizes \(\sum_{g\in B}a_g\) among independent bundles of size \(k\).

Proof. Put \(H_j=\{g_1,\ldots,g_j\}\). Greedy stopped after \(k\) acceptances satisfies

\(|I_k\cap H_j|=\min\{k,r_t(H_j)\}.\)

To justify this, if greedy has accepted \(k\) goods within \(H_j\), the equality follows immediately. Otherwise, its accepted set is maximal independent in \(H_j\): a previously rejected good cannot augment the final accepted set, since independence of that augmentation would, by heredity, imply independence when the good was processed. A maximal independent set in a matroid has cardinality equal to the rank, by the augmentation axiom.

Every independent bundle \(B\) of size \(k\) satisfies

\(|B\cap H_j|\leq\min\{k,r_t(H_j)\}.\)

The identity

\(\displaystyle \sum_{g\in B}a_g=a_{g_m}k+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(a_{g_j}-a_{g_{j+1}})|B\cap H_j|\)

therefore proves the claim: all coefficients in the latter sum are nonnegative, and \(I_k\) attains every prefix bound. This argument permits negative weights. \(\square\)

Consequently, evaluate

\(\displaystyle b_k+\sum_{g\in I_k}a_g\)

for every \(0\leq k\leq R\), and return a maximizing \(I_k\). No independent bundle has size greater than \(R\), so this solves the optimization problem exactly.

One greedy scan supplies all these candidates. It uses at most \(m\) independence tests, plus a rank query for \(R\). Sorting, evaluation, and all rational arithmetic take polynomial time in \(m\) and the coefficient encoding length. The returned optimizer is a vertex of \(P_t\).

3. The polyhedral algorithmic tools and their hypotheses

We use the following standard results for well-described rational polytopes:

  • Exact linear optimization and strong separation are polynomial-time equivalent.
  • A strong separation oracle permits exact rational linear optimization, including returning an optimal vertex.
  • Given a rational point in a polytope in \(\mathbb R^d\), constructive Carathéodory decomposition returns an exact convex combination of at most \(d+1\) vertices.

The running times and oracle-call counts are polynomial in the dimension, a bound on inequality encoding lengths, and the relevant input encoding lengths. These results accommodate lower-dimensional polytopes. See [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Chapter 6, including Theorem 6.5.11](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4).

Here the required encoding bound is polynomial and known independently of the matroids. To verify this, consider any convex hull of zero-one vectors in \(\mathbb R^d\). If its affine dimension is \(h\), select \(h\) coordinate positions whose projection is injective on its affine hull. Its projection is a full-dimensional zero-one polytope in \(\mathbb R^h\). Each facet hyperplane can be determined from \(h\) affinely independent zero-one vertices; determinant formulas give integer coefficients bounded in magnitude by \(h!\). The affine hull in the original coordinates likewise has equations obtained by elimination on zero-one vertices, with polynomial encoding length. Lifting the projected facet inequalities supplies a description of polynomial encoding length.

Thus each \(P_t\) has a known inequality-encoding bound polynomial in \(m\). Together with Section 2, the oracle equivalence supplies a polynomial-time strong separation oracle for \(P_t\), using polynomially many rank queries. Constructive decomposition is also available for \(P_t\).

4. An exact formulation of the allocation problem

Define the global feasible polytope

\(\displaystyle Q=\left\{((y_t,z_t))_{t\in T}:(y_t,z_t)\in P_t\text{ for every }t,\quad \sum_t\mu_t y_{t,g}\leq q_g\text{ for every }g\right\}.\)

Its ambient dimension is \(d=\tau(2m+1)\). It is bounded because every coordinate lies in \([0,1]\), and it is nonempty because every type can receive the empty bundle.

There is an exact correspondence between feasible allocations and points of \(Q\).

Given an allocation, define

\(\displaystyle y_{t,g}=\frac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_{B:g\in B}x_{t,B},\qquad z_{t,k}=\frac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_{B:|B|=k}x_{t,B}.\)

The positivity of \(\mu_t\) makes these expressions well-defined. Since \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\), they form a convex combination of the generating points of \(P_t\). The supply constraints give membership in \(Q\).

Conversely, for a point of \(Q\), choose a convex decomposition

\(\displaystyle (y_t,z_t)=\sum_j\lambda_{t,j}(\chi_{B_{t,j}},e_{|B_{t,j}|}),\qquad \lambda_{t,j}\geq0,\quad \sum_j\lambda_{t,j}=1.\)

Set \(x_{t,B_{t,j}}=\mu_t\lambda_{t,j}\), combining repetitions if necessary. These masses satisfy the type totals, and their usage of class \(g\) is exactly \(\sum_t\mu_t y_{t,g}\). Every assigned bundle is independent.

A separation oracle for \(Q\) first checks its explicit supply inequalities and then calls the separation oracle for each \(P_t\). A separating inequality for a type block is also valid for \(Q\).

The inequality encoding lengths for \(Q\) are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\): the type-block bounds were established above, and the supply coefficients are input rationals. Hence exact rational optimization over \(Q\) is polynomial-time.

5. Implementing every refinement exactly

Because every allowed bundle is independent, \(r_t(B)=|B|\). The relevant statistics are therefore linear functions on \(Q\):

\(\displaystyle u_t=\mu_t\sum_{k=0}^{m}kz_{t,k},\qquad p_t=\mu_t\sum_{k=1}^{m}z_{t,k}.\)

When \(f(0)\) is finite,

\(\displaystyle W=\sum_t\mu_tw_t\sum_{k=0}^{m}f(k)z_{t,k}.\)

When \(f(0)=-\infty\), the finite welfare stage is instead

\(\displaystyle W_+=\sum_t\mu_tw_t\sum_{k=1}^{m}f(k)z_{t,k}.\)

Thus no infinite coefficient enters a linear program.

For finite \(f(0)\), successively maximize the ordered list

\(W,\ u_{t_1},\ldots,u_{t_\tau}.\)

For \(f(0)=-\infty\), successively maximize

\(\displaystyle \sum_tp_t,\ p_{t_1},\ldots,p_{t_\tau},\ W_+,\ u_{t_1},\ldots,u_{t_\tau}.\)

After each maximization, add an equality fixing that objective to its exact optimum before proceeding. This preserves all preceding optima and implements precisely the prescribed lexicographic priorities. There are respectively \(\tau+1\) and \(2\tau+2\) stages.

We must also bound the encoding lengths of these successive optimum values. Every stage restricts the preceding feasible polytope to an optimal face. Consequently, each resulting polytope is a face of the original \(Q\), and its vertices are vertices of \(Q\).

By Cramer’s rule, vertices of a rational polytope in polynomial dimension, with polynomial inequality encoding lengths, have polynomial encoding length. Each stage objective also has polynomial encoding length. Its optimum is attained at a vertex of the current face, hence at a vertex of \(Q\), so every optimum value has a uniform polynomial encoding bound. This prevents cumulative growth of the equalities beyond polynomial size.

Separation for each successive face consists of checking the added equalities and then separating over \(Q\). The exact optimization theorem therefore applies at every stage, including when the optimal face has lower dimension.

6. Recovering the requested bundle masses

Let \(((y_t^*,z_t^*))_t\) be an exact optimal point after the final stage.

For each type, apply constructive Carathéodory decomposition to \((y_t^*,z_t^*)\in P_t\). Since \(P_t\subseteq\mathbb R^{2m+1}\), this returns at most \(2m+2\) vertices:

\(\displaystyle (y_t^*,z_t^*)=\sum_{j=1}^{s_t}\lambda_{t,j}(\chi_{B_{t,j}},e_{|B_{t,j}|}),\qquad s_t\leq2m+2.\)

Section 1 ensures that these vertices identify independent bundles. Output the positive masses

\(x_{t,B_{t,j}}=\mu_t\lambda_{t,j},\)

combining any repetitions.

This preserves the optimized marginals and size distributions exactly. Section 4 then gives feasibility, while Section 5 shows that every objective in the refinement sequence retains its optimum.

All decomposition coefficients have polynomial encoding length, and decomposition uses polynomially many calls to the separation oracles already constructed. The total output contains at most \(\tau(2m+2)\) nonzero masses, each attached to a bundle representable by \(m\) bits.

This completes the polynomial-time algorithm and the proof of exact recovery.
