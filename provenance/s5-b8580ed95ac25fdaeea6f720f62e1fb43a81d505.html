Given a finite type set \(T\), a simplicial complex \(K\) specifying feasible type supports, rational type masses \(\mu_t\), weights \(a_t\), and quota \(q\), clone each type to \(r\mu_t\) voters at every admissible scale \(r\), with quota \(rq\). Count critical voter–coalition pairs under the paper’s equal-coalition-counting convention and, for instances where the resulting type-power vector converges, compute its limit to additive error \(\varepsilon\).

Input also includes positive binary-rational weights \(a_t\), a positive binary-rational quota \(q\), a simplicial complex \(K\) on \(T\) given by its \(m\) explicitly listed facets, and binary-rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\). A coalition \(S\subseteq N_r\) is feasible exactly when its represented type set belongs to \(K\); include the empty coalition as feasible and losing. Write \(W(S)=\sum_{v\in S}a_{t(v)}\).

Partial result: exact entropy comparison captures the remaining complexity

I do not establish polynomial-time solvability or $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness for the general problem.

The new results proved below are:

  • Polynomial-time approximation of the promised limit is equivalent, under polynomial-time oracle reductions, to an exact comparison problem for two explicitly specified entropy maxima.
  • The reduction from entropy comparison uses only three disjoint facets, and every constructed instance satisfies the convergence promise, including instances arising from equal entropy maxima.
  • The promised limit can be approximated in polynomial space. The relevant strict entropy-comparison language belongs to $\exists\mathbb R\cap\mathrm{co}\text{-}\exists\mathbb R$.

Thus overlaps, convergence verification, and numerical evaluation of the limiting coefficients can all be removed from the remaining complexity question.

Throughout, rational inputs are understood to be encoded by binary numerators and denominators, as in the rational normalization used in the notes.

1. The exact entropy-comparison problem

A profile is a finite list of positive rational masses $\lambda_i$ and positive integer weights $b_i$. Its masses need not sum to one.

Given a positive rational quota $c$, define its capacity
$$ \beta=\sum_i\lambda_i b_i. $$
For $0<c<\beta$, define
$$ \alpha(\lambda,b;c)= \max\left\{ \sum_i\lambda_i h(x_i): 0\leq x_i\leq1,\quad \sum_i\lambda_i b_i x_i=c \right\}, $$
where
$$ h(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x) $$
has its continuous endpoint values.

The comparison problem $\mathcal E$ receives two profiles and a common quota $c$, with both capacities strictly exceeding $c$, and returns whether their entropy maxima satisfy
$$ \alpha_1<\alpha_2,\qquad \alpha_1=\alpha_2,\qquad\text{or}\qquad \alpha_1>\alpha_2. $$

The main reduction theorem is:

Theorem. The promised limit-approximation problem has a polynomial-time algorithm if and only if $\mathcal E$ has a polynomial-time algorithm.

The reverse implication uses polynomially many exact comparisons. The forward implication uses one call to the limit-approximation algorithm, with an error parameter having polynomial bit length.

We first establish the limit formula and quantitative estimates needed to prove this theorem.

2. Saddle parameters and critical-pair asymptotics

Scale the society’s weights and quota by a positive integer $A$ so that
$$ b_t=Aa_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad c=Aq. $$
This preserves all criticality inequalities. Let $D$ be the least common multiple of the reduced mass denominators. The admissible population sizes are exactly $r=Dn$.

For a type set $F$, put
$$ \beta_F=\sum_{t\in F}\mu_t b_t. $$
Let $C_t^F(r)$ count critical pairs when every coalition using only types in $F$ is permitted.

2.1 The saddle parameter

Suppose $0<c<\beta_F$. The function
$$ f_F(\theta)= \sum_{t\in F} \mu_t b_t\frac{e^{\theta b_t}}{1+e^{\theta b_t}} $$
is continuous and strictly increasing from zero to $\beta_F$. Consequently there is a unique $\theta_F\in\mathbb R$ with
$$ f_F(\theta_F)=c. $$

Define
$$ p_{F,t}=\frac{e^{\theta_F b_t}}{1+e^{\theta_F b_t}}, \qquad V_F=\sum_{t\in F} \mu_t b_t^2p_{F,t}(1-p_{F,t}), $$
and
$$ \alpha_F= \sum_{t\in F}\mu_t\log(1+e^{\theta_F b_t}) -c\theta_F. $$
Every $p_{F,t}$ lies strictly between zero and one, so $V_F>0$.

For any $\theta\in\mathbb R$ and $b>0$, strict concavity of $h$ gives
$$ h(x)\leq \log(1+e^{\theta b})-\theta bx, $$
with equality exactly at
$$ x=\frac{e^{\theta b}}{1+e^{\theta b}}. $$
Indeed, the derivative of the difference between the right side and $h(x)$ vanishes exactly there, and its second derivative is positive.

Applying this inequality coordinatewise at $\theta_F$ proves that $\alpha_F$ equals the constrained entropy maximum in Section 1. Its unique maximizing vector is $(p_{F,t})_{t\in F}$. In particular, $\alpha_F>0$.

2.2 The critical-pair coefficient

Write
$$ g_F=\gcd\{b_t:t\in F\}, \qquad \delta_F(r)= g_F\left\lceil\frac{rc}{g_F}\right\rceil-rc. $$
For $t\in F$, define
$$ R_{F,t}= \frac{\mu_t}{\sqrt{V_F}} \begin{cases} \displaystyle \frac{g_Fe^{\theta_Fg_F}} {e^{\theta_Fg_F}-1} \tanh\left(\frac{\theta_Fb_t}{2}\right), &\theta_F\ne0,\\[6pt] \displaystyle \frac{b_t}{2}, &\theta_F=0. \end{cases} $$
Set $R_{F,t}=0$ for $t\notin F$. These coefficients are positive on $F$.

Lemma. For every $t\in F$,
$$ C_t^F(r)= \frac{\sqrt r\,e^{r\alpha_F}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\, e^{-\theta_F\delta_F(r)}R_{F,t}(1+o(1)) $$
through all admissible population sizes.

Proof. Fix a voter $v$ of type $t$. Independently include each other voter of type $u$ with probability $p_{F,u}$, and let $Z$ be their total integer weight.

The number of coalitions of these other voters having weight $\ell$ is
$$ \frac{e^{r\alpha_F}}{1+e^{\theta_Fb_t}}\, e^{-\theta_F(\ell-rc)}\Pr(Z=\ell). $$
This follows directly by multiplying the Bernoulli probabilities: a coalition of weight $\ell$ has tilted probability proportional to $e^{\theta_F\ell}$.

The voter is critical precisely when
$$ rc-b_t\leq Z<rc. $$
The multiples of $g_F$ in this interval are
$$ \ell_j=rc+\delta_F(r)-b_t+jg_F, \qquad 0\leq j<\frac{b_t}{g_F}. $$

We claim that, uniformly over these finitely many points,
$$ \Pr(Z=\ell_j)= \frac{g_F}{\sqrt{2\pi rV_F}}(1+o(1)). $$
To justify this, write $r=Dn$. A full block of voters contains $D\mu_u$ voters of each type $u\in F$. Its tilted total weight $Y$ has mean $Dc$, variance $DV_F$, and maximal lattice span $g_F$. Its support contains zero and each $b_u$, and all support values are multiples of $g_F$.

For $Y/g_F$, the characteristic function has modulus strictly below one on $[-\pi,\pi]\setminus\{0\}$: equality would force every supported integer to have the same phase, contradicting span one. Near zero, its centered logarithm is
$$ -\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y/g_F)}2s^2+O(|s|^3). $$
Fourier inversion for sums of independent blocks therefore gives the stated local Gaussian estimate. Explicitly, on $|s|\leq n^{-2/5}$, the substitution $s=x/\sqrt n$ gives the Gaussian integral. Between this interval and a fixed neighborhood of zero, the integrand is bounded by $e^{-\kappa ns^2}$; outside that neighborhood it decays geometrically. A bounded displacement from the mean contributes a phase tending to one.

The variable $Z$ consists of $n-1$ full blocks and one fixed block with $v$ removed. Conditioning on that last block introduces only finitely many bounded displacements. This proves the claimed estimate.

Multiplying by $r\mu_t$ choices of $v$ gives
$$ C_t^F(r)= \frac{\sqrt r\,e^{r\alpha_F}}{\sqrt{2\pi V_F}}\, \frac{\mu_tg_F}{1+e^{\theta_Fb_t}} \sum_{j=0}^{b_t/g_F-1} e^{\theta_F(b_t-\delta_F(r)-jg_F)} (1+o(1)). $$
The factor $g_F$ here repairs the omitted factor identified by the verifier.

For $\theta_F\ne0$, the geometric sum evaluates to
$$ \frac{g_F}{1+e^{\theta_Fb_t}} \sum_{j=0}^{b_t/g_F-1} e^{\theta_F(b_t-jg_F)} = \frac{g_Fe^{\theta_Fg_F}} {e^{\theta_Fg_F}-1} \tanh\left(\frac{\theta_Fb_t}{2}\right). $$
For $\theta_F=0$, it equals $b_t/2$. Substitution proves the lemma. $\square$

3. A limit formula that counts coalitions once

Delete any duplicate entries in the facet list.

If $B\subsetneq F$ and $\beta_B\geq c$, the entropy maximum for $B$ is strictly below $\alpha_F$ whenever $\beta_F>c$. To see this, extend vectors on $B$ by zero on $F\setminus B$. The resulting constraint set is compact and excludes the unique maximizing vector for $F$, whose coordinates are all positive. Continuity and uniqueness therefore give a strict inequality.

If $\beta_B=c$, positivity of masses and weights forces every coordinate in its constraint set to equal one, so its entropy maximum is zero.

For each type,
$$ 0\leq \sum_F C_t^F(r)-C_t(r) \leq \sum_{F<G} C_t^{F\cap G}(r). $$
A critical pair belonging to exactly $k$ facets contributes $k-1$ to the left-hand overcount and $\binom{k}{2}$ to the right-hand sum. This proves the inequality pair by pair.

Distinct facets have proper intersections. The preceding strict entropy inequality and the asymptotic lemma show that intersections contribute negligibly relative to the largest interior-facet exponential rate. An intersection of capacity exactly $c$ contributes only linearly in $r$; one of capacity below $c$ contributes nothing.

3.1 The boundary case

If no facet has capacity exceeding $c$, let
$$ \mathcal B=\{F:\beta_F=c\}. $$
The capacity hypothesis ensures $\mathcal B\ne\varnothing$.

The winning coalitions are exactly the full voter sets of these facets, and every voter in each such coalition is critical. Hence
$$ P_t^*= \frac{\mu_t|\{F\in\mathcal B:t\in F\}|} {\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal B}\sum_{u\in F}\mu_u}. $$
This is an exact rational formula computable in polynomial time.

3.2 The interior case

Suppose some facet has capacity exceeding $c$, and define
$$ \mathcal M= \left\{ F:\beta_F>c,\quad \alpha_F=\max_{\beta_G>c}\alpha_G \right\}. $$

There is an infinite admissible subsequence on which every $\delta_F(r)$ vanishes. For example, if $v$ is the reduced denominator of $c$, take $r$ to be multiples of
$$ Dv\prod_{\beta_F>c}g_F. $$
Then $rc$ is a multiple of every relevant $g_F$.

Along this subsequence, the asymptotic lemma and the overlap estimate give
$$ P_t(r)\longrightarrow \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal M}R_{F,t}} {\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal M}\sum_{u\in F}R_{F,u}}. $$
The convergence promise makes this subsequential limit equal to the full limit. Therefore
$$ \boxed{ P_t^*= \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal M}R_{F,t}} {\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal M}\sum_{u\in F}R_{F,u}}. } $$

This derivation uses the promise without requiring an algorithm to verify it.

4. Explicit bounds on the coefficients

The following bounds are the key new ingredient for handling exact entropy ties.

Consider any profile with $0<c<\beta$, and suppress the facet subscript. Put
$$ M=\sum_i\lambda_i,\qquad b_{\min}=\min_i b_i,\qquad g=\gcd_i b_i, $$
$$ \delta=\min(c,\beta-c),\qquad Q=\frac14\sum_i\lambda_i b_i^2, \qquad v=\frac{b_{\min}\delta}{2}. $$

Let $\theta$, $V$, and $R_i$ be its saddle parameter, variance, and coefficients.

4.1 Bounds on the saddle and variance

If $\theta<0$, then
$$ c=f(\theta)\leq \beta e^{-|\theta|b_{\min}}. $$
If $\theta>0$, then
$$ \beta-c\leq \beta e^{-\theta b_{\min}}. $$
Consequently,
$$ |\theta|g\leq |\theta|b_{\min} \leq \log\frac{\beta}{\delta}. $$

Differentiation gives, for every real $x$,
$$ \frac{b_{\min}}2 \min(f(x),\beta-f(x)) \leq f'(x)\leq Q. $$
For $x\leq0$, each inclusion probability is at most one half, so
$$ p_i(1-p_i)\geq \frac{p_i}{2}. $$
For $x\geq0$, each is at least one half, so
$$ p_i(1-p_i)\geq \frac{1-p_i}{2}. $$
Multiplying by $\lambda_i b_i^2$ and using $b_i\geq b_{\min}$ proves the lower inequality. The upper inequality follows from $p_i(1-p_i)\leq1/4$.

At the root,
$$ v\leq V\leq Q. $$

4.2 Bounds on total prefactors

For $\theta\ne0$, set
$$ x=|\theta|g,\qquad y_i=|\theta|b_i, $$
so $y_i\geq x$.

If $\theta<0$, the coefficient factor before $\lambda_i/\sqrt V$ is
$$ \frac{g}{e^x-1}\tanh(y_i/2) \geq \frac{g}{e^x+1}. $$
If $\theta>0$, that factor is
$$ \frac{g}{1-e^{-x}}\tanh(y_i/2) \geq \frac{g}{1+e^{-x}}\geq\frac g2. $$
Using $e^x\leq\beta/\delta$, and including the continuous value at $\theta=0$, gives
$$ R_i\geq \frac{\lambda_i g\delta}{(\beta+\delta)\sqrt V}. $$

For an upper bound, define continuous functions
$$ \psi(s)= \begin{cases} \displaystyle\frac{s}{1-e^{-s}},&s\ne0,\\ 1,&s=0, \end{cases} \qquad \chi(y)= \begin{cases} \displaystyle\frac{\tanh y}{y},&y\ne0,\\ 1,&y=0. \end{cases} $$
The coefficient has the nonsingular representation
$$ R_i= \frac{\lambda_i b_i}{2\sqrt V} \psi(\theta g)\chi(\theta b_i/2). $$
Here
$$ 0<\chi(y)\leq1,\qquad 0<\psi(s)\leq1+\max(s,0). $$
The latter inequality follows from $e^s\geq1+s$ for $s>0$ and from
$$ \psi(-s)=\frac{s}{e^s-1}\leq1 $$
for $s>0$. Therefore
$$ R_i\leq \frac{\lambda_i b_i}{2\sqrt V} \left(1+\log\frac{\beta}{\delta}\right). $$

Let
$$ K=\sum_i R_i. $$
Using
$$ \sqrt Q\leq Q+1,\qquad \frac1{\sqrt V}\leq1+\frac1v,\qquad \log\frac{\beta}{\delta}\leq\frac{\beta}{\delta}, $$
we obtain explicit rational bounds
$$ \boxed{ \ell= \frac{Mg\delta}{(\beta+\delta)(Q+1)} \leq K\leq \frac{\beta}{2} \left(1+\frac{\beta}{\delta}\right) \left(1+\frac1v\right) =U. } $$

Both $\ell>0$ and $U$ have polynomial bit length in the profile input. Indeed, they use rational sums, products, differences, division, and an integer gcd, with only polynomially many operations. In particular,
$$ \ell\geq2^{-\operatorname{poly}(B)}, \qquad U\leq2^{\operatorname{poly}(B)} $$
when the profile encoding length is $B$.

These bounds do not depend on an entropy separation.

5. Numerical evaluation after dominant facets are known

Lemma. Given a profile and $0<c<\beta$, its coefficients $R_i$ can be approximated to absolute error $\rho>0$ in time polynomial in the profile encoding length and $\log(1/\rho)$.

Proof. The preceding saddle bound supplies a bracket $[-B_0,B_0]$, with $B_0$ an integer of polynomial magnitude, containing $\theta$.

Approximate bisection is certified using the derivative lower bound. Suppose an evaluation at $x$ is within $\xi$ of $f(x)$. If it certifies the sign of $f(x)-c$, take the corresponding half-interval. Otherwise,
$$ |f(x)-c|\leq2\xi. $$
Choose $\xi\leq\delta/4$. Between $x$ and the root, the values of $f$ then lie in $[c-\delta/2,c+\delta/2]$, so
$$ f'\geq\frac{b_{\min}\delta}{4}. $$
Thus
$$ |x-\theta|\leq\frac{8\xi}{b_{\min}\delta}. $$
This permits certified root approximation with polynomially many bisections and polynomially many precision bits.

It remains to check that coefficient evaluation requires only polynomial root precision. Write
$$ J=\frac14\sum_i\lambda_i b_i^3. $$
For the variance as a function of the saddle parameter,
$$ |V'(x)|\leq J. $$
Also,
$$ 0\leq\psi'(s)\leq1,\qquad |\chi'(y)|\leq1. $$
For $\psi$, differentiation and the inequalities $e^s\geq1+s$ and $e^{-s}\geq1-s$ prove the assertion for positive arguments; negative arguments follow from $\psi(-s)=\psi(s)-s$. For $\chi$, use
$$ \chi(y)=\int_0^1\operatorname{sech}^2(ty)\,dt; $$
differentiation bounds its derivative by $\int_0^1 2t\,dt=1$.

Choose a rational neighborhood of $\theta$ with radius at most
$$ \min\left(\frac1g,\frac{v}{2J}\right). $$
Within it, $V\geq v/2$ and $|xg|\leq B_0+1$. Differentiating the displayed representation of $R_i$ gives the explicit bound
$$ \left|\frac{dR_i}{dx}\right| \leq \frac{\lambda_i b_i}{2} \left[ \left(g+\frac{(B_0+2)b_i}{2}\right) \left(1+\frac2v\right) + \frac{(B_0+2)J}{2} \left(1+\frac2v\right)^3 \right]. $$
This rational upper bound has polynomial bit length. Consequently, root accuracy with polynomially many bits suffices for the requested coefficient accuracy.

For completeness, the function evaluations also have polynomial bit complexity. If $c=\beta/2$, then $\theta=0$ exactly and
$$ R_i=\frac{\lambda_i b_i}{2\sqrt Q}. $$
Otherwise, the upper derivative bound yields
$$ |\theta|\geq\frac{|c-\beta/2|}{Q}>0. $$
This rational lower bound has polynomial bit length. Approximating the root within half this bound prevents a spurious zero and controls divisions in $\psi$ and $\chi$.

Exponential arguments of polynomial magnitude can be evaluated to the required precision by Taylor expansion and range reduction. For larger arguments, logistic and hyperbolic-tangent tails can be truncated once their weighted contribution is below the prescribed error. All multiplying coefficients have polynomial bit length, so the truncation threshold has polynomial magnitude. The positive lower bound on $V$ similarly permits polynomial-time square-root and reciprocal evaluation. $\square$

Now suppose $\mathcal E$ has a polynomial-time algorithm. Use it to identify exactly the maximum-entropy facets $\mathcal M$. This takes polynomially many comparisons.

Let
$$ \ell_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal M}\ell_F. $$
Approximate every relevant $R_{F,t}$ within
$$ \rho=\frac{\varepsilon\ell_{\mathrm{tot}}}{8m\tau}. $$
This requires polynomially many precision bits. The total denominator error and each numerator error are at most
$$ m\tau\rho=\frac{\varepsilon\ell_{\mathrm{tot}}}{8}. $$
Since the true denominator is at least $\ell_{\mathrm{tot}}$, normalizing the approximate coefficients changes each coordinate by less than $\varepsilon$.

Together with the rational boundary formula, this proves one direction of the reduction theorem.

6. Exact entropy comparison from a promised limit approximation

We now prove the converse, including equality detection and the convergence promise.

Take an instance of $\mathcal E$: two profiles
$$ (\lambda_{1,i},b_{1,i}), \qquad (\lambda_{2,i},b_{2,i}) $$
and common quota $c$, with $\beta_1>c$ and $\beta_2>c$.

Put
$$ M_j=\sum_i\lambda_{j,i}, \qquad s=\frac1{2(1+M_1+M_2)}, \qquad q'=sc. $$

Create disjoint type sets $F_1,F_2$ by copying the two profiles. The copied masses and weights are
$$ \mu_{j,i}=s\lambda_{j,i}, \qquad a_{j,i}=b_{j,i}. $$
Their total mass is strictly below $1/2$, and their capacities strictly exceed $q'$.

Scaling masses and quota by the same factor scales the entropy objective by that factor, without changing its constraint vectors. Hence
$$ \alpha_{F_1}=s\alpha_1,\qquad \alpha_{F_2}=s\alpha_2. $$

6.1 Enforcing convergence

Two extra types will form a third, losing facet.

Give both extra types weight $q'/2$. Choose an integer $A$ making every weight integral after multiplication by $A$. Because $Aq'/2$ is an integer,
$$ c'=Aq' $$
is an integer.

For the two winning facets, let
$$ g_j'=\gcd_i(Ab_{j,i}), \qquad H=g_1'g_2'. $$
Give one extra type mass
$$ \nu=\frac1{4H} $$
and the other mass
$$ 1-s(M_1+M_2)-\nu. $$
The second mass is greater than $1/4$, so both are positive. All masses sum to one.

Let $F_0$ contain exactly these two extra types, and specify the complex by the three disjoint facets
$$ F_0,\quad F_1,\quad F_2. $$
The capacity of $F_0$ is
$$ \bigl(1-s(M_1+M_2)\bigr)\frac{q'}2<q', $$
so it is losing.

Let $D'$ be the least common multiple of the resulting mass denominators. Since $\nu=1/(4H)$ is reduced,
$$ 4H\mid D'. $$
For every admissible $r$, therefore,
$$ g_j'\mid rc'\qquad(j=1,2). $$
Both winning facets have $\delta_{F_j}(r)=0$ throughout the entire admissible sequence.

The asymptotic lemma consequently proves convergence, whether the two entropies are equal or unequal. The constructed society always satisfies the promise.

All construction steps have polynomial bit complexity.

6.2 Distinguishing equality from strict dominance

Let
$$ K_j=\sum_{t\in F_j}R_{F_j,t}. $$
Because the winning facets are disjoint, the limiting total power on $F_1$ is
$$ S_1^*= \sum_{t\in F_1}P_t^* = \begin{cases} 0,&\alpha_1<\alpha_2,\\[2pt] \displaystyle\frac{K_1}{K_1+K_2},&\alpha_1=\alpha_2,\\[6pt] 1,&\alpha_1>\alpha_2. \end{cases} $$

Compute the rational coefficient bounds from Section 4 for the constructed winning facets:
$$ 0<\ell_j\leq K_j\leq U_j. $$
Define
$$ \eta=\frac{\min(\ell_1,\ell_2)}{U_1+U_2}. $$
This is a positive rational of polynomial bit length. In the equality case,
$$ \eta\leq S_1^*\leq1-\eta. $$

Let $\tau'$ be the constructed support size. Request coordinate error
$$ \varepsilon'=\frac{\eta}{8\tau'}. $$
If $\widehat P$ is the returned approximation and
$$ \widehat S_1=\sum_{t\in F_1}\widehat P_t, $$
then
$$ |\widehat S_1-S_1^*|\leq\frac{\eta}{8}. $$

The following thresholds therefore identify the comparison exactly:

  • If $\widehat S_1<\eta/2$, then $\alpha_1<\alpha_2$.
  • If $\widehat S_1>1-\eta/2$, then $\alpha_1>\alpha_2$.
  • Otherwise, $\alpha_1=\alpha_2$.

For a tie, the estimate lies between $7\eta/8$ and $1-7\eta/8$, so it cannot enter either strict-comparison region.

The required error parameter has polynomial bit length, even though it can be exponentially small in value. This is legitimate because the target running-time bound includes precision in the input length.

This completes the reduction theorem.

7. A polynomial-space upper bound

Exact entropy comparison admits a polynomial-size reduction to the existential theory of the reals.

Take two profiles with common quota $c$. Choose an integer $E$ clearing all mass denominators and the quota denominator, and write
$$ n_{j,i}=E\lambda_{j,i}\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad d=Ec\in\mathbb Z_{>0}. $$
These integers have polynomial bit length.

For profile $j$, define integer polynomials
$$ Q_j(z)= \sum_i n_{j,i}b_{j,i}z^{b_{j,i}} \prod_{k\ne i}(1+z^{b_{j,k}}) - d\prod_k(1+z^{b_{j,k}}) $$
and
$$ U_j(z)= \prod_i(1+z^{b_{j,i}})^{n_{j,i}}. $$

For $z>0$, the denominators $1+z^{b_{j,i}}$ are positive. Thus $Q_j(z)=0$ is equivalent to the saddle equation. It has exactly one positive solution,
$$ z_j=e^{\theta_j}. $$
At this solution,
$$ e^{E\alpha_j}=\frac{U_j(z_j)}{z_j^d}. $$
