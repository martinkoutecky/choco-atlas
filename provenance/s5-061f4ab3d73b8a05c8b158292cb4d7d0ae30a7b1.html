The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) goods, \(\tau\) valuation types \(v_t\in\mathrm{GGS}(2,M)\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and positive rational per-capita supplies \(\beta_j\), compute nonnegative prices \(p_j\) and assignment masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\), where each \(S\subseteq\Omega\) is an integral bundle. Require \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le\beta_j\), \(x_{t,S}>0\Rightarrow S\in\arg\max_{R\subseteq\Omega}(v_t(R)-p(R))\), and zero price for every good with unused supply.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(\Omega\) be a set of \(m\ge1\) goods. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) valuation types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), encoded as binary rationals, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type is specified by binary rational singleton values \(a_{tj}\) and a common binary rational \(M\ge0\): \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\), \(v_t(\{j\})=a_{tj}\), and \(v_t(S)=M\) whenever \(|S|\ge2\). The class \(\mathrm{GGS}(2,M)\) requires \(0\le a_{tj}\le M\) and \(a_{tj}+a_{tk}\ge M\) for distinct goods \(j,k\).
Supplies \(\beta_j>0\) are binary rational per-capita quantities. Bundles are subsets of \(\Omega\), so each individual receives at most one unit of each good; a type's mass may be split among bundles. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Compute exact nonnegative rational prices \(p_j\) and assignment masses \(x_{t,S}\), represented by their nonzero entries, with omitted entries zero. Write \(p(S)=\sum_{j\in S}p_j\).
Require \(\sum_{S\subseteq\Omega}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le\beta_j\), and \(x_{t,S}>0\Rightarrow S\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{R\subseteq\Omega}(v_t(R)-p(R))\); all utility ties are permitted. Also require \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}<\beta_j\Rightarrow p_j=0\).

Is this equilibrium computation problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies with one unit of each good and a common \(M\), an ascending auction terminates at a Walrasian allocation and equilibrium; the result states termination without a polynomial bound in binary input length. [Ben-Zwi, Lavi, and Newman](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.1153v3).

The problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) by an explicit rational linear program. Splitting each type’s mass among bundles makes the welfare program directly applicable.

1. Reduction to bundles of size at most two

Define
\[ \]
\( \mathcal B=\{\varnothing\}\cup\{\{j\}:j\in\Omega\}\cup\{\{j,k\}:j,k\in\Omega,\ j\ne k\}. \)

Its cardinality is \(K=1+m+\binom m2\).

If \(R\subseteq\Omega\) has cardinality at least three, choose any pair \(T\subseteq R\). Then \(v_t(T)=v_t(R)=M\). Replacing an assignment of \(R\) by an assignment of \(T\) therefore preserves welfare and weakly decreases every good’s consumption. Applying this replacement to all larger bundles shows that restricting assignments to \(\mathcal B\) preserves the maximum feasible welfare.

Moreover, for every nonnegative price vector \(p\),
\(p(T)\le p(R)\), so
\(v_t(T)-p(T)\ge v_t(R)-p(R)\).
Thus larger bundles cannot yield strictly greater utility than every bundle in \(\mathcal B\). They may tie, which the question permits.

2. A polynomial-size primal and dual

Introduce a nonnegative variable \(x_{t,S}\) for each type \(t\) and each \(S\in\mathcal B\). Consider the welfare program:

\( \displaystyle \text{maximize }\quad P(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{S\in\mathcal B}v_t(S)x_{t,S}, \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle \sum_{S\in\mathcal B}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau), \)

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal B\\j\in S}}x_{t,S}\le\beta_j\qquad(j\in\Omega), \)

and \(x_{t,S}\ge0\).

This program is feasible: assign every type its entire mass on the empty bundle. Its feasible region is bounded because \(0\le x_{t,S}\le\mu_t\). In particular, it attains an optimum. Its objective is at most \(M\), since every bundle’s value is at most \(M\) and the total assigned mass is one.

Let \(u_t\) be the unrestricted dual variable for type \(t\)’s equality constraint, and let \(p_j\ge0\) be the dual variable for good \(j\)’s supply constraint. The dual is

\( \displaystyle \text{minimize }\quad D(u,p)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tu_t+\sum_{j\in\Omega}\beta_jp_j, \)

subject to

\( u_t+p(S)\ge v_t(S)\qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau,\ S\in\mathcal B), \)

and \(p_j\ge0\).

The empty-bundle constraints imply \(u_t\ge0\). Written explicitly, the remaining constraints are

\( u_t+p_j\ge a_{tj}, \)

\( u_t+p_j+p_k\ge M\qquad(j\ne k). \)

The dual is feasible by taking \(p_j=0\) and \(u_t=M\). By linear-programming strong duality, there exist optimal primal and dual solutions with \(P(x)=D(u,p)\).

3. Optimal solutions satisfy every equilibrium condition

Take any optimal primal solution \(x\) and optimal dual solution \((u,p)\). Define consumption of good \(j\) by

\( \displaystyle q_j=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal B\\j\in S}}x_{t,S}. \)

Using the type-mass equalities, the primal-dual gap can be expanded as

\( \displaystyle D(u,p)-P(x)=\sum_{j\in\Omega}p_j(\beta_j-q_j)+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{S\in\mathcal B}x_{t,S}\bigl(u_t+p(S)-v_t(S)\bigr). \)

Every summand is nonnegative by primal and dual feasibility. Strong duality makes their sum zero, so each summand is zero.

Consequently, if \(q_j<\beta_j\), then \(p_j=0\). This is exactly the required condition on unsold supply.

Also, whenever \(x_{t,S}>0\),

\( u_t+p(S)=v_t(S), \)

and hence \(v_t(S)-p(S)=u_t\).

We must verify optimality against all bundles, including those omitted from the program. For \(R\in\mathcal B\), dual feasibility gives

\( v_t(R)-p(R)\le u_t. \)

For \(|R|\ge3\), choose distinct \(j,k\in R\). Since prices are nonnegative, the pair constraint gives

\( u_t+p(R)\ge u_t+p_j+p_k\ge M=v_t(R). \)

Thus \(v_t(R)-p(R)\le u_t\) for every \(R\subseteq\Omega\). Every positively assigned bundle attains this upper bound and therefore belongs to the full demand set.

Finally, set \(x_{t,R}=0\) for all \(R\notin\mathcal B\). The primal constraints give the required type masses and supply inequalities. Together with the preceding arguments, this produces an equilibrium satisfying every condition in the question.

4. Exact computation and output length

We use the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly represented rational linear program can be solved, including recovery of an exact rational optimum when one exists, in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. This concerns bit complexity and exact solutions; see the discussion of classical algorithms in [Gleixner and Steffy, Section 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.12820).

Here the primal has \(\tau K\) variables and \(\tau+m\) equality or supply constraints, besides nonnegativity. The dual has \(\tau+m\) variables and \(\tau K\) bundle constraints, besides price nonnegativity. All coefficients are input rationals or elements of \(\{0,1\}\). Constructing either program therefore requires only polynomially many bits in \(m,\tau,L\).

Solve both programs exactly and output the dual prices and the nonzero primal assignment entries. The exact LP theorem guarantees polynomial output bit length. There are at most \(\tau K\) nonzero assignment entries, and every represented bundle has size at most two.

The argument includes \(m=1\), when pair constraints are absent, and \(M=0\). It does not require the inequalities \(a_{tj}+a_{tk}\ge M\); the stated valuation class satisfies all hypotheses used.

The finite-society auction result concerns allocation to individual bidders, whereas the present problem permits fractional assignment masses. The welfare-program framework also appears in [Ben-Zwi, Lavi, and Newman, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.1153v3). Here, the polynomial-size reduction and fractional assignments suffice without an auction iteration bound.
