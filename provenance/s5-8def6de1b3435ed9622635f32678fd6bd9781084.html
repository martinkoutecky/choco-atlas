The continuous mirror question, as graded

For fixed \(K\), budget \(B\), and class valuation bounds \(\theta_j\), an instance is an ordered finite stream of cohorts \((q_s,j_s,v_s,r_s)\), where \(q_s\) is positive rational mass and each cohort profile is \((j_s,v_s,r_s)\). The policy learns each cohort only on arrival, chooses a per-unit allocation \(x_s\in[0,r_s]\) irrevocably, and must satisfy \(\sum_s q_s x_s\le B\). Its group utility is \(U_j(x)=\sum_{s:j_s=j}q_s v_s x_s\). Characterize the Pareto frontier of achievable pairs \((\alpha,\beta)\), where \(\alpha\) is the worst-case ratio of offline optimal total utility to the policy’s total utility, and \(\beta\) bounds \(\frac{1}{K}\sum_j U_j(w)/U_j(x)\) for every feasible alternative \(w\) on every completed stream. Determine the minimum feasible \(\beta\), the minimum \(\alpha\) for each feasible \(\beta\), and policies attaining the frontier, including how the answers depend on finite profile support.

Status and scope

The full question remains unresolved when $K>1$, $h>1$, and valuations vary. The new result proved here is an exact Pareto frontier when
$$ \theta_1=\cdots=\theta_K=1, $$
for every finite support bound $h$ and for unrestricted finite support. Repeated arrivals, arbitrary masses, and arbitrary positive rate limits are all allowed.

I also prove the exact frontiers for $h=1$ and for $K=1$, and necessary inequalities for general valuation bounds. These inequalities show that the supplied [unrestricted benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15782v1) does not hold for the capacities permitted in this task.

1. Normalization

For each cohort, put
$$ c_s=\frac{q_sr_s}{B}, \qquad z_s=\frac{q_sx_s}{B}. $$
Then
$$ 0\le z_s\le c_s, \qquad \sum_s z_s\le1, \qquad u_j(z):=\frac{U_j(x)}B =\sum_{s:j_s=j}v_sz_s. $$
This transformation preserves both guarantees, including the zero-denominator conventions. Conversely, a normalized allocation gives
$$ x_s=\frac{Bz_s}{q_s}. $$

We therefore work with budget $1$. Any positive rational capacity $c$ used below is a valid cohort: take $q=Bc$ and $r=1$. Aggregating cohorts with the same profile gives the required support masses.

2. Exact frontier when all valuations equal one

Assume throughout this section that $\theta_j=1$ for every class. Thus every valuation is $1$.

Define
$$ p= \begin{cases} \min\{K,h\},&\text{with support bound }h,\\ K,&\text{with unrestricted finite support}. \end{cases} $$
At most $p$ classes can occur. Distinct rates within a class have no effect on utility beyond their normalized capacities.

For $d>p-1$, define
$$ F_p(d)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac1{d-p+i}, $$
and let $D_p$ be the unique solution of
$$ F_p(D_p)=1. $$
For $p=1$, this gives $D_1=1$. For $p>1$,
$$ p<D_p<2p-1. $$
Indeed, $F_p$ is continuous and strictly decreasing, $F_p(p)>1$, and $F_p(2p-1)<1$.

For $d\ge D_p$, put
$$ A_p(d)= \left( 1-\sum_{i=2}^{p}\frac1{d-p+i} \right)^{-1}. $$
An empty sum is zero.

Theorem. When every valuation equals $1$, a pair $(\alpha,\beta)\in[1,\infty)^2$ is achievable exactly when
$$ \boxed{ \beta\ge \max\left\{1,\frac{D_p}{K}\right\}, \qquad \alpha\ge A_p(K\beta). } $$

Consequently:

  • The minimum feasible fairness factor is

$$ \boxed{\beta_{\min}=\max\left\{1,\frac{D_p}{K}\right\}.} $$

  • At every feasible $\beta$, the minimum feasible efficiency factor is

$$ \boxed{\alpha_{\min}(\beta)=A_p(K\beta).} $$

  • Both values are attained by the policy constructed below.
  • If $p>1$, the Pareto frontier is the graph of $\alpha_{\min}(\beta)$ over $\beta\ge\beta_{\min}$.
  • If $p=1$, the unique Pareto point is $(1,1)$.

2.1. Necessary inequalities

Write $d=K\beta$. Consider a stream of $p$ cohorts in distinct classes, each having capacity $1$. Let $b_i$ be the allocation to its $i$th cohort.

After the $i$th cohort, consider an alternative completion: append one cohort of capacity $\varepsilon$ in each of the remaining $p-i$ classes. Choose positive rational $\varepsilon$ with $(p-i)\varepsilon<1$.

A feasible comparator allocates $1-(p-i)\varepsilon$ to the current class and $\varepsilon$ to each appended class. Each appended class contributes at least $1$ to the sum of utility ratios: its policy allocation cannot exceed its capacity. If any relevant policy utility is zero, fairness already fails. Otherwise,
$$ \frac{1-(p-i)\varepsilon}{b_i}+p-i\le d. $$
The policy's allocation $b_i$ is fixed before this continuation. Letting rational $\varepsilon$ decrease to zero gives
$$ b_i\ge\frac1{d-p+i}. $$

Stopping after the first cohort additionally gives
$$ b_1\ge\frac1\alpha. $$
Therefore budget feasibility requires
$$ \frac1{\min\{\alpha,d-p+1\}} +\sum_{i=2}^{p}\frac1{d-p+i}\le1. $$
In particular, $F_p(d)\le1$, so $d\ge D_p$. Also,
$$ \frac1\alpha \le 1-\sum_{i=2}^{p}\frac1{d-p+i}, $$
which gives $\alpha\ge A_p(d)$.

Every construction uses at most $p$ profiles, so it respects the stated support bound.

2.2. A budget lemma

Use $p$ coordinate slots, assigning an unused slot to each newly observed class. Unused slots have allocation zero.

For a nonnegative vector $u=(u_1,\ldots,u_p)$, define
$$ Q_i(u)= \max_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,p\}\setminus\{i\}} \frac{1-\sum_{j\in S}u_j}{d-|S|}. $$
Here $d\ge K\ge p$, so every denominator is positive. The empty subset shows that
$$ \frac1d\le Q_i(u)\le\frac1{d-p+1}. $$
Also, $Q_i$ is nonincreasing in every other coordinate.

A fair update increases coordinate $i$ toward $Q_i(u)$, without increasing it if it already exceeds that value.

Put
$$ t_k=\frac1{d-p+k}, \qquad 1\le k\le p. $$

Rank bound. Suppose fair updates start from $v$, and let $v_{(k)}$ and $u_{(k)}$ denote the $k$th largest initial and final coordinates. Then
$$ u_{(k)}\le\max\{v_{(k)},t_k\}. $$

To prove this, first suppose at least $k-1$ other coordinates are at least $t$, where $t\ge t_k$. A subset $S$ of size $\ell$ contains at least
$$ b=\max\{0,\ell-(p-k)\} $$
of these coordinates. Hence
$$ \frac{1-\sum_{j\in S}u_j}{d-\ell} \le \frac{1-bt}{d-\ell} \le t. $$
The last inequality follows from
$$ d-\ell+b\ge d-p+k, \qquad t\ge\frac1{d-p+k}. $$
Thus $Q_i(u)\le t$ whenever $k-1$ other coordinates are at least $t$.

Now set $t=\max\{v_{(k)},t_k\}$. Initially, at most $k-1$ coordinates exceed $t$. An update cannot create a $k$th such coordinate: at that update, the other $k-1$ coordinates would force its cap to be at most $t$. This proves the rank bound.

Budget consequence. If
$$ \sum_i v_i=a\ge t_1, $$
then every sequence of fair updates satisfies
$$ \sum_i u_i \le a+\sum_{i=2}^{p}t_i. $$

Indeed, the rank bound gives
$$ \sum_i u_i \le \sum_{i=1}^{p}\max\{v_{(i)},t_i\} = a+\sum_i t_i-\sum_i\min\{v_{(i)},t_i\}. $$
We claim that the last sum is at least $t_1$.

If $v_{(i)}\le t_i$ for all $i$, that sum equals $a\ge t_1$. Otherwise choose $i$ with $v_{(i)}>t_i$. For every $j\le i$, both $v_{(j)}$ and $t_j$ are at least $t_i$, so
$$ \sum_j\min\{v_{(j)},t_j\}\ge i t_i\ge t_1. $$
The final inequality follows explicitly from
$$ \frac{i}{d-p+i}-\frac1{d-p+1} = \frac{(i-1)(d-p)} {(d-p+i)(d-p+1)} \ge0. $$

This budget argument does not assume budget feasibility in advance.

2.3. The attaining policy

Fix a feasible $\beta$, let $d=K\beta$, and set
$$ a=1-\sum_{i=2}^{p}t_i=\frac1{A_p(d)}. $$
Because $F_p(d)\le1$,
$$ t_1\le a\le1. $$

Maintain the cumulative class allocations $u_i$. On a cohort of class $i$ and capacity $c$, allocate in two steps:

  • Allocate greedily toward the total efficiency target:

$$ \Delta_0= \min\left\{c,\left(a-\sum_j u_j\right)_+\right\}. $$
Increase $u_i$ by $\Delta_0$.

  • Allocate from the remaining cohort capacity toward its fairness cap:

$$ \Delta_1= \min\left\{c-\Delta_0,\left(Q_i(u)-u_i\right)_+\right\}. $$
Increase $u_i$ by $\Delta_1$.

The cohort receives $\Delta_0+\Delta_1$. The two steps are computations within one cohort decision; they require no extra arrivals or profiles.

Budget feasibility

Before the total first reaches $a$, every cohort is fully allocated unless that cohort reaches the target.

At the instant the total reaches $a$, including an intermediate point within a cohort, let the allocation vector be $v$. Subsequent allocations are fair updates. The budget lemma gives
$$ \sum_i u_i \le a+\sum_{i=2}^{p}t_i=1. $$
If the total never reaches $a$, it remains below $1$. Capacity feasibility follows directly from the allocation rule.

Efficiency

If the total never reaches $a$, every cohort has been fully allocated, so the policy is optimal.

Otherwise its final utility is at least $a$, whereas the offline optimum is at most $1$. Therefore
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\le \frac1a\sum_i u_i =A_p(d)\sum_i u_i. $$

Fairness

Let $C_i$ be the final cumulative capacity of a present class. Call class $i$ fully served if $u_i=C_i$.

Every present class has positive allocation. This follows either from the greedy step or from $Q_i(u)\ge1/d$.

If a class is not fully served, some cohort in that class was partially rejected. At that time its cumulative allocation was at least its fairness cap. Since other allocations only increase,
$$ u_i\ge Q_i(u) $$
at completion for every class that is not fully served.

If all present classes are fully served, every comparator ratio is at most $1$, so their sum is at most $p\le d$.

Otherwise choose a class $i$ with minimum allocation among the classes that are not fully served, and write $t=u_i$. Let $S$ consist of the fully served classes whose allocations are smaller than $t$. Every class outside $S$ has allocation at least $t$.

For a feasible comparator with class allocations $w_j$,
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_j\frac{w_j}{u_j} &\le \sum_{j\in S}\frac{w_j}{u_j} +\frac{1-\sum_{j\in S}w_j}{t}\\ &= \frac1t+ \sum_{j\in S}w_j\left(\frac1{u_j}-\frac1t\right)\\ &\le |S|+\frac{1-\sum_{j\in S}u_j}{t}. \end{aligned} $$
The last inequality uses $w_j\le C_j=u_j$ for $j\in S$, and positive coefficients.

By the definition of $Q_i$,
$$ t\ge Q_i(u) \ge \frac{1-\sum_{j\in S}u_j}{d-|S|}. $$
Budget feasibility makes the numerator nonnegative. The preceding ratio sum is consequently at most $d=K\beta$, proving fairness.

This completes the theorem.

2.4. Example and Pareto interpretation

For $K=2$ and $h\ge2$, or unrestricted support,
$$ D_2=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}. $$
Thus
$$ \boxed{ \beta_{\min}=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{4}, \qquad \alpha_{\min}(\beta)=\frac{2\beta}{2\beta-1}. } $$
At minimum fairness, the optimal efficiency factor is
$$ \alpha_{\min}(\beta_{\min})=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}. $$

For $p>1$, $A_p(d)$ is strictly decreasing in $d$: each term in its denominator's subtracted sum strictly decreases. Hence every point on the stated curve is Pareto optimal. For each finite feasible $\beta$, its efficiency value is a minimum, not merely an infimum.

In particular, fairness factor $1$ is impossible in the displayed two-class example, contradicting the supplied unrestricted benchmark under this task's capacity assumptions.

3. Exact frontier for one profile

For arbitrary valuation bounds, suppose $h=1$. Every cohort has the same class, valuation, and rate.

Allocate greedily:
$$ z_s=\min\left\{c_s,1-\sum_{t<s}z_t\right\}. $$
This achieves the offline optimum. Only one class is present, and its comparator-to-policy utility ratio is at most $1$. Thus the fairness expression is at most $1/K$.

Within the required parameter domain, every pair is achievable, the unique Pareto point is $(1,1)$, and the minimum efficiency factor is $1$ for every $\beta\ge1$.

4. Exact frontier for one class

For $\theta\ge1$, define
$$ C_1(\theta)=1, $$
$$ C_h(\theta)=h-(h-1)\theta^{-1/(h-1)} \quad\text{for }h\ge2, $$
and
$$ C_\infty(\theta)=1+\ln\theta. $$

Theorem. For $K=1$, with support bound $h$, the achievable region is exactly
$$ \alpha\ge C_h(\theta_1), \qquad \beta\ge C_h(\theta_1). $$
For unrestricted finite support, replace $C_h$ by $C_\infty$.

The unique Pareto point is $(C_h(\theta_1),C_h(\theta_1))$, or its unrestricted counterpart. At every feasible $\beta$, the minimum efficiency factor is the same $C_h(\theta_1)$.

4.1. Lower bound

For one class, fairness is equivalent to
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\le\beta u_1. $$
Both guarantees therefore require competitiveness with factor
$$ c=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}. $$

Present capacity-one cohorts at increasing prices
$$ 1=p_1\le\cdots\le p_n\le\theta_1. $$
Let $V_i$ be the policy's utility after cohort $i$. Stopping there gives
$$ V_i\ge\frac{p_i}{c}. $$
Writing the allocation at cohort $i$ as $(V_i-V_{i-1})/p_i$, summation by parts yields
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{V_i-V_{i-1}}{p_i} &= \frac{V_n}{p_n} +\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}V_i \left(\frac1{p_i}-\frac1{p_{i+1}}\right)\\ &\ge \frac1c \left[ 1+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \left(1-\frac{p_i}{p_{i+1}}\right) \right]. \end{aligned} $$
The coefficients are nonnegative. By the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality,
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{p_i}{p_{i+1}} \ge (n-1)\left(\frac{p_1}{p_n}\right)^{1/(n-1)} \ge (n-1)\theta_1^{-1/(n-1)}. $$
Equal successive ratios, with endpoints $1$ and $\theta_1$, maximize the bracket and give $C_n(\theta_1)$. Rational increasing prices approximate this choice arbitrarily closely. Thus $n=h$ establishes $c\ge C_h(\theta_1)$. The case $h=1$ follows directly from the first cohort.

For unrestricted support,
$$ \lim_{n\to\infty}C_n(\theta_1)=1+\ln\theta_1, $$
so the corresponding lower bound follows.

4.2. Attaining policy

Let $F_s$ be the offline optimum for the prefix ending at cohort $s$, with $F_0=0$. For $C=C_h(\theta_1)$, or $C=C_\infty(\theta_1)$, allocate
$$ z_s=\frac{F_s-F_{s-1}}{Cv_s}. $$
Adding a cohort increases the prefix optimum by at most $v_sc_s$. Hence $0\le z_s\le c_s$. Utility telescopes:
$$ \sum_{t\le s}v_tz_t=\frac{F_s}{C}. $$

To prove budget feasibility, represent an offline optimal prefix allocation on resource positions in $[0,1]$. A position is vacant initially. An arriving cohort fills vacant positions or replaces lower-valued holdings. This maintains an optimum: the occupied positions retain the highest available values up to total measure $1$.

A position that is never occupied contributes zero to
$$ \sum_s\frac{F_s-F_{s-1}}{v_s}. $$
An occupied position undergoes a strictly increasing chain of positive values
$$ p_1,\ldots,p_n. $$
Its contribution is
$$ 1+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{p_i}{p_{i+1}}\right). $$
There are finitely many updates, so a finite partition of $[0,1]$ makes this calculation positionwise exact.

With at most $h$ profiles, $n\le h$. For $h=1$, each contribution is at most $1$. For $h\ge2$, pad a shorter chain with repeated values and apply the preceding arithmetic–geometric mean bound. Each contribution is at most $C_h(\theta_1)$.

For unrestricted support, use
$$ 1-\frac{p_i}{p_{i+1}} \le \ln\frac{p_{i+1}}{p_i}. $$
Each contribution is then at most $1+\ln\theta_1$.

Integrating over the unit budget gives
$$ \sum_s z_s\le1. $$
The policy therefore attains both claimed guarantees.

5. Necessary inequalities for general valuation bounds

Write $d=K\beta$. Choose $p$ distinct classes in an order $\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(p)$, and positive integers $n_1,\ldots,n_p$. For bounded support require
$$ \sum_i n_i\le h. $$
For unrestricted support, any finite choice is permitted.

Every achievable pair must satisfy
$$ \boxed{ \sum_{i=1}^{p} \frac{C_{n_i}(\theta_{\sigma(i)})}{d-p+i}\le1, } $$
and
$$ \boxed{ \frac{C_{n_1}(\theta_{\sigma(1)})} {\min\{\alpha,d-p+1\}} + \sum_{i=2}^{p} \frac{C_{n_i}(\theta_{\sigma(i)})}{d-p+i} \le1. } $$

To prove these, present one block per chosen class. Its block consists of $n_i$ capacity-one cohorts at increasing valuations.

At a prefix within block $i$, ending at valuation $v$, replace every remaining block by one valuation-one cohort of capacity $\varepsilon$ in its class. A comparator spends $1-(p-i)\varepsilon$ on the current cohort and $\varepsilon$ on each appended class. Consequently the current class utility $V_i(v)$ must satisfy
$$ \frac{v(1-(p-i)\varepsilon)}{V_i(v)}+p-i\le d. $$
Taking rational $\varepsilon$ to zero gives
$$ V_i(v)\ge\frac{v}{d-p+i}. $$
Within the first block, stopping also requires $V_1(v)\ge v/\alpha$.

Apply the summation-by-parts calculation from Section 4 separately to each block. Prices approximating equal successive ratios force the resource expenditures in the displayed inequalities. Summing expenditures proves both claims.

For unrestricted support, put
$$ a_j=1+\ln\theta_j, \qquad S=\sum_j a_j. $$
Choose all classes in decreasing order and let each block length tend to infinity. Every achievable pair satisfies
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{a_{K-i+1}}{d-K+i}\le1. $$
Let $D_\infty$ be the unique root where this sum equals $1$. If $K>1$, then
$$ D_\infty>S. $$
Indeed, at $d=S$, every denominator is positive and at most $S$, and at least one is strictly smaller. The sum is therefore strictly greater than
$$ \frac{\sum_j a_j}{S}=1. $$
Strict monotonicity gives the claimed root inequality.

Thus, under the task's assumptions,
$$ \boxed{ \beta\ge\frac{D_\infty}{K}> \frac1K\sum_j(1+\ln\theta_j) \quad\text{when }K>1. } $$
The supplied minimum-fairness formula cannot characterize this model.

6. Running times and numerical precision

All bounds below concern $m=1$. The policies require neither the support list nor future cohorts nor $T$ in advance.

6.1. The exact policy for valuations equal to one

There is no need to enumerate subsets in $Q_i$. For each subset size, its maximum comes from the smallest other allocations.

If $g$ classes have appeared, keep their allocations sorted and represent the remaining $p-g$ zero slots implicitly. Let
$$ s_1\le\cdots\le s_{g-1} $$
be the other observed allocations. Then
$$ Q_i(u)= \max_{0\le\ell\le g-1} \frac{1-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}s_j} {d-(p-g)-\ell}. $$
Including all unused zero slots maximizes every term with nonnegative numerator; negative terms cannot maximize $Q_i$, which is positive.

A scan computes this maximum in $O(g)$ arithmetic operations. Updating the sorted list also takes $O(g)$ operations. Since $g\le\min\{p,\tau,T\}$, the total count is
$$ O\!\left(K+p+T\min\{p,\tau\}\right), $$
using $O(K+\min\{p,\tau\})$ state entries.

This is a real-arithmetic count for exact real parameters.

For a rational parameter $d$ of binary length $P$, all allocations are rational. A conservative bound on intermediate operand length is
$$ H=O\!\left((T+1) \left[L+p\bigl(P+\log(p+1)\bigr)\right]\right). $$
For example, one can maintain common denominators, adding the incoming capacity denominator and the relevant denominator factors from $d-k$ at each update. Rational arithmetic with reduction has a conservative schoolbook bound of $O(H^3)$ per operation. Thus a complete bit-time bound is
$$ O\!\left( \left[K+p+T\min\{p,\tau\}\right]H^3 \right). $$

For $p>1$, rational bisection on $[p,2p-1]$ computes an upper approximation to $D_p$ within $2^{-n}$ using
$$ O\!\left(p[n+\log(p+1)]\right) $$
rational arithmetic operations, with operand lengths polynomial in $p$ and $n$. Taking
$$ d=\max\{K,\text{that upper approximation}\} $$
gives a feasible rational policy whose parameters converge to the minimum-fairness point. Exact attainment at an irrational root is a real-allocation statement; this approximation is the finite-bit implementation guarantee.

6.2. One profile and one class

The one-profile greedy policy uses $O(T)$ rational operations and constant allocation state.

For the one-class prefix-optimum policy, aggregate capacities by observed valuation and maintain them in sorted order. There are at most $\tau$ entries. Recomputing $F_s$ costs $O(\tau)$ operations per arrival, so the total is $O(T\tau)$ operations and $O(\tau)$ entries. Under a support bound, $\tau\le\min\{h,T\}$.

If a rational conservative approximation $C'\ge C$ has length $P$, the policy using $C'$ has guarantees $(C',C')$. A complete conservative bit-time bound for its online work is
$$ O\!\left(T\tau(TL+P)^3\right). $$

For additive accuracy $\varepsilon=2^{-n}$:

  • For finite $h\ge2$, bisect $\theta^{-1/(h-1)}$ from below to accuracy $\varepsilon/(h-1)$, then set

$$ C'=h-(h-1)t^-. $$
This gives $C\le C'\le C+\varepsilon$, with $P=O(n+\log(h+1))$, and preparation time polynomial in $h,L,n$. The case $h=1$ is exact.

  • For unrestricted support, a certified rational upper approximation to $\ln\theta$ gives the same relaxation. To obtain it, write $\theta=2^e t$ with $1\le t<2$, and use

$$ \ln t= 2\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty} \frac{z^{2\ell+1}}{2\ell+1}, \qquad z=\frac{t-1}{t+1}\le\frac13. $$
The same series with $z=1/3$ computes $\ln2$. Its geometric tail bound yields accuracy $\varepsilon$ with $O(n+\log(L+1))$ terms, hence polynomial preparation time and output length in $L,n$.

These preparation costs, together with the displayed online bit bound, account for the full rational implementation.
