The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\), common per-capita convex piecewise-linear bid costs and feasible sets, and rational polyhedral aggregate market constraints, define \(J_\mu(\alpha)\) as the minimum dispatch cost when only coalition mass \(\alpha_t\) of type \(t\) may participate. Compute the utility densities \((u_0,(u_t)_t)\) satisfying \(u_t\ge 0\), \(u_0+\sum_t\mu_tu_t=-J_\mu(\mu)\), and \(u_0+\sum_t\alpha_tu_t\ge -J_\mu(\alpha)\) for every proper \(\alpha\in[0,\mu]\), lexicographically maximizing \((\sum_t\mu_tu_t,-\sum_t\mu_t(u_t-v_t)^2)\), where \(v_t=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}[J_\mu(\mu-\delta e_t)-J_\mu(\mu)]/\delta\).

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a rational polytope \(X_t\subseteq\mathbb R_+^m\) containing \(0\), and a nonnegative convex piecewise-linear per-capita bid cost \(b_t:X_t\to\mathbb R_+\), given explicitly by rational affine pieces, with \(b_t(0)=0\); these data are common to all individuals of that type.
The input also gives a rational polyhedron \(P\) of aggregate dispatches \(z=(z_t)_t\) and auxiliary variables \(y\in\mathbb R^p\), and an explicitly represented rational convex piecewise-linear auxiliary cost \(d(z,y)\), which may be signed.
For coalition masses \(\alpha\in[0,\mu]\), define \(J_\mu(\alpha)=\min\{\sum_{t:\alpha_t>0}\alpha_t b_t(z_t/\alpha_t)+d(z,y):(z,y)\in P,\ z_t\in\alpha_t X_t\}\). At \(\alpha_t=0\), require \(z_t=0\) and assign zero bid cost; an infeasible dispatch has value \(+\infty\).
Assume \(J_\mu(\mu)\) is finite and attained, and, for every type, dispatch remains feasible with finite attained value after sufficiently small positive mass removals. Require the limits \(v_t=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}[J_\mu(\mu-\delta e_t)-J_\mu(\mu)]/\delta\) to be finite, where \(e_t\) is the coordinate unit vector.
The continuous maximum-payment core selection is the utility vector \((u_0,(u_t)_t)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R_+^\tau\) satisfying \(u_0+\sum_t\mu_tu_t=-J_\mu(\mu)\) and \(u_0+\sum_t\alpha_tu_t\geq-J_\mu(\alpha)\) for every \(\alpha\in[0,\mu]\setminus\{\mu\}\), including \(\alpha=0\), that lexicographically maximizes \((\sum_t\mu_tu_t,-\sum_t\mu_t(u_t-v_t)^2)\). The operator participates in every coalition, and \(u_t\) is utility per unit mass.

\(0\leq\alpha\leq\mu,\qquad (z,y)\in P,\)

\(A_tz_t\leq\alpha_th_t,\qquad s_t\geq a_{tk}^{\mathsf T}z_t+\beta_{tk}\alpha_t\quad\text{for every }t,k,\)

\(D\geq q_\ell^{\mathsf T}z+r_\ell^{\mathsf T}y+\gamma_\ell\quad\text{for every }\ell.\)

The variable \(D\) is unrestricted in sign.

For \(\alpha_t>0\), division by \(\alpha_t\) shows that the constraints for type \(t\) are exactly

\(z_t\in\alpha_tX_t,\qquad s_t\geq\alpha_tb_t(z_t/\alpha_t).\)

Indeed, a nonzero vector satisfying these inequalities would be a nonzero recession direction of the bounded polytope \(X_t\), which is impossible. The remaining cost constraints then give \(s_t\geq0\). Thus minimizing over \(s_t\) assigns precisely the required zero bid cost.

Consequently, for every fixed coalition mass vector,

\(J_\mu(\alpha)=\min\left\{\sum_ts_t+D:(\alpha,z,y,s,D)\in\mathcal Q\right\},\)

with value \(+\infty\) when the section is infeasible.

All sign restrictions are included in \(Mw\geq r\), so the coordinates of \(w\) can otherwise be treated as free variables.

3. Monotonicity, finiteness, and convexity

For \(0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq\mu\), every dispatch feasible at \(\alpha\) remains feasible at \(\beta\), with no greater cost.

To verify this, suppose first that \(\alpha_t>0\). Since \(X_t\) is convex and contains \(0\),

\(z_t/\beta_t=(\alpha_t/\beta_t)(z_t/\alpha_t)+(1-\alpha_t/\beta_t)0\in X_t.\)

Convexity and \(b_t(0)=0\) imply

\(\beta_tb_t(z_t/\beta_t)\leq\alpha_tb_t(z_t/\alpha_t).\)

If \(\alpha_t=0\), then \(z_t=0\), and both relevant bid costs are zero. The dispatch and auxiliary variables remain unchanged, so the constraints in \(P\) and the auxiliary cost are unchanged. Taking infima gives

\(J_\mu(\beta)\leq J_\mu(\alpha).\)

Set \(J_*=J_\mu(\mu)\). In particular, every feasible coalition has cost at least \(J_*\). Its dispatch LP therefore has a finite optimum, and the standard attainment theorem for linear programming gives an attained optimum. Thus no smaller feasible coalition has value \(-\infty\), even though \(d\) may be signed.

The extended-real function \(J_\mu\) is also convex. For two feasible coalition mass vectors, take their optimal lifted dispatches in \(\mathcal Q\). Every convex combination remains in \(\mathcal Q\), and its objective is the same convex combination of the objectives. Minimization proves the convexity inequality. When an endpoint is infeasible, that inequality is automatic.

\(u_0=-J_*-\mu^{\mathsf T}u.\)

\(u\geq0,\qquad\lambda\geq0,\qquad M^{\mathsf T}\lambda=c+E^{\mathsf T}u,\qquad r^{\mathsf T}\lambda=J_*+\mu^{\mathsf T}u.\)

5. Computing the marginal limits exactly

\(\delta u_t\leq J_\mu(\mu-\delta e_t)-J_*.\)

Dividing by \(\delta\) and taking the stipulated limit yields

\(u_t\leq v_t\quad\text{for every }u\in\mathcal C.\)

The reverse attainability statement also holds:

\(v_te_t\in\mathcal C.\)

\(f_t(s)=J_\mu(\mu-se_t)-J_*,\qquad 0\leq s\leq\mu_t.\)

This is an extended-real convex function with \(f_t(0)=0\). Whenever \(s>0\) and \(f_t(s)\) is finite, convexity gives, for \(0<h<s\),

\(f_t(h)\leq(h/s)f_t(s).\)

Taking \(h\downarrow0\) therefore proves

\(f_t(s)\geq sv_t.\)

Now take any feasible coalition \(\alpha\), and put \(x=\mu-\alpha\) and \(\beta=\mu-x_te_t\). Since \(\alpha\leq\beta\), monotonicity gives

\(J_\mu(\alpha)-J_*\geq J_\mu(\beta)-J_*=f_t(x_t)\geq v_tx_t.\)

For \(x_t=0\), the last inequality follows directly from monotonicity. Infeasible coalitions impose no restriction. Since \(v_t\geq0\), these inequalities prove \(v_te_t\in\mathcal C\).

We have consequently established the exact identity

\(v_t=\max\{u_t:(u,\lambda)\in\mathcal E\}.\)

Thus all marginal limits can be computed by \(\tau\) LPs, without numerical differentiation.

Moreover, \(0\in\mathcal C\) by monotonicity, and

\(\mathcal C\subseteq\prod_t[0,v_t].\)

The set \(\mathcal C\) is closed because it is an intersection of closed halfspaces; infeasible coalitions contribute vacuous constraints. The assumed finiteness of every \(v_t\) therefore makes \(\mathcal C\) nonempty and compact.

6. The lexicographic selection

\(S_*=\max\{\mu^{\mathsf T}u:(u,\lambda)\in\mathcal E\}.\)

Compactness of \(\mathcal C\) ensures that this maximum is finite and attained.

Then solve the convex quadratic program

\(\min\left\{\sum_t\mu_t(u_t-v_t)^2:(u,\lambda)\in\mathcal E,\ \mu^{\mathsf T}u=S_*\right\}.\)

Its projected feasible set is a nonempty compact face of \(\mathcal C\), so a minimum exists. Because every \(\mu_t>0\), its objective is strictly convex in \(u\). Consequently, the minimizing type utility vector is unique. The dual witness \(\lambda\) need not be unique.

This is a rational convex quadratic program with a positive semidefinite Hessian. The exact polynomial-time theorem of [Kozlov, Tarasov, and Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189) applies to rational convex quadratic objectives over rational polyhedra, including semidefinite Hessians and unbounded feasible polyhedra. Here feasibility and attainment have already been proved. It therefore returns an exact rational optimum in polynomial bit time.

Finally, set

\(u_0=-J_*-S_*.\)

These two optimization stages implement precisely the requested lexicographic objective.
