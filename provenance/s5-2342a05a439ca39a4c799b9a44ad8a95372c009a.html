The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type and good-class sets \(T\) and \(Q\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_q\), and rational nonnegative values \(w_t(q)\), find a finite-support distribution \(x_{t,B}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(B=(b_q)_{q\in Q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{Q}\) satisfying \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B}b_qx_{t,B}=\rho_q\), and, for every \(t\) and every bundle \(B\) with \(x_{t,B}>0\), \(v_t(B)+\sum_{t',B'}x_{t',B'}\ell_t(B')\ge V_t\), where \(v_t(B)=\sum_qb_qw_t(q)\), \(V_t=\sum_q\rho_qw_t(q)\), and \(\ell_t(B)=\min_{q:b_q>0}w_t(q)\) with \(\ell_t(\varnothing)=0\); output such an allocation or report infeasibility.

There are \(m=|Q|\) good classes, with binary-rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_q\ge0\) and values \(w_t(q)\ge0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; running times are measured in \(m,\tau,L\).

Partial progress: a polynomial algorithm for two-value profiles

The general polynomial-time versus NP-hardness question remains unresolved here. I prove a new special case allowing arbitrary support size, type masses, supplies, and overlapping sets of valued classes:

If each type’s valuations take at most one strictly positive value, a fair complete allocation can be computed in polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$.

The construction satisfies a stronger, pairwise inequality and uses at most $m+\tau$ occupied type–bundle pairs. I also prove that arbitrary instances admit arbitrarily accurate approximate allocations, while explaining why this does not establish exact feasibility.

1. A sufficient pairwise condition

For an allocation $x$, consider the condition
$$ v_t(B)\ge v_t(B')-\ell_t(B') $$
for every occupied pair $(t,B)$ and every occupied pair $(s,B')$.

This condition implies the fairness requirement in the question. Indeed, fixing an occupied pair $(t,B)$, multiplying the inequalities by $x_{s,B'}$, and summing gives
$$ v_t(B)\sum_{s,B'}x_{s,B'} \ge \sum_{s,B'}x_{s,B'}v_t(B') - \sum_{s,B'}x_{s,B'}\ell_t(B'). $$
The total allocation mass is $1$. Complete allocation and additivity give
$$ \sum_{s,B'}x_{s,B'}v_t(B') = \sum_q w_t(q)\sum_{s,B'}b'_q x_{s,B'} = \sum_q\rho_qw_t(q) = V_t. $$
Consequently,
$$ v_t(B)+\sum_{s,B'}x_{s,B'}\ell_t(B')\ge V_t. $$

The argument includes empty bundles and zero-valued goods.

2. A rounding lemma that controls bundle size

Lemma. Given a nonnegative rational vector $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_d)$, one can compute a rational distribution on at most $d+1$ integral vectors $Y$ such that
$$ \mathbb E[Y_j]=a_j, $$
$$ Y_j\in\{\lfloor a_j\rfloor,\lceil a_j\rceil\}, $$
and
$$ \sum_jY_j \in \left\{ \left\lfloor\sum_j a_j\right\rfloor, \left\lceil\sum_j a_j\right\rceil \right\}. $$
Coordinates with $a_j=0$ remain zero. The construction takes polynomial time in the binary encoding of $a$.

Proof. Write
$$ a_j=n_j+f_j, \qquad n_j=\lfloor a_j\rfloor, \qquad 0\le f_j<1, $$
and define
$$ F_0=0, \qquad F_j=\sum_{h=1}^j f_h. $$
For $u\in[0,1)$, put
$$ Y_j(u) = n_j+\lfloor F_j+u\rfloor-\lfloor F_{j-1}+u\rfloor. $$
Because $F_j-F_{j-1}=f_j\in[0,1)$, the difference of floors belongs to $\{0,1\}$. If $f_j=0$, it is always zero.

For every nonnegative real number $z$,
$$ \int_0^1\lfloor z+u\rfloor\,du=z. $$
This follows by writing $z=k+\theta$, with $k$ integral and $0\le\theta<1$: the integrand is $k$ on an interval of length $1-\theta$ and $k+1$ on an interval of length $\theta$. Hence
$$ \mathbb E[Y_j] = n_j+F_j-F_{j-1} = a_j. $$
Also,
$$ \sum_jY_j(u) = \sum_jn_j+\lfloor F_d+u\rfloor, $$
which has the required two possible totals.

Each function $\lfloor F_j+u\rfloor$ has at most one breakpoint in $(0,1)$. These breakpoints partition $[0,1)$ into at most $d+1$ intervals. On each interval, the entire vector $Y(u)$ is constant. Taking interval lengths as probabilities gives the claimed finite rational distribution.

All breakpoints, probabilities, and coordinates are obtained by polynomially many rational arithmetic operations and have polynomial bit length. This proves the lemma.

3. Polynomial-time allocation for two-value profiles

3.1 Statement and normalization

Theorem. Suppose that, for every type $t$, there is a positive rational number $c_t$ such that
$$ w_t(q)\in\{0,c_t\} \qquad(q\in Q). $$
Then a fair complete allocation can be computed in polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$. It can be chosen to satisfy the pairwise condition of Section 1 and to have at most $m+\tau$ occupied type–bundle pairs.

An all-zero valuation row is included by choosing $c_t=1$. The hypothesis is polynomial-time checkable: all strictly positive entries in a row must be equal.

Define the liked classes of type $t$ by
$$ A_t=\{q:w_t(q)>0\}, $$
and its normalized bundle value by
$$ u_t(B)=\sum_{q\in A_t}b_q. $$
Thus
$$ v_t(B)=c_tu_t(B). $$
For normalized valuations, the least-good value of a nonempty bundle is $1$ if every present class is liked, and $0$ otherwise.

Let
$$ Q^+=\bigcup_t A_t, \qquad Q^0=Q\setminus Q^+. $$
Every class in $Q^0$ is valued at zero by every type.

We first allocate the supplies in $Q^+$.

3.2 A fractional flow with an ordering property

For every $q\in A_t$, introduce a nonnegative rational flow variable $g_{tq}$. Require
$$ \sum_{t:q\in A_t}g_{tq}=\rho_q \qquad(q\in Q^+). $$
Here $g_{tq}$ is the mass of copies of class $q$ assigned to type $t$.

Define the expected number of liked goods per recipient of type $t$ by
$$ r_t=\frac{\sum_{q\in A_t}g_{tq}}{\mu_t}. $$
Choose a flow minimizing
$$ \Phi(g) = \sum_t\frac{\left(\sum_{q\in A_t}g_{tq}\right)^2}{\mu_t} = \sum_t\mu_t r_t^2. $$

This optimization is an explicitly listed rational convex quadratic program with at most $m\tau$ variables. Its feasible region is nonempty: assign each useful class to any type liking it. It is bounded because
$$ 0\le g_{tq}\le\rho_q. $$
Its Hessian is positive semidefinite, since the objective is a sum of positive multiples of squares of linear forms.

I use the standard exact rational convex quadratic programming theorem: a convex quadratic objective with rational positive semidefinite Hessian, subject to rational linear constraints, can be optimized exactly in polynomial bit time; when an optimum exists, an optimal rational point of polynomial encoding length can be returned. The preceding observations check its hypotheses here. This is the theorem of [Kozlov–Tarasov–Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189).

The optimal flow has the following key property:
$$ g_{sq}>0,\quad q\in A_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_s\le r_t. $$

To prove it, suppose instead that $s\ne t$, $g_{sq}>0$, $q\in A_t$, and $r_s>r_t$. Transfer a positive amount $\delta$ of class $q$ from $s$ to $t$. For sufficiently small $\delta$, this preserves feasibility. The objective change is
$$ 2\delta(r_t-r_s) + \delta^2\left(\frac1{\mu_t}+\frac1{\mu_s}\right). $$
Its linear coefficient is negative, so the change is negative for sufficiently small positive $\delta$. This contradicts optimality. The case $s=t$ is immediate.

3.3 Rounding separately within each type

For type $t$, apply the rounding lemma to the vector
$$ a_{tq}=\frac{g_{tq}}{\mu_t} \qquad(q\in Q^+), $$
taking absent flow coordinates to be zero.

Let $p_{t,B}$ be the resulting distribution and set
$$ x_{t,B}=\mu_t p_{t,B}. $$
Every occupied bundle of type $t$ contains only classes in $A_t$, because zero mean coordinates remain zero. Therefore
$$ u_t(B)=|B|, \qquad |B|=\sum_qb_q. $$
The size guarantee in the lemma gives
$$ |B|\in\{\lfloor r_t\rfloor,\lceil r_t\rceil\}. $$
Moreover, positive multiplicity of class $q$ in an occupied bundle of type $s$ implies
$$ g_{sq}>0. $$

Type masses are correct, and every useful supply is allocated exactly:
$$ \sum_{t,B}b_qx_{t,B} = \sum_t\mu_t\frac{g_{tq}}{\mu_t} = \rho_q. $$

3.4 Proof of the pairwise condition

Fix any occupied recipient bundle $(t,B)$ and any occupied comparison bundle $(s,B')$.

If type $t$ likes no class present in $B'$, then $u_t(B')=0$, and the normalized pairwise inequality holds by nonnegativity.

Otherwise, some present class $q$ belongs to $A_t$. Since $B'$ is occupied by type $s$, we have $g_{sq}>0$. The flow ordering property yields
$$ r_s\le r_t. $$
Consequently,
$$ |B'| \le \lceil r_s\rceil \le \lfloor r_t\rfloor+1, $$
whereas
$$ u_t(B)=|B|\ge\lfloor r_t\rfloor. $$

There are two cases.

Every present class of $B'$ is liked by $t$. Then its normalized least-good value is $1$, and
$$ u_t(B')-1 = |B'|-1 \le \lfloor r_t\rfloor \le u_t(B). $$

Some present class of $B'$ is disliked by $t$. At least one copy contributes zero to $u_t(B')$, so
$$ u_t(B') \le |B'|-1 \le \lfloor r_t\rfloor \le u_t(B). $$
Its normalized least-good value is zero.

These cases prove the normalized pairwise inequality. Multiplying by $c_t>0$ gives
$$ v_t(B)\ge v_t(B')-\ell_t(B'). $$

3.5 Allocating universally zero-valued classes

The supplies in $Q^0$ remain to be allocated.

Among the currently occupied bundles, choose one, say $(s,B_*)$, with minimum size
$$ k=|B_*|. $$
Let its positive mass be $p$.

Allocate all supplies in $Q^0$ within this mass. Specifically, apply the rounding lemma to conditional means
$$ a_q=\frac{\rho_q}{p} \qquad(q\in Q^0), $$
and replace mass $p$ on $B_*$ by the resulting masses on bundles $B_*+Y$.

This allocates all remaining supplies exactly. Every recipient’s value of her own bundle is unchanged, since all added classes are universally zero-valued.

Before this modification, every occupied recipient had normalized own value equal to bundle size and therefore at least $k$. This remains true afterward. For every observer $t$ and every modified comparison bundle,
$$ u_t(B_*+Y)=u_t(B_*)\le |B_*|=k. $$
Thus every occupied recipient has normalized own value at least the value of every modified comparison bundle. Since least-good values are nonnegative, the pairwise inequality holds for these comparisons.

Comparisons with unmodified bundles retain their previous inequalities: recipient values have not changed, and the unmodified comparison bundles have not changed.

Therefore the complete allocation still satisfies the pairwise condition, and Section 1 proves the required fairness.

3.6 Running time, bit length, and support

The exact quadratic program returns flows of polynomial bit length. Dividing by positive input masses preserves polynomial bit length. The rounding lemma uses polynomially many arithmetic operations, so the initial allocation has polynomial encoding length and at most
$$ \tau(|Q^+|+1) $$
occupied pairs.

The zero-valued-class step replaces one pair by at most $|Q^0|+1$ pairs. Its conditional means also have polynomial bit length, since $p$ is a positive rational of polynomial encoding length.

To obtain the sharper support bound, list the constructed pairs $(t_j,B_j)$ and consider their moment columns
$$ c_j=(e_{t_j},B_j)\in\mathbb Q^{\tau+m}. $$
The nonnegative moment system
$$ \sum_jz_jc_j=(\mu,\rho), \qquad z_j\ge0, $$
is nonempty and bounded: its type equations imply $z_j\le\mu_{t_j}$.

Standard exact rational linear programming can return a vertex of a nonempty bounded rational polyhedron in polynomial bit time. One way to obtain a vertex is to minimize coordinates successively, fixing each optimum; each step restricts to a face, and the final point is unique. All intermediate optimum coordinates have polynomial bit length, because they occur at vertices of the original rational polyhedron and satisfy determinant bounds.

A vertex of this moment system has at most $m+\tau$ positive coordinates. Indeed, if more columns were positive, they would be linearly dependent, and a sufficiently small perturbation in both directions along a dependence would preserve feasibility, contradicting extremality.

This compression retains only previously occupied pairs. The pairwise condition therefore survives. Type masses and all supplies are preserved, so fairness follows again from Section 1.

If there are no good classes, assigning each type its mass on the empty bundle handles the instance directly. The same construction also covers all-zero valuation profiles.

This completes the theorem.

4. Arbitrarily accurate allocations for arbitrary valuations

For general valuations, the finite-agent theorem does yield approximate existence.

Proposition. For every valid instance and every positive rational $\varepsilon$, there is a complete rational allocation satisfying
$$ v_t(B)+\sum_{s,B'}x_{s,B'}\ell_t(B') \ge V_t-\varepsilon $$
for every occupied pair $(t,B)$.

Moreover, such an allocation exists with at most $m+2\tau$ occupied pairs and polynomial encoding length in $m,\tau,L$ and the encoding length of $\varepsilon$.

Proof. The empty-class case is immediate. Otherwise set
$$ M=\max_{t,q}w_t(q). $$
Choose an integer $n\ge2$ clearing all denominators of the masses and supplies and satisfying
$$ \frac{M}{n-1}\le\varepsilon. $$
Such an $n$ can be chosen with polynomial binary length: take a common denominator $D$ and a sufficiently large multiple of $D$.

Create $n\mu_t$ agents of type $t$ and $n\rho_q$ individual goods of class $q$. These counts are integral. The agents have nonnegative additive valuations, so the [Kobayashi–Mahara finite-agent theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.00236v3) applies and gives a complete PROPavg allocation.

Aggregate recipients by their type and bundle, assigning mass $1/n$ per recipient. For observer type $t$, write its whole-society correction as
$$ C_t=\frac1n\sum_i\ell_t(B_i). $$
For a recipient of type $t$ holding $B$, the other-agent correction is
$$ \frac{nC_t-\ell_t(B)}{n-1}. $$
The proportional share is exactly $V_t$, because the finite society contains total value $nV_t$ for that observer. Hence finite-agent fairness gives
$$ v_t(B)+C_t \ge V_t-\frac{C_t-\ell_t(B)}{n-1}. $$
Since $C_t\le M$ and $\ell_t(B)\ge0$,
$$ v_t(B)+C_t \ge V_t-\frac{M}{n-1} \ge V_t-\varepsilon. $$

For the support bound, associate each occupied pair with the column
$$ \left(e_t,B,(\ell_s(B))_{s\in T}\right) \in\mathbb Q^{m+2\tau}. $$
A vertex of the nonnegative moment system preserving type masses, supplies, and all corrections has at most $m+2\tau$ positive entries. Every retained bundle remains fair against the same corrections, with the same error $\varepsilon$.

Finally, every original bundle coordinate is bounded by $n\rho_q$, which has polynomial bit length. Each correction has denominator dividing $n$ times a common denominator of the input valuations. Thus the moment-system coefficients and right-hand side have polynomial bit length. Clearing denominators and applying Cramer’s rule to a vertex gives polynomial bit length for its masses as well.

This proves the proposition.

The expanded finite society can have exponentially many agents and goods. Consequently, this proposition does not provide a polynomial-time algorithm in the original binary input length.

5. Why approximate existence does not settle exact feasibility

A limit argument must preserve supplies despite unrestricted bundle sizes. Coordinatewise convergence of allocation masses does not ensure this.

For example, take two types $a,b$, both of mass $1/2$, and two classes $q,r$, with
$$ \rho_q=2,\qquad \rho_r=1, $$
$$ w_a(q)=w_a(r)=1, \qquad w_b(q)=w_b(r)=0. $$
For every integer $n\ge2$, define
$$ x_{a,(4,0)}=\frac12, \qquad x_{b,(0,n)}=\frac1n, \qquad x_{b,\varnothing}=\frac12-\frac1n, $$
omitting zero entries.

This allocates both supplies exactly. Type $a$ has threshold $V_a=3$ and receives value $4$, so its fairness inequality holds. All type-$b$ inequalities are trivial.

Nevertheless, as $n$ tends to infinity, the mass carrying class $r$ vanishes. The coordinatewise limiting distribution puts mass $1/2$ on $(a,(4,0))$ and mass $1/2$ on $(b,\varnothing)$, allocating no copies of $r$.

This example is feasible and does not refute universal existence. It shows that even a sequence of exact fair allocations can lose supply in its limit. Thus the approximate-existence proposition cannot, by itself, establish exact feasibility.

6. Remaining open points

The theorem above resolves the computational problem for arbitrary two-value profiles, including different positive values across types and arbitrary overlapping liked sets.

For unrestricted valuations, this argument establishes neither a polynomial-time algorithm nor NP-hardness. It also leaves universal exact feasibility open.
