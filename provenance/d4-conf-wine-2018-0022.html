The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, per-capita supply \(s\), and explicit normalized, nondecreasing, subadditive valuation tables \(v_t(0),\ldots,v_t(Q)\), define \(\mathrm{OPT}\) as the maximum of \(\sum_{t,q} y_{t,q}v_t(q)\), subject to \(\sum_q y_{t,q}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,q} qy_{t,q}\le s\), and \(y_{t,q}\ge0\). Does there exist a uniform per-item price \(p\), computable from the instance, such that every measurable arrival schedule with type masses \(\mu_t\), and every adversarial resolution of ties, yields welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/3\)? Each infinitesimal buyer chooses an integral bundle size maximizing \(v_t(q)-pq\), subject to available stock; aggregate stock and welfare evolve with arriving mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(m:=Q\ge1\) be the maximum integral bundle size, let \(s>0\) be rational per-capita supply, and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type has an explicit nonnegative rational valuation table \(v_t(0),\ldots,v_t(m)\), normalized, nondecreasing, and subadditive. Define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max\{\sum_{t,q}y_{t,q}v_t(q):\sum_qy_{t,q}=\mu_t,\ \sum_{t,q}qy_{t,q}\le s,\ y_{t,q}\ge0\}\), with \(q\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\).
An arrival schedule is a measurable map \(a:[0,1]\to T\) satisfying \(\lambda(a^{-1}(t))=\mu_t\), where \(\lambda\) is Lebesgue measure. A uniform static price \(p\ge0\) is posted before arrivals.
An outcome consists of measurable integral choices \(q(x)\) and absolutely continuous remaining stock \(R(x)\ge0\), with \(R(0)=s\) and \(R'(x)=-q(x)\) almost everywhere. While \(R(x)>0\), choose any \(q(x)\in\arg\max_{0\le j\le m}\{v_{a(x)}(j)-pj\}\); after exhaustion choose \(q(x)=0\). Thus a mass element \(dx\) choosing \(q\) consumes \(q\,dx\), and exhaustion can split arriving type mass.
Welfare is \(W=\int_0^1v_{a(x)}(q(x))\,dx\); all measurable resolutions of demand ties are adversarial.

Does every instance admit a nonnegative rational uniform static price \(p\), computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), guaranteeing \(W\ge\mathrm{OPT}/3\) for every arrival schedule and every adversarial resolution of ties?

For finite buyers with symmetric subadditive valuations, uniform static prices guarantee \(1/3\) of optimal welfare, and no larger constant is universally guaranteed. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.06623)
No result settling this precise nonatomic approximation question was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made valuation entries nonnegative binary rationals and supply a positive binary rational, so computational input size is defined.
  • Identified m with the table endpoint Q, required Q to be a positive integer, and retained the given mass normalization.
  • Formalized measurable arrivals on the unit interval with Lebesgue type masses.
  • Interpreted an infinitesimal bundle choice as a per-capita integral quantity: positive aggregate stock permits every listed bundle, consumption scales with arriving mass, and service stops exactly at exhaustion.
  • Restricted computed prices to nonnegative rationals and made the algorithmic requirement polynomial in m, tau, and total input bit length L.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Yes. The following construction gives the requested guarantee in polynomial time. In fact, the proof does not require subadditivity.

1. Demand and a critical price

For each type \(t\) and price \(z\ge 0\), define
\[ U_t(z):=\max_{0\le q\le m}\{v_t(q)-zq\}, \qquad D_t(z):=\arg\max_{0\le q\le m}\{v_t(q)-zq\}. \]
Write \(d_t^-(z):=\min D_t(z)\) and \(d_t^+(z):=\max D_t(z)\), and define
\[ F(z):=sz+\sum_t\mu_t U_t(z). \]

For every feasible allocation \(y\) in the definition of \(\mathrm{OPT}\), the inequality \(v_t(q)\le U_t(z)+zq\) gives
\[ \sum_{t,q}y_{t,q}v_t(q) \le \sum_t\mu_tU_t(z)+z\sum_{t,q}qy_{t,q} \le F(z). \]
Consequently, \(\mathrm{OPT}\le F(z)\) for every \(z\ge0\).

We can compute a rational minimizer \(b\) of \(F\) by evaluating it at the finite set
\[ \mathcal B:= \{0\}\cup \left\{ \frac{v_t(k)-v_t(j)}{k-j}: t\in T,\ 0\le j<k\le m \right\}. \]
Every member is nonnegative by monotonicity. All changes in the maximizing affine functions defining \(U_t\) occur at members of \(\mathcal B\). Thus \(F\) is affine between consecutive members. Beyond the largest member, every type uniquely demands zero, so \(F(z)=sz\), which is strictly increasing. It follows that a global minimum occurs in \(\mathcal B\).

Set
\[ r_t:=d_t^-(b),\qquad h_t:=d_t^+(b),\qquad C:=\sum_t\mu_t r_t,\qquad H:=\sum_t\mu_t h_t. \]
Then
\[ C\le s, \qquad\text{and, if }b>0,\qquad s\le H. \]

Here is an explicit justification. At price \(b\), every nonmaximizing quantity has a strictly positive utility gap. Since there are finitely many quantities, these gaps remain positive under sufficiently small price changes. Among the quantities maximizing at \(b\), increasing the price selects \(r_t\), whereas decreasing it selects \(h_t\). Therefore, for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[ F(b+\varepsilon)=F(b)+\varepsilon(s-C). \]
Minimality gives \(C\le s\). If \(b>0\), we can also take \(\varepsilon<b\), obtaining
\[ F(b-\varepsilon)=F(b)+\varepsilon(H-s). \]
Minimality then gives \(H\ge s\).

Finally, define
\[ A:=\sum_t\mu_t v_t(r_t). \]
Since \(r_t\in D_t(b)\), we have \(U_t(b)=v_t(r_t)-br_t\). Hence
\[ \mathrm{OPT}\le F(b)=A+b(s-C)\le A+bs. \tag{1} \]

2. A rational price guaranteeing welfare \(A\)

We construct a price \(p_{\mathrm{high}}>b\) at which every type uniquely demands \(r_t\).

For every \(t\) and \(j<r_t\), let
\[ g_{t,j}:=U_t(b)-\bigl(v_t(j)-bj\bigr). \]
These gaps are strictly positive: equality would put \(j\) in \(D_t(b)\), contradicting the minimality of \(r_t\).

Choose
\[ \delta:= \min\left( \{1\}\cup \left\{ \frac{g_{t,j}}{2(r_t-j)}: t\in T,\ 0\le j<r_t \right\} \right), \qquad p_{\mathrm{high}}:=b+\delta. \]
This also defines \(\delta=1\) when the second set is empty.

For \(j<r_t\), the utility advantage of \(r_t\) over \(j\) at \(p_{\mathrm{high}}\) is
\[ g_{t,j}-\delta(r_t-j)\ge g_{t,j}/2>0. \]
For \(j>r_t\), that advantage is
\[ U_t(b)-\bigl(v_t(j)-bj\bigr)+\delta(j-r_t)>0. \]
Thus \(D_t(p_{\mathrm{high}})=\{r_t\}\).

Fix any arrival schedule and any outcome at this price. The total consumption if everyone receives their demand is
\[ \int_0^1r_{a(x)}\,dx=C\le s. \]
We claim that the outcome gives \(q(x)=r_{a(x)}\) almost everywhere.

Indeed, absolute continuity and the stock equation imply
\[ R(x)=s-\int_0^xq(u)\,du. \]
Thus \(R\) is continuous and nonincreasing. Before exhaustion, uniqueness of demand forces \(q(x)=r_{a(x)}\). If exhaustion occurs at some \(e<1\), then
\[ s=\int_0^e r_{a(x)}\,dx \le \int_0^1r_{a(x)}\,dx=C\le s. \]
All inequalities are therefore equalities, and \(\int_e^1r_{a(x)}\,dx=0\). Since this integrand is nonnegative, it vanishes almost everywhere after exhaustion. The claim follows; exhaustion only at the endpoint has no effect on the integral.

Consequently, every outcome at \(p_{\mathrm{high}}\) has welfare
\[ W=\int_0^1v_{a(x)}(r_{a(x)})\,dx =\sum_t\mu_t v_t(r_t)=A. \tag{2} \]

If \(b=0\), inequality (1) already gives \(\mathrm{OPT}\le A\). Therefore \(p_{\mathrm{high}}\) settles this case.

3. A second price guaranteeing welfare \(bs/2\)

Suppose now that \(b>0\), and set \(p_{\mathrm{low}}:=b/2\).

We first establish demand monotonicity, including arbitrary ties. If \(z_1<z_2\), \(q_1\in D_t(z_1)\), and \(q_2\in D_t(z_2)\), optimality gives
\[ v_t(q_1)-z_1q_1\ge v_t(q_2)-z_1q_2, \]
\[ v_t(q_2)-z_2q_2\ge v_t(q_1)-z_2q_1. \]
Adding yields \((z_2-z_1)(q_1-q_2)\ge0\), so \(q_1\ge q_2\).

In particular, every quantity demanded by type \(t\) at \(p_{\mathrm{low}}\) is at least \(h_t\).

Every outcome at \(p_{\mathrm{low}}\) exhausts supply by time \(1\). To prove this, suppose instead that \(R(1)>0\). Monotonicity of stock then implies \(R(x)>0\) throughout the interval. Hence
\[ \int_0^1q(x)\,dx \ge\int_0^1h_{a(x)}\,dx =H\ge s. \]
But the stock equation gives \(\int_0^1q(x)\,dx=s-R(1)<s\), a contradiction.

While stock is positive, demand optimality against the zero bundle, together with \(v_t(0)=0\), gives \(v_{a(x)}(q(x))\ge p_{\mathrm{low}}q(x)\). After exhaustion both quantities are zero. Integrating and using \(R(1)=0\), we obtain
\[ W\ge p_{\mathrm{low}}\int_0^1q(x)\,dx =\frac{bs}{2}. \tag{3} \]
This bound holds for every schedule and every measurable resolution of ties.

4. Price selection and polynomial running time

When \(b=0\), output \(p_{\mathrm{high}}\). Otherwise, output
\[ p:= \begin{cases} p_{\mathrm{high}},& A\ge bs/2,\\ p_{\mathrm{low}},& A<bs/2. \end{cases} \]
Equations (2) and (3) guarantee
\[ W\ge M:=\max\{A,bs/2\}. \]
Since \(A\le M\) and \(bs\le2M\), inequality (1) implies
\[ \mathrm{OPT}\le A+bs\le3M\le3W. \]
Thus the selected price guarantees \(W\ge\mathrm{OPT}/3\).

The set \(\mathcal B\) has \(O(\tau m^2)\) members. Evaluating \(F\) at each member requires \(O(\tau m)\) rational operations. Computing \(r_t,h_t,A,C,H\), and \(\delta\) requires polynomially many further operations. Every candidate price is formed from input rationals by differences and division by integers of magnitude at most \(m\); all subsequent sums, comparisons, and price calculations have polynomial bit length in \(m,\tau,L\). Exact rational arithmetic therefore implements the entire construction in polynomial time.

The output is a nonnegative rational price, and the proof covers all the instances in the question.
