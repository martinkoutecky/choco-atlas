The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of shareholder types \(T\), each specifying ownership density \(q_t\) and value \(v_t\), and a rational population measure \(\mu\) with \(\sum_t\mu_tq_t=1\), can one construct in time polynomial in the input size a finite-description anonymous direct mechanism? For every aggregate report measure, the mechanism assigns each reported type an ownership density \(z_t\) and scaled payment \(p_t\), conserves the asset and money, is dominant-strategy truthful and ex-post individually rational relative to initial ownership, and achieves expected aggregate welfare at least \(1-\frac{1}{e}\) times the full-information optimum.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless population of total mass \(1\), given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There is one divisible asset, so \(m=1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type \(t=(q_t,v_t)\) has public initial ownership density \(q_t\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\) and private linear value \(v_t\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\), with \(\sum_t\mu_tq_t=1\). The designer knows \(T\) and \(\mu\); only values are reported, and reports must lie in \(T\) and preserve the reporter's public ownership density.
An admissible aggregate report measure \(\nu\) has nonnegative rational masses and preserves every ownership-class mass: \(\sum_{t:q_t=q}\nu_t=\sum_{t:q_t=q}\mu_t\). An anonymous direct mechanism specifies ownership densities \(z_t(\nu)\geq0\) and signed payment densities \(p_t(\nu)\in\mathbb R\), including menus for reports with zero mass; positive payments are charges.
For every admissible \(\nu\) and every realization of mechanism randomness, require \(\sum_t\nu_tz_t(\nu)=1\) and \(\sum_t\nu_tp_t(\nu)=0\). A type's utility density is \(v_tz_t-p_t\), and its initial utility density is \(v_tq_t\); densities have no individual upper bound.
An individual's deviation leaves \(\nu\) unchanged. Require dominant-strategy truthfulness and ex-post individual rationality for every random realization: \(v_tz_t(\nu)-p_t(\nu)\geq v_tz_s(\nu)-p_s(\nu)\) whenever \(q_s=q_t\), and \(v_tz_t(\nu)-p_t(\nu)\geq v_tq_t\), for all \(t\) and admissible \(\nu\).
At truthful reports, welfare is \(W=\sum_t\mu_tv_tz_t(\mu)\), and the full-information optimum is \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_{t\in T}v_t\). Expectation is over mechanism randomness only.
A finite description means a finite binary program computing the allocation and payment densities on rational aggregate reports; construction and expected evaluation time must be polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and, for evaluation, the report bit length.

Can such mechanisms be constructed in polynomial time for every input society while guaranteeing \(\mathbb E[W]\geq(1-\frac1e)\mathrm{OPT}\), or is this approximation guarantee unattainable under these requirements?

For finitely many shareholders with public shares and independently distributed private linear values, a DSIC, ex-post individually rational, strongly budget-balanced mechanism achieves expected welfare at least \(1-\frac1e\) times expected optimal welfare. [Blumrosen and Dobzinski](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04876)
No result settling this exact anonymous atomless construction problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized population mass to one, retaining the stated unit aggregate ownership normalization; ownership and value densities are nonnegative binary rationals.
  • Treated ownership as public and values as private, matching the discrete partnership model; reports are restricted to supported types with the same ownership density.
  • Restricted aggregate report inputs to rational measures preserving the public ownership-class masses.
  • Interpreted the population as atomless: unilateral individual deviations do not change the aggregate report measure, and report menus remain defined at zero report mass.
  • Interpreted scaled payments as signed payment densities, positive for charges, with quasi-linear utility and strong budget balance.
  • Allowed arbitrary nonnegative ownership densities rather than imposing the finite-agent upper bound of one on a density.
  • Required truthfulness, individual rationality, and conservation for each realization of randomness, consistent with the posted-price mechanisms underlying the anchor.
  • Interpreted the given measure as the realized society rather than an unspecified prior over societies; welfare expectation is over mechanism randomness only.
  • Made finite description computationally meaningful by requiring a finite binary evaluator with polynomial expected evaluation time on rational reports.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Yes. Under the stated atomless deviation model, a deterministic mechanism can achieve welfare exactly equal to \(\mathrm{OPT}\), with polynomial construction and evaluation time.

Construction. Fix any admissible aggregate report measure \(\nu\). Preservation of every ownership-class mass implies
\[ \sum_t\nu_t=1, \qquad \sum_t\nu_tq_t=1. \]
Define the highest value with positive report mass, its total mass, and its initial ownership by
\[ B=\max\{v_t:\nu_t>0\}, \qquad M=\sum_{t:v_t=B}\nu_t, \qquad Q=\sum_{t:v_t=B}\nu_tq_t. \]
These quantities are well defined: total report mass is positive, \(M>0\), and \(0\le Q\le1\). Set
\[ D=\frac{1-Q}{M}\ge0. \]

For every report \(t\), including reports with zero mass, define
\[ z_t= \begin{cases} 0,&v_t<B,\\ q_t+D,&v_t\ge B, \end{cases} \qquad p_t=B(z_t-q_t). \]
Thus reports below the price \(B\) sell their initial ownership, while reports at or above that price retain their initial ownership and receive an additional density \(D\).

The use of \(v_t\ge B\) is essential: a zero-mass report can have value above \(B\), and its menu must also satisfy the required constraints.

Feasibility and strong budget balance. No positive-mass report has value above \(B\). Consequently,
\[ \sum_t\nu_tz_t = \sum_{t:v_t=B}\nu_t(q_t+D) = Q+MD = 1. \]
Allocations are nonnegative because \(q_t\ge0\) and \(D\ge0\). Moreover,
\[ \sum_t\nu_tp_t = B\left(\sum_t\nu_tz_t-\sum_t\nu_tq_t\right) = B(1-1) = 0. \]
Both equalities hold at every admissible aggregate.

Dominant-strategy truthfulness. Fix an admissible \(\nu\), a true type \(t\) with value \(v=v_t\) and ownership density \(q=q_t\), and an allowed alternative report \(s\). The ownership restriction gives \(q_s=q\).

An individual deviation leaves \(\nu\) unchanged, so it leaves \(B\) and \(D\) unchanged. The true type’s utility from reporting \(s\) is therefore
\[ vz_s-p_s=(v-B)z_s+Bq. \]
Within ownership class \(q\), every report below \(B\) has allocation \(0\), and every report at or above \(B\) has allocation \(q+D\).

If \(v<B\), the coefficient \(v-B\) is negative, so truthful reporting selects the smaller allocation and maximizes utility. If \(v>B\), that coefficient is positive, so truthful reporting selects the larger allocation and maximizes utility. If \(v=B\), utility equals \(Bq\) under every allowed report.

Hence all required truthfulness inequalities hold, including those involving zero-mass reports.

Ex-post individual rationality. If \(v_t<B\), truthful utility is
\[ v_tz_t-p_t=Bq_t\ge v_tq_t. \]
If \(v_t\ge B\), truthful utility is
\[ v_t(q_t+D)-BD = v_tq_t+(v_t-B)D \ge v_tq_t. \]
These arguments apply to every type at every admissible aggregate. The mechanism is deterministic, so they also establish the requirements for every realization of randomness.

Exact welfare at truthful reports. Let \(H=\max_{t\in T}v_t=\mathrm{OPT}\). Every input type has positive truthful mass, so at \(\nu=\mu\) we have \(B=H\). Only types with value \(H\) receive positive allocations. Feasibility therefore gives
\[ W = \sum_t\mu_tv_tz_t(\mu) = H\sum_t\mu_tz_t(\mu) = H = \mathrm{OPT}. \]
In particular,
\[ \mathbb E[W]=\mathrm{OPT}\ge \left(1-\frac1e\right)\mathrm{OPT}. \]

Finite description and polynomial running time. The constructor outputs a fixed rational-arithmetic program containing the input type list. Evaluation scans the report masses to compute \(B\), \(M\), and \(Q\), performs the single division defining \(D\), and outputs the allocation and payment formulas above.

All outputs are rational. If the aggregate report has bit length \(R\), the intermediate and output rationals have bit lengths polynomial in \(L+R+\tau\): sums and products can be represented using common denominators whose bit lengths are bounded by sums of the input denominator lengths, and division by the nonzero rational \(M\) preserves polynomial bit length. Standard exact rational arithmetic therefore yields polynomial construction and evaluation time.

Allocation densities may be large when \(M\) is small, but their binary descriptions remain polynomial in length, and the problem explicitly permits unbounded individual densities.

The decisive premise is that an individual cannot change the aggregate, hence cannot change the price \(B\). That premise permits the exact construction above; the [finite-shareholder result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04876) is not needed.
