1. Aggregating the type masses

Write
$$ X_t=\mu_t x_t,\qquad Y_t=\mu_t y_t,\qquad B_t=\mu_t b_t. $$

The utility function is positively homogeneous:
$$ U_t(\gamma x,\gamma y)=\gamma U_t(x,y) \qquad(\gamma\geq 0). $$

Consequently, multiplication by $\mu_t$ maps bundles affordable with per-buyer budget $b_t$ bijectively to aggregate bundles affordable with budget $B_t$. It preserves utility maximization and exact expenditure. The capacity constraints become
$$ \sum_tX_{t,i,r}\leq \bar D^{\mathrm{MEC}}_{i,r}, \qquad \sum_tY_{t,c}\leq \bar D^{\mathrm{RAN}}_c. $$

Thus the mass model is exactly equivalent to a finite Fisher market with $\tau$ aggregate buyers. This transformation has polynomial bit complexity and requires no replication of buyers.

All subsequent utility variables refer to aggregate utilities.

2. A quadratic characterization of equilibrium

This section establishes existence, uniqueness of equilibrium utilities, and a polynomial-size algebraic formulation for arbitrary instances.

Abbreviate the supplies by $D_{i,r}$ and $D_c$. Let $p_{i,r}$ and $\eta_c$ be compute and radio prices. Define the costs of executing or uploading one unit of utility by
$$ C_{t,i}=\sum_r p_{i,r}d^{\mathrm{MEC}}_{t,r}, \qquad E_{t,c}=\eta_cd^{\mathrm{RAN}}_{t,c}, $$
and put
$$ a_t=\min_iC_{t,i}, \qquad \beta_t=\min_cE_{t,c}. $$

2.1. Minimum expenditure for a prescribed utility

For a bundle $(X_t,Y_t)$, define
$$ z_i^0=\min_r\frac{X_{t,i,r}}{d^{\mathrm{MEC}}_{t,r}}, \qquad w_c^0=\frac{Y_{t,c}}{d^{\mathrm{RAN}}_{t,c}}. $$

If its utility is at least $v$, then
$$ \sum_i z_i^0\geq v, \qquad \sum_c w_c^0\geq v. $$
Its expenditure is therefore at least
$$ \sum_iC_{t,i}z_i^0+\sum_cE_{t,c}w_c^0 \geq (a_t+\beta_t)v. $$

Equality is attainable by placing all execution at a node attaining $a_t$ and all uploads at a cell attaining $\beta_t$. Hence the minimum expenditure needed for utility $v$ is exactly
$$ (a_t+\beta_t)v. $$

At an equilibrium, $a_t+\beta_t$ must be positive. Otherwise arbitrarily large utility would be obtainable at zero cost, so no finite bundle could maximize utility. Individual optimality consequently gives
$$ u_t=\frac{B_t}{a_t+\beta_t}>0. $$

Every equilibrium can be replaced by a canonical allocation of the form
$$ X_{t,i,r}=d^{\mathrm{MEC}}_{t,r}z_{t,i}, \qquad Y_{t,c}=d^{\mathrm{RAN}}_{t,c}w_{t,c}, $$
where
$$ \sum_i z_{t,i}=\sum_cw_{t,c}=u_t. $$

Indeed, scale the vectors $z^0$ and $w^0$ above down to these totals. The resulting bundle is coordinatewise no larger than the original bundle. Its expenditure is at least $(a_t+\beta_t)u_t=B_t$ and at most the original expenditure $B_t$, so it also costs exactly $B_t$. Every reduction in a positively priced good must therefore be zero. Thus clearing of positively priced goods is preserved.

2.2. The polynomial system

Consider nonnegative variables
$$ p_{i,r},\ \eta_c,\ a_t,\ \beta_t,\ z_{t,i},\ w_{t,c}, $$
and strictly positive variables $u_t$. Require:
$$ \sum_i z_{t,i}=u_t, \qquad \sum_cw_{t,c}=u_t, $$
$$ \sum_t d^{\mathrm{MEC}}_{t,r}z_{t,i}\leq D_{i,r}, \qquad \sum_t d^{\mathrm{RAN}}_{t,c}w_{t,c}\leq D_c, $$
$$ C_{t,i}\geq a_t, \qquad E_{t,c}\geq\beta_t, $$
$$ z_{t,i}(C_{t,i}-a_t)=0, \qquad w_{t,c}(E_{t,c}-\beta_t)=0, $$
$$ u_t(a_t+\beta_t)=B_t, $$
and
$$ p_{i,r}\left(D_{i,r}-\sum_t d^{\mathrm{MEC}}_{t,r}z_{t,i}\right)=0, $$
$$ \eta_c\left(D_c-\sum_t d^{\mathrm{RAN}}_{t,c}w_{t,c}\right)=0. $$

Every equation or inequality has degree at most two. There are polynomially many variables and constraints, with rational coefficients of polynomial total encoding length.

A solution produces an equilibrium: the canonical bundle has utility $u_t$ and expenditure
$$ \sum_iC_{t,i}z_{t,i}+\sum_cE_{t,c}w_{t,c} =(a_t+\beta_t)u_t=B_t. $$
The expenditure lower bound from Section 2.1 excludes any affordable bundle with larger utility. Capacity feasibility and clearing follow from the remaining constraints.

Conversely, canonicalizing any equilibrium as above produces a solution. Equality in the expenditure lower bound forces execution and uploads to use only minimum-cost nodes and cells, which gives the displayed complementarity equations.

2.3. Existence

Let $\mathcal P$ be the polytope of nonnegative $(z,w,u)$ satisfying the capacity constraints and the two total-equality constraints above.

This polytope is compact. Strictly positive requirements bound every $z_{t,i}$ and $w_{t,c}$ through a capacity constraint, and the equality constraints then bound $u_t$. It also contains a point with every $u_t>0$: give each type a sufficiently small positive utility, distributing execution and uploads evenly.

Consider
$$ F(u)=\sum_tB_t\log u_t. $$
It attains a maximum on the positive-utility part of $\mathcal P$. To verify this, take a maximizing sequence. Compactness gives a convergent subsequence. If any limiting utility were zero, its positive-weight logarithm would tend to $-\infty$, while all other logarithms remain bounded above. This contradicts the finite objective value available at a positive feasible point.

Let $(z^*,w^*,u^*)$ be a maximizer and define
$$ g_t=\frac{B_t}{u_t^*}. $$

For every $(z,w,u)\in\mathcal P$, differentiation along the feasible segment starting at the maximizer gives
$$ \sum_tg_t(u_t-u_t^*)\leq0. $$
Thus $(z^*,w^*,u^*)$ also solves the linear program maximizing $\sum_tg_tu_t$ over $\mathcal P$.

Its dual minimizes
$$ \sum_{i,r}p_{i,r}D_{i,r}+\sum_c\eta_cD_c $$
subject to nonnegative capacity multipliers and
$$ a_t+\beta_t\geq g_t,\qquad C_{t,i}\geq a_t,\qquad E_{t,c}\geq\beta_t, $$
where the equality multipliers $a_t,\beta_t$ are initially unrestricted.

The primal is feasible and bounded, so linear-programming strong duality supplies an optimal dual solution. Complementary slackness gives all the quadratic equations above. Since $u_t^*>0$, some $z_{t,i}^*>0$ and some $w_{t,c}^*>0$. Their complementary-slackness equations imply
$$ a_t=C_{t,i}\geq0,\qquad \beta_t=E_{t,c}\geq0. $$
Also $a_t+\beta_t=g_t$, giving $u_t^*(a_t+\beta_t)=B_t$.

Therefore the quadratic system is nonempty and an equilibrium exists.

2.4. Uniqueness of equilibrium utilities

Let $u$ be an equilibrium utility vector, and set $g_t=a_t+\beta_t=B_t/u_t$.

For any positive feasible utility vector $v$, choose a canonical feasible allocation realizing it. The minimum-cost inequalities give
$$ \sum_tg_tv_t \leq \sum_{i,r}p_{i,r}D_{i,r}+\sum_c\eta_cD_c. $$
At equilibrium, exact expenditure and clearing imply that the right-hand side equals $\sum_tB_t=\sum_tg_tu_t$. Hence
$$ \sum_tg_t(v_t-u_t)\leq0. $$

The tangent inequality for the logarithm now gives
$$ F(v)-F(u) \leq \sum_t\frac{B_t}{u_t}(v_t-u_t) \leq0. $$
Thus every equilibrium utility vector maximizes $F$. Because all $B_t>0$, this objective is strictly concave in $u$, so that utility vector is unique.

2.5. Algebraic consequences

The quadratic characterization shows that the decision problem

Does an equilibrium exist whose designated price is at least a given rational threshold?

belongs to $\exists\mathbb R$, the class of problems polynomially reducible to existential real polynomial feasibility.

It also shows that an equilibrium with real algebraic coordinates always exists. Here one uses the standard real-algebraic sampling theorem: a nonempty semialgebraic set defined over $\mathbb Q$ contains an algebraic point, and such a point can be found effectively. The hypotheses hold because the displayed system is finite, rational, and nonempty. Sampling and real univariate representations are described in [Basu’s survey on algorithms in real algebraic geometry](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.1534).

Standard algebraic arithmetic and root isolation convert a sampled point into defining integer polynomials and rational isolating intervals for its coordinates. This establishes effective exact computability, without asserting polynomial running time.

Let $e$ have entry $1/q_j$ on both rows of block $j$, and let $h$ have entry $1$ on each $T_j$ row and $0$ on each $E_j$ row. Since
$$ h^{\mathsf T}A_0=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}, $$
the actual requirement matrix satisfies
$$ A=HA_0,\qquad H=I+\delta eh^{\mathsf T}. $$

The supply vector is $D=HD_0$, where $D_0$ has entries $3,2$ in each block. The displayed block utility equalities therefore imply
$$ Au=D. $$

Define
$$ s=h^{\mathsf T}e=\sum_{j=0}^n\frac1{q_j}, \qquad \kappa=\frac{\delta}{1+\delta s}. $$
Then
$$ H^{-1}=I-\kappa eh^{\mathsf T}. $$

Set the compute prices to
$$ \pi=H^{-\mathsf T}\lambda. $$
Writing
$$ W=e^{\mathsf T}\lambda =\frac14\sum_{j=0}^nq_j(1+z_j), $$
we obtain
$$ \pi_{T_j}=\lambda_{T_j}-\kappa W, \qquad \pi_{E_j}=\lambda_{E_j}. $$

4.1. Positivity

Since $q_j^2-a_j$ is a positive integer,
$$ \lambda_{T_j} =\frac{q_j^2-a_j}{2(1+z_j)} \geq\frac14. $$
Also $z_j\geq1/2$, so
$$ \lambda_{E_j}\geq\frac{q_j^2}{8}\geq\frac12. $$

Finally,
$$ W\leq\frac Q2, \qquad \kappa W\leq\frac{\delta Q}{2}\leq\frac18. $$
Consequently,
$$ \pi_{T_j}\geq\frac18>0, \qquad \pi_{E_j}>0. $$

4.2. Individual optimality and clearing

The price transformation gives
$$ A^{\mathsf T}\pi=A_0^{\mathsf T}\lambda=(B_t/u_t)_t. $$

Give each type aggregate quantities
$$ X_{t,r}=A_{r,t}u_t,\qquad Y_t=u_t, $$
and set the radio price to zero.

With one compute node and one radio cell, utility at least $v$ requires at least $A_{r,t}v$ of every compute resource and $v$ of radio. Its minimum expenditure at these prices is
$$ v\sum_r\pi_rA_{r,t}=vB_t/u_t. $$
Thus the proposed bundle has utility $u_t$, spends exactly $B_t$, and maximizes utility.

4.3. Every equilibrium has the same prices

Section 2.4 implies that every equilibrium has the same utility vector $u$.

$$ \sum_tu_t=3N<3N+1, $$

$$ A^{\mathsf T}\rho=(B_t/u_t)_t=A^{\mathsf T}\pi. $$

5. A square-root-sum comparison reduction

For the distinguished block,
$$ \lambda_{T_0}=1. $$
Its uniquely forced price is therefore
$$ \alpha=\pi_{T_0} =1-\frac{\kappa}{4} \left(Q+1+\sum_{j=1}^n\sqrt{a_j}\right). $$

Given an integer threshold $K$, define the rational threshold
$$ \theta =1-\frac{\kappa}{4}(Q+1+K). $$
Since $\kappa>0$,
$$ \sum_{j=1}^n\sqrt{a_j}\leq K \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha\geq\theta. $$

The construction is polynomial-time:

  • Each $q_j$ is computed using integer squaring and comparisons; its bit length is $O(1+\log a_j)$.
  • A suitable $\delta$ is obtained by rounding $1/(4Q)$ down to a power of two.
  • Since every $q_j$ is a power of two, $s=\sum_j1/q_j$, $\kappa$, and $\theta$ have polynomial bit length.
  • There are $O(n^2)$ requirement entries, each of polynomial bit length.

Zero radicands, if allowed in the source problem, can simply be removed.

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from square-root-sum comparison to equilibrium price-threshold decision. Its output instances have strictly positive dyadic requirements and supplies and uniquely determined compute prices.

The square-root-sum problem is a standard exact-comparison problem; see [Allender, Bürgisser, Kjeldgaard-Pedersen, and Miltersen](https://www3.math.tu-berlin.de/algebra/work/focs7.pdf). The reduction above is proved directly and does not supply an $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness reduction.

6. Exponential explicit-polynomial output

Specialize the construction to
$$ a_j=p_j\qquad(1\leq j\leq n), $$
where $p_1,\ldots,p_n$ are distinct primes. Then
$$ \alpha=A_*-\frac{\kappa}{4}\sum_{j=1}^n\sqrt{p_j}, \qquad A_*=1-\frac{\kappa}{4}(Q+1)\in\mathbb Q. $$

6.1. Polynomial input length

Choose the first $n$ primes. Bertrand’s postulate implies $p_j\leq2^j$, so the integers $p_j,q_j$ have $O(n)$ bits. Also $Q$ and a suitable dyadic $\delta$ have $O(n+\log n)$ bits.

There are $6(n+1)^2$ compute-requirement entries. Requirements, supplies, masses, and per-buyer budgets all have polynomial bit length. Thus
$$ m=2n+3,\qquad \tau=3n+3,\qquad L=O(n^3). $$

6.2. Multiquadratic independence

For completeness, the field
$$ K_n=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{p_1},\ldots,\sqrt{p_n}) $$
has degree $2^n$, with basis
$$ \left\{\prod_{j\in J}\sqrt{p_j}:J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\right\}. $$

Here is an induction establishing this fact. Alongside the degree statement, prove that a positive rational $r$ has a square root in $K_k$ exactly when its square class is generated by the square classes of $p_1,\ldots,p_k$.

The assertion holds for $K_0=\mathbb Q$. Assume it holds for $k-1$. Distinct prime valuations show that the square class of $p_k$ is not generated by those of the previous primes. Hence $\sqrt{p_k}\notin K_{k-1}$, and adjoining it doubles the degree.

If $\sqrt r=a+b\sqrt{p_k}$ with $a,b\in K_{k-1}$, squaring and using uniqueness of this quadratic-extension representation gives
$$ 2ab=0. $$
Thus either $\sqrt r\in K_{k-1}$ or $\sqrt{r/p_k}\in K_{k-1}$. The induction hypothesis proves the asserted square-class characterization. Its converse follows by multiplying the corresponding square roots. This completes the induction and establishes the product basis.

Independent sign changes of the square roots define field automorphisms. They send $\alpha$ to
$$ \alpha_\varepsilon =A_*-\frac{\kappa}{4} \sum_{j=1}^n\varepsilon_j\sqrt{p_j}, \qquad \varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^n. $$

These $2^n$ values are distinct: equality between two would give a nontrivial rational linear relation among the basis elements $\sqrt{p_j}$. Therefore $\alpha$ has degree $2^n$.

6.3. Every conjugate is positive

For every sign vector,
$$ 1+\sum_{j=1}^n\varepsilon_j\sqrt{p_j} \leq 1+\sum_{j=1}^n\sqrt{p_j} \leq Q, $$
because $\sqrt{p_j}<q_j$ and $1<q_0=2$. Hence
$$ \alpha_\varepsilon =1-\frac{\kappa}{4} \left(Q+1+\sum_{j=1}^n\varepsilon_j\sqrt{p_j}\right) \geq1-\frac{\kappa Q}{2} \geq\frac78>0. $$

Thus the uniquely forced price has $2^n$ distinct positive real conjugates.

6.4. Every defining polynomial is large

Let $P\in\mathbb Z[X]$ be any nonzero polynomial satisfying $P(\alpha)=0$. Each field automorphism fixes its coefficients, so
$$ P(\alpha_\varepsilon)=0 $$
for every sign vector. Consequently, $P$ has at least $2^n$ distinct positive roots.

Descartes’ rule of signs bounds the number of positive roots, counted with multiplicity, by the number of sign changes in the nonzero coefficient sequence. If $P$ has $s$ nonzero coefficients, that sequence has at most $s-1$ sign changes. Therefore
$$ s\geq2^n+1. $$

This applies to every defining polynomial, including reducible and nonminimal ones. It excludes polynomial-size sparse encodings as well as dense coefficient lists.

Since the input length is $O(n^3)$, writing the defining polynomial of this single forced coordinate already requires superpolynomial time. Its rational isolating interval cannot remove that output requirement.
