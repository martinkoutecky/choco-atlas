Lemma 2 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(r\ge1\), compute the Lebesgue volume \(V_r\) of the parameter region \(\mathcal P_r\) consisting of \(\lambda\in(0,1)\) and \(r\) frontier points \(v_i=(a_i,b_i)\in[0,\lambda]\times[0,1-\lambda]\), pairwise incomparable under \(\preceq\) and in decreasing lexicographic order, with at least one \(i\) satisfying \(a_i>0\) and \(b_i<1-\lambda\). Here \((a,b)\preceq(c,d)\) iff \(a\le c\) and \(a+b\le c+d\), and the frontier defines \(W=\{q:\exists i,\ v_i\preceq q\}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society supplied as input is represented by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here total mass is normalized to \(1\), there are \(\tau=2\) ordered types, and their masses vary over \((\lambda,1-\lambda)\).
For an integer \(r\ge1\), let \(\mathcal P_r\subseteq\mathbb R^{2r+1}\) consist of tuples \((\lambda,a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_r,b_r)\) with \(0<\lambda<1\) and \(v_i=(a_i,b_i)\in D_\lambda=[0,\lambda]\times[0,1-\lambda]\).
Define \((a,b)\preceq(c,d)\) iff \(a\le c\) and \(a+b\le c+d\). Require the \(v_i\) to be pairwise incomparable, in strictly decreasing lexicographic order, and require some \(i\) with \(a_i>0\) and \(b_i<1-\lambda\).
A coalition is a mass vector \(q\in D_\lambda\); its winning status is defined by \(W=\{q\in D_\lambda:\exists i,\ v_i\preceq q\}\).
Let \(V_r\) be the ordinary Lebesgue volume of \(\mathcal P_r\) in the displayed coordinates, integrating over all real coordinates, including real \(\lambda\). The sole input is \(r\), encoded in unary, with bit length \(L\); compute \(V_r\) exactly as a rational number. Running times are measured in \(r,\tau,L\); no alternatives parameter \(m\) occurs.

Is exact computation of \(V_r\) polynomial-time in \(r\) and \(L\), or is it NP-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?

For finite societies, the number of nonisomorphic complete simple games with exactly two voter types and \(r\) shift-minimal winning coalitions has an explicit enumeration formula for every \(r\ge1\). [Kurz and Tautenhahn](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.3045)

Result

Exact computation is polynomial-time solvable. For every integer \(r\geq 1\),
\(V_r=\dfrac{1}{2^{r-1}(2r+1)!}\).

The proof uses the prescribed Lebesgue volume over real coordinates, including real \(\lambda\).

1. Characterizing the ordered antichains

Write \(s_i=a_i+b_i\).

Suppose \(i<j\). Strictly decreasing lexicographic order gives either \(a_i>a_j\), or \(a_i=a_j\) and \(b_i>b_j\). The second possibility would imply \(v_j\preceq v_i\), contradicting incomparability. Thus \(a_i>a_j\).

Given \(a_i>a_j\), the relation \(v_i\preceq v_j\) is impossible, while \(v_j\preceq v_i\) holds exactly when \(s_j\leq s_i\). Consequently, incomparability requires \(s_i<s_j\).

Conversely, if the \(a_i\) strictly decrease and the \(s_i\) strictly increase, every pair is incomparable and the required lexicographic order holds. Hence the order and incomparability requirements are equivalent to
\(a_1>\cdots>a_r\) and \(s_1<\cdots<s_r\).

For \(r=1\), both chains impose no conditions.

2. A change of coordinates with unit Jacobian

Introduce
\(u=a_r\), \(t=b_1\), and \(h=\lambda-a_1\).
For \(1\leq i<r\), introduce
\(x_i=a_i-a_{i+1}\) and \(y_i=s_{i+1}-s_i\).

These are \(2r+1\) coordinates. Set
\(X=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}x_i\) and \(Y=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}y_i\), with empty sums equal to zero.

The inverse transformation is
\(a_i=u+\sum_{j=i}^{r-1}x_j\),
\(b_i=t+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(x_j+y_j)\), and
\(\lambda=u+X+h\).

In particular,
\(a_1=u+X\), \(b_r=t+X+Y\), and
\(1-\lambda-b_r=1-u-t-h-2X-Y\).

The absolute Jacobian determinant is \(1\). Indeed:

  • The reconstruction \(a_r=u\), \(a_i=a_{i+1}+x_i\) has determinant of absolute value \(1\).
  • For fixed \(a_i\), the reconstruction \(b_1=t\), \(b_{i+1}=b_i+x_i+y_i\) likewise has determinant of absolute value \(1\).
  • Finally, \(\lambda=u+X+h\) has derivative \(1\) with respect to \(h\).

Ordering input coordinates as \((u,x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1},t,y_1,\ldots,y_{r-1},h)\) and output coordinates as \((a_1,\ldots,a_r,b_1,\ldots,b_r,\lambda)\) makes the full Jacobian block triangular with these diagonal blocks. Coordinate permutations do not change its absolute determinant.

3. Identifying the volume with a weighted simplex

Let \(T_r\) be the closed region defined by
\(u,t,h,x_i,y_i\geq 0\) and
\(u+t+h+2\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}x_i+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}y_i\leq 1\).

Every point of \(\mathcal P_r\) maps into \(T_r\): the antichain conditions give positive \(x_i,y_i\); the rectangle bounds give \(u,t,h\geq 0\); and \(b_r\leq 1-\lambda\) gives the final inequality.

Conversely, every interior point of \(T_r\) maps into \(\mathcal P_r\). To verify this, put
\(k=1-u-t-h-2X-Y>0\).
All coordinates of an interior point are positive, so:

  • The \(a_i\) strictly decrease and the \(s_i\) strictly increase whenever \(r>1\).
  • Each \(a_i\) satisfies \(0<a_i\leq a_1<\lambda\), because \(u,h>0\).
  • Each \(b_i\) satisfies \(0<b_i\leq b_r<1-\lambda\), because \(t,k>0\).
  • We have \(\lambda=u+X+h>0\) and \(1-\lambda=b_r+k>0\).

Thus the extra existence requirement is satisfied as well: every \(i\) has \(a_i>0\) and \(b_i<1-\lambda\). This verification also applies when \(r=1\).

The transformed region therefore contains the interior of \(T_r\) and is contained in \(T_r\). Their difference lies in the boundary of \(T_r\), a finite union of hyperplanes of Lebesgue measure zero. By the unit Jacobian,
\(V_r=\operatorname{vol}_{2r+1}(T_r)\).

Now replace each \(x_i\) by \(z_i=2x_i\). The resulting region is the standard simplex in dimension \(2r+1\), and the volume factor is \(2^{-(r-1)}\).

For completeness, the standard simplex in dimension \(n\) has volume \(1/n!\): inductively, its slice at last coordinate \(q\in[0,1]\) has volume \((1-q)^{n-1}/(n-1)!\), whose integral is \(1/n!\). Consequently,
\(V_r=2^{-(r-1)}/(2r+1)!\), as claimed.

4. Exact computation and bit complexity

Compute the integer
\(D_r=2^{r-1}(2r+1)!\)
and output the rational number \(1/D_r\). This representation is already reduced.

Its denominator has bit length
\(B=O(r\log(r+1))\), since
\(D_r\leq 2^{r-1}(2r+1)^{2r+1}\).

Computing the factorial uses \(O(r)\) integer multiplications, each on at most \(B\) bits. Even schoolbook multiplication therefore gives \(O(rB^2)\) bit operations; multiplication by \(2^{r-1}\) is a binary shift. Including input reading, the running time is polynomial in \(r\) and \(L\), and the output length is polynomial as well.

Thus the target question is settled by an explicit polynomial-time algorithm.
