The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over buyer valuation schedules and referral roles, a measurable block or graphon referral relation, and a specified supply scaling, determine the largest \(\epsilon\) achievable by a measurable allocation-and-transfer mechanism that is individually incentive compatible and individually rational, respects diffusion feasibility and supply, and gives each truthful buyer utility between \(\epsilon\phi_i\) and \(\phi_i\), where \(\phi_i\) is the buyer’s expected marginal welfare contribution under a random-priority ordering of the population.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless probability space partitioned into the \(\tau\) positive-mass type blocks specified by \(\mu\); their masses are binary rationals summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. The finite input has bit length \(L\).
Each type specifies a normalized valuation schedule on \(\{0,\ldots,m\}\), with nonnegative, nonincreasing rational marginal values, and a referral role. A symmetric binary block relation specifies actual neighbors, and a nonempty set of blocks consists of the seller's initial neighbors. Supply is a binary rational \(Q\in(0,m]\).
Buyers may report any normalized schedule with nonnegative, nonincreasing marginal values and suppress any measurable subset of their actual outgoing referrals, but cannot invent referrals. Reports and retained referral relations must be Borel measurable. A buyer is feasible precisely when reachable from the seller by a finite directed path of retained referrals.
A possibly randomized measurable direct mechanism assigns quantities \(q(x)\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\) and payments \(p(x)\in\mathbb R\), with finite expected utilities \(u(x)=\mathbb E[v_x(q(x))-p(x)]\). In every realization, \(\int q\,d\mu\le Q\); infeasible buyers receive zero quantity and payment, and feasible outcomes cannot depend on infeasible buyers' reports. Transfers of either sign are allowed; no revenue constraint is imposed.
Define the random-priority benchmark at any report profile as follows. Sample \(N\) buyers independently from \(\mu\), add a tagged buyer \(x\) and the seller, inherit the reported referrals, and use supply \(k_N=\lfloor(N+1)Q\rfloor\). For a coalition \(B\), let \(W_N(B)\) be maximum reported welfare under integer quantities bounded by \(m\), using only buyers reachable from the seller within \(B\); set \(W_N(B)=0\) when the seller is absent.
For a uniform permutation of these \(N+2\) agents, let \(B_x\) precede \(x\), and define \(\phi_x=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb E[W_N(B_x\cup\{x\})-W_N(B_x)]\), where expectation includes sampling and permutation. This fixes the per-buyer normalization of continuous marginal contribution.
Require dominant-strategy individual incentive compatibility for both valuation and referral reports, individual rationality, and \(\epsilon\phi_x\le u(x)\le\phi_x\) whenever \(x\) reports truthfully, for every admissible profile of other reports. A positive factor is infeasible if a required benchmark is infinite.
What is the sharp Shapley-fairness factor uniformly achievable in this continuous model as a function of \(m,\tau,Q\)? Can a positive factor be guaranteed, or must the worst-case factor be \(0\)?
For finite societies selling \(k\) homogeneous items, Permutation Diffusion Auction is individually incentive compatible, individually rational, and \(1/(k+1)\)-Shapley fair; one realization is computable in polynomial time. Its fairness bound is tight for that mechanism when \(k=1\). [Verified source](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/38746/42708).
No result settling the specified continuous model was located in the literature search. Computational bounds should be expressed in \(m,\tau,L\) and any additional parameters.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Selected the finite binary block-relation option, since an arbitrary measurable graphon has no specified finite computational encoding; actual adjacency is symmetric, while reported invitations are directed.
  • Normalized buyer mass to one and chose extensive supply scaling: N sampled buyers plus a tagged buyer receive floor((N+1)Q) discrete items. Each buyer has demand bounded by m.
  • Required nonnegative diminishing marginal valuations and retained integer quantities per buyer; divisibility occurs through buyer mass rather than interpolation of individual valuation schedules.
  • Made continuous random priority and its normalization explicit through sampled finite societies, including the seller in every permutation, and used the limsup of expected tagged-buyer marginal contributions.
  • Allowed arbitrary measurable referral suppression and valuation misreports, and retained the requirements concerning infeasible reports, all other-report profiles, and expected utilities.
  • Allowed negative payments and imposed no non-deficit constraint, consistently with the discrete mechanism's permitted subsidies.
  • Used Borel report profiles to make sampling well-defined; required finite expected utilities and treated an infinite required benchmark as incompatible with positive fairness.
  • Interpreted the approximation target as the sharp uniform fairness guarantee, rather than exact instance-by-instance optimization of epsilon.

Result and interpretation. Taking “uniformly achievable” to mean over all admissible societies with the specified parameters, the sharp factor is

\[ \epsilon^*(m,\tau,Q)= \begin{cases} 1,&\tau=1,\\ 0,&\tau\ge 2. \end{cases} \]

Here and below, a factor of \(0\) imposes \(0\le u(x)\le\phi_x\), including when \(\phi_x=+\infty\).

The obstruction for \(\tau\ge2\) uses the requirement that fairness hold for each truthful buyer against every admissible profile of other reports. It does not imply that every society with multiple types has factor \(0\): if every block consists of initial neighbors, the exact-fairness construction below applies.

1. An infinite benchmark when \(\tau\ge2\).

Construct a society consisting of one noninitial block \(B\), of mass \(b=1/2\), and \(\tau-1\) initial blocks \(A_1,\ldots,A_{\tau-1}\), whose positive dyadic masses sum to \(1/2\). The actual symmetric block relation connects each \(A_i\) to \(B\), with no other edges.

Give block \(B\) the valuation \(v_B(q)=q\). Give block \(A_i\) the valuation \(v_{A_i}(q)=(i-1)q\). These schedules satisfy all valuation hypotheses, and the schedules together with referral roles distinguish the \(\tau\) types.

Fix a buyer \(x\in A_1\). Thus \(x\) has zero valuation. Consider this report profile:

  • Buyer \(x\) reports truthfully, retaining all referrals to \(B\).
  • Every other initial buyer reports zero valuation and suppresses all referrals.
  • Buyers in \(B\) report their valuation \(v_B(q)=q\) and retain their actual referrals.

On an interval realization of the society, this is a Borel profile: singling out \(x\) changes a Borel relation on a singleton.

Represent the uniform permutation by independent uniform arrival times. Conditional on the tagged buyer’s arrival time being \(t\), the seller precedes \(x\) with probability \(t\). The number of sampled buyers belonging to \(B\) and preceding \(x\) is

\[ Z_N(t)\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(N,bt). \]

Before \(x\) arrives, block \(B\) is unreachable. All reachable buyers have reported zero valuation, so the predecessor coalition has welfare zero.

If the seller precedes \(x\), adding \(x\) makes every preceding sampled buyer in \(B\) reachable. Its resulting welfare is exactly

\[ \min\{mZ_N(t),k_N\}. \]

Consequently, the expected marginal contribution is

\[ M_N=\int_0^1 t\,\mathbb E\!\left[\min\{mZ_N(t),\lfloor(N+1)Q\rfloor\}\right]\,dt. \]

For each fixed \(t\), the binomial law of large numbers gives \(Z_N(t)/N\longrightarrow bt\) in probability. Since \(0\le mZ_N(t)/N\le m\), bounded convergence in expectation and then dominated convergence in \(t\) yield

\[ \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{M_N}{N} =\int_0^1 t\min\{mbt,Q\}\,dt>0. \]

The last inequality follows from \(m,b,Q>0\). Therefore \(M_N\longrightarrow+\infty\), and

\[ \phi_x=+\infty. \]

This is a required benchmark for a truthful buyer. Every positive factor is therefore infeasible under the problem’s explicit convention concerning infinite benchmarks.

The mechanism assigning zero quantities and zero payments is individually incentive compatible, individually rational, and satisfies the factor-\(0\) bounds: welfare is monotone under coalition inclusion, so every benchmark is nonnegative. This proves the sharp uniform factor \(0\) for \(\tau\ge2\).

2. The benchmark when every buyer is an initial neighbor.

This includes every society with \(\tau=1\). Referrals then have no effect on feasibility, either in the full society or within a coalition containing the seller.

Write a buyer’s reported marginal values as

\[ a_1(y)\ge\cdots\ge a_m(y)\ge0, \]

and define aggregate marginal demand above price \(z\) by

\[ D(z)=\int\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbf 1_{\{a_j(y)>z\}}\,d\mu(y). \]

For the tagged buyer \(x\), put

\[ d_x(z)=\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbf 1_{\{a_j(x)>z\}}. \]

Changing one buyer’s report does not change \(D\), because a singleton has measure zero.

We first establish the benchmark formula

\[ \phi_x =\int_0^1 t\int_0^\infty d_x(z)\mathbf 1_{\{tD(z)<Q\}}\,dz\,dt. \tag{1} \]

Conditional on the tagged arrival time \(t\), let

\[ C_N(t,z)=\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf 1_{\{U_i<t\}} \sum_{j=1}^m\mathbf 1_{\{a_j(Y_i)>z\}}. \]

Here the sampled buyers \(Y_i\) and their arrival times \(U_i\) are independent.

For any finite collection of nonincreasing marginal schedules, maximum welfare with capacity \(k\) is the sum of its \(k\) largest marginal values, or all marginal values if fewer are available. Such a selection can respect each buyer’s prefix constraint because its marginals are nonincreasing. The layer-cake identity therefore gives the tagged welfare increment, conditional on the seller preceding \(x\), as

\[ \Delta_N(t)= \int_0^\infty \left[ \min\{k_N,C_N(t,z)+d_x(z)\} -\min\{k_N,C_N(t,z)\} \right]dz. \]

For fixed \(t,z\), the summands defining \(C_N(t,z)\) are independent, identically distributed, and bounded by \(m\). Hence

\[ \frac{C_N(t,z)}N\longrightarrow tD(z) \]

almost surely.

Since \(k_N/N\longrightarrow Q\), the integrand defining \(\Delta_N(t)\) converges to \(d_x(z)\) when \(tD(z)<Q\), and to zero when \(tD(z)>Q\).

For each \(z\), the equality \(tD(z)=Q\) holds for at most one \(t\). Fubini’s theorem makes the exceptional set null in the product integration. Moreover, the integrand is bounded by \(d_x(z)\), whose integral is the finite number \(v_x(m)\). Dominated convergence, together with the probability \(t\) that the seller precedes \(x\), proves (1). Thus the benchmark’s limsup is an actual limit.

Integrating first over \(t\) also gives

\[ \phi_x= \frac12\int_0^\infty d_x(z)\min\left\{1,\frac{Q}{D(z)}\right\}^{\!2}\,dz, \tag{2} \]

where the minimum is defined to be \(1\) when \(D(z)=0\). In particular, \(\phi_x\le v_x(m)/2\), even when other buyers’ reported valuations are unbounded across the population.

3. Posted prices representing the benchmark.

For \(t\in(0,1)\), define

\[ c_t=\inf\{z\ge0:tD(z)\le Q\}. \]

The function \(D\) is nonincreasing and right-continuous. Also \(D(z)\longrightarrow0\): every reported schedule has finite marginal values, and its marginal count is bounded by \(m\). Consequently, \(c_t\) is finite and

\[ tD(c_t)\le Q. \tag{3} \]

Monotonicity gives \(D(z)>Q/t\) for \(z<c_t\), and \(D(z)\le Q/t\) for \(z>c_t\). The equality cases already excluded in deriving (1) therefore imply

\[ \phi_x=\int_0^1 tS_x(c_t)\,dt, \tag{4} \]

where

\[ S_x(c)=\max_{0\le q\le m}\{v_x(q)-cq\} =\sum_{j=1}^m(a_j(x)-c)_+. \]

The final equality follows directly by summing the nonincreasing marginal net values.

4. A measurable lottery satisfying supply in every realization.

Independent buyer lotteries would not by themselves establish realization-wise supply feasibility. The following correlated lottery does.

Fix a Borel measurable coordinate \(r:X\to[0,1]\) having a uniform distribution under \(\mu\). Such a coordinate exists on an atomless measurable probability space; see [Lemma A.1 on atomless probability spaces](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~wang/files/e-book-final.pdf).

For fixed \(t\), let

\[ d_t(y)=\#\{j:a_j(y)>c_t\}, \qquad G_{t,d}=\{y:d_t(y)=d\}. \]

If \(M_{t,d}=\mu(G_{t,d})>0\), define, for \(y\in G_{t,d}\),

\[ R_t(y)= \frac{\mu\{z\in G_{t,d}:r(z)\le r(y)\}}{M_{t,d}}. \]

Set \(R_t(y)=0\) on classes of mass zero.

The conditional distribution of \(r\) on every positive-mass class is atomless, since \(\mu\{r=s\}=0\) for each \(s\). Its cumulative distribution function is continuous. The probability integral transform therefore shows that \(R_t\) is uniform within each such class.

Draw independent uniform random variables \(T\in(0,1)\) and \(U\in[0,1)\). Select buyer \(y\) when

\[ (R_T(y)+U)\bmod1<T. \]

A selected buyer receives \(d_T(y)\) units and pays \(c_Td_T(y)\). An unselected buyer receives and pays zero.

For every fixed realization \(T=t,U=u\), selection takes exactly a fraction \(t\) of the measure in each positive-mass demand class. Hence

\[ \int q\,d\mu =t\sum_{d=0}^m dM_{t,d} =tD(c_t) \le Q, \]

by (3). Classes of mass zero do not affect this integral.

All quantities are integers between \(0\) and \(m\). The outcome functions are Borel measurable: \(c_t\) is monotone in \(t\), the demand classes are jointly measurable, and their conditional cumulative distribution functions are measurable parameter integrals.

Crucially, every individual buyer, including one in a zero-mass class, is selected with conditional probability \(t\), because \(U\) is uniform.

5. Incentives, utilities, and sharpness.

Fix all other reports and a buyer \(x\). An individual deviation leaves \(D\) and all prices \(c_t\) unchanged.

Conditional on \(T=t\), a report inducing quantity \(d\) gives expected true utility

\[ t\bigl(v_x(d)-c_td\bigr). \]

Truthful reporting induces the number of strictly positive marginal net values. This quantity maximizes \(v_x(q)-c_tq\); marginal values equal to the price contribute zero. Truthful valuation reporting is therefore optimal conditional on every \(t\), and hence optimal in expectation.

Referral reports do not affect the mechanism. Since every buyer is an initial neighbor, their suppression cannot change feasibility. Thus truthful valuation and referral reporting is a dominant strategy.

For a truthful buyer, expected utility is, by (4),

\[ u(x)=\int_0^1 tS_x(c_t)\,dt=\phi_x. \]

It is nonnegative, establishing individual rationality and exact Shapley fairness.

Utilities are finite under every report. Indeed, \(c_t\le c_1<\infty\), so payments are bounded by \(mc_1\), and a buyer’s true value is bounded by \(v_x(m)\).

Finally, no uniform factor larger than \(1\) can satisfy the required upper bound whenever a benchmark is positive. For example, if all buyers have all \(m\) marginal values equal to \(1\), equation (2) gives

\[ \phi_x=\frac{Q^2}{2m}>0. \]

Thus the achieved factor \(1\) is sharp for \(\tau=1\).

6. Computational scope and the finite-auction result.

The impossibility witness uses dyadic masses, integer marginal values, and a finite block graph. Its finite society description has size polynomial in \(m,\tau\), and the encoding length of \(Q\). The suppressed-referral profile also has a finite description: all initial buyers except one named buyer suppress their referrals.

Arbitrary Borel report profiles have no specified finite encoding, so a runtime bound solely in \(m,\tau,L\) would not be justified. If a reported valuation profile is supplied as \(r\) constant pieces with rational masses and total description length \(L_{\mathrm{rep}}\), equation (2) computes a tagged benchmark exactly by sorting at most \(rm+m\) marginal values and integrating a rational step function. This takes \(O(rm\log(rm+m))\) comparisons and polynomial bit time in \(m,r,L_{\mathrm{rep}}\). In particular, the homogeneous truthful \(\tau=1\) profile has polynomial-time benchmark computation in \(m,L\).

The cited [finite PDA theorem](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/38746/42708) gives factor \(1/(k+1)\). Under the present sampling normalization, \(k_N\) grows with \(N\), so that guarantee tends to zero. This alone would not prove impossibility; the infinite-benchmark construction above supplies the needed proof.
