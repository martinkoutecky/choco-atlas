Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite room classes \(H\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) over resident types \(t=(A_t,e_t)\), and rational per-capita supplies \(f_h\) of unowned rooms, maximize the mass receiving an acceptable room. Choose mass \(r_t\) retaining its endowment, mass \(y_{t,h}\) assigned to acceptable class \(h\), and unassigned mass \(u_t\), subject to \(r_t+\sum_h y_{t,h}+u_t=\mu_t\), S-IR restrictions, and \(\sum_t y_{t,h}\le f_h+\sum_{t:e_t=h}(\mu_t-r_t)\) for each \(h\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(H\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) room classes. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types \(t=(A_t,e_t)\) with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(A_t\subseteq H\), \(e_t\in H\cup\{\bot\}\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(\bot\) means no endowment.
Each endowed resident owns one distinct room of class \(e_t\). Unowned rooms have nonnegative binary rational per-capita supplies \(f_h\). Acceptable rooms give utility \(1\); other rooms and being unassigned give utility \(0\).
Choose nonnegative real masses \(r_t,y_{t,h},u_t\), representing retention of the resident's own room, assignment to class \(h\), and being unassigned, respectively. Require \(r_t+\sum_{h\in H}y_{t,h}+u_t=\mu_t\), with \(y_{t,h}=0\) whenever \(h\notin A_t\) or \(h=e_t\).
Strong individual rationality requires retaining one's own room or receiving a strictly better room: if \(e_t\in A_t\), require \(r_t=\mu_t\); if \(e_t\in H\setminus A_t\), require \(u_t=0\); if \(e_t=\bot\), require \(r_t=0\).
For every \(h\in H\), require \(\sum_t y_{t,h}\le f_h+\sum_{t:e_t=h}(\mu_t-r_t)\). Rooms may remain unused. Maximize satisfied mass \(\sum_{t:e_t\in A_t}r_t+\sum_{t,h}y_{t,h}\).
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is exact maximization of satisfied mass under these constraints solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely listed residents and individual rooms, a polynomial-time strategyproof mechanism maximizes the number satisfied subject to strong individual rationality. [Aziz (2018)](https://www.mechanism-design.org/arch/v003-1/p_04.pdf)
No result settling exactly this mass formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized resident masses to sum to one, interpreting room supplies in the same per-capita units; supplies are nonnegative binary rationals.
  • Allowed a null endowment and arbitrary acceptable subsets, including empty subsets, following the published acceptable-set formulation; took the room-class set to be nonempty.
  • Expanded S-IR: acceptable endowments must be retained, owners of unacceptable endowments cannot become unassigned, and residents without endowments may receive an acceptable room or remain unassigned.
  • Distinguished retention of one's own room from assignment to a room of the same class by setting assignment variables for the endowment class to zero.
  • Included retained acceptable endowments in the objective and allowed unused rooms.
  • Treated allocation masses as divisible nonnegative real quantities, with no tie-breaking requirement for welfare maximization.

Result

Exact maximization is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Both an optimal allocation and its exact rational objective value can be computed.

The constraints already constitute a rational linear program with at most \(\tau(m+2)\) variables and polynomial encoding length. Below is a more explicit reduction to minimum-cost circulation that also gives a running-time bound without expanding masses into individual residents.

1. Eliminate the fixed decisions

Partition the support into
\[ P=\{t:e_t\in A_t\},\qquad B=\{t:e_t\in H\setminus A_t\},\qquad N=\{t:e_t=\bot\}. \]

For \(t\in P\), strong individual rationality and the mass equation force \(r_t=\mu_t\), \(u_t=0\), and every \(y_{t,h}=0\). These types contribute the constant
\[ M_P=\sum_{t\in P}\mu_t \]
to satisfaction and release no rooms.

For each \(t\in B\cup N\), define the assigned mass
\[ x_t=\sum_{h\in A_t}y_{t,h}. \]
The remaining type constraints are exactly \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\), with
\[ (r_t,u_t)= \begin{cases} (\mu_t-x_t,0),&t\in B,\\ (0,\mu_t-x_t),&t\in N. \end{cases} \]
Here the restriction against assignment to the endowed class holds automatically: \(e_t\notin A_t\) for \(t\in B\), and \(e_t=\bot\) for \(t\in N\).

For each class \(h\), write
\[ d_h=\sum_{t\in B\cup N}y_{t,h}, \qquad a_h=\sum_{\substack{t\in B\\e_t=h}}x_t. \]
Thus \(d_h\) is assigned room mass and \(a_h\) is released endowment mass. The supply constraints become \(d_h\le f_h+a_h\), and the objective becomes
\[ M_P+\sum_{t\in B\cup N}x_t. \]

Since \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), both \(d_h\le1\) and \(a_h\le1\). Consequently, replacing \(f_h\) by \(\bar f_h=\min\{f_h,1\}\) preserves feasibility: when \(f_h\ge1\), either version of the class constraint is automatic.

2. Construct a compressed circulation network

Use one reservoir vertex \(s\), one room vertex \(v_h\) for each \(h\in H\), and one type vertex \(w_t\) for each \(t\in B\cup N\).

Every arc has lower bound zero. Add the following arcs:

| Arc | Condition | Capacity | Cost per unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(s\to v_h\) | Every \(h\in H\) | \(\bar f_h\) | \(0\) |
| \(v_h\to s\) | Every \(h\in H\) | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| \(v_h\to w_t\) | \(t\in B\cup N,\ h\in A_t\) | \(\mu_t\) | \(-1\) |
| \(w_t\to v_{e_t}\) | \(t\in B\) | \(\mu_t\) | \(0\) |
| \(w_t\to s\) | \(t\in N\) | \(\mu_t\) | \(0\) |

A circulation is a nonnegative arc flow satisfying all capacities and flow conservation at every vertex. The zero circulation is feasible, and every capacity is finite.

From a circulation to an allocation

Given a circulation \(\varphi\), set
\[ y_{t,h}=\varphi(v_h,w_t) \]
on assignment arcs, and set all other assignment variables to zero.

Conservation at \(w_t\) implies that \(x_t=\sum_h y_{t,h}\) equals the flow on its unique outgoing arc. Its capacity therefore gives \(x_t\le\mu_t\). Reconstruct \(r_t,u_t\) using Section 1.

At \(v_h\), conservation gives
\[ d_h+\varphi(v_h,s)=a_h+\varphi(s,v_h). \]
Nonnegativity and the capacity on \(s\to v_h\) imply
\[ d_h\le a_h+\bar f_h\le a_h+f_h. \]
Thus all original constraints hold.

Only assignment arcs have nonzero cost. Hence
\[ \operatorname{cost}(\varphi) =-\sum_{t\in B\cup N}x_t, \]
and the resulting satisfied mass is \(M_P-\operatorname{cost}(\varphi)\).

From an allocation to a circulation

Conversely, take any feasible allocation. Put \(y_{t,h}\) on each assignment arc, \(x_t\) on each arc \(w_t\to v_{e_t}\) for \(t\in B\), and \(x_t\) on each arc \(w_t\to s\) for \(t\in N\).

Set
\[ \varphi(s,v_h)=\max\{d_h-a_h,0\}, \qquad \varphi(v_h,s)=\max\{a_h-d_h,0\}. \]

The supply constraint ensures \(\varphi(s,v_h)\le\bar f_h\). Also, \(a_h\le1\) ensures \(\varphi(v_h,s)\le1\). All remaining capacities follow from \(x_t\le\mu_t\) and \(y_{t,h}\le x_t\).

Conservation holds at every type vertex by the definition of \(x_t\), and at every room vertex because
\[ d_h+\max\{a_h-d_h,0\} =a_h+\max\{d_h-a_h,0\}. \]
Finally,
\[ \sum_h(d_h-a_h)=\sum_{t\in N}x_t. \]
Therefore the reservoir’s outgoing flow minus its incoming flow is zero as well.

This proves equivalence in both directions, including preservation of the objective. If \(C^\star\) is the minimum circulation cost, the exact optimum is
\[ \operatorname{OPT}=M_P-C^\star. \]

3. Exact computation and running time

Set
\[ n=m+\tau+1,\qquad q=m\tau+\tau+2m. \]
The network has at most \(n\) vertices and \(q\) arcs and can be constructed by examining at most \(m\tau\) type–class pairs.

The capacitated minimum-cost flow theorem of [Orlin (1993)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.41.2.338) gives an exact algorithm using
\[ O\!\left(q\log n\,(q+n\log n)\right) \]
arithmetic operations. Minimum-cost circulation is its zero vertex-balance case. The hypotheses hold here: the network is finite, costs are linear, capacities are nonnegative and finite, and a feasible circulation exists.

For completeness, binary rational capacities do not require a resident expansion. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses and supplies. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Multiplying capacities by \(D\) gives integer capacities bounded by \(D\), without changing the graph.

The standard circulation integrality theorem applies: the directed incidence matrix is totally unimodular, and integer capacity bounds preserve integral vertices. Because the circulation polytope is nonempty and bounded, a linear objective attains its minimum at a vertex. Thus the scaled problem has an integral optimal circulation, which yields an exact rational solution after division by \(D\). Every allocation coordinate lies in \([0,1]\) and is a multiple of \(1/D\), so its encoding length is \(O(L)\).

The strongly polynomial flow algorithm has polynomially bounded intermediate encoding lengths. Including scaling, network construction, and output reconstruction, the total bit running time is
\[ (m\tau+L)^{O(1)}. \]

This completely settles the stated optimization problem for the explicitly listed support.
