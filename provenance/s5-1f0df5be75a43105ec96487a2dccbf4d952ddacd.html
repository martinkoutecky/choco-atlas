The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given proposer and receiver populations distributed over finite supports with rational shares \(\mu_a\) and \(\nu_b\), population ratio \(\rho\), pairwise dating rates \(\delta_{ab}\), proposer capacities \(c_a\), receiver exposure caps \(q_b\), and exposure weights \(w_{ab}\), compute the mass allocation prescribed by ECDA. Process type pairs in descending \(\delta_{ab}\), with a fixed tie-breaking order, allocating up to the remaining proposer capacity \(c_a\mu_a\) and receiver exposure budget \(\rho q_b\nu_b\); for \(w_{ab}=0\), the receiver budget does not constrain that pair.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be nonempty proposer and receiver supports. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) positive-mass types, with masses encoded as binary rationals; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Write \(m=|A||B|\).
The shares satisfy \(\sum_a\mu_a=\sum_b\nu_b=1\). Input positive binary rationals \(N,\rho\), representing proposer population mass and the receiver-to-proposer population ratio, positive integers \(c_a\), nonnegative rational caps \(q_b\), and rational rates and weights \(\delta_{ab},w_{ab}\in[0,1]\).
An allocation \(x_{ab}\) is recommendation mass divided by \(N\). The bound of one recommendation per individual pair becomes \(0\le x_{ab}\le N\rho\mu_a\nu_b\); proposer capacities are \(c_a\mu_a\), and receiver exposure budgets are \(\rho q_b\nu_b\).
Define mass ECDA by initializing \(x=0\), ordering all pairs by descending \(\delta_{ab}\) with a supplied strict tie-breaking order, and processing each pair once.
At pair \((a,b)\), set \(r_a=c_a\mu_a-\sum_{b'}x_{ab'}\), \(s_b=\rho q_b\nu_b-\sum_{a'}w_{a'b}x_{a'b}\), and \(x_{ab}=\min\{N\rho\mu_a\nu_b,r_a,s_b/w_{ab}\}\); omit the third term when \(w_{ab}=0\).
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including the tie-breaking order; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\), with all numerical parameters encoded in \(L\).

Is exact computation of this mass ECDA allocation solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Result

Exact computation of the specified mass ECDA allocation is polynomial-time solvable. An elementary implementation uses

\(O\!\left(m\log(m+1)L^2+(m+\tau)L^3\right)\)

bit operations and outputs every allocation entry as an exact rational of \(O(L)\) bits.

The finite-population greedy-pass result appears in [Theorem 2 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19689v1#S4.SS3). For the mass formulation, the additional issue is bounding the bit lengths of intermediate rationals. The proof below establishes this directly from the supplied definition.

1. Exact algorithm and correctness

Define the pair bounds, proposer capacities, and receiver budgets by

\(U_{ab}=N\rho\mu_a\nu_b,\qquad C_a=c_a\mu_a,\qquad E_b=\rho q_b\nu_b.\)

Sort the \(m\) pairs by descending \(\delta_{ab}\), resolving equal rates using the supplied strict order. Initialize \(x_{ab}=0\), \(r_a=C_a\), and \(s_b=E_b\).

At each pair \((a,b)\), compute

\(x_{ab}=\min\{U_{ab},r_a,s_b/w_{ab}\}\)

when \(w_{ab}>0\), and compute \(x_{ab}=\min\{U_{ab},r_a\}\) when \(w_{ab}=0\). Then update only

\(r_a\leftarrow r_a-x_{ab},\qquad s_b\leftarrow s_b-w_{ab}x_{ab}.\)

These updates maintain the identities

\(r_a=C_a-\sum_{b'}x_{ab'},\qquad s_b=E_b-\sum_{a'}w_{a'b}x_{a'b}.\)

Indeed, they hold initially, and processing a pair changes precisely the corresponding summand in each identity. Thus the maintained residuals equal those in the task’s definition at every step.

All residuals remain nonnegative: the chosen allocation satisfies \(x_{ab}\le r_a\), and, when \(w_{ab}>0\), it satisfies \(w_{ab}x_{ab}\le s_b\). When \(w_{ab}=0\), the receiver residual is unchanged. Consequently, every step is well-defined and the algorithm produces exactly the specified allocation.

2. A common-denominator invariant

For a rational number \(z\), let \(d(z)\) denote its positive denominator in lowest terms. Define the integer

\(D_0=d(N)d(\rho)\prod_{a\in A}d(\mu_a)\prod_{b\in B}\bigl(d(\nu_b)d(q_b)\bigr).\)

The denominator of every \(U_{ab}\), \(C_a\), and \(E_b\) divides \(D_0\). This follows because the denominator of a product divides the product of its factors’ denominators, and \(c_a\) is an integer.

Write the weight of the \(t\)-th processed pair, when positive, as \(p_t/h_t\) in lowest terms. Define

\(D_t=D_{t-1}p_th_t\)

for a positive weight, and \(D_t=D_{t-1}\) for a zero weight.

Claim. After processing \(t\) pairs, the reduced denominator of every allocation entry and every residual divides \(D_t\).

Proof. Initially, allocation entries are zero and the residual denominators divide \(D_0\).

Suppose the claim holds before processing a pair of positive weight \(p/h\), and write \(D=D_{t-1}\). The denominators of \(U_{ab}\) and \(r_a\) divide \(D\). Since the denominator of \(s_b\) divides \(D\), write \(s_b=S/D\) for an integer \(S\). Then

\(s_b/w_{ab}=Sh/(Dp).\)

Its reduced denominator divides \(Dp\). The minimum is one of these three rational candidates, so the denominator of the selected \(x_{ab}\) also divides \(Dp\).

It follows that the denominator of \(r_a-x_{ab}\) divides \(Dp\). The denominator of \(w_{ab}x_{ab}\) divides \(Dph\), so the denominator of \(s_b-w_{ab}x_{ab}\) divides \(Dph=D_t\). Unchanged entries and residuals have denominators dividing \(D\), which itself divides \(D_t\).

For a zero weight, both candidates have denominators dividing \(D\), the proposer update preserves this property, and the receiver residual is unchanged. This proves the claim by induction. \(\square\)

Each positive weight contributes its numerator and denominator to this product only once. Moreover, each denominator used in \(D_0\) comes from a distinct input field. Therefore,

\(\log_2 D_m\le \log_2 D_0+\sum_{t:\,w_t>0}(\log_2 p_t+\log_2 h_t)=O(L).\)

Reducing input fractions cannot increase their numerator or denominator lengths. Thus this bound applies regardless of whether the supplied fractions are already reduced.

3. Numerator and intermediate-size bounds

Because the shares are positive and sum to one on each side, \(\mu_a\le1\) and \(\nu_b\le1\). Nonnegativity of the maintained residuals gives

\(0\le r_a\le c_a\mu_a,\qquad 0\le s_b\le\rho q_b\nu_b,\qquad 0\le x_{ab}\le U_{ab}.\)

Every input numerator and denominator has at most \(L\) bits. Consequently,

\(c_a\le2^L,\qquad N,\rho,q_b\le2^L,\)

and every positive input weight satisfies \(w_{ab}\ge2^{-L}\). These deliberately coarse bounds imply

\(U_{ab}\le2^{2L},\qquad r_a\le2^L,\qquad s_b\le2^{2L},\qquad s_b/w_{ab}\le2^{3L}.\)

By the denominator invariant, every stored residual, allocation entry, and minimum candidate has a denominator of \(O(L)\) bits. Its magnitude is at most \(2^{O(L)}\), so its reduced numerator also has \(O(L)\) bits.

The remaining temporary expressions have the same bit-length order. Multiplication, division, addition, subtraction, and cross-multiplication of a constant number of fractions with \(O(L)\)-bit numerators and denominators produce integers of \(O(L)\) bits before reduction. Reducing each arithmetic result therefore maintains \(O(L)\)-bit rational representations throughout the algorithm.

In particular, the exact output has total length \(O(mL)\).

4. Bit complexity and conclusion

Two input rates can be compared exactly by cross-multiplication using \(O(L^2)\) bit operations with schoolbook integer multiplication. The supplied tie order can be stored as integer ranks. Comparison sorting therefore costs

\(O\!\left(m\log(m+1)L^2\right).\)

Initializing the bounds and residuals takes \(O(m+\tau)\) rational operations. The greedy pass uses a constant number of rational operations and comparisons per pair, because it maintains residuals rather than recomputing sums.

For completeness, elementary rational arithmetic on the fractions above costs at most \(O(L^3)\) bit operations per operation, including reduction. Integer multiplication and long division cost \(O(L^2)\); Euclid’s algorithm uses \(O(L)\) divisions, since its positive remainders decrease by at least a factor of two every two divisions. This gives the stated conservative reduction bound.

The overall running time is therefore

\(O\!\left(m\log(m+1)L^2+(m+\tau)L^3\right),\)

with \(O((m+\tau)L)\) bits of storage. All dependence on the numerical parameters is accounted for through their binary encoding length \(L\); no expansion into individual populations is required.

This settles the stated exact-computation question in polynomial time.
