The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite applicant and position category sets \(I\) and \(J\), rational distributions over applicant types \(t=(i,j_0,\pi)\) and position types \(u=(j,i_0,\rho)\), and a stable incumbent mass coupling \(\kappa\) with those marginals supported only where \(j=j_0\) and \(i=i_0\), may the preference rankings be changed so that the blocking-pair density reaches \(\beta\) within swap budget \(\varepsilon\)? An edit transfers mass only between types with the same own category and incumbent partner category; its cost is transferred mass times Kendall swap distance between rankings, summed over both sides. For edited distributions \(\nu,\omega\), the blocking-pair density is \(B(\nu,\omega)=\sum_{t,u}\nu_t\omega_u\,\mathbf{1}[\operatorname{rk}_{\pi_t}(j)<\operatorname{rk}_{\pi_t}(j_0)\ \wedge\ \operatorname{rk}_{\rho_u}(i)<\operatorname{rk}_{\rho_u}(i_0)]\); the decision question is whether feasible edits of total cost at most \(\varepsilon\) yield \(B(\nu,\omega)\ge\beta\).

The input also gives a rational incumbent coupling \(\kappa\), listed by its positive entries, with marginals \(\mu,\eta\), supported only on pairs satisfying \(j=j_0\) and \(i=i_0\). All mass is matched, and incumbent partners remain fixed throughout editing.

Given binary rational \(\varepsilon\ge0\) and \(\beta\in[0,1]\), decide whether edits exist with \(C(P,Q)\le\varepsilon\) and \(B(\nu,\omega)\ge\beta\).

Is this continuous blocking-pair-density decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Result

The continuous decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. The proof allows arbitrary real transports, mass splitting, and rankings absent from the input support.

We reduce from Balanced Biclique: given a bipartite graph \(G=(V,W,E)\) and an integer \(k\), decide whether there are subsets \(S\subseteq V\), \(T\subseteq W\) with \(|S|=|T|=k\) and \(S\times T\subseteq E\). This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; see the introduction of [Peeters, *The maximum edge biclique problem is NP-complete*](https://cgi.di.uoa.gr/~vassilis/co/biclique.pdf). We restrict to \(1\le k\le\min\{|V|,|W|\}\), since instances outside this range are trivial.

The key is a quantitative gap that remains valid for fractional choices.

1. A fractional biclique gap

For vectors \(x\in[0,1]^V\) and \(y\in[0,1]^W\), define

\(F_G(x,y)=\sum_{(v,w)\in E}x_vy_w.\)

Lemma 1. If \(G\) contains no balanced biclique of size \(k\), then

\(\sum_vx_v+\sum_wy_w\le 2k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F_G(x,y)\le k^2-\tfrac14.\)

Proof. Put \(a=\sum_vx_v\) and \(b=\sum_wy_w\).

If \(|a-k|\ge\tfrac12\), nonnegativity and \(a+b\le2k\) give

\(F_G(x,y)\le ab\le a(2k-a)=k^2-(a-k)^2\le k^2-\tfrac14.\)

The same calculation applies with \(b\) in place of \(a\) if \(|b-k|\ge\tfrac12\).

It remains to consider

\(k-\tfrac12<a<k+\tfrac12,\qquad k-\tfrac12<b<k+\tfrac12.\)

Fix these sums. Holding \(y\) fixed, maximize the linear function \(F_G(\,\cdot\,,y)\) over

\(P_a=\{z\in[0,1]^V:\sum_vz_v=a\}.\)

This nonempty bounded polytope has an extreme-point maximizer \(x^*\). Every extreme point of \(P_a\) has at most one coordinate strictly between zero and one: if two coordinates were fractional, small opposite perturbations of them would preserve the sum and express the point as the midpoint of two distinct feasible points.

Holding \(x^*\) fixed, similarly choose an extreme-point maximizer \(y^*\) with sum \(b\). Thus

\(F_G(x,y)\le F_G(x^*,y^*).\)

An extreme point with sum in \((k-\tfrac12,k+\tfrac12)\) has at least \(k\) coordinates at least \(\tfrac12\). Indeed, it either has at least \(k\) coordinates equal to one, or has \(k-1\) such coordinates and one fractional coordinate greater than \(\tfrac12\).

Choose \(k\) such coordinates on each side. Since they do not form a biclique, some pair \((v,w)\) among them is absent from \(E\). Consequently,

\(F_G(x^*,y^*)=ab-\sum_{(v,w)\in(V\times W)\setminus E}x_v^*y_w^*\le ab-\tfrac14\le k^2-\tfrac14,\)

where the last inequality follows from \(a+b\le2k\). This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

2. A ranking-distance bound

Lemma 2. Suppose an initial ranking places the incumbent category first and a target category in position \(r\). Every ranking that places the target above the incumbent has Kendall distance at least \(r-1\) from the initial ranking.

Proof. The incumbent–target comparison must reverse. For each of the \(r-2\) categories initially between them, at least one of its comparisons with these two categories must also reverse; otherwise the target could not precede the incumbent.

These are at least \(r-1\) distinct reversed comparisons. Each adjacent swap changes only one unordered comparison, so at least \(r-1\) swaps are necessary. \(\square\)

This bound applies to every destination ranking separately, hence also to arbitrary split transport plans.

3. Constructing the instance

Let \(n=|V|+|W|\). Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\ge n+1\), and set

\(h=N^{-1},\qquad D=64nk^2,\qquad H=64k(D+n)N.\)

All these parameters have polynomial magnitude in \(n\).

Categories, masses, and incumbent coupling

Introduce applicant categories

\(I=\{a_v:v\in V\}\cup\{c_w:w\in W\}\cup\{a_*\}\cup\{z_1^I,\ldots,z_H^I\},\)

and position categories

\(J=\{d_v:v\in V\}\cup\{b_w:w\in W\}\cup\{b_*\}\cup\{z_1^J,\ldots,z_H^J\}.\)

All labels within a side are distinct. The categories \(z_\ell^I,z_\ell^J\) carry no mass. This is permitted by the stated hypotheses. Transport preserves an agent’s own category, so these categories remain without mass throughout editing.

There is exactly one initial ranking for each of the following supported identities:

| Index | Applicant identity \((i,j_0)\) | Position identity \((j,i_0)\) | Mass on each side |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(v\in V\) | \((a_v,d_v)\) | \((d_v,a_v)\) | \(h\) |
| \(w\in W\) | \((c_w,b_w)\) | \((b_w,c_w)\) | \(h\) |
| Auxiliary pair | \((a_*,b_*)\) | \((b_*,a_*)\) | \(1-nh\) |

The last mass is positive because \(N\ge n+1\). Both societies have total mass one.

The incumbent coupling matches the two types in each row, with the indicated mass. It therefore has the required marginals and satisfies \(j=j_0\) and \(i=i_0\).

Call applicants of category \(a_v\) and positions of category \(b_w\) active. All other supported agents are inactive.

Initial rankings

For active applicant \(a_v\), concatenate the following blocks:

  • Its incumbent category \(d_v\).
  • The categories \(z_1^J,\ldots,z_D^J\).
  • All categories \(b_w\) with \((v,w)\in E\), in any fixed order.
  • The categories \(z_{D+1}^J,\ldots,z_H^J\).
  • Every remaining category of positive mass, in any fixed order.

For active position \(b_w\), use the symmetric ranking:

  • Its incumbent category \(c_w\).
  • The categories \(z_1^I,\ldots,z_D^I\).
  • All categories \(a_v\) with \((v,w)\in E\).
  • The categories \(z_{D+1}^I,\ldots,z_H^I\).
  • Every remaining category of positive mass.

Every inactive agent ranks its incumbent first, then all \(H\) barrier categories, then all remaining categories.

These are strict complete rankings. Everyone initially ranks their incumbent first, so \(B(\mu,\eta)=0\).

Budget and threshold

Set

\(\varepsilon=2kh(D+n),\qquad \beta=h^2k^2.\)

Both numbers, and all input masses, are dyadic rationals. Also \(0<\beta<1\), since \(k\le n/2<N\).

The combined support size is \(\tau=2(n+1)\), and

\(m=2(n+1+H).\)

Because \(k\le n\) and \(N<2(n+1)\), we have \(H=O(n^5)\). There are \(2(n+1)\) complete rankings, each of polynomial length, and \(n+1\) positive coupling entries. Thus the construction and its total bit length are polynomial in the source input size.

4. Completeness

Suppose \(S\subseteq V\) and \(T\subseteq W\) form a balanced biclique of size \(k\).

For every \(v\in S\), edit its entire applicant mass by moving its incumbent downward past the first \(D\) barrier categories and all its graph neighbors. This costs exactly \(D+\deg_G(v)\) swaps per unit mass.

Perform the symmetric edit for every active position indexed by \(w\in T\). Leave every other ranking unchanged. These edits preserve all required identities and incumbent partners.

Their total cost is at most

\(h\sum_{v\in S}(D+\deg_G(v))+h\sum_{w\in T}(D+\deg_G(w))\le2kh(D+n)=\varepsilon.\)

For every \((v,w)\in S\times T\), both edited agents prefer the other’s category to their incumbent category. Each such pair of category groups contributes \(h^2\), giving

\(B(\nu,\omega)\ge h^2|S||T|=h^2k^2=\beta.\)

Thus every yes instance of Balanced Biclique produces a yes instance of the continuous problem.

5. Soundness for arbitrary real transports

Suppose \(G\) has no balanced biclique of size \(k\). Consider arbitrary admissible transport plans \(P,Q\) of total cost \(C\le\varepsilon\).

Activation fractions

For each active applicant \(a_v\), let \(x_v\) be the fraction of its mass whose destination ranking places at least one graph-neighbor category above its incumbent. Define \(y_w\) symmetrically for active positions.

Thus \(x_v,y_w\in[0,1]\). Every graph neighbor initially lies in position at least \(D+2\). Lemma 2 therefore gives

\(C\ge h(D+1)\left(\sum_vx_v+\sum_wy_w\right).\)

Consequently, with \(\delta=n/D=1/(64k^2)\),

\(\sum_vx_v+\sum_wy_w\le\frac{\varepsilon}{h(D+1)}\le2k(1+\delta).\)

The scaled vectors \(\bar x=x/(1+\delta)\) and \(\bar y=y/(1+\delta)\) satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma 1. Hence

\(F_G(x,y)\le(1+\delta)^2(k^2-\tfrac14).\)

Since \(0<\delta\le1\), we have \((1+\delta)^2\le1+3\delta\), and therefore

\(F_G(x,y)\le k^2-\tfrac14+3\delta k^2=k^2-\tfrac{13}{64}.\)

Call blocking pairs between active category groups \(a_v,b_w\) with \((v,w)\in E\) graph pairs, and let their total density be \(B_{\mathrm{graph}}\).

For a particular graph edge, the applicant mass preferring \(b_w\) to its incumbent is at most \(hx_v\); the corresponding position mass is at most \(hy_w\). Because the density uses product masses,

\(B_{\mathrm{graph}}\le h^2F_G(x,y)\le h^2(k^2-\tfrac{13}{64}).\)

This argument does not require integral activation fractions.

Bounding every other blocking pair

For an active agent, call a category of positive mass far if it is neither the incumbent category nor a graph-neighbor category. For an inactive agent, every category of positive mass other than its incumbent is far.

Every far category initially lies in position at least \(H+2\). For active agents, this follows because all \(H\) barrier categories precede the remaining positive-mass categories; for inactive agents, it follows directly from their ranking definition.

Call a destination ranking far-reaching if it places at least one far category above its incumbent. Let \(R_A\) and \(R_P\) be the total masses assigned to far-reaching rankings on the applicant and position sides.

Lemma 2, applied separately to each transport entry ending in such a ranking, gives

\(C\ge(H+1)(R_A+R_P).\)

A blocking pair that is not a graph pair must have at least one endpoint with a far-reaching ranking. Indeed, if neither endpoint has such a ranking, an inactive endpoint cannot prefer any positive-mass alternative; two active endpoints can mutually prefer each other only along a graph edge.

Let \(B_{\mathrm{other}}\) be the density of all these other blocking pairs. Each side has total mass one, so summing the endpoint-indicator bound against product masses gives

\(B_{\mathrm{other}}\le R_A+R_P\le\frac{C}{H+1}\le\frac{\varepsilon}{H}.\)

By the chosen parameters,

\(\frac{\varepsilon}{H}=\frac{2kh(D+n)}{64k(D+n)N}=\frac{h^2}{32}.\)

Combining the two density bounds yields

\(B(\nu,\omega)\le h^2(k^2-\tfrac{13}{64})+\frac{h^2}{32}=h^2(k^2-\tfrac{11}{64})<h^2k^2=\beta.\)

Thus no admissible real transport of cost at most \(\varepsilon\) reaches the threshold when the source graph has no required biclique.

6. Conclusion

The construction satisfies every input hypothesis, has polynomial size, and is a yes instance exactly when the source graph contains a balanced biclique of size \(k\). Its soundness proof covers unrestricted destination rankings and arbitrary real mass splitting.

Therefore the continuous blocking-pair-density decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. A running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Source problem and scope

The source problem is stated precisely: find subsets of size \(k\) in the two specified parts of a bipartite graph whose cross product consists entirely of edges. 2. Lemma 1: fractional biclique gap

Every step is valid.

  • Since all coordinates are nonnegative and edges form a subset of the cross product,

\[ F_G(x,y)\le ab. \]
From \(a+b\le2k\), multiplication by \(a\ge0\) gives
\[ ab\le a(2k-a)=k^2-(a-k)^2. \]
Thus \(|a-k|\ge\tfrac12\) yields the claimed gap. The symmetric argument for \(b\) is also correct.

  • In the remaining case, the fixed-sum polytopes are nonempty because they contain the given vectors. They are compact polytopes, so each linear objective has an extreme-point maximizer. The two successive maximizations preserve the respective sums and cannot decrease the objective.
  • The perturbation argument correctly shows that an extreme point has at most one fractional coordinate: two strictly fractional coordinates permit sufficiently small opposite perturbations within the coordinate bounds.
  • With an integer \(k\) and a sum in \((k-\tfrac12,k+\tfrac12)\), such an extreme point has at least \(k\) coordinates at least \(\tfrac12\). The argument covers integral sums as well: a sum equal to \(k\) gives exactly \(k\) unit coordinates.
  • Choosing \(k\) qualifying coordinates on each side must expose a missing edge when the graph has no balanced biclique of size \(k\). Its product contribution is at least \(\tfrac14\). Subtracting this contribution from \(ab\) is valid.
  • Finally, nonnegativity and \(a+b\le2k\) imply \(ab\le k^2\), giving the stated bound.

The boundary cases \(|a-k|=\tfrac12\) or \(|b-k|=\tfrac12\) belong to the first cases. The lemma also applies when \(k=1\).

3. Lemma 2: ranking-distance lower bound

The comparison-counting argument is correct.

The incumbent–target comparison must reverse. For each category initially between them, preserving both its comparison with the incumbent and its comparison with the target would force the incumbent to precede that category and that category to precede the target. This contradicts placing the target above the incumbent.

The comparisons counted for different intermediate categories are distinct, and none is the incumbent–target comparison. Thus at least \(r-1\) comparisons reverse. An adjacent swap changes exactly one unordered comparison, so the Kendall distance is at least \(r-1\).

Applying this lower bound to each destination ranking separately is valid for split transports. It does not impose integrality or any restriction on destination support.
