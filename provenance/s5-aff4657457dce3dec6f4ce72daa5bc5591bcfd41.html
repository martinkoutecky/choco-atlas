The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over finitely many student types, where each type specifies school preferences and priority categories, and school capacities measured as fractions of the cohort, define continuum DA and full-consent EADA by allowing type mass to split at priority cutoffs. For each society, compute the maximum mass \(I^*(\mu)\) that can be reassigned from continuum DA so every unit weakly prefers its new school, and the mass improved by continuum EADA and DA+TTC. Characterize the worst-case ratios \(I^*(\mu)/I_M(\mu)\), over societies with support at most \(\tau\) and positive denominator, for those mechanisms and for Pareto-efficient mechanisms that weakly Pareto-dominate continuum DA.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society \(P\) is given by its support: \(\tau\) student types with positive binary-rational masses \(a_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\) schools with binary-rational capacities \(q_s\in(0,1]\), and an unlimited outside option \(\varnothing\). Each type has strict preferences over schools and \(\varnothing\), and an integer priority category at each school; smaller categories have higher priority. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and any additional parameters.
Represent type \(t\) by labelled units \((t,u)\), where \(u\in[0,a_t)\), with Lebesgue mass \(\lambda\). School priorities order units lexicographically by category, support-list index \(t\), and \(u\). Assignments are measurable maps to schools or \(\varnothing\), respecting capacities. Comparisons hold almost everywhere; a strict Pareto improvement must benefit positive mass.
Continuum DA is synchronous student-proposing deferred acceptance under these fixed priorities: schools retain their highest-priority applicant mass up to capacity, splitting intervals at cutoffs; units stop applying upon reaching \(\varnothing\). Write its assignment as \(D_P\).
Full-consent EADA repeatedly runs DA on the residual society, permanently fixes assignments at every school that rejected no positive mass during that run, and removes those schools and their assigned units. Treat \(\varnothing\) as an under-demanded school.
For DA+TTC, form positive-mass blocks \((t,s)\) of units initially assigned to \(s\) by DA, including \(\varnothing\). Each remaining block points to the lexicographically first remaining owner block of its favorite school among remaining endowments. Simultaneously execute every directed cycle, including self-cycles, exchanging the minimum remaining block mass around that cycle and removing the participating units in increasing \(u\) order; repeat until no mass remains.
For any feasible assignment \(A\) weakly Pareto-dominating \(D_P\), define \(I(A,P)=\lambda\{(t,u):A(t,u)\succ_t D_P(t,u)\}\), \(I^*(P)=\max_{A\succeq D_P}I(A,P)\), and \(I_M(P)=I(M(P),P)\). Pareto efficiency excludes any feasible weak Pareto improvement benefiting positive mass. Define \(\rho_M(\tau)=\sup_{P:\,|\operatorname{supp}(P)|\leq\tau,\ I_M(P)>0}I^*(P)/I_M(P)\), allowing any finite number of schools.

What are the sharp worst-case ratios \(\rho_{\mathrm{EADA}}(\tau)\) and \(\rho_{\mathrm{DA+TTC}}(\tau)\), and the sharp universal bound on \(\rho_M(\tau)\) over Pareto-efficient mechanisms \(M\) that weakly Pareto-dominate continuum DA, whenever the defining domains are nonempty?

For discrete societies with \(n>4\), full-consent EADA and DA+TTC have ratio \((n-1)/2\), which bounds the ratio of every Pareto-efficient mechanism that Pareto-dominates DA. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12871v3)
Leshno and Lo define and characterize continuum school-choice TTC using trade-balance equations; this concerns priority-based TTC rather than the DA-endowment mechanism defined here. No result settling these particular continuum ratios was located. [Source](https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/leshnolo_-_simple_ttc.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total student mass to one and made ordinary school capacities positive rational fractions; included the unlimited outside option in strict preferences.
  • Resolved priority-category ties by support-list order and a persistent within-type label, so priority cutoffs split type mass while preserving individual identities across mechanism runs.
  • Defined Pareto comparisons almost everywhere and required positive mass for strict improvement, excluding changes confined to null sets.
  • Specified full-consent EADA through the under-demanded-school procedure, interpreting rejection as rejection of positive mass and including the outside option.
  • Specified ownership ties and fractional cycle execution for DA+TTC using ordered type–DA-assignment blocks, bottleneck mass, and persistent labels.
  • Used suprema rather than maxima for worst-case ratios, restricted to positive denominators, and left the number of schools unrestricted as in the support-bounded question.

Result

For every integer \(\tau\ge 2\),

\[ \rho_{\mathrm{EADA}}(\tau)=\rho_{\mathrm{DA+TTC}}(\tau)=+\infty. \]

Moreover, over Pareto-efficient mechanisms that weakly Pareto-dominate continuum DA,

\[ \sup_M\rho_M(\tau)=+\infty \qquad (\tau\ge 2). \]

For \(\tau=1\), all the defining domains are empty.

The unboundedness already occurs with two types and dyadic masses and capacities. Below, one family gives exact instance ratios for both specified mechanisms.

1. A two-type family

Choose an integer \(h\ge 3\), and put \(k=2^h-6\) and \(\varepsilon=2^{-h}=1/(k+6)\).

There are two types, \(A\) and \(B\), with masses \(3\varepsilon\) and \((k+3)\varepsilon\), respectively. Their support-list indices are \(1\) and \(2\).

The schools are \(b,x,a_1,\ldots,a_k,y,d\). Thus \(m=k+4\). Capacities are

\[ q_x=q_y=2\varepsilon,\qquad q_b=q_d=q_{a_i}=\varepsilon. \]

Their sum is \(1\). Preferences are

\[ A:\quad b\succ x\succ a_1\succ\cdots\succ a_k\succ y\succ\varnothing\succ d, \]

\[ B:\quad x\succ a_1\succ\cdots\succ a_k\succ y\succ b\succ d\succ\varnothing. \]

At \(x\) and \(y\), type \(A\) has priority category \(0\), and type \(B\) has category \(1\). At every other school, these categories are reversed.

Partition type \(A\) into intervals \(A_0,A_1,A_2\), and type \(B\) into intervals \(B_0,\ldots,B_{k+2}\), each of mass \(\varepsilon\), in increasing \(u\) order. For example, \(B_i\) denotes the units with \(u\in[i\varepsilon,(i+1)\varepsilon)\).

These intervals are only notation for measurable sets. All DA cutoffs in this construction fall on their boundaries: applicant masses and capacities are integer multiples of \(\varepsilon\). Thus the following interval-by-interval execution is exact.

2. Exact execution of DA

The DA assignment is

| Interval | DA school |
|---|---|
| \(A_0,A_1\) | \(x\) |
| \(A_2\) | \(y\) |
| \(B_{i-1}\), for \(1\le i\le k\) | \(a_i\) |
| \(B_k\) | \(y\) |
| \(B_{k+1}\) | \(b\) |
| \(B_{k+2}\) | \(d\) |

Here is the rejection history establishing this assignment.

In round \(1\), all of \(A\) applies to \(b\), which retains \(A_0\) and rejects \(A_1,A_2\). All of \(B\) applies to \(x\), which retains \(B_0,B_1\) and rejects \(B_2,\ldots,B_{k+2}\).

In round \(2\), \(A_1,A_2\) displace \(B_0,B_1\) at \(x\). Meanwhile, the initially rejected \(B\)-intervals begin moving through \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\).

For each \(i\), school \(a_i\) first receives \(B_{i+1},\ldots,B_{k+2}\), retaining \(B_{i+1}\). One round later it receives \(B_{i-1},B_i\), retains \(B_{i-1}\), and rejects \(B_i,B_{i+1}\). This follows inductively from the previous school's rejections, starting with \(B_0,B_1\) rejected from \(x\).

Consequently, \(y\) first receives \(B_{k+2}\), and then \(B_k,B_{k+1}\). It retains \(B_k,B_{k+1}\) and rejects \(B_{k+2}\). The remaining chain is

\[ B_{k+2}\to b,\quad A_0\to x,\quad A_2\to a_1\to\cdots\to a_k\to y,\quad B_{k+1}\to b,\quad B_{k+2}\to d. \]

Each arrow after the first is caused by the preceding displacement. In particular, \(A_2\) is rejected at every \(a_i\), because type \(B\) has higher priority there. At \(y\), it displaces \(B_{k+1}\). At \(b\), \(B_{k+1}\) displaces \(B_{k+2}\), since its \(u\) is smaller.

This gives the displayed DA assignment.

Every proper school except \(d\) rejected positive mass:

  • \(b\) and \(x\) rejected positive mass immediately.
  • Each \(a_i\) rejected positive mass in the initial \(B\)-wave.
  • \(y\) rejected \(B_{k+2}\).

Only \(B_{k+2}\) ever applies to \(d\), and nobody reaches \(\varnothing\). Hence precisely \(d\) and \(\varnothing\) are initially under-demanded.

3. Exact execution of EADA

The first EADA step fixes \(B_{k+2}\) at \(d\), and removes \(d\) and \(\varnothing\).

Run DA again. The same rejection waves now involve only \(B_0,\ldots,B_{k+1}\). School \(y\) receives exactly \(B_k,B_{k+1}\), so it rejects nobody. No \(B\)-interval subsequently applies to \(b\). The residual DA assignment is therefore

\[ A_0\mapsto b,\quad A_1,A_2\mapsto x,\quad B_{i-1}\mapsto a_i,\quad B_k,B_{k+1}\mapsto y. \]

School \(y\) is the only under-demanded school in this run. Indeed, \(b\) rejects \(A_1,A_2\), \(x\) rejects \(B\)-mass, and every \(a_i\) rejects mass in the later rejection wave. EADA fixes \(B_k,B_{k+1}\) at \(y\).

For completeness, consider the subsequent runs. If the remaining \(B\)-intervals are \(B_0,\ldots,B_{j-1}\), the remaining schools are \(b,x,a_1,\ldots,a_j\). DA assigns

\[ A_0\mapsto b,\quad A_1,A_2\mapsto x,\quad B_{i-1}\mapsto a_i \quad(1\le i\le j). \]

To verify the last assertion, \(B_0,B_1\), when present, are first retained at \(x\) and then displaced by \(A_1,A_2\). The initially rejected \(B\)-mass and this later wave cascade through the \(a_i\)'s in priority order. Only \(B_{j-1}\) ever applies to the last school \(a_j\), so \(a_j\) rejects nobody. Each earlier \(a_i\) rejects positive mass; \(b\) and \(x\) also reject positive mass. Thus EADA fixes \(B_{j-1}\) at \(a_j\). This argument also covers \(j=1\), when the sole \(B\)-interval moves from \(x\) directly to \(a_1\).

After all these intervals have been fixed, \(x\) fixes \(A_1,A_2\), followed by \(b\) fixing \(A_0\).

Exactly three intervals improve relative to DA:

\[ A_0:x\to b,\qquad A_2:y\to x,\qquad B_{k+1}:b\to y. \]

Consequently,

\[ I_{\mathrm{EADA}}(P_k)=3\varepsilon. \]

4. Exact execution of DA+TTC

Initially, every \(A\)-block points to \((B,b)\), and every \(B\)-block points to \((A,x)\). The unique cycle is

\[ (A,x)\longrightarrow(B,b)\longrightarrow(A,x). \]

Its minimum block mass is \(\varepsilon\). Under the specified increasing-\(u\) removal rule, it exchanges \(A_0\) and \(B_{k+1}\).

The remaining \((A,x)\)-block, consisting of \(A_1\), then executes a self-cycle. Subsequently, the blocks \((B,a_1),\ldots,(B,a_k)\) execute self-cycles in that order: each owns the highest remaining school in both types' preferences.

After these disappear, the two \(y\)-blocks execute self-cycles. The lexicographically first owner is initially \((A,y)\), because \(A\) has the smaller support-list index. Finally, \((B,d)\) executes its self-cycle.

Thus only

\[ A_0:x\to b,\qquad B_{k+1}:b\to x \]

improve, and

\[ I_{\mathrm{DA+TTC}}(P_k)=2\varepsilon. \]

5. The exact maximum improvement

Consider the feasible assignment

\[ A_0\mapsto b,\quad A_1\mapsto x,\quad A_2\mapsto a_k, \]

\[ B_0\mapsto x,\quad B_{i-1}\mapsto a_{i-1}\quad(2\le i\le k), \]

\[ B_k,B_{k+1}\mapsto y,\qquad B_{k+2}\mapsto d. \]

Every school receives exactly its capacity. All changed intervals strictly improve. The improved mass is

\[ (k+3)\varepsilon. \]

This is also an upper bound, including for arbitrary measurable assignments that split these intervals.

Let \(F\) weakly Pareto-dominate DA. Every unit's DA school is above \(\varnothing\), so no unit can be assigned to \(\varnothing\) under \(F\). Since proper capacities sum to \(1\), every proper school must remain full.

Three disjoint sets supply at least \(3\varepsilon\) unimproved mass:

  • Mass at \(d\). Only \(B_{k+2}\) can weakly accept \(d\) relative to its DA assignment. Hence its entire mass \(\varepsilon\) must remain there.
  • Mass initially at \(x\) of type \(A\). This mass is \(2\varepsilon\), and its only strictly better school is \(b\), of capacity \(\varepsilon\). At least \(\varepsilon\) therefore remains unimproved.
  • Mass initially at \(y\). Apart from the already fixed \(B_{k+2}\), only \(A_2,B_k,B_{k+1}\) can weakly accept \(y\). Since \(y\) needs \(2\varepsilon\) and \(B_{k+1}\) supplies at most \(\varepsilon\), at least \(\varepsilon\) of \(A_2\cup B_k\) stays at \(y\), unimproved.

Therefore

\[ I^*(P_k)=1-3\varepsilon=(k+3)\varepsilon. \]

The exact instance ratios are consequently

\[ \frac{I^*(P_k)}{I_{\mathrm{EADA}}(P_k)} =\frac{k+3}{3}, \qquad \frac{I^*(P_k)}{I_{\mathrm{DA+TTC}}(P_k)} =\frac{k+3}{2}. \]

Since \(k=2^h-6\) is unbounded, both worst-case ratios are infinite for every support cap \(\tau\ge2\).

6. DA+TTC is an admissible efficient mechanism

To establish the universal mechanism bound, it remains to check efficiency against the full feasible assignment space, which permits unused capacity.

Let \(C\) be the number of positive DA blocks, so \(C\le\tau(m+1)\).

Termination and dominance. Every remaining block's own endowment school remains available. Thus its chosen school is weakly preferred to its DA school. Every executed cycle preserves total assigned mass at every school. Its minimum-mass exchange exhausts at least one block, so there are at most \(C\) rounds. The resulting assignment \(T\) is feasible and weakly Pareto-dominates DA.

Unused capacities. DA has the following nonwastefulness property: if positive mass strictly prefers \(s\) to its DA assignment, then \(s\) is full. That mass applied to \(s\) and was rejected; a positive rejection fills the school, and its retained mass never subsequently decreases.

Hence, if \(s\) has unused DA capacity, almost no unit prefers \(s\) to its DA assignment. Since \(T\) dominates DA, almost no unit prefers \(s\) to \(T\).

Efficiency. Suppose \(F\) weakly Pareto-dominates \(T\) and strictly benefits positive mass. Choose the earliest TTC round containing positive mass that benefits under \(F\). Units removed earlier retain their \(T\)-assignments almost everywhere.

A unit removed in this round received its favorite school among all schools with remaining endowment mass. Its improvement must therefore use a school whose endowment was already exhausted, or which had no DA endowment.

If that school was full under DA, all its capacity was assigned to earlier-removed units, whose assignments remain unchanged under \(F\). It cannot accommodate positive new mass.

If it had unused DA capacity, the preceding nonwastefulness argument excludes positive mass strictly preferring it to \(T\). The outside option likewise cannot be a strict improvement, since \(T\) is weakly above the outside option.

There are finitely many schools, so positive benefited mass would require one of these impossible destinations to receive positive mass. This proves Pareto efficiency.

DA+TTC is therefore among the mechanisms in the universal question. Its infinite ratio establishes

\[ \sup_M\rho_M(\tau)=+\infty\qquad(\tau\ge2). \]

7. The one-type domains

With one type, DA fills schools in that type's preference order, assigning smaller \(u\) first, until all mass is placed or the outside option is reached.

This assignment is Pareto-efficient. Starting with the most preferred school, its assigned units cannot move upward. If that school is full, nobody else can enter without displacing them. Continuing down the preference list gives the same conclusion at every occupied school. If a school is only partly filled, all remaining mass is already assigned there, so there is no lower-assigned mass that could benefit.

Thus every mechanism weakly dominating DA must equal DA almost everywhere. DA+TTC returns DA. EADA also returns DA: deleting under-demanded schools and their assigned tails preserves the greedy assignments of the remaining prefix.

Hence the defining domains for \(\tau=1\) are empty. For \(\tau\ge2\), they are nonempty for both specified mechanisms and for every Pareto-efficient mechanism weakly dominating DA, because DA is inefficient in the constructed family.

8. Finite representation and running times

The construction has \(\tau=2\), \(m=k+4\), and \(L=O(m\log m)\). Its input and the assignment certificates above can be produced in \(O(m\log m)\) bit operations.

For general inputs, let \(H\) be a common denominator of all masses and capacities, chosen as their denominators' product. Then \(\log H\le L\). Partition the society into \(H\) cells of mass \(1/H\). All DA cutoffs lie on cell boundaries, so synchronous DA on these cells exactly simulates continuum DA.

A conservative bound for straightforward simulation is

\[ O\!\left(H^2(m+1)^2\log(H+1)\operatorname{poly}(L)\right) \]

bit operations per DA run, and

\[ O\!\left(H^2(m+1)^3\log(H+1)\operatorname{poly}(L)\right) \]

for EADA. These bounds are pseudopolynomial in \(H\), which can be exponential in \(L\).

Once the \(C\le\tau(m+1)\) DA blocks are available, the specified TTC procedure takes at most \(C\) rounds and can be implemented in

\[ O\!\left(C^2(m+1)\operatorname{poly}(L)\right) \]

bit operations.

Finally, \(I^*(P)\) is an attained maximum. Introduce a variable for the mass from each DA block assigned to each weakly preferred destination, impose block-mass equalities and school-capacity inequalities, and maximize the mass sent to strictly preferred destinations. This is a nonempty compact rational transportation polytope. Conversely, every feasible vector is realized by measurably partitioning each block. It has \(O(\tau(m+1)^2)\) variables, so standard rational linear programming computes \(I^*(P)\) in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time once DA is available.

The [cited discrete bound](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12871v3) concerns the number of students. The exact discrete simulation of our family has \(k+6\) cells, despite having only two support types.

The first two rounds have the stated outcomes.

The propagation through the \(a_i\)'s also checks out: the initial wave reaches \(a_i\) with \(B_{i+1},\ldots,B_{k+2}\), followed one round later by \(B_{i-1},B_i\). Within type \(B\), smaller \(u\) has higher priority, so the eventual retained interval is \(B_{i-1}\).

At \(y\), the first applicant interval is \(B_{k+2}\), followed by \(B_k,B_{k+1}\). Its capacity is \(2\varepsilon\), so the stated rejection follows.

The final displacement chain is consistent with both preferences and priorities:

Thus the displayed DA assignment is correct. Every proper school except \(d\) rejects positive mass; neither \(d\) nor the outside option does.

3. The EADA execution and improvement count are correct

After fixing \(B_{k+2}\) at \(d\), the residual DA execution leaves \(A_0\) at \(b\), \(A_1,A_2\) at \(x\), and \(B_k,B_{k+1}\) at \(y\). School \(y\) receives exactly its capacity and rejects no mass.

The subsequent residual description is valid, including \(j=1\). The last remaining \(a_j\) receives only \(B_{j-1}\), while earlier schools reject mass. Consequently, the stated sequence of fixed assignments follows.

Exactly \(A_0,A_2,B_{k+1}\) improve, giving
\(I_{\mathrm{EADA}}(P_k)=3\varepsilon\).

As an additional arithmetic check, direct finite-cell simulations for \(h=3,4,5\) reproduced the displayed DA assignment and the three improving EADA cells. These checks supplement the written argument.

4. The DA+TTC execution and improvement count are correct

Initially, \(b\) is type \(A\)'s favorite remaining endowment, and \(x\) is type \(B\)'s. The unique cycle is therefore the stated two-block cycle.

Its exchanged mass is \(\varepsilon\). Increasing-\(u\) removal selects \(A_0\) from the \(A,x\) block and all of \(B_{k+1}\) from the \(B,b\) block.

The remaining \(A,x\) block then points to itself. The \(B,a_i\) self-cycles occur in preference order. At \(y\), the support-list ordering selects the \(A,y\) block first, followed by the \(B,y\) block.

Only \(A_0\) and \(B_{k+1}\) improve. Hence
\(I_{\mathrm{DA+TTC}}(P_k)=2\varepsilon\).

5. The exact value of \(I^*(P_k)\) is established

The proposed alternative assignment fills every school exactly. Each changed interval strictly improves, and the changed intervals have total mass \((k+3)\varepsilon\).

The upper bound applies to arbitrary measurable assignments, including assignments that split the displayed intervals:

  • Every unit's DA assignment is strictly above the outside option. Weak dominance therefore excludes outside assignments almost everywhere. Since proper capacities sum to \(1\), every proper school must remain full.
  • Only \(B_{k+2}\) can weakly accept \(d\), forcing its entire mass \(\varepsilon\) to remain unimproved.
  • The \(2\varepsilon\) of type \(A\) initially at \(x\) can strictly improve only at \(b\), whose capacity is \(\varepsilon\). At least \(\varepsilon\) remains unimproved.
  • After the forced assignment at \(d\), the possible occupants of \(y\) are \(A_2,B_k,B_{k+1}\). Filling its \(2\varepsilon\) capacity forces at least \(\varepsilon\) of \(A_2\cup B_k\) to remain there, unimproved.

These three unimproved sets are disjoint. Therefore
\(I^*(P_k)=1-3\varepsilon=(k+3)\varepsilon\).

Both exact instance ratios follow, and their divergence establishes the claimed worst-case ratios.

6. DA+TTC is an admissible Pareto-efficient mechanism

The termination and dominance argument is valid. A remaining block always retains its own school as an available endowment, and every round exhausts at least one block. Cycle exchanges preserve assigned mass at every school.

The DA nonwastefulness argument correctly handles unused capacity: a school that rejects positive mass becomes full, and its held mass never decreases.

The efficiency argument also checks out. Under a hypothetical weak improvement, earlier-removed units must retain their assignments almost everywhere. Units in the earliest positively benefited round received their favorite available endowment school. Their strict improvements would therefore require an exhausted school or a school without a DA endowment.

For an exhausted school that was full under DA, earlier-removed units already occupy its entire capacity. For a school with unused DA capacity, nonwastefulness excludes positive mass preferring it to the TTC assignment. The outside option cannot strictly improve any unit.

Thus DA+TTC is Pareto-efficient over the full feasible assignment space. Its unbounded ratio proves the stated universal bound \(\sup_M\rho_M(\tau)=+\infty\). This does not assert that every individual admissible mechanism has an infinite ratio.

7. The one-type claim is correct

With one type, the assignment proceeds greedily through the common preference order. The occupied higher-ranked schools cannot admit additional mass under weak dominance because their existing occupants cannot move to a better available destination.

The partly filled-school and outside-option cases are covered by the argument. DA is therefore Pareto-efficient, and strict preferences force any weakly dominating assignment to agree with it almost everywhere. The defining domains are empty.

The family’s input-size estimate is consistent with its explicit preference lists and dyadic numerical data.
