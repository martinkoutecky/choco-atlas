The anchor (named result in the paper)

Proposition 5 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a fixed finite set of welfare-response types \(T\), rational shares \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and finite weak and strict comparisons between profiles \(x,y\in\mathbb{Q}^T\), decide whether there exist \(c\in\mathbb{R}\) and \(a_t\in\mathbb{R}\) such that \(U_\mu(x)=c\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_t^2+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_t x_t\) respects every comparison, weakly or strictly as specified.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq 3\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; it may vary between instances.
The remaining input consists of finite sets \(R,P\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^T\times\mathbb{Q}^T\) of weak and strict comparisons. Welfare coordinates may have either sign. Write \(m=|R|+|P|\), and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
A rationalization consists of unrestricted real coefficients \(c\) and \((a_t)_{t\in T}\), defining \(U_\mu(x)=c\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_t^2+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_t x_t\).
It must satisfy \(U_\mu(x)\geq U_\mu(y)\) for every \((x,y)\in R\) and \(U_\mu(x)>U_\mu(y)\) for every \((x,y)\in P\). Zero coefficients and empty comparison sets are allowed.

Is deciding whether such a rationalization exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

For finitely many individual welfare coordinates, spherical rationalizability has an exact dual characterization: no probability distribution over observed comparisons assigning positive weight to strict comparisons can simultaneously equalize both the mean welfare vectors and the mean squared norms of their two sides. [Source](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~fechenique/published/orthogonal.pdf)
No result settling this exact mass-weighted decision problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted the fixed finite type set as fixed within each instance, with support size varying between instances.
  • Required at least three supported types to carry over the underlying model's dimension condition.
  • Defined m as the number of supplied comparisons and L as the total binary input length.
  • Made comparison orientation explicit: the first profile is weakly or strictly preferred to the second.
  • Retained unrestricted signed welfare coordinates and coefficients, total indifference, and possibly empty comparison sets, as permitted by the discrete definitions.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\), including when the support size \(\tau\) varies. It reduces exactly to rational linear feasibility with \(m\) inequalities and \(\tau+1\) unrestricted variables. The running time is \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations, hence \(L^{O(1)}\) under the stated explicit encoding.

If \(P=\varnothing\), zero coefficients immediately rationalize every weak comparison. The argument below handles arbitrary nonempty \(P\).

2. Linear formulation

Define the known weighted quadratic feature
\[ Q_\mu(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_t^2. \]
Set \(b_t=\mu_t a_t\). Because every \(\mu_t>0\), this is an invertible change of unrestricted real variables: \(a_t=b_t/\mu_t\). Thus
\[ U_\mu(x)=cQ_\mu(x)+\sum_{t\in T}b_t x_t. \]

Index the observations by \(j=1,\ldots,m\), retaining both designations if a pair belongs to both \(R\) and \(P\). Write observation \(j\) as \((x^j,y^j)\), and define
\[ d_j=\left(Q_\mu(x^j)-Q_\mu(y^j),\ (x_t^j-y_t^j)_{t\in T}\right)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\tau+1}. \]
For \(z=(c,(b_t)_{t\in T})\), direct substitution gives
\[ U_\mu(x^j)-U_\mu(y^j)=d_j\cdot z. \]
Consequently, rationalizability is precisely feasibility of \(d_j\cdot z\geq0\) for weak observations and \(d_j\cdot z>0\) for strict observations. Negative welfare coordinates cause no difficulty: all entries of \(d_j\) are known rational numbers.

3. Exact removal of strict inequalities

Let \(h_j=0\) for a weak observation and \(h_j=1\) for a strict observation. Consider the ordinary, non-strict linear system
\[ d_j\cdot z\geq h_j,\qquad j=1,\ldots,m. \]

We prove that this system is feasible exactly when the original comparisons are rationalizable.

A solution to this system satisfies every original comparison, since a strict observation has utility difference at least \(1>0\).

Conversely, suppose \(z\) satisfies the original comparisons and \(P\neq\varnothing\). Because there are finitely many strict observations,
\[ \delta=\min_{\substack{j:\text{strict}\\\text{observation}}}d_j\cdot z \]
exists and is strictly positive. Replace \(z\) by \(z/\delta\). Every weak difference remains nonnegative, and every strict difference becomes at least \(1\). The rescaled vector therefore satisfies the non-strict system. When \(P=\varnothing\), the two systems already coincide.

This proves the reduction without requiring any advance bound on the original strict margins.

4. Encoding size and running time

We verify that the reduction is polynomial in the binary input length.

Let \(H\) be the product of all positive denominators appearing in the input, and put \(D=H^3\). Since the input has total length \(L\), the integer \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits. Moreover:

  • Every denominator of \(x_t^j-y_t^j\) divides \(D\).
  • Every denominator of \(\mu_t(x_t^j)^2\) and \(\mu_t(y_t^j)^2\) divides \(D\).

Hence multiplying each inequality by \(D>0\) yields an equivalent integer system
\[ Az\geq Dh,\qquad A_{j,\cdot}=Dd_j. \]

Each integer entry has \(O(L)\) bits. To check magnitude as well as denominator size, every input coordinate has absolute value at most \(2^L\), while positivity and normalization of the masses imply
\[ \left|Q_\mu(x^j)-Q_\mu(y^j)\right| \leq\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\left((x_t^j)^2+(y_t^j)^2\right) \leq2^{2L+1}. \]
Also, \(\left|x_t^j-y_t^j\right|\leq2^{L+1}\). Multiplication by the \(O(L)\)-bit integer \(D\) preserves the \(O(L)\) entry-length bound.

The integer system therefore has total encoding length
\[ B=O\!\left((1+m(\tau+1))L\right). \]
Using elementary integer arithmetic, forming it takes \(O((1+m\tau)L^2)\) bit operations: there are \(O(1+m\tau)\) operations on integers of \(O(L)\) bits.

We now use the standard polynomial-time linear-feasibility theorem: feasibility of a finite rational linear system is decidable in time polynomial in its encoding length. This theorem applies to unrestricted variables and does not require the feasible region to be bounded or full-dimensional; those cases are covered by the exact ellipsoid algorithm. See the [MIT notes on the ellipsoid method](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-251j-introduction-to-mathematical-programming-fall-2009/e13f5e6650f7be1e21101a2e7bd0d5b8_MIT6_251JF09_lec18.pdf).

Thus a running-time bound, displaying the dependence on the requested parameters, is
\[ O((1+m\tau)L^2)+\left((1+m(\tau+1))L\right)^{O(1)}. \]
Since the support and comparisons are explicitly listed, \(m,\tau=O(L)\), so this is polynomial in \(L\). No expansion of rational masses into a list of individuals is needed.

5. Exact mass-weighted dual characterization

The same formulation also proves the counterpart of the supplied dual characterization.

For a finite matrix \(M\) and vector \(h\), Farkas’ lemma states that the system \(Mz\geq h\), with unrestricted \(z\), is infeasible exactly when there is \(\lambda\geq0\) such that
\[ M^\mathsf{T}\lambda=0,\qquad h^\mathsf{T}\lambda>0. \]
Apply it with row \(j\) of \(M\) equal to \(d_j\). All hypotheses hold because this is a finite real linear system. Its infeasibility is therefore equivalent to the existence of nonnegative weights satisfying
\[ \sum_{j=1}^m\lambda_j(x_t^j-y_t^j)=0\quad\text{for every }t\in T, \]
\[ \sum_{j=1}^m\lambda_j\left(Q_\mu(x^j)-Q_\mu(y^j)\right)=0, \qquad \sum_{\substack{j:\text{strict}\\\text{observation}}}\lambda_j>0. \]
The last inequality implies \(\sum_j\lambda_j>0\), so the weights can be normalized to sum to \(1\).

Thus rationalization fails exactly when a probability distribution over observed comparisons, giving positive total weight to strict comparisons, equalizes both the mean welfare vectors and the mean weighted quadratic features of the two sides. If a pair has both designations, merging its two weights gives a distribution on \(R\cup P\); conversely, any weight on such a pair can be assigned to its strict designation.

This is the mass-weighted analogue of [Chambers and Echenique, Proposition 5](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~fechenique/published/orthogonal.pdf), derived here directly for the specified input.

The problem is therefore polynomial-time solvable. Under polynomial-time many-one reductions, it could also be \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
