The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\), a list of \(\tau\) rankings \(t\in\mathcal{S}_m\) with binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, and rational \(\epsilon<0.05\), suppose \(\mu=(1-\eta)M(\phi,\pi^*)+\eta\nu\) for unknown \(\phi<1\), central ranking \(\pi^*\), \(\eta\le\epsilon\), and arbitrary distribution \(\nu\) over rankings. Output \((\tilde\phi,\tilde\pi)\) with probability at least \(2/3\) such that \(\|v_{\pi^*}-v_{\tilde\pi}\|_2\le O(\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon)/(1-\phi))\), \(|\phi-\tilde\phi|\le O(\epsilon/\sqrt m)\), and \(D_{\mathrm{TV}}(M(\phi,\pi^*),M(\tilde\phi,\tilde\pi))\le O(\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon))\), in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\), where \(L\) is the input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge2\), and let \(\mathcal S_m\) denote strict rankings of the candidates \([m]\). Write \(v_\pi=(\pi^{-1}(1),\ldots,\pi^{-1}(m))\).
The Kendall–tau Mallows distribution is \(M(\phi,\pi)(t)=\phi^{d_{\mathrm{KT}}(t,\pi)}/Z_m(\phi)\), where \(Z_m(\phi)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal S_m}\phi^{d_{\mathrm{KT}}(\sigma,\pi)}\); at \(\phi=0\), it is the point mass at \(\pi\).
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct rankings with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); unlisted rankings have mass zero. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings, and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
The input also includes binary-rational \(0<\epsilon<0.05\), with the promise \(\mu=(1-\eta)M(\phi,\pi^*)+\eta\nu\) for unknown real \(0\le\phi<1\), \(\pi^*\in\mathcal S_m\), \(0\le\eta\le\epsilon\), and an arbitrary probability distribution \(\nu\) on \(\mathcal S_m\).
Produce \(\tilde\pi\in\mathcal S_m\) and binary-rational \(\tilde\phi\in[0,1]\) such that, simultaneously with probability at least \(2/3\),
\(\|v_{\pi^*}-v_{\tilde\pi}\|_2\le C\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon)/(1-\phi)\), \(|\phi-\tilde\phi|\le C\epsilon/\sqrt m\), and \(D_{\mathrm{TV}}(M(\phi,\pi^*),M(\tilde\phi,\tilde\pi))\le C\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon)\).
Here \(C\) is an absolute constant, and the guarantee is required for every admissible hidden decomposition.

Is this promised estimation problem solvable by a randomized algorithm in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon)\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite samples, [Liu–Moitra](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06380) establish polynomial-time estimation with these error orders from \((m/\epsilon)^2\operatorname{poly}(\log(m/\epsilon))\) independent Mallows samples after an adversary observes them and replaces an \(\epsilon\)-fraction.
No published result for this exact explicit-support estimation problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required m at least two: with one candidate the Mallows distribution does not identify phi, so the stated parameter guarantee cannot hold.
  • Specified positive epsilon, nonnegative phi below one, and contamination rate between zero and epsilon.
  • Specified strict complete rankings, zero mass outside the supplied support, and total input bit length including epsilon.
  • Defined the phi-zero Mallows model as a point mass and allowed a binary-rational output parameter in the closed interval [0,1].
  • Made the hidden constants absolute and required the simultaneous probability guarantee for every admissible hidden decomposition.

Conclusion

The problem is solvable by a randomized algorithm in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon)\).

The finite-sample theorem applies after sampling from the explicitly given society. The needed reduction requires a bound on the random contamination count; conditioning on that count would not suffice. We give an unconditional coupling below.

In fact, after enlarging the absolute constant, the algorithm has a single success event on which its guarantees hold for all admissible hidden decompositions.

1. The finite-sample theorem used

We use [Liu–Moitra, Theorem 1.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06380). For a corruption parameter \(0<\alpha<0.1\), their polynomial-time estimator takes sufficiently many independent samples from an unknown Mallows model, after an adversary replaces at most an \(\alpha\)-fraction, and simultaneously guarantees

\(\displaystyle \|v_{\pi^*}-v_{\widehat\pi}\|_2 \le A\frac{\alpha\log(1/\alpha)}{1-\phi}, \qquad |\phi-\widehat\phi|\le A\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt m}, \)

\(\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\left(M(\phi,\pi^*),M(\widehat\phi,\widehat\pi)\right) \le A\alpha\log(1/\alpha), \)

with failure probability at most \((\alpha/m)^8\), for an absolute constant \(A\). Its sample requirement is \((m/\alpha)^2\operatorname{poly}(\log(m/\alpha))\).

Their final selection uses parameter values \(i\alpha/m^2\), so the selected parameter is a rational of polynomial bit length; see Section 7.1 of the same paper.

Set \(\alpha=2\epsilon\). The input restriction \(\epsilon<0.05\) gives \(\alpha<0.1\), as required.

Choose an integer \(N\) above the theorem’s sample threshold and satisfying

\(\displaystyle N\ge \frac{3\log 100}{\epsilon}.\)

Thus \(N=\operatorname{poly}(m,1/\epsilon)\).

2. Mixture samples satisfy the theorem through a coupling

Fix any admissible hidden decomposition and write

\(\displaystyle P=M(\phi,\pi^*),\qquad \mu=(1-\eta)P+\eta\nu,\qquad \eta\le\epsilon.\)

Independently for each \(i\in[N]\), generate

\(\displaystyle X_i\sim P,\qquad Y_i\sim\nu,\qquad B_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\eta),\)

with all these variables mutually independent. Define \(W_i=X_i\) when \(B_i=0\), and \(W_i=Y_i\) when \(B_i=1\). Then the \(W_i\) are independent samples from \(\mu\).

Let \(K=\sum_i B_i\). Since \(\eta\le\epsilon\), \(K\) is stochastically dominated by \(\operatorname{Binomial}(N,\epsilon)\). The multiplicative Chernoff bound gives

\(\displaystyle \Pr(K>2\epsilon N) \le \exp(-\epsilon N/3) \le \frac1{100}. \)

Now construct a second sample vector \(T\):

  • If \(K\le\lfloor2\epsilon N\rfloor\), perform all the replacements indicated by the \(B_i\), so \(T=W\).
  • Otherwise, perform no replacements, so \(T=(X_1,\ldots,X_N)\).

For every realization, \(T\) is obtained from independent samples from \(P\) by replacing at most \(\lfloor\alpha N\rfloor\) entries. Consequently, it is a valid strongly contaminated sample for the Liu–Moitra theorem. A randomized adversary is allowed here: its guarantee follows by averaging the guarantees for fixed adversary randomness.

Moreover, this coupling satisfies

\(\displaystyle \Pr(T\ne W)\le\frac1{100}. \)

Using the same estimator randomness on both vectors, its output on genuine independent \(\mu\)-samples therefore obeys all three theorem bounds with probability at least

\(\displaystyle 1-(\alpha/m)^8-\frac1{100}.\)

This argument does not condition the clean sample on the event that the contamination count is small.

Finally,

\(\displaystyle \alpha\log(1/\alpha) =2\epsilon\log(1/(2\epsilon)) \le2\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon). \)

Hence these are precisely the requested error orders, up to absolute constants.

3. Sampling from the explicit support in bounded polynomial time

We now implement the sampling step using the binary-rational input, with a fixed bound on the number of random bits.

List the support as \(t_1,\ldots,t_\tau\), and compute its cumulative masses

\(\displaystyle c_0=0,\qquad c_j=\sum_{k=1}^j\mu_{t_k}.\)

These rational numbers have polynomial bit length: a common denominator can be taken to be the product of the input denominators, whose bit length is bounded by their total input length.

Choose an integer \(b\) such that

\(\displaystyle 2^b\ge100N\tau.\)

For each sample, draw \(J\) uniformly from \(\{0,\ldots,2^b-1\}\), set

\(\displaystyle U=\frac{J+1/2}{2^b},\)

and return \(t_j\) when \(c_{j-1}\le U<c_j\).

Let \(\bar\mu\) be the resulting sampling distribution. For any interval, the number of equally spaced grid points it contains differs from its length times \(2^b\) by at most \(2\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\bar\mu,\mu)\le\frac{\tau}{2^b}, \)

and, by independently coupling the coordinates,

\(\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\bar\mu^{\otimes N},\mu^{\otimes N}) \le N D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\bar\mu,\mu) \le\frac1{100}. \)

Thus using these bounded-time samples decreases the success probability by at most another \(1/100\). For every fixed admissible decomposition, all three estimator bounds still hold with probability at least

\(\displaystyle 1-(\alpha/m)^8-\frac2{100}>\frac9{10}.\)

All cumulative-mass computations and comparisons take polynomial time in \(\tau,L,\log N\). The sample list has polynomial length, and invoking the polynomial-time finite-sample estimator consequently gives total running time

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon).\)

4. Dyadic output and its effect on total variation

If “binary-rational” requires a terminating binary expansion, round the selected rational parameter to a dyadic number. We prove a uniform bound justifying this step, including near either endpoint.

Let \(D=\binom m2\). For every fixed central ranking \(\pi\) and \(q,r\in[0,1]\),

\(\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{TV}}(M(q,\pi),M(r,\pi))\le D|q-r|. \)

To prove this, use the inversion-sequence representation of permutations. Each permutation corresponds to a unique sequence with its \(i\)-th entry in \(\{0,\ldots,i-1\}\), and its Kendall–tau distance from \(\pi\) is the sum of those entries. Hence

\(\displaystyle Z_m(q)=\prod_{i=1}^m S_i(q), \qquad S_i(q)=\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}q^k. \)

For \(q\in[0,1]\),

\(\displaystyle S_i'(q) =\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}kq^{k-1} \le(i-1)\sum_{k=0}^{i-2}q^k \le(i-1)S_i(q). \)

It follows that

\(\displaystyle \frac{Z_m'(q)}{Z_m(q)} =\sum_{i=1}^m\frac{S_i'(q)}{S_i(q)} \le D. \)

Write \(p_t(q)=q^{d_{\mathrm{KT}}(t,\pi)}/Z_m(q)\). Differentiating these finite probability formulas and applying the triangle inequality gives

\(\displaystyle \frac12\sum_t|p_t'(q)| \le\frac{Z_m'(q)}{Z_m(q)} \le D. \)

The formulas extend continuously to the endpoints. Integrating the last inequality proves the asserted total-variation bound.

Choose a dyadic mesh \(h\le\epsilon/m^2\), and round \(\widehat\phi\) to a mesh point \(\widetilde\phi\in[0,1]\). Then

\(\displaystyle |\widetilde\phi-\widehat\phi|\le\frac{\epsilon}{m^2}, \qquad D_{\mathrm{TV}}(M(\widetilde\phi,\widehat\pi),M(\widehat\phi,\widehat\pi)) \le\frac{\epsilon}{2}. \)

This adds at most \(\epsilon/\sqrt m\) to the parameter error and at most \(\epsilon/2\) to the distribution error. It does not change the ranking. The dyadic output requires only \(O(\log m+\log(1/\epsilon))\) bits.

We have therefore proved that, for some absolute constant \(B\), every fixed admissible hidden decomposition satisfies the requested three bounds with constant \(B\) and success probability exceeding \(9/10\).

5. One success event works for all hidden decompositions

We can strengthen the preceding conclusion to handle the universal quantifier even if it requires one output to satisfy every admissible decomposition simultaneously.

Let \(\mathcal D\) be the nonempty collection of admissible decompositions of this input. For \(d\in\mathcal D\), denote its Mallows parameters by \((\phi_d,\pi_d)\), and let \(E_d\) be the event that the algorithm’s output satisfies all three bounds with constant \(B\) for that decomposition.

The algorithm uses only the input society, so all \(E_d\) are events on the same probability space. We proved \(\Pr(E_d)>9/10\) for every \(d\). Thus, for any \(d,e\),

\(\displaystyle \Pr(E_d\cap E_e)>4/5.\)

In particular, there exists an output satisfying both sets of bounds. Applying the triangle inequality to such an output proves the deterministic compatibility inequalities

\(\displaystyle |\phi_d-\phi_e|\le\frac{2B\epsilon}{\sqrt m}, \)

\(\displaystyle \|v_{\pi_d}-v_{\pi_e}\|_2 \le B\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon) \left(\frac1{1-\phi_d}+\frac1{1-\phi_e}\right). \)

Also, every admissible model \(P_d=M(\phi_d,\pi_d)\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_d,\mu)\le\eta_d\le\epsilon,\)

so \(D_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_d,P_e)\le2\epsilon\).

Choose one reference decomposition \(d_0\) such that

\(\displaystyle 1-\phi_{d_0}\ge \frac12\sup_{d\in\mathcal D}(1-\phi_d). \)

Such a choice exists because the supremum is positive. This is a choice for the proof; the algorithm need not find it. For every \(d\),

\(\displaystyle \frac1{1-\phi_{d_0}}\le\frac2{1-\phi_d}.\)

On the single event \(E_{d_0}\), compatibility and the triangle inequality now give, for every \(d\in\mathcal D\),

\(\displaystyle \|v_{\pi_d}-v_{\widetilde\pi}\|_2 \le\frac{5B\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon)}{1-\phi_d}, \)

\(\displaystyle |\phi_d-\widetilde\phi|\le\frac{3B\epsilon}{\sqrt m}, \)

\(\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_d,M(\widetilde\phi,\widetilde\pi)) \le B\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon)+2\epsilon. \)

Since \(\log(1/\epsilon)>\log20\), the last expression is also at most an absolute constant times \(\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon)\).

Thus a single absolute constant \(C\) makes all requested bounds hold for every admissible decomposition on \(E_{d_0}\), whose probability exceeds \(9/10\), and therefore exceeds \(2/3\).

6. The sparse-support case

There is also a simple special case. If \(\tau<m!\), some ranking has zero society mass. A Mallows model with \(\phi>0\) assigns positive mass to every ranking, and \(1-\eta>0\); hence every admissible decomposition must have \(\phi=0\).

Its central ranking then has society mass at least \(1-\epsilon>1/2\), so it is uniquely determined. Returning that ranking and parameter \(0\) gives zero error. The sampling reduction above also covers the full-support case where this shortcut is unavailable.
