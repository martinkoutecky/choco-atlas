Result and interpretation

Both decision problems are in \(\mathrm P\). Mass FILI, with its designated strategy and an admissible implementation of its finite assessment phase, attains the optimum. Under the stability flag’s additional assumptions, this equilibrium is coalitionally stable.

The optimum has an explicit formula: match workers in decreasing order of maximum output \(F_iE_i\) to tasks in decreasing order of quality, and count revenue only on high-quality tasks.

I use the usual divisible-population interpretation: each side is a unit-mass atomless standard probability space, represented by \([0,1)\). Public priorities resolve individual ties. For the implementation below, choose one common, report-independent public priority order for equal assessed outputs. The assessment schedule and its completion on unsuccessful-learning histories are part of the mechanism.

The finite-population assertions in the question appear in Theorems 2 and 4 of the [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.02439v6). The proof below establishes the mass result directly.

1. Effort incentives and the high/low dichotomy

Define

\[ w_i=F_iE_i,\qquad a_{ij}=\alpha F_i^2g_j-C_i, \]

and let \(\mathcal H\) and \(\mathcal L\) denote the high- and low-quality task types, respectively.

For a high-quality task,

\[ a_{ij}\geq \alpha f_{\min}^2g_j-c_{\max}>0. \]

For a low-quality task,

\[ a_{ij}\leq \alpha f_{\max}^2g_j-c_{\min}<0. \]

Thus these signs are independent of the worker type.

Worker utility at effort \(e\) is \(a_{ij}e^2\). Since both \(0\) and \(E_i\) belong to the permitted grid, its maximum is

\[ U_i^{\max}(g_j)= \begin{cases} (\alpha F_i^2g_j-C_i)E_i^2,&j\in\mathcal H,\\ 0,&j\in\mathcal L. \end{cases} \]

The maximizing effort is uniquely \(E_i\) on high-quality tasks and uniquely \(0\) on low-quality tasks. Moreover, \(U_i^{\max}(g)\) is strictly increasing among high-quality tasks, and every high-quality task is strictly preferred to every low-quality task.

The designated assessment ranking also orders tasks by decreasing quality: for fixed \(i\),

\[ (\alpha F_i^2g_j-C_i)E_i^2 \]

is strictly increasing in \(g_j\), including on low-quality tasks.

Finally, all high-quality tasks have greater quality than all low-quality tasks, because

\[ \frac{c_{\max}}{\alpha f_{\min}^2} \geq \frac{c_{\min}}{\alpha f_{\max}^2}. \]

If \(c_{\min}=0\), there are no low-quality tasks.

2. A revenue upper bound for every admissible mechanism

This bound uses individual rationality and permanent matching; it does not assume that effort is eventually constant.

Fix an admissible outcome. Almost every worker has a permanent partner after some finite, possibly individual-dependent, time.

Consider a type-\(i\) worker whose permanent partner has low-quality type \(j\). Put \(b_{ij}=-a_{ij}>0\). The worker’s finite initial history contributes zero to the Cesàro limit. Consequently, nonnegative long-run utility implies

\[ 0\leq U=-b_{ij}\lim_{T\to\infty} \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^{T}e_t^2. \]

The squared-effort averages are nonnegative, so their limit is zero. By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[ 0\leq \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^{T}e_t \leq \left(\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^{T}e_t^2\right)^{1/2} \longrightarrow 0. \]

Hence this worker’s long-run gross revenue is zero.

For a worker permanently assigned to high-quality type \(j\), every operational-period revenue is at most \(F_iE_ig_j=w_ig_j\). The finite initial history again disappears from the average.

All period revenues are uniformly bounded by \(W_{\max}\max_jg_j\). Applying the bounded reverse-Fatou inequality to individual revenue averages therefore gives

\[ R\leq \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\sum_{j\in\mathcal H}x_{ij}w_ig_j. \]

In particular, no uniform bound on individual stabilization times was needed.

Define effective qualities

\[ h_j= \begin{cases} g_j,&j\in\mathcal H,\\ 0,&j\in\mathcal L. \end{cases} \]

Every admissible individually rational outcome is bounded by the transportation optimum

\[ R_{\mathrm{tr}}= \max_x\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}w_ih_j, \]

subject to the prescribed row and column masses.

3. Exact solution of the transportation problem

Relabel workers so that \(w_1\geq\cdots\geq w_\tau\), and tasks so that \(g_1>\cdots>g_m\). Then \(h_1\geq\cdots\geq h_m\).

Set

\[ P_0=Q_0=0,\qquad P_i=\sum_{r=1}^{i}\mu_r,\qquad Q_j=\sum_{s=1}^{j}\nu_s. \]

The monotone coupling is

\[ x^*_{ij} = \max\!\left\{ 0,\, \min(P_i,Q_j)-\max(P_{i-1},Q_{j-1}) \right\}. \]

It is feasible because it consists of intersections of the worker intervals \([P_{i-1},P_i)\) with the task intervals \([Q_{j-1},Q_j)\).

Here is a direct proof of optimality.

For any feasible \(x\), put

\[ y_i^{(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{ij}. \]

Then \(0\leq y_i^{(k)}\leq\mu_i\) and \(\sum_i y_i^{(k)}=Q_k\). Among all vectors satisfying these constraints, the largest value of \(\sum_iw_i y_i^{(k)}\) is obtained by taking the first \(Q_k\) mass of workers in decreasing output order.

To verify this last statement, choose a cutoff value \(\lambda\). The greedy vector \(y^*\) fills types with \(w_i>\lambda\), leaves types with \(w_i<\lambda\) empty, and takes the required residual mass from types with \(w_i=\lambda\). For any feasible \(y\),

\[ \sum_iw_i(y_i-y_i^*) = \sum_i(w_i-\lambda)(y_i-y_i^*) \leq 0. \]

Each summand is nonpositive: above the cutoff \(y_i^*=\mu_i\), below it \(y_i^*=0\), and at it the coefficient vanishes.

Now expand the transportation objective as

\[ \sum_{i,j}w_ih_jx_{ij} = h_m\sum_iw_i\mu_i + \sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(h_k-h_{k+1}) \sum_iw_i y_i^{(k)}. \]

All coefficients \(h_k-h_{k+1}\) are nonnegative. The monotone coupling simultaneously gives the greedy vector for every prefix \(Q_k\), so it maximizes this expression.

Thus the exact upper bound is

\[ R^*= \sum_{j\in\mathcal H}g_j \sum_iw_ix^*_{ij}. \]

Equivalently, if \(W(s)=w_i\) on \([P_{i-1},P_i)\), then

\[ R^*= \sum_{j\in\mathcal H}g_j \int_{Q_{j-1}}^{Q_j}W(s)\,ds. \]

4. FILI attains the bound and is an equilibrium

First consider a completed assessment phase in which every worker has assessed every task type.

Under the designated strategy, the assessed output of a type-\(i\) worker is \(w_i\) on every task type. All workers report the same task order, namely decreasing \(g_j\).

Deferred acceptance therefore fills the highest-quality task with the highest-ranked worker mass, then fills the second-highest task with the highest-ranked remaining mass, and continues in this way. This follows directly from its retention rule:

  • Everyone initially proposes to the highest-quality task.
  • That task retains its highest-ranked capacity.
  • Rejected mass proposes to the next task, which retains the highest-ranked remaining mass.
  • The process continues through the task list.

Consequently, the distribution of maximum outputs assigned to each task is exactly the monotone distribution from Section 3. Ties can change the identities assigned, but not these output distributions or revenue.

Operational effort is \(E_i\) on high-quality tasks and zero on low-quality tasks. FILI therefore realizes \(R^*\).

Unilateral deviations

A worker can never produce more than \(w_i\) in assessment. Thus an assessment deviation can only lower that worker’s priority at a task; public tie priority cannot be improved by a report.

Suppose the worker’s designated assignment is task \(j\). The worker masses assigned to tasks \(1,\ldots,j-1\) are truthful and exert maximum assessment effort. Inductively, each such task retains its original mass: those workers propose there in the designated order, and the deviator cannot outrank workers who previously caused its rejection.

Hence the deviator cannot obtain a strictly higher-quality task. This assertion is pointwise under the public cutoff convention, apart from null cutoff-boundary sets; that is sufficient for the required almost-everywhere equilibrium condition.

If its designated task is high-quality, no effort strategy at the same or a lower-quality task exceeds its designated utility. If its designated task is low-quality, its utility is zero, and a strict improvement would require gaining a high-quality task, which the preceding argument excludes.

Finite assessment and reporting payoffs vanish from long-run utility. After permanent implementation, reports cannot change the partner, and operational effort already maximizes utility. This excludes arbitrary history-dependent unilateral effort and report deviations.

Individual rationality

Worker utilities are positive on high-quality tasks and zero on low-quality tasks.

For clients, write

\[ H(z)=z-\alpha z^2. \]

Since \(0\leq z\leq W_{\max}\) and \(\alpha W_{\max}<1/2\), one has \(H(z)\geq0\). Client utility is \(g_jH(z)\), so it is nonnegative in every period, under any feasible effort.

5. Learning task types without observing latent types

The preceding argument must not silently give the planner task-type labels. The following finite implementation supplies them from permitted observations and remains robust against every potentially profitable deviation used in the proof.

Let

\[ q=2^{-(L+2)}. \]

Every positive input mass exceeds or equals \(2^{-L}\), so \(q\) is smaller than the mass of the highest-quality task type. The mechanism is a member of a public family indexed by \(q\); executing it requires no latent-type lookup.

Its assessment phase is as follows.

  • Initial assessment. Use an arbitrary public full matching. Every worker’s designated effort is its privately known \(E_i\).
  • Select survey workers. Select the highest-output \(q\) mass, using the common public priority for output ties.
  • Survey all clients. Partition the public client coordinate into \(1/q\) blocks of mass \(q\). In successive periods, match the survey workers to each block. Match all remaining individuals feasibly.
  • Recover qualities. Whenever a client has positive observed output \(z\) and gross revenue \(r\), the planner learns its actual quality as \(r/z\). If some positive client mass remains unidentified after the survey, permanently implement a fixed public fallback matching. Otherwise proceed.
  • Complete type assessments. Group clients by their now-observed qualities. Let \(D\) be the least common multiple of the denominators of their masses. Arrange their quality groups consecutively on \([0,1)\). In period \(r\), match worker coordinate \(u\) to client coordinate \((u+r/D)\bmod 1\), for \(r=0,\ldots,D-1\).

Each task interval has endpoints that are multiples of \(1/D\) and has positive integer length in these units. Every worker therefore visits every task type during this schedule. Record, for example, the first assessment output on each identified task type. Then collect rankings and apply mass deferred acceptance.

All selections and matching rules use public identities, observed output and revenue, and worker reports. In particular, the planner never needs to recover worker productivity, cost, or effort bound. Workers can obtain their ranking directly from the payments and costs they experience at assessment effort \(E_i\).

The fallback makes the mechanism admissible on unsuccessful-learning histories: it still fixes all partners after finitely many periods.

Why profitable deviations cannot disrupt the survey

Under the designated profile, the selected survey workers are the first \(q\) mass in the common maximum-output priority order. FILI assigns all of them to the highest-quality task, because its mass exceeds \(q\).

Each survey worker therefore receives its maximum possible long-run utility across all tasks and effort strategies. If all tasks are low-quality, this maximum is zero; otherwise it is its full-effort utility on the highest-quality task.

Consequently:

  • A survey worker has no profitable unilateral deviation.
  • No coalition whose members all strictly improve almost everywhere can contain a positive mass of survey workers.

For a potentially profitable unilateral deviation by another worker, or an all-strictly-improving coalition, the survey workers thus follow their designated strategy. Their initial outputs remain unchanged. Every other worker’s initial output can only decrease relative to its maximum, so the selected survey group also remains unchanged, up to null sets.

Its members exert positive maximum effort while surveying every client block. All client qualities are therefore recovered, and the fallback is not reached under any potentially profitable deviation under consideration.

This establishes the learning premise used in Section 4. It also supplies the learning premise for the coalitional argument below.

6. Coalitional stability

There are two separate issues: coordinated manipulation within FILI and permanent rematching outside it.

Coordinated strategies within FILI

Let \(A_j\) be the worker mass assigned to task type \(j\) under the designated outcome, with tasks ordered by decreasing quality.

Suppose a measurable coalition with positive worker mass makes every member strictly better off almost everywhere. By Section 5, its deviation leaves the survey successful. Nonmembers then submit truthful rankings and retain their maximum assessment scores; coalition members cannot increase their assessment scores.

No positive mass of \(A_1\) can belong to this coalition: its members already obtain their maximum possible utility over all tasks and efforts.

Thus \(A_1\) is truthful almost everywhere. It proposes to task 1 and retains that task. Indeed, it was the highest-ranked capacity at that task under truthful scores, and outsiders’ scores cannot increase.

With task 1 occupied by this unchanged mass, no coalition member originally in \(A_2\) can strictly improve: it cannot obtain task 1, and no lower-quality task offers more than its designated utility. Hence \(A_2\) is also truthful almost everywhere. After rejection by task 1, it proposes to task 2, where it is the highest-ranked remaining capacity and is retained.

Repeating this argument through the finite task list excludes positive coalition worker mass from every \(A_j\). This contradicts the supposed positive worker mass of the coalition.

Clients have no action affecting this mechanism’s rankings or physical observations: payments are compulsory, and client reports are unused. A coalition with only clients cannot change the outcome. Thus there is no all-strictly-improving internal coalition.

This proves exactly the stated all-strict-improvement requirement; it does not assert resistance to coalitions containing helpers who merely remain indifferent.

Permanent rematching outside FILI

Suppose a worker currently assigned to quality \(g_a\) and a client of quality \(g_b\), currently assigned a worker of maximum output \(w_k\), could both strictly improve by permanently matching together.

The worker can strictly improve only if task \(b\) is high-quality and

\[ g_b>g_a. \]

This follows from its designated effort being optimal and from the utility comparisons in Section 1.

The monotone matching then implies

\[ w_k\geq w_i. \]

For completeness, \(H\) is strictly increasing on \([0,W_{\max}]\): whenever \(z'<z\),

\[ H(z)-H(z') =(z-z')\bigl(1-\alpha(z+z')\bigr)>0, \]

because \(\alpha(z+z')\leq2\alpha W_{\max}<1\).

The client’s designated utility is \(g_bH(w_k)\), since task \(b\) is high-quality. Under any effort sequence of the proposed worker, its long-run utility is at most

\[ g_bH(w_i)\leq g_bH(w_k). \]

The client therefore cannot strictly improve. No worker–client pair can make both members strictly better off.

A permanent rematching by a coalition consists of new pairs within that coalition. If every member strictly improved almost everywhere, almost every new pair would make both members strictly better off, contradicting the preceding result. Outside options of zero cannot strictly improve any member’s designated nonnegative utility either.

Thus FILI is coalitionally stable. The additional productivity–cost–effort ordering required by the stability flag is sufficient; under the present high/low dichotomy, this proof in fact does not need that ordering.

7. Decision algorithm and running times

The algorithm computes \(R^*\) and accepts precisely when \(R^*\geq B\).

| Stability flag | Decision complexity | FILI attains the optimum |
|---|---|---|
| Off | \(\mathrm P\) | Yes |
| On, with the required ordering | \(\mathrm P\) | Yes, with coalitional stability |

Let \(n=m+\tau\). Count the encodings of \(\alpha\), \(\delta\), and \(B\) in \(L\). If the stated \(L\) omits the threshold encoding, replace \(L\) below by \(L+\operatorname{bits}(B)\).

The algorithm performs these steps:

  • Compute \(w_i=F_iE_i\) and classify tasks using exact rational comparisons.
  • Sort worker outputs and task qualities.
  • Sweep the two sorted mass lists. At each step allocate the smaller remaining mass and advance at least one list.
  • Accumulate its exact revenue contribution and compare the resulting rational with \(B\).

The sweep has at most \(m+\tau-1\) positive allocations.

All intermediate rationals can be represented with \(O(L)\) bits after reduction: a common denominator can be formed from products of the input denominators, whose total bit length is \(O(L)\); the relevant formulas involve only constant-degree products of input values.

Using a conservative \(O(L^3)\) bound for exact rational arithmetic, the decision running time is

\[ O\!\left((m+\tau)\log(m+\tau+1)L^3\right). \]

The same bound accommodates checking the input hypotheses, including \(E_i/\delta\in\mathbb N\), the high/low inequalities, and the ordering required by the stability flag. The sparse monotone coupling uses \(O((m+\tau)L)\) bits.

The effort grid is never enumerated. Its encoded size enters through \(L\); the possibly exponential number \(1+E_i/\delta\) of effort levels does not enter the running time.

Finally, computational decision time must be distinguished from assessment duration. The implementation in Section 5 uses at most

\[ 1+2^{L+2}+D+1 \]

periods before permanent implementation, with \(D\leq2^L\). This can be exponential in \(L\), and that phenomenon is unavoidable for a literal assessment of every worker on a rare task type: if that type has mass \(\nu_j\), assessing unit worker mass on it requires at least \(1/\nu_j\) periods.

Nevertheless, the schedule is finite on every history, and the support-based algorithm computes the exact infinite-horizon optimum without executing that schedule.
