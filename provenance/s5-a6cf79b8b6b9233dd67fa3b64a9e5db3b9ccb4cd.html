The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a population scale \(N\), role counts \(N_i\), a rational reversible interaction kernel \(K\) satisfying \(N_iK_{ij}=N_jK_{ji}\), a fixed quasi-majority function \(f\), and initial opinion counts by role, consider independent synchronous updates using the paper's local switching probabilities. What is a multiplicative approximation to \(t_{N^{-c}}\), the least round \(t\) by which exact consensus occurs with probability at least \(1-N^{-c}\), and can it be computed in time parameterized by the number of roles, input bit length, and \(\log N\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Types are initial role–opinion pairs \((i,b)\), with \(m\) roles and \(b\in\{0,1\}\); masses \(w_{ib}\) sum to \(1\). Input includes binary \(N\geq2\), with \(N_i=N(w_{i0}+w_{i1})\) positive integers and \(a_i(0)=Nw_{i0}\) integers. Let \(L\) be total input bit length.
The rational kernel \(K\) is irreducible, row-stochastic, and satisfies \(N_iK_{ij}=N_jK_{ji}\). Its largest absolute eigenvalue apart from the simple eigenvalue \(1\) is \(\lambda\) (set \(\lambda=0\) for one role); impose \(\lambda\leq C N^{-1/4}\) for fixed \(C\geq1\).
Fix \(f:[0,1]\to[0,1]\), independent of the input, admitting exact rational evaluation in polynomial time. It is \(C^2\), satisfies \(f(0)=0\), \(0<f(1/2)<1\), and, for \(H_f(x)=x(1-f(1-x))+(1-x)f(x)\), satisfies \(H_f(x)<x\) on \((0,1/2)\), \(H_f'(1/2)>1\), and \(H_f'(0)<1\).
Given zero-opinion counts \(a_i(t)\), put \(q_i(t)=\sum_jK_{ij}a_j(t)/N_j\). Each round independently across individuals, a zero switches to one with probability \(f(1-q_i(t))\), and a one switches to zero with probability \(f(q_i(t))\). Equivalently, \(a_i(t+1)\) is the sum of independent \(\operatorname{Bin}(a_i(t),1-f(1-q_i(t)))\) and \(\operatorname{Bin}(N_i-a_i(t),f(q_i(t)))\), independently across roles conditional on the current state.
Let \(T=\inf\{t\geq0:\sum_i a_i(t)\in\{0,N\}\}\) and, for fixed rational \(c>0\), let \(t_{N^{-c}}=\min\{t\geq0:\Pr(T\leq t)\geq1-N^{-c}\}\), with value \(\infty\) if this set is empty. A factor-two approximation is \(\widehat t\) satisfying \(t_{N^{-c}}\leq\widehat t\leq2t_{N^{-c}}\); preserve values \(0\) and \(\infty\) exactly.

Is computing a factor-two approximation to \(t_{N^{-c}}\) polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L,\log N\), or NP-hard? Does it admit time \(g(m)\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,\log N)\) for some computable \(g\)?

These are bounds on the stochastic process, rather than an algorithm for computing its consensus-time quantile. Status and new partial result

The general complexity questions remain unresolved. I prove a polynomial-time factor-two approximation on a nontrivial family of inputs, including arbitrary numbers of roles and unequal binary role masses.

Write
$$ a=\min\left\{\sum_i a_i(0),\,N-\sum_i a_i(0)\right\}, \qquad \rho=H_f'(0). $$

The following results hold for each fixed permitted $f,C,c$.

Theorem 1. Fix a rational $b$ with $0<b<1/4$. On the promised instances satisfying
$$ a\leq N^b, $$
a factor-two approximation to $t_{N^{-c}}$ is computable in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,\log N$. The algorithm preserves $0$ and $\infty$ exactly.

Theorem 2. If $\rho>0$, Theorem 1 holds for every fixed rational $b$ with $0<b<1$. Moreover, uniformly over these instances with $a\geq1$,
$$ t_{N^{-c}} = \frac{\log a+c\log N}{-\log\rho}+O(1) \qquad(N\longrightarrow\infty). $$
The constant in $O(1)$ depends only on $f,C,c,b$.

Thus the requested parameterized running time also exists on these restricted families. Neither theorem covers all initial configurations.

1. Definitions and a drift estimate

Let
$$ \pi_i=\frac{N_i}{N},\qquad x_i=\frac{a_i}{N_i},\qquad \alpha=\sum_i\pi_i x_i,\qquad A=\sum_i a_i=N\alpha. $$
All quantities in this section refer to an arbitrary current state.

The update rule is invariant under exchanging the opinion labels: row stochasticity gives $K(\mathbf1-x)=\mathbf1-Kx$. We may therefore label the initially less numerous opinion as zero. Its initial count is then $A_0=a$.

Consensus states are absorbing, since $f(0)=0$. Consequently,
$$ \Pr(T>t)=\Pr(0<A_t<N). $$

Differentiating $H_f$ at zero gives
$$ \rho=1-f(1)+f'(0). $$
Because $f(0)=0$ and $f$ is nonnegative, its right derivative satisfies $f'(0)\geq0$. Also $f(1)\leq1$. Hence
$$ 0\leq\rho<1. $$
If $\rho=0$, both nonnegative summands vanish:
$$ f(1)=1,\qquad f'(0)=0. $$

1.1 Spectral estimates

Use the weighted inner product
$$ \langle u,v\rangle_\pi=\sum_i\pi_i u_i v_i. $$
Detailed balance makes $K$ self-adjoint in this inner product. It also makes $\pi$ stationary, because
$$ \sum_i\pi_iK_{ij} =\pi_j\sum_iK_{ji} =\pi_j. $$

Put
$$ v=x-\alpha\mathbf1,\qquad d=Kx-\alpha\mathbf1=Kv. $$
The vector $v$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf1$. Therefore the definition of $\lambda$ implies
$$ \|Kv\|_\pi\leq\lambda\|v\|_\pi. $$
Since $x_i^2\leq x_i$,
$$ \|v\|_\pi^2 =\sum_i\pi_i x_i^2-\alpha^2 \leq\alpha(1-\alpha). $$
It follows that
$$ \sum_i\pi_i d_i=0,\qquad \sum_i\pi_i d_i^2\leq\lambda^2\alpha(1-\alpha), $$
and
$$ \left|\sum_i\pi_i x_i d_i\right| =|\langle v,Kv\rangle_\pi| \leq\lambda\alpha(1-\alpha). $$
For one role, $v=0$, so these statements agree with the convention $\lambda=0$.

1.2 Conditional mean

Let $B=\max_{[0,1]}|f''|$, and write
$$ \mu=\mathbb E[A_{t+1}\mid a(t)]. $$
Taylor expansion around $\alpha$ gives
$$ \frac{\mu}{N}-H_f(\alpha) = \bigl(f'(1-\alpha)-f'(\alpha)\bigr) \sum_i\pi_i x_i d_i+R, $$
where
$$ |R|\leq\frac B2\sum_i\pi_i d_i^2. $$
Indeed, the first-order coefficient for role $i$ is
$$ x_i f'(1-\alpha)+(1-x_i)f'(\alpha), $$
and its constant part disappears after summing against $d_i$.

The mean value theorem yields
$$ |f'(1-\alpha)-f'(\alpha)|\leq B|1-2\alpha|. $$
Consequently,
$$ \left|\frac{\mu}{N}-H_f(\alpha)\right| \leq B\left(\lambda|1-2\alpha|+\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\right) \alpha(1-\alpha). $$

A stochastic matrix has all eigenvalues of absolute value at most one, so $\lambda\leq1$. Since $H_f$ is $C^2$, $H_f(0)=0$, and $H_f'(0)=\rho$, Taylor’s theorem now supplies a fixed constant $D>0$ such that
$$ \boxed{ |\mu-\rho A| \leq D\left(\frac{A^2}{N}+\lambda A\right). } $$
This estimate uses no lower bound on individual role masses.

1.3 Conditional moment inequalities

Conditional on the current state, $A_{t+1}$ is a sum of independent Bernoulli variables. If $Y$ denotes any such sum with mean $\mu$, then
$$ \mathbb E[Y^2]\leq\mu^2+\mu $$
and, for every $\theta>0$,
$$ \mathbb E[e^{\theta Y}] \leq\exp\bigl((e^\theta-1)\mu\bigr). $$
The first inequality follows from $\operatorname{Var}(Y)\leq\mu$. For the second, multiply the Bernoulli generating functions and use $1+z\leq e^z$.

We will also use
$$ \Pr(Y\geq k)\leq\frac{\mu^k}{k!} \qquad(k\geq1). $$
To prove it, write $(Y)_k=Y(Y-1)\cdots(Y-k+1)$. Independence gives
$$ \mathbb E[(Y)_k]\leq\mu^k, $$
because the left side sums products over distinct ordered indices, while the expansion of $\mu^k$ includes these products and further nonnegative terms. On $Y\geq k$, $(Y)_k\geq k!$.

2. Positive derivative: an additive-constant quantile estimate

Assume $0<\rho<1$, and fix $0<b<1$. Suppose
$$ 1\leq a\leq N^b. $$
Choose
$$ \eta=\frac{1+b}{2},\qquad M=\lfloor N^\eta\rfloor. $$
For sufficiently large $N$, $a\leq M/2$ and $M<N$.

For states with $A\leq M$, the drift estimate and the spectral promise imply
$$ r_-A\leq\mathbb E[A_{t+1}\mid a(t)]\leq r_+A, $$
where
$$ r_\pm=\rho\pm\varepsilon_N,\qquad \varepsilon_N=D\left(N^{\eta-1}+CN^{-1/4}\right). $$
Here $\varepsilon_N\log N\longrightarrow0$. For sufficiently large $N$,
$$ 0<r_-<r_+\leq r_0:=\frac{1+\rho}{2}<1. $$

2.1 Exponentially unlikely excursions

Choose fixed $\theta>0$ small enough that
$$ r_0(e^\theta-1)\leq r_1\theta, \qquad r_1:=\frac{1+r_0}{2}<1. $$
Such a choice exists because $(e^\theta-1)/\theta\longrightarrow1$ as $\theta\downarrow0$.

Let
$$ \sigma=\inf\{t\geq0:A_t>M\}. $$
Before $\sigma$, the conditional exponential-moment bound gives
$$ \mathbb E[e^{\theta A_{t+1}}\mid a(t)] \leq e^{r_0A_t(e^\theta-1)} \leq e^{\theta A_t}. $$
Thus $e^{\theta A_{t\wedge\sigma}}$ is a nonnegative supermartingale. Its expectation is at most $e^{\theta a}$; on $\sigma\leq t$, it is at least $e^{\theta M}$. Therefore, for every $t$,
$$ \Pr(\sigma\leq t)\leq e^{-\theta(M-a)}. $$
Taking the increasing union over $t$ gives
$$ \Pr(\sigma<\infty)\leq e^{-\theta(M-a)}. $$

There is also a uniform one-step bound. For any state with $A\leq M$, exponential Markov inequality gives
$$ \Pr(A_{t+1}>M\mid a(t)) \leq e^{-\theta M+r_0M(e^\theta-1)} \leq e^{-\kappa M}, $$
where $\kappa=\theta(1-r_1)>0$.

2.2 First and second moments of the killed process

Define
$$ Z_t= \begin{cases} A_t,&\sigma>t,\\ 0,&\sigma\leq t, \end{cases} \qquad u_t=\mathbb E[Z_t],\qquad v_t=\mathbb E[Z_t^2]. $$

Killing can only decrease the next count, so
$$ u_{t+1}\leq r_+u_t, \qquad u_t\leq a r_+^t. $$
The expected count discarded in a transition is at most
$$ N\Pr(A_{t+1}>M\mid a(t)) \leq d_N:=Ne^{-\kappa M}. $$
Hence
$$ u_{t+1}\geq r_-u_t-d_N, $$
and iteration yields
$$ u_t\geq a r_-^t-\frac{d_N}{1-r_-}. $$

The Bernoulli second-moment inequality gives
$$ v_{t+1}\leq r_+^2v_t+r_+u_t. $$
Using $u_t\leq a r_+^t$ and $v_0=a^2$, we obtain
$$ v_t \leq a^2r_+^{2t} + a r_+^t\frac{1-r_+^t}{1-r_+} \leq a^2r_+^{2t}+\frac{a r_+^t}{1-r_+}. $$

Fix any constant $B_0>0$. Uniformly for $0\leq t\leq B_0\log N$, for sufficiently large $N$,
$$ r_-^t\geq\frac12\rho^t,\qquad r_+^t\leq2\rho^t, \qquad \frac{d_N}{1-r_-}\leq\frac14a\rho^t. $$
For the first two assertions, when $\varepsilon_N\leq\rho/2$,
$$ |\log r_\pm-\log\rho| \leq\frac{2\varepsilon_N}{\rho}; $$
multiplication by $B_0\log N$ tends to zero. The third assertion follows because $d_N$ decreases faster than every negative power of $N$, whereas $a\rho^t\geq N^{-B_0|\log\rho|}$.

Put $z=a\rho^t$. The preceding bounds imply
$$ u_t\geq\frac z4, \qquad v_t\leq4z^2+\frac{2z}{1-r_0}. $$
Cauchy–Schwarz, applied to $Z_t\mathbf1_{\{Z_t>0\}}$, gives
$$ \Pr(Z_t>0)\geq\frac{u_t^2}{v_t} \geq k_0\min\{1,z\}, $$
where the fixed positive constant
$$ k_0=\frac{1}{64+32/(1-r_0)} $$
suffices.

On $Z_t>0$, the original chain is in a state with $1\leq A_t\leq M<N$, so consensus has not occurred. Conversely, outside the excursion event, survival requires $Z_t>0$. We have therefore proved
$$ \boxed{ k_0\min\{1,a\rho^t\} \leq\Pr(T>t) \leq2a\rho^t+e^{-\theta(M-a)} } $$
uniformly for $t\leq B_0\log N$.

2.3 Locating the quantile

Let
$$ d_\rho=-\log\rho,\qquad t_*=\frac{\log a+c\log N}{d_\rho}. $$
Then
$$ a\rho^{t_*}=N^{-c}, \qquad \frac{c\log N}{d_\rho} \leq t_* \leq\frac{(b+c)\log N}{d_\rho}. $$
Choose $B_0>(b+c)/d_\rho+1$.

At
$$ t_{\mathrm{up}} =\left\lceil t_*+\frac{\log4}{d_\rho}\right\rceil, $$
we have $a\rho^{t_{\mathrm{up}}}\leq N^{-c}/4$. For sufficiently large $N$, the excursion bound is at most $N^{-c}/2$. Hence
$$ \Pr(T>t_{\mathrm{up}})\leq N^{-c}. $$

At
$$ t_{\mathrm{low}} =\left\lfloor t_*-\frac{\log(2/k_0)}{d_\rho}\right\rfloor, $$
we have
$$ a\rho^{t_{\mathrm{low}}}\geq\frac{2}{k_0}N^{-c}. $$
For sufficiently large $N$, $N^{-c}<k_0/2$, and the lower survival bound therefore gives
$$ \Pr(T>t_{\mathrm{low}})>N^{-c}. $$
Both times lie in the range where the bounds apply. Thus
$$ t_{\mathrm{low}}<t_{N^{-c}}\leq t_{\mathrm{up}}, $$
which proves
$$ t_{N^{-c}}=t_*+O(1) $$
uniformly over $1\leq a\leq N^b$.

2.4 A polynomial-time factor-two algorithm

Choose and hardwire a rational number $r$ satisfying
$$ \rho<r<\sqrt\rho. $$
Define
$$ \widehat t=\min\{t\geq0:a r^t\leq N^{-c}\}. $$
If
$$ \gamma=\frac{-\log\rho}{-\log r}, $$
then $1<\gamma<2$ and
$$ \widehat t=\lceil\gamma t_*\rceil. $$

There is a fixed $D_0$ such that $|t_{N^{-c}}-t_*|\leq D_0$ for all sufficiently large instances under consideration. Since $t_*$ tends to infinity uniformly, eventually
$$ (\gamma-1)t_*\geq D_0, \qquad (2-\gamma)t_*\geq2D_0+1. $$
These inequalities imply
$$ t_{N^{-c}}\leq\widehat t\leq2t_{N^{-c}}. $$

The defining comparison is exact and polynomial-time. Write $c=u/v$ and $r=p/q$, with positive integers $u,v,p,q$. Then
$$ a r^t\leq N^{-c} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a^vN^up^{tv}\leq q^{tv}. $$
Only $O(\log N)$ candidate rounds are needed, and all integers in these comparisons have $O(\log N)$ bits, with constants depending on the fixed parameters.

This proves Theorem 2 for sufficiently large $N$. Bounded populations are handled in Section 4.

3. Zero derivative: polynomial computation by truncation

Assume $\rho=0$, and fix $0<b<1/4$. Suppose
$$ 1\leq a\leq N^b. $$
Put
$$ \delta=\frac14-b>0. $$

The drift estimate gives
$$ \mathbb E[A_1] \leq D\left(\frac{a^2}{N}+CN^{-1/4}a\right) \leq D_1N^{-\delta} $$
for a fixed constant $D_1$. Here $2b-1\leq b-1/4=-\delta$.

For any fixed integer $K\geq1$, every state with $1\leq A\leq K$ satisfies
$$ \mathbb E[A_{t+1}\mid a(t)] \leq D_KN^{-1/4} =:s_N, $$
where $D_K$ is fixed.

Choose fixed integers $K,H$ such that
$$ \delta(K+1)>c+2, \qquad \frac{H-2}{4}>c+2. $$

3.1 Controlling discarded trajectories

Consider the event that the count first exceeds $K$ at one of the times $1,\ldots,H$.

For the first round, the conditional mean is at most $D_1N^{-\delta}$. At any later round before such an excursion, it is at most $D_KN^{-1/4}\leq D_KN^{-\delta}$. The factorial-moment bound from Section 1 therefore implies that the probability of an excursion by time $H$ is at most
$$ E_N = \frac{H D_*^{K+1}}{(K+1)!} N^{-\delta(K+1)}, \qquad D_*=\max\{D_1,D_K\}. $$
In particular,
$$ E_N=o(N^{-(c+2)}). $$

Kill the count chain whenever, after the initial round, its count is zero or exceeds $K$. Let $P_t$ denote its surviving mass at time $t$, for $1\leq t\leq H$. Because $N>K$ for sufficiently large $N$,
$$ 0\leq\Pr(T>t)-P_t\leq E_N. $$
Each surviving state has one-step survival probability at most $s_N$, by Markov’s inequality. Thus
$$ P_t\leq s_N^{t-1}, $$
and
$$ \Pr(T>H-1)\leq s_N^{H-2}+E_N<N^{-c} $$
for sufficiently large $N$. Consequently,
$$ 1\leq t_{N^{-c}}\leq H-1. $$

We also have, for $1\leq t\leq H-1$,
$$ \boxed{ \Pr(T>t+1)\leq s_N\Pr(T>t)+E_N. } $$
Indeed, on surviving paths with $A_t\leq K$, conditional survival for another round is at most $s_N$. Surviving paths with $A_t>K$ belong to the excursion event.

3.2 The truncated state space

Let
$$ \mathcal S_K = \left\{y\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^m: 1\leq\sum_i y_i\leq K,\ y_i\leq N_i\right\}. $$
Its size $S$ satisfies
$$ S\leq\binom{m+K}{K}-1=O(m^K). $$
Since $K$ is fixed, this is polynomial in $m$.

Let $v_1$ be the probabilities of moving from the given initial state into $\mathcal S_K$, and let $M_K$ be the transition matrix restricted to $\mathcal S_K$. Then
$$ P_t=v_1M_K^{t-1}\mathbf1. $$

These probabilities must be approximated rather than expanded as enormous exact rationals. The following observation supplies the required binary-input computation.

Numerical observation. Given a destination count $k\leq K$, a probability
$$ \Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)=k) =\binom nk p^k(1-p)^{n-k} $$
can be approximated, with certified lower and upper rational bounds of width at most $\varepsilon$, in time polynomial in the bit lengths of $n,p$ and $\log(1/\varepsilon)$.

To justify this, $\binom nk$ is computed using at most $K$ factors, and has $O(K\log N)$ bits. The power $(1-p)^{n-k}$ is computed by binary exponentiation with directed rounding. All power factors lie in $[0,1]$; rounding errors are amplified by at most $O(N\log N)$. Increasing the working precision by $O(\log N)$ bits therefore controls these errors. Multiplication by the coefficient requires only another $O(K\log N)$ bits. The cases $p=0,1$ and $k>n$ are handled exactly.

For a transition from $y$ to $z\in\mathcal S_K$, the probability is
$$ \prod_i \sum_{h=0}^{z_i} \Pr\bigl(\operatorname{Bin}(y_i,1-f(1-q_i))=h\bigr) \Pr\bigl(\operatorname{Bin}(N_i-y_i,f(q_i))=z_i-h\bigr), $$
with impossible terms interpreted as zero. This formula also applies when $y$ is the initial state, whose counts need not be bounded by $K$.

Every requested destination count is at most $K$. Each $q_i$ and each value of $f$ can be evaluated exactly with polynomial bit complexity. The numerical observation, followed by interval addition and multiplication, therefore computes certified lower approximations to every entry of $v_1$ and $M_K$ in polynomial time.

3.3 A threshold test that avoids equality difficulties

Put $\beta_N=N^{-c}$. Compute a dyadic rational $r_N$ satisfying
$$ r_N\leq\beta_N<2r_N. $$
For example, if $c=u/v$, choose the least integer $k$ with
$$ 2^{kv}\geq N^u, $$
and set $r_N=2^{-k}$. This uses integers of $O(\log N)$ bits.

Increase the fixed population cutoff so that
$$ s_N\leq\frac1{64}, \qquad E_N\leq\frac{r_N}{128}. $$

Compute entrywise lower approximations to $v_1,M_K$, each with error at most
$$ \varepsilon=\frac{r_N}{128HS}. $$
Because these approximations are entrywise lower bounds, their row sums are at most one. Exact propagation for the fixed number $H$ of rounds gives lower estimates $\widetilde P_t$ satisfying
$$ 0\leq P_t-\widetilde P_t\leq HS\varepsilon=\frac{r_N}{128}. $$
This follows by induction: the initial total error is at most $S\varepsilon$, and each subsequent multiplication adds at most $S\varepsilon$ to the total error.

Combining numerical and excursion errors gives
$$ 0\leq\Pr(T>t)-\widetilde P_t\leq\frac{r_N}{64}. $$

Return the first $t\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$ satisfying
$$ \widetilde P_t\leq\frac{r_N}{4}. $$
Every such $t$ satisfies
$$ \Pr(T>t)\leq\frac{17r_N}{64}<\beta_N, $$
so the returned value is at least the true quantile.

Let $q=t_{N^{-c}}$. We know $1\leq q\leq H-1$, and the one-step survival bound gives
$$ \Pr(T>q+1) \leq\frac{\beta_N}{64}+\frac{r_N}{128} <\frac{5r_N}{128} <\frac{r_N}{4}. $$
Thus the test succeeds by round $q+1$. The returned value consequently satisfies
$$ q\leq\widehat t\leq q+1\leq2q. $$
No decision about equality at the original quantile threshold is required.

There are $O(m^K)$ states, a fixed number of rounds, and polynomially many transition evaluations. The required precision has polynomial bit length. This proves Theorem 1 for $\rho=0$ and sufficiently large $N$.

4. Bounded populations: a polynomial-time factor-two lemma

To complete the algorithms above for all population sizes, it remains to treat $N$ below a fixed cutoff. The count state space then has constant size. Exact iteration need not be efficient: a rational transition probability can be extremely small as a function of $L$. The following lemma handles that issue.

Lemma. For a rational finite Markov chain with a fixed number of states, a fixed initial state, and an absorbing target set, the quantile at threshold
$$ \beta=1-N^{-u/v} $$
admits a polynomial-time factor-two approximation, preserving $0$ and $\infty$. Here $u,v$ are fixed positive integers and $N$ is bounded by a fixed constant.

The running time is polynomial in the encoding length of the transition matrix.

4.1 Absorption probability and infinite quantiles

Initial membership in the target set gives quantile zero. Assume otherwise, and let $S\geq1$ be the number of non-target states.

Let $D$ consist of non-target states reachable from the initial state without hitting the target and able to reach the target. Every state in $D$ has a positive-probability path to the target of length at most $S$.

If $Q_D$ is the restricted transition matrix, these paths imply that some fixed positive fraction of mass leaves $D$ in each block of $S$ rounds. Hence $Q_D^t\longrightarrow0$. The eventual absorption probabilities are obtained from
$$ (I-Q_D)h_D=r_D, $$
where $r_D$ gives one-step probabilities of entering the target. This constant-dimensional rational system is solved with polynomial bit complexity. Let $h$ be the resulting absorption probability from the initial state.

If $h<\beta$, the quantile is infinite. If $h>\beta$, it is finite.

If $h=\beta$, the quantile is finite exactly when the directed graph induced by $D$ is acyclic. A reachable cycle can be repeated arbitrarily many times before following a positive-probability path to the target, so successful paths have unbounded lengths and the limit $h$ is never attained at finite time. If $D$ is acyclic, every successful path reaches the target within $S$ rounds.

All comparisons with $\beta$ are exact: for rational $z\in[0,1]$,
$$ z\geq\beta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N^u(1-z)^v\leq1. $$
In the finite equality case, exact iteration for at most $S$ rounds finds the quantile.

4.2 Quantitative bounds when $h>\beta$

Let $d$ be a denominator of the rational number $h$, and put $g=h-\beta>0$. The positive rational number
$$ N^{-u}-(1-h)^v $$
is at least $1/(N^ud^v)$. Since $x\mapsto x^v$ has derivative at most $v$ on $[0,1]$, the mean value theorem implies
$$ g\geq G:=\frac{1}{vN^ud^v}. $$
The bit length of $G$ is polynomial in the input length.

Let $p_0$ be the least positive transition probability and put $p=p_0^S$. Every state in $D$ reaches the target within $S$ rounds with probability at least $p$.

Write $F(t)=\Pr(T\leq t)$. Conditioning on the state at time $t$ gives
$$ F(t+S)-F(t)\geq p\bigl(h-F(t)\bigr). $$
Indeed, the remaining successful probability is the sum of current non-target state probabilities multiplied by their eventual absorption probabilities. For each state in $D$, absorption within $S$ rounds has probability at least $p$, and its eventual absorption probability is at most one.

Consequently,
$$ h-F(kS)\leq(1-p)^k. $$
Choose
$$ H=S\left\lceil\frac{2}{pG}\right\rceil. $$
Then
$$ h-F(H)\leq e^{-2/G}<G\leq h-\beta, $$
so $F(H)>\beta$. The bit length of $H$ is polynomial in the input length.

4.3 Approximate binary search with a certified answer

First compute $F(t)$ exactly for $0\leq t\leq4S$. If the quantile occurs there, return it exactly. Otherwise, the quantile is greater than $4S$, and we have a bracket
$$ 4S< t_\beta\leq H. $$

Set
$$ \epsilon=\frac{pG}{64}. $$
For any binary-search time $t$, a rational approximation $\widetilde F(t)$ with
$$ |\widetilde F(t)-F(t)|\leq\epsilon $$
is computable in time polynomial in the matrix encoding length and $\log t$. Binary matrix exponentiation suffices: the matrices are stochastic, their dimension is fixed, and working precision of
$$ O\bigl(\log t+\log(1/\epsilon)\bigr) $$
bits controls accumulated rounding errors. One may use downward rounding to keep intermediate matrices substochastic; the row-sum error then obeys the usual product bound and is at most $O(t\log t)$ times the rounding unit.

At a candidate $t$, use exact algebraic threshold comparisons to proceed as follows:

  • If $\widetilde F(t)<\beta-2\epsilon$, then $F(t)<\beta$.
  • If $\widetilde F(t)>\beta+2\epsilon$, then $F(t)>\beta$.
  • Otherwise, $|F(t)-\beta|\leq3\epsilon$.

In the third case,
$$ h-F(t)\geq G-3\epsilon\geq G/2. $$
The block increment inequality gives
$$ F(t+S)-F(t)\geq pG/2. $$
It also gives
$$ F(t)-F(t-S) \geq p\bigl(h-F(t-S)\bigr) \geq pG/2. $$
Since $3\epsilon<pG/2$, these imply
$$ F(t-S)<\beta<F(t+S). $$
Thus
$$ t-S<t_\beta\leq t+S. $$
The binary search starts above $4S$, so $t\geq4S$. Returning $t+S$ is therefore valid:
$$ t_\beta\leq t+S\leq2(t-S)<2t_\beta. $$

If no third case occurs, ordinary binary search ends with adjacent integer endpoints and identifies the quantile exactly. There are polynomially many search iterations because $\log H$ is polynomially bounded. This proves the lemma.

Thus the state-space size is a constant.

At bounded $N$, every binomial exponent is bounded. Exact rational evaluation of $f$ therefore gives a rational transition matrix with polynomial encoding length. The lemma applies and preserves zero and infinite quantiles.
