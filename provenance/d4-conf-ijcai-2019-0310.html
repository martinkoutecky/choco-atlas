The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite role-type set \(\Theta\), rational mass distribution \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{\Theta}\) with \(\sum_{\theta}\mu_\theta=1\), binary friendship matrix \(W_{\theta,\theta'}\), finite undirected topology \(G=(V,E)\), rational capacities \(b_v\), and threshold \(\sigma\), decide whether there exists \(x_{\theta,v}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_v x_{\theta,v}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta,v}\le b_v\), with total welfare \(\sum_{\theta,v}x_{\theta,v}u_{\theta,v}(x)\ge\sigma\), where \(u_{\theta,v}(x)\) is the neighboring friend-mass fraction.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) role types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), where \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. All mass is strategic and freely assignable.
Input also includes a symmetric matrix \(W\in\{0,1\}^{\Theta\times\Theta}\), a simple undirected topology \(G=(V,E)\) with \(m=|V|\ge3\), positive binary rational capacities \(b_v\) with \(\sum_v b_v>1\), and a binary rational threshold \(\sigma\). The entry \(W_{\theta,\theta'}\) specifies friendship between individuals of the indicated roles; diagonal entries may be either value.
An assignment consists of real masses \(x_{\theta,v}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_vx_{\theta,v}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta,v}\le b_v\); roles may split and share vertices.
Define \(d_v(x)=\sum_{w:\{v,w\}\in E}\sum_{\theta'}x_{\theta',w}\) and \(f_{\theta,v}(x)=\sum_{w:\{v,w\}\in E}\sum_{\theta'}W_{\theta,\theta'}x_{\theta',w}\). Set \(u_{\theta,v}(x)=f_{\theta,v}(x)/d_v(x)\) when \(d_v(x)>0\), and \(u_{\theta,v}(x)=0\) otherwise. Mass at the same vertex contributes neither to numerator nor denominator.
The decision problem asks whether some assignment satisfies \(\sum_{\theta,v}x_{\theta,v}u_{\theta,v}(x)\ge\sigma\); no equilibrium condition is imposed. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is this continuous welfare-threshold decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete social Schelling games, welfare-threshold decision is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even with all agents strategic and topology of maximum degree \(2\). [Elkind et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0038.pdf)
No result settling this exact mass formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the friendship matrix symmetric, matching an undirected social network; allowed either diagonal value because a role represents distinct individuals whose within-role friendship must be specified.
  • Defined utility using total mass at adjacent vertices as the denominator, excluding same-vertex mass and assigning zero utility when neighboring mass is zero.
  • Made all mass strategic and freely assignable, matching the anchor's fully strategic restriction; imposed no equilibrium requirement.
  • Specified real-valued assignments, allowing role splitting and vertex sharing subject to capacities.
  • Required positive rational capacities, total capacity strictly greater than one, and at least three topology vertices, carrying over available locations and the discrete model's strict excess of locations over agents.
  • Specified a binary rational threshold with no sign restriction, matching the discrete threshold domain; defined m as the topology size and L as total binary input length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Proven result

The continuous welfare-threshold problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(m=3\), the topology is the triangle \(K_3\), and \(\sigma=1\). Thus hardness holds on a topology of maximum degree \(2\).

The proof uses a polynomial-time reduction from PARTITION. Its key feature is that welfare \(1\) forces certain pairs of roles to remain entirely together, despite the permission to split roles and share vertices.

1. Welfare \(1\) forces every occupied role-location pair to have utility \(1\)

Write the welfare as \(F(x)=\sum_{\theta,v}x_{\theta,v}u_{\theta,v}(x)\).

Since \(W\) has entries in \(\{0,1\}\), we have \(0\le f_{\theta,v}(x)\le d_v(x)\). Together with the zero-denominator convention, this gives \(0\le u_{\theta,v}(x)\le1\). Also, mass conservation gives \(\sum_{\theta,v}x_{\theta,v}=1\). Consequently,

\(F(x)\le1,\qquad 1-F(x)=\sum_{\theta,v}x_{\theta,v}\bigl(1-u_{\theta,v}(x)\bigr).\)

Every summand on the right is nonnegative. Therefore, if \(F(x)\ge1\), then \(F(x)=1\), and every pair with \(x_{\theta,v}>0\) satisfies \(u_{\theta,v}(x)=1\). In particular, such a pair must have \(d_v(x)>0\).

2. A lemma that prevents splitting

Lemma. Suppose the topology is complete, and two positive-mass roles \(p,q\) satisfy \(W_{p,q}=W_{q,p}=0\). In every assignment with welfare \(1\), all mass of both roles is concentrated at one common vertex.

Proof. Choose a vertex \(v\) with \(x_{p,v}>0\), which exists because \(\mu_p>0\). By Section 1, \(d_v(x)>0\) and \(u_{p,v}(x)=1\).

For every \(w\ne v\), completeness makes \(w\) a neighbor of \(v\). Since role \(q\) is unfriendly to role \(p\),

\(d_v(x)-f_{p,v}(x)=\sum_{w\ne v}\sum_{\theta'}(1-W_{p,\theta'})x_{\theta',w}\ge\sum_{w\ne v}x_{q,w}.\)

Utility \(1\) implies \(d_v(x)-f_{p,v}(x)=0\). Hence all mass of \(q\) lies at \(v\), so \(x_{q,v}=\mu_q>0\). Applying the same argument to role \(q\) at \(v\) shows that all mass of \(p\) also lies there. This proves the lemma.

3. Reduction from PARTITION

PARTITION on positive binary-encoded integers is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; see [Karp’s original paper](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9). We use the form with positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) satisfying

\(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i=2B,\)

and ask whether some subset sums to \(B\). Restricting to even total preserves hardness: multiplying every input integer by \(2\) preserves the existence of an equal partition and makes the total even.

Construct the following instance.

  • Topology: \(G=K_3\), with vertices \(v_1,v_2,v_3\).
  • Roles: For every item \(i\), introduce two roles \(p_i,q_i\), each with mass \(\mu_{p_i}=\mu_{q_i}=a_i/(4B)\). Thus \(\tau=2n\).
  • Friendship: Set \(W_{p_i,q_i}=W_{q_i,p_i}=0\) for every \(i\), and set every other entry to \(1\), including all diagonal entries.
  • Capacities: Set \(b_{v_1}=b_{v_2}=(2B+1)/(4B)\) and \(b_{v_3}=1/(4B)\).
  • Threshold: Set \(\sigma=1\).

All masses are positive, and their sum is

\(\sum_{i=1}^n(\mu_{p_i}+\mu_{q_i})=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{a_i}{2B}=1.\)

All capacities are positive, and

\(b_{v_1}+b_{v_2}+b_{v_3}=1+\frac{3}{4B}>1.\)

Thus the construction satisfies every input requirement. All fractions have binary-encoded integer numerators and denominators.

4. An equal partition yields welfare \(1\)

Suppose \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=B\).

Place both roles of each item in \(I\) entirely at \(v_1\), and both roles of every remaining item entirely at \(v_2\). Leave \(v_3\) empty.

Each occupied vertex receives mass \(B/(2B)=1/2\), within its capacity. Every occupied role sees neighboring mass \(1/2\). Its sole unfriendly role is its paired role, which lies at the same vertex and therefore contributes neither to its numerator nor to its denominator. All neighboring mass is friendly.

Hence every occupied role-location pair has utility \(1\), and the welfare is \(1\).

5. Welfare \(1\) yields an equal partition

Conversely, suppose an assignment has welfare at least \(\sigma=1\). Section 1 gives welfare exactly \(1\).

By the lemma, each pair \(p_i,q_i\) must be concentrated together at one vertex. Its total mass is \(a_i/(2B)\). No pair can occupy \(v_3\), because

\(\frac{a_i}{2B}\ge\frac{1}{2B}>\frac{1}{4B}=b_{v_3}.\)

Therefore all pairs occupy either \(v_1\) or \(v_2\).

For \(j\in\{1,2\}\), let \(A_j\) be the sum of the integers corresponding to pairs placed at \(v_j\). Capacity feasibility implies

\(\frac{A_j}{2B}\le\frac{2B+1}{4B},\qquad A_j\le B+\frac12.\)

Since \(A_j\) is an integer, \(A_j\le B\). Every item is assigned to one of these two vertices, so \(A_1+A_2=2B\). These inequalities force \(A_1=A_2=B\), giving an equal partition.

This argument covers arbitrary real assignments: splitting is excluded by the welfare condition itself.

6. Encoding size and computational consequence

The constructed instance has \(m=3\), \(\tau=2n\), and total binary encoding length

\(L=O\bigl(n^2+n\log(B+1)\bigr).\)

The quadratic term accounts for the explicitly supplied friendship matrix. If \(S\) denotes the source instance’s binary length, the construction takes \(O(S^2)\) bit operations when fractions are supplied as numerator-denominator pairs; requiring reduced fractions still gives polynomial time.

We have therefore established a polynomial-time many-one reduction from PARTITION. An algorithm running in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) for the stated problem would decide PARTITION in polynomial time. Consequently, the problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and has no such polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
