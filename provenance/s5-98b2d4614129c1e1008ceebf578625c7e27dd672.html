The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a continuous society with finite type set \(T=\{1,\ldots,k\}\), rational masses \(\mu_a\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), rational innate opinions \(s_a\in[0,1]\), resistances \(\alpha_a\in(0,1]\), a symmetric nonnegative rational contact matrix \(W\), and rational walk weights \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_R\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), let \(d_a=\sum_b\mu_bW_{ab}>0\), \(Q_{ab}=\mu_bW_{ab}/d_a\), and \(Q_\beta=\sum_{r=1}^R\beta_rQ^r\). Given a rational tolerance \(\eta>0\), compute \(\widehat z\) such that \(\|\widehat z-z^\star\|_\infty\le\eta\), where \(z^\star=\Lambda s+(I-\Lambda)Q_\beta z^\star\) and \(\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\): the types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), with \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has rational innate opinion \(s_a\in[0,1]\) and resistance \(\alpha_a\in(0,1]\). Contacts are specified by a symmetric nonnegative rational matrix \(W\); its support graph is connected, and \(d_a=\sum_b\mu_bW_{ab}>0\) for every type. Diagonal contacts are allowed between members of the same type.
The input explicitly lists \(R\ge1\) rational weights \(\beta_r\ge0\), with \(\sum_{r=1}^R\beta_r=1\). Define \(Q_{ab}=\mu_bW_{ab}/d_a\), \(Q_\beta=\sum_{r=1}^R\beta_rQ^r\), and \(\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_\tau)\).
Let \(z^\star\) be the unique equilibrium satisfying \(z^\star=\Lambda s+(I-\Lambda)Q_\beta z^\star\). Given binary-rational \(\eta>0\), the computational problem is to produce a rational vector \(\widehat z\) satisfying \(\|\widehat z-z^\star\|_\infty\le\eta\).
Let \(m\) count positive unordered contact pairs, including diagonal pairs, and let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including the tolerance. State running times in \(m,\tau,R,L\).

Can this additive approximation problem be solved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,R,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agent networks, an additive infinity-norm error bound is known for a sparsified iteration, depending on spectral approximation tolerance, iteration count, agent count, and minimum resistance; it is not a polynomial bit-complexity classification. [Zhang, Xu, Zhang, and Chen](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.03569v3).
No result settling exactly the mass-supported approximation problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Removed zero-mass types and used tau for the positive-mass support size.
  • Carried over connectedness of the underlying contact network as connectedness of the contact support graph.
  • Retained diagonal type contacts: distinct agents of the same type can interact, so a loop-free agent graph does not require a zero diagonal in the type contact matrix.
  • Specified that the walk weights are explicitly listed, the output vector is rational, and L includes every rational input and the tolerance.
  • Defined m as the number of positive unordered type contact pairs, including diagonal pairs.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,R,L\). In fact, the equilibrium can be computed exactly as a rational vector of polynomial encoding length. No lower bound on the numerical value of the minimum resistance is needed.

Throughout, rational inputs use their usual binary numerator-and-denominator encoding.

1. Existence and uniqueness. Write \(P=Q_\beta\) and \(A=(I-\Lambda)P\). Because each \(d_a\) is positive,
\[ Q_{ab}\ge 0,\qquad \sum_b Q_{ab}=1. \]
Thus every \(Q^r\), and consequently \(P\), is row-stochastic. Set \(\alpha_{\min}=\min_a\alpha_a>0\). The induced infinity norm satisfies
\[ \|A\|_\infty=\max_a\sum_b A_{ab}=1-\alpha_{\min}<1. \]
Therefore the Neumann series \(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A^k\) converges and is the inverse of \(I-A\). In particular,
\[ z^\star=(I-A)^{-1}\Lambda s \]
exists uniquely.

This argument establishes invertibility. The algorithm below uses exact elimination, whose running time will depend on input bit lengths rather than on \(\alpha_{\min}^{-1}\).

2. Integer representations and their bit lengths. Let \(K\) be the product of the denominators of all rational input scalars. Their total encoding length is at most \(L\), so \(K\le 2^L\). Define integers
\[ M_a=K\mu_a,\quad V_{ab}=KW_{ab},\quad p_a=K\alpha_a,\quad u_a=Ks_a,\quad b_r=K\beta_r. \]
The hypotheses give
\[ \sum_a M_a=K,\qquad 0<p_a\le K,\qquad 0\le u_a\le K,\qquad b_r\ge0,\qquad \sum_r b_r=K. \]

Define the positive integer
\[ \delta_a=\sum_b M_bV_{ab}=K^2d_a. \]
Each input contact weight is at most \(2^L\). Hence
\[ 1\le\delta_a \le K\max_b V_{ab} \le K^2 2^L \le 2^{3L}. \]
Moreover,
\[ Q_{ab}=\frac{M_bV_{ab}}{\delta_a}. \]

Let \(D=\prod_{a=1}^{\tau}\delta_a\), and let \(U=DQ\). Since every \(\delta_a\) divides \(D\), \(U\) is an integer matrix. It is nonnegative and satisfies
\[ D\le2^{3\tau L},\qquad U\mathbf1=D\mathbf1. \]
Inductively, \(U^r\mathbf1=D^r\mathbf1\). Nonnegativity then gives
\[ 0\le (U^r)_{ab}\le D^r. \]

Set
\[ H=KD^R,\qquad C=\sum_{r=1}^{R}b_rD^{R-r}U^r. \]
These are integer quantities, and substitution shows that
\[ P=\frac{C}{H}. \]
Furthermore,
\[ C\mathbf1 =\sum_{r=1}^{R}b_rD^{R-r}D^r\mathbf1 =H\mathbf1. \]
Thus \(0\le C_{ab}\le H\), and
\[ H\le2^{(3\tau R+1)L}. \]

In particular, all powers and weighted sums needed to construct \(P\) admit integer representations with bit length growing only linearly in \(R\).

3. Construction of a bounded integer system. Put \(E=K^2H\), and define an integer matrix \(G\) and integer vector \(h\) by
\[ G_{ab}=E\,\mathbf1_{\{a=b\}}-K(K-p_a)C_{ab}, \qquad h_a=Hp_au_a. \]
Direct substitution yields
\[ G=E\bigl[I-(I-\Lambda)P\bigr], \qquad h=E\Lambda s. \]
Consequently, \(G\) is nonsingular and \(Gz^\star=h\).

The previously established inequalities imply
\[ |G_{ab}|\le E,\qquad |h_a|\le E,\qquad E\le2^{(3\tau R+3)L}. \]
For a convenient uniform bound, define
\[ b=2+(3\tau R+3)L. \]
Every entry of \(G\) and \(h\) therefore has at most \(b\) bits, up to a constant for its sign.

The construction also has a polynomial operation count. The matrix \(U\) has at most \(2m\) nonzero entries. Compute successive powers by multiplying the current dense matrix on the right by sparse \(U\). Each multiplication uses \(O(\tau m)\) integer operations. Precomputing the powers of \(D\) and accumulating \(C\) uses \(O(R\tau^2)\) further operations.

Every partial sum in these matrix multiplications is nonnegative and bounded by the corresponding final entry. Likewise, partial sums defining \(C\) are bounded by \(H\). Thus construction intermediates have \(O(b)\) bits. Including input conversion and degree computation, a conservative bound for construction is
\[ O\!\left(\bigl[L+m+\tau+R\tau(m+\tau)\bigr]b^2\right) \]
bit operations, using schoolbook integer multiplication and division.

4. Exact elimination has polynomial intermediate bit lengths. Here is a verification of the required elimination bound.

Consider any integer matrix \(F\) of order \(\tau\) with entries of absolute value at most \(E\). Every minor of order \(j\) satisfies, by the determinant expansion,
\[ |\det F_{I,J}|\le j!E^j\le\tau!E^\tau. \]
Define
\[ B=2+\tau\bigl(b+\lceil\log_2(\tau+1)\rceil\bigr). \]
All such minors have \(O(B)\) bits.

Perform exact rational Gaussian elimination with row pivoting, reducing fractions after each operation. After eliminating \(k\) columns, let \(F_0\) denote the current row permutation of the original matrix, and let \(F_{0,[k],[k]}\) be its nonsingular leading pivot block. Every entry of the remaining Schur complement has the form
\[ \frac{ \det \begin{pmatrix} F_{0,[k],[k]} & F_{0,[k],j}\\ F_{0,i,[k]} & F_{0,ij} \end{pmatrix}} {\det F_{0,[k],[k]}}. \]
Indeed, multiplying the displayed block matrix on the left by
\[ \begin{pmatrix} I&0\\ -F_{0,i,[k]}F_{0,[k],[k]}^{-1}&1 \end{pmatrix} \]
preserves its determinant and makes it block upper triangular. This proves the displayed ratio identity.

Its numerator and denominator are minors of a row permutation of \(F\), so every reduced Schur-complement entry has \(O(B)\) bits. Each elimination update performs a constant number of rational operations on such entries; its temporary numerators and denominators also have \(O(B)\) bits. The reduced partial products of pivots are, up to sign, determinants of leading pivot blocks, so determinant accumulation obeys the same bound.

If no nonzero pivot exists in an active column, the remaining block has a zero column and the determinant is zero. Otherwise, elimination computes the determinant in \(O(\tau^3)\) rational operations. Using ordinary integer arithmetic and Euclidean reduction, each rational operation costs at most \(O(B^3)\) bit operations. Hence one determinant costs
\[ O(\tau^3B^3). \]

Apply this procedure to \(G\) and to the \(\tau\) matrices \(G^{(a)}\) obtained by replacing column \(a\) of \(G\) with \(h\). All their entries are bounded by \(E\). Since \(\det G\ne0\), Cramer’s rule gives the exact answer
\[ z^\star_a=\frac{\det G^{(a)}}{\det G}. \]
The determinant computations cost \(O(\tau^4B^3)\) bit operations in total, and the output numerators and denominators have \(O(B)\) bits.

5. Complexity and approximation guarantee. Combining the bounds gives the explicit polynomial running time
\[ O\!\left( \bigl[L+m+\tau+R\tau(m+\tau)\bigr]b^2 +\tau^4B^3 \right), \]
where
\[ b=2+(3\tau R+3)L,\qquad B=2+\tau\bigl(b+\lceil\log_2(\tau+1)\rceil\bigr). \]
This is a conservative bound; sharper exact linear-algebra algorithms can improve it.

Outputting \(\widehat z=z^\star\) gives
\[ \|\widehat z-z^\star\|_\infty=0\le\eta. \]
Thus the additive approximation problem is polynomial-time solvable. The algorithm operates directly on the \(\tau\) supported types and never expands the masses into individual agents. Symmetry and connectedness are compatible with the proof but are not needed beyond the stipulated positive degrees.

The sparsified iterative estimation studied by [Zhang, Xu, Zhang, and Chen](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.03569v3) addresses a different algorithmic objective. The classification here follows from the explicit rational representation and exact elimination bounds.
