The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over types \((x,g)\in\mathbb{Q}\times 2^{[m]}\), with rational group weights \(w_j\ge 0\), can one compute in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) a deterministic facility rule \(F\) such that \(F^\mu(y)=\max_j w_j\sum_{(x,g):j\in g}\mu_{x,g}|x-y|\) is at most \(2\min_z F^\mu(z)\), while preserving individual strategyproofness on every finite realization? For each \(N\) with \(N\mu\) integral, a deviation by an agent of type \((x,g)\) to location \(x'\) changes the distribution to \(\mu'=\mu-\frac1N\delta_{(x,g)}+\frac1N\delta_{(x',g)}\), and must satisfy \(|F(\mu)-x|\le |F(\mu')-x|\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \((x,g)\) with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, with \(\sum_{x,g}\mu_{x,g}=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Computational inputs have \(x\in\mathbb Q\), \(g\subseteq[m]\), \(m\ge1\), and public weights \(w_j\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\); \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Memberships may overlap. Empty groups contribute zero; assume \(\max_j w_j\sum_{(x,g):j\in g}\mu_{x,g}>0\). For one facility at \(y\in\mathbb R\), define \(C_w^\mu(y)=\max_{j\in[m]}w_j\sum_{(x,g):j\in g}\mu_{x,g}|x-y|\).
A deterministic rule \(F_w\) assigns one facility location to each society, independently of its finite realization size. Extend its domain to finite supports with real locations and rational masses; require an exactly represented rational output on rational inputs.
Realization-wise individual strategyproofness means that, for every society, every positive integer \(N\) with all \(N\mu_{x,g}\) integral, every supported \((x,g)\), and every report \(x'\in\mathbb R\), setting \(\mu'=\mu-N^{-1}\delta_{(x,g)}+N^{-1}\delta_{(x',g)}\) gives \(|F_w(\mu)-x|\le |F_w(\mu')-x|\). Memberships and weights remain fixed, and \(\delta\) denotes unit point mass.

Does there exist a deterministic, realization-wise individually strategyproof rule \(F_w\), evaluable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) on rational inputs, such that \(C_w^\mu(F_w(\mu))\le2\min_{z\in\mathbb R}C_w^\mu(z)\) for every admissible society?

For finite agent profiles with public overlapping memberships and nonnegative weights, BALANCED is a polynomial-time deterministic strategyproof \(2\)-approximation for maximum weighted total group cost. [Guo and Zhou](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01048v2).
No result settling this exact support-encoded distribution problem with strategyproofness on every finite realization was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize masses to sum to one, so a finite realization of size N assigns mass 1/N to each agent.
  • Exclude societies with no positive-weight occupied group: the stated BALANCED minimum over the real line is undefined in that degenerate case, including in the checked full version.
  • Allow empty memberships and empty groups for the total-cost objective; empty groups contribute zero.
  • Keep weights and memberships public and fixed under deviations, and require one rule independent of realization size.
  • Extend the rule to real reported locations with rational masses to preserve the discrete model's real-valued deviations; polynomial running time applies to rational inputs.
  • Require exact rational outputs on rational inputs to specify the computational output representation.
  • Use the multiplicative inequality directly, including when the optimum is zero; do not require any particular tie-breaking beyond determinism.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Yes. BALANCED can be evaluated directly using masses, and the resulting rule satisfies strategyproofness for every finite realization. The proof below is independent of the finite-profile theorem.

Construction and well-definedness. Fix the public weights \(w\). For a society \(\mu\), define
\[ \]
For each group \(j\) and location \(y\), let

\(T_j=\sum_{(x,g):\,j\in g}\mu_{x,g},\qquad L_j(y)=\sum_{(x,g):\,j\in g,\ x\le y}\mu_{x,g},\qquad R_j(y)=T_j-L_j(y).\)

Set

\(A(y)=\max_{j\in[m]}w_jL_j(y),\qquad B(y)=\max_{j\in[m]}w_jR_j(y),\)

and define

\(F_w(\mu)=\min\{y\in\mathbb R:A(y)\ge B(y)\}.\)

This is the mass formulation of the cut rule in [Guo and Zhou, Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01048v2).

To verify that the minimum exists, let \(a\) and \(b\) be the smallest and largest supported locations, and put \(M=\max_j w_jT_j>0\). For \(y<a\), we have \(A(y)=0<M=B(y)\). At \(y=b\), we have \(A(b)=M>B(b)=0\). Moreover, \(A\) is nondecreasing, \(B\) is nonincreasing, and both change only at supported locations. Consequently, the rule returns the first supported location satisfying the inequality.

The definition applies equally to finite supports with real locations and rational masses. It makes no reference to a realization size.

Two boundary inequalities. Write \(f=F_w(\mu)\). Define

\(L_j^-=\sum_{(x,g):\,j\in g,\ x<f}\mu_{x,g},\qquad R_j^+=\sum_{(x,g):\,j\in g,\ x\ge f}\mu_{x,g},\)

and

\(A^-=\max_j w_jL_j^-,\qquad B^+=\max_j w_jR_j^+.\)

Then

\(A(f)\ge B(f),\qquad A^-<B^+.\)

The first inequality is the defining condition at \(f\). For the second, finiteness of the support allows us to choose \(t<f\) with no supported location in \((t,f)\). Thus \(L_j(t)=L_j^-\) and \(R_j(t)=R_j^+\) for every group. Since every \(t<f\) fails the defining condition, \(A^-<B^+\).

Proof of the approximation guarantee. Let

\(E_j(y)=w_j\sum_{(x,g):\,j\in g}\mu_{x,g}|x-y|,\qquad C(y)=\max_jE_j(y).\)

We prove \(C(f)\le 2C(z)\) for every \(z\in\mathbb R\).

First suppose \(z>f\), and write \(d=z-f\). For every \(x\le f\),

\(|x-f|-|x-z|=-d.\)

For every \(x>f\), the triangle inequality gives

\(|x-f|-|x-z|\le d.\)

Summing within group \(j\), and discarding the nonpositive contribution from locations at most \(f\), gives

\(E_j(f)-E_j(z)\le d\,w_jR_j(f)\le d\,B(f).\)

On the other hand, every location \(x\le f\) has distance at least \(d\) from \(z\). Hence

\(C(z)\ge d\,A(f)\ge d\,B(f).\)

Combining these inequalities yields \(E_j(f)\le E_j(z)+C(z)\le 2C(z)\) for every \(j\), and therefore \(C(f)\le 2C(z)\).

Now suppose \(z<f\), and write \(d=f-z\). For every \(x\ge f\),

\(|x-f|-|x-z|=-d,\)

whereas for every \(x<f\), the triangle inequality gives

\(|x-f|-|x-z|\le d.\)

Consequently,

\(E_j(f)-E_j(z)\le d\,w_jL_j^-\le d\,A^-.\)

Every location \(x\ge f\) has distance at least \(d\) from \(z\), so

\(C(z)\ge d\,B^+\ge d\,A^-.\)

Again \(E_j(f)\le 2C(z)\) for every group. The case \(z=f\) is immediate.

For completeness, the minimum of \(C\) is attained. Each \(E_j\), and hence \(C\), is continuous. Choose a group with \(w_jT_j>0\). The triangle inequality implies

\(C(y)\ge E_j(y)\ge w_jT_j|y|-w_j\sum_{(x,g):\,j\in g}\mu_{x,g}|x|,\)

which tends to infinity as \(|y|\) tends to infinity. Thus continuity and coercivity imply existence of a minimizer. Applying the established inequality at that minimizer proves

\(C_w^\mu(F_w(\mu))\le 2\min_{z\in\mathbb R}C_w^\mu(z).\)

Proof of realization-wise individual strategyproofness. Fix a supported type \((x,g)\), a permitted realization size \(N\), and a report \(x'\). Put \(\varepsilon=1/N\) and

\(\mu'=\mu-\varepsilon\delta_{(x,g)}+\varepsilon\delta_{(x',g)}.\)

Since \(N\mu_{x,g}\) is a positive integer, \(\varepsilon\le\mu_{x,g}\), so \(\mu'\) is a valid society. Group totals \(T_j\) remain unchanged. Write \(f=F_w(\mu)\) and \(f'=F_w(\mu')\).

We first record a monotonicity fact. For fixed totals \(T_j\), the predicate

\(\max_j w_j\ell_j\ge\max_j w_j(T_j-\ell_j)\)

is nondecreasing in the vector \((\ell_j)_{j\in[m]}\) under coordinatewise order. Indeed, increasing that vector increases the left-hand maximum and decreases the right-hand maximum. In particular, any vector coordinatewise below a failing vector also fails.

If \(x=f\), truthful cost is zero, so the desired inequality holds.

Suppose \(x<f\).

  • If \(x'\ge f\), then for every \(y<f\), the moved mass can only decrease the left-prefix masses: \(L'_j(y)\le L_j(y)\) for every \(j\). All original cuts below \(f\) fail; monotonicity implies that all modified cuts below \(f\) fail too. Therefore \(f'\ge f\), and \(|f'-x|\ge |f-x|\).
  • If \(x'<f\), the prefix masses at \(f\) are unchanged, so the modified cut at \(f\) succeeds. To exclude an earlier successful cut, take any \(y<f\) and set \(s=\max\{y,x,x'\}<f\). Every unmoved mass counted in \(L'_j(y)\) is counted in \(L_j(s)\). The relocated mass, if counted in \(L'_j(y)\), is also accounted for at its original location \(x\le s\). Thus \(L'_j(y)\le L_j(s)\) for every \(j\). The original cut at \(s\) fails, so monotonicity implies that the modified cut at \(y\) fails. Hence \(f'=f\).

Suppose instead that \(x>f\).

  • If \(x'>f\), neither the original nor the reported location contributes to any left-prefix mass at \(y\le f\). All cuts at \(y\le f\) are therefore unchanged, giving \(f'=f\).
  • If \(x'\le f\), moving the mass left increases every left-prefix mass coordinatewise. In particular, the modified cut at \(f\) succeeds. Hence \(f'\le f\), and \(|f'-x|\ge |f-x|\).

These cases establish

\(|F_w(\mu)-x|\le |F_w(\mu')-x|.\)

The argument works for every permitted \(N\) and every real report \(x'\), proving the required realization-wise strategyproofness.

Polynomial-time evaluation and exact output. On rational inputs, sort the supported types by location. Compute each group total \(T_j\), then scan the distinct locations in increasing order. At each location, add the masses of every type at that location to the appropriate group prefixes before testing \(A(y)\ge B(y)\). Return the first successful location.

This uses \(O(\tau\log\tau+m\tau)\) rational comparisons and arithmetic operations. Their bit complexity is polynomial in \(L\): if \(D\) is the product of the input mass denominators, its bit length is at most the sum of their bit lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Every group prefix can be represented as an integer between zero and \(D\), divided by \(D\). Multiplication by input weights and exact comparison therefore involve integers of polynomial bit length.

The output is one of the input locations, so it is an exactly represented rational of input-bounded bit length. No finite realization is constructed.

Thus the rule satisfies all the requested properties.
