The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite resource catalogue \(R\), rationally weighted support \(T\) of valuation types, rational valuations \(v_t:2^R\to\mathbb{Q}\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_r\), decide whether every feasible aggregate allocation with no welfare-increasing one-resource transfer between positive-mass cohorts maximizes total welfare.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(R\) be a finite resource catalogue, with \(m=|R|\). A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of its \(\tau\) positive-mass valuation types, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each valuation \(v_t:2^R\to\mathbb Q\) is encoded by a finite list of propositional formulas over resource variables with binary rational weights: \(v_t(S)\) is the sum of weights of formulas satisfied when exactly the variables in \(S\) are true. Weights may have either sign; neither monotonicity nor \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\) is required. Supplies \(\rho_r\in\mathbb Q\) satisfy \(0<\rho_r\leq1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An aggregate allocation consists of real masses \(x_{t,S}\geq0\), satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq R}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni r}x_{t,S}=\rho_r\). Its welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_t\sum_{S\subseteq R}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\); each individual holds a bundle containing at most one copy of each resource, and all supply is allocated.
A one-resource transfer chooses positive-mass cohorts \((t,S)\), \((u,U)\), with \(r\in S\setminus U\), and moves an equal mass \(0<\varepsilon\leq\min\{x_{t,S},x_{u,U}\}\) from \(S\) to \(S\setminus\{r\}\) and from \(U\) to \(U\cup\{r\}\), respectively. Types \(t=u\) are allowed. The transfer improves welfare exactly when \(v_t(S\setminus\{r\})-v_t(S)+v_u(U\cup\{r\})-v_u(U)>0\); zero-gain transfers do not count as improvements. Unrestricted budget-balanced monetary side payments are permitted.
Call an allocation terminal if it admits no welfare-improving one-resource transfer.

Is deciding whether every feasible terminal allocation maximizes welfare over all feasible allocations solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?

For finite agents, the analogous universal optimality property is NP-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions, even with two agents, for succinct valuation languages including weighted propositional formulas. [Chevaleyre, Endriss and Maudet](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10458-009-9088-7)
No complexity classification for this exact distribution-over-types problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The valuation encoding was unspecified; chose weighted propositional formulas with binary rational weights, a succinct encoding covered by the discrete hardness result.
  • Normalized total society mass to one and restricted catalogue supplies to positive binary rationals at most one, consistent with per-capita availability and bundles containing at most one copy of each resource.
  • Defined aggregate allocations as real masses of type–bundle cohorts, with exact supply equalities because every resource must be allocated.
  • Defined a transfer as moving the same positive mass of donors and recipients, allowing cohorts of the same valuation type to trade.
  • Retained arbitrary signed, possibly nonmonotone and unnormalized valuations, unrestricted budget-balanced side payments, and strict welfare improvement from the discrete definition.
  • Used polynomial-time Turing reductions for the hardness alternative, matching the anchor's reduction convention.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. More precisely, it is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions.

Hardness holds even with one valuation type, every supply equal to \(1/2\), and a valuation represented by a single propositional formula of weight \(1\).

Hardness reduction

Let \(\Phi\) be a propositional formula on variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\). Introduce four fresh resource variables \(a,b,c,d\), and set

\(R=\{z_1,\ldots,z_n,a,b,c,d\}\).

Construct a society with one type of mass \(1\), supply \(\rho_r=1/2\) for every resource, and valuation encoded by the single formula

\(\Psi=\Phi\wedge a\wedge b\wedge\neg c\wedge\neg d\)

with weight \(1\). Thus \(v(S)=1\) if the assignment corresponding to \(S\) satisfies \(\Psi\), and \(v(S)=0\) otherwise. This construction has polynomial encoding length.

A terminal allocation that always exists

Define \(x^0\) by

\(x^0_{\varnothing}=x^0_R=1/2\),

with every other coordinate zero. Its total mass is \(1\), and each resource occurs in the full-bundle cohort alone, so every supply constraint is satisfied.

The guard in \(\Psi\) gives

\(v(\varnothing)=v(R)=0\).

For every resource \(r\), it also gives

\(v(\{r\})=v(R\setminus\{r\})=0\).

Indeed, a singleton cannot contain both \(a\) and \(b\). A bundle obtained by removing one resource from \(R\) still contains at least one of \(c,d\), whereas \(\Psi\) requires both to be absent.

Every possible one-resource transfer at \(x^0\) takes a resource from the full-bundle cohort to the empty-bundle cohort. Its welfare gain per unit mass is therefore

\(v(R\setminus\{r\})-v(R)+v(\{r\})-v(\varnothing)=0\).

Since zero-gain transfers do not count as improvements, \(x^0\) is terminal. Its welfare is zero.

Unsatisfiable formulas give universal optimality

If \(\Phi\) is unsatisfiable, then \(\Psi\) is unsatisfiable. Consequently, \(v(S)=0\) for every bundle \(S\).

Every feasible allocation then has welfare zero. In particular, every feasible terminal allocation maximizes welfare.

Satisfiable formulas give a suboptimal terminal allocation

Suppose \(\Phi\) has a satisfying assignment \(\sigma\). Define

\(S_\sigma=\{z_i:\sigma(z_i)=\mathrm{true}\}\cup\{a,b\}\).

Then \(v(S_\sigma)=1\). Its complement has value zero because it contains neither \(a\) nor \(b\).

Allocate mass \(1/2\) to each of \(S_\sigma\) and \(R\setminus S_\sigma\). This allocation is feasible: each resource belongs to exactly one of these two bundles. Its welfare is \(1/2\).

In fact, this is optimal. Every positive-value bundle contains \(a\), so every feasible allocation \(x\) satisfies

\(W(x)=\sum_{S:v(S)=1}x_S\leq\sum_{S\ni a}x_S=\rho_a=1/2\).

Thus the optimal welfare is \(1/2\), while the terminal allocation \(x^0\) has welfare zero. Universal optimality fails.

We have proved the exact equivalence

\(\Phi\text{ is unsatisfiable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{every feasible terminal allocation in the constructed instance is optimal}.\)

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from \(\mathrm{UNSAT}\), proving \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness. Equivalently, a polynomial-time algorithm for \(\mathrm{SAT}\) can construct this instance, query the universal-optimality problem once, and negate the answer. This proves the requested \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions.

Polynomial-size certificates for failure

For completeness, the general problem also belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\). The issue requiring proof is that allocations may initially have exponentially many positive coordinates and arbitrary real masses.

Write \(p=m+\tau\). The feasibility equations have the form \(Ax=b\), where \(A\) has \(p\) rows. The column corresponding to \((t,S)\) has a \(1\) in type row \(t\), and a \(1\) in resource row \(r\) exactly when \(r\in S\). All other entries are zero.

Terminality is preserved when support shrinks

Suppose \(x\) is terminal and \(y\) is feasible with

\(\operatorname{supp}(y)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(x)\).

Any eligible donor–recipient pair at \(y\) also has positive mass at \(x\). The transfer’s gain depends only on the types, bundles, and transferred resource, not on their masses. If it had positive gain at \(y\), a sufficiently small positive transfer would therefore improve \(x\), contradicting terminality.

Hence \(y\) is terminal.

Sparse rational allocations suffice

Suppose universal optimality fails. Choose a terminal allocation \(x\) whose welfare is strictly below the optimum, and let \(J=\operatorname{supp}(x)\).

Consider the feasible polytope restricted to coordinates in \(J\). It is nonempty because it contains \(x\). It is closed and bounded: each coordinate satisfies \(0\leq x_{t,S}\leq\mu_t\leq1\). A linear functional on a nonempty compact polytope attains its minimum at an extreme point. Choose such a welfare-minimizing extreme point \(y\).

Then

\(W(y)\leq W(x)<W^\star\),

and \(y\) is terminal by support preservation.

Every extreme point of this restricted polytope has at most \(p\) positive coordinates. To verify this, suppose its positive-support columns were linearly dependent. There would be a nonzero vector \(h\), supported on those coordinates, with \(Ah=0\). Since all those coordinates are positive, sufficiently small perturbations \(y+\varepsilon h\) and \(y-\varepsilon h\) would both be feasible and distinct. Their midpoint is \(y\), contradicting extremality.

Likewise, the full feasible polytope is nonempty and compact, so welfare has an optimal extreme point \(z\) with at most \(p\) positive coordinates. We obtain

\(W(z)>W(y)\).

These extreme points have polynomial-bit rational coordinates. Here is an explicit bound. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators appearing in the masses and supplies. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). If an extreme point has \(k\leq p\) positive coordinates, their columns are linearly independent. Select \(k\) rows making the corresponding square matrix \(B\) nonsingular.

The positive coordinates solve \(Bq=b'\). By Cramer’s rule,

\(q_i=\det(B_i)/(D\det(B))\),

where \(B_i\) replaces column \(i\) of \(B\) by the integral vector \(Db'\). Since \(B\) has entries in \(\{0,1\}\), and each entry of \(Db'\) lies between zero and \(D\), the determinant expansion gives

\(|\det(B)|\leq k!\quad\text{and}\quad|\det(B_i)|\leq k!D\).

Each coordinate therefore has bit length \(O(L+p\log(p+1))\). Listing at most \(p\) bundles, types, and masses gives a polynomial-size encoding.

Verification

A certificate for failure consists of these two allocations \(y,z\). A verifier checks:

  • Their nonnegative masses, type-mass constraints, and supply constraints.
  • Terminality of \(y\), by checking every ordered pair of its positive cohorts and every resource eligible for transfer.
  • The strict inequality \(W(z)>W(y)\).

There are at most \(p^2m\) transfer checks. Each required valuation is evaluated by substituting the bundle’s truth assignment into the input formulas and summing their rational weights. All checks use polynomial-time exact rational arithmetic.

A valid certificate proves that \(y\) is terminal and suboptimal. Conversely, the preceding argument supplies such a certificate whenever universal optimality fails. Thus failure belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), and universal optimality belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\).

Together with the reduction, this proves \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-completeness and settles the requested hardness alternative.
