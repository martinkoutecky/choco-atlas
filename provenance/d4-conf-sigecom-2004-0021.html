The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(r\) good types with utilities \(w_j\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\) and per-capita supplies \(q_j\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\), consider a unit-mass population of identical agents with additive utility \(u(b)=\sum_{j=1}^r w_jb_j\) for integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^r\). Choose masses \(x_b\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_bx_b=1\) and \(\sum_b b_jx_b=q_j\) for every \(j\), minimizing \(\rho(x)=\max_{x_b>0}u(b)/\min_{x_b>0}u(b)\), with value \(+\infty\) if a used bundle has zero utility.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported agent types, not possible types. Here agents are identical, with total mass \(1\), so \(\tau=1\).
There are \(r\ge1\) good types with utilities \(w_j\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) and per-capita supplies \(q_j\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), all encoded in binary. Write \(m=r\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each agent receives an integral bundle \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r\), with utility \(u(b)=\sum_{j=1}^r w_jb_j\). An allocation consists of real masses \(x_b\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_bx_b=1\) and \(\sum_b b_jx_b=q_j\) for every \(j\).
Minimize \(\rho(x)=\sup_{x_b>0}u(b)/\inf_{x_b>0}u(b)\), assigning value \(+\infty\) if a used bundle has zero utility or used utilities are unbounded. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}=\inf_x\rho(x)\). Algorithmic allocations are represented by lists of integral bundles and binary rational masses.

Does this continuous minimum envy-ratio problem admit a PTAS: for every fixed rational \(\varepsilon>0\), can a feasible allocation satisfying \(\rho(x)\le(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is achieving this guarantee NP-hard for some fixed \(\varepsilon>0\)?

For finitely many identical agents and explicitly listed indivisible goods with positive additive utilities, a PTAS exists, and an FPTAS exists when the number of agents is fixed. [Lipton–Markakis–Mossel–Saberi](https://procaccia.info/courses/15896s16/docs/paper12a.pdf)
A later result improves the discrete FPTAS for a fixed number of agents. [Kanellopoulos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18241) No result addressing exactly the continuous model above was located in the search.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

A PTAS for the continuous model

The problem admits a PTAS. For every fixed rational \(\varepsilon>0\), a feasible allocation with binary encoded rational masses and ratio at most \((1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}\) can be computed in time polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\).

The proof uses a configuration LP and a rounding procedure that preserves every type’s supply exactly. Finite populations appear only in structural existence proofs; the algorithm never expands the binary supplies into individual goods or agents.

For context, the discrete PTAS is stated in [Lipton–Markakis–Mossel–Saberi, Theorem 3.3](https://procaccia.info/courses/15896s16/docs/paper12a.pdf). The argument below establishes the guarantee directly for the continuous model.

1. Finite-ratio feasibility and attainment

Discard types with \(q_j=0\). Every feasible allocation assigns zero copies of such types to every used bundle, because its corresponding supply equation has nonnegative summands.

Write

\(\displaystyle Q=\sum_j q_j,\qquad \mu=\sum_j w_jq_j.\)

If \(Q<1\), every feasible allocation uses the zero bundle. Indeed,

\(\displaystyle 1-x_0=\sum_{b\ne0}x_b\le\sum_b\Bigl(\sum_jb_j\Bigr)x_b=Q.\)

Thus \(\mathrm{OPT}=+\infty\). A polynomial-size feasible allocation assigns mass \(q_j\) to the unit bundle \(e_j\) and mass \(1-Q\) to the zero bundle. This settles that case.

Henceforth assume \(Q\ge1\), so at least one type remains.

We first show that a finite-ratio allocation exists. Put \(q_j=k_j+f_j\), where \(k_j=\lfloor q_j\rfloor\) and \(0\le f_j<1\), and let \(F_j=\sum_{i\le j}f_i\), with \(F_0=0\). For uniform \(\theta\in[0,1)\), define

\(\displaystyle b_j(\theta)=k_j+\lfloor F_j+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor F_{j-1}+\theta\rfloor.\)

The floor difference is either zero or one, its expectation is \(f_j\), and

\(\displaystyle \sum_jb_j(\theta)=\sum_jk_j+\lfloor F_r+\theta\rfloor\in\{\lfloor Q\rfloor,\lceil Q\rceil\}.\)

Consequently, every bundle is nonempty and \(\mathbb E b_j=q_j\). The finitely many breakpoints of this construction are rational, so it gives a rational allocation.

Let

\(\displaystyle w_{\min}=\min_jw_j,\qquad H=\sum_jw_j\lceil q_j\rceil,\qquad R_0=H/w_{\min}.\)

The allocation just constructed has ratio at most \(R_0\).

Every allocation of ratio at most \(R_0\) has maximum utility at most \(R_0\mu\): its minimum utility is at most its mean \(\mu\). Since all weights are positive, there are only finitely many integral bundles of utility at most \(R_0\mu\). Therefore only finitely many objective values can occur among allocations of ratio at most \(R_0\), each determined by two bundle utilities. The minimum is attained.

For an optimal support, the equations defining its masses form a feasible rational linear system with nonnegativity constraints. It has a rational solution; dropping zero masses cannot worsen the ratio. Thus an optimal allocation exists with finite support and rational masses.

2. Isolating large goods

We now describe a preprocessing operation determined entirely by the input.

Maintain remaining agent mass \(t\) and remaining type set \(J\), initially \(t=1\) and \(J\) equal to all retained types. Whenever \(t>0\), define the remaining average

\(\displaystyle T_J=\frac{\sum_{j\in J}w_jq_j}{t}.\)

If the set

\(\displaystyle A=\{j\in J:w_j\ge T_J\}\)

is nonempty, prescribe mass \(q_j\) of singleton bundle \(e_j\) for each \(j\in A\), remove these types, and replace \(t\) by \(t-\sum_{j\in A}q_j\). Repeat until either \(t=0\) or no such type remains.

This operation is well defined. If \(h=\sum_{j\in A}q_j\), then

\(\displaystyle T_Jh\le\sum_{j\in A}w_jq_j\le T_Jt,\)

so \(h\le t\). If \(h=t\), no supply remains outside \(A\). If \(h<t\), the new average is at most \(T_J\), since the removed utility is at least \(T_Jh\). Hence the successive averages are nonincreasing.

At most \(m\) iterations occur.

Lemma 1: preprocessing preserves an optimum

There exists an optimal allocation in which every peeled good is assigned as its prescribed singleton.

Proof. Take a rational optimal allocation from Section 1. Multiply all masses by a common denominator \(N\). This realizes the allocation as a finite allocation to \(N\) agents with \(Nq_j\) goods of each type.

Consider one preprocessing stage, whose active agents have average utility \(T_J\). Process individual goods of the types in \(A\), marking their recipients inactive once the good has been isolated.

The active average remains at most \(T_J\): each previously isolated good has value at least \(T_J\). Let a designated good of value \(w\ge T_J\) lie in a bundle with additional goods. There is another active agent of utility at most the active average, hence at most \(w\).

Move one additional good of value \(p\) to such an agent. If the recipient’s old utility is \(s\le w\) and the donor’s old utility is \(d\), then

\(\displaystyle s+p\le w+p\le d,\qquad d-p\ge w\ge s.\)

Both new utilities lie between the two old utilities. Thus neither the global minimum decreases nor the global maximum increases.

Repeat until the designated good is alone, then mark its recipient inactive. Its value is at least the current active average, so removing it cannot increase that average. The argument therefore continues through the entire stage and then through subsequent stages.

The resulting allocation has the prescribed singleton masses and ratio no larger than the optimum. It is consequently optimal. \(\square\)

If preprocessing ends with \(t=0\), these singleton masses form an exact optimal allocation, completing that case.

Otherwise, let \(J\) be the remaining types and define

\(\displaystyle T=\frac{\sum_{j\in J}w_jq_j}{t}>0.\)

Every remaining weight satisfies \(w_j<T\). Every peeled singleton has value at least \(T\). Let \(W\) be the largest peeled weight, taking \(W=0\) if no type was peeled.

3. A bounded residual optimum

Lemma 2: residual utilities can be bounded

There is an optimal allocation respecting the prescribed singletons in which every residual bundle has utility in \([T/2,2T]\).

Proof. Again use a finite realization of the rational allocation supplied by Lemma 1. Transfers below involve only residual agents and goods.

Suppose a residual agent has minimum utility \(s<T/2\). Some residual agent has utility \(d\ge T\). Since each residual good has value less than \(T\), this donor has at least two goods. Its smallest good has value \(p\le d/2\). Transfer this good to the minimum agent. Then

\(\displaystyle s+p<s+d/2<T/2+d/2\le d,\)

and

\(\displaystyle d-p\ge d/2\ge T/2>s.\)

Thus both new utilities lie between \(s\) and \(d\). Moreover, the sum of squared utilities decreases by

\(\displaystyle d^2+s^2-(d-p)^2-(s+p)^2=2p(d-s-p)>0.\)

There are finitely many allocations of these fixed finite inventories. Repeating this operation therefore terminates, and termination gives minimum residual utility at least \(T/2\).

Next, if some residual utility is \(d>2T\), select a residual agent of minimum utility \(s\le T\), and transfer any good of value \(p<T\) from the former to the latter. Then

\(\displaystyle d-p>T\ge s,\qquad s+p<2T<d.\)

The minimum remains at least \(T/2\), the global utility interval does not expand, and the same squared-utility calculation is strictly positive because \(d-s-p>0\). Finiteness again implies termination, now with maximum residual utility at most \(2T\).

Neither phase increases the global ratio, so the resulting allocation is optimal. \(\square\)

Fix this optimal allocation, and denote its minimum and maximum residual utilities by \(a\) and \(b\). Then

\(\displaystyle T/2\le a\le T\le b\le2T.\)

Because peeled singletons have value at least \(T\),

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{OPT}=\frac{\max\{W,b\}}{a}.\)

This bounded residual scale is the key to the approximation.

4. Rounding while preserving exact expectations

We need two constructive rounding facts.

Lemma 3: rational distributions with small support

Let \(v\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^d\).

  • Given positive rational weights \(p_i\), one can compute a rational distribution on integral vectors \(B\) such that

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E B=v,\qquad |p\cdot B-p\cdot v|\le\max_i p_i\)

for every supported vector.

  • If the coordinates are partitioned into groups and \(\sum_{i\in G}v_i\) is an integer for each group \(G\), one can compute a rational distribution on integral vectors \(B\) such that

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E B=v,\qquad \sum_{i\in G}B_i=\sum_{i\in G}v_i\)

for every supported vector and every group.

Both procedures take polynomial time in their rational input length. Their support sizes can be bounded by \(2(d+1)\) and \(d+1\), respectively.

Proof. Subtract the coordinatewise floor of \(v\), leaving a vector \(f\in[0,1)^d\).

For the first assertion, maintain a distribution on vectors in \([0,1]^d\), each satisfying \(p\cdot z=p\cdot f\). Initially it consists only of \(f\).

Whenever a supported vector has two fractional coordinates \(i,j\), move in the direction

\(\displaystyle e_i/p_i-e_j/p_j.\)

Move in both directions until reaching the boundary of the cube. The original vector is a rational convex combination of the two endpoints. Replacing it by these endpoints preserves its expectation and weighted sum, and each endpoint has fewer fractional coordinates.

After each generation, reduce support to at most \(d+1\) while preserving its expectation. Explicitly, if there are too many points \(z_s\), find a nonzero rational affine dependence

\(\displaystyle \sum_s\beta_s=0,\qquad \sum_s\beta_sz_s=0.\)

For current probabilities \(\pi_s\), subtract \(\lambda\beta_s\), where

\(\displaystyle \lambda=\min_{\beta_s>0}\pi_s/\beta_s.\)

The probabilities remain nonnegative, preserve their sum and expectation, and at least one becomes zero. Repeat as necessary.

After at most \(d\) generations, each supported vector has at most one fractional coordinate. Round that coordinate to zero or one with probabilities preserving its expectation. This changes the weighted sum by at most its weight. Restoring the floors proves the first assertion, with at most \(2(d+1)\) points.

For the second assertion, make the same pairwise moves with unit weights, choosing both coordinates inside a single group. Each group’s sum stays integer. A group with exactly one fractional coordinate would have a noninteger sum, so whenever any fractional coordinate remains, some group contains a pair available for rounding. The procedure therefore ends with integral vectors, retaining every group sum. Support compression gives at most \(d+1\) points.

For completeness, these operations have polynomial bit complexity. During pairwise rounding, each remaining fractional coordinate’s weighted value is a sum of original weighted fractional coordinates minus weights of coordinates already fixed to one. There are at most \(d\) terms, so all point coordinates have polynomial encoding length. Splitting probabilities therefore have polynomial encoding length. Integer affine dependencies of polynomial encoding length are obtainable by rational Gaussian elimination. Using a common denominator for the probabilities, each split or elimination increases its bit length by a polynomial amount. There are polynomially many such operations. \(\square\)

5. The configuration LP

Choose

\(\displaystyle k=\left\lceil\max\{32,32/\varepsilon\}\right\rceil,\qquad \delta=1/k,\qquad \Delta=T/k.\)

Call a residual type small if \(w_j<T/k\), and large otherwise.

For a large type, round its weight upward to

\(\displaystyle \widehat w_j=\frac{T}{k^2}\left\lceil\frac{k^2w_j}{T}\right\rceil.\)

Then

\(\displaystyle w_j\le\widehat w_j\le(1+\delta)w_j,\)

because \(w_j\ge T/k\). Each rounded weight is \(\nu T/k^2\) for some integer \(\nu\in\{k,\ldots,k^2\}\).

Define the aggregate supplies and small-good utility by

\(\displaystyle Q_\nu=\sum_{\widehat w_j=\nu T/k^2}q_j,\qquad S=\sum_{\text{small }j}w_jq_j.\)

A configuration is an integral vector \(c=(c_\nu)_{\nu=k}^{k^2}\) with rounded utility

\(\displaystyle V_c=\frac{T}{k^2}\sum_{\nu=k}^{k^2}\nu c_\nu\le3T.\)

Every configuration contains at most \(3k\) large goods. Thus the number of configurations depends only on \(k\), and hence only on \(\varepsilon\).

Enumerate all grid pairs \((\ell,h)\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \ell,h\in\Delta\mathbb Z,\qquad T/3\le\ell\le T,\qquad \ell\le h\le3T.\)

For each pair, retain configurations with \(V_c\le h\) and solve the following rational LP. Here \(z_c\) is the mass of configuration \(c\), and \(y_c\) is the total small-good utility assigned to that mass:

\(\displaystyle z_c,y_c\ge0,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_cz_c=t,\qquad \sum_cc_\nu z_c=Q_\nu\quad\text{for every }\nu,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_cy_c=S,\)

\(\displaystyle \max\{0,\ell-V_c\}z_c\le y_c\le(h-V_c)z_c.\)

For each feasible pair, record

\(\displaystyle R(\ell,h)=\frac{\max\{W,h\}}{\ell}.\)

Select a feasible pair minimizing this quantity.

A sufficiently good pair is feasible

Use the optimal allocation from Lemma 2, and set

\(\displaystyle \ell_*=\Delta\left\lfloor a/\Delta\right\rfloor,\qquad h_*=\Delta\left\lceil(1+\delta)b/\Delta\right\rceil.\)

The bounds on \(a,b\), together with \(\delta\le1/32\), imply that this pair is enumerated:

\(\displaystyle T/3\le\ell_*\le T,\qquad \ell_*\le h_*\le3T.\)

Round each bundle’s large-good weights upward while retaining its actual small-good utility. Its resulting utility lies between its original utility and \((1+\delta)\) times that utility, hence between \(\ell_*\) and \(h_*\). Grouping bundles by their large-good configurations gives a feasible LP solution.

Furthermore,

\(\displaystyle \ell_*\ge a-\delta T\ge(1-2\delta)a,\)

and

\(\displaystyle h_*\le(1+\delta)b+\delta T\le(1+2\delta)b.\)

Therefore the selected pair satisfies

\(\displaystyle R(\ell,h)\le R(\ell_*,h_*)\le\frac{1+2\delta}{1-2\delta}\mathrm{OPT}.\)

6. Restoring the original types and integral bundles

Take a rational solution of the selected LP. For each configuration with \(z_c>0\), put

\(\displaystyle s_c=y_c/z_c.\)

The LP guarantees

\(\displaystyle \ell\le V_c+s_c\le h.\)

We construct a distribution of original bundles for this configuration population.

Large goods

For each original large type \(j\) in rounded class \(\nu\), prescribe the expected number

\(\displaystyle A_j=c_\nu q_j/Q_\nu.\)

If \(Q_\nu=0\), its supply equation forces \(c_\nu=0\) in every configuration of positive mass, so that class can be omitted.

For each class,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j\text{ in class }\nu}A_j=c_\nu\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.\)

Apply Lemma 3(2), grouping coordinates by rounded class. Every resulting integral bundle contains exactly \(c_\nu\) goods of class \(\nu\), while its expected original counts are \(A_j\). Its actual large-good utility therefore lies in

\(\displaystyle [V_c/(1+\delta),V_c].\)

Across all configuration populations, each original large type has precisely its required supply, since

\(\displaystyle \sum_cz_cA_j=\frac{q_j}{Q_\nu}\sum_cz_cc_\nu=q_j.\)

Small goods

If \(S=0\), there are no supported small types and every \(y_c=0\).

If \(S>0\), prescribe, for each small type,

\(\displaystyle D_j=q_js_c/S.\)

These expectations have weighted utility exactly \(s_c\):

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\text{small }j}w_jD_j=s_c.\)

Apply Lemma 3(1). Every resulting small-good bundle has utility in

\(\displaystyle [s_c-\delta T,s_c+\delta T],\)

and the aggregate supply of every small type is exactly correct:

\(\displaystyle \sum_cz_cD_j=\frac{q_j}{S}\sum_cz_cs_c=\frac{q_j}{S}\sum_cy_c=q_j.\)

For each configuration, take the product of its large-good and small-good distributions and multiply their probabilities by \(z_c\). Finally add the prescribed peeled singletons.

The resulting allocation has total mass one, integral bundles, rational masses, and exactly the original supply of every type.

Every residual bundle’s utility lies between

\(\displaystyle \frac{V_c}{1+\delta}+s_c-\delta T \ge\frac{\ell}{1+\delta}-\delta T\)

and

\(\displaystyle V_c+s_c+\delta T\le h+\delta T.\)

7. Approximation guarantee

Because \(\ell\ge T/3\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{\ell}{1+\delta}-\delta T \ge\left(\frac1{1+\delta}-3\delta\right)\ell \ge(1-4\delta)\ell>0.\)

Every peeled singleton has value at least \(T\), so this lower bound also holds for the complete allocation.

Also, \(h\ge\ell\ge T/3\), giving

\(\displaystyle h+\delta T\le(1+3\delta)h.\)

Consequently, the output allocation satisfies

\(\displaystyle \rho(x)\le \frac{1+3\delta}{1-4\delta}R(\ell,h) \le \frac{(1+3\delta)(1+2\delta)} {(1-4\delta)(1-2\delta)} \,\mathrm{OPT}.\)

For \(0<\delta\le1/32\), the excess of this factor over one equals

\(\displaystyle \frac{11\delta-2\delta^2}{1-6\delta+8\delta^2} \le\frac{176}{13}\delta<14\delta.\)

Since \(k\ge32/\varepsilon\), we have \(\delta\le\varepsilon/32\). Thus

\(\displaystyle \rho(x)\le(1+14\delta)\mathrm{OPT}\le(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}.\)

8. Running time and output encoding

For fixed \(\varepsilon\), the integer \(k\), the number of configurations, and the number of grid pairs are constants.

Preprocessing takes at most \(m\) iterations. Its quantities can be computed directly from sums of the original rational supplies and utilities, so they have polynomial encoding length.

Each configuration LP is explicitly listed, has rational coefficients of polynomial encoding length, and has bounded variables: \(0\le z_c\le t\) and \(0\le y_c\le3Tt\). Standard polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore decides feasibility and produces a rational solution of polynomial encoding length.

Lemma 3 constructs the original-type distributions in polynomial time. Each configuration contributes at most \(2(m+1)^2\) bundles through the product construction. Thus the final allocation has \(O_\varepsilon(m^2)\) listed bundles, all with polynomial-length integral coordinates and rational masses.

The finite realizations used in Lemmas 1 and 2 establish existence only. None is constructed by the algorithm. Its running time is polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\) for every fixed rational \(\varepsilon>0\), as required with \(\tau=1\).
