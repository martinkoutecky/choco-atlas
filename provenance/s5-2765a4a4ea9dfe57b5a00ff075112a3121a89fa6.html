The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses over finitely many complete agent types on each side, with each type specifying integer quotas and rankings of acceptable counterpart types, compute a critical relaxed-stable assignment whose matched-pair mass is at least \(\frac{2}{3}\) of the maximum among such assignments. Agents remain individuals with integral bundles, and relaxed stability applies pairwise as in Definition 6; criticality minimizes the integrated total deficiency. Require a finite encoding of the assignment and running time polynomial in the number of supported types and the input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society has two nonempty sides \(A,B\), each a disjoint union of atomless intervals representing supported types. Input gives the \(\tau\) positive-mass types and their binary rational masses; normalize total mass across both sides to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(m\) count mutually acceptable type pairs and \(L\) be total input bit length.
Each type specifies binary integer quotas \(0\le q^-\le q^+\), with \(q^+>0\), and a weak ranking of all its acceptable counterpart types. Acceptability is mutual; individuals of the same type are indifferent. Lists may be incomplete, and ties remain ties.
An assignment \(M\) is a measurable reciprocal simple partnership relation: each individual \(u\) has a finite set \(M(u)\) of distinct acceptable partners and degree \(d_M(u)=|M(u)|\le q^+(u)\). Several partners may have the same type. Require mass balance: for every measurable \(S\subseteq A,T\subseteq B\), \(\int_S |M(u)\cap T|\,d\mu(u)=\int_T |M(v)\cap S|\,d\mu(v)\).
An unmatched acceptable pair \((a,b)\) blocks if each endpoint is under its upper quota or strictly prefers the other endpoint to some current partner. Relaxed stability requires every blocking pair to satisfy either \(d_M(a)=q^+(a)\) and \(d_M(b')\le q^-(b')\) for every \(b'\in M(a)\) with \(b\succ_a b'\), or the symmetric condition at \(b\). These requirements hold for all individuals and pairs outside a single null set.
Define \(D(M)=\int_{A\cup B}\max\{0,q^-(u)-d_M(u)\}\,d\mu(u)\) and \(s(M)=\int_A d_M(u)\,d\mu(u)\). Criticality minimizes \(D\) over all assignments, without imposing relaxed stability. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the supremum of \(s\) among critical relaxed-stable assignments, when this set is nonempty.
A finite assignment encoding gives a partition into finitely many classes, each contained in one type, with positive rational class masses \(w_i\) and binary nonnegative integer counts \(k_{ij}\): every member of class \(i\) has exactly \(k_{ij}\) distinct partners in class \(j\). Require \(w_i k_{ij}=w_j k_{ji}\); the encoding represents a reciprocal, mass-balanced simple realization of these counts.
Can a finitely encoded critical relaxed-stable assignment \(M\) with \(s(M)\ge\frac{2}{3}\mathrm{OPT}\) always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is achieving this approximation guarantee NP-hard?
For finite individual societies, critical relaxed-stable matchings always exist and a polynomial-time \(\frac{2}{3}\)-approximation is known. [Nasre–Nimbhorkar–Ranjan](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.12325v3)
Continuum many-to-many models have existence results for pairwise and tree stability, under different definitions; these do not settle critical relaxed stability or this approximation guarantee. No result settling this precise continuous problem was located. [Greinecker–Vocke](https://www2.uibk.ac.at/downloads/c9821000/wpaper/2026-02.pdf)

2. Configurations and compatibility

A configuration $c$ of type $t$ specifies integers
$$ n_c(r,h)\geq 0,\qquad h\in\{0,1\}, $$
for each acceptable counterpart type $r$. Here $h=0$ means that the partner’s degree is at most its own lower quota, and $h=1$ means that it exceeds that quota.

Define
$$ d_c=\sum_{r,h}n_c(r,h)\leq Q_t, \qquad h(c)=\mathbf 1_{\{d_c>\ell_t\}}. $$
There are finitely many configurations.

For acceptable type $r$, define willingness and justification by
$$ W_c(r) \iff d_c<Q_t \ \text{or}\ \sum_{s:\,r\succ_t s}\sum_h n_c(s,h)>0, $$
and
$$ J_c(r) \iff d_c=Q_t \ \text{and}\ \sum_{s:\,r\succ_t s}n_c(s,1)=0. $$
These predicates use strict preference, preserving ties.

Opposite-side configurations $c,d$ are incompatible if their types are acceptable and
$$ W_c(t(d))\land W_d(t(c)) \land\neg J_c(t(d))\land\neg J_d(t(c)). $$
A support is compatible if it contains no incompatible pair.

Lemma 3: compatibility characterizes relaxed stability

Every relaxed-stable assignment has compatible positive-mass configuration support. Conversely, an assignment with compatible configuration support is relaxed-stable outside any null set containing its exceptional configurations.

Proof. First remove the exceptional null set and all individuals connected to it by a finite partnership path. This removes only a null set: mass balance implies that individuals having a partner in a null set form a null set, and the iteration is countable.

Suppose incompatible configurations $c,d$ both occur with positive mass. Their Cartesian product has positive product measure. The partnership relation inside that product has measure zero, because every section is finite and the underlying measures are atomless. Fubini’s theorem therefore supplies an unmatched pair between the configurations. Both endpoints are willing and neither supplies a justification, contradicting relaxed stability.

Conversely, compatibility says that every acceptable configuration pair either lacks simultaneous willingness or has a justification at an endpoint. This is precisely what relaxed stability requires for every unmatched pair. $\square$

6. Equal masses and unit upper quotas: an exact finite reduction

Assume now that
$$ \ell_t=0,\qquad Q_t=1,\qquad \mu_t=\frac1{\tau} $$
for every supported type.

Construct a finite instance $G$ with one vertex per type and the given acceptable pairs and rankings. Let $\nu_{\mathrm{st}}(G)$ be its maximum weakly stable matching cardinality.

Theorem 9: splitting types cannot improve the optimum in this case

Under these assumptions,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=\frac{\nu_{\mathrm{st}}(G)}{\tau}. $$

Proof.

First, relaxed stability equals weak stability. Every current partner has degree one and hence exceeds its lower quota zero. An underfull endpoint cannot justify a blocking pair. A full endpoint willing to replace its partner has a strictly worse partner of degree one, so it cannot justify the pair either.

Also, every assignment has deficiency zero, so criticality is automatic.

Second, finite stable matchings lift. Replace each matched type pair by a measure-preserving bijection between its equal-mass intervals. Each matched individual has degree one. For an acceptable type pair that is a finite matching edge, its endpoints cannot strictly improve upon their current partner type. For any other type pair, finite weak stability excludes simultaneous willingness. Thus the lift is continuum stable and has partnership mass equal to the finite cardinality divided by $\tau$.

This proves
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\geq\frac{\nu_{\mathrm{st}}(G)}{\tau}. $$

Third, round a continuum assignment. Given a continuum stable assignment, define
$$ x_{ab} = \tau\int_{A_a}|M(u)\cap B_b|\,d\mu(u). $$
Then
$$ x_{ab}\geq 0,\qquad \sum_{e\ni t}x_e\leq 1,\qquad \sum_{ab}x_{ab}=\tau s(M). $$
Let
$$ u_t=1-\sum_{e\ni t}x_e $$
be the fraction of type $t$ that is unmatched, and let $E_+$ contain the edges with $x_e>0$.

Consider the polytope
$$ F= \left\{ y\geq 0: y_e=0\text{ for }e\notin E_+,\ \sum_{e\ni t}y_e\leq 1,\ \sum_{e\ni t}y_e=1\text{ if }u_t=0 \right\}. $$
It contains $x$.

Every vertex of $F$ is integral. Here is a direct proof. At a nonintegral point, consider the graph of edges whose values lie strictly between zero and one. If it has a cycle, that cycle is even because the graph is bipartite. Alternating sufficiently small positive and negative perturbations around the cycle preserve all incident sums.

If it has no cycle, a component containing an edge is a tree. Choose a path between two leaves and alternate perturbations along it. Internal sums remain unchanged. At either leaf there is exactly one positive fractional incident edge and no incident edge of value one; thus its incident sum is strictly between zero and one. Its upper constraint has slack, and it cannot be subject to an equality constraint. Both perturbation directions are therefore feasible. In either case the point is not a vertex.

Maximizing $\sum_e y_e$ over $F$ consequently gives an integral matching $N$ with
$$ |N|\geq\sum_e x_e=\tau s(M). $$

It remains to verify stability. If a vertex is unmatched in $N$, its unmatched configuration had positive mass in $M$, because vertices with $u_t=0$ were forced to be matched. If it is matched to type $r$ in $N$, that partner-type configuration had positive mass in $M$, because the chosen edge lies in $E_+$.

Therefore every configuration selected by $N$ occurred with positive mass in $M$. A blocking pair for $N$ would select two simultaneously willing positive-mass configurations from $M$. As shown above, neither can justify such a pair. They would be incompatible, contradicting Lemma 3.

Thus $N$ is finite stable, giving
$$ \nu_{\mathrm{st}}(G)\geq\tau s(M). $$
Taking the supremum over $M$ proves the reverse inequality. $\square$

Consequence 1: exact threshold decision is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete

For finite stable marriage with ties and incomplete lists, deciding whether a weakly stable matching of cardinality at least a given integer exists is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete. [Manlove–Irving–Iwama–Miyazaki–Morita, Lemma 2.1](https://www.dcs.gla.ac.uk/~davidm/pubs/TR-1999-43.pdf).

Map each finite participant to a supported type of mass $1/\tau$, with lower quota zero and upper quota one. Preserve acceptable pairs and rankings, including ties. Map the cardinality threshold $k$ to $K=k/\tau$.

This construction has polynomial input length and two nonempty sides. Theorem 9 proves equivalence of the threshold answers. Together with Corollary 7, this proves $\mathrm{NP}$-completeness of the exact threshold problem for the continuous model.

Consequence 2: the requested approximation is polynomial in this special case

Apply the finite $\frac{2}{3}$-approximation algorithm to $G$. Its hypotheses allow incomplete lists and ties, and here all quotas are one with lower quotas zero. [Nasre–Nimbhorkar–Ranjan, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.12325v3).

If its output is $N$, the bijection lift satisfies
$$ s(M)=\frac{|N|}{\tau} \geq \frac23\frac{\nu_{\mathrm{st}}(G)}{\tau} = \frac23\mathrm{OPT}. $$
It uses one class per type, with counts in $\{0,1\}$, so the required encoding has polynomial length and is computed in polynomial time.

7. What remains open

The exact-hardness result does not establish hardness of the requested approximation. Indeed, the same restricted instances admit a polynomial-time $\frac{2}{3}$-approximation.

For general binary quotas and rational masses, the compact certificate bound removes one source of excessive size: an optimum needs only polynomially many configurations, and its value has polynomial bit length. Two gaps remain:

  • No polynomial-time method has been proved for selecting compatible configurations achieving the $\frac{2}{3}$ guarantee.
  • No polynomial bound has been proved for converting the compact grouped-count description into the required class-to-class integer-count encoding.

A resolution requires either a construction addressing both gaps or a hardness reduction for the approximation guarantee itself.
