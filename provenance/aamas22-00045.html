The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(T\) of complete easy/difficult worker types with rational mass vector \(\mu\), chore classes \(R\), rational \(p>1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\rho\), find a finite-support rational kernel \(y_{t,B}\) over integer bundles \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{R}\), where \(d_t(B)=\sum_{r\in R}d_t(r)B_r\) and \(d_t(r)\in\{1,p\}\), satisfying \(\sum_B y_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}y_{t,B}B_r=\rho_r\), such that every ordered pair of supported assignments \((t,B),(u,D)\) satisfies chore-EF1—either \(B=\varnothing\) or some \(r\) with \(B_r>0\) has \(d_t(B-\mathbf e_r)\le d_t(D)\)—and no feasible \(y'\) admits a type-preserving coupling \(\pi_{t,B,D}\) with weak cost improvement everywhere and strict improvement on positive mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) complete worker types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|R|\) chore classes, binary-rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_r\ge0\), and a common binary-rational \(p>1\). Each type specifies \(d_t(r)\in\{1,p\}\) for every class; constant-cost types are allowed. Write \(d_t(B)=\sum_r d_t(r)B_r\).
A feasible allocation is a finite-support nonnegative rational array \(y_{t,B}\), over integer bundles \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{R}\), satisfying \(\sum_B y_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}y_{t,B}B_r=\rho_r\) for every \(r\). Bundles and rational entries are encoded in binary.
It is EF1 if every ordered pair \((t,B),(u,D)\) with \(y_{t,B}>0\) and \(y_{u,D}>0\) satisfies \(B=\mathbf0\) or \(d_t(B-\mathbf e_r)\le d_t(D)\) for some \(r\) with \(B_r>0\), where \(\mathbf e_r\) is the unit bundle of class \(r\).
It is Pareto optimal if no feasible allocation \(y'\) admits a nonnegative rational coupling \(\pi_{t,B,D}\) with \(\sum_D\pi_{t,B,D}=y_{t,B}\), \(\sum_B\pi_{t,B,D}=y'_{t,D}\), and \(d_t(D)\le d_t(B)\) whenever \(\pi_{t,B,D}>0\), with \(\sum_{t,B,D:\,d_t(D)<d_t(B)}\pi_{t,B,D}>0\). The competing allocation need not satisfy EF1.

Is finding a feasible EF1 and Pareto-optimal allocation solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard, where \(L\) is the total input bit length?

For finite lists of agents and individual bivalued chores, an EF1 and Pareto-optimal allocation always exists and can be found in polynomial time ([verified result](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~nisarg/papers/chores.pdf)).
No result settling the exact support-encoded mass problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one, so supplies are per capita; required nonempty society support and positive masses.
  • Specified supplies as nonnegative binary rationals, allowing zero supply.
  • Allowed constant-cost types: the discrete model permits them, and its all-difficult-type rescaling is an algorithmic normalization rather than a domain restriction.
  • Interpreted feasible Pareto competitors as finite-support rational allocations with the same masses and supplies; specified both coupling marginals and positive-mass strict improvement.
  • Required Pareto competitors only to be feasible, without imposing EF1.
  • Specified binary encoding of integer bundles and rational output masses, and defined the input bit-length parameter.

Partial results for the support-encoded problem

The general polynomial-time versus NP-hardness question remains unresolved here. I prove several additional results:

  • Pareto optimality has a combinatorial characterization by negative cycles on the supported types.
  • Every non-Pareto-optimal allocation admits an improvement that moves one chore per participating bundle and preserves each participating worker’s bundle size.
  • An explicitly supplied allocation can be weakly Pareto improved to a Pareto-optimal allocation in polynomial time, preserving each worker’s bundle size.
  • The requested EF1 and Pareto-optimal allocation can be constructed in polynomial time when the total per-capita supply is at most one, and in another regime of integer total supply.
  • A concrete example shows that keeping each type’s average bundle size fixed can preclude obtaining EF1 and Pareto optimality simultaneously.

For completeness, I also give self-contained proofs of existence and polynomial-size witnesses, using only the supplied finite-agent existence theorem as an external result.

Throughout, repeated occurrences of the same pair $(t,B)$ are combined by summing their masses.

1. A combinatorial characterization of Pareto optimality

Fix a feasible allocation $y$. Define
$$ x_{tr}=\sum_B y_{t,B}B_r, \qquad n_t=\sum_r x_{tr}, \qquad h_{tr}=\frac{d_t(r)-1}{p-1}\in\{0,1\}. $$
Thus
$$ d_t(r)=p^{h_{tr}}. $$

Construct a directed multigraph $G_x$ with vertex set $T$. For every triple $(s,t,r)$ with $x_{sr}>0$, include an arc from $s$ to $t$, labeled $r$, of weight
$$ w(s,t,r)=h_{tr}-h_{sr}. $$
Every arc weight belongs to $\{-1,0,1\}$.

Theorem 1

The following conditions are equivalent:

  • $y$ is Pareto optimal under the coupling definition in the question.
  • $G_x$ has no negative directed cycle.
  • $x$ minimizes

$$ \sum_{t,r}h_{tr}z_{tr} $$
over nonnegative matrices satisfying
$$ \sum_r z_{tr}=n_t, \qquad \sum_t z_{tr}=\rho_r. $$

  • There are integers

$$ 0\le k_t\le\tau-1, \qquad 0\le j_r\le\tau, $$
satisfying
$$ j_r\le k_t+h_{tr} $$
for every pair $(t,r),$ with equality whenever $x_{tr}>0$.

  • $x$ is aggregate fractionally Pareto optimal: no nonnegative real matrix $z$ with column sums $\rho_r$ weakly decreases every type’s aggregate cost and strictly decreases at least one.

Proof: a negative cycle gives a coupling Pareto improvement

Suppose $G_x$ has a negative cycle. Choose one having the minimum possible number of arcs. It is simple: a closed walk with a repeated vertex splits into shorter closed walks, and if its total weight is negative, at least one of those walks has negative weight.

Write its distinct vertices as
$$ t_1,\ldots,t_\ell, $$
with indices interpreted cyclically. Let the arc from $t_i$ to $t_{i+1}$ have label $r_i$. In particular,
$$ x_{t_i r_i}>0. $$

I claim that every vertex satisfies
$$ h_{t_i r_{i-1}}\le h_{t_i r_i}. $$

Otherwise, at some vertex $v=t_i$, the incoming class is difficult and the outgoing class is easy. Let $s=t_{i-1}$ and $u=t_{i+1}$. The sum of the two incident arc weights is
$$ 1-h_{s r_{i-1}}+h_{u r_i}. $$
Because $x_{s r_{i-1}}>0$, there is also an arc directly from $s$ to $u$ labeled $r_{i-1}$. Its weight satisfies
$$ h_{u r_{i-1}}-h_{s r_{i-1}} \le 1-h_{s r_{i-1}} \le 1-h_{s r_{i-1}}+h_{u r_i}. $$
Replacing the two arcs by this shortcut produces a shorter negative cycle, a contradiction. If the original cycle has two vertices, this shortcut is a loop of weight zero, and the displayed inequality already contradicts negativity.

For every $i$, choose a supported bundle $B_i$ of type $t_i$ containing a chore of class $r_i$. Such a bundle exists because $x_{t_i r_i}>0$. Define
$$ D_i=B_i-\mathbf e_{r_i}+\mathbf e_{r_{i-1}}. $$
These bundles are nonnegative integer bundles and have the same cardinalities as $B_i$. Their cost changes are
$$ d_{t_i}(D_i)-d_{t_i}(B_i) =(p-1)\bigl(h_{t_i r_{i-1}}-h_{t_i r_i}\bigr) \le0. $$
Moreover,
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{\ell} \bigl(h_{t_i r_{i-1}}-h_{t_i r_i}\bigr) = \sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w(t_i,t_{i+1},r_i)<0, $$
so at least one participating type strictly improves.

Set
$$ \varepsilon=\min_i y_{t_i,B_i}>0. $$
Begin with the identity coupling, and for each $i$ redirect mass $\varepsilon$ from $B_i$ to $D_i$. The participating types are distinct, so each original array entry is modified at most once. If $D_i=B_i$, the corresponding modification simply cancels.

The resulting allocation is feasible because
$$ \sum_i(D_i-B_i)=\mathbf0. $$
The coupling is rational, every worker weakly improves, and at least mass $\varepsilon$ strictly improves. Consequently, $y$ is not Pareto optimal.

This argument also proves the promised local property: whenever Pareto optimality fails, an improvement exists using at most $\tau$ supported bundles, with each affected worker exchanging one outgoing chore for one incoming chore.

Proof: no negative cycle gives bounded integer certificates

Suppose $G_x$ has no negative cycle. Add a new source with a zero-weight arc to every type, and let $a_t$ be its shortest-path distance to $t$.

The distances are integers. A shortest path can be chosen simple, so after its initial source arc it uses at most $\tau-1$ arcs. Since each arc has weight at least $-1$, and a direct zero-weight source arc is available,
$$ -(\tau-1)\le a_t\le0. $$

For every arc labeled $r$ from $s$ to $t$,
$$ a_t-a_s\le h_{tr}-h_{sr}. $$
Set
$$ k_t=-a_t, \qquad j_r=\min_t(k_t+h_{tr}). $$
These integers have the asserted bounds.

If $x_{sr}>0$, the displayed shortest-path inequality implies, for every $t$,
$$ k_s+h_{sr}\le k_t+h_{tr}. $$
Therefore
$$ j_r=k_s+h_{sr}. $$
This establishes condition 4.

Proof: certificates imply aggregate fractional Pareto optimality

Assume condition 4, and define positive rational numbers
$$ \lambda_t=p^{k_t}, \qquad q_r=p^{j_r}. $$
Then
$$ q_r\le\lambda_t d_t(r), $$
with equality whenever $x_{tr}>0$.

For every nonnegative matrix $z$ having the prescribed class supplies,
$$ \sum_{t,r}\lambda_t d_t(r)z_{tr} \ge \sum_r q_r\rho_r = \sum_{t,r}\lambda_t d_t(r)x_{tr}. $$
Because every $\lambda_t$ is positive, an aggregate Pareto improvement would strictly decrease the left-hand weighted cost. Hence condition 5 holds.

To see that condition 5 implies condition 1, let $y'$ and $\pi$ be a coupling Pareto improvement and put
$$ z_{tr}=\sum_D y'_{t,D}D_r. $$
For every type,
$$ \sum_r d_t(r)(x_{tr}-z_{tr}) = \sum_{B,D}\pi_{t,B,D} \bigl(d_t(B)-d_t(D)\bigr). $$
Every summand is nonnegative. Positive mass strictly improves, so at least one type has a strict aggregate decrease. This contradicts condition 5.

Together with the negative-cycle argument, these implications establish the equivalence of conditions 1, 2, 4, and 5.

Proof: equivalence with transportation optimality

Under condition 4, put
$$ a_t=-k_t, \qquad b_r=j_r. $$
Then
$$ a_t+b_r\le h_{tr}, $$
with equality on the positive entries of $x$.

Every matrix $z$ with the prescribed row and column sums therefore satisfies
$$ \sum_{t,r}h_{tr}z_{tr} \ge \sum_t a_t n_t+\sum_r b_r\rho_r = \sum_{t,r}h_{tr}x_{tr}. $$
Thus condition 2 implies condition 3.

Conversely, the coupling improvement constructed from a negative cycle preserves every participating worker’s bundle size. Its aggregate assignment consequently preserves all row sums $n_t$. Its transportation objective changes by
$$ \varepsilon\sum_i w(t_i,t_{i+1},r_i)<0. $$
Thus a negative cycle contradicts condition 3. This completes the proof. $\square$

Consequences for verification

The graph has $\tau$ vertices and at most $\tau^2m$ arcs. Negative-cycle detection is polynomial-time. The aggregate quantities $x_{tr}$ can be computed exactly in polynomial time in the explicit allocation length.

The integer certificate in condition 4 has polynomial encoding length. Its corresponding rational weights also do: if $p$ has binary encoding length at most $L$, then $p^{k_t}$ and $p^{j_r}$ have encoding length bounded by a polynomial in $\tau$ and $L$.

Two further consequences follow directly from the graph definition:

  • For a feasible allocation, Pareto optimality depends only on the positive type-class pairs $x_{tr}>0$, not their magnitudes.
  • For a fixed binary pattern $h_{tr}$ and fixed feasible allocation, Pareto optimality is independent of the particular common value $p>1$.

Neither consequence asserts independence of EF1 from $p$.

2. Polynomial-time Pareto improvement preserving individual bundle sizes

The local exchange theorem can be strengthened to a polynomial-time construction. Repeatedly making local exchanges alone would not establish a polynomial running-time bound.

Theorem 2

Given an explicitly listed feasible rational allocation $y$, a Pareto-optimal feasible rational allocation $\widehat y$ and a rational coupling from $y$ to $\widehat y$ can be computed in polynomial time in the input and allocation lengths, such that:

  • every worker weakly improves;
  • every worker retains the same number of chores.

If $y$ is not Pareto optimal, the coupling gives positive mass a strict improvement.

Proof

Index the positive entries of $y$ by atoms $a$. An atom has type $t(a)$, original bundle $B_a$, and mass $w_a>0$. Define its integer counts
$$ K_a=\sum_r(B_a)_r, \qquad H_a=\sum_r h_{t(a)r}(B_a)_r. $$

Consider the rational linear program
$$ \min_{z\ge0}\ \sum_{a,r}h_{t(a)r}z_{ar} $$
subject to
$$ \sum_r z_{ar}=w_aK_a, $$
$$ \sum_r h_{t(a)r}z_{ar}\le w_aH_a, $$
and
$$ \sum_a z_{ar}=\rho_r. $$

The initial assignment $z_{ar}=w_a(B_a)_r$ is feasible. The feasible region is bounded, since each variable is bounded by its class supply. The program has polynomially many variables and constraints, with polynomial-length rational coefficients. Exact rational linear programming therefore gives an optimal rational solution $z^*$ of polynomial encoding length.

It remains to realize $z^*$ by integer bundles while respecting the constraints separately for every original atom.

Fix an atom $a$, and let
$$ \beta_a=\frac{\sum_r h_{t(a)r}z^*_{ar}}{w_a}. $$
Then
$$ 0\le\beta_a\le H_a\le K_a. $$
Write
$$ b_a=\lfloor\beta_a\rfloor, \qquad \delta_a=\beta_a-b_a. $$

Use two count profiles:

| Easy count | Difficult count | Conditional mass |
|---|---|---|
| $K_a-b_a$ | $b_a$ | $1-\delta_a$ |
| $K_a-b_a-1$ | $b_a+1$ | $\delta_a$ |

Discard a row of zero mass. Every retained count is nonnegative. If $\delta_a>0$, the integer inequalities $\beta_a\le H_a\le K_a$ imply
$$ b_a+1\le H_a\le K_a. $$
Thus every profile has exactly $K_a$ chores and at most $H_a$ difficult chores.

The expected difficult count is $\beta_a$, and the expected easy count is $K_a-\beta_a$.

For each category whose expected count is positive, select a class in that category with probability proportional to $z^*_{ar}$, and put all chores of that category in the selected class. Make the easy and difficult selections independently. A category with zero expected count is omitted; its count is zero in every retained profile.

This produces integer bundles whose mean class quantities, conditional on atom $a$, are exactly
$$ \frac{z^*_{ar}}{w_a}. $$
Assign their unconditional masses by multiplying by $w_a$. Record these masses as coupling entries from $B_a$ to the generated bundles, combining repeated occurrences.

Every generated bundle $D$ has cardinality $K_a$ and difficult count at most $H_a$. Therefore
$$ d_{t(a)}(D) \le K_a+(p-1)H_a =d_{t(a)}(B_a). $$
The resulting allocation $\widehat y$ is feasible and weakly improves every worker.

I now prove that it is Pareto optimal. Suppose otherwise. Theorem 1 supplies a cyclic improvement of $\widehat y$ that preserves individual bundle sizes and weakly decreases every affected worker’s difficult count, with a strict decrease for some positive mass.

Compose that cyclic coupling with the constructed coupling from $y$ to $\widehat y$. Explicitly, if the first coupling is $\pi$ and the cyclic coupling is $\sigma$, composition uses
$$ \widetilde\pi_{t,B,E} = \sum_{D:\,\widehat y_{t,D}>0} \frac{\pi_{t,B,D}\sigma_{t,D,E}} {\widehat y_{t,D}}. $$
This is a finite rational coupling with the required marginals.

Every worker originating from atom $a$ still has exactly $K_a$ chores and at most $H_a$ difficult chores. Its aggregate class quantities therefore give another feasible solution of the linear program. The positive strict decrease in difficult counts gives a strictly smaller objective, contradicting optimality of $z^*$.

If $y$ was not Pareto optimal, its negative-cycle improvement already gives a feasible solution of the same program with objective strictly below the initial value. Hence the optimal value is strictly smaller, and the constructed weak-improvement coupling must have positive strict-improvement mass.

There are at most two profiles and polynomially many class pairs per atom. Floors, probabilities, bundle coordinates, and generated masses all have polynomial binary length. Thus the entire construction is polynomial-time. $\square$

3. Two polynomial-time regimes for the requested allocation

Write
$$ S=\sum_r\rho_r. $$
For $S=0$, the allocation giving every worker the empty bundle is feasible, EF1, and Pareto optimal.

The following constructions work with arbitrary support size.

Proposition 3: total supply at most one

If
$$ 0<S\le1, $$
an EF1 and Pareto-optimal allocation can be found in polynomial time.

Proof

Set
$$ n_t=S\mu_t. $$
Find a matrix $x$ minimizing the binary transportation objective from Theorem 1, with row sums $n_t$ and column sums $\rho_r$.

This transportation problem is feasible: the matrix
$$ x_{tr}=\frac{n_t\rho_r}{S} $$
has the required margins. It is bounded and has polynomial-size rational data, so an optimum can be found in polynomial time.

Define
$$ y_{t,\mathbf e_r}=x_{tr}, \qquad y_{t,\mathbf0}=\mu_t-n_t=\mu_t(1-S). $$
Discard zero entries.

These entries have the prescribed masses and supplies, and their aggregate assignment is $x$. Every nonempty supported bundle is a singleton. Removing its sole chore leaves cost zero, so every ordered EF1 comparison holds.

Transportation optimality and Theorem 1 establish Pareto optimality. $\square$

Proposition 4: an integer-supply regime

Suppose
$$ S=k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}, \qquad p(k-1)\le k. $$
Then an EF1 and Pareto-optimal allocation can be found in polynomial time.

Proof

Choose a transportation-optimal matrix $x$ with row sums
$$ n_t=k\mu_t $$
and column sums $\rho_r$, as above. Define
$$ y_{t,k\mathbf e_r}=\frac{x_{tr}}{k}. $$

The type-mass equations follow from
$$ \sum_r\frac{x_{tr}}k=\mu_t. $$
The class-supply equations follow because each such bundle contains $k$ chores of its class. Its aggregate assignment is again $x$, so Theorem 1 gives Pareto optimality.

Every supported bundle contains exactly $k$ chores. For any comparing type $t$, removing one chore from its own bundle leaves cost at most
$$ p(k-1)\le k. $$
Every other supported bundle has cost at least $k$ under $d_t$. Thus every ordered EF1 comparison holds.

The integer $k$ may be numerically large, but its binary length is polynomial in the input length. The construction has at most $\tau m$ entries and uses polynomial-length rational operations. $\square$

For example, Proposition 4 covers arbitrary supported types and supplies with $S=2$ and $1<p\le2$.

4. A obstruction to fixing type-average bundle sizes

Theorem 2 preserves each worker’s bundle size, but it need not preserve EF1. The following example proves a stronger limitation: specified type-average bundle sizes can admit an EF1 allocation and a Pareto-optimal allocation, while admitting no allocation satisfying both.

Take three classes $a,b,c$, three types of mass $1/3$, and $p=2$:

| Type | $d_t(a)$ | $d_t(b)$ | $d_t(c)$ | Initial bundle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $2$ | $1$ | $1$ | $3\mathbf e_c$ |
| $2$ | $2$ | $1$ | $2$ | $\mathbf e_a$ |
| $3$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $\mathbf e_b+\mathbf e_c$ |

The supplies are
$$ \rho_a=\frac13, \qquad \rho_b=\frac13, \qquad \rho_c=\frac43, $$
and the aggregate type chore counts are
$$ n_1=1, \qquad n_2=\frac13, \qquad n_3=\frac23. $$

The initial allocation is EF1

Type $1$ has own cost $3$ and residual cost $2$ after removing a chore. Both other bundles have cost $2$ under $d_1$.

Type $2$ holds a singleton, so its residual cost is zero.

Type $3$ has residual cost $1$; every supported bundle has cost at least $1$ under $d_3$.

These observations verify all ordered EF1 comparisons.

A Pareto improvement destroys EF1

Exchange the class-$a$ chore of type $2$ with the class-$b$ chore of type $3$. The bundles become
$$ 3\mathbf e_c, \qquad \mathbf e_b, \qquad \mathbf e_a+\mathbf e_c. $$
Own costs change from
$$ (3,2,2) \quad\text{to}\quad (3,1,2). $$
Thus this is a Pareto improvement preserving every bundle size.

The resulting allocation is Pareto optimal: every assigned chore is easy for its recipient, so its binary transportation objective is zero.

It is not EF1. Type $1$ still has residual cost $2$, whereas the type-$2$ bundle has cost $1$ under $d_1$.

No EF1 and Pareto-optimal allocation has these same type-average sizes

Suppose an allocation has these supplies, masses, and aggregate type counts and is Pareto optimal. The displayed improved allocation shows that the minimum transportation objective for these margins is zero. Theorem 1 therefore forces the supposed allocation also to assign only easy chores.

Type $2$ can receive only class $b$. Its aggregate count $1/3$ uses the entire class-$b$ supply. Hence type $1$, whose easy classes are $b$ and $c$, can receive only class $c$.

Type $2$ has average bundle size
$$ \frac{n_2}{\mu_2}=1. $$
Consequently, some supported type-$2$ bundle is $j\mathbf e_b$ with integer $j\le1$.

For every nonempty supported type-$1$ bundle $q\mathbf e_c$, EF1 relative to that bundle requires
$$ q-1\le j\le1, $$
so $q\le2$. Empty bundles also have size at most $2$. This contradicts type $1$’s required average bundle size
$$ \frac{n_1}{\mu_1}=3. $$

Thus no EF1 and Pareto-optimal allocation preserves these aggregate type counts. In particular, selecting suitable type-average chore counts is a genuine part of the general construction problem.

5. Existence and polynomial-size witnesses

Existence

I use the supplied finite-agent theorem: every finite chore instance with a common pair of costs $\{1,p\}$ admits an EF1 and Pareto-optimal integral allocation. Constant-cost agents are permitted by the bivalued definition. See [Ebadian, Peters, and Shah, Definition 2.4 and Theorem 3.7](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~nisarg/papers/chores.pdf).

Choose an integer $Q$ clearing all input mass and supply denominators. Expand the instance into
$$ Q\mu_t $$
agents of type $t$ and
$$ Q\rho_r $$
individual chores of class $r$. Apply the finite theorem, and let $y_{t,B}$ be the number of agents of type $t$ receiving bundle $B$, divided by $Q$.

This allocation is feasible and EF1.

Its aggregate graph cannot contain a negative cycle. Indeed, the exchange argument in Theorem 1 would select one actual finite agent of each participating type and cyclically exchange their chores, giving an integral Pareto improvement of the finite allocation. That contradicts its Pareto optimality.

Theorem 1 now establishes Pareto optimality under the required rational-coupling definition.

The expansion may be exponentially large; this is an existence proof, not a polynomial-time algorithm.

Bundle encoding bound

For a supported nonempty bundle $B$ of type $t$, let
$$ M_t(B)=\max_{r:\,B_r>0}d_t(r). $$
Removing a maximum-cost chore minimizes the residual cost. Thus EF1 implies
$$ d_t(B)-M_t(B)\le d_t(D) $$
for every supported bundle $D$, regardless of its holder’s type.

Average over the population, whose total mass is one:
$$ d_t(B)-M_t(B)\le d_t(\rho). $$
Since $M_t(B)\le p$ and $d_t(\rho)\le pS$,
$$ \sum_r B_r\le d_t(B)\le p(S+1). $$
Therefore every supported bundle has cardinality at most
$$ K=\lfloor p(S+1)\rfloor. $$
The binary length of $K$ is polynomial in the input length.

Support compression

Start with an existing EF1 and Pareto-optimal allocation. Take its positive weighted-cost certificate from Theorem 1. Every supported pair $(t,B)$ satisfies
$$ \lambda_t d_t(B)=\sum_r q_rB_r. $$

Associate to that pair the integer column
$$ (\mathbf e_t,B)\in\mathbb Z^{\tau+m}. $$
Consider nonnegative reweightings of these finitely many columns satisfying the original type-mass and class-supply equations. The resulting polytope is nonempty and bounded.

At a vertex, the columns with positive weights are linearly independent. Otherwise, a nonzero dependence supported on those columns would permit sufficiently small perturbations in both directions while retaining nonnegativity and all equations. Hence a vertex has at most
$$ m+\tau $$
positive entries.

Reweighting to such a vertex preserves:

  • feasibility, by the defining equations;
  • EF1, because the retained support is a subset of the original support;
  • Pareto optimality, because every retained bundle still attains the weighted-cost lower bound.

Finally, the vertex weights have polynomial binary length. To verify this explicitly, put $N=m+\tau$ and clear the right-hand-side denominators with an integer $Q_0$ of polynomial binary length. A vertex with $k\le N$ positive weights is determined by an invertible $k$-by-$k$ integer submatrix.

All coefficient entries are bounded by $K$. If $V$ bounds the absolute cleared right-hand-side entries, Cramer’s rule gives weight denominators bounded in absolute value by
$$ Q_0\,k!K^k $$
and numerators bounded by
$$ k!\max\{K,V\}^k. $$
Both bounds have polynomial binary length. There are at most $N$ bundles, each with polynomial-length coordinates.

Thus every instance has a polynomial-size EF1 and Pareto-optimal witness. Feasibility and EF1 can be checked directly, and Theorem 1 supplies a polynomial-time Pareto test.

6. What remains open

These results remove existence, output size, Pareto verification, and Pareto improvement of a supplied allocation as obstacles.

They do not give a polynomial-time method for choosing an EF1-compatible efficient allocation for arbitrary support and arbitrary supplies. The obstruction in Section 4 shows why optimizing assignments at previously chosen type-average chore counts is insufficient.

No general polynomial-time construction or hardness reduction is proved here.
