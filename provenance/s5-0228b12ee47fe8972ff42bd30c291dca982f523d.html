Theorem 7.11 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a target \(p\), a finite support \(T\) of ranking-and-price types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), a per-capita budget \(B\), and an affected-share limit \(\alpha\), is there an allocation \(x_{t,j}\geq 0\) for integral upward shifts \(j\) such that \(\sum_j x_{t,j}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,j>0}x_{t,j}\leq\alpha\), \(\sum_{t,j}\pi_t(j)x_{t,j}\leq B\), and \(p\)'s resulting Borda score is at least every other candidate's?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq2\) candidates and let \(p\in C\). A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of its \(\tau\geq1\) ranking-and-price types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a strict complete ranking and an explicitly tabulated, nondecreasing price function \(\pi_t:\{0,\ldots,r_t-1\}\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\), with \(\pi_t(0)=0\), where \(r_t\) is the initial position of \(p\); prices are binary encoded.
A shift \(j\in\{0,\ldots,r_t-1\}\) moves \(p\) upward by exactly \(j\) positions, preserving all other candidates' relative order. Mass of each type may be divided arbitrarily among these shifts; \(\pi_t(j)\) is the cost per unit mass.
Given binary rationals \(B\geq0\) and \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), feasibility means that real allocations \(x_{t,j}\geq0\) satisfy \(\sum_jx_{t,j}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,j>0}x_{t,j}\leq\alpha\), and \(\sum_{t,j}\pi_t(j)x_{t,j}\leq B\), and make \(p\) a Borda co-winner, with scores aggregated by mass. Ties are accepted.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\), including the encodings of \(B\) and \(\alpha\).

Is this continuous feasibility problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
If it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, is it \(\mathrm{FPT}\) parameterized by \(\tau\)?

Discrete Borda Shift Bribery is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete ([Elkind–Faliszewski–Slinko](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~slinko/Research/swap.pdf)); with an affected-voter bound it is \(\mathrm{W}[2]\)-hard even for unit prices ([Bredereck et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.01253)).
Continuous Borda Shift Bribery with a cost budget alone is polynomial-time solvable; that result does not impose the additional affected-share bound ([Koutecký et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25444v1)).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total mass to one so that the affected-share limit and per-capita budget have their stated meanings.
  • Specified strict complete rankings, at least two candidates, and a nonempty positive-mass support.
  • Used explicitly tabulated nonnegative integer prices, nondecreasing with shift length and zero for no shift, following the discrete definition.
  • Restricted shifts to the distance from p to the top; larger requests in the discrete definition merely saturate at the top.
  • Specified nonnegative rational budgets, rational affected-share limits between zero and one, divisible real allocations, and acceptance of Borda ties.
  • Used support size as the integer parameter for the conditional FPT question because the normalized affected-share limit does not itself supply a discrete affected-voter count.

Conclusion

The problem is in \(\mathrm P\), even with both bounds imposed simultaneously. The affected-share bound adds one linear inequality to a polynomial-size rational linear program. Consequently, the problem is also \(\mathrm{FPT}\) parameterized by \(\tau\).

1. Borda scores after a shift

Number ranking positions from \(\!1\) to \(m\), and assign Borda score \(m-k\) to position \(k\). Let \(q_t(c)\) be candidate \(c\)'s initial position in type \(t\), so \(q_t(p)=r_t\).

For each allowed shift \(j\), define
\[ h_{t,j}(c)=\mathbf 1\{r_t-j\leq q_t(c)<r_t\}, \qquad c\neq p. \]
The indicator equals one precisely when \(p\) passes \(c\). Such a candidate moves down one position; every other opponent keeps its position. Thus the scores per unit mass after shift \(j\) are

\(s_{t,j}(p)=m-r_t+j,\)

\(s_{t,j}(c)=m-q_t(c)-h_{t,j}(c)\quad(c\neq p).\)

Their difference is therefore

\(d_{t,j}(c):=s_{t,j}(p)-s_{t,j}(c)=q_t(c)-r_t+j+h_{t,j}(c).\)

These coefficients are explicitly computable integers. Since both scores lie between zero and \(m-1\), they satisfy \(\lvert d_{t,j}(c)\rvert\leq m-1\).

2. An exact linear-programming formulation

Introduce one real variable \(x_{t,j}\) for every listed type \(t\in T\) and every \(j\in\{0,\ldots,r_t-1\}\). Consider the following system:

\(x_{t,j}\geq0\quad\text{for every }t,j,\)

\(\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r_t-1}x_{t,j}=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t,\)

\(\displaystyle\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{j=1}^{r_t-1}x_{t,j}\leq\alpha,\)

\(\displaystyle\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{j=0}^{r_t-1}\pi_t(j)x_{t,j}\leq B,\)

\(\displaystyle\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{j=0}^{r_t-1}d_{t,j}(c)x_{t,j}\geq0 \quad\text{for every }c\in C\setminus\{p\}.\)

Equivalence proof. Given a feasible allocation in the question, its allocated masses satisfy the first four families of constraints by definition. By linear aggregation of Borda scores, the left-hand side of the final inequality is exactly the total score of \(p\) minus that of \(c\). Hence every final inequality holds.

Conversely, a solution of this system divides each type's mass among its allowed shifts, respects both bounds, and gives \(p\) at least the score of every opponent. Arbitrary mass division is expressly permitted, so these variables themselves constitute an admissible allocation. Thus \(p\) is a co-winner.

This proves equivalence in both directions, including equality at the budgets and ties between candidates.

3. Encoding size and running time

Let \(N=\sum_{t\in T}r_t\leq\tau m\) be the number of variables. Apart from nonnegativity, there are \(\tau+m+1\) constraints. Replacing each equality by two inequalities preserves these bounds up to a constant factor.

All score coefficients have binary length \(O(\log(m+1))\). The price coefficients and the right-hand sides \(\mu_t,B,\alpha\) are copied directly from the input. In particular, no denominator expansion or enumeration of unlisted types is required.

Consequently, with nonnegativity represented as variable bounds, a dense encoding of this linear program has length

\(K=O\!\left(L+\tau m(\tau+m)\log(m+1)\right).\)

We apply the standard polynomial-time rational linear-programming theorem: feasibility of a finite system of rational linear inequalities over the reals can be decided exactly in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. Its hypotheses hold here because every coefficient and right-hand side is rational and the whole system is explicitly available. This theorem handles feasible sets of lower dimension, so accepted ties cause no difficulty.

Constructing the system and deciding feasibility therefore takes

\(\left(L+\tau m(\tau+m)\log(m+1)\right)^{O(1)}\)

bit operations, with an absolute constant exponent. The bound includes the encodings of \(B\) and \(\alpha\). Since the input explicitly lists complete rankings and all support types, both \(m\) and \(\tau\) are bounded by a polynomial in \(L\); hence this is polynomial in \(L\).

4. Complexity implications

The cited discrete hardness results concern indivisible voters, whose shift choices are discrete. Arbitrary splitting removes that restriction in the present model. See [Elkind–Faliszewski–Slinko](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~slinko/Research/swap.pdf) and [Bredereck et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.01253).

The cost-only continuous tractability result uses polynomially many allowed shifts per support type; our formulation shows explicitly that imposing the affected-share bound preserves this property. See [Koutecký et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25444v1).

Thus the problem is polynomial-time solvable, and cannot be \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\). Its polynomial-time algorithm also establishes \(\mathrm{FPT}\) with parameter \(\tau\), since its running time is \(L^{O(1)}\), with an exponent independent of \(\tau\).
