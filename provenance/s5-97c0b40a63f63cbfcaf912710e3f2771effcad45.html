The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), per-institution endowments \(z_t\), and exposure densities \(h_{tu}\), where each type-\(t\) institution owes \(h_{tu}/n\) to each type-\(u\) institution in an \(n\)-institution replication, choose payment rates \(\lambda_t\in[0,1]\) and type-level flows \(x_{tu}=\mu_t\mu_u\lambda_t h_{tu}\). Compute the greatest clearing flow by maximizing \(\sum_{t,u}x_{tu}\) subject to \(\mu_tz_t+\sum_s x_{st}-\sum_u x_{tu}\ge0\) for every \(t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a nonnegative rational per-institution endowment \(z_t\); nonnegative rational exposure densities \(h_{tu}\) specify liabilities from type \(t\) to type \(u\). The society is nonatomic: within-type exposures \(h_{tt}\) are allowed, but an institution owes nothing to itself.
All institutions use proportional division. For rates \(\lambda_t\in[0,1]\), define aggregate flows \(x_{tu}=\mu_t\mu_u\lambda_t h_{tu}\) and aggregate equities \(e_t=\mu_tz_t+\sum_s x_{st}-\sum_u x_{tu}\).
A flow is clearing if \(e_t\ge0\) for every type and \(e_t=0\) whenever \(\sum_u x_{tu}<\mu_t\sum_u\mu_u h_{tu}\). Greatest means componentwise greatest among clearing flows; rates on zero-liability rows need not be unique.
The proposed computational problem maximizes \(\sum_{t,u}x_{tu}\) over the stated flows and rates subject to \(e_t\ge0\), to obtain the greatest clearing flow. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including any decision threshold; there is no separate parameter \(m\) in this model.

Is exact computation of the greatest clearing flow by this maximization polynomial-time in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is deciding whether its total payment is at least an input rational threshold \(B\) NP-hard?

For finite institution lists with proportional division and nonnegative endowments and liabilities, the greatest clearing payment matrix is the unique optimal payment matrix of a linear program, hence computable in polynomial time for rational inputs. Result

Exact computation of the greatest clearing flow is polynomial-time in \(\tau\) and \(L\). The threshold decision problem therefore belongs to \(\mathrm P\); NP-hardness under polynomial-time many-one reductions would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof below works directly with the mass-input formulation, including positive within-type exposures. It establishes the analogue of the finite-network LP characterization in [Csóka and Herings, Theorem 4.2](https://www.mechanism-design.org/arch/v007-1/p_02.pdf).

1. The proposed maximization is a linear program

For \(t,u\in T\), define the aggregate liability coefficient, aggregate endowment, and total aggregate liability by

\( a_{tu}=\mu_t\mu_u h_{tu},\qquad q_t=\mu_tz_t,\qquad d_t=\sum_{u\in T}a_{tu}. \)

All these numbers are nonnegative rationals. For a rate vector \(\lambda\), the induced flow, equity, and total payment are

\( x_{tu}=a_{tu}\lambda_t,\qquad e_t(\lambda)=q_t+\sum_{s\in T}a_{st}\lambda_s-d_t\lambda_t,\qquad F(\lambda)=\sum_{t\in T}d_t\lambda_t. \)

Consequently, the proposed maximization is exactly the following rational linear program:

\( \text{maximize }\sum_{t\in T}d_t\lambda_t \)

subject to

\( d_t\lambda_t-\sum_{s\in T}a_{st}\lambda_s\le q_t \quad(t\in T),\qquad 0\le\lambda_t\le1 \quad(t\in T). \)

Let \(K\) denote its feasible set. Because \(q_t\ge0\), the zero vector belongs to \(K\). The set \(K\) is closed and contained in the compact cube \([0,1]^T\), so it is compact. Thus the continuous objective \(F\) attains its maximum.

It remains to prove that every optimizer induces the greatest clearing flow.

2. Every optimizer dominates every feasible flow

Lemma. If \(\lambda,\eta\in K\), their coordinatewise maximum \(v\), defined by \(v_t=\max\{\lambda_t,\eta_t\}\), also belongs to \(K\).

Proof. Fix \(t\). Choose \(\rho\in\{\lambda,\eta\}\) such that \(v_t=\rho_t\). Feasibility of \(\rho\), together with \(a_{st}\ge0\) and \(v_s\ge\rho_s\), gives

\( d_tv_t=d_t\rho_t \le q_t+\sum_{s\in T}a_{st}\rho_s \le q_t+\sum_{s\in T}a_{st}v_s. \)

Also \(0\le v_t\le1\). These inequalities hold for every \(t\), proving feasibility. \(\square\)

Now let \(\lambda^*\) be any optimizer and let \(\eta\in K\). Suppose there were a type \(t\) with \(d_t>0\) and \(\eta_t>\lambda_t^*\). By the lemma, \(v=\max\{\lambda^*,\eta\}\) is feasible. Since every \(d_s\ge0\),

\( F(v)-F(\lambda^*) =\sum_{s\in T}d_s(v_s-\lambda_s^*) \ge d_t(\eta_t-\lambda_t^*)>0, \)

contradicting optimality. Therefore,

\( \lambda_t^*\ge\eta_t \quad\text{for every }t\text{ with }d_t>0. \)

If \(d_t=0\), nonnegativity of the coefficients implies \(a_{tu}=0\) for every \(u\). Such a row induces zero flow for every rate. Hence, for every feasible \(\eta\) and every \(t,u\),

\( a_{tu}\lambda_t^*\ge a_{tu}\eta_t. \)

Thus every optimizer induces a flow that componentwise dominates all feasible flows.

This also proves uniqueness of the optimal flow matrix: any two optimizers induce flows that dominate each other. Rates can differ only on zero-liability rows.

3. Every optimizer satisfies the clearing condition

Let \(\lambda^*\) be an optimizer. Its equities are nonnegative by feasibility.

Suppose the additional clearing condition failed at some type \(t\). Then

\( d_t\lambda_t^*<d_t \quad\text{and}\quad e_t(\lambda^*)>0. \)

The strict payment inequality implies \(d_t>0\) and \(\lambda_t^*<1\). Set

\( \varepsilon =\frac12\min\left\{1-\lambda_t^*,\frac{e_t(\lambda^*)}{d_t}\right\}>0, \)

and increase only the rate of type \(t\) by \(\varepsilon\), obtaining \(\lambda'\).

The rate bounds remain satisfied. For any \(s\ne t\), its equity changes by

\( e_s(\lambda')-e_s(\lambda^*)=a_{ts}\varepsilon\ge0. \)

For type \(t\), the change includes both its additional incoming within-type payment and its additional outgoing payment:

\( e_t(\lambda') =e_t(\lambda^*)-(d_t-a_{tt})\varepsilon \ge e_t(\lambda^*)-d_t\varepsilon \ge\frac12e_t(\lambda^*)>0. \)

Thus \(\lambda'\) is feasible. But

\( F(\lambda')-F(\lambda^*)=d_t\varepsilon>0, \)

contradicting optimality.

Every optimizer therefore satisfies \(e_t=0\) whenever outgoing payment is strictly below total liability. When \(d_t=0\), that strict inequality cannot hold, so the clearing condition imposes no additional requirement.

The optimal flow is consequently clearing. Every clearing flow is feasible for the LP, so Section 2 proves that this flow is the greatest clearing flow.

Within-type exposures. The argument allows \(a_{tt}>0\) throughout. Such payments count toward total payment and appear on both sides of the equity balance. A within-type aggregate includes payments between distinct institutions of that type, so it need not vanish when institutions owe nothing to themselves.

4. Polynomial encoding size and exact computation

Use the usual binary numerator-and-denominator encoding of rational inputs. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses, endowments, and exposure densities. Its binary length is \(O(L)\), because the sum of the binary lengths of those denominators is bounded by the total input length.

Each \(a_{tu}\) has denominator dividing \(D^3\), each \(q_t\) has denominator dividing \(D^2\), and each \(d_t\) has denominator dividing \(D^3\). Multiplying the objective and every constraint by the positive integer \(D^3\) therefore gives an equivalent LP with integer coefficients.

Each input rational has magnitude at most \(2^L\), and \(\mu_t\le1\). Consequently, \(a_{tu}\) and \(q_t\) are at most \(2^L\), while \(d_t\le\tau2^L\). After multiplication by \(D^3\), all LP coefficients have binary length \(O(L+\log\tau)\).

The LP has \(\tau\) variables and \(3\tau\) inequalities. Its total encoding length is therefore \(O(\tau^2(L+\log\tau))\), and its construction takes polynomial time.

We apply the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a rational LP admits a deterministic algorithm, polynomial in its dimensions and binary encoding length, that returns an exact rational optimizer when its optimum is finite and attained. This is the classical [polynomial-time solvability theorem for linear programming](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319). Here all hypotheses hold: the coefficients are rational, the feasible set is nonempty, and it is compact.

Exact output also has polynomial size. A bounded rational polytope has an optimal vertex. At a vertex, \(\tau\) linearly independent active constraints determine the coordinates. If their integer coefficients have magnitude at most \(M\), Hadamard’s inequality bounds the determinants in Cramer’s rule by \(\tau^{\tau/2}M^\tau\). Their binary lengths are therefore \(O(\tau(\log M+\log\tau))\), which is polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

After computing an exact optimizer, forming every \(x_{tu}^*=a_{tu}\lambda_t^*\) takes polynomial time and produces the unique greatest clearing flow. There is no expansion into an institution list or additional population-size parameter.

5. Threshold decision

For an input rational threshold \(B\), compute the exact optimum \(F(\lambda^*)\) and compare it with \(B\) using rational arithmetic. All numbers involved have polynomial binary length.

Equivalently, the answer is yes precisely when the LP constraints together with

\( \sum_{t\in T}d_t\lambda_t\ge B \)

are feasible. This gives a direct polynomial-time decision procedure, including equality at the threshold.
