The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite horizon \(H\), resource classes \(R\) with per-capita supplies \(\rho_r\), agent types \(A\) with utilities \(w_{ar}\), bounded patience and occupation-time laws, and a known finite-support rational law over arriving cohort-mass vectors \(\xi_t\), does there exist a causal policy, computable in time polynomial in the encoded instance size, such that for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \(\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{ALG}] \geq (1/2-\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}]\)? The policy allocates waiting type mass to resource classes over time; \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is the feasible fluid allocation that knows the cohort trajectory in advance.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Time consists of \(H\geq1\) rounds. There are \(m\geq1\) resource classes, initially entirely free, with positive binary-rational capacities \(\rho_r\), and an explicitly specified eligibility relation \(E\subseteq A\times R\); eligible matches have nonnegative binary-rational utilities \(w_{ar}\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(A\) is the union of cohort supports and \(\tau=|A|\), not the number of possible types. Masses and capacities use a fixed population reference unit; cohort masses need not sum to one.
For each round \(t\), an explicitly listed finite-support rational distribution specifies the nonnegative rational cohort vector \(\xi_t\). These vectors are independent across rounds, their laws are known in advance, and \(\xi_t\) is revealed at the start of round \(t\).
Each type has a rational patience law \(p_a(d)\), supported on \(\{0,\ldots,D\}\): exactly \(p_a(d)\xi_{t,a}\) of its arriving mass has deadline \(t+d\), revealed upon arrival. This mass can be matched through that round, or leaves unmatched afterward.
Each eligible pair has a rational occupation law \(q_{ar}(k)\), supported on \(\{1,\ldots,K\}\). Allocating mass \(x\) to that pair in round \(s\) earns \(w_{ar}x\) immediately; exactly \(q_{ar}(k)x\) occupies capacity during rounds \(s,\ldots,s+k-1\), returning before round \(s+k\). These proportions apply to every allocation, independently of its patience subdivision.
A policy may split waiting mass among eligible classes and rounds, but each mass unit is matched at most once; matches cannot be revoked, and occupied mass never exceeds capacity. Returns precede arrivals and allocation in each round. Allocations cease after round \(H\). A causal policy observes only arrivals through the current round and may randomize.
Let \(\mathrm{ALG}\) be its total utility and \(\mathrm{OPT}\) the maximum feasible fluid utility knowing the entire arrival trajectory. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; \(H,D,K\) are also encoded in unary.

Does there exist an algorithm that, for every instance and rational \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), constructs and executes a causal policy in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,H,D,K,L,1/\varepsilon\), satisfying \(\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{ALG}]\geq(1/2-\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}]\)?

For discrete online matching with non-identical reusable resources and patient agents under known stochastic arrivals, an online algorithm achieves competitive ratio \(1/2-\varepsilon\). [Dong et al.](https://chienjuho.com/pub/AAMAS21_ScarceMatching.pdf)
Related work uses fluid approximations to obtain a \(1-1/e\) guarantee in a large-inventory reusable-resource allocation model; this does not establish the guarantee for the patient fluid model defined here. [Goyal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03983)

1. A reservation lemma for independent cohorts

First consider an auxiliary allocation problem.

There is a finite set $\mathcal V$ of bins, with positive rational capacities $C_v$. A reservation consumes bin capacity permanently. In round $t$, an independent outcome $i\in I_t$ is revealed, with probability $\pi_{t,i}>0$. It specifies the masses $M_{t,i,g}$ of a finite collection of groups $g\in G_t$.

Group $g$ may be assigned to bins in a specified set $S_{t,g}$. Assigning mass $x$ to bin $v$ earns $c_{t,g,v}x$, where $c_{t,g,v}\geq0$ is fixed. Mass may be split, but its total assignment cannot exceed its supply.

Let $\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{res}}$ denote the maximum assignment utility knowing every outcome.

Lemma. There is a deterministic causal reservation policy, constructible and executable in polynomial time in the explicit rational input length, such that
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}_{\mathrm{res}}] \geq \frac12\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{res}}]. $$

1.1 A conditional-outcome linear program

Use nonnegative variables $b_{t,i,g,v}$ for allowed assignments, and set all other variables to zero. Write
$$ \bar b_{t,g,v} = \sum_{i\in I_t}\pi_{t,i}b_{t,i,g,v}. $$

Maximize
$$ V=\sum_{t,g,v}c_{t,g,v}\bar b_{t,g,v} $$
subject to
$$ \sum_v b_{t,i,g,v}\leq M_{t,i,g} \qquad\text{for every }t,i,g, $$
$$ \sum_g b_{t,i,g,v}\leq C_v \qquad\text{for every }t,i,v, $$
and
$$ \sum_{t,g}\bar b_{t,g,v}\leq C_v \qquad\text{for every }v. $$

Its optimal value $V^\star$ satisfies
$$ V^\star\geq\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{res}}]. $$

To prove this, select an offline optimal assignment $Y_{t,g,v}$ for every outcome trajectory and define
$$ b_{t,i,g,v} = \mathbb E[Y_{t,g,v}\mid I_t=i]. $$
The supply constraints follow by conditioning the offline supply constraints. An offline assignment reserves at most $C_v$ in bin $v$ altogether, so its assignments from any one round also total at most $C_v$; conditioning gives the second constraint family. Taking expectations of the total bin-capacity constraints gives the third family. The objective equals $\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{res}}]$.

There are finitely many trajectories, and each trajectory’s feasible assignment set is a nonempty compact polytope. Thus the selected optima exist, and conditional expectations pose no measurability problem.

1.2 The policy and its feasibility

Fix an optimal LP solution. Precompute
$$ \alpha_{v,t} = 1-\frac{1}{2C_v} \sum_{u<t,g}\bar b_{u,g,v}. $$
The LP constraints imply
$$ \frac12\leq\alpha_{v,t}\leq1. $$

Let $F_{v,t}$ be the unreserved capacity of bin $v$ before round $t$. Upon observing outcome $i$, reserve
$$ X_{t,g,v} = \frac{F_{v,t}}{2C_v\alpha_{v,t}}b_{t,i,g,v}. $$

Inductively, $0\leq F_{v,t}\leq C_v$. Consequently,
$$ 0\leq \frac{F_{v,t}}{2C_v\alpha_{v,t}} \leq1. $$
For every group,
$$ \sum_vX_{t,g,v} \leq \sum_vb_{t,i,g,v} \leq M_{t,i,g}. $$
For every bin,
$$ \sum_gX_{t,g,v} \leq \frac{F_{v,t}}{2C_v\alpha_{v,t}}C_v = \frac{F_{v,t}}{2\alpha_{v,t}} \leq F_{v,t}. $$
Thus every reservation is feasible.

1.3 Expected utility

I prove inductively that
$$ \mathbb E[X_{t,g,v}\mid I_t=i] = \frac12b_{t,i,g,v}. $$

The induction hypothesis for earlier rounds yields
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[F_{v,t}] &= C_v-\sum_{u<t,g}\mathbb E[X_{u,g,v}]\\ &= C_v-\frac12\sum_{u<t,g}\bar b_{u,g,v}\\ &= C_v\alpha_{v,t}. \end{aligned} $$

The unreserved capacity $F_{v,t}$ depends only on earlier outcomes. Independence across rounds therefore implies that it is independent of $I_t$. Hence
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[X_{t,g,v}\mid I_t=i] &= \frac{b_{t,i,g,v}}{2C_v\alpha_{v,t}} \mathbb E[F_{v,t}\mid I_t=i]\\ &=\frac12b_{t,i,g,v}. \end{aligned} $$
The base case uses $F_{v,1}=C_v$ and $\alpha_{v,1}=1$.

Summing utilities proves
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}_{\mathrm{res}}] = \frac12V^\star \geq \frac12\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{res}}]. $$

The equality is with half the LP value; equality with half the expected offline optimum is not asserted.

2. A block reservation theorem for the patient fluid model

Discard resource classes with no eligible type. If none remain, both utilities are zero.

For every eligible pair, define the occupation tail
$$ Q_{ar}(h)=\sum_{k=h+1}^{K}q_{ar}(k), \qquad h\geq0. $$
An allocation of mass $x$ in round $s$ occupies exactly $Q_{ar}(u-s)x$ in round $u\geq s$. In particular, $Q_{ar}(0)=1$, the tails are nonincreasing, and $Q_{ar}(h)=0$ for $h\geq K$.

For an active resource class $r$, define
$$ U_r(h)=\max_{a:(a,r)\in E}Q_{ar}(h). $$

Choose an integer block length $\ell_r\in\{1,\ldots,H\}$ for which
$$ \beta_r = \min_{a:(a,r)\in E}Q_{ar}(\ell_r-1) >0. $$
Such a choice always exists: $\ell_r=1$ gives $\beta_r=1$.

Partition rounds into consecutive blocks
$$ B_{r,j} = \{(j-1)\ell_r+1,\ldots,\min(j\ell_r,H)\}, \qquad j=1,\ldots,n_r, $$
where
$$ n_r=\left\lceil\frac{H}{\ell_r}\right\rceil. $$

Set
$$ G_r = 1+\sum_{j=0}^{n_r-2}U_r(1+j\ell_r), \qquad \lambda_r=\frac{\beta_r}{G_r}, $$
with an empty sum interpreted as zero.

Theorem. There is a deterministic polynomial-time causal policy satisfying
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] \geq \frac12\left(\min_r\lambda_r\right) \mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]. $$

2.1 Offline allocations satisfy a block supply bound

Fix a trajectory and a feasible allocation.

Consider block $B_{r,j}$, and let $e_{r,j}$ be its last round. Every allocation to class $r$ started in that block has age at most $\ell_r-1$ at round $e_{r,j}$. Therefore its occupation fraction in that round is at least $\beta_r$.

If $T_{r,j}$ is the total mass allocated to class $r$ during the block, capacity feasibility at its last round gives
$$ \beta_rT_{r,j}\leq\rho_r. $$
All occupation from earlier blocks is nonnegative, so omitting it preserves this inequality. Consequently,
$$ T_{r,j}\leq\frac{\rho_r}{\beta_r}. $$

This also holds for the final, possibly shorter block.

2.2 Constructing the reservation instance

Create one bin $(r,j)$ for every resource block, with capacity
$$ C_{r,j}=\frac{\rho_r}{\beta_r}. $$

The groups arriving in round $t$ are the patience subdivisions $(a,d)$, with masses
$$ M_{t,i,(a,d)}=p_a(d)z_{t,i,a}, $$
where $z_{t,i}$ is the revealed cohort outcome.

Group $(a,d)$ may reserve bin $(r,j)$ precisely when $(a,r)\in E$ and
$$ B_{r,j}\cap\{t,\ldots,\min(H,t+d)\}\neq\varnothing. $$
Its utility per reserved mass is $w_{ar}$.

Every original feasible allocation induces a feasible offline reservation assignment: assign each match to the bin containing its matching round. Supply constraints are unchanged, and Section 2.1 proves the bin-capacity constraints. Thus, pathwise,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{res}}\geq\mathrm{OPT}. $$

For completeness, an original offline optimum exists: its allocation amounts can be indexed by arrival round, type, patience subdivision, resource class, and matching round. Supply and occupation constraints are finitely many closed linear inequalities, and each variable is bounded by its subdivision’s finite supply. The feasible set is nonempty and compact.

Apply the reservation lemma to obtain reservations $X_{t,a,d,r,j}$.

2.3 Scheduling the actual allocations

For every allowed reservation, define the service round
$$ s(t,d,r,j)=\max\{t,(j-1)\ell_r+1\}. $$
The eligibility of the bin guarantees that this round belongs to the block and does not exceed either $H$ or $t+d$.

A reservation of amount $X_{t,a,d,r,j}$ produces an actual allocation of amount
$$ \lambda_rX_{t,a,d,r,j} $$
in that service round.

This is causal. At arrival, the policy fixes the future service round and amount using only the current cohort and earlier reservations. Since $0<\lambda_r\leq1$, the actual scheduled amounts from a patience subdivision total at most its reservation total, which is at most its arriving mass. Divisibility permits assigning disjoint pieces to these scheduled matches.

Every piece is served by its deadline, and no piece is served twice.

2.4 Physical capacity feasibility

Pathwise, the total actual allocation mass started in block $B_{r,j}$ is at most
$$ \lambda_rC_{r,j} = \frac{\rho_r}{G_r}. $$

Fix round $u$ in block $B_{r,c}$. Allocations started in the current block occupy at most their total mass, hence at most $\rho_r/G_r$.

For an earlier block $B_{r,b}$, its last round is $b\ell_r$. Every allocation from that block has age at least $u-b\ell_r$, so its occupation fraction is at most
$$ U_r(u-b\ell_r). $$
Because $u\geq(c-1)\ell_r+1$ and $U_r$ is nonincreasing,
$$ U_r(u-b\ell_r) \leq U_r(1+(c-1-b)\ell_r). $$

Therefore total occupation in class $r$ at round $u$ is at most
$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{\rho_r}{G_r} \left( 1+\sum_{b=1}^{c-1} U_r(1+(c-1-b)\ell_r) \right) &= \frac{\rho_r}{G_r} \left( 1+\sum_{j=0}^{c-2}U_r(1+j\ell_r) \right)\\ &\leq\rho_r. \end{aligned} $$
The last inequality follows from $c\leq n_r$ and the definition of $G_r$.

This bounds occupation including all allocations made in round $u$. Thus it also ensures that their total mass fits the capacity free immediately before allocation. After round $H$, occupation only decreases.

The calculation uses the stipulated fact that occupation proportions apply to every allocation, independently of its patience subdivision.

2.5 Utility guarantee

Every reserved piece is served within the horizon. Since utilities are nonnegative,
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] &= \sum_{t,a,d,r,j} w_{ar}\lambda_r \mathbb E[X_{t,a,d,r,j}]\\ &\geq \left(\min_r\lambda_r\right) \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}_{\mathrm{res}}]\\ &\geq \frac12\left(\min_r\lambda_r\right) \mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{res}}]\\ &\geq \frac12\left(\min_r\lambda_r\right) \mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]. \end{aligned} $$
This completes the theorem.

3. Consequences

3.1 Full return before the next round: the requested guarantee

Suppose $q_{ar}(1)=1$ for every eligible pair.

Choose $\ell_r=1$. Then $\beta_r=1$ and $U_r(h)=0$ for every $h\geq1$, so
$$ G_r=1, \qquad \lambda_r=1. $$
The theorem gives
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] \geq\frac12\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]. $$

This proves the requested guarantee for arbitrary patience when all allocations return before the next round, including the case $K=1$.

Here the bins are simply resource class–round pairs. Reserving a future bin upon arrival avoids any need to assume independence between a waiting cohort and physical free capacity.

3.2 A mixture of immediate-return and permanent classes

Suppose every active resource class satisfies one of these conditions:

  • Every eligible occupation law is concentrated on length $1$.
  • Every eligible occupation law is supported on lengths at least $H$.

For an immediate-return class, choose $\ell_r=1$, as above.

For a permanent class, choose $\ell_r=H$. Then $n_r=1$, $G_r=1$, and
$$ \beta_r=\min_aQ_{ar}(H-1)=1. $$
Thus every class has $\lambda_r=1$, and again
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] \geq\frac12\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]. $$

The two kinds of class may coexist, with arbitrary eligibility and utilities.

3.3 Deterministic occupation length depending only on class

Suppose each class $r$ has an integer $k_r$ such that
$$ q_{ar}(k_r)=1 \qquad \text{for every eligible }a. $$

If $k_r=1$, the preceding calculation gives $\lambda_r=1$. If $k_r\geq H$, choose $\ell_r=H$, again giving $\lambda_r=1$.

Otherwise, $2\leq k_r<H$. Choose $\ell_r=k_r$. Then $\beta_r=1$ and
$$ U_r(h)= \begin{cases} 1,&h<k_r,\\ 0,&h\geq k_r. \end{cases} $$
There are at least two blocks. In the sum defining $G_r$, the term with $j=0$ equals $U_r(1)=1$, and all terms with $j\geq1$ vanish. Hence
$$ G_r=2, \qquad \lambda_r=\frac12. $$

Therefore, with arbitrary patience and possibly different deterministic lengths across classes,
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] \geq\frac14\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]. $$

This bound does not deteriorate with $D$, $H$, or the deterministic occupation lengths.

3.4 A general bound controlled by occupation length

For unrestricted occupation laws, choose $\ell_r=1$ for every class. Then
$$ \beta_r=1, \qquad G_r=1+\sum_{h=1}^{H-1}U_r(h). $$

Each summand is at most $1$, and all summands with $h\geq K$ vanish. Consequently,
$$ G_r\leq\min\{K,H\}. $$
The theorem gives
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] \geq \frac{1}{2\min\{K,H\}}\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]. $$

This supplies a guarantee controlled by occupation length rather than patience.

One can obtain the stronger instance-dependent guarantee
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] \geq \frac12 \left[ \min_r \max_{\substack{1\leq\ell\leq H\\ \min_aQ_{ar}(\ell-1)>0}} \frac{ \min_{a:(a,r)\in E}Q_{ar}(\ell-1) }{ 1+\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil H/\ell\rceil-2} U_r(1+j\ell) } \right] \mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}], $$
by optimizing each class’s block length before solving the reservation LP.

3.5 Occupation laws close to immediate return

Suppose, for some $\delta\in[0,1]$,
$$ q_{ar}(1)\geq1-\delta \qquad \text{for every eligible pair}. $$
For every $h\geq1$,
$$ Q_{ar}(h)\leq1-q_{ar}(1)\leq\delta. $$
Using $\ell_r=1$ therefore gives
$$ G_r\leq1+(\min\{K,H\}-1)\delta, $$
and hence
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] \geq \frac{1}{ 2\bigl(1+(\min\{K,H\}-1)\delta\bigr) } \mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]. $$

In particular, the requested factor holds whenever
$$ (\min\{K,H\}-1)\delta \leq \frac{2\varepsilon}{1-2\varepsilon}. $$
This follows by rearranging
$$ \frac{1}{2(1+x)}\geq\frac12-\varepsilon. $$

4. Polynomial construction and execution

Let
$$ M=\sum_{t=1}^{H}|I_t| $$
be the total number of explicitly listed arrival outcomes. Zero-probability outcomes can be deleted, and identical cohort vectors can be merged, so the observed vector identifies its outcome.

There are at most $mH$ bins and $\tau(D+1)$ groups per cohort. The reservation LP therefore has at most
$$ M\tau(D+1)mH $$
variables, with polynomially many constraints.

Occupation tails, their maxima and minima, and all admissible block-length ratios can be computed with polynomially many rational operations. Their bit lengths are polynomial: each is formed from polynomially many input rationals by sums, comparisons, products, or a quotient with a nonzero denominator.

The LP is rational, zero is feasible, and supply constraints bound every variable. The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore supplies an optimal rational solution of polynomial encoding length. The relevant input-size and solution-size facts are summarized in [MIT’s linear programming notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-854j-advanced-algorithms-fall-2005/d974aef980258f2a796171e96717d646_n11_lp.pdf).

Execution also has polynomial bit complexity. For each bin and possible current outcome, precompute
$$ \kappa_{v,t,i} = \frac{\sum_gb_{t,i,g,v}}{2C_v\alpha_{v,t}}. $$
These are polynomial-length rationals in $[0,1]$. The remaining virtual capacity obeys
$$ F_{v,t+1}=F_{v,t}(1-\kappa_{v,t,I_t}), $$
so it is a product of at most $H$ precomputed rational factors and its initial capacity. Its numerator and denominator consequently have polynomial bit length. Individual reservations, their scaled service amounts, and the polynomially many scheduled sums also have polynomial bit length.

All schedules use explicitly represented type–deadline groups. Neither construction nor execution enumerates arrival trajectories or individual mass units.

The resulting running time is polynomial in $m,\tau,H,D,K,L$. The policies do not use $\varepsilon$, so their running time also meets the requested polynomial dependence on $1/\varepsilon$.

5. What remains unresolved

The block theorem does not establish the requested guarantee for every instance. For example, with one class, deterministic occupation length $2$, and $H\geq3$, its best admissible block-length ratio is $\lambda_r=\frac12$. It therefore proves only a $\frac14$ guarantee, below $\frac12-\varepsilon$ when $\varepsilon<\frac14$.

This is a limitation of the proved construction, not an impossibility result.

The reservation method resolves the dependence issue by making virtual capacity depend only on earlier cohorts. Its remaining loss arises when reservations from different service blocks overlap in physical occupation. The proof bounds that overlap by $G_r$; it does not show that this loss is necessary.

The cited [Dong et al. theorem](https://chienjuho.com/pub/AAMAS21_ScarceMatching.pdf) uses a discrete model with one sampled agent per round. Its application to the present correlated, divisible cohorts would require an additional reduction or argument, which is not supplied here.

For context, a universal factor strictly exceeding $\frac12$ is impossible. The following proof restates the barrier independently of the notes.

Take one resource of capacity $1$, $H=K=2$, and $D=0$. Both types have occupation length $2$. Round one brings mass $1$ with utility $1$. Independently, round two brings mass $1$ with utility $1/p$ with probability $p$, and otherwise no mass, where $p=2^{-b}$ and $b\geq1$.

For any causal policy, let $X\in[0,1]$ be its first-round allocation. No part returns before round two. Independence gives
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] \leq \mathbb E[X] + p\frac1p\mathbb E[1-X] =1. $$
The offline optimum serves the second cohort whenever it arrives, and otherwise serves the first. Thus
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]=2-p, $$
and every causal policy has ratio at most
$$ \frac{1}{2-p}. $$
As $b$ increases, this approaches $\frac12$. This rules out every universal constant above $\frac12$, while leaving the requested $\frac12-\varepsilon$ guarantee unresolved.
