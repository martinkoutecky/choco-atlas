The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite role graph \(G_T=(T,E)\), rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), edge values \(w\), a fixed matching \(M\) with \(\mu_t=\mu_u\) for each \(tu\in M\), and an initial per-capita allocation \(x^0\), can one compute an allocation \(y\) such that every matched pair's clipped balancing update changes either endpoint by at most \(\varepsilon\), and what is the worst-case update complexity in \(|T|\), \(|E|\), the input bit length, and \(\varepsilon\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input includes a finite simple undirected role graph \(G_T=(T,E)\), with \(m=|E|\), positive integer edge values \(w_{tu}\), and an arbitrary fixed matching \(M\) satisfying \(\mu_t=\mu_u\) for every \(tu\in M\).
All members of a type receive the same per-capita allocation. Matching \(tu\) pairs the entire masses of those types; its value is per pair. A valid allocation satisfies \(x_t\geq0\), \(x_t+x_u=w_{tu}\) on \(M\), and \(x_t=0\) for unmatched types.
Given a valid binary rational initial allocation \(x^0\) and binary rational tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\), define \(\alpha_t(x)=\max(\{0\}\cup\{w_{tv}-x_v:tv\in E\setminus M\})\).
For \(tu\in M\), the clipped balancing update \(B_{tu}\) replaces \(x_t\) by \(\min\{w_{tu},\max\{0,(w_{tu}+\alpha_t(x)-\alpha_u(x))/2\}\}\) and \(x_u\) by \(w_{tu}-(B_{tu}x)_t\), leaving other coordinates unchanged. This rule applies even when \(w_{tu}-\alpha_t(x)-\alpha_u(x)<0\).
Seek a valid rational allocation \(y\) with \(\|B_{tu}y-y\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon\) for every \(tu\in M\). For update complexity, start at \(x^0\), repeatedly choose any edge violating this inequality and apply its full update, stopping when none violates it.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length and \(W=\max(\{1\}\cup\{w_e:e\in E\})\); measure update counts over all permitted edge choices and running times in \(\tau,m,L\) and the stated accuracy parameters.

Is computing such an allocation \(y\) polynomial-time solvable in \(\tau,m,L\), or NP-hard?
Is the worst-case update count polynomial in \(\tau,m,L,\max\{1,W/\varepsilon\}\), or can it be superpolynomial in these quantities?

For finite-agent networks, arbitrary sequential clipped updates converge to a fixed point under non-starvation; a thresholded variant has an exponential upper bound \(O((W/\varepsilon)2^{|V|+|E|})\). [Azar et al.](https://www.nikhildevanur.com/pubs/edgebalancing.pdf)

Proven results and remaining question

The allocation-computation problem is polynomial-time solvable in $\tau,m,L$: an exact rational fixed point can be computed by a polynomial-size linear program.

For arbitrary sequential full updates, I prove the uniform bound
$$ O\!\left(2^{\tau+m}\max\{1,W/\varepsilon\}\right). $$
I do not settle whether the general worst-case update count is polynomial or can be superpolynomial.

The additional progress is two polynomial update bounds:

  • On a bipartite role graph, every permitted trajectory starting from a subsolution or supersolution is monotone and takes fewer than $|M|W/\varepsilon$ updates. This includes the two extreme allocations.
  • For equal-value paths and cycles, every permitted trajectory, from any valid initial allocation, takes fewer than $|M|(W/\varepsilon)^2$ updates. A more general sufficient condition is proved below.

All arguments are given here independently of the notes. If $M=\varnothing$, validity forces the zero allocation and no updates occur; henceforth the nontrivial case is $M\neq\varnothing$.

1. Feasible allocations and the change produced by an update

Write $k=|M|$ and $N=\tau+m$. The feasible set is
$$ P=\left\{ x\in\mathbb R^\tau: x_t\geq0,\quad x_t+x_u=w_{tu}\ \text{for }tu\in M,\quad x_t=0\ \text{for unmatched }t \right\}. $$

Because $M$ is a matching, equal splitting on each matched edge gives a feasible allocation. Every matched coordinate is bounded by its matched-edge value, so
$$ 0\leq x_t\leq W \qquad(x\in P). $$
Thus $P$ is a nonempty compact rational polytope. Every balancing update preserves $P$.

For a matched vertex $t$, define
$$ r_t(x)=x_t-\alpha_t(x). $$
The definition of the outside option gives the useful identity
$$ r_t(x)= \min\left( \{x_t\}\cup \{x_t+x_v-w_{tv}:tv\in E\setminus M\} \right). $$

Fix $tu\in M$. The outside options $\alpha_t$ and $\alpha_u$ do not change when only $x_t,x_u$ change: the graph is simple, and the edge $tu$ is excluded from their outside options. The unconstrained balancing target for $x_t$ therefore differs from its current value by
$$ \frac{w_{tu}+\alpha_t(x)-\alpha_u(x)}2-x_t = \frac{r_u(x)-r_t(x)}2. $$

Suppose the clipped update increases $x_t$ by $d>0$ and decreases $x_u$ by $d$. Clipping can only shorten the positive unconstrained displacement, so
$$ r_u(x)-r_t(x)\geq2d. $$
Also,
$$ \|B_{tu}x-x\|_\infty=d. $$

2. A self-contained potential inequality

Introduce a multiset of $N$ affine functions, retaining repeated values:
$$ \{\ell_j(x):1\leq j\leq N\} = \{x_t:t\in T\} \cup \{x_t+x_u-w_{tu}:tu\in E\}. $$
Sort their values as
$$ s_1(x)\leq\cdots\leq s_N(x), $$
and define
$$ \Phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^N2^{-i}s_i(x). $$

Lemma. Every balancing update satisfies
$$ \Phi(B_{tu}x)-\Phi(x) \geq 2^{-N}\|B_{tu}x-x\|_\infty. $$

Proof. A zero update needs no argument. Otherwise, exchange the names $t,u$ if necessary so that $x_t$ increases by $d>0$.

Parameterize the segment joining the two allocations by
$$ x(h)=x+h(e_t-e_u), \qquad 0\leq h\leq d, $$
where $e_t$ is the coordinate unit vector at $t$.

The changing slack functions divide into two disjoint groups:

  • The increasing group consists of $x_t$ and the nonmatching edge slacks incident to $t$; each increases at rate $1$.
  • The decreasing group consists of $x_u$ and the nonmatching edge slacks incident to $u$; each decreases at rate $1$.

These groups are disjoint because the only edge joining $t$ and $u$ is their matched edge, whose slack stays zero. All remaining functions are constant.

Put $a=r_t(x)$ and $b=r_u(x)$. By the identity in Section 1, the minimum values in the increasing and decreasing groups at parameter $h$ are, respectively,
$$ a+h,\qquad b-h. $$
Since $b-a\geq2d$, for every $0\leq h<d$,
$$ a+h<b-h. $$

There are only finitely many crossing parameters between nonidentical affine functions. On each intervening open interval, the sorted potential is affine. Let $q$ be the first rank occupied by an increasing function. No decreasing function precedes rank $q$, because the minimum increasing value is strictly below every decreasing value. Hence, on that interval,
$$ \frac{d}{dh}\Phi(x(h)) \geq 2^{-q}-\sum_{i=q+1}^N2^{-i} = 2^{-N}. $$
Additional increasing functions only improve this lower bound. Functions that coincide throughout an interval have equal slopes, so their ordering causes no difficulty.

The sorted potential is continuous and piecewise affine. Integrating the displayed derivative bound gives
$$ \Phi(x(d))-\Phi(x(0))\geq2^{-N}d, $$
which proves the lemma. $\square$

This is the sorted-slack potential used in the cited convergence analysis; that paper also obtains an exponential thresholded-update bound. [Azar et al., Section 4](https://www.nikhildevanur.com/pubs/edgebalancing.pdf)

3. Polynomial-time computation of an exact rational fixed point

For $1\leq q\leq N$, put
$$ S_q(x)=\sum_{i=1}^q s_i(x). $$
The sum of the $q$ smallest values has the representation
$$ S_q(x)= \max_{a\in\mathbb R} \left( qa-\sum_{j=1}^N\max\{a-\ell_j(x),0\} \right). $$

Indeed, for any $a$,
$$ \sum_{j=1}^N\max\{a-s_j(x),0\} \geq \sum_{j=1}^q(a-s_j(x)), $$
which proves that the expression is at most $S_q(x)$. Equality holds at $a=s_q(x)$, including when values are tied.

The potential can be written as
$$ \Phi(x)= 2^{-N}S_N(x) + \sum_{q=1}^{N-1}2^{-(q+1)}S_q(x). $$
To check this, the coefficient of $s_i(x)$ on the right is
$$ 2^{-N}+\sum_{q=i}^{N-1}2^{-(q+1)}=2^{-i}. $$

Every slack lies in $[-W,2W]$. Consider the linear program with variables $x$, $a_q$, and $z_{qj}$:
$$ \text{maximize}\quad 2^{-N}\sum_{j=1}^N\ell_j(x) + \sum_{q=1}^{N-1}2^{-(q+1)} \left(qa_q-\sum_{j=1}^Nz_{qj}\right), $$
subject to
$$ x\in P, $$
$$ -W\leq a_q\leq2W \qquad(1\leq q<N), $$
and
$$ 0\leq z_{qj}\leq3W,\qquad z_{qj}\geq a_q-\ell_j(x) \qquad(1\leq q<N,\ 1\leq j\leq N). $$

For a fixed $x$, any feasible auxiliary variables give an objective at most $\Phi(x)$, by the preceding representation of $S_q$. Equality is attained simultaneously for all $q$ by choosing
$$ a_q=s_q(x),\qquad z_{qj}=\max\{s_q(x)-\ell_j(x),0\}. $$
These choices satisfy the bounds because all slacks lie in $[-W,2W]$. Consequently, the allocation component of every optimal LP solution maximizes $\Phi$ over $P$.

The LP has $O(\tau+N^2)$ variables and constraints. All coefficients have polynomial binary encoding length: the coefficients $2^{-q}$ require $O(N)$ bits, and $W$ has encoding length bounded by the input length. Its feasible region is nonempty and bounded.

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore computes an exact rational optimizer of polynomial encoding length in polynomial time.

Let $y$ be its allocation component. If some matched-edge update changed $y$, the lemma would imply
$$ \Phi(B_{tu}y)>\Phi(y). $$
But $B_{tu}y\in P$, contradicting maximality. Thus
$$ B_{tu}y=y \qquad\text{for every }tu\in M. $$

This proves polynomial-time solvability of the requested allocation problem, independently of $x^0$ and without dependence on $W/\varepsilon$.

4. General sequential-update bound

Every permitted update has displacement strictly greater than $\varepsilon$. By the lemma, it increases $\Phi$ by strictly more than $2^{-N}\varepsilon$.

Because every slack lies in $[-W,2W]$,
$$ -W(1-2^{-N}) \leq\Phi(x)\leq 2W(1-2^{-N}). $$
The entire possible range of the potential is therefore less than $3W$.

If $K>0$ permitted updates occur, telescoping gives
$$ K\,2^{-N}\varepsilon < \Phi(x^K)-\Phi(x^0) < 3W. $$
Hence
$$ K<3\,2^{\tau+m}\frac{W}{\varepsilon}. $$

This bounds every finite prefix, and therefore excludes an infinite permitted trajectory. It holds for every permitted edge-choice sequence, without a fairness assumption. It proves an exponential upper bound, but neither alternative in the requested general classification.

5. Polynomial bound for monotone bipartite trajectories

Assume the role graph is bipartite, with parts $A,B$. Index the matched edges as
$$ a_i b_i\in M,\qquad a_i\in A,\quad b_i\in B, $$
and write
$$ c_i=w_{a_i b_i},\qquad z_i=x_{a_i},\qquad x_{b_i}=c_i-z_i. $$
Thus the feasible coordinates satisfy $0\leq z_i\leq c_i$.

Let $F_i(z)$ be the balancing target for $z_i$:
$$ F_i(z)= \min\left\{c_i,\max\left\{0, \frac{c_i+\alpha_{a_i}(z)-\alpha_{b_i}(z)}2 \right\}\right\}. $$

Every term in $\alpha_{a_i}$ arising from a matched neighbor $b_j$ has the form
$$ w_{a_i b_j}-c_j+z_j, $$
and terms arising from unmatched neighbors are constant. Therefore $\alpha_{a_i}$ is coordinatewise nondecreasing.

Similarly, the nonconstant terms in $\alpha_{b_i}$ have the form
$$ w_{b_i a_j}-z_j, $$
so $\alpha_{b_i}$ is coordinatewise nonincreasing. It follows that every $F_i$ is coordinatewise nondecreasing.

Call $z$ a subsolution if
$$ z_i\leq F_i(z)\qquad\text{for every }i. $$

Suppose $z$ is a subsolution and coordinate $i$ is updated, producing $z'$. Then $z'\geq z$. For $j\neq i$, monotonicity gives
$$ F_j(z')\geq F_j(z)\geq z_j=z'_j. $$
For $j=i$,
$$ F_i(z')\geq F_i(z)=z'_i. $$
Thus $z'$ remains a subsolution. By induction, every subsequent update increases its chosen coordinate.

Each permitted update increases $\sum_i z_i$ by more than $\varepsilon$, while its total possible increase is at most $\sum_i c_i$. Consequently, whenever $K>0$,
$$ K<\frac{\sum_i c_i}{\varepsilon} \leq\frac{kW}{\varepsilon}. $$

The reversed argument applies to a supersolution, defined by $z_i\geq F_i(z)$: every update decreases its coordinate and the same bound holds.

In particular, $z=0$ is always a subsolution, and $z_i=c_i$ for all $i$ is always a supersolution. Thus both extreme allocations have this polynomial update bound for every permitted schedule.

Limitation. An arbitrary valid initial allocation need not be a subsolution or a supersolution. This argument does not bound arbitrary bipartite trajectories.

6. Polynomial bound from a quadratic energy

Here is a different condition allowing an arbitrary valid initial allocation.

Assume:

  • Every matched vertex has at most one incident edge in $E\setminus M$.
  • For every nonmatching edge joining two matched vertices $t,v$,

$$ w_{tv}\geq\max\{c_t,c_v\}, $$
where $c_t$ denotes the value of the matched edge incident to $t$.

These conditions hold when the role graph has maximum degree at most $2$ and all edge values are equal. They therefore cover equal-value paths and cycles with arbitrary matchings.

Under these assumptions, a matched vertex $t$ with an outside neighbor $v$ satisfies
$$ \alpha_t(x)=w_{tv}-x_v. $$
For matched $v$, this follows from $x_v\leq c_v\leq w_{tv}$; for unmatched $v$, it follows from $x_v=0$. A matched vertex without an outside neighbor has $\alpha_t=0$.

Let $D$ be the set of matched vertices without an outside neighbor. Let $H$ be the set of nonmatching edges with at least one matched endpoint. Define
$$ Q(x)= \frac12\sum_{t\in D}x_t^2 + \frac12\sum_{tv\in H}(x_t+x_v-w_{tv})^2. $$

Every matched vertex participates in exactly one term of this energy. For a matched edge $tu$, its two endpoints participate in distinct terms, because $tu\notin H$.

Consider a feasible displacement $x+h(e_t-e_u)$. The term involving $t$ has its unsquared expression increased by $h$; that expression initially equals $r_t(x)$. The corresponding expression involving $u$ initially equals $r_u(x)$ and decreases by $h$. Hence
$$ Q(x+h(e_t-e_u)) = Q(x)+(r_t(x)-r_u(x))h+h^2. $$
The feasible interval is
$$ -x_t\leq h\leq x_u. $$
The unconstrained minimizer is $(r_u-r_t)/2$. By Section 1, clipping this minimizer to the feasible interval gives exactly the stipulated balancing update.

Let $d$ denote its signed displacement. Since $d$ minimizes this convex quadratic on an interval containing $0$, its first-order optimality condition gives
$$ \bigl(r_t-r_u+2d\bigr)(-d)\geq0. $$
Therefore
$$ Q(x)-Q(B_{tu}x) = -(r_t-r_u)d-d^2 \geq d^2. $$
Every permitted update consequently decreases $Q$ by more than $\varepsilon^2$.

It remains to bound $Q$. Each vertex expression is in $[0,W]$. Each edge expression in $H$ has absolute value at most $W$:

  • If both endpoints are matched, $0\leq x_t+x_v\leq2w_{tv}$ by the weight assumption, so the expression lies in $[-w_{tv},w_{tv}]$.
  • If one endpoint is unmatched, the expression is $x_t-w_{tv}$, with both quantities in $[0,W]$.

There are at most $2k$ energy terms: every term contains at least one matched vertex, and distinct terms contain disjoint sets of matched vertices. Thus
$$ 0\leq Q(x)\leq kW^2. $$

Telescoping over $K>0$ permitted updates now gives
$$ K\varepsilon^2<Q(x^0)\leq kW^2, $$
and therefore
$$ K<k\left(\frac W\varepsilon\right)^2. $$

This bound holds for every permitted schedule and every valid initial allocation under the two stated structural assumptions.

7. Why the quadratic argument does not extend directly

The same energy can increase when a matched vertex has several outside edges.

For an explicit valid example, take eight types
$$ t,u,v_1,v_1',v_2,v_2',q,q' $$
with mass $1/8$ each. All edge values are $1$. Let
$$ M=\{tu,v_1v_1',v_2v_2',qq'\}, $$
and let the nonmatching edges be
$$ tv_1,\quad tv_2,\quad uq. $$
Choose the valid initial allocation
$$ (x_t,x_u)=\left(\frac12,\frac12\right), $$
$$ (x_{v_1},x_{v_1'})=\left(\frac9{10},\frac1{10}\right), \qquad (x_{v_2},x_{v_2'})=\left(\frac1{10},\frac9{10}\right), $$
$$ (x_q,x_{q'})=\left(\frac12,\frac12\right). $$

The outside options are
$$ \alpha_t=\frac9{10},\qquad \alpha_u=\frac12, $$
so updating $tu$ increases $x_t$ by $1/5$. The three nonmatching edge slacks change from
$$ \left(\frac25,-\frac25,0\right) \quad\text{to}\quad \left(\frac35,-\frac15,-\frac15\right). $$
All vertex terms in the proposed energy remain unchanged. Its change is therefore
$$ \frac12\left[ \left(\frac35\right)^2+ \left(-\frac15\right)^2+ \left(-\frac15\right)^2- \left(\frac25\right)^2- \left(-\frac25\right)^2 \right] = \frac3{50}>0. $$

Thus this quadratic energy is not a general decreasing potential. The example is an obstruction to that proof method, not a superpolynomial lower bound.

8. Masses and the unresolved general classification

The masses do not enter the outside options, updates, or stopping condition. Their matching-endpoint equality condition ensures admissibility of the society representation but does not alter a valid trajectory.

Conversely, any finite-vertex instance in the stipulated integer-weight class can be represented by one type per vertex with uniform mass. If masses are required to be dyadic, pad with isolated unmatched vertices to the next power-of-two size and use uniform dyadic masses. The padding introduces fewer than twice as many vertices and changes no outside option or trajectory on the original vertices.

Therefore the type formulation retains the general finite-graph update-count problem. The LP settles allocation computation, and the two additional arguments settle substantial restricted classes of sequential trajectories. A polynomial upper bound or a superpolynomial lower-bound family for arbitrary graphs, initial allocations, and permitted schedules remains unproved here.
