The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of complete agent types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), finite type-level bilateral contract classes \(Q\) with endpoints \(b(q),s(q)\in\Theta\), bundle families \(\mathcal B_\theta\), and fully substitutable type valuations \(u_\theta\) with dealer valuations represented by compatible-pair matching costs, compute \((\lambda,p)\) such that \(\sum_{B\in\mathcal B_\theta}\lambda_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{B\ni q}\lambda_{b(q),B}=\sum_{B\ni q}\lambda_{s(q),B}\) for every \(q\), and \(\lambda_{\theta,B}>0\Rightarrow B\in\arg\max_{B'\in\mathcal B_\theta}U_\theta(B';p)\), where \(U_\theta(B;p)=u_\theta(B)+\sum_{q\in B:s(q)=\theta}p_q-\sum_{q\in B:b(q)=\theta}p_q\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(\Theta\) be a finite set of agent types with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), and let \(Q\) be finite bilateral contract classes with distinct buyer and seller types \(b(q),s(q)\). A type may buy some classes and sell others. Each type has feasible bundles \(\mathcal B_\theta\) of its incident classes and rational valuations \(u_\theta(B)\). For a dealer type, feasible bundles pair every purchase with a compatible sale, and \(u_\theta(B)\) is the negative minimum total cost of such a pairing; compatible pairs and their rational costs are given. Other feasible bundles and valuations are listed explicitly.
At prices \(p\), define \(U_\theta(B;p)=u_\theta(B)+\sum_{q\in B:s(q)=\theta}p_q-\sum_{q\in B:b(q)=\theta}p_q\) and \(V_\theta(p)=\max_{B\in\mathcal B_\theta}U_\theta(B;p)\). Assume full substitutability: \(V_\theta(p\wedge p')+V_\theta(p\vee p')\leq V_\theta(p)+V_\theta(p')\) for all price vectors \(p,p'\).
A mass equilibrium has \(\lambda_{\theta,B}\geq0\), \(\sum_B\lambda_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{B\ni q}\lambda_{b(q),B}=\sum_{B\ni q}\lambda_{s(q),B}\) for every \(q\), and \(\lambda_{\theta,B}>0\) only if \(B\) maximizes \(U_\theta(\cdot;p)\).

Is computing a mass equilibrium \((\lambda,p)\) in P or NP-hard under this input representation?

For indivisible agents, full substitutability guarantees a competitive equilibrium by a constructive existence result [Hatfield et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1086/673402). A later network-flow formulation gives a strongly polynomial equilibrium algorithm for that discrete model [Candogan, Epitropou, and Vohra](https://irihs.ihs.ac.at/id/eprint/4027/1/es-323.pdf). Neither result establishes a complexity bound for the mass equilibrium specified above.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable: one can compute a mass equilibrium, represented by prices and a polynomial-size list of positive bundle masses, or determine that no mass equilibrium exists. If every type admits the empty bundle, an equilibrium always exists.

The proof uses a polynomial-size rational linear program and a constructive decomposition of fractional bipartite matchings. Full substitutability is not needed for this mass-equilibrium result.

For dealers, the pairing representation means that feasible bundles are precisely the endpoint sets of matchings of compatible purchase–sale pairs.

1. A necessary feasibility qualification

As written, the assumptions do not guarantee existence, because they do not explicitly allow every type to choose the empty bundle.

For example, take one class \(q\), buyer and seller masses both equal to \(1\), buyer feasible family \(\{\{q\}\}\), and seller feasible family \(\{\varnothing\}\). Give both feasible bundles valuation \(0\). The indirect utilities are \(V_b(p)=-p_q\) and \(V_s(p)=0\); both satisfy the stated substitutability inequality with equality. Nevertheless, market clearing is impossible.

Thus the precise algorithmic statement is “compute an equilibrium or report nonexistence.” Under the usual empty-bundle assumption, the latter outcome cannot occur.

2. A polynomial-size welfare formulation

Partition the types into explicitly represented types \(N\) and dealers \(D\). For an incident class, define the signed incidence

\(d_{\theta q}(B)=\mathbf 1_{\{q\in B,\ s(q)=\theta\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{q\in B,\ b(q)=\theta\}}.\)

Then \(U_\theta(B;p)=u_\theta(B)+\sum_q p_qd_{\theta q}(B)\).

For dealer \(\theta\), let \(I_\theta\) be its purchase classes, \(O_\theta\) its sale classes, and \(E_\theta\subseteq I_\theta\times O_\theta\) its compatible pairs. Write \(c_{\theta ij}\) for the cost of pair \((i,j)\). A compatible matching uses each incident class at most once.

Introduce the following nonnegative variables:

  • \(x_{\theta B}\) for each listed bundle of each \(\theta\in N\);
  • \(y_{\theta ij}\) for each \((i,j)\in E_\theta\).

Consider the linear program with objective

\(\displaystyle \max\ \sum_{\theta\in N}\sum_{B\in\mathcal B_\theta}u_\theta(B)x_{\theta B}-\sum_{\theta\in D}\sum_{(i,j)\in E_\theta}c_{\theta ij}y_{\theta ij}.\)

Its constraints are:

Type masses: for every \(\theta\in N\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{B\in\mathcal B_\theta}x_{\theta B}=\mu_\theta.\)

Dealer capacities: for every \(\theta\in D\) and every \(v\in I_\theta\cup O_\theta\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{e\in E_\theta:\ v\in e}y_{\theta e}\leq\mu_\theta.\)

Market clearing: for every \(q\in Q\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\theta\in N}\sum_{B\in\mathcal B_\theta}d_{\theta q}(B)x_{\theta B}+\sum_{\theta\in D}\sum_{(i,j)\in E_\theta}\bigl(\mathbf 1_{\{j=q\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{i=q\}}\bigr)y_{\theta ij}=0.\)

Call this program \(P\).

Its numbers of variables and constraints are polynomial in the input length. Its coefficients are rational input data or signed incidence coefficients. Every variable is bounded by its type’s mass, so a feasible \(P\) has an attained, finite optimum.

The dealer capacities are sufficient because of the following constructive fact.

3. Dealer decomposition lemma

Lemma. Let \(G=(I,O,E)\) be a bipartite graph, let \(\mu\geq0\), and suppose \(y_e\geq0\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{e\ni v}y_e\leq\mu\quad\text{for every }v\in I\cup O.\)

There is a polynomial-time decomposition

\(\displaystyle y=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\delta_k\mathbf 1_{M_k},\qquad \delta_k>0,\qquad \sum_{k=1}^{K}\delta_k=\mu,\)

where each \(M_k\) is a matching, possibly empty, and \(K\) is polynomial in the graph size. When \(\mu=0\), the empty list suffices.

Proof. If \(\mu=0\), all entries of \(y\) vanish. If there are no vertices and \(\mu>0\), assign mass \(\mu\) to the empty matching. Otherwise, write \(m=|I|\), \(n=|O|\), and let \(Y\) be the nonnegative \(m\)-by-\(n\) matrix whose compatible entries are \(y_e\) and whose other entries are zero.

Let \(r\) and \(c\) be its row-sum and column-sum vectors. Form the square matrix

\(\displaystyle Z=\begin{pmatrix}Y&\operatorname{diag}(\mu\mathbf 1-r)\\ \operatorname{diag}(\mu\mathbf 1-c)&Y^{\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix}.\)

Every row sum and every column sum of \(Z\) equals \(\mu\).

More generally, suppose a nonnegative square residual matrix has all row and column sums equal to \(t>0\). Its positive-entry support has a perfect matching: for any set \(S\) of rows, all their mass lies in their neighboring columns \(N(S)\), so

\(\displaystyle t|S|\leq t|N(S)|.\)

Thus Hall’s condition holds. Find such a perfect matching, let \(\delta\) be the smallest matrix entry on it, and subtract \(\delta\) from every matching entry. The residual matrix is nonnegative and has all row and column sums equal to \(t-\delta\).

Each iteration eliminates at least one positive entry and never creates a new one. Consequently, at most \((m+n)^2\) iterations are required. The subtracted weights sum to \(\mu\).

Restricting each chosen perfect matching to the upper-left block gives a matching in \(G\). These restrictions, with the same weights, give the claimed decomposition of \(y\). Empty restrictions are allowed.

Each perfect matching is computable in polynomial time. All arithmetic consists of comparisons and subtractions of rational entries. A common denominator for the initial entries has polynomial bit length, and remains a common denominator throughout, so the arithmetic also has polynomial bit complexity. \(\square\)

Apply this lemma separately to each dealer solution of \(P\). For a matching \(M_k\), define its bundle \(B_k\) to be its endpoint set and assign mass \(\delta_k\) to \(B_k\), merging repeated bundles.

The resulting bundle masses have the correct type totals and incident-class marginals. Together with \(\lambda_{\theta B}=x_{\theta B}\) for explicitly represented types, they therefore give a feasible mass allocation.

Conversely, any feasible mass allocation yields a feasible solution of \(P\): for every positive dealer bundle, select a minimum-cost compatible pairing and aggregate its pair incidences using the bundle’s mass. Such a pairing exists by dealer feasibility.

Hence \(P\) is feasible exactly when a feasible mass allocation exists.

4. The dual provides supporting prices

For each explicitly represented type introduce a free dual variable \(z_\theta\). For each dealer and incident class introduce a nonnegative variable \(a_{\theta v}\). Use free variables \(-p_q\) for the market-clearing equalities.

The dual of \(P\) is

\(\displaystyle \min\ \sum_{\theta\in N}\mu_\theta z_\theta+\sum_{\theta\in D}\mu_\theta\sum_{v\in I_\theta\cup O_\theta}a_{\theta v},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle z_\theta\geq u_\theta(B)+\sum_qp_qd_{\theta q}(B)\quad(\theta\in N,\ B\in\mathcal B_\theta),\)

and

\(\displaystyle a_{\theta i}+a_{\theta j}\geq-c_{\theta ij}+p_j-p_i\quad(\theta\in D,\ (i,j)\in E_\theta),\)

with \(a_{\theta v}\geq0\).

The signs follow directly from the primal balance column: a dealer pair has coefficient \(-1\) at its purchase class and \(+1\) at its sale class.

Define

\(\displaystyle h_\theta=z_\theta\quad(\theta\in N),\qquad h_\theta=\sum_{v\in I_\theta\cup O_\theta}a_{\theta v}\quad(\theta\in D).\)

Claim. Every dual-feasible solution satisfies \(U_\theta(B;p)\leq h_\theta\) for every feasible bundle.

For explicitly represented types, this is a dual constraint.

For a dealer, let \(M\) be a minimum-cost pairing of \(B\). Since \(M\) covers every class of \(B\) exactly once,

\(\displaystyle U_\theta(B;p)=\sum_{(i,j)\in M}(-c_{\theta ij}+p_j-p_i).\)

The pair constraints and nonnegativity of \(a\) give

\(\displaystyle U_\theta(B;p)\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in M}(a_{\theta i}+a_{\theta j})=\sum_{v\in B}a_{\theta v}\leq h_\theta.\)

This proves the claim.

5. Optimal solutions yield an equilibrium

Assume \(P\) is feasible. Choose optimal primal and dual solutions. They exist, and their objective values agree by finite-dimensional linear-programming strong duality. Denote the common value by \(W^*\).

Decompose each optimal dealer vector as in Section 3, obtaining feasible bundle masses \(\lambda\). Let their actual welfare be

\(\displaystyle W(\lambda)=\sum_\theta\sum_{B\in\mathcal B_\theta}\lambda_{\theta B}u_\theta(B).\)

For any matching \(M\) with endpoint bundle \(B\),

\(\displaystyle u_\theta(B)=-\min_{\text{pairings }M'\text{ of }B}\sum_{e\in M'}c_{\theta e}\geq-\sum_{e\in M}c_{\theta e}.\)

Consequently, decomposition cannot decrease the objective:

\(\displaystyle W(\lambda)\geq W^*.\)

On the other hand, market clearing cancels total price transfers, so

\(\displaystyle W(\lambda)=\sum_{\theta,B}\lambda_{\theta B}U_\theta(B;p).\)

Using the dual utility bounds and the type-mass equalities gives

\(\displaystyle W(\lambda)\leq\sum_{\theta,B}\lambda_{\theta B}h_\theta=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta h_\theta=W^*.\)

Therefore all these inequalities are equalities. In particular,

\(\displaystyle 0=\sum_{\theta,B}\lambda_{\theta B}\bigl(h_\theta-U_\theta(B;p)\bigr).\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Thus whenever \(\lambda_{\theta B}>0\),

\(\displaystyle U_\theta(B;p)=h_\theta.\)

Since every feasible bundle has utility at most \(h_\theta\), every positive-mass bundle maximizes utility. The constructed allocation already satisfies type masses and market clearing. Hence \((\lambda,p)\) is a mass equilibrium.

This argument also proves existence whenever a feasible mass allocation exists, independently of substitutability.

6. Complexity and output size

The algorithm is:

  • Construct \(P\) and its dual.
  • Solve these polynomial-size rational linear programs exactly, or report primal infeasibility.
  • Decompose each dealer vector into matching masses.
  • Convert matchings to endpoint bundles and output the positive masses together with \(p\).

Rational linear programming admits polynomial-time algorithms in the binary encoding length; see, for example, the [MIT notes on the ellipsoid algorithm](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-854j-advanced-algorithms-fall-2008/c61e649bf05c7123da984519955da0bd_lec9.pdf). Here the programs have polynomial encoding length, and optimal rational solutions of polynomial bit length can be selected.

For dealer \(\theta\), the decomposition produces at most \((|I_\theta|+|O_\theta|)^2\) matchings, apart from the trivial empty-graph case. Explicitly represented types already have polynomially many listed bundles. Thus the entire equilibrium has a polynomial-size sparse representation; every unlisted bundle mass is zero.

No replication by a common denominator of the masses is used. The bound is polynomial in their binary encoding length, rather than in the corresponding number of hypothetical indivisible agents.

Accordingly, the requested computational problem is in \(\mathrm P\), with infeasibility detection if empty-bundle feasibility is not assumed. This establishes a polynomial-time bound, without asserting a strongly polynomial bound.

Every step in the welfare comparison is justified.
