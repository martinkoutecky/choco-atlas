The continuous mirror question, as graded

1. Result and explicit allocation path

All the limits exist. The limiting path has at most one breakpoint. Evaluating it to the requested precision is \(\#\mathrm{P}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions, and hence \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard in that sense.

Define the coordinate means and their maximum by

\(\displaystyle m_k=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t r_k(t),\qquad M=\max_{1\leq k\leq d}m_k.\)

If \(M=0\), then every reference is zero, and

\(\displaystyle a_t(e)=e\qquad(t\in T).\)

Suppose henceforth that \(M>0\). Let \(K\) contain the coordinates with mean \(M\), retaining just one representative of each distinct coordinate profile \((r_k(t))_{t\in T}\). Thus, identical coordinate profiles are merged.

Let \((G_t)_{t\in T}\) be independent standard normal random variables. For \(k\in K\), define

\(\displaystyle Z_k=\sum_{t\in T}\sqrt{\mu_t}\,r_k(t)G_t,\)

and set

\(\displaystyle p_k=\mathbb P\bigl(Z_k>Z_\ell\text{ for every }\ell\in K\setminus\{k\}\bigr),\qquad b_t=\sum_{k\in K}p_k r_k(t).\)

The maximizer is unique almost surely: for distinct \(k,\ell\in K\),

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Var}(Z_k-Z_\ell)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\bigl(r_k(t)-r_\ell(t)\bigr)^2>0.\)

Consequently, \(\sum_{k\in K}p_k=1\).

The limiting per-agent awards are

\(\displaystyle \boxed{a_t(e)=\min\{e/M,1\}\,b_t+(e-M)_+.}\)

Here \(x_+=\max\{x,0\}\). The type allocation requested in the question is \(\mu_t a_t(e)\).

2. An exact finite-replication decomposition

Fix an admissible \(q\), write \(E=qe\), and define

\(\displaystyle F_q(S)=\min\{h_q(S),E\}.\)

Because all references are nonnegative, \(h_q\) is monotone. For an agent \(i\) with predecessors \(P_i\), the ordinary capped marginal is

\(\displaystyle F_q(P_i\cup\{i\})-F_q(P_i)=\min\{h_q(P_i\cup\{i\})-h_q(P_i),(E-h_q(P_i))_+\}.\)

This is the specified award whenever \(i\) is not last.

If \(i\) is last, then \(P_i\cup\{i\}=N_q\). Its actual award is \(E-F_q(P_i)\), so its award exceeds the capped marginal by

\(\displaystyle E-F_q(N_q)=(E-h_q(N_q))_+.\)

The complete society has

\(\displaystyle h_q(N_q)=\max_k\sum_{t\in T}q\mu_t r_k(t)=qM.\)

Each agent is last with probability \(1/q\). Therefore, for a tagged agent \(i\) of type \(t\),

\[\displaystyle a_t^{(q)}(e)=\mathbb E\bigl[F_q(P_i\cup\{i\})-F_q(P_i)\bigr]+(e-M)_+.\tag{1}\]

This identity holds for every admissible \(q\). In particular, it accounts exactly for the possibly large award of the last agent.

Let \(R_t=\max_k r_k(t)\). For every predecessor set,

\(\displaystyle 0\leq h_q(P_i\cup\{i\})-h_q(P_i)\leq R_t.\)

Thus the capped marginal in (1) also lies between \(0\) and \(R_t\), independently of \(q\).

3. Proof of the replication limit

3.1. Representing a random permutation

Assign independent uniform arrival times in \((0,1)\) to all agents and order them by arrival time. This produces a uniformly random permutation.

Condition on the tagged agent having arrival time \(u\in(0,1)\). For each type \(s\), its number \(C_{q,s}\) of predecessors has distribution

\(\displaystyle C_{q,s}\sim\operatorname{Bin}\bigl(q\mu_s-\mathbf 1_{\{s=t\}},u\bigr).\)

These counts are independent across types.

Define the predecessor sum in coordinate \(k\) by

\(\displaystyle H_{q,k}=\sum_{s\in T}r_k(s)C_{q,s}.\)

Its conditional mean is \(qum_k-u r_k(t)\), and its variance is of order \(q\). Chebyshev’s inequality, applied to the finitely many coordinates, gives

\[\displaystyle H_{q,k}/q\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}um_k,\qquad h_q(P_i)/q\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}uM.\tag{2}\]

3.2. Fluctuations select among tied coordinates

For fixed \(u\in(0,1)\), every binomial sample size tends to infinity because each \(\mu_s>0\). The binomial central limit theorem gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{C_{q,s}-qu\mu_s}{\sqrt{qu(1-u)}}\Longrightarrow\sqrt{\mu_s}\,G_s.\)

Indeed, centering at the exact mean changes the numerator by only the constant \(-u\mathbf 1_{\{s=t\}}\), and the variance divided by \(qu(1-u)\) tends to \(\mu_s\). Independence gives joint convergence over all types.

Taking linear combinations yields, jointly over \(k\in K\),

\[\displaystyle \frac{H_{q,k}-quM}{\sqrt{qu(1-u)}}\Longrightarrow Z_k.\tag{3}\]

Coordinates with mean below \(M\) can be excluded from the maximum with probability tending to one. By (2), their deficits relative to any coordinate in \(K\) grow linearly in \(q\). Adding the tagged agent changes each coordinate by at most \(R_t\), so it cannot overcome these deficits with probability bounded away from zero.

Among coordinates in \(K\), let \(W_q\) be a maximizing coordinate, with any fixed rule for finite-\(q\) ties. By (3) and the almost-sure uniqueness of the Gaussian maximizer,

\[\displaystyle \mathbb P(W_q=k)\longrightarrow p_k.\tag{4}\]

If \(K\) has at least two elements, its largest-minus-second-largest normalized predecessor sum converges in distribution to the corresponding Gaussian gap. That Gaussian gap is positive almost surely. It follows that the unnormalized gap exceeds the fixed number \(R_t\) with probability tending to one.

On this event, adding the tagged agent cannot change the maximizing coordinate. Therefore,

\(\displaystyle h_q(P_i\cup\{i\})-h_q(P_i)=r_{W_q}(t)\)

with probability tending to one. If \(K\) is a singleton, the same conclusion follows directly from the exclusion of lower-mean coordinates.

The marginal is bounded by \(R_t\), so (4) implies

\[\displaystyle \mathbb E\bigl[h_q(P_i\cup\{i\})-h_q(P_i)\mid U_i=u\bigr]\longrightarrow b_t.\tag{5}\]

3.3. Clipping and integration over arrival time

If \(uM<e\), equation (2) implies that \(h_q(P_i)+R_t<qe\) with probability tending to one. Hence clipping does not affect the marginal asymptotically, and its conditional expectation tends to \(b_t\).

If \(uM>e\), equation (2) implies that \(h_q(P_i)\geq qe\) with probability tending to one. The capped marginal consequently has conditional expectation tending to zero.

The conditional expectations are bounded by \(R_t\). The exceptional equation \(uM=e\) holds at at most one arrival time. Dominated convergence therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \lim_{q\to\infty}\mathbb E\bigl[F_q(P_i\cup\{i\})-F_q(P_i)\bigr]=\int_0^1 b_t\mathbf 1_{\{uM<e\}}\,du=\min\{e/M,1\}b_t.\)

Combining this with (1) proves the stated formula through all admissible integers.

Finally, if \(M=0\), positivity of the masses and nonnegativity of the references imply that every reference is zero. Only the last agent receives anything; its award is \(qe\). Thus \(a_t^{(q)}(e)=e\) exactly, proving the remaining case.

4. Properties of the resulting path

Since every retained coordinate has mean \(M\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t b_t=\sum_{k\in K}p_k\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t r_k(t)=M.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_t(e)=\min\{e,M\}+(e-M)_+=e.\)

The limiting path is therefore efficient, continuous, and piecewise affine:

  • For \(0<e\leq M\), the per-agent awards are \(e b_t/M\).
  • For \(e\geq M\), they are \(b_t+e-M\).

If there is a unique distinct coordinate profile of maximal mean, then \(b_t\) is simply that coordinate’s reference. If there are exactly two distinct maximal profiles, their Gaussian difference is a nondegenerate centered normal variable, so each has probability \(1/2\). The hardness below concerns unrestricted numbers of tied maximal profiles.

5. Evaluation hardness

We give a polynomial-size reduction from exact counting of linear extensions.

The theorem used here is that computing the number of linear extensions of an arbitrary finite poset is \(\#\mathrm{P}\)-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions. This is the result of [Brightwell and Winkler, *Counting linear extensions is #P-complete*](https://doi.org/10.1145/103418.103441).

5.1. Constructing the society

Let \(P\) be a poset on \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and let \(\ell(P)\) denote its number of linear extensions. List its strict comparable pairs as

\(\displaystyle (a_j,b_j),\qquad j=1,\ldots,m,\qquad a_j\prec_P b_j.\)

An antichain has \(\ell(P)=n!\), which can be returned directly. We may therefore assume \(n\geq2\) and \(m\geq1\). The transitive relation can be listed in polynomial time, with \(m\leq n(n-1)/2\).

Choose a power of two \(A\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle A\geq16mn!,\)

and put \(B=A+1\).

There are types \(v_1,\ldots,v_n,z_+,z_-\), padded with additional types to a total support size \(\tau\), where \(\tau\) is the smallest power of two at least \(n+2\). Give every listed type mass \(1/\tau\). These masses are positive dyadic rationals.

Use \(d=m+1\) coordinates, labeled \(0,1,\ldots,m\). Define

\(\displaystyle r_0(t)=B+\mathbf 1_{\{t=z_+\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{t=z_-\}},\)

and, for \(j=1,\ldots,m\),

\(\displaystyle r_j(t)=B+A\mathbf 1_{\{t=v_{a_j}\}}-A\mathbf 1_{\{t=v_{b_j}\}}.\)

Every reference is a positive integer: the smallest possible value is \(B-A=1\). Each coordinate’s references sum to \(\tau B\), so every coordinate has mean \(B\). Query the estate \(e=B\).

5.2. Extracting a Gaussian probability from one award

Let the independent standard normals associated with the types be denoted by \(G_t\). All Gaussian coordinate scores have the common term

\(\displaystyle \frac{B}{\sqrt{\tau}}\sum_{t\in T}G_t.\)

After subtracting this term and multiplying by \(\sqrt{\tau}\), coordinate \(0\) has score

\(\displaystyle H=G_{z_+}-G_{z_-},\)

and coordinate \(j\) has score

\(\displaystyle A X_j,\qquad X_j=G_{v_{a_j}}-G_{v_{b_j}}.\)

Thus coordinate \(0\) wins with probability

\[\displaystyle p_0=\mathbb P(A X_j\leq H\text{ for every }j).\tag{6}\]

Equalities have probability zero. The reference of type \(z_+\) is \(B+1\) in coordinate \(0\) and \(B\) in every other coordinate. Because the query satisfies \(e=M=B\), the limit formula gives

\[\displaystyle a_{z_+}(B)=B+p_0.\tag{7}\]

5.3. Comparing with the number of linear extensions

Define

\(\displaystyle p_*=\mathbb P(X_j\leq0\text{ for every }j).\)

The independent continuous random variables \(G_{v_1},\ldots,G_{v_n}\) have each of their \(n!\) strict orderings with equal probability. The inequalities defining \(p_*\) say exactly that the increasing ordering is a linear extension of \(P\). Therefore,

\[\displaystyle p_*=\ell(P)/n!.\tag{8}\]

If the events defining \(p_0\) and \(p_*\) differ, at least one comparison changes when its threshold moves from \(0\) to \(H/A\). Hence, for some \(j\),

\(\displaystyle |X_j|\leq |H|/A.\)

For each \(j\), \(X_j\) and \(H\) are independent normals of variance \(2\). The density of \(X_j\) is bounded by \(1/(2\sqrt{\pi})\), and \(\mathbb E|H|=2/\sqrt{\pi}\). Conditioning on \(H\) gives

\(\displaystyle \mathbb P(|X_j|\leq|H|/A)\leq\frac{\mathbb E|H|}{A\sqrt{\pi}}=\frac{2}{\pi A}<\frac1A.\)

A union bound, requiring no independence among the \(X_j\), now yields

\[\displaystyle |p_0-p_*|\leq m/A.\tag{9}\]

5.4. Precision, rounding, and input size

Choose a dyadic error tolerance satisfying

\(\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{16n!}.\)

Let \(\widehat a_{z_+}\) be an evaluator’s answer with absolute error at most \(\varepsilon\). Equations (7)–(9) imply

\(\displaystyle \left|n!(\widehat a_{z_+}-B)-\ell(P)\right|\leq n!\varepsilon+\frac{n!m}{A}\leq\frac18<\frac12.\)

Rounding \(n!(\widehat a_{z_+}-B)\) to the nearest integer therefore recovers \(\ell(P)\) exactly.

All parameters have polynomial encoding length. In particular,

\(\displaystyle \log A=O(n\log n+\log m),\qquad \log(1/\varepsilon)=O(n\log n),\)

and \(\tau<2(n+2)\), while \(d=m+1\). Factorial computation, construction of the society, and rounding all require polynomial time.

Thus a polynomial-time evaluator in \(d,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\) would compute exact linear-extension counts in polynomial time. Evaluation is consequently \(\#\mathrm{P}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions, even with uniform dyadic masses, strictly positive integer references, and all coordinate means equal. In particular, such a deterministic evaluator would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
