The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite resource catalogue \(R\), a finite support \(T\) of represented utility functions \(u_t:2^R\to\mathbb{Q}\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to one, and rational per-capita supplies \(q_r\), decide whether every feasible state \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\), satisfying \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:r\in S}x_{t,S}=q_r\), that admits no rational mass 1-deal maximizes \(W(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}u_t(S)\). A rational mass 1-deal moves some positive mass of type-\(t\) depots from bundle \(S\) to \(S\setminus\{r\}\) and the same mass of type-\(t'\) depots from \(S'\) to \(S'\cup\{r\}\), where \(r\in S\) and \(r\notin S'\), and is rational when \(u_t(S\setminus\{r\})-u_t(S)+u_{t'}(S'\cup\{r\})-u_{t'}(S')>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(R\) be a resource catalogue with \(m=|R|\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) utility types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Utilities use the \(k\)-additive representation, with input integer \(k\ge3\): each type lists signed binary-rational coefficients \(c_{t,B}\) for subsets \(B\subseteq R\) with \(|B|\le k\), and \(u_t(S)=\sum_{B\subseteq S,\,|B|\le k}c_{t,B}\). Omitted coefficients are zero; constant terms are allowed, and neither nonnegativity nor monotonicity is required.
Supplies are binary rationals \(q_r\in[0,1]\). A feasible state consists of real masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\), indexed by \(t\in T\) and \(S\subseteq R\), satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq R}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\subseteq R:r\in S}x_{t,S}=q_r\). Its welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\subseteq R}x_{t,S}u_t(S)\).
A mass 1-deal chooses \(t,t'\in T\), bundles \(S,S'\subseteq R\), a resource \(r\in S\setminus S'\), and a real amount \(0<\varepsilon\le\min\{x_{t,S},x_{t',S'}\}\). It transfers that mass from \((t,S)\) to \((t,S\setminus\{r\})\) and from \((t',S')\) to \((t',S'\cup\{r\})\); \(t=t'\) is allowed, since distinct depots may have the same type.
With unrestricted budget-balanced side payments, the deal is rational precisely when \(u_t(S\setminus\{r\})-u_t(S)+u_{t'}(S'\cup\{r\})-u_{t'}(S')>0\); zero-gain deals are excluded. Here “rational” describes acceptability, not the arithmetic domain of \(\varepsilon\).
Let \(D\) ask whether every feasible state admitting no rational mass 1-deal maximizes \(W\) over all feasible states. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

Is \(D\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?

For discrete agents, the anchor states \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness of recognizing this property, including \(k\)-additive utilities for fixed \(k\ge3\); the [later formulation](https://www.illc.uva.nl/~ulle/pubs/files/ChevaleyreEtAlJAAMAS2010.pdf) establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions, even with two agents. No result settling this exact mass version was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The utility encoding was unspecified. Selected the anchor's k-additive representation, with k at least three supplied as input, sparse signed binary-rational coefficients, and an optional constant term.
  • Restricted per-capita supplies to binary rationals in [0,1], as bundles contain at most one copy of each catalogue resource.
  • Specified real-valued state masses and transfer amounts. Interpreted rationality as the strict welfare-improvement criterion with unrestricted budget-balanced side payments.
  • Allowed transfers between distinct depots of the same type; the discrete requirement of distinct participating agents does not require distinct utility types.
  • Specified the transfer amount's availability bounds and maximization over the entire feasible state space.
  • Used polynomial-time Turing reductions in the hardness target, matching the precise reduction convention established in the later formulation.

Result

\(D\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. In fact, it is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions.

Hardness already holds with \(\tau=2\), fixed \(k=3\), masses \(\mu_a=1/4\) and \(\mu_b=3/4\), all supplies \(q_r=1/2\), and \(u_b\equiv0\).

1. Construction from weighted MAX-CUT

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete weighted MAX-CUT decision problem: given a finite simple graph \(G=(V,E)\), binary-encoded integer edge weights \(w_{ij}\), and a positive integer \(K\), determine whether some \(A\subseteq V\) has cut weight at least \(K\). This is the MAX CUT problem in [Karp’s original paper](https://cgi.di.uoa.gr/~sgk/teaching/grad/handouts/karp.pdf).

Write its cut-weight function as

\(C(A)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}\mathbf{1}_{|\{i,j\}\cap A|=1}.\)

Introduce one new resource \(s\), and let \(R=V\cup\{s\}\). Define two utility types by

\(u_b(S)=0,\)

\(u_a(S)=\mathbf{1}_{s\in S}\bigl(C(S\cap V)-K+\tfrac12\bigr).\)

Set \(\mu_a=1/4\), \(\mu_b=3/4\), and \(q_r=1/2\) for every \(r\in R\).

These utilities have the required \(3\)-additive representation. Indeed, putting \(b_i=\mathbf{1}_{i\in S}\) and \(z=\mathbf{1}_{s\in S}\), we have

\(u_a(S)=(-K+\tfrac12)z+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}(zb_i+zb_j-2zb_ib_j).\)

Thus the only potentially nonzero coefficients are

  • \(c_{a,\{s\}}=-K+\tfrac12\);
  • \(c_{a,\{s,i\}}=\sum_{j:\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}\);
  • \(c_{a,\{s,i,j\}}=-2w_{ij}\) for \(\{i,j\}\in E\).

All coefficients are binary rationals, and every listed subset has size at most \(3\).

We prove the exact equivalence

\(D\text{ answers yes}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \max_{A\subseteq V}C(A)<K.\)

2. An explicit stable state

Consider the state \(x^0\) with nonzero masses

\(x^0_{a,\varnothing}=\tfrac14,\qquad x^0_{b,R}=\tfrac12,\qquad x^0_{b,\varnothing}=\tfrac14.\)

It is feasible: the type masses are correct, and every resource is held by mass \(1/2\). Its welfare is \(0\).

It admits no rational mass \(1\)-deal:

  • Every deal between two type-\(b\) depots has gain \(0\).
  • Type \(a\) holds no resource, so it cannot be a donor.
  • When type \(a\) receives a vertex resource \(i\), its utility gain is \(u_a(\{i\})-u_a(\varnothing)=0\).
  • When type \(a\) receives \(s\), its utility gain is \(u_a(\{s\})-u_a(\varnothing)=-K+\tfrac12<0\).

The type-\(b\) donor contributes zero utility change. These cases exhaust all deals available at \(x^0\). Since zero-gain deals are excluded, \(x^0\) is stable.

3. A qualifying cut makes the stable state suboptimal

Suppose \(C(A)\ge K\) for some \(A\subseteq V\), and put \(B=A\cup\{s\}\).

Define another state \(y\) by

\(y_{a,B}=\tfrac14,\qquad y_{b,R\setminus B}=\tfrac14,\qquad y_{b,R}=\tfrac14,\qquad y_{b,\varnothing}=\tfrac14,\)

with masses added if any named bundles coincide.

This state is feasible. A resource in \(B\) is held by the type-\(a\) mass and the type-\(b\) mass at \(R\). A resource outside \(B\) is held by the type-\(b\) masses at \(R\setminus B\) and \(R\). In either case its total holding mass is \(1/2\).

Its welfare satisfies

\(W(y)=\tfrac14\bigl(C(A)-K+\tfrac12\bigr)\ge\tfrac18>0=W(x^0).\)

Therefore \(x^0\) is stable but suboptimal, and \(D\) answers no.

4. Without a qualifying cut, every stable state is optimal

Suppose no cut has weight at least \(K\). Since cut weights are integers,

\(C(A)\le K-1\qquad\text{for every }A\subseteq V.\)

Consequently,

\(u_a(S)=0\quad\text{if }s\notin S,\qquad u_a(S)\le-\tfrac12\quad\text{if }s\in S.\)

All bundle utilities are therefore nonpositive. Every feasible state has welfare at most \(0\), and \(x^0\) attains \(0\). Hence the optimal welfare is exactly \(0\).

Now let \(x\) be an arbitrary stable feasible state, allowing arbitrary real masses. The total type-\(b\) mass holding \(s\) is at most its total supply \(1/2\). Thus

\(\sum_{S':\,s\notin S'}x_{b,S'}\ge\mu_b-q_s=\tfrac14.\)

There is therefore a type-\(b\) bundle \(S'\) lacking \(s\) with positive mass.

If some type-\(a\) bundle \(S\) containing \(s\) also had positive mass, we could transfer \(s\) from \((a,S)\) to \((b,S')\), using any positive \(\varepsilon\le\min\{x_{a,S},x_{b,S'}\}\). Its gain would be

\(u_a(S\setminus\{s\})-u_a(S)+u_b(S'\cup\{s\})-u_b(S')=-u_a(S)\ge\tfrac12>0.\)

That would contradict stability. Hence every positive-mass type-\(a\) bundle lacks \(s\), and \(W(x)=0\). Thus every stable state is optimal.

This proves the claimed equivalence, including arbitrary mixtures and real deal amounts.

5. Membership in \(\mathrm{coNP}\)

For completeness, we show that a no instance of \(D\) has a polynomial-size certificate.

Let \(h=m+\tau\). Write feasibility as \(Ax=b\), where there is one row for each type-mass constraint and one for each resource-supply constraint. Every column of \(A\) belongs to \(\{0,1\}^{h}\).

Suppose \(x\) is a stable, suboptimal feasible state, possibly with irrational masses. Let \(H\) be its support. Consider the feasible polytope restricted to variables indexed by \(H\), and choose a vertex \(\widehat{x}\) minimizing welfare on that polytope. The polytope is nonempty and compact: it contains \(x\), and every coordinate lies between \(0\) and its type mass. Therefore

\(W(\widehat{x})\le W(x).\)

Moreover, \(\widehat{x}\) is stable. Its support is contained in \(H\), so any positive-gain deal available at \(\widehat{x}\) would also have been available at \(x\).

Choose a welfare-maximizing vertex \(\widehat{y}\) of the full feasible polytope. Suboptimality gives

\(W(\widehat{y})>W(x)\ge W(\widehat{x}).\)

Each vertex has at most \(h\) positive coordinates. Otherwise its positive-support columns would be linearly dependent, permitting a sufficiently small nonzero perturbation in both directions while preserving feasibility, contrary to being a vertex.

These vertices have rational coordinates with polynomial bit length. To see this explicitly, their positive coordinates solve a nonsingular square subsystem of size at most \(h\). Its matrix has entries in \(\{0,1\}\), and its right-hand side consists of input masses and supplies. Clearing denominators and applying Cramer’s rule gives coordinate bit lengths \(O(L+h\log(h+1))\); the determinant bound follows from Hadamard’s inequality.

Thus a certificate consists of the at most \(h\) positive entries of each of \(\widehat{x}\) and \(\widehat{y}\). A verifier checks:

  • Exact feasibility of both states.
  • Nonpositive deal gain for every ordered pair of positive-support depots of \(\widehat{x}\) and every eligible resource, including pairs of the same type.
  • The strict inequality \(W(\widehat{y})>W(\widehat{x})\).

There are at most \(h^2m\) deal-gain checks. Utility evaluation scans the explicitly listed coefficients, so verification uses \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) bit operations. In particular, no enumeration of all possible utility types or bundles is needed.

Therefore \(\overline{D}\in\mathrm{NP}\), and \(D\in\mathrm{coNP}\).

6. Reduction complexity and conclusion

For a source instance of binary length \(\ell\), the construction has \(m=|V|+1\), \(\tau=2\), and \(k=3\). It lists \(O(|V|+|E|)\) utility coefficients, has \(L=\operatorname{poly}(\ell)\), and is computable in polynomial time.

The equivalence above is a many-one reduction from the complement of weighted MAX-CUT to \(D\). Together with membership, this proves \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-completeness.

To solve weighted MAX-CUT using a \(D\)-oracle, construct this instance, make one oracle query, and negate its answer. This proves the requested \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions. Consequently, a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\), already on the fixed-\(k=3\), fixed-\(\tau=2\) instances constructed here.
