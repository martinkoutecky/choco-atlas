The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 6 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(S\) of duty classes, a rational per-capita supply vector \(\rho\in([0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q})^{S}\), and an identical negative-Boolean utility \(u:2^{S}\to\{0,-1\}\), decide whether there exists a distribution \(x=(x_B)_{B\subseteq S}\) with \(x_B\ge0\), \(\sum_{B\subseteq S}x_B=1\), and \(\sum_{B\ni s}x_B=\rho_s\) for every \(s\in S\), such that every pair of bundles in the support satisfies the paper's \(EFX^{+}_{0}\) condition; if so, compute such an \(x\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Here there is one utility type of mass \(1\), so \(\tau=1\).
Let \(S\) be a finite set of \(m\) duty classes and \(\rho\in([0,1]\cap\mathbb Q)^S\). A Boolean circuit \(C\) on bundle incidence vectors specifies the common utility \(u(B)=-C(\mathbf 1_B)\), with \(C(\mathbf 0)=0\); no monotonicity or additivity is assumed. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including the circuit and supplies.
An allocation is a distribution \(x_B\geq0\) over \(B\subseteq S\), with \(\sum_{B\subseteq S}x_B=1\) and \(\sum_{B\ni s}x_B=\rho_s\) for every \(s\in S\). Each person receives at most one duty from each class; bundles assigned to different people may overlap.
For these utilities, \(EFX^+_0\) means that every ordered pair \(A,B\) with \(x_A,x_B>0\) satisfies either \(u(A)\geq u(B)\), or \(u(A)=-1\), \(u(B)=0\), \(A\neq\emptyset\), and \(u(A\setminus\{s\})=0\) for every \(s\in A\). Thus deletion of a zero-marginal duty from an envying bundle must also eliminate envy.
The computational problem is to decide whether such an allocation exists and, when it does, compute one as a list of positive bundle masses encoded as binary rationals.

Is this feasibility problem in \(\mathrm P\), with a feasible allocation computable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finitely many agents and indivisible items, identical normalized negative-Boolean utilities always admit an \(EFX^+_0\) allocation, computable in polynomial time with an evaluation oracle ([Bérczi et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2024.114596)).
No result settling exactly the distribution problem with prescribed per-capita supplies was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored the normalization u(∅)=0 required by the discrete definition; imposed no monotonicity or additivity.
  • Specified a Boolean circuit for −u because the graded question gives no utility encoding. This supplies a finite, polynomially evaluable input representation consistent with the discrete evaluation-oracle model.
  • Represented the identical-utility society by one type of total mass 1, distinguishing its support size τ=1 from the support of the allocation.
  • Made explicit that bundles contain at most one duty per class and may overlap across recipients, as dictated by the graded marginal-supply constraints.
  • Expanded EFX⁺₀ into its equivalent normalized negative-Boolean condition, retaining zero-marginal deletions and nonemptiness.
  • Specified sparse binary-rational encoding of a computed distribution and defined L to include all input data.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The feasibility problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even with \(\tau=1\) and every supply in \(\{1/2,1\}\). Every feasible instance admits a certificate consisting of at most \(m+1\) bundles with polynomial-length binary-encoded rational masses. Consequently, deciding feasibility and computing an allocation in polynomial time would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Reduction from satisfiability

Let \(\varphi(y_1,\ldots,y_n)\) be an instance of \(\mathrm{3SAT}\). Construct the duty classes

\(S=\{a,b,z,v_1,\ldots,v_n\}\),

with supplies

\(\rho_a=\rho_b=1,\qquad \rho_z=\rho_{v_i}=1/2\quad(1\leq i\leq n).\)

There is one utility type, of mass \(1\). For an incidence vector \(w\in\{0,1\}^{S}\), define

\(C(w)=(w_a\lor w_b)\land w_z\land\neg\varphi(w_{v_1},\ldots,w_{v_n}),\)

and set \(u(B)=-C(\mathbf 1_B)\).

This circuit has size polynomial in the size of \(\varphi\), and \(C(\mathbf 0)=0\). Thus the construction satisfies the normalization and all encoding requirements.

We prove that an \(EFX^+_0\) allocation exists exactly when \(\varphi\) is satisfiable.

2. A satisfying assignment gives an allocation

Suppose \(t=(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\) satisfies \(\varphi\). Define

\(B_1=\{a,b,z\}\cup\{v_i:t_i=1\},\)

\(B_0=\{a,b\}\cup\{v_i:t_i=0\}.\)

Assign mass \(1/2\) to each bundle.

Both bundles contain \(a,b\), so their supplies equal \(1\). Exactly one bundle contains \(z\), and exactly one contains each \(v_i\), so all remaining supplies equal \(1/2\).

For \(B_1\), the assignment encoded by the variable duties satisfies \(\varphi\); hence \(C(\mathbf 1_{B_1})=0\). For \(B_0\), the selector \(z\) is absent; hence \(C(\mathbf 1_{B_0})=0\). Both supported bundles therefore have utility \(0\).

Every ordered pair of supported bundles satisfies the first alternative in the stated \(EFX^+_0\) condition. This is a feasible allocation, with two binary-rational masses.

3. Any feasible allocation gives a satisfying assignment

Suppose \(x\) is a feasible \(EFX^+_0\) allocation.

First, every supported bundle contains both \(a\) and \(b\). Indeed,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{B:a\notin B}x_B=1-\rho_a=0.\)

Nonnegativity implies that every bundle omitting \(a\) has zero mass. The same argument applies to \(b\).

Second, there is a supported bundle \(B_0\) omitting \(z\), because

\(\displaystyle \sum_{B:z\notin B}x_B=1-\rho_z=1/2>0.\)

Every such bundle has utility \(0\), by the definition of \(C\).

Now suppose a supported bundle \(A\) had utility \(-1\). It contains \(a,b\), and deleting \(a\) leaves the disjunction \(w_a\lor w_b\) true because \(b\) remains. All other circuit inputs are unchanged. Consequently,

\(C(\mathbf 1_{A\setminus\{a\}})=C(\mathbf 1_A)=1,\)

so

\(u(A\setminus\{a\})=-1.\)

For the ordered pair \((A,B_0)\), the first fairness alternative fails because \(-1<0\). The second alternative also fails: deletion of the duty \(a\in A\) does not raise the utility to \(0\). This contradicts \(EFX^+_0\).

Thus every supported bundle has utility \(0\).

Finally, the selector supply gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{B:z\in B}x_B=1/2>0.\)

Choose a supported bundle \(B_1\) containing \(z\). It also contains \(a,b\). Therefore

\(0=C(\mathbf 1_{B_1})=\neg\varphi(\mathbf 1_{B_1\cap\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}}),\)

where the final incidence vector is indexed by the variable duties. Hence \(B_1\) encodes a satisfying assignment.

This proves a polynomial-time many-one reduction from \(\mathrm{3SAT}\), establishing \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

4. Polynomial-size certificates

We now prove membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), including the required bound on rational encoding length.

Suppose an instance has a feasible allocation, and let \(\mathcal T\) be its positive support. Fairness is hereditary under restricting support: every ordered pair in a subset of \(\mathcal T\) already satisfies the fairness condition.

For each bundle \(B\), define the column vector

\(v_B=(1,\mathbf 1_B)\in\{0,1\}^{m+1},\)

and let \(r=(1,\rho)\). The allocation constraints say

\(\displaystyle r=\sum_{B\in\mathcal T}x_Bv_B.\)

Among representations of \(r\) using nonnegative coefficients on \(\mathcal T\), choose one with the fewest positive coefficients. Write its support as \(B_1,\ldots,B_k\), with positive coefficients \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k\).

The columns \(v_{B_i}\) are linearly independent. To prove this, suppose

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_i v_{B_i}=0\)

for a nonzero real vector \(\lambda\). The first coordinate gives \(\sum_i\lambda_i=0\), so \(\lambda\) has both positive and negative entries. Set

\(\displaystyle t=\min_{\lambda_i>0}\frac{\alpha_i}{\lambda_i}>0.\)

Then \(\alpha_i-t\lambda_i\) is nonnegative for every \(i\), at least one coefficient becomes zero, and the represented vector remains \(r\). This contradicts minimality.

It follows that \(k\leq m+1\). Select \(k\) rows of the column matrix forming a nonsingular \(k\times k\) matrix \(M\). Its entries are integers in \(\{0,1\}\), and

\(M\alpha=r_J,\)

where \(r_J\) consists of the selected coordinates of \(r\).

Write the input supplies as \(\rho_s=p_s/q_s\), with binary-encoded integers, and set \(D=\prod_{s\in S}q_s\). The binary length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\), and \(Dr_J\) is an integer vector whose entries lie between \(0\) and \(D\).

Cramer's rule gives

\(\displaystyle \alpha_i=\frac{\det M_i}{D\det M},\)

where \(M_i\) is obtained from \(M\) by replacing column \(i\) with \(Dr_J\). Hadamard's determinant inequality yields

\(|\det M|\leq k^{k/2},\qquad |\det M_i|\leq Dk^{k/2}.\)

Indeed, every column of \(M\) has Euclidean norm at most \(\sqrt{k}\), while the replacement column has norm at most \(D\sqrt{k}\). Thus each coefficient has numerator and denominator of binary length

\(O\!\left(L+k\log(k+1)\right).\)

These coefficients give an allocation on a subset of \(\mathcal T\), so fairness is preserved.

A certificate therefore lists at most \(m+1\) incidence vectors and polynomial-length positive rational masses. A verifier checks normalization and supplies by exact rational arithmetic, evaluates the circuit on the listed bundles, and checks every ordered pair and the required single-duty deletions. All these operations take polynomial time.

Hence feasibility belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), completing the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness proof.

5. Relation to the finite-agent theorem

The finite-agent existence theorem concerns allocations of individually distinct items. It does not impose the additional requirement that each person receive at most one item from each duty class. [Bérczi et al., Theorem 9](https://real.mtak.hu/206338/1/1-s2.0-S0304397524002111-main.pdf), therefore, does not guarantee an allocation satisfying the constraints here.

In the reduction, supply \(1\) forces every person to receive both compulsory classes. Deleting one of them from a utility-\(-1\) bundle leaves its utility unchanged, which is precisely the obstruction detected by the stated \(EFX^+_0\) condition.
