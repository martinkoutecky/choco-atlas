The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an integrity constraint \(IC\) and a finite support \(S\subseteq\operatorname{Mod}(IC)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), compute an AVR or MVR winner \(b\in S\) and establish that \(D_\mu(b)\le 2\min_{x\in\operatorname{Mod}(IC)}D_\mu(x)\), strictly when the minimum is positive, where \(D_\mu(x)=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t H(x,t)\). For fixed \(m\), establish the stronger AVR bound \(D_\mu(b)\le \frac{2(m-1)}{m}\min_{x\in\operatorname{Mod}(IC)}D_\mu(x)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge2\), and let \(IC\) be a consistent propositional formula over \(m\) binary issues; feasible ballots are \(\operatorname{Mod}(IC)\subseteq\{0,1\}^m\).
A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(S\subseteq\operatorname{Mod}(IC)\) of \(\tau\) distinct types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau=|S|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including \(IC\); state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Write \(H\) for Hamming distance, \(D_\mu(x)=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_tH(x,t)\), and \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu=\min_{x\in\operatorname{Mod}(IC)}D_\mu(x)\). Define \(\mathrm{AVR}_\mu=\arg\min_{b\in S}D_\mu(b)\).
For \(q_j=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_tt_j\), define \(a^w_j=\mathbf1[q_j\ge\tfrac12]\), \(a^s_j=\mathbf1[q_j>\tfrac12]\), and \(\mathrm{Maj}_\mu=\{a^w,a^s\}\). Define \(\mathrm{MVR}_\mu=\arg\min_{b\in S}\min_{a\in\mathrm{Maj}_\mu}H(b,a)\). All minimizer ties are retained; majority ballots need not satisfy \(IC\).

Can a winner of each rule be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with every \(b\in\mathrm{AVR}_\mu\cup\mathrm{MVR}_\mu\) satisfying \(D_\mu(b)\le2\mathrm{OPT}_\mu\), strictly whenever \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu>0\)?
For each fixed \(m\ge2\), does every \(b\in\mathrm{AVR}_\mu\) additionally satisfy \(D_\mu(b)\le\frac{2(m-1)}m\mathrm{OPT}_\mu\)?

For finite electorates, both rules are polynomial-time computable and strict \(2\)-approximations of the feasible minimum-distance rule; AVR additionally has the fixed-dimension bound \(2(m-1)/m\). [Endriss and Grandi (2014)](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/8798/8798-13-12326-1-2-20201228.pdf).
No separate result explicitly treating this rational-mass input model was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Defined IC as a consistent propositional formula and included its encoding in the total input bit length L.
  • Specified the weighted AVR and MVR definitions, retaining every minimizing ballot and using exactly the weak and strict majority ballots for MVR.
  • Required the approximation guarantees for every tied winner, matching the discrete approximation convention.
  • Restricted m to at least 2: the stated fixed-dimension factor becomes zero at m=1 and cannot bound positive optimum instances.
  • Allowed singleton support, since distinct types need not correspond to distinct voters and repeated identical ballots are admissible.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Yes. Both rules are polynomial-time computable in the rational-mass model, every winner satisfies the strict approximation guarantee, and AVR satisfies the stated fixed-dimension bound. The latter bound is sharp.

The proofs below establish these claims directly for weighted support, extending the finite-electorate bounds of [Endriss and Grandi (2014)](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/8798/8798-13-12326-1-2-20201228.pdf).

1. Exact computation and running time

Write each mass as \(\mu_t=u_t/v_t\), with positive integers encoded in binary. Define
\(Q=\prod_{t\in S}v_t\) and \(w_t=u_t(Q/v_t)\).
Then every \(w_t\) is a positive integer, \(\sum_t w_t=Q\), and \(\mu_t=w_t/Q\).

Because the denominator encodings are included in \(L\), the bit length of \(Q\), and hence of every \(w_t\), is \(O(L)\). Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, these integers can be computed in \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations.

For each issue, compute \(N_j=\sum_{t\in S}w_tt_j\), so \(q_j=N_j/Q\). For every \(b\in S\), its AVR score can then be computed as the integer
\[ Q D_\mu(b)=\sum_{j:b_j=0}N_j+\sum_{j:b_j=1}(Q-N_j). \]
These scores have \(O(L+\log m)\) bits. Comparing them returns an AVR winner, or all AVR winners.

The majority ballots are computed by the exact comparisons \(2N_j\ge Q\) and \(2N_j>Q\). Computing the two Hamming distances for each support type and comparing their minima returns an MVR winner, or all MVR winners.

Thus a sufficient bound for computing either rule, including all its winners, is
\[ O\!\left(L+\tau L^2+m\tau(L+\log m)\right) \]
bit operations. Every returned ballot is feasible because it belongs to the supplied support.

2. The unconstrained minimum

Define
\[ C=\sum_{j=1}^m\min\{q_j,1-q_j\}. \]
For any ballot \(x\), exchanging the two finite sums in the definition of \(D_\mu\) gives
\[ D_\mu(x)=\sum_{j:x_j=0}q_j+\sum_{j:x_j=1}(1-q_j). \]
Each coordinate contributes at least \(\min\{q_j,1-q_j\}\). Both specified majority ballots attain this minimum on every coordinate, including tied coordinates. Consequently,
\[ D_\mu(a)=C\quad\text{for every }a\in\mathrm{Maj}_\mu, \qquad C\le\mathrm{OPT}_\mu. \]

If \(C=0\), each coordinate is unanimous. Positivity of all masses therefore implies that every support type is the same ballot \(a\). This ballot is feasible, so both rules return \(a\), with \(D_\mu(a)=\mathrm{OPT}_\mu=0\). In particular, \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu>0\) implies \(C>0\).

3. Every MVR winner satisfies the strict bound

Fix an arbitrary \(b\in\mathrm{MVR}_\mu\). Choose \(a\in\mathrm{Maj}_\mu\) attaining \(r=\min_{a'\in\mathrm{Maj}_\mu}H(b,a')\).

For every \(t\in S\), the definition of an MVR winner gives
\[ r\le\min_{a'\in\mathrm{Maj}_\mu}H(t,a')\le H(t,a). \]
By the triangle inequality,
\[ H(b,t)\le H(b,a)+H(a,t)=r+H(a,t)\le2H(a,t). \]

If \(r>0\), retain the exact zero contribution from \(t=b\). Since \(\mu_b>0\),
\[ D_\mu(b) =\sum_{t\ne b}\mu_tH(b,t) \le2\sum_{t\ne b}\mu_tH(a,t) =2C-2\mu_b r <2C \le2\mathrm{OPT}_\mu. \]

If \(r=0\), then \(b=a\), so \(D_\mu(b)=C\le\mathrm{OPT}_\mu\). Whenever \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu>0\), this also implies \(D_\mu(b)<2\mathrm{OPT}_\mu\).

This proves the required bound for every MVR winner, without breaking any minimizer ties.

For every \(b\in\mathrm{AVR}_\mu\) and every \(c\in\mathrm{MVR}_\mu\), we have \(D_\mu(b)\le D_\mu(c)\), because \(c\in S\). Hence every AVR winner inherits the same guarantee.

4. The fixed-dimension AVR bound

Fix any \(a\in\mathrm{Maj}_\mu\), and define
\[ p_j=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\mathbf1[t_j\ne a_j]. \]
Then \(p_j=\min\{q_j,1-q_j\}\) and \(\sum_jp_j=C\).

Let \(V=\min_{b\in S}D_\mu(b)\), the common objective value of all AVR winners.

If \(a\in S\), then \(V=C\): the inequality \(V\le C\) follows by selecting \(a\), and \(V\ge C\) follows from the unconstrained lower bound. Since \(m\ge2\),
\[ V=C\le\frac{2(m-1)}m\,C. \]

Suppose instead that \(a\notin S\). Every support ballot differs from \(a\) on at least one coordinate, so
\[ C=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_tH(a,t)\ge\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t=1. \]

The minimum support objective is at most its weighted average:
\[ V\le\sum_{t\in S}\mu_tD_\mu(t) =\sum_{t,u\in S}\mu_t\mu_uH(t,u). \]
For coordinate \(j\), disagreement occurs when exactly one of the two ballots differs from \(a_j\). Its contribution to this double sum is therefore
\(p_j(1-p_j)+(1-p_j)p_j=2p_j(1-p_j)\).
Thus
\[ V\le2\sum_{j=1}^mp_j(1-p_j) =2C-2\sum_{j=1}^mp_j^2. \]
Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\sum_jp_j^2\ge C^2/m\). Using \(C\ge1\), we obtain
\[ V\le2C-\frac{2C^2}{m} \le2C-\frac{2C}{m} =\frac{2(m-1)}m\,C. \]

Both cases yield, for every \(b\in\mathrm{AVR}_\mu\),
\[ D_\mu(b)\le\frac{2(m-1)}m\,C \le\frac{2(m-1)}m\,\mathrm{OPT}_\mu. \]
The proof holds uniformly for every \(m\ge2\); fixing \(m\) is unnecessary.

5. Sharpness of the AVR constant

Take a tautological integrity constraint and support consisting of the \(m\) unit vectors \(e_1,\ldots,e_m\), each with mass \(1/m\). Every coordinate has marginal \(1/m\), so \(C=1\), and the feasible all-zero ballot attains \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu=1\).

Distinct unit vectors have Hamming distance \(2\). Hence every support ballot has objective
\[ D_\mu(e_i)=\frac{2(m-1)}m. \]
All are AVR winners, proving that the fixed-dimension constant cannot be improved.
