The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an ordered sequence of \(m\) items, matroid types \(T\) with rank oracles, rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and per-item supply \(\eta=1/N\), each arriving item may be assigned to mass at most \(\eta\) of currently eligible facilities. Each facility receives an integral bundle independent in its type's matroid, while the aggregate mass assigned may be fractional. What is the best worst-case competitive ratio achievable online against the offline optimum for the same population and item supplies, by an algorithm polynomial in \(m\), \(\lvert T\rvert\), and the input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Type \(t\) has a matroid \(\mathcal M_t\). Each type comprises a nonatomic mass of facilities, each holding an integral independent bundle.
There are \(m\geq1\) distinct items and an integer \(N\geq1\), encoded in binary; every item has supply \(\eta=1/N\). Types, masses, \(m\), and \(N\) are known initially. Items arrive in an adversarial sequence unknown in advance; rank oracles accept only subsets of items revealed so far, and each call costs one unit.
Initially every bundle is empty. At arrival of item \(j\), the algorithm may split any mass currently holding bundle \(S\) into rational submasses and give \(j\) to selected submasses only if \(S\cup\{j\}\) is independent in their type's matroid. The total selected mass across all types is at most \(\eta\). Decisions are irrevocable; unused supply expires.
If \(q_{t,S}\) is the final mass of type \(t\) holding bundle \(S\), welfare is \(W=\sum_{t,S}q_{t,S}|S|\). Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) maximize this welfare with full knowledge of the sequence, under the same bundle and supply constraints.
Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the explicit input, including masses and \(N\). Polynomial time means \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) computation and oracle calls, including the representation of allocation decisions; dependence polynomial in \(N\) is not allowed.
For randomized algorithms, competitiveness is measured by \(\mathbb E[W]\geq\rho\,\mathrm{OPT}\) for every fixed instance with \(\mathrm{OPT}>0\), against an oblivious adversary.

What is the supremum worst-case competitive ratio achievable by polynomial-time online algorithms for this continuous allocation problem, allowing randomization?

For finitely many agents with matroid rank utilities, a randomized polynomial-time online algorithm achieves \(1-1/e\) competitiveness. [Source](https://mzlatin.github.io/online-sap.pdf)
For fractional online submodular assignment, a deterministic \(1-1/e\)-competitive algorithm is known; that formulation does not explicitly maintain masses of integral facility bundles. No result explicitly treating the support-encoded population above was located. [Source](https://mzlatin.github.io/online-sap.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The graded question omitted the objective; use total mass-weighted bundle cardinality, matching unweighted matroid rank welfare.
  • Interpret each positive type mass as nonatomic, allowing rational splits among facilities with different integral bundles.
  • Set N to an arbitrary positive binary-encoded integer; impose no requirement that N times each type mass be integral.
  • Make assignments irrevocable and let unused item supply expire, following the matroid welfare setting.
  • Resolve the online-information ambiguity by withholding future items and restricting oracle queries to revealed items, consistent with the general online assignment definition. The full version retains the broader oracle wording in its welfare subsection.
  • Allow randomized algorithms and use an oblivious adversary with expected welfare and a purely multiplicative competitive guarantee.
  • Require polynomial computation, oracle calls, and allocation representation in m, support size, and explicit input bit length, rather than in the numerical value of N.

Result

The supremum is \(1-1/e\). It is attained by a deterministic polynomial-time online algorithm that explicitly maintains rational masses of integral independent bundles.

The proof uses the stipulated oracle model: the algorithm learns matroid information only through rank queries on revealed items.

1. Facilities ordered by a continuous priority

Let \(E\) be the set of all items. Write \(r_t\) and \(\operatorname{cl}_t\) for the rank function and closure operator of \(\mathcal M_t\).

Represent the facilities of type \(t\) by coordinates \(u\in[0,1)\), with mass measure \(\mu_t\,du\). Let \(S_t(u)\) be the bundle held at coordinate \(u\).

The algorithm maintains the following invariant:

\(u<v\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{cl}_t(S_t(u))\supseteq \operatorname{cl}_t(S_t(v)).\)

Thus earlier facilities have larger matroid spans. Bundles themselves need not be nested.

Initially every bundle is empty, so the invariant holds.

The allocation rule

Suppose item \(j\) arrives. By the invariant, the facilities of type \(t\) for which \(j\) is already spanned form a prefix. Let its length be \(a_t\in[0,1]\). Up to endpoints of zero mass,

\(j\in\operatorname{cl}_t(S_t(u)) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u<a_t.\)

Because \(S_t(u)\) is independent, item \(j\) can be added exactly when \(u\geq a_t\).

Define

\(H(b)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t(b-a_t)_+,\qquad b\in[0,1],\)

where \((z)_+=\max\{z,0\}\).

Choose a cutoff \(b_j\) as follows:

  • If \(H(1)\leq\eta\), set \(b_j=1\).
  • Otherwise, choose the unique \(b_j\in(0,1)\) satisfying \(H(b_j)=\eta\).

Give item \(j\) to every eligible facility with coordinate \(u<b_j\). Consequently, type \(t\) receives it on the interval \([a_t,b_j)\) when \(a_t<b_j\).

The allocated mass is \(H(b_j)\leq\eta\), and every updated bundle is independent.

Proof that the span invariant is preserved

Fix a type and coordinates \(u<v\). Let their bundles before the arrival be \(A\) and \(B\), respectively, so that

\(\operatorname{cl}_t(A)\supseteq\operatorname{cl}_t(B).\)

There are four cases.

  • Neither facility receives \(j\). The inclusion is unchanged.
  • Both receive \(j\). Monotonicity and idempotence of closure give

\(\operatorname{cl}_t(A\cup\{j\})\supseteq \operatorname{cl}_t(B\cup\{j\})\).

  • Only the earlier facility receives \(j\). Enlarging its bundle preserves the inclusion.
  • Only the later facility receives \(j\). Then \(v<b_j\), hence \(u<b_j\). The earlier facility was therefore skipped because \(j\in\operatorname{cl}_t(A)\). Since \(B\cup\{j\}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_t(A)\), closure monotonicity and idempotence imply

\(\operatorname{cl}_t(B\cup\{j\})\subseteq \operatorname{cl}_t(A)\).

This proves the invariant inductively.

We will also need a consequence of the allocation rule: after item \(j\) is processed, every facility with coordinate \(u<b_j\) spans \(j\). Such a facility either already spanned \(j\), or received it. Subsequent allocations only enlarge bundles, so this remains true thereafter.

2. Polynomial implementation and rational encoding

The continuous description admits an explicit implementation using rational splits.

For each type, maintain a partition of \([0,1)\) into intervals, each labeled by its integral bundle. Initially there is one interval.

On arrival of \(j\), test each current interval bundle \(S\) using

\(r_t(S\cup\{j\})=|S|+1.\)

All queried items have been revealed. The span invariant ensures that the eligible intervals form a suffix, so these tests determine \(a_t\).

The function \(H\) is piecewise linear with rational breakpoints \(a_t\) and rational slopes. Sorting its breakpoints and solving a linear equation determines \(b_j\) exactly.

Within each existing interval, eligibility is constant. Only the interval containing \(b_j\) can require a new split. Thus, after \(k\) arrivals, each type has at most \(k+1\) intervals. In particular:

  • The final representation has at most \(\tau(m+1)\) interval records.
  • The total number of rank calls is \(O(\tau m^2)\).
  • Each bundle is an explicitly stored subset of revealed items.

It remains to bound the rational bit lengths.

Choose a common denominator \(d\) for the input masses, and write

\(\mu_t=c_t/d,\qquad c_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad \sum_t c_t=d.\)

Taking the product of the input denominators gives \(\log d=O(L)\).

Maintain a common coordinate denominator \(Q\), initially \(Q=N\). Suppose a newly computed cutoff satisfies \(b_j<1\), and let

\(K=\{t:a_t<b_j\},\qquad C=\sum_{t\in K}c_t.\)

The equation \(H(b_j)=1/N\) gives

\(b_j= \frac{d/N+\sum_{t\in K}c_ta_t}{C}.\)

Here \(1\leq C\leq d\). Since every \(a_t\) has denominator dividing \(Q\), and \(N\) divides \(Q\), the new cutoff has denominator dividing \(QC\). Replace \(Q\) by \(QC\).

After at most \(m\) such updates,

\(\log Q\leq\log N+m\log d=O(mL).\)

An interval’s physical mass is \(\mu_t\) times its length, so its denominator divides \(dQ\), also of polynomial bit length. Every split and allocation therefore uses rational masses with polynomial encoding size.

This proves polynomial running time and representation size in \(m,\tau,L\), with no dependence polynomial in \(N\).

3. A bound valid for every offline allocation

Consider any feasible offline allocation. Let \(y_{tj}\) be the mass of type \(t\) receiving item \(j\), and write

\(y_t(A)=\sum_{j\in A}y_{tj}.\)

Supply feasibility gives

\(\sum_t y_{tj}\leq\eta\qquad\text{for every item }j.\)

For every type \(t\) and subset \(A\subseteq E\), independence of facility bundles gives

\(y_t(A)\leq\mu_t r_t(A).\)

Indeed, if \(q_{t,S}\) is its final bundle distribution, then

\(y_t(A)=\sum_S q_{t,S}|S\cap A| \leq\sum_S q_{t,S}r_t(A) =\mu_t r_t(A).\)

Its welfare is \(V=\sum_{t,j}y_{tj}\).

These inequalities apply in particular to \(\mathrm{OPT}\). There is no issue of unattained optimization: feasible final distributions form a compact finite-dimensional polytope, indexed by the finitely many independent bundles. Every such distribution can be implemented offline by prepartitioning facilities according to their designated final bundles and delivering their items as they arrive; hereditary independence makes every intermediate bundle feasible.

4. Proof of \(1-1/e\) competitiveness

Define

\(g(u)=e^{u-1},\qquad G(u)=\int_0^u g(v)\,dv=e^{u-1}-e^{-1},\qquad \gamma=G(1)=1-e^{-1}.\)

These functions are used only in the analysis. The algorithm performs rational arithmetic.

For the algorithm’s final bundles, set

\(F_t(u)=\operatorname{cl}_t(S_t(u)).\)

The span invariant makes these flats decreasing in \(u\). For each type-item pair, let \(A_{tj}\) be the length of the prefix of facilities finally spanning \(j\). Then, up to endpoints,

\(j\in F_t(u)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u<A_{tj}.\)

The consequence established in Section 1 gives

\(A_{tj}\geq b_j\qquad\text{for every }t,j.\)

For each item define the nonnegative coefficient

\(\beta_j=1-g(b_j).\)

Because \(G\) is increasing,

\(\beta_j+G(A_{tj}) \geq 1-g(b_j)+G(b_j) =\gamma.\)

Bounding the offline welfare

Using the supply inequalities and nonnegativity of the coefficients,

\(\gamma V \leq\sum_j\eta\beta_j+ \sum_{t,j}G(A_{tj})y_{tj}.\)

For each type, the prefix characterization gives

\(\sum_jG(A_{tj})y_{tj} =\int_0^1g(u)y_t(F_t(u))\,du.\)

Applying the rank inequality from Section 3,

\(\int_0^1g(u)y_t(F_t(u))\,du \leq\mu_t\int_0^1g(u)r_t(F_t(u))\,du.\)

Closure preserves rank, and the algorithm’s bundles are independent. Therefore

\(r_t(F_t(u))=|S_t(u)|.\)

Define

\(\mathcal D= \eta\sum_j\beta_j+ \sum_t\mu_t\int_0^1g(u)|S_t(u)|\,du.\)

We have proved

\(\gamma V\leq\mathcal D.\)

Bounding \(\mathcal D\) by the algorithm’s welfare

Let

\(B=\sum_t\mu_t\int_0^1g(u)|S_t(u)|\,du.\)

At arrival \(j\), let \(h_j=H(b_j)\) be the mass allocated. This is exactly the welfare increment.

Only selected facilities increase their bundle size, each by one. All selected coordinates are at most \(b_j\). Since \(g\) is increasing,

\(\Delta B\leq g(b_j)h_j.\)

If \(b_j<1\), the algorithm allocates the full supply, so \(h_j=\eta\). Consequently,

\(\Delta\mathcal D =\Delta B+\eta\beta_j \leq g(b_j)\eta+\eta(1-g(b_j)) =\eta=h_j.\)

If \(b_j=1\), then \(\beta_j=0\) and \(g(b_j)=1\), giving again

\(\Delta\mathcal D\leq h_j.\)

Initially \(B=0\). Summing over arrivals yields

\(\mathcal D\leq\sum_jh_j=W.\)

Taking the offline allocation to be optimal proves

\(W\geq(1-1/e)\,\mathrm{OPT}.\)

This guarantee is deterministic and holds for every arrival sequence.

5. Matching upper bound against an oblivious adversary

We prove an upper bound even for computationally unrestricted randomized algorithms.

Let \(n\) tend to infinity through powers of two. Set

\(m=\tau=N=n,\qquad \mu_t=1/n.\)

These masses have finite binary expansions.

Choose a uniformly random permutation \(\pi\) of the types, before the algorithm runs. Items arrive in the fixed order \(1,\ldots,n\). Type \(\pi(k)\) has a rank-one matroid whose nonloops are precisely items \(1,\ldots,k\).

Thus item \(j\) can be allocated exactly to the surviving types

\(R_j=\{\pi(j),\ldots,\pi(n)\}.\)

Every fixed permutation has \(\mathrm{OPT}=1\): allocate item \(j\), of mass \(1/n\), to the entire type \(\pi(j)\). This allocates every item’s supply feasibly.

The construction is oblivious: both the matroids and the arrival sequence are fixed before the run.

What the restricted oracles reveal

We may strengthen the algorithm by revealing \(R_j\) completely at every arrival.

Every surviving type has all revealed items as nonloops. Its rank oracle therefore returns rank one on every nonempty revealed subset. Such queries reveal no distinction among the surviving types concerning future compatibility.

Conditioned on the revealed history and the algorithm’s randomness so far, the next departing type \(\pi(j)\) remains uniformly distributed over \(R_j\).

An expected-capacity inequality

Scale all masses by \(n\). Each type now has capacity one, and each item has supply one.

Let \(C_j\) be the total remaining capacity among \(R_j\), immediately before item \(j\). Let \(X_j\) be the amount allocated at that arrival, and set

\(x_j=\mathbb E[X_j],\qquad k_j=n-j+1.\)

We have \(0\leq x_j\leq1\). After the allocation, the uniformly selected departing type carries, in expectation, a fraction \(1/k_j\) of the remaining capacity. Hence, for \(j<n\),

\(\mathbb E[C_{j+1}] =\frac{k_j-1}{k_j}\bigl(\mathbb E[C_j]-x_j\bigr).\)

Define \(u_j=\mathbb E[C_j]/k_j\). Since \(C_1=n\),

\(u_1=1,\qquad u_{j+1}=u_j-\frac{x_j}{k_j}.\)

At the last arrival, \(x_n\leq\mathbb E[C_n]=u_n\). Telescoping therefore gives

\(\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{x_j}{n-j+1}\leq1.\)

Converting this to a welfare bound

For any integer \(1\leq\lambda\leq n\),

\(\sum_{j=1}^n x_j =\lambda\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{x_j}{k_j} +\sum_{j=1}^n x_j\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{k_j}\right) \leq\lambda+\sum_{k=\lambda+1}^n\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{k}\right).\)

The inequality uses the weighted-capacity bound, \(x_j\leq1\) for positive coefficients, and \(x_j\geq0\) for negative coefficients. Simplifying,

\(\sum_{j=1}^n x_j \leq n-\lambda\sum_{k=\lambda+1}^n\frac1k.\)

Returning to the original mass scale,

\(\mathbb E[W]\leq 1-\frac{\lambda}{n}\sum_{k=\lambda+1}^n\frac1k.\)

Choose \(\lambda=\max\{1,\lfloor n/e\rfloor\}\). Then \(\lambda/n\to1/e\). The integral bounds

\(\log\frac{n+1}{\lambda+1} \leq\sum_{k=\lambda+1}^n\frac1k \leq\log\frac n\lambda\)

show that the harmonic sum tends to one. Thus the displayed welfare upper bound tends to \(1-1/e\).

Since every permutation has optimum one, averaging implies that for each algorithm there is a fixed permutation whose expected competitive ratio is at most this bound. Any claimed ratio strictly greater than \(1-1/e\) is contradicted by sufficiently large \(n\).

Together with the deterministic construction, this settles the supremum and its attainment.
