1. Classification

Feasibility and exact minimization are solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). A feasible instance has a rational optimal plan of polynomial encoding length, and such a plan can be computed exactly.

Consequently, additive approximation is also solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\): the exact algorithm already returns a plan meeting every positive additive tolerance.

The proof uses an explicit reduction to rational convex quadratic programming. All dedications retain the real-valued meaning specified in the question.

2. The feasible region

Let
\(I=\{(t,w,j):t\text{ is eligible for }w,\ j\in[w_s,w_e]\}\).
Introduce one variable \(y_{t,w,j}\) for each element of \(I\), and interpret all other dedication coordinates as zero.

Write \(K\) for the polyhedron defined by

\(y_{t,w,j}\geq0\quad((t,w,j)\in I),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{w:(t,w,j)\in I}y_{t,w,j}\leq k_{t,j}\quad(t\in T,\ j\in M),\)

and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,j:(t,w,j)\in I}\mu_t y_{t,w,j}\geq d_w\quad(w\in W).\)

These are exactly the plan constraints. In particular, neither budgets nor targets affect feasibility.

There are at most \(\tau|W||M|\leq\tau m^2\) variables. All constraints have rational coefficients, and their explicit encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Thus rational linear programming decides feasibility in polynomial time.

Furthermore, nonnegativity and each monthly capacity constraint imply
\(0\leq y_{t,w,j}\leq k_{t,j}\).
Hence \(K\) is bounded. It is closed because it is defined by finitely many non-strict linear inequalities. If \(K\) is nonempty, it is compact, so the continuous objective \(F\) attains its minimum.

3. An exact quadratic formulation of the budget penalties

For a scalar \(r\), define
\(r_+=\max\{0,r\}\).
Then
\(\min(0,r)^2=(-r)_+^2\).

For every project \(p\), introduce two nonnegative variables \(u_p,v_p\), with linear constraints

\(u_p\geq b_p-c_p(y),\qquad v_p\geq c_p(y)-b_p.\)

Consider the quadratic objective

\(\displaystyle G(y,u,v)= \alpha\sum_{t,p,j}\mu_t\bigl(z_{t,p,j}(y)-q_{t,p,j}\bigr)^2 +\beta\sum_p u_p^2+\gamma\sum_p v_p^2.\)

For every feasible triple, the auxiliary constraints imply

\(u_p\geq(b_p-c_p(y))_+,\qquad v_p\geq(c_p(y)-b_p)_+.\)

Since squaring is nondecreasing on nonnegative arguments and \(\beta,\gamma\) are nonnegative, it follows that

\(G(y,u,v)\geq F(y).\)

Conversely, every \(y\in K\) has the feasible lift

\(u_p=(b_p-c_p(y))_+,\qquad v_p=(c_p(y)-b_p)_+,\)

for which \(G(y,u,v)=F(y)\). Therefore the original problem and this quadratic program have the same optimal value.

This argument also covers zero weights. It does not require auxiliary variables of an arbitrary optimizer to equal the positive parts.

For completeness, we can make the auxiliary feasible region bounded without changing this equivalence. Define

\(\displaystyle C=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t e_t\sum_{j\in M}k_{t,j}.\)

For any \(y\in K\), positivity of costs and nonnegativity of dedications give

\(\displaystyle 0\leq c_p(y) \leq\sum_t\mu_t e_t\sum_j\sum_w y_{t,w,j} \leq C.\)

Thus both canonical auxiliary coordinates are at most
\(U_p=C+|b_p|\).
Add the bounds
\(u_p\leq U_p,\ v_p\leq U_p\).
Every original feasible plan still has its canonical lift, and the resulting quadratic program has a compact feasible region.

Finally, if \((y^*,u^*,v^*)\) minimizes this program, then

\(\displaystyle \min_{y\in K}F(y) \leq F(y^*) \leq G(y^*,u^*,v^*) =\min_{y\in K}F(y).\)

Consequently, its projected plan \(y^*\) is an exact optimizer of the original problem.

4. Convexity and encoding length

Each target discrepancy is an affine function of \(y\), squared and multiplied by the nonnegative rational coefficient \(\alpha\mu_t\). Each auxiliary penalty is also a square with a nonnegative rational coefficient.

More explicitly, collect all variables into \(x=(y,u,v)\). For rational vectors \(a_\ell\), rational scalars \(h_\ell\), and nonnegative rational coefficients \(\lambda_\ell\), the objective has the form

\(\displaystyle G(x)=\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell(a_\ell^{\mathsf T}x-h_\ell)^2.\)

Its Hessian is

\(\displaystyle H=2\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell a_\ell a_\ell^{\mathsf T}.\)

For every real vector \(r\),

\(\displaystyle r^{\mathsf T}Hr =2\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell(a_\ell^{\mathsf T}r)^2\geq0.\)

Hence \(H\) is positive semidefinite, including when some or all weights vanish. The program is a convex quadratic program with rational linear constraints. No square roots of masses or weights are needed.

Its size is polynomial:

  • It has at most \(\tau|W||M|+2|P|\) variables.
  • It has \(O(\tau|W||M|+\tau|M|+|W|+|P|)\) inequalities.
  • Its objective has at most \(\tau|P||M|+2|P|\) squared affine terms.

All coefficients, including \(C\), arise from polynomially many rational additions and multiplications. Their binary encoding lengths are polynomially bounded: multiplication adds numerator and denominator lengths, and a sum can use the product of its denominators, whose length is at most their total length. Expanding the objective into its rational quadratic matrix therefore also takes polynomial time and space.

The explicit month list and explicit support are essential here. The construction neither enumerates unlisted types nor expands masses into individual researchers.

5. Exact polynomial-time optimization

We use the following classical theorem in the binary Turing model:

Rational convex quadratic programming theorem. Given a rational symmetric positive semidefinite matrix \(H\), rational vectors \(h,a\), and a rational constraint matrix \(A\), a quadratic program minimizing
\(\frac12x^{\mathsf T}Hx+h^{\mathsf T}x\)
subject to \(Ax\leq a\) can be solved exactly in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. In particular, for a nonempty bounded feasible region, the algorithm returns a rational optimal point and the exact rational optimal value.

This is the exact polynomial-time result of [Kozlov, Tarasov, and Khachiyan, *The polynomial solvability of convex quadratic programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189).

Every hypothesis has been verified: the constructed program has rational data, a positive semidefinite Hessian, and, when the planning instance is feasible, a nonempty compact feasible region. A rational constant term in its objective can be omitted during optimization and restored when reporting the value.

Applying the theorem and projecting onto the dedication coordinates yields an exact rational optimal plan in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Its output length is polynomially bounded as well. Evaluating the original formula at that plan gives the exact rational minimum.

6. Additive approximation and hardness

For any promised rational tolerance \(0<\varepsilon<1\), run the exact algorithm. On a feasible instance its output satisfies

\(F(y^*)=\min F\leq\min F+\varepsilon.\)

The optimization routine is independent of \(\varepsilon\), so it satisfies the requested polynomial running-time bound in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\).

Thus both exact optimization and additive approximation admit polynomial-time algorithms. Under the usual polynomial-time reductions, an \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness result for these tasks would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). This establishes binary polynomial-time solvability; it does not assert a strongly polynomial algorithm.
