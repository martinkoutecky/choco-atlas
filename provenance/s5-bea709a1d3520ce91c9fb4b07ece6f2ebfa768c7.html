The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational total masses \(A,Q\) of identical collectors and tasks, with \(Q>A\), a finite initial partition into coalition mass pairs \((a_j,q_j)\), and rational \(\mu,\lambda,\delta\), does every maximal sequence of legal switches terminate after finitely many moves? Fix \(\beta=\tfrac12\) and reliable direct transmission. For a coalition with \(a,q>0\), let \(\rho=q\lambda/(a\mu)\); when \(\rho<1\), let \(D(a,q)=\rho^2/[2a\mu(1-\rho)]\), \(L(a,q)=q\lambda\), \(V(a,q)=\delta L(a,q)^\beta/D(a,q)^{1-\beta}\), and the per-member payoff be \(u(a,q)=V(a,q)/(a+q)\). Give empty or unstable coalitions value zero. A cohort may move any positive rational mass to another coalition or a singleton if its destination payoff is strictly higher; it records the coalition it left, while any remaining mass retains its prior history. Collector mass cannot leave if that would strand positive task mass without collectors. A negative answer means an infinite legal switch sequence exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) denotes support size, not the number of possible types. Here the two types are collectors and tasks, with unnormalized masses \(0<A<Q\), and \(\mu,\lambda,\delta>0\) are binary rationals.
The input also specifies \(k\) coalition mass pairs \((a_j,q_j)\), with nonnegative rational coordinates, \(a_j+q_j>0\), \(\sum_j a_j=A\), and \(\sum_j q_j=Q\). Use \(\tau,k,L\) for running times, where \(L\) is total input bit length; there is no candidate parameter \(m\).
For persistent membership identities, realize each type as a separate half-open interval of length \(A\) or \(Q\), with Lebesgue mass; assign consecutive subintervals to initial coalitions in input order. Coalitions and moving cohorts are finite unions of half-open intervals with rational endpoints; coalition equality means equality of these labeled member sets.
Fix reliable direct transmission and \(\beta=\tfrac12\). For \(a,q>0\) and \(\rho=q\lambda/(a\mu)<1\), define \(D(a,q)=\rho^2/[2a\mu(1-\rho)]\), \(V(a,q)=\delta\sqrt{q\lambda/D(a,q)}\), and payoff per unit mass \(u(a,q)=V(a,q)/(a+q)\). Set \(V=u=0\) for coalitions missing either type or having \(\rho\ge1\).
Each member label has an initially empty history of coalitions it has left. Its preference score for a coalition is zero if that exact member set is in its history, and otherwise is \(u\) evaluated at that coalition's masses.
A switch transfers any positive rational mass of one type from a coalition \(S\) to another existing coalition \(T\), or to a new coalition by taking \(T=\varnothing\). Every moving member must strictly prefer the post-switch destination to the pre-switch source, using its preference scores before the history update; ties forbid a move, and other members' consent is unnecessary.
A collector switch is forbidden if it leaves positive task mass and zero collector mass in the source. Moving members add the entire pre-switch source member set to their histories; all other histories remain unchanged. Delete empty coalitions; histories are never reset, and the environment is fixed.
A sequence is maximal if it is infinite, or finite with no legal switch remaining; impose no fairness requirement or bound on rational denominators. The decision problem asks whether every maximal sequence from the given input is finite; a negative instance admits an infinite legal sequence.
Is this universal finite-termination decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
For finite players, termination of the history-dependent switch process from every initial partition is established, without a polynomial-time bound ([Saad et al.](https://people.cs.vt.edu/~irchen/6204/pdf/Saad-TMC11.pdf)). No result settling this exact continuous decision problem was located.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). In fact, every input either admits no initial legal switch, or admits an infinite legal sequence involving only two initial coalitions, with each moving label moving exactly once.

For \(k\geq 2\), define \(M_j=\max_{i\neq j}a_i\). The exact criterion is:

Every maximal sequence is finite if and only if either \(k=1\), or both of the following conditions hold:

  • \(q_j>0\) for every \(j\);
  • \(\lambda q_j\geq\mu(a_j+M_j)\) for every \(j\).

This criterion requires \(\mathrm{O}(\tau+k)\) rational arithmetic operations and can be evaluated in \(\mathrm{O}((\tau+k)L^2)\) bit operations using elementary integer arithmetic.

1. Payoff depends only on the mass ratio

Write \(c=\mu/\lambda\). For a productive coalition, meaning \(a,q>0\) and \(q/a<c\), direct substitution gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{q\lambda}{D(a,q)}=\frac{2a^2\mu^2(a\mu-q\lambda)}{q\lambda}.\)

Consequently, writing \(r=q/a\),

\(\displaystyle u(a,q)=U(r),\qquad U(r)=\frac{\delta\mu}{1+r}\sqrt{\frac{2(\mu-\lambda r)}{\lambda r}},\qquad 0<r<c.\)

The function \(U\) is strictly decreasing: the positive factor \(1/(1+r)\) decreases strictly, and the expression inside the square root is \(2(c/r-1)\), which also decreases strictly.

Extend \(U\) by zero for \(r\geq c\). This extension is nonincreasing. Thus:

  • For fixed \(q>0\), increasing \(a>0\) cannot decrease the payoff.
  • For fixed \(a>0\), increasing \(q>0\) cannot increase the payoff.
  • Between two productive coalitions, the smaller ratio gives strictly greater payoff.

The exceptional definition \(u(a,0)=0\) remains in force; we will not apply ratio monotonicity across \(q=0\).

A switch to a new coalition is always impossible: its moving cohort contains only one type, so its destination score is zero, while its source score is nonnegative.

2. Two constructions of infinite legal sequences

In both constructions, we transfer disjoint consecutive subintervals of an initial source interval. Therefore, every moving label has an empty history immediately before its move. Its preference scores are exactly the corresponding payoffs.

All cuts below are rational. At every finite step, coalitions remain finite unions of half-open intervals with rational endpoints.

2.1. Task transfers

Let distinct initial coalitions \(S,T\) satisfy

\(\displaystyle q_S>0,\quad a_T>0,\quad \lambda q_T<\mu a_T,\quad a_Tq_S>a_Sq_T.\)

These conditions imply an infinite legal sequence of task transfers from \(S\) to \(T\), including when \(q_T=0\).

Choose the positive rational number

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=\frac12\min\left\{q_S,\ \frac{\mu a_T}{\lambda}-q_T,\ \frac{a_Tq_S-a_Sq_T}{a_S+a_T}\right\}.\)

Every quantity in the minimum is positive. In particular,

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon<q_S,\qquad \lambda(q_T+\varepsilon)<\mu a_T,\qquad a_S(q_T+\varepsilon)<a_T(q_S-\varepsilon).\)

For \(n\geq 0\), set \(s_n=\varepsilon(1-2^{-n})\). At step \(n\geq 1\), transfer the next task subinterval of mass \(s_n-s_{n-1}=\varepsilon 2^{-n}\).

The pre-switch source has masses \((a_S,q_S-s_{n-1})\), and the post-switch destination has masses \((a_T,q_T+s_n)\). The destination is productive because

\(\displaystyle 0<q_T+s_n\leq q_T+\varepsilon<c a_T.\)

Moreover,

\(\displaystyle a_S(q_T+s_n)\leq a_S(q_T+\varepsilon)<a_T(q_S-\varepsilon)\leq a_T(q_S-s_{n-1}).\)

If the source payoff is zero, the productive destination is strictly preferred. Otherwise, \(a_S>0\), and dividing the last inequality by \(a_Sa_T\) shows that the destination has a strictly smaller ratio than the source. It is again strictly preferred.

Each moving label has empty history, so every switch is legal. The source retains positive task mass because \(s_n<\varepsilon<q_S\). This gives infinitely many legal switches.

2.2. Collector transfers

Suppose an initial collector transfer from \(S\) to \(T\), of rational mass \(x_0\), satisfies

\(\displaystyle 0<x_0<a_S,\qquad u(a_T+x_0,q_T)>u(a_S,q_S).\)

The strict inequality implies \(q_T>0\) and a productive post-switch destination.

Perform this first transfer. Put \(R=(a_S-x_0)/2>0\), and then transfer successive disjoint collector subintervals of masses \(R2^{-n}\), for \(n\geq 1\).

The total collector mass transferred is at most

\(\displaystyle x_0+R=\frac{a_S+x_0}{2}<a_S.\)

Thus the source always retains collectors, satisfying the prohibition against leaving tasks without collectors.

Every subsequent destination payoff is at least \(u(a_T+x_0,q_T)\), because collectors are being added while positive task mass stays fixed. Every subsequent source payoff is at most \(u(a_S,q_S)\): removing collectors cannot improve its payoff when \(q_S>0\), and its payoff remains zero when \(q_S=0\).

Hence every subsequent transfer satisfies

\(\displaystyle u(\text{post-switch destination})>u(\text{pre-switch source}).\)

The moving labels again have empty histories. This is an infinite legal sequence.

Both constructions are maximal under the question’s definition, which declares every infinite sequence maximal.

3. Proof of the criterion

3.1. The stated conditions imply termination

If \(k=1\), there is no other existing destination, and a new destination has zero payoff. No switch is legal.

Now suppose \(k\geq 2\), every \(q_j>0\), and

\(\displaystyle \lambda q_j\geq\mu(a_j+M_j)\quad\text{for every }j.\)

Since \(M_j\geq 0\), every initial coalition has zero payoff.

A task transfer to \(T\) increases its task mass without changing its collectors. Since initially \(\lambda q_T\geq\mu a_T\), its post-switch payoff remains zero.

A collector transfer of mass \(x\) from \(S\) to \(T\) satisfies \(x\leq a_S\leq M_T\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle \mu(a_T+x)\leq\mu(a_T+M_T)\leq\lambda q_T.\)

Its post-switch destination also has zero payoff. Equality gives zero payoff by the stated definition, so it does not permit a switch.

A new destination likewise has zero payoff. All histories are initially empty, so no destination is strictly preferred to its source. No first switch exists, and every maximal sequence is finite.

3.2. A coalition without tasks implies nontermination

Suppose \(k\geq 2\) and \(q_T=0\) for some \(T\). Nonemptiness gives \(a_T>0\).

Because \(Q>0\), another coalition \(S\) has \(q_S>0\). Then

\(\displaystyle \lambda q_T=0<\mu a_T,\qquad a_Tq_S>a_Sq_T=0.\)

The task-transfer construction applies. Hence an infinite legal sequence exists.

For the remaining cases, assume every \(q_j>0\).

3.3. A productive initial coalition implies nontermination

Suppose some initial coalition \(C\) is productive. Choose any other coalition \(B\), which exists because \(k\geq 2\).

If \(B\) has zero payoff, then either \(a_B=0\), or \(q_B/a_B\geq c\). In either case,

\(\displaystyle a_Cq_B>a_Bq_C.\)

The task-transfer construction applies from \(B\) to \(C\).

If \(B\) is also productive and its ratio differs from that of \(C\), transfer tasks from the coalition with the larger ratio to the coalition with the smaller ratio. The smaller-ratio coalition satisfies the capacity condition, and the strict ratio comparison is exactly the cross-product condition in Section 2.1.

Finally, suppose both productive coalitions have the same ratio \(r\). Transfer \(x_0=a_B/2\) collectors from \(B\) to \(C\). The destination ratio becomes

\(\displaystyle \frac{q_C}{a_C+a_B/2}<\frac{q_C}{a_C}=r<c.\)

Its payoff is therefore strictly greater than the pre-switch source payoff \(U(r)\). The source retains positive collector mass, and the collector-transfer construction applies.

Thus any productive initial coalition implies an infinite legal sequence.

3.4. Failure of a capacity inequality implies nontermination

It remains to consider partitions in which every coalition has positive task mass and zero payoff. Therefore,

\(\displaystyle \lambda q_j\geq\mu a_j\quad\text{for every }j.\)

Suppose the proposed criterion fails at \(T\):

\(\displaystyle \lambda q_T<\mu(a_T+M_T).\)

Choose \(S\neq T\) with \(a_S=M_T\), and define

\(\displaystyle h=\frac{\lambda q_T}{\mu}-a_T.\)

The two preceding inequalities give \(0\leq h<a_S\). In particular, \(a_S>0\). Choose

\(\displaystyle x_0=\frac{h+a_S}{2},\)

so that \(0<x_0<a_S\) and

\(\displaystyle a_T+x_0>\frac{\lambda q_T}{\mu}.\)

Transferring \(x_0\) collectors makes the destination productive. Its payoff is strictly positive, whereas the source’s initial payoff is zero. The source retains collectors. The collector-transfer construction therefore supplies an infinite legal sequence.

These cases cover every failure of the stated criterion, completing the proof.

4. Algorithm and complexity

For \(k=1\), return yes. Otherwise:

  • If some \(q_j=0\), return no.
  • Find the two largest collector masses at distinct indices, retaining multiplicities in case of ties. These determine every \(M_j\).
  • Return yes exactly when \(\lambda q_j\geq\mu(a_j+M_j)\) for every \(j\).

There are \(\mathrm{O}(k)\) rational comparisons and arithmetic operations. Each comparison can be performed by cross-multiplying integers of \(\mathrm{O}(L)\) bits. Including the support input gives the asserted \(\mathrm{O}((\tau+k)L^2)\) bit bound. Here \(\tau=2\); no enumeration of possible types is involved.

The parameter \(\delta>0\) scales every positive payoff equally and does not affect the criterion.

The cited finite-player proof invokes the finiteness of the partition space. Our infinite sequences instead use fresh cohorts and infinitely many distinct rational cuts, while respecting persistent histories throughout. Thus they do not contradict that finite-player argument. [Saad et al., Theorem 1](https://people.cs.vt.edu/~irchen/6204/pdf/Saad-TMC11.pdf)
