The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of complete types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), and a differentiable coalition-worth function \(V\) with \(V(0)=0\), a gradient oracle, and bounded partial derivatives, estimate \(A_t=\mu_t\int_0^1 \partial_t V(s\mu)\,ds\) for every type. Can reverse-paired samples \(U_j\) and \(1-U_j\) achieve \(\Pr[\|\widehat A-A\|_\infty\le\epsilon]\ge 1-\delta\) with polynomially many oracle calls, and under what conditions do they reduce variance?

Let \(m\) be the maximum encoded type-description length and \(L\) the total rational-input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The estimator admits a uniform polynomial resource bound. Pairing, however, does not uniformly reduce variance: it strictly improves a coordinate exactly when that coordinate’s reflected gradient values have negative covariance.

1. Well-definedness and unbiasedness. Write \(\mu=(\mu_t)_{t\in T}\), and define
\[ \]
\(f_t(s)=\partial_tV(s\mu)\), \(a_t=\int_0^1 f_t(s)\,ds\), and \(\mu_*=\max_{t\in T}\mu_t\).

Differentiability need not make the gradient continuous, so measurability requires justification. Let \(O\) be the open neighborhood on which \(V\) is differentiable. Compactness of the segment \(\{s\mu:0\le s\le1\}\subset O\) permits a sequence \(h_n>0\), tending to zero, such that \(s\mu+h_ne_t\in O\) for every \(s\in[0,1]\). Since \(V\) is continuous, each function

\(\displaystyle s\longmapsto\frac{V(s\mu+h_ne_t)-V(s\mu)}{h_n}\)

is continuous. Their pointwise limit is \(f_t\). Thus \(f_t\) is Borel measurable. The hypothesis \(|f_t(s)|\le B\) makes it Lebesgue integrable and square integrable.

For each coordinate define the independent, identically distributed variables

\(\displaystyle Y_{j,t}=\frac{\mu_t}{2}\bigl[f_t(U_j)+f_t(1-U_j)\bigr].\)

Both \(U_j\) and \(1-U_j\) are uniform on \([0,1]\), so

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E Y_{j,t}=\mu_ta_t=A_t,\qquad \widehat A_t=\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^kY_{j,t}.\)

Consequently the estimator is unbiased. The strong law of large numbers also applies, because the variables are bounded. Since \(T\) is finite, \(\|\widehat A-A\|_\infty\) tends to zero almost surely as \(k\) tends to infinity.

2. Uniform concentration and resource bounds. The gradient bound gives

\(\displaystyle -B\mu_t\le Y_{j,t}\le B\mu_t.\)

Hoeffding’s inequality states that independent variables in an interval of length \(r\) satisfy the two-sided sample-mean bound \(2\exp(-2k\epsilon^2/r^2)\). Applying it here, with \(r=2B\mu_t\), gives

\(\displaystyle \Pr\bigl[|\widehat A_t-A_t|>\epsilon\bigr]\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{k\epsilon^2}{2B^2\mu_t^2}\right).\)

A union bound therefore yields

\(\displaystyle \Pr\bigl[\|\widehat A-A\|_\infty>\epsilon\bigr]\le 2\tau\exp\!\left(-\frac{k\epsilon^2}{2B^2\mu_*^2}\right).\)

This argument requires independence across the sampled pairs; it does not require independence across coordinates. In particular, it suffices to take

\(\displaystyle k=\left\lceil\frac{2B^2\mu_*^2}{\epsilon^2}\log\frac{2\tau}{\delta}\right\rceil.\)

To implement a sufficient sample count using only elementary arithmetic and comparisons, let \(q\) be the smallest positive integer satisfying \(2^{-q}\le\delta/(2\tau)\), and use

\(\displaystyle k=\max\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{2B^2\mu_*^2q}{\epsilon^2}\right\rceil\right\}.\)

Then the failure probability is at most \(2\tau e^{-q}\le2\tau2^{-q}\le\delta\). Repeated halving computes \(q\) in \(O(q)\) operations. Doubling followed by integer binary search computes the ceiling in \(O(1+\log(1+w))\) operations, where \(w=2B^2\mu_*^2q/\epsilon^2\).

Each pair requires two calls to the full-gradient oracle and \(O(\tau)\) arithmetic operations. Hence the resources, including input processing, are

\(\displaystyle 2k\text{ oracle calls},\qquad O(\tau m+L+\tau k+q)\text{ arithmetic and comparison operations}.\)

Since \(\mu_*\le M\),

\(\displaystyle k=O\!\left(1+B^2M^2\epsilon^{-2}\bigl(\log(2\tau)+\log(\delta^{-1})\bigr)\right).\)

These bounds are polynomial in the parameters specified in the question and hold uniformly over every admissible \(V\). No continuity modulus or higher derivative bound is needed.

3. Necessary and sufficient conditions for strict variance reduction. Fix a coordinate \(t\). Define

\(\displaystyle \sigma_t^2=\int_0^1(f_t(s)-a_t)^2\,ds,\qquad c_t=\int_0^1(f_t(s)-a_t)(f_t(1-s)-a_t)\,ds.\)

Thus \(c_t=\operatorname{Cov}(f_t(U),f_t(1-U))\) for a uniform \(U\).

For comparison, let \(W_1,\ldots,W_{2k}\) be independent uniforms and set

\(\displaystyle \widetilde A_t=\frac{\mu_t}{2k}\sum_{i=1}^{2k}f_t(W_i).\)

Independence gives

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Var}(\widetilde A_t)=\frac{\mu_t^2\sigma_t^2}{2k}.\)

The two evaluations inside a pair have equal variance \(\sigma_t^2\) and covariance \(c_t\). Independence between pairs therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Var}(\widehat A_t)=\frac{\mu_t^2}{2k}(\sigma_t^2+c_t).\)

Because \(\mu_t>0\), the exact criterion is

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Var}(\widehat A_t)<\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde A_t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c_t<0.\)

Pairing strictly reduces every coordinate’s variance if and only if \(c_t<0\) for every \(t\in T\). Equality holds precisely when \(c_t=0\), and pairing increases a coordinate’s variance precisely when \(c_t>0\). A coordinate with \(\sigma_t^2=0\) cannot improve strictly.

There is also an equivalent symmetry criterion. Define the centered symmetric and antisymmetric parts

\(\displaystyle e_t(s)=\frac{f_t(s)+f_t(1-s)}2-a_t,\qquad o_t(s)=\frac{f_t(s)-f_t(1-s)}2.\)

They satisfy \(e_t(1-s)=e_t(s)\) and \(o_t(1-s)=-o_t(s)\). Substitution \(s\mapsto1-s\) shows that \(\int_0^1e_t(s)o_t(s)\,ds=0\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sigma_t^2=\int_0^1e_t(s)^2\,ds+\int_0^1o_t(s)^2\,ds,\qquad c_t=\int_0^1e_t(s)^2\,ds-\int_0^1o_t(s)^2\,ds.\)

Strict improvement is therefore equivalent to

\(\displaystyle \int_0^1o_t(s)^2\,ds>\int_0^1e_t(s)^2\,ds.\)

Moreover, Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(|c_t|\le\sigma_t^2\). For a coordinate of positive variance, the paired-to-independent variance ratio lies in \([0,2]\).

4. Smooth examples attaining both extremes. These examples satisfy all hypotheses, including \(V(0)=0\).

First take

\(\displaystyle V(x)=B\sum_{t\in T}\left(\frac{x_t^2}{\mu_t}-x_t\right).\)

Then \(\partial_tV(x)=B(2x_t/\mu_t-1)\), which lies in \([-B,B]\) on \(D\). Along the sampling segment,

\(\displaystyle f_t(s)=B(2s-1),\qquad f_t(s)+f_t(1-s)=0.\)

Thus \(A_t=\widehat A_t=0\) exactly, while

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Var}(\widetilde A_t)=\frac{\mu_t^2B^2}{6k}>0.\)

Pairing strictly improves every coordinate and eliminates its sampling variance.

Conversely, take

\(\displaystyle V(x)=B\sum_{t\in T}\left(\frac{4x_t^3}{3\mu_t^2}-\frac{2x_t^2}{\mu_t}+x_t\right).\)

Now \(\partial_tV(x)=B(2x_t/\mu_t-1)^2\in[0,B]\) on \(D\), and

\(\displaystyle f_t(s)=B(2s-1)^2=f_t(1-s).\)

The integrals of \((2s-1)^2\) and \((2s-1)^4\) over \([0,1]\) are \(1/3\) and \(1/5\), respectively. Hence

\(\displaystyle \sigma_t^2=B^2\left(\frac15-\frac19\right)=\frac{4B^2}{45}>0,\qquad c_t=\sigma_t^2.\)

Pairing therefore doubles every coordinate’s variance at the same number of gradient evaluations.

The cited [Owen paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.0528) combines local antithetic sampling with scrambled nets under smoothness assumptions. The concentration guarantee proved here follows directly from bounded independent pairs.
