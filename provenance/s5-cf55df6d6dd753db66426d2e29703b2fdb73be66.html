The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(P\) and \(Q\), rational masses \((\alpha_p)_{p\in P}\) and \((\beta_q)_{q\in Q}\) each summing to \(1\), strict rankings of opposite-side types, a common strictly increasing rank disutility function \(d\), and a nonnegative nonincreasing rational piecewise-constant weight density \(\lambda\) on \([0,2]\), find a stable mass matching \(x\) minimizing its GGI. A feasible \(x\) has \(x_{pq}\ge 0\), row sums \(\alpha_p\), and column sums \(\beta_q\); it is stable when \(A_{pq}B_{pq}=0\) for every \((p,q)\), where \(A_{pq}=\sum_{q':q\succ_p q'}x_{pq'}\) and \(B_{pq}=\sum_{p':p\succ_q p'}x_{p'q}\). Its disutility measure is \(\eta_x=\sum_{p,q}x_{pq}(\delta_{d(\operatorname{rk}_p(q))}+\delta_{d(\operatorname{rk}_q(p))})\); minimize \(\int_0^2\lambda(s)D_x^\downarrow(s)\,ds\), where \(D_x^\downarrow\) is the nonincreasing quantile function of \(\eta_x\). Determine the computational complexity in the number of types and input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P,Q\) be disjoint nonempty finite type sets, with \(m=\max\{|P|,|Q|\}\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|P|+|Q|\) positive-mass types, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each side has total mass \(1\).
Each type strictly ranks all opposite-side types; \(\operatorname{rk}_p(q)\) and \(\operatorname{rk}_q(p)\) are their ranks, starting at \(1\). Input includes a common table \(0\le d(1)<\cdots<d(m)\) of binary rationals and a nonnegative nonincreasing piecewise-constant density \(\lambda:[0,2]\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\), specified by \(k\) intervals with rational endpoints and values. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A feasible mass matching has real entries \(x_{pq}\ge0\), with \(\sum_qx_{pq}=\alpha_p\) and \(\sum_px_{pq}=\beta_q\). It is stable precisely when \(A_{pq}B_{pq}=0\) for every pair, where \(A_{pq}=\sum_{q':q\succ_p q'}x_{pq'}\) and \(B_{pq}=\sum_{p':p\succ_q p'}x_{p'q}\).
Define \(\eta_x=\sum_{p,q}x_{pq}(\delta_{d(\operatorname{rk}_p(q))}+\delta_{d(\operatorname{rk}_q(p))})\), where \(\delta_a\) is unit point mass at \(a\). Let \(D_x^\downarrow\) be its nonincreasing quantile on the mass coordinate \([0,2]\), without probability renormalization. Minimize \(G(x)=\int_0^2\lambda(s)D_x^\downarrow(s)\,ds\) over stable mass matchings.

Is exact minimization of \(G\) over stable mass matchings solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite individual agents, GGI-optimal stable marriage is NP-hard; a polynomial-time \(2\)-approximation is known. [Gilbert–Spanjaard](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.08453).
A polynomial-time solution is also reported for distributions over discrete stable marriages when GGI aggregates agents' expected disutilities, a different objective and feasible model. [Gilbert–Spanjaard](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.08453). No result settling the exact mass problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted both supports to positive masses and made both type sets nonempty; retained total mass 1 on each side.
  • Made rankings complete and strict, with ranks starting at 1, as in the discrete definition.
  • Specified the disutility function through its finite input table of nonnegative binary rationals; allowed zero because the examples and hardness construction explicitly use d(1)=0.
  • Specified rational breakpoints and values for the weight density and included their encoding in the input bit length; imposed no weight normalization.
  • Made matching entries real and interpreted the quantile on total mass 2 rather than normalizing the disutility measure to a probability distribution.
  • Defined m as the maximum side support size, tau as the total support size, k as the number of density intervals, and L as total input bit length.

Result

Exact minimization is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\), already when both sides have the same number of types and all masses are equal.

The crucial point is that allowing divisible masses does not improve the optimum in this restricted case. This follows from concavity of the objective and the fact that stability is preserved when the support of a matching is reduced.

1. The objective is concave in the matching masses

Write \(d_i=d(i)\), and define
\[ \]
\( \Lambda(t)=\int_0^t\lambda(s)\,ds \quad (0\le t\le 2). \)

Since \(\lambda\) is nonincreasing, \(\Lambda\) is concave: the average value of \(\lambda\) on an interval to the left is at least its average on an interval to the right, so the secant slopes of \(\Lambda\) are nonincreasing.

For every feasible matching \(x\), define the cumulative mass at disutility at least \(d_i\) by
\( C_i(x)=\eta_x([d_i,\infty)), \quad 2\le i\le m. \)

Explicitly,
\( C_i(x)=\sum_{p,q}x_{pq}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{\{\operatorname{rk}_p(q)\ge i\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{\operatorname{rk}_q(p)\ge i\}}\bigr). \)

Thus each \(C_i\) is linear in \(x\). Moreover, \(\eta_x\) has total mass \(2\), because \(\sum_{p,q}x_{pq}=1\).

For almost every mass coordinate \(s\), the descending quantile satisfies
\( D_x^\downarrow(s)=d_1+\sum_{i=2}^m(d_i-d_{i-1})\mathbf{1}_{\{s<C_i(x)\}}. \)

Indeed, the coordinates with disutility at least \(d_i\) occupy precisely the initial interval of length \(C_i(x)\); the expression then telescopes to the appropriate disutility level. Quantile conventions at interval endpoints do not affect the integral.

Consequently,
\( G(x)=d_1\Lambda(2)+\sum_{i=2}^m(d_i-d_{i-1})\Lambda(C_i(x)). \)

Every coefficient \(d_i-d_{i-1}\) is positive. Since \(\Lambda\) is concave and each \(C_i\) is linear, \(G\) is concave on the entire feasible transportation polytope.

In particular, for feasible matchings \(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(r)}\) and nonnegative coefficients \(\theta_j\) summing to \(1\),
\( G\bigl(\sum_j\theta_jx^{(j)}\bigr)\ge\sum_j\theta_jG(x^{(j)}). \)

2. Stability is preserved by reducing support

Lemma. Suppose \(x\) is stable and \(y\) is feasible with
\( \operatorname{supp}(y)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(x), \)
where \(\operatorname{supp}(x)=\{(p,q):x_{pq}>0\}\). Then \(y\) is stable.

Proof. Fix a pair \((p,q)\). Stability of \(x\), together with nonnegativity, implies that either \(A_{pq}(x)=0\) or \(B_{pq}(x)=0\).

If \(A_{pq}(x)=0\), every entry appearing in this sum is zero, since all summands are nonnegative. Support containment makes the corresponding entries of \(y\) zero as well. Hence \(A_{pq}(y)=0\).

The same reasoning applies if \(B_{pq}(x)=0\). Therefore
\( A_{pq}(y)B_{pq}(y)=0 \)
for every pair, proving stability. \(\square\)

3. Equal masses admit an optimal discrete matching

Assume now that \(|P|=|Q|=n\) and
\( \alpha_p=\beta_q=\frac1n \)
for all types.

Given any feasible \(x\), the matrix \(z=nx\) is doubly stochastic: it is nonnegative and every row and column sums to \(1\).

The Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem states that every doubly stochastic matrix is a finite convex combination of permutation matrices. Its hypotheses therefore hold, and we can write
\( z=\sum_{j=1}^r\theta_jM^{(j)}, \qquad \theta_j>0,\qquad \sum_j\theta_j=1. \)

Each permutation matrix in this decomposition is supported on \(z\). To see this explicitly, if \(z_{pq}=0\), then
\( 0=\sum_j\theta_jM^{(j)}_{pq}. \)
Nonnegativity and positivity of the coefficients force every \(M^{(j)}_{pq}=0\).

Set \(x^{(j)}=M^{(j)}/n\). These are feasible matchings, and
\( x=\sum_j\theta_jx^{(j)}. \)

If \(x\) is stable, the support lemma proves that every \(x^{(j)}\) is stable. Furthermore, stability of \(M^{(j)}/n\) is exactly ordinary stability of the corresponding perfect matching: a pair blocks precisely when both endpoints strictly prefer each other to their assigned partners.

Concavity now gives
\( G(x)\ge\sum_j\theta_jG(x^{(j)})\ge\min_jG(x^{(j)}). \)

Thus every stable mass matching has a discrete stable matching with no greater objective value. Conversely, every ordinary stable marriage gives a stable mass matching by assigning mass \(1/n\) to its edges. Consequently,
\( \min_{x\text{ stable mass matching}}G(x)=\min_{M\text{ ordinary stable marriage}}G(M/n). \)

The right-hand minimum exists because ordinary stable marriages exist and there are finitely many perfect matchings.

4. Polynomial reduction from discrete GGI stable marriage

The source problem has \(n\) agents on each side, complete strict preferences, a common strictly increasing nonnegative rational disutility table \(d\), and nonnegative nonincreasing rational weights
\( w_1\ge\cdots\ge w_{2n}\ge0. \)

For a stable marriage \(M\), let \(a_1^\downarrow(M),\ldots,a_{2n}^\downarrow(M)\) be its agents’ disutilities in descending order. Its objective is
\( H(M)=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}w_j a_j^\downarrow(M). \)

Exact minimization of this objective is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\) by [Gilbert–Spanjaard, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.08453). The proof uses a common strictly increasing rational table and nonnegative nonincreasing integer weights. Its numbers have polynomial binary encoding length: for a source instance with \(c\) clauses, the powers occurring in the construction have exponents bounded by \(c+1\), and hence bit length \(O(c\log c)\).

Construct a mass instance by retaining the types, preferences, and disutility table, assigning every type mass \(1/n\), and defining
\( \lambda(s)=w_j \quad\text{for}\quad \frac{j-1}{n}\le s<\frac{j}{n}, \qquad 1\le j\le2n. \)

The value at \(s=2\) can be set to \(w_{2n}\). This density is nonnegative, nonincreasing, and has at most \(2n\) intervals.

For \(x=M/n\), every agent’s disutility contributes mass \(1/n\) to \(\eta_x\). Therefore its descending quantile equals \(a_j^\downarrow(M)\) on the \(j\)-th interval above, almost everywhere. Hence
\( G(M/n)=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{2n}w_j a_j^\downarrow(M)=\frac{H(M)}n. \)

Combining this identity with Section 3 yields
\( \min_{x\text{ stable}}G(x)=\frac1n\min_{M\text{ stable}}H(M). \)

The reduction has \(m=n\), \(\tau=2n\), and \(k\le2n\). Each added mass and endpoint has \(O(\log n)\) bits, so the output length and construction time are polynomial in the source input length. An exact mass optimizer would therefore compute the discrete optimum by multiplying its optimal value by \(n\).

If “binary rational” is intended to require terminating binary expansions for the masses, the reduction still works: first pad the source instance to a power-of-two side size using dummy pairs that rank each other first. Original agents place all dummies last; extend the table increasingly and append zero objective weights. Every discrete stable marriage must match each dummy pair together. The cited hard construction has \(d(1)=0\), so these pairs add only zero disutilities and preserve the objective. The padded size is less than twice the original size, and the resulting equal masses and interval endpoints are dyadic.

Conclusion

The reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hardness}\) for the exact mass problem as stated. A polynomial-time exact algorithm in \(m,\tau,k,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The essential bridge is fully proved: under equal masses, support-preserving decomposition and concavity ensure that divisible stable matchings cannot improve upon the best ordinary stable marriage.
