The continuous mirror question, as graded

For fixed \(m\), \(w\), and \(\bar{\sigma}\), determine the worst-case distortion of SNTV over candidate metric spaces with candidate-distance ratio at most \(\bar{\sigma}\) and finite rational-support voter-location distributions \(\mu\). Each type \(t\) has location \(x_t\), mass \(\mu_t\), and truthful favorite \(a_t\); SNTV selects the \(w\) candidates with greatest ballot masses, using a fixed tie-break. The quantity to determine is the supremum of \(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu}(C_{\mathrm{SNTV}})/\min_{|C|=w}\mathrm{SC}_{\mu}(C)\) over those instances.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix integers \(m\ge2\), \(2\le w\le m\), and a finite real bound \(\bar{\sigma}\ge1\). Candidates \(M=\{1,\ldots,m\}\) occupy distinct points \(y_j\) in an arbitrary metric space \((S,d)\), with \(\sigma=\max_{j\ne k}d(y_j,y_k)/\min_{j\ne k}d(y_j,y_k)\le\bar{\sigma}\).
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types \(t=(x_t,a_t)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Here \(x_t\in S\) and \(a_t\in\arg\min_{j\in M}d(x_t,y_j)\); any nearest candidate is an admissible truthful ballot, and types may share a location.
The support size \(\tau\) counts listed positive-mass types, not possible types. State any running times in \(m,\tau,L,w\), where \(L\) is the total bit length of rational input data.
SNTV elects the \(w\) candidates with greatest ballot masses \(b_j=\sum_{t:a_t=j}\mu_t\), resolving ties by a fixed total order on candidate labels.
For a committee \(C\subseteq M\), define \(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu}(C)=\sum_t\mu_t\min_{j\in C}d(x_t,y_j)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}=\min_{|C|=w}\mathrm{SC}_{\mu}(C)\). Ratios with zero denominator are \(1\) when the numerator is zero and \(+\infty\) otherwise.
Let \(D(m,w,\bar{\sigma})\) be the supremum of \(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu}(C_{\mathrm{SNTV}})/\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}\) over all such metric spaces, candidate locations, and societies of arbitrary finite support.

What is the sharp worst-case approximation ratio \(D(m,w,\bar{\sigma})\) of SNTV in this model?

For finite electorates, SNTV has distortion at most \(1+2\sigma\), uniformly in the electorate size and committee size ([verified source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.09386v2)).

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact worst-case ratio

The sharp ratio is \(D(m,w,\bar{\sigma})=1\) when \(w=m\), and

\(D(m,w,\bar{\sigma})=1+2\bar{\sigma}\) when \(2\le w<m\).

For every \(w<m\), the latter value is attained by a society with exactly \(w+1\) positive-mass types and dyadic rational masses.

1. Upper bound, including zero optimum cost

Let \(C\) be the SNTV committee and \(B\) an optimal committee. Define

\(\delta=\min_{j\ne k}d(y_j,y_k)>0\), \(\Delta=\max_{j\ne k}d(y_j,y_k)\), and \(b(A)=\sum_{j\in A}b_j\).

For each type \(t\), put \(r_t=d(x_t,y_{a_t})\), \(p_t=d(x_t,C)\), and \(q_t=d(x_t,B)\). Truthfulness gives \(r_t\le q_t\).

If \(a_t\in C\), then \(p_t=r_t\le q_t\). If \(a_t\notin C\), choose any \(c\in C\). The triangle inequality gives

\(p_t\le d(x_t,y_c)\le r_t+d(y_{a_t},y_c)\le q_t+\Delta\).

Consequently,

\(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu}(C)\le \mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}+\Delta\bigl(1-b(C)\bigr)\). \[\tag{1}\]

Next, suppose \(a_t\notin B\), and choose \(h\in B\) minimizing \(d(x_t,y_h)\). Since \(a_t\ne h\), truthfulness and the triangle inequality imply

\(\delta\le d(y_{a_t},y_h)\le r_t+q_t\le 2q_t\).

Summing over these types yields

\(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}\ge \dfrac{\delta}{2}\bigl(1-b(B)\bigr)\). \[\tag{2}\]

SNTV selects a committee of maximum total ballot mass, so \(b(C)\ge b(B)\). Combining this with (1) and (2),

\(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu}(C)\le \mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}+\Delta\bigl(1-b(B)\bigr)\le \left(1+\dfrac{2\Delta}{\delta}\right)\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}\le (1+2\bar{\sigma})\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}\).

If \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}=0\), this inequality forces \(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu}(C)=0\), so the stipulated ratio is \(1\). Otherwise, division proves the desired upper bound. This independently establishes the bound stated in the [supplied source, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.09386v2).

2. A construction attaining the bound

Assume \(2\le w<m\), and write \(s=\bar{\sigma}\).

List the candidate labels in their fixed tie-breaking order as \(j_1,\ldots,j_m\), with earlier labels preferred. Set

\(C=\{j_1,\ldots,j_w\}\), \(a=j_1\), \(c=j_2\), and \(\ell=j_{w+1}\).

Thus \(a\ne c\), and \(\ell\notin C\).

Candidate metric and one additional location

Give the candidates the following distances:

  • Every pair of distinct candidates in \(M\setminus\{\ell\}\) has distance \(1\).
  • Candidate \(\ell\) has distance \(s\) from every other candidate.

These distances define a metric. A triangle containing \(\ell\) has side lengths \(s,s,1\), which satisfy the triangle inequalities because \(s\ge1\); all other triangles have side lengths \(1,1,1\).

To add a voter location \(x\), form the complete weighted graph on the candidates with those edge lengths, add edges of length \(1/2\) from \(x\) to \(y_a\) and \(y_c\), and use shortest-path distance.

This operation preserves all candidate distances: any portion of a candidate-to-candidate path passing through \(x\) can be replaced by the edge from \(y_a\) to \(y_c\), of the same length \(1\), or deleted if it returns to the same candidate. Hence it creates no shorter candidate-to-candidate path.

The new distances are therefore

\(d(x,y_a)=d(x,y_c)=1/2\),

\(d(x,y_j)=3/2\) for \(j\in M\setminus\{a,c,\ell\}\), and

\(d(x,y_\ell)=s+1/2\).

In particular, \(a\) and \(c\) are precisely the nearest candidates to \(x\). The minimum candidate distance is \(1\), the maximum is \(s\), and thus \(\sigma=s=\bar{\sigma}\).

Society and election outcome

Choose \(k=\lceil\log_2(w+1)\rceil\) and \(\alpha=2^{-k}\). Then \(\alpha\le 1/(w+1)\), so \(1-w\alpha\ge\alpha>0\).

Use the following types:

| Location | Ballot | Mass |
|---|---|---|
| \(x\) | \(a\) | \(\alpha\) |
| \(y_c\) | \(c\) | \(1-w\alpha\) |
| \(y_j\), for each \(j\in C\setminus\{a,c\}\) | \(j\) | \(\alpha\) |
| \(y_\ell\) | \(\ell\) | \(\alpha\) |

The third row is empty when \(w=2\). There are exactly \(w+1\) types. Their masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to

\(\alpha+(1-w\alpha)+(w-2)\alpha+\alpha=1\).

Every ballot is truthful: candidate-location voters have a unique nearest candidate, while the type at \(x\) may truthfully vote for \(a\).

The ballot masses are

\(b_c=1-w\alpha\ge\alpha\), \(b_j=\alpha\) for \(j\in(C\setminus\{c\})\cup\{\ell\}\), and \(b_j=0\) otherwise.

The fixed tie-breaking order therefore makes SNTV elect exactly \(C\).

Exact costs and optimality

Under \(C\), the type at \(x\) incurs cost \(1/2\), the type at \(y_\ell\) incurs cost \(s\), and every other type incurs zero cost. Hence

\(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu}(C)=\alpha/2+s\alpha\).

Consider \(B=(C\setminus\{a\})\cup\{\ell\}\). It contains \(c\), so the type at \(x\) still incurs cost \(1/2\). Every other listed location is a candidate in \(B\), giving

\(\mathrm{SC}_{\mu}(B)=\alpha/2\).

Moreover, every committee gives the type at \(x\) cost at least \(1/2\), because its distance to every candidate is at least \(1/2\). All other costs are nonnegative. Thus every committee has social cost at least \(\alpha/2\), proving

\(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}=\alpha/2\).

The resulting ratio is exactly

\(\dfrac{\alpha/2+s\alpha}{\alpha/2}=1+2s=1+2\bar{\sigma}\).

Together with the upper bound, this settles every case with \(w<m\).

3. The case \(w=m\)

There is only one feasible committee, namely \(M\). SNTV and the optimum therefore have identical costs. Their ratio is \(1\), including when both costs vanish.
