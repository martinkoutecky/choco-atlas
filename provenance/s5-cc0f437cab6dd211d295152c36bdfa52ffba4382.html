The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a candidate set \(C\), committee size \(k\), and a finite support \(\mathcal T\subseteq 2^C\) of \(\tau\) approval types with positive rational masses \(\mu_A\) summing to \(1\), compute an integral committee \(W\subseteq C\) with \(|W|=k\) such that, for every nonempty \(T\subseteq C\) and \(1\le \ell\le \min(k,|T|)\), \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal T:\,|A\cap T|\ge \ell>|A\cap W|}\mu_A\le |T|/(k+1)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a finite candidate set with \(m=|C|\), and let \(k\in\mathbb Z\) satisfy \(1\le k\le m\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal T\subseteq 2^C\) of \(\tau\) approval types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_A\) summing to \(1\). Empty approval sets are allowed; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An admissible outcome is an integral committee \(W\subseteq C\), \(|W|=k\), satisfying, for every nonempty \(T\subseteq C\) and integer \(1\le\ell\le\min(k,|T|)\),
\(\displaystyle\sum_{A\in\mathcal T:\,|A\cap T|\ge\ell>|A\cap W|}\mu_A\le\frac{|T|}{k+1}.\)
Equality is permitted: a Droop violation requires mass strictly greater than the displayed threshold. Any admissible committee may be returned.

Is constructing an admissible committee solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is this construction problem NP-hard?

For explicitly listed discrete voters, Droop-FJR committees always exist and can be constructed in polynomial time using \(O(kmn)\) rational operations ([Ai](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05417v1)).
A stronger discrete axiom, Droop-FJR+, also has a polynomial-time construction ([Strengthening Full Justified Representation](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11500v1)); no result explicitly treating this binary-rational support construction problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified the omitted committee-size domain as an integer between 1 and m, following the discrete election definition.
  • Explicitly allowed empty approval sets, as the discrete definition imposes no nonemptiness restriction on individual approval sets.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length and retained binary rational support encoding.
  • Allowed any committee satisfying the condition, since the construction question specifies no outcome selection or tie-breaking rule.

Result

The construction problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

We give a direct algorithm on the listed approval types, without expanding rational masses into discrete voters. It uses \(O(km\tau)\) rational operations, with intermediate numbers of \(O(L+k^2\log(k+1))\) bits.

The construction adapts the residual-budget greedy payment cap in [*Strengthening Full Justified Representation*, Section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11500v1). The correctness and bit complexity for rational masses are proved below.

1. Definitions and algorithm

Write \(u_A(X)=|A\cap X|\), and set the candidate price to \(\rho=1/(k+1)\).

Maintain a paid candidate set \(P\), initially empty, and one remaining budget \(r_A\) per listed type, initially \(r_A=\mu_A\). Fix orders on candidates and types.

Process levels \(h=k,k-1,\ldots,1\). At each level, repeat the following while \(|P|<k\):

  • A type is active if \(u_A(P)<h\). Its offer is \(\alpha_A=r_A/(h-u_A(P))\). Inactive types offer zero.
  • For each unselected candidate, compute \(\operatorname{bid}_h(c)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal T:\,c\in A}\alpha_A\).
  • If every unselected candidate has bid strictly below \(\rho\), finish this level.
  • Otherwise choose the first candidate \(c\) with bid at least \(\rho\). Collect exactly \(\rho\) from its active approving types: starting with \(R=\rho\), process these types in order, charge \(x_{A,c}=\min(\alpha_A,R)\), and replace \(R\) by \(R-x_{A,c}\). Other types pay zero.
  • Subtract the charges from the budgets, add \(c\) to \(P\), and recompute all offers before another purchase.

Offers remain fixed during each charging procedure. Affordability ensures that the procedure collects exactly \(\rho\). Every charge satisfies \(0\le x_{A,c}\le\alpha_A\le r_A\), so budgets remain nonnegative.

Stop immediately upon purchasing \(k\) candidates. If fewer are purchased after all levels, add arbitrary unselected candidates to obtain a committee \(W\) of size \(k\).

There is no eligibility requirement on \(|A|\). In particular, empty approval types retain their entire budgets and contribute no bids.

For every state, budget accounting gives

\(\displaystyle \mu_A=r_A+\sum_{c\in A\cap P}x_{A,c},\qquad \sum_{A\in\mathcal T}r_A=1-\rho|P|.\)

2. Historical-payment invariant

Lemma 1. At any state in level \(h\), including immediately after a purchase, every active type satisfies

\(\displaystyle x_{A,c}\le\frac{r_A}{h-u_A(P)}\quad\text{for every previously purchased candidate }c.\)

Here and below, uncharged historical payments are zero.

Proof. Initially all historical payments are zero.

Suppose a purchase occurs at level \(h\), and consider a type that remains active afterward.

If it does not approve the purchased candidate, its budget and utility are unchanged, and its new payment is zero.

Otherwise, let its previous gap be \(d\), its budget be \(r\), its offer be \(a=r/d\), and its new payment be \(x\le a\). Remaining active implies \(d\ge2\). Its new offer satisfies

\(\displaystyle \frac{r-x}{d-1}-a=\frac{a-x}{d-1}\ge0.\)

Thus the new offer dominates both the previous offer and the new payment. By induction it dominates every historical payment.

Finally, when the level decreases from \(h+1\) to \(h\), every type active at the new level was active at the preceding level. Its budget and utility are unchanged, while its positive gap decreases, so its offer cannot decrease.

These transitions prove the invariant. The same calculation applies after the final purchase, even if the algorithm stops there. \(\square\)

3. Accounting for an approved comparison set

Lemma 2. At a state in level \(h\), let \(A\) be active and let \(B\subseteq A\) satisfy \(|B|\ge h\). Then

\(\displaystyle \sum_{c\in B\cap P}x_{A,c}+|B\setminus P|\alpha_A\ge\mu_A.\)

Proof. Put \(u=u_A(P)\), \(q=|B\cap P|\), and \(\alpha=\alpha_A=r_A/(h-u)\).

The type’s total historical payment is \(\mu_A-r_A\). Of its \(u\) approved paid candidates, exactly \(u-q\) lie outside \(B\). Lemma 1 bounds each corresponding payment by \(\alpha\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \sum_{c\in B\cap P}x_{A,c}\ge\mu_A-r_A-(u-q)\alpha.\)

Also, \(|B\setminus P|\ge h-q\). Therefore the expression in the lemma is at least

\(\displaystyle \mu_A-r_A-(u-q)\alpha+(h-q)\alpha=\mu_A-r_A+(h-u)\alpha=\mu_A.\)

\(\square\)

4. The case where all seats are purchased

Suppose the algorithm purchases its \(k\)-th candidate in level \(g\). Its final paid set is then \(W\), and its total remaining budget is exactly

\(\displaystyle \sum_A r_A=1-k\rho=\rho.\)

Lemma 3. A violation for this committee cannot have threshold \(\ell\le g\).

Proof. Suppose such a violation exists. Let

\(\displaystyle S=\{A\in\mathcal T:u_A(T)\ge\ell>u_A(W)\},\qquad M=\sum_{A\in S}\mu_A.\)

The violation assumption is \(M>\rho|T|\).

For every \(A\in S\), its final utility \(u=u_A(W)\) satisfies \(u\le\ell-1<g\). Apply Lemma 1 to the final state in level \(g\), with \(a=r_A/(g-u)\). Budget accounting gives

\(\displaystyle \mu_A=r_A+\sum_{c\in A\cap W}x_{A,c}\le r_A+ua=ga.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle r_A\ge\mu_A\frac{g-u}{g}\ge\mu_A\frac{g-\ell+1}{g}.\)

Since \(S\) is nonempty and its types approve at least \(\ell\) candidates in \(T\), we have \(|T|\ge\ell\). Summing the preceding bounds yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_A r_A\ge M\frac{g-\ell+1}{g}>\rho\ell\frac{g-\ell+1}{g}\ge\rho.\)

The last inequality follows from

\(\displaystyle \ell(g-\ell+1)-g=(\ell-1)(g-\ell)\ge0,\)

because \(1\le\ell\le g\). This contradicts the exact remaining budget \(\rho\). \(\square\)

5. Correctness of the output

Theorem. The algorithm returns an admissible committee.

Proof. Suppose its output \(W\) violates the required inequality for some nonempty \(T\) and allowed threshold \(\ell\). Define \(S\) and \(M\) as in Lemma 3. Then

\(\displaystyle M>\rho|T|.\)

We first show that level \(\ell\) finished because no candidate was affordable.

If fewer than \(k\) candidates were purchased, every level finished this way. Otherwise, let \(g\) be the final purchase level. Lemma 3 gives \(\ell>g\), so level \(\ell\) finished before the algorithm reached level \(g\).

Consider the terminal state of level \(\ell\), and denote its paid set by \(P\). Since \(P\subseteq W\), every type in \(S\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle u_A(P)\le u_A(W)<\ell,\)

so it is active. Apply Lemma 2 with \(B=A\cap T\), whose size is at least \(\ell\). Summing gives

\(\displaystyle M\le\sum_{A\in S}\left(\sum_{c\in A\cap T\cap P}x_{A,c}+|A\cap(T\setminus P)|\alpha_A\right).\)

Now sum the same quantities by candidate. Each candidate in \(T\cap P\) received total payment exactly \(\rho\), so its payments from types in \(S\) total at most \(\rho\). For each candidate in \(T\setminus P\), its offers from types in \(S\) total at most its bid, which is strictly below \(\rho\) at this terminal state. Thus

\(\displaystyle M\le\rho|T\cap P|+\rho|T\setminus P|=\rho|T|.\)

This contradicts \(M>\rho|T|\).

Therefore no strict Droop violation exists. The algorithm returns exactly \(k\) candidates, and equality with the threshold is permitted, as required. \(\square\)

6. Polynomial bit complexity

There are at most \(k\) purchases and at most \(k\) terminal level checks. Each check computes all offers and bids in \(O(m\tau)\) rational operations; charging and updating utilities take \(O(\tau)\) operations. The total is \(O(km\tau)\).

To bound the sizes of those rationals, write each input mass as \(\mu_A=p_A/q_A\), with positive integer denominator \(q_A\). Define

\(\displaystyle D_0=(k+1)\prod_{A\in\mathcal T}q_A,\qquad H=k!,\qquad D_t=D_0H^t.\)

The bit length of \(D_0\) is \(O(L+\log(k+1))\).

We prove that after \(t\) purchases, every budget’s reduced denominator divides \(D_t\). This holds initially. At the next purchase, every active gap is an integer between \(1\) and \(k\), hence divides \(H\). Therefore every offer has denominator dividing \(D_tH\).

The candidate price also has denominator dividing \(D_tH\). During charging, the remaining amount \(R\) is obtained by subtracting offers or earlier remaining amounts. Thus every charge has denominator dividing \(D_tH\), as does every updated budget. This proves the induction.

Bids and intermediate charging amounts likewise have denominators dividing \(D_tH\). All are between zero and one: in particular, each bid is at most the total remaining budget. Using a common denominator, or reducing fractions after each operation, therefore bounds numerator and denominator lengths by

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(L+(k+1)\log H+\log(k+1)\right)=O\!\left(L+k^2\log(k+1)\right).\)

Exact arithmetic and comparisons on numbers of this size take polynomial time. Together with the operation count, this proves polynomial running time in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

The construction and its guarantee are fully proved; no unresolved subclaim is needed.
