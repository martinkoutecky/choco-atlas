The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) resources, \(K\) ordered arrival cohorts with rational masses \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_K\) summing to \(1\), normalized positive demand vectors \(d^1,\ldots,d^K\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^m\), and \(q_k=\sum_{\ell\le k}\rho_\ell\), compute at each epoch \(k\) an irrevocable per-unit allocation \(a^k_\ell=y^k_\ell d^\ell\) maximizing \(M_k\) subject to \(y^k_\ell\ge M_k\), \(y^k_\ell\ge y^{k-1}_\ell\) for \(\ell<k\), and \(\sum_{\ell\le k}\rho_\ell y^k_\ell d^\ell_r\le q_k\) for every resource \(r\); determine whether the rule satisfies continuum analogues of SI, DEF, DPO, and a specified strategyproofness notion in time polynomial in \(m\), \(K\), and encoding length \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

For each cohort \(k\), \(d^k\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^m\) is normalized by \(\max_r d^k_r=1\); a unit mass of that cohort receiving \(y d^k\) has utility \(y\). At epoch \(k\), choose \(y^k_1,\ldots,y^k_k,M_k\) maximizing \(M_k\), subject to \(y^k_\ell\ge M_k\), \(y^k_\ell\ge y^{k-1}_\ell\) for \(\ell<k\), and \(\sum_{\ell\le k}\rho_\ell y^k_\ell d^\ell_r\le q_k\) for every \(r\).

SI means \(y^k_\ell\ge1\). DEF means that if type \(i\) prefers type \(j\)'s epoch-\(k\) allocation, then \(j<i\) and \(y^k_j=y^{i-1}_j\). DPO means no feasible allocation using at most \(q_k\) of every resource Pareto-dominates the epoch-\(k\) allocation. Strategyproofness means no cohort can obtain strictly higher true utility at any epoch by reporting another normalized demand vector, holding all other reports fixed.

Target. Is computing this continuous Dynamic-DRF rule and deciding whether it satisfies SI, DEF, DPO, and strategyproofness solvable in time polynomial in \(m\), \(K\), and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results. For a finite sequence of \(n\) unit-mass agents, Dynamic DRF satisfies SI, DEF, DPO, and strategyproofness and is implementable in polynomial time. No result for the stated cohort-mass formulation was verified.

1. Result and a specification issue

Under the standard DRF interpretation, the cohort formulation is polynomial-time solvable. Positive rational cohort masses do not introduce an integer optimization problem. Moreover:

  • DEF, DPO, and strategyproofness hold.
  • SI for a given demand profile can be checked in polynomial time.
  • SI holds for every demand profile exactly when \(q_k-q_{k-1}\ge \rho_k\) for every \(k\), with \(q_0=0\). In particular, all four properties hold when \(q_k=\sum_{\ell\le k}\rho_\ell\).

I prove these statements directly below, without replacing cohorts by unit agents.

The qualification matters: the question specifies utilities only on bundles proportional to a cohort’s true demand. DEF and strategyproofness also require utilities on other bundles. The proof uses the standard extension

\(u_i(x)=\min_{1\le r\le m}x_r/d^i_r.\)

I also assume that the masses \(\rho_i>0\) and capacities \(q_k\ge0\) are rational input data, and that capacities are nondecreasing and fixed independently of reports. Let \(L\) include their binary encoding lengths. These assumptions make the rule well-defined on every admissible report profile.

2. Unique allocation at each epoch

Fix an epoch \(k\). Write

\(a_{\ell r}=\rho_\ell d^\ell_r,\qquad b_\ell=y^{k-1}_\ell\ \text{for }\ell<k,\qquad b_k=0.\)

For \(t\ge0\), define

\(F_r(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^k a_{\ell r}\max\{b_\ell,t\}.\)

The previous allocation, extended by zero for the new cohort, is feasible: its resource consumption is at most \(q_{k-1}\le q_k\). Thus \(F_r(0)\le q_k\).

Each \(F_r\) is continuous, piecewise linear, and strictly increasing on the nonnegative real numbers. Strict increase follows because the new cohort contributes \(a_{kr}t\), with \(a_{kr}>0\). Also, \(F_r(t)\) tends to infinity. Consequently, there is a unique nonnegative number \(t_r\) satisfying

\(F_r(t_r)=q_k.\)

Set \(t_*=\min_r t_r\). Then the unique optimal allocation is

\(M_k=t_*,\qquad y^k_\ell=\max\{b_\ell,t_*\}.\)

To prove optimality, any feasible allocation with objective value \(M\ge0\) must satisfy \(y_\ell\ge\max\{b_\ell,M\}\). Therefore \(F_r(M)\le q_k\) for every resource, which implies \(M\le t_*\). Conversely, the displayed allocation is feasible and attains \(t_*\).

For uniqueness, choose a resource \(r_*\) with \(t_{r_*}=t_*\). The displayed allocation uses exactly \(q_k\) of that resource. Every feasible optimizer must be componentwise at least the displayed allocation. Increasing any component strictly increases consumption of resource \(r_*\), because every \(a_{\ell r_*}\) is positive. Hence no component can be larger.

In particular, every epoch saturates at least one resource, and

\(y^k_k=M_k,\qquad y^k_\ell=\max\{y^{k-1}_\ell,M_k\}\quad(\ell<k).\)

3. Exact polynomial-time computation

Sort the breakpoints \(b_\ell\). Between consecutive breakpoints, let \(A\) be the indices whose allocations equal the common level \(t\). On that interval,

\(F_r(t)=t\sum_{\ell\in A}a_{\ell r}+\sum_{\ell\notin A}a_{\ell r}b_\ell.\)

Thus the candidate root on that interval is

\(t=\dfrac{q_k-\sum_{\ell\notin A}a_{\ell r}b_\ell}{\sum_{\ell\in A}a_{\ell r}}.\)

The denominator is positive because \(A\) includes the new cohort. Scanning the intervals finds each \(t_r\), after which one takes their minimum. Sorting and scanning require \(O(k\log k+mk)\) rational arithmetic operations per epoch.

It remains to justify polynomial *bit* complexity; iterating rational programs alone would not establish this.

Choose a common denominator \(D\) for all input coefficients \(a_{\ell r}\) and capacities \(q_k\), and write

\(a_{\ell r}=A_{\ell r}/D,\qquad q_k=B_k/D,\)

where the integers involved have polynomial encoding length.

Suppose all previous allocation levels have a common denominator \(H\), so \(b_\ell=V_\ell/H\). A candidate level then has the form

\(t=\dfrac{B_kH-\sum_{\ell\notin A}A_{\ell r}V_\ell}{H\sum_{\ell\in A}A_{\ell r}}.\)

For the selected level, put \(S=\sum_{\ell\in A}A_{\ell r}\). All updated allocations have the common denominator \(HS\). The binary length of \(S\) is polynomial in the input length; hence the binary length of the maintained common denominator grows by only a polynomial amount at each epoch.

Numerators also have polynomial length. Indeed, normalization provides a coordinate with \(d^\ell_r=1\), and feasibility and nonnegativity imply

\(0\le y^k_\ell\le q_k/\rho_\ell.\)

Combining this magnitude bound with the denominator bound proves that every arithmetic operation uses polynomially many bits. The entire allocation sequence is therefore exactly computable in time polynomial in \(m,K,L\).

4. A structural lemma

Repeated application of the update formula gives

\(y^k_i=\max_{i\le s\le k}M_s.\)

Consequently, for \(j>i\),

\(y^k_j\le y^k_i.\)

For \(j<i\), the same recurrence gives

\(y^k_j=\max\{y^{i-1}_j,y^k_i\}.\)

Thus we have the following implication, independent of the masses:

\(y^k_j>y^k_i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad j<i\ \text{and}\ y^k_j=y^{i-1}_j.\)

This lemma supplies both DEF and the central step of strategyproofness.

5. DEF and DPO

For DEF, the utility that cohort \(i\) assigns to cohort \(j\)’s per-unit bundle is

\(u_i(y^k_jd^j)=y^k_j\min_r(d^j_r/d^i_r).\)

Choose a coordinate \(r\) with \(d^i_r=1\). Since \(d^j_r\le1\), the minimum ratio is at most one. Therefore

\(u_i(y^k_jd^j)\le y^k_j.\)

Strict preference for \(j\)’s bundle implies \(y^k_j>y^k_i\). The structural lemma then gives exactly \(j<i\) and \(y^k_j=y^{i-1}_j\), as required.

For DPO, choose a saturated resource \(r_*\). Suppose alternative per-unit bundles \(x^\ell\) Pareto-dominate the allocation. Under the stated utility function, maintaining cohort \(\ell\)’s utility requires

\(x^\ell_r\ge y^k_\ell d^\ell_r\quad\text{for every }r.\)

Strictly improving some cohort \(h\) requires strict inequality for every coordinate of that cohort, since \(d^h_r>0\). Because \(\rho_h>0\), aggregate consumption of resource \(r_*\) would then be strictly greater than

\(\sum_{\ell\le k}\rho_\ell y^k_\ell d^\ell_{r_*}=q_k.\)

This contradicts feasibility. Hence DPO holds.

6. Strategyproofness

Fix a cohort \(i\), its true demand \(d^i\), and an alternative normalized report \(\widehat d^i\). Hold all other reports, masses, and capacities fixed. Use hats for the resulting allocation sequence.

Define

\(c=\min_r\widehat d^i_r/d^i_r.\)

Positivity gives \(c>0\). Choosing a coordinate where \(d^i_r=1\) shows \(c\le1\). Cohort \(i\)’s true utility under its alternative report is

\(u_i(\widehat y^k_i\widehat d^i)=c\widehat y^k_i.\)

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that \(h\ge i\) is the first epoch with

\(c\widehat y^h_i>y^h_i.\)

If \(h=i\), the arrival formula gives \(\widehat y^h_i=\widehat M_h\).

If \(h>i\), cohort \(i\)’s reported allocation must increase at epoch \(h\). Otherwise, by the choice of \(h\) and irreversibility,

\(c\widehat y^h_i=c\widehat y^{h-1}_i\le y^{h-1}_i\le y^h_i,\)

a contradiction. The update formula again gives \(\widehat y^h_i=\widehat M_h\).

Since \(c\le1\) and allocations are nonnegative, it follows that

\(\widehat M_h=\widehat y^h_i>y^h_i.\)

We now show that every other cohort receives at least its original allocation level at epoch \(h\).

  • If \(y^h_j\le y^h_i\), then \(\widehat y^h_j\ge\widehat M_h>y^h_i\ge y^h_j\).
  • If \(y^h_j>y^h_i\), the structural lemma gives \(j<i\) and \(y^h_j=y^{i-1}_j\). The two histories coincide before \(i\) arrives, and the deviating history is irreversible. Therefore \(\widehat y^h_j\ge\widehat y^{i-1}_j=y^{i-1}_j=y^h_j\).

Meanwhile, cohort \(i\)’s resource consumption strictly increases in every coordinate:

\(\widehat y^h_i\widehat d^i_r\ge c\widehat y^h_i d^i_r>y^h_i d^i_r.\)

All other cohorts’ resource consumption weakly increases. The original epoch-\(h\) allocation saturates some resource, so the deviating allocation exceeds its capacity. This contradiction proves strategyproofness at every epoch.

The proof treats the entire positive mass \(\rho_i\) as the deviating cohort.

7. SI: exact checks and a universal characterization

For a fixed profile, the new cohort receives the minimum level \(M_k\). Hence SI at epoch \(k\) is equivalent to \(M_k\ge1\), or equivalently,

\(\sum_{\ell<k}\rho_\ell d^\ell_r\max\{y^{k-1}_\ell,1\}+\rho_kd^k_r\le q_k\quad\text{for every }r.\)

This is an exact polynomial-time check.

For SI on every demand profile, the exact condition is

\(q_k-q_{k-1}\ge\rho_k\quad\text{for every }k,\qquad q_0=0.\)

Sufficiency. Inductively suppose all previous cohorts have utility at least one. Keeping their previous allocations and assigning utility one to the new cohort consumes at most

\(q_{k-1}+\rho_kd^k_r\le q_{k-1}+\rho_k\le q_k\)

of resource \(r\). Thus the epoch program admits \(M_k\ge1\). The same argument starts the induction at epoch one.

Necessity. Consider the admissible profile \(d^\ell=(1,\ldots,1)\) for every cohort. At epoch \(k-1\), saturation means the aggregate allocated amount is exactly \(q_{k-1}\) on every resource. Irreversibility preserves at least that amount. Giving the new cohort utility at least one requires at least \(\rho_k\) additional units of every resource. Therefore SI requires \(q_k\ge q_{k-1}+\rho_k\). At epoch one it requires \(q_1\ge\rho_1\).

Thus, under the explicit assumptions above, deciding universal satisfaction of all four properties reduces to checking these capacity inequalities.

8. What remains unspecified in the literal statement

The utility extension is substantive. Utilities on demand rays alone do not imply strategyproofness.

For example, take two unit-mass cohorts, two resources, \(q_1=1\), \(q_2=2\), and

\(d^1=(1,1),\qquad d^2=(1,1/2).\)

The truthful rule gives both cohorts level one at epoch two. Reporting \(\widehat d^2=(1/2,1)\) also gives both reported levels one.

Now define cohort two’s true utility by \(v_2(x)=2x_2\). This satisfies every specified on-ray equality:

\(v_2(yd^2)=y.\)

Nevertheless, its truthful bundle has utility one, while its misreported bundle has utility two. Thus the on-ray utility specification permits a profitable deviation. Under Leontief utility, the preceding theorem instead proves that no profitable deviation exists.

Accordingly, the mathematical result for the standard cohort model is complete, but applying it unconditionally to the literal question requires confirming the missing utility and input assumptions.
