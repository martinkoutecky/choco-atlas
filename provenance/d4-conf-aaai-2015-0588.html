The continuous mirror question, as graded

Fix \(K \in \{\mathrm{UI\text{-}FP}, \mathrm{UI\text{-}UP}, \mathrm{UI\text{-}CP}\}\). An instance gives \(p\), a finite support \(E\) of labeled comparisons \(e=(\alpha,\beta,v)\) over \(\{0,1\}^p\), positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_e\) summing to \(1\), and a rational threshold \(q \in [0,1]\). Does there exist a tree \(T \in K\) that satisfies comparisons of total mass at least \(q\), that is, \(\sum_{e \in E:\,T\text{ satisfies }e}\mu_e \ge q\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix \(K\in\{\mathrm{UI\text{-}FP},\mathrm{UI\text{-}UP},\mathrm{UI\text{-}CP}\}\). There are \(p\ge1\) binary issues and outcomes in \(\{0,1\}^p\).
A partial lexicographic preference tree is a finite ordered binary tree: each internal node tests an issue and prefers either \(1\) or \(0\); no issue repeats along a root-to-leaf path. An outcome follows the left child for the preferred value and the right child otherwise. Its rank \(r_T(\alpha)\) is the position of its leaf in left-to-right leaf order.
A tree is collapsible if some ordering of its used issues contains every root-to-leaf issue sequence as a subsequence. In \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}FP}\), every path has the same issue sequence and every node prefers \(1\). In \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}UP}\), every path has the same issue sequence and all occurrences of each issue share a preferred value. The class \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}CP}\) consists of the remaining collapsible trees. Paths may omit issues; a single leaf is allowed in the first two classes. There is no tree-size bound.
A comparison type is \(e=(\alpha,\beta,v)\), with distinct outcomes \(\alpha,\beta\) and \(v\in\{0,1\}\). It is satisfied when \(r_T(\alpha)<r_T(\beta)\) for \(v=1\), or \(r_T(\alpha)=r_T(\beta)\) for \(v=0\).
A society is given by its support: the list \(E\) of its \(\tau\) comparison types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_e\), where \(\sum_{e\in E}\mu_e=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. The input also includes a binary-rational threshold \(q\in[0,1]\). Let \(m=p\) denote the number of issues and \(L\) the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

For each fixed \(K\), is deciding whether there exists \(T\in K\) with \(\sum_{e\in E:\,T\text{ satisfies }e}\mu_e\ge q\) solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

The unweighted discrete problem of satisfying at least a given number of comparisons is NP-complete for each of these three classes ([Liu and Truszczynski, 2015](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/9403/9262)).
No separate result for this exact normalized rational-mass formulation was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified that comparison labels belong to {0,1} and that the two outcomes are distinct, as required by the discrete definition.
  • Defined the tree classes through actual binary trees, so conditional preference tables cannot describe structures that fail to correspond to a valid collapsible tree.
  • Preserved the stated residual meaning of UI-CP: collapsible trees outside UI-UP.
  • Allowed omitted issues and the single-leaf tree where the class permits them, and imposed no tree-size or conditional-dependency bound.
  • Took p to be a positive integer and used m=p to express running times under the required society convention; tau counts supported comparisons.
  • Defined L as the total binary input length, including masses and threshold.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

For every fixed \(K\in\{\mathrm{UI\text{-}FP},\mathrm{UI\text{-}UP},\mathrm{UI\text{-}CP}\}\), the decision problem is NP-complete, and hence NP-hard. Normalization of the masses does not remove the unweighted hardness.

1. The known result and its applicability

We use the supplied unweighted NP-completeness result: given a set \(E\) of distinct comparison types and an integer \(k\), with \(1\le k\le |E|\), deciding whether some \(T\in K\) satisfies at least \(k\) members of \(E\) is NP-complete.

This is Theorem 6 of [Liu and Truszczynski (2015)](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/9403/9262). Its definitions match the present task: binary issues, strict and indifference comparisons, collapsibility, and the three stated preference classes. In particular, the paper defines \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}CP}\) as the remaining collapsible trees.

The reduction below uses this theorem as its hardness premise.

2. Reduction to normalized rational masses

Fix \(K\), and take an unweighted instance \((E,k)\), where \(\tau=|E|\). Retain exactly the same issues and comparison types, and set

\(\mu_e=1/\tau\quad(e\in E),\qquad q=k/\tau.\)

All masses are positive and sum to \(1\). For every admissible tree \(T\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{e\in E:\,T\text{ satisfies }e}\mu_e =\frac{|\{e\in E:T\text{ satisfies }e\}|}{\tau}.\)

Consequently, the weighted score is at least \(q\) if and only if \(T\) satisfies at least \(k\) comparisons. The admissible trees are unchanged, so this equivalence holds separately for each fixed \(K\).

If rationals are represented by binary numerators and denominators, each new mass and the threshold require \(O(\log(\tau+1))\) bits. Thus this is a polynomial-time many-one reduction.

The following construction also handles the stricter interpretation that “binary-rational” means a dyadic rational, with a finite binary expansion.

Enumerate \(E=\{e_1,\ldots,e_\tau\}\). Let \(D\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(D\ge\tau^2\), and write

\(D=a\tau+b,\qquad a=\lfloor D/\tau\rfloor,\qquad 0\le b<\tau.\)

Then \(a\ge\tau\). Assign

\(\mu_{e_i}=(a+1)/D\) for \(1\le i\le b\), and \(\mu_{e_i}=a/D\) otherwise, and set \(q=ka/D\).

These masses are positive dyadic rationals, and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_{e_i} =\frac{b(a+1)+(\tau-b)a}{D}=1.\)

Also, \(0<q\le1\), since \(k\le\tau\) and \(a\tau\le D\).

Suppose \(T\) satisfies exactly \(t\) comparisons.

  • If \(t\ge k\), its score is at least \(ta/D\ge ka/D=q\).
  • If \(t\le k-1\), its score is at most \((k-1)(a+1)/D<ka/D=q\). The strict inequality follows from

\(ka-(k-1)(a+1)=a-(k-1)\ge\tau-k+1\ge1.\)

Thus the reduction is valid even with exclusively dyadic masses and threshold.

Because \(D\le2\tau^2\), all added numbers have \(O(\log(\tau+1))\) bits. With explicit outcome strings, this construction takes \(O(m\tau+\tau\log^2(\tau+1))\) bit operations using elementary arithmetic, and produces an instance of length \(O(m\tau+\tau\log(\tau+1))\).

This proves NP-hardness for all three classes.

3. Polynomial certificates despite unbounded tree size

We now prove membership in NP for arbitrary input masses.

For \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}FP}\), a certificate is the common sequence of distinct issues. For \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}UP}\), it additionally specifies one preferred bit per issue. Each certificate describes the corresponding full binary tree implicitly.

To compare two outcomes, inspect this sequence until the first issue on which they differ. Its preferred bit determines their strict order. If no selected issue distinguishes them, they reach the same leaf. Each comparison therefore requires at most \(m\) issue inspections.

For \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}CP}\), we need a compression argument that preserves both the comparisons and membership in the residual class.

Compression lemma. If a tree \(T\in\mathrm{UI\text{-}CP}\) exists, there is a tree \(T'\in\mathrm{UI\text{-}CP}\) with at most \(2(2\tau+2)m+1\) nodes that induces exactly the same strict-order and equality relations on all comparisons in \(E\).

Proof. Let \(S\) contain all outcomes appearing in \(E\), so \(|S|\le2\tau\). Select every root-to-leaf path followed by an outcome in \(S\).

Since \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}FP}\subseteq\mathrm{UI\text{-}UP}\), the tree \(T\) fails at least one defining condition of \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}UP}\):

  • Two root-to-leaf paths have different issue sequences; or
  • Two nodes testing the same issue have different preferred bits.

In the first case, also select those two paths. In the second case, select a root-to-leaf path through each of the two nodes. Thus at most \(2\tau+2\) paths have been selected.

Replace every subtree containing no selected leaf by a single leaf. Call the resulting tree \(T'\). Every selected path remains unchanged.

There are at most \((2\tau+2)m\) retained internal nodes: each belongs to a selected path, and each such path contains at most \(m\) internal nodes. A full binary tree with \(n\) internal nodes has \(2n+1\) nodes, giving the claimed bound.

Every path of \(T'\) is an original path or an initial segment of one. Hence the original issue ordering witnesses collapsibility of \(T'\), after restriction to its used issues.

The selected witnesses to failure of \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}UP}\) survive unchanged. Therefore \(T'\) remains in \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}CP}\).

Finally, two outcomes in \(S\) that originally reached the same leaf still do so. If they reached different leaves, their first branching node remains, and their left-to-right order is preserved. Thus every comparison in \(E\) has exactly its original satisfaction status. \(\square\)

A certificate for this class consists of \(T'\) and an ordering of its used issues. The verifier checks the tree structure, increasing issue positions along each path, and failure of the \(\mathrm{UI\text{-}UP}\) conditions. It then evaluates all supported comparisons.

4. Exact verification and conclusion

After determining the satisfied comparisons, the verifier sums their masses exactly and compares the sum with \(q\).

A common denominator obtained by multiplying the input denominators has bit length \(O(L)\): its bit length is bounded by the sum of their encoded bit lengths. Consequently, elementary integer arithmetic suffices for exact verification in polynomial time.

Combining certificate construction, structural checks, comparison evaluation, and arithmetic, a straightforward implementation has the conservative verification bound

\(O\bigl((m(\tau+1)+L)^3\bigr)\)

in bit operations. This establishes membership in NP for every fixed \(K\).

Together with the reduction, all three problems are NP-complete. A polynomial-time algorithm for any one of them would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
