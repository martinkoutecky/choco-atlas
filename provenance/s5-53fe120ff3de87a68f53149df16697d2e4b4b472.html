The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given ordered service times \(s=1,\ldots,q\), types \(t\in T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), and each type’s jobs, earliest feasible times, and per-operator endowments \(h_t(s)\), define aggregate supply \(b_s=\sum_t\mu_t h_t(s)\). Decide whether there is a feasible allocation \(x_{t\pi}\) of type mass to whole-job schedules \(\pi\) such that no positive-mass coalition can reassign its selected members to schedules using only their collective initial endowment, with every participating operator weakly earlier on every job and strictly earlier on at least one; return such an allocation or report that none exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Service slots are \(S=\{1,\ldots,q\}\), ordered from earliest to latest. Type \(t\) specifies a finite set of labeled jobs \(J_t\), earliest feasible slots \(e_{tj}\in S\), and initial ownership indicators \(h_t(s)\in\{0,1\}\). Empty job sets are permitted.
A whole-job schedule \(\pi\in\mathcal P_t\) is an injective map \(J_t\to S\) satisfying \(\pi(j)\ge e_{tj}\). Each type also supplies an initial schedule \(\pi_t^I\in\mathcal P_t\) with \(h_t(\pi_t^I(j))=1\) for every job.
Write \(a_{t\pi s}=|\{j\in J_t:\pi(j)=s\}|\) and \(b_s=\sum_t\mu_t h_t(s)\). Assume excess supply: \(\sum_t\mu_t|J_t|<\sum_s b_s\).
An allocation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t\pi}\) satisfying \(\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P_t}x_{t\pi}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,\pi}x_{t\pi}a_{t\pi s}\le b_s\) for every slot; unused capacity is allowed.
Write \(\rho\prec_t\pi\) when \(\rho(j)\le\pi(j)\) for every job and the inequality is strict for at least one job. Schedules with opposing job improvements are incomparable.
A blocking coalition is a nonnegative real array \(y_{t\pi\rho}\), supported only on \(\rho\prec_t\pi\), with \(\sum_{t,\pi,\rho}y_{t\pi\rho}>0\), \(\sum_\rho y_{t\pi\rho}\le x_{t\pi}\), and \(\sum_{t,\pi,\rho}y_{t\pi\rho}a_{t\rho s}\le\sum_{t,\pi,\rho}y_{t\pi\rho}h_t(s)\) for every slot. Thus selected operators may pool only their initial endowments, and every participant must strictly improve. An allocation is in the weak core if no such coalition exists.
Is deciding whether a weak-core allocation exists in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? Can an allocation, when one exists, be constructed in time polynomial in \(m=\sum_t|J_t|\), \(\tau\), \(q\), and the total input bit length \(L\)?
For finite societies, TradeCycle always returns a weak-core schedule ([Schummer–Vohra](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/schummer/ftp/research/slots/slots.pdf)). No result settling this exact mass-allocation problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized positive type masses to sum to one; aggregate capacities use the same normalization.
  • Interpreted each service time as a distinct slot, with binary per-operator ownership and injective whole-job schedules, matching the discrete slot model.
  • Restored a feasible initial schedule within each operator's owned slots and the strict excess-supply assumption.
  • Allowed empty job sets, as the discrete model does; such operators cannot participate in a coalition requiring every participant to strictly improve.
  • Required strict improvement for every participating operator, with no job becoming later, rather than requiring only one improving coalition member.
  • Formalized selection and reassignment by masses indexed by type, current schedule, and replacement schedule; coalition masses may be arbitrary nonnegative reals.
  • Used rational allocation masses for finite binary output, and allowed unused capacity.
  • Interpreted coalition feasibility as a partial reassignment of participating operators, consistent with the anchor's proof, without requiring a reassignment of outsiders.
  • Defined m as the total number of jobs listed across support types and L as the total input bit length.

Status and proved results

I do not settle the general $\mathrm P$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness question. I establish three further results:

  • General decision and construction admit an algorithm running in

$$ 2^{O(q^2\log(q+1))}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$
Thus both are polynomial-time solvable for every fixed number of slots.

  • A weak-core allocation can be constructed in polynomial time when the initial ownership sets are linearly ordered by inclusion, with arbitrarily many jobs per type.
  • Weak-core membership of an explicitly listed allocation has a polynomial-size linear-programming formulation. This removes the separation-oracle theorem needed in the previous argument.

I also give self-contained proofs of the supporting price characterization and the $\mathrm{NP}$ upper bound. All results concern whole-job schedules injective into the original slot set.

1. Blocking depends only on support

Write
$$ H_t=\{s\in S:h_t(s)=1\},\qquad n_t=|J_t|. $$
The supplied initial schedule implies
$$ n_t\le |H_t|\le q. $$

For an allocation $x$, let
$$ A(x)=\{(t,\pi):x_{t\pi}>0\}. $$
For every supported source schedule and strict improvement, define
$$ d_{t\pi\rho}=a_{t\rho}-h_t\in\{-1,0,1\}^q. $$
Here vectors are indexed by slots, and vector inequalities are componentwise.

Lemma 1. An allocation $x$ is blocked if and only if there exist nonnegative real coefficients $\lambda_{t\pi\rho}$, indexed by $(t,\pi)\in A(x)$ and $\rho\prec_t\pi$, such that
$$ \sum_{t,\pi,\rho}\lambda_{t\pi\rho}>0, \qquad \sum_{t,\pi,\rho}\lambda_{t\pi\rho}d_{t\pi\rho}\le 0. $$

Proof. A blocking coalition supplies these coefficients directly. A source outside $A(x)$ cannot participate because its participant bound is zero.

Conversely, suppose such coefficients exist. Set
$$ g_{t\pi}=\sum_{\rho\prec_t\pi}\lambda_{t\pi\rho}. $$
There are finitely many schedules. Every source with $g_{t\pi}>0$ has $x_{t\pi}>0$, so
$$ \varepsilon= \min_{g_{t\pi}>0}\frac{x_{t\pi}}{g_{t\pi}} $$
is positive. Taking $y_{t\pi\rho}=\varepsilon\lambda_{t\pi\rho}$ satisfies all participant bounds and resource inequalities, and its total mass is positive. Hence it blocks $x$. $\square$

Consequently, reducing an allocation’s support cannot create a blocking coalition, provided the new masses still form a feasible allocation.

2. Integer price certificates, with a zero coordinate

Proposition 2. An allocation $x$ is in the weak core if and only if there exists $p\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^q$ satisfying
$$ p\cdot(a_{t\rho}-h_t)>0 $$
for every $(t,\pi)\in A(x)$ and every $\rho\prec_t\pi$.

For $q\ge 2$, whenever such a certificate exists, one exists with
$$ p\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^q,\qquad \min_s p_s=0,\qquad p_s\le (q-1)! $$
for every slot.

These prices are certificates against blocking; no budget condition is imposed on the allocated schedules themselves.

Proof of the characterization. Let $\mathcal D$ be the finite collection of improvement vectors from supported sources. If $\mathcal D$ is empty, there is no possible participant in a blocking coalition, and $p=0$ is a certificate.

Suppose $\mathcal D$ is nonempty. A certificate rules out blocking by Lemma 1: every nonzero nonnegative combination of vectors in $\mathcal D$ has positive scalar product with $p$, whereas a componentwise nonpositive vector has nonpositive scalar product with $p$.

For the converse, suppose $x$ is unblocked. Lemma 1 implies that
$$ K=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal D) $$
is disjoint from $\mathbb R_{\le 0}^q$. Indeed, a point in their intersection would give coefficients in Lemma 1 summing to one.

Minimize the continuously differentiable function
$$ f(k)=\sum_{s=1}^q\max\{k_s,0\}^2 $$
over the compact set $K$, obtaining a minimizer $k^*$. Define
$$ p_s=\max\{k_s^*,0\}. $$
Disjointness gives $p\ne 0$. For any $d\in\mathcal D$, the segment from $k^*$ to $d$ lies in $K$. The directional derivative at its minimizing endpoint is nonnegative, so
$$ p\cdot(d-k^*)\ge 0. $$
Since
$$ p\cdot k^*=\sum_s p_s^2>0, $$
we obtain $p\cdot d>0$ for every $d\in\mathcal D$. $\square$

Proof of the integer bound. Every improvement vector satisfies
$$ \sum_s d_{t\pi\rho,s}=n_t-|H_t|\le 0. $$
Given a separating vector $p$, put $c=\min_s p_s$ and $\widetilde p=p-c\mathbf 1$. Then $\widetilde p\ge 0$, some coordinate $s_0$ is zero, and
$$ \widetilde p\cdot d =p\cdot d-c\sum_s d_s \ge p\cdot d>0 $$
for every improvement vector.

For nonempty $\mathcal D$, scale $\widetilde p$ so that $d\cdot\widetilde p\ge 1$ for every $d\in\mathcal D$. Consider
$$ Q_{s_0}= \{v\ge 0:v_{s_0}=0,\ d\cdot v\ge 1\text{ for every }d\in\mathcal D\}. $$
This polyhedron is nonempty. Minimizing $\sum_s v_s$ attains a minimum: its intersection with a sublevel set determined by any feasible point is compact. Its minimizing face is compact and has an extreme point, which is also an extreme point of $Q_{s_0}$.

Delete coordinate $s_0$, leaving dimension $r=q-1$. At an extreme point, the active constraint normals span $\mathbb R^r$: otherwise a nonzero direction orthogonal to all active normals would permit sufficiently small perturbations in both directions, contradicting extremality.

Choose $r$ independent active constraints. Their matrix $A$ has entries in $\{-1,0,1\}$ and their right-hand side has entries in $\{0,1\}$. By Cramer’s rule, multiplying the extreme point by $|\det A|$ produces integer coordinates. The determinant expansion bounds both $|\det A|$ and every numerator determinant by $r!$. Thus the resulting vector $P$ satisfies
$$ 0\le P_s\le r!,\qquad P_{s_0}=0,\qquad d\cdot P\ge|\det A|\ge 1. $$
For empty $\mathcal D$, use $P=0$. $\square$

When $q=1$, every feasible job set has size at most one, and every type has a unique feasible schedule. There are no strict improvements, so the supplied initial allocation is already in the weak core.

3. A polynomial-size LP for checking a listed allocation

I use the following standard algorithmic theorem: feasibility of an explicitly given finite system of rational linear equalities and inequalities is decidable in time polynomial in its total binary encoding length. When feasible, a rational feasible point can be returned in polynomial time. This theorem applies to unbounded and lower-dimensional polyhedra. Exact optimization with finite attained optimum also has polynomial bit complexity.

Fix a supported schedule $\pi$ of type $t$ and a job $j_0\in J_t$. Define a bipartite graph with jobs on one side and slots on the other. Its allowed edges satisfy
$$ e_{tj}\le s\le\pi(j), $$
with the additional restriction
$$ s<\pi(j_0) $$
for job $j_0$.

Call this graph $G_{t\pi j_0}$. Its job-covering matchings are precisely the feasible schedules that weakly improve every job and strictly improve job $j_0$. Thus
$$ \{\rho:\rho\prec_t\pi\} = \bigcup_{j_0\in J_t} \{\text{job-covering matchings of }G_{t\pi j_0}\}. $$

First discard graphs without a job-covering matching; their corresponding conditions are vacuous. For each remaining graph, consider the assignment LP
$$ \min\sum_{(j,s)\in E(G)}p_s z_{js} $$
subject to
$$ \sum_{s:(j,s)\in E(G)}z_{js}=1\quad(j\in J_t), $$
$$ \sum_{j:(j,s)\in E(G)}z_{js}\le 1\quad(s\in S), \qquad z_{js}\ge 0. $$

Its feasible polytope is integral. To verify this, introduce nonnegative slack variables for slot constraints and negate the job rows. Each edge column then has one entry $-1$ and one entry $1$, and each slack column has a single entry $1$.

This matrix is totally unimodular. For any square submatrix, a column with at most one nonzero permits determinant expansion and induction. If every column has two nonzeros, they are opposite, so the sum of the rows is zero and the determinant vanishes. Consequently every nonsingular basis has determinant $1$ or $-1$. The integer right-hand side therefore makes every vertex integral.

The feasible polytope is bounded, so its minimum is attained at an integral vertex and equals the minimum cost of a whole-job matching.

The dual has unrestricted variables $\alpha_j$ and nonnegative variables $v_s$:
$$ \max\left(\sum_j\alpha_j-\sum_s v_s\right) $$
subject to
$$ \alpha_j-v_s\le p_s\qquad((j,s)\in E(G)). $$
The primal is feasible and bounded, so linear-programming duality gives an attained dual optimum.

It follows that every matching in this graph has cost at least $p\cdot h_t+1$ if and only if there exist dual variables satisfying
$$ \alpha_j-v_s\le p_s\qquad((j,s)\in E(G)), $$
$$ v_s\ge 0,\qquad \sum_j\alpha_j-\sum_s v_s\ge p\cdot h_t+1. $$

Construct one common price vector $p\ge 0$ and a separate block of dual variables for every retained graph $G_{t\pi j_0}$. Impose the displayed inequalities for every block.

Proposition 3. This explicitly constructed LP is feasible exactly when the listed allocation is in the weak core.

Proof. If the LP is feasible, weak duality shows that every strict improvement from every supported source has price at least $p\cdot h_t+1$. Proposition 2 rules out blocking.

Conversely, if the allocation is unblocked, Proposition 2 supplies prices giving strictly positive margins for all improvements. There are finitely many improvements, so scaling makes all margins at least one. Strong duality supplies the required dual variables for every retained graph. $\square$

If the allocation lists $R$ positive schedule masses, there are at most $Rq$ graph blocks. The LP has $O(q+Rq^2)$ variables and $O(Rq^3)$ constraints, with small integer coefficients. Constructing the graphs and detecting job-covering matchings are polynomial-time operations.

Thus membership is polynomial-time decidable in the society’s encoding length and the length of the explicitly listed allocation. No implicit-polyhedron feasibility theorem is needed.

4. Sparse rational witnesses and the $\mathrm{NP}$ upper bound

Proposition 4. If a weak-core allocation exists, one exists with at most $\tau+q$ positive schedule masses and polynomial encoding length. Hence general weak-core existence belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

Proof. Suppose $x$ is a weak-core allocation. Restrict variables to its support and introduce capacity slacks $z_s\ge 0$:
$$ \sum_{\pi:(t,\pi)\in A(x)}x'_{t\pi}=\mu_t\qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau), $$
$$ \sum_{(t,\pi)\in A(x)}x'_{t\pi}a_{t\pi s}+z_s=b_s \qquad(s=1,\ldots,q). $$
This system is feasible. It is bounded because
$$ 0\le x'_{t\pi}\le\mu_t,\qquad 0\le z_s\le b_s. $$
It therefore has a vertex.

At a vertex, the columns corresponding to positive variables must be linearly independent. Otherwise a nonzero kernel direction supported on positive variables would permit small feasible perturbations in both directions. Since there are $\tau+q$ equations, at most $\tau+q$ variables are positive, including slacks.

The new allocation’s support is contained in $A(x)$, so Lemma 1 shows that it remains unblocked.

For its encoding length, let $D$ be the product of the input mass denominators. Then $\log D\le L$, and every coordinate of the right-hand side, after multiplication by $D$, is an integer between $0$ and $D$.

Choose a nonsingular basis matrix of rank $r\le\tau+q$, using independent equation rows. Its entries belong to $\{0,1\}$. Its determinant has absolute value at most $r!$. Replacing one column by the scaled right-hand side gives a determinant of absolute value at most $Dr!$, because each determinant term contains exactly one right-hand-side entry.

Cramer’s rule therefore bounds numerators and denominators of basic masses by $Dr!$. Their bit lengths are polynomial in $L+\tau+q$.

Each schedule lists at most $q$ slot indices. Proposition 2 also supplies a polynomial-length integer price certificate. A verifier checks allocation feasibility directly and checks all price inequalities by the matching problems from Section 3. All these operations take polynomial time in the certificate and input lengths. $\square$

5. Decision and construction with the number of slots as parameter

Theorem 5. Weak-core existence can be decided, and a weak-core allocation can be constructed when one exists, in time
$$ 2^{O(q^2\log(q+1))}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

Proof. The case $q=1$ was settled in Section 2. Assume $q\ge 2$ and put
$$ B=(q-1)!. $$

Enumerate every integer vector satisfying
$$ 0\le p_s\le B,\qquad \min_s p_s=0. $$
There are at most
$$ q(B+1)^{q-1} $$
such vectors: choose a zero coordinate and enumerate the others, allowing duplicates.

For each vector $p$, enumerate every feasible schedule of every type. Since $n_t\le q$, each type has at most
$$ \frac{q!}{(q-n_t)!}\le q! $$
injective schedules.

Retain precisely the schedules in
$$ A_t(p)= \left\{ \pi\in\mathcal P_t: p\cdot(a_{t\rho}-h_t)\ge 1 \text{ for every }\rho\prec_t\pi \right\}. $$
This set can be computed by enumerating pairs of feasible schedules and testing componentwise improvement. The matching method in Section 3 is another way to perform these tests.

Now solve the explicit allocation LP using only retained schedules:
$$ x_{t\pi}\ge 0\qquad(\pi\in A_t(p)), $$
$$ \sum_{\pi\in A_t(p)}x_{t\pi}=\mu_t, $$
$$ \sum_t\sum_{\pi\in A_t(p)}x_{t\pi}a_{t\pi s}\le b_s \qquad(s\in S). $$
It has at most $\tau q!$ variables and $\tau+q$ type and capacity constraints.

If this LP is feasible, return a rational feasible allocation. Every supported schedule satisfies the price inequalities, so Proposition 2 proves that the returned allocation is in the weak core.

If a weak-core allocation exists, Proposition 2 gives an enumerated integer price vector certifying it. Every schedule in its support belongs to $A_t(p)$, so the allocation LP for that vector is feasible. Therefore rejecting all vectors correctly certifies nonexistence.

Finally,
$$ \log\bigl(q(B+1)^{q-1}\bigr) =O(q^2\log(q+1)), $$
and
$$ \log(q!)=O(q\log(q+1)). $$
Schedule enumeration, pairwise improvement tests, and each explicitly represented rational LP thus give the claimed running time. $\square$

This algorithm uses the listed support types directly; it does not expand masses into individual operators.

6. Polynomial construction for nested ownership sets

The following result allows arbitrary $q$, $\tau$, and job counts.

Theorem 6. Suppose the family of ownership sets is linearly ordered by inclusion:
$$ H_t\subseteq H_u\quad\text{or}\quad H_u\subseteq H_t \qquad\text{for every }t,u. $$
Then a weak-core allocation can be constructed in time polynomial in $m,\tau,q,L$.

Construction. For each type $t$, choose a feasible schedule using only $H_t$ that minimizes
$$ C_t(\pi)=\sum_{j\in J_t}\pi(j). $$
The supplied initial schedule guarantees feasibility. This is a minimum-cost job-covering bipartite matching problem, with edges
$$ (j,s)\quad\text{when}\quad s\in H_t,\ s\ge e_{tj}, $$
and edge cost $s$. Section 3’s integral assignment LP and polynomial-time rational LP theorem give a polynomial-time construction.

Call the resulting schedule $\pi_t$, and allocate
$$ x_{t\pi_t}=\mu_t. $$
For empty job sets, use the unique empty schedule.

Allocation feasibility. Each $\pi_t$ uses owned slots and is injective. Hence
$$ a_{t\pi_t s}\le h_t(s), $$
so
$$ \sum_t\mu_t a_{t\pi_t s} \le \sum_t\mu_t h_t(s)=b_s. $$

Core proof. Suppose a blocking coalition exists. Let
$$ T^+= \left\{ t:\sum_{\rho\prec_t\pi_t}y_{t\pi_t\rho}>0 \right\}. $$
This set is nonempty. Since the ownership sets form a finite chain, choose $t^*\in T^+$ whose ownership set is maximal among the participating types. Then
$$ H_t\subseteq H_{t^*}\qquad(t\in T^+). $$

At every slot outside $H_{t^*}$, coalition ownership is zero. The resource inequalities and nonnegativity therefore force coalition consumption there to be zero. Thus every alternative schedule with positive coalition weight uses only slots in $H_{t^*}$.

Because $t^*\in T^+$, some $\rho\prec_{t^*}\pi_{t^*}$ has positive weight. This schedule is feasible and uses only $H_{t^*}$. Moreover, componentwise improvement with at least one strict inequality gives
$$ \sum_{j\in J_{t^*}}\rho(j) < \sum_{j\in J_{t^*}}\pi_{t^*}(j). $$
This contradicts the defining minimum-cost property of $\pi_{t^*}$. Hence no blocking coalition exists. $\square$

In particular, the theorem covers identical ownership sets across all types. Its proof does not require excess supply, so it applies throughout the promised excess-supply domain.

7. What remains open

The results establish polynomial verification, polynomial-size witnesses, an exact algorithm for fixed $q$, and a polynomial construction for nested ownership sets.

They do not determine whether every valid society admits a weak-core allocation. They also do not settle decision or polynomial-time construction for unrestricted $q$ and ownership sets that are not linearly ordered by inclusion. The price-enumeration algorithm is exponential in $q$, and its correctness supplies neither a general polynomial-time algorithm nor a hardness reduction.
