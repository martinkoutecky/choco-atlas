The continuous mirror question, as graded

Result

The continuous decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when all per-unit vote weights equal \(1\) and all type masses are equal. Consequently, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and is not in \(\mathrm{P}\) unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Exact characterization of continuous feasibility

Define the full vote capacity of type \(t\) by \(w_t=a_t\mu_t\), and let \(N=N_H(t^\star)\) be the target’s neighborhood. For a subset \(S\subseteq N\), write \(W(S)=\sum_{t\in S}w_t\).

Lemma. The instance is feasible if and only if there is a clique \(S\subseteq N\), possibly empty, satisfying
\[ W(S)+w_{t^\star}\ge Q. \]

Proof. Suppose feasible masses exist, and let \(C\) be their positive support outside the target. By hypothesis, \(C\subseteq N\) is a clique. Since \(x_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\delta\le\mu_{t^\star}\),
\[ Q\le\sum_{t\in C}a_tx_t+a_{t^\star}\delta \le W(C)+w_{t^\star}. \]
Thus \(C\) satisfies the claimed capacity condition.

Conversely, suppose a clique \(S\subseteq N\) satisfies that condition.

If \(Q\le w_{t^\star}\), choose all \(x_t=0\) and \(\delta=Q/a_{t^\star}\). Positivity of \(Q\) and \(a_{t^\star}\) gives \(0<\delta\le\mu_{t^\star}\), and the required inequalities become \(0<Q= a_{t^\star}\delta\).

If \(Q>w_{t^\star}\), put \(b=Q-w_{t^\star}>0\). Then \(W(S)\ge b\), so \(\alpha=b/W(S)\) satisfies \(0<\alpha\le1\). Choose \(x_t=\alpha\mu_t\) for \(t\in S\), choose \(x_t=0\) otherwise, and choose \(\delta=\mu_{t^\star}\). The positive support is exactly \(S\), and
\[ \sum_{t\ne t^\star}a_tx_t=b<Q, \qquad \sum_{t\ne t^\star}a_tx_t+a_{t^\star}\delta=b+w_{t^\star}=Q. \]
Here strictness follows from \(w_{t^\star}>0\). Every constraint is therefore satisfied. \(\square\)

Thus the continuous freedom removes the need to match a particular vote total: the remaining task is to find a clique with sufficient full capacity.

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate is simply a subset \(S\subseteq T\setminus\{t^\star\}\), encoded by an incidence vector of length \(\tau\). The verifier checks:

  • Every vertex of \(S\) is adjacent to \(t^\star\).
  • Every pair of distinct vertices of \(S\) is adjacent.
  • \(W(S)+w_{t^\star}\ge Q\).

The lemma proves that these checks are both necessary and sufficient; real numbers need not appear in the certificate.

For completeness, the numerical check takes polynomial bit time. Write the input rationals as \(a_t=p_t/q_t\), \(\mu_t=u_t/v_t\), and \(Q=p_Q/q_Q\), with positive integer denominators. The integer
\[ D=q_Q\prod_{t\in T}q_tv_t \]
is a common denominator for \(Q\) and every \(w_t\). Its bit length is \(O(L)\), because the total bit length of its factors is at most \(L\). The scaled integers \(DQ\) and \(Dw_t\), and their sums, also have \(O(L)\) bits.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, computing these scaled values takes \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations. The graph checks take \(O(\tau^2)\) time after constructing an adjacency matrix. Hence certificate verification takes \(O(\tau L^2+\tau^2)\) time, proving membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

3. Hardness with equal masses and unit per-unit weights

Reduce from the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem CLIQUE: given a simple graph \(G=(V,E)\) with \(n\ge1\) vertices and an integer \(1\le k\le n\), decide whether \(G\) contains a clique of size at least \(k\).

Choose \(\tau\) to be the smallest power of two satisfying \(\tau\ge n+1\). Create one type for each vertex of \(G\), the target type \(t^\star\), and \(\tau-n-1\) additional types. Set
\[ \mu_t=\frac1\tau,\qquad a_t=1\quad\text{for every }t\in T, \qquad Q=\frac{k+1}{\tau}. \]

The compatibility graph preserves \(G\) on the original vertex types, joins \(t^\star\) to every original vertex, and leaves every additional type isolated.

All input requirements hold: masses are positive and sum to \(1\), weights are positive, and \(0<Q\le1=\sum_ta_t\mu_t\). Moreover, all masses and the quota are dyadic rationals, so the construction works even under that narrower interpretation of “binary rational.”

Forward direction. If \(G\) contains a clique with at least \(k\) vertices, select exactly \(k\) of them. Give each selected type its full mass \(1/\tau\), give every other non-target type mass zero, and take \(\delta=1/\tau\). Then
\[ \sum_{t\ne t^\star}a_tx_t=\frac{k}{\tau} <\frac{k+1}{\tau}=Q =\sum_{t\ne t^\star}a_tx_t+a_{t^\star}\delta. \]
Compatibility holds by construction.

Reverse direction. Suppose the continuous instance is feasible. Its positive non-target support \(C\) contains only original vertex types, because the additional types are not adjacent to \(t^\star\). It is therefore a clique in \(G\). The mass bounds imply
\[ \frac{k+1}{\tau}=Q \le\sum_{t\in C}x_t+\delta \le\frac{|C|+1}{\tau}, \]
and hence \(|C|\ge k\). Thus \(G\) contains the required clique.

Since \(\tau<2(n+1)\), the output has length \(O(n^2+n\log n)\) using an adjacency-matrix encoding, and the construction takes polynomial time. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

The universal-target graph construction also appears in Theorem 2 of the supplied [discrete hardness paper](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2014/aamas/p37.pdf). The argument above separately verifies normalization and both directions for continuous masses.

4. Running time in \(\tau\) and \(L\)

A direct exact algorithm enumerates every subset of \(N_H(t^\star)\), checks whether it is a clique, and tests the lemma’s capacity condition. There are at most \(2^{\tau-1}\) subsets.

After the integer preprocessing described above, each subset requires \(O(\tau^2+\tau L)\) bit operations: pairwise compatibility checks and addition of at most \(\tau\) integers of \(O(L)\) bits. The resulting running time is
\[ O\!\left(\tau L^2+2^{\tau-1}(\tau^2+\tau L)\right). \]

In particular, the problem is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed support size \(\tau\). No separate dimension or candidate parameter is used.
