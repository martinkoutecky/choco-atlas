The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite connected undirected role graph \(H=(T,F)\) of maximum degree six, positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, and rational \(\varepsilon>0\), consider an atomless population with mass \(\mu_t\) of each role \(t\). Agents are adjacent exactly when their roles are adjacent in \(H\). For a measurable coalition \(j\), let \(x_{t,j}\) be its mass of role \(t\) and \(S_j=\{t:x_{t,j}>0\}\). Distinct roles in \(S_j\) have their shortest-path distances in \(H[S_j]\); distinct agents of the same role have distance two if that role has a neighbor in \(S_j\), and infinite distance otherwise. Their utility is the average inverse distance to coalition members, and total welfare is the integral of utility over the population. Find a finite rational mass table defining a partition with welfare within \(\varepsilon\) of the supremum over measurable partitions.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(H=(T,F)\) be a finite connected simple unweighted undirected role graph of maximum degree six, with \(m=|T|\ge 2\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) denotes support size, not the number of possible types. Here every role has positive mass, so \(\tau=m\), and \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\).
The population is the disjoint union of atomless intervals of lengths \(\mu_t\), one per role. Agents are adjacent exactly when their distinct roles are adjacent in \(H\).
For a measurable coalition \(C_j\), write \(x_{t,j}\) for its role masses, \(M_j=\sum_t x_{t,j}\), and \(S_j=\{t:x_{t,j}>0\}\). For distinct supported roles, \(d_j(t,s)\) is their shortest-path distance in \(H[S_j]\), with disconnected distance \(\infty\); set \(d_j(t,t)=2\) if \(t\) has a neighbor in \(S_j\), and \(\infty\) otherwise.
For \(M_j>0\), role-\(t\) utility is \(u_{t,j}=M_j^{-1}\sum_{s\in S_j}x_{s,j}/d_j(t,s)\), with \(1/\infty=0\). Zero-mass coalitions give utility zero; zero-mass role occurrences do not supply paths. Welfare is the population integral of utility, equivalently \(W=\sum_{j:M_j>0}\sum_{t\in S_j}x_{t,j}u_{t,j}\).
Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the supremum of welfare over measurable partitions with measurable utility. Given binary-rational \(\varepsilon>0\), the computational problem is to return a finite nonnegative rational table \((x_{t,j})\) with \(\sum_jx_{t,j}=\mu_t\), positive column sums, and \(W\ge\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon\).

Is this computational problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length including \(\varepsilon\), or is it NP-hard?

Discrete social-distance welfare maximization, with utility normalized by coalition size, is NP-hard on connected unweighted undirected graphs of maximum degree six. [Source](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/10607/10466)
No result settling this exact atomless mass-table problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the role graph simple and unweighted, with at least two roles, matching the intended nondegenerate connected social-graph setting.
  • Realized each role by an atomless interval and normalized utility by total coalition mass, carrying over normalization by coalition size.
  • Assigned zero utility to zero-mass coalitions and excluded zero-mass role occurrences from paths, consistently with the stipulated positive-mass support rule.
  • Allowed arbitrary measurable partitions with measurable utility in the supremum, while requiring the returned partition to have a finite rational mass table.
  • Defined m as the number of roles and L as total binary input length, including epsilon; polynomial time therefore refers to binary precision rather than inverse epsilon.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Polynomial-time solvability

The computational problem is solvable in polynomial time. The proof uses role splitting to reduce the welfare supremum to a convex program indexed by the cliques of \(H\). Maximum degree six makes this program polynomial-sized.

Throughout, singleton cliques are included. Their contribution in the convex program is an auxiliary upper bound, which will be realized approximately by adding positive masses of neighboring roles.

1. An upper bound on coalition welfare

Let \(A\) be the adjacency matrix of \(H\), let \(J\) be the all-ones matrix, and define

\[ B=\frac12(J+A),\qquad q(p)=p^{\mathsf T}Bp. \]

Thus \(B_{tt}=1/2\), an adjacent pair has coefficient \(1\), and a distinct nonadjacent pair has coefficient \(1/2\).

For any positive-mass coalition with mass vector \(x\), total mass \(M\), and normalized composition \(p=x/M\), its welfare contribution is at most \(Mq(p)\). Indeed:

  • An adjacent pair has reciprocal distance at most \(1\).
  • A distinct nonadjacent pair has distance at least \(2\), or infinite distance, so its reciprocal distance is at most \(1/2\).
  • A same-role pair has reciprocal distance either \(1/2\) or zero.

Consequently, every coefficient in the coalition’s welfare quadratic form is bounded above by the corresponding coefficient of \(B\).

2. Exact decomposition into clique-supported compositions

Lemma. Every probability vector \(p\) admits a finite decomposition

\[ p=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_\ell p^\ell,\qquad q(p)=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_\ell q(p^\ell), \]

where the weights are nonnegative and sum to one, and every \(p^\ell\) is supported on a clique of \(H\).

Proof. Suppose the support of \(p\) contains nonadjacent vertices \(u,v\). Define

\[ p^u=p+p_v(e_u-e_v),\qquad p^v=p-p_u(e_u-e_v),\qquad \lambda=\frac{p_u}{p_u+p_v}. \]

Both new vectors are probability vectors. Their supports are strictly smaller, and

\[ p=\lambda p^u+(1-\lambda)p^v. \]

Along the line joining them, \(q\) is affine: its quadratic coefficient in direction \(e_u-e_v\) is

\[ (e_u-e_v)^{\mathsf T}B(e_u-e_v) =B_{uu}+B_{vv}-2B_{uv} =\frac12+\frac12-1=0. \]

Therefore,

\[ q(p)=\lambda q(p^u)+(1-\lambda)q(p^v). \]

Repeat this operation whenever a support contains a nonadjacent pair. Each branch strictly decreases support size, so the process terminates after finitely many steps. Every terminal support is a clique, giving the claimed decomposition. \(\square\)

For a clique-supported probability vector, all distinct supported roles are adjacent. Hence

\[ q(p)=1-\frac12\|p\|_2^2. \]

For a nonnegative clique-supported mass vector \(x\), define

\[ M(x)=\sum_t x_t,\qquad h(x)=\frac{\|x\|_2^2}{M(x)},\qquad g(x)=M(x)-\frac12h(x) \]

when \(M(x)>0\), and define \(h(0)=g(0)=0\).

Thus \(g(x)=M(x)q(x/M(x))\). For a singleton-supported vector, \(g(x)=M(x)/2\); this is an auxiliary value, not its actual isolated-coalition welfare.

We will also need convexity of \(h\). For nonzero nonnegative vectors \(a,b\), a direct calculation gives

\[ h(a)+h(b)-h(a+b) = \frac{M(a)M(b)}{M(a)+M(b)} \left\|\frac{a}{M(a)}-\frac{b}{M(b)}\right\|_2^2 \ge 0. \]

The same inequality holds if one vector is zero. Since \(h\) is positively homogeneous, this proves its convexity. Therefore \(g\) is concave and positively homogeneous, and consequently

\[ g(a+b)\ge g(a)+g(b). \]

Both functions are continuous at zero because \(0\le h(x)\le M(x)\).

3. The convex program bounds every measurable partition

Let \(\mathcal K\) be the family of all nonempty cliques of \(H\). Introduce one column \(x^K\) for each \(K\in\mathcal K\), with entries outside \(K\) fixed to zero. Consider the program

\[ f_*= \min \left\{ \sum_{K\in\mathcal K}h(x^K): x^K\ge 0,\quad \sum_{K\ni t}x_t^K=\mu_t\ \text{for every }t \right\}. \]

Its feasible set is nonempty: assign each role entirely to its singleton column. It is compact, and the objective is continuous and convex. Thus its minimum exists.

Because all feasible tables have total mass one, the corresponding maximum auxiliary welfare is

\[ V=1-\frac12f_* =\max\sum_{K\in\mathcal K}g(x^K). \]

We claim that \(\mathrm{OPT}\le V\), including for partitions with infinitely many coalitions.

A finite-measure population has at most countably many disjoint positive-mass coalitions. Write their vectors as \(x^j\), their masses as \(M_j\), and their actual welfare contributions as \(w_j\). Section 1 gives

\[ w_j\le M_jq(x^j/M_j). \]

Apply the decomposition lemma to \(x^j/M_j\), and scale each resulting component by \(M_j\) times its decomposition weight. This produces clique-supported vectors \(z^{j,\ell}\) satisfying

\[ \sum_\ell z^{j,\ell}=x^j,\qquad \sum_\ell g(z^{j,\ell}) =M_jq(x^j/M_j)\ge w_j. \]

Aggregate all vectors with the same clique support. Superadditivity of \(g\) shows that aggregation cannot decrease their total auxiliary welfare. This remains valid for countably many vectors: apply the inequality to finite partial sums and pass to the limit using continuity. All masses and contributions are nonnegative and summable.

The remaining agents belong to zero-mass coalitions and have utility zero by the stipulated convention. Assign their remaining role masses to singleton columns. This gives a feasible table for the convex program whose auxiliary welfare is at least the original partition’s welfare.

Thus every admissible partition has welfare at most \(V\), proving

\[ \mathrm{OPT}\le V. \]

4. The upper bound is attainable as a supremum

We now show that \(V\) is the exact welfare supremum.

Take any feasible clique table \(X=(x^K)\), and delete its zero columns. Write

\[ M_K=\sum_t x_t^K. \]

For any \(0<\delta<1\), define a new table by

\[ y_t^K=(1-\delta)x_t^K+\delta M_K\mu_t. \]

Its column sums remain \(M_K\). Its row sums are exactly correct, because

\[ \sum_K y_t^K =(1-\delta)\mu_t+\delta\mu_t\sum_KM_K =\mu_t. \]

Moreover, every retained column contains positive mass of every role.

Let \(R\) be the full-graph reciprocal-distance matrix:

\[ R_{ts}=\frac1{d_H(t,s)}\quad(t\ne s),\qquad R_{tt}=\frac12. \]

The diagonal convention is valid because connectedness and \(m\ge 2\) give every role a neighbor. Since every column of \(Y\) supports all roles, its actual welfare uses precisely this matrix.

All entries of \(R\) are nonnegative. On any clique, its restriction agrees with \(B\). Therefore,

\[ \frac{(y^K)^{\mathsf T}Ry^K}{M_K} \ge (1-\delta)^2 \frac{(x^K)^{\mathsf T}Rx^K}{M_K} =(1-\delta)^2g(x^K). \]

Summing over columns gives

\[ W(Y)\ge(1-\delta)^2\sum_Kg(x^K). \]

Every such finite table is realized by a measurable partition: cut each role interval into pieces of the prescribed lengths and combine pieces with the same column index. Utility is constant on each role piece and hence measurable.

Apply this construction to a maximizing table \(X\), and let \(\delta\) tend to zero. It follows that \(\mathrm{OPT}\ge V\). Together with Section 3,

\[ \boxed{\mathrm{OPT}=1-\frac12f_*}. \]

5. Polynomial size and rational optimization

It remains to justify polynomial time in the binary input model, including logarithmic dependence on the required accuracy.

Number of variables

Every clique containing a fixed vertex \(t\) is determined by a subset of its neighbors. Since \(t\) has at most six neighbors, it belongs to at most \(2^6=64\) cliques.

Consequently, if

\[ N=\sum_{K\in\mathcal K}|K| \]

is the number of mass variables, then \(N\le64m\). The cliques can be enumerated by inspecting all neighbor subsets and removing duplicates.

An exact rational separation oracle

Eliminate each singleton variable using its row equation:

\[ x_t^{\{t\}} =\mu_t-\sum_{\substack{K\ni t\\K\ne\{t\}}}x_t^K. \]

Let \(z\) denote the remaining variables. Their feasible set \(P\) is explicitly given by nonnegativity and the row inequalities that make the eliminated singleton variables nonnegative.

Write \(f(z)=\sum_Kh(x^K(z))\). Consider the bounded epigraph

\[ E=\{(z,s):z\in P,\ f(z)\le s\le2\}. \]

This is a closed convex set. On \(P\),

\[ 0\le f(z)\le\sum_KM_K=1. \]

It has a polynomial-time exact rational separation oracle:

  • A violated inequality of \(P\), or \(s>2\), directly supplies a separating hyperplane.
  • Otherwise, evaluate \(f(z)\) exactly using rational arithmetic.
  • If \(s<f(z)\), use a supporting hyperplane of \(f\).

For a positive-mass block, the supporting coefficients are explicitly

\[ \frac{\partial h(x)}{\partial x_t} =\frac{2x_t}{M(x)} -\frac{\|x\|_2^2}{M(x)^2}. \]

At a zero block, the zero vector is a valid supporting coefficient vector on the nonnegative orthant because \(h(x)\ge0\). Substitution of the eliminated variables therefore supplies rational supporting coefficients for \(f\) on \(P\).

All evaluations and separation coefficients have encoding length polynomial in the query length and input length.

Explicit inner and outer radii

Let \(d_t\) be the number of cliques containing \(t\), and choose the feasible table

\[ x_t^{K,0}=\frac{\mu_t}{d_t}\qquad(t\in K). \]

Let \(z^0\) be its free coordinates and \(\mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t\). Every table entry at this point is at least \(\mu_{\min}/N\).

Set

\[ r=\frac{\mu_{\min}}{2N^2}. \]

A Euclidean perturbation of \(z^0\) of norm at most \(r\) changes each free coordinate by at most \(r\), and each eliminated singleton coordinate by at most \(Nr\). Thus all coordinates remain nonnegative.

The ball of radius \(r\) around \((z^0,3/2)\) is consequently contained in \(E\): its \(z\)-coordinates remain feasible, and its last coordinate remains between \(1\) and \(2\), while \(f(z)\le1\).

Also, \(E\) lies in a ball around the origin of radius \(N+2\). Since the masses are positive binary rationals,

\[ \log(1/r)=O(L+\log N). \]

Application of the ellipsoid method

We use the standard rational ellipsoid optimization theorem: a bounded convex body with a polynomial-time rational strong separation oracle and known inner and outer radii admits additive approximation of a rational linear objective in time polynomial in the dimension, the input and oracle encoding parameters, the logarithms of those radii, and \(\log(1/\eta)\). See Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver, [*Geometric Algorithms and Combinatorial Optimization*, Chapters 3–4](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4).

Here the objective is simply to minimize \(s\) over \(E\). Its optimum is \(f_*\). All the theorem’s hypotheses have been verified above.

For completeness, near-optimal feasibility has adequate interior volume even if the optimum lies on the boundary. Let \(a_*=(z_*,f_*)\) be an optimal point and \(b=(z^0,3/2)\). Convexity shows that mixing \(a_*\) with the radius-\(r\) ball around \(b\), with weight \(\lambda=\eta/4\), gives a ball of radius \(\lambda r\) contained in \(E\). For \(0<\eta\le1\), its last coordinates are at most

\[ f_*+\lambda(3/2-f_*)+\lambda r \le f_*+2\lambda =f_*+\eta/2. \]

Thus the required near-optimal region contains a ball whose logarithmic inverse radius is polynomially bounded. The ellipsoid method can return an exactly feasible rational point with \(f(z)\le f_*+\eta\), rather than requiring an exact optimizer.

It follows that such a rational table is computable in polynomial time, with polynomial output encoding length.

6. The required output table

Let

\[ e=\min\{\varepsilon,1\}. \]

Compute a feasible rational clique table \(X\) satisfying

\[ f(X)\le f_*+e. \]

Its auxiliary welfare \(F(X)=1-\tfrac12f(X)\) satisfies

\[ F(X)\ge\mathrm{OPT}-\frac e2. \]

Delete zero columns and apply the construction from Section 4 with

\[ \delta=\frac e4. \]

The resulting table \(Y\) is rational, has exact row sums \(\mu_t\), and has positive column sums. Since \(F(X)\le1\),

\[ W(Y)\ge(1-\delta)^2F(X) \ge F(X)-2\delta \ge\mathrm{OPT}-e \ge\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon. \]

There are at most \(64m\) output columns, and the dense output table has polynomial size. Every step runs in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), with \(\tau=m\).

The discrete hardness premise does not transfer to this model: the proof relies on splitting role masses, and on the same-role reciprocal-distance contribution of \(1/2\) once a neighbor is present.
