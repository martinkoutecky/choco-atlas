The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of good classes \(G\), rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_g\), and a shared binary \mathrm{XOS} valuation \(v:2^G\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) with value-oracle access, assign masses \(\lambda_B\ge 0\) to integral bundles \(B\subseteq G\) subject to \(\sum_{B\subseteq G}\lambda_B=1\) and \(\sum_{B\ni g}\lambda_B\le \sigma_g\) for every \(g\). Compute or approximate \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\lambda)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{B:v(B)>0}\lambda_B\log v(B)\right)\), assigning value \(0\) whenever positive mass receives a zero-valued bundle.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a set of \(m\ge 1\) good classes, with supplies \(\sigma_g\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\) per unit of population. Each individual may receive at most one good from each class; unused supply is allowed.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here there is one type, with mass \(1\), so \(\tau=1\).
The shared valuation \(v:2^G\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) is \(\mathrm{XOS}\), normalized by \(v(\varnothing)=0\), and has binary marginals: \(v(S\cup\{g\})-v(S)\in\{0,1\}\). Access is through unit-cost value queries; an explicit list of additive clauses is not supplied.
An allocation assigns masses \(\lambda_B\ge 0\) to integral bundles \(B\subseteq G\), with \(\sum_B\lambda_B=1\) and \(\sum_{B\ni g}\lambda_B\le\sigma_g\) for every \(g\). Its welfare is \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\lambda)=\exp(\sum_{B:v(B)>0}\lambda_B\log v(B))\), unless some zero-valued bundle receives positive mass, in which case its welfare is \(0\).
Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the maximum feasible welfare, and let \(L\) be the bit length of the explicitly supplied input. Computational allocations are represented by their positive bundle masses as binary rationals.

Is finding a feasible allocation with \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\lambda)\ge\mathrm{OPT}/1.0042\) possible in time and value-query count polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is obtaining this approximation \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete agents, approximation within \(1.0042\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even with identical, succinctly represented binary \(\mathrm{XOS}\) valuations; a polynomial-time \(288\)-approximation exists with value queries. [Barman–Verma](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02656v2)
For additive valuations, the related configuration relaxation maximizing expected log value admits a polynomial-time \((1+\varepsilon)\)-approximation for every \(\varepsilon>0\), with runtime polynomial in input size and \(1/\varepsilon\). [Feng–Li](https://theoretics.episciences.org/16348/pdf)
No result settling the stated binary \(\mathrm{XOS}\) mass-allocation problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made normalization and the binary-marginals meaning of binary XOS explicit, following the discrete definitions.
  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational supplies and a nonempty set of good classes; supplies need not sum to one.
  • Interpreted the shared valuation as one population type of total mass one, and integral bundles as allowing at most one good per class.
  • Specified rational support encoding for computational allocations and defined L to exclude the inaccessible description of the valuation oracle.
  • Selected the anchor's multiplicative approximation factor 1.0042 rather than exact optimization; retained zero-optimum instances with the stated zero-welfare convention.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result: the approximation is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard

Obtaining a feasible allocation with welfare at least $\mathrm{OPT}/1.0042$ is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard, already with one type and supply $2/5$ for every good.

The reduction below constructs valuations whose value queries are computable deterministically in polynomial time. Its main ingredient is an encoding of satisfying assignments into balanced bundles. A polynomial interpolation argument makes it possible to evaluate the valuation without finding a satisfying assignment first.

1. Baseline valuation and its exact optimum

Let $N$ be a positive multiple of $60$. Partition the goods into public sets $A,B$, each of size $N$, and give every good supply $2/5$. Define

$$ h=\frac N4,\qquad a=\frac{7N}{10},\qquad b=\frac{14N}{15},\qquad c=\frac{2N}{5}. $$

The baseline valuation is

$$ v_0(S)= \max\left\{ \min(|S|,h), \min(|S\cap A|,a), |S\cap B| \right\}. $$

A truncated cardinality function $\min(|S\cap U|,k)$ is the maximum of the binary additive functions $S\mapsto |S\cap F|$ over all $k$-element subsets $F\subseteq U$. Thus $v_0$ is $\mathrm{XOS}$. Its constituents are normalized, integer-valued, monotone, and increase by at most one when a good is added. Their maximum therefore has binary marginals. Every nonempty bundle has positive value.

We first prove

$$ \mathrm{OPT}_0=a^{4/7}b^{3/7}. $$

Upper bound

Set

$$ \eta=\log b-1,\qquad \alpha=\frac{1+\log(a/b)}{a},\qquad \beta=\frac1b. $$

Since $a/b=3/4$, we have $0<\alpha<1/a$ and $\beta>0$.

For $0<k\le a$, the tangent inequality for the logarithm gives

$$ \log k \le \log a+\frac{k-a}{a} \le \log a+\alpha(k-a) =\eta+\alpha k. $$

The second inequality holds because $k-a\le0$ and $\alpha<1/a$. For every $t>0$, the tangent inequality at $b$ gives

$$ \log t\le\log b+\frac{t-b}{b}=\eta+\beta t. $$

Also,

$$ \frac bh=\frac{56}{15}>e, $$

so $\log h<\eta$. Applying these bounds to whichever constituent realizes $v_0(S)$ shows that every nonempty $S$ satisfies

$$ \log v_0(S) \le \eta+\alpha|S\cap A|+\beta|S\cap B|. $$

In a feasible allocation, the expected consumption from each group is at most $c$. Consequently, every positive-welfare feasible allocation satisfies

$$ \log\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\lambda) \le \eta+c\alpha+c\beta. $$

Using $c/a=4/7$ and $c/b=3/7$, direct substitution gives

$$ \eta+c\alpha+c\beta = \frac47\log a+\frac37\log b. $$

Zero-welfare allocations also satisfy the resulting welfare upper bound.

Attainment with rational masses

Arrange each group cyclically. Assign mass $4/(7N)$ to each of the $N$ cyclic windows of length $a$ in $A$, and mass $3/(7N)$ to each of the $N$ cyclic windows of length $b$ in $B$.

The masses sum to one. Each good in $A$ lies in exactly $a$ of its group's windows, and each good in $B$ lies in exactly $b$ of its group's windows. Their respective consumptions are

$$ a\frac4{7N}=\frac25, \qquad b\frac3{7N}=\frac25. $$

The window values are $a$ and $b$, respectively. This feasible allocation attains $a^{4/7}b^{3/7}$ and proves the asserted formula. Its support and rational masses have polynomial encoding length.

2. Balanced additional clauses produce a sufficient welfare gap

Suppose we add five binary additive clauses supported on sets $T_0,\ldots,T_4$ such that

$$ |T_i|=\frac{4N}{5} $$

and every good belongs to exactly two of these sets.

Mass $1/5$ on each $T_i$ is feasible and gives every individual value $4N/5$. Conversely, normalization and binary marginals imply $v(S)\le |S|$ for every bundle. Jensen's inequality therefore bounds the welfare of every positive-welfare feasible allocation by

$$ \exp\left(\sum_S\lambda_S\log v(S)\right) \le \sum_S\lambda_Sv(S) \le \sum_S\lambda_S|S| \le \frac{4N}{5}. $$

Thus any valuation containing such five clauses has optimum exactly $4N/5$.

This exceeds $1.0042\,\mathrm{OPT}_0$. To prove the inequality without numerical approximation, let

$$ H(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x). $$

The baseline formula gives

$$ \log\mathrm{OPT}_0=\log c+H(4/7). $$

Moreover,

$$ H(1/2)=\log2,\qquad H'(1/2)=0,\qquad H''(x)=-\frac1{x(1-x)}\le-4. $$

Integrating the last inequality twice from $1/2$ to $4/7$ yields

$$ H(4/7) \le \log2-2\left(\frac47-\frac12\right)^2 = \log2-\frac1{98}. $$

Since $4N/5=2c$, it follows that

$$ \log\frac{4N/5}{\mathrm{OPT}_0}\ge\frac1{98}. $$

Finally,

$$ \log(1.0042) = \log\left(1+\frac{21}{5000}\right) \le\frac{21}{5000}<\frac1{98}. $$

Therefore,

$$ \frac{4N/5}{1.0042}>\mathrm{OPT}_0. $$

3. A deterministic polynomial recovery lemma

We will encode assignments by low-degree polynomials. The following elementary lemma supplies the required recovery algorithm.

Lemma. Let $d\ge1$, let

$$ \ell\ge10^{11}d, $$

and let $\mathbb F_q$ be a finite field with $q\ge\ell$. Let $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_\ell$ be distinct field elements. Given lists $L_j\subseteq\mathbb F_q$ with $|L_j|\le9600$, one can deterministically list, in time polynomial in $q,\ell,d$, all polynomials $P\in\mathbb F_q[X]$ satisfying

$$ \deg P<d $$

and

$$ \left|\{j:P(\xi_j)\in L_j\}\right|\ge\frac{\ell}{200}. $$

Proof. Put

$$ W=\left\lfloor\frac{\ell}{400}\right\rfloor. $$

Seek a nonzero bivariate polynomial $Q(X,Y)$, using only monomials $X^rY^s$ with

$$ 0\le r\le\left\lfloor\frac W2\right\rfloor, \qquad 0\le s\le\left\lfloor\frac{W}{2d}\right\rfloor, $$

such that

$$ Q(\xi_j,x)=0 \quad \text{for every }j\text{ and every }x\in L_j. $$

These are at most $9600\ell$ homogeneous linear equations.

Because $\ell\ge800$, we have $W\ge\ell/800$. The number of available monomials is at least

$$ \frac{W^2}{4d} \ge \frac{\ell^2}{2560000d} > 9600\ell, $$

where the strict inequality follows from $\ell/d\ge10^{11}$. Hence a nonzero $Q$ exists and can be found by Gaussian elimination over $\mathbb F_q$.

Every available monomial satisfies

$$ r+(d-1)s\le W. $$

Consequently, for $\deg P<d$, the univariate polynomial $Q(X,P(X))$ has degree at most $W$. If $P$ has at least $\ell/200$ list agreements, this polynomial vanishes at more than $W$ distinct points. The polynomial root bound implies

$$ Q(X,P(X))\equiv0. $$

It remains to enumerate all polynomial solutions of this identity in deterministic polynomial time. We give the enumeration explicitly.

At any stage, remove the largest common power of $X$ from the current nonzero bivariate polynomial $R(X,Y)$. After this normalization,

$$ R(0,Y)\not\equiv0. $$

Enumerate its roots $\alpha\in\mathbb F_q$ by testing all $q$ field elements. For each root, substitute

$$ Y=\alpha+XY' $$

and normalize again by removing the largest common power of $X$. Repeating this procedure for $d$ stages enumerates possible coefficient sequences of polynomial solutions of degree less than $d$.

Indeed, if $P$ is a solution, then $P(0)$ is a root of $R(0,Y)$. Writing

$$ P(X)=P(0)+XP_1(X) $$

shows that $P_1$ is a solution of the corresponding substituted and normalized polynomial. Repetition preserves every genuine solution.

We next bound the branching. Give a normalized node the weight $\deg_Y R(0,Y)$. Suppose $\alpha$ has multiplicity $t$ in $R(0,Y)$. Expand around $\alpha$:

$$ R(X,\alpha+Z)=\sum_{u,v}c_{u,v}X^uZ^v. $$

Here $c_{0,v}=0$ for $v<t$, while $c_{0,t}\ne0$. After $Z=XY'$, the smallest occurring exponent $\rho$ of $X$ satisfies $\rho\le t$. Its coefficient has degree at most $\rho$ in $Y'$. Thus the normalized child has weight at most $t$.

The sum of root multiplicities is at most the parent's weight. Therefore the sum of the positive child weights never exceeds the parent weight. Nodes of weight zero have no possible completion and are discarded. If $D=\deg_Y Q$, there are at most $D$ retained nodes at each depth.

The degree in $Y$ never exceeds $D$, and each substitution increases the degree in $X$ by at most $D$. Thus all intermediate polynomial sizes remain polynomially bounded. After $d$ stages, check each resulting $P$ directly for the identity $Q(X,P(X))\equiv0$ and for the required list agreements.

This deterministic procedure has polynomial running time and lists every required polynomial. $\square$

4. Balanced bundles indexed by assignments

Let $\varphi$ be a Boolean satisfiability instance with $d\ge1$ variables. Padding with an unused variable handles the case of no variables.

Choose

$$ \ell=12\left\lceil\frac{10^{11}d}{12}\right\rceil, $$

and let $q$ be the smallest power of $5$ satisfying $q\ge\ell$. Then

$$ \ell\le q<5\ell. $$

We use $\mathbb F_q$ and its trace to $\mathbb F_5$:

$$ \operatorname{Tr}(x) = x+x^5+\cdots+x^{5^{r-1}}, \qquad q=5^r. $$

The standard finite-field existence theorem applies because $5$ is prime and $r$ is a positive integer. A representation of $\mathbb F_q$ can be constructed deterministically in time polynomial in $q$: enumerate monic degree-$r$ polynomials over $\mathbb F_5$ until finding an irreducible one, testing reducibility by division by all monic polynomials of degree at most $r/2$. Both enumerations have size polynomial in $q$.

The trace is an $\mathbb F_5$-linear surjection onto $\mathbb F_5$. For completeness, its values lie in $\mathbb F_5$ because taking fifth powers leaves the displayed sum unchanged. Its defining polynomial is nonzero and has degree $5^{r-1}<q$, so it cannot vanish on all of $\mathbb F_q$. A nonzero linear map into the one-dimensional space $\mathbb F_5$ is surjective.

Choose distinct $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_\ell\in\mathbb F_q$. Encode an assignment $a=(a_0,\ldots,a_{d-1})\in\{0,1\}^d$ by

$$ P_a(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}a_kX^k. $$

The field elements $0,1$ here encode the corresponding Boolean values.

Goods and labels

Each of $A$ and $B$ contains goods indexed by

$$ (j,z,y)\in \{1,\ldots,\ell\}\times\mathbb F_q\times\mathbb F_5. $$

Hence

$$ N=5q\ell. $$

Since $\ell$ is divisible by $12$, $N$ is divisible by $60$, as required in Section 1. Give every good supply $2/5$.

For any polynomial $P$ of degree less than $d$, label a good in either group by

$$ \operatorname{Tr}(P(\xi_j)z)+y\in\mathbb F_5. $$

For $i\in\mathbb F_5$, define $T_i(P)$ to contain, from both groups, precisely the goods with labels in $\{i,i+1\}$, with addition in $\mathbb F_5$.

For each fixed $j,z$, varying $y$ produces each of the five labels exactly once. Therefore,

$$ |T_i(P)\cap A|=|T_i(P)\cap B|=2q\ell=\frac{2N}{5}, $$

and

$$ |T_i(P)|=\frac{4N}{5}. $$

Every good belongs to exactly two of the five sets $T_i(P)$.

The valuation

Define

$$ v_\varphi(S)= \max\left( v_0(S), \max_{\substack{a\in\{0,1\}^d\\\varphi(a)=1}} \max_{i\in\mathbb F_5}|S\cap T_i(P_a)| \right). $$

If $\varphi$ has no satisfying assignment, the second collection is absent and $v_\varphi=v_0$.

This is a maximum of binary additive clauses, including the clauses representing the truncated terms of $v_0$. It is therefore $\mathrm{XOS}$, normalized, nonnegative, integer-valued, and has binary marginals.

If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, any satisfying assignment supplies five sets satisfying Section 2. Hence

$$ \mathrm{OPT}_\varphi= \frac{4N}{5}. $$

If it is unsatisfiable, then

$$ \mathrm{OPT}_\varphi=\mathrm{OPT}_0. $$

To complete the reduction, we must prove that $v_\varphi(S)$ is computable in polynomial time.

5. A clause exceeding the baseline has substantial correlation

Fix a query bundle $S$, and write

$$ s=|S\cap A|, \qquad t=|S\cap B|, \qquad f=v_0(S). $$

If $s+t\le h$, then $f=s+t$, and no intersection-cardinality clause can exceed $f$.

Suppose $s+t>h$. We claim

$$ f-\frac25(s+t)\ge\frac N{50}. $$

If $s\le a$, then $f\ge\max(s,t)$ and $f\ge h$. Thus

$$ \frac25(s+t)\le\frac45f, $$

and

$$ f-\frac25(s+t)\ge\frac15f\ge\frac N{20}. $$

If $s>a$ and $t\le a$, then $s\le N$ and $f\ge a$, giving

$$ f-\frac25(s+t) \ge a-\frac25(N+a) = \frac N{50}. $$

If $s>a$ and $t\ge a$, then $f\ge t$, giving

$$ f-\frac25(s+t) \ge t-\frac25(N+t) \ge \frac35a-\frac25N = \frac N{50}. $$

These cases exhaust the possibilities.

Consequently, whenever a clause indexed by $P,i$ exceeds the baseline,

$$ |S\cap T_i(P)|>f, $$

it satisfies

$$ |S\cap T_i(P)|-\frac25|S|>\frac N{50}. $$

The following section turns this correlation into short local lists.

6. Orthogonality gives local lists of size at most $9600$

Fix $i\in\mathbb F_5$. A block $j$ consists of its goods in both $A$ and $B$, and has size

$$ M=10q. $$

For $x\in\mathbb F_q$, let $u_x$ be the real vector on one block whose coordinate at $(z,y)$, in either copy, is

$$ u_x(z,y) = \mathbf 1_{\{\operatorname{Tr}(xz)+y\in\{i,i+1\}\}} -\frac25. $$

Exactly $2/5$ of the coordinates have their indicator equal to one. Therefore,

$$ \|u_x\|^2=\frac6{25}M. $$

The vectors $u_x$ are pairwise orthogonal. To verify this, take $x\ne x'$. For each pair of desired labels $u,v\in\mathbb F_5$, the equations

$$ \operatorname{Tr}(xz)+y=u, \qquad \operatorname{Tr}(x'z)+y=v $$

are equivalent to

$$ \operatorname{Tr}((x-x')z)=u-v $$

and one uniquely determined choice of $y$. Surjectivity of the nonzero trace functional gives exactly $q/5$ choices of $z$. Thus, over all $5q$ coordinates in one copy, the pair of labels is uniform on $\mathbb F_5^2$. Its two indicators have joint mean $4/25$ and individual means $2/5$, so their centered product has mean zero. The same holds in the second copy.

Let $r_j$ be the indicator vector of the query goods in block $j$, and define the rational score

$$ D_{j,x}=\langle r_j,u_x\rangle. $$

Orthogonality gives Bessel's inequality in the form

$$ \sum_{x\in\mathbb F_q}D_{j,x}^2 \le \frac6{25}M\|r_j\|^2 \le \frac6{25}M^2. $$

Put

$$ \theta=\frac1{200}, \qquad L_j=\{x\in\mathbb F_q:D_{j,x}\ge\theta M\}. $$

Then

$$ |L_j|\,\theta^2M^2 \le \frac6{25}M^2, $$

so

$$ |L_j|\le\frac{6}{25\theta^2}=9600. $$

All scores and lists can be computed exactly by enumerating field elements and block coordinates. Their rational comparisons require no approximation.

Now suppose $T_i(P)$ exceeds the baseline on $S$. By Section 5 and $N=M\ell/2$,

$$ \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}D_{j,P(\xi_j)} = |S\cap T_i(P)|-\frac25|S| > \frac{M\ell}{100} = 2\theta M\ell. $$

Let $r$ be the number of indices with $P(\xi_j)\in L_j$. Every score is at most $M$, while every score outside its list is less than $\theta M$. Hence

$$ \sum_jD_{j,P(\xi_j)} \le rM+(\ell-r)\theta M. $$

Combining the last two inequalities gives

$$ r(1-\theta)>\theta\ell, $$

and therefore

$$ r>\frac{\ell}{200}. $$

Thus every clause exceeding the baseline is indexed by a polynomial that the recovery lemma lists.

7. Exact polynomial-time value evaluation

Here is a deterministic evaluator for $v_\varphi(S)$.

  • Compute $v_0(S)$.
  • For each of the five indices $i$, construct the lists $L_1,\ldots,L_\ell$ from Section 6.
  • Apply the recovery lemma to these lists.
  • For every recovered polynomial, check whether all its $d$ coefficients belong to $\{0,1\}$ and whether the corresponding assignment satisfies $\varphi$.
  • For each polynomial passing those checks, compute $|S\cap T_i(P)|$. Return the maximum of these values and $v_0(S)$.

Every additional value considered by this evaluator is an actual clause of $v_\varphi$, because its assignment has been checked against $\varphi$. Therefore the returned value cannot exceed $v_\varphi(S)$.

Conversely, every clause exceeding $v_0(S)$ is recovered by Sections 5–6 and the recovery lemma. Its satisfying assignment passes the checks. Thus every improvement over the baseline is considered, and the returned value equals $v_\varphi(S)$ exactly.

This evaluator also produces a satisfying assignment whenever it detects a strict improvement over the baseline.

The size and running-time bounds are polynomial. Specifically,

$$ \ell=O(d),\qquad q=O(d),\qquad m=2N=10q\ell=O(d^2). $$

The constants are large but fixed. Field construction, score computation, interpolation, polynomial recovery, and assignment verification all take time polynomial in $m$ and the length of $\varphi$.

Accordingly, the reduction supplies a succinct deterministic polynomial-time value evaluator, rather than an explicit exponential list of clauses. The explicitly supplied society has one type of mass one and identical rational supplies $2/5$, so its input length is polynomially bounded as well.

8. Extracting a satisfying assignment from an approximate allocation

Suppose a deterministic algorithm, using polynomial time and polynomially many value queries, always returns a feasible allocation $\lambda$ satisfying

$$ \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\lambda) \ge \frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{1.0042}. $$

Given $\varphi$, construct the instance above and simulate every value query with the evaluator from Section 7.

If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, Sections 2 and 4 imply

$$ \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\lambda) \ge \frac{4N/5}{1.0042} > \mathrm{OPT}_0. $$

The allocation consequently has positive welfare.

There must be a positive-mass output bundle $S$ for which

$$ v_\varphi(S)>v_0(S). $$

Otherwise the valuation of every supported bundle would equal its baseline valuation. The allocation is feasible for the baseline instance because the supplies are identical, so its welfare would be at most $\mathrm{OPT}_0$, contradicting the strict inequality.

Scan the positive-mass bundles in the output. Their number is polynomially bounded by the algorithm's running time, because the required output representation lists those bundles and their rational masses. For each bundle, run the evaluator. On a bundle with a strict improvement, the evaluator produces a satisfying assignment.

If $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable, no candidate can pass the assignment check, so this procedure never produces a false satisfying assignment. Thus:

  • For satisfiable $\varphi$, the approximation guarantee ensures that the scan produces a satisfying assignment.
  • For unsatisfiable $\varphi$, the scan produces none.

This decides Boolean satisfiability in polynomial time. Since Boolean satisfiability is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete, obtaining the stated approximation is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard.

The same witness extraction shows that a randomized polynomial-time algorithm succeeding with probability at least $2/3$ on every instance would place satisfiability in $\mathrm{RP}$: it could return a satisfying witness with that probability and could never accept an unsatisfiable formula.

The reduction establishes the requested hardness directly for mass allocations, with $\tau=1$, equal supplies, binary $\mathrm{XOS}$ valuations, and exact polynomial-time value evaluation.
