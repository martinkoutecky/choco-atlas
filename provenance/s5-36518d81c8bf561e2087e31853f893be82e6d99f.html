The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(P,Q\), rational masses \(\mu_p,\nu_q\) each summing to \(1\), and strict complete rankings of opposite-side types, can one compute in time polynomial in \(|P|+|Q|\) and the input bit length \(L\) a rational flow \(x_{pq}\ge 0\) with row sums \(\mu_p\), column sums \(\nu_q\), and no pair \((p,q)\) for which both positive mass of \(p\) is assigned below \(q\) and positive mass of \(q\) is assigned below \(p\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P,Q\) be nonempty finite type sets. Each side's society is given by its support: its list of types with positive mass, each mass encoded as a binary rational; \(\sum_{p\in P}\mu_p=\sum_{q\in Q}\nu_q=1\). Put \(\tau=|P|+|Q|\), the total support size, not the number of possible types, and \(m=|P||Q|\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type has a strict complete ranking \(\succ_p\) or \(\succ_q\) of opposite-side types. Every pair is acceptable, and all mass must be matched.
A feasible allocation is a rational matrix \(x\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{P\times Q}\) with \(\sum_qx_{pq}=\mu_p\) and \(\sum_px_{pq}=\nu_q\). It is stable if no \((p,q)\) satisfies both \(\sum_{q':\,q\succ_p q'}x_{pq'}>0\) and \(\sum_{p':\,p\succ_q p'}x_{p'q}>0\).

Is computing a stable allocation solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete stable marriage admits a polynomial-time implementation of Scarf's algorithm yielding a stable matching.
The continuous problem is already covered by the real-valued bipartite stable allocation model, for which a polynomial-time algorithm is known: [Dean and Munshi, Faster Algorithms For Stable Allocation Problems](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Excluded zero-mass types from the input support and required both type sets to be nonempty.
  • Made explicit that all pairs are acceptable and all mass must be matched, consistent with complete acceptable preferences and balanced total masses.
  • Defined the size parameters as the total support size and the number of possible cross-side pairs; no equality of the two support cardinalities is imposed.

Result

Computing a stable allocation is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). The cited stable-allocation algorithm applies, including in the binary rational bit model.

1. The known algorithm

We use the following result of Dean and Munshi: a bipartite stable-allocation instance with strict preferences, vertex quotas, edge capacities, and their prescribed dummy vertices has a stable allocation computable in \(O(e\log n)\) arithmetic operations, where \(n\) and \(e\) count vertices and edges. Their blocking condition requires an unsaturated edge whose endpoints both have positive allocation to worse partners. See Sections 2–3, particularly Lemma 5, of [*Faster Algorithms For Stable Allocation Problems*](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

The following reduction verifies the hypotheses and the correspondence with the question.

2. Edge capacities preserve the required stability condition

Give vertex \(p\) quota \(\mu_p\), vertex \(q\) quota \(\nu_q\), and each edge capacity

\(u_{pq}=\min\{\mu_p,\nu_q\}.\)

Every feasible allocation in the question already satisfies \(x_{pq}\le u_{pq}\), by its row and column sums. Thus these capacities impose no additional restriction.

Define the worse-partner masses

\(A_{pq}=\sum_{q':\,q\succ_p q'}x_{pq'},\qquad B_{pq}=\sum_{p':\,p\succ_q p'}x_{p'q}.\)

Because all summands are nonnegative, \(A_{pq}>0\) is equivalent to the existence of a worse partner \(q'\) with \(x_{pq'}>0\); the analogous equivalence holds for \(B_{pq}\).

If both masses are positive, feasibility gives

\(x_{pq}\le\mu_p-A_{pq}<\mu_p,\qquad x_{pq}\le\nu_q-B_{pq}<\nu_q.\)

Consequently, \(x_{pq}<u_{pq}\). The additional edge-capacity condition in the cited model is therefore automatic for every blocking pair in the question. Conversely, a blocking pair in that model has both worse-partner masses positive.

Hence the two stability definitions agree on allocations with the prescribed original marginals.

3. Dummy vertices cannot leave original mass unmatched

To match the paper’s setup explicitly, add a dummy vertex \(p_0\) on the left and \(q_0\) on the right, each with quota \(1\). Add edges with capacities

\(u_{p q_0}=\mu_p,\qquad u_{p_0 q}=\nu_q,\qquad u_{p_0q_0}=1.\)

Each original vertex places the opposite dummy last. Each dummy ranks all original opposite vertices in any strict order, followed by the other dummy.

These are precisely the required dummy quota and preference conventions: the dummy left quota equals the total original right quota, and both augmented sides have total quota \(2\). All capacities satisfy the endpoint-quota bounds. Feasibility is also explicit: assign \(x_{pq}=\mu_p\nu_q\), assign \(x_{p_0q_0}=1\), and assign zero to the other dummy edges.

Let \(y\) be a stable allocation returned for this augmented instance. Put

\(t=\sum_{p\in P}y_{p q_0}.\)

Summing the original row and column equations separately gives

\(\sum_{p,q}y_{pq}=1-t,\qquad \sum_{q\in Q}y_{p_0q}=t.\)

Suppose \(t>0\). There exist \(p\in P\) and \(q\in Q\) with \(y_{p q_0}>0\) and \(y_{p_0q}>0\). Their quota equations imply

\(y_{pq}\le\mu_p-y_{p q_0}<\mu_p,\qquad y_{pq}\le\nu_q-y_{p_0q}<\nu_q.\)

Thus \(y_{pq}<u_{pq}\). Moreover, \(p\) prefers \(q\) to \(q_0\), and \(q\) prefers \(p\) to \(p_0\). Therefore \((p,q)\) blocks \(y\), a contradiction.

It follows that \(t=0\). Nonnegativity then forces every edge between an original vertex and a dummy to carry zero allocation. Restricting \(y\) to \(P\times Q\) gives exactly the required marginals. By Section 2, this restriction is stable under the question’s definition.

The augmented graph has

\(n=\tau+2,\qquad e=m+\tau+1.\)

Its construction and preference lists have polynomial encoding length.

4. Binary rational arithmetic

An arithmetic-operation bound alone does not establish polynomial bit complexity. We now verify that distinction.

Write each input mass as an integer numerator divided by a positive integer denominator. Let \(D\) be the product of all these denominators. Since their encodings form part of the input,

\(\log_2 D\le L.\)

Multiply every augmented quota and capacity by \(D\). All become integers; every individual quota and capacity is at most \(D\). Positive scaling preserves feasibility and blocking conditions.

For exact arithmetic, use the explicit implementation of the paper’s augmenting-path algorithm without dynamic trees. It takes \(O(en)\) operations. Its numerical updates use minima of residual capacities and deficits, followed by additions and subtractions along paths or cycles; at most \(2e+n\) augmentations occur. These properties are established in [Section 3 of the cited paper](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

Starting from integer data, every such update preserves integrality. Actual edge allocations and residual capacities lie between \(0\) and \(D\). Vertex totals and deficits can be computed from at most \(n\) incident quantities, so the explicit implementation needs only integers with \(O(L+\log n)\) bits. Additions, subtractions, comparisons, and minima on these integers take polynomial bit time.

Computing \(D\), constructing the scaled instance, and running this implementation therefore take time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Finally, divide each original-edge output \(z_{pq}\) by \(D\). The resulting allocation

\(x_{pq}=z_{pq}/D\)

is rational, feasible, and stable. Since \(0\le z_{pq}\le D\), its entire matrix has polynomial output length.

Conclusion

The cited algorithm, together with the verified reduction and exact-arithmetic argument, completely settles the question: a stable allocation can be computed in polynomial time in the support-based input parameters.
