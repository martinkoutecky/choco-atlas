Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given \(m\geq3\) alternatives, a society is specified by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a binary-rational utility vector \(u_t\in\Delta_m\), with pairwise distinct coordinates. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Let \(c=(1/m,\ldots,1/m)\) and \(S^2_m(R)=\{p\in\Delta_m:\|p-c\|_2\leq R\}\); this is a ball intersected with the simplex.
Define \(\Gamma_m=\{2^j/\sqrt{m(m-1)}:j\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0},\ 2^j<m-1\}\cup\{(m-1)/\sqrt{m(m-1)}\}\), with no repeated radii.
For each \(t\in T\) and \(R\in\Gamma_m\), let \(p_{t,R}\) maximize \(u_t^\top p\) over \(S^2_m(R)\); resolve any nonuniqueness by lexicographically maximizing coordinates in alternative-index order.
The expected lottery is \(q(a)=\frac{1}{2m}+\frac{1}{2|\Gamma_m|}\sum_{R\in\Gamma_m}\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p_{t,R}(a)\). Compute it exactly, represented by arithmetic expression DAGs with binary-rational constants and operations \(+, -, \times, /, \sqrt{\cdot}\), using nonnegative square roots.

Result

Exact computation is polynomial-time solvable in the Turing model for the specified arithmetic-DAG output.

The key is that every decision determining an individual lottery can be made by exact rational comparisons. After those decisions, each individual lottery requires just one shared square-root expression.

The individual optimization appears in Algorithm 1 and Theorem 11 of [Bhaskar and Ghosh, *On the Welfare of Cardinal Voting Mechanisms*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol122-fsttcs2018/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2018.27/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2018.27.pdf). The proof below is self-contained and supplies the bit-complexity analysis for the specified input and output.

1. Replace the radius by a rational parameter

For any \(p\in\Delta_m\),

\(\displaystyle \|p-c\|_2^2=\sum_{i=1}^m p_i^2-\frac1m.\)

Consequently, putting \(D=R^2+1/m\), the individual optimization is equivalent to

\(\displaystyle \max\left\{u^\top p:p_i\geq0,\ \sum_i p_i=1,\ \sum_i p_i^2\leq D\right\}.\)

Every specified radius has the form \(R=h/\sqrt{m(m-1)}\), where \(h\) is a positive integer satisfying \(h\leq m-1\). Thus

\(\displaystyle D=\frac{m-1+h^2}{m(m-1)}\)

is rational and has bit length \(O(\log m)\). There are \(g:=|\Gamma_m|=O(\log m)\) such parameters.

For \(h=m-1\), we have \(D=1\). Every simplex point satisfies \(\sum_i p_i^2\leq(\sum_i p_i)^2=1\), so the feasible set is the whole simplex. Because the utility coordinates are distinct, its unique maximizer is the vertex corresponding to the largest utility.

It remains to handle \(1/m<D<1\).

2. An exact characterization of the individual maximizer

Fix one utility vector and sort its coordinates, recording the permutation of alternative indices:

\(\displaystyle v_1>v_2>\cdots>v_m.\)

For a real number \(\lambda<v_2\), define

\(\displaystyle a_i(\lambda)=\max\{v_i-\lambda,0\},\qquad H(\lambda)=\sum_i a_i(\lambda),\)

and

\(\displaystyle F(\lambda)=\frac{\sum_i a_i(\lambda)^2}{H(\lambda)^2}.\)

There are at least two positive terms, so \(H(\lambda)>0\).

Existence of a threshold

The function \(F\) is continuous. On an open interval where exactly the first \(k\) coordinates are positive, define

\(\displaystyle \bar v_k=\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k v_i,\qquad V_k=\sum_{i=1}^k(v_i-\bar v_k)^2.\)

Here \(k\geq2\), and distinctness gives \(V_k>0\). Direct expansion gives

\(\displaystyle H(\lambda)=k(\bar v_k-\lambda),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^k(v_i-\lambda)^2=k(\bar v_k-\lambda)^2+V_k,\)

and hence

\(\displaystyle F(\lambda)=\frac1k+\frac{V_k}{k^2(\bar v_k-\lambda)^2}.\)

Differentiating within this interval,

\(\displaystyle F'(\lambda)=\frac{2V_k}{k^2(\bar v_k-\lambda)^3}>0.\)

There are only finitely many support-change points. Continuity across those points and strict increase on each intervening interval show that \(F\) is strictly increasing throughout \((-\infty,v_2)\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \lim_{\lambda\to-\infty}F(\lambda)=\frac1m,\qquad \lim_{\lambda\uparrow v_2}F(\lambda)=1.\)

The first limit follows from the formula with \(k=m\); the second follows because only \(a_1(\lambda)\) remains bounded away from zero.

Therefore, for every \(D\in(1/m,1)\), the intermediate value theorem gives a unique \(\lambda<v_2\) satisfying \(F(\lambda)=D\).

Define

\(\displaystyle p_i=\frac{a_i(\lambda)}{H(\lambda)}.\)

Then \(p\in\Delta_m\) and \(\sum_i p_i^2=D\).

Optimality and uniqueness

Write \(H=H(\lambda)>0\). By construction,

\(\displaystyle v_i=Hp_i+\lambda\quad\text{when }p_i>0,\)

and

\(\displaystyle v_i\leq Hp_i+\lambda=\lambda\quad\text{when }p_i=0.\)

For any feasible \(x\), nonnegativity and \(\sum_i x_i=1\) therefore imply

\(\displaystyle v^\top x\leq H p^\top x+\lambda.\)

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\(\displaystyle p^\top x\leq\|p\|_2\|x\|_2\leq D.\)

On the other hand,

\(\displaystyle v^\top p=H\sum_i p_i^2+\lambda=HD+\lambda.\)

Thus \(v^\top x\leq v^\top p\).

If equality holds, then \(p^\top x=D\), so equality holds in Cauchy–Schwarz. Consequently \(x\) is a nonnegative scalar multiple of \(p\). Their coordinate sums are both one, forcing \(x=p\).

This proves that the individual maximizer is unique. The prescribed lexicographic rule therefore makes no further choice.

3. Find the positive support using rational comparisons

Suppose the maximizer has exactly \(k\) positive coordinates. Necessarily \(2\leq k\leq m\). On that support,

\(\displaystyle p_i=\frac1k+s(v_i-\bar v_k),\qquad s=\frac1H>0.\)

Squaring and summing yields

\(\displaystyle D=\frac1k+s^2V_k.\)

Set

\(\displaystyle A_k=D-\frac1k.\)

The actual support therefore satisfies \(A_k>0\), and

\(\displaystyle s=\sqrt{\frac{A_k}{V_k}}.\)

We can identify that support by scanning \(k=2,\ldots,m\).

For each \(k\), first require \(A_k>0\). Define

\(\displaystyle \delta_k=\bar v_k-v_k>0.\)

The smallest proposed positive coordinate is positive exactly when

\(\displaystyle \frac1k-s\delta_k>0.\)

Since both \(1/k\) and \(s\delta_k\) are positive, squaring is equivalent to the strict rational inequality

\[\displaystyle A_k\delta_k^2<\frac{V_k}{k^2}.\tag{1}\]

If \(k<m\), also define

\(\displaystyle \delta_{k+1}=\bar v_k-v_{k+1}>0.\)

The threshold for the proposed lottery is

\(\displaystyle \lambda=\bar v_k-\frac1{ks}.\)

All omitted utilities are at most this threshold exactly when \(v_{k+1}\leq\lambda\), equivalently

\(\displaystyle s\delta_{k+1}\geq\frac1k.\)

Squaring the nonnegative quantities gives the rational inequality

\[\displaystyle A_k\delta_{k+1}^2\geq\frac{V_k}{k^2}.\tag{2}\]

For \(k=m\), condition (2) is omitted.

The actual support established in Section 2 satisfies these tests, so the scan always succeeds. Conversely, any accepted \(k\) produces a feasible vector with precisely its first \(k\) coordinates positive and with the threshold inequalities used in the optimality proof. It is therefore the unique maximizer.

The strict inequality in (1) and weak inequality in (2) handle zero-coordinate boundaries exactly. Candidates with \(A_k\leq0\) are safely skipped because the actual support satisfies \(A_k=s^2V_k>0\).

Thus no comparison involving a square root is required.

4. Polynomial bit complexity of the rational work

Sorting rational utility coordinates uses exact cross-multiplication. The quantities needed for the support tests can be computed from the prefix sums

\(\displaystyle S_k=\sum_{i=1}^k v_i,\qquad W_k=\sum_{i=1}^k v_i^2,\)

using

\(\displaystyle \bar v_k=\frac{S_k}{k},\qquad V_k=W_k-\frac{S_k^2}{k}.\)

We now bound their bit lengths.

For a fixed type, write its input coordinates as rational numerator–denominator pairs, and let \(Q\) be the product of their denominators. Since their combined encoding length is at most \(L\), the bit length of \(Q\) is \(O(L)\). Each coordinate can be written as \(v_i=B_i/Q\), where \(0\leq B_i\leq Q\).

Consequently:

  • \(S_k\) has a representation with denominator \(Q\) and numerator at most \(mQ\).
  • \(W_k\) has a representation with denominator \(Q^2\) and numerator at most \(mQ^2\).
  • \(\bar v_k\), \(V_k\), and the differences appearing in (1) and (2) have numerator and denominator bit lengths \(O(L+\log m)\).
  • Each side of either comparison is obtained by a bounded number of further rational operations, so its bit length remains \(O(L+\log m)\).

An implementation may reduce rational fractions after each operation; the displayed representations bound the reduced bit lengths as well. Exact arithmetic and comparison on integers of these lengths take polynomial time.

Sorting costs \(O(m\log m)\) rational comparisons per type. Prefix sums cost \(O(m)\) rational operations per type, and scanning supports costs \(O(m)\) rational operations per type–radius pair. The total rational work is therefore

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(\tau m\log m+\tau mg\right)\)

operations on polynomial-length integers. Enumerating the radii also takes polynomial time.

5. Construct the exact expected-lottery DAG

For every nonterminal type–radius pair, after finding its support \(k\), create the shared expression

\(\displaystyle s_{t,R}=\sqrt{\frac{D-1/k}{V_k}}.\)

Its radicand is strictly positive. For the sorted alternative indices \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\), emit

\(\displaystyle p_{t,R}(a_i)=\frac1k+s_{t,R}(v_i-\bar v_k)\quad\text{for }i\leq k,\)

and \(p_{t,R}(a_i)=0\) for \(i>k\). At the terminal radius, emit the already identified vertex.

Finally, for each original alternative index \(a\), build

\(\displaystyle q(a)=\frac1{2m}+\frac1{2g}\sum_{R\in\Gamma_m}\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\,p_{t,R}(a).\)

This is exactly the requested formula. The masses are used directly as rational coefficients.

A shared DAG with \(m\) designated outputs has \(O(m\tau g)\) nodes and at most \(\tau(g-1)\) square-root nodes. Every constant has polynomial bit length. If “binary-rational constants” means dyadic constants specifically, any rational coefficient can instead be emitted as a division of two binary-encoded integer constants.

All square roots are nonnegative square roots, and all divisions have nonzero denominators. DAG indices and constant encodings also have polynomial total length. Hence constructing and writing the exact output takes polynomial Turing time.

Conclusion

The individual lotteries are uniquely determined, their positive supports are computable using polynomial-time rational comparisons, and their weighted aggregation has a polynomial-size exact arithmetic-DAG representation. This completely settles the specified computation problem as polynomial-time solvable.
