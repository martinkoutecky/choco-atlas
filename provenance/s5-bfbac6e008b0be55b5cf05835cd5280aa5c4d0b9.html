Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(Q\) be a nonempty category set, \(m=|Q|\), with nonnegative rational additive values \(u_\theta(q)\) and nonnegative rational per-capita supplies \(\lambda_q\).
A share \(s(V,n)\ge0\) depends only on an agent's value multiset and the number of agents; it is feasible if every finite additive instance admits a complete allocation giving every agent at least its share.
Fix an increasing integer sequence \(N_k\to\infty\), with \(N_k\ge2\), \(N_k\mu_\theta\in\mathbb Z\), and \(N_k\lambda_q\in\mathbb Z\). At scale \(k\), there are \(N_k\mu_\theta\) type-\(\theta\) agents and \(N_k\lambda_q\) indivisible category-\(q\) goods.
The supplied nonnegative rational targets satisfy \(r_\theta=\lim_{k\to\infty}s(V_\theta^{(k)},N_k)\), where \(V_\theta^{(k)}\) contains \(N_k\lambda_q\) occurrences of \(u_\theta(q)\) for each \(q\).
A deterministic online policy knows the types, utilities, and supplies; an adaptive adversary orders the goods. Each arrival is observed and immediately assigned irrevocably to one agent; every good must be assigned.
Write \(\eta_{\theta,b}\) for the population mass holding integral bundle \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{Q}\), initially \(\eta_{\theta,0}=\mu_\theta\). Assigning one category-\(q\) good moves mass \(1/N_k\) from \(b\) to \(b+e_q\), where \(e_q\) is the corresponding unit vector.
Let \(\alpha^*\) be the supremum of \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) attainable by limiting causal policies against every adversarial order, requiring \(u_\theta\cdot b\ge\alpha r_\theta\) at every terminal state with positive mass. The convergence and realizability requirements for such limiting policies remain unspecified.
Is deciding \(\alpha^*\ge a\), for a binary-rational threshold \(a\in[0,1]\), in \(\mathrm P\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), and any additional encoded parameters.
For finite societies, every feasible share can be guaranteed online using exact per-agent value multisets; the construction is polynomial when a share-achieving offline allocation is computable in polynomial time, with online overhead \(O(M^2n)\) for \(M\) goods and \(n\) agents.
That guarantee concerns explicit finite instances and does not classify the limiting optimization problem stated here.
