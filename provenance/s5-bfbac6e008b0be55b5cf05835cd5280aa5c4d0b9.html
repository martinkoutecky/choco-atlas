The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational population distribution \(\mu\), finite category set \(Q\), additive values \(u_\theta(q)\), per-capita supplies \(\lambda_q\), an exact integer scaling sequence \(N_k\) with \(N_k\mu_\theta,N_k\lambda_q\in\mathbb Z\), and a target \(r_\theta\) obtained from the paper’s share \(s\) along that sequence, compute the supremal \(\alpha\) for the limit of the original one-good online process on \(N_k\lambda_q\) indivisible copies, against every adversarial arrival order, such that every positive-mass type-\(\theta\) state \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{Q}\) ends with \(u_\theta\cdot b\ge\alpha r_\theta\). Represent the limiting policy by mass transitions \(\eta_{\theta,b}\to\eta_{\theta,b+e_q}\), while every underlying agent receives an integral bundle.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(T\): the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each mass \(\mu_\theta\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). The support size \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
Let \(Q\) be a finite category set, \(m=|Q|\), with nonnegative rational additive values \(u_\theta(q)\) and nonnegative rational per-capita supplies \(\lambda_q\).
A share \(s(V,n)\) is a nonnegative function of an agent's value multiset and the number of agents; it is feasible if every finite additive-goods instance admits a complete allocation giving every agent at least her share.
For an integer sequence \(N_k\to\infty\) satisfying \(N_k\mu_\theta,N_k\lambda_q\in\mathbb Z\), the replicated instance has \(N_k\) agents, \(N_k\mu_\theta\) of each type, and \(N_k\lambda_q\) copies of category \(q\). Let \(V_{\theta,k}\) contain \(N_k\lambda_q\) occurrences of \(u_\theta(q)\) for each \(q\), and assume the finite targets \(r_\theta=\lim_{k\to\infty}s(V_{\theta,k},N_k)\) exist.
The allocator knows the frequency information in advance. An adaptive adversary chooses the arrival order subject to the prescribed supplies; each arrival reveals its full valuation vector and must immediately be assigned irrevocably to exactly one agent.
Represent population mass in integral bundle state \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{Q}\) by \(\eta_{\theta,b}\), initially \(\eta_{\theta,0}=\mu_\theta\). Allocation moves mass from \(b\) to \(b+e_q\), where \(e_q\) is the category unit vector, and must arise as a limit of the replicated one-good processes.
Let \(\alpha^*\) be the supremum of \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) for which such an online limiting policy guarantees \(u_\theta\cdot b\ge\alpha r_\theta\) at every terminal state with \(\eta_{\theta,b}>0\), against every admissible adaptive arrival order.
Is computing \(\alpha^*\) polynomial-time solvable, or NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), the total input bit length \(L\), and the parameters of the sequence and share representations.
For finite societies, every feasible share is attainable online with exact frequency predictions; a polynomial-time offline share-achieving algorithm yields a polynomial-time online algorithm, with allocation overhead \(\mathcal O(M^2n)\) for \(M\) goods and \(n\) agents.
