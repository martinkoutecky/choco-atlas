The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a finite set of item categories \(\Omega\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), and a price increment \(\varepsilon\), let each complete bidder type \(t\) have a monotone, \(\alpha\)-near-submodular valuation \(v_t(S)\) over integral bundles \(S\subseteq\Omega\). In a specified mass-SMRA with uniform category prices, type-state masses choose truthful conditional bids, category capacity is \(q_j\), and standing-high-bid mass is updated with a fixed measurable tie rule. Is every terminal outcome’s welfare at least \(\beta\,\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q)\), where \(\mathrm{OPT}\) maximizes welfare over feasible allocations of integral bundles subject to the type masses and category supplies; in particular, does the bound \(\beta=1/(1+\alpha)\) hold, with \(\alpha\)-individual rationality almost everywhere?

Conclusion

Both assertions are false for the specified auction, even with one category and \(\alpha=1\).

The welfare assertion fails because the bid increment is positive. The rationality assertion has a further obstruction: retained incumbents can face repeated price increases without placing new bids. This failure persists as the increment tends to zero.

1. Welfare: the worst-case ratio is zero

For one category \(\Omega=\{a\}\), every normalized monotone valuation is submodular: the only permitted pair in the near-submodularity condition is \(A=B=\varnothing\). Thus all examples below satisfy the condition with \(\alpha=1\).

The immediate counterexample has one type of mass \(1\), supply \(q_a=1\), and
\[ v(\{a\})=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. \]
Its conditional bid has utility \(-\varepsilon/2\), so the auction stops immediately. Consequently,
\[ W=0, \qquad \mathrm{OPT}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0. \]

The failure also occurs with positive bidding activity. Fix \(\varepsilon=1\), \(q_a=1\), and a positive dyadic rational \(\delta<1\). Use the following support, in the displayed order:

| Type | Mass | Value of \(\{a\}\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Low | \(1-\delta\) | \(1/2\) |
| High | \(\delta\) | \(2\) |

Initially, the low type strictly prefers the empty bid, while the high type strictly bids for \(a\). All high individuals receive it, and its price becomes \(1\).

In the next round, high individuals already hold the only category. Low individuals obtain conditional utility \(1/2-2<0\) from bidding. Thus this is terminal, with
\[ W=2\delta. \]
Since supply is \(1\) and both valuations are positive, allocating \(a\) to everyone is feasible and optimal. Hence
\[ \mathrm{OPT} =\frac{1-\delta}{2}+2\delta =\frac{1+3\delta}{2}, \qquad \frac{W}{\mathrm{OPT}} =\frac{4\delta}{1+3\delta}. \]

This ratio tends to zero as \(\delta\) tends to zero. For any prescribed rational \(\alpha\ge1\), these valuations are also \(\alpha\)-near-submodular, and choosing
\[ 0<\delta<\frac{1}{1+4\alpha} \]
gives
\[ W<\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{1+\alpha}. \]

Thus no positive multiplicative welfare guarantee holds under the stated hypotheses, even among instances with a nonempty updating round.

2. Rationality: retained holders can be overcharged arbitrarily

This construction uses fixed positive valuations and permits arbitrarily small increments.

Let \(\Omega=\{a\}\), \(q_a=3/4\), and choose integers \(K\ge2\) and \(n\ge2\). Set \(\varepsilon=1/n\). The support consists of two types:

| Type | Interval | Mass | Value of \(\{a\}\) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Low | \([0,1/2)\) | \(1/2\) | \(1\) |
| High | \([1/2,1)\) | \(1/2\) | \(K\) |

All valuations are normalized, monotone, and \(1\)-near-submodular.

Write
\[ J_0=[1/2,3/4), \qquad J_1=[3/4,1). \]

First update

At price zero, every individual strictly bids for \(a\), since \(1>\varepsilon\). The first \(3/4\) mass is selected. Therefore
\[ p_a^1=\frac1n, \qquad H_a^1=[0,1/2)\cup J_0. \]

Subsequent updates

Suppose that after \(r\ge1\) updates,
\[ p_a^r=\frac rn, \qquad H_a^r=[0,1/2)\cup J_i \]
for some \(i\in\{0,1\}\).

Every low individual holds \(a\), so has no category available to bid for. The high holders likewise cannot bid. The remaining high individuals, precisely \(J_{1-i}\), bid if and only if
\[ K-\frac{r+1}{n}>0. \]
At equality, the empty bid wins by minimum cardinality.

Whenever this inequality holds, newcomers have mass \(1/4\), and all are selected. The remaining capacity is \(1/2\). The first \(1/2\) mass of incumbents is exactly the entire low interval. Consequently,
\[ p_a^{r+1}=\frac{r+1}{n}, \qquad H_a^{r+1}=[0,1/2)\cup J_{1-i}. \]

This proves the asserted pattern inductively.

Terminal state and violation

There are exactly \(nK-1\) updating rounds. At their end,
\[ p_a=K-\frac1n. \]
The unallocated high individuals would obtain utility
\[ K-(p_a+\varepsilon)=0, \]
so choose the empty bid. Everyone else holds \(a\); hence the auction terminates.

Throughout, every low individual remains allocated \(a\). At termination, for every individual in its interval,
\[ v_{\mathrm{Low}}(\{a\})=1 < K-\frac1n =p_a. \]
Thus \(1\)-individual rationality fails on a set of mass \(1/2\).

More generally, for any prescribed \(\alpha\ge1\), choose \(K>\alpha+1/n\). The same valuations satisfy \(\alpha\)-near-submodularity, but
\[ \alpha v_{\mathrm{Low}}(\{a\}) =\alpha < p_a. \]

The ratio of price to the low type’s value is \(K-1/n\), so the violation is unbounded. For fixed \(K\), letting \(n\) tend to infinity makes \(\varepsilon\) tend to zero while the terminal price tends to \(K\). Therefore this failure does not disappear with small increments.

For completeness, this family’s welfare is
\[ W=\frac12+\frac K4, \qquad \mathrm{OPT}=\frac K2+\frac14. \]
The optimal allocation gives \(a\) to all high individuals and to mass \(1/4\) of low individuals. Its optimality follows because high value exceeds low value and total supply is \(3/4\). Hence
\[ \frac{W}{\mathrm{OPT}}=\frac{K+2}{2K+1}\longrightarrow\frac12. \]
This family demonstrates the rationality failure; it does not itself violate the welfare bound with \(\alpha=1\).

3. Finite termination and exact simulation

The counterexamples do not depend on a termination ambiguity. In fact, every specified instance terminates.

For each category define its maximum marginal value
\[ D_j= \max_{\substack{t\\A\subseteq\Omega\setminus\{j\}}} \bigl(v_t(A\cup\{j\})-v_t(A)\bigr). \]

If a chosen conditional bid \(T\) contains \(j\), optimality and minimum-cardinality tie-breaking imply
\[ v_t(S\cup T)-v_t\bigl(S\cup(T\setminus\{j\})\bigr) > p_j^r+\varepsilon. \]
Indeed, if the left side were smaller, deleting \(j\) would improve utility; if equal, deleting it would preserve utility and reduce cardinality.

Thus any price increase satisfies
\[ p_j^{r+1}=p_j^r+\varepsilon<D_j. \]
Starting from zero, category \(j\) can therefore increase at most
\[ C_j=\max\left\{0,\left\lceil\frac{D_j}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil-1\right\} \]
times. Every nonterminal round increases at least one category: a positive-mass set of nonempty bids is the finite union of the sets \(N_j\), so some \(N_j\) has positive mass. Consequently, the number \(R\) of updating rounds satisfies
\[ R\le\sum_{j\in\Omega}C_j. \]

An exact simulation can maintain a partition into intervals on which type and provisional bundle are constant. Initially there are \(\tau\) intervals. Each category update introduces at most two new endpoints, one for the newcomer prefix and one for the incumbent prefix. After \(R\) updates there are therefore at most \(\tau+2mR\) intervals.

Enumerating conditional bids on each interval and performing the prefix selections yields a bit-running-time bound of
\[ O\!\left( (R+1)(\tau+2mR)m\,2^m \operatorname{poly}\!\bigl(L+m+\log(R+2)\bigr) \right). \]
Endpoints remain integer multiples of the reciprocal of a common denominator of the input masses and supplies, whose binary length is \(O(L)\). Prices are integer multiples of \(\varepsilon\). Thus the arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity in the displayed arguments. The bound depends on the numerical ratios \(D_j/\varepsilon\), and need not be polynomial in \(L\).

4. Why the cited proof does not transfer

The rationality induction in [Bousquet, Cai and Vetta, Section 3.1](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/cai/publication/smra/smra.pdf) uses the fact that an incumbent retained across a round keeps the same item price. The specified mass rule permits retention together with a price increase; Section 2 above exhibits the resulting failure.

Their welfare proof also uses full allocation of every positive-price item. Here a positive-price category can remain undersupplied, as in Section 1.

Finally, terminal conditional optimality in the stated auction gives, for any comparison bundle \(A\), almost everywhere,
\[ v_t(A) \le v_t(S\cup A) \le v_t(S)+p(A\setminus S)+\varepsilon|A\setminus S|. \]
The first inequality is monotonicity. The second compares the empty terminal bid with \(A\setminus S\). Thus positive increments must also be accounted for in any attempted welfare argument.

These counterexamples completely settle both assertions for the auction as specified.

The partition argument is sound. Within each interval, type and bundle determine the same unique conditional bid. Each newcomer or incumbent prefix selection adds at most one boundary inside an existing interval. Simultaneous updates therefore add at most two boundaries per category, yielding the stated interval bound.

Exhaustive conditional-bid enumeration, price summation, and prefix selection fit the displayed running-time bound.

The arithmetic claim is also valid. Initial endpoints lie on the rational lattice generated by input masses and supplies. Prefix cuts preserve that lattice. This establishes an exact numerical-parameter-dependent simulation bound. It does not establish polynomial-time solvability in binary input length or a hardness classification.
