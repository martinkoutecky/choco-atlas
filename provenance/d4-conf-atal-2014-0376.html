The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given goods \(U\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_e\), and a finite set of types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) and public menus \(D_t\) of at most two whole bundles, determine the best worst-case welfare approximation ratio achievable in polynomial time by a truthful-in-expectation mechanism with the paper’s verification rule. The mechanism specifies a lottery \(\pi_r(S\mid\mu)\) for each reported type \(r\), with aggregate assignment masses \(x_{t,S}=\mu_t\pi_t(S\mid\mu)\); feasibility requires each type’s mass to be assigned and total use of each good to be at most \(q_e\). Truthfulness requires that, for each true type \(t\) and permissible report \(r\), the expected value under \(\pi_t(\cdot\mid\mu)\) is at least that under \(\pi_r(\cdot\mid\mu)\). Compare welfare with the optimum feasible mass allocation, or prove a lower bound on the achievable ratio.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(U\) contain \(m\ge2\) goods, with positive binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_e\). A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Each type \(t=(D_t,v_t)\) has an immutable public menu \(D_t\) of one or two distinct nonempty bundles and private nonnegative rational values. Values respect inclusion within the menu; zero values and ties are allowed, and \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\). The declaration domain contains every such valuation on the same public menu, including declarations outside the society's support.
A mechanism uses no payments and supplies a lottery \(\pi_r(\cdot\mid\mu)\) on \(D_r\cup\{\varnothing\}\) for every report \(r\). Individuals are nonatomic: an individual's deviation leaves \(\mu\) fixed. Lottery probabilities must be computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and the queried report's bit length.
For supported types, set \(x_{t,S}=\mu_t\pi_t(S\mid\mu)\). Feasibility means \(\sum_{S\in D_t\cup\{\varnothing\}}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\in D_t:e\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_e\); individuals receive whole bundles, while aggregate masses may be split.
Verification checks only the awarded nonempty bundle. A report \(r\) by true type \(t\) is permissible exactly when \(D_r=D_t\) and \(v_r(S)\le v_t(S)\) for every nonempty \(S\) with \(\pi_r(S\mid\mu)>0\); any positive probability of detection carries a prohibitive penalty. Truthfulness in expectation requires \(\sum_S\pi_t(S\mid\mu)v_t(S)\ge\sum_S\pi_r(S\mid\mu)v_t(S)\) for every permissible report, every rational true valuation on a public menu occurring in the society, and every society.
Welfare is \(W=\sum_t\sum_{S\in D_t}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\). Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be maximum welfare over feasible mass allocations without incentive constraints; an approximation factor \(\alpha\ge1\) guarantees \(W\ge\mathrm{OPT}/\alpha\) on every instance with \(\mathrm{OPT}>0\).

What is the optimal worst-case welfare approximation ratio achievable by polynomial-time truthful-in-expectation mechanisms in this mass model?

For discrete bidders, no truthful-in-expectation mechanism achieves approximation factor at most \(1.09\), even with two bidders, two goods, and public menus of at most two bundles, regardless of computation time. A polynomial-time universally truthful mechanism achieves \(O(\sqrt m)\). [Source](https://research.tees.ac.uk/files/6554847/586887.pdf)

5. The extremal gap reduces to two goods and one common menu

Let $\rho_{\mathrm{one}}$ be the supremum of $\mathrm{OPT}/F$ over instances with two goods $a,b$ in which every supported type has the common menu
$$ \bigl\{\{a\},\{b\}\bigr\}. $$
The two bundles are incomparable, so every pair of nonnegative values is valid.

Reduction theorem.
$$ \rho=\rho_{\mathrm{one}}. $$
Moreover, for every fixed $m\ge2$, the extremal gap among instances with exactly $m$ goods also equals $\rho_{\mathrm{one}}$.

Proof. The inequality $\rho_{\mathrm{one}}\le\rho$ follows because these instances belong to the original model. I prove the reverse inequality.

Fix an arbitrary instance and an unrestricted optimum $x^*$. Partition its supported types by their public menus. For each menu $D$ and bundle $S\in D$, define
$$ c_{D,S}=\sum_{t:D_t=D}x^*_{t,S}. $$
Let $V_D$ be the welfare contributed by that group, so
$$ \sum_D V_D=\mathrm{OPT}. $$

I construct a dominance-feasible table within every group. It will consume at most $c_{D,S}$ mass of each bundle and obtain welfare at least $V_D/\rho_{\mathrm{one}}$.

Groups with at most one positively allocated bundle

If all capacities $c_{D,S}$ are zero, then $V_D=0$; award nothing.

Otherwise, suppose only one bundle $S$ has positive capacity. Solve
$$ \max_y\sum_{t:D_t=D}v_t(S)y_t $$
subject to
$$ 0\le y_t\le\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t:D_t=D}y_t\le c_{D,S}. $$
The original allocation restricted to this group is feasible, so the optimum is at least $V_D$.

Averaging probabilities at equal $S$ values yields monotonicity in that value, by the exchange argument of Section 3. Awarding only $S$ therefore satisfies all supported incentive constraints, and hence all dominance constraints. Its welfare is at least $V_D$, which is at least $V_D/\rho_{\mathrm{one}}$.

This case includes singleton menus and two-bundle menus for which the optimum allocated only one bundle.

Groups with two positive bundle capacities

Let $D=\{S,T\}$ and suppose
$$ c_{D,S}>0,\qquad c_{D,T}>0. $$
Construct an auxiliary two-good instance:

  • Map $S$ to $\{a\}$ and $T$ to $\{b\}$.
  • Keep every group type’s mass and two numerical values.
  • Set supplies to $c_{D,S}$ and $c_{D,T}$.
  • If the group’s total mass $M_D$ is less than one, add a zero-valued type of mass $1-M_D$.

The auxiliary supplies are positive. Its masses sum to one, and all declarations are valid because its bundles are incomparable. Duplicate zero-valued types, if present, may be merged.

The original group allocation, with the added zero-valued mass receiving nothing, is feasible in the auxiliary instance. Therefore its unrestricted optimum satisfies
$$ \mathrm{OPT}_D\ge V_D. $$
By the definition of $\rho_{\mathrm{one}}$, its dominance-LP optimum satisfies
$$ F_D\ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}_D}{\rho_{\mathrm{one}}} \ge\frac{V_D}{\rho_{\mathrm{one}}}. $$

Take an optimal auxiliary dominance table. Set the lotteries of all zero-valued types to the empty lottery. This preserves welfare and feasibility. It also preserves dominance constraints: constraints whose true type has zero values are identically zero, while replacing a zero-valued reported type’s lottery by the empty lottery only decreases the right-hand side of other constraints.

Discard the added zero-valued mass and map the two bundles back to $S,T$. The resulting group table satisfies its dominance constraints, uses at most its two designated capacities, and obtains welfare at least $V_D/\rho_{\mathrm{one}}$.

Combining the groups

Dominance inequalities compare only types having the same public menu, so the constructed group tables can be combined.

For each original good $e$, total consumption is at most
$$ \sum_D\sum_{\substack{S\in D\\e\in S}}c_{D,S} = \sum_t\sum_{\substack{S\in D_t\\e\in S}}x^*_{t,S} \le q_e. $$
Thus the combined table is feasible for the original dominance LP. Its welfare is at least
$$ \sum_D\frac{V_D}{\rho_{\mathrm{one}}} = \frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{\rho_{\mathrm{one}}}. $$
Consequently,
$$ F\ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{\rho_{\mathrm{one}}}, $$
which proves $\rho\le\rho_{\mathrm{one}}$.

For exactly $m>2$ goods, any auxiliary two-good instance can be embedded by adding $m-2$ unused goods with positive supplies. This changes neither optimum. Hence the same supremum applies for every $m\ge2$. $\square$
