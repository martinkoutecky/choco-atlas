The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational referee ability types \(\alpha_1<\cdots<\alpha_\tau\) in \([\ell,1]\), where \(\ell=3-\sqrt{7}\), rational masses \(\mu_i\ge 0\) with \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\), and a fixed editor ability \(e\in[\ell,1]\), choose nonnegative pair masses \(y_{ij}\) for \(i\le j\). Require \(2y_{hh}+\sum_{i<h}y_{ih}+\sum_{j>h}y_{hj}=\mu_h\) for each \(h\). Maximize \(\sum_{i\le j}y_{ij}q(\alpha_i,\alpha_j,e)\), where \(q(x,y,z)=\frac{1}{32}\left(4(4+x+y)+\frac{4(x-y)^2}{(4-xy)z}+(4-xy)z\right)\). Return an optimal mass pairing \(y\); the total article mass is \(1/2\).

The editor has a rational ability \(e\in[\ell,1]\), supplied as input and held fixed across articles. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. Mass is divisible; a same-type pair uses two distinct portions of that type's mass.
The reliability of an article assigned referee abilities \(x,y\) is \(q(x,y,e)\), where \(q(x,y,z)=\frac1{32}\left(4(4+x+y)+\frac{4(x-y)^2}{(4-xy)z}+(4-xy)z\right)\).
Choose real pair masses \(y_{ij}\ge0\) for \(1\le i\le j\le\tau\), subject to \(2y_{hh}+\sum_{i<h}y_{ih}+\sum_{j>h}y_{hj}=\mu_h\) for every \(h\). Thus all referee mass is used and total article mass is \(\sum_{i\le j}y_{ij}=1/2\).
The optimization problem is to return an optimal rational pairing maximizing \(\sum_{i\le j}y_{ij}q(\alpha_i,\alpha_j,e)\); any optimum is acceptable.

Conclusion

The exact divisible-mass pairing problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(\tau\) and \(L\). It is a rational linear program with polynomially many variables and polynomial encoding length. Its feasible region is nonempty and bounded, and an exact rational optimum can be returned in polynomial time.

1. Rational linear-program formulation

Let \(N=\tau(\tau+1)/2\), and index the coordinates of \(y\in\mathbb R^N\) by pairs \((i,j)\) with \(i\le j\). Define the matrix \(A\in\mathbb Z^{\tau\times N}\) by giving its columns:

  • The column for \((h,h)\) is \(2v_h\).
  • The column for \((i,j)\), where \(i<j\), is \(v_i+v_j\).

Here \(v_h\) is the \(h\)-th standard unit vector. Thus the prescribed mass constraints are exactly \(Ay=\mu\), \(y\ge0\).

Set \(c_{ij}=q(\alpha_i,\alpha_j,e)\). The requested optimization is precisely

\(\displaystyle \max\{c^{\mathsf T}y:Ay=\mu,\ y\ge0\}.\)

Every coefficient \(c_{ij}\) is rational. Indeed, the abilities and editor ability are rational, and the only potentially vanishing denominator in the formula satisfies

\(\displaystyle (4-\alpha_i\alpha_j)e\ge3\ell>0,\)

because \(\alpha_i,\alpha_j\le1\) and \(\ell=3-\sqrt7>0\).

Evaluating each coefficient requires a fixed number of rational arithmetic operations. Each input rational has encoding length at most \(L\), so every resulting coefficient has encoding length \(O(L)\) and can be computed in polynomial time. Consequently, even a dense encoding of the entire linear program has length \(O(\tau^3+\tau^2L)\), which is polynomial in the requested parameters.

2. Feasibility and attainment

The assignment \(y_{hh}=\mu_h/2\), with all off-diagonal coordinates zero, is feasible. It uses two distinct portions of each type’s mass, as permitted by the hypothesis.

Every column of \(A\) has column sum \(2\). Summing all mass constraints therefore gives

\(\displaystyle 2\sum_{i\le j}y_{ij}=\sum_h\mu_h=1.\)

Hence every feasible coordinate belongs to \([0,1/2]\). The feasible region is closed, bounded, and nonempty, so it is compact. The linear objective is continuous and therefore attains its maximum.

3. An optimal rational pairing has polynomial encoding length

For completeness, we prove an explicit bound on the size of an optimal pairing.

Choose an optimum \(y^*\) with the smallest possible number of positive coordinates, and let \(S\) be its positive support. The columns of \(A\) indexed by \(S\) must be linearly independent.

To prove this, suppose otherwise. There is a nonzero vector \(d\), supported on \(S\), such that \(Ad=0\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both \(y^*+\varepsilon d\) and \(y^*-\varepsilon d\) are feasible. Optimality of \(y^*\) then forces \(c^{\mathsf T}d=0\).

Moreover, summing the coordinates of \(Ad=0\) gives \(2\sum_kd_k=0\). Thus \(d\) has both positive and negative coordinates. Taking

\(\displaystyle t=\min_{k:d_k<0}\frac{y_k^*}{-d_k}>0\)

makes \(y^*+td\) feasible, preserves its objective value, and removes at least one positive coordinate without introducing any outside \(S\). This contradicts the choice of \(y^*\).

It follows that \(|S|\le\tau\). The matrix \(A\) has row rank \(\tau\), since its diagonal-pair columns form \(2I_\tau\). Extend the independent columns indexed by \(S\) to a basis matrix \(B\in\mathbb Z^{\tau\times\tau}\). All coordinates outside this basis are zero, and the basis coordinates satisfy \(By_B^*=\mu\).

Write each mass as \(\mu_h=p_h/d_h\), and let \(D=\prod_hd_h\). Then \(D\mu\) is integral and \(\log_2D=O(L)\). Cramer’s rule gives

\(\displaystyle y_{B,k}^*=\frac{\det B^{(k)}}{D\det B},\)

where \(B^{(k)}\) replaces column \(k\) of \(B\) by \(D\mu\).

Every column of \(B\) has Euclidean norm at most \(2\). Also, \(\|D\mu\|_2\le\|D\mu\|_1=D\), using positivity and \(\sum_h\mu_h=1\). Hadamard’s inequality therefore yields

\(\displaystyle 1\le|\det B|\le2^\tau,\qquad |\det B^{(k)}|\le D\,2^{\tau-1}.\)

Thus every coordinate of this optimum is rational with numerator and denominator of encoding length \(O(L+\tau)\). An exact optimal output has polynomial size.

4. Exact polynomial-time algorithm

We use the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem: a linear program with explicitly encoded rational data can be solved in time polynomial in its binary encoding length, returning an exact rational optimal solution whenever it is feasible with finite optimum. Exact polynomial-time methods and rational reconstruction are described in [Gleixner–Steffy, Section 1.3](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/7507.pdf).

All hypotheses have been verified: the constructed data are rational and have polynomial encoding length; the feasible region is nonempty; and the optimum is attained and finite. Therefore the algorithm is:

  • Compute all \(c_{ij}\) exactly.
  • Construct \(A\), \(\mu\), and the rational linear program above.
  • Solve it with an exact polynomial-time rational LP algorithm.
  • Return its rational coordinates \(y_{ij}\).

The running time is polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\). This directly solves the stated divisible-mass problem, without expanding masses into discrete referees or requiring a characterization of the optimal pairing order.
