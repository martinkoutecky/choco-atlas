The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite outcome space \(\mathcal O\), costs \(c\), a subadditive merge operation, budget \(B\), explicit comparison set \(C\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) complete preference types, compute an efficiently sampleable distribution over pairs \((o^*,\nu)\). Require \(c(o^*)\le B\) and, in every realization, \(\sum_{t:o\succ_t o^*}\nu_t\le \gamma c(o)/B\) for every \(o\in C\), where \(0\le \nu_t\le \mu_t\). Require also \(\mathbb E[\nu_t]\ge \lambda\mu_t\) for every type, with \(\lambda=1-e^{-\alpha(1-\theta)}\) and \(\gamma=(\alpha+1)/(2\theta)\), for fixed \(\alpha>0\) and \(\theta\in(0,1)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An instance explicitly lists a finite outcome space \(\mathcal O\), with \(m=|\mathcal O|\ge2\), binary-rational costs and budget \(B>0\), a merge table, and a comparison set \(\bot\in C\subseteq\mathcal O\). The outside option satisfies \(c(\bot)=0\), all other costs are positive, and \(\min_{o\ne\bot}c(o)=1\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type explicitly specifies a strict total order on \(\mathcal O\), with \(\bot\) last; \(\succeq_t\) denotes its weak extension.
The supplied operation \(\oplus:\mathcal O^2\to\mathcal O\) satisfies \(c(o\oplus o')\le c(o)+c(o')\) and \(o\oplus o'\succeq_t o,o'\) for every supported type and every pair of outcomes.
Fix rational constants \(\alpha>0\) and \(\theta\in(0,1)\), and set \(\lambda=1-e^{-\alpha(1-\theta)}\) and \(\gamma=(\alpha+1)/(2\theta)\).
The computational problem is to construct a finitely encoded sampler for a distribution over pairs \((o^*,\nu)\), with binary-rational retained masses, satisfying in every realization \(c(o^*)\le B\), \(0\le\nu_t\le\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t:o\succ_t o^*}\nu_t\le\gamma c(o)/B\) for every \(o\in C\), and satisfying \(\mathbb E[\nu_t]\ge\lambda\mu_t\) for every type. Retained mass need not sum to one. Sampling is exact and may use expected polynomial time; \(L\) is the total input bit length.

For each fixed \(\alpha,\theta\), can this construction problem be solved in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,|C|,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a finite list of \(n\) voters, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(n\) and \(|C|\) is established for randomized partial-core solutions with these same \(\lambda,\gamma\) guarantees ([Song and Nguyen, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04312)). No result settling the exact support-encoded mass formulation was located.

Result

The construction problem is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed rational \(\alpha>0\) and \(\theta\in(0,1)\). The sampler can have at most \(m\) possible outputs, with dyadic retained masses, and can sample exactly in expected polynomial time.

The key is that the outcomes are explicitly listed. Conditional on any chosen outcome, the permitted retained-mass vectors form a rational polytope. Their conditional averages therefore remain permitted.

1. A polynomial-size linear program

Let \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) index the supported types, and define
\[ \]
\(F=\{a\in\mathcal O:c(a)\le B\}\),
\(S(a,o)=\{t\in T:o\succ_t a\}\), and
\(g_o=\gamma c(o)/B\).

The set \(F\) is nonempty because \(\bot\in F\).

Consider variables \(p_a\) for \(a\in F\), variables \(x_{a,t}\) for \(a\in F,t\in T\), and a variable \(\rho\). Solve the following rational linear program:

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad &\rho\\ \text{subject to}\quad &\sum_{a\in F}p_a=1,\qquad p_a\ge0,\\ &0\le x_{a,t}\le\mu_t p_a && (a\in F,\ t\in T),\\ &\sum_{t\in S(a,o)}x_{a,t}\le g_o p_a && (a\in F,\ o\in C),\\ &\sum_{a\in F}x_{a,t}\ge\rho\mu_t && (t\in T),\\ &0\le\rho\le1. \end{aligned} \)

Here \(p_a\) represents an outcome probability, while \(x_{a,t}\) represents that outcome’s contribution to type \(t\)’s expected retained mass.

Exactness of the formulation. Suppose a distribution satisfies all realization constraints and guarantees expected retention at least \(\rho\mu_t\). Set

\(p_a=\Pr(o^*=a)\), and
\(x_{a,t}=\mathbb E[\nu_t\,\mathbf1_{\{o^*=a\}}]\).

Taking expectations of the realization constraints, after multiplying by \(\mathbf1_{\{o^*=a\}}\), gives every constraint of the linear program.

Conversely, given a feasible point, discard outcomes with \(p_a=0\). Their associated \(x_{a,t}\) are zero. For each remaining outcome define

\(v^a_t=x_{a,t}/p_a\).

Dividing the corresponding constraints by \(p_a\) yields

\(0\le v^a_t\le\mu_t\), and
\(\displaystyle\sum_{t\in S(a,o)}v^a_t\le g_o\quad(o\in C)\).

Thus choosing \((a,v^a)\) with probability \(p_a\) satisfies every realization constraint. Its expectations satisfy

\(\displaystyle\mathbb E[v_t]=\sum_{a\in F}x_{a,t}\ge\rho\mu_t\).

In particular, rational feasible points give finitely encoded rational distributions.

2. Existence with a positive margin

We prove a slightly stronger existence statement: there is a constant \(R>\lambda\), depending only on \(\alpha,\theta\), such that the linear-program optimum is at least \(R\). This also permits dyadic output masses.

We use the following finite-voter existence theorem: under the supplied merging assumption, for every \(\beta>0\), there exists a distribution over feasible outcomes and retained voter subsets such that each voter is retained with probability at least \(1-e^{-\beta}\), and each realization satisfies the partial-core inequality with factor \(\beta+1\). This is Theorem 1 of [Song and Nguyen, *Ordinal Lindahl Equilibrium for Voting*](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04312v1#S4).

Its hypotheses hold here: the outcome space is finite, preferences are strict with \(\bot\) last, costs have the required normalization, and the given operation satisfies subadditive cost and unanimous weak improvement.

Passing from finite voters to rational masses

Choose a positive integer \(N\) such that \(N\mu_t\) is an integer for every type. Consider a finite society containing \(N\mu_t\) copies of type \(t\). This society is used only in the existence proof; the algorithm never constructs it.

Apply the finite-voter theorem. For any fixed \(s\in(0,1]\), define in each realization

\(\displaystyle w_t=\frac{s}{N}\,\bigl|\{\text{retained copies of type }t\}\bigr|.\)

Then

\(0\le w_t\le\mu_t\),
\(\displaystyle\mathbb E[w_t]\ge s(1-e^{-\beta})\mu_t\), and
\(\displaystyle\sum_{t\in S(o^*,o)}w_t\le s(\beta+1)c(o)/B.\)

For alternatives costing more than \(B\), the unscaled voter inequality holds trivially because \((\beta+1)c(o)/B>1\). Consequently these inequalities apply to every comparison in \(C\).

Case 1: \(\gamma\ge2\)

Take \(\beta=\gamma-1\) and \(s=1\). This gives retention

\(R=1-e^{-(\gamma-1)}\)

with the required factor \(\gamma\).

Write \(r=\alpha(1-\theta)\), so \(\lambda=1-e^{-r}\). Since \(\gamma\ge2\), we have \(\alpha\ge4\theta-1\). Hence

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} \gamma-1-r &=\frac{\alpha(1-2\theta+2\theta^2)+1-2\theta}{2\theta}\\ &\ge \frac{(4\theta-1)(1-2\theta+2\theta^2)+1-2\theta}{2\theta}\\ &=4\theta^2-5\theta+2\\ &=4(\theta-5/8)^2+7/16>0. \end{aligned} \)

The inequality uses \(1-2\theta+2\theta^2>0\). Thus \(R>\lambda\).

Case 2: \(0<\gamma<2\)

Take \(\beta=1\) and \(s=\gamma/2\). This gives the required factor \(\gamma\) and retention

\(\displaystyle R=\frac{\gamma}{2}(1-e^{-1}).\)

To compare this with \(\lambda\), observe that for every \(x\ge0\),

\(\displaystyle (1-e^{-x})^2\le x/2.\)

Indeed, the difference \(h(x)=x/2-(1-e^{-x})^2\) satisfies \(h(0)=0\) and

\(\displaystyle h'(x)=2(e^{-x}-1/2)^2\ge0.\)

Using this inequality with \(x=\alpha(1-\theta)\), and using \(\alpha+1\ge2\sqrt{\alpha}\), gives

\( \displaystyle \frac{\lambda}{\gamma} =\frac{2\theta(1-e^{-\alpha(1-\theta)})}{\alpha+1} \le\theta\sqrt{\frac{1-\theta}{2}} \le\sqrt{\frac{2}{27}}. \)

The last inequality follows by maximizing \(\theta^2(1-\theta)\): its derivative is \(\theta(2-3\theta)\), so its maximum on \([0,1]\) is \(4/27\).

Finally,

\(\displaystyle \sqrt{\frac{2}{27}}<\frac3{10}<\frac{1-e^{-1}}2.\)

The first inequality follows by squaring; the second follows from \(e>5/2\). Therefore \(R>\lambda\) in this case as well.

In both cases, choose a fixed rational constant \(\delta\) satisfying

\(0<\delta<R-\lambda.\)

Such a constant can be found using certified rational approximations to the exponential. Since \(\alpha,\theta\) are fixed, this computation is independent of the instance.

The constructed distribution is feasible for the linear program with \(\rho=R\). Consequently its optimum satisfies

\(\rho^*\ge R>\lambda+\delta.\)

3. Polynomial-time construction and dyadic masses

The linear program has at most \(m\tau+m+1\) variables and \(O(m\tau+m|C|+\tau)\) constraints. Its coefficients are rational with bit length polynomial in \(L\), because \(\gamma\) is a fixed rational constant.

The feasible region is nonempty: take \(p_\bot=1\), all \(x_{a,t}=0\), and \(\rho=0\). It is bounded because every variable lies between zero and one.

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore supplies an exact rational optimal solution of polynomial encoding length. All its hypotheses—rational coefficients, finite explicit description, feasibility, and boundedness—hold. No computation or comparison involving \(\lambda\) is needed by this optimization algorithm.

For each positive \(p_a\), compute \(v^a_t=x_{a,t}/p_a\). These rational numbers have polynomial encoding length. If arbitrary binary-encoded rational retained masses are permitted, they already provide the required sampler.

To obtain dyadic retained masses as well, let

\(\mu_{\min}=\min_{t\in T}\mu_t>0\),

and choose an integer \(K\ge0\) such that

\(2^{-K}\le\delta\mu_{\min}.\)

Because the input masses have binary encoding length at most \(L\) and \(\delta\) is fixed, \(K=O(L+1)\) suffices. Define

\(\displaystyle \widehat v^a_t=2^{-K}\lfloor2^K v^a_t\rfloor.\)

Rounding decreases each coordinate. Thus every realization constraint remains satisfied, including the upper bounds by \(\mu_t\). Moreover,

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} \sum_{a\in F}p_a\widehat v^a_t &\ge\sum_{a\in F}x_{a,t}-2^{-K}\\ &\ge\rho^*\mu_t-\delta\mu_{\min}\\ &\ge(\rho^*-\delta)\mu_t\\ &\ge\lambda\mu_t. \end{aligned} \)

The resulting distribution has at most \(m\) outputs \((a,\widehat v^a)\), each of polynomial encoding length.

4. Exact sampling

Write the positive rational probabilities \(p_a\) with a common denominator \(D\), and let \(w_a=Dp_a\). Taking the product of their denominators gives a suitable \(D\) of polynomial bit length. The integers \(w_a\) sum to \(D\).

Generate a uniform integer from \(\{0,\ldots,D-1\}\) by rejection sampling: generate \(\lceil\log_2D\rceil\) fair bits and reject the resulting integer if it is at least \(D\). For \(D=1\), sampling is deterministic. Otherwise each attempt succeeds with probability greater than \(1/2\).

Partition the accepted integers into consecutive intervals of lengths \(w_a\), and output the associated pair \((a,\widehat v^a)\). This samples the specified distribution exactly, with expected polynomial running time.

Thus construction takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,|C|,L\), and every required guarantee holds.
