2. Definitions and correspondence with the anchor

For each action $(i,d)$, assume finite expected reward $a_{i,d}\geq0$ and finite expected processing time $b_{i,d}>0$. A stationary lottery satisfies

$$ P(i,d)\geq0,\qquad \sum_{i\in I}\sum_{d\in D}P(i,d)=1. $$

Define

$$ T(P)=\sum_{i,d}P(i,d)b_{i,d}, \qquad \rho_i(P)=\frac{\sum_dP(i,d)a_{i,d}}{T(P)}. $$

The denominator is positive because it is a convex combination of strictly positive numbers.

In the anchor, these statistics are

$$ a_{i,d}=\mathbb E\!\left[R_{i,1}\mathbf1_{\{X_{i,1}\leq d\}}\right], \qquad b_{i,d}=\mathbb E[\min\{X_{i,1},d\}]. $$

Its positive completion times and positive finite deadlines imply $b_{i,d}>0$; its bounded deadline-truncated rewards imply $a_{i,d}<\infty$. Proposition 2 gives precisely the displayed rate expression. Sections 2–3 model finite groups and deadlines, omit individual assignment within groups, and allow arbitrary positive utility weights. Definition 3 optimizes the limiting stationary objective. [Cayci, Gupta, and Eryilmaz, *Group-Fair Online Allocation in Continuous Time*](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/9ec0cfdc84044494e10582436e013e64-Paper.pdf).

For $x>0$, let

$$ u_\alpha(x)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{x^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha},&\alpha\ne1,\\ \log x,&\alpha=1. \end{cases} $$

Use the one-sided extensions

$$ u_\alpha(0)= \begin{cases} 0,&0<\alpha<1,\\ -\infty,&\alpha\geq1. \end{cases} $$

The mass and weighted objectives are, respectively,

$$ F_\alpha(P)=\sum_i\mu_i u_\alpha\!\left(\frac{\rho_i(P)}{\mu_i}\right), \qquad G_{\alpha,w}(P)=\sum_iw_i u_\alpha(\rho_i(P)). $$

All these extended sums are defined: no summand is $+\infty$.

3. Proven claim: exact objective equivalence

If $\alpha\ne1$ and $\rho_i(P)>0$, then

$$ \mu_i u_\alpha\!\left(\frac{\rho_i(P)}{\mu_i}\right) = \frac{\mu_i\bigl(\rho_i(P)/\mu_i\bigr)^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha} = \mu_i^\alpha u_\alpha(\rho_i(P)). $$

This equality also holds at zero rates under the specified extensions. Thus

$$ F_\alpha(P)=G_{\alpha,w}(P), \qquad w_i=\mu_i^\alpha>0. $$

For $\alpha=1$, including the extended interpretation at zero,

$$ \mu_i\log\!\left(\frac{\rho_i(P)}{\mu_i}\right) = \mu_i\log\rho_i(P)-\mu_i\log\mu_i. $$

Consequently,

$$ F_1(P)=G_{1,w}(P)-\sum_i\mu_i\log\mu_i, \qquad w_i=\mu_i>0. $$

The subtracted constant is finite and independent of $P$. Therefore, in every case,

$$ \operatorname*{arg\,max}_P F_\alpha(P) = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_P G_{\alpha,w}(P). $$

This includes all ties and degenerate extended-valued cases.

4. Precise stationary characterization and its domain

Define the optimal reward-to-time ratio of group $i$ by

$$ r_i^*=\max_{d\in D}\frac{a_{i,d}}{b_{i,d}}. $$

Choose any maximizing deadline $d_i^*$ and write $\tau_i=b_{i,d_i^*}>0$. The paper denotes this mean processing time by $\mu_i$; here $\tau_i$ avoids confusion with the group mass.

When every $r_i^*>0$, the stationary lottery characterized by Proposition 5, for $\alpha\ne1$, is

$$ c_i=w_i^{1/\alpha}(r_i^*)^{1/\alpha-1}, \qquad \phi_i^*=\frac{c_i}{\sum_jc_j}, $$

$$ P^*(i,d)= \mathbf1_{\{d=d_i^*\}} \frac{c_i/\tau_i}{\sum_jc_j/\tau_j}. $$

For logarithmic utility, Corollary 1(i) gives

$$ \phi_i^*=\frac{w_i}{\sum_jw_j}, \qquad P^*(i,d)= \mathbf1_{\{d=d_i^*\}} \frac{w_i/\tau_i}{\sum_jw_j/\tau_j}. $$

Here $\phi_i^*$ is the processing-time fraction, rather than the action-selection probability. These are the relevant stationary formulas from [Proposition 5 and Corollary 1(i)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/9ec0cfdc84044494e10582436e013e64-Paper.pdf). Positive finite ratios and weights make every numerator and normalization positive and finite. No uniqueness of the maximizing deadline is required.

5. Proof of the characterization

Reduction to processing-time fractions

For any lottery, set

$$ q_{i,d}=\frac{P(i,d)b_{i,d}}{T(P)}. $$

Then $q_{i,d}\geq0$ and $\sum_{i,d}q_{i,d}=1$. Conversely, every such $q$ determines a lottery through

$$ P(i,d)= \frac{q_{i,d}/b_{i,d}} {\sum_{j,e}q_{j,e}/b_{j,e}}. $$

Indeed, putting $H=\sum_{j,e}q_{j,e}/b_{j,e}>0$, this lottery has $T(P)=1/H$ and recovers the original $q$.

Write

$$ r_{i,d}=\frac{a_{i,d}}{b_{i,d}}, \qquad \phi_i=\sum_dq_{i,d}. $$

The rates satisfy

$$ \rho_i(P)=\sum_dq_{i,d}r_{i,d}\leq r_i^*\phi_i. $$

Every vector $\phi_i\geq0$ with $\sum_i\phi_i=1$ attains equality simultaneously: take $q_{i,d_i^*}=\phi_i$ and all other entries zero.

Since $u_\alpha$ is increasing, maximizing $G_{\alpha,w}$ therefore reduces exactly to maximizing

$$ H_{\alpha,w}(\phi)=\sum_iw_i u_\alpha(r_i^*\phi_i) $$

over this simplex.

All groups have positive reward-to-time ratios

Assume $r_i^*>0$ for every $i$. On positive arguments,

$$ u_\alpha'(x)=x^{-\alpha}>0, \qquad u_\alpha''(x)=-\alpha x^{-\alpha-1}<0. $$

Thus each summand is strictly concave.

For $\alpha\ne1$, put

$$ c_i=w_i^{1/\alpha}(r_i^*)^{1/\alpha-1}, \qquad Z=\sum_ic_i, \qquad \phi_i^*=\frac{c_i}{Z}. $$

Every $\phi_i^*$ is positive, and direct differentiation gives

$$ \left. \frac{d}{dx}\bigl[w_i u_\alpha(r_i^*x)\bigr] \right|_{x=\phi_i^*} = w_i(r_i^*)^{1-\alpha}(\phi_i^*)^{-\alpha} = Z^\alpha. $$

The derivatives are therefore equal across groups. Concavity gives, for every feasible $\phi$,

$$ H_{\alpha,w}(\phi) \leq H_{\alpha,w}(\phi^*) + Z^\alpha\sum_i(\phi_i-\phi_i^*) = H_{\alpha,w}(\phi^*). $$

For $0<\alpha<1$, the tangent inequality extends to zero by continuity. For $\alpha>1$, a zero coordinate makes the left side $-\infty$, so the inequality remains valid. Strict concavity makes $\phi^*$ the unique maximizing time-fraction vector.

For $\alpha=1$, let $W=\sum_iw_i$ and $\phi_i^*=w_i/W$. Then

$$ \left. \frac{d}{dx}\bigl[w_i\log(r_i^*x)\bigr] \right|_{x=\phi_i^*} = \frac{w_i}{\phi_i^*}=W. $$

The same tangent argument proves optimality and uniqueness; boundary vectors have objective $-\infty$.

Applying the inverse transformation from $q$ to $P$ yields exactly the lotteries in Section 4. The optimal stationary weighted values are

$$ \max_PG_{\alpha,w}(P)=\frac{Z^\alpha}{1-\alpha} \qquad(\alpha\ne1), $$

and

$$ \max_PG_{1,w}(P) = \sum_iw_i\log\!\left(\frac{r_i^*w_i}{W}\right). $$

For the power case, the value follows by substituting $\phi_i^*=c_i/Z$ and using $c_i^\alpha=w_i(r_i^*)^{1-\alpha}$.

Zero-reward groups when $0<\alpha<1$

Let

$$ I_+=\{i:r_i^*>0\}. $$

If $I_+$ is empty, all $a_{i,d}$ are zero because $b_{i,d}>0$ and $a_{i,d}\geq0$. Every rate and both objectives are zero. Every lottery is optimal; no normalized formula is needed.

Suppose $I_+$ is nonempty. Zero-reward groups contribute zero utility. For a feasible $\phi$, put $s=\sum_{i\in I_+}\phi_i$.

If $s=0$, its objective is zero, whereas allocating positive time to a positive-reward group gives strictly positive utility. Such a vector is not optimal.

If $0<s<1$, replace $\phi_i$ by $\phi_i/s$ on $I_+$ and by zero elsewhere. This is feasible, increases every positive rate among those groups, and increases at least one strictly. Strict increase of the utilities proves that the original vector is not optimal.

Hence every optimum allocates all processing time to $I_+$. Applying the preceding positive-ratio proof on $I_+$ gives the characterization, with all sums restricted to $I_+$ and zero lottery probability elsewhere. Its normalizations are positive.

Zero-reward groups when $\alpha\geq1$

If some $r_i^*=0$, then $\rho_i(P)=0$ for every lottery. Its positively weighted utility is $-\infty$, while all other summands are finite or $-\infty$. Therefore,

$$ F_\alpha(P)=G_{\alpha,w}(P)=-\infty \qquad\text{for every }P. $$

There is no finite-valued feasible objective. Under extended-valued maximization, every lottery attains the common value $-\infty$. The positive-ratio characterization is not applicable.

Conversely, if every $r_i^*>0$, giving positive probability to one positive-reward action per group produces simultaneous positive rates. Thus this condition is exactly what is needed for a finite-valued feasible objective when $\alpha\geq1$.

6. Consequence for the mass formulation

For the mass weights, the power-utility coefficients simplify to

$$ c_i=\mu_i(r_i^*)^{1/\alpha-1}. $$

For logarithmic utility,

$$ \phi_i^*=\frac{\mu_i}{\sum_j\mu_j}. $$

These substitutions preserve the feasible lotteries and require no list of individual workers. Zero-reward cases have been completely accounted for above.

Accordingly, the finite-support mass formulation is already covered by the weighted stationary optimization problem: its nondegenerate characterization is the anchor’s formula with the displayed weights, and its degenerate cases are elementary. This establishes the stated specialization, without asserting an additional computational-complexity classification.
