The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite catalogue \(\mathcal G\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) over complete valuation types \(u_t:2^{\mathcal G}\to\mathbb Z\), and a superadditive seller reserve function \(u_0\), compute a continuous NPW equilibrium when catalogue supply scales to one unit of each item per unit of buyer mass. Output anonymous bundle prices \(p(S)\), assignment masses \(x_{t,S}\), unsold-package masses \(r_S\), and a distribution \(\lambda_\gamma\) over catalogue partitions. Require \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_t x_{t,S}+r_S=\sum_{\gamma\ni S}\lambda_\gamma\) for each nonempty \(S\), and \(\sum_\gamma\lambda_\gamma=1\); assignments must use bundles maximizing \(u_t(S)-p(S)\), supported partitions must maximize partition revenue, and \(r_S>0\) only if \(p(S)=u_0(S)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(\mathcal G\) be a catalogue of \(m\geq1\) items. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Supply is one unit of each item.
Each type's valuation \(u_t:2^{\mathcal G}\to\mathbb Z\) and the seller's reserve function \(u_0:2^{\mathcal G}\to\mathbb Z\) are given by complete tables, may have either sign, vanish at \(\varnothing\), and satisfy \(u_i(A\cup B)\geq u_i(A)+u_i(B)\) whenever \(A\cap B=\varnothing\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. Buyers have quasilinear utility and no binding payment budget.
Let \(\Gamma\) be all partitions of \(\mathcal G\) into nonempty bundles. A continuous nonlinear pricing Walrasian equilibrium consists of anonymous rational prices \(p(S)\geq u_0(S)\), with \(p(\varnothing)=0\), and nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,S}\), \(r_S\), and \(\lambda_\gamma\).
Require \(\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal G}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_t x_{t,S}+r_S=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma:S\in\gamma}\lambda_\gamma\) for every nonempty \(S\), and \(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\lambda_\gamma=1\).
Require \(x_{t,S}>0\) only when \(S\in\arg\max_{A\subseteq\mathcal G}\{u_t(A)-p(A)\}\), \(\lambda_\gamma>0\) only when \(\gamma\in\arg\max_{\delta\in\Gamma}\sum_{A\in\delta}p(A)\), and \(r_S>0\) only when \(p(S)=u_0(S)\). Tied maximizers may share mass. Represent the partition distribution by its positive entries, with omitted entries zero.

Is computing such a continuous equilibrium solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Discrete: the sincere ascending auction terminates at an NPW equilibrium in finitely many rounds; this is a finite-termination guarantee, not a polynomial-time bound ([Sun–Yang](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/economics/documents/discussionpapers/2014/1406.pdf)).

Polynomial-time solvability

The stated continuous equilibrium can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The algorithm also produces a partition distribution with polynomially many positive entries and rational numbers of polynomial encoding length.

The complete-table representation is essential: it makes a construction of size \(O(3^m)\) polynomial in the input length. The proof below applies even without superadditivity.

1. A graph representing all partitions

Fix an ordering of \(\mathcal G\). Construct a directed graph whose vertices are all subsets \(A\subseteq\mathcal G\). For every nonempty \(A\), and every \(S\subseteq A\) containing the least item of \(A\), introduce an edge

\(e=(A,A\setminus S)\),

labelled by \(S\). Write \(a(e)\), \(b(e)\), and \(\ell(e)\) for its tail, head, and label.

Each edge strictly decreases the number of remaining items, so the graph is acyclic. Its source is \(\mathcal G\), and its sink is \(\varnothing\).

Claim. Paths from \(\mathcal G\) to \(\varnothing\) are in bijection with partitions of \(\mathcal G\).

Proof. Along a path, each edge removes a nonempty subset of the remaining items. Thus its labels are pairwise disjoint and cover \(\mathcal G\).

Conversely, given a partition, first remove its unique bundle containing the least item of \(\mathcal G\). Then remove its unique bundle containing the least remaining item, and continue. This constructs a valid path. At every step the next bundle is forced, proving uniqueness. \(\square\)

The graph has \(N=2^m\) vertices and

\(\displaystyle E=\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq\mathcal G}2^{|A|-1}=\frac{3^m-1}{2}\)

edges.

2. Partition distributions as unit flows

For a vertex \(A\), let \(\delta^+(A)\) and \(\delta^-(A)\) denote its outgoing and incoming edges. Define

\(\displaystyle d_A=\begin{cases}1,&A=\mathcal G,\\-1,&A=\varnothing,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\)

A nonnegative unit flow satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{e\in\delta^+(A)}f_e-\sum_{e\in\delta^-(A)}f_e=d_A\qquad(A\subseteq\mathcal G).\)

Every partition distribution induces such a flow by adding its mass to each edge on its corresponding path.

Conversely, every such flow decomposes into at most \(E\) positive path flows. Here is a constructive proof. Start at the source and follow positive-flow edges. At each intermediate vertex, positive incoming flow and conservation guarantee a positive outgoing edge. Acyclicity ensures that this process reaches the sink. Subtract the minimum edge flow along the resulting path and record that amount as its weight.

Each iteration eliminates at least one positive edge. After at most \(E\) iterations the residual flow is zero: a nonzero residual with zero source flow would be a nonnegative circulation in an acyclic graph, which is impossible. Indeed, a tail of a positive edge having no positive incoming edge would violate conservation. The recorded weights sum to one.

For each nonempty bundle, define its flow marginal by

\(\displaystyle y_S=\sum_{e:\ell(e)=S}f_e.\)

The path-partition bijection therefore gives

\(\displaystyle y_S=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma:S\in\gamma}\lambda_\gamma.\)

In particular, \(0\le y_S\le1\), since a partition contains any given bundle at most once.

3. An explicit welfare linear program

Consider the following primal linear program, with nonnegative variables \(x_{t,S}\), \(r_S\), and \(f_e\):

\(\displaystyle \text{maximize }\quad W(x,r)=\sum_t\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal G}u_t(S)x_{t,S}+\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\mathcal G}u_0(S)r_S.\)

Its constraints are:

  • Type masses:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal G}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau).\)

  • Bundle balance:

\(\displaystyle \sum_t x_{t,S}+r_S-\sum_{e:\ell(e)=S}f_e=0\qquad(\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\mathcal G).\)

  • Unit flow:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{e\in\delta^+(A)}f_e-\sum_{e\in\delta^-(A)}f_e=d_A\qquad(A\subseteq\mathcal G).\)

This LP is feasible. Assign every buyer to \(\varnothing\), retain the grand bundle with \(r_{\mathcal G}=1\), and send one unit of flow along the edge labelled \(\mathcal G\).

Its feasible region is bounded. The type equations imply \(0\le x_{t,S}\le\mu_t\). The flow decomposition implies \(0\le f_e\le1\) and \(0\le y_S\le1\). Bundle balance then implies \(0\le r_S\le1\). Thus the primal has an attained finite optimum.

Introduce unrestricted dual variables \(\alpha_t\) for the type equations, \(p_S\) for bundle balance, and \(q_A\) for flow conservation. The dual is

\(\displaystyle \text{minimize }\quad \sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t+q_{\mathcal G}-q_\varnothing,\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t\ge0,\)

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t+p_S\ge u_t(S)\qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau,\ S\ne\varnothing),\)

\(\displaystyle p_S\ge u_0(S)\qquad(S\ne\varnothing),\)

and

\(\displaystyle q_{a(e)}-q_{b(e)}\ge p_{\ell(e)}\qquad(e\text{ an edge}).\)

The first inequalities arise from the variables \(x_{t,\varnothing}\), because \(u_t(\varnothing)=0\).

By linear programming strong duality, a dual optimum exists and has the same value as the primal optimum. Adding a common constant to all potentials changes neither feasibility nor objective, so normalize \(q_\varnothing=0\). Set \(p(\varnothing)=0\) and \(p(S)=p_S\) otherwise.

4. Optimal solutions satisfy every equilibrium condition

Take optimal primal and dual solutions. Their objective difference equals

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal G}x_{t,S}\bigl(\alpha_t+p(S)-u_t(S)\bigr)+\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}r_S\bigl(p(S)-u_0(S)\bigr)+\sum_e f_e\bigl(q_{a(e)}-q_{b(e)}-p(\ell(e))\bigr).\)

To verify this identity, use the type equations for the terms involving \(\alpha_t\), bundle balance for the terms involving prices, and flow conservation for the potential terms.

Every summand is nonnegative by primal and dual feasibility. Strong duality makes their sum zero. Consequently, every summand with a positive primal multiplier has zero slack.

Buyer optimality

Dual feasibility gives, for every bundle including \(\varnothing\),

\(\displaystyle u_t(S)-p(S)\le\alpha_t.\)

If \(x_{t,S}>0\), the zero-slack condition gives

\(\displaystyle u_t(S)-p(S)=\alpha_t.\)

Since \(\mu_t>0\) and the type masses sum correctly, at least one bundle receives positive mass for each type. Hence \(\alpha_t\) is the actual maximum utility, and every positively allocated bundle is a demand maximizer.

Reserve prices and retention

Dual feasibility gives \(p(S)\ge u_0(S)\). If \(r_S>0\), zero slack gives \(p(S)=u_0(S)\). The empty-set reserve condition also holds because both values are zero.

Seller optimality

For any partition \(\gamma\), sum the edge inequalities along its corresponding path. The potentials telescope, yielding

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\in\gamma}p(S)\le q_{\mathcal G}-q_\varnothing=q_{\mathcal G}.\)

Now decompose the optimal flow into positive path flows as in Section 2. Every edge of any recorded path had positive flow in the original optimal solution. Its dual inequality is therefore tight. Summing those equalities gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\in\gamma}p(S)=q_{\mathcal G}\)

for every partition assigned positive mass.

Thus all supported partitions maximize total price over \(\Gamma\). Finally, the decomposition preserves bundle marginals, so the required bundle balances and \(\sum_\gamma\lambda_\gamma=1\) hold.

This verifies all conditions in the question, including allocation among tied maximizers.

5. Polynomial running time and output size

There are

\(\displaystyle \tau N+(N-1)+E=O(\tau2^m+3^m)\)

primal variables and \(O(\tau+2^m)\) equality constraints. Apart from the supplied valuations and masses, the LP coefficients belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\).

Because the valuation and reserve tables are complete, \(N=2^m=O(L)\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle 3^m\le(2^m)^2=N^2.\)

Consequently, the explicit primal and dual LPs have encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

We use the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: an explicitly encoded rational LP can be solved exactly in polynomial bit complexity, producing rational optimal solutions of polynomial encoding length whenever its optimum is finite and attained. Polynomial-time exact optimization and recovery of dual solutions are established in [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, *The ellipsoid method and its consequences in combinatorial optimization*](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/10046/10046D.pdf). Here the data are rational and the primal feasibility and boundedness hypotheses were verified above.

The subsequent flow decomposition takes at most \(E\) iterations, each finding a path of at most \(m\) edges. Its arithmetic also has polynomial bit complexity. Explicitly, take the product \(D\) of the denominators of the computed edge flows. Its binary length is bounded by their total encoding length. All residual flows and extracted path weights have denominators dividing \(D\), because decomposition uses only comparisons and subtraction.

The output therefore consists of rational prices, buyer masses, retained masses, and at most \((3^m-1)/2\) positive partition entries, all of polynomial total encoding length.

Hence the exact mass-and-partition formulation is polynomial-time solvable. This establishes an algorithm for the continuous model independently of any bound on the number of ascending-auction rounds.
