The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a candidate set \(C\), a committee size \(K\), and \(\tau\) approval types \(A_t\subseteq C\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), find a committee \(S\subseteq C\) of size \(K\) such that, for every \(Q\subseteq C\), \(\sum_{t:\,|A_t\cap Q|>|A_t\cap S|}\mu_t < 3.651|Q|/K\). Can this be done in expected time polynomial in \(|C|\), \(\tau\), and the mass encoding length \(L\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a finite candidate set with \(m=|C|\), and let \(K\) be an integer satisfying \(1\le K\le m\).
A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) distinct approval types \(A_t\subseteq C\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval sets; let \(L\) be the total input bit length. Empty approval sets are allowed.
A committee \(S\subseteq C\), with \(|S|=K\), is \(3.651\)-approximately stable if every nonempty \(Q\subseteq C\) satisfies \(\sum_{t:\,|A_t\cap Q|>|A_t\cap S|}\mu_t<3.651|Q|/K\). Ties do not contribute to this mass; candidates remain indivisible.

Can a \(3.651\)-approximately stable committee always be found in expected time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,K\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For explicitly listed finite voters, a \(3.651\)-approximately stable committee of any feasible size can be found in expected polynomial time ([Gao–Sun–Vondrák](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00130v1)).
No result settling this search problem for binary-rational masses represented by their support was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Excluded the empty deviating committee: including it under the stated strict inequality forces 0<0 and contradicts the intended discrete existence result; the latest available arXiv version retains this slip.
  • Restricted the committee size to an integer satisfying 1≤K≤|C|, as required by the discrete algorithm's input domain.
  • Allowed empty approval sets, since the discrete definition imposes no nonemptiness requirement.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length and made explicit that the approval types constitute distinct support elements.

Result

A $3.651$-approximately stable committee can always be found in deterministic polynomial time in $m,\tau,L,K$. In fact, a newer weighted-election theorem gives the stronger factor $2$.

The result used here is Theorem 1 of Chan–Lin–Wang, *Sequential Phragmén Guarantees 2-Approximate Core Stability*, submitted in September 2026. It applies directly to positive voter weights. Below I state its guarantee, check its hypotheses, and prove the required bit-complexity bound for the support representation. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29646v1#Thmtheorem1)

1. The weighted theorem being used

For a load vector $\ell$, weighted sequential Phragmén assigns each candidate $c$ the unique funding height $h_c$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{t:c\in A_t}w_t\max\{0,h_c-\ell_t\}=1. $$
Starting with zero loads, it repeatedly selects an unelected candidate of minimum funding height and raises each approving voter’s load to the maximum of its current load and that height.

Known theorem, Chan–Lin–Wang. Suppose the voter set is finite, every voter has positive real weight, every candidate has at least one approver, and $1\le K\le |C|$. Under any tie-breaking, this rule returns a size-$K$ committee $S$ such that, for every nonempty $Q\subseteq C$,
$$ \sum_{t:\,|A_t\cap Q|>|A_t\cap S|}w_t < \frac{2|Q|}{K}\sum_t w_t. $$
Empty approval sets are permitted, and deviations may overlap the elected committee. [Model and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29646v1)

We invoke this theorem as an established result; the computational argument below uses exact arithmetic.

2. Handling unapproved candidates

Define
$$ C^+=\bigcup_{t=1}^{\tau}A_t. $$

If $|C^+|<K$, choose every candidate in $C^+$ and add arbitrary candidates until the committee has size $K$. Then every type receives its maximum possible utility:
$$ |A_t\cap S|=|A_t|. $$
Consequently, no type strictly prefers any deviation. The requested inequalities hold because their right-hand sides are positive.

Now suppose $|C^+|\ge K$. Run the weighted rule on $C^+$. Its candidates all have approvers, and its committee size is feasible.

For any original deviation $Q\subseteq C$, put $Q^+=Q\cap C^+$. Every approval set is contained in $C^+$, so
$$ |A_t\cap Q|=|A_t\cap Q^+| $$
for every type. If $Q^+=\varnothing$, the preferring mass is zero. Otherwise, a stability bound for $Q^+$ also bounds $Q$, since $|Q^+|\le |Q|$.

Thus it suffices to solve the instance on $C^+$.

3. Integer weights without expanding the electorate

Write the input masses as
$$ \mu_t=\frac{p_t}{d_t}, $$
where $p_t,d_t$ are positive integers, and define
$$ D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}d_t, \qquad w_t=p_t\frac{D}{d_t}. $$
Then every $w_t$ is a positive integer, and normalization gives
$$ \sum_t w_t=D, \qquad \mu_t=\frac{w_t}{D}. $$

The bit length of $D$ is bounded by the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence by $O(L)$. Each $w_t\le D$ also has $O(L)$ bits. These integers can therefore be computed in polynomial time.

Treat each support type as one weighted voter, with weight $w_t$ and approval set $A_t$. There are exactly $\tau$ weighted voters. No replicated electorate is constructed.

All hypotheses of the weighted theorem now hold on $C^+$: finitely many voters and candidates, positive weights, approval-count utilities, an approver for every candidate, and feasible committee size.

4. Exact polynomial-time implementation

We prove that the rule can be executed using integers of polynomial bit length.

Funding heights simplify along an execution

Initially all loads are zero. Funding functions are continuous and, once positive, strictly increasing; they tend to infinity because each candidate has a positive-weight approver. Thus their funding heights exist uniquely.

Increasing loads can only decrease a candidate’s funding function at any fixed height. Therefore every remaining candidate’s funding height weakly increases after an update.

Since each selected candidate has minimum funding height, the selected heights are nondecreasing. After any round, every load is zero or a previously selected height. Consequently, before the next selection, every available candidate’s funding height is at least every current load.

For an available candidate $c$, write
$$ W_c=\sum_{t:c\in A_t}w_t. $$
At its funding height the maximum operators can therefore be removed, giving the exact formula
$$ h_c= \frac{1+\sum_{t:c\in A_t}w_t\ell_t}{W_c}. $$

A common-denominator implementation

After $j$ selections, represent the loads as
$$ \ell_t=\frac{a_{t,j}}{E_j}, $$
with nonnegative integers $a_{t,j}$ and a positive integer $E_j$. Initially,
$$ E_0=1,\qquad a_{t,0}=0. $$

For each available candidate compute
$$ N_c=E_j+\sum_{t:c\in A_t}w_ta_{t,j}. $$
Its funding height is
$$ h_c=\frac{N_c}{E_jW_c}. $$
Because $E_j$ is common to all candidates, choose a candidate minimizing $N_c/W_c$. Comparisons are exact integer cross-multiplications. Fix an ordering of candidates to resolve ties deterministically.

If $c$ is selected, update
$$ E_{j+1}=E_jW_c $$
and
$$ a_{t,j+1}= \begin{cases} N_c,&c\in A_t,\\ a_{t,j}W_c,&c\notin A_t. \end{cases} $$
Approvers receive load $h_c$; other loads remain unchanged. The preceding funding-height argument proves that this implements precisely the weighted rule.

Bit-length bound

Each selection increases total weighted load by exactly one, by its defining funding equation. Hence after $j$ selections,
$$ \sum_t w_t\ell_t=j, \qquad \sum_t w_ta_{t,j}=jE_j. $$
Since $w_t\ge1$ and all numerators are nonnegative,
$$ a_{t,j}\le jE_j. $$
Also,
$$ 1\le W_c\le D, \qquad E_j\le D^j. $$
For every available candidate,
$$ N_c \le E_j+\sum_t w_ta_{t,j} =(j+1)E_j. $$

Therefore all load numerators, denominators, candidate scores, and comparison products have
$$ O\!\left(K\log(D+1)+\log(K+1)\right) = O\!\left(KL+\log(K+1)\right) $$
bits.

There are $K$ rounds. Computing all candidate scores takes $O(m\tau)$ integer operations per round; comparisons and updates require only polynomially many additional operations. Integer arithmetic on the stated bit lengths takes polynomial time. This proves deterministic running time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,K$.

5. Transferring the guarantee to the original masses

Let $S$ be the committee returned on $C^+$. For any nonempty original deviation $Q$, if $Q^+=Q\cap C^+$ is nonempty, the weighted theorem gives
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t:\,|A_t\cap Q|>|A_t\cap S|}\mu_t &= \frac1D \sum_{t:\,|A_t\cap Q^+|>|A_t\cap S|}w_t\\ &< \frac{2|Q^+|}{K}\\ &\le \frac{2|Q|}{K}\\ &< \frac{3.651|Q|}{K}. \end{aligned} $$
The last inequality is strict because $Q$ is nonempty. If $Q^+=\varnothing$, the preferring mass is zero and the same conclusion holds.

Thus the exact factor requested in the task is achieved without rounding masses or expanding voters. The newer weighted theorem settles the gap left open in the supplied notes.
