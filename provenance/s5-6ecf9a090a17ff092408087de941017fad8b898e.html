The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) candidates, nonnegative rational adjacent-swap weights \(w_1,\ldots,w_{n-1}\), and a society supported on \(\tau\) rankings with positive rational masses \(\mu_\sigma\) summing to \(1\), return a ranking \(\pi\) such that \(\sum_\sigma \mu_\sigma d_w(\pi,\sigma) \le 2\min_{\rho\in S_n}\sum_\sigma \mu_\sigma d_w(\rho,\sigma)\), where \(d_w\) is the minimum total weight of adjacent swaps transforming one ranking into another.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given \(m\ge 1\) candidates, let rankings be strict total orders in \(S_m\). The input includes nonnegative binary-rational weights \(w_1,\ldots,w_{m-1}\); swapping the entries currently occupying positions \(i\) and \(i+1\) costs \(w_i\), independently of their identities. Define \(d_w(\pi,\sigma)\) as the minimum total cost of any finite sequence of adjacent swaps transforming \(\pi\) into \(\sigma\). Zero weights, including an all-zero weight vector, are allowed.
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct rankings with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\sigma\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number \(m!\) of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Set \(F(\pi)=\sum_{\sigma}\mu_\sigma d_w(\pi,\sigma)\). The required output is any ranking \(\pi\) satisfying \(F(\pi)\le 2\min_{\rho\in S_m}F(\rho)\), including when the minimum is zero.

Does this problem admit a polynomial-time \(2\)-approximation in \(m,\tau,L\), or is achieving this approximation guarantee NP-hard?

For finite unweighted lists of rankings and arbitrary nonnegative adjacent-swap weights, a polynomial-time \(2\)-approximation for the same aggregation objective is known. [Farnoud, Milenkovic, and Touri](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1471v1)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Renamed the candidate count from n to m to express running times using the required convention; defined L as the total input bit length.
  • Made explicit that adjacent-swap weights depend on current positions, independently of candidate identities, as in the discrete definition.
  • Allowed any qualifying output ranking without a tie-breaking rule, and made explicit that the multiplicative guarantee applies when the optimum is zero.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The problem admits a deterministic polynomial-time \(2\)-approximation, including when the optimum is zero. Rational masses can be incorporated directly into an assignment problem.

For \(m=1\), output the unique ranking. Henceforth assume \(m\ge2\), and label the candidates by \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\).

Distance bound. Write \(r_\pi(a)=\pi^{-1}(a)\) for the position of candidate \(a\). Define \(x_1=0\) and \(x_p=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}w_i\), and put

\(\displaystyle D_w(\pi,\sigma)=\sum_{a=1}^{m}|x_{r_\pi(a)}-x_{r_\sigma(a)}|.\)

The known pointwise bound is

\(\displaystyle \frac12D_w(\pi,\sigma)\le d_w(\pi,\sigma)\le D_w(\pi,\sigma).\)

This is [Proposition 16 of Farnoud, Milenkovic, and Touri](https://arxiv.org/html/1212.1471v1#S3). Its hypotheses match this task: their Definition 10 assigns nonnegative costs to adjacent transpositions of positions and minimizes over all transforming sequences. Set the cost of transposition \((i\,\,i+1)\) to \(w_i\). Their definition permits zero weights, so the bound applies without a positivity assumption.

Define the auxiliary objective

\(\displaystyle G(\pi)=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mu_\sigma D_w(\pi,\sigma).\)

Multiplying the pointwise inequalities by the nonnegative masses and summing gives, for every ranking \(\pi\),

\(\displaystyle \frac12G(\pi)\le F(\pi)\le G(\pi).\)

Algorithm and guarantee. Construct a complete bipartite graph whose two vertex classes are candidates and positions. Give edge \((a,p)\) the rational cost

\(\displaystyle c_{a,p}=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mu_\sigma|x_p-x_{r_\sigma(a)}|.\)

Compute an exact minimum-cost perfect matching. Output the ranking \(\widehat\pi\) placing candidate \(a\) at position \(p\) whenever \((a,p)\) belongs to the matching.

Every perfect matching specifies a strict total order, and conversely every strict total order specifies a perfect matching. The matching corresponding to \(\pi\) has cost

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a=1}^{m}c_{a,r_\pi(a)} =\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mu_\sigma \sum_{a=1}^{m}|x_{r_\pi(a)}-x_{r_\sigma(a)}| =G(\pi).\)

Thus \(\widehat\pi\) minimizes \(G\) exactly. Let \(\pi^\star\) minimize \(F\); such a ranking exists because \(S_m\) is finite. Then

\(\displaystyle F(\widehat\pi)\le G(\widehat\pi)\le G(\pi^\star)\le2F(\pi^\star).\)

This proves the required approximation guarantee.

In particular, if \(F(\pi^\star)=0\), the same inequalities yield \(0\le F(\widehat\pi)\le0\). The algorithm therefore returns an exact zero-cost ranking in that case. If all weights vanish, every edge cost and every ranking’s objective value vanish.

Bit complexity. The matching can be solved with exact integer arithmetic. Write the input rationals as \(w_i=u_i/v_i\) and \(\mu_\sigma=a_\sigma/b_\sigma\), with positive denominators. Set

\(\displaystyle Q_w=\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}v_i,\qquad Q_\mu=\prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}b_\sigma.\)

Define integers \(W_i=Q_ww_i\), \(M_\sigma=Q_\mu\mu_\sigma\), and \(X_p=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}W_i\). The integer edge costs

\(\displaystyle C_{a,p}=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)} M_\sigma|X_p-X_{r_\sigma(a)}|\)

satisfy \(C_{a,p}=Q_wQ_\mu c_{a,p}\). Multiplication by this common positive factor preserves all optimal matchings.

The products of denominators have bit length \(O(L)\), because the sum of the input denominators’ bit lengths is at most \(L\). Consequently all scaled numbers and edge costs have bit length \(O(B)\), where \(B=L+\lceil\log_2(m+\tau+1)\rceil\).

Computing inverse rankings takes \(O(\tau m)\) operations; constructing the costs takes \(O(\tau m^2)\) integer arithmetic operations. The Hungarian algorithm uses \(O(m^3)\) arithmetic operations, with its intermediate integer quantities also having \(O(B)\) bits. Using schoolbook arithmetic, a conservative total bound, including denominator scaling, is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left((\tau m^2+m^3)\bigl(L+\log(m+\tau+1)\bigr)^2\right)\)

bit operations. This is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\); no expansion into repeated votes is required.

The cited distance bound applies to arbitrary nonnegative positional swap weights, the assignment problem incorporates rational masses directly, and the stated running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).   • Definitions and cited distance bound

The position convention \(r_\pi(a)=\pi^{-1}(a)\) agrees with the task: rankings list candidates by position.

I checked [Definition 10 and Proposition 16 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1212.1471v1#S3). Definition 10 minimizes the sum of a nonnegative weight function over sequences of adjacent transpositions. The paper’s permutation convention makes these transpositions swaps of positions. Thus assigning weight \(w_i\) to the transposition of positions \(i\) and \(i+1\) gives precisely the task’s distance.

Proposition 16 states the two inequalities used by the prover. Its auxiliary distance sums, over candidates, the adjacent-edge weights between their two positions. Since all weights are nonnegative, that sum equals
\[ |x_{r_\pi(a)}-x_{r_\sigma(a)}|. \]
Consequently the prover’s \(D_w\) is exactly the auxiliary distance appearing in the proposition. No positivity, monotonicity, or normalization hypothesis is missing; zero weights are permitted.

  • Passing to the weighted objectives

Multiplication by each nonnegative mass preserves both inequalities, and summation over the finite support gives
\[ \tfrac12G(\pi)\le F(\pi)\le G(\pi). \]
This holds for every ranking. The argument does not require equal masses or expansion into repeated votes.

  • Assignment formulation

The complete bipartite graph has exactly one vertex for each candidate and each position. A perfect matching assigns every candidate to exactly one position and fills every position exactly once, so it corresponds bijectively to a strict total order.

The displayed matching-cost identity is correct: substituting the edge costs and exchanging two finite sums produces exactly \(G(\pi)\). Therefore an exact minimum-cost perfect matching minimizes \(G\) over all rankings. Nonnegative rational costs and ties cause no difficulty; any minimizing matching is sufficient.

  • Approximation guarantee

A minimizer \(\pi^\star\) of \(F\) exists because \(S_m\) is finite. Every link in
\[ F(\widehat\pi)\le G(\widehat\pi)\le G(\pi^\star)\le2F(\pi^\star) \]
is justified: respectively by the upper distance bound, optimality of the matching, and the lower distance bound. This establishes precisely the required guarantee.

  • Boundary and degenerate cases

For \(m=1\), there is a unique ranking and its objective value is zero.

If the optimum is zero, the same inequality chain forces \(F(\widehat\pi)=0\), because all distances and masses are nonnegative. There is no division by the optimum.

If all weights vanish, every transformation has zero cost, all auxiliary coordinates coincide, and every matching cost and ranking objective is zero. The algorithm remains valid. Partial zero-weight vectors are covered by the cited bound as well.

  • Exact arithmetic and running time

Both denominator products are positive integers, including when some weight numerators vanish. The scaled weights and masses are integers, and the identity
\[ C_{a,p}=Q_wQ_\mu c_{a,p} \]
follows directly from the definitions. Scaling by this common positive factor preserves optimal matchings.

The denominator products have bit length bounded by the sum of the input denominator bit lengths. Multiplication by input numerators and summation over positions and support add only \(O(L+\log(m+\tau+1))\) bits. Thus the claimed \(O(B)\) bound for integer costs is valid.

Inverting the support rankings and constructing the costs take the stated numbers of operations. The standard cubic Hungarian algorithm applies to this complete square integer-cost assignment problem. Its intermediate quantities have polynomially bounded magnitude, with at most an additional logarithmic dependence on \(m\), already included in \(B\). Denominator scaling also fits within the displayed conservative bound.

Hence
\[ O\!\left((\tau m^2+m^3)(L+\log(m+\tau+1))^2\right) \]
is a valid polynomial bit-operation bound.
