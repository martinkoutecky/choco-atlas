The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an initial candidate set \(X\), three new candidates \(Y\), a target \(x^\star\in X\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over rankings of \(X\), is there a completion plan \(q_{t,\pi}\ge 0\) such that \(\sum_{\pi:\,\pi|_X=t}q_{t,\pi}=1\) for each type \(t\), and \(x^\star\) is a \(4\)-approval winner under the resulting mass \(\bar\mu_\pi=\sum_t\mu_tq_{t,\pi}\), with favorable tie-breaking?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(X\) be the initial candidate set, with \(m=|X|\ge 5\), let \(Y\) be a disjoint set of exactly three new candidates, and let \(x^\star\in X\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) strict rankings of \(X\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
For each \(t\in T\), let \(E(t)\) consist of all strict rankings \(\pi\) of \(X\cup Y\) whose restriction to \(X\) is \(t\); the new candidates may be inserted anywhere and in any order.
A completion plan consists of real numbers \(q_{t,\pi}\ge 0\) for \(\pi\in E(t)\), satisfying \(\sum_{\pi\in E(t)}q_{t,\pi}=1\). It induces mass \(\bar\mu_\pi=\sum_{t:\pi\in E(t)}\mu_tq_{t,\pi}\).
Apply \(4\)-approval to the completed society, with scores weighted by \(\bar\mu\). Favorable tie-breaking means that \(x^\star\) wins whenever its score is at least that of every candidate in \(X\cup Y\).

Is deciding whether such a winning completion plan exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite lists of indivisible voters, this problem with \(4\)-approval and exactly three new candidates is NP-complete. [Discrete result](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/7640/7501)
The discrete hardness extends to every fixed approval threshold \(K\ge 3\) and number of new candidates \(k\ge 3\). [Later result](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~lang/papers/clmmx-mss12.pdf)
No result settling this exact divisible-mass completion problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified strict rankings, disjoint candidate sets, and unrestricted insertion and ordering of all three new candidates, following the discrete extension definition.
  • Made probability normalization explicit: positive support masses sum to one.
  • Specified real-valued completion shares and mass-weighted scores, allowing a type's mass to split among completions.
  • Specified that favorable tie-breaking compares the target with both initial and new candidates.
  • Restricted the initial domain to at least five candidates so that initial 4-approval has a nonconstant scoring vector, consistent with the scoring-rule definition.

Divisible-mass completion is polynomial-time solvable

Theorem. The stated decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{P}\). A winning completion exists exactly when a rational linear program with \(4\tau\) variables is feasible.

1. The possible approval sets

Write each original ranking as \(t=(x_{t,1},\ldots,x_{t,m})\), and let \(r_t(x)\) denote the position of \(x\) in \(t\).

Consider an extension of \(t\), and let \(j\) be the number of new candidates among its first four positions. Since there are exactly three new candidates, \(j\in\{0,1,2,3\}\). Exactly \(4-j\) old candidates are approved.

These old candidates must be the first \(4-j\) candidates of \(t\). Indeed, if an old candidate is approved, every old candidate preceding it in \(t\) precedes it in the extension and is also approved.

Conversely, for every \(j\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) and every subset \(S\subseteq Y\) with \(|S|=j\), the approval set

\(\displaystyle S\cup\{x_{t,1},\ldots,x_{t,4-j}\}\)

is attainable. To realize it, rank the members of \(S\) first, then all members of \(X\) in order \(t\), then the members of \(Y\setminus S\). This ranking restricts to \(t\), and its first four positions have exactly the specified candidates. The assumption \(m\ge5\) ensures all required old prefixes exist.

Thus, for old candidates, only the value of \(j\) matters.

2. A linear feasibility formulation

For each \(t\in T\) and \(j\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), introduce a variable \(z_{t,j}\). It represents the actual society mass assigned to extensions of \(t\) that approve exactly \(j\) new candidates.

Require

\(\displaystyle z_{t,j}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{j=0}^{3}z_{t,j}=\mu_t\quad(t\in T).\)

For each \(x\in X\), define the linear expression

\(\displaystyle s_x(z)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{j=0}^{3}z_{t,j}\,\mathbf{1}\{r_t(x)\le4-j\}.\)

By the preceding section, this is precisely the score of \(x\) for any completion with these aggregate masses.

Define also

\(\displaystyle h(z)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{j=0}^{3}jz_{t,j}.\)

This is the sum of the scores of the three new candidates: mass \(z_{t,j}\) contributes approval to exactly \(j\) of them.

The remaining constraints are

\(\displaystyle s_x(z)\le s_{x^\star}(z)\quad(x\in X\setminus\{x^\star\}),\)

and

\(\displaystyle h(z)\le3s_{x^\star}(z).\)

We prove that this system is feasible exactly when a winning completion exists.

3. Necessity

Suppose a winning completion plan \(q\) exists. For an extension \(\pi\), let \(j(\pi)\) count its approved new candidates. Set

\(\displaystyle z_{t,j}=\mu_t\sum_{\substack{\pi\in E(t)\\j(\pi)=j}}q_{t,\pi}.\)

These variables are nonnegative. Since the four possible values of \(j(\pi)\) partition \(E(t)\), their sum for each \(t\) is \(\mu_t\).

Section 1 shows that every old candidate \(x\) has score \(s_x(z)\). Therefore, because \(x^\star\) wins, every old-candidate inequality holds.

Let \(s_y\) be the score of a new candidate \(y\in Y\). Counting approvals to new candidates gives

\(\displaystyle h(z)=\sum_{y\in Y}s_y.\)

Winning implies \(s_y\le s_{x^\star}(z)\) for each of the three new candidates. Summing these inequalities yields \(h(z)\le3s_{x^\star}(z)\). Hence the linear system is feasible.

4. Sufficiency and explicit reconstruction

Suppose the linear system has a feasible solution \(z\). Fix an ordering of \(Y\).

For every \(t\), every \(j\), and every subset \(S\subseteq Y\) of size \(j\), let \(\pi_{t,S}\) be the canonical extension constructed in Section 1: first \(S\) in the fixed order, then \(X\) in order \(t\), then \(Y\setminus S\) in the fixed order.

Define

\(\displaystyle q_{t,\pi_{t,S}}=\frac{z_{t,j}}{\mu_t\binom{3}{j}}\quad\text{when }|S|=j,\)

and assign probability zero to all other extensions. For fixed \(t\), distinct subsets give distinct approval sets and hence distinct canonical extensions, so this assignment is well defined. Since \(\mu_t>0\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\pi\in E(t)}q_{t,\pi} =\sum_{j=0}^{3}\binom{3}{j}\frac{z_{t,j}}{\mu_t\binom{3}{j}} =\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{3}z_{t,j}}{\mu_t}=1.\)

Thus this is an admissible completion plan.

Its old-candidate scores are \(s_x(z)\). To compute a new candidate’s score, observe that a uniformly chosen subset of \(Y\) of size \(j\) contains any fixed \(y\in Y\) with probability \(j/3\). For \(j=0\) this is zero; for \(j\in\{1,2,3\}\), it follows from

\(\displaystyle \frac{\binom{2}{j-1}}{\binom{3}{j}}=\frac{j}{3}.\)

Consequently, each new candidate has score

\(\displaystyle s_y=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{j=0}^{3}\frac{j}{3}z_{t,j}=\frac{h(z)}{3}\le s_{x^\star}(z).\)

The old-candidate inequalities likewise ensure that every old competitor’s score is at most that of \(x^\star\). Favorable tie-breaking therefore makes \(x^\star\) a winner. This proves sufficiency.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

The system has \(4\tau\) variables, \(\tau\) mass equalities, \(m-1\) old-candidate inequalities, one new-candidate inequality, and \(4\tau\) nonnegativity constraints.

After moving terms to the left, every coefficient is an integer of absolute value at most three. The only nonzero right-hand sides are the input rationals \(\mu_t\). Computing the ranks \(r_t(x)\) takes time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and a dense encoding of the system has length \(O(m\tau+\tau^2+L)\).

We use the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: feasibility of finitely many non-strict linear inequalities with rational coefficients can be decided in time polynomial in their total binary encoding length. Equalities can be replaced by two inequalities, so the theorem applies directly here. It decides exact feasibility over the reals, including boundary cases where candidates tie.

By Sections 3 and 4, this feasibility decision answers the original question exactly.

6. Relation to the discrete hardness result

The [cited discrete result, Proposition 3](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/7640/7501), concerns indivisible voters. Our reconstruction splits each support mass among several extensions, which the present problem explicitly permits. An indivisible voter cannot generally realize that split. Thus the discrete hardness result does not obstruct this polynomial-time algorithm for divisible mass.
