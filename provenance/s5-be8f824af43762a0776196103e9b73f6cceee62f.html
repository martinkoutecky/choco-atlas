The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(\theta\in T\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\), rational additive valuations \(v_\theta(j)\), positive rational entitlements \(w_\theta\), and item classes \(j\in J\) with rational per-capita supplies \(s_j\), find an explicitly listed, polynomial-size rational assignment \(\pi_{\theta,B}\) of mass to integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{J}\). Require \(\sum_B\pi_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,B}\pi_{\theta,B}B_j=s_j\), and that every pair of positive-mass bundle groups \((\theta,B),(\phi,D)\) satisfy WEF1T as evaluated by type \(\theta\): either \(v_\theta(B)/w_\theta\ge v_\theta(D)/w_\phi\), or transferring one item from either bundle to the other makes that inequality hold. Is such an assignment computable in time polynomial in \(|T|\), \(|J|\), and the input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=|J|\) item classes, with supplies \(s_j\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\), additive valuations \(v_\theta(j)\in\mathbb{Q}\) of either sign, and entitlements \(w_\theta\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\). All rational inputs are binary encoded; \(L\) is their total bit length.
Each person receives an integral bundle \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}^{J}\) of item copies. Find an explicitly listed assignment of nonnegative rational masses \(\pi_{\theta,B}\), with total binary encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), satisfying \(\sum_B\pi_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B}\pi_{\theta,B}B_j=s_j\).
For every ordered pair of positive-mass groups \((\theta,B),(\phi,D)\), including groups of the same type, require WEF1T: either \(v_\theta(B)/w_\theta\geq v_\theta(D)/w_\phi\), or some \(j\) with \(B_j>0\) satisfies \(v_\theta(B-e_j)/w_\theta\geq v_\theta(D+e_j)/w_\phi\), or some \(j\) with \(D_j>0\) satisfies \(v_\theta(B+e_j)/w_\theta\geq v_\theta(D-e_j)/w_\phi\), where \(e_j\) denotes one copy of class \(j\).

Is this mass-allocation problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many individually listed agents and indivisible items, WEF1T allocations of additive mixed manna with positive entitlements are computable in polynomial time. [Garg–Sharma](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12966v1)
More recently, polynomial-time WEF1 computation and existence of finite lotteries that are weighted envy-free in expectation and WEF1T in every realization have been established for finite mixed-manna instances. [Lin–Liu–Tao–Zhou](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02724v1)
No result settling the exact support-encoded mass problem above was located; fairness within each lottery realization does not impose comparisons between every pair of bundles appearing across realizations.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Supplies were not assigned a sign domain; they are nonnegative rationals, allowing zero supply.
  • Valuation signs are unrestricted, including zero, as required by additive mixed manna; entitlements are positive and need no normalization.
  • The transfer condition is stated explicitly in both directions and requires a copy in the donor bundle. The erroneous removal from the receiving bundle in the original definition is interpreted as addition, consistent with its proof and the later literature's definition.
  • Mass assignments are nonnegative rationals, and polynomial output size means total binary encoding length, including bundle multiplicities.
  • Pairwise fairness includes ordered comparisons between distinct bundle groups of the same type; masses affect feasibility but do not multiply individual entitlements.

Result

The problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The algorithm outputs at most \(\tau(2m+2)\) positive-mass groups, each with polynomial binary encoding length.

The essential step is to compress weighted picking sequences on a replicated finite society. The goods-and-chores decomposition follows the finite-agent approach of [Garg–Sharma](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12966v1). Below, both the fairness guarantees and the compression are proved explicitly. All output bundles coexist in one finite allocation, so the proof establishes every required comparison across supported bundles.

1. An exact finite representation

Write every mass and supply as a ratio of integers, and let \(N\) be the product of their positive denominators. Define

\(n_\theta=N\mu_\theta\), and \(Q_j=Ns_j\).

These are integers, with \(n_\theta>0\), \(Q_j\geq0\), and \(\sum_\theta n_\theta=N\).

Consider a conceptual finite instance containing:

  • \(n_\theta\) agents of type \(\theta\), indexed by \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n_\theta\}\);
  • \(Q_j\) individual copies of class \(j\).

Each agent \((\theta,k)\) has valuation \(v_\theta\) and entitlement \(w_\theta\). Each conceptual agent represents population mass \(1/N\).

The bit lengths of \(N,n_\theta,Q_j\), and \(K=\sum_jQ_j\) are polynomial in the input length. Their numerical values can nevertheless be exponential. We will therefore construct this allocation without enumerating its agents or copies.

Partition the item classes into

\(J^+=\{j:\max_\theta v_\theta(j)>0\}\),

\(J^0=\{j:\max_\theta v_\theta(j)=0\}\),

and \(J^-=\{j:\max_\theta v_\theta(j)<0\}\).

A class in \(J^0\) has a zero-valuing type and is nonpositive to every type. Every class in \(J^-\) is strictly negative to every type.

2. Finite picking procedures and their fairness guarantees

Fix tie-breaking by type index, then agent index; break item-class ties by class index. In this section, \(i,\ell\) denote individual conceptual agents, with entitlements \(w_i,w_\ell\).

2.1 Forward picking for \(J^+\)

Initialize every agent’s pick count to zero. While copies from \(J^+\) remain:

  • An agent is active if some remaining copy has strictly positive value to that agent.
  • Choose an active agent minimizing its pick count divided by its entitlement.
  • Give that agent a most valuable remaining copy and increment its pick count.

Every remaining class in \(J^+\) has a positive-valuing agent, so this procedure allocates all such copies. Each agent receives only positively valued copies.

An agent’s successive turns have normalized times

\(0,1/w_i,2/w_i,\ldots\).

The procedure processes turns in increasing normalized time, skipping agents after they become inactive. Inactivity is permanent because the available set only shrinks.

Goods guarantee. If \(G_i,G_\ell\) are the resulting bundles, then either

\(v_i(G_i)/w_i\geq v_i(G_\ell)/w_\ell\),

or there is a copy \(g\in G_\ell\), positive to \(i\), such that

\(v_i(G_i)/w_i\geq v_i(G_\ell-g)/w_\ell\).

Proof. For \(x\geq0\), let \(A_i(x)\) count copies in \(G_i\) valued strictly above \(x\) by \(i\), and let \(A_\ell(x)\) count such copies in \(G_\ell\).

Suppose \(A_\ell(x)>0\). Let the last such copy received by \(\ell\) be its \(q\)-th pick. Its normalized time is \(t=(q-1)/w_\ell\).

That copy was available before time \(t\). Consequently, agent \(i\) remained active until that pick, and every earlier pick by \(i\) had value strictly above \(x\). There are exactly \(\lceil w_i t\rceil\) turns of \(i\) with normalized time strictly below \(t\). Thus

\(A_i(x)/w_i\geq t=(q-1)/w_\ell\geq(A_\ell(x)-1)/w_\ell\).

When \(A_\ell(x)=0\), the inequality

\(A_i(x)/w_i\geq\max\{A_\ell(x)-1,0\}/w_\ell\)

also holds.

If \(G_\ell\) contains a copy positive to \(i\), choose a maximum-valued such copy \(g\). Removing \(g\) leaves exactly \(\max\{A_\ell(x)-1,0\}\) copies valued above \(x\), for every \(x\geq0\). Integrating the inequality over \(x\) gives

\(v_i(G_i)/w_i\geq\sum_{e\in G_\ell-g}\max\{v_i(e),0\}/w_\ell\geq v_i(G_\ell-g)/w_\ell\).

Here the integrands are finite step functions, and \(G_i\) contains only positive-valued copies.

If \(G_\ell\) contains no copy positive to \(i\), then \(v_i(G_\ell)\leq0\leq v_i(G_i)\). This proves the guarantee. \(\square\)

Now assign all copies of each class in \(J^0\) to agent \(k=1\) of a chosen zero-valuing type. Include these copies in its goods bundle. The guarantee remains valid: an owner’s value is unchanged, while every other agent’s value for that owner’s bundle can only decrease.

2.2 Reversed picking for \(J^-\)

Let \(K^-=\sum_{j\in J^-}Q_j\). First construct a sequence of \(K^-\) turns by repeatedly choosing an agent minimizing its pick count divided by its entitlement, starting all counts at zero.

Execute this sequence in reverse order. On each turn, give the selected agent its most valuable remaining chore copy.

Chores guarantee. For the resulting bundles \(C_i,C_\ell\), either

\(v_i(C_i)/w_i\geq v_i(C_\ell)/w_\ell\),

or some copy \(c\in C_i\) satisfies

\(v_i(C_i-c)/w_i\geq v_i(C_\ell)/w_\ell\).

Proof. Put \(d_i(e)=-v_i(e)>0\). If \(C_i\) is empty, the first inequality holds.

Otherwise, choose \(c\) maximizing \(d_i\) in \(C_i\). For \(x\geq0\), let \(D_i(x)\) and \(D_\ell(x)\) count copies of disutility strictly above \(x\) in the respective bundles.

Suppose \(D_i(x)>0\). Consider the first such copy received by \(i\) during reversed execution. Its forward normalized time is \(t=r/w_i\), for some integer \(r\geq0\).

Because \(i\) selects a minimum-disutility remaining copy, every copy remaining at that turn has disutility strictly above \(x\). Therefore every subsequent pick, by any agent, has disutility strictly above \(x\).

All turns of \(\ell\) with normalized time strictly below \(t\) belong to the forward prefix and occur subsequently in reverse. Their number is \(\lceil w_\ell t\rceil\), so

\(D_\ell(x)/w_\ell\geq t=r/w_i\).

By the choice of the first high-disutility pick of \(i\), all its high-disutility picks are among its turns indexed \(0,\ldots,r\). Hence \(D_i(x)\leq r+1\), and

\(\max\{D_i(x)-1,0\}/w_i\leq D_\ell(x)/w_\ell\).

This also holds when \(D_i(x)=0\). Removing the fixed maximum-disutility copy \(c\) leaves exactly \(\max\{D_i(x)-1,0\}\) high-disutility copies at every threshold. Integration yields

\(d_i(C_i-c)/w_i\leq d_i(C_\ell)/w_\ell\),

which is the required valuation inequality. \(\square\)

2.3 Combining the two allocations gives WEF1T

Give agent \(i\) the integral bundle \(B_i=G_i+C_i\).

Fix any ordered pair \(i,\ell\). From the goods guarantee, choose \(a\geq0\) so that

\(v_i(G_\ell)/w_\ell-v_i(G_i)/w_i\leq a/w_\ell\).

Specifically, take \(a=0\) if the pair is already goods-envy-free; otherwise take \(a=v_i(g)>0\) for its witnessing copy \(g\in G_\ell\).

Similarly, the chores guarantee gives \(b\geq0\) such that

\(v_i(C_\ell)/w_\ell-v_i(C_i)/w_i\leq b/w_i\).

Take \(b=0\) when the chores comparison is already envy-free; otherwise take \(b=-v_i(c)>0\) for its witnessing copy \(c\in C_i\).

The total normalized envy gap therefore satisfies

\(\Delta=v_i(B_\ell)/w_\ell-v_i(B_i)/w_i\leq a/w_\ell+b/w_i\).

If \(a=b=0\), there is no envy. Otherwise:

  • If \(a\geq b\), then \(a>0\), and transferring \(g\) from \(B_\ell\) to \(B_i\) decreases the gap by \(a(1/w_i+1/w_\ell)\), which is at least \(a/w_\ell+b/w_i\).
  • If \(b>a\), transferring \(c\) from \(B_i\) to \(B_\ell\) decreases the gap by \(b(1/w_i+1/w_\ell)\), again at least \(a/w_\ell+b/w_i\).

Thus every ordered pair satisfies exactly the WEF1T condition in the question.

3. A polynomial-time oracle for turns

Write \(w_\theta=a_\theta/b_\theta\), with positive integers \(a_\theta,b_\theta\). Define

\(H=\prod_{\theta\in T}a_\theta\), and \(h_\theta=Hb_\theta/a_\theta\).

Each \(h_\theta\) is a positive integer of polynomial bit length.

The turn indexed \(r\geq0\) of agent \((\theta,k)\) becomes the event

\((rh_\theta,\theta,k)\).

Order all events lexicographically. Their first coordinates are \(H\) times their normalized turn times. This event order implements the forward weighted picking sequence.

A cut is an event \(E=(t,\alpha,b)\), interpreted as including every event through \(E\). Also allow the initial cut \(\bot\), before every event.

Let \(F_\theta(E)\) count events of type \(\theta\) through cut \(E\). Direct counting gives

\(F_\theta(E)=n_\theta\lceil t/h_\theta\rceil+\delta_\theta(E)\),

where \(\delta_\theta(E)\) equals:

  • \(n_\theta\), if \(h_\theta\) divides \(t\) and \(\theta<\alpha\);
  • \(b\), if \(\theta=\alpha\);
  • zero otherwise.

Set \(F_\theta(\bot)=0\).

For a nonempty type subset \(S\), let \(\operatorname{Select}(S,R)\) return its \(R\)-th event, for any positive integer \(R\).

This oracle is polynomial-time computable. Through integer timestamp \(t\geq0\), the number of events from \(S\) is

\(G_S(t)=\sum_{\theta\in S}n_\theta(\lfloor t/h_\theta\rfloor+1)\).

Binary-search the least integer \(t\) with \(G_S(t)\geq R\). An upper bound is \(R\max_\theta h_\theta\): even a single agent supplies \(R\) events by that time.

The number of events strictly before this timestamp is

\(\sum_{\theta\in S}n_\theta\lceil t/h_\theta\rceil\).

Subtract it from \(R\). The remaining rank lies in the blocks of types satisfying \(h_\theta\mid t\). Scanning those blocks in type order identifies both \(\theta\) and \(k\). No agents or events are enumerated.

Two consequences will be used:

  • The \(q\)-th event from \(S\) after cut \(C\) is

\(\operatorname{Select}(S,\sum_{\theta\in S}F_\theta(C)+q)\).

  • If \(M=\sum_{\theta\in S}F_\theta(C)\) and \(q\leq M\), the \(q\)-th event from \(S\) in descending order through \(C\) is

\(\operatorname{Select}(S,M-q+1)\).

The predecessor of an event is also computable: subtract one from its rank among all types and call \(\operatorname{Select}\), using \(\bot\) when that rank becomes zero.

4. Compressed allocation by exhaustion phases

4.1 Goods phases

Maintain remaining counts \(q_j\), initially \(Q_j\), and a current cut \(C\), initially \(\bot\).

During a phase, every active type \(\theta\) has a fixed most valuable positive remaining class \(f_\theta\). It stays fixed until an item class is exhausted.

For each class \(j\), set \(S_j=\{\theta:f_\theta=j\}\). For nonempty \(S_j\), compute

\(E_j=\operatorname{Select}(S_j,\sum_{\theta\in S_j}F_\theta(C)+q_j)\).

This is the event at which class \(j\) would run out if the current choices continued. Let \(E\) be the earliest candidate event.

Between cuts \(C\) and \(E\), type \(\theta\) receives exactly

\(F_\theta(E)-F_\theta(C)\)

copies of class \(f_\theta\). Update the supplies and replace \(C\) by \(E\).

This reproduces forward picking exactly. Before the earliest exhaustion, preferences over available classes do not change. Each active agent’s earlier events have already been processed; inactive types never reactivate. At least one class is exhausted per phase, so there are at most \(|J^+|\) phases.

Assign the classes in \(J^0\) as specified in Section 2.1.

4.2 Chores phases

If \(K^-=0\), this phase is empty. Otherwise initialize

\(C=\operatorname{Select}(T,K^-)\).

The unprocessed chore turns are precisely all events through \(C\), executed in descending order.

During a phase, let \(f_\theta\) be type \(\theta\)’s most valuable remaining chore class, and again set \(S_j=\{\theta:f_\theta=j\}\). Put

\(M_j=\sum_{\theta\in S_j}F_\theta(C)\).

Whenever \(S_j\) is nonempty and \(M_j\geq q_j\), compute

\(E_j=\operatorname{Select}(S_j,M_j-q_j+1)\).

Choose the latest candidate \(E\), since it is the first exhaustion encountered in descending execution. Let \(D\) be its predecessor. The phase processes the event interval \((D,C]\), allocating

\(F_\theta(C)-F_\theta(D)\)

copies of class \(f_\theta\) to type \(\theta\). Update supplies and set \(C=D\).

A candidate always exists while chores remain. Indeed, the number of unprocessed events equals the number of remaining copies. The sets \(S_j\) partition all types. If every \(M_j<q_j\), summing would contradict that equality.

The phase exactly reproduces reversed picking until its first exhaustion. There are at most \(|J^-|\) phases.

Thus the total number of goods and chores phases is at most \(m\).

5. Recovering a polynomial-size list of bundles

Aggregate type counts alone are insufficient: we must recover each agent’s bundle. The event structure makes this possible.

For a particular agent \((\theta,k)\), let \(F_{\theta,k}(E)\) count its events through cut \(E=(t,\alpha,b)\). Then

\(F_{\theta,k}(E)=\lceil t/h_\theta\rceil+\mathbf{1}_{\{k\leq p_\theta(E)\}}\),

where \(p_\theta(E)\) equals:

  • \(n_\theta\), if \(h_\theta\mid t\) and \(\theta<\alpha\);
  • \(b\), if \(\theta=\alpha\);
  • zero otherwise.

At \(\bot\), both terms are zero.

Consequently, the number of copies assigned to agent \((\theta,k)\) during a phase with event interval \((C,E]\) is

\(F_{\theta,k}(E)-F_{\theta,k}(C)\).

As a function of \(k\), this changes only at the two endpoint thresholds \(p_\theta(C)\) and \(p_\theta(E)\). The same observation applies to a reversed phase’s interval \((D,C]\).

For each type:

  • Collect these endpoint thresholds from every allocation phase.
  • Add the threshold after \(k=1\) if that agent receives neutral copies.
  • Partition \(\{1,\ldots,n_\theta\}\) into the resulting consecutive intervals.
  • Evaluate one representative index per interval to obtain its bundle.

Every agent in an interval receives the same bundle. There are at most two internal thresholds per phase, plus one for neutral copies. Hence each type has at most \(2m+2\) intervals.

For an interval containing \(c\) agents and having bundle \(B\), output mass

\(\pi_{\theta,B}=c/N\).

If several intervals have the same type and bundle, combine their masses. The resulting list has at most \(\tau(2m+2)\) positive-mass groups.

Because this list represents the conceptual allocation exactly,

\(\sum_B\pi_{\theta,B}=n_\theta/N=\mu_\theta\),

and

\(\sum_{\theta,B}\pi_{\theta,B}B_j=Q_j/N=s_j\).

Every ordered pair of different supported groups is represented by a pair of conceptual agents. Section 2 therefore proves its WEF1T comparison, including comparisons between groups of the same type. A group’s comparison with itself satisfies the first inequality immediately.

6. Bit complexity and conclusion

All numerical operations use integers of polynomial bit length.

To check this explicitly, every actually processed event has timestamp at most \(K\max_\theta h_\theta\): its individual turn index is at most the total number of allocated copies. Consequently,

\(F_\theta(C)\leq n_\theta(K\max_\phi h_\phi+1)\).

Every rank queried by the oracle is bounded by a sum of such counts plus a remaining supply. Its bit length is therefore polynomial in \(L,m,\tau\). Binary search, exact integer arithmetic, and scans over the types take polynomial time.

There are at most \(m\) allocation phases, each using at most \(m\) selection queries. Recovering the final bundles requires only polynomially many threshold evaluations.

Finally, every bundle coordinate is at most \(Q_j\), and every listed mass has numerator at most \(N\) and denominator \(N\). Together with the bound of \(\tau(2m+2)\) groups, this proves polynomial total output encoding length.

Thus the compressed algorithm satisfies supply conservation, type-mass conservation, and all required WEF1T comparisons in polynomial time.
