The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\tau\) supported types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), strict rankings \(\succ_t\) over acceptable partner types, and mutually acceptable pairs, with each type listing at most three partner types, minimize blocking-pair mass over feasible matching masses. For \(t\ne u\), let \(x_{tu}=x_{ut}\ge0\) be the mass paired across types, and let \(x_{tt}\ge0\) be the mass of within-type pairs; require \(2x_{tt}+\sum_{u\ne t}x_{tu}\le\mu_t\). Define \(y_{tv}=x_{tv}\) for \(v\ne t\) and \(y_{tt}=2x_{tt}\), and \(q_{t,u}=\mu_t-\sum_{v:\,v\succeq_t u}y_{tv}\), the mass unmatched or partnered with a type ranked below \(u\). Minimize \(B(x)=\sum_{\{t,u\}:t\ne u}q_{t,u}q_{u,t}+\frac12\sum_{t:\{t,t\}\text{ acceptable}}q_{t,t}^2\), where \(v\succeq_t u\) means \(t\) ranks \(v\) at least as highly as \(u\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(E\) be the mutually acceptable unordered type pairs, allowing \(\{t,t\}\). Each type strictly ranks its nonempty set of at most three acceptable partner types; every acceptable partner is preferred to being unmatched. Within-type pairing joins distinct individuals of that type, and its partner type counts once toward the list bound.
Write \(m=|E|\). Feasible matching masses are real numbers \(x_{tu}=x_{ut}\ge0\), zero for unacceptable pairs, satisfying \(2x_{tt}+\sum_{u\ne t}x_{tu}\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\); unmatched mass is allowed, with no cardinality requirement.
Set \(y_{tv}=x_{tv}\) for \(v\ne t\), \(y_{tt}=2x_{tt}\), and \(q_{t,u}=\mu_t-\sum_{v:\,v\succeq_t u}y_{tv}\) for acceptable \(\{t,u\}\), where \(\succeq_t\) means at least as preferred.
Define blocking-pair mass by \(B(x)=\sum_{\{t,u\}\in E,\,t\ne u}q_{t,u}q_{u,t}+\frac12\sum_{t:\,\{t,t\}\in E}q_{t,t}^2\).
Given a nonnegative binary rational threshold \(b\), decide whether some feasible \(x\) satisfies \(B(x)\le b\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including masses and threshold; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is this continuous blocking-pair-mass decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite agents with strict incomplete lists of length at most three, minimizing blocking pairs is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and inapproximable within \(1017/1016-\delta\) for \(0<\delta\le1/1016\), unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\); lists of length at most two admit a linear-time algorithm. Result. The problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). In fact, every valid instance is a YES-instance: there always exists a feasible allocation with \(B(x)=0\). This follows from Scarf’s lemma, including for unequal masses and within-type pairs.

1. The existence theorem. We use the following matrix formulation of Scarf’s lemma.

Let \(A,C\) have \(n\) rows and \(n+k\) columns, and let \(h\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}\). Suppose:

  • The first \(n\) columns of \(A\) form the identity matrix.
  • The set \(\{z\geq 0:Az=h\}\) is nonempty and bounded.
  • For every row \(i\), every additional column \(e\), and every identity column \(j\neq i\), we have \(C_{ii}<C_{ie}<C_{ij}\).

Then there exists \(z\geq 0\) with \(Az=h\) such that, for every column \(e\), some row \(i\) satisfies \(C_{ie}\leq C_{ij}\) for every column \(j\) with \(z_j>0\). This is the domination property. [Scarf’s lemma, Theorem 5 in Biró–Fleiner](https://real.mtak.hu/26264/7/BF16do_last.pdf#page=6).

We now construct matrices satisfying these hypotheses and translate domination into the exact condition needed for the objective.

2. Encoding matching masses. Index rows by \(T\) and introduce one slack variable \(s_t\) for each type, representing unmatched mass. Index the remaining variables by \(E\), with \(x_e=x_{tu}\) for \(e=\{t,u\}\).

Define the incidence matrix \(D\) as follows:

  • For \(e=\{t,u\}\) with \(t\neq u\), its column has coefficient \(1\) in rows \(t,u\) and coefficient \(0\) elsewhere.
  • For \(e=\{t,t\}\), its column has coefficient \(2\) in row \(t\) and coefficient \(0\) elsewhere.

Set \(A=[I\ D]\), \(z=(s,x)\), and \(h=\mu\). The equation \(Az=\mu\) is precisely the collection of equations
\(\displaystyle s_t+2x_{tt}+\sum_{u\neq t}x_{tu}=\mu_t\).
Consequently, its nonnegative solutions correspond exactly to feasible matching masses and their unmatched masses.

This system is nonempty: take \(s=\mu\) and \(x=0\). It is bounded because summing its rows gives
\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}s_t+2\sum_{e\in E}x_e=1\).
Thus every coordinate is bounded. Also, every coordinate of \(\mu\) is positive by hypothesis.

3. Encoding preferences. Let \(d_t\leq 3\) be the number of acceptable partner types of \(t\). Assign an incident edge corresponding to the partner of rank \(r\), where rank \(1\) is best, the score \(d_t+1-r\). Thus incident scores belong to \(\{1,2,3\}\), and larger scores mean better partners.

Define \(C\) by:

  • \(C_{t,s_t}=0\).
  • \(C_{t,s_u}=5\) for \(u\neq t\).
  • \(C_{t,e}=d_t+1-r\) when \(e\) is incident to \(t\) and its partner has rank \(r\).
  • \(C_{t,e}=4\) when \(e\) is not incident to \(t\).

Every edge-column score lies strictly between \(0\) and \(5\). Therefore all the hypotheses of Scarf’s lemma hold. Obtain a nonnegative solution \(z=(s,x)\) with the domination property.

4. Domination forces zero blocking mass. Fix an acceptable edge \(e\), and let \(t\) be a row that dominates it.

First, \(t\) must be incident to \(e\). Indeed, the positive row total \(\mu_t\) implies that some column \(j\) satisfies both \(A_{tj}>0\) and \(z_j>0\). Such a column is either \(s_t\) or an incident edge, so \(C_{tj}\leq 3\). If \(e\) were nonincident, domination would require \(4=C_{te}\leq C_{tj}\), a contradiction.

Write \(u\) for the partner represented by \(e\) at endpoint \(t\), allowing \(u=t\). Since \(C_{te}\geq 1\), domination implies \(s_t=0\): otherwise the positive column \(s_t\) would require \(C_{te}\leq C_{t,s_t}=0\).

Likewise, every incident edge with positive allocation has score at least \(C_{te}\). By the definition of scores and strictness of preferences, every positively assigned partner type of \(t\) is at least as preferred as \(u\).

Using \(y_{tt}=2x_{tt}\) and \(y_{tv}=x_{tv}\) otherwise, the row equation therefore gives
\(\displaystyle \mu_t=\sum_{v:\,v\succeq_t u}y_{tv}\),
and hence \(q_{t,u}=0\).

We have proved:

  • For each off-diagonal acceptable pair \(\{t,u\}\), at least one of \(q_{t,u},q_{u,t}\) is zero.
  • For each acceptable within-type pair \(\{t,t\}\), its only incident row gives \(q_{t,t}=0\).

Every summand in \(B(x)\) is consequently zero. Furthermore, for any feasible allocation, each \(q_{t,u}\) is nonnegative because its defining sum is a subset of the total assigned mass of \(t\). Thus \(B\) is always nonnegative, and the allocation above proves \(\min_x B(x)=0\).

The coefficient \(2\) in the loop column is essential to this verification: it ensures that domination yields the condition involving \(y_{tt}=2x_{tt}\), exactly as specified in the task.

5. Why finite-agent hardness does not transfer. Consider three types \(a,b,c\), each of mass \(1/3\), with no within-type pairs and preferences
\(b\succ_a c\), \(c\succ_b a\), and \(a\succ_c b\).
Set \(x_{ab}=x_{bc}=x_{ca}=1/6\).

Every type is fully assigned. Each edge is the less-preferred edge at one endpoint, where its corresponding \(q\) equals zero. Hence \(B(x)=0\).

For three individual agents with these preferences, however, every matching has a blocking pair. If the matched pair is \(\{a,b\}\), then \(\{b,c\}\) blocks; the other two possibilities follow cyclically. The empty matching also has blocking pairs. Splitting mass therefore changes the existence question substantively.

6. Decision algorithm and running time. Since the threshold satisfies \(b\geq 0\), the proved zero-objective allocation always satisfies \(B(x)\leq b\).

The decision algorithm is simply return YES on every input satisfying the stated promises. Its decision work is \(O(1)\); including reading the input, its running time is \(O(L+m+\tau)=O(L)\), with constant auxiliary space if the input is streamed. This does not require expanding rational masses into individual agents.
