The adaptive recomputation bootstrap is consistent

Under the stated assumptions, the weak limit exists and the conditional bootstrap law consistently estimates it. In fact, the threshold condition can be weakened to any sequence \(\delta_N>0\) tending to zero.

The key is that, with finitely many item types, differentiability forces every positively valued item to have a unique winning type at the population multipliers. This makes the equilibrium map exactly affine in the masses near the population mass vector.

For context, [Liao and Kroer, Section 4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02303v3#S4.SS4) study an adaptive proximal bootstrap based on sampled items. The advertiser-mass result here follows directly from the finite-support structure proved below.

1. Smoothness forces unique winners

Write \(\beta^*=\beta(\mu)\), and define

\(F(\beta)=\sum_{j=1}^m s_j f_j(\beta),\qquad f_j(\beta)=\max_{1\leq k\leq\tau}\beta_kv_{kj}.\)

Because the logarithmic term is smooth throughout the positive orthant, the assumed smoothness of \(H_\mu\) implies that \(F\) is differentiable at \(\beta^*\).

Let \(A_j=\{k:\beta_k^*v_{kj}=f_j(\beta^*)\}\). For any direction \(h\), the one-sided directional derivative is

\(f_j'(\beta^*;h)=\max_{k\in A_j}v_{kj}h_k.\)

Indeed, the finitely many inactive linear functions have strictly smaller values at \(\beta^*\), so they cannot attain the maximum under sufficiently small perturbations in a fixed direction. Consequently,

\(f_j'(\beta^*;h)+f_j'(\beta^*;-h)=\max_{k\in A_j}v_{kj}h_k-\min_{k\in A_j}v_{kj}h_k\geq0.\)

Differentiability of \(F\) gives

\(0=F'(\beta^*;h)+F'(\beta^*;-h)=\sum_{j=1}^m s_j\bigl(f_j'(\beta^*;h)+f_j'(\beta^*;-h)\bigr).\)

Every summand is nonnegative, and every supply is positive. Thus every summand must vanish.

Suppose an item \(j\) has a positive maximum and two distinct winning types \(k,\ell\). Then \(v_{kj}>0\). Taking \(h=e_k\), the directional-derivative sum for that item equals \(v_{kj}>0\): among its active types, the \(k\)-coordinate contributions include both \(v_{kj}\) and zero. This is a contradiction.

Hence every item with positive maximum has a unique winner. If an item has zero maximum, positivity of every \(\beta_k^*\) and nonnegativity of valuations imply that all its valuations are zero.

For each nonzero item, let \(w(j)\) denote its unique winner, and define

\(a_k=\sum_{\substack{j:\,w(j)=k}}s_jv_{kj}.\)

The strict winning inequalities persist in an open neighborhood \(U\) of \(\beta^*\). Since there are finitely many items, one common neighborhood suffices. Throughout \(U\),

\(F(\beta)=\sum_{k=1}^{\tau}a_k\beta_k.\)

Thus the price term is locally linear.

2. The equilibrium map is locally affine

Define the paced and unpaced sets by

\(P=\{k:\beta_k^*<1\},\qquad I=\{k:\beta_k^*=1\}.\)

Locally,

\(\partial_kH_\mu(\beta)=a_k-\mu_kb_k/\beta_k.\)

For \(k\in P\), both positive and negative sufficiently small coordinate perturbations are feasible. Optimality therefore gives

\(a_k=\mu_kb_k/\beta_k^*>0.\)

For \(k\in I\), strict complementarity gives

\(a_k-\mu_kb_k<0.\)

For a positive mass vector \(q\) near \(\mu\), define

\(\gamma_k(q)=q_kb_k/a_k\quad(k\in P),\qquad \gamma_k(q)=1\quad(k\in I).\)

There is a neighborhood \(V\) of \(\mu\), relative to the probability simplex, such that every \(q\in V\) has positive coordinates and satisfies all of the following:

  • \(\gamma(q)\in U\);
  • \(0<\gamma_k(q)<1\) for \(k\in P\);
  • \(q_kb_k>a_k\) for \(k\in I\).

These facts follow from continuity and the strict inequalities established at \(\mu\).

We now prove that \(\gamma(q)\) is the global minimizer. At this point,

\(\partial_kH_q(\gamma(q))=0\quad(k\in P),\qquad \partial_kH_q(\gamma(q))=a_k-q_kb_k<0\quad(k\in I).\)

For every feasible \(y\in(0,1]^\tau\),

\(\nabla H_q(\gamma(q))^\mathsf{T}(y-\gamma(q))=\sum_{k\in I}(a_k-q_kb_k)(y_k-1)\geq0.\)

Convexity therefore implies \(H_q(y)\geq H_q(\gamma(q))\). Moreover, \(H_q\) is strictly convex because every coefficient \(q_kb_k\) of the strictly convex function \(-\log\beta_k\) is positive. Hence its minimizer is unique, and

\(\beta(q)=\gamma(q)\qquad(q\in V).\)

Existence also holds for arbitrary mass vectors on their supported coordinates: the feasible point with all coordinates equal to one has finite objective, while the objective diverges if any supported coordinate tends to zero. Its relevant sublevel set is therefore compact. The same argument applies after imposing any of the prescribed equality constraints.

Define the diagonal matrix \(D\) by

\(D_{kk}=b_k/a_k=\beta_k^*/\mu_k\quad(k\in P),\qquad D_{kk}=0\quad(k\in I).\)

We have proved the exact local identity

\(\beta(q)-\beta^*=D(q-\mu)\qquad(q\in V).\)

In particular, every unpaced coordinate remains exactly equal to one throughout this mass neighborhood.

3. The multiplier limit law

Let

\(\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\mu)-\mu\mu^\mathsf{T}.\)

The observations are independent categorical vectors with mean \(\mu\) and covariance \(\Sigma\). They are bounded, so the multivariate central limit theorem applies:

\(\sqrt N(\hat\mu_N-\mu)\Rightarrow Z,\qquad Z\sim\mathcal N_\tau(0,\Sigma).\)

The law of large numbers gives \(\mathbb P(\hat\mu_N\in V)\to1\). On this event, the exact local identity yields

\(\sqrt N(\hat\beta_N-\beta^*)=D\sqrt N(\hat\mu_N-\mu).\)

Two random vectors that agree with probability tending to one have the same weak limit, whenever either limit exists. Therefore

\(\sqrt N(\hat\beta_N-\beta^*)\Rightarrow J,\qquad J=\mathcal N_\tau(0,D\Sigma D^\mathsf{T}).\)

This Gaussian law may be singular. Its coordinates indexed by \(I\) are identically zero.

4. The adaptive constraints are eventually correct

Shrink \(V\), if necessary, so that for some fixed \(g>0\),

\(\beta_k(q)\leq1-g\qquad(q\in V,\ k\in P).\)

When \(P\) is nonempty, such a \(g\) exists because every population paced coordinate has a strictly positive gap below one and the local map is continuous. When \(P\) is empty, this condition is vacuous.

For all sufficiently large \(N\), \(\delta_N<g\). Whenever \(\hat\mu_N\in V\), the unpaced coordinates equal one, while the paced coordinates are at most \(1-g\). Consequently,

\(\hat I_N=I.\)

Thus \(\mathbb P(\hat I_N=I)\to1\). This conclusion uses only positivity of \(\delta_N\) and convergence to zero; its rate is immaterial.

Choose \(r>0\) such that the relative ball

\(B_r(\mu)=\{q:\ q\text{ is a probability vector},\ \|q-\mu\|_2<r\}\)

is contained in \(V\). Let \(E_N\) be the event that \(\|\hat\mu_N-\mu\|_2<r/2\) and \(\hat I_N=I\). Then \(\mathbb P(E_N)\to1\).

Write \(\mathbb P^b\) and \(\mathbb E^b\) for conditional bootstrap probability and expectation. The multinomial covariance formula gives

\(\mathbb E^b\|\hat\mu_N^b-\hat\mu_N\|_2^2=\bigl(1-\|\hat\mu_N\|_2^2\bigr)/N\leq1/N.\)

On \(E_N\), if \(\hat\mu_N^b\notin B_r(\mu)\), then \(\|\hat\mu_N^b-\hat\mu_N\|_2\geq r/2\). Markov’s inequality therefore gives

\(\mathbb P^b(\hat\mu_N^b\notin B_r(\mu))\leq4/(Nr^2).\)

If both \(E_N\) occurs and \(\hat\mu_N^b\in B_r(\mu)\), the unconstrained minimizer already equals one on every coordinate in \(I=\hat I_N\). It therefore also minimizes the adaptively constrained objective. Uniqueness gives

\(\beta_N^b=\beta(\hat\mu_N^b).\)

Using the affine identity at both mass vectors, we obtain

\(\sqrt N(\beta_N^b-\hat\beta_N)=D\sqrt N(\hat\mu_N^b-\hat\mu_N).\)

All absent-type cases are covered by the exceptional events above, because every mass vector in \(V\) has full support.

5. Conditional convergence in bounded-Lipschitz distance

We first justify the required conditional multinomial limit.

For any deterministic sequence of probability vectors \(p_N\to\mu\), consider independent categorical vectors \(Y_{N,1},\ldots,Y_{N,N}\) with probability vector \(p_N\). For each fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^\tau\), the centered scalar variables \(t^\mathsf{T}(Y_{N,i}-p_N)\) are uniformly bounded. Their variances converge to \(t^\mathsf{T}\Sigma t\).

If this limiting variance is positive, boundedness verifies the Lindeberg condition, so the scalar triangular-array central limit theorem applies. If it is zero, the normalized sum converges to zero in probability by its variance. The Cramér–Wold theorem consequently gives

\(N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^N(Y_{N,i}-p_N)\Rightarrow\mathcal N_\tau(0,\Sigma).\)

Applying the fixed linear map \(D\) gives the limit \(J\).

Let \(K_N(p)\) denote the law of this transformed normalized sum. We have shown that for every deterministic \(p_N\to\mu\),

\(d_{\mathrm{BL}}(K_N(p_N),J)\to0,\)

because bounded-Lipschitz distance metrizes weak convergence on finite-dimensional Euclidean space.

Since \(\hat\mu_N\to\mu\) in probability, this also implies

\(d_{\mathrm{BL}}(K_N(\hat\mu_N),J)\xrightarrow{p}0.\)

For completeness, the implication follows by the subsequence criterion: every subsequence has a further subsequence on which \(\hat\mu_N\to\mu\) almost surely; on each such sample path, the deterministic-sequence result applies.

Conditionally on the observations, \(K_N(\hat\mu_N)\) is exactly the law of \(D\sqrt N(\hat\mu_N^b-\hat\mu_N)\).

Finally, bounded-Lipschitz test functions have absolute value at most one. On \(E_N\), the two bootstrap vectors in Section 4 disagree with conditional probability at most \(4/(Nr^2)\). Hence, on \(E_N\),

\(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(\mathcal L^b\!\left(\sqrt N(\beta_N^b-\hat\beta_N)\right),J\right)\leq\frac{8}{Nr^2}+d_{\mathrm{BL}}(K_N(\hat\mu_N),J).\)

The right-hand side tends to zero in probability, and \(\mathbb P(E_N^c)\to0\). This proves the requested consistency.
