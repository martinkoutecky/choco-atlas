The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of types \(T\), each with a strict ranking of \(m\) objects and an institutionally assigned tie-break priority class for each object, rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and rational capacities \(q_o\) summing to \(1\), find a feasible mass assignment \(x_{t,o}\). For object \(o\), order types by Rawlsian claim, with a larger rank number giving a stronger claim, then by tie-break class. Call \(x\) stable if there are no \(t,o\) with \(x_{t,o'}>0\) for some \(o'\) ranked below \(o\) by \(t\), and lower-priority \(s\) with \(x_{s,o}>0\). Determine whether every such instance has a stable assignment that, for each type and rank cutoff, assigns at least as much mass to objects at or above that cutoff as every other stable assignment; compute one if it exists.

Each type has a strict ranking of all objects and an institutional priority class \(c_{t,o}\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) at each object, with smaller classes preceding larger ones. Write \(r_t(o)\) for the position of \(o\) in type \(t\)'s ranking, starting at \(1\). Type \(t\) has strictly higher priority than \(s\) at \(o\) when \(r_t(o)>r_s(o)\), or when \(r_t(o)=r_s(o)\) and \(c_{t,o}<c_{s,o}\). Equal ranks and equal classes remain tied, without further refinement.

Call a stable assignment \(x^\star\) type-optimal if, for every stable assignment \(y\), type \(t\), and cutoff \(k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), \(\sum_{o:r_t(o)\leq k}x^\star_{t,o}\geq\sum_{o:r_t(o)\leq k}y_{t,o}\).

Does every instance admit a type-optimal stable assignment?
Is deciding whether one exists, and computing one when it does, solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is the existence decision problem NP-hard?

1. Universal existence fails

Take two objects $A,B$ and two types $a,b$, both ranking $A$ above $B$. Set
$$ \mu_a=\mu_b=q_A=q_B=\frac12. $$
Choose classes
$$ c_{a,A}=c_{b,A}=1,\qquad c_{a,B}=1,\qquad c_{b,B}=2. $$
Thus the types are distinct but tied at $A$.

Every feasible assignment has the form
$$ x_{a,A}=\alpha,\qquad x_{a,B}=\frac12-\alpha,\qquad x_{b,A}=\frac12-\alpha,\qquad x_{b,B}=\alpha, $$
where $0\leq\alpha\leq\frac12$.

Every such assignment is stable. A blocking configuration could only involve a type assigned positive mass at $B$ claiming $A$. Neither type has strictly higher priority than the other at $A$.

The assignment with $\alpha=\frac12$ gives $a$ its entire mass at its first choice; the assignment with $\alpha=0$ does the same for $b$. A type-optimal assignment would therefore require
$$ x_{a,A}\geq\frac12,\qquad x_{b,A}\geq\frac12, $$
contradicting the capacity $q_A=\frac12$.

2. Stable assignments always exist

Although type-optimality can fail, stability itself always exists.

Choose a positive integer $D$ clearing the denominators of all masses and capacities. Replace type $t$ by $D\mu_t$ individual agents and object $o$ by $Dq_o$ individual slots. Both totals equal $D$.

Each agent ranks all slots consistently with its type’s object ranking, ordering slots within an object arbitrarily. Each slot strictly ranks all agents, preserving every original strict type-priority comparison and refining the remaining ties arbitrarily. Such a refinement exists because the original priorities are ordered lexicographically by descending rank and then ascending institutional class.

Run agent-proposing deferred acceptance. Each agent proposes to each slot at most once, so the algorithm terminates. No agent finishes unmatched: an unmatched agent that exhausted its list would imply that all $D$ slots hold other agents, contradicting the presence of only $D$ agents.

The resulting matching is stable. If an agent prefers a slot to its final slot, it previously proposed there and was rejected. That slot’s final occupant ranks strictly above the rejected agent.

Aggregate the matching by types and objects and divide the counts by $D$. This gives a feasible assignment. An aggregate blocking configuration would yield an individual assigned to the worse object and a slot at the desired object occupied by the lower-priority type. They would block the individual matching, a contradiction.

This establishes nonemptiness. The expansion may be exponential in $L$ and is used here only as an existence proof.

3. A representation using one priority cutoff per object

Write $t\succ_o s$ for the specified strict priority comparison.

For each object $o$, label its distinct priority levels by
$$ \ell_o(t)\in\{1,\ldots,d_o\},\qquad d_o\leq\tau, $$
with smaller labels indicating higher priority. Thus
$$ t\succ_o s\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \ell_o(t)<\ell_o(s). $$
These labels are obtained by sorting the pairs $(r_t(o),c_{t,o})$ by descending first coordinate and ascending second coordinate, assigning equal pairs the same label.

A cutoff profile chooses
$$ p_o\in\{1,\ldots,d_o\} $$
for every object. Define
$$ b_t(p)= \min\left( \{r_t(a):a\in O,\ \ell_a(t)<p_a\}\cup\{m\} \right). $$
Define the allowed entries
$$ E(p)= \{(t,o):\ell_o(t)\leq p_o,\ r_t(o)\leq b_t(p)\}, $$
and the transportation face
$$ P(p)= \left\{ x\geq0: \sum_o x_{t,o}=\mu_t,\ \sum_t x_{t,o}=q_o,\ x_{t,o}=0\text{ outside }E(p) \right\}. $$

Proposition

If $\mathcal S$ denotes the set of stable assignments, then
$$ \mathcal S= \bigcup_{p\in\prod_o\{1,\ldots,d_o\}}P(p). $$

Proof that every assignment in a face is stable. Suppose $x\in P(p)$ and $x_{s,o}>0$. Then $\ell_o(s)\leq p_o$.

If $t\succ_o s$, then
$$ \ell_o(t)<\ell_o(s)\leq p_o. $$
Consequently, the definition of $b_t(p)$ gives
$$ b_t(p)\leq r_t(o). $$
Every positive entry in row $t$ has rank at most $b_t(p)$. Therefore $t$ receives no mass at an object worse than $o$, and cannot block against $s$ at $o$.

Proof that every stable assignment belongs to a face. Let $x$ be stable. Since $q_o>0$, each column has a positive entry. Set
$$ p_o=\max\{\ell_o(s):x_{s,o}>0\}. $$
All positive entries at $o$ satisfy the priority cutoff.

If $\ell_o(t)<p_o$, there is a positive occupant at $o$ with priority strictly below $t$. Stability then implies
$$ x_{t,a}=0\qquad\text{whenever }r_t(a)>r_t(o). $$
Applying this implication at every object where $\ell_o(t)<p_o$ shows that all positive entries in row $t$ have rank at most $b_t(p)$. Hence $x\in P(p)$. $\square$

There are at most
$$ \prod_o d_o\leq\tau^m $$
such faces.

Each face is closed and bounded, since $0\leq x_{t,o}\leq\mu_t$. The union is finite. Together with Section 2, this proves that $\mathcal S$ is nonempty and compact.

4. An alternative representation using worst assigned ranks

For a profile
$$ w=(w_t)_{t\in T}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}^{T}, $$

$$ E(w)= \left\{ (s,o): r_s(o)\leq w_s,\ \text{there is no }t\text{ with }r_t(o)<w_t\text{ and }t\succ_o s \right\}. $$

5. The unique candidate is automatically stable

For each type and cutoff, define
$$ F_{t,k}(x)=\sum_{o:r_t(o)\leq k}x_{t,o}, \qquad M_{t,k}=\max_{x\in\mathcal S}F_{t,k}(x), \qquad M_{t,0}=0. $$
The maxima exist by compactness.

Define the candidate matrix by successive differences:
$$ \widehat x_{t,o} = M_{t,r_t(o)}-M_{t,r_t(o)-1}. $$
Since prefix totals are nondecreasing and $M_{t,m}=\mu_t$,
$$ \widehat x_{t,o}\geq0,\qquad \sum_o\widehat x_{t,o}=\mu_t. $$

Lemma: the candidate has no blocking configuration

This holds even before its column sums are checked.

Proof. Suppose, to the contrary, that
$$ r_t(o')>r_t(o),\qquad \widehat x_{t,o'}>0,\qquad \widehat x_{s,o}>0,\qquad t\succ_o s. $$
Put $k=r_t(o)$. Telescoping and the positive entry below cutoff $k$ give
$$ M_{t,k} = \sum_{a:r_t(a)\leq k}\widehat x_{t,a} <\mu_t. $$
For every stable assignment $y$,
$$ F_{t,k}(y)\leq M_{t,k}<\mu_t. $$
Thus every stable assignment gives $t$ some positive mass at an object worse than $o$. Because $t\succ_o s$, stability forces
$$ y_{s,o}=0 \qquad\text{for every }y\in\mathcal S. $$
Writing $j=r_s(o)$, this means
$$ F_{s,j}(y)=F_{s,j-1}(y) \qquad\text{for every }y\in\mathcal S. $$
Their maxima are equal, so
$$ \widehat x_{s,o}=M_{s,j}-M_{s,j-1}=0, $$
a contradiction. $\square$

Exact existence criterion

A type-optimal stable assignment exists if and only if
$$ \sum_t\widehat x_{t,o}=q_o \qquad\text{for every }o. $$
When it exists, it is uniquely equal to $\widehat x$.

Proof. Any type-optimal stable assignment must attain every $M_{t,k}$: dominance gives one inequality, and membership in $\mathcal S$ gives the other. Its coordinates are therefore the successive differences defining $\widehat x$.

Conversely, if the column equalities hold, $\widehat x$ is feasible and is stable by the lemma. Its prefix totals equal the maxima, so it is type-optimal. $\square$

Equivalently, it suffices to check
$$ \sum_t\widehat x_{t,o}\leq q_o \qquad\text{for every }o, $$
because the candidate’s row sums and the capacities both sum to $1$.

7. Arithmetic and the general oracle upper bound

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the input masses and capacities. It clears all denominators and has binary length $O(L)$.

Lemma: prefix maxima lie on the denominator grid

For every $t,k$,
$$ DM_{t,k}\in\mathbb Z. $$

Proof. Scale a nonempty transportation face by $D$. Its margins are integers.

At a vertex, the bipartite graph of positive entries is a forest. Otherwise, an even cycle permits opposite alternating perturbations of sufficiently small positive size. Both perturbations preserve margins, required zeros, and nonnegativity, contradicting extremality.

At a leaf of this forest, its unique incident positive entry equals the corresponding integer residual margin. Remove that edge and update the adjacent margin. Repetition proves that all positive entries are integers.

A linear objective on a nonempty compact polytope attains its maximum at a vertex. Therefore the maximum of $DF_{t,k}$ on each face is an integer. Taking the maximum over the finitely many faces proves the assertion. $\square$

For an integer $j$, the threshold question
$$ \text{Does some }x\in\mathcal S\text{ satisfy }DF_{t,k}(x)\geq j? $$
belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

Indeed, if a real assignment satisfies the threshold, maximize that prefix on a face containing it and choose a maximizing vertex. The scaled vertex is an integer certificate. Its entries lie between $0$ and $D$, and its margins, threshold, and stability can all be checked using polynomially many exact integer operations.

8. The exact two-object criterion

For completeness, the supplied two-object criterion can be proved directly.

Let
$$ U=\{t:A\text{ is first}\},\qquad V=\{t:B\text{ is first}\}, $$
and write
$$ M=\sum_{t\in U}\mu_t,\qquad N=\sum_{t\in V}\mu_t. $$
For a feasible assignment, let
$$ z=\sum_{t\in U}x_{t,A},\qquad w=\sum_{t\in V}x_{t,B}. $$
The column constraints give
$$ z-w=q_A-N. $$
The feasible range is
$$ \max\{0,q_A-N\}\leq z\leq\min\{M,q_A\}. $$

Only first-choice objects can be claimed. At $A$, a claimant from $U$ has rank $1$, while an occupant from $V$ has rank $2$ and therefore higher priority. Hence all possible blocks are within $U$ at $A$, or within $V$ at $B$.

Within either group, stability requires first-choice mass to be allocated in increasing institutional-class order. A later class cannot receive positive first-choice mass while an earlier class remains unsatisfied. Division within a tied class is unrestricted.

For a class $C$, let $H_C$ be the total mass in earlier classes and $S_C$ its own total mass. At group first-choice budget $b$, its allocation is necessarily
$$ \phi_C(b)=\min\{S_C,\max\{0,b-H_C\}\}. $$
Conversely, allocations following these class totals are stable.

The simultaneous maximum first-choice budgets are
$$ B_U=\min\{M,q_A\},\qquad B_V=\min\{N,q_B\}. $$
They are jointly feasible: substituting $z=B_U$ into the balance equation gives $w=B_V$. Class-order allocation realizes them stably.

Criterion. A type-optimal stable assignment exists if and only if every class partly funded at its group’s maximum budget contains exactly one type. Here “partly funded” means
$$ H_C<B<H_C+S_C. $$

Necessity. Dominance over a stable assignment realizing both maximum budgets forces a type-optimal assignment to realize those budgets itself.

Suppose a partly funded class has at least two types, and put $R=B-H_C$. Then $0<R<S_C$. For each $t\in C$, a stable maximum-budget assignment can give $t$ first-choice mass $\min\{\mu_t,R\}$, distributing the remainder among the other tied types. But
$$ \sum_{t\in C}\min\{\mu_t,R\}>R. $$
If some $\mu_t\geq R$, its contribution is $R$ and another positive-mass type contributes positively. Otherwise the sum is $S_C>R$. No assignment can simultaneously dominate these individual possibilities.

Sufficiency. Allocate both maximum budgets in class order. Fully funded types attain their entire masses at their first choices. An unfunded class receives zero at every smaller feasible budget. A partly funded singleton receives its largest possible first-choice allocation because $\phi_C$ is nondecreasing.

Thus every type receives its maximum possible first-choice mass. With two objects, this is type-optimality. Empty groups and budgets at class boundaries are included in the argument.

The criterion and assignment are computable by sorting classes and taking exact rational cumulative sums. For one object, the unique feasible assignment is trivially type-optimal.

9. What remains open

The strict-priority lattice results cited in the task concern stability after all priority ties have been refined. They do not establish a greatest assignment among all assignments stable under the original shared classes. [Ekici–Ertemel–Yenmez](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.29080v1), [corrected Baïou–Balinski paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302)

The remaining problem is to compute or otherwise assess the simultaneous prefix maxima in polynomial time when both dimensions vary, or to prove hardness under the exact rank-induced priorities and divisible-quantity model. The automatic-stability lemma reduces the final test to column feasibility, but it does not make computing those maxima polynomial.
