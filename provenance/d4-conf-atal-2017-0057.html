The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given τ exposure classes with rational masses \(M_i>0\) summing to \(1\), a symmetric rational interaction matrix \(W\), and rational \(\alpha\le \tfrac12\), decide whether there exist belief masses \(\beta_i\in[0,M_i]\) with \(\sum_i\beta_i<\tfrac12\) and a finite sequence of weakly improving positive-mass opinion transfers that starts truthfully and ends at an equilibrium where at least half the society expresses \(1\). For class \(i\), let \(q_i\) be its fraction expressing \(1\); a type-\((i,b)\) individual choosing \(s\in\{0,1\}\) receives \(U_{ib}(s;q)=\alpha\mathbf{1}[s=b]+(1-\alpha)\sum_j M_jW_{ij}((1-q_j)\mathbf{1}[s=0]+q_j\mathbf{1}[s=1])\). Each transfer moves positive mass of one complete type from its current opinion to the other, which must give at least as much payoff at the state before the transfer; the terminal state must be an equilibrium.

Input also includes a symmetric nonnegative rational matrix \(W\) and rational \(0<\alpha\le\tfrac12\). Diagonal entries represent interaction within an exposure class and may be positive. There are \(m=2\) opinions; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

An equilibrium requires every opinion occupied by positive mass of a complete type to maximize that type's utility.

Is deciding whether some \(\beta\) with \(\sum_i\beta_i<\tfrac12\) admits a finite permitted sequence from the truthful state to an equilibrium satisfying \(\sum_{i,b}z_{ib}\ge\tfrac12\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite simple unweighted graphs and \(0<\alpha\le\tfrac12\), a suitable minority belief assignment can be computed in polynomial time whenever the graph is outside the forbidden families: complete or edgeless graphs, and, for even \(n\), a clique plus one isolated vertex or graphs with minimum degree at least \(n-2\). The main new result below is a complete polynomial-time characterization when all off-diagonal entries of $W$ are equal. Diagonal entries remain arbitrary and nonnegative. Consequently, this resolves every instance with $\tau\le2$, as well as every diagonal interaction matrix.

1. Utility differences and monotonicity

Let
$$ x_i=z_{i0}+z_{i1},\qquad a=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}>0, \qquad H_i(x)=\sum_jW_{ij}(2x_j-M_j). $$

Since $M_jq_j=x_j$, subtraction of the utilities gives
$$ \frac{U_{ib}(1;q)-U_{ib}(0;q)}{1-\alpha} = H_i(x)+(2b-1)a. $$

Write this normalized difference as $D_{ib}(z)$. Thus
$$ D_{i0}(z)=H_i(x)-a, \qquad D_{i1}(z)=H_i(x)+a. $$

An upward transfer, from opinion $0$ to opinion $1$, is permitted exactly when $D_{ib}(z)\ge0$ before the transfer. A downward transfer is permitted exactly when $D_{ib}(z)\le0$.

For a positive-capacity complete type, equilibrium requires
$$ z_{ib}>0\Longrightarrow D_{ib}(z)\ge0, \qquad z_{ib}<c_{ib}\Longrightarrow D_{ib}(z)\le0. $$

All entries of $W$ are nonnegative. Therefore componentwise comparison gives
$$ z\le z' \Longrightarrow D_{ib}(z)\le D_{ib}(z') $$
for every complete type. This remains valid when diagonal entries are positive.

2. General bounded dynamics

Fix an arbitrary real belief vector $\beta$.

Starting from truth, repeatedly transfer the entire belief-$0$ capacity of any class whose belief-$0$ type is still at opinion $0$ and satisfies $H_i(x)\ge a$. Only positive-capacity types are selected. Each class is selected at most once, so this uses at most $\tau$ transfers. Call the terminal state $U$.

Its belief-$1$ coordinates are $U_{i1}=\beta_i$, while each belief-$0$ coordinate is either zero or its full capacity. Every positive-capacity belief-$0$ type left at zero satisfies
$$ D_{i0}(U)<0. $$
The inequality is strict because ties permit a further transfer.

Lemma 1. Every state reachable from truth by a finite permitted sequence lies componentwise below $U$.

Proof. Truth lies below $U$. Assume the bound holds before a move.

A downward move preserves the bound. An upward move of a belief-$1$ type cannot exceed $U_{i1}$, which is its capacity. The same holds for a belief-$0$ type whose coordinate in $U$ equals its capacity.

For any remaining positive-capacity belief-$0$ type, monotonicity gives
$$ D_{i0}(z)\le D_{i0}(U)<0. $$
Its upward move is prohibited. Induction proves the assertion, including arbitrary partial transfers. $\square$

Starting from $U$, now repeatedly remove the entire opinion-$1$ mass of a type with $D_{ib}(z)<0$. These are permitted downward transfers. Every type is removed at most once, so there are at most $2\tau$ such transfers. Let $E$ be the terminal state.

Lemma 2. The state $E$ is an equilibrium and dominates every equilibrium lying below $U$.

Proof. A positive-capacity type remaining at opinion $1$ satisfies $D_{ib}(E)\ge0$ by termination.

A positive-capacity type at opinion $0$ either was already at zero in $U$, or was removed. In the first case its difference was strictly negative at $U$. In the second case its difference was strictly negative when removed. Subsequent downward transfers cannot increase that difference. Thus its difference is strictly negative at $E$. These comparisons prove equilibrium.

Now let $y\le U$ be any equilibrium, allowing partially split types. Track the downward construction with current state $v$, initially $U$. Suppose $y\le v$ and the construction removes type $(i,b)$ because $D_{ib}(v)<0$. If $y_{ib}>0$, equilibrium and monotonicity would imply
$$ 0\le D_{ib}(y)\le D_{ib}(v)<0, $$
a contradiction. Hence $y_{ib}=0$, and the removal preserves $y\le v$. Induction gives $y\le E$. $\square$

It follows that $E$ is the greatest reachable equilibrium. In particular,
$$ \text{a reachable majority equilibrium exists} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_{i,b}E_{ib}\ge\frac12. $$

It is reached using at most $3\tau$ transfers. For rational $\beta$, its construction takes polynomial time: a transfer changing $x_k$ by $\Delta$ updates the fields by
$$ H_i\leftarrow H_i+2W_{ik}\Delta. $$
There are only polynomially many arithmetic operations, and every state coordinate is zero or a capacity. Consequently, all rational encoding lengths remain polynomial in the input and belief-vector encoding lengths.

3. A polynomial-time necessary test

Call a belief vector a first-transfer witness if
$$ \sum_j\beta_j<\frac12 $$
and, for some class $i$,
$$ \beta_i<M_i,\qquad H_i(\beta)\ge a. $$

These conditions mean that a positive mass of belief-$0$ individuals can move upward directly from truth.

Lemma 3. Every yes-instance has a first-transfer witness.

Proof. If no belief-$0$ type ever moves upward, total opinion-$1$ mass cannot exceed the total belief-$1$ capacity, which is strictly below $\tfrac12$.

Consider the first upward transfer of a belief-$0$ type in a successful sequence. Before that transfer, every belief-$0$ coordinate is zero and every belief-$1$ coordinate is at most its capacity. Hence $x\le\beta$. If class $i$ makes this transfer, monotonicity gives
$$ a\le H_i(x)\le H_i(\beta). $$
Its belief-$0$ capacity is positive, so $\beta_i<M_i$. $\square$

Existence of a first-transfer witness is polynomial-time decidable. For each class $i$, maximize $\varepsilon$ subject to
$$ \begin{aligned} &0\le\beta_j\le M_j &&\text{for every }j,\\ &\sum_j\beta_j\le\frac12-\varepsilon,\\ &\beta_i\le M_i-\varepsilon,\\ &H_i(\beta)\ge a,\\ &0\le\varepsilon\le1. \end{aligned} $$

The optimum is positive exactly when class $i$ has a first-transfer witness. Indeed, a witness supplies a positive common slack for the two strict inequalities, and positive $\varepsilon$ enforces both.

These are rational linear programs of polynomial encoding length. The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore applies.

For arbitrary symmetric $W$, sufficiency of this test is not proved here. The next section establishes sufficiency for a substantial family.

4. Exact characterization for a common off-diagonal weight

Assume $\tau\ge2$ and
$$ W_{ij}=w\ge0\qquad\text{whenever }i\ne j. $$

Define
$$ d_i=W_{ii}-w, \qquad h_i=\min(M_i,1-M_i). $$

Although $d_i$ may be negative, nonnegativity of $W_{ii}$ gives $d_i\ge-w$. Writing $T=\sum_i x_i$, the fields become
$$ H_i(x)=w(2T-1)+d_i(2x_i-M_i). $$

Theorem 4. For this family, the instance is a yes-instance exactly when at least one of the following conditions holds:

  • For some class $i$,

$$ |d_i|h_i>a. $$

  • For some class $i$,

$$ M_i>\frac12,\qquad W_{ii}=0,\qquad w(1-M_i)=a. $$

Thus this family is polynomial-time solvable.

4.1. Necessity

By Lemma 3, a yes-instance has a first-transfer witness in some class $i$. Put
$$ B=\sum_j\beta_j<\frac12,\qquad s=2B-1<0,\qquad y_i=2\beta_i-M_i. $$
At truth,
$$ H_i(\beta)=ws+d_i y_i\ge a, \qquad \beta_i<M_i. $$

If $d_i=0$, then $H_i(\beta)=ws\le0$, which is impossible.

If $d_i>0$, the two strict bounds $\beta_i<M_i$ and $\beta_i<\tfrac12$ imply
$$ y_i<M_i,\qquad y_i<1-M_i. $$
Consequently,
$$ a\le H_i(\beta)<d_i h_i, $$
which gives condition 1.

Suppose instead $d_i=-k_i<0$. Here $k_i>0$ and $w\ge k_i$, so $w>0$.

If $M_i\le\tfrac12$, then $y_i\ge-M_i$ and
$$ H_i(\beta)=ws-k_i y_i \le ws+k_iM_i <k_iM_i=k_i h_i. $$
Again condition 1 follows.

If $M_i>\tfrac12$, put $C_i=1-M_i$. Since the other classes have total capacity $C_i$,
$$ \beta_i\ge B-C_i, \qquad y_i\ge s-C_i. $$
Therefore
$$ H_i(\beta) \le (w-k_i)s+k_iC_i = W_{ii}s+k_iC_i. $$

If $W_{ii}>0$, this is strictly below $k_iC_i$, so condition 1 follows. If $W_{ii}=0$, it gives $k_iC_i\ge a$. A strict inequality gives condition 1; equality gives condition 2 because $k_i=w$.

This proves necessity.

4.2. A monotone completion lemma

The sufficiency constructions use the following observation.

Lemma 5. Suppose a reachable state has all belief-$1$ types entirely at opinion $1$, and every positive-capacity type currently at opinion $1$ weakly prefers that opinion. Suppose also every belief-$0$ type is entirely at one opinion.

Repeatedly transferring the full capacity of an available belief-$0$ type upward reaches an equilibrium, and total opinion-$1$ mass never decreases.

Proof. Every transfer is permitted by its pre-transfer comparison. Fields only increase, so every previously occupied opinion $1$ remains utility maximizing. Each transferred type thereafter weakly prefers opinion $1$.

At termination, every positive-capacity belief-$0$ type left at opinion $0$ has $H_i<a$; otherwise another transfer would be available. Thus every occupied opinion maximizes utility. There are at most $\tau$ transfers. $\square$

4.3. Sufficiency when $d_i<0$

Suppose $d_i=-k_i<0$ and condition 1 holds for class $i$. Write
$$ p=M_i,\qquad C=1-p. $$

Case $p\le\tfrac12$

Here $k_i p>a$. Choose a sufficiently small rational $\varepsilon>0$ satisfying
$$ \varepsilon<\frac p2, \qquad k_i p-2w\varepsilon\ge a. $$

Set
$$ \beta_i=0,\qquad \beta_j=M_j\frac{\tfrac12-\varepsilon}{C} \quad(j\ne i). $$
Because $C\ge\tfrac12$, these beliefs satisfy the capacity bounds. Their total is $\tfrac12-\varepsilon$.

At truth,
$$ H_i(\beta)=k_i p-2w\varepsilon\ge a. $$
Transfer the entire mass $p$ of class $i$ upward. Afterwards,
$$ T=\frac12-\varepsilon+p>\frac12. $$

For every other class,
$$ 2x_j-M_j=M_j\frac{p-2\varepsilon}{C}. $$
This quantity lies between zero and $p-2\varepsilon$. Using $d_j\ge-w$ gives
$$ \begin{aligned} H_j(x) &=2w(p-\varepsilon) +d_jM_j\frac{p-2\varepsilon}{C}\\ &\ge2w(p-\varepsilon)-w(p-2\varepsilon)\\ &=wp>0. \end{aligned} $$
Thus every belief-$1$ type weakly prefers opinion $1$. The transferred belief-$0$ type also does, because its field cannot decrease during the transfer. Lemma 5 supplies a majority equilibrium.

Case $p>\tfrac12$

Now $k_iC>a$. Choose sufficiently small rational $\varepsilon>0$ and set
$$ \beta_j=M_j\quad(j\ne i), \qquad \beta_i=p-\frac12-\varepsilon. $$
Require $\varepsilon<p-\tfrac12$, ensuring $\beta_i\ge0$. The total belief mass is $\tfrac12-\varepsilon$.

At truth,
$$ H_i(\beta)=k_iC-2W_{ii}\varepsilon. $$
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, this is at least $a$. Transfer the belief-$0$ capacity of class $i$ upward.

The resulting state has everyone expressing opinion $1$. The belief-$0$ type of class $i$ weakly prefers opinion $1$ by monotonicity. Every other class contains only belief-$1$ individuals, whose field at the all-$1$ state is nonnegative. Thus this is an equilibrium.

The equality case

Suppose condition 2 holds. Give everyone outside class $i$ belief $1$ and everyone in class $i$ belief $0$:
$$ \beta_i=0,\qquad \beta_j=M_j\quad(j\ne i). $$
Its total belief-$1$ mass is $1-M_i<\tfrac12$. Since $W_{ii}=0$,
$$ H_i(\beta)=w(1-M_i)=a. $$
A tied full transfer of class $i$ is permitted. Afterwards everyone expresses opinion $1$.

The class-$i$ belief-$0$ type remains indifferent because its field is still $a$. All other types have belief $1$ and nonnegative fields. The resulting state is an equilibrium.

4.4. Sufficiency when $d_i>0$

We may assume that no negative-$d_j$ class satisfies either sufficient condition; those cases are already resolved. Consequently, every class with $d_j<0$ satisfies
$$ |d_j|\min(M_j,1-M_j)\le a. $$

For any strict-minority belief vector, $\beta_j\le M_j$ and $\beta_j<\tfrac12$. Hence
$$ d_j(2\beta_j-M_j)\ge-a \qquad\text{whenever }d_j<0. $$

For positive $d_i$, define
$$ t_i=\frac{M_i+a/d_i}{2}. $$
Condition 1 is equivalent to
$$ t_i<M_i,\qquad t_i<\frac12. $$
Call such a positive-$d_i$ class eligible.

An eligible class with mass at least $\tfrac12$

Suppose $M_i\ge\tfrac12$ and class $i$ is eligible. Then
$$ d_i(1-M_i)>a. $$
Choose small rational $\varepsilon>0$ and set
$$ \beta_i=\frac12-\varepsilon,\qquad \beta_j=0\quad(j\ne i). $$
At truth,
$$ H_i(\beta) = d_i(1-M_i)-2(d_i+w)\varepsilon>a $$
for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.

Transfer the belief-$0$ capacity of class $i$ upward. Class $i$ now contributes mass $M_i\ge\tfrac12$. Its occupied types prefer opinion $1$, and there are no belief-$1$ types elsewhere. Lemma 5 completes the state to a majority equilibrium.

All eligible classes have mass below $\tfrac12$

Choose an eligible class $i$ minimizing $t_i$. Put
$$ t=t_i,\qquad r=\frac12-t,\qquad \ell_i=M_i-t>0. $$

For each $j\ne i$, define
$$ \ell_j= \begin{cases} \dfrac{M_j-a/d_j}{2}, &d_j>0\text{ and }d_jM_j>a,\\[6pt] 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
In every case,
$$ 0\le\ell_j<\frac{M_j}{2}. $$

We first show that
$$ \ell_j<r\qquad(j\ne i). $$
Suppose otherwise. Then $\ell_j>0$, and
$$ t_j=M_j-\ell_j\le M_j-r<M_j. $$

If $M_j<\tfrac12$, then
$$ t_j<M_j-r<\frac12-r=t_i. $$
Thus $j$ is eligible and contradicts the minimal choice of $t_i$.

If $M_j\ge\tfrac12$, using $M_j\le1-M_i$ gives
$$ t_j\le M_j-r \le1-M_i-r =\frac12-\ell_i<\frac12. $$
Thus $j$ would be an eligible class of mass at least $\tfrac12$, contrary to the current case.

Next choose a nonempty subset $J$ of classes other than $i$ such that
$$ \sum_{j\in J}M_j\ge r, \qquad \sum_{j\in J}\ell_j<r. $$

Such a subset exists. If some $M_j\ge r$, use that singleton, since $\ell_j<r$. Otherwise all those masses are below $r$. Their total is $1-M_i>\tfrac12>r$, so choose an inclusion-minimal subset with total mass $S\ge r$. Minimality gives $S<2r$, and therefore
$$ \sum_{j\in J}\ell_j<\frac S2<r. $$

Let
$$ S=\sum_{j\in J}M_j,\qquad R=\sum_{j\in J}\ell_j,\qquad \lambda=\frac{r-R}{S-R}. $$
Then $0<\lambda\le1$. Define a balanced belief vector $\gamma$ by
$$ \gamma_i=t,\qquad \gamma_j=\ell_j+\lambda(M_j-\ell_j)\quad(j\in J), $$
and $\gamma_j=0$ otherwise. It satisfies
$$ \sum_j\gamma_j=\frac12, \qquad \gamma_j>\ell_j\quad(j\in J). $$

Choose $k\in J$, set
$$ A=1+\frac{w}{d_i}, $$
and perturb the beliefs by
$$ \beta_i=t+A\varepsilon,\qquad \beta_k=\gamma_k-(A+1)\varepsilon, $$
leaving all other coordinates unchanged.

For sufficiently small rational $\varepsilon>0$, these beliefs satisfy
$$ 0\le\beta_j\le M_j,\qquad \beta_i<M_i,\qquad \beta_k>\ell_k, \qquad \sum_j\beta_j=\frac12-\varepsilon. $$
At truth,
$$ \begin{aligned} H_i(\beta) &=-2w\varepsilon+d_i(2t+2A\varepsilon-M_i)\\ &=a+2d_i\varepsilon>a. \end{aligned} $$
Thus the belief-$0$ capacity of class $i$ can be transferred upward.

After that transfer, total opinion-$1$ mass is
$$ T=\frac12+\ell_i-(A+1)\varepsilon>\frac12 $$
when $\varepsilon$ is sufficiently small.

We verify that every belief-$1$ type prefers opinion $1$ at this state.

For a selected class with $d_j>0$, the inequality $\beta_j>\ell_j$ implies
$$ d_j(2\beta_j-M_j)>-a. $$
This also holds when $\ell_j=0$: positive belief mass gives
$$ d_j(2\beta_j-M_j)>-d_jM_j\ge-a. $$
For $d_j=0$, the diagonal contribution is zero. For $d_j<0$, the previously proved minority bound gives
$$ d_j(2\beta_j-M_j)\ge-a. $$
Since $T>\tfrac12$, adding $w(2T-1)\ge0$ preserves all required inequalities. Classes outside $J\cup\{i\}$ have no belief-$1$ mass. Class $i$ has field at least $a$ by monotonicity.

The transferred belief-$0$ type likewise prefers opinion $1$. Lemma 5 therefore completes this state to a majority equilibrium.

This finishes the proof of Theorem 4.

4.5. Complexity and special cases

The criterion involves only rational comparisons and arithmetic of polynomial encoding length. Recognizing a common off-diagonal entry also takes polynomial time.

All sufficiently small parameters used above can be chosen rationally: each construction imposes finitely many strict affine inequalities that hold for all sufficiently small positive parameters.

For a diagonal matrix, take $w=0$. The equality exception disappears, giving the particularly simple characterization
$$ \boxed{ \text{yes} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists i:\; W_{ii}\min(M_i,1-M_i)> \frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}. } $$

For a constant matrix, every $d_i=0$, so the answer is always no.

Every symmetric matrix with $\tau=2$ has a common off-diagonal entry, namely $w=W_{12}$. Thus Theorem 4 resolves all two-class instances.

For $\tau=1$, necessarily $M_1=1$. At any truthful strict-minority state,
$$ H_1(\beta)=W_{11}(2\beta_1-1)\le0<a. $$
Lemma 3 therefore excludes a yes-instance. Hence all instances with $\tau\le2$ are polynomial-time decidable.

5. General rational certificates and exponential-time decision

The following establishes the general upper bounds without assuming a common off-diagonal weight.

5.1. Patterns and their explicit support constraints

A pattern specifies:

  • For each class, one of the three support alternatives

$$ \beta_i=0,\qquad 0<\beta_i<M_i,\qquad \beta_i=M_i. $$

  • An ordered list of distinct classes whose belief-$0$ types undergo full upward transfers.
  • A terminal bit $s_{ib}$ for each complete type, defining

$$ y_{i0}=s_{i0}(M_i-\beta_i), \qquad y_{i1}=s_{i1}\beta_i. $$

Every declared support alternative is imposed as a constraint. In particular, an interior declaration imposes both
$$ \beta_i>0,\qquad M_i-\beta_i>0. $$
These constraints determine exactly which complete types have positive capacity.

Before a listed class is transferred, its aggregate coordinates are $M_j$ for previously listed classes and $\beta_j$ for all others. Impose
$$ M_i-\beta_i>0,\qquad H_i(x)\ge a $$
for each listed transfer. These are affine inequalities in $\beta$. Let $P$ be the resulting state.

Require
$$ y\le P. $$
For every positive-capacity type, impose
$$ D_{ib}(y)\ge0\quad\text{if }s_{ib}=1, \qquad D_{ib}(y)\le0\quad\text{if }s_{ib}=0. $$
Finally impose
$$ \sum_i\beta_i<\frac12,\qquad \sum_{i,b}y_{ib}\ge\frac12. $$

All constraints are linear because capacities and state coordinates are affine functions of $\beta$.

5.2. Equivalence to the original decision problem

A feasible pattern gives permitted transfers reaching $P$. By Lemma 1,
$$ P\le U. $$
Its candidate $y$ is an equilibrium below $P$, so Lemma 2 gives
$$ y\le E. $$
Thus $E$ is a reachable majority equilibrium.

Conversely, for any yes-instance, take its upward closure $U$ and greatest reachable equilibrium $E$. The construction in Section 2 provides an upward list, while every complete type in $E$ is entirely at one opinion. These choices give a feasible pattern.

Therefore pattern feasibility is equivalent to the original existential problem.

5.3. Strict inequalities and rational witnesses

For a fixed pattern, introduce $0\le\varepsilon\le1$. Replace every strict inequality $f(\beta)>0$ by
$$ f(\beta)\ge\varepsilon, $$
and maximize $\varepsilon$.

This includes all interior-support inequalities, positive transferred capacities, and the strict minority condition.

The original system is feasible exactly when the optimum is positive. A strictly feasible point has a positive minimum slack across the finitely many strict inequalities; conversely, positive common slack enforces all of them.

The program has polynomially many rational coefficients of polynomial encoding length. Its feasible region is closed and bounded. If its optimum is positive, an optimal vertex exists. Such a vertex is determined by a full-rank rational linear system of polynomial dimension. Clearing denominators and applying determinant bounds in Cramer’s rule gives polynomial encoding length.

Consequently, every yes-instance has a polynomial-length rational belief vector. Given that vector, a verifier checks the strict minority condition and runs the fixed-belief algorithm of Section 2. Hence the unrestricted problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

There are $3^\tau$ support choices and $4^\tau$ terminal masks. The number of ordered lists of distinct classes is
$$ \sum_{k=0}^{\tau}\frac{\tau!}{(\tau-k)!} = \tau!\sum_{r=0}^{\tau}\frac1{r!} \le e\,\tau!. $$
Enumerating the patterns and solving their linear programs gives the exact running-time bound
$$ 2^{O(\tau\log(\tau+1))}\operatorname{poly}(L). $$

6. Remaining open question

The new characterization settles common-off-diagonal matrices, including all instances with at most two exposure classes. It does not settle arbitrary symmetric matrices.

A concrete possible route to a polynomial-time solution is to prove that the first-transfer test in Section 3 is sufficient for every symmetric nonnegative $W$. Theorem 4 proves this implication for the common-off-diagonal family. No proof or counterexample for the general implication is established here.

The [cited paper](https://cs.au.dk/~iannis/publ/C85-aamas17.pdf) studies discrete preference games with individual players. No theorem from it is used to infer the complexity of the present real-mass model.
