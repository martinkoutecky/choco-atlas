The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates C, committee size k, rational masses over explicit approval types, and a fixed tie-breaking order, compute mass-GJCR's partial committee, complete it with the k-|W| candidates of largest approval mass, and compute its exact weighted utilitarian ratio against the best size-k committee.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq1\) candidates, let \(1\leq k\leq m\) be an integer, and fix a strict candidate order for all ties. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) distinct approval types \(A_j\subseteq C\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(p_j\), with \(\sum_jp_j=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. State any running times in \(m,\tau,L,k\). Assume at least one supported type is nonempty.
Mass-GJCR starts with \(W=\varnothing\). For each unselected candidate \(c\) and integer \(1\leq\ell\leq k\), define \(s_{c,\ell}(W)=\sum_{j:c\in A_j,\ |A_j\cap W|<\ell}p_j\). A pair is eligible when \(s_{c,\ell}(W)\geq\ell/k\). Repeatedly add the candidate belonging to an eligible pair of maximum mass, breaking candidate ties by the fixed order; stop when no pair is eligible or \(|W|=k\).
Set \(a(c)=\sum_{j:c\in A_j}p_j\). Complete \(W\) to \(\widehat W\) by adding the \(k-|W|\) unselected candidates with largest \(a(c)\), using the same tie order. Define \(U(S)=\sum_jp_j|A_j\cap S|\) and the exact weighted utilitarian ratio \(R=U(\widehat W)/\max_{S\subseteq C,\ |S|=k}U(S)\).
Does mass-GJCR completed by Approval Voting guarantee \(R\geq 2/\sqrt{k}-1/k\) on every such society, or can its worst-case ratio be smaller?
For finite unweighted electorates, GJCR completed by Approval Voting guarantees \(2/\sqrt{k}-1/k\) ([Brill and Peters](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08187)). No result addressing exactly this rational-mass formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total mass to one, so the discrete group-size threshold becomes a mass threshold of \(\ell/k\).
  • Require an integer committee size \(1\leq k\leq m\), making the quota and completion well-defined.
  • Interpret mass-GJCR by maximizing the entire mass of eligible approvers over all eligible candidate-level pairs; voter masses are retained between iterations.
  • Restrict additions to unselected candidates and stop at committee size \(k\), resolving the abbreviated definition's omitted restrictions.
  • Use the fixed strict candidate order for both greedy selection and completion; ties between levels for the same candidate do not affect the committee.
  • Allow empty approval types but require at least one nonempty supported type, ensuring a positive denominator for the ratio.

Result

The guarantee holds: every society in the question satisfies \(R\geq 2/\sqrt{k}-1/k\). Rational masses cannot produce a smaller ratio.

The proof is an exact replication argument, using the finite-electorate guarantee in [Brill and Peters, Theorem 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08187#S4.Thm9). We verify eligibility, maximization, stopping, ties, completion, and welfare scaling.

1. The size cap does not change the greedy rule

First, we prove that the greedy procedure cannot make more than \(k\) selections, even if the explicit size cap is removed.

At selection step \(t\), choose an eligible pair \((c_t,\ell_t)\) attaining the maximum mass, and write \(q_t=s_{c_t,\ell_t}(W_{t-1})\). Define its qualifying types by
\(T_t=\{j:c_t\in A_j,\ |A_j\cap W_{t-1}|<\ell_t\}\).

Every remaining pair’s mass is nonincreasing as the committee grows. Consequently, an ineligible pair cannot become eligible, and the successive selected masses satisfy \(q_1\geq q_2\geq\cdots\).

For bookkeeping, charge type \(j\) an amount \(p_j/q_t\) at step \(t\) when \(j\in T_t\), and zero otherwise. The total charge at each step is
\(\sum_{j\in T_t}p_j/q_t=q_t/q_t=1\).

We prove that each type’s cumulative charge never exceeds \(kp_j\). Suppose \(j\in T_t\), and put \(h=|A_j\cap W_{t-1}|\). This type has previously been charged at most \(h\) times, because each charge requires selecting a candidate it approves. Each previous charge is at most \(p_j/q_t\), by monotonicity of the selected masses. Its cumulative charge after this step is therefore at most
\((h+1)p_j/q_t\leq \ell_t p_j/q_t\leq kp_j\),
where the first inequality uses \(h<\ell_t\), and the second uses eligibility \(q_t\geq\ell_t/k\). Types outside \(T_t\) retain their previous charges, establishing the assertion inductively.

Thus \(t\) selections imply
\(t\leq\sum_jkp_j=k\).
An eligible pair after the \(k\)-th selection would permit one more selection and contradict this inequality. Hence stopping at size \(k\) agrees with stopping when no eligible pair exists.

2. Exact replication preserves every greedy choice

Write each mass as \(p_j=u_j/v_j\), where \(u_j,v_j\) are positive integers. Choose a positive common multiple \(N\) of all denominators, and put \(n_j=Np_j\). Then every \(n_j\) is a positive integer and \(\sum_jn_j=N\).

Construct a finite unweighted electorate with \(n_j\) voters approving exactly \(A_j\). Keep the same candidates, committee size, and candidate tie order.

For any committee \(W\), unselected candidate \(c\), and level \(\ell\), let \(B_{c,\ell}(W)\) consist of all replicated voters who approve \(c\) and have fewer than \(\ell\) approved members in \(W\). Exactly,
\(|B_{c,\ell}(W)|=Ns_{c,\ell}(W)\).
Therefore,
\(s_{c,\ell}(W)\geq\ell/k\) if and only if \(|B_{c,\ell}(W)|\geq\ell N/k\).

Finite GJCR considers groups of voters approving a common candidate whose members all have fewer than \(\ell\) approved committee members. For fixed \(c,\ell,W\), every such group is a subset of \(B_{c,\ell}(W)\). Thus:

  • An eligible group exists precisely when the full group \(B_{c,\ell}(W)\) is eligible.
  • The largest eligible group for this pair is the full group \(B_{c,\ell}(W)\).

Maximizing eligible group size is consequently identical to maximizing eligible mass: every objective value is multiplied by the same positive integer \(N\). Candidate ties are preserved and resolved by the same order. Ties between levels for a single candidate have no effect on the committee.

Starting from the empty committee, induction now shows that both procedures select exactly the same candidate at every step. Section 1 verifies that their stopping conditions also agree. Hence their greedy committees \(W\) coincide.

3. Completion and welfare are preserved

In the replicated electorate, candidate \(c\)’s approval count is \(Na(c)\). Approval Voting completion therefore ranks all remaining candidates exactly as in the mass society, including ties. Both procedures produce the same completed committee \(\widehat W\).

Let \(\operatorname{SW}_N(S)\) denote unweighted utilitarian welfare in the replicated electorate. For every committee \(S\),
\(\operatorname{SW}_N(S)=\sum_j n_j|A_j\cap S|=NU(S)\).
It follows that
\(\max_{|S|=k}\operatorname{SW}_N(S)=N\max_{|S|=k}U(S)\).

The denominator is positive: a nonempty supported type has positive mass, and a candidate it approves can be included in a size-\(k\) committee.

4. Applying the finite-electorate theorem

Brill and Peters’ Theorem 9 states that finite unweighted GJCR completed by Approval Voting guarantees utilitarian ratio at least \(2/\sqrt{k}-1/k\).

The replicated instance satisfies its hypotheses: it has a positive finite number of voters, approval ballots over the same finite candidate set, and \(1\leq k\leq m\). Sections 1–3 identify our output with a permitted output of that rule and preserve its ratio exactly. Therefore,
\(R=U(\widehat W)/\max_{|S|=k}U(S)=\operatorname{SW}_N(\widehat W)/\max_{|S|=k}\operatorname{SW}_N(S)\geq 2/\sqrt{k}-1/k\).

The replication is purely a proof construction; its potentially large electorate introduces no approximation or limiting argument.
