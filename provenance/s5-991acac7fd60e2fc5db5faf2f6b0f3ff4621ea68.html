The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many agent types \(A\) and item classes \(O\), each type \(a\) has rational mass \(\mu_a>0\), an initial class \(o_a\), and a strict preference list of length at most four; the masses sum to \(1\), and all types may trade with one another. A state records \(y_{a,o}\), the mass of type \(a\) holding class \(o\). A legal step chooses types \(a,b\), classes \(o,p\), and rational mass \(q>0\) currently held in those type-class pairs, with \(p\succ_a o\) and \(o\succ_b p\), and exchanges mass \(q\) between the two classes. Given a target type \(a^\star\), target class \(o^\star\), and rational threshold \(0<\varepsilon\leq\mu_{a^\star}\), is there a finite sequence of legal steps after which \(y_{a^\star,o^\star}\geq\varepsilon\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(O\) be a finite set of \(m\) item classes. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types \(A\) with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_a\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type \(a\) has an initial class \(o_a\in O\) and a strict preference order \(\succ_a\) on a subset \(S_a\subseteq O\), with \(o_a\in S_a\) and \(1\leq |S_a|\leq4\). Unlisted classes are unacceptable.
A state consists of nonnegative rational masses \(y_{a,o}\), with \(y_{a,o}=0\) for \(o\notin S_a\), \(\sum_o y_{a,o}=\mu_a\), and \(\sum_a y_{a,o}=\sum_{a:o_a=o}\mu_a\). Initially \(y_{a,o_a}=\mu_a\) and all other entries are zero.
Any distinct types \(a,b\) may trade: choose distinct classes \(o,p\) and rational \(0<q\leq\min\{y_{a,o},y_{b,p}\}\), provided \(p\succ_a o\) and \(o\succ_b p\). Subtract \(q\) from \(y_{a,o},y_{b,p}\), add \(q\) to \(y_{a,p},y_{b,o}\), and leave other entries unchanged.
Given \(a^\star\in A\), \(o^\star\in O\), and binary rational \(0<\varepsilon\leq\mu_{a^\star}\), reachability means that some finite sequence of legal trades, possibly empty, yields \(y_{a^\star,o^\star}\geq\varepsilon\).

Is this reachability problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For indivisible objects, one initially held by each agent, REACHABLE OBJECT is NP-complete when every pair may trade and preference lists have length at most four; lists of length at most three admit a linear-time algorithm. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.04219)
No result settling exactly the mass reachability problem defined above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified that each preference list orders an acceptable subset, includes the initial class, and counts that class toward the bound of four; unlisted classes cannot be held or received.
  • Made the initial state explicit and fixed each class's inventory to its initially endowed mass; no separate supplies are input.
  • Specified rational nonnegative state entries, conservation constraints, the available-mass bound on trades, and the exact four-entry update.
  • Allowed an empty sequence, as in the discrete reachability definition.
  • Defined m as the number of item classes and L as the total input bit length, including the threshold.

Result

The problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). It is an instance of rational continuous Petri-net coverability, for which a polynomial-time algorithm is known.

If \(o^\star\notin S_{a^\star}\), the answer is immediately no. If \(o^\star=o_{a^\star}\), the initial state already satisfies the target. The proof below handles the remaining cases.

1. The applicable theorem

A continuous Petri net has a finite set \(P\) of places and nonnegative integer matrices \(\mathrm{Pre},\mathrm{Post}\), with one column per transition. Its marking is a vector \(z\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{P}\).

A transition \(t\) may fire by a positive rational amount \(q\) when, coordinatewise,

\(z\geq q\,\mathrm{Pre}_{\cdot,t}\).

The resulting marking is

\(z'=z+q(\mathrm{Post}_{\cdot,t}-\mathrm{Pre}_{\cdot,t})\).

Known theorem. Given such a net and binary rational markings \(z^0,h\), one can decide in polynomial time whether some finite sequence of rational firings reaches a marking \(z\geq h\). This is rational continuous coverability. The theorem is stated explicitly in Proposition 2.4 of Blondin, Finkel, Haase and Haddad, *The Logical View on Continuous Petri Nets*, attributing the result to Fraca and Haddad. [Source, Section 2.3](https://michaelblondin.com/papers/BFHH17.pdf).

Thus the theorem concerns precisely finite rational executions, including exact attainment of a threshold.

2. Constructing the net

Create one place for each acceptable type–class pair:

\(P=\{(a,o):a\in A,\ o\in S_a\}\).

The initial marking is

\(z^0_{a,o}=\begin{cases}\mu_a,&o=o_a,\\0,&o\neq o_a.\end{cases}\)

For every unordered pair of distinct types, fix an ordering \(a,b\). For each ordered pair of distinct classes \(o,p\) satisfying

\(o,p\in S_a\cap S_b,\qquad p\succ_a o,\qquad o\succ_b p,\)

create a transition \(t=(a,b,o,p)\).

Its input column has coefficient \(1\) at \((a,o)\) and \((b,p)\), and zero elsewhere. Its output column has coefficient \(1\) at \((a,p)\) and \((b,o)\), and zero elsewhere.

These four places are distinct because \(a\neq b\) and \(o\neq p\).

Consequently, firing this transition by amount \(q>0\) is permitted exactly when

\(q\leq\min\{z_{a,o},z_{b,p}\}\),

and makes exactly the four changes specified by the corresponding legal trade.

3. Exact correspondence with the society

Claim. The markings reachable from \(z^0\) by finite rational firing sequences are exactly the society states reachable by finite legal trade sequences, after omitting unacceptable coordinates.

Proof. Every legal trade has a transition in the construction. Its enabling condition and coordinate changes agree with the trade definition, so every trade sequence produces the same marking through the corresponding firing sequence.

Conversely, every constructed transition satisfies both strict preference conditions. Its firing amount satisfies the required availability bound. It therefore represents a legal trade, provided its current marking represents a society state.

That last condition follows inductively from conservation. A firing removes and adds the same amount within each participating type, so for every type \(c\),

\(\sum_{r\in S_c}z_{c,r}=\mu_c\).

It also removes and adds the same amount within each exchanged class, so for every class \(r\),

\(\sum_{c:r\in S_c}z_{c,r}=\sum_{c:o_c=r}\mu_c\).

Both identities hold initially and remain true after every firing. Nonnegativity follows from the enabling condition, rationality follows from rational firing amounts, and unacceptable coordinates are identically zero because they have no places.

Thus every reachable marking represents a valid society state, and every firing sequence translates into a legal trade sequence. The empty sequences correspond as well. This proves the claim.

4. Encoding the threshold

Assume \(o^\star\in S_{a^\star}\). Define the rational target marking \(h\) by

\(h_{a^\star,o^\star}=\varepsilon,\qquad h_{a,o}=0\ \text{for all other places}.\)

Since every marking is nonnegative,

\(z\geq h\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z_{a^\star,o^\star}\geq\varepsilon.\)

Together with the correspondence proved above, this gives an exact equivalence between the original reachability question and rational continuous coverability of \(h\) from \(z^0\).

In particular, the reduction preserves the non-strict threshold even when \(\varepsilon=\mu_{a^\star}\).

5. Polynomial size and conclusion

There are at most \(4\tau\) places. For each unordered pair of types, at most \(16\) ordered class pairs need to be examined. Hence there are at most \(16\binom{\tau}{2}\) transitions.

Every matrix entry is \(0\) or \(1\). The nonzero initial and target coordinates are copied directly from the input masses and \(\varepsilon\). Therefore the constructed instance has binary encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and can be constructed in polynomial time. Only supported types are used.

Applying the cited coverability algorithm proves the claimed polynomial-time upper bound.

The same construction also works for arbitrary explicitly listed preference lengths: it then has at most \(m\tau\) places and \(m^2\binom{\tau}{2}\) transitions. Thus the length-four restriction is unnecessary for this upper bound. The cited hardness for indivisible objects does not transfer to this divisible model.
