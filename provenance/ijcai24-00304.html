The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau\) summing to \(1\) and rational linear trajectory types \(v^j(t)=a_jt+b_j\) on \([0,1]\), compute a linear curve \(W(t)=at+b\) with \(0\le b\le1\) and \(0\le a+b\le1\) minimizing \(\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j\max_{t\in[0,1]}|v^j(t)-W(t)|\).

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) distinct trajectory types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_j\), where \(\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type is \(v^j(t)=a_jt+b_j\) on \([0,1]\), with binary rational coefficients satisfying \(0\le b_j\le1\) and \(0\le a_j+b_j\le1\); slopes may be negative.

Exact polynomial-time solution

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. An exact minimizer can be computed using two weighted medians and clipping, in \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) arithmetic operations and \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2)\) bit operations with elementary integer arithmetic.

The equal-weight case appears as Theorem 5 of the [cited paper](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0304.pdf). The following proof handles binary rational masses directly.

1. Reduce each trajectory to its endpoints

Define the input endpoints by \(x_j=b_j\) and \(y_j=a_j+b_j\). By hypothesis, \((x_j,y_j)\in[0,1]^2\).

Similarly, write the output endpoints as \(x=b\) and \(y=a+b\). Every feasible output corresponds uniquely to \((x,y)\in[0,1]^2\), through \(b=x\) and \(a=y-x\).

For any \(t\in[0,1]\),

\( v^j(t)-W(t)=(1-t)(x_j-x)+t(y_j-y). \)

Consequently, the triangle inequality gives

\( |v^j(t)-W(t)| \le (1-t)|x_j-x|+t|y_j-y| \le \max\{|x_j-x|,|y_j-y|\}. \)

Both endpoint deviations occur in the maximization. Hence

\( \max_{t\in[0,1]}|v^j(t)-W(t)| =\max\{|x_j-x|,|y_j-y|\}. \)

Thus the objective is

\( F(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j\max\{|x-x_j|,|y-y_j|\}. \)

This reduction does not require nonnegative slopes.

2. Separate the unconstrained objective

For all real numbers \(r,s\),

\( \max\{|r|,|s|\}=\frac{|r+s|+|r-s|}{2}. \)

Indeed, if \(|r|\ge |s|\), then \(r+s\) and \(r-s\) have the same weak sign, so their absolute values sum to \(2|r|\). The other case follows by interchanging \(r\) and \(s\).

Introduce coordinates

\( u=x+y,\qquad z=x-y, \)

and corresponding input coordinates

\( u_j=x_j+y_j=a_j+2b_j,\qquad z_j=x_j-y_j=-a_j. \)

The transformation is invertible, with

\( x=\frac{u+z}{2},\qquad y=\frac{u-z}{2}. \)

Applying the identity to each objective term gives

\( F(x,y)=\frac12\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j|u-u_j| +\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j|z-z_j|\right). \)

Therefore, over all of \(\mathbb R^2\), the two coordinates can be optimized independently.

3. Weighted medians minimize absolute deviation

For numbers \(c_1,\ldots,c_\tau\) with positive masses summing to one, call \(m\) a weighted median if

\( \sum_{c_j<m}\mu_j\le\frac12 \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{c_j>m}\mu_j\le\frac12. \)

Such a median can be obtained by sorting the entries and choosing the first value at which cumulative mass reaches or exceeds \(1/2\). The mass strictly below that value is less than \(1/2\), and the mass strictly above it is at most \(1/2\), including when entries are tied.

We prove that \(m\) minimizes \(H(q)=\sum_j\mu_j|q-c_j|\).

Suppose first that \(q>m\), and put \(d=q-m\). For every \(c_j\le m\),

\( |q-c_j|-|m-c_j|=d. \)

For every other entry, the reverse triangle inequality gives a difference at least \(-d\). Writing \(A=\sum_{c_j\le m}\mu_j\), the median condition implies \(A\ge1/2\), and therefore

\( H(q)-H(m)\ge Ad-(1-A)d=(2A-1)d\ge0. \)

If \(q<m\), apply the same argument to the entries with \(c_j\ge m\), whose total mass is at least \(1/2\). Equality is immediate when \(q=m\). This proves the claim.

Choose weighted medians \(u^\star\) of the \(u_j\) and \(z^\star\) of the \(z_j\), using the given masses. By Section 2,

\( x^\star=\frac{u^\star+z^\star}{2},\qquad y^\star=\frac{u^\star-z^\star}{2} \)

is a global minimizer of \(F\) over \(\mathbb R^2\).

4. Enforce feasibility without increasing cost

The preceding minimizer may lie outside the feasible square. Define

\( C(r)=\min\{1,\max\{0,r\}\}. \)

For every \(c\in[0,1]\), one has \(|C(r)-c|\le|r-c|\): this is equality when \(r\in[0,1]\); when \(r<0\), it follows from \(c\le c-r\); and when \(r>1\), from \(1-c\le r-c\).

Since every input endpoint belongs to \([0,1]\), this implies, for each type,

\( \max\{|C(x^\star)-x_j|,|C(y^\star)-y_j|\} \le \max\{|x^\star-x_j|,|y^\star-y_j|\}. \)

Set \(\widehat x=C(x^\star)\) and \(\widehat y=C(y^\star)\). Then \((\widehat x,\widehat y)\) is feasible and \(F(\widehat x,\widehat y)\le F(x^\star,y^\star)\). Global optimality of \((x^\star,y^\star)\) gives the reverse inequality, so equality holds.

An exact feasible minimizer is therefore

\( \boxed{W(t)=(\widehat y-\widehat x)t+\widehat x}. \)

Here the clipping applies to the two endpoint values; the returned trajectory is their linear interpolation.

5. Exact implementation and running time

Write each mass as \(\mu_j=p_j/q_j\), where \(p_j,q_j\) are positive binary integers. Compute

\( D=\prod_{j=1}^{\tau}q_j,\qquad M_j=p_j(D/q_j). \)

These are exact integers satisfying \(M_j>0\) and \(\sum_jM_j=D\). Weighted medians can thus be found by sorting the transformed coordinates and choosing the first entry for which the cumulative integer weight \(S\) satisfies \(2S\ge D\). No voters need to be replicated.

The complete algorithm is:

  • Compute the integer weights \(M_j\) and transformed coordinates \(u_j=a_j+2b_j\), \(z_j=-a_j\).
  • Sort each coordinate list and find its weighted median.
  • Invert the coordinate transformation, clip both endpoints, and return the linear interpolation.

Computing the weights takes \(O(\tau)\) integer arithmetic operations. The two sorts take \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational comparisons, and the remaining scans take \(O(\tau)\) operations.

For the bit bound, the binary length of \(D\) is at most a constant plus the sum of the input denominator lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Because \(M_j=D\mu_j\le D\), all weights and cumulative sums also have \(O(L)\) bits. Each transformed coordinate and each output coefficient uses only constantly many rational operations on input coordinates or selected medians, so their encodings likewise have \(O(L)\) bits.

Using elementary multiplication and division, each required arithmetic operation or rational comparison costs \(O(L^2)\) bit operations. Thus a valid total bound is

\( \boxed{O\!\left(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2\right)} \)

bit operations, with an exact rational output of length \(O(L)\). The bound depends only on the support size and total input length.
