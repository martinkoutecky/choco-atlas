The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\) with rational population shares \(\mu_t\), \(k\ge3\) advertisers, and rational demand reports \(b_i\) with \(\sum_i b_i\le1\), is there a polynomial type-oracle direct mechanism over allocations \(x_{i,t}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_i x_{i,t}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{i,t}=b_i\), which is strategyproof for advertisers with true capacities \(\tilde b_i\) and achieves welfare at least \((e-1)/e\) times the optimal feasible welfare? The oracle specifies diffusion utilities \(F_i(x)\) under continuous counterparts of the paper’s submodularity, adverse-competition, MeI, and AgI assumptions.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(m=\tau\) counts available type coordinates; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(k\ge3\) advertisers. Reports \(b_i\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) satisfy \(\sum_i b_i\le1\). Put \(D=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{k\times\tau}:\sum_i x_{i,t}\le\mu_t\}\) and \(D(b)=\{x\in D:\sum_t x_{i,t}=b_i\text{ for every }i\}\).
Utilities \(F_i:D\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) are continuous, vanish whenever \(x_i=0\), are nondecreasing in their own allocation vector, and are nonincreasing in each competitor's allocation vector, wherever the compared allocations belong to \(D\).
Mechanism indifference means \(W(x):=\sum_iF_i(x)=G(\sum_i x_i)\); agent indifference means \(F_i(x)=H_i(x_i,\sum_{j\ne i}x_j)\).
On \([0,\mu]\), \(G\) is nondecreasing and DR-submodular: \(G(y+\delta e_t)-G(y)\ge G(z+\delta e_t)-G(z)\) whenever \(y\le z\), \(\delta\ge0\), and both increments stay in the box; \(e_t\) is the coordinate unit vector.
An exact value oracle returns all \(F_i(x)\) at rational \(x\in D\), with rational answers of polynomial bit length in the query length and input length \(L\). Count oracle calls and bit operations; mechanisms return rational allocations and may randomize.
An advertiser's true capacity is \(\tilde b_i\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\): its utility is \(F_i(x)\) when \(\sum_t x_{i,t}\le\tilde b_i\), and \(-\infty\) otherwise. There are no transfers. Strategyproofness requires \(\mathbb E F_i(M(r,b_{-i}))\le\mathbb E F_i(M(\tilde b_i,b_{-i}))\) for every \(0\le r\le\tilde b_i\) and every feasible truthful comparison profile.

Does there exist a strategyproof direct mechanism, running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L)\) time, that returns an allocation in \(D(b)\) almost surely and guarantees \(\mathbb E W(M(b))\ge\frac{e-1}{e}\max_{x\in D(b)}W(x)\) for every admissible instance and report profile, or is this approximation guarantee impossible in this polynomial-time oracle model?

For finite seed sets, a polynomial-time randomized strategyproof mechanism achieves welfare at least \(\frac{e-1}{e}\) of optimum under mechanism indifference, agent indifference, and adverse competition with \(k\ge3\), including disjoint demand-satisfying allocations ([source](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/Papers/strategy-proof-influence-journal.pdf)).

Partial result: a polynomial-time strategyproof guarantee arbitrarily close to the target

The exact existence-or-impossibility question remains unresolved. I prove the following uniform improvement over the fixed-support enumeration in the notes.

Theorem. For every rational accuracy parameter $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, fixed independently of the reports, there is a deterministic strategyproof mechanism that returns a rational allocation in $D(b)$ and satisfies
$$ W(M_\varepsilon(b)) \ge \left(1-e^{-1}-\varepsilon\right) \max_{x\in D(b)}W(x). $$
Its oracle calls and bit operations are polynomial in $\tau,k,L$, the encoding length of $\varepsilon$, and $\varepsilon^{-1}$.

The construction needs neither differentiability nor a supplied modulus of continuity. Its main new ingredient is a monotonicity-preserving wrapper for aggregate approximation algorithms.

1. Reduction to aggregate welfare

Write
$$ B=\sum_i b_i, \qquad K_B=\left\{y\in[0,\mu]:\sum_t y_t=B\right\}. $$

Every allocation in $D(b)$ has aggregate in $K_B$. Conversely, for $B>0$ and $y\in K_B$, the allocation
$$ x_{i,t}=\frac{b_i}{B}y_t $$
belongs to $D(b)$: its column sums are $y_t\le\mu_t$, and its row sums are $b_i$. At $B=0$, the unique feasible allocation is zero.

The set $K_B$ is nonempty because $B\mu\in K_B$, and it is compact. Assigning all of $y$ to advertiser $1$ shows that
$$ G(y)=F_1(y,0,\ldots,0), $$
because all other utilities vanish. Thus $G$ is continuous, nonnegative, and satisfies $G(0)=0$. Consequently,
$$ \operatorname{OPT}(B):=\max_{y\in K_B}G(y) = \max_{x\in D(b)}W(x). $$

At every rational $y\in[0,\mu]$, one oracle call evaluates $G(y)$ by assigning all of $y$ to advertiser $1$. Such queries are permitted because the oracle domain is $D$, rather than only $D(b)$.

2. Fixed-aggregate linearity and strategyproofness

I first prove the structural fact needed to turn monotone aggregate welfare into strategyproofness.

Fix $y\in[0,\mu]$. For advertiser $i$ and $a\in[0,y]$, define
$$ \phi_i^y(a)=H_i(a,y-a). $$
This is realized by assigning $a$ to advertiser $i$ and $y-a$ to one competitor. Hence $\phi_i^y$ is continuous, and $\phi_i^y(0)=0$.

For every partition $a_1+\cdots+a_k=y$, the two indifference assumptions imply
$$ \sum_i\phi_i^y(a_i)=G(y). $$

For distinct advertisers $i,j$, choose a third advertiser $\ell$, using $k\ge3$. Compare the partitions assigning $a$ to $i$ or to $j$, and $y-a$ to $\ell$. They give
$$ \phi_i^y(a)+\phi_\ell^y(y-a) = G(y) = \phi_j^y(a)+\phi_\ell^y(y-a). $$
Thus the functions $\phi_i^y$ coincide; denote their common value by $\phi^y$.

If $a,b\ge0$ and $a+b\le y$, compare a partition among three advertisers with the partition obtained by combining $a$ and $b$. This gives
$$ \phi^y(a)+\phi^y(b)+\phi^y(y-a-b) = \phi^y(a+b)+\phi^y(y-a-b), $$
and therefore
$$ \phi^y(a+b)=\phi^y(a)+\phi^y(b). $$

For each coordinate with $y_t>0$, set
$$ p_t(y)=\frac{\phi^y(y_te_t)}{y_t}. $$
Restricted additivity gives
$$ \phi^y(qy_te_t)=q\phi^y(y_te_t) $$
for rational $q\in[0,1]$: subdivide the coordinate segment into equal pieces and add them. Continuity extends this equality to real $q\in[0,1]$. Decomposing any $a\in[0,y]$ into its coordinate components now yields
$$ \phi^y(a)=\sum_t p_t(y)a_t. $$
For $y_t=0$, set $p_t(y)=0$; every admissible $a$ then has $a_t=0$. Nonnegativity of utilities implies $p_t(y)\ge0$.

Consequently, for every allocation with aggregate $y$,
$$ F_i(x)=\sum_t p_t(y)x_{i,t}, \qquad G(y)=\sum_t p_t(y)y_t. $$
In particular, proportional allocations satisfy
$$ F_i\!\left(\left(\frac{b_j}{B}y\right)_j\right) = \frac{b_i}{B}G(y). $$

Now suppose an aggregate algorithm depends on reports only through $B$, returns $Y_B\in K_B$, and has nondecreasing achieved welfare
$$ V(B)=G(Y_B). $$
Allocate proportionally.

To check strategyproofness, fix advertiser $i$ and put
$$ c=\sum_{j\ne i}b_j. $$
At a positive report $r$, its utility is
$$ \frac{r}{r+c}V(r+c). $$
Both factors are nonnegative and nondecreasing in $r$, so their product is nondecreasing. At report zero, its utility is zero. Exact row sums also ensure that every permitted underreport stays within true capacity.

Thus nondecreasing achieved aggregate welfare is sufficient for strategyproofness of this proportional mechanism.

3. A scaling consequence of DR-submodularity

We will use
$$ G(\alpha y)\ge\alpha G(y) \qquad \text{for }y\in[0,\mu],\quad \alpha\in[0,1]. $$
Here is a proof under precisely the stated DR assumption.

First, coordinatewise DR extends to nonnegative vector increments. If $u\le v$, $h\ge0$, and $u+h,v+h$ lie in the box, telescope both increments coordinate by coordinate. At each step the two intermediate bases remain ordered, so the stated DR inequality applies. Summing gives
$$ G(u+h)-G(u)\ge G(v+h)-G(v). $$

Fix $y$ and define $g(s)=G(sy)$ on $[0,1]$. Applying the vector inequality with
$$ u=ay,\qquad v=\frac{a+b}{2}y,\qquad h=\frac{b-a}{2}y $$
shows that, for $0\le a\le b\le1$,
$$ g\!\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right) \ge\frac{g(a)+g(b)}2. $$
Repeated midpoint interpolation proves the concavity inequality for dyadic interpolation parameters. Continuity then proves it for every interpolation parameter. Hence $g$ is concave. Since $g(0)=0$,
$$ G(\alpha y)=g(\alpha)\ge\alpha g(1)=\alpha G(y). $$

An immediate consequence is that, whenever $0<s\le B$,
$$ \operatorname{OPT}(s)\ge\frac{s}{B}\operatorname{OPT}(B). $$
Indeed, scaling an optimizer in $K_B$ by $s/B$ produces a point in $K_s$.

4. A polynomial-time aggregate approximation algorithm

Fix an integer $N>\tau$ and a rational budget $s\in(0,1]$. I construct a deterministic algorithm $A_N(s)$ satisfying
$$ A_N(s)\in K_s, \qquad G(A_N(s)) \ge \beta_N\left(1-\frac{\tau}{N}\right)\operatorname{OPT}(s), $$
where
$$ \beta_N=1-\left(1-\frac1N\right)^N\ge1-e^{-1}. $$

4.1. The clipped integer lattice

Set
$$ \delta=\frac{s}{N}, \qquad c_t=\left\lceil\frac{\mu_t}{\delta}\right\rceil, \qquad \psi_t(j)=\min\{\delta j,\mu_t\}. $$
For integer vectors $0\le z\le c$, define
$$ f(z)=G(\psi(z)). $$

This function is nondecreasing and satisfies integer diminishing returns. To verify the latter, take $z\le z'$ with both coordinate increments permitted. Write
$$ u=\psi(z),\qquad u'=\psi(z'), $$
and
$$ d=\psi_t(z_t+1)-\psi_t(z_t), \qquad d'=\psi_t(z'_t+1)-\psi_t(z'_t). $$
The increments of $\psi_t$ consist of full increments $\delta$, followed possibly by one smaller final increment. Thus $d\ge d'\ge0$. Monotonicity and DR-submodularity of $G$ give
$$ \begin{aligned} f(z+e_t)-f(z) &=G(u+de_t)-G(u)\\ &\ge G(u+d'e_t)-G(u)\\ &\ge G(u'+d'e_t)-G(u')\\ &=f(z'+e_t)-f(z'). \end{aligned} $$
Every point in these comparisons lies in the box.

4.2. Greedy optimization on the lattice

Start at $z^0=0$. At each of $N$ steps, increment a coordinate maximizing
$$ f(z^h+e_t)-f(z^h) $$
among coordinates with $z_t^h<c_t$, using fixed tie-breaking.

An available coordinate always exists before step $N$, because
$$ \sum_t c_t\ge\frac{1}{\delta}=\frac{N}{s}\ge N. $$

Let
$$ f^\star= \max\left\{f(z):0\le z\le c,\ z\text{ integer},\ \sum_tz_t\le N\right\}, $$
and let $z^\star$ attain this finite maximum.

At a greedy state $z$, put $w=\max\{z,z^\star\}$ coordinatewise. There are at most $N$ unit increments from $z$ to $w$, because
$$ \sum_t(w_t-z_t)\le\sum_tz_t^\star\le N. $$
Integer diminishing returns bounds every such increment by the largest available marginal at $z$, denoted $\Delta(z)$. Monotonicity therefore gives
$$ f^\star-f(z) \le f(w)-f(z) \le N\Delta(z). $$
The greedy update consequently satisfies
$$ f(z^{h+1}) \ge f(z^h)+\frac{f^\star-f(z^h)}{N}. $$
Since $f(0)=0$, induction gives
$$ f(z^N)\ge\beta_N f^\star. $$
The elementary inequality $1-u\le e^{-u}$ gives $\beta_N\ge1-e^{-1}$.

4.3. Comparison with the continuous optimum

Let $y^\star\in K_s$ maximize $G$, and put
$$ \alpha=1-\frac{\tau}{N}. $$
For the approximation analysis only, define
$$ \widehat z_t= \left\lceil\frac{\alpha y_t^\star}{\delta}\right\rceil. $$
Because $\alpha y_t^\star\le\mu_t$, we have $\widehat z_t\le c_t$. Furthermore,
$$ \sum_t\widehat z_t \le \frac{\alpha s}{\delta}+\tau = \alpha N+\tau = N. $$
Thus $\widehat z$ is feasible for $f^\star$. Coordinatewise,
$$ \psi_t(\widehat z_t)\ge\alpha y_t^\star. $$
Monotonicity and the scaling inequality imply
$$ f^\star \ge f(\widehat z) \ge G(\alpha y^\star) \ge\alpha\operatorname{OPT}(s). $$
The optimizer and its rounded coordinates are used only in this proof; the algorithm does not compute them.

The raw output $\psi(z^N)$ has total mass at most $N\delta=s$. Fill the remaining mass to reach $s$, without decreasing any coordinate, using the rational water-filling operation defined next. The resulting vector $A_N(s)$ belongs to $K_s$ and has welfare at least $f(z^N)$.

The greedy procedure uses at most $\tau N$ oracle calls. It stores coordinate counts rather than expanding the possibly large capacities $c_t$ into individual items.

5. Water-filling paths that eventually coincide

For any rational $a\in[0,\mu]$, write $s=\sum_ta_t$. For $B\in[s,1]$, define its extension $E(a,B)$ by
$$ E_t(a,B)=\max\{a_t,\min\{\lambda,\mu_t\}\}, $$
where $\lambda\ge0$ is chosen so that
$$ \sum_t E_t(a,B)=B. $$

Such a $\lambda$ exists: the sum is a continuous nondecreasing function of $\lambda$, ranging from $s$ to $1$. The resulting vector is unique. Indeed, if two choices of $\lambda$ give the same sum, their coordinate vectors are ordered; equal sums then force equality in every coordinate.

This operation has the following properties.

  • It gives $E(a,B)\in K_B$ and $E(a,B)\ge a$.
  • For fixed $a$, it is coordinatewise nondecreasing in $B$.
  • It is rational and computable in polynomial time.

For the second property, choose the least admissible $\lambda$. This choice is nondecreasing with $B$, as are all the coordinates. For the third, sort the breakpoints $a_t,\mu_t$. Between consecutive breakpoints, the sum is affine in $\lambda$ with an integer slope. Solving the appropriate affine equation uses rational arithmetic of polynomial bit complexity.

Define the baseline path
$$ w(B)=E(0,B). $$

The crucial property is eventual coincidence:
$$ B\ge\tau s \quad\Longrightarrow\quad E(a,B)=w(B). $$

To prove it, let $\lambda_0$ be a water level representing $w(B)$. Since
$$ B=\sum_t\min\{\lambda_0,\mu_t\}\le\tau\lambda_0, $$
we have
$$ \lambda_0\ge\frac B\tau\ge s\ge a_t $$
for every $t$. Also $a_t\le\mu_t$. Hence
$$ \max\{a_t,\min\{\lambda_0,\mu_t\}\} = \min\{\lambda_0,\mu_t\}. $$
The same water level therefore produces the baseline vector from the lower bounds $a$, proving the claim.

This property lets us discard sufficiently small-budget candidates exactly, rather than merely approximately.

6. A polynomial-time monotonicity wrapper

Here is the general wrapper.

Suppose a deterministic algorithm $A$ returns a rational vector $A(s)\in K_s$ for every rational $s\in(0,1]$, and satisfies
$$ G(A(s))\ge\rho\operatorname{OPT}(s). $$
Its definition, including tie-breaking, must be independent of the current report profile.

Fix a rational $q>1$, and define the report-independent budgets
$$ s_j=q^{-j}, \qquad j=0,1,2,\ldots. $$
Write $a_j=A(s_j)$.

At a reported total budget $B>0$, evaluate the baseline $w(B)$ and the candidates
$$ E(a_j,B) \qquad \text{for indices satisfying } \frac B\tau<s_j\le B. $$
Return a candidate $Y_B$ with maximum welfare, with fixed tie-breaking. At $B=0$, return zero.

6.1. Monotonicity of achieved welfare

Conceptually, every index with $s_j\le B$ supplies an extension at budget $B$. Every omitted index satisfies $s_j\le B/\tau$, and therefore
$$ E(a_j,B)=w(B). $$
Thus the finite candidate list represents all those extensions exactly.

Let $0<B_1\le B_2$. If the selected candidate at $B_1$ is the baseline, its welfare cannot decrease along the baseline path. Otherwise it is an extension from some $s_j\le B_1$. That same extension is defined at $B_2$ and is coordinatewise nondecreasing. At $B_2$, either it is explicitly considered or it has become the baseline.

In either case,
$$ G(Y_{B_2})\ge G(Y_{B_1}). $$
The comparison from $B_1=0$ follows from nonnegativity. Hence the wrapper has nondecreasing achieved welfare on all rational budgets.

6.2. Approximation ratio

For $B>0$, choose the largest $s_j$ not exceeding $B$. Then
$$ \frac Bq\le s_j\le B. $$
Its extension is represented in the candidate list, either explicitly or by the baseline. Therefore
$$ \begin{aligned} G(Y_B) &\ge G(E(a_j,B))\\ &\ge G(a_j)\\ &\ge\rho\operatorname{OPT}(s_j)\\ &\ge\rho\frac{s_j}{B}\operatorname{OPT}(B)\\ &\ge\frac{\rho}{q}\operatorname{OPT}(B). \end{aligned} $$

Thus the wrapper turns a $\rho$-approximation into a monotone $\rho/q$-approximation.

6.3. Polynomial implementation

For $\tau=1$, the baseline is the unique feasible aggregate, so the problem is solved exactly. Assume $\tau\ge2$.

The number of explicitly evaluated indices is at most
$$ 1+\frac{\log\tau}{\log q}. $$
Indeed, their budgets lie in an interval with multiplicative width $\tau$.

The infinite definition introduces no computational dependence on infinitely small budgets. Starting at $s_0=1$, exact rational multiplication by $q^{-1}$ locates the first budget not exceeding $B$, after which only the above finite interval is processed.

All candidates are defined independently of reports. In particular, increasing the report changes which candidates are evaluated, but does not change the allocation associated with a fixed index.

7. The resulting strategyproof mechanism and its complexity

Fix rational $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, and set
$$ \eta=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}, \qquad q=1+\eta, \qquad N=\left\lceil\frac{\tau}{\eta}\right\rceil. $$
Use $A_N$ from Section 4 inside the wrapper.

Its aggregate approximation ratio is at least
$$ \frac{\beta_N(1-\tau/N)}{1+\eta} \ge (1-e^{-1})\frac{1-\eta}{1+\eta}. $$
Writing $a=1-e^{-1}$ and using $0<a<1$,
$$ a\frac{1-\eta}{1+\eta} = a-\frac{2a\eta}{1+\eta} \ge a-2\eta = 1-e^{-1}-\varepsilon. $$

Return
$$ x_{i,t}= \begin{cases} \displaystyle\frac{b_i}{B}Y_{B,t},&B>0,\\[6pt] 0,&B=0. \end{cases} $$
This allocation is rational and belongs to $D(b)$. The wrapper makes $G(Y_B)$ nondecreasing, so Section 2 proves strategyproofness. Mechanism indifference gives
$$ W(x)=G(Y_B), $$
which proves the announced welfare guarantee.

For $\tau\ge2$, the number of oracle calls is
$$ O\!\left(\frac{\tau^2\log\tau}{\varepsilon^2}\right). $$
Indeed, $N=O(\tau/\varepsilon)$, each aggregate computation uses at most $\tau N$ calls, and there are $O(\log\tau/\varepsilon)$ candidates.

For completeness, the bit complexity is also polynomial. A positive rational total budget encoded by the input satisfies $B\ge2^{-O(L)}$. Since
$$ \log(1+\eta)\ge\frac{\eta}{2} \qquad \text{for }0<\eta<1, $$
all required indices are
$$ O\!\left(\frac{L+\log\tau}{\eta}\right). $$
The numerator and denominator lengths of $s_j=q^{-j}$ grow at most linearly in $j$ times the encoding length of $q$. All grid queries, water-filling operations, and proportional outputs therefore have polynomial encoding length. The promised polynomial answer lengths permit exact comparisons in polynomial time.

No discretization parameter is chosen from the report’s encoding length. The accuracy parameter is fixed across report comparisons, and the infinite family of reference budgets is fixed mathematically; only its exactly redundant portion is omitted during computation.

8. What remains open

The proved result substantially narrows the gap: the continuous formulation admits polynomial-time, exactly demand-satisfying, strategyproof mechanisms with ratios arbitrarily close to $1-e^{-1}$.

It does not settle the exact target. For positive $\varepsilon$, the established guarantee retains a discretization and budget-spacing loss. Sending $\varepsilon$ to zero yields a family of increasingly expensive mechanisms, rather than a single polynomial-time mechanism attaining the exact factor. Nor does this construction imply an oracle lower bound excluding that factor.
