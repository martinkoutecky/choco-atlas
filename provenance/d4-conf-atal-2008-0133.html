The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite rational value set \(T\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\), and \(q\in(0,1)\), assume some \(\lambda\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\) satisfies \(\mu(>\lambda)<q<\mu(\ge\lambda)\). For finite populations with empirical value distributions converging to \(\mu\) and numbers of indivisible slots \(m_N\) satisfying \(m_N/N\to q\), what is the greatest common limiting per-buyer rebate achievable by anonymous feasible VCG redistribution rules that preserve the VCG allocation and make each buyer’s rebate depend only on other buyers’ reports?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(T\subseteq\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, with each mass encoded as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible reports. Masses sum to \(1\). Input also includes rational \(q\in(0,1)\), with \(\mu((\lambda,\infty))<q<\mu([\lambda,\infty))\) for some \(\lambda\in T\).
For each \(N\), there are \(N\) unit-demand buyers and \(1\le m<N\) identical indivisible slots. Values and reports belong to \([0,\infty)\); observed profiles belong to \(T^N\). Use the VCG allocation and payments, breaking allocation ties uniformly among tied buyers.
An anonymous redistribution rule is a permutation-invariant function \(r_{N,m}:[0,\infty)^{N-1}\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\); buyer \(i\) receives \(r_{N,m}(v_{-i})\) in addition to the VCG outcome. Feasibility requires \(\sum_i r_{N,m}(v_{-i})\le\operatorname{VCG}_{N,m}(v)\) for every report profile \(v\in[0,\infty)^N\), where \(\operatorname{VCG}_{N,m}\) denotes total VCG revenue.
Call \(a\ge0\) achievable if there exists one feasible family \((r_{N,m})\) such that, for every sequence \(v^{(N)}\in T^N\) whose empirical masses converge to \(\mu\), and every sequence \(m_N/N\to q\), \(\max_{1\le i\le N}|r_{N,m_N}(v^{(N)}_{-i})-a|\to0\). Let \(R(\mu,q)\) be the supremum of achievable \(a\).

What is \(R(\mu,q)\), and is deciding \(R(\mu,q)\ge b\), for an input rational \(b\ge0\), in P or NP-hard?
State limiting-problem running times in \(\tau\) and total input bit length \(L\); any finite-population running times must additionally specify dependence on \(N\) and \(m\).

Known finite-population result: feasible redistribution rules are undominated under pointwise comparison of every buyer’s rebate exactly when each rebate saturates the residual-revenue bound obtained by varying that buyer’s report; the original statement uses a minimum. [Guo–Conitzer](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~conitzer/undominatedAAMAS08.pdf).
The later characterization uses an infimum, allowing unrestricted report domains. [Guo–Markakis–Apt–Conitzer](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/undominatedJAIR13.pdf). No result settling the exact limiting problem above was identified in the search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made q rational and introduced a binary-rational threshold b to define the computational decision problem.
  • Normalized total society mass to 1 and omitted zero-mass types from the supplied support.
  • Interpreted slots as identical goods allocated to unit-demand buyers.
  • Distinguished observed values in T from the unrestricted nonnegative report domain; feasibility holds at every report profile, including zero reports.
  • Resolved allocation ties uniformly among tied buyers to preserve anonymity.
  • Interpreted a common limiting rebate as uniform convergence across all buyers, for every population and slot sequence satisfying the stated limits.
  • Allowed a mechanism family to depend on the supplied society but required the same family to work for every convergent population sequence.
  • Used a supremum for the greatest achievable limiting rebate without assuming that an optimum is attained.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact value and complexity

Under the stated strict cutoff hypothesis,
\[ R(\mu,q)=q\lambda. \]
The supremum is attained by a single feasible family that works for every society satisfying this hypothesis. Deciding \(R(\mu,q)\ge b\) is in \(\mathrm P\).

1. VCG revenue

For a report profile \(v\in[0,\infty)^N\), write its reports in nonincreasing order as \(v_{(1)},\ldots,v_{(N)}\).

The total VCG revenue is
\[ \operatorname{VCG}_{N,m}(v)=m\,v_{(m+1)}. \]

Indeed, let \(W=\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{(j)}\) be the optimal welfare. If buyer \(i\) is allocated a slot, the optimal welfare without that buyer is \(W-v_i+v_{(m+1)}\). The welfare of the other buyers in the chosen allocation is \(W-v_i\), so the buyer’s externality payment is \(v_{(m+1)}\). An unallocated buyer pays zero.

This calculation holds for every realized choice among tied buyers. Consequently, uniform tie breaking gives the same total revenue, also when payments are interpreted in expectation.

2. A feasible family on the entire report domain

For an opponents’ profile \(w\in[0,\infty)^{N-1}\), let \(s_{m+1}(w)\) denote the \((m+1)\)-st largest entry of the length-\(N\) profile obtained by adjoining a zero to \(w\). Define
\[ r_{N,m}(w)=\frac{m}{N}s_{m+1}(w). \]

For \(m\le N-2\), this is \(m/N\) times the opponents’ \((m+1)\)-st largest report. For \(m=N-1\), it is zero. This is the Bailey–Cavallo rule, whose multi-unit formula appears in [Guo–Markakis–Apt–Conitzer, Section 5.1](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/undominatedJAIR13.pdf#page=21). We verify feasibility directly.

The rule is permutation invariant and nonnegative. Fix any full report profile \(v\). Replacing \(v_i\) by zero cannot increase any order statistic: for every threshold, the number of entries exceeding that threshold cannot increase. Hence
\[ s_{m+1}(v_{-i})\le v_{(m+1)}. \]
Summing over buyers gives
\[ \sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{N,m}(v_{-i}) \le \sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{m}{N}v_{(m+1)} =m\,v_{(m+1)} =\operatorname{VCG}_{N,m}(v). \]

Thus this family is feasible for every report profile in \([0,\infty)^N\), including reports outside \(T\). It does not depend on \(\mu\) or \(q\).

3. The strict cutoff makes all rebates equal eventually

Set
\[ h=\mu((\lambda,\infty)), \qquad g=\mu([\lambda,\infty)). \]
The hypothesis is \(h<q<g\).

Consider arbitrary sequences allowed by the definition of achievability. Let \(H_N\) count the reports strictly above \(\lambda\), and let \(G_N\) count the reports at least \(\lambda\). Because \(T\) is finite and its empirical masses converge,
\[ \frac{H_N}{N}\to h, \qquad \frac{G_N}{N}\to g. \]
Together with \(m_N/N\to q\), this implies
\[ \frac{m_N-H_N}{N}\to q-h>0, \qquad \frac{G_N-m_N}{N}\to g-q>0. \]
Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[ H_N\le m_N, \qquad G_N\ge m_N+2. \]

These inequalities show that both the \((m_N+1)\)-st and \((m_N+2)\)-nd largest reports equal \(\lambda\).

More directly, after deleting any buyer, at most \(H_N\le m_N\) remaining reports are strictly above \(\lambda\), and at least \(G_N-1\ge m_N+1\) remaining reports are at least \(\lambda\). Thus the opponents’ \((m_N+1)\)-st largest report is exactly \(\lambda\). Also, \(G_N\le N\) implies \(m_N\le N-2\), so the zero-padding case is irrelevant eventually.

The constructed family consequently satisfies, for every buyer simultaneously,
\[ r_{N,m_N}(v^{(N)}_{-i})=\frac{m_N}{N}\lambda \]
for all sufficiently large \(N\). Hence
\[ \max_i\left|r_{N,m_N}(v^{(N)}_{-i})-q\lambda\right| =\lambda\left|\frac{m_N}{N}-q\right| \to0. \]

The sequences were arbitrary, so \(q\lambda\) is achievable.

4. Feasibility gives the matching upper bound

Suppose \(a\) is achievable by any feasible family.

There exists a sequence of observed profiles with empirical masses converging to \(\mu\): enumerate the types, assign \(\lfloor N\mu(t)\rfloor\) buyers to each of the first \(\tau-1\) types, and assign the remaining buyers to the last type. The errors in all empirical masses tend to zero. Choose also integers \(m_N\) with \(1\le m_N<N\) and \(m_N/N\to q\).

By Section 3, the VCG cutoff on these profiles eventually equals \(\lambda\). Write
\[ \varepsilon_N =\max_i\left|r_{N,m_N}(v^{(N)}_{-i})-a\right|. \]
Achievability gives \(\varepsilon_N\to0\). Feasibility then implies
\[ a-\varepsilon_N \le \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{N,m_N}(v^{(N)}_{-i}) \le \frac{\operatorname{VCG}_{N,m_N}(v^{(N)})}{N} =\frac{m_N}{N}\lambda. \]
Taking limits yields \(a\le q\lambda\).

Together with the achievable construction, this proves \(R(\mu,q)=q\lambda\), with the supremum attained. The case \(\lambda=0\) is included: its exact answer is zero.

5. Exact computation and running times

Limiting problem

Sort the support as \(t_1>\cdots>t_\tau\), and define cumulative masses \(S_0=0\) and
\[ S_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\mu(t_\ell). \]
Find the index \(j\) satisfying \(S_{j-1}<q<S_j\). The promised hypothesis ensures that it exists and is unique, and \(\lambda=t_j\). Return \(qt_j\); for the decision problem, compare \(qt_j\) with \(b\) exactly.

This takes \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational comparisons and \(O(\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations.

For an explicit bit bound, let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including \(b\) for the decision problem. Use the product of the mass denominators as a common denominator. Its bit length is \(O(L)\), and the cumulative-mass numerators also have \(O(L)\) bits because the masses sum to one. All required rational comparisons and products involve integers of \(O(L)\) bits.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic therefore gives a conservative bound of
\[ O\!\left(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2\right) \]
bit operations. This establishes membership in \(\mathrm P\). The limiting algorithm has no dependence on \(N\) or \(m\).

Finite-population evaluation

For completeness, the witnessing rule also has an efficient implementation:

  • A single rebate can be evaluated using \(O(N)\) comparisons by deterministic order-statistic selection.
  • All rebates can be evaluated using \(O(N\log(N+1))\) comparisons and \(O(N)\) additional arithmetic operations.

For the second bound, sort the full profile. When \(m\le N-2\), deleting a buyer of rank at most \(m+1\) makes the opponents’ \((m+1)\)-st report equal to \(v_{(m+2)}\); deleting any later-ranked buyer makes it equal to \(v_{(m+1)}\). Arbitrarily assigning ranks within ties preserves this formula. When \(m=N-1\), every rebate is zero.

These bounds hold uniformly over \(1\le m<N\). For rational reports whose numerators and denominators have at most \(B\) bits, all rebates can, conservatively, be evaluated in
\[ O\!\left(N\log(N+1)(B+\log N)^2\right) \]
bit operations. The factor \(m/N\) contributes at most \(O(\log N)\) bits.
