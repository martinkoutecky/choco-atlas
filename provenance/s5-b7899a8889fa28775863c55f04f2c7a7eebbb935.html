The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(T\), localities \(L\), and services \(S\), an instance lists rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), integer needs \(\nu_{ts}\), a strict locality ranking for each type, finite priority classes at each locality, and rational per-capita capacities \(b_{\ell s}\). Find whether there is a rational assignment \(x_{t\ell}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_\ell x_{t\ell}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\nu_{ts}x_{t\ell}\le b_{\ell s}\) that is weakly envy-free and typewise first-order stochastically dominates every other feasible weakly envy-free assignment; if so, output it. For \(u\) with \(x_{u\ell}>0\), define \(G_{u\ell s}(x)=\sum_{v:\,v\triangleright_\ell u}\nu_{vs}\sum_{\ell':\,\ell\succeq_v\ell'}x_{v\ell'}\). A violation occurs if some higher-priority type \(t\) has positive mass assigned to a locality it ranks below \(\ell\), while \(G_{u\ell s}(x)+\epsilon\nu_{us}\le b_{\ell s}\) for every service \(s\) needed by \(u\), for some \(\epsilon>0\). Typewise first-order stochastic dominance means that for every type \(t\) and every prefix \(P\) of its locality ranking, the assignment gives at least as much \(t\)-mass to \(P\) as any other feasible weakly envy-free assignment.

A locality can accommodate a positive slice of \(t\) alone if some \(0<a\le\mu_t\) satisfies \(a\nu_{ts}\le b_{\ell s}\) for every service. Every type must have such a non-null locality; each locality strictly prioritizes types it can accommodate alone over those it cannot.
A feasible assignment has rational entries \(x_{t\ell}\ge0\), satisfies \(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L}x_{t\ell}=\mu_t\), and satisfies \(\sum_t\nu_{ts}x_{t\ell}\le b_{\ell s}\) at every non-null locality and service.

Partial result: corrected hardness and a stronger upper bound

Let $\mathrm{UD}$ denote the decision problem in the question. I prove
$$ \mathrm{UD}\text{ is }\mathrm{coNP}\text{-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions}, \qquad \mathrm{UD}\in\mathrm P^{\mathrm{NP}}. $$

The hardness holds even with needs in $\{0,1\}$, strictly positive capacities, and at most three priority classes at each locality.

Consequently, polynomial-time decision would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$. The problem is also $\mathrm{NP}$-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. However, I do not establish $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness under polynomial-time many-one reductions, which is the usual interpretation of unqualified $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness. Under that interpretation, the requested classification remains partial.

The proof below repairs both demand-indexing errors identified in the verifier report. It also proves that the unique possible universally dominant assignment is determined by prefix suprema, and that its feasibility alone suffices for universal dominance.

1. An exact characterization of weak envy-freeness

For a type $v$ and a non-null locality $\ell$, define its mass at $\ell$ or worse by
$$ D_v(\ell;x) = \sum_{\ell':\,\ell\succeq_v\ell'}x_{v\ell'}. $$
Thus
$$ G_{u\ell s}(x) = \sum_{v:\,v\triangleright_\ell u}\nu_{vs}D_v(\ell;x). $$

In particular, $G_{u\ell s}$ excludes $u$ itself. A type’s contribution to another type’s demand must be distinguished from the demand indexed by that type.

Lemma 1. A feasible assignment $x$ is weakly envy-free if and only if, for every non-null $\ell$, every $u$ with $x_{u\ell}>0$, and every service $s$ with $\nu_{us}>0$,
$$ G_{u\ell s}(x)<b_{\ell s}. $$

Proof. Suppose all these strict inequalities hold. If $u$ has at least one positive need, take
$$ \epsilon = \min\left\{ x_{u\ell}, \min_{s:\,\nu_{us}>0} \frac{b_{\ell s}-G_{u\ell s}(x)}{\nu_{us}} \right\}. $$
Every term is positive, so $\epsilon>0$ and all required accommodation inequalities hold. If $u$ has no positive needs, take $\epsilon=x_{u\ell}$.

Conversely, suppose $x_{u\ell}>0$, $\nu_{us}>0$, and
$$ G_{u\ell s}(x)\ge b_{\ell s}. $$
No positive $\epsilon$ can satisfy the accommodation inequality for this service.

We must also establish the violation’s higher-priority-demand condition. If every type strictly above $u$ had zero mass strictly below $\ell$, then
$$ G_{u\ell s}(x) = \sum_{v:\,v\triangleright_\ell u}\nu_{vs}x_{v\ell}. $$
Feasibility and nonnegativity would give
$$ G_{u\ell s}(x)+\nu_{us}x_{u\ell}\le b_{\ell s}, $$
contradicting $G_{u\ell s}(x)\ge b_{\ell s}$ and $\nu_{us}x_{u\ell}>0$. Hence some strictly higher-priority type has positive mass strictly below $\ell$, and a violation exists.

At the null locality, unlimited capacity always permits a positive slice. This completes the proof. $\square$

2. A polynomial-size reduction from unsatisfiability

Take a $3$-CNF formula $F$ with $n\ge1$ variables and $c\ge1$ nonempty clauses, each containing at most three literal occurrences. Unsatisfiability remains $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete under these restrictions: an unused variable and a nonempty tautological clause can supply the size conditions without changing satisfiability.

Index the $K$ literal occurrences by $r$. Write:

  • $i(r)$ for the variable of occurrence $r$;
  • $\sigma(r)\in\{+,-\}$ for its sign;
  • $j(r)$ for its clause;
  • $k_j$ for the number of occurrences in clause $j$.

Set
$$ N=n+K, \qquad \delta=\frac{1}{10(N+1)}, \qquad M=2n+2K+2. $$
In particular,
$$ N\delta<\frac1{10}. $$

We first give unnormalized masses and capacities. Dividing every mass, capacity, and assignment entry by $M$ produces the actual instance. This scaling preserves feasibility, Lemma 1’s strict inequalities, and all prefix-dominance comparisons.

Types and needs

There are variable types $V_i^+,V_i^-$ of mass $1$, occurrence types $H_r$ of mass $2$, and a distinguished type $Z$ of mass $2$. Their total mass is $M$.

The services are
$$ q_1,\ldots,q_n,\quad v,\quad w,\quad s_1,\ldots,s_c. $$
All needs are zero or one:

| Type | Services with need $1$ |
|---|---|
| $V_i^\sigma$ | $q_i,v$ |
| $H_r$ | $q_{i(r)},s_{j(r)}$ |
| $Z$ | Every service |

Localities and capacities

The non-null localities are $C$, variable localities $A_1,\ldots,A_n$, and occurrence localities $B_1,\ldots,B_K$.

At $A_i$, give every service capacity $\delta$, except
$$ b_{A_i,q_i}=b_{A_i,v}=1. $$

At $B_r$, give every service capacity $\delta$, except
$$ b_{B_r,q_{i(r)}}=b_{B_r,s_{j(r)}}=\frac25. $$

At $C$, set
$$ b_{C,q_i}=2K+2n+4,\qquad b_{C,v}=2n+4,\qquad b_{C,w}=2,\qquad b_{C,s_j}=2k_j. $$

Rankings and priorities

Complete the following specifications to strict rankings:

  • $Z$: $C$ first, then all $A_i,B_r$, then $\varnothing$.
  • $V_i^\sigma$: $A_i$ first, then all other $A_h,B_r$, then $C$, then $\varnothing$.
  • $H_r$: $B_r$ first, $C$ second, then all other $A_i,B_h$, then $\varnothing$.

Priority classes, from highest to lowest, are:

| Locality | Ordered priority classes |
|---|---|
| $C$ | All $H_r$; then $Z$; then all variable types |
| $A_i$ | $Z$; then all variable types; then all $H_r$ |
| $B_r$ | $Z$; then $V_{i(r)}^{\sigma(r)}$; then every remaining type |

Every capacity is strictly positive. Thus every type can be accommodated alone at every locality by a sufficiently small positive slice. Both accommodation hypotheses in the question therefore hold.

The normalized instance has
$$ m=N+1,\qquad \tau=2n+K+1,\qquad d=n+c+2. $$
All numbers, needs, rankings, and priority partitions have total encoding length polynomial in the formula length.

3. Positive allocation of $Z$ at $C$ forces satisfiability

Lemma 2. If a feasible weakly envy-free assignment $x$ has $x_{Z,C}>0$, then $F$ is satisfiable.

Proof. Work with unnormalized quantities.

At $C$, precisely the occurrence types strictly outrank $Z$. The only locality that $H_r$ ranks above $C$ is $B_r$. Hence, for each clause $j$,
$$ G_{Z,C,s_j}(x) = \sum_{r:\,j(r)=j}\left(2-x_{H_r,B_r}\right) = 2k_j-\sum_{r:\,j(r)=j}x_{H_r,B_r}. $$
Since $x_{Z,C}>0$ and $Z$ needs $s_j$, Lemma 1 gives
$$ G_{Z,C,s_j}(x)<2k_j. $$
Thus every clause contains an occurrence $r$ with $x_{H_r,B_r}>0$.

Fix such an occurrence, and put $i=i(r)$ and $\sigma=\sigma(r)$. At $B_r$, exactly $Z$ and $V_i^\sigma$ strictly outrank $H_r$. Both need $q_i$. Therefore
$$ G_{H_r,B_r,q_i}(x) = D_Z(B_r;x)+D_{V_i^\sigma}(B_r;x). $$
This is the corrected demand index: it is $G$ indexed by $H_r$, not by the variable type.

Lemma 1 and nonnegativity imply
$$ D_{V_i^\sigma}(B_r;x) \le G_{H_r,B_r,q_i}(x) <\frac25. $$
The variable type has total mass $1$, so its mass strictly above $B_r$ exceeds $3/5$.

For each $A_h$ with $h\ne i$, feasibility for $q_i$ gives
$$ x_{V_i^\sigma,A_h}\le\delta. $$
For every $B_h$, feasibility for $v$ gives
$$ x_{V_i^\sigma,B_h}\le\delta. $$
There are $N-1$ auxiliary localities other than $A_i$. Since $C$ and $\varnothing$ are below $B_r$ in this variable type’s ranking, its mass strictly above $B_r$ is at most
$$ x_{V_i^\sigma,A_i}+(N-1)\delta. $$
Consequently,
$$ x_{V_i^\sigma,A_i} > \frac35-(N-1)\delta > \frac12. $$

At $A_i$, feasibility for $q_i$ gives
$$ x_{V_i^+,A_i}+x_{V_i^-,A_i}\le1. $$
Thus both signs of the same variable cannot have mass exceeding $1/2$ at $A_i$.

Choose the sign with mass exceeding $1/2$ whenever one exists, and choose the remaining variables arbitrarily. Every clause has an occurrence with positive allocation at its $B_r$, and the preceding argument shows that this occurrence’s sign is chosen. This truth assignment satisfies every clause. $\square$

4. Unsatisfiable formulas yield a universally dominant assignment

Define $x^0$ by
$$ x^0_{H_r,C}=2,\qquad x^0_{V_i^\sigma,C}=1, $$
and
$$ x^0_{Z,A_i}=\delta,\qquad x^0_{Z,B_r}=\delta. $$
Assign $Z$’s remaining mass $2-N\delta$ to $\varnothing$. All unspecified entries are zero.

Lemma 3. For every formula, $x^0$ is feasible and weakly envy-free.

Proof. At each auxiliary locality, the only assigned type is $Z$, with mass $\delta$. Its load in every service is $\delta$, within every capacity.

Let $h_i$ count the occurrences involving variable $i$. At $C$, the loads are
$$ 2h_i+2\text{ for }q_i,\qquad 2n\text{ for }v,\qquad 0\text{ for }w,\qquad 2k_j\text{ for }s_j. $$
They satisfy the specified capacities because $h_i\le K$.

Each occurrence type assigned at $C$ has highest priority. Each positive auxiliary slice of $Z$ also has highest priority. Their demand quantities are zero, strictly below the positive relevant capacities.

For a variable type assigned at $C$, its higher-priority demand is
$$ G_{V_i^\sigma,C,q_i}(x^0)=2h_i+2, \qquad G_{V_i^\sigma,C,v}(x^0)=2. $$
Here the occurrence types contribute $2h_i$ to the first expression, and $Z$ contributes its full mass $2$ to both. Both expressions are strictly below their capacities at $C$. Lemma 1 applies. $\square$

Lemma 4. If $F$ is unsatisfiable, $x^0$ is universally dominant.

Proof. Let $y$ be any feasible rational weakly envy-free assignment. Lemma 2 implies
$$ y_{Z,C}=0. $$
At every auxiliary locality $\ell$, capacity for $w$ gives
$$ y_{Z,\ell}\le\delta, $$
because $Z$ needs one unit of $w$ per unit mass.

For any auxiliary $\ell$, $Z$’s mass strictly above $\ell$ is therefore at most $N\delta$. Consequently,
$$ D_Z(\ell;y)\ge2-N\delta>1. $$
This bounds $Z$’s contribution to lower-priority types’ demand. It does not bound $G_{Z,\ell,s}$, which is zero because $Z$ has highest priority there.

At every $A_h$, $Z$ strictly outranks every variable type and needs its service $q_i$. Thus
$$ G_{V_i^\sigma,A_h,q_i}(y) \ge D_Z(A_h;y) >1 \ge b_{A_h,q_i}. $$
Lemma 1 prohibits positive allocation of any variable type at any $A_h$.

At every $B_r$, use service $v$ instead:
$$ G_{V_i^\sigma,B_r,v}(y) \ge D_Z(B_r;y) >1 >\delta=b_{B_r,v}. $$
So variable types receive no mass at any $B_r$ either. Every variable type is assigned only to $C$ and $\varnothing$.

Now consider $H_r$ at its first choice $B_r$. Its associated variable type $V_{i(r)}^{\sigma(r)}$ strictly outranks it there, needs $q_{i(r)}$, and has its entire mass at $C$ or $\varnothing$, both below $B_r$. Hence
$$ G_{H_r,B_r,q_{i(r)}}(y)\ge1>\frac25. $$
Lemma 1 gives $y_{H_r,B_r}=0$.

These restrictions prove every prefix comparison:

  • A variable type receives its entire mass at $C$ under $x^0$, while $y$ assigns it only to $C$ or $\varnothing$.
  • An occurrence type receives its entire mass at its second choice $C$ under $x^0$, while $y$ assigns zero mass to its first choice.
  • $Z$ receives zero mass at $C$ in both assignments. At every auxiliary locality, $y$ assigns at most $\delta$, while $x^0$ assigns exactly $\delta$. Each prefix before the null locality therefore has at least as much mass under $x^0$; the full prefix has equal mass.

Together with Lemma 3, this establishes universal dominance. $\square$

5. Satisfiable formulas yield no universally dominant assignment

Lemma 5. If $F$ is satisfiable, the constructed instance has no universally dominant assignment.

Proof. Fix a satisfying truth assignment. Let $\sigma_i$ be its selected sign for variable $i$, and put $\beta=1/10$.

Construct $y^1$ as follows:

  • Assign all mass $2$ of $Z$ to $C$.
  • Assign all mass $1$ of $V_i^{\sigma_i}$ to $A_i$, for each $i$.
  • For every true literal occurrence $r$, assign mass $\beta$ of $H_r$ to $B_r$.
  • Assign all remaining mass to $\varnothing$.

This is feasible. A used $A_i$ has loads $1$ for $q_i,v$ and zero for other services. A used $B_r$ has loads $\beta$ for $q_{i(r)},s_{j(r)}$. At $C$, only $Z$ is assigned, with load $2$ in every service; this fits because each $k_j\ge1$.

For weak envy-freeness, at each used $A_i$, the only higher-priority type is $Z$, whose entire mass is strictly above $A_i$. Thus the relevant demand is zero.

At a used $B_r$, the higher-priority types are $Z$ and its associated selected variable type. They are respectively assigned at $C$ and $A_{i(r)}$, both strictly above $B_r$ in their own rankings. Again the relevant demand is zero.

At $C$, let $a_j\ge1$ count the true occurrences of clause $j$. Then
$$ G_{Z,C,s_j}(y^1)=2k_j-\beta a_j<2k_j. $$
For each $q_i$, the higher-priority demand is at most $2K$, strictly below $2K+2n+4$. For $v,w$, it is zero. Lemma 1 proves that $y^1$ is weakly envy-free.

Fix any variable $i$. Construct $y^2$ by changing only its two variable types:
$$ y^2_{V_i^{\sigma_i},A_i}=\frac45, \qquad y^2_{V_i^{-\sigma_i},A_i}=\frac15, $$
with their remaining masses assigned to $\varnothing$. Keep all other allocations from $y^1$.

Feasibility remains valid because the combined variable load at $A_i$ is still $1$. These two types are tied at $A_i$, and the higher-priority demand from $Z$ remains zero.

For a used $B_r$ involving variable $i$, the associated selected variable now contributes $1/5$ to demand for $q_i$, and zero for its clause service. The contribution of $Z$ remains zero. Thus
$$ G_{H_r,B_r,q_i}(y^2)=\frac15<\frac25, \qquad G_{H_r,B_r,s_{j(r)}}(y^2)=0. $$
All other required strict inequalities remain unchanged. Therefore $y^2$ is weakly envy-free.

A universally dominant assignment $x$ would have to dominate both $y^1$ and $y^2$. Since $A_i$ is the first choice of both variable types, this would force
$$ x_{V_i^{\sigma_i},A_i}\ge1, \qquad x_{V_i^{-\sigma_i},A_i}\ge\frac15. $$
Their load for $q_i$ would exceed capacity $1$, contradicting feasibility. $\square$

Lemmas 4 and 5 establish the many-one reduction
$$ F\text{ is unsatisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad I(F)\in\mathrm{UD}. $$

Thus $\mathrm{UD}$ is $\mathrm{coNP}$-hard. A satisfiability algorithm can also make one $\mathrm{UD}$ query and negate its answer, proving $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions.

6. The prefix-supremum assignment

Return to an arbitrary instance with normalized masses.

Let $\mathcal W$ be the set of feasible rational weakly envy-free assignments. It is nonempty: assigning every type entirely to $\varnothing$ belongs to $\mathcal W$.

For type $t$, list its ranking as
$$ \ell_{t1}\succ_t\cdots\succ_t\ell_{t,m+1}, $$
with $\ell_{t,m+1}=\varnothing$. Define
$$ q_{tk} = \sup_{y\in\mathcal W} \sum_{j=1}^{k}y_{t,\ell_{tj}}, \qquad q_{t0}=0. $$
These values are nondecreasing in $k$, and $q_{t,m+1}=\mu_t$. Define
$$ x^*_{t,\ell_{tk}}=q_{tk}-q_{t,k-1}. $$
Thus $x^*$ is nonnegative, has the required row sums, and dominates every member of $\mathcal W$ in every prefix. Rationality will be proved below.

Lemma 6. Every positive non-null entry of $x^*$ satisfies all the strict-demand inequalities in Lemma 1.

Proof. Suppose $\ell=\ell_{uk}$ and $x^*_{u\ell}>0$. Then
$$ q_{uk}>q_{u,k-1}. $$
By the definition of supremum, some $y\in\mathcal W$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{j=1}^{k}y_{u,\ell_{uj}}>q_{u,k-1}. $$
Its preceding prefix is at most $q_{u,k-1}$, so $y_{u\ell}>0$.

For any type $v$, the mass at $\ell$ or worse equals its total mass minus its prefix strictly above $\ell$. Since $x^*$ dominates every prefix of $y$,
$$ D_v(\ell;x^*)\le D_v(\ell;y). $$
Nonnegative needs therefore give
$$ G_{u\ell s}(x^*)\le G_{u\ell s}(y). $$
Because $y\in\mathcal W$ and $y_{u\ell}>0$, Lemma 1 implies, whenever $\nu_{us}>0$,
$$ G_{u\ell s}(x^*)\le G_{u\ell s}(y)<b_{\ell s}. $$
$\square$

Corollary 7. Once the rationality of $x^*$ is established, a universally dominant assignment exists exactly when $x^*$ is feasible. If one exists, it is $x^*$ and is unique.

Proof. If $x^*$ is feasible, Lemmas 6 and 1 show that it is weakly envy-free. Its prefix values are the corresponding suprema, so it is universally dominant.

Conversely, a universally dominant $x$ belongs to $\mathcal W$, so every prefix value is at most its supremum $q_{tk}$. Dominance of every $y\in\mathcal W$ gives the reverse inequality after taking the supremum. Hence every prefix of $x$ equals $q_{tk}$, forcing $x=x^*$. $\square$

7. Rationality, output length, and a $\mathrm P^{\mathrm{NP}}$ algorithm

Support regions and their closures

For each proposed positive non-null support
$$ E\subseteq T\times\bigl(\mathcal L\setminus\{\varnothing\}\bigr), $$
let $R_E$ be the real assignments satisfying feasibility together with
$$ x_{u\ell}=0\quad\text{outside }E, \qquad x_{u\ell}>0\quad\text{inside }E, $$
and
$$ G_{u\ell s}(x)<b_{\ell s} \quad \text{for }(u,\ell)\in E,\ \nu_{us}>0. $$
By Lemma 1, $\mathcal W$ is the union of the rational points in these regions.

Introduce $0\le\eta\le1$, replace the required positivity conditions by $x_{u\ell}\ge\eta$, and replace each strict demand condition by
$$ G_{u\ell s}(x)\le b_{\ell s}-\eta. $$
Maximize $\eta$. The region $R_E$ is nonempty precisely when the optimum is positive: a strict point supplies a positive common margin, and a positive-margin solution supplies a strict point.

This is a bounded rational linear program with polynomially many variables and constraints. If its optimum is positive, an optimal vertex supplies a rational point $z\in R_E$ of polynomial encoding length. Polynomial-time rational linear programming also makes this an effective polynomial-time support test. [Khachiyan, *A polynomial algorithm in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

For a nonempty $R_E$, let $P_E$ replace its strict inequalities by weak inequalities. It is a bounded rational polytope, and
$$ \overline{R_E}=P_E. $$
Indeed, fix $z\in R_E$. For any $w\in P_E$,
$$ (1-\lambda)w+\lambda z\in R_E \qquad(0<\lambda\le1), $$
because every required positive entry and strict slack receives a positive contribution from $z$.

Each prefix objective attains its maximum over $P_E$ at a rational vertex $w$. Taking rational $\lambda$ and rational $z,w$ in the displayed combination approaches that maximum through rational points of $R_E$. Consequently,
$$ q_{tk} = \max_{E:\,R_E\ne\varnothing} \ \max_{w\in P_E} \sum_{j=1}^{k}w_{t,\ell_{tj}}. $$
There are finitely many supports. Thus every $q_{tk}$ is rational.

Enumerating the at most $2^{m\tau}$ supports and solving their linear programs already gives an exact
$$ 2^{m\tau}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,d,L) $$
decision-and-construction algorithm.

A uniform polynomial encoding bound

Put $p=\tau(m+1)$, the number of assignment variables.

The constraints of each $P_E$ have integer coefficients drawn from needs, zero, and one, with rational right-hand sides from the input. Clearing the right-hand-side denominator in each row gives integer coefficients and right-hand sides of absolute value at most $2^{2L+2}$.

A vertex is determined by $p$ independent active equations. Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound
$$ |\det A|\le p!\left(2^{2L+2}\right)^p $$
therefore bound the numerator and denominator lengths of every vertex coordinate polynomially in $p,L$.

For example, the integer
$$ B=p\left(2L+2+\left\lceil\log_2(p+1)\right\rceil\right)+2 $$
is a sufficient denominator-bit bound. All coordinates obtained from one active system share its determinant as a denominator. Hence every prefix value at that vertex has reduced denominator at most $2^B$.

Since normalized prefix values lie in $[0,1]$, their reduced numerators also have at most $B+1$ bits. Each $q_{tk}$ is one such vertex value. Consecutive differences therefore have polynomial encoding length, proving that $x^*$ has polynomial encoding length.

The margin program has only one additional variable; the same argument gives a uniform polynomial encoding bound for a strict point $z$ in every nonempty $R_E$.

Exact computation with an $\mathrm{NP}$ oracle

Fix $t,k$ and a rational threshold $a$. Consider the predicate:

There exist a support $E$, a rational strict point $z\in R_E$, and a rational vertex $w\in P_E$ whose $(t,k)$ prefix value is at least $a$.

This predicate belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$:

  • $E$ has $m\tau$ bits.
  • The preceding determinant bounds supply polynomial-size witnesses $z,w$.
  • Their constraints can be checked by exact rational arithmetic.
  • Vertexhood can be checked by verifying that the active constraint normals, including the equality constraints, have rank $p$.

By the maximum formula above, the predicate holds exactly when $q_{tk}\ge a$.

Starting with the interval $[0,1]$, binary search using this predicate produces an interval $[a,b]$ containing $q_{tk}$ with
$$ b-a<2^{-2B}. $$
This requires only polynomially many queries.

Extract an $\mathrm{NP}$ witness for the predicate at threshold $a$ by the usual witness-prefix queries: fix the certificate bits successively, querying whether an accepting completion exists. The certificate length is polynomial, so this also uses polynomially many queries.

Let $r$ be its vertex prefix value. Then

Both $r$ and $q_{tk}$ have reduced denominators at most $2^B$. Two distinct such rationals differ by at least $2^{-2B}$. Since the interval is shorter, necessarily $r=q_{tk}$.

Thus every prefix supremum can be computed exactly in polynomial time with an $\mathrm{NP}$ oracle. There are only $\tau(m+1)$ prefixes. Construct $x^*$ from their consecutive differences and check its capacity inequalities.

Corollary 7 now proves
$$ \mathrm{UD}\in\mathrm P^{\mathrm{NP}}, $$
and proves polynomial-time construction with an $\mathrm{NP}$ oracle whenever a universally dominant assignment exists.

8. Two additional consequences

One non-null locality is polynomial-time solvable

Suppose $m=1$, with non-null locality $\ell$. Every type ranks $\ell$ above $\varnothing$, so
$$ G_{u\ell s}(x) = D_{us} := \sum_{v:\,v\triangleright_\ell u}\mu_v\nu_{vs}, $$
independently of $x$.

Call $u$ eligible if
$$ D_{us}<b_{\ell s} \qquad \text{for every }s\text{ with }\nu_{us}>0. $$
By Lemma 1, weakly envy-free assignments assign zero mass at $\ell$ to every ineligible type.

For an eligible type define
$$ \alpha_u = \min\left\{ \mu_u,\ \min_{s:\,\nu_{us}>0}\frac{b_{\ell s}}{\nu_{us}} \right\}, $$
with the inner minimum interpreted as $+\infty$ for a zero-needs type. Set $\alpha_u=0$ for an ineligible type.

The amount $\alpha_u$ is individually attainable by assigning that amount of $u$ to $\ell$ and every remaining slice to $\varnothing$. Universal dominance therefore forces every type to receive exactly $\alpha_u$ at $\ell$.

A universally dominant assignment exists exactly when
$$ \sum_u\nu_{us}\alpha_u\le b_{\ell s} \qquad\text{for every }s. $$
If these inequalities hold, assign $\alpha_u$ to $\ell$ and $\mu_u-\alpha_u$ to $\varnothing$. This gives polynomial-time decision and construction.

Existence can fail even here with strict priorities: two unit-needs types of mass $1/2$ and capacity $3/4$ are both eligible, but their individually attainable first-choice maxima sum to $1$.

Testing a supplied assignment is $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete

Hardness follows from the reduction: the explicit assignment $x^0$ is universally dominant exactly when the source formula is unsatisfiable.

For membership, feasibility and weak envy-freeness are directly checkable in polynomial time using Lemma 1.

If a feasible weakly envy-free candidate $x$ fails universal dominance, some $y\in\mathcal W$ strictly improves a type’s prefix. Fix its support $E$ and add that improvement to the margin program:
$$ \sum_{\ell\in P}y_{t\ell} \ge \sum_{\ell\in P}x_{t\ell}+\eta. $$
The original witness supplies a positive common margin. An optimal vertex with positive margin supplies a witness of polynomial encoding length, now measured in the combined instance-and-candidate input length. Its feasibility, weak envy-freeness, and strict improvement are polynomially checkable. Non-universality therefore belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

9. What is settled and what remains open

The corrected reduction rigorously excludes polynomial-time general decision unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$. It establishes many-one $\mathrm{coNP}$-hardness and Turing $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness, but it does not reverse the yes/no direction needed for many-one $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness.

The upper bound is strengthened to $\mathrm P^{\mathrm{NP}}$, with construction in polynomial time using an $\mathrm{NP}$ oracle. Whenever a universal assignment exists, it is unique and has polynomial output length.

There is also a conditional answer to the construction question: if general decision were polynomial-time solvable, then general construction would be polynomial-time solvable as well. Indeed, the hardness proof would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, eliminating the oracle from the construction algorithm.
