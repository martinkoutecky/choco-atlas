Status and interpretation

Partial result. I do not establish either membership in $\mathrm P$ or $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness for the exact decision problem.

The new results proved below are:

  • Once the deterministic orbit enters $\Delta_\epsilon$ at a time $t\geq1$, it stays there forever.
  • The decision problem has a polynomial-time many-one reduction to $\mathrm{PosSLP}$, strengthening the previous oracle upper bound.
  • On yes-instances, the least entry time and exact state can be computed in time polynomial in $L$ and the actual output length.

I also give self-contained proofs of the logarithmic entry bound, an exponential-time exact algorithm, and an exponential output-length lower bound.

“Binary rational” means an integer numerator and a positive integer denominator encoded in binary. Finite binary expansions alone are not preserved for every rational $\epsilon$.

1. Recurrences and the simplex

Define
$$ c=1-3p=\frac{1+6\epsilon}{2}, \qquad f=1-p=\frac56+\epsilon, \qquad d=a-b. $$

Absent types have mass zero. Thus the state always has three coordinates, although its support size can be smaller.

For every simplex point,
$$ p\leq r_x\leq1-2p, \qquad r_a+r_b+r_q=3p+c=1. $$
Consequently, the updated coordinates are nonnegative. Their sum is
$$ a(1-r_b)+qr_a+b(1-r_a)+qr_b+ar_b+br_a+qr_q =a+b+q=1. $$
Every iterate therefore remains in the simplex.

Expanding the update gives
$$ \begin{aligned} a'&=fa+pq+ca(q-b),\\ b'&=fb+pq+cb(q-a),\\ q'&=p+c(2ab+q^2). \end{aligned} $$
Subtracting the first two equations yields
$$ d'=d(f+cq). $$
The multiplier is positive. Hence the promised positive bias remains positive.

Since
$$ 2ab=\frac{(1-q)^2-d^2}{2}, $$
the recurrence for the undecided mass becomes
$$ q'=\frac13+\frac{3c}{2}\left(q-\frac13\right)^2-\frac c2d^2. $$

Write the target endpoints as
$$ h=\frac{2\sqrt{\epsilon}}{1+6\epsilon}, \qquad u=1-\frac{p^3}{4}. $$
The latter is the specified upper endpoint, because
$$ \frac{1-6\epsilon}{12}=\frac p2. $$
Two identities used below are
$$ c^2h^2=\epsilon, \qquad f+\frac c3=1+2\epsilon. $$

Also,
$$ h^2=\frac{4\epsilon}{(1+6\epsilon)^2}\leq\frac16<\frac14. $$
Indeed, the first inequality is equivalent to
$$ 24\epsilon\leq(1+6\epsilon)^2, $$
whose right-hand side minus left-hand side is $(1-6\epsilon)^2$. In particular, $h<1/2$.

2. Entry and deterministic persistence

2.1. The upper endpoint holds after one update

At a simplex point with positive bias,
$$ 0<d\leq1, \qquad q\leq1-d. $$
Therefore,
$$ \begin{aligned} f-d' &=f(1-d)-cdq\\ &\geq(1-d)(f-cd)\\ &\geq(1-d)(f-c)\\ &=2p(1-d)\geq0. \end{aligned} $$
Thus
$$ 0<d_t\leq f<u \qquad(t\geq1), $$
where $f<u$ follows from
$$ u-f=p-\frac{p^3}{4}>0. $$

Consequently, for every $t\geq1$,
$$ d_t\in\Delta_\epsilon \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_t\geq h. $$

2.2. Persistence after time one

Proposition. For every $t\geq1$,
$$ d_t\geq h \quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_{t+1}\geq h. $$

Proof. Suppose first that $d_{t-1}\leq2h$. The completed-square recurrence implies
$$ q_t\geq\frac13-\frac c2d_{t-1}^2. $$
Hence
$$ \begin{aligned} f+cq_t &\geq f+\frac c3-\frac{c^2}{2}d_{t-1}^2\\ &\geq1+2\epsilon-2c^2h^2\\ &=1. \end{aligned} $$
It follows that $d_{t+1}\geq d_t\geq h$.

Suppose instead that $d_{t-1}>2h$. Each bias multiplier is at least $f$, so
$$ d_{t+1}\geq f^2d_{t-1}>2f^2h>h, $$
because $f>5/6$ and therefore $2f^2>1$. This proves the proposition. $\square$

Together with the upper-endpoint bound, this shows that entry at any time $t\geq1$ implies persistence forever.

The restriction to times at least one matters: the proposition does not assert that every initial simplex point with $d_0\geq h$ also has $d_1\geq h$.

2.3. An explicit logarithmic entry bound

If $d\leq h$, then
$$ q'\geq Q_h :=\frac13-\frac c2h^2 =\frac{1+3\epsilon}{3(1+6\epsilon)}. $$
Thus
$$ f+cQ_h=1+\frac{3\epsilon}{2} =:g>1. $$

Define, using rational arithmetic,
$$ j=\min\{k\geq0:f\eta g^k\geq1\}, \qquad H=j+1. $$
Because $f\eta<1$, we have $j\geq1$.

Suppose $d_0<h$ and no entry occurs through time $H$. Then every $d_t$ through that time is below $h$. We have
$$ d_1\geq f\eta. $$
For each $t$ with $1\leq t<H$, the inequality $d_{t-1}<h$ gives $q_t\geq Q_h$, and hence
$$ d_{t+1}\geq gd_t. $$
It follows that
$$ d_H\geq f\eta g^{H-1}\geq1, $$
contradicting $d_H<h$.

The other initial cases are immediate. If $d_0\in[h,u]$, entry occurs at time zero. If $d_0>u$, then
$$ d_1\geq fd_0>fu >\frac56\cdot\frac{863}{864} >\frac12>h. $$
Here $u>863/864$ follows from $p<1/6$. Since $d_1\leq f<u$, entry occurs at time one.

Therefore every promised input has a first entry time at most
$$ H=\mathcal O_\epsilon\!\left(1+\log\frac1\eta\right). $$
A positive rational with binary encoding length at most $L$ is at least $2^{-L}$, so
$$ H=\mathcal O_\epsilon(L). $$

Computing $H$ requires $\mathcal O_\epsilon(L)$ multiplications by a fixed rational. All intermediate integers have $\mathcal O_\epsilon(L)$ bits. A conservative school-arithmetic preprocessing bound is $\mathcal O_\epsilon(L^3)$.

3. Exact comparisons and the decision reduction

3.1. Inclusive endpoint comparisons

Write the fixed rational $h^2$ as
$$ h^2=\frac ZW, $$
with positive integers $Z,W$.

For a positive rational bias $d=X/D$, with $D>0$,
$$ d\geq h \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad WX^2-ZD^2\geq0. $$
Both sides being compared before squaring are positive, so this equivalence is exact and accepts equality.

The upper endpoint is rational and is compared by cross multiplication. Its comparison is needed only at time zero.

3.2. Integer circuits for iterates

Write the fixed rational parameter in lowest terms as
$$ p=\frac PV, \qquad C=V-3P>0. $$
For an integer common-denominator representation
$$ (a,b,q)=\frac{(A,B,Q)}D, \qquad A+B+Q=D, $$
the next state has the representation
$$ \begin{aligned} \widehat A&=(V-P)AD+PQD+CA(Q-B),\\ \widehat B&=(V-P)BD+PQD+CB(Q-A),\\ \widehat Q&=PD^2+C(2AB+Q^2),\\ \widehat D&=VD^2. \end{aligned} $$
These are division-free integer expressions.

A common initial denominator is obtained by multiplying the input denominators. Its bit length, and the bit lengths of the initial numerators, are $\mathcal O(L)$.

If fractions are not reduced, the denominator satisfies
$$ D_t=V^{2^t-1}D_0^{2^t}. $$
The recurrences add constantly many arithmetic gates per update. Binary input integers can be constructed from $1$ using $\mathcal O(L)$ additions and multiplications. Thus every state through time $H$ has a division-free integer circuit with $\mathcal O_\epsilon(L)$ gates.

The problem $\mathrm{PosSLP}$ asks whether the integer computed by such a circuit is strictly positive. This is its standard definition in [Allender et al., *On the Complexity of Numerical Analysis*](https://people.cs.rutgers.edu/~allender/papers/slp.pdf).

3.3. One sign query suffices for decision

First test initial membership in polynomial time.

  • If the initial state belongs to the target, answer yes.
  • Otherwise, if $K=0$, answer no.
  • If $K\geq H$, answer yes by the entry bound.

In the remaining case,
$$ 1\leq K<H, $$
and the initial state is outside the target.

By persistence, membership at times $1,\ldots,K$ is monotone as a Boolean predicate. Consequently,
$$ \exists t\in[0,K]:d_t\in\Delta_\epsilon \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_K\geq h. $$
Build the circuit computing
$$ J_K=W(A_K-B_K)^2-ZD_K^2. $$
Because $J_K$ is an integer,
$$ J_K\geq0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad J_K+1>0. $$
A single $\mathrm{PosSLP}$ instance therefore decides the inclusive comparison. For the preprocessing cases, output a constant positive or zero circuit as appropriate.

Proved conclusion: the exact decision problem has a polynomial-time many-one reduction to $\mathrm{PosSLP}$.

For a successful input whose initial state is outside the target, binary search on the monotone predicate over
$$ 1\leq t\leq\min\{K,H\} $$
finds the least entry time using $\mathcal O(\log(H+1))$ $\mathrm{PosSLP}$ queries. This does not provide an ordinary polynomial-time sign algorithm.

4. A bounded-cancellation lemma

The following arithmetic fact controls the lengths of reduced rational representations.

Lemma. Suppose $A,B,Q$ are nonnegative integers with
$$ \gcd(A,B,Q)=1, \qquad D=A+B+Q>0. $$
Form $\widehat A,\widehat B,\widehat Q$ by the integer update above, and let
$$ e=\gcd(\widehat A,\widehat B,\widehat Q). $$
Then
$$ e\mid9VC^2. $$

Proof. The coordinate sum identity gives
$$ \widehat A+\widehat B+\widehat Q=VD^2. $$
In particular, $e>0$.

Fix a prime $\ell$. For a nonzero integer $x$, let $v_\ell(x)$ be the exponent of $\ell$ in $x$, and put $v_\ell(0)=+\infty$. Set
$$ k=v_\ell(e), \quad v=v_\ell(V), \quad z=v_\ell(C), \quad \delta=v_\ell(D), \quad r=v_\ell(3). $$
The coordinate sum implies
$$ k\leq v+2\delta. $$

We claim that
$$ \min\{k,\delta\}\leq z+r. $$
Suppose otherwise. From
$$ CA(Q-B)=\widehat A-\bigl((V-P)A+PQ\bigr)D $$
and the elementary valuation inequality for a sum, we obtain
$$ v_\ell(A(Q-B))\geq\min\{k,\delta\}-z>r. $$
Similarly,
$$ v_\ell(B(Q-A))>r. $$

Define
$$ U=A(A+2B)=AD-A(Q-B), \qquad T=B(2A+B)=BD-B(Q-A). $$
Since $\delta>z+r$, both $U$ and $T$ have valuation strictly greater than $r$.

The polynomial identities
$$ \begin{aligned} 3A^3&=(3A+2B)U-4AT,\\ 3B^3&=(2A+3B)T-4BU \end{aligned} $$
follow by expansion.

Also $\delta>0$. If both $A$ and $B$ were divisible by $\ell$, then
$$ Q=D-A-B $$
would be divisible by $\ell$, contradicting primitivity. Thus at least one of $A,B$ is an $\ell$-adic unit.

If $A$ is a unit, the first identity has left-hand valuation exactly $r$, whereas every term on the right has valuation greater than $r$, a contradiction. If $B$ is a unit, the second identity gives the same contradiction. This proves the claim.

Put $M=z+r$. If $k\leq M$, then $k\leq v+2M$. If $k>M$, the claim forces $\delta\leq M$, and again
$$ k\leq v+2\delta\leq v+2M. $$
Thus
$$ v_\ell(e)\leq v_\ell(V)+2v_\ell(C)+2v_\ell(3) =v_\ell(9VC^2). $$
This holds for every prime $\ell$, proving the divisibility. $\square$

5. Exact algorithms and output-sensitive construction

5.1. Primitive denominators

Represent each state by its primitive integer triple:
$$ \mu_t=\frac{(A_t,B_t,Q_t)}{D_t}, \qquad \gcd(A_t,B_t,Q_t)=1, \qquad D_t=A_t+B_t+Q_t. $$

The integer $D_t$ is the least common denominator of the coordinates. Indeed, if $E$ is another integer common denominator, then $D_t$ divides each of
$$ EA_t,\quad EB_t,\quad EQ_t. $$
An integer linear combination of $A_t,B_t,Q_t$ equals $1$, so $D_t$ divides $E$.

After computing the unreduced update, divide its three numerators and denominator by $e_t$. The lemma yields
$$ D_{t+1}=\frac{VD_t^2}{e_t} \geq\frac{D_t^2}{9C^2}. $$
Put
$$ B_\epsilon=\log_2(9C^2). $$
Then
$$ \log_2D_{t+1}\geq2\log_2D_t-B_\epsilon. $$

5.2. Polynomial time in the actual successful output length

Suppose the first entry time is $R\leq K$, and let $S$ be the total bit length of the returned time and reduced coordinate fractions.

The product of the three output denominators is a common denominator, so
$$ \log_2D_R\leq S. $$
Applying the preceding denominator inequality backwards gives, for every $0\leq i\leq R$,
$$ \begin{aligned} \log_2D_i &\leq2^{-(R-i)}\log_2D_R +B_\epsilon\bigl(1-2^{-(R-i)}\bigr)\\ &\leq S+B_\epsilon. \end{aligned} $$
Because the state lies in the simplex, its primitive numerators are at most $D_i$. The unreduced next-state integers have $\mathcal O_\epsilon(S+1)$ bits as well.

An exact algorithm can therefore scan successive states, reduce after every update, test membership, and stop at the first entry. Using school multiplication and binary gcd, a conservative bound on successful inputs is
$$ \mathcal O_\epsilon\!\left( m\tau+L^3+(R+1)(S+1)^2+S \right). $$
Since $R=\mathcal O_\epsilon(L)$, this is polynomial in $L$ and $S$.

This establishes an output-sensitive bound for successful state construction. It does not establish polynomial-time rejection of no-instances.

5.3. Unconditional exponential-time algorithm

Let
$$ T=\min\{K,H\}. $$
Scan the states in increasing order through time $T$, stopping at the first successful comparison. If none succeeds, answer no.

Even without cancellation, every integer through time $t$ has
$$ \mathcal O_\epsilon((L+1)2^t) $$
bits. Reducing after every update cannot increase this bound. Summing the arithmetic costs gives
$$ \mathcal O_\epsilon\!\left( m\tau+L^3+(L+1)^2\,4^T+S \right), $$
where $S=0$ for a no-instance or a decision-only computation. Because $T=\mathcal O_\epsilon(L)$, this is
$$ 2^{\mathcal O_\epsilon(L)}. $$

For decision alone, the branch $K\geq H$ is answered during polynomial preprocessing.

The full task, including output, is polynomial-time solvable on either of these subclasses:

  • $K=\mathcal O(\log L)$.
  • $\eta\geq L^{-A}$ for a fixed constant $A$.

In the second case, $H=\mathcal O_{\epsilon,A}(\log L)$.

All these bounds concern the stipulated setting $m=2$ and $\tau\leq3$. The $m\tau$ term records support initialization; no variable-$m$ classification is claimed.

6. Exponentially long required output

The bounded-cancellation lemma gives a shorter proof of exponential output length, without requiring a valuation induction for a particular prime.

Fix any admissible rational $\epsilon$. For sufficiently large integers $s$, choose
$$ a_0=\frac12+2^{-s}, \qquad b_0=\frac12-2^{1-s}, \qquad q_0=2^{-s}, $$
and set
$$ \eta=d_0=3\cdot2^{-s}, \qquad K=H. $$
These coordinates are positive and sum to one. Their primitive common denominator is
$$ D_0=2^s, $$
because the numerator of $q_0$ is $1$.

Let $R$ be the first entry time. Since $q_t\leq1$,
$$ d_{t+1}\leq Gd_t, \qquad G=f+c=2-4p>1. $$
For sufficiently large $s$, we have $d_0<h$, so entry requires
$$ h\leq d_R\leq3\cdot2^{-s}G^R. $$
Consequently,
$$ R\geq \frac{s\log2+\log(h/3)}{\log G} =\Omega_\epsilon(s). $$
The entry bound also gives $R=\mathcal O_\epsilon(s)$.

Iterating the denominator inequality yields
$$ \log_2D_R \geq(s-B_\epsilon)2^R+B_\epsilon =2^{\Omega_\epsilon(s)}. $$
The output length satisfies $S\geq\log_2D_R$.

The masses and $\eta$ use $\Theta(s)$ bits, whereas $K=H=\mathcal O_\epsilon(s)$ uses only $\mathcal O_\epsilon(\log s)$ bits. Thus the total input length is
$$ L_s=\Theta_\epsilon(s), $$
and the required output satisfies
$$ S\geq2^{\Omega_\epsilon(L_s)}. $$

Proved conclusion: for every fixed admissible rational $\epsilon$, the full explicit-output task cannot run in time polynomial in $L$ alone. This output lower bound does not imply decision hardness.

7. Finite stochastic dynamics

The stochastic assertion follows from Theorem 1 of [d’Amore, Clementi, and Natale, *Phase Transition of a Non-Linear Opinion Dynamics with Noisy Interactions*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07423).

Its hypotheses match the stated finite model: two opinions and an undecided state; independent synchronous uniform sampling, including possible self-sampling; observation probabilities $1-2p,p,p$; fixed $p=1/6-\epsilon$ with $\epsilon\in(0,1/6)$; and initial signed count bias at least $\gamma\sqrt{n\log n}$ for fixed $\gamma>0$.

Writing the count bias as $D_t^{(n)}=A_t-B_t$, the theorem gives constants
$$ C_0,C_1,\lambda,\kappa>0, $$
depending on the fixed parameters, such that for sufficiently large $n$, with probability at least $1-n^{-\kappa}$, there is an integer $R_n\leq C_0\log n$ satisfying
$$ D_t^{(n)}\in n\Delta_\epsilon \qquad \text{for every } R_n\leq t\leq R_n+\lfloor C_1n^\lambda\rfloor. $$
Thus entry occurs within $\mathcal O(\log n)$ rounds and persists for polynomially many rounds with high probability.

The normalized one-round conditional expectation is $F_p$, but nonlinear iteration does not commute with expectation. This stochastic theorem therefore supplies no exact deterministic bit-complexity classification.

8. What remains open

The deterministic reachability question has been reduced to one exact comparison of a polynomial-size integer circuit from a restricted family. Persistence removes the need to check every time, and bounded cancellation settles output-sensitive construction on yes-instances.

The unresolved issue is the complexity of deciding those circuit signs without expanding exponentially long integers. No polynomial-time sign algorithm or valid $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness reduction is proved here.
