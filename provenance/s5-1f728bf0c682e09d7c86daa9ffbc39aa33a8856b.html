The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) and a valid ranking pattern \(\sigma\) on all \(A\subseteq[m]\) with \(|A|\ge2\), find a finite-support distribution of binary-rational masses \(\mu_\pi\ge0\) over complete rankings \(\pi\in\Pi_m\), summing to \(1\), such that for every \(A\subseteq[m]\) and distinct \(i,j\in A\), \(\sigma(A,i)<\sigma(A,j)\) if and only if \(\alpha_i^\mu(A)>\alpha_j^\mu(A)\), and \(\sigma(A,i)=\sigma(A,j)\) if and only if \(\alpha_i^\mu(A)=\alpha_j^\mu(A)\), where \(\alpha_i^\mu(A)=\sum_{\pi:\,i\text{ is ranked first in }A\text{ by }\pi}\mu_\pi\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge2\), \([m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), and let \(\Pi_m\) be the complete strict rankings of \([m]\). The input explicitly lists \(\sigma(A,i)\) for every \(A\subseteq[m]\) with \(|A|\ge2\) and \(i\in A\), with total binary input length \(L\). Validity means \(\sigma(A,i)\in\{1,\ldots,|A|\}\) and \(\sigma(A,i)=1+|\{j\in A:\sigma(A,j)<\sigma(A,i)\}|\); ties are allowed, and no consistency between different subsets is required.
A society is represented by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary-encoded rational numerator divided by a positive binary-encoded denominator. Here types are rankings in \(\Pi_m\), masses sum to \(1\), and \(\tau\) is the support size, not \(m!\). The society is the output, with no prescribed support bound.
For \(i\in A\), define \(\alpha_i^\mu(A)=\sum_{\pi:\,i\text{ is first in }A\text{ under }\pi}\mu_\pi\). Construct a society satisfying, for every \(|A|\ge2\) and distinct \(i,j\in A\), \(\sigma(A,i)<\sigma(A,j)\iff\alpha_i^\mu(A)>\alpha_j^\mu(A)\) and \(\sigma(A,i)=\sigma(A,j)\iff\alpha_i^\mu(A)=\alpha_j^\mu(A)\). Measure running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and the output mass bit length \(B\); polynomial-time construction means total time polynomial in \(m,L\), including writing the output.

Can such a society always be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

Discrete: every valid ranking pattern, including ties, is realized by a finite nonempty electorate with strict individual rankings. Rational probability distributions realizing every pattern also exist; the verified existence and construction results give no polynomial-time classification for the explicit support encoding. [De Santis–Spizzichino (2023)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10203-023-00393-2)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified m at least 2 and defined validity using competition ranks, as in the discrete definition.
  • Chose an explicit table encoding for the ranking pattern and defined L as its total binary input length.
  • Interpreted binary rational as a rational with binary-encoded numerator and denominator, rather than restricting denominators to powers of two.
  • Applied the support convention to the constructed output society; tau and output mass bit length B are output quantities, with no imposed support bound.
  • Measured polynomial-time construction against input length, including output writing, rather than allowing an unrestricted polynomial dependence on output size.
  • Restricted concordance requirements to subsets of size at least two, the domain on which the ranking pattern is defined.

Result

The search problem is deterministically polynomial-time solvable under the stated explicit encoding.

We give a direct construction with support size \(\tau\le m2^{m-1}\) and \(O(m\log m)\) bits per output mass. The proof handles ties exactly and does not require the quoted existence theorem.

1. Prescribe nearby winning probabilities

Extend the input to singletons by setting \(\sigma(\{i\},i)=1\). For every nonempty \(A\subseteq[m]\), put \(k=|A|\) and define the integer
\[ c_i(A)=\sum_{j\in A}\sigma(A,j)-k\sigma(A,i),\qquad i\in A. \]

Then
\[ \sum_{i\in A}c_i(A)=0, \qquad |c_i(A)|\le k(k-1)\le m^2. \]

Indeed, the first identity follows by summing the definition, and the bound follows because every rank lies between \(1\) and \(k\).

Choose the common denominator
\[ D=2^{m+1}m^2m! \]
and prescribe
\[ p_i(A)=\frac1{|A|}+\frac{c_i(A)}D. \]

These numbers sum to \(1\) on each nonempty subset. Moreover,
\[ p_i(A)-p_j(A) =\frac{|A|}{D}\bigl(\sigma(A,j)-\sigma(A,i)\bigr). \]

Consequently, the prescribed probabilities have exactly the required strict comparisons and equalities. It remains to realize them by a probability distribution over strict rankings.

2. Encode rankings as paths on the subset lattice

Consider the directed graph whose vertices are all subsets of \([m]\), with an edge
\[ (S,i):S\longrightarrow S\setminus\{i\} \]
for each nonempty \(S\) and each \(i\in S\).

A path from \([m]\) to \(\varnothing\) removes every candidate exactly once. Its removal sequence is therefore a strict ranking, listed from most preferred to least preferred.

For a probability distribution over such paths, let \(f(S,i)\) denote the probability that the path uses edge \((S,i)\). Its winning probabilities satisfy
\[ \alpha_i(A)=\sum_{S\supseteq A}f(S,i). \]

To justify this identity, consider the unique edge at which a particular path removes \(i\). The remaining set at that moment contains \(A\) exactly when no other member of \(A\) has already been removed. This is precisely the condition that \(i\) is first in \(A\).

We now construct suitable edge probabilities directly.

3. Invert the winning probabilities and prove flow conservation

For every edge \((S,i)\), define
\[ f(S,i)=\sum_{A\supseteq S}(-1)^{|A|-|S|}p_i(A). \]

This formula inverts the preceding relation. Explicitly,
\[ \sum_{S\supseteq A}f(S,i) = \sum_{T\supseteq A}p_i(T) \sum_{A\subseteq S\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T|-|S|} = p_i(A). \]

The inner sum equals \((1-1)^{|T\setminus A|}\), so it vanishes unless \(T=A\), when it equals \(1\).

For every nonempty \(S\), let
\[ \operatorname{out}(S)=\sum_{i\in S}f(S,i), \qquad \operatorname{in}(S)=\sum_{j\notin S}f(S\cup\{j\},j). \]

Expanding both expressions and collecting terms indexed by \(A\supseteq S\) gives
\[ \operatorname{out}(S)-\operatorname{in}(S) = \sum_{A\supseteq S}(-1)^{|A|-|S|} \sum_{i\in A}p_i(A). \]

Here the outgoing expression contributes candidates in \(S\). Subtracting the incoming expression contributes candidates in \(A\setminus S\) with the same sign, because its exponent is smaller by one.

Since the prescribed probabilities sum to \(1\),
\[ \operatorname{out}(S)-\operatorname{in}(S) = \sum_{A\supseteq S}(-1)^{|A|-|S|} = \begin{cases} 1,&S=[m],\\ 0,&\varnothing\ne S\subsetneq[m]. \end{cases} \]

Thus \(f\) satisfies the conservation equations for a unit flow, provided its edge values are nonnegative.

4. Prove every edge value is positive

First consider the baseline probabilities \(p_i^0(A)=1/|A|\). They arise from a uniformly random strict ranking.

For \(s=|S|\), the corresponding edge probability is
\[ f^0(S,i)=\frac{(m-s)!(s-1)!}{m!}. \]

Indeed, a ranking uses edge \((S,i)\) exactly when its first \(m-s\) positions contain \([m]\setminus S\), its next position contains \(i\), and its final positions contain \(S\setminus\{i\}\). There are \((m-s)!(s-1)!\) such rankings.

By the inversion identity already proved, \(f^0\) is the inverse transform of \(p^0\). Hence
\[ f(S,i)-f^0(S,i) = \frac1D\sum_{A\supseteq S}(-1)^{|A|-|S|}c_i(A). \]

There are \(2^{m-|S|}\) terms, each of absolute value at most \(m^2\). Therefore
\[ |f(S,i)-f^0(S,i)| \le\frac{m^2 2^{m-|S|}}D \le\frac1{2m!}. \]

Both factorials in the numerator defining \(f^0(S,i)\) are at least \(1\), so
\[ f^0(S,i)\ge\frac1{m!}. \]

It follows that
\[ f(S,i)\ge\frac1{2m!}>0. \]

We have therefore constructed a positive unit flow whose winning probabilities are exactly the prescribed \(p_i(A)\).

5. Construct the explicitly supported society

Every edge flow is an integer multiple of \(1/D\). This follows because \(|A|\) divides \(m!\), so
\[ Dp_i(A)=\frac{D}{|A|}+c_i(A) \]
is an integer. Consequently,
\[ h(S,i)=Df(S,i) \]
is a positive integer flow of value \(D\).

Decompose this integer flow as follows:

  • If the residual flow has positive value, choose a path from \([m]\) to \(\varnothing\) using positive residual edges.
  • Let \(w\) be the smallest residual value on that path.
  • Record the path’s ranking with mass \(w/D\), and subtract \(w\) from every edge of the path.
  • Repeat until the residual source value is zero.

A positive path always exists while the source value is positive. After entering any nonempty internal vertex along a positive edge, conservation implies that some outgoing edge is positive. Each step decreases the subset size, so the path reaches \(\varnothing\).

Subtracting a path preserves conservation at internal vertices and preserves nonnegativity. Each iteration makes at least one previously positive edge zero; zero edges cannot become positive again. Thus the number of iterations is at most the number of edges,
\[ E=\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[m]}|S|=m2^{m-1}. \]

When the source value becomes zero, every residual edge is zero. Otherwise, take a nonempty set \(S\) of largest cardinality having a positive outgoing residual edge. It cannot be the source. Conservation then supplies a positive incoming edge from a strictly larger set, contradicting maximality.

The recorded weights therefore sum to \(D\) and reproduce every original edge flow. They define a society with positive rational masses summing to \(1\). The support has at most \(E\) rankings. In fact, recorded paths are distinct: each recorded path contains an edge exhausted in that iteration, which no later path can use.

Finally, the path identity and inversion give
\[ \alpha_i^\mu(A)=\sum_{S\supseteq A}f(S,i)=p_i(A). \]

By the difference formula in Section 1, this society satisfies both required equivalences, including all ties.

6. Polynomial running time and output length

The input contains
\[ N=\sum_{|A|\ge2}|A|=m2^{m-1}-m=E-m \]
listed ranking values. Each requires at least one input bit, so \(N\le L\), and therefore \(E\le L+m\).

The construction uses only polynomially many integer operations:

  • Computing all centered scores and prescribed numerators takes polynomial time in \(m,L\).
  • Directly evaluating all inverse sums takes \(m3^{m-1}\) summands. For each candidate, every other candidate lies either outside \(A\), in \(A\setminus S\), or in \(S\). Moreover,

\[ m3^{m-1}\le m4^{m-1}=\frac{E^2}{m}\le E^2. \]

  • The decomposition performs at most \(E\) iterations. Each path has \(m\) edges and can be found by inspecting at most \(m\) outgoing candidates at each step.

The common denominator satisfies
\[ \log_2 D=m+1+2\log_2m+\log_2(m!)=O(m\log m). \]

Every output numerator \(w\) is at most \(D\), so each mass requires \(O(m\log m)\) bits. Intermediate inverse sums also have \(O(m\log m)\) bits: they sum at most \(2^m\) integers of magnitude at most \(D+m^2\).

Writing a ranking requires \(O(m\log m)\) bits. Thus the total output length, including all masses, is
\[ O(\tau m\log m)\subseteq O((L+m)m\log m). \]

All computation and output writing therefore take time polynomial in \(m,L\). This settles the stated search problem.
