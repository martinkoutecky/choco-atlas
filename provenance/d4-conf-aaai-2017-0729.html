The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite role types \(A\), item types \(O\), rational role masses \(\mu_a\), item supplies \(\beta_o\), an ordered template of blocks \((a_q,\lambda_q)\), fixed rankings for nonstrategic roles, a common additive utility \(u_h\) for strategic cohort \(h\), and threshold \(\theta\), does there exist a strict report \(\widehat{\succ}_h\) such that sequential highest-ranked allocation of block mass, with marginal blocks split across item types, gives \(h\) average utility at least \(\theta\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(A\) of \(\tau\) role types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), normalized by \(\sum_{a\in A}\mu_a=1\). Here \(\tau=|A|\) is the support size, not the number of possible preference types. There are \(m\geq1\) divisible item types \(O\), each with positive binary-rational supply \(\beta_o\).
The input includes a strategic role \(h\in A\), complete strict rankings over \(O\) for every other role, pairwise distinct positive binary-rational utility densities \(u_h(o)\), and a binary-rational threshold \(\theta\). Utilities are additive; the truthful ranking of \(h\) is induced by \(u_h\).
An explicitly listed ordered template \((a_q,\lambda_q)_{q=1}^{Q}\) specifies \(a_q\in A\) and binary-rational block masses \(0<\lambda_q\leq\mu_{a_q}\), with \(\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lambda_q=\sum_{o\in O}\beta_o\). Roles may recur, and their cumulative block mass need not equal their population mass.
In block \(q\), allocate exactly \(\lambda_q\) item mass to role \(a_q\): take as much as possible from its highest-ranked item type with remaining supply, then continue down its ranking until the block is filled. Every block of \(h\) uses the same chosen complete strict report \(\widehat{\succ}_h\); all other reports and the template are fixed.
Let \(x_{h,o}\) be the total mass of item type \(o\) allocated to \(h\). Its average utility is \(\mu_h^{-1}\sum_{o\in O}u_h(o)x_{h,o}\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,Q,L\).

Is deciding whether some report \(\widehat{\succ}_h\) gives \(\mu_h^{-1}\sum_{o\in O}u_h(o)x_{h,o}\geq\theta\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,Q,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For indivisible items, the utility-threshold problem with one manipulating agent and an unrestricted number of other agents is NP-complete ([Aziz et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/10586/10445)); it is polynomial-time solvable for each fixed number of agents ([Xiao and Ling](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06747)).
No result settling this exact distribution-and-block problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized positive role masses to sum to one because the society is a distribution; retained arbitrary positive item supplies.
  • Interpreted the strategic cohort as one designated role h whose members share a single report across all its blocks.
  • Interpreted a block mass as participating population mass receiving one unit of item mass per unit of participation, so each block mass is at most its role mass; allowed repeated participation without a cumulative population-mass constraint, preserving repeated turns.
  • Required total block mass to equal total supply, reflecting complete allocation and ensuring every block can be filled.
  • Specified complete strict rankings and a fixed report throughout the sequence; block splitting exhausts preferred supplies in order and involves no discretionary split or tie-breaking.
  • Retained strictly positive, pairwise distinct utilities consistent with a strict truthful ranking, and encoded utility densities and the threshold as binary rationals.
  • Defined average utility as the cohort's total additive utility divided by its population mass, rather than by its cumulative allocated mass.
  • Made the template explicit and introduced its length Q and the total input bit length L for running-time statements.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete when the support size \(\tau\) is part of the input. Hardness holds even when \(Q=m\) and every item supply and every block mass equals the same dyadic rational.

The proof embeds indivisible sequential allocation exactly: although the items are divisible, the constructed instances never split an item type.

1. The source problem

We use the following established result: given indivisible items, fixed complete strict rankings of the other agents, an explicitly specified picking sequence, positive integer utilities for one manipulator, and an integer threshold \(T\), deciding whether a single complete strict report attains utility at least \(T\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. The utilities are consistent with a strict truthful ranking and hence pairwise distinct. This is the decision problem proved hard in [Aziz et al., Theorem 2](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/10586/10445).

Write the agents as \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), with manipulator \(1\), and the items as \(O\), where \(|O|=m\). Denote the utilities by \(v(o)\).

We may assume \(n\geq2\): an additional nonmanipulating agent with no turns changes no allocation. We may also assume the picking sequence has exactly \(m\) turns. If a source instance stops earlier, append turns of a nonmanipulating agent until all items are allocated. These appended turns do not change the manipulator’s bundle.

Let the resulting sequence be \((i_1,\ldots,i_m)\).

2. Polynomial reduction

Let \(K\) be the least power of two satisfying \(K\geq n\), and put \(c=1/K\).

Create one role for each source agent, so \(A=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), \(\tau=n\), and \(h=1\). Define population masses by

\(\displaystyle \mu_i=c\quad(1\leq i<n),\qquad \mu_n=1-(n-1)c.\)

These are positive dyadic rationals. Indeed,

\(\displaystyle \mu_n=\frac{K-n+1}{K}\geq\frac1K=c,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_i=1.\)

In particular, \(\mu_h=c\).

For every item type set \(\beta_o=c\). Copy the rankings of all nonmanipulating agents, and set \(u_h(o)=v(o)\). These densities are positive, pairwise distinct integers, so they satisfy the target input requirements.

Set \(Q=m\) and use the template

\(\displaystyle (a_q,\lambda_q)=(i_q,c)\qquad(1\leq q\leq m).\)

Every block satisfies \(0<\lambda_q=c\leq\mu_{a_q}\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lambda_q=mc=\sum_{o\in O}\beta_o.\)

Finally, set \(\theta=T\). Thus every target input condition holds. Repeated roles cause no difficulty because the problem imposes no constraint equating cumulative block mass with population mass.

If the source input has bit length \(S\), the constructed input has length \(O(S+(n+m)\log n)\): the copied data retain their encodings, and each new mass uses \(O(\log n)\) bits. The construction therefore takes polynomial time.

3. Exact allocation equivalence

Fix an arbitrary complete strict report of the manipulator, and use that same report in both instances.

We prove by induction on \(q\) that, after turn or block \(q\), an item is unallocated in the source instance exactly when its target supply remains \(c\); otherwise its target supply is zero. Whenever an item is allocated, its recipient is identical in both instances.

Before the first turn, every source item is available and every target supply equals \(c\).

Suppose the statement holds before turn \(q\). There are \(m-q+1\) available source items, so at least one remains. Agent \(i_q\) chooses its highest-ranked available item, say \(o\).

By the induction hypothesis, the item types with positive target supply are exactly the available source items. Role \(a_q=i_q\) uses the same ranking, so it also selects \(o\). Its remaining supply is \(c\), equal to the block mass \(\lambda_q\). Consequently, the block takes all of \(o\), fills immediately, and takes no other item type. This establishes the induction step.

Let \(B\) be the manipulator’s source bundle. The induction gives

\(\displaystyle x_{h,o}=\begin{cases}c,&o\in B,\\0,&o\notin B.\end{cases}\)

Since \(\mu_h=c\), its target average utility is exactly

\(\displaystyle \mu_h^{-1}\sum_{o\in O}u_h(o)x_{h,o}=\frac1c\sum_{o\in B}v(o)c=\sum_{o\in B}v(o).\)

This equality holds for every complete strict report. Hence some source report attains utility at least \(T\) if and only if some target report attains average utility at least \(\theta\). The reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

4. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) and running times

A certificate is a permutation of the \(m\) item types, encoding the strategic report in \(O(m\log(m+1))\) bits.

For exact verification, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all numerical input fields. Because their total encoding length is at most \(L\), \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits. Multiplying all numerical fields by \(D\) produces integers of \(O(L)\) bits.

Simulate each block by scanning its role’s ranking and repeatedly allocating the minimum of the remaining block mass and remaining item supply. All allocation arithmetic then uses integers. Every block fills: at its start, total remaining supply equals the sum of its mass and all future block masses, and therefore is at least its own mass.

Let \(X_o=Dx_{h,o}\), \(U_o=Du_h(o)\), \(M_h=D\mu_h\), and \(\Theta=D\theta\). Since \(\mu_h>0\), the threshold test is exactly

\(\displaystyle \sum_{o\in O}U_oX_o\geq M_h\Theta.\)

All intermediate integers have \(O(L)\) bits: allocations are bounded by input supplies, and summing at most \(m\) terms adds only \(O(\log m)\) bits.

Scanning at most \(m\) entries per block, including input and certificate checks, gives a conservative verification bound of \(O((\tau+Q)mL^2)\) bit operations using elementary integer arithmetic. Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Enumerating all reports gives an exact algorithm running in \(O(m!(\tau+Q)mL^2)\) bit operations. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,Q,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
