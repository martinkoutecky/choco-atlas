Theorem 1 (Social efficiency) — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\subseteq[0,1]\) of valuation types with rational masses \(\mu_t\geq 0\) summing to \(1\), and slot classes \(\ell=1,\ldots,q\) with weights \(w_\ell\) and per-capita supplies \(a_\ell\geq 0\), compute an assignment \(x_{t\ell}\geq 0\) maximizing \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{\ell=1}^q \theta_t w_\ell x_{t\ell}\), subject to \(\sum_\ell x_{t\ell}\leq\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{t\ell}=a_\ell\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]\) of \(\tau\) distinct valuation types, each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=q\geq1\) slot classes with binary rational weights \(w_1\geq\cdots\geq w_q\geq0\) and binary rational per-capita supplies \(a_\ell\geq0\), where \(0<\sum_{\ell=1}^q a_\ell<1\).
A type \(t\) has valuation \(\theta_t=t\); receiving a class \(\ell\) slot gives utility \(t w_\ell\), and receiving no slot gives zero utility.
An assignment \(x_{t\ell}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}\) specifies the mass of type \(t\) receiving class \(\ell\); type masses may be split, and each individual receives at most one slot.
Compute an assignment maximizing \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{\ell=1}^q t w_\ell x_{t\ell}\), subject to \(\sum_{\ell=1}^q x_{t\ell}\leq\mu_t\) for every \(t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}x_{t\ell}=a_\ell\) for every \(\ell\). Any maximizer is acceptable when there are ties.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

Is this exact welfare maximization problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

For a finite list of agents and \(1\leq k<n\) slots, assigning the highest valuations to slots in decreasing weight order maximizes social welfare; this gives a polynomial-time allocation algorithm ([social-efficiency result](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0155962)).
No separate complexity result for the distributional problem above was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required rational valuation types, weights, and supplies, all encoded in binary, to specify a finite computational input.
  • Identified the undefined valuation θ_t with the support value t and identified m with the number q of slot classes.
  • Restricted weights to be nonnegative: the published version still omits their sign, but negative slot weights invalidate the intended discrete social-efficiency claim.
  • Required positive total supply strictly below unit population mass, carrying over the discrete condition 1 ≤ k < n; individual zero-supply classes remain allowed.
  • Removed zero-mass types from the support, as required by the fixed society convention.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The exact welfare maximization problem is polynomial-time solvable. Assign the highest-valued available mass to slot classes in decreasing weight order, splitting a type’s mass whenever necessary. This extends the finite-agent ordering rule in the [cited paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0155962); the distributional case is proved directly below.

Construction and feasibility. Sort the support as \(t_1>\cdots>t_\tau\), and write \(\mu_i=\mu_{t_i}\). Define cumulative masses and supplies by

\(B_0=A_0=0,\qquad B_i=\sum_{h=1}^{i}\mu_h,\qquad A_\ell=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}a_r.\)

Thus \(B_\tau=1\) and \(0<A_m<1\). Partition the population mass interval \([0,1)\) into type intervals \(I_i=[B_{i-1},B_i)\). Give class \(\ell\) to the mass interval \(J_\ell=[A_{\ell-1},A_\ell)\). Explicitly, output

\(x^*_{t_i\ell}=\max\{0,\min(B_i,A_\ell)-\max(B_{i-1},A_{\ell-1})\}.\)

This is the length of \(I_i\cap J_\ell\).

Because the type intervals partition \([0,1)\), their intersections with \(J_\ell\) have total length \(a_\ell\). Hence every class supply is met exactly. Because the class intervals are disjoint, their intersections with \(I_i\) have total length at most \(\mu_i\). Thus every type-capacity constraint holds. The remaining interval \([A_m,1)\) receives no slot.

Proof of optimality. Let \(x\) be any feasible assignment. For each prefix of classes \(1,\ldots,k\), define

\(y_i^{(k)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}x_{t_i\ell},\qquad Y_j^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}y_i^{(k)}.\)

Feasibility gives \(0\leq y_i^{(k)}\leq\mu_i\) and \(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}y_i^{(k)}=A_k\). Consequently,

\(Y_j^{(k)}\leq B_j\quad\text{and}\quad Y_j^{(k)}\leq A_k,\)

so \(Y_j^{(k)}\leq\min(B_j,A_k)\).

For the constructed assignment, the first \(k\) class intervals together equal \([0,A_k)\), while the first \(j\) type intervals together equal \([0,B_j)\). Therefore its corresponding cumulative mass satisfies

\(Y_j^{*,(k)}=\min(B_j,A_k).\)

Set \(t_{\tau+1}=0\). Expanding and collecting coefficients gives the identity

\(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}t_i y_i^{(k)} =\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}(t_j-t_{j+1})Y_j^{(k)}.\)

Indeed, the coefficient of \(y_i^{(k)}\) on the right is \(\sum_{j=i}^{\tau}(t_j-t_{j+1})=t_i\). All differences \(t_j-t_{j+1}\) are nonnegative. The cumulative bounds therefore imply

\(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}t_i y_i^{(k)} \leq\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}(t_j-t_{j+1})\min(B_j,A_k) =\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}t_i y_i^{*,(k)}.\)

Thus the constructed assignment maximizes the total valuation assigned to every prefix of classes simultaneously.

Now set \(w_{m+1}=0\). A second coefficient expansion yields

\(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}t_iw_\ell x_{t_i\ell} =\sum_{k=1}^{m}(w_k-w_{k+1})\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}t_i y_i^{(k)}.\)

To verify this, the coefficient of \(t_i x_{t_i\ell}\) on the right is \(\sum_{k=\ell}^{m}(w_k-w_{k+1})=w_\ell\). Every coefficient \(w_k-w_{k+1}\) is nonnegative. Applying the preceding prefix inequality for each \(k\) proves that the welfare of \(x\) is at most the welfare of \(x^*\).

This argument includes equal weights, zero weights, zero supplies, and a type with valuation zero.

Algorithm and exact running time. After sorting the types, compute the nonzero interval intersections with a two-pointer sweep. Equivalently, maintain the remaining mass of the current type and the remaining supply of the current class; assign their minimum, subtract it from both, and advance every exhausted pointer. Skip zero-supply classes.

Each positive assignment exhausts a type or a class. There are at most \(\tau+m-1\) positive entries: if \(h\) type intervals intersect \([0,A_m)\) and \(p\) classes have positive supply, their combined partition has at most \(h+p-1\leq\tau+m-1\) intervals.

The algorithm uses \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1)+m+\tau)\) arithmetic operations and comparisons. These operations can be performed exactly with polynomially bounded bit lengths:

  • Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all masses and supplies. Their total encoding length is at most \(L\), so \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits.
  • Replace masses and supplies by the integers \(D\mu_i\) and \(Da_\ell\). These are at most \(D\), since each mass and supply is at most one. The sweep uses integer comparisons and subtractions, and outputs each positive entry as its integer mass divided by \(D\).
  • Compare valuation types exactly by cross multiplication of their input numerators and denominators.

Using elementary integer arithmetic, each multiplication or division involved costs \(O(L^2)\) bit operations. Constructing the scaled data and performing the sort and sweep therefore costs

\(O\bigl((\tau\log(\tau+1)+m+\tau)L^2\bigr)\)

bit operations with sparse output. If all \(m\tau\) entries must be explicitly written, a conservative bound is

\(O\bigl((\tau\log(\tau+1)+m+\tau)L^2+m\tau L\bigr).\)

Both bounds are polynomial in the binary input length. No expansion into individual agents is required.
