The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite \(K\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\), rational family masses \(\mu\in\Delta(K)\), and rational capacity \(\kappa>0\), compute an exactly represented finite-support lottery \(P\) over \(Z(\mu,\kappa)=\{z:0\le z_k\le\mu_k,\ \sum_{k\in K}kz_k\le\kappa\}\), implemented by exchangeably selecting whole families from each nonatomic type class. The induced mechanism must lexicographically maximize type admission rates \(q_k=\mathbb{E}_P[z_k]/\mu_k\), be Pareto-optimal and envy-free, resist every measurable positive-mass hybrid adjunctive split/merge report, and achieve \(u(P)\ge\frac{1}{2}u^*(\mu,\kappa)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here \(\varnothing\ne K\subset\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(m=\max K\), \(\mu_k>0\), \(\sum_k\mu_k=1\), and \(\kappa>0\) is rational. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; measure running times in \(m,\tau,L\), with polynomial time meaning polynomial in \(\tau,L\), since \(m\) is binary encoded.
Type \(k\) consists of a nonatomic family space of measure \(\mu_k\); each family has \(k\) indivisible members and succeeds only when all its members are admitted. Capacity is measured in individual mass, so individual families have zero capacity cost and there is no restriction \(k\le\kappa\).
An outcome selects measurable sets of whole families, with selected type masses \(z\in Z(\mu,\kappa)=\{z:0\le z_k\le\mu_k,\ \sum_k kz_k\le\kappa\}\). A mechanism outputs a finite lottery \(P\) with rational weights and rational outcome vectors, together with a finitely described randomized implementation giving almost every type-\(k\) family admission probability \(q_k=\mathbb E_P[z_k]/\mu_k\). Type anonymity is the intended meaning of exchangeable selection here; correlations are part of the implementation.
Leximin optimality means lexicographically maximizing the vector \((q_k)_{k\in K}\) sorted in nondecreasing order. Require ex-ante Pareto optimality for family admission probabilities and ex-post maximality against adding a positive mass of unselected families.
Envy-freeness means, for almost every pair of families \(A,B\), that \(\Pr[A\subseteq S]\ge\Pr[B\subseteq S\text{ and }(S\setminus B)\cup A\text{ is feasible}]\), where \(S\) is the selected set of individuals.
A hybrid adjunctive report chooses a measurable positive-family-mass coalition \(C\), adds a finite mass of uninterested individuals, and measurably repartitions these and all coalition members into nonempty finite reported families; no individual is duplicated, outsiders remain unchanged, and every new family contains an original coalition member. Reports may both split and merge families and must have finite size support.
After a report, let \(\nu'_j\) be the absolute reported family masses and \(T'=\sum_j\nu'_j\); evaluate the mechanism at \(\mu'_j=\nu'_j/T'\) and \(\kappa'=\kappa/T'\). The mechanism is defined mathematically also for real masses, although computation is required only on rational inputs. A true family's deviating utility is the probability that all its original members are admitted, regardless of their reported groups; hybrid adjunctive group strategyproofness forbids a report that strictly improves this utility for almost every family in \(C\).
Define \(u(P)=\mathbb E_P[\sum_k kz_k]/\kappa\) and \(u^*(\mu,\kappa)=\max_{z\in Z(\mu,\kappa)}\sum_k kz_k/\kappa\).

Is computing such a mechanism, simultaneously satisfying leximin optimality, both Pareto requirements, envy-freeness, hybrid adjunctive group strategyproofness, and \(u(P)\ge\tfrac12u^*(\mu,\kappa)\), polynomial-time solvable in \(\tau,L\), or NP-hard?

For explicitly listed discrete families, IPMAX has all these guarantees and runs in time polynomial in the number of families and numeric capacity. [Arbiv–Aumann](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/20405)
A later discrete result gives an FPTAS for leximin and explicitly notes the unary-capacity qualification of the exact algorithm; no result settling the precise nonatomic mechanism problem above was located. [Hartman et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10395v3#S11.SS2)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted K to the nonempty positive-mass support and interpreted mu as family mass, with total initial family mass one.
  • Interpreted capacity as individual mass. The discrete individual-family fit condition becomes automatic for nonatomic families; imposing k <= kappa would mix units and force every initial family to fit simultaneously.
  • Interpreted lexicographic maximization as leximin of the sorted type admission rates, rather than maximization in a fixed type order.
  • Used the discrete anonymity requirement—equal admission marginals within each size—as the meaning of exchangeable selection, and included the randomized implementation because a lottery over mass vectors alone does not determine joint admission events.
  • Required rational lottery weights and outcome vectors, and a finite description of the implementation.
  • Included both ex-ante and ex-post Pareto optimality; interpreted ex-post additions and coalition improvements modulo null sets.
  • Carried over the feasible-replacement definition of envy and the hybrid restriction that every contrived family contains an original coalition member.
  • Preserved absolute individual mass and capacity across reports, renormalizing reported family masses and capacity together after splitting or merging.
  • Allowed real reported masses with finite size support so that the strategic requirement covers every measurable report; limited the computational requirement to rational inputs.
  • Defined utilization as expected admitted individual mass divided by capacity, and its benchmark as the maximum over feasible outcomes.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(\tau,L\). The mechanism below satisfies all the requested properties and, more strongly, achieves \(u(P)=u^*(\mu,\kappa)\).

The proof uses the intended interpretation of anonymity: equal admission probabilities within each type, with correlations permitted.

1. The uniquely optimal probability vector

Define total individual mass and a common admission probability by

\[ W=\sum_{k\in K}k\mu_k, \qquad p=\min\left\{1,\frac{\kappa}{W}\right\}. \]

For any feasible lottery, linearity of expectation gives

\[ \sum_{k\in K}k\mu_kq_k =\mathbb E_P\left[\sum_{k\in K}kz_k\right] \le\kappa. \]

Consequently, if \(t=\min_{k\in K}q_k\), then \(tW\le\kappa\). Since admission probabilities also satisfy \(t\le1\), every feasible lottery has \(t\le p\).

The vector \(q_k=p\) is feasible: the outcome

\[ z_k=p\mu_k \]

satisfies all coordinate bounds and uses individual mass \(pW=\min\{W,\kappa\}\).

Moreover, this is the unique probability vector attaining minimum probability \(p\). If \(\kappa<W\) and all \(q_k\ge p\), then

\[ 0\le\sum_{k\in K}k\mu_k(q_k-p) \le\kappa-pW=0. \]

Every coefficient \(k\mu_k\) is positive, so every \(q_k=p\). If \(\kappa\ge W\), attaining minimum probability \(p=1\) likewise forces every coordinate to equal \(1\).

Thus \(q_k=p\) is uniquely leximin optimal.

2. A finitely described implementation

Output the one-outcome lottery

\[ P=\delta_z, \qquad z=(p\mu_k)_{k\in K}. \]

Although its outcome vector is deterministic, the implementation randomizes which families realize those selected masses.

For the canonical realization of a support-only input, represent type-\(k\) families by \(x\in[0,1)\), with family measure \(\mu_k\,dx\). Draw one uniform random variable \(U\in[0,1)\). Select the whole family at coordinate \(x\) precisely when

\[ \operatorname{frac}(x+U)<p, \]

where \(\operatorname{frac}\) denotes fractional part.

For each fixed \(U\), the selected coordinates form a circular interval of Lebesgue measure \(p\). Hence selected type mass is exactly \(p\mu_k\), and capacity is respected in every realization.

For each fixed family coordinate \(x\), uniformity of \(U\) gives

\[ \Pr[\operatorname{frac}(x+U)<p]=p. \]

Thus every family has the required probability, even though family selections are correlated.

This construction also works on arbitrary nonatomic family spaces. The nonatomic divisibility lemma states that a measurable set of finite measure contains a measurable subset of any prescribed intermediate measure. Its hypotheses hold for each type space. Recursively bisecting that space produces a measurable uniform coordinate: the successive binary partition labels have every length-\(n\) prefix with normalized measure \(2^{-n}\), so their binary expansion has uniform distribution on \([0,1)\). Pulling the circular-interval rule back through this coordinate preserves both calculations above. Injectivity is unnecessary.

The implementation therefore has a finite symbolic description: one uniform draw and the displayed selection rule on the canonical type circles.

3. Pareto properties and utilization

Ex-ante Pareto optimality

If \(\kappa<W\), a feasible vector weakly improving every \(q_k=p\) and strictly improving one would have

\[ \sum_{k\in K}k\mu_kq_k>pW=\kappa, \]

contradicting feasibility. If \(\kappa\ge W\), every family already succeeds with probability \(1\).

The same argument covers family-specific competitors, even without anonymity. Let \(\eta_k\) be type-\(k\) family measure, and let \(r_k(A)\) be a competitor’s admission probability for family \(A\). By Tonelli’s theorem, its expected individual usage is

\[ \sum_{k\in K}k\int r_k(A)\,d\eta_k(A). \]

Weakly increasing probabilities almost everywhere, with strict increases on a set of positive family mass, makes this integral strictly exceed \(\kappa\) when capacity is scarce. Thus the mechanism is Pareto optimal under the usual almost-everywhere interpretation as well.

Ex-post maximality

When \(\kappa<W\), every realization uses exactly \(\kappa\). Adding a positive mass of unselected whole families increases individual usage strictly, because every family size is positive. Such an addition is infeasible.

When \(\kappa\ge W\), the implementation selects all families. There are no unselected families to add.

Optimal utilization

Any outcome uses at most both \(W\) and \(\kappa\), and our outcome attains their minimum. Therefore

\[ u(P)=\frac{\min\{W,\kappa\}}{\kappa} =u^*(\mu,\kappa). \]

In particular, the required factor-\(\tfrac12\) guarantee holds.

4. Envy-freeness

For any families \(A,B\), the event on the right-hand side of the required envy inequality is contained in the event that \(B\) is admitted. Hence

\[ \Pr\!\left[B\subseteq S\text{ and }(S\setminus B)\cup A \text{ is feasible}\right] \le\Pr[B\subseteq S] =p =\Pr[A\subseteq S]. \]

This proves envy-freeness without requiring any further property of the swap event.

5. Hybrid adjunctive group strategyproofness

Extend the same formulas and circular-interval implementation to real masses.

Consider any permitted report. Let \(D\ge0\) denote the individual mass of added uninterested individuals. Because the report repartitions all original individuals without duplication and leaves outsiders unchanged, conservation of individual mass gives

\[ \sum_j j\nu'_j=W+D. \]

The reported support is finite and consists of positive finite sizes. Consequently, \(T'=\sum_j\nu'_j\) is finite and positive.

Applying the mechanism to the normalized reported instance gives every reported family admission probability

\[ p' =\min\left\{1, \frac{\kappa/T'}{\sum_j j(\nu'_j/T')}\right\} =\min\left\{1,\frac{\kappa}{W+D}\right\} \le p. \]

The normalization therefore cancels exactly. Splitting and merging cannot raise the common reported probability; adding individuals can only decrease it.

Now fix any true coalition family \(A\), and choose one of its original members \(a\). Let \(G(a)\) be the reported family containing that member. Since the mechanism admits reported families whole, every realization satisfies

\[ \{\text{all original members of }A\text{ are admitted}\} \subseteq \{G(a)\text{ is admitted}\}. \]

Taking probabilities yields

\[ \Pr[\text{all original members of }A\text{ are admitted}] \le p'\le p. \]

This argument permits arbitrary correlations and arbitrary splitting or merging: it uses only one original member as a necessary condition for true-family success.

The circular-interval implementation assigns probability \(p'\) to every reported coordinate, so the bound does not depend on transferring an almost-everywhere assertion through the reporting map. Every true family covered by the report is bounded in this way; in particular, almost every coalition family is.

Before reporting, each true family succeeds with probability \(p\). Therefore no permitted report strictly improves the utilities of almost every family in a positive-mass coalition. This proves hybrid adjunctive group strategyproofness.

6. Binary complexity and the discrete comparison

On rational inputs, compute \(W\), then \(p\), then the \(\tau\) coordinates \(p\mu_k\). Output these coordinates with lottery weight \(1\) and the implementation rule.

All calculations use exact rational arithmetic. A common denominator for the input masses can be taken to be the product of their denominators, whose binary length is at most the sum of their input lengths. Summation, multiplication by the binary-encoded sizes, and division by \(W\) therefore produce numbers with binary lengths polynomial in \(L\). There are only polynomially many arithmetic operations, and the output has polynomial length.

Thus construction takes time polynomial in \(\tau,L\). There is no iteration up to numeric \(m\) or numeric capacity.

The discrete knapsack obstacle does not transfer to this model: nonatomic families allow the exact selected masses \(p\mu_k\) while keeping each family whole. The finite-family formulation and its unary-capacity qualification are described explicitly in [Hartman et al., Section 11.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10395v3#S11.SS2). The proof here establishes the requested nonatomic result directly.
