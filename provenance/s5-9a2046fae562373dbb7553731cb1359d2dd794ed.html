Result

The search problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in $m,k,\tau,L$, for sufficiently large universal constants $C,C'>0$.

I interpret the constants as being chosen to obtain the stated universal discrepancy guarantee. The case $k=1$ is immediate: give every good to the sole group.

The missing bit-complexity justification in the previous argument can be supplied. Below, I prove a partial-coloring lemma using rational arithmetic, with explicit rounding and progress guarantees, and then derive the allocation bound.

1. Reduction to support matrices

Put $N=\tau_{\max}$ and define
$$ A^i\in[0,1]^{\tau_i\times m}, \qquad A^i_{t,g}=a_{it,g}. $$
The input promises
$$ \sum_{u=t}^{\tau_i}A^i_{u,g}\leq\Delta^i_t $$
for every $i,t,g$.

Every listed type imposes an individual discrepancy constraint. Consequently, the masses $\mu_i(t)$ do not affect either the promises or the required output. Each listed type can be represented by one matrix row, without replication.

These are precisely the structured matrices appearing in [Theorem 13 of the supplied paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03682v1). Rather than relying on its construction’s arithmetic model, we establish the required algorithm below.

2. A rational deterministic partial-coloring lemma

The proof uses a multiplicative-weight potential, as in [Levy–Ramadas–Rothvoss](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.08752). The rational implementation and its error analysis are given explicitly.

Lemma 1. Let $p\geq1$. Suppose
$$ v_1,\ldots,v_q\in\mathbb Q^p, \qquad \|v_j\|_2\leq1, $$
and let $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_q$ be positive rationals satisfying
$$ \sum_{j=1}^q\exp(-\lambda_j^2/16)\leq p/64. $$
Given $x^0\in[-1,1]^p\cap\mathbb Q^p$, a deterministic algorithm produces $x^\star\in[-1,1]^p\cap\mathbb Q^p$ such that at least half its coordinates belong to $\{-1,1\}$ and
$$ \langle v_j,x^\star-x^0\rangle\leq70\lambda_j $$
for every $j$. Its running time is polynomial in $p,q$ and the total binary input length.

2.1. Preliminary reductions

Zero vectors may be omitted. If there are no remaining constraints, round all coordinates to endpoints.

If $p\leq16$, the entropy assumption implies, for every constraint,
$$ \exp(-\lambda_j^2/16)\leq1/4, \qquad \lambda_j>4. $$
Any endpoint vector therefore satisfies
$$ |\langle v_j,x^\star-x^0\rangle| \leq2\sqrt p \leq8 <2\lambda_j. $$
Thus assume $p>16$.

Remove constraints with $\lambda_j^2>4p$. They are automatically satisfied because every two points of the box differ in Euclidean norm by at most $2\sqrt p<\lambda_j$.

If constraints remain, put
$$ \ell=\min\{1,\min_j\lambda_j\}, \qquad \delta=\frac1{4p}, \qquad T_0=\frac{8p}{\delta^2}=128p^3. $$
Choose positive dyadic rationals $\varepsilon,\eta$ satisfying
$$ \varepsilon\leq\frac{\ell\delta^2}{100p^2}, \qquad \eta\leq\frac1{100T_0}. $$
Choose each within a factor of two of its displayed upper bound. Their descriptions have polynomial length.

Round $x^0$ to the nearest point of the coordinatewise $\varepsilon$-grid in $[-1,1]^p$, obtaining $x^{(0)}$. The grid contains both endpoints, and
$$ \|x^{(0)}-x^0\|_2\leq\sqrt p\,\varepsilon. $$

Initialize rational weights
$$ w_j^{(0)}=2^{-\lceil\lambda_j^2\rceil}. $$
Since $\ln2>1/16$,
$$ \sum_jw_j^{(0)} \leq \sum_j\exp(-\lambda_j^2/16) \leq p/64. $$

2.2. Construction of one step

Suppose fewer than half the coordinates of the current grid point $x$ are endpoints. Let $w_j$ denote its current weights.

Protect every constraint satisfying either
$$ \lambda_j\leq1 \quad\text{or}\quad w_j\geq1/2. $$
Define
$$ f_j=\frac1{1+8\delta^2\lambda_j^2/p}, $$
$$ g=\sum_jw_jf_j\lambda_jv_j, \qquad M=\sum_jw_jf_j\lambda_j^2v_jv_j^{\mathsf T}. $$
These quantities are rational.

Let $U$ be the subspace of vectors $z$ satisfying:

  • $z_h=0$ at every endpoint coordinate of $x$;
  • $\langle z,x\rangle=0$;
  • $\langle z,g\rangle=0$;
  • $\langle z,v_j\rangle=0$ for every protected constraint.

The invariant $\sum_jw_j\leq p/64$, proved below, implies that at most $p/32$ constraints have weight at least $1/2$. Also,
$$ |\{j:\lambda_j\leq1\}| \leq e^{1/16}p/64 <p/32. $$
There are more than $p/2$ nonendpoint coordinates. Hence
$$ \dim U>p/2-p/16-2\geq p/4. $$

Let $P$ be the orthogonal projector onto $U$. It is an exactly computable rational matrix. Write $c_h=Pe_h$. Then
$$ \sum_h\|c_h\|_2^2=\operatorname{tr}P=\dim U $$
and
$$ \sum_hc_h^{\mathsf T}Mc_h =\operatorname{tr}(PM) \leq\operatorname{tr}M. $$
The final inequality holds because $M$ is positive semidefinite and $I-P$ is an orthogonal projector.

Consequently, some nonzero $c_h$ satisfies
$$ \frac{c_h^{\mathsf T}Mc_h}{\|c_h\|_2^2} \leq \frac{\operatorname{tr}M}{\dim U} \leq \frac4p\sum_jw_jf_j\lambda_j^2. $$
Find such a column by exact rational comparisons. Choose a dyadic $t$ with
$$ \|c_h\|_2\leq t<2\|c_h\|_2 $$
and set $z=c_h/t$. Thus
$$ \frac12<\|z\|_2\leq1, \qquad z^{\mathsf T}Mz \leq\frac4p\sum_jw_jf_j\lambda_j^2. $$
Finding $t$ requires only comparisons of rational squares with powers of two.

Choose the largest $\rho\in(0,1]$ for which
$$ y=x+\delta\rho z\in[-1,1]^p. $$
This is an exact rational minimum over the coordinate boundary times. Round $y$ to the $\varepsilon$-grid, obtaining $x^+$. Coordinates already at endpoints stay fixed. If $\rho<1$, at least one additional coordinate reaches an endpoint exactly and remains there after rounding.

For each constraint, put
$$ s_j=\lambda_j\delta\rho\langle v_j,z\rangle $$
and form
$$ \widetilde w_j=w_jf_j(1+s_j+s_j^2). $$
Finally, round each $\widetilde w_j$ downward to a positive dyadic number $w_j^+$ satisfying
$$ (1-\eta)\widetilde w_j\leq w_j^+\leq\widetilde w_j. $$
Such rounding is implemented by retaining sufficiently many leading binary digits.

2.3. Weight invariants

Because $\lambda_j\leq2\sqrt p$,
$$ |s_j| \leq2\sqrt p\,\delta \leq1/2. $$
The condition $\langle z,g\rangle=0$ cancels the linear terms:
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_j\widetilde w_j &=\sum_jw_jf_j+\delta^2\rho^2z^{\mathsf T}Mz\\ &\leq\sum_jw_jf_j+\frac{4\delta^2}{p} \sum_jw_jf_j\lambda_j^2\\ &\leq\sum_jw_j. \end{aligned} $$
The last inequality follows from
$$ 1-f_j=\frac{8\delta^2\lambda_j^2}{p}f_j. $$
Downward rounding preserves this inequality. Thus
$$ \sum_jw_j\leq p/64 $$
throughout.

Every individual weight also remains at most $1$. Initially this holds. If $w_j\geq1/2$, protection gives $s_j=0$, so its weight decreases. Otherwise,
$$ \widetilde w_j \leq w_j(1+|s_j|+s_j^2) <\frac12\cdot\frac74 =\frac78. $$

2.4. Coordinate progress

Write $e=x^+-y$. Then
$$ \|e\|_2\leq\sqrt p\,\varepsilon. $$
Since both $y$ and $x^+$ lie in the box,
$$ \big|\|x^+\|_2^2-\|y\|_2^2\big| \leq3p\varepsilon \leq3\delta^2/100. $$
Orthogonality to $x$ gives
$$ \|y\|_2^2=\|x\|_2^2+\delta^2\rho^2\|z\|_2^2. $$
Therefore a full step, meaning $\rho=1$, increases squared norm by at least
$$ \delta^2/4-3\delta^2/100 \geq\delta^2/5. $$
A shortened step decreases squared norm by at most $3\delta^2/100$.

There are at most $p$ shortened steps, because each fixes a new coordinate. If $F$ is the number of full steps, the bound $\|x\|_2^2\leq p$ implies
$$ F\delta^2/5-3p\delta^2/100\leq p. $$
Consequently, the algorithm terminates after fewer than
$$ 5p/\delta^2+2p\leq T_0 $$
steps, with at least half the coordinates fixed.

2.5. Discrepancy, including all grid errors

Let $T\leq T_0$ be the number of steps, and let $e^{(0)}$ be the initial grid error. The total grid error satisfies
$$ \left\|e^{(0)}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}e^{(t+1)}\right\|_2 \leq(T_0+1)\sqrt p\,\varepsilon \leq\ell. $$

If $\lambda_j\leq1$, its unrounded displacement is zero in every step because it is always protected. Hence
$$ |\langle v_j,x^\star-x^0\rangle| \leq\ell \leq\lambda_j. $$

Now suppose $\lambda_j>1$. For $|s|\leq1/2$,
$$ e^s\leq1+s+s^2, $$
as follows from Taylor’s formula and $e^{1/2}<2$. Thus
$$ \ln(1+s+s^2)\geq s. $$
Also,
$$ \ln f_j\geq-8\delta^2\lambda_j^2/p, \qquad \ln(1-\eta)\geq-2\eta, $$
and
$$ \ln w_j^{(0)} =-\lceil\lambda_j^2\rceil\ln2 \geq-2\lambda_j^2. $$
Summing the logarithmic weight updates gives
$$ \ln w_j^{(T)} \geq -2\lambda_j^2+\sum_ts_j^{(t)} -\frac{8T\delta^2\lambda_j^2}{p}-2T\eta. $$
The unrounded increments and the grid-error estimate imply
$$ \sum_ts_j^{(t)} \geq \lambda_j\langle v_j,x^\star-x^0\rangle-\lambda_j\ell. $$
Using $T\leq8p/\delta^2$, $\ell\leq1<\lambda_j$, and $2T\eta\leq1$, we obtain
$$ \ln w_j^{(T)} \geq \lambda_j\langle v_j,x^\star-x^0\rangle -68\lambda_j^2. $$
Since $w_j^{(T)}\leq1$, this proves
$$ \langle v_j,x^\star-x^0\rangle\leq68\lambda_j. $$
The stated constant $70$ covers all cases.

2.6. Polynomial bit complexity

Here is the implementation justification.

The coordinate grid has denominator bit length
$$ O(\log p+\log(1/\ell)), $$
which is polynomial in the input length. Choose $\eta=2^{-B}$ with $B=O(\log T_0)$ and retain $B+1$ leading binary digits in every weight update.

Because $\lambda_j^2\leq4p$, we have
$$ f_j\geq1/3, \qquad 1+s_j+s_j^2\geq3/4, \qquad 1-\eta\geq1/2. $$
Thus each rounded weight is at least one eighth of its previous value. Throughout,
$$ 2^{-(4p+1+3T_0)}\leq w_j\leq1. $$
The rounded dyadic weights therefore have polynomial descriptions.

At every step, the inputs defining $U$, $g$, and $M$ have polynomial bit length. To compute $P$, select independent rows of a rational matrix $H$ defining $U$ and use
$$ P=I-H^{\mathsf T}(HH^{\mathsf T})^{-1}H. $$
Rational Gaussian elimination computes this expression in polynomial bit time; determinant bounds give polynomial-length numerators and denominators. The dyadic normalization of a nonzero projector column, the boundary time $\rho$, and the intermediate weight updates likewise have polynomial bit length.

Grid rounding resets the coordinate descriptions after each step, and mantissa rounding resets the weight descriptions. There is consequently no uncontrolled growth of denominators across steps. With at most $128p^3$ steps, the total bit cost is polynomial. This proves Lemma 1.

3. Partial coloring for structured matrices

A matrix $B\in[0,1]^{n\times p}$ is $D$-structured if
$$ D_1\geq\cdots\geq D_n>0 $$
and
$$ \sum_{u=j}^nB_{u,g}\leq D_j $$
for every row $j$ and column $g$.

Put
$$ R=D_1/D_n, \qquad H=2+\lceil\log_2R\rceil. $$
Then
$$ 2\leq H\leq\frac32\log_2(4R). $$

For nonzero rows define
$$ W_j=\frac{\|B_j\|_2^2}{HD_j}. $$
For every $z>0$,
$$ |\{j:W_j>z\}|\leq\frac{2p}{z}. $$
To prove this, divide the bounds into intervals $[b,2b)$ with dyadic $b$. There are at most $H$ occupied intervals. Within one interval, the suffix condition at its first row bounds the total entry sum by $2bp$. Since entries belong to $[0,1]$,
$$ \|B_j\|_1\geq\|B_j\|_2^2. $$
Every counted row in that interval therefore has entry sum exceeding $zHb$, so the interval contains at most $2p/(zH)$ counted rows. Summing establishes the claim.

For any $a>0$, integrating this counting bound yields
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_j e^{-a/W_j} &=\int_0^\infty \frac{a}{z^2}e^{-a/z}|\{j:W_j>z\}|\,dz\\ &\leq2pa\int_0^\infty z^{-3}e^{-a/z}\,dz\\ &=\frac{2p}{a}. \end{aligned} $$
The last integral equals $1/a^2$, by substitution $u=a/z$.

Fix $K=256$. For each nonzero row choose dyadic numbers $U_j,S_j$ satisfying
$$ \|B_j\|_2\leq U_j<2\|B_j\|_2, \qquad \sqrt{HD_j}\leq S_j<2\sqrt{HD_j}. $$
These are found by rational square comparisons. Define
$$ v_j=B_j/U_j, \qquad \lambda_j=KS_j/U_j. $$
Then
$$ \lambda_j^2\geq\frac{K^2}{4W_j}. $$
Including both $v_j$ and $-v_j$ gives
$$ \sum_j2e^{-\lambda_j^2/16} \leq\frac{256p}{K^2} \leq p/64. $$

Apply Lemma 1 to these signed constraints, after mapping a starting point $x\in[0,1]^p$ to $2x-\mathbf1$. Mapping the output back produces $y\in[0,1]^p$ with at least half its coordinates integral and
$$ |\langle B_j,y-x\rangle| \leq35\lambda_jU_j \leq70K\sqrt{HD_j}. $$
Thus one structured partial-coloring step has row error at most
$$ C_1\sqrt{D_j\log_2(4R)} $$
for a universal constant $C_1$, in deterministic polynomial bit time.

Zero rows are omitted. If all restricted rows are zero, round the remaining coordinates arbitrarily. If no fractional coordinates remain, stop.

4. Full rounding of a structured matrix

Lemma 2. Suppose $B\in[0,1]^{n\times M}\cap\mathbb Q^{n\times M}$ is $D$-structured, with $D_1\leq n$. Put
$$ \beta=D_n, \qquad R=D_1/D_n. $$
For every rational $x^0\in[0,1]^M$, one can deterministically find $y\in\{0,1\}^M$ in polynomial bit time such that
$$ |\langle B_j,y-x^0\rangle| \leq C_0\log_2(4n/\beta) \sqrt{D_j\log_2(4R)} $$
for a universal constant $C_0$.

Proof. First obtain an extreme point of
$$ \{x\in[0,1]^M:Bx=Bx^0\}. $$
This is a nonempty bounded rational polytope. The standard polynomial bit-time theorem for rational linear programming supplies an exact extreme point.

Such a point has at most $\operatorname{rank}B\leq n$ fractional coordinates: otherwise a nonzero nullspace vector supported on those coordinates would allow movement in both directions while preserving all equalities, contradicting extremality.

Apply the structured partial-coloring step to the fractional columns until their number $p$ is at most $\beta/4$, or is zero. Column restriction preserves the suffix promises. Each step halves $p$, so there are at most
$$ \lceil\log_2(4n/\beta)\rceil $$
such steps. Their total row error is at most
$$ C_2\log_2(4n/\beta)\sqrt{D_j\log_2(4R)}. $$

It remains to justify rounding the tail when $0<p\leq\beta/4$. Set
$$ J=\lceil\log_2(128n/p)\rceil $$
and choose a dyadic $\lambda$ with
$$ 4\sqrt J\leq\lambda<8\sqrt J. $$
Normalize each nonzero restricted row using the same dyadic upper approximation to its norm as above. Apply Lemma 1 with both signs and common parameter $\lambda$. Its entropy condition holds because
$$ 2n e^{-\lambda^2/16} \leq2ne^{-J} \leq p/64. $$
Here $J\geq\ln(128n/p)$.

Since every restricted row has norm at most $\sqrt p$, this tail step incurs error at most
$$ C_3\sqrt{p\log_2(128n/p)}. $$

Let $p_0$ be the initial tail size. Subsequent sizes satisfy $p_\ell\leq p_0\,2^{-\ell}$. The function
$$ u\longmapsto u\log_2(128n/u) $$
is increasing on $(0,n]$, because its derivative is
$$ \frac{\ln(128n/u)-1}{\ln2}>0. $$
Writing $a=\log_2(128n/p_0)$, the total tail error is therefore at most
$$ C_3\sqrt{p_0} \sum_{\ell\geq0}2^{-\ell/2}\sqrt{a+\ell} \leq C_4\sqrt{p_0a}. $$
The final inequality follows from
$$ \sqrt{a+\ell}\leq\sqrt a+\sqrt\ell $$
and convergence of both resulting geometric series.

Using $p_0\leq\beta/4$ and the same monotonicity,
$$ p_0a \leq\frac{\beta}{4}\log_2(512n/\beta). $$
Because $\beta\leq n$, $D_j\geq\beta$, and $\log_2(4R)\geq2$, this tail error is absorbed into the bound in Lemma 2.

There are only $O(\log(n+1))$ partial-coloring calls altogether. Their inputs and outputs have polynomial descriptions by Lemma 1 and the rational threshold construction. This proves both assertions. $\square$

5. Recursive construction of the partition

Consider a node containing $q\geq2$ groups and the goods currently assigned to those groups. Let $n$ be its total number of support rows, so
$$ n\leq qN. $$

Restrict each group matrix to these goods. Stack all rows in nonincreasing order of their original bounds $\Delta^i_t$. Give the stacked row corresponding to $(i,t)$ the bound
$$ D_j=\min\{q\Delta^i_t,n\}. $$

This stacked matrix is $D$-structured. Indeed, for a row with original bound $\Delta$, all subsequent rows from any fixed group lie in the suffix whose bounds are at most $\Delta$, or in a subset of that suffix. Nonnegativity and the original suffix promise bound their column sum by $\Delta$. Summing over the $q$ groups gives $q\Delta$. Independently, the column sum is at most $n$, since every entry is at most $1$.

Let $\beta=\min_jD_j$ and $R=D_1/D_n$. Clipping positive numbers at a common upper bound cannot increase their maximum ratio, so
$$ R\leq r, \qquad \beta\geq\min\{qd,n\}. $$
Since $n\leq qN$ and $d\leq N$,
$$ \frac n\beta\leq\frac Nd. $$

Split the groups into children of sizes $\lfloor q/2\rfloor$ and $\lceil q/2\rceil$. Apply Lemma 2 with starting vector
$$ x^0=\frac{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}{q}\mathbf1. $$
Assign the selected goods to the first child and their complement to the second child. For every row $(i,t)$, the splitting error is at most
$$ F\sqrt{q\Delta^i_t}, \qquad F=C_0\log_2(4N/d)\sqrt{\log_2(4r)}. $$
The complementary selection has the same absolute error.

Repeat recursively until each leaf consists of one group. Empty sets of goods require no rounding. Complementary selections ensure that the resulting bundles are disjoint and cover all goods.

6. Accumulating the errors

Fix a group $i$ and a supported type $t$. Along its root-to-leaf path, let $q_s$ be the successive numbers of groups and let $z_s$ be this type’s value for the goods at that node. Thus
$$ q_0=k, \qquad q_h=1. $$
The split bound gives
$$ \left| \frac{z_{s+1}}{q_{s+1}}-\frac{z_s}{q_s} \right| \leq F\sqrt{\Delta^i_t}\, \frac{\sqrt{q_s}}{q_{s+1}}. $$

For every nonleaf node,
$$ q_s/3\leq q_{s+1}\leq2q_s/3. $$
Therefore
$$ \frac{\sqrt{q_s}}{q_{s+1}}\leq\frac3{\sqrt{q_s}}. $$
Reading the sizes backward from the leaf gives
$$ q_s\geq(3/2)^{h-s}. $$
Hence
$$ \sum_{s=0}^{h-1}\frac{\sqrt{q_s}}{q_{s+1}} \leq 3\sum_{u=1}^{\infty}(2/3)^{u/2} =:B_0, $$
where $B_0$ is universal.

Telescoping now proves
$$ \left| \left\langle a_{it},v_i-\mathbf1/k\right\rangle \right| \leq B_0C_0 \log_2(4N/d)\sqrt{\log_2(4r)} \sqrt{\Delta^i_t}. $$
Taking universal constants with $CC'\geq B_0C_0$ gives exactly the requested guarantee.

7. Bit complexity of the allocation algorithm

There are $k-1$ nonleaf nodes. Each invokes rational linear programming and $O(\log(\tau+1))$ partial-coloring calls.

Every stacked bound has the form
$$ \min\{q\Delta^i_t,n\}, $$
so its binary description is polynomial in $L,\log k,\log\tau$. Restricted valuations remain original input rationals. Dyadic norm approximations and thresholds require only rational square comparisons; their exponents have polynomial magnitude in the input bit length.

Within Lemma 1:

  • the iteration bound is $128p^3$, with $p\leq m$;
  • the grid precision depends on $\log(1/\ell)$, which is bounded by the threshold description length;
  • rounded weights and coordinates have polynomial descriptions;
  • all projector computations and boundary calculations use exact rational arithmetic.

After every partial-coloring call, fractional coordinates lie on its explicitly bounded dyadic grid. Thus successive calls do not accumulate uncontrolled denominator growth. Their rounding errors are already included in Lemmas 1 and 2.

Very small $d$ or very large $r$ affect binary precision, not a numerical iteration count involving $1/d$ or $r$. The complete construction therefore runs in deterministic time polynomial in $m,k,\tau,L$.
