The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational finite-support society \(\mu\) over bidder classes \(k\) and finite signals \(s\), with nonnegative monotone anonymous values \(V_k(z;\nu)\) satisfying measure-SOS, can one compute in time polynomial in the support size, type descriptions, and input bit length a direct rate mechanism \(a_k(r;\nu),p_k(r;\nu)\) such that, for every background society \(\nu\), class \(k\), true signal \(z\), and report \(r\), truthful utility satisfies \(a_k(z;\nu)V_k(z;\nu)-p_k(z;\nu)\ge a_k(r;\nu)V_k(z;\nu)-p_k(r;\nu)\) and is nonnegative, feasibility satisfies \(\sum_{k,r}\nu_{k,r}a_k(r;\nu)\le 1\), and welfare satisfies \(\sum_{k,s}\mu_{k,s}a_k(s;\mu)V_k(s;\mu)\ge \max_{(k,s):\mu_{k,s}>0}V_k(s;\mu)/3.31543\)? The allocation and payment rates are the high-multiplicity normalization of per-agent probabilities and payments, with utilities scaled by the same factor.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An atomless population has total mass \(1\), public roles \(r\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), and private signals in explicitly listed finite sets \(S_r\subseteq\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) containing \(0\). Let \(K=\sum_r|S_r|\). Each agent can receive either zero or one identical unit; supply is a fixed rational density \(\rho>0\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \(t=(r,s)\) with positive mass \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Reported distributions \(\nu\) preserve public role masses.
Public valuations \(V_r(s;\nu)\) are nonnegative, strictly increasing in \(s\), and exactly evaluable as binary rationals; let \(C\) bound evaluation cost and \(L\) denote input bit length.
Distributional monotonicity and SOS mean the following: for every finite list of roles \(r_j\) and positive rational weights \(w_j\) summing to \(1\) with the prescribed role masses, the lift \(F(z)=V_r(z_0;\sum_{j=1}^Nw_j\delta_{(r_j,z_j)})\), where \(\delta_t\) is unit mass at type \(t\), is weakly increasing in every coordinate and satisfies \(F(z'_{-i},b)-F(z'_{-i},a)\le F(z_{-i},b)-F(z_{-i},a)\) whenever \(z'_{-i}\ge z_{-i}\) coordinatewise and \(b\ge a\), for every coordinate \(i\), including \(0\).
A direct mechanism specifies allocation probabilities \(x_r(a;\nu)\in[0,1]\) and expected payments \(p_r(a;\nu)\in\mathbb R\) for every permissible report, including types absent from the support. A unilateral report changes neither the agent's public role nor the aggregate \(\nu\).
Ex-post IC requires \(x_r(s;\nu)V_r(s;\nu)-p_r(s;\nu)\ge x_r(a;\nu)V_r(s;\nu)-p_r(a;\nu)\) for every \(r,s,a,\nu\); ex-post IR requires the truthful utility to be nonnegative. Both use expectations over mechanism randomness.
Feasibility requires an implementation by measurable sets of recipients of mass at most \(\rho\) in every realization, with the stated individual allocation probabilities. At truth, \(y_t=x_r(s;\mu)\), and \(\mathrm{OPT}_\rho(\mu)=\max\{\sum_t\mu_ty_tV_r(s;\mu):0\le y_t\le1,\ \sum_t\mu_ty_t\le\rho\}\).

Can an ex-post IC and IR direct mechanism be constructed and evaluated in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L,C\), guaranteeing \(\sum_t\mu_tx_r(s;\mu)V_r(s;\mu)\ge\mathrm{OPT}_\rho(\mu)/3.31543\) for every admissible society, or is this approximation guarantee unattainable in polynomial time?

For finite single-item auctions with public SOS valuations, a polynomial-time ex-post IC and IR mechanism achieves a \(3.31543\)-approximation ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.06507)).
For binary signals, a tight \(2\)-approximation exists in the discrete model ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08806)). No result settling the specified atomless model was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one; retained positive rational supply density, including densities exceeding one.
  • Made roles public and immutable, and required reported distributions to preserve their masses.
  • Specified explicitly listed finite ordered rational signal domains containing zero, so permissible deviations and their encoding are defined.
  • Defined distributional SOS through all finite weighted signal-coordinate lifts, including the tagged agent's own signal coordinate; carried over nonnegative valuations, weak monotonicity, and strict own-signal monotonicity.
  • Specified exact rational valuation evaluation and included evaluation cost and signal-domain size in the running-time parameters.
  • Interpreted unilateral deviations as individual atomless deviations, which leave the aggregate distribution unchanged; required mechanism rules for reports outside the occupied support.
  • Required one uniform mechanism with guarantees at every admissible society, rather than a mechanism tailored to a known truthful distribution.
  • Interpreted integral bundles as zero or one unit per individual, with realized aggregate feasibility and allocation probabilities implemented by measurable recipient sets.
  • Used expected quasilinear utility for randomized ex-post IC and IR, and allowed signed payments because no payment-sign restriction was imposed.

Result

The guarantee is attainable in polynomial time. In fact, the specified atomless model admits a welfare-optimal ex-post IC and IR mechanism.

The decisive premise is that a unilateral report leaves the aggregate distribution \(\nu\) unchanged. Thus an agent cannot change a price computed from \(\nu\). Distributional monotonicity and SOS are unnecessary for this construction.

1. Construction

Fix a reported distribution \(\nu\). Write its positive-mass support as \(T_\nu\), and define \(v_t=V_r(s;\nu)\) for \(t=(r,s)\).

If \(\rho\geq1\), allocate to every agent and charge zero. This is feasible, IC, IR, and optimal because all valuations are nonnegative.

Henceforth suppose \(0<\rho<1\). Sort the supported types in nonincreasing order of \(v_t\). Let \(c\) be the value of the first type at which cumulative mass reaches or exceeds \(\rho\). Define

\(H=\sum_{t\in T_\nu:\,v_t>c}\nu_t,\qquad B=\sum_{t\in T_\nu:\,v_t=c}\nu_t,\qquad q=\dfrac{\rho-H}{B}.\)

The choice of \(c\) implies \(H<\rho\leq H+B\). Consequently, \(B>0\) and \(0<q\leq1\).

For every permissible report, including reports absent from \(T_\nu\), set

\(x_r(a;\nu)= \begin{cases} 1,&V_r(a;\nu)>c,\\ q,&V_r(a;\nu)=c,\\ 0,&V_r(a;\nu)<c, \end{cases} \qquad p_r(a;\nu)=c\,x_r(a;\nu).\)

The same cutoff and tie probability apply to all roles. The expected allocated mass is exactly

\(\sum_{t\in T_\nu}\nu_t x_t=H+qB=\rho.\)

2. Ex-post IC and IR

Fix any \(\nu\), role \(r\), true signal \(s\), and alternative report \(a\). Put \(v=V_r(s;\nu)\).

By the stipulated atomless deviation rule, reporting \(a\) leaves \(\nu\), and hence \(c\) and \(q\), unchanged. The agent’s expected utility from report \(a\) is therefore

\(x_r(a;\nu)v-p_r(a;\nu)=x_r(a;\nu)(v-c).\)

There are three cases.

  • If \(v>c\), truthful reporting gives allocation probability \(1\). Since every alternative probability belongs to \([0,1]\), truthful reporting maximizes utility, yielding \(v-c>0\).
  • If \(v<c\), truthful reporting gives allocation probability \(0\) and utility zero. Every alternative report gives utility at most zero because \(v-c<0\).
  • If \(v=c\), every report gives utility zero, including the truthful report.

Thus truthful reporting weakly dominates every alternative report, and truthful utility is nonnegative. This proves ex-post IC and IR for every permissible report and every \(\nu\).

In particular, absent reports cause no difficulty: their allocations and payments are explicitly defined by the same price rule.

3. Feasibility in every realization

We now prove the required implementation by measurable recipient sets.

Let \((A,\mathcal A,\lambda)\) be the atomless population space, with \(\lambda(A)=1\). At the reported profile, let \(A_>\) and \(A_=\) be the measurable sets of agents whose reported valuations exceed and equal \(c\), respectively. Their masses are \(H\) and \(B\).

We use the standard atomless divisibility theorem: if a finite measure is atomless, every measurable set of mass \(b\) has a measurable subset of any prescribed mass in \([0,b]\). The restriction of \(\lambda\) to \(A_=\) satisfies these hypotheses.

Repeatedly bisecting \(A_=\) into equal-mass measurable subsets produces a measurable function \(h:A_=\to[0,1)\) with uniform pushforward under normalized measure. Explicitly, the successive binary partition labels define the binary digits of \(h\); every dyadic interval of length \(2^{-n}\) has preimage mass \(B2^{-n}\), which determines the uniform distribution. Exceptional binary-expansion points form a null set and can be assigned arbitrary values.

Draw \(U\) uniformly from \([0,1)\), and choose the recipient set

\(R_U=A_>\ \cup\ \{i\in A_=:(h(i)+U)\bmod1<q\}.\)

For every fixed realization \(U=u\), rotation preserves uniform measure, so

\(\lambda(R_u)=H+qB=\rho.\)

For every individual \(i\in A_=\), the random variable \((h(i)+U)\bmod1\) is uniform. Hence that individual receives a unit with probability \(q\). Agents in \(A_>\) receive with probability \(1\), and all remaining agents receive with probability \(0\).

Charge each recipient \(c\) and each nonrecipient zero. Expected payments are exactly \(c\,x_r(a;\nu)\).

This implements the stated probabilities and payments while respecting supply in every realization.

For rational input, the lottery also has a compact finite description. Write \(q=u/d\) with integers \(0<u\leq d\). Draw \(J\) uniformly from \(\{0,\ldots,d-1\}\), and select a tied agent precisely when

\((\lfloor d\,h(i)\rfloor+J)\bmod d<u.\)

Each realization selects \(u\) equal-mass cells, and each individual is selected for exactly \(u\) of the \(d\) possible shifts. Thus the same feasibility and marginal-probability conclusions hold.

4. Exact welfare optimality

Evaluate the mechanism at truth, \(\nu=\mu\). Write \(v_t=V_r(s;\mu)\) and \(x_t=x_r(s;\mu)\).

Let \(z\) be any feasible allocation in the optimization defining \(\mathrm{OPT}_\rho(\mu)\). Then

\(\sum_t\mu_t(x_t-z_t)v_t = \sum_t\mu_t(x_t-z_t)(v_t-c) + c\left(\rho-\sum_t\mu_tz_t\right).\)

Every term on the right is nonnegative:

  • If \(v_t>c\), then \(x_t=1\), so \(x_t-z_t\geq0\) and \(v_t-c>0\).
  • If \(v_t<c\), then \(x_t=0\), so \(x_t-z_t\leq0\) and \(v_t-c<0\).
  • If \(v_t=c\), the corresponding product is zero.
  • The final term is nonnegative because \(c\geq0\) and \(z\) is feasible.

Therefore the mechanism’s welfare is at least that of every feasible \(z\). Its own allocation is feasible, so

\(\sum_t\mu_t x_r(s;\mu)V_r(s;\mu)=\mathrm{OPT}_\rho(\mu).\)

The requested approximation follows immediately.

5. Polynomial-time construction and evaluation

On an explicitly encoded rational distribution:

  • Evaluate the valuations of its supported types.
  • Sort them and compute \(c,H,B,q\).
  • For a requested report, evaluate its valuation and apply the cutoff rule.
  • To output the complete allocation and payment table, evaluate all \(K\) permissible types.

At the given society, this requires at most \(K\) valuation evaluations and sorting \(\tau\) supported values. Rational comparisons, sums, and divisions have polynomial bit complexity: their operand lengths are polynomially bounded by the input length, the number of types, and the lengths of the exactly evaluated valuations. In the usual bit-cost model, those output lengths are bounded by the evaluation cost \(C\).

Consequently, construction and evaluation take time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L,C\). The mechanism is specified by one algorithm for all reported distributions; it requires no enumeration of possible distributions or individual agents.

6. Why the discrete-auction barrier does not apply

In the cited finite-auction model, a bidder’s deviation changes the signal profile that determines valuations and allocations. [Lu, Sun, and Zhou’s formulation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.06507) explicitly compares allocations at different signal profiles.

Here, the task explicitly fixes \(\nu\) during a unilateral deviation. That permits an endogenous market-clearing price to remain fixed throughout each agent’s IC comparison. The proof above establishes optimality directly under that premise.
