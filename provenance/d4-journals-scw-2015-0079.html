The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a fixed set of alternatives, a finite support \(T\) of complete respondent-report types, and binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), let each type specify its paired-comparison outcomes and which pairs were compared, represented by \(A^t\) and \(M^t\). Form \(A_\mu=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t A^t\), \(M_\mu=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t M^t\), the Laplacian \(L_\mu\), and \(s_\mu=A_\mu e\). Assuming the comparison graph of \(M_\mu\) is connected, compute the unique rating \(q\) satisfying \(L_\mu q=s_\mu\) and \(e^\top q=0\), and return its induced ranking.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(N=\{X_1,\ldots,X_m\}\), with \(m\geq1\), be the input alternatives. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) respondent-report types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type specifies a subset of unordered pairs compared and, for each compared pair, binary-rational outcomes \(r^t_{ij},r^t_{ji}\geq0\) satisfying \(r^t_{ij}+r^t_{ji}=1\). Reports may contain ties and cycles. Set \(M^t_{ij}=M^t_{ji}=1\) and \(A^t_{ij}=r^t_{ij}-r^t_{ji}=-A^t_{ji}\) for compared pairs; all other entries, including diagonals, are zero.
Define \(A_\mu=\sum_t\mu_tA^t\), \(M_\mu=\sum_t\mu_tM^t\), \((L_\mu)_{ij}=-(M_\mu)_{ij}\) for \(i\neq j\), and \((L_\mu)_{ii}=\sum_j(M_\mu)_{ij}\). Let \(e\) be the all-ones vector and \(s_\mu=A_\mu e\). Assume the graph with edges where \((M_\mu)_{ij}>0\) is connected.
Compute the exact normalized least-squares rating \(q\) satisfying \(L_\mu q=s_\mu\) and \(e^\top q=0\), and its weak ranking: \(X_i\succeq X_j\) exactly when \(q_i\geq q_j\), retaining equality as a tie. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Conclusion

The exact normalized rating and its weak ranking are solvable in deterministic time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The proof below establishes a binary bit-complexity bound, including exact detection of ties.

Csató’s paper allows nonnegative real comparison weights, defines the normalized solution by the stated equations, and gives an \(O(m^3)\) arithmetic-operation bound. The additional issue here is controlling the bit lengths of exact computations. [Csató, Sections 2–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.06778)

1. A nonsingular system for the normalized rating

If \(m=1\), the answer is \(q_1=0\). Henceforth assume \(m\geq2\).

Write \(w_{ij}=(M_\mu)_{ij}\). Because the masses are positive and sum to \(1\), and every reported outcome lies in \([0,1]\), we have
\(0\leq w_{ij}\leq1\), \(\lvert(A_\mu)_{ij}\rvert\leq w_{ij}\), and consequently \(\lvert(s_\mu)_i\rvert\leq m-1\).

The matrix \(A_\mu\) is skew-symmetric, so
\(e^\top s_\mu=e^\top A_\mu e=0\).

For every \(x\in\mathbb R^m\), the Laplacian identity is
\(x^\top L_\mu x=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}(x_i-x_j)^2\).
This follows by expanding the diagonal and off-diagonal terms in the quadratic form. Every summand is nonnegative. Equality therefore requires \(x_i=x_j\) on each positive-weight edge. By connectedness, this requires \(x\) to be constant.

Define \(C=L_\mu+ee^\top\). Then
\(x^\top Cx=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}(x_i-x_j)^2+(e^\top x)^2\).
If this expression vanishes, connectedness makes \(x\) constant, and \(e^\top x=0\) then makes \(x=0\). Thus \(C\) is positive definite and invertible.

Let \(q\) be the unique solution of \(Cq=s_\mu\). Multiplying by \(e^\top\), using \(e^\top L_\mu=0\), gives
\(m\,e^\top q=e^\top s_\mu=0\).
Hence \(e^\top q=0\), and therefore \(L_\mu q=s_\mu\).

Conversely, any vector satisfying the two required equations satisfies \(Cq=s_\mu\). Thus solving this nonsingular system computes exactly the required normalized rating.

2. Clearing denominators with polynomial bit length

Use the usual binary encoding of rational numerators and denominators. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses and reported outcomes, counting each input occurrence separately. The sum of their binary lengths is at most \(L\), so \(\log_2 D\leq L\).

Every term contributing to \(M_\mu\) is a mass \(\mu_t\). Every term contributing to \(A_\mu\) is a signed product \(\mu_t r^t_{ij}\). The denominators of both factors occur in the product defining \(D\), so multiplying each such term by \(D\) gives an integer.

Consequently,
\(B=D(L_\mu+ee^\top)\) and \(c=Ds_\mu\)
are an integer matrix and an integer vector. The required rating is the unique solution of \(Bq=c\).

The bounds from Section 1 imply
\(\lvert B_{ij}\rvert\leq mD\) and \(\lvert c_i\rvert\leq(m-1)D\).
Indeed, a diagonal entry of \(L_\mu+ee^\top\) is at most \(m\), while an off-diagonal entry is \(1-w_{ij}\in[0,1]\).

Thus, setting \(H=mD\), every entry of the augmented matrix \([B\mid c]\) has absolute value at most \(H\), with bit length \(O(L+\log m)\).

These integers can be constructed directly from the support list, using at most \(O(\tau m^2)\) contributions. Each contribution and each accumulated sum has polynomial bit length. This construction therefore takes polynomial bit time and requires no enumeration of types outside the given support.

3. The exact output has polynomial length

For any \(k\times k\) submatrix \(T\) of \([B\mid c]\), the determinant’s permutation expansion gives
\(\lvert\det T\rvert\leq k!H^k\).
For \(1\leq k\leq m\), this is at most \(m^mH^m\). Hence every such determinant has bit length
\(K=O(m(L+\log(m+1)))\).

Since \(B\) is invertible, Cramer’s rule gives
\(q_i=\det(B^{(i)})/\det(B)\),
where \(B^{(i)}\) replaces column \(i\) of \(B\) by \(c\). Both determinants satisfy the preceding bound. Reducing the fraction cannot increase its numerator or denominator.

Therefore every rating has numerator and denominator of \(O(K)\) bits, and the entire exact rating vector has polynomial output length.

This output bound alone does not yet prove polynomial computation time; the next section controls intermediate arithmetic.

4. Exact elimination takes polynomial bit time

Run rational Gaussian elimination on \(Bq=c\), reducing fractions after each operation.

Because \(B\) is positive definite, every leading principal submatrix is positive definite: extend a nonzero vector on its coordinates by zeros and apply positive definiteness of \(B\). Thus every leading principal submatrix is invertible, and elimination needs no pivot exchanges.

Suppose the first \(k\) variables have been eliminated, and let \(P=\{1,\ldots,k\}\). For remaining indices \(i,j\), the current coefficient and right-hand side are
\(S_{ij}=B_{ij}-B_{iP}B_{PP}^{-1}B_{Pj}\)
and
\(d_i=c_i-B_{iP}B_{PP}^{-1}c_P\).

Their determinant representations are
\(S_{ij}=\det\begin{pmatrix}B_{PP}&B_{Pj}\\B_{iP}&B_{ij}\end{pmatrix}/\det(B_{PP})\)
and
\(d_i=\det\begin{pmatrix}B_{PP}&c_P\\B_{iP}&c_i\end{pmatrix}/\det(B_{PP})\).

To verify these identities, subtract \(B_{iP}B_{PP}^{-1}\) times the upper block rows from the last row. This preserves the determinant and leaves a block triangular matrix whose last diagonal entry is respectively \(S_{ij}\) or \(d_i\). For \(k=0\), the formulas mean the original entries, with denominator \(1\).

All numerator and denominator determinants satisfy Section 3’s bound. Therefore every reduced coefficient and right-hand-side entry throughout forward elimination has \(O(K)\) bits. Each elimination update uses a constant number of rational operations, so its temporary integers also have \(O(K)\) bits. Integer arithmetic and fraction reduction on these integers take polynomial time. There are \(O(m^3)\) updates.

Back substitution also has polynomial bit complexity. Each already-computed solution coordinate has \(O(K)\) bits by Section 3, and each triangular coefficient has \(O(K)\) bits. A product therefore has \(O(K)\) bits. Summing at most \(m\) such rational terms, even using the product of their denominators as a common denominator, uses at most \(O(mK)\) bits. Division by the nonzero pivot preserves a polynomial bound; reduction gives the next coordinate with \(O(K)\) bits.

Thus the entire exact solve takes polynomial bit time.

5. Computing the weak ranking

Write each computed coordinate as \(q_i=a_i/b_i\), with \(b_i>0\). Then
\(q_i\geq q_j\) holds exactly when \(a_i b_j\geq a_j b_i\), and equality holds exactly when the two cross-products coincide.

The integers involved have polynomial bit length. Sorting the coordinates and grouping equal values therefore computes the weak ranking in polynomial time while retaining every tie.

Hence the requested computation is polynomial-time solvable. An NP-hardness classification under standard polynomial-time reductions would consequently imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
