The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), positive rational voting weights \(a_t\), and rational quota fraction \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), form finite weighted voting games with \(M\mu_t\) voters of type \(t\) and quota \(\lambda M\sum_t\mu_t a_t\), for admissible replication sizes \(M\) at which the masses and quota align with the integer weight lattice. For each game, let \(\eta_t(M)\) count coalitions of the other voters in which a type-\(t\) voter is critical, and set \(\Pi_t(M)=\frac{M\mu_t\eta_t(M)}{\sum_s M\mu_s\eta_s(M)}\). Prove the limit of \((\Pi_t(M))_{t\in T}\) exists independently of the admissible replication sequence, and compute it to additive error \(\varepsilon\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The support size \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Each type has a positive binary-rational voting weight \(a_t\); the quota fraction is binary rational \(\lambda\in(0,1)\). Let \(k=|\{a_t:t\in T\}|\).
Let \(g>0\) be the rational lattice span defined by \(a_t/g\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(\gcd_{t\in T}(a_t/g)=1\). A positive integer \(M\) is admissible when every \(M\mu_t\) is integral and \(q_M/g\) is integral, where \(q_M=\lambda M\sum_t\mu_ta_t\).
For admissible \(M\), let \(N_M\) contain \(M\mu_t\) distinct voters of each type \(t\). A coalition wins exactly when its weight is at least \(q_M\). For a fixed type-\(t\) voter \(i\), define \(\eta_t(M)=|\{S\subseteq N_M\setminus\{i\}:q_M-a_t\leq\sum_{j\in S}a_{t(j)}<q_M\}|\) and \(\Pi_t(M)=M\mu_t\eta_t(M)/(\sum_sM\mu_s\eta_s(M))\).
The computational problem takes binary-rational \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) and asks for rational approximations to the limiting type shares, with additive error at most \(\varepsilon\) in each coordinate. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including \(\varepsilon\); there is no varying candidate parameter \(m\).

Does \(\Pi^*=\lim_{M\to\infty,\ M\text{ admissible}}\Pi(M)\) always exist independently of the admissible sequence, and is computing its coordinatewise \(\varepsilon\)-approximation polynomial-time in \(\tau,L\), or NP-hard?
If NP-hard, is this computation FPT parameterized by \(k\), with running time \(f(k)(\tau+L)^{O(1)}\)?

Discrete Banzhaf computation admits an \(O(n^k)\) algorithm for \(k\) weight values, an XP bound; unrestricted exact computation is NP-hard, even for 2-unbalanced games. [Aziz–Paterson](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.2497)
For bounded integer weights with greatest common divisor one and convergent weight frequencies, limiting Banzhaf power ratios at arbitrary quota fractions are known; this covers the replication setting after lattice normalization. The uniform binary-input complexity question remains unverified here. [de Raaij–Duchin–Procaccia–Tucker-Foltz](https://jamie.tuckerfoltz.com/Papers/2026-07-PropensityBanzhaf/main.pdf)

Result

The limit always exists independently of the admissible sequence. Its coordinatewise approximation is computable in deterministic time \((\tau+L)^{O(1)}\). Consequently, the problem is FPT parameterized by \(k\), even with \(f(k)=1\).

The proof below establishes the limit directly and gives explicit bounds for a polynomial-time numerical algorithm. It does not require a uniform convergence rate for the finite societies.

1. Admissibility and exponential tilting

Normalize the weights by their lattice span:
\[ w_t=\frac{a_t}{g}\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad \gcd_{t\in T}w_t=1, \qquad W=\sum_t\mu_tw_t. \]

The normalized quota is \(Q_M=q_M/g=\lambda MW\). Since the numbers \(\mu_t\) and \(\lambda W\) are rational, every multiple of a suitable positive integer is admissible. Thus there are arbitrarily large admissible \(M\).

For every admissible \(M\), the denominator defining \(\Pi(M)\) is positive. Indeed, adding voters in any order starts with a losing coalition and ends with a winning coalition, so some voter is pivotal.

Define
\[ m(\theta)=\sum_t\mu_tw_t\frac{e^{\theta w_t}}{1+e^{\theta w_t}}. \]
Its derivative is
\[ m'(\theta)= \sum_t\mu_tw_t^2 \frac{e^{\theta w_t}}{(1+e^{\theta w_t})^2}>0. \]
Moreover, \(m(\theta)\) tends to \(0\) and \(W\) as \(\theta\) tends to negative and positive infinity, respectively. Consequently, there is a unique real \(\theta\) satisfying
\[ m(\theta)=\lambda W. \]
Because \(m(0)=W/2\), this \(\theta\) has the sign of \(\lambda-1/2\).

Fix this \(\theta\), and put
\[ z=e^\theta, \qquad p_t=\frac{z^{w_t}}{1+z^{w_t}}, \qquad V=\sum_t\mu_tw_t^2p_t(1-p_t)>0. \]

For a fixed type-\(t\) voter, independently include every other type-\(s\) voter with probability \(p_s\). Let \(X_{t,M}\) be the resulting integer-valued coalition weight. Then
\[ \mathbb EX_{t,M}=Q_M-w_tp_t, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(X_{t,M}) =MV-w_t^2p_t(1-p_t). \]

2. A lattice local limit estimate

We need the following estimate: for any integer sequence \(\ell_M\) satisfying
\[ \ell_M-\mathbb EX_{t,M}=O(1), \]
along admissible \(M\),
\[ \Pr(X_{t,M}=\ell_M) =\frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt{2\pi MV}}. \]

Here all weights and probabilities are fixed while \(M\) increases. We prove the estimate to make the lattice hypotheses explicit.

Write
\[ n_s(M)=M\mu_s-\mathbf1_{\{s=t\}}, \qquad \phi_s(u)=1-p_s+p_se^{iw_su}. \]
The characteristic function of \(X_{t,M}\) is
\[ \Phi_M(u)=\prod_s\phi_s(u)^{n_s(M)}. \]

For every \(s\),
\[ |\phi_s(u)|^2 =1-4p_s(1-p_s)\sin^2(w_su/2). \]
Thus \(|\phi_s(u)|=1\) precisely when \(w_su\) is a multiple of \(2\pi\). The gcd assumption implies that all these equalities hold simultaneously only when \(u\) is a multiple of \(2\pi\).

Fix a sufficiently small \(u_0>0\). On the compact set \(u_0\leq |u|\leq\pi\), the continuous function
\[ G(u)=\prod_s|\phi_s(u)|^{\mu_s} \]
therefore has maximum \(\rho<1\). For sufficiently large \(M\), every \(n_s(M)\geq M\mu_s/2\). Since \(|\phi_s(u)|\leq1\), it follows that
\[ |\Phi_M(u)|\leq G(u)^{M/2}\leq\rho^{M/2} \]
on this compact set.

Choose \(u_0\) small enough that \(|w_su|\leq\pi\) for every \(s\) whenever \(|u|\leq u_0\). Using \(|\sin y|\geq2|y|/\pi\) for \(|y|\leq\pi/2\), and \(1-x\leq e^{-x}\), gives
\[ |\Phi_M(u)|\leq \exp\!\left(-\frac{MV}{\pi^2}u^2\right) \]
for sufficiently large \(M\) and \(|u|\leq u_0\).

Taylor expansion of the centered characteristic function around zero gives
\[ \log\!\left(e^{-iu\mathbb EX_{t,M}}\Phi_M(u)\right) = -\frac12\operatorname{Var}(X_{t,M})u^2 +O(M|u|^3). \]
The remainder bound follows because there are finitely many fixed weights and probabilities, and each factor is nonzero in a neighborhood of zero. Hence, for fixed real \(y\),
\[ e^{-iy\mathbb EX_{t,M}/\sqrt M} \Phi_M(y/\sqrt M) \longrightarrow e^{-Vy^2/2}. \]

Fourier inversion for integer-valued random variables yields
\[ \Pr(X_{t,M}=\ell_M) =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{-iu\ell_M}\Phi_M(u)\,du. \]
After substituting \(u=y/\sqrt M\) in the central interval, the bounded displacement of \(\ell_M\) from the mean makes its additional phase tend to \(1\). The Gaussian bound above permits dominated convergence. The remaining interval contributes \(O(\rho^{M/2})\). Therefore
\[ \sqrt M\,\Pr(X_{t,M}=\ell_M) \longrightarrow \frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-Vy^2/2}\,dy =\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi V}}, \]
as claimed.

3. The limiting shares

Let
\[ F_M(z)=\prod_s(1+z^{w_s})^{M\mu_s}. \]
If \(c_{t,M}(\ell)\) counts coalitions of weight \(\ell\) excluding the designated type-\(t\) voter, exponential tilting gives the exact identity
\[ c_{t,M}(\ell) = \frac{F_M(z)}{1+z^{w_t}}\, z^{-\ell}\Pr(X_{t,M}=\ell). \]

Because the quota is integral, the swing interval consists precisely of the weights \(Q_M-d\) for \(1\leq d\leq w_t\). Consequently,
\[ \eta_t(M)= \frac{F_M(z)z^{-Q_M}}{1+z^{w_t}} \sum_{d=1}^{w_t}z^d \Pr(X_{t,M}=Q_M-d). \]
For each such \(d\),
\[ Q_M-d-\mathbb EX_{t,M}=w_tp_t-d \]
is fixed. Applying the local estimate to this finite sum gives
\[ \eta_t(M)= \frac{F_M(z)z^{-Q_M}}{\sqrt{2\pi MV}} \left(H_t(z)+o(1)\right), \qquad H_t(z)=\frac{\sum_{d=1}^{w_t}z^d}{1+z^{w_t}}>0. \]

The common factor cancels in the type shares, proving
\[ \Pi_t^*=\frac{\mu_tH_t(z)}{\sum_s\mu_sH_s(z)}. \]
This proves existence independently of the admissible sequence.

If \(\lambda=1/2\), then \(z=1\) and \(H_t(1)=w_t/2\). Hence
\[ \Pi_t^*=\frac{\mu_ta_t}{\sum_s\mu_sa_s}. \]

Otherwise, summing the geometric progression gives
\[ H_t(z)=\frac{z}{z-1}\frac{z^{w_t}-1}{z^{w_t}+1} =\frac{z}{z-1}\tanh(\theta w_t/2). \]
Cancelling the common factor, and using oddness when \(\theta<0\), yields
\[ \Pi_t^* = \frac{\mu_t\tanh(|\theta|w_t/2)} {\sum_s\mu_s\tanh(|\theta|w_s/2)}. \]

For computation, normalize directly by the maximum original weight:
\[ v_t=\frac{a_t}{a_{\max}}, \qquad r=\min_t v_t>0, \qquad A=\sum_t\mu_tv_t, \qquad b=\min(\lambda,1-\lambda). \]
When \(\lambda\neq1/2\), the number \(x_*=|\theta|\max_t w_t\) is the unique positive solution of
\[ D(x):=\sum_t\frac{\mu_tv_t}{1+e^{xv_t}}=bA. \]
The computational formula is therefore
\[ \Pi_t^* = \frac{\mu_t\tanh(x_*v_t/2)} {\sum_s\mu_s\tanh(x_*v_s/2)}. \]

This agrees with the ordinary Banzhaf specialization of the limiting ratio formula in [de Raaij–Duchin–Procaccia–Tucker-Foltz](https://jamie.tuckerfoltz.com/Papers/2026-07-PropensityBanzhaf/main.pdf); the argument above proves the needed specialization directly.

4. Explicit bounds for locating the root

Assume henceforth that \(\lambda\neq1/2\), and set
\[ d=bA, \qquad \ell=4(1/2-b)>0. \]

Writing \(s(u)=1/(1+e^u)\), differentiation gives
\[ -D'(x)=\sum_t\mu_tv_t^2s(xv_t)(1-s(xv_t)). \]
Since \(s(u)(1-s(u))\leq1/4\) and \(v_t^2\leq v_t\),
\[ -D'(x)\leq A/4. \]
Thus
\[ (1/2-b)A=D(0)-D(x_*)\leq x_*A/4, \]
so \(x_*\geq\ell\).

Let \(J\) be the least positive integer such that \(2^{-J}\leq b\), and put \(R=(J+1)/r\). For \(x\geq0\),
\[ D(x)\leq Ae^{-xr}. \]
Because \(e>2\), this implies
\[ D(R)\leq Ae^{-(J+1)} <A2^{-(J+1)} \leq d/2. \]
Therefore
\[ x_*\in[\ell,R]. \]

There is also a useful global conditioning bound. Since \(x\geq0\) implies \(s(xv_t)\leq1/2\),
\[ -D'(x) \geq\frac r2\sum_t\mu_tv_ts(xv_t) =\frac r2D(x). \]
Integration yields, for any \(x,y\geq0\),
\[ |\log D(x)-\log D(y)|\geq\frac r2|x-y|. \]

All rational quantities just defined have polynomial bit length. Also \(J=O(L)\), so \(\log R\) is polynomially bounded in the input length, even when \(R\) itself is large.

5. Controlling the normalized shares

Define, for \(x>0\),
\[ B_t(x)=\tanh(xv_t/2), \qquad B(x)=\sum_s\mu_sB_s(x), \qquad P_t(x)=\frac{\mu_tB_t(x)}{B(x)}. \]

For every \(x\geq\ell\), the denominator has the explicit lower bound
\[ B(x)\geq\beta, \qquad \beta=\ell r/4>0. \]
Indeed, \(B_t(x)\geq\tanh(\ell r/2)\). Because \(0<\ell r/2\leq1\), concavity of \(\tanh\), together with \(\tanh(1)>1/2\), gives \(\tanh(\ell r/2)\geq\ell r/4\).

Furthermore,
\[ 0\leq B_t'(x)\leq1/2, \qquad 0\leq B'(x)\leq1/2. \]
Differentiating the normalized coordinate and using \(0\leq P_t(x)\leq1\) gives
\[ |P_t'(x)| \leq \frac{\mu_t/2+P_t(x)/2}{B(x)} \leq\frac1\beta \]
for \(x\geq\ell\).

Choose the rational tolerances
\[ \delta=\frac{\varepsilon\beta}{4}, \qquad \eta=\frac{rd\delta}{8}, \qquad \xi=\frac{\varepsilon\beta}{16}. \]
Their logarithmic inverse magnitudes are polynomially bounded in \(L\).

If \(\widehat x\geq\ell\) and \(|\widehat x-x_*|\leq\delta\), the mean value theorem gives
\[ |P_t(\widehat x)-\Pi_t^*|\leq\varepsilon/4. \]

Suppose additionally that rational numbers \(\widehat B_t\in[0,1]\) satisfy
\[ |\widehat B_t-B_t(\widehat x)|\leq\xi. \]
Then \(\widehat B=\sum_t\mu_t\widehat B_t\) satisfies \(|\widehat B-B(\widehat x)|\leq\xi\), so \(\widehat B\geq\beta-\xi>0\). The rational output
\[ \widehat\Pi_t=\frac{\mu_t\widehat B_t}{\widehat B} \]
obeys
\[ |\widehat\Pi_t-P_t(\widehat x)| \leq\frac{2\xi}{\beta-\xi} \leq\frac{4\xi}{\beta} =\varepsilon/4. \]
Combining the two errors gives \(|\widehat\Pi_t-\Pi_t^*|\leq\varepsilon/2\), which is stronger than required.

6. Polynomial-time numerical evaluation

We first justify evaluating the necessary elementary functions.

Given a nonnegative rational \(u\) and rational accuracy \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), choose an integer \(p\) with \(2^{-p}\leq\alpha/4\), and let \(H=p+2\).

If \(u\geq H\), approximate \(e^{-u}\) by zero. Its error is at most \(2^{-H}\).

Otherwise \(u<H\). Choose an integer \(n\geq\max(6H,p+2)\), and compute the exact rational Taylor sum
\[ T=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{u^j}{j!}. \]
The ratios of consecutive omitted terms are at most \(1/2\). Using \(n!\geq(n/e)^n\) and \(e<3\),
\[ 0\leq e^u-T \leq\frac{2H^n}{n!} \leq2\left(\frac{3H}{n}\right)^n \leq2^{1-n} \leq2^{-p}. \]
Since \(T,e^u\geq1\), the approximation \(E=1/T\) satisfies \(|E-e^{-u}|\leq2^{-p}\).

Thus \(e^{-u}\) can be approximated in time polynomial in the bit length of \(u\) and \(\log(1/\alpha)\). The Taylor degree is \(O(p)\), and its exact rational arithmetic has polynomial bit length.

Finally, use
\[ s(u)=\frac{e^{-u}}{1+e^{-u}}, \qquad \tanh(u/2)=\frac{1-e^{-u}}{1+e^{-u}}. \]
As functions of \(e^{-u}\in[0,1]\), these have derivative magnitudes at most \(1\) and \(2\), respectively. The preceding precision therefore suffices to approximate both within \(\alpha\), with outputs in their proper ranges. Large exponential arguments require only the truncation branch.

We now locate \(x_*\) by approximate bisection on \([\ell,R]\). At a midpoint \(x\), compute a rational \(\widehat D(x)\) with
\[ |\widehat D(x)-D(x)|\leq\eta. \]
This is achieved by approximating each \(s(xv_t)\) within \(\eta\): the coefficient sum is \(A\leq1\).

Use these rules:

  • If \(\widehat D(x)>d+\eta\), then \(D(x)>d\), so replace the lower endpoint by \(x\).
  • If \(\widehat D(x)<d-\eta\), then \(D(x)<d\), so replace the upper endpoint by \(x\).
  • Otherwise, return \(x\).

In the last case, \(|D(x)-d|\leq2\eta\leq d/4\). Hence
\[ |\log D(x)-\log d| \leq\frac{2|D(x)-d|}{d} \leq\frac{4\eta}{d}. \]
The conditioning bound from Section 4 then gives
\[ |x-x_*|\leq\frac{8\eta}{rd}=\delta. \]

If this stopping condition does not occur earlier, stop once the bracket width is at most \(2\delta\), and return its midpoint. The bracket always contains \(x_*\), so the same accuracy follows.

The number of iterations is
\[ O\!\left(1+\log\frac R\delta\right), \]
which is polynomially bounded in \(L\). Midpoints have polynomial bit length, and every function evaluation uses polynomial precision. Evaluate the final hyperbolic tangents within \(\xi\), then output the rational normalized coordinates from Section 5.

7. Complexity conclusion

When \(\lambda=1/2\), the limiting shares are computed exactly by rational arithmetic. Otherwise, the algorithm above produces rational answers satisfying the requested coordinatewise error in time \((\tau+L)^{O(1)}\).

The algorithm never enumerates lattice weights or finite coalitions. Exponentially large normalized integer weights therefore do not produce a pseudopolynomial running time.

The finite-game hardness and bounded-weight-value enumeration results in [Aziz–Paterson](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.2497) concern exact discrete computation. They do not imply hardness for this limiting approximation problem.

Thus the stated problem is polynomial-time solvable, and therefore FPT in \(k\). NP-hardness under standard polynomial-time reductions would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

2. The lattice local limit estimate

The exponent comparison is valid: for sufficiently large admissible \(M\), every \(n_s(M)\geq M\mu_s/2\). This includes the type whose designated voter was removed.

The central Gaussian bound has the correct constant. The stated sine inequality and modulus identity give

\( |\phi_s(u)|\leq\exp(-2p_s(1-p_s)w_s^2u^2/\pi^2) \).

The centered logarithmic expansion is justified near zero: the finitely many factors are nonzero there, their weights and probabilities remain fixed, and the total number of factors grows linearly with \(M\). Consequently, its remainder is \(O(M|u|^3)\).

Fourier inversion applies because the random variable is integer-valued. After scaling, the bounded displacement from the mean contributes a phase tending to one. The Gaussian bound supplies an integrable dominating function, and the exterior contribution remains negligible even after multiplication by \(\sqrt M\).

Thus the stated local estimate is proved. Its constants may depend on the fixed society; this causes no problem because the numerical algorithm does not use this estimate to choose a finite replication size.
