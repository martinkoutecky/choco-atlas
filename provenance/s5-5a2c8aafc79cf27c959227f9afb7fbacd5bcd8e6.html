Theorem 3.1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given binary valuation types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), a horizon \(T\), item kinds \(G\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_{r,g}\), compute a finite-support rational assignment \(\lambda_{\theta,h}\). Each history \(h=(B_1,\ldots,B_T)\) gives one household integral bundles of goods in each round; the assignments must sum to each type's mass, use exactly each round's supplies, and satisfy TEFX at every prefix between every pair of positive-mass type-history assignments. Is there an algorithm with polynomial running time and polynomial output support in \(|G|, T, |\Theta|\), and the input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), normalized by \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also specifies \(m=|G|\geq1\) item kinds, \(T\geq1\) rounds, additive valuations \(v_\theta(g)\in\{0,1\}\), and nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_{r,g}\). Every supplied kind is valued positively by some supported type. All rounds are known in advance; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A household history \(h=(B_1,\ldots,B_T)\) consists of finite integral multisets of item kinds, encoded by binary multiplicities \(b_{r,g}(h)\). Copies received in earlier rounds remain with that household. Write \(H_t(h)=\biguplus_{r\leq t}B_r\).
An assignment has finitely many nonzero rational masses \(\lambda_{\theta,h}\geq0\), with \(\sum_h\lambda_{\theta,h}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,h}\lambda_{\theta,h}b_{r,g}(h)=q_{r,g}\) for every \(r,g\).
TEFX requires, for every \(t\), every ordered pair \((\theta,h),(\phi,k)\) of positive-mass assignments, and every individual copy \(x\in H_t(k)\), that \(v_\theta(H_t(h))\geq v_\theta(H_t(k)\setminus\{x\})\). This includes copies valued zero by \(\theta\), and comparisons between different histories of the same type.

Does constructing such a TEFX assignment admit an algorithm with running time and output support size polynomial in \(m,T,\tau,L\), or is this construction problem NP-hard?

For finite societies with strong binary valuations, a TEFX allocation exists and can be computed in finite time; the stated guarantee does not bound the running time polynomially ([Choi–Li](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17224v1)).
No result settling this exact rational-mass construction problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized society mass to one so that supplies are per capita; all listed type masses are strictly positive binary rationals.
  • Carried over strong binary valuations: values are zero or one, and every kind with positive supply is liked by some supported type.
  • Allowed nonnegative rational supplies and empty rounds, with a positive integer horizon and a nonempty set of item kinds.
  • Interpreted bundles as finite multisets permitting multiple distinct copies of a kind; encoded multiplicities in binary.
  • Interpreted histories as cumulative ownership without transfers between rounds, while allowing the entire history to be computed with advance knowledge of all supplies.
  • Specified TEFX using removal of every individual copy, including copies the comparing type values at zero, and imposed comparisons between histories assigned to the same type.

1. Exact inequalities and existence

For a history $h$, define
$$ c_{t,g}(h)=\sum_{r\leq t}b_{r,g}(h), \qquad u_{\theta,t}(h)=\sum_g v_\theta(g)c_{t,g}(h). $$

Define
$$ \varepsilon_{\theta,t}(h)= \begin{cases} \min\{v_\theta(g):c_{t,g}(h)>0\},&H_t(h)\neq\varnothing,\\ 0,&H_t(h)=\varnothing. \end{cases} $$

Because valuations are binary, $\varepsilon_{\theta,t}(h)\in\{0,1\}$.

For every ordered pair of positive-mass entries, TEFX is equivalent to
$$ u_{\theta,t}(h)\geq u_{\theta,t}(k)-\varepsilon_{\theta,t}(k). $$

Indeed, when the comparison bundle is nonempty, deleting a minimum-valued copy maximizes the remaining value. For an empty comparison bundle, the displayed inequality holds automatically by nonnegativity, just as the original comparisons are vacuous. In particular, deleting a zero-valued copy is correctly included.

If every supply vanishes, assigning the empty history to every type immediately solves the instance. Existence for general supplies follows from the finite-society guarantee stipulated in the question.

Choose a common denominator $D$ for all masses and supplies. Create $D\mu_\theta$ agents of type $\theta$ and $Dq_{r,g}$ copies in each round–kind position. These are integers, and the population size is $D$. Every supplied copy is liked by at least one agent: its kind is liked by a supported type, whose positive mass produces at least one agent.

The stipulated finite-society theorem therefore supplies a TEFX allocation. Grouping agents by type and history and dividing group sizes by $D$ gives a rational-mass assignment. Group comparisons inherit TEFX; a history’s comparison with itself holds by nonnegative valuations. No polynomial running-time conclusion is drawn from this expansion. [Choi–Li, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17224v1)

2. Every instance has a polynomial-size output

Put
$$ Q=\sum_{r,g}q_{r,g}, \qquad W_\theta=\sum_{r,g}v_\theta(g)q_{r,g}. $$

2.1 Bounding every supported history

Consider any TEFX assignment and define
$$ M_\theta=\min_{\lambda_{\theta,h}>0}u_{\theta,T}(h). $$

This minimum exists because the support is finite and type $\theta$ has positive total mass.

Comparison from a minimizing type-$\theta$ history to any supported history $k$ gives
$$ u_{\theta,T}(k)\leq M_\theta+1. $$

Furthermore,
$$ \mu_\theta M_\theta \leq \sum_h\lambda_{\theta,h}u_{\theta,T}(h) \leq \sum_{\phi,k}\lambda_{\phi,k}u_{\theta,T}(k) =W_\theta. $$

The middle inequality uses nonnegativity. The last equality follows from the supply equations and additivity. Thus
$$ u_{\theta,T}(k)\leq \frac{W_\theta}{\mu_\theta}+1. $$

A copy occurring in a positive-mass history must have positive supply in its round: otherwise a supply equation would express zero as a sum containing a positive term. Every such copy is therefore liked by at least one supported type. Consequently,
$$ |H_T(k)| \leq \sum_\theta u_{\theta,T}(k) \leq \sum_\theta\left(\frac{W_\theta}{\mu_\theta}+1\right). $$

Hence every supported multiplicity is bounded by the integer
$$ K= \left\lceil \sum_\theta\left(\frac{W_\theta}{\mu_\theta}+1\right) \right\rceil. $$

Although $K$ can be numerically large, its binary length is polynomial in the input parameters. To see this, write $\mu_{\min}=\min_\theta\mu_\theta$ and observe that
$$ K\leq \left\lceil \tau\left(\frac{Q}{\mu_{\min}}+1\right) \right\rceil. $$

The numerator and denominator lengths needed to compute $Q$ and $\mu_{\min}$ are polynomial in $L$.

2.2 Compressing the support

Set $d=\tau+mT$. Associate with each positive entry the integer column
$$ a_{\theta,h} = \bigl(e_\theta,(b_{r,g}(h))_{r,g}\bigr) \in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{d}. $$

The assignment equations are
$$ \sum_{\theta,h}\lambda_{\theta,h}a_{\theta,h} = b, \qquad b=\bigl((\mu_\theta)_\theta,(q_{r,g})_{r,g}\bigr). $$

Suppose the supported columns are linearly dependent. Since they are integer columns, there is a nonzero rational dependence $\delta$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{\theta,h}\delta_{\theta,h}a_{\theta,h}=0. $$

Summing the type-coordinate equations yields $\sum_{\theta,h}\delta_{\theta,h}=0$. Thus $\delta$ has both positive and negative entries. Define
$$ \alpha= \min_{\delta_{\theta,h}>0} \frac{\lambda_{\theta,h}}{\delta_{\theta,h}}, \qquad \lambda'_{\theta,h} = \lambda_{\theta,h}-\alpha\delta_{\theta,h}. $$

These new masses are nonnegative, preserve every assignment equation, and eliminate at least one supported entry. TEFX is preserved because no new history is introduced.

Repeating leaves linearly independent columns, so there are at most $d$ positive entries.

2.3 Bounding the rational mass encodings

Let the remaining number of columns be $s\leq d$. Select $s$ rows forming an invertible integer matrix $C$. Its entries have absolute value at most $B=\max\{1,K\}$.

Choose a common denominator $D_0$ for the input masses and supplies. Its binary length is polynomial in $L$. Multiplying the selected right-hand side by $D_0$ gives an integer vector $z$ of polynomial encoding length.

Cramer’s rule gives
$$ \lambda_i= \frac{\det(C_i(z))}{D_0\det C}, $$
where $C_i(z)$ replaces column $i$ by $z$. Hadamard’s inequality gives
$$ |\det C|\leq s^{s/2}B^s. $$

The numerator determinants satisfy the same bound with $B$ replaced by $\max\{B,\max_j|z_j|\}$. Therefore all masses have polynomial binary encoding length.

Proven conclusion. Every valid instance has a TEFX assignment with at most $\tau+mT$ positive entries and polynomial total encoding length.

This establishes short outputs, not an efficient way to discover their histories.

2.4 A finite exponential-time construction

There are at most
$$ N_{\mathrm{col}}=\tau(K+1)^{mT} $$
candidate type–history columns.

Enumerate their linearly independent subsets of size at most $d$. For each subset:

  • Check all pairwise TEFX inequalities.
  • Solve the assignment equations by exact Gaussian elimination.
  • Accept if the equations hold and all resulting masses are nonnegative, deleting zero-mass entries.

The compressed assignment proved above guarantees success. Since
$$ \log N_{\mathrm{col}} \leq \log\tau+mT\log(K+1), $$
this gives a running time bounded by $2^{\operatorname{poly}(m,T,\tau,L)}$.

3. An exact characterization by utility-minimum profiles

This characterization makes the remaining history-selection issue explicit.

For any TEFX assignment, define, at every round,
$$ M_{\theta,t} = \min_{\lambda_{\theta,h}>0}u_{\theta,t}(h). $$

These are nonnegative integers. They are nondecreasing in $t$, because every individual cumulative utility is nondecreasing. If
$$ W_{\theta,t}=\sum_{r\leq t,g}v_\theta(g)q_{r,g}, $$
then the argument from Section 2 gives
$$ \mu_\theta M_{\theta,t}\leq W_{\theta,t}. $$

For a fixed nonnegative integer profile $M=(M_{\theta,t})$, call a history $h$ admissible for type $\phi$ if
$$ u_{\phi,t}(h)\geq M_{\phi,t} $$
and, for every supported type $\theta$,
$$ u_{\theta,t}(h) \leq M_{\theta,t}+\varepsilon_{\theta,t}(h), $$
at every round $t$. Histories also exclude round–kind positions with zero supply.

Profile lemma. An assignment is TEFX if and only if its supported histories are admissible for their respective types for some nonnegative integer profile $M$.

Proof. For necessity, use the actual minima. The lower inequalities hold by definition. For an upper inequality, compare a minimizing type-$\theta$ history with $h$.

For sufficiency, let $(\theta,h)$ and $(\phi,k)$ be supported entries. Admissibility gives
$$ u_{\theta,t}(h) \geq M_{\theta,t} \geq u_{\theta,t}(k)-\varepsilon_{\theta,t}(k), $$
which is exactly TEFX. $\square$

In particular, every type’s supported own utilities differ by at most one at every round:
$$ M_{\phi,t}\leq u_{\phi,t}(h)\leq M_{\phi,t}+1. $$

If a type-$\phi$ history contains a copy that $\phi$ values at zero by round $t$, its own utility must equal $M_{\phi,t}$.

For fixed $M$, constructing an assignment becomes a linear feasibility problem over admissible history columns. The next result shows that optimizing over those columns can already be hard.

4. A fixed-profile history optimization problem is NP-complete

Consider the following auxiliary decision problem, with $T=1$:

  • Binary valuations are given, and every kind is liked by some type.
  • A nonnegative integer profile $M_\theta$ and a designated type $U$ are given.
  • Each kind has a nonnegative integer profit $w_g$.
  • The question is whether there is a history admissible for $U$ whose profit is at least a given threshold.

The supply and type-mass equations are not imposed on this single history.

4.1 Membership in NP

For an admissible history,
$$ v_\theta(h)\leq M_\theta+1 $$
for every type. Since every kind is liked by at least one type,
$$ |h|\leq\sum_\theta(M_\theta+1). $$

Thus its binary multiplicities have polynomial encoding length. Admissibility and profit can be checked in polynomial time. The auxiliary decision problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

4.2 Reduction from independent set

Let $\Gamma=(V,E)$ be a simple graph with $n=|V|\geq3$.

Introduce:

  • A vertex kind $g_v$ for every $v\in V$.
  • A filler kind $f$.
  • A private kind $p_j$ for every index $j\in V\sqcup E$.

The supported types are $U$ and one constraint type $\theta_j$ for each $j\in V\sqcup E$.

Their liked kinds are:

  • $U$ likes every $g_v$ and $f$.
  • $\theta_v$ likes precisely $g_v$ and $p_v$.
  • For $e=\{u,v\}$, $\theta_e$ likes precisely $g_u,g_v,p_e$.

All other values are zero. Every kind is liked by a supported type.

Set
$$ M_U=n+1, \qquad M_{\theta_j}=1. $$

Give profit one to each vertex kind and zero to every other kind.

Let $h$ be admissible for $U$. Its own lower bound gives
$$ v_U(h)\geq n+1. $$

For each constraint type,
$$ v_{\theta_j}(h)\leq 1+\varepsilon_{\theta_j}(h)\leq2. $$

That constraint type cannot like every copy in $h$: otherwise
$$ v_{\theta_j}(h)=|h|\geq v_U(h)\geq n+1>2, $$
a contradiction. Therefore $\varepsilon_{\theta_j}(h)=0$, and
$$ v_{\theta_j}(h)\leq1. $$

The vertex constraints imply
$$ b_{g_v}(h)+b_{p_v}(h)\leq1, $$
so each vertex multiplicity is zero or one. The edge constraints imply
$$ b_{g_u}(h)+b_{g_v}(h)+b_{p_e}(h)\leq1. $$

Consequently,
$$ S(h)=\{v:b_{g_v}(h)=1\} $$
is an independent set, and the history’s profit is $|S(h)|$.

Conversely, for any independent set $S$, define $h_S$ to contain one copy of each $g_v$ with $v\in S$ and $n+1-|S|$ copies of $f$.

Its own utility is $n+1$. It contains at least one filler copy, which every constraint type values at zero. Every vertex or edge constraint type values $h_S$ at at most one, because $S$ is independent. Hence $h_S$ is admissible and has profit $|S|$.

We have proved the exact identity
$$ \max_{h\text{ admissible for }U} \sum_g w_gb_g(h) = \alpha(\Gamma), $$
where $\alpha(\Gamma)$ is the maximum independent-set size.

For completeness, independent set is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard by the standard reduction from $\mathrm{3SAT}$: create three literal-occurrence vertices per clause, join all vertices in each clause, and join occurrences of complementary literals. An independent set of size equal to the number of clauses chooses one consistent literal per clause, and exists exactly when the formula is satisfiable. This produces graphs with $n\geq3$ whenever there is at least one clause.

Thus the auxiliary decision problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete.

4.3 The hard profiles are realized by easy TEFX assignments

The reduction does not use an artificial profile that cannot occur in a valid society.

Write $r=|V\sqcup E|$ and enumerate the constraint types by $j=1,\ldots,r$. Choose dyadic masses
$$ \mu_{\theta_j}=2^{-(j+1)}, \qquad \mu_U=\frac12+2^{-(r+1)}. $$

These masses are positive and sum to one. Put
$$ \delta=2^{-\left(\lceil\log_2 n\rceil+3\right)}, $$
so $n\delta\leq1/8<\mu_U$.

Assign:

  • Each constraint type its one-copy history $\{p_j\}$, with its entire type mass.
  • For each vertex $v$, mass $\delta$ of type $U$ the history consisting of $g_v$ and $n$ copies of $f$.
  • The remaining type-$U$ mass $\mu_U-n\delta$ the history consisting of $n+1$ copies of $f$.

Define supplies by
$$ q_{1,g_v}=\delta, \qquad q_{1,p_j}=\mu_{\theta_j}, \qquad q_{1,f}=(n+1)\mu_U-n\delta. $$

These are positive dyadic rationals of polynomial encoding length, and the displayed assignment satisfies all supply equations.

Every constraint type has own utility one. Every $U$ history has own utility $n+1$. Each constraint type values every $U$ history at at most one and has a zero-valued filler copy in that history. Different constraint types value each other’s private histories at zero; $U$ also values all those private histories at zero. These observations verify every TEFX comparison. Equivalently, all histories satisfy the profile lemma.

The actual minima are exactly the prescribed profile.

Consequence. Even knowing a realizable utility-minimum profile does not make arbitrary linear optimization over its history columns polynomial-time solvable unless $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$.

Limitation. This is not a reduction to the original construction problem. In fact, the society just constructed already has the explicit polynomial-size TEFX assignment displayed above. An algorithm for the original problem need not produce a maximum-profit history.

5. A polynomial temporal construction for disjoint liking components

Let
$$ G^+=\left\{g:\sum_r q_{r,g}>0\right\}. $$

Form the bipartite graph on supported types and $G^+$, joining $\theta$ to $g$ when $v_\theta(g)=1$.

Assume every component containing a kind is complete bipartite. Thus, within each component, every type likes every kind, and no type likes supplied kinds from another component.

Types with no incident supplied kind receive the empty history. We construct allocations independently in the remaining components.

5.1 Construction within one component

Let its type set be $\Theta_C$, kind set be $G_C$, and total household mass be
$$ a_C=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_C}\mu_\theta>0. $$

Order its round–kind positions chronologically, in any order within each round. Write their supplies as $x_1,\ldots,x_s$, where $s=T|G_C|$, and put
$$ c_0=0, \qquad c_j=\sum_{i\leq j}x_i. $$

For $z\in[0,a_C)$, define
$$ b_j(z)= \left|\left\{k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}: z+ka_C\in[c_{j-1},c_j) \right\}\right|. $$

These multiplicities can be computed directly in binary:
$$ b_j(z)= \left\lceil\frac{c_j-z}{a_C}\right\rceil - \left\lceil\frac{c_{j-1}-z}{a_C}\right\rceil. $$

For each position,
$$ \int_0^{a_C}b_j(z)\,dz=x_j. $$

Indeed, intersect $[c_{j-1},c_j)$ with the consecutive intervals $[ka_C,(k+1)a_C)$ and translate each intersection into $[0,a_C)$. Their lengths sum to $x_j$.

The multiplicity vector changes only at residues of the endpoints $c_j$ modulo $a_C$. These breakpoints partition $[0,a_C)$ into at most $s+1$ intervals. Choose one interior representative per nonempty interval. Let its history be $h_i$ and its rational length be $\ell_i$. Assign
$$ \lambda_{\theta,h_i} = \frac{\mu_\theta}{a_C}\ell_i \qquad (\theta\in\Theta_C). $$

Since $\sum_i\ell_i=a_C$, type masses are correct. For each round–kind position,
$$ \sum_{\theta\in\Theta_C,i} \lambda_{\theta,h_i}b_j(h_i) = \sum_i\ell_i b_j(h_i) =x_j. $$

Thus supplies are correct.

5.2 Temporal fairness

Let
$$ Q_{C,t}=\sum_{r\leq t,g\in G_C}q_{r,g}. $$

Chronological ordering makes all positions through round $t$ correspond to the initial interval $[0,Q_{C,t})$. Counting lattice points in that interval shows that every cumulative bundle size is either
$$ \left\lfloor\frac{Q_{C,t}}{a_C}\right\rfloor \quad\text{or}\quad \left\lceil\frac{Q_{C,t}}{a_C}\right\rceil. $$

Every type in the component values every supplied copy there at one. Therefore, for any nonempty comparison bundle and any deleted copy,
$$ v_\theta(H_t(k)\setminus\{x\}) = |H_t(k)|-1 \leq \left\lceil\frac{Q_{C,t}}{a_C}\right\rceil-1 \leq \left\lfloor\frac{Q_{C,t}}{a_C}\right\rfloor \leq |H_t(h)|. $$

This proves all comparisons within the component, including comparisons between histories of the same type.

For comparisons across components, the comparing type values every copy in the other component at zero, so TEFX holds by nonnegativity.

5.3 Complexity

The construction uses rational addition, residues, sorting, and integer rounding on polynomial-length numbers. Large multiplicities are computed in binary; copies are never enumerated.

The support size is at most
$$ \sum_C|\Theta_C|(T|G_C|+1), $$
plus the isolated types, and therefore at most $\tau(mT+1)$.

Support compression can reduce this to $\tau+mT$ in polynomial time on these already known columns. There are polynomially many elimination steps. Integer dependence coefficients can be chosen using minors of the column matrix, with polynomial binary length. Maintaining a common denominator for the masses adds at most the dependence-coefficient length at each step, so intermediate rational encodings remain polynomial.

Proven conclusion. Under the complete-bipartite-component hypothesis, arbitrary-round TEFX construction takes polynomial time and admits output support at most $\tau+mT$.

6. Corrected TFNP formulation and the hardness obstruction

Under the standard explicit-table encoding, Section 2 supplies a fixed polynomial $p(L)$ bounding the length of some valid assignment for every valid input.

Define the search relation to accept only assignments that:

  • Have at most $\tau+mT$ positive entries.
  • Have encoding length at most $p(L)$.
  • Satisfy the type, supply, and TEFX conditions.

The explicit length restriction repairs the issue identified by the verifier. Existence of a short output alone would not make the unrestricted validity relation polynomially balanced.

Verification is polynomial-time: exact rational arithmetic checks the assignment equations, while cumulative multiplicities and the inequalities from Section 1 check TEFX without enumerating copies. Invalid input encodings can receive a distinguished trivial output.

This length-restricted, total, polynomially verifiable relation belongs to $\mathrm{TFNP}$. [Megiddo–Papadimitriou, Section 1](https://theory.stanford.edu/~megiddo/pdf/papadimX.pdf)

There is also a precise obstruction to a common hardness reduction. Suppose polynomial-time functions $f$ and $g$ satisfy:

  • For every Boolean formula $F$, $f(F)$ is a valid society.
  • For every valid TEFX assignment $A$ of $f(F)$, $g(F,A)$ correctly decides whether $F$ is satisfiable.

An unsatisfiable formula then has a polynomial certificate: a short valid assignment $A$, followed by verification that $g(F,A)$ reports unsatisfiability. Such a certificate exists by Section 2. No satisfiable formula has one, by decoder correctness.

Thus $\mathrm{UNSAT}\in\mathrm{NP}$, which implies $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$.

This does not prove polynomial construction. It also does not turn the fixed-profile optimization hardness into hardness of the original total search problem.

7. What remains unresolved

Polynomial support and polynomial output encoding are established. The new hardness result shows that an algorithm cannot generally rely on polynomial-time maximization of arbitrary linear profits over fixed-profile histories unless $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$.

The unresolved issue is how to find compatible histories and masses together for general overlapping valuations across multiple rounds—or how to establish hardness of that original construction problem under a precise search reduction.

6. $\mathrm{TFNP}$ formulation and reduction obstruction

Assessment: correct.

The explicit length restriction makes the relation polynomially balanced. Section 2 guarantees a witness within a uniform polynomial bound under the explicit-table encoding.

Validity of an input and validity of a bounded output are polynomially checkable. The distinguished output for invalid inputs makes the relation total on all input strings. These are exactly the relevant $\mathrm{TFNP}$ conditions. [Megiddo–Papadimitriou, Section 1](https://theory.stanford.edu/~megiddo/pdf/papadimX.pdf)

The conditional reduction obstruction is sound. Its decisive hypothesis is decoder correctness for every valid assignment. Under that hypothesis, a short verified assignment decoded as unsatisfiable is an $\mathrm{NP}$ certificate for unsatisfiability. Such certificates exist for unsatisfiable formulas and cannot exist for satisfiable ones.

The conclusion $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$ follows. The prover correctly limits this argument to the reduction it specifies; it does not establish a general prohibition on all possible hardness notions.
