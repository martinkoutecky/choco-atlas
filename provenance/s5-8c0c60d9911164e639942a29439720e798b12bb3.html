Proposition 1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\ell}\) of participation types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), where \(t_k=1\) means type \(t\) belongs to the coalition at rank \(k\), compute for each \(t\in T\) its minimum adjacent-swap distance \(\iota(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell} t_k\sum_{k'=k+1}^{\ell}(1-t_{k'})\) to having its coalition memberships form a prefix of the ranking, and rank the supported types by increasing \(\iota(t)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=\ell\geq 1\) be the number of coalition occurrences in a strict ranking, indexed from worst to best; repeated coalitions are allowed. A participation type \(t\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}\) records membership in each occurrence.
A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) distinct types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. Require \(\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}t_k\geq1\) for every supported type.
For each type separately, a move swaps two adjacent coalition occurrences at unit cost. Unanimous support means that its memberships form a nonempty prefix when the ranking is read from best to worst. Moves preserve membership counts; different types may use different swap sequences.
Compute every minimum distance \(\iota(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}t_k\sum_{k'=k+1}^{\ell}(1-t_{k'})\) and the weak ranking of supported types by increasing distance, retaining equal distances as ties.

Can these distances and their induced weak ranking be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computation \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite individuals and a strict ranking of coalition occurrences, the minimum adjacent-swap distance to unanimous support equals the displayed inversion count ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18228v1#S3.SS1)). This result supplies an exact formula, rather than a complexity classification, and does not analyze societies encoded by rational masses.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Indexed ranks from worst to best, so the supplied inversion formula measures distance to a prefix read from best to worst.
  • Excluded the all-zero type: the definition requires a nonempty unanimously supporting prefix, which cannot be reached by swaps without an initial membership.
  • Specified unit-cost adjacent swaps separately for each type, rather than requiring a common rearrangement for all types.
  • Retained equal distances as ties, matching the inversion-induced weak ranking.
  • Set m equal to ell, the number of coalition occurrences, and defined L as total input bit length.

Result

The distances and their induced weak ranking are computable in deterministic polynomial time. The rational masses do not enter either computation: each distance depends only on its explicitly listed participation type.

1. Proof of the minimum-distance formula

Fix a supported type \(t\), and let \(r=\sum_{k=1}^{m}t_k\). By hypothesis, \(r\geq 1\). Since swaps preserve membership counts, unanimous support requires the membership sequence, read from worst to best, to become \(0^{m-r}1^r\).

For any binary sequence \(s\), define its inversion count by
\(I(s)=\sum_{1\leq k<j\leq m}s_k(1-s_j)\).
Thus an inversion is a membership occurrence preceding a nonmembership occurrence.

Swapping two adjacent entries leaves all inversion contributions involving other positions unchanged: every other position lies either before both swapped positions or after both. Consequently:

  • Swapping adjacent entries \(10\) to \(01\) decreases \(I\) by exactly one.
  • Swapping \(01\) to \(10\) increases \(I\) by exactly one.
  • Swapping equal entries leaves \(I\) unchanged.

The target sequence has inversion count zero. Therefore, every successful sequence of swaps has length at least \(I(t)\).

Conversely, whenever \(I(s)>0\), some adjacent entries are \(10\). Indeed, choose an inversion with endpoints \(k<j\); the sequence starts with a one at \(k\) and reaches a zero at \(j\), so it contains a consecutive transition from one to zero. Swap such adjacent entries. Each step decreases \(I\) by exactly one, so after exactly \(I(t)\) steps the inversion count is zero. A binary sequence with no inversions has all its zeros before all its ones; preservation of \(r\) therefore makes it precisely \(0^{m-r}1^r\).

Every binary swap used here is realized by swapping the corresponding adjacent coalition occurrences. Repeated coalitions cause no obstruction because positions represent occurrences.

Hence the minimum distance is
\(\iota(t)=I(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}t_k\sum_{j=k+1}^{m}(1-t_j)\).
This agrees with [Proposition 1 of the supplied source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18228v1#S3.SS1).

2. Computing the distances and ties

For each listed type, scan its entries from best to worst, namely in the order \(m,m-1,\ldots,1\). Maintain two integer counters, initially \(z=d=0\).

  • If the current entry is zero, increase \(z\) by one.
  • If the current entry is one, increase \(d\) by \(z\).

Immediately before processing position \(k\), the counter satisfies
\(z=\sum_{j=k+1}^{m}(1-t_j)\):
it counts exactly the zeros already encountered. Thus processing a one adds precisely its contribution to the displayed inversion formula. At termination, \(d=\iota(t)\).

Compute this value for every supported type. Sort the resulting records by increasing distance, then group consecutive records with equal distances. Sorting places every smaller distance before every larger distance, and grouping retains precisely the required ties.

3. Bit complexity and rational masses

For a type with \(r\) ones, there are only \(r(m-r)\) possible one–zero pairs. Therefore,
\(0\leq\iota(t)\leq r(m-r)\leq\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor\).
The distance and scan counters consequently require \(O(\log(m+1))\) bits.

All scans take \(O(\tau m\log(m+1))\) bit operations. Comparison sorting uses \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) comparisons. Each comparison takes \(O(\log(m+1))\) bit operations. Allowing also for moving records containing binary support indices gives the polynomial bound
\(O\!\left(L+\tau m\log(m+1)+\tau\log(\tau+1)\bigl(\log(m+1)+\log(\tau+1)\bigr)\right)\).

The masses are promised to be valid positive rational numbers. Their numerators and denominators need no arithmetic: the requested distances and ranking do not depend on their values. Reading their encoding is covered by the \(O(L)\) term. In particular, the algorithm never expands a rational mass into a population of individuals or enumerates unsupported types.

Thus the requested function belongs to \(\mathrm{FP}\). Under polynomial-time reductions, an \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness classification would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
