The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), positive rational weights \(a_t\), a target type \(t^\star\), and a quota fraction \(\rho\in(0,1)\), consider each admissible population size \(N\) with \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb{Z}\). Set the quota to \(\lceil \rho N\bar a\rceil\), where \(\bar a=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_ta_t\). Let \(\Phi_N\) be the total Shapley–Shubik index of the \(t^\star\) players; let \(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{split}}\) be that total when every such player splits into two identities of weight \(a_{t^\star}/2\). Decide whether \(\liminf_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu_t\in\mathbb{Z}\ \forall t}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{split}}-\Phi_N)>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a positive binary rational weight \(a_t\). The input also specifies \(t^\star\in T\) and a binary rational \(\rho\in(0,1)\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; running times are measured in \(\tau,L\), with no separate \(m\) parameter.
For each sufficiently large integer \(N\) satisfying \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\) for every \(t\), form a weighted voting game with \(N\mu_t\) distinct players of weight \(a_t\), and quota \(q_N=\lceil\rho N\bar a\rceil\), where \(\bar a=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_ta_t\). A coalition wins precisely when its weight is at least \(q_N\).
Let \(\Phi_N\) be the sum of the Shapley–Shubik indices of the type \(t^\star\) players. Simultaneously replace every such player by two distinct identities, each of weight \(a_{t^\star}/2\), keeping \(q_N\) fixed. Let \(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{split}}\) sum the indices of all these replacement identities in the resulting game.

Is deciding whether \(\liminf_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\ \forall t\in T}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{split}}-\Phi_N)>0\) solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a finite explicitly listed electorate with binary integer weights and quota, deciding whether one designated player's equal two-way split strictly increases its combined Shapley–Shubik power is NP-hard ([Bachrach–Elkind](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas08/proceedings/pdf/paper/AAMAS08_0085.pdf)); the [expanded treatment](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.3869) retains this result.
On the continuum side, [Neyman](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.13.4.556) establishes existence of asymptotic values for weighted majority games; that result does not formulate this simultaneous-splitting decision problem.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified that the quota fraction is a binary rational, so the problem has a finite binary input.
  • Used the formal winning condition that coalition weight meets or exceeds the quota.
  • Kept the original quota fixed after splitting, and counted every replacement identity as a distinct player in the resulting game.
  • Restricted the sequence to sufficiently large admissible population sizes, ensuring that the grand coalition wins; removing finitely many sizes leaves the liminf unchanged.
  • Defined total input bit length L and used support size tau as the structural parameter; this problem has no separate candidate or alternative count m.

1. Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. Every valid input has answer NO. In fact, both aggregate powers converge, and
\( \lim_N\Phi_N=\lim_N\Phi_N^{\mathrm{split}} =\frac{\mu_{t^\star}a_{t^\star}}{\bar a}. \)
Consequently,
\( \lim_N(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{split}}-\Phi_N)=0. \)

All limits here and below are taken through the admissible integers. This set is unbounded because the masses are rational.

The proof must control individual indices to accuracy \(o(N^{-1})\), since the designated group contains a number of players proportional to \(N\). We establish that control explicitly.

2. An exact integral representation

Assign every player an independent uniform arrival time in \([0,1]\). Their arrival order is a uniformly random permutation: ties have probability zero, and exchangeability gives every permutation equal probability.

Consider a player of weight \(w\), with quota \(Q\). Conditional on its arrival time being \(p\), every other player independently precedes it with probability \(p\). Let \(S_N(p)\) denote their total weight. The player is pivotal exactly when
\( Q-w\leq S_N(p)<Q. \)
Thus its Shapley–Shubik index is
\( \phi_N=\int_0^1 \Pr\!\left(Q-w\leq S_N(p)<Q\right)\,dp. \)

We next estimate the integrated probability of each possible predecessor total.

3. An integrated lattice lemma

Lemma. Fix positive integers \(b_1,\ldots,b_k\) satisfying
\( \gcd(b_1,\ldots,b_k)=1, \)
and positive constants \(c_1,\ldots,c_k\). Suppose the integer multiplicities satisfy
\( n_j(N)=Nc_j+O(1). \)
Define
\( A=\sum_jc_jb_j,\qquad B=\sum_jc_jb_j^2. \)
For each \(p\in[0,1]\), let
\( S_N(p)=\sum_{j=1}^k b_jX_{j,N}(p), \)
where the variables \(X_{j,N}(p)\) are independent and have distributions
\( X_{j,N}(p)\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n_j(N),p). \)

Fix \(\rho\in(0,1)\). For every integer sequence \(z_N\) satisfying
\( z_N=\rho NA+O(1), \)
we have
\( \int_0^1\Pr(S_N(p)=z_N)\,dp =\frac{1}{NA}+o(N^{-1}). \)

All constants in this lemma may depend on the fixed weights and proportions.

3.1. Uniform local approximation

Write
\( M_N=\sum_jn_j(N)b_j=NA+O(1), \qquad V_N=\sum_jn_j(N)b_j^2=NB+O(1). \)
The mean and variance of \(S_N(p)\) are \(pM_N\) and \(p(1-p)V_N\).

For every compact interval \(I\subset(0,1)\), we first prove, uniformly over \(p\in I\) and \(z\in\mathbb Z\),
\( \Pr(S_N(p)=z) = G_N(p,z)+O(N^{-1}), \)
where
\( G_N(p,z)= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi p(1-p)V_N}} \exp\!\left(-\frac{(z-pM_N)^2}{2p(1-p)V_N}\right). \)

Let
\( F_N(\theta,p)= \mathbb E\exp\!\left(i\theta(S_N(p)-pM_N)\right). \)
For one Bernoulli contribution,
\( \left|\mathbb E e^{i\theta b_j(X-p)}\right|^2 = 1-4p(1-p)\sin^2(b_j\theta/2). \)

Choose a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\). For \(|\theta|\leq\delta\), continuous logarithms of these characteristic functions exist, and Taylor expansion gives
\( \log F_N(\theta,p) = -\frac12p(1-p)V_N\theta^2+R_N(\theta,p), \qquad |R_N(\theta,p)|\leq CN|\theta|^3. \)
Uniformity follows because \(I\) is compact, the weights are fixed, and there are \(O(N)\) independent contributions.

The modulus identity also gives
\( \operatorname{Re}\log F_N(\theta,p)\leq-cN\theta^2 \)
on this interval. Indeed, for sufficiently small \(\delta\), each \(\sin^2(b_j\theta/2)\) is bounded below by a positive constant times \(b_j^2\theta^2\); also \(p(1-p)\) is bounded below on \(I\).

Both logarithms therefore have real parts bounded above by a negative constant times \(N\theta^2\). Using
\( e^x-e^y=(x-y)\int_0^1e^{(1-s)y+sx}\,ds, \)
we obtain
\( \left|F_N(\theta,p) -e^{-p(1-p)V_N\theta^2/2}\right| \leq CN|\theta|^3e^{-c'N\theta^2}. \)
Its integral over \([-\delta,\delta]\) is \(O(N^{-1})\), since
\( N\int_{\mathbb R}|\theta|^3e^{-c'N\theta^2}\,d\theta =O(N^{-1}). \)

On \(\delta\leq|\theta|\leq\pi\), the function
\( \sum_jc_j\sin^2(b_j\theta/2) \)
has a strictly positive minimum. To verify this, simultaneous vanishing would require \(b_j\theta\in2\pi\mathbb Z\) for every \(j\). Bézout’s identity and the greatest-common-divisor assumption would then imply \(\theta\in2\pi\mathbb Z\), impossible on this compact set. Since \(n_j(N)\geq Nc_j/2\) for sufficiently large \(N\), the modulus identity consequently yields
\( |F_N(\theta,p)|\leq e^{-c''N}. \)

Finally, Fourier inversion gives
\( \Pr(S_N(p)=z) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{-i\theta(z-pM_N)}F_N(\theta,p)\,d\theta. \)
The inverse Fourier transform over \(\mathbb R\) of
\( e^{-p(1-p)V_N\theta^2/2} \)
is \(G_N(p,z)\). Its integral outside \([-\delta,\delta]\) is exponentially small. The preceding bounds prove the claimed uniform \(O(N^{-1})\) approximation.

3.2. Integrating the local approximation

Set
\( J_N=[\rho-N^{-2/5},\,\rho+N^{-2/5}]. \)
For sufficiently large \(N\), this interval lies inside a fixed compact subinterval of \((0,1)\).

Outside \(J_N\),
\( |z_N-pM_N| = |NA(\rho-p)+O(1)| \geq \frac A2N^{3/5}. \)
The sum \(S_N(p)\) consists of independent variables with ranges of lengths \(b_j\); the sum of their squared range lengths is \(V_N=O(N)\). Hoeffding’s inequality therefore gives
\( \Pr(S_N(p)=z_N) \leq 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{2(z_N-pM_N)^2}{V_N}\right) \leq 2e^{-cN^{1/5}}. \)
Hence the integral outside \(J_N\) is \(o(N^{-1})\).

Inside \(J_N\), the integrated error in the local approximation is
\( O(N^{-1}|J_N|)=O(N^{-7/5})=o(N^{-1}). \)

It remains to integrate \(G_N\). Put
\( p_N=z_N/M_N=\rho+O(N^{-1}), \qquad p=p_N+u/\sqrt N. \)
Then \(z_N-pM_N=-uM_N/\sqrt N\). The transformed integration intervals tend to all of \(\mathbb R\), because their endpoints are asymptotic to \(\pm N^{1/10}\).

Since
\( M_N/N\longrightarrow A,\qquad V_N/N\longrightarrow B, \)
the transformed integrand for \(N\int_{J_N}G_N(p,z_N)\,dp\) converges pointwise to
\( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\rho(1-\rho)B}} \exp\!\left(-\frac{A^2u^2}{2\rho(1-\rho)B}\right). \)
On the transformed intervals it is bounded by \(Ce^{-cu^2}\): \(p\) stays in a compact subinterval of \((0,1)\), and \(M_N^2/(NV_N)\) stays bounded away from zero. Extending it by zero elsewhere, dominated convergence therefore applies. It yields
\( \lim_N N\int_{J_N}G_N(p,z_N)\,dp = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\rho(1-\rho)B}} \int_{\mathbb R} e^{-A^2u^2/(2\rho(1-\rho)B)}\,du =\frac1A. \)

Combining the central integral, its approximation error, and the tail bound proves the lemma.

4. Replicated types have power proportional to weight

We now deduce a general consequence that permits the number of identities to change.

Suppose category \(j\) contains \(Nc_j\) players of fixed positive rational weight \(w_j\), where every \(c_j\) is a positive rational. The proportions need not sum to one. Let
\( W=\sum_jc_jw_j, \)
and suppose the quota satisfies
\( Q_N=\rho NW+O(1), \qquad 0<\rho<1. \)

Claim. Every player in category \(j\) has index
\( \phi_{j,N}=\frac{w_j}{NW}+o(N^{-1}), \)
so that category’s aggregate power converges to
\( \frac{c_jw_j}{W}. \)

Proof. Because the weights are positive rationals, there is a positive rational \(h\) such that
\( b_j=w_j/h\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad \gcd(b_1,\ldots,b_k)=1. \)
For example, clear the denominators and then divide the resulting integers by their greatest common divisor. Scaling weights and quota by \(1/h\) leaves the game unchanged.

Write
\( R_N=Q_N/h,\qquad A=W/h=\sum_jc_jb_j. \)
Thus
\( R_N=\rho NA+O(1). \)

Fix one category-\(j\) player. After excluding that player, the predecessor multiplicities are \(Nc_\ell\), except that category \(j\) has multiplicity \(Nc_j-1\). These satisfy every hypothesis of the lemma.

The predecessor total \(S_N(p)\), in scaled units, is integer-valued. The pivotal interval
\( R_N-b_j\leq S_N(p)<R_N \)
contains exactly the \(b_j\) integers
\( \lceil R_N\rceil-b_j,\, \lceil R_N\rceil-b_j+1,\, \ldots,\, \lceil R_N\rceil-1. \)
Every one of these integer sequences equals \(\rho NA+O(1)\). Applying the lemma to each, and using the integral representation, gives
\( \phi_{j,N} = \frac{b_j}{NA}+o(N^{-1}) = \frac{w_j}{NW}+o(N^{-1}). \)
There are only \(b_j\) terms, and \(b_j\) is fixed as \(N\) grows.

Players within a category have equal indices by permutation symmetry. Multiplying by their number \(Nc_j\) proves the aggregate limit. This also shows explicitly why lattice effects and quota rounding do not alter that limit.

5. Applying the claim before and after splitting

Before splitting, take
\( c_t=\mu_t,\qquad w_t=a_t. \)
Then \(W=\bar a\), and
\( q_N=\lceil\rho N\bar a\rceil=\rho N\bar a+O(1). \)
Therefore
\( \lim_N\Phi_N = \frac{\mu_{t^\star}a_{t^\star}}{\bar a}. \)

After splitting, use the categories
\( c_t'=\mu_t,\quad w_t'=a_t \quad(t\ne t^\star), \)
and
\( c_{t^\star}'=2\mu_{t^\star}, \qquad w_{t^\star}'=a_{t^\star}/2. \)
Their total weight per unit \(N\) remains
\( W' = \sum_{t\ne t^\star}\mu_ta_t + 2\mu_{t^\star}(a_{t^\star}/2) =\bar a. \)
The quota is unchanged and thus satisfies the same required asymptotic relation. The claim applies even though the category proportions now sum to \(1+\mu_{t^\star}\). It gives
\( \lim_N\Phi_N^{\mathrm{split}} = \frac{2\mu_{t^\star}(a_{t^\star}/2)}{\bar a} = \frac{\mu_{t^\star}a_{t^\star}}{\bar a}. \)

Subtracting these convergent sequences establishes
\( \liminf_N(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{split}}-\Phi_N)=0. \)
