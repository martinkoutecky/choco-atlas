The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite list of student types with rational masses, strict college rankings, priority tiers, and per-capita college quotas, does the mass analogue of irrelevance of low-tier agents imply that the profile produced by iterative mass-based DA is a P-SSN against deviations by measurable subpopulations, including fractional parts of a type? A passively weak deviation has positive mass of strict beneficiaries, while every coalition member weakly prefers the joint-deviation outcome to the outcome when only those beneficiaries deviate.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(a_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. There are \(m\geq1\) colleges, binary-rational quotas \(q_c\in[0,1]\), and total input bit length \(L\). Each type has a strict true ranking over colleges and the unmatched option \(\varnothing\), and an acceptability flag and integer priority tier at each college. Any algorithmic running times are stated in \(m,\tau,L\) and the number \(K+1\) of peeling stages.
Realize type \(t\) as \(\{t\}\times[0,a_t)\), with Lebesgue mass \(\lambda\). Colleges use responsive choice: reject unacceptable students and retain the highest-priority mass up to quota, ordering acceptable students lexicographically by increasing tier, type index, and interval coordinate. These priorities remain fixed under reports and population removal.
A report is any strict ranking over colleges and \(\varnothing\). Mass DA uses simultaneous student-proposing rounds with the preceding college choices; exhausted students propose to \(\varnothing\), which accepts them permanently. Its outcome \(D(r)\) is the almost-everywhere eventual assignment, allowing countably many rounds.
Define iterative mass DA by starting with the whole society and, at stage \(k\), running truthful DA on the remaining population with the original quotas, obtaining \(\mu_k\). Let \(L_k\) be the students making a proposal, including to \(\varnothing\), in that run's final nonempty proposal round; remove precisely \(L_k\). Consider inputs for which these runs terminate in finitely many rounds and the peeling exhausts the society up to null sets at stage \(K\geq1\).
For \(x\in L_k\), define \(r^*(x)\) by listing \(\mu_k(x),\mu_{k-1}(x),\ldots,\mu_0(x),\varnothing\), deleting repeated entries after their first occurrence, and placing all unlisted colleges below \(\varnothing\) in college-index order. Set \(\mu^*=D(r^*)\).
Mass irrelevance of low-tier agents means that, whenever \(k'<k\), \(\mu^*(x)\succ_x\mu^*(y)\) for \(\lambda\otimes\lambda\)-almost every \((x,y)\in L_k\times L_{k'}\); these peeling tiers are distinct from college priority tiers.
A deviation consists of a measurable coalition \(S\) of positive mass and measurable alternative reports \(r'_S\). Write \(\nu=D(r'_S,r^*_{I\setminus S})\), \(T=\{x\in S:\nu(x)\succeq_x\mu^*(x)\}\), and \(\nu'=D(r'_T,r^*_{I\setminus T})\). It is passively weak if \(\lambda(\{x\in S:\nu(x)\succ_x\mu^*(x)\})>0\) and \(\nu(x)\succeq_x\nu'(x)\) almost everywhere on \(S\). A P-SSN admits no such deviation; coalitions may contain arbitrary measurable fractions of a type.

Does mass irrelevance of low-tier agents always imply that \(r^*\) is a P-SSN under these conventions, or is there a counterexample?

For finite students with strict college priorities and responsive preferences, irrelevance of low-tier agents implies that the iterative-DA report profile is a P-SSN ([Li–Inohara–Kitamura](https://doi.org/10.4236/tel.2017.75084)).
The discrete iterative construction also supports a strictly strong Nash equilibrium ([Bando](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2014.05.009)); no result settling the precisely defined mass implication was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total student mass to one and interpreted per-capita quotas as fixed binary-rational capacities in [0,1], without rescaling them after population removal.
  • Included the unmatched option in true and reported strict rankings and retained college-side unacceptability, both of which are present in the discrete model.
  • Resolved unspecified priority ties by a fixed deterministic order using type index and interval coordinate after the college's priority tier; assignments and welfare comparisons concern individual students rather than lotteries.
  • Defined mass DA through simultaneous proposal rounds and almost-everywhere eventual assignments, including countably many rounds for deviation outcomes.
  • Made last proposers explicit through the final nonempty proposal round, treating exhaustion as a proposal to the unmatched option.
  • Restricted the iterative construction to inputs on which its truthful runs and peeling terminate finitely, so that last-proposer sets and the produced profile exist; retained the source definition's condition K >= 1.
  • Expanded the iterative report lists into complete strict rankings by deleting duplicates and placing omitted colleges below the unmatched option in fixed order.
  • Interpreted mass irrelevance as an almost-everywhere condition across pairs of students in different peeling stages, rather than across college priority tiers.
  • Restored the counterfactual subgroup T to all weak beneficiaries, including indifferent members, as in the formal discrete definition; positive mass is required only for strict beneficiaries.
  • Allowed arbitrary measurable coalitions and reports, including irrational-mass fractions of types, with welfare requirements holding almost everywhere.

Conclusion

There is a counterexample under the stated conventions. In the example below, every coalition member strictly prefers the full deviation outcome to the outcome when only \(T\) deviates. All DA runs terminate finitely.

The counterexample also exposes a problem with importing the quoted finite implication to this unrestricted-report definition.

Society, preferences, and priorities

There are six types and five colleges, indexed \(A,B,C,D,E\). Write \(I_t=\{t\}\times[0,a_t)\), where

\(a_3=3/8\), and \(a_t=1/8\) for \(t\in\{1,2,4,5,6\}\).

Every college has quota \(1/8\), and every type is acceptable to every college. The strict true rankings are:

| Type | True ranking |
|---|---|
| \(1\) | \(B\succ E\succ A\succ C\succ D\succ\varnothing\) |
| \(2\) | \(C\succ\varnothing\succ A\succ B\succ D\succ E\) |
| \(3\) | \(C\succ A\succ\varnothing\succ B\succ D\succ E\) |
| \(4\) | \(D\succ A\succ\varnothing\succ B\succ C\succ E\) |
| \(5\) | \(B\succ A\succ\varnothing\succ C\succ D\succ E\) |
| \(6\) | \(A\succ\varnothing\succ B\succ C\succ D\succ E\) |

Assign integer priority tiers according to these orders, from highest to lowest priority:

| College | Priority order of types |
|---|---|
| \(A\) | \(4,5,6,3,1,2\) |
| \(B\) | \(6,1,5,2,3,4\) |
| \(C\) | \(2,3,1,4,5,6\) |
| \(D\) | \(5,4,1,2,3,6\) |
| \(E\) | \(1,2,3,4,5,6\) |

Thus a type’s integer tier is its position in the corresponding row. Within a type, priority follows increasing interval coordinate, as required.

Every accepted type in the calculations below has mass exactly \(1/8\). Type \(3\), of mass \(3/8\), is always rejected in its entirety. Consequently, no choice in these runs splits a type, and the calculations apply directly to the specified mass choice rules.

Truthful peeling

Stage zero

The truthful DA rounds are:

| Round | New proposals | Rejections |
|---|---|---|
| \(1\) | \(1,5\) to \(B\); \(2,3\) to \(C\); \(4\) to \(D\); \(6\) to \(A\) | Types \(5,3\) |
| \(2\) | Types \(5,3\) to \(A\) | Types \(3,6\) |
| \(3\) | Types \(3,6\) to \(\varnothing\) | None |

In round \(1\), \(B\) retains type \(1\), and \(C\) retains type \(2\). In round \(2\), \(A\) chooses type \(5\) over both its incumbent type \(6\) and the newly proposing type \(3\).

Hence \(L_0=I_3\cup I_6\), and the stage-zero assignments by type are

\((\mu_0(1),\ldots,\mu_0(6))=(B,C,\varnothing,D,A,\varnothing)\).

Stage one

The remaining types are \(1,2,4,5\). In round \(1\), types \(1,5\) propose to \(B\), type \(2\) to \(C\), and type \(4\) to \(D\). Only type \(5\) is rejected.

In round \(2\), type \(5\) proposes to \(A\) and is accepted. Therefore \(L_1=I_5\). Every remaining type receives the same assignment as at stage zero.

Stage two

The remaining types \(1,2,4\) propose respectively to \(B,C,D\), and all are accepted in round \(1\). Thus

\(L_2=I_1\cup I_2\cup I_4\).

The peeling exhausts the society, with \(K=2\).

The constructed profile and irrelevance

Every type’s assignments in all stages in which it participates are identical. Therefore its constructed report lists that assignment, followed by \(\varnothing\), and then the unlisted colleges in index order. For types \(3,6\), the report starts with \(\varnothing\).

Under \(r^*\), the first-round proposals are to distinct colleges: type \(1\) proposes to \(B\), type \(2\) to \(C\), type \(4\) to \(D\), and type \(5\) to \(A\). Types \(3,6\) propose to \(\varnothing\). Everyone is accepted immediately, so

\((\mu^*(1),\ldots,\mu^*(6))=(B,C,\varnothing,D,A,\varnothing)\).

Mass irrelevance holds:

  • The only assignment in \(L_0\) is \(\varnothing\), and type \(5\), in \(L_1\), strictly prefers its assignment \(A\) to \(\varnothing\).
  • Types \(1,2,4\), in \(L_2\), receive respectively \(B,C,D\). Each strictly prefers its assignment to both \(A\), the assignment in \(L_1\), and \(\varnothing\), the assignment in \(L_0\).

These comparisons hold throughout the relevant type intervals, hence satisfy the required almost-everywhere product-measure condition.

A passively weak deviation

Take the measurable coalition

\(S=I_1\cup I_4\cup I_5\cup I_6\),

of mass \(1/2\). Its alternative reports are constant within each type:

| Type | Alternative report |
|---|---|
| \(1\) | \(E\succ\varnothing\succ A\succ B\succ C\succ D\) |
| \(4\) | \(D\succ A\succ\varnothing\succ B\succ C\succ E\) |
| \(5\) | \(B\succ D\succ\varnothing\succ A\succ C\succ E\) |
| \(6\) | \(A\succ B\succ\varnothing\succ C\succ D\succ E\) |

When all of \(S\) deviates, the first-round proposals are type \(1\) to \(E\), type \(2\) to \(C\), type \(3\) to \(\varnothing\), type \(4\) to \(D\), type \(5\) to \(B\), and type \(6\) to \(A\). All are accepted. Thus

\((\nu(1),\ldots,\nu(6))=(E,C,\varnothing,D,B,A)\).

Type \(1\) is strictly worse off because \(B\succ_1 E\). Type \(4\) is unchanged, while types \(5,6\) strictly improve. Consequently,

\(T=I_4\cup I_5\cup I_6\),

and the strictly improving coalition members have total mass \(1/4\).

DA when only \(T\) deviates

Type \(1\) now uses its baseline report \(B\succ\varnothing\), with all other colleges below \(\varnothing\). The rounds are:

| Round | New proposals | Rejections |
|---|---|---|
| \(1\) | \(1,5\) to \(B\); \(2\) to \(C\); \(3\) to \(\varnothing\); \(4\) to \(D\); \(6\) to \(A\) | Type \(5\) |
| \(2\) | Type \(5\) to \(D\) | Type \(4\) |
| \(3\) | Type \(4\) to \(A\) | Type \(6\) |
| \(4\) | Type \(6\) to \(B\) | Type \(1\) |
| \(5\) | Type \(1\) to \(\varnothing\) | None |

Each rejection follows directly from the priority table: \(1\) outranks \(5\) at \(B\), \(5\) outranks \(4\) at \(D\), \(4\) outranks \(6\) at \(A\), and \(6\) outranks \(1\) at \(B\). Therefore

\((\nu'(1),\ldots,\nu'(6))=(\varnothing,C,\varnothing,A,D,B)\).

For every coalition member, the full deviation is strictly preferable to this outcome:

| Type | Comparison |
|---|---|
| \(1\) | \(E\succ_1\varnothing\) |
| \(4\) | \(D\succ_4 A\) |
| \(5\) | \(B\succ_5 D\) |
| \(6\) | \(A\succ_6 B\) |

Thus \(\nu(x)\succeq_x\nu'(x)\) everywhere on \(S\), and a positive mass strictly improves relative to \(\mu^*\). This is a passively weak deviation. Hence \(r^*\) is not a P-SSN.

Why the quoted finite result does not resolve these conventions

This example also has an exact finite realization: replace each mass \(1/8\) by one student, type \(3\) by three students, and each quota by one seat. Break priorities between the three type-\(3\) students by coordinate order. Every displayed round remains unchanged. Thus the counterexample also contradicts the unqualified finite implication when deviations are interpreted exactly as here.

The [proof of Proposition 2 in Li–Inohara–Kitamura](https://content.scirp.org/pdf/tel_2017072816050995.pdf) assumes deviating reports of the form “deviation outcome, original truthful outcome, unmatched.” That normalization does not preserve passive deviations in this example.

Indeed, normalizing the reports of \(T\) gives type \(4\) the list \(D,\varnothing\), type \(5\) the list \(B,A,\varnothing\), and type \(6\) the list \(A,\varnothing\). With only these normalized reports deviating, type \(5\) is rejected from \(B\), takes \(A\), and displaces type \(6\) to \(\varnothing\). The outcome is exactly \(\mu^*\). Type \(1\) would then receive \(B\) without participating, which it prefers to its full-deviation assignment \(E\); passivity is lost.

The unrestricted reports allowed in the question permit precisely the additional proposals that sustain the threat.
