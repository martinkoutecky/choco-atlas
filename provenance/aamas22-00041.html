The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite complete role types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a role-symmetric support relation \(A\subseteq T\times T\), a baseline action \(d_0:T\to T\cup\{\mathrm{vote}\}\), target role \(r\in T\), normalized budget \(B\), and target mass \(\theta\), choose a finite mass-valued acyclic delegation forest whose subcohort masses sum to \(\mu_t\), whose arcs obey \(A\), and whose changed-delegation mass \(\sum_v a_v\mathbf{1}[p(v)\ne d_0(\ell(v))]\) is at most \(B\); decide whether at least \(\theta\) mass has a delegation root of type \(r\), and maximize that rooted mass in the optimization version.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) complete role types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; there are no separate candidates, so set \(m=\tau\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Input also specifies a directed relation \(A\subseteq T\times T\), a baseline action \(d_0:T\to T\cup\{\mathrm{vote}\}\), a target role \(r\in T\), and binary-rational numbers \(B,\theta\in[0,1]\). Every baseline delegation \(d_0(t)=s\) obeys \((t,s)\in A\), and the baseline delegation arcs are acyclic. Voting directly is always permitted.
Roles determine all delegation permissions and baseline actions: any subcohort of type \(t\) may delegate to any distinct subcohort of type \(s\) exactly when \((t,s)\in A\). The relation need not be reciprocal.
A solution is a finite directed forest with vertices \(v\) carrying labels \(\ell(v)\in T\) and positive real masses \(a_v\), satisfying \(\sum_{v:\ell(v)=t}a_v=\mu_t\). Each vertex either votes directly or has one outgoing arc to its parent; every delegation arc obeys \(A\), and every chain terminates at a voting root.
Define \(p(v)=\mathrm{vote}\) at a root and \(p(v)=\ell(u)\) when \(u\) is its parent. The change cost is \(C=\sum_v a_v\mathbf{1}[p(v)\ne d_0(\ell(v))]\); changing the recipient within the same role costs nothing. There are no recipient capacities.
Let \(\rho(v)\) be the root reached from \(v\), including \(v\) itself when it is a root. The target-rooted mass is \(M_r=\sum_{v:\ell(\rho(v))=r}a_v\), summed over all roots of role \(r\). The decision problem asks whether a solution satisfies \(C\le B\) and \(M_r\ge\theta\); the optimization version maximizes \(M_r\) subject to \(C\le B\).

Is this continuous decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite indivisible voters, maximizing one voter's accumulated weight by changing a budgeted number of delegation choices is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even with unit weights; its optimization version has no polynomial-time approximation ratio better than \(1-1/e\) unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). Result

The continuous decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The proof requires more than an ordinary linear relaxation: positive-mass recipients must actually exist and have paths to voting roots. These requirements can be enforced by a polynomial sequence of linear programs and graph reachability computations.

For optimization, the supremum is computable exactly in polynomial time, and its attainment is decidable in polynomial time. However, a maximizing forest need not exist, even under the stated baseline-acyclicity assumption.

1. Aggregate variables

Partition vertices into two classes according to their terminal roots:

  • Class \(1\): vertices whose terminal root has role \(r\).
  • Class \(0\): vertices whose terminal root has a role different from \(r\).

For every \((t,s)\in A\) and \(k\in\{0,1\}\), introduce a nonnegative variable \(x_{ts}^k\). It represents the own mass of class-\(k\) vertices of role \(t\) that delegate to role \(s\). It does not represent the accumulated mass carried by that delegation.

Also introduce nonnegative variables \(z_t^k\), representing the mass of class-\(k\) voting roots of role \(t\). Require

\[ z_t^1=0\quad(t\ne r),\qquad z_r^0=0. \]

For every \(t\in T\), mass conservation is

\[ \sum_{k\in\{0,1\}}\left(z_t^k+\sum_{s:(t,s)\in A}x_{ts}^k\right)=\mu_t. \]

Define the linear cost and target-mass functions by

\[ c(x,z)= \sum_{k\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{(t,s)\in A} \mathbf 1[s\ne d_0(t)]x_{ts}^k + \sum_{k\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{t\in T} \mathbf 1[\mathrm{vote}\ne d_0(t)]z_t^k \]

and

\[ f(x,z)=\sum_{t\in T}\left(z_t^1+\sum_{s:(t,s)\in A}x_{ts}^1\right). \]

Every forest induces these variables by summing vertex masses according to their class and action. Its cost and target-rooted mass are exactly \(c(x,z)\) and \(f(x,z)\).

Thus the decision constraints yield a rational polytope \(P\): nonnegativity, mass conservation, the root restrictions, and

\[ c(x,z)\le B,\qquad f(x,z)\ge\theta. \]

There are \(N=2|A|+2\tau\) variables. Every variable is at most \(1\), so \(P\) is bounded.

The missing requirement is whether an aggregate point can actually be realized by a finite forest.

2. Exact forest-realization lemma

For a nonnegative aggregate point satisfying mass conservation and the root restrictions, define, for each class \(k\),

\[ E_k=\{(t,s)\in A:x_{ts}^k>0\}, \qquad V_k=\{t\in T:z_t^k>0\}. \]

Let \(R_k\) be the roles from which some role in \(V_k\) is reachable in the directed graph \((T,E_k)\). A path of length zero is permitted, so \(V_k\subseteq R_k\).

Lemma. The aggregate point is realizable by a finite forest if and only if

\[ x_{ts}^k>0\ \Longrightarrow\ s\in R_k \]

for every delegation coordinate.

Necessity

Suppose \(x_{ts}^k>0\) in a forest. Some positive-mass class-\(k\) vertex of role \(t\) delegates to a vertex of role \(s\).

Follow that recipient’s chain to its voting root. Every delegation along this chain contributes positive own mass to the corresponding class-\(k\) coordinate, and the root contributes positive mass to the corresponding \(z^k\) coordinate. Consequently, this chain gives a directed walk from \(s\) to \(V_k\), using only arcs in \(E_k\).

Hence \(s\in R_k\).

Sufficiency

Assume the condition holds. We construct a forest realizing every coordinate exactly.

For each positive delegation coordinate \(x_{ts}^k\), choose a simple directed path from \(s\) to \(V_k\) in \((T,E_k)\). Create a fresh vertex path beginning with the delegation \(t\to s\), continuing along the chosen path, and ending at a voting root.

For each positive root coordinate \(z_t^k\), also create a fresh singleton voting root.

All these paths use distinct vertices. Their labels may repeat: even if the initial role \(t\) appears later on its path, the corresponding vertices are distinct subcohorts.

Let \(K\) be the total number of vertices created. Each positive coordinate contributes one path with at most \(\tau+1\) vertices, so

\[ K\le N(\tau+1). \]

Let \(h\) be the smallest positive aggregate coordinate, and assign every created vertex mass

\[ \delta=\frac{h}{2K}. \]

Each vertex consumes mass from its corresponding action coordinate. A coordinate occurs at most \(K\) times, so its total consumption is at most

\[ K\delta=\frac h2. \]

Therefore every positive coordinate still has a positive residual.

Allocate these residuals as follows:

  • For \(z_t^k\), create a voting root of role \(t\) carrying the residual.
  • For \(x_{ts}^k\), create a vertex of role \(t\) carrying the residual, and delegate it to the role-\(s\) vertex immediately following \(t\) on that coordinate’s previously created path.

Every added delegating vertex is a new leaf attached to an existing path. Thus no directed cycle is created, and every chain terminates at a voting root.

All vertices have positive mass, every delegation respects \(A\), and every coordinate is realized exactly. Consequently, role masses, cost, and target-rooted mass are preserved.

This construction uses at most \(N(\tau+1)+N\) vertices. It relies on the absence of recipient capacities.

3. Polynomial-time support pruning

We use the standard theorem that rational linear programming is solvable in time polynomial in its encoding length, with exact rational optimal solutions of polynomial bit length. See, for example, [Nemirovski’s linear-programming lecture notes](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~nemirovs/Lec_EMCO.pdf).

Finding all coordinates that can be positive

For any nonempty current polytope \(P\), maximize each coordinate separately over \(P\).

For every coordinate whose maximum is positive, choose a maximizing point. Average these chosen points, obtaining a point \(w\in P\).

Because all coordinates are nonnegative,

\[ w_i>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{some point of }P\text{ has coordinate }i\text{ positive}. \]

Thus \(w\) has maximal support: the support of every point in \(P\) is contained in the support of \(w\).

Computing \(w\) requires at most \(N\) linear-program optimizations.

The algorithm

Start with the decision polytope from Section 1. Repeat:

  • If the current polytope is empty, return NO.
  • Compute a maximal-support point \(w\).
  • For each class \(k\), construct its positive-action graph and compute \(R_k\).
  • If every positive delegation coordinate \(w_{ts}^k\) has \(s\in R_k\), return YES and apply the realization lemma.
  • Otherwise, for every positive coordinate with \(s\notin R_k\), add the equation \(x_{ts}^k=0\), and repeat.

Why pruning is sound

Maintain the invariant that every forest satisfying the original decision constraints induces a point in the current polytope.

Suppose a positive coordinate \(w_{ts}^k\) has \(s\notin R_k\). If a feasible forest had \(x_{ts}^k>0\), necessity in the realization lemma would give a path from \(s\) to a class-\(k\) voting root using positive coordinates of that forest.

But all those coordinates belong to the support of \(w\), by maximality of its support. The same path would therefore exist in the graph constructed from \(w\), contradicting \(s\notin R_k\).

Hence every feasible forest has \(x_{ts}^k=0\). Adding that equation preserves the invariant.

Termination and correctness

Every pruning round sets to zero at least one coordinate that was positive at the current point. Such a coordinate cannot already have been fixed to zero. There are only \(N\) coordinates, so there are at most \(N\) pruning rounds.

If the algorithm returns NO, the invariant shows that no feasible forest exists.

If it returns YES, the realization lemma produces a finite forest whose cost is at most \(B\) and whose target-rooted mass is at least \(\theta\).

Therefore the algorithm decides the problem exactly.

Running time and witness size

There are at most \(N+1\) rounds, each using at most \(N\) linear-program optimizations and two graph reachability computations. Pruning adds only coordinate-zero equations, so every linear program has encoding length polynomial in \(L\) and \(N\).

Since

\[ N=2|A|+2\tau=O(\tau^2), \]

the running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(m=\tau\).

The averaged LP solutions have polynomial rational bit length. The realization construction uses polynomially many vertices, and its masses are obtained through rational arithmetic of polynomial bit length. Thus every YES instance also has a polynomial-size rational forest witness.

4. Optimization: supremum and attainment

Run the same pruning algorithm with the budget constraint but without the target threshold. Let \(Q\) be the final polytope and \(w\) its maximal-support point.

The baseline forest is feasible with cost zero: use one vertex of mass \(\mu_t\) for each role and its baseline action. Baseline acyclicity ensures every chain terminates at a voting root. Therefore this run cannot end with an empty polytope.

Compute

\[ S=\max_{q\in Q}f(q) \]

by rational linear programming.

Every budget-feasible forest induces a point in \(Q\), by soundness of pruning. Hence its target-rooted mass is at most \(S\).

Conversely, let \(q^\star\) attain the displayed LP maximum. For any \(0<\lambda\le1\), define

\[ q_\lambda=(1-\lambda)q^\star+\lambda w. \]

This point belongs to \(Q\). Its support equals the support of \(w\), because all coordinates are nonnegative and every coordinate positive in \(q^\star\) is also positive in \(w\).

Therefore the realization lemma applies to \(q_\lambda\), and

\[ f(q_\lambda)=(1-\lambda)S+\lambda f(w)\longrightarrow S \]

as \(\lambda\) tends to zero.

Thus \(S\) is exactly the supremum over finite forests. It is rational and computable in polynomial time.

To decide whether the supremum is attained, run the decision algorithm with threshold \(\theta=S\). A YES answer produces a maximizing forest; a NO answer certifies nonattainment. Arbitrarily close rational forests can also be constructed using \(q_\lambda\).

5. A supremum that is not attained

Consider three roles \(T=\{r,s,t\}\), with

\[ \mu_r=\frac14,\qquad \mu_s=\frac14,\qquad \mu_t=\frac12. \]

Let

\[ A=\{(t,s),(s,t),(t,r)\}, \]

and let the baseline actions be

\[ d_0(r)=\mathrm{vote},\qquad d_0(s)=\mathrm{vote},\qquad d_0(t)=s. \]

The baseline delegation graph is acyclic. Set \(B=\frac18\).

For any forest, let \(x\) be its target-rooted mass of role \(s\), and let \(y\) be the mass of role-\(t\) vertices delegating directly to role \(r\).

Every target-rooted role-\(s\) vertex must delegate, changing its baseline action. Every role-\(t\) vertex delegating to \(r\) also changes its baseline action. These are disjoint sets of vertices, so

\[ C\ge x+y. \]

If any role-\(s\) or role-\(t\) mass reaches \(r\), its chain must contain a \(t\to r\) arc. Its source vertex has positive mass, so \(y>0\). In that case,

\[ M_r\le\mu_r+\mu_t+x \le\frac34+B-y <\frac78. \]

If no role-\(s\) or role-\(t\) mass reaches \(r\), then \(M_r\le\frac14\). Thus no budget-feasible forest attains \(\frac78\).

Nevertheless, for every \(0<\varepsilon<\frac18\), construct the chain

\[ t_{\mathrm{bulk}}\to s_{\mathrm{target}}\to t_{\mathrm{connector}}\to r \]

with respective masses

\[ \frac12-\varepsilon,\qquad \frac18-\varepsilon,\qquad \varepsilon,\qquad \frac14. \]

The remaining role-\(s\) mass, \(\frac18+\varepsilon\), votes directly.

The only changed actions are those of \(s_{\mathrm{target}}\) and \(t_{\mathrm{connector}}\). Hence

\[ C=\left(\frac18-\varepsilon\right)+\varepsilon=\frac18, \]

while

\[ M_r=\frac78-\varepsilon. \]

Therefore the supremum is \(\frac78\), but no maximizing forest exists.

This nonattainment does not affect the decision classification: exact feasibility, including equality at a proposed threshold, is decided by the support-pruning algorithm.
