The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) good categories, \(\tau\) buyer types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, rational per-unit values, and rational category supplies \(q_s\) summing to two, each buyer wants two distinct units and every welfare-maximizing allocation gives almost every buyer two units. The welfare benchmark is the maximum of the bundle-allocation LP over type masses and category capacities. Is there an algorithm polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the binary input length that outputs a compact state-feedback pricing rule, evaluable in polynomial time, which posts category prices using only the remaining type masses and supplies and achieves the benchmark for every measurable arrival path with type distribution \(\mu\) and every measurable choice among utility-maximizing bundles?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) buyer types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq1\) categories with positive rational supplies \(q_s\) summing to \(2\), and nonnegative rational per-unit values \(v_{ts}\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
A bundle is \(k\in K:=\{k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^m:\sum_s k_s\leq2\}\), with value \(\sum_s v_{ts}k_s\). Two distinct units may come from the same category; empty and singleton bundles are allowed.
The benchmark \(W^*\) is the maximum of \(\sum_{t,k}x_{tk}\sum_s v_{ts}k_s\), subject to \(x_{tk}\geq0\), \(\sum_kx_{tk}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,k}k_sx_{tk}\leq q_s\). Assume every maximizing allocation assigns two units to almost every buyer, equivalently \(x_{tk}=0\) whenever \(\sum_s k_s<2\).
An arrival path is a measurable map \(a:[0,1]\to\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) with \(\lambda(a^{-1}(t))=\mu_t\), where \(\lambda\) is Lebesgue measure. Remaining masses and supplies obey \(r_t(u)=\mu_t-\int_0^u\mathbf1_{a(z)=t}\,dz\) and \(h_s(u)=q_s-\int_0^u k_s(z)\,dz\).
A state-feedback rule posts finite nonnegative prices \(p=P(r,h)\), using the fixed input and only the current remaining masses and supplies, without observing the next type. At time \(u\), a buyer chooses any bundle maximizing \(\sum_s(v_{a(u)s}-p_s)k_s\) among \(k\in K\) with \(k_s=0\) if \(h_s(u)=0\); ties, including zero-utility purchases, are unrestricted. Supplies must remain nonnegative.
The rule must permit continuation through \(u=1\) under every arrival path and every measurable maximizing choice, and every resulting trajectory must satisfy \(\int_0^1\sum_s v_{a(u)s}k_s(u)\,du=W^*\). A compact rule is a finite program with rational constants, arithmetic and comparisons, interpreted exactly on real states; its description and operation count are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and rational-state evaluation has bit complexity polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,B\), where \(B\) is the state's encoding length.
Is synthesizing such a compact state-feedback pricing rule for every promised input solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this synthesis problem NP-hard?
For finite bi-demand markets where every welfare-maximizing allocation fills every buyer's demand, optimal dynamic prices exist and can be computed in polynomial time, regardless of arrival order and utility ties ([verified discrete result](https://real.mtak.hu/174639/1/Dual_approach_to_dynamic_pricing.pdf)). No result settling exactly the continuous synthesis problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Values and posted prices were unsigned in the graded question; both are nonnegative, as in the discrete market definition.
  • Wanting two units is an upper bound on purchases: empty and singleton bundles remain available, including under utility ties.
  • Distinct units need not belong to distinct categories; a bundle may contain two units from one category.
  • Categories with zero supply are deleted, leaving positive supplies and allowing one category.
  • The bundle-allocation benchmark explicitly includes bundles of every size up to two; the full-demand condition is a promise about every maximizer of this LP.
  • Measurable arrivals are parameterized by Lebesgue buyer mass on the unit interval, and inventory consumption is defined by integration.
  • A category is available to an infinitesimal buyer whenever its remaining supply is positive; inventory feasibility is imposed on the resulting trajectory.
  • Continuous feedback must allow trajectories to continue through the entire arrival interval under arbitrary measurable maximizing choices, preventing vacuous guarantees caused by stalled dynamics.
  • Compactness and evaluation are defined using finite arithmetic-and-comparison programs, with exact real-state semantics and polynomial bit complexity on rational states.

A polynomial-time construction

The synthesis problem is polynomial-time solvable under the stated promise.

The permission to buy two units from the same category is essential to the argument. It allows the welfare problem to be represented by a transportation linear program, and allows a small price perturbation to force a unique bundle of the form \(2e_s\).

I use the final formatting instruction and write mathematics with \( \) delimiters.

1. The residual welfare problem

For nonnegative remaining masses \(r\) and supplies \(h\), define

\(F(r,h):=\max\left\{\sum_{t,s}v_{ts}y_{ts}:y_{ts}\geq0,\ \sum_s y_{ts}\leq2r_t,\ \sum_t y_{ts}\leq h_s\right\}.\)

Here \(y_{ts}\) is the quantity of category \(s\) allocated to type \(t\).

This linear program has exactly the same optimal value as the residual bundle-allocation problem. Indeed, a bundle allocation induces \(y_{ts}=\sum_k k_sx_{tk}\). Conversely, any feasible \(y\) can be implemented by

\(x_{t,2e_s}=y_{ts}/2,\qquad x_{t,0}=r_t-\frac12\sum_s y_{ts},\)

with every other bundle receiving zero mass. These quantities are nonnegative, have total mass \(r_t\), and preserve category consumption and welfare. In particular, \(F(\mu,q)=W^*\).

The dual is

\(F(r,h)=\min\left\{2\sum_t r_t\alpha_t+\sum_s h_s\pi_s:\alpha_t,\pi_s\geq0,\ \alpha_t+\pi_s\geq v_{ts}\right\}.\)

The primal is nonempty and compact. The dual is feasible, for example with \(\alpha_t=V\), \(\pi_s=0\), where \(V:=\max_{t,s}v_{ts}\). Thus finite-dimensional linear-programming duality applies.

Call an edge \((t,s)\) legal at \((r,h)\) if some optimal residual solution has \(y_{ts}>0\).

We first give an exact polynomial-operation procedure that produces an optimal dual satisfying:

  • \(\alpha_t+\pi_s=v_{ts}\) precisely on legal edges;
  • \(\alpha_t=0\) precisely when some optimal solution leaves type \(t\)'s quantity constraint slack;
  • \(\pi_s=0\) precisely when some optimal solution leaves category \(s\)'s supply constraint slack.

The procedure must work with real capacities, because the feedback is evaluated at real states.

2. Computing the required dual

2.1. Compute an optimal residual flow

Put \(d_t:=2r_t\) and \(U:=1+\sum_t d_t+\sum_s h_s\). Construct a circulation network with vertices \(o\), the types, and the categories, and arcs:

| Arc | Capacity | Cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(o\to t\) | \(d_t\) | \(0\) |
| \(t\to s\) | \(U\) | \(-v_{ts}\) |
| \(s\to o\) | \(h_s\) | \(0\) |

Conservation forces the flow on \(o\to t\) to equal \(\sum_s y_{ts}\), and the flow on \(s\to o\) to equal \(\sum_t y_{ts}\). Consequently, feasible circulations correspond exactly to feasible residual allocations, with circulation cost equal to minus welfare. The capacities \(U\) never bind.

Use minimum-mean cycle cancellation to compute an optimal circulation. The Goldberg–Tarjan theorem bounds the number of cancellations by \(O(NM^2\log N)\), where \(N\) and \(M\) are the network's numbers of vertices and arcs. Each minimum mean cycle is computable with polynomially many arithmetic operations and comparisons. The network model permits real capacities, and the bound does not depend on their denominators. Its hypotheses hold here: capacities are finite and nonnegative, and the zero circulation is feasible. See [Network Flow Algorithms, Sections 1.1 and 4.5](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~eva/Network.Flow.Algorithms.pdf).

Fix the resulting optimal allocation \(y\).

2.2. Describe its optimal dual face by difference constraints

Construct a directed graph on the same vertices, with the following arcs and costs:

  • \(t\to s\), of cost \(-v_{ts}\), for every pair;
  • \(s\to t\), of cost \(v_{ts}\), whenever \(y_{ts}>0\);
  • \(t\to o\), of cost \(0\), for every type;
  • \(o\to t\), of cost \(0\), whenever \(\sum_s y_{ts}<d_t\);
  • \(o\to s\), of cost \(0\), for every category;
  • \(s\to o\), of cost \(0\), whenever \(\sum_t y_{ts}<h_s\).

For node potentials \(z\), impose

\(c_{ij}+z_i-z_j\geq0\)

on every graph arc. Set

\(\alpha_t=z_t-z_o,\qquad \pi_s=z_o-z_s.\)

These inequalities say exactly that the dual is feasible and complementary to \(y\). This follows from the duality-gap identity

\[ \text{not used} \]

More explicitly, the identity is

\(2\sum_t r_t\alpha_t+\sum_s h_s\pi_s-\sum_{t,s}v_{ts}y_{ts} =\sum_t\alpha_t\left(d_t-\sum_s y_{ts}\right) +\sum_s\pi_s\left(h_s-\sum_t y_{ts}\right) +\sum_{t,s}(\alpha_t+\pi_s-v_{ts})y_{ts}.\)

Every term on the right is nonnegative. The reverse arcs and slack arcs impose exactly the equalities needed to make every term zero. Therefore the potential system describes the entire optimal dual face, modulo adding a common constant to all potentials.

An optimal dual exists, so this graph has no negative-cost cycle. Adding a temporary source with zero-cost arcs to every vertex, shortest-path distances give feasible potentials \(z\).

2.3. Make every non-forced inequality strict

Let \(\bar c_{ij}:=c_{ij}+z_i-z_j\), and consider the graph of arcs with \(\bar c_{ij}=0\). Compute its strongly connected components.

Assign each component an integer rank in \(\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\), decreasing strictly along every arc of its condensation graph. Let \(a_i\) denote the rank of the component containing \(i\).

Let \(\rho\) be the smallest positive reduced cost, using \(\rho=1\) if there is none, and put

\(\delta:=\frac{\min\{1,\rho\}}{2N},\qquad z'_i:=z_i+\delta a_i.\)

For an originally positive reduced-cost arc,

\(c_{ij}+z'_i-z'_j\geq\bar c_{ij}-\delta(N-1)>0.\)

An originally zero arc between different components becomes strictly positive. An originally zero arc within a component remains zero.

Thus the new potentials remain feasible, and an arc has zero reduced cost precisely when it belongs to a zero-cost directed cycle. Define \(\alpha,\pi\) from \(z'\).

2.4. Verify the three required properties

First, if some optimal allocation has \(y_{ts}>0\), complementary slackness with the computed dual gives \(\alpha_t+\pi_s=v_{ts}\).

Conversely, suppose \(\alpha_t+\pi_s=v_{ts}\). If the current \(y_{ts}>0\), the edge is already legal. Otherwise, \(t\to s\) belongs to a zero-cost simple directed cycle in the difference-constraint graph.

That cycle consists of genuine residual arcs. The only arcs added beyond the circulation's residual graph are:

  • \(t\to o\) when the allocated quantity of type \(t\) is zero;
  • \(o\to s\) when the allocated quantity of category \(s\) is zero.

An added arc of the first kind can occur in a simple cycle only in the two-arc cycle \(o\to t\to o\): no category has a reverse-flow arc into a type with zero allocated quantity. Similarly, an added arc of the second kind can occur in a simple cycle only in \(o\to s\to o\). Neither two-arc cycle contains \(t\to s\).

Every arc of the chosen cycle therefore has positive residual capacity. Sending a sufficiently small positive amount around it preserves feasibility and cost, and makes \(y_{ts}>0\). Hence the edge is legal.

The same argument verifies the slack properties. If a row is already slack, complementary slackness gives \(\alpha_t=0\). If it is full and \(\alpha_t=0\), the arc \(t\to o\) belongs to a zero-cost cycle. For a positive full row, that cycle is a genuine residual cycle whose augmentation decreases the allocated quantity of type \(t\), producing an optimal solution with row slack. A zero-capacity row has no incoming graph arcs, so \(\alpha_t=0\) cannot occur there. Conversely, slack in any optimal solution forces \(\alpha_t=0\) by complementary slackness.

Applying the corresponding argument to \(o\to s\) proves the category-supply statement.

This establishes all three properties without assuming a rational state.

3. The pricing rule

At a nonnegative state, compute the dual above and put

\(\gamma_{ts}:=\alpha_t+\pi_s-v_{ts}.\)

Define

\(\eta:=\min\left(\{1\}\cup\{\alpha_t:\alpha_t>0\}\cup\{\gamma_{ts}:\gamma_{ts}>0\}\right),\qquad \varepsilon:=\frac{\eta}{4(m+1)}.\)

The union contains \(1\), so \(\eta>0\). For each category with \(h_s>0\), post

\(p_s:=\pi_s+\varepsilon s.\)

For a category with \(h_s\leq0\), post \(p_s:=V+1\). For definiteness, on states with negative coordinates, perform the internal computation after replacing those coordinates by zero.

All prices are finite and nonnegative. The computation uses only the fixed input and \((r,h)\).

Consider an active type \(t\), meaning \(r_t>0\).

If \(\alpha_t=0\), every available category has strictly negative net value:

\(v_{ts}-p_s\leq-\varepsilon s<0.\)

The unique maximizing bundle is empty.

If \(\alpha_t>0\), complementary slackness implies that the row of \(t\) is full in an optimal allocation. Thus there is an available legal category. Its net value is \(\alpha_t-\varepsilon s>0\).

Every illegal category is strictly inferior to every legal category: if \(j\) is legal and \(s\) is illegal, their net-value difference is

\((v_{tj}-p_j)-(v_{ts}-p_s) =\gamma_{ts}+\varepsilon(s-j) \geq\gamma_{ts}-\varepsilon(m-1)>0.\)

Among legal categories, the smallest index has uniquely highest net value. Since its net value is strictly positive and repeated categories are allowed, the unique maximizing bundle is

\(2e_j,\qquad j=\min\{s:(t,s)\text{ is legal at }(r,h)\}.\)

Consequently, every active type has a unique maximizing bundle. It is either empty or consists of two units of a legal category.

4. A welfare invariant for every existing trajectory

The extension at nonpositive supplies prevents supplies from becoming negative. Indeed, \(p_s=V+1\) makes category \(s\) strictly undesirable whenever \(h_s\leq0\), so \(h'_s=0\) there. An absolutely continuous supply function starting nonnegative cannot enter a negative interval: on such an interval its derivative would vanish almost everywhere, contradicting continuity at the interval's left endpoint.

Remaining masses are nonnegative by the definition of an arrival path. Moreover, \(r_{a(u)}(u)>0\) almost everywhere: after a type's remaining mass reaches zero, its future arrival set has measure zero.

Define earned welfare up to time \(u\) by

\(G(u):=\int_0^u\sum_s v_{a(z)s}k_s(z)\,dz.\)

We prove the invariant

\(G(u)+F(r(u),h(u))=W^*.\)

First, \(F\) is Lipschitz on the nonnegative domain. An optimal dual can always be chosen with all coordinates in \([0,V]\): clipping a larger coordinate to \(V\) preserves dual feasibility and cannot increase its objective. The dual representation therefore gives

\(|F(r,h)-F(\tilde r,\tilde h)| \leq V\left(2\|r-\tilde r\|_1+\|h-\tilde h\|_1\right).\)

Thus \(F(r(u),h(u))\) is absolutely continuous.

At a time where both the state and this composed value function are differentiable, fix the computed optimal dual \((\alpha,\pi)\). Dual feasibility gives the supporting inequality

\(F(\tilde r,\tilde h)-F(r,h) \leq2\sum_t\alpha_t(\tilde r_t-r_t)+\sum_s\pi_s(\tilde h_s-h_s).\)

Applying it along the trajectory, with time increments of both signs, yields

\(\frac{d}{du}F(r(u),h(u)) =-2\alpha_{a(u)}-\sum_s\pi_s k_s(u).\)

If the buyer purchases nothing, \(\alpha_{a(u)}=0\), and the right side is zero. If the buyer purchases \(2e_s\), legality gives \(v_{a(u)s}=\alpha_{a(u)}+\pi_s\), so the right side is \(-2v_{a(u)s}\). Therefore

\(\frac{d}{du}\bigl(G(u)+F(r(u),h(u))\bigr)=0\)

almost everywhere. Absolute continuity and \(F(\mu,q)=W^*\) prove the invariant.

In particular, every trajectory that reaches time \(1\) obtains welfare \(W^*\), because \(r(1)=0\) and \(F(0,h(1))=0\).

It remains to prove that continuation always exists.

5. Geometry of the promised optimal allocations

Compute a dual \((\alpha^0,\pi^0)\) at the initial state using Section 2, and let

\(E:=\{(t,s):\alpha^0_t+\pi^0_s=v_{ts}\}.\)

The promise implies that every initial optimal transportation allocation fills every row. Since total demand and total supply both equal \(2\), every column is also full. The strict properties established above therefore imply

\(\alpha^0_t>0\quad\text{and}\quad\pi^0_s>0\)

for every type and category.

Complementary slackness now characterizes initial optimal allocations: they fill all rows and columns and use only edges in \(E\).

For each \((t,s)\in E\), define the vector \(g_{ts}:=(e_t,e_s)\) in \(\mathbb R^{\tau+m}\), and the finitely generated cone

\(C_E:=\left\{\sum_{(t,s)\in E}y_{ts}g_{ts}:y_{ts}\geq0\right\}.\)

A residual state has \(b:=(2r,h)\in C_E\) precisely when its rows and columns can be filled using \(E\).

At any such state, an \(E\)-supported filling attains the objective of the fixed initial dual:

\(F(r,h)=2\sum_t r_t\alpha^0_t+\sum_s h_s\pi^0_s.\)

Because every coordinate of \(\alpha^0,\pi^0\) is positive, all residual optimal allocations fill their rows and columns; because inequalities outside \(E\) are strict, they use only \(E\). Thus residual optimal allocations are exactly the representations of \(b\) in \(C_E\).

Let \(\mathcal F(b)\) be the minimal face of \(C_E\) containing \(b\). A standard fact about finitely generated cones gives

\((t,s)\text{ is legal at }(r,h) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g_{ts}\in\mathcal F(b).\)

Here is the verification. If a representation of \(b\) uses \(g_{ts}\) with positive coefficient, the defining property of a face forces \(g_{ts}\) into every face containing \(b\). Conversely, \(b\) lies in the relative interior of its minimal face. For \(g_{ts}\in\mathcal F(b)\), a sufficiently small \(\theta>0\) therefore satisfies \(b-\theta g_{ts}\in\mathcal F(b)\). Representing the latter point proves that \(b\) has a representation with a positive coefficient on \(g_{ts}\).

6. Continuation for every measurable arrival path

Fix any permitted arrival path.

Suppose the current time is \(u_0<1\), and \(b(u_0)\) lies in the relative interior of a face \(\mathcal F\) of \(C_E\). For every type represented by some generator of \(\mathcal F\), define

\(\sigma_{\mathcal F}(t):=\min\{s:g_{ts}\in\mathcal F\}.\)

Every active type has such a generator. A type without one has zero remaining mass and consequently does not arrive again except on a null set.

While the state remains in the relative interior of \(\mathcal F\), its legal edges are constant. Section 3 consequently forces the purchase \(2e_{\sigma_{\mathcal F}(a(u))}\) almost everywhere. The candidate state is therefore

\(b(u)=b(u_0)-2\int_{u_0}^u g_{a(z),\sigma_{\mathcal F}(a(z))}\,dz,\)

with the integrand set to zero on the irrelevant null set of arrivals from exhausted types.

This candidate is continuous, lies in the linear span of \(\mathcal F\), and has bounded derivative. Because its starting point lies in the relative interior, it remains there for a positive time. Continue until time \(1\), or until its first encounter with the relative boundary of \(\mathcal F\).

At a boundary encounter, the limiting point is still in the closed face \(\mathcal F\), so supplies remain nonnegative. Its minimal face is a proper face of \(\mathcal F\). Restart the same construction there.

Every boundary encounter before time \(1\) strictly decreases face dimension. Since the cone has dimension at most \(\tau+m\), there are only finitely many encounters. The construction therefore reaches time \(1\). Purchases are measurable because each phase uses a fixed map from types to categories composed with the measurable arrival path. Choices at the finitely many phase endpoints can be assigned arbitrarily among maximizers without affecting any integral.

This constructs a complete trajectory for every arrival path.

It also proves continuation from every admissible trajectory prefix. To see this, combine a prefix ending at \(u\) with an optimal residual allocation. The invariant says that their combined welfare is \(W^*\). Their combined bundle allocation is feasible for the original instance, so the promise makes it full. Complementary slackness with \((\alpha^0,\pi^0)\) makes its consumption entirely \(E\)-supported and exhausts every original supply. Hence the residual state satisfies \((2r(u),h(u))\in C_E\), and the construction above applies from that state.

Finally, at every reachable state, every active row is full in all optimal residual allocations. Section 3 therefore gives a unique maximizing two-unit bundle almost everywhere. Unrestricted utility ties cannot obstruct continuation or change welfare.

7. Compactness and complexity

The feedback program consists of the circulation algorithm, shortest paths, strongly connected components, topological ranking, and the explicit price formulas.

Its circulation network has \(\tau+m+1\) vertices and \(\tau m+\tau+m\) arcs. All graph routines use polynomially many arithmetic operations and comparisons. Minimum-mean cycle cancellation supplies a polynomial operation bound even at irrational real states.

For rational-state evaluation, bit complexity is also polynomial. Take a common denominator \(Q\) for the state capacities; its binary length is polynomial in the state's encoding length \(B\). Cycle cancellation uses additions, subtractions, and minima of capacities. Every resulting flow is a multiple of \(1/Q\) and is bounded by \(U\), so flow and residual-capacity encodings remain polynomial in \(B,m,\tau\). The costs are fixed input rationals; minimum-mean-cycle computations, shortest-path potentials, and the two perturbations have encoding lengths polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Consequently, exact evaluation has bit complexity polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,B\).

Synthesis simply instantiates this fixed program with the input dimensions and rational values. Its description length and synthesis time are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). All computations use one vertex per support type.

Thus the program is a compact state-feedback pricing rule with the required continuation and welfare guarantees.
