The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational mass distributions \(\mu\) over item types and \(\nu\) over worker types, with fixed supports, item-type qualities \(q_j\), and worker-type rating laws with means \(a_{rj}\), suppose at least an \(\alpha\) fraction of worker mass is reliable, its ratings have independent errors, and its means satisfy the paper’s \( (L,\epsilon_0) \)-monotonicity condition relative to \(q\). With access to the paper’s sparse worker-item rating and curator-judgment process at the type level, can a randomized algorithm select masses \(x_j\in[0,\mu_j]\), \(\sum_j x_j=\beta\), such that \(\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_jx_jq_j\ge Q_\beta^* - ((L+1)\epsilon+\epsilon_0)\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\), using query bounds and computation polynomial in the support sizes and input encoding length, independent of the replication scale?

Each society is specified by its positive-mass support, with masses encoded as binary rationals; support size counts listed types, not possible types. Write \(m\) for the item support size, \(\tau\) for the worker support size, and \(L\) for the input bit length.
The known distributions satisfy \(\mu_j,\nu_r>0\) and \(\sum_j\mu_j=\sum_r\nu_r=1\). Known rational parameters satisfy \(0<\alpha,\beta\le1\), \(0<\epsilon,\delta<1\), \(\lambda>0\), and \(\epsilon_0\ge0\).
Item qualities \(q_j\in[0,1]\), worker rating laws, and the reliable set \(C\) are unknown. Reliability is constant within each worker type, and \(\sum_{r\in C}\nu_r\ge\alpha\).
A reliable worker query on types \((r,j)\) returns a fresh independent rating in \([0,1]\), with mean \(a_{rj}\), satisfying \(q_j-q_{j'}\le\lambda(a_{rj}-a_{rj'})+\epsilon_0\) whenever \(q_j\ge q_{j'}\). Unreliable replies may depend on previous replies and collude. Curator queries return independent ratings in \([0,1]\) with mean \(q_j\); replies are binary rationals of at most \(L\) bits.
The sparse access process samples \(s\) worker occurrences independently from \(\nu\) and \(t\ge s\) item occurrences independently from \(\mu\), retaining their type labels. For integers \(k,k_0\le t\), assign each worker–item occurrence pair independently with probability \(k/t\), then delete assignments until every occurrence has degree at most \(2k\), before obtaining worker replies. Afterwards, independently select each item occurrence with probability \(k_0/t\) in each of two curator batches; repeated type queries produce fresh replies.
Define \(Q_\beta^*=\beta^{-1}\max\{\sum_j y_jq_j:0\le y_j\le\mu_j,\ \sum_jy_j=\beta\}\); tied qualities permit arbitrary splitting of mass.

Does a randomized algorithm output rational masses \(0\le x_j\le\mu_j\), \(\sum_jx_j=\beta\), with \(\beta^{-1}\sum_jx_jq_j\ge Q_\beta^*-((\lambda+1)\epsilon+\epsilon_0)\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\), using sample sizes, query bounds, and computation polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\lambda,\alpha^{-1},\beta^{-1},\epsilon^{-1},\log(1/\delta)\), independently of any replication scale; or is achieving this approximation NP-hard?

Result

The stated mass-and-oracle problem admits a randomized polynomial-time algorithm. In fact, the algorithm achieves additive error \(\epsilon\), which is stronger than the requested error \((\lambda+1)\epsilon+\epsilon_0\).

The permitted polynomial dependence on the listed support size \(m\) is essential to this construction. The algorithm uses curator ratings and ignores worker ratings. It nevertheless follows the specified access process, with curator selection probability tending to zero.

The nontrivial issue is that some listed types can have exponentially small masses. Sampling enough occurrences of every type could therefore take exponential time. We avoid this by estimating sufficiently massive types and bounding the total contribution of the remaining types.

1. Parameters and algorithm

Let

\( h=\left\lceil\log_2(4m/\delta)\right\rceil,\qquad \eta=\epsilon/4,\qquad \theta=\frac{\beta\epsilon}{2m}, \)

and define integers

\( n=\left\lceil\frac{8h}{\epsilon^2}\right\rceil,\qquad B=\left\lceil\frac{2n+8h}{\theta}\right\rceil. \)

Use the access-process parameters

\( s=1,\qquad t=B^2,\qquad k=1,\qquad k_0=B. \)

These satisfy \(t\ge s\) and \(k,k_0\le t\). Worker–item assignments have probability \(1/t\), and each curator batch selects each item occurrence with probability \(1/B\).

Run the worker assignment, deletion, and reply steps as prescribed. Ignore their replies. After deletion there are at most two worker queries.

Run both prescribed curator batches independently. Use only the first batch for estimation. For each listed item type \(j\), let \(N_j\) be its number of selected occurrences in that batch. Set \(\widehat q_j\) to the average of its curator replies when \(N_j>0\), and set \(\widehat q_j=0\) otherwise. Thus always \(0\le\widehat q_j\le1\).

Sort the listed types by nonincreasing \(\widehat q_j\), breaking ties by their input order. Starting with remaining mass \(\beta\), assign each successive type as much mass as possible, up to its capacity \(\mu_j\), until the remaining mass is zero. Output the resulting vector \(x\).

Because \(\sum_j\mu_j=1\ge\beta\), this procedure always produces rational masses satisfying

\( 0\le x_j\le\mu_j,\qquad \sum_jx_j=\beta. \)

2. Accurate estimates for sufficiently massive types

Define

\( H=\{j:\mu_j\ge\theta\}. \)

This set is used for analysis; the algorithm does not need to identify it.

For a fixed type \(j\), each of the \(t\) independent item occurrences has type \(j\) and is selected in the first curator batch with probability \(\mu_j/B\). Therefore

\( N_j\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(t,\mu_j/B),\qquad v_j:=\mathbb E[N_j]=B\mu_j. \)

For \(j\in H\), our choice of \(B\) gives

\( v_j\ge B\theta\ge2n+8h. \)

The binomial lower-tail Chernoff inequality states that a binomial variable \(N\) of mean \(v\) satisfies \(\Pr(N<v/2)\le\exp(-v/8)\). Since \(n\le v_j/2\), it follows that

\( \Pr(N_j<n) \le\exp(-v_j/8) \le\exp(-h). \)

A union bound over \(H\) yields

\( \Pr(\text{some }j\in H\text{ has }N_j<n) \le m\exp(-h). \)

Next, condition on the sampled item labels and curator selection decisions. For any type with \(N_j\ge n\), its selected curator replies are independent variables in \([0,1]\), each with mean \(q_j\). Hoeffding’s inequality consequently gives

\( \Pr\bigl(|\widehat q_j-q_j|>\eta \mid\text{labels and selections}\bigr) \le2\exp(-2N_j\eta^2) \le2\exp(-n\epsilon^2/8) \le2\exp(-h). \)

Applying another union bound, the event

\( E=\{|\widehat q_j-q_j|\le\eta\text{ for every }j\in H\} \)

has probability at least \(1-3m\exp(-h)\).

Finally, \(h\ge\log_2(4m/\delta)\) implies

\( \exp(-h)\le2^{-h}\le\frac{\delta}{4m}. \)

Hence

\( \Pr(E)\ge1-\frac{3\delta}{4}. \)

This argument uses no lower bound on the smallest positive input mass.

3. Optimization and approximation guarantee

Let the feasible set be

\( \mathcal F=\{y:0\le y_j\le\mu_j,\ \sum_jy_j=\beta\}. \)

It is nonempty, since \(y_j=\beta\mu_j\) is feasible. It is also compact. Therefore there exists \(x^*\in\mathcal F\) maximizing \(\sum_jy_jq_j\), and

\( \sum_jx_j^*q_j=\beta Q_\beta^*. \)

The greedy construction of \(x\) maximizes the estimated objective \(\sum_jy_j\widehat q_j\) over \(\mathcal F\). Here is an explicit verification. Let \(c\) be the estimated quality of the last type receiving positive mass. All types with estimated quality greater than \(c\) are filled to capacity, and all types with estimated quality less than \(c\) receive zero mass. For any \(y\in\mathcal F\),

\( \sum_j(y_j-x_j)\widehat q_j =\sum_j(y_j-x_j)(\widehat q_j-c) \le0. \)

The equality uses \(\sum_j(y_j-x_j)=0\). Each summand is nonpositive: above the threshold \(y_j-x_j\le0\); below it \(y_j-x_j\ge0\); at it the second factor is zero. In particular,

\( \sum_j(x_j^*-x_j)\widehat q_j\le0. \)

Suppose now that \(E\) holds. Put \(d_j=x_j^*-x_j\). Feasibility implies

\( \sum_j|d_j|\le\sum_j(x_j^*+x_j)=2\beta, \qquad |d_j|\le\mu_j. \)

Using the estimated-objective inequality,

\( \sum_jd_jq_j =\sum_jd_j\widehat q_j+\sum_jd_j(q_j-\widehat q_j) \le\sum_j|d_j|\,|q_j-\widehat q_j|. \)

On \(H\), the estimation error is at most \(\eta\). Outside \(H\), it is at most one because both qualities and estimates belong to \([0,1]\). Consequently,

\( \sum_jd_jq_j \le \eta\sum_{j\in H}|d_j| +\sum_{j\notin H}|d_j| \le2\beta\eta+\sum_{j\notin H}\mu_j. \)

Every type outside \(H\) has mass less than \(\theta\), so

\( \sum_{j\notin H}\mu_j\le m\theta=\frac{\beta\epsilon}{2}. \)

Since \(\eta=\epsilon/4\), we obtain

\( \sum_j(x_j^*-x_j)q_j\le\beta\epsilon. \)

Dividing by \(\beta>0\) proves

\( \frac1\beta\sum_jx_jq_j\ge Q_\beta^*-\epsilon. \)

Because \(\lambda>0\) and \(\epsilon_0\ge0\), this implies the requested inequality

\( \frac1\beta\sum_jx_jq_j \ge Q_\beta^*-\bigl((\lambda+1)\epsilon+\epsilon_0\bigr). \)

4. Polynomial resources and bit complexity

The parameter bounds are

\( h=O\bigl(1+\log m+\log(1/\delta)\bigr), \)

\( n=O(h\epsilon^{-2}),\qquad B=O\left(\frac{mh}{\beta\epsilon^3}\right),\qquad t=O\left(\frac{m^2h^2}{\beta^2\epsilon^6}\right). \)

Thus all sample sizes are polynomial in the permitted parameters. The construction uses one worker occurrence, at most two worker replies, and at most \(2t\) curator replies. The expected total number of curator replies is \(2B\). Both deterministic and expected query bounds are polynomial.

The curator selection probability is \(k_0/t=1/B\). Thus the proof does not require selecting every item occurrence.

Each curator reply has at most \(L\) bits. Exact sums and averages of at most \(t\) such rationals have polynomial bit length: a product of their denominators has at most \(tL\) bits. Rational comparison, sorting, and the greedy mass allocation therefore require polynomial computation. The output masses also have polynomial bit length.

For completeness, exact rational sampling can be implemented with a bounded running time and a small additional failure probability:

  • A rational categorical distribution can be represented using integer weights with a common denominator whose bit length is polynomial in \(L\).
  • To sample a uniform integer below a positive integer \(D\), draw \(\lceil\log_2D\rceil\) independent bits and reject values at least \(D\). Each attempt succeeds with probability at least \(1/2\). The case \(D=1\) is deterministic.
  • The prescribed process needs at most \(A=4t+1\) elementary rational draws: \(t+1\) type draws, \(t\) assignment coins, and \(2t\) curator-selection coins. Limit each rejection sampler to

\( R=\left\lceil\log_2(4A/\delta)\right\rceil \)
attempts.

  • If any sampler exhausts its attempts, terminate and output the feasible fallback \(x_j=\beta\mu_j\).

Coupling this implementation to unlimited rejection sampling, the probability of any exhaustion is at most

\( A2^{-R}\le\delta/4. \)

Outside that event, all generated samples and coins agree exactly with the prescribed process. Combining this sampling failure probability with the statistical failure probability from Section 2 gives total failure probability at most \(\delta\). The capped implementation has polynomial worst-case computation.

No bound depends on a replication scale or on an inverse minimum mass.

5. Scope of the conclusion

The [original finite-population paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.05374v1) and its [later full version](https://cs.stanford.edu/~jsteinhardt/publications/crowdsourcing/paper.pdf) seek curator budgets independent of the number of individual items. The present question permits polynomial dependence on the listed support size \(m\), which allows the curator-only construction above.

The proof settles the specified problem under that resource allowance. It does not establish the smaller curator budgets of those finite-population results.
