The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite item kinds \(E\), valuation types \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), rational per-person supplies \(q_e\ge 0\), and normalized monotone submodular type valuations on integral bundles, define \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)=\sup_{\sigma}\min_{x:\sigma_x>0}v_t(x)\), where \(\sigma\) is a finite-support distribution on integral bundles with mean bundle \(q\). With comparison-oracle access to the valuations, can a randomized algorithm polynomial in the binary input length, \(|E|\), and \(|T|\) find rational masses \(\pi_{t,x}\ge0\) of finite support satisfying \(\sum_x\pi_{t,x}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,x}\pi_{t,x}x_e=q_e\) for each \(e\), and \(v_t(x)\ge 0.142\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) for every assigned bundle, while satisfying EFL across all positive-mass bundles?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(E\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) item kinds; bundles are count vectors \(x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}\), with no per-bundle capacity bound. Write \(b_e\) for the unit vector of kind \(e\).
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Supplies \(q_e\ge0\) are binary rationals; let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Each type has a finite-valued normalized monotone valuation \(v_t:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) with diminishing returns: \(v_t(x+b_e)-v_t(x)\ge v_t(y+b_e)-v_t(y)\) whenever \(x\le y\). Copies of each kind are interchangeable.
A comparison query takes binary-encoded bundles \(x,y\) and returns whether \(v_t(x)\ge v_t(y)\); the bundles represent disjoint collections of copies, even when their kind supports overlap.
Define \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)=\sup_{\sigma}\min_{x:\sigma_x>0}v_t(x)\), where \(\sigma\) ranges over finite-support probability distributions on integral bundles satisfying \(\sum_x\sigma_x x=q\).
An allocation consists of finitely many binary-rational masses \(\pi_{t,x}\ge0\), satisfying \(\sum_x\pi_{t,x}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,x}\pi_{t,x}x_e=q_e\) for every \(e\).
It is EFL if, for every \((t,x),(s,y)\) with positive assigned mass, either \(\sum_e y_e\le1\), or some \(e\) with \(y_e>0\) satisfies both \(v_t(x)\ge v_t(y-b_e)\) and \(v_t(x)\ge v_t(b_e)\).
Is there a randomized comparison-oracle algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time that, on every such instance, with probability at least \(2/3\) outputs an EFL allocation satisfying \(v_t(x)\ge0.142\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) whenever \(\pi_{t,x}>0\)?
For finite agents and distinct indivisible items, a polynomial-time comparison-query sampler always produces EFL and achieves simultaneous \(0.142\)-MMS with positive probability; a slightly smaller ratio is claimed with high probability. [Feige–Fine](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06451v1)
No result settling this exact mass formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Integral bundles mean nonnegative integer count vectors with interchangeable copies and no per-bundle capacity bound.
  • Submodularity on count vectors means diminishing returns, matching submodularity on distinct interchangeable copies.
  • Comparison queries use binary-encoded count vectors representing disjoint copies; overlap of item kinds does not prohibit a query, and equality returns true.
  • EFL applies to every ordered pair of positive-mass assignments, including assignments of the same type, and removes exactly one copy.
  • The benchmark permits real probabilities in its finite-support distributions; output allocation masses must be binary rationals.
  • Randomized algorithmic success means probability at least two-thirds on every instance, with oracle queries and explicit output included in the polynomial running time.

Partial progress: an EFL guarantee depending on the number of kinds

The general randomized polynomial-time algorithm remains unresolved. I prove two further results:

  • Every EFL allocation gives every positively assigned bundle at least $1/(m+1)$ of its type’s mass benchmark. Consequently, for $m\leq 6$, EFL alone implies the requested $0.142$ guarantee.
  • If each type’s valuation depends only on total bundle size, there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm using no comparison queries that produces an EFL allocation attaining the full benchmark.

The first result is conditional on having an EFL allocation; it does not supply a general efficient construction.

1. Preliminary inequalities

Write
$$ |z|=\sum_{e\in E}z_e. $$

Fix a type and temporarily suppress its subscript. Set
$$ w_e=v(b_e),\qquad f_e(n)=v(nb_e) $$
for nonnegative integers $n$.

Normalization, monotonicity, and diminishing returns imply
$$ 0\leq v(z)\leq\sum_{e\in E}w_ez_e. $$
Indeed, construct $z$ by adding its copies individually. Each marginal contribution of kind $e$ is nonnegative and is at most its marginal contribution at the empty bundle, namely $w_e$.

The same assumptions imply subadditivity:
$$ v(u+z)\leq v(u)+v(z). $$
To prove this, enumerate the copies of $z$ and let $z^{(j)}$ be the partial count vector after its first $j$ copies. If the next copy has kind $e$, diminishing returns gives
$$ v(u+z^{(j)}+b_e)-v(u+z^{(j)}) \leq v(z^{(j)}+b_e)-v(z^{(j)}). $$
Summing these inequalities proves subadditivity. In particular,
$$ v(z)\leq\sum_{e\in E}f_e(z_e). $$

Define the univariate increments
$$ \Delta_e(n)=f_e(n)-f_e(n-1),\qquad n\geq1. $$
They satisfy
$$ 0\leq\Delta_e(n+1)\leq\Delta_e(n)\leq w_e. $$
The middle inequality follows by applying diminishing returns to the bundles $(n-1)b_e$ and $nb_e$.

For completeness, the benchmark is finite and its feasible set is nonempty. Independent floor-or-ceiling rounding of each coordinate of $q$ gives a finite-support distribution with mean $q$. Every feasible distribution $\sigma$ satisfies
$$ \min_{z:\sigma_z>0}v(z) \leq \sum_z\sigma_zv(z) \leq \sum_e w_eq_e. $$
Therefore
$$ 0\leq\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}(q)\leq\sum_e w_eq_e<\infty. $$
The arguments below do not require attainment of this supremum.

2. A dimension-dependent guarantee for every EFL allocation

Theorem

Let $\pi$ be any feasible EFL allocation, and fix a positively assigned pair $(t,x)$. Define
$$ a=v_t(x), \qquad I=\{e\in E:q_e>0,\ v_t(b_e)\leq a\}, \qquad k=|I|. $$
Then
$$ \mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\leq(k+1)a. $$
Consequently,
$$ v_t(x)\geq\frac{1}{m+1}\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q). $$

Proof

Throughout this proof, all values refer to type $t$. Suppress its subscript and aggregate the allocation across types:
$$ \rho_y=\sum_s\pi_{s,y}. $$
Thus $\rho$ is a finite-support probability distribution satisfying
$$ \sum_y\rho_y y=q. $$

Step 1: expensive singleton kinds occupy singleton bundles

Define
$$ H=\{e\in E:w_e>a\}. $$

Every assigned bundle containing a kind in $H$ must be a singleton. To see this, suppose $\rho_y>0$, $|y|\geq2$, and $y$ contains a copy of a kind in $H$. EFL relative to the receiving pair $(t,x)$ requires a kind $e$ satisfying
$$ a\geq v(y-b_e), \qquad a\geq w_e. $$
If $e\in H$, the second inequality fails. If $e\notin H$, removing that copy leaves a copy of a kind in $H$, so monotonicity gives
$$ v(y-b_e)\geq w_h>a $$
for some $h\in H$. The first inequality fails as well.

Put
$$ h=\sum_{e\in H}q_e. $$
Supply conservation and the preceding observation show that $h$ equals the total assigned mass of singleton bundles whose kinds belong to $H$.

The receiving bundle $x$ contains no kind in $H$, since otherwise monotonicity would give $v(x)>a$. Its assigned mass is positive, so
$$ h<1. $$
Write
$$ p_0=1-h>0. $$
This is the total assigned mass of bundles containing no kind in $H$.

Step 2: EFL bounds the coordinate counts after one deletion

For each $e\in I$, consider the initial interval
$$ \{n\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}:f_e(n)\leq a\}. $$
It contains $0$ and $1$.

If this interval is finite, let $K_e$ be its largest element and define
$$ c_e=f_e(K_e), \qquad \delta_e=f_e(K_e+1)-f_e(K_e). $$
Then
$$ K_e\geq1,\qquad c_e\leq a,\qquad 0\leq\delta_e\leq w_e\leq a. $$

If the interval is infinite, define
$$ c_e=\sup_{n\geq0}f_e(n)\leq a, \qquad \delta_e=0. $$

For every supported bundle $y$ containing no kind in $H$ and satisfying $|y|\geq2$, choose one EFL witness $e(y)$. It satisfies
$$ v(y-b_{e(y)})\leq a. $$
Its kind belongs to $I$: its supply is positive, and the EFL singleton inequality gives $w_{e(y)}\leq a$.

Let
$$ d_e= \sum_{\substack{y:\rho_y>0,\ y\text{ contains no kind in }H\\ |y|\geq2,\ e(y)=e}} \rho_y. $$
Exactly one witness is chosen for each such nonsingleton bundle, hence
$$ \sum_{e\in I}d_e\leq p_0. $$

Fix $e\in I$ with finite $K_e$. The remainder $y-b_{e(y)}$ has value at most $a$. By monotonicity, its kind-$e$ subbundle also has value at most $a$, so its kind-$e$ count is at most $K_e$. Thus
$$ y_e\leq K_e+\mathbf 1_{\{e(y)=e\}}. $$
For singleton and empty bundles containing no kind in $H$, the bound $y_e\leq K_e$ holds because $K_e\geq1$.

Bundles containing a kind in $H$ are singleton bundles and contribute no supply of kind $e\in I$. Averaging therefore gives
$$ q_e\leq p_0K_e+d_e $$
for every $e\in I$ with finite $K_e$.

Step 3: a supporting-line inequality

For finite $K_e$, decreasing increments imply
$$ f_e(n)\leq c_e+\delta_e(n-K_e) $$
for every nonnegative integer $n$.

For $n>K_e$, each increment after $K_e$ is at most $\delta_e$, so
$$ f_e(n)-f_e(K_e)\leq(n-K_e)\delta_e. $$
For $n<K_e$, each increment between $n$ and $K_e$ is at least $\delta_e$, so
$$ f_e(K_e)-f_e(n)\geq(K_e-n)\delta_e. $$
These are precisely the required inequalities.

For infinite $K_e$, the definition of $c_e$ gives
$$ f_e(n)\leq c_e $$
for every $n$.

Step 4: bound every feasible benchmark distribution

Take any feasible finite-support benchmark distribution $\sigma$, let $Z$ have that distribution, and write
$$ r=\min_{z:\sigma_z>0}v(z). $$

Let $A$ be the event that $Z$ contains no kind in $H$, and put $p=\Pr(A)$. A bundle containing a kind in $H$ contains at least one such copy. Therefore
$$ \Pr(A^{\mathrm c}) \leq \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{e\in H}Z_e\right] =h, $$
and hence
$$ p\geq p_0>0. $$

For each $e\in I$,
$$ \mathbb E[Z_e\mid A]\leq\frac{q_e}{p}. $$
If $K_e$ is finite, Step 2 gives
$$ \mathbb E[Z_e\mid A]-K_e \leq \frac{p_0K_e+d_e}{p}-K_e \leq \frac{d_e}{p_0}. $$
Using the supporting-line inequality,
$$ \mathbb E[f_e(Z_e)\mid A] \leq c_e+\frac{\delta_ed_e}{p_0}. $$
For infinite $K_e$, this expectation is at most $c_e$.

Kinds with zero supply have zero count almost surely. On $A$, every other kind lies in $I$. Subadditivity therefore yields
$$ r \leq \mathbb E[v(Z)\mid A] \leq \sum_{e\in I}c_e+ \frac{1}{p_0}\sum_{e\in I}\delta_ed_e. $$
Since $\sum_e d_e\leq p_0$,
$$ r\leq \sum_{e\in I}c_e+\max_{e\in I}\delta_e \leq ka+a. $$
Here the maximum over an empty set is defined to be zero. Thus the argument also covers $I=\varnothing$ and $a=0$.

Taking the supremum over all feasible $\sigma$ proves the theorem. $\square$

A sharper form of the proved bound

The proof establishes
$$ \mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q) \leq \sum_{e\in I}c_e+\max_{e\in I}\delta_e. $$
For finite $K_e$, decreasing increments additionally give
$$ K_e\delta_e\leq f_e(K_e)=c_e\leq a. $$
If every coordinate valuation satisfies $f_e(n)\leq a$ for all $n$, then all the $\delta_e$ vanish and the bound improves to
$$ \mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\leq ka. $$

3. Consequences and a limitation

EFL suffices for the target ratio when there are at most six kinds

For $m\leq6$,
$$ \frac{1}{m+1}\geq\frac17>\frac{71}{500}=0.142. $$
Thus every feasible EFL allocation satisfies the requested value guarantee in this regime.

More generally, the same conclusion holds whenever every positively assigned pair has at most six positive-supply kinds whose singleton values do not exceed its receiving value.

This gives an efficient certificate for a proposed allocation. If it has $K$ positive pairs, feasibility can be checked by exact rational arithmetic. EFL can be checked using at most
$$ 2mK^2 $$
comparison queries: for each ordered pair, try every kind present in the target bundle and check the two required inequalities. The running time is polynomial in the input and candidate-allocation encoding lengths.

For $m\leq6$, passing these checks also certifies the $0.142$ guarantee, without computing any benchmark. Finding a suitably encoded EFL allocation remains a separate task.

Arbitrary EFL allocations can lose a factor proportional to the number of kinds

For every integer $m\geq2$, consider one type of mass $1$, supplies
$$ q_e=1\qquad(e\in E), $$
and valuation
$$ v(z)=\sum_{e\in E}\min\{z_e,1\}. $$
This valuation is normalized, monotone, and has diminishing returns.

Assign mass $1/m$ to each bundle $mb_e$. The allocation is feasible, since its expected count of every kind is $1$. Every supported bundle has value $1$.

It is EFL. For any receiving bundle and target $mb_e$,
$$ v(mb_e-b_e)=1, \qquad v(b_e)=1, $$
which equal the receiving value.

However,
$$ \mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}(q)=m. $$
The deterministic bundle $\sum_e b_e$ attains value $m$ and has mean $q$, while no bundle has value exceeding $m$.

Thus the supported bundles attain exactly a $1/m$ fraction of the benchmark. In particular, for $m\geq8$, EFL alone does not imply the requested $0.142$ guarantee.

A favorable EFL allocation also exists in this example: assign the bundle $\sum_e b_e$ to everyone. The example limits what can be inferred from EFL alone.

4. A polynomial-time exact algorithm for valuations depending only on total size

Assume the additional promise that, for every type $t$, there is a finite-valued nondecreasing function $g_t$ such that
$$ v_t(z)=g_t(|z|). $$
Under the original diminishing-returns assumption, the increments of $g_t$ are nonincreasing. The construction below requires only its monotonicity.

Put
$$ Q=\sum_e q_e,\qquad k=\lfloor Q\rfloor. $$

Benchmark formula

Every feasible finite-support distribution has a supported bundle of size at most $k$. Otherwise its expected size would be at least $k+1>Q$. Consequently,
$$ \mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\leq g_t(k). $$

I now construct a rational distribution with mean $q$ whose supported bundle sizes belong to $\{k,k+1\}$. This proves
$$ \mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)=g_t(k). $$

Constructing the distribution

Enumerate the kinds as $1,\ldots,m$. Write
$$ q_i=c_i+r_i,\qquad c_i=\lfloor q_i\rfloor,\qquad 0\leq r_i<1. $$
Define
$$ S_0=0,\qquad S_i=\sum_{j=1}^i r_j, \qquad R=S_m=\ell+\eta, $$
where $\ell=\lfloor R\rfloor$ and $0\leq\eta<1$.

For $u\in[0,1)$, define
$$ z_i(u)= \#\{j\in\mathbb Z:S_{i-1}\leq u+j<S_i\}. $$
Because the interval has length $r_i<1$, each $z_i(u)$ is either $0$ or $1$.

If $U$ is uniform on $[0,1)$, then
$$ \mathbb E[z_i(U)]=r_i. $$
Indeed,
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[z_i(U)] &= \sum_{j\in\mathbb Z} \int_0^1 \mathbf 1_{[S_{i-1},S_i)}(u+j)\,du\\ &= \sum_{j\in\mathbb Z} \operatorname{length}\bigl([S_{i-1},S_i)\cap[j,j+1)\bigr)\\ &=S_i-S_{i-1}=r_i. \end{aligned} $$
Only finitely many summands are nonzero.

The intervals $[S_{i-1},S_i)$ partition $[0,R)$, so
$$ \sum_i z_i(u) = \#\{j\in\mathbb Z:0\leq u+j<R\} = \ell+\mathbf 1_{\{u<\eta\}}. $$
Therefore the bundle
$$ x(u)=c+z(u) $$
has total size $k$ or $k+1$.

To obtain an explicit finite rational distribution, form the distinct breakpoints
$$ B=\{0,1\}\cup \{S_i-\lfloor S_i\rfloor:1\leq i\leq m\}. $$
Write them in increasing order as
$$ 0=d_0<d_1<\cdots<d_J=1. $$
There are at most $m+1$ intervals.

Each vector $z(u)$ is constant on every open interval $(d_{j-1},d_j)$: a coordinate can change only when $u$ equals the fractional part of one of its interval endpoints. Choose
$$ u_j=\frac{d_{j-1}+d_j}{2}, \qquad p_j=d_j-d_{j-1}, \qquad x^{(j)}=x(u_j). $$
Then
$$ p_j>0,\qquad \sum_jp_j=1,\qquad \sum_jp_jx^{(j)}=q, $$
and every $x^{(j)}$ has size $k$ or $k+1$.

All the $p_j$ and all bundle coordinates have polynomial binary encoding length.

The allocation and its guarantees

For every type, output
$$ \pi_{t,x^{(j)}}=\mu_t p_j, $$
combining duplicate bundles if necessary. There are at most $\tau(m+1)$ positive pairs.

The type-mass constraints follow from $\sum_jp_j=1$. The supply constraints follow from $\sum_t\mu_t=1$ and $\sum_jp_jx^{(j)}=q$.

Every assigned bundle satisfies
$$ v_t(x^{(j)}) \geq g_t(k) =\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q). $$

To check EFL, consider any supported receiving bundle $x$ and target bundle $y$. If $|y|\leq1$, EFL is automatic. Otherwise $k\geq1$, and
$$ |y|-1\leq k\leq|x|. $$
For any kind $e$ present in $y$, monotonicity gives
$$ v_t(x)=g_t(|x|) \geq g_t(|y|-1) =v_t(y-b_e), $$
and
$$ v_t(x)\geq g_t(1)=v_t(b_e). $$
Thus the allocation is EFL.

The algorithm uses exact rational arithmetic, sorting at most $m+2$ breakpoints, and computing at most $m+1$ bundles. Sums and products of the input rationals have polynomial encoding length. Hence its running time and output length are polynomial in $m,\tau,L$. It uses no comparison queries and succeeds deterministically.

5. What remains open

These results do not settle the original question for arbitrary diminishing-returns valuations.

The dimension-dependent theorem removes the benchmark requirement once EFL has been obtained for $m\leq6$, but it does not construct such an allocation efficiently. For unrestricted $m$, the example above shows that an algorithm must select EFL allocations with additional value guarantees.

The cited finite-copy sampler supplies simultaneous EFL and exact $0.142$-MMS with positive probability. Its conversion into a polynomial-time algorithm on the succinct mass input, with success probability at least $2/3$, remains unproved here.
