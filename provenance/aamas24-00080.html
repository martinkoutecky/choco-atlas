The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type weights \(w_a>0\), masses \(\mu_a\) with \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\), quota \(q\), and accuracy \(\eta>0\), let mass coalitions be \(z\in\prod_a[0,\mu_a]\) with \(\nu_\mu(z)=1\) exactly when \(\sum_a w_a z_a\ge q\). Find a payoff density \(y\ge0\) with \(\sum_a\mu_a y_a=1\) and an \(\eta\)-accurate estimate of \(\min_y\max_{z}(\nu_\mu(z)-\langle y,z\rangle)\), together with a payoff whose every mass-coalition deficit is within \(\eta\) of optimal.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_a\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a positive binary rational voting-weight density \(w_a\). The binary rational quota satisfies \(0<q\le\sum_a\mu_aw_a\), and the binary rational accuracy is \(\eta>0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; there is no separate alternative-count parameter \(m\).
A mass coalition is any real vector \(z\in\prod_a[0,\mu_a]\), including the empty and grand coalitions; its value is \(\nu_\mu(z)=\mathbf{1}\{\sum_aw_az_a\ge q\}\).
A feasible payoff density is \(y\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{\tau}\) with \(\sum_a\mu_ay_a=1\); coalition payments are \(\sum_ay_az_a\). Define \(D(y)=\max_z(\nu_\mu(z)-\sum_ay_az_a)\) and \(\varepsilon^*=\min_yD(y)\).
The approximation problem asks for a rational estimate \(\widehat\varepsilon\) and a rational feasible density \(\widehat y\) satisfying \(|\widehat\varepsilon-\varepsilon^*|\le\eta\) and \(D(\widehat y)\le\varepsilon^*+\eta\).

Does this additive approximation problem admit an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,1/\eta)\) time, or is achieving these guarantees NP-hard?

For finite games, the verified approximation guarantee says that bounding every nonempty coalition's loss \([\max\{0,v(C)-\varepsilon-\sum_{i\in C}p_i\}]^2/(2|C|)\) by \(\gamma^2\) bounds every deficit by \(\varepsilon+\sqrt{2n}\gamma\), where \(n\) is the number of players ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03928)).
For measurable player spaces, least-core containment in the Mas-Colell bargaining set is known when the grand coalition has maximal worth ([source](https://holzman.technion.ac.il/files/2012/09/leastcore.pdf)); no result settling the stated mass approximation problem was located.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact solution

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, even exactly. Define the total voting weight \(W=\sum_{a=1}^{\tau}\mu_aw_a\). Then

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon^*=1-\frac{q}{W},\qquad y_a^*=\frac{w_a}{W}\quad(a=1,\ldots,\tau).\)

The proof uses the stated real-mass coalition model, in which every type can be included in an arbitrary fraction.

Proof

Because all masses and voting-weight densities are positive, \(W>0\). The quota assumption gives \(r:=q/W\in(0,1]\).

Lower bound for every feasible density. Let \(y\) be any feasible payoff density, and consider the coalition \(z_a=r\mu_a\). Since \(0<r\le1\), this coalition satisfies \(0\le z_a\le\mu_a\). Its voting weight is

\(\displaystyle \sum_aw_az_a=r\sum_aw_a\mu_a=rW=q,\)

so it is winning. Its payment is

\(\displaystyle \sum_ay_az_a=r\sum_a\mu_ay_a=r,\)

where the last equality follows from payoff feasibility. Consequently, \(D(y)\ge1-r\). Since this holds for every feasible \(y\), we have \(\varepsilon^*\ge1-r\).

A density attaining the bound. Set \(y_a^*=w_a/W\). These coordinates are positive, and

\(\displaystyle \sum_a\mu_ay_a^*=\frac{\sum_a\mu_aw_a}{W}=1,\)

so \(y^*\) is feasible.

For any winning coalition \(z\), its payment satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_ay_a^*z_a=\frac{\sum_aw_az_a}{W}\ge\frac qW=r.\)

Thus its deficit is at most \(1-r\). For any losing coalition, the deficit is \(-\sum_ay_a^*z_a\le0\le1-r\). These two cases cover every coalition, including the empty and grand coalitions. Therefore \(D(y^*)\le1-r\).

The lower bound already proved applies to \(y^*\), giving \(D(y^*)=1-r\). Hence \(\varepsilon^*=1-q/W\), as claimed. The boundary case \(q=W\) is included and yields \(\varepsilon^*=0\).

Algorithm and bit complexity

Compute \(W\) by exact rational arithmetic and return

\(\displaystyle \widehat\varepsilon=1-\frac qW,\qquad \widehat y_a=\frac{w_a}{W}.\)

These outputs are rational. They satisfy both requested guarantees with zero error:

\(\displaystyle |\widehat\varepsilon-\varepsilon^*|=0,\qquad D(\widehat y)=\varepsilon^*.\)

For completeness, write each input rational as an integer numerator divided by a positive integer denominator. A common denominator for the products \(\mu_aw_a\) is the product of their input denominators. Its bit length is bounded by the sum of those denominator bit lengths, hence by \(O(L)\). The corresponding numerators and their sum have bit length \(O(L+\log\tau)\). Forming \(q/W\) and every \(w_a/W\) likewise produces rational numbers of polynomial bit length.

Thus polynomially many integer arithmetic operations on polynomial-length integers suffice. The running time and total output length are polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), independently of \(1/\eta\), and therefore meet the requested bound.
