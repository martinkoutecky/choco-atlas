The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) party-choice types with rational masses \(\mu_p>0\), \(\sum_p\mu_p=1\), and a fixed full-supply slate of indivisible seats with binary-encoded positive integer weights \(w_1\ge\cdots\ge w_k\), let \(\omega=\sum_t w_t\), \(q(p)=\omega\mu_p\), and \(\ell_o(p)=\max\{\sum_{t\in S}w_t:S\subseteq[k],\ |S|\le\lfloor k\mu_p\rfloor,\ \sum_{t\in S}w_t\le q(p)\}\). Find an assignment \(\sigma:[k]\to[m]\) such that \(\sum_{t:\sigma(t)=p}w_t\ge\ell_o(p)\) for every \(p\), whenever such an assignment exists.

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here each type chooses one party, all \(m\geq1\) parties have positive mass, and \(\tau=m\).
The masses satisfy \(\mu_p>0\) and \(\sum_{p=1}^{m}\mu_p=1\). There are \(k\geq1\) indivisible seats with binary-encoded integer weights \(w_1\geq\cdots\geq w_k\geq1\). Every party can fill all seats.
Define \(\omega=\sum_{t=1}^{k}w_t\), \(q(p)=\omega\mu_p\), and \(\ell_o(p)=\max\{\sum_{t\in S}w_t:S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\},\ |S|\leq\lfloor k\mu_p\rfloor,\ \sum_{t\in S}w_t\leq q(p)\}\); the empty subset is allowed.
Find an assignment \(\sigma:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to\{1,\ldots,m\}\) satisfying \(\sum_{t:\sigma(t)=p}w_t\geq\ell_o(p)\) for every party whenever such an assignment exists. The cardinality bound defines the quota only; it does not constrain the assignment. On infeasible inputs the output is unrestricted.

Is this search problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?

Result

The search problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions, already for \(m=\tau=2\) and \(\mu_1=\mu_2=\tfrac12\). This is the restriction established by [Proposition 3 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18102v2#S8.SS1). Here is the reduction with the cardinality and encoding details explicit.

Two-party feasibility

For arbitrary positive masses with \(\mu_1+\mu_2=1\), choose a subset \(S\) attaining \(\ell_o(1)\); such a subset exists because the maximization is finite and includes the empty subset. Give \(S\) to party \(1\) and its complement to party \(2\). Their weights satisfy
\(\displaystyle r(1)=\ell_o(1),\qquad r(2)=\omega-\ell_o(1)\geq\omega-q(1)=q(2)\geq\ell_o(2).\)

Thus every two-party instance is feasible. The complement need not satisfy a cardinality bound, since that bound defines the quota only.

Reduction from Partition

Use the standard \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness of binary-encoded Partition: given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\), decide whether some subset sums to half their total.

Construct two supported types, each of mass \(\tfrac12\), and use the integers \(a_t\), sorted nonincreasingly, as seat weights. Write \(A=\sum_t a_t=\omega\). Query any oracle solving the stated search problem, and accept exactly when its output satisfies \(2r(1)=A\).

If Partition is a yes-instance, one side of an equal-sum partition has at most \(\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) elements. That side witnesses
\(\displaystyle \ell_o(1)=\ell_o(2)=A/2,\)
because both quota maximizations are identical and bounded above by \(A/2\). Every valid oracle output therefore gives both parties weight \(A/2\).

Conversely, an output satisfying \(2r(1)=A\) itself supplies an equal-sum partition. Hence the test cannot accept a no-instance. Every query is feasible by the preceding proof.

Encoding and running time

Let \(N\) be the Partition input length and \(L\) the constructed input length. The construction has \(m=\tau=2\), preserves \(k\), and satisfies \(L=O(N)\): weights are merely reordered, and both masses have constant-size binary encodings.

Sorting takes \(O(k\log(k+1)L)\) bit operations; summing and checking the output takes \(O(kL)\). If the search solver runs in time \(T(m,\tau,k,L)\), the reduction runs in
\(\displaystyle O(k\log(k+1)L)+T(2,2,k,L).\)

It uses one oracle call and works for every valid oracle output. Consequently, a solver polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\) would imply \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).

The prover’s claim is proved. The argument establishes \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions, already for \(m=\tau=2\) with equal masses. No major or minor issues were found.

  • The construction satisfies the input requirements.

Each constructed instance has exactly two supported types, with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_1=\mu_2=\tfrac12\). Their masses sum to \(1\). The positive integer Partition inputs become positive integer seat weights, sorted into the required nonincreasing order. The construction imposes no cardinality restriction on assignments.

The invoked source problem is stated precisely: Partition on binary-encoded positive integers asks whether an indexed subset sums to half the total. Its standard \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness applies to this domain. Repeated weights cause no ambiguity because subsets refer to seat indices.

  • The two-party feasibility argument is correct.

The maximization defining \(\ell_o(1)\) has a finite, nonempty candidate family, since it includes the empty subset. Consequently, an attaining subset \(S\) exists.

Giving \(S\) to party \(1\) and every remaining seat to party \(2\) yields
\[ r(1)=\ell_o(1),\qquad r(2)=\omega-\ell_o(1)\geq\omega-q(1)=q(2)\geq\ell_o(2). \]
Each step follows from the definitions: \(\ell_o(1)\leq q(1)\), the masses sum to \(1\), and \(\ell_o(2)\leq q(2)\).

The complement’s cardinality is irrelevant under the task’s assignment rules. This establishes feasibility of every oracle query, including queries arising from no-instances of Partition.

  • The forward direction handles the cardinality bound correctly.

If an equal-sum partition exists, its two sides have cardinalities summing to \(k\). Therefore, at least one side has cardinality at most \(\lfloor k/2\rfloor\). Its weight is \(A/2\), so it is admissible in both quota maximizations: the masses, cardinality bounds, and weight bounds are identical for the two parties.

Thus the asserted equality \(\ell_o(1)=\ell_o(2)=A/2\) follows. Every valid oracle output gives each party weight at least \(A/2\). Since their weights sum to \(A\), both weights must equal \(A/2\). The acceptance test therefore accepts every yes-instance, regardless of which valid assignment the oracle returns.

  • The reverse direction is correct.

An assignment satisfying \(2r(1)=A\) identifies a subset of the input weights summing to \(A/2\). Its complement also sums to \(A/2\), so the original Partition instance is a yes-instance.

Consequently, no valid oracle output can cause false acceptance. If \(A\) is odd, the equality cannot hold because \(r(1)\) is an integer. The case \(k=1\) is also covered: both quota cardinality bounds are zero, feasibility still holds, and the positive single weight cannot be partitioned equally.

  • The encoding and running-time bounds are valid.

Sorting preserves the weight encodings, and the additional party and mass information has constant size. Hence \(L=O(N)\). All intermediate sums have \(O(L)\) bits. The stated bounds \(O(k\log(k+1)L)\) for sorting and \(O(kL)\) for summation and checking are valid conservative bit-operation bounds.

There is exactly one oracle call, on parameters \(m=\tau=2\), with unchanged \(k\) and polynomial input length. The displayed total running time therefore establishes the claimed polynomial-time Turing reduction.

  • The citation supports the stated restriction.

[Proposition 3 and its appendix proof](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18102v2#S8.SS1) use two parties with equal votes and a Partition reduction. The prover’s explicit rational-mass construction matches this restriction and independently supplies the necessary verification.

No additional clarification or repair is needed. The proof establishes hardness; its exclusion of a polynomial-time solver is correctly conditional on \(\mathsf{P}\ne\mathsf{NP}\).
