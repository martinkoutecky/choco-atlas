The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of roommate types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), compatibility between types, type-level rankings of compatible partner types, and prescribed unmatched masses \(u_t\) whose positive support is independent, decide whether a measurable reciprocal matching of an atomless population with type masses \(\mu_t\) exists that leaves mass \(u_t\) of each type unmatched and is popular. Popularity means that for every alternative feasible matching on the same population, the mass preferring the proposed matching’s partner type is at least the mass preferring the alternative’s; agents indifferent between partner types contribute to neither side. Restrict to instances with no stable matching, as in the setting of Theorem 2.

1. Every society admits a stable measurable matching

A finite existence theorem

I use the following known theorem about ordinally stable fractional matchings.

For any finite simple compatibility graph with weak preference orders over acceptable neighbors, there exist symmetric numbers \(f_{ij}\geq 0\), supported on compatible pairs, satisfying \(\sum_j f_{ij}\leq 1\), such that every compatible pair \(\{i,j\}\) satisfies at least one of

\[ \sum_{h:\,h\succeq_i j}f_{ih}=1, \qquad \sum_{h:\,h\succeq_j i}f_{jh}=1. \]

Here equality means that the vertex is completely assigned to partners it weakly prefers to the other endpoint. This existence theorem includes incomplete lists and ties; isolated vertices can simply remain unmatched. See Proposition 3.5 of [Chen–Roy–Sorge, *Fractional Matchings under Preferences: Stability and Optimality*](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0013.pdf).

Applying the theorem to rational masses

Choose a positive integer \(D\) such that \(q_t=D\mu_t\) is an integer for every \(t\). Because the masses sum to one, \(\sum_t q_t=D\).

Replace type \(t\) by \(q_t\) finite vertices. Two vertices are compatible precisely when their types are compatible. Each vertex ranks partner types according to its type’s ranking and ties all vertices of the same partner type.

This finite graph meets the theorem’s hypotheses. Apply it to obtain \(f\), and define

\[ x_{ts}=\frac1D\sum_{\substack{i:\,t(i)=t\\j:\,t(j)=s}}f_{ij}, \qquad v_t=\mu_t-\sum_{s\in N_G(t)}x_{ts}. \]

Symmetry of \(f\) gives \(x_{ts}=x_{st}\), and the degree inequalities give \(v_t\geq 0\).

Partition each interval \(X_t\) into subintervals of lengths \(x_{ts}\), one for each compatible \(s\), and a remaining subinterval of length \(v_t\). Pair the two subintervals corresponding to \(x_{ts}=x_{st}\) by a translation and its inverse. Fix the remaining subintervals pointwise. This constructs a measurable, measure-preserving involution \(M\).

The quantities \(v_t\) are its unmatched masses; they need not equal the prescribed \(u_t\).

Checking the exact stability definition

For a compatible type pair \(\{t,s\}\), let

\[ b_t(s)=\lambda\{x\in X_t:s\succ_t p_M(x)\}. \]

The blocking pairs between these two types form the Cartesian product of the two sets measured by \(b_t(s)\) and \(b_s(t)\). Consequently, their product measure is \(b_t(s)b_s(t)\).

For a finite vertex \(i\) of type \(t\), define

\[ d_i(s)=1-\sum_{j:\,t(j)\succeq_t s}f_{ij}. \]

Every \(d_i(s)\) is nonnegative, and the construction gives

\[ b_t(s)=\frac1D\sum_{i:\,t(i)=t}d_i(s). \]

Suppose \(b_t(s)b_s(t)>0\). Then some vertex \(i\) of type \(t\) has \(d_i(s)>0\), and some vertex \(j\) of type \(s\) has \(d_j(t)>0\). These vertices are compatible. Neither endpoint satisfies the equality required by ordinal stability, contradicting the finite theorem.

Thus \(b_t(s)b_s(t)=0\) for every compatible type pair. There are finitely many such pairs, so the entire blocking-pair set has zero product measure. Therefore \(M\) is stable under the definition in the question.

Proved conclusion. Every input society admits a stable measurable matching, without imposing prescribed unmatched masses.

The cloning argument is an existence proof; \(D\) can be exponentially large in the binary input length. No polynomial-time construction is needed to conclude that the promised domain is empty. On that domain, a constant-output algorithm takes \(O(1)\) time, or \(O(L)\) if reading the input is required.

2. Popularity forces at most two outcomes per type

This result uses the question’s same-individual voting rule.

Proposition. If \(M\) is popular, then for every type \(t\), at most two outcomes in \(N_G(t)\cup\{\bot\}\) occur on subsets of \(X_t\) of positive measure.

Proof. Suppose three outcomes \(a\succ_t b\succ_t c\) all occur with positive measure. By nonatomicity, choose equally sized measurable subsets \(A_a,A_b,A_c\) of their respective outcome classes, each of measure \(\delta>0\).

Let \(\pi\) be a measure-preserving bijection that cyclically maps

\[ A_a\longrightarrow A_b,\qquad A_b\longrightarrow A_c,\qquad A_c\longrightarrow A_a, \]

and fixes everyone else. Define \(N=\pi M\pi^{-1}\). Conjugation preserves measurability, measure preservation, and the involution property. Because \(\pi\) preserves types, compatibility is also preserved.

Individuals formerly receiving \(b\) now receive \(a\), and those formerly receiving \(c\) now receive \(b\). Both groups prefer \(N\). Individuals formerly receiving \(a\) now receive \(c\) and prefer \(M\).

Every other individual has the same partner type as before. This includes individuals outside \(X_t\): their partners may change identity, but retain their type. Conjugation also transports fixed points correctly when \(c=\bot\).

Therefore the vote margin favoring \(N\) is \(2\delta-\delta=\delta>0\), contradicting popularity. \(\square\)

In particular, if \(u_t>0\), a popular matching can give type \(t\) at most one compatible partner type of positive mass.

3. An exact linear program for same-individual popularity

Fix a measurable matching \(M\). Partition the population into its positive-measure outcome classes

\[ B_{t,a}=\{x\in X_t:p_M(x)=a\}. \]

Index these classes by \(i\in Q\). Write \(t_i\) for the class’s type, \(a_i\) for its outcome under \(M\), and \(b_i=\lambda(B_i)>0\).

Let \(F\) contain all unordered class pairs \(\{i,j\}\) whose underlying types are compatible. Define the vote of type \(t\) for outcome \(s\) against outcome \(a\) by

\[ \operatorname{vote}_t(s,a)= \begin{cases} 1&\text{if }s\succ_t a,\\ 0&\text{if }s=a,\\ -1&\text{if }a\succ_t s. \end{cases} \]

Set

\[ w_{ij}=\operatorname{vote}_{t_i}(t_j,a_i) +\operatorname{vote}_{t_j}(t_i,a_j), \]

and let \(c_i=0\) when \(a_i=\bot\), and \(c_i=-1\) otherwise.

The challenger’s optimization problem

Consider nonnegative variables \(z_{ij}\) for \(\{i,j\}\in F\) and \(r_i\), satisfying

\[ \sum_{j:\{i,j\}\in F}z_{ij}+r_i=b_i \qquad(i\in Q). \]

Maximize

\[ \sum_{\{i,j\}\in F}w_{ij}z_{ij}+\sum_{i\in Q}c_i r_i. \tag{1} \]

Every feasible alternative matching \(N\) gives such variables: \(z_{ij}\) is the mass it pairs between \(B_i\) and \(B_j\), and \(r_i\) is the mass it leaves unmatched in \(B_i\). Measure preservation and the involution property make the paired masses symmetric. Objective (1) is exactly the vote margin favoring \(N\).

Conversely, every feasible vector \((z,r)\) can be realized by an alternative matching on the same individuals. Partition each nonatomic class \(B_i\) into the required portions, pair equally sized portions using measure-preserving bijections, and fix the unmatched portions. These constructions hold modulo null sets, as permitted in the question.

Hence (1) computes the maximum vote margin of an actual measurable challenger. It imposes no restrictions on the challenger’s unmatched masses.

Since \(M\) itself achieves zero, \(M\) is popular precisely when the optimum is zero.

Duality and a characterization depending only on support

The dual minimizes \(\sum_i b_i\alpha_i\), subject to

\[ \alpha_i+\alpha_j\geq w_{ij} \quad(\{i,j\}\in F), \qquad \alpha_i\geq c_i \quad(i\in Q). \tag{2} \]

The primal is feasible and bounded. The dual is feasible, for example with every \(\alpha_i=1\). Finite-dimensional linear-programming duality therefore applies, with attained optimal values.

Let \(H\subseteq E\) be the set of type pairs used by \(M\) with positive mass. Write \(x_{ts}>0\) for their paired masses. The dual objective is

\[ \sum_i b_i\alpha_i = \sum_{\{t,s\}\in H} x_{ts}\bigl(\alpha_{t,s}+\alpha_{s,t}\bigr) + \sum_{t:u_t>0}u_t\alpha_{t,\bot}. \tag{3} \]

Every term on the right is nonnegative: the corresponding constraints in (2) have right-hand side zero. Thus the objective is zero exactly when

\[ \alpha_{t,s}+\alpha_{s,t}=0 \quad(\{t,s\}\in H), \qquad \alpha_{t,\bot}=0 \quad(t:u_t>0). \tag{4} \]

We obtain the following complete characterization.

Proposition. A matching with positive type-pair support \(H\) and unmatched support \(U\) is popular if and only if the finite linear system (2), (4) is feasible.

Its coefficients depend only on preferences, \(H\), and \(U\). Consequently, once paired masses with these supports are feasible, popularity is independent of their particular positive values and of how individuals realize them.

  • Mass feasibility: find \(x_e>0\) for \(e\in H\) satisfying

\[ \sum_{e\in H:\,t\in e}x_e=\mu_t-u_t \qquad(t\in T). \]

Strict positivity is tested by introducing \(0\leq\delta\leq1\), requiring \(x_e\geq\delta\), and maximizing \(\delta\). Accept precisely when its optimum is positive. For empty \(H\), simply check that every \(\mu_t-u_t\) is zero.

The internal rankings are \(C\succ_B A\) and \(B\succ_C D\); each endpoint has its sole neighbor as its only acceptable partner.

Prescribe

\[ u_A=u_D=\frac14, \qquad u_B=u_C=0. \]

Then \(U=\{A,D\}\), \(N_G(U)=\{B,C\}\), and \(k=0\).

Match mass \(1/8\) along each of \(AB\) and \(CD\). For the dual characterization, assign:

  • potential \(-1\) to the matched endpoint classes;
  • potential \(0\) to the unmatched endpoint classes;
  • potential \(1\) to both internal classes.

Along \(AB\) and \(CD\), the class-pair vote weights are zero for matched endpoint individuals and one for unmatched endpoint individuals. The corresponding potential sums are exactly zero and one. Along \(BC\), both endpoints prefer the new partner, so its weight and potential sum are both two. The unmatched constraints also hold, and the dual objective is zero.

Thus the matching is popular.

However, every matching with these prescribed masses is unstable. Endpoint compatibility forces mass \(1/8\) along \(AB\) and \(CD\), exhausting both internal types. All individuals of \(B\) and \(C\) then strictly prefer each other, producing blocking pairs of product measure \(1/64>0\).

The society nevertheless has a stable matching: pair all of \(B\) with all of \(C\), leaving both endpoint types completely unmatched.

This example shows that prescribed-mass existence remains substantive after dropping the impossible promise, including at \(k=0\). It also exposes a crucial difference from the discrete setting: a type in \(U\) may contain both matched and unmatched individuals.
