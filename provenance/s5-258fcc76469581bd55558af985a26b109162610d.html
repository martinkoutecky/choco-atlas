Result

The promised problem is in \(\mathrm P\): an exact welfare-maximizing mass assignment and supporting Walrasian prices can be computed in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,d,n,s,\tau,L)\).

The key is a structural consequence of the gross-substitutes promise on the actual whole-plan valuation. For nonnegative prices, rejection and plans placing every stage on one execution unit suffice to attain each type’s maximum utility. There are at most \(\tau n\) such plans, and their feasibility and values can be computed directly.

The proof below uses even the weaker, quantity-based gross-substitutes condition, so it also applies when indivisible resource copies are treated as separately priced items.

1. Definitions and the gross-substitutes condition

For each supplied type \(h\)\), define its total resource demand by

\(\displaystyle T_h=\sum_{v\in V_h}\rho_v>0.\)

Every nonrejection whole plan has exactly this total demand:

\(\displaystyle \sum_l a_{hq,l}=T_h.\)

This follows because every stage is assigned to exactly one execution unit. It holds regardless of eligibility, capacity, latency, and local-only restrictions.

Choose a common resource unit \(\delta>0\) such that every stage demand is an integer multiple of \(\delta\). Such a unit exists because all demands are rational. The argument uses this unit conceptually and never enumerates its copies.

At strictly positive prices \(r\), let

\(\displaystyle D_h(r)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{z\in\delta\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{n}}\bigl(v_h(z)-r\cdot z\bigr).\)

We use the following quantity-based gross-substitutes property: if \(r'\geq r\) and \(z\in D_h(r)\), there exists \(z'\in D_h(r')\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle z'_l\geq z_l\quad\text{whenever }r'_l=r_l.\)

This is the weak multi-unit GS condition stated in [Shioura–Tamura, Section 4.1](https://orsj.org/wp-content/or-archives50/pdf/e_mag/Vol.58_01_061.pdf). If the promise instead uses separately priced indivisible copies, it implies this condition: give all copies of a resource the same price and apply item-based GS; preserving unchanged-price copies preserves their quantities.

Only strictly positive prices occur in our application of GS. Their demand sets are nonempty: the valuation is bounded, and excess resources beyond a value-attaining plan can be removed to reduce payment.

2. A structural lemma for whole-plan demand

Fix a type \(h\), and temporarily suppress its subscript. Write \(T=T_h\) and \(B=B_h\). Include rejection in all plan maxima.

Lemma. For every nonnegative price vector \(p\), maximum whole-plan utility is attained by rejection or by a feasible plan assigning every stage to one execution unit.

Proof. Define

\(\displaystyle U(p)=\max_{q\in Q_h\cup\{\bot\}}\bigl(w_q-p\cdot a_q\bigr).\)

The maximum exists because there are finitely many assignments. Rejection ensures \(U(p)\geq0\).

First, for every strictly positive price vector \(r\),

\(\displaystyle \max_z\bigl(v_h(z)-r\cdot z\bigr)=\max_{q\in Q_h\cup\{\bot\}}\bigl(w_q-r\cdot a_q\bigr).\)

Indeed, if \(v_h(z)>0\), some feasible plan \(q\) attains it and satisfies \(a_q\leq z\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle v_h(z)-r\cdot z=w_q-r\cdot z\leq w_q-r\cdot a_q.\)

If \(v_h(z)=0\), its utility is at most rejection’s utility. Conversely, choosing \(z=a_q\) gives \(v_h(a_q)\geq w_q\), and choosing \(z=0\) realizes rejection.

If \(U(p)=0\), rejection proves the lemma. Suppose \(U(p)>0\), and choose a maximizing nonrejection plan \(q\), with resource vector \(a=a_q\). Set

\(\displaystyle t=\frac{U(p)}{T},\qquad r=p+t\mathbf1.\)

All coordinates of \(r\) are strictly positive. Every nonrejection plan \(q'\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle w_{q'}-r\cdot a_{q'}=w_{q'}-p\cdot a_{q'}-tT\leq U(p)-tT=0.\)

The selected plan achieves equality, and rejection also has utility zero. By the preceding identity, maximum valuation utility at \(r\) is zero. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle v_h(a)-r\cdot a\geq w_q-r\cdot a=0,\)

so \(a\in D_h(r)\).

Choose any coordinate \(j\) with \(a_j>0\). Keep its price unchanged and raise all other prices to a number \(M\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle M>\max_i r_i,\qquad M\delta>B.\)

Call the resulting vector \(r'\). Since costs are nonnegative, \(v_h(z)\leq B\). Thus any bundle having a positive coordinate outside \(j\) has utility strictly less than zero at \(r'\). Rejection has utility zero, so every demanded bundle at \(r'\) uses only coordinate \(j\).

By GS, there exists \(z'\in D_h(r')\) with \(z'_j\geq a_j>0\). Since \(r'_j>0\) and its utility is nonnegative, \(v_h(z')>0\). A whole plan attaining this value must use only execution unit \(j\). Its resource vector is therefore \(T e_j\).

In fact \(z'=T e_j\). Any larger quantity would be unused by that value-attaining plan, and deleting the excess would strictly improve utility. Let \(q^{(j)}\) be this feasible plan. Its nonnegative utility at \(r'\) gives

\(\displaystyle w_{q^{(j)}}-T r_j\geq0.\)

Hence its utility at the original prices satisfies

\(\displaystyle w_{q^{(j)}}-T p_j=w_{q^{(j)}}-T r_j+tT\geq U(p).\)

By definition of \(U(p)\), equality holds. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

This argument applies to multi-stage requests. Its substantive conclusion is that the actual GS promise rules out any strict priced advantage from distributing a whole plan across execution units.

3. Constructing a polynomial-size plan list

For each type \(h\) and execution unit \(l\), there is exactly one assignment placing every stage on \(l\). Denote it by \(q^{(l)}_h\).

Check its whole-plan feasibility directly:

  • every stage must be eligible for \(l\) and satisfy its local-only restriction;
  • its total demand must satisfy \(T_h\leq K_l\);
  • every request edge must satisfy \(\ell(l,l)\leq b_{vv'}\).

Let \(J_h\) contain the units passing these checks. For \(l\in J_h\), the value is explicitly computable:

\(\displaystyle w_{hl}=B_h-\alpha |E_h|\ell(l,l)-\beta\frac{T_h}{K_l}-\zeta.\)

There is exactly one used domain because the request is nonempty.

The lemma establishes, for every \(p\geq0\),

\(\displaystyle U_h(p)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{w_{hl}-T_hp_l:l\in J_h\}\bigr).\)

Thus these directly constructed plans implement the exact nonnegative-price demand optimum, without solving an implicit placement problem or querying a supplied valuation oracle.

4. The reduced linear program

Write the supplied region as

\(\displaystyle P=\{x\geq0:Ax\leq c\},\)

where the rows of \(A\) are the descendant-leaf incidence vectors and the individual upper-bound rows. The matrix is nonnegative and has at most \(R=m+n\) rows.

Introduce masses \(y_{hl}\) for \(l\in J_h\), together with rejection masses \(y_{h\bot}\). Solve the rational linear program with objective

\(\displaystyle \max\sum_h\sum_{l\in J_h}y_{hl}w_{hl},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle y_{h\bot}+\sum_{l\in J_h}y_{hl}=\mu_h\quad\text{for every }h,\)

\(\displaystyle x=\sum_h\sum_{l\in J_h}y_{hl}T_he_l,\qquad Ax\leq c,\)

and nonnegativity of all masses.

It is feasible by rejecting every request. Its feasible mass vectors are bounded because each type’s masses sum to \(\mu_h\). Therefore an optimum exists.

Its dual has variables \(\lambda_h\geq0\) and \(\gamma\geq0\), objective

\(\displaystyle \min\sum_h\mu_h\lambda_h+c\cdot\gamma,\)

and constraints

\(\displaystyle \lambda_h+T_h(A^\mathsf T\gamma)_l\geq w_{hl}\quad(l\in J_h).\)

The condition \(\lambda_h\geq0\) is the rejection-column constraint. Define resource prices by

\(\displaystyle p=A^\mathsf T\gamma\geq0.\)

Exactness for all implicit plans

Take optimal primal and dual solutions of this reduced program. Its dual constraints and the demand lemma imply

\(\displaystyle \lambda_h\geq U_h(p)\geq w_{hq}-p\cdot a_{hq}\)

for every implicit feasible whole plan \(q\), as well as rejection.

Consequently, any feasible assignment using arbitrary implicit plans has welfare at most

\(\displaystyle \sum_{h,q}y_{hq}w_{hq}\leq\sum_h\mu_h\lambda_h+p\cdot x\leq\sum_h\mu_h\lambda_h+\gamma\cdot c.\)

The last inequality uses \(Ax\leq c\) and \(p=A^\mathsf T\gamma\).

By strong LP duality, this upper bound equals the reduced assignment’s welfare. Since that assignment is also feasible for the full problem, it is an exact welfare optimum for the full implicit plan set.

5. Supporting Walrasian prices

Complementary slackness gives, for every positive-mass selected plan,

\(\displaystyle w_{hq}-p\cdot a_{hq}=\lambda_h.\)

All implicit plan utilities are at most \(\lambda_h\), as established above. Thus every positive-mass plan maximizes its type’s utility over the entire implicit menu, including rejection.

Supply complementary slackness gives

\(\displaystyle \gamma_i\bigl(c_i-(Ax)_i\bigr)=0\quad\text{for every supply row }i.\)

Therefore, for every \(z\in P\),

\(\displaystyle p\cdot z=\gamma\cdot Az\leq\gamma\cdot c=\gamma\cdot Ax=p\cdot x.\)

Hence \(x\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{z\in P}p\cdot z\). The computed assignment and prices satisfy both specified Walrasian conditions.

Indeed, supporting prices always exist in this mass-splitting model, even without GS: applying the same finite primal–dual argument to all whole-plan columns proves existence. GS supplies the polynomial reduction; unrestricted splitting supplies the convexity needed for equilibrium existence. Thus valid instances never require a nonexistence output.

6. Bit complexity and output size

There are at most \(\tau(n+1)\) reduced plan variables, including rejection, and at most \(\tau+m+n\) equality or inequality constraints apart from nonnegativity.

Checking the single-unit plans requires polynomially many rational operations: scan stage restrictions and request edges for each execution unit. All totals, plan values, and LP coefficients have bit lengths polynomial in the original input length. Standard exact rational linear programming therefore computes optimal primal and dual solutions in

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{poly}(m,d,n,s,\tau,L)\)

bit operations.

Choose an optimal basic feasible mass assignment. After introducing supply slacks, there are at most \(\tau+m+n\) constraint rows, so at most \(\tau+m+n\) plan masses are nonzero. Each selected plan is explicitly represented by assigning its type’s stages to its selected unit. The masses and prices have polynomial bit length by the standard determinant bounds for rational LP solutions.

No enumeration of possible request types, implicit plans, or indivisible resource copies is required. In particular, \(\tau\) counts only supplied positive-mass types.

7. Relation to the cited results

The revised [Agentic Service Markets result](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05614v3) explicitly assumes per-agent value oracles for its stated polynomial algorithm. The proof here implements the needed priced optimization directly from the supplied whole-plan model.

The conclusion depends on imposing GS on the actual induced valuation. GS of an encapsulated catalogue alone would not establish the lemma unless the encapsulation preserves the stated whole-plan feasibility and values exactly.
