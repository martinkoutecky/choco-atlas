The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite utility types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\), item types \(i\in[m]\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_i\), fixed finite satiation bounds \(B_i\), and marginal utilities \(v_{\theta i1}\ge\cdots\ge v_{\theta iB_i}\ge0\), define \(u_\theta(z)=\sum_i\sum_{j=1}^{z_i}v_{\theta ij}\) for \(z\in\mathcal Z:=\prod_i\{0,\ldots,B_i\}\). Choose mass variables \(x_{\theta,z}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_zx_{\theta,z}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,z}z_i x_{\theta,z}\le q_i\), maximize \(W_\infty(x)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{\theta,z}x_{\theta,z}\log u_\theta(z)\right)\), with \(\log 0=-\infty\), and output a feasible allocation with \(W_\infty\ge\mathrm{OPT}_\infty/2\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\ge1\) utility types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) item types, nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_i\), and finite positive integer satiation bounds \(B_i\), given as part of the input. All marginal utilities are listed explicitly as binary rationals satisfying \(v_{\theta i1}\ge\cdots\ge v_{\theta iB_i}\ge0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For \(\mathcal Z=\prod_{i=1}^m\{0,\ldots,B_i\}\), define \(u_\theta(z)=\sum_{i=1}^m\sum_{j=1}^{z_i}v_{\theta ij}\). An allocation assigns nonnegative real masses \(x_{\theta,z}\) to integer bundles, with \(\sum_zx_{\theta,z}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,z}z_i x_{\theta,z}\le q_i\) for every \(i\); unused supply is allowed.
Define \(W_\infty(x)=\exp(\sum_{\theta,z:x_{\theta,z}>0}x_{\theta,z}\log u_\theta(z))\), using \(\log0=-\infty\) and \(\exp(-\infty)=0\), and let \(\mathrm{OPT}_\infty\) be its maximum over feasible allocations. Outputs represent allocations by listing their nonzero binary-rational masses and bundles.

Does this problem admit an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,\sum_i B_i,L)\) that outputs a feasible allocation with \(W_\infty(x)\ge\mathrm{OPT}_\infty/2\), or is achieving this factor \(2\) guarantee NP-hard?

For finitely many individual agents with nonnegative separable, piecewise-linear concave utilities and integer item supplies, a polynomial-time factor \(2\) approximation to Nash social welfare is known ([Anari, Mai, Oveis Gharan, and Vazirani](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.05191v3)).
No result settling this precise mass-allocation approximation question was verified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Supplies were not explicitly assigned a sign; require nonnegative rational supplies, allowing zero supply and unused items.
  • Specify nonempty societies and item-type sets, and positive integer satiation bounds fixed within each instance but supplied as input; marginal-utility tables are explicit.
  • Marginal utilities were not explicitly required to be rational; require binary-rational utilities and define L as total input bit length.
  • Resolve zero-mass terms by omitting them from the logarithmic sum; any positive mass receiving zero utility makes welfare zero, and zero-optimum instances remain included.
  • Specify sparse binary-rational output allocations so polynomial running time does not require enumerating every possible bundle.

Result

The mass-allocation problem admits a polynomial-time factor $2$ approximation.

In fact, for every rational accuracy parameter $0<\delta\le1$, the construction below outputs a feasible allocation $x$ satisfying
$$ W_\infty(x)\ge e^{-\delta}\mathrm{OPT}_\infty $$
in time polynomial in $m,\tau,\sum_i B_i,L$, and $1/\delta$.

The construction also produces dyadic masses when the input masses and supplies are dyadic. Thus it works whether “binary-rational” means dyadic rational or rational encoded in binary.

1. Positive welfare and the logarithmic objective

Write
$$ N=\sum_{i=1}^m B_i, \qquad V_{\theta i}(k)=\sum_{j=1}^k v_{\theta ij}, \qquad U_\theta=\sum_{i=1}^m V_{\theta i}(B_i). $$
Let
$$ \mathcal Z_\theta^+=\{z\in\mathcal Z:u_\theta(z)>0\}. $$

An allocation has positive welfare exactly when all its nonzero masses are assigned to bundles in the corresponding sets $\mathcal Z_\theta^+$. On such allocations, define
$$ F(x)=\sum_{\theta}\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_\theta^+} x_{\theta,z}\log u_\theta(z). $$
Then $W_\infty(x)=e^{F(x)}$.

If no feasible allocation uses only positive-utility bundles, every feasible allocation has welfare zero. In this case, assigning mass $\mu_\theta$ to the zero bundle for each type solves the approximation problem.

Otherwise, the feasible allocations using only positive-utility bundles form a nonempty compact polytope: there are finitely many bundles, every mass lies between zero and its type mass, and all constraints are closed. The function $F$ is linear with finite coefficients on this polytope. Consequently, it attains a maximum. Denote an optimizer by $x^*$ and its objective by $F^*$, so that
$$ \mathrm{OPT}_\infty=e^{F^*}. $$

The algorithm below detects which of these two cases holds.

2. A polynomial-size set of rounded utility states

If $U_\theta=0$ for any type $\theta$, that type always receives zero utility. Because $\mu_\theta>0$, this implies $\mathrm{OPT}_\infty=0$. Henceforth suppose every $U_\theta>0$.

For each type, let $a_\theta$ be the smallest strictly positive marginal utility listed for that type. Every positive-utility bundle satisfies
$$ a_\theta\le u_\theta(z)\le U_\theta. $$

Fix $0<\delta\le1$ and put
$$ t=1+\frac{\delta}{3m}. $$
For each type, construct the rational grid
$$ g_{\theta h}=a_\theta t^h, \qquad h=0,\ldots,H_\theta, $$
where $H_\theta$ is the smallest nonnegative integer satisfying
$$ g_{\theta H_\theta}\ge U_\theta. $$

Define downward rounding by
$$ \rho_\theta(0)=0, $$
and, for $a_\theta\le y\le U_\theta$, let $\rho_\theta(y)$ be the largest grid value not exceeding $y$. It satisfies
$$ \frac{y}{t}\le\rho_\theta(y)\le y. $$

The grid has polynomial size. Indeed, for $0<s\le1$,
$$ \log(1+s)=\int_0^s\frac{dr}{1+r}\ge\frac{s}{2}. $$
Applying this with $s=\delta/(3m)$ gives
$$ H_\theta\le 1+\frac{6m}{\delta}\log\frac{U_\theta}{a_\theta}. $$
The numbers $a_\theta$ and $U_\theta$ have polynomial binary encoding length, so $\log(U_\theta/a_\theta)$ is polynomially bounded in the input length. All grid values also have polynomial encoding length: they are rational powers of a rational number, with polynomially bounded exponents.

For a bundle $z$, recursively define its rounded partial utilities by
$$ r_{\theta,0}(z)=0, \qquad r_{\theta,i}(z)= \rho_\theta\!\left( r_{\theta,i-1}(z)+V_{\theta i}(z_i) \right). $$

This recursion is well-defined. Every argument is at most $U_\theta$. A positive argument is at least $a_\theta$: either its previous state is positive, or the newly added prefix utility is positive.

Let the actual partial utility be
$$ S_{\theta,i}(z)=\sum_{\ell=1}^iV_{\theta\ell}(z_\ell). $$
Induction gives
$$ t^{-i}S_{\theta,i}(z) \le r_{\theta,i}(z) \le S_{\theta,i}(z). $$
For the lower bound, assuming the claim at stage $i-1$,
$$ \begin{aligned} r_{\theta,i}(z) &\ge \frac{r_{\theta,i-1}(z)+V_{\theta i}(z_i)}{t}\\ &\ge t^{-i}S_{\theta,i-1}(z)+t^{-1}V_{\theta i}(z_i)\\ &\ge t^{-i}S_{\theta,i}(z). \end{aligned} $$
The upper bound follows from downward rounding and the induction hypothesis.

In particular, rounding preserves whether utility is positive. For every positive-utility bundle,
$$ \log u_\theta(z)-m\log t \le \log r_{\theta,m}(z) \le \log u_\theta(z). $$
Since $\log(1+s)\le s$,
$$ m\log t\le\frac{\delta}{3}. $$

3. Representing bundle distributions by flows

For each type $\theta$, construct a layered directed acyclic multigraph.

Its states at each layer are zero and the grid values. Its source is state zero at layer zero. At layer $i-1$, from state $r$, add an edge for each choice
$$ k\in\{0,\ldots,B_i\} $$
to state
$$ r'=\rho_\theta\bigl(r+V_{\theta i}(k)\bigr) $$
at layer $i$, whenever this expression is defined. Label that edge by item count $k$.

Add an edge from each positive state at layer $m$ to a sink. There is no sink edge from state zero. Unreachable states may be discarded.

Every bundle corresponds to its sequence of item-count choices. It gives a source-to-sink path exactly when its utility is positive. Conversely, every source-to-sink path gives a positive-utility bundle. If its terminal state is $r$, then
$$ \log r\le\log u_\theta(z)\le\log r+m\log t. $$

The number of edges is bounded by
$$ O\!\left( \sum_\theta (H_\theta+2) \sum_i(B_i+1) \right), $$
which is polynomial in the required parameters and $1/\delta$.

Let $f_{\theta,e}$ be the mass flowing along an edge. Require flow conservation, source outflow $\mu_\theta$, and nonnegative flows. Couple the type graphs by the supply constraints
$$ \sum_\theta \sum_{\substack{e\text{ at item layer }i}} k(e)f_{\theta,e} \le q_i. $$

These constraints preserve supplies exactly. Path decomposition converts a feasible flow into an allocation, and the consumption of item $i$ is precisely the expression above. Conversely, any allocation using only positive-utility bundles induces a feasible flow.

It follows that this flow system is feasible exactly when positive welfare is feasible.

4. Optimizing with rational coefficients

For each positive terminal state $r$, compute a rational number $c_{\theta,r}$ satisfying
$$ |c_{\theta,r}-\log r|\le\eta, \qquad \eta=\frac{\delta}{6}. $$
A polynomial-time construction of these numbers is given in Section 7.

Maximize the rational linear objective
$$ C(f)= \sum_{\theta,r>0} c_{\theta,r}f_{\theta,r\to\mathrm{sink}} $$
over the flow system.

The computational theorem used here is the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: an explicitly represented rational linear program can be tested for feasibility, and a bounded feasible program can be optimized exactly with a polynomial-size rational output, in time polynomial in its encoding length.

Its hypotheses hold. The graph and all coefficients have polynomial encoding length. Every feasible edge flow lies between zero and its type mass, so the feasible set is bounded.

If the system is infeasible, return the zero-bundle allocation. Section 3 proves that its welfare, zero, equals $\mathrm{OPT}_\infty$.

Otherwise let $f^A$ be an optimal flow. Define its true rounded objective by
$$ G(f)= \sum_{\theta,r>0} f_{\theta,r\to\mathrm{sink}}\log r. $$
Total sink flow is $\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1$, so
$$ |C(f)-G(f)|\le\eta. $$

Let $f^*$ be the flow induced by $x^*$. The rounding bound gives
$$ G(f^*)\ge F^*-\frac{\delta}{3}. $$
Optimality of $f^A$ therefore implies
$$ \begin{aligned} G(f^A) &\ge C(f^A)-\eta\\ &\ge C(f^*)-\eta\\ &\ge G(f^*)-2\eta\\ &\ge F^*-\frac{2\delta}{3}. \end{aligned} $$

Decompose $f^A$ into source-to-sink paths by repeatedly removing the minimum flow along a positive-flow path. Each removal zeros at least one edge, so the number $K$ of paths is at most the total number of graph edges.

Let $\alpha_\ell$ be the resulting path masses, and let each path specify its type and bundle. This gives a feasible rational allocation $x^A$ with polynomially many entries. Because actual utility is at least rounded terminal utility,
$$ F(x^A)\ge G(f^A)\ge F^*-\frac{2\delta}{3}. $$

The next step ensures the desired output encoding, including dyadic output for dyadic input.

5. Rounding masses and restoring feasibility

Choose an integer $R\ge1$ satisfying
$$ \log\frac{U_\theta}{a_\theta}\le R \qquad\text{for every }\theta. $$
Such an $R$ of polynomial magnitude can be computed using rational comparisons with powers of two. For example, take the maximum, over types, of the least positive integer $R_\theta$ satisfying
$$ \frac{U_\theta}{a_\theta}\le2^{R_\theta}. $$
Then $\log(U_\theta/a_\theta)\le R_\theta$ because $\log2<1$.

Write the input masses and supplies as rational fractions. Let $D$ be the product of their denominators. Its binary encoding length is at most the sum of the input denominator lengths, hence polynomial. Every $\mu_\theta$ and $q_i$ is an integer multiple of $1/D$. If these inputs are dyadic, $D$ is a power of two.

Choose $P$ so that
$$ 2^P\ge\frac{3KR}{\delta}, \qquad s=\frac{1}{D2^P}. $$
Round each path mass down:
$$ \alpha'_\ell = s\left\lfloor\frac{\alpha_\ell}{s}\right\rfloor. $$

For each type, let its missing mass be
$$ d_\theta= \mu_\theta- \sum_{\ell:\,\theta(\ell)=\theta}\alpha'_\ell. $$
Let the remaining supply after retaining these rounded masses be
$$ q'_i= q_i-\sum_\ell\alpha'_\ell z_i(\ell). $$
Both quantities are nonnegative multiples of $s$.

We restore the missing masses using singleton bundles. Construct a bipartite flow network:

  • The source-to-type edge has capacity $d_\theta$.
  • A type-to-item edge is present when $v_{\theta i1}>0$, with capacity $1$.
  • The item-to-sink edge has capacity $q'_i$.

A flow saturating all source-to-type edges exists. To prove this, consider the removed mass
$$ \beta_\ell=\alpha_\ell-\alpha'_\ell $$
from each path. Its bundle has positive utility. Hence it contains an item type $i$ with $z_i(\ell)\ge1$ and $v_{\theta(\ell)i1}>0$; the latter follows from the nonincreasing marginal utilities.

Replace that removed mass by the corresponding singleton bundle. This supplies exactly $d_\theta$ to each type. Its consumption of item $i$ is at most the consumption of item $i$ in the removed bundles, which is at most $q'_i$. These replacements witness a feasible saturating flow.

All network capacities are multiples of $s$. Scaling by $1/s$ gives integer capacities. A polynomial-time integral maximum-flow algorithm therefore produces a saturating flow whose unscaled values $y_{\theta i}$ are multiples of $s$.

Define the final allocation $\widehat x$ by retaining the masses $\alpha'_\ell$ and adding mass $y_{\theta i}$ on the singleton bundle of item $i$.

This allocation is feasible, gives everyone positive utility, and has polynomially many entries. Its masses are rational multiples of $s$, and are dyadic whenever the input masses and supplies are dyadic.

6. Welfare guarantee

The total removed mass satisfies
$$ d:=\sum_\theta d_\theta = \sum_\ell(\alpha_\ell-\alpha'_\ell) \le Ks \le K2^{-P}. $$
Thus
$$ dR\le\frac{\delta}{3}. $$

Every original bundle of type $\theta$ has utility at most $U_\theta$. Every positive singleton assigned to that type has utility at least $a_\theta$. Therefore
$$ \begin{aligned} F(\widehat x)-F(x^A) &= -\sum_\ell\beta_\ell\log u_{\theta(\ell)}(z(\ell)) +\sum_{\theta,i}y_{\theta i}\log v_{\theta i1}\\ &\ge -\sum_\theta d_\theta\log U_\theta +\sum_\theta d_\theta\log a_\theta\\ &= -\sum_\theta d_\theta \log\frac{U_\theta}{a_\theta}\\ &\ge-dR\\ &\ge-\frac{\delta}{3}. \end{aligned} $$
Combining this with Section 4,
$$ F(\widehat x)\ge F^*-\delta. $$
Exponentiating gives
$$ W_\infty(\widehat x) \ge e^{-\delta}\mathrm{OPT}_\infty. $$

For the requested guarantee, set $\delta=1/2$. Since
$$ \log2=\int_1^2\frac{dr}{r}>\frac12, $$
we obtain
$$ W_\infty(\widehat x) \ge e^{-1/2}\mathrm{OPT}_\infty > \frac{\mathrm{OPT}_\infty}{2} $$
whenever the optimum is positive. The zero-optimum case was already handled exactly.

7. Computing the logarithmic coefficients

For completeness, rational logarithms can be approximated here using only polynomial-time rational arithmetic.

Given a positive rational $g$, find an integer $e$ such that
$$ h=g2^{-e}\in[1,2). $$
The magnitude of $e$ is bounded by the binary encoding length of $g$, and it can be found by rational comparisons with powers of two.

Put
$$ w=\frac{h-1}{h+1}\in[0,1/3]. $$
Integrating the geometric series gives
$$ \log h = 2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{2j+1}}{2j+1}. $$
For the rational truncation
$$ S_n(w)=2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{w^{2j+1}}{2j+1}, $$
the remainder satisfies
$$ 0\le\log h-S_n(w) \le \frac{2w^{2n+1}}{1-w^2} \le \frac94\,3^{-(2n+1)}. $$
The same formula with $w=1/3$ approximates $\log2$.

Consequently,
$$ c=eS_n(1/3)+S_n(w) $$
satisfies
$$ |c-\log g| \le (|e|+1)\frac94\,3^{-(2n+1)}. $$
Choosing $n$ polynomially bounded in $\log(|e|+1)$ and $\log(1/\eta)$ makes this error at most $\eta$. The truncations have polynomial rational encoding length and are computable in polynomial time.

8. Running time and conclusion

The grids have polynomially many states. The layered graphs, rational linear program, and singleton flow network all have polynomial encoding length. Path decomposition and mass conversion also take polynomial time, and the final allocation has polynomially many explicitly listed bundles and masses.

For fixed $\delta=1/2$, the running time is therefore polynomial in
$$ m,\quad \tau,\quad \sum_i B_i,\quad L. $$

The support size is used directly throughout; no population expansion is required. The target question is settled by the polynomial-time algorithm above.
