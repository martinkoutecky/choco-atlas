The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu\) on \(\tau\) interchangeable types and a bounded stochastic oracle that returns an independent reward sample for each queried coalition mass \(z\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\), with \(F(0)=0\), known \(F(\mu)\), and feasible mass-scale strong supermodularity \(F(z+he_i+ke_j)-F(z+he_i)-F(z+ke_j)+F(z)\ge\kappa hk\), can an adaptive algorithm return, with probability at least \(1-\delta\), a type-symmetric allocation \(b\) satisfying \(\sum_t b_t=F(\mu)\) and \(\sum_t q_tb_t\ge F(\mu\odot q)\) for every \(q\in[0,1]^\tau\), using polynomially many samples and polynomial computation in \(\tau\), the input bit length, \(\kappa^{-1}\), and \(\log(\delta^{-1})\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; there is no separate ground-set parameter \(m\).
Coalitions are mass vectors in \(D=\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\); agents of the same type are interchangeable. An unknown function \(F:D\to[0,1]\) satisfies \(F(0)=0\), and the exact binary rational \(F(\mu)\) is supplied.
At each rational query \(z\in D\), an oracle draws a reward in \([0,1]\) with mean \(F(z)\), independently of the past conditional on the query. For requested precision \(p\), it returns a dyadic approximation with absolute error at most \(2^{-p}\); reading the reply costs polynomial time in \(p\).
A supplied binary rational \(\kappa>0\) guarantees \(F(z+he_i+ke_j)-F(z+he_i)-F(z+ke_j)+F(z)\ge\kappa hk\) for every \(i,j\), including \(i=j\), and all positive real \(h,k\) for which the four arguments lie in \(D\); \(e_i\) is the coordinate unit vector.
A type-symmetric allocation is a rational vector \(b\), where \(b_t\) is the total payment to type \(t\), distributed uniformly at rate \(b_t/\mu_t\). It belongs to the expected core exactly when \(\sum_t b_t=F(\mu)\) and \(\sum_t q_tb_t\ge F(\mu\odot q)\) for every real \(q\in[0,1]^\tau\), where \(\odot\) denotes coordinatewise multiplication.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including a supplied rational \(\delta\in(0,1)\).

Is exact type-symmetric expected-core allocation learnable with probability at least \(1-\delta\), using \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,\kappa^{-1},\log(\delta^{-1}))\) samples and computation, or is this allocation search problem NP-hard?

For finite societies with bounded independent rewards, known grand-coalition expectation, and marginal increases of at least \(\varsigma>0\) under every proper coalition nesting, an algorithm returns an exact expected-core point with probability at least \(1-\delta\), using \(O(n^{15}\varsigma^{-4}\log(n\delta^{-1}\varsigma^{-1}))\) samples and polynomial computation. [Discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07067v3).
No result settling this precise mass-and-sampling problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one because the society is a distribution over types; all listed masses are strictly positive.
  • Fixed the reward bound to [0,1], matching the discrete reward model, and required the known grand-coalition expectation to be a binary rational.
  • Included equal type indices in the strong-supermodularity condition: two distinct discrete agents can share a type, so two disjoint positive pieces of that type must also be covered. Quantified over all feasible positive real increments.
  • Interpreted b_t as the total type payment, rather than payment per unit mass, as dictated by the stated efficiency and coalition inequalities; required a finitely encoded rational output.
  • Specified rational oracle queries and arbitrary requested dyadic sample precision, charging for reply precision, to make polynomial computation meaningful without assuming exact real arithmetic.
  • Restricted the failure probability to (0,1), and defined L as the total binary input length.
  • Read discrete strictness as applying only to proper coalition nesting: the displayed definition otherwise contradicts itself when the two coalitions coincide; the latest available arXiv version retains this slip.

Exact core allocation: statistical impossibility and a succinct-sampler hardness reduction

1. Proven results and scope

The requested polynomial learning guarantee is impossible in the stated oracle model. Even with two supported types and fixed curvature, some instances require exponentially many samples in the binary input length.

There is also a new computational hardness result: when the reward oracle is represented by a succinct polynomial-time sampler, every exact core allocation can encode the number of satisfying assignments of a Boolean formula. I give an explicit reduction below. In particular, this representation of the allocation problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard.

These statements concern different access models. The original problem supplies sampling access without a finite representation of the unknown function. Ordinary finite-input $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness therefore needs a representation convention. The hardness theorem below uses an explicitly defined succinct-sampler convention; the statistical impossibility theorem applies directly to the original formulation.

For $\tau=1$, the allocation $b_1=F(\mu)$ is valid, as follows from the separate-convexity argument in Section 5. The obstruction already appears at $\tau=2$.

2. A two-type family and its exact cores

Let $\varepsilon$ be a rational number with $0<\varepsilon\le 1/8$, and set
$$ \mu=(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon), \qquad \kappa=\frac14. $$
For $z\in D$, define
$$ u=\frac{z_1}{\varepsilon}, \qquad v=\frac{z_2}{1-\varepsilon}. $$
Thus $u,v\in[0,1]$.

For a rational parameter $a\in[1/4,3/8]$, consider
$$ F_a(z) = a u+\left(\frac12-a\right)v +\frac18(z_1+z_2)^2. $$

Verification of all functional promises

Both linear coefficients are nonnegative and sum to $1/2$. Since $z_1+z_2\le1$,
$$ 0\le F_a(z)\le\frac58. $$
Moreover,
$$ F_a(0)=0, \qquad F_a(\mu)=\frac58. $$
The supplied grand-coalition value is therefore independent of $a$.

Write $s=z_1+z_2$. For every permitted choice of $i,j,h,k$, including $i=j$, the second difference is
$$ \begin{aligned} &F_a(z+he_i+ke_j)-F_a(z+he_i)-F_a(z+ke_j)+F_a(z)\\ &\qquad= \frac18\bigl((s+h+k)^2-(s+h)^2-(s+k)^2+s^2\bigr)\\ &\qquad=\frac14hk. \end{aligned} $$
Thus the curvature promise holds with equality.

Exact characterization of the core

The two singleton corner values are
$$ F_a(\varepsilon,0)=a+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}, $$
and
$$ F_a(0,1-\varepsilon) = \frac12-a+\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^2}{8}. $$
Efficiency and the singleton constraints imply
$$ b_2=\frac58-b_1, \qquad b_1\in I_a, $$
where
$$ I_a= \left[ a+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}, \; a+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{8} \right]. $$

These conditions are sufficient for every real coalition fraction. Indeed, $F_a$ is convex in each coordinate separately. Applying the chord inequality in both coordinates gives
$$ \begin{aligned} F_a(\mu\odot(u,v)) \le{}& u(1-v)F_a(\varepsilon,0)\\ &+(1-u)vF_a(0,1-\varepsilon) +uvF_a(\mu). \end{aligned} $$
The omitted empty-corner term is zero. Substituting the singleton constraints and efficiency bounds this expression by
$$ u(1-v)b_1+(1-u)vb_2+uv(b_1+b_2) = ub_1+vb_2. $$

Consequently, the exact continuum core is precisely
$$ \left\{ \left(b_1,\frac58-b_1\right):b_1\in I_a \right\}. $$
Its interval width is
$$ |I_a|=\frac{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}4>0. $$
All endpoints are rational, so rational valid allocations exist.

3. Unconditional exponential sample lower bound

Fix an integer $n\ge3$, and choose
$$ \varepsilon=2^{-n}, \qquad a_0=\frac14, \qquad a_1=\frac14+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. $$
These two environments have exactly the same supplied input: the same masses, support size, curvature, and grand-coalition value.

Their cores are disjoint, because
$$ \inf I_{a_1}-\sup I_{a_0} = \frac{\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon)}4>0. $$

For each environment, use an oracle returning independent Bernoulli rewards with mean $F_{a_r}(z)$. The replies are exactly zero or one, so they satisfy every requested dyadic precision with zero error. Precision requests provide no additional information.

Information obtained from one query

At a query $z$, write
$$ p_r=F_{a_r}(z). $$
Then
$$ p_1-p_0=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(u-v). $$
Because $a_1\le5/16$,
$$ p_1\ge\frac{3}{16}(u+v), \qquad 1-p_1\ge\frac38. $$

For $0<q<1$, Bernoulli relative entropy satisfies
$$ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\operatorname{Ber}(p)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(q)) \le \frac{(p-q)^2}{q(1-q)}. $$
To verify this inequality, apply $\log x\le x-1$ to both terms:
$$ \begin{aligned} &p\log\frac pq+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}\\ &\qquad\le p\left(\frac pq-1\right) +(1-p)\left(\frac{1-p}{1-q}-1\right) = \frac{(p-q)^2}{q(1-q)}. \end{aligned} $$

If $u+v>0$, applying this bound gives
$$ \begin{aligned} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\operatorname{Ber}(p_0)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(p_1)) &\le \frac{32}{9}\varepsilon^2 \frac{(u-v)^2}{u+v}\\ &\le \frac{32}{9}\varepsilon^2(u+v)\\ &\le \frac{64}{9}\varepsilon^2 <8\varepsilon^2. \end{aligned} $$
Here $|u-v|\le u+v$. At $u=v=0$, both rewards are deterministically zero, so the divergence is zero.

Thus the bound $8\varepsilon^2$ holds uniformly over every permitted query.

Adaptive queries and successful output

Consider any randomized algorithm using at most $T$ samples. Pad early termination with queries at zero. Let $P_r$ be its transcript law in environment $r$, including its internal randomness and output.

The algorithm’s internal randomness has the same distribution in both environments. Conditional on that randomness and a common history, its next query is the same in both environments. The relative-entropy chain rule therefore yields
$$ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_0\Vert P_1)\le8T\varepsilon^2. $$
Adding the output cannot increase relative entropy, since the output is determined by the algorithm’s randomness and observations.

Suppose the algorithm succeeds with probability at least $1-\delta$ in each environment, where $0<\delta<1/2$. Choose a threshold strictly between the two core intervals, and let $E$ be the event that the output’s first payment lies below that threshold. Then
$$ P_0(E)\ge1-\delta, \qquad P_1(E)\le\delta. $$

Coarsening a probability distribution to an event and its complement cannot increase relative entropy. Hence
$$ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_0\Vert P_1) \ge d(P_0(E)\Vert P_1(E)), $$
where
$$ d(x\Vert y) = x\log\frac xy+(1-x)\log\frac{1-x}{1-y}. $$
We use the extended-value conventions: a term with zero numerator is zero, and a term with positive numerator and zero denominator is $+\infty$.

For $0<y<x<1$,
$$ \frac{\partial d}{\partial x} = \log\frac{x(1-y)}{y(1-x)}>0, \qquad \frac{\partial d}{\partial y} = \frac{y-x}{y(1-y)}<0. $$
The resulting monotonicity bound extends to boundary probabilities by limits. Consequently,
$$ d(P_0(E)\Vert P_1(E)) \ge d(1-\delta\Vert\delta) = (1-2\delta)\log\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}. $$
Combining the bounds proves
$$ T\ge \frac{(1-2\delta)\log((1-\delta)/\delta)} {8\varepsilon^2}. $$

In particular, for the fixed supplied confidence parameter $\delta=1/4$,
$$ T\ge\frac{\log3}{16}\,4^n. $$
The supplied binary input length is $L=\Theta(n)$, whereas $\tau=2$ and $\kappa^{-1}=4$ are fixed. This contradicts the requested uniform bound
$$ \operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,\kappa^{-1},\log(\delta^{-1})). $$

This impossibility holds regardless of computational power.

4. New result: every exact allocation can recover a satisfying-assignment count

I now specify a computational representation and prove hardness for it.

In the succinct-sampler representation, an instance additionally contains a description of a randomized procedure which, on rational query $z$ and requested precision $p$, implements the permitted reward oracle in time polynomial in its description length, the query length, and $p$. The expected reward function must satisfy all the original promises.

The reduction below uses a fixed sampling procedure with a Boolean formula embedded in its description.

Construction from a Boolean formula

Let $\varphi$ be a Boolean formula on $n\ge1$ variables, and let
$$ N_\varphi = \left|\{x\in\{0,1\}^n:\varphi(x)=1\}\right|. $$
Set
$$ \varepsilon=2^{-(n+4)}, \qquad \mu=(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon), \qquad \kappa=\frac14, \qquad F(\mu)=\frac58. $$
Define the hidden parameter
$$ a_\varphi = \frac14+\frac{N_\varphi}{8\cdot2^n}, $$
and let the expected reward function be $F_{a_\varphi}$ from Section 2.

Since $0\le N_\varphi\le2^n$,
$$ \frac14\le a_\varphi\le\frac38. $$
Every functional promise therefore follows from Section 2.

Crucially, constructing the sampler does not require knowing $N_\varphi$.

A polynomial-time sampler

At each query, draw a fresh uniform assignment
$$ X\in\{0,1\}^n $$
and produce the underlying reward
$$ R_\varphi(z,X) = F_{1/4}(z)+\frac18\varphi(X)(u-v). $$
If $\varphi(X)=0$, this reward equals $F_{1/4}(z)$; if $\varphi(X)=1$, it equals $F_{3/8}(z)$. Thus
$$ 0\le R_\varphi(z,X)\le\frac58. $$

Its mean is
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[R_\varphi(z,X)] &= F_{1/4}(z) +\frac{N_\varphi}{8\cdot2^n}(u-v)\\ &= F_{a_\varphi}(z). \end{aligned} $$
Fresh independent assignments give the required independence conditional on each query, including adaptively selected queries.

For requested precision $p$, return
$$ Y= 2^{-p}\left\lfloor 2^pR_\varphi(z,X)\right\rfloor. $$
This is dyadic and satisfies
$$ |Y-R_\varphi(z,X)|<2^{-p}. $$

For rational $z$, the reward is rational. Evaluating $\varphi(X)$, computing the displayed rational expression, and rounding it require polynomial time in $|\varphi|$, $n$, the query length, and $p$. The sampler description has polynomial length and is constructed deterministically from $\varphi$.

Decoding from any exact core allocation

Let $b$ be any exact type-symmetric core allocation for this instance. Section 2 gives
$$ a_\varphi+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{8} \le b_1 \le a_\varphi+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}. $$
In particular,
$$ 0\le b_1-a_\varphi<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}. $$
Multiplying by $8\cdot2^n$ gives
$$ 0\le 8\cdot2^n(b_1-a_\varphi) < 8\cdot2^n\frac{\varepsilon}{4} = \frac18. $$
Since
$$ 8\cdot2^n\left(a_\varphi-\frac14\right)=N_\varphi, $$
we obtain
$$ N_\varphi \le 8\cdot2^n\left(b_1-\frac14\right) < N_\varphi+\frac18. $$
Therefore every valid output satisfies the exact decoding identity
$$ \boxed{ N_\varphi = \left\lfloor 8\cdot2^n\left(b_1-\frac14\right) \right\rfloor. } $$
The decoder uses rational arithmetic and runs in polynomial time in $n$ and the output length.

This is a polynomial-time reduction from counting satisfying assignments to exact core allocation in the succinct-sampler representation. In particular, deciding whether the decoded integer is positive decides Boolean satisfiability. By the standard $\mathrm{NP}$-completeness of Boolean satisfiability, this representation of exact core allocation is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard.

The construction always has rational core points with polynomial encoding length: for example,
$$ b_1=a_\varphi+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}, \qquad b_2=\frac58-b_1. $$
Thus hardness does not arise from excessive output length or nonexistence of rational solutions.

Consequence for the original sampling model

The same sampler can be used to simulate an instance of the original oracle problem without revealing $\varphi$ to the allocation algorithm.

If the requested polynomial learner existed, construct the above instance, simulate its oracle, and decode its output. With $\delta=1/4$, this would decide satisfiability with probability at least $3/4$ in polynomial time. It would also compute $N_\varphi$ with that probability.

This establishes a precise polynomial-time sampling-oracle reduction. Its consequence for satisfiability is conditional on the existence of a learner; Section 3 already rules that learner out unconditionally.

5. General structure and an exact-allocation upper bound

For completeness, the following results explain why accurate expectation access makes allocation computationally straightforward.

Write
$$ N=[\tau], \qquad \mu_*=\min_i\mu_i, \qquad V(S)=F(\mu\odot\mathbf1_S), $$
and abbreviate
$$ b(S)=\sum_{i\in S}b_i, \qquad \mu(S)=\sum_{i\in S}\mu_i. $$

Separate convexity and reduction to corners

Fix all coordinates except coordinate $i$, and let $f$ be the resulting one-dimensional restriction. For $a<b$ in its domain, the diagonal promise with equal increments $(b-a)/2$ gives
$$ f(a)+f(b)-2f\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right) \ge \frac{\kappa(b-a)^2}{4}. $$
In particular, $f$ is midpoint convex and bounded in $[0,1]$.

Here is an explicit continuity argument. Fix an interior point $x$, and choose $d>0$ such that $[x-d,x+d]$ lies in the domain. For $0<|h|<d$, let $r$ be the largest nonnegative integer satisfying
$$ 2^r|h|\le d. $$
Iterating midpoint convexity yields the dyadic chord inequality, hence
$$ f(x+h) \le (1-2^{-r})f(x)+2^{-r}f(x+2^rh) \le f(x)+2^{-r}. $$
The same bound holds for $f(x-h)$. Midpoint convexity at $x$ then gives
$$ f(x+h)\ge2f(x)-f(x-h)\ge f(x)-2^{-r}. $$
Thus
$$ |f(x+h)-f(x)|\le2^{-r}. $$
As $h\to0$, the maximal admissible $r$ tends to infinity, so the bound tends to zero. This proves interior continuity.

For arbitrary chord endpoints, approximate an interpolation parameter in $(0,1)$ by dyadic parameters. The interpolated points converge to an interior point, where continuity holds. Passing to the limit in the dyadic chord inequalities proves convexity, including chords with boundary endpoints.

Therefore $F$ is separately convex. Repeated interpolation gives, for every real $q\in[0,1]^\tau$,
$$ F(\mu\odot q) \le \sum_{S\subseteq N}w_S(q)V(S), $$
where
$$ w_S(q)= \prod_{i\in S}q_i \prod_{i\notin S}(1-q_i). $$
These weights are nonnegative and satisfy
$$ \sum_{S\subseteq N}w_S(q)=1, \qquad \sum_{S\ni i}w_S(q)=q_i. $$
Consequently, if $b(S)\ge V(S)$ for every corner,
$$ F(\mu\odot q) \le \sum_Sw_S(q)b(S) = \sum_iq_ib_i. $$
The converse follows by taking $q=\mathbf1_S$.

Thus the continuum core constraints are exactly the corner constraints, together with efficiency.

Quantitative slack from two opposite orders

For $A\subseteq B\subseteq N$ and $i\notin B$, telescope the mixed-difference promise over the coordinates in $B\setminus A$. Each application uses increments $\mu_i$ and $\mu_j$ at a corner where neither coordinate is present, so all four arguments lie in $D$. This gives
$$ V(B\cup\{i\})-V(B) \ge V(A\cup\{i\})-V(A) +\kappa\mu_i\mu(B\setminus A). $$

Fix a permutation $\pi$. Let $P_i^\pi$ denote the set of types preceding $i$, and define its marginal vector by
$$ x_i^\pi = V(P_i^\pi\cup\{i\})-V(P_i^\pi). $$
For each $i\in S$, apply the preceding inequality with
$$ A=P_i^\pi\cap S, \qquad B=P_i^\pi. $$
Summing over $i\in S$, the marginals along the order restricted to $S$ telescope to $V(S)$, because $V(\varnothing)=0$. Hence
$$ x^\pi(S)-V(S) \ge \kappa \sum_{\substack{i\in S,\ j\notin S\\ j\text{ precedes }i\text{ in }\pi}} \mu_i\mu_j. $$

Let $\bar\pi$ be the reverse order, and set
$$ c=\frac{x^\pi+x^{\bar\pi}}2. $$
Each pair crossing $S$ and its complement contributes in exactly one of the two orders. Therefore
$$ c(S)-V(S) \ge \frac{\kappa}{2}\mu(S)(1-\mu(S)). $$
Both full marginal sums telescope, so
$$ c(N)=F(\mu). $$

Estimating the two chains preserves exact membership

Assume $\tau\ge2$, and choose
$$ \eta= \min\left\{\frac14,\frac{\kappa\mu_*}{16}\right\}. $$
Estimate the proper prefix values in $\pi$ and $\bar\pi$ to absolute error at most $\eta$. Use the exact values zero and $F(\mu)$ at the empty and grand coalitions. Form the estimated marginal vectors and their average $\widehat c$ using exact rational arithmetic.

Telescoping gives exact efficiency:
$$ \widehat c(N)=F(\mu). $$
Each marginal uses two endpoint values, so
$$ |\widehat c_i-c_i| \le2\eta \le\frac{\kappa\mu_i}{8}. $$
Let $e=\widehat c-c$. Since $e(N)=0$,
$$ |e(S)| \le \frac{\kappa}{8} \min\{\mu(S),1-\mu(S)\}. $$
For a proper nonempty $S$,
$$ \mu(S)(1-\mu(S)) \ge \frac12\min\{\mu(S),1-\mu(S)\}. $$
Combining these inequalities,
$$ \widehat c(S)-V(S) \ge \frac{\kappa}{8} \min\{\mu(S),1-\mu(S)\}>0. $$
The empty and grand constraints hold exactly. By the corner reduction, $\widehat c$ belongs to the exact continuum core.

Explicit concentration and approximation accounting

There are at most
$$ M=2(\tau-1) $$
proper prefix values to estimate. Choose precision $p$ satisfying
$$ 2^{-p}\le\frac{\eta}{2}, $$
and take
$$ K= \left\lceil 2\eta^{-2}\log\frac{2M}{\delta} \right\rceil $$
samples at each value.

The version of Hoeffding’s inequality used here is: for independent random variables $X_1,\ldots,X_K\in[0,1]$,
$$ \Pr\left( \left| \frac1K\sum_{\ell=1}^K(X_\ell-\mathbb EX_\ell) \right|>r \right) \le2e^{-2Kr^2}. $$
At each fixed query, the underlying rewards satisfy these independence, boundedness, and mean hypotheses. With $r=\eta/2$, their average differs from the desired value by at most $\eta/2$, except with probability at most
$$ 2e^{-K\eta^2/2}\le\frac{\delta}{M}. $$

Let $Y_\ell$ be the observed dyadic approximation to $X_\ell$. Regardless of whether the approximation errors are biased or dependent,
$$ \left| \frac1K\sum_\ell Y_\ell-\frac1K\sum_\ell X_\ell \right| \le2^{-p}\le\frac{\eta}{2}. $$
Therefore each observed average has total error at most $\eta$ on the concentration event. A union bound gives simultaneous accuracy with probability at least $1-\delta$.

All estimates and the resulting allocation are rational. The sample bound is
$$ O\left( \tau\max\{1,\kappa^{-2}\mu_*^{-2}\} \log\frac{\tau}{\delta} \right). $$
The query encodings have polynomial length. The reply precision and arithmetic bit lengths are polynomial in $L+\log K$, and the total computation is polynomial in the input length and number of samples.

Consequence for efficient expectation representations

Suppose instead that a finite representation of $F$ allows deterministic dyadic approximation of $F(z)$ to precision $p$ in polynomial time in the representation length, query length, and $p$.

The same construction needs only $2(\tau-1)$ approximate expectation evaluations. Moreover,
$$ \log(\eta^{-1}) = O\left( 1+\log^+(\kappa^{-1})+\log(\mu_*^{-1}) \right) = O(L), $$
because positive binary rationals have logarithmic reciprocals bounded by their encoding lengths.

It follows that an exact rational core point is computable in polynomial time under such an expectation representation. The succinct-sampler hardness theorem therefore concerns extracting sufficiently accurate expectations from sampler descriptions.

6. Relation to the discrete result and remaining scope

For the two-type corner game in Section 2, the strict marginal-increase gap is
$$ V(\{1,2\})-V(\{1\})-V(\{2\})+V(\varnothing) = \frac{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}4. $$
This is exponentially small in its binary mass encoding. The inverse-strictness dependence in the supplied [discrete theorem, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07067v3#S5.Thmtheorem18), therefore permits an exponential sample bound here.

The original model’s polynomial learning guarantee is conclusively disproved. The new reduction additionally establishes $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness for succinct polynomial-time sampler representations, with every valid allocation recovering a satisfying-assignment count. A conventional finite-input hardness classification of the original purely black-box wording still requires an explicit representation or reduction convention.
