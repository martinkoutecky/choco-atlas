Theorem 4.3 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite alternative set \(A\) and a rational mass vector \(\bar\mu\) over rankings, compute an alternative \(x\) and a fractional domination coupling \(z\) with row and column marginals \(\bar\mu\), supported on pairs where ranking \(r\) ranks \(x\) at least as highly as the top alternative of ranking \(s\). Determine and certify the worst-case \(\operatorname{MaxAvg}\) distortion of this group-oblivious rule over all hidden finite type distributions with ranking marginal \(\bar\mu\), and whether a ratio of \(5\) is tight under the chosen group-multiplicity regime.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) contain \(m\ge2\) alternatives. A society is given by its support: a list \(R\) of \(\tau\) strict rankings with positive binary-rational masses \(\bar\mu_r\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the input bit length.
An admissible output is \(x\in A\) and a nonnegative rational coupling \(z\) satisfying \(\sum_s z_{rs}=\bar\mu_r\), \(\sum_r z_{rs}=\bar\mu_s\), and \(z_{rs}=0\) unless \(x\succeq_r\operatorname{top}(s)\), where \(\succeq_r\) means earlier in ranking \(r\), or equal.
A hidden realization has finitely many types \(t\), each with positive rational mass \(p_t\), ranking \(r_t\in R\), location \(v_t\) in a common metric space containing \(A\), and group label \(g_t\). Require \(\sum_{t:r_t=r}p_t=\bar\mu_r\) and \(a\succ_{r_t}b\Rightarrow d(v_t,a)\le d(v_t,b)\); equal distances may be ordered arbitrarily. Locations may coincide.
Allow any finite number \(k\ge2\) of hidden groups, each with positive mass, with no lower bound on group masses. The same ranking may occur at different locations and in different groups.
Define \(C_I(a)=\operatorname{MaxAvg}_I(a)=\max_g\frac{\sum_{t:g_t=g}p_t d(v_t,a)}{\sum_{t:g_t=g}p_t}\) and \(D_x(\bar\mu)=\sup_I C_I(x)/\min_{a\in A}C_I(a)\), over compatible hidden realizations; use \(0/0=1\) and a positive numerator divided by zero as \(+\infty\). A deterministic rule \(M\) sees only \((A,\bar\mu)\), fixes all selection ties deterministically, and has distortion \(\sup_{A,\bar\mu}D_{M(A,\bar\mu)}(\bar\mu)\).

What are the profile-dependent worst-case ratios \(D_x(\bar\mu)\) for admissible outputs, and the sharp uniform distortion guarantee for rules returning them?
Can an admissible \((x,z)\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) with distortion at most \(5\), and is \(5\) optimal among all deterministic group-oblivious ordinal rules under this group regime?

For finite agents, domination-perfect-matching rules have MaxAvg distortion at most \(5\), and every deterministic group-oblivious ordinal rule has distortion at least \(5-\varepsilon\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\), even with two alternatives and equal-sized groups. Results

Every admissible output satisfies \(D_x(\bar\mu)\le 5\). An admissible rational coupling can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and \(5\) is optimal among all deterministic group-oblivious ordinal rules in the stated regime.

The profile-dependent ratios need not equal \(5\). Below is an exact characterization by a one-parameter family of linear programs. In particular, with two alternatives, if \(p\) is the mass preferring an admissible winner \(x\), then

\( \displaystyle D_x(\bar\mu)= \begin{cases} 1+2/p,&\frac12\le p<1,\\ 1,&p=1. \end{cases} \)

All statements below use the permitted convention that equal distances may be ordered arbitrarily.

1. Polynomial-time construction of an admissible output

Define the top-choice mass of candidate \(c\) by

\( \displaystyle q_c=\sum_{s:\operatorname{top}(s)=c}\bar\mu_s. \)

The weighted ranking-matching lemma states that, for arbitrary voter and candidate probability weights, some candidate \(x\) admits a fractional matching supported on edges \(x\succeq_r c\), with the prescribed voter and candidate marginals. This is the existence result established constructively by FractionalVeto. [PluralityVeto, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.07098v2)

Apply it with voter set \(R\), voter weights \(\bar\mu_r\), candidate set \(A\), and candidate weights \(q_c\). These are nonnegative probability weights, as required. Thus some \(x\) admits numbers \(f_{rc}\) satisfying

\( \displaystyle \sum_c f_{rc}=\bar\mu_r,\qquad \sum_r f_{rc}=q_c,\qquad f_{rc}=0\ \text{unless }x\succeq_r c. \)

For every \(s\), the quantity \(q_{\operatorname{top}(s)}\) is positive because \(\bar\mu_s>0\). Set

\( \displaystyle z_{rs} =f_{r,\operatorname{top}(s)} \frac{\bar\mu_s}{q_{\operatorname{top}(s)}}. \)

Summing over rankings with the same top choice gives \(\sum_s z_{rs}=\bar\mu_r\). Summing over \(r\) gives \(\sum_r z_{rs}=\bar\mu_s\). Its support also has the required domination property. Hence an admissible output exists.

To compute one, test candidates in a fixed deterministic order. For each \(x\), construct a flow network with:

  • a left and a right copy of \(R\);
  • source capacities \(\bar\mu_r\) and sink capacities \(\bar\mu_s\);
  • an arc of capacity \(1\) from \(r\) to \(s\) exactly when \(x\succeq_r\operatorname{top}(s)\).

A flow of value \(1\) is precisely an admissible coupling. Existence guarantees that some test succeeds.

There are \(m\) networks, each with \(O(\tau)\) vertices and \(O(\tau^2)\) arcs. Multiplying capacities by the product of the input denominators gives integer capacities whose bit lengths are \(O(L)\). A polynomial-time maximum-flow algorithm therefore yields a rational coupling in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). No expansion into individual voters or enumeration of unsupported rankings is needed.

2. Direct continuous proof of the upper bound

Fix an admissible \((x,z)\) and a compatible hidden realization \(I\). Define the overall average cost

\( \displaystyle B_I(a)=\sum_t p_t d(v_t,a). \)

Because this is a mass-weighted average of the group averages,

\( \displaystyle B_I(a)\le C_I(a). \)

For each supported ranking, define its conditional average distance vector

\( \displaystyle h_r(b)= \frac{1}{\bar\mu_r}\sum_{t:r_t=r}p_t d(v_t,b). \)

Whenever \(z_{rs}>0\), admissibility and ordinal consistency imply

\( \displaystyle h_r(x)\le h_r(\operatorname{top}(s)),\qquad h_s(\operatorname{top}(s))\le h_s(a). \)

The triangle inequality, averaged within the relevant rankings, consequently gives

\( \displaystyle d(x,a) \le h_r(a)+h_r(x) \le 2h_r(a)+d(a,\operatorname{top}(s)) \le 2h_r(a)+2h_s(a). \)

Multiply by \(z_{rs}\) and sum. Both marginals of \(z\) are \(\bar\mu\), so

\( \displaystyle d(x,a)\le 4\sum_r\bar\mu_r h_r(a)=4B_I(a). \)

For every group, its average distance to \(x\) is at most its average distance to \(a\), plus \(d(x,a)\). Therefore

\( \displaystyle C_I(x)\le C_I(a)+d(x,a) \le C_I(a)+4B_I(a) \le 5C_I(a). \)

This holds for every alternative \(a\). If the optimal cost is zero, it also forces \(C_I(x)=0\), giving ratio \(1\) under the specified convention. Otherwise, divide by the optimal cost. Thus \(D_x(\bar\mu)\le 5\).

3. Exact profile-dependent characterization

The following formula eliminates the unknown number of groups, types, and metric-space points.

Distance-extension constraints

Let \(\delta=(\delta_{bc})_{b,c\in A}\) be a candidate pseudometric: it is symmetric and nonnegative, has zero diagonal, and satisfies every triangle inequality.

For ranking \(r\), let \(K_r(\delta)\) consist of vectors \(h\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{A}\) satisfying, for all candidates \(b,c\),

\( \displaystyle |h(b)-h(c)|\le\delta_{bc}\le h(b)+h(c), \)

and

\( \displaystyle b\succ_r c\ \Longrightarrow\ h(b)\le h(c). \)

These are linear inequalities when the absolute-value inequality is written as two inequalities.

They are exactly the conditions needed to extend the candidate pseudometric by a point with candidate distances \(h\) and ranking \(r\). To verify sufficiency, connect the new point to each candidate \(b\) by an edge of length \(h(b)\), retaining the candidate edges of lengths \(\delta_{bc}\), and take shortest-path distances. The sum inequalities prevent shortcuts between candidates through the new point; the difference inequalities prevent shortcuts in its prescribed distances.

The same construction works simultaneously for any finite collection of such vectors. Identifying points at zero distance yields a metric space. When candidate distances are positive, the alternatives remain distinct.

The linear programs

Fix \(a\in A\), \(r,s\in R\), and \(\theta\in[0,1]\). Define \(V_{x,a}^{r,s}(\theta)\) as the supremum of the linear-program objective

\( \displaystyle \theta u(x)+(1-\theta)v(x), \)

with variables \(\delta\), vectors \(h_t\) for \(t\in R\), and vectors \(u,v\), subject to

\( \displaystyle h_t\in K_t(\delta)\quad(t\in R),\qquad u\in K_r(\delta),\qquad v\in K_s(\delta), \)

and

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in R}\bar\mu_t h_t(a)\le1,\qquad \theta u(a)+(1-\theta)v(a)\le1. \)

For fixed \(\theta\), this is an LP with \(O(m^2+\tau m)\) variables and \(O(m^3+\tau m^2)\) constraints.

The exact answer for every admissible \(x\) is

\( \displaystyle \boxed{ D_x(\bar\mu)= \max_{\substack{a\in A\\r,s\in R}} \ \sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}V_{x,a}^{r,s}(\theta). } \)

Thus general profiles admit an exact finite optimization characterization. Polynomial-time evaluation of this formula is not asserted; polynomial-time construction of an admissible output was proved separately.

Proof: every hidden realization is bounded by the formula

Consider a realization with positive optimal cost. Choose an optimal alternative \(a\), and scale all distances so that \(C_I(a)=1\). Choose a group attaining \(C_I(x)\).

The overall conditional ranking vectors \(h_t\) belong to \(K_t(\delta)\), and

\( \displaystyle \sum_t\bar\mu_t h_t(a)=B_I(a)\le1. \)

Within the selected group, average the distance vectors separately for each ranking present. Denote these vectors by \(u_t\), and their conditional masses by \(\lambda_t\). Averaging preserves every inequality defining \(K_t(\delta)\). Moreover,

\( \displaystyle \sum_t\lambda_t=1,\qquad \sum_t\lambda_t u_t(a)\le1,\qquad \sum_t\lambda_t u_t(x)=C_I(x). \)

Hold these distance vectors fixed and maximize \(\sum_t\lambda_t u_t(x)\) over the simplex, subject to \(\sum_t\lambda_t u_t(a)\le1\). An optimum exists because the feasible set is nonempty and compact.

An extreme optimum has at most two positive coordinates. Indeed, with at least three positive coordinates, there is a nonzero perturbation \(w\), supported on them, satisfying both \(\sum_t w_t=0\) and \(\sum_t w_tu_t(a)=0\). Sufficiently small perturbations in both directions remain feasible, contradicting extremality.

Consequently, some rankings \(r,s\) and mixing weight \(\theta\) produce a feasible LP point whose objective is at least \(C_I(x)\), the normalized distortion.

Realizations with zero optimal cost have distortion \(1\) by Section 2. Every LP has value at least \(1\), witnessed by \(\delta_{bc}=0\) and all distance coordinates equal to \(1\). This covers the zero-cost case as well.

Proof: every feasible LP value can be approached by hidden realizations

First consider feasible data with rational coordinates, rational \(\theta\), and positive distances between distinct alternatives. Write

\( \displaystyle T=\theta u(x)+(1-\theta)v(x),\quad M=\theta u(a)+(1-\theta)v(a),\quad B=\sum_t\bar\mu_t h_t(a). \)

Thus \(M\le1\) and \(B\le1\). Define the ranking distribution

\( \displaystyle q_t=\theta\mathbf1_{t=r}+(1-\theta)\mathbf1_{t=s}. \)

For sufficiently small positive rational \(\varepsilon\), construct two groups:

  • The first has mass \(\varepsilon\), with conditional masses \(\theta\) and \(1-\theta\) at the points described by \(u,v\). Omit zero-mass types.
  • The second has, for each ranking \(t\), mass \(\bar\mu_t-\varepsilon q_t>0\) at the point described by \(h_t\).

The extension construction supplies a common metric space. The total mass of every ranking is exactly \(\bar\mu_t\), and all type masses are positive rationals.

The first group has average costs \(M\) at \(a\) and \(T\) at \(x\). The second group’s average cost at \(a\) is

\( \displaystyle \frac{B-\varepsilon\sum_tq_t h_t(a)}{1-\varepsilon} \longrightarrow B. \)

Hence \(C_I(a)\longrightarrow\max\{M,B\}\le1\), while \(C_I(x)\ge T\). Comparing with \(a\), the supremum of the actual distortion is therefore at least \(T\).

It remains to justify allowing real data and candidate pseudometrics in the LP. For any feasible point and \(\eta>0\), replace

\( \displaystyle \delta_{bc}\ \text{by }\delta_{bc}+2\eta\quad(b\ne c), \)

and add \(\eta\) to every distance coordinate. All extension and ranking inequalities remain valid. Dividing all distances by \(1+\eta\) restores both budget inequalities. Distinct candidates now have positive distances, and the objective becomes \((T+\eta)/(1+\eta)\), which tends to \(T\).

Rational points are dense in the joint distance cone: its defining inequalities have rational coefficients, and approximation within the rational linear span of the minimal face preserves them. Approximate these distance data and \(\theta\) by rational values. If either budget slightly exceeds \(1\), divide all distances by the maximum of \(1\) and the two budget values. This factor tends to \(1\), so the objective converges.

Thus rational, genuine-metric instances approach every feasible LP value. This proves the reverse inequality and the exact formula.

In particular, the supremum can be approached with two groups and at most \(\tau+2\) types.

4. Explicit two-alternative ratios

Let \(A=\{x,a\}\), and let \(p\) be the mass ranking \(x\) above \(a\).

Admissibility

A row ranking \(a\) above \(x\) can send mass only to columns whose top choice is \(x\). Its total mass is \(1-p\), whereas those columns have capacity \(p\). Therefore admissibility requires \(1-p\le p\).

Conversely, when \(1/2\le p<1\), the coupling, with rows and columns ordered by \(x\)-first and \(a\)-first rankings, is

\( \displaystyle z= \begin{pmatrix} 2p-1&1-p\\ 1-p&0 \end{pmatrix}. \)

It has the required marginals and support. For \(p=1\), the sole supported ranking couples to itself. Thus \(x\) is admissible exactly when \(p\ge1/2\).

Upper bound

Put \(\Delta=d(x,a)\). Every voter ranking \(x\) above \(a\) satisfies

\( \displaystyle \Delta\le d(v,x)+d(v,a)\le2d(v,a). \)

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle C_I(a)\ge B_I(a)\ge\frac{p\Delta}{2}. \)

Together with \(C_I(x)\le C_I(a)+\Delta\), this bounds the distortion by \(1+2/p\).

Matching lower bound

Suppose \(p<1\). Choose rational \(0<\varepsilon<1-p\), and put

\( \displaystyle t=\frac{p}{2(1-\varepsilon)}. \)

On the real line, place \(x\) at \(0\) and \(a\) at \(1\). Use two groups:

  • The first has mass \(\varepsilon\), ranks \(a\) above \(x\), and is located at \(1+t\).
  • The second contains mass \(p\) at \(1/2\), ranking \(x\) above \(a\), and mass \(1-p-\varepsilon\) at \(1\), ranking \(a\) above \(x\).

The midpoint ranking is compatible because its two distances are equal. Both groups have average cost \(t\) at \(a\). Their average costs at \(x\) are respectively \(1+t\) and \(1-t\). Therefore

\( \displaystyle \frac{C_I(x)}{\min_b C_I(b)} =\frac{1+t}{t} =1+\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)}p. \)

Letting positive rational \(\varepsilon\) tend to zero proves \(D_x=1+2/p\).

If \(p=1\), every voter satisfies \(d(v,x)\le d(v,a)\). This inequality holds within every group, so \(x\) always minimizes the objective and \(D_x=1\).

5. Sharp uniform distortion

Consider the fixed two-alternative profile with mass \(1/2\) on each ranking. Whatever a deterministic ordinal rule selects, name its selected alternative \(x\). The preceding construction applies with \(p=1/2\), giving ratios

\( \displaystyle 5-4\varepsilon \)

for arbitrarily small positive rational \(\varepsilon\). Thus every deterministic group-oblivious ordinal rule has uniform distortion at least \(5\), whether or not it returns admissible outputs.

Section 2 proves the matching upper bound for every rule returning admissible outputs, and Section 1 provides such a polynomial-time rule. Therefore the sharp uniform guarantee is exactly \(5\).
