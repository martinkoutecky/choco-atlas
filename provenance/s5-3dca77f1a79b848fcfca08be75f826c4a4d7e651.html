The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given items \(M\), a finite set \(T\) of rational monotone valuation types \(v_t:2^M\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), consider a measurable population in which each person receives an integral bundle. A feasible aggregate allocation has masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_j\); let \(W^*=\max\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\). For a monotone, loser-independent \(c\)-approximate allocator acting pointwise on reports, define an individual’s critical price by varying a tagged person’s report while holding the rest of the population fixed. If each round applies SIMPLIFY and charges these critical prices, does the average welfare reach at least \(W^*/(c+1)-o(1)\) when almost every individual has vanishing external regret, and what runtime bounds follow from mass-allocation and tagged-price oracles?

Result

The guarantee holds under the stated pointwise strong loser-independence assumption. In fact, there is a finite-horizon bound
\( \displaystyle \frac1H\sum_{r=1}^H W_r\geq \frac{W^*-\mathcal R_H}{c+1}, \)
where \(W_r\) is the true welfare in round \(r\), and \(\mathcal R_H\) is an integrated external-regret term satisfying \(\mathcal R_H\to0\).

The essential step is to prove an integral bound on the singleton-defined tagged prices. This is the atomless analogue of the price bound used in the finite-population argument; the proof below establishes it directly for the prices in this question. See [Lucier–Borodin, Lemma 3.3](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/Papers/equilibria-greedy-CAs.pdf) for the finite analogue.

1. Consequences of the two allocation axioms

All report modifications in this section affect only one individual \(a\); everybody else's reports remain fixed.

Lemma 1: lowering unallocated coordinates

Suppose two admissible reports differ only at a nonempty bundle \(U\), whose reported value has decreased. If the original allocation to \(a\) is different from \(U\), then the entire allocation remains unchanged.

Proof. Write \(d\) for the original profile and \(e\) for the modified profile. If \(A(d)\neq A(e)\), strong loser-independence must have a witness at \(a\), and its bundle must be \(U\), since this is the only changed coordinate. Because \(A_a(d)\neq U\), the witness requires \(A_a(e)=U\).

Increasing this coordinate back to its original value would preserve allocation \(U\), by componentwise monotonicity. This contradicts \(A_a(d)\neq U\). Thus \(A(d)=A(e)\). ∎

Two consequences will be used repeatedly.

  • A losing report stays losing when lowered. If \(f\leq g\) componentwise and \(A_a(g,d_{-a})=\varnothing\), then \(A_a(f,d_{-a})=\varnothing\).
  • A winning report can be extracted. If \(A_a(g,d_{-a})=U\neq\varnothing\), then

\( A_a((U,g(U)),d_{-a})=U. \)

For both consequences, lower the coordinates from \(g\) to the target report in increasing order of cardinality. Every intermediate report is monotone: proper subsets have already reached their target values, while proper supersets retain their original values. Normalization and rationality are also preserved.

For the first consequence, every lowered coordinate is unallocated, so Lemma 1 applies at every step. For the second, monotonicity of \(g\) gives \((U,g(U))\leq g\), and the coordinate \(U\) does not change. Again Lemma 1 applies at every step.

Lemma 2: the tagged winning bids are upward closed

Fix a simplified profile of opponents and a nonempty requested bundle \(S\). If bid \((S,b)\) wins \(S\), then every rational bid \((S,b')\) with \(b'>b\) also wins \(S\).

Proof. On the higher-bid profile, the individual's allocation is either \(S\) or empty. If it were empty, lowering that report to \((S,b)\) would preserve an empty allocation by the first consequence above. This contradicts the assumed win. ∎

Consequently, for the tagged threshold \(\theta_a(S,d_{-a})\):

  • a winning bid \(b\) satisfies \(0\leq\theta_a(S,d_{-a})\leq b\);
  • every rational \(b>\theta_a(S,d_{-a})\) wins;
  • every rational \(b<\theta_a(S,d_{-a})\) loses.

The second statement follows from the definition of infimum and upward closure. No assertion about winning at equality is needed.

For a truthful bid \((S,v_t(S))\), its counterfactual utility is therefore
\( \displaystyle u_a((S,v_t(S)),d_{-a})=\bigl(v_t(S)-\theta_a(S,d_{-a})\bigr)_+. \)

Here an infinite threshold gives utility zero. At equality, either allocation gives utility zero. A zero-valued bid simplifies to the zero action and also gives utility zero.

2. The integral tagged-price bound

Let \(d\) be any simplified measurable profile with finite integrated declared welfare, and let \(y\) be any feasible measurable allocation. Set the price of the empty bundle to zero.

We prove
\( \displaystyle \int_0^1\theta_a(y(a),d_{-a})\,d\lambda(a)\leq c\int_0^1d_a(A_a(d))\,d\lambda(a). \)

Write \(\theta(a)=\theta_a(y(a),d_{-a})\). For each positive integer \(n\), choose a measurable, finitely valued rational function \(b_n\) as follows. If \(y(a)=\varnothing\), set \(b_n(a)=0\). Otherwise, for finite \(\theta(a)\), set
\( \displaystyle b_n(a)=\max\!\left\{0,\min\!\left\{n,\frac{\lceil 2^n\theta(a)\rceil-1}{2^n}\right\}\right\}, \)
and set \(b_n(a)=n\) when \(\theta(a)=+\infty\).

Thus \(b_n(a)\to\theta(a)\), and whenever \(b_n(a)>0\), we have \(b_n(a)<\theta(a)\).

Fix \(n\), abbreviate \(b=b_n\), and define the admissible report profile
\( \displaystyle e_a(T)=\max\{d_a(T),(y(a),b(a))(T)\}. \)

We claim that no changed coordinate can witness strong loser-independence between \(d\) and \(e\).

Indeed, if \(e_a(U)\neq d_a(U)\), then
\( y(a)\subseteq U,\qquad e_a(U)=b(a)>d_a(U),\qquad b(a)>0. \)

There are two possible kinds of witness.

  • If \(A_a(d)=U\), extraction gives a win under \((U,d_a(U))\). This report is componentwise at most \((y(a),b(a))\). The latter report cannot lose: otherwise lowering a losing report would preserve an empty allocation, contradicting that extracted win.
  • If \(A_a(e_a,d_{-a})=U\), extraction gives a win under \((U,b(a))\). This report is likewise componentwise at most \((y(a),b(a))\), so the same argument shows that the latter cannot lose.

In either case, all opponents are simplified. Hence the nonempty allocation under \((y(a),b(a))\) must be exactly \(y(a)\). But \(b(a)<\theta(a)\), which forbids that win.

Strong loser-independence therefore implies \(A(e)=A(d)\) pointwise. The first case above also proves that the declared value of every allocated bundle is unchanged. Consequently,
\( \displaystyle \int e_a(A_a(e))\,d\lambda=\int d_a(A_a(d))\,d\lambda. \)

Apply the approximation guarantee to the admissible profile \(e\). Since \(y\) is feasible,
\( \displaystyle c\int d_a(A_a(d))\,d\lambda =c\int e_a(A_a(e))\,d\lambda \geq\int e_a(y(a))\,d\lambda \geq\int b_n(a)\,d\lambda. \)

Finally, Fatou's lemma gives the desired bound:
\( \displaystyle \int\theta(a)\,d\lambda \leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int b_n(a)\,d\lambda \leq c\int d_a(A_a(d))\,d\lambda. \)

In particular, these prices are finite almost everywhere along any fixed feasible comparison allocation.

3. Realizing the optimal mass allocation

The LP defining \(W^*\) is feasible: assign everybody the empty bundle. Its feasible region is closed and bounded, since \(0\leq x_{t,S}\leq\mu_t\). Hence an optimum \(x^*\) exists.

Subdivide each type interval into measurable subintervals of lengths \(x^*_{t,S}\), assigning bundle \(S\) on the corresponding subinterval. This produces a feasible allocation \(y\) with
\( \displaystyle \int_0^1v_{t(a)}(y(a))\,d\lambda(a)=W^*. \)

A rational optimal extreme point can be chosen. Moreover, it has at most \(\tau+m\) positive coordinates. To see this, associate with each variable its column of type-membership and item-incidence coefficients, in dimension \(\tau+m\). If more than \(\tau+m\) coordinates were positive, their columns would be linearly dependent. A sufficiently small perturbation in either direction along that dependence would preserve all type totals and all item usages while maintaining nonnegativity, contradicting extremality.

Thus \(y\) can be realized using at most \(\tau+m\) positive-length rational intervals.

4. From pointwise regret to aggregate welfare

Let
\( \displaystyle V=\max_t v_t(M),\qquad W_r=\int v_{t(a)}(A_a(d^r))\,d\lambda(a). \)

Because the actual bids are truthful single-minded bids almost everywhere, the declared and true welfare coincide almost everywhere. Every actual winner pays between zero and its bid, so actual utilities lie in \([0,V]\).

For each individual, let \(u_a^r\) be actual utility, and let \(u_{a,S}^r\) be counterfactual utility from the fixed truthful bid on \(S\). Include the zero action among the comparisons, and define
\( \displaystyle R_H(a)=\max\!\left\{0,\max_{S\subseteq M}\frac1H\sum_{r=1}^H(u_{a,S}^r-u_a^r)\right\},\qquad \mathcal R_H=\int R_H(a)\,d\lambda(a). \)

There are only \(2^m\) comparison actions. Thus the assumed vanishing regret against every fixed action implies \(R_H(a)\to0\) almost everywhere. Lemma 2 gives \(0\leq u_{a,S}^r\leq V\), and therefore \(0\leq R_H(a)\leq V\). Dominated convergence yields \(\mathcal R_H\to0\).

Use the fixed optimal allocation \(y\) constructed above. In every round,
\( \displaystyle v_{t(a)}(y(a))\leq \theta_a(y(a),d_{-a}^r)+u_{a,y(a)}^r. \)

Averaging and integrating, then applying the price bound and regret inequality, gives
\( \displaystyle W^* \leq \frac cH\sum_{r=1}^H W_r +\int\frac1H\sum_{r=1}^H u_a^r\,d\lambda(a) +\mathcal R_H. \)

Payments are nonnegative, so integrated actual utility is at most true welfare. Hence
\( \displaystyle W^*\leq(c+1)\frac1H\sum_{r=1}^H W_r+\mathcal R_H. \)

This proves the asserted guarantee.

5. Implementation bounds and their limits

The following are bounds for representing the optimum, evaluating a supplied bidding sequence, and calculating its counterfactual utilities. The qualitative regret assumption alone does not specify a learning algorithm or a convergence rate.

Optimal welfare and its realization

Put \(N=2^m\). The welfare LP has \(\tau N\) variables and \(\tau+m\) principal constraints. Standard exact rational linear programming computes \(W^*\) and an optimal allocation in time polynomial in
\( m\tau N+L+L_\mu, \)
where \(L_\mu\) is the total encoding length of the masses.

The mass-encoding term needs attention: as worded, \(L\) includes supplies and valuation tables, but does not explicitly include masses. A bit-complexity bound must include \(L_\mu\), or charge their encoding to \(B\).

Since the valuation tables are explicit, \(\tau2^m\) is already bounded by their encoding length. This LP bound is therefore polynomial in the full input length. The resulting optimal interval allocation has at most \(\tau+m\) positive-length cells and polynomial encoding length.

Evaluating the rounds

On a partition with at most \(K\) cells, SIMPLIFY needs at most \(O(K2^m)\) value comparisons, plus polynomial work for bundle cardinalities, ordering, and encodings. For reports already given as truthful single-minded bids, simplification leaves them unchanged.

If one allocation-oracle call and one price-oracle call return the complete refinements for a round, evaluating \(H\) supplied rounds takes
\( \displaystyle O\!\left(H\left[C_A+C_\theta+ K2^m\,\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L+L_\mu+B)\right]\right). \)

This includes refinement handling and exact welfare summation. If the price oracle instead prices separate tag cells, replace the one price call per round by at most \(K\) calls. These are upper bounds; processing already simplified reports is cheaper.

Counterfactual utilities

There are at most \(N=2^m\) distinct truthful bundle actions per individual. Their utilities are determined entirely by the tagged thresholds through Lemma 2; allocation tie decisions at equality are unnecessary.

If a tagged-price query returns the refinement for all individuals for one prescribed bundle, full counterfactual feedback needs at most \(2^m\) price queries per round. If queries are cell-specific, at most \(K2^m\) suffice. Thus a conservative bound for evaluating all comparison utilities over \(H\) rounds is
\( \displaystyle O\!\left(H\left[C_A+K2^m C_\theta+ K2^m\,\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L+L_\mu+B)\right]\right), \)
with the supplied \(K,B\) bounds covering the relevant refinements and outputs.

Arithmetic bounds here use the stipulated finite-encoding oracle model. Exact price computation is charged to \(C_\theta\); a mass-allocation oracle alone does not supply tagged prices.

What does not follow

The hypotheses supply no bounds on \(K,B,C_A,C_\theta\) in terms of \(m,\tau,L,c,H\). Consequently, they give oracle-relative implementation bounds, not an unconditional efficient implementation of every admissible rule.

Nor does vanishing regret give a uniform horizon for achieving a specified error. Any admissible low-regret sequence can be prefixed by an arbitrarily long block of zero bids. This changes each individual's cumulative regret by only a finite amount, preserving vanishing average regret, while every round of the prefix has zero welfare. A quantitative welfare convergence bound therefore requires a quantitative regret bound.
