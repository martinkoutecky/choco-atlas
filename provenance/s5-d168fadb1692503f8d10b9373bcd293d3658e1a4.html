The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(T\) of types with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\), type-level roommate rankings and acceptability, and a graph on types whose paths induce the paper's \(k\)-connection extensions, decide whether a stable mass matching exists. Choose symmetric flows \(x_{tu}=x_{ut}\ge 0\) only on mutually acceptable type pairs and single masses \(s_t\ge 0\), with \(\sum_{u\in T}x_{tu}+s_t=\mu_t\). For each mutually acceptable pair \(t,u\), let \(B_{t,u}=s_t+\sum_{v:\,u\succ_t v}x_{tv}\), the mass of type \(t\) that is single or strictly prefers type \(u\) to its assigned partner type; require \(B_{t,u}=0\) or \(B_{u,t}=0\). Decide whether such a matching exists and return one if it does.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; there is no independent candidate parameter, so set \(m=\tau\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For each type \(t\), input disjoint original and inferred acceptable sets \(A_t,I_t\), each ranked by a weak order, and an unwanted set \(U_t\). Either endpoint declaring the other unwanted forbids that pair; acceptable lists exclude forbidden pairs. Inferred lists may be empty.
Construct the simple undirected graph \(G\) on \(T\): distinct \(t,u\) are adjacent exactly when \(u\in A_t\) or \(t\in A_u\), and their pair is not forbidden. Write \(d(t,u)\) for shortest-path distance, with disconnected distance \(\infty\). Input \(k\geq0\) as a binary integer.
Refine ties within \(I_t\) by increasing distance from \(t\), retaining equal-distance ties. Append to \(A_t\), then \(I_t\), all remaining nonforbidden types \(u\neq t\) with \(d(t,u)\leq k\), ranked by increasing distance with ties. Denote the resulting strict preference relation by \(\succ_t\) and acceptable set by \(C_t\); every acceptable partner is preferred to being single.
A mass matching consists of real flows \(x_{tu}=x_{ut}\geq0\) and single masses \(s_t\geq0\), with \(x_{tu}=0\) unless \(u\in C_t\) and \(t\in C_u\), and \(\sum_{u\in T}x_{tu}+s_t=\mu_t\). A listed same-type partner is allowed: \(x_{tt}\) denotes participating endpoint mass, pairing distinct individuals of that type.
For every mutually acceptable pair, including an acceptable same-type pair, define \(B_{t,u}=s_t+\sum_{v\in C_t:\,u\succ_t v}x_{tv}\). Stability requires \(B_{t,u}=0\) or \(B_{u,t}=0\); ties do not constitute strict improvements.
Is deciding whether a stable mass matching exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), including the binary encoding of \(k\), or is it NP-hard? In the polynomial-time case, can a matching also be returned in polynomial time?
Discrete stable roommates with ties, incomplete lists, and these connection extensions is NP-complete. [Fidan–Erdem](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1471068425100252)
For equal vertex masses, ordinally stable fractional roommate matchings with ties always exist and are computable in polynomial time. [Chen–Roy–Sorge](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0013.pdf)
For strict preferences, nonbipartite stable allocation with vertex and edge capacities has polynomial-time algorithms; this is a nearby capacitated formulation. [Dean–Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf)

Result

Every valid instance admits a stable mass matching, and one can be returned in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), with \(m=\tau\). This includes the binary encoding of \(k\).

The proof uses nonbipartite stable allocation, with an auxiliary vertex for each acceptable same-type pair.

1. Polynomial preprocessing and tie refinement

Every finite distance between distinct vertices of \(G\) is at most \(\tau-1\). Thus, when constructing the extensions, we can replace \(k\) by

\(\displaystyle k'=\min\{k,\tau-1\}.\)

Disconnected distances remain infinite and never satisfy the extension condition. All distances, inferred-list refinements, and appended lists can therefore be computed in polynomial time, without iterating \(k\) times.

For each type, retain only mutually acceptable partners. Removing the other entries does not change any blocking quantity: their flows must be zero.

Now break every remaining tie arbitrarily, obtaining a strict total order \(\widehat{\succ}_t\) that preserves every comparison in \(\succ_t\). Define

\(\displaystyle \widehat B_{t,u}=s_t+\sum_{v:\,u\widehat{\succ}_t v}x_{tv}.\)

For every feasible matching and mutually acceptable pair,

\(\displaystyle 0\leq B_{t,u}\leq \widehat B_{t,u},\)

because every original strict comparison remains strict after refinement. Consequently, stability for the refined orders implies stability for the prescribed orders. It suffices to construct a matching stable under these strict refinements.

2. The stable-allocation theorem used

Consider a finite loopless undirected graph \(H\). Each vertex \(v\) has a positive integer quota \(b(v)\) and a strict order over its incident edges; each edge \(e\) has a positive integer capacity \(c(e)\).

An allocation \(z\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle 0\leq z(e)\leq c(e),\qquad \sum_{e\ni v}z(e)\leq b(v).\)

For an incident edge \(e\), define

\(\displaystyle D_v(e)=b(v)-\sum_{f\ni v}z(f)+\sum_{\substack{f\ni v\\v\text{ prefers }e\text{ to }f}}z(f).\)

Thus \(D_v(e)\) is the sum of unused quota and allocation on strictly worse edges. Stability requires that every unsaturated edge \(e=vw\) satisfy \(D_v(e)=0\) or \(D_w(e)=0\).

Known theorem. Such an instance always has a half-integral stable allocation, and one can be found deterministically in time polynomial in the graph size and the binary lengths of its quotas and capacities. Here half-integral means \(z(e)\in\frac12\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\). This is the weakly polynomial algorithm of Biró–Fleiner; their later paper explicitly records both the algorithm and half-integrality in Section 4.2. See [Biró–Fleiner, *The integral stable allocation problem on graphs*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1572528610000046) and [their subsequent paper, Section 4.2](https://real.mtak.hu/26264/7/BF16do_last.pdf).

We apply this theorem to integer capacities obtained by clearing denominators.

3. Construction of the allocation instance

Write each mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/q_t\), with positive integers encoded in binary. Set

\(\displaystyle Q=\prod_{t\in T}q_t,\qquad b_t=Q\mu_t.\)

Every \(b_t\) is a positive integer.

Construct \(H\) as follows.

  • For every original type \(t\), create a vertex with quota \(b_t\).
  • For every mutually acceptable pair of distinct types \(t,u\), create an edge \(tu\) with capacity \(\min\{b_t,b_u\}\).
  • Whenever \(t\in C_t\), create a private auxiliary vertex \(a(t)\), with quota \(b_t\), and the single edge \(t\,a(t)\), with capacity \(b_t\).

At an original vertex \(t\), order its incident edges according to \(\widehat{\succ}_t\), replacing the same-type entry \(t\), if present, by \(a(t)\). An auxiliary vertex has only one incident edge.

This graph is loopless, has strict preferences, and has positive integer quotas and capacities. It therefore meets the theorem’s hypotheses. Isolated original vertices can simply receive zero allocation; all other components can be processed independently.

Let \(z\) be a stable allocation returned by the theorem. Define

\(\displaystyle x_{tu}=x_{ut}=\frac{z(tu)}Q\quad(t\neq u),\)

and, for an acceptable same-type pair,

\(\displaystyle x_{tt}=\frac{z(t\,a(t))}Q.\)

All other flows are zero. Finally, set

\(\displaystyle s_t=\frac{b_t-\sum_{e\ni t}z(e)}Q.\)

These quantities are nonnegative, and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{u\in T}x_{tu}+s_t=\frac{b_t}Q=\mu_t.\)

The auxiliary edge contributes once to the quota of the original vertex. Hence it represents exactly the participating endpoint mass \(x_{tt}\), as required by the task’s convention.

4. Proof of stability

For an edge at an original vertex \(t\) representing partner \(u\), the construction gives the identity

\(\displaystyle D_t(e)=Q\widehat B_{t,u}.\)

Indeed, unused quota becomes \(Qs_t\), and allocation on strictly worse edges becomes the corresponding scaled sum of worse-partner flows.

Distinct-type pairs

Let \(t,u\) be mutually acceptable and distinct.

If \(z(tu)<\min\{b_t,b_u\}\), allocation stability gives

\(\displaystyle D_t(tu)=0\quad\text{or}\quad D_u(tu)=0.\)

The identity above therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \widehat B_{t,u}=0\quad\text{or}\quad\widehat B_{u,t}=0.\)

If instead the edge is saturated, choose an endpoint attaining its capacity, say \(b_t\leq b_u\). Then \(z(tu)=b_t\). Feasibility and nonnegativity force every other allocation at \(t\), and its unused quota, to be zero. Consequently \(D_t(tu)=0\), so again \(\widehat B_{t,u}=0\).

Thus every distinct-type pair satisfies the required refined stability condition.

Same-type pairs

Suppose \(t\in C_t\), and write \(e=t\,a(t)\).

If \(z(e)=b_t\), the original vertex \(t\) has no unused quota or allocation on other edges. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \widehat B_{t,t}=0.\)

If \(z(e)<b_t\), the auxiliary vertex has positive unused quota. Since it has no other incident edges,

\(\displaystyle D_{a(t)}(e)=b_t-z(e)>0.\)

The edge is unsaturated, so allocation stability forces \(D_t(e)=0\). Hence again

\(\displaystyle \widehat B_{t,t}=0.\)

This is exactly the stability requirement for a same-type pair: its two blocking quantities coincide.

The constructed matching is therefore stable for the refined orders. By Section 1, it is also stable for the prescribed orders.

5. Bit complexity and returned matching

The denominator product has polynomial binary length:

\(\displaystyle \log_2 Q=\sum_{t\in T}\log_2 q_t=O(L).\)

Although its numerical value may be large, computing it and the integer quotas requires only polynomial-time binary arithmetic. Since \(\mu_t\leq1\), every quota and capacity is at most \(Q\), so each has \(O(L)\) bits.

The allocation graph has at most \(2\tau\) vertices and at most

\(\displaystyle \frac{\tau(\tau-1)}2+\tau\)

edges. The cited weakly polynomial algorithm consequently runs in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\).

Moreover, its half-integral allocation yields

\(\displaystyle x_{tu},s_t\in\frac1{2Q}\mathbb Z_{\geq0}.\)

All these masses lie between zero and one, so each output value has \(O(L)\) bits. Returning the entire matching thus also takes polynomial time.

The distinction from discrete stable roommates is substantive: the algorithm uses fractional allocations, including half-integral values after scaling. It imposes no discrete pairing constraint on type masses.
