The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite non-bipartite graph \(H=(T,E)\) of types, rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), shared weak rankings, quotas \(q_t=\mu_t\), and rational partnership-mass capacities \(b_e\leq\min(\mu_t,\mu_u)\), find an exact rational mass assignment \(x\) satisfying the quotas and capacities that is stable: no residual-capacity edge \(e=tu\) admits an arbitrarily small \(\varepsilon>0\) that both endpoint choice functions accept on \(e\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(H=(T,E)\) be a finite simple non-bipartite graph, \(m=|E|\), and \(L\) the total input bit length. Set \(q_t=\mu_t\), and give each edge \(e=tu\) a binary-rational capacity \(0\le b_e\le\min(\mu_t,\mu_u)\).
Each type shares an explicitly given weak ranking of its incident edges, represented by ordered indifference classes \(\pi_{t,1},\ldots,\pi_{t,k_t}\). For an offered vector \(0\le z\le b\), define \(C_t(z)=z\) if \(\sum_e z_e\le q_t\). Otherwise retain all offers in classes above the first class reaching the quota, reject all classes below it, and retain \(\min(r,z_e)\) on each edge of that class, choosing \(r\ge0\) so the total retained mass equals \(q_t\).
An assignment is \(x\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{E}\) with \(x_e\le b_e\) and \(\sum_{e\ni t}x_e\le q_t\). Write \(x_t\) for its incident vector and \(\mathbf1_e\) for the corresponding unit vector. An edge \(e=tu\) blocks if, for arbitrarily small \(0<\varepsilon\le b_e-x_e\), both \(C_t(x_t+\varepsilon\mathbf1_e)_e>x_e\) and \(C_u(x_u+\varepsilon\mathbf1_e)_e>x_e\). Stability means absence of blocking edges.
Is finding an exact rational stable assignment solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

  • Interpreted quotas as upper bounds, not mandatory equalities, following the allocation domain.
  • Specified the cutting-level choice rule within ties; weak rankings alone do not determine endpoint choice functions.
  • Interpreted shared rankings as one ranking shared by all mass of each type, allowing different types to have different rankings.
  • Required nonnegative capacities and a simple loopless graph.
  • Defined m as the edge count and L as the total binary input bit length.
  • Specified acceptance of an increment as retaining strictly more mass on that edge, allowing displacement of existing assignments.

Conclusion

Finding an exact rational stable assignment is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). This follows from the cited real-valued algorithm, after checking that the choice rule and blocking definition coincide and that rational inputs admit an exact polynomial-time implementation.

1. The applicable theorem

Karzanov’s Theorem 6.2 establishes existence and a strongly polynomial algorithm for stable assignments on non-bipartite graphs with real edge capacities, vertex quotas, and mixed choice functions generated by weak orders. Its defining equation (6.1) preserves offers in better classes, rejects offers in worse classes, and retains \(\min\{r,z_e\}\) in the critical class. This is precisely the rule in the question. [Theorem 6.2 and equation (6.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24433v1#S6)

Apply this theorem with vertices \(T\), capacities \(b_e\), quotas \(q_t=\mu_t\), and ordered classes \(\pi_{t,1},\ldots,\pi_{t,k_t}\). All required data are explicitly supplied. The normalization \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\) and bounds \(b_{tu}\leq\min\{\mu_t,\mu_u\}\) impose additional restrictions within the theorem’s domain.

Moreover, the theorem’s acceptable assignments are exactly the feasible assignments here: the rule fixes every vector whose total is at most its quota, whereas an offered vector exceeding its quota is reduced to total \(q_t\).

2. Equivalence of the blocking definitions

The source calls an edge interesting at an endpoint if some positive increase of its offer makes the endpoint retain strictly more on that edge. We verify that this is equivalent to willingness for arbitrarily small increases.

For this choice rule, substitutability states that, whenever \(z\geq z'\) coordinatewise,

\(C_t(z)\wedge z'\leq C_t(z')\),

where \(\wedge\) denotes coordinatewise minimum. This property is proved for mixed choice functions in Lemma 2.1 of the underlying paper; its hypotheses apply because the present rule is exactly that mixed choice function. [Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11556v2#S2)

Suppose an endpoint \(t\) accepts an increase on \(e\) for some \(\delta>0\), meaning

\(C_t(x_t+\delta\mathbf1_e)_e>x_e\).

For any \(0<\varepsilon\leq\delta\), apply substitutability to \(z=x_t+\delta\mathbf1_e\) and \(z'=x_t+\varepsilon\mathbf1_e\). Its \(e\)-coordinate gives

\(C_t(x_t+\varepsilon\mathbf1_e)_e\geq\min\{C_t(x_t+\delta\mathbf1_e)_e,\ x_e+\varepsilon\}>x_e\).

Thus willingness for one increase implies willingness for every smaller positive increase.

If both endpoints are interesting, let their witnessing increments be \(\delta_t,\delta_u>0\). Every positive \(\varepsilon\leq\min\{\delta_t,\delta_u\}\) witnesses simultaneous willingness. Conversely, arbitrarily small simultaneous witnessing increments provide an individual witness at each endpoint.

Consequently, the theorem’s stable assignments are exactly those requested.

3. Exact rational computation and bit complexity

To make the rational-input implication explicit, write each quota and capacity as a fraction with positive integer denominator. Let \(D\) be the product of these denominators. Since their encodings are included in \(L\), the binary encoding length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). Hence the scaled data

\(Q_t=Dq_t,\qquad B_e=Db_e\)

are integers with polynomial total encoding length.

This scaling preserves the choice rule. If \(\widehat C_t\) denotes the rule with quota \(Dq_t\), then

\(\widehat C_t(Dz)=D\,C_t(z)\).

Indeed, class totals and quota comparisons scale by \(D\), and a cutting level \(r\) becomes \(Dr\), because \(\min\{Dr,Dz_e\}=D\min\{r,z_e\}\). Feasibility and blocking therefore also scale bijectively.

Run the real-valued stable-assignment algorithm on \((H,B,Q,\pi)\). The scaled assignment is still allowed to be fractional.

Here “strongly polynomial” includes the encoding requirement: for integer numerical inputs, intermediate data have polynomial encoding length. The underlying paper states this computational convention explicitly. [Computational-complexity discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11556v2#S1)

Exact arithmetic applies: cutting levels are obtained by sorting the critical-class offers and solving a linear equation with integer coefficients; the rotation computations use rational linear algebra, with polynomial encoding bounds established in Proposition 5.1. [Proposition 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11556v2#S5)

Thus the algorithm returns a rational stable assignment \(X\) using polynomially many operations on numbers of polynomial encoding length. Exact integer and rational arithmetic implements those operations in polynomial bit time. The graph has \(\tau\) vertices and \(m\) edges, so the resulting running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Finally, output \(x_e=X_e/D\). Scaling proves that \(x\) is feasible and stable for the original instance, and dividing by \(D\) preserves polynomial encoding length. This completes the polynomial-time reduction and the proof.
