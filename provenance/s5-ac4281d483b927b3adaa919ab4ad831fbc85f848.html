The continuous mirror question, as graded

Fix an alternative set \(X\), a crash fraction \(\rho\), and a tolerance \(\varepsilon\). For each sufficiently large \(N\), \(N\) processes receive private rankings from \(L(X)\), with empirical type distributions converging to a fixed-support society \(\mu\); at most \(t_N=\lfloor \rho N\rfloor\) processes may crash. Does there exist a protocol family, uniform over these input profiles, such that in every admissible synchronous execution the correct processes' output distribution \(\nu_N\) has Kendall-tau diameter at most \(\varepsilon\), and their decisions respect every pair unanimously ranked by the correct processes? The paper's cyclic witness has support on \(m\) rankings with mass \(1/m\) each, and its impossibility applies when \(\rho m>1\) and \(\varepsilon<\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(X\) have \(m\ge 2\) alternatives, and let \(L(X)\) denote strict linear rankings. A society \(\mu\) is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, with masses summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(\rho\in(0,1)\) and \(\varepsilon\ge 0\) be binary rationals, and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; express running times in \(m,\tau,L,N\), distinguishing rounds from local computation.
For each \(N\), there are \(N\) identified processes in a synchronous complete message-passing network, with at most \(t_N=\lfloor\rho N\rfloor\) crash failures. A crashing process follows its protocol until crashing, possibly sending to only some recipients in its last round; crashes may occur after decisions. Correct processes are those that never crash.
Each process privately receives \(R_i\in L(X)\). Consider every sequence of input profiles whose empirical distributions \(\mu_N\) converge to \(\mu\) in total variation, without a prescribed convergence rate; rankings outside the support are allowed. A uniform family is generated by one deterministic algorithm given public \(N,X,\mu,\rho,\varepsilon\), the process identity, and its private ranking.
For every such profile sequence, there must exist \(N_0\), independent of execution choices, such that for every \(N\ge N_0\) and every admissible execution, every correct process irrevocably decides \(S_i\in L(X)\). Writing \(C_N\) for the correct processes, set \(\nu_N=|C_N|^{-1}\sum_{i\in C_N}\delta_{S_i}\). Require \(\operatorname{diam}_{KT}(\operatorname{supp}\nu_N)\le\varepsilon\), using unnormalized Kendall-tau distance, and require \(a\succ_{S_i}b\) for every \(i\in C_N\) whenever \(a\succ_{R_j}b\) for every \(j\in C_N\).

Given \((X,\mu,\rho,\varepsilon)\), does such a uniform protocol family exist, or is this asymptotic agreement task impossible?

For unrestricted finite input profiles, synchronous preference aggregation with unanimity among correct processes is impossible when \(1\le t<N\), \(m\ge N/t\), and \(\varepsilon<\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor\).   • Normalized society masses to sum to one and made the crash fraction and tolerance binary rational inputs, with 0 < ρ < 1 and ε ≥ 0.
  • Used strict rankings for both inputs and decisions, at least two alternatives, and unnormalized Kendall-tau distance, following the finite task.
  • Specified synchronous complete message passing and allowed crashes after decisions; correctness means never crashing in the execution.

A complete finite characterization

The task is decidable. Below is a finite necessary-and-sufficient criterion, together with a uniform protocol when the criterion holds. The computation can be very large; no claim of computational efficiency is made.

For \(\varepsilon\ge \binom m2\), every process can simply output its input without communicating. The characterization below also covers the nontrivial range.

1. Definitions and the criterion

Let \(\mathcal R=L(X)\), and write \(M=m!\). For \(R\in\mathcal R\), define its ordered comparisons by

\[ O(R)=\{(a,b):a\ne b,\ a\succ_R b\}. \]

For a probability distribution \(\eta\) on \(\mathcal R\), define the dissent mass against \(a\succ b\) by

\[ d_\eta(a,b)=\sum_{R:\,b\succ_R a}\eta(R). \]

Define the threshold relation

\[ H=\{(a,b):a\ne b,\ d_\mu(a,b)\le\rho\}. \]

For each \(E\subseteq H\), consider the rational polytope

\[ \mathcal P_E= \left\{ q\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\mathcal R}: \sum_Rq_R=1,\quad \sum_{R:\,b\succ_R a}q_R\le\rho \text{ for every }(a,b)\in E \right\}. \]

It is nonempty because \(\mu\in\mathcal P_E\). Let

\[ A_E=\{R\in\mathcal R:\text{some }q\in\mathcal P_E\text{ has }q_R>0\}. \]

Thus \(A_E\) includes every ranking that can occur with positive mass in a distribution satisfying the inequalities for \(E\), including rankings outside the given support.

For a ranking \(R\), its safe outputs relative to \(E\) are

\[ \mathcal S(R,E)=\{S\in\mathcal R:E\cap O(R)\subseteq O(S)\}. \]

This set is always nonempty: it contains \(R\).

Criterion. A uniform protocol family exists if and only if, for every \(E\subseteq H\), there is a function \(f_E:A_E\to\mathcal R\) satisfying

\[ f_E(R)\in\mathcal S(R,E)\quad\text{for every }R\in A_E \]

and

\[ KT(f_E(R),f_E(R'))\le\varepsilon \quad\text{for every }R,R'\in A_E. \]

This is a finite criterion involving rational linear programs and finite searches over rankings.

2. An exact lemma for one finite profile

Fix \(N\), a crash budget \(t<N\), and an identified input profile \(\mathbf R=(R_1,\ldots,R_N)\). Let \(A(\mathbf R)\) be its set of occurring rankings, and define

\[ E(\mathbf R,t)= \left\{ (a,b):a\ne b,\ |\{j:b\succ_{R_j}a\}|\le t \right\}. \]

Lemma. There is a deterministic protocol satisfying termination, correct-process unanimity, and the diameter bound on this profile in every admissible execution if and only if there is a function \(f:A(\mathbf R)\to\mathcal R\) such that

\[ f(R)\in\mathcal S(R,E(\mathbf R,t)) \]

for every occurring \(R\), and the image of \(f\) has Kendall-tau diameter at most \(\varepsilon\).

Moreover, one universal algorithm works on every profile satisfying this condition.

Necessity: crashes after decisions

Suppose a protocol succeeds on the profile in every execution. Run it without crashes. All processes decide; denote their outputs by \(S_i\).

Fix a process \(i\) and a comparison \((a,b)\in E(\mathbf R,t)\cap O(R_i)\). Let

\[ D=\{j:b\succ_{R_j}a\}. \]

There are at most \(t\) processes in \(D\), and \(i\notin D\). Modify the execution by crashing exactly the processes in \(D\), after all decisions in the original execution.

Every process has the same history through its decision. In the modified execution, all correct inputs rank \(a\) above \(b\). Correct-process unanimity therefore forces \(a\succ_{S_i}b\).

Consequently,

\[ S_i\in\mathcal S(R_i,E(\mathbf R,t)). \]

The crash-free execution also gives \(KT(S_i,S_j)\le\varepsilon\) for every pair of processes. Choose one process of each occurring input type and use its output to define \(f\).

This late-crash argument is the threshold-unanimity mechanism in Lemma 26 of [Wood–Mendes–Pulaj](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol324-opodis2024/LIPIcs.OPODIS.2024.32/LIPIcs.OPODIS.2024.32.pdf); its application here has been proved directly.

Sufficiency: gather inputs, then select safe outputs

Use the following algorithm.

  • Initially, each process knows its own identified input.
  • For \(t+1\) rounds, each process sends its entire set of known identified inputs to every process and takes the union of received information.
  • Let \(K\) be the resulting set of known process identities. Put

\[ h=N-|K|,\qquad b=t-h. \]

Define

\[ E_K= \left\{ (a,b'):a\ne b',\ |\{j\in K:b'\succ_{R_j}a\}|\le b \right\}. \]

  • Exhaustively find a safe selector for the input types represented in \(K\), relative to \(E_K\), whose image has diameter at most \(\varepsilon\). Break ties using a fixed public ordering. Each process outputs the selected ranking for its own input type.

We verify each point needed for correctness.

Common gathered information. Among \(t+1\) rounds, at least one round contains no crash. In such a round, every process alive throughout the round receives every other live process’s complete knowledge. Their knowledge sets therefore become identical.

After that round, surviving processes retain identical knowledge: later crashes only remove senders, and no new private inputs are introduced. Hence every correct process obtains the same \(K\) and the same identified rankings.

Every correct process’s input belongs to \(K\). Thus every identity outside \(K\) is faulty, so \(h\le t\) and \(b\ge0\).

Existence of the selector. Assume the full profile has a selector \(f\) from the lemma’s condition. For every \((a,b')\in E_K\), the full-profile dissent count is at most

\[ |\{j\in K:b'\succ_{R_j}a\}|+h \le b+h=t. \]

Therefore \(E_K\subseteq E(\mathbf R,t)\). Restricting \(f\) to the types represented in \(K\) gives a valid selector for the search in step 4.

Unanimity. Suppose all correct inputs rank \(a\) above \(b'\). Every dissenting process in \(K\) is faulty. The \(h\) identities outside \(K\) are also faulty and are disjoint from those dissenters. Hence

\[ h+|\{j\in K:b'\succ_{R_j}a\}|\le t. \]

It follows that \((a,b')\in E_K\). Each correct process’s input contains that comparison, so its safe output contains it too.

Agreement and termination. Correct processes use the same gathered data and the same deterministic selector. Their outputs belong to its image, so their diameter is at most \(\varepsilon\). The search is finite, and therefore all correct processes decide.

For profiles without a selector, define the algorithm to output its own input when the search fails. This makes the algorithm uniform and terminating for all inputs; the lemma guarantees correctness on every profile satisfying its condition.

3. Proof of the asymptotic characterization

Sufficiency

Assume every \(E\subseteq H\) has a selector as stated in the criterion.

Choose \(\delta>0\) smaller than every positive support mass of \(\mu\), and smaller than every positive number of the form

\[ d_\mu(a,b)-\rho. \]

There are finitely many such numbers. If the second collection is empty, it imposes no restriction on \(\delta\).

Consider any empirical distribution \(p\) with

\[ \|p-\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\delta. \]

Every type in the support of \(\mu\) then occurs in \(p\). Also, total variation bounds the change in the probability of each comparison event. Thus, whenever \(d_\mu(a,b)>\rho\), we have \(d_p(a,b)>\rho\).

For the profile with empirical distribution \(p\), let

\[ E=E(\mathbf R,t_N). \]

Every comparison in \(E\) satisfies

\[ d_p(a,b)\le\frac{t_N}{N}\le\rho. \]

Consequently \(E\subseteq H\), and \(p\in\mathcal P_E\). Every occurring ranking therefore belongs to \(A_E\). Restrict the criterion’s selector \(f_E\) to those occurring types. It satisfies the finite-profile lemma.

Now use the universal algorithm from that lemma, with \(t=t_N\). For every sequence \(\mu_N\to\mu\), there is an \(N_0\) such that

\[ \|\mu_N-\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\delta \]

for all \(N\ge N_0\). At every such \(N\), the algorithm succeeds in every admissible execution.

This \(N_0\) depends only on the input-profile sequence and the fixed \(\delta\), so it is independent of execution choices. No convergence rate is required.

Necessity

Suppose the criterion fails for some \(E\subseteq H\): there is no safe selector on \(A_E\) with diameter at most \(\varepsilon\).

First construct a rational distribution \(q\in\mathcal P_E\) with support exactly \(A_E\). For each \(R\in A_E\), maximize \(q_R\) over \(\mathcal P_E\). This maximum is positive. The polytope is bounded and rational, so it has a rational maximizing point. Average one such point for every \(R\in A_E\).

The average lies in \(\mathcal P_E\), has positive mass on every member of \(A_E\), and has zero mass outside \(A_E\), by the definition of \(A_E\).

Also, \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\subseteq A_E\), since \(\mu\in\mathcal P_E\). For integers \(k\ge2\), put

\[ p^{(k)}=\left(1-\frac1k\right)\mu+\frac1kq. \]

Then \(p^{(k)}\) is rational, belongs to \(\mathcal P_E\), has support exactly \(A_E\), and satisfies

\[ \|p^{(k)}-\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\frac1k. \]

Choose strictly increasing integers \(N_k\) divisible by the denominators of every coordinate of \(p^{(k)}\) and by the denominator of \(\rho\). There is an \(N_k\)-process profile with empirical distribution exactly \(p^{(k)}\), and

\[ t_{N_k}=\rho N_k. \]

Because \(p^{(k)}\in\mathcal P_E\), every comparison in \(E\) has at most \(t_{N_k}\) dissenting inputs. Hence

\[ E\subseteq E(\mathbf R^{(k)},t_{N_k}). \]

A selector satisfying the finite-profile lemma would, upon weakening its constraints from \(E(\mathbf R^{(k)},t_{N_k})\) to \(E\), be a forbidden selector on \(A_E\). Therefore no protocol succeeds on this profile in every admissible execution.

Complete these profiles to a sequence indexed by all positive integers \(N\). At indices other than \(N_k\), use profiles obtained by rounding the support masses of \(\mu\) to integer counts summing to \(N\). Their total variation error tends to zero. The complete sequence therefore converges to \(\mu\).

At every \(N_k\), any proposed protocol fails in some admissible execution. Thus no \(N_0\) can satisfy the task’s requirement. This proves necessity.

4. Decision procedure and running times

Relabel the public alternatives by integers so that rankings can be represented as permutations.

The criterion gives this decision procedure:

  • Enumerate all \(M=m!\) rankings and compute \(H\).
  • Enumerate every \(E\subseteq H\).
  • Compute \(A_E\) by solving, for each ranking \(R\), the linear program maximizing \(q_R\) over \(\mathcal P_E\), and testing whether its optimum is positive.
  • Enumerate functions \(f_E:A_E\to\mathcal R\), checking safety and all pairwise Kendall-tau distances.
  • Answer that a family exists exactly when every \(E\) passes.

The linear programs have \(M\) variables, at most \(m(m-1)\) comparison inequalities, nonnegativity constraints, and one normalization equality. Their rational coefficients have bit length bounded polynomially in \(L\). Standard polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore applies, with exact comparisons of optimal values.

A conservative decision-time bound is

\[ O\!\left( 2^{m(m-1)}(m!)^{m!+2} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \right). \]

This concerns local computation, not communication rounds.

The uniform protocol uses exactly

\[ t_N+1=\lfloor\rho N\rfloor+1 \]

communication rounds. If its gathered set contains \(k\) distinct input rankings, then \(k\le\min(N,m!)\), and a conservative per-process local bit-computation bound is

\[ O\!\left( (t_N+1)N^2\bigl(m\log m+\log(N+1)\bigr) + (m!)^{k+2}\operatorname{poly}(m,N,\tau,L) \right). \]

The first term accounts for exchanging and merging complete input tables. The second accounts for enumerating rankings, computing distances, and searching for a selector. Unbounded message size is permitted by the stated model.

Crucially, these bounds use all \(m!\) rankings where needed. Replacing \(m!\) by the support size \(\tau\) would incorrectly exclude permitted outlier inputs.

5. A simpler exact characterization when \(\varepsilon<1\)

Kendall-tau distance is integer valued. Thus, for \(0\le\varepsilon<1\), the diameter requirement is exact consensus.

Corollary. For \(0\le\varepsilon<1\), a family exists if and only if the directed relation \(H\) is acyclic. When it exists, zero communication rounds suffice.

Sufficiency

Suppose \(H\) is acyclic. Choose a strict linear ranking \(S\) extending \(H\), and have every process output \(S\).

For each \(a\succ_S b\), we claim

\[ \mu(\{R:a\succ_R b\})>\rho. \]

Otherwise \(d_\mu(b,a)\le\rho\), so \((b,a)\in H\), contradicting that \(S\) extends \(H\).

All these inequalities have positive margins. Along every convergent profile sequence, eventually more than \(t_N\) inputs support every comparison in \(S\). Crashing at most \(t_N\) processes therefore cannot leave all correct processes unanimously opposing any comparison in \(S\).

Consequently every correct-process unanimous comparison agrees with \(S\). Consensus and termination are immediate.

Necessity

Suppose \(H\) contains a directed cycle. Take arbitrarily large \(N\) divisible by the denominators of \(\mu\) and \(\rho\), and use profiles with empirical distribution exactly \(\mu\).

Every edge \((a,b)\in H\) has at most \(t_N\) dissenters. It also has a supporting input, since its support mass is at least \(1-\rho>0\).

If a protocol succeeded, its crash-free outputs would all equal one ranking \(S\). The late-crash argument from the finite-profile lemma, applied to a supporting process for each edge, would force \(S\) to contain every edge of \(H\). A strict linear ranking cannot contain a directed cycle. Hence these profiles are impossible, and inserting them into a convergent sequence proves asymptotic impossibility.

Thus the general criterion resolves the task, including equality cases and vanishing fractions of unsupported rankings.
