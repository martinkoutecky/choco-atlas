The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite mediator and advertiser sets \(M\) and \(A\), a finite set of user cost tiers \(K\), rational cohort masses \(\mu_{m,k}\), public rational advertiser capacities \(u_a\), and a bound \(h\) on each mediator’s total cohort mass and each advertiser’s capacity, does there exist a deterministic direct mechanism that chooses assignment masses \(x_{m,k,a}\) and transfers, is truthful for each mediator’s cohort-measure report and each advertiser’s value report, is individually rational and budget balanced, and achieves at least \(1-5h/q^\star\) times optimal gain from trade, where \(q^\star\) is the optimal traded mass?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) and \(A\) be nonempty finite sets of mediators and advertisers, and put \(m=|M|+|A|\). Users are nonstrategic divisible mass, partitioned among mediators. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \((i,c_k)\) with positive mass \(\mu_{i,k}\), each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Costs \(c_k\) and advertiser values \(v_a\) are nonnegative binary rationals. Masses need not sum to one. Public capacities and a public bound satisfy \(0<u_a\le h\) and \(\sum_k\mu_{i,k}\le h\), with rational \(h>0\).
A mediator privately knows its masses and costs. It may withhold any rational submass and report arbitrary nonnegative rational costs for retained subcohorts, splitting cohorts if desired, but cannot fabricate mass. Assignments refer to the retained physical subcohorts, whose true costs determine utility. Advertisers report only their values; capacities are public.
At truthful reports, feasible assignment masses satisfy \(x_{i,k,a}\ge0\), \(\sum_a x_{i,k,a}\le\mu_{i,k}\), and \(\sum_{i,k}x_{i,k,a}\le u_a\). Define \(G(x)=\sum_{i,k,a}(v_a-c_k)x_{i,k,a}\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_xG(x)\). To specify optimal traded mass, fix a report-independent order of players and compare equal numerical costs and values lexicographically using that order; within a mediator, use its reported subcohort order. Pair increasing supply costs with decreasing demand values at equal cumulative mass ranks, retaining pairs whose refined value exceeds refined cost. Let \(q^\star\) be the resulting traded mass.
If mediator \(i\) receives \(t_i\) and advertiser \(a\) pays \(p_a\), their utilities are respectively \(t_i-\sum_{k,a}c_kx_{i,k,a}\) and \(v_a\sum_{i,k}x_{i,k,a}-p_a\), evaluated using true costs and values. Require dominant-strategy truthfulness against all permitted unilateral reports, individual rationality for truthful players regardless of others’ reports, and no deficit \(\sum_a p_a\ge\sum_i t_i\) at every report profile.
Does there exist a deterministic direct mechanism, computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), satisfying these requirements and guaranteeing \(G(x)\ge(1-5h/q^\star)\mathrm{OPT}\) at every truthful profile with \(q^\star>0\), or is this guarantee impossible under these requirements? Here \(L\) is the total binary input length; when \(q^\star=0\), require \(G(x)\ge0\).
For indivisible users and public advertiser capacities, a deterministic truthful, individually rational, no-deficit mechanism achieves \((1-5\gamma/q)\mathrm{OPT}\), where \(\gamma\) bounds each mediator’s user count and each advertiser’s capacity and \(q\) is canonical optimal trade size ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.08717)). The same guarantee appears in the later removal-and-threshold-pricing treatment ([source](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~fferdinando3/cfp/OPTMAS18/papers/paper_4.pdf)). No result settling this precise divisible-mass report model was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Added the missing private advertiser values and numerical tier costs, with nonnegative binary-rational domains.
  • Used unnormalized finite user mass, matching the discrete model’s unrestricted total user count; advertiser capacities and the public mass bound are positive binary rationals.
  • Made cohort masses and costs private to mediators, while capacities and the bound are public.
  • Translated subset reporting into withholding rational submass and arbitrary cost reports on retained physical subcohorts; splitting is allowed, fabrication is excluded, and reports may introduce new cost tiers.
  • Specified fractional assignment feasibility, transfers, and utilities, including evaluation at true costs after misreporting.
  • Interpreted budget balance as no deficit, allowing surplus, and truthfulness as weak dominant-strategy truthfulness.
  • Resolved nonunique optimal traded mass with a sorted cumulative-mass benchmark and fixed symbolic tie-breaking; the zero-trade case receives a separate nonnegative-gain requirement.
  • Quantified over all admissible profiles rather than asking for a mechanism tailored to one known truthful instance, and required polynomial time in support size and binary encoding length.

1. Result and conventions

The guarantee is achievable. Below is a deterministic mechanism with a direct proof for divisible rational mass, including arbitrary rational splitting and withholding.

The construction uses the removal and threshold-pricing idea of the cited [Price by Removal Mechanism](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.08717). The proof below does not assume that truthfulness transfers automatically from indivisible users.

All definitions below apply to the current reports. Write \(s_i\) for mediator \(i\)’s reported total mass, so \(s_i\le h\). A reported subcohort has one reported cost and positive rational mass. Its *refined cost* includes the tie-breaking information specified in the question. Refined demand values use the same prescribed ordering. Numerical payments use numerical costs and values, without tie-breaking information.

Order supply by increasing refined cost and demand by decreasing refined value. Represent each ordered block of mass \(b\), starting at cumulative rank \(r\), by the interval \((r,r+b]\). Thus an order statistic at a block boundary belongs to the block ending there. This endpoint convention will also be used for thresholds.

Let \(q\) denote the canonical traded mass at the current reports. At truthful reports, \(q=q^\star\).

2. Elementary facts about canonical trade

Write \(c(r)\) and \(d(r)\) for the numerical supply cost and demand value at rank \(r\); their refined versions determine whether that rank trades.

For any feasible total traded mass \(z\), the largest possible total advertiser value is obtained from the highest-valued demand prefix of mass \(z\), and the smallest possible total supply cost is obtained from the cheapest supply prefix of mass \(z\). These two prefixes can be paired feasibly because every supply type can be assigned to every advertiser. Consequently, the best gain at total mass \(z\) is

\(F(z)=\int_0^z [d(r)-c(r)]\,dr.\)

The numerical function \(d(r)-c(r)\) is nonincreasing. The refined comparison also changes from successful to unsuccessful at most once: supply keys increase with rank, whereas demand keys decrease. Every canonically retained rank has nonnegative numerical gain, and every excluded rank has nonpositive numerical gain. Therefore,

\(\mathrm{OPT}=F(q)\)

when the reports are truthful. This remains valid when the refined ordering includes or excludes some zero-gain trades.

We also need a deletion fact. Removing supply mass at most \(b\) moves every remaining increasing supply order statistic forward by at most \(b\) ranks. Indeed, among the original first \(r+b\) units, at least \(r\) units remain. In refined comparisons this gives

\(c_{\mathrm{remaining}}(r)\le c(r+b),\)

whenever the right-hand side exists. Removing demand mass at most \(b\) similarly gives

\(d_{\mathrm{remaining}}(r)\ge d(r+b).\)

These inequalities hold for the refined keys as well as their numerical components.

Let \(q_i\) be canonical traded mass after deleting all reported supply of mediator \(i\), with demand unchanged. Then

\(\max\{0,q-s_i\}\le q_i\le q.\)

The upper bound follows because deletion can only increase supply order statistics. For the lower bound, at almost every rank \(r<q-s_i\), the remaining supply cost is at most the original cost at \(r+s_i\), while demand at \(r\) is at least demand at \(r+s_i\). The original comparison at \(r+s_i\) succeeds, so the remaining comparison at \(r\) succeeds.

3. The mechanism

For each mediator \(i\), compute \(q_i\).

If \(q_i\le4h\), declare that mediator’s eligible mass empty.

If \(q_i>4h\), set \(r_i=q_i-4h\). In the supply list with mediator \(i\) deleted, find the subcohort containing rank \(r_i\), using the endpoint convention above. Let \(K_i\) be that subcohort’s refined cost, and let \(C_i\) be its numerical cost.

A reported subcohort of mediator \(i\) is *eligible* exactly when its refined cost is strictly below \(K_i\). Notice that the threshold subcohort belongs to another mediator. Thus equality of numerical costs is resolved by the fixed player order, independently of mediator \(i\)’s subcohort ordering.

Let \(E_i\) be mediator \(i\)’s eligible supply, let \(S_i\) be its mass, and put \(S=\sum_i S_i\).

If \(S=0\), make no assignments and set all transfers to zero.

Otherwise, put \(C=\max_{i:q_i>4h}C_i\). Assign all eligible supply to the highest-ranked real demand prefix of mass \(S\). A deterministic implementation pairs the ordered eligible supply blocks with the ordered demand blocks greedily. Let \(y_a\) be advertiser \(a\)’s assigned mass.

Pay each mediator \(i\) with a finite threshold

\(t_i=C_iS_i.\)

A mediator without a finite threshold receives zero.

To define advertiser payments, consider a quantity auction for exactly \(S\) mass, with the real advertisers and an auxiliary bidder having value \(C\) and capacity \(S\). Let \(W_{-a}(S;C)\) be the maximum numerical value obtainable in this auction when real advertiser \(a\) is absent. Charge

\(p_a=W_{-a}(S;C)-\sum_{b\ne a}v_b y_b.\)

All values in this formula are reports. This is the VCG externality payment for the auxiliary-bidder auction. The next section proves that assigning all eligible mass to the specified real demand prefix is always a welfare-maximizing allocation of that auction.

4. All eligible supply is assigned; individual rationality and no deficit

Suppose a threshold \(K_i\) exists. Its selected rank in the supply list without \(i\) is \(r_i=q_i-4h\). Its corresponding rank \(T_i\) in the full supply list satisfies

\(T_i\le r_i+s_i\le q-3h<q.\)

The corresponding rank is obtained by adding the mass of mediator \(i\) preceding the threshold subcohort; this added mass is at most \(s_i\).

Let \(K\) be the largest refined threshold among the finite thresholds. Its numerical component is \(C\). Every eligible supply subcohort has refined cost strictly below \(K\): its cost is below its own threshold, and its own threshold is at most \(K\).

Let \(B\) be the full supply mass strictly preceding \(K\), and let \(T\) be the full rank of the selected point defining \(K\). The selected point lies a positive distance into its subcohort, so

\(S\le B<T\le q-3h.\)

The canonical comparison at rank \(T\) succeeds. Consequently, all real demand ranks up to \(S\) have refined values above \(K\), and hence numerical values at least \(C\).

It follows that the mechanism can assign all eligible supply to real advertisers. Moreover, its allocation maximizes numerical welfare in the auxiliary-bidder auction: it selects the highest-valued real prefix, and none of those values is below the auxiliary value \(C\). Numerical ties with the auxiliary bidder do not change welfare.

For each advertiser \(a\), the allocation to the other real advertisers together with auxiliary allocation \(y_a\) is feasible in the auction without \(a\). Therefore,

\(W_{-a}(S;C)\ge \sum_{b\ne a}v_b y_b+Cy_a,\)

and hence \(p_a\ge Cy_a\).

Summing gives

\(\sum_a p_a\ge CS\ge\sum_i C_iS_i=\sum_i t_i.\)

Thus there is no deficit at every admissible report profile.

Write \(W=\sum_a v_a y_a\). The auxiliary auction with advertiser \(a\) present can imitate an optimal allocation without \(a\), so \(W\ge W_{-a}(S;C)\). Hence

\(p_a=W_{-a}(S;C)-W+v_a y_a\le v_a y_a.\)

A truthful advertiser therefore has nonnegative utility, regardless of others’ reports.

Every eligible subcohort of mediator \(i\) has numerical reported cost at most \(C_i\). If that mediator reports truthfully, its received transfer covers its true assigned costs. Thus truthful mediators also have nonnegative utility regardless of others’ reports.

5. Dominant-strategy truthfulness for mediators

Fix mediator \(i\) and all other players’ reports.

Its threshold is computed after deleting its entire reported supply. Thus the existence of its threshold, and its value \(C_i\), are independent of every permitted report by \(i\).

If the threshold is absent, mediator \(i\) receives zero allocation and transfer under every report.

Otherwise, every eligible reported subcohort is assigned in full and paid at numerical rate \(C_i\), by Section 4. This remains true even if mediator \(i\)’s report changes other mediators’ thresholds.

For physical mass with true unit cost \(c\), assigning that mass contributes \(C_i-c\) per unit to mediator \(i\)’s utility. Under any permitted report, its utility is therefore at most the integral of the positive part \(\max\{C_i-c,0\}\) over its entire physical supply. Withholding cannot increase this upper bound, and splitting or changing reported costs cannot change the true contribution of assigned physical mass.

Truthful reporting attains the bound: every subcohort with numerical true cost below \(C_i\) is eligible, every subcohort above \(C_i\) is ineligible, and mass with numerical cost equal to \(C_i\) contributes zero whether it is eligible or not.

Hence truthful reporting weakly dominates every permitted withholding, splitting, and cost-reporting deviation.

6. Dominant-strategy truthfulness for advertisers

Fix advertiser \(a\), all mediator reports, and all other advertiser reports. Let \(u=u_a\le h\).

For the proof only, enlarge advertiser \(a\)’s reporting options by allowing it to choose its position among players having the same numerical cost or value. The relative order of all other players remains fixed. Apply exactly the mechanism above to each such insertion. Proving dominance over this larger set proves dominance for the original report space.

Choose a numerical value \(V\) strictly larger than all reported supply costs and all other advertisers’ values. Let \(Q\) be canonical traded mass when advertiser \(a\) reports \(V\). Its demand block is then first.

For any report \(s\) by \(a\), let \(t(s)\) be the total capacity of real advertisers preceding its demand block. Thus its block occupies ranks \((t(s),t(s)+u]\). Write \(q_i(s)\) for canonical trade after removing mediator \(i\), under report \(s\).

We establish three facts.

*First, for every report \(s\) and mediator \(i\),*

\(\max\{0,Q-h\}\le q_i(s)\le Q.\)

The upper bound follows because the high report \(V\) improves demand order statistics, while deleting a mediator worsens supply order statistics.

For the lower bound, consider almost every \(r<Q-h\). Deleting mediator \(i\) shifts supply ranks by at most \(h\), so its remaining refined cost at rank \(r\) is at most the full supply cost at rank \(r+h\).

Deleting advertiser \(a\) from the high-report demand removes its first \(u\) mass. Adding its demand back at any reported value can only improve demand order statistics. Thus demand under \(s\), at rank \(r\), is at least high-report demand at rank \(r+u\), which is at least high-report demand at rank \(r+h\).

The high-report canonical comparison succeeds at rank \(r+h<Q\). Chaining these refined inequalities proves that the comparison defining \(q_i(s)\) succeeds at rank \(r\).

*Second, every report with \(t(s)\ge Q-3h\) receives zero allocation.*

If \(Q\le4h\), every \(q_i(s)\le4h\), so eligible supply is empty under every report.

Otherwise, every finite threshold has remaining-supply rank at most \(Q-4h\), and corresponding full-supply rank at most \(Q-3h\). Taking the largest refined threshold, exactly as in Section 4, shows that eligible mass satisfies \(S(s)\le Q-3h\).

The mechanism assigns eligible mass to the first \(S(s)\) real demand ranks. Advertiser \(a\)’s demand begins after rank \(t(s)\), so it receives zero whenever \(t(s)\ge Q-3h\). Its payment is then also zero: the chosen allocation already uses only other advertisers and is feasible without \(a\), so \(W_{-a}=W\).

*Third, all reports satisfying \(t(s)\le Q-2h\) produce identical thresholds and eligible supply.*

Assume \(Q>4h\). Under such a report,

\(t(s)+u\le Q-h.\)

Both advertiser \(a\)’s reported block and its high-report block therefore end by rank \(Q-h\). Beyond that rank, the demand sequences are identical: in each sequence, the remaining demand consists of the same other advertisers in the same order.

The first fact gives \(q_i(s)\ge Q-h\) and \(q_i(V)\ge Q-h\). The comparisons defining these trade masses coincide at every rank above \(Q-h\). Since successful canonical comparisons form a prefix, this proves \(q_i(s)=q_i(V)\).

The supply ordering is unchanged. Thus all threshold subcohorts, eligibility decisions, eligible mass \(S\), and reserve \(C\) are identical throughout this region.

We now prove dominance. If \(Q\le4h\), every report yields zero utility, so the conclusion holds. Assume \(Q>4h\), and let \(v\) be advertiser \(a\)’s true value.

If its truthful report satisfies \(t\le Q-2h\), all reports in that region use one fixed auxiliary-bidder auction. Its VCG payment has the form

\(p_a=W_{-a}-\sum_{b\ne a}v_b y_b.\)

Consequently, its true utility equals true total auction welfare, including auxiliary welfare, minus the fixed constant \(W_{-a}\). Truthful bidding maximizes that total welfare, so it dominates all reports in the region. Reports outside the region have \(t>Q-2h>Q-3h\), hence yield zero utility. Truthful utility is nonnegative by Section 4.

It remains to consider a truthful report with \(t>Q-2h\). Its utility is zero. We construct a comparison report with start rank

\(t_0\in[Q-3h,Q-2h].\)

The possible insertion ranks among the other advertisers are their cumulative capacities. Consecutive ranks differ by at most \(h\). Their final cumulative capacity is at least

\(Q-u\ge Q-h>Q-2h,\)

whereas the first rank is zero. The first cumulative rank at least \(Q-3h\) therefore lies in the displayed interval. The enlarged tie-reporting options allow advertiser \(a\) to realize this insertion rank with a nonnegative rational value \(v_0\).

Since \(t_0<t\), its numerical value satisfies \(v_0\ge v\). If its true value were \(v_0\) and its truthful insertion were this comparison report, the preceding case would establish dominance. Its truthful utility would nevertheless be zero, because \(t_0\ge Q-3h\). Therefore every report \(s\) satisfies

\(v_0y_a(s)-p_a(s)\le0.\)

Since \(y_a(s)\ge0\) and \(v\le v_0\), it follows that

\(vy_a(s)-p_a(s)\le0.\)

Thus the original truthful report, whose utility is zero, dominates every deviation. Advertiser truthfulness is proved.

7. The gain-from-trade guarantee

Now suppose all reports are truthful.

Section 4 shows that every selected supply cost is at most \(C\) and every selected demand value is at least \(C\). Consequently, the mechanism always has \(G(x)\ge0\).

If \(q^\star=0\), this is the required conclusion. If \(0<q^\star\le5h\), then \(\mathrm{OPT}\ge0\) and \(1-5h/q^\star\le0\), so the desired inequality also follows.

Assume \(q=q^\star>5h\), and put \(R=q-5h>0\). We first prove that the entire cheapest supply prefix of mass \(R\) is eligible.

For every mediator \(i\), Section 2 gives \(q_i\ge q-h>4h\), so its threshold exists and

\(r_i=q_i-4h\ge R.\)

Consider any supply subcohort of mediator \(i\) having positive mass in the full supply prefix of length \(R\). Let \(b\) be the full supply mass strictly preceding that subcohort. Then \(b<R\).

After removing mediator \(i\), the mass with refined costs preceding this subcohort’s key is at most \(b\). Since \(r_i\ge R>b\), the threshold subcohort at rank \(r_i\) must have a strictly larger refined cost. The considered subcohort is therefore eligible. This proves the prefix inclusion, including subcohorts crossing rank \(R\).

In particular, \(S\ge R\). Decompose the mechanism’s truthful gain as

\(G(x)=\int_0^S[d(r)-C]\,dr+\int_E[C-c]\,d\mu,\)

where \(E\) is the eligible supply.

Both integrands are nonnegative on their respective domains, by Section 4. Eligible supply contains the cheapest prefix of mass \(R\). Therefore,

\(G(x)\ge\int_0^R[d(r)-C]\,dr+\int_0^R[C-c(r)]\,dr=\int_0^R[d(r)-c(r)]\,dr.\)

Let \(g(r)=d(r)-c(r)\). This is nonincreasing and nonnegative on the canonical traded interval. Its average over the first \(R\) ranks is at least its average over all \(q\) ranks. Explicitly, when \(R<q\), integrating \(g(r)\ge g(s)\) over \(0<r<R<s<q\) gives

\((q-R)\int_0^R g(r)\,dr\ge R\int_R^q g(s)\,ds.\)

Rearranging yields

\(\int_0^R g(r)\,dr\ge \frac{R}{q}\int_0^q g(r)\,dr.\)

Using canonical optimality from Section 2, we conclude

\(G(x)\ge\frac{q-5h}{q}\mathrm{OPT}=\left(1-\frac{5h}{q^\star}\right)\mathrm{OPT}.\)

8. Polynomial-time implementation

The mechanism operates directly on rational blocks; it never expands mass into individual units.

Sort reported supply subcohorts and advertiser demand blocks by their refined orders. Canonical trade masses are obtained by merging their cumulative rational endpoints. Repeating this computation with each mediator removed yields all \(q_i\), and each threshold is found by a cumulative-mass scan.

Eligibility, \(S_i\), and \(S\) require further scans. Greedy assignment between eligible supply and the selected demand prefix gives a polynomial-size list of nonzero assignment masses.

For each advertiser \(a\), compute \(W_{-a}(S;C)\) by filling mass \(S\) from the remaining advertiser blocks and the auxiliary block in decreasing numerical value order.

These procedures use polynomially many rational comparisons, additions, subtractions, and multiplications. All intermediate numerators and denominators have polynomial bit length in the encoded input length: cumulative masses are sums of input rationals, and welfare and transfers are sums of products of rational values and masses. Thus the mechanism runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). For a split report, its encoded subcohorts are processed in time polynomial in that report’s length.

The endpoint convention is part of the algorithm, so exact rational boundary ranks require neither perturbations nor limits. The construction and proofs therefore settle the stated divisible-mass report model.
