The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_t\) of advertiser types, where type \(t\) has bid \(b_t\) and CTR function \(\theta_t\), and per-position supply \(\rho\) across \(m\) ordered slots, find mass assignments \(x_{t,S}\) to the empty bundle or an interval \(S\), together with common total bundle prices \(p(S)\). Require \(\sum_S x_{t,S}\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,S:\,j\in S}x_{t,S}\le\rho\), and that every \(S\) with \(x_{t,S}>0\) maximizes \(b_t\theta_t(S)-p(S)\), including the empty bundle. Determine whether this can be computed with the paper’s competitive-equilibrium conditions and welfare guarantees: \(1/4\) for XOS CTRs and \(O(\log\log m/\log m)\) for subadditive CTRs.

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses need not sum to one.
Each type has a nonnegative rational bid \(b_t\) and rational interval CTR values, promised to extend to a nonnegative monotone set function \(\theta_t\) on \(2^M\), with \(\theta_t(\varnothing)=0\) and \(\theta_t(M)=1\). Consider separately XOS and subadditive extensions. Each slot has binary-rational supply \(\rho>0\); \(L\) is the total input bit length.
An allocation consists of rational masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\), satisfying \(\sum_{S\in\mathcal I}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le\rho\). Empty assignments represent unserved advertisers.
For market clearance, include an auxiliary zero-valued type of mass \(m\rho\), assigned the residual supply so that every slot constraint becomes equality; its remaining mass receives the empty bundle. Include this type in the equilibrium conditions below.
Common total bundle prices satisfy \(p(\varnothing)=0\) and \(p(S)\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\cup\{+\infty\}\). Infinite prices exclude bundles from the offered menu; every positively assigned bundle has finite price. Prices need not be additive.
Require \(x_{t,S}>0\) only when \(S\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{T\in\mathcal I}\{b_t\theta_t(T)-p(T)\}\), allowing arbitrary splitting among ties. Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, with welfare exactly \(\mathrm{OPT}\). A deterministic algorithm suffices. Moreover, the proof applies to arbitrary explicitly supplied interval valuations; neither the XOS nor the subadditive promise is needed.

The crucial feature is that advertisers’ masses may be split among bundles. Thus the welfare linear program already describes admissible allocations. For comparison, the finite-advertiser algorithm of [Ghiasi et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0043.pdf) rounds a configuration LP; that rounding is unnecessary here.

1. The welfare LP and its dual

Write \(\mathcal I^+=\mathcal I\setminus\{\varnothing\}\) and \(v_t(S)=b_t\theta_t(S)\). Consider the following linear program, whose variables describe only nonempty assignments:

\(\displaystyle \max_{x\ge0}\ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{S\in\mathcal I^+}v_t(S)x_{t,S}\),

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\in\mathcal I^+}x_{t,S}\le\mu_t\quad(t=1,\ldots,\tau)\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal I^+\\j\in S}}x_{t,S}\le\rho\quad(j=1,\ldots,m)\).

Every feasible solution becomes an allocation satisfying the required type equalities by setting

\(\displaystyle x_{t,\varnothing}=\mu_t-\sum_{S\in\mathcal I^+}x_{t,S}\).

Conversely, deleting the empty assignments from any feasible allocation gives a feasible LP solution with the same welfare. Hence this LP has optimum \(\mathrm{OPT}\).

Its dual has a nonnegative variable \(u_t\) for each type constraint and a nonnegative variable \(q_j\) for each slot constraint:

\(\displaystyle \min_{u,q\ge0}\ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tu_t+\rho\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_j\),

subject to

\(\displaystyle u_t+\sum_{j\in S}q_j\ge v_t(S)\quad(t=1,\ldots,\tau,\ S\in\mathcal I^+)\).

The primal is feasible, since the zero vector is feasible. Its feasible region is bounded, because \(0\le x_{t,S}\le\mu_t\). The dual is feasible: take \(q_j=0\) and \(u_t=b_t\), using the promised monotonicity and normalization to obtain \(\theta_t(S)\le1\).

Consequently, strong LP duality gives optimal primal and dual solutions \(x,u,q\) with equal objective values. Choose rational optimal solutions.

2. Optimal dual variables support advertiser demand

For each slot define its residual supply by

\(\displaystyle r_j=\rho-\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal I^+\\j\in S}}x_{t,S}\ge0\).

Expanding the difference between the dual and primal objectives gives

\(\displaystyle 0=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}u_tx_{t,\varnothing}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_jr_j+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{S\in\mathcal I^+}x_{t,S}\left(u_t+\sum_{j\in S}q_j-v_t(S)\right)\).

Every summand is nonnegative, by primal and dual feasibility. Therefore:

  • If \(x_{t,S}>0\) for a nonempty interval \(S\), then \(v_t(S)-\sum_{j\in S}q_j=u_t\).
  • If \(x_{t,\varnothing}>0\), then \(u_t=0\).
  • If \(r_j>0\), then \(q_j=0\).

Define common bundle prices by

\(\displaystyle p(S)=\sum_{j\in S}q_j\quad(S\in\mathcal I)\).

These prices are rational and nonnegative, and \(p(\varnothing)=0\). All bundles have finite prices.

Dual feasibility implies that every nonempty bundle satisfies \(v_t(S)-p(S)\le u_t\). The empty bundle also satisfies this inequality, since its utility is zero and \(u_t\ge0\).

Every positively assigned nonempty bundle attains utility \(u_t\). Whenever the empty bundle is positively assigned, it also attains utility \(u_t=0\). Thus every positive assignment is utility-maximizing over the entire menu, including all unserved advertiser mass.

3. Auxiliary type, clearance, and payments

Give the auxiliary zero-valued type, denoted \(0\), the assignments

\(\displaystyle x_{0,\{j\}}=r_j\quad(j=1,\ldots,m)\),

\(\displaystyle x_{0,\varnothing}=m\rho-\sum_{j=1}^{m}r_j\),

and zero mass on every other bundle.

These assignments are feasible because \(0\le r_j\le\rho\), so \(\sum_jr_j\le m\rho\). Their total mass is exactly \(m\rho\), and they make every slot constraint an equality.

The auxiliary type’s maximum utility is zero: every price is nonnegative, and the empty bundle has price zero. If \(r_j>0\), the preceding calculation gives \(q_j=0\), hence \(p(\{j\})=0\). Therefore every positively assigned auxiliary bundle is utility-maximizing as well.

All payments are nonnegative and go to the seller. In fact, total payments, including the auxiliary type, are

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\sum_{S\in\mathcal I}x_{t,S}p(S)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_j\left(\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal I\\j\in S}}x_{t,S}\right)=\rho\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_j\).

The auxiliary type pays zero. No subsidy or outside transfer is used.

We have therefore constructed an equilibrium satisfying every stated requirement. Its real-advertiser welfare is the primal optimum, namely \(\mathrm{OPT}\).

4. Polynomial-time computation and approximation guarantees

There are \(m(m+1)/2\) nonempty intervals. The primal has \(\tau m(m+1)/2\) variables and \(\tau+m\) resource constraints; the dual also has polynomial size.

All coefficients are rational with encoding lengths polynomial in \(L\): the objective coefficients are products of the supplied bids and CTR values. The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore computes exact rational optimal primal and dual solutions with polynomial encoding length. Computing the empty assignments, residual assignments, and bundle prices requires only polynomially many rational arithmetic operations.

In particular, the algorithm never expands a mass into individual advertisers. Its running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), including when masses have very large binary-encoded numerators or denominators.

The output is deterministic and satisfies \(W(x)=\mathrm{OPT}\). Hence its expected welfare meets both requested guarantees. With natural logarithms, one may take \(c=1\), since \(0<\log\log m/\log m<1\) for \(m\ge3\).
