The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational society masses \( \mu_t \), initial labels \(b_t \in \{0,1\}\), a rational row-stochastic interaction matrix \(A=(a_{ut})\), and a budget \(K\), decide whether there exist \(p_t \in [0,1]\) such that \( \sum_t a_{ut}p_t \le \frac{1}{2} \) for every \(u\), and \( \sum_t \mu_t |p_t-b_t| \le K \), with \( \sum_t \mu_t b_t < \frac{1}{2} \).

Definitions.
Let \(T\) be finite, \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{T}\) satisfy \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), let \(f_t\in\{0,1\}\) satisfy \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t f_t<1/2\), and let \(W\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{T imes T}\) be symmetric. Choose \(y_{t,0},y_{t,1}\ge0\) with \(y_{t,0}+y_{t,1}=\mu_t\).
A choice is feasible when \(\sum_{u\in T}W_{tu}y_{u,1}\le\sum_{u\in T}W_{tu}y_{u,0}\) for every \(t\in T\), and has cost \(\sum_{t:f_t=0}y_{t,1}+\sum_{t:f_t=1}y_{t,0}\). Its decision form additionally asks whether cost is at most a rational bound \(B\).

Target.

Theorem

The rational-mass Eliminating Majority Illusion decision problem is polynomial-time solvable under the standard binary encoding of rational numbers. More precisely, it reduces directly to rational linear-program feasibility.

Consequently, it is not \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions unless \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).

1. Elimination of redundant variables

For every \(t\in T\), define

\[ x_t:=y_{t,1}. \]

The equality \(y_{t,0}+y_{t,1}=\mu_t\) then forces

\[ y_{t,0}=\mu_t-x_t. \]

Thus nonnegativity of \(y_{t,0}\) and \(y_{t,1}\) is equivalent to

\[ 0\leq x_t\leq \mu_t. \]

This correspondence between choices \((y_{t,0},y_{t,1})_{t\in T}\) and vectors \(x\) satisfying these box constraints is bijective.

2. Feasibility constraints are linear

For each \(t\in T\), the majority constraint becomes

\[ \sum_{u\in T}W_{tu}x_u \leq \sum_{u\in T}W_{tu}(\mu_u-x_u). \]

Moving the terms involving \(x\) to the left gives the equivalent inequality

\[ 2\sum_{u\in T}W_{tu}x_u \leq \sum_{u\in T}W_{tu}\mu_u. \]

Define the rational constant

\[ b_t:=\sum_{u\in T}W_{tu}\mu_u. \]

The feasibility conditions are therefore exactly

\[ 0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t \quad\text{for every }t\in T, \]

together with

\[ 2\sum_{u\in T}W_{tu}x_u\leq b_t \quad\text{for every }t\in T. \]

3. The cost is linear

Let

\[ F:=\{t\in T:f_t=1\}, \qquad p:=\sum_{t\in F}\mu_t. \]

The hypothesis states that \(p<1/2\). For the vector \(x\), the cost is

\[ \begin{aligned} C(x) &= \sum_{t:f_t=0}x_t + \sum_{t:f_t=1}(\mu_t-x_t)\\ &= p+\sum_{t\in T}(1-2f_t)x_t. \end{aligned} \]

Hence the bound \(C(x)\leq B\) is precisely the linear inequality

\[ \sum_{t\in T}(1-2f_t)x_t\leq B-p. \]

The decision problem is thus equivalent to feasibility of the following rational linear system:

\[ 0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t \quad\text{for every }t\in T, \]

\[ 2\sum_{u\in T}W_{tu}x_u\leq b_t \quad\text{for every }t\in T, \]

and

\[ \sum_{t\in T}(1-2f_t)x_t\leq B-p. \]

4. Correctness of the reduction

Suppose the original instance admits a choice of cost at most \(B\). Setting \(x_t=y_{t,1}\) gives the box constraints, the transformed majority inequalities, and the transformed cost inequality. Hence the linear system is feasible.

Conversely, suppose \(x\) satisfies the displayed linear system. Define

\[ y_{t,1}:=x_t, \qquad y_{t,0}:=\mu_t-x_t. \]

The box constraints imply that these values are nonnegative and sum to \(\mu_t\). Reversing the algebra in Sections 2 and 3 shows that all original majority constraints hold and that the cost is at most \(B\). Thus the original instance is feasible.

Therefore the reduction is exact in both directions.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

Let \(L\) denote the binary encoding length of the input. The constructed linear program has \(|T|\) variables and at most \(3|T|+1\) inequalities.

All coefficients are rational. Each \(b_t\) is a sum of \(|T|\) products of input rationals, while \(p\) is a sum of input rationals. Exact rational multiplication and addition produce numerators and denominators whose bit lengths are polynomial in \(L\). Thus the entire transformed linear program has encoding length polynomial in \(L\).

Feasibility of an explicitly encoded rational linear program can be decided in time polynomial in its number of variables, number of inequalities, and coefficient bit length, for example by the ellipsoid method. Applying that theorem to the constructed system gives a polynomial-time decision algorithm. It also yields an exact rational feasible solution whenever one exists.

Importantly, this runtime is polynomial in the binary encoding length of the masses and does not expand the masses into a population whose size is a common denominator.

6. Additional structural simplification

Nonnegativity of \(W\) gives a more specific packing formulation. Given any feasible \(x\), define

\[ x'_t= \begin{cases} x_t,&f_t=1,\\ 0,&f_t=0. \end{cases} \]

Because \(W_{tu}\geq0\), replacing \(x\) by \(x'\) can only decrease every left-hand side \(2\sum_u W_{tu}x_u\). It therefore preserves feasibility. It also decreases the cost by exactly \(\sum_{t:f_t=0}x_t\).

Consequently, some optimal solution satisfies \(x_t=0\) whenever \(f_t=0\). The optimization problem can equivalently be written as

\[ \max \sum_{t\in F}x_t \]

subject to

\[ 0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t \quad\text{for }t\in F, \]

and

\[ 2\sum_{u\in F}W_{tu}x_u \leq \sum_{u\in T}W_{tu}\mu_u \quad\text{for every }t\in T. \]

If the optimum of this packing linear program is \(M\), then the minimum relabelling cost is exactly \(p-M\).

This also explains why the known indivisible-agent hardness does not transfer: the indivisible problem contains binary relabelling decisions, whereas the rational-mass formulation permits every \(x_t\) in the full interval \([0,\mu_t]\), leaving an ordinary linear program.

Conclusion

The rational-mass problem admits an exact polynomial-time algorithm. Symmetry of \(W\), normalization \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and the hypothesis \(p<1/2\) are not needed for polynomial-time solvability; they concern the intended majority-illusion interpretation. Nonnegativity of \(W\) is needed only for the additional packing simplification, not for the basic linear-program reduction.
