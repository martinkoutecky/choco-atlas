The continuous mirror question, as graded

For each sufficiently large \(k\), does there exist a finite catalogue \(\mathcal H\) of equal-size \(n\) undirected linear-threshold community templates and rational community masses \(\mu_H\), with \(\sum_H\mu_H=1\), such that assigning each community \(k\) seeds by template-wise greedy yields aggregate infected-mass ratio at most \(1-(1-1/k)^k+Ck^{-1/5}\) relative to the best template-wise \(k\)-seed assignments, for some constant \(C\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a finite nonempty catalogue \(\mathcal H\) of simple, unweighted, undirected graphs on the labelled vertex set \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_H\), where \(\sum_H\mu_H=1\). The support size is \(\tau=|\mathcal H|\), not the number of possible templates. Let \(m=n\) and let \(L\) be the total encoding bit length; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L,k\).
Communities have no edges between them. In a template, seeds are initially infected; independently, each nonisolated vertex samples an integer threshold uniformly from \(\{1,\ldots,\deg_H(v)\}\), and becomes permanently infected when at least that many neighbors are infected. Unseeded isolated vertices remain uninfected. Write \(\sigma_H(S)\) for the expected final number infected, including seeds.
For \(1\leq k\leq n\), a template-wise assignment chooses exactly \(k\) distinct vertices \(S_H\) in every template and uses that same set throughout its community mass. Its infected mass is \(I((S_H)_H)=\sum_H\mu_H\sigma_H(S_H)/n\); define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_{(S_H)_H:\,|S_H|=k}I((S_H)_H)\).
Template-wise greedy starts from the empty set in each template and repeatedly adds a vertex maximizing the exact marginal increase in \(\sigma_H\), breaking ties by smallest vertex label. Assume exact influence-value oracle access. Let \(I_{\mathrm{gr}}\) be its infected mass.

Do there exist constants \(C>0\) and \(k_0\) such that, for every integer \(k\geq k_0\), some \(n\geq k\) and society as defined above satisfy \(I_{\mathrm{gr}}/\mathrm{OPT}\leq 1-(1-1/k)^k+Ck^{-1/5}\)?

For a single finite undirected graph, greedy has examples with ratio at most \(1-(1-1/k)^k+O(k^{-1/5})\), while its universal guarantee is \(1-(1-1/k)^k+\Omega(k^{-3})\). [Schoenebeck, Tao, and Yu](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.11679)
No separate result specifically formulated for distributional societies was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified templates as simple, unweighted, undirected graphs, with unrestricted vertex eligibility for seeding.
  • Specified the threshold distribution, permanent infection, inclusion of seeds in influence, and the behavior of isolated vertices.
  • Interpreted community masses as normalized community shares, with each vertex carrying its community mass divided by the common template size; excluded edges between communities.
  • Interpreted template-wise assignments as one deterministic k-element seed set per template, applied throughout that template's mass, without a shared seed budget.
  • Fixed greedy ties by smallest vertex label and retained exact influence-value oracle access.
  • Required positive support masses, a nonempty catalogue, and n at least k; allowed catalogue size and template size to depend on k.
  • Made the asymptotic quantifiers explicit: C and k_0 are uniform over k.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Answer and single-graph input

Yes. Singleton supports are allowed. More generally, the template-wise objective separates into independent single-graph objectives, so no separate distributional construction is needed.

We use the following known result, supplied in the question: there exist constants \(C>0\) and \(k_0\) such that, for every integer \(k\geq k_0\), there is a finite simple, unweighted, undirected graph \(G_k\), with at least \(k\) vertices, and a valid greedy trajectory whose final seed set \(B_k\) satisfies
\( \frac{\sigma_{G_k}(B_k)} {\max_{|S|=k}\sigma_{G_k}(S)} \leq 1-(1-1/k)^k+Ck^{-1/5}. \)

This is [Schoenebeck–Tao–Yu, Theorem 3.2 and Example 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.11679#S3). Their construction is indexed by every sufficiently large integer \(k\). Definition 2.1 uses exactly the threshold process in this question: independent uniform integer thresholds, seeds initially infected, and unseeded isolated vertices never infected.

Enforcing the prescribed tie-breaking rule

A greedy trajectory permitted by that theorem can be realized under smallest-label tie-breaking.

Fix its ordered selections \(b_1,\ldots,b_k\). Relabel \(b_i\) by \(i\), and assign the remaining vertices distinct labels greater than \(k\). Let \(\pi\) be this relabelling and \(H_k\) the resulting graph.

For every seed set \(S\), we have
\( \sigma_{H_k}(\pi(S))=\sigma_{G_k}(S). \)
Indeed, relabelling preserves degrees and adjacency. Coupling each vertex’s threshold with its relabelled counterpart makes the infected sets correspond at every iteration, hence also at termination.

We now prove by induction that smallest-label greedy selects \(1,\ldots,k\) in that order. Suppose it has selected \(1,\ldots,i-1\). By influence invariance and validity of the original trajectory, vertex \(i\) maximizes the marginal influence. Moreover, every other unselected vertex has label greater than \(i\). Thus the prescribed tie-breaking selects \(i\). This proves the induction.

Relabelling also preserves the optimum, because it bijects the seed sets of size \(k\). Consequently, the displayed approximation bound holds for the required deterministic greedy rule on \(H_k\).

Why the society objective separates

For any society, define
\( M_H=\max_{|S|=k}\sigma_H(S) \)
and let \(B_H\) be its prescribed greedy seed set.

Every feasible assignment satisfies
\( I((S_H)_H)\leq \frac1n\sum_{H\in\mathcal H}\mu_H M_H. \)
Conversely, each maximum is attained because there are finitely many seed sets. Choosing an attaining set independently in every template is feasible. Hence
\( \mathrm{OPT}=\frac1n\sum_{H\in\mathcal H}\mu_H M_H. \)

Since seeds remain infected, \(M_H\geq k>0\). Therefore, defining
\( r_H=\frac{\sigma_H(B_H)}{M_H}, \qquad w_H=\frac{\mu_HM_H}{\sum_{J\in\mathcal H}\mu_JM_J}, \)
we obtain
\( \frac{I_{\mathrm{gr}}}{\mathrm{OPT}} =\sum_{H\in\mathcal H}w_Hr_H. \)
The weights are positive and sum to one. Thus the society ratio is a weighted average of its single-template ratios. Conversely, every single-graph ratio occurs in a singleton society.

Constructing the required society

For each \(k\geq k_0\), take the relabelled graph \(H_k\), put \(n=|V(H_k)|\), and choose
\( \mathcal H=\{H_k\},\qquad \mu_{H_k}=1. \)

This is an admissible society: its support is finite and nonempty, its sole mass is a positive binary rational, and \(n\geq k\). There are no edges between communities because there is only one template community.

The common normalization cancels:
\( \frac{I_{\mathrm{gr}}}{\mathrm{OPT}} = \frac{\sigma_{H_k}(\{1,\ldots,k\})/n} {\max_{|S|=k}\sigma_{H_k}(S)/n} \leq 1-(1-1/k)^k+Ck^{-1/5}. \)

The constants from the single-graph theorem therefore establish exactly the requested statement.
