The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a finite support \(T\) of rankings with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), an initially unique Borda winner \(p\), and a rational threshold \(\kappa>0\), does there exist a destination ranking \(d\in T\) and transfers \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) for \(t\ne d\), with \(\sum_{t\ne d}x_t\ge\kappa\), such that the resulting masses \(\mu'_t=\mu_t-x_t\) for \(t\ne d\) and \(\mu'_d=\mu_d+\sum_{t\ne d}x_t\) leave \(p\) the unique Borda winner?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given candidates \(C\), with \(m=|C|\geq 2\), a society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) distinct strict rankings of \(C\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Use Borda with scores weighted by mass. The input also contains an initially unique winner \(p\in C\) and a binary rational threshold \(\kappa>0\). Ties with another candidate disqualify \(p\) as a unique winner.
A permitted transfer chooses one destination \(d\in T\) and real amounts \(0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t\) for \(t\neq d\). Destination mass cannot leave; transferred mass adopts ranking \(d\).
The resulting masses are \(\mu'_t=\mu_t-x_t\) for \(t\neq d\) and \(\mu'_d=\mu_d+\sum_{t\neq d}x_t\). Decide whether such a transfer exists with \(\sum_{t\neq d}x_t\geq\kappa\) and \(p\) still the unique Borda winner.

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

The discrete version, where whole voters switch to one existing destination party while preserving the unique Borda winner, is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. [Guo, Shrestha and Yang](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2015/aamas/p1431.pdf)
No result settling exactly the mass-transfer version was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified strict rankings, following the discrete preference domain.
  • Specified real transfer amounts, allowing arbitrary division of mass.
  • Required at least two candidates to exclude a vacuous election; allowed singleton support.
  • Specified mass-weighted Borda scores and strict unique-winner semantics, with no tie-breaking.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length, including the threshold; retained every positive rational threshold, including infeasible values above total mass.

Result

The mass-transfer decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

For each existing destination, a rational linear program can determine whether enough mass can move while all of \(p\)'s score differences remain strictly positive. The strict inequalities are handled by maximizing a common winning margin.

1. Score differences after a transfer

For ranking \(t\) and candidate \(c\), let \(\operatorname{pos}_t(c)\) be the position of \(c\), starting at \(1\), and define its Borda score by
\[ \]
\(b_t(c)=m-\operatorname{pos}_t(c)\).

For every opponent \(q\in C\setminus\{p\}\), define
\[ \]
\(a_{tq}=b_t(p)-b_t(q)\), and \(\Delta_q=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_{tq}\).

The premise that \(p\) is initially the unique winner means precisely that \(\Delta_q>0\) for every opponent \(q\).

Fix a destination \(d\in T\). Transferring mass \(x_t\) from ranking \(t\) to ranking \(d\) changes the score difference between \(p\) and \(q\) by \(x_t(a_{dq}-a_{tq})\). Indeed, the resulting difference is

\(\displaystyle M_{dq}(x) =\left(\mu_d+\sum_{t\ne d}x_t\right)a_{dq} +\sum_{t\ne d}(\mu_t-x_t)a_{tq} =\Delta_q+\sum_{t\ne d}(a_{dq}-a_{tq})x_t. \)

Consequently, \(p\) remains the unique Borda winner if and only if \(M_{dq}(x)>0\) for every \(q\ne p\).

2. A linear program for each destination

For destination \(d\), introduce the \(\tau-1\) transfer variables \(x_t\), together with one margin variable \(\varepsilon\). Consider the following linear program:

Maximize \(\varepsilon\), subject to

  • \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\in T\setminus\{d\}\);
  • \(\displaystyle\sum_{t\ne d}x_t\ge\kappa\);
  • \(\displaystyle\Delta_q+\sum_{t\ne d}(a_{dq}-a_{tq})x_t\ge\varepsilon\) for every \(q\in C\setminus\{p\}\);
  • \(0\le\varepsilon\le1\).

The bound \(\varepsilon\le1\) makes the feasible region bounded and does not affect whether a positive margin exists. All constraints are non-strict.

If this program is feasible, its feasible region is closed and bounded, hence compact. Therefore its objective attains a maximum; denote that maximum by \(\varepsilon_d^*\).

Lemma. A permitted successful transfer to \(d\) exists if and only if this program is feasible and \(\varepsilon_d^*>0\).

Proof. Suppose a successful transfer \(x\) exists. Its transferred mass meets the threshold and every opponent's margin is positive. Since there are finitely many opponents and \(m\ge2\), the number

\(\displaystyle \eta=\min_{q\ne p}M_{dq}(x)\)

exists and is positive. Set \(\varepsilon=\min\{1,\eta\}>0\). Then \((x,\varepsilon)\) satisfies every constraint, so \(\varepsilon_d^*\ge\varepsilon>0\).

Conversely, suppose the program is feasible and \(\varepsilon_d^*>0\). At an optimal solution, the transfer amounts obey their permitted bounds and meet the threshold. Every opponent satisfies \(M_{dq}(x)\ge\varepsilon_d^*>0\), so \(p\) is the unique winner. This proves the lemma.

In particular, an optimum of zero does not cause acceptance: it certifies only that nonnegative margins are attainable under the threshold constraint.

3. Polynomial encoding length and exact computation

We use the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: feasibility can be decided, and a finite optimal value computed exactly, in time polynomial in the binary encoding length of the linear program. This is polynomial time in the bit model, as established by Khachiyan's linear-programming theorem. [Khachiyan, *A polynomial algorithm in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

We check the required encoding bound explicitly.

Each \(a_{tq}\) is an integer satisfying \(|a_{tq}|\le m-1\), and thus \(|a_{dq}-a_{tq}|\le2(m-1)\). These coefficients require \(O(\log m)\) bits.

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=u_t/v_t\), with positive integer denominator, and let \(Q=\prod_{t\in T}v_t\). The binary length of \(Q\) is \(O(L)\), because the sum of the input denominators' binary lengths is at most \(L\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \Delta_q =\frac{\sum_{t\in T}u_t(Q/v_t)a_{tq}}{Q}. \)

Because the masses sum to one, \(|\Delta_q|\le m-1\). Its displayed integer numerator therefore has absolute value at most \(Q(m-1)\). Hence every \(\Delta_q\) has a rational representation using \(O(L+\log m)\) bits, computable by polynomial-time integer arithmetic.

Each destination's program has exactly \(\tau\) variables and \(2\tau+m\) inequalities. Its entire encoding length is therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). All hypotheses of the rational linear-programming theorem are satisfied.

Exact optimization distinguishes \(\varepsilon_d^*=0\) from \(\varepsilon_d^*>0\); no assumed numerical tolerance is needed.

4. Decision algorithm and conclusion

The algorithm is:

  • Compute the Borda differences \(a_{tq}\) and initial margins \(\Delta_q\).
  • For each \(d\in T\), solve its linear program.
  • Accept if some program is feasible with positive optimal margin; otherwise reject.

The lemma proves correctness for each destination. Enumerating all of \(T\) proves correctness for the full problem, since a permitted transfer must select one of these destinations.

There are \(\tau\) programs, each of polynomial encoding length. Thus the total running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). This also covers \(\tau=1\): the transfer sum is then zero, so the positive threshold makes the program infeasible.

The discrete hardness result does not conflict with this conclusion. Whole-voter transfers require integer transfer counts; the present problem permits arbitrary real amounts and therefore admits the linear programs above. The cited discrete Borda result is Theorem 6 of [Guo, Shrestha and Yang](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2015/aamas/p1431.pdf).
