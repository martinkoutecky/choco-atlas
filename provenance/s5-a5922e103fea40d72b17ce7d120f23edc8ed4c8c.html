The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite reportable type catalogue \(\mathcal T\) of size \(K\), with each type \(\theta\) specified by a rational budget \(B_\theta\) and a rational piecewise-linear, nondecreasing subadditive value curve \(v_\theta:[0,1]\to\mathbb R_{\ge 0}\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) with support size \(\tau\), let per-capita supply be a fixed \(\rho\in(0,1)\). Does there exist a mechanism, computable in time polynomial in \(K,\tau,L\), that maps each reported distribution to an allocation-and-payment menu \((q(\mu,r),p(\mu,r))\) for every report \(r\in\mathcal T\), is feasible with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta q(\mu,\theta)\le\rho\), individually rational and budget-feasible for truthful reports, and dominant-strategy truthful at fixed \(\mu\) for an infinitesimal agent? Can it guarantee \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\min\{v_\theta(q(\mu,\theta)),B_\theta\}\ge \bar W^*(\mu,\rho)/O(\log^2(2+\tau))\), where \(\bar W^*(\mu,\rho)=\max_{\sum_\theta\mu_\theta q_\theta\le\rho}\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\min\{v_\theta(q_\theta),B_\theta\}\)?

2. Preliminaries

Set
$$ f_\theta(x)=\min\{v_\theta(x),B_\theta\}. $$
This is a continuous, nondecreasing, rational piecewise-linear function.

Capping preserves subadditivity

For nonnegative $a,b,B$,
$$ \min\{a+b,B\}\le \min\{a,B\}+\min\{b,B\}. $$
Indeed, if either $a$ or $b$ is at least $B$, the right-hand side is at least $B$. Otherwise the right-hand side equals $a+b$.

Therefore, whenever $x+y\le1$,
$$ \begin{aligned} f_\theta(x+y) &\le \min\{v_\theta(x)+v_\theta(y),B_\theta\}\\ &\le f_\theta(x)+f_\theta(y). \end{aligned} $$

Repeated subadditivity consequently gives, for every positive integer $h$,
$$ f_\theta(x)\le h f_\theta(x/h). $$
All partial sums in this application lie in $[0,x]$, so the stated domain restriction on subadditivity is respected.

3. An auxiliary common-cap construction

$$ \mu_{\max}Q\le\frac{\rho}{2}. $$

Budget-constrained demand

$$ D_\theta(t)= \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{0\le x\le\min\{Q,B_\theta/t\}} \bigl(v_\theta(x)-tx\bigr). $$

$$ \ell_\theta(t)=\min D_\theta(t),\qquad h_\theta(t)=\max D_\theta(t). $$
The set need not be an interval.

Demand monotonicity. If $0<s<t$, $x\in D_\theta(s)$, and $y\in D_\theta(t)$, then $x\ge y$.

To prove this, suppose $y>x$. Because $y$ is affordable at price $t$, both $x$ and $y$ are affordable at both prices. Optimality gives
$$ v_\theta(x)-sx\ge v_\theta(y)-sy, $$
and
$$ v_\theta(y)-ty\ge v_\theta(x)-tx. $$
Adding yields
$$ (t-s)(x-y)\ge0, $$
a contradiction.

A low-price branch

For each type with $B_\theta>0$ and $v_\theta(Q)>0$, let $a_\theta$ be the smallest quantity attaining $v_\theta(Q)$. For all other types, put $a_\theta=0$.

There is a positive rational price $t_0$ for which
$$ D_\theta(t_0)=\{a_\theta\} $$
for every supported type.

$$ \frac{v_\theta(a_\theta)-v_\theta(x)}{a_\theta-x}>0. $$
Choose $t_0$ smaller than every such ratio and every positive ratio $B_\theta/a_\theta$, over all active types.

Then $a_\theta$ is affordable and gives strictly greater utility than every preceding graph vertex. Piecewise linearity gives the same strict comparison for every $x<a_\theta$. For $x>a_\theta$, monotonicity and the definition of $a_\theta$ imply
$$ v_\theta(x)=v_\theta(a_\theta), $$
so positive pricing makes $x$ strictly worse. Zero-budget types can afford only zero, and types with $v_\theta(Q)=0$ uniquely demand zero.

If
$$ \sum_{\theta\in S}\mu_\theta a_\theta\le\rho, $$
these demands give a feasible common-price menu attaining $f_\theta(Q)$ for every supported type. It is therefore optimal for $\bar W_Q^*$.

Henceforth suppose their aggregate demand exceeds $\rho$.

A price straddling the supply

At prices above every slope in the supported valuation descriptions, demand is zero. Define
$$ t_*= \inf\left\{ t\ge t_0: \sum_{\theta\in S}\mu_\theta\ell_\theta(t)\le\rho \right\}. $$
Demand monotonicity makes the set inside this infimum an upper set. In particular, $t_*\ge t_0>0$.

I claim that
$$ \sum_{\theta\in S}\mu_\theta\ell_\theta(t_*) \le\rho \le \sum_{\theta\in S}\mu_\theta h_\theta(t_*). $$

$$ z_n=\min\{z,B_\theta/t_n\} $$

$$ v_\theta(x_n)-t_nx_n \ge v_\theta(z_n)-t_nz_n $$
proves $x\in D_\theta(t)$.

Now take sequences approaching $t_*$ from above and below. Compactness permits simultaneous convergent subsequences for all supported demand choices. Above $t_*$, aggregate minimum demand is at most $\rho$; below $t_*$, it exceeds $\rho$. Their limiting demand vectors establish the two claimed inequalities.

Selecting tied demands without splitting a type

$$ \mu_\theta\bigl(h_\theta(t_*)-\ell_\theta(t_*)\bigr) \le\mu_\theta Q \le\frac{\rho}{2}. $$
Because aggregate maximum demand is at least $\rho$, the process reaches $\rho/2$. Its final aggregate allocation $S$ satisfies
$$ \frac{\rho}{2}\le S\le\rho. $$

Every selected quantity is a demand maximizer at the same price. Assign
$$ p_\theta=t_*d_\theta. $$
This is a supported truthful menu: any affordable supported outcome is among the quantities over which the type optimized. Comparison with zero gives individual rationality, and demand feasibility gives budget feasibility.

Welfare guarantee

$$ W=\sum_{\theta\in S}\mu_\theta f_\theta(d_\theta). $$

$$ f_\theta(d_\theta)\ge t_*d_\theta, $$
and hence
$$ W\ge t_*S\ge\frac{t_*\rho}{2}. $$

$$ f_\theta(d_\theta)\ge f_\theta(z_\theta)-t_*z_\theta. $$
If $t_*z_\theta>B_\theta$, the right-hand side is negative. Otherwise $z_\theta$ is affordable, and demand optimality gives
$$ v_\theta(d_\theta) \ge v_\theta(z_\theta)-t_*z_\theta+t_*d_\theta \ge f_\theta(z_\theta)-t_*z_\theta. $$
The final right-hand side is also at most $B_\theta$, so capping the left-hand side preserves the inequality.

$$ \bar W_Q^*\le W+t_*\rho\le3W. $$

Thus the auxiliary construction gives a $3$-approximation with real entries. The cap condition controls jumps without requiring connected demand sets.

4. Polynomial-time computation and rational outputs

Two issues require verification: finding the price exactly, and replacing possibly irrational entries by rational entries while preserving exact incentives.

Demand changes occur at algebraic prices of degree at most two

Insert $Q$ into each supported valuation description. At price $t>0$, a utility maximum occurs at an affordable graph vertex or at the budget boundary $B_\theta/t$, when that boundary lies in $[0,Q]$. Extreme demand maximizers also occur among these candidates.

For a fixed graph vertex $x$, utility is
$$ v_\theta(x)-tx. $$
If the budget boundary lies on a segment
$$ v_\theta(q)=aq+b, $$
its utility is
$$ \frac{aB_\theta}{t}+b-B_\theta. $$

The relevant price events are:

  • Affordability and budget-boundary segment changes, at prices $B_\theta/x$ for positive graph vertices $x$.
  • Equalities between utilities of two fixed vertices.
  • Equalities between a fixed vertex and a budget-boundary candidate.

The second kind has rational roots. The third kind is described by
$$ x t^2+\bigl(b-B_\theta-v_\theta(x)\bigr)t+aB_\theta=0. $$
Include every positive real root of every nonzero polynomial of these forms. Extraneous roots are harmless.

If the total number of supported graph vertices is $N$, there are $O(N^2)$ events, with rational coefficients of polynomial bit length.

Between consecutive events:

  • Candidate affordability and budget-boundary segments are fixed.
  • Utility comparisons have fixed signs, or hold identically.
  • The ordering of candidate quantities is fixed.

Consequently, each minimum or maximum demand is either a fixed rational vertex or $B_\theta/t$. Aggregate minimum demand has the form
$$ A+\frac{D}{t} $$
with rational $A,D$. An interior threshold price therefore solves
$$ A+\frac{D}{t}=\rho, $$
and is rational. A threshold at an event has algebraic degree at most two.

Real roots of bounded-degree rational polynomials can be isolated and ordered exactly in polynomial bit time. Distinct roots in this collection admit separating rational samples of polynomial bit length. Thus scanning the event points and intervening intervals finds $t_*$ and its extreme demands in polynomial time.

All quantities and payments in the auxiliary menu belong to the common field generated by $t_*$. That field has degree at most two. Exact comparisons, valuation evaluations, and the demand-selection procedure therefore have polynomial bit complexity.

A positive rational benchmark lower bound

Define
$$ V=\max_{\theta\in S}\mu_\theta f_\theta(Q). $$
Allocating $Q$ only to a maximizing type is feasible because
$$ \mu_\theta Q\le\rho/2. $$
Therefore
$$ V\le\bar W_Q^*. $$

If $V=0$, every $f_\theta(Q)=0$, and monotonicity implies $\bar W_Q^*=0$. The all-zero menu handles this case.

Suppose $V>0$. The auxiliary menu satisfies
$$ W\ge\bar W_Q^*/3\ge V/3. $$

Using exact algebraic comparisons and rational bisection, compute a rational number $R$ satisfying
$$ W-\frac{V}{12}\le R\le W. $$
This requires polynomial time: $V$ has polynomial bit length, and
$$ W\le\sum_{\theta\in S}\mu_\theta B_\theta $$
provides a rational upper bound of polynomial bit length. Furthermore,
$$ R\ge\frac34W\ge\frac14\bar W_Q^*. $$

A rational linear program preserves incentives

For each auxiliary quantity $d_j$, and each supported valuation $v_i$, choose a closed rational segment containing $d_j$ on which $v_i$ is affine. Also choose such a segment for $f_j$ at $d_j$.

Introduce variables
$$ q_j,\ p_j\quad(j\in S),\qquad \delta. $$
Constrain every $q_j$ to the intersection of its selected segments and to $[0,Q]$. On these intervals, write the selected affine formulas as
$$ v_i(q_j)=a_{ij}q_j+b_{ij},\qquad f_j(q_j)=c_jq_j+e_j. $$

Impose supply feasibility and individual constraints:
$$ \sum_{j\in S}\mu_jq_j\le\rho, $$
$$ 0\le p_j\le B_j,\qquad p_j\le a_{jj}q_j+b_{jj}. $$

For every pair $i,j$, classify affordability using the auxiliary payment $t_*d_j$.

If $t_*d_j\le B_i$, impose
$$ p_j\le B_i $$
and
$$ a_{ii}q_i+b_{ii}-p_i \ge a_{ij}q_j+b_{ij}-p_j. $$

If $t_*d_j>B_i$, impose
$$ p_j\ge B_i+\delta. $$

Finally impose
$$ 0\le\delta\le1,\qquad \sum_{j\in S}\mu_j(c_jq_j+e_j)\ge R, $$
and maximize $\delta$.

This is a rational linear program. The auxiliary menu is a real feasible point with some $\delta>0$: take the minimum of $1$ and its finitely many strictly positive unaffordability gaps. If there are no unaffordable pairs, take $\delta=1$.

Hence the optimum has $\delta>0$. Exact rational linear programming produces a rational optimal solution in polynomial time. Its incentive constraints are exact:

  • Pairs classified affordable remain affordable and satisfy their utility inequalities.
  • Pairs classified unaffordable remain strictly unaffordable.

The segment constraints ensure that every affine formula used is the actual valuation or capped valuation at the resulting quantity. The final supported menu therefore satisfies all required constraints and has welfare at least
$$ R\ge\bar W_Q^*/4. $$

The program has $2\tau+1$ variables, $O(\tau^2)$ constraints, and polynomial coefficient bit length. Extending its menu to unsupported reports completes the mechanism in $\operatorname{poly}(m,K,\tau,L)$ time, with $m=1$. Fixing orders and deterministic optimization and tie rules makes the mechanism deterministic.

5. Comparing with the unrestricted benchmark

Now choose
$$ Q=\min\left\{1,\frac{\rho}{2\mu_{\max}}\right\}, $$
which satisfies the cap condition used above.

Let $(q_\theta^*)_{\theta\in S}$ be an unrestricted benchmark optimizer. Such an optimizer exists because the feasible region is compact and the objective is continuous. Supply feasibility implies, separately for every supported type,
$$ q_\theta^*\le\min\{1,\rho/\mu_\theta\}. $$

Put
$$ \beta=\frac{\mu_{\max}}{\mu_{\min}},\qquad h=\lceil2\beta\rceil. $$
If $Q=1$, the capped and unrestricted benchmarks coincide. Otherwise,
$$ \frac{q_\theta^*}{Q} \le\frac{2\mu_{\max}}{\mu_\theta} \le2\beta\le h. $$

Define
$$ z_\theta=\min\{q_\theta^*,Q\}. $$
These quantities remain supply-feasible. Subadditivity and monotonicity give
$$ f_\theta(q_\theta^*) \le h f_\theta(q_\theta^*/h) \le h f_\theta(z_\theta). $$
The last inequality follows because $q_\theta^*/h\le Q$ and $q_\theta^*/h\le q_\theta^*$.

Therefore
$$ \bar W_Q^* \ge\sum_{\theta\in S}\mu_\theta f_\theta(z_\theta) \ge\frac{\bar W^*}{h}. $$
Combining this with the common-cap theorem proves
$$ W(\mu)\ge \frac{\bar W^*(\mu,\rho)} {4\lceil2\mu_{\max}/\mu_{\min}\rceil}. $$

For equal masses, $\beta=1$, yielding the promised $8$-approximation. If $\mu_{\max}\le\rho/2$, then $Q=1$, yielding the stronger $4$-approximation.

For completeness, when $\tau=1$, assigning $(\rho,0)$ to every catalogue report is exactly optimal: the unique supported type has mass one, and monotonicity makes quantity $\rho$ benchmark-optimal.

6. What remains open

The proved approximation depends on the mass ratio. That ratio can be arbitrarily large even when $\tau=2$, so it cannot be bounded by a function of $\log(2+\tau)$.

The outstanding difficulty is therefore more specific than nonconcavity alone: it is handling heterogeneous nonconcave valuations together with arbitrarily unequal supported masses. The common cap controls incentive-compatible demand jumps, but truncating rare types at that cap can lose too much benchmark welfare.

The finite-agent deterministic logarithmic theorem provides relevant background, but does not establish the required distribution-menu guarantee. [Dobzinski–Paes Leme](https://arxiv.org/html/1304.7048).

No impossibility excluding the requested approximation follows from the results proved here.
