Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be finite supports of complete preference types over opposite-side categories, including acceptability, with men's types allowed ties and women's types strict. Give each side total mass \(1\), with binary-rational type masses \(\mu_x\) and \(\nu_y\). A flow \(\pi_{xy}\geq 0\) has row and column sums bounded by those masses; it is weakly stable if no mutually acceptable pair \((x,y)\) has positive mass on each side assigned below the other, counting unmatched mass as below every acceptable type. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\nu)\) be the maximum total matched mass of a stable flow. Is there a polynomial-time anonymous algorithm returning a stable flow of mass at least \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\nu)/1.5\), such that for every man type \(x\) with \(\mu_x>0\), the truthful lottery \(\pi_{xy}/\mu_x\) stochastically dominates the limiting lottery after moving \(\epsilon\) mass from \(x\) to any reported type \(x'\) and taking \(\epsilon\downarrow0\)? $$ (n-u)p(u)+u\,r(u)=nk. $$

Conversely, transfer mass $\delta$ from $x_T$ to report $x_F$. Category $b$ is also the true top category of $x_T$, so the left limit exists and
$$ r(u)\geq p(u-), \qquad p(u-):=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}p(u-\delta). $$

These limits are taken along admissible rational perturbations.

1.3. These inequalities force exact equality

$$ p(u)=r(u)=k=\frac12. $$

$$ p(u_0-)\leq r(u_0)<k. $$

$$ p(v)<k $$
for every admissible $v$.

$$ r(v+h)>k. $$

$$ p(v)\geq r(v+)\geq k, $$

$$ p(u)\geq r(u+)\geq k. $$

3. A matching upper bound with one male category and two female categories

Assume now that
$$ A=\{a\},\qquad |B|=2. $$

A female type either accepts $a$ or accepts nobody. For each female category $b$, define its usable supply
$$ C_b= \sum_{\substack{y\in Y\\b(y)=b\\y\text{ accepts }a}}\nu_y. $$

Categories with $C_b=0$ are unavailable.

3.1. Mechanism

Run the following eating process over $t\in[0,1]$.

Each male type $x$ has aggregate eating rate $\mu_x$. It divides this rate equally among its most preferred acceptable categories that still have positive residual usable supply. If none remains, its eating rate is zero.

Let $q_x(b)$ be the amount eaten from category $b$, divided by $\mu_x$. The unmatched probability is
$$ q_x(\varnothing)=1-\sum_bq_x(b). $$

Distribute consumption proportionally among the accepting female types within each category:
$$ \pi_{xy} = \mu_xq_x(b(y))\frac{\nu_y}{C_{b(y)}} $$
when $y$ accepts $a$ and $C_{b(y)}>0$. All other entries are zero.

The rule is deterministic, treats every member of a reported type identically, and is independent of support-list order.

3.2. Feasibility and stability

Each normalized male eating rate is at most one, for at most one unit of time. Category consumption never exceeds its usable supply. Proportional distribution therefore respects every male and female capacity and uses only mutually acceptable pairs.

For stability, consider a mutually acceptable pair $(x,y)$, where $b(y)=b$. Since there is only one male category, the only feasible outcome that $y$ regards as strictly below $x$ is unmatched.

If $y$ has unmatched mass, proportional distribution implies that category $b$ has residual supply at time one. Thus $b$ was available throughout the process. Type $x$ consequently ate at normalized rate one throughout, always from categories weakly preferred to $b$. Its final lottery has no mass strictly below $b$.

Hence the pair cannot have positive mass strictly below the prospective partner on both sides. The flow is weakly stable.

3.3. Deviation limits exist

Fix a society, a positive-mass type $x$, and a permitted report $x'$. Include $x'$ as a zero-mass type initially if necessary.

For an available-category set $D$, let
$$ u_{zb}(D) $$
be type $z$’s normalized eating rate from $b$. Its values are determined by the report and $D$. The aggregate rate is
$$ R_b(D)=\sum_z\mu_z u_{zb}(D). $$

Under the perturbation,
$$ R_b^\delta(D)-R_b(D) = \delta\bigl(u_{x'b}(D)-u_{xb}(D)\bigr). $$
Thus every rate for a fixed available set converges to its original value.

There are at most two category-exhaustion events. Their behavior under perturbation is as follows.

  • An initially zero consumption rate cannot cause a positive fixed supply to exhaust during $[0,1]$ under a sufficiently small perturbation: its perturbed rate is bounded by a constant times $\delta$.
  • A unique first exhaustion time converges, because it is a ratio of a fixed positive supply to a positive consumption rate. The surviving category has positive residual supply at that event, so the first exhausted category remains the same for sufficiently small perturbations. Its subsequent exhaustion time, if relevant, also converges by the same ratio calculation.
  • If both categories exhaust simultaneously, their original pre-exhaustion rates are positive. At the first perturbed exhaustion, the other category has residual supply bounded by a constant times $\delta$. Removing its competitor cannot decrease its consumption rate: a type previously eating that category either continues at its old rate or increases its rate. The second category therefore exhausts within a time bounded by a constant times $\delta$ of the first.

Events at time one are handled by restricting these event neighborhoods to $[0,1]$. It follows that the perturbed and original available-category sets agree outside intervals whose total length tends to zero.

The receiving report’s normalized rates are bounded by one. Therefore
$$ q_{x'}^\delta(b) \longrightarrow \int_0^1u_{x'b}(D(t))\,dt, $$
where $D(t)$ is the original availability process.

Proportional distribution uses fixed female masses and fixed $C_b$, so every female-type coordinate converges as well. The unmatched coordinate converges by normalization.

3.4. Limiting stochastic dominance

Let $U$ consist of the first several acceptable tiers of $x$’s true preference order.

Against the original availability process, truthful type $x$ eats in $U$ at normalized rate one exactly when some category of $U$ is available. Otherwise its rate in $U$ is zero.

Every alternative report eats in $U$ at rate at most that same indicator. Integrating gives
$$ \sum_{b\in U}q_x(b) \geq \sum_{b\in U}\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}q_{x'}^\delta(b). $$

The upper contour containing all truly acceptable categories and unmatched has truthful probability one, since the truthful process never assigns an unacceptable category. Its dominance inequality is therefore automatic. The contour containing all outcomes is also automatic.

These are all upper contours of the true weak order. Thus the truthful lottery stochastically dominates the deviation limit, including when the deviation changes acceptability.

3.5. Polynomial running time

There are at most two exhaustion events. Computing aggregate rates and the final flow uses
$$ O(\tau^2) $$
rational arithmetic operations, besides reading the input.

For an explicit bit bound, take a common denominator $D$ for all input masses by multiplying their denominators. Then
$$ \log_2D\leq L. $$
Because at most two categories share a type’s eating rate, aggregate rates have denominators dividing $2D$.

The first exhaustion duration is a ratio of numbers with $O(L)$ bits. Updating residual supplies uses a bounded number of additions and multiplications of such rationals; the second duration does likewise. Since there are only two events, all these numbers have $O(L+\log\tau)$ bits. The final proportional allocations add only a bounded number of rational multiplications and divisions per entry and have the same asymptotic bit bound.

Using elementary integer arithmetic, the running time is
$$ O\!\left(\tau^2(L+\log\tau)^2\right), $$
up to input-reading costs. In particular, it is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

4. The eating mechanism achieves $\frac43$ on this restricted domain

Let $N$ be the total mass of men accepting at least one category with positive usable supply.

If all usable categories exhaust by time one, the mechanism consumes all usable supply. No feasible flow can exceed that mass.

If no usable category exhausts before time one, every active man eats throughout $[0,1]$. The mechanism matches mass $N$, which no feasible flow can exceed.

The remaining case has exactly one category, say $a_0$, exhausting at time
$$ 0<t<1, $$
while the other category remains available at time one.

Let:

  • $\alpha$ be the male mass whose only acceptable usable category is $a_0$;
  • $\beta$ be the male mass accepting the surviving category;
  • $C$ be the usable supply of $a_0$.

Then $N=\alpha+\beta$. Every man counted in $\beta$ is fully matched. Every man counted in $\alpha$ eats until time $t$ and then stops. Hence the mechanism’s matched mass is
$$ M=\beta+\alpha t. $$

Before the first exhaustion, the consumption rate $R$ of $a_0$ is constant and satisfies $R\leq N$. Since $C=Rt$,
$$ t\geq\frac CN. $$
Therefore
$$ M\geq\beta+\frac{\alpha C}{N}. $$

Every feasible flow matches at most $\beta$ from the second group and at most $\min(\alpha,C)$ from the first. Consequently,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\leq\beta+\min(\alpha,C). $$

Set
$$ z=\frac\alpha N,\qquad h=\frac CN. $$
The two bounds become
$$ M\geq N(1-z+zh), \qquad \mathrm{OPT}\leq N(1-z+\min(z,h)). $$

If $h\geq z$, then
$$ M\geq N(1-z+z^2) = N\left(\left(z-\frac12\right)^2+\frac34\right) \geq\frac34N \geq\frac34\mathrm{OPT}. $$

If $h\leq z$, subtracting three times the upper bound from four times the lower bound gives
$$ N\bigl((1-z)+(4z-3)h\bigr). $$
For $z\geq\frac34$, this is nonnegative. For $z<\frac34$, the coefficient of $h$ is negative, so $h\leq z$ gives
$$ (1-z)+(4z-3)h \geq (1-z)+(4z-3)z = (2z-1)^2 \geq0. $$

Thus in every case,
$$ M\geq\frac34\mathrm{OPT}. $$

Together with Section 2, this proves that $\frac43$ is the optimal approximation ratio when $|A|=1$ and $|B|=2$.

5. Why the same eating rule does not establish the general $\frac32$ upper bound

Consider one male category and sixteen female categories:
$$ A=\{a\},\qquad B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_{16}\}. $$

Male type $x_i$ has mass $\frac1{16}$ and preferences
$$ b_1\succ b_2\succ\cdots\succ b_i, $$
with all remaining categories unacceptable. Female type $y_i$ has category $b_i$, mass $\frac1{16}$, and accepts only $a$.

A perfect stable flow assigns $x_i$ to $y_i$. Every female column is full and all assigned men have category $a$, so no woman has mass strictly below any acceptable prospective partner. Therefore
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=1. $$

Apply the same eating rule. Initially all men eat $b_1$. After $b_1$ exhausts, $x_1$ stops and all remaining men eat $b_2$. This continues in order.

The time when the first $k$ categories have exhausted is
$$ T_k=\sum_{j=16-k+1}^{16}\frac1j. $$
Exact arithmetic gives
$$ T_{10}=\frac{134159}{144144}<1, \qquad T_{11}=\frac{158183}{144144}>1. $$

Thus the process fully consumes the first ten categories, then consumes category $b_{11}$ at aggregate rate $\frac6{16}$ until time one. Its matched mass is
$$ M = \frac{10}{16} +\frac6{16}(1-T_{10}) = \frac{250225}{384384}. $$
Since
$$ \frac23-M=\frac{6031}{384384}>0, $$
this is below the requested $\frac23\mathrm{OPT}$ guarantee.

This calculation rules out extending this eating rule unchanged as a proof of the general upper bound. It does not rule out other mechanisms.

The lower bound now excludes every ratio strictly below $\frac43$, using the task’s precise limiting deviation model. The matching upper bound is proved only for one male category and two female categories.

Neither result settles whether a polynomial-time $\frac32$ mechanism exists for arbitrary category sets, or whether every ratio in $[\frac43,\frac32)$ is impossible.
