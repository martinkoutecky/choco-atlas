The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a fixed experiment protocol, a finite support of revealed-preference graph types \(G_t\) on its observation set, rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, and rational \(0\le\epsilon<1\), determine the least integer \(d\ge1\) such that \(\sum_{t:\operatorname{RPDim}(G_t)\le d}\mu_t\ge1-\epsilon\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An experiment protocol specifies a common labeled observation set \(V=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(m\ge1\) part of the input. Types are loopless directed graphs on \(V\), supplied as adjacency matrices; cycles are allowed. Realizations may differ independently across types, with no prescribed numerical prices or bundles.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \(G_t\) with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including the binary rational \(0\le\epsilon<1\); state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
An RP realization of \(G=(V,E)\) in dimension \(d\ge1\) consists of real vectors \(p_i\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^d\setminus\{0\}\) and \(x_i\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^d\). For distinct \(i,j\), require \(\langle p_i,x_i-x_j\rangle>0\) when \((i,j)\in E\), and \(\langle p_i,x_i-x_j\rangle<0\) otherwise; off-diagonal expenditure ties are excluded. Define \(\operatorname{RPDim}(G)\) as the least such positive integer dimension.
Define \(D_\epsilon=\min\{d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}:\sum_{t:\operatorname{RPDim}(G_t)\le d}\mu_t\ge1-\epsilon\}\).

Is computing \(D_\epsilon\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a single graph, the discrete characterization is \(\operatorname{RPDim}(G)=\operatorname{RPDim}(G^+)=\operatorname{signrank}(M(G^+))-1\): \(G^+\) adds vertices \(s,t\) and exactly the new edges \((s,v),(v,t),(s,t)\) for \(v\in V\); \(M(H)\) has zero diagonal, positive signs on edges, and negative signs elsewhere. Sign rank is the minimum rank of a real matrix with those prescribed signs. [Discrete characterization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.10878).
Computing RP dimension was explicitly left open. [Discrete complexity discussion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.10878). Deciding sign rank at most \(3\) for arbitrary matrices with only positive and negative signs is NP-hard. [Sign-rank hardness](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.04486).

Result

Computing $D_\epsilon$ is NP-hard. More precisely, deciding whether $D_\epsilon\le 3$ is NP-hard even when $\tau=1$, $\mu_1=1$, and $\epsilon=0$.

The proof constructs an RP graph from a planar point–line realizability instance. Duplicated point vertices allow us to extract a planar realization from any three-dimensional RP realization. Additional vertices and carefully chosen signs make the converse hold as well.

1. The NP-hard source problem

A simple pseudoline arrangement consists of $n$ pseudolines such that every pair crosses exactly once and no three meet. Each pseudoline has a designated positive and negative side. Every two-dimensional cell therefore has a sign vector in $\{-,+\}^n$.

We use two known results:

  • Deciding whether a simple pseudoline arrangement is combinatorially equivalent to an arrangement of straight lines is NP-hard. The arrangement can be supplied combinatorially, for example by a wiring diagram. This is simple stretchability. [Shor’s hardness theorem](https://math.mit.edu/~shor/papers/Stretchability_NP-hard.pdf).
  • For simple arrangements, the complete set of two-dimensional cell sign vectors determines the combinatorial equivalence class, up to reorienting individual pseudolines. [Bhangale–Kopparty, Lemma 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.04486).

Let $P$ be a source arrangement. Enumerate its cell sign vectors as
$$ S=\{\sigma^1,\ldots,\sigma^N\}\subseteq\{-,+\}^n, \qquad N=1+\frac{n(n+1)}2. $$
These vectors can be obtained in polynomial time from the subdivision described by a wiring diagram: adjacent cells differ in the coordinate of the wire separating them.

Call $S$ planarly realizable if there are nonconstant affine functions
$$ f_i(y)=b_i-a_i\cdot y, \qquad a_i\in\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}, $$
and points $y_1,\ldots,y_N\in\mathbb R^2$ such that
$$ \operatorname{sign}(f_i(y_j))=\sigma_i^j \qquad \text{for every }i,j. $$

Then
$$ P\text{ is stretchable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\text{ is planarly realizable}. $$

The forward implication follows by taking representative points in the cells of a straight-line realization.

For the reverse implication, an arrangement of $n$ affine lines has at most $N$ regions. Indeed, its $k$th line intersects the previous lines in at most $k-1$ distinct points and therefore creates at most $k$ new regions. Equality requires distinct lines, no parallel pair, and no triple intersection.

Distinct realized sign vectors lie in distinct regions: the set realizing any fixed strict sign vector is an intersection of open halfplanes and is consequently convex. Thus realizing all $N$ vectors forces exactly $N$ regions and a simple line arrangement whose complete region-sign set is $S$. The reconstruction result above applies to two simple arrangements, proving stretchability.

We also obtain the following useful data from $P$. For each coordinate $i$, choose two cells adjacent across a segment of pseudoline $i$. Their vectors, denoted
$$ \sigma^{u_i},\sigma^{v_i}, $$
differ exactly in coordinate $i$. For $k\ne i$, define their common sign by
$$ \alpha_{ki}=\sigma_k^{u_i}=\sigma_k^{v_i}. $$

The reduction below uses only $S$ and these adjacent pairs.

2. Construction of the graph

Construct a loopless directed graph $G$ with vertices
$$ r_1,\ldots,r_n,\quad o,\quad c_1^-,\ldots,c_N^-,\quad c_1^+,\ldots,c_N^+. $$
Thus its number of vertices is
$$ m=n+2N+1=n^2+2n+3. $$

Assign distinct auxiliary heights
$$ h(r_i)=\frac{i}{n+1}, \qquad h(o)=0, \qquad h(c_j^-)=-j, \qquad h(c_j^+)=j+1. $$
These heights specify graph edges; they are not prescribed realization data.

Define every off-diagonal edge as follows.

  • For a vertex $w$ equal to $o$ or any $c_j^\pm$, put

$$ (w,z)\in E(G) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h(w)>h(z). $$

  • From a vertex $r_i$, put

$$ (r_i,c_j^-),(r_i,c_j^+)\in E(G) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sigma_i^j=+. $$

  • Put

$$ (r_i,o)\in E(G) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sigma_i^1=+. $$

  • For $i\ne k$, put

$$ (r_i,r_k)\in E(G) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha_{ik}=+. $$

These rules specify every ordered pair of distinct vertices.

We will prove
$$ S\text{ is planarly realizable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{RPDim}(G)\le 3. $$

3. From a planar realization to an RP realization

Assume $S$ has a planar realization by functions $f_i(y)=b_i-a_i\cdot y$ and points $y_j$.

Choosing the line vertices’ bundles

For each $i$, the values of $f_i$ at $y_{u_i}$ and $y_{v_i}$ have opposite signs. Hence their segment contains a point $q_i$ satisfying
$$ f_i(q_i)=0. $$

For $k\ne i$, the values of $f_k$ at both endpoints have sign $\alpha_{ki}$. An affine function at a point of the segment is a convex combination of its endpoint values. Therefore
$$ \operatorname{sign}(f_k(q_i))=\alpha_{ki}. $$
In particular, $q_i$ lies on none of the other lines.

Assign preliminary bundles in $\mathbb R^3$:
$$ x_{r_i}=(q_i,h(r_i)), \qquad x_o=(y_1,0), \qquad x_{c_j^\pm}=(y_j,h(c_j^\pm)). $$
Write $Y_w\in\mathbb R^2$ for the first two coordinates of $x_w$.

For every $i$ and every $w\ne r_i$, the value $f_i(Y_w)$ is nonzero. Its sign is exactly the sign prescribed for the ordered pair $(r_i,w)$:

  • it is $\sigma_i^j$ at $c_j^\pm$;
  • it is $\sigma_i^1$ at $o$;
  • it is $\alpha_{ik}$ at $r_k$, where $k\ne i$.

Choosing prices and preserving the signs

Choose a positive real number $\delta$ satisfying
$$ 0<\delta< \min_{\substack{1\le i\le n\\w\ne r_i}} \frac{|f_i(Y_w)|}{1+|h(r_i)-h(w)|}. $$
The minimum is positive because it ranges over finitely many strictly positive quantities.

Set preliminary prices
$$ p_{r_i}=(a_i,\delta), \qquad p_o=p_{c_j^\pm}=(0,0,1). $$

Since $a_i\cdot q_i=b_i$, for $w\ne r_i$ we have
$$ \begin{aligned} p_{r_i}\cdot(x_{r_i}-x_w) &=a_i\cdot(q_i-Y_w)+\delta(h(r_i)-h(w))\\ &=f_i(Y_w)+\delta(h(r_i)-h(w)). \end{aligned} $$
Our choice of $\delta$ makes the perturbation strictly smaller in absolute value than $|f_i(Y_w)|$. Thus this expenditure difference has precisely the required sign.

For $w$ equal to $o$ or a point vertex,
$$ p_w\cdot(x_w-x_z)=h(w)-h(z). $$
The heights are distinct, so these differences are nonzero and have the prescribed signs. All off-diagonal comparisons are therefore satisfied.

Making all prices and bundles nonnegative

The preliminary prices may have negative first or second coordinates. Every price, however, has a strictly positive third coordinate.

Choose $K>0$ large enough that, for every preliminary price $p$,
$$ p_1+Kp_3>0, \qquad p_2+Kp_3>0. $$
Define
$$ T= \begin{pmatrix} 1&0&K\\ 0&1&K\\ 0&0&1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \widehat p_w=Tp_w, \qquad \widehat x_w=T^{-T}x_w. $$
Every coordinate of every $\widehat p_w$ is positive. Moreover,
$$ \widehat p_w\cdot(\widehat x_w-\widehat x_z) =p_w\cdot(x_w-x_z). $$

Finally, choose a common vector $v\in\mathbb R^3$ large enough coordinatewise that
$$ \widetilde x_w=\widehat x_w+v\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^3 $$
for every vertex $w$. Translation does not change bundle differences. Hence the pairs $(\widehat p_w,\widetilde x_w)$ satisfy all requirements of an RP realization.

This proves
$$ S\text{ planarly realizable} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{RPDim}(G)\le3. $$

The numerical choices above establish existence only. The reduction outputs just the graph’s adjacency matrix.

4. Extracting a planar realization from an RP realization

Conversely, suppose $G$ has an RP realization in dimension at most $3$. Append zero coordinates if necessary, obtaining vectors in $\mathbb R^3$.

For every vertex $w$, define the affine function
$$ F_w(x)=p_w\cdot(x_w-x). $$
Because $p_o\ne0$, the set
$$ H=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:F_o(x)=0\} $$
is an affine plane.

For every $j$, the graph construction gives
$$ (o,c_j^-)\in E(G), \qquad (o,c_j^+)\notin E(G). $$
Therefore
$$ F_o(x_{c_j^-})>0, \qquad F_o(x_{c_j^+})<0. $$
Their segment meets $H$ at a point $z_j$ strictly between its endpoints.

For every $i$, the two ordered pairs $(r_i,c_j^-)$ and $(r_i,c_j^+)$ have the same prescribed sign $\sigma_i^j$. Thus
$$ F_{r_i}(x_{c_j^-}) \quad\text{and}\quad F_{r_i}(x_{c_j^+}) $$
both have sign $\sigma_i^j$. Since $F_{r_i}$ is affine, its value at $z_j$ is a convex combination of these two values. Consequently,
$$ \operatorname{sign}(F_{r_i}(z_j))=\sigma_i^j. $$

Each coordinate $i$ takes both signs among the vectors in $S$, as witnessed by the chosen adjacent pair. Hence $F_{r_i}|_H$ takes both positive and negative values and is nonconstant.

Identify $H$ with $\mathbb R^2$ by any affine coordinate system. The functions $F_{r_i}|_H$ and points $z_j$ give a planar realization of $S$. This proves the reverse implication:
$$ \operatorname{RPDim}(G)\le3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad S\text{ planarly realizable}. $$

Combining both directions with Section 1,
$$ P\text{ is stretchable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{RPDim}(G)\le3. $$

5. Polynomial size and transfer to the quantile

The source has $N=O(n^2)$ cell sign vectors, and the graph has $m=O(n^2)$ vertices.

The vectors and adjacent pairs can be extracted in polynomial time from the source diagram. Alternatively, once $S$ is listed, comparing all pairs of its vectors finds the required adjacent pairs using $O(nN^2)$ sign comparisons. Constructing the adjacency matrix then uses $O(m^2)$ comparisons of signs or indices. A conservative bit-operation bound for these latter steps is
$$ O\!\left((nN^2+m^2)\log(n+N+1)\right), $$
which is polynomial in the source size. The output adjacency matrix has $m^2$ entries.

Now label the graph vertices by $\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and construct the society
$$ \tau=1,\qquad G_1=G,\qquad \mu_1=1,\qquad\epsilon=0. $$
These masses and tolerance satisfy all input requirements, and
$$ D_0=\operatorname{RPDim}(G). $$
Thus
$$ P\text{ is stretchable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D_0\le3. $$

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction to the quantile threshold problem. An algorithm computing $D_\epsilon$ would decide that threshold by computing its output and comparing it with $3$.

Therefore computing $D_\epsilon$ is NP-hard, and a running time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$ would imply $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$.

6. Finite range and the distributional overhead

For completeness, the quantile always exists. Every graph on $m$ vertices has an RP realization in dimension $m$: take $x_i=e_i$ and set
$$ (p_i)_j= \begin{cases} 1,&j=i,\\ 0,&(i,j)\in E,\\ 2,&j\ne i\text{ and }(i,j)\notin E. \end{cases} $$
Then, for $i\ne j$,
$$ p_i\cdot(x_i-x_j)=1-(p_i)_j, $$
which is $1$ on edges and $-1$ on nonedges. Appending zero coordinates preserves realizability. Consequently,
$$ 1\le D_\epsilon\le m. $$
In particular, $m=1$ is trivial; the hardness concerns variable $m$.

The support size introduces only polynomial aggregation work. Given an oracle deciding $\operatorname{RPDim}(G)\le d$, one threshold evaluation for $D_\epsilon$ uses $\tau$ oracle calls followed by an exact mass comparison. Binary search uses at most
$$ \tau\lceil\log_2m\rceil $$
oracle calls.

To justify the arithmetic bound, write the masses and tolerance with integer denominators and multiply all comparisons by their denominator product $Q$. Its bit length is $O(L)$. Schoolbook integer arithmetic gives $O(\tau L^2)$ preprocessing and $O(\tau L)$ per mass comparison. Including graph handling, the additional work is bounded by
$$ O\!\left(\tau m^2+\tau L^2+\tau L\log(m+1)\right). $$

The hardness proved above already holds with singleton support, so it does not depend on this aggregation step.
