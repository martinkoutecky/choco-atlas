The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite slot catalogue \(G\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) over complete dichotomous valuation types \(v_t\), and rational per-capita capacities \(\kappa_g\) for repeated indivisible slot copies, construct a mass allocation \(x_{t,B,p}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(B\) and subsidies \(p\in\{0,1\}\) satisfying \(\sum_{B,p}x_{t,B,p}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B,p}x_{t,B,p}B_g=\kappa_g\), and \(v_t(B)+p\ge v_t(B')+p'\) whenever \(x_{t,B,p}>0\) and \(x_{u,B',p'}>0\); determine whether this can be done exactly in time polynomial in the catalogue, type, capacity, and encoding sizes without expanding the clone population.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(G\) be a finite slot catalogue, \(m=|G|\), with binary-rational per-capita capacities \(\kappa_g\ge0\). An integral bundle is a multiplicity vector \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\); copies of the same slot are interchangeable, and a bundle may contain several copies.
Each type has an exact value oracle for \(v_t:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), with \(v_t(0)=0\) and \(v_t(B+e_g)-v_t(B)\in\{0,1\}\) for every bundle and slot, where \(e_g\) adds one copy of slot \(g\). No additivity or submodularity is assumed.
An allocation is a finitely supported family of rational masses \(x_{t,B,p}\ge0\), with \(p\in\{0,1\}\), satisfying \(\sum_{B,p}x_{t,B,p}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B,p}x_{t,B,p}B_g=\kappa_g\) for every type and slot. Empty bundles are allowed; there is no additional total-subsidy budget.
Require \(v_t(B)+p\ge v_t(B')+p'\) whenever \(x_{t,B,p}>0\) and \(x_{u,B',p'}>0\), including comparisons within the same type. Thus envy-freeness applies to every realized bundle and subsidy, rather than expected utilities.
Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the rational input. Bundle coordinates and output masses are encoded in binary; running time includes query encoding and ordinary bit operations, with exact value-oracle calls charged as oracle operations.

Can a feasible allocation be constructed, or infeasibility determined, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) in this value-oracle model, without expanding a clone population; or is the problem \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even for types whose values are evaluable in polynomial time?

For finitely many agents and explicitly listed indivisible goods, normalized dichotomous valuations always admit a complete envy-free allocation with subsidies in \(\{0,1\}\), computable in polynomial time with value-oracle access ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.07419v1)).
No result settling the exact continuous problem specified above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive type masses to sum to one, interpreting capacities as supplies per unit population.
  • Interpreted repeated slot copies as interchangeable and bundles as unrestricted nonnegative integer multiplicity vectors; no per-person copy limit was specified.
  • Extended dichotomous valuations to the entire multiset domain by requiring every additional-copy marginal to be zero or one, and retained normalization at the empty bundle.
  • Retained the anchor's exact value-oracle model because no finite valuation encoding was supplied; specified bit accounting and restricted the hardness alternative to polynomial-time evaluable types.
  • Required a finite rational allocation representation with binary-encoded bundle multiplicities and masses, and allowed an infeasibility answer because continuous feasibility was not established.
  • Made explicit that envy comparisons include distinct allocations within one type and that no total-subsidy budget is imposed.

1. Removing a common integral bundle

Write
$$ K_g=\lfloor\kappa_g\rfloor, \qquad \rho_g=\kappa_g-K_g, $$
so that $0\leq\rho_g<1$. Define
$$ w_t(C)=v_t(K+C)-v_t(K). $$

Adding copies one at a time proves monotonicity of $v_t$. Consequently, $w_t$ is nonnegative, normalized, and has marginals in $\{0,1\}$.

A feasible allocation for the shifted valuations and capacities $\rho$ gives a feasible allocation for the original instance by replacing every bundle $C$ with $K+C$. Indeed, the capacity becomes
$$ \sum_{t,C,p}x_{t,C,p}(K_g+C_g) = K_g\sum_t\mu_t+\rho_g = \kappa_g. $$
For every comparing type $t$, this replacement adds the same constant $v_t(K)$ to both sides of every envy inequality.

All constructions below therefore concern the residual instance. If $G$ is empty, giving every type the empty bundle with subsidy zero solves the problem immediately.

2. A polynomial construction from a finite bundle menu

Lemma 1: menus with value variation at most one

Suppose we are given integral residual bundles
$$ Q_1,\ldots,Q_k $$
and nonnegative rational frequencies $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{a=1}^k\lambda_a=1, \qquad \sum_{a=1}^k\lambda_aQ_a=\rho. $$
Suppose also that, for every type,
$$ \max_a w_t(Q_a)-\min_a w_t(Q_a)\leq 1. $$

Then a feasible allocation using these bundles and subsidies in $\{0,1\}$ can be constructed in time polynomial in the encoded menu, $m,\tau,L$, using at most $\tau k$ value queries.

Proof

Discard zero-frequency bundles and merge duplicates. Query
$$ a_{t,a}=w_t(Q_a). $$
Solve the rational transportation linear program
$$ \max\sum_{t,a}a_{t,a}y_{t,a} $$
subject to
$$ \sum_a y_{t,a}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_t y_{t,a}=\lambda_a, \qquad y_{t,a}\geq 0. $$
It is feasible because $y_{t,a}=\mu_t\lambda_a$ satisfies the constraints. The feasible region is bounded, so an optimum exists. Standard polynomial-time rational linear programming produces an optimal solution of polynomial encoding length.

Create one graph vertex $i$ for every positive cell of this solution. Write its type and bundle as $t_i$ and $Q_i$. Put
$$ c_{ij}=w_{t_i}(Q_j)-w_{t_i}(Q_i) $$
on every directed edge.

There is no positive-weight directed cycle. Otherwise, take a simple positive cycle and reassign a sufficiently small positive rational mass along it: the mass removed from vertex $i$ receives the next vertex’s bundle, retaining type $t_i$. Type totals and bundle frequencies remain unchanged. Choosing the transferred mass no larger than the smallest mass on the cycle preserves nonnegativity. The objective increases by the transferred mass times the cycle weight, contradicting optimality.

Every closed walk consequently has nonpositive weight: repeatedly splitting a closed walk at repeated vertices decomposes it into directed cycles.

The menu hypothesis gives
$$ c_{ji}\geq -1. $$
If a walk from $i$ to $j$ has weight $W$, closing it with edge $(j,i)$ yields
$$ W+c_{ji}\leq 0. $$
Therefore every walk has weight at most $1$.

Let $p_i$ be the maximum weight of a walk starting at $i$, including the empty walk of weight zero. All edge weights are integers. Hence the maximum exists and
$$ p_i\in\{0,1\}. $$
Prepending edge $(i,j)$ to a maximizing walk from $j$ proves
$$ p_i\geq c_{ij}+p_j. $$
Equivalently,
$$ w_{t_i}(Q_i)+p_i \geq w_{t_i}(Q_j)+p_j. $$
These are precisely all required realized-outcome envy inequalities.

The subsidies can be computed by negating edge weights, adding a terminal vertex with zero-weight incoming edges, and finding shortest paths. There are no negative cycles. The graph has at most $\tau k$ vertices, so this takes polynomial time.

Retaining the transportation masses and attaching these subsidies preserves all type totals and capacities. This proves the lemma. $\square$

Corollary 2: total fractional capacity at most one

For arbitrary admissible valuations, there is a polynomial-time construction whenever
$$ R:=\sum_g\rho_g\leq 1. $$

Proof

Use the menu
$$ Q_0=0,\qquad Q_g=e_g $$
with frequencies
$$ \lambda_0=1-R,\qquad \lambda_g=\rho_g. $$
These frequencies sum to one and have mean bundle $\rho$.

Normalization and the marginal condition give
$$ w_t(0)=0,\qquad w_t(e_g)\in\{0,1\}. $$
Thus every type’s values on this menu vary by at most one. Lemma 1 applies.

Computing the menu values requires at most $\tau(m+1)$ original value queries: query $v_t(K)$ and $v_t(K+e_g)$. All query coordinates have polynomial binary length. $\square$

This extends the previously established case of at most one nonintegral capacity to instances with arbitrarily many fractional coordinates, provided their sum is at most one.

Corollary 3: bounded gain throughout the residual cube

A polynomial-time construction also exists whenever
$$ v_t(K+\mathbf 1)-v_t(K)\leq 1 \qquad\text{for every type }t, $$
where $\mathbf 1$ has one copy of every slot.

Proof

For a parameter $u\in[0,1)$, define
$$ Q(u)_g= \begin{cases} 1,&u<\rho_g,\\ 0,&u\geq\rho_g. \end{cases} $$
The breakpoints among the $\rho_g$ partition $[0,1)$ into at most $m+1$ intervals. On each interval the bundle is constant. Give that bundle the frequency equal to the interval’s length.

This produces a rational menu with mean $\rho$, since
$$ \int_0^1 Q(u)_g\,du=\rho_g. $$
Each menu bundle satisfies $0\leq Q(u)\leq\mathbf 1$ coordinatewise. Monotonicity therefore gives
$$ 0\leq w_t(Q(u))\leq w_t(\mathbf 1)\leq 1. $$
Apply Lemma 1. $\square$

3. Binary additive valuations with arbitrary capacities

Theorem 4

Suppose the valuations are promised to be binary additive:
$$ v_t(B)=\sum_g d_{t,g}B_g, \qquad d_{t,g}\in\{0,1\}. $$
Then a feasible allocation can be constructed in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$, with $O(\tau m)$ value queries and without clone expansion.

The additive promise is an additional hypothesis for this theorem. It is not inferred from the marginal condition.

Proof

Query $v_t(e_g)$ to obtain $d_{t,g}$. The shifted valuations remain
$$ w_t(C)=\sum_g d_{t,g}C_g. $$
Call a slot *approved* by a type when $d_{t,g}=1$.

The construction first partitions the types and approved slots into ordered groups. It then rounds each type’s fractional bundle.

3.1. Constructing the groups

Let $T$ be the set of remaining types and $H$ the set of remaining slots. Initially, these are all types and slots.

Solve the linear program
$$ \max \ell $$
subject to
$$ f_{t,g}\geq 0, \qquad f_{t,g}=0\quad\text{if }d_{t,g}=0, $$
$$ \sum_{g\in H}f_{t,g}=\ell\mu_t \quad(t\in T), $$
$$ \sum_{t\in T}f_{t,g}\leq\rho_g \quad(g\in H), \qquad \ell\geq 0. $$

The zero solution is feasible. Moreover,
$$ \ell\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t = \sum_{t\in T,g\in H}f_{t,g} \leq \sum_{g\in H}\rho_g. $$
Because all remaining type masses are positive, $\ell$ is bounded. The flow variables are bounded as well. Hence an optimum exists and is computable by rational linear programming.

Fix an optimal solution $(\ell,f)$. Construct a directed graph with vertices $T$, $H$, and a terminal vertex $z$, and edges:

  • $t\to g$ whenever $d_{t,g}=1$;
  • $g\to t$ whenever $f_{t,g}>0$;
  • $g\to z$ whenever $\sum_t f_{t,g}<\rho_g$.

Let $S$ be the set of types from which $z$ is unreachable.

The set $S$ is nonempty. Suppose instead that every type reaches $z$. Choose a simple path from each type $t$ to $z$. Along its path, increase forward type-to-slot flows and decrease backward slot-to-type flows by $\varepsilon\mu_t$.

For each such path, the starting type’s row sum increases by $\varepsilon\mu_t$, intermediate type row sums are unchanged, and only the final slot’s column sum increases. Every decreased flow was positive, and every final slot had positive slack. There are finitely many chosen paths, so a sufficiently small positive rational $\varepsilon$ makes all simultaneous changes feasible. The new row sums are
$$ (\ell+\varepsilon)\mu_t. $$
This contradicts maximality of $\ell$.

Let
$$ H_S=\{g\in H:\text{some }t\in S\text{ approves }g\}. $$
Every $g\in H_S$ is also unable to reach $z$, because some edge $t\to g$ starts in $S$. Therefore:

  • Its column is saturated:

$$ \sum_t f_{t,g}=\rho_g. $$

  • It has no positive flow to a type outside $S$. Such a flow would create an edge $g\to t$ to a type that reaches $z$.

Types in $S$ have no approved slots outside $H_S$. Thus
$$ \sum_{g\in H_S}\rho_g = \ell\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t. $$

Record $S$ as the next type group, $H_S$ as its slot group, and $\ell$ as its level. Record the flow restricted to these types and slots. Remove $S$ and $H_S$, and repeat.

At least one type is removed at each step, so there are at most $\tau$ steps. Denote the recorded groups by
$$ S_1,\ldots,S_q, \qquad H_1,\ldots,H_q, $$
and their levels by $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_q$.

These levels are nondecreasing:
$$ \ell_1\leq\ell_2\leq\cdots\leq\ell_q. $$
Indeed, when a group is removed, its slots carry no flow to remaining types. Restricting the current optimum to remaining types and slots is therefore feasible at the current level. The next optimum cannot be smaller.

The construction also establishes the essential preference property:
$$ t\in S_i,\quad g\in H_j,\quad j>i \quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_{t,g}=0. $$
Otherwise $g$ would have been removed with $H_i$.

Any slots left after all types have been removed are approved by no type.

3.2. Rounding one type’s fractional bundle

For $t\in S_i$, define
$$ a_{t,g}=\frac{f_{t,g}}{\mu_t} \quad(g\in H_i). $$
Every positive coordinate is approved by $t$, and
$$ \sum_{g\in H_i}a_{t,g}=\ell_i. $$

We need an integral-bundle distribution with coordinate expectations $a_{t,g}$ and bundle sizes only $\lfloor\ell_i\rfloor$ and $\lceil\ell_i\rceil$.

Order $H_i$ as $g_1,\ldots,g_h$. Write
$$ a_{t,g_j}=k_j+b_j, \qquad k_j=\lfloor a_{t,g_j}\rfloor, \qquad 0\leq b_j<1. $$
Set
$$ F_0=0,\qquad F_j=\sum_{r=1}^j b_r. $$
For $u\in[0,1)$, define
$$ C_{g_j}(u) = k_j+\lfloor F_j+u\rfloor-\lfloor F_{j-1}+u\rfloor. $$
All other coordinates are zero.

Since $F_j-F_{j-1}=b_j\in[0,1)$, the difference of the two floors is either zero or one. Thus $C(u)$ is an integral nonnegative bundle.

For every nonnegative real number $a$,
$$ \int_0^1\lfloor a+u\rfloor\,du=a. $$
To verify this, write $a=n+r$ with $n$ integral and $0\leq r<1$. The integrand equals $n$ on an interval of length $1-r$ and $n+1$ on an interval of length $r$.

Consequently,
$$ \int_0^1 C_{g_j}(u)\,du = k_j+F_j-F_{j-1} = a_{t,g_j}. $$
The bundle size telescopes:
$$ |C(u)| = \sum_j k_j+\lfloor F_h+u\rfloor \in \{\lfloor\ell_i\rfloor,\lceil\ell_i\rceil\}. $$

The floors change only at at most $h$ rational breakpoints. Partitioning $[0,1)$ at those breakpoints yields at most $h+1$ bundles with rational probabilities. This is an explicit finite construction; no sampling is needed.

Define
$$ U_i=\max\{1,\lceil\ell_i\rceil\}, \qquad p(C)=U_i-|C|. $$
Then $p(C)\in\{0,1\}$:

  • If $\ell_i=0$, the bundle is empty and $p=1$.
  • If $\ell_i$ is a positive integer, every bundle has size $\ell_i$ and $p=0$.
  • Otherwise, the two possible sizes differ by one and the corresponding subsidies are one and zero.

Give each resulting outcome its probability multiplied by $\mu_t$. Every allocated slot is approved by its recipient, so every realized outcome of type $t\in S_i$ has residual subsidized utility
$$ w_t(C)+p(C)=|C|+p(C)=U_i. $$

3.3. Checking every envy comparison

Consider a type $t\in S_i$ and an outcome $(C',p')$ assigned to a type in $S_j$.

If $j\leq i$, binary additivity gives
$$ w_t(C')\leq |C'|. $$
Therefore
$$ w_t(C')+p' \leq |C'|+p' = U_j \leq U_i. $$
The last inequality follows from the nondecreasing levels.

If $j>i$, type $t$ approves none of the slots in $H_j$, so
$$ w_t(C')=0. $$
Hence
$$ w_t(C')+p'=p'\leq 1\leq U_i. $$

These cases include comparisons within a group and within a type. Thus all realized-outcome envy inequalities hold.

For every slot assigned to a group, expected consumption equals its capacity:
$$ \sum_{t\in S_i}\mu_t a_{t,g} = \sum_{t\in S_i}f_{t,g} = \rho_g. $$

3.4. Allocating universally unapproved slots

Let $H_0$ be the slots left after grouping. Every type values all copies of these slots at zero.

Choose any positive allocation entry of mass $x_*$. For each $g\in H_0$, set
$$ a_g=\frac{\rho_g}{x_*}. $$
Refine this entry by adding
$$ Z_g(u)=\lfloor a_g\rfloor+ \begin{cases} 1,&u<a_g-\lfloor a_g\rfloor,\\ 0,&u\geq a_g-\lfloor a_g\rfloor, \end{cases} $$
for $u\in[0,1)$. Partition at the fractional parts of the $a_g$. This creates at most $|H_0|+1$ entries and gives
$$ x_*\int_0^1 Z_g(u)\,du=x_*a_g=\rho_g. $$
Keep the original subsidy on every refined entry.

Because all added slots are unapproved by every type, every type evaluates each refined outcome exactly as it evaluated the original outcome. Envy-freeness is preserved.

3.5. Complexity and encoding bounds

There are at most $\tau$ linear programs, each with at most $\tau m$ flow variables. Their coefficients are subsets of the original rational data, so their input lengths remain polynomial throughout the construction.

Each optimal flow and level has polynomial encoding length. Division by $\mu_t$, summation, taking floors, and forming interval lengths preserve polynomial encoding length. Bundle coordinates can be numerically large, but their binary lengths remain polynomial.

Rounding gives at most $\tau(m+1)$ entries before the final refinement, which adds at most $m$ entries. The only value queries are the $O(\tau m)$ singleton queries establishing the binary additive coefficients.

Adding the common baseline $K$ completes the original allocation. $\square$

4. Universal feasibility and short certificates

These facts apply to the full valuation class, independently of the preceding algorithms.

Proposition 5: every admissible instance is feasible

Choose a positive integer $D$ clearing the denominators of every $\mu_t$ and $\rho_g$.

Create a finite instance with $D\mu_t$ agents of each type and $D\rho_g$ labeled goods of each slot. The number of agents is exactly $D$.

For a labeled subset $S$, let $C(S)$ count its copies by slot and define
$$ \widehat w_t(S)=w_t(C(S)). $$
These set valuations are normalized and have marginals in $\{0,1\}$: adding one labeled good adds one copy of its slot.

The finite-agent theorem supplied in the question guarantees a complete envy-free allocation with subsidies in $\{0,1\}$ for these valuations. Completeness is also explicit in the termination condition of its algorithm. [Barman, Krishna, Narahari, and Sadhukhan, Theorem 4 and Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.07419v1).

Aggregate agents receiving the same type, multiplicity bundle, and subsidy, dividing their counts by $D$. The resulting rational masses have the required type totals and residual capacities. Each comparison between positive aggregated entries is witnessed by a comparison between agents in the finite allocation.

Adding $K$ gives a feasible allocation for the original instance. This expansion proves existence; its size need not be polynomial in $L$. $\square$

Proposition 6: a polynomial-size feasible certificate exists

Take the residual allocation just constructed. For every positive triple $j=(t,C,p)$, form a column
$$ a_j=(e_t,C)\in\mathbb Z^{\tau+m}. $$
The masses satisfy
$$ Ax=b, \qquad x\geq 0, \qquad b=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau,\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m). $$

If the positive columns are dependent, choose a nonzero rational vector $h$ supported on them with $Ah=0$. Summing the type rows yields
$$ \sum_jh_j=0. $$
Thus $h$ has both positive and negative entries. Set
$$ \eta=\min_{j:h_j>0}\frac{x_j}{h_j}, \qquad x'=x-\eta h. $$
This preserves the equations and nonnegativity and removes at least one positive entry. Repeating yields independent positive columns, so the final support size $s$ satisfies
$$ s\leq m+\tau. $$
Deleting outcomes and changing their positive masses preserve envy-freeness, since the surviving comparisons were already satisfied.

For the encoding bound, take $D$ to be the product of the relevant input denominators. Its bit length is $O(L)$. Every residual coordinate in the expanded allocation satisfies
$$ 0\leq C_g\leq D\rho_g<D. $$

Select $s$ rows giving a nonsingular square matrix $M$ on the retained columns. For the corresponding right-hand side $b'$, Cramer’s rule gives
$$ x_j= \frac{\det M_j(Db')}{D\det M}, $$
where $M_j(Db')$ replaces column $j$ by $Db'$.

All entries in both determinant matrices have absolute value at most $D$. The permutation expansion therefore gives
$$ |\det M|\leq s!D^s, \qquad |\det M_j(Db')|\leq s!D^s. $$
Each mass consequently has polynomial binary encoding length. Each original coordinate $K_g+C_g$ does as well. With at most $m+\tau$ entries, the complete certificate has polynomial size.

Such a certificate is polynomial-time verifiable with value-oracle access: check the rational equalities, then query every type on every realized bundle and check all envy inequalities. Oracle answers have polynomial length because telescoping the marginal condition gives
$$ 0\leq v_t(B)\leq\sum_g B_g. $$
For polynomial-time evaluable valuations, this is an ordinary polynomial-time verifier. $\square$

5. What remains unresolved

Universal feasibility settles the decision component: every admissible instance has answer “feasible.” It does not supply a succinct construction.

The algorithms above settle substantial construction cases, but not arbitrary nonadditive instances with larger residual capacity and larger value variation.

The additive proof cannot be extended merely by replacing additive coefficients with singleton values. For example,
$$ w(C)=\max\{0,|C|-1\} $$
is normalized and has marginals in $\{0,1\}$, yet every singleton has value zero while bundles of size at least two have positive value. Thus singleton approvals do not describe either bundle utility or which added copies all types ignore. Those identities are essential in Theorem 4.

Likewise, Lemma 1 supplies an algorithm once a suitable menu is known; it does not prove that a polynomial-size menu with value variation at most one for every type can always be found.

No general polynomial-time construction, oracle lower bound, or construction-hardness reduction is established here.
