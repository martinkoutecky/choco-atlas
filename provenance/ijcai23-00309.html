The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite post set \(P\), finite applicant-type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), type-specific eligibility and weak rankings, and rational targets \(\ell(p)\le u(p)\), choose masses \(x_{t,p}\ge0\) with \(\sum_p x_{t,p}\le\mu_t\). With \(y_p=\sum_t x_{t,p}\) and \(d_p=\max\{0,\ell(p)-y_p,y_p-u(p)\}\), minimize \(\sum_p d_p\), then among its minimizers maximize the paper's rank-maximal signature order on assigned mass by rank.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P\) be a nonempty finite post set with \(m=|P|\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) applicant types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Each type \(t\) has an explicitly listed eligibility set \(E_t\subseteq P\) and a weak ranking of all posts in \(E_t\). Successive indifference classes have ranks \(1,2,\ldots\), with tied posts sharing a rank; write \(\operatorname{rk}_t(p)\) for this rank and \(r\le m\) for the largest rank used, taking \(r=0\) if all eligibility sets are empty.
Each post has finite binary rational targets \(0\le\ell(p)\le u(p)\), measured in the same mass units as the society. These are soft targets, with no hard post capacities.
An allocation consists of divisible masses \(x_{t,p}\ge0\), with \(x_{t,p}=0\) for \(p\notin E_t\) and \(\sum_{p\in P}x_{t,p}\le\mu_t\); the remaining mass is unassigned. Set \(y_p=\sum_{t\in T}x_{t,p}\), \(d_p=\max\{0,\ell(p)-y_p,y_p-u(p)\}\), and \(D(x)=\sum_{p\in P}d_p\).
For \(1\le i\le r\), set \(s_i(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{p\in E_t:\operatorname{rk}_t(p)=i}x_{t,p}\). The objective is first to minimize \(D(x)\), then among its minimizers to lexicographically maximize \((s_1(x),\ldots,s_r(x))\). Unassigned mass is not a further signature coordinate to optimize; any allocation attaining the optimal signature is acceptable.

Is computing an exact optimal allocation for this continuous problem possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is the problem NP-hard?

For finite individual applicants, minimizing total deviation and then maximizing the rank signature admits a polynomial-time algorithm; the analogous maximum-deviation and fairness variants do too ([source](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0309.pdf)). No result settling this exact divisible-mass formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made eligibility binding by requiring zero allocation to ineligible posts, while retaining the possibility of unassigned mass.
  • Required nonnegative finite rational lower and upper targets; retained their soft nature without imposing hard capacities or an additional upper bound of one.
  • Encoded weak rankings as successive indifference classes with consecutive ranks and preserved ties without breaking them.
  • Specified that only assigned-mass rank coordinates are lexicographically maximized; unassigned mass is not an additional optimization objective.
  • Required a nonempty post set, allowed empty eligibility sets, and defined the largest rank as zero when no eligible posts exist.

1. Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, with an exact rational optimal allocation as output. At most \(r+1\le m+1\) rational linear programs suffice. The essential point is to bound the encoding lengths of the optimal values fixed between successive solves.

2. A bounded linear formulation

Let \(\mathcal E=\{(t,p):t\in T,\ p\in E_t\}\), and let \(N=|\mathcal E|\le \tau m\). Introduce allocation variables \(x_{t,p}\) only for \((t,p)\in\mathcal E\), and one auxiliary variable \(z_p\) for each post.

Write \(y_p=\sum_{t:(t,p)\in\mathcal E}x_{t,p}\). Define the rational polytope \(Q\) by the following constraints:

  • \(x_{t,p}\ge 0\) for every \((t,p)\in\mathcal E\), and \(\sum_{p\in E_t}x_{t,p}\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\in T\).
  • \(z_p\ge 0\), \(z_p\ge\ell(p)-y_p\), and \(z_p\ge y_p-u(p)\) for every \(p\in P\).
  • \(z_p\le\ell(p)+1\) for every \(p\in P\).

The final inequalities are auxiliary bounds; they do not restrict allocations. Indeed, every feasible allocation satisfies
\[ 0\le y_p\le\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1. \]
Consequently, using \(\ell(p)\ge 0\) and \(u(p)\ge 0\),
\[ 0\le d_p(x)=\max\{0,\ell(p)-y_p,y_p-u(p)\} \le\max\{\ell(p),1\}\le\ell(p)+1. \]
Thus every feasible allocation lifts to a point \((x,z)\in Q\) by taking \(z_p=d_p(x)\). Conversely, the allocation coordinates of every point of \(Q\) form a feasible allocation.

The polytope \(Q\) is nonempty: take \(x=0\) and \(z_p=\ell(p)\). It is bounded because each allocation coordinate is at most its type’s mass, and each auxiliary coordinate has an explicit upper bound. Hence \(Q\) is compact.

Define the linear objective
\[ C(x,z)=\sum_{p\in P}z_p. \]
For every \((x,z)\in Q\), the defining inequalities give \(C(x,z)\ge D(x)\). Since every feasible allocation has a lift attaining equality, it follows that
\[ D^*:=\min_{(x,z)\in Q}C(x,z) =\min_{x\text{ feasible}}D(x). \]

Moreover, whenever \(C(x,z)=D^*\), we have
\[ D^*\le D(x)\le C(x,z)=D^*. \]
Thus \(D(x)=D^*\). Each difference \(z_p-d_p(x)\) is nonnegative, and their sum is zero, so \(z_p=d_p(x)\) for every post.

Therefore the allocation projection of
\[ F_0=\{(x,z)\in Q:C(x,z)=D^*\} \]
is exactly the set of minimum-deviation allocations.

3. Exact lexicographic optimization

First solve the linear program minimizing \(C\) over \(Q\), obtaining its exact value \(D^*\).

For each \(i=1,\ldots,r\), successively define
\[ a_i=\max_{(x,z)\in F_{i-1}}s_i(x), \qquad F_i=\{(x,z)\in F_{i-1}:s_i(x)=a_i\}. \]
These are linear programs because
\[ s_i(x)= \sum_{\substack{(t,p)\in\mathcal E\\\operatorname{rk}_t(p)=i}}x_{t,p} \]
is linear. Operationally, the program for coordinate \(i\) has the constraints of \(Q\), together with
\[ C(x,z)=D^*, \qquad s_j(x)=a_j\quad\text{for }1\le j<i. \]

Every maximum exists: \(F_0\) is nonempty and compact, and each subsequent \(F_i\) is a nonempty closed subset of \(F_{i-1}\).

To prove correctness, choose any \((x^*,z^*)\in F_r\). Its allocation has minimum deviation. Suppose another minimum-deviation allocation \(\widehat x\) had a lexicographically larger signature. Let \(i\) be the first coordinate where its signature differs from that of \(x^*\). Then
\[ s_j(\widehat x)=a_j\quad\text{for }j<i, \qquad s_i(\widehat x)>a_i. \]
The lift \((\widehat x,d(\widehat x))\) belongs to \(F_{i-1}\), contradicting the definition of \(a_i\).

Hence every allocation projected from \(F_r\) meets the stated objective. The last optimization solve supplies such a point.

If \(r=0\), all eligibility sets are empty, so the zero allocation is the unique feasible allocation and can be returned directly. No objective for unassigned mass is added.

4. Polynomial encoding lengths and running time

We use the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem: a linear program with binary rational data can be solved in time polynomial in its encoding length, returning an exact rational optimal solution when its feasible region is nonempty and its optimum is finite. This is the classical polynomial-time solvability theorem for linear programming. [Khachiyan, *A polynomial algorithm in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

The remaining issue is whether the values \(D^*,a_1,\ldots,a_r\) inserted into later programs retain polynomial encoding length. We establish a bound using the original polytope \(Q\).

Let \(n=N+m\) be its number of variables. Its inequality matrix has entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\); its right-hand sides are among zero, the masses, the targets and their negatives, and the values \(\ell(p)+1\).

Let \(q\) be the product of the positive denominators of all input rational numbers. Then \(\log_2 q=O(L)\), every right-hand side \(b_k\) satisfies \(qb_k\in\mathbb Z\), and
\[ |qb_k|\le 2^{O(L)}. \]

At any vertex \(v\) of \(Q\), there are \(n\) linearly independent tight constraints. To justify this even when \(Q\) has lower dimension, suppose the tight constraint normals did not span the ambient space. A nonzero direction orthogonal to all of them would preserve their equalities. Taking sufficiently small steps in both signs of that direction would also preserve every remaining, strictly satisfied inequality, contradicting that \(v\) is a vertex.

Choose such a tight subsystem and write it as \(Av=b\), where \(A\) is a nonsingular integer matrix with entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). Applying Cramer’s rule to \(A(qv)=qb\) gives
\[ v_j=\frac{\det A_j}{q\det A}, \]
where \(A_j\) replaces column \(j\) of \(A\) with \(qb\).

Hadamard’s inequality yields
\[ |\det A|\le n^{n/2}, \qquad |\det A_j|\le n^{n/2}2^{O(L)}. \]
Therefore each coordinate of every vertex has numerator and denominator encoding length
\[ O\bigl(L+n\log(n+1)\bigr). \]
At a fixed vertex, the displayed Cramer expressions have a common denominator. Thus a sum of at most \(n\) coordinates—including \(C\) or any \(s_i\)—has the same polynomial encoding bound.

Now \(F_0\) is a face of \(Q\), and each \(F_i\) is a face of \(F_{i-1}\). Indeed, an optimum set of a linear objective is a face: if a strict convex combination attains the optimum, both endpoints must attain it. Applying this observation inductively also shows that each \(F_i\) is a face of \(Q\).

Every vertex of a face is a vertex of the original polytope. Since a linear objective on a nonempty compact polytope attains its optimum at a vertex, each of \(D^*,a_1,\ldots,a_r\) is the value of its respective objective at some vertex of \(Q\). The preceding encoding bound therefore applies uniformly to all values fixed during the algorithm; their bit lengths do not accumulate recursively.

Each program has:

  • \(n=N+m\le\tau m+m\) variables;
  • \(O(N+\tau+m+r)\) constraints, representing equalities by pairs of inequalities if needed;
  • polynomial total binary encoding length in \(m,\tau,L\).

All programs are nonempty and bounded, as established above, so the exact rational LP theorem applies at every stage. There are at most \(m+1\) solves. Returning the allocation coordinates of the final solution, with zero entries for ineligible pairs, therefore takes time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

This proves the claimed exact polynomial-time algorithm.
