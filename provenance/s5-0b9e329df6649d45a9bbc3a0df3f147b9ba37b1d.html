The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_t\) of complete pair types, where each type specifies its center and compatibility and value relations, and allowed cycle patterns \(e\) of length at most three, does there exist a feasible mass plan \(x_e\ge 0\) with \(\sum_e \ell_e x_e\ge q\) that is rejection-proof? Feasibility requires \(\sum_e a_{te}x_e\le \mu_t\) for every type \(t\), where \(a_{te}\) counts its participants in pattern \(e\) and \(\ell_e\) is the pattern's transplant count. For each center \(i\), define its best rejection response \(R_i(x)\) by maximizing \(\sum_e w_{i,e}y_e\) subject to the same type-mass capacities, allowing only internal patterns and proposed shared patterns, with \(y_e\le x_e\) for shared patterns. Require \(R_i(x)\le \sum_e w_{i,e}x_e\) for every center.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) donor–recipient pair types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a center \(c(t)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\); every center has positive total mass. Compatibility between distinct pairs is specified by a directed Boolean table on types. There are no non-directed donors or chains.
Let \(E\) contain all compatible directed cycle patterns of lengths two and three, identified up to cyclic rotation. Repeated types represent distinct participating pairs; self-donation is excluded. Let \(a_{te}\) count occurrences of type \(t\) in pattern \(e\), and let \(\ell_e=\sum_t a_{te}\). The input supplies nonnegative binary-rational center values \(w_{i,e}\), with \(w_{i,e}=0\) when \(e\) has no participant from center \(i\); these values need not sum to \(\ell_e\).
A pattern is internal to center \(i\) if all its participants belong to \(i\), and shared if it involves multiple centers. Write \(E_i\) for the internal patterns and \(S_i\) for the shared patterns involving \(i\).
A mass plan \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E}\) is feasible when \(\sum_{e\in E}a_{te}x_e\le\mu_t\) for every type \(t\). For each center define
\(R_i(x)=\max\{\sum_{e\in E_i\cup S_i}w_{i,e}y_e:y\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E_i\cup S_i},\ \sum_{e\in E_i\cup S_i}a_{te}y_e\le\mu_t\ \forall t,\ y_e\le x_e\ \forall e\in S_i\}\).
Thus internal cycles may use withdrawn or previously unmatched mass, while shared cycles can only be retained in proposed amounts. A plan is rejection-proof when \(R_i(x)\le\sum_{e\in E}w_{i,e}x_e\) for every center; indifference permits acceptance.

Given these data and a nonnegative binary-rational threshold \(q\), is deciding whether a feasible rejection-proof plan satisfies \(\sum_{e\in E}\ell_e x_e\ge q\) solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the total input bit length \(L\).

The discrete decision problem is \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\)-complete even with two centers, unit transplant values, no chains, and maximum cycle length three ([published result](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2023.10.015)).
For fixed maximum cycle length, its discrete unit-value version is FPT in the required number \(k\) of transplants, with running time \(2^{O(k\log k)}+n^{O(1)}\) on \(n\) pairs ([later result](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.IPEC.2025.9)). The literature search found no result settling the mass problem defined here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to total mass one and required each listed center to control positive mass.
  • Interpreted complete types through an explicit directed compatibility table and enumerated all compatible cycle patterns of lengths two and three, with no donors or chains.
  • Allowed repeated types within a pattern as distinct participants, excluded self-donation, and identified cyclic rotations.
  • Made the unspecified value relations explicit as nonnegative rational values for each center and cycle pattern, zero for uninvolved centers; retained the distinction between center value and transplant count.
  • Restricted a center's response to its own internal patterns and proposed shared patterns involving it, with unrestricted internal replanning and fractional retention of shared patterns.
  • Specified a nonnegative rational threshold, real divisible plan variables, acceptance under indifference, and total input bit length L.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, already with \(m=2\) and center values in \(\{0,1,3,4\}\).

Writing \(N=|E|=O(\tau^3)\), an exact deterministic algorithm runs in time

\(2^{\tau+3N}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)=2^{O(\tau^3)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\).

Thus there is no algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). The hardness proof uses the arbitrary center values permitted in the question.

1. Construction from satisfiability

We reduce from \(\mathrm{3SAT}\). Let the formula have variables \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) and clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_p\), where \(n,p\geq1\).

Choose

\(\displaystyle d=\left\lceil\log_2(p+1)\right\rceil,\qquad \epsilon=2^{-d},\qquad b=\left\lceil\log_2(9n+2p+1)\right\rceil,\qquad \rho=2^{-b}.\)

In particular, \(p\epsilon<1\).

Types, centers, and masses

For each variable \(X_j\), introduce nine types:

  • Center \(1\): \(A_j,B_j,P_j^+,Q_j^+,P_j^-,Q_j^-\).
  • Center \(2\): \(C_j,H_j^+,H_j^-\).

Every one of these types has mass \(\rho\).

For each clause \(C_h\), introduce two center-\(2\) types \(K_h,Z_h\), each of mass \(\rho\epsilon\).

Finally, introduce an isolated center-\(2\) type \(I\), with mass

\(\displaystyle \mu_I=1-\rho(9n+2p\epsilon)>0.\)

The inequality follows from \(2^b\geq9n+2p+1>9n+2p\epsilon\). All masses are positive dyadic rationals, their sum is \(1\), and both centers have positive total mass. The support size is

\(\tau=9n+2p+1.\)

Compatibility and values

For each variable, add the directed arcs of these five cycles:

| Pattern | Classification | Center-\(1\) value |
|---|---|---:|
| \((A_j,B_j)\) | Internal | \(3\) |
| \((A_j,P_j^+,Q_j^+)\) | Internal | \(1\) |
| \((B_j,P_j^-,Q_j^-)\) | Internal | \(1\) |
| \((A_j,C_j,H_j^+)\) | Shared | \(4\) |
| \((B_j,C_j,H_j^-)\) | Shared | \(4\) |

For every occurrence of \(X_j\) in clause \(C_h\), add the arcs of the shared cycle

\((P_j^+,K_h,Z_h).\)

For every occurrence of \(\neg X_j\) in clause \(C_h\), add the arcs of

\((P_j^-,K_h,Z_h).\)

Each clause cycle has center-\(1\) value \(4\). Set every center-\(2\) value to \(0\). Add no other arcs.

Set the transplant threshold to

\(\displaystyle q=\rho(6n+3p\epsilon).\)

The construction, including its explicit Boolean compatibility table, has size polynomial in \(n+p\).

2. Verification of the cycle patterns

The compatible patterns of lengths two and three are exactly those just listed.

First, the only reciprocal pair of arcs is \(A_j\leftrightarrow B_j\), so these are the only two-cycles.

For three-cycles:

  • A cycle containing \(K_h\) must follow \(K_h\to Z_h\), then \(Z_h\to P_j^\pm\), and close through \(P_j^\pm\to K_h\). These are exactly the clause cycles. A cycle containing \(Z_h\) necessarily contains \(K_h\), because \(K_h\) is its only predecessor.
  • A cycle containing \(H_j^+\) must use \(C_j\to H_j^+\to A_j\), and consequently is \((A_j,C_j,H_j^+)\). The argument for \(H_j^-\) is identical. Every cycle containing \(C_j\) contains one of these two types.
  • Among the remaining vertices, a three-cycle containing \(Q_j^+\) must be \((A_j,P_j^+,Q_j^+)\), and one containing \(Q_j^-\) must be \((B_j,P_j^-,Q_j^-)\). After excluding these vertices, only the disjoint reciprocal pairs \(A_j,B_j\) remain, which create no three-cycle.

There are no diagonal arcs. Consequently, repeated-type patterns of lengths two or three cannot occur: such a pattern would require a diagonal arc. The isolated type participates in no pattern.

This verifies that no unintended pattern can contribute to the threshold or to a deviation.

3. Every successful plan determines a satisfying assignment

Let \(x\) be a feasible rejection-proof plan for the constructed instance.

For variable \(j\), denote its five cycle amounts by

\(\displaystyle z_j=x_{(A_j,B_j)},\quad a_j=x_{(A_j,P_j^+,Q_j^+)},\quad b_j=x_{(B_j,P_j^-,Q_j^-)},\)

\(\displaystyle u_j=x_{(A_j,C_j,H_j^+)},\qquad v_j=x_{(B_j,C_j,H_j^-)}.\)

Feasibility gives

\(\displaystyle u_j+z_j+a_j\leq\rho,\qquad v_j+z_j+b_j\leq\rho,\qquad u_j+v_j\leq\rho.\)

Rejection-proofness forces the preferred internal cycle amount

We claim that

\(\displaystyle z_j=\min\{\rho-u_j,\rho-v_j\}.\)

Feasibility gives the upper bound. Suppose it is strict. Choose

\(\displaystyle 0<\delta\leq\min\{\rho-u_j-z_j,\rho-v_j-z_j\}.\)

Keep every shared cycle and every other gadget unchanged. Increase \(z_j\) by \(\delta\), and reduce \(a_j,b_j\) by, respectively,

\(\displaystyle r_A=\max\{0,u_j+z_j+a_j+\delta-\rho\},\qquad r_B=\max\{0,v_j+z_j+b_j+\delta-\rho\}.\)

The choice of \(\delta\) and original feasibility imply

\(\displaystyle 0\leq r_A\leq\min\{\delta,a_j\},\qquad 0\leq r_B\leq\min\{\delta,b_j\}.\)

The resulting deviation is feasible. Its usage of \(A_j,B_j\) satisfies their capacities by construction; usage of every other type either stays fixed or decreases. All proposed shared amounts are retained.

Center \(1\)'s utility increases by

\(\displaystyle 3\delta-r_A-r_B\geq\delta>0,\)

contradicting rejection-proofness. This proves the claim.

Transplant bound and its equality conditions

The transplant contribution of variable gadget \(j\) is

\(\displaystyle T_j=3u_j+3v_j+2z_j+3a_j+3b_j.\)

Using the two capacity inequalities,

\(\displaystyle T_j\leq3(u_j+v_j)+2z_j+3(2\rho-u_j-v_j-2z_j)=6\rho-4z_j\leq6\rho.\)

If \(T_j=6\rho\), then \(z_j=0\). The preceding claim therefore implies

\(\max\{u_j,v_j\}=\rho.\)

Together with \(u_j+v_j\leq\rho\), this forces exactly one of

\(\displaystyle (u_j,v_j)=(\rho,0)\quad\text{or}\quad(u_j,v_j)=(0,\rho).\)

Moreover, equality in the transplant bound forces \(a_j+b_j=2\rho-u_j-v_j=\rho\). Thus:

  • If \((u_j,v_j)=(\rho,0)\), then \(a_j=0\) and \(b_j=\rho\).
  • If \((u_j,v_j)=(0,\rho)\), then \(a_j=\rho\) and \(b_j=0\).

The threshold forces every clause to use a true literal

Let \(r_h\) be the sum of all clause-cycle amounts belonging to clause \(C_h\). Its type \(K_h\) gives

\(r_h\leq\rho\epsilon.\)

Because the enumerated patterns are exhaustive, the total transplant amount satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_e\ell_e x_e=\sum_{j=1}^nT_j+3\sum_{h=1}^p r_h\leq6n\rho+3p\rho\epsilon=q.\)

Consequently, reaching the threshold forces equality everywhere:

\(\displaystyle T_j=6\rho\ \text{for every }j,\qquad r_h=\rho\epsilon\ \text{for every }h.\)

Define \(X_j\) to be true when \(u_j=\rho\), and false when \(v_j=\rho\).

If \(X_j\) is true, the internal cycle \((B_j,P_j^-,Q_j^-)\) has amount \(\rho\), exhausting \(P_j^-\). Therefore no clause cycle using the negative literal \(\neg X_j\) can have positive amount.

If \(X_j\) is false, the internal cycle \((A_j,P_j^+,Q_j^+)\) similarly exhausts \(P_j^+\), preventing positive allocation to its positive literal.

Each clause has \(r_h=\rho\epsilon>0\), so at least one of its literal cycles has positive amount. That literal must be true. Hence the extracted assignment satisfies the formula.

4. Every satisfying assignment gives a successful plan

Conversely, fix a satisfying assignment.

For every true variable, allocate:

\(\displaystyle u_j=\rho,\qquad b_j=\rho,\qquad v_j=z_j=a_j=0.\)

For every false variable, allocate:

\(\displaystyle v_j=\rho,\qquad a_j=\rho,\qquad u_j=z_j=b_j=0.\)

For each clause, choose one true literal and allocate \(\rho\epsilon\) to its clause cycle. Allocate zero to all other patterns.

Feasibility and transplant total

Every variable gadget is feasible. Its selected shared cycle and its opposite internal three-cycle use disjoint center-\(1\) types.

A true literal's port \(P_j^\pm\) is unused by the internal cycle. Across all clauses, its clause-cycle usage is at most

\(p\rho\epsilon<\rho,\)

so its capacity is respected. Each clause uses its two center-\(2\) types at exactly their capacities.

The plan therefore is feasible and has transplant amount

\(\displaystyle 6n\rho+3p\rho\epsilon=q.\)

Rejection-proofness

Center \(2\) has utility \(0\) in every plan and deviation, so its rejection condition holds.

Consider a deviation by center \(1\). In a true variable gadget, only the proposed shared cycle with \(A_j\) can be retained. Write its retained amount as \(r\), and write the three internal amounts as \(z,a,b\). Their capacities imply

\(\displaystyle r+z+a\leq\rho,\qquad z+b\leq\rho,\qquad 0\leq r\leq\rho.\)

The deviation's utility from these variable cycles is bounded by

\(\displaystyle 4r+3z+a+b\leq4r+3z+(\rho-r-z)+(\rho-z)=2\rho+3r+z.\)

Since \(z\leq\rho-r\), this is at most

\(\displaystyle 3\rho+2r\leq5\rho.\)

The proposed plan attains \(5\rho\) in this gadget: its shared cycle contributes \(4\rho\), and its internal three-cycle contributes \(\rho\). The same calculation applies to a false variable after exchanging the two signs.

All clause cycles are shared, so each deviation amount is bounded by its proposed amount. Their combined deviation utility is therefore at most

\(4p\rho\epsilon.\)

Summing these bounds, every deviation has center-\(1\) utility at most

\(\displaystyle 5n\rho+4p\rho\epsilon,\)

which is exactly the proposed utility. Thus the plan is rejection-proof. Equality is accepted under the stated indifference rule.

We have proved the required equivalence, establishing \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness with two centers.

5. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) and running time

We now consider an arbitrary instance.

Let \(T_i=\{t:c(t)=i\}\) and \(F_i=E_i\cup S_i\). For a feasible proposed plan \(x\), the deviation constraints for types outside \(T_i\) are redundant. Such types occur only in shared patterns of \(F_i\), and therefore

\(\displaystyle \sum_{e\in F_i}a_{te}y_e\leq\sum_{e\in S_i}a_{te}x_e\leq\mu_t\qquad(t\notin T_i).\)

Thus the deviation LP can retain only resource constraints for \(t\in T_i\).

Assign a nonnegative dual variable \(\alpha_t\) to each such resource constraint, and a nonnegative dual variable \(\beta_{ie}\) to each shared upper bound. Its dual is

\(\displaystyle \min\left\{\sum_{t\in T_i}\mu_t\alpha_t+\sum_{e\in S_i}x_e\beta_{ie}\right\},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_i}a_{te}\alpha_t+\mathbf{1}_{e\in S_i}\beta_{ie}\geq w_{i,e}\qquad(e\in F_i).\)

Here the \(\beta\)-term is omitted for internal patterns.

The deviation primal is feasible, because it contains the restriction of \(x\). It is bounded: every pattern in \(F_i\) consumes at least one type in \(T_i\), whose mass is finite. Linear-programming strong duality therefore applies, with an attained dual optimum.

Define the nonnegative resource slacks and dual slacks by

\(\displaystyle s_t=\mu_t-\sum_{e\in E}a_{te}x_e,\)

\(\displaystyle d_{ie}=\sum_{t\in T_i}a_{te}\alpha_t+\mathbf{1}_{e\in S_i}\beta_{ie}-w_{i,e}.\)

Every pattern containing a type \(t\in T_i\) belongs to \(F_i\). Consequently, the dual objective minus the proposed center utility equals

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_i}\alpha_t s_t+\sum_{e\in F_i}x_e d_{ie}.\)

All summands are nonnegative. Since the proposed restriction is a feasible deviation, rejection-proofness is equivalent to the existence of feasible dual variables satisfying

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t s_t=0\quad\text{for every type }t,\qquad x_e d_{ie}=0\quad\text{for every }i\text{ and }e\in F_i.\)

The shared-upper-bound complementary conditions require nothing further: the proposed restriction has \(y_e=x_e\).

Finite enumeration of linear systems

For each displayed product, select one factor and impose that it equals zero. After these selections, all conditions are linear:

  • Global feasibility and nonnegativity.
  • The transplant threshold.
  • Dual feasibility and nonnegativity.
  • The selected zero-factor equations.

There are \(\tau\) resource products. Also,

\(\displaystyle \sum_i|F_i|\leq3N,\)

because a pattern of length at most three involves at most three centers. Thus at most \(\tau+3N\) binary selections are needed.

Every branch is a rational linear-feasibility problem with polynomially many variables, constraints, and coefficient bits. It can be solved in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\). Enumerating all branches gives the stated bound

\(\displaystyle 2^{\tau+3N}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)=2^{O(\tau^3)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L).\)

Finally, any nonempty branch has a rational feasible point of polynomial encoding length. Indeed, after introducing nonnegative slack variables, a basic feasible solution has coordinates given by ratios of determinants of polynomial-size rational matrices; clearing denominators and applying determinant bounds gives polynomial bit length. Guessing the branch and such a point, then checking all constraints by exact rational arithmetic, is a polynomial-time verifier.

Therefore the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). Together with the reduction, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.
