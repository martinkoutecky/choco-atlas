The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of structural roles \(R\), opinions \(I\), initial masses \(\mu_{r,i}\) with preferred mass \(\sum_{r\in R}\mu_{r,0}\ge\eta>0\), and adoption probabilities \(\alpha_0=1\) and \(\alpha_i\le 1-\epsilon\) for \(i\ne0\), consider synchronous biased-voter updates on symmetric, regular type-level interaction kernels chosen adaptively subject to conductance lower bounds \(\phi_t\). What cumulative conductance \(\sum_{t\le T}\phi_t\) suffices in the worst case for the induced mass dynamics to reach preferred-opinion mass at least \(1-\delta\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(R\) be a finite set of \(k\) fixed structural roles and \(I=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\), with \(m\ge2\), the opinions. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \((r,i)\) with positive initial mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Missing masses are zero, \(\sum_{r,i}\mu^0_{r,i}=1\), and every role has positive total mass.
Assume rational parameters \(0<\eta<1-\delta<1\), \(0<\epsilon\le1\), \(\alpha_0=1\), and \(0\le\alpha_i\le1-\epsilon\) for \(i\ne0\), with \(\sum_r\mu^0_{r,0}\ge\eta\). Mass is nonatomic and evolves deterministically.
Before round \(t\), an adversary observing all previous states chooses a nonnegative symmetric contact-flow matrix \(C^t\) on the current types, satisfying \(\sum_b C^t_{ab}=\mu^{t-1}_a\). Thus every individual has total contact rate one; within-type contacts are allowed. For positive-mass types, \(C^t_{ab}/\mu^{t-1}_a\) is the probability of sampling type \(b\).
Define conductance by \(\Phi(C^t,\mu^{t-1})=\inf_{x\in[0,1]^{R\times I},\,0<\sum_a\mu^{t-1}_a x_a\le1/2}\frac{\sum_{a,b}C^t_{ab}x_a(1-x_b)}{\sum_a\mu^{t-1}_a x_a}\); the coordinates \(x_a\) allow a measurable cut to split a type's mass. Require \(\Phi(C^t,\mu^{t-1})\ge\phi_t\), where the feasible sequence \(0\le\phi_t\le1\) is fixed in advance.
Synchronous adoption uses the state before the round, retaining the old opinion when adoption fails: \(\mu^t_{r,i}=\mu^{t-1}_{r,i}-\sum_{s,j}\alpha_j C^t_{(r,i),(s,j)}+\alpha_i\sum_{s,j}C^t_{(r,j),(s,i)}\). Structural roles remain fixed.
Let \(p_t=\sum_r\mu^t_{r,0}\). Any algorithmic running time should be stated in \(m,k,\tau,L,T\) and the numerical parameters, where \(L\) is the binary input length for the finite horizon under consideration.

What is the optimal worst-case sufficient cumulative-conductance budget \(B(k,m,\eta,\epsilon,\delta)\) guaranteeing \(p_T\ge1-\delta\) whenever \(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\phi_t\ge B\), uniformly over admissible initial societies and adaptive contact matrices; or is there no finite such uniform budget?

For finite populations on dynamic regular graphs, a fixed positive popularity gap and at least \(c\log n\) initially preferred agents give preferred consensus with probability at least \(1-1/n\) once cumulative conductance reaches \(b\log n\), for suitable constants. [Source](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol055-icalp2016/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.146/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.146.pdf)
A related asynchronous binary voter model on complete graphs has a deterministic mean-field limit; it does not cover these synchronous adaptive contact matrices. No result settling this precise mass problem was located. [Source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10955-020-02625-w)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total mass to one, made masses nonatomic, and interpreted induced mass dynamics as deterministic adoption flows.
  • Made structural roles immutable and allowed the adversary to choose contacts between current role-opinion types, matching its access to the current opinion configuration.
  • Defined symmetric regular interaction through symmetric contact flows whose row sums equal current type masses, ensuring equal per-individual contact rates despite unequal type masses.
  • Allowed within-type contacts, which represent contacts between distinct individuals in the same nonatomic type.
  • Defined conductance over cuts that may split type masses, avoiding dependence on the arbitrary partition into types.
  • Specified synchronous updates using pre-round opinions and retention of the old opinion after failed adoption.
  • Required a conductance lower-bound sequence fixed in advance and restricted attention to feasible sequences.
  • Pinned down nonnegative adoption probabilities, rational input parameters, at least two opinions, positive role masses, and the nontrivial domain eta < 1-delta.
  • Interpreted the requested budget as a uniform guarantee over all societies and adaptive kernels, with explicit dependence on role count, opinion count, initial preferred mass, popularity gap, and tolerance.

Exact optimal budget

A finite uniform budget exists. Its exact value is independent of \(k\) and \(m\), provided \(k\ge 1\) and \(m\ge 2\).

Write \(q=1-\delta\). All logarithms below are natural.

For \(0<p\le \tfrac12\), define
\[ c(p)=\frac{1-p}{2-p}, \qquad G(p)=p\bigl(1+\epsilon c(p)\bigr). \]
Here and below, displayed mathematics uses the requested delimiters:

\(\displaystyle c(p)=\frac{1-p}{2-p},\qquad G(p)=p\bigl(1+\epsilon c(p)\bigr).\)

When \(\eta<\tfrac12\), define \(A_\eta(x)\), for \(\eta\le x\le\tfrac12\), by the following finite recurrence. Start at \(u_0=\eta\). Repeatedly take the full step \(u_{j+1}=G(u_j)\) while \(G(u_j)\le x\). At the resulting last value \(u_j\le x\), set

\(\displaystyle A_\eta(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}c(u_\ell) +\frac{x/u_j-1}{\epsilon}. \)

If \(u_j=x\), the last summand is zero. The recurrence terminates because \(c(p)\ge\tfrac13\) on \(0<p\le\tfrac12\).

The exact smallest sufficient budget is as follows.

  • Initially at least half preferred: if \(\eta\ge\tfrac12\),

\(\displaystyle B(k,m,\eta,\epsilon,\delta) =\frac1\epsilon\log\frac{1-\eta}{\delta}. \)

  • The target is at most half: if \(\eta<q\le\tfrac12\),

\(\displaystyle B(k,m,\eta,\epsilon,\delta)=A_\eta(q). \)

  • The trajectory must cross half: if \(\eta<\tfrac12<q\), let

\(\displaystyle D=\left\{p\in[\eta,\tfrac12]:G(p)\ge\tfrac12\right\}, \qquad w(p)=\min\{1-p,G(p),q\}. \)

Then

\(\displaystyle B(k,m,\eta,\epsilon,\delta) = \max_{p\in D} \left[ A_\eta(p) +\frac{w(p)/p-1}{\epsilon} +\frac1\epsilon\log\frac{1-w(p)}{\delta} \right]. \)

This is a one-variable maximization of an explicit continuous function on a nonempty compact set. Thus the formula determines a finite number using only \(\eta,\epsilon,\delta\).

“Smallest sufficient” includes equality: cumulative conductance at least this value guarantees \(p_T\ge q\). For every strictly smaller budget, some admissible society and a finite, predetermined sequence of contact matrices fail to reach \(q\).

1. Preferred-mass drift and feasible conductance

For one round, suppress time superscripts. Let \(P\) denote the preferred types and \(N\) the other types, and put

\(\displaystyle p=\sum_{a\in P}\mu_a, \qquad Q=\sum_{a\in P,\ b\in N}C_{ab}. \)

Summing the adoption equation over preferred types and using symmetry gives

\(\displaystyle p^+-p = \sum_{r,s}\sum_{i\ne0} (1-\alpha_i)C_{(r,0),(s,i)} \ge \epsilon Q. \)

Consequently, preferred mass never decreases.

Every feasible conductance is at most \(\tfrac12\). Indeed, the fractional cut \(x_a=\tfrac12\) for every type has cut mass \(\tfrac12\), numerator \(\tfrac14\), and ratio \(\tfrac12\).

Conversely, \(C_{ab}=\mu_a\mu_b\) has conductance exactly \(\tfrac12\): for a cut of mass \(s=\sum_a\mu_a x_a\), its ratio is \(1-s\). Thus the feasible range is precisely \(0\le\phi\le\tfrac12\).

The probabilistic regular-graph theorem in the [cited ICALP paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol055-icalp2016/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.146/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.146.pdf) is not needed for the argument below.

2. Sharp minimum contact flow in one round

Let \(a=\min\{p,1-p\}\). Define

\(\displaystyle f(p,\phi) = \max\left\{ \phi a,\; a(1-a)-(1-2\phi)(1-a)^2 \right\}. \)

We prove that conductance at least \(\phi\) implies \(Q\ge f(p,\phi)\), and that equality is attainable.

Two-group conductance calculation

Aggregate contacts into the preferred and nonpreferred groups. The resulting matrix is

\(\displaystyle H= \begin{pmatrix} p-Q&Q\\ Q&1-p-Q \end{pmatrix}. \)

Cuts constant within each group are permitted in the original model. Therefore its conductance is at most the conductance of \(H\).

Relabel the groups so their masses are \(a\) and \(1-a\). For fractional coordinates \(X,Y\), write

\(\displaystyle s=aX+(1-a)Y,\qquad d=a(1-a)-Q. \)

The cut numerator is

\(\displaystyle s(1-s)-d(X-Y)^2. \)

First suppose \(d\ge0\). At fixed \(0<s\le\tfrac12\), the greatest possible value of \(|X-Y|\) is

  • \(s/a\), when \(s\le a\);
  • \((1-s)/(1-a)\), when \(a\le s\le\tfrac12\).

To check this, the largest \(X-Y\) is obtained by filling the smaller group first. The opposite orientation has maximum \(Y-X=s/(1-a)\), which is no larger on either interval.

Hence the minimum cut ratio at mass \(s\) is

  • \(1-s-ds/a^2\), when \(s\le a\);
  • \(1-s-d(1-s)^2/((1-a)^2s)\), when \(a\le s\le\tfrac12\).

The first expression decreases with \(s\). The second is concave in \(s\), since its second derivative is

\(\displaystyle -\frac{2d}{(1-a)^2s^3}\le0. \)

Their joint infimum therefore occurs at \(s=a\) or \(s=\tfrac12\). It equals

\(\displaystyle \Phi(H) = \min\left\{ \frac Qa,\; \frac12-\frac{a(1-a)-Q}{2(1-a)^2} \right\}. \)

If \(d\le0\), every cut ratio is at least \(1-s\ge\tfrac12\), and \(X=Y=\tfrac12\) gives equality. Thus \(\Phi(H)=\tfrac12\).

For \(0\le\phi\le\tfrac12\), these formulas show exactly that

\(\displaystyle \Phi(H)\ge\phi \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Q\ge f(p,\phi). \)

In particular, every admissible round satisfies

\(\displaystyle p^+\ge p+\epsilon f(p,\phi). \)

Attainment with any number of roles

Choose positive role weights \(\rho_r\) summing to one. Use only opinions \(0\) and \(1\), with

\(\displaystyle \mu_{r,0}=\rho_rp,\qquad \mu_{r,1}=\rho_r(1-p),\qquad \alpha_1=1-\epsilon. \)

Given \(H\), set

\(\displaystyle C_{(r,i),(s,j)}=\rho_r\rho_sH_{ij}. \)

This matrix is symmetric and has the required row sums.

For an arbitrary fractional cut, define its mean coordinate within opinion \(i\) by \(X_i=\sum_r\rho_r x_{r,i}\). Its mass and numerator equal those of the two-group cut \((X_0,X_1)\). Conversely, every such two-group cut is realized by taking coordinates constant across roles. Thus this lifted matrix has exactly the conductance of \(H\).

The adoption equation also preserves the product form and gives

\(\displaystyle p^+=p+\epsilon Q. \)

Taking \(Q=f(p,\phi)\) realizes the lower bound exactly. The construction uses initial support size \(2k\), and all roles have positive mass.

3. Reduction to bounded scalar steps

Define the scalar transition

\(\displaystyle F_\phi(p)=p+\epsilon f(p,\phi). \)

We first verify that \(F_\phi\) is nondecreasing in \(p\).

As a function of \(a\in[0,\tfrac12]\), the first branch of \(f\) has derivative \(\phi\). Whenever the second branch is active, necessarily \(\phi\ge\tfrac13\) and

\(\displaystyle \frac{1-2\phi}{1-\phi}\le a\le\frac12. \)

Its derivative is

\(\displaystyle 3-4\phi-4(1-\phi)a, \)

which lies between \(1-2\phi\) and \(4\phi-1\), both in \([0,1]\). Thus \(f\), as a function of \(a\), is nondecreasing and has slope at most one. Since \(a=\min\{p,1-p\}\), the slope of \(F_\phi\) is everywhere nonnegative, using \(\epsilon\le1\). Continuity handles the branch boundaries.

For any fixed sequence \((\phi_t)\), the scalar trajectory starting at \(\eta\) therefore lies below every admissible actual trajectory starting at \(p_0\ge\eta\). Section 2 shows that this scalar trajectory can itself be realized.

We next show that, for maximizing cumulative conductance before reaching a target, it suffices to use steps satisfying

\(\displaystyle \phi\le c(a)=\frac{1-a}{2-a}. \)

Such steps have \(f(p,\phi)=\phi a\).

Suppose instead that \(\phi>c(a)\). Put \(c=c(a)\) and \(h=\phi-c\). Since \(\tfrac13\le c\le\tfrac12\),

\(\displaystyle 0<h\le\tfrac16.\)

The original transition has increment

\(\displaystyle \epsilon f(p,\phi) = \epsilon\left[ca+2(1-a)^2h\right]. \)

Replace it by a step of conductance \(c\), followed by one of conductance \(h\). After the first step let the preferred mass be \(p_1=p+\epsilon ca\), and set \(a_1=\min\{p_1,1-p_1\}\). The second step is in its linear branch because \(h\le\tfrac16\le c(a_1)\). Its endpoint is

\(\displaystyle p_2=p+\epsilon ca+\epsilon h a_1. \)

Since \(a_1\le\tfrac12\le2(1-a)^2\), this endpoint is no greater than the original endpoint. The replacement preserves the cumulative conductance.

Monotonicity of subsequent scalar transitions preserves this comparison. Applying the replacement successively transforms any failing finite schedule into another failing finite schedule, with the same budget, whose every step obeys

\(\displaystyle p^+=p+\epsilon\phi\min\{p,1-p\}, \qquad \phi\le c\!\left(\min\{p,1-p\}\right). \)

4. Greedy steps are optimal below half

We prove that the maximum cumulative conductance of a scalar schedule from \(\eta\) to a fixed \(x\le\tfrac12\) is \(A_\eta(x)\).

Throughout such a schedule,

\(\displaystyle p^+=p(1+\epsilon\phi),\qquad 0\le\phi\le c(p). \)

Consider adjacent controls \(a,b\), starting at mass \(p\), with endpoint

\(\displaystyle p_2=p(1+\epsilon a)(1+\epsilon b)\le\tfrac12. \)

Swapping into decreasing order

Suppose \(a\le b\). Swapping them preserves the endpoint and cost.

The new first step is feasible because

\(\displaystyle b\le c\bigl(p(1+\epsilon a)\bigr)\le c(p). \)

Also \(c'(z)=-1/(2-z)^2\), so \(|c'(z)|\le4/9\) for \(z\le\tfrac12\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle c\bigl(p(1+\epsilon b)\bigr) \ge c\bigl(p(1+\epsilon a)\bigr) -\frac29(b-a) \ge b-\frac29(b-a)\ge a. \)

Thus the second step remains feasible. Repeated adjacent swaps arrange the controls in nonincreasing order.

Moving budget into the first step

Now suppose \(a\ge b\). Increase the first control to \(s\), and adjust the second to \(b(s)\) while preserving the pair endpoint:

\(\displaystyle (1+\epsilon s)(1+\epsilon b(s)) =(1+\epsilon a)(1+\epsilon b). \)

Then

\(\displaystyle b'(s)=-\frac{1+\epsilon b(s)}{1+\epsilon s}. \)

Continue until \(s=c(p)\) or \(b(s)=0\). Feasibility of the second step is preserved: while both controls are nonnegative,

\(\displaystyle \frac{d}{ds} \left[ b(s)-c\bigl(p(1+\epsilon s)\bigr) \right] \le-\frac23+\frac29<0. \)

Here \(s\le\tfrac12\), and all intermediate masses remain at most \(p_2\le\tfrac12\).

The pair cost does not decrease, because

\(\displaystyle \frac{d}{ds}\bigl(s+b(s)\bigr) = \frac{\epsilon(s-b(s))}{1+\epsilon s}\ge0. \)

Starting with the largest control first, apply this operation repeatedly. Unless the first step becomes full, each operation eliminates a later step. Reorder the remaining tail when necessary; the swapping argument preserves feasibility. Thus, after finitely many operations, either:

  • the first control is \(c(p)\); or
  • only one step remains, and it reaches the prescribed endpoint.

Repeat on the tail after a full first step. The resulting schedule is precisely the recurrence defining \(A_\eta(x)\), and its cost is at least the original cost.

Conversely, that recurrence is a feasible schedule reaching \(x\). Hence \(A_\eta(x)\) is the exact maximum.

The function \(A_\eta\) is continuous and strictly increasing. Between consecutive greedy endpoints \(u_j,u_{j+1}\), it is affine with positive slope \(1/(\epsilon u_j)\); the formulas agree at each endpoint.

5. Above half: an exact limiting budget

Suppose a scalar trajectory starts at \(v\ge\tfrac12\), and let \(z=1-p\). For the restricted schedules from Section 3,

\(\displaystyle z^+=z(1-\epsilon\phi).\)

Therefore, if the trajectory still fails at the final time,

\(\displaystyle \epsilon\sum_t\phi_t \le \log\frac{1-v}{1-p_T} < \log\frac{1-v}{\delta}. \)

The first inequality follows from \(-\log(1-y)\ge y\).

The bound is sharp as a supremum. Take a constant conductance \(h>0\), with \(h\le\tfrac13\). Then every step is in its linear branch and

\(\displaystyle 1-p_n=(1-v)(1-\epsilon h)^n. \)

Choose the largest integer \(n\) for which \(p_n<q\). As \(h\downarrow0\),

\(\displaystyle nh\longrightarrow \frac1\epsilon\log\frac{1-v}{\delta}. \)

Indeed, \(n\) differs by at most one from the real threshold
\(\log(\delta/(1-v))/\log(1-\epsilon h)\), and
\(-\log(1-\epsilon h)/h\to\epsilon\).

This proves the first budget formula and will supply the final part of trajectories crossing half.

Target at most half

A failing restricted trajectory ends at some \(p_T<q\le\tfrac12\). Section 4 gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\phi_t \le A_\eta(p_T)<A_\eta(q). \)

Conversely, greedy trajectories ending at \(x<q\) have costs \(A_\eta(x)\), which approach \(A_\eta(q)\) as \(x\uparrow q\). This proves the second formula, including sufficiency at equality.

Target above half

Assume \(\eta<\tfrac12<q\).

If a failing trajectory never reaches half, its cost is at most \(A_\eta(\tfrac12)\). The proposed budget is strictly larger: the candidate \(p=\tfrac12\) contributes

\(\displaystyle A_\eta(\tfrac12)+\frac1\epsilon\log\frac{1/2}{\delta}. \)

Otherwise, let \(p<\tfrac12\) be the mass immediately before the first crossing step, and let \(v\ge\tfrac12\) be its endpoint. The step satisfies

\(\displaystyle v=p(1+\epsilon\phi), \qquad \frac12\le v\le G(p), \qquad v<q \)

\(\displaystyle A_\eta(p) +\frac{v/p-1}{\epsilon} +\frac1\epsilon\log\frac{1-v}{\delta}. \)

At fixed \(p\), maximize this expression over

\(\displaystyle \frac1\epsilon\left(\frac1p-\frac1{1-v}\right). \)

Its unconstrained maximum is at \(v=1-p\). Since \(1-p\ge\tfrac12\), its constrained maximum is at

\(\displaystyle v=w(p)=\min\{1-p,G(p),q\}.\)

This gives exactly the third budget formula.
