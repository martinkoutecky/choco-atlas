The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over ideal locations on \(\mathbb{R}\), let a randomized mechanism choose a facility location \(Y\). For each \(\gamma\), determine the minimum worst-case ratio \(\mathbb{E}[\max_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}|x-Y|]/(\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)/2)\), subject to normalized standard deviation at most \(\gamma\) on every positive-diameter society and group strategyproofness against deviations by positive-mass submeasures.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is a finitely supported probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb R\), given by the list of its \(\tau\) distinct ideal locations and their positive binary-rational masses, summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
A mechanism \(M\) maps every such society to a distribution of one facility location \(Y\in\mathbb R\); individual cost is \(c_M(x;\mu)=\mathbb E_{Y\sim M(\mu)}|x-Y|\). Locations and reports may be any real numbers. On rationally encoded inputs, let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(\tau,L\) and any encoded parameters, with the number of facilities fixed at \(m=1\).
A coalition is any nonzero binary-rational submeasure \(\sigma\le\mu\). It may split and relocate its mass into any finitely supported binary-rational measure \(\nu\) on \(\mathbb R\) with \(\nu(\mathbb R)=\sigma(\mathbb R)\), producing \(\mu'=\mu-\sigma+\nu\). Group strategyproofness means that every such deviation has some \(x\in\operatorname{supp}\sigma\) with \(c_M(x;\mu')\ge c_M(x;\mu)\): a coalition cannot make all its members strictly better off in expected cost.
Write \(D_\mu=\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)\), \(C_\mu(Y)=\max_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}|x-Y|\), and \(o_\mu=D_\mu/2\). Require finite second moments of \(C_\mu(Y)\); on singleton societies require the facility at the unique ideal location almost surely.
For \(\gamma\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), let \(\mathcal M_\gamma\) contain all group-strategyproof mechanisms satisfying \(\operatorname{sd}_{Y\sim M(\mu)}(C_\mu(Y))/o_\mu\le\gamma\) on every society with \(D_\mu>0\).

What is the optimal approximation tradeoff \(R^*(\gamma)=\inf_{M\in\mathcal M_\gamma}\sup_{\mu:D_\mu>0}\mathbb E_{Y\sim M(\mu)}[C_\mu(Y)]/o_\mu\), for every \(\gamma\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\)?

For finite-agent societies, group-strategyproof mechanisms achieve approximation ratio \(3/2+\alpha\) and normalized standard deviation \(1/2-\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\in[0,1/2]\); a matching worst-case lower bound holds even under individual strategyproofness ([source](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~dwajc/pdfs/procaccia18.pdf)).
No result settling this exact mass-coalition tradeoff was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized standard deviation means the standard deviation of maximum cost divided by the unconstrained optimal maximum cost, not the standard deviation of the facility location.
  • Masses sum to one; ideal locations and reports remain unrestricted real numbers, with bit-complexity statements applying to rationally encoded inputs.
  • Coalitions can remove any positive rational submeasure, split its mass, and redistribute it while preserving total mass and leaving noncoalition reports unchanged.
  • Group strategyproofness excludes deviations making every coalition member strictly better off in expected distance, matching the anchor's convention.
  • The variance budget ranges over all nonnegative real values and applies uniformly across positive-diameter societies.
  • Singleton societies output their unique ideal location almost surely, preserving the zero-optimum convention of bounded approximation.
  • The optimal value is defined as an infimum over mechanisms, without assuming attainment; admissible mechanisms have finite second moments.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The optimal tradeoff is

\[ R^*(\gamma)=\max\left\{\frac32,\,2-\gamma\right\} \qquad\text{for every }\gamma\ge0. \]

The infimum is attained. Rational masses allow every individual mass deviation to be represented by a finite coalition of equally weighted agents. This representation is used only in the proof; implementing the optimal mechanism requires no population expansion.

1. Finite-agent results used

We use the finite-agent results supplied in the question, in the following precise form.

For every \(\alpha\in[0,1/2]\), the mechanism that selects uniformly from the four indexed locations

\[ a,\qquad \frac{a+b}{2}-\alpha(b-a),\qquad \frac{a+b}{2}+\alpha(b-a),\qquad b, \]

where \(a,b\) are the extreme reports, is group strategyproof in expected distance. Coincident indexed locations retain their combined probability.

Moreover, for every \(r\in[3/2,2)\), any individually strategyproof two-agent mechanism with worst-case expected approximation ratio at most \(r\) satisfies

\[ \sup_{u\ne v} \frac{\operatorname{sd}\!\left(\max\{|u-Y|,|v-Y|\}\right)} {|u-v|/2} \ge 2-r. \]

These are the upper construction and worst-case lower bound of [Theorems 2.3 and 2.4 in the cited paper](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~dwajc/pdfs/procaccia18.pdf). The latter is a supremum statement; it does not assert a pointwise bound at every profile.

2. Construction and mass-coalition strategyproofness

Fix \(\gamma\ge0\), and put \(s=\min\{\gamma,1/2\}\). For a society \(\mu\), let

\[ a=\min\operatorname{supp}\mu,\qquad b=\max\operatorname{supp}\mu,\qquad D=b-a. \]

Define \(M_s(\mu)\) by selecting each of the following four indexed locations with probability \(1/4\):

\[ a,\qquad a+sD,\qquad b-sD,\qquad b. \]

This is the finite-agent construction above with \(\alpha=1/2-s\). On a singleton society, all four locations coincide with its unique ideal location.

We now verify group strategyproofness for every permitted mass deviation, including splitting.

Let \(\sigma\le\mu\) be a nonzero coalition and let \(\nu\) be a permitted report measure of equal total mass. Set \(\mu'=\mu-\sigma+\nu\). Because all masses involved are rational and both supports are finite, there is a positive integer \(N\) such that every atomic mass of \(\mu,\sigma,\nu\) is an integer multiple of \(1/N\).

Represent \(\mu\) by \(N\) labelled agents, placing exactly \(N\mu(\{x\})\) agents at each \(x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\). Select exactly \(N\sigma(\{x\})\) of those agents for the deviating coalition. This is possible because \(\sigma\le\mu\), and the selected coalition is nonempty.

Assign reports to the selected agents so that exactly \(N\nu(\{z\})\) selected agents report each \(z\in\operatorname{supp}\nu\). Such an assignment exists because

\[ \sum_z N\nu(\{z\})=\sum_x N\sigma(\{x\}). \]

The resulting empirical report measure is exactly \(\mu'\).

The finite-agent mechanism depends only on the extreme reports. Its output distribution before and after this deviation therefore agrees exactly with \(M_s(\mu)\) and \(M_s(\mu')\), respectively. Finite-agent group strategyproofness supplies a selected agent, whose true location is some \(x\in\operatorname{supp}\sigma\), such that

\[ c_{M_s}(x;\mu')\ge c_{M_s}(x;\mu). \]

This is precisely the required mass-coalition condition. The argument accommodates any number of reported locations and any permitted splitting of coalition mass.

3. Exact approximation and standard deviation

Suppose \(D>0\), and write \(o=D/2\). Since \(0\le s\le1/2\), all four output locations lie in \([a,b]\).

At either endpoint, the maximum cost equals \(D=2o\). At either inner location, it equals

\[ (1-s)D=2(1-s)o. \]

Thus \(C_\mu(Y)/o\) takes the two values \(2\) and \(2(1-s)\), each with probability \(1/2\), allowing coincidence when \(s=0\). Consequently,

\[ \frac{\mathbb E[C_\mu(Y)]}{o}=2-s, \qquad \frac{\operatorname{sd}(C_\mu(Y))}{o}=s. \]

The distribution has finite support, so its second moment is finite. Together with the singleton condition and Section 2, this proves \(M_s\in\mathcal M_\gamma\). Hence

\[ R^*(\gamma)\le 2-\min\{\gamma,1/2\} =\max\{3/2,2-\gamma\}. \]

4. Lower bound

Let \(M\in\mathcal M_\gamma\), and denote its worst-case expected approximation ratio by \(r\). If \(r\) is infinite, the desired lower bound is immediate. Assume henceforth that \(r\) is finite.

Define a two-agent mechanism by

\[ F(u,v)=M\!\left(\frac12\delta_u+\frac12\delta_v\right). \]

Moving either agent’s report is an allowed mass deviation: select the submeasure of mass \(1/2\) at that agent’s true location and relocate it to the reported location. This remains valid when both agents occupy the same location. The mass-coalition condition therefore implies that \(F\) is individually strategyproof.

The singleton requirement gives \(F(u,u)=\delta_u\). For \(u\ne v\), its maximum-cost objective and optimum coincide with those of the corresponding society. Thus \(F\) has approximation ratio at most \(r\), finite second moments, and normalized standard deviation at most \(\gamma\) at every nondegenerate profile.

A direct proof that \(r\ge3/2\)

Let \(Y\sim F(0,1)\). The triangle inequality gives

\[ \mathbb E|Y|+\mathbb E|1-Y|\ge1. \]

At least one endpoint therefore has expected cost at least \(1/2\).

If \(\mathbb E|1-Y|\ge1/2\), strategyproofness prevents the agent at \(1\) from improving by reporting \(2\). For \(Y'\sim F(0,2)\), this yields

\[ \mathbb E|1-Y'|\ge\frac12. \]

For every real \(y\),

\[ \max\{|y|,|2-y|\}=1+|y-1|. \]

The optimum at \((0,2)\) equals \(1\), so this profile has expected approximation ratio at least \(3/2\).

If instead \(\mathbb E|Y|\ge1/2\), let the agent at \(0\) report \(-1\). Strategyproofness gives expected distance from \(0\) at least \(1/2\) under \(F(-1,1)\). Using

\[ \max\{|y+1|,|y-1|\}=1+|y|, \]

and again an optimum of \(1\), gives the same lower bound. Hence \(r\ge3/2\).

The variance-dependent lower bound

Suppose \(0\le\gamma<1/2\) and, for contradiction, \(r<2-\gamma\). The preceding argument gives

\[ \frac32\le r<2. \]

All hypotheses of the finite-agent lower bound in Section 1 have now been verified for \(F\). Applying it gives

\[ \sup_{u\ne v} \frac{\operatorname{sd}(C_{u,v}(Y))}{|u-v|/2} \ge 2-r>\gamma, \]

contradicting the uniform standard-deviation bound inherited from \(M\).

Therefore \(r\ge2-\gamma\) when \(\gamma<1/2\). When \(\gamma\ge1/2\), the bound \(r\ge3/2\) suffices. Together with Section 3, this proves the stated formula.

5. Running time

The mechanism scans the \(\tau\) distinct locations to find the two extremes, then computes at most four output atoms. It uses \(O(\tau)\) comparisons and \(O(1)\) additional arithmetic operations, with the number of facilities fixed at \(m=1\).

For rationally encoded locations of total input length \(L\) and a rational parameter \(\gamma\) of bit length \(B\), elementary exact integer arithmetic gives the conservative bound

\[ O\!\left(\tau(L+B)^2\right) \]

bit operations. The output description has length \(O(L+B)\). Masses need not be expanded into agents.

For an arbitrary unencoded real parameter, the four formulas define the mechanism mathematically; a bit running time requires an encoding or computational representation of that parameter.
