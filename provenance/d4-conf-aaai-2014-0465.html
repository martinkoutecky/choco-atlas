The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(K\) options, an unknown society \(\mu\) over complete weak rankings, and an oracle that answers a queried pair by sampling a fresh member of the society, design an adaptive algorithm that returns a ranking \(\tau\) with \(d_M(\tau,\prec^{CO_\epsilon}_\mu)<\rho\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\), while bounding or minimizing its expected number of pair queries.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=K\ge 2\). A society is specified by the list of its \(\tau\) complete weak ranking types with positive binary rational masses summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types, and \(L\) is the encoding bit length.
To preserve unknown-society elicitation, this list describes the oracle's instance and is hidden from the algorithm. Each query chooses distinct options and independently draws a fresh type, revealing only their comparison; its numerical outcome is \(1\) for preference for the first option, \(0\) for the second, and \(1/2\) for indifference.
Write \(y_{ij}\) for the expected outcome. Given binary rational \(\epsilon>0\), define \(d_i^*=|\{j\ne i:y_{ij}>1/2+\epsilon\}|\) and \(s_i^*=|\{j\ne i:|y_{ij}-1/2|\le\epsilon\}|\).
The target strict partial order is \(o_i\prec^{CO_\epsilon}_\mu o_j\) exactly when \(d_i^*+s_i^*<d_j^*\); equal or overlapping score intervals impose no comparison.
For a permutation \(r\), with rank \(1\) best, define \(d_M(r,\prec)=\min_{r'\in\mathcal L(\prec)}\max_i|r_i-r'_i|\), where linear extensions satisfy \(o_i\prec o_j\Rightarrow r'_j<r'_i\).
Given \(\rho\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and binary rational \(0<\delta<1\), require \(\Pr[d_M(r,\prec^{CO_\epsilon}_\mu)<\rho]\ge1-\delta\). Count oracle queries separately from computation, expressing bounds in \(m,\tau,L,\rho,\epsilon^{-1},\log(1/\delta)\).

Is this PAC rank elicitation problem solvable in polynomial expected time and polynomial expected queries in these parameters, or is it NP-hard?

Known: finite societies are covered by an adaptive PAC algorithm with an instance-dependent expected-query bound. Its guarantee is formulated for stochastic pairwise observations and also covers exactly the distribution-sampling oracle above, rather than only finite-agent inputs. I prove both statements below. Throughout, $L$ retains its stated meaning: the hidden society’s encoding length.

1. A deterministic interval lemma

Write
$$ a_i=d_i^*,\qquad b_i=d_i^*+s_i^*. $$
Thus $a_i\le b_i$, and the target comparison is
$$ o_i\prec^{CO_\epsilon}_\mu o_j \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b_i<a_j. $$

This relation is irreflexive because $b_i\ge a_i$. It is transitive: if $b_i<a_j$ and $b_j<a_k$, then
$$ b_i<a_j\le b_j<a_k. $$
It is therefore a strict partial order.

Lemma. Suppose that numbers $c_i$ satisfy
$$ a_i\le c_i\le b_i $$
for every $i$. Sorting the options by decreasing $c_i$, with arbitrary tie breaking, produces a linear extension of the target order.

Proof. Every required comparison satisfies
$$ c_i\le b_i<a_j\le c_j. $$
Consequently, $j$ appears before $i$, as the definition of a linear extension requires. Thus the resulting permutation $r$ belongs to $\mathcal L(\prec^{CO_\epsilon}_\mu)$, and
$$ d_M(r,\prec^{CO_\epsilon}_\mu)=0. $$
This proves the lemma.

The algorithm therefore needs to find one score inside each interval; it need not identify the interval endpoints.

2. A uniform polynomial query algorithm

If $\epsilon\ge 1/2$, all pairwise means belong to the insensitive region. Every interval is $[0,m-1]$, so any permutation succeeds without queries.

Assume henceforth that $0<\epsilon<1/2$. Define
$$ B=\binom m2,\qquad k=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{2B}{\delta}\right\rceil,\qquad n=\left\lceil k\epsilon^{-2}\right\rceil. $$

For each pair $i<j$, query $(i,j)$ exactly $n$ times and let $\widehat y_{ij}$ be the sample mean. Define
$$ \widehat y_{ji}=1-\widehat y_{ij}. $$
This is justified because, for every complete weak ranking type, reversing a comparison replaces its outcome $Y_{ij}$ by $1-Y_{ij}$, including indifference. Hence
$$ y_{ji}=1-y_{ij}, \qquad |\widehat y_{ji}-y_{ji}|=|\widehat y_{ij}-y_{ij}|. $$

Compute
$$ \widehat d_i = \left|\left\{j\ne i:\widehat y_{ij}>\frac12\right\}\right|, $$
and output the permutation obtained by sorting these integers in decreasing order.

Probability guarantee

For each sampled pair, the observations are independent, have the same distribution, and belong to $[0,1]$: each query draws a fresh type from the fixed society. Hoeffding’s inequality therefore gives
$$ \Pr\!\left[|\widehat y_{ij}-y_{ij}|\ge\epsilon\right] \le 2\exp(-2n\epsilon^2). $$
A union bound yields
$$ \Pr\!\left[\exists i<j: |\widehat y_{ij}-y_{ij}|\ge\epsilon\right] \le 2B\exp(-2k) \le 2B\,2^{-k} \le\delta. $$

Let $E$ denote the complementary event, including the inferred reverse estimates. On $E$, fix an option $i$.

If $y_{ij}>1/2+\epsilon$, then
$$ \widehat y_{ij}>y_{ij}-\epsilon>\frac12, $$
so this strong win contributes to $\widehat d_i$.

If $y_{ij}<1/2-\epsilon$, then
$$ \widehat y_{ij}<y_{ij}+\epsilon<\frac12, $$
so this strong loss does not contribute.

Every remaining comparison satisfies $|y_{ij}-1/2|\le\epsilon$ and can contribute at most one. Consequently,
$$ d_i^*\le\widehat d_i\le d_i^*+s_i^*. $$
The interval lemma applies. Thus, on $E$, the output is an exact linear extension. Since $\rho$ is a positive integer,
$$ \Pr\!\left[d_M(r,\prec^{CO_\epsilon}_\mu)<\rho\right] \ge\Pr(E)\ge1-\delta. $$

Means exactly at $1/2\pm\epsilon$ belong to the insensitive set, so the proof requires no separation from either boundary.

Query and computation bounds

The deterministic query count is
$$ Q=Bn = O\!\left( m^2\epsilon^{-2} \left(\log m+\log(1/\delta)\right) \right). $$
It also bounds the expected query count. Neither $\tau$ nor the hidden society encoding is used.

The observations can be processed exactly. For each pair, maintain
$$ T_{ij}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}2Y_{ij}^{(\ell)}. $$
Each summand belongs to $\{0,1,2\}$, and
$$ \widehat y_{ij}>\frac12 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T_{ij}>n. $$
The counters require $O(\log(n+1))$ bits. Once $k$ and $n$ have been computed, response processing, score computation, and sorting require
$$ O\!\left((Q+m^2)\log(m(n+1))\right) $$
bit operations.

If $D$ denotes the total encoding length of the visible numerical parameters, computing $k$ and $n$ adds polynomial time in $D$. Thus the complete algorithm is polynomial in the requested parameters together with $D$. This statement does not redefine $L$.

The supplied paper uses the same stochastic comparison outcomes $0,1/2,1$ and unknown-distribution sampling model. The proof above is independent of its adaptive algorithm. 3. Why the visible encoding issue is substantive

The following result strengthens the verifier’s observation that numerical values do not bound their rational encoding lengths.

Proposition. Suppose visible inputs are supplied on a sequential input tape, with each rational encoded as its binary numerator, a delimiter, and its binary denominator, without leading zeros. Reading or traversing an input symbol costs a computation step. Then no uniform algorithm can satisfy the PAC requirement on all instances while having both expected computation and expected queries bounded by polynomials in exactly
$$ m,\tau,L,\rho,\epsilon^{-1},\log(1/\delta). $$

This proposition concerns a specified, conventional input model. It does not assert the same obstruction for every possible input-access convention.

Proof

Fix
$$ m=2,\qquad \rho=1,\qquad \epsilon=\frac18. $$

Consider two societies, each supported on the two strict rankings:

  • In society $\mu_+$, $o_1$ is preferred to $o_2$ with probability $3/4$, and the reverse ranking has probability $1/4$.
  • In society $\mu_-$, these probabilities are reversed.

Both have fixed support size $\tau=2$ and fixed society encoding length $L$. Their pairwise means are
$$ y_{12}^{(+)}=\frac34, \qquad y_{12}^{(-)}=\frac14. $$
Because $\epsilon=1/8$, each target is a total order. The successful permutation places $o_1$ first under $\mu_+$ and $o_2$ first under $\mu_-$. Since $\rho=1$, the required distance must be zero.

Suppose an algorithm with the asserted polynomial bounds exists. Let $W$ be its computation cost plus its number of oracle queries.

For every integer $N\ge1$, consider
$$ \delta_N^{\mathrm e} = \frac{2^N}{2^{N+2}+1}. $$
These fractions are reduced, and
$$ \frac15<\delta_N^{\mathrm e}<\frac14. $$
All listed parameters therefore remain bounded independently of $N$. The assumed polynomial bounds imply the existence of a constant $C\ge1$ such that
$$ \mathbb E_{\mu_+,\delta_N^{\mathrm e}}[W]\le C $$
for every $N$.

Set $H=\lceil4C\rceil$ and choose $N>H$. Markov’s inequality gives
$$ \Pr_{\mu_+,\delta_N^{\mathrm e}}[W>H]\le\frac14. $$
The PAC guarantee gives probability greater than $3/4$ of outputting the permutation with $o_1$ first. Hence the event
$$ A= \{\text{halt by cost }H\text{ and output }o_1\text{ first}\} $$
has probability at least $1/2$.

Now replace the visible failure parameter by
$$ \delta_N^{\mathrm h} = \frac{2^N}{2^{N+M}+1}, $$
where $M$ will be chosen below. This fraction is also reduced. Its encoded numerator is identical to that of $\delta_N^{\mathrm e}$ and contains $N+1$ bits.

Because $N>H$, a computation of cost at most $H$ cannot reach the delimiter following that numerator. The two visible inputs are therefore indistinguishable throughout every execution counted by $A$. Under the same society $\mu_+$, their early-execution distributions coincide. Thus
$$ \Pr_{\mu_+,\delta_N^{\mathrm h}}(A)\ge\frac12. $$

Next compare the two societies on the common hard input. For either orientation of the queried pair, every possible observation has positive probability under both societies, and the ratio
$$ \frac{\Pr_{\mu_-}(\text{observation})} {\Pr_{\mu_+}(\text{observation})} $$
is either $3$ or $1/3$.

Fix the algorithm’s internal randomness. Along any transcript containing $q$ queries, its likelihood under $\mu_-$ is therefore at least $3^{-q}$ times its likelihood under $\mu_+$. Adaptive query choices cause no additional factor: identical previous observations and internal randomness determine identical next queries.

Every transcript in $A$ has $q\le H$. Summing over such transcripts and averaging over internal randomness gives
$$ \Pr_{\mu_-,\delta_N^{\mathrm h}}(A) \ge 3^{-H}\Pr_{\mu_+,\delta_N^{\mathrm h}}(A) \ge \frac{1}{2\cdot3^H}. $$

On $\mu_-$, every output in $A$ is incorrect. Choose an integer $M$ such that
$$ 2^{-M}<\frac{1}{2\cdot3^H}. $$
Then
$$ \delta_N^{\mathrm h} < 2^{-M} < \frac{1}{2\cdot3^H}. $$
The algorithm’s error probability on this valid hard input exceeds its requested failure probability, contradicting the PAC guarantee. This proves the proposition.

4. Scope of the conclusion

The substantive elicitation result is proved: polynomially many oracle queries suffice, and the computation after numerical preprocessing is polynomial. Including visible numerical encoding lengths gives a complete polynomial-time algorithm.

For the society-only interpretation of $L$, the full requested computation bound is impossible in the sequential input model just specified. This obstruction already occurs for two options and two ranking types; it does not arise from combinatorial optimization.

An unqualified choice between “polynomial-time solvable in exactly the listed parameters” and “NP-hard” still requires an input-access convention. No NP-hardness claim is established here.
