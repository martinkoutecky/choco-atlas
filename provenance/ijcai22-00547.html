The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational \(\mu\in\Delta_m\), a designated candidate \(c_1\), and a common channel \(P=(p_{ij})\), let \(\widehat{\tau}_j=\sum_i\mu_i p_{ij}\) and \(q_{ij}=\mu_i p_{ij}/\widehat{\tau}_j\). Under the theorem's posterior-sign promise, compute the minimum deleted mass \(d_\infty(\mu,P)=\min\{\sum_jx_j:0\le x_j\le\widehat{\tau}_j,\ \mu_1-\sum_jq_{1j}x_j\ge\mu_i-\sum_jq_{ij}x_j\ \forall i\ge2\}\), an optimal \(x\), and \(d_\infty(\mu,P)/d_0(\mu)\), where \(d_0(\mu)=\sum_{i=2}^m\max\{0,\mu_i-\mu_1\}\). A second question is to determine, for randomized response and \(\mu_i>\mu_1\), the largest \(\epsilon\) for which deleting all reported mass is the only feasible attack.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge2\) candidates under plurality, with designated candidate \(c_1\); ties count as wins, including when no mass remains.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here types are chosen candidates, \(\mu_i>0\), \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\), and \(\tau=m\); \(\tau\) denotes support size, not the number of possible types.
The common channel has binary-rational entries \(p_{ij}>0\), with \(\sum_jp_{ij}=1\), where \(p_{ij}\) is the probability of reporting \(j\) given true type \(i\). Set \(\widehat{\tau}_j=\sum_i\mu_i p_{ij}\) and \(q_{ij}=\mu_i p_{ij}/\widehat{\tau}_j\).
Deleting reported mass \(x_j\in[0,\widehat{\tau}_j]\) costs \(x_j\) and removes true-type mass \(q_{ij}x_j\). Define \(F(\mu,P)=\{x:0\le x_j\le\widehat{\tau}_j,\ \mu_1-\sum_jq_{1j}x_j\ge\mu_i-\sum_jq_{ij}x_j\ \text{for every }i\ge2\}\).
Assume that for each report \(j\), either \(q_{ij}>q_{1j}\) for every \(i\ge2\), or \(q_{ij}<q_{1j}\) for every \(i\ge2\). Let \(d_\infty=\min_{x\in F(\mu,P)}\sum_jx_j\) and \(d_0=\sum_{i=2}^m\max\{0,\mu_i-\mu_1\}\); require \(d_0>0\).
For the separate threshold question, assume \(\mu_i>\mu_1\) for every \(i\ge2\), omit the sign promise, and use randomized response: \(p_{ii}(\epsilon)=e^\epsilon/(e^\epsilon+m-1)\), \(p_{ij}(\epsilon)=1/(e^\epsilon+m-1)\) for \(i\ne j\), with \(\epsilon\ge0\).

Is exact computation of \(d_\infty\), an optimal \(x\), and \(d_\infty/d_0\) polynomial-time solvable, or NP-hard, with running time measured in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length?
For randomized response, what is \(\sup\{\epsilon\ge0:F(\mu,P(\epsilon))=\{\widehat{\tau}(\epsilon)\}\}\), and is this supremum attained?

Known results: the finite-voter asymptotic deletion-cost ratio is characterized by an efficiently computable LP under the stated sign promise. The continuous objective above is exactly that LP after scaling by total population; thus its complexity target is already settled by the existing formulation. Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact computation. All three requested quantities are polynomial-time computable in \(m,\tau,L\). For randomized response, the supremum is \(\log(\max_{i\ge2}\mu_i/\mu_1)\), and it is not attained.

1. Proof of polynomial-time exact computation.

The defining inequalities give the rational linear program

\(\displaystyle \min \sum_{j=1}^m x_j,\qquad 0\le x_j\le\widehat{\tau}_j,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^m(q_{ij}-q_{1j})x_j\ge\mu_i-\mu_1\quad(2\le i\le m).\)

It has \(m=\tau\) variables and \(3m-1\) inequalities. Its optimum is exactly \(d_\infty\).

First, this program is feasible. Positivity of the input entries implies \(\widehat{\tau}_j>0\), and

\(\displaystyle \sum_jq_{ij}\widehat{\tau}_j=\sum_j\mu_i p_{ij}=\mu_i.\)

Thus full deletion, \(x=\widehat{\tau}\), leaves every candidate with zero mass and satisfies all winning inequalities. The feasible region is closed and bounded, so the minimum exists.

Second, the program has polynomial binary encoding length. Each \(\widehat{\tau}_j=\sum_i\mu_i p_{ij}\) is obtained by summing \(m\) rational products, and each \(q_{ij}=\mu_i p_{ij}/\widehat{\tau}_j\) is obtained by division by a positive rational. Products, these sums, divisions, and differences all have bit length polynomial in the total input length \(L\). Consequently, the entire LP can be constructed in polynomial time.

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming computes an exact rational optimum and an exact rational optimal solution whenever a finite optimum exists. Both hypotheses were verified above. Polynomial output length also follows from the usual vertex bound: after clearing denominators, Cramer’s rule expresses vertex coordinates using determinants whose bit lengths are polynomial in the LP encoding length.

Finally, \(d_0=\sum_{i=2}^m\max\{0,\mu_i-\mu_1\}\) is exactly computable using rational comparisons and additions. Since \(d_0>0\), exact division computes \(d_\infty/d_0\) in polynomial time.

This agrees with the existing asymptotic LP characterization under the sign promise in [Tao et al., Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00771#page=4). For the continuous problem defined here, the direct LP argument actually works without that promise.

2. Proof of the randomized-response threshold.

Assume throughout this part that \(\mu_i>\mu_1\) for every \(i\ge2\). Define

\(\displaystyle M=\max_{i\ge2}\mu_i,\qquad R=\frac{M}{\mu_1}>1,\qquad t=e^\epsilon\ge1,\qquad D=t+m-1.\)

For randomized response,

\(\displaystyle p_{ij}=\frac{1+(t-1)\mathbf 1_{\{i=j\}}}{D},\qquad \widehat{\tau}_j=\frac{1+(t-1)\mu_j}{D}>0.\)

Write \(y_j=\widehat{\tau}_j-x_j\) for retained reported mass. The remaining true mass of candidate \(i\) is

\(\displaystyle r_i=\mu_i-\sum_jq_{ij}x_j=\sum_jq_{ij}y_j.\)

Hence feasibility means \(0\le y_j\le\widehat{\tau}_j\) and \(r_1\ge r_i\) for every opponent. Full deletion corresponds exactly to \(y=0\).

Choose an opponent \(k\) with \(\mu_k=M\). Its posterior advantage over candidate \(1\) is

\(\displaystyle q_{kj}-q_{1j}= \begin{cases} \dfrac{M-\mu_1t}{D\widehat{\tau}_1},&j=1,\\[4pt] \dfrac{Mt-\mu_1}{D\widehat{\tau}_k},&j=k,\\[4pt] \dfrac{M-\mu_1}{D\widehat{\tau}_j},&j\notin\{1,k\}. \end{cases}\)

If \(t<R\), the first numerator is positive. The other numerators are positive because \(M>\mu_1\) and \(t\ge1\). Therefore any nonzero \(y\ge0\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle r_k-r_1=\sum_j(q_{kj}-q_{1j})y_j>0.\)

Such a vector is infeasible. Thus \(F(\mu,P(\epsilon))=\{\widehat{\tau}(\epsilon)\}\) whenever \(t<R\).

Conversely, suppose \(t\ge R\). Retain all mass reported as candidate \(1\), deleting all other reports:

\(\displaystyle y_1=\widehat{\tau}_1,\qquad y_j=0\quad(j\ge2).\)

This gives

\(\displaystyle r_1=\frac{\mu_1t}{D},\qquad r_i=\frac{\mu_i}{D}\quad(i\ge2).\)

Since \(\mu_1t\ge M\ge\mu_i\), this is feasible and retains positive mass. Full deletion is therefore not unique.

We have proved the exact equivalence

\(\displaystyle F(\mu,P(\epsilon))=\{\widehat{\tau}(\epsilon)\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0\le\epsilon<\log R.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sup\{\epsilon\ge0:F(\mu,P(\epsilon))=\{\widehat{\tau}(\epsilon)\}\}=\log\!\left(\frac{\max_{i\ge2}\mu_i}{\mu_1}\right),\)

and this supremum is not attained.

3. The boundary and the source’s uniqueness claim.

At \(t=R\), the coefficient \(q_{k1}-q_{11}\) is zero, while every coefficient \(q_{kj}-q_{1j}\) with \(j\ge2\) is strictly positive. The inequality \(r_k-r_1\le0\) therefore forces \(y_j=0\) for every \(j\ge2\).

Conversely, every \(y=\alpha e_1\) with \(0\le\alpha\le\widehat{\tau}_1\) is feasible, because \(\mu_1t=M\ge\mu_i\). Thus the entire boundary feasible set is

\(\displaystyle F(\mu,P(\log R))=\{\widehat{\tau}(\log R)-\alpha e_1:0\le\alpha\le\widehat{\tau}_1(\log R)\}.\)

In particular, its optimal deletion cost is \(1-\widehat{\tau}_1(\log R)<1\).

The non-strict uniqueness statement in [Tao et al., Lemma 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00771#page=11) therefore fails at equality for this continuous model. The proof’s tightness argument cannot force full deletion in coordinate \(1\) when that coordinate has zero coefficient. The feasible segment above supplies the explicit boundary correction.
