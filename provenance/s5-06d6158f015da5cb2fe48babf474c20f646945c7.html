Lemma 4.1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of complete patient-donor compatibility types with program ownership, rational masses summing to one, a type-level directed compatibility relation, and a rational coverage threshold, determine whether there are nonnegative masses \(x_q\) on allowed two- and three-cycle templates \(q\) such that type use satisfies \( \sum_q a_{tq}x_q\le\mu_t \) for every type \(t\), total coverage satisfies \( \sum_q |q|x_q\ge\kappa \), and no program \(i\) can choose rejected masses \(0\le r_q\le x_q\) and replacement masses \(z_q\ge0\), supported only on cycles internal to \(i\), with \( \sum_q a_{tq}(x_q-r_q+z_q)\le\mu_t \) for every \(t\) and \( \sum_q b_{iq}(x_q-r_q+z_q)>\sum_q b_{iq}x_q \).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) complete patient–donor types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Each type has one owner \(o(t)\in\{1,\ldots,p\}\), and every program owns a supported type; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also specifies a directed compatibility relation \(E\subseteq T\times T\) with \(m=|E|\), and a binary rational threshold \(\kappa\in[0,1]\). Compatibility depends only on types; an arc means donor-to-patient compatibility between distinct pairs.
Let \(Q\) contain all cyclic type sequences of length \(2\) or \(3\) whose consecutive arcs, including the closing arc, belong to \(E\), identified up to cyclic rotation. Repeated types are allowed and represent distinct pairs; there are no one-pair cycles or chains.
For \(q\in Q\), let \(a_{tq}\) count occurrences of \(t\), let \(|q|=\sum_t a_{tq}\), and let \(b_{iq}=\sum_{t:o(t)=i}a_{tq}\). A template is \(i\)-internal when all its positions have owner \(i\).
A packing consists of real masses \(x_q\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_q a_{tq}x_q\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\); its coverage is \(\sum_q|q|x_q\).
Program \(i\) rejects \(x\) if there exist real masses \(0\le r_q\le x_q\) and \(z_q\ge0\), with \(z_q=0\) outside \(i\)-internal templates, such that \(\sum_q a_{tq}(x_q-r_q+z_q)\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\) and \(\sum_q b_{iq}(x_q-r_q+z_q)>\sum_q b_{iq}x_q\). Rejection may remove arbitrary template masses, including none; there is no bound on the number removed. A packing is rejection-proof when no program rejects it.

Is deciding whether a rejection-proof packing of coverage at least \(\kappa\) exists solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,p\) and the total input bit length \(L\).

For discrete vertex-disjoint cycle packings of lengths \(2\) and \(3\), ETH rules out time \(2^{o(n\log n)}\), even with two programs; this also holds when each rejection removes at most any fixed \(c\ge2\) cycles. The unrestricted discrete problem admits time \(2^{O(k\log k)}+n^{O(1)}\), where \(k\) is the coverage threshold. [Source](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol358-ipec2025/LIPIcs.IPEC.2025.9/LIPIcs.IPEC.2025.9.pdf)
No result settling this exact continuous problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Defined complete types operationally: ownership and donor-to-patient compatibility are constant within each type.
  • Defined templates as all compatible cyclic sequences of length two or three, allowing repeated types to represent distinct pairs and counting their multiplicities; diagonal compatibility concerns distinct pairs, never self-transplantation.
  • Restricted the normalized coverage threshold to [0,1], and omitted empty programs by requiring each program to own a supported type.
  • Specified real divisible masses for allocations and deviations, with strict improvement in owned covered mass and no restriction on rejected templates or their number.
  • Defined m as the number of compatibility arcs and L as total input bit length.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-complete}\) when \(p\) is part of the input. This holds even with dyadic masses, no compatibility loops, and only internal cycles of length \(2\) and external cycles of length \(3\).

Writing \(N=|Q|\), a given rational packing can be checked for rejection-proofness in polynomial time. The existence problem admits an exact algorithm with running time

\(\displaystyle \binom{N+\tau}{\tau}\,p\,(N+\tau+L)^{O(1)}.\)

Since \(N=O(m\tau)\), this is at most

\(\displaystyle 2^{O(\tau\log(m+2))}(m+\tau+p+L)^{O(1)}.\)

The proof follows.

1. A linear-programming characterization of rejection

Let \(T_i=\{t:o(t)=i\}\), and let \(Q_i\) denote the \(i\)-internal templates. Fix a feasible packing \(x\), and define its unused type masses by

\(\displaystyle s_t=\mu_t-\sum_{q\in Q}a_{tq}x_q\ge 0.\)

The maximum improvement available to program \(i\) is the optimum of the following linear program:

\(\displaystyle \max\left\{\sum_{q\in Q_i}|q|z_q-\sum_{q\in Q}b_{iq}r_q\right\},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{q\in Q_i}a_{tq}z_q-\sum_{q\in Q}a_{tq}r_q\le s_t\qquad(t\in T_i),\)

and \(0\le r_q\le x_q\), \(z_q\ge 0\).

These are exactly the necessary capacity constraints: at types outside \(T_i\), removing template mass can only decrease consumption, and internal additions consume no such types. Thus program \(i\) rejects precisely when this optimum is strictly positive.

The LP is feasible because \(r=z=0\) is feasible. It is bounded: \(r\le x\), and the capacity constraints imply that every internal addition is bounded by the owned masses. Consequently, strong LP duality applies.

Its dual has variables \(y_t\ge 0\) for \(t\in T_i\) and \(\lambda_q\ge 0\) for \(q\in Q\):

\(\displaystyle \min\left\{\sum_{t\in T_i}s_ty_t+\sum_{q\in Q}x_q\lambda_q\right\},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_i}a_{tq}y_t\ge |q|\qquad(q\in Q_i),\)

and

\(\displaystyle \lambda_q\ge\sum_{t\in T_i}a_{tq}y_t-b_{iq}\qquad(q\in Q).\)

The dual is feasible, for example with all \(y_t=1\) and all \(\lambda_q=0\). Every term in its objective is nonnegative.

It follows that program \(i\) does not reject \(x\) if and only if there are \(y_t\ge 0\), \(t\in T_i\), satisfying all three conditions:

  • For every \(q\in Q_i\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_i}a_{tq}y_t\ge |q|.\)

  • For every \(t\in T_i\),

\(\displaystyle s_ty_t=0.\)

  • For every \(q\) with \(x_q>0\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_i}a_{tq}y_t\le b_{iq}.\)

Indeed, a dual solution of value zero gives these conditions. Conversely, given these conditions, setting

\(\displaystyle \lambda_q=\max\left\{0,\sum_{t\in T_i}a_{tq}y_t-b_{iq}\right\}\)

produces a dual solution of value zero.

This characterization also proves that checking a rational packing requires only \(p\) polynomial-size LPs.

2. Hardness construction

We reduce from Independent Set on simple \(3\)-regular graphs, which is NP-complete. The hardness of maximum independent set on cubic graphs is established in [Alimonti and Kann, Section 3](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/aspnes/pinewiki/attachments/MarkovChains/alimanti-mis-cubic.pdf).

Let the source instance be a simple \(3\)-regular graph \(G=(V,F)\), with \(n=|V|\), and threshold \(k\). We may assume \(0\le k\le n/2\). Indeed, if \(I\) is independent, all \(3|I|\) edges incident with \(I\) go to its complement, whose total degree is \(3(n-|I|)\); hence \(|I|\le n/2\). Larger thresholds are therefore trivially NO-instances.

We first describe unnormalized capacities. Scaling every capacity and packing variable by the same positive factor preserves feasibility and rejection-proofness.

2.1 Types and programs

For each vertex \(v\), introduce:

  • Types \(a_v,b_v\), with capacities \(1,2\), respectively, owned by a main program \(P_v\).
  • Twelve distinct port types \(L_{vj},R_{vj}\), for \(j\in\{1,\ldots,6\}\), each with capacity \(1\).

Indices \(j\) are cyclic modulo \(6\). For every \(v,j\), introduce a separate wire program \(W_{vj}\), owning precisely \(R_{vj}\) and \(L_{v,j+1}\).

For each edge \(e\in F\), introduce a resource type \(g_e\), of capacity \(1\). All resource types belong to one additional program \(P_*\).

Order the three edges incident with \(v\) as \(e_1(v),e_2(v),e_3(v)\), and define

\(\displaystyle (s_{v1},\ldots,s_{v6})=(a_v,b_v,g_{e_1(v)},b_v,g_{e_2(v)},g_{e_3(v)}).\)

Consecutive signals, including the last and first, are distinct.

2.2 Compatibility arcs

Include exactly the arcs appearing in these cyclic sequences:

  • Main internal cycles: \(M_v=(a_v,b_v)\).
  • Wire internal cycles: \(H_{vj}=(R_{vj},L_{v,j+1})\).
  • External cycles: \(C_{vj}=(s_{vj},L_{vj},R_{vj})\).

There are no other arcs.

The external templates have three distinct owners: the signal owner, \(W_{v,j-1}\), and \(W_{vj}\).

2.3 Verification that there are no unintended templates

Partition the nonisolated types into signals, left ports, and right ports. The arc classes are:

  • Signal-to-signal arcs only between \(a_v\) and \(b_v\).
  • Signal-to-left-port arcs.
  • Left-port-to-right-port arcs.
  • Right-port-to-signal arcs.
  • Bidirectional wire arcs between \(R_{vj}\) and \(L_{v,j+1}\).

There are no loops, so every cyclic sequence of length \(2\) or \(3\) has distinct types.

The only bidirectional pairs are \(M_v\) and \(H_{vj}\). Hence these are exactly the templates of length \(2\).

For length \(3\):

  • A cycle entirely among ports is impossible because port arcs alternate between left and right ports.
  • A cycle entirely among signals is impossible because the signal subgraph consists of disjoint bidirectional pairs.
  • A cycle with two signals and one port is impossible: a signal enters the port subgraph through a left port, and a left port has no outgoing arc to a signal.

Therefore every remaining cycle must have the form signal–left port–right port. Starting from \(s_{vj}\) and entering \(L_{vj}\), the next right port is either \(R_{vj}\) or \(R_{v,j-1}\).

The first choice gives exactly \(C_{vj}\). The second closes only if \(s_{vj}=s_{v,j-1}\), which was excluded by the signal ordering.

Thus the complete template catalogue is precisely the one specified above.

3. Rejection-proofness forces binary choices

Consider any rejection-proof packing in the unnormalized construction. Write \(x_{vj}\) for its mass on \(C_{vj}\), \(h_{vj}\) for its mass on \(H_{vj}\), and \(w_v\) for its mass on \(M_v\).

3.1 Wire programs enforce equality

A wire program owns two capacity-\(1\) types and can cover both internally with mass \(1\) on \(H_{vj}\).

If its current coverage were less than \(2\), it could remove the entire packing and add that internal mass, thereby strictly improving. Hence every rejection-proof packing covers both of its types fully.

Their capacity equations are consequently

\(\displaystyle x_{vj}+h_{vj}=1,\qquad x_{v,j+1}+h_{vj}=1.\)

Thus \(x_{vj}=x_{v,j+1}\). Going around the six wire programs yields

\(\displaystyle x_{v1}=\cdots=x_{v6}=:u_v,\qquad h_{vj}=1-u_v,\)

with \(0\le u_v\le 1\).

3.2 Main programs exclude fractional choices

The main program \(P_v\) receives external mass \(u_v\) on \(a_v\) and total external mass \(2u_v\) on \(b_v\). Its capacity constraints include

\(\displaystyle u_v+w_v\le 1,\qquad 2u_v+w_v\le 2.\)

Suppose \(0<u_v<1\), and set

\(\displaystyle \delta=\min\{u_v,1-u_v\}>0.\)

Program \(P_v\) can remove mass \(\delta\) from \(C_{v1}\), whose signal is \(a_v\), and add mass \(\delta\) to \(M_v\).

Consumption of \(a_v\) is unchanged. New consumption of \(b_v\) is feasible because

\(\displaystyle 2u_v+w_v+\delta\le 2u_v+(1-u_v)+(1-u_v)=2.\)

All other type consumptions decrease or remain unchanged. The program loses coverage \(\delta\) from the removed external mass and gains \(2\delta\) internally, a strict improvement of \(\delta\).

This contradicts rejection-proofness. Therefore

\(\displaystyle u_v\in\{0,1\}.\)

If \(u_v=0\), rejection-proofness forces \(w_v=1\): otherwise the program could add positive internal mass without removing anything. If \(u_v=1\), the capacity of \(a_v\) forces \(w_v=0\).

3.3 Resource types enforce independence

For an edge \(e=\{v,w\}\), its resource type appears once among the six external templates at each endpoint. Its capacity constraint is

\(\displaystyle u_v+u_w\le 1.\)

Since all \(u_v\) are binary, the set

\(\displaystyle I=\{v:u_v=1\}\)

is independent in \(G\).

4. Every independent set gives a rejection-proof packing

Conversely, let \(I\subseteq V\) be independent. Set \(u_v=1\) for \(v\in I\), and \(u_v=0\) otherwise. Assign

\(\displaystyle x_{C_{vj}}=u_v,\qquad x_{H_{vj}}=1-u_v,\qquad x_{M_v}=1-u_v.\)

This is feasible:

  • Every port has consumption exactly \(1\).
  • Consumption of \(a_v\) is \(1\).
  • Consumption of \(b_v\) is \(1+u_v\le 2\).
  • A resource type \(g_{\{v,w\}}\) has consumption \(u_v+u_w\le 1\).

It is rejection-proof:

  • Every wire program already covers its entire owned capacity \(2\).
  • A selected main program already covers its entire owned capacity \(3\).
  • An unselected main program has no positive external mass containing its types. After any permitted deviation, its coverage comes solely from its one internal template and is at most \(2\), which it already achieves.
  • Program \(P_*\) has no internal template. Removing mass cannot increase its coverage.

Thus rejection-proof packings correspond exactly to independent sets.

Their unnormalized coverage is

\(\displaystyle 12(n-|I|)+2(n-|I|)+18|I|=14n+4|I|.\)

Here the three terms count wire internal cycles, main internal cycles, and external cycles, respectively.

4.1 Normalization and threshold

The total capacity of the construction so far is

\(\displaystyle W=3n+12n+|F|=\frac{33n}{2}.\)

Let \(D\) be the least power of two strictly exceeding \(W\). Add one isolated type \(\omega\), owned by \(P_*\), with capacity \(D-W>0\). It belongs to no template.

Divide every capacity by \(D\), obtaining positive dyadic masses summing to \(1\). Set

\(\displaystyle \kappa=\frac{14n+4k}{D}.\)

This is a valid threshold because

\(\displaystyle 14n+4k\le 16n<W<D.\)

The resulting society has a rejection-proof packing of coverage at least \(\kappa\) if and only if \(G\) has an independent set of size at least \(k\).

The construction has

\(\displaystyle \tau=\frac{31n}{2}+1,\qquad m=32n,\qquad p=7n+1,\qquad N=13n.\)

Its encoding length is \(O(n\log n)\), and it can be produced in \(O(n\log n)\) bit operations. This proves NP-hardness.

5. Polynomial-size witnesses and an exact algorithm

It remains to justify membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), since the definition allows real packing masses.

Let

\(\displaystyle P=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^{N}:Ax\le\mu\}\)

be the ordinary packing polytope. It is nonempty and bounded: every template consumes some supported type, so each coordinate has a finite upper bound.

5.1 A rejection-proof vertex always suffices

Suppose \(x\in P\) is rejection-proof. Choose the dual vectors from Section 1 for every program. Since each type has exactly one owner, write these vectors collectively as \(y_t\), \(t\in T\).

Define

\(\displaystyle H=\{t:y_t>0\}\)

and

\(\displaystyle Z=\left\{q:\text{for some }i,\ \sum_{t\in T_i}a_{tq}y_t>b_{iq}\right\}.\)

Consider the set

\(\displaystyle F=\{v\in P:(Av)_t=\mu_t\text{ for }t\in H,\ v_q=0\text{ for }q\in Z\}.\)

The characterization in Section 1 gives \(x\in F\). This set is a face of \(P\), because it is obtained by making some packing inequalities and nonnegativity inequalities tight.

Every \(v\in F\) is rejection-proof using the same dual vectors:

  • Positive prices occur only at saturated types.
  • Positive template masses occur only where every program’s supported-template inequality holds.
  • The internal-template dual inequalities are unchanged.

Maximizing coverage over the nonempty compact face \(F\) therefore produces a rejection-proof vertex of \(F\), with coverage at least that of \(x\). A vertex of a face is a vertex of the original polytope \(P\): any convex decomposition in \(P\) must preserve the tight inequalities defining the face.

Consequently, whenever a qualifying real packing exists, a qualifying rational vertex of \(P\) exists.

All vertices of this rational polytope have polynomial encoding length. More explicitly, their nonzero coordinates are obtained by solving systems with coefficients in \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) and right-hand sides drawn from \(\mu\); determinant bounds give polynomially many bits.

Such a vertex is an NP certificate. Feasibility and coverage are checked directly, and rejection-proofness is checked using the LPs in Section 1. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), completing the NP-completeness proof.

5.2 Vertex enumeration and running time

Let a vertex have positive support \(S\), of size \(h\). The tight type constraints restricted to \(S\) must have rank \(h\). Otherwise, a nonzero direction supported on \(S\) would preserve all tight constraints, and sufficiently small perturbations in both directions would remain feasible, contradicting extremality.

Therefore \(h\le\tau\), and some set \(U\) of \(h\) tight type constraints has invertible matrix \(A_{U,S}\).

An exact algorithm enumerates every such pair \(S,U\), solves

\(\displaystyle A_{U,S}x_S=\mu_U,\qquad x_q=0\quad(q\notin S),\)

and checks feasibility, coverage, and rejection-proofness.

The number of pairs is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{h=0}^{\min(N,\tau)}\binom Nh\binom\tau h=\binom{N+\tau}{\tau}.\)

Each candidate requires polynomial-time rational linear algebra and \(p\) polynomial-size LPs. Hence the running time is

\(\displaystyle \binom{N+\tau}{\tau}\,p\,(N+\tau+L)^{O(1)}.\)

Templates can be enumerated by scanning closed walks of lengths \(2\) and \(3\), retaining one representative per cyclic rotation. With an adjacency table, this takes \(O(\tau^2+m\tau)\) word operations, plus input processing. In particular,

\(\displaystyle N\le m+m\tau=O(m\tau).\)

Using the standard binomial bound yields the stated overall bound

\(\displaystyle 2^{O(\tau\log(m+2))}(m+\tau+p+L)^{O(1)}.\)

The hardness construction uses \(p=\Theta(\tau)\); it settles the requested problem with \(p\) included in the input.
