The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates C, committee size k, and τ supported approval types A with positive rational masses μ_A summing to 1, is there an algorithm running in poly(m,τ,L) time that returns an integral size-k committee W satisfying all of the following? For every ℓ∈[k] and unelected candidate c, ∑_{A:c∈A, |A∩W|<ℓ} μ_A < ℓ/k (mass EJR+); no fractional committee x∈[0,1]^C with ∑_c x_c=k has ∑_{c∈A}x_c ≥ α★|A∩W| for every supported A, strictly for at least one; no size-k committee strictly improves every supported type's utility; and PAV_μ(W) ≥ γ★ max_{|W'|=k} PAV_μ(W'), where PAV_μ(W)=∑_A μ_A H_{|A∩W|}.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a set of \(m\) candidates and let \(k\) be an integer with \(2\le k\le m-1\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal S\) of \(\tau\ge1\) distinct nonempty approval sets \(A\subseteq C\), each with a positive binary rational mass \(\mu_A\), with \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal S}\mu_A=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval sets; \(L\) is the total binary input length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L,k\).
An integral committee is \(W\subseteq C\) with \(|W|=k\); a fractional committee is \(x\in[0,1]^C\) with \(\sum_{c\in C}x_c=k\). Define \(u_A(W)=|A\cap W|\) and \(u_A(x)=\sum_{c\in A}x_c\).
Let \(H_t\) denote the harmonic numbers, with \(H_0=0\), and set \(\operatorname{PAV}_{\mu}(W)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal S}\mu_AH_{u_A(W)}\). Define \(h(z)=\int_0^z(1-e^{-y})/y\,dy\) for \(z\ge0\), interpreting the integrand at zero as one; let \(\alpha^\star\) be the unique positive solution of \(h(\alpha^\star)=1\), and let \(\gamma^\star=h(1)\).
A qualifying committee must simultaneously satisfy:

  • Mass EJR+: for every \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and \(c\in C\setminus W\), \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal S:\,c\in A,\,u_A(W)<\ell}\mu_A<\ell/k\).
  • \(\alpha^\star\)-fractional Pareto optimality: no fractional committee \(x\) has \(u_A(x)\ge\alpha^\star u_A(W)\) for every \(A\in\mathcal S\), with at least one inequality strict.
  • Weak Pareto optimality: no integral committee \(W'\) has \(u_A(W')>u_A(W)\) for every \(A\in\mathcal S\).
  • \(\operatorname{PAV}_{\mu}(W)\ge\gamma^\star\max_{W'\subseteq C:\,|W'|=k}\operatorname{PAV}_{\mu}(W')\).

Is finding a qualifying committee solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k\), or is it NP-hard?

For explicitly listed, equally weighted voters, a polynomial-time algorithm returns an integral committee satisfying all four guarantees ([Peters, Vaish, and Yadav](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07554v1)).
No result settling this exact search problem with binary rational masses was located.

  • Restricted the committee size to an integer between 2 and m−1, as required by the discrete instance definition.
  • Required a nonempty support consisting of nonempty approval sets, carrying over the assumption that every voter approves at least one candidate.
  • Defined the previously unspecified constants alpha-star and gamma-star by their exact integral formulas.
  • Defined L as the total binary input length, including approval sets, masses, and committee size.

Result

Finding a qualifying committee is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k\), including for binary rational masses.

The key is a rounding-slack bound that depends on the normalized total mass, rather than on the smallest positive mass. The proof below gives an explicit algorithm and handles numerical precision using rational arithmetic.

1. Objectives and analytic facts

Write \(P(W)=\operatorname{PAV}_{\mu}(W)\), and define the feasible fractional polytope
\[ \mathcal P=\left\{x\in[0,1]^C:\sum_{c\in C}x_c=k\right\}. \]
Define
\[ \Psi(x)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal S}\mu_Ah(u_A(x)), \qquad \Psi^\star=\max_{x\in\mathcal P}\Psi(x). \]

Extend \(P\) to arbitrary subsets using the same harmonic formula. Its multilinear extension is
\[ F(x)=\mathbb E[P(R(x))], \]
where \(R(x)\) includes each candidate independently with probability \(x_c\).

We use the following two scalar inequalities, proved in Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3 of [Peters, Vaish, and Yadav](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07554v1#S2): for every nonnegative integer \(t\),
\[ h(\alpha^\star t)\ge H_t, \qquad h(t)\ge\gamma^\star H_t. \tag{1} \]
Their hypotheses concern only \(t\), so they apply unchanged to utilities of approval types with arbitrary positive masses.

Also,
\[ h'(z)=\int_0^1e^{-sz}\,ds, \qquad h''(z)=-\int_0^1s e^{-sz}\,ds. \tag{2} \]
Consequently, \(h\) is strictly increasing and concave, \(h(z)\le z\), and \(-1/2\le h''(z)\le0\).

2. A rounding-slack bound independent of individual masses

Lemma. For every \(x\in\mathcal P\),
\[ F(x)-\Psi(x)\ge\frac{\Psi(x)^2}{64mk^2}. \tag{3} \]

Proof. Fix an approval type \(A\), and put
\[ u=\sum_{c\in A}x_c, \qquad Q=\sum_{c\in A}x_c^2. \]
Both satisfy \(Q\le u\le k\).

The geometric-series formula for harmonic numbers gives
\[ H_j=\int_0^1\frac{1-(1-s)^j}{s}\,ds. \]
Independence in \(R(x)\), together with substitution in the definition of \(h\), therefore gives
\[ \mathbb E[H_{|A\cap R(x)|}]-h(u) = \int_0^1 \frac{e^{-su}-\prod_{c\in A}(1-sx_c)}{s}\,ds. \tag{4} \]
The integrand is nonnegative because \(1-z\le e^{-z}\).

For \(0\le s\le1/(2k)\), the inequality
\(\log(1-z)\le-z-z^2/2\) gives
\[ \prod_{c\in A}(1-sx_c) \le e^{-su-s^2Q/2}. \]
On this interval, \(su\le1/2\) and \(s^2Q/2\le1\). Using
\(e^{-su}\ge1/2\) and \(1-e^{-a}\ge a/2\) for \(0\le a\le1\), we obtain
\[ e^{-su}-\prod_{c\in A}(1-sx_c) \ge\frac{s^2Q}{8}. \]
Restricting (4) to that interval yields
\[ \mathbb E[H_{|A\cap R(x)|}]-h(u) \ge\frac{Q}{64k^2}. \tag{5} \]

Since \(|A|\le m\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(Q\ge u^2/m\). The normalization \(\sum_A\mu_A=1\) then implies
\[ \sum_A\mu_AQ_A \ge \frac1m\sum_A\mu_Au_A(x)^2 \ge \frac1m\left(\sum_A\mu_Au_A(x)\right)^2 \ge \frac{\Psi(x)^2}{m}. \]
The last inequality uses \(h(z)\le z\). Multiplying (5) by the masses and summing proves (3). \(\square\)

This bound contains no dependence on \(\min_A\mu_A\).

3. The algorithm and its initialization guarantee

Set
\[ \eta=\frac1{4096m^3}, \qquad \beta=\frac1{2k^3(k+1)}. \]

The algorithm performs three steps:

  • Compute a rational \(\widehat x\in\mathcal P\) satisfying

\(\Psi(\widehat x)\ge\Psi^\star-\eta\).

  • Apply deterministic pipage rounding with respect to \(F\), obtaining \(W_0\).
  • Starting from \(W_0\), perform any swap increasing \(P\) by at least \(\beta\), until no such swap exists. Return \(W\).

Section 7 supplies the rational implementation of the first two steps.

Because every approval set is nonempty, the uniform fractional committee \(x_c=k/m\) gives every type utility at least \(k/m\). For \(0\le z\le1\), (2) and \(e^{-sz}\ge1-sz\) imply \(h'(z)\ge1/2\), and hence \(h(z)\ge z/2\). Therefore,
\[ \Psi^\star\ge h(k/m)\ge\frac{k}{2m}. \]
Since \(\eta\le k/(4m)\), the approximate optimum satisfies
\[ \Psi(\widehat x)\ge\frac{k}{4m}. \]
Applying (3),
\[ F(\widehat x)-\Psi(\widehat x) \ge \frac1{1024m^3} =4\eta. \]
Pipage rounding preserves or increases \(F\), so
\[ P(W_0)\ge F(\widehat x) \ge\Psi^\star+3\eta. \tag{6} \]
Every subsequent swap increases \(P\). Thus the returned committee satisfies
\[ P(W)\ge\Psi^\star. \tag{7} \]

4. Fractional Pareto optimality and PAV approximation

Suppose a fractional committee \(y\) satisfies
\(u_A(y)\ge\alpha^\star u_A(W)\) for every type, with at least one inequality strict.

Strict monotonicity of \(h\), inequality (1), and positivity of every mass give
\[ \Psi(y) = \sum_A\mu_Ah(u_A(y)) > \sum_A\mu_AH_{u_A(W)} = P(W). \]
This contradicts \(\Psi(y)\le\Psi^\star\le P(W)\). Therefore \(W\) is \(\alpha^\star\)-fractionally Pareto optimal.

For an optimal integral PAV committee \(O\), its incidence vector belongs to \(\mathcal P\). The other inequality in (1) gives
\[ \Psi^\star \ge\Psi(\mathbf1_O) = \sum_A\mu_Ah(u_A(O)) \ge\gamma^\star P(O). \]
Together with (7), this proves the required PAV approximation.

5. Mass EJR+

At termination, every swap has gain strictly less than \(\beta\).

Suppose mass EJR+ fails. There are an unelected candidate \(c\), an integer \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), and a collection
\[ \mathcal B=\{A\in\mathcal S:c\in A,\ u_A(W)<\ell\} \]
whose total mass \(b\) satisfies \(b\ge\ell/k\).

Consider all \(k\) swaps replacing one member of \(W\) by \(c\).

For a type \(A\in\mathcal B\), put \(r=u_A(W)\le\ell-1\). Its unweighted total contribution over these swaps is
\[ \frac{k-r}{r+1}\ge\frac{k-\ell+1}{\ell}. \]
Indeed, removing an approved winner and adding \(c\) leaves its utility unchanged; removing an unapproved winner increases its utility by one.

Every type outside \(\mathcal B\) contributes at least \(-1\) in total: a type approving \(c\) contributes nonnegatively, while a type not approving \(c\) loses a total of exactly one if its current utility is positive, and zero otherwise.

Thus the sum of the weighted swap gains is at least
\[ b\frac{k-\ell+1}{\ell}-(1-b) = b\frac{k+1}{\ell}-1 \ge\frac1k. \]
Some swap consequently has gain at least \(1/k^2>\beta\), contradicting termination. This also handles equality at the mass threshold, proving the required strict EJR+ inequalities.

6. Weak Pareto optimality

Suppose an integral committee \(W'\) strictly improves every type. Define
\[ X=W'\setminus W, \qquad Y=W\setminus W', \qquad r=|X|=|Y|. \]
Here \(1\le r\le k\). Consider the \(r^2\) swaps removing a member of \(Y\) and adding a member of \(X\).

Fix a type \(A\), and put
\[ u=|A\cap W|, \qquad a=|A\cap X|, \qquad b=|A\cap Y|. \]
Strict improvement and integrality imply \(a\ge b+1\).

If \(u=0\), then \(b=0\), and this type's total unweighted contribution is \(ra\ge1\).

If \(u\ge1\), its contribution is
\[ \Gamma_A = \frac{a(r-b)}{u+1} - \frac{b(r-a)}u. \]
This expression increases with \(a\). Substituting its smallest permitted value \(a=b+1\) gives
\[ \Gamma_A \ge \frac{r(u-b)+b(b+1)}{u(u+1)}. \]
The numerator is a positive integer: if \(u>b\), its first term is positive; otherwise \(u=b\ge1\), and its second term is positive. Since \(u\le k\),
\[ \Gamma_A\ge\frac1{k(k+1)}. \]
The same bound holds when \(u=0\).

After multiplying by masses and summing, the total gain over the \(r^2\) swaps is at least \(1/[k(k+1)]\). Hence some swap has gain at least
\[ \frac1{r^2k(k+1)} \ge \frac1{k^3(k+1)} = 2\beta. \]
This contradicts termination. Therefore \(W\) is weakly Pareto optimal.

7. Rational computation and running time

Approximate concave optimization

Here is an elementary implementation that avoids assuming exact real arithmetic.

From (2), for every vector \(v\),
\[ -v^{\mathsf T}\nabla^2\Psi(x)v \le \frac12\sum_A\mu_A \left(\sum_{c\in A}v_c\right)^2 \le \frac m2\|v\|_2^2. \]
Also, any \(x,y\in\mathcal P\) satisfy
\(\|x-y\|_2^2\le\|x-y\|_1\le2k\). Consequently, for \(0\le\lambda\le1\),
\[ \Psi(x+\lambda(y-x)) \ge \Psi(x)+\lambda\nabla\Psi(x)\cdot(y-x) -\frac{mk}{2}\lambda^2. \tag{8} \]

Run conditional-gradient iterations. At iteration \(t\), approximate each gradient coordinate to error at most
\[ \delta=\frac{\eta}{8k}. \]
Let \(v_t\) be the incidence vector of the \(k\) candidates with largest approximate gradient coordinates, and set
\[ x_{t+1} = (1-\lambda_t)x_t+\lambda_tv_t, \qquad \lambda_t=\frac2{t+2}. \]

A linear objective on \(\mathcal P\) is maximized by selecting its \(k\) largest coordinates. Because any two points of \(\mathcal P\) have distance at most \(2k\) in the \(1\)-norm, the approximate gradient introduces a linear-optimization error of at most \(2k\delta=\eta/4\).

Let \(g_t=\Psi^\star-\Psi(x_t)\). Concavity and (8) imply
\[ g_{t+1} \le (1-\lambda_t)g_t +\lambda_t\frac\eta4 +\frac{mk}{2}\lambda_t^2. \]
The first iteration gives \(g_1\le\eta/4+mk/2\). Induction then yields
\[ g_t\le\frac\eta4+\frac{2mk}{t+2} \qquad(t\ge1). \]
Thus
\[ T=\left\lceil\frac{4mk}{\eta}\right\rceil =O(m^4k) \]
iterations suffice.

The gradient evaluations also use rational arithmetic. From (2),
\[ h'(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{(-z)^j}{(j+1)!} +\text{error}, \qquad |\text{error}|\le\frac{k^{N+1}}{(N+2)!} \quad(0\le z\le k). \]
Choosing \(N=O(k+\log(1/\delta))\) makes the error at most \(\delta\). Computing every type's derivative to that accuracy also approximates every weighted gradient coordinate to error at most \(\delta\), because the masses sum to one.

Moreover, for \(t\ge1\),
\[ x_t=\frac2{t(t+1)} \sum_{j=0}^{t-1}(j+1)v_j. \]
Thus the fractional coordinates have polynomial-size rational encodings.

Exact pipage rounding

For two fractional coordinates \(c,d\), move along \(x+s(\mathbf1_c-\mathbf1_d)\) until an endpoint makes a coordinate integral.

The function \(F\) is convex along this line. To verify this, a type approving both candidates has mixed second derivative equal to an expectation of
\[ H_{j+2}-2H_{j+1}+H_j = -\frac1{(j+1)(j+2)}. \]
Types not approving both contribute zero. Hence the mixed derivative is nonpositive, and the second derivative along the exchange direction is nonnegative.

Choose the endpoint with larger \(F\). Convexity guarantees that this endpoint does not decrease \(F\). At most \(m\) steps produce an integral committee.

For each type, its distribution of approved candidates in \(R(x)\) is computed by multiplying the factors \((1-x_c)+x_cz\). This takes \(O(m^2)\) rational operations, so the complete rounding phase takes \(O(\tau m^3)\) rational operations. Endpoint updates preserve the common coordinate denominator.

Local search and total complexity

Every accepted swap increases \(P\) by at least \(\beta\), while \(P(W)\le H_k\). Therefore there are at most
\[ \frac{H_k}{\beta} = 2k^3(k+1)H_k = O(k^4(1+\log k)) \]
accepted swaps. One exhaustive swap scan costs \(O(\tau mk)\) rational operations.

The resulting arithmetic-operation bound is
\[ O\!\left( m^4k(\tau m+m\log m) +\tau m^3 +\tau m k^5(1+\log k) \right). \tag{9} \]

For completeness, rational bit lengths remain polynomial. A common denominator for the input masses has bit length at most \(L\). Combining this with the coordinate denominators, Taylor-polynomial denominators, and harmonic-number denominators bounds all rational operands by
\[ B=O(L+m\log(mk)) \]
bits. Using elementary integer arithmetic, a conservative bit-running-time bound is (9) multiplied by \(O(B^3)\).

Neither \(\alpha^\star\) nor \(\gamma^\star\) needs to be computed or compared numerically: their exact guarantees follow from (1), (6), and (7).

Conclusion

The algorithm returns an integral committee satisfying all four requirements in polynomial bit time. Binary rational masses require neither voter expansion nor a running-time dependence on the reciprocal of the smallest mass.
