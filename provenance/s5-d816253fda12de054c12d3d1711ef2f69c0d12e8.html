The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), candidate centers \(M\), a metric \(d\) on \(T\cup M\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), \(k\), and \(\rho\ge1\), with the promise that a \(\rho\)-proportional \(k\)-center set exists, compute \(X\subseteq M\), \(|X|=k\), with \(C_\mu(X)\le8\,\mathrm{OPT}_\rho\) and proportionality \(1+27(1+\rho)\), where \(C_\mu(X)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tD_t(X)\), \(D_t(X)=\min_{x\in X}d(t,x)\), and \(\mathrm{OPT}_\rho\) is the minimum \(C_\mu\) over \(\rho\)-proportional sets.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(T\subseteq M\), where \(M\) is a finite set of \(m\) candidate centers. Input includes a finite rational metric \(d\) on \(M\), an integer \(1\le k\le m\), and a binary rational \(\rho\ge1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For \(X\subseteq M\) with \(|X|=k\), define \(D_t(X)=\min_{x\in X}d(t,x)\) and \(C_\mu(X)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tD_t(X)\).
For \(\alpha\ge1\), call \(X\) \(\alpha\)-proportional if \(\sum_{t:\,D_t(X)>\alpha d(t,y)}\mu_t<1/k\) for every \(y\in M\). Thus blocking requires strict improvement for mass at least \(1/k\), including equality at the quota; masses may be split within types.
Promise that a \(\rho\)-proportional set exists, and let \(\mathrm{OPT}_\rho=\min\{C_\mu(Z):Z\subseteq M,\ |Z|=k,\ Z\text{ is }\rho\text{-proportional}\}\).

Is there an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\) that computes \(X\subseteq M\), \(|X|=k\), satisfying both \(C_\mu(X)\le8\mathrm{OPT}_\rho\) and \(\bigl(1+27(1+\rho)\bigr)\)-proportionality, or is achieving these simultaneous approximation guarantees NP-hard?

For finite lists of agents whose locations are candidate centers, a polynomial-time algorithm achieves these same cost and proportionality guarantees, using blocking quota \(\lceil n/k\rceil\). [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03674)
No result settling the stated problem with binary rational masses and running time polynomial in support size and input bit length was identified in the literature search.

  • Defined proportionality by strictly improving mass at least 1/k, replacing the discrete ceiling quota by its normalized mass analogue; equality at the quota blocks.

Result

There is a deterministic algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\) achieving both stated guarantees.

We prove this by a rational linear program and an explicit rounding procedure. The proof follows the ball-constraint approach of [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03674), but uses auxiliary assignment rows at every candidate, including candidates with zero population mass. No expansion into individual agents is required.

1. Mass radii encode approximate proportionality

For \(y\in M\), define
\[ R_y=\min\left\{r\ge 0:\sum_{\substack{t\in T\\d(t,y)\le r}}\mu_t\ge \frac1k\right\}. \]
The minimum exists because total mass is \(1\). It is one of the distances from \(y\) to the support. By its definition,
\[ \sum_{\substack{t\in T\\d(t,y)<R_y}}\mu_t<\frac1k. \]

We will use two implications.

Necessity. If \(Z\) is \(\rho\)-proportional, then
\[ D_y(Z)\le (1+\rho)R_y\qquad(y\in M). \]
Indeed, the closed ball of radius \(R_y\) contains mass at least \(1/k\). Proportionality therefore implies that some type \(t\) in this ball satisfies \(D_t(Z)\le \rho d(t,y)\). Choosing a nearest center \(z\in Z\) for this type gives
\[ d(y,z)\le d(y,t)+d(t,z) \le (1+\rho)d(t,y)\le (1+\rho)R_y. \]

Sufficiency. Suppose \(X\) satisfies \(D_y(X)\le \beta R_y\) for every \(y\in M\), where \(\beta>0\). Then \(X\) is \((1+\beta)\)-proportional.

To prove this, fix \(y\). If a type \(t\) satisfies \(D_t(X)>(1+\beta)d(t,y)\), the triangle inequality gives
\[ (1+\beta)d(t,y)<D_t(X) \le d(t,y)+D_y(X) \le d(t,y)+\beta R_y. \]
Hence \(d(t,y)<R_y\). All strictly improving types lie in the strict interior of the radius-\(R_y\) ball, whose mass is less than \(1/k\).

Both implications handle equality at the quota and the case \(R_y=0\).

2. A polynomial-size rational relaxation

Set \(\gamma=1+\rho\). Extend the weights to all candidates by
\[ w_i=\begin{cases}\mu_i,&i\in T,\\0,&i\notin T.\end{cases} \]

Use opening variables \(u_j\) and assignment variables \(z_{ij}\), with both indices ranging over \(M\). Solve the rational linear program
\[ \min \sum_{i\in M}w_i\sum_{j\in M}d(i,j)z_{ij} \]
subject to
\[ \sum_{j\in M}z_{ij}=1,\qquad z_{ij}\le u_j,\qquad 0\le z_{ij},u_j\le 1, \]
\[ \sum_{j\in M}u_j\le k, \qquad \sum_{\substack{j\in M\\d(y,j)\le\gamma R_y}}u_j\ge 1 \quad(y\in M). \]

The rows for \(i\notin T\) carry zero objective weight; they add no population mass.

Every \(\rho\)-proportional set of size \(k\) yields a feasible integral solution, by the necessity implication above. Thus the promise ensures feasibility, and the optimal LP value \(F\) satisfies
\[ F\le \mathrm{OPT}_\rho. \]

Fix an optimal opening vector \(u\). Independently replace each assignment row by a minimum-cost assignment under the capacities \(u_j\), obtained by taking capacity in increasing order of distance. Define
\[ c_i=\sum_{j\in M}d(i,j)z_{ij}. \]
This replacement preserves feasibility and cannot increase the objective. By optimality,
\[ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t c_t=F. \]

The individual optimality of every row, including zero-weight rows, will be useful below.

3. Representatives and opening consolidation

Process all candidates in nondecreasing order of \(c_i\), breaking ties consistently. Whenever an unassigned candidate \(s\) is encountered, select it as a representative and assign to it every currently unassigned \(i\) satisfying
\[ d(i,s)\le 4c_i. \]
Let \(S\) be the selected representatives and let \(\phi(i)\in S\) be the representative assigned to \(i\).

By construction,
\[ c_{\phi(i)}\le c_i,\qquad d(i,\phi(i))\le 4c_i. \]
Distinct representatives satisfy
\[ d(s,v)>4\max\{c_s,c_v\}. \]
Indeed, the later representative would otherwise have been assigned to the earlier one.

Give representative \(s\) the consolidated demand
\[ a_s=\sum_{\substack{t\in T\\\phi(t)=s}}\mu_t. \]
Some \(a_s\) may be zero. Nevertheless,
\[ \sum_{s\in S}a_sc_s\le F, \qquad \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t d(t,\phi(t))\le 4F. \]

Now map every candidate \(j\) to a nearest representative \(v(j)\), using consistent tie-breaking. Write \(V_s=\{j:v(j)=s\}\), and define
\[ h_s=\min\left\{1,\sum_{j\in V_s}u_j\right\}. \]
Then
\[ \sum_{s\in S}h_s\le k. \]

We next prove that \(h_s\ge 1/2\). If \(c_s>0\), at least half of assignment row \(s\) lies within distance \(2c_s\): otherwise its average distance would exceed \(c_s\). Every candidate \(j\) in that ball is uniquely closest to \(s\) among the representatives, since for \(v\ne s\),
\[ d(j,v)\ge d(s,v)-d(s,j) >4c_s-d(s,j)\ge d(s,j). \]
Consequently,
\[ \sum_{j\in V_s}u_j\ge \sum_{\substack{j\in M\\d(s,j)\le2c_s}}z_{sj}\ge\frac12. \]
If \(c_s=0\), the metric property forces \(z_{ss}=u_s=1\), so \(h_s=1\).

Thus every \(h_s\) lies in \([1/2,1]\), and \(|S|\le2k\).

4. The cost bound for consolidated demand

If \(|S|\le k\), open all representatives. The consolidated demand then incurs zero cost, and the original cost is at most \(4F\).

For the remainder, assume \(q=|S|>k\), so \(q\ge2\). For each \(s\in S\), let \(n(s)\) be a nearest other representative and put
\[ \delta_s=d(s,n(s)). \]

If \(j\notin V_s\), its chosen representative \(v(j)\ne s\) satisfies \(d(j,v(j))\le d(j,s)\). Therefore
\[ \delta_s\le d(s,v(j)) \le d(s,j)+d(j,v(j))\le2d(s,j). \]

When \(h_s<1\), the assignment mass of row \(s\) inside \(V_s\) is at most \(\sum_{j\in V_s}u_j=h_s\). At least \(1-h_s\) lies outside, at distance at least \(\delta_s/2\). When \(h_s=1\), the following inequality is immediate. In both cases,
\[ (1-h_s)\delta_s\le2c_s. \]
Define
\[ P(h)=\sum_{s\in S}a_s(1-h_s)\delta_s. \]
It follows that
\[ P(h)\le2\sum_{s\in S}a_sc_s\le2F. \]

We now replace \(h\) by a vector \(\bar h\) with entries in \(\{1/2,1\}\), preserving every entry already equal to \(1\).

Let \(p=|\{s:h_s=1\}|\), let \(\ell=q-p\), and set
\[ b=2k-q-p. \]
The inequalities
\[ p+\frac{\ell}{2}\le\sum_s h_s\le k,\qquad k<q \]
give \(0\le b<\ell\).

Among the \(\ell\) representatives with \(h_s<1\), set \(\bar h_s=1\) for the \(b\) largest values of \(a_s\delta_s\), and set the remaining entries to \(1/2\). Keep all original unit entries equal to \(1\). Then
\[ \sum_s\bar h_s=k. \]

Also \(P(\bar h)\le P(h)\). To verify this explicitly, write \(r_s=2h_s-1\) on the nonunit entries. These numbers lie in \([0,1]\) and satisfy \(\sum_s r_s\le b\). For nonnegative coefficients, choosing the \(b\) largest coefficients maximizes their scalar product with a vector in this box having sum at most \(b\). Since minimizing \(P\) is equivalent to maximizing that scalar product, the claimed inequality follows.

5. Integral rounding by a forest

We require a rounding with the following property:

Every representative is either opened or has its nearest other representative opened.

Choose \(n(s)\) by distance, breaking distance ties using one fixed ordering of \(S\). Consider the directed graph whose arcs are \(s\to n(s)\).

Every directed cycle has length two. Indeed, along a directed cycle the nearest-neighbor distances cannot increase, so they are all equal. If a cycle had length at least three, take its least vertex \(s\) in the fixed ordering and let its successor be \(v\). Vertex \(s\) is a nearest neighbor of \(v\), so tie-breaking cannot choose another cycle vertex after \(v\) in preference to \(s\). This contradicts the assumed cycle.

Each weak component of a finite graph with one outgoing arc per vertex has exactly one directed cycle: following arcs reaches a cycle, and the endpoints of every arc reach the same cycle. Thus each component here has one pair of opposite arcs. Suppressing duplicate undirected edges leaves a connected graph with one fewer edge than vertices, hence a tree. The resulting undirected graph is therefore a forest.

Open every representative with \(\bar h_s=1\). Let
\[ H=\{s:\bar h_s=1/2\}. \]
The forest induced by \(H\) is bipartite. In each component, open the smaller of its two color classes. This opens at most \(|H|/2\) additional representatives, so the resulting set \(X_0\) satisfies
\[ |X_0|\le|\{s:\bar h_s=1\}|+\frac{|H|}{2}=k. \]

Every edge inside \(H\) has an opened endpoint. Hence if \(s\in H\) is not opened, then \(n(s)\) is opened: it is either a unit representative or the other endpoint of an edge inside \(H\). Therefore
\[ \sum_{s\in S}a_sD_s(X_0) \le\sum_{s\in H}a_s\delta_s =2P(\bar h)\le4F. \]

Returning to the original types and using the triangle inequality,
\[ C_\mu(X_0) \le\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t d(t,\phi(t)) +\sum_{s\in S}a_sD_s(X_0) \le4F+4F=8F. \]

6. Preservation of every required ball

Fix \(y\in M\), and put \(r=\gamma R_y\). The LP supplies
\[ \sum_{\substack{j\in M\\d(y,j)\le r}}u_j\ge1. \]

For any \(j\) in this ball, assignment row \(j\) could obtain one full unit of assignment from facilities inside the same ball, all at distance at most \(2r\) from \(j\). Because that row was minimized,
\[ c_j\le2r. \]
Consequently,
\[ d(j,\phi(j))\le8r. \]
The nearest representative \(v(j)\) is no farther from \(j\), so
\[ d(y,v(j))\le d(y,j)+d(j,v(j))\le9r. \]

Thus all opening mass originally inside the radius-\(r\) ball is mapped to representatives inside the radius-\(9r\) ball. Capping each representative’s mass at \(1\) preserves the fact that total mass is at least \(1\): if any uncapped mass is at least \(1\), that representative alone suffices; otherwise no relevant mass is capped. Hence
\[ \sum_{\substack{s\in S\\d(y,s)\le9r}}h_s\ge1. \]

If \(|S|\le k\), all representatives are opened, giving \(D_y(X_0)\le9r\).

Otherwise, consider the representatives within distance \(9r\) of \(y\). If one has \(\bar h_s=1\), it is opened. If none does, there must be at least two: a unique representative would have \(h_s=1\), which the construction preserves.

Choose two distinct such representatives \(s,v\). Then
\[ \delta_s\le d(s,v)\le18r. \]
The integral rounding opens either \(s\) or \(n(s)\). Therefore
\[ D_y(X_0)\le d(y,s)+\delta_s\le27r. \]

We have proved, in every case,
\[ D_y(X_0)\le27\gamma R_y\qquad(y\in M). \]
Section 1 now implies that \(X_0\) is \(\bigl(1+27(1+\rho)\bigr)\)-proportional.

Finally, add arbitrary unused candidates until exactly \(k\) centers are open. Adding centers only decreases all nearest-center distances. The resulting \(X\) retains proportionality and satisfies
\[ C_\mu(X)\le8F\le8\mathrm{OPT}_\rho. \]

7. Bit complexity

Each radius is computed by sorting the support distances and adding rational masses, with exact comparison against \(1/k\). The LP has \(m^2+m\) variables and polynomially many constraints. Its rational coefficients have bit length polynomial in \(L\), so standard exact rational linear programming returns an optimal solution of polynomial encoding length in polynomial time.

Assignment minimization, representative selection, opening consolidation, coefficient sorting, forest coloring, and padding use polynomially many arithmetic operations and comparisons on rationals of polynomial bit length. In particular, zero-weight auxiliary rows require only \(m-\tau\) additional rows; they require no copies of agents or perturbation of masses.

Thus the entire algorithm runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\), proving the requested algorithmic alternative.
