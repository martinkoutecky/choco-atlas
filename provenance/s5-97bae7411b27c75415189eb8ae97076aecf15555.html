The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\), \(N\ge m\), and an empirical distribution \(\mu\) with \(\mu_u=n_u/N\) over finitely many normalized utility types \(u:M\to[0,1]\), let \(J^*\) sample a type according to \(\mu\), then choose its top candidate with probability \(1/2\) or uniformly among its \(\lfloor m^{1/3}\rfloor\) top candidates otherwise. Is \(J^*\) truthful when one voter’s report transfers mass \(1/N\) from their true type to a reported type, and does it guarantee expected per-capita welfare at least \(c m^{-2/3}\max_{c\in M}\sum_u\mu_u u(c)\) for some universal \(c>0\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with integers \(N\ge m\ge2\). A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Require \(\mu_u=n_u/N\), where \(n_u\) are positive integers summing to \(N\). Each type has binary-rational utilities \(u:M\to[0,1]\), with \(\min_j u(j)=0\) and \(\max_j u(j)=1\); ties are allowed. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L,\log N\).
Rank candidates by decreasing reported utility, breaking ties in favor of smaller candidate labels. The mechanism \(J^*(\mu)\) samples a type according to \(\mu\), then chooses its top candidate with probability \(1/2\), and otherwise chooses uniformly from its top \(\lfloor m^{1/3}\rfloor\) candidates. Define \(W_\mu(j)=\sum_u\mu_u u(j)\).
A voter of true type \(u\) may report any normalized binary-rational type \(v\), including one outside the support, producing \(\mu'=\mu-N^{-1}\delta_u+N^{-1}\delta_v\). Truthfulness means \(\mathbb E[u(J^*(\mu))]\ge\mathbb E[u(J^*(\mu'))]\) for every such deviation.

Is \(J^*\) truthful under these deviations, and does there exist a universal \(\gamma>0\) such that \(\mathbb E[W_\mu(J^*(\mu))]\ge\gamma m^{-2/3}\max_{j\in M}W_\mu(j)\) for every admissible society?

Lee establishes truthfulness and an \(\Omega(m^{-2/3})\) welfare ratio for this finite-voter mechanism when \(N\ge m\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.00538).
The empirical-distribution formulation above describes exactly the same societies and deviations; consequently, the stated target is already covered, rather than an open continuous analogue.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized utilities attain both 0 and 1, as required by the discrete domain; this implies m is at least 2.
  • Utility ties are allowed and favor the smaller candidate label.
  • Utilities are binary rationals so that the requested input bit length is defined.
  • Reports may introduce types outside the initial support and must satisfy the same utility normalization.
  • Truthfulness compares expected utility evaluated using the deviating voter's true type.
  • The welfare guarantee is uniform over admissible societies; gamma denotes its constant to avoid confusing it with a candidate.

Conclusion

\(J^*\) is truthful under the stated deviations. However, the proposed welfare guarantee is false: there are admissible societies with \(N=m\) whose welfare ratio is at most \(4m^{-5/6}\).

The cited paper states the affirmative welfare claim in Theorem 4, but the explicit construction below contradicts that claim for the mechanism defined in the question. [Lee, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.00538).

1. Truthfulness

Write \(k=\lfloor m^{1/3}\rfloor\). For a reported type \(v\), let \(f(v)\) be its first-ranked candidate and \(T(v)\) its set of top \(k\) candidates, using the prescribed tie-breaking. Conditional on selecting a voter reporting \(v\), the mechanism uses the lottery

\( q_v=\frac12\delta_{f(v)}+\frac1{2k}\sum_{j\in T(v)}\delta_j. \)

Fix a true type \(u\). Since \(f(u)\) maximizes \(u\),

\( u(f(u))\ge u(f(v)). \)

Moreover, \(T(u)\) maximizes the sum of true utilities among all sets of \(k\) candidates:

\( \sum_{j\in T(u)}u(j)\ge\sum_{j\in T(v)}u(j). \)

Indeed, if a set omits a member of \(T(u)\), exchanging an included candidate outside \(T(u)\) for an omitted candidate inside it cannot decrease the sum. Repeating these exchanges proves the inequality; ties cause no difficulty.

Consequently,

\( \mathbb E_{q_u}[u]\ge\mathbb E_{q_v}[u]. \)

The society’s outcome lottery is \(\sum_w\mu_wq_w\). Replacing one voter’s report changes this lottery by \((q_v-q_u)/N\). Therefore,

\( \mathbb E[u(J^*(\mu))]-\mathbb E[u(J^*(\mu'))] =\frac1N\bigl(\mathbb E_{q_u}[u]-\mathbb E_{q_v}[u]\bigr)\ge0. \)

This proves truthfulness for every permitted report, including reports outside the support.

2. An admissible counterexample family

Choose any integer \(h\ge1\), and put

\( r=2^h,\qquad m=N=r^6,\qquad k=r^2,\qquad c=r^5,\qquad d=m/2. \)

Thus \(k=\lfloor m^{1/3}\rfloor\). Partition the candidate labels into

  • \(D=\{1,\ldots,k-1\}\);
  • \(B=\{k,\ldots,k+d-1\}\);
  • \(C=\{k+d,\ldots,m-1\}\);
  • the distinguished candidate \(a=m\).

Their sizes are \(k-1\), \(d\), \(d-k\), and \(1\), respectively.

Partition \(B\) into \(d/k=r^4/2\) disjoint blocks of size \(k\).

Block types

For every block \(G\), include \(2r\) voters of the type

\( u_G(j)= \begin{cases} 1,&j\in G\cup\{a\},\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \)

The total number of these voters is

\( (2r)\frac{d}{k}=r^5=c. \)

Every candidate of \(G\) has a smaller label than \(a\). Hence these voters’ top \(k\) candidates are exactly \(G\), and both branches of the mechanism choose a candidate in \(G\).

Singleton types

Let \(b=N-c\). For each \(j\in C\), include \(n_j\in\{1,2\}\) voters with utility

\( s_j(\ell)= \begin{cases} 1,&\ell=j,\\ 0,&\ell\ne j, \end{cases} \)

choosing the multiplicities so that \(\sum_{j\in C}n_j=b\).

Such multiplicities exist because

\( b-|C|=r^6/2-r^5+r^2 =r^5(r/2-1)+r^2\ge0 \)

and

\( 2|C|-b=r^5-2r^2=r^2(r^3-2)\ge0. \)

Explicitly, give multiplicity \(2\) to \(b-|C|\) candidates of \(C\), and multiplicity \(1\) to the others.

For a singleton type, the favorite is \(j\), while its top \(k\) set is \(\{j\}\cup D\): after its unique utility-\(1\) candidate, tie-breaking selects the \(k-1\) smallest labels.

Input requirements

There are exactly \(c+b=N\) voters. Every type has minimum utility \(0\), maximum utility \(1\), and binary-rational utilities.

Each support mass is either \(2r/N\), \(1/N\), or \(2/N\). Because \(N\) is a power of two, all masses are dyadic rationals. The support size is

\( \tau=\frac{r^4}{2}+\frac{r^6}{2}-r^2. \)

Thus every member of this family satisfies all the stated requirements.

3. Exact welfare analysis

Use total welfare \(H(j)=NW_\mu(j)\). The construction gives

\( H(j)= \begin{cases} 0,&j\in D,\\ 2r,&j\in B,\\ n_j,&j\in C,\\ c,&j=a. \end{cases} \)

Since \(r\ge2\), we have \(c=r^5>2r\) and \(c>2\). Therefore \(a\) is the unique welfare maximizer, with total welfare \(c\). It is never selected.

When a block voter is sampled, the winning candidate has total welfare \(2r\). These voters contribute

\( \frac{c}{N}(2r)=2 \)

to expected total welfare.

When a singleton voter of type \(s_j\) is sampled, the winner is \(j\) with probability \(\frac12+\frac1{2k}\); otherwise it is a candidate of \(D\), whose welfare is zero. Hence

\( \mathbb E[H(J^*(\mu))] =2+\frac{1+1/k}{2N}\sum_{j\in C}n_j^2. \)

Because \(n_j\in\{1,2\}\), we have \(n_j^2\le2n_j\). Consequently,

\( \mathbb E[H(J^*(\mu))] \le2+(1+1/k)\frac{b}{N}\le4. \)

Normalization cancels in the welfare ratio, giving

\( \frac{\mathbb E[W_\mu(J^*(\mu))]}{\max_jW_\mu(j)} =\frac{\mathbb E[H(J^*(\mu))]}{c} \le\frac4{r^5}=4m^{-5/6}. \)

In particular,

\( m^{2/3} \frac{\mathbb E[W_\mu(J^*(\mu))]}{\max_jW_\mu(j)} \le\frac4r\longrightarrow0. \)

For any proposed \(\gamma>0\), choosing a power of two \(r>4/\gamma\) therefore violates the requested guarantee.

4. Representation and running time

The empirical-distribution formulation does coincide with the finite-voter formulation: replicate each support type \(u\) exactly \(n_u\) times. Uniform voter sampling gives type probability \(n_u/N\), and changing one report gives precisely the specified deviation. This correspondence transfers the counterexample as well.

The exact outcome lottery and expected welfare can be computed directly from the support, without expanding it into \(N\) voters. Define

\( F_j=\sum_u n_u\,\mathbf1_{\{f(u)=j\}}, \qquad T_j=\sum_u n_u\,\mathbf1_{\{j\in T(u)\}}. \)

Then

\( \Pr[J^*(\mu)=j]=\frac{kF_j+T_j}{2Nk}, \qquad W_\mu(j)=\frac1N\sum_u n_u u(j). \)

Sorting each type requires \(O(\tau m\log m)\) rational comparisons. Using schoolbook integer arithmetic and a common denominator for the utility entries gives the conservative deterministic bit-complexity bound

\( O\!\left((\tau m\log m+m)(L+\log N+\log m)^2\right) \)

for computing the lottery, expected welfare, and optimal welfare exactly.
