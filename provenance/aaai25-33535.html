The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), budget densities \(b_t>0\), valuation vectors \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^m\), unit supply per unit population mass, initial prices \(p^0\), stepsize \(\eta\) satisfying the paper’s explicit lower-bound conditions, and accuracy \(\epsilon>0\), define \(D_t(p)=\arg\max\{v_t\cdot x:p\cdot x\le b_t,\ x\ge0\}\). Iteratively choose \(x_t^k\in D_t(p^k)\), set \(z_j^k=\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}^k-1\), and update \(p_j^{k+1}=p_j^k+\eta z_j^k\); compute \(p^K\) and type-level demands yielding \(\lVert p^K-p^\star\rVert_2\le\epsilon\), or an \(\epsilon\)-approximate market equilibrium.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq1\) divisible goods, each with supply \(1\). Budgets \(b_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\) are per unit mass; valuations \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^m\) satisfy \(\lVert v_t\rVert_1=1\), and every good has positive valuation for some supported type.
Let \(B=\sum_t\mu_tb_t\), \(B_{\min}=\min_t\mu_tb_t\), \(\tilde p_j=\frac{B_{\min}}{4m}\max_t\frac{v_{tj}}{\lVert v_t\rVert_\infty}\), and \(\gamma=\min_j\tilde p_j\).
For positive prices, define \(D_t(p)=\operatorname{argmax}\{v_t\cdot x:p\cdot x\leq b_t,\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^m\}\). The unique equilibrium price vector \(p^\star\) admits bundles \(x_t^\star\in D_t(p^\star)\) with \(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}^\star=1\) for every good; write \(\rho=\min_jp_j^\star\).
Inputs include rational \(p^0\geq\tilde p\) with \(\sum_jp_j^0=B\), accuracy \(\epsilon>0\), and a constant rational stepsize promised to satisfy \(0<\eta\leq\min\{\gamma/(4m),\,2\epsilon^2\rho/[9B^2(2B/\gamma+m)^2]\}\).
At each discrete iteration choose rational \(x_t^k\in D_t(p^k)\), allowing arbitrary splitting among tied optimal goods, and update \(p_j^{k+1}=p_j^k+\eta(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}^k-1)\), without projection.
The computational task is to find a finite \(K\), the resulting \(p^K\), and demands \(x_t^K\in D_t(p^K)\), with \(\lVert p^K-p^\star\rVert_2\leq\epsilon\); demand choices are made by the algorithm. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including \(p^0,\eta,\epsilon\).

Is this trajectory-constrained computational task solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite buyers, [Nan, Gao and Kroer](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12526v2) establish geometric last-iterate convergence to a stepsize-dependent neighborhood, for every demand selection; this is not a polynomial bit-complexity guarantee.
[Orlin](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/DagSemProc.10171.2) gives a strongly polynomial algorithm for unrestricted linear Fisher equilibrium computation. Neither verified result establishes polynomial bit complexity for the trajectory-constrained task above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored valuation normalization and the exclusion of all-zero valuation columns; normalized rows also exclude all-zero rows.
  • Interpreted budgets as densities and used each supported type's total budget, mass times budget density, in the minimum-budget initialization bound.
  • Restored the total-budget normalization of initial prices used by the accuracy corollary.
  • Added the accuracy-dependent stepsize promise from the accuracy corollary, replacing its squared-distance tolerance by epsilon squared because the graded question requests Euclidean distance at most epsilon.
  • Resolved nonunique demands by allowing the algorithm to choose rational optimal bundles, including splits among tied goods.
  • Selected the stated price-distance alternative and retained the requirement that the output prices arise from the supplied constant-stepsize dynamics; final optimal demands need not clear the market.
  • Specified binary rational numerical inputs and measured polynomial time in total binary input length, rather than treating inverse accuracy or inverse stepsize as unary parameters.

1. Aggregation and a computable lower bound on equilibrium prices

Define each type’s aggregate budget and aggregate allocation by
$$ w_t=\mu_tb_t,\qquad y_t=\mu_tx_t. $$
The substitution $y_t=\mu_tx_t$ is a bijection between the original demand problem and a finite buyer’s demand problem with valuation $v_t$ and budget $w_t$: the budget constraint becomes $p\cdot y_t\leq w_t$, and the objective is multiplied by the positive constant $\mu_t$.

Thus the society is exactly equivalent to a finite Fisher market with $\tau$ buyers, budgets $w_t$, and unit supplies. Its price update is
$$ p_j^{k+1}=p_j^k+\eta\left(\sum_ty_{tj}^k-1\right). $$

At any positive price vector, put
$$ r_t(p)=\max_j\frac{v_{tj}}{p_j}. $$
For every feasible bundle $y_t$,
$$ v_t\cdot y_t\leq r_t(p)\,p\cdot y_t\leq r_t(p)w_t. $$
Spending the entire budget on a maximizing good attains equality. Since $r_t(p)>0$, every optimal bundle exhausts its budget and purchases only maximizing goods.

At equilibrium, market clearing consequently gives
$$ \sum_jp_j^\star =\sum_tp^\star\cdot y_t^\star =\sum_tw_t =B. $$

Lemma. Every equilibrium coordinate satisfies
$$ p_j^\star\geq4\tilde p_j, \qquad\text{and hence}\qquad \rho\geq4\gamma. $$

Proof. Market clearing and nonnegativity imply $y_{tj}^\star\leq1$. Therefore
$$ r_t(p^\star)w_t =v_t\cdot y_t^\star \leq m\lVert v_t\rVert_\infty. $$
For every $j$ with $v_{tj}>0$, the definition of $r_t$ now yields
$$ p_j^\star \geq\frac{v_{tj}}{r_t(p^\star)} \geq\frac{w_t}{m}\frac{v_{tj}}{\lVert v_t\rVert_\infty} \geq\frac{B_{\min}}{m}\frac{v_{tj}}{\lVert v_t\rVert_\infty}. $$
For $v_{tj}=0$, the final inequality also holds. Maximizing over $t$ proves
$$ p_j^\star \geq\frac{B_{\min}}m \max_t\frac{v_{tj}}{\lVert v_t\rVert_\infty} =4\tilde p_j. $$
Taking the minimum over $j$ proves the second assertion. $\square$

This bound allows us to choose a stopping index without computing $p^\star$.

2. An explicit stopping index

We use the following specialization of Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 of [Nan, Gao and Kroer](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12526v2). For a finite linear Fisher market with positive budgets, normalized nonnegative valuations, no zero valuation columns, initial prices $p^0\geq\tilde p$ summing to $B$, and
$$ 0<\eta\leq\frac{\gamma}{4m}, $$
every demand selection satisfies
$$ p_j^k\geq\tilde p_j-2m\eta>0 $$
and
$$ \lVert p^k-p^\star\rVert_2^2 \leq \left(1-\frac{4\eta\rho}{9B^2}\right)^k \lVert p^0-p^\star\rVert_2^2 + \frac{9\eta B^2}{4\rho} \left(\frac{2B}{\gamma}+m\right)^2. $$
The constants follow from their quadratic-growth modulus and excess-demand bound, using
$$ \tilde p_j-2m\eta\geq\frac{\tilde p_j}{2}, \qquad \left(1+\frac{\eta}{\tilde p_j-2m\eta}\right)B \leq\frac{3B}{2}. $$

All hypotheses hold after the aggregation in Section 1: budgets $w_t$ are positive, valuations and supplies are unchanged, every column has a positive valuation, and the initial-price and stepsize conditions are input promises.

The second stepsize promise makes the additive term at most $\epsilon^2/2$. Moreover, because both $p^0$ and $p^\star$ are nonnegative and sum to $B$,
$$ \lVert p^0-p^\star\rVert_2\leq2B. $$
Consequently,
$$ \lVert p^k-p^\star\rVert_2^2 \leq 4B^2\left(1-\frac{4\eta\rho}{9B^2}\right)^k +\frac{\epsilon^2}{2}. $$

Define the input-computable rational
$$ \delta=\frac{16\eta\gamma}{9B^2}. $$
Section 1 gives $4\eta\rho/(9B^2)\geq\delta$. The geometric factors are nonnegative: indeed, $\rho\leq B$, $\gamma\leq B/(4m)$, and therefore
$$ 0<\frac{4\eta\rho}{9B^2} \leq\frac{4\eta}{9B} \leq\frac{1}{36m^2}<1. $$
We obtain
$$ \lVert p^k-p^\star\rVert_2^2 \leq4B^2(1-\delta)^k+\frac{\epsilon^2}{2}. $$

Let $M$ be the least nonnegative integer satisfying
$$ 2^M\geq\max\left\{1,\frac{8B^2}{\epsilon^2}\right\}, $$
and choose
$$ K=\left\lceil\frac{M}{\delta}\right\rceil. $$
If $M=0$, then $4B^2\leq\epsilon^2/2$, so the displayed distance bound already suffices at $K=0$. Otherwise,
$$ (1-\delta)^K \leq e^{-\delta K} \leq e^{-M} \leq2^{-M}. $$
Thus in either case,
$$ \lVert p^K-p^\star\rVert_2^2 \leq4B^2\,2^{-M}+\frac{\epsilon^2}{2} \leq\epsilon^2. $$

The index $K$ guarantees success for every demand selection and uses only input data.

3. A general doubly exponential algorithm

Fix this deterministic demand rule: each type spends its entire budget on the smallest-index good maximizing $v_{tj}/p_j^k$.

The rule produces rational optimal demands. In aggregate form, define
$$ s_j^k=\sum_{\{t:\,t\text{ chooses }j\}}w_t. $$
Then each coordinate obeys
$$ p_j^{k+1} =p_j^k+\eta\left(\frac{s_j^k}{p_j^k}-1\right). $$
Every $s_j^k$ is a subset sum of the input aggregate budgets.

The stopping index is at most exponential

For ordinary explicit binary rational encoding, every positive input rational lies between $2^{-L}$ and $2^L$. In particular,
$$ B\leq2^L,\qquad B_{\min}\geq2^{-2L},\qquad \eta\geq2^{-L},\qquad \epsilon\geq2^{-L}. $$
Every good has a positive valuation entry, and $\lVert v_t\rVert_\infty\leq1$. Hence
$$ \gamma\geq\frac{2^{-3L}}{4m}. $$
It follows that
$$ \delta\geq\frac{4}{9m}\,2^{-6L}, \qquad M\leq4L+3. $$
The explicit input has $m,\tau=O(L)$, so
$$ K\leq2^{O(L)}. $$
The rational parameters defining $K$ have $O(L)$ bits: sums can use a common denominator obtained by multiplying input denominators, whose total encoded length is at most $L$. Thus $K$ itself can be computed in polynomial time.

Exact arithmetic grows by at most a factor of two per step

Let $H_k$ be the maximum numerator or denominator bit length among the reduced price coordinates at iteration $k$.

All aggregate budgets have a common denominator $Q$ with $O(L)$ bits. Write
$$ s_j^k=\frac{S_j^k}{Q}, \qquad \eta=\frac EF, \qquad p_j^k=\frac AD. $$
Since $0\leq s_j^k\leq B\leq2^L$, both $S_j^k$ and $Q$ have $O(L)$ bits. The update is exactly
$$ p_j^{k+1} = \frac{FQA^2-EQAD+ES_j^kD^2}{FQAD}. $$
Each displayed numerator and denominator has at most $2H_k+cL$ bits for an absolute constant $c$. Reduction of a fraction cannot increase these lengths. Therefore
$$ H_{k+1}\leq2H_k+cL, $$
and induction gives
$$ H_k\leq(H_0+cL)2^k=O(L\,2^k). $$

Optimal-good comparisons, subset sums, rational updates, and fraction reductions all take polynomial time in $L$ and $H_k$, using elementary integer arithmetic. Summing these costs through iteration $K$ gives
$$ 2^{O(K+L)} \leq2^{\,2^{O(L)}}. $$
At iteration $K$, the same rule supplies the required final demands
$$ x_{tj}^K= \begin{cases} b_t/p_j^K,&j\text{ is the chosen good for }t,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
Their encoding lengths satisfy the same bound.

This proves a general explicit-output algorithm with doubly exponential running time.

5. Denominators grow without cancellation

Scale the first coordinate by $\eta$:
$$ y_k=\frac{p_1^k}{\eta}, \qquad C=\frac{a}{\eta}=2^{n-1}. $$
Then
$$ y_{k+1}=y_k-1+\frac{C}{y_k}, \qquad y_0=\frac{3\cdot2^{n-1}+1}{2}. $$

We prove that the reduced expression $y_k=A_k/D_k$ has $A_k$ odd, $D_k$ even, and satisfies
$$ A_{k+1}=A_k^2-A_kD_k+CD_k^2, \qquad D_{k+1}=A_kD_k. $$
The assertions hold initially. Suppose they hold at iteration $k$, and write
$$ N=A_k^2-A_kD_k+CD_k^2. $$
The integer $N$ is odd. Moreover,
$$ \gcd(N,D_k)=\gcd(A_k^2,D_k)=1, $$
and
$$ \gcd(N,A_k) =\gcd(CD_k^2,A_k) =\gcd(C,A_k) =1. $$
The second calculation uses $\gcd(A_k,D_k)=1$; the last equality uses that $C$ is a power of two and $A_k$ is odd. Hence $\gcd(N,A_kD_k)=1$, so no cancellation occurs. The new numerator is positive because $y_{k+1}>0$. This completes the induction.

Since $p_1^k>a$,
$$ \frac{A_k}{D_k}>C. $$
Therefore
$$ D_{k+1}=A_kD_k>CD_k^2\geq D_k^2. $$
Starting from $D_0=2$, induction yields
$$ D_k\geq2^{\,2^k}. $$

Returning to the price coordinate,
$$ p_1^k=\frac{A_k}{2^nD_k}. $$
This fraction is reduced because $A_k$ is odd and coprime to $D_k$. Its denominator therefore requires at least $2^k+1$ binary digits.

Every successful endpoint has $K\geq2^{n-3}$, so writing its first price coordinate requires at least
$$ 2^K\geq2^{\,2^{n-3}} $$
bits.

Allowing an unreduced fraction does not help. If $A/Q$ is reduced and $A/Q=P/R$ with $R>0$, then $AR=PQ$ and coprimality implies $Q\mid R$. Thus an alternative denominator cannot be smaller.

Because $L=\Theta(n)$, every successful explicit output on this family has length
$$ 2^{\,2^{\Omega(L)}}. $$
This excludes polynomial running time even for algorithms that skip intermediate iterations internally. Unique demands also exclude any escape through tie splitting.
