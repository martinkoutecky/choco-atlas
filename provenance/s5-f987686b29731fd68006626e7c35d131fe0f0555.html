The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite supports of doctor and hospital types, rational type masses, capacities per hospital type, responsive rankings, and a target stable matching flow \(x_{ij}\), does there exist a finite preference-preserving augmentation and a finite sequence of mass-cohort entries and exits whose stable proposal-chain continuations reach \(x_{ij}\), with all temporary mass absent at termination?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, with masses encoded as binary rationals; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(n\) doctor types and \(m\) hospital types, with \(\tau=n+m\), masses \(a_i,b_j>0\), and integer capacities \(q_j\geq1\) per unit hospital mass. Masses need not sum to one. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and the capacities \(q_j\).
Each type strictly ranks opposite-side types and the unmatched option \(\emptyset\); copies of one type are indifferent. Hospital preferences are responsive. At active masses \(\alpha_i,\beta_j\), a flow \(y\geq0\) satisfies \(\sum_j y_{ij}\leq\alpha_i\) and \(\sum_i y_{ij}\leq q_j\beta_j\), and uses only mutually acceptable pairs.
A flow is stable if no mutually acceptable pair \((i,j)\) has both positive doctor mass unmatched or assigned below \(j\), and positive hospital capacity vacant or occupied by doctor types ranked below \(i\). The input includes a binary-rational target flow \(x\), promised stable at the terminal masses.
An augmentation adds finitely many positive-rational-mass temporary types, with freely chosen fixed preferences and positive integer hospital capacities. Each original type's extended ranking preserves its ranking of original opposite-side types and \(\emptyset\); original capacities remain fixed. The augmentation may depend on \(x\).
A history starts empty and changes the active population by entry or exit of one positive-rational-mass cohort of one type per event. Cohorts retain their preferences across repeated exits and entries, and active mass never exceeds the augmented population. Exiting doctors remove their assignments; exiting hospital mass \(\delta\) removes chosen assignments totaling at most \(q_j\delta\), leaving the remaining capacity constraints satisfied.
After entry, entering doctors or hospital seats propose; after exit, abandoned doctors or vacated hospital seats propose. A proposer offers its currently unassigned mass to acceptable opposite-side types in descending order. A recipient retains its highest-ranked acceptable mass up to its active quota, keeping incumbents before incoming mass of the same type; rejected and displaced mass continues proposing, excluding recipients that rejected that portion during this continuation. Hospital-seat mass is measured in doctor-capacity units. Processing order and subdivisions into rational portions may be chosen.
Every continuation must finish after finitely many proposals at a stable flow before the next event; a zero-length continuation is allowed when the inherited flow is already stable. At termination all original mass is present, all temporary mass is absent, and the flow is exactly \(x\).

Is deciding whether such an augmentation and history exist solvable in polynomial time, or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, on the promised stable-target inputs?

1. Polynomial-time criterion

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable.

For a target flow $x$, define its support graph $G_x$: its vertices are the $n$ original doctor types and $m$ original hospital types, and it has an edge $(i,j)$ exactly when $x_{ij}>0$.

Theorem. A promised stable target $x$ admits an augmentation and history satisfying the question’s rules if and only if $G_x$ is a forest.

Cycle detection gives a polynomial-time decision algorithm. A conservative bit-operation bound is
$$ O((m\tau+\tau)L^2). $$

For a forest target, the construction below uses $\tau$ temporary types, all temporary hospital capacities equal to one, $O(\tau)$ population events, and $O(m\tau+\tau)$ proposals. An explicit construction can be produced in
$$ O((\tau^2+m\tau+\tau)L^2) $$
bit operations. These bounds have no numerical factor $q_j$ or $\sum_jq_j$; capacities enter through their binary encoding in $L$.

The proof works directly with mass flows.

2. The structure of a cycle in a stable support

We first use a symmetric formulation. Each vertex has a quota, strictly ranks opposite-side types and $\emptyset$, and may hold mass up to its quota. Hospital quotas are measured in doctor-capacity units.

For a positive assignment edge, call an endpoint its owner if that endpoint is full and the edge is its worst occupied partner type. Strict rankings imply that a vertex owns at most one edge.

Lemma 1. Every positive edge of a stable flow has an owner.

Proof. Consider a positive mutually acceptable edge $(p,r)$. If neither endpoint owns it, then:

  • $p$ has positive unassigned mass or positive mass assigned below $r$;
  • $r$ has positive unassigned quota or positive mass assigned below $p$.

These are precisely the two conditions for blocking. Stability rules them out simultaneously. Hence at least one endpoint owns the edge. $\square$

Lemma 2. Let a stable flow have a simple support cycle. Every vertex on that cycle is full. The cycle can be indexed by proposer types $p_1,\ldots,p_k$ and recipient types $r_1,\ldots,r_k$, with indices modulo $k$, so that its edges are
$$ (p_\ell,r_\ell),\qquad (p_\ell,r_{\ell+1}), $$
and
$$ r_\ell\succ_{p_\ell}r_{\ell+1}, \qquad p_{\ell-1}\succ_{r_\ell}p_\ell. $$
Moreover, $r_{\ell+1}$ is the worst occupied recipient of $p_\ell$, and $p_\ell$ is the worst occupied proposer at $r_\ell$.

Proof. A cycle with $2k$ edges has $2k$ vertices. Every cycle edge has an owner among these vertices, while each vertex owns at most one edge. Consequently, every cycle vertex owns exactly one cycle edge, and every cycle edge has exactly one owner.

Orient each cycle edge from its owner to its other endpoint. Each cycle vertex then has one outgoing and one incoming edge, so this orientation is a directed cycle. Index it as
$$ p_\ell\longrightarrow r_{\ell+1}\longrightarrow p_{\ell+1}. $$
The owned edges are worst occupied partners. The other cycle partner is distinct and therefore strictly preferred. Ownership also implies that every cycle vertex is full. $\square$

In particular, these statements apply to any augmented market, because its type rankings remain strict and hospital preferences are responsive.

3. Finite proposal continuations cannot create a stable support cycle

We prove the main obstruction.

Fix one proposal direction during a continuation. Write $A_p$ for proposer quotas and $C_r$ for recipient quotas. These quotas remain fixed throughout the continuation.

Say that recipient $r$ is closed to $p$ if it is full and every held proposer type is weakly preferred to $p$. Here “weakly” includes equality of types.

Three consequences of the retention rule will be used:

  • A closed recipient rejects incoming mass of type $p$: better incumbents remain, and incumbents of type $p$ take precedence over incoming mass of that type.
  • If a recipient rejects a positive portion of type $p$, it is closed to $p$ after processing that offer.
  • Once closed to $p$, a recipient remains closed to $p$ throughout the continuation. Its quota is fixed, and proposals can only replace held mass by higher-ranked mass.

3.1 An invariant for assigned mass

For a current flow $z$, consider the property
$$ z_{pr}>0,\quad s\succ_p r,\quad (p,s)\text{ mutually acceptable} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad s\text{ is closed to }p. $$
Call this property $I$.

Lemma 3. Property $I$ holds immediately after a proposer entry or a recipient exit from a stable flow, and remains true after every proposal.

Proof.

Before the population event, stability implies $I$. Positive assignment of $p$ below $s$ supplies the proposer-side blocking condition, so $s$ must have neither vacancy nor a lower-ranked occupant.

A proposer entry changes no recipient quota or inherited assignment, so it preserves $I$.

Now consider a recipient exit reducing quota by $\Delta$. Suppose that recipient previously was full, with quota $C$, and the exit removes assigned mass $R$. The exit rule and remaining feasibility give
$$ R\leq\Delta, \qquad C-R\leq C-\Delta. $$
Therefore $R=\Delta$. The recipient remains full, and its remaining occupants are a subset of its former occupants. Thus any closedness condition needed by a retained assignment survives the exit. All other recipients are unchanged.

Finally, consider proposals. Closedness persists, so previously assigned edges retain property $I$. Before a portion is accepted at $r$, descending proposals require rejection of that portion by every mutually acceptable recipient it prefers to $r$. Each such rejection makes that recipient closed to the proposer type, and closedness persists. Thus newly accepted assignments also satisfy $I$. Recipients of zero quota are automatically closed. $\square$

3.2 The cycle obstruction

Lemma 4. If the inherited support is a forest, a finite continuation satisfying the proposal rules cannot finish at a stable flow whose support contains a cycle.

Proof. Suppose, to obtain a contradiction, that the final stable flow contains a cycle indexed as in Lemma 2.

Because the inherited support is a forest and the continuation has finitely many proposals, there is a last proposal after which all edges of this cycle become positive and remain positive until termination.

An individual offer adds at most one support edge. Hence immediately before that proposal, exactly one cycle edge is absent; every other cycle edge is present.

The newly added edge cannot be $(p_\ell,r_\ell)$. Indeed, $(p_\ell,r_{\ell+1})$ was already positive and
$$ r_\ell\succ_{p_\ell}r_{\ell+1}. $$
Property $I$ makes $r_\ell$ closed to $p_\ell$, preventing acceptance of incoming $p_\ell$ mass.

Thus the newly added edge is $(p_\ell,r_{\ell+1})$.

Immediately before this offer, the edges
$$ (p_{\ell+1},r_{\ell+1}), \qquad (p_{\ell+1},r_{\ell+2}) $$
are positive. Property $I$ makes $r_{\ell+1}$ full with types weakly preferred to $p_{\ell+1}$. Since it holds positive $p_{\ell+1}$ mass, that is its lowest-ranked occupied type. Also,
$$ p_\ell\succ_{r_{\ell+1}}p_{\ell+1}. $$

Let $t>0$ be the accepted incoming mass. The edge $(p_{\ell+1},r_{\ell+1})$ remains positive after the offer. The retention rule therefore displaces exactly $t$ mass of $p_{\ell+1}$: it cannot displace a higher-ranked incumbent while retaining positive mass of the lowest-ranked incumbent.

Define the total currently unassigned mass of cycle proposer types by
$$ U_C=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k} \left(A_{p_\ell}-\sum_r z_{p_\ell r}\right). $$
The creating offer assigns $t$ previously unassigned $p_\ell$ mass and unassigns $t$ mass of $p_{\ell+1}$. Thus it leaves $U_C$ unchanged. Before the offer, $U_C\geq t$, so immediately afterward
$$ U_C>0. $$

We show that $U_C$ cannot decrease while every cycle edge remains positive.

An offer from a proposer outside the cycle can only leave $U_C$ unchanged or displace cycle proposer mass, increasing it.

Consider an offer from $p_\ell$.

  • Every mutually acceptable recipient strictly preferred to $r_{\ell+1}$ is closed to $p_\ell$, by property $I$ and the positive assignment at $r_{\ell+1}$. Such recipients reject the offer.
  • A portion cannot reach a recipient below $r_{\ell+1}$ in its proposal order. That would require an earlier rejection by $r_{\ell+1}$, making it permanently closed to $p_\ell$. This contradicts its currently positive assignment of the strictly lower-ranked type $p_{\ell+1}$.
  • At $r_{\ell+1}$, accepted mass displaces an equal amount of $p_{\ell+1}$ mass, as long as the latter cycle edge remains positive. Hence $U_C$ is unchanged.

Therefore $U_C$ stays positive until termination.

But Lemma 2 says every cycle proposer is full in the final stable flow, which gives $U_C=0$. This contradiction proves the lemma. $\square$

3.3 Application to every event direction

Doctor entry and hospital exit initiate doctor proposals. Their recipient quotas are hospital capacities.

Hospital entry and doctor exit initiate hospital-seat proposals. Their recipient quotas are doctor masses. Responsiveness allows all seats of a hospital type to be treated as proposer mass with the same preference order. An exiting doctor cohort of mass $\delta$ removes at most $\delta$ assigned mass, exactly the recipient-exit hypothesis used in Lemma 3.

Thus Lemma 4 applies in both directions.

4. Necessity of forest support

The empty initial flow has forest support.

Immediately after any population event, inherited assignments have only been removed or retained; no assignment edge has yet been added. Its support is therefore a subgraph of the previous stable support.

Inductively, if the previous stable support is a forest, the inherited support is a forest, and Lemma 4 shows that the next stable support is also a forest. A zero-length continuation has the same conclusion.

This induction includes all temporary types and all repeated cohort entries and exits. At termination the only active types are original types, and the flow is $x$. Consequently,
$$ \text{existence of a permitted augmented history} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G_x\text{ is a forest}. $$

Finiteness of each continuation is used explicitly in selecting the last proposal that creates the final cycle.

5. Constructing a history for every forest target

Assume henceforth that $x$ is stable and $G_x$ is a forest. If either side is empty, $x=0$ and entering the original population suffices. We give the construction for nonempty sides.

Write
$$ c_j=q_jb_j,\qquad u_i=a_i-\sum_hx_{ih},\qquad v_j=c_j-\sum_kx_{kj}. $$

5.1 Ordering the target hospitals

We need an order of hospitals in which every doctor’s positively assigned hospitals appear in that doctor’s preference order.

For each doctor $i$ with positive assignments, list those hospitals as
$$ h_{i,1}\succ_i h_{i,2}\succ_i\cdots\succ_i h_{i,k_i}, $$
and add directed arcs
$$ h_{i,1}\longrightarrow h_{i,2} \longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow h_{i,k_i}. $$

Lemma 5. This directed hospital graph is acyclic.

Proof. Ignore isolated doctor vertices in $G_x$, retaining all hospital vertices. Let $n_+$ be the number of remaining doctors, $E$ the number of assignment edges, and $c$ the number of connected components. Every component contains a hospital, and the graph is a forest, so
$$ E=n_++m-c. $$

Replace each doctor’s star by the undirected path through its hospital neighbors in the displayed order. This preserves connectivity among hospital vertices: the star and its replacement path connect exactly the same neighbors.

The resulting hospital multigraph has $m$ vertices, $c$ components, and
$$ \sum_i(k_i-1)=E-n_+=m-c $$
edges. A graph with these component and edge counts is a forest, since each connected component needs at least one fewer edge than vertices, with equality exactly for a tree.

The directed hospital graph is an orientation of this forest, and therefore has no directed cycle. $\square$

Fix a topological order of this graph. In particular, whenever $x_{ih}>0$, $x_{ij}>0$, and $h\succ_i j$, hospital $h$ precedes hospital $j$.

5.2 Stability separation, including the equality case

For a mutually acceptable pair $(i,j)$, define
$$ d_{ij}=u_i+\sum_{h:\,j\succ_i h}x_{ih}. $$
This is the terminal mass of doctor type $i$ that strictly wants hospital $j$. Set
$$ S_j=\{k:x_{kj}>0\}, \qquad B_j=\{i:(i,j)\text{ mutually acceptable and }d_{ij}>0\}. $$

Stability gives the following facts:

  • If $B_j$ is nonempty, then $v_j=0$.
  • If $i\in B_j$ and $k\in S_j$, then either $k=i$ or $k\succ_j i$.

For the first fact, vacancy together with $d_{ij}>0$ would block. For the second, occupied mass of a type below $i$ would block.

When $S_j$ is nonempty, let $w_j$ be its lowest-ranked member. The second fact implies that every member of $B_j$ other than $w_j$ lies strictly below $w_j$. It also implies
$$ S_j\cap B_j\subseteq\{w_j\}. $$
When $S_j$ is empty, $v_j=c_j>0$, so $B_j$ is empty.

5.3 Temporary types and fixed preferences

Introduce the following types.

For each original doctor type $i$, introduce a temporary hospital type $P_i$, of mass $a_i$ and capacity one. It finds only $i$ acceptable. Doctor $i$ ranks $P_i$ first and finds every other temporary hospital unacceptable.

For each original hospital type $j$, introduce a temporary doctor type $T_j$, of mass $c_j$. It finds only $j$ acceptable. Hospital $j$ ranks it:

  • immediately below $w_j$ when $S_j$ is nonempty;
  • immediately above $\emptyset$ when $S_j$ is empty.

All other original hospitals find $T_j$ unacceptable.

Thus $T_j$ lies below every target assignee at $j$ and above every member of $B_j\setminus\{w_j\}$. The possible equality case $w_j\in B_j$ is handled by the ordering of releases below.

Complete rankings strictly below $\emptyset$ for all remaining unacceptable types. These extensions preserve every original ranking over original types and $\emptyset$.

Partition the temporary populations into fixed cohorts:

  • $P_i$ has a cohort of mass $x_{ij}$ for each positive target edge, and a residual cohort of mass $u_i$ when positive.
  • $T_j$ has a cohort of mass $x_{ij}$ for each positive target edge, and a residual cohort of mass $v_j$ when positive.

Zero-mass cohorts are omitted. These cohort masses sum to the specified temporary populations.

5.4 Building the initial assignment

Starting empty, enter:

  • all original hospitals;
  • all temporary doctor cohorts, filling each $j$ with $T_j$ mass $c_j$;
  • all private hospital cohorts;
  • all original doctors, each obtaining its first choice $P_i$.

Each cohort enters in its own event.

Every intermediate flow is stable. Before original doctors enter, all present temporary doctors obtain their unique acceptable hospital, and private hospitals have no active acceptable doctors. Each original doctor subsequently obtains its first choice. The indicated assignments are achieved by the prescribed proposals, with zero-length continuations when the inherited flow is already stable.

5.5 Installing target assignments

Process original hospitals in the topological order from Lemma 5. At hospital $j$, process its positive target edges in any order.

To install edge $(i,j)$, exit the corresponding $P_i$ cohort of mass $x_{ij}$. Because active $P_i$ capacity is filled entirely with type $i$, this abandons exactly $x_{ij}$ original doctor mass.

We maintain the following assignment invariant:

  • unreleased original doctor mass remains at its first-choice private hospital;
  • released original doctor mass has precisely its installed target assignments;
  • each original hospital contains its installed target mass and enough $T_j$ mass to fill its capacity;
  • displaced temporary doctors are unmatched.

Consider the abandoned mass of type $i$. Its private hospital, if still active, is full with incumbent mass of type $i$, so it rejects incoming mass of that type. Other private hospitals are unacceptable.

Let $h\succ_i j$ be an original mutually acceptable hospital.

If $x_{ih}>0$, hospital $h$ has already been processed. Because $x_{ij}>0$ is assigned below $h$, we have $d_{ih}>0$. Stability therefore gives $v_h=0$. Hospital $h$ holds exactly its complete target column, all of whose types are weakly preferred to $i$. It rejects the proposing mass, including against incumbents of the same type.

If $x_{ih}=0$, then $i\in B_h\setminus S_h$. Every target assignee at $h$, and its temporary holder $T_h$, is strictly preferred to $i$. Hospital $h$ is full and again rejects the mass.

Thus the released mass reaches $j$.

Let $A$ be the already installed target mass there. Since the current edge has not yet been installed,
$$ A+x_{ij}\leq\sum_kx_{kj}\leq c_j. $$
Consequently at least $x_{ij}$ holder mass remains. Type $i$ and every installed target assignee rank above $T_j$, so hospital $j$ accepts all $x_{ij}$ and displaces exactly that much $T_j$ mass. Choose the corresponding holder cohort for displacement.

The displaced holder finds only $j$ acceptable and has just been rejected there. Its continuation ends. The assignment invariant is preserved.

Stability after each installation

We check all possible blocking pairs.

An original doctor portion still at $P_i$ has its first choice.

Suppose released original mass of type $i$ wants an original hospital $h$. Then $i\in B_h$.

If $i\notin S_h$, every target assignee and the holder at $h$ rank strictly above $i$.

If $i\in S_h$, then $i=w_h$. The released mass wanting $h$ is at some target hospital below $h$. The topological order therefore ensured that $h$ was completely processed before that release. Also $v_h=0$. Thus $h$ is full with target types weakly preferred to $i$.

In either case, the hospital-side blocking condition fails.

A private hospital still active is full with its only acceptable type. An unmatched temporary doctor $T_h$ faces a full hospital containing only its own type or target types strictly above it. No other temporary pair is mutually acceptable.

Hence the resulting flow is stable after every installation. Each continuation has finitely many proposals.

5.6 Releasing unmatched target mass

After all target edges have been installed, $P_i$ holds exactly $u_i$ mass. Exit its residual cohort when $u_i>0$.

At every mutually acceptable original hospital $h$, the inequality $u_i>0$ gives $i\in B_h$. Hence $v_h=0$, and $h$ is full with its target column, consisting of types weakly preferred to $i$. Every proposal is rejected, leaving exactly $u_i$ unmatched.

The stability check from the preceding subsection continues to apply. After these exits, all private hospitals are absent and the original-original flow is exactly $x$.

5.7 Removing the temporary doctors

First exit all unmatched $T_j$ cohorts. These exits change no assignments and preserve stability.

The remaining $T_j$ mass is exactly $v_j$, assigned to $j$. When $v_j>0$, stability of $x$ gives $B_j=\emptyset$. Exit this residual cohort.

The resulting vacancy cannot block with an original doctor, because $B_j=\emptyset$. No other temporary doctor finds $j$ acceptable, and all unmatched $T_j$ mass has already exited. Thus the inherited flow is stable and a zero-length continuation is permitted.

After all such exits, all original mass is present, every temporary type is absent, and the flow is exactly $x$.

This proves sufficiency.

6. Algorithm and quantitative bounds

On the promised input, construct $G_x$ and test whether it contains an undirected cycle. Return yes exactly when it is a forest. Sections 4 and 5 prove correctness.

There are at most $nm$ possible support edges, so this requires
$$ O(nm+\tau)\subseteq O(m\tau+\tau) $$
graph operations, in addition to reading the input. The conservative bit-operation bound stated in Section 1 follows.

For a yes-instance, let $E$ be the number of positive target edges. Forest support gives $E\leq\tau$. The construction uses:

  • $n$ temporary hospital types and $m$ temporary doctor types;
  • at most $E+n$ private-hospital cohorts;
  • at most $E+m$ holder-doctor cohorts;
  • $O(\tau)$ events;
  • $O(m\tau+\tau)$ proposals, since each released original doctor portion visits at most $m$ original hospitals and its private hospital.

Explicit extended rankings contain $O(\tau^2)$ entries.

All numerical data have $O(L)$ bits. To see this directly, take the product of the denominators of all input rationals as a common denominator. Its bit length is at most $L$. Input masses, target entries, row and column residuals, and products $q_jb_j$ then have integer numerators of $O(L)$ bits. This gives the construction bound
$$ O((\tau^2+m\tau+\tau)L^2) $$
using ordinary exact integer arithmetic.

No expansion into individual doctors or individual hospital seats is needed.

7. A stable family ruled out even with augmentation

Consider two doctor and two hospital types, all masses and capacities equal to one, with all original pairs acceptable:
$$ H_1\succ_{D_1}H_2,\qquad H_2\succ_{D_2}H_1, $$
$$ D_2\succ_{H_1}D_1,\qquad D_1\succ_{H_2}D_2. $$

For rational $0<t<1$, let
$$ x= \begin{pmatrix} t&1-t\\ 1-t&t \end{pmatrix}. $$
This target is stable: both hospitals are full, and a doctor wanting more of its preferred hospital encounters only its own type and a strictly preferred doctor type. Neither doctor has mass below its less-preferred hospital.

Its support is a four-cycle. The necessity theorem therefore rules out every permitted augmented history for this target. The two endpoint targets have forest support and are reachable.

1. Ownership and cycle structure

Lemma 1 is valid. On a positive assignment edge, an endpoint that does not own the edge must either have unused quota or hold positive mass at a strictly worse partner. These are exactly the relevant blocking conditions. Mutual acceptability is available because the edge carries positive flow.

Strict rankings between types ensure that a full vertex has a unique worst occupied partner type, so it owns at most one edge. Indifference between copies of the same type does not affect this statement.

Lemma 2 is valid. A simple cycle has equally many vertices and edges. Every cycle edge needs an owner at one of its endpoints, and every vertex can own at most one edge. Consequently, every cycle vertex owns exactly one cycle edge, and every cycle edge has exactly one owner.

Orienting edges from their owners therefore gives one incoming and one outgoing edge at each cycle vertex. The resulting orientation is a directed cycle. The displayed indexing and preference inequalities follow from ownership: the owned edge is the worst occupied partner, and the other cycle partner is distinct. Ownership also establishes fullness.

The argument allows additional occupied edges outside the selected cycle; the owned cycle edge remains the worst occupied partner among all occupied edges.

2. Closedness and the proposal invariant

The three stated properties of closedness follow from the retention rule:

  • A full recipient holding only types weakly preferred to the incoming type rejects that incoming mass, including when its worst incumbents have the same type.
  • Rejection of positive incoming mass leaves the recipient full with types weakly preferred to the rejected type.
  • With fixed quota, subsequent proposals cannot worsen the recipient’s held types.

The incumbent priority within a type is essential to the first property and is explicitly supplied by the task.

Lemma 3 is valid. Before an event, an assignment below a mutually acceptable recipient supplies the proposer-side blocking condition. Stability therefore forces that recipient to be closed.

A proposer entry preserves recipient quotas and inherited assignments. For a recipient exit, the displayed inequalities correctly imply that a previously full recipient loses exactly the quota reduction in assignments and remains full. Its remaining occupants are drawn from its former occupants, so the required ranking condition survives.

During proposals, previously established closedness persists. Descending proposal order, together with the rejection exclusion rule, supplies the needed closedness for assignments newly accepted at a lower-ranked recipient. The zero-quota case is also covered.

3. The finite-continuation obstruction

Lemma 4 is valid. Finiteness permits selection of the last transition after which every edge of the chosen final cycle is positive and remains positive. Because one offer adds at most one support edge, immediately before that transition exactly one selected cycle edge is absent.

The two possibilities for the new edge are handled correctly:

  • An edge of the form $(p_\ell,r_\ell)$ cannot be newly accepted. The existing assignment at the worse recipient $r_{\ell+1}$ makes $r_\ell$ closed to $p_\ell$.
  • For an edge of the form $(p_\ell,r_{\ell+1})$, the other cycle assignments make $r_{\ell+1}$ full with $p_{\ell+1}$ as its lowest occupied type. Since positive $p_{\ell+1}$ mass survives the offer, acceptance of mass $t$ displaces exactly $t$ mass of that type.

Thus the creating offer leaves $U_C$ unchanged while ensuring $U_C>0$.

The subsequent nondecrease argument is also sound. Offers from outside the cycle cannot assign unassigned cycle proposer mass. A cycle proposer cannot obtain acceptance above its designated worse cycle recipient because those recipients are closed.
