The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\tau\) types with rational masses \(\mu_a\), nonnegative rational biases \(b_a\), a symmetric nonnegative rational interaction matrix \(K\), and a budget \(B\), consider populations of size \(N\) whose type proportions approach \(\mu\), with type-\(a\) agents interacting with type-\(b\) agents at strength \(K_{ab}/N\). Choose fractions \(x_a\in[0,1]\) of each type to pin active, subject to \(\sum_a\mu_a x_a\le B\). For free-agent type magnetizations \(r_a\in[-1,1]\), set \(q_a=x_a+(1-x_a)r_a\), and let \(r^*(x)\) be the unique maximizer of \(\frac12\sum_{a,b}\mu_a\mu_b K_{ab}q_aq_b+\sum_a\mu_a b_aq_a+\sum_a\mu_a(1-x_a)s(r_a)\), where \(s\) is binary entropy. Define \(\mathcal M(x)=\sum_a\mu_a q_a^*(x)\). Determine whether an algorithm polynomial in \(\tau\), input bit length \(L\), and \(1/\epsilon\) can return feasible \(x\) with \(\mathcal M(x)\ge(1-1/e-\epsilon)\mathrm{OPT}\), or establish a complexity barrier for this approximation problem.

1. Result

The requested approximation is achievable in deterministic polynomial time under the uniqueness promise.

The proof below establishes fractional diminishing returns, gives a polynomial bit-time equilibrium evaluation routine, and reduces the problem to monotone submodular maximization under one knapsack constraint. The numerical evaluation argument includes convergence and rational rounding explicitly.

Throughout, inequalities between vectors are coordinatewise. Write
$$ v_a=1-x_a,\qquad w_a=\mu_av_a, $$
and define
$$ \kappa=\sum_{a,b}\mu_a\mu_bK_{ab},\qquad \beta=\sum_a\mu_ab_a,\qquad C=2\kappa+2\beta+1. $$

2. A concave formulation of the nonnegative equilibrium

2.1. Restriction to nonnegative free magnetizations

For any $r\in[-1,1]^\tau$, replace $r_a$ by $|r_a|$. The resulting magnetization satisfies
$$ x_a+v_a|r_a|\ge |x_a+v_ar_a|. $$
Consequently, every interaction product after replacement is at least its original value: if the original product is nonnegative, compare absolute values; if it is negative, the new product is nonnegative. Bias contributions also do not decrease, and entropy is unchanged.

Thus a nonnegative global maximizer always exists. On feasible controls, the uniqueness promise identifies it with the prescribed equilibrium.

Set $z_a=r_a^2$ and
$$ G_x(z)=\Phi_x(\sqrt z),\qquad z\in[0,1]^\tau. $$
Symmetry of $K$ gives
$$ G_x(z) =c_x+ \sum_a w_a\left(b_a+\sum_b\mu_bK_{ab}x_b\right)\sqrt{z_a} +\frac12\sum_{a,b}\mu_a\mu_bK_{ab}v_av_b\sqrt{z_az_b} +\sum_a w_ag(z_a), $$
where $c_x$ is independent of $z$ and $g(z)=s(\sqrt z)$.

Square roots and geometric means are concave, and all their coefficients here are nonnegative. For completeness, concavity of the geometric mean follows from Cauchy–Schwarz:
$$ \sqrt{((1-t)u+tu')((1-t)v+tv')} \ge (1-t)\sqrt{uv}+t\sqrt{u'v'}. $$

2.2. Quantitative strict concavity

Integrating the power series for $\operatorname{arctanh}r$ yields
$$ g(z)=\log 2-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^k}{2k(2k-1)}. $$
The series converges uniformly on $[0,1]$, so it also gives the endpoint values. On $(0,1)$, termwise differentiation gives
$$ g''(z) =-\sum_{k=2}^{\infty} \frac{k-1}{2(2k-1)}z^{k-2} \le-\frac16. $$
The resulting strong-concavity inequality extends to the endpoints by continuity. Therefore,
$$ G_x((1-t)z+tz') \ge (1-t)G_x(z)+tG_x(z') +\frac{t(1-t)}{12}\sum_a w_a(z_a-z'_a)^2. $$

Every unpinned coordinate has $w_a>0$. Hence $G_x$ has a unique maximizer on those coordinates. Fully pinned coordinates are irrelevant to both its value and the resulting magnetization.

Let $z^*(x)$ denote this maximizer, with arbitrary values in fully pinned coordinates, and define
$$ q_a^+(x)=x_a+v_a\sqrt{z_a^*(x)},\qquad M^+(x)=\sum_a\mu_aq_a^+(x). $$
This defines a nonnegative equilibrium throughout the entire control cube, including infeasible controls. On feasible controls,
$$ M^+(x)=\mathcal M(x). $$

Applying strong concavity between $z^*(x)$ and an arbitrary $z$, using maximality at the intermediate point, dividing by $t$, and letting $t\downarrow0$, gives the useful gap bound
$$ G_x(z^*(x))-G_x(z) \ge \frac1{12}\sum_a w_a(z_a^*(x)-z_a)^2. $$

3. Continuity and conversion of objective accuracy

For fixed $z$, let $d=\|x-y\|_\infty$. Each nonnegative magnetization changes by at most $d$. An interaction product changes by at most $2d$, and the entropy contribution changes by at most $d\log2$. Thus
$$ |G_x(z)-G_y(z)| \le(\kappa+\beta+\log2)d \le Cd. $$

Writing $z_x=z^*(x)$ and $z_y=z^*(y)$, maximality of $z_y$ implies
$$ G_x(z_x)-G_x(z_y)\le2Cd. $$
The gap bound therefore gives
$$ \sum_a w_a(z_{x,a}-z_{y,a})^2\le24Cd. $$

For nonnegative $u,v$,
$$ |\sqrt u-\sqrt v|\le |u-v|^{1/2}. $$
Hölder’s inequality, together with $\sum_a w_a\le1$, consequently gives
$$ \sum_a w_a|\sqrt{z_{x,a}}-\sqrt{z_{y,a}}| \le \left(\sum_a w_a(z_{x,a}-z_{y,a})^2\right)^{1/4}. $$
Changing the control itself contributes at most $d$, so
$$ |M^+(x)-M^+(y)| \le d+(24Cd)^{1/4}. $$

Each component $q_a^+$ is also continuous. At a control with $x_a<1$, the preceding weighted squared-distance bound controls its $z_a$ coordinate because $w_a>0$. At a control with $x_a=1$,
$$ 0\le1-q_a^+(y)\le1-y_a. $$
Since the feasible set is compact, $\mathrm{OPT}$ is attained.

The same Hölder calculation proves an accuracy conversion that does not involve the smallest positive weight. If
$$ G_x(z^*(x))-G_x(z)\le\Delta, $$
then
$$ \left| \sum_a\mu_a\bigl[x_a+v_a\sqrt{z_a}\bigr]-M^+(x) \right| \le(12\Delta)^{1/4}. $$

4. Monotonicity and fractional diminishing returns

We prove that $M^+$ is monotone and satisfies
$$ M^+(x+te_i)-M^+(x) \ge M^+(y+te_i)-M^+(y) $$
whenever $x\le y$, $t\ge0$, and both incremented controls belong to the cube.

4.1. Positive-bias perturbation and stability

First increase every bias by a fixed $\lambda>0$. Let
$$ A_{ab}=\mu_bK_{ab},\qquad h=b+\lambda\mathbf1+Aq. $$
At the nonnegative maximizer, every unpinned free magnetization lies strictly between zero and one. Indeed, its objective derivative is
$$ w_a\bigl(h_a-\operatorname{arctanh}r_a\bigr). $$
It is positive at $r_a=0$, since $h_a\ge\lambda$, and tends to negative infinity as $r_a\uparrow1$. Stationarity gives $r=\tanh h$ on the unpinned coordinates. Accordingly, the magnetization satisfies
$$ q=x+(1-x)\odot\tanh(b+\lambda\mathbf1+Aq). $$
This identity also holds on pinned coordinates.

Its Jacobian with respect to $q$ is
$$ J=\operatorname{diag}\bigl((1-x_a)(1-r_a^2)\bigr)A. $$
It is entrywise nonnegative. Moreover,
$$ (q-Jq)_a =x_a+(1-x_a) \left[ \tanh h_a-(h_a-b_a-\lambda)\operatorname{sech}^2h_a \right]>0. $$
For an unpinned coordinate, this follows from $h_a>0$, $b_a+\lambda>0$, and
$$ \tanh h-h\operatorname{sech}^2h>0\qquad(h>0). $$
The latter function vanishes at zero and has derivative
$$ 2h\operatorname{sech}^2h\tanh h>0. $$
For a pinned coordinate, $(q-Jq)_a=1$.

Because $q>0$, the weighted maximum norm
$$ \|u\|_q=\max_a\frac{|u_a|}{q_a} $$
satisfies
$$ \|J\|_q\le\max_a\frac{(Jq)_a}{q_a}<1. $$
It follows that
$$ (I-J)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}J^k $$
exists and is entrywise nonnegative.

The implicit-function theorem now applies on the interior of the control cube. Continuity of the equilibrium, already proved above and equally valid for the perturbed biases, identifies its local solution with the maximizing branch.

4.2. First and second derivatives

For a nonnegative control direction $u$, differentiating the equilibrium identity gives
$$ (I-J)q_u=u\odot(1-r). $$
Thus $q_u\ge0$.

For two nonnegative directions $u,v$, a second differentiation gives
$$ (I-J)q_{uv} = -(1-r^2)\odot \bigl[u\odot(Aq_v)+v\odot(Aq_u)\bigr] -2(1-x)\odot r\odot(1-r^2) \odot(Aq_u)\odot(Aq_v). $$
Every term on the right is entrywise nonpositive. Therefore $q_{uv}\le0$.

In particular, every first coordinate derivative of every component of $q$ is nonnegative, and every second coordinate derivative, including a repeated-coordinate derivative, is nonpositive. The same statements hold for their positive weighted sum $M^+$.

Integrating the first derivatives proves monotonicity. The second-derivative signs show that $\partial_iM^+$ decreases when any control coordinate increases. Integrating $\partial_iM^+$ over an increment of length $t$ proves the stated diminishing-returns inequality in the interior.

Continuity extends these inequalities to the boundary. Explicitly, replace a boundary control $x$ by
$$ x^{(\delta)}=\delta\mathbf1+(1-2\delta)x $$
and an increment $t$ by $(1-2\delta)t$, then let $\delta\downarrow0$.

4.3. Removing the perturbation

For each fixed control, the perturbed and original concave objectives differ uniformly by at most $\lambda$, because their difference is
$$ \lambda\sum_a\mu_aq_a\in[0,\lambda]. $$
The original-objective gap at a perturbed maximizer is consequently at most $2\lambda$. The accuracy conversion from Section 3 shows that the corresponding magnetizations differ by at most
$$ (24\lambda)^{1/4}. $$
They therefore converge to $M^+$ as $\lambda\downarrow0$.

Passing to the limit proves monotonicity and diminishing returns for the original biases on the entire cube.

5. A polynomial bit-time numerical optimization lemma

The following lemma supplies the finite-precision guarantee needed for equilibrium evaluation.

Lemma. Let
$$ Q=[\rho,1-\rho]^n,\qquad 0<\rho\le\frac14, $$
where $\rho$ is rational. Let $F$ be convex and differentiable on a neighborhood of $Q$, with Euclidean Lipschitz constant at most a known rational $H\ge1$. Suppose rational approximations to $F(c)$ and $\nabla F(c)$ at rational $c\in Q$ can be computed in polynomial time in the input and point encoding lengths and the requested number of accuracy bits.

For rational $0<\zeta\le1$, a deterministic algorithm returns rational $c\in Q$ satisfying
$$ F(c)\le\min_QF+2\zeta $$
in polynomial bit time in those input lengths and $\log(1/\zeta)$.

5.1. A ball in a good sublevel set

Let $z^*$ minimize $F$ on $Q$, and let $c_0=\frac12\mathbf1$. Set
$$ t=\frac{\zeta}{8Hn},\qquad z_0=(1-t)z^*+tc_0,\qquad r=\frac{\zeta}{64Hn}=\frac t8. $$
Each coordinate of $z_0$ is at distance at least $t/4$ from the boundary of $Q$. Therefore $B(z_0,r)\subseteq Q$.

The diameter of $Q$ is at most $n$. By Lipschitz continuity,
$$ F(z_0)-F(z^*)\le Htn=\frac{\zeta}{8}. $$
Every point in $B(z_0,r)$ has additional objective increase at most
$$ Hr=\frac{\zeta}{64n}. $$
Thus the convex sublevel set
$$ D=\{z\in Q:F(z)\le F(z^*)+\zeta/2\} $$
contains a Euclidean ball of radius $r$.

5.2. Cuts from approximate gradients

Initialize an ellipsoid centered at $c_0$ with shape matrix $n^2I$. It contains $Q$.

At a current center $c$ outside $Q$, use a violated coordinate constraint to give a central halfspace containing $Q$.

At a center $c\in Q$, compute rational values $\widehat F(c)$ and $d$ satisfying
$$ |\widehat F(c)-F(c)|\le\frac{\zeta}{4},\qquad \|d-\nabla F(c)\|_2\le\frac{\zeta}{8n}. $$
Record this center and its approximate value. If $d=0$, convexity gives
$$ F(c)-F(z^*) \le \nabla F(c)\cdot(c-z^*) \le\frac{\zeta}{8}, $$
so return $c$. Otherwise use the central cut
$$ d\cdot(z-c)\le0. $$

Suppose, for contradiction, that every recorded center has
$$ F(c)>F(z^*)+\zeta. $$
For every $z\in D$, convexity then implies
$$ \nabla F(c)\cdot(z-c) \le F(z)-F(c)<-\frac{\zeta}{2}. $$
The gradient approximation changes this inner product by at most $\zeta/8$, because $\|z-c\|_2\le n$. Hence every such cut contains $D$. Cuts at infeasible centers contain $D$ as well.

We next show that a polynomial number of rationally rounded ellipsoid updates makes this impossible.

5.3. Exact central-cut update

For $n\ge2$, write
$$ E(c,P)=\{z:(z-c)^\mathsf TP^{-1}(z-c)\le1\}, $$
with $P$ positive definite. For a nonzero cut normal $d$, the exact update is
$$ c'=c-\frac{Pd}{(n+1)\sqrt{d^\mathsf TPd}}, $$
$$ P'=\frac{n^2}{n^2-1} \left( P-\frac{2}{n+1} \frac{Pdd^\mathsf TP}{d^\mathsf TPd} \right). $$

These formulas give an ellipsoid containing the retained half. To verify this, transform the original ellipsoid to the unit ball and the cut to its first coordinate being nonpositive. Write a point as $(u,w)$, where
$$ -1\le u\le0,\qquad \|w\|_2^2\le1-u^2. $$
Its squared norm in the updated ellipsoid is at most
$$ \frac{(n+1)^2}{n^2} \left(u+\frac1{n+1}\right)^2 +\frac{n^2-1}{n^2}(1-u^2) = 1+\frac{2(n+1)}{n^2}u(u+1) \le1. $$

The volume ratio is
$$ \gamma_n= \left(\frac{n^2}{n^2-1}\right)^{n/2} \sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}. $$
Expanding the logarithms gives
$$ \log\gamma_n = -\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2k(2k-1)n^{2k-1}} \le-\frac1{2n}. $$
Also, Cauchy–Schwarz in the $P$ inner product gives
$$ \frac{n^2}{(n+1)^2}P \preceq P' \preceq\frac{n^2}{n^2-1}P. $$

5.4. Rational rounding with controlled inflation

Choose an integer $q$ with $2^q\ge n/r$, and set
$$ T=8n^2(q+1),\qquad \theta=\frac1{100n^2},\qquad \ell=4^{-(T+1)}. $$
Choose a fixed dyadic mesh $h$ satisfying
$$ h\le\frac{\theta\ell}{8n}. $$

At each update, approximate and round $c'$ to a rational mesh point $\widetilde c$ so that
$$ \|\widetilde c-c'\|_2\le\theta\sqrt\ell. $$
Round $P'$ symmetrically to a mesh matrix $D'$ so that
$$ \|D'-P'\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\frac{\theta\ell}{2}. $$
The chosen mesh permits both bounds. Replace the shape matrix by
$$ \widetilde P =(1+\theta)^2 \left(D'+\frac{\theta\ell}{2}I\right). $$

The exact-update spectral bounds and induction show that, during these $T$ updates, every $P'$ satisfies $P'\succeq\ell I$. Indeed, the minimum eigenvalue decreases at each exact update by a factor no smaller than $4/9$, and rounding as defined above does not decrease it.

Let
$$ S=D'+\frac{\theta\ell}{2}I. $$
The rounding bounds give
$$ P'\preceq S\preceq P'+\theta\ell I \preceq(1+\theta)P'. $$
For a point $z\in E(c',P')$, the triangle inequality in the $S^{-1}$ norm gives
$$ \|z-\widetilde c\|_{S^{-1}} \le1+\frac{\|\widetilde c-c'\|_2}{\sqrt\ell} \le1+\theta. $$
Therefore
$$ E(c',P')\subseteq E(\widetilde c,\widetilde P). $$
Moreover,
$$ P'\preceq\widetilde P\preceq(1+\theta)^3P'. $$
Thus rounding inflates volume by at most $(1+\theta)^{3n/2}$, and each rounded update decreases volume by a factor at most
$$ \exp\left(-\frac1{2n}+\frac{3n\theta}{2}\right) \le\exp\left(-\frac1{4n}\right). $$

After $T$ updates, the volume is less than that of a radius-$r$ ball, since
$$ T>4n^2\log(n/r). $$
Under the contradiction assumption, every ellipsoid contains $D$, which contains such a ball. This is impossible.

Hence some recorded center has objective gap at most $\zeta$. Returning the recorded center with smallest approximate value gives true gap at most
$$ \zeta+2\left(\frac{\zeta}{4}\right)\le2\zeta. $$

For $n=1$, use rational intervals and midpoint cuts instead. Each update halves the interval length, and the same preserved-sublevel argument applies.

5.5. Bit complexity

Here $q$ and $T$ are polynomial in $n$, the encoding length of $H$, and $\log(1/\zeta)$. The quantities $\ell$ and $h$ therefore have polynomial encoding lengths.

The largest shape eigenvalue grows by a factor less than two per rounded update. Hence it is at most $n^22^T$. Each center displacement is bounded by the square root of the preceding largest eigenvalue, plus the rounding error. All center coordinates consequently have polynomially many magnitude bits. Rounding bounds their denominator lengths as well.

Each rounded shape matrix has polynomial encoding length. Computing the next rational $P'$ temporarily uses only polynomially many bits, followed by rounding back to the fixed mesh. The only irrational operation in the center update is a square root of a positive rational. Since that rational has polynomial encoding length, it can be approximated to the required mesh accuracy by bisection in polynomial bit time.

All oracle queries have polynomial encoding length and request polynomially many accuracy bits. Rational arithmetic, rounding, and comparisons likewise take polynomial time. This proves the lemma.

6. Polynomial-time evaluation of $M^+$

We apply the lemma to the convex minimization objective
$$ F=-G_x. $$

6.1. Removing endpoint singularities

Let $0<\rho\le1/4$. Clip every unpinned $z$ coordinate to $[\rho,1-\rho]$. Each square root changes by at most $\sqrt\rho$.

For $r,r'\in[0,1]$,
$$ |s(r)-s(r')|\le2\sqrt{|r-r'|}. $$
To verify this, put $d=|r-r'|$. Since $|s'(r)|=\operatorname{arctanh}r$ is increasing, the largest entropy change over an interval of length $d$ occurs at the right endpoint and equals $s(1-d)$. With $t=d/2$, this is
$$ -t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t). $$
The inequalities
$$ -(1-t)\log(1-t)\le t,\qquad t\log(1/t)\le\frac2e\sqrt t $$
give the claimed bound. The second inequality follows by maximizing $u e^{-u/2}$ for $u=\log(1/t)$.

Thus clipping decreases $G_x$ by at most
$$ (\kappa+\beta)\sqrt\rho+2\rho^{1/4} \le(\kappa+\beta+2)\rho^{1/4}. $$
For a desired full-objective gap $\Delta$, choose dyadic $\rho$ satisfying
$$ \rho\le\frac14,\qquad \rho\le \left(\frac{\Delta}{2(\kappa+\beta+2)}\right)^4. $$
The loss from clipping is then at most $\Delta/2$, and $\rho$ has polynomial encoding length in $L+\log(1/\Delta)$.

6.2. Lipschitz and oracle bounds

Drop fully pinned coordinates, and let $n$ be the remaining dimension. If $n=0$, the magnetization is exactly one.

For a remaining coordinate,
$$ \frac{\partial G_x}{\partial z_a} = \frac{w_a}{2\sqrt{z_a}} \left[ b_a+\sum_b\mu_bK_{ab}q_b -\operatorname{arctanh}\sqrt{z_a} \right]. $$
Let
$$ U=\max_a\left(b_a+\sum_b\mu_bK_{ab}\right). $$
On the truncated box, $\operatorname{arctanh}\sqrt{z_a}\le\log(4/\rho)$. A valid rational Euclidean Lipschitz bound is therefore
$$ H=\frac{n(U+4/\rho)}{\rho}. $$
Its encoding length is polynomial.

Function values and gradients can be approximated in polynomial bit time. Square roots are computed by bisection. For logarithms of positive rationals, normalize the argument as $2^kt$ with $1\le t\le2$ and use
$$ \log t = 2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \frac{u^{2j+1}}{2j+1}, \qquad u=\frac{t-1}{t+1}\in[0,1/3]. $$
The tail decreases geometrically; the same series computes $\log2$. The arguments in our objective and gradient are bounded away from their singularities by quantities with polynomial encoding length. Thus polynomially many guard bits suffice for all products, divisions, and sums.

The numerical lemma with $\zeta=\Delta/4$ returns a rational point whose truncated objective gap is at most $\Delta/2$. Including clipping loss, its full gap is at most $\Delta$.

6.3. Magnetization accuracy

Given rational $0<\eta<1$, take
$$ \Delta=\frac{\eta^4}{192}. $$
The accuracy conversion in Section 3 gives magnetization error at most
$$ (12\Delta)^{1/4}=\frac{\eta}{2}. $$
Compute the remaining square roots and weighted sum to error at most $\eta/2$, then clamp the rational answer to $[0,1]$.

This produces a deterministic rational oracle satisfying
$$ 0\le\widehat M(x)\le1,\qquad |\widehat M(x)-M^+(x)|\le\eta, $$
in polynomial time in the input and control encoding lengths and $\log(1/\eta)$. Fix all rounding and tie choices, so repeated evaluations at the same control return the same value.

7. A polynomial-size submodular knapsack reduction

If $B=0$, positivity of the masses makes $x=0$ the only feasible control, so return it. Suppose henceforth that $B>0$.

The uniform control $x_a=B$ is feasible. Since $q_a^+(x)\ge x_a$,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\ge B. $$

Choose $p\ge1$ and $\delta=2^{-p}$ satisfying
$$ \delta\le\frac{\epsilon B}{8},\qquad \delta\le\frac{(\epsilon B/8)^4}{24C}. $$
Because positive rational $B$ has $\log(1/B)=O(L)$ and $C$ has magnitude at most $2^{O(L)}$, we can choose
$$ p=O(L+\log(1/\epsilon)). $$

Round any control downward by
$$ \bar x_a = \delta\min\{\lfloor x_a/\delta\rfloor,\,2^p-1\}. $$
The rounded control remains feasible, and $\|x-\bar x\|_\infty\le\delta$, including when $x_a=1$. The continuity bound proves
$$ M^+(x)-M^+(\bar x)\le\frac{\epsilon B}{4}. $$

Introduce $N=\tau p$ items, indexed by $(a,j)$ for $0\le j<p$. Item $(a,j)$ contributes $\delta2^j$ to coordinate $a$ and has positive rational cost
$$ c_{a,j}=\mu_a\delta2^j. $$
For a set $S$ of items, define
$$ x_a(S)=\sum_{j:(a,j)\in S}\delta2^j,\qquad f(S)=M^+(x(S)). $$
Every set gives a control in the cube, because the total contribution to a coordinate is $1-\delta$. Binary expansion represents every rounded control exactly.

The function $f$ is nonnegative and monotone. It is submodular because, for $S\subseteq T$ and an item $(a,j)\notin T$, fractional diminishing returns gives
$$ f(S\cup\{(a,j)\})-f(S) \ge f(T\cup\{(a,j)\})-f(T). $$

Let $V$ be the optimal item-set value under budget $B$. Rounding an optimal original control shows
$$ V\ge\mathrm{OPT}-\frac{\epsilon B}{4}. $$
Evaluations on item sets exceeding the budget use the established extension $M^+$, so they require no uniqueness promise outside the feasible region.

8. Exact submodularity from approximate rational evaluations

Set
$$ \eta=\frac{\epsilon B}{8(N^2+1)}. $$
Use Section 6 to define a deterministic rational function $\widehat f$ satisfying
$$ 0\le\widehat f(S)\le1,\qquad |\widehat f(S)-f(S)|\le\eta. $$

Approximate evaluations need not preserve submodularity. Define the corrected rational function
$$ g(S)=\widehat f(S)+2\eta|S|(2N-|S|). $$
We verify that $g$ is exactly nonnegative, monotone, and submodular.

Nonnegativity is immediate. Adding an item changes $\widehat f$ by at least $-2\eta$, while the correction increases by
$$ 2\eta(2N-2|S|-1)\ge2\eta. $$
