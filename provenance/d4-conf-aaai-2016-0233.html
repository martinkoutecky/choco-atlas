The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), per-unit values \(\bar v_t\), fatigue rates \(\delta_t\ge 0\), and feed mass \(\rho\in(0,1]\), choose a measurable schedule \(z:[0,\rho]\to T\) with \(|\{x:z(x)=t\}|\le\mu_t\) to maximize \(W(z)=\int_0^\rho \bar v_{z(x)}\exp(-\int_0^x\delta_{z(y)}\,dy)\,dx\). For every supplied total order \(\pi\) on \(T\), does the best schedule whose nonempty type blocks follow \(\pi\) achieve at least half the unrestricted optimum?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(T\): the list of its \(\tau=|T|\ge1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Each type has binary rational per-unit value \(\bar v_t\ge0\) and finite fatigue rate \(\delta_t\ge0\). The input also includes binary rational capacity \(\rho\in(0,1]\) and a strict total order \(\pi\) on \(T\).
A feasible schedule chooses a length \(\ell\in[0,\rho]\) and a measurable map \(z:[0,\ell]\to T\), with \(\operatorname{Leb}\{x:z(x)=t\}\le\mu_t\) for every type; unused capacity is a vacant suffix.
Its welfare is \(W(z)=\int_0^\ell \bar v_{z(x)}\exp\bigl(-\int_0^x\delta_{z(y)}\,dy\bigr)\,dx\). There is no position-dependent attenuation.
A schedule respects \(\pi\) if, up to null sets, each used type occupies one contiguous interval and these intervals follow \(\pi\); types may be omitted and their masses partially used. Unrestricted schedules may split and revisit types.
Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}_\pi\) be the supremal welfare over unrestricted and order-respecting feasible schedules, respectively. Any running times are measured in \(\tau\) and total input bit length \(L\); this model has no separate ambient parameter \(m\).

Does \(\mathrm{OPT}_\pi\ge\tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}\) hold for every admissible instance and every supplied order \(\pi\), or can this guarantee fail?

For finite ads with nonnegative values and continuation probabilities in \([0,1]\), the best subsequence of any prescribed order attains at least half the unrestricted welfare when position-dependent attenuation is absent; the guarantee also holds for constant position factors. [Farina–Gatti](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07397).
The [later full version](https://www.mit.edu/~gfarina/2017/adopting-cascade-model-truthful-jair17/adopting-cascade-model-truthful-jair17.pdf) retains this guarantee and explicitly permits vacant suffixes. No result settling the exact continuous model above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored the discrete option of leaving a vacant suffix: feed mass is a capacity, so schedules may stop at any length up to rho rather than being required to fill rho.
  • Specified nonnegative binary rational per-unit values and binary rational finite fatigue rates, making the numerical input and its bit length explicit.
  • Removed zero-mass entries and treated T as the positive-mass support.
  • Specified that an order-respecting schedule uses at most one contiguous block per type, permits omission and partial use, and is interpreted up to null sets.
  • Used supremal welfare to avoid assuming that an unrestricted measurable optimum is attained; stated the guarantee as an inequality so zero-welfare instances remain defined.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result

The guarantee holds for every admissible instance and every supplied order:

\[ \mathrm{OPT}_{\pi}\ge \tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}. \]

We prove this directly for measurable schedules, including schedules that split and revisit types. The argument gives a continuous analogue of [Farina–Gatti’s Theorem 1](https://www.mit.edu/~gfarina/2017/adopting-cascade-model-truthful-jair17/adopting-cascade-model-truthful-jair17.pdf).

2. Freeze the amounts used by an arbitrary schedule

Fix a feasible schedule \(z:[0,\ell]\to T\). For each type define its cumulative usage and total usage by

\[ a_t(x)=\int_0^x\mathbf 1_{\{z(y)=t\}}\,dy, \qquad a_t=a_t(\ell). \]

Thus \(0\le a_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_ta_t=\ell\le\rho\).

Define

\[ \Phi_t(u)=\int_0^u e^{-\delta_t s}\,ds = \begin{cases} (1-e^{-\delta_t u})/\delta_t,&\delta_t>0,\\ u,&\delta_t=0, \end{cases} \]

and let

\[ R_t=\bar v_t\Phi_t(a_t), \qquad C_t=e^{-\delta_t a_t}. \]

Here \(R_t\) is the welfare of a contiguous block of type \(t\), of length \(a_t\), when its incoming attenuation equals one. Its continuation factor is \(C_t\). Finiteness of the fatigue rates implies \(0<C_t\le1\), and

\[ 1-C_t=\delta_t\Phi_t(a_t). \]

For a subsequence \(t_1,\ldots,t_k\) of the supplied order \(\pi\), define

\[ V(t_1,\ldots,t_k) = \sum_{i=1}^k R_{t_i}\prod_{j<i}C_{t_j}. \]

Concatenating these blocks gives exactly this welfare. Such a schedule is feasible: its total length is at most \(\ell\), and each type uses at most its original amount \(a_t\). Zero-length blocks can simply be omitted.

Let \(B\) be the maximum of \(V\) over all subsequences of \(\pi\), including the empty subsequence. This maximum exists because there are finitely many subsequences. Consequently,

\[ 0\le B\le\mathrm{OPT}_{\pi}. \]

3. A finite telescoping inequality

We claim that

\[ \sum_{t\in T}\bigl(R_t-B(1-C_t)\bigr)_+\le B, \qquad u_+=\max\{u,0\}. \]

To prove this, put \(g_t=R_t-B(1-C_t)\), and list the types for which \(g_t>0\) in their supplied order as \(t_1,\ldots,t_k\). Set

\[ Q_i=\prod_{j<i}C_{t_j}, \qquad P=\prod_{j=1}^k C_{t_j}>0. \]

Empty products equal one. Since every continuation factor is at most one, \(Q_i\ge P\). The definition of \(B\), followed by telescoping, gives

\[ \begin{aligned} B &\ge V(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\\ &=\sum_{i=1}^k Q_i\bigl(B(1-C_{t_i})+g_{t_i}\bigr)\\ &=B(1-P)+\sum_{i=1}^k Q_i g_{t_i}\\ &\ge B(1-P)+P\sum_{i=1}^k g_{t_i}. \end{aligned} \]

Subtracting \(B(1-P)\) and dividing by the strictly positive \(P\) proves the claim. The same calculation covers the empty subsequence.

4. Bound the original measurable schedule

Write

\[ A(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\delta_t a_t(x), \qquad S(x)=e^{-A(x)}. \]

The functions \(a_t\), \(A\), and \(S\) are absolutely continuous. Almost everywhere,

\[ S'(x)=-\delta_{z(x)}S(x). \]

Therefore,

\[ \begin{aligned} W(z) &=\int_0^\ell \bar v_{z(x)}S(x)\,dx\\ &=B\bigl(1-S(\ell)\bigr) +\sum_{t\in T}(\bar v_t-B\delta_t) \int_{\{x:z(x)=t\}}S(x)\,dx. \end{aligned} \]

We now bound each integral without making any assumption about how often a type is revisited. Nonnegative fatigue rates imply

\[ A(x)\ge\delta_t a_t(x), \qquad S(x)\le e^{-\delta_t a_t(x)}. \]

Moreover, the chain rule for absolutely continuous functions gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_{\{x:z(x)=t\}}e^{-\delta_t a_t(x)}\,dx &=\int_0^\ell e^{-\delta_t a_t(x)}a_t'(x)\,dx\\ &=\Phi_t(a_t). \end{aligned} \]

Dropping terms with negative coefficients in the welfare identity, and applying these bounds to the remaining terms, yields

\[ \begin{aligned} W(z) &\le B\bigl(1-S(\ell)\bigr) +\sum_{t\in T}(\bar v_t-B\delta_t)_+\Phi_t(a_t)\\ &=B\bigl(1-S(\ell)\bigr) +\sum_{t\in T}\bigl(R_t-B(1-C_t)\bigr)_+\\ &\le B\bigl(2-S(\ell)\bigr)\\ &\le 2B\\ &\le 2\mathrm{OPT}_{\pi}. \end{aligned} \]

The second equality uses \(\Phi_t(a_t)\ge0\) and \(1-C_t=\delta_t\Phi_t(a_t)\). The next inequality is precisely the telescoping inequality proved above.

Since \(z\) was an arbitrary feasible measurable schedule, taking the supremum proves

\[ \mathrm{OPT}\le2\mathrm{OPT}_{\pi}. \]

No existence assumption for an unrestricted maximizing schedule is needed. The argument uses at most one block per supported type, hence at most \(\tau\) blocks.
