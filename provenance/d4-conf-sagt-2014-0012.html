The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite item classes \(M\), rational supplies \(\rho\in\Delta(M)\), finite complete valuation types \(T\) with normalized utilities \(u_t:M\to[0,1]\) or unit-sum utilities, a rational society distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), and a rational threshold \(r\), compute the fluid random-priority allocation \(x^{\mathrm{RP}_\infty}\) arising from integral replications \(N\mu_t\) and \(N\rho_j\), compute \(W_{\mathrm{RP}}=\sum_{t,j}x^{\mathrm{RP}_\infty}_{t,j}u_t(j)\) and \(W^*=\max\{\sum_{t,j}x_{t,j}u_t(j):\sum_jx_{t,j}=\mu_t,\ \sum_tx_{t,j}=\rho_j,\ x_{t,j}\ge0\}\), and decide whether \(W_{\mathrm{RP}}/W^*\ge r\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) be finite, with rational supply shares \(\rho\in\Delta(M)\), and let \(T\) be finite, with rational type masses \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\). Each type has rational utilities \(u_t\), either in \([0,1]\) with maximum \(1\), or nonnegative with sum \(1\). Fix a strict ranking consistent with each type’s utilities to break ties.
For each eligible \(N\) with integral \(N\mu_t\) and \(N\rho_j\), uniformly order the \(N\) agents and let each take their highest-ranked available item. Let \(x^{\mathrm{RP}_\infty}_{t,j}\) be the limit of the expected number of type-\(t\) agents receiving item \(j\), divided by \(N\). Set \(W_{\mathrm{RP}}=\sum_{t,j}x^{\mathrm{RP}_\infty}_{t,j}u_t(j)\) and \(W^*=\max\{\sum_{t,j}x_{t,j}u_t(j):\sum_jx_{t,j}=\mu_t,\ \sum_tx_{t,j}=\rho_j,\ x_{t,j}\geq0\}\). Assume \(W^*>0\).
Is computing \(x^{\mathrm{RP}_\infty}\) and deciding, for rational \(r\), whether \(W_{\mathrm{RP}}/W^*\geq r\) possible in polynomial time, or is the threshold decision NP-hard?
For finite assignments, random priority has a \(\Theta(n^{-1/2})\) worst-case welfare ratio, while computing its exact allocation matrix is \(\#\mathrm{P}\)-complete. In replica economies, the continuum random-priority allocation equals the continuum probabilistic-serial allocation.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Both the exact allocation \(x^{\mathrm{RP}_\infty}\) and the welfare-threshold decision are computable in polynomial time in the binary input length. In particular, the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{P}\); NP-hardness under polynomial-time many-one reductions would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Reduction to continuum probabilistic serial

Write \(m=|M|\) and \(h=|T|\). Discard zero-mass types and zero-supply items, restoring their zero rows and columns in the final output. Restrict each fixed strict ranking to the remaining items.

We use the replica-limit equivalence stated in the question: the limiting random-priority allocation is the continuum probabilistic-serial allocation. This is the relevant equivalence established by [Che and Kojima](https://www.columbia.edu/~yc2271/files/publications/psrp-Final.pdf). Its setting applies here: there are finitely many item and preference types, fixed strict rankings, and fixed masses and supplies replicated proportionally. Indeed, if \(N_0\) is the least common multiple of all denominators of \(\mu\) and \(\rho\), the eligible population sizes are exactly the positive multiples of \(N_0\).

In continuum probabilistic serial, every agent consumes at unit speed their highest-ranked remaining item. Thus a type of mass \(\mu_t\) consumes at aggregate speed \(\mu_t\). We compute aggregate masses, matching the normalization in the definition of \(x^{\mathrm{RP}_\infty}\).

2. Exact exhaustion algorithm

Initialize residual supplies \(s_j=\rho_j\), allocations \(x_{t,j}=0\), and elapsed time \(\tau=0\). While some residual supply is positive, perform the following stage.

  • For each type \(t\), let \(b_t\) be its highest-ranked item among those with \(s_j>0\).
  • Set the consumption rate of item \(j\) to

\(q_j=\sum_{\{t:b_t=j\}}\mu_t\).

  • Set the stage duration to

\(\delta=\min_{\{j:q_j>0\}}s_j/q_j\).

  • Update every residual supply by \(s_j\leftarrow s_j-q_j\delta\), every chosen allocation entry by \(x_{t,b_t}\leftarrow x_{t,b_t}+\mu_t\delta\), and time by \(\tau\leftarrow\tau+\delta\).

All comparisons and updates use exact rational arithmetic. Simultaneously exhausted items are all removed before the next stage.

We now prove correctness and termination.

At every stage, each positive-mass type chooses exactly one available item. Consequently, \(\sum_jq_j=\sum_t\mu_t=1\). In particular, a positive consumption rate exists. Moreover, \(q_j>0\) implies \(s_j>0\), so \(\delta>0\).

The choice of \(\delta\) makes all updated supplies nonnegative and exhausts at least one previously available item. Hence there are at most \(m\) stages.

For any time strictly before the end of a stage, none of its initially available items has been exhausted. Therefore each type continues to prefer the same item throughout that stage. The stated rates and allocation increments consequently agree exactly with continuum probabilistic serial. At the endpoint, removing all exhausted items and recomputing choices gives its next stage. Induction establishes agreement throughout the algorithm.

To verify the time horizon, initially \(\sum_js_j=1-\tau=1\). Each stage decreases total residual supply by \(\delta\sum_jq_j=\delta\), so the invariant \(\sum_js_j=1-\tau\) holds. Nonnegative residual supplies imply \(\tau\leq1\). At termination all residual supplies vanish, so \(\tau=1\).

Every type therefore consumes for total duration one, giving \(\sum_jx_{t,j}=\mu_t\). Every supply is exhausted, giving \(\sum_tx_{t,j}=\rho_j\). The output is exactly continuum probabilistic serial and, by the given equivalence, exactly \(x^{\mathrm{RP}_\infty}\).

3. Polynomial bit complexity

A polynomial number of stages alone does not establish polynomial running time: intermediate rational numbers must also have polynomial encoding length. We prove that explicitly.

Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. Choose \(Q\) as the product of the denominators of the type masses and \(R\) as the product of the denominators of the supply shares. Then all \(Q\mu_t\) and \(R\rho_j\) are integers, and \(\log_2Q+\log_2R=O(L)\).

Every stage rate has the form \(q_j=a_j/Q\), where \(a_j\) is an integer with \(0\leq a_j\leq Q\).

We maintain a common, not necessarily reduced, denominator \(D\) for all residual supplies, allocation entries, and elapsed time. Initially \(D=R\) suffices. Suppose a stage begins with this property. Write \(s_j=b_j/D\), and choose an item \(j_*\) attaining the minimum defining \(\delta\). Put \(a_*=a_{j_*}>0\) and \(b_*=b_{j_*}\). Then
\(\delta=Qb_*/(Da_*)\).

The updated residual supply of any item \(j\) is
\(s'_j=(b_ja_*-a_jb_*)/(Da_*)\).

Writing \(c_t=Q\mu_t\), the allocation increment for type \(t\) is
\(\mu_t\delta=c_tb_*/(Da_*)\).

The old entries and elapsed time can also be expressed over \(Da_*\), and the time increment has that denominator. Thus \(D'=Da_*\) is a valid new common denominator.

After \(k\leq m\) stages, we consequently have
\(D_k=R\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}a_*^{(\ell)}\), and hence
\(\log_2D_k\leq\log_2R+k\log_2Q=O((m+1)L)\).

All stored supplies, allocations, and elapsed times lie in \([0,1]\), so their numerators over this common denominator have the same polynomial bit bound. The products used for updates and rational comparisons also have polynomial bit length.

A direct implementation finds all current choices using \(O(hm)\) ranking inspections per stage and performs polynomially many arithmetic operations. There are at most \(m\) stages, and every operation involves polynomial-length integers. This proves exact polynomial-time computation of \(x^{\mathrm{RP}_\infty}\). No population of size \(N_0\) needs to be constructed.

4. Welfare optimization and exact threshold comparison

Once the allocation is known, compute
\(W_{\mathrm{RP}}=\sum_{t,j}x^{\mathrm{RP}_\infty}_{t,j}u_t(j)\)
by exact rational arithmetic. There are \(hm\) summands of polynomial encoding length, so this computation and its result have polynomial bit complexity.

The optimization defining \(W^*\) is a rational linear program with \(hm\) variables, \(h+m\) equality constraints, and nonnegativity constraints.

Its feasible set is nonempty: \(x_{t,j}=\mu_t\rho_j\) satisfies both sets of marginals. It is bounded, since every feasible entry satisfies \(0\leq x_{t,j}\leq\mu_t\leq1\). Its coefficients have polynomial encoding length because they are input rationals or integers of constant size.

The polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore applies: a finite rational optimum can be computed exactly in polynomial time in the encoding length. In particular, we can compute \(W^*\) exactly.

By the assumed strict positivity of \(W^*\), the requested condition is equivalent to
\(W_{\mathrm{RP}}-rW^*\geq0\).
This is an exact rational comparison of polynomial-length numbers, including when equality holds.

The argument applies to both utility normalizations in the question. Finite-population counting hardness does not obstruct this algorithm: the replica limit is computed through its continuum characterization, without computing finite random-priority probabilities.
