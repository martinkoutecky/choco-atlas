The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite applicant and position type sets with rational mass vectors \(\mu\) and \(\nu\), public utilities, independent symmetric mean-zero shock laws, and parameters \(B\) and \(\delta\), can one compute a finite description of an adaptive measurable interview policy and matching rule that uses at most \(B\) interviews per agent almost everywhere and, with probability at least \(1-\delta\), yields a measure-preserving bijection supported on interviewed pairs whose blocking-pair set has product measure zero?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Applicants and positions are two copies \(A,P\) of the unit interval with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda_A,\lambda_P\), partitioned into type intervals of masses \(\mu,\nu\), respectively; each mass vector sums to \(1\). A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Let \(m\) be the position support size, \(\tau\) the combined support size, and \(L\) the total binary input length.
Public values \(u_s,v_t\in\mathbb Q\) depend only on applicant type \(s\) and position type \(t\). Two explicitly listed finite-support rational probability laws \(F_A,F_P\) are symmetric about zero. For every pair \((a,p)\), shocks \(\varepsilon^A_{ap}\sim F_A\) and \(\varepsilon^P_{pa}\sim F_P\) are mutually independent across pairs and sides, on the canonical product probability space, and initially hidden.
An interview reveals both shocks. Observed utilities are \(V(a,p)=v_{t(p)}+\varepsilon^A_{ap}\) and \(U(p,a)=u_{s(a)}+\varepsilon^P_{pa}\) on interviewed pairs, and the corresponding public values otherwise. All partners are acceptable; ties do not constitute strict improvements.
A finite policy description is a rational-constant real-arithmetic program with comparisons and access to previously revealed interview histories. It specifies successive parallel interview rounds as measurable partial bijections, with measurable inverses, and eventually a matching map; it terminates after finitely many rounds almost surely. Unrevealed shocks cannot be queried. Policy outputs and the success event must be measurable; joint measurability of the latent shock field in agents and randomness is not assumed.
Given binary \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}\) and rational \(0<\delta<1\), a feasible policy uses at most \(B\) distinct interviews per agent almost everywhere, almost surely. With probability at least \(1-\delta\), its output \(M:A\to P\) is a measure-preserving bijection modulo null sets, lies on interviewed pairs almost everywhere, and its measurable blocking set \(\{(a,p):V(a,p)>V(a,M(a)),\ U(p,a)>U(p,M^{-1}(p))\}\) has \((\lambda_A\otimes\lambda_P)\)-measure zero.

Is deciding whether such a policy exists, and producing a finite description when feasible, polynomial-time solvable, or is its feasibility problem NP-hard? Express running times in \(m,\tau,L,B,\delta\), with polynomial time measured in the total binary input length.

For finite balanced markets, an adaptive polynomial-time algorithm finds an interim-stable matching and uses \(O(\log^2 n)\) interviews per agent with high probability ([Ashlagi–Chen–Roghani–Saberi](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol325-itcs2025/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.12/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.12.pdf)).
Independent random matching in continuum populations has been constructed on specially enlarged agent–probability spaces ([Duffie–Sun](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703034)); that result does not address this interview-policy feasibility problem. No result settling the problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized both sides to mass one and represented individual agents by atomless unit intervals partitioned according to their type masses.
  • Made public utilities rational, with arbitrary signs, and preserved their dependence solely on the prospective partner's type.
  • Encoded shock laws by finite lists of rational outcomes and probabilities; shocks are identically distributed within each side and independent across pairs and sides.
  • Used the canonical product probability space for latent shocks, requiring measurable policy outputs and success events without imposing joint measurability of the entire latent field.
  • Specified finite descriptions as rational-constant real-arithmetic programs operating in parallel interview rounds, with access only to revealed histories and almost-sure finite termination.
  • Interpreted interviews as individual encounters counted by distinct partners, rather than interviewed mass; imposed the budget almost everywhere almost surely.
  • Required a complete bijection modulo null sets, retained universal acceptability, and used strict improvements on both sides for blocking.
  • Set B to a positive binary integer and delta to a rational strictly between zero and one; added infeasibility detection to make the computational question precise.

A polynomial-time classification on the stated canonical product space

The feasibility problem is polynomial-time solvable. Every feasible instance admits a deterministic policy using exactly one interview per agent and succeeding with probability \(1\).

This classification depends essentially on the requirement that the success event be measurable on the canonical product probability space. In particular, if both shock laws are nondegenerate, no feasible policy exists, regardless of the finite interview budget.

1. Definitions and exact feasibility criterion

Let \(K_A,K_P\) be the supports of the two shock laws, retaining only atoms of positive probability. Define

\[ c_A=-\min K_A,\qquad c_P=-\min K_P. \]

By symmetry, these numbers are nonnegative. Moreover, \(c_A=0\) exactly when \(F_A\) is concentrated at zero, and likewise for \(c_P\).

Merge applicant types having equal public values. Write the resulting distinct values and aggregate masses as

\[ \alpha_1>\cdots>\alpha_k,\qquad a_1,\ldots,a_k. \]

Similarly, merge position types having equal public values:

\[ \beta_1>\cdots>\beta_\ell,\qquad b_1,\ldots,b_\ell. \]

Define cumulative masses

\[ A_i=\sum_{r=1}^{i}a_r,\qquad P_j=\sum_{r=1}^{j}b_r. \]

Theorem. Under the assumptions in the question:

  • If \(c_A>0\) and \(c_P>0\), the instance is infeasible.
  • If \(c_A=c_P=0\), the instance is feasible.
  • If \(c_A>0\) and \(c_P=0\), the instance is feasible exactly when, for every \(i<k\), there is a \(j<\ell\) satisfying

\[ A_i=P_j,\qquad \beta_j-\beta_{j+1}\geq c_A. \]

  • If \(c_A=0\) and \(c_P>0\), the instance is feasible exactly when, for every \(j<\ell\), there is an \(i<k\) satisfying

\[ P_j=A_i,\qquad \alpha_i-\alpha_{i+1}\geq c_P. \]

A condition indexed by an empty set is vacuous. Thus, for example, if positions have no shocks and all applicants have the same public value, feasibility always holds.

We prove the theorem below.

2. A countable-coordinate lemma

Let \(I\) index all shock coordinates, including both sides, and let \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\) be their canonical product probability space.

Lemma. Every event in the product sigma-algebra depends on only countably many coordinates. Every positive-probability event in its completion contains a positive-probability product-measurable event depending on only countably many coordinates.

Here, an event \(C\) “depends on” \(J\subseteq I\) if membership in \(C\) is unchanged whenever all coordinates in \(J\) are unchanged.

Proof. Let \(\mathcal D\) consist of events depending on some countable coordinate set. This collection contains every finite-coordinate cylinder. It is closed under complements and countable unions: for a countable union, take the union of the countable determining sets. Therefore \(\mathcal D\) contains the sigma-algebra generated by the cylinders.

For the completion statement, let \(E\) have positive probability. There are product-measurable events \(E_0,N\), with \(\mathbb P(N)=0\), such that \(E\triangle E_0\subseteq N\). Then

\[ C=E_0\setminus N\subseteq E,\qquad \mathbb P(C)=\mathbb P(E)>0. \]

Both \(E_0\) and \(N\) depend on countably many coordinates, so \(C\) does too. \(\square\)

Consequently, if a policy has a measurable success event of positive probability, we can find a successful realization in which all but countably many shock coordinates take their minimum support values.

To justify this carefully, intersect the success event with measurable probability-one sets on which the required almost-sure termination and policy properties hold. Apply the lemma to obtain a positive-probability event \(C\) determined by a countable set \(J\). Choose any \(\omega_0\in C\), keep its coordinates on \(J\), and set every other applicant-side coordinate to \(-c_A\) and every other position-side coordinate to \(-c_P\). The resulting realization \(\omega_*\) still belongs to \(C\).

We do not assert that this adverse realization has positive probability. Its membership in \(C\) follows from countable-coordinate dependence.

Only countably many agents are endpoints of coordinates in \(J\). Hence, in this realization, almost every matched applicant receives shock \(-c_A\), and almost every matched position receives shock \(-c_P\).

3. Interviewed pairs have product measure zero

Fix a realization at which the policy terminates after finitely many rounds.

Each interview round is the graph of a measurable partial bijection. Its graph is measurable, and each vertical section contains at most one point. Fubini’s theorem therefore gives product measure zero. The finite union \(G\) of all interview graphs also has product measure zero.

Thus, outside \(G\), utilities are the public values. This observation applies even when the interview maps were chosen adaptively.

For a successful matching, work on conull sets where the matching and its inverse are genuine measurable bijections. Discarding the additional countable exceptional agents from Section 2 does not change any type-flow masses.

4. Both nondegenerate laws imply infeasibility

Suppose \(c_A,c_P>0\) and a feasible policy exists. Since \(1-\delta>0\), Section 2 supplies a successful realization \(\omega_*\) with minimum shocks outside countably many coordinates.

For original types \(s,t\), let \(x_{st}\) be the mass of applicants of type \(s\) matched to positions of type \(t\). Measure preservation implies that this is also the mass of positions of type \(t\) matched to applicants of type \(s\). Since the masses sum to \(1\), some \(x_{st}>0\).

Let \(S\) be these applicants and \(T\) these positions, after removing the null exceptional sets. Their matched utilities are

\[ V(a,M(a))=v_t-c_A\quad(a\in S), \qquad U(p,M^{-1}(p))=u_s-c_P\quad(p\in T). \]

For every \((a,p)\in(S\times T)\setminus G\), the pair is uninterviewed, so

\[ V(a,p)=v_t>v_t-c_A,\qquad U(p,a)=u_s>u_s-c_P. \]

Every such pair blocks. Because \(G\) has product measure zero, these blocking pairs have measure \(x_{st}^{\,2}>0\), contradicting success.

Therefore no feasible policy exists when both laws are nondegenerate.

5. One nondegenerate law: necessity

Assume \(c_A>0\) and \(c_P=0\). Again use the successful adverse realization \(\omega_*\).

Let \(x_{ij}\) be the matching mass between applicant value group \(\alpha_i\) and position value group \(\beta_j\). Then

\[ \sum_jx_{ij}=a_i,\qquad \sum_ix_{ij}=b_j. \]

Suppose \(i<i'\), \(x_{ij}>0\), and \(x_{i'j'}>0\). An applicant in the first flow has matched utility \(\beta_j-c_A\). A position in the second flow is matched to public applicant value \(\alpha_{i'}\).

On the positive-measure rectangle between these populations, almost every pair is uninterviewed. Such a position strictly prefers the applicant because \(\alpha_i>\alpha_{i'}\). Consequently, zero-measure blocking requires

\[ \beta_j-c_A\geq\beta_{j'}. \]

Thus every pair of positive flows with \(i<i'\) satisfies

\[ \beta_j-\beta_{j'}\geq c_A>0. \]

In particular, \(j<j'\).

This has three consequences:

  • A position value group cannot be split between distinct applicant value groups.
  • The applicant group owning position group \(j\) is nondecreasing in \(j\).
  • Every applicant group owns a nonempty consecutive block of position groups, because every row and column has positive total mass.

Let \(h_i\) be the last position group assigned to applicant group \(i\). The first \(i\) applicant groups then receive exactly the first \(h_i\) position groups, giving

\[ A_i=P_{h_i}. \]

Apply the positive-flow inequality to groups \(h_i\) and \(h_i+1\), owned by applicant groups \(i\) and \(i+1\). It gives

\[ \beta_{h_i}-\beta_{h_i+1}\geq c_A. \]

These are precisely the conditions in part 3 of the theorem.

Exchanging applicants and positions proves the necessity of part 4.

6. Construction and sufficiency

A finite-description sorting map

Sort the original applicant type intervals by decreasing public applicant value, placing equal-value types consecutively. Map them measure-preservingly onto consecutive rank intervals of their respective masses. Do the same for positions.

Denote these rank maps by \(R_A,R_P\), and define

\[ M=R_P^{-1}\circ R_A. \]

Away from interval endpoints, these maps are translations between rational intervals. They have finite descriptions using rational arithmetic and comparisons.

For completeness, endpoints can also be handled by finite programs without sacrificing exact type preservation. Each positive-length interval can be mapped bijectively and measure-preservingly onto a half-open rank interval. After translating its interior to \((0,h)\), the only nontrivial endpoint repairs are:

  • To map \((0,h)\) onto \([0,h)\), put \(z_n=h/2^{n+1}\), send \(z_0\) to \(0\), send \(z_{n+1}\) to \(z_n\), and fix all other points.
  • To map \([0,h]\) onto \([0,h)\), send \(h\) to \(z_0\), send \(z_n\) to \(z_{n+1}\), and fix all other points.
  • A right-closed, left-open interval is handled by reflection.

These are measurable bijections with measurable inverses and preserve measure because they change a translation or reflection only on countably many points. Membership in the sequence is recognized by repeatedly doubling a positive coordinate until it reaches or exceeds \(h/2\); this terminates for every positive input. The inverse maps have analogous finite programs.

Consequently, \(M\) and its inverse have finite rational-constant descriptions and preserve types under the sorted rank allocation.

The policy performs one interview round along \(M\), ignores the revealed shocks, and outputs \(M\).

Both laws concentrated at zero

Suppose an off-matching pair \((a,p)\) satisfies

\[ u_{s(a)}>u_{s(M^{-1}(p))}. \]

The sorted applicant ranks then put \(a\) before \(M^{-1}(p)\). Since position values decrease with position rank,

\[ v_{t(M(a))}\geq v_{t(p)}. \]

The applicant therefore does not strictly prefer \(p\). No off-matching pair blocks, and a matched pair cannot strictly improve upon itself. Thus the policy succeeds for every realization.

Applicant shocks only

Assume the criterion in part 3 holds. Its cumulative equalities assign each applicant value group a consecutive block of whole position value groups.

Consider an off-matching pair for which the position strictly prefers the applicant. Since position shocks are identically zero, the applicant belongs to a strictly higher applicant value group than the position’s matched applicant.

Across the intervening boundary, the criterion implies

\[ v_{t(M(a))}-v_{t(p)}\geq c_A. \]

Every applicant shock is at least \(-c_A\), so

\[ V(a,M(a)) \geq v_{t(M(a))}-c_A \geq v_{t(p)} =V(a,p). \]

The applicant does not strictly prefer the position. Hence there are no blocking pairs, for any realization.

The position-shocks-only case follows by exchanging the sides.

In every feasible case, the blocking set is empty. Therefore its measurability does not require joint measurability of the revealed shock field. All policy outputs are deterministic and measurable, and the success event is the whole probability space.

This completes the theorem.

7. Decision algorithm and binary running time

The algorithm is:

  • Find the minimum support atom of each shock law.
  • Sort and aggregate equal public values on each side.
  • Apply the appropriate case of the theorem.
  • When feasible, output the sorting policy from Section 6.

The cumulative-mass tests use a merge scan of the two increasing cumulative lists. All equalities and gap comparisons are exact rational operations.

There are at most \(\tau\) value groups in total and at most \(m\) position value groups. Sorting takes \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational comparisons; the subsequent scans and construction take \(O(\tau)\) rational operations, apart from reading the shock laws.

Let \(N\) denote the total binary input length, including \(B\) and \(\delta\). Using elementary integer arithmetic, a conservative bit-complexity bound for deciding feasibility and producing the policy is

\[ O\!\left(N^3+\tau\log(\tau+1)N^2\right). \]

The policy description has size \(O(\tau N)\). Intermediate rational constants have \(O(N)\) bits: cumulative masses can be represented using a common denominator whose bit length is bounded by the total denominator input length.

If \(L\) already denotes the entire input length, take \(N=L\). Otherwise, include the binary encoding lengths of \(B\) and \(\delta\) in \(N\).

There is no numerical dependence on \(B\) or \(\delta\) beyond reading their encodings: the stipulated \(B\geq1\) allows the one-round construction, and \(\delta<1\) forces positive success probability for the necessity argument.

8. Scope of the conclusion

The countable-coordinate argument is specific to the stated canonical product space and measurable success event. It does not use a continuum law of large numbers.

The cited [finite-market interview algorithm](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol325-itcs2025/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.12/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.12.pdf) concerns finitely many agents, where a measurable event can depend on every shock coordinate. [Duffie–Sun](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703034) construct independent continuum matching using enlarged measure spaces. Neither setting supplies the countable-coordinate restriction used here in the same form.
