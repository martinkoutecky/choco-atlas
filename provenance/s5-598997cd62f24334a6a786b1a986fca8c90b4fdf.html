Answer: \(\rho^*=3\)

The anchor (named result in the paper)

Under the maximum cost objective, any deterministic strategyproof mechanism has an approximation ratio of at least \(3\). — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite multiset \(A\) of at least two rational site slots and a finite-support rational location distribution \(\mu\) for agents interested in both facilities, determine \(\inf_F\sup_{A,\mu}\mathrm{MC}_{\mu}(F(A,\mu))/\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{MC}}(A,\mu)\), where \(F\) deterministically selects two distinct slots and is group-strategyproof against every positive-mass coalition's location-report changes; is this optimum exactly \(3\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A=(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\) be a public multiset of rational site slots, with \(m\ge2\). A feasible outcome selects two distinct slot indices; their coordinates may coincide. All agents require both facilities, receive no payments, and have cost \(c((y_1,y_2),x)=\max\{|x-y_1|,|x-y_2|\}\).
A society is given by its support: \(\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\delta_{x_i}\), with distinct \(x_i\in\mathbb Q\), positive binary rational masses \(w_i\), and \(\sum_iw_i=1\). Here \(\tau\ge1\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Define \(\mathrm{MC}_{\mu}(y)=\max_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}c(y,x)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{MC}}(A,\mu)=\min_{y\text{ feasible}}\mathrm{MC}_{\mu}(y)\).
A coalition and its reports are represented by a nonnegative, finite-support rational measure \(\gamma\) on \(\mathbb Q^2\), whose first marginal \(\lambda\) satisfies \(\lambda\le\mu\) and \(\lambda(\mathbb Q)>0\); its second marginal \(\nu\) gives the reported locations. Thus portions of a type's mass may split among reports, and the new report distribution is \(\mu'=\mu-\lambda+\nu\).
A deterministic mechanism \(F\), defined on every admissible input, is group-strategyproof if every such deviation has some \(x\in\operatorname{supp}\lambda\) with \(c(F(A,\mu),x)\le c(F(A,\mu'),x)\). This forbids coalitions whose entire participating mass strictly benefits. Tie choices are deterministic parts of \(F\).
Let \(\mathcal G\) contain all such mechanisms, without a running-time restriction, and define \(\rho^*=\inf_{F\in\mathcal G}\sup_{A,\mu}\frac{\mathrm{MC}_{\mu}(F(A,\mu))}{\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{MC}}(A,\mu)}\), using \(0/0=1\) and \(b/0=+\infty\) for \(b>0\).

What is \(\rho^*\); is it exactly \(3\), smaller than \(3\), or larger than \(3\)?

For finite agents requiring both facilities, every deterministic strategyproof mechanism has maximum-cost approximation ratio at least \(3\), and a deterministic group-strategyproof mechanism achieves \(3\). [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.03026)
No result settling this exact rational mass and coalition model was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total mass to one because the input is a distribution; allowed any nonempty support.
  • Interpreted distinct slots as distinct multiset occurrences, allowing coincident facility coordinates when multiplicity permits.
  • Defined maximum cost as the maximum individual cost over positive-mass support, without multiplying costs by masses.
  • Allowed coalitions to take rational portions of types and split them among finitely many rational location reports, preserving participating mass.
  • Used the supplied discrete definition of group strategyproofness: a deviation is prohibited when all participating types strictly benefit.
  • Included zero-optimum instances using ratio conventions 0/0 = 1 and positive/0 = infinity.
  • Optimized over all deterministic group-strategyproof mechanisms, with no added efficiency requirement; deterministic tie choices belong to each mechanism.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result

The exact value is \(\rho^*=3\). The upper bound holds even when participating mass splits among arbitrarily many rational reports.

2. Mechanism and two elementary lemmas

Sort the slot indices by coordinate, breaking coordinate ties by original index. Write the resulting coordinates as \(b_1\le\cdots\le b_m\). Each adjacent pair \(P_k=(b_k,b_{k+1})\) uses two distinct slot indices, even when its coordinates coincide.

Define
\(f_k(x)=c(P_k,x)=\max\{b_{k+1}-x,x-b_k\}\).
For \(t_k=(b_k+b_{k+1})/2\) and \(h_k=(b_{k+1}-b_k)/2\), this becomes
\(f_k(x)=h_k+|x-t_k|\).

For every rational \(x\), let \(K(x)\) be the smallest index minimizing \(f_k(x)\). Define the mechanism
\(F_0(A,\mu)=P_{K(\ell)}\), where \(\ell=\min\operatorname{supp}\mu\).

Thus the mechanism selects the leftmost type’s favorite adjacent pair, with a fixed deterministic tie rule.

Lemma 1: Adjacent pairs suffice for individual minimization.

Consider any feasible pair with sorted slot indices \(p<q\). Since \(b_{p+1}\le b_q\), for every \(x\),
\(c(P_p,x)=\max\{b_{p+1}-x,x-b_p\}\le\max\{b_q-x,x-b_p\}=c((b_p,b_q),x)\).

Consequently, minimizing individual cost over adjacent pairs gives the minimum over all feasible outcomes. In particular, \(F_0(A,\mu)\) minimizes cost for type \(\ell\).

Lemma 2: Ordered cost differences and choices.

For \(j<k\), the function \(f_j(x)-f_k(x)\) is nondecreasing in \(x\).

Indeed, \(t_j\le t_k\), and
\(f_j(x)-f_k(x)=h_j-h_k+|x-t_j|-|x-t_k|\).
The last two terms have difference \(t_j-t_k\) when \(x\le t_j\), difference \(2x-t_j-t_k\) when \(t_j\le x\le t_k\), and difference \(t_k-t_j\) when \(x\ge t_k\). These expressions join continuously and are nondecreasing.

It follows that \(K(u)\le K(v)\) whenever \(u<v\). To verify this implication, suppose instead that \(j=K(u)>K(v)=k\). Set \(D(x)=f_k(x)-f_j(x)\), which is nondecreasing by the preceding argument. Optimality at \(u\) and \(v\) gives \(D(u)\ge0\) and \(D(v)\le0\). Hence \(D(u)=D(v)=0\). But at \(u\), the smaller index \(k\) then also minimizes cost, contradicting the tie rule defining \(K(u)=j\).

Both lemmas allow repeated slot coordinates.

3. Group-strategyproofness for splitting coalitions

Fix an admissible input and an allowed deviation \(\gamma\), with first marginal \(\lambda\), second marginal \(\nu\), and resulting distribution \(\mu'=\mu-\lambda+\nu\).

This distribution is admissible: it is nonnegative, rational, and finitely supported, and its total mass is \(1\), because \(\nu(\mathbb Q)=\lambda(\mathbb Q)\). Write \(\ell'=\min\operatorname{supp}\mu'\), \(k=K(\ell)\), and \(j=K(\ell')\).

If \(\lambda(\{\ell\})>0\), then \(\ell\) participates. Lemma 1 shows that the original outcome minimizes this type’s cost over every feasible outcome. Therefore
\(c(F_0(A,\mu),\ell)\le c(F_0(A,\mu'),\ell)\),
as required.

Suppose instead that \(\lambda(\{\ell\})=0\). The original positive mass at \(\ell\) remains in \(\mu'\), so \(\ell'\le\ell\).

If \(\ell'=\ell\), the outcome is unchanged. If \(\ell'<\ell\), Lemma 2 gives \(j\le k\). For \(j=k\), the outcome is again unchanged. For \(j<k\), optimality at \(\ell\) gives
\(f_j(\ell)-f_k(\ell)\ge0\).
The difference is nondecreasing by Lemma 2. Hence, for every original type \(x\ge\ell\),
\(f_j(x)-f_k(x)\ge f_j(\ell)-f_k(\ell)\ge0\).

Since \(\lambda\le\mu\), every participating type is an original type. Thus every participating type has cost after the deviation at least its original cost. The coalition is nonempty, so the required witness exists.

This proves \(F_0\in\mathcal G\), including deviations that remove only part of a type’s mass or split it among several reports.

4. Approximation upper bound

Let \(r=\max\operatorname{supp}\mu\), and let \(y^*\) be an optimal feasible outcome. Put \(R=\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{MC}}(A,\mu)\).

Both extreme types have cost at most \(R\) under \(y^*\). For either facility coordinate \(z\) in \(y^*\), the triangle inequality gives
\(r-\ell\le |r-z|+|z-\ell|\le2R\).

By Lemma 1,
\(c(F_0(A,\mu),\ell)\le c(y^*,\ell)\le R\).

For every supported type \(x\), applying the triangle inequality to each selected facility gives
\(c(F_0(A,\mu),x)\le c(F_0(A,\mu),\ell)+|x-\ell|\le R+(r-\ell)\le3R\).

Therefore \(\mathrm{MC}_\mu(F_0(A,\mu))\le3\,\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{MC}}(A,\mu)\). If \(R=0\), this inequality forces the mechanism’s maximum cost to be zero, so the stipulated ratio is \(1\). Thus \(\rho^*\le3\).

5. Matching lower bound

The following rational construction adapts Appendix A.6 of [Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.03026).

Fix \(F\in\mathcal G\), rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), and
\(A=(-1,-1,1,1)\), \(\mu=\tfrac12\delta_{-\varepsilon}+\tfrac12\delta_\varepsilon\).
Choose \(s\in\{-1,1\}\) occurring in \(F(A,\mu)\). Type \(x=-s\varepsilon\) has cost \(1+\varepsilon\).

Let this type’s entire mass report \(z=-s(2+\varepsilon)\), using
\(\gamma=\tfrac12\delta_{(x,z)}\).
The resulting distribution is
\(\mu'=\tfrac12\delta_z+\tfrac12\delta_{s\varepsilon}\).

Selecting both slots at \(-s\) would give participating type \(x\) cost \(1-\varepsilon\). Group-strategyproofness therefore forbids that outcome at \(\mu'\). Every other feasible outcome contains coordinate \(s\), giving type \(z\) cost \(3+\varepsilon\). Hence the mechanism’s maximum cost is \(3+\varepsilon\).

Both slots at \(-s\) give maximum cost \(1+\varepsilon\). This is optimal because the support diameter is \(2+2\varepsilon\). The ratio is consequently \((3+\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon)\). Letting rational \(\varepsilon\) decrease to zero proves that every \(F\in\mathcal G\) has worst-case ratio at least \(3\).

Together with the upper bound, this establishes \(\rho^*=3\).

6. Running time

The achieving mechanism uses \(O(m\log m+\tau)\) exact rational comparisons and arithmetic operations: sort the slots, find the minimum support coordinate, and scan the adjacent pairs. All intermediate rational operands have bit length \(O(L)\), giving the conservative bit bound \(O((m\log m+\tau)L^2)\) with elementary integer arithmetic. No expansion of rational masses into individual agents is needed.
