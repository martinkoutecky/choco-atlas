The continuous mirror question, as graded

For each batch size \(m\), let a society be a rational distribution \(\mu\) over types \(p\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\), where \(p\) is the number of positive labels among an agent's \(m\) points. Agents report counts \(r\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\), and a deterministic rule \(F_m\) maps the reported mass vector \(\nu\) to \(c^+\) or \(c^-\). Define \(R_\mu(c^+)=\sum_p\mu_p(m-p)/m\), \(R_\mu(c^-)=\sum_p\mu_p p/m\), and \(r^*(\mu)=\min_c R_\mu(c)\). What is the infimum, over families of rules \((F_m)_m\) that are mass-group-strategyproof for every society, of their worst-case truthful approximation ratio \(\sup_{m,\mu}R_\mu(F_m(\mu))/r^*(\mu)\); is it \(3\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

For each integer \(m\geq1\), types and reports are counts in \(\{0,\ldots,m\}\). A society is a probability distribution \(\mu\), given by the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types and their binary rational masses; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
A deterministic rule \(F_m\) maps rational reported distributions to \(\{c^+,c^-\}\), without payments. Type \(p\) minimizes its true loss \(\ell_p(c^+)=(m-p)/m\), \(\ell_p(c^-)=p/m\); when \(2p=m\), it is indifferent. Outcome ties may be resolved arbitrarily by the rule.
A coalition deviation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(a_{pr}\), with \(b_p=\sum_r a_{pr}\leq\mu_p\), producing \(\nu_r=\mu_r-b_r+\sum_p a_{pr}\). Coalition members may report truthfully, and a type's mass may split among reports.
The rule is mass-group-strategyproof if no such deviation makes every participating type \(p\) with \(b_p>0\) weakly better off and a positive mass strictly better off, comparing \(F_m(\nu)\) with \(F_m(\mu)\) using true losses.
Define \(R_\mu(c)=\sum_p\mu_p\ell_p(c)\) and \(r^*(\mu)=\min_c R_\mu(c)\). Set \(0/0=1\) and \(x/0=+\infty\) for \(x>0\).
What is \(\inf_{(F_m)_m}\sup_{m\geq1,\mu} R_\mu(F_m(\mu))/r^*(\mu)\), where each \(F_m\) is mass-group-strategyproof for every rational society? Is this infimum \(3\)?
For finite societies, no deterministic strategyproof mechanism achieves \(3-\varepsilon\) for any \(\varepsilon>0\), even with two agents holding equally many points; a deterministic strategyproof \(3\)-approximation exists ([source](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2008/AAAI08-020.pdf)).
No result settling the rational mass problem defined here was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the batch-size domain explicit as positive integers and normalized society mass to 1.
  • Defined mass-group-strategyproofness using rational coalition masses that may split among reports, including truthful coalition members, with weak improvement for everyone and strict improvement on positive mass.
  • Retained indifferent types and evaluated incentives by true losses; allowed arbitrary deterministic outcome tie-breaking.
  • Defined the approximation ratio at zero optimal risk by 0/0=1 and positive loss divided by zero equal to infinity.
  • Imposed no computational or continuity restriction on rule families, matching the graded question.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The infimum is \(+\infty\), not \(3\). The decisive feature is that an indifferent type may assist a coalition containing a strict beneficiary who reports truthfully. For comparison, restricting the question to odd \(m\) gives infimum \(3\).

Even \(m\): every admissible rule has unbounded ratio. Fix an even integer \(m\geq2\), put \(t=m/2\), and let \(\delta_p\) denote unit mass at type \(p\). For any rational \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), define
\[ \mu_\epsilon=\epsilon\delta_0+(1-2\epsilon)\delta_t+\epsilon\delta_m, \qquad \nu_\epsilon^-=(1-\epsilon)\delta_0+\epsilon\delta_m, \qquad \nu_\epsilon^+=\epsilon\delta_0+(1-\epsilon)\delta_m. \]
Here and below these expressions mean rational probability distributions.

There are two possible outcomes at \(\mu_\epsilon\).

  • Suppose \(F_m(\mu_\epsilon)=c^+\). Consider the coalition specified by

\(a_{00}=\epsilon\) and \(a_{t0}=1-2\epsilon\), with every other \(a_{pr}=0\).
Thus type \(0\) participates truthfully, and all indifferent mass reports \(0\). The resulting distribution is \(\nu_\epsilon^-\). If its outcome were \(c^-\), participating type \(0\) would reduce its loss from \(1\) to \(0\), while participating type \(t\) would retain loss \(1/2\). This would be a forbidden deviation. Consequently,
\(F_m(\nu_\epsilon^-)=c^+\).
At this society,
\(R_{\nu_\epsilon^-}(c^+)=1-\epsilon\) and
\(r^*(\nu_\epsilon^-)=R_{\nu_\epsilon^-}(c^-)=\epsilon\).
The approximation ratio is therefore \((1-\epsilon)/\epsilon\).

  • Suppose \(F_m(\mu_\epsilon)=c^-\). Use instead

\(a_{mm}=\epsilon\) and \(a_{tm}=1-2\epsilon\), with every other entry zero.
This produces \(\nu_\epsilon^+\). An outcome \(c^+\) would strictly benefit participating type \(m\), while type \(t\) remained indifferent. Hence strategyproofness forces
\(F_m(\nu_\epsilon^+)=c^-\).
Here again the selected risk is \(1-\epsilon\), whereas the optimal risk is \(\epsilon\).

All specified masses are nonnegative rational numbers, and each participating type contributes exactly its available mass. Thus both deviations satisfy the definition.

Taking \(\epsilon=2^{-n}\) for integers \(n\geq2\) proves that every mass-group-strategyproof rule for this fixed even \(m\) has supremum ratio \(+\infty\). The witnessing true society has support size \(\tau=3\), and the society with the large ratio has \(\tau=2\); no mass splitting is required.

In particular, \(m=2\) already forces every admissible family to have infinite worst-case ratio. Constant rules are admissible, so the class of families is nonempty. Therefore
\[ \inf_{(F_m)_m}\sup_{m\geq1,\mu} \frac{R_\mu(F_m(\mu))}{r^*(\mu)} =+\infty. \]

Odd \(m\): the exact fixed-\(m\) optimum. To distinguish the indifference obstruction from the remaining problem, fix odd \(m\), and set
\(k=(m-1)/2\), \(h=(m+1)/2\), and \(\alpha=h/(2m)\).
The optimal worst-case ratio for this fixed \(m\) is
\((3m-1)/(m+1)\).

For the upper bound, let \(P(\mu)=\sum_{p\geq h}\mu_p\), and choose \(c^+\) exactly when \(P(\mu)\geq1/2\).

There are no indifferent types. A beneficial switch from \(c^-\) to \(c^+\) therefore requires every participating type to satisfy \(p\geq h\). For any such deviation,
\[ P(\nu)-P(\mu) =\sum_{p,r}a_{pr} \bigl(\mathbf{1}_{r\geq h}-\mathbf{1}_{p\geq h}\bigr) \leq0. \]
It cannot cross the threshold upwards. For a beneficial switch from \(c^+\) to \(c^-\), every participating type satisfies \(p\leq k\), and the same expression is nonnegative. It cannot cross downwards. This proves mass-group-strategyproofness, including arbitrary splitting.

Write \(x=\sum_p\mu_p p/m\). The two risks are \(1-x\) and \(x\). If the rule chooses \(c^+\), then \(x\geq(h/m)P(\mu)\geq\alpha\). If it chooses \(c^-\), then \(1-x\geq(h/m)(1-P(\mu))>\alpha\). Whenever the selected outcome is suboptimal, its ratio is consequently at most
\[ \frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}=\frac{3m-1}{m+1}. \]
When it is optimal, the ratio is \(1\), including the stipulated zero-risk convention. The rule takes \(O(\tau)\) exact rational operations, or \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations using schoolbook arithmetic.

For the matching lower bound, consider
\[ A=\tfrac12\delta_m+\tfrac12\delta_k,\qquad B=\tfrac12\delta_m+\tfrac12\delta_0,\qquad C=\tfrac12\delta_h+\tfrac12\delta_0. \]
Any admissible rule must satisfy \(F_m(A)=F_m(B)\): otherwise, the negative type at whichever society receives \(c^+\) can report as the other negative type and obtain \(c^-\). Likewise, \(F_m(B)=F_m(C)\), because the changing types \(m\) and \(h\) both strictly prefer \(c^+\).

If their common outcome is \(c^+\), society \(C\) has ratio \((1-\alpha)/\alpha\). If it is \(c^-\), society \(A\) has that ratio, since its mean is \(1-\alpha\). This proves the lower bound. Taking the supremum over odd \(m\) gives \(3\).

Relation to the cited mechanism. The [paper’s Mechanism 1](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2008/AAAI08-020.pdf) assigns local ties to the positive side. Its corresponding mass rule chooses \(c^+\) at \(\mu_\epsilon\) and \(c^-\) at \(\nu_\epsilon^-\). The explicit coalition above therefore violates the present definition. Assigning an indifferent report to a voting side does not remove its true indifference.
