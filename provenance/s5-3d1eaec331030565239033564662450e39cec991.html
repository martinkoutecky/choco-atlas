The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a fixed finite seller-buyer network motif and a finite set of buyer types \(t=(v,R)\), with society described by a mass distribution \(\mu\) over type profiles on the motif and \(k\) items supplied per motif, construct a computable direct mechanism that assigns integral bundles and payments within each motif, is strategy-proof and individually rational, and guarantees normalized aggregate surplus at least \(\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor/k\) of optimum.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix a directed motif with seller \(s\), \(m\geq1\) labelled buyer positions, fixed seller invitations, and \(k\geq1\) identical items per copy. Each buyer has type \(t_i=(v_i,R_i)\), where \(R_i\) is a subset of her outgoing motif neighbors and \(v_{i,1}\geq\cdots\geq v_{i,k}\geq0\) are binary rational marginal values.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) complete motif profiles with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational, summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible profiles; copies have no connections or item transfers between them. Running times are measured in \(m,\tau,k\) and total input bit length \(L\).
A buyer may report any nonnegative, nonincreasing rational valuation vector and any invitation set \(R'_i\subseteq R_i\). A buyer participates exactly when reachable from \(s\) through reported invitations.
A deterministic direct mechanism, defined also on report profiles outside the support, assigns quantities \(q_i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\) with \(\sum_iq_i\leq k\) and signed rational payments \(p_i\) to the seller; unreachable buyers have \(q_i=p_i=0\). Unsold items and subsidies are allowed, with no budget-balance requirement.
Utility is quasilinear: \(\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_i}v_{i,\ell}-p_i\). Require dominant-strategy truthfulness in both valuations and invitations, and ex post individual rationality, for every rational true profile and every admissible report profile of the other buyers.
Define normalized aggregate surplus by \(W(M,\mu)=k^{-1}\sum_{\theta\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}\mu(\theta)\sum_i\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_i^M(\theta)}v_{i,\ell}\). Define \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\) by maximizing surplus separately in each profile over integral allocations to buyers reachable under truthful invitations, with the same normalization; use inequalities directly when \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)=0\).

Does a computable mechanism satisfying these conditions exist for every such motif and society, guaranteeing \(W(M,\mu)\geq(\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor/k)\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\), or is this approximation guarantee unattainable?

For finite buyer networks with decreasing marginal values, GAPG is stated to be strategy-proof and individually rational with approximation guarantee \(\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor/k\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12422).
No result addressing exactly the support-described society of independent motifs above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted society types as complete motif profiles and normalized their positive masses to sum to one.
  • Interpreted the motif as fixing possible directed connections and seller invitations, while buyer invitation sets remain private; copies neither communicate nor pool items.
  • Specified nonnegative decreasing binary rational marginal values and positive integer item supply.
  • Retained the finite support of true profiles but allowed arbitrary rational valuation reports and withholding any subset of invitations; required the mechanism and incentive conditions beyond the support.
  • Interpreted integral assignments as deterministic quantities within each copy, with signed rational payments, subsidies and unsold items permitted, and no budget-balance constraint.
  • Defined normalized surplus per supplied item and the benchmark using truthful reachability and separate integral optimization in each motif; zero optimum is handled by the guarantee inequality.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Such a mechanism exists. In fact, it can achieve \(W(M,\mu)=\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\) for every society, with polynomial-time computation. The proof uses the permitted signed payments and is independent of the cited [GAPG theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12422).

1. Definition of the mechanism

Let \(N=\{1,\ldots,m\}\). For any rational report profile \(\hat\theta=(\hat v,\hat R)\), including profiles outside the support, let \(G(\hat R)\) be the directed graph consisting of the fixed seller invitations and the reported buyer invitations. Let \(A(\hat R)\subseteq N\) be its buyers reachable from \(s\).

For each buyer, define her reported total value for a quantity by \(\hat V_i(q)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}\hat v_{i,\ell}\), with \(\hat V_i(0)=0\). Define the feasible allocation set
\(\mathcal F(\hat R)=\{q\in\{0,\ldots,k\}^{m}:\sum_iq_i\leq k,\ q_i=0\text{ for }i\notin A(\hat R)\}\).

Choose an allocation \(q^*(\hat\theta)\) maximizing \(\sum_i\hat V_i(q_i)\) over \(\mathcal F(\hat R)\), using a fixed deterministic rule to resolve ties.

For each buyer \(i\), delete vertex \(i\) and all its incident edges from \(G(\hat R)\). Let \(B_i(\hat R_{-i})\) be the buyers reachable from \(s\) in the resulting graph. Define
\(H_i(\hat\theta_{-i})=\max\{\sum_{j\ne i}\hat V_j(x_j):x\in\{0,\ldots,k\}^{m},\ \sum_jx_j\leq k,\ x_j=0\text{ for }j\notin B_i(\hat R_{-i})\}\).

The notation is justified: deleting \(i\) removes all edges controlled by \(i\), and the optimization uses no valuation of \(i\). Consequently, \(H_i\) is independent of both components of \(i\)'s report.

Set the payment to the seller to
\(p_i(\hat\theta)=H_i(\hat\theta_{-i})-\sum_{j\ne i}\hat V_j(q_j^*(\hat\theta))\).

These payments may be negative, as the task permits.

2. Feasibility and unreachable buyers

Allocation feasibility follows directly from the definition of \(\mathcal F(\hat R)\).

Suppose \(i\notin A(\hat R)\). No path from \(s\) to any reachable buyer can pass through \(i\), since its prefix would make \(i\) reachable. Thus deleting \(i\) leaves the reachable buyer set unchanged:
\(B_i(\hat R_{-i})=A(\hat R)\).

Also, \(q_i^*(\hat\theta)=0\). The optimization defining \(H_i\) therefore has exactly the same feasible allocations and objective as the allocation optimization. Hence
\(H_i(\hat\theta_{-i})=\sum_{j\ne i}\hat V_j(q_j^*(\hat\theta))\),
so \(p_i(\hat\theta)=0\).

Thus every unreachable buyer has both zero allocation and zero payment.

3. Dominant-strategy truthfulness

Fix a buyer \(i\), an arbitrary rational true type \(t_i=(v_i,R_i)\), and arbitrary admissible reports \(\hat\theta_{-i}\) of the other buyers. Write \(V_i(q)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}v_{i,\ell}\), and define
\(F_i(q)=V_i(q_i)+\sum_{j\ne i}\hat V_j(q_j)\).

Let \(q^T\) be the allocation when \(i\) reports truthfully. Because her reported valuation then equals her true valuation,
\(F_i(q^T)=\max_{q\in\mathcal F(R_i,\hat R_{-i})}F_i(q)\).

Consider any joint deviation \((\tilde v_i,\tilde R_i)\) with \(\tilde R_i\subseteq R_i\), and let \(q^D\) be its resulting allocation. Its reported graph is a subgraph of the truthful graph. Every buyer reachable under the deviation is therefore reachable under truthfulness, giving
\(\mathcal F(\tilde R_i,\hat R_{-i})\subseteq\mathcal F(R_i,\hat R_{-i})\).

In particular, \(q^D\) is feasible for the truthful optimization. Thus \(F_i(q^T)\geq F_i(q^D)\), regardless of the deviating valuation report.

Under either report, the payment formula gives the true utility
\(u_i=F_i(q)-H_i(\hat\theta_{-i})\).
The baseline is identical under the two reports, so
\(u_i(t_i,\hat\theta_{-i})\geq u_i((\tilde v_i,\tilde R_i),\hat\theta_{-i})\).

This proves dominant-strategy truthfulness against simultaneous valuation and invitation deviations. The argument applies to every rational profile, without any support restriction.

4. Ex post individual rationality

Keep the arbitrary true type and other reports from Section 3. Let \(x\) attain the maximum defining \(H_i\).

Every buyer reachable after deleting \(i\) remains reachable when \(i\) is present and reports \(R_i\). Consequently, \(x\), extended by \(x_i=0\), is feasible for the truthful allocation optimization. Moreover,
\(F_i(x)=H_i(\hat\theta_{-i})\).

Optimality of \(q^T\) gives \(F_i(q^T)\geq H_i(\hat\theta_{-i})\). Therefore truthful utility satisfies
\(u_i(t_i,\hat\theta_{-i})=F_i(q^T)-H_i(\hat\theta_{-i})\geq0\).

This establishes ex post individual rationality for every rational true type and every admissible profile of other buyers’ reports.

5. Computability and running time

For any reachable buyer set, welfare maximization can be computed by sorting its marginal values in decreasing order, breaking ties first by buyer label and then by increasing marginal index. Select the first \(k\) entries, or every entry if fewer exist.

This selection consists of a prefix of each buyer’s marginal sequence: whenever an entry \((i,\ell)\) is selected, every preceding entry \((i,h)\), with \(h<\ell\), occurs earlier in the ordering. It therefore defines a feasible quantity allocation.

Every feasible allocation corresponds to at most \(k\) marginal entries. Since all values are nonnegative, its welfare cannot exceed the sum selected by this rule. The rule consequently computes an optimal allocation.

Compute reachability and perform this optimization once in the reported graph and once after deleting each buyer. Each optimization sorts at most \(mk\) entries, and there are \(m+1\) optimizations. Graph traversal, rational comparisons, sums, and payment subtraction all take polynomial time in \(m\), \(k\), and the incoming report’s bit length. The resulting payments are rational with polynomial bit length: every welfare sum contains at most \(k\) input marginal values.

The mechanism is thus a uniform polynomial-time algorithm on all rational reports. Evaluating it throughout the given support, and computing the aggregate surplus, takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\). Its definition does not depend on the support or its masses.

6. Aggregate guarantee

For every truthful profile \(\theta\), the mechanism maximizes precisely the reachable-buyer welfare appearing in the definition of \(\mathrm{OPT}\). Hence
\(W(M,\mu)=k^{-1}\sum_{\theta\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}\mu(\theta)\max_{q\in\mathcal F(R(\theta))}\sum_iV_i^\theta(q_i)=\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\).

Since \(0\leq\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor/k\leq1\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\geq0\), this implies
\(W(M,\mu)\geq(\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor/k)\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\).
If \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)=0\), the equality already proves the required inequality directly.

Independent copies can use this same mechanism separately, respecting the prohibition on transfers of items between copies.
