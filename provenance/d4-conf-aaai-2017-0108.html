The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) alternatives with known item features and a rational finite-support society \(\mu\) over complete rankings, accessible through independent type samples, define \(P^\mu_{ij}=\Pr_{t\sim\mu}[i\succ_t j]\). If \(P^\mu\in\mathcal P_n(\psi,r,A)\cap\mathcal P_n^{ST}\) and satisfies the theorem's margin and conditioning assumptions, how many uniformly sampled item pairs and independent type samples per pair suffice for an algorithm to return a ranking whose normalized pairwise disagreement with \(P^\mu\) is at most \(\varepsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge2\) alternatives. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) strict complete rankings with positive binary-rational masses summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Known features form a full-row-rank matrix \(A\in\mathbb Q^{d\times m}\), where \(1\le d\le m\). A fixed strictly increasing bijection \(\psi:(0,1)\to\mathbb R\), and its inverse, are computable to requested precision in polynomial time.
Define \(P_{ij}=\Pr_{t\sim\mu}[i\succ_t j]\) for \(i\ne j\), and \(P_{ii}=1/2\). Promise that \(\psi(P)=A^\top B A\) entrywise for some real \(B\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}\) with \(\operatorname{rank}(B)\le r\le d\), and that \(P_{ij}>1/2\) and \(P_{jk}>1/2\) imply \(P_{ik}>1/2\).
Require \(p_{\min}=\min_{i\ne j}P_{ij}>0\), \(\Delta=\min_{i\ne j}|\psi(P_{ij})-\psi(1/2)|>0\), and that \(\psi\) is \(\ell\)-Lipschitz on \([p_{\min}/2,1-p_{\min}/2]\). Write \(\kappa=\sigma_{\min}(A)/\sigma_{\max}(A)>0\), with \(\kappa^4\le C_2d\) for the fixed universal constant appearing below.
A sampling experiment chooses \(s\le\binom m2\) distinct unordered pairs uniformly without replacement. Each selected pair receives \(K\ge1\) fresh independent draws from \(\mu\), each yielding its binary comparison outcome; draws are independent across pairs as well.
For a strict output ranking \(\sigma\), define \(D(\sigma,P)=\binom m2^{-1}\sum_{i<j}\mathbf1\{\sigma\text{ places the majority-preferred alternative below the other}\}\). Let \(0<\varepsilon,\delta<1\); state running times in \(m,\tau,L,d,r,s,K\) and the accuracy, confidence, margin, Lipschitz, and conditioning parameters.

Is producing \(\sigma\) with \(D(\sigma,P)\le\varepsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\) polynomial-time solvable, or NP-hard? What budgets \(s\) and \(K\) of uniformly sampled pairs and independent type samples per pair suffice?

The [known algorithmic guarantee](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.02661) asserts error at most \(\varepsilon\), with failure probability at most \(3/m^3\), when \(s>48C_2^2d^2\log(m)(1+\gamma)^2/(\kappa^8\varepsilon^2\Delta^4)\) and \(K\ge16(1+\gamma)s\ell^2\log(m)/(m^2\Delta^2\varepsilon)\), using \(\gamma=5\).
That guarantee is formulated for independent binary observations with means \(P_{ij}\), without requiring a finite list of agents. Independent type sampling has this observation law, so the distributional sampling model is already within its stated scope.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Renamed the number of alternatives to m and the sampled-pair count to s, reserving L for input bit length.
  • Normalized positive rational masses to sum to one and interpreted complete rankings as strict rankings.
  • Retained the mandatory explicit support input alongside sampling access; did not silently replace it with an unknown society.
  • Used distinct unordered pairs sampled uniformly without replacement, with fresh independent type draws for every comparison, matching the non-active comparison experiment.
  • Restricted the link domain to (0,1): a strictly increasing bijection from [0,1] onto the real line is impossible, and the stated logit and probit examples require open endpoints.
  • Made positive probability and transformed-margin conditions explicit, excluding deterministic comparisons and majority ties; assigned diagonal probabilities 1/2.
  • Required rational full-row-rank features and effective link evaluation to define bit complexity; retained the stated inverse condition number and its inequality.
  • Defined disagreement according to the stated intended loss, resolving the displayed formula's ambiguous ranking-order notation.

1. Conclusion and interpretation

The promised problem is polynomial-time solvable. Because the support and its masses are explicitly given, an exact majority-consistent ranking can be computed deterministically in polynomial time.

For the intended learning interpretation, where the algorithm uses only the sampled comparisons, the following budgets also suffice for every \(\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)\). Write \(N=\binom m2\), and take

\(\displaystyle s=\min\left\{N,\left\lceil\frac{m\log m+\log(2/\delta)}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil\right\},\)

\(\displaystyle K=\max\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{\ell^2}{2\Delta^2}\log\frac{2s}{\delta}\right\rceil\right\}.\)

A polynomial-time algorithm then produces \(\sigma\) satisfying \(D(\sigma,P)\le\varepsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\).

These are sufficient budgets, without a claim of optimality. The supplied feature-based guarantee gives an additional budget in its feasible confidence regime, stated in Section 5.

2. The majority relation is a strict total order

Define \(i\succ^\star j\) to mean \(P_{ij}>1/2\).

Every supported ranking is strict and complete, so \(P_{ij}+P_{ji}=1\). Moreover, \(\Delta>0\) and strict monotonicity of \(\psi\) imply \(P_{ij}\ne1/2\) whenever \(i\ne j\). Consequently, exactly one of \(i\succ^\star j\) and \(j\succ^\star i\) holds.

The promised transitivity condition is precisely transitivity of \(\succ^\star\). Thus \(\succ^\star\) is a strict total order. Let \(\sigma^\star\) denote its unique ranking. By definition,

\(\displaystyle D(\sigma^\star,P)=0.\)

This is the property that makes the present promise problem computationally tractable.

3. Exact computation from the given support

Let the supported rankings be \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_\tau\), with masses \(w_1,\ldots,w_\tau\). Compute

\(\displaystyle P_{ij}=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}w_t\,\mathbf1\{i\succ_{\rho_t}j\}.\)

All comparisons with \(1/2\) can be performed exactly using rational arithmetic.

For completeness, write \(w_t=a_t/b_t\), and choose the common denominator \(Q=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t\). Its bit length is at most the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence \(O(L)\). The integers \(W_t=Qw_t\) satisfy \(\sum_t W_t=Q\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle P_{ij}>1/2\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}W_t\mathbf1\{i\succ_{\rho_t}j\}>Q.\)

No evaluation of \(\psi\), recovery of \(B\), or numerical margin estimation is needed.

After computing the majority relation, assign each alternative its number of majority wins and sort decreasingly. An alternative in position \(k\) of the strict total order has exactly \(m-k\) wins, so this recovers \(\sigma^\star\).

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, a conservative running-time bound is \(O(\tau m^2L^2)\) bit operations. The algorithm is deterministic and returns error zero. Its runtime has no dependence on inverse accuracy, margin, or conditioning parameters.

Thus the literal explicit-support problem requires no samples. If the sampling experiment must nevertheless be executed, any permitted sample budget can be ignored by this algorithm.

4. A polynomial-time algorithm using only sampled comparisons

4.1. Converting the transformed margin into a probability margin

Define

\(\displaystyle g=\min_{i\ne j}|P_{ij}-1/2|.\)

The complementarity identity implies \(P_{ij}\in[p_{\min},1-p_{\min}]\). Both \(P_{ij}\) and \(1/2\) therefore belong to the interval on which \(\psi\) is \(\ell\)-Lipschitz. Hence, for every distinct pair,

\(\displaystyle \Delta\le|\psi(P_{ij})-\psi(1/2)| \le\ell|P_{ij}-1/2|.\)

It follows that \(g\ge\Delta/\ell>0\).

A certified upper bound on \(\ell\) and a certified positive lower bound on \(\Delta\) may replace their exact values in the budget.

4.2. Algorithm

Let \(S\) be the uniformly sampled set of \(s\) unordered pairs.

For each selected pair, estimate its comparison probability by its empirical mean over the \(K\) draws. Orient an edge according to the empirical majority, breaking an empirical tie by a fixed rule.

If the resulting directed graph is acyclic, output any topological ordering. Otherwise, output an arbitrary strict ranking.

We prove that, with probability at least \(1-\delta\), the graph is acyclic and every one of its topological orderings has the required error.

4.3. All sampled majority directions are correct with high probability

Fix the selected set \(S\). For any selected pair, its observations are independent Bernoulli random variables with the corresponding mean \(P_{ij}\).

The one-sided Hoeffding inequality states that the probability an empirical mean deviates from its expectation by at least \(t>0\) in a specified direction is at most \(\exp(-2Kt^2)\). An incorrect orientation, including an unfavourably broken empirical tie, requires such a deviation with \(t=|P_{ij}-1/2|\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle \Pr(\text{incorrect orientation for this pair}\mid S) \le e^{-2Kg^2} \le e^{-2K\Delta^2/\ell^2}.\)

The stated choice of \(K\) makes this at most \(\delta/(2s)\). A union bound gives

\(\displaystyle \Pr(\text{some sampled orientation is incorrect})\le\delta/2.\)

When all orientations are correct, the graph is a subgraph of the strict total order \(\sigma^\star\), so it is acyclic.

4.4. Uniformly sampled pairs exclude every sufficiently inaccurate ranking

Fix a ranking \(\sigma\) with \(D(\sigma,P)>\varepsilon\). Let \(T_\sigma\) be its set of inverted pairs relative to \(\sigma^\star\), and write \(t=|T_\sigma|\). Then \(t>\varepsilon N\).

If \(s>N-t\), the sampled set must intersect \(T_\sigma\). Otherwise, sampling without replacement gives

\(\displaystyle \Pr(S\cap T_\sigma=\varnothing) =\frac{\binom{N-t}{s}}{\binom Ns} =\prod_{a=0}^{s-1}\left(1-\frac{t}{N-a}\right) \le\left(1-\frac{t}{N}\right)^s \le e^{-st/N} \le e^{-\varepsilon s}.\)

There are \(m!\) strict rankings. Thus

\(\displaystyle \Pr\bigl(\exists\sigma:\ D(\sigma,P)>\varepsilon,\ S\cap T_\sigma=\varnothing\bigr) \le m!e^{-\varepsilon s}.\)

If the stated choice of \(s\) is smaller than \(N\), then \(\log(m!)\le m\log m\) shows that this probability is at most \(\delta/2\). If \(s=N\), the event is impossible.

4.5. Combining the events

With probability at least \(1-\delta\), both of the following hold:

  • Every sampled edge has its true majority direction.
  • Every ranking with error greater than \(\varepsilon\) reverses at least one sampled edge.

On this event, the algorithm returns a topological ordering and hence reverses no sampled edge. The second property forces its error to be at most \(\varepsilon\).

This proves the claimed guarantee.

4.6. Running time and total observations

The number of independent type draws is \(sK\), with

\(\displaystyle sK=O\left( s\left[1+\frac{\ell^2}{\Delta^2}\log\frac{2s}{\delta}\right] \right).\)

With comparison observations supplied, constructing empirical means and topologically sorting the graph take polynomial time in \(m,s,K\). A straightforward implementation, including uniform-pair generation, uses expected

\(\displaystyle O\left( L+m^2\log(m+1)+sK\log(K+1)+(m+s)\log(m+1) \right)\)

bit operations.

If draws are simulated from the given support, first store each ranking’s position array and the cumulative integer masses \(W_t\). A draw can then be generated by selecting a uniform integer in \(\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}\), locating its cumulative-mass interval, and looking up the pair’s order. Exact rejection sampling and binary search give expected \(O(L\log(\tau+1))\) bit operations per draw. The resulting total runtime remains polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,s,K\).

These sampling budgets depend polynomially on \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) and \(\ell/\Delta\), and logarithmically on \(\delta^{-1}\). They require no dependence on \(d,r,\kappa\). If the probability margin is exponentially small in \(L\), this observation-only sample budget can be exponential in \(L\); the deterministic explicit-support algorithm remains polynomial in \(L\).

5. The supplied feature-based guarantee

Using the guarantee stipulated in the question, with \(\gamma=5\), define

\(\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{FLR}} =\frac{1728C_2^2d^2\log m} {\kappa^8\varepsilon^2\Delta^4}, \qquad s_{\mathrm{FLR}}=\lfloor T_{\mathrm{FLR}}\rfloor+1.\)

Provided \(s_{\mathrm{FLR}}\le N\), sufficient budgets are

\(\displaystyle s=s_{\mathrm{FLR}},\)

\(\displaystyle K\ge \max\left\{1, \left\lceil \frac{96s\ell^2\log m} {m^2\Delta^2\varepsilon} \right\rceil \right\}.\)

The stated failure probability is at most \(3/m^3\), so these budgets meet the requested confidence whenever \(\delta\ge3/m^3\). This is the feature-based guarantee of [Niranjan and Rajkumar](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.02661).

Its observation-law hypothesis holds here: a fresh type draw produces a Bernoulli outcome with mean \(P_{ij}\), and the prescribed independence of type draws gives independence of observations. The finite support size \(\tau\) introduces no additional statistical condition.

The strict pair threshold must remain feasible: when \(s_{\mathrm{FLR}}>N\), replacing it by \(N\) does not satisfy that theorem’s hypothesis. For either infeasible feature budgets or confidence \(\delta<3/m^3\), Section 4 supplies a proved polynomial-time alternative with the requested guarantee.
