The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 1 (Offline EF1 Upper Bound: Unweighted) — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many unweighted interval request types \(j\in\mathcal{J}\), with rational intervals and rational per-worker supplies \(q_j\), compute the maximum accepted request mass in a unit-mass population of interchangeable workers. Each worker receives a whole nonoverlapping bundle \(S\); choose a distribution \(x_S\) with \(\sum_S x_S=1\), \(\sum_{S:j\in S}x_S\le q_j\), and EF1 across every pair of bundles in the support. Compare this optimum with the unconstrained optimum under the same supplies.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) worker types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Here workers are interchangeable, so \(\tau=1\), with total mass \(1\).
The offline input comprises \(m\ge1\) request types \(\mathcal J\), each with a unit value, a half-open interval \([s_j,e_j)\) with rational \(0\le s_j<e_j\), and a positive binary-rational supply \(q_j\) per unit worker mass. Supplies need not sum to \(1\).
Let \(\mathcal B\) contain all subsets \(S\subseteq\mathcal J\) of pairwise nonoverlapping intervals, including \(\emptyset\); a worker receives one whole bundle, containing at most one request of each type.
An allocation is a distribution \(x_S\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\in\mathcal B}x_S=1\) and \(\sum_{S\ni j}x_S\le q_j\) for every \(j\). Unused supply may be rejected. Its accepted mass is \(V(x)=\sum_S |S|x_S\).
Require EF1 between every pair of positive-mass bundles: \(\bigl||S|-|R|\bigr|\le1\) whenever \(x_Sx_R>0\), including comparisons with an empty bundle.
Let \(U\) be the maximum of \(V\) without EF1 and \(F\) its maximum with EF1; thus \(U/F\) measures the intrinsic efficiency loss. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

What is the smallest worst-case ratio \(\alpha\) achievable by a polynomial-time algorithm returning an EF1 distribution with \(V(x)\ge U/\alpha\)? In particular, is \(\alpha=3/2\) achievable, and is it tight?

For finitely many identical workers and indivisible requests, a polynomial-time EF1 algorithm achieves at least \(2/3\) of the unconstrained optimum; discrete instances force ratios approaching \(3/2\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15159v1)
No result settling this precise distribution-over-whole-bundles approximation question was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required positive-length half-open intervals with nonnegative rational endpoints, excluding empty intervals to match the intended scheduling domain.
  • Required positive rational supplies, omitting zero-supply request types; supplies are not normalized and may exceed one.
  • Included empty bundles and unused workers in EF1 comparisons, and retained permission to reject requests.
  • Interpreted request types as supplies of copies, with at most one copy per worker because positive-length copies overlap.
  • Applied the society-support convention to workers: there is one worker type of mass one. Used m for the number of request types because the continuous model has no finite worker-count parameter.
  • Interpreted comparison with the unconstrained optimum as an approximation guarantee against that benchmark, rather than an exact-optimization target.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The optimal worst-case guarantee is \(\alpha=3/2\). It is achievable in polynomial time and tight. In fact, an optimal EF1 distribution can be computed exactly in polynomial time. The proof uses the finite-worker theorem stated in the task.

1. Computing the EF1 optimum. Replace each supply by \(\bar q_j=\min\{q_j,1\}\). This preserves feasibility because a distribution of total mass \(1\) uses each type with mass at most \(1\).

For \(k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\), let \(F_k\) denote the optimum restricted to bundles whose sizes belong to \(\{k,k+1\}\cap\{0,\ldots,m\}\), when this restriction is feasible. Every such distribution is EF1. Conversely, in any EF1 distribution, let \(k\) be its minimum positive-mass bundle size. Every positive-mass bundle then has size \(k\) or \(k+1\). Consequently, \(F=\max_k F_k\), omitting infeasible restrictions.

We construct a polynomial-size linear program for each \(F_k\).

List all distinct interval endpoints as \(t_1<\cdots<t_p\), where \(p\le 2m\). Construct a directed graph with vertices \((t_i,c)\), for \(1\le i\le p\) and \(0\le c\le m\), together with a sink \(z\). Its source is \(a=(t_1,0)\). Include these arcs:

  • Idle arcs \((t_i,c)\longrightarrow(t_{i+1},c)\).
  • For each request \(j\) and each \(0\le c<m\), a request arc \((s_j,c)\longrightarrow(e_j,c+1)\), labeled \(j\).
  • Terminal arcs \((t_p,c)\longrightarrow z\) for \(c\in\{k,k+1\}\cap\{0,\ldots,m\}\).

Every nonterminal arc strictly increases time, so this graph is acyclic. A source-to-sink path selects pairwise nonoverlapping intervals, in chronological order. Its terminal layer records the number selected. A type cannot occur twice on a path: after traversing its request arc, time has advanced strictly past its start. Conversely, every bundle of an allowed size defines such a path, inserting idle arcs between its requests. Half-open intervals ensure that consecutive requests with \(e_j=s_\ell\) are permitted.

Put a nonnegative variable \(f_b\) on each arc \(b\). Require a unit source-to-sink flow: net outflow is \(1\) at \(a\), net inflow is \(1\) at \(z\), and flow is conserved elsewhere. For each type \(j\), impose

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\text{request arcs }b\text{ labeled }j} f_b\le \bar q_j.\)

Maximize

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\text{request arcs }b} f_b.\)

To verify this formulation in both directions, route each bundle’s mass along its corresponding path. This produces a feasible flow with the same supply usage and objective value.

Conversely, a nonnegative unit flow in this acyclic graph decomposes into source-to-sink paths. Explicitly, follow positive-flow arcs from the source; conservation prevents getting stuck before reaching the sink. Subtract the minimum flow on the resulting path and repeat. Each iteration eliminates at least one positive-flow arc. After the source outflow is exhausted, any remaining flow would be a nonzero conserved flow in an acyclic graph, which is impossible. Thus the path weights sum to \(1\). Interpreting them as bundle masses preserves every supply constraint and the objective. All resulting bundles have the permitted cardinalities.

Therefore this LP computes \(F_k\) exactly, and choosing the best feasible \(k\) computes \(F\).

Each graph has \(O(m^2)\) vertices and arcs. Each LP has \(O(m^2)\) variables and constraints, with rational coefficients and polynomial binary encoding length. The rational linear-programming theorem supplies an exact rational optimum in polynomial bit time whenever the LP is feasible and bounded; infeasibility is also detectable. Here boundedness follows from unit flow and acyclicity. [Polynomial-time linear programming](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html).

The algorithm solves \(m+1\) such LPs. Its running time is \((m+1)\operatorname{poly}(m,L)\), hence \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations with \(\tau=1\). Path decomposition returns \(O(m^2)\) positive-mass bundles with polynomial-length rational weights. There is no dependence on the numerical values of supply denominators.

Allowing terminal arcs from every layer gives, by the same argument, a polynomial-size LP for \(U\).

2. Transferring the finite-worker guarantee. We use the following precise consequence of the supplied theorem: for any finite collection of unit-value, positive-length, half-open intervals and any number \(N\) of identical workers, there is a feasible schedule whose worker loads differ pairwise by at most \(1\) and whose accepted count is at least two-thirds of the unconstrained optimum. Coincident intervals are permitted. [Theorem 1 of the supplied source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15159v1#Thmtheorem1).

Choose a rational unconstrained optimal distribution \(x^*\). Its existence follows, for example, from the unrestricted flow LP and rational path decomposition above. Let \(N\) be a common multiple of the denominators of its positive weights and of all \(\bar q_j\).

Construct a finite instance with \(N\) workers and \(N\bar q_j\) labeled copies of interval \(j\). These are integers. Give bundle \(S\) to exactly \(Nx^*_S\) workers, using distinct copies for every occurrence of each type. Enough copies exist because

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\ni j}Nx^*_S\le N\bar q_j.\)

This schedule accepts exactly \(NU\) requests.

Conversely, any schedule in the finite instance induces a feasible distribution by choosing a worker uniformly and recording its bundle of types. A worker cannot receive two copies of the same type, since those copies have the same positive-length interval and therefore overlap. Supply usage is at most \(\bar q_j\), and the induced distribution’s value is the accepted count divided by \(N\). Hence the finite unconstrained optimum is exactly \(NU\).

All hypotheses of the finite-worker theorem hold: workers are identical, values are unit, intervals have positive length and rational nonnegative endpoints, and each copy is indivisible. Apply it and average the resulting schedules over the workers. The induced distribution is EF1—including comparisons with empty bundles—and has value at least

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{N}\cdot\frac23 NU=\frac23 U.\)

Thus \(F\ge 2U/3\). The polynomial-time algorithm above returns value \(F\), proving the \(3/2\) guarantee. The potentially large denominator-clearing expansion is used only for this existence proof.

3. Tightness in the distribution model. Consider this normalized long/short construction. Fix a power of two \(h\ge2\). Take \(2h\) short types \(I_i=[i-1,i)\), each with supply \(1/h\), and one long type \(A=[0,2h)\) with supply \(1-1/h\). Thus \(m=2h+1\), and all supplies are positive binary rationals.

The total supply is \(3-1/h\). It is accepted by assigning mass \(1/h\) to the bundle of all short types and mass \(1-1/h\) to \(\{A\}\). Therefore \(U=3-1/h\).

If an EF1 distribution accepts the long type with positive mass, it contains a singleton bundle; every supported bundle consequently has size at most \(2\), giving \(V\le2\). If it rejects the long type, the total short supply gives \(V\le2\).

Finally, assign mass \(1/h\) to each of the \(h\) bundles \(\{I_{2r-1},I_{2r}\}\). This is feasible and EF1, with value \(2\). Hence

\(\displaystyle F=2,\qquad \frac UF=\frac32-\frac{1}{2h}\longrightarrow\frac32.\)

For every \(\alpha<3/2\), sufficiently large \(h\) makes \(U/F>\alpha\). No EF1 allocation, regardless of computational power, can meet that guarantee.
