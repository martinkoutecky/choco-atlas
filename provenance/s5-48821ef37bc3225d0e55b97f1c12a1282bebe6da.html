The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), observed masses \(0\leq\nu_t\leq\mu_t\), signals \(\tilde q_t\), and accuracies \(\epsilon,\eta>0\), suppose there are \(w_t\geq0\) and \(\theta\in\mathbb R\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t w_t^2=1\) and \(\sum_t\nu_t(\tilde q_t-q_{w,\theta}(t))^2\leq\eta^2\). Find normalized weights \(v_t\geq0\) and a threshold \(\phi\) such that \(\sum_t\mu_t v_t^2=1\) and \(\bigl[\sum_t\nu_t(q_{v,\phi}(t)-\tilde q_t)^2\bigr]^{1/2}\leq\epsilon+\eta\), where \(q_{v,\phi}(t)=\mu_t^{-1/2}\mathbb E[\operatorname{sgn}(\sum_s\sqrt{\mu_s}v_s Z_s-\phi)Z_t]\u005c) for independent standard Gaussian Z_t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. There is no separate candidate parameter \(m\).
Inputs also include binary rationals \(0\leq\nu_t\leq\mu_t\), signals \(\tilde q_t\in\mathbb R\), and accuracies \(\epsilon,\eta>0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including the accuracies.
For independent standard Gaussian variables \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_\tau\), nonnegative real weights \(u_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_tu_t^2=1\), and \(h\in\mathbb R\), define \(q_{u,h}(t)=\mu_t^{-1/2}\mathbb E[\operatorname{sgn}(\sum_s\sqrt{\mu_s}u_sZ_s-h)Z_t]\), where \(\operatorname{sgn}(x)=1\) for \(x\geq0\) and \(-1\) otherwise.
The promise is that some such \(w,\theta\) satisfies \(\sum_t\nu_t(\tilde q_t-q_{w,\theta}(t))^2\leq\eta^2\).
The reconstruction problem asks for \(v_t\geq0\) and finite \(\phi\in\mathbb R\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_tv_t^2=1\) and \(\bigl[\sum_t\nu_t(q_{v,\phi}(t)-\tilde q_t)^2\bigr]^{1/2}\leq\epsilon+\eta\). Only these coordinate signals are observed; no constant coefficient is supplied.
For finite output representation, encode \(v_t=a_t/\sqrt{\sum_s\mu_sa_s^2}\) using a nonzero nonnegative rational vector \(a\), and encode \(\phi\) as a rational.

Is this promised continuous reconstruction problem solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard under promise-preserving reductions?

For Boolean inputs, partial Chow reconstruction has a randomized algorithm achieving error \(O(\epsilon)\) with probability \(1-\delta\) in time \(n^2\log n\log(1/\delta)\,2^{\operatorname{poly}(1/\epsilon)}\); it is polynomial in \(n\) for fixed accuracy. Result and input interpretation

The reconstruction problem is deterministically solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\) under the standard finite binary input model.

An encoding qualification is necessary: arbitrary unencoded real signals do not define a bit-complexity problem. I interpret the supplied signals and accuracies as binary rationals, consistent with the stipulated total input length \(L\). The proof also applies to other numerical encodings that permit approximation to \(p\) binary digits in time polynomial in \(L,p\).

The algorithm below returns precisely the required rational encoding and achieves error at most \(\eta+\epsilon\).

1. Gaussian signals form a punctured convex ball

Write \(C=\sqrt{2/\pi}\), and define the weighted seminorms
\(\|x\|_\mu=(\sum_t\mu_tx_t^2)^{1/2}\) and
\(\|x\|_\nu=(\sum_t\nu_tx_t^2)^{1/2}\).

Because \(0\leq\nu_t\leq\mu_t\), every vector satisfies
\(\|x\|_\nu\leq\|x\|_\mu\).

Lemma 1. For every admissible \(u,h\),
\(q_{u,h}(t)=C e^{-h^2/2}u_t\).

Proof. Put \(b_t=\sqrt{\mu_t}u_t\) and \(Y=\sum_tb_tZ_t\). The normalization gives \(\sum_tb_t^2=1\), so \(Y\) is standard Gaussian. Moreover, \(Z_t-b_tY\) is a centered Gaussian variable independent of \(Y\), since its covariance with \(Y\) is zero. Thus

\(\mathbb E[\operatorname{sgn}(Y-h)Z_t] =b_t\mathbb E[\operatorname{sgn}(Y-h)Y]\).

Writing \(\varphi(y)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/2}\), we have

\(\mathbb E[\operatorname{sgn}(Y-h)Y] =2\int_h^\infty y\varphi(y)\,dy =2\varphi(h)\).

Multiplication by \(\mu_t^{-1/2}\) proves the formula. \(\square\)

Consequently, the set of exactly realizable signal vectors is

\(\mathcal Q=\{x\in\mathbb R^\tau:x_t\geq0,\ 0<\|x\|_\mu\leq C\}\).

Indeed, one inclusion follows from Lemma 1. Conversely, for any such \(x\), put \(r=\|x\|_\mu\), \(u=x/r\), and \(h=\sqrt{2\log(C/r)}\). Then \(h\) is finite, \(\|u\|_\mu=1\), and Lemma 1 gives \(q_{u,h}=x\).

The closure of \(\mathcal Q\) is therefore the convex set

\(K=\{x:x_t\geq0,\ \|x\|_\mu\leq C\}\).

The origin is the only point of \(K\) requiring an infinite threshold for exact realization. The positive error allowance will let us approximate it with a finite rational threshold.

2. Projection onto a slightly smaller rational ball

Choose the positive rational

\(\delta=\min\{\epsilon/8,\ 1/16\}\).

Compute a rational \(c\) satisfying

\(C-\delta\leq c\leq C,\qquad c\geq1/2\).

Section 5 justifies polynomial-time computation of this number. Define

\(K_c=\{x:x_t\geq0,\ \|x\|_\mu\leq c\}\).

Let \(q^*\) be the signal vector supplied by the promise. Then \(q^*\in K\) and
\(\|q^*-\tilde q\|_\nu\leq\eta\). The vector \(y=(c/C)q^*\) belongs to \(K_c\), and

\(\|y-q^*\|_\nu \leq\|y-q^*\|_\mu =(1-c/C)\|q^*\|_\mu \leq C-c \leq\delta\).

Therefore

\(\min_{x\in K_c}\|x-\tilde q\|_\nu\leq\eta+\delta.\)

We next construct a rational feasible vector within an additional \(\delta\) of this minimum.

3. The projection requires only a scalar bisection

Let
\(J=\{t:\nu_t>0,\ \tilde q_t>0\}\).
Set \(z_t=\tilde q_t\) for \(t\in J\), and \(z_t=0\) otherwise.

Restricting to vectors that vanish outside \(J\) loses no optimum: setting such a coordinate to zero preserves feasibility and does not increase its contribution to the error. On coordinates in \(J\), the unconstrained optimum is \(z_t\).

If \(\|z\|_\mu\leq c\), take \(x=z\). This is an exact minimizer over \(K_c\).

Otherwise, for \(t\in J\), define

\(d_t=\nu_t/\mu_t,\qquad x_t(\lambda)=\dfrac{d_tz_t}{d_t+\lambda}\),

and set \(x_t(\lambda)=0\) outside \(J\). Let

\(S(\lambda)=\sum_t\mu_tx_t(\lambda)^2\).

Here \(J\) is nonempty, \(S(0)>c^2\), and \(S\) is continuous and strictly decreasing to zero. There is a unique \(\lambda^*>0\) with \(S(\lambda^*)=c^2\).

Lemma 2. The vector \(x^*=x(\lambda^*)\) minimizes
\(F(y)=\sum_t\nu_t(y_t-\tilde q_t)^2\) over \(K_c\).

Proof. For fixed \(\lambda\geq0\), the expression

\(F(y)+\lambda\|y\|_\mu^2\)

separates by coordinates. Minimizing each quadratic over \(y_t\geq0\) gives exactly \(x_t(\lambda)\); on coordinates with both coefficients zero, choose zero.

Thus, for every \(y\in K_c\),

\(F(x^*)+\lambda^*c^2 \leq F(y)+\lambda^*\|y\|_\mu^2 \leq F(y)+\lambda^*c^2\).

Cancelling the last term proves the claim. \(\square\)

We can approximate \(x^*\) with exact rational arithmetic. Put

\(S_0=\|z\|_\mu^2,\qquad \Lambda=1+S_0/c^2,\qquad M=1+S_0,\qquad d_{\min}=\min_{t\in J}d_t.\)

Since \(d_t\leq1\),

\(x_t(\lambda)\leq z_t/(1+\lambda)\).

Writing \(D=S_0/c^2\), this gives

\(S(\Lambda)\leq S_0/(1+\Lambda)^2 =c^2D/(2+D)^2 \leq c^2.\)

Hence \([0,\Lambda]\) brackets \(\lambda^*\). Bisect this interval, comparing the rational number \(S(\lambda)\) exactly with the rational number \(c^2\). Maintain a lower endpoint with \(S\geq c^2\) and an upper endpoint with \(S\leq c^2\).

Stop once the interval width is at most \(\delta d_{\min}/M\), and let \(\widehat\lambda\) be its upper endpoint. Output provisionally \(x=x(\widehat\lambda)\). This vector is rational, nonnegative, and belongs to \(K_c\).

For any nonnegative \(\lambda,\lambda'\),

\(\left|x_t(\lambda)-x_t(\lambda')\right| =\dfrac{d_tz_t|\lambda-\lambda'|} {(d_t+\lambda)(d_t+\lambda')} \leq\dfrac{z_t}{d_{\min}}|\lambda-\lambda'|.\)

Consequently,

\(\|x(\lambda)-x(\lambda')\|_\nu \leq\dfrac{\|z\|_\nu}{d_{\min}}|\lambda-\lambda'| \leq\dfrac{M}{d_{\min}}|\lambda-\lambda'|,\)

where we used \(\|z\|_\nu\leq\sqrt{S_0}\leq1+S_0=M\). Our stopping rule therefore implies \(\|x-x^*\|_\nu\leq\delta\).

Combining this with Section 2 proves, in both projection cases,

\(\|x-\tilde q\|_\nu\leq\eta+2\delta.\)

4. Conversion to the required rational output

Define \(f(h)=C e^{-h^2/2}\) for \(h\geq0\). Choose an integer \(B\geq2\) with \(2^{-B}\leq\delta\). Since \(C<1\), \(e>2\), and \(B^2/2\geq B\),

\(f(B)\leq e^{-B}\leq2^{-B}\leq\delta.\)

Nonzero projected vector

If \(x\neq0\), use the rational vector \(a=x\). Let \(r=\|x\|_\mu\), and let the output encoding represent

\(v_t=\dfrac{a_t}{\sqrt{\sum_s\mu_sa_s^2}}=\dfrac{x_t}{r}.\)

Then \(v_t\geq0\) and \(\|v\|_\mu=1\) exactly.

We construct a rational \(\phi\in[0,B]\) satisfying
\(|f(\phi)-r|\leq\delta\).
This does not require exact transcendental comparisons.

Bisect \([0,B]\). At each midpoint \(m\), approximate \(f(m)\) and \(r\) to absolute error at most \(\delta/16\). Let \(\widehat D\) be their approximate difference.

  • If \(|\widehat D|\leq\delta/4\), return \(\phi=m\). The true error is at most \(\delta/4+2(\delta/16)<\delta\).
  • Otherwise, the sign of \(\widehat D\) is the true sign of \(f(m)-r\). If it is positive, replace the lower endpoint by \(m\); if negative, replace the upper endpoint.

If the interval width becomes at most \(\delta\), return its upper endpoint.

To verify the last rule, observe that

\(|f'(h)|=Ch e^{-h^2/2}\leq Ce^{-1/2}<1.\)

If \(r\geq f(B)\), there is a root of \(f(h)=r\) in the bracket, so the returned endpoint has error at most its distance from that root, hence at most \(\delta\). If \(r<f(B)\), every reliable comparison moves the lower endpoint; the upper endpoint remains \(B\), whose error is less than \(\delta\). Early termination already has the stated error bound.

By Lemma 1,

\(q_{v,\phi}-x=(f(\phi)-r)v.\)

Since \(\|v\|_\nu\leq\|v\|_\mu=1\), it follows that

\(\|q_{v,\phi}-x\|_\nu\leq\delta.\)

Zero projected vector

If \(x=0\), take \(a=e_1\) and \(\phi=B\). This is allowed because \(\tau\geq1\) and \(\mu_1>0\). The encoded vector \(v\) satisfies \(\|v\|_\mu=1\), and

\(\|q_{v,B}\|_\nu=f(B)\|v\|_\nu\leq\delta.\)

Thus the same approximation bound holds.

In either case, the final output satisfies

\(\|q_{v,\phi}-\tilde q\|_\nu \leq\|q_{v,\phi}-x\|_\nu+\|x-\tilde q\|_\nu \leq\eta+3\delta \leq\eta+\epsilon.\)

5. Polynomial bit complexity

All required computations have polynomial bit complexity; in particular, none require polynomial dependence on \(1/\epsilon\).

Rational projection. The rational quantities \(S_0,\Lambda,M,d_{\min}\) have polynomial bit length. Therefore the scalar bisection uses at most

\(O\!\left(1+\log_2\!\left(\dfrac{\Lambda M}{\delta d_{\min}}\right)\right)\)

iterations, which is polynomial in \(\tau,L\). Endpoints can be maintained as dyadic multiples of \(\Lambda\), so their bit lengths grow only linearly with the iteration count. Each comparison and each output coordinate uses rational arithmetic on polynomial-length integers.

Square roots and \(C\). Square roots of bounded nonnegative rationals can be approximated by rational bisection, comparing squares exactly. For completeness, polynomial-precision computation of \(\pi\) follows from Machin’s identity

\(\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)\)

and the alternating series

\(\arctan z=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\dfrac{(-1)^jz^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_N, \qquad |R_N|\leq\dfrac{|z|^{2N+1}}{2N+1}.\)

Only \(O(p)\) terms are needed for \(p\) binary digits. Rational arithmetic and square-root bisection then approximate \(C=\sqrt{2/\pi}\) to the same precision. Approximating \(C\) within \(\delta/4\) and subtracting \(\delta/4\) supplies a rational \(c\in[C-\delta/2,C]\), as required.

Threshold computation. We may choose \(B=O(1+\log_2(1/\delta))\). Thus threshold bisection takes \(O(1+\log_2(B/\delta))\) iterations and produces a rational \(\phi\) of polynomial bit length.

To evaluate \(e^{-t}\), where \(t=h^2/2\) and \(0\leq h\leq B\), use its Taylor polynomial through degree \(n-1\). Its remainder has absolute value at most \(t^n/n!\). Since \(n!\geq(n/e)^n\) and \(e<3\), choosing an integer \(n\geq\max\{6t,p\}\) makes this remainder at most \(2^{-p}\). Here \(t=O(B^2)\), so the number of terms and all intermediate rational bit lengths are polynomial in the input length and requested precision. Combining this evaluation with an approximation of \(C\) gives the certified evaluations used in Section 4.

Finally, the rational vector \(a\) and rational threshold \(\phi\) have polynomial total encoding length. Their normalization is represented exactly by the prescribed encoding, so no additional rounding of \(v\) is needed.

Conclusion

The Gaussian coordinate identity reduces reconstruction to projection onto a nonnegative weighted ball. A scalar rational bisection computes the projection to sufficient accuracy, and a second certified bisection produces a finite rational threshold. This gives a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm with the required error bound and output representation.
