The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite rational worker masses \(\mu_{r,\pi}\) and job masses \(\nu_s\) on a common scale, where worker types have region \(r\) and a fixed ranking \(\pi\) of job regions, and jobs have region \(s\), compatibility holds exactly when \(r\neq s\). Let \(r_1\) be a region with the largest worker mass and assume \(\sum_{r\neq r_1}\mu_r\geq\sum_{s\neq r_1}\nu_s\), where \(\mu_r=\sum_\pi\mu_{r,\pi}\). Fix an order of worker types within the two worker tubes \(W_1,W_{-1}\); process each type’s mass in that order, assigning it to the earliest job tube \(J_{-1},J_1\) with compatible available mass and following \(\pi\) within that tube. Does this execution always produce a flow of maximum total mass among feasible matchings?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(R\) be a finite set of \(m\geq1\) regions. Worker types are pairs \((r,\pi)\), where \(r\in R\) and \(\pi\) is a strict total ranking of \(R\); job types are regions \(s\in R\). Compatibility holds exactly when \(r\neq s\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) worker and job types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. Worker masses \(\mu_{r,\pi}\) and job masses \(\nu_s\) use a common scale and need not sum to one; absent types have zero mass.
Set \(\mu_r=\sum_\pi\mu_{r,\pi}\). A designated region \(r_1\) satisfies \(\mu_{r_1}=\max_{r\in R}\mu_r\), and inputs satisfy \(\sum_{r\neq r_1}\mu_r\geq\sum_{s\neq r_1}\nu_s\).
The worker tubes are \(W_1=\{(r,\pi):r=r_1\}\) and \(W_{-1}=\{(r,\pi):r\neq r_1\}\); the job tubes are \(J_{-1}=R\setminus\{r_1\}\) and \(J_1=\{r_1\}\). A strict order of supported worker types within each worker tube is part of the input.
Process \(W_1\) before \(W_{-1}\). For each type, repeatedly select the earliest job tube in \((J_{-1},J_1)\) containing compatible positive residual mass, then its highest-ranked available compatible region under \(\pi\). Transfer the minimum of the worker and job residual masses, irrevocably, until the worker mass is exhausted or no compatible mass remains; leave any remaining worker mass unmatched.
A feasible matching is a nonnegative flow \(x_{r,\pi,s}\), zero when \(r=s\), with \(\sum_s x_{r,\pi,s}\leq\mu_{r,\pi}\) and \(\sum_{r,\pi}x_{r,\pi,s}\leq\nu_s\). Its total matched mass is \(\sum_{r,\pi,s}x_{r,\pi,s}\).

Is deciding whether the specified execution yields a feasible matching of maximum total mass solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite unit agents, this two-by-two procedure produces a maximum-cardinality matching under the stated regularity condition for every strict worker and job order (Bó and Chen, 2026). That result does not explicitly treat rational masses.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Defined m as the number of regions and tau as the combined positive support size of worker and job types.
  • Masses remain on a common, unnormalized scale; absent types have zero mass.
  • Made rankings strict total orders of regions, and made within-tube worker-type orders part of the input.
  • Made r_1 a designated maximizer, allowing ties in regional worker mass.
  • Specified both tube partitions, worker-tube processing order, divisible transfers, fallback to the second job tube, and unmatched residual mass.
  • Defined feasible mass matchings using worker and job capacity inequalities, allowing partial assignment.

Result

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, every input satisfying the stated assumptions yields a maximum-total-mass matching, regardless of the specified within-tube orders and rankings.

The proof applies directly to rational masses.

1. Notation and an upper bound

Write

\(a=\mu_{r_1},\qquad B=\sum_{r\neq r_1}\mu_r,\qquad C=\sum_{s\neq r_1}\nu_s,\qquad d=\nu_{r_1}.\)

Thus the total worker and job masses are \(M=a+B\) and \(N=C+d\). The assumptions give

\(B\geq C\quad\text{and}\quad \mu_r\leq a\text{ for every }r\in R.\)

Every feasible matching has total mass at most \(M\) and at most \(N\). Moreover, workers in \(W_1\) can only match jobs in \(J_{-1}\), so their matched mass is at most \(C\). Workers in \(W_{-1}\) contribute at most \(B\). Consequently, every feasible matching has total mass at most

\(U=\min\{M,N,B+C\}.\)

We will prove that the specified execution attains \(U\).

Each transfer preserves feasibility: it uses a compatible pair and transfers no more than either residual mass. Each positive transfer exhausts a worker type or job region, which remains exhausted thereafter. Hence the execution terminates after finitely many transfers.

2. The first phase matches exactly \(\min\{a,C\}\)

Every worker in \(W_1\) is compatible with every region in \(J_{-1}\), and incompatible with \(J_1\).

Therefore, during the first phase, workers continue taking mass from \(J_{-1}\) until either all worker mass \(a\) is exhausted or all job mass \(C\) is exhausted. The matched mass in this phase is exactly

\(w=\min\{a,C\}.\)

The residual job mass in \(J_{-1}\) is \(C-w\), and the job mass \(d\) in \(J_1\) is unchanged.

3. Every job in \(J_{-1}\) is filled

If \(a\geq C\), the first phase already fills all of \(J_{-1}\).

Suppose instead that \(a<C\). The second phase starts with residual job mass

\(D=C-a>0\)

in \(J_{-1}\). Assume, for a contradiction, that some region \(t\neq r_1\) has positive residual job mass at the end of the execution.

Job residual masses only decrease. Thus region \(t\) has positive residual job mass throughout the second phase.

Consider any worker type in \(W_{-1}\) whose origin is different from \(t\). Throughout its processing, region \(t\) supplies compatible positive residual mass in \(J_{-1}\). The procedure therefore always selects \(J_{-1}\) for this worker type and cannot stop before exhausting its worker mass.

It follows that all worker mass in \(W_{-1}\) with origin different from \(t\) is matched to \(J_{-1}\). This mass is

\(B-\mu_t.\)

Since \(J_{-1}\) starts the second phase with mass \(D\) and retains positive mass at the end, its matched mass during that phase is strictly less than \(D\). Hence

\(B-\mu_t<D=C-a.\)

But the assumptions imply

\(B-\mu_t\geq C-a=D,\)

because \(B\geq C\) and \(\mu_t\leq a\). This is a contradiction.

Thus, in both cases, every job in \(J_{-1}\) is filled.

4. The execution attains the upper bound

Let \(q\) be the mass matched to \(J_1\). Only workers in \(W_{-1}\) can contribute to \(q\).

By Sections 2 and 3, workers in \(W_{-1}\) match exactly \(C-w\) mass to \(J_{-1}\). Feasibility therefore gives

\(q\leq d,\qquad q\leq B-C+w.\)

If \(q<d\), then \(J_1\) retains positive mass at the end, and hence throughout the second phase. Every worker in \(W_{-1}\) is compatible with \(J_1\), so none can finish processing with unmatched mass. Therefore,

\(B=(C-w)+q,\)

giving \(q=B-C+w\).

If \(q=d\), feasibility already gives \(d\leq B-C+w\). Together, these cases establish

\(q=\min\{d,B-C+w\}.\)

The execution’s total matched mass is consequently

\(T=C+q=\min\{C+d,B+w\}.\)

Since \(w=\min\{a,C\}\), we have

\(B+w=\min\{B+a,B+C\}=\min\{M,B+C\}.\)

Therefore,

\(T=\min\{M,N,B+C\}=U.\)

Section 1 bounds every feasible matching by \(U\), and the execution is feasible and attains \(U\). This proves its optimality.

The argument also covers \(m=1\): then \(B=C=0\), and compatibility permits no positive matching.

5. Polynomial-time implementation

On inputs promised to satisfy the assumptions, the decision algorithm can simply return YES. Validating those assumptions requires only polynomial-time rational arithmetic.

The execution itself can also be computed in polynomial time. Let \(k\) be the number of supported worker types and \(j\) the number of supported job regions, so \(k+j=\tau\).

Every positive transfer permanently exhausts a worker type or job region. Thus there are at most \(\tau\) positive transfers. Finding the selected region by scanning a worker’s ranking takes \(O(m)\) time per selection; there are also at most \(k\) unsuccessful terminal selections.

For exact arithmetic, write the supported masses as \(n_i/d_i\) with binary-encoded integers and take the common denominator \(D=\prod_i d_i\). Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Scaling every mass by \(D\) produces integers of polynomial bit length, and all subsequent residual masses are bounded by the scaled input totals. Each subtraction, comparison, and minimum therefore has polynomial bit complexity.

Hence exact simulation, computation of the optimal value above, and comparison all run in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
