Proposition 2 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite compatibility graph \(H=(U,F)\), a finite set of center types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), and per-center supplies \(b_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{U}\), define each coalition mass \(x\in[0,\mu]\) to have supply \(s_u(x)=\sum_{t\in T}b_{t,u}x_t\) and worth \(\nu(x)=\max\{\sum_{\{u,v\}\in F}z_{uv}: z_{uv}\ge0,\ \sum_{e\ni u}z_e\le s_u(x)\ \forall u\in U\}\). Decide whether there is a nonnegative type-payoff vector \(p\) such that \(\sum_t\mu_t p_t=\nu(\mu)\) and \(\sum_t x_t p_t\ge\nu(x)\) for every coalition mass \(x\in[0,\mu]\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(H=(U,F)\) be a finite simple undirected compatibility graph, with \(m=|U|\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) center types \(T\) with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Thus \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a supply vector \(b_t\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{U}\), encoded in binary. A coalition is any real vector \(x\in\mathbb R^T\) satisfying \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\); its supply is \(s_u(x)=\sum_{t\in T}b_{t,u}x_t\).
Its worth is \(\nu(x)=\max\{\sum_{e\in F}z_e:z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{F},\ \sum_{e\ni u}z_e\le s_u(x)\text{ for every }u\in U\}\).
A core payoff is a vector \(p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\) satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tp_t=\nu(\mu)\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}x_tp_t\ge\nu(x)\) for every coalition \(x\). Here \(p_t\) is payoff per unit mass. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is deciding whether a core payoff exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite agents owning disjoint vertex sets, with coalition worth equal to the maximum integral matching size in their induced subgraph, core existence belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\) and is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete. [Chen, Hatschka and Simola](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18449v1)
For that discrete problem, polynomial-time solvability holds when each agent owns at most two vertices, while \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness holds already with at most three. [Benedek et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-025-02200-9)
On the continuous side, cores of nonatomic linear production games have been characterized; the verified source does not state a complexity classification for the binary-support decision problem above. [Billera and Raanan](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.6.3.420)

  • Removed zero-mass types from the input support, as required by the fixed society encoding convention.
  • Specified a simple undirected compatibility graph, consistent with the discrete matching model.
  • Specified that coalition coordinates, matching quantities, and payoff rates are real, and that payoff is measured per unit mass.
  • Defined m as the number of compatibility vertices and L as the total binary input length.

Result

Core existence is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, every valid instance has a core payoff, and one can construct such a payoff in time \( \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \).

The proof uses the fractional matching program exactly as stated in the task.

1. The grand coalition’s primal and dual programs

Write the grand coalition’s supply as

\( S_u=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t b_{t,u} \).

Its worth is the optimal value of

\( \displaystyle \max_{z\ge 0}\left\{\sum_{e\in F}z_e:\sum_{e\ni u}z_e\le S_u\text{ for every }u\in U\right\}. \)

This program is feasible because \( z=0 \) is feasible. Its feasible region is bounded: for every edge \( e=\{u,v\} \), feasibility implies \( 0\le z_e\le\min(S_u,S_v) \). Thus its optimum is finite and attained.

The linear-programming dual is

\( \displaystyle \min_{y\ge 0}\left\{\sum_{u\in U}S_uy_u:y_u+y_v\ge 1\text{ for every }\{u,v\}\in F\right\}. \)

Indeed, the primal constraint for vertex \( u \) has dual variable \( y_u \), and the column for edge \( \{u,v\} \) has a coefficient of one in exactly the two endpoint constraints.

The dual is feasible: \( y_u=1 \) for every vertex suffices. By strong linear-programming duality, there is an optimal dual vector \( y^* \) satisfying

\( \displaystyle \sum_{u\in U}S_uy_u^*=\nu(\mu). \)

These conclusions also cover \( F=\varnothing \), when both optimal values are zero.

2. Constructing and verifying a core payoff

Define the payoff per unit mass of type \( t \) by

\( \displaystyle p_t=\sum_{u\in U}b_{t,u}y_u^*. \)

Because supplies and dual variables are nonnegative, \( p_t\ge 0 \).

For efficiency, interchange the finite sums:

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\mu_tp_t =\sum_{u\in U}y_u^*\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tb_{t,u} =\sum_{u\in U}S_uy_u^* =\nu(\mu). \)

It remains to verify every coalition constraint. Fix any coalition \( x \) with \( 0\le x_t\le\mu_t \), and any matching vector \( z \) feasible for its supply \( s(x) \). Dual feasibility, followed by primal feasibility and nonnegativity of \( y^* \), gives

\( \displaystyle \sum_{e\in F}z_e \le \sum_{\{u,v\}\in F}(y_u^*+y_v^*)z_{\{u,v\}} = \sum_{u\in U}y_u^*\sum_{e\ni u}z_e \le \sum_{u\in U}y_u^*s_u(x). \)

By the definition of supply and payoff,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{u\in U}y_u^*s_u(x) = \sum_{t\in T}x_t\sum_{u\in U}b_{t,u}y_u^* = \sum_{t\in T}x_tp_t. \)

Taking the maximum over all feasible \( z \) therefore yields

\( \displaystyle \nu(x)\le\sum_{t\in T}x_tp_t. \)

Since \( x \) was arbitrary, every coalition constraint holds. Together with nonnegativity and efficiency, this proves that \( p \) is a core payoff.

3. Binary encoding and running time

The proof gives the following construction:

  • Compute the \( m \) rational supplies \( S_u \).
  • Solve the grand coalition’s dual program.
  • Compute the \( \tau \) rational payoffs \( p_t \).

For an explicitly bounded dual formulation, impose \( y_u\le 1 \). This does not change the optimum. To see this, replace any feasible \( y \) by \( \widehat y_u=\min(y_u,1) \). For an edge, either both endpoint coordinates remain unchanged, or an endpoint becomes one and the other remains nonnegative; hence its covering constraint still holds. Since \( S_u\ge 0 \), truncation cannot increase the objective.

Consequently, one may solve a rational linear program with \( m \) variables and \( |F|+2m \) inequalities over a nonempty bounded feasible region. Its objective coefficients have polynomial encoding length: each \( S_u \) is a sum of at most \( \tau \) products of input rationals, and a common denominator can be obtained by multiplying their denominators, whose total bit length is polynomial in \( L \).

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore applies. Since \( |F|\le m(m-1)/2 \), solving this program, together with the \( O(m\tau) \) rational arithmetic operations before and after it, takes \( \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \) bit operations. The returned rational payoff also has polynomial encoding length.

For the existence decision alone, every valid input receives “yes”; the polynomial construction establishes the stronger search result.

4. Why the discrete hardness does not transfer

The cited discrete model uses coalition subsets and maximum integral matchings. ([Chen, Hatschka and Simola](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18449v1)) Here, coalition worth is already a linear-programming optimum. Its optimal dual prices certify both grand-coalition efficiency and all coalition inequalities, as proved above. Thus the cited discrete hardness does not establish hardness for this continuous model.
