The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of employer and worker types with rational masses \(\mu_v>0\), summing to one, per-person quotas \(q_v\), weak orders over compatible counterpart types, aggregate channel capacities \(B_e\), and per-unit costs \(c_e\), find a stable flow \(x_e\) minimizing \(\sum_e c_e x_e\). Require \(0\le x_e\le B_e\), \(\sum_{e\in E_v}x_e\le Q_v:=\mu_v q_v\), and mixed-type stability: no unsaturated channel is in the choice-rule tail at both endpoints, using aggregate quota \(Q_v\).

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) employer and worker types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_v\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Let \(G=(F\sqcup W,E)\) be the compatibility graph, with one channel per compatible type pair, and put \(m=|E|\). Input includes positive rational per-person quotas \(q_v\), positive rational channel capacities \(B_e\), rational costs \(c_e\) of either sign, and weak orders on incident channels. Let \(Q_v=\mu_vq_v\), and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
A feasible flow has real entries satisfying \(0\le x_e\le B_e\) and \(\sum_{e\in E_v}x_e\le Q_v\), where \(E_v\) is the set of channels incident to \(v\).
Write the preference ties at \(v\) as \(\pi_v^1,\ldots,\pi_v^{k_v}\), best first. If \(\sum_{e\in E_v}x_e<Q_v\), define \(T_v(x)=E_v\).
Otherwise let \(i\) be the first index with \(\sum_{j\le i}\sum_{e\in\pi_v^j}x_e=Q_v\), put \(r_v=\max_{e\in\pi_v^i}x_e\), and define \(T_v(x)=\bigcup_{j<i}\pi_v^j\cup\{e\in\pi_v^i:x_e<r_v\}\). Thus equal largest allocations in the critical tie are all excluded from the tail; no tie-breaking is used.
A flow is mixed-type stable if no channel \(e=fw\) satisfies both \(x_e<B_e\) and \(e\in T_f(x)\cap T_w(x)\). The optimization problem is to find a stable flow minimizing \(\sum_{e\in E}c_ex_e\).

Applicable theorem

Karzanov’s Theorem 7.1 states that minimum-cost mixed-type stable assignments on finite bipartite graphs are computable in strongly polynomial time. Its model allows real allocations, positive edge capacities and vertex quotas, weak preference orders, and costs of either sign. Exact correspondence of the models

Construct the theorem’s instance on the given graph, with vertex quota \(Q_v=\mu_vq_v\), edge capacity \(B_e\), cost \(c_e\), and the given ordered preference ties. There are exactly \(\tau\) vertices and \(m\) edges.

The feasible assignments are precisely the feasible flows in the question, because their capacity and quota inequalities coincide.

We next check stability. Fix a feasible flow \(x\) and a vertex \(v\).

If \(\sum_{e\in E_v}x_e<Q_v\), both definitions give \(T_v(x)=E_v\).

Otherwise, the total equals \(Q_v\). Nonnegativity implies that the cumulative tie sums are nondecreasing and eventually equal \(Q_v\). Thus the first such index \(i\) exists, and
\[ \sum_{j<i}\sum_{e\in\pi_v^j}x_e<Q_v =\sum_{j\leq i}\sum_{e\in\pi_v^j}x_e. \]
Moreover, every allocation in a later tie is zero: the displayed prefix already equals the total.

Consequently, this is also the source’s critical tie. For a quota-filling assignment, its cutting height is the maximum allocation in that tie, and its head consists of all edges attaining that maximum. Its tail therefore equals
\[ \bigcup_{j<i}\pi_v^j \;\cup\; \{e\in\pi_v^i:x_e<\max_{a\in\pi_v^i}x_a\}, \]
exactly the tail specified in the question. [Source: Section 2, head and tail definitions and equation (2.6)](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11556v2).

Hence an edge blocks in either model exactly when it is unsaturated and belongs to both endpoint tails. The stable sets and objective functions coincide, so Theorem 7.1 yields an optimal stable flow.

Polynomial binary complexity

The products \(Q_v=\mu_vq_v\) are computable exactly in polynomial time. Their total binary encoding length is polynomial in \(L\): multiplying rational numerators and denominators adds their bit lengths.

To make the binary complexity implication explicit, clear denominators. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all \(Q_v\) and \(B_e\), and let \(K\) be the product of the cost denominators. Both have bit length polynomial in \(L\). Set
\[ \widetilde Q_v=DQ_v,\qquad \widetilde B_e=DB_e,\qquad \widetilde c_e=Kc_e. \]
These data are integers of polynomial encoding length.

The map \(\widetilde x=Dx\) is a bijection between feasible flows of the two instances. It preserves quota equality, the critical tie, comparisons within that tie, and edge saturation. Therefore it also preserves stability. The transformed objective satisfies
\[ \sum_e\widetilde c_e\widetilde x_e =KD\sum_ec_ex_e. \]
Since \(KD>0\), minimizers correspond.

The source’s strongly polynomial guarantee includes polynomial storage bounds for integer input data. Thus its polynomial number of arithmetic operations can be implemented with polynomial binary running time on this transformed instance. Dividing the resulting allocation by \(D\) returns an exact optimum for the original instance. [Source: Introduction, computational complexity discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11556v2).

The requested complexity classification is therefore settled.
