The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\) over \(\tau\) complete linear-utility and budget types \(t=(u_t,b_t)\), and rational per-capita supplies \(s_j>0\), compute equilibrium prices \(p\ge 0\) and per-person bundles \(x_t\ge 0\) such that each \(x_t\) maximizes \(u_t^\top x\) subject to \(p^\top x\le b_t\), while \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}=s_j\) for every good; equivalently, compute a rational \(\epsilon\)-KKT point of the weighted Eisenberg–Gale program.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) types \(t=(u_t,b_t)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) divisible goods with rational supplies \(s_j>0\). All budgets \(b_t>0\) and utility coefficients \(u_{tj}\ge0\) are rational. Every type values some good positively, and every good is valued positively by some supported type. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An equilibrium consists of rational prices \(p\ge0\) and per-person bundles \(x_t\ge0\), where \(x_t\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{z\ge0,\;p^\top z\le b_t}u_t^\top z\), \(p^\top x_t=b_t\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}=s_j\) for every good. Any maximizing bundle is permitted when demand is nonunique.
Is computing such an exact equilibrium polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
For finite-buyer linear Fisher markets, restarted PDHCG has a stated linear convergence guarantee with exact primal updates and suitable instance-dependent restart intervals ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06258v1)); exact equilibrium computation also has a polynomial-time algorithm ([Devanur et al.](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/market-equilibrium-via-a-primal-dual-algorithm-for-a-convex-program/)).
Polynomial-time results for a continuum of goods concern a different continuous model ([Gao–Kroer](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16684)); no source explicitly settling this supported-society formulation was verified.

1. Reduction to a finite Fisher market

Construct a market with one buyer for each supported type \(t\), budget \(B_t=\mu_t b_t\), and utility coefficients \(u_{tj}\). Retain the supplies \(s_j\).

For fixed prices \(p\), associate a per-person bundle \(x_t\) with the aggregate bundle \(y_t=\mu_t x_t\). Because \(\mu_t>0\), this map is a bijection between the two budget-feasible sets:

\(x_t\ge 0,\ p^\top x_t\le b_t \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y_t\ge 0,\ p^\top y_t\le B_t.\)

Moreover, \(u_t^\top y_t=\mu_t u_t^\top x_t\). Multiplication by the positive constant \(\mu_t\) preserves every utility comparison. Consequently,

\(x_t\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{z\ge 0,\ p^\top z\le b_t}u_t^\top z \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y_t\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{w\ge 0,\ p^\top w\le B_t}u_t^\top w.\)

Budget exhaustion and market clearing are also preserved:

\(p^\top y_t=\mu_t p^\top x_t,\qquad \sum_t y_{tj}=\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}.\)

Thus equilibria of the society correspond bijectively to equilibria of this finite market, with inverse transformation \(x_t=y_t/\mu_t\). This argument preserves the entire set of maximizing bundles, so nonunique demand causes no difficulty.

2. Normalize the supplies

To use the unit-supply formulation of the Fisher theorem, define coefficients \(v_{tj}=s_j u_{tj}\). Give every good supply \(1\), retaining budgets \(B_t\).

Let \(q\) and \(a_t\) be prices and allocations in this normalized market. Recover the society’s prices and per-person bundles by

\(p_j=\dfrac{q_j}{s_j},\qquad x_{tj}=\dfrac{s_j a_{tj}}{\mu_t}.\)

Here is a direct verification.

Market clearing. Since \(\sum_t a_{tj}=1\),

\(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj} =s_j\sum_t a_{tj} =s_j.\)

Budget exhaustion. Since \(q^\top a_t=B_t\),

\(p^\top x_t =\dfrac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_j q_j a_{tj} =\dfrac{B_t}{\mu_t} =b_t.\)

Utility maximization. Take any original feasible bundle \(z\ge 0\) with \(p^\top z\le b_t\), and define \(c_j=\mu_t z_j/s_j\). Then

\(q^\top c=\mu_t p^\top z\le B_t,\qquad v_t^\top c=\mu_t u_t^\top z.\)

Likewise, \(v_t^\top a_t=\mu_t u_t^\top x_t\). Optimality of \(a_t\) therefore implies

\(\mu_t u_t^\top x_t =v_t^\top a_t \ge v_t^\top c =\mu_t u_t^\top z.\)

Dividing by \(\mu_t>0\) proves the required optimality of \(x_t\).

3. Exact computation and bit complexity

We use the following established result: a finite linear Fisher market with positive rational budgets, unit supplies, and nonnegative rational utility coefficients admits exact rational equilibrium computation in polynomial time, provided every buyer values some good and every good has a potential buyer. The rational-input normalization appears in Section 2, and the polynomial algorithm is established in Theorem 8.12 of [Devanur–Papadimitriou–Saberi–Vazirani](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/a22-devanur-jacm08.pdf).

All hypotheses hold for the normalized instance:

  • There are exactly \(\tau\) buyers and \(m\) goods.
  • Every budget \(B_t=\mu_t b_t\) is positive and rational.
  • Every coefficient \(v_{tj}=s_j u_{tj}\) is nonnegative and rational.
  • Since \(s_j>0\), \(v_{tj}>0\) precisely when \(u_{tj}>0\). Hence no utility row or column is identically zero.

The reduction also has polynomial encoding length. Each \(B_t\) and \(v_{tj}\) is a product of two input rationals. The numerator and denominator bit lengths of such a product are at most the sums of the corresponding input bit lengths, up to a constant. Thus the normalized instance has total bit length \(L'\) bounded by a polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

For completeness, conversion to the integer-data formulation does not require expanding agents or enumerating monetary units. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all \(B_t\), and let \(D_t\) be the product of the denominators in utility row \(v_t\). Replace budgets by \(D B_t\) and coefficients by \(D_t v_{tj}\). These are integers. Row scaling preserves utility maximizers; common budget scaling is reversed by dividing prices by \(D\). Each product denominator has bit length bounded by the sum of its factors’ bit lengths, so this integer instance still has polynomial encoding length.

Apply the exact Fisher algorithm, undo any integer scaling, and then use the recovery formulas in Section 2. The cited result supplies rational equilibrium prices and an equilibrium allocation with polynomial encoding length. Multiplying or dividing their coordinates by the input rationals \(s_j\) and \(\mu_t\) takes polynomial time and preserves polynomial output length.

Therefore the complete algorithm runs in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\) and returns an equilibrium satisfying every condition in the question.

4. Role of the other models

The classification follows from the exact finite-buyer theorem and the proved reduction. A linear convergence guarantee alone would still require an exact recovery argument and suitable bit-complexity bounds. Results concerning a continuum of goods are unnecessary here: the given society has finitely many goods and finitely supported types.
