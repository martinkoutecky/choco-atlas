Theorem 1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given alternatives \(C\) and a support list of complete rankings \((\sigma^r,\mu_r)_{r=1}^{\tau}\), where each \(\mu_r>0\) is a binary rational and \(\sum_r \mu_r=1\), find a permutation \(\pi\) minimizing \(\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}\mu_r d_{\mathrm{AP}}(\pi,\sigma^r)\), with \(d_{\mathrm{AP}}\) exactly as defined in the paper.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq 1\) alternatives. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) distinct strict complete rankings \(\sigma^r\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_r\), with \(\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}\mu_r=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
For a permutation \(\pi\), let \(\pi_i\) be its item at position \(i\), and let \(\sigma(x)\) denote the position of item \(x\) in \(\sigma\). Define \(d_{\mathrm{AP}}(\pi,\sigma)=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\frac{m}{2(j-1)}\mathbf{1}\{\sigma(\pi_i)>\sigma(\pi_j)\}\). This distance is asymmetric: its weights depend on positions in \(\pi\).
Find a permutation \(\pi\) minimizing \(\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}\mu_r d_{\mathrm{AP}}(\pi,\sigma^r)\); any minimizer is allowed. The input distribution is arbitrary, with no generative-model promise.

Is finding such a minimizing permutation solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For an explicitly listed finite multiset of rankings, minimizing average AP distance is NP-hard. [Source](https://epasto.org/papers/aaai2016-permutations-full.pdf)
No separate complexity classification for arbitrary binary-rational mass inputs was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified a nonempty alternative set; the single-alternative case uses the empty sum for AP distance.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length.
  • Allowed any optimal permutation when several minimizers exist.
  • Made explicit that the input distribution is arbitrary, matching the anchor's arbitrary-multiset domain.

Classification

Finding a minimizing permutation is NP-hard, even when all masses are dyadic rationals (fractions whose denominators are powers of two). Thus a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

For \(m=1\), the objective is zero. The hardness concerns the problem with unbounded \(m\).

Known hardness result and its applicability

We use the established theorem that, given an explicitly listed nonempty multiset \(P=(\sigma^1,\ldots,\sigma^N)\), finding a permutation minimizing
\(H_P(\pi)=\frac1N\sum_{s=1}^{N}d_{\mathrm{AP}}(\pi,\sigma^s)\)
is NP-hard.

The cited paper defines the distance with weights determined by positions in the first argument, exactly as in this task. Its Theorem 1 and the paragraph immediately following it establish hardness for minimizing average AP distance over arbitrary multisets. Thus both the distance orientation and the absence of a distributional promise match our setting. [De Stefani, Epasto, Upfal, and Vandin, Section 3](https://epasto.org/papers/aaai2016-permutations-full.pdf)

We give a polynomial-time reduction from this problem to the requested support representation. The construction also handles the stricter interpretation of “binary-rational” as dyadic.

A constant-cost padding identity

For a ranking \(\rho\), let \(\rho^{\mathrm{rev}}\) denote its reversal. For every permutation \(\pi\),

\(d_{\mathrm{AP}}(\pi,\rho)+d_{\mathrm{AP}}(\pi,\rho^{\mathrm{rev}})=\frac{m(m-1)}2.\)

Indeed, for each pair \(i<j\), strictness implies that exactly one of \(\rho\) and \(\rho^{\mathrm{rev}}\) ranks \(\pi_i\) after \(\pi_j\). Consequently, the sum of the two distances is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=2}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\frac{m}{2(j-1)} =\sum_{j=2}^{m}\frac m2 =\frac{m(m-1)}2.\)

For \(m=1\), both sums are empty and the identity still holds.

Reduction to distinct support and dyadic masses

Take a source instance \(P=(\sigma^1,\ldots,\sigma^N)\), where \(N\geq1\).

Let \(T\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(T\geq2N\). Then \(2N\leq T<4N\), and \(k=(T-2N)/2\) is a nonnegative integer.

Construct a multiset \(Q\) as follows:

  • Include two copies of each occurrence in \(P\).
  • Fix any ranking \(\rho\), and include \(k\) copies each of \(\rho\) and \(\rho^{\mathrm{rev}}\).

The total number of occurrences is \(2N+2k=T\).

Now list the distinct rankings occurring in \(Q\) as \(\eta^1,\ldots,\eta^\tau\). Let \(a_r\) be the multiplicity of \(\eta^r\), and assign mass \(\mu_r=a_r/T\).

These masses are positive, sum to one, and are dyadic because \(T\) is a power of two. The rankings in the resulting support are distinct by construction.

For every candidate permutation \(\pi\), its target objective is

\(\displaystyle F_Q(\pi)=\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}\mu_r d_{\mathrm{AP}}(\pi,\eta^r) =\frac2T\sum_{s=1}^{N}d_{\mathrm{AP}}(\pi,\sigma^s) +\frac{k}{T}\bigl(d_{\mathrm{AP}}(\pi,\rho)+d_{\mathrm{AP}}(\pi,\rho^{\mathrm{rev}})\bigr).\)

Applying the padding identity gives

\(\displaystyle F_Q(\pi)=\frac{2N}{T}H_P(\pi)+\frac{k}{T}\frac{m(m-1)}2.\)

The coefficient \(2N/T\) is positive, and the second term is independent of \(\pi\). Therefore the two objectives have exactly the same minimizing permutations. This also verifies that returning any minimizer is sufficient.

Polynomial encoding size and conclusion

The constructed support satisfies \(\tau\leq N+2\). Since \(T<4N\), each numerator \(a_r\) and the denominator \(T\) require \(O(\log(N+1))\) bits. After relabeling alternatives by integers, the total output length is

\(L=O\bigl((N+2)(m\log(m+1)+\log(N+1))\bigr).\)

Multiplicity counting and construction of the support take polynomial time in the explicitly listed source input size.

Hence an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) for the requested problem would solve the known NP-hard multiset problem in polynomial time. The reduction proves the claimed NP-hardness, including for dyadic masses.
