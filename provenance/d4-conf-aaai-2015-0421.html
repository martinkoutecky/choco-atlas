The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a sequence of search instances with fixed τ cohort types \(t_j\), counts \(n_j(N)/N\to\mu_j>0\), type search sets \(A_j^{(N)}\), and common intersection \(D_N=\bigcap_j A_j^{(N)}\), assume \(|D_N|/N\to\infty\) and each \(r_j=\lim_N |A_j^{(N)}|/|D_N|\) exists. Run Mechanism 1 and let \(p_j^{(N)}\) be a type-\(j\) agent's simplified-game win probability. Characterize the limiting type allocation shares \(q_j=\lim_N n_j(N)p_j^{(N)}\) and the limiting expected-total-digs ratio to the simplified-game benchmark; in particular, does \(\limsup_N \mathbb{E}[\#\text{ digs with mechanism}]/\mathbb{E}[\#\text{ digs in benchmark}]\le 1/(2R)\) hold for \(R=\min_j r_j\)?

A society is specified by the support \(t_1,\ldots,t_\tau\) and positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_j\), with \(\sum_j\mu_j=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
For each \(N\), there are \(N\) agents on a finite island \(S_N\), with \(n_j(N)\) agents sharing the nonempty search set \(A_j^{(N)}\subseteq S_N\), \(\sum_j n_j(N)=N\), and \(n_j(N)/N\to\mu_j\).
Write \(D_N=\bigcap_j A_j^{(N)}\), \(d_N=|D_N|>0\), and assume \(d_N/N\to\infty\) and \(|A_j^{(N)}|/d_N\to r_j\in[1,\infty)\); put \(R=\min_j r_j\).
In the benchmark, each agent independently chooses a uniform random permutation of her search set; the treasure is independently uniform on \(D_N\). Every agent digs once per period until the first discovery period ends, with simultaneous discoverers tied uniformly. Let \(p_j^{(N)}\) be one type-\(j\) agent's winning probability.
The mechanism independently assigns every location of \(D_N\) to an individual agent with that agent's benchmark winning probability. Agents search only their assignments, one new location per period while any remain, in an arbitrary order chosen without knowledge of the treasure; all digging ends after the discovery period.
Let \(M_N\) and \(B_N\) be the expected total numbers of digs under the mechanism and benchmark, respectively, including every dig in the discovery period. Expectations include treasure and mechanism or benchmark randomness. Any running times should use \(m_N=|S_N|\), \(\tau\), \(N\), and total input bit length \(L_N\).

Do \(q_j=\lim_{N\to\infty}n_j(N)p_j^{(N)}\) and \(\rho=\lim_{N\to\infty}M_N/B_N\) exist for every such sequence and depend only on \((\mu_j,r_j)_{j=1}^{\tau}\)? If so, what are their values and the sharp welfare-ratio guarantee as a function of these data?

For finite societies, the independent-assignment mechanism preserves benchmark winning probabilities and never increases expected total digs. Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Both limits exist and depend only on the stated data. Define
\[ \lambda=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\frac{\mu_j}{r_j}. \]
Then
\[ \boxed{q_j=\frac{\mu_j/r_j}{\lambda}}, \qquad \boxed{\rho=\frac{\lambda}{2} =\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\frac{\mu_j}{r_j}}. \]
Thus the sharp guarantee using all the data is the exact limiting ratio \(\lambda/2\). In particular,
\[ \rho\leq \frac{1}{2R}, \]
with equality exactly when every \(r_j=R\), since every \(\mu_j\) is positive.

The proof below is self-contained.

1. Benchmark discovery times. Write \(a_{j,N}=|A_j^{(N)}|\) and \(h_N=d_N/N\). By assumption, \(h_N\to\infty\). Since \(n_j(N)/N\to\mu_j>0\), every cohort is nonempty for all sufficiently large \(N\); throughout the limiting argument we restrict to those \(N\).

Conditional on any treasure location \(z\in D_N\), its position in one type-\(j\) agent’s random permutation is uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,a_{j,N}\}\). These positions are independent across agents. Their joint distribution does not depend on \(z\), so they remain independent after averaging over the treasure.

Let \(T_{j,N}\) be the minimum position among type-\(j\) agents, and let \(T_N=\min_jT_{j,N}\) be the benchmark discovery period. Put \(\alpha_j=\mu_j/r_j\), so \(\lambda=\sum_j\alpha_j>0\).

For each fixed \(x\geq0\), eventually \(\lfloor h_Nx\rfloor<a_{j,N}\), and
\[ \Pr(T_{j,N}/h_N>x) =\left(1-\frac{\lfloor h_Nx\rfloor}{a_{j,N}}\right)^{n_j(N)}. \]
Indeed, \(a_{j,N}/h_N=N(a_{j,N}/d_N)\to\infty\). Moreover,
\[ n_j(N)\frac{\lfloor h_Nx\rfloor}{a_{j,N}}\longrightarrow\alpha_jx, \qquad n_j(N)\left(\frac{\lfloor h_Nx\rfloor}{a_{j,N}}\right)^2\longrightarrow0. \]
The first convergence follows from \(n_j(N)/N\to\mu_j\) and \(a_{j,N}/d_N\to r_j\); the second follows because the fraction is \(O(1/N)\) and \(n_j(N)\leq N\). Applying \(\log(1-u)=-u+O(u^2)\) therefore gives
\[ \Pr(T_{j,N}/h_N>x)\longrightarrow e^{-\alpha_jx}. \]

Different cohorts use disjoint collections of independent permutations, so their minima are independent. Consequently,
\[ (T_{1,N}/h_N,\ldots,T_{\tau,N}/h_N) \ \xrightarrow{\mathrm d}\ (X_1,\ldots,X_\tau), \]
where the \(X_j\) are independent exponential random variables with rates \(\alpha_j\). This joint convergence follows from the product of the marginal distribution functions, whose limiting product is continuous.

2. Cohort winning probabilities. By symmetry among agents of the same type,
\[ Q_{j,N}:=n_j(N)p_j^{(N)} \]
is exactly the probability that the selected winner belongs to cohort \(j\).

If \(T_{j,N}<T_{k,N}\) for all \(k\ne j\), cohort \(j\) certainly wins. Conversely, cohort \(j\) can win only if \(T_{j,N}\leq T_{k,N}\) for all \(k\ne j\). Thus
\[ \Pr(T_{j,N}<T_{k,N}\text{ for every }k\ne j) \leq Q_{j,N} \leq \Pr(T_{j,N}\leq T_{k,N}\text{ for every }k\ne j). \]
Both comparison events have boundaries contained in coordinate-equality hyperplanes. The limiting independent exponential variables have zero probability on these hyperplanes. The joint convergence established above therefore makes both bounds converge to the same value:
\[ \Pr(X_j<X_k\text{ for every }k\ne j) =\int_0^\infty \alpha_j e^{-\alpha_jx} \prod_{k\ne j}e^{-\alpha_kx}\,dx =\frac{\alpha_j}{\lambda}. \]
The sandwich proves \(q_j=\alpha_j/\lambda\), including the effect of simultaneous discoveries. For \(\tau=1\), the comparison conditions are vacuous and the same conclusion holds.

This also describes actual cohort allocation shares. If \(C_{j,N}\) counts locations assigned to cohort \(j\), independent assignment gives
\[ C_{j,N}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(d_N,Q_{j,N}). \]
Hence \(\mathbb E[C_{j,N}/d_N]=Q_{j,N}\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(C_{j,N}/d_N)\leq1/(4d_N)\), so \(C_{j,N}/d_N\to q_j\) in probability.

3. Expected benchmark digs. Every agent can dig through period \(T_N\): the treasure belongs to every search set, and \(T_N\leq\min_j a_{j,N}\). Counting all digs in the discovery period therefore gives
\[ B_N=N\,\mathbb E[T_N]. \]

We must justify convergence of the expectation, not merely convergence in distribution. Define
\[ H_N=\sum_j\frac{n_j(N)}{a_{j,N}}. \]
Then
\[ h_NH_N =\sum_j\frac{n_j(N)/N}{a_{j,N}/d_N} \longrightarrow\lambda. \]
For integers \(0\leq t\leq\min_j a_{j,N}\), independence gives
\[ \Pr(T_N>t)=\prod_j(1-t/a_{j,N})^{n_j(N)} \leq e^{-H_Nt}. \]
For larger \(t\), the probability is zero, so the inequality remains valid.

Consequently, for every \(x\geq0\),
\[ \Pr(T_N/h_N>x) \leq e^{H_N}e^{-(h_NH_N)x}. \]
Because \(H_N\to0\) and \(h_NH_N\to\lambda>0\), this is eventually bounded by the integrable function \(e\,e^{-\lambda x/2}\). The pointwise limit of the tail probability is \(e^{-\lambda x}\), by the independent exponential limits. Dominated convergence applied to the tail integral now yields
\[ \frac{\mathbb E[T_N]}{h_N} =\int_0^\infty\Pr(T_N/h_N>x)\,dx \longrightarrow\frac1\lambda. \]
Therefore
\[ \boxed{\frac{B_N}{d_N}\longrightarrow\frac1\lambda}. \]

4. An exact mechanism-cost identity. Fix \(N\), and condition on a realized assignment and its treasure-independent search orders. Write \(d=d_N\), and let \(K_i\) be the number of locations assigned to individual agent \(i\), so \(\sum_iK_i=d\).

The treasure remains uniform among these \(d\) locations. If it has rank \(k\) in agent \(i\)’s assignment, discovery occurs in period \(k\), and agent \(\ell\) makes exactly \(\min(K_\ell,k)\) digs. This counts every dig made during that period. Thus the conditional expected total is
\[ \frac1d\sum_{i=1}^N\sum_{k=1}^{K_i} \sum_{\ell=1}^N\min(K_\ell,k). \]

For nonnegative integers \(a,b\), define \(F(a,b)=\sum_{k=1}^a\min(b,k)\). If \(a\geq b\), direct summation gives
\[ F(a,b)=ab-\frac{b(b-1)}2, \qquad F(b,a)=\frac{b(b+1)}2. \]
Interchanging \(a,b\) handles the other case, proving
\[ F(a,b)+F(b,a)=ab+\min(a,b). \]
Symmetrizing the conditional expectation therefore gives the exact identity
\[ \mathbb E[\text{total mechanism digs}\mid\text{assignment and orders}] =\frac d2+\frac1{2d}\sum_{i,\ell=1}^N\min(K_i,K_\ell). \]

The diagonal terms sum to \(d\), while
\[ \sum_{i,\ell}\min(K_i,K_\ell) \leq\sum_{i,\ell}K_i=Nd. \]
Averaging over assignments and orders yields
\[ \frac{d_N+1}{2}\leq M_N\leq\frac{d_N+N}{2}. \]
Since \(N/d_N\to0\),
\[ \boxed{\frac{M_N}{d_N}\longrightarrow\frac12}. \]
This conclusion holds for any treasure-independent partition into \(N\) assignments; its leading term does not require assumptions about assignment-size concentration.

For the specified mechanism, winning probabilities are preserved exactly: the only agent who can discover a location is its owner, and each possible treasure location is assigned to individual agent \(i\) with probability \(p_i^{(N)}\).

5. Ratio and sharpness. Combining the two cost limits gives
\[ \frac{M_N}{B_N} =\frac{M_N/d_N}{B_N/d_N} \longrightarrow\frac{\lambda}{2}. \]
Because \(r_j\geq R\) and \(\sum_j\mu_j=1\),
\[ \lambda=\sum_j\frac{\mu_j}{r_j}\leq\frac1R. \]
Positivity of every \(\mu_j\) makes equality equivalent to \(r_j=R\) for every \(j\).

The bound based only on \(R\) is attainable. For any fixed \(\tau\geq2\), take a common set \(D_N\) of size \(N^2\) and pairwise disjoint additional sets \(E_{j,N}\), disjoint from \(D_N\), each of size \(\lfloor(R-1)N^2\rfloor+1\). Set \(A_j^{(N)}=D_N\cup E_{j,N}\), and choose integer cohort counts whose fractions converge to the prescribed masses. Then the intersection is exactly \(D_N\), all size ratios converge to \(R\), and the proved formula gives \(\rho=1/(2R)\). When \(\tau=1\), necessarily \(D_N=A_1^{(N)}\), so \(R=r_1=1\), and again equality holds.
