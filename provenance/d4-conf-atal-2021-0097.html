The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(T\) of complete \(3\)-demand budget-additive department types, rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\ge 0\) for \(m\) staff categories, decide whether a Walrasian equilibrium exists and compute one if it does. Represent an allocation by masses \(x_{t,a}\ge 0\) assigned to integral category-count bundles \(a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^m\) with \(\sum_j a_j\le 3\), satisfying \(\sum_a x_{t,a}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,a}a_jx_{t,a}\le q_j\). A supporting price vector \(p\ge 0\) must make every bundle receiving positive mass utility-maximizing for its type, with \(p_j=0\) whenever category \(j\) has slack supply.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) staff categories with binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\ge0\). Each type specifies binary-rational values \(w_{tj}\ge0\) and a valuation cap \(B_t\ge0\). Its complete bundle domain is \(A=\{a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\sum_j a_j\le3\}\), including the empty bundle and repeated units of a category; on this domain, \(v_t(a)=\min\{B_t,\sum_jw_{tj}a_j\}\).
Utility at prices \(p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\) is \(v_t(a)-p\cdot a\); the valuation cap imposes no spending constraint. Demand maximizes this utility over all \(a\in A\), with all ties allowed.
An equilibrium consists of masses \(x_{t,a}\ge0\) and prices \(p\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_a x_{t,a}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,a}a_jx_{t,a}\le q_j\), and \(x_{t,a}>0\Rightarrow a\in\arg\max_{b\in A}(v_t(b)-p\cdot b)\). Whenever \(\sum_{t,a}a_jx_{t,a}<q_j\), require \(p_j=0\).
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is deciding equilibrium existence and computing an equilibrium whenever one exists solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is the existence decision problem \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete equilibrium existence and computation is strongly \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even for identical \(3\)-demand budget-additive valuations ([Deligkas–Melissourgos–Spirakis](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2021/pdfs/p413.pdf)).
For a related continuum model, equilibrium existence is known with arbitrary quasilinear valuations and exact market clearing; that result does not state the computational complexity of this support-encoded problem ([Azevedo–Weyl–White](https://eduardomazevedo.github.io/papers/Azevedo-et-al-Walrasian.pdf)).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Defined a complete department type by nonnegative binary-rational category values and a nonnegative binary-rational valuation cap, with budget-additive values on all permitted bundles.
  • Interpreted the budget as a valuation cap, not an affordability constraint, following the discrete quasilinear utility definition.
  • Retained the graded question's category-count bundles, allowing repeated units of a category and treating all bundles of total count at most three, including the empty bundle, as available choices.
  • Allowed mass to split among any tied utility-maximizing bundles, without prescribed tie-breaking.
  • Removed zero-mass entries from the support and defined L as the total binary input length.

Result

Every valid instance has an equilibrium, and an equilibrium can be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The crucial feature is that each type’s mass may be divided among several demanded bundles. This makes the allocation problem a linear program whose dual supplies equilibrium prices.

1. The bundle domain has polynomial size

Write \(n=|A|\). Introducing a slack coordinate \(a_0=3-\sum_{j=1}^m a_j\) gives a bijection between \(A\) and the nonnegative integer solutions of \(\sum_{j=0}^m a_j=3\). Consequently,

\( n=\binom{m+3}{3}=O(m^3). \)

This count includes the empty bundle and all bundles containing repeated units.

Enumerate these bundles and compute \(v_t(a)=\min\{B_t,\sum_j w_{tj}a_j\}\) for every \(t,a\). Each sum contains at most three units, so every computed valuation has binary encoding length \(O(L)\). Thus the valuation table can be constructed in \(O(\tau m^3\operatorname{poly}(L+\log(m+1)))\) bit operations.

2. The welfare linear program and its dual

Consider the following primal linear program, with variables \(x_{t,a}\):

\( \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad& \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{a\in A}v_t(a)x_{t,a}\\ \text{subject to}\quad& \sum_{a\in A}x_{t,a}=\mu_t &&(t\in T),\\ & \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{a\in A}a_jx_{t,a}\le q_j &&(1\le j\le m),\\ & x_{t,a}\ge0 &&(t\in T,\ a\in A). \end{aligned} \)

This program allocates masses of whole bundles; it does not replace an individual’s bundle by a fractional bundle.

The primal is feasible: set \(x_{t,0}=\mu_t\) and all other variables to zero. Its feasible set is closed and bounded, because \(0\le x_{t,a}\le\mu_t\). Its objective is also bounded explicitly by

\( \sum_{t,a}v_t(a)x_{t,a} \le \sum_t B_t\sum_a x_{t,a} = \sum_t\mu_tB_t. \)

Associate a free dual variable \(u_t\) with each type equality and a nonnegative dual variable \(p_j\) with each supply inequality. The dual is

\( \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad& \sum_{t\in T}\mu_tu_t+\sum_{j=1}^m q_jp_j\\ \text{subject to}\quad& u_t+\sum_{j=1}^m p_ja_j\ge v_t(a) &&(t\in T,\ a\in A),\\ & p_j\ge0 &&(1\le j\le m). \end{aligned} \)

The constraint for the empty bundle implies \(u_t\ge0\), although the equality multiplier was initially unrestricted.

The dual is feasible: take \(p=0\) and \(u_t=B_t\). Strong linear-programming duality applies because the primal is feasible with finite optimum. Hence both programs attain their optima, and their optimal objective values agree.

3. Optimal solutions give an equilibrium

Let \(x\) be any optimal primal solution and \((u,p)\) any optimal dual solution. Define the aggregate allocation of category \(j\) by

\( y_j=\sum_{t,a}a_jx_{t,a}. \)

Using the type equalities, the difference between the dual and primal objective values is

\( \begin{aligned} \sum_t\mu_tu_t+\sum_jq_jp_j-\sum_{t,a}v_t(a)x_{t,a} ={}& \sum_{t,a}x_{t,a}\bigl(u_t+p\cdot a-v_t(a)\bigr)\\ &+\sum_jp_j(q_j-y_j). \end{aligned} \)

Every summand on the right is nonnegative:

  • \(x_{t,a}\ge0\), and dual feasibility gives \(u_t+p\cdot a-v_t(a)\ge0\).
  • \(p_j\ge0\), and primal feasibility gives \(q_j-y_j\ge0\).

Optimality makes the left side zero. Therefore every summand on the right is zero.

Demand optimality. If \(x_{t,a}>0\), then

\( u_t=v_t(a)-p\cdot a. \)

For every \(b\in A\), dual feasibility gives

\( v_t(b)-p\cdot b\le u_t=v_t(a)-p\cdot a. \)

Thus every bundle receiving positive mass maximizes the type’s utility over the complete domain \(A\). Ties cause no difficulty.

Unused supply. For every category \(j\),

\( p_j(q_j-y_j)=0. \)

If \(y_j<q_j\), this equality forces \(p_j=0\).

Together with primal feasibility and \(p\ge0\), these are exactly all the equilibrium conditions in the question. This proves existence for every valid instance, including instances with zero supplies or zero valuations.

The valuation cap enters only through the objective coefficients \(v_t(a)\). No spending constraint is introduced.

4. Exact polynomial-time computation

The primal has

\( N=\tau\binom{m+3}{3}=O(\tau m^3) \)

variables and \(m+\tau\) supply and type constraints, besides nonnegativity. The dual has \(m+\tau\) variables and \(N\) bundle constraints, besides price nonnegativity.

All coefficients are rational with encoding length \(O(L+\log(m+\tau+2))\). For example, a dense encoding of either program has total length bounded by

\( S= O\!\left( (N+1)(m+\tau+1)(L+\log(m+\tau+2)) \right). \)

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a rational LP can be optimized exactly in time polynomial in its total binary encoding length, with a rational optimal solution of polynomial encoding length whenever its finite optimum is attained. The feasibility, finiteness, and attainment hypotheses were verified above for both programs.

The algorithm is therefore:

  • Enumerate \(A\) and compute all \(v_t(a)\).
  • Solve the primal and dual exactly.
  • Return the primal masses \(x_{t,a}\) and dual prices \(p_j\).

Its running time is

\( \operatorname{poly}\!\left( \tau\binom{m+3}{3},\,m+\tau,\,L \right) = \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). \)

The returned masses and prices have polynomial total binary encoding length. No expansion of a type into individual agents, and no enumeration of types outside the listed support, is needed.

For the existence decision problem, every valid input is a yes-instance.

5. Why the discrete hardness does not transfer

The discrete model allocates indivisible items by a partition among finitely many agents. An individual agent must receive one bundle. That integrality requirement is absent from the mass variables in this task. See the allocation and equilibrium definitions in [Deligkas–Melissourgos–Spirakis](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2021/pdfs/p413.pdf).

Here, a positive mass of identical agents can be divided among tied utility-maximizing bundles. This is the convexification mechanism also discussed by [Azevedo–Weyl–White](https://eduardomazevedo.github.io/papers/Azevedo-et-al-Walrasian.pdf). The proof above establishes the computational conclusion directly for the stated support encoding and supply inequalities.
