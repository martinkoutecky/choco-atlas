The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational group masses \(\mu_u\) with \(\sum_{u\in U}\mu_u=1\), capacity mass \(\kappa\), ranked quota masses \(\eta_t^j\), and group targets \(\delta_u\), decide whether there exists \(f_{u,t,j}\ge0\) with \(t\in u\cup\{t_0\}\) such that \(\sum_{t,j}f_{u,t,j}\le\mu_u\), \(\sum_{u:t\in u}f_{u,t,j}\le\eta_t^j\), \(\sum_{u,t,j}f_{u,t,j}\le\kappa\), every group receives at least \(\delta_u\) mass, and \(f\) is rank-maximal by maximizing total selected mass and then lexicographically maximizing \(R_j=\sum_{u,t}f_{u,t,j}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(T\) be a set of \(m\) privilege types. A society is given by its support: a list \(U\subseteq 2^T\) of \(\tau\) distinct groups with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_u\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible type combinations; the empty group is allowed.
Input also includes \(r\ge1\) ordered ranks, nonnegative binary-rational capacity \(\kappa\), quota masses \(\eta_t^j\), and group targets \(\delta_u\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Introduce a general type \(t_0\notin T\), available to every group, with \(\eta_{t_0}^r=\kappa\) and \(\eta_{t_0}^j=0\) for \(j<r\). Smaller ranks have higher priority; quotas are separate capacities for each type and rank.
An allocation consists of real masses \(f_{u,t,j}\ge0\), defined only for \(t\in u\cup\{t_0\}\) and \(1\le j\le r\). Mass may be split among eligible types and ranks, but each portion is counted once.
Write \(a_u=\sum_{t,j}f_{u,t,j}\), \(F=\sum_u a_u\), and \(R_j=\sum_{u,t}f_{u,t,j}\). Feasibility means \(a_u\le\mu_u\), \(\sum_{u:t\in u\cup\{t_0\}}f_{u,t,j}\le\eta_t^j\), and \(F\le\kappa\).
Call an allocation rank-maximal if it lexicographically maximizes \((F,R_1,\ldots,R_r)\) over all feasible allocations, without imposing the group targets. Ties are allowed.
Decide whether a rank-maximal allocation exists with \(a_u\ge\delta_u\) for every \(u\in U\).

Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,r,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Discrete validity checking admits a polynomial-time flow algorithm, as stated by [Sun and Yokoo](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/33547/35702). No result settling this exact rational-mass formulation was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The general type's quotas were unspecified: set its quota to the capacity mass at the largest rank and to zero at every other rank, and include all groups in its quota constraint.
  • The optimization comparison class was ambiguous: impose group targets only on the sought allocation, while defining rank maximality against all feasible allocations without those targets.
  • Unspecified domains were fixed as nonnegative binary-rational capacity, quotas, and targets, with real divisible allocation masses; capacity may exceed total population mass.
  • Groups are distinct privilege-type combinations; the empty combination remains eligible for general seats. The support lists only groups with positive mass.
  • Ranked quotas are separate per-rank capacities rather than cumulative bounds, and allocations tied in the full lexicographic objective are all admissible.

Result

The decision problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,r,L\).

The algorithm computes the lexicographic optimum without group targets, retains every allocation attaining that optimum, and then checks whether any such allocation satisfies the targets. Exact rational linear programming suffices; below we also prove that all intermediate optimum values have polynomial encoding length.

1. A polynomial-size allocation polytope

Let \(E=\{(u,t,j):u\in U,\ t\in u\cup\{t_0\},\ 1\le j\le r\}\). There are

\(n=|E|=r\sum_{u\in U}(|u|+1)\le r\tau(m+1)\)

allocation variables.

Let \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^E\) be defined by precisely the feasibility constraints in the question:

  • \(f_{u,t,j}\ge0\) for every \((u,t,j)\in E\);
  • \(\sum_{t\in u\cup\{t_0\}}\sum_{j=1}^r f_{u,t,j}\le\mu_u\) for every \(u\in U\);
  • \(\sum_{u:(u,t,j)\in E}f_{u,t,j}\le\eta_t^j\) for every type and rank;
  • \(\sum_{(u,t,j)\in E}f_{u,t,j}\le\kappa\).

Thus \(P\) has \(n+\tau+(m+1)r+1\) inequalities and a polynomial-size rational description. Its variables refer only to the supplied support \(U\); no enumeration of \(2^T\) is required.

The zero allocation belongs to \(P\). Moreover, nonnegativity and the group constraints imply \(0\le f_{u,t,j}\le\mu_u\le1\). Consequently, \(P\) is a nonempty compact polytope.

Define linear objectives \(c_0(f)=F(f)\) and \(c_j(f)=R_j(f)\) for \(1\le j\le r\). Every objective coefficient is either zero or one.

As a useful check, \(\max_{f\in P}F(f)=\min\{\kappa,1\}\). The upper bound follows from total capacity and \(\sum_u\mu_u=1\). To attain it, put \(s=\min\{\kappa,1\}\), assign \(f_{u,t_0,r}=s\mu_u\), and set every other variable to zero. All group constraints hold because \(s\le1\), and the general-type quota and total capacity hold because the total assigned mass is \(s\le\kappa\).

2. Compute the entire set of rank-maximal allocations

Set \(P_{-1}=P\). For \(i=0,1,\ldots,r\), sequentially compute

\(\alpha_i=\max\{c_i(f):f\in P_{i-1}\}\),

and define

\(P_i=\{f\in P_{i-1}:c_i(f)=\alpha_i\}\).

Each step maximizes a linear function over a nonempty compact polytope. Hence its maximum is attained, and \(P_i\) remains nonempty and compact. Each step adds only one equality.

Claim. \(P_r\) is exactly the set of rank-maximal allocations.

Proof. If \(f\in P_r\), then \(c_i(f)=\alpha_i\) for every \(i\). Suppose a feasible allocation \(g\) had a lexicographically larger objective vector, and let \(i\) be its first differing coordinate. Its preceding coordinates equal \(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{i-1}\), so \(g\in P_{i-1}\). But \(c_i(g)>\alpha_i\), contradicting the definition of \(\alpha_i\).

Conversely, suppose \(f\in P\setminus P_r\). Let \(i\) be the first index for which \(f\notin P_i\). Then \(f\in P_{i-1}\), and therefore \(c_i(f)<\alpha_i\). Any \(g\in P_i\) agrees with \(f\) on all preceding coordinates and has a larger coordinate at index \(i\). Thus \(f\) is not rank-maximal. This proves the claim.

Now check feasibility of the rational linear system consisting of \(f\in P_r\) and

\(\sum_{t\in u\cup\{t_0\}}\sum_{j=1}^r f_{u,t,j}\ge\delta_u\quad\text{for every }u\in U.\)

By the claim, this system is feasible exactly when the answer to the original question is yes. In particular, the targets enter only this final feasibility check.

3. Exact bit complexity

We use the standard polynomial-time rational linear-programming theorem: rational LP feasibility is decidable in polynomial time in the binary encoding length, and a finite attained optimum can be computed exactly in polynomial time. See [Karger’s notes on polynomial-time LP algorithms](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html).

To apply this theorem repeatedly, we must bound the encoding lengths of the added values \(\alpha_i\). We give a uniform bound using vertices of the original polytope \(P\).

Write \(P=\{f:Af\le b\}\), including nonnegativity among these inequalities. Every entry of \(A\) belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all numerical input entries, and put \(B=\max\{1,\max_\ell|Db_\ell|\}\). Both \(D\) and \(B\) have binary encoding length \(O(L)\).

At any vertex \(v\) of \(P\), the active constraint normals span \(\mathbb R^n\). Indeed, otherwise a nonzero direction orthogonal to every active normal would allow sufficiently small perturbations in both directions while preserving all inequalities, contradicting that \(v\) is a vertex.

We can therefore select \(n\) independent active constraints, giving \(Mv=b'\), where \(M\) is a nonsingular integer matrix with entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). Let \(M_k\) be obtained by replacing column \(k\) of \(M\) by \(Db'\). Cramer’s rule gives

\(v_k=\dfrac{\det M_k}{D\det M}.\)

Hadamard’s determinant inequality gives

\(1\le|\det M|\le n^{n/2}\quad\text{and}\quad|\det M_k|\le Bn^{n/2}.\)

The second bound follows because the replacement column has Euclidean norm at most \(B\sqrt n\), and every other column has norm at most \(\sqrt n\).

For any objective \(c_i\), its coefficients are zero or one. Thus \(c_i(v)\) has denominator \(D\det M\) and a numerator of absolute value at most \(nBn^{n/2}\). Its rational encoding length is consequently

\(O\!\left(L+n\log(n+1)\right).\)

It remains to justify applying this bound to every \(\alpha_i\). Each \(P_i\) is the set maximizing a linear function over \(P_{i-1}\), so it is a face of \(P_{i-1}\). A vertex of such a face is also a vertex of the containing polytope: if that vertex were a nontrivial convex combination of two points of the containing polytope, maximality and linearity would force both points into the maximizing face, contradicting extremality there.

Inductively, every vertex of \(P_i\) is a vertex of \(P\). Since a nonempty compact polytope has a vertex, choose one in \(P_i\); at that vertex, \(c_i=\alpha_i\). Hence every \(\alpha_i\) has the uniform polynomial encoding bound above. There is no successive growth beyond that bound.

Each intermediate LP therefore has polynomially many variables and constraints, with polynomially encoded rational coefficients. The final target system has the same property.

4. Conclusion

The procedure uses at most \(r+1\) exact LP optimizations followed by one exact LP feasibility check. Its total running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,r,L\), and a feasible final LP also supplies a rational rank-maximal allocation meeting all targets.

This proof directly settles the rational-mass formulation. It does not require extending the discrete flow algorithm of [Sun and Yokoo, Theorem 5](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/33547/35702).
