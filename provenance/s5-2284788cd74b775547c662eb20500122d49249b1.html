The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite worker-type and task-type supports \(R\) and \(Q\), rational mass distributions \(\mu\) and \(\nu\), population totals \(N_W\) and \(N_T\), and a fixed nondecreasing price table \(P\) with exactly two distinct values, find a feasible rational assignment of mass \(x_{r,q}\ge 0\) maximizing \(\sum_{r,q} P(s,\ell)x_{r,q}\), where \(q=(s,\ell,c)\). Enforce worker and task mass capacities \(N_W\mu_r\) and \(N_T\nu_q\), qualification \(r_s\ge\ell\), and affordability \(P(s,\ell)\le c\). For every eligible pair \((r,q)\), require that the mass of type-\(r\) workers unmatched or assigned below price \(P(s,\ell)\) and the mass of type-\(q\) tasks unmatched or assigned to workers with skill level below \(r_s\) cannot both be positive; determine the complexity in the support sizes and binary input length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge1\) be the number of skills. Worker types are \(r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\); task types are \(q=(s,\ell,c)\), where \(s\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), \(\ell\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and \(c\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\).
Each society is given by its support: a list of types with positive binary rational masses. The nonempty supports \(R,Q\) carry distributions \(\mu,\nu\), each summing to \(1\); write \(\tau_W=|R|\), \(\tau_T=|Q|\), and \(\tau=\tau_W+\tau_T\). These are support sizes, not numbers of possible types.
Population totals \(N_W,N_T\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) are supplied in binary. A prescribed input price table \(P\) has exactly two distinct positive rational values, includes every task's skill-level pair, and is nondecreasing in level within each skill. Write \(p_q=P(s,\ell)\).
An eligible pair \((r,q)\) satisfies \(r_s\ge\ell\) and \(p_q\le c\). An assignment has \(x_{r,q}\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), zero on ineligible pairs, with \(\sum_{q\in Q}x_{r,q}\le N_W\mu_r\) and \(\sum_{r\in R}x_{r,q}\le N_T\nu_q\).
Define unmatched masses \(u_r=N_W\mu_r-\sum_{q\in Q}x_{r,q}\) and \(v_q=N_T\nu_q-\sum_{r\in R}x_{r,q}\).
The assignment is stable if every eligible \((r,q)\), with \(q=(s,\ell,c)\), satisfies \(\bigl(u_r+\sum_{q'\in Q:p_{q'}<p_q}x_{r,q'}\bigr)\bigl(v_q+\sum_{r'\in R:r'_s<r_s}x_{r',q}\bigr)=0\). Equal prices and equal relevant skill levels are indifferences; no tie-breaking is imposed.
The optimization problem is to find a feasible stable assignment maximizing \(\sum_{r\in R,q\in Q}p_qx_{r,q}\).

Is this continuous revenue-maximization problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau_W\), \(\tau_T\), and the total binary input length \(L\).

For finite individual workers and tasks, revenue-maximizing stable allocation under prescribed prices is NP-hard even with two distinct prices ([Xia–Muthukrishnan](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/13299/13299-64-16816-1-2-20201228.pdf)).
Maximum-welfare ordinally stable fractional matching is NP-hard for general bipartite preferences with ties ([Chen–Roy–Sorge](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0013.pdf)); this broader result does not settle the stated skill-and-price restriction. No result settling this exact mass problem was identified.

Result

The continuous problem is NP-hard when \(m\) is part of the input. The finite hardness result stated in the question transfers exactly: allowing fractional assignments does not improve the optimum when all type capacities are integers.

The key point is that the product stability condition describes a finite union of transportation polytopes, each of which has integral vertices under integer capacities.

1. Fixing the stability choices

Write
\[ a_r=N_W\mu_r,\qquad b_q=N_T\nu_q, \]
and let \(E\) be the set of eligible pairs. For \((r,q)\in E\), with \(q=(s,\ell,c)\), define
\[ A_{r,q}(x)=u_r+\sum_{q':p_{q'}<p_q}x_{r,q'}, \qquad B_{r,q}(x)=v_q+\sum_{r':r'_s<r_s}x_{r',q}. \]

Both quantities are nonnegative for every feasible assignment. Consequently, stability is equivalent to requiring, for each eligible pair, at least one of
\[ A_{r,q}(x)=0,\qquad B_{r,q}(x)=0. \]

Because these expressions are sums of nonnegative terms, their vanishing has the following exact interpretations:

  • \(A_{r,q}(x)=0\) requires \(\sum_{q'}x_{r,q'}=a_r\) and \(x_{r,q'}=0\) whenever \(p_{q'}<p_q\).
  • \(B_{r,q}(x)=0\) requires \(\sum_{r'}x_{r',q}=b_q\) and \(x_{r',q}=0\) whenever \(r'_s<r_s\).

A branch chooses one of these two requirements for every eligible pair. Its feasible set therefore consists of the original capacity constraints, some additional zero entries, and some rows or columns required to attain their capacities.

Every point in a branch is stable. Conversely, every stable assignment belongs to at least one branch.

2. Integral rounding within a branch

Lemma. Suppose every \(a_r\) and \(b_q\) is an integer. For every feasible stable assignment \(x\), there is a feasible stable assignment \(y\) with integer entries and revenue at least that of \(x\).

Proof. Choose a branch containing \(x\). Delete all assignment edges required to have zero flow, and record its forced-full rows and columns.

Set \(U=\sum_r a_r\). Construct a circulation network with source vertex \(o\), sink vertex \(t\), and one vertex for each worker and task type:

  • The arc \(o\to r\) has upper capacity \(a_r\). Its lower bound is \(a_r\) for a forced-full row, and zero otherwise.
  • Each remaining assignment edge becomes an arc \(r\to q\), with lower bound zero and upper capacity \(U\).
  • The arc \(q\to t\) has upper capacity \(b_q\). Its lower bound is \(b_q\) for a forced-full column, and zero otherwise.
  • The arc \(t\to o\) has lower bound zero and upper capacity \(U\).

Flow conservation makes the flows on \(r\to q\) exactly the assignment variables in the chosen branch. Thus branch assignments and feasible circulations correspond bijectively. Maximize
\[ \sum_{r\to q}p_q f_{r,q}. \]

All arc bounds are integers. We verify that every vertex of this circulation polytope is integral.

Suppose a feasible circulation has a noninteger arc flow. Consider the undirected subgraph consisting of its noninteger-flow arcs. No vertex can have exactly one incident such arc: conservation would equate that noninteger flow, with a sign, to an integer combination of all other incident flows. Hence every nonisolated vertex of this subgraph has degree at least two, so the subgraph contains a cycle.

Perturb flow around that cycle, adding or subtracting according to each arc’s orientation. This preserves conservation. Every affected flow lies strictly between its integer bounds, so sufficiently small perturbations in both directions preserve all bounds. The original circulation is therefore not a vertex.

The circulation polytope is nonempty and bounded, so its linear objective attains a maximum at a vertex. That vertex is integral and has objective at least that of \(x\). Its corresponding assignment \(y\) remains in the chosen branch and is therefore stable. \(\square\)

In particular, with integer capacities, the fractional and integral stable revenue optima coincide. The lemma does not require two prices; it applies to the stated product condition with any prescribed prices.

3. Reduction from finite individuals

Use the finite individual hardness theorem supplied in the question: revenue maximization under the same skill-and-price preferences is NP-hard even with two prescribed price values. This is Theorem 9 of [Xia–Muthukrishnan](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/13299/13299-64-16816-1-2-20201228.pdf).

Take such a finite instance with \(n_W\) workers and \(n_T\) tasks. Group identical worker types and identical task types. Let their multiplicities be \(a_r\) and \(b_q\), respectively. Construct the mass instance by setting
\[ N_W=n_W,\qquad \mu_r=\frac{a_r}{n_W}, \qquad N_T=n_T,\qquad \nu_q=\frac{b_q}{n_T}. \]

Retain the original skills, types, and price table. Thus eligibility, both price values, and price monotonicity are preserved. The distributions are positive rational distributions, and the resulting type capacities are exactly the integer multiplicities.

We now verify preservation of stability in both directions.

Aggregating a finite stable matching

For a finite matching \(M\), let \(x_{r,q}\) count its matched pairs of types \(r,q\).

For an eligible type pair \((r,q)\), \(A_{r,q}(x)>0\) means that some individual worker of type \(r\) is unmatched or assigned to a strictly cheaper task. Likewise, \(B_{r,q}(x)>0\) means that some individual task of type \(q\) is unmatched or assigned to a worker with strictly lower relevant skill.

If both quantities were positive, these two witnessing individuals would form an eligible blocking pair. Hence a stable \(M\) aggregates to a stable \(x\). Revenue is unchanged.

Expanding an integral stable assignment

Conversely, let \(y\) be an integral feasible stable type assignment. For each \((r,q)\), match \(y_{r,q}\) distinct workers of type \(r\) to distinct tasks of type \(q\). The row and column bounds ensure that this can be done without reusing individuals.

If the resulting finite matching had a blocking pair of types \(r,q\), its worker would contribute positively to \(A_{r,q}(y)\), and its task would contribute positively to \(B_{r,q}(y)\). This contradicts stability of \(y\). Again, revenue is unchanged.

Together with the rounding lemma, these implications give
\[ \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}} = \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{finite}}. \]

Grouping types and encoding their multiplicities takes polynomial time in the finite input length. Therefore an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau_W,\tau_T,L\) for the mass problem would solve the supplied NP-hard finite problem in polynomial time. This proves the claimed hardness.

4. Binary encoding and running times

The reduction uses \(\tau_W\le n_W\) and \(\tau_T\le n_T\); it does not interpret support sizes as counts of possible types.

For arbitrary rational capacities, choose
\[ D= \operatorname{den}(N_W)\operatorname{den}(N_T) \prod_{r\in R}\operatorname{den}(\mu_r) \prod_{q\in Q}\operatorname{den}(\nu_q), \]
where denominators are taken in reduced form. Its binary length is \(O(L)\), and all \(Da_r,Db_q\) are integers.

Scaling assignments and capacities by \(D\) multiplies each stability product by \(D^2\), preserving stability. Applying the lemma after scaling shows that an optimal assignment can be chosen with every entry an integer multiple of \(1/D\). Each such entry has polynomial binary length.

Stable assignments also exist: the scaled integer instance can conceptually be expanded into finitely many individuals, ties refined, and deferred acceptance applied. A matching stable under the refinement has no pair that strictly blocks under the original preferences, and its aggregation is stable. This expansion is only an existence argument, not part of the algorithm below.

An exact algorithm works directly with the encoded capacities:

  • Enumerate the at most \(2^{|E|}\) branches.
  • Solve the associated maximum-cost circulation for each feasible branch.
  • Return the best solution, divided by \(D\) if scaling was used.

Each network has \(\tau_W+\tau_T+2\) vertices and at most \(|E|+\tau_W+\tau_T+1\) arcs. Polynomial-time circulation algorithms operate on the binary encoding, so the resulting running time is
\[ 2^{|E|}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau_W,\tau_T,L) \;\le\; 2^{\tau_W\tau_T}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau_W,\tau_T,L). \]

Moreover, the revenue-threshold decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\): a yes-instance has a polynomial-size rational assignment certificate, and feasibility, every stability product, and revenue can all be checked by exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

Thus the requested classification is NP-hard, even with exactly two prescribed prices; a polynomial-time exact algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
