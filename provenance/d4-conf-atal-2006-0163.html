The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite item set \(I\), a rational distribution \(\mu\) over positive-valued single-minded incumbent bid types \(t=(B_t,v_t)\), and two explicit colluders, can the colluders win all items with zero VCG payment?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Consider a forward auction with free disposal on an item set \(I\), where \(m=|I|\geq 2\) and each item has unit supply. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each incumbent type is \(t=(B_t,v_t)\), with \(\varnothing\neq B_t\subseteq I\), positive rational \(v_t\), and mass \(\mu_t>0\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Serving mass \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) consumes \(x_t\) units of every item in \(B_t\) and contributes welfare \(v_tx_t\).
Two explicit colluders may each submit one bid \((C_c,w_c)\), choosing a nonempty bundle \(C_c\subseteq I\) and an unrestricted nonnegative rational value \(w_c\). Their acceptance variables are \(z_c\in\{0,1\}\); accepting a colluder consumes one unit of every item in its bundle.
Winner determination maximizes \(a=\sum_t v_tx_t+\sum_{c=1}^{2}w_cz_c\), subject to \(\sum_{t:s\in B_t}x_t+\sum_{c:s\in C_c}z_c\leq1\) for every \(s\in I\). Among maximizers, choose the lexicographically largest \((z_1,z_2,x_1,\ldots,x_\tau)\), using the input order of types.
Let \(a_{-c}\) be the maximum welfare after removing colluder \(c\), leaving the incumbent society unchanged. Its Clarke-pivot payment is \(p_c=a_{-c}-(a-w_cz_c)\). The decision problem asks whether bids exist for which \(z_1=z_2=1\), \(C_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_2=I\), and \(p_1=p_2=0\); no self-enforcement requirement is imposed. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is this continuous zero-payment collusion decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite incumbent bidders, the corresponding two-colluder decision problem is NP-complete: partitioning items into two parts so that no positive incumbent bundle lies wholly in either part is NP-complete. [Conitzer–Sandholm](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/vcgfailuresAAMAS06.pdf)
No result settling the exact mass-incumbent problem defined here was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized incumbent masses to sum to one because the question specifies a distribution; values are positive binary rationals.
  • Specified the continuous allocation convention: accepted incumbent mass consumes that same quantity of every item in its demanded bundle, with welfare linear in accepted mass and unit supply per item.
  • Kept the two explicit colluders as individual, all-or-nothing bidders, each choosing one bundle and an unrestricted nonnegative rational bid value, following the discrete single-minded bidding model.
  • Excluded empty demanded bundles and required at least two items, avoiding empty-bundle positive bids and allowing both colluders to receive nonempty bundles.
  • Interpreted winning all items as acceptance of both colluders on disjoint bundles whose union is the item set, with each colluder paying zero.
  • Specified free disposal and Clarke-pivot payments computed by removing only the relevant explicit colluder.
  • Fixed lexicographic tie-breaking using colluder order followed by incumbent input order.
  • Retained ordinary collusion without adding a self-enforcement or true-valuation constraint.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-complete}\), even when every incumbent value equals \(1\) and every mass is dyadic. Thus a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The key characterization is exact: successful bids exist if and only if the items admit a partition into two nonempty parts such that every incumbent bundle intersects both parts.

1. Incumbent welfare on available items

For \(S\subseteq I\), define
\[ V(S)=\sum_{t:B_t\subseteq S}\mu_t v_t, \qquad V=V(I). \]

This is exactly the maximum incumbent welfare when only the items in \(S\) are available.

Indeed, a type whose bundle is not contained in \(S\) cannot receive positive mass. All remaining types can simultaneously receive their full masses: for every available item \(s\),
\[ \sum_{t:B_t\subseteq S,\ s\in B_t}\mu_t \leq \sum_t\mu_t=1. \]
This allocation attains \(V(S)\). Conversely, the upper bounds \(x_t\leq\mu_t\) imply that no feasible allocation exceeds \(V(S)\).

Because every \(\mu_t v_t\) is strictly positive,
\[ V(S)=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{no incumbent bundle is contained in }S. \]
Also, \(V>0\).

2. Exact characterization of successful bids

Fix a partition \(C_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_2=I\) with both parts nonempty.

If both colluders are accepted, every item’s entire supply is consumed. Since every incumbent bundle is nonempty, this forces \(x_t=0\) for every type. Consequently,
\[ a=w_1+w_2. \]

After removing colluder \(1\), there are two possibilities. Rejecting colluder \(2\) yields maximum welfare \(V\). Accepting colluder \(2\) leaves exactly \(C_1\) available to incumbents and yields maximum welfare \(w_2+V(C_1)\). Therefore,
\[ a_{-1}=\max\{V,w_2+V(C_1)\}, \]
and, conditional on both colluders being accepted,
\[ p_1=\max\{V-w_2,V(C_1)\}. \]
Likewise,
\[ p_2=\max\{V-w_1,V(C_2)\}. \]

Since \(V(C_1)\) and \(V(C_2)\) are nonnegative, zero payments require
\[ V(C_1)=V(C_2)=0, \qquad w_1\geq V,\qquad w_2\geq V. \]
In particular, every incumbent bundle must intersect both parts.

Conversely, suppose every incumbent bundle intersects both parts. Then \(V(C_1)=V(C_2)=0\). Choose the rational bids
\[ w_1=w_2=V. \]
Accepting both colluders yields welfare \(2V\). Accepting exactly one yields welfare \(V\), because no incumbent can be served alongside that colluder. Accepting neither yields welfare \(V\). Since \(V>0\), every maximizing allocation accepts both colluders. Thus the specified tie-breaking rule also accepts both.

The payment formulas now give \(p_1=p_2=0\).

We have proved:
\[ \text{successful bids exist} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{there is a nonempty two-part partition splitting every }B_t. \]

3. Polynomial-time reduction establishing hardness

Use the known \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-complete}\) problem of partitioning a ground set into two parts so that no listed subset lies wholly in either part. This is DIVIDE-SUBSETS with two parts, equivalently hypergraph two-colorability; see Theorem 3 of [Conitzer–Sandholm](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/vcgfailuresAAMAS06.pdf).

We may assume the ground set \(I\) has at least two items and the family consists of \(q\geq1\) distinct nonempty subsets \(B_1,\ldots,B_q\). These restrictions preserve hardness: instances with an empty subset, no subsets, or fewer than two items are directly decidable and can be replaced by fixed instances satisfying the restrictions. For example, on two items \(a,b\), the family consisting of \(\{a,b\}\) is a yes-instance, whereas the family consisting of \(\{a\}\) is a no-instance. Duplicate subsets can be deleted.

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two strictly greater than \(q\). Construct the society as follows:

  • For each \(B_j\), create type \((B_j,1)\) with mass \(1/N\).
  • Add type \((I,1)\) with mass \((N-q)/N\).
  • If \((I,1)\) already occurs, merge its two masses into one support entry.

Every resulting mass is a positive dyadic rational, all values equal \(1\), and the masses sum to
\[ \frac{q}{N}+\frac{N-q}{N}=1. \]
Merging preserves these properties. The support has at most \(q+1\) entries.

The additional bundle \(I\) intersects both parts of every nonempty partition. Consequently, the constructed society admits a partition splitting all its bundles if and only if the original family does. Because \(q\geq1\), any partition splitting the original family necessarily has both parts nonempty.

By the characterization in Section 2, the original instance is therefore a yes-instance if and only if the constructed auction admits successful zero-payment bids.

Finally, \(N\leq2q\), so each mass requires only \(O(\log q)\) bits. The construction and its output length are polynomial in the source input length. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hardness}\) under all the stated auction hypotheses.

4. Membership and conclusion

A certificate is an assignment of each item to one of two parts. Verify that both parts are nonempty and that every listed incumbent bundle contains an item from each part. This requires time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Section 2 proves that this verification accepts exactly the yes-instances. No unbounded bid values or continuous allocations need to be included in the certificate.

Hence the continuous zero-payment collusion decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-complete}\).
