The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite country set \(C\), refugee types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita country quotas \(q_c\), and strict preferences over countries for each type and over types for each country, with every pair acceptable, construct a stable mass assignment \(x_{tc}\ge 0\) maximizing \(\sum_{t,c}x_{tc}\). Require \(\sum_c x_{tc}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{tc}\le q_c\); stability means no type has positive mass assigned to a less-preferred country or left unassigned while a country has spare capacity or assigns positive mass to a lower-ranked type.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a nonempty country set, with \(m=|C|\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each country has a nonnegative binary-rational capacity \(q_c\), measured in the same mass units. Each type strictly ranks all countries, and each country strictly ranks all supported types; every pair is acceptable, and assignment is preferred to being unassigned.
An assignment is a real matrix \(x\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_c x_{tc}\leq\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{tc}\leq q_c\). Mass may be split among countries.
A pair \((t,c)\) blocks \(x\) precisely when both conditions hold: \(\sum_d x_{td}<\mu_t\) or \(x_{td}>0\) for some \(d\) with \(c\succ_t d\); and \(\sum_s x_{sc}<q_c\) or \(x_{sc}>0\) for some \(s\) with \(t\succ_c s\). Stability excludes every blocking pair, including pairs already carrying positive mass.
The computational problem is to construct an exact rational stable assignment maximizing \(\sum_{t,c}x_{tc}\). Let \(L\) denote the total input bit length.

Is this maximum-mass stable assignment problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite college-admissions instances with complete acceptability, stable matchings of maximum cardinality exist, and deferred acceptance constructs one ([van Basshuysen, 2017](https://doi.org/10.3390/g8040041)).
For real-valued stable allocation with strict preferences, vertex quotas, and pair capacities, a strongly polynomial construction algorithm is known ([Baïou–Balinski, corrected version, 2002](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302)); this result concerns construction of a stable allocation.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted per-capita quotas as nonnegative capacities in units of the normalized total refugee mass, without requiring their sum to equal one.
  • Made complete acceptability include preference for assignment over remaining unassigned.
  • Applied blocking to every type-country pair, including pairs with positive assigned mass; divisibility permits increasing an existing assignment.
  • Specified real feasible allocations and exact rational computational output, with total input bit length L.

Result

The problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Every stable assignment has total mass \(\min\{1,\sum_{c\in C}q_c\}\), so constructing any stable assignment solves the optimization problem.

1. Stability forces maximum feasible mass

Write \(Q=\sum_{c\in C}q_c\) and \(M(x)=\sum_{t\in T,c\in C}x_{tc}\). Feasibility gives \(M(x)\leq 1\) and \(M(x)\leq Q\).

Suppose a stable assignment satisfied \(M(x)<\min\{1,Q\}\). Since
\(\sum_t\mu_t=1>M(x)\), some type \(t\) would have \(\sum_c x_{tc}<\mu_t\). Likewise, since \(Q>M(x)\), some country \(c\) would have \(\sum_t x_{tc}<q_c\).

For this pair \((t,c)\), both blocking conditions hold through their unused-capacity alternatives. Complete acceptability therefore makes \((t,c)\) a blocking pair, contradicting stability. This argument applies whether or not \(x_{tc}\) is positive.

Consequently, every stable assignment satisfies
\(M(x)=\min\{1,Q\}\). It remains to construct one with exact rational entries in polynomial time.

2. Exact reduction to stable allocation

For each feasible assignment and pair \((t,c)\), define the amounts assigned to partners ranked at least as highly as the specified partner:

\(A_{tc}(x)=x_{tc}+\sum_{d:\,d\succ_t c}x_{td}\),

\(B_{tc}(x)=x_{tc}+\sum_{s:\,s\succ_c t}x_{sc}\).

Because preferences are strict and complete,
\(\mu_t-A_{tc}(x)=\bigl(\mu_t-\sum_d x_{td}\bigr)+\sum_{d:\,c\succ_t d}x_{td}\).

Every term on the right is nonnegative. Thus the type’s blocking condition holds exactly when \(A_{tc}(x)<\mu_t\). Similarly,
\(q_c-B_{tc}(x)=\bigl(q_c-\sum_s x_{sc}\bigr)+\sum_{s:\,t\succ_c s}x_{sc}\),

so the country’s blocking condition holds exactly when \(B_{tc}(x)<q_c\). Therefore the specified stability condition is precisely:

For every pair \((t,c)\), either \(A_{tc}(x)=\mu_t\) or \(B_{tc}(x)=q_c\).

The Baïou–Balinski construction theorem applies to finite bipartite allocation instances with strict preferences, positive vertex quotas, and nonnegative pair capacities. Its stability criterion requires, on every pair below its pair capacity, saturation of at least one endpoint by partners ranked at least as highly. Its inductive algorithm constructs such an allocation using polynomially many arithmetic operations and comparisons. See [the corrected paper, Section 2, Theorem 2 and Corollary 1](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

To apply it, let \(C_+=\{c\in C:q_c>0\}\). If \(C_+\) is empty, return the zero matrix: no country can satisfy its blocking condition.

Otherwise, use types as one side, countries in \(C_+\) as the other, vertex quotas \(\mu_t\) and \(q_c\), and the given strict preferences restricted to these vertices. Give every pair capacity \(2\).

These pair capacities impose no additional feasibility restriction: every feasible entry satisfies \(x_{tc}\leq\mu_t\leq 1<2\). Consequently, every pair is below its pair capacity, and the theorem’s stability criterion is exactly the criterion proved above.

Finally, restore zero columns for countries outside \(C_+\). Such a country has neither unused capacity nor positive assigned mass, so no pair involving it can block. The returned matrix is therefore stable under the original definition, including its treatment of pairs carrying positive mass.

3. Exact rational output and bit complexity

The reduced instance has \(\tau\) type vertices, at most \(m\) country vertices, and at most \(m\tau\) pairs. Its encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

For completeness, exact arithmetic does not require expanding rational masses into individual agents. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses and capacities. Its binary length is bounded by the sum of their denominator lengths, hence is \(O(L)\). Multiplying all quotas and pair capacities by \(D\) produces integer data of polynomial bit length.

The inductive algorithm’s numerical operations are additions, subtractions, minima, and comparisons; hence integer input remains integer throughout. These operations are specified in [Section 2 of the corrected paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

Let \(P\) bound its number of arithmetic operations, polynomially in \(m,\tau\). Each addition or subtraction increases the maximum bit length by at most one; minima and comparisons do not increase it. Thus all intermediate integers have bit length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and the entire computation takes polynomial bit time.

Divide the resulting entries by \(D\). Uniform positive scaling preserves feasibility, strict deficit inequalities, and positivity, so this yields an exact rational stable assignment for the original instance. Section 1 proves that its mass is optimal.
