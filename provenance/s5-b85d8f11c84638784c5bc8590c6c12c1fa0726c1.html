The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of social roles \(R\), a rational mass distribution \(\mu\) over complete agent types (including role and initial signal), a mass-dependent role interaction kernel, and signal laws with variances \(\sigma_r^2\), choose role-level conformity parameters \(\delta_r\in(-1,1)\) so the induced continuum opinion dynamics converge and the expected squared error of the limiting consensus from the true state is minimized. Determine an optimal vector and its error.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An atomless society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types \((r_i,z_i)\), with role \(r_i\in R\), rational initial signal \(z_i\), and positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_i\), where \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\). Here \(m=|R|\), every role has positive mass, and \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Write \(p_r=\sum_{i:r_i=r}\mu_i\). The conditional signal law in role \(r\) assigns probability \(\mu_i/p_r\) to \(z_i\); it has common mean \(\theta\in\mathbb Q\) and positive variance \(\sigma_r^2\). These are exact population proportions, with no additional random aggregate shocks.
The interaction input consists of nonnegative binary-rational affinities \(a_{rs}\) and binary-rational self-confidence weights \(d_r\in(0,1)\). The directed role graph is strongly connected, and \(\sum_s a_{rs}p_s>0\) for every role. Define the mass-dependent peer kernel \(k_{rs}=a_{rs}/\sum_u a_{ru}p_u\).
Choose real role-level conformity parameters \(\delta_r\in(-1,1)\). Initially \(x_i(0)=z_i\). At each integer time \(t\geq0\), expressed opinions are the unique simultaneous solution of \(s_i(t)=(1-\delta_{r_i})x_i(t)+\delta_{r_i}q_{r_i}(t)\), where \(q_r(t)=\sum_j k_{r,r_j}\mu_j s_j(t)\).
True opinions then update by \(x_i(t+1)=d_{r_i}x_i(t)+(1-d_{r_i})q_{r_i}(t)\). Peer averages include other agents of the same type and role; an individual's own atomless mass contributes nothing.
A vector is admissible if these dynamics converge to a common consensus for every real initial type-opinion vector. For the supplied signals, denote that consensus by \(c_\delta\) and minimize \(\operatorname{MSE}(\delta)=\mathbb E[(c_\delta-\theta)^2]\), with expectation under the fixed population law above. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including any threshold.

Can an optimal admissible conformity vector and its minimum MSE be computed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is deciding whether some admissible vector has MSE at most a given rational \(b\) NP-hard?

For finite agents with independent unbiased signals and positive variances, individually adjustable conformity attains minimum MSE \((\sum_i\sigma_i^{-2})^{-1}\) within a closed strongly connected group, with equality precisely when consensus influence times signal variance is constant ([Buechel–Hellmann–Klößner](https://www.unifr.ch/amabe/en/assets/public/documents/articles/buechel__hellmann__kloessner_dec14_-_opinion_dynamics.pdf)).
For symmetric continuum interactions, convergence of the opinion distribution is known ([Hendrickx–Olshevsky](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.0355)); this concerns a different dynamics. No result settling the exact optimization problem defined here was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one and identified m with the number of supported roles.
  • Interpreted the supplied masses over complete role-and-signal types as exact population proportions. Conditional signal laws have the common unbiased mean required by the discrete model, positive variances, and no additional aggregate randomness.
  • Represented the unspecified mass-dependent kernel by rational nonnegative role affinities normalized against role masses.
  • Retained separate positive self-confidence weights below one, preserving the distinction between an individual's true opinion and peers' expressed opinions.
  • Allowed interactions with other members of the same type and role; excluded only the individual herself, whose mass is zero.
  • Required a strongly connected role graph to represent one closed strongly connected group with a single consensus.
  • Required convergence for every initial type-opinion vector, rather than convergence only for the supplied signal realization.
  • Added a rational MSE threshold to make the hardness side of the exact optimization question precise.

Result

The problem as defined is polynomial-time solvable. An optimal admissible vector is \(\delta_r=0\) for every role, and the minimum MSE is exactly \(0\). The threshold decision therefore answers yes exactly when \(b\geq 0\).

The fixed population law and common conditional mean make the objective degenerate. The substantive issue is proving that the proposed vector is admissible for every initial type-opinion vector. We prove this below.

1. Reduction to role means

Define the role transition matrix by

\(P_{rs}=k_{rs}p_s=\dfrac{a_{rs}p_s}{\sum_u a_{ru}p_u}\).

Every denominator is positive by hypothesis. Thus \(P\) is nonnegative and row-stochastic. Since every \(p_s>0\), its positive entries have exactly the same pattern as the affinities, so its directed graph is strongly connected.

For an arbitrary initial vector and its subsequent trajectory, define the true and expressed role means by

\(y_r(t)=\dfrac{1}{p_r}\sum_{i:r_i=r}\mu_i x_i(t),\qquad h_r(t)=\dfrac{1}{p_r}\sum_{i:r_i=r}\mu_i s_i(t)\).

Write \(D=\operatorname{diag}(d_r)\), \(\Delta=\operatorname{diag}(\delta_r)\), and let \(\mathbf 1\) denote the all-ones vector. Averaging the expression equations within each role gives

\(h(t)=(I-\Delta)y(t)+\Delta q(t),\qquad q(t)=Ph(t)\).

Consequently,

\((I-\Delta P)h(t)=(I-\Delta)y(t)\).

This system is uniquely solvable: the induced infinity norm satisfies

\(\|\Delta P\|_\infty=\max_r|\delta_r|<1\),

so the convergent Neumann series \(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(\Delta P)^\ell\) is the inverse of \(I-\Delta P\). Once \(h(t)\) is determined, so are \(q(t)=Ph(t)\) and every \(s_i(t)\). This also verifies uniqueness of the simultaneous expressed opinions.

Averaging the true-opinion update gives

\(y(t+1)=Dy(t)+(I-D)q(t)\).

Subtracting this equation, in role \(r_i\), from the individual update gives the exact identity

\(x_i(t+1)-y_{r_i}(t+1)=d_{r_i}\bigl(x_i(t)-y_{r_i}(t)\bigr)\).

Hence

\(x_i(t)-y_{r_i}(t)=d_{r_i}^{\,t}\bigl(x_i(0)-y_{r_i}(0)\bigr)\).

Because \(0<d_r<1\), these deviations vanish. Thus convergence of all role means to a common value implies convergence of all type opinions to that value.

2. The supplied signals converge to \(\theta\) for every allowed vector

The common conditional-mean assumption says precisely that

\(y_r(0)=\dfrac{1}{p_r}\sum_{i:r_i=r}\mu_i z_i=\theta\)

for every role.

Suppose \(y(t)=\theta\mathbf 1\). Since \(P\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1\),

\((I-\Delta P)(\theta\mathbf 1)=(I-\Delta)(\theta\mathbf 1)\).

By uniqueness of the expressed role means, \(h(t)=\theta\mathbf 1\), and therefore \(q(t)=\theta\mathbf 1\). The role update then gives \(y(t+1)=\theta\mathbf 1\). Induction proves these identities for every time.

The individual update consequently becomes

\(x_i(t+1)=d_{r_i}x_i(t)+(1-d_{r_i})\theta\),

whose solution is

\(x_i(t)=\theta+d_{r_i}^{\,t}(z_i-\theta)\).

Thus, for every allowed conformity vector, the supplied signals converge to the common value \(\theta\). In particular, every admissible vector satisfies \(c_\delta=\theta\).

This does not yet establish feasibility: admissibility requires convergence from arbitrary initial vectors.

3. Zero conformity is admissible

Choose \(\delta_r=0\) for all roles. Then \(h(t)=y(t)\), and the role dynamics are

\(y(t+1)=By(t),\qquad B=D+(I-D)P\).

We verify convergence directly.

The matrix \(B\) is nonnegative and row-stochastic. Its diagonal entries satisfy \(B_{rr}\geq d_r>0\). Moreover, whenever \(P_{rs}>0\), we have \(B_{rs}\geq(1-d_r)P_{rs}>0\). Hence the graph of \(B\) is strongly connected.

If \(m=1\), then \(B=(1)\), so the single role mean is constant. The deviation identity from Section 1 proves convergence of every type opinion to that mean.

Now suppose \(m\geq2\), and put \(N=m-1\). Between any two distinct roles there is a directed simple path of length at most \(N\). Positive diagonal entries allow each such path to be extended to length exactly \(N\); for equal endpoints, use diagonal steps throughout. Each path contributes a positive product to the corresponding entry of \(B^N\). Therefore every entry of \(B^N\) is positive.

Let

\(C=B^N,\qquad \varepsilon=\min_{r,s}C_{rs}>0\).

Since each row of \(C\) sums to one, \(m\varepsilon\leq1\). For a real vector \(v\), define

\(\operatorname{osc}(v)=\max_r v_r-\min_r v_r\).

We claim that

\(\operatorname{osc}(Cv)\leq(1-m\varepsilon)\operatorname{osc}(v)\).

If \(m\varepsilon=1\), every entry of \(C\) equals \(1/m\), so \(Cv\) is constant. Otherwise, define

\(Q_{rs}=\dfrac{C_{rs}-\varepsilon}{1-m\varepsilon}\).

Then \(Q\) is nonnegative and row-stochastic, and

\(Cv=\varepsilon\left(\sum_s v_s\right)\mathbf 1+(1-m\varepsilon)Qv\).

Every coordinate of \(Qv\) lies between the minimum and maximum coordinates of \(v\), which proves the claim.

Iteration gives

\(\operatorname{osc}(y(kN))\leq(1-m\varepsilon)^k\operatorname{osc}(y(0))\longrightarrow0\).

Because \(B\) is row-stochastic, \(\min_r y_r(t)\) is nondecreasing and \(\max_r y_r(t)\) is nonincreasing. Both are bounded by the initial extrema. Their difference tends to zero by the preceding bound and monotonicity, so both converge to the same real number \(c\). Every coordinate \(y_r(t)\), lying between these extrema, converges to \(c\).

Finally, Section 1 shows \(x_i(t)-y_{r_i}(t)\to0\). Thus every type opinion converges to \(c\), for every real initial vector. Zero conformity is admissible.

4. Optimality and exact complexity

Under the specified fixed population law, \(c_\delta\) is a deterministic scalar. Consequently,

\(\operatorname{MSE}(\delta)=\sum_i\mu_i(c_\delta-\theta)^2=(c_\delta-\theta)^2\).

Section 2 proves that this equals \(0\) for every admissible vector. Section 3 provides an admissible vector. Since a squared error is nonnegative, the minimum is attained and equals \(0\).

The exact algorithm is therefore:

  • Output the rational vector \(\delta=(0,\ldots,0)\).
  • Output minimum MSE \(0\).
  • For the threshold problem, answer yes if and only if \(b\geq0\).

The vector requires only \(O(m)\) output symbols. Reading the input and comparing the rational threshold with zero takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). No approximation of real parameters or spectral quantities is needed.

5. Why positive signal variances do not change the answer

The role variances describe heterogeneity inside the fixed population, while every initial role average is exactly \(\theta\). Indeed, the explicit trajectory shows that the conditional variance of true opinions in role \(r\) at time \(t\) is \(d_r^{\,2t}\sigma_r^2\), which tends to zero.

The [finite-agent result quoted in the question](https://www.unifr.ch/amabe/en/assets/public/documents/articles/buechel__hellmann__kloessner_dec14_-_opinion_dynamics.pdf) concerns independent random signals and the resulting random consensus. Here the stipulated exact role proportions eliminate randomness in the role averages. A nonzero variance-based objective would require a different stochastic model; none is assumed in this proof.
