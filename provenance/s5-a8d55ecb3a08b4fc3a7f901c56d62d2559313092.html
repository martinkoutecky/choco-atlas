The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of secondary weight types \(T\), masses \(r_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}r_t=1\), per-member weights \(v_t>0\), and parameters \(\lambda,q\), compute the pair of minimum coalition masses: \(\min \sum_{t\in T}x_t\) subject to \(0\le x_t\le r_t\) and \(\lambda+(1-\lambda)\frac{\sum_{t\in T}v_tx_t}{\sum_{t\in T}v_tr_t}\ge q\); and \(\inf \sum_{t\in T}x_t\) subject to \(0\le x_t\le r_t\) and \((1-\lambda)\frac{\sum_{t\in T}v_tx_t}{\sum_{t\in T}v_tr_t}>1-q\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society of participating secondary voters is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(r_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_{t\in T}r_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a positive binary-rational per-member weight \(v_t\). The binary-rational parameters satisfy \(1/2<q<1\), \(0<\lambda<q\), and \(q>1-\lambda\).
There is one primary player, whose normalized voting weight is \(\lambda\); the secondary society has total voting weight \(1-\lambda\). Put \(W=\sum_{t\in T}v_tr_t\). A secondary coalition selects arbitrary real masses \(0\le x_t\le r_t\), has mass \(\sum_t x_t\), and voting weight \((1-\lambda)\sum_t v_tx_t/W\). The denominator remains fixed when a coalition is selected.
Compute the pair \((\alpha,\beta)\), where \(\alpha=\min\{\sum_t x_t:0\le x_t\le r_t,\ \lambda+(1-\lambda)\sum_t v_tx_t/W\ge q\}\) and \(\beta=\inf\{\sum_t x_t:0\le x_t\le r_t,\ (1-\lambda)\sum_t v_tx_t/W>1-q\}\). These measure secondary support needed to win with the primary player and secondary mass needed to make the complementary coalition lose, respectively.
Can this pair be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and the total input bit length \(L\), or is its computation NP-hard?
For the discrete uniform version with \(n\) secondary players, the minimum counts are \(\alpha_d=n-\rho\) and \(\beta_d=1+\rho\), where \(\rho=\lfloor n(1-q)/(1-\lambda)\rfloor\); hence \(\alpha_d+\beta_d=n+1\). [Source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00182-020-00751-z).
No result settling this exact continuous problem was identified in the literature search.

  • Specified binary-rational weights and parameters to make exact computational complexity well-defined; positive rational weights preserve the discrete positive-integer weight model up to common scaling.
  • Removed zero-mass types from the supplied list and required nonempty support, following the fixed support convention.
  • Made coalition masses arbitrary real numbers and retained the infimum for the strict blocking condition.
  • Treated the supplied society as the already participating population, so its normalization stays fixed and there is no additional attendance or quorum choice.
  • Used tight minimum coalition sizes rather than arbitrary efficiency lower bounds, following the anchor's proof and intended interpretation.

Result

The pair \((\alpha,\beta)\) is exactly computable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and the total input bit length \(L\). The algorithm sorts the supported types by decreasing per-member weight and fills them in that order. The strict inequality defining \(\beta\) affects attainment, but not the value of the infimum.

1. Reduce to two weight thresholds

Write \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), and define the unnormalized coalition weight and mass by

\(V(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}v_i x_i\), \(\qquad M(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}x_i\).

Because all masses and weights are positive, \(W=\sum_i v_i r_i>0\). Define

\(A=W\frac{q-\lambda}{1-\lambda}\), \(\qquad B=W\frac{1-q}{1-\lambda}\).

Multiplying the original inequalities by the positive quantity \(W/(1-\lambda)\) gives

\(\alpha=\min\{M(x):0\le x_i\le r_i,\ V(x)\ge A\}\),

\(\beta=\inf\{M(x):0\le x_i\le r_i,\ V(x)>B\}\).

The hypotheses imply \(0<A<W\): positivity follows from \(q>\lambda\), and the upper bound follows from \(q<1\). They also imply \(0<B<W\): positivity follows from \(q<1\), and the upper bound follows from \(q>\lambda\). Moreover, \(A+B=W\).

2. Exact formula for a non-strict threshold

Relabel the types so that

\(v_1\ge v_2\ge\cdots\ge v_\tau>0\),

breaking ties arbitrarily. Define the prefix sums

\(R_0=C_0=0\), \(\qquad R_k=\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_i\), \(\qquad C_k=\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_i r_i\).

For any \(z\in(0,W)\), let

\(k(z)=\min\{k:C_k\ge z\}\),

which exists because \(C_\tau=W\). Set

\(F(z)=R_{k(z)-1}+\frac{z-C_{k(z)-1}}{v_{k(z)}}\).

Claim. The minimum coalition mass subject to \(V(x)\ge z\) is exactly \(F(z)\).

Proof. Put \(k=k(z)\). By its definition,

\(C_{k-1}<z\le C_k\).

Consequently, the coalition

\(x_i^*=r_i\) for \(i<k\), \(\qquad x_k^*=\frac{z-C_{k-1}}{v_k}\), \(\qquad x_i^*=0\) for \(i>k\)

satisfies every bound \(0\le x_i^*\le r_i\). It has \(V(x^*)=z\) and \(M(x^*)=F(z)\).

To prove optimality, consider any coalition satisfying the bounds. Since \(v_i-v_k\ge0\) for \(i<k\), and \(v_i-v_k\le0\) for \(i>k\),

\(V(x)-v_kM(x)=\sum_{i<k}(v_i-v_k)x_i+\sum_{i>k}(v_i-v_k)x_i\)

\(\hspace{2em}\le\sum_{i<k}(v_i-v_k)r_i=C_{k-1}-v_kR_{k-1}\).

Thus, if \(V(x)\ge z\), then

\(M(x)\ge R_{k-1}+\frac{z-C_{k-1}}{v_k}=F(z)\).

The constructed coalition attains this bound, proving the claim.

3. The strict threshold has the same infimum

Claim. For every \(z\in(0,W)\),

\(\inf\{M(x):0\le x_i\le r_i,\ V(x)>z\}=F(z)\),

and this infimum is not attained.

Proof. The inequality proved above shows that \(V(x)>z\) implies

\(v_kM(x)\ge V(x)-C_{k-1}+v_kR_{k-1}>z-C_{k-1}+v_kR_{k-1}\),

so \(M(x)>F(z)\).

Conversely, let \(x^*\) be the coalition constructed in Section 2, and let \(r=(r_1,\ldots,r_\tau)\) be the full society. For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), define

\(x^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-\varepsilon)x^*+\varepsilon r\).

This coalition satisfies the coordinate bounds because it is a convex combination of two coalitions satisfying them. Furthermore,

\(V(x^{(\varepsilon)})=(1-\varepsilon)z+\varepsilon W=z+\varepsilon(W-z)>z\),

while, using \(\sum_i r_i=1\),

\(M(x^{(\varepsilon)})=(1-\varepsilon)F(z)+\varepsilon\longrightarrow F(z)\).

Therefore \(F(z)\) is the infimum. Every coalition satisfying the strict threshold has larger mass, so it is not attained.

4. Algorithm and exact bit complexity

The resulting formulas are

\(\boxed{\alpha=F\!\left(W\frac{q-\lambda}{1-\lambda}\right),\qquad \beta=F\!\left(W\frac{1-q}{1-\lambda}\right).}\)

The algorithm is:

  • Sort the \(\tau\) types by decreasing \(v_i\), using exact rational comparisons.
  • Compute \(R_k\), \(C_k\), and \(W=C_\tau\).
  • Compute \(A\) and \(B\) exactly.
  • Find the first prefix crossing each threshold and evaluate the corresponding formula for \(F\).

Sorting takes \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational comparisons. The remaining work takes \(O(\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations.

To verify polynomial bit complexity, write each input rational as an integer numerator divided by a positive integer denominator. Let \(D\) be the product of all input denominators. Its binary length is at most \(O(L)\), and expressing every input over denominator \(D\) gives numerators of \(O(L)\) bits.

Each product \(v_i r_i\) can then be represented over denominator \(D^2\). Prefix sums have numerators of \(O(L+\log\tau)\) bits. The thresholds and the two evaluations of \(F\) require only a fixed number of further additions, multiplications, and divisions of these rationals. Their numerator and denominator lengths therefore remain \(O(L+\log\tau)\). Exact comparisons likewise use cross-products of polynomial bit length.

Standard integer arithmetic consequently implements every operation in polynomial time. The outputs are exact rational numbers with polynomial encoding length; they need not be dyadic even when all inputs are dyadic.

5. Uniform case and the discrete comparison

If all per-member weights equal a common value \(v>0\), then \(W=v\) and \(V(x)=vM(x)\). Hence

\(\alpha=\frac{q-\lambda}{1-\lambda}\), \(\qquad \beta=\frac{1-q}{1-\lambda}\), \(\qquad \alpha+\beta=1\).

For the uniform discrete model, the supplied identity \(\alpha_d+\beta_d=n+1\) is established in the [cited paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00182-020-00751-z). The difference follows directly from the integer restriction: writing \(b=(1-q)/(1-\lambda)\), the smallest integer strictly greater than \(nb\) is \(\lfloor nb\rfloor+1\), whereas the continuous problem takes an infimum over arbitrarily divisible masses.

Thus the exact continuous problem is polynomial-time solvable, with both threshold values justified explicitly.
