Given \(A\), lexicographic tie-breaking, a rational \(\mu\) over \(T=L(A)\), and \(a,b\) with \(p_\mu(a)=p_\mu(b)>\max_{c\notin\{a,b\}}p_\mu(c)\), determine the equilibrium winner under the paper's truthful-start sequential direct-best-response dynamics for every replication \(N\) with \(N\mu_R\in\mathbb Z\); equivalently, compute the high-multiplicity limit. Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(m\ge 2\), and break plurality ties by smallest index. Types are strict rankings in \(L(A)\).
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_R\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) denote the input bit length.
Define \(p_\mu(c)=\sum_{R:\operatorname{top}(R)=c}\mu_R\). The input includes \(a<b\) with \(p_\mu(a)=p_\mu(b)>p_\mu(c)\) for every \(c\notin\{a,b\}\), a binary-encoded positive integer \(N\) satisfying \(N\mu_R\in\mathbb Z\), and a candidate \(w\in A\).
The replicated society has \(N\mu_R\) unit-weight voters of type \(R\), initially voting truthfully. At each step one voter changes its reported top candidate to the resulting winner, which must strictly improve its outcome and be its most preferred winner attainable by any unilateral report, according to its fixed truthful ranking.
The scheduler must choose such a move whenever one exists; otherwise the process terminates. Let \(E_N(\mu)\) be the set of terminal winners over all such schedules.

Is deciding whether \(w\in E_N(\mu)\), uniformly for every admissible replication \(N\), in \(\mathrm P\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), and \(\log N\).

For discrete truthful profiles with exactly two potential winners, the terminal winner is uniquely their pairwise-majority winner, with pairwise ties resolved lexicographically. Potential winners comprise the current winner, candidates tied with it, and candidates preceding it whose score is one lower. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.08853v3).
This discrete characterization requires exactly two potential winners; the mass-level strict-gap promise does not impose that condition on every finite replication.

Classification

The uniform problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard, even when all masses are dyadic, $N$ is the least admissible replication, and $N\leq 4m^2$.

Every larger admissible replication is nevertheless decidable in polynomial time. The hardness therefore lies precisely in the finite-replication exception identified in the notes.

The proof uses a published hardness construction, together with a new reduction enforcing the task’s two-leader promise.

1. Elementary properties of the dynamics

For a reported profile with scores $s(c)$, define the effective scores
$$ q(c)=s(c)-\frac{c}{m+1}. $$
They are distinct, and their maximum identifies the plurality winner with the required tie-breaking rule. Write $M=\max_c q(c)$ and define
$$ P=\{c:q(c)+1>M\}. $$
This is the potential-winner set in the question: besides the winner, it contains candidates tied with it and preceding candidates whose score is one lower.

Lemma 1

Starting truthfully, every legal move has the following properties:

  • The voter does not currently report the winner.
  • The maximum effective score strictly increases.
  • The potential-winner set shrinks, and the candidate abandoned by the voter leaves that set permanently.

Consequently, every schedule terminates after at most $N(m-1)$ moves.

Proof

First consider a voter reporting a nonwinner $d$. The current winner’s score is unaffected by removing this voter’s vote. Therefore the voter’s attainable winners are exactly
$$ P\setminus\{d\}. $$
Indeed, reporting a different candidate $y$ makes $y$ win exactly when $q(y)+1>M$; retaining the current report preserves the current winner.

A legal move to $y$ consequently gives
$$ M'=q(y)+1>M. $$
Every candidate outside $P$ had $q(c)+1<M$. Equality is impossible because the effective scores have distinct fractional parts. Such a candidate receives no vote in this move, so it remains outside the new potential-winner set. Furthermore, the abandoned candidate satisfies
$$ q'(d)+1=q(d)<M<M', $$
and therefore leaves that set.

It remains to prove that a voter reporting the current winner cannot make a legal move. We prove this simultaneously with the preceding properties by induction on the number of moves.

Initially, every voter reporting the winner receives its truthful top outcome and cannot improve. Now suppose all preceding moves have the stated properties, and voter $i$ reports the current winner $c$.

If $i$ has never moved, $c$ is its truthful top candidate. Otherwise, consider its last move. Immediately before that move, let the maximum effective score be $M_0$, the potential-winner set be $P_0$, and its abandoned report be $d$. The voter chose $c$ as its favorite member of $P_0\setminus\{d\}$.

Every candidate outside $P_0$, and also $d$, has subsequently remained outside the potential-winner set. Its effective score cannot increase, because every preceding move was directed to a potential winner. Thus, for every such candidate $y$, its current score satisfies
$$ q(y)+1<M_0. $$

Consider the most recent move that elected the current winner $c$. Its previous winner $z\neq c$ retained its vote count, because that move came from a nonwinner. By the inductive increase of the maximum,
$$ q(z)\geq M_0 $$
in the current state. Removing voter $i$’s vote from $c$ does not change $z$’s score. Hence no candidate outside $P_0\setminus\{d\}$ can become the winner under any unilateral report by $i$. Every other attainable candidate is less preferred than $c$. Thus $i$ cannot improve.

This completes the induction.

Whenever a voter abandons a candidate, that candidate is thereafter outside the potential-winner set: either it was already outside, or the move removes it. It can never receive a subsequent move. No voter can therefore return to an abandoned report, so each voter moves at most $m-1$ times. $\square$

2. The external hardness construction

I use the X3C construction in Appendix B, Theorem 5.3 of [*On Iterative Voting Outcomes in Plurality Elections*](https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/comsoc2025/comsoc2025-papers/106.pdf).

Its polynomial-size truthful profile $P_I$, constructed from an X3C instance $I$, has these properties:

  • There are $M$ candidates and an integer $3\leq H\leq M$. Candidate $f$ has one vote; every other candidate has $H$ votes.
  • Candidates $u,v$ are first and second in tie-breaking, and the target $t$ differs from them.
  • Every $u$-supporter ranks $u\succ v\succ t$ first; every $v$-supporter ranks $v\succ u\succ t$ first.
  • Any legal prefix first increasing $t$’s score implies that $I$ has an exact cover.
  • An exact cover gives a schedule terminating at $t$, with $u$ outside the potential-winner set.

The fourth property follows from the reverse argument on pages 22–23, which extracts the cover from the prefix through the first increase of $t$. The forward construction explicitly eliminates $u$.

Thus the result used here concerns truthful profiles, unit voters, strict rankings, direct best responses, and lexicographic tie-breaking, exactly as required.

3. Enforcing the promised score pattern

Let $n_0$ be the number of voters in $P_I$. Its score pattern gives
$$ n_0=(M-1)H+1. $$

Add two candidates $a,b$, placed after every original candidate in tie-breaking, and add $H+1$ truthful supporters of each. Their rankings begin respectively
$$ a\succ u\succ t, \qquad b\succ u\succ t. $$
Complete these rankings by placing all remaining original candidates next, followed by the remaining new candidates.

For every original voter, preserve its original ranking and append all new candidates below every original candidate.

To obtain dyadic masses, let $Q$ be the smallest power of two satisfying
$$ Q\geq n_0+2(H+1). $$
Add
$$ r=Q-n_0-2(H+1) $$
padding candidates, each with one truthful supporter. Place them after the original candidates but before $a,b$ in tie-breaking. Each padding voter ranks its own candidate first, followed by $u$, then $t$, then the remaining original candidates, and finally the remaining new candidates.

Relabel according to this tie-breaking order. In particular,
$$ a=m-1,\qquad b=m. $$

The resulting truthful profile has $Q$ voters and scores
$$ s(a)=s(b)=H+1, $$
while every original candidate has score at most $H$, and every padding candidate has score $1$. It satisfies the task’s strict-gap promise.

Merge identical rankings and assign each resulting type its multiplicity divided by $Q$. Set
$$ N=Q,\qquad w=t. $$
All masses are positive dyadic rationals, and they sum to $1$.

The original candidate $f$ still has exactly one voter. Its type therefore has mass $1/Q$, so the least admissible replication is exactly
$$ D=Q=N. $$

4. A successful schedule yields an exact cover

Suppose the constructed society has a schedule terminating at $t$.

By Lemma 1, $t$ remains a potential winner throughout this schedule. The maximum effective score strictly increases from its initial value, whose associated integer score is $H+1$. Therefore $t$, initially at score $H$, must gain a vote.

Consider the prefix through its first gain. Throughout the preceding portion, $s(t)=H$. Since $t$ remains potential, the current winner must have score $H+1$ and must follow $t$ in tie-breaking.

The scores of $u,v$ cannot change

Suppose there were a first move changing either score.

An incoming vote would raise its recipient from $H$ to $H+1$. Since both $u,v$ precede $t$, the recipient would win and exclude $t$ from the potential-winner set.

For an outgoing vote, the voter is still an original truthful supporter. A $u$-supporter’s best attainable winner is $v$, and a $v$-supporter’s best attainable winner is $u$: the other candidate still has score $H$, precedes the current winner, and is ranked second. This again raises a candidate preceding $t$ to $H+1$ and excludes $t$.

Both possibilities contradict Lemma 1. Hence, before $t$ first gains,
$$ s(u)=s(v)=H. $$

No added voter can move

Consider a first move by an added voter. It still reports its truthful top candidate.

If that candidate currently wins, the voter cannot improve. Otherwise, retaining its report cannot elect its truthful top. Candidate $u$ is attainable: adding a vote raises its score to $H+1$, and it precedes the current winner. Since the voter ranks $u$ second, its best response elects $u$. That excludes $t$.

Thus no added voter moves before the first gain of $t$.

Nor can an original voter move to a new candidate. Every original ranking places all original candidates above all new ones. Candidate $u$ is attainable unless the voter reports $u$; in that case, $v$ is attainable and ranked second. A new candidate therefore cannot be its best response.

Consequently, all added scores remain fixed throughout this prefix, and every move is between original candidates.

Projecting the prefix to $P_I$

The first move must give an original candidate $y$ its first additional vote. Its score becomes $H+1$, and it precedes both added leaders, so it wins in the constructed profile.

This move is also legal from the truthful source profile $P_I$. The mover does not report $u$, as proved above. In both profiles, its attainable original winners are the score-$H$ candidates other than its current report, including $u$. It chooses the same favorite $y$, and $y\succ u$.

After this first move, the winner is an original candidate with score $H+1$. All subsequent original score vectors coincide in the two profiles. By Lemma 1, a mover does not report the current winner, so that winner retains its score. The added leaders, also at $H+1$ but following every original candidate, do not affect which original candidates the voter can elect.

The projected moves are therefore legal source moves, including the first gain of $t$. The external construction’s fourth property implies that $I$ has an exact cover.

5. An exact cover yields a terminal winner $t$

Conversely, suppose $I$ has an exact cover. Take the source schedule terminating at $t$ with $u$ outside its potential-winner set.

Execute the same moves by the original voters in the constructed society, leaving every added voter unchanged.

The first source move gives its destination score $H+1$. Because all original candidates precede $a,b$, the destination also wins in the constructed society. The attainable original outcomes coincide, and all new candidates are ranked below them, so the move remains a best response.

Thereafter, the source winner has score at least $H+1$. Each source move comes from a nonwinner, leaving that winner’s score unchanged. The fixed added leaders cannot prevent any original destination that beats this retained winner from winning. Thus every source move remains legal.

At the end, the constructed winner is $t$.

For an original voter, any unilateral report that elects an original candidate in the constructed profile elects the same candidate among the original votes alone. Source terminality rules out an improvement. A new winner is ranked below $t$, so it cannot improve the outcome either.

For an added voter, its truthful top cannot be elected by retaining its current report; a padding candidate additionally remains too far behind. Candidate $u$ is unattainable because it lies outside the potential-winner set, and removing this voter’s vote leaves the current winner $t$ unchanged. Every remaining candidate is ranked below $t$.

Hence no voter has an improving move. The constructed state is terminal, proving
$$ I\text{ has an exact cover} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\in E_Q(\mu). $$

6. Size and running-time bounds

The reduction is polynomial. The source construction has polynomially many candidates and voters, and
$$ Q<2\bigl((M+1)H+3\bigr)\leq 4M^2. $$
The padding uses fewer than $Q$ candidates and voters. Since the constructed candidate count satisfies $m\geq M$,
$$ N=Q\leq 4m^2. $$
The support representation and all rankings have polynomial bit length.

Therefore membership is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard even under the additional restrictions
$$ N=D,\qquad N\leq 4m^2, $$
with dyadic masses. On this restricted subclass it is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete: Lemma 1 supplies certificates of at most $N(m-1)\leq 4m^3$ moves, and move validity and terminality are polynomial-time checkable from type-report counters.

For the general binary-encoded problem, an exact deterministic algorithm uses counters $x_{R,c}$ for voters of type $R$ currently reporting $c$. Its number of states is
$$ K=\prod_R\binom{N\mu_R+m-1}{m-1} \leq (N+1)^{\tau(m-1)}. $$
Enumerating legal transitions and searching reachable terminal states takes
$$ O\!\left( (N+1)^{\tau(m-1)} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log N) \right). $$
A nondeterministic simulation stores these counters and a move counter, using polynomial space. Together with Lemma 1 and $\mathrm{NPSPACE}=\mathrm{PSPACE}$, this gives a $\mathrm{PSPACE}$ upper bound for the general succinct problem.

Larger admissible replications

Write each reduced mass as $\mu_R=u_R/v_R$ and set
$$ D=\operatorname{lcm}_R v_R. $$
Integrality of every $N\mu_R$ is equivalent to $D\mid N$, so write $N=kD$.

Define
$$ h=Dp_\mu(a)=Dp_\mu(b), \qquad \delta_c=D\bigl(p_\mu(a)-p_\mu(c)\bigr). $$
These are integers, with $\delta_c\geq 1$. At replication $kD$, the truthful potential-winner set is
$$ \{a,b\}\cup\{c<a:k\delta_c=1\}. $$
For $k\geq 2$, it is exactly $\{a,b\}$. The truthful, unit-weight, strict-ranking hypotheses of the supplied [two-potential-winner theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.08853v3#A1) all hold. Consequently,
$$ E_N(\mu)= \begin{cases} \{a\},& \displaystyle\sum_{R:a\succ_R b}\mu_R\geq \frac12,\\[2mm] \{b\},& \displaystyle\sum_{R:a\succ_R b}\mu_R<\frac12. \end{cases} $$
The same formula applies at $N=D$ whenever there is no additional potential winner.

With
$$ B=L+\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil+1, $$
these branches can be decided in a conservative $O(m\tau B^3)$ bit operations, without expanding the electorate.

Thus larger replications admit a polynomial algorithm, while a uniform polynomial algorithm including the least replication would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.
