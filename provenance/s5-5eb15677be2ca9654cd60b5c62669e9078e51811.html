Theorem thm:NP-USW — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many teacher types \(T\), student categories \(G\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-teacher category supplies \(\sigma_g\), quantiles \(\tau_t\), and values \(v_t(g)\), consider finite multisets \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{G}\) as integral bundles. Choose nonnegative masses \(x_{t,b}\) with finite support satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_gx_{t,b}=\sigma_g\) for every \(g\). What is the supremum of \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}v_t(b)\), and is it attained by a feasible finite-support allocation? Equivalently, for a rational threshold \(q\), decide whether some feasible allocation has welfare at least \(q\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|T|\geq1\) teacher types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|G|\geq1\) student categories, binary-rational supplies \(\sigma_g\geq0\) per unit teacher mass, nonnegative binary-rational values \(v_t(g)\), and binary-rational quantiles \(\theta_t\in[0,1)\). Supplies need not sum to one. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including a rational threshold \(q\).
A bundle is \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{G}\), with \(|b|=\sum_g b_g\). Set \(v_t(0)=0\); otherwise sort the category values with multiplicities \(b_g\) in nondecreasing order and take the value at rank \(\max\{1,\lceil\theta_t|b|\rceil\}\). Equal values require no tie-breaking.
An allocation consists of real masses \(x_{t,b}\geq0\), with finitely many positive entries, satisfying \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_gx_{t,b}=\sigma_g\) for every category. Bundle sizes have no upper bound. Define \(W(x)=\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}v_t(b)\) and \(W^*=\sup_x W(x)\).

Is deciding whether a feasible finite-support allocation satisfies \(W(x)\geq q\) in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
What is \(W^*\), and under what conditions is it attained by a feasible finite-support allocation?

For finite agents and indivisible goods, the corresponding unconstrained welfare threshold problem is NP-complete, with hardness already for homogeneous quantiles below one. [Aziz, Narang and Suzuki](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17869v3#S4.SS1).
No result settling this exact mass-allocation problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized teacher masses to sum to one so that category supplies are measured per unit teacher mass; category supplies are nonnegative and need not sum to one.
  • Required nonempty teacher support and category set, and encoded values, quantiles, supplies and the threshold as binary rationals.
  • Carried over nonnegative goods values and the anchor's restriction that every quantile is strictly below one; renamed quantiles to theta_t to reserve tau for support size.
  • Defined the omitted bundle valuation using the lower order statistic, counting category multiplicities and using rank one at quantile zero.
  • Allowed empty bundles and assigned them value zero, the natural goods convention where the supplied definition and latest full version leave their value undefined.
  • Interpreted allocation masses as real numbers, retaining finite support and imposing no bound on integral bundle sizes.

Result

The threshold problem is in \(\mathrm P\). The supremum is the value of a transportation LP and is independent of the quantiles. It need not be attained, but attainment can also be decided in polynomial time.

The proof below gives an exact finite-dimensional attainment criterion, including the strict inequalities arising from the quantile convention.

1. The LP determining the supremum

Define

\(V=\max\left\{\sum_{t,g}v_t(g)u_{tg}:u_{tg}\geq0,\ \sum_g u_{tg}\leq\mu_t,\ \sum_tu_{tg}\leq\sigma_g\right\}.\)

Its variables describe teacher masses receiving singleton bundles; remaining teachers receive empty bundles. The supply constraints permit unused supply.

The dual LP is

\(V=\min\left\{\sum_t\mu_ta_t+\sum_g\sigma_gp_g:a_t\geq0,\ p_g\geq0,\ a_t+p_g\geq v_t(g)\right\}.\)

The primal is nonempty and bounded, so both LPs have optimal solutions. Fix a rational optimal dual solution \((a,p)\).

Upper bound

For a nonempty bundle \(b\), choose a category \(g\) occurring at its specified quantile rank. Then

\(v_t(b)=v_t(g)\leq a_t+p_g\leq a_t+\sum_h p_hb_h.\)

For the empty bundle, the same bound follows from \(v_t(0)=0\) and \(a_t\geq0\). Consequently, every feasible allocation satisfies

\(W(x)\leq\sum_t\mu_ta_t+\sum_g\sigma_gp_g=V.\)

Approaching the bound while consuming all supply

We first record a simple construction. Given teacher mass \(M>0\) and a nonnegative consumption vector \(d\), choose a positive integer \(N\) satisfying

\(N\geq\frac{\sum_gd_g}{M}.\)

Assign mass \(d_g/N\) to bundle \(Ne_g\), where \(e_g\) is the category unit vector, and assign the remaining mass to the empty bundle. This consumes exactly \(d\), using finitely many bundles. In particular, feasible allocations always exist.

Let \(u\) be an optimal singleton solution. Together with empty bundles, it gives each type its full mass and has welfare \(V\). Write \(c_g=\sum_tu_{tg}\leq\sigma_g\).

For any \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), retain a proportion \(1-\varepsilon\) of this allocation. The remaining teacher mass is \(\varepsilon\mu_t\) for each type, and the remaining supply is

\(d_g=\sigma_g-(1-\varepsilon)c_g\geq0.\)

Choose any type \(t_0\). Use its remaining mass \(\varepsilon\mu_{t_0}>0\) to consume all of \(d\) by the construction above, and give empty bundles to the other remaining teachers. Nonnegative valuations imply that the resulting feasible allocation has welfare at least \((1-\varepsilon)V\).

Thus

\(\boxed{W^*=V.}\)

This equality does not assert attainment.

2. Bundles that can appear in an optimal allocation

Call a bundle tight for type \(t\) if

\(v_t(b)=a_t+\sum_gp_gb_g.\)

For every feasible allocation,

\(V-W(x)=\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}\left(a_t+\sum_gp_gb_g-v_t(b)\right).\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Hence an allocation attains \(V\) if and only if every bundle receiving positive mass is tight.

Partition the categories into

\(F=\{g:p_g>0\},\qquad Z=\{g:p_g=0\}.\)

For every \(g\in Z\), dual feasibility gives \(v_t(g)\leq a_t\).

Tight bundles containing a positive-price item

Suppose a tight nonempty bundle has quantile category \(g\). Equality must hold in both inequalities

\(v_t(g)\leq a_t+p_g\leq a_t+\sum_hp_hb_h.\)

If the bundle contains a positive-price item, these equalities imply that:

  • it contains exactly one positive-price item, from category \(g\);
  • \(v_t(g)=a_t+p_g\);
  • all its other items have zero price.

The additional zero-price items all have values at most \(a_t\), strictly below the anchor value \(a_t+p_g\). If there are \(k\) such additional items, tightness therefore requires the quantile rank to select the last item.

Define

\(K_t=\begin{cases}0,&\theta_t=0,\\[2pt]\left\lceil\frac{\theta_t}{1-\theta_t}\right\rceil-1,&0<\theta_t<1.\end{cases}\)

For positive \(\theta_t\), selecting the anchor is equivalent to

\(\left\lceil\theta_t(k+1)\right\rceil>k \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \theta_t(k+1)>k \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k<\frac{\theta_t}{1-\theta_t}.\)

Thus the allowed counts are exactly \(0\leq k\leq K_t\). For \(\theta_t=0\), the rank is one, so only \(k=0\) is allowed.

Consequently, these tight bundles consist of one tight positive-price anchor and at most \(K_t\) arbitrary zero-price items.

Tight bundles containing only zero-price items

If \(a_t=0\), all zero-price items have value zero. Every bundle made entirely from zero-price items, including the empty bundle, is tight.

Now suppose \(a_t>0\). Define

\(H_t=\{g\in Z:v_t(g)=a_t\},\qquad D_t=\{g\in Z:v_t(g)<a_t\}.\)

A tight bundle must contain some items from \(H_t\). Let \(h\) and \(\ell\) be its counts from \(H_t\) and \(D_t\), respectively.

For \(\theta_t=0\), the allowed count pairs are

\(S_t=\{(h,0):h\in\mathbb Z,\ h\geq1\}.\)

For positive \(\theta_t\), the quantile has value \(a_t\) exactly when its rank exceeds \(\ell\). Equivalently,

\(\ell<\theta_t(h+\ell).\)

Write

\(\frac{\theta_t}{1-\theta_t}=\frac{A_t}{B_t}\)

with positive coprime integers \(A_t,B_t\). The allowed count pairs are therefore

\(S_t=\{(h,\ell)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^2:h\geq1,\ B_t\ell\leq A_th-1\}.\)

The subtraction of one is essential: it represents the strict quantile inequality exactly.

3. Computing the count hulls in polynomial time

Let \(C_t=\operatorname{conv}(S_t)\), where convex hull means finite convex combinations.

For \(\theta_t=0\),

\(C_t=\{(h,0):h\geq1\}.\)

For positive \(\theta_t\), abbreviate \(A=A_t\), \(B=B_t\), and define the bounded rational polytope

\(Q=\{(h,\ell)\in\mathbb R^2:1\leq h\leq B,\ \ell\geq0,\ B\ell\leq Ah-1\}.\)

Then

\(C_t=\operatorname{conv}(Q\cap\mathbb Z^2)+ \operatorname{cone}\{(1,0),(B,A)\}.\)

Here the cone consists of nonnegative real combinations of the indicated vectors.

Proof. Take an allowed integer pair with \(h>B\).

If \(\ell\geq A\), subtract \((B,A)\). The resulting pair remains allowed, because

\(B(\ell-A)-A(h-B)=B\ell-Ah\leq-1,\)

and its coordinates satisfy \(h-B\geq1\), \(\ell-A\geq0\).

If \(\ell<A\), subtract \((h-B)(1,0)\). The resulting pair \((B,\ell)\) is allowed, since

\(B\ell\leq B(A-1)\leq AB-1.\)

Repeating these reductions expresses every allowed integer pair as an integer point of \(Q\) plus nonnegative integer combinations of the two rays.

Conversely, adding either integer ray preserves the defining inequalities. A nonnegative real combination of the rays is a finite convex combination of integer combinations: round each coefficient down or up and interpolate. This proves the stated equality using finite convex combinations. \(\square\)

The polytope \(Q\) is nonempty, since \((1,0)\in Q\), and bounded, since \(0\leq\ell\leq A-1\). Its dimension is at most two and its encoding length is polynomial in that of \(\theta_t\).

We use the fixed-dimensional integer-hull theorem: the vertices of the integer hull of a bounded rational polytope can be computed in polynomial time when the dimension is fixed. This is the purely integer case of [Hildebrand, Oertel and Weismantel, Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.2520). Its hypotheses hold here with two integer coordinates and no continuous coordinates.

Thus we can compute polynomial-size sets of integer vertices \(\mathcal V_t\) and integer rays \(\mathcal R_t\) such that

\(C_t=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal V_t)+\operatorname{cone}(\mathcal R_t).\)

For positive \(\theta_t\), use the integer-hull vertices of \(Q\) and the rays \((1,0),(B_t,A_t)\). For \(\theta_t=0\), use the single vertex \((1,0)\) and the single ray \((1,0)\).

4. An exact finite-dimensional attainment criterion

The following criterion uses any fixed optimal dual solution \((a,p)\).

For \(g\in F\), introduce nonnegative anchor masses \(y_{tg}\), requiring

\(y_{tg}=0\quad\text{unless}\quad v_t(g)=a_t+p_g.\)

Set \(Y_t=\sum_{g\in F}y_{tg}\). Introduce nonnegative variables:

  • \(z_t\): teacher mass receiving bundles made entirely from zero-price items;
  • \(d_{tg}\), for \(g\in Z\): consumption of zero-price items in anchor bundles;
  • \(c_{tg}\), for \(g\in Z\): consumption in bundles made entirely from zero-price items.

Require

\(Y_t+z_t=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t,\)

\(\sum_ty_{tg}=\sigma_g\quad\text{for every }g\in F,\)

\(\sum_t(d_{tg}+c_{tg})=\sigma_g\quad\text{for every }g\in Z,\)

and

\(\sum_{g\in Z}d_{tg}\leq K_tY_t\quad\text{for every }t.\)

Also require the implication

\(z_t=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_{tg}=0\text{ for every }g\in Z.\)

For types with \(a_t=0\), no further restriction is needed.

For each type with \(a_t>0\), set

\(\mathsf H_t=\sum_{g\in H_t}c_{tg},\qquad \mathsf D_t=\sum_{g\in D_t}c_{tg}.\)

Introduce nonnegative variables \(\lambda_{tu}\), for \(u\in\mathcal V_t\), and \(\rho_{tr}\), for \(r\in\mathcal R_t\), satisfying

\(\sum_{u\in\mathcal V_t}\lambda_{tu}=z_t,\)

\((\mathsf H_t,\mathsf D_t)= \sum_{u\in\mathcal V_t}\lambda_{tu}u+ \sum_{r\in\mathcal R_t}\rho_{tr}r.\)

When \(z_t>0\), these equations say precisely that

\(\left(\frac{\mathsf H_t}{z_t},\frac{\mathsf D_t}{z_t}\right)\in C_t.\)

Attainment theorem

The supremum \(W^*=V\) is attained by a feasible finite-support allocation if and only if this system has a solution.

Necessity. An attaining allocation uses only tight bundles. Aggregate their teacher masses and category consumptions according to the two bundle classes characterized in Section 2.

Each anchor bundle contains at most \(K_t\) additional items, giving the decoration inequality. If \(z_t=0\), its associated consumption is zero. For positive \(z_t\) and \(a_t>0\), the average count pair is a finite convex combination of elements of \(S_t\), hence belongs to \(C_t\). Its vertex-and-ray representation gives the remaining equations.

Sufficiency. We construct tight bundles realizing any solution.

For anchor bundles of type \(t\), suppose \(Y_t>0\). Their desired mean decoration vector is \(d_t/Y_t\), whose coordinate sum is at most \(K_t\). This vector belongs to

\(\operatorname{conv}\{k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{Z}:|k|\leq K_t\}.\)

Indeed, for positive \(K_t\), that convex hull is the simplex with vertices \(0\) and \(K_te_g\). For \(K_t=0\), the decoration vector is zero. Use such a finite decoration mixture for every anchor category of type \(t\). This consumes exactly \(d_t\) and produces only tight bundles. If \(Y_t=0\), the decoration inequality already forces \(d_t=0\).

For a type with \(a_t=0\) and \(z_t>0\), consume \(c_t\) using mass \(z_t\) by the finite construction from Section 1. All these bundles are tight.

For a type with \(a_t>0\) and \(z_t>0\), membership in \(C_t\) gives a finite distribution of allowed integer count pairs with the required means. To realize the category consumptions, for each count pair choose a high category with probabilities \(c_{tg}/\mathsf H_t\), and put all its high items in that category. Similarly, when \(\mathsf D_t>0\), choose a low category with probabilities \(c_{tg}/\mathsf D_t\), and put all its low items there. These choices give the required expected consumption of every category and preserve the allowed count pair. If \(\mathsf D_t=0\), every pair having positive probability has zero low count.

When \(z_t=0\), the implication ensures that no corresponding consumption needs realization.

The resulting allocation has finite support, satisfies every mass and supply equality, and uses only tight bundles. Therefore its welfare equals \(V\). \(\square\)

5. Testing the criterion in polynomial time

All parts of the criterion are linear except

\(z_t=0\Longrightarrow c_t=0.\)

Dropping this implication would be incorrect: the ray variables could describe consumption at zero teacher mass.

Nevertheless, these implications can be handled with polynomially many LPs.

Start with \(J=T\). For the current set \(J\), solve the linear system from Section 4, omitting the implications but adding

\(z_t=0,\qquad c_{tg}=0\quad\text{for }t\notin J,\ g\in Z.\)

Call its feasible set \(P_J\).

  • If \(P_J\) is empty, reject attainment.
  • For every \(t\in J\), maximize \(z_t\) over \(P_J\).
  • Let \(J'\) contain exactly those types for which this maximum is positive.
  • If \(J'\neq J\), replace \(J\) by \(J'\) and repeat.
  • If \(J'=J\), accept attainment.

The maxima exist. The mass and consumption variables are bounded by the given masses and supplies. The variables \(\lambda\) are bounded by \(z_t\); the ray coefficients \(\rho\) are bounded by \(\mathsf H_t\), since every chosen ray has positive first coordinate.

Correctness

Any solution of the exact criterion survives every iteration. If an iteration removes type \(t\), then \(z_t=0\) throughout the current relaxed feasible set. An exact solution must consequently have \(c_t=0\), so the new restrictions preserve it. Thus rejection is sound.

At a stable nonempty \(J\), each \(t\in J\) has a feasible witness with \(z_t>0\). Average these witnesses. Convexity gives a point of \(P_J\) with \(z_t>0\) simultaneously for every \(t\in J\). For all other types, both \(z_t\) and \(c_t\) are zero. Therefore every omitted implication holds, and the point satisfies the exact criterion. If \(J\) is empty, any point of \(P_J\) already satisfies all implications.

Bit complexity

The singleton LP has polynomial size. An optimal rational dual solution has polynomial encoding length. The integers \(K_t,A_t,B_t\) have polynomial encoding length, even when their numerical values are large. The two-dimensional integer-hull computations produce polynomial-size representations.

Each iteration uses at most \(\tau\) LP optimizations, and every nonterminal iteration removes at least one type. Hence the entire attainment test runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). All zero tests and positivity tests use exact rational LP values.

6. The threshold decision

Compute \(V\) by the singleton LP.

  • If \(q>V\), the answer is no.
  • If \(q<V\), the answer is yes: Section 1 gives finite-support allocations with welfare approaching \(V\).
  • If \(q=V\), the answer is yes exactly when the polynomial-time attainment test accepts.

Thus the exact existential threshold problem, including equality at a potentially unattained supremum, belongs to \(\mathrm P\).

7. Attainment examples

A sufficient condition

If \(\sum_g\sigma_g\leq1\), the supremum is attained using only singleton and empty bundles.

To prove this, take an optimal singleton solution consuming total supply \(C\). Its unused teacher capacity is \(1-C\), while its unused supply is \(\sum_g\sigma_g-C\leq1-C\). Distribute the remaining supply among the unused teacher capacities as additional singletons.

This cannot decrease welfare because all values are nonnegative. It cannot increase welfare above \(V\), because it remains feasible for the singleton LP. It therefore yields an exact-supply allocation attaining \(V\).

A nonattained supremum

Take one teacher type of mass one and two categories, with

\(v(g)=1,\qquad v(d)=0,\qquad \sigma_g=\sigma_d=1,\qquad \theta=\frac12.\)

The singleton LP has value \(V=1\), so \(W^*=1\).

Suppose an allocation attained welfare one. Every positive-mass bundle would have value one and hence contain at least one good item. Total good consumption is one and teacher mass is one, so every positive-mass bundle must contain exactly one good item.

For \(\theta=\frac12\), the bound from Section 2 is \(K=0\): a bundle containing exactly one good item cannot contain any bad item and still have value one. Thus the allocation consumes no bad items, contradicting \(\sigma_d=1\).

Therefore this instance has supremum one, but no feasible finite-support allocation attains it.
