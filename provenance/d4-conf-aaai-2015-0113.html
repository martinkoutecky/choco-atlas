The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely encoded valuation types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), and square land whose area scales with household count \(N\), compute envy-free allocations of non-overlapping whole square plots to households, maximizing the minimum type utility relative to per-capita cake value; determine the limiting guarantee as \(N\) grows.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(T\) lists the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a nonnegative rational piecewise-constant density \(f_t\) on a common partition of \([0,1]^2\) into \(m\) axis-parallel rectangles with rational coordinates, normalized by \(\int f_t=1\).
For an integer population scale \(N\ge2\), the land is \(C_N=[0,\sqrt N]^2\), and \(V_{t,N}(A)=\int_{A/\sqrt N}f_t(x)\,dx\). Households form the nonatomic space \(H_N=\bigsqcup_{t\in T}\{t\}\times[0,N\mu_t]\), with Lebesgue mass.
An allocation measurably assigns each household one whole axis-parallel square contained in \(C_N\), or an empty plot; unused land is permitted. Square coordinates may be real, and utility is \(V_{t,N}\) of the assigned plot.
There must be a conull subset of households on which distinct households' plots have disjoint interiors and every household weakly prefers its own plot to every other household's plot.
Define \(g_N(T,\mu,f)=\sup_Q\min_{t\in T}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{h\in H_{N,t}}N V_{t,N}(Q_h)\), where the supremum ranges over these envy-free allocations. Thus utility is measured relative to per-capita cake value \(1/N\).
Positive-threshold feasibility asks whether such an allocation gives almost every household relative utility at least an input binary rational \(q>0\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\), and \(\log N\).

Is positive-threshold feasibility in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
What is \(\liminf_{N\to\infty}g_N(T,\mu,f)\) for a fixed encoded society, and its infimum over all such societies?

For two finite agents, a constructive envy-free square allocation guarantees \(1/4\) of each agent's cake value, and this universal guarantee is tight ([source](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/9309/9309-13-12837-1-2-20201228.pdf)).
For arbitrary finite \(n\), envy-free square allocations with value greater than \(1/(4n^2)\) per agent are known ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.03938v2)). No result for this exact nonatomic-household model was located.

Conclusion and interpretation

For the exact nonatomic-household model stated in the question, positive-threshold feasibility is in \(\mathrm{P}\): every valid instance with \(q>0\) is infeasible. Moreover, for every encoded society and every integer \(N\ge 2\),

\[ g_N(T,\mu,f)=0. \]

Consequently, both the requested lower limit and its infimum over societies equal \(0\).

The obstruction is assigning a whole square separately to each point of a nonatomic household space. It persists for every \(N\), every support size, and every permitted density. Below I also prove the stronger structural consequence of envy-freeness.

1. Countably many households can receive positive-area squares

Fix \(N\) and any admissible allocation. Let \(S\subseteq H_N\) be the conull subset on which distinct households’ plots have disjoint interiors. Define

\[ A=\{h\in S:Q_h\text{ has positive area}\}. \]

Claim. \(A\) is countable.

Proof. Enumerate the rational points of the plane as \((r_j)_{j\ge 1}\). Every positive-area square has nonempty Euclidean interior, which therefore contains a rational point. For each \(h\in A\), let \(j(h)\) be the least index satisfying \(r_{j(h)}\in\operatorname{int}(Q_h)\).

If distinct households \(h,k\in A\) had \(j(h)=j(k)\), their plots’ interiors would intersect at that rational point, contradicting the defining property of \(S\). Thus \(h\mapsto j(h)\) is an injection into the positive integers. This proves the claim.

Every singleton household has Lebesgue mass zero, so the countable set \(A\) also has household mass zero. Since \(S\) is conull, almost every household receives an empty or zero-area plot.

2. Almost every household has zero utility

For each type \(t\), the valuation is absolutely continuous with respect to planar Lebesgue measure. In particular, if a plot \(Q\) has area zero, then its scaled image \(Q/\sqrt N\) also has area zero and

\[ V_{t,N}(Q)=\int_{Q/\sqrt N}f_t(x)\,dx=0. \]

Together with Section 1, this proves that, for every \(t\in T\),

\[ N V_{t,N}(Q_h)=0 \quad\text{for almost every }h\in H_{N,t}. \]

Here the exceptional set remains null within each type interval because it is contained in the union of the globally null set \(H_N\setminus S\) and the countable set \(A\).

Each type interval has positive mass \(N\mu_t>0\), and utilities are nonnegative. Hence its essential infimum is exactly zero:

\[ \operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{h\in H_{N,t}}N V_{t,N}(Q_h)=0. \]

This conclusion already follows from interior-disjointness; envy-freeness is unnecessary for it.

3. Envy-freeness forces every conull plot to have zero value to every type

There is a stronger conclusion for the allocations in the question.

Claim. For every envy-free allocation and its witnessing conull set \(S\),

\[ V_{t,N}(Q_h)=0 \quad\text{for every }t\in T\text{ and every }h\in S. \]

Proof. Fix \(t\in T\). Since \(S\cap H_{N,t}\) has positive mass and \(A\) has mass zero, choose a household \(h_t\in(S\cap H_{N,t})\setminus A\). Its own utility is zero.

For every other \(h\in S\), envy-freeness gives

\[ 0=V_{t,N}(Q_{h_t})\ge V_{t,N}(Q_h). \]

Nonnegativity gives the reverse inequality. The plot of \(h_t\) itself also has value zero, proving the claim for all \(h\in S\). Since \(t\) was arbitrary, the claim follows.

Conversely, an allocation satisfying interior-disjointness on a conull set is envy-free there whenever every assigned plot on that set has zero value to every supported type: all comparisons are between zeros.

Thus, positive-area squares can occur on the witnessing conull set only if every supported type values them at zero.

4. Positive-threshold feasibility and running time

A quantitative packing bound makes the threshold obstruction explicit. Let \(M\) be the largest density value among all types and partition rectangles. The finite piecewise-constant encoding implies \(M<\infty\), and normalization implies \(M>0\).

For every physical plot \(Q\),

\[ N V_{t,N}(Q)\le M\,\operatorname{area}(Q). \]

Therefore a household whose relative utility is at least \(q>0\) must receive a square of physical area at least \(q/M\).

For any finite collection of such households in \(S\), disjoint interiors imply that their square areas sum to at most the land area \(N\). Consequently, the number of these households is at most

\[ \left\lfloor\frac{NM}{q}\right\rfloor. \]

Indeed, a larger collection would contain a finite subcollection violating this area bound. Thus only finitely many households in \(S\) can meet a fixed positive threshold. They have household mass zero, whereas \(H_N\) has mass \(N>0\).

The decision algorithm therefore returns NO on every valid input. Under the stated input promises, its decision time is \(O(1)\), independent of \(m,\tau,L,\log N\). If the complete encoding must first be read, the running time is \(O(L+\log N)\), or \(O(L)\) when \(L\) already includes the encoding of \(N\). No geometric computation or enumeration of households is required.

5. Exact optimum and asymptotics

Section 2 proves that every admissible allocation has objective value zero. Assigning the empty plot to every household is measurable, interior-disjoint, and envy-free, and achieves zero. Therefore

\[ g_N(T,\mu,f)=0 \quad\text{for every integer }N\ge 2. \]

It follows immediately that

\[ \liminf_{N\to\infty}g_N(T,\mu,f)=0, \]

and the infimum of this quantity over all encoded societies is also \(0\).

The finite-agent guarantees cited in the question concern a different household space: finitely many agents can each receive a positive-area square. They therefore do not contradict this obstruction. See the [two-agent paper](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/9309/9309-13-12837-1-2-20201228.pdf) and the [finite-agent paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.03938v2). Replacing the nonatomic households with finitely many households would change the mathematical problem; increasing \(N\) within the stated model does not remove the obstruction.
