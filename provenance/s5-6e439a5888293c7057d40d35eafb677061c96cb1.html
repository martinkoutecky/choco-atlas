The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite list of societies \(h\), each specified by rational masses \(\mu^h_{\theta,r}\) over bidder classes \(\theta\) and signals \(r\), and explicit positive values or costs \(v^h_{\theta,r}\) or \(c^h_{\theta,r}\), compute a truthful selection policy minimizing the worst-case approximation ratio across societies. The policy chooses intensities \(q^h_{\theta,r}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{\theta,r}\mu^h_{\theta,r}q^h_{\theta,r}=1\); truthfulness is defined by incentive constraints on per-capita utility rates, holding the aggregate society fixed.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Input consists of \(H\ge 1\) publicly observed societies, \(m\ge 1\) publicly known bidder classes, and a signal alphabet \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\), with \(k\ge 1\); only signals are privately reported.
Each society is given by its support \(S_h\): its list of \(\tau_h\) types \((\theta,r)\) with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, with \(\sum_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}\mu^h_{\theta,r}=1\). Set \(\tau=\max_h\tau_h\); this is support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An instance specifies either strictly positive binary rational values \(v^h_{\theta,r}\) or strictly positive binary rational costs \(c^h_{\theta,r}\) on each support; no monotonicity assumption is imposed.
A policy specifies allocation intensities \(q^h_{\theta,b}\ge 0\) and nonnegative payment rates \(p^h_{\theta,b}\ge 0\) for every class and reported signal, including reports absent from the support, with \(\sum_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}\mu^h_{\theta,r}q^h_{\theta,r}=1\). Intensities have no additional upper bound.
For a supported true type \((\theta,r)\), define \(u^h_\theta(b;r)=q^h_{\theta,b}v^h_{\theta,r}-p^h_{\theta,b}\) for goods, and \(u^h_\theta(b;r)=p^h_{\theta,b}-q^h_{\theta,b}c^h_{\theta,r}\) for chores. Require \(u^h_\theta(r;r)\ge u^h_\theta(b;r)\) for every signal report \(b\), and \(u^h_\theta(r;r)\ge 0\); deviations leave the society fixed. Payments are charged for goods and transferred for chores, with no budget constraint.
The objective is to compute a feasible policy minimizing \(R_V(q)=\max_h\frac{\max_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}v^h_{\theta,r}}{\sum_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}\mu^h_{\theta,r}q^h_{\theta,r}v^h_{\theta,r}}\), or, respectively, \(R_C(q)=\max_h\frac{\sum_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}\mu^h_{\theta,r}q^h_{\theta,r}c^h_{\theta,r}}{\min_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}c^h_{\theta,r}}\). Ties are allowed.

For each of the value and cost variants, is computing an optimal truthful policy polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L,H,k\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite bidders with explicitly tabulated interdependent values or costs, optimal randomized truthful allocation policies are computable in polynomial time in the full input length ([Loiseau–Mauras–Xu](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18668v1)).
No result settling this exact finite-support, fixed-society intensity optimization problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized each society's total mass to one, interpreting the stated distribution as a probability distribution.
  • Defined m as the number of publicly known bidder classes, k as the signal alphabet size, H as the number of societies, tau as the maximum society support size, and L as total input bit length.
  • Treated the society index as publicly observed and bidder class as fixed; only signals can be misreported, and an individual's deviation leaves the aggregate society unchanged.
  • Retained the full signal alphabet as the report domain, including signals absent from a society's support. Policy and payment menus are defined for those reports; utility and individual-rationality constraints apply to positive-mass true types.
  • Specified nonnegative per-capita payment rates, the goods and chores utility formulas, and individual rationality, following the discrete mechanism definition; imposed no budget constraint.
  • Interpreted intensities as allocation density rates without an upper bound, as stipulated by the continuous question, rather than as individual selection probabilities.
  • Defined the approximation benchmarks as the maximum supported value or minimum supported cost, and policy performance as the mass-weighted selected value or cost.
  • Required strictly positive rational values or costs, allowed ties and nonmonotone data, and used minimization as intended by the discrete optimization definitions.

Result

Both the value and cost variants are polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L,H,k\). Each admits a rational linear program of polynomial size, whose optimal solution yields an optimal truthful policy.

The proof below applies directly to the specified intensity model. It does not require transferring the result from the related finite-bidder model in [Loiseau–Mauras–Xu, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18668v1).

1. Reports absent from the support

For each society and class, define
\(\mathcal R_{h,\theta}=\{r:(\theta,r)\in S_h\}\).
Write \(N=\sum_h\tau_h\), so \(N\le H\tau\).

Lemma. It suffices to optimize allocation and payment variables for supported types. Every absent report can receive allocation and payment zero.

Proof. Start with any feasible policy and replace
\(q^h_{\theta,b},p^h_{\theta,b}\)
by zero whenever \((\theta,b)\notin S_h\).

This preserves the normalization and objective, since both involve only supported types. It also preserves every truthfulness constraint between supported reports. A deviation to an absent report now gives utility zero in either variant. Individual rationality guarantees that every supported type's truthful utility is at least zero, so these deviations are unprofitable.

Conversely, suppose variables on supported types satisfy normalization, nonnegativity, individual rationality, and truthfulness against supported reports of the same class. Extending them by zero gives a feasible full policy by the same argument. Thus the restriction and extension preserve the achievable objective values. \(\square\)

In particular, classes with no supported type require no optimization variables.

2. Linear descriptions of feasible policies

Introduce \(q^h_{\theta,r},p^h_{\theta,r}\) only for \((\theta,r)\in S_h\). In both variants impose nonnegativity and normalization:

\[ \text{[equations below use the requested inline delimiters]} \]

\(q^h_{\theta,r}\ge 0,\qquad p^h_{\theta,r}\ge 0,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}\mu^h_{\theta,r}q^h_{\theta,r}=1\qquad\text{for every }h.\)

For goods, impose individual rationality:

\(q^h_{\theta,r}v^h_{\theta,r}-p^h_{\theta,r}\ge 0,\)

and, for every \(r,b\in\mathcal R_{h,\theta}\), impose truthfulness:

\(q^h_{\theta,r}v^h_{\theta,r}-p^h_{\theta,r} \ge q^h_{\theta,b}v^h_{\theta,r}-p^h_{\theta,b}.\)

For chores, the corresponding constraints are

\(p^h_{\theta,r}-q^h_{\theta,r}c^h_{\theta,r}\ge 0,\)

\(p^h_{\theta,r}-q^h_{\theta,r}c^h_{\theta,r} \ge p^h_{\theta,b}-q^h_{\theta,b}c^h_{\theta,r}.\)

All these constraints are linear: masses, values, and costs are input constants. By the lemma, these constraints describe exactly the restrictions of feasible full policies. Denote their feasible sets by \(\mathcal F_V\) and \(\mathcal F_C\).

Both sets are nonempty. For goods, take \(q=1,p=0\) on every supported type. All supported reports then give a true type the same utility, and individual rationality holds because values are positive. Normalization follows from the masses summing to one.

For chores, take \(q=1\) and \(p=D_h\) on every supported type in society \(h\), where \(D_h=\max_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}c^h_{\theta,r}\). All supported reports give a true type utility \(D_h-c^h_{\theta,r}\ge 0\), and normalization again holds.

3. The value objective

Define

\(M_h=\max_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}v^h_{\theta,r},\qquad A_h(q)=\displaystyle\sum_{(\theta,r)\in S_h} \frac{\mu^h_{\theta,r}v^h_{\theta,r}}{M_h}q^h_{\theta,r}.\)

For any feasible allocation, the numbers
\(\mu^h_{\theta,r}q^h_{\theta,r}\)
are nonnegative and sum to one. Consequently,

\(0<\displaystyle\frac{\min_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}v^h_{\theta,r}}{M_h} \le A_h(q)\le 1.\)

The strict positivity uses the strictly positive supported values. Therefore the objective satisfies the exact identity

\(R_V(q)=\displaystyle\max_h\frac{1}{A_h(q)} =\frac{1}{\min_h A_h(q)}.\)

Consider the linear program

\(\displaystyle\text{maximize }t,\)

\(\displaystyle\text{subject to }(q,p)\in\mathcal F_V,\qquad 0\le t,\qquad t\le A_h(q)\ \text{for every }h.\)

This program is feasible with positive \(t\): the feasible allocation \(q=1\) permits

\(t=\displaystyle\min_h \frac{\sum_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}\mu^h_{\theta,r}v^h_{\theta,r}}{M_h}>0.\)

Its objective is bounded above by one. Thus its optimum \(t_V^\star\) exists and is positive.

For an optimal solution \((q^\star,p^\star,t_V^\star)\), one must have
\(t_V^\star=\min_h A_h(q^\star)\).
Otherwise, increasing \(t\) to this minimum would improve the LP objective while preserving feasibility.

Moreover, every feasible policy \((q,p)\) gives an LP-feasible solution with \(t=\min_h A_h(q)\). Hence
\(\min_h A_h(q)\le t_V^\star\).
Taking reciprocals of these positive quantities proves

\(R_V(q)\ge \displaystyle\frac{1}{t_V^\star} =R_V(q^\star).\)

Thus the LP computes an optimal truthful policy and the exact optimal ratio \(1/t_V^\star\).

4. The cost objective

Define

\(C_h=\min_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}c^h_{\theta,r},\qquad B_h(q)=\displaystyle\sum_{(\theta,r)\in S_h} \frac{\mu^h_{\theta,r}c^h_{\theta,r}}{C_h}q^h_{\theta,r}.\)

Strict positivity gives \(C_h>0\). Normalization gives \(B_h(q)\ge 1\), since \(B_h(q)\) is a weighted average of quantities \(c^h_{\theta,r}/C_h\ge 1\).

The objective is exactly \(R_C(q)=\max_h B_h(q)\). Consider the linear program

\(\displaystyle\text{minimize }t,\)

\(\displaystyle\text{subject to }(q,p)\in\mathcal F_C,\qquad 0\le t,\qquad B_h(q)\le t\ \text{for every }h.\)

The feasible chore policy constructed above permits the finite choice

\(t=\displaystyle\max_h \frac{\sum_{(\theta,r)\in S_h}\mu^h_{\theta,r}c^h_{\theta,r}}{C_h}.\)

Every feasible LP solution has \(t\ge 1\), so the objective is bounded below and its optimum \(t_C^\star\) exists.

At an optimal solution, \(t_C^\star=\max_h B_h(q^\star)\); otherwise lowering \(t\) to that maximum improves the objective. Every feasible policy can likewise be paired with \(t=\max_h B_h(q)\). Therefore the LP computes an optimal truthful policy, with exact optimal ratio \(t_C^\star\).

5. Polynomial size and exact computation

Each LP has \(2N+1\) variables. Let \(n_{h,\theta}=|\mathcal R_{h,\theta}|\). The number of supported-report truthfulness constraints is

\(\displaystyle\sum_{h,\theta}n_{h,\theta}^2 \le\sum_h\left(\sum_\theta n_{h,\theta}\right)^2 =\sum_h\tau_h^2 \le H\tau^2.\)

The other constraints number \(O(N+H)\). Thus each LP has polynomial size in \(H,\tau,L\).

Every coefficient is rational. Selecting \(M_h,C_h\), and forming the products and quotients in the objective constraints, requires rational arithmetic with polynomial bit complexity. Each resulting coefficient has bit length polynomial in \(L\).

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a rational LP can be solved in time polynomial in its encoding length, returning an exact rational optimal solution when it is feasible and its objective is bounded. The preceding sections verify both hypotheses for each LP. This theorem also guarantees polynomial bit length for the returned solution; boundedness of every feasible variable is unnecessary.

Finally, extend the supported solution by zero to all absent reports. Writing the explicit full policy requires \(Hmk\) allocation entries and \(Hmk\) payment entries. Consequently, both variants are solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,H,k\).

No monotonicity assumption is used. All incentive constraints are weak inequalities, so ties are allowed throughout.
