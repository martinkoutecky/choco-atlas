$$ v_j=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta z_{\theta j}. $$

$$ c_x(p)\le1 $$

Conversely, the displayed conditions imply $b_x\in[\ell,1]$, make $x$ affordable, and make every strictly preferred bundle unaffordable. The strict ranking then makes $x$ the unique demand. $\square$

Also, every static equilibrium satisfies
$$ 0\le p_j\le1. $$

3. Totality and membership in $\mathrm{PPAD}$

$$ N=\sum_\theta|\Psi_\theta|, \qquad R=\max_\theta(|\Psi_\theta|-1). $$

$$ r_\theta(x)\in\{0,\ldots,|\Psi_\theta|-1\}, $$
increasing with preference, with $r_\theta(0)=0$. Define
$$ K=\frac{R+1}{\varepsilon_b}, \qquad U_{\theta x}(p) =r_\theta(x)-K\max\{0,p\cdot x-\ell\}. $$

These auxiliary utilities are continuous piecewise-linear functions with polynomial-bit rational coefficients.

Two demand properties

First, every utility-maximizing bundle has cost strictly below $1$. The zero bundle has utility $0$, whereas $p\cdot x\ge1$ implies
$$ U_{\theta x}(p) \le R-K\varepsilon_b =-1. $$

Second, every utility-maximizing bundle $x$ is demanded at
$$ b_x=\max\{\ell,p\cdot x\}. $$

This budget belongs to $[\ell,1)$. If a preferred bundle $y$ were affordable there, then
$$ \max\{0,p\cdot y-\ell\} \le\max\{0,p\cdot x-\ell\}. $$

Since $r_\theta(y)>r_\theta(x)$, this would give
$$ U_{\theta y}(p)>U_{\theta x}(p), $$
a contradiction.

A continuous fixed-point map

Use variables
$$ (p,q)\in[0,1]^{m+N}, $$
and write
$$ D_j(q)=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\sum_{x\in\Psi_\theta}q_{\theta x}x_j. $$

Let $\Delta_\theta$ be the probability simplex on $\Psi_\theta$, and let $\Pi_{\Delta_\theta}$ denote Euclidean projection onto it. Define
$$ F_j^p(p,q) =\min\{1,\max\{0,p_j+D_j(q)-\kappa_j\}\}, $$
$$ F_\theta^q(p,q) =\Pi_{\Delta_\theta}\bigl(q_\theta+U_\theta(p)\bigr). $$

This is a continuous self-map of a nonempty compact convex cube. Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem therefore gives a fixed point.

At a fixed point, $q_\theta\in\Delta_\theta$. The projection optimality condition gives
$$ U_\theta(p)\cdot(z-q_\theta)\le0 \quad\text{for every }z\in\Delta_\theta. $$

Thus $q_\theta$ maximizes the corresponding linear functional, so every positively weighted bundle maximizes auxiliary utility.

No price coordinate can equal $1$: if $p_j=1$, every bundle containing $j$ costs at least $1$, so $D_j(q)=0$. But then
$$ F_j^p(p,q) =\min\{1,\max\{0,1-\kappa_j\}\}<1, $$
contradicting the fixed-point equation.

For the remaining prices, that equation implies
$$ p_j=0\Longrightarrow D_j(q)\le\kappa_j, $$
$$ 0<p_j<1\Longrightarrow D_j(q)=\kappa_j. $$

The witnessing budgets established above therefore turn this fixed point into a static equilibrium.

Why the map has a polynomial-size linear circuit

For a vector $v$ of length $a$, sort its coordinates as
$$ v_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge v_{(a)}. $$

Then simplex projection is
$$ t=\max_{1\le h\le a} \frac{\sum_{i=1}^h v_{(i)}-1}{h}, \qquad \Pi_\Delta(v)_i=\max\{v_i-t,0\}. $$

To verify this formula, let $t_*$ be the unique number satisfying
$$ \sum_i\max\{v_i-t_*,0\}=1. $$

Every prefix satisfies
$$ \sum_{i=1}^h v_{(i)}-ht_*\le1. $$

Equality holds for the nonempty prefix consisting of coordinates strictly above $t_*$. Hence the displayed maximum is exactly $t_*$. The resulting vector is nonnegative and sums to $1$. On its positive coordinates, $v_i-\Pi_\Delta(v)_i=t_*$; on its zero coordinates, $v_i\le t_*$. These facts imply the projection optimality condition.

Sorting can be implemented with polynomially many $\min$ and $\max$ gates. The complete map consequently uses only addition, subtraction, $\min$, $\max$, and multiplication by rational constants.

Etessami and Yannakakis’s Theorem 26 states that exact fixed-point search for such circuits, $\mathrm{Linear\text{-}FIXP}$, equals $\mathrm{PPAD}$. The cube domain and polynomial-size rational circuit satisfy its hypotheses. Thus the present construction belongs to $\mathrm{PPAD}$. [Etessami–Yannakakis, Theorem 26](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/kousha/nash_focs07_full_j_spec_issue_sub.pdf)

5. Polynomial-time construction for fixed $m$

This section gives an additional algorithmic result beyond the supplied notes.

Consider the following affine functions of prices:
$$ p_j,\qquad p_j-1, $$
$$ p\cdot x-\ell,\qquad p\cdot x-1, $$
and, for bundles of the same type,
$$ p\cdot(y-x). $$

Constant functions have a fixed sign and can be omitted from the arrangement. The remaining zero sets form at most
$$ H=O(m+N^2) $$
hyperplanes.

Partition the price cube $[0,1]^m$ by the signs of these functions, allowing signs $-$, $0$, and $+$. Lower-dimensional faces are included.

On each nonempty sign cell:

  • The set $J$ of positive-price goods is constant.
  • Every reachable-bundle set $S_\theta(p)$ is constant, by the lemma in Section 2.

Thus, after choosing any representative price vector in a cell, the existence of an equilibrium using that cell reduces to the probability LP
$$ q_{\theta x}\ge0, \qquad \sum_{x\in S_\theta}q_{\theta x}=1, $$
$$ D_j(q)\le\kappa_j, \qquad D_j(q)=\kappa_j\quad(j\in J). $$

There are no price variables in this LP.

Enumerating all cells

The cells can be enumerated incrementally. When inserting a hyperplane, test the three possible extensions of each existing sign pattern.

A proposed pattern is feasible exactly when the following LP has positive optimum $\delta$:
$$ 0\le p_j\le1, \qquad 0\le\delta\le1, $$
$$ h_i(p)=0\quad\text{for zero signs}, $$
$$ h_i(p)\ge\delta\quad\text{for positive signs}, $$
$$ -h_i(p)\ge\delta\quad\text{for negative signs}. $$

If there are no strict constraints, maximizing $\delta$ simply gives $1$. Otherwise, feasibility of the strict sign pattern supplies a positive common margin. Conversely, a positive margin certifies the pattern. This also produces a polynomial-bit rational representative.

For completeness, the number of faces of an arrangement of $H$ hyperplanes in dimension $m$ is at most
$$ \sum_{r=0}^m2^r\binom Hr. $$

One proof uses the recurrence
$$ f(H,m)\le f(H-1,m)+2f(H-1,m-1). $$

An existing face that is cut by the new hyperplane is replaced by two sides and their intersection, increasing the count by two. Its intersection is a distinct face of the induced arrangement on the new hyperplane. The recurrence, with $f(0,m)=f(H,0)=1$, gives the displayed bound. Intersecting cells with the cube cannot increase their number, because each intersection is convex.

The incremental procedure therefore uses
$$ O(H^{m+1}) $$
LP calls, each with polynomial input size.

For fixed $m$, this is polynomial time. Section 3 guarantees that at least one cell has a feasible probability LP. Its rational representative, its probabilities, and the witnessing budgets give an exact static construction; Section 1 supplies the initial allocation and all dynamic conditions.

Proven conclusion: for every fixed $m$, the entire exact construction problem is polynomial-time solvable. The running-time exponent grows with $m$, so this does not give a polynomial-time algorithm when $m$ is part of the input.

6. $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hardness

I state precisely the external hardness result used here.

Othman, Papadimitriou, and Rubinstein prove that computing a finite-agent
$$ \left(\sqrt{\frac{km}{2}},\beta\right) $$
approximate CEEI is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-complete for a suitable polynomially small positive $\beta$. Here $k$ bounds bundle cardinality, and budgets belong to $[1,1+\beta]$. Their hardness construction uses polynomial-size explicit preference lists over bundles and positive integer capacities. The listed acceptable bundles can be followed by the empty outside option, ranked last. [The Complexity of Fairness through Equilibrium, Theorem 2 and hardness gadgets](https://arxiv.org/html/1312.6249v3)

The transfer to our problem requires a rounding lemma.

Rounding lemma

Suppose $n$ agents have rational lotteries over binary bundles of cardinality at most $k$. Let $T$ be the aggregate expected allocation. In polynomial time, one can select one supported bundle per agent, obtaining aggregate allocation $a$ with
$$ \|a-T\|_2^2\le\frac{km}{2}. $$

Proof. On the given supports, consider
$$ \lambda_{ix}\ge0, \qquad \sum_x\lambda_{ix}=1, \qquad \sum_i\sum_x\lambda_{ix}x=T. $$

This bounded rational polytope is nonempty. Find a vertex by the successive coordinate-optimization procedure described in Section 4.

If $s_i$ is the number of positive variables for agent $i$, linear independence of positive columns gives
$$ \sum_i s_i\le n+m, \qquad \sum_i(s_i-1)\le m. $$

Independently draw $X_i$ from these vertex lotteries. For an independent copy $X_i'$,
$$ \mathbb E\|X_i-\mathbb EX_i\|_2^2 =\frac12\mathbb E\|X_i-X_i'\|_2^2. $$

Two binary bundles of cardinality at most $k$ have squared distance at most $2k$, and equal draws have distance zero. Hence
$$ \mathbb E\|X_i-\mathbb EX_i\|_2^2 \le k\left(1-\sum_x\lambda_{ix}^2\right). $$

For $s_i\ge2$, Cauchy–Schwarz and elementary algebra give
$$ 1-\sum_x\lambda_{ix}^2 \le1-\frac1{s_i} \le\frac{s_i-1}{2}. $$

For $s_i=1$, the variance is zero. Independence eliminates cross terms, so
$$ \mathbb E\left\|\sum_iX_i-T\right\|_2^2 \le\frac{k}{2}\sum_i(s_i-1) \le\frac{km}{2}. $$

Derandomize by conditional expectations. If some choices have been fixed, write their sum as $a_0$, and let the remaining independent draws have total mean $M$. The conditional expectation is
$$ \|a_0+M-T\|_2^2 +\sum_{\text{remaining }i} \mathbb E\|X_i-\mathbb EX_i\|_2^2. $$

For each candidate next bundle, this is computable by rational arithmetic. Choose a candidate minimizing it. The minimum does not exceed the probability-weighted average, so the conditional expectation never increases. At termination it is the actual squared error. $\square$

Reduction

Take an instance of the cited finite-agent problem, with $n$ agents and capacities $c_j>0$. It is enough to make each agent a support type, setting
$$ \mu_i=\frac1n, \qquad \kappa_j=\frac{c_j}{n}. $$

Identical types may optionally be combined. The support size is at most $n$, and the transformation is polynomial in the explicit input length.

Choose
$$ \varepsilon_b= \min\left\{\frac{\beta}{1+\beta},\frac1{2m}\right\}, \qquad \varepsilon_n=\varepsilon_f=1. $$

These satisfy all stipulated error restrictions.

A static solution to our problem gives finite-agent lotteries with aggregate expectation
$$ T=nd, $$
satisfying
$$ T_j\le c_j, \qquad p_j>0\Longrightarrow T_j=c_j. $$

Apply the rounding lemma, retaining for every selected bundle a budget witnessing its demand.

For the finite-agent clearing-error vector, define
$$ z_j= \begin{cases} a_j-c_j,&p_j>0,\\ \max\{a_j-c_j,0\},&p_j=0. \end{cases} $$

If $p_j>0$, then $z_j=a_j-T_j$. If $p_j=0$, then $T_j\le c_j$, which implies
$$ |z_j|\le|a_j-T_j|. $$

Therefore
$$ \|z\|_2^2\le\|a-T\|_2^2\le\frac{km}{2}. $$

Finally, divide prices and the selected budgets by $\ell$. This preserves every affordability comparison and hence every demanded bundle. The scaled budgets belong to
$$ [1,1/\ell]\subseteq[1,1+\beta], $$
because $\varepsilon_b\le\beta/(1+\beta)$.

We have obtained the approximate CEEI required by the external hardness theorem. This proves $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hardness, and together with Section 3 proves $\mathrm{PPAD}$-completeness.

The reduction uses the conventional search formulation with polynomially bounded solution descriptions, whose existence Section 4 proves. For unrestricted finite descriptions, the decoding procedure is polynomial in the input and returned-output lengths. Any polynomial-time construction algorithm necessarily returns polynomial-length output.

7. What remains unresolved

The construction has polynomial-size rational witnesses, is total, and has polynomial-time verification. Verification scans the explicit rankings to check each budget’s demand, checks probabilities and static clearing, and checks the initial constant-flow schedule. Section 1 then proves all required dynamic inequalities. Thus the polynomially bounded search formulation belongs to $\mathrm{TFNP}$.

This explains why ordinary decision-problem $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness is not an automatic consequence of the equilibrium hardness.

More precisely, suppose there were a polynomial-time reduction from satisfiability to this total search problem, together with a polynomial-time decoder such that every valid construction decoded to the correct satisfiability answer. An unsatisfiable formula would then have a polynomial-size certificate consisting of a valid construction whose decoded answer is “unsatisfiable.” Totality supplies that certificate. For a satisfiable formula, correctness of the decoder excludes such a certificate. Therefore
$$ \mathrm{UNSAT}\in\mathrm{NP}, $$
which implies
$$ \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}. $$

This implication concerns the stated reduction convention; it is not an unconditional separation theorem.

The proved classification is therefore $\mathrm{PPAD}$-completeness, with a polynomial-time algorithm for fixed $m$. A polynomial-time algorithm for unrestricted $m$ would yield polynomial-time algorithms for every $\mathrm{PPAD}$ search problem. Neither such an algorithm nor $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness under the specified decision-decoding convention is established here.
