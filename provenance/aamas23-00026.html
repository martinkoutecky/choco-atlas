Corollary 5.16 — class: algorithmic

We can find an optimal house allocation for an instance on an undirected 𝑛-agent graph 𝐺 that is the disjoint union of cliques in time 𝑂(𝑛𝑟+2), where 𝑟 is the number of cliques. The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite catalogue \(S\) of clique sizes, rational component masses \(\rho_s\), and rational masses \(\nu_j\) of house values \(v_1<\cdots<v_q\) satisfying \(\sum_{s\in S}s\rho_s=\sum_{j=1}^q\nu_j\), choose masses \(\lambda_{s,b}\) over nondecreasing bundles \(b\in\{v_1,\ldots,v_q\}^s\) such that \(\sum_b\lambda_{s,b}=\rho_s\) and \(\sum_{s,b}\mathrm{mult}_{v_j}(b)\lambda_{s,b}=\nu_j\); minimize \(\sum_{s,b}\lambda_{s,b}\sum_{1\le p<\ell\le s}(b_\ell-b_p)\), or decide whether the optimum is at most a given rational threshold.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given an undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\) with \(|V|=n\), distinct rational values \(a_1<\cdots<a_n\), and rational \(B\), let \(S_n\) be the bijections \(\pi:V o[n]\). For \(\pi\in S_n\), define \(c(\pi)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}|a_{\pi(u)}-a_{\pi(v)}|\).

A feasible atomless allocation is a distribution \(\lambda\in\Delta(S_n)\), where \(\lambda_\pi\ge0\) and \(\sum_{\pi\in S_n}\lambda_\pi=1\); its aggregate envy is \(\sum_{\pi\in S_n}\lambda_\pi c(\pi)\). Decide whether some feasible \(\lambda\) has aggregate envy at most \(B\).

Target: Is this continuous graphical-house-allocation decision problem NP-hard, or solvable in polynomial time?

Nearest known results: The corresponding discrete problem, which asks whether a single bijection \(\pi\) satisfies \(c(\pi)\le B\), is NP-hard even with identical valuations, by a linear-time reduction from Minimum Bisection. No result for the atomless distributional formulation is assumed here.

Prover output (gpt-5.6-sol / xhigh, Bolzano prover prompt)

Conclusion

The continuous distributional problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. In particular, allowing distributions over bijections does not improve the optimum: a linear objective over the probability simplex attains its minimum at a single bijection.

Convexification lemma

Let

\[ m(G,a)=\min_{\pi\in S_n} c(\pi). \]

This minimum exists because \(S_n\) is finite and nonempty.

For every \(\lambda\in\Delta(S_n)\), the inequality \(c(\pi)\geq m(G,a)\) holds for every \(\pi\in S_n\). Hence

\[ \sum_{\pi\in S_n}\lambda_\pi c(\pi) \geq \sum_{\pi\in S_n}\lambda_\pi m(G,a) = m(G,a)\sum_{\pi\in S_n}\lambda_\pi = m(G,a). \]

Conversely, choose a minimizer \(\pi^\star\in S_n\), so that \(c(\pi^\star)=m(G,a)\). Define the Dirac distribution

\[ \lambda^{\star}_{\pi^\star}=1, \qquad \lambda^{\star}_{\pi}=0 \quad\text{for every }\pi\neq\pi^\star. \]

This distribution belongs to \(\Delta(S_n)\), and its aggregate envy is

\[ \sum_{\pi\in S_n}\lambda^\star_\pi c(\pi) = c(\pi^\star) = m(G,a). \]

Therefore

\[ \min_{\lambda\in\Delta(S_n)} \sum_{\pi\in S_n}\lambda_\pi c(\pi) = \min_{\pi\in S_n}c(\pi). \]

Equivalence of the decision problems

The preceding identity gives

\[ \exists\,\lambda\in\Delta(S_n): \sum_{\pi\in S_n}\lambda_\pi c(\pi)\leq B \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists\,\pi\in S_n:c(\pi)\leq B. \]

Indeed, the forward implication follows because the minimum single-bijection cost is at most every distributional average. The reverse implication follows by placing probability \(1\) on the qualifying bijection.

Thus the continuous problem and the corresponding discrete problem define exactly the same language of yes-instances.

Complexity consequence

By the stated known result, deciding whether there is a bijection \(\pi\) with \(c(\pi)\leq B\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The identity transformation, which leaves \(G\), \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), and \(B\) unchanged, is therefore a polynomial-time many-one reduction from the discrete problem to the continuous problem. Hence the continuous problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

It also belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). A certificate is a bijection \(\pi:V\to[n]\). One can verify bijectivity and compute

\[ c(\pi)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E} \left|a_{\pi(u)}-a_{\pi(v)}\right| \]

using exact rational arithmetic in time polynomial in the binary input length. The sum contains at most \(\binom{n}{2}\) rational terms, and the bit lengths of the intermediate numerators and denominators remain polynomially bounded. The verifier then compares \(c(\pi)\) with \(B\).

Consequently, the continuous graphical-house-allocation decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. This conclusion uses the stated definition \(\Delta(S_n)\), which permits Dirac distributions.
