The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given worker types \(\Theta\) with masses \(\mu_\theta\), entitlement intensities \(e_\theta\) satisfying \(\sum_\theta \mu_\theta e_\theta=1\), class-level ordinal rankings \(\pi_\theta\), class-constant competences \(u_{\theta b}\), and chore classes \(B\) with per-capita supplies \(\rho_b\), where \(N\rho_b\) indivisible copies of class \(b\) are interchangeable and tied in the ordinal domain, find masses \(z_{\theta,S}\) over integral class-count bundles \(S\) such that \(\sum_S z_{\theta,S}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,S}z_{\theta,S}k_b(S)=\rho_b\), every supported bundle injects into \(q_\theta=\lfloor\rho e_\theta\rfloor+1\) WSD-PROP1 slots with class rank \(R_\theta(b)\ge(\ell-1)/e_\theta\), and \(\sum_S z_{\theta,S}k_b(S)=\mu_\theta e_\theta\rho_b\) for every \(\theta,b\) to realize the BoBW marginal guarantee.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) worker types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) chore classes \(B\), with positive binary rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_b\); write \(\rho=\sum_b\rho_b\). Each type has a positive binary rational entitlement intensity \(e_\theta\), with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta e_\theta=1\), a strict ordering \(\pi_\theta\) of classes from greatest to least disutility, and binary rational competences \(u_{\theta b}\in[0,1]\). Copies within a class are tied and interchangeable; competences impose no additional constraint or objective.
Define the cumulative supply rank \(R_\theta(b)=\sum_{c:\pi_\theta^{-1}(c)\leq\pi_\theta^{-1}(b)}\rho_c\) and \(q_\theta=\lfloor\rho e_\theta\rfloor+1\). A bundle \(S\) is an integral count vector \(k(S)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\), including the empty bundle.
Call \(S\) admissible for type \(\theta\) if its individual chore occurrences can be injected into distinct slots \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,q_\theta\}\), with an occurrence of class \(b\) eligible precisely when \(R_\theta(b)\geq(\ell-1)/e_\theta\). This is the WSD-PROP1 bundle condition used here.
Find a finite list of nonnegative binary rational bundle masses \(z_{\theta,S}\), with binary-encoded bundle counts, satisfying \(\sum_S z_{\theta,S}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,S}z_{\theta,S}k_b(S)=\rho_b\), and \(\sum_Sz_{\theta,S}k_b(S)=\mu_\theta e_\theta\rho_b\) for every \(\theta,b\); every bundle with positive mass must be admissible. The last equalities are the uniform entitlement marginals required for the best-of-both-worlds guarantee. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Is this continuous allocation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is its feasibility problem NP-hard?

For explicitly listed agents and indivisible chores with strict item rankings, a lottery with uniform entitlement marginals, ex-ante weighted SD envy-freeness, and ex-post WSD-PROP1 is computable in polynomial time ([Prakash and Nimbhorkar](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15479)).
No result settling this exact continuous, binary-encoded class-supply formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized worker masses to sum to one; defined rho as total per-capita chore supply and m as the number of chore classes.
  • Required positive rational supplies and entitlement intensities. Intensities have no upper bound of one: they represent population-scaled individual entitlements.
  • Defined the previously unspecified class rank as cumulative per-capita supply through the class, ordered from greatest to least disutility. Tied copies share the cumulative right endpoint; class indices alone do not measure supply rank.
  • Used strict rankings between classes and ties only within classes.
  • Treated N as a replication interpretation rather than an input or a finite-population constraint; the requested allocation is a distribution of integral bundles over worker mass.
  • Allowed empty bundles, consistent with unused slots and the intended up-to-one-chore relaxation.
  • Specified competence values in [0,1], as in the discrete model; retained them as input without adding an optimization objective absent from the graded question.
  • Specified a finite rational bundle-mass representation with binary-encoded integral counts and total input bit length L.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Polynomial-time construction

Every valid instance is feasible. An exact allocation can be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), using at most \(\tau(m+1)\) bundles with positive mass.

The construction rounds cumulative entitlement supplies with a common shift. Its proof is self-contained; it does not require extending the explicitly listed matching formulation of [Prakash and Nimbhorkar](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15479).

1. Characterization of admissible bundles

Fix a type \(\theta\). Write its classes in decreasing disutility order as \(b_1,\ldots,b_m\), and define

\[ a_0=0,\qquad a_i=e_\theta\sum_{r=1}^{i}\rho_{b_r}, \qquad c_i=\lfloor a_i\rfloor+1. \]

Thus \(a_m=e_\theta\rho\) and \(c_m=q_\theta\). Because supplies and entitlement intensities are positive, the sequence \(a_i\) is strictly increasing.

An occurrence of class \(b_i\) is eligible for slot \(\ell\) exactly when

\[ \ell-1\le e_\theta R_\theta(b_i)=a_i. \]

Consequently, its eligible slots are precisely \(1,\ldots,c_i\).

Lemma. A nonnegative integral count vector \(k\) is admissible for \(\theta\) if and only if

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{i}k_{b_r}\le c_i \qquad\text{for every }i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}. \]

Proof. For necessity, every occurrence among classes \(b_1,\ldots,b_i\) must occupy one of the first \(c_i\) slots: its own eligible-slot set is contained in that set. Injectivity therefore gives the inequality.

For sufficiency, list all occurrences in class order \(b_1,\ldots,b_m\), and assign the \(j\)-th occurrence to slot \(j\). If that occurrence has class \(b_i\), then

\[ j\le\sum_{r=1}^{i}k_{b_r}\le c_i. \]

Hence its slot is eligible. The inequality for \(i=m\) ensures that every assigned slot is at most \(q_\theta\). The assignment is injective. The empty bundle satisfies the same characterization. \(\square\)

2. A finite rounding construction

Continue with this fixed type. For \(t\in[0,1)\), define counts

\[ k_{b_i}(t)=\lfloor a_i+t\rfloor-\lfloor a_{i-1}+t\rfloor. \]

These are nonnegative integers because \(a_i>a_{i-1}\). Also, \(\lfloor a_0+t\rfloor=0\), so their prefix sums telescope:

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{i}k_{b_r}(t)=\lfloor a_i+t\rfloor \le \lfloor a_i\rfloor+1=c_i. \]

The lemma proves that every resulting bundle is admissible.

Only finitely many bundles arise. Let \(f_i=a_i-\lfloor a_i\rfloor\) be the fractional part of \(a_i\). Form the set of rational endpoints

\[ T=\{0,1\}\cup\{1-f_i:1\le i\le m,\ f_i>0\}. \]

Remove duplicates and sort them:

\[ 0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_s=1. \]

There are at most \(m+2\) endpoints, so \(s\le m+1\). Each function \(\lfloor a_i+t\rfloor\) is constant on every half-open interval \([t_j,t_{j+1})\). Therefore the entire count vector is constant there.

For each \(j\in\{0,\ldots,s-1\}\), output the bundle \(S_j\) with count vector \(k(t_j)\) and mass

\[ z_{\theta,S_j}=\mu_\theta(t_{j+1}-t_j). \]

If an identical bundle appears more than once, combine its masses. All masses are positive rational numbers, and every output bundle is admissible.

3. Exact verification of the marginals

The interval lengths sum to one, giving

\[ \sum_j z_{\theta,S_j} =\mu_\theta\sum_j(t_{j+1}-t_j) =\mu_\theta. \]

To verify the class marginals, first consider any \(A\ge0\). Write \(A=n+f\), where \(n=\lfloor A\rfloor\) and \(0\le f<1\). On \([0,1)\), the value of \(\lfloor A+t\rfloor\) is \(n\) on an interval of length \(1-f\), and \(n+1\) on an interval of length \(f\). Thus

\[ \int_0^1\lfloor A+t\rfloor\,dt =n(1-f)+(n+1)f=A. \]

Our finite partition contains every breakpoint of the relevant floor functions. Consequently, for each class \(b_i\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_j(t_{j+1}-t_j)k_{b_i}(t_j) &=\int_0^1\bigl(\lfloor a_i+t\rfloor-\lfloor a_{i-1}+t\rfloor\bigr)\,dt\\ &=a_i-a_{i-1}\\ &=e_\theta\rho_{b_i}. \end{aligned} \]

Multiplying by \(\mu_\theta\) proves the required uniform entitlement marginal:

\[ \sum_j z_{\theta,S_j}k_{b_i}(S_j) =\mu_\theta e_\theta\rho_{b_i}. \]

Apply this construction independently to every supported type. For each class \(b\), normalization of the entitlement intensities then yields

\[ \sum_{\theta,S}z_{\theta,S}k_b(S) =\sum_\theta\mu_\theta e_\theta\rho_b =\rho_b. \]

Hence all requested mass, supply, marginal, and admissibility conditions hold.

4. Polynomial bit complexity

It remains to check that the construction is polynomial in the binary input length, rather than in the numerical supplies or slot counts.

Write \(\rho_b=p_b/d_b\) and \(e_\theta=h_\theta/g_\theta\), with positive integers encoded in binary. Define

\[ D=\prod_b d_b,\qquad H_\theta=g_\theta D. \]

For the fixed type, every cumulative quantity has the form

\[ a_i=\frac{N_i}{H_\theta}, \qquad N_i=h_\theta\sum_{r=1}^{i}p_{b_r}\frac{D}{d_{b_r}}. \]

The integers \(D,H_\theta,N_i\) have bit lengths bounded by \(O(L+\log m)\): products add factor bit lengths, and summing at most \(m\) terms adds at most \(O(\log m)\) bits.

Every endpoint has denominator \(H_\theta\). More explicitly, when \(N_i\bmod H_\theta\) is nonzero, its associated breakpoint is

\[ \frac{H_\theta-(N_i\bmod H_\theta)}{H_\theta}. \]

Thus endpoint generation and sorting use polynomially many operations on integers of polynomial bit length. At an endpoint \(t_j=T_j/H_\theta\), cumulative rounded counts are computed directly as

\[ \lfloor a_i+t_j\rfloor =\left\lfloor\frac{N_i+T_j}{H_\theta}\right\rfloor. \]

No chore occurrences or slots need to be enumerated.

If \(\mu_\theta=v_\theta/w_\theta\), the corresponding output mass is exactly

\[ z_{\theta,S_j} =\frac{v_\theta(T_{j+1}-T_j)}{w_\theta H_\theta}, \]

which also has polynomial bit length. If “binary rational” specifically means dyadic rational, these masses remain dyadic because the input denominators, and therefore the displayed denominator, are powers of two.

There are at most \(m+1\) bundles per type, each containing \(m\) binary-encoded counts. The algorithm performs \(O(\tau m^2)\) count computations and polynomially many additional arithmetic operations, all on numbers of polynomial bit length. Its running time and output length are therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

5. Conclusion

The continuous class-supply formulation is always feasible and polynomial-time solvable under the stated assumptions. The construction preserves the uniform entitlement marginals exactly and outputs at most \(\tau(m+1)\) admissible bundles. Competences require no processing because they impose neither constraints nor an objective.
