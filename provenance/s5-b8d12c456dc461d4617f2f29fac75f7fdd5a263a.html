The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) candidates, approval-ballot types \(p^1,\ldots,p^\tau\in\{0,1\}^m\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau\) summing to \(1\), and rational \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), find a committee \(x\in\{0,1\}^m\) minimizing \(\max\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{\tau}q_\ell\|p^\ell-x\|_1:0\le q_\ell\le\mu_\ell,\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{\tau}q_\ell=\alpha\}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) distinct approval types \(p^\ell\in\{0,1\}^m\), with \(m\ge2\), each having positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_\ell\), and \(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\tau}\mu_\ell=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval types.
The input also includes binary-rational \(\alpha\in(0,1)\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length. A committee is any \(x\in\{0,1\}^m\), with unrestricted cardinality, including the empty committee; any minimizing committee is acceptable.
Define \(F_\alpha(x)=\max\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{\tau}q_\ell\|p^\ell-x\|_1:q\in\mathbb R^\tau,\ 0\le q_\ell\le\mu_\ell,\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{\tau}q_\ell=\alpha\}\). Thus mass may be split within a type, and the objective is the unnormalized aggregate Hamming distance.

Is exact minimization of \(F_\alpha(x)\) over committees solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard, with \(\alpha\) part of the input?

For finite electorates, minimizing the sum of the \(k\) largest Hamming distances, for input \(1\le k<n\), has an exact compact mixed-integer linear formulation ([Ponce et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.09225)).
The discrete problem is NP-hard; its minimax special case has an NP-complete decision version even for balanced elections ([Amanatidis et al.](https://amanatidis.sites.aueb.gr/approval_aamas15.pdf)). No result treating exactly the mass-based formulation above was located.

  • Specified unrestricted committee cardinality, including the empty committee, following the anchor's binary-vector domain; imposed no optional committee-size constraint.
  • Specified at least two candidates for the multiwinner setting and at least one positive-mass support type.
  • Made support types distinct, with duplicate ballots represented by their combined mass.
  • Specified real-valued submasses, preserving the graded question's divisible-mass interpretation.
  • Specified that any optimum is acceptable, so no committee tie-breaking rule is required.
  • Defined L as total input bit length, including alpha, and made alpha an input parameter.

Result

Exact minimization is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, with \(\alpha\) part of the input. This holds even when every mass and \(\alpha\) are dyadic rationals with polynomial-length encodings. The associated decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

The reduction preserves the objective pointwise up to a positive scalar, so it works regardless of which minimizing committee an algorithm returns.

1. A lemma identifying the minimax regime

For a committee \(x\), define \(d_\ell(x)=\|p^\ell-x\|_1\) and \(D(x)=\max_{1\le\ell\le\tau}d_\ell(x)\).

Lemma. If \(0<\alpha\le\min_\ell\mu_\ell\), then every committee satisfies
\[ F_\alpha(x)=\alpha D(x). \]

Proof. Every feasible \(q\) satisfies
\[ \sum_{\ell=1}^{\tau}q_\ell d_\ell(x) \le D(x)\sum_{\ell=1}^{\tau}q_\ell =\alpha D(x). \]
Choose an index \(j\) with \(d_j(x)=D(x)\). Setting \(q_j=\alpha\) and \(q_\ell=0\) for \(\ell\ne j\) is feasible because \(\alpha\le\mu_j\). This choice attains \(\alpha D(x)\), proving the equality. \(\square\)

Thus, allowing mass to be split does not prevent an exact embedding of minimax: one farthest type can supply the entire selected mass.

2. Polynomial-time hardness reduction

We use the following published result: given an explicitly listed election with binary ballots \(b^1,\ldots,b^n\) and an integer radius \(r\), deciding whether there exists an unrestricted binary committee \(z\) satisfying
\[ \max_{1\le i\le n}\|b^i-z\|_1\le r \]
is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. This is the problem called MINIMAX in Amanatidis et al.; their definitions distinguish it from the cardinality-constrained variant, and Theorem 1 establishes hardness even for balanced elections. [Amanatidis et al., definitions and Theorem 1](https://amanatidis.sites.aueb.gr/approval_aamas15.pdf)

To ensure the target requirement \(m\ge2\), append two zero coordinates to every source ballot. This preserves the optimum: a center \((z,u_1,u_2)\) has distance
\[ \|(b^i,0,0)-(z,u_1,u_2)\|_1 =\|b^i-z\|_1+u_1+u_2, \]
so choosing both added coordinates to be zero is optimal.

Remove duplicate padded ballots and call the resulting distinct types \(p^1,\ldots,p^\tau\). Removing duplicates preserves the maximum distance for every committee.

Let
\[ h=\left\lceil\log_2\bigl(\max\{2,\tau\}\bigr)\right\rceil, \qquad N=2^h, \qquad \alpha=\frac1N. \]
Assign masses
\[ \mu_\ell=\frac1N\quad(1\le\ell<\tau), \qquad \mu_\tau=\frac{N-\tau+1}{N}. \]

These satisfy all target hypotheses:

  • The support types are distinct.
  • Since \(N\ge\tau\), every mass is positive and at least \(1/N=\alpha\).
  • Their sum is \((\tau-1+N-\tau+1)/N=1\).
  • Since \(N\ge2\), we have \(0<\alpha<1\).
  • All masses and \(\alpha\) are dyadic, with encoding length \(O(\log(\tau+1))\) per number.

Duplicate removal and construction are polynomial-time operations. The resulting society has total encoding length \(O(\tau m+\tau\log(\tau+1))\), hence polynomial in the explicitly listed source instance.

By the lemma, every target committee satisfies
\[ F_\alpha(x)=\frac1N\max_{1\le\ell\le\tau}\|p^\ell-x\|_1. \]
Consequently,
\[ \min_x F_\alpha(x)\le\frac rN \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \min_z\max_{1\le i\le n}\|b^i-z\|_1\le r. \]
The threshold \(r/N\) also has polynomial encoding length. This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction to the mass-based decision problem, proving \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

Moreover, any exact minimizing committee yields a minimax center after deleting the two added coordinates. Therefore, an algorithm returning any minimizing committee in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

3. Exact evaluation and decision membership

For completeness, consider the decision problem with an additional binary-rational threshold \(B\): does some committee satisfy \(F_\alpha(x)\le B\)?

A certificate is the committee \(x\). Its objective can be evaluated exactly in polynomial time.

Compute and reorder the distances and their corresponding masses so that \(d_1\ge\cdots\ge d_\tau\). Define \(M_0=0\) and \(M_j=\sum_{i=1}^j\mu_i\). Since \(M_\tau=1>\alpha\), there is a least index \(t\) with \(M_t\ge\alpha\). Then
\[ F_\alpha(x) =\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\mu_i d_i +(\alpha-M_{t-1})d_t. \]

To justify this formula, the displayed value is attained by filling every type before \(t\), taking mass \(\alpha-M_{t-1}\) from type \(t\), and taking nothing afterward. Conversely, any feasible \(q\) satisfies
\[ \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}q_i d_i =\alpha d_t+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}q_i(d_i-d_t) \le\alpha d_t+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\mu_i(d_i-d_t). \]
Here the terms after \(t\) are nonpositive, while before \(t\) we use \(q_i\le\mu_i\). The upper bound equals the claimed formula.

Computing distances, sorting, performing rational arithmetic, and comparing with \(B\) all take polynomial time in the input length. Thus the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), and the reduction proves it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

Conclusion

The mass-based formulation contains unrestricted binary minimax exactly as a special case with succinct dyadic masses. Hence exact minimization is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard; fractional selection within approval types does not remove that hardness.
