The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite seller and buyer type sets with rational masses, linear utility parameters, and integer price intervals for each type pair, compute a stable mass allocation in time polynomial in the type support and binary input length. Each unit of mass receives at most one match at an integer price, and for every possible type-pair contract and allowed price, at least one side has zero mass that strictly prefers that contract to its current assignment.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S\) and \(B\) be finite seller and buyer type sets. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|S|+|B|\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Masses need not sum to one, and the two sides may have unequal total mass. Set \(m=|S||B|\), and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
For every \((s,b)\in S\times B\), the input gives binary rational coefficients \(a_{sb},c_{sb}>0\), \(d_{sb},e_{sb}\in\mathbb Q\), and binary integer bounds \(\ell_{sb}\le h_{sb}\), possibly negative. At integer price \(p\), utilities are \(u_{sb}(p)=a_{sb}p+d_{sb}\) and \(v_{sb}(p)=-c_{sb}p+e_{sb}\). Unmatched utility is zero; ties are allowed and blocking requires strict improvement.
An allocation is a finitely supported nonnegative rational array \(x_{sbp}\), for \(p\in[\ell_{sb},h_{sb}]\cap\mathbb Z\), with \(\sum_{b,p}x_{sbp}\le\mu_s\) and \(\sum_{s,p}x_{sbp}\le\nu_b\). Each entry matches equal portions of seller and buyer mass at that price; residual mass remains unmatched. Positive entries must give both sides nonnegative utility.
For an allowed contract \((s,b,p)\), define \(D_s(b,p)\) as the unmatched mass of \(s\) if \(u_{sb}(p)>0\), plus all assigned mass \(x_{sb'q}\) with \(u_{sb'q}(q)<u_{sb}(p)\). Define \(D_b(s,p)\) analogously using buyer utilities. The allocation is stable if \(D_s(b,p)=0\) or \(D_b(s,p)=0\) for every allowed contract.

Can a stable mass allocation always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite individual agents with integer prices, [Ali–Farooq](https://orsj.org/wp-content/or-archives50/pdf/e_mag/Vol.54_01_001.pdf) establish finite termination of a stable-outcome algorithm, without a polynomial bound in binary input length.
For finite individual agents with real-valued prices, [Farooq](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225161399_A_polynomial-time_algorithm_for_a_stable_matching_problem_with_linear_valuations_and_bounded_side_payments) gives an algorithm polynomial in the number of agents. No result settling the exact mass problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified positive rational utility slopes and arbitrary rational intercepts, retaining the discrete model's monotonicity while making the input binary encodable.
  • Retained nonempty inclusive integer price intervals and permitted negative bounds, as in the discrete model.
  • Used arbitrary positive rational masses without normalization or equal totals, since the discrete model permits unequal populations.
  • Restored individual rationality, including zero-utility contracts, and assigned unmatched mass utility zero.
  • Allowed ties without tie-breaking and required strict improvement on both sides for blocking.
  • Represented allocations by finitely supported rational contract masses, allowing portions of the same type to receive different partners or prices.
  • Defined dissatisfied mass using each portion's actual assignment utility, rather than a type's average utility.
  • Defined m as the number of seller–buyer type pairs and L as the total binary input length.

Proven partial results

The unrestricted complexity question remains open in this write-up. I prove two additional algorithmic results:

  • For every fixed number $\tau$ of supported types, a stable allocation is computable in time polynomial in the binary input length, even when price intervals and masses are large.
  • With unrestricted $\tau$, a polynomial algorithm exists for a subclass with slopes constant within each type, integer normalized intercepts, and bounds containing all individually rational prices.

The first result follows from an exact integer linear formulation with $6m+2\tau$ bounded integer variables. Its size is polynomial in the original input length. This does not establish polynomial time when $\tau$ grows.

1. Minimum utilities and integer cutoffs

For a feasible, individually rational allocation, define each type’s minimum realized utility: take the minimum over its positive assigned portions, also including zero when its unmatched mass is positive. Denote these minima by $\alpha_s$ and $\beta_b$.

They exist because the allocation has finite support and every supported type has positive mass. Individual rationality implies that they are nonnegative.

For every allowed contract,

$$ D_s(b,p)>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u_{sb}(p)>\alpha_s, $$

and analogously,

$$ D_b(s,p)>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_{sb}(p)>\beta_b. $$

Indeed, a positive dissatisfied mass identifies a positive portion receiving strictly less utility than the offered utility. Conversely, a positive portion attaining the minimum contributes to dissatisfied mass whenever the offered utility exceeds that minimum. This includes unmatched portions, whose utility is zero.

Consequently, stability is equivalent to the absence of an allowed integer price satisfying both

$$ a_{sb}p+d_{sb}>\alpha_s, \qquad -c_{sb}p+e_{sb}>\beta_b. $$

This gives polynomial-time verification of a listed allocation: a blocking price exists for a pair precisely when

$$ \max\left\{ \ell_{sb}, \left\lfloor\frac{\alpha_s-d_{sb}}{a_{sb}}\right\rfloor+1 \right\} \le \min\left\{ h_{sb}, \left\lceil\frac{e_{sb}-\beta_b}{c_{sb}}\right\rceil-1 \right\}. $$

All sums, comparisons, and rounding operations use exact rational arithmetic.

The following alternative description will be useful.

Lemma 1 — cutoff characterization. Fix a pair, write its bounds as $\ell,h$, and let $\alpha,\beta$ be proposed thresholds. There is no allowed integer price strictly exceeding both thresholds if and only if there exists an integer

$$ \ell-1\le k\le h $$

such that

$$ k\ge\ell \quad\Longrightarrow\quad ak+d\le\alpha, $$

and

$$ k<h \quad\Longrightarrow\quad -c(k+1)+e\le\beta. $$

Proof. Suppose there is no blocking price. If some allowed price gives seller utility at most $\alpha$, let $k$ be the largest such price. Otherwise, set $k=\ell-1$.

The seller inequality holds whenever it applies. If $k<h$, the allowed price $k+1$ gives seller utility strictly above $\alpha$. Absence of blocking therefore forces its buyer utility to be at most $\beta$.

Conversely, suppose such a cutoff exists. An allowed integer price $p\le k$ gives seller utility at most $\alpha$, by monotonicity. An allowed integer price $p\ge k+1$ gives buyer utility at most $\beta$. These two cases cover every integer price. At either boundary, the corresponding empty case requires no inequality. Thus no price strictly exceeds both thresholds. $\square$

Proposed thresholds need not equal the eventual realized minima. It suffices that every realized utility is at least its proposed threshold and that Lemma 1 excludes strict improvement above both proposed thresholds.

2. Existence on a rational grid and sparse support

These facts are needed to prove completeness of the integer formulation. Here they are proved without assuming an efficient algorithm for the original market.

Let $Q$ be the product of the denominators of all input masses. Then

$$ N_s=Q\mu_s, \qquad N_b=Q\nu_b $$

are positive integers. The binary length of $Q$ is at most the sum of those denominator lengths, hence is polynomial in $L$.

Lemma 2 — existence on the mass grid. A stable allocation exists whose entries are integer multiples of $1/Q$.

Proof. Replace seller type $s$ by $N_s$ seller individuals and buyer type $b$ by $N_b$ buyer individuals. Each individual represents mass $1/Q$.

For every seller individual, buyer individual, and individually rational allowed price, introduce a bilateral contract. There are finitely many such contracts. Each individual ranks contracts by its utility, with arbitrary strict tie-breaking; individually rational contracts tied with unmatched utility zero are placed above being unmatched.

Run seller-proposing deferred acceptance on these contract lists. A free seller proposes its best unproposed contract. The receiving buyer retains the better of that contract and its currently held contract, releasing the other seller if necessary. Each contract is proposed at most once, so the procedure terminates.

To verify stability of the resulting individual matching, consider a contract strictly preferred by both endpoints to their final assignments. Its seller must have proposed it: proposals occur in decreasing preference order, and an unmatched seller has exhausted its list. Once its buyer receives that proposal, the buyer’s held contract can only improve. The buyer therefore cannot strictly prefer the proposed contract to its final assignment. This is a contradiction.

Aggregate the resulting matching by type pair and price, assigning mass $1/Q$ for each matched individual pair. The allocation is feasible, individually rational, and lies on the stated grid.

If an aggregate contract had positive dissatisfied mass on both sides, there would be a seller individual and a buyer individual whose current utilities are both strictly below that contract’s utilities. Their current utilities are nonnegative, so the proposed contract is individually rational and appears in their lists. Strict utility comparisons survive tie-breaking, contradicting individual stability. $\square$

This expansion can be enormous. It proves existence, not a polynomial running-time bound.

Lemma 3 — sparse stable support. If both sides are nonempty, a stable allocation exists on the same grid with at most $\tau-1$ positive entries. In particular, it uses at most one price for each type pair.

Proof. Start with the allocation from Lemma 2. Regard each positive contract entry as an edge of a bipartite multigraph on the supported types.

If its support contains a cycle, alternate plus and minus signs around that cycle. Two parallel edges constitute a cycle of length two. Let $\varepsilon$ be the smallest mass on a minus edge. Add $\varepsilon$ to every plus edge and subtract it from every minus edge.

Every type’s assigned total remains unchanged. All entries remain nonnegative, at least one disappears, and no previously absent contract enters the support. Individual rationality is unchanged.

Stability is also preserved. For each allowed contract, at least one side originally had zero dissatisfied mass. That side’s unmatched mass is unchanged, and its new positive support is a subset of its old positive support. Thus it still has no positive inferior portion and still has zero dissatisfied mass.

The operation preserves the $1/Q$ grid. Repeating it eventually leaves a forest. A forest on $\tau$ vertices has at most $\tau-1$ edges and has no parallel edges. $\square$

If either side is empty, the empty allocation is stable.

The allocation furnished by Lemma 3 has polynomial encoding length: prices lie between binary input endpoints, and each mass is an integer multiple of $1/Q$ bounded by an input mass. Thus polynomial-size stable certificates always exist. Any statement about a polynomially balanced certificate relation should explicitly restrict certificates to this bounded format; arbitrary rational allocations need not have bounded encoding length.

3. An exact bounded integer linear formulation

Assume both sides are nonempty.

Let $T$ be the product of the denominators of all utility coefficients. Define integer coefficients

$$ \bar a_{sb}=Ta_{sb}, \quad \bar c_{sb}=Tc_{sb}, \quad \bar d_{sb}=Td_{sb}, \quad \bar e_{sb}=Te_{sb}. $$

The first two are positive. At every integer price, scaled utilities are integers.

Define the positive integer

$$ U= \max\left( \{1\}\cup \left\{ \begin{array}{l} |\bar a_{sb}\ell_{sb}+\bar d_{sb}|,\; |\bar a_{sb}h_{sb}+\bar d_{sb}|,\\ |-\bar c_{sb}\ell_{sb}+\bar e_{sb}|,\; |-\bar c_{sb}h_{sb}+\bar e_{sb}| \end{array} : (s,b)\in S\times B \right\} \right). $$

An affine function on an interval lies between its endpoint values. Therefore every allowed scaled utility lies in $[-U,U]$. Both $T$ and $U$ have polynomial binary length.

Variables and constraints

For each seller, introduce integer variables $A_s,f_s$, with $f_s$ binary. For each buyer, introduce integer variables $B_b,g_b$, with $g_b$ binary. Impose

$$ 0\le A_s\le Uf_s, \qquad 0\le B_b\le Ug_b. $$

The proposed utility thresholds are $A_s/T$ and $B_b/T$. A binary variable equal to one will require its type to be fully matched.

For each pair, introduce six integer variables

$$ Y_{sb},\ z_{sb},\ p_{sb},\ k_{sb},\ \lambda_{sb},\ \eta_{sb}, $$

where $z_{sb},\lambda_{sb},\eta_{sb}$ are binary.

The mass constraints are

$$ N_sf_s\le\sum_bY_{sb}\le N_s, \qquad N_bg_b\le\sum_sY_{sb}\le N_b, $$

and

$$ 0\le Y_{sb}\le\min\{N_s,N_b\}z_{sb}. $$

Thus $Y_{sb}/Q$ is the assigned pair mass, and positive mass forces $z_{sb}=1$.

Require an allowed assigned price:

$$ \ell_{sb}\le p_{sb}\le h_{sb}. $$

The utility constraints for assigned mass are

$$ \bar a_{sb}p_{sb}+\bar d_{sb} \ge A_s-2U(1-z_{sb}), $$

$$ -\bar c_{sb}p_{sb}+\bar e_{sb} \ge B_b-2U(1-z_{sb}). $$

When $z_{sb}=1$, these impose the proposed thresholds. When $z_{sb}=0$, they are automatic: their left sides are at least $-U$, while their right sides are at most $-U$.

It remains to encode the cutoff characterization. Put

$$ W_{sb}=h_{sb}-\ell_{sb}+1. $$

Impose

$$ \ell_{sb}-1\le k_{sb}\le h_{sb}, $$

$$ k_{sb}\le \ell_{sb}-1+W_{sb}(1-\lambda_{sb}), $$

$$ k_{sb}\ge h_{sb}-W_{sb}(1-\eta_{sb}), $$

and

$$ \bar a_{sb}k_{sb}+\bar d_{sb} \le A_s+U\lambda_{sb}, $$

$$ -\bar c_{sb}(k_{sb}+1)+\bar e_{sb} \le B_b+U\eta_{sb}. $$

The flag $\lambda_{sb}=1$ forces $k_{sb}=\ell_{sb}-1$; the flag $\eta_{sb}=1$ forces $k_{sb}=h_{sb}$. Thus each inequality can be relaxed only when its corresponding side of the cutoff has no allowed prices.

There are exactly $6m+2\tau$ integer variables. Every coefficient and bound is explicitly computable with polynomial binary length, and the entire system has polynomial encoding length.

Soundness

Proposition 4. Every feasible integer solution yields a stable allocation by setting

$$ x_{sb,p_{sb}}=\frac{Y_{sb}}Q $$

for pairs with positive $Y_{sb}$, and setting all other entries to zero.

Proof. The mass constraints give feasibility. Positive mass forces $z_{sb}=1$, so assigned utilities are at least the nonnegative proposed thresholds.

If a seller has positive unmatched mass, then $\sum_bY_{sb}<N_s$. The constraint $N_sf_s\le\sum_bY_{sb}$ forces $f_s=0$, hence $A_s=0$. Its unmatched portions therefore also receive at least its proposed threshold. The buyer argument is identical.

Fix a pair and an allowed integer price $q$.

If $q\le k_{sb}$, then $\lambda_{sb}=1$ is impossible: that flag would force $k_{sb}=\ell_{sb}-1$, below every allowed price. Hence $\lambda_{sb}=0$, and monotonicity gives

$$ \bar a_{sb}q+\bar d_{sb} \le \bar a_{sb}k_{sb}+\bar d_{sb} \le A_s. $$

If $q\ge k_{sb}+1$, then $\eta_{sb}=1$ is impossible, because it would force $k_{sb}=h_{sb}$. Consequently,

$$ -\bar c_{sb}q+\bar e_{sb} \le -\bar c_{sb}(k_{sb}+1)+\bar e_{sb} \le B_b. $$

These cases cover every integer $q$. No allowed contract strictly exceeds both proposed thresholds. Because every realized utility is at least its proposed threshold, no contract has positive dissatisfied mass on both sides. The allocation is stable. $\square$

Completeness

Proposition 5. The integer system is feasible for every valid market input.

Proof. Choose the stable grid allocation from Lemma 3. It uses at most one price per pair. Set $Y_{sb}$ equal to its pair mass multiplied by $Q$; set $z_{sb}=1$ exactly for its positive entries. For a used pair, take its actual price as $p_{sb}$; for an unused pair, choose any allowed price.

Take its actual minimum realized utilities and set

$$ A_s=T\alpha_s, \qquad B_b=T\beta_b. $$

These are integers: every assigned utility multiplied by $T$ is integral, as is unmatched utility zero. They lie between zero and $U$.

Set $f_s=1$ exactly when seller $s$ is fully matched, and set $g_b$ similarly. A type with unmatched mass has minimum utility zero, so all threshold and mass constraints hold. Assigned-price utility constraints hold by the definition of the minima. For unused pairs, the relaxed constraints are automatic as shown above.

For every pair, Lemma 1 supplies a cutoff $k_{sb}$. Set $\lambda_{sb}=1$ when $k_{sb}=\ell_{sb}-1$, and zero otherwise; set $\eta_{sb}=1$ when $k_{sb}=h_{sb}$, and zero otherwise.

The boundary constraints hold. Every unrelaxed utility inequality follows from Lemma 1. A relaxed seller inequality also holds, because

$$ \bar a_{sb}(\ell_{sb}-1)+\bar d_{sb} < \bar a_{sb}\ell_{sb}+\bar d_{sb} \le U \le A_s+U. $$

Likewise, a relaxed buyer inequality holds because

$$ -\bar c_{sb}(h_{sb}+1)+\bar e_{sb} < -\bar c_{sb}h_{sb}+\bar e_{sb} \le U \le B_b+U. $$

Thus every constraint is satisfied. $\square$

4. Polynomial time for fixed support size

Theorem 6. For each fixed $\tau_0$, stable allocations for inputs with $\tau\le\tau_0$ can be computed in time polynomial in $L$.

Proof. Use the following established theorem: for every fixed number of integer variables, feasibility of integer linear inequalities is decidable in polynomial time in their binary encoding length, and a feasible integer point can be produced when one exists. This is the constructive fixed-dimensional integer programming theorem of [Lenstra](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1983i/art.pdf).

The system above has integral coefficients, bounded variables, and polynomial encoding length. Moreover,

$$ m=|S||B|\le\left\lfloor\frac{\tau^2}{4}\right\rfloor, $$

so its number of integer variables is at most

$$ 6\left\lfloor\frac{\tau_0^2}{4}\right\rfloor+2\tau_0. $$

This is a constant when $\tau_0$ is fixed. Lenstra’s theorem therefore supplies a feasible solution in polynomial time. Proposition 5 guarantees feasibility, and Proposition 4 converts the solution to a stable allocation. $\square$

This settles, for example, the case of two supported types on each side with arbitrarily large binary price intervals and rational masses. The theorem supplies no uniform polynomial bound when $\tau$ is part of the growing input.

5. An unrestricted-size subclass solved by linear programming

There is also a polynomial result that allows $\tau$ to grow.

Assume that there are positive rational numbers $a_s,c_b$ such that

$$ a_{sb}=a_s, \qquad c_{sb}=c_b $$

for all pairs. Define normalized intercepts

$$ \delta_{sb}=\frac{d_{sb}}{a_s}, \qquad \varepsilon_{sb}=\frac{e_{sb}}{c_b}, $$

and assume they are integers. Thus normalized utilities are

$$ \frac{u_{sb}(p)}{a_s}=p+\delta_{sb}, \qquad \frac{v_{sb}(p)}{c_b}=-p+\varepsilon_{sb}. $$

Finally, assume that whenever $\delta_{sb}+\varepsilon_{sb}\ge0$, the price bounds satisfy

$$ \ell_{sb}\le-\delta_{sb}, \qquad h_{sb}\ge\varepsilon_{sb}. $$

This says that the allowed interval contains every individually rational real price for that pair.

Theorem 7. Under these assumptions, a stable integer-price mass allocation can be computed in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

Proof. Put

$$ w_{sb}=\delta_{sb}+\varepsilon_{sb}\in\mathbb Z. $$

Solve the transportation linear program

$$ \max\sum_{s,b}w_{sb}y_{sb} $$

subject to

$$ y_{sb}\ge0, \qquad \sum_by_{sb}\le\mu_s, \qquad \sum_sy_{sb}\le\nu_b. $$

Its dual is

$$ \min\left(\sum_s\mu_sr_s+\sum_b\nu_bt_b\right) $$

subject to

$$ r_s\ge0, \qquad t_b\ge0, \qquad r_s+t_b\ge w_{sb}. $$

We use the standard rational linear programming theorem: a rational linear program can be solved exactly in polynomial time in its binary encoding length, including production of optimal primal and dual solutions when finite optima exist.

Here the primal is feasible, since $y=0$ is feasible, and its feasible region is bounded by the mass constraints. The dual is feasible: take all $t_b=0$ and all $r_s$ equal to $\max\{0,\max_{s,b}w_{sb}\}$. Thus finite optimal primal and dual solutions exist and can be computed in polynomial time.

For such solutions, their zero duality gap is

$$ \begin{aligned} 0={}& \sum_s r_s\left(\mu_s-\sum_by_{sb}\right) +\sum_b t_b\left(\nu_b-\sum_sy_{sb}\right)\\ &+\sum_{s,b}y_{sb}(r_s+t_b-w_{sb}). \end{aligned} $$

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore:

  • If $y_{sb}>0$, then $r_s+t_b=w_{sb}$.
  • Positive unmatched seller mass implies $r_s=0$.
  • Positive unmatched buyer mass implies $t_b=0$.

Round the dual utilities asymmetrically:

$$ R_s=\lceil r_s\rceil, \qquad T_b=\lfloor t_b\rfloor. $$

They are nonnegative integers.

For every pair, since $\lfloor t_b\rfloor>t_b-1$,

$$ R_s+T_b > r_s+t_b-1 \ge w_{sb}-1. $$

Both $R_s+T_b$ and $w_{sb}$ are integers, so

$$ R_s+T_b\ge w_{sb}. $$

For a positive-mass pair, complementary slackness and integrality of $w_{sb}$ give the stronger equality

$$ T_b =\lfloor w_{sb}-r_s\rfloor =w_{sb}-\lceil r_s\rceil, $$

hence

$$ R_s+T_b=w_{sb}. $$

Assign its mass $y_{sb}$ at the integer price

$$ p_{sb}=R_s-\delta_{sb}. $$

The resulting normalized utilities are exactly

$$ p_{sb}+\delta_{sb}=R_s, \qquad \varepsilon_{sb}-p_{sb}=T_b. $$

They are nonnegative. Consequently,

$$ -\delta_{sb}\le p_{sb}\le\varepsilon_{sb}. $$

The assumed bounds make this an allowed price.

Every assigned seller portion of type $s$ receives utility $a_sR_s$, independent of its partner. Every assigned buyer portion of type $b$ receives utility $c_bT_b$. If a type has unmatched mass, its corresponding dual variable, and hence its rounded utility, is zero. Thus these are exactly the minimum realized utilities, including unmatched portions.

Suppose an allowed contract at price $p$ blocked. Division by the positive type-specific slopes would give

$$ p+\delta_{sb}>R_s, \qquad \varepsilon_{sb}-p>T_b. $$

Adding yields

$$ w_{sb}>R_s+T_b, $$

contradicting the rounded dual inequality. The allocation is stable.

The LP has $m$ primal variables and $\tau$ capacity constraints. Its coefficients have polynomial binary length, and exact rounding and price construction are polynomial-time operations. $\square$

The argument includes unequal total masses and partially unmatched types. It relies on all three additional hypotheses: common slopes within types, integer normalized intercepts, and sufficiently wide price bounds.

6. What remains open

The general problem now has an exact polynomial-size formulation as bounded integer linear feasibility, and it is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed support size. This removes any need to enumerate price intervals in those cases.

For growing $\tau$, the integer programming theorem used above supplies no polynomial bound. Conversely, reducing this problem to integer programming does not establish hardness.

Neither a uniform polynomial algorithm for the unrestricted coefficients and bounds nor a search-hardness reduction is proved here.
