Consequently, the problem is polynomial-time solvable when both $m$ and $B$ are fixed constants.

The finite search preserves exact exhaustion positions and finite residual stocks; it does not replace the mechanism by a continuum approximation.

1. Basic bounds and the finite outcome alphabet

Put
$$ S=\sum_{h\in H}\rho_h,\qquad B=\lceil S\rceil,\qquad \lambda=S-B+1. $$
The hypotheses imply $0<\lambda\le1$.

There are exactly $NS$ allocations: every remaining good is allocated, including goods with zero marginal value. Thus there are $B-1$ complete traversals followed by a prefix of length
$$ E(N)=N\lambda. $$
When $S$ is integral, this last prefix is a complete traversal.

Because $j_t\le q$ is fixed and $\lambda>0$, every tagged agent receives exactly $B$ goods for all sufficiently large admissible $N$. This conclusion is independent of the report.

For a supported type $t$, define
$$ A_t=\max_{k,h}a_{t,k,h}=\max_h v_t(e_h). $$
For sufficiently large $N$, all classes remain available at its first turn: fewer than $q$ preceding allocations cannot exhaust a stock $N\rho_h$ tending to infinity. Truthful reporting therefore obtains a first good worth $A_t$. By monotonicity,
$$ U_t^{(N)}\ge A_t. $$
Every bundle $x$ with $B$ goods satisfies
$$ v_t(x) =\max_k\sum_h a_{t,k,h}x_h \le A_t\sum_hx_h =BA_t. $$
Consequently, if $A_t>0$, every eventual ratio is at most $B$. If $A_t=0$, nonnegativity implies that every true coefficient is zero, so the ratio is always $1$ under the prescribed convention.

This proves the universal bound
$$ \sup_{t,w}\limsup_{N\to\infty} \frac{U_{t\to w}^{(N)}}{U_t^{(N)}}\le B. $$
In particular, $R\ge B$ is a sufficient YES-condition, checkable in $O(L+mL^2)$ bit operations using schoolbook rational arithmetic.

For later use, define the finite set
$$ \mathcal X_B= \left\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{H}:\sum_hx_h=B\right\}. $$
For fixed $t$, every sufficiently large truthful or deviating utility belongs to
$$ \{v_t(x):x\in\mathcal X_B\}. $$
Thus every fixed report produces a ratio sequence taking values in a finite set, after finitely many initial terms. Its limit superior is a value occurring infinitely often. This observation does not assert eventual constancy.

2. The unavoidable initial commitment

Lemma 1. Suppose an agent starts empty with every class available. Under any allowed XOS report, it keeps selecting its first chosen class for as long as that class remains available.

Proof. Write the report as
$$ w(x)=\max_\ell b_\ell\cdot x, \qquad A=\max_{\ell,h}b_{\ell,h}. $$
Let $c$ be the first chosen class. Then $w(e_c)=A$, and some clause has coefficient $A$ on $c$. Hence
$$ w(ne_c)=nA $$
for every nonnegative integer $n$.

For every class $h$,
$$ w(ne_c+e_h)\le(n+1)A=w((n+1)e_c). $$
Thus $c$ is a marginal maximizer.

Suppose an earlier class $h$ tied with $c$. For $n>0$, equality would require a clause with coefficient $A$ on both $c$ and $h$, because all coefficients are at most $A$. Consequently $w(e_h)=A$. For $n=0$, the same conclusion follows directly from the tie. In either case, the initial tie rule would have chosen $h$ before $c$, a contradiction. Therefore $c$ continues to be chosen. $\square$

The next result shows that, once this first class disappears, there is no analogous restriction on a finite prescribed sequence of choices.

3. Realizing an arbitrary sequence after the first exhaustion

Lemma 2. Fix a class $c$, an integer $d\ge1$, and a word
$$ h_1,\ldots,h_\ell\in H\setminus\{c\}. $$
There is a nonnegative rational XOS report with $\ell+1$ clauses having the following behavior:

  • It selects $c$ whenever its bundle consists only of $c$-goods and $c$ remains available.
  • Starting from bundle $de_c$ with $c$ unavailable, at its $u$th subsequent turn it selects $h_u$, provided $h_u$ is available.

Every coefficient lies in $[0,1]$ and has
$$ O(\log(d+\ell+1)) $$
bits.

Proof. If $\ell=0$, use $w(x)=x_c$. Assume $\ell\ge1$, and set
$$ \eta=\frac{1}{16(\ell+1)^2}, \qquad \varepsilon=\frac{\eta}{8(\ell+1)}, \qquad C=d-\frac14. $$
Include the clause $x_c$. For each $r\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}$, include the clause with coefficients
$$ b_{r,c}=\frac{C-\eta r^2/2}{d}, $$
and, for $h\ne c$,
$$ b_{r,h}=\frac12+\eta r+\varepsilon\mathbf1_{\{h=h_r\}}. $$

These coefficients are nonnegative and strictly less than $1$. Indeed,
$$ 0<\frac{d-\frac14-\frac1{32}}{d} <b_{r,c}<1, $$
while
$$ b_{r,h}\le\frac12+\frac1{16}+\frac1{128}<1. $$
Their numerators and denominators have the asserted bit length.

At a bundle $ne_c$, the clause $x_c$ gives value $n$. At a candidate bundle $(n+1)e_c$, it gives $n+1$, whereas every other candidate $ne_c+e_h$, with $h\ne c$, has value strictly below $n+1$. Thus $c$ is selected whenever it remains available during this initial phase.

Now suppose $c$ is unavailable and the bundle contains exactly $d$ copies of $c$ and $u-1$ other goods, where $1\le u\le\ell$. For any candidate next good, let $y$ be the resulting bundle. Its total count outside $c$ is $u$. The value of clause $r$ at $y$ is
$$ C+\frac u2+\eta\left(ru-\frac{r^2}{2}\right) +\varepsilon y_{h_r}. $$
The difference between the terms excluding $\varepsilon$, for clauses $u$ and $r$, is
$$ \frac{\eta}{2}(u-r)^2. $$
For $r\ne u$, this is at least $\eta/2$. The perturbation can reduce the difference by at most
$$ \varepsilon u\le\varepsilon\ell<\frac{\eta}{8}. $$
Hence clause $u$ strictly dominates all the other scripted clauses at every candidate bundle.

It also dominates the original clause $x_c$: its value is at least
$$ C+\frac u2+\frac{\eta u^2}{2} \ge d+\frac14>d. $$
Therefore clause $u$ determines the value of every candidate next bundle. Among available classes, its coefficient on $h_u$ is uniquely largest. If $h_u$ is available, it is selected. $\square$

Notice that the proof works for every distribution of the previously acquired goods outside $c$. The report uses the number of subsequent turns as a clock.

4. A finite canonical family of reports

For a word
$$ s=(s_1,\ldots,s_B)\in H^B, $$
let $c=s_1$ and let $d$ be the length of its initial run of $c$. Retain only words in which $c$ never appears after that initial run. Associate to each retained word the report from Lemma 2, using the suffix
$$ s_{d+1},\ldots,s_B. $$
Call this its *canonical report*. There are at most $m^B$ such reports.

Theorem 3. A limiting violation exists if and only if one exists using a canonical report. Moreover, the supremum of all limiting incentive ratios is a finite rational number attained by a canonical report for some supported type.

Proof. Canonical reports are allowed reports, so one implication is immediate.

Fix a type $t$ and an arbitrary report $w$. For sufficiently large $N$, record both the tagged agent’s deviating word and its truthful word, each of length $B$. There are only finitely many pairs of words.

By Section 1, the limiting ratio is a value recurring infinitely often. Choose an infinite subsequence on which a fixed pair of words attains this value. Let the deviating word begin with $d$ copies of class $c$.

Lemma 1 implies that $c$ cannot occur after this initial run: the first different choice is possible only after $c$ has exhausted, and exhausted classes never return. Thus the word has a canonical report.

On each sufficiently large $N$ in the chosen subsequence, replacing $w$ by that canonical report reproduces the entire allocation. To verify this, induct on chronological allocation turns. Untagged agents have unchanged reports. At tagged turns, the canonical report selects $c$ while it remains available, and subsequently selects the prescribed suffix class by Lemma 2. Therefore the stocks and all previous bundles agree at every turn.

The canonical report consequently attains the original limiting ratio on an infinite subsequence, so its own limit superior is at least that ratio.

The canonical family is finite. Taking its maximum therefore gives an attained upper bound on every allowed report, and this maximum is itself attained by an allowed report. Finiteness and rationality follow from Section 1. $\square$

Since $\ell=B-d$, each witnessing report has
$$ \ell+1=B-d+1\le B $$
clauses and coefficient bit length $O(\log(B+1))$. Its explicit description has length
$$ O\!\left(mB\log(B+1)\right). $$

This removes the unbounded report quantifier, but $B$ can be exponentially large in $L$.

5. A bound on truthful class changes

The following result is independent of the canonical-report construction.

Lemma 4. Fix a nonempty available set $A$ and an XOS valuation with $k$ clauses. Along consecutive turns during which $A$ remains unchanged, the agent changes its chosen class at most $k-1$ times.

Proof. For each clause $\ell$, define
$$ b_\ell=\max_{h\in A}a_{\ell,h}, $$
and let $c_\ell$ be the earliest class attaining this maximum. At bundle $x$, put
$$ s_\ell(x)=a_\ell\cdot x+b_\ell. $$
Maximizing marginal value is equivalent to maximizing $v(x+e_h)$, because $v(x)$ is independent of $h$. Consequently, the chosen class is the earliest $c_\ell$ among clauses maximizing $s_\ell(x)$.

Suppose the current chosen class is $c$. Among maximizing clauses with $c_\ell=c$, select one, denoted $k_0$, with largest $b_\ell$. Write $b=b_{k_0}$ and $M=s_{k_0}(x)$.

After selecting $c$, for any clause $\ell$ with $b_\ell\le b$,
$$ s_\ell(x+e_c) =s_\ell(x)+a_{\ell,c} \le M+b_\ell \le M+b =s_{k_0}(x+e_c). $$
Equality requires
$$ s_\ell(x)=M,\qquad a_{\ell,c}=b_\ell=b. $$
Then $c$ maximizes clause $\ell$’s coefficients on $A$, so $c_\ell\le c$. Since clause $\ell$ was already maximizing at $x$ and the mechanism selected $c$, the tie rule also gives $c_\ell\ge c$. Thus $c_\ell=c$.

It follows that the selected clause coefficient $b$ can never decrease from one turn to the next, and must strictly increase whenever the chosen class changes. There are at most $k$ possible values $b_\ell$. Hence at most $k-1$ class changes occur. $\square$

An execution has at most $m$ nonempty availability phases. There are at most $m-1$ transitions between phases. Applying Lemma 4 within each phase gives the total bound
$$ m(K_t-1)+(m-1)=mK_t-1 $$
on class changes for a truthful agent. Equivalently, its allocation word has at most $mK_t$ maximal constant runs.

This bound does not yet yield a polynomial algorithm for the threshold problem, because arbitrary deviations can use the much larger canonical family.

6. Exact encoding of an execution

I now give a finite decision procedure that retains all exhaustion-boundary effects.

Let $D$ be the least common multiple of $q$ and the reduced denominators of the supplies. The admissible population sizes are exactly
$$ N=Dn,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}. $$
Both $D$ and $B$ have $O(L)$ bits.

Fix a tagged type $t$ and a canonical word. Encode two executions at the same $N$: the truthful execution and the execution with its canonical report.

6.1 Exhaustion data

For each execution and each class $h$, introduce:

  • its exhaustion traversal $r_h\in\{1,\ldots,B\}$;
  • its exhaustion position $b_h\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$ within that traversal.

The allocation at $(r_h,b_h)$ is the last allocation of class $h$.

An ordinary turn $(r,i)$ therefore has class $h$ available precisely when
$$ r<r_h \quad\text{or}\quad (r=r_h\text{ and }i\le b_h). $$
It is an active allocation turn precisely when
$$ r<B \quad\text{or}\quad (r=B\text{ and }i\le E), \qquad E=N\lambda. $$

We enumerate the finite choices of exhaustion traversals. Within each execution, we also enumerate a weak ordering of the named positions
$$ b_1,\ldots,b_m,E,j_t, $$
including possible equalities, and their residues modulo $q$. The residue of $E$ is shared between the two executions.

Write
$$ b_h=qz_h+s_h,\qquad E=qz_E+s_E, \qquad 0\le s_h,s_E<q. $$
Once residues have been enumerated, the integer variables are
$$ n,\quad z_E,\quad z_1^{\mathrm{truth}},\ldots,z_m^{\mathrm{truth}}, \quad z_1^{\mathrm{dev}},\ldots,z_m^{\mathrm{dev}}. $$
There are $2m+2$ variables.

Ordering conditions, position bounds, and $E=D\lambda n$ are integer linear constraints.

We additionally impose
$$ Dn\ge q,\qquad D\rho_h n\ge q+1\ \text{for every }h, \qquad E\ge q. $$
These conditions hold for every sufficiently large admissible $N$. They ensure that all tagged agents receive $B$ goods and have every class available at their first turn.

6.2 Cohorts with constant histories

In either execution, split the agent positions into:

  • singleton positions corresponding to distinct named positions;
  • open intervals between these positions, together with the initial and final intervals.

There are at most $m+2$ singletons and $m+3$ intervals.

Within an interval, agents with the same residue modulo $q$ have the same type and the same availability pattern at every traversal. Their bundle histories are therefore identical. The tagged agent is isolated at its singleton $j_t$.

For example, the number of positions of residue $a$, where $1\le a\le q$, in an interval $(u,v)$ is
$$ \left\lfloor\frac{v-1-a}{q}\right\rfloor - \left\lfloor\frac{u-a}{q}\right\rfloor. $$
Because endpoint residues have been fixed, this is an affine integer expression in the variables. The final interval uses
$$ \left\lfloor\frac{N-a}{q}\right\rfloor - \left\lfloor\frac{u-a}{q}\right\rfloor. $$

For every interval-residue cohort and every singleton, simulate its bundle through the $B$ traversals using the branch’s availability pattern. Truthful choices are computed directly from the input clauses. The deviating tagged agent follows its prescribed word; a branch is rejected if the requested class is unavailable or the word violates its initial commitment.

If an interval-residue cohort has an active turn with no available class, impose that its population count is zero. Such a cohort then contributes nothing. A singleton with this defect rejects the branch.

Thus every nonempty cohort has a completely determined, constant final count vector.

6.3 Supply equalities and exhaustion checks

For each execution and class $h$, impose
$$ \sum_C \operatorname{count}(C)\,x_{C,h}=D\rho_h n, $$
where $C$ ranges over its cohorts and $x_C$ is the final bundle computed for that cohort.

These are linear equations: each $x_{C,h}$ is constant within the branch, and every cohort population is affine in the integer variables.

Finally, require that the singleton turn $(r_h,b_h)$ is active and actually selects class $h$. Distinct classes cannot exhaust at the same allocation turn.

Lemma 5. Integer solutions of these constraints correspond exactly to executions having the branch’s stated exhaustion data and tagged behavior.

Proof. Every actual execution with these branch data satisfies the constraints, by counting its allocations.

Conversely, consider an integer solution and the simulated allocation sequence. No simulated allocation of class $h$ occurs after $(r_h,b_h)$, and that designated turn allocates $h$. The supply equality says that the total simulated number of $h$-allocations is exactly its stock.

Before its designated exhaustion allocation, therefore, fewer than all copies of $h$ have been consumed: the designated allocation itself is still to come. After that allocation, exactly all copies have been consumed. Thus the stipulated availability pattern is correct at every chronological turn.

Induction over turns now establishes that every simulated truthful choice is the actual marginal-maximizing choice. Lemma 2 establishes the same correspondence for the canonical report. The active turns number
$$ (B-1)N+E=NS, $$
so the mechanism stops exactly where stipulated. $\square$

In particular, constant residual stocks are preserved by the affine counting equations.

7. Testing whether a branch occurs infinitely often

Within a combined branch, the tagged truthful and deviating bundles are fixed vectors. Let their true utilities be $V$ and $W$.

A branch can witness a violation precisely when
$$ W>RV $$
and its integer solutions have unbounded $n$. This comparison respects the prescribed zero conventions: if $V=W=0$, it is false, as required for $R\ge1$.

Write the branch constraints as a rational polyhedron
$$ P=\{x:Ax\le b\}, $$
with integer variables $x$ and distinguished coordinate $n$.

Lemma 6. The integer solutions in $P$ have unbounded $n$ if and only if both systems below have integer solutions:
$$ Ax\le b $$
and
$$ A\delta\le0,\qquad \delta_n\ge1. $$

Proof. If both have integer solutions $x_0$ and $\delta$, then
$$ x_0+k\delta\in P $$
for every nonnegative integer $k$, and its $n$ coordinate tends to infinity.

For the converse, take integer solutions $x_j$ with $n_j\to\infty$. Every remaining variable is a quotient of a position between $1$ and $Dn_j$, so its ratio to $n_j$ is bounded. Pass to a convergent subsequence of $x_j/n_j$, with limit $\gamma$. Dividing the inequalities by $n_j$ gives
$$ A\gamma\le0,\qquad \gamma_n=1. $$
A nonempty system of rational linear inequalities has a rational solution; this follows, for example, by Fourier–Motzkin elimination and choosing rational values between the resulting rational bounds. Hence the homogeneous system with distinguished coordinate $1$ has a rational solution. Clearing denominators gives an integer $\delta$ with $A\delta\le0$ and $\delta_n\ge1$. $\square$

We use the standard fixed-dimension integer-programming theorem: integer feasibility with $d$ variables and encoding length $\ell$ is decidable in
$$ 2^{O(d^3)}\operatorname{poly}(\ell) $$
time. The theorem applies to general linear inequality systems, including unbounded feasible regions. See [Lenstra’s original paper](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1983i/art.pdf) and the running-time statement in [Kannan’s paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/800061.808749).

Here $d=2m+2$. All constraints have integer coefficients after clearing denominators, and polynomial encoding length in $m,q,L$. Thus Lemma 6 is tested by two such integer-feasibility calls.

8. Correctness and running time of the finite search

The algorithm enumerates supported types, canonical words, and the combined execution branches above. It returns NO exactly when a branch with $W>RV$ has unbounded integer solutions.

If it returns NO, Lemmas 5 and 6 provide arbitrarily large admissible $N$ with the same canonical report and ratio exceeding $R$. This is a limiting violation.

Conversely, suppose a limiting violation exists. Theorem 3 supplies a canonical witnessing report. There are finitely many pairs of tagged words and finitely many exhaustion branches. On an infinite subsequence, the words, exhaustion traversals, position orderings, and residues are all fixed. The corresponding branch has $W>RV$ and unbounded integer solutions, so the algorithm finds it.

An upper bound on the number of branches is
$$ \tau\,m^B B^{2m} q^{2m+1}(m+2)^{2m+4}. $$
The factors respectively account for the type, canonical word, exhaustion traversals, residues, and two weak orderings.

A branch has $O(qm)$ cohorts. Direct simulation costs
$$ O\!\left(B(q+1)(m+3)KmL^2\right) $$
bit operations: at each turn, all candidate marginal maximizers can be evaluated in $O(Km)$ arithmetic operations. Bundle counts and all resulting coefficients have $O(L+\log B)=O(L)$ bits.

Including the two integer-feasibility tests yields the valid overall bound
$$ \tau\,m^B B^{2m+1}q^{2m+1}2^{O(m^3)} \operatorname{poly}(m,q,K,L). $$
Since $B\le2^{O(L)}$, this is at most
$$ 2^{\,2^{O(L)}}. $$

For fixed $m$ and fixed $B$, the displayed bound is polynomial in $\tau,q,K,L$. This proves an exact polynomial-time special case extending to any fixed number of classes when total supply is also bounded.

9. What remains open

The canonical-report theorem resolves one obstacle identified in the notes: arbitrary finite reports can be replaced by a finite, explicitly bounded family. The exhaustion encoding resolves another: finite residual stocks can be handled exactly.

The remaining obstacle is computational. Enumerating allocation words costs $m^B$, and the exhaustion encoding also branches exponentially with the number of classes. I have not proved that these searches can be replaced by a polynomial algorithm, nor constructed a hardness reduction for the specified threshold problem.
