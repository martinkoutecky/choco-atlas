The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(T\) of unit-density bundle types with rational item sizes and values \(v_i=s_i\), per-capita capacity \(C\), and a rational society distribution \(\mu\), determine the best worst-case expected-welfare ratio over mechanisms that assign each individual an integral subset of their reported bundle, satisfy aggregate capacity, and are truthful in expectation against every hiding report \(r\subseteq t\). The benchmark is \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\infty}(\mu)=\min\{C,S(\mu)\}\), where \(S(\mu)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t s(t)\); give an optimal mechanism and its running time.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(E\) be a catalogue of \(m\) item templates with binary-rational sizes and values \(0<s_i=v_i\le C\), where \(C>0\) is binary rational. Each individual owns a private copy of a bundle \(t\subseteq E\); reporting is restricted to \(r\subseteq t\), including the empty report, with no invented items or altered sizes or values. Write \(s(A)=\sum_{i\in A}s_i\).
A society is nonatomic, has total mass \(1\), and is given by its support: the list \(T\) of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass \(\mu_t\) as a binary rational. Thus \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible bundles. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An anonymous mechanism specifies, for every reported society \(\mu\) and every report \(r\subseteq E\), a lottery \(q_\mu(\cdot\mid r)\) over integral subsets \(A\subseteq r\), including reports of zero mass. Allocations must admit a realization on the nonatomic population with aggregate size at most \(C\) almost surely; no separate capacity bound is imposed on an individual's bundle.
Set \(u_\mu(r)=\sum_{A\subseteq r}q_\mu(A\mid r)s(A)\). Truthfulness in expectation requires \(u_\mu(t)\ge u_\mu(r)\) for every \(r\subseteq t\subseteq E\) and every reported society \(\mu\): an individual deviation leaves \(\mu\) unchanged. There are no payments.
Welfare is \(W_M(\mu)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tu_\mu(t)\), and the benchmark is \(\mathrm{OPT}_\infty(\mu)=\min\{C,\sum_{t\in T}\mu_ts(t)\}\). A universal approximation guarantee \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) requires \(W_M(\mu)\ge\alpha\,\mathrm{OPT}_\infty(\mu)\) for every admissible catalogue, capacity, and rational society with positive benchmark. Ties are allowed, with any needed deterministic choices resolved by a fixed order on item labels.

What is the largest universal worst-case expected-welfare ratio achievable by these mechanisms? Is it attained, and what mechanism attains it, with what running time in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For finite societies, a randomized mechanism truthful in expectation cannot guarantee more than \(1/(5\phi-7)\), where \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\); a universally truthful randomized mechanism achieves \(2/3\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06080)
No result settling this exact nonatomic formulation was located in the literature search.

Result

The largest universal ratio is \(1\), and it is attained by a mechanism computable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

The decisive features are that an individual deviation leaves the reported society unchanged and that capacity constrains the population’s aggregate allocation. In particular, the formulation expressly permits allocating an individual a bundle whose size exceeds \(C\).

Mechanism and truthfulness

Fix a catalogue, capacity, and reported society \(\mu\). Define its total supply by
\[ S_\mu=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t s(t), \]
and set
\[ p_\mu= \begin{cases} 1,&S_\mu=0,\\ \min\{1,C/S_\mu\},&S_\mu>0. \end{cases} \]

For every report \(r\subseteq E\), including reports of zero mass, prescribe the lottery
\[ q_\mu(\cdot\mid r) =p_\mu\delta_r+(1-p_\mu)\delta_{\varnothing}. \]
Thus the individual receives its entire reported bundle with probability \(p_\mu\), and receives nothing otherwise. Here \(\delta_A\) denotes the point mass at allocation \(A\). For the empty report, the two terms combine into the point mass at the empty allocation.

Its expected utility is
\[ u_\mu(r)=p_\mu s(r). \]
If \(r\subseteq t\), positivity of item sizes gives \(s(r)\le s(t)\). Because \(\mu\), and therefore \(p_\mu\), remains fixed under an individual deviation,
\[ u_\mu(r)=p_\mu s(r)\le p_\mu s(t)=u_\mu(t). \]
This proves truthfulness in expectation for every inclusion pair, whether or not either report has positive mass.

A capacity-feasible realization

The preceding marginal lotteries require correlation across individuals. We now construct that correlation explicitly.

Since all input numbers are rational, write \(p_\mu=a/b\), with integers \(b\ge1\) and \(0\le a\le b\). Realize the society on the disjoint union of spaces
\[ X_t=\{t\}\times[0,1), \]
where the measure on \(X_t\) is \(\mu_t\) times Lebesgue measure. This is a nonatomic population of total mass \(1\) with the prescribed type masses.

Choose one common random integer \(J\), uniformly from \(\{0,\ldots,b-1\}\). For an individual \((t,x)\), put \(h(x)=\lfloor bx\rfloor\), and allocate
\[ A_J(t,x)= \begin{cases} t,&(h(x)+J)\bmod b<a,\\ \varnothing,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

For any fixed individual, addition of \(h(x)\) permutes the possible values of \(J\) modulo \(b\). Exactly \(a\) values satisfy the allocation condition. Consequently, that individual receives its entire bundle with probability \(a/b=p_\mu\), exactly as prescribed by \(q_\mu\). All individuals reporting the same bundle therefore have the same marginal lottery, as required by the stated anonymity condition.

For any fixed realization \(J\), exactly \(a\) of the \(b\) intervals
\[ [h/b,(h+1)/b),\qquad h\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\}, \]
are served within each type. The served mass of type \(t\) is therefore exactly \(\mu_t a/b\). Hence, in every realization, aggregate size is
\[ \int_X s(A_J(t,x))\,d\nu(t,x) =\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\frac ab\,s(t) =p_\mu S_\mu =\min\{C,S_\mu\}. \]
Thus capacity holds in every realization, and in particular almost surely.

The construction also works on any given nonatomic finite measure space: the standard divisibility property of nonatomic measures allows each positive-mass type class to be partitioned into \(b\) measurable pieces of equal measure. The same cyclic selection then applies.

Welfare and optimality

The mechanism’s welfare is
\[ W_M(\mu)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p_\mu s(t) =p_\mu S_\mu =\mathrm{OPT}_\infty(\mu). \]
It therefore achieves ratio \(1\) whenever the benchmark is positive.

For completeness, this benchmark is an upper bound for every feasible mechanism. In each realization, allocating only subsets of reported bundles bounds aggregate size by \(S_\mu\); feasibility also bounds it by \(C\). Because values equal sizes, expected welfare equals expected aggregate size. This equality follows by integration of the marginal utilities; the integrand is measurable and bounded by the finite number \(s(E)\). Consequently,
\[ W_M(\mu)\le\min\{C,S_\mu\}. \]
The achieved ratio is therefore optimal.

Running time and representation

Compute \(s(t)\) for each supported type, then compute \(S_\mu\) and \(p_\mu\). This uses \(O(m\tau+m+\tau)\) arithmetic operations.

All intermediate integers can have \(O(L)\) bits: use a common denominator formed from the catalogue and capacity denominators, and another formed from the mass denominators. Their products have bit lengths bounded by the corresponding total input lengths. The required sums, products, and reduced fraction \(a/b\) consequently also have \(O(L)\) bits. A conservative bound using elementary integer arithmetic is
\[ O\bigl((m\tau+m+\tau+1)L^3\bigr) \]
bit operations.

The mechanism is represented succinctly by \(p_\mu\) and the rule “allocate the whole report or the empty bundle.” It does not enumerate all \(2^m\) reports. Likewise, the realization is specified by \(a,b,J\) and the cyclic interval rule; it does not enumerate the potentially exponentially many intervals.

Sampling \(J\) exactly takes expected \(O(L)\) random bits and expected polynomial time, using rejection sampling. Thus lottery specification takes deterministic polynomial time, and exact sampling takes expected polynomial time.

The cited [finite-society paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06080) constrains the sum of sizes selected from finitely many agents. Here, nonatomicity permits serving an exact fraction of every type while preserving identical marginal lotteries. That difference enables the capacity-feasible realization above.
