The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given reservation shares \(\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^n\) summing to \(1\), a finite horizon \(H\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) department types \(t\), each specifying vacancies \(q_{t,s}\) per period, compute a finite-support lottery over integral roster paths for each type. Every unit of mass must receive one integral path that is monotone and, at every prefix \(h\), assigns category \(j\) either \(\lfloor\alpha_j Q_{t,h}\rfloor\) or \(\lceil\alpha_j Q_{t,h}\rceil\), where \(Q_{t,h}=\sum_{s\le h}q_{t,s}\); the mass-weighted assignment must equal \(\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_jQ_{t,h}\) for each \(j,h\). For finite implementations with \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb{Z}\), do independent roster draws within each type recover Theorem 3’s university-level deviation bounds, and can the policy be computed efficiently when roster period \(k\) is explicit?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq2\) reservation categories with \(\alpha\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^{m}\) and \(\sum_j\alpha_j=1\). A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) department types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Type \(t\) specifies binary-encoded vacancies \(q_{t,s}\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\) for \(1\leq s\leq H\), where \(H\geq1\), and \(Q_{t,h}=\sum_{s\leq h}q_{t,s}\).
Let \(k\) be the least common denominator of the shares, supplied explicitly in unary. A roster is a word \(\sigma\in\{1,\ldots,m\}^{k}\), repeated indefinitely; \(A_{\sigma,j}(r)\) counts category \(j\) among its first \(r\) positions. Every supported roster must satisfy \(A_{\sigma,j}(r)\in\{\lfloor\alpha_jr\rfloor,\lceil\alpha_jr\rceil\}\) for every \(r\geq0\). It induces the integral path \(a_{t,j,h}=A_{\sigma,j}(Q_{t,h})\), with nondecreasing coordinates and \(\sum_j a_{t,j,h}=Q_{t,h}\).
Compute, for each type, an explicit finite lottery \(p_{t,\sigma}\) with binary-rational probabilities, satisfying \(\mathbb E_{p_t}[A_{\sigma,j}(r)]=\alpha_jr\) for \(0\leq r\leq k\). Allocating fraction \(p_{t,\sigma}\) of type \(t\)'s mass to that roster must therefore give \(\sum_t\mu_t\mathbb E_{p_t}[a_{t,j,h}]=\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_jQ_{t,h}\) for every \(j,h\). Let \(L\) be the total binary bit length of the shares, masses and vacancy data.
For a finite implementation, let \(N\geq2\) satisfy \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\), draw one roster independently for each department, and retain it throughout the horizon. Write \(S_{j,h}\) for its university total and \(F_{j,h}=N\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_jQ_{t,h}\). The desired bounds are \(\Pr(S_{j,h}-F_{j,h}\geq b)<e^{-b^2/(3N)}\) and \(\Pr(S_{j,h}-F_{j,h}\leq-b)<e^{-b^2/(2N)}\), separately for every \(j,h\) and real \(b>0\).

Can these lotteries be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,H,k,L\), or is their construction NP-hard?
Do independent finite implementations of such lotteries satisfy both stated deviation bounds?

For finite departments, [Evren–Khanna](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.11963v2) establish monotonicity, department quota, ex ante proportionality and these deviation bounds, with a polynomial-time roster sampler on the explicit roster table.
[Gölz–Peters–Procaccia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11061) establish quota, house monotonicity and ex ante proportionality for stochastic apportionment. No result settling this exact explicit-lottery mass formulation was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Use m for the number of categories; use N for the number of departments in a finite implementation.
  • Normalize the positive department masses to sum to one; require at least two categories and a positive finite horizon, while allowing zero vacancies.
  • Make vacancies nonnegative binary-encoded integers and require every vacancy to be assigned to exactly one category.
  • Define k as the least common denominator of the reservation shares and make its explicit size part of the input through unary encoding.
  • Represent paths by periodic length-k rosters and retain quota at every seat prefix, reflecting the discrete policy's applicability to arbitrary vacancy histories.
  • Retain ex ante proportionality within each type, through unbiased roster-prefix counts, rather than requiring only the aggregate mass equality.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result and encoding. The lotteries can be constructed deterministically in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,H,k,L\). Each type can use the same lottery, supported on at most \(k\) rosters. Independent finite implementations satisfy both requested deviation bounds; in fact, either tail is bounded by \(e^{-2b^2/N}\).

Here “binary-rational” means a rational represented by a binary numerator and denominator. The dyadic-only interpretation is addressed at the end.

The prefix-flow formulation is closely related to [Evren–Khanna, Appendix A.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.11963v2#A2). The deterministic decomposition proved below supplies an explicit lottery of polynomial size.

1. Encoding quota-respecting rosters as circulations.

Set \(n_j=k\alpha_j\). These are positive integers satisfying \(\sum_j n_j=k\).

Construct a directed graph with vertices \(o,z\), position vertices \(v_r\) for \(1\leq r\leq k\), and category vertices \(c_{j,r}\) for \(1\leq j\leq m\), \(1\leq r\leq k\). Give its edges the following integer lower and upper bounds:

| Edge | Lower bound | Upper bound |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(o\to v_r\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |
| \(v_r\to c_{j,r}\) | \(0\) | \(1\) |
| \(c_{j,r}\to c_{j,r+1}\), \(r<k\) | \(\lfloor rn_j/k\rfloor\) | \(\lceil rn_j/k\rceil\) |
| \(c_{j,k}\to z\) | \(n_j\) | \(n_j\) |
| \(z\to o\) | \(k\) | \(k\) |

A circulation means an edge assignment satisfying these bounds and flow conservation at every vertex. Denote its feasible set by \(P\).

Every integral circulation determines a roster: conservation at \(v_r\) implies that exactly one edge \(v_r\to c_{j,r}\) carries flow \(1\); assign category \(j\) to position \(r\).

Conservation along the category chain then gives

\[ \text{flow on }c_{j,r}\to c_{j,r+1} =\sum_{s=1}^{r}\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma_s=j\}} =A_{\sigma,j}(r). \]

Consequently, its edge bounds enforce quota at every prefix before \(k\), and the terminal edge enforces \(A_{\sigma,j}(k)=n_j\). Conversely, every roster satisfying these prefix constraints defines an integral circulation by these formulas.

There is a particular fractional circulation \(f\in P\), given by

\[ f(o,v_r)=1,\qquad f(v_r,c_{j,r})=\frac{n_j}{k},\qquad f(c_{j,r},c_{j,r+1})=\frac{rn_j}{k}, \]

\[ f(c_{j,k},z)=n_j,\qquad f(z,o)=k. \]

To check conservation, position vertices use \(\sum_j n_j/k=1\); category vertices use
\((r-1)n_j/k+n_j/k=rn_j/k\); and \(o,z\) use \(\sum_j n_j=k\). Every edge value lies within its specified bounds. Moreover, \(kf\) is integral.

2. A constructive integer-decomposition lemma.

We prove the property needed to turn \(f\) into a short explicit lottery.

Lemma. Let \(P\) be the feasible circulations of a directed graph with integer bounds \(\ell_e,u_e\). If \(G\) is integral and \(G/D\in P\) for a positive integer \(D\), then \(G\) is the sum of \(D\) integral members of \(P\). Such a decomposition can be computed using at most \(D-1\) bounded-circulation feasibility computations.

Proof. For \(D=1\), take \(G\) itself. For \(D>1\), define tightened bounds

\[ \ell'_e=\max\{\ell_e,\ G_e-(D-1)u_e\},\qquad u'_e=\min\{u_e,\ G_e-(D-1)\ell_e\}. \]

The circulation \(G/D\) satisfies these tightened bounds. Indeed, it satisfies the original bounds, and

\[ \frac{G_e}{D}-\bigl(G_e-(D-1)u_e\bigr) =(D-1)\left(u_e-\frac{G_e}{D}\right)\geq0, \]

\[ \bigl(G_e-(D-1)\ell_e\bigr)-\frac{G_e}{D} =(D-1)\left(\frac{G_e}{D}-\ell_e\right)\geq0. \]

Thus the tightened instance is feasible. Its bounds are integers, so it has an integral feasible circulation \(g\), computable in polynomial time.

For completeness, this integrality and algorithmic assertion follows from the standard reduction to maximum flow. Subtract the lower bounds, give edge \(e\) residual capacity \(u'_e-\ell'_e\), and put

\[ b_v=\sum_{e\text{ entering }v}\ell'_e- \sum_{e\text{ leaving }v}\ell'_e. \]

Add an auxiliary source with an edge of capacity \(b_v\) to every vertex with \(b_v>0\), and an edge of capacity \(-b_v\) from every vertex with \(b_v<0\) to an auxiliary sink. Feasibility is equivalent to saturating all auxiliary-source edges. A fractional feasible circulation proves that this saturation is possible. An integral maximum-flow algorithm, such as Edmonds–Karp, finds an integral saturating flow in polynomial time; adding back the lower bounds yields \(g\).

Because \(g\) satisfies the tightened bounds,

\[ (D-1)\ell_e\leq G_e-g_e\leq(D-1)u_e. \]

Also, \(G-g\) is an integral circulation. Therefore \((G-g)/(D-1)\in P\), and induction decomposes the residual into \(D-1\) integral members of \(P\). Together with \(g\), these sum to \(G\). \(\square\)

Apply the lemma with \(G=kf\) and \(D=k\). It produces integral circulations \(g^{(1)},\ldots,g^{(k)}\) satisfying

\[ f=\frac1k\sum_{a=1}^{k}g^{(a)}. \]

Let their corresponding rosters be \(\sigma^{(1)},\ldots,\sigma^{(k)}\).

3. The explicit lottery, periodicity, and running time.

For every type \(t\), define

\[ p_{t,\sigma} =\frac{\bigl|\{a\in\{1,\ldots,k\}:\sigma^{(a)}=\sigma\}\bigr|}{k}, \]

listing only rosters with positive probability. This merges duplicates and leaves at most \(k\) supported rosters.

Averaging the assignment-edge flows gives, for every position \(r\) and category \(j\),

\[ \frac1k\sum_{a=1}^{k}\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma^{(a)}_r=j\}} =f(v_r,c_{j,r})=\alpha_j. \]

Summing over positions proves the required prefix expectations:

\[ \mathbb E_{p_t}[A_{\sigma,j}(r)] =\sum_{s=1}^{r}\alpha_j=\alpha_jr \qquad(0\leq r\leq k). \]

It remains to verify the infinite repetition requirement. For any nonnegative integer \(q\), write \(q=dk+r\), where \(0\leq r<k\). Every supported roster has exactly \(n_j\) occurrences of category \(j\) per block, so

\[ A_{\sigma,j}(q)=dn_j+A_{\sigma,j}(r), \qquad \alpha_jq=dn_j+\alpha_jr. \]

Since \(dn_j\) is integral, quota at \(r\) implies quota at \(q\). The same identity proves

\[ \mathbb E_{p_t}[A_{\sigma,j}(q)]=\alpha_jq. \]

In particular, at every supplied cumulative vacancy count,

\[ \sum_t\mu_t\mathbb E_{p_t}[a_{t,j,h}] =\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_jQ_{t,h}. \]

Nondecreasing coordinates and \(\sum_j a_{t,j,h}=Q_{t,h}\) follow directly from counting letters in successive prefixes.

The network has \(2+k+mk\) vertices and \(k+2mk+1\) edges. The initial integer circulation \(kf\) has edge values at most \(k^2\). All residual values and tightened bounds therefore have polynomial bit length. The algorithm makes at most \(k-1\) polynomial-time maximum-flow calls.

Each output probability has the form \(c/k\), with \(1\leq c\leq k\), and requires \(O(\log k)\) bits. Explicitly repeating the lottery for all types uses

\[ O\bigl(\tau k^2\log m+\tau k\log k\bigr) \]

bits. Thus both computation and output size are polynomial in the requested parameters.

The vacancy magnitudes do not affect the network. If induced paths are also wanted, compute \(Q_{t,h}\), divide it by \(k\), and use the preceding block identity. These are binary arithmetic operations; no expansion into individual vacancies or departments is needed.

4. Independent finite implementations: both deviation bounds.

Fix a category \(j\) and period \(h\). Index the finite departments by \(i=1,\ldots,N\), with exactly \(N\mu_t\) departments of type \(t\). Let

\[ x_i=\alpha_jQ_{t(i),h},\qquad X_i=A_{\sigma_i,j}(Q_{t(i),h}). \]

The quota and expectation properties give

\[ X_i\in\{\lfloor x_i\rfloor,\lceil x_i\rceil\}, \qquad \mathbb E[X_i]=x_i. \]

Define \(B_i=X_i-\lfloor x_i\rfloor\) and \(p_i=x_i-\lfloor x_i\rfloor\). Then \(B_i\) is a Bernoulli variable with expectation \(p_i\), including the deterministic case \(p_i=0\). Independent roster draws make these variables independent. Furthermore,

\[ S_{j,h}-F_{j,h} =\sum_{i=1}^{N}(B_i-p_i). \]

Here \(\sum_i x_i=F_{j,h}\) follows from the prescribed counts \(N\mu_t\).

We give a moment-generating-function proof valid for every real \(b>0\). For a Bernoulli variable of mean \(p\), set

\[ g_p(\lambda) =\log(1-p+pe^\lambda)-p\lambda. \]

Then \(g_p(0)=g'_p(0)=0\), and

\[ g''_p(\lambda)=\theta_\lambda(1-\theta_\lambda)\leq\frac14, \qquad \theta_\lambda=\frac{pe^\lambda}{1-p+pe^\lambda}. \]

Taylor’s formula with integral remainder, valid for either sign of \(\lambda\), gives

\[ g_p(\lambda) =\lambda^2\int_0^1(1-s)g''_p(s\lambda)\,ds \leq\frac{\lambda^2}{8}. \]

Writing \(Z=S_{j,h}-F_{j,h}\), independence therefore implies

\[ \mathbb E[e^{\lambda Z}]\leq e^{N\lambda^2/8} \qquad(\lambda\in\mathbb R). \]

For \(\lambda>0\), Markov’s inequality yields

\[ \Pr(Z\geq b)\leq e^{-\lambda b+N\lambda^2/8}, \qquad \Pr(Z\leq-b)\leq e^{-\lambda b+N\lambda^2/8}. \]

Choosing \(\lambda=4b/N\) proves

\[ \Pr(Z\geq b)\leq e^{-2b^2/N}, \qquad \Pr(Z\leq-b)\leq e^{-2b^2/N}. \]

Because \(b>0\),

\[ e^{-2b^2/N}<e^{-b^2/(3N)}, \qquad e^{-2b^2/N}<e^{-b^2/(2N)}. \]

These give exactly the two requested strict inequalities. The proof applies to any lotteries satisfying the stated quota and expectation requirements, provided departments draw independently. Retaining a roster across periods creates dependence across periods, which does not affect these separate bounds for each fixed \(j,h\).

5. If “binary-rational” means dyadic only.

Under that alternative interpretation, a finite lottery with dyadic probabilities can exist only if every share is dyadic: at the first position,

\[ \alpha_j=\Pr(\sigma_1=j) \]

is a finite sum of dyadic probabilities. For example, shares \((1/3,2/3)\) would be infeasible.

Conversely, if every share is dyadic, their least common denominator \(k\) is a power of two, and the construction above gives dyadic probabilities \(c/k\). Thus this interpretation also has a polynomial-time feasibility-and-construction algorithm, with feasibility exactly characterized by dyadic shares.
