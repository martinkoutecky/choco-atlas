The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite applicant types \(P\) and job types \(Q\), rational masses \(\mu_p,\nu_q\), a compatibility graph \(E\), applicant rankings, and two scenarios with the same masses and graph that differ only in the ranking of one positive-mass type \(p^\star\), decide whether there is a single measurable matching \(M\) of the atomless populations such that, in each scenario \(s\), every feasible measurable matching \(N\) satisfies \(\Delta_s(M,N)\ge 0\). Each individual is matched to at most one compatible counterpart or left unmatched; \(\Delta_s\) is the mass preferring their assignment under \(M\) minus the mass preferring their assignment under \(N\), using the paper’s voting and abstention rules.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P,Q\) be nonempty finite sets of applicant and job types, with compatibility graph \(E\subseteq P\times Q\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|P|+|Q|\) positive-mass types, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Normalize \(\sum_p\mu_p+\sum_q\nu_q=1\), allowing unequal total masses on the two sides. Set \(m=|Q|\), and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Represent each type by a disjoint Lebesgue interval of its mass. A feasible measurable matching is a measure-preserving measurable bijection, with measurable inverse, between measurable subsets of the applicant and job populations, using only compatible type pairs; all remaining individuals are unmatched. Matchings are understood up to null sets.
In each scenario \(s\in\{1,2\}\), applicants of type \(p\) strictly rank their compatible job types, are indifferent between individuals of the same job type, and rank unmatched last. Jobs are indifferent between all compatible applicants and strictly prefer being matched to being unmatched.
The scenarios have identical masses and compatibility graph; only the ranking of one positive-mass applicant type \(p^\star\) differs. The same individual matching must be used in both scenarios.
An individual votes for the matching giving a strictly preferred assignment and otherwise abstains. Define \(\Delta_s(M,N)\) as the total mass on both sides voting for \(M\) minus that voting for \(N\). A matching \(M\) is robust popular when \(\Delta_s(M,N)\geq0\) for every feasible measurable matching \(N\) and both scenarios.

Is deciding whether a robust popular measurable matching exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite individual populations with strict applicant preferences and jobs tying all compatible applicants, robust popular matching existence under one applicant’s ranking change has a polynomial-time algorithm ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25209)).
No result settling this exact atomless decision problem was located.

Status and new partial results

The general polynomial-time versus NP-hard classification remains unresolved. I establish three additional results:

  • A polynomial-time algorithm when all job masses equal a common unit, all applicant masses are integer multiples of that unit, and the changed type has exactly one unit. Binary-encoded multiplicities may be arbitrarily large.
  • An exact algorithm running in

$$ \bigl[4(m+1)^2\bigr]^{|P|}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$
Consequently, the problem is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed number of applicant types.

  • A scenario-merging theorem for a fixed flow: unless the changed type is split between two jobs whose relative order reverses, robustness can be certified by one common job certificate. For an aligned pair of supported jobs, robustness is equivalent to popularity in an explicitly constructed single hybrid scenario.

I first prove the finite characterization needed for these results.

1. Type flows and measurable realizations

Write
$$ \Gamma(p)=\{q\in Q:(p,q)\in E\}, $$
and denote the unmatched outcome by $0$.

For a measurable matching, let $x_{pa}$ be the mass of type-$p$ applicants receiving outcome $a$. Feasibility is exactly
$$ x_{pa}\geq 0,\qquad x_{p0}+\sum_{q\in\Gamma(p)}x_{pq}=\mu_p, $$
together with
$$ u_q:=\nu_q-\sum_{p:q\in\Gamma(p)}x_{pq}\geq 0. $$
Let
$$ T=\sum_{(p,q)\in E}x_{pq}. $$

Every such flow is realizable: partition applicant and job intervals into the required lengths and match corresponding pieces by translations.

For comparisons with an arbitrary existing matching, its assignment classes need not be intervals. The following measure-theoretic fact supplies the required realizations.

Measurable realization fact. Two measurable subsets of bounded intervals having the same positive Lebesgue measure admit a measure-preserving measurable bijection with measurable inverse, modulo null sets.

To justify this, replace a measurable set $A$ by a Borel representative modulo a null set and put
$$ F_A(t)=\lambda\bigl(A\cap(-\infty,t]\bigr). $$
This function is continuous and nondecreasing. Its quantile map
$$ G_A(v)=\inf\{t:F_A(t)\geq v\}, \qquad 0<v<\lambda(A), $$
pushes Lebesgue measure forward to Lebesgue measure restricted to $A$. Continuity gives $F_A(G_A(v))=v$. Noninjectivity of $F_A$ occurs on its constant intervals; their interiors are disjoint and countable, each intersects $A$ in measure zero, and their endpoints form a countable set. After removing these exceptional sets, $F_A$ and $G_A$ are measurable inverses. Composing these maps for two sets proves the fact.

Thus assignment classes can be partitioned into prescribed masses, and their pieces can be matched measurably. Zero-mass pieces require no map.

2. An exact linear program for challengers

Fix a feasible flow $x$, a matching realizing it, and a scenario $s$.

For $a\in\Gamma(p)\cup\{0\}$ and $q\in\Gamma(p)$, define
$$ \sigma_s(p;q,a)= \begin{cases} 1,&q\text{ is strictly preferred to }a,\\ 0,&q=a,\\ -1,&a\text{ is strictly preferred to }q. \end{cases} $$
In particular, $\sigma_s(p;q,0)=1$.

Let $z_{pa,q}$ be the mass currently receiving $a$ that a challenger assigns to $q$. The possible arrays are exactly those satisfying
$$ z_{pa,q}\geq 0,\qquad \sum_{q\in\Gamma(p)}z_{pa,q}\leq x_{pa}, $$
and
$$ \sum_{p:q\in\Gamma(p)} \sum_{a\in\Gamma(p)\cup\{0\}}z_{pa,q}\leq\nu_q. $$
Necessity follows from applicant conservation and job capacities. Sufficiency follows by partitioning the current assignment classes and applying the measurable realization fact.

If the challenger matches mass $T_N$, jobs contribute $T_N-T$ votes in its favor: jobs matched in both matchings abstain, and the difference between the masses matched exclusively in the two matchings is $T_N-T$.

Applicants contribute
$$ -T+\sum_{p,a,q}z_{pa,q} \left(\sigma_s(p;q,a)+\mathbf 1_{\{a\neq0\}}\right). $$
Indeed, a previously matched applicant left unmatched contributes $-1$, whereas a previously unmatched applicant left unmatched contributes zero.

Consequently,
$$ \Delta_s(N,M) = -2T+\sum_{p,a,q}w_s(p,a,q)z_{pa,q}, $$
where
$$ w_s(p,a,q) = 1+\mathbf 1_{\{a\neq0\}}+\sigma_s(p;q,a). $$
The weights equal $2$ for $a=0$, and equal $3,2,1$ for a previously matched applicant receiving a better, identical, or worse job type.

Let $V_s(x)$ be the maximum weighted objective over these constraints. The challenger $N=M$ gives value $2T$. Therefore
$$ x\text{ is popular in scenario }s \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V_s(x)=2T. $$

In particular, popularity depends only on the type flow. Every realization of a robust popular flow is one common individual matching popular in both scenarios.

3. Three-valued certificates and the support bound

Certificate theorem. A feasible flow $x$ is popular in scenario $s$ if and only if there exists
$$ \beta^s\in\{0,1,2\}^{Q} $$
such that:

  • If $u_q>0$, then $\beta^s_q=0$.
  • If $x_{p0}>0$, then $\beta^s_q=2$ for every $q\in\Gamma(p)$.
  • If $x_{pa}>0$ and $a\in\Gamma(p)$, then

$$ \beta^s_q-\beta^s_a\geq\sigma_s(p;q,a) \qquad(q\in\Gamma(p)). $$

Proof of necessity

The challenger linear program has dual
$$ \min\left( \sum_{p,a}x_{pa}\alpha_{pa} + \sum_q\nu_q\beta_q \right), $$
subject to
$$ \alpha_{pa}\geq0,\qquad \beta_q\geq0,\qquad \alpha_{pa}+\beta_q\geq w_s(p,a,q). $$
The primal is feasible and bounded; the dual is feasible, for example with $\alpha_{pa}=3$ and $\beta_q=0$. Strong duality and complementary slackness therefore apply.

Suppose $x$ is popular. The self-challenger, with $z_{pa,a}=x_{pa}$ for matched outcomes, is optimal.

For $x_{pa}>0$ with $a\neq0$, complementary slackness gives
$$ \alpha_{pa}+\beta_a=2. $$
Hence $\beta_a\leq2$, and the remaining dual inequalities give
$$ \beta_q-\beta_a\geq\sigma_s(p;q,a). $$

A positive unmatched row is unused by the self-challenger. Its capacity constraint has positive slack, so $\alpha_{p0}=0$. Thus every compatible $\beta_q\geq2$.

An unsaturated job has $\beta_q=0$. A saturated job has positive incoming mass because $\nu_q>0$, so the preceding matched-row equality bounds its coordinate by $2$. Therefore every coordinate lies in $[0,2]$, and the unmatched-row constraints force the required coordinates to equal $2$.

Finally, floor every coordinate. For an integer $k$,
$$ r-t\geq k \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lfloor r\rfloor-\lfloor t\rfloor\geq k. $$
All comparison bounds are integers, and forced values $0$ and $2$ remain unchanged. This yields the claimed certificate.

Proof of sufficiency

Given such a certificate, set
$$ \alpha_{pa}=2-\beta_a $$
on positive matched rows, and set $\alpha_{p0}=0$ on positive unmatched rows. On zero-mass rows set $\alpha_{pa}=3$.

These variables are nonnegative and dual feasible by conditions 2 and 3. Their objective is
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{p,a\neq0}x_{pa}(2-\beta_a)+\sum_q\nu_q\beta_q &=2T+\sum_q u_q\beta_q\\ &=2T. \end{aligned} $$
The last equality uses condition 1. The self-challenger already attains $2T$, so weak duality proves $V_s(x)=2T$.

This proves the theorem.

Consequence: at most two supported outcomes

If two distinct jobs $a,b$ receive positive mass from the same applicant type, and $a$ is preferred to $b$, applying condition 3 in both directions gives
$$ \beta_a-\beta_b=1. $$
Three supported jobs ordered $a\succ b\succ c$ would require
$$ \beta_a-\beta_b=1,\qquad \beta_b-\beta_c=1,\qquad \beta_a-\beta_c=1, $$
which is impossible.

If unmatched mass is positive, all compatible coordinates equal $2$, so two distinct matched outcomes cannot both be supported. Moreover, any positively assigned job must be the top compatible job: a better job would require a certificate difference of at least $1$ between two coordinates equal to $2$.

Thus every popular flow gives each applicant type at most two positive-mass outcomes, counting unmatched. In a robust flow, a type with both matched and unmatched mass receives its matched mass at a common top job.

4. Polynomial time for uniform job masses and a one-unit changed type

Theorem. Suppose there is a positive rational $h$ such that
$$ \nu_q=h\quad(q\in Q),\qquad \mu_p=k_ph\quad(p\in P), $$
where every $k_p$ is a positive integer, and
$$ k_{p^\star}=1. $$
Then robust popular measurable matching existence is decidable in polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$.

The proof invokes the finite-population theorem supplied in the question: with strict applicant preferences, jobs tying all compatible applicants, and only one applicant changing rankings, robust popular existence is polynomial-time decidable. This is the result stated in [the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25209).

Step 1: work in units of mass $h$

Divide all masses and flows by $h$. Job capacities become $1$ and applicant supplies become $k_p$.

Multiplying every population mass by a common positive number multiplies every voting margin by that number. It therefore preserves popularity and robustness. The unit-scaled instance need not have total mass $1$.

Step 2: truncate large multiplicities

Define
$$ k'_p=\min(k_p,m+1). $$
I claim that replacing $k_p$ by $k'_p$ preserves robust existence.

In any feasible unit-scaled flow, the total matched mass is at most $m$. If $k_p>m+1$, type $p$ consequently has positive unmatched mass. Keep all its matched assignments and replace its unmatched amount by
$$ x'_{p0}=m+1-\sum_{q\in\Gamma(p)}x_{pq}. $$
This remains at least $1$. Thus truncation preserves exactly which rows have positive mass, as well as every job occupancy. Every certificate condition from Section 3 is unchanged.

Conversely, restore the omitted supply as additional unmatched mass. A truncated type with supply $m+1$ already has positive unmatched mass, so restoration again changes no certificate condition.

Applying this argument simultaneously to all truncated types proves the equivalence.

Step 3: an atomless solution implies an integral type flow

Fix certificates for a robust solution of the truncated instance.

Allow a matched edge precisely when its comparison inequalities hold in both scenarios. Allow an unmatched edge precisely when all compatible job coordinates equal $2$ in both certificates. Require job $q$ to be saturated whenever either certificate coordinate is positive.

The resulting feasibility problem is a network-flow problem. Applicant supplies are the integers $k'_p$, job upper capacities are $1$, and required job lower capacities are also $1$.

All bounds are integral. Subtracting lower bounds reduces feasibility to an ordinary integral-capacity flow problem; an integral feasible flow therefore exists whenever a real feasible flow exists. Hence a robust measurable solution implies a robust type flow with integral entries.

Step 4: exact equivalence with a finite instance

Create $k'_p$ individual applicants of type $p$ and one individual job of each type $q$.

Because each job type has only one individual representative, each applicant’s preferences over compatible individual jobs are strict. Jobs tie all compatible applicants. Since $k'_{p^\star}=1$, exactly one individual applicant changes rankings.

An integral type flow lifts to a finite individual matching.

To check that popularity transfers exactly, fix any finite matching and group its applicants by type and current outcome. Its challenger program from Section 2 has integral row capacities and integral job capacities. It is a weighted bipartite flow problem, so it has an integral optimum. Every integral challenger array is realizable by a finite matching: allocate the indicated numbers of applicants from each assignment class to the indicated jobs.

Thus the maximum finite voting margin equals the maximum in the atomless challenger program. A finite matching is popular in either scenario exactly when its type flow is popular measurably in that scenario.

Consequently, robust existence in the finite and truncated atomless instances is equivalent.

Step 5: polynomial input size

The expanded finite instance has at most
$$ m+\sum_{p\in P}k'_p \leq m+|P|(m+1) $$
individuals. Its compatibility edges and preference lists also have polynomial size.

The integers $k_p=\mu_p/h$ can be computed using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial binary time. Truncation uses comparisons with $m+1$, so exponentially large multiplicities cause no expansion.

The finite theorem now gives the claimed polynomial-time decision algorithm.

5. A common certificate when the supported order agrees

This section concerns a fixed feasible flow, rather than searching over all flows.

Scenario-merging theorem. Suppose $x_{p^\star0}=0$, and the changed type’s positive matched support is either:

  • one job $a$; or
  • two jobs $a,b$ with $a\succ b$ in both scenarios.

There is an explicitly constructible strict hybrid ranking such that
$$ x\text{ is robust popular} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\text{ is popular in the hybrid scenario}. $$
Moreover, whenever $x$ is robust, one certificate certifies it in both original scenarios.

Step 1: construct the hybrid ranking

For support $\{a\}$, put above $a$ precisely the jobs ranked above $a$ in at least one scenario. Put all remaining compatible jobs below $a$, ordering each group arbitrarily.

For support $\{a,b\}$ with $a\succ b$ in both scenarios, partition the other compatible jobs into
$$ U=\{q:q\succ_s a\text{ for some }s\}, $$
$$ V=\{q\notin U:q\succ_s b\text{ for some }s\}, $$
and the remaining set $W$. Use the strict ranking
$$ U\succ a\succ V\succ b\succ W, $$
with arbitrary strict orders within the groups.

For every positively supported outcome $c$ and every compatible job $q$, this construction gives
$$ \sigma_H(p^\star;q,c) = \max\{\sigma_1(p^\star;q,c),\sigma_2(p^\star;q,c)\}. $$
For example, a job in $U$ is better than both supported jobs in at least one scenario. A job in $V$ is below $a$ in both scenarios and above $b$ in at least one. A job in $W$ is below both in both scenarios.

Thus hybrid popularity immediately implies popularity in each original scenario: every challenger’s hybrid voting margin is at least its margin in either original scenario.

The reverse implication requires a common certificate.

Step 2: a difference-constraint lemma

Represent an inequality
$$ y_v-y_u\geq c $$
by a directed edge $u\to v$ of weight $c$.

Such a system is feasible if and only if its graph has no directed cycle of positive total weight.

Necessity follows by summing inequalities around a cycle. For sufficiency, add a new source with zero-weight edges to all vertices. If no cycle has positive weight, every walk can have its repeated-vertex cycles removed without decreasing its weight. Maximum walk weights from the source are therefore finite and attained, and satisfy every edge inequality.

A useful consequence is:

Lemma. Suppose two feasible difference-constraint systems have a common set of inequalities, and every noncommon inequality in either system has the same tail vertex $a$. Then their union is feasible.

Otherwise the union would contain a positive simple cycle. Such a cycle uses at most one edge with tail $a$, hence at most one noncommon edge. It would therefore lie entirely in one original system, contradicting that system’s feasibility.

For integer edge weights, the construction above produces integer potentials.

Step 3: apply the lemma to certificates

For a fixed flow, all certificate conditions other than the changed type’s comparison inequalities are common to both scenarios.

Introduce a reference variable $\beta_r=0$. Bounds and forced values are difference constraints:
$$ 0\leq\beta_q\leq2,\qquad \beta_q=0,\qquad \beta_q=2, $$
as appropriate. Every comparison inequality is also a difference constraint.

With one supported job $a$, all scenario-dependent inequalities have tail $a$. The lemma produces a common feasible certificate.

With two supported jobs $a,b$ ordered $a\succ b$ in both scenarios, the comparisons between the supported jobs force
$$ \beta_a-\beta_b=1 $$
in both systems. Substitute
$$ \beta_b=\beta_a-1 $$
throughout.

After this substitution, every changed-type comparison inequality has tail $a$. All other transformed inequalities remain common. The lemma again produces a simultaneous solution.

The transformed bounds ensure that reconstructed coordinates lie in $[0,2]$. Integer potentials, shifted so that the reference coordinate is zero, give a certificate in $\{0,1,2\}^{Q}$.

This common certificate satisfies the maximum of the two comparison bounds on every supported changed-type row, hence certifies popularity in the hybrid scenario. The theorem follows.

Unmatched outcomes and the precise obstruction

If the changed type is entirely unmatched, its votes against every challenger are independent of its ranking.

If it has both unmatched mass and matched mass in a robust flow, Section 3 forces its matched outcome to be a common top job. Its comparisons from that outcome are then identical in both scenarios, so any certificate for one scenario also certifies the other.

Together with the support bound, this proves:

Corollary. For a robust popular flow, a common certificate exists unless the changed type supports two jobs whose relative order reverses. In that reversed-pair case, a common certificate is impossible.

The impossibility follows because the two scenarios would require simultaneously
$$ \beta_a-\beta_b=1 \quad\text{and}\quad \beta_b-\beta_a=1. $$

This is a statement about merging certificates for a given flow. It does not yet provide a polynomial-time existence algorithm with prescribed supported outcomes.

6. An exact algorithm parameterized by applicant types

Let $n=|P|$.

Theorem. General robust popular existence is decidable in time
$$ \bigl[4(m+1)^2\bigr]^n\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

Step 1: enumerate outcome supports

For every applicant type, choose a nonempty set
$$ A_p\subseteq\Gamma(p)\cup\{0\}, \qquad |A_p|\leq2. $$
There are at most $(m+1)^2$ choices per type.

Let
$$ J=\bigcup_{p\in P}(A_p\setminus\{0\}). $$
Then $|J|\leq2n$.

We allow selected outcomes to receive zero mass. This avoids strict feasibility constraints; completeness will follow by selecting the actual positive support of a solution.

Step 2: enumerate a set of jobs required to be saturated

Choose
$$ S\subseteq J. $$
There are at most $2^{2n}$ choices.

For each scenario, independently solve the following difference-constraint system:
$$ 0\leq\beta_q^s\leq2, $$
$$ \beta_q^s=0\qquad(q\notin S), $$
$$ \beta_q^s=2 \qquad (0\in A_p,\ q\in\Gamma(p)), $$
and
$$ \beta_q^s-\beta_a^s\geq\sigma_s(p;q,a) \qquad (a\in A_p\setminus\{0\},\ q\in\Gamma(p)). $$
The difference-constraint criterion from Section 5 gives a polynomial-time feasibility test and, when feasible, an integer solution.

Step 3: solve a separate transportation problem

Restrict type $p$ to outcomes in $A_p$. Impose applicant conservation, job upper capacities $\nu_q$, and saturation
$$ \sum_p x_{pq}=\nu_q\qquad(q\in S). $$
This is a rational network-flow feasibility problem of polynomial size.

The certificate systems and transportation problem are independent once $A_p$ and $S$ have been fixed.

Correctness

If all three feasibility tests succeed, every positive flow row belongs to its selected support and satisfies the required certificate inequalities. Every unsaturated job lies outside $S$, so both certificate coordinates are zero. The certificate theorem proves robustness.

Conversely, suppose a robust flow exists. Choose each $A_p$ to be its actual positive support, which has size at most two. Choose
$$ S=\{q:\beta_q^1>0\text{ or }\beta_q^2>0\} $$
from certificates of that flow.

Every job in $S$ is saturated. Since its mass is positive, it receives positive incoming flow, so $S\subseteq J$. The existing flow and certificates satisfy all enumerated systems. Hence the algorithm accepts.

Multiplying the numbers of choices proves the runtime bound. In particular, for every fixed $n$, the runtime is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

The same certificate characterization also proves membership in $\mathrm{NP}$: guess the two three-valued certificates and check their permitted-edge transportation problem in polynomial time.

7. A robust reversed-pair example

The exceptional case in Section 5 actually occurs.

Take
$$ P=\{p^\star\},\qquad Q=\{a,b\}, $$
with
$$ \mu_{p^\star}=\frac12,\qquad \nu_a=\nu_b=\frac14. $$
Both pairs are compatible. The rankings are
$$ a\succ_1 b,\qquad b\succ_2 a. $$
Use the common flow
$$ x_{p^\star a}=x_{p^\star b}=\frac14, \qquad x_{p^\star0}=0. $$

Both jobs are saturated. Scenario 1 has certificate
$$ (\beta_a^1,\beta_b^1)=(1,0), $$
and scenario 2 has certificate
$$ (\beta_a^2,\beta_b^2)=(0,1). $$
In each scenario, the better supported job has certificate value exactly one greater than the worse supported job, so all certificate conditions hold. The flow is robust popular.

Nevertheless, no common certificate exists.

Furthermore, taking the pointwise maximum of the changed type’s challenger votes would regard a move from $a$ to $b$ as an improvement and also regard a move from $b$ to $a$ as an improvement. Exchanging the two applicant pieces would then have a positive margin in this artificial comparison rule, although it ties the original matching in each actual scenario.

Thus replacing robustness by independently choosing the worse scenario for each individual is invalid.

8. What remains open

These results do not settle the unrestricted decision problem.

The finite theorem transfers rigorously to the uniform-job subclass in Section 4 because truncation keeps the finite expansion polynomial, applicants retain strict preferences, and exactly one individual applicant changes rankings. Arbitrary rational job masses and a larger changed-type mass do not satisfy that reduction’s hypotheses.

For a fixed flow, scenario merging is now understood: an aligned supported pair admits a single hybrid certificate; a reversed supported pair necessarily requires distinct certificates. What remains is an efficient search over feasible supports, saturations, and certificates—or a hardness reduction exploiting that search.
