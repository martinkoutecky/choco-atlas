The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type graph \(H=(T,F)\), rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), rational \(\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac12]\), and nonnegative rational type-pair values \(w\), define \(\nu_w(q)\) for \(0\le q\le\mu\) as the maximum of \(\sum_{tu\in F}w_{tu}z_{tu}\) subject to \(2z_{tt}+\sum_{u\ne t:tu\in F}z_{tu}\le q_t\) for each \(t\), and \(z\ge0\). Does there exist a polynomial-time algorithm that outputs \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\) with \(\sum_t x_t=\nu_w(\mu)\), \(\sum_t(q_t/\mu_t)x_t\ge(\tfrac12-\varepsilon)\nu_w(q)\) for every \(q\le\mu\), \(q\ne\mu\), and a universal constant \(C\) such that \(\|x(w)-x(w')\|_1\le(C/\varepsilon)\|w-w'\|_1\) for all nonnegative \(w,w'\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Let \(H=(T,F)\) be an undirected type graph, allowing loops and having no parallel edges; put \(m=|F|\). Each unordered pair in \(F\) has a nonnegative binary-rational value \(w_{tu}\). Let \(\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac12]\) be binary rational, and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A coalition is any real mass vector \(q\) with \(0\le q\le\mu\), componentwise. Define \(\nu_w(q)=\max\{\sum_{tu\in F}w_{tu}z_{tu}:z\ge0,\;2z_{tt}+\sum_{u\ne t:tu\in F}z_{tu}\le q_t\text{ for every }t\}\), taking \(z_{tt}=0\) when \(tt\notin F\).
An allocation \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\) gives total payoff \(x_t\) to type \(t\), distributed uniformly within that type. It is admissible if \(\sum_t x_t=\nu_w(\mu)\) and \(\sum_t(q_t/\mu_t)x_t\ge(\tfrac12-\varepsilon)\nu_w(q)\) for every \(0\le q\le\mu\) with \(q\ne\mu\).
For fixed \(H,\mu,\varepsilon\), an allocation rule \(A_{H,\mu,\varepsilon}\) is defined on all nonnegative real weight vectors; its rational-input outputs must be exactly computable as binary-rational vectors.
Does there exist such a deterministic allocation rule, computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), that always returns an admissible allocation and satisfies \(\|A_{H,\mu,\varepsilon}(w)-A_{H,\mu,\varepsilon}(w')\|_1\le(C/\varepsilon)\|w-w'\|_1\) for all \(w,w'\ge0\), with a universal constant \(C\) independent of \(H,\mu,\varepsilon\)?
For discrete matching games, where coalition value is maximum matching weight, a polynomial-time deterministic rule achieves a \((\tfrac12-\varepsilon)\)-approximate core allocation with Lipschitz constant \(O(\varepsilon^{-1})\). Result

Yes. In fact, there is a deterministic polynomial-time rule, independent of \(\varepsilon\), that satisfies every coalition inequality with factor \(\tfrac23\) and has Lipschitz constant \(\tfrac92\).

Consequently, the requested guarantee holds with the universal constant \(C=\tfrac94\), because \(\varepsilon\le\tfrac12\).

Here binary-rational computation means exact rational computation using binary-encoded numerators and denominators. The construction below produces outputs of polynomial encoding length.

1. Dual covers and coalition inequalities

Define the dual-cover polyhedron
\[ \mathcal P(w)= \left\{ p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}: p_t+p_u\ge w_{tu}\text{ for }tu\in F,\ t\ne u,\quad 2p_t\ge w_{tt}\text{ for }tt\in F \right\}. \]

The matching linear program in the question has dual
\[ \nu_w(q)=\min_{p\in\mathcal P(w)}\sum_t q_t p_t. \]
Indeed, its primal is feasible, and every edge variable is bounded by the capacities of its incident types. Its optimum is therefore finite, so linear-programming duality applies.

For any \(p\in\mathcal P(w)\), weak duality gives
\[ \sum_t q_t p_t\ge\nu_w(q) \qquad(0\le q\le\mu). \]

More generally, write \(a_t=x_t/\mu_t\). For any nonnegative factor \(\alpha\), the coalition inequalities are equivalent to
\[ a_t+a_u\ge\alpha w_{tu}\quad(t\ne u), \qquad 2a_t\ge\alpha w_{tt}. \]

To verify necessity for a nonloop edge \(tu\), choose a positive amount \(h\) sufficiently small that the coalition with \(q_t=q_u=h\) and all other coordinates zero is proper and feasible. Matching amount \(h\) on this edge gives \(\nu_w(q)\ge h w_{tu}\), hence \(a_t+a_u\ge\alpha w_{tu}\). For a loop, use \(q_t=2h\) instead.

For sufficiency, multiply each edge inequality by the corresponding feasible matching variable and sum. The capacity constraints and nonnegativity of \(a\) imply
\[ \sum_t q_ta_t\ge\alpha\sum_{tu\in F}w_{tu}z_{tu}. \]
Taking the maximum over feasible \(z\) proves the coalition inequality.

2. A quadratic selection of dual covers

For \(s\ge0\), define
\[ P(w,s)= \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{p\in\mathcal P(w)} \sum_t\mu_t\left(\frac12p_t^2+s p_t\right). \]

The feasible set is nonempty: a sufficiently large constant vector belongs to it. Because every \(\mu_t\) is positive, the objective is strictly convex and coercive. Thus \(P(w,s)\) exists and is unique.

Put \(W(w)=\max_{e\in F}w_e\), with \(W(w)=0\) when \(F\) is empty. Every coordinate of \(P(w,s)\) is at most \(W(w)\). To see this, replace any coordinate exceeding \(W(w)\) by \(W(w)\). Every incident edge constraint remains satisfied, while the objective strictly decreases.

The following sensitivity lemma is the central property of this selection.

Lemma. For nonnegative weights \(w,w'\) and parameters \(s,s'\ge0\),
\[ \|P(w,s)-P(w',s')\|_\infty \le \|w-w'\|_1+|s-s'|. \]

Finite affine pieces

First, along any affine parameter segment, \(P(w,s)\) is continuous and piecewise affine, with finitely many pieces.

Here is a justification that also handles degeneracy. Write the inequalities defining \(\mathcal P(w)\) as \(Gp\ge b(w)\), including nonnegativity. The first-order optimality condition expresses the objective gradient as a nonnegative combination of active constraint normals. Such a combination can be chosen to use linearly independent normals: if the normals in its support are dependent, subtract a suitable multiple of a dependence until one coefficient becomes zero, and repeat.

For each possible independent subset \(I\), solve the equality-constrained quadratic problem with constraints \(G_Ip=b_I(w)\). Its minimizer and multipliers are affine functions of \((w,s)\), since its Hessian is the fixed positive definite matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(\mu)\). The conditions that this candidate is feasible and its multipliers are nonnegative are linear inequalities in the parameters.

There are finitely many subsets \(I\); their candidate regions cover the parameter domain by optimality. On overlaps, candidates agree by uniqueness. Restricting these regions to a parameter segment proves the asserted continuous, finite piecewise affine representation. Refining the pieces further makes the full active constraint set constant on each open piece.

Differentiation on an affine piece

Let \(d\) denote the derivative of \(P(w,s)\) on one such piece. If \(v\) satisfies the homogeneous equations of all active constraints, first-order optimality gives
\[ \sum_t\mu_t(p_t+s)v_t=0. \]
Differentiating, with the active set fixed, gives
\[ \sum_t\mu_t(d_t+\dot s)v_t=0. \tag{1} \]

Represent the active nonloop constraints by a graph. Along its edges, a derivative satisfying homogeneous active equations obeys \(d_t+d_u=0\). Consequently:

  • A component containing an odd cycle has derivative zero everywhere.
  • An active loop or active nonnegativity constraint forces derivative zero at its vertex and therefore throughout its component.
  • Every remaining component is bipartite, and its homogeneous derivatives have the form \(d_t=a\sigma_t\), where \(\sigma_t\in\{-1,1\}\) is a bipartition sign.

Call the first two kinds of components *fixed*, and the remaining components *free*.

Varying \(s\) alone

Hold \(w\) fixed and vary \(s\) at unit speed. On a fixed component, \(d=0\). On a free component \(B\), substitute \(d_t=a\sigma_t\) and take \(v_t=\sigma_t\) on that component in (1). This yields
\[ a\sum_{t\in B}\mu_t+\sum_{t\in B}\mu_t\sigma_t=0. \]
Therefore \(|a|\le1\), and hence \(\|d\|_\infty\le1\).

Integrating over the finitely many affine pieces proves
\[ \|P(w,s)-P(w,s')\|_\infty\le|s-s'|. \tag{2} \]

Varying one edge weight alone

Hold \(s\) fixed and vary one edge weight at unit speed.

If its constraint is inactive on the piece, all active right-hand sides are constant. Then \(d\) is a homogeneous tangent vector, and using \(v=d\) in (1) gives \(\sum_t\mu_td_t^2=0\). Thus \(d=0\).

Otherwise, remove this one constraint from the active system. Components not incident to the removed constraint have derivative zero: on any free such component, using its bipartition sign as \(v\) in (1) forces its coefficient \(a\) to vanish.

Suppose first that the perturbed edge is a nonloop \(tu\). Its derivative equation is
\[ d_t+d_u=1. \tag{3} \]

If \(t,u\) lie in the same remaining component, that component must be free. Opposite bipartition signs would contradict (3). Equal signs give absolute derivative \(\tfrac12\) throughout the component.

If \(t,u\) lie in different components, two fixed components would contradict (3). If exactly one is fixed, the free component has absolute derivative \(1\). If both are free, choose their signs so that \(\sigma_t=\sigma_u=1\). Write their derivatives as \(a\sigma\) and \(b\sigma\), and their total masses as \(M_A,M_B>0\). Equation (3) gives \(a+b=1\). The tangent vector equal to \(\sigma\) on the first component and \(-\sigma\) on the second preserves the removed constraint as well, so (1) gives
\[ M_Aa-M_Bb=0. \]
Hence
\[ a=\frac{M_B}{M_A+M_B}, \qquad b=\frac{M_A}{M_A+M_B}, \]
both belonging to \([0,1]\).

For a perturbed loop at \(t\), the derivative equation is \(2d_t=1\). Its remaining component must be free and has absolute derivative \(\tfrac12\).

Thus in every possible case \(\|d\|_\infty\le1\). Integrating over affine pieces, and then changing the edge coordinates one at a time, proves
\[ \|P(w,s)-P(w',s)\|_\infty\le\|w-w'\|_1. \tag{4} \]
Combining (2) and (4) proves the lemma.

3. The allocation rule and its coalition guarantee

For \(w\ne0\), define
\[ W=W(w),\qquad p=P(w,W),\qquad V=\nu_w(\mu),\qquad S=\sum_t\mu_tp_t. \]
Set
\[ A_{H,\mu,\varepsilon}(w)_t=\frac{V}{S}\mu_tp_t, \]
and set \(A_{H,\mu,\varepsilon}(0)=0\).

If \(w\ne0\), some supported edge has positive weight. Positive masses allow a positive matching amount on that edge, including when it is a loop. Therefore \(V>0\). Dual feasibility gives \(S\ge V\), so the formula is well-defined.

Let \(r\) be an optimal dual cover for the grand coalition. It can be chosen with \(0\le r_t\le W\): clipping any coordinate to \(W\) preserves feasibility and cannot increase its linear objective. Thus \(\sum_t\mu_tr_t=V\).

By optimality of \(p\) for the quadratic objective,
\[ WS \le \sum_t\mu_t\left(\frac12p_t^2+Wp_t\right) \le \sum_t\mu_t\left(\frac12r_t^2+Wr_t\right) \le \frac32WV. \]
Since \(W>0\), it follows that
\[ V\le S\le\frac32V, \qquad \frac23\le\frac VS\le1. \tag{5} \]

The allocation is nonnegative and sums exactly to \(V\). For every coalition \(0\le q\le\mu\), weak duality and (5) give
\[ \sum_t\frac{q_t}{\mu_t}A_{H,\mu,\varepsilon}(w)_t = \frac VS\sum_tq_tp_t \ge \frac23\nu_w(q). \]
This includes all proper coalitions. Since \(\tfrac23\ge\tfrac12-\varepsilon\), the allocation is admissible.

At \(w=0\), all coalition values and all allocated payoffs are zero, so admissibility also holds.

4. Uniform Lipschitz bound

Write \(p(w)=P(w,W(w))\) and \(y_t(w)=\mu_tp_t(w)\). The sensitivity lemma and
\[ |W(w)-W(w')|\le\|w-w'\|_1 \]
imply
\[ \|p(w)-p(w')\|_\infty\le2\|w-w'\|_1. \]
Using the normalization \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), we obtain
\[ \|y(w)-y(w')\|_1 \le2\|w-w'\|_1. \tag{6} \]

Also,
\[ |V(w)-V(w')|\le\frac12\|w-w'\|_1. \tag{7} \]
Indeed, summing the grand-coalition capacity constraints gives \(2\sum_ez_e\le1\), including loops. In particular, \(z_e\le\tfrac12\) for every feasible matching vector. Its objective changes by at most \(\tfrac12\|w-w'\|_1\); taking maxima proves (7).

For nonzero \(w,w'\), let \(S=\|y(w)\|_1\), \(S'=\|y(w')\|_1\), \(a=V/S\), and \(a'=V'/S'\). By (5), \(a\le1\). Therefore
\[ \begin{aligned} \|ay-a'y'\|_1 &\le a\|y-y'\|_1+|a-a'|S'\\ &=a\|y-y'\|_1+|a(S'-S)+V-V'|\\ &\le2\|y-y'\|_1+|V-V'|. \end{aligned} \]
Combining this inequality with (6) and (7) yields
\[ \|A_{H,\mu,\varepsilon}(w)-A_{H,\mu,\varepsilon}(w')\|_1 \le\frac92\|w-w'\|_1. \tag{8} \]

If one weight vector is zero, the left-hand side equals the other allocation’s total welfare. Inequality (7) bounds it by \(\tfrac12\|w-w'\|_1\), so (8) still holds.

Finally, \(\varepsilon\le\tfrac12\) implies
\[ \frac92\le\frac{9}{4\varepsilon}. \]
Thus \(C=\tfrac94\) satisfies the requested estimate.

5. Exact computation and bit complexity

The construction uses one linear program to compute \(V\) and one strictly convex quadratic program to compute \(p\). Both have \(\tau\) variables and polynomially many constraints.

The relevant standard theorem is that a convex quadratic program with rational coefficients, a rational polyhedral feasible set, and an attained finite optimum can be solved exactly in polynomial bit complexity. This is the theorem of [Kozlov, Tarasov, and Khachiyan, *The polynomial solvability of convex quadratic programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189).

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • The Hessian is the rational positive definite matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(\mu)\).
  • Every constraint coefficient is rational.
  • The feasible set is nonempty.
  • Coercivity ensures attainment.
  • Strict convexity makes the selected minimizer unique.

For completeness, the minimizer has polynomial rational encoding length. Select linearly independent active constraint normals supporting its first-order optimality condition, as in the sensitivity proof. With their row matrix \(B\), the minimizer and associated multipliers solve the nonsingular rational system
\[ \begin{pmatrix} \operatorname{diag}(\mu)&-B^{\mathsf T}\\ B&0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} p\\ \lambda \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -W\mu\\ b \end{pmatrix}. \]
Nonsingularity follows from positive definiteness of \(\operatorname{diag}(\mu)\) and row independence of \(B\). Clearing denominators and applying determinant bounds shows that all solution coordinates have polynomial encoding length.

Exact rational linear programming computes \(V\) with polynomial encoding length as well. Forming \(S\) and \(V\mu_tp_t/S\) requires only polynomially many exact rational arithmetic operations and preserves polynomial encoding length.

Therefore the rule is deterministic, exactly computable on rational inputs, and runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Together with admissibility and (8), this settles the question.
