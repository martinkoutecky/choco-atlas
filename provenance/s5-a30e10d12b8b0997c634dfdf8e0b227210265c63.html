The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an atomless population with rational role masses \(\alpha_a\le\alpha_b\le\alpha_c\) summing to \(1\), fixed \(K\ge1\), types \(t=(d,j)\) for \(d\in\{a,b,c\}\) and \(j\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\), masses \(\mu_{d,j}=\alpha_d/(K+1)\), ranks \(r_{d,j}=1-\alpha_d/\alpha_c+j\alpha_d/(K\alpha_c)\), and balanced type-level weights, can one construct a measurable partition into coalitions with no positive-mass coalition whose members all strictly prefer it to their assigned coalitions? A type \((d,j)\) member’s utility in coalition \(X\) is \(r_{d,j}-\bar r_d(X)+\sum_{e\ne d}w_e^d\bar r_e(X)\), omitting terms for absent roles, where \(\bar r_e(X)\) is the mass-weighted average rank of role \(e\) in \(X\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=3\) be the number of roles. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For a fixed integer \(K\ge1\), take rational role masses \(0<\alpha_a\le\alpha_b\le\alpha_c\) with \(\alpha_a+\alpha_b+\alpha_c=1\). Types are \(t=(d,j)\), where \(d\in\{a,b,c\}\), \(j\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\), \(\mu_{d,j}=\alpha_d/(K+1)\), and \(r_{d,j}=1-\alpha_d/\alpha_c+j\alpha_d/(K\alpha_c)\); thus \(\tau=3(K+1)\).
Balanced weights are specified by a binary rational \(p\in[0,1]\): \(w_b^a=w_c^b=w_a^c=p\) and \(w_c^a=w_b^c=w_a^b=1-p\). Weights are independent of rank and coalition.
Realize each type as a disjoint interval \(T_{d,j}\) of Lebesgue measure \(\mu_{d,j}\), and write \(\Omega=\bigcup_{d,j}T_{d,j}\). Types may be split arbitrarily among coalitions.
For a measurable coalition \(X\), put \(x_{d,j}=\lambda(X\cap T_{d,j})\) and \(\bar r_d(X)=\sum_jx_{d,j}r_{d,j}/\sum_jx_{d,j}\) when its role mass is positive; otherwise put \(\bar r_d(X)=0\). A member of type \((d,j)\) has utility \(u_{d,j}(X)=r_{d,j}-\bar r_d(X)+\sum_{e\ne d}w_e^d\bar r_e(X)\).
An admissible outcome is a finite measurable partition into positive-mass coalitions, represented by their rational type-mass vectors summing to the society's support masses. A partition \(\pi\) is core stable if no measurable \(X\subseteq\Omega\) with \(\lambda(X)>0\) satisfies \(u_{d,j}(X)>u_{d,j}(\pi(i))\) for almost every \(i\in X\cap T_{d,j}\), for every type. Blocking coalitions may have arbitrary real type masses; coalitions containing only one or two roles are allowed.

Is finding a core stable admissible partition, or correctly reporting that none exists, solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,K\), or is this problem NP-hard?

For finite agents with positive consecutive integer ranks within each role, a common highest rank, and balanced weights, a core stable partition always exists ([Lazarova–Dimitrov](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4336/1/2/159)).
No result settling this exact atomless construction problem was located in the literature search.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. For every society in the stated family, the grand-coalition partition \(\pi^\star=\{\Omega\}\) is core stable.

The grand coalition also appears in the proof of the cited finite-agent theorem. [Lazarova–Dimitrov, Theorem 1](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4336/1/2/159). The proof below establishes stability directly for the atomless model, including blockers with arbitrary real masses and absent roles.

1. Role means in the grand coalition

For each role \(d\in\{a,b,c\}\), define its minimum rank by

\(\ell_d=1-\alpha_d/\alpha_c\).

The ordering of the role masses implies \(0\le\ell_d<1\). The prescribed ranks can be written as

\(r_{d,j}=\ell_d+\dfrac{j}{K}(1-\ell_d)\).

Consequently, every rank in role \(d\) is at least \(\ell_d\). Because all its types have the same mass, its mean rank in the grand coalition is

\(Q_d:=\bar r_d(\Omega)=\dfrac{1}{K+1}\sum_{j=0}^{K}r_{d,j}=\ell_d+\dfrac{1-\ell_d}{K(K+1)}\dfrac{K(K+1)}{2}=\dfrac{1+\ell_d}{2}\).

The weights satisfy, for every role \(d\),

\(w_e^d\ge0\quad(e\ne d),\qquad \sum_{e\ne d}w_e^d=1\).

These properties hold throughout \(p\in[0,1]\), including its endpoints.

2. Every proposed blocker has a role that does not improve

Let \(X\subseteq\Omega\) be any measurable coalition with \(\lambda(X)>0\). Define its set of present roles by

\(S=\left\{d:\sum_{j=0}^{K}\lambda(X\cap T_{d,j})>0\right\}\).

This set is nonempty. For every role, put

\(q_d=\bar r_d(X),\qquad \Delta_d=q_d-Q_d\),

using the prescribed convention \(q_d=0\) for absent roles.

For a present role \(d\in S\), the mean \(q_d\) is a weighted average of ranks that are all at least \(\ell_d\). Hence

\(\Delta_d\ge\ell_d-\dfrac{1+\ell_d}{2}=\dfrac{\ell_d-1}{2}\ge-\dfrac12\).

For an absent role \(d\notin S\), we instead have

\(\Delta_d=-Q_d=-\dfrac{1+\ell_d}{2}\le-\dfrac12\).

Choose \(d^\star\in S\) maximizing \(\Delta_d\) among present roles. The two inequalities above imply that it also maximizes \(\Delta_d\) among all roles: every present-role difference is at least \(-1/2\), and every absent-role difference is at most \(-1/2\). Thus

\(\Delta_e\le\Delta_{d^\star}\qquad\text{for every role }e\).

For any type \((d^\star,j)\), cancellation of its own rank gives the utility difference

\(u_{d^\star,j}(X)-u_{d^\star,j}(\Omega)=-\Delta_{d^\star}+\sum_{e\ne d^\star}w_e^{d^\star}\Delta_e\).

By nonnegativity and normalization of the weights,

\(\sum_{e\ne d^\star}w_e^{d^\star}\Delta_e\le\sum_{e\ne d^\star}w_e^{d^\star}\Delta_{d^\star}=\Delta_{d^\star}\).

Therefore

\(u_{d^\star,j}(X)-u_{d^\star,j}(\Omega)\le0\)

for every type of role \(d^\star\).

Because \(d^\star\in S\), a positive mass of members of \(X\) belongs to that role. Since there are finitely many types, at least one such type has positive mass in \(X\). Its members do not strictly improve, so \(X\) fails the blocking condition.

This applies to every positive-mass measurable \(X\), including coalitions with only one or two roles. Consequently, \(\pi^\star=\{\Omega\}\) is core stable.

3. Construction and bit complexity

Output a partition containing exactly one coalition, represented by the vector

\(\left(\mu_{d,j}\right)_{d\in\{a,b,c\},\,j\in\{0,\ldots,K\}}\).

This coalition is measurable, has total mass \(1\), and its entries are the rational masses supplied in the input. It is therefore an admissible outcome.

In the stated explicit-support representation, construction consists of copying the supported type masses into one coalition vector. It takes \(O(L+\tau)\) time and has output bit length \(O(L+\tau)\), which is polynomial in the requested parameters.

Every promised input has this stable outcome, so the algorithm always returns the grand coalition.
