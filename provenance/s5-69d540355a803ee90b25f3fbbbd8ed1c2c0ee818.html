The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\tau\) additive valuation types with rational shares \(\mu_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), population scale \(N\), and \(m\) goods categories with integral supplies \(b_g\) scaling with \(N\), does every instance admit a sparse rational allocation \(x_{t,z}\ge 0\) of mass \(N\mu_t\) of each type to integral bundles \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^m\), using at most \(b_g\) units of each category, such that whenever \(x_{t,z},x_{s,z'}>0\), \(v_t(z)\ge(1-\varepsilon)v_t(z'-e_g)\) for every \(g\) with \(z'_g>0\), and at most \(O((N/\varepsilon)^{0.67})\) goods are unused? Can such an allocation be found in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\), where \(L\) is the input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) valuation types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational \(\mu_t>0\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
The input also gives a binary integer population scale \(N\geq1\), \(m\geq1\) goods categories with binary integer supplies \(b_g\geq0\), nonnegative rational additive valuation coefficients, and rational \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]\). Supply per unit population is \(b_g/N\), with no restriction on these ratios. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An allocation lists rational masses \(x_{t,z}\geq0\) assigned to integral bundles \(z\in\prod_{g=1}^m\{0,\ldots,b_g\}\), satisfying \(\sum_zx_{t,z}=N\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}z_g\leq b_g\). Sparse means that the list has total binary encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\).
Require \(v_t(z)\geq(1-\varepsilon)v_t(z'-e_g)\) whenever \(x_{t,z},x_{s,z'}>0\) and \(z'_g>0\), including comparisons within one type; \(e_g\) is the unit vector for category \(g\). This includes removal of goods with zero value, and allows equality.
The unused quantity is \(U=\sum_g\bigl(b_g-\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}z_g\bigr)\), which may be fractional.

Does every instance admit a sparse allocation satisfying these conditions with \(U\leq C(N/\varepsilon)^{0.67}\) for some universal constant \(C>0\)?
Is constructing allocations with this guarantee possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For explicitly listed discrete agents and goods, a polynomial-time algorithm achieves \((1-\varepsilon)\)-EFX with \(O((n/\varepsilon)^{0.67})\) unallocated goods ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.08753)). A later discrete result establishes the stronger existence bound \(\widetilde O((n/\varepsilon)^{1/2})\) ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07638)).
No result settling this exact mass-allocation problem was identified in the search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative rational valuation coefficients, allowing zero values and equality in fairness comparisons.
  • Took the population scale N to be a positive binary integer, while allowing individual type masses N mu_t to be nonintegral.
  • Interpreted supplies scaling with N through the supplied ratios b_g/N, without imposing a ratio bound absent from the discrete setting.
  • Restricted every individual integral bundle to the available stock coordinatewise, as in discrete allocation.
  • Defined sparse as polynomial total binary encoding length in m, tau, L, and 1/epsilon.
  • Measured unused goods by the total residual supply, allowing fractional residuals under mass allocation.
  • Interpreted the hidden constant in the unused-goods guarantee as universal and independent of all inputs.

2. External input and rational sparsification

2.1. The discrete theorem used

The external input is the discrete result stated in the question: there is a universal constant $K$ such that, for every finite additive instance with $n$ agents and every $\delta\in(0,1/2]$, a $(1-\delta)$-EFX allocation exists with at most
$$ K(n/\delta)^{67/100} $$
unallocated goods. See [Corollary 1.3 of *Fixed-point cycles and EFX allocations*](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.08753).

We use the convention requiring the inequality after removal of every good. If a formulation assumes positive coefficients, the nonnegative case follows by perturbation: replace every coefficient by $c_{tg}+1/r$, apply the theorem, and let $r$ tend to infinity. For each fixed finite instance there are only finitely many allocations, so one allocation recurs along an infinite subsequence. Its weak fairness inequalities pass to the limit, while its number of unallocated goods remains unchanged.

2.2. Sparsification lemma

Lemma 1. Suppose a fair allocation exists on a finite family of integral bundles whose coordinates have polynomial binary encoding length. Its masses may be real. Then there is a rational fair allocation with at most $\tau+m$ positive entries and no larger unused quantity. Its encoding length is polynomial in the input length and the coordinate encoding length.

Proof. Let $S$ be the positive support of the given allocation. Restrict subsequent allocations to $S$ and maximize
$$ \sum_{(t,z)\in S}|z|_1y_{t,z} $$
subject to
$$ \sum_{z:(t,z)\in S}y_{t,z}=a_t,\qquad \sum_{(t,z)\in S}z_gy_{t,z}\leq b_g,\qquad y_{t,z}\geq0. $$
The original allocation is feasible. Every variable belonging to type $t$ is bounded by $a_t$, so the feasible set is compact and an optimum exists.

Add one nonnegative slack variable per supply constraint. There are $r=\tau+m$ equality rows. Their integer coefficient matrix has full row rank: the slack columns isolate the supply rows, and each type has at least one permitted column because $a_t>0$.

An optimum can be chosen with linearly independent positive columns. Indeed, a dependence among positive columns permits sufficiently small feasible perturbations in both directions. Optimality forces zero objective change along the dependence. Moving to a boundary removes a positive variable while preserving optimality. Repetition establishes the assertion. There are consequently at most $r$ positive variables, including slacks.

Extend these independent columns to a basis. Clear the denominators of the rational right-hand side and apply Cramer’s rule. If the matrix entries have bit length at most $B$, Hadamard’s determinant bound gives polynomial numerator and denominator lengths, bounded by
$$ O\!\left(r(B+L+\log r)\right). $$
This bound is independent of $|S|$.

The new support is a subset of $S$, so every required fairness comparison survives. Maximizing allocated quantity does not increase unused quantity. $\square$

For the original capped problem, this lemma proves that sparsity is automatic whenever the desired allocation exists. It does not provide a method for finding its support.

3. Quantitative bounds for replicated allocations

Temporarily permit bundles exceeding the original caps. Consider any finite-support $(1-\delta)$-EFX allocation satisfying the original population totals and supply inequalities, where $\delta\in(0,1/2]$. Write $\alpha=1-\delta$.

3.1. A uniform bound on a category count

Lemma 2. If $c_{tg}>0$, every supported bundle satisfies
$$ z_g\leq 1+\frac{V_t}{\alpha a_tc_{tg}}. $$

Proof. If $z_g=0$, the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, fairness against this bundle gives, for every supported bundle $u$ assigned to type $t$,
$$ v_t(u)\geq\alpha v_t(z-e_g) \geq\alpha c_{tg}(z_g-1). $$
The comparison remains valid when $u=z$, by nonnegativity.

Multiply by the masses of type $t$ and sum:
$$ \sum_u x_{t,u}v_t(u) \geq \alpha a_tc_{tg}(z_g-1). $$
On the other hand, nonnegative coefficients and the supply inequalities imply
$$ \sum_u x_{t,u}v_t(u) \leq\sum_{s,u}x_{s,u}v_t(u) \leq V_t. $$
Combining the bounds proves the claim. $\square$

In particular, whenever some supported type values category $g$ positively, its count is bounded independently of the replication factor.

3.2. Vanishing mass beyond an original cap

Lemma 3. Suppose $b_g>0$ and a supported type $t$ satisfies
$$ c_{tg}>0,\qquad a_tc_{tg}b_g\geq V_t. $$
Let
$$ M_g=\sum_{s,z:z_g>b_g}x_{s,z} $$
be the total mass assigned a bundle exceeding the original cap in category $g$. Then
$$ M_g\leq\delta\frac{V_t}{c_{tg}}. $$

Proof. If $M_g=0$, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise choose a supported bundle exceeding the cap. Comparing every type-$t$ bundle with that bundle after removal of a category-$g$ good gives
$$ v_t(u)\geq\alpha c_{tg}b_g $$
for every supported type-$t$ bundle $u$.

A type-$t$ bundle that itself exceeds the cap has value at least $c_{tg}(b_g+1)$. Its excess above this common lower bound is therefore at least
$$ c_{tg}(b_g+1)-\alpha c_{tg}b_g =c_{tg}(1+\delta b_g) \geq c_{tg}. $$
A cap-exceeding bundle assigned to another type also has type-$t$ value at least $c_{tg}$.

Thus, summing type-$t$ valuations over the entire allocation,
$$ V_t \geq\sum_{s,u}x_{s,u}v_t(u) \geq a_t\alpha c_{tg}b_g+c_{tg}M_g. $$
Consequently,
$$ c_{tg}M_g \leq V_t-\alpha a_tc_{tg}b_g \leq V_t-\alpha V_t =\delta V_t. $$
Dividing by $c_{tg}>0$ proves the result. $\square$

The non-strict hypothesis is essential to the improvement: equality permits cap-exceeding bundles at finite approximation parameters, but their total mass must vanish as $\delta$ tends to zero.

4. Replication with a vanishing approximation parameter

Call a positive-supply category covered if some supported type values it positively.

Proposition 4. If every positive-supply category is covered, then, with the original bundle caps removed, there exists a complete exact EFX allocation on a fixed finite bundle space. It can be chosen rational and sparse, with polynomial encoding length.

Proof. For each positive-supply category $g$, choose a type $t(g)$ with $c_{t(g)g}>0$, and define
$$ H_g= \left\lfloor 1+\frac{2V_{t(g)}}{a_{t(g)}c_{t(g)g}} \right\rfloor. $$
Set $H_g=0$ when $b_g=0$. These integers have polynomial encoding length: they are obtained by rational arithmetic from the input.

Let $D$ be a positive integer clearing the denominators of all $a_t$. For each positive integer $j$, set
$$ k_j=Dj^4,\qquad \delta_j=\frac1{2j}. $$
Form a discrete instance with $k_ja_t$ agents of type $t$ and $k_jb_g$ copies of category $g$. All these numbers are integers. The discrete theorem applies to its nonnegative additive valuations.

Aggregate each resulting allocation by type and category-count vector, then divide its masses by $k_j$. Denote the resulting allocation by $x^{(j)}$. It has type totals $a_t$ and satisfies the original supply inequalities.

Its unused quantity satisfies
$$ U_j \leq \frac{K}{k_j} \left(\frac{k_jN}{\delta_j}\right)^{67/100} = \frac{K(2DN)^{67/100}}{D}\,j^{-13/20}. $$
Hence $U_j$ tends to zero.

By Lemma 2 and $1-\delta_j\geq1/2$, every supported bundle belongs to the fixed finite set
$$ \mathcal H=\prod_g\{0,\ldots,H_g\}. $$
Each coordinate $x^{(j)}_{t,z}$ lies in $[0,a_t]$. The sequence therefore has a convergent subsequence in this finite-dimensional compact box. Write its limit as $x^*$.

Population totals and supply inequalities pass to the limit. Since $U_j$ tends to zero, $x^*$ uses every supply exactly.

To verify exact EFX, suppose $x^*_{t,z}>0$ and $x^*_{s,z'}>0$. Along the convergent subsequence, both entries are positive eventually. For every $g$ with $z'_g>0$, the discrete inequalities then give
$$ v_t(z)\geq(1-\delta_j)v_t(z'-e_g). $$
Letting $j$ tend to infinity yields
$$ v_t(z)\geq v_t(z'-e_g). $$
This includes zero-valued removals.

Finally apply Lemma 1. The coordinate bounds $H_g$ have polynomial encoding length, so the resulting complete exact EFX allocation is rational and sparse. $\square$

Both the uniform bundle bound and the vanishing charity are needed here. Without a fixed finite bundle space, convergence of masses alone would not establish that all supplies survive the limit.

5. A stronger sufficient condition for the original problem

Theorem 5. Suppose that for every category $g$ with $b_g>0$, some supported type $t$ satisfies
$$ c_{tg}>0,\qquad a_tc_{tg}b_g\geq v_t(b). $$
Then the original capped instance admits a sparse, rational, complete exact EFX allocation. In particular, it satisfies the requested fairness condition with $U=0$ for every permitted $\varepsilon$.

Proof. Use the replicated allocations from Proposition 4, choosing the witnessing type from the hypothesis for each category.

Lemma 2 gives the uniform bound
$$ z_g \leq 1+\frac{V_t}{(1-\delta_j)a_tc_{tg}} \leq 1+2b_g. $$
Thus all allocations lie in a fixed finite bundle space.

Lemma 3 gives
$$ \sum_{s,z:z_g>b_g}x^{(j)}_{s,z} \leq \delta_j\frac{V_t}{c_{tg}} \longrightarrow0. $$
Every entry exceeding the original category-$g$ cap therefore has zero limiting mass. Categories of zero supply already have zero counts in every replicated allocation.

The limiting allocation consequently respects every original cap. Proposition 4’s argument proves that it is complete and exact EFX. Lemma 1 makes it rational and sparse. $\square$

This strengthens the earlier condition
$$ v_t(b)<(1-\varepsilon)a_tc_{tg}b_g $$
in two ways: the factor $1-\varepsilon$ disappears, and equality is allowed. The resulting conclusion is exact EFX.

For example, take $m\geq2$, $N=m+1$, equal supplies $b_g=B$, and three supported types with populations
$$ a_1=m,\qquad a_2=a_3=\frac12. $$

$$ a_1c_{1g}b_g=mB=v_1(b). $$
Theorem 5 applies, although the type populations are nonintegral and the previous strict condition fails for every positive approximation parameter. When $B=2$, the common-floor construction also fails its stated sufficient condition, and total supply exceeds population.

6. Universally zero-valued categories in the uncapped relaxation

The covering assumption in Proposition 4 can be removed for the uncapped relaxation.

Theorem 6. If the constraints $z_g\leq b_g$ are dropped, every instance admits a sparse, rational, complete exact EFX allocation with polynomial encoding length.

Proof. First omit positive-supply categories valued at zero by every supported type. Apply Proposition 4 to the remaining categories. If there are none, start with the empty bundle assigned to every type.

Let $F$ be the finite set of distinct bundles in the resulting rational allocation. Write
$$ \lambda_z=\sum_t x_{t,z},\qquad \ell_t=\min_{z:x_{t,z}>0}v_t(z). $$
Exact EFX implies that, for every $w\in F$ and every remaining category $g$ with $w_g>0$,
$$ \ell_t\geq v_t(w-e_g). $$

Redistribute the existing bundle frequencies by solving the transportation linear program with constraints
$$ \sum_z y_{t,z}=a_t,\qquad \sum_t y_{t,z}=\lambda_z, $$
allowing $(t,z)$ only when $v_t(z)\geq\ell_t$, and maximizing
$$ \sum_{t,z}y_{t,z}v_t(z). $$
The original allocation is feasible. A rational optimum exists with polynomial encoding length. Every feasible redistribution remains exact EFX, by the displayed bound involving $\ell_t$.

Construct a directed graph on its positive type–bundle entries. Put an edge from $(t,z)$ to $(s,w)$ precisely when
$$ v_t(z)<v_t(w). $$
This graph has no directed cycle. Indeed, along a cycle one could transfer a sufficiently small equal mass so that each participating type receives the next bundle instead of its present bundle. Type totals and bundle frequencies would remain unchanged. Every new assignment would be permitted because its utility strictly increases. The objective would strictly increase, contradicting optimality.

A finite acyclic directed graph has a vertex with no incoming edge. Choose such a positive entry $(t_0,z_0)$, of mass $\lambda>0$. The absence of incoming edges means
$$ v_t(u)\geq v_t(z_0) $$
for every positively supported entry $(t,u)$.

For each omitted category $g$, write
$$ \frac{b_g}{\lambda}=q_g+r_g,\qquad q_g=\left\lfloor\frac{b_g}{\lambda}\right\rfloor,\qquad 0\leq r_g<1. $$
Split the mass $\lambda$ into at most $m+1$ pieces by partitioning $[0,1)$ at the distinct values $r_g$. On the piece corresponding to parameter $u$, append
$$ q_g+\mathbf1\{u<r_g\} $$
goods of omitted category $g$ to $z_0$.

The appended category-$g$ consumption is exactly
$$ \lambda(q_g+r_g)=b_g. $$
All valuations remain unchanged. Removing an appended zero-valued good leaves target value $v_t(z_0)$, which is at most every supported type-$t$ recipient’s own value. Removing an original good remains covered by the previous exact EFX inequalities. Thus the enlarged allocation is complete and exact EFX.

The rational mass $\lambda$ has polynomial encoding length, so the integers $q_g$, the breakpoints, and the new masses do as well. Applying Lemma 1 once more gives at most $\tau+m$ positive entries. $\square$

The appended counts may exceed $b_g$ when $\lambda<1$. Thus this theorem does not remove the cap obstruction in the original question.

Here is a concrete obstruction to that particular extension procedure.

Take $N=3$, two types with populations
$$ a_1=\frac65,\qquad a_2=\frac95, $$
valuations
$$ v_1(z)=z_1,\qquad v_2(z)=z_2, $$
and supplies $(1,5)$. The following capped allocation is complete and exact EFX:

| Type | Bundle | Mass |
|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $(0,0)$ | $1/5$ |
| $1$ | $(1,0)$ | $1$ |
| $2$ | $(0,2)$ | $2/5$ |
| $2$ | $(0,3)$ | $7/5$ |

The type totals are correct, and consumption is
$$ 1,\qquad 2\cdot\frac25+3\cdot\frac75=5. $$
For type $1$, every positively valued target is a singleton. For type $2$, removal from the larger valued bundle leaves value $2$, equal to its minimum supported own value. These observations verify every exact EFX comparison.

Now add a universally zero-valued category with supply and cap $B$. If the existing type assignments and first two bundle coordinates are retained, an entry receiving this category must be unenvied before its addition: removing the zero-valued good leaves its old value.

Only $(0,0)$ and $(0,2)$ have this property. Their total mass is
$$ \frac15+\frac25=\frac35. $$
Consequently, the cap $B$ permits allocation of at most $3B/5$ units of the new category, leaving at least $2B/5$ unused.

This is not a counterexample to the task: the first two categories may be rearranged. It proves that extending a previously chosen fair support can leave arbitrarily many goods unused, so that procedure cannot establish the universal bound.

Theorem 5 establishes the requested guarantee, with $U=0$, under a checkable weak valuation condition. Theorem 6 shows that arbitrary rational masses themselves do not prevent complete exact EFX in the uncapped relaxation.

The general capped case remains unsettled. In particular, the arguments do not eliminate cap violations when Theorem 5’s witnesses are unavailable.

No polynomial-time construction in the required succinct model follows from replication and compactness. The replication factors grow without bound, and sparsification requires a compatible support to be available.

For completeness, the decision problem with a rational unused-quantity threshold belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$: Lemma 1 provides a polynomial-size certificate, and all population, supply, unused-quantity, and fairness conditions can be checked by exact rational arithmetic.
