Result

Both the unrestricted support-encoded divisible problem and the problem under the displayed restriction belong to \(\mathrm{P}\).

The unrestricted problem admits an exact rational linear-programming formulation with running time \(\operatorname{poly}(m\tau+mH+HK,L)\). In particular, its dependence on the binary-encoded scale \(N\) is through its bit length, not its numerical value.

Because the restriction uses all ordered department pairs, it forces equal travel times and a common linear waiting cost. I prove polynomial-time solvability for arbitrary travel times and waiting functions first, then resolve that restricted case explicitly.

1. Exact linear-programming formulation

Compute every eligibility set \(E_a\) using the supplied treatment and travel data. If any \(E_a\) is empty, answer no. Otherwise, let

\(r=\sum_{a\in A}|E_a|\le m\tau,\qquad c_{a,s}=d(u_a,v_s)-\gamma q_{s,p_a}.\)

Introduce continuous variables \(g_{a,s}\ge0\) for eligible pairs, together with \(y_{s,t}\ge0\) and \(z_{s,t}\ge0\) for every department and slot. The interpretation is \(g_{a,s}=Nx_{a,s}\); these variables have no integrality requirement.

Consider the following rational linear feasibility system:

\( \sum_{s\in E_a}g_{a,s}=N\mu_a \quad(a\in A), \)

\( y_{s,t}=\sum_{a:t_a=t,\ s\in E_a}g_{a,s}\quad(s\in S,\ 1\le t\le H), \)

\( z_{s,t}\ge a_h^s\bigl(y_{s,t}+\alpha_t y_{s,t-1}\bigr)+b_h^s \quad(s\in S,\ 1\le t\le H,\ 1\le h\le k_s), \)

\( \sum_{a\in A}\sum_{s\in E_a}c_{a,s}g_{a,s} +\sum_{s\in S}\sum_{t=1}^H z_{s,t}\le NB. \)

Here \(y_{s,0}=\bar\ell_s\) is a fixed constant.

Proof of equivalence

Suppose a feasible assignment \(x\) has objective at most \(B\). Set \(g_{a,s}=Nx_{a,s}\), define \(y\) as in the question, and set

\(z_{s,t}=w_s(y_{s,t}+\alpha_t y_{s,t-1}).\)

The assignment and arrival equalities hold. Each epigraph inequality holds because \(w_s\) is the maximum of its listed affine functions. Also, \(z_{s,t}\ge0\): workloads are nonnegative, and \(w_s\) is nondecreasing with \(w_s(0)=0\). Multiplying the original objective inequality by \(N>0\) gives the final linear inequality.

Conversely, suppose the linear system is feasible. Define \(x_{a,s}=g_{a,s}/N\) on eligible pairs and zero elsewhere. Its assignment equalities give \(\sum_{s\in E_a}x_{a,s}=\mu_a\), and its arrival variables agree with those required by the question. The epigraph inequalities imply

\(z_{s,t}\ge\max_h\{a_h^s(y_{s,t}+\alpha_t y_{s,t-1})+b_h^s\} =w_s(\ell_{s,t}).\)

Consequently, the original objective is at most

\(\displaystyle \frac1N\left(\sum_{a,s\in E_a}c_{a,s}g_{a,s}+\sum_{s,t}z_{s,t}\right)\le B.\)

Thus the linear system is feasible exactly when the answer is yes.

Moreover, when all eligibility sets are nonempty, the assignment domain is a nonempty compact product of simplices. The objective is continuous, so its minimum is attained.

2. Size and exact running time

The formulation has:

  • \(r+2mH\) variables;
  • \(\tau+mH\) equalities;
  • \(HK\) epigraph inequalities;
  • one budget inequality, together with nonnegativity constraints.

The number of nonzero coefficients is \(O(r+\tau+mH+HK)\). For example, each assignment variable appears in one mass equality, one arrival equality, and the budget inequality.

Every constructed rational coefficient has bit length \(O(L)\). This includes \(N\mu_a\), \(NB\), \(d(u_a,v_s)-\gamma q_{s,p_a}\), and the products involving slopes, carry-over factors, and initial counts. Each uses only a bounded number of arithmetic operations on input rationals.

Let \(D=m\tau+\tau+mH+HK+1\). A sparse encoding therefore has length \(O(DL)\); even a dense encoding has length \(O(D^2L)\).

The standard exact rational linear-programming feasibility theorem gives a deterministic algorithm polynomial in the encoded system length. Its hypotheses hold here: there are finitely many linear constraints, and every coefficient is an explicitly computable rational. Hence the decision problem takes

\(\operatorname{poly}(m\tau+mH+HK,L)\)

bit operations. Because the support, waiting functions, and slot factors are explicitly listed, this is polynomial in the total input length.

There is no expansion into \(N\) requests, and \(N\mu_a\) need not be an integer. This establishes polynomial bit complexity; it does not assert a strongly polynomial algorithm for the unrestricted problem.

3. What the displayed restriction implies

Define

\(D_{\mathrm{tr}}=\min_{a,\ s\ne\tilde s}\{d(u_a,v_s)-d(u_a,v_{\tilde s})\},\)

\(R_{\mathrm{sl}}=\max_{s\ne\tilde s}\{a_{k_s}^s-a_1^{\tilde s}\}.\)

For every travel difference, its negative also occurs because both ordered pairs occur. Therefore \(D_{\mathrm{tr}}\le0\).

Write \(l_s=a_1^s\) and \(u_s=a_{k_s}^s\). For any distinct departments \(s,\tilde s\),

\((u_s-l_{\tilde s})+(u_{\tilde s}-l_s) =(u_s-l_s)+(u_{\tilde s}-l_{\tilde s})\ge0.\)

At least one of the two terms on the left is nonnegative, so \(R_{\mathrm{sl}}\ge0\). The displayed restriction consequently yields

\(0\ge D_{\mathrm{tr}}\ge R_{\mathrm{sl}}\ge0.\)

Both extrema must equal zero.

Every travel difference is now nonnegative, as is its negative. Thus, for every supported type \(a\),

\(d(u_a,v_s)=d(u_a,v_{\tilde s})\quad\text{for all departments }s,\tilde s.\)

Also, for distinct departments,

\(u_s\le l_{\tilde s}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{\tilde s}\le l_s.\)

Combining these with \(l_s\le u_s\) and \(l_{\tilde s}\le u_{\tilde s}\) gives

\(l_s\le u_s\le l_{\tilde s}\le u_{\tilde s}\le l_s.\)

All four quantities are equal. Since \(m\ge2\), every department has a distinct partner. Therefore each department has zero slope range, and all departments share one slope \(\beta\ge0\). The stipulated strict ordering of listed slopes forces \(k_s=1\). Finally, \(w_s(0)=0\) forces its sole intercept to vanish. Hence

\(w_s(n)=\beta n\quad\text{for every department and every }n\ge0.\)

Conversely, equal travel times for each supported type and these common linear waiting functions make both extrema zero, so they satisfy the restriction.

4. Explicit optimum under the restriction

Assume all eligibility sets are nonempty. Define

\(M_t=\sum_{a:t_a=t}\mu_a,\qquad C_0=\sum_s\bar\ell_s,\)

and let \(\delta_a\) denote the common travel time for type \(a\). For every feasible assignment,

\(\sum_s y_{s,t}=NM_t,\qquad \sum_{t=1}^H M_t=1.\)

The total waiting contribution is therefore independent of the assignment:

\(\displaystyle \frac1N\sum_{s,t}w_s(\ell_{s,t}) =\beta\left(1+\sum_{t=1}^{H-1}\alpha_{t+1}M_t+\frac{\alpha_1C_0}{N}\right). \)

Indeed, the new arrivals contribute \(N\); initial carry-over contributes \(\alpha_1C_0\); and arrivals in slot \(t<H\) contribute an additional \(N\alpha_{t+1}M_t\) in the next slot.

Let \(Q_a=\max_{s\in E_a}q_{s,p_a}\). Because \(\gamma\ge0\), assigning all mass of type \(a\) to a department attaining \(Q_a\) minimizes that type’s linear cost. There are no further assignment constraints, so these choices can be made independently.

The exact optimum is consequently

\(\displaystyle V^*=\sum_{a\in A}\mu_a(\delta_a-\gamma Q_a) +\beta\left(1+\sum_{t=1}^{H-1}\alpha_{t+1}M_t+\frac{\alpha_1C_0}{N}\right). \)

For \(H=1\), the intermediate sum is empty. The answer is yes exactly when \(V^*\le B\).

After indexing the supplied data, checking the restriction, computing eligibility and quality maxima, and evaluating this formula require \(O(m\tau+K+H)\) rational arithmetic/comparison operations. Intermediate reduced rationals have \(O(L)\) bits: a common denominator can be taken as \(N\) times the product of all input rational denominators, and every expanded summand involves only a bounded number of input factors.

Including preprocessing and schoolbook exact rational arithmetic, a conservative bit-operation bound is

\(O\bigl((m\tau+K+H+L)L^2\bigr).\)

The individual-request flow formulations discussed in [Leo et al., Section 3.3](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/handle/11585/516410/e1dcb339-bf5e-7715-e053-1705fe0a6cc9/PP_Emergency%20Department%20Management%20in%20Lazio.pdf) provide context. The proof here establishes the support-encoded divisible result directly, including arbitrary rational carry-over factors.
