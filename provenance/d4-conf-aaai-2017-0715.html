The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an ordered sequence of \(\tau\) cohorts with binary-rational masses \(\mu_i\) summing to \(1\), each cohort consists of individually strategic members sharing a complete reservation type \(\theta_i=(a_i,d_i,t_i,c_i,v_i)\), including its arrival stage; future cohorts are hidden. With normalized capacity \(B\), request densities \(\rho_i=v_i/(c_it_i)\in[1,k]\), and lengths \(t_i\in[1,T]\), each member must receive either a complete reservation or none. What is the supremal worst-case expected social-welfare competitive ratio achievable by a randomized, individually truthful online policy that chooses accepted mass and feasible start times as each cohort arrives, compared with the offline optimum for the same cohort sequence?

A society is given by its support: an ordered list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_i\), with \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There is one reusable resource kind, so \(m=1\); running-time parameters are \(m,\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), and \(B,k,T\).
Each type comprises an exogenous submission time \(s_i\) and reservation data \(\theta_i=(a_i,d_i,t_i,c_i,v_i)\). All numerical data are binary rationals, \(B>0\), \(k,T\geq1\), \(s_i\leq a_i\), \(a_i+t_i\leq d_i\), \(1\leq t_i\leq T\), \(0<c_i\leq B\), and \(1\leq v_i/(c_it_i)\leq k\). Submission times are nondecreasing; equal-time cohorts arrive in the given order.
Each cohort is atomless and its members act individually. Serving a member means committing to an uninterrupted reservation of intensity \(c_i\) for length \(t_i\), starting in \([a_i,d_i-t_i]\); partial service earns no value. Serving mass \(x\) contributes welfare \(xv_i\) and resource load \(xc_i\) while its reservations run.
A realized allocation may distribute accepted members across start times: let \(q_i\) be their start-time mass measure, supported on \([a_i,d_i-t_i]\), with \(x_i=q_i(\mathbb R)\leq\mu_i\). Feasibility requires \(\sum_i c_iq_i((u-t_i,u])\leq B\) at every time \(u\). Welfare is \(\sum_i v_ix_i\); \(\operatorname{OPT}(I)\) is the supremum feasible welfare with the entire sequence known.
An online mechanism knows \(B,k,T\) and total mass \(1\), but learns each cohort only when it arrives. It immediately and irrevocably assigns acceptance, start times, and payments, with feasibility in every realization.
Rejected members pay nothing and have utility zero; accepted members pay \(p\geq0\) and have utility \(v_i-p\). Require individual rationality and universal individual truthfulness: for every fixed random seed and other members’ reports, truthful reporting maximizes each member’s utility. Admissible reports satisfy \(\hat a\geq a\), \(\hat d\leq d\), \(\hat t\geq t\), \(\hat c\geq c\), and otherwise the stated type domain; value may be misreported. Submission time is not strategic. Individual deviations must have defined outcomes even though they have zero mass.

What is the supremal competitive ratio \(R^*(B,k,T)=\sup_M\inf_I \mathbb E[W_M(I)]/\operatorname{OPT}(I)\) over these randomized truthful online mechanisms, where the infimum ranges over all finite admissible sequences fixed independently of the mechanism’s randomness?

For discrete reservations, randomized mechanisms have the published welfare upper bound \(2/\log_2(8kT)\); truthful randomized mechanisms attain \(1/(42\lceil\log_2 k\rceil\lceil\log_2 T\rceil)\) when \(k,T>1\). [Source](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/10596/10455).

Exact answer under the stated normalization

The normalization makes every instance uncongested: total mass is \(1\), and every member’s intensity is at most \(B\). Consequently, \(R^*(B,k,T)=1\) for every admissible \(B,k,T\).

I interpret the target as the explicitly specified atomless model. There is no capacity-constrained case within these premises; such a case would require changing a hypothesis.

Feasibility and the offline optimum

For any start-time measures satisfying \(q_i(\mathbb R)\leq\mu_i\), and every time \(u\),
\[ \sum_i c_iq_i((u-t_i,u]) \leq \sum_i c_iq_i(\mathbb R) \leq \sum_i c_i\mu_i \leq B\sum_i\mu_i =B. \]
Thus the capacity constraint holds regardless of how accepted members are distributed across their admissible start times.

In particular, let \(\delta_{a_i}\) denote unit point mass at \(a_i\), and choose \(q_i=\mu_i\delta_{a_i}\). This serves every member of type \(i\) starting at \(a_i\). The start is admissible because \(a_i+t_i\leq d_i\), and the preceding inequality proves feasibility.

Every allocation has welfare at most \(\sum_i\mu_iv_i\), since \(v_i>0\) and its accepted masses satisfy \(x_i\leq\mu_i\). The full-service allocation attains this bound. Therefore
\[ \operatorname{OPT}(I)=\sum_i\mu_iv_i>0. \]

A universally truthful online mechanism

Upon receiving any admissible report \((\hat a,\hat d,\hat t,\hat c,\hat v)\), accept the member, assign start time \(\hat a\), reserve intensity \(\hat c\) for duration \(\hat t\), and charge zero.

This decision is immediate and irrevocable. Since submission time is nonstrategic and precedes the reported start, the reservation never starts before submission.

Feasibility holds even when members submit different admissible reports. Indeed, every reported intensity is at most \(B\), so the aggregate load at any time is at most \(B\) times the society’s total mass, namely \(B\).

The rule also defines an outcome for every zero-mass individual deviation: that individual receives the reported reservation for free. Changing one individual’s report does not change any aggregate load.

To verify truthfulness, fix a member’s true type and all other reports. Truthful reporting provides complete service and utility \(v\). An admissible deviation also provides complete service for the true requirements: its assigned start \(\hat a\) satisfies
\[ \hat a\geq a,\qquad \hat a+t\leq\hat a+\hat t\leq\hat d\leq d, \]
and its reserved intensity satisfies \(\hat c\geq c\). Its payment remains zero, so its utility remains \(v\). Value misreports likewise change neither allocation nor payment. Hence truthful reporting maximizes utility, and individual rationality holds. The mechanism is deterministic, so this proves universal individual truthfulness.

Competitive ratio and scope

On every instance, the mechanism obtains welfare \(\sum_i\mu_iv_i=\operatorname{OPT}(I)\), establishing a competitive ratio of \(1\).

Conversely, every realization of any permitted mechanism is feasible and therefore has welfare at most \(\operatorname{OPT}(I)\). Taking expectations proves that no mechanism can have a competitive ratio exceeding \(1\).

The quoted discrete bounds concern a different capacity model, where each accepted request consumes its stated intensity without multiplication by population mass. [The cited paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/10596/10455) therefore does not contradict this conclusion. Here, the combined assumptions \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\) and \(c_i\leq B\) eliminate resource competition entirely.
