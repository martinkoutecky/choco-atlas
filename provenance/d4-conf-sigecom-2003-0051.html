The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) item categories, \(\tau\) types with known MAX-SUM-ALL read-once valuations \(v_t\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and rational per-person supplies \(b_i\), compute the exact maximum welfare and a maximizing allocation. Each person receives an integral bundle \(S\subseteq[n]\); the allocation assigns mass \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) of type \(t\) to bundle \(S\), with \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:i\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_i\). The objective is to maximize \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), with complexity measured in \(n\), \(\tau\), the formula sizes, and input bit length \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge1\) item categories. A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types.
Each type has an explicitly given read-once valuation tree: each category labels at most one leaf, which returns its positive binary-rational weight when present in a bundle and zero otherwise. Internal gates have at least two children: SUM adds their outputs, MAX takes their maximum, and ALL adds their outputs if every output is positive and returns zero otherwise. The root defines \(v_t(S)\) for \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), including the empty bundle.
Supplies \(b_i\ge0\) are binary rationals. A feasible allocation assigns masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) with \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:i\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_i\). Each person receives an integral bundle; supplies may remain unused. Write \(W(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_x W(x)\). Let \(F\) be the total formula size and \(L\) the total input bit length.

Is finding a feasible allocation with \(W(x)>\mathrm{OPT}/2\) whenever \(\mathrm{OPT}>0\) solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,F,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For two discrete agents with these valuations, obtaining welfare strictly greater than \(\mathrm{OPT}/2\) is NP-hard, while welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/2\) is attainable in polynomial time. [Zinkevich, Blum, and Sandholm](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/www/polytime_preference_elicitation.acmec03.pdf)
No result settling this precise mass-allocation approximation question was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The unspecified numerical encoding of valuation weights is fixed to positive binary rationals, preserving the positive-weight valuation domain.
  • Read-once means each category appears at most once per valuation; categories may be omitted. Unary gates are suppressed, and internal gates have at least two children.
  • Per-person supplies are nonnegative binary rationals, with no upper bound; unused supply and empty bundles are allowed.
  • The strict approximation guarantee applies only when the optimum is positive; when it is zero, any feasible allocation is acceptable.
  • The number of item categories is denoted by m instead of n to match the required running-time convention.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, an exactly optimal allocation can be computed, with polynomially many supported bundles and rational masses of polynomial encoding length.

The proof constructs a polynomial-size linear program and explicitly converts any feasible solution into an allocation of integral bundles.

1. Removing items that do not contribute to value

Fix a type and its valuation tree. For a node \(u\), let \(v_u\) be the valuation defined by the subtree rooted at \(u\).

Lemma 1. Every bundle \(S\) has a subbundle \(S'\subseteq S\) with the same root valuation and the following structure:

  • A subtree whose retained valuation is zero contains no retained items.
  • At an active SUM node, a nonempty collection of children is active.
  • At an active MAX node, exactly one child is active.
  • At an active ALL node, every child is active.

Here, “active” means that the retained subtree valuation is positive.

Proof. Proceed recursively. If \(v_u(S)=0\), discard every item below \(u\). Otherwise:

  • At a leaf, retain its category.
  • At a SUM node, recursively retain the positive-valued children and discard the others.
  • At a MAX node, choose a child attaining the positive maximum, recursively retain its items, and discard every other child’s items.
  • At an ALL node, every child has positive value; recursively retain all of them.

Children have disjoint category sets because the tree is read-once. Consequently, these operations preserve the value at \(u\) and retain only items originally present. Applying the construction at the root proves the claim. \(\square\)

Call the resulting bundles pruned bundles. For such a bundle, its value equals the sum of the weights of its retained leaves: SUM and active ALL nodes add their children’s values, while an active MAX node has only one active child.

Replacing every allocated bundle by its pruned subbundle preserves welfare and weakly decreases every category’s consumption. Thus an optimal allocation can be assumed to use pruned bundles.

2. A polynomial-size linear program

For each type \(t\) and each node \(u\) of its tree, introduce a variable \(z_{t,u}\), representing the mass for which that node is active. Let \(r_t\) denote the root, \(C(u)\) the children of \(u\), and \(w_{t,\ell}\) the weight of leaf \(\ell\).

Impose nonnegativity \(z_{t,u}\ge 0\), root bounds \(z_{t,r_t}\le\mu_t\), and these gate constraints:

| Gate at \(u\) | Constraints |
|---|---|
| SUM | \(z_{t,c}\le z_{t,u}\) for every \(c\in C(u)\), and \(z_{t,u}\le\sum_{c\in C(u)}z_{t,c}\) |
| MAX | \(\sum_{c\in C(u)}z_{t,c}=z_{t,u}\) |
| ALL | \(z_{t,c}=z_{t,u}\) for every \(c\in C(u)\) |

For category \(i\), impose the supply constraint
\(\sum_{t,\ell:\,\ell\text{ labels }i}z_{t,\ell}\le b_i\).

Maximize the linear objective
\(Q(z)=\sum_{t,\ell\text{ a leaf}}w_{t,\ell}z_{t,\ell}\).

The program has \(O(F)\) variables and \(O(F+m+\tau)\) constraints, with polynomial input encoding length. It is feasible at \(z=0\). Every child variable is at most its parent variable, so every variable for type \(t\) is at most \(\mu_t\); hence the feasible region is bounded.

Every allocation of pruned bundles induces a feasible LP solution by taking activation masses. In particular, the SUM lower bound follows because every active SUM node has at least one active child. Its objective equals the allocation’s welfare. Therefore the LP optimum is at least \(\mathrm{OPT}\).

It remains to prove the converse, including efficient reconstruction.

3. Realizing activation masses by integral bundles

Lemma 2. Every rational feasible LP solution \(z\) can be converted, in polynomial time, into an allocation with welfare \(Q(z)\).

Proof. Treat each type separately and suppress the type subscript. Represent its mass by the interval \(I=[0,\mu)\). We construct, for every node \(u\), a set \(A_u\subseteq I\) of measure \(z_u\). These sets will specify which people activate that node.

Set \(A_r=[0,z_r)\). Construct children’s sets from their parent’s set, proceeding from the root downward.

Suppose \(A_u\) has measure \(a=z_u\). If \(a=0\), every child variable is zero, and we assign empty sets to all children. Assume henceforth that \(a>0\).

Because \(A_u\) is a finite union of half-open intervals, traversing those intervals from left to right gives a measure-preserving bijection \(\phi_u:[0,a)\to A_u\). We specify child sets in this measure coordinate and then apply \(\phi_u\).

ALL. Set \(A_c=A_u\) for every child. The equalities \(z_c=a\) give the required measures.

MAX. Order the children as \(c_1,\ldots,c_k\), and put \(s_0=0\) and \(s_j=\sum_{h=1}^jz_{c_h}\). Since \(s_k=a\), the intervals \([s_{j-1},s_j)\) partition \([0,a)\). Define
\(A_{c_j}=\phi_u([s_{j-1},s_j))\).

Thus the children partition the parent’s active mass, and each has its prescribed measure.

SUM. Use the same cumulative sums \(s_j\). Here \(0\le z_{c_j}\le a\) and \(s_k\ge a\). Let \(B_j\subseteq[0,a)\) be the image of \([s_{j-1},s_j)\) under reduction modulo \(a\), and define \(A_{c_j}=\phi_u(B_j)\).

An interval of length \(z_{c_j}\le a\) has modulo-\(a\) image of measure exactly \(z_{c_j}\). Moreover, the consecutive intervals together form \([0,s_k)\), which contains \([0,a)\). Their modulo-\(a\) images therefore cover \([0,a)\). Consequently, the children have their prescribed measures and cover \(A_u\).

This construction gives the following pointwise properties:

  • Every child’s active set lies inside its parent’s.
  • At an active SUM node, at least one child is active.
  • At an active MAX node, exactly one child is active.
  • At an active ALL node, all children are active.

For a person at position \(q\in I\), assign precisely the categories of leaves \(\ell\) satisfying \(q\in A_\ell\).

An inactive node has no active descendant, so its subtree value is zero. An active leaf has positive weight. Induction upward now shows that every active internal node has positive value and that its value equals the sum of the weights of its active descendant leaves. In particular, each person receives an integral pruned bundle with its intended value.

Leaf \(\ell\) is assigned to mass exactly \(z_\ell\). Thus, after performing this construction for every type, category consumption is exactly the left-hand side of its LP supply constraint, and total welfare is \(Q(z)\). The entire interval of each type is allocated, including the people receiving the empty bundle.

Polynomial reconstruction and output size

Each constructed set is a finite union of intervals. At a MAX or SUM gate, a child’s set in the parent’s measure coordinate has at most two interior boundary points. Pulling it back through \(\phi_u\) introduces at most two new endpoints in the original person interval; all other endpoints are inherited from the parent’s set.

Thus a tree with \(N\) nodes introduces only \(O(N)\) distinct endpoints. Partitioning \(I\) at all these endpoints produces \(O(N)\) intervals on each of which every leaf’s activity is constant. Output one bundle and its interval length for each nonempty interval. This gives \(O(N)\) supported bundles for that type.

For bit complexity, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of \(\mu\) and the values \(z_u\). Its bit length is at most the sum of their encoding lengths. Every endpoint constructed above is an integer multiple of \(1/D\): the operations use sums, differences, and reduction modulo a quantity that is itself such a multiple. Pullback through \(\phi_u\) uses only translations of interval coordinates.

All endpoints lie in \([0,\mu]\subseteq[0,1]\), so endpoints and output masses have encoding length polynomial in that of \(z\). Interval manipulation and extraction of the bundles therefore take polynomial time. \(\square\)

4. Exact optimization and the strict guarantee

Lemma 1 shows that the LP optimum is at least \(\mathrm{OPT}\). Lemma 2 converts an optimal LP solution into a feasible allocation with the same objective, proving that the LP optimum is also at most \(\mathrm{OPT}\). Hence they are equal.

Polynomial-time rational linear programming applies because the program is explicitly given, feasible, bounded, and has polynomial encoding length. It produces an optimal rational solution of polynomial encoding length. Lemma 2 then produces an explicit optimal allocation in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,F,L\).

Therefore the returned allocation satisfies \(W(x)=\mathrm{OPT}\), and whenever \(\mathrm{OPT}>0\), it satisfies \(W(x)>\mathrm{OPT}/2\).

5. Why discrete-agent hardness does not transfer

The [cited discrete-agent result](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/www/polytime_preference_elicitation.acmec03.pdf) requires choosing a single bundle for each agent. Here each supported type may split its mass among multiple bundles, and supply constraints restrict their aggregate consumption. The reconstruction above uses precisely this permitted splitting while keeping every person’s bundle integral.

The number of supported types enters only through the explicitly supplied trees and root bounds. No enumeration of unsupported types is needed.
