The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over customer types, where each type has a finite menu \(D_t\) of power demands and valuations \(v_t(d)\), with demands in the positive quadrant and angles at most \(\pi/2-\delta\), choose masses \(x_{t,d}\ge 0\) maximizing \(\sum_t\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}v_t(d)\), subject to \(\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}=\mu_t\) for every type and \(\left|\sum_t\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}d\right|\le c\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The parameter \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has an explicitly listed finite menu \(D_t\) containing \(0\), with \(v_t(0)=0\). Capacity \(c>0\), demand coordinates, and nonnegative valuations are binary rationals. Fix \(0<\delta<\pi/2\).
Every nonzero demand \(d\) has nonnegative coordinates, \(\lVert d\rVert_2\le c\), and \(\arg(d)\in[0,\pi/2-\delta]\). Valuations are monotone on each menu under coordinatewise magnitude comparison between demands having the same coordinate signs, with \(0\) below every demand.
An allocation consists of real masses \(x_{t,d}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}=\mu_t\) for every type and \(\lVert\sum_t\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}d\rVert_2\le c\). Its welfare is \(\sum_t\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}v_t(d)\); let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the maximum welfare.
Let \(m=\sum_t|D_t|\) and let \(L\) be the total binary input length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and the accuracy parameter \(\varepsilon\), treating \(\delta\) as fixed.

Does this mass-allocation problem admit a PTAS returning a feasible allocation of welfare at least \((1-\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}\) for every \(0<\varepsilon<1\), or is achieving this guarantee NP-hard for some fixed \(\varepsilon\)?

The discrete multi-minded complex-demand knapsack problem in this angular domain admits a truthful PTAS with welfare guarantee \((1-3\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}\) and no capacity violation ([Chau–Elbassioni–Khonji](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.3907)).
A related fractional multiple-choice AC scheduling relaxation admits feasible additive-error approximation in time polynomial in input bit length and the logarithm of inverse error ([Combinatorial Optimization of AC Electric Power Systems](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~sid.chau/FnT/FnT.pdf)).

The stated problem is exactly solvable in polynomial time, and hence admits a PTAS. The normalization \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), together with \(\lVert d\rVert_2\le c\), makes the capacity constraint redundant.

I first prove this conclusion under the given premises. I then address a capacity-active variant, allowing individual demands to exceed \(c\), and prove that it also admits a feasible PTAS.

1. Exact solution under the stated assumptions

For any nonnegative allocation satisfying the type-mass equalities,

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}=\sum_t\mu_t=1.\)

The triangle inequality and the individual demand bounds therefore give

\(\displaystyle \left\lVert\sum_t\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}d\right\rVert_2 \le \sum_t\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}\lVert d\rVert_2 \le c\sum_t\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d} =c. \)

Thus every allocation satisfying the type-mass equalities is feasible.

For each supported type, choose

\(\displaystyle d_t^\star\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{d\in D_t}v_t(d).\)

This maximum exists because the menu is finite and nonempty. Allocate \(x_{t,d_t^\star}=\mu_t\) and set every other coordinate to zero. The allocation is feasible by the preceding calculation.

For any feasible allocation \(x\), nonnegativity of its coordinates implies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}v_t(d) \le v_t(d_t^\star)\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d} =\mu_t v_t(d_t^\star). \)

Summing over types proves

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{OPT}=\sum_t\mu_t v_t(d_t^\star), \)

and our allocation attains this value exactly. This argument includes the case \(\mathrm{OPT}=0\).

Bit complexity

Scan each explicitly listed menu and compare rational valuations exactly. Comparing \(a/b\) with \(p/q\), where denominators are positive, amounts to comparing the integers \(aq\) and \(pb\). Since each input integer has at most \(L\) bits, schoolbook multiplication gives an \(O(L^2)\) bound per comparison.

There are at most \(m\) comparisons. Copying the selected masses into the output takes at most \(O(\tau L+m)\) time. Consequently, a conservative total bound is

\(\displaystyle O(mL^2+\tau L).\)

All output masses are input rationals or zero. The running time is independent of \(\varepsilon\) and \(\delta\), and no types outside the supplied support are enumerated. The angular and valuation-monotonicity assumptions are unnecessary for this exact solution.

2. A capacity-active variant also admits a PTAS

To address the nontrivial capacity question, consider the variant obtained by dropping only the bounds \(\lVert d\rVert_2\le c\). Retain nonnegative demand coordinates, rational input, the mass constraints, and the zero option with zero valuation.

The following construction proves a feasible PTAS for this broader variant.

Rational approximation of the capacity disk

Fix \(0<\varepsilon<1\), and define

\(\displaystyle N=\lceil 1/\varepsilon\rceil,\qquad \alpha=1-\frac1N.\)

Then \(N\ge2\) and \(\alpha\ge1-\varepsilon\). For \(j=0,\ldots,N\), put

\(\displaystyle s_j=\frac jN,\qquad u_j=\left(\frac{1-s_j^2}{1+s_j^2},\frac{2s_j}{1+s_j^2}\right). \)

Each \(u_j\) is a rational unit vector in the first quadrant. Its angle is \(\theta_j=2\arctan(s_j)\). These angles increase from \(0\) to \(\pi/2\). Since the derivative of \(2\arctan(s)\) is at most \(2\), consecutive angles differ by at most \(2/N\). Every angle in the first quadrant is therefore within \(1/N\) of some \(\theta_j\).

Define the rational polyhedron

\(\displaystyle P=\{z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2:u_j\cdot z\le\alpha c\text{ for }j=0,\ldots,N\}. \)

It has the two required containment properties:

  • If \(z\ge0\) and \(\lVert z\rVert_2\le\alpha c\), then Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(u_j\cdot z\le\alpha c\) for every \(j\), so \(z\in P\).
  • If \(z\in P\) and \(z\ne0\), choose \(u_j\) whose angle differs from that of \(z\) by \(h\), with \(|h|\le1/N\). The elementary inequality \(\cos h\ge1-h^2/2\) gives

\(\displaystyle \cos h\ge1-\frac1{2N^2}\ge1-\frac1N=\alpha. \)

Hence \(\alpha\lVert z\rVert_2\le u_j\cdot z\le\alpha c\). Since \(\alpha>0\), this proves \(\lVert z\rVert_2\le c\). The conclusion also holds for \(z=0\).

Thus \(P\) contains the quarter disk of radius \(\alpha c\) and lies within the quarter disk of radius \(c\).

Linear program and welfare guarantee

Maximize the original welfare subject to the type-mass equalities, nonnegativity, and the rational linear inequalities

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}(u_j\cdot d)\le\alpha c, \qquad j=0,\ldots,N. \)

Every feasible solution of this linear program is feasible for the original capacity constraint, by the second containment property.

An optimum \(x^\star\) of the capacity-active problem exists: the product of the type simplices is compact, the capacity constraint defines a closed subset, and the all-zero-demand allocation establishes nonemptiness.

Construct an allocation \(y\) by setting

\(\displaystyle y_{t,d}=\alpha x^\star_{t,d}\quad(d\ne0), \qquad y_{t,0}=\alpha x^\star_{t,0}+(1-\alpha)\mu_t. \)

Its coordinates are nonnegative and sum to \(\mu_t\) for each type. Its aggregate demand is \(\alpha\) times that of \(x^\star\), so its norm is at most \(\alpha c\). The first containment property makes \(y\) feasible for the linear program. Because the zero option has zero valuation, its welfare is \(\alpha\mathrm{OPT}\).

An exact optimum of the rational linear program therefore has welfare at least

\(\displaystyle \alpha\mathrm{OPT}\ge(1-\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT},\)

with no capacity violation.

Running time

The linear program has \(m\) variables, \(\tau\) mass equalities, and \(N+1\) additional inequalities. Each coefficient has \(O(L+\log N)\) bits. Its feasible region is nonempty and bounded, since the zero-demand allocation is feasible and \(0\le x_{t,d}\le\mu_t\).

The polynomial-time solvability theorem for rational linear programming therefore applies and returns an exact rational optimum in time

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon).\)

This establishes a PTAS even for the capacity-active variant. For the task as stated, the stronger exact algorithm in Section 1 settles the question.
