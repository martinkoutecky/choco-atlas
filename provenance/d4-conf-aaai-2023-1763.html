1. Answer

The allocation problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\).

The specified item-block updates satisfy a geometric bound on the expected extrapolated objective gap. Their rate, however, contains a numerical condition parameter that can be exponentially large in the input bit length. Indeed, even with two types, two goods, and fixed \(\epsilon=1/2\), these updates can require exponentially many iterations.

Below I prove both statements, give explicit conditioning constants, and distinguish exact rational arithmetic from an implementation with controlled rounding.

All logarithms are natural. All norms and projections are Euclidean.

2. Preliminary facts and equivalence of the objectives

Write

\( c_t=\mu_tB_t,\qquad S_t=\sum_jv_{tj},\qquad U_t=\frac{S_t}{\mu_t},\qquad R_t=\frac{U_t}{a_t}=\frac{W}{c_t}. \)

These quantities are positive. For every \(x\in X\),

\( 0\le u_t(x):=v_t^\top x_t\le U_t, \)

because feasibility implies \(x_{tj}\le1/\mu_t\).

The allocation \(x^0_{tj}=B_t/W\) is feasible and satisfies \(u_t(x^0)=a_t>0\). The set \(X\) is compact, and \(G\) is upper semicontinuous with the stipulated value at zero utility. Consequently, an optimum exists, and every optimum has positive utility for every type.

Optimal utilities are at least the extrapolation thresholds

Let \(x^\star\) maximize \(G\), and write \(u_t^\star=u_t(x^\star)\). The optimality conditions give nonnegative supply multipliers \(p_j\) satisfying

\( \frac{B_tv_{tj}}{u_t^\star}\le p_j, \)

with equality whenever \(x_{tj}^\star>0\), and

\( p_j\left(1-\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}^\star\right)=0. \)

These conditions follow either from the normal cone of the allocation polytope or from the usual convex-program optimality conditions. A strictly feasible allocation with all utilities positive exists, so the latter application is valid.

Multiplying the equality on positive coordinates by \(\mu_tx_{tj}^\star\) and summing gives

\( \sum_jp_j =\sum_jp_j\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}^\star =\sum_tc_t =W. \)

Hence

\( \frac{B_tS_t}{u_t^\star} =\sum_j\frac{B_tv_{tj}}{u_t^\star} \le\sum_jp_j=W, \)

which proves

\( u_t^\star\ge a_t. \)

The extrapolated problem has exactly the same optimizers

Each \(h_t\) is twice continuously differentiable, with

\( h_t''(u)= \begin{cases} c_t/a_t^2,&0\le u<a_t,\\ c_t/u^2,&u\ge a_t. \end{cases} \)

Thus \(h_t\) is strictly convex on \([0,U_t]\).

At \(x^\star\), the value and gradient of \(\widetilde f\) agree with those of \(-G\), because \(u_t^\star\ge a_t\). The first-order optimality inequality for \(-G\) therefore also holds for \(\widetilde f\). Convexity implies that \(x^\star\) minimizes \(\widetilde f\).

Strict convexity in the utility vector implies that every minimizer of \(\widetilde f\) has the same utility vector \(u^\star\). Therefore

\( X^\star:=\arg\min_{x\in X}\widetilde f(x) =\{x\in X:u(x)=u^\star\} =\arg\max_{x\in X}G(x), \)

and

\( \min_{x\in X}\widetilde f(x)=-G^\star. \)

Define the extrapolated gap by

\( \Delta(x)=\widetilde f(x)+G^\star. \)

In particular, \(\Delta(x)\ge0\).

This is the extrapolation principle used for finite Fisher markets by [Gao and Kroer](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/f75526659f31040afeb61cb7133e4e6d-Paper.pdf). The argument above verifies it directly for the per-capita coordinates and weighted supply constraints in this question.

3. A polynomial-time rational optimization algorithm

The proof in this section does not use item-block dynamics.

Let

\( n=m\tau,\qquad \mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t,\qquad B_{\min}=\min_tB_t,\qquad \mathcal R=\frac{n}{\mu_{\min}}. \)

The set \(X\) lies in the ball of radius \(\mathcal R\) centered at the origin.

Consider the rational polytope

\( D=\{x\in X:u_t(x)\ge a_t/2\text{ for every }t\}. \)

It contains every optimizer. On \(D\), minimize the normalized objective

\( \phi(x)=-\sum_tc_t\log\frac{u_t(x)}{a_t}. \)

Its gradient is rational at every rational point of \(D\). Moreover,

\( \|\nabla\phi(x)\|_2 \le\|\nabla\phi(x)\|_1 =\sum_t\frac{c_tS_t}{u_t(x)} \le2\sum_t\frac{c_tS_t}{a_t} =2W. \)

Thus \(\phi\) is \(2W\)-Lipschitz on \(D\).

An explicitly bounded interior ball

Put

\( z=\frac34x^0,\qquad r_0=\frac{B_{\min}}{4W}. \)

Since \(W\ge B_{\min}\), we have \(r_0\le1/4\). The ball of radius \(r_0\) about \(z\) lies in \(D\):

  • Every coordinate of \(z\) is at least \(3r_0\).
  • Each weighted supply constraint has slack \(1/4\), and \(\|\mu\|_2\le1\).
  • The utility constraint for type \(t\) has slack \(a_t/4\), while

\( r_0\|v_t\|_2\le\frac{B_{\min}S_t}{4W}\le a_t/4. \)

Now choose

\( \theta=\min\left\{\frac12,\frac{\epsilon}{24W\mathcal R}\right\}, \qquad w=(1-\theta)x^\star+\theta z. \)

Convexity of \(D\) shows that the ball of radius \(\theta r_0\) about \(w\) lies in \(D\). Every point \(y\) in that ball satisfies

\( \phi(y)-\phi(x^\star) \le4W\mathcal R\theta+2W\theta r_0 \le6W\mathcal R\theta \le\epsilon/4. \)

The first term uses convexity and the Lipschitz bound between \(z\) and \(x^\star\); the second uses the Lipschitz bound inside the ball.

Ellipsoid implementation and bit complexity

Apply the rational central-cut ellipsoid method, initially enclosing \(D\) in the ball of radius \(\mathcal R\).

At a center outside \(D\), use a violated rational linear constraint. At a center \(x\in D\), use the rational gradient cut

\( \nabla\phi(x)^\top(y-x)\le0. \)

If \(\phi(x)>\phi(x^\star)+\epsilon/2\), this cut retains the entire ball constructed above: every point of that ball has smaller objective value, and the convexity inequality implies that it satisfies the cut.

The standard rational ellipsoid volume bound therefore guarantees that within

\( O\left(n^2\left[1+\log\frac{\mathcal R}{\theta r_0}\right]\right) \)

iterations, some feasible queried center has objective gap at most \(\epsilon/2\). Otherwise all cuts would retain a ball whose volume exceeds the final ellipsoid volume.

The hypotheses needed for the rational ellipsoid implementation hold:

  • The constraint coefficients and the radius bounds have polynomial encoding length.
  • Feasibility can be checked exactly using rational arithmetic.
  • The gradient oracle is rational and computable in time polynomial in the query encoding length and input length.
  • Objective values can be computed to absolute error \(\epsilon/8\) in polynomial time.

For the last point, each positive rational ratio \(u_t(x)/a_t\) has polynomial encoding length. Its logarithm can be evaluated by scaling into \([1,2]\) and using the convergent series

\( \log r =2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{((r-1)/(r+1))^{2k+1}}{2k+1}. \)

On \([1,2]\), the series parameter is at most \(1/3\), so the number of terms is linear in the desired precision. The weights \(c_t\) affect the required precision only through their encoding lengths.

Return the feasible queried center with smallest approximate objective. Its true gap is at most \(3\epsilon/4\), and hence at most \(\epsilon\).

Because

\( \log\mathcal R,\quad \log(1/r_0),\quad \log(1/\theta) \)

are polynomially bounded in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\), this proves the claimed polynomial-time rational solvability.

4. Explicit conditioning and expected convergence of the exact updates

Define

\( \kappa_t=\frac{c_t}{U_t^2}, \qquad \kappa=\min_t\kappa_t, \qquad \Lambda=\max_jL_j, \qquad \ell=m\Lambda. \)

We have \(\kappa>0\) and \(\Lambda>0\), because every valuation vector is nonzero. Also, throughout the feasible utility box,

\( \kappa_t\le h_t''(u)\le c_t/a_t^2. \)

Consequently:

  • The function \(\sum_th_t(u_t)\) is \(\kappa\)-strongly convex in the utility vector.
  • The stated \(L_j\) is a valid smoothness constant for block \(j\).
  • The full gradient of \(\widetilde f\) is \(\ell\)-Lipschitz on \(X\), since

\( \max_t\frac{c_t\|v_t\|_2^2}{a_t^2}\le m\Lambda. \)

A Hoffman constant and an explicit rational upper bound

Let \(A\) be the utility matrix, so \(Ax=u(x)\), and write \(X=\{x:Cx\le d\}\), using the weighted supply constraints and nonnegativity constraints.

A relative Hoffman constant \(H\) is any constant satisfying

\( \operatorname{dist}(x,X^\star) \le H\|Ax-u^\star\|_2 \qquad(x\in X). \)

Here is an input-derived upper bound that avoids leaving \(H\) unspecified.

Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of all \(\mu_t\) and \(v_{tj}\), and put

\( V=\max\{1,\max_{t,j}v_{tj}\}, \qquad \overline H=Q^n(nV)^{n-1}. \)

Then \(H\le\overline H\).

To prove this, fix \(x\in X\), let \(z\) be its projection onto \(X^\star\), and put \(d_x=x-z\). Polyhedral projection optimality gives

\( d_x=A^\top\alpha+C_I^\top\beta, \qquad \beta\ge0, \)

where the constraints indexed by \(I\) are active at \(z\). Since the rows of \(A\) are independent, dependent active inequality rows can be eliminated, while maintaining nonnegative coefficients, until

\( M=\begin{bmatrix}A\\C_I\end{bmatrix} \)

has independent rows. Indeed, a dependence modulo the span of \(A\) permits subtracting a suitable multiple of that dependence from \(\beta\), eliminating at least one positive coefficient.

Writing \(\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)\), feasibility of \(x\) gives

\( \|d_x\|_2^2 =\alpha^\top(Ax-u^\star)+\beta^\top C_I(x-z) \le\|\gamma\|_2\|Ax-u^\star\|_2. \)

Also,

\( \|d_x\|_2=\|M^\top\gamma\|_2 \ge\sigma_{\min}(M)\|\gamma\|_2. \)

Thus the required bound holds with \(1/\sigma_{\min}(M)\).

If \(M\) has \(k\) rows, a nonzero \(k\)-column minor has absolute determinant at least \(Q^{-k}\). Moreover, every singular value is at most \(\|M\|_F\le nV\). Hence

\( \sigma_{\min}(M) \ge\frac{Q^{-k}}{(nV)^{k-1}}, \)

and therefore \(1/\sigma_{\min}(M)\le\overline H\), as claimed.

In particular,

\( \log_2\overline H=O(n(L+\log n)). \)

The bound is conservative, but it is explicit, rational, and has polynomial encoding length.

In what follows, \(H\) can be any certified upper bound; choosing \(H=\overline H\) makes every constant computable from the input. Define

\( \lambda=\frac{\kappa}{H^2}, \qquad \rho=\frac1m\min\left\{1,\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda}\right\}. \)

Proof of geometric convergence

For \(x\in X\), let \(z\) be its nearest point in \(X^\star\). Strong convexity in utilities and the Hoffman inequality imply

\( \widetilde f(z) \ge\widetilde f(x) +\nabla\widetilde f(x)^\top(z-x) +\frac{\lambda}{2}\|z-x\|_2^2. \)

For a common quadratic coefficient \(\Lambda\), define

\( q_\Lambda(x)= \min_{y\in X} \left\{ \nabla\widetilde f(x)^\top(y-x) +\frac{\Lambda}{2}\|y-x\|_2^2 \right\}. \)

Taking \(y=x+s(z-x)\), where \(s=\min\{1,\lambda/\Lambda\}\), yields

\( q_\Lambda(x)\le-s\Delta(x). \)

Indeed, the previous inequality bounds the linear term by
\(-s\Delta(x)-s\lambda\|z-x\|_2^2/2\), and the quadratic term contributes
\(\Lambda s^2\|z-x\|_2^2/2\). Their squared-distance coefficient is nonpositive.

The feasible set is a product over item columns. Each exact block update minimizes its block quadratic model with coefficient \(L_j\le\Lambda\). Block smoothness and uniform sampling therefore give

\( \mathbb E[\Delta(x^{k+1})\mid x^k] \le\Delta(x^k)+\frac1m q_\Lambda(x^k) \le(1-\rho)\Delta(x^k). \)

A block with \(L_j=0\) has zero valuations throughout that column, so its model and objective change are both zero.

Consequently,

\( \boxed{\mathbb E[\Delta(x^k)] \le(1-\rho)^k\Delta(x^0).} \)

This matches the conditioning-dependent rate structure in [Nan, Gao, and Kroer, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00506); the proof above handles the weighted constraints directly.

The initial gap has the useful bound

\( \Delta(x^0) =G^\star-G(x^0) \le\sum_tc_t\log R_t \le W\log\tau \le(\tau-1)W. \)

For the middle inequality, \(c_t/W\) is a probability vector and the sum is \(W\) times its entropy.

If \(\tau=1\), the initial allocation is already optimal. Henceforth assume \(\tau\ge2\), and set

\( D_0=(\tau-1)W, \qquad M=D_0+\frac32W=(\tau+\tfrac12)W. \)

Since every \(h_t\) decreases on \([0,U_t]\),

\( \Delta(x)\le\widetilde f(0)+G^\star =\Delta(x^0)+\frac32W \le M \qquad(x\in X). \)

5. Transferring convergence to logarithmic accuracy

An expected bound on \(\Delta\) must not simply be identified with an expected bound on \(G^\star-G\): the objectives differ below the thresholds \(a_t\).

Strong convexity at an optimum gives the more detailed inequality

\( \Delta(x)\ge \frac12\sum_t\kappa_t(u_t(x)-u_t^\star)^2. \)

The first-order term is nonnegative by optimality.

A deterministic small-gap implication

Define

\( \delta_{\mathrm{safe}} =\min_t\frac{c_t}{8R_t^2}, \qquad R_{\max}=\max_tR_t. \)

If \(\Delta(x)\le\delta_{\mathrm{safe}}\), then

\( |u_t(x)-u_t^\star| \le U_t\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta(x)}{c_t}} \le a_t/2. \)

Thus \(u_t(x)\ge a_t/2\) for every type.

For \(a_t/2\le u<a_t\), let

\( d_t(u)=-c_t\log u-h_t(u). \)

We have \(d_t(a_t)=d_t'(a_t)=0\), and

\( 0\le d_t''(u) =\frac{c_t}{u^2}-\frac{c_t}{a_t^2} \le\frac{3c_t}{a_t^2}. \)

Integrating twice proves

\( 0\le d_t(u) \le\frac{3c_t}{2a_t^2}(a_t-u)^2. \)

Since \(u_t^\star\ge a_t\), summing and using the strong-convexity inequality yields

\( G^\star-G(x) \le(1+3R_{\max}^2)\Delta(x) \quad\text{whenever }\Delta(x)\le\delta_{\mathrm{safe}}. \)

Therefore, with

\( \delta= \min\left\{ \delta_{\mathrm{safe}}, \frac{\epsilon}{1+3R_{\max}^2} \right\}, \)

we have the certified implication

\( \boxed{\Delta(x)\le\delta \ \Longrightarrow\ G^\star-G(x)\le\epsilon.} \)

For the exact dynamics this gives

\( \Pr\{G^\star-G(x^k)>\epsilon\} \le \min\left\{1,\frac{D_0(1-\rho)^k}{\delta}\right\}. \)

Expected logarithmic accuracy with positive output utilities

An unconditional expected logarithmic guarantee can be obtained by a rational mixture with the initial allocation.

Put

\( \beta=\min\left\{\frac12,\frac{\epsilon}{2D_0}\right\}, \qquad \widehat x^k=(1-\beta)x^k+\beta x^0, \qquad C_R=\sum_tc_tR_t^2. \)

Every utility of \(\widehat x^k\) is at least \(\beta a_t\). Compare it with
\((1-\beta)x^\star+\beta x^0\). Convexity of \(-G\), the derivative bound for logarithms above \(\beta a_t\), and Cauchy–Schwarz give

\( G^\star-G(\widehat x^k) \le \beta D_0+ \frac{1-\beta}{\beta}\sqrt{2C_R\Delta(x^k)}. \)

The Cauchy–Schwarz calculation used here is

\( \sum_t\frac{c_t|u_t(x)-u_t^\star|}{a_t} \le \sqrt{\sum_t\kappa_t(u_t(x)-u_t^\star)^2} \sqrt{\sum_t\frac{c_t^2}{a_t^2\kappa_t}} \le\sqrt{2C_R\Delta(x)}. \)

Taking expectations and applying Jensen’s inequality proves

\( \mathbb E[G^\star-G(\widehat x^k)] \le \beta D_0+ \frac{1-\beta}{\beta}\sqrt{2C_RD_0}\,(1-\rho)^{k/2}. \)

In particular, expected gap at most \(\epsilon\) follows whenever

\( k\ge \frac1\rho \log^+\left(\frac{8C_RD_0}{\epsilon^2\beta^2}\right), \)

where \(\log^+r=\max\{0,\log r\}\).

A rational stopping certificate

For a guarantee that holds for every returned allocation, define

\( \mathcal C(x)= \max_{z\in X}\nabla\widetilde f(x)^\top(x-z). \)

Convexity gives \(\Delta(x)\le\mathcal C(x)\). If \(g=\nabla\widetilde f(x)\), then \(g_{tj}\le0\), and the product structure of \(X\) gives the exact rational formula

\( \mathcal C(x) = \sum_{t,j}g_{tj}x_{tj} + \sum_j\max_t\frac{-g_{tj}}{\mu_t}. \)

Thus the stopping test \(\mathcal C(x)\le\delta\) requires no logarithmic evaluations.

Let \(D_X=2\mathcal R\), an upper bound on the diameter of \(X\). Smoothness, optimality, and the Hoffman inequality imply

\( \mathcal C(x) \le \Delta(x)+\ell D_X\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta(x)}{\lambda}}. \)

For completeness, take a nearest optimum \(z\) and a minimizer \(y\) of the linear function \(g^\top y\) over \(X\). Decompose

\( g^\top(x-y) =(g-\nabla\widetilde f(z))^\top(x-y) +\nabla\widetilde f(z)^\top(x-z) +\nabla\widetilde f(z)^\top(z-y). \)

The three terms are bounded respectively by
\(\ell D_X\|x-z\|_2\), \(\Delta(x)\), and zero. Finally,
\(\|x-z\|_2\le\sqrt{2\Delta(x)/\lambda}\).

Define

\( \eta= \min\left\{ \frac{\delta}{2}, \frac{\lambda\delta^2}{8\ell^2D_X^2} \right\}. \)

Then

\( \Delta(x)\le\eta \ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal C(x)\le\delta \ \Longrightarrow\ G^\star-G(x)\le\epsilon. \)

For exact updates stopped at the first passing certificate, Markov’s inequality gives expected iteration count

\( O\left( \frac{1+\log^+(D_0/\eta)}{\rho} \right). \)

Indeed, the probability of not having stopped by iteration \(k\) is at most
\(\min\{1,D_0(1-\rho)^k/\eta\}\); summing these tails proves the bound.

6. Rational arithmetic and bit running time

Exact updates

For a rational vector \(y\), Euclidean projection onto \(K_\mu\) has the form

\( z_t=\max\{0,y_t-\gamma\mu_t\}. \)

If \(\sum_t\mu_t\max\{0,y_t\}\le1\), then \(\gamma=0\). Otherwise, for the positive-coordinate set \(I\),

\( \gamma= \frac{\sum_{t\in I}\mu_ty_t-1} {\sum_{t\in I}\mu_t^2}. \)

Sorting the breakpoints \(y_t/\mu_t\) identifies \(I\) in
\(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational arithmetic operations. All derivatives are rational:

\( h_t'(u)= \begin{cases} -c_t/u,&u\ge a_t,\\ c_t(u-2a_t)/a_t^2,&0\le u<a_t. \end{cases} \)

Hence every exact iterate is rational.

However, a constant arithmetic-operation count is not a constant bit cost. Utility reciprocals and the projection can enlarge denominators. Using a common denominator for the current allocation gives the conservative encoding-length recurrence

\( b_{k+1} \le C(\tau+1)b_k+\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \)

for a universal constant \(C\). To see this, each utility numerator has size
\(O(b_k+\operatorname{poly}(L))\); a common denominator for the updated column can include the product of at most \(\tau\) such numerators, followed by the rational projection denominator.

Thus a \(K\)-step exact run has a valid bit-time upper bound

\( (C(\tau+1))^{O(K)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). \)

One way to obtain a guaranteed output with this bound in expectation is to run independent exact trials of length

\( K= \left\lceil \frac1\rho\log\frac{4M}{\eta} \right\rceil, \)

accepting a trial only when its final certificate passes. Its expected extrapolated gap is at most \(\eta/4\), so acceptance probability is at least \(3/4\). The number of trials has bounded expectation.

This is a conservative bound, not a claim that exact denominators always grow at that rate. It explains why the geometric iteration theorem alone is insufficient for a polynomial bit-time conclusion.

A controlled-rounding implementation

A substantially better bit bound follows with one explicit modification: after an exact block update, round each updated coordinate downward to a dyadic grid of spacing \(q=2^{-b}\). Downward rounding preserves feasibility.

On all of \(X\),

\( \|\nabla\widetilde f(x)\|_2 \le\|\nabla\widetilde f(x)\|_1 \le2W=:J. \)

Rounding changes the updated block by norm at most \(\tau q\). Therefore the rounded process satisfies

\( \mathbb E[\Delta(x^{k+1})\mid x^k] \le(1-\rho)\Delta(x^k)+J\tau q. \)

Choose

\( b= \left\lceil \log_2^+\frac{4J\tau}{\rho\eta} \right\rceil. \)

Then \(J\tau q\le\rho\eta/4\), and

\( \mathbb E[\Delta(x^k)] \le(1-\rho)^k\Delta(x^0)+\eta/4. \)

Check the rational certificate after every \(K\) updates, using the \(K\) above. From any feasible starting state, the bound \(\Delta\le M\) implies that the next check fails with conditional probability at most \(1/2\). \( s=O\bigl(n^2(L+b+\log(n+1))\bigr) \)

7. Exponentially slow exact dynamics on two types and two goods

The lack of a polynomial iteration guarantee is substantive.

Choose a dyadic \(q=2^{-b}\) with \(b\ge2\), and let

\( \mu_1=1-q,\qquad \mu_2=q,\qquad B_1=B_2=1/q, \)

and

\( v_1=(1,0),\qquad v_2=(0,1). \)

Take fixed accuracy \(\epsilon=1/2\). Here

\( W=1/q,\qquad a_1=a_2=1,\qquad x^0_{tj}=1. \)

The optimum assigns each good entirely to its interested type, so

\( u_1^\star=\frac1{1-q}, \qquad u_2^\star=\frac1q. \)

Let \(s=x_{22}\). Only updates of good \(2\) change \(s\), and its block smoothness constant is

\( L_2=\mu_2B_2=1. \)

As long as \(s\ge1\), the unprojected update increases \(s\) by \(1/s\). Projection cannot increase it beyond that candidate, so

\( s^+\le s+1/s. \)

Projection also cannot decrease this coordinate below its old value. To verify this, let \(d=1/s\). The old column is feasible, and the candidate increases only its second coordinate by \(d\). At projection multiplier \(\gamma=d/q\), that coordinate has returned to its old value, while every other coordinate has weakly decreased. The weighted sum is therefore at most one. The chosen multiplier cannot exceed \(d/q\), proving \(s^+\ge s\).

Thus \(s\ge1\) throughout, and each update of good \(2\) satisfies

\( (s^+)^2 \le(s+1/s)^2 =s^2+2+1/s^2 \le s^2+3. \)

After \(k\) total updates,

\( s^2\le1+3k. \)

Meanwhile, each type’s utility is individually bounded above by its optimum utility. Since \(c_2=\mu_2B_2=1\), the objective gap satisfies

\( G^\star-G(x) \ge\log\frac{1/q}{s}. \)

Accuracy \(\epsilon=1/2\) therefore requires

\( s\ge\frac{e^{-1/2}}q, \)

and hence

\( \boxed{ k\ge\frac{e^{-1}q^{-2}-1}{3}. } \)

This lower bound holds for every sequence of sampled items. The instance has \(m=\tau=2\), fixed accuracy, and input length \(O(b)\), whereas the required number of updates is \(\Omega(2^{2b})\).

Therefore the allocation problem is polynomial-time solvable, while these particular dynamics can have exponential running time even before exact-arithmetic costs are considered.
