The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses over a finite set of complete bidder valuation and behavior types, proportional item capacities, a polyhedral competitor space, and acceptable overcharge \(\kappa\), define the unit-bidder first-order limit of unnormalised welfare for a capacity-scaled residual market with one distinguished competitor \(e\). Compute \(e_\kappa\) in the competitor space whose scaled welfare equals \(\kappa\) plus the minimum scaled welfare, and output its induced \(f\)-VCG price.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A residual society is given by its support: \(\tau\) complete bidder valuation and behavior types with positive binary-rational masses \(q_t\), where \(\sum_t q_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq1\) item categories with positive rational capacities \(c_j\).
Each valuation \(v_t\) is an explicit table on all \(d=2^m\) bundles, with nonnegative rational entries, free disposal, and \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\). Each bidder receives at most one bundle; items may remain unsold.
For capacity vector \(b\geq0\), define background welfare \(W(b)=\max\{\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S):x_{t,S}\geq0,\ \sum_Sx_{t,S}\leq q_t,\ \sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\leq b_j\}\). Thus background masses may split among bundles.
The distinguished bidder has a revealed rational valuation \(v\) in a nonempty compact rational polytope \(C\), given by \(r\) inequalities; every vector in \(C\) is a nonnegative free-disposal valuation with empty-bundle value zero. A rational \(\kappa\geq0\) is acceptable: every relevant bidder behavior type accepts an overcharge of exactly \(\kappa\) at every nonempty-bundle bid.
At scale \(N\), background masses and capacities are \(Nq_t\) and \(Nc_j\), while the distinguished bidder receives one whole bundle. For sufficiently large integers \(N\), let \(T_N(e)=\max_{S\subseteq[m]}\{e(S)+N W(c-\mathbf1_S/N)\}\), where \(\mathbf1_S\) is the bundle incidence vector.
Define unit-bidder first-order welfare by \(H(e)=\lim_{N\to\infty}[T_N(e)-N W(c)]\), and bundle-specific background change by \(D(S)=\lim_{N\to\infty}N[W(c-\mathbf1_S/N)-W(c)]\).
Fix lexicographic tie-breaking and let \(S_v\) maximize \(v(S)+D(S)\). The induced limiting \(f\)-VCG price is \(p_e(v)=H(e)-H(v)+v(S_v)\); the competitor changes the price, while the allocation uses the revealed valuation.
Promise that some \(e_\kappa\in C\) satisfies \(H(e_\kappa)=\kappa+\min_{e\in C}H(e)\).

Is exact computation of such an \(e_\kappa\) and its induced price polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,d,r,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length, or is this promised problem NP-hard?

For finite bidders, a competitor with welfare equal to minimum welfare plus \(\kappa\) can be computed using polynomially many winner-determination oracle calls and additional polynomial time; this is an oracle-relative guarantee. [Balcan, Prasad, and Sandholm](https://sid-prasad.github.io/papers/aaai-25.pdf).
No result settling this precisely specified first-order mass problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the residual society's masses to one and interpreted capacities as proportional to residual background mass; the distinguished bidder is added separately.
  • Defined the continuous background by divisible masses allocated among whole-bundle alternatives, while retaining one indivisible bundle for the distinguished bidder.
  • Defined scaled welfare as the first-order change in unnormalised welfare after subtracting background welfare; no integer rounding of background masses or capacities is used.
  • Used explicit complete valuation tables, nonnegative values, free disposal, zero empty-bundle value, and permitted unsold items.
  • Required positive item capacities so every bundle remains feasible for the distinguished bidder at sufficiently large scales.
  • Took the competitor space to be a nonempty compact rational polytope of valid valuations and supplied the distinguished bidder's revealed valuation to determine its price.
  • Added an attainability promise because the required welfare level need not be reachable within a constrained competitor space.
  • Fixed lexicographic tie-breaking for the distinguished bidder's first-order efficient bundle.
  • Treated acceptability as a behavioral promise at the specified overcharge, rather than requiring an encoding of an otherwise unused acceptance function.

Exact polynomial-time computation

The promised problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,d,r,L\). The proof gives an exact rational algorithm using at most \(2d+2\) linear-programming calls.

1. The background dual and its optimal face

Write \(p(S)=\sum_{j\in S}p_j\). Define the background dual feasible polyhedron
\[ Q=\left\{(\alpha,p)\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{\tau+m}: \alpha_t+p(S)\geq v_t(S)\text{ for every }t,S\right\}. \]
For every nonnegative capacity vector \(b\), linear-programming duality gives
\[ W(b)=\min_{(\alpha,p)\in Q} \left(\sum_t q_t\alpha_t+\sum_j b_jp_j\right). \]

The hypotheses for duality hold: the primal is feasible by taking all allocations zero, and it is bounded because \(0\leq x_{t,S}\leq q_t\). The dual is feasible, for example with \(p=0\) and \(\alpha_t=\max_S v_t(S)\).

Let \(w=W(c)\), and define
\[ F=\left\{(\alpha,p)\in Q: \sum_tq_t\alpha_t+\sum_jc_jp_j=w\right\}. \]
This is a nonempty compact optimal face. Indeed, nonnegativity and the strict positivity of every \(q_t,c_j\) imply
\[ 0\leq\alpha_t\leq \frac{w}{q_t}, \qquad 0\leq p_j\leq\frac{w}{c_j}. \]

We will prove that
\[ \boxed{D(S)=-\max_{(\alpha,p)\in F}p(S).} \]

2. Exact first-order background changes

Fix a bundle \(S\), and put
\[ M_S=\max_{(\alpha,p)\in F}p(S). \]
The maximum exists by compactness.

Let \(V\) be the set of vertices of \(Q\). This set is finite: each vertex is uniquely specified by a choice of linearly independent tight constraints from the finite defining system.

For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), every coordinate of \(c-\varepsilon\mathbf1_S\) is positive. Consequently, the dual objective at this capacity vector has compact sublevel sets within \(Q\): all variables are nonnegative and all objective coefficients are strictly positive. Its optimal face is therefore compact and contains a vertex of \(Q\). Hence
\[ W(c-\varepsilon\mathbf1_S) = \min_{u=(\alpha^u,p^u)\in V} \left(\sum_tq_t\alpha_t^u+\sum_jc_jp_j^u-\varepsilon p^u(S)\right). \]

Let \(V_0\) consist of the vertices optimal at \(c\). Because \(F\) is a compact face of \(Q\), its vertices belong to \(V_0\), and maximizing \(p(S)\) over \(F\) attains its maximum at such a vertex. Thus
\[ \min_{u\in V_0} \left(w-\varepsilon p^u(S)\right)=w-\varepsilon M_S. \]

For each \(u\in V\setminus V_0\), define its strictly positive objective gap
\[ \gamma_u=\sum_tq_t\alpha_t^u+\sum_jc_jp_j^u-w>0. \]
Its perturbed objective exceeds \(w-\varepsilon M_S\) by
\[ \gamma_u-\varepsilon\bigl(p^u(S)-M_S\bigr). \]
If \(p^u(S)\leq M_S\), this expression is positive for every positive \(\varepsilon\). Otherwise, it is positive whenever
\[ \varepsilon<\frac{\gamma_u}{p^u(S)-M_S}. \]
There are only finitely many vertices, so all these conditions hold simultaneously for sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\). Therefore,
\[ W(c-\varepsilon\mathbf1_S)=w-\varepsilon M_S \]
throughout a sufficiently small interval.

Taking \(\varepsilon=1/N\) proves that the defining limit exists and that
\[ D(S)=-M_S. \]
In particular, \(D(\varnothing)=0\).

This proof uses vertices only to establish the limit; the algorithm does not enumerate them.

3. Computing every background change and \(H\)

First compute \(w\) by solving the given background welfare LP. For each bundle \(S\), compute \(D(S)\) using the rational LP
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad &\sum_{j\in S}p_j\\ \text{subject to}\quad &(\alpha,p)\in Q,\\ &\sum_tq_t\alpha_t+\sum_jc_jp_j=w. \end{aligned} \]
Negate the resulting optimum.

Each of these LPs is feasible and bounded because its feasible set is \(F\).

There are finitely many bundles, so the small-perturbation identities from Section 2 hold simultaneously for all bundles once \(N\) is sufficiently large. For every valuation \(e\), it follows that
\[ \begin{aligned} T_N(e)-Nw &=\max_S\left\{e(S)+N\bigl[W(c-\mathbf1_S/N)-w\bigr]\right\}\\ &=\max_S\{e(S)+D(S)\}. \end{aligned} \]
Thus the unit-bidder limit also exists, with
\[ \boxed{H(e)=\max_S\{e(S)+D(S)\}.} \]

4. Finding a competitor at the promised level

Represent the given polytope as \(C=\{e:Ae\leq a\}\). Compute its minimum first-order welfare by the epigraph LP
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad &h\\ \text{subject to}\quad &Ae\leq a,\\ &e(S)+D(S)\leq h\qquad\text{for every bundle }S. \end{aligned} \]
Its optimum is exactly
\[ h_{\min}=\min_{e\in C}H(e). \]
Indeed, for each fixed \(e\), the smallest feasible \(h\) is \(H(e)\). The minimum exists because \(C\) is nonempty and compact and \(H\) is continuous.

Set \(z=h_{\min}+\kappa\). For each bundle \(T\), solve the linear feasibility problem
\[ \begin{aligned} &Ae\leq a,\\ &e(S)+D(S)\leq z\qquad\text{for every bundle }S,\\ &e(T)+D(T)=z. \end{aligned} \]

Every feasible solution to any of these systems satisfies \(H(e)=z\): the inequalities give \(H(e)\leq z\), while the equality gives \(H(e)\geq z\).

Conversely, if \(e\in C\) satisfies \(H(e)=z\), at least one bundle \(T\) attains the finite maximum defining \(H(e)\). That \(e\) satisfies the corresponding feasibility system. Consequently,
\[ \{e\in C:H(e)=z\} = \bigcup_T \left\{e\in C: e(S)+D(S)\leq z\ \text{for all }S,\ e(T)+D(T)=z\right\}. \]
The promise ensures that this union is nonempty. Solving at most \(d\) feasibility LPs therefore produces an exact rational \(e_\kappa\).

5. Computing the allocation and price

Scan all bundles and select the prescribed lexicographic maximizer
\[ S_v\in\arg\max_S\{v(S)+D(S)\}. \]
All comparisons are between exact rational numbers.

Since \(H(v)=v(S_v)+D(S_v)\), the required price is
\[ \begin{aligned} p_{e_\kappa}(v) &=H(e_\kappa)-H(v)+v(S_v)\\ &=\boxed{h_{\min}+\kappa-D(S_v)}. \end{aligned} \]
In particular, all competitors at the promised welfare level induce the same price.

6. Binary complexity

The background primal has \(\tau d\) variables and \(\tau+m\) resource constraints. Each LP computing \(D(S)\) has \(\tau+m\) variables and at most \(\tau d+\tau+m+2\) inequalities when its equality is encoded as two inequalities. The minimum-welfare LP has \(d+1\) variables and \(r+d\) inequalities. Each target-level feasibility LP has \(d\) variables and \(r+d+2\) inequalities.

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: feasibility and bounded optimization can be solved exactly in time polynomial in the binary description length, with polynomial-length rational outputs. The rational encoding bounds and polynomial-time LP algorithms are presented in [Goemans’s linear-programming notes, Sections 11–13](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-854j-advanced-algorithms-fall-2008/908fe54d659623efadda2d033b895caf_notes_lp.pdf).

All initial LP coefficients have polynomial encoding length in the given input. The computed value \(w\), then the values \(D(S)\), and finally \(h_{\min}\) have polynomial encoding length by that theorem. Consequently, every subsequent LP also has polynomial description length. All stated feasibility and boundedness hypotheses have been verified above.

The algorithm makes one background optimization call, at most \(d\) derivative calls, one minimum-welfare call, and at most \(d\) target-level feasibility calls. Its remaining work consists of constructing these systems and performing polynomially many rational operations.

Enumerating the \(d=2^m\) bundles is permitted by the requested complexity bound and by their explicit representation in the input. Thus this is an exact polynomial-time algorithm for the specified promised problem.
