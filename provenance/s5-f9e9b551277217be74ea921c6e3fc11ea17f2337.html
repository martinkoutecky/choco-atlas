The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite need types \(Q\), integral bundles \(B\), rational utilities \(u(q,b)\), resource use, transitions \(P(q'\mid q)\), a rational initial distribution \(\mu_0\) over \((q,z)\), per-capita supplies, horizon \(H\), discount factor \(\gamma_p<1\), and \(\epsilon>0\), choose feasible mass flows \(x_t(q,z,b)\). Each person receives an integral bundle and updates memory to \(z'=\gamma_p z+u(q,b)\); need types then transition according to \(P\). Find a plan within \(\epsilon\) of the maximum of \(\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}[(1-\lambda)\bar u_t+\lambda(W(\mu_{t+1})-W(\mu_t))]\), where \(\bar u_t\) is mean utility and \(W(\mu)=\operatorname{ess\,inf}_{(q,z)\sim\mu}z\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\geq1\) be the number of resource categories. Input explicitly lists nonempty finite sets \(Q\) of need types and \(B\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\) of allowed bundles, nonnegative rational resource-use vectors \(c(q,b)\), per-period per-capita supplies \(s\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^{m}\), and utilities \(u(q,b)\in[0,u_{\max}]\), with rational \(u_{\max}>0\). Include a null bundle with zero utility and resource use for every need type.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \((q,z)\) with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the initial support size, not the number of possible types; masses sum to \(1\), and initial memories are rational and lie in \([0,u_{\max}/(1-\gamma_p)]\).
Also given are an action-independent rational stochastic matrix \(P(q'\mid q)\), rational \(\gamma_p\in[0,1)\), rational \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), a unary horizon \(H\geq1\), and rational additive tolerance \(\epsilon>0\). Let \(L\) be the total binary length of the rational data and bundle descriptions.
A feasible plan splits masses among integral bundles: \(x_t(q,z,b)\geq0\), \(\sum_bx_t(q,z,b)=\mu_t(q,z)\), and \(\sum_{q,z,b}c(q,b)x_t(q,z,b)\leq s\) coordinatewise at each \(t\in\{0,\ldots,H-1\}\). Each individual receives one bundle; supplies renew each period and cannot be stored.
The distribution evolves exactly by \(\mu_{t+1}(q',z')=\sum_{q,z,b:\,z'=\gamma_pz+u(q,b)}P(q'\mid q)x_t(q,z,b)\). Masses sharing the same resulting type are merged; no rounding of memories is part of the model.
Define \(\bar u_t=\sum_{q,z,b}u(q,b)x_t(q,z,b)\) and \(W(\mu)=\min\{z:\mu(q,z)>0\}\). The objective is \(J(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}[(1-\lambda)\bar u_t+\lambda(W(\mu_{t+1})-W(\mu_t))]\).

Can a feasible plan with \(J(x)\geq\sup_{y\text{ feasible}}J(y)-\epsilon\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,|Q|,|B|,H,L,\max\{1,1/\epsilon\}\), or is this additive-approximation problem NP-hard?

For finite-agent discounted memories, a uniform grid of width \(\Delta>0\) needs at most \(\lceil u_{\max}/((1-\gamma_p)\Delta)\rceil\) bins per coordinate, independently of the horizon; this is a discretization bound, not a planning complexity classification. [Kumar and Yeoh](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01154v2)
No result settling this exact distributional mass-flow planning problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one, making supplies and the efficiency term per-capita quantities.
  • Required nonnegative bounded utilities, a positive utility bound, a rational discount factor in [0,1), and initial memories within the invariant discounted-memory interval.
  • Made the omitted fairness weight an input rational in [0,1], following the weighted reward definition rather than fixing an experimental value.
  • Specified explicit bundle and resource-use tables, nonnegative supplies and resource use, and a null bundle to ensure feasibility.
  • Interpreted supplies as renewed per-period upper bounds, allowing unused resources and excluding storage.
  • Specified exact mass splitting, action-independent need transitions after the memory update, and merging of identical resulting types.
  • Encoded the horizon in unary and made the requested accuracy additive, with polynomial dependence on inverse tolerance.
  • Used a supremum to define the optimal benchmark without assuming attainment.

Result

The additive-approximation problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with one resource category, one initial support type, two bundles, deterministic need transitions, and fixed \(\gamma_p=\tfrac12\) and \(\epsilon=\tfrac14\).

Consequently, the stated polynomial running-time guarantee cannot hold unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Source problem and construction

We reduce from PARTITION: given binary-encoded positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), determine whether some subset has sum half their total. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. See [Garey and Johnson, problem SP12](https://perso.limos.fr/~palafour/PAPERS/PDF/Garey-Johnson79.pdf).

We may assume the total is even, writing \(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i=2K\). This restriction preserves hardness: multiplying every input integer by two preserves the answer and makes the total even.

Construct the following planning instance:

  • \(m=1\), \(B=\{0,1\}\), and \(s=\tfrac12\).
  • \(Q=\{q_1,\ldots,q_{n+1}\}\).
  • The need transition is deterministic: \(q_i\) transitions to \(q_{i+1}\) for \(1\leq i\leq n\), and \(q_{n+1}\) is absorbing.
  • The initial distribution puts mass \(1\) on \((q_1,0)\), so \(\tau=1\).
  • \(H=n\), \(\gamma_p=\tfrac12\), \(\lambda=1\), and \(\epsilon=\tfrac14\).
  • For every need type, bundle \(0\) has zero utility and resource use, while \(c(q,1)=1\).
  • For \(1\leq i\leq n\), set

\(u(q_i,1)=2^{n-i}a_i\).
Set \(u(q_{n+1},1)=0\).

  • Set \(u_{\max}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}2^{n-i}a_i\).

All input requirements hold. In particular, the transition matrix is action-independent and stochastic, the null bundle is available, and the initial memory \(0\) belongs to the required interval.

The construction has polynomial encoding length. Each utility \(2^{n-i}a_i\) requires at most \(n\) additional binary digits beyond those of \(a_i\); the transition matrix has \((n+1)^2\) entries; and the unary horizon has length \(n\). Thus \(L\) and every other input parameter are polynomially bounded in the PARTITION input length.

2. Terminal memories are integral subset sums

At period \(i-1\), every individual has need type \(q_i\), irrespective of previous allocations.

Consider any bundle history \(d_1,\ldots,d_n\), where \(d_i\in\{0,1\}\). Let \(z_t\) denote its memory after \(t\) periods. We claim that

\(z_t=2^{n-t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}a_i d_i\), for \(0\leq t\leq n\).

For \(t=0\), both sides are zero. If the formula holds at \(t<n\), then the memory update gives

\(z_{t+1}=\tfrac12z_t+2^{n-(t+1)}a_{t+1}d_{t+1} =2^{n-(t+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}a_i d_i\).

This proves the claim by induction. In particular,

\(z_n=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i d_i\).

Every positive-mass terminal type arises from at least one such history: this follows inductively from the flow equation, because a positive outgoing mass contributes to its corresponding successor type. Merging equal memories does not introduce new memory values.

Therefore, every terminal memory is an integer subset sum of the PARTITION instance, even when allocations split masses.

3. Resource feasibility bounds the mean terminal memory

Fix any feasible plan. Define the mass receiving bundle \(1\) at period \(i-1\) by

\(p_i=\sum_z x_{i-1}(q_i,z,1)\).

Since bundle \(1\) consumes one unit and the supply is \(\tfrac12\), feasibility implies \(0\leq p_i\leq\tfrac12\).

Let \(M_t=\sum_{q,z}z\,\mu_t(q,z)\) be the mean memory. Summing the exact memory-update equation yields

\(M_i=\tfrac12M_{i-1}+2^{n-i}a_i p_i\).

Starting from \(M_0=0\) and iterating this recurrence gives

\(M_n=\sum_{i=1}^n 2^{-(n-i)}2^{n-i}a_i p_i =\sum_{i=1}^n a_i p_i \leq\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^n a_i=K\).

The terminal distribution has total mass \(1\), so its minimum memory cannot exceed its mean. Hence

\(W(\mu_n)\leq M_n\leq K\).

Because \(\lambda=1\), the objective telescopes exactly:

\(J(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}\bigl(W(\mu_{t+1})-W(\mu_t)\bigr) =W(\mu_n)-W(\mu_0)=W(\mu_n)\).

Thus every feasible plan satisfies \(J(x)\leq K\).

4. A partition produces a feasible plan attaining the bound

Suppose a subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i=K\). Its complement \(S^c\) also has sum \(K\).

Assign mass \(\tfrac12\) to each of two bundle histories:

  • The first receives bundle \(1\) exactly at indices in \(S\).
  • The second receives bundle \(1\) exactly at indices in \(S^c\).

At every period, exactly one of these histories receives bundle \(1\). Resource consumption is therefore exactly \(\tfrac12\), satisfying the renewed supply constraint.

To verify that this prescription remains valid under mandatory merging, let \(z_t^S\) and \(z_t^{S^c}\) be the respective memories, and define

\(x_t(q,z,b)= \tfrac12\mathbf{1}_{\{(q,z,b)=(q_{t+1},z_t^S,\mathbf{1}_{\{t+1\in S\}})\}} +\tfrac12\mathbf{1}_{\{(q,z,b)=(q_{t+1},z_t^{S^c},\mathbf{1}_{\{t+1\in S^c\}})\}}\).

Define \(\mu_t\) analogously by omitting the bundle coordinate. Then \(\sum_bx_t(q,z,b)=\mu_t(q,z)\). Each of the two contributions follows the prescribed transition and memory update, so their sum satisfies the exact evolution equation.

If the memories coincide, their masses simply add at the same type; allocating those contributions to different bundles is permitted mass splitting. Thus no persistent individual labels are required.

Both terminal memories equal \(K\). Consequently \(J(x)=K\), and the preceding upper bound proves

\(\sup_{y\text{ feasible}}J(y)=K\)

whenever the PARTITION instance is a yes-instance.

5. Without a partition, every plan loses at least one

Suppose the PARTITION instance is a no-instance. By Section 2, terminal memories are integer subset sums. Since no subset sums to \(K\), no terminal type can have memory \(K\).

Section 3 gives \(W(\mu_n)\leq K\). Since \(W(\mu_n)\) is an integer and cannot equal \(K\), it follows that

\(J(x)=W(\mu_n)\leq K-1\)

for every feasible plan. Therefore,

\(\sup_{y\text{ feasible}}J(y)\leq K-1\).

This is a fixed additive gap, independent of the numerical sizes of the PARTITION weights.

6. An additive approximation decides PARTITION

Suppose the requested polynomial-time algorithm existed. Apply it to the constructed instance with \(\epsilon=\tfrac14\).

For a yes-instance, its output would satisfy

\(J(x)\geq K-\tfrac14\).

Since every feasible objective value in this construction is an integer and at most \(K\), the returned objective must equal \(K\).

For a no-instance, every feasible output satisfies \(J(x)\leq K-1\).

For an explicitly returned flow plan, its objective can be evaluated in polynomial time from the final-period flows:

\(J(x)=\min\{\tfrac12z+u(q_n,b):x_{n-1}(q_n,z,b)>0\}\).

Indeed, these are precisely the positive-mass terminal memories, and merging does not affect their minimum. The returned output length is bounded by the algorithm’s running time. Hence inspecting this minimum decides PARTITION in polynomial time.

The same reduction also works for a policy representation that permits polynomial-time extraction of a positive-flow history. In a yes-instance, the approximation forces the terminal minimum to equal \(K\); the mean bound then forces every positive-mass terminal memory to equal \(K\). Any extracted history therefore supplies a partition certificate.

Thus computing the requested additive approximation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

7. Scope of the result

The hardness does not depend on using the endpoint \(\lambda=1\). For any fixed rational \(\lambda\in(0,1]\), define

\(U_*=\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^n2^{n-i}a_i\).

Resource feasibility gives \(\sum_t\bar u_t\leq U_*\). In a yes-instance, the complementary-history construction attains both this utility bound and terminal minimum \(K\), giving optimum \((1-\lambda)U_*+\lambda K\). In a no-instance, every plan has objective at most \((1-\lambda)U_*+\lambda K-\lambda\). Taking the fixed tolerance \(\epsilon=\lambda/2\) again distinguishes the two cases.

The construction uses utilities whose numerical magnitudes may be exponential, while their binary descriptions remain polynomial. This is allowed by the question’s hypotheses and running-time parameters. The [Kumar–Yeoh](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01154v2) memory boundedness observation does not contradict the reduction: a grid bound depending numerically on \(u_{\max}\) need not be polynomial in its binary encoding length.
