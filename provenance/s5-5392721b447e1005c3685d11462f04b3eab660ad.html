The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), per-capita endowments \(z_t\), liabilities \(\ell_{tu}\), and rational piecewise-linear bankruptcy rules \(d_t\), compute the componentwise least flow \(q^{-}\) satisfying \(q_{tu}=\mu_t d_{tu}(a_t(q))\), where \(a_t(q)=z_t+\frac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_s q_{st}\); determine whether type-by-type maximal affordable updates reach \(q^{-}\) in finitely many rounds and give the exact computational complexity in \(\tau\) and input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a nonnegative rational per-capita endowment \(z_t\) and nonnegative rational liabilities \(\ell_{tu}\) per member toward destination type \(u\), with \(\ell_{tt}=0\). Let \(m\) count positive liability pairs.
Each division rule \(d_t:\mathbb R_{\geq0}\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{T}\) is continuous, componentwise nondecreasing, and explicitly specified by rational breakpoints and affine pieces; for every \(E\geq0\), require \(d_{tu}(E)\leq\ell_{tu}\) and \(\sum_u d_{tu}(E)=\min\{E,\sum_u\ell_{tu}\}\). Let \(K\) be the total number of specified pieces and \(L\) the total input bit length.
For aggregate flows \(0\leq q_{tu}\leq\mu_t\ell_{tu}\), define \(a_t(q)=z_t+\mu_t^{-1}\sum_s q_{st}\). A clearing flow satisfies \(q_{tu}=\mu_t d_{tu}(a_t(q))\); denote its componentwise least solution by \(q^-\).
A maximal-update process starts at \(q=0\). One round selects any type whose row strictly increases under replacement by \(\mu_t d_t(a_t(q))\), performs that replacement, and leaves other rows unchanged; it stops exactly when no such type exists. Selection is unrestricted, without a fairness requirement.
The computational problem is to output \(q^-\) exactly and decide whether every maximal-update process stops at \(q^-\) after finitely many rounds.

Is this combined computational problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is it NP-hard?

In integer networks, every decentralized process making positive affordable feasible updates terminates at the least clearing matrix; this is a finite-convergence result, not a polynomial bit-complexity bound. Even proportional divisible networks admit infinite maximal-update processes. [Csóka–Herings](https://unipub.lib.uni-corvinus.hu/3554/7/Csoka_P.pdf)
Least clearing with monotone piecewise-linear division rules is computable in polynomial time; this settles flow computation but does not classify the finite-termination decision above. [Besting–Hoefer–Huth](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol364-stacs2026/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.14/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.14.pdf)

Result and reduction convention

The combined computational problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions, even using a single oracle call. More precisely, its universal-termination decision component is \(\mathsf{coNP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. Thus a polynomial-time algorithm for the combined problem would imply \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).

The distinction matters: the reduction below maps a yes instance of Subset Sum to a no instance of universal termination. I do not claim many-one \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness of the universal-termination language.

The hardness holds even for societies with a unique clearing flow that is explicitly computable.

1. Every finite terminal process ends at the least clearing flow

Write \(F(q)_{tu}=\mu_t d_{tu}(a_t(q))\). The map \(F\) is continuous and componentwise nondecreasing, and maps the liability box into itself.

For completeness, iteration of \(F\) from zero is increasing and bounded. Its componentwise limit is a fixed point by continuity. Every fixed point dominates every iterate, so this limit is the least fixed point \(q^-\).

Every state reachable by maximal updates satisfies

\(q\leq F(q)\quad\text{and}\quad q\leq q^-.\)

Both inequalities hold initially. Suppose they hold at \(q\), and an update replaces row \(t\), producing \(q'\). Then \(q'\geq q\). For an unchanged row \(s\),

\(q'_s=q_s\leq F_s(q)\leq F_s(q').\)

For the updated row,

\(q'_t=F_t(q)\leq F_t(q').\)

Also, monotonicity and \(q\leq q^-\) give \(q'_t=F_t(q)\leq F_t(q^-)=q^-_t\); the unchanged rows remain below \(q^-\).

At termination, the inequality \(q\leq F(q)\), together with the absence of any strictly increasing row replacement, implies \(q=F(q)\). Since \(q^-\) is least and \(q\leq q^-\), necessarily \(q=q^-\).

Consequently, the decision question reduces to whether any infinite legal update process exists.

2. Subset Sum and the society construction

We reduce from binary-encoded positive-integer Subset Sum, whose \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness is standard; see the [Cornell lecture notes](https://courses.cs.cornell.edu/cs4820/2018fa/lectures/subset_sum.pdf).

Let the input consist of positive integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) and a positive target \(B\). Put \(W=\sum_i w_i\). We may assume \(B<W\): appending an item of weight \(B+1\) enforces this inequality without changing whether a subset sums to \(B\).

Create source types \(S_1,\ldots,S_n\), feedback types \(A,B_0\), and a sink \(D\). Choose the smallest power of two \(N\) with \(N\geq n+3\), and add inert types until the support has size \(N\). Every type has mass \(1/N\). Thus every mass is positive and dyadic, and the masses sum to one.

All unspecified endowments, liabilities, and division-rule coordinates are zero.

Sources. Type \(S_i\) has endowment \(w_i\), its sole liability is \(\ell_{S_iA}=w_i\), and

\(d_{S_iA}(E)=\min\{E,w_i\}.\)

Feedback type \(B_0\). Its sole liability is \(\ell_{B_0A}=1/2\), and

\(d_{B_0A}(E)=\min\{E,1/2\}.\)

Feedback type \(A\). Set \(h=B-1/2\) and \(R=W+1\). Its liabilities are

\(\ell_{AB_0}=1/2,\qquad \ell_{AD}=R-1/2.\)

Define

\(f(E)=\min\left\{\frac12,\frac12\max\{E-h,0\}\right\},\qquad g(E)=\min\{E,R\}-f(E),\)

and set \(d_{AB_0}=f\), \(d_{AD}=g\).

The sink and inert types have zero division rules.

Verification of the division-rule hypotheses

Since \(B\geq1\), we have \(h>0\). Since \(W>B\), we have \(h+1=B+1/2<R\).

The function \(f\) is continuous, with slopes \(0,1/2,0\) on the intervals separated by \(h\) and \(h+1\). It takes values in \([0,1/2]\).

The function \(g\) is continuous, and its slopes on the four intervals separated by \(h,h+1,R\) are respectively \(1,1/2,1,0\). Therefore \(g\) is nonnegative and nondecreasing. Its maximum is \(g(R)=R-1/2\).

Moreover,

\(f(E)+g(E)=\min\{E,R\}=\min\{E,\ell_{AB_0}+\ell_{AD}\}.\)

Thus all division rules satisfy every stated hypothesis. All breakpoints and affine coefficients are rational, and there are no self-liabilities.

The construction has \(\tau=N=O(n)\), \(m=n+3\), and \(K=O(n)\). Its total encoding length is polynomial in the Subset Sum input length: the magnitude of \(W\) contributes only its binary bit length.

3. Exact update dynamics

A source has no incoming liabilities. Its first update pays \(w_i/N\) to \(A\), and it can never update again.

At any reachable state, define

\(b=N\sum_i q_{S_iA},\qquad x=Nq_{AB_0},\qquad y=Nq_{B_0A},\qquad p=Nq_{AD}.\)

The value \(b\) is exactly the sum of the weights of the sources already updated. In particular, \(b\) is an integer. Also, \(0\leq x,y\leq1/2\).

Because the masses are equal,

\(a_A=b+y,\qquad a_{B_0}=x.\)

An update of \(A\) therefore performs the simultaneous replacement

\((x,p)\leftarrow(f(b+y),g(b+y)),\)

whereas an update of \(B_0\) performs \(y\leftarrow x\).

These equations account for the entire nontrivial dynamics.

4. A target subset produces an infinite process

Suppose some subset \(I\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}w_i=B\).

First update exactly those sources. Every such update is strict. Then \(b=B\), while \(x=y=p=0\).

Now alternate updates of \(A\) and \(B_0\) forever, leaving the remaining sources unselected. Let \(x_k\) denote \(x\) after the \(k\)-th update of \(A\), with \(x_0=0\). Immediately before this update, \(y=x_{k-1}\). Since \(x_{k-1}<1/2\),

\(x_k=f(B+x_{k-1})=\frac14+\frac12x_{k-1}.\)

Induction gives

\(x_k=\frac12(1-2^{-k}).\)

Thus \(x_k>x_{k-1}\) for every positive integer \(k\), while \(x_k<1/2\).

Every update of \(A\) strictly increases its payment to \(B_0\); its other payment cannot decrease by monotonicity. Every following update of \(B_0\) strictly increases \(y\) from \(x_{k-1}\) to \(x_k\).

Hence this is an infinite legal maximal-update process. Leaving other enabled sources unselected is permitted because fairness is not required.

5. Without a target subset, every process terminates

Suppose no subset sums to \(B\). Assume, for contradiction, that an infinite legal process exists.

Each source can update at most once. Hence, after some finite round, no source updates again. On this infinite tail, \(b\) is a constant integer subset sum different from \(B\). Only \(A\) and \(B_0\) can update.

There are two cases.

Case \(b\leq B-1\). Since \(y\leq1/2\),

\(b+y\leq B-1/2=h,\)

so \(f(b+y)=0\). The invariant from Section 1 gives \(x\leq f(b+y)=0\), hence \(x=0\). The same invariant for \(B_0\) gives \(y\leq x=0\).

Thus \(B_0\) cannot update. The input of \(A\) is the constant \(b\), so \(A\) can update at most once. This tail cannot be infinite.

Case \(b\geq B+1\). For every \(y\geq0\),

\(b+y\geq B+1>h+1,\)

so \(f(b+y)=1/2\).

Before the first update of \(A\) on this tail, \(x\) is constant, so \(B_0\) can update at most once. Any update of \(A\) sets \(x=1/2\) permanently. Thereafter \(B_0\) can update at most once, setting \(y=1/2\) permanently. Once \(y\) is fixed, \(A\)'s complete replacement row is fixed, so \(A\) can update at most once more.

Again, the tail cannot be infinite.

Both cases contradict the assumed infinite process. Therefore every maximal-update process terminates, and Section 1 shows that it terminates at \(q^-\).

We have proved the exact equivalence:

\(\exists I:\sum_{i\in I}w_i=B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{there exists an infinite maximal-update process}.\)

6. Clearing is explicit in the constructed instances

At any clearing flow, every source necessarily pays \(w_i/N\), so \(b=W\). Since \(W\geq B+1\), the clearing equations force \(x=1/2\) and then \(y=1/2\).

Furthermore, \(W+1/2<R\), so

\(p=g(W+1/2)=W.\)

Thus the constructed society has a unique clearing flow, namely

\(q^-_{S_iA}=w_i/N,\qquad q^-_{AB_0}=q^-_{B_0A}=1/(2N),\qquad q^-_{AD}=W/N,\)

with all other entries zero. Its exact computation is therefore immediate on this family.

For general inputs, the known least-clearing algorithm applies after replacing each type by a bank with aggregate endowment \(\mu_tz_t\), liabilities \(\mu_t\ell_{tu}\), and payment functions \(P_{tu}(X)=\mu_t d_{tu}(X/\mu_t)\). These functions preserve continuity, monotonicity, piecewise affinity, and the budget identity. This verifies the model correspondence with [Besting–Hoefer–Huth, Theorem 8](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol364-stacs2026/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.14/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.14.pdf).

7. Computational consequence

The construction is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from Subset Sum to the complement of universal termination. Equivalently, it reduces the complement of Subset Sum to universal termination, proving \(\mathsf{coNP}\)-hardness of that decision component.

An algorithm for the combined task solves Subset Sum with one call: construct this society, obtain its termination Boolean, and negate that Boolean. This proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness under polynomial-time Turing reductions and excludes polynomial-time solvability unless \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).
