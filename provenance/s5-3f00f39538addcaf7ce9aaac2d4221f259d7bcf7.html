The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given supported applicant types \(X\) and slot types \(Y\), binary-rational masses \(\mu_x,\nu_y\), type rankings, acceptable type pairs \(E\), and socially connected pairs \(H\subseteq E\), choose match masses \(z_{xy}\geq 0\) subject to \(\sum_y z_{xy}\leq\mu_x\) and \(\sum_x z_{xy}\leq\nu_y\). For each \((x,y)\in H\), let \(d_x(y)=\mu_x-\sum_{y':\,y'\succeq_x y}z_{xy'}\) and \(d_y(x)=\nu_y-\sum_{x':\,x'\succeq_y x}z_{x'y}\); require \(d_x(y)=0\) or \(d_y(x)=0\), and maximize \(\sum_{(x,y)\in E}z_{xy}\). Is this optimum computable in time polynomial in the number of supported types and input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be disjoint sets of applicant and slot types. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|X|+|Y|\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_x,\nu_y\). Masses need not sum to one, and the two sides may have different total masses. Set \(m=|Y|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Let \(E\subseteq X\times Y\) be the mutually acceptable pairs and \(H\subseteq E\) the socially connected pairs. Each applicant type has at most two acceptable slot types; slot lists have no length bound. Each type has a total preorder on its acceptable neighbors, allowing ties on both sides; every acceptable partner is strictly preferred to being unmatched. Write \(\succeq_v\) for weak preference, retaining ties without breaking them.
A matching assigns real masses \(z_{xy}\geq0\) to pairs in \(E\), with \(\sum_{y:(x,y)\in E}z_{xy}\leq\mu_x\) and \(\sum_{x:(x,y)\in E}z_{xy}\leq\nu_y\).
For \((x,y)\in H\), define \(d_x(y)=\mu_x-\sum_{y':(x,y')\in E,\ y'\succeq_x y}z_{xy'}\) and \(d_y(x)=\nu_y-\sum_{x':(x',y)\in E,\ x'\succeq_y x}z_{x'y}\). Call the matching weakly socially stable precisely when \(d_x(y)=0\) or \(d_y(x)=0\) for every \((x,y)\in H\). Its size is \(\sum_{(x,y)\in E}z_{xy}\).

Is maximum-size weakly socially stable mass matching computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard (for deciding whether size at least an input binary-rational threshold \(q\geq0\) is attainable)?

The discrete counterpart with applicant lists of length at most two and ties on both sides has a published \(O(n^{3/2}\log n)\) algorithmic claim, where \(n\) counts individual agents ([Shrivastava–Rangan](https://aircconline.com/ijcsit/V8N1/8116ijcsit06.pdf)); its [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.03523) was subsequently withdrawn due to a change in algorithm.
For unit-capacity bipartite ordinal fractional matching with every acceptable pair able to block, maximizing the number of fully matched agents is polynomial-time solvable without ties and NP-complete with ties ([Chen–Roy–Sorge](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0013.pdf)); these results do not classify the stated mass objective with the applicant-list bound and social restrictions. No result settling this exact formulation was identified.

Classification

The threshold decision problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete. In particular, maximum-size weakly socially stable mass matching is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard.

Hardness holds even when all masses are positive integers, both sides have strict preferences, and the social graph $H$ is a matching. The reduction below applies directly to real-valued assignments; it does not assume integrality.

1. Source problem and construction

We reduce from binary-encoded SUBSET SUM: given positive integers $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ and an integer target $T$, decide whether some index set $I$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{i\in I}a_i=T. $$
We use the standard $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness of this problem. See [Koiliaris–Xu, *Subset Sum Made Simple*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08248).

Write
$$ S=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i. $$
We may restrict to instances with $0<T<S$. Indeed, targets outside this range are immediately decidable: $T=0$ and $T=S$ are yes-instances, while $T<0$ and $T>S$ are no-instances. A reduction to the restricted problem can replace these cases by fixed restricted yes- or no-instances.

For each index $i$, introduce three applicant types $c_i,f_i,h_i$, and one private slot type $y_i$. Introduce two additional slot types $U,V$.

The masses and applicant preferences are:

| Applicant type | Mass | Strict preference list |
|---|---:|---|
| $c_i$ | $a_i$ | $y_i\succ_{c_i}U$ |
| $f_i$ | $a_i$ | $y_i$ |
| $h_i$ | $2a_i$ | $V\succ_{h_i}y_i$ |

The slot masses are
$$ \nu_{y_i}=2a_i,\qquad \nu_U=T,\qquad \nu_V=2(S-T). $$
Every mass is positive.

Slot $y_i$ has the strict order
$$ h_i\succ_{y_i}c_i\succ_{y_i}f_i. $$
Slot $U$ accepts exactly $c_1,\ldots,c_n$, in that strict order. Slot $V$ accepts exactly $h_1,\ldots,h_n$, in that strict order.

These lists define $E$. Set
$$ H=\{(c_i,y_i):1\le i\le n\}. $$
Thus every applicant has at most two acceptable slots, and the social pairs have distinct applicant and slot endpoints.

The decision threshold is the total slot mass:
$$ q=\sum_{y\in Y}\nu_y =2S+T+2(S-T) =4S-T. $$
Every feasible matching has size at most $q$. Consequently, attaining size at least $q$ is equivalent to filling every slot completely.

2. A subset-sum solution gives a stable matching of size $q$

Suppose $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{i\in I}a_i=T. $$
Define the following assignments, with every unlisted assignment zero.

For $i\in I$, assign
$$ z_{c_iU}=a_i,\qquad z_{h_iy_i}=2a_i. $$
Applicant $f_i$ remains unmatched.

For $i\notin I$, assign
$$ z_{c_iy_i}=a_i,\qquad z_{f_iy_i}=a_i,\qquad z_{h_iV}=2a_i. $$

Every applicant capacity is respected, and each private slot receives exactly $2a_i$. The common slots receive
$$ \sum_i z_{c_iU}=\sum_{i\in I}a_i=T $$
and
$$ \sum_i z_{h_iV} =2\sum_{i\notin I}a_i =2(S-T). $$
Hence all slots are full and the matching has size $q$.

To verify social stability, consider $(c_i,y_i)\in H$.

If $i\notin I$, applicant $c_i$ is fully assigned to its first choice, so
$$ d_{c_i}(y_i)=a_i-z_{c_iy_i}=0. $$
If $i\in I$, slot $y_i$ is filled entirely by $h_i$, whom it strictly prefers to $c_i$. Thus
$$ d_{y_i}(c_i) =2a_i-z_{h_iy_i}-z_{c_iy_i} =0. $$
Every social pair therefore satisfies the required disjunction.

3. Any stable matching of size $q$ gives a subset-sum solution

Now suppose a weakly socially stable matching has size at least $q$. As observed above, its size is exactly $q$ and every slot is full.

For each $i$, write
$$ p_i=z_{c_iy_i},\qquad u_i=z_{c_iU},\qquad w_i=z_{h_iy_i},\qquad v_i=z_{h_iV}. $$
Applicant feasibility gives
$$ p_i+u_i\le a_i,\qquad w_i+v_i\le 2a_i. $$
Fullness of $U$ and $V$ gives
$$ \sum_i u_i=T,\qquad \sum_i v_i=2(S-T). $$
Summing the capacities of the $h_i$ applicants consequently yields
$$ \sum_i w_i \le 2S-\sum_i v_i =2T. $$

Define
$$ I=\{i:p_i<a_i\}. $$
For every $i\in I$, the applicant deficit on the social pair is positive:
$$ d_{c_i}(y_i)=a_i-p_i>0. $$
Social stability therefore requires the slot deficit to vanish. Its weak preference prefix through $c_i$ consists exactly of $h_i,c_i$, so
$$ d_{y_i}(c_i)=2a_i-w_i-p_i=0. $$
It follows that
$$ w_i=2a_i-p_i \ge a_i+u_i, $$
where the inequality uses $p_i+u_i\le a_i$.

If $i\notin I$, then $p_i=a_i$, and applicant feasibility forces $u_i=0$. Consequently,
$$ \sum_{i\in I}u_i=T. $$
We now obtain
$$ 2T \ge \sum_i w_i \ge \sum_{i\in I}w_i \ge \sum_{i\in I}(a_i+u_i) =\sum_{i\in I}a_i+T. $$
Therefore
$$ \sum_{i\in I}a_i\le T. $$
On the other hand, $u_i\le a_i$ gives
$$ T=\sum_{i\in I}u_i\le\sum_{i\in I}a_i. $$
Together these inequalities prove
$$ \sum_{i\in I}a_i=T. $$

This argument permits arbitrary real assignments. Moreover, equality forces $u_i=a_i$ for every $i\in I$, because the nonnegative quantities $a_i-u_i$ sum to zero. Then $p_i=0$. Thus attaining the threshold forces each selected controller to move its entire mass to $U$.

We have proved the equivalence
$$ \text{the SUBSET SUM instance is a yes-instance} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{size at least }q\text{ is attainable}. $$

4. Encoding size and hardness consequence

The constructed instance has
$$ m=n+2,\qquad \tau=4n+2,\qquad |E|=5n,\qquad |H|=n. $$
It introduces only the integers $a_i$, $2a_i$, $T$, $2(S-T)$, and $4S-T$. Their binary encodings have polynomial length in the SUBSET SUM input length.

More precisely, if the source input has bit length $N$, a conventional indexed encoding gives
$$ L=O\!\left(N+n\log(n+1)\right). $$
The sums, masses, and preference lists can all be constructed in time polynomial in $N$.

Hence this is a polynomial-time many-one reduction. A maximum-size algorithm running in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ would decide SUBSET SUM in polynomial time. Such an algorithm therefore cannot exist unless $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$.

The reduction establishes hardness for binary-encoded integer masses; it does not establish strong $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness.

5. Membership in $\mathrm{NP}$ for the full rational formulation

For completeness, we prove membership for the stated problem, including ties and arbitrary positive binary-rational masses.

Let $b_x=\mu_x$ and $b_y=\nu_y$. For a type $v$ and an acceptable neighbor $u$, define its weak preference prefix
$$ A_v(u)=\{u'\in N(v):u'\succeq_v u\}. $$
Feasibility and nonnegativity imply
$$ \sum_{u'\in A_v(u)}z_{vu'}=b_v $$
if and only if $v$ is fully matched and every assignment outside $A_v(u)$ is zero. Indeed, the total incident mass cannot exceed $b_v$, so saturation of the prefix leaves no mass available outside it.

A certificate chooses one endpoint of each social pair. The verifier requires the deficit at that chosen endpoint to vanish. Every stable matching admits such a choice, and any matching satisfying all the chosen requirements is socially stable.

For each type, intersect all prefixes required by the certificate. If at least one prefix is required, the type must be full and its assignments must lie within that intersection. If none is required, impose neither restriction.

Construct a directed network with source $s$, sink $t$, and all type vertices:

  • The arc $s\to x$ has upper bound $\mu_x$, and lower bound $\mu_x$ if applicant $x$ must be full, otherwise zero.
  • Each permitted pair has an arc $x\to y$ with lower bound zero and upper bound $\min(\mu_x,\nu_y)$. A pair is permitted precisely when it obeys all prefix restrictions at both endpoints.
  • The arc $y\to t$ has upper bound $\nu_y$, and lower bound $\nu_y$ if slot $y$ must be full, otherwise zero.

Flow conservation shows that feasible flows correspond exactly to matchings satisfying the certificate. Flow value is matching size.

Here are the lower-bound implementation details. Scale all masses by the product $D$ of their input denominators. Every bound becomes an integer. Add a return arc $t\to s$ with lower bound zero and upper bound
$$ D\sum_{x\in X}\mu_x. $$
This upper bound is sufficient for every feasible matching.

For each arc, subtract its lower bound from its capacity. At vertex $v$, put
$$ \delta(v)= \sum_{\text{arcs entering }v}\ell_e - \sum_{\text{arcs leaving }v}\ell_e, $$
where $\ell_e$ denotes the lower bound. Add an auxiliary source $s'$ with an arc $s'\to v$ of capacity $\delta(v)$ when $\delta(v)>0$, and an auxiliary sink $t'$ with an arc $v\to t'$ of capacity $-\delta(v)$ when $\delta(v)<0$.

A feasible circulation exists exactly when a maximum $s'$-to-$t'$ flow saturates every arc leaving $s'$. This follows by restoring the lower bounds: conservation then holds precisely when the added flow has net outflow $\delta(v)$ at each original vertex. The total positive and negative demands agree because $\sum_v\delta(v)=0$.

After finding a feasible circulation, remove the auxiliary vertices, their arcs, and the return arc. Maximize the remaining feasible $s$-to-$t$ flow using its residual network, with backward residual capacity equal to current flow minus the lower bound. This preserves every lower and upper bound. When no augmenting path remains, the usual reachable-set cut bound proves maximality.

The verifier accepts exactly when this maximum flow, divided by $D$, is at least $q$.

Because applicant degree is at most two, both networks have $O(\tau)$ vertices and arcs. The integer capacities have
$$ O\!\left(L+\log(\tau+2)\right) $$
bits. Using Edmonds–Karp for the feasibility and maximization calls gives the conservative bit-time bound
$$ O\!\left( L^3+\tau^3\bigl(L+\log(\tau+2)\bigr) \right). $$
The certificate uses $|H|\le 2|X|\le 2\tau$ bits. Thus verification is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$, proving membership in $\mathrm{NP}$.

Combining this with the reduction proves the claimed $\mathrm{NP}$-completeness.
