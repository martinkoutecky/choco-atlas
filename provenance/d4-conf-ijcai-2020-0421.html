The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) binary issues and a distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) complete conditional approval ballot types, with rational masses summing to \(1\), suppose each supported type has maximum dependency in-degree at most \(1\) and the undirected union of their dependency graphs has treewidth at most \(2\). Find an assignment \(s\in\{0,1\}^m\) minimizing \(\sum_t \mu_t\delta_t(s)\), where \(\delta_t(s)\) is the number of issues on which type \(t\) is dissatisfied.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq 1\) binary issues. A ballot type \(t\) specifies a loopless directed dependency graph \(G_t\) on the issues, with maximum in-degree at most \(1\); directed cycles are allowed. Write \(P_{t,j}\) for the predecessors of issue \(j\).
For every issue \(j\) and every assignment \(a\in\{0,1\}^{P_{t,j}}\), the type explicitly specifies an approval set \(A_{t,j}(a)\subseteq\{0,1\}\). Empty approval sets and approval of both values are allowed; if \(P_{t,j}\) is empty, there is one unconditional approval set.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass \(\mu_t\) as a binary rational, where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
The undirected graph obtained by taking the union of all supported dependency graphs and forgetting edge orientations has treewidth at most \(2\).
For a common assignment \(s\in\{0,1\}^m\), define \(\delta_t(s)=|\{j:s_j\notin A_{t,j}(s|_{P_{t,j}})\}|\). The optimization problem is to return any assignment minimizing \(\sum_t\mu_t\delta_t(s)\); ties may be resolved arbitrarily.

Is computing such a minimizing assignment solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite unweighted electorates under these same graph restrictions, Conditional Minisum is exactly solvable in polynomial time (Markakis and Papasotiropoulos, 2020). The stated discrete result does not explicitly classify the formulation with support-encoded rational masses.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted complete ballots as explicitly specifying an approval set for every predecessor assignment, allowing empty sets and approval of both values as in the discrete definition.
  • Made dependency graphs loopless and allowed directed cycles; no acyclicity restriction is imposed by the anchor.
  • Allowed any minimizing assignment to be returned, since no prescribed tie-breaking rule is part of the optimization problem.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length and took m and the support size to be positive integers.

Result

Computing a minimizing assignment is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), including when the dependency graphs contain directed cycles and the masses are support-encoded binary rationals.

The unweighted result appears as Theorem 3 of [Markakis and Papasotiropoulos (2020)](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2020/0043.pdf). The proof below directly handles rational masses and gives an explicit polynomial bit-complexity bound.

1. Expressing the objective with unary and binary factors

Let \(V=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), and let \(H\) be the undirected union of the supported dependency graphs. By hypothesis, \(H\) has treewidth at most \(2\).

For each supported type \(t\) and issue \(j\), define a dissatisfaction indicator \(d_{t,j}\).

  • If \(P_{t,j}=\varnothing\), define \(d_{t,j}(b)=1\) when \(b\notin A_{t,j}(\varnothing)\), and \(d_{t,j}(b)=0\) otherwise.
  • If \(P_{t,j}=\{i\}\), define \(d_{t,j}(a,b)=1\) when \(b\notin A_{t,j}(a)\), and \(d_{t,j}(a,b)=0\) otherwise.

The in-degree bound ensures that these are all cases. Thus every term involves either one issue or two issues joined by an edge of \(H\), and

\(\displaystyle F(s):=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\delta_t(s)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mu_t d_{t,j}\bigl(s|_{P_{t,j}\cup\{j\}}\bigr).\)

Empty approval sets and approval of both values simply produce valid indicator tables. Directed cycles cause no difficulty: every indicator is evaluated on the common assignment \(s\), without requiring a sequential evaluation of issues.

2. Clearing denominators preserves polynomial bit length

Write each mass as \(\mu_t=p_t/q_t\), with positive integers \(p_t,q_t\). Reduction to lowest terms is unnecessary. Define

\(\displaystyle D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}q_t,\qquad w_t=p_t(D/q_t).\)

Then \(w_t\) is a positive integer, \(\sum_t w_t=D\), and

\(\displaystyle C(s):=D F(s)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_t d_{t,j}\bigl(s|_{P_{t,j}\cup\{j\}}\bigr).\)

Since \(D>0\), the minimizing assignments of \(C\) and \(F\) coincide.

The sum of the encoded denominator lengths is at most \(L\), so \(D\) has at most \(L+1\) bits. Furthermore, \(w_t\leq D\), because the positive masses sum to \(1\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle 0\leq C(s)\leq mD.\)

Aggregate all contributions with the same scope into one table. This gives a representation

\(\displaystyle C(s)=\sum_{j\in V}U_j(s_j)+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(H)}Q_{ij}(s_i,s_j),\)

where each unary table has two entries and each binary table has four entries. When contributions use opposite edge orientations, their arguments are reordered into the same fixed vertex order before aggregation.

Thus the problem is exact minimization of a sum of integer unary and binary tables on \(H\).

3. Constructing an elimination order of width at most two

We prove that the promised graph permits the following greedy procedure: repeatedly choose a vertex of degree at most \(2\), join its two neighbors if necessary, and delete the vertex.

A tree decomposition consists of a tree of vertex bags such that every vertex occurs in a bag, every edge has both endpoints in some bag, and the bags containing any fixed vertex form a connected subtree. Its width is the largest bag size minus one.

Lemma 1. Every nonempty graph of treewidth at most \(2\) has a vertex of degree at most \(2\).

Proof. Take a tree decomposition whose bags have size at most \(3\). Repeatedly remove any leaf bag contained in its neighboring bag. This preserves the decomposition properties.

If only one bag remains, the graph has at most three vertices, so every vertex has degree at most \(2\).

Otherwise, choose a leaf bag \(B\) with neighboring bag \(B'\). Since \(B\not\subseteq B'\), choose \(v\in B\setminus B'\). The connectedness condition implies that \(v\) occurs only in \(B\). Every neighbor of \(v\) must therefore belong to \(B\), by the edge-covering condition. Hence \(\deg(v)\leq |B|-1\leq2\). \(\square\)

Lemma 2. Deleting a vertex of degree at most \(2\) and joining its two neighbors, if it has two, preserves treewidth at most \(2\).

Proof. For degree zero or one, the operation is vertex deletion.

If \(v\) has exactly two neighbors \(a,b\), the resulting graph is obtained by contracting the edge \(va\): contraction deletes \(v\), identifies it with \(a\), and adds precisely the potentially missing edge \(ab\).

For completeness, neither deletion nor contraction increases treewidth. Deletion is implemented by removing the deleted vertex from every bag. For contraction of an edge \(xy\), replace \(x,y\) by the merged vertex in every bag. Bag sizes do not increase. The bags containing the merged vertex are connected because the subtrees for \(x\) and \(y\) intersect in a bag covering \(xy\). Vertex and edge coverage are preserved. \(\square\)

Together, the lemmas show that greedy elimination always succeeds. Let its order be \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\), and let \(N_i\) be the neighbors of \(v_i\) immediately before its elimination. Then \(|N_i|\leq2\), and every vertex in \(N_i\) occurs later in the order.

An adjacency-matrix implementation can construct this order using \(O(m^3)\) elementary graph operations by recomputing degrees at each step.

4. Exact elimination and recovery of an assignment

Maintain a constant and unary or binary tables on the remaining variables. Every binary table has its scope on an edge of the current elimination graph.

When eliminating \(v_i\), let \(\mathcal F_i\) be the current tables whose scopes contain \(v_i\). Every other variable appearing in these tables belongs to \(N_i\). For each assignment \(a\in\{0,1\}^{N_i}\), calculate

\(\displaystyle h_i(a)=\min_{b\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal F_i}f\bigl((b,a)|_{\operatorname{scope}(f)}\bigr).\)

Here \((b,a)\) assigns value \(b\) to \(v_i\) and assigns the values specified by \(a\) to \(N_i\). Store one minimizing value \(b_i(a)\), choosing either value in a tie.

Remove the tables in \(\mathcal F_i\) and insert \(h_i\), merging it with an existing table of the same scope if necessary. If \(N_i=\varnothing\), add \(h_i\) to the maintained constant.

Because \(|N_i|\leq2\), the new table has at most four entries. If its scope has two vertices, the fill operation has joined those vertices, preserving the graph invariant.

Correctness invariant. After eliminating \(v_1,\ldots,v_{i-1}\), the maintained function on the remaining variables equals the minimum of the original \(C\) over those eliminated variables.

This is true initially. For the inductive step, fix an assignment of all remaining variables other than \(v_i\). Tables outside \(\mathcal F_i\) do not depend on \(v_i\), so

\(\displaystyle \min_{s_{v_i}}\left(\sum_{f\notin\mathcal F_i}f+\sum_{f\in\mathcal F_i}f\right)=\sum_{f\notin\mathcal F_i}f+h_i.\)

This is exactly the replacement performed by the algorithm, proving the invariant. Once all variables have been eliminated, the remaining constant is therefore \(\min_s C(s)\).

To recover an assignment, process the vertices in reverse order. When processing \(v_i\), every vertex in \(N_i\) has already received a value. Set

\(\displaystyle s_{v_i}=b_i(s|_{N_i}).\)

Each stored choice attains the corresponding elimination minimum. Reversing the elimination equalities therefore gives \(C(s)=\min_x C(x)\). This assignment also minimizes \(F\).

5. Bit complexity and conclusion

It remains to bound the sizes of intermediate integers.

Initially, the sum of the maximum entries of all tables is at most

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_t=mD.\)

Aggregation preserves this bound because the maximum of a sum is at most the sum of the maxima. During elimination,

\(\displaystyle \max_a h_i(a)\leq\sum_{f\in\mathcal F_i}\max_x f(x).\)

Hence replacing the consumed tables by \(h_i\), and merging tables, cannot increase the sum of their maximum entries, including the maintained constant. All entries are nonnegative. Every intermediate integer is consequently at most \(mD\).

Set \(B=L+\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil+3\). Every cost entry and arithmetic operand can be stored with \(O(B)\) bits.

Using elementary integer arithmetic:

  • Computing \(D\) and the weights takes \(O(\tau B^2)\) bit operations.
  • Constructing and aggregating the local tables takes \(O(m\tau B^2)\) bit operations, with this conservative bound including index handling.
  • Graph elimination takes \(O(m^3B^2)\) bit operations under a conservative accounting.
  • Once tables are aggregated, an eliminated vertex has at most one unary and two incident binary tables. Each elimination therefore requires only constantly many additions and comparisons on \(O(B)\)-bit integers.

A valid overall running-time bound is thus

\(\displaystyle O\!\left((m^3+m\tau+\tau)\bigl(L+\log_2(m+1)+3\bigr)^2\right).\)

The algorithm works directly with the \(\tau\) supported types. Although \(D\) may have exponentially large numerical value, it has only polynomially many bits, and no expansion into an unweighted electorate is needed.

Therefore the stated support-encoded rational-mass optimization problem admits an exact polynomial-time algorithm.
