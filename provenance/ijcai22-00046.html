The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite male and female type sets \(A,B\), rational masses \(p,q\), strict rankings for every type, and a designated accomplice type \(a^\star\), run batch deferred acceptance with one common alternative ranking \(\rho\) for all mass of \(a^\star\). Decide whether \(\rho\) can leave the accomplice type's partner distribution first-order stochastically no worse while making every female type's partner distribution weakly no worse, and compute a report inducing a greatest feasible female outcome when one exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be nonempty finite male and female type sets, with positive binary-rational masses \(p_a,q_b\) satisfying \(\sum_a p_a=\sum_b q_b=1\). Every type has a strict total ranking of all opposite-side types, all acceptable. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) positive-mass types and their masses, not the universe of possible types. Set \(m=\max\{|A|,|B|\}\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Batch male-proposing deferred acceptance starts with all male mass proposing to its first choice. In each synchronous round, each female type \(b\) pools new proposals with held mass, retains its most-preferred mass up to capacity \(q_b\), splitting a male type's mass at the cutoff if necessary, and rejects the remainder. Each rejected portion proposes next round to the next female type in its report; held portions wait. Stop when no proposals remain. Portions of the same male type are interchangeable.
Write \(x^\rho_{ab}\) for the resulting matched mass when all mass of a designated \(a^\star\in A\) uses one permutation \(\rho\) of \(B\), while every other type reports truthfully; write \(x^0\) for the truthful outcome. Partner distributions are \(x^\rho_{ab}/p_a\) for male types and \(x^\rho_{ab}/q_b\) for female types. Compare them by first-order stochastic dominance under true rankings.
A report is feasible if the accomplice's distribution and every female type's distribution weakly dominate their truthful distributions. The truthful report is permitted; strict improvement is not required. A greatest feasible female outcome is induced by a feasible report and weakly dominates, for every female type, the outcome of every feasible report.

Can feasibility and existence of a greatest feasible female outcome be decided, and a report inducing that outcome computed whenever it exists, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this continuous problem NP-hard?

For finite markets with \(n\) men and \(n\) women and strict complete preferences, a greatest no-regret one-for-all outcome always exists, and an inducing report is computable in \(O(n^3)\) time ([verified source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.08774)). No result settling the mass-based problem defined above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified positive supported masses and normalized each side to total mass one, preserving the discrete model's balanced market.
  • Specified strict complete rankings with every opposite-side type acceptable, following the discrete preference domain.
  • Defined batch deferred acceptance through synchronous divisible proposals, capacity-based retention, partial rejection, and advancement of rejected portions; within-type portions are interchangeable.
  • Specified normalized partner distributions and evaluation under true rankings.
  • Permitted the truthful permutation and retained weak improvement without requiring strict improvement, consistent with the anchor's optimal-strategy formulation and the graded question.
  • Defined greatest relative to all feasible reports and included deciding its existence, since the graded question does not guarantee existence.
  • Defined m as the larger side's support size, tau as total support size, and L as total input bit length.

1. Definitions and elementary properties

Write $r=|A|$ and $s=|B|$. A complete allocation is a nonnegative matrix $x$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{b\in B}x_{ab}=p_a,\qquad \sum_{a\in A}x_{ab}=q_b. $$

For a specified preference profile, an allocation is stable if no pair $(a,b)$ satisfies both
$$ \sum_{c:\,b\succ_a c}x_{ac}>0, \qquad \sum_{d:\,a\succ_b d}x_{db}>0. $$
Thus both endpoints of a blocking pair must have positive mass assigned to strictly worse types.

For a male ranking $b_1\succ_a\cdots\succ_a b_s$, define
$$ C_a^x(j)=\sum_{i=1}^{j}x_{ab_i},\qquad C_a^x(0)=0. $$
Female prefix masses are defined analogously. Since each normalization factor $p_a$ or $q_b$ is fixed and positive, stochastic dominance is equivalent to comparison of these unnormalized prefix masses.

Choose a common denominator $D$ for all input masses. Multiplying the encoded denominators gives a choice with $\log D=O(L)$. Every mass occurring in DA remains a multiple of $1/D$.

For termination, label the total mass as $D$ units. Each unit proposes to each female type at most once, so there are at most $Ds$ unit proposals. Hence synchronous DA terminates.

Its terminal allocation is complete. Indeed, an unmatched unit would have been rejected by every female type. Each rejecting female type was full at rejection and remains full thereafter. All female capacity would therefore be occupied, contradicting the existence of unmatched male mass. Balance gives completeness on both sides.

The outcome is stable under the reported profile. If positive mass of $a$ finishes below $b$, some mass of $a$ was rejected by $b$. From that rejection onward, $b$ remains full of types weakly preferred to $a$, so it cannot finish with positive mass strictly below $a$.

2. Dominance and the exact feasibility characterization

2.1. DA dominates every stable allocation for male types

Lemma 1. Fix any reported profile, and let $x$ be its synchronous male-proposing DA outcome. For every complete stable allocation $y$ under that profile, every male type weakly prefers $x$ by stochastic dominance, and every female type weakly prefers $y$.

Proof. Let $T_{ab}(t)$, $R_{ab}(t)$ and $H_{ab}(t)$ denote cumulative proposed mass, cumulative rejected mass and currently held mass after round $t$. Then
$$ H_{ab}(t)=T_{ab}(t)-R_{ab}(t). $$
For the reported ranking $b_1,\ldots,b_s$ of $a$, synchronization gives
$$ T_{ab_1}(t)=p_a,\qquad T_{ab_j}(t)=R_{ab_{j-1}}(t-1)\quad(j>1). $$

We prove by induction that
$$ R_{ab_j}(t)\le p_a-C_a^y(j) $$
for every $a,j,t$. It holds before proposals begin. Assuming it through round $t-1$ gives
$$ T_{ab_j}(t)\le p_a-C_a^y(j-1). $$

Suppose the rejection bound fails for $a,b=b_j$ in round $t$. Then
$$ H_{ab}(t) < \bigl(p_a-C_a^y(j-1)\bigr) - \bigl(p_a-C_a^y(j)\bigr) = y_{ab}. $$
Since the bound previously held, $b$ newly rejected positive mass of $a$. Consequently, $b$ is full and holds no type strictly worse than $a$. Comparing its held column with the complete column of $y$, there is some $c\succ_b a$ with
$$ H_{cb}(t)>y_{cb}. $$

If $b$ occupies position $k$ in $c$’s ranking, the proposal bound yields
$$ H_{cb}(t)\le T_{cb}(t)\le p_c-C_c^y(k-1). $$
Therefore
$$ p_c-C_c^y(k-1)-y_{cb}>0. $$
This is precisely positive mass of $c$ assigned below $b$ in $y$. Together with $y_{ab}>0$ and $c\succ_b a$, it makes $(c,b)$ a blocking pair for $y$, a contradiction.

At termination, the mass rejected by $b_j$ is exactly the mass assigned below the first $j$ choices. Hence
$$ C_a^x(j)=p_a-R_{ab_j}(\mathrm{final})\ge C_a^y(j). $$

For female dominance, fix a female type $b$ and a prefix $P$ of its ranking. Suppose
$$ \sum_{a\in P}H_{ab}(t)>\sum_{a\in P}y_{ab}. $$
Some $c\in P$ has $H_{cb}(t)>y_{cb}$, which, by the same proposal bound, implies that $c$ has positive mass below $b$ in $y$. Moreover,
$$ \sum_{a\in P}y_{ab} < \sum_{a\in P}H_{ab}(t) \le q_b, $$
so $y$ assigns positive mass at $b$ to a type outside $P$. That type is strictly below $c$, again giving a blocking pair. Thus every held female prefix is bounded above by the corresponding prefix of $y$, including at termination. $\square$

2.2. Feasibility fixes the accomplice’s whole row

Lemma 2. A report $\rho$ is feasible if and only if its outcome $z=x^\rho$ is stable under the true profile and
$$ z_{a^\star b}=x^0_{a^\star b}\qquad(b\in B). $$
Under every feasible report, every other male type weakly worsens relative to truth.

Proof. Suppose first that $\rho$ is feasible. Reported stability excludes every true blocking pair not involving $a^\star$.

If $(a^\star,b)$ were a true blocking pair, then $z$ would have positive mass at $b$ below $a^\star$. Female stochastic improvement implies
$$ \sum_{d:\,a^\star\succ_b d}x^0_{db} \ge \sum_{d:\,a^\star\succ_b d}z_{db} >0. $$
Truthful stability therefore implies
$$ \sum_{c:\,b\succ_{a^\star}c}x^0_{a^\star c}=0. $$
Accomplice stochastic improvement then gives
$$ \sum_{c:\,b\succ_{a^\star}c}z_{a^\star c}=0, $$
contradicting the other condition for this blocking pair. Thus $z$ is truly stable.

Lemma 1 applied to the true profile says that $x^0$ weakly dominates $z$ for every male type. Feasibility gives the reverse dominance for $a^\star$. Equality of all his prefixes, followed by consecutive differences, proves equality of his entire row.

Conversely, true stability of $z$ implies female dominance over $x^0$ by Lemma 1. Equality of the accomplice’s row supplies his feasibility condition. The statement about other male types also follows from Lemma 1. $\square$

This characterizes outcomes of permitted reports. It does not establish that every truly stable allocation with the fixed accomplice row is attainable.

3. New structural result: frozen female columns

Put
$$ h_b=x^0_{a^\star b},\qquad G_b=\{a\in A:a\succ_b a^\star\}. $$

The following proposal accounting gives more than stochastic dominance.

Lemma 3. For every feasible report $\rho$, every female type $b$ with $h_b>0$, and every $a\in G_b$,
$$ x^\rho_{ab}\ge x^0_{ab}. $$

Proof. For a truthful male type $a\ne a^\star$, its total mass proposed to $b$ is
$$ T^\rho_{ab} = p_a-\sum_{c:\,c\succ_a b}x^\rho_{ac}. $$
Each portion proposes to $b$ exactly when its eventual partner is $b$ or is below $b$ in its unchanged ranking.

By Lemma 2, the truthful prefix on the right is at least the manipulated prefix. Hence
$$ T^\rho_{ab}\ge T^0_{ab}. $$

Because $h_b>0$, Lemma 2 says that $b$ retains positive accomplice mass in both outcomes. If $b$ ever rejected positive mass of a type $a\succ_b a^\star$, it would thereafter remain full of types weakly preferred to $a$. It could not finish holding positive mass of $a^\star$. Therefore no such rejection occurs in either execution, and
$$ x^\rho_{ab}=T^\rho_{ab}\ge T^0_{ab}=x^0_{ab}. $$
$\square$

Theorem 4 — Frozen-column theorem. Suppose $h_b>0$ and
$$ \sum_{a:\,a^\star\succ_b a}x^0_{ab}=0. $$
Then every feasible report preserves the whole column of $b$:
$$ x^\rho_{ab}=x^0_{ab}\qquad(a\in A). $$

Proof. Female stochastic improvement implies
$$ 0\le \sum_{a:\,a^\star\succ_b a}x^\rho_{ab} \le \sum_{a:\,a^\star\succ_b a}x^0_{ab} =0. $$
Thus both columns contain no mass below $a^\star$.

The accomplice entry is $h_b$ in both columns. Their mass strictly above $a^\star$ is consequently $q_b-h_b$ in both:
$$ \sum_{a\in G_b}x^\rho_{ab} = q_b-h_b = \sum_{a\in G_b}x^0_{ab}. $$
Every summand on the left is at least its corresponding summand on the right by Lemma 3. Equality of the sums forces equality of every summand. Together with the accomplice entry and the zero entries below it, this proves column equality. $\square$

3.1. Only the worst positively matched female type can escape this restriction

Let
$$ S=\{b\in B:h_b>0\}, $$
and let $w$ be the worst member of $S$ in the accomplice’s true ranking.

For every $b\in S\setminus\{w\}$, the truthful allocation assigns positive accomplice mass below $b$, namely at $w$. Truthful stability therefore requires
$$ \sum_{a:\,a^\star\succ_b a}x^0_{ab}=0. $$
Theorem 4 applies.

Thus:

Corollary 5. Every feasible outcome preserves the entire column of every $b\in S\setminus\{w\}$. All changes are confined to
$$ (B\setminus S)\cup\{w\}. $$
The column of $w$ is also fixed if its truthful column has no mass below $a^\star$.

This is a restriction on complete allocations, not merely on female welfare.

3.2. A new polynomially solvable case

Corollary 6. If
$$ x^0_{a^\star b}>0\qquad\text{for every }b\in B, $$
then every feasible report produces exactly $x^0$. In particular, truth induces a greatest feasible female outcome.

Proof. Corollary 5 fixes every column except possibly $w$. For each male type $a$, the remaining entry is forced by its row total:
$$ x^\rho_{aw} = p_a-\sum_{b\ne w}x^\rho_{ab} = p_a-\sum_{b\ne w}x^0_{ab} = x^0_{aw}. $$
Hence the whole allocation is unchanged. $\square$

More generally, define
$$ F= \left\{ b:h_b>0 \text{ and } \sum_{a:\,a^\star\succ_b a}x^0_{ab}=0 \right\}. $$
If $|B\setminus F|\le 1$, the same row-total argument shows that every feasible outcome equals truth.

These conditions are checkable in polynomial time using the outcome-evaluation result in Section 5.

4. Exact counterexamples to possible reductions

4.1. Preserving the order of positive partners can exclude an improvement

Let
$$ A=\{a^\star,c,d\},\qquad B=\{b_0,b_1,b_2\}, $$
with masses
$$ (p_{a^\star},p_c,p_d)=\left(\frac12,\frac14,\frac14\right), \qquad (q_{b_0},q_{b_1},q_{b_2})=\left(\frac14,\frac14,\frac12\right). $$

Preferences are:

| Male type | True ranking |
|---|---|
| $a^\star$ | $b_1\succ b_0\succ b_2$ |
| $c$ | $b_2\succ b_0\succ b_1$ |
| $d$ | $b_0\succ b_2\succ b_1$ |

| Female type | Ranking |
|---|---|
| $b_0$ | $c\succ d\succ a^\star$ |
| $b_1$ | $a^\star\succ c\succ d$ |
| $b_2$ | $d\succ a^\star\succ c$ |

Measure mass in units of $\beta=1/4$. Rows below are ordered $a^\star,c,d$, and columns $b_0,b_1,b_2$.

Under truth, $b_1$ retains one unit of accomplice mass. Its other unit is rejected by $b_0$, which holds $d$, and then fits at $b_2$ alongside $c$. Therefore
$$ x^0=\beta \begin{pmatrix} 0&1&1\\ 0&0&1\\ 1&0&0 \end{pmatrix}. $$

Now report
$$ \rho:\quad b_2\succ b_1\succ b_0. $$
In the first round, $b_2$ holds both accomplice units and rejects $c$. Next, $c$ displaces $d$ at $b_0$. Then $d$ proposes to $b_2$ and displaces one accomplice unit, which goes to $b_1$. Thus
$$ x^\rho=\beta \begin{pmatrix} 0&1&1\\ 1&0&0\\ 0&0&1 \end{pmatrix}. $$
The accomplice’s row is unchanged. At $b_0$, $c$ replaces $d$; at $b_2$, $d$ replaces $c$. Both females strictly improve, while $b_1$ is unchanged. The report is feasible.

The truthful positive partners of $a^\star$ are ordered $b_1\succ b_2$. There are exactly three reports preserving this order:
$$ (b_0,b_1,b_2),\qquad (b_1,b_0,b_2),\qquad (b_1,b_2,b_0). $$
Each produces $x^0$. Indeed, any proposal to $b_0$ is rejected without affecting $d$; $b_1$ retains one accomplice unit; and only one accomplice unit reaches $b_2$, where it fits alongside $c$. No other male is displaced.

Consequently, restricting reports to preserve the order of positively matched types can exclude a feasible improvement.

This example is not a counterexample to greatest-outcome existence. It refutes that proposed restriction on reports.

4.2. Stochastic strategyproofness fails for a male type

Use the same masses, but change preferences to:

| Male type | True ranking |
|---|---|
| $a^\star$ | $b_0\succ b_1\succ b_2$ |
| $c$ | $b_2\succ b_0\succ b_1$ |
| $d$ | $b_1\succ b_0\succ b_2$ |

| Female type | Ranking |
|---|---|
| $b_0$ | $d\succ a^\star\succ c$ |
| $b_1$ | $a^\star\succ c\succ d$ |
| $b_2$ | $a^\star\succ d\succ c$ |

In units of $\beta$, truthful DA proceeds as follows. One accomplice unit is rejected by $b_0$ and displaces $d$ at $b_1$. Type $d$ then displaces the other accomplice unit at $b_0$. That unit reaches $b_1$, which rejects one of its two accomplice units onward to $b_2$. Hence
$$ x^0=\beta \begin{pmatrix} 0&1&1\\ 0&0&1\\ 1&0&0 \end{pmatrix}. $$

Under
$$ \rho:\quad b_0\succ b_2\succ b_1, $$
one accomplice unit stays at $b_0$, and the rejected unit fits at $b_2$ beside $c$. Type $d$ remains at $b_1$. Thus
$$ x^\rho=\beta \begin{pmatrix} 1&0&1\\ 0&0&1\\ 0&1&0 \end{pmatrix}. $$

The accomplice’s normalized truthful and manipulated distributions are respectively
$$ \left(0,\frac12,\frac12\right), \qquad \left(\frac12,0,\frac12\right) $$
in his true ranking. The first prefix strictly increases, and the second remains equal. Thus the manipulation strictly improves his distribution by stochastic dominance.

It is not feasible: $b_0$ worsens from $d$ to $a^\star$, and $b_1$ worsens from $a^\star$ to $d$.

Therefore stochastic strategyproofness for a single mass type cannot be assumed. Lemma 2 genuinely uses female feasibility as well as accomplice feasibility.

4.3. Stable allocations with the fixed row need not be attainable

Take the same male masses, but female masses
$$ (q_{b_0},q_{b_1},q_{b_2}) = \left(\frac12,\frac14,\frac14\right), $$
and preferences
$$ \begin{aligned} a^\star &: b_0\succ b_1\succ b_2,\\ c &: b_1\succ b_2\succ b_0,\\ d &: b_2\succ b_1\succ b_0, \end{aligned} \qquad \begin{aligned} b_0 &: a^\star\succ c\succ d,\\ b_1 &: d\succ c\succ a^\star,\\ b_2 &: c\succ d\succ a^\star. \end{aligned} $$

Every report produces
$$ x^0=\beta \begin{pmatrix} 2&0&0\\ 0&1&0\\ 0&0&1 \end{pmatrix}. $$
To see this, $c$ and $d$ propose to $b_1$ and $b_2$ in the first round and are held permanently. Both females rank them above $a^\star$, so accomplice proposals cannot displace them. All accomplice mass eventually reaches the otherwise vacant $b_0$.

Nevertheless,
$$ y=\beta \begin{pmatrix} 2&0&0\\ 0&0&1\\ 0&1&0 \end{pmatrix} $$
is truly stable and preserves the accomplice’s row. Type $c$ prefers $b_1$ to its assigned $b_2$, but $b_1$ prefers its assigned $d$ to $c$. The analogous desired deviation by $d$ is rejected by $b_2$’s preference for $c$. The accomplice already has its first choice.

Both $b_1$ and $b_2$ strictly prefer $y$. Thus optimizing over all truly stable allocations with the fixed row can select an unattainable allocation.

5. General algorithmic bounds

5.1. A specified report can be evaluated in polynomial time

The external algorithmic result used here is the strongly polynomial algorithm of Dean and Munshi for bipartite stable allocation with strict rankings, vertex quotas and edge capacities. It returns the proposer-optimal allocation; its augmentation operations preserve integrality on integral input. See Section 3 and Lemma 5 of [*Faster Algorithms for Stable Allocation Problems*](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

Apply it to the complete bipartite graph on the supplied support, using quotas $p_a,q_b$ and capacities
$$ u_{ab}=\min\{p_a,q_b\}. $$
These capacities impose no additional restriction. If an edge is saturated, one endpoint assigns its entire quota there, so both endpoints cannot have strictly worse assigned partners.

Every stable allocation is complete: otherwise balance supplies underfilled vertices on both sides, and their acceptable connecting edge blocks. Thus the source’s unmatched-mass convention causes no discrepancy.

There are $rs\le m^2$ edges and $\tau$ vertices. Lemma 1 identifies synchronous DA as proposer-optimal: it maximizes every male prefix and hence every male row lexicographically. Therefore the algorithm computes exactly $x^\rho$.

After scaling by $D$, quotas and capacities are integers of $O(L)$ bits. Integral augmentations use residual-capacity minima and additions/subtractions; the polynomial number of operations therefore has polynomial bit cost.

Feasibility is then checked by the required prefix inequalities. Existence of some feasible report is always affirmative because truth is permitted.

5.2. Greatest-outcome existence belongs to $\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{NP}}$

For each female type $b$, list its ranking as
$$ a_{b,1}\succ_b\cdots\succ_b a_{b,r}. $$
Define integer coordinates
$$ V_{b,k}(\rho) = D\sum_{i=1}^{k}x^\rho_{a_{b,i},b}, \qquad 1\le k<r. $$
Every coordinate lies between $0$ and $D$. These coordinates exactly encode female stochastic dominance; the omitted final prefixes are constant.

For each coordinate, the threshold question
$$ \text{Does some feasible report }\rho \text{ satisfy }V_{b,k}(\rho)\ge T? $$
belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$. A certificate is the permutation, and Section 5.1 gives polynomial verification.

Binary search therefore computes
$$ M_{b,k}=\max_{\rho\text{ feasible}}V_{b,k}(\rho) $$
using polynomially many oracle queries, because $\log D=O(L)$.

Now ask one further $\mathrm{NP}$ question: does a feasible report attain every $M_{b,k}$ simultaneously?

If yes, its outcome dominates every feasible outcome coordinatewise and is greatest. Conversely, a greatest feasible outcome must attain each coordinate maximum, because it dominates a feasible report attaining that maximum. Thus this query exactly decides greatest-outcome existence.

When the answer is affirmative, an inducing report is recovered by fixing permutation entries successively and asking whether each tentative prefix extends to a feasible report attaining all maxima. At most $s^2$ extension queries suffice.

Consequently,
$$ \text{greatest-outcome existence}\in \Delta_2^p=\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{NP}}, $$
and report construction belongs to $\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}$.

If $r=1$, there are no nonconstant female coordinates, and truth already induces the unique female outcome.

6. What remains open

The frozen-column theorem substantially restricts where feasible improvements can occur and settles the full-support accomplice case. It does not settle the unrestricted problem.

The three examples establish concrete obstacles to possible reductions: positive-partner order cannot simply be fixed, stochastic strategyproofness does not hold for a mass type, and stable allocations with the required row can be unattainable. The cited finite-market theorem remains valid in its individual-agent setting, but does not directly remove these obstacles in the mass model. [Hosseini, Umar and Vaish, *Two for One & One for All*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.08774).

No polynomial algorithm or hardness reduction for the unrestricted existence-and-construction problem is proved here.
