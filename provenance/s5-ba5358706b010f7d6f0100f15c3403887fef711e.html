The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(\Theta\) of complete valuation types with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), explicit rational valuations \(u_\theta(B)\), shared type weights \(w_\theta(B)\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\hat q_j\), compute a welfare-optimal allocation of mass \(x_{\theta B}\) to integral bundles \(B\in\mathcal B_k=\{B\in\mathbb Z_+^G:\sum_j B_j\le k\}\), together with additive good prices \(p_j\ge0\). Require \(\sum_B x_{\theta B}\le\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,B}B_jx_{\theta B}\le\hat q_j\), and \(\sum_{\theta,B}w_\theta(B)u_\theta(B)x_{\theta B}\) to be maximal; for \(\epsilon\) at least \(2\delta_w\max_\theta\max_B u_\theta(B)\), every bundle assigned positive mass to type \(\theta\) must be within \(\epsilon\) of that type’s best net utility at prices \(p\), and unassigned types must have no bundle with net utility above \(\epsilon\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) contain \(m\ge1\) good types, fix an integer \(k\ge1\), and set \(\mathcal B_k=\{B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}:\sum_jB_j\le k\}\).
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) complete types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), normalized by \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Input includes explicit binary-rational tables \(u_\theta(B)\ge0\), with \(u_\theta(0)=0\), and shared type weights \(w_\theta(B)\in[1-\delta_w,1+\delta_w]\), where \(0\le\delta_w<1\); supplies \(\hat q_j\ge0\) are binary rationals. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The binary-rational tolerance satisfies \(\epsilon\ge2\delta_w\max_{\theta,B}u_\theta(B)\). Buying nothing has utility and price zero; bundle prices are \(P_p(B)=\sum_jB_jp_j\), with \(p_j\ge0\).
Compute rational masses \(x_{\theta B}\ge0\) maximizing \(\sum_{\theta,B}w_\theta(B)u_\theta(B)x_{\theta B}\) over \(\sum_Bx_{\theta B}\le\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B}B_jx_{\theta B}\le\hat q_j\), together with rational prices \(p\).
Write \(V_\theta(p)=\max\{0,\max_{B\in\mathcal B_k}(u_\theta(B)-P_p(B))\}\). Require \(x_{\theta B}>0\Rightarrow u_\theta(B)-P_p(B)\ge V_\theta(p)-\epsilon\), and \(\sum_Bx_{\theta B}<\mu_\theta\Rightarrow V_\theta(p)\le\epsilon\); ties may be split arbitrarily.

For fixed \(k\), is this allocation-and-pricing problem in P or NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\) for every fixed \(k\). An exact optimal solution of the weighted allocation LP, together with an exact optimal solution of its dual, gives the required allocation and prices. Rational masses need not be expanded into individual agents.

The [Tang–Subramanian paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.01457) also uses allocation LP dual variables to construct prices. The argument below directly proves the claim for the normalized mass formulation in the question.

1. The allocation LP and its dual.

Write \(S=|\mathcal B_k|\), \(c_{\theta B}=w_\theta(B)u_\theta(B)\), and \(U=\max_{\theta,B}u_\theta(B)\). Introducing a slack coordinate \(B_0=k-\sum_{j\in G}B_j\) gives a bijection between \(\mathcal B_k\) and the nonnegative integer solutions of \(B_0+\sum_{j\in G}B_j=k\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle S=\binom{m+k}{k}.\)

The primal LP is precisely the weighted allocation problem:

\(\displaystyle \max_{x\ge0}\ \sum_{\theta,B}c_{\theta B}x_{\theta B},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_Bx_{\theta B}\le\mu_\theta\quad\text{for every }\theta,\qquad \sum_{\theta,B}B_jx_{\theta B}\le\hat q_j\quad\text{for every }j.\)

Its dual has one variable \(a_\theta\ge0\) for each type constraint and one variable \(p_j\ge0\) for each supply constraint:

\(\displaystyle \min_{a,p\ge0}\ \sum_\theta\mu_\theta a_\theta+\sum_j\hat q_jp_j,\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle a_\theta+\sum_jB_jp_j\ge c_{\theta B}\quad\text{for every }\theta,B.\)

The primal is feasible because \(x=0\) is feasible. Its feasible region is closed and bounded: each coordinate satisfies \(0\le x_{\theta B}\le\mu_\theta\). Thus its optimum is finite and attained.

The dual is feasible: take \(p=0\) and \(a_\theta=\max_Bc_{\theta B}\). By linear programming strong duality, the dual also attains its optimum, and the optimal objective values coincide. All coefficients are rational, so exact rational optimal solutions can be computed by polynomial-time rational LP algorithms.

Choose any primal optimum \(x\) and any dual optimum \((a,p)\).

2. Complementary slackness, including unallocated mass.

For any feasible primal and dual pair, their objective gap equals

\(\displaystyle \sum_\theta a_\theta\left(\mu_\theta-\sum_Bx_{\theta B}\right) +\sum_jp_j\left(\hat q_j-\sum_{\theta,B}B_jx_{\theta B}\right) +\sum_{\theta,B}x_{\theta B}\left(a_\theta+P_p(B)-c_{\theta B}\right). \)

This identity follows by expanding the sums and cancelling terms. Every summand is nonnegative by primal and dual feasibility. For our optimal pair the gap is zero, so every summand is zero. In particular,

\(\displaystyle x_{\theta B}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ a_\theta=w_\theta(B)u_\theta(B)-P_p(B),\)

and

\(\displaystyle \sum_Bx_{\theta B}<\mu_\theta\ \Longrightarrow\ a_\theta=0.\)

These conclusions apply directly to rational masses, regardless of how small a positive mass is.

3. The prices satisfy both required conditions.

Set \(d=\delta_w U\). The hypotheses imply, for every type and bundle,

\(\displaystyle |w_\theta(B)u_\theta(B)-u_\theta(B)|\le d,\qquad 2d\le\epsilon.\)

Dual feasibility therefore gives, for every bundle \(C\),

\(\displaystyle u_\theta(C)-P_p(C) =c_{\theta C}-P_p(C)+(1-w_\theta(C))u_\theta(C) \le a_\theta+d. \)

Because \(a_\theta+d\ge0\), the outside option also satisfies this upper bound. Hence

\(\displaystyle V_\theta(p)\le a_\theta+d.\)

If \(x_{\theta B}>0\), complementary slackness gives

\(\displaystyle u_\theta(B)-P_p(B) =a_\theta+(1-w_\theta(B))u_\theta(B) \ge a_\theta-d. \)

Combining the last two inequalities proves

\(\displaystyle u_\theta(B)-P_p(B) \ge V_\theta(p)-2d \ge V_\theta(p)-\epsilon. \)

If \(\sum_Bx_{\theta B}<\mu_\theta\), complementary slackness instead gives \(a_\theta=0\). Thus

\(\displaystyle V_\theta(p)\le d\le\epsilon.\)

These are exactly the two requested pricing conditions. The argument includes the empty bundle, since its utility and price are both zero. It also covers \(\epsilon=0\): the assumed tolerance bound then forces \(d=0\).

Thus any primal optimum paired with any dual optimum yields a valid answer.

4. Running time and output size.

There are \(N=\tau S=\tau\binom{m+k}{k}\) primal variables and \(m+\tau\) constraints, apart from nonnegativity. The dual has \(m+\tau\) variables and \(N\) constraints.

Enumerating the bundles takes \(O(mS(1+\log(k+1)))\) bit operations using a direct recursive enumeration of their coordinates. Multiplying the rational table entries to form \(c_{\theta B}\) takes polynomial time in \(L\); the resulting coefficients have total encoding length \(O(L)\).

Using a dense matrix representation, the total LP encoding length is bounded by

\(\displaystyle H=O\!\left(L+(m+1)\tau\binom{m+k}{k}(1+\log(k+1))\right). \)

The standard exact rational LP theorem computes optimal rational primal and dual solutions in time polynomial in their encoding length. Its hypotheses hold here: the LPs have rational coefficients, are feasible, and have attained finite optima. Including enumeration and construction, a valid bit-complexity bound is therefore

\(\displaystyle \left(L+(m+1)\tau\binom{m+k}{k}(1+\log(k+1))\right)^{O(1)}. \)

The returned allocation and prices have encoding lengths polynomial in \(H\). This bound depends on the binary encoding of the masses, rather than on their denominators’ numerical values.

For fixed \(k\), \(\binom{m+k}{k}=O_k(m^k)\), so the running time is

\(\displaystyle \left(L+\tau m^{k+1}\right)^{O(1)},\)

with constants allowed to depend on the fixed \(k\).

Indeed, even when \(k\) varies, the problem remains polynomial-time in the length of the stipulated explicit tables: those tables ensure \(N\le O(L)\), while the supplies ensure \(m\le O(L)\), making the displayed LP encoding bound polynomial in \(L\).
