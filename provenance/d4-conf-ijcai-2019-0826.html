The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two candidates \(c_1,c_2\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) over voter types, a target \(c_2\), recipient mass \(\kappa\), per-capita budget \(B\), and explicit rational piecewise-linear willingness functions \(f_t\), is there a feasible payment schedule that makes \(c_2\) win in the deterministic nonatomic electorate?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses sum to \(1\).
There are \(m=2\) candidates under plurality. Each type specifies a strict preference and an explicitly encoded continuous piecewise-linear function \(f_t:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]\), with rational breakpoints and affine coefficients, including its final ray; monotonicity is not required.
Let \(T_1\) be the types initially supporting \(c_1\), and let \(\beta\) be the initial mass supporting the target \(c_2\). Assume \(\beta<1/2\). Ties favor \(c_1\).
The inputs also include binary rationals \(B>0\) and \(0<\kappa\le1-\beta\), representing budget per unit of total society mass and exact recipient mass.
A payment schedule consists of finite nonnegative Borel measures \(\nu_t\) on \([0,\infty)\), for \(t\in T_1\), satisfying \(\nu_t([0,\infty))\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t\in T_1}\nu_t([0,\infty))=\kappa\), and \(\sum_{t\in T_1}\int x\,d\nu_t(x)\le B\). Thus portions of one type may receive different payments, including zero.
A recipient portion of mass \(z\) paid \(x\) transfers exactly \(zf_t(x)\) votes from \(c_1\) to \(c_2\); unselected mass retains its original vote. Write \(S=\sum_{t\in T_1}\int f_t(x)\,d\nu_t(x)\). The deterministic winning objective is \(W=1\) when \(\beta+S>1/2\), and \(W=0\) otherwise.
Let \(s\) count all encoded linear pieces and \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,s,L\), with \(B,\kappa\) included in \(L\).

Does maximizing \(W\) admit a multiplicative \(a\)-approximation for some fixed finite \(a\ge1\) in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,s,L\), or is obtaining such an approximation NP-hard?

For finite electorates, maximizing the target's winning probability admits no multiplicative \(g(k)\)-approximation in FPT time parameterized by recipient count \(k\), for any computable \(g\), assuming \(\mathrm{FPT}\ne\mathrm{W}[1]\), even with two candidates and continuous willingness functions. [Source](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0023.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one, so the per-capita budget B is also the total spending bound.
  • Types include initial strict preferences as well as willingness functions; imposed an initial strict majority for c1.
  • Restricted actual recipients to initial c1 supporters, following the stated exclusion of target supporters from the bribed electorate; required exact recipient mass kappa within the available mass.
  • Allowed different payments to different portions of a type, represented by measures, and retained the discrete permission to select zero-payment recipients.
  • Interpreted willingness deterministically as the fraction of each recipient portion that switches; unselected voters retain their original preferences regardless of f_t(0).
  • Used continuous rational piecewise-linear functions on the entire nonnegative payment domain, without monotonicity or a prescribed value at zero.
  • Resolved unspecified ties in favor of c1, requiring c2 to obtain a strict majority.
  • Preserved the winning-probability approximation objective as a deterministic zero-or-one victory objective; asked about polynomial-time multiplicative approximation because recipient mass supplies no canonical integer FPT parameter.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result

The problem is exactly solvable in polynomial time. In particular, it admits a multiplicative \(1\)-approximation in \((m+\tau+s+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations, with \(m=2\).

The essential property is the permission to split a type’s mass among different payments. This gives an exact finite linear programming formulation, even when willingness functions are nonmonotone.

2. Reduction to finitely many payment amounts

Fix a type \(t\in T_1\). Write its distinct piece endpoints as

\(\displaystyle 0=q_{t,0}<q_{t,1}<\cdots<q_{t,r_t},\)

where the final ray starts at \(q_{t,r_t}\). If the function has only a final ray, take \(r_t=0\). Define \(v_{t,j}=f_t(q_{t,j})\).

First, the final ray is constant. Indeed, if its affine expression is \(ax+b\), then \(a>0\) eventually violates \(f_t(x)\le 1\), and \(a<0\) eventually violates \(f_t(x)\ge 0\). Thus \(a=0\). Consequently, replacing any payment \(x\ge q_{t,r_t}\) by \(q_{t,r_t}\) preserves transferred votes and weakly decreases cost.

Next, suppose \(q_{t,j}\le x<q_{t,j+1}\). Define

\(\displaystyle \lambda=\frac{q_{t,j+1}-x}{q_{t,j+1}-q_{t,j}}.\)

Then \(0\le\lambda\le1\) and

\(\displaystyle x=\lambda q_{t,j}+(1-\lambda)q_{t,j+1}.\)

Because \(f_t\) is affine on this interval and continuous at its endpoints,

\(\displaystyle f_t(x)=\lambda v_{t,j}+(1-\lambda)v_{t,j+1}.\)

Therefore, a recipient portion of mass \(z\) paid \(x\) can instead be split into portions of masses \(z\lambda\) and \(z(1-\lambda)\), paid the two endpoints. Recipient mass, cost, and transferred votes are all preserved.

This replacement also applies rigorously to arbitrary Borel measures. For each \(x\), let \(w_{t,j}(x)\) be the endpoint weights just described: on a bounded interval, only its two endpoint weights are nonzero; on the final ray, the last endpoint has weight one. These are nonnegative Borel functions satisfying

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=0}^{r_t}w_{t,j}(x)=1,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=0}^{r_t}q_{t,j}w_{t,j}(x)\le x,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=0}^{r_t}v_{t,j}w_{t,j}(x)=f_t(x).\)

Given a feasible measure \(\nu_t\), set

\(\displaystyle y_{t,j}=\int w_{t,j}(x)\,d\nu_t(x),\qquad \widehat{\nu}_t=\sum_{j=0}^{r_t}y_{t,j}\delta_{q_{t,j}},\)

where \(\delta_q\) denotes unit point mass at \(q\). Integrating the three identities or inequalities proves that \(\widehat{\nu}_t\) has the same recipient mass and transferred votes as \(\nu_t\), and no greater cost.

Thus every feasible schedule can be replaced by a schedule supported on the encoded endpoints, preserving its winning status. No monotonicity assumption was used.

3. The exact linear program

Introduce a nonnegative variable \(y_{t,j}\) for the recipient mass of type \(t\) paid \(q_{t,j}\). Solve the following rational linear program:

\(\displaystyle \text{maximize }\quad \sum_{t\in T_1}\sum_{j=0}^{r_t}v_{t,j}y_{t,j},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=0}^{r_t}y_{t,j}\le\mu_t\qquad(t\in T_1),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_1}\sum_{j=0}^{r_t}y_{t,j}=\kappa,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_1}\sum_{j=0}^{r_t}q_{t,j}y_{t,j}\le B,\)

\(\displaystyle y_{t,j}\ge0\qquad(t\in T_1,\ 0\le j\le r_t).\)

Call its optimal value \(V\).

Every feasible vector defines an admissible schedule by taking \(\nu_t=\sum_jy_{t,j}\delta_{q_{t,j}}\); its transferred vote mass equals the objective value. Conversely, Section 2 transforms every admissible schedule into a feasible vector with exactly the same transferred vote mass. Hence the linear program captures the original optimization of \(S\) exactly.

The feasible set is nonempty. Since the two candidates exhaust the initial support, \(\sum_{t\in T_1}\mu_t=1-\beta\). Set

\(\displaystyle y_{t,0}=\frac{\kappa}{1-\beta}\mu_t,\qquad y_{t,j}=0\quad(j>0).\)

The assumption \(\kappa\le1-\beta\) ensures the type capacity constraints, the total recipient mass is \(\kappa\), and the cost is zero.

The feasible set is closed and bounded: each coordinate satisfies \(0\le y_{t,j}\le\mu_t\). It is therefore compact, so its continuous linear objective attains a maximum. In particular, \(V\) is attained by an admissible schedule.

4. Bit complexity and the strict winning threshold

Let \(p_t\) be the number of encoded pieces of \(f_t\), including its final ray. After removing duplicate endpoints, \(r_t+1\le p_t\). Thus the number of variables is

\(\displaystyle N=\sum_{t\in T_1}(r_t+1)\le s.\)

Apart from nonnegativity constraints, there are at most \(\tau\) type capacity inequalities, one recipient mass equality, and one budget inequality.

All coefficients are rational. Each \(v_{t,j}\) is obtained by evaluating an encoded rational affine expression at an encoded rational endpoint, so its bit length is \(O(L)\). A dense encoding of the entire linear program has length \(O((s+\tau)^2L)\).

We use the standard rational linear programming theorem: a linear program with binary rational data can be solved exactly, including producing a rational optimal solution when a finite optimum exists, in time polynomial in its encoding length. Exact recovery, rather than merely approximate optimization, is explicitly discussed in [Vishnoi’s linear programming notes](https://nisheethvishnoi.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/lecture71.pdf). Its hypotheses hold here: the data are rational, the feasible set is nonempty, and the optimum is finite and attained.

Consequently, an optimal vector \(y^*\), its value \(V\), and the associated finite schedule can be computed in \((m+\tau+s+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations for \(m=2\). Their encodings have polynomial length. This bound includes the binary encodings of \(B\) and \(\kappa\).

Compare \(V\) and \(\frac12-\beta\) using exact rational arithmetic:

  • If \(V>\frac12-\beta\), the recovered schedule makes \(c_2\) win, so the optimal winning value is \(1\).
  • If \(V\le\frac12-\beta\), every admissible schedule satisfies \(S\le V\), so none makes \(c_2\) win. The optimal winning value is \(0\).

Equality is therefore handled correctly under the specified tie rule.

5. Approximation consequence and relation to the cited result

The algorithm returns an optimal schedule for \(W\). It consequently satisfies \(W_{\mathrm{alg}}\ge W_{\mathrm{opt}}/a\) for every fixed finite \(a\ge1\), including \(a=1\).

The cited finite-electorate hardness theorem concerns selecting indivisible voters and maximizing a stochastic winning probability. [Its Theorem 1](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0023.pdf) does not establish hardness for this deterministic nonatomic model. Here, divisible recipient mass and affine interpolation yield the exact linear program proved above.
