The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite rational support \(T\subseteq[0,1]\times[0,1)\) with masses \(\mu_{x,a}\ge0\) summing to \(1\), compute \(y\in[0,1]\) minimizing \(\mathrm{SC}_\mu(y)=\sum_{(x,a)\in T}\mu_{x,a}(|y-x|-a)_+/(1-a)\), and require the endpoint mechanism to be strategyproof under every rational clone expansion in which one clone may change only its reported location.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list of \(\tau\) rational types \((x,a)\in[0,1]\times[0,1)\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_{x,a}\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. There is one facility, so \(m=1\); state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Thresholds are public and fixed; only locations may be misreported. Define \(c(y;x,a)=\max\{|y-x|-a,0\}/(1-a)\) and \(\mathrm{SC}_\mu(y)=\sum_{(x,a)}\mu_{x,a}c(y;x,a)\), for \(y\in[0,1]\). The computational problem is to output an exact rational minimizer.
Define the endpoint mechanism using \(B=\{0,1\}\cup(\{x-a,x+a:(x,a)\in T\}\cap[0,1])\): it outputs the smallest \(z\in B\) satisfying \(\sum_{x+a\le z}\mu_{x,a}/(1-a)\ge\sum_{x-a>z}\mu_{x,a}/(1-a)\).
For every positive integer \(N\) with all \(N\mu_{x,a}\) integral, a clone expansion has \(N\mu_{x,a}\) clones of type \((x,a)\), each of mass \(1/N\). A single clone may report any rational \(x'\in[0,1]\), keeping its threshold and mass fixed; aggregate the resulting reports before applying the mechanism. Strategyproofness means \(c(f(\mu);x,a)\le c(f(\mu');x,a)\) for every such deviation, evaluated at the clone's true type.

Is exact social-cost minimization for these support-encoded societies solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? Does the defined endpoint mechanism always output a minimizer and satisfy strategyproofness under every rational clone expansion?

For finite agent lists, the smallest-endpoint balance mechanism is strategyproof and minimizes social cost, and its finite candidate set gives a polynomial-time algorithm ([Zhou et al., 2023](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2023/pdfs/p2170.pdf)).
No result for exactly this support-encoded problem with the stated clone requirement was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Discard zero-mass entries so that the listed types are precisely the positive-mass support.
  • Interpret the undefined endpoint mechanism as the mass-weighted smallest-endpoint balance rule, retaining its asymmetric endpoint inequalities and leftmost tie-breaking.
  • Include the facility interval boundaries and exclude candidate endpoints outside the interval, making the mechanism well-defined when threshold plateaus extend beyond the feasible domain.
  • Define rational clone expansions as equal-mass clones for every common-denominator multiple, with one clone changing only its rational reported location.
  • Treat thresholds as public and fixed, evaluate deviations using true costs, and require exact rational output.
  • Identify the generic size parameter m with the number of facilities, fixed at one here.

Result

Exact social-cost minimization is polynomial-time solvable for \(m=1\). The defined endpoint mechanism returns the leftmost minimizer and is strategyproof under every rational clone expansion.

A direct implementation takes \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2)\) bit operations using schoolbook integer arithmetic. This bound uses the support size \(\tau\), without enumerating clones.

The finite-agent result appears as Mechanism 1 and Theorem 1 in [Zhou et al. (2023)](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2023/pdfs/p2170.pdf). The proof below independently establishes the stated support-encoded version, including boundary handling and the clone requirement.

1. The balance expression is a right derivative

Index the support by \(i\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), and define

\(\ell_i=x_i-a_i,\qquad r_i=x_i+a_i,\qquad w_i=\mu_i/(1-a_i)>0.\)

Write \(u_+=\max\{u,0\}\). Since \(\ell_i\le r_i\), direct case analysis gives

\(\max\{|y-x_i|-a_i,0\}=(\ell_i-y)_++(y-r_i)_+.\)

Consequently, extending the same formula to real \(y\), the social cost is the convex, continuous, piecewise-linear function

\(F(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\bigl((\ell_i-y)_++(y-r_i)_+\bigr).\)

Its right derivative is

\(g(y)=F'_+(y)=\sum_{r_i\le y}w_i-\sum_{\ell_i>y}w_i.\)

Indeed, the right derivatives of \((\ell_i-y)_+\) and \((y-r_i)_+\) are respectively \(-1\) when \(y<\ell_i\) and \(1\) when \(y\ge r_i\), and are otherwise zero.

Thus \(g\) is nondecreasing and right-continuous. Its only possible changes occur at the endpoints \(\ell_i,r_i\). When \(a_i=0\), both endpoints coincide and the corresponding jump is \(2w_i\); the formula remains valid.

The mechanism’s balance condition is exactly \(g(z)\ge0\).

2. The mechanism selects the leftmost minimizer

Let \(f\) be the mechanism’s output. First, this output exists: since \(\ell_i=x_i-a_i\le1\),

\(g(1)=\sum_{r_i\le1}w_i\ge0,\)

and \(1\in B\).

We claim that

\(g(t)<0\quad\text{for every }t\in[0,f),\qquad g(t)\ge0\quad\text{for every }t\in[f,1].\)

For the first assertion, choose the largest candidate \(b\in B\) satisfying \(b\le t\). It exists because \(0\in B\). There are no endpoints in \((b,t]\), so \(g(t)=g(b)\). Since \(b<f\), minimality of the selected candidate gives \(g(b)<0\).

The second assertion follows from \(g(f)\ge0\) and monotonicity of \(g\). In particular,

\(f=\min\{t\in[0,1]:g(t)\ge0\}.\)

Because \(F\) is continuous and piecewise linear, for \(u<v\) its change is the sum of its slopes times the lengths of the finitely many linear pieces, equivalently

\(F(v)-F(u)=\int_u^v g(t)\,dt.\)

If \(y<f\), all slopes on \([y,f)\) are strictly negative, hence \(F(f)<F(y)\). If \(y>f\), all slopes on \([f,y)\) are nonnegative, hence \(F(y)\ge F(f)\).

Therefore \(f\) minimizes social cost, and no point smaller than \(f\) is a minimizer. This proves that the mechanism returns the leftmost minimizer, including when the minimum is attained on an interval or at a boundary.

3. Exact computation from the support

There are at most \(2\tau+2\) candidates. The following sweep computes \(f\).

  • Compute \(\ell_i,r_i,w_i\) and sort the distinct candidates in \(B\).
  • Compute \(g(0)\). If it is nonnegative, return \(0\).
  • Visit the remaining candidates in increasing order. At each candidate \(z\), add \(w_i\) for every endpoint \(\ell_i=z\), and add \(w_i\) for every endpoint \(r_i=z\). Process all endpoints at \(z\) before checking the sign.
  • Return the first candidate with nonnegative balance.

Both kinds of endpoint contribute an increase of \(w_i\): crossing \(\ell_i\) removes a negative summand, while crossing \(r_i\) introduces a positive summand. Thus the sweep maintains exactly \(g(z)\). Section 2 proves its correctness.

For completeness, all arithmetic can be performed with integers of polynomial bit length. Write the input mass and threshold as

\(\mu_i=s_i/t_i,\qquad a_i=u_i/v_i.\)

Then

\(w_i=n_i/d_i,\qquad n_i=s_i v_i,\qquad d_i=t_i(v_i-u_i)>0.\)

The total bit length of these numerators and denominators is \(O(L)\). Set

\(D=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}d_i,\qquad W_i=n_i(D/d_i).\)

The integer \(D\), every \(W_i\), and every sum of the \(W_i\) have \(O(L)\) bits: product bit lengths add, and summation contributes at most \(O(\log(\tau+1))\) additional bits, which is absorbed by \(O(L)\).

Replace the rational balance \(g\) by the integer balance

\(G(z)=\sum_{r_i\le z}W_i-\sum_{\ell_i>z}W_i=Dg(z).\)

Since \(D>0\), the signs agree exactly.

Constructing these integers costs \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations. Each rational endpoint has \(O(L)\) bits, and each exact endpoint comparison costs \(O(L^2)\). Sorting and sweeping therefore take

\(T(1,\tau,L)=O(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2).\)

The output is a boundary or an input-derived endpoint, so its rational encoding also has \(O(L)\) bits. This establishes polynomial-time exact minimization for the specified \(m=1\) problem; no claim about larger \(m\) is needed.

4. Strategyproofness for every clone expansion

Fix any permitted positive integer \(N\), and select one clone with true type \((x,a)\). Its mass is \(1/N\). Define

\(\ell=x-a,\qquad r=x+a,\qquad w=1/(N(1-a))>0.\)

Let \(H(t)\) denote the combined slope contribution of all other clones. The selected clone’s contribution is \(w\,s_x(t)\), where

\(s_x(t)=-1\text{ if }t<\ell,\quad s_x(t)=0\text{ if }\ell\le t<r,\quad s_x(t)=1\text{ if }t\ge r.\)

The middle interval is empty when \(a=0\). Hence the truthful balance is

\(g(t)=H(t)+w\,s_x(t).\)

After a report \(x'\in[0,1]\), the threshold and mass remain fixed, so the new balance is

\(\widetilde g(t)=H(t)+w\,s_{x'}(t).\)

Aggregation does not change either expression: identical reports simply have their masses added. Thus Section 2 applies to both aggregated societies. Write their outputs as \(y\) and \(\widetilde y\).

Case 1: The truthful output gives zero cost

If \(\ell\le y\le r\), then \(c(y;x,a)=0\). All costs are nonnegative, so no report can improve it.

Case 2: The truthful output lies to the left

Suppose \(y<\ell\). Then \(s_x(y)=-1\), while \(s_{x'}(y)\ge-1\). Therefore

\(\widetilde g(y)\ge g(y)\ge0.\)

The minimum-crossing characterization from Section 2 implies \(\widetilde y\le y\). Both outcomes lie to the left of \(\ell\), and consequently

\(c(\widetilde y;x,a)=\frac{\ell-\widetilde y}{1-a}\ge\frac{\ell-y}{1-a}=c(y;x,a).\)

Case 3: The truthful output lies to the right

Suppose \(y>r\). For every \(t\in[r,y)\), the truthful clone contributes its largest possible slope, namely \(w\). Thus

\(\widetilde g(t)\le g(t)<0,\)

where strict negativity follows from Section 2 because \(t<y\).

Moreover, \(r\in[0,1]\), since \(0\le r<y\le1\). For every \(t\in[0,r)\), monotonicity gives

\(\widetilde g(t)\le\widetilde g(r)<0.\)

Hence \(\widetilde g(t)<0\) for every \(t\in[0,y)\). Its first nonnegative point must satisfy \(\widetilde y\ge y\). Therefore

\(c(\widetilde y;x,a)=\frac{\widetilde y-r}{1-a}\ge\frac{y-r}{1-a}=c(y;x,a).\)

These three cases exhaust all truthful outcomes and prove the required inequality for every clone and every rational location report. Since \(N\) was arbitrary, strategyproofness holds under every rational clone expansion.
