The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite valuation-type set \(\mathcal T\subseteq[0,1]^m\) and rational per-capita item supplies \(q_j\), consider finite markets of size \(N\) whose type counts approximate \(N\mu\) and whose supplies are \(Nq_j\). Determine whether an anonymous Groves rebate functional \(R\), defined as the limit of finite-market rebates on the background type distribution, can attain a specified worst-case limiting ratio of welfare retained after payments to efficient welfare over all \(\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal T)\); if so, compute an optimal \(R\) and its tagged-agent allocation and payment limits, subject to dominant-strategy truthfulness, individual rationality, and weak budget balance.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Input consists of a nonempty finite set \(\mathcal T\subseteq\mathbb Q^m\cap[0,1]^m\), positive rational supplies \(q_j\) with \(\sum_jq_j<1\), and a rational threshold \(\rho\in[0,1]\); assume some type has a positive coordinate. Agents have unit demand, quasilinear utilities, and value zero when unassigned; their type and report domain is \(\mathcal T\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational, summing to one. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Write \(k=|\mathcal T|\) and let \(L\) denote the binary input length; state running times in \(m,k,\tau,L\).
For each \(N\) making every \(Nq_j\) integral, a market has \(N\) agents and \(Nq_j\) copies of item \(j\). Choose uniformly among efficient feasible assignments. Let \(F_N(v)\) be efficient welfare and \(F_N(v_{-i})\) efficient welfare after deleting agent \(i\), retaining the same supplies.
An anonymous Groves mechanism has payments \(t_i(v)=F_N(v_{-i})-\sum_{\ell\ne i}v_\ell(a_\ell)-r_N(v_{-i})\), where \(r_N\) is an arbitrary signed real function of the multiset of other reports. Require dominant-strategy truthfulness, individual rationality, and \(\sum_it_i(v)\ge0\) at every finite profile and every selected assignment.
A family \((r_N)\) induces an admissible limiting functional \(R\) if, for every rational society \(\mu\) and every sequence of profiles with empirical distributions tending to \(\mu\), \(r_N(v_{-i_N})\to R(\mu)\) for every choice of tagged agents \(i_N\). Rebates are per agent and are not divided by \(N\).
Its guarantee is \(G=\inf_{\mu}\inf_{v^{(N)}\to\mu}\liminf_{N\to\infty}(F_N(v^{(N)})-\sum_it_i(v^{(N)}))/F_N(v^{(N)})\), where the inner infimum ranges over empirical-profile sequences and the ratio is defined as one when \(F_N=0\).
For tagged-agent outputs at a rational \(\mu\), use exact replicas with counts \(N\mu_t\), taking \(N\) through common denominator multiples of \(\mu\) and \(q\); tag an existing agent of any positive-mass type. Allocation limits mean assignment-probability limits, and payment limits mean expected-payment limits.

Is deciding whether an admissible limiting functional can achieve \(G\ge\rho\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? What is the complexity of computing an optimal \(R\) and its tagged-agent allocation and payment limits?

For finite markets, suitable geometrically consistent simplicial partitions allow an optimal Groves payment function to be constructed by solving an LP on partition vertices and interpolating; this is a conditional algorithmic result, not a polynomial-time bound in the market input size. [Dufton et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/8262/8121)
No verified result settling this precise distributional limiting problem was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Valuation encoding and the report domain were unspecified: valuations are binary rationals and both true types and reports belong to the given finite set.
  • The allocation model was unspecified: agents have unit demand, item supplies count identical copies of each item type, and unassigned agents receive value zero.
  • Supply conditions were unspecified: each supply is positive and total per-capita supply is strictly below one, carrying over the shortage condition that there are fewer physical items than agents; the valuation set contains a nonzero type.
  • Societies have total mass one and binary rational positive support masses; the worst-case quantifier ranges over these rational societies.
  • The raw Groves function has an extensive welfare component: the limiting rebate is the signed per-agent redistribution r_N, equivalently efficient deleted-agent welfare minus the raw Groves function.
  • Admissibility requires truthfulness, individual rationality, and weak budget balance at every finite profile, rather than only after taking limits.
  • Supply integrality is handled by taking market sizes through denominator multiples of the supplies; empirical type counts may otherwise approach the limiting society arbitrarily.
  • The rebate limit is required to depend only on the background society, independently of the approximating profiles and the deleted agent.
  • The worst-case limiting ratio uses an infimum over societies and approximating sequences followed by a liminf; zero efficient welfare is assigned ratio one.
  • Ties are resolved uniformly among efficient assignments. Tagged-agent outputs use exact rational replicas and existing positive-mass agents, with allocation probabilities and expected payments, because arbitrary approximating sequences need not have identical tagged-agent limits.

Partial progress

I do not settle whether the unrestricted decision problem is in $\mathrm P$. I prove an exact characterization of its optimum, existence of an optimal limiting functional, and the following complexity bounds:

  • The threshold decision problem belongs to $\mathrm{coNP}$.
  • An optimal continuous, piecewise affine functional can be constructed in $2^{\operatorname{poly}(m,k)}\operatorname{poly}(L)$ time.
  • At a queried society of support size $\tau$, tagged allocation limits can be approximated in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon))$ time. Tagged payment limits have this complexity conditional on evaluating the chosen functional.

Consequently, $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness under polynomial many-one reductions would imply $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$. These results leave the polynomial-time question open.

1. Finite-market identities

Write
$$ \Delta_i=F_N(v)-F_N(v_{-i}). $$
Deleting agent $i$ from an efficient assignment, and conversely extending an assignment by leaving $i$ unassigned, gives
$$ 0\le \Delta_i\le v_i(a_i). $$
Thus the payment and truthful utility are
$$ t_i=v_i(a_i)-\Delta_i-r_N(v_{-i}), \qquad u_i=\Delta_i+r_N(v_{-i}). $$
Define total VCG revenue by
$$ B_N(v)=F_N(v)-\sum_i\Delta_i. $$
Then
$$ 0\le B_N(v)\le F_N(v), \qquad \sum_i t_i=B_N(v)-\sum_i r_N(v_{-i}). $$
These identities hold for every selected efficient assignment. In particular, total revenue is independent of that selection.

Any rebate depending only on the other reports preserves dominant-strategy truthfulness: with the other reports fixed, the agent’s utility is true reported-profile welfare minus a term independent of that agent’s report. Truthful reporting selects a maximizer of that welfare.

For nonnegative rebates, individual rationality is immediate. No deficit is equivalent to
$$ \sum_i r_N(v_{-i})\le B_N(v). $$

Consequences of admissibility

For every sequence converging to a rational society $\mu$, admissibility implies
$$ \max_i\left|r_N(v_{-i})-R(\mu)\right|\longrightarrow0. $$
Otherwise, tags chosen along a violating subsequence would contradict admissibility. Hence
$$ \frac1N\sum_i r_N(v_{-i})\longrightarrow R(\mu). $$

Every admissible $R$ is also continuous on rational societies. Indeed, suppose rational societies $\mu_n$ converge to a rational $\mu$. Choose increasingly large exact replicas of $\mu_n$, at sizes $N_n$, so that a tagged rebate differs from $R(\mu_n)$ by less than $1/n$. Complete these profiles at the other admissible sizes with profiles converging to $\mu$. Admissibility at $\mu$ then implies $R(\mu_n)\to R(\mu)$.

2. The welfare LP and its optimal dual face

Index all types by $t\in\mathcal T$. For any nonnegative mass vector $s$, retaining supplies $q$, define
$$ f(s)= \max\left\{ \sum_{t,j}t_jx_{tj}: x_{tj}\ge0,\ \sum_jx_{tj}\le s_t,\ \sum_tx_{tj}\le q_j \right\}. $$
Its dual is
$$ f(s)= \min\left\{ s\cdot u+q\cdot p: u_t,p_j\ge0,\ u_t+p_j\ge t_j \right\}. $$
Because $t_j\in[0,1]$, clipping any dual coordinate above one preserves feasibility and cannot increase the objective. We therefore use the fixed rational polytope
$$ D= \left\{ (u,p)\in[0,1]^{k+m}:u_t+p_j\ge t_j \right\}, $$
and its optimal face
$$ D^*(s)= \left\{(u,p)\in D:s\cdot u+q\cdot p=f(s)\right\}. $$

The assignment matrix is a bipartite network matrix and is totally unimodular. Thus, when $s_t=n_t/N$ and all supplies $Nq_j$ are integral,
$$ F_N(v)=Nf(s), \qquad F_N(v_{-i})=Nf(s-e_t/N) $$
for a type-$t$ agent.

A lattice property, proved directly

If $(u,p)$ and $(u',p')$ belong to $D^*(s)$, then both
$$ (u\vee u',p\wedge p') \quad\text{and}\quad (u\wedge u',p\vee p') $$
are feasible. For example, if the smaller of $p_j,p'_j$ is $p_j$, then
$$ \max\{u_t,u'_t\}+\min\{p_j,p'_j\}\ge u_t+p_j\ge t_j. $$
The other feasibility assertion follows symmetrically.

The sum of the two new objective values equals $2f(s)$. Since each feasible objective is at least $f(s)$, both new points are optimal.

Compactness and repeated joins and meets consequently give optimal points
$$ (u^+(s),p^-(s)), \qquad (u^-(s),p^+(s)), $$
where the superscripts denote coordinatewise maximum and minimum over the optimal face. In particular, these coordinatewise extrema are attained simultaneously.

Define
$$ b_-(s)=q\cdot p^-(s) =f(s)-s\cdot u^+(s), $$
and
$$ b_+(s)=q\cdot p^+(s) =f(s)-s\cdot u^-(s). $$
They satisfy
$$ 0\le b_-(s)\le b_+(s)\le f(s). $$

For a queried society, $f(\mu)$, $b_-(\mu)$ and $b_+(\mu)$ are computable exactly by three rational LPs: the welfare LP, followed by minimization and maximization of $q\cdot p$ on its optimal dual face. Types outside the support can be omitted: their dual utility coordinates impose no additional restriction on prices. These computations therefore take
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
bit operations.

3. Lowest and highest limiting VCG revenues

The following lemma supplies the missing local constraints.

Lemma. For every real society $\mu$ and every empirical-profile sequence $s_N\to\mu$,
$$ b_-(\mu) \le \liminf_N\frac{B_N}{N} \le \limsup_N\frac{B_N}{N} \le b_+(\mu). $$
For rational $\mu$, exact replicas eventually have revenue per agent exactly $b_-(\mu)$. There also exists a sequence converging to $\mu$ along which a subsequence has revenue per agent exactly $b_+(\mu)$.

Proof of the bounds

The polytope $D$ has finitely many vertices, and $f$ is the minimum of their affine objective functions. At $\mu$, every nonoptimal vertex has a positive objective gap. Therefore, in a sufficiently small neighborhood of $\mu$, only vertices belonging to $D^*(\mu)$ can be optimal.

For sufficiently large $N$, this observation applies both to $s_N$ and to $s_N-e_t/N$ whenever type $t$ is present.

Choose a vertex optimal at the deleted profile. Using its affine objective at $s_N$ gives
$$ \Delta_t^{(N)}\le u_t^+(\mu). $$
Choosing instead a vertex optimal at $s_N$ gives
$$ \Delta_t^{(N)}\ge u_t^-(\mu). $$
Consequently,
$$ f(s_N)-s_N\cdot u^+(\mu) \le \frac{B_N}{N} \le f(s_N)-s_N\cdot u^-(\mu). $$
Taking limits proves the bounds.

Exact replicas attain the lower endpoint

At a fixed rational $\mu$, for sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$, only vertices optimal at $\mu$ can minimize the objective at $\mu-\epsilon e_t$. Among those vertices, minimization selects the largest $u_t$. Thus
$$ f(\mu-\epsilon e_t)=f(\mu)-\epsilon u_t^+(\mu) $$
for sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$, when $\mu_t>0$.

Hence, in sufficiently large exact replicas,
$$ \Delta_t^{(N)}=u_t^+(\mu), \qquad \frac{B_N}{N}=b_-(\mu). $$

A perturbation attains the upper endpoint

Fix an optimal primal allocation at $\mu$. Since total supply is less than one, some supported type $z$ has positive unassigned mass. Complementary slackness gives
$$ u_z=0 \qquad\text{for every }(u,p)\in D^*(\mu). $$
Explicitly, the primal-dual objective difference is a sum of nonnegative terms, one of which is
$$ \left(\mu_z-\sum_jx_{zj}\right)u_z. $$
Its coefficient is positive, so optimality forces $u_z=0$.

Consider
$$ s_\epsilon=\mu+\epsilon(\mu-e_z). $$
For sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$, this is a society with the same support. On $D^*(\mu)$, its objective changes by
$$ \epsilon(\mu-e_z)\cdot u=\epsilon\mu\cdot u. $$
Therefore, for sufficiently small $\epsilon$, its optimal face consists precisely of the minimizers of $\mu\cdot u$ over $D^*(\mu)$.

The lattice minimum $u^-(\mu)$ attains that minimum. Because every supported weight $\mu_t$ is positive, every minimizer has
$$ u_t=u_t^-(\mu) \qquad(t\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)). $$
It follows that
$$ b_-(s_\epsilon)=b_+(\mu). $$
If $\mu$ is rational, choose rational $\epsilon_n\downarrow0$ and sufficiently large exact replicas of $s_{\epsilon_n}$. Complete these replicas to a profile sequence converging to $\mu$. The chosen subsequence has the claimed revenue.

If $\mu=e_z$, the perturbation is zero. In that case all supported utility coordinates are already zero and $b_-(\mu)=b_+(\mu)$, so exact replicas suffice. This completes the proof.

4. Exact characterization of the optimal guarantee

Let
$$ S=\left\{\mu\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^k:\sum_t\mu_t=1\right\}. $$

Theorem. The optimal guarantee exists and equals
$$ G_{\mathrm{opt}} = 1- \max_{\substack{\mu\in S\\f(\mu)>0}} \frac{b_+(\mu)-b_-(\mu)}{f(\mu)}. $$
The maximum is attained at a rational society. An optimal limiting functional can be chosen nonnegative, continuous on $S$, rational, and piecewise affine.

Equivalently, the threshold answer is yes precisely when
$$ b_+(\mu)-b_-(\mu)\le(1-\rho)f(\mu) $$
for every rational society $\mu$.

Necessity

Exact replicas, no deficit, and uniform rebate convergence imply
$$ R(\mu)\le b_-(\mu) $$
at every rational society.

If $f(\mu)>0$, the upper-endpoint sequence from Section 3 implies
$$ G \le 1-\frac{b_+(\mu)-R(\mu)}{f(\mu)} \le 1-\frac{b_+(\mu)-b_-(\mu)}{f(\mu)}. $$
Indeed, along its selected subsequence,
$$ \frac{F_N-\sum_i t_i}{F_N} = 1- \frac{B_N/N-N^{-1}\sum_i r_N(v_{-i})}{F_N/N} $$
converges to the first expression on the right. A subsequence is sufficient for an upper bound on the relevant $\liminf$.

A finite rational subdivision

Enumerate the vertices of $D$. Their affine objectives are
$$ \ell_a(s)=s\cdot u^a+q\cdot p^a. $$
Intersect $S$ with the hyperplane arrangement
$$ \ell_a(s)=\ell_b(s). $$
This produces a finite rational polyhedral complex. On the relative interior of each cell $C$:

  • $D^*(s)$ is a fixed face;
  • $f(s)$ is affine;
  • $b_-(s)$ and $b_+(s)$ are constants.

Denote the latter constants by $b_{-,C}$ and $b_{+,C}$.

If $v$ lies in the closure of $C$, then the cell’s optimal dual face is contained in $D^*(v)$. Hence
$$ b_-(v)\le b_{-,C}, \qquad b_+(v)\ge b_{+,C}. $$

Let $V$ be the finite set of vertices of this complex, and set
$$ D_V= \max_{\substack{v\in V\\f(v)>0}} \frac{b_+(v)-b_-(v)}{f(v)}. $$
There is at least one vertex with positive welfare: a pure society supported on a type with a positive coordinate has positive welfare.

For $\mu$ in the relative interior of $C$, express it as
$$ \mu=\sum_{v\in\operatorname{vert}(\overline C)}\alpha_vv, \qquad \alpha_v\ge0,\quad\sum_v\alpha_v=1. $$
Affineness gives
$$ f(\mu)=\sum_v\alpha_vf(v). $$
Also,
$$ b_+(v)-b_-(v)\ge b_{+,C}-b_{-,C}. $$
If the latter difference is positive, every such vertex has positive welfare, because an optimal dual point with price vector $p_C^+$ remains optimal at $v$ and
$$ f(v)\ge q\cdot p_C^+=b_{+,C}>0. $$
It follows that
$$ b_{+,C}-b_{-,C} \le \sum_v\alpha_v\bigl(b_+(v)-b_-(v)\bigr) \le D_V f(\mu). $$
When the difference is zero, the same conclusion is immediate.

Thus the maximum in the theorem equals $D_V$ and is attained at a rational vertex.

Construction of a continuous optimal functional

Put
$$ g=1-D_V. $$
Take a geometrically consistent rational simplicial refinement of the complex. Such a refinement can be obtained recursively: choose a rational relative interior point in every nonempty face, triangulate its boundary, and cone that triangulation to the chosen point. The shared choices on common faces make the refinement consistent.

At every vertex $v$ of the refinement, prescribe
$$ R(v)=b_-(v), $$
and interpolate affinely on each simplex. This defines a continuous nonnegative $R$.

Fix a cell $C$ and a simplex contained in its closure. Every vertex $v$ of that simplex satisfies
$$ R(v)\le b_{-,C} $$
and, by the already proved global bound,
$$ R(v) \ge b_+(v)-(1-g)f(v) \ge b_{+,C}-(1-g)f(v). $$
Affine interpolation therefore gives, throughout $C$,
$$ 0\le R(\mu)\le b_-(\mu), $$
and
$$ R(\mu)\ge b_+(\mu)-(1-g)f(\mu). $$

Realization by finite, feasible Groves rebates

More generally, suppose $R:S\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ is continuous and satisfies $R(\mu)\le b_-(\mu)$.

For a multiset $w$ of $N-1$ reports, let $\nu_w$ be its normalized empirical distribution, and define
$$ c_N(w)=\min_{t\in\mathcal T}\frac{B_N(w\cup\{t\})}{N}, $$
$$ r_N(w)=\min\{R(\nu_w),c_N(w)\}. $$
All admissible sizes have $N>1$, since every supply is positive and total supply is less than one.

The rebate is anonymous and nonnegative. For any full profile $v$,
$$ r_N(v_{-i})\le\frac{B_N(v)}N, $$
so summing proves no deficit. Individual rationality and truthfulness follow from Section 1.

For a convergent profile sequence and any tags, $\nu_{v_{-i}}\to\mu$. Every possible completion $v_{-i}\cup\{t\}$ also converges to $\mu$. Section 3 and finiteness of $\mathcal T$ give
$$ \liminf_N c_N(v_{-i})\ge b_-(\mu)\ge R(\mu). $$
Continuity now yields
$$ r_N(v_{-i})\longrightarrow R(\mu). $$
Thus this family induces $R$ in exactly the required sense.

Guarantee, including zero-welfare societies

For $f(\mu)>0$, the revenue bounds and rebate convergence give
$$ \liminf_N \frac{F_N-\sum_i t_i}{F_N} \ge 1-\frac{b_+(\mu)-R(\mu)}{f(\mu)} \ge g. $$

If $f(\mu)=0$, every supported type must be the zero vector: any supported positive coordinate could receive positive mass of its item and produce positive welfare.

Along a sequence converging to this society, eventually the number of nonzero agents is smaller than every item supply. All such agents can simultaneously receive an item attaining their individual maximum value. Therefore,
$$ F_N=\sum_i\max_jv_{ij}, \qquad \Delta_i=\max_jv_{ij}, \qquad B_N=0. $$
Nonnegative rebates and no deficit force every rebate to be zero. The retained-welfare ratio is one, including the specified zero-welfare convention.

The constructed family consequently achieves $g$, completing the theorem.

5. Computational consequences

Exponential-time exact construction

Let
$$ d=k+m,\qquad h=km+2d. $$
The boxed dual polytope has at most $h^d$ vertices. These can be enumerated by choosing active constraints and solving the corresponding rational linear systems.

Cramer’s rule bounds each dual vertex’s encoding length by $\operatorname{poly}(m,k,L)$. The arrangement hyperplanes inherit polynomial encoding length. Its vertices likewise have polynomial encoding length, since each is determined by at most $k$ independent rational equations.

An arrangement of $H$ hyperplanes in dimension at most $k-1$ has $H^{O(k)}$ faces. Here
$$ H\le h^{2d}+k. $$
Enumerating faces and their rational simplicial refinement therefore gives a conservative running-time bound
$$ 2^{\operatorname{poly}(m,k)}\operatorname{poly}(L). $$
At every relevant vertex, the required welfare and price extrema are computed by polynomial-size LPs.

This constructs both $G_{\mathrm{opt}}$ and an explicit optimal piecewise affine $R$. For fixed $m$ and $k$, the algorithm is polynomial in $L$.

The threshold decision problem is in $\mathrm{coNP}$

If the threshold answer is no, Section 4 supplies a rational arrangement vertex $\mu$ satisfying
$$ b_+(\mu)-b_-(\mu)>(1-\rho)f(\mu). $$
Its binary encoding length is polynomial in $m,k,L$.

A verifier checks that $\mu$ is a society, solves the welfare LP and the two optimal-face price LPs, and verifies this strict rational inequality. These operations take
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,k,L) $$
time. Hence failure of the threshold has polynomial certificates, proving membership in $\mathrm{coNP}$.

Under polynomial many-one reductions, an $\mathrm{NP}$-hard language in $\mathrm{coNP}$ would imply $\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{coNP}$, and taking complements would give equality.

Computing the optimal numerical value

The vertex argument also shows that $G_{\mathrm{opt}}$ is rational with polynomial encoding length. With a threshold oracle, binary search followed by rational reconstruction therefore computes it using polynomially many queries of polynomial length. Since a $\mathrm{coNP}$ oracle can be implemented by complementing an $\mathrm{NP}$ oracle answer, this gives
$$ G_{\mathrm{opt}}\in\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}. $$
This does not establish a polynomial-time algorithm without an oracle, or a compact representation of an optimal functional.

6. A multidimensional class solved exactly

Consider the domain
$$ \mathcal T=\{c_je_j:1\le j\le m\}, \qquad c_j\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1]. $$
Thus each type values exactly one item.

Put
$$ H=\sum_jq_jc_j, \qquad C=\min_jq_jc_j. $$

Proposition. For this class,
$$ G_{\mathrm{opt}}=\frac CH, \qquad R(\mu)=C $$
is optimal.

For a finite profile with $n_j$ agents of type $c_je_j$, welfare is
$$ F_N=\sum_jc_j\min\{n_j,Nq_j\}. $$
Deleting one such agent loses $c_j$ if $n_j\le Nq_j$, and loses zero otherwise. Consequently,
$$ B_N=N\sum_{j:n_j>Nq_j}q_jc_j. $$
Since
$$ \sum_jn_j=N>N\sum_jq_j, $$
at least one type is oversupplied relative to its preferred item. Therefore $B_N\ge NC$. Constant rebates $C$ are feasible and give
$$ \frac{F_N-B_N+NC}{F_N}\ge\frac CH. $$

For the upper bound, choose $a$ with $q_ac_a=C$, and take
$$ \mu_j=q_j\quad(j\ne a), \qquad \mu_a=1-\sum_{j\ne a}q_j>q_a. $$
Here
$$ f(\mu)=H,\qquad b_-(\mu)=C,\qquad b_+(\mu)=H. $$
The theorem gives $G_{\mathrm{opt}}\le C/H$.

The threshold test and optimal constant functional require $O(m)$ rational arithmetic operations and polynomial bit complexity.

For example, with types $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ and supplies $q_1=q_2=1/4$, the optimal guarantee is $1/2$. Thus coordinatewise minimum valuations being zero does not force the multidimensional optimum to be zero.

7. Tagged allocation and payment limits

For allocation counting, I treat identical copies as unlabeled: an assignment records each agent’s item type or unassignment. The guarantee and functional results above do not depend on this convention.

Fix a rational queried society $\mu$, and use only its $\tau$ supported types. Introduce an unassignment column $j=0$. Define the optimal-allocation polytope
$$ P(\mu)= \left\{ x: \begin{array}{l} x_{tj}\ge0,\\ \sum_{j=0}^m x_{tj}=\mu_t,\\ \sum_t x_{tj}\le q_j\quad(j\ge1),\\ \sum_{t,j\ge1}t_jx_{tj}=f(\mu) \end{array} \right\}. $$
Let $x^*(\mu)$ uniquely maximize
$$ \mathcal H(x)=-\sum_{t,j=0}^m x_{tj}\log x_{tj}, \qquad 0\log0=0, $$
over this polytope. Existence follows from compactness and continuity; uniqueness follows from strict concavity.

Then a tagged type-$t$ agent’s limiting probabilities are
$$ \pi_{tj}=\frac{x^*_{tj}(\mu)}{\mu_t}. $$

Proof by counting

For an integral aggregate allocation with counts $Nx_{tj}$, the number of assignments realizing it is
$$ \prod_t\frac{(N\mu_t)!}{\prod_{j=0}^m(Nx_{tj})!}. $$
Uniform Stirling bounds give
$$ \log(\text{count}) = N\left(\mathcal H(x)+\sum_t\mu_t\log\mu_t\right) + O\!\left(\tau(m+1)\log(N+1)\right). $$
There are at most $(N+1)^{\tau(m+1)}$ aggregate arrays.

Optimal feasible lattice arrays approach $x^*$ along every exact-replica sequence. To see this, take a common denominator $D$ of $\mu$ and $q$. The polytope before imposing optimal welfare is a network polytope; its vertices become integral after multiplication by $D$. Its welfare-optimal face introduces no new vertices. Express $x^*$ as a convex combination of its vertices and approximate the coefficients by multiples of $1/n$, summing to one. For $N=nD$, this gives optimal lattice arrays converging to $x^*$.

Outside any fixed neighborhood of $x^*$, uniqueness and compactness give a positive entropy gap. Its exponential counting effect dominates the polynomial number of arrays and the Stirling errors. Aggregate allocations therefore concentrate at $x^*$.

Conditional on an aggregate array, symmetry within type $t$ gives assignment probability $x_{tj}/\mu_t$. Concentration proves the claimed limit.

Payment limits

Section 3 proves that, in sufficiently large exact replicas, a tagged type-$t$ agent’s deletion loss is $u_t^+(\mu)$. Therefore
$$ \overline t_t = \sum_{j=1}^m t_j\pi_{tj} -u_t^+(\mu) -R(\mu). $$
The deletion loss is computable exactly by maximizing $u_t$ on the optimal dual face. Computing all supported losses takes one welfare LP and $\tau$ face LPs, in
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
time.

8. Polynomial-time approximation of tagged outputs

Here I supply the quantitative details missing from the earlier argument.

Let
$$ n=\tau(m+1), \qquad \mu_{\min}=\min_{t\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mu_t. $$
It is enough to consider $0<\varepsilon\le1$.

Smoothing and conversion to probability error

For $0<\delta\le1/2$, define
$$ \mathcal H_\delta(x) = -\sum_{t,j}(x_{tj}+\delta)\log(x_{tj}+\delta). $$
Integrating the absolute derivative of $z\log z$, separately near zero and away from zero, gives
$$ \sup_{x\in[0,1]^n} |\mathcal H_\delta(x)-\mathcal H(x)| \le n\delta\bigl(3+\log(1/\delta)\bigr). $$

A precise optimization argument

$$ g(y^*)+3KR_0\theta<g(y^*)+\eta/4. $$

$$ \nabla g(y)\cdot(z-y)<-\eta/4. $$
The gradient approximation changes this inner product by at most $\eta/8$, since feasible points are at distance at most $2R_0$. Thus every gradient cut would preserve the ball.

The ellipsoid volume bound makes that impossible after
$$ O\!\left( d'^2\log\frac{R_0}{\theta r_0} \right) $$
cuts. Hence some feasible queried point has objective error at most $\eta/2$. Selecting by approximate values increases this error by at most $\eta/4$.

A zero approximate gradient can be handled immediately: its true norm is at most $\eta/(16R_0)$, so convexity bounds the objective error by $\eta/8$.

All cuts have polynomial encoding length. The radius, Lipschitz, and precision bounds show that the rational implementation takes polynomial bit time. Dimension one uses interval bisection with the same argument.
