The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite complete assignment types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), explicit finite sets \(B_t\) of integral bundles with rankings \(\succ_t\), and rational linear upper bounds on per-capita resource use, choose a feasible mass allocation \(z\) with \(z_{t,b}\geq 0\) and \(\sum_{b\in B_t}z_{t,b}=\mu_t\). Uniform within-type randomization gives type \(t\) the bundle lottery \(p_t(b)=z_{t,b}/\mu_t\). Require that no feasible lottery over mass allocations gives every positive-mass type a first-order-stochastically weakly preferred bundle lottery and at least one type a strictly preferred one.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau=|T|\) complete types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. A type specifies preferences, individual feasibility, resource coefficients, and any policy characteristics defining equals.
There are \(m\geq1\) object types. Each type has an explicitly listed, finite, nonempty set \(B_t\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^m\), closed under coordinatewise reductions, and a strict total ranking \(\succ_t\) of its bundles. Equals are precisely individuals of the same complete type.
There are \(k\) resource bounds, with binary-rational coefficients \(a_{r,t,o}\geq0\) and budgets \(q_r\geq0\). A mass allocation is feasible when \(z_{t,b}\geq0\), \(\sum_{b\in B_t}z_{t,b}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{b\in B_t}\sum_{o=1}^m a_{r,t,o}b_o z_{t,b}\leq q_r\) for every \(r\).
Uniform randomization within each type gives every individual of type \(t\) the bundle lottery \(p_t(b)=z_{t,b}/\mu_t\), satisfying equal treatment of equals. For a lottery over feasible mass allocations, the induced bundle lottery is \(\mathbb E[z_{t,b}]/\mu_t\); every realized mass allocation must be feasible.
Call \(z\) ordinally efficient if no such lottery gives every positive-mass type a first-order-stochastically weakly preferred bundle lottery, with strict preference for at least one type. Let \(s=\sum_{t\in T}|B_t|\) and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Can an ordinally efficient rational mass allocation be constructed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,s,L\), or is this construction problem NP-hard?

For finite agents, serial dictatorship with each group of equals consecutive, followed by uniform redistribution within groups, constructs an assignment satisfying equal treatment of equals and ordinal efficiency under downward-closed feasibility; it is polynomial-time with explicit bundles and rankings and polynomial-time feasibility checks.
[Shende (2020)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.01178) gives a polynomial-time constrained ordinally efficient and within-type envy-free rule for finite unit-demand random assignments under arbitrary linear constraints. This is a neighboring model; the search identified no result stated for exactly the mass-allocation problem above.

  • Required nonnegative integral bundle coordinates, strict total rankings, and downward-closed bundle sets, carrying over the anchor's downward-closed feasibility domain; in particular, the zero bundle is available.
  • Specified linear resource use through nonnegative type-dependent coefficients on bundle coordinates and nonnegative per-capita budgets, allowing zero budgets.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

An ordinally efficient rational mass allocation can be constructed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,s,L\), by solving one rational linear program.

1. The feasible polytope. For every resource \(r\), type \(t\), and bundle \(b\in B_t\), define \(c_{r,t,b}=\sum_{o=1}^{m}a_{r,t,o}b_o\). Let \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^s\) be the polytope defined by the given constraints:

\(z_{t,b}\geq0,\qquad \sum_{b\in B_t}z_{t,b}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{b\in B_t}c_{r,t,b}z_{t,b}\leq q_r.\)

This polytope is nonempty. Indeed, nonemptiness and downward closure of \(B_t\) imply \(\mathbf0\in B_t\). Assigning all mass of every type to \(\mathbf0\) consumes no resources and is feasible because every \(q_r\geq0\).

Moreover, every feasible coordinate satisfies \(0\leq z_{t,b}\leq\mu_t\). Thus \(P\) is bounded and closed, hence compact.

2. Lotteries over feasible mass allocations do not enlarge the attainable set. Let \(Z\) be any random mass allocation whose realizations belong to \(P\). Its coordinates are bounded, so their expectations exist. Write \(\bar z=\mathbb E[Z]\). Linearity of expectation gives

\(\bar z_{t,b}\geq0,\qquad \sum_{b\in B_t}\bar z_{t,b}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,b}c_{r,t,b}\bar z_{t,b}=\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{t,b}c_{r,t,b}Z_{t,b}\right]\leq q_r.\)

Consequently, \(\bar z\in P\), and its induced bundle lotteries are exactly those induced by \(Z\). Conversely, every \(y\in P\) is implementable by the constant lottery concentrated on \(y\).

Therefore, the efficiency definition in the question is equivalent to absence of stochastic-dominance improvement by any point of \(P\). This equivalence includes lotteries whose expectations have irrational coordinates.

3. A linear objective strictly increases under every dominance improvement. For each type, put \(n_t=|B_t|\) and list its bundles in preference order:

\(b_{t,1}\succ_t b_{t,2}\succ_t\cdots\succ_t b_{t,n_t}.\)

For every proper prefix, define its mass by \(C_{t,j}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{j}z_{t,b_{t,i}}\), where \(1\leq j<n_t\).

Since both allocations have type mass \(\mu_t>0\), the bundle lottery induced by \(y\) first-order-stochastically weakly dominates that induced by \(z\) precisely when

\(C_{t,j}(y)\geq C_{t,j}(z)\quad\text{for every }1\leq j<n_t.\)

Strict stochastic dominance means that at least one of these inequalities is strict. Indeed, the full-prefix probability is always \(1\); if every proper-prefix probability agrees, taking consecutive differences shows that the two bundle lotteries agree coordinatewise.

Define the linear objective

\(F(z)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{j=1}^{n_t-1}C_{t,j}(z)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_t}(n_t-i)z_{t,b_{t,i}}.\)

The equality follows because coordinate \(z_{t,b_{t,i}}\) appears in precisely \(n_t-i\) proper prefixes. Empty sums, including those for singleton bundle sets, are zero.

If \(y\) weakly stochastically improves every type and strictly improves at least one type, every summand in

\(F(y)-F(z)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{j=1}^{n_t-1}\bigl(C_{t,j}(y)-C_{t,j}(z)\bigr)\)

is nonnegative and at least one is positive. Hence \(F(y)>F(z)\).

4. Construction and correctness. Solve the linear program \(\max\{F(z):z\in P\}\) exactly, and return a rational optimizer \(z^*\). An optimum exists because \(P\) is nonempty and compact.

Suppose some lottery over feasible mass allocations weakly stochastically improved every type’s lottery relative to \(z^*\), with a strict improvement for at least one type. By the expectation argument, its expectation would be a point \(y\in P\) inducing those same improvements. The objective calculation would give \(F(y)>F(z^*)\), contradicting optimality.

Thus \(z^*\) is ordinally efficient under exactly the definition in the question. Uniform randomization within each type gives all individuals of that complete type the same bundle lottery, so equal treatment of equals also holds.

5. Polynomial bit complexity. The linear program has \(s\) variables, \(\tau\) mass equalities, \(k\) resource inequalities, and \(s\) nonnegativity inequalities. Its objective coefficients are integers between \(0\) and \(s-1\).

Computing all resource coefficients requires at most \(kms\) rational multiplications and additions. Each coefficient is a sum of \(m\) products of explicitly encoded input numbers. Products of their denominators provide common denominators of polynomial bit length; consequently, both construction time and the resulting linear-program encoding length are polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,s,L\).

We apply the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem: a linear program with rational data can be solved in time polynomial in its binary encoding length, returning an exact rational optimizer whenever the feasible set is nonempty and the optimum is finite. Both hypotheses were verified above. Polynomial-time solvability of rational linear programming is the classical ellipsoid-method result; see [Gács and Lovász, *Khachiyan’s linear programming algorithm*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0196-6774(80)90002-4).

For completeness, there is also a direct polynomial bound on the output length. A linear objective on a nonempty compact polytope has an optimal vertex. At such a vertex, \(s\) linearly independent active constraints determine the coordinates. Clearing denominators in each selected row produces integer coefficients of polynomial bit length. If their absolute values, including right-hand sides, are bounded by \(2^D\), Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s inequality bound every numerator and denominator determinant by \(s^{s/2}2^{sD}\). Thus each coordinate has bit length \(O(sD+s\log s)\), which is polynomial in the input parameters.

The construction therefore works directly with the listed support and bundles, irrespective of the magnitudes of the mass denominators. Downward closure is used only to guarantee nonemptiness through the zero-bundle allocation.
