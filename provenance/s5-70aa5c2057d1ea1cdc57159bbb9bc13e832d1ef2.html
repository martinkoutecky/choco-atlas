The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given male types \(I\) and female types \(J\), rational masses \(\mu_i,\nu_j\) summing to one on each side, and strict rankings over mutually acceptable type pairs \(E_T\), find a feasible flow \(x_{ij}\geq 0\) with \(\sum_j x_{ij}\leq\mu_i\) and \(\sum_i x_{ij}\leq\nu_j\). Let \(r_i=\mu_i-\sum_k x_{ik}\), \(s_j=\nu_j-\sum_\ell x_{\ell j}\), \(a_{ij}=r_i+\sum_{k:\,j\succ_i k}x_{ik}\), and \(b_{ij}=s_j+\sum_{\ell:\,i\succ_j\ell}x_{\ell j}\). Can type processors connected along \(E_T\), sending \(O(\log(\tau+L))\)-bit messages per round, find a flow satisfying \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E_T}a_{ij}b_{ij}\leq\varepsilon\sum_{(i,j)\in E_T}\mu_i\nu_j\) in deterministic \(\operatorname{poly}(L,\varepsilon^{-1})\log^5(\tau+2)\) rounds, or randomized \(\operatorname{poly}(L,\varepsilon^{-1})\log^2((\tau+L)/(\delta\varepsilon^3))\) rounds with failure probability at most \(\delta\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I,J\) be disjoint finite supports of male and female types, with positive binary rational masses satisfying \(\sum_i\mu_i=\sum_j\nu_j=1\). The society is given by its support list; \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Set \(m=\max\{|I|,|J|\}\), and let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including rational parameters \(0<\varepsilon,\delta<1\).
Each type strictly ranks its neighbors in the mutual acceptability graph \(E_T\subseteq I\times J\); every acceptable partner is preferred to remaining unmatched. A feasible output is a rational flow \(x\) supported on \(E_T\), with \(x_{ij}\geq0\), \(\sum_jx_{ij}\leq\mu_i\), and \(\sum_ix_{ij}\leq\nu_j\).
Define \(r_i=\mu_i-\sum_kx_{ik}\), \(s_j=\nu_j-\sum_\ell x_{\ell j}\), \(a_{ij}=r_i+\sum_{k:j\succ_i k}x_{ik}\), and \(b_{ij}=s_j+\sum_{\ell:i\succ_j\ell}x_{\ell j}\), with sums restricted to acceptable pairs. The required approximation is \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E_T}a_{ij}b_{ij}\leq\varepsilon\sum_{(i,j)\in E_T}\mu_i\nu_j\).
There is one processor per type, initially knowing only its own mass, side, ranking, neighbor identifiers, and unique identifier of \(O(\log(\tau+2))\) bits, together with \(\tau,L,\varepsilon,\delta\). Communication is synchronous along \(E_T\), with distinct messages of \(O(\log(\tau+L))\) bits per neighbor per round and unrestricted local computation; both endpoints must output the same incident flow values. Complexity is measured in rounds as a function of \(m,\tau,L,\varepsilon,\delta\).

Can the required approximation be found deterministically in \(\operatorname{poly}(L,\varepsilon^{-1})\log^5(\tau+2)\) rounds, and can it be found randomly in \(\operatorname{poly}(L,\varepsilon^{-1})\log^2((\tau+L)/(\delta\varepsilon^3))\) rounds with failure probability at most \(\delta\), or is either bound impossible?

For discrete societies with \(n\) agents per side, at most \(\varepsilon|E|\) blocking pairs can be achieved in \(O(\varepsilon^{-3}\log^5 n)\) deterministic rounds or \(O(\varepsilon^{-3}\log^2(n/(\delta\varepsilon^3)))\) randomized rounds. Conclusion

Both bounds are achievable as stated. In fact, there is a deterministic algorithm that produces a rational feasible flow satisfying \(a_{ij}b_{ij}=0\) on every acceptable edge, in \(O((L+1)^4)\) rounds.

The relevant feature of the formulation is that \(L\) is the total binary input length. Consequently, \(\tau=O(L)\), and a polynomial dependence on \(L\) permits gathering and solving each connected component centrally. Below, all communication respects the stipulated message size.

1. Constructing an exact stable rational flow

Fix a connected component \(C\) of the acceptability graph. Write its masses as \(\mu_i=p_i/q_i\) and \(\nu_j=u_j/v_j\), with positive integer denominators. Define the component’s common denominator by

\[ Q_C=\prod_{i\in I\cap C}q_i\prod_{j\in J\cap C}v_j. \]

Each \(\mu_iQ_C\) and \(\nu_jQ_C\) is a positive integer. Moreover, the sum of the binary lengths of these denominators is at most \(L\), so \(\log_2 Q_C\leq L\).

Replace each male type \(i\) by \(\mu_iQ_C\) individual copies and each female type \(j\) by \(\nu_jQ_C\) individual copies. Copies of two types find one another acceptable exactly when their types form an edge of \(E_T\).

Each copy orders acceptable copies first by the original strict ranking of their types, and then, within a type, by copy index. Thus every copy has a strict preference list, and every acceptable copy is preferred to remaining unmatched.

The numbers of copies on the two sides may differ. This causes no difficulty for the following deferred-acceptance procedure:

  • An unmatched male copy with an untried acceptable female copy proposes to its most preferred such copy.
  • A female copy retains the most preferred proposal among its current partner and the new proposer, rejecting the other.
  • Continue until no unmatched male copy has an untried acceptable female copy.

This procedure terminates because each acceptable pair receives at most one proposal.

For completeness, its terminal matching \(M_C\) has no blocking pair. Suppose a male copy \(u\) and female copy \(w\) mutually preferred one another to their terminal partners, with unmatched copies treating every acceptable copy as preferable. Since \(u\) proposes in preference order, it must have proposed to \(w\). She therefore rejected it immediately or subsequently. At that rejection she retained a copy she preferred to \(u\); her retained partner can subsequently only improve. Hence she cannot prefer \(u\) to her terminal partner, a contradiction.

Let \(N_{ij}\) count the matched copy pairs whose types are \(i,j\), and set \(x_{ij}=N_{ij}/Q_C\).

These values are feasible: each copy participates in at most one matched pair, so \(\sum_jx_{ij}\leq\mu_i\) and \(\sum_ix_{ij}\leq\nu_j\). They are supported on acceptable edges.

Now fix an acceptable edge \((i,j)\). Let \(A_{ij}\) count male copies of \(i\) that are unmatched or matched to a type ranked below \(j\). Let \(B_{ij}\) count female copies of \(j\) that are unmatched or matched to a type ranked below \(i\). By the definitions in the question, \(a_{ij}=A_{ij}/Q_C\) and \(b_{ij}=B_{ij}/Q_C\).

If both counts were positive, choose one copy counted by each. Their types are mutually acceptable, and each copy strictly prefers the other to its current partner, because preferences between different types preserve the original rankings. They would form a blocking pair of \(M_C\). Therefore \(A_{ij}B_{ij}=0\), proving \(a_{ij}b_{ij}=0\).

Applying this construction independently in every component gives a feasible flow with \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E_T}a_{ij}b_{ij}=0\). Isolated vertices simply remain unmatched.

2. The output has polynomial binary length

For every edge in \(C\), \(0\leq N_{ij}\leq Q_C\), since the total mass on either side of \(C\) is at most \(1\). Consequently, both \(N_{ij}\) and \(Q_C\) have \(O(L+1)\) binary bits. The representation \(N_{ij}/Q_C\) need not be reduced.

There are at most \(\tau^2\) acceptable edges. Including their endpoint identifiers, the complete output list therefore has binary length \(O(\tau^2(L+\log(\tau+2)))=O((L+1)^3)\).

The copy construction can be exponentially large, but it is finite. Its computation occurs entirely at the component’s root, and unrestricted local computation is expressly allowed.

3. A deterministic implementation with bounded messages

We give a conservative communication schedule sufficient for the claimed bound.

Each vertex’s input record contains its identifier, side, mass, and ranked neighbor identifiers. Across all vertices, these records require at most

\[ O\bigl(L+\tau^2\log(\tau+2)+\tau\log(L+2)\bigr) \]

bits, including framing information. Since \(\tau=O(L)\), this is \(O((L+1)^3)\).

Choose a publicly known polynomial bound \(B=O((L+1)^3)\) large enough for both an entire component’s input records and its output list. Such a bound depends only on the known parameters and fixed encoding conventions.

Build a spanning forest. For \(\tau\) rounds, vertices flood the smallest identifier they have seen. Every vertex then knows its component’s minimum identifier: every component has diameter at most \(\tau-1\). That vertex becomes the root.

A breadth-first wave from each root builds a spanning tree in another \(O(\tau)\) rounds. Each vertex records its depth and parent, breaking simultaneous choices by identifier. One further exchange identifies children. These messages contain only identifiers, depths, and control information, requiring \(O(\log(\tau+L))\) bits.

Gather the inputs. Process tree depths in decreasing order. Reserve \(B\) rounds for each depth. Every vertex at that depth sends its collected subtree records to its parent as a framed, padded bit stream.

Inductively, all descendant records are available before a vertex’s sending phase. Each stream fits within the bound \(B\). A parent can receive different streams from its different children simultaneously, as permitted by the model. Thus all component roots obtain their full component inputs within \(O(\tau B)\) rounds.

Compute locally. Each root performs the construction from Section 1 and serializes the resulting edge-flow list. This requires no additional communication.

Return the outputs. Process depths in increasing order, again reserving \(B\) rounds per depth. Every vertex at the current depth sends the entire component output list to each child. After \(O(\tau B)\) rounds, every vertex has the same list as its component root.

Each endpoint extracts its incident values from this identical list, so both endpoints output precisely the same rational value even when their edge is outside the spanning tree.

During serialization, messages need carry only a constant number of data bits and control information. Thus they satisfy the required \(O(\log(\tau+L))\)-bit bound.

The total number of rounds is \(O(\tau+\tau B)=O((L+1)^4)\).

4. Comparison with the requested bounds

The algorithm achieves exact stability, so it satisfies the required approximation for every \(0<\varepsilon<1\).

Its deterministic round count is bounded by \(O((L+1)^4\log^5(\tau+2))\), which has the requested form \(\operatorname{poly}(L,\varepsilon^{-1})\log^5(\tau+2)\).

The same deterministic algorithm is also a randomized algorithm with failure probability zero. Its round count is bounded by \(O((L+1)^4\log^2((\tau+L)/(\delta\varepsilon^3)))\), which has the second requested form.

These conclusions apply to arbitrary strict rankings and arbitrary positive binary rational masses. The [Ostrovsky–Rosenbaum theorem](https://web.cs.ucla.edu/~rafail/PUBLIC/181.pdf) provides polylogarithmic rounds in the discrete society’s size; the present argument instead uses the polynomial dependence on total input length permitted by this question.

2. Construction of a stable matching of copies

For a component \(C\), the product \(Q_C\) contains every mass denominator as a factor. Hence all copy counts \(\mu_iQ_C\) and \(\nu_jQ_C\) are positive integers.

The bound \(\log_2 Q_C\leq L\) follows from summing the logarithms of the denominators and bounding each logarithm by its encoded binary length. Thus the copy instance is finite, although potentially exponentially large.

The specified ordering gives each copy a strict preference list: original type rankings determine comparisons between different types, and copy indices resolve comparisons within a type. Acceptability remains mutual.

The deferred-acceptance procedure applies even when the two sides have unequal numbers of copies. Its termination argument is valid because proposals are restricted to previously untried pairs.

The terminal-stability proof is also valid:

  • A male copy that ends unmatched has exhausted its acceptable list.
  • A male copy that ends matched has proposed to every acceptable copy it prefers to its terminal partner.
  • Consequently, either male copy in a purported blocking pair must previously have proposed to the female copy.
  • Rejection implies that the female copy retained someone she preferred, and subsequent replacements only improve her partner.

These statements contradict the female copy’s required preference in a blocking pair. No external stable-matching theorem is needed.

3. Aggregation establishes the required stability condition

The definition \(x_{ij}=N_{ij}/Q_C\) produces nonnegative rational values supported on acceptable edges. The row and column capacity inequalities follow directly from the number of copies of each type and the fact that a matching uses each copy at most once.

The identities \(a_{ij}=A_{ij}/Q_C\) and \(b_{ij}=B_{ij}/Q_C\) correctly account for unmatched copies and copies assigned to strictly worse types.

If both counts were positive, the selected male and female copies would each strictly prefer the other to their current assignment. Their preferences follow from the original rankings between different types, so the within-type ordering does not affect this argument. They would form a blocking pair.

Therefore \(a_{ij}b_{ij}=0\) on every acceptable edge. Combining components introduces no additional acceptable edges, and isolated vertices can remain unmatched. The resulting sum is zero and satisfies the approximation inequality.

The bound \(N_{ij}\leq Q_C\) is correct: the total mass on either side of any component is at most the globally normalized mass \(1\).

There are at most \(\tau^2\) acceptable edges. Including endpoint identifiers gives the stated output-length bound
\(O(\tau^2(L+\log(\tau+2)))=O((L+1)^3)\).

The potentially exponential local computation is expressly permitted by the model. This establishes a polynomial round bound; it does not establish polynomial sequential computation time.

Processing depths in decreasing order ensures that a vertex has received all descendant records before transmitting its subtree records. Simultaneous reception from multiple children is allowed because the model permits a distinct message on every incident edge in each round. Output distribution. Processing depths in increasing order ensures that each sending vertex already possesses the component output list. Broadcasting that list to children takes another \(O(\tau B)\) rounds. Message size. Serializing records across multiple rounds avoids assuming that a mass or flow value fits in one message. Constant-sized data chunks and the stated control information satisfy the \(O(\log(\tau+L))\)-bit restriction.

The total \(O(\tau+\tau B)=O((L+1)^4)\) round bound follows.
