The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a finite support \(\mathcal A\subseteq 2^C\) of approval types, and positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_A\) summing to \(1\), find a nonempty committee \(S\subseteq C\) maximizing \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A\mathbf 1[|A\cap S|\ge\lceil |S|/2\rceil]\). Classify the exact problem in terms of \(|C|\), \(|\mathcal A|\), and the mass encoding length; in particular, determine the complexity of deciding whether the optimum is \(1\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a finite candidate set with \(m=|C|\ge 1\). A society is given by its support: an explicit list \(\mathcal A\subseteq 2^C\) of \(\tau=|\mathcal A|\) approval types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_A\), where \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval types; let \(L\) be the total mass encoding length. Empty approval types are allowed.
A committee is any nonempty subset \(S\subseteq C\); its size is unrestricted. Type \(A\) is satisfied exactly when \(|A\cap S|\ge\lceil |S|/2\rceil\), so equality counts as satisfaction.
Define \(F(S)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A\mathbf 1[|A\cap S|\ge\lceil |S|/2\rceil]\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}=\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq C}F(S)\). Any maximizing committee is an acceptable optimizer.

Is computing an exact maximizing committee polynomial-time solvable or NP-hard, and is deciding whether \(\operatorname{OPT}=1\) in \(\mathrm P\) or NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

For explicitly listed, equally weighted voters, deciding whether some nonempty committee satisfies every voter is NP-hard; computing majority-threshold winners is reported FPT in either the number of candidates or the number of voters ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.06641)).

  • Define L as the total binary encoding length of the masses; approval sets are listed explicitly.

1. Result

Computing an exact maximizing committee is NP-hard. Deciding whether \(\operatorname{OPT}=1\) is NP-complete. Thus neither problem has a polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

Both problems are fixed-parameter tractable in either \(\m\) or \(\tau\), with polynomial dependence on \(\L\). The arguments below establish this directly for the support-mass representation.

2. Satisfaction and unanimity

Write the distinct support types as \(\mathcal A=\{A_1,\ldots,A_\tau\}\). For every committee \(\S\), integer-valuedness gives
\[ \]
A type \(\A_i\) is satisfied exactly when \(\2|A_i\cap S|-|S|\ge 0\): this is equivalent to \(\|A_i\cap S|\ge\lceil |S|/2\rceil\), including equality.

Since every mass is strictly positive and the masses sum to \(\1\),

\(\displaystyle F(S)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\text{ satisfies every }A_i.\)

Consequently, deciding whether \(\operatorname{OPT}=1\) depends only on the support, not on the particular positive masses. An empty approval type makes this decision immediately negative, because committees must be nonempty.

3. Hardness for the support-mass formulation

We use the established theorem that, given explicitly listed voters, deciding whether a nonempty committee satisfies every voter under the threshold \(\|S|/2\) is NP-hard. This is precisely Theorem 8 of [Faliszewski, Slinko, and Talmon](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.06641). Its threshold agrees with the present ceiling threshold because intersection sizes are integers, and its committees are explicitly required to be nonempty.

Take an instance of that problem with \(\n\ge 1\) explicitly listed voters and \(\m\) candidates. Remove duplicate approval ballots, obtaining distinct types \(\A_1,\ldots,A_\tau\), where \(\1\le\tau\le n\). Removing duplicates preserves the existence of a committee satisfying everyone.

To ensure short encodings even if “binary-rational” is interpreted as requiring dyadic rationals, set

\(\displaystyle D=2^{\lceil\log_2\tau\rceil},\qquad \mu_{A_i}=\frac1D\ (1\le i<\tau),\qquad \mu_{A_\tau}=\frac{D-\tau+1}{D}.\)

All these masses are positive: \(\D\ge\tau\). Their sum is

\(\displaystyle \frac{\tau-1+D-\tau+1}{D}=1.\)

Each mass has encoding length \(\O(\log(\tau+1))\), so the total length is \(\L=O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\). Deduplication and constructing these masses take polynomial time in the original explicit input length.

By Section 2,

\(\displaystyle \text{the original voters admit a unanimously satisfactory nonempty committee} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{OPT}=1 \text{ in the constructed society}.\)

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction, proving NP-hardness of the unanimity decision problem.

The problem also belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). A certificate is the \(\m\)-bit incidence vector of a nonempty committee. For each support type, count its approved committee members and check the threshold. This takes \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time and does not require summing masses. Hence the problem is NP-complete.

Finally, an exact optimizer would solve this decision problem: compute its maximizing committee and check whether it satisfies every support type. Therefore computing any exact maximizing committee is NP-hard, regardless of how ties are broken.

4. Exact algorithm parameterized by candidates

Enumerate the \(\2^m-1\) nonempty committees, compute each score exactly, and retain a maximizer.

Here is an explicit bit-complexity bound under the usual binary numerator/denominator encoding. Write \(\mu_{A_i}=a_i/b_i\), and define

\(\displaystyle Q=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}b_i,\qquad w_i=a_i(Q/b_i).\)

Then \(\w_i\) are positive integers, \(\sum_i w_i=Q\), and

\(\displaystyle F(S)=\frac1Q\sum_{i:\,A_i\text{ is satisfied by }S}w_i.\)

The binary lengths of \(\Q\) and every \(\w_i\) are \(\O(L)\). Using elementary integer arithmetic, preprocessing takes \(\O(L^3)\) bit operations. For each committee, straightforward counting and score summation take \(\O(m\tau\log(m+1)+\tau L)\) bit operations.

Thus a conservative exact running time is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(L^3+2^m\bigl(m\tau\log(m+1)+\tau L\bigr)\right).\)

In particular, both optimization and unanimity decision have running time \(\2^m\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\).

5. Exact algorithm parameterized by support size

Candidate signatures and integer feasibility

For each candidate \(\c\), define its signature

\(\displaystyle T(c)=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}:c\in A_i\}.\)

Let \(\mathcal T\) be the set of signatures occurring among candidates, let \(\d=|\mathcal T|\), and let \(\n_T\) count candidates with signature \(\T\). Then

\(\displaystyle d\le\min\{m,2^\tau\}.\)

Introduce one integer variable \(\x_T\) per occurring signature, representing how many candidates of that signature the committee contains. Exactly the realizable nonempty committees, up to interchange of candidates with the same signature, are described by

\(\displaystyle 0\le x_T\le n_T\quad(T\in\mathcal T),\qquad \sum_{T\in\mathcal T}x_T\ge1,\qquad x_T\in\mathbb Z.\)

For such a committee,

\(\displaystyle |S|=\sum_Tx_T,\qquad |A_i\cap S|=\sum_{T:\,i\in T}x_T.\)

Therefore satisfaction of type \(\A_i\) is exactly the linear inequality

\(\displaystyle \sum_{T\in\mathcal T}\bigl(2\mathbf1[i\in T]-1\bigr)x_T\ge0.\)

For any \(\B\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), impose these inequalities for every \(\i\in B\), together with the bounds and nonemptiness constraint. This integer program is feasible exactly when some nonempty committee satisfies all types indexed by \(\B\).

All coefficients are integers. The satisfaction coefficients are \(\{-1,1\}\), the bounds are at most \(\m\), and the program has \(\d\) integer variables and \(\O(d+\tau)\) inequalities. Its encoding length is polynomial in \(\m,\tau\).

Fixed-dimensional integer programming decides feasibility in time \(\2^{O(d^3)}\operatorname{poly}(H)\), where \(\H\) is the program’s binary encoding length. This conservative bound follows from Lenstra’s algorithm; it is stated explicitly in [Kannan’s primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/800061.808749). All its hypotheses hold here. A feasible vector can also be recovered; alternatively, bounded-coordinate binary search uses \(\O(d\log(m+1))\) feasibility calls.

Optimization by enumerating satisfied-type requirements

Enumerate all \(\2^\tau\) subsets \(\B\). For each feasible subset, compute

\(\displaystyle W(B)=\sum_{i\in B}\mu_{A_i},\)

and choose a feasible subset \(\B_{\max}\) of largest weight. Recover a committee satisfying its requirements.

To prove optimality, let \(\S^\star\) be an optimal committee and let \(\B^\star\) be its actual set of satisfied types. Then \(\B^\star\) is feasible and \(\W(B^\star)=\operatorname{OPT}\).

Conversely, for every feasible \(\B\), a witnessing committee \(\S_B\) satisfies at least those types. Since masses are positive,

\(\displaystyle W(B)\le F(S_B)\le\operatorname{OPT}.\)

Thus the largest feasible requirement weight is exactly \(\operatorname{OPT}\). Any committee witnessing feasibility of \(\B_{\max}\) has score at least that weight, and hence is an exact optimizer.

The resulting running time is

\(\displaystyle 2^\tau\,2^{O(d^3)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \;\le\; 2^{O(8^\tau)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L).\)

For unanimity decision, solve just the integer program with \(\B=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\). Its running time is

\(\displaystyle 2^{O(d^3)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \;\le\; 2^{O(8^\tau)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L).\)

These bounds establish fixed-parameter tractability in the support size \(\tau\), with polynomial dependence on the binary mass encoding length \(\L\). No expansion into individual voters is needed.
