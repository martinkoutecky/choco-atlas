The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(k\) goods, \(\tau\) rational utility vectors \(v_t\), type masses \(\mu_t\), and per-capita supply \(q\) of each good, find prices \(p_i \ge 0\) and allocations \(x_{ti} \ge 0\) maximizing \(\sum_{t,i} v_{ti}x_{ti}\), subject to \(\sum_i x_{ti} \le \mu_t\), \(\sum_t x_{ti} \le q\), and the envy-free condition that whenever \(x_{ti}>0\), good \(i\) maximizes the type's utility \(v_{ti}-p_i\) among the goods and the outside option of utility \(0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m:=k\ge2\) goods, nonnegative binary-rational unit-demand values \(v_{ti}\), and a common binary-rational per-capita supply \(q>0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An allocation \(x_{ti}\ge0\) is the mass of type \(t\) receiving good \(i\), with \(\sum_i x_{ti}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{ti}\le q\). Prices \(p_i\ge0\) are common to all types; the outside option has utility \(0\).
Write \(U_t=\max\{0,\max_i(v_{ti}-p_i)\}\). Envy-freeness requires \(x_{ti}>0\Rightarrow v_{ti}-p_i=U_t\), and \(\sum_i x_{ti}<\mu_t\Rightarrow U_t=0\). Tied maximizing choices may receive arbitrary shares of a type's mass; supply may remain unsold.
The objective is to maximize \(\sum_{t,i}v_{ti}x_{ti}\). For the decision version, the input also includes a nonnegative binary-rational welfare threshold \(W\).

Is deciding whether an envy-free allocation and prices achieve welfare at least \(W\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete unit-demand welfare-optimal envy-free pricing is polynomial-time solvable offline; a published streaming result asserts that approximating every coordinate of a welfare-optimal price vector requires \(\Omega(\ell)\) space, even with two goods and \(\ell\) copies per good. No result settling the exact finite-support rational-mass decision problem above was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total society mass to one, making q a per-capita supply.
  • Use nonnegative binary-rational values and a strictly positive binary-rational supply; allow at least two goods and identify m with k.
  • Complete envy-freeness for unallocated mass: the outside option must maximize utility whenever some mass of a type receives no good.
  • Allow the allocation to resolve utility ties by splitting type mass among maximizing goods and the outside option.
  • Permit unsold supply without imposing an additional market-clearing condition.
  • Introduce a binary-rational welfare threshold W to state the decision complexity question, and let L include its encoding.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

In fact, the maximum welfare over envy-free outcomes equals the maximum welfare over all feasible allocations. Prices supporting an optimal allocation are obtained from the dual of the welfare linear program.

1. The welfare linear program and its dual

Consider the linear program \((P)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad & \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{m}v_{ti}x_{ti}\\ \text{subject to}\quad & \sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{ti}\le\mu_t &&(t=1,\ldots,\tau),\\ & \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}x_{ti}\le q &&(i=1,\ldots,m),\\ & x_{ti}\ge0 &&(t=1,\ldots,\tau,\ i=1,\ldots,m). \end{aligned} \]

Its feasible solutions are exactly the feasible allocations, without imposing envy-freeness. The zero allocation is feasible, and every variable satisfies \(0\le x_{ti}\le\mu_t\). Consequently, the feasible region is compact and the optimum is finite and attained.

Associate nonnegative dual variables \(u_t\) with the type constraints and \(p_i\) with the supply constraints. The dual program \((D)\) is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad & \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tu_t+q\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_i\\ \text{subject to}\quad & u_t+p_i\ge v_{ti} &&(t=1,\ldots,\tau,\ i=1,\ldots,m),\\ & u_t\ge0,\quad p_i\ge0. \end{aligned} \]

The dual is feasible: take \(p_i=0\) and \(u_t=\max_i v_{ti}\). By linear-programming strong duality, there are optimal solutions \(x^*\) and \((u^*,p^*)\) with equal objective values.

2. Optimal dual variables support an envy-free allocation

We prove that \(x^*\), together with the common prices \(p^*\), satisfies every envy-freeness requirement in the question.

For any primal and dual feasible solutions, the objective difference is

\[ \begin{aligned} &\sum_t\mu_tu_t+q\sum_i p_i-\sum_{t,i}v_{ti}x_{ti}\\ &\qquad= \sum_tu_t\left(\mu_t-\sum_i x_{ti}\right) +\sum_i p_i\left(q-\sum_t x_{ti}\right) +\sum_{t,i}(u_t+p_i-v_{ti})x_{ti}. \end{aligned} \]

Every summand on the right is nonnegative by feasibility. For the optimal solutions, the difference is zero by strong duality. Therefore,

\(x^*_{ti}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ u^*_t+p^*_i=v_{ti}\),

and

\(\sum_i x^*_{ti}<\mu_t\ \Longrightarrow\ u^*_t=0\).

Define the actual maximum utility at these prices by

\(U_t=\max\{0,\max_i(v_{ti}-p^*_i)\}\).

Dual feasibility and \(u^*_t\ge0\) imply \(U_t\le u^*_t\). We now prove equality for every type.

  • If \(u^*_t=0\), then \(0\le U_t\le u^*_t=0\), so \(U_t=u^*_t\).
  • If \(u^*_t>0\), the second implication above forces \(\sum_i x^*_{ti}=\mu_t\). Since \(\mu_t>0\), some \(x^*_{ti}>0\). For that good, the first implication gives \(v_{ti}-p^*_i=u^*_t\), hence \(U_t\ge u^*_t\). Thus again \(U_t=u^*_t\).

It follows that

\(x^*_{ti}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ v_{ti}-p^*_i=U_t\),

and

\(\sum_i x^*_{ti}<\mu_t\ \Longrightarrow\ U_t=0\).

These are exactly the required envy-freeness conditions. They also cover types split among tied goods or between a good and the outside option.

For completeness, the same objective identity gives

\(\sum_t x^*_{ti}<q\ \Longrightarrow\ p^*_i=0\).

This additional property is permitted by the problem, which allows unsold supply.

3. Equality of the two welfare optima

Let \(F\) be the optimal value of \((P)\).

Every envy-free allocation is primal feasible, so its welfare is at most \(F\). Conversely, Section 2 constructs an envy-free allocation and prices whose welfare is exactly \(F\).

Therefore, the maximum envy-free welfare equals \(F\). In particular, the decision answer is yes if and only if \(F\ge W\).

4. Exact polynomial-time algorithm

Use the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: a linear program with rational coefficients can be solved exactly in time polynomial in its binary description length, including recovery of a rational optimal solution when a finite optimum exists.

The hypotheses hold here:

  • \((P)\) has \(m\tau\) variables and \(\tau+m\) capacity constraints, besides nonnegativity.
  • All coefficients are input rationals or the constants \(0\) and \(1\).
  • Its description length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
  • As established above, it is feasible and has an attained finite optimum.

Thus we can compute \(F\) exactly and compare it with the rational threshold \(W\) in polynomial time. If an explicit envy-free outcome is desired, solve \((D)\) as well and output \(x^*,p^*\); the preceding proof verifies that outcome.

No expansion of a mass into discrete copies is needed. Binary-encoded masses and supply appear directly as linear-program coefficients.

The streaming setting studied in the [cited dissertation, Chapter 5](https://api.drum.lib.umd.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/a5a3004e-fb03-47f5-86b3-c9fe8fea3146/content) imposes memory restrictions. Such restrictions do not preclude this offline polynomial-time algorithm.
