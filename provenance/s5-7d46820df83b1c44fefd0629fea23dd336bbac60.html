A polynomial number of arithmetic operations is insufficient unless intermediate rational numbers also have polynomial bit length. Here that can be proved explicitly.

  • For nonzero exponential rate, the primitive introduces only powers of two, with exponent at most $n+1$.
  • Substitution of endpoints introduces only integer coefficients.

Consequently, all polynomial coefficients of $g_\ell$ can be stored over the common denominator
$$ H_\ell=B^\ell J^{\ell-1}. $$
Its binary length is
$$ O(\ell n\log(n+2)). $$

Numerators during convolution

Here is a direct bound on coefficient growth. For a polynomial, let its coefficient norm be the sum of the absolute values of its coefficients.

The integer numerators of the two kernel polynomials have coefficient norm at most
$$ A=2(n+1)2^n n!. $$
This follows by bounding the norm of $(r^2-z^2)^{h-1}$ by $2^n$, each derivative coefficient multiplier by $n!$, and the number of derivatives by $n+1$.

Let $N_\ell$ be the maximum coefficient norm of a numerator polynomial stored over $H_\ell$. One convolution step has the following bounds:

  • Substituting $z-u$ for $z$ increases coefficient norm by at most $2^n$.
  • Polynomial multiplication multiplies coefficient norms.
  • Clearing an integration denominator with $J$ and taking a primitive increases norm by at most $J(n+1)n!$.
  • Substituting an endpoint increases norm by at most $(s+1)^{n+1}$.
  • Only polynomially many contributions are merged.

For example, the following uniform bound suffices:
$$ N_{\ell+1} \leq 100(s+1)^6\,N_\ell A\,2^n J(n+1)n!(s+1)^{n+1}. $$
The logarithm of the multiplier is $O(n\log(n+s+2))$. Induction gives
$$ \log_2(N_\ell+1)=O(\ell n\log(n+s+2)). $$
Thus all convolution coefficients have polynomial binary length.

$$ E_0= B^sJ^s((k-1)!)^2(C!)^{n+2}. $$
