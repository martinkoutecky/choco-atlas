Partial results: two polynomial-time subclasses and a stronger feasibility theorem

The general computational question remains unresolved here. The following results are proved:

  • Arbitrary additive valuations: for every prescribed supply vector, a distribution with spread at most $2\Lambda$ can be constructed using $m$ value queries and polynomially many bit operations.
  • Supplies near a cube vertex: if

$$\sum_{g=1}^m\min\{q_g,1-q_g\}\leq2,$$
a distribution with spread at most $5\Lambda$ can be constructed with no value queries and polynomially many bit operations.

  • Every promised instance is feasible, even with the additional requirement that every supported bundle has cardinality either

$$\left\lfloor\sum_gq_g\right\rfloor \quad\text{or}\quad \left\lceil\sum_gq_g\right\rceil.$$
Such a distribution can have spread strictly less than $4\Lambda$ and at most $m+1$ positive rational masses of polynomial bit length.

All three results use only normalization and the marginal bound; subadditivity and global nonnegativity are unnecessary for these results. The third result adds a cardinality restriction to the feasibility conclusions in the notes.

1. Preliminary bounds and elimination of empty bundles

Write $[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and let $\mathbf 1_S$ denote the incidence vector of $S$. The marginal promise implies
$$ |v(S)-v(T)|\leq\Lambda|S\mathbin{\triangle}T| $$
for all bundles $S,T$: remove the elements of $S\setminus T$ and then add the elements of $T\setminus S$, applying the marginal bound at every step. In particular,
$$ |v(\{g\})|\leq\Lambda. $$

We will sometimes construct a distribution allowing the empty bundle and then remove it.

Lemma 1. Suppose rational masses $x_S\geq0$, including possibly $x_\emptyset$, satisfy
$$ \sum_Sx_S=1, \qquad \sum_{S:g\in S}x_S=q_g. $$
If $\sum_gq_g\geq1$, the empty mass can be eliminated while preserving every marginal. The only new bundles needed are singletons.

Proof. Put $z=x_\emptyset$. If $z=0$, nothing is required. Otherwise,
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{S\ne\emptyset}(|S|-1)x_S &=\sum_gq_g-\sum_{S\ne\emptyset}x_S\\ &=\sum_gq_g-(1-z)\\ &\geq z. \end{aligned} $$
Consequently, there are rational numbers $y_S$ for bundles of size at least two satisfying
$$ 0\leq y_S\leq x_S, \qquad \sum_{|S|\geq2}(|S|-1)y_S=z. $$
They can be chosen greedily: take whole available masses until the remaining amount is at most the next bundle's capacity, and take the appropriate fraction of that bundle's mass.

Remove the empty mass. For every selected bundle $S$, remove mass $y_S$ from $S$ and add mass $y_S$ to each singleton $\{g\}$ with $g\in S$.

Every category marginal is preserved. The change in total mass is
$$ -z-\sum_Sy_S+\sum_S|S|y_S =-z+\sum_S(|S|-1)y_S =0. $$
All resulting masses are nonnegative and rational, and all supported bundles are nonempty. $\square$

If the initial masses have common denominator $D$, the greedy procedure uses whole masses except for at most one bundle. Its resulting masses therefore have a common denominator dividing $Dd$ for some integer $d$ with $1\leq d\leq m-1$. Thus this operation preserves polynomial bit length and adds at most $m$ distinct bundles.

2. Proven polynomial-time algorithm for additive valuations

Here additivity is an additional hypothesis:
$$ v(S)=\sum_{g\in S}a_g, \qquad a_g=v(\{g\}). $$
The algorithm does not infer additivity from singleton queries. The coefficients may have either sign.

Theorem 2. Under this additional hypothesis, the required distribution can be constructed with $m$ value queries and polynomially many bit operations. Its spread is at most $2\Lambda$, and its support has at most $2m+1$ bundles.

Construction

Query all singleton values and put
$$ M=\max_g|a_g|\leq\Lambda. $$
Initially, assign mass one to the empty bundle.

Process the categories in any fixed order. When processing category $g$:

  • If $a_g\geq0$, select total mass exactly $q_g$ from the currently lowest-valued bundles.
  • If $a_g<0$, select total mass exactly $q_g$ from the currently highest-valued bundles.

Split the boundary mass if necessary. Add category $g$ to every selected bundle.

Before this step, no bundle contains $g$. Thus the operation gives category $g$ marginal exactly $q_g$ and preserves all previously established marginals.

Spread invariant

We prove that the current value spread never exceeds $M$.

Initially it is zero. Suppose the current minimum and maximum values are $\ell,h$, where
$$ h-\ell\leq M. $$
Consider a step with $a_g=a\geq0$. The cases $q_g=0$ and $q_g=1$ respectively leave all values unchanged or shift all values by $a$, so they preserve the spread.

Otherwise, there is a threshold $t\in[\ell,h]$ such that every selected bundle has value at most $t$, and every unselected bundle has value at least $t$. After the step:

  • selected values lie in $[\ell+a,t+a]$;
  • unselected values lie in $[t,h]$.

Differences within either group are at most $h-\ell\leq M$. For a selected old value $u$ and an unselected value $w$,
$$ (u+a)-w\leq t+a-t=a\leq M, $$
and
$$ w-(u+a)\leq h-\ell-a\leq M. $$
Therefore the new spread is at most $M$.

For a negative coefficient, negate all current values. Selecting the highest original values becomes selecting the lowest negated values, and adding $a_g$ becomes adding the nonnegative number $-a_g$. The same argument applies. This proves the invariant.

Removing the empty bundle

After all categories have been processed, the distribution realizes $q$ and has spread at most $M$.

If it contains the empty bundle, its supported values include zero. Consequently every supported value belongs to $[-M,M]$. Apply Lemma 1. Every newly introduced singleton also has value in $[-M,M]$, so the resulting spread is at most
$$ 2M\leq2\Lambda<5\Lambda+1. $$

Query and bit complexity

At each category step, at most one existing mass is split. Thus the intermediate support has size at most $m+1$. Lemma 1 adds at most $m$ singleton bundles.

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the input supplies. Its bit length is at most the total supply encoding length, hence at most $L$. Every mass before empty elimination is a multiple of $1/D$: splitting subtracts sums of existing masses from a supply $q_g$. Lemma 1 increases the denominator by at most a factor of $m-1$.

Bundle values used for sorting are sums of at most $m$ rational singleton answers. Their bit lengths are polynomial in $m,L$. Sorting polynomially many bundles at each of $m$ steps, updating masses, and eliminating the empty bundle therefore require polynomially many bit operations. $\square$

3. Proven polynomial-time algorithm when the supplies are near a cube vertex

Define
$$ \delta=\sum_{g=1}^m\min\{q_g,1-q_g\}. $$

Theorem 3. If $\delta\leq2$, the required distribution can be constructed with no value queries and polynomially many bit operations. Its spread is at most $5\Lambda$.

A distribution supported near a fixed bundle

Set
$$ R=\{g:q_g>1/2\}, \qquad p_g=\min\{q_g,1-q_g\}. $$
We first construct a distribution of flip sets $F$ satisfying
$$ \Pr(g\in F)=p_g, \qquad |F|\leq2. $$
The desired preliminary bundle will be
$$ S=R\mathbin{\triangle}F. $$

Define cumulative sums
$$ c_0=0, \qquad c_i=\sum_{g=1}^ip_g. $$
For $u\in[0,1)$, let
$$ F(u)= \left\{ g:\text{there is an integer }k \text{ with }u+k\in[c_{g-1},c_g) \right\}. $$

Each interval $[c_{g-1},c_g)$ has length $p_g\leq1/2$. Its image modulo one therefore has Lebesgue measure $p_g$, giving
$$ \Pr_{u\in[0,1)}(g\in F(u))=p_g. $$

Moreover, the intervals partition $[0,\delta)$. Each interval contains at most one point of the form $u+k$. Hence
$$ |F(u)|=\big|\{k\in\mathbb Z:u+k\in[0,\delta)\}\big|. $$
This count is either $\lfloor\delta\rfloor$ or $\lceil\delta\rceil$, apart from endpoint conventions that have measure zero. In particular, when $\delta\leq2$,
$$ |F(u)|\leq2. $$

The flip set is constant between consecutive breakpoints given by the fractional parts of $c_0,\ldots,c_m$. Sort these breakpoints together with zero and one. For every interval of positive length, choose its rational midpoint and give the corresponding flip set mass equal to the interval length. This explicitly constructs at most $m+1$ rational atoms.

For $g\notin R$, inclusion in $S$ has probability $p_g=q_g$. For $g\in R$, inclusion has probability $1-p_g=q_g$. Therefore the resulting preliminary bundles have exactly the prescribed marginals.

Every such bundle satisfies
$$ |S\mathbin{\triangle}R|\leq2. $$
Writing $c=v(R)$, the marginal bound gives
$$ v(S)\in[c-2\Lambda,c+2\Lambda]. $$

Removing the empty bundle and bounding the spread

If the preliminary distribution has no empty mass, its spread is at most $4\Lambda$.

Otherwise, a supported flip set satisfies $R\mathbin{\triangle}F=\emptyset$, so $F=R$ and $|R|\leq2$. Normalization and the marginal bound imply
$$ |c|=|v(R)|\leq2\Lambda. $$

Apply Lemma 1. Old supported values belong to $[c-2\Lambda,c+2\Lambda]$, and newly introduced singleton values belong to $[-\Lambda,\Lambda]$. The spread is consequently at most
$$ \begin{aligned} &\max\{c+2\Lambda,\Lambda\} -\min\{c-2\Lambda,-\Lambda\}\\ &\qquad =\max\{4\Lambda,c+3\Lambda,3\Lambda-c,2\Lambda\}\\ &\qquad \leq5\Lambda. \end{aligned} $$

Bit complexity

All $p_g$, cumulative sums, breakpoints, and preliminary masses have a common denominator dividing the product $D$ of the supply denominators. That product has bit length at most $L$. Midpoints are used only to identify bundles and have denominator dividing $2D$.

Sorting the breakpoints, identifying the flip sets, merging identical bundles, and applying Lemma 1 take polynomially many bit operations. The construction never queries $v$. Its correctness follows entirely from the marginal promise. $\square$

For example, this theorem includes all instances with $\sum_gq_g\leq2$, because $\delta\leq\sum_gq_g$, but it also includes supplies close to any other cube vertex.

4. Proven universal feasibility with balanced cardinalities

The following geometric argument is included in full because the existence conclusion alone does not establish an efficient algorithm.

A geometric covering lemma

Lemma 4. Let $P$ be a polytope with vertex set $V$. Suppose its vertices have integer labels $\ell(u)$, and every edge has endpoints whose labels differ by at most one. Then
$$ P= \bigcup_{k\in\mathbb Z} \operatorname{conv}\{u\in V:\ell(u)\in\{k,k+1\}\}. $$

Proof. We first establish an auxiliary separation property.

Partition $V$ as $A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B\mathbin{\dot\cup}C$, where $C$ is nonempty, and suppose no edge joins $A$ to $B$. Put
$$ Q=\operatorname{conv}(A\cup C), \qquad T=\operatorname{conv}(B\cup C). $$
We claim that
$$ P=Q\cup T, \qquad Q\cap T=\operatorname{conv}(C), $$
and every edge of $Q$ that is not an edge of $P$ has both endpoints in $C$.

We use the elementary fact that, for an affine functional $h$, vertices satisfying $h(u)>a$ induce a connected graph whenever that set is nonempty. Indeed, a nonmaximizing vertex has an incident edge along which $h$ strictly increases: the feasible cone at a vertex is generated by its incident edge directions. Repeated improvement reaches the maximizing face without leaving the strict superlevel set. The graph of the maximizing face is connected; this also follows by improvement paths for a generic affine functional on that face.

Suppose first that $z\in P$ lies outside both $Q$ and $T$. Strict separation supplies affine functionals $a,b$ satisfying
$$ a(z)=b(z)=0, $$
with $a<0$ on $A\cup C$ and $b<0$ on $B\cup C$. For $\theta\in[0,1]$, maximize
$$ h_\theta=\theta a+(1-\theta)b $$
over $P$. The maximum is nonnegative because $z\in P$. Every vertex of $C$ has strictly negative value, so no maximizing face contains a vertex of $C$.

At $\theta=0$, all maximizing vertices belong to $A$; at $\theta=1$, they belong to $B$. The parameter sets where some maximizing vertex belongs to $A$, respectively $B$, are closed, nonempty, and cover $[0,1]$. They therefore intersect. At an intersection parameter the maximizing face contains both colors and no vertex of $C$. Its connected graph must contain an edge joining $A$ to $B$, a contradiction. Thus $P=Q\cup T$.

Next suppose $z\in Q\cap T$ but $z\notin\operatorname{conv}(C)$. Separation gives an affine functional $h$ with
$$ h(z)>\max_{u\in C}h(u). $$
A convex representation using $A\cup C$ must contain a vertex of $A$ above this threshold. A representation using $B\cup C$ must similarly contain a vertex of $B$ above it. The strict superlevel graph is connected and contains no vertex of $C$, again forcing an edge from $A$ to $B$. This proves the intersection assertion.

Finally, consider an edge $e$ of $Q$ with an endpoint in $A$, and a point $z$ in its relative interior. Since $e$ is a face of $Q$, its intersection with $\operatorname{conv}(C)$ is the convex hull of its endpoints belonging to $C$. Consequently $z\notin\operatorname{conv}(C)$ and hence $z\notin T$.

A neighborhood of $z$ misses the closed set $T$. Because $P=Q\cup T$, the polytopes $P$ and $Q$ agree there. The dimension of the smallest face containing $z$ is determined by locally feasible two-sided directions, so that dimension is one in both polytopes. Thus $z$ lies in the relative interior of an edge of $P$. The endpoints of $e$ are vertices of $P$ and must be the endpoints of this edge. This proves the auxiliary property.

We now induct on $|V|$. If all labels lie in two consecutive integers, the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, let $H$ be the largest label and set
$$ A=\{u:\ell(u)\leq H-2\}, \quad C=\{u:\ell(u)=H-1\}, \quad B=\{u:\ell(u)=H\}. $$
Both $A$ and $B$ are nonempty. The edge condition forbids edges between them, and connectivity of the polytope graph ensures $C\ne\emptyset$.

The auxiliary property covers $P$ by $Q$ and $T$. The vertices of $T$ already have labels $H-1,H$. Every edge of $Q$ either is an original edge or has both endpoints labeled $H-1$. Thus the edge-label condition persists in $Q$, which has fewer vertices. Applying induction to $Q$ completes the proof. $\square$

The cardinality polytope

Put
$$ Q_0=\sum_gq_g, \qquad r=\lfloor Q_0\rfloor, \qquad s=\lceil Q_0\rceil, $$
and consider
$$ P=\left\{ z\in[0,1]^m: r\leq\sum_gz_g\leq s \right\}. $$
The supply assumptions give $r\geq1$, and $q\in P$.

Every vertex of $P$ is integral. To see this, suppose a feasible point has at least two fractional coordinates. A sufficiently small perturbation in direction $e_i-e_j$ preserves the coordinate sum and remains in the cube in both directions, so the point is not a vertex.

If exactly one coordinate is fractional and the sum constraints are both slack, that coordinate can be perturbed in both directions. If a sum constraint is tight, its integer right-hand side, together with all other coordinates being integral, forces the remaining coordinate to be integral. This proves the assertion.

Hence the vertices of $P$ correspond exactly to bundles of cardinality $r$ or $s$. They are all nonempty.

Every edge of $P$ changes at most two coordinates between its endpoints. Indeed, let $k$ be the number of fractional coordinates at a relative interior point of an edge. If no sum constraint is active, the smallest face there has dimension $k$. If a sum constraint is active, its dimension is $k-1$. Since the face is an edge, respectively $k=1$ or $k=2$. These fractional coordinates are precisely the coordinates in which the endpoints differ.

Applying the covering lemma

Label each vertex by
$$ \ell(\mathbf 1_S)= \left\lfloor\frac{v(S)}{2\Lambda}\right\rfloor. $$
Edge endpoints differ in at most two coordinates, so their values differ by at most $2\Lambda$. Their integer labels therefore differ by at most one.

Lemma 4 gives a representation of $q$ as a convex combination of vertices with labels $k,k+1$ for some integer $k$. Every corresponding value lies in
$$ [2k\Lambda,\,2(k+2)\Lambda). $$
The resulting distribution has spread strictly less than $4\Lambda$, uses only nonempty bundles, and every bundle has cardinality $r$ or $s$.

This proves universal feasibility with the stated additional cardinality constraint.

Rational witnesses of polynomial size

Choose a representation with the smallest possible positive support. Its columns
$$ c_S=(1,\mathbf 1_S) $$
are linearly independent. Otherwise a nonzero dependence among them has coefficients summing to zero and therefore has both signs. Moving the masses along this dependence until one becomes zero preserves nonnegativity, total mass, and every marginal, contradicting minimality.

There are consequently at most $m+1$ positive masses. Select independent rows of the columns to obtain an invertible zero-one matrix $A$ of order $t\leq m+1$. The masses solve
$$ Ax=b, $$
where $b$ consists of selected entries of $(1,q)$. Thus they are rational.

Let $D$ be the product of the supply denominators. By Cramer's rule, mass denominators divide $D|\det A|$. Hadamard's inequality gives
$$ |\det A|\leq t^{t/2}. $$
After multiplying the replacement column by $D$, the numerator determinants have absolute value at most $Dt^{t/2}$. Each mass therefore has bit length
$$ O\bigl(L+m\log(m+1)\bigr). $$

Such a witness can be verified using at most $m+1$ value queries and polynomially many bit operations.

5. What remains open

The additive algorithm requires additivity. The algorithm of Section 3 requires $\delta\leq2$. Neither condition covers general promised valuations and supplies.

The geometric argument establishes feasibility and small witnesses, but it does not give an efficient method for locating the relevant label classes and bundles. In particular, it does not provide a polynomial-time separation oracle for their convex hulls.

The [cited discrete partition result](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14609v4) concerns partitions and has a running-time bound involving the numerical value of $\Lambda$. It does not itself supply the required bound for binary-encoded parameters and arbitrary prescribed marginals.

Accordingly, no polynomial-query algorithm or NP-hardness reduction for the full problem is established here. Since every promised instance is feasible, a hardness argument must address the search for a distribution rather than distinguish feasible from infeasible promised instances.
