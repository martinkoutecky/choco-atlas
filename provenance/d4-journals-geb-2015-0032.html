1. Reduction to one aggregate valuation

Put
$$ f(S)=\bar v_\mu(S)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t v_t(S). $$
Because the coefficients are positive, $f$ is normalized, nonnegative, monotone, and submodular. For example, for all $A,B\subseteq[m]$,
$$ f(A)+f(B)-f(A\cup B)-f(A\cap B) = \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t \bigl(v_t(A)+v_t(B)-v_t(A\cup B)-v_t(A\cap B)\bigr) \ge 0. $$

An exact query to $f(S)$ can be implemented using exactly $\tau$ individual-type queries, followed by the weighted sum. Consequently, a one-type algorithm making $Q$ queries can be run on $f$ using $\tau Q$ individual-type queries. Its fixed range, approximate range-maximization property, and approximation guarantee are preserved.

Conversely, restrict a society algorithm to $\tau=1$ and $\mu_1=1$. This gives a one-type algorithm with exactly the same guarantee and range condition.

Thus polynomial-query attainability is equivalent in the two models. In particular, a dependence on the number of possible types plays no role: only the explicitly listed support incurs query overhead.

For $k=0$, the sole feasible outcome is $\varnothing$, and the optimum is zero. Below, assume $k\ge 1$. The bounds concern the worst case over admissible $k$; some individual values, such as $k=1$ and $k=m$, admit exact optimization with few queries.

2. A modular lower bound for submodular valuations

We will repeatedly use the following elementary lemma.

Lemma. Let $f$ be normalized, monotone, and submodular, and let $A\subseteq[m]$. There are nonnegative weights $w_i$, supported on $A$, such that
$$ \sum_{i\in A}w_i=f(A), \qquad f(S)\ge \sum_{i\in S\cap A}w_i \quad\text{for every }S\subseteq[m]. $$

Proof. Order $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_s\}$, define
$$ A_j=\{a_1,\ldots,a_j\}, \qquad w_{a_j}=f(A_j)-f(A_{j-1}), $$
and set all other weights to zero. Monotonicity gives nonnegativity, and telescoping gives their sum.

For $B\subseteq A$, insert its elements in the specified order. Immediately before inserting $a_j\in B$, the previously inserted set is contained in $A_{j-1}$. Submodularity therefore makes its marginal contribution at least $w_{a_j}$. Telescoping these contributions gives
$$ f(B)\ge \sum_{i\in B}w_i. $$
Finally, monotonicity gives $f(S)\ge f(S\cap A)$. This proves the lemma. $\square$

3. An exact fixed-range construction

Write
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_k(f)=\max_{|S|\le k}f(S). $$

3.1. A basic construction

Identify $[m]$ with a cyclic order, and let $W_1,\ldots,W_m$ be the cyclic windows of length $k$. Consider the range consisting of point masses on all singletons and all these windows. Query their values and select a maximizing set.

First, submodularity and normalization imply
$$ f(O)\le \sum_{i\in O}f(\{i\}) \le k\max_i f(\{i\}) $$
for every feasible $O$. Hence the best singleton has value at least $\operatorname{OPT}_k(f)/k$.

Second, apply the lemma with $A=[m]$. Each item belongs to exactly $k$ cyclic windows, so
$$ \sum_{j=1}^m f(W_j) \ge \sum_{j=1}^m\sum_{i\in W_j}w_i = k f([m]) \ge k\operatorname{OPT}_k(f). $$
Thus the best window has value at least $(k/m)\operatorname{OPT}_k(f)$.

The construction therefore achieves
$$ \max\left\{\frac1k,\frac{k}{m}\right\} \ge \frac1{\sqrt m} $$
using at most $2m\tau$ individual-type queries.

We next strengthen this guarantee.

3.2. A family with simultaneous coverage

Assume $m\ge256$, and put $\ell=\ln m$. It remains to address the middle regime
$$ \sqrt{\frac m\ell}\le k\le\sqrt{m\ell}, $$
since outside this regime the preceding construction already achieves at least $\sqrt{\ell/m}$.

Define
$$ r=\left\lceil k\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{m}}\right\rceil. $$
We will prove that there is a fixed list $S_1,\ldots,S_N$ of $k$-element sets, where
$$ N=64m^9, $$
with the following property. For every $k$-element set $O$, write
$$ J_O=\{j:|S_j\cap O|=r\}. $$
Then $J_O$ is nonempty and, for every $i\in O$,
$$ \frac{|\{j\in J_O:i\in S_j\}|}{|J_O|} \ge \frac{r}{4k}. $$

This property depends only on $m,k$.

Probability of an intersection of size $r$. Let $S$ be a uniformly random $k$-element set. For a fixed $O$ of size $k$, let
$$ p=\Pr(|S\cap O|=r), \qquad \lambda=\frac{k^2}{m}. $$
The probability $p$ is independent of the particular $O$.

Set $a=k\sqrt{\ell/m}$. The middle-regime assumptions give
$$ 1\le a\le\ell, \qquad r\le2a, \qquad \frac{r^2}{4\ell}\le\lambda\le\ell. $$
Also, $m\ge256$ implies $m\ge16\ell$. Consequently,
$$ k\le\frac m4, \qquad r\le\frac k2. $$

Using falling factorials, the hypergeometric probability is
$$ p= \binom{k}{r} \frac{(k)_r(m-k)_{k-r}}{(m)_k}. $$
The bounds
$$ \binom{k}{r}\ge\left(\frac kr\right)^r, \qquad (k)_r\ge\left(\frac k2\right)^r, \qquad (m-k)_{k-r}\ge(m-2k)^{k-r}, \qquad (m)_k\le m^k $$
therefore imply
$$ p\ge \left(\frac{\lambda}{2r}\right)^r \left(1-\frac{2k}{m}\right)^{k-r}. $$
For $0\le x\le1/2$, $\ln(1-x)\ge-2x$. Applying this with $x=2k/m$ gives
$$ p\ge \left(\frac{\lambda}{2r}\right)^r e^{-4\lambda} \ge \left(\frac{r}{8\ell}\right)^r e^{-4\ell}. $$
The inequality $x\ln(A/x)\le A/e$ for positive $x,A$ now yields
$$ p\ge e^{-(4+8/e)\ell}\ge m^{-7}. $$

Simultaneous coverage. Draw $S_1,\ldots,S_N$ independently and uniformly from the $k$-element sets. For fixed $O$ and $i\in O$, define
$$ A_O=|J_O|, \qquad B_{O,i}=|\{j\in J_O:i\in S_j\}|. $$
Then
$$ A_O\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,p), \qquad B_{O,i}\sim\operatorname{Bin}\left(N,\frac{pr}{k}\right). $$
The second assertion follows because, conditional on intersection size $r$, the intersection is a uniform $r$-element subset of $O$.

For a binomial variable $X$ of mean $\nu$, the multiplicative Chernoff bounds give
$$ \Pr(X>2\nu)\le e^{-\nu/3}, \qquad \Pr(X<\nu/2)\le e^{-\nu/8}. $$
Their hypotheses hold because the sampled sets are independent.

Since $p\ge m^{-7}$ and $r/k\ge1/m$,
$$ Np\ge64m^2, \qquad \frac{Npr}{k}\ge64m. $$
Thus each of the events
$$ A_O>2Np, \qquad B_{O,i}<\frac{Npr}{2k} $$
has probability at most $e^{-8m}$.

There are at most $(m+1)2^m$ events under consideration. The union bound gives total failure probability at most
$$ (m+1)2^m e^{-8m}<1. $$
Therefore a fixed list exists for which all the desired inequalities hold. In particular, $B_{O,i}>0$, so $A_O>0$, and
$$ \frac{B_{O,i}}{A_O} \ge \frac{Npr/(2k)}{2Np} = \frac{r}{4k}. $$

Hence some set in the list has value at least
$$ \frac{r}{4k}\operatorname{OPT}_k(f) \ge \frac14\sqrt{\frac{\ln m}{m}}\operatorname{OPT}_k(f). $$

Use the range of point masses on the list, the singletons, and the cyclic windows. In the middle regime, the list gives the displayed guarantee; outside it, the singletons or windows give the stronger bound $\sqrt{\ln m/m}$.

For $m<256$, use the basic construction. Together these constructions achieve, uniformly in $k$,

4. A matching bound for polynomially generated ranges

The preceding order is optimal under an additional restriction on the range.

Proposition. Fix $d\ge0$. Suppose, for each $m,k$, the range is contained in the convex hull of at most $m^d$ fixed feasible lotteries. For some admissible $k$, its worst-case approximation guarantee is
$$ O_d\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log m}{m}}\right). $$
This holds even with unlimited queries and computation.

Proof. For sufficiently large $m$, choose
$$ k=\left\lfloor\sqrt{m\ln m}\right\rfloor, \qquad q=\frac{k}{2m}. $$
Let the generating lotteries be $D_1,\ldots,D_n$, with $n\le m^d$. Define their marginal inclusion probabilities
$$ x_{j,i}=\Pr_{S\sim D_j}(i\in S). $$
Feasibility implies
$$ 0\le x_{j,i}\le1, \qquad \sum_i x_{j,i}\le k. $$

Independently include each item in a random set $O$ with probability $q$. Put
$$ X_j=\sum_{i\in O}x_{j,i}. $$
Independence, the inequality $1+u\le e^u$, and convexity of the exponential give
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[e^{X_j}] &=\prod_i(1-q+qe^{x_{j,i}})\\ &\le\exp\left(q\sum_i(e^{x_{j,i}}-1)\right)\\ &\le\exp\left(q(e-1)\sum_i x_{j,i}\right)\\ &\le\exp\left(\frac{(e-1)k^2}{2m}\right) \le m. \end{aligned} $$
Here $e^x-1\le(e-1)x$ for $0\le x\le1$, and $(e-1)/2<1$.

Set $C=d+3$. Markov's inequality yields
$$ \Pr(X_j\ge C\ln m)\le m^{1-C}. $$
A union bound over all generators bounds the probability of any such event by $m^{-2}$.

Meanwhile, $|O|$ is binomial with mean $k/2$. The same Chernoff bounds give
$$ \Pr(|O|<k/4)\le e^{-k/16}, \qquad \Pr(|O|>k)\le e^{-k/6}. $$
For sufficiently large $m$,
$$ m^{-2}+e^{-k/16}+e^{-k/6}<1. $$
Thus there exists a set $O$ satisfying
$$ \frac k4\le |O|\le k, \qquad X_j<C\ln m\quad\text{for every }j. $$

Consider the additive valuation
$$ f_O(S)=|S\cap O|. $$
It is normalized, nonnegative, monotone, and submodular. Its feasible optimum is $|O|\ge k/4$. For each generating lottery,
$$ \mathbb E_{S\sim D_j}[f_O(S)] = \sum_{i\in O}x_{j,i} < C\ln m. $$
Linearity preserves this upper bound throughout their convex hull. Therefore every distribution in the range has approximation ratio at most
$$ \frac{4C\ln m}{k} = O_d\left(\sqrt{\frac{\ln m}{m}}\right). $$
$\square$

This proposition establishes the optimal order for polynomially generated ranges. It does not establish the optimal order in the question: polynomial query complexity does not require a range to have polynomially many generators.
