The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\) over a finite set \(T\), per-capita supplies \(q_r\) of item classes \(r\in R\), and an adversarial measurable arrival stream containing total mass \(q_r\) of each class, each type \(t\) has a nonnegative, possibly nonmonotone diminishing-returns-submodular utility \(F_t\) on integral bundle-count vectors. Does there exist an efficient deterministic causal mass-flow policy that routes arriving item mass among cohorts of agents while every agent holds an integral bundle, and guarantees final welfare at least \(\frac14\) of the offline optimum for every stream, or can this guarantee be ruled out?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless population of total mass \(1\), given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
For item classes \(R\), the input gives positive rational per-capita supplies \(q_r\) and positive integer bundle bounds \(K_r\). Write \(m=\sum_r K_r\) and \(D=\prod_r\{0,\ldots,K_r\}\). Each type has a nonnegative, possibly nonmonotone integer-lattice DR-submodular utility \(F_t:D\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), given by a value oracle; \(F_t(0)\) need not vanish. Let \(L\) include the input bit length and a bound on oracle-answer bit lengths.
An adversary supplies nonnegative integrable measurable arrival rates \(a_r(s)\) on \(s\in[0,1]\), with \(\int_0^1a_r(s)\,ds=q_r\). Supplies and bounds are known beforehand, but future rates are hidden. Utilities are fixed beforehand; oracle queries may involve only classes whose positive mass has already arrived.
A deterministic causal policy maintains cohort masses \(x_{t,b}(s)\ge0\), initially \(x_{t,0}(0)=\mu_t\), with \(\sum_bx_{t,b}(s)=\mu_t\). Allocating class \(r\) means transferring cohort mass from \(b\) to \(b+e_r\in D\), consuming the same item mass. Transfers must conserve nonnegative cohort stocks, and their total rate for class \(r\) cannot exceed \(a_r(s)\). Unused arrivals are discarded immediately; buffering, removal, and reassignment are forbidden. Cohorts may be split, but every individual always holds an integral bundle.
Welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}F_t(b)\). The offline benchmark is \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max\{W(y):y\ge0,\ \sum_by_{t,b}=\mu_t,\ \sum_{t,b}b_ry_{t,b}\le q_r\text{ for every }r\}\).
Use a uniform arithmetic-controller model: controller description length, maintained-state dimension, compact flow-output size, and arithmetic operations and value queries per evaluation must be polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\); measurable arrival rates are external signals, and the induced flows must be well defined for every admissible stream.

Does an efficient deterministic causal mass-flow policy guarantee \(W(x(1))\ge\tfrac14\mathrm{OPT}\) for every admissible instance and adversarial measurable stream, or is this guarantee impossible?

Discrete adversarial online submodular welfare admits no positive constant competitive ratio for deterministic algorithms, while a randomized polynomial-time algorithm achieves \(\tfrac14\) in expectation. [Ganz–Nuti–Schwartz](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.07746)
Continuous online DR-submodular packing has competitive algorithms for monotone utilities evaluated directly on fractional vectors; this is a different model from integral-bundle cohort welfare. [Sadeghi–Eghbali–Fazel](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.00312) No result settling the precise cohort problem was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized society mass to one so that the stated per-capita supplies equal total arrival masses.
  • Specified a finite integer box using input bounds K_r, and defined m as their sum. The discrete model has a finite item ground set; nonnegative DR-submodularity on the entire unbounded integer lattice would preclude nonmonotonicity.
  • Retained nonnegative utilities without imposing zero utility for the empty bundle, and specified rational value-oracle answers with bounded bit length.
  • Carried over restricted online oracle access: queries cannot involve unrevealed classes. Once a class has arrived, queries may use its counts throughout the specified box.
  • Represented measurable arrivals by integrable rates on a fixed time interval, with known positive supplies and unknown future rates.
  • Made allocation immediate and irreversible, allowed discarding and deterministic cohort splitting, and forbade buffering, removal, and reassignment.
  • Defined the offline optimum over cohort distributions with the same bundle bounds and supply constraints.
  • Defined efficiency through a uniform arithmetic controller with polynomial description, state, compact output, and work per evaluation, because an arbitrary measurable stream has no finite binary encoding.

Resolution

Yes. Under the stated class-level oracle restriction, there is an efficient deterministic causal mass-flow policy satisfying

\(W(x(1))\ge \tfrac14\mathrm{OPT}.\)

The construction below proves the slightly stronger inequality

\(W(x(1))\ge \tfrac14\mathrm{OPT}+\tfrac12 W(x(0)).\)

The key is to separate destination commitments from physical delivery. Once a class has positive arrived mass, its known total supply permits the policy to commit destinations for that class. Items are then delivered to those destinations as they arrive. Commitments consume no items and change no individual's actual bundle.

A sparsification argument keeps both the commitments and the actual cohort distribution polynomial in size.

1. Lifting the lattice utilities to labeled copies

For every class \(r\), introduce \(K_r\) auxiliary labels

\(U_r=\{(r,1),\ldots,(r,K_r)\},\qquad U=\bigcup_r U_r.\)

Thus \(|U|=m\). For \(S\subseteq U\), define its count vector by

\(c(S)_r=|S\cap U_r|,\)

and define

\(f_t(S)=F_t(c(S)).\)

These labels are planning devices; physical items remain indistinguishable within each class.

Each \(f_t\) is a nonnegative submodular set function. Indeed, if \(S\subseteq T\) and \(i\in U_r\setminus T\), then \(c(S)\le c(T)\), and both relevant increments lie in \(D\). The lattice DR property gives

\(f_t(S\cup\{i\})-f_t(S)\ge f_t(T\cup\{i\})-f_t(T).\)

Associate with each label \(i\in U_r\) the auxiliary supply

\(c_i=q_r/K_r.\)

Lifting an offline allocation

Fix any feasible offline allocation \(y\). For each type \(t\), construct an auxiliary random set \(O_t\):

  • Choose bundle \(b\) with probability \(y_{t,b}/\mu_t\).
  • Conditional on \(b\), choose uniformly a subset of \(U_r\) of size \(b_r\), for every class \(r\).

Then

\(\sum_t\mu_t\,\mathbb E[f_t(O_t)]=W(y).\)

For every \(i\in U_r\), writing \(w_{t,i}=\Pr[i\in O_t]\), we have

\(w_{t,i}=\frac{1}{\mu_t K_r}\sum_b b_r y_{t,b},\)

and consequently

\(0\le w_{t,i}\le 1,\qquad \sum_t\mu_t w_{t,i}\le q_r/K_r=c_i.\)

This equalization across the auxiliary labels is what handles arbitrary supplies and bundle bounds.

2. A weighted capped-greedy lemma

We first prove the local inequality used by the planner.

Lemma. Let \(\delta_t\in\mathbb R\), let the positive weights be \(\mu_t\), and let \(c>0\). In decreasing order of positive \(\delta_t\), choose fractions \(\alpha_t\in[0,\tfrac12]\), filling each fraction to \(\tfrac12\) until the budget

\(\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t\le c\)

is exhausted. Set \(\alpha_t=0\) when \(\delta_t\le0\). Define

\(g=\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t\delta_t.\)

For every \(w\) satisfying

\(0\le w_t\le1,\qquad \sum_t\mu_t w_t\le c,\)

we have

\(\sum_t\mu_t w_t(1-\alpha_t)\delta_t\le g.\)

Proof. If every positive marginal receives fraction \(\tfrac12\), then

\(\sum_t\mu_t w_t(1-\alpha_t)\delta_t \le \tfrac12\sum_{\delta_t>0}\mu_t\delta_t=g.\)

Otherwise the budget is exhausted. Let \(\lambda>0\) be the cutoff marginal and let \(H=\{t:\delta_t>\lambda\}\). Every member of \(H\) receives fraction \(\tfrac12\); every marginal below \(\lambda\) receives zero. Therefore

\(g=c\lambda+\tfrac12\sum_{t\in H}\mu_t(\delta_t-\lambda).\)

For any feasible \(w\),

\(\sum_t\mu_t w_t(1-\alpha_t)\delta_t \le c\lambda+\sum_{t\in H}\mu_t w_t(\tfrac12\delta_t-\lambda).\)

Using \(0\le w_t\le1\), this is at most

\(c\lambda+\sum_{t\in H}\mu_t\max\{\tfrac12\delta_t-\lambda,0\}.\)

For \(\delta_t>\lambda\),

\(\max\{\tfrac12\delta_t-\lambda,0\}\le\tfrac12(\delta_t-\lambda).\)

The claimed inequality follows. \(\square\)

3. A deterministic planner with polynomial support

Represent each type by the common label interval \(\Omega=[0,1]\), with type-\(t\) mass measure \(\mu_t\,du\). This is simply an implementation of atomless cohort splitting.

The planner maintains a partition of \(\Omega\) into intervals. On each interval \(\sigma\), of length \(p_\sigma\), it stores a tuple of planned sets

\((S_{\sigma,1},\ldots,S_{\sigma,\tau}).\)

Initially there is one interval, and every planned set is empty.

Consider processing one auxiliary label \(i\), with supply \(c_i\). On interval \(\sigma\), calculate

\(\delta_{\sigma,t}=f_t(S_{\sigma,t}\cup\{i\})-f_t(S_{\sigma,t}).\)

Apply the capped-greedy lemma to these marginals, with weights \(\mu_t\) and budget \(c_i\), obtaining fractions \(\alpha_{\sigma,t}\).

3.1 Creating candidate splits

There is a distribution supported on at most \(\tau+1\) subsets \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) whose inclusion probabilities are \(\alpha_{\sigma,t}\).

Explicitly, sort the fractions as

\(\alpha_{\sigma,\pi(1)}\ge\cdots\ge\alpha_{\sigma,\pi(\tau)},\)

put \(\alpha_{\sigma,\pi(\tau+1)}=0\), and use:

  • the empty subset with weight \(1-\alpha_{\sigma,\pi(1)}\);
  • the subset \(\{\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(j)\}\) with weight \(\alpha_{\sigma,\pi(j)}-\alpha_{\sigma,\pi(j+1)}\).

Let \(z_{\sigma,J}\) denote these weights multiplied by \(p_\sigma\). Their deterministic interpretation is to split interval \(\sigma\) into pieces of lengths \(z_{\sigma,J}\). On the piece indexed by \(J\), commit label \(i\) to precisely the types in \(J\).

We now sparsify these candidate splits before making the commitments.

3.2 Sparsification

Preserve the following linear quantities:

  • For every old interval \(\sigma\),

\(\sum_J z_{\sigma,J}=p_\sigma.\)

  • For every type \(t\), its total inclusion mass,

\(P_t=\sum_{\sigma,J:t\in J}z_{\sigma,J}.\)

  • For every type \(t\), its marginal-gain sum,

\(B_t=\sum_{\sigma,J:t\in J}z_{\sigma,J}\delta_{\sigma,t}.\)

If there are \(N\) old intervals, these are \(N+2\tau\) linear equalities.

A nonnegative solution preserving them can be found with at most \(N+2\tau\) positive variables. Here is a direct deterministic algorithm. Whenever the positive-variable columns are linearly dependent, choose a nonzero null vector \(v\) supported on those columns, with some positive coordinate, and replace \(z\) by

\(z-\lambda v,\qquad \lambda=\min_{v_j>0}z_j/v_j.\)

All preserved quantities remain unchanged, all variables remain nonnegative, and at least one positive variable becomes zero. Repeat until at most \(N+2\tau\) variables remain positive.

Assign the resulting pieces contiguously within each old interval, in a fixed deterministic order. Previous commitments remain unchanged. The new partition therefore has at most \(N+2\tau\) intervals.

After all \(m\) labels, the number of planning intervals is at most

\(N_{\max}=1+2\tau m.\)

3.3 Properties preserved by sparsification

Before sparsification, capped greedy gives

\(P_t\le\tfrac12,\qquad C_i:=\sum_t\mu_tP_t\le c_i.\)

Both inequalities remain true afterward. The planned welfare gain is

\(G_i=\sum_t\mu_t B_t\ge0,\)

and it also remains unchanged.

Moreover, for every \(w\) satisfying the hypotheses of the capped-greedy lemma, summing that lemma over the old intervals gives

\(\sum_t\mu_t w_t\left(\sum_\sigma p_\sigma\delta_{\sigma,t}-B_t\right)\le G_i.\)

This inequality is preserved because every \(B_t\) is preserved. It does not require the sparsified routing fractions to satisfy a conditional cap within each old interval.

4. Competitive analysis of the planner

Process the auxiliary labels in any order that groups all labels of each class together. The causal choice of that order will be specified below.

Let \(S_t^j\) be type \(t\)'s planned set after \(j\) labels, viewed as a random set by choosing \(u\) uniformly from \(\Omega\). This randomness is only an analytical description of deterministic population shares.

Write

\(V_j=\sum_t\mu_t\,\mathbb E[f_t(S_t^j)],\qquad V_0=W(x(0)).\)

Let \(U_{\le j}\) be the first \(j\) processed labels. Independently of the population label, sample the lifted offline sets \(O_t\) from Section 1. Define the hybrid potential

\(H_j=\sum_t\mu_t\,\mathbb E[f_t(S_t^j\cup(O_t\cap U_{\le j}))].\)

Initially \(H_0=V_0\).

4.1 Bounding each hybrid increment

Suppose the current label is \(i\), and write \(w_t=\Pr[i\in O_t]\). These probabilities satisfy

\(0\le w_t\le1,\qquad \sum_t\mu_t w_t\le c_i.\)

For an old interval \(\sigma\), let

\(Q_{\sigma,t}=\sum_{J:t\in J}z_{\sigma,J}\)

be its mass committed to type \(t\).

The current label enters the hybrid set of type \(t\) if either it belongs to \(O_t\) or the planner assigns it to that type. Its total probability mass on interval \(\sigma\) is

\(p_\sigma w_t+Q_{\sigma,t}(1-w_t).\)

The previous hybrid set contains the previous planned set. Submodularity therefore bounds the hybrid marginal by \(\delta_{\sigma,t}\), including when this marginal is negative. Consequently,

\(H_j-H_{j-1} \le\sum_{\sigma,t}\mu_t[p_\sigma w_t+Q_{\sigma,t}(1-w_t)]\delta_{\sigma,t}.\)

Rearranging gives

\(H_j-H_{j-1} \le G_i+\sum_t\mu_t w_t\left(\sum_\sigma p_\sigma\delta_{\sigma,t}-B_t\right) \le2G_i.\)

Since \(V_j-V_{j-1}=G_i\), summation yields

\(H_m\le2V_m-V_0.\)

4.2 A nonnegative-submodular sampling inequality

We use the following elementary fact.

Lemma. If \(h:2^U\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) is submodular and a random set \(A\) satisfies \(\Pr[i\in A]\le p\) for every \(i\), then

\(\mathbb E[h(A)]\ge(1-p)h(\varnothing).\)

No independence is required.

Proof. Order the elements by decreasing inclusion probabilities \(p_1\ge\cdots\ge p_m\). Let \(T_j\) be the first \(j\) elements and put

\(d_j=h(T_j)-h(T_{j-1}).\)

Diminishing returns, applied while inserting the elements of any set \(A\) in this order, gives

\(h(A)\ge h(\varnothing)+\sum_{j:j\in A}d_j.\)

Taking expectations and telescoping,

\(\mathbb E[h(A)] \ge(1-p_1)h(\varnothing) +\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(p_j-p_{j+1})h(T_j) +p_mh(U).\)

All terms after the first are nonnegative. Since \(p_1\le p\), the result follows. \(\square\)

Every label has terminal inclusion probability at most \(\tfrac12\) for every type: this was preserved when that label was processed, and later steps never remove labels.

For each fixed offline set \(O\), the function

\(h(S)=f_t(S\cup O)\)

is nonnegative and submodular. Applying the lemma with \(p=\tfrac12\), then averaging over \(O_t\), gives

\(H_m\ge\tfrac12\sum_t\mu_t\,\mathbb E[f_t(O_t)] =\tfrac12 W(y).\)

Combining the two potential bounds,

\(V_m\ge\tfrac14 W(y)+\tfrac12 V_0.\)

This holds for every feasible offline allocation \(y\), hence

\(V_m\ge\tfrac14\mathrm{OPT}+\tfrac12 W(x(0)).\)

It remains to implement the planner causally and physically.

5. Causal revelation without a “first positive instant” assumption

For every class, maintain its cumulative arrived supply

\(A_r(s)=\int_0^s a_r(v)\,dv.\)

Its revelation time is

\(u_r=\inf\{s:A_r(s)>0\}.\)

Because \(A_r\) is continuous and nondecreasing,

\(A_r(s)>0\quad\text{exactly when}\quad s>u_r.\)

Thus the revealed classes at every time form a prefix of the order obtained by sorting classes by \(u_r\), with class index breaking ties.

These onset times can be recovered by a finite-dimensional causal controller. Maintain an additional clock

\(z_r(s)=\int_0^s \mathbf 1_{\{A_r(v)=0\}}\,dv.\)

Then

\(z_r(s)=\min\{s,u_r\}.\)

For every currently revealed class, \(z_r(s)=u_r\). The controller can therefore sort the revealed classes causally.

At each evaluation:

  • Identify the classes with \(A_r(s)>0\).
  • Sort them by their stored onset clocks and class indices.
  • Recompute the deterministic planner for this revealed prefix, processing all \(K_r\) labels of each class consecutively.

Recomputation is polynomial, as shown below. Since every planning step depends only on its preceding prefix, extending the prefix changes none of the earlier commitments or their interval locations.

Every oracle query uses only revealed classes. Planned bundles may include items of those classes that have not yet physically arrived; the stated oracle restriction permits this because it restricts classes, not the amount of arrived supply.

This definition avoids querying at an onset time itself. The set on which \(A_r(s)=0\) carries zero arriving item mass:

\(\int_{\{s:A_r(s)=0\}}a_r(s)\,ds=0.\)

Therefore no positive mass is lost while the class is unrevealed.

6. Physical delivery of the commitments

For every processed label \(i\in U_r\), and every recipient type \(t\), record its committed recipient intervals. Each record has the form

\((t,i,I),\)

meaning that every type-\(t\) individual with population label in \(I\) is to receive one physical item of class \(r\).

For each class \(r\), concatenate these records into a fixed deterministic queue. A record has item workload

\(\mu_t|I|.\)

The total queue workload is

\(C_r=\sum_{i\in U_r}C_i\le\sum_{i\in U_r}c_i=q_r.\)

Subsequent planning only refines existing intervals; it never changes these recipient sets.

6.1 Delivery rule

Class \(r\)'s arrived supply serves its queue in order.

If \(A_r(s)<C_r\), allocate the current arrival rate to the current queue record. Within a record \(I=[\ell,h]\) belonging to type \(t\), serve individuals in increasing population-label order. If the record's completed workload is \(d\), its served portion is

\([\ell,\ell+d/\mu_t].\)

Once the queue is complete, discard further arrivals of that class immediately.

This uses no buffering. An arriving item is either allocated immediately to the current recipient or discarded.

Each individual receives only committed labels, and each label at most once. Its actual bundle therefore remains integral and satisfies every bound \(K_r\). Since \(A_r(1)=q_r\ge C_r\), every commitment is fulfilled by time \(1\).

Thus terminal actual welfare equals terminal planned welfare:

\(W(x(1))=V_m.\)

6.2 Compact cohort representation

All commitment endpoints belong to the refined planning partition, which has at most \(N_{\max}\) intervals.

At any time, each class queue contributes at most one additional moving delivery boundary. Consequently, the actual bundle is constant on a common partition with at most

\(N_{\max}+|R|\)

intervals, for every type. The actual cohort distribution therefore has a representation with at most

\(\tau(N_{\max}+|R|)\)

records.

All stocks are nonnegative by construction, and their sum for type \(t\) is exactly \(\mu_t\).

6.3 Adjacent-bundle flows, including simultaneous deliveries

Here are explicit class-by-class flows inducing this cohort distribution.

Inside a queue record of type \(t\), class \(r\)'s delivery boundary moves with velocity

\(v_r(s)=a_r(s)/\mu_t.\)

At an isolated boundary, type-\(t\) mass crosses from its bundle immediately to the right to that bundle plus \(e_r\), at rate \(\mu_t v_r(s)=a_r(s)\).

If several delivery boundaries for the same type coincide, order their classes by decreasing current boundary velocity,

\(v_{r_1}\ge\cdots\ge v_{r_k}.\)

Let \(b\) be the bundle immediately to their right. Use the adjacent-bundle path

\(b,\ b+e_{r_1},\ b+e_{r_1}+e_{r_2},\ldots,\ b+\sum_{j=1}^k e_{r_j},\)

with class-\(r_j\) flow rate \(\mu_t v_{r_j}\) on its corresponding edge.

An intermediate cohort's net incoming rate is

\(\mu_t(v_{r_j}-v_{r_{j+1}})\ge0.\)

For equal velocities, an intermediate zero stock has equal incoming and outgoing flows. Thus these paths conserve nonnegative stocks and implement simultaneous deliveries through integral bundles.

To verify that these flows induce the stated stocks, at almost every time every moving boundary has an instantaneous derivative. Distinct boundaries give the single-edge formula above. Coincident boundaries separate to first order according to their velocities; the intermediate interval lengths grow at exactly the velocity differences appearing above. This gives the cohort conservation equations.

Queue changes and revelations are finite in number. At fixed interval endpoints, times with a positive crossing rate have zero measure; flows may be set to zero there. The resulting measurable flows induce the same absolutely continuous cohort masses.

There is at most one nonzero transfer for each class in this description, and its rate never exceeds \(a_r(s)\).

7. Uniform efficiency and well-definedness

The construction meets the arithmetic-controller requirements.

  • Planning support: at most \(1+2\tau m\) intervals.
  • Queue size: each auxiliary-label step creates at most \(N+2\tau\) pieces, each involving at most \(\tau\) types. Across all steps, the number of queue records is polynomial in \(m,\tau\).
  • Actual cohort representation: at most \(\tau(1+2\tau m+|R|)\) records.
  • Flow output: at most \(|R|\) nonzero adjacent-bundle transfers.
  • Maintained clocks: the \(2|R|\) variables \(A_r,z_r\).
  • Evaluation cost: at most \(m\) planning steps, each using polynomially many oracle queries, sorting operations, and linear-algebra operations.

The rational planning coefficients also have polynomial bit length. Capped-greedy fractions are formed from the input masses and supplies. Sparsification matrices contain only these fixed structural coefficients and oracle marginal values.

More explicitly, clear matrix denominators and choose null vectors using integer minors. Their bit lengths are polynomially bounded by the matrix dimensions and oracle-answer bit bound. In one support-reduction step, a common denominator for the current weights need only be multiplied by a nonzero integer null-vector coordinate. Thus denominator bit lengths grow additively by polynomial amounts. There are polynomially many reductions and planning steps.

For every admissible measurable stream, the cumulative supplies and onset clocks are uniquely defined. The planner is a deterministic function of their revealed prefix. Queue service is then an explicit function of the cumulative supplies. The construction therefore defines measurable flows and nonnegative cohort stocks for every admissible stream, including overlapping, interrupted, and arbitrarily irregular arrivals.

No random choices are made by the policy. All distributions used above describe deterministic interval shares or auxiliary variables in the proof.

8. Conclusion

The terminal physical allocation fulfills every planned commitment, so the planner's bound gives

\(W(x(1))\ge\tfrac14\mathrm{OPT}+\tfrac12W(x(0))\ge\tfrac14\mathrm{OPT}.\)

The cited discrete deterministic hardness concerns indivisible allocation decisions; atomless cohort splitting supplies the deterministic population shares used here. Its conclusion therefore does not prohibit this policy. [Ganz–Nuti–Schwartz](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.07746)
