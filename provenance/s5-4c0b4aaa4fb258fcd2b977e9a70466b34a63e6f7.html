The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given  m  ordered resource classes, \tau supported types with rational masses \mu_t, interval eligibility, rational values  v_j>0, and rational per-capita supplies  b_j, each member must receive an integral bundle. Let  x_{t,a} be the mass of type  t receiving bundle  a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m, with  a_j=0 outside its interval. Subject to \sum_a x_{t,a}=\mu_t, \sum_{t,a}x_{t,a}a_j\le b_j for every class, and \sum_jv_ja_j\ge q for every used bundle, maximize  q. Does this problem admit a (1-\epsilon)-approximation in time polynomial in  m, \tau, and input bit length  L for each fixed \epsilon>0?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge1\) ordered resource classes. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a nonempty eligibility interval \([\ell_t,r_t]\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\).
Require the enclosure property: whenever one eligibility interval contains another, they share at least one endpoint. Identical intervals are allowed.
Class \(j\) has binary rational value \(v_j>0\), common to all eligible types, and finite binary rational per-capita supply \(b_j\ge0\). Supply may remain unused.
An individual receives an integral bundle \(a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\), with \(a_j=0\) outside its eligibility interval; there is no individual multiplicity bound. Types may be split among bundles.
An allocation consists of finitely supported nonnegative real masses \(x_{t,a}\) satisfying \(\sum_a x_{t,a}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,a}x_{t,a}a_j\le b_j\). Its guaranteed utility is the largest \(q\ge0\) such that \(\sum_jv_ja_j\ge q\) whenever \(x_{t,a}>0\). Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the maximum guaranteed utility, and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Does this problem admit a \((1-\epsilon)\)-approximation in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\) for every fixed \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\)?

The discrete interval allocation result originally states a PTAS; the [later version](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.00082) establishes a PTAS under the enclosure property and also records strong NP-hardness for that discrete class. The [original statement](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/opus/volltexte/2013/4393/pdf/35.pdf) allows arbitrary eligibility intervals.
No result settling exactly the continuous formulation above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Added the enclosure property from the later version to carry over the conditions of the supported discrete approximation result.
  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one, so supplies are measured per capita.
  • Specified nonempty eligibility intervals, positive numbers of classes and supported types, and finite nonnegative supplies; zero supplies and zero optimum are allowed.
  • Specified nonnegative allocation masses with finite support and no individual multiplicity bound, retaining the graded question's integral bundles.
  • Retained unused supply, consistent with the later version's explicit observation that assigning every item is unnecessary for the max-min objective.

Result

Yes. For every fixed \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\), a \((1-\epsilon)\)-approximation can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with an allocation of polynomial support size.

The proof works for arbitrary nonempty eligibility sets. Thus the enclosure property is unnecessary for this continuous formulation.

The key is to encode distributions over integral bundles by a polynomial-size flow linear program. Binary encoding handles large multiplicities; rounding affects only the guaranteed utility, while supply constraints remain exact.

1. Bounds, attainment, and positive feasibility

Write \(E_t=[\ell_t,r_t]\), and define \(U=\sum_{j=1}^m b_jv_j\) and \(v_{\min}=\min_{1\le j\le m}v_j>0\).

Every allocation guaranteeing utility \(q\) satisfies
\[ \]
\(q=q\sum_t\mu_t\le\sum_{t,a}x_{t,a}\sum_jv_ja_j\le\sum_jv_jb_j=U.\)

Here the first inequality uses the guarantee for every bundle of positive mass, and the second uses each supply constraint.

We will use the following elementary trimming fact.

Lemma 1. If an integral bundle has utility at least \(q>0\), items can be removed until the resulting bundle still has utility at least \(q\) and satisfies \(a_j\le\lceil q/v_j\rceil\) for every class \(j\).

Proof. Repeatedly remove an item whenever the remaining utility is at least \(q\). This process terminates because the initial bundle is finite. In the resulting bundle, whenever \(a_j>0\), removing one item of class \(j\) would violate the threshold. Consequently,
\(a_jv_j-v_j\le\sum_i v_i a_i-v_j<q\).
Thus \(a_j<q/v_j+1\), which, because \(a_j\) is integral, implies \(a_j\le\lceil q/v_j\rceil\). Eligibility is preserved and resource consumption only decreases. \(\square\)

This also proves that the maximum defining \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is attained. If \(U=0\), only utility zero is possible. If \(U>0\), any positive-guarantee allocation can be trimmed into the finite collection of eligible bundles satisfying \(a_j\le\lceil U/v_j\rceil\). The zero allocation belongs to this collection as well. Allocations supported on this finite collection have guaranteed utilities drawn from a finite set of bundle utilities. Therefore a largest achievable guarantee exists, and trimming shows that it equals the unrestricted optimum.

We can test whether this optimum is positive by solving the following rational feasibility problem:

\(y_{tj}\ge0,\qquad y_{tj}=0\text{ for }j\notin E_t,\)

\(\sum_{j\in E_t}y_{tj}=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t,\qquad \sum_t y_{tj}\le b_j\quad\text{for every }j.\)

Lemma 2. This system is feasible if and only if \(\mathrm{OPT}>0\). When feasible, it gives an allocation guaranteeing \(v_{\min}\).

Proof. A feasible solution allocates the one-item bundle of class \(j\) to mass \(y_{tj}\) of type \(t\). Every such bundle has utility at least \(v_{\min}\).

Conversely, every bundle in a positive-guarantee allocation is nonempty. Choose one item from each bundle and remove the others. The resulting masses define a feasible solution of the displayed system, because resource consumption cannot increase. \(\square\)

If the system is infeasible, output the zero allocation. Henceforth assume it is feasible, so \(v_{\min}\le\mathrm{OPT}\le U\).

2. Binary encoding at a fixed threshold

Fix a positive rational threshold \(q\) and a rational constant \(\delta\in(0,1)\).

For each class \(j\), let \(B_j=\lceil q/v_j\rceil\), and let \(d_j\) be the smallest positive integer such that \(2^{d_j}>B_j\).

Introduce \(d_j\) binary choices for class \(j\). Choice \(k\), for \(0\le k<d_j\), represents a chunk of \(2^k\) items of that class. Selecting a subset of these chunks represents every multiplicity from zero through \(2^{d_j}-1\), including every multiplicity at most \(B_j\).

Let \(N=\sum_{j=1}^m d_j\), \(K=\lceil N/\delta\rceil\), and \(R=K-N\). Since \(\delta<1\), we have \(R>0\), and \(R/K=1-N/K\ge1-\delta\).

For a chunk \(i\) of class \(j(i)\) and multiplicity \(c_i\), define its rounded weight by
\(w_i=\min\{R,\lfloor Kc_iv_{j(i)}/q\rfloor\}\).

We call a chunk selection acceptable when its rounded weights sum to at least \(R\).

Lemma 3: soundness. Every acceptable selection represents an eligible integral bundle of utility at least \((1-\delta)q\), provided all its chunks belong to eligible classes.

Proof. For each selected chunk,
\(c_iv_{j(i)}\ge(q/K)w_i\).
Adding these inequalities gives utility at least
\((q/K)R\ge(1-\delta)q\).
The represented multiplicities are nonnegative integers, and the eligibility assertion follows from the restriction on selected chunks. \(\square\)

Lemma 4: completeness. Every eligible integral bundle of utility at least \(q\) can be replaced by an acceptable chunk selection that consumes no more of any resource.

Proof. First apply Lemma 1. Each resulting multiplicity is at most \(B_j\), so it has a binary representation using the introduced chunks. Let \(S\) be the selected chunks. Their total true utility is at least \(q\), and \(|S|\le N\).

Set \(z_i=\lfloor Kc_iv_{j(i)}/q\rfloor\). Then
\(\sum_{i\in S}z_i\ge(K/q)\sum_{i\in S}c_iv_{j(i)}-|S|\ge K-N=R\).

If some \(z_i\ge R\), its capped weight alone is \(R\). Otherwise no selected weight is capped, and their sum is at least \(R\). Thus the selection is acceptable. Its multiplicities equal those of the trimmed bundle, so it consumes no more resources than the original bundle. \(\square\)

3. A polynomial-size flow linear program

For each type \(t\), list all chunks whose classes belong to \(E_t\), in any fixed order. Let their number be \(N_t\le N\).

Construct a layered directed graph with vertices \((i,s)\), where \(0\le i\le N_t\) and \(0\le s\le R\). At layer \(i\), corresponding to chunk \(i+1\), include two edges from each state:

  • A skip edge from \((i,s)\) to \((i+1,s)\).
  • A take edge from \((i,s)\) to \((i+1,\min\{R,s+w_{i+1}\})\).

Retain these as distinct edges even when their endpoints coincide. A path from \((0,0)\) to \((N_t,R)\) selects exactly an acceptable subset of eligible chunks: its final state is the capped sum of their rounded weights.

Introduce nonnegative edge-flow variables \(f_{t,e}\). Require a flow of value \(\mu_t\) from \((0,0)\) to \((N_t,R)\), with flow conservation at every other vertex.

A take edge for a chunk of class \(j\) and multiplicity \(c_i\) has resource coefficient \(c_i\) for class \(j\), and zero for other classes. Skip edges have all coefficients zero. Add the supply inequalities
\(\sum_t\sum_{\substack{e\text{ a take edge}\\\text{for class }j}}c_e f_{t,e}\le b_j\)
for every class \(j\).

Call this rational linear system \(P(q,\delta)\).

Proposition 5.

  • If an allocation guaranteeing \(q\) exists, then \(P(q,\delta)\) is feasible.
  • From any feasible solution of \(P(q,\delta)\), one can construct a finite-support allocation guaranteeing at least \((1-\delta)q\), satisfying the original supplies exactly.

Proof. For the first assertion, replace each original bundle by the selection supplied by Lemma 4. Route its mass along the corresponding path. Type masses are preserved, and resource consumption does not increase, so all flow and supply constraints hold.

For the second assertion, decompose each type’s flow into source-to-sink paths. Such a decomposition exists because the graph is finite and acyclic. Explicitly, follow positive-flow edges from the source to the sink, subtract the minimum flow along that path, and repeat. Conservation ensures that the walk reaches the sink; every subtraction removes at least one positive-flow edge. Consequently, at most the number of edges is needed.

Assign each path’s mass to the integral bundle represented by its selected chunks. Path masses sum to \(\mu_t\). Resource consumption equals the corresponding linear combination of take-edge flows, hence satisfies the original supply inequalities. Every resulting bundle has utility at least \((1-\delta)q\) by Lemma 3. \(\square\)

4. Encoding and running time

We invoke the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: feasibility of a finite rational linear system can be decided in time polynomial in its total binary encoding length, and a feasible system has a rational solution of polynomial encoding length that can be returned.

Its hypotheses apply here: every coefficient and right-hand side is rational, and the system is explicitly finite.

For fixed \(\delta\), \(P(q,\delta)\) has polynomial size in \(m,\tau,L\) and the encoding length of \(q\):

  • The integer \(B_j=\lceil q/v_j\rceil\) has polynomial encoding length. Consequently, its binary length \(d_j\), and hence \(N\), are polynomially bounded.
  • We have \(R<K=\lceil N/\delta\rceil\). Each type’s graph therefore has at most \((N+1)(R+1)\) vertices and \(2N(R+1)\) edges.
  • Chunk multiplicities are powers of two whose encoding lengths are at most \(d_j\).
  • Rounded weights are computable by exact rational arithmetic and integer division. They are bounded by \(R\).
  • The remaining coefficients are the input masses and supplies.

Thus the LP can be constructed and solved in polynomial time. The path decomposition also runs in polynomial time and returns polynomially many bundles. Their multiplicities and rational masses have polynomial encoding lengths.

5. Threshold search and approximation guarantee

Fix \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\). Choose a fixed positive rational \(\delta\le\epsilon/3\).

After the positivity test, enumerate all thresholds
\(q_k=v_{\min}(1+\delta)^k\)
with integer \(k\ge0\) and \(q_k\le U\). Solve \(P(q_k,\delta)\) at each threshold. Return the allocation obtained at the largest threshold whose LP is feasible.

At least the first threshold is accepted: Lemma 2 gives an allocation guaranteeing \(q_0=v_{\min}\), and Proposition 5 gives LP feasibility.

Let \(k_*\) be the largest integer with \(q_{k_*}\le\mathrm{OPT}\). This threshold belongs to the enumerated list because \(\mathrm{OPT}\le U\). An optimal allocation exists by Section 1, so Proposition 5 implies that its LP is feasible. Therefore the chosen threshold is at least \(q_{k_*}\).

By maximality,
\(\mathrm{OPT}<q_{k_*}(1+\delta)\).
The returned allocation consequently guarantees at least
\((1-\delta)q_{k_*}>\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta}\mathrm{OPT}\).

Finally,
\(\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta}=1-\frac{2\delta}{1+\delta}\ge1-2\delta\ge1-\epsilon\).
This proves the requested approximation guarantee. If \(\mathrm{OPT}=0\), the earlier zero allocation already satisfies it.

There are at most \(1+\log(U/v_{\min})/\log(1+\delta)\) tested thresholds. The binary encoding lengths of \(U\) and \(v_{\min}\) are polynomial in \(L\), so \(\log(U/v_{\min})\) is polynomially bounded. For fixed \(\delta\), both the number of thresholds and their rational encoding lengths are polynomial. Combined with Section 4, the total running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

No monotonicity of the rounded LP’s acceptance predicate is required: the algorithm explicitly tests the geometric list.

6. Relation to the discrete result

The cited [later discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.00082) proves a PTAS under the enclosure property and records strong NP-hardness for that discrete class.

The proof above independently settles the continuous formulation. Its decisive feature is that path flows may be decomposed into arbitrary rational masses of individuals. That decomposition is an allocation in the stated model; an allocation to individually listed discrete players would require a separate integrality argument.

All eligibility restrictions were enforced by excluding ineligible chunks. No interval or enclosure property was used.
