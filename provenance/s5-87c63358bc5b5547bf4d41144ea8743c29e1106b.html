The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) products \(P\), a recommendation size \(k<n\), rational \(\alpha\in(0,1]\), and a finite set of customer types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\) and rational relevance vectors \(v_t:P\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), find nonnegative masses \(x_{t,A}\) over bundles \(A\subseteq P\) of size \(k\). Require \(\sum_A x_{t,A}=\mu_t\) for each type; for every pair with \(x_{t,A},x_{s,B}>0\), require \(\sum_{p\in A}v_t(p)\ge\sum_{p\in B}v_t(p)-\max_{p\in B}v_t(p)\); and require at least \(\lceil n-\alpha k\rceil\) products to have exposure \(e_p=\sum_{t,A:p\in A}x_{t,A}\ge\alpha k/n\).

Result

Every instance satisfying the stated hypotheses is feasible. Consequently, the decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). This follows from the given discrete FairRec guarantee by choosing a finite population size that clears all relevant denominators.

The construction below applies to arbitrary support size \(\tau\), arbitrary permitted ties, and the endpoint \(\alpha=1\).

1. Choose an exact discrete population

Write the input rationals as
\(\mu_t=c_t/d_t\) and \(\alpha=a/b\), with positive integer numerators and denominators. Define

\(D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}d_t,\qquad N=mbD.\)

For every type, let \(N_t=N\mu_t\). Since \(d_t\) divides \(N\), each \(N_t\) is a positive integer. Moreover,

\(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}N_t=N\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t=N.\)

Create \(N_t\) discrete customers of type \(t\), each with relevance vector \(v_t\). Thus the discrete population has exactly \(N\) customers.

The exposure parameter is now an integer:

\(\displaystyle \ell=\left\lfloor\frac{\alpha Nk}{m}\right\rfloor=\frac{\alpha Nk}{m}=akD\ge1.\)

Also, \(N\ge m\) and \(k\ge1\), so \(k<m\le Nk\). These verify all numerical hypotheses of the discrete FairRec guarantee stated in the question.

2. Apply FairRec and aggregate

By that guarantee, FairRec assigns each discrete customer \(i\) a bundle \(R_i\) of exactly \(k\) distinct products, with:

  • EF1 between every pair of discrete customers;
  • positive integer exposure \(E_p\ge1\) for every product;
  • exposure \(E_p\ge\ell\) for at least \(m(1-\ell/(N+1))\) products.

These are the guarantees of [Theorem 7.2 and Lemma 7.2.4 in the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.01180), with its customer and product parameters renamed.

Define the mass recommendation by

\(\displaystyle x_{t,A}=\frac{|\{i:\text{customer }i\text{ has type }t,\ R_i=A\}|}{N}.\)

All these masses are nonnegative, and for each type,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{A\in\mathcal B}x_{t,A}=\frac{N_t}{N}=\mu_t.\)

Thus the type-mass constraints hold exactly.

3. Verify the support-based EF1 condition

Suppose \(x_{t,A}>0\) and \(x_{s,B}>0\).

If \((t,A)\ne(s,B)\), choose discrete customers \(i,j\) witnessing these two positive masses. They are distinct, and discrete EF1 supplies some \(p\in B\) such that

\(v_t(A)\ge v_t(B)-v_t(p).\)

Since \(v_t(p)\le\max_{q\in B}v_t(q)\), this implies

\(v_t(A)\ge v_t(B)-\max_{q\in B}v_t(q).\)

This argument includes \(t=s\) and \(A\ne B\): the two different bundles necessarily have distinct witnessing customers.

If \((t,A)=(s,B)\), the required inequality holds directly because

\(v_t(A)\ge v_t(A)-\max_{p\in A}v_t(p),\)

using nonnegative relevance values. Hence every required support comparison holds, including all comparisons within one type.

4. Verify both exposure requirements

Aggregation gives, for every product,

\(\displaystyle e_p=\sum_{t,A:p\in A}x_{t,A}=\frac{E_p}{N}\ge\frac1N>0.\)

Thus positivity survives exactly; no limiting argument is needed.

Because \(\ell/N=\alpha k/m\), every product with discrete exposure at least \(\ell\) has mass exposure at least the required threshold.

Let \(q=|\{p:E_p\ge\ell\}|\). FairRec guarantees

\(\displaystyle q\ge m\left(1-\frac{\ell}{N+1}\right) =m-\alpha k\frac{N}{N+1} >m-\alpha k.\)

Since \(q\) is an integer,

\(\displaystyle |\{p:e_p\ge\alpha k/m\}|\ge q\ge\lceil m-\alpha k\rceil.\)

This completes the feasibility proof.

5. Running times

Decision. On the promised domain of valid instances, the algorithm always returns YES: \(\mathrm O(1)\) time after accepting the promise, or \(\mathrm O(L)\) including reading the input. If input validity must also be checked, elementary exact rational arithmetic gives an \(\mathrm O(L^3)\) bit-time bound. Thus decision is polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

The explicit construction above. Computing the scaling quantities takes polynomial time in \(L\). FairRec then uses

\(\displaystyle \mathrm O(Nmk)=\mathrm O\!\left(m^2k\,b\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}d_t\right)\)

arithmetic operations.

Since the total encoded bit length of these denominators is at most \(L\), we have \(b\prod_t d_t\le2^L\). Therefore this construction uses \(\mathrm O(m^2k2^L)\) arithmetic operations; elementary exact arithmetic and aggregation give the conservative bit-time bound \(\mathrm O(L^3+m^2k2^L L^2)\).

The exponential population expansion establishes existence. The decision algorithm requires no such expansion, because the proof establishes feasibility for every valid input.
