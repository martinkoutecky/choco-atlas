The continuous mirror question, as graded

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) complete rule-package types \(t\), each carrying an explicitly listed finite definite Horn expression \(R_t\) over \(V\) and a positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Background knowledge \(B\) consists of pairwise disjointness constraints \((p\land q)\to\bot\), with distinct atoms occurring in \(F_\mu=\bigcup_tR_t\). Write \(E(p)=\{q\in V:B\models(p\land q)\to\bot\}\), and require \(E(p)\ne\varnothing\) for every atom occurring in \(F_\mu\). All other entailments below use ordinary propositional semantics without adjoining \(B\).
For a definite clause \(\phi\), write \(A(\phi)\) for its antecedent set and \(c(\phi)\) for its consequent; non-trivial means \(c(\phi)\notin A(\phi)\). Write \(\phi^{+q}\), \(\phi^{-p}\), and \(\phi^{q\setminus p}\) for adding, deleting, and replacing an antecedent atom, respectively.

A clause \(\phi\) is coherent with \(H\) if \(H\setminus\{\phi\}\not\models\phi\) and, in \(K=H\cup\{\phi\}\), no \(\psi\in H\) with \(c(\psi)\in E(c(\phi))\) satisfies \(\psi\Rightarrow_K\phi\) or \(\phi\Rightarrow_K\psi\). An expression is coherent if each of its clauses is coherent with it.
An expression \(H\) is not in conflict if every ordered pair \(\alpha,\beta\in H\) satisfying \(\alpha\Rightarrow_H\beta\) and \(c(\alpha)\in E(c(\beta))\) admits \(r\in A(\alpha)\setminus A(\beta)\) and \(q\in E(r)\) such that \(\beta^{+q}\) is coherent with \(H\setminus\{\beta\}\).
Promise that \(F_\mu\) is not in conflict, is non-redundant (\(F_\mu\setminus\{\phi\}\not\models\phi\) for every \(\phi\in F_\mu\)), and is acyclic: its clause graph, with an edge \(\alpha\to\beta\) when \(c(\alpha)\in A(\beta)\), has no directed cycle.

1. Semantics and the necessary compatibility repair

Write $F=F_\mu$, and regard expressions as sets of clauses. For an expression $H$ and an initial fact set $S$, let $\operatorname{cl}_H(S)$ be the result of repeatedly adding $c(\theta)$ whenever $\theta\in H$ and $A(\theta)$ is already present.

Every atom added by this procedure is entailed by $H\cup S$. Conversely, the final set is closed under every rule of $H$, so the interpretation making exactly that set true satisfies $H\cup S$. Consequently,

$$ H\models \left(\bigwedge S\right)\to c \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c\in\operatorname{cl}_H(S). $$

There are at most $m$ strict enlargements, so this computation is polynomial in the explicitly represented expression and vocabulary. In particular,

$$ \alpha\Rightarrow_H\beta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A(\beta)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_H(A(\alpha)). $$

Because $B$ consists exclusively of pairwise disjointness constraints, $E$ is symmetric and has no self-exclusions. Indeed, an assignment making only $p$ true satisfies $B$. Moreover, if the constraint involving distinct $p,q$ is absent, the assignment making exactly $p,q$ true satisfies $B$. Thus $E$ is exactly the adjacency relation of the listed disjointness constraints.

A necessary condition for existence of a common ground is

$$ A(\phi)\cap E(c(\phi))=\varnothing \qquad\text{for every }\phi\in F. $$

If $q$ belongs to this intersection, then $(\bigwedge A(\phi))\to q$ is a tautology, contradicting (P3).

The literal promises do not ensure this condition. For example, take

$$ V=\{a,b\},\qquad R_1=\{a\to b\},\qquad \mu_1=1,\qquad B=\{(a\land b)\to\bot\}. $$

The union is non-redundant and acyclic. It is coherent and not in conflict because there are no clauses with excluded consequents. Both occurring atoms have excludents. Nevertheless, (P3) requires $G\not\models a\to a$, which is impossible.

Thus the literal problem is not a total search problem. For the substantive results below, I explicitly impose the necessary antecedent-compatibility condition displayed above. This changes the input class; it does not settle its general complexity.

3. Antecedent compatibility makes (P3) follow from (P1) and (P5)

First observe that, for non-trivial definite clauses $\phi,\psi$,

$$ \{\phi\}\models\psi \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c(\phi)=c(\psi) \quad\text{and}\quad A(\phi)\subseteq A(\psi). $$

To prove this, start forward chaining from $A(\psi)$ using only $\phi$. Since $c(\psi)\notin A(\psi)$, the rule must produce $c(\psi)$. Its only possible new atom is $c(\phi)$, establishing equality of consequents. Before that rule fires, only the initial facts are available, so its antecedent must be contained in $A(\psi)$.

Lemma. Suppose $F$ is non-redundant and antecedent-compatible. Every $G$ satisfying (P1) and (P5) satisfies (P3).

Proof. Non-redundancy excludes tautological input clauses. Fix $\phi\in F$ and $q\in E(c(\phi))$. By (P5), there is a non-trivial $\psi\in G$ such that $\{\phi\}\models\psi$. Hence

$$ c(\psi)=c(\phi),\qquad A(\phi)\subseteq A(\psi). $$

Suppose $q\in\operatorname{cl}_G(A(\phi))$. Compatibility gives $q\notin A(\phi)$, so some rule $\gamma\in G$ producing $q$ must fire. Its antecedent is therefore derivable from $A(\phi)$, and consequently from the larger fact set $A(\psi)$. Thus

$$ \psi\Rightarrow_G\gamma, \qquad c(\gamma)=q\in E(c(\psi)). $$

This contradicts coherence of $G$. $\square$

4. A polynomial construction satisfying (P1)–(P5)

Theorem. Suppose $F$ is non-redundant, not in conflict, and antecedent-compatible. In polynomial time one can construct an expression $H$ satisfying (P1)–(P5), with exactly one descendant of each input clause. Each descendant is either the original clause or its weakening by one added antecedent atom.

Acyclicity is not needed for this theorem.

Construction

Start with $H=F$. Maintain a designated descendant $d_\theta\in H$ for each $\theta\in F$, initially $d_\theta=\theta$.

Whenever $H$ has an ordered pair $\alpha,\beta$ such that

$$ \alpha\Rightarrow_H\beta, \qquad c(\alpha)\in E(c(\beta)), $$

choose such a pair. The invariant proved below ensures that both clauses are unchanged originals.

Since $F\models H$, this pair also satisfies $\alpha\Rightarrow_F\beta$. The original not-in-conflict promise therefore supplies

$$ r\in A(\alpha)\setminus A(\beta),\qquad q\in E(r), $$

such that

$$ \delta=\beta^{+q} $$

is coherent with $F\setminus\{\beta\}$. Find such a pair $r,q$ by enumerating the at most $m^2$ possibilities and checking coherence.

Replace $\beta$ by this single clause $\delta$:

$$ H'=(H\setminus\{\beta\})\cup\{\delta\}, \qquad d_\beta=\delta. $$

Invariants

Throughout the construction:

  • Each descendant strengthens its parent’s antecedent and keeps its consequent.
  • $H$ is non-redundant.
  • Every changed descendant is coherent with $H$.
  • For every $\theta\in F$,

$$ F\setminus\{\theta\}\not\models d_\theta. $$

Initially these statements follow from non-redundancy of $F$; the third is vacuous.

Assume they hold before an update. Any excluded comparable pair consists of unchanged originals, since a changed descendant is coherent with $H$. Thus the construction’s choice of $\alpha,\beta$ is justified.

Put

$$ F'=(F\setminus\{\beta\})\cup\{\delta\}. $$

The parent-descendant relation gives

$$ F'\models H', \qquad H\models H', \qquad F\setminus\{\beta\}\models H\setminus\{\beta\}. $$

The original coherence test gives

$$ F\setminus\{\beta\}\not\models\delta. $$

Consequently $H\setminus\{\beta\}\not\models\delta$, so the new clause is non-redundant.

We next verify its exclusion condition. Let $\gamma\in H\setminus\{\beta\}$ have consequent excluded from $c(\delta)$, and let $\theta$ be its original parent.

If $\delta\Rightarrow_{H'}\gamma$, then, using $F'\models H'$ and $A(\theta)\subseteq A(\gamma)$,

$$ F'\cup A(\delta)\models\bigwedge A(\theta). $$

This contradicts coherence of $\delta$ with $F\setminus\{\beta\}$.

For the reverse direction, suppose $\gamma\Rightarrow_{H'}\delta$.

  • If $\gamma$ is unchanged, then $\gamma=\theta$, and $F'\models H'$ transfers this relation to $F'$, giving the same coherence contradiction.
  • If $\gamma$ is changed, its coherence with the previous $H$ gives

$$ H\cup A(\gamma)\not\models\bigwedge A(\beta). $$

But $H\models H'$ and $A(\beta)\subseteq A(\delta)$, so $\gamma\Rightarrow_{H'}\delta$ would imply the excluded entailment above.

Thus $\delta$ is coherent with $H'$.

For any retained clause $\gamma$, the expression $H'\setminus\{\gamma\}$ is entailed by $H\setminus\{\gamma\}$. Hence it cannot newly entail $\gamma$, and retained clauses stay non-redundant. Relations between retained clauses also cannot be newly introduced, because $H\models H'$ and their antecedents have not changed. Together with the check against $\delta$, this preserves coherence of every previously changed descendant.

Finally, invariant 4 is unchanged for retained descendants and holds for $\delta$ by its original coherence test. This completes the induction.

The inserted clause cannot equal a retained descendant: that would make it entailed by $F\setminus\{\beta\}$. Nor can it equal $\beta$: its coherence test would contradict the original offending pair. Thus each update changes one previously unchanged original, and no changed descendant is changed again.

Termination and postulates

There are at most $|F|$ updates. At termination there are no excluded comparable pairs; non-redundancy therefore gives (P1).

If $F$ is coherent, the construction performs no update, giving (P2). Every descendant is entailed by its parent, proving (P4). Every input has a descendant, and non-redundancy excludes trivial descendants, proving (P5). Section 3 supplies (P3).

Each iteration uses a polynomial number of forward-chaining and coherence tests on expressions with at most $|F|$ clauses and at most $m$ antecedent atoms per clause. Together with the iteration bound, this proves polynomial running time in $m,\tau,L$. $\square$

5. Why this does not settle (P6)

The following example shows that (P6) can fail for an unchanged clause. In particular, input non-redundancy does not imply that a stakeholder cannot entail an antecedent deletion.

Take

$$ V=\{a,r,s,p,h,c,d\}, $$

with background constraints

$$ (p\land h)\to\bot,\qquad (r\land s)\to\bot,\qquad (a\land d)\to\bot,\qquad (c\land d)\to\bot. $$

Let the two packages, each of mass $1/2$, be

$$ \begin{aligned} R_1&=\{\beta:a\to p,\quad \gamma:(a\land p)\to c\},\\ R_2&=\{\alpha:(a\land r)\to h,\quad \eta:s\to d\}. \end{aligned} $$

Verification of the promises

Every occurring atom has an excludent, and every input antecedent is compatible with its consequent.

The four input clauses have different consequents, each absent from its own antecedent. Removing any one removes the only rule producing its consequent, proving non-redundancy.

The clause graph has only the edge $\beta\to\gamma$, so it is acyclic.

The only ordered pair with excluded consequents and a derivation relation is $(\alpha,\beta)$. Here $\alpha\Rightarrow_F\beta$ because $a\in A(\alpha)$. Choose

$$ r\in A(\alpha)\setminus A(\beta), \qquad s\in E(r). $$

The weakening

$$ \delta:(a\land s)\to p $$

is coherent with $F\setminus\{\beta\}$. To verify this, after inserting $\delta$, the only excluded-consequent pairs are $\delta,\alpha$ and $\gamma,\eta$. Neither $r$ nor $s$ can be derived, so the first pair has no relation in either direction. Starting from $A(\gamma)$ cannot produce $s$, while starting from $A(\eta)=\{s\}$ cannot produce $a$, so the second pair also has no relation. All four consequents remain unique, proving non-redundancy.

Thus the input is not in conflict.

A coherent result violating (P6)

The construction of Section 4 may return

$$ H=\{\delta,\alpha,\gamma,\eta\}. $$

It is coherent by the preceding verification, and satisfies (P1)–(P5) by the theorem.

Apply (P6) to the unchanged clause $\gamma$ and its antecedent atom $p$. Since $E(p)=\{h\}$, the sole replacement is

$$ \chi=\gamma^{h\setminus p}:(a\land h)\to c. $$

The expression $H\cup\{\chi\}$ is coherent. Its forward closures from clause antecedents are:

| Clause | Closure of its antecedent under $H\cup\{\chi\}$ |
|---|---|
| $\delta$ | $\{a,s,p,c,d\}$ |
| $\alpha$ | $\{a,r,h,c\}$ |
| $\gamma$ | $\{a,p,c\}$ |
| $\eta$ | $\{s,d\}$ |
| $\chi$ | $\{a,h,c\}$ |

These closures rule out all relations between excluded-consequent pairs: $\delta,\alpha$, and $\eta$ with either $\gamma$ or $\chi$.

For non-redundancy, $\delta,\alpha,\eta$ still have unique consequents absent from their antecedents. After removing $\gamma$, the facts $\{a,p\}$ cannot produce $h$, so $\chi$ cannot produce $c$. After removing $\chi$, the facts $\{a,h\}$ cannot produce $p$, so $\gamma$ cannot produce $c$. Hence both clauses with consequent $c$ are also non-redundant.

But $R_1$ entails both

$$ \gamma^{-p}:a\to c \quad\text{and}\quad \gamma^{h\setminus p}:(a\land h)\to c, $$

using $\beta$ followed by $\gamma$. Thus the coherence premise of (P6) is true, while its conclusion is false.

This exposes a specific gap in transferring the published proof: its treatment of unchanged clauses invokes non-redundancy to exclude stakeholder entailment of deletions, whereas such entailment may use the clause itself, as it does here. [Journal article, appendix proof of Lemma 4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-024-09681-6)

This example does not prove that the input has no common ground. It proves that the construction above does not establish the general result, and identifies a further issue requiring repair.

6. A complete polynomial result for separated antecedents and consequents

Define

$$ X=\bigcup_{\theta\in F}A(\theta), \qquad Y=\{c(\theta):\theta\in F\}. $$

Consider the additional hypotheses

$$ X\cap Y=\varnothing, \qquad E(x)\subseteq X\quad\text{for every }x\in X. $$

These conditions allow arbitrary packages and arbitrarily many incoherences. They forbid rule chaining through consequent atoms and keep the available antecedent exclusions within $X$.

Because $E$ is symmetric, no head in $Y$ excludes an atom in $X$. Antecedent compatibility therefore follows automatically.

Theorem. On this subclass, the construction of Section 4 computes a common ground satisfying all six postulates in polynomial time.

Proof. The previous theorem supplies (P1)–(P5). It remains to prove (P6).

Every descendant has antecedent contained in $X$ and consequent in $Y$: an added atom $q$ belongs to $E(r)\subseteq X$. Thus neither input expressions nor constructed expressions can derive new atoms of $X$.

Fix a final clause $\phi=d_\beta$.

Original antecedent atoms

Let $p\in A(\beta)$ and put

$$ D=A(\phi)\setminus\{p\}. $$

Suppose some $R_t$ entails $D\to c(\beta)$. Since $D\subseteq X$ and rules cannot add atoms of $X$, a rule $\theta\in R_t$ producing $c(\beta)$ must have

$$ c(\theta)=c(\beta),\qquad A(\theta)\subseteq D. $$

This rule cannot be $\beta$, because $p\in A(\beta)$ but $p\notin D$. Hence $\theta\in F\setminus\{\beta\}$, and $A(\theta)\subseteq D\subseteq A(\phi)$ gives

$$ F\setminus\{\beta\}\models\phi. $$

This contradicts invariant 4 from Section 4. Therefore

$$ R_t\not\models\phi^{-p} \qquad\text{for every type }t. $$

The conclusion of (P6) consequently holds for every original antecedent atom.

The added antecedent atom

The remaining case is a changed descendant

$$ \phi=\beta^{+q}, \qquad q\notin A(\beta). $$

At its insertion, the construction selected an offending original pair $\alpha,\beta$ and an atom

$$ r\in A(\alpha)\setminus A(\beta), \qquad q\in E(r). $$

Since all antecedents lie in $X$ and no rule derives an atom of $X$, the relation $\alpha\Rightarrow_H\beta$ at that time implies the actual set inclusion

$$ A(\beta)\subseteq A(\alpha). $$

Let $d_\alpha$ be the final descendant of $\alpha$. It satisfies

$$ A(\alpha)\subseteq A(d_\alpha), \qquad c(d_\alpha)=c(\alpha)\in E(c(\beta)). $$

Symmetry gives $r\in E(q)$. Consider the replacement

$$ \chi=\phi^{r\setminus q}. $$

Its antecedent satisfies

$$ A(\chi)=A(\beta)\cup\{r\} \subseteq A(\alpha) \subseteq A(d_\alpha). $$

Therefore, in $G\cup\{\chi\}$,

$$ d_\alpha\Rightarrow_{G\cup\{\chi\}}\chi. $$

Their consequents are excluded, so this enlarged expression is incoherent. Thus, for the added atom $q$, at least one replacement fails the coherence test required by the premise of (P6).

Every antecedent atom of $\phi$ has now been covered. Hence (P6) holds for every final clause, completing the proof. $\square$

7. What remains open

The masses are rigorously eliminated from the complexity question. There is also a polynomial construction meeting the first five postulates on the antecedent-compatible class, and a complete polynomial construction for the separated subclass above.

The remaining general difficulty is (P6) when rule packages contain chains. Such chains can make a type entail an antecedent deletion while using the very clause whose non-redundancy is being checked. The example in Section 5 shows why that possibility cannot be dismissed.

No general construction satisfying (P6), and no NP-hardness reduction for the repaired class, is proved here.
