The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite worker types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), task classes \(R\), type costs \(c_t\), utilities \(u_{tr}\), eligibility constraints, per-capita task capacities \(q_r\), and normalized budget \(b\), consider equal-mass refinements uniformly permuted, with capacities and budget scaled with population. With only arrived workers observable, does there exist a causal truthful policy in the fluid limit that assigns worker mass irrevocably over time, respects the budget \(b\), and achieves expected welfare at least \\(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}(\mu,q,b)/(24\beta)\), where \\(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}\) maximizes \\(\sum_{t,r}u_{tr}y_{tr}\) subject to type-mass, task-capacity, and payment-budget constraints?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is represented by its support: \(T\) lists the \(\tau\) types of positive mass, with binary-rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(R\) contain \(m\) task classes, with eligibility relation \(E\subseteq T\times R\), rational capacities \(q_r>0\), budget \(b>0\), task-independent costs \(c_t\geq0\), and utilities \(u_{tr}>0\) on \(E\). A supplied rational \(\beta>1\) bounds the ratio of the largest to smallest eligible utility; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
For sample regularity, let \(g(\gamma)\) greedily allocate supply \(\mu_t/2\) into capacities \(q_r\), processing eligible pairs satisfying \(c_t/u_{tr}\leq\gamma\) in decreasing utility order, with fixed index-based ties. Assume \(\{\gamma\geq0:\gamma\sum_{(t,r)\in E}u_{tr}g_{tr}(\gamma)\leq b/\beta\}\) has a largest element \(\gamma_*\), and \(\sum_tg_{tr}(\gamma_*)=q_r\) for every class.
For compatible integers \(n\to\infty\), refine type \(t\) into \(n\mu_t\) workers of mass \(1/n\), create \(nq_r\) identical unit slots of class \(r\), and use payment budget \(nb\). Workers arrive in a uniformly random permutation at times \(i/n\). The mechanism knows only \(n,R,q,b,\beta\) initially; the support description specifies the instance but is hidden from it. Eligibility, utilities, and a nonnegative cost bid are revealed upon arrival; only costs are strategic.
Each worker must immediately be rejected or assigned one eligible slot, with an irrevocable payment; rejected workers receive zero. Require individual rationality, the payment budget for every realization, and dominant-strategy truthfulness for every fixed random seed, with worker payoff equal to payment minus true cost when assigned. A fluid policy is a limit in distribution of the normalized cumulative allocations and payments of such causal mechanisms; randomness includes arrival order and mechanism randomization.
Define \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}=\max\{\sum_{(t,r)\in E}u_{tr}y_{tr}:y\geq0,\ \sum_r y_{tr}\leq\mu_t,\ \sum_t y_{tr}\leq q_r,\ \sum_{(t,r)\in E}c_ty_{tr}\leq b\}\). This benchmark uses true costs, not incentive payments. Assume \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}>0\); the large-market condition is \(u_{\max}/(n\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}})\to0\). State computational bounds in \(m,\tau,L\), and, for refinement implementations, \(n\).

Does a single causal truthful fluid policy exist for all admissible societies that respects budget \(b\) and achieves expected welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}/(24\beta)\), or is this approximation guarantee impossible?

The discrete random-order matching result gives a truthful, budget-feasible \(24\beta\)-competitive guarantee against the fractional cost-budget benchmark, under large-market and sample-saturation assumptions. [Vaze–Coupechoux](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00581)
A later discrete result claims ratio \(24\) under an additional probabilistic cost–utility assumption; it does not establish the specified fluid model. No result settling this exact fluid question was located. [Vaze](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.09012)

Resolution

Yes. A single deterministic posted-price mechanism has a causal truthful fluid limit for every admissible society. In fact, its welfare is at least \(2\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}/(3\beta)\), which exceeds the requested guarantee.

The proof uses the stated saturation assumption directly. It does not require transferring the sample-and-price analysis of [Vaze–Coupechoux](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00581) to the fluid model.

1. Saturation certifies an affordable allocation

Write \(Q=\sum_{r\in R}q_r\), and let \(g=g(\gamma_*)\). Define \(U_g=\sum_{(t,r)\in E}u_{tr}g_{tr}\).

By the assumptions,

\( \sum_t g_{tr}=q_r,\qquad \sum_r g_{tr}\leq\mu_t/2,\qquad \gamma_*U_g\leq b/\beta. \)

Consequently, \(\sum_{(t,r)\in E}g_{tr}=Q\), and \(0<Q\leq1/2\). Positivity follows because \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}>0\), so there is at least one task class.

Let \(u_{\min}\) and \(u_{\max}\) be the smallest and largest eligible utilities. They exist and are positive because the eligible set is finite and nonempty. Since \(U_g\geq u_{\min}Q\) and \(u_{\max}/u_{\min}\leq\beta\),

\( \gamma_*u_{\max}\leq \frac{bu_{\max}}{\beta U_g}\leq\frac{bu_{\max}}{\beta u_{\min}Q}\leq\frac bQ. \)

Define the public price \(P=b/Q\). Whenever \(g_{tr}>0\), the pair survived the threshold filter, so

\( c_t\leq\gamma_*u_{tr}\leq\gamma_*u_{\max}\leq P. \)

Thus \(g\) fills every class using half the population supply, and every type it uses has cost at most the public price \(P\). The mechanism need not know \(g\), \(\gamma_*\), or the support.

2. A universal refinement mechanism

Fix an index order on the known task classes. For every compatible refinement size \(n\), use this mechanism:

  • Initialize class \(r\) with \(nq_r\) available slots.
  • Upon a worker’s arrival, reject it if its bid exceeds \(P\).
  • Otherwise, assign it to the first eligible class with an available slot, if one exists, and pay \(P\).
  • If no eligible slot is available, reject it and pay zero.

All decisions and payments are immediate and irrevocable. The mechanism uses only the public budget and capacities, the current worker’s report, and the remaining capacities.

Truthfulness and individual rationality

Fix an arrival order, all other workers’ reports, and any random seed. The state before a particular worker arrives is independent of that worker’s bid.

If no eligible slot is available, every bid gives payoff zero. Otherwise, every bid at most \(P\) produces the same assignment and payment \(P\), while every bid above \(P\) produces rejection.

For true cost \(c\):

  • If \(c<P\), acceptance gives payoff \(P-c>0\), so a truthful bid maximizes payoff.
  • If \(c>P\), acceptance gives negative payoff, while truthful rejection gives zero.
  • If \(c=P\), acceptance and rejection both give zero.

This proves dominant-strategy truthfulness for every fixed realization. Truthful accepted workers receive at least their true cost, establishing individual rationality.

Budget feasibility

There are exactly \(nQ\) slots. Every accepted worker receives \(P\). Therefore, for every arrival order and every bid profile, total payments satisfy

\( \text{total payments}\leq nQP=nb. \)

3. Welfare bound for every arrival order

Consider truthful bids and any compatible \(n\). Let \(x_{tr}\) be the final allocation normalized by \(n\), and put

\( x_t=\sum_r x_{tr},\qquad X=\sum_{(t,r)\in E}x_{tr}. \)

Let \(D\) be the set of classes filled completely by the mechanism:

\( D=\{r:\sum_t x_{tr}=q_r\}. \)

Define \(H\) to contain the affordable types eligible for at least one ultimately unfilled class:

\( H=\{t:c_t\leq P,\ \exists r\notin D\text{ with }(t,r)\in E\}. \)

Every worker of every type in \(H\) is accepted. Indeed, an ultimately unfilled class has an available slot throughout the arrival sequence: its remaining capacity only decreases. Thus each such worker arrives with an eligible available slot and has bid at most \(P\). Consequently, \(x_t=\mu_t\) for every \(t\in H\).

Now split the certificate \(g\) according to whether its destination class is filled:

\( Q=\sum_{r\in D}\sum_t g_{tr}+\sum_{r\notin D}\sum_t g_{tr}. \)

For the first term,

\( \sum_{r\in D}\sum_t g_{tr}=\sum_{r\in D}q_r\leq X. \)

For the second term, every positive \(g_{tr}\) with \(r\notin D\) has \(t\in H\), by Section 1. The half-supply constraint therefore gives

\( \sum_{r\notin D}\sum_t g_{tr}\leq\sum_{t\in H}\frac{\mu_t}{2}=\frac12\sum_{t\in H}x_t\leq\frac X2. \)

Combining these inequalities yields

\( Q\leq\frac32X,\qquad X\geq\frac23Q. \)

Let \(W_n\) denote normalized welfare. Every assigned eligible pair has utility at least \(u_{\min}\), so \(W_n\geq u_{\min}X\geq2u_{\min}Q/3\).

Every feasible solution of the benchmark LP assigns total mass at most \(Q\). Hence \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}\leq u_{\max}Q\), and therefore

\( W_n\geq\frac23u_{\min}Q\geq\frac{2u_{\min}}{3u_{\max}}\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}\geq\frac{2}{3\beta}\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}. \)

This bound holds for every arrival order, without an asymptotic error.

4. Existence of a single fluid limit

It remains to show that the same refinement mechanisms converge, rather than merely possessing instance-dependent subsequential limits.

For analysis, let \(A_t^n(s)\) be the normalized number of type-\(t\) arrivals by time \(s\in[0,1]\). Let \(Y_{tr}^n(s)\) be the normalized cumulative allocation to pair \((t,r)\).

Linearly interpolate these paths between arrival times. This is only an analytical device: it changes each allocation coordinate by at most \(1/n\), so the actual causal step paths have the same limit.

Uniform convergence of arrival paths

At time \(k/n\), the type-\(t\) count is hypergeometric. Its normalized expectation is \(\mu_t k/n\), and its variance is

\( \operatorname{Var}(A_t^n(k/n))=\frac{k(n-k)}{n^2(n-1)}\mu_t(1-\mu_t)\leq\frac1{4n}. \)

The variance formula follows by summing indicator variances and their without-replacement covariances, which equal \(-\mu_t(1-\mu_t)/(n-1)\).

Chebyshev’s inequality gives convergence at each point of a fixed finite time mesh. A union bound gives simultaneous convergence across that mesh and the finitely many support types. Monotonicity then bounds the error between mesh points by the endpoint errors plus the mesh width. Taking increasingly fine meshes establishes

\( \max_{t\in T}\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|A_t^n(s)-\mu_t s|\ \longrightarrow\ 0 \)

in probability.

Identification and uniqueness of allocation limits

Each interpolated allocation coordinate is nondecreasing and \(1\)-Lipschitz. The collection of allocation paths is therefore relatively compact in the uniform topology.

We now identify any limit along a deterministic sequence whose arrival paths converge uniformly to \(\mu_t s\).

For a nonempty set \(S\) of classes with remaining capacity, define

\( a_S(t)=\min\{r\in S:(t,r)\in E\} \)

when \(c_t\leq P\) and that set is nonempty. Otherwise, leave \(a_S(t)\) undefined. Define the corresponding rates

\( \lambda_{tr}(S)= \begin{cases} \mu_t,&a_S(t)=r,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad \lambda_r(S)=\sum_t\lambda_{tr}(S). \)

Consider an interval strictly inside a period on which a limiting allocation path has the same set \(S\) of classes with positive remaining capacity.

If \(a_S(t)=r\), then class \(r\) has remaining capacity bounded away from zero on every compact subinterval. Uniform convergence ensures that it is available in sufficiently large refinements throughout that subinterval. Thus every type-\(t\) arrival is accepted into \(r\), except possibly when assigned to an earlier eligible class outside \(S\).

Those earlier classes are already filled in the limit. Their total allocation increments on the subinterval converge to zero. Hence the limiting allocation rate to \((t,r)\) is exactly \(\mu_t\).

If \(a_S(t)\) is undefined because no eligible class belongs to \(S\), all allocations of type \(t\) go to already filled classes, whose increments vanish. If \(c_t>P\), that type is always rejected. Its limiting allocation rate is also zero.

Therefore every limit has rates \(\lambda_{tr}(S)\) until another class fills.

These rates determine a unique trajectory by the following finite recursion. Start at time \(s=0\) with remaining capacities \(z_r=q_r\). In a phase with active set \(S\), advance for duration

\( \delta=\min\left\{1-s,\ \min_{\substack{r\in S\\\lambda_r(S)>0}}\frac{z_r}{\lambda_r(S)}\right\}, \)

interpreting the inner minimum as infinite if all rates are zero. Increase allocations by \(\delta\lambda_{tr}(S)\), decrease capacities accordingly, and remove every class whose capacity becomes zero.

Every phase ending before time \(1\) removes at least one class. Thus there are at most \(m\) exhaustion events. The recursion uniquely determines a continuous path \(Y\).

For completeness, each limiting class-allocation path is nondecreasing and bounded by its capacity. Once a class reaches capacity it stays there, so the active set changes at most \(m\) times. The preceding interval argument therefore identifies every subsequential limit with this recursion; continuity identifies the phase endpoints as well.

Compactness and uniqueness imply that \(Y^n\) converges uniformly in probability to \(Y\). Indeed, any sequence staying a fixed distance from \(Y\), despite uniformly convergent arrival paths, would have a convergent subsequence with a different limit, contradicting uniqueness.

Payments, causality, and welfare

Normalized cumulative payments are exactly \(P\sum_{(t,r)\in E}Y_{tr}^n(s)\). They consequently converge uniformly in probability to

\( Z(s)=P\sum_{(t,r)\in E}Y_{tr}(s). \)

Since \(\sum_{t,r}Y_{tr}(s)\leq Q\), the fluid payment budget satisfies \(Z(s)\leq PQ=b\) at every time.

The limit is obtained from the same causal, universally truthful mechanism for every society. The support-dependent recursion above describes its trajectory; it supplies no information to the mechanism.

Terminal welfare converges because it is a finite linear combination of terminal allocation coordinates. Passing the bound from Section 3 to the limit gives

\( W=\sum_{(t,r)\in E}u_{tr}Y_{tr}(1)\geq\frac{2}{3\beta}\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}\geq\frac{1}{24\beta}\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}. \)

The limit is deterministic, so its expected welfare equals \(W\).

5. Computational bounds

The construction distinguishes the hidden support description from the information used online.

  • Refinement implementation: Computing \(P\) takes \(O(m)\) rational arithmetic operations. Scanning the classes takes \(O(m)\) operations per worker, for \(O(nm)\) operations overall. The maintained state consists of \(m\) capacity counters and the price. For binary-encoded rational reports, bit complexity is polynomial in \(n,m,L+\log n\).
  • Fluid trajectory from a supplied support: Each phase’s assignment choices and rates can be computed in \(O(m\tau)\) arithmetic operations. With at most \(m\) exhaustion events, the total is \(O(m^2\tau)\). Exact rational arithmetic gives bit complexity polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\): each phase introduces only sums of input masses and divisions by such rates, and there are at most \(m\) phases.
  • Benchmark computation: The rational LP has at most \(m\tau\) variables and \(\tau+m+1\) substantive constraints, so standard rational linear programming computes its value in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

The large-market condition holds automatically for each fixed society because \(u_{\max}\) is fixed and \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{LP}}>0\). The welfare bound already holds at every compatible refinement size.
