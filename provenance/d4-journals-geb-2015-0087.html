The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau\), intrinsic opinions \(s_w,s_L,s_1,\ldots,s_\tau\), a distinguished source with baseline self-weight \(1\), and a leaf cohort of mass \(\mu_L\) listening to it with unit weight, choose exposure masses \(x_i\in[0,\mu_i]\) from isolated item types. The mean-field equilibrium is \(z_w=(s_w+\sum_i x_i s_i)/(1+\sum_i x_i)\), each item remains at \(s_i\), and leaves have \(z_L=(s_L+z_w)/2\); minimize \(\mu_L((z_L-s_L)^2+(z_L-z_w)^2)\), excluding the source cost, and return an optimal \(x\) and equilibrium.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given binary-encoded rational masses \(\mu_L,\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau\geq 0\) and intrinsic opinions \(s_w,s_L,s_1,\ldots,s_\tau\), choose exposure masses \(0\leq x_i\leq\mu_i\) from otherwise isolated item types. The source has baseline self-weight \(1\); its equilibrium opinion is \(z_w=(s_w+\sum_i x_i s_i)/(1+\sum_i x_i)\). Items remain at \(s_i\), and a leaf cohort of mass \(\mu_L\) listens to the source with unit weight, so \(z_L=(s_L+z_w)/2\).
Minimize \(\mu_L((z_L-s_L)^2+(z_L-z_w)^2)\), excluding the source’s cost, and return optimal exposures and the equilibrium.

Is this optimization problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents, selecting the best set of unit-weight outgoing edges from one source is NP-hard [Bindel, Kleinberg, and Oren](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~bindel/paper/focs11-opinions-full.pdf). Optimal weighting of one specified added edge is polynomial-time computable [Bindel, Kleinberg, and Oren](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~bindel/papers/2015-geb.pdf).

Result and encoding assumption

The problem is solvable exactly in polynomial time, assuming the intrinsic opinions, like the masses, are binary-encoded rationals. The algorithm below returns rational optimal exposures and the complete equilibrium. No restriction on the signs of the intrinsic opinions is needed.

1. Reduce the objective to the source’s opinion

Write \(D(x)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}x_i\) and \(f(x)=(s_w+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}x_i s_i)/D(x)\). The feasible set is the box \(B=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}[0,\mu_i]\), and \(D(x)\geq 1\) throughout this box.

Since \(z_w=f(x)\) and \(z_L=(s_L+f(x))/2\), we have

\(\displaystyle z_L-s_L=\frac{f(x)-s_L}{2},\qquad z_L-z_w=-\frac{f(x)-s_L}{2}.\)

Consequently, the objective is exactly

\(\displaystyle C(x)=\frac{\mu_L}{2}\bigl(f(x)-s_L\bigr)^2.\)

If \(\mu_L=0\), every feasible exposure vector is optimal; return \(x_i=0\), \(z_w=s_w\), \(z_L=(s_L+s_w)/2\), and \(z_i=s_i\). Henceforth suppose \(\mu_L>0\).

2. Compute the extreme attainable source opinions

Because \(B\) is nonempty and compact and \(f\) is continuous, the quantities \(a=\min_{x\in B}f(x)\) and \(b=\max_{x\in B}f(x)\) exist.

Lemma. A minimizer can be chosen to expose every type with \(s_i<a\) fully and every other type not at all. A maximizer can be chosen to expose every type with \(s_i>b\) fully and every other type not at all.

Proof. For any feasible change of a single coordinate by \(\delta\), direct subtraction gives

\(\displaystyle f(x+\delta e_i)-f(x)=\frac{\delta(s_i-f(x))}{D(x)+\delta}.\)

The denominator is positive because the modified vector is feasible.

At a minimizer \(x\), we have \(f(x)=a\). If \(s_i<a\) and \(x_i<\mu_i\), a positive feasible change strictly decreases \(f\), a contradiction. Thus \(x_i=\mu_i\). If \(s_i>a\) and \(x_i>0\), a negative feasible change strictly decreases \(f\), also a contradiction. Thus \(x_i=0\). When \(s_i=a\), setting \(x_i=0\) leaves \(f\) unchanged by the same identity. Performing these changes proves the minimum assertion.

At a maximizer, a positive feasible change strictly increases \(f\) when \(s_i>b\), and a negative feasible change strictly increases \(f\) when \(s_i<b\). Therefore those coordinates must respectively be full and zero. Coordinates with \(s_i=b\) can again be set to zero without changing \(f\). This proves the maximum assertion.

Now sort the indices so that \(s_{\pi(1)}\leq\cdots\leq s_{\pi(\tau)}\). Define the prefix ratios

\(\displaystyle A_k=\frac{s_w+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{\pi(j)}s_{\pi(j)}}{1+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{\pi(j)}}\qquad(0\leq k\leq\tau).\)

The empty prefix has \(A_0=s_w\). Every prefix is feasible, and the lemma supplies a minimizing prefix: precisely the types with opinions strictly below \(a\). Hence

\(\displaystyle a=\min_{0\leq k\leq\tau}A_k.\)

Similarly, sort in descending order, \(s_{\rho(1)}\geq\cdots\geq s_{\rho(\tau)}\), and compute

\(\displaystyle B_k=\frac{s_w+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{\rho(j)}s_{\rho(j)}}{1+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{\rho(j)}}.\)

Then \(b=\max_{0\leq k\leq\tau}B_k\). Save the corresponding feasible exposure vectors \(x^{-}\) and \(x^{+}\), attaining \(a\) and \(b\).

This construction also covers zero item masses and the case of no item types.

3. Construct optimal exposures

The desired source opinion is the closest point to \(s_L\) in the interval \([a,b]\):

\(\displaystyle y=\min\{b,\max\{a,s_L\}\}.\)

If \(a=b\), return \(x^{*}=x^{-}\). Otherwise define

\(\displaystyle D_-=1+\sum_i x_i^{-},\qquad D_+=1+\sum_i x_i^{+},\)

\(\displaystyle t=\frac{D_-(y-a)}{D_-(y-a)+D_+(b-y)},\qquad x^{*}=(1-t)x^{-}+t x^{+}.\)

Since \(a\leq y\leq b\) and \(a<b\), the denominator defining \(t\) is positive and \(0\leq t\leq1\). Thus \(x^{*}\in B\).

The numerator and denominator of \(f\) are affine functions of the exposures. Therefore

\(\displaystyle f(x^{*})=\frac{(1-t)D_-a+tD_+b}{(1-t)D_-+tD_+}.\)

The definition of \(t\) gives

\(\displaystyle (1-t)D_-(y-a)=tD_+(b-y),\)

which, upon rearrangement, shows that \(f(x^{*})=y\).

This calculation proves that every point of \([a,b]\) is attainable. By the definitions of \(a\) and \(b\), no point outside it is attainable. Minimizing \(C(x)\) is therefore exactly the problem of minimizing the squared distance to \(s_L\) over this interval, whose minimizer is \(y\).

The returned equilibrium and optimal value are

\(\displaystyle z_w^{*}=y,\qquad z_L^{*}=\frac{s_L+y}{2},\qquad z_i^{*}=s_i,\qquad C^{*}=\frac{\mu_L}{2}(y-s_L)^2.\)

4. Polynomial bit complexity

Let \(L\) denote the total binary input length. Sorting requires polynomially many rational comparisons. Prefix sums compute all candidate ratios using linearly many rational arithmetic operations after sorting.

Their bit lengths are polynomial in \(L\): each prefix numerator or denominator is a sum of input rationals or products of two input rationals. A common denominator can be formed by multiplying their denominators; its bit length is at most the sum of the constituent bit lengths. Numerators likewise have polynomial bit length. Exact comparisons use cross multiplication of integers of polynomial bit length.

The interpolation formula uses a constant number of additional rational operations to compute \(t\), followed by a constant number per exposure coordinate. Thus the exposures, equilibrium, and objective value all have polynomial bit length and are computable in polynomial time.

5. Relation to discrete edge-selection hardness

The cited hardness result concerns selecting a discrete set of unit-weight edges; its source-cost-excluded construction is stated in Claim 6.1 of the [2011 paper](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~bindel/paper/focs11-opinions-full.pdf).

Here the exposures vary continuously throughout a box. In particular, the interpolation in Section 3 is feasible, whereas it need not represent a discrete edge set. The proof above therefore establishes tractability for the stated continuous optimization problem without contradicting that hardness result.
