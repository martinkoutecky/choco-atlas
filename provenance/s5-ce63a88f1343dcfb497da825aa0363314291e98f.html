The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), capacities \(\omega_t\), good classes \(J\) with rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), and rational values \(v_{tj}\), define coalition welfare \(W(x)=\max\{\sum_{t,j}v_{tj}y_{tj}:\sum_j y_{tj}\leq\omega_t x_t,\ \sum_t y_{tj}\leq b_j,\ y_{tj}\geq0\}\). For each \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), let \(g(\lambda)\) be the minimum-Euclidean-norm supergradient of \(W\) at \(\lambda\mu\). Determine the exact computational complexity, in \(|T|\), \(|J|\), and the input bit length, of computing the type shares \(A_t=\mu_t\int_0^1g_t(\lambda)\,d\lambda\) for all \(t\in T\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|J|\geq1\) divisible good classes with positive binary rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\). Each type has a positive integer capacity \(\omega_t\), encoded in binary, and signed binary rational additive values \(v_{tj}\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; goods and capacity may remain unused.
Define welfare on the entire nonnegative orthant by \(W(x)=\max\{\sum_{t\in T,j\in J}v_{tj}y_{tj}:\sum_jy_{tj}\leq\omega_tx_t,\ \sum_ty_{tj}\leq b_j,\ y_{tj}\geq0\}\), for \(x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{T}\); coalition masses satisfy \(0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t\), and supplies remain fixed.
For \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), let \(g(\lambda)\) be the supergradient of \(W\) at \(\lambda\mu\) having minimum Euclidean norm, with supergradients taken relative to \(\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{T}\). Define \(A_t=\mu_t\int_0^1g_t(\lambda)\,d\lambda\), using the Lebesgue integral.

Is computing all shares \(A_t\) exactly possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this function problem \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?

For finite agents and indivisible goods, exact Shapley-value computation for the game whose coalition worth is its maximum allocation welfare is \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete ([Greco–Scarcello](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.3418)).
An algorithm for Aumann–Shapley prices of transportation cost functions is known ([Samet–Tauman–Zang](https://www.tau.ac.il/~samet/papers/transpotation.pdf)); this is a related result, with no verified complexity classification for the minimum-norm-supergradient shares defined here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive support masses to sum to one, interpreting supplies as per-capita supplies for the full society.
  • Retained positive integer capacities from the discrete allocation definition and specified binary encoding; valuations remain signed rationals.
  • Took the type support and good-class list to be nonempty and retained only classes with positive supply.
  • Defined welfare on the entire nonnegative orthant and used that domain for supergradients, rather than the coalition box or probability simplex.
  • Specified that coalition masses lie between zero and their society masses, supplies stay fixed, and goods and capacity may remain unused.
  • Specified the Lebesgue integral and polynomial-time Turing reductions for hardness of the exact function problem.

Result

Computing all the shares exactly is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. This holds even when every capacity is \(\omega_t=1\).

The proof uses four share-oracle calls. Polynomial bounds on the lengths of their exact rational outputs are included below.

1. Dual prices and the specified supergradient

Write the allocation value as a function of capacities:
\[ \Phi(z,b)=\max\left\{\sum_{t,j}v_{tj}y_{tj}: \sum_jy_{tj}\leq z_t,\quad \sum_ty_{tj}\leq b_j,\quad y\geq0\right\}. \]
Its dual feasible region is
\[ D=\{(p,q)\geq0:p_t+q_j\geq v_{tj}\text{ for every }t,j\}, \]
and strong LP duality gives
\[ \Phi(z,b)=\min_{(p,q)\in D}(z\cdot p+b\cdot q). \]

Here and below, equations displayed on separate lines are understood as mathematical expressions.

For the society in the question, \(W(x)=\Phi((\omega_tx_t)_t,b)\). Its supergradients relative to the entire nonnegative orthant are exactly
\[ \partial^+W(x)= \{(\omega_tp_t)_t:(p,q)\text{ is dual optimal at }x\}. \tag{1} \]

To prove the nontrivial inclusion, let \(g\) be a supergradient. Every coordinate of \(g\) is nonnegative: otherwise the supergradient inequality at \(x+re_t\), for sufficiently large \(r\), contradicts \(W(x+re_t)\geq0\). Put \(p_t=g_t/\omega_t\). Directly optimizing over \(x'\geq0\) gives
\[ \sup_{x'\geq0}\bigl(W(x')-g\cdot x'\bigr) = \sum_j b_j\max\{0,\max_t(v_{tj}-p_t)\}. \tag{2} \]
Indeed, for any allocation one may choose \(x'_t=\sum_jy_{tj}/\omega_t\); subsequently each good can be allocated independently to a type maximizing its positive net value.

The supergradient inequality says that the supremum in (2) equals \(W(x)-g\cdot x\). Consequently, setting
\[ q_j=\max\{0,\max_t(v_{tj}-p_t)\} \]
produces an optimal dual solution. The reverse inclusion in (1) follows from the affine upper bound supplied by any feasible dual solution.

For positive capacities and supplies, every optimal dual solution satisfies
\[ 0\leq p_t\leq \max\{0,\max_jv_{tj}\}, \qquad 0\leq q_j\leq V, \quad V=\max\{0,\max_{t,j}v_{tj}\}. \tag{3} \]
For example, decreasing a \(p_t\) above its stated bound preserves feasibility and strictly reduces the objective.

The optimal dual solutions also have a componentwise least \(p\). Given two optimal pairs, both
\[ (\min(p,p'),\max(q,q')), \qquad (\max(p,p'),\min(q,q')) \]
are feasible. Their objective values sum to the sum of the original optimal values, so both are optimal. Compactness from (3), followed by finitely many coordinatewise minima, establishes the assertion. Since all coordinates are nonnegative, this least \(p\) minimizes \(\sum_t\omega_t^2p_t^2\). Thus it gives precisely the supergradient requested in the question.

2. Exceptional parameters and output lengths

We need the following encoding fact.

Lemma 1. Consider \(\Phi(\lambda a,b)\), where all entries of \(a,b\) are positive rationals. Its dual optimum is unique except possibly at parameters of the form
\[ \lambda=\frac{\sum_{j\in S}b_j}{\sum_{t\in R}a_t}, \qquad \varnothing\neq R\subseteq T,\quad \varnothing\neq S\subseteq J. \tag{4} \]
If \(a\) has at most three distinct entries, the corresponding normalized shares have polynomial-length rational encodings.

Proof. Fix a primal optimum \(y\), and suppose there are two dual optima. Form the bipartite graph containing precisely the edges on which \(y_{tj}>0\).

Along each such edge, both dual solutions satisfy \(p_t+q_j=v_{tj}\). If a connected component has a coordinate that is zero in both dual solutions, their differences vanish throughout that component.

Otherwise, the average of the two dual solutions has every coordinate in that component strictly positive. Complementary slackness then makes every capacity and supply constraint in that component tight. No positive allocation crosses between components, so its total allocated quantity satisfies
\[ \lambda\sum_{t\in R}a_t=\sum_{j\in S}b_j. \]
An isolated vertex necessarily has zero dual coordinate. This proves uniqueness outside (4).

There are finitely many dual vertices. Hence the unique optimum is constant on every open interval between consecutive exceptional parameters.

Let \(D_v\) be a common denominator of the values. Every dual vertex has coordinates with denominators dividing \(D_v\): its tight-edge graph must have a zero coordinate in each component, since an unanchored component permits a small shift of all its \(p\)-coordinates against all its \(q\)-coordinates. Starting at a zero coordinate, the tight equations express every other coordinate as a signed sum of values.

Now clear denominators by writing \(a_t=A_t/D_a\) and \(b_j=B_j/D_b\), with integer numerators. A parameter in (4) has denominator dividing
\[ D_b\sum_{t\in R}A_t. \]
If \(a\) has at most three distinct entries, there are at most \((\tau+1)^3\) distinct positive subset sums \(\sum_{t\in R}A_t\). Each has polynomial bit length. Therefore all exceptional parameters have a common denominator of polynomial bit length. Integrating the constant dual prices between these parameters, and multiplying by the normalized masses, preserves polynomial bit length. \(\square\)

This argument also establishes measurability and boundedness of the specified selector. Its values at the finitely many exceptional parameters do not affect its integral.

For later use, arbitrary positive capacity rates \(a\) can be represented by valid societies. Set
\[ S=\sum_ta_t,\qquad \omega_t=1,\qquad \mu_t=a_t/S,\qquad b_j^{\mathrm{soc}}=b_j/S. \tag{5} \]
Then the normalized welfare is \(\Phi(Sx,b)/S\), and its dual type prices are unchanged. If the share oracle returns \(A_t^{\mathrm{soc}}\), multiplication by \(S\) gives
\[ B_t(a,b):=a_t\int_0^1p_t(\lambda a,b)\,d\lambda. \tag{6} \]

3. Recovering a perturbed welfare integral from shares

Let a capacity-allocation instance have positive rates \(a\), positive supplies \(b\), and values bounded above by \(V\). Fix
\[ 0<\alpha<1,\qquad 0<d<\tfrac12\min_jb_j,\qquad K>V. \]
Define
\[ F_d(\lambda)=\Phi(\lambda a,b-\lambda d\mathbf1). \tag{7} \]

Add one dummy type for every good \(j\). Its capacity rate is \(d\), its value for good \(j\) is \(K\), and its other values are \(-1\).

Every optimum fills each dummy’s capacity. Otherwise, available quantity of its good can be assigned to it, removing an equal quantity from an original type if necessary; this strictly increases welfare because \(K>V\). The augmented welfare consequently equals
\[ Kmd\lambda+F_d(\lambda). \]

Let \(p^{D}_j(\lambda)\) be the selected dual price of dummy \(j\). Positive dummy allocation implies
\[ p^{D}_j(\lambda)=K-q_j(\lambda), \tag{8} \]
where \((p,q)\) is an optimal dual for the original problem with supplies \(b-\lambda d\mathbf1\).

For an endpoint \(c\in\{\alpha,1\}\), query the augmented instance with all its capacity rates multiplied by \(c\), using normalization (5). After undoing that normalization, its dummy share is
\[ Z_j(c)=d\int_0^c p^{D}_j(\lambda)\,d\lambda. \]
Thus two share queries recover, exactly,
\[ Q_j:=\int_\alpha^1q_j(\lambda)\,d\lambda = K(1-\alpha)-\frac{Z_j(1)-Z_j(\alpha)}d. \tag{9} \]

At every differentiability point of \(F_d\), any optimal dual satisfies
\[ F_d'(\lambda)=a\cdot p-d\sum_jq_j, \]
because its affine objective supports the scalar value function at that parameter. Dual optimality therefore gives
\[ F_d(\lambda)-\lambda F_d'(\lambda)=b\cdot q. \]
Integration by parts yields
\[ \boxed{ \int_\alpha^1F_d(\lambda)\,d\lambda = \frac{ \sum_jb_jQ_j+F_d(1)-\alpha F_d(\alpha) }{2}. } \tag{10} \]
The two endpoint values are computable by exact rational LP optimization. Absolute continuity needed here follows from the finite piecewise-linear representation of \(F_d\).

Finally, decreasing supplies by \(\lambda d\) changes welfare by at most \(Vmd\):
\[ 0\leq\Phi(\lambda a,b)-F_d(\lambda)\leq Vmd. \tag{11} \]
For this bound, remove enough allocation from each diminished supply constraint; the total removed quantity is at most \(md\), and its value per unit is at most \(V\).

Equations (9)–(11) provide the required controlled integral recovery.

4. The published parametric-flow ingredient

We use the counting networks in Lemmas 3.1–3.2 and the proof of Lemma 3.2 of [Disser–Skutella, *The Simplex Algorithm Is NP-Mighty*](https://www2.mathematik.tu-darmstadt.de/~disser/pdfs/DisserSkutella18.pdf).

Given positive integer Partition entries \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\), use their normalization
\[ \varepsilon=\frac1{24\sum_iw_i}, \qquad a_i=\varepsilon w_i, \qquad \gamma=\varepsilon/5. \]
Their hypotheses hold: the entries are multiples of \(\varepsilon\), and their sum is less than \(1/12\).

The construction gives two networks \(N_+\) and \(N_-=N_+\setminus\{e_0\}\), with polynomial descriptions and common maximum-flow value \(F=2^{n+1}\). They are acyclic, have positive integer capacities, have no arcs entering their source or leaving their sink, and every other vertex has positive outgoing capacity.

Their successive-shortest-path flows agree at every flow value \(2j\). In each ensuing pair of unit augmentations, the marginal costs agree unless the corresponding signed Partition sum is zero. In that case, for some \(h_j\), they are:

| Network | First marginal cost | Second marginal cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(N_-\) | \(h_j\) | \(h_j\) |
| \(N_+\) | \(h_j-\gamma\) | \(h_j+\gamma\) |

Let \(C_\pm(f)\) denote minimum cost at flow value \(f\). The shortest-path augmentations also give the minimum-cost flows at fractional intermediate values: the initial zero flow is optimal because the networks are acyclic, and shortest residual-path augmentation preserves optimality.

Thus each zero signed sum contributes a triangular gap between \(C_-\) and \(C_+\), of height \(\gamma\) and base \(2\). Other blocks contribute zero. Consequently,
\[ \int_0^F\bigl(C_-(f)-C_+(f)\bigr)\,df = \gamma\,\#\left\{ I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}: \sum_{i\in I}w_i=\sum_{i\notin I}w_i \right\}. \tag{12} \]
In particular, this integral is zero for a no-instance and at least \(\gamma\) for a yes-instance.

5. Encoding these flows as allocation welfare

Use the vertices and arcs of \(N_+\). Write \(U_e\) for arc capacity and \(c_e\) for arc cost, and put
\[ C_r=\sum_{e\text{ leaving }r}U_e, \qquad U=\sum_eU_e. \]
There are \(N\) vertices. The source satisfies \(C_s=F\), the sink satisfies \(C_t=0\), and every other \(C_r\) is a positive integer.

Choose
\[ C=\max\{1,\max_e|c_e|\},\quad H=NC,\quad \rho=H+1,\quad \kappa=2H+C+1. \]

Make one allocation type for each vertex and one good for each arc, with supply \(U_e\). For \(e=(u,v)\), set
\[ v_{u,e}=\kappa+\rho\,\mathbf1_{\{u\neq s\}}, \qquad v_{v,e}=\kappa+\rho\,\mathbf1_{\{v\neq s\}}-c_e. \tag{13} \]
All other entries are \(-1\). For the minus instance, change only the head’s value for \(e_0\) to \(-1\). Retain that good and its supply in both instances.

At parameter \(f\), give every nonsink type capacity \(C_r\), and the sink type capacity \(f\). Let the resulting welfare be \(\phi_\pm(f)\). We claim
\[ \phi_\pm(f) = \kappa U+\rho(U-C_s+f)-C_\pm(f). \tag{14} \]

Given a minimum-cost flow \(x\), allocate \(U_e-x_e\) of good \(e\) to its tail and \(x_e\) to its head. Flow conservation gives row totals
\[ C_s-f,\qquad f,\qquad C_r \]
for the source, sink, and other vertices, respectively. This allocation has the value in (14).

To verify optimality, the optimal flow’s residual network has no negative-cost cycle. Shortest-path potentials, after shifting the source potential to zero and reversing signs, give numbers \(h_r\) satisfying
\[ h_s=0,\qquad |h_r|\leq H, \qquad c_{uv}+h_v-h_u\geq0 \]
on every forward residual arc.

Define allocation-dual variables
\[ p_r=\rho\mathbf1_{\{r\neq s\}}+h_r, \qquad q_e=\kappa-\min\{h_u,c_e+h_v\}. \tag{15} \]
They are nonnegative. Both inequalities associated with (13) hold, and they are tight wherever the constructed allocation is positive: forward residual feasibility gives tightness for allocated tail slack; backward residual feasibility gives tightness for allocated head flow.

Every good is exhausted; every nonsource row is tight; and the source has \(p_s=0\). Complementary slackness proves (14).

For the minus instance, the forbidden good \(e_0\) is entirely assigned to its tail; set \(q_{e_0}=\kappa-h_u\). Its forbidden head inequality is automatic. The same proof applies.

All these values are bounded above by
\[ V=\kappa+\rho+C. \tag{16} \]

6. Replacing fixed capacities by a homogeneous mass ray

Set
\[ Q=1+\max_{r\neq t}C_r. \]
Choose a positive dyadic rational \(\delta\) satisfying \(\delta Q\leq1/2\), and write
\[ \alpha=1-\delta,\qquad R=F/\delta. \]
Because \(F\) is an integer and \(\delta\) is dyadic, \(R\) is an integer.

Give each nonsink type capacity rate \(Q\), and the sink capacity rate \(R\). Add a private good for every vertex. Its supply is
\[ P_r=Q-C_r\quad(r\neq t), \qquad P_t=R-F. \tag{17} \]
The owning type values it at \(H_0=V+1\); all other types value it at \(-1\).

For \(\lambda\in[\alpha,1]\), every private good is completely allocated to its owner. The owner has sufficient capacity, and its private value strictly exceeds every regular value. Its remaining regular capacity is
\[ C_r-Q(1-\lambda)\quad(r\neq t), \qquad f(\lambda)=R(\lambda-\alpha)\quad(r=t). \tag{18} \]
These quantities are nonnegative. The sink’s regular capacity ranges from zero to \(F\).

Let \(\widetilde W_\pm(\lambda)\) be the welfare on this homogeneous ray, and let
\[ P_0=H_0\sum_rP_r. \]
Removing capacity changes regular welfare by at most \(V\) times the removed quantity, so
\[ \left| \widetilde W_\pm(\lambda)-P_0-\phi_\pm(f(\lambda)) \right| \leq V(N-1)Q\delta. \tag{19} \]

The private-good contribution is identical in both instances. Combining (12), (14), (19), and the linear substitution \(f=R(\lambda-\alpha)\), therefore gives
\[ \left| \frac1\delta\int_\alpha^1 (\widetilde W_+-\widetilde W_-)\,d\lambda - \frac1F\int_0^F(C_--C_+)\,df \right| \leq2V(N-1)Q\delta. \tag{20} \]

Every supply in the constructed instances is a positive integer.

7. Choosing errors and making the oracle calls

Put \(G=\gamma/F\). Choose \(\delta\) above small enough that
\[ \delta\leq \min\left\{ \tfrac12,\frac1{2Q}, \frac{G}{16V(N-1)Q} \right\}. \tag{21} \]
Then the error in (20) is at most \(G/8\).

Let \(M\) be the number of goods after adding the private goods. Choose a positive dyadic rational
\[ d\leq\min\left\{\tfrac12,\frac{G}{16H_0M}\right\}. \tag{22} \]
Apply Section 3 separately to the plus and minus instances, using dummy value \(K=H_0+1\). Two share calls per instance compute exactly
\[ J_\pm= \frac1\delta\int_\alpha^1 \Phi_\pm(\lambda a,b-\lambda d\mathbf1)\,d\lambda. \]
By (11), their difference approximates the left-hand average in (20) within \(2H_0Md\leq G/8\).

Consequently,
\[ \left| (J_+-J_-) - \frac1F\int_0^F(C_--C_+)\,df \right|\leq G/4. \tag{23} \]

For a no-instance of Partition, \(J_+-J_-\leq G/4\). For a yes-instance, \(J_+-J_-\geq3G/4\). Comparing the exactly computed difference with \(G/2\) decides Partition.

All choices in (21)–(22) have polynomial bit length: each bound is a positive rational of polynomial bit length, so a sufficiently small dyadic rational can be selected with polynomially many bits.

Finally, every oracle query has only three distinct capacity rates:
\[ cQ,\qquad cR,\qquad cd, \qquad c\in\{\alpha,1\}. \]
Normalization (5) gives positive rational masses summing to one, positive rational supplies, and capacities \(\omega_t=1\). Its support and goods counts are polynomial in the Partition input size. Lemma 1 ensures that every returned exact share has polynomial bit length.

Thus the four oracle calls, the exact endpoint LP computations, and all subsequent rational arithmetic constitute a polynomial-time Turing reduction. This proves the claimed \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for the precise minimum-norm-supergradient shares in the question.
