The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite value support \(V\), a rational law \(F\) on \(V\), and positive supply and demand rates \(\alpha_S,\alpha_B\), let \(\mu_S=\alpha_S F\) and \(\mu_B=\alpha_B F\). At each integral scale \(N\), draw \(N\alpha_S\) sellers and \(N\alpha_B\) buyers independently from \(F\), add one buyer drawn from \(F\), and run BTR. Does normalized expected BTR gains from trade converge to the optimal mass-matching gains from trade for \((\mu_S,\mu_B)\), and what is the limiting gap from the baseline fluid optimum?

Let \(V\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\) be nonempty, with \(m=|V|\), and let \(F(v)>0\) be binary rational probabilities summing to \(1\). Let \(\alpha_S,\alpha_B\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\), and set \(\mu_S(v)=\alpha_SF(v)\), \(\mu_B(v)=\alpha_BF(v)\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) denotes support size, not the number of possible types. Here each side has \(\tau=m\); its total mass need not be \(1\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and any additional parameters.
Each seller supplies one item and each buyer wants at most one. Define the baseline fluid optimum \(G^*=\max_{x\geq0}\sum_{s,b\in V}(b-s)x_{s,b}\), subject to \(\sum_bx_{s,b}\leq\mu_S(s)\) and \(\sum_sx_{s,b}\leq\mu_B(b)\); matching masses may be split and left unmatched.
For every positive integer \(N\) with \(N\alpha_S,N\alpha_B\in\mathbb{Z}\), draw \(N\alpha_S\) sellers and \(N\alpha_B\) buyers independently from \(F\), then add one independent buyer from \(F\).
Define BTR on a realized market by sorting buyers \(b_1\geq\cdots\geq b_{n_B}\) and sellers \(s_1\leq\cdots\leq s_{n_S}\), breaking ties in favor of buyers and then by fixed IDs. Let \(q=\max(\{0\}\cup\{j\leq\min(n_S,n_B):b_j\geq s_j\})\), and set \(b_{n_B+1}=-\infty\).
If \(q=0\), no trade occurs. Otherwise BTR trades pairs \((s_i,b_i)\) for \(i\leq q\) when \(b_{q+1}\geq s_q\), and for \(i\leq q-1\) otherwise. Its realized gains from trade are the sum of \(b_i-s_i\) over these pairs. Let \(A_N\) be their expectation divided by \(N\).

Does \(A_N\) converge to \(G^*\) for every such input, or can this convergence fail, and what is the limiting gap \(\Delta=\lim_{N\to\infty}(A_N-G^*)\) along admissible scales?
When this limit exists, is its exact computation polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is deciding \(\Delta\geq c\) for a binary rational threshold \(c\) NP-hard?

For finite markets with all values i.i.d., BTR with one extra buyer has expected gains from trade at least the expected optimum before augmentation. [Verified finite-market result](https://yannai.gonch.name/scientific/papers/2019-bk-gft-arxiv-v2.pdf).
No result addressing precisely this finite-support fluid limit and its limiting gap was identified in the literature search.

Result

Convergence always holds. For every input in the question,
\[ \lim_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N\alpha_S,N\alpha_B\in\mathbb Z}}A_N=G^*, \qquad \Delta=0. \]
Consequently, exact computation of the gap and deciding \(\Delta\geq c\) are polynomial-time solvable.

The proof below also gives an explicit convergence bound. Write \(D=\max V-\min V\).

1. BTR loses at most one pair’s gains

For nonnegative capacity vectors \(a=(a_v)_{v\in V}\) and \(d=(d_v)_{v\in V}\), define
\[ H(a,d)= \max_{x\geq0} \left\{ \sum_{s,b\in V}(b-s)x_{s,b}: \sum_bx_{s,b}\leq a_s,\ \sum_sx_{s,b}\leq d_b \right\}. \]
Thus \(G^*=H(\mu_S,\mu_B)\). Scaling feasible allocations in both directions shows that
\[ H(ta,td)=tH(a,d)\qquad(t>0). \]

Consider any finite realized market with both sides nonempty, and sort its values as in the question. Its fractional optimum equals
\[ O=\sum_{i=1}^{q}(b_i-s_i). \]

Here is a proof that includes ties and unequal market sizes. Set \(s_{n_S+1}=+\infty\) and retain \(b_{n_B+1}=-\infty\). If \(q>0\), choose a finite price \(p\) satisfying
\[ \max\{s_q,b_{q+1}\}\leq p\leq\min\{b_q,s_{q+1}\}. \]
This interval is nonempty: \(s_q\leq b_q\); the same-side orderings give the other inequalities, except possibly \(b_{q+1}\leq s_{q+1}\), which follows from maximality of \(q\) when both entries exist.

Write \(z_+=\max\{z,0\}\). Every feasible fractional allocation satisfies
\[ \sum_{s,b}(b-s)x_{s,b} \leq \sum_s(p-s)_+a_s+\sum_b(b-p)_+d_b, \]
because \(b-s\leq(p-s)_++(b-p)_+\), followed by the capacity constraints. For the realized integer capacities, the right-hand side equals
\[ \sum_{i=1}^{q}(p-s_i)+\sum_{i=1}^{q}(b_i-p) =\sum_{i=1}^{q}(b_i-s_i). \]
The sorted matching of these pairs attains that bound. If \(q=0\), then \(b_1<s_1\); choosing \(p\) between them gives an upper bound of zero, attained by making no trades.

By definition, BTR either realizes \(O\) or deletes the last of these pairs. All included gains are nonnegative and at most \(D\). Therefore its realized gains \(T\) satisfy
\[ 0\leq O-T\leq D. \]

Now let \(O_N\) be the optimum before adding the extra buyer, \(O_N^+\) the optimum afterward, and \(T_N\) the augmented BTR gains. Adding the buyer cannot decrease the optimum. Conversely, removing that buyer’s pair from an optimal augmented matching loses at most \(D\), so
\[ 0\leq O_N^+-O_N\leq D. \]
Together with the BTR bound, this gives the pathwise estimate
\[ O_N-D\leq T_N\leq O_N+D. \]
In particular,
\[ \left|A_N-\frac{\mathbb E O_N}{N}\right|\leq\frac DN. \]

2. The fluid optimum is stable under capacity errors

For any two pairs of capacity vectors,
\[ |H(a,d)-H(a',d')| \leq D\bigl(\|a-a'\|_1+\|d-d'\|_1\bigr). \]

To prove this, take an optimal allocation for \(H(a,d)\), removing any negative-gain edges first. Such removal preserves feasibility and cannot decrease its objective.

For each seller row whose allocated mass exceeds \(a'_s\), delete precisely its excess. The total mass deleted in this step is at most \(\sum_s(a_s-a'_s)_+\). Next delete excess mass from each buyer column relative to \(d'_b\). Since row deletions only decrease column totals, this second deletion removes at most \(\sum_b(d_b-d'_b)_+\).

The remaining allocation is feasible for \(H(a',d')\). Every deleted unit carried gains between zero and \(D\). Hence
\[ H(a,d)-H(a',d') \leq D\left(\sum_s(a_s-a'_s)_++\sum_b(d_b-d'_b)_+\right). \]
Interchanging the two capacity pairs proves the stated bound.

3. Quantitative convergence to the fluid optimum

Let \(C_{S,N}(v)\) and \(C_{B,N}(v)\) count the original sellers and buyers of value \(v\). Define
\[ a_N(v)=\frac{C_{S,N}(v)}N,\qquad d_N(v)=\frac{C_{B,N}(v)}N. \]
The finite-market optimality result and homogeneity give
\[ \frac{O_N}{N}=H(a_N,d_N). \]
Their means are exactly the baseline capacities:
\[ \mathbb E a_N(v)=\alpha_SF(v)=\mu_S(v),\qquad \mathbb E d_N(v)=\alpha_BF(v)=\mu_B(v). \]

Each seller count has binomial variance \(N\alpha_SF(v)(1-F(v))\). Therefore, by Cauchy–Schwarz,
\[ \mathbb E\|a_N-\mu_S\|_1 \leq \sqrt{\frac{\alpha_S}{N}} \sum_{v\in V}\sqrt{F(v)(1-F(v))}. \]
Another application of Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[ \left(\sum_v\sqrt{F(v)(1-F(v))}\right)^2 \leq m\left(1-\sum_vF(v)^2\right) \leq m-1, \]
where the last inequality uses \(\sum_vF(v)^2\geq1/m\). Consequently,
\[ \mathbb E\|a_N-\mu_S\|_1 \leq\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_S(m-1)}N}. \]
The identical argument yields
\[ \mathbb E\|d_N-\mu_B\|_1 \leq\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_B(m-1)}N}. \]

Applying the stability bound proves
\[ \left|\frac{\mathbb E O_N}{N}-G^*\right| \leq D(\sqrt{\alpha_S}+\sqrt{\alpha_B}) \sqrt{\frac{m-1}{N}}. \]

We also have \(\mathbb E O_N/N\leq G^*\). Indeed, for each realization choose an optimal allocation for capacities \((a_N,d_N)\). Its expected allocation satisfies the baseline capacity constraints, and its objective is \(\mathbb E O_N/N\). The expectation is well-defined because there are finitely many count configurations for each \(N\).

Combining these facts with the deterministic BTR comparison gives
\[ -D\left( (\sqrt{\alpha_S}+\sqrt{\alpha_B})\sqrt{\frac{m-1}{N}} +\frac1N \right) \leq A_N-G^* \leq\frac DN. \]
Both bounds tend to zero. The admissible scales form an unbounded sequence: every multiple of the least common multiple of the reduced denominators of \(\alpha_S,\alpha_B\) is admissible. Thus the asserted limit exists along all admissible scales and equals zero.

4. Exact computation and threshold complexity

For every promised-valid input, the exact answer is the rational number \(\Delta=0\). Thus
\[ \Delta\geq c\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c\leq0. \]

Let \(L_c\) denote the binary length of the threshold when it is not already included in \(L\). Including input reading, exact gap computation takes \(O(L+m+\tau)\) bit time, and threshold decision takes \(O(L+L_c+m+\tau)\) bit time. After parsing, computing the gap requires only outputting zero. There is no dependence on \(N\), and here each side has \(\tau=m\).

The threshold problem therefore belongs to polynomial time; NP-hardness under polynomial-time many-one reductions would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
