For every society distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal P)\), every true ranking \(t\in\mathcal P\), every report \(r\in\mathcal P\), and every utility vector \(u\) consistent with \(t\) and satisfying \(\mathrm{URBI}(\rho)\), require \(u\cdot(a_{\mu,\beta}(t)-a_{\mu,\beta}(r))\ge 0\), where \(a_{\mu,\beta}=(1-\beta)a^{\mathrm{RSD}}_\mu+\beta a^{\mathrm{ABM}}_\mu\) is the tagged infinitesimal agent’s assignment lottery against background \(\mu\). Find the largest \(\beta\in[0,1]\) satisfying all these constraints.

Partial results: a broad zero-mixing criterion and constant support compression

The general $\mathrm P$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness question remains unresolved here. I prove two new results:

  • If four positive capacities sum to less than one, then $\beta_{\max}(q,\rho)=0$ for every $\rho>0$. A counterexample uses three ranking types and has encoding length polynomial in $m,L$.
  • If every four positive capacities sum to at least one, then every supplied rational society has an equivalent society with at most $57$ ranking types, preserving every tagged-agent lottery under both mechanisms.

For the support-compression theorem, I interpret binary rationals as arbitrary rationals whose numerators and denominators are encoded in binary, as in the verified interpretation in NOTES. The zero-mixing criterion also holds if society masses must be dyadic.

1. Assignment, strategyproofness, and boundary cases

Complete assignment

Under RSD, the background mass processed before priority coordinate $\xi<1$ is exactly $\xi$. Consequently, total remaining capacity is at least $1-\xi>0$. Every tagged agent with coordinate below one therefore receives an object.

Under ABM, let $U$ be the remaining unassigned background mass. Conservation of capacity gives total remaining capacity at least $U$. Every round leaving positive unassigned mass must contain an insufficient-capacity object. Such an object exhausts and rejects positive background mass. Thus every nonterminal round removes at least one positive-capacity object, and the process terminates after at most $m$ rounds.

In its terminal round, every available object has sufficient capacity for its applicants; otherwise positive background mass would be rejected. By the specified convention, all its cutoffs are one. Any tagged agent still unassigned is therefore accepted in that round.

Hence all tagged-agent lotteries have total probability one.

RSD is strategyproof

Fix the background society and a tagged priority coordinate. Background availability does not depend on the tagged agent’s report. Truthful RSD chooses the highest-utility available object. Any other report chooses an object with weakly lower true utility. Integrating over the coordinate proves
$$ u\cdot\left(a^{\mathrm{RSD}}_\mu(t)-a^{\mathrm{RSD}}_\mu(r)\right)\ge0. $$

Each hybrid incentive constraint is a closed affine inequality in $\beta$, satisfied at zero. Its feasible set within $[0,1]$ is therefore a closed interval containing zero. Their intersection is likewise a nonempty closed interval. Consequently, $\beta_{\max}$ exists, and testing $\beta_{\max}\ge b$ is equivalent to testing universal feasibility at $\beta=b$.

In particular, $b=0$ always gives an affirmative answer.

Degenerate cases

If $m\ge3$ and $\rho=0$, no strictly consistent utility satisfies URBI. Indeed, for a true ranking $(j_1,\ldots,j_m)$, strict consistency gives
$$ u_{j_2}-u_{j_m}>0, $$
whereas URBI applied to $j_1\succ j_2$ requires this quantity to be at most zero.

With at most two positive-capacity objects, ABM is strategyproof for the tagged agent. To see this, let $j$ be its preferred positive-capacity object, and let $P_j$ be its first-round background demand. If $q_j\ge P_j$, applying to $j$ first obtains it surely. If $q_j<P_j$, it exhausts in round one; applying elsewhere first can never obtain $j$ later. Reports placing $j$ first have the same lottery. Truth therefore maximizes the probability of the preferred object.

Together with RSD strategyproofness, this proves
$$ \beta_{\max}(q,\rho)=1 $$
whenever at most two capacities are positive. It also completes the remaining cases with $\rho=0$, so $\beta_{\max}(q,0)=1$ for every instance.

The results below concern $\rho>0$.

2. Four capacities below one force zero mixing

Theorem

Suppose there are four distinct positive-capacity objects $A,B,C,D$ satisfying
$$ q_A+q_B+q_C+q_D<1. $$
Then
$$ \boxed{\beta_{\max}(q,\rho)=0\qquad\text{for every }\rho>0.} $$

A witness society has three ranking types. Its encoding length is
$$ L_\mu=O(m\log m+L). $$
The witness can be constructed in time polynomial in $m,L$, including when masses must be dyadic.

Construction

Write
$$ a=q_A,\qquad p=q_B,\qquad c=q_C,\qquad d=q_D, $$
and set
$$ S=a+p+c+d,\qquad \delta=1-S>0. $$

Let $N=M\setminus\{A,B,C,D\}$, and fix an ordering $\sigma$ of all objects in $N$. Its total capacity satisfies
$$ Q=\sum_{j\in N}q_j\ge1-S=\delta>0. $$
In particular, $N$ contains a positive-capacity object.

Define
$$ h=\frac{1+S}{2},\qquad k=\frac{1+h}{2},\qquad x=\frac{a}{h},\qquad H=1-x, $$
and then
$$ v=\frac{d}{H},\qquad z=\frac{c}{k-v},\qquad y=H-z. $$

These are well-defined positive masses. First, $h>S>a+d$ and $h<1$, so $0<x<1$ and
$$ H(h-v)=h-a-d>0. $$
Thus $v<h<k<1$, giving $z>0$. Moreover,
$$ \begin{aligned} y(h-v) &=H(h-v)-z(h-v)\\ &=h-a-d-c\frac{h-v}{k-v}\\ &>h-a-d-c\\ &=h-S+p\\ &>p. \end{aligned} $$
It follows that $y>0$. By construction,
$$ x+y+z=1. $$
Define
$$ e=v+\frac{p}{y}. $$
The preceding inequality gives
$$ v<e<h<k<1. $$

Use the following three ranking types:

| Mass | Ranking |
|---|---|
| $x$ | $A\succ B\succ C\succ\sigma\succ D$ |
| $y$ | $D\succ B\succ\sigma\succ C\succ A$ |
| $z$ | $D\succ C\succ\sigma\succ B\succ A$ |

Here placing $\sigma$ in a ranking means inserting its entire ordered list.

The tagged agent’s true ranking and report are
$$ t:A\succ B\succ C\succ\sigma\succ D, $$
$$ r:A\succ C\succ B\succ\sigma\succ D. $$

RSD gives identical tagged lotteries

Initially, $A$ receives rate $x$, while $D$ receives rate $H=y+z$. Their candidate exhaustion coordinates are $h$ and $v$, respectively. Since $v<h$, object $D$ exhausts first.

Afterward, the second type consumes $B$ at rate $y$, and the third type consumes $C$ at rate $z$. Their candidate exhaustion coordinates are
$$ v+\frac{p}{y}=e<h, \qquad v+\frac{c}{z}=k>h. $$

After $B$ exhausts at $e$, the second type consumes objects in $\sigma$. We must verify that this family cannot disappear before $A$ exhausts. Its total consumption through coordinate $h$ is
$$ \begin{aligned} y(h-e) &=y(h-v)-p\\ &=Hh-d-z(h-v)-p\\ &=h-a-d-p-c+z(k-h)\\ &=h-S+z(k-h). \end{aligned} $$
Because
$$ h-S=\frac{\delta}{2}, \qquad k-h=\frac{\delta}{4}, \qquad z<1, $$
we obtain
$$ y(h-e)<\frac{3\delta}{4}<\delta\le Q. $$

Thus at least one positive-capacity object in $\sigma$ remains available throughout this interval. Exhaustions within $\sigma$ do not change the second type’s consumption of that family. They therefore do not change the rates of $A$ or $C$.

This verifies the proposed schedule through $h$: $D$ exhausts at $v$, $B$ at $e$, and $A$ at $h$, while $C$ remains available at $h$.

Both tagged reports select $A$ before $h$. After $h$, objects $A,B,D$ are exhausted, so the two reports induce the same effective order, namely $C$ followed by $\sigma$. Therefore
$$ a^{\mathrm{RSD}}_\mu(t)=a^{\mathrm{RSD}}_\mu(r). $$

ABM strictly rewards the misreport

In round one, only the first type applies to $A$, and the other two types apply to $D$. Their cutoffs are
$$ \frac{a}{x}=h, \qquad \frac{d}{y+z}=v. $$
Both objects exhaust.

In round two:

  • The first type’s remaining tail, with coordinates above $h$, applies to $B$.
  • The second type’s remaining tail, with coordinates above $v$, also applies to $B$.
  • The third type’s remaining tail applies to $C$.

Object $B$ has cutoff $e<h$, because below $h$ only the second type contributes applicants, and
$$ y(e-v)=p. $$
Object $C$ has cutoff $k$, since
$$ z(k-v)=c. $$
Both exhaust in round two.

The tagged agent receives $A$ for coordinates at most $h$ under either report. For coordinates above $h$:

  • Under truth, it applies to $B$ and is rejected because $e<h$.
  • Under the misreport, it applies to $C$ and is accepted precisely through coordinate $k$.

After round two, all four special objects are exhausted. The remaining background mass is $\delta$, and the objects in $\sigma$ have total capacity at least $\delta$. Both reports then follow the same order $\sigma$.

Consequently, for every coordinate $\xi\in(h,k]$, the misreport obtains $C$, while truth obtains an object $J(\xi)\in N$. For coordinates outside this interval, their assignments coincide.

For every utility strictly consistent with $t$,
$$ u_C>u_{J(\xi)}. $$
Thus
$$ u\cdot\left(a^{\mathrm{ABM}}_\mu(t)-a^{\mathrm{ABM}}_\mu(r)\right) = -\int_h^k\left(u_C-u_{J(\xi)}\right)\,d\xi <0. $$

There exists such a utility satisfying URBI for every $\rho>0$. Namely, put
$$ \gamma=\min\{\rho,1/2\}, $$
and, writing $t=(j_1,\ldots,j_m)$, choose
$$ u_{j_i}=\gamma^{i-1}\quad(i<m), \qquad u_{j_m}=0. $$
This vector is strictly consistent. Its minimum is zero, and for $i<\ell<m$,
$$ u_{j_\ell} =\gamma^{\ell-i}u_{j_i} \le\rho u_{j_i}. $$
Comparisons with the last-ranked object also satisfy URBI.

Since the RSD lotteries coincide, every $\beta>0$ violates the hybrid incentive constraint for this utility. Since $\beta=0$ is feasible, the theorem follows.

Encoding length and dyadic masses

All displayed masses are obtained through a constant number of rational operations on four capacities. Their binary encoding lengths are therefore $O(L)$. The three rankings require $O(m\log m)$ bits.

To cover dyadic-mass societies, observe that the proof only needs positive normalized masses and the strict conditions
$$ v<e<h<k<1, \qquad y(h-e)<\delta, $$
where, for general masses,
$$ h=\frac{a}{x},\qquad v=\frac{d}{y+z},\qquad e=v+\frac{p}{y},\qquad k=v+\frac{c}{z}. $$

These conditions hold at the constructed point and persist in a neighborhood of it. More quantitatively, substitute $z=1-x-y$ and clear the positive denominators. This gives finitely many strict polynomial inequalities of bounded degree, with coefficient encoding length $O(L)$. Their positive values at the constructed rational point are at least $2^{-O(L)}$, because those values also have encoding length $O(L)$.

On the unit square, the polynomials have Lipschitz bounds at most $2^{O(L)}$. Approximating $x,y$ on a sufficiently fine dyadic grid with $O(L)$ denominator bits, and setting $z=1-x-y$, therefore preserves every strict inequality and positivity. The same proof applies to these dyadic masses.

Decision consequence

Let $S_4$ be the sum of the four smallest positive capacities, when at least four exist. If
$$ \rho>0,\qquad S_4<1, $$
then the target decision has the exact answer
$$ \beta_{\max}(q,\rho)\ge b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b=0. $$

Selecting those capacities and performing the comparison takes time polynomial in $m,L$.

3. The remaining regime has at most three nonterminal exhaustions

Assume henceforth that
$$ \sum_{j\in F}q_j\ge1 $$
for every set $F$ of four positive-capacity objects. This condition is understood vacuously when fewer than four capacities are positive.

Under either mechanism, exhausting an object means assigning background mass equal to its entire capacity.

For RSD, four objects cannot exhaust before coordinate one: that would require assigned mass at least one, while the assigned mass at coordinate $\xi<1$ is only $\xi$. Thus there are at most three exhausted objects before coordinate one, and at most four positive-length priority intervals.

For ABM, four objects cannot have exhausted after a round that leaves positive unassigned background mass. Their capacities would already account for at least the whole society. Since every nonterminal round exhausts an object, ABM has at most three nonterminal rounds and at most four rounds in total.

Finally, under either mechanism, at most four positive-capacity objects can exhaust even when terminal exhaustions are included. Indeed, any five such objects have total capacity at least
$$ 1+q_{\min}>1, $$
where $q_{\min}$ is the smallest positive capacity. Their exhaustion would require assigning more than total mass one.

These conclusions hold for every society; they do not depend on its support size.

4. Constant support compression in the remaining regime

Theorem

Under the capacity condition of Section 3, every supplied rational society $\mu$ has a rational society $\widehat\mu$, supported on at most $57$ of its ranking types, such that
$$ a^K_{\widehat\mu}(s)=a^K_\mu(s) $$
for every report $s$ and both mechanisms $K$.

The compressed society can be computed in time
$$ (1+\tau)^{57}(1+m)^8 \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L+L_\mu). $$
Its masses have encoding length polynomial in $m,L+L_\mu$.

This is a bound for a supplied society. It does not bound a counterexample’s encoding length using only the outer input $m,L$.

Exact schedules and their computation

First compute the original schedules.

For RSD, between exhaustion events, each supported type chooses a fixed available object. Sum its masses to obtain each object’s rate, and advance to the smallest positive ratio of remaining capacity to rate, stopping at coordinate one. Remove all simultaneous exhaustions.

For ABM, each supported type’s unassigned coordinates form a tail $(h_s,1]$. At cutoff $c$, its accepted mass is
$$ \mu(s)\max\{c-h_s,0\}, $$
and its new tail endpoint is $\max\{h_s,c\}$.

For applicants $A$ to an insufficient-capacity object with remaining capacity $Q$, the cutoff solves
$$ \sum_{s\in A}\mu(s)\max\{c-h_s,0\}=Q. $$
Here $0<Q<\sum_{s\in A}\mu(s)(1-h_s)$. The applicant-mass function is continuous and strictly increasing once it becomes positive, so the solution is unique. Sorting the endpoints gives its piecewise-linear formula and permits exact rational computation.

Both computations take time polynomial in $m,\tau,L+L_\mu$. To check bit complexity, let $B=L+L_\mu$ and choose a common denominator $D$ for the supplied capacities and masses, with $\log D=O(B)$. Write $\mu(s)=n_s/D$, so $\sum_s n_s=D$.

After fixing the appropriate linear piece, every binding cutoff or RSD event coordinate satisfies an equation of the form
$$ P c=K+\sum_\ell N_\ell c_\ell, $$
where the coefficients are integers and $P$ is a positive subset sum of the $n_s$, hence $P\le D$. There are at most $m$ new exhaustion coordinates under either mechanism. Inductively, their denominators divide a product of at most $m$ such integers. Their bit lengths, and those of all derived coefficients below, are therefore polynomial in $m,B$.

Preserved equalities

Fix the original schedules.

Let $X_R$ be the positive-capacity objects exhausted under RSD, including exhaustions at coordinate one. Let $X_B$ be those exhausted under ABM, including terminal-round exhaustions. Section 3 proves
$$ |X_R|\le4,\qquad |X_B|\le4. $$
Let $E\le4$ be the number of positive-length RSD intervals and $R\le4$ the number of ABM rounds.

For each original supported type, record:

  • Its RSD choice indicator for every interval and every object in $X_R$.
  • Its ABM applicant-tail length and accepted-tail length for every round and every object in $X_B$.

For unavailable objects, record zero. For ABM, compute the type’s hypothetical tail endpoints using the fixed original schedule.

Preserving the weighted sums of these features preserves:

  • All RSD consumption rates for objects in $X_R$.
  • Every ABM demand and accepted mass for objects in $X_B$.

Together with normalization, the number of equality rows is at most
$$ |X_R|E+2|X_B|R+1 \le16+32+1 =49. $$

Capacity inequalities for the other objects

Let $c^K_{s,j}$ be the total consumption of object $j$ per unit mass of type $s$, when that type follows the fixed original schedule of mechanism $K$.

Every original supported type is completely assigned under each fixed schedule. Hence
$$ c^K_{s,j}\ge0, \qquad \sum_j c^K_{s,j}=1. $$

For an object $j\notin X_K$ with positive capacity, its original final consumption
$$ C^K_j=\sum_s\mu(s)c^K_{s,j} $$
satisfies $C^K_j<q_j$.

Choose a positive rational $\varepsilon$ satisfying
$$ \varepsilon\le\frac{q_{\min}}{10} $$
and
$$ \varepsilon\le\frac12(q_j-C^K_j) $$
for every such mechanism-object pair. If there are no such pairs, only the first bound is needed.

Consider the polytope of alternative masses $\alpha_s$ on the original support, defined by:

  • $\alpha_s\ge0$;
  • the at most $49$ preserved equalities;
  • for each positive-capacity $j\notin X_K$,

$$ \sum_s\alpha_s c^K_{s,j}\le q_j-\varepsilon. $$

The original masses satisfy all these constraints, so the polytope is nonempty. Normalization makes it bounded.

At most eight capacity inequalities can be active

At any feasible point,
$$ \sum_j\sum_s\alpha_s c^K_{s,j}=1. $$

Suppose five capacity inequalities for distinct objects were active under one mechanism. The assigned mass to those objects would equal
$$ \sum_{j\in F}(q_j-\varepsilon). $$
The capacity condition implies
$$ \sum_{j\in F}q_j\ge1+q_{\min}. $$
Therefore their assigned mass would be at least
$$ 1+q_{\min}-5\varepsilon \ge1+\frac{q_{\min}}2 >1, $$
a contradiction.

Thus at most four capacity inequalities are active for RSD, and at most four for ABM: at most eight altogether.

A vertex has support at most $57$

A nonempty bounded polytope has a vertex. For completeness, successively minimizing its coordinates produces nested nonempty compact faces; after all coordinates have been fixed, the resulting face is a singleton, hence a vertex.

Take a vertex $\alpha$, and let its positive support have size $\kappa$. If
$$ \kappa>49+8, $$
there is a nonzero perturbation supported on those $\kappa$ coordinates that preserves every equality and every active capacity inequality: the corresponding homogeneous system has fewer rows than columns.

Both signs of a sufficiently small perturbation preserve positivity on the support and preserve all inactive inequalities, which have positive slack. This expresses $\alpha$ as the midpoint of two distinct feasible points, contradicting that it is a vertex. Hence
$$ \kappa\le57. $$

The vertex is rational. Otherwise stated, the active rows restricted to its support must have rank $\kappa$, by the same perturbation argument. Selecting $\kappa$ independent rational rows expresses its masses as the unique solution of a rational square linear system.

The schedules are preserved

For RSD, the rates of every exhausted object are preserved interval by interval. Their cumulative consumptions and exhaustion coordinates therefore remain unchanged. Every other object has fixed-schedule final consumption at most $q_j-\varepsilon$; its cumulative consumption at every earlier coordinate is no larger. It cannot introduce an additional exhaustion. Induction over the original intervals gives the same availability schedule.

For ABM, induct over rounds. For objects in $X_B$, preserved previous accepted masses give the same remaining capacities, and preserved demands determine the same sufficient or insufficient case. In an insufficient case, the preserved accepted mass shows that the original cutoff solves the capacity equation; uniqueness gives the same cutoff. In a sufficient case, the cutoff remains one.

For an object outside $X_B$, its cumulative accepted mass plus its current demand is at most its fixed-schedule final consumption, which is strictly below $q_j$. Thus it continues to accept all applicants and remains available.

Every original nonterminal round has an insufficient-capacity object with demand strictly exceeding acceptance. Both quantities are preserved, so the new society cannot terminate prematurely. The original terminal round still accepts every remaining tail.

The same schedules therefore hold under $\alpha$. Tagged lotteries depend only on the availability schedules, cutoffs, and report, so all tagged lotteries are preserved.

Constructing the compressed society

The feature matrices and $\varepsilon$ can be constructed in time polynomial in $m,\tau,B$.

To find the sparse feasible point, enumerate:

  • Supports of size at most $57$.
  • Sets of at most eight active capacity rows.
  • Square subsystems of the selected equality and active rows.

Solve each nonsingular subsystem exactly, set other masses to zero, and check all constraints. The vertex proved above appears in this enumeration. The resulting running time is bounded by
$$ (1+\tau)^{57}(1+m)^8 \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,B). $$

Every solved system has size at most $57$, and its coefficients have polynomial bit length. Determinant bounds therefore give mass encoding lengths polynomial in $m,B$.

5. What this settles and what remains open

The first theorem settles a substantial part of the outer input space: whenever four positive capacities sum to less than one and $\rho>0$, the answer is negative for every $b>0$. This strengthens the isolated five-object example in NOTES to a general capacity criterion.

All remaining positive-$\rho$ instances satisfy the capacity condition in Section 3. In that regime, both processes have at most four stages, and every rational counterexample has a schedule-equivalent witness supported on at most $57$ types.

These facts still do not establish a polynomial outer algorithm. The compression theorem’s coefficients depend on the supplied society’s event coordinates and cutoffs. Its mass-encoding bound depends on $L_\mu$, rather than only on $m,L$. Moreover, naïvely enumerating supports among all $m!$ rankings is not polynomial in $m$.

Thus neither the supplied-society algorithm nor the [finite-setting enumeration result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.2558) completes the universal continuum quantification. A general polynomial-time procedure or a valid hardness reduction remains missing.
