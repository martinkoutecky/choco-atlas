Theorem 1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) resources and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) complete workload and entitlement types, with per-capita demands \(a_{tj}\ge 0\), entitlement intensities \(w_t\ge 0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t w_t=1\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t a_{tj}\ge 1\) for every resource \(j\), find service rates \(x_t\in[0,1]\) such that \(\sum_t\mu_t x_t a_{tj}\le 1\) for every \(j\), and for each type \(t\), either \(x_t=1\) or there is a saturated resource \(j\) with \(\sum_s\mu_s x_s a_{sj}=1\) and \(x_t a_{tj}\ge w_t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) divisible resources, each of capacity \(1\). Type \(t\) has rational per-capita demands \(a_{tj}\ge0\) and entitlement intensity \(w_t\ge0\); assume \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), \(\sum_t\mu_t w_t=1\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t a_{tj}\ge1\) for every resource \(j\).
A single service rate \(x_t\in[0,1]\) applies to the entire mass of type \(t\) and scales all its demands proportionally. A valid allocation satisfies \(\sum_t\mu_t x_t a_{tj}\le1\) for every \(j\); every type \(t\) must either have \(x_t=1\) or have some resource \(j\) satisfying both \(\sum_s\mu_s x_s a_{sj}=1\) and \(x_t a_{tj}\ge w_t\).
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; the computational problem is to find an exact rational valid allocation.

Is finding such an allocation polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite lists of users, existence is guaranteed, with a numerical construction using differential equations. [Dolev et al.](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/info/people/halpern/papers/complaints.pdf)
Gutman and Nisan subsequently claim a polynomial-time algorithm for bottleneck-based fair allocation with Leontief or satiable Leontief utilities. [Fair Allocation Without Trade](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2012/papers/4D_5.pdf)
The finite-support mass formulation is a reparametrization of that capped proportional-demand problem, so the requested target cannot be presented as an established open question.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the distribution normalization explicit: positive support masses sum to one.
  • Specified binary rational demands and entitlement intensities, and defined L as total binary input length.
  • Interpreted the requested allocation as exact rational service rates, with one common rate per complete type.
  • Retained unrestricted nonnegative per-capita demands: the finite-user upper bound on fractions of capacity does not impose an upper bound of one on per-capita demand intensities.

Polynomial-time exact allocation

Finding an exact rational valid allocation is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\).

The proof combines a capped logarithmic optimization problem with a final rational linear program. The latter step is necessary: an approximate convex optimum alone does not establish the requested exact-output guarantee.

The multiplier argument is closely related to Gutman and Nisan’s market proof of bottleneck-based fairness; their Section 5 also treats satiation. Below, the exact rational computation is proved explicitly. [Gutman and Nisan, *Fair Allocation Without Trade*](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2012/papers/4D_5.pdf)

1. Reparametrization

For each supported type, define

\(\displaystyle d_{tj}=\mu_ta_{tj},\qquad e_t=\mu_tw_t,\qquad C_j(x)=\sum_t d_{tj}x_t.\)

Since every supported mass is positive, the requested fairness condition is equivalent to:

  • \(x_t=1\); or
  • some resource \(j\) satisfies \(C_j(x)=1\) and \(d_{tj}x_t\ge e_t\).

The numbers \(d_{tj},e_t\) are nonnegative rationals of polynomial total encoding length, and \(\sum_t e_t=1\). In particular, \(0\le e_t\le1\).

Set

\(\displaystyle D=\max\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{d_{tj}:t,j\}\bigr).\)

All subsequent constructions use one variable per supported type.

2. A logarithmic program supplies bottleneck witnesses

First take arbitrary rational numbers \(b_t>0\) satisfying \(\sum_t b_t=1\). Consider

\(\displaystyle \max F_b(x)=\sum_t b_t\log x_t,\qquad 0<x_t\le1,\qquad C_j(x)\le1\quad\text{for every }j.\)

A strictly feasible point exists: take every coordinate equal to \(1/(2\tau D)\). The objective tends to negative infinity if any coordinate tends to zero. Consequently, it attains its maximum at a positive vector \(x^*\). Strict concavity makes this maximizer unique.

Slater’s condition holds, so the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions give multipliers \(p_j,q_t\ge0\) with

\(\displaystyle \frac{b_t}{x_t^*}=\sum_jp_jd_{tj}+q_t,\)

\(\displaystyle p_j\bigl(1-C_j(x^*)\bigr)=0,\qquad q_t(1-x_t^*)=0.\)

Multiplying the stationarity equations by \(x_t^*\), summing over types, and using complementary slackness gives

\(\displaystyle 1=\sum_t b_t=\sum_jp_jC_j(x^*)+\sum_tq_tx_t^*=\sum_jp_j+\sum_tq_t.\)

Thus \(\sum_jp_j\le1\).

Suppose \(x_t^*<1\). Then \(q_t=0\), and

\(\displaystyle b_t=\sum_jp_jd_{tj}x_t^*.\)

Because \(b_t>0\), the sum \(P=\sum_jp_j\) is positive. Therefore some \(j\) with \(p_j>0\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle d_{tj}x_t^*\ge\frac{b_t}{P}\ge b_t.\)

Complementary slackness gives \(C_j(x^*)=1\). Hence \(x^*\) satisfies the required bottleneck condition with entitlements \(b_t\).

We also obtain a quantitative coordinate bound. If \(x_t^*<1\), then

\(\displaystyle b_t=x_t^*\sum_jp_jd_{tj}\le Dx_t^*.\)

If \(x_t^*=1\), the same bound \(x_t^*\ge b_t/D\) holds because \(b_t\le1\le D\). Writing \(b_{\min}=\min_t b_t\), we have proved

\(\displaystyle x_t^*\ge\frac{b_{\min}}D\quad\text{for every }t.\)

3. A uniform rational gap for selected witnesses

Choose a set \(S\) of types to receive full service, and choose one resource \(j(t)\) for each \(t\notin S\). These choices specify a linear system.

To measure its feasibility, minimize \(z\) subject to

\(\displaystyle 0\le y_t\le1,\qquad C_j(y)\le1,\qquad 0\le z\le1,\)

\(\displaystyle y_t\ge1-z\quad(t\in S),\)

\(\displaystyle C_{j(t)}(y)\ge1-z,\qquad d_{t,j(t)}y_t\ge e_t-z\quad(t\notin S).\)

Call this linear program the *witness LP*. It is always feasible: \(y=0,z=1\) satisfies every constraint. Its feasible region is compact.

We now give a positive lower bound on every nonzero optimal value, uniformly over all witness choices.

Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of all \(d_{tj}\) and \(e_t\), written in reduced form. Define the integer

\(\displaystyle H=\max\bigl(\{Q\}\cup\{Qd_{tj}:t,j\}\cup\{Qe_t:t\}\bigr),\)

and put

\(\displaystyle n=\tau+1,\qquad T=(nH)^n,\qquad g=T^{-1}.\)

Multiplying every constraint by \(Q\) produces integer coefficients and right-hand sides of absolute value at most \(H\).

A linear optimum is attained at a vertex. At any vertex, \(n\) linearly independent active constraints determine the \(n\) coordinates \((y,z)\). Their coefficient matrix \(B\) satisfies, by Hadamard’s inequality,

\(\displaystyle |\det B|\le n^{n/2}H^n\le T.\)

Cramer’s rule expresses the coordinate \(z\) as an integer divided by \(\det B\). Thus, if the optimal value is positive, it is at least \(g\).

We have proved the following implication:

If a witness LP has a feasible point with \(z<g\), its exact optimal value is zero.

The encoding lengths of \(Q,H,T,g\) are polynomial in the original input length and \(\tau\). Indeed, the bit length of a product is bounded by the sum of the factor bit lengths, and \(\log T=n(\log n+\log H)\).

4. Computing a sufficiently close rational approximation

Choose

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=\frac g4,\qquad \eta=\frac{\varepsilon}{4\tau},\qquad b_t=\frac{e_t+\eta}{1+\tau\eta},\qquad \delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{4\tau D}.\)

Every \(b_t\) is positive, including when \(e_t=0\), and \(\sum_t b_t=1\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle |b_t-e_t|=\frac{\eta|1-\tau e_t|}{1+\tau\eta}\le\tau\eta=\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.\)

Let \(x^*\) maximize the logarithmic program for these \(b_t\). We will compute a rational, exactly feasible \(\widehat x\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \|\widehat x-x^*\|_\infty\le\delta.\)

A bounded domain for numerical optimization

Set

\(\displaystyle \ell=\frac{b_{\min}}{2D},\qquad K=\{x:\ell\le x_t\le1,\ C_j(x)\le1\text{ for every }j\}.\)

Section 2 shows that \(x_t^*\ge2\ell\), so restriction to \(K\) preserves the optimum.

The polytope \(K\) has a known interior ball. Let every coordinate of \(c\) equal \(3\ell/2\), and set \(r=\ell/(4\tau D)\). Since \(\tau b_{\min}\le1\),

\(\displaystyle C_j(c)\le\frac{3\tau b_{\min}}4\le\frac34,\qquad \ell<c_t\le\frac34.\)

A Euclidean perturbation of norm at most \(r\) changes any coordinate by at most \(r\le\ell/4\), and changes a resource load by at most

\(\displaystyle \sqrt{\tau}Dr=\frac{\ell}{4\sqrt{\tau}}\le\frac18.\)

Thus the ball of radius \(r\) centered at \(c\) lies in \(K\). Also, \(K\subseteq[0,1]^\tau\).

On \(K\), the gradient is rational at rational points:

\(\displaystyle (\nabla F_b(x))_t=\frac{b_t}{x_t}.\)

Its Euclidean norm is at most \(G=\tau/\ell\), and

\(\displaystyle \log\ell\le F_b(x)\le0.\)

Logarithms of positive rationals can be evaluated to any prescribed binary precision in polynomial time.

Why the approximation takes polynomial time

We use the standard rational ellipsoid method: exact violated linear inequalities provide cuts outside \(K\), and rational supergradients provide cuts inside \(K\). Its iteration and bit-complexity bounds are polynomial in the dimension and the logarithms of the relevant radii and precision. [Grötschel, Lovász and Schrijver, *The Ellipsoid Method and Its Consequences in Combinatorial Optimization*](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/10046/10046D.pdf)

For completeness, the necessary near-optimal-volume bound can be checked directly. For a desired objective error \(0<\gamma<1\), choose an integer \(M\ge\max\{1,-\log\ell\}\), and define

\(\displaystyle \lambda=\frac{\gamma}{8M},\qquad v=(1-\lambda)x^*+\lambda c,\qquad \rho=\min\left\{\lambda r,\frac{\gamma}{8G}\right\}.\)

Convexity of \(K\) implies that the ball of radius \(\lambda r\) centered at \(v\) lies in \(K\). Concavity gives

\(\displaystyle F_b(v)\ge F_b(x^*)-\lambda M=F_b(x^*)-\frac{\gamma}{8}.\)

The Lipschitz bound then implies that throughout the ball of radius \(\rho\),

\(\displaystyle F_b(x)\ge F_b(x^*)-\frac{\gamma}{4}.\)

If every feasible ellipsoid center had objective value less than \(F_b(x^*)-\gamma/2\), every gradient cut would retain this entire ball: concavity implies

\(\displaystyle F_b(y)\le F_b(x)+\nabla F_b(x)\cdot(y-x).\)

Consequently, the usual ellipsoid volume bound forces a feasible center within objective error \(\gamma/2\) after polynomially many iterations. Evaluating feasible centers to error \(\gamma/8\) and selecting the largest evaluated value produces an exactly feasible rational point within objective error \(\gamma\). All logarithmic radius bounds are polynomial here.

Finally, \(F_b\) is strongly concave on \(K\):

\(\displaystyle -\nabla^2F_b(x)=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{b_t}{x_t^2}\right)\succeq b_{\min}I.\)

The first-order optimality inequality at \(x^*\) therefore yields, for every \(x\in K\),

\(\displaystyle F_b(x^*)-F_b(x)\ge\frac{b_{\min}}2\|x-x^*\|_2^2.\)

Run the approximation procedure with

\(\displaystyle \gamma=\frac{b_{\min}\delta^2}{2}.\)

The resulting exactly feasible rational \(\widehat x\) satisfies \(\|\widehat x-x^*\|_\infty\le\delta\). All required precision parameters have polynomial binary encoding length, so this computation takes polynomial time.

5. Selecting witnesses and enforcing them exactly

Select the full-service types by

\(\displaystyle S=\{t:\widehat x_t\ge1-\delta\}.\)

For \(t\notin S\), the distance bound implies \(x_t^*<1\). Section 2 therefore supplies some resource \(j\) with

\(\displaystyle C_j(x^*)=1,\qquad d_{tj}x_t^*\ge b_t.\)

For that resource,

\(\displaystyle C_j(\widehat x)\ge1-\tau D\delta=1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4},\)

and

\(\displaystyle d_{tj}\widehat x_t\ge b_t-D\delta\ge e_t-\tau\eta-D\delta\ge e_t-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.\)

Thus we can find \(j(t)\) simply by scanning the resources for the rational inequalities

\(\displaystyle C_j(\widehat x)\ge1-\varepsilon,\qquad d_{tj}\widehat x_t\ge e_t-\varepsilon.\)

At least one resource passes these tests for every \(t\notin S\).

Construct the witness LP using these choices. The point \((y,z)=(\widehat x,\varepsilon)\) is feasible:

  • its capacity and box constraints hold exactly;
  • for \(t\in S\), \(\widehat x_t\ge1-\delta\ge1-\varepsilon\);
  • the selected resource inequalities hold by construction.

Since \(\varepsilon=g/4<g\), Section 3 implies that this LP has exact optimal value zero.

Solve it by a polynomial-time rational linear-programming algorithm, and output its rational vector \(y\). At \(z=0\), its constraints give

\(\displaystyle y_t=1\quad(t\in S),\)

and

\(\displaystyle C_{j(t)}(y)=1,\qquad d_{t,j(t)}y_t\ge e_t\quad(t\notin S).\)

The equality follows by combining the selected lower bound with the capacity upper bound.

Because \(\mu_t>0\), division by \(\mu_t\) gives \(y_ta_{t,j(t)}\ge w_t\). Hence \(y\) is an exact valid allocation for the original society.

6. Complexity conclusion

The construction performs one polynomial-precision convex optimization, a resource scan, and one rational linear-programming computation. Every intermediate encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and the final LP has \(\tau+1\) variables and polynomially many constraints.

The final allocation is rational with polynomial encoding length. Zero entitlements and zero demand coordinates are included in the proof, and no mass is expanded into individual users.
