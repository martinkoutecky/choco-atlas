8. Approximation guarantees and exact boundary behavior

Polynomial time under a separation promise

Define
$$ F=\max\{s_*-N,\ s_*R-B\}. $$
Feasibility is equivalent to $F\le0$.

Suppose a positive integer $p$ is given and the input is promised to satisfy
$$ F\le-2^{-p} \qquad\text{or}\qquad F\ge2^{-p}. $$
Then the decision is solvable in
$$ O\!\left(\tau m^3\operatorname{poly}(T+p)\right) $$
bit operations.

To prove this, all rational coefficients have magnitude at most $2^{O(T)}$, while $x,y=O(T)$. If approximations $\widehat x,\widehat y$ each have error at most $e\le1$, then
$$ \left| \gamma(\lambda_i\widehat x+\nu_i)\widehat y -\gamma(\lambda_i x+\nu_i)y \right| \le \gamma\bigl[\lambda_i(x+y+1)+\nu_i\bigr]e \le2^{O(T)}e. $$
Worker-threshold errors have the same type of bound. Also,
$$ |\max_j a_j-\max_j b_j| \le\max_j|a_j-b_j|. $$
Hence precision $p+O(T)$ gives an approximation to $F$ with error less than $2^{-p-2}$, which determines its sign under the promise.

An unconditional partial decision procedure

Without any separation promise, successively refine certified intervals for $F$.

  • If the interval lies strictly above zero, output infeasible.
  • If it lies strictly below zero, output feasible.

The procedure terminates whenever $F\ne0$: once the interval error is smaller than $|F|/2$, it determines the sign.

Moreover, Proposition 3 showed that $s_*\ne N$. Thus
$$ F=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s_*<N\ \text{and}\ s_*R=B. $$
The only inputs on which this procedure may fail to terminate are feasible inputs with exact budget equality.

In particular, the complement of the feasibility problem is recursively enumerable: on every infeasible input, $F>0$, and refinement eventually certifies this.
