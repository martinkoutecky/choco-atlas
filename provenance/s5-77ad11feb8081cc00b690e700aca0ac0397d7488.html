The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite firm-type and worker-type supports \(I\) and \(J\), rational type masses \(\alpha_i\) and \(\beta_j\), rational pair weights \(w_{ij}\), and anonymous choice maps \(C_i:\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{J}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{J}\) and \(D_j:\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{I}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{I}\), find a stable mass assignment maximizing \(\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}w_{ij}x_{ij}\). A mass assignment specifies distributions \(z_{i,b}\) and \(y_{j,d}\) over integral individual bundles, summing to \(\alpha_i\) and \(\beta_j\), with each assigned bundle individually rational and \(x_{ij}=\sum_b b_jz_{i,b}=\sum_d d_i y_{j,d}\). Stability means there are no bundle patterns \(b,d\) with \(z_{i,b}>0\) and \(y_{j,d}>0\) for which both sides choose an added partner of the other type, meaning \(C_i(b+e_j)_j>b_j\) and \(D_j(d+e_i)_i>d_i\). The choice maps satisfy the paper’s substitutability, consistency, and cardinal-monotonicity conditions, with quota-filling on the worker side; determine the exact maximum value and an optimizing assignment.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I,J\) be nonempty finite supports of firm and worker types, with positive binary-rational masses \(\alpha_i,\beta_j\) satisfying \(\sum_i\alpha_i+\sum_j\beta_j=1\). A society is given by its support: \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) counts positive-mass types, not possible types. Let \(E\subseteq I\times J\) specify mutually acceptable pairs, \(m=|E|\), and \(w_{ij}\in\mathbb Q\) be unrestricted-sign weights.
Choice maps \(C_i:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{J}\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{J}\) and \(D_j:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{I}\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{I}\) act on counts of acceptable partners; unacceptable coordinates are zero. They are supplied by evaluation oracles on binary-encoded vectors. Write \(|v|=\sum_a v_a\); all vector inequalities and minima are coordinatewise.
Each choice map \(H\) satisfies \(0\le H(v)\le v\), substitutability \(u\le v\Rightarrow\min\{u,H(v)\}\le H(u)\), consistency \(H(v)\le u\le v\Rightarrow H(u)=H(v)\), and cardinal monotonicity \(u\le v\Rightarrow |H(u)|\le|H(v)|\). Given binary-integer capacities \(k_i\ge0\) and quotas \(q_j\ge1\), require \(|C_i(v)|\le k_i\) and worker quota-filling \(|D_j(v)|=\min\{q_j,|v|\}\).
An assignment consists of nonnegative real bundle masses \(z_{i,b},y_{j,d}\), indexed by integral acceptable-partner bundles with \(|b|\le k_i\), \(|d|\le q_j\), satisfying \(\sum_bz_{i,b}=\alpha_i\), \(\sum_dy_{j,d}=\beta_j\), and \(x_{ij}=\sum_b b_jz_{i,b}=\sum_d d_i y_{j,d}\). Positive-mass bundles must satisfy \(C_i(b)=b\) and \(D_j(d)=d\).
The assignment is stable if no \((i,j)\in E\) and positive-mass bundles \(b,d\) satisfy both \(C_i(b+e_j)_j>b_j\) and \(D_j(d+e_i)_i>d_i\), where \(e_a\) is the appropriate unit vector. Additional partners are distinct individuals, including when their types already occur in a bundle.
Maximize \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij}\); an infeasible instance has value \(-\infty\). Let \(L\) be the total binary length of the numerical input, including capacities, quotas, and any decision threshold. Polynomial oracle time means polynomially many bit operations and oracle calls in \(m,\tau,L\), with polynomial-length queries; charge evaluation time separately.

Is exact maximum-weight stable mass assignment polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\) in this oracle model, or is its rational-threshold decision problem NP-hard even for polynomial-time evaluable choice maps?

For finite individual agents, maximum-weight stable matching under these set-choice axioms and worker quota-filling takes \(O(|F|^3|W|^3T)\) time, with choice-evaluation cost \(T\). [Faenza–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.06763v2).
Distributional many-to-many models have existence results for pairwise-stable outcomes, but these do not establish the complexity of this exact optimization problem; no result settling it was located. [Greinecker–Vocke](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26902v1).

Result

The rational-threshold decision problem is NP-hard even for polynomial-time evaluable choice maps. The reduction uses only two firm types, worker quotas equal to one, dyadic masses, weights in \(\{0,1\}\), and capacities bounded by the number of worker types. Every constructed instance has a stable assignment.

In addition, the decision problem has polynomial-size certificates verifiable in polynomial oracle time. Thus, with polynomial-time evaluable choice maps and the stated axioms promised, it is NP-complete.

1. The source problem

We use the following known result: deciding whether an explicitly represented circulant graph contains a clique of a specified size is NP-complete. The reduction establishing this result preserves clique sizes and produces a graph with polynomially many vertices. [Codenotti–Gerace–Vigna, Section 2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002437959810126X).

The complement of a circulant graph is circulant, and cliques in a graph are independent sets in its complement. Consequently, the following problem is NP-complete:

Given a simple undirected circulant graph \(G\) on vertex set \(\mathbb Z_n\) and an integer \(r\), decide whether \(G\) has an independent set of size at least \(r\).

We restrict to \(1\le r<n\). The excluded cases are polynomial-time decidable and can be replaced by fixed yes- or no-instances satisfying this restriction.

A circulant graph has the property that every translation \(a\mapsto a+t\), for \(t\in\mathbb Z_n\), is an automorphism. In particular, translations preserve independent sets.

2. A polynomial-time choice map encoding independence

Introduce worker types \(\mathbb Z_n\cup\{\star\}\). For a count vector \(v\), define

\(\displaystyle S(v)=\{a\in\mathbb Z_n:v_a>0\}.\)

Define the main firm’s choice map \(C\) by

\(\displaystyle C(v)_a=\min\{v_a,1\}\qquad(a\in\mathbb Z_n),\)

and

\(\displaystyle C(v)_\star= \begin{cases} \min\{v_\star,1\},&S(v)\text{ is independent in }G,\\ 0,&S(v)\text{ is not independent in }G. \end{cases}\)

Thus, the firm retains one worker of every offered vertex type. It retains a special worker precisely when the offered vertex types form an independent set.

We verify every required axiom on arbitrary nonnegative integral count vectors.

Bounds and capacity

Every output coordinate lies between zero and its input coordinate. Also,

\(\displaystyle |C(v)|=|S(v)|+C(v)_\star\le n+1.\)

We therefore set the main firm’s capacity to \(n+1\).

Substitutability

Let \(u\le v\). For a vertex coordinate \(a\),

\(\displaystyle \min\{u_a,C(v)_a\}=\min\{u_a,1\}=C(u)_a.\)

For the special coordinate, if \(C(v)_\star=0\), the required inequality is immediate. Otherwise, \(S(v)\) is independent. Since \(S(u)\subseteq S(v)\), the set \(S(u)\) is independent too, and

\(\displaystyle \min\{u_\star,C(v)_\star\}=\min\{u_\star,1\}=C(u)_\star.\)

Hence \(\min\{u,C(v)\}\le C(u)\).

Consistency

Suppose \(C(v)\le u\le v\). Whenever \(v_a>0\), we have \(C(v)_a=1\), so \(u_a>0\). Whenever \(v_a=0\), we have \(u_a=0\). Thus \(S(u)=S(v)\), and all vertex output coordinates agree.

If this common set is not independent, both special outputs are zero. If it is independent, either \(v_\star=0\), which forces \(u_\star=0\), or \(C(v)_\star=1\), which forces \(u_\star\ge1\). In either case the special outputs agree. Therefore \(C(u)=C(v)\).

Cardinal monotonicity

Let \(u\le v\). If \(S(u)=S(v)\), the vertex outputs agree and the special output cannot decrease.

Otherwise, \(|S(v)|-|S(u)|\ge1\), while the special output can decrease by at most one. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle |C(v)|-|C(u)|=|S(v)|-|S(u)|+C(v)_\star-C(u)_\star\ge0.\)

Evaluation cost

The map requires testing which coordinates are positive and checking whether the resulting vertex set contains an edge of \(G\). Given an adjacency matrix, this takes polynomial time in \(n\) and the query’s binary length. Its description has polynomial size.

3. The society and objective

There are two firm types, \(f\) and \(f_0\). Type \(f\) uses \(C\). Type \(f_0\) is isolated, has capacity zero, and chooses the zero bundle.

Every worker type accepts only \(f\). Its quota is one, and its choice map is

\(\displaystyle D_j(v)=\min\{v_f,1\}e_f.\)

These maps satisfy the three choice axioms: their only relevant scalar function is \(t\mapsto\min\{t,1\}\). In particular, for \(u\le v\), substitutability follows from \(\min\{u,\min\{v,1\}\}=\min\{u,1\}\); consistency follows by considering whether the chosen count is zero or one. Cardinal monotonicity and quota-filling follow directly.

Choose an integer \(h\) with

\(\displaystyle 2^h>n(r+2),\)

and put \(\delta=2^{-h}\). Assign masses

\(\displaystyle \alpha_f=n\delta,\qquad \alpha_{f_0}=1-n(r+2)\delta,\)

\(\displaystyle \beta_a=r\delta\quad(a\in\mathbb Z_n),\qquad \beta_\star=n\delta.\)

All these masses are positive dyadic rationals, and their sum is one.

The acceptable-pair set is

\(\displaystyle E=\{(f,j):j\in\mathbb Z_n\cup\{\star\}\}.\)

Set \(w_{f,\star}=1\) and all other weights to zero. The objective is therefore \(x_{f,\star}\), and the decision threshold is

\(\displaystyle t=n\delta.\)

The constructed society has \(\tau=n+3\) positive-mass types and \(m=n+1\) acceptable pairs. Taking the smallest possible \(h\) gives \(h=O(\log n)\), since \(r<n\). All numerical data and oracle descriptions have polynomial encoding length.

4. Correctness of the reduction

Lemma: every vertex worker is matched in every stable assignment

Fix a vertex type \(a\). Individual rationality implies that every positive-mass firm bundle has \(b_a\in\{0,1\}\). Worker quota one implies

\(\displaystyle x_{f,a}\le\beta_a=r\delta<n\delta=\alpha_f.\)

Suppose \(x_{f,a}<\beta_a\). Because type \(a\) accepts only \(f\), a positive mass of its workers then has the empty bundle.

Also, the mass of type-\(f\) firms whose bundles omit \(a\) equals

\(\displaystyle \alpha_f-x_{f,a}>0.\)

Choose a positive-mass firm bundle \(b\) omitting \(a\). By definition,

\(\displaystyle C(b+e_a)_a=1>b_a=0.\)

An empty type-\(a\) worker accepts \(f\), since \(D_a(e_f)_f=1\). These two positive-mass bundles form a blocking pair, a contradiction.

Therefore,

\(\displaystyle x_{f,a}=\beta_a=r\delta\qquad(a\in\mathbb Z_n).\)

A threshold-achieving assignment yields a large independent set

In every individually rational firm bundle, \(b_\star\le1\). Thus

\(\displaystyle x_{f,\star}\le\alpha_f=n\delta=t.\)

If a stable assignment reaches the threshold, equality holds. Since \(\sum_bz_{f,b}=\alpha_f\), every positive-mass type-\(f\) bundle must then have \(b_\star=1\).

Individual rationality of such a bundle forces its vertex set

\(\displaystyle S(b)=\{a:b_a=1\}\)

to be independent.

Normalize the firm bundle masses by dividing by \(\alpha_f\). This gives a probability distribution on independent sets. By the preceding lemma, each vertex has inclusion probability

\(\displaystyle \frac{x_{f,a}}{\alpha_f}=\frac{r\delta}{n\delta}=\frac rn.\)

Hence the expected size of the sampled independent set is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a\in\mathbb Z_n}\frac rn=r.\)

At least one supported independent set has size at least \(r\): otherwise every supported set would have size at most \(r-1\), contradicting this expectation.

A large independent set yields a threshold-achieving stable assignment

Conversely, suppose \(G\) has an independent set of size at least \(r\). Choose a subset \(S_0\) of size exactly \(r\).

For each \(t\in\mathbb Z_n\), let \(S_t=S_0+t\). Each \(S_t\) is independent. Assign mass \(\delta\) of type-\(f\) firms the bundle containing one special worker and one worker of every type in \(S_t\). If bundles coincide, add their masses.

The total firm mass is \(n\delta=\alpha_f\). Every vertex occurs in exactly \(r\) of these translated sets: for each \(s\in S_0\), there is exactly one translation satisfying \(s+t=a\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle x_{f,a}=r\delta=\beta_a,\qquad x_{f,\star}=n\delta=\beta_\star.\)

Assign every worker its bundle \(e_f\), and assign the isolated firms the empty bundle.

All firm bundles are individually rational because their vertex sets are independent. All worker bundles are individually rational. Moreover, every worker rejects an additional firm of type \(f\):

\(\displaystyle D_j(e_f+e_f)_f=1.\)

Since every acceptable pair involves \(f\), no pair blocks. The assignment is stable and attains \(t\).

We have proved the exact equivalence:

\(\displaystyle G\text{ has an independent set of size at least }r \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{a stable assignment has value at least }n\delta.\)

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction.

Every constructed instance is feasible

For completeness, feasibility does not encode the source answer.

If \(G\) has an edge, assign firm mass \(r\delta\) the bundle containing all vertex types and no special worker. Assign the remaining firm mass \((n-r)\delta\) the special-only bundle.

Match every vertex worker. Match special-worker mass \((n-r)\delta\), leaving special-worker mass \(r\delta\) unmatched.

Both firm bundles are individually rational. Vertex workers reject additional firms. Firms with the all-vertex bundle reject the special type because its vertex set is not independent; firms with the special-only bundle reject an additional special worker. Hence this assignment is stable.

If \(G\) is edgeless, the threshold-achieving construction above applies.

5. Polynomial certificates

This also establishes NP-completeness for polynomial-time evaluable choice maps.

For any instance, the bundle index sets are finite because capacities and quotas are finite. Stability excludes finitely many simultaneous positive bundle pairs. Equivalently, the stable assignments form a finite union of rational polytopes, obtained by allowing only supports containing no blocking bundle pair. Every such polytope is bounded by the type-mass equations. Thus any feasible instance has an attained rational optimum.

Suppose some stable assignment reaches a rational threshold. Restrict attention to its positive bundle support, and maximize the objective over the corresponding polytope. Every point remains stable: removing positive bundles cannot introduce a blocking pair.

There are \(\tau\) type-mass equations and \(m\) edge-balance equations. An optimal extreme point therefore has at most \(m+\tau\) positive bundle variables. Indeed, more positive columns would be linearly dependent, allowing a sufficiently small perturbation in both directions that preserves nonnegativity and all equations, contradicting extremality.

Every bundle coordinate has polynomial binary length. The equality matrix has integral entries bounded by capacities and quotas. Selecting independent rows and columns, Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound give polynomial binary length for the extreme point’s masses: its matrix dimension is at most \(m+\tau\), its entries have at most polynomially many bits, and the input mass denominators have total binary length at most \(L\).

A certificate lists these bundles and rational masses. A verifier checks individual rationality, mass equations, edge balances, the objective threshold, and all potential blocking pairs between listed bundles. This requires polynomially many bit operations and oracle calls with polynomial-length queries.

6. Relation to the finite-agent theorem

The reduction satisfies precisely the stated count-vector axioms. It does not supply permutation-invariant deterministic set choices on labelled individuals: choosing exactly one member from several individuals of the same type requires an additional tie-breaking rule.

That rule can introduce blocking pairs involving replacements by individuals of the same type. The task’s blocking test instead asks whether the chosen count of that type increases. Consequently, the finite-agent theorem cannot be applied merely by expanding masses into labelled copies. Its hypotheses do not establish an optimization-preserving equivalence with this model. [Faenza–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.06763v2).

The hardness already holds with polynomial-size capacities and dyadic denominators. Therefore exact optimization in polynomial oracle time would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
