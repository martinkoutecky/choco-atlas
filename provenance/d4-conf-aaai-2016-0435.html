The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of provider types \(T\), population masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and type profiles specifying depots, per-provider demand templates, revenues, costs, and vehicle capacities, define \(v(x)\) as the profit of the fluid routing game for coalition masses \(0\leq x\leq\mu\), with demand and vehicle mass scaled by type mass and route flows allowed to be fractional. Assume \(v\) is differentiable, \(v(0)=0\), and \(v(\mu)>0\); define \(\phi_t=\mu_t\int_0^1 \frac{\partial v(s\mu)}{\partial x_t}\,ds\) and \(\rho_t=\phi_t/v(\mu)\), with \(\phi_t\geq0\). Find nonnegative payment rates \(p_t\) and minimum subsidy \(\delta\geq0\) such that \(\mu_t p_t=(v(\mu)+\delta)\rho_t\) for every \(t\), and \(\sum_t x_t p_t\geq v(x)\) for every coalition mass vector \(x\in[0,\mu]\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) provider types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Type \(t\) has depot \(d_t\), a disjoint finite request template \(M_t\), and fleet size \(b_t>0\) per unit provider mass. All vehicles have common capacity \(Q>0\). Request \(j\) has quantity \(0<q_j\leq Q\) and revenue \(r_j>0\). Traversal costs \(c_{uv}\geq0\) are common to all providers on the complete directed network of depots and requests. All numerical input is binary rational; \(m\) is the number of network vertices and \(L\) the total input bit length.
A route from \(d_t\) returns to \(d_t\), visits no other depot, visits each selected request once, and satisfies \(\sum_{j\text{ visited}}q_j\leq Q\). Its profit is the sum of visited-request revenues minus traversal costs. Requests may be left unserved.
For a coalition \(x\in[0,\mu]\), assign nonnegative real flows \(z_{t,R}\) to such routes. Require \(\sum_R z_{t,R}\leq b_tx_t\) and, for every \(j\in M_u\), \(\sum_{t,R:j\in R}z_{t,R}\leq x_u\). Define \(v(x)\) as the maximum total route profit weighted by these flows; route families are implicit, not listed in the input.
Assume \(v(0)=0\), \(v(\mu)>0\), and differentiability on \([0,\mu]\), interpreted relative to this domain at its boundary. Define \(\phi_t=\mu_t\int_0^1\partial_t v(s\mu)\,ds\) and \(\rho_t=\phi_t/v(\mu)\); assume the integrals exist and \(\phi_t\geq0\). These quantities are defined by the routing instance, not supplied by an oracle.
A feasible subsidized allocation consists of rates \(p_t\geq0\) and subsidy \(\delta\geq0\) satisfying \(\mu_tp_t=(v(\mu)+\delta)\rho_t\) for every type and \(\sum_t x_tp_t\geq v(x)\) for every \(x\in[0,\mu]\).

Is deciding, for a binary-rational bound \(B\geq0\), whether a feasible allocation exists with \(\delta\leq B\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

For discrete cooperative vehicle-routing games, an iterative allocation algorithm achieves stability and proportionality to normalized Shapley values with minimum subsidy; this is a correctness result, without a polynomial-time guarantee ([source](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/9912/9771)).
No result settling the exact continuous problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made fractional routing precise using flows over capacity-feasible routes: each unit of route flow serves one unit of every visited request template and consumes one unit of vehicle mass.
  • The continuous question scales vehicle mass but does not specify fleet endowments; introduced positive rational per-type fleet sizes and allowed each available vehicle unit one route. The discrete formulation has no explicit fleet-size bound.
  • Retained the discrete model's homogeneous common vehicle capacity, common traversal costs, disjoint request templates, optional service, and return to the originating depot.
  • Chose nonnegative rational traversal costs without imposing a triangle inequality, which the anchor's minimum-subsidy argument does not require.
  • Interpreted differentiability at the boundary relative to the coalition domain and explicitly required existence of the defining integrals.
  • Used the rational subsidy-bound decision version of minimum-subsidy optimization, with routing instances supplied explicitly and route families implicit; allocation proportions must be computed from the instance.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Every instance satisfying the stated assumptions admits a feasible allocation with \(\delta=0\). Thus the promised decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\): always answer “yes.” Reading the input takes \(O(L)\) time; no route enumeration or numerical optimization is required.

The differentiability assumption is consequential: together with homogeneity, differentiability at the origin forces the entire value function to be linear. Moreover, zero subsidy also follows under the weaker assumption of differentiability only along the interior proportional path.

1. Homogeneity of the routing value.

There are finitely many admissible routes, since each route is determined by its depot and an ordered list of distinct requests. For any coalition \(x\), its feasible flow set is nonempty, closed, and bounded: zero flows are feasible, and each route flow is bounded by its provider’s fleet constraint. Hence the maximum defining \(v(x)\) exists.

Fix \(x\in[0,\mu]\) and \(\lambda>0\) such that \(\lambda x\in[0,\mu]\). Multiplication by \(\lambda\) gives a bijection between the feasible flows for \(x\) and those for \(\lambda x\). Indeed, every fleet bound and every request bound scales by exactly \(\lambda\). The objective scales by the same factor. Therefore

\[ v(\lambda x)=\lambda v(x). \]

Also, \(v(x)\geq0\), because serving no requests is feasible.

2. Differentiability at the origin forces linearity.

By differentiability relative to the box at \(0\), there is a vector \(a\in\mathbb{R}^{\tau}\) such that

\[ v(h)=v(0)+a\cdot h+o(\lVert h\rVert) \]

as \(h\to0\) within \([0,\mu]\).

Fix a nonzero \(x\in[0,\mu]\), and substitute \(h=\varepsilon x\), where \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Since \(v(0)=0\) and homogeneity gives \(v(\varepsilon x)=\varepsilon v(x)\), we obtain

\[ \varepsilon v(x)=\varepsilon a\cdot x+o(\varepsilon\lVert x\rVert). \]

Dividing by \(\varepsilon\) and taking the limit proves

\[ v(x)=a\cdot x \qquad\text{for every }x\in[0,\mu]. \]

For each type \(t\), positivity of \(\mu_t\) permits a coalition \(h e_t\) with \(h>0\). Thus \(h a_t=v(h e_t)\geq0\), proving \(a_t\geq0\).

At every interior point \(s\mu\), where \(0<s<1\), the partial derivative is consequently \(\partial_t v(s\mu)=a_t\). Endpoint values do not affect the assumed integrals. Hence

\[ \phi_t=\mu_t a_t, \qquad v(\mu)=\sum_t\mu_t a_t, \qquad \rho_t=\frac{\mu_t a_t}{v(\mu)}. \]

Choose \(p_t=a_t\) and \(\delta=0\). These rates are nonnegative, and proportionality holds because

\[ \mu_t p_t=\mu_t a_t=v(\mu)\rho_t. \]

For every coalition,

\[ \sum_t x_t p_t=a\cdot x=v(x), \]

so all stability constraints hold with equality. Because subsidies must be nonnegative, the minimum subsidy is exactly \(0\).

3. The conclusion does not depend solely on differentiability at the origin.

Even if differentiability at the origin were omitted, differentiability at every \(s\mu\), \(0<s<1\), would suffice for zero subsidy.

First, \(v\) is concave. If \(z\) and \(z'\) are optimal flows for \(x\) and \(x'\), respectively, then \(\theta z+(1-\theta)z'\) is feasible for \(\theta x+(1-\theta)x'\). Its objective is \(\theta v(x)+(1-\theta)v(x')\), proving concavity.

Fix \(s\in(0,1)\), set \(y=s\mu\), and write \(g(s)=\nabla v(y)\). Concavity implies the supporting inequality

\[ v(x)\leq v(y)+g(s)\cdot(x-y) \]

for every \(x\in[0,\mu]\). Explicitly, apply concavity to \(y+\varepsilon(x-y)\), rearrange, divide by \(\varepsilon>0\), and let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\); differentiability at the interior point \(y\) justifies the limit.

Homogeneity gives \(v(s\mu)=s v(\mu)\). Differentiating this identity in \(s\) yields

\[ g(s)\cdot\mu=v(\mu), \qquad g(s)\cdot y=v(y). \]

The supporting inequality therefore becomes \(v(x)\leq g(s)\cdot x\). Integrating over \(s\) gives

\[ v(x)\leq\sum_t x_t\frac{\phi_t}{\mu_t}, \qquad \sum_t\phi_t=v(\mu). \]

Thus \(p_t=\phi_t/\mu_t\), together with \(\delta=0\), again satisfies stability and proportionality. Nonnegativity follows from the assumed \(\phi_t\geq0\).

4. Running time and scope.

Since \(B\geq0\), every promised input satisfies the requested subsidy bound. The decision algorithm uses \(O(1)\) work beyond reading the input, or \(O(L)\) total time, which is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

This conclusion concerns the decision problem on inputs satisfying the stated promises. It does not require verifying differentiability or computing a numerical allocation.
