Theorem 4.2 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given schools, a finite set of complete student types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational priority scores and utilities, capacity shares, and an application limit \(\ell\), compute an \(\varepsilon\)-equilibrium of the nonatomic deferred-acceptance game. A strategy specifies for each type a distribution over ordered lists of at most \(\ell\) schools; the resulting type mass is assigned by school priorities up to capacity, splitting mass uniformly at cutoff ties. Require that no infinitesimal student of any type can gain more than \(\varepsilon\) by changing their list.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) complete student types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Each type represents nonatomic student mass; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) schools, with binary-rational capacity shares \(c_j\in(0,1]\), priority scores \(s_j(t)\in[0,1]\), and utilities \(v_j(t)\geq0\). Unmatched utility is \(0\); applications have no cost. The inputs include an integer \(1\leq\ell\leq m\) and a binary rational \(\varepsilon>0\); \(L\) denotes the total input bit length.
Let \(A\) contain all ordered lists of distinct schools of length at most \(\ell\), including the empty list. A strategy \(p_t\in\Delta(A)\) allocates mass \(\mu_t p_t(a)\) to list \(a\), independently of tie-breaking lotteries.
Use student-proposing deferred acceptance on this mass. Schools rank by decreasing score, breaking score ties with independent uniform student–school lottery numbers, fixed throughout the procedure and unknown when lists are chosen. Schools tentatively retain their highest-ranked applicant mass up to capacity; rejected mass proceeds down its list. Use the limiting assignment if infinitely many rounds occur. Lottery integration determines the uniform fractions admitted at cutoff ties.
Let \(U_t(a;p)\) be the expected utility of an infinitesimal type-\(t\) student submitting \(a\), against the unchanged population strategy \(p\), averaging over its lotteries. An \(\varepsilon\)-equilibrium satisfies \(\max_{a\in A}U_t(a;p)-\sum_{a\in A}p_t(a)U_t(a;p)\leq\varepsilon\) for every supported type \(t\). Represent strategies by explicit lists with nonzero binary-rational probabilities.

Is computing such an \(\varepsilon\)-equilibrium polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,\ell,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{PPAD}\)-hard?

For finitely many students whose types are sampled without replacement, exact symmetric equilibrium computation is in \(\mathrm{FIXP}\) and interim approximate computation is in \(\mathrm{PPAD}\), measured against the explicitly expanded agent-form payoff table. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.09089).
Convergence results for continuous type distributions retain finitely many students; they do not address this nonatomic mass game. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.09089). No result settling the stated mass-game complexity question was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total student mass to one and expressed each school's positive capacity as a share of that mass; imposed no restriction on total school capacity.
  • Retained nonnegative utilities, scores in the unit interval, zero unmatched utility, costless applications, and lists of distinct schools, including the empty list.
  • Resolved uniform tie-breaking through independent school-specific lotteries fixed throughout deferred acceptance and unobserved when strategies are selected; included the limiting assignment for potentially infinite nonatomic deferred-acceptance runs.
  • Interpreted approximate equilibrium as typewise expected regret before list randomization, matching the discrete interim mixed-equilibrium definition.
  • Specified explicit sparse rational strategy representations and binary-rational accuracy as part of the computational problem; accuracy contributes to the total input bit length.
  • Used polynomial-time solvability versus PPAD-hardness because the anchor is a search-class membership result, rather than an NP-hardness or approximation result.

Partial result: arbitrary priorities with one application

The general polynomial-time versus $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hardness question remains unresolved. I prove the following additional result, which does not require tied priorities.

Theorem. If $\ell=1$, then:

  • An exact equilibrium exists with real-valued probabilities.
  • For every $\varepsilon>0$, an $\varepsilon$-equilibrium exists with polynomial-size dyadic probabilities and at most $m$ singleton lists plus the empty list per type.
  • Computing such an $\varepsilon$-equilibrium belongs to $\mathrm{PPAD}$ under the given support representation.

This establishes membership, not hardness or polynomial-time solvability.

For bit-complexity statements, I use the intended interpretation that utilities and priority scores are supplied as binary rationals. For scores, only their school-specific weak orderings are needed.

1. Exact admission probabilities when $\ell=1$

Index the empty list by $0$ and the singleton list $(j)$ by $j$. Write

$$ p_{tj}=p_t((j)),\qquad p_{t0}=p_t(\varnothing),\qquad x_{tj}=\mu_t p_{tj}. $$

Thus each type’s probability vector belongs to

$$ \Delta_k=\left\{z\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{k}:\sum_{j=0}^{m}z_j=1\right\}, \qquad k=m+1. $$

For type $t$ and school $j$, define the higher-priority and equal-priority applicant masses by

$$ H_{tj}=\sum_{u:\,s_j(u)>s_j(t)}x_{uj}, \qquad B_{tj}=\sum_{u:\,s_j(u)=s_j(t)}x_{uj}. $$

In particular,

$$ x_{tj}\leq B_{tj}. $$

With singleton applications, no rejected student makes another application. Deferred acceptance therefore amounts to selecting the highest-ranked applicants at each school. The admission probability of a prospective type-$t$ applicant is exactly

$$ q_{tj}(p)= \begin{cases} 0,&H_{tj}\geq c_j,\\ 1,&H_{tj}<c_j\text{ and }B_{tj}=0,\\ \min\{1,(c_j-H_{tj})/B_{tj}\}, &H_{tj}<c_j\text{ and }B_{tj}>0. \end{cases} $$

Indeed, higher-score applicants take capacity first. If capacity remains, uniform independent lotteries distribute the residual capacity uniformly within the equal-score tier. An absent tier clears an infinitesimal application whenever higher-priority mass is strictly below capacity.

Consequently, for rational $p$, all singleton payoffs and regrets can be computed exactly in polynomial time. This includes deviations to unused singleton lists.

If every utility is zero, the all-empty profile is an exact equilibrium. More generally, put

$$ V=\max_{t,j}v_j(t). $$

If $\varepsilon\geq V$, the all-empty profile is already an $\varepsilon$-equilibrium. Henceforth assume

$$ 0<\varepsilon<V. $$

Normalize utilities and the target accuracy by

$$ a_{tj}=\frac{v_j(t)}{V}, \qquad e=\frac{\varepsilon}{V}. $$

Thus $a_{tj}\in[0,1]$ and $e\in(0,1)$. Define

$$ u_{tj}(p)=a_{tj}q_{tj}(p),\qquad u_{t0}(p)=0, $$

and normalized regret

$$ r_t(p)= \max_{0\leq j\leq m}u_{tj}(p) -\sum_{j=0}^{m}p_{tj}u_{tj}(p). $$

The original equilibrium requirement is exactly $r_t(p)\leq e$.

2. A continuous overestimate with a uniform weighted error bound

The probabilities $q_{tj}$ need not be continuous when an unused priority tier lies immediately below higher-priority mass that exactly fills capacity. The following approximation handles that boundary explicitly.

For $\delta>0$, define

$$ q^\delta_{tj}(p)= \min\left\{ 1,\, \frac{[c_j-H_{tj}+\delta]_+}{B_{tj}+\delta} \right\}, \qquad [z]_+=\max\{z,0\}. $$

This is an auxiliary continuous function; it is not asserted to be the admission rule of another deferred-acceptance instance.

Lemma 1. For every profile, type and school,

$$ q^\delta_{tj}\geq q_{tj}, \qquad B_{tj}\bigl(q^\delta_{tj}-q_{tj}\bigr)\leq\delta. $$

Proof. Fix $t,j$, abbreviate $H=H_{tj}$ and $B=B_{tj}$, and put $r=c_j-H$.

If $r\leq0$, then $q=0$ and

$$ 0\leq q^\delta \leq\frac{\delta}{B+\delta}. $$

Both claims follow.

If $r>0$ and $B=0$, both probabilities equal $1$, and the weighted error is zero.

If $B>0$ and $r\geq B$, both probabilities again equal $1$.

Finally, if $0<r<B$, then

$$ q=\frac rB, \qquad q^\delta=\frac{r+\delta}{B+\delta}, $$

so

$$ q^\delta-q= \frac{\delta(B-r)}{B(B+\delta)}\geq0, $$

and therefore

$$ B(q^\delta-q)= \frac{\delta(B-r)}{B+\delta}\leq\delta. $$

These cases exhaust the possibilities. $\square$

Let

$$ u^\delta_{tj}=a_{tj}q^\delta_{tj}, \qquad u^\delta_{t0}=0, $$

and define

$$ r^\delta_t(p)= \max_j u^\delta_{tj}(p) -\sum_jp_{tj}u^\delta_{tj}(p). $$

Lemma 2. For every profile,

$$ r_t(p)\leq r^\delta_t(p)+\frac{m\delta}{\mu_t}. $$

Proof. Lemma 1 implies $u_{tj}\leq u^\delta_{tj}$. Hence

$$ \begin{aligned} r_t(p) &\leq \max_j u^\delta_{tj} -\sum_jp_{tj}u_{tj}\\ &= r^\delta_t(p) +\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{tj}(u^\delta_{tj}-u_{tj}). \end{aligned} $$

Since $a_{tj}\leq1$ and $\mu_t p_{tj}=x_{tj}\leq B_{tj}$,

$$ p_{tj}(u^\delta_{tj}-u_{tj}) \leq \frac{B_{tj}}{\mu_t} (q^\delta_{tj}-q_{tj}) \leq\frac{\delta}{\mu_t}. $$

Summing over the schools proves the claim. $\square$

The distinction between pointwise error and weighted error is essential. At unused actions the pointwise error can be large, but the auxiliary rule overestimates those deviations. Its total overestimate on a type’s actual strategy is uniformly bounded.

3. A fixed-point formulation for the auxiliary problem

Let

$$ D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}\Delta_k. $$

For $p\in D$, define

$$ \bar u^\delta_t(p)=\sum_jp_{tj}u^\delta_{tj}(p), $$

$$ d_{tj}(p)=u^\delta_{tj}(p)-\bar u^\delta_t(p), \qquad g_{tj}(p)=[d_{tj}(p)]_+, \qquad G_t(p)=\sum_jg_{tj}(p). $$

Consider the continuous map $F^\delta:D\to D$ given by

$$ F^\delta_{tj}(p)= \frac{p_{tj}+g_{tj}(p)}{1+G_t(p)}. $$

The numerator is nonnegative, and its sum over $j$ equals the denominator, so this is indeed a self-map of $D$.

Lemma 3. If

$$ \|F^\delta(p)-p\|_\infty\leq\alpha, $$

then every type satisfies

$$ r^\delta_t(p)\leq\sqrt{k(k+1)\alpha}. $$

In particular, every fixed point has zero auxiliary regret.

Proof. Fix a type and suppress its index. Since all normalized payoffs lie in $[0,1]$,

$$ |d_j|\leq1,\qquad 0\leq g_j\leq1,\qquad G\leq k. $$

The residual assumption gives

$$ |g_j-p_jG| =(1+G)|F^\delta_j(p)-p_j| \leq(k+1)\alpha. $$

By the definition of the average payoff,

$$ \sum_jp_jd_j=0. $$

Also, $d_jg_j=g_j^2$. Therefore

$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_jg_j^2 &=\sum_jd_jg_j\\ &=\sum_jd_j(g_j-p_jG)\\ &\leq\sum_j|d_j|\,|g_j-p_jG|\\ &\leq k(k+1)\alpha. \end{aligned} $$

Regret is $\max_jd_j=\max_jg_j$, because the maximum payoff is at least its probability-weighted average. Thus

$$ r^\delta_t(p)\leq \left(\sum_jg_j^2\right)^{1/2}, $$

which proves the assertion. $\square$

Since $D$ is nonempty, compact and convex, Brouwer’s theorem supplies a fixed point of $F^\delta$ for every $\delta>0$.

4. Exact equilibrium existence for arbitrary priorities when $\ell=1$

To pass from auxiliary equilibria to an exact equilibrium, we need the appropriate one-sided continuity property of the original regrets.

Lemma 4. Each original singleton payoff $u_{tj}$ is lower semicontinuous. Each average payoff

$$ \bar u_t(p)=\sum_jp_{tj}u_{tj}(p) $$

is continuous. Consequently, $r_t$ is lower semicontinuous.

Proof. Where $B_{tj}>0$, the admission rule can be written as

$$ q_{tj}= \min\left\{1,\frac{[c_j-H_{tj}]_+}{B_{tj}}\right\}, $$

and is continuous.

At a profile with $B_{tj}=0$ and $H_{tj}<c_j$, sufficiently nearby profiles satisfy

$$ c_j-H'_{tj}>B'_{tj}, $$

so their admission probabilities equal $1$. Thus continuity holds there too.

At a profile with $B_{tj}=0$ and $H_{tj}\geq c_j$, the admission probability equals $0$. Its lower semicontinuity follows from nonnegativity. Multiplication by the nonnegative constant $a_{tj}$ preserves lower semicontinuity.

For an average-payoff summand $p_{tj}u_{tj}$, continuity holds where $B_{tj}>0$. If $B_{tj}=0$, then $p_{tj}=0$, since $\mu_t>0$ and $\mu_tp_{tj}\leq B_{tj}$. Along any convergent sequence approaching that profile,

$$ 0\leq p'_{tj}u_{tj}(p')\leq p'_{tj}\longrightarrow0. $$

Hence that summand is continuous everywhere, and so is their finite sum.

A finite maximum of lower semicontinuous functions is lower semicontinuous. Subtracting the continuous average payoff therefore makes $r_t$ lower semicontinuous. $\square$

Proposition 5. An exact equilibrium with real-valued probabilities exists when $\ell=1$.

Proof. For every positive integer $n$, choose a fixed point $p^{(n)}$ of $F^{1/n}$. Lemmas 2 and 3 give

$$ 0\leq r_t(p^{(n)})\leq\frac{m}{n\mu_t} $$

for every type.

Compactness of $D$ supplies a convergent subsequence with limit $p^*\in D$. Lower semicontinuity gives

$$ r_t(p^*)\leq \liminf r_t(p^{(n)})=0. $$

Regret is always nonnegative, so every type has regret zero at $p^*$. $\square$

This proves existence with real probabilities. It does not assert that an exact equilibrium has a binary-rational representation.

5. Polynomial-size dyadic approximate equilibria

Put

$$ \mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t, \qquad \delta=\frac{e\mu_{\min}}{4m}, \qquad \rho=\frac{e\delta}{20}. $$

In particular, $0<\delta<1$.

First, the auxiliary payoffs have a quantitative continuity bound. If

$$ \max_t\|p_t-p'_t\|_1\leq\rho, $$

then each higher-priority mass and equal-priority mass changes by at most $\rho$, because total population mass is $1$.

For fixed $B$, the function

$$ (H,B)\longmapsto \min\left\{1,\frac{[c-H+\delta]_+}{B+\delta}\right\} $$

is $1/\delta$-Lipschitz in $H$. For fixed $H$, its nonconstant derivative in $B$ has magnitude

$$ \frac{[c-H+\delta]_+}{(B+\delta)^2} \leq\frac1{B+\delta}\leq\frac1\delta; $$

the first inequality applies on the nonconstant portion, where the ratio is at most $1$. The function is continuous at its piece boundaries, so these bounds are global. Hence

$$ |q^\delta_{tj}(p)-q^\delta_{tj}(p')| \leq\frac{2\rho}{\delta}. $$

It follows that the maximum auxiliary payoff changes by at most $2\rho/\delta$, while the average auxiliary payoff changes by at most

$$ \frac{2\rho}{\delta}+\rho. $$

The extra $\rho$ accounts for changing the averaging probabilities. Thus

$$ |r^\delta_t(p)-r^\delta_t(p')| \leq\frac{4\rho}{\delta}+\rho \leq\frac{5\rho}{\delta} =\frac e4. $$

Now take an exact fixed point $p^\delta$ of $F^\delta$. Choose an integer $b$ such that

$$ 2k\,2^{-b}\leq\rho. $$

For every type, round its $m$ singleton probabilities down to multiples of $2^{-b}$ and put all remaining probability on the empty list. The resulting vector $p'_t$ is nonnegative, sums exactly to $1$, and satisfies

$$ \|p'_t-p^\delta_t\|_1\leq2m\,2^{-b}\leq\rho. $$

Therefore

$$ r^\delta_t(p')\leq\frac e4, $$

and Lemma 2 yields

$$ r_t(p') \leq\frac e4+\frac{m\delta}{\mu_t} \leq\frac e2. $$

Under binary-rational input encoding,

$$ \log(1/e),\ \log(1/\mu_{\min})=O(L). $$

Consequently, one can choose

$$ b=O(L+\log m). $$

There are only $\tau(m+1)$ possible nonzero probabilities in this construction. Each nonempty list has length $1$, and zero probabilities can be omitted.

This proves the polynomial-size dyadic representation claim without assuming continuity of the original deviation payoffs.

6. A polynomial reduction to approximate Brouwer

I now justify the $\mathrm{PPAD}$ membership claim, including the map’s numerical properties and the conversion to the required output.

I use the standard approximate-Brouwer theorem: weak fixed-point search belongs to $\mathrm{PPAD}$ for uniformly polynomial-time computable families of continuous self-maps with a polynomially bounded continuity modulus, with accuracy encoded in binary. A sufficient continuity condition is a Lipschitz bound whose logarithm is polynomial in the input length. This theorem is stated, in a more general approximate-evaluation form, as [Theorem 6.8 of Chen, Paparas and Yannakakis](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~paparas/a20-chen.pdf).

6.1 Polynomial evaluation and continuity

For $p,p'\in D$, put $h=\|p-p'\|_\infty$. Each $H_{tj}$ and $B_{tj}$ changes by at most $h$:

$$ \sum_u\mu_u|p_{uj}-p'_{uj}|\leq h. $$

Thus

$$ |u^\delta_{tj}(p)-u^\delta_{tj}(p')| \leq\frac{2h}{\delta}. $$

The average auxiliary payoff changes by at most

$$ \left(k+\frac2\delta\right)h, $$

and therefore every $g_{tj}$ changes by at most $Kh$, where

$$ K=k+\frac4\delta. $$

The sum $G_t$ changes by at most $kKh$. In the formula for $F^\delta$, numerators are at most $2$ and denominators are at least $1$. Comparing the two quotients gives the global bound

$$ \|F^\delta(p)-F^\delta(p')\|_\infty \leq C_Fh, \qquad C_F=1+(2k+1)K. $$

The logarithm of $C_F$ is polynomially bounded in $m$ and $L$.

All formulas use rational sums, products, comparisons, positive parts and divisions with positive denominators. They have polynomially many operations and a fixed number of arithmetic layers apart from the summations. Their intermediate rational encoding lengths are polynomial in the input and evaluation-point lengths. Thus exact rational evaluation takes polynomial time.

6.2 An explicit cube formulation

To avoid any domain-conversion or feasibility issue, define a retraction from the cube onto each simplex. For a block $z_t\in[0,1]^k$, put

$$ S_t=\sum_jz_{tj}, $$

$$ P_{tj}(z)= \frac{z_{tj}+[1-S_t]_+/k}{\max\{1,S_t\}}. $$

The coordinates are nonnegative and sum to $1$. Moreover, $P$ is the identity on $D$.

If two cube points differ by at most $h$ in sup norm, their block sums differ by at most $kh$. Each numerator in $P$ changes by at most $2h$, each denominator by at most $kh$, numerators are at most $1$, and denominators are at least $1$. Therefore

$$ \|P(z)-P(z')\|_\infty\leq(k+2)h. $$

Define the cube self-map

$$ \Gamma(z)=F^\delta(P(z)). $$

It is polynomial-time computable and has Lipschitz constant at most $(k+2)C_F$.

Choose

$$ \alpha=\frac{e^2}{16k(k+1)}. $$

The approximate-Brouwer theorem applies with target residual $\alpha/(k+2)$, whose binary encoding length is polynomial. It supplies a rational cube point $z$ satisfying

$$ \|z-\Gamma(z)\|_\infty\leq\frac{\alpha}{k+2}. $$

Put $p=P(z)$. This is exactly feasible. Since $F^\delta(p)\in D$ and $P$ is the identity on $D$,

$$ \begin{aligned} \|p-F^\delta(p)\|_\infty &=\|P(z)-P(\Gamma(z))\|_\infty\\ &\leq(k+2)\|z-\Gamma(z)\|_\infty\\ &\leq\alpha. \end{aligned} $$

Lemma 3 now gives

$$ r^\delta_t(p)\leq\frac e4. $$

Finally, apply the dyadic rounding from Section 5, with error at most $\rho$ per type, to obtain $p'$. Its auxiliary regret increases by at most $e/4$. Lemma 2 then gives

$$ r_t(p') \leq \frac e4+\frac e4+\frac{m\delta}{\mu_t} \leq\frac{3e}{4}<e. $$

Multiplying by $V$ proves the required $\varepsilon$-equilibrium condition.

The reduction, decoding and rounding are polynomial-time operations. The output explicitly lists at most $m$ singleton actions and the empty action per type, with polynomial-size dyadic probabilities. This completes the $\mathrm{PPAD}$ membership proof for $\ell=1$.

7. Why the continuity argument fails for longer lists

The continuity of average payoffs in Lemma 4 is specific to single applications. Here is a counterexample with $\ell=2<m$ where a vanishing strategy perturbation changes the payoffs of lists used with probability $1$.

There are three schools, with

$$ c_1=c_2=\frac14,\qquad c_3=1, $$

and three types, with

$$ \mu_a=\mu_b=\frac14,\qquad \mu_z=\frac12. $$

Utilities are

$$ (v_1(a),v_2(a),v_3(a))=(2,1,0), $$

$$ (v_1(b),v_2(b),v_3(b))=(1,2,0), $$

while type $z$ values every school at zero.

School $1$ ranks $b$ above $a$ above $z$. School $2$ ranks $a$ above $z$ above $b$. These orders can be encoded using scores $1,1/2,0$. Scores at school $3$ are immaterial.

For $0\leq d<1/2$, let

$$ p_a((1,2))=1,\qquad p_b((2,1))=1, $$

$$ p_z((2))=d,\qquad p_z(\varnothing)=1-d. $$

All submitted nonempty lists respect nonincreasing utility order.

The outcome at $d=0$

Each of schools $1$ and $2$ receives exactly its capacity in first-choice applications. Deferred acceptance terminates immediately:

$$ a\longmapsto1,\qquad b\longmapsto2. $$

Both used lists give utility $2$.

The outcome at every $d>0$

Write

$$ c=\frac14,\qquad w=\frac d2>0. $$

Here $w$ is the mass of type $z$ applying to school $2$.

At school $1$, suppose cumulative type-$b$ arrivals have mass $y\leq c$. All of them outrank type $a$ and are retained. Exactly mass $y$ of type $a$ is consequently rejected and proceeds to school $2$.

At school $2$, suppose cumulative type-$a$ arrivals have mass $x\leq c$. It retains all mass $x$ of type $a$ first, then as much of type $z$ as fits, and finally type $b$. Thus the cumulative rejected mass of type $b$ is

$$ \min\{c,x+w\}. $$

Starting with no type-$a$ arrivals at school $2$, alternating these two steps gives cumulative type-$a$ rejection masses

$$ x_n=\min\{c,nw\}. $$

This follows by induction from

$$ x_{n+1}=\min\{c,x_n+w\},\qquad x_0=0. $$

After at most $\lceil c/w\rceil$ such cycles, all type $a$ is at school $2$ and all type $b$ is at school $1$. Both schools are filled by their highest-priority supported type; every type-$z$ applicant is rejected.

Hence, for every $d>0$,

$$ a\longmapsto2,\qquad b\longmapsto1, $$

and both used lists give utility $1$.

The profiles converge to the $d=0$ profile, but

$$ U_a((1,2);p^{(d)})= \begin{cases} 2,&d=0,\\ 1,&d>0, \end{cases} $$

with the same discontinuity for type $b$.

Indeed, every displayed profile is an exact equilibrium. At $d=0$, types $a,b$ obtain their maximum possible utility. At $d>0$, each type’s preferred school is completely occupied by a strictly higher-priority type, so its best achievable utility is $1$, attained by its submitted list. Type $z$ is indifferent among all actions.

Thus even equilibrium profiles and utility-ordered lists can exhibit discontinuous used-list payoffs. The continuity argument in Section 4 cannot simply be transferred to $\ell=2$.

8. Status of the target question

The new results settle existence, polynomial-size approximate representation and $\mathrm{PPAD}$ membership for $\ell=1$ with arbitrary priorities. They leave open whether that restricted problem has a polynomial-time algorithm or is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hard.

For general $2\leq\ell<m$, the argument supplies neither an equilibrium-construction algorithm nor a hardness reduction. The explicit cascade example shows why the single-application proof requires an additional idea before it can address those instances.
