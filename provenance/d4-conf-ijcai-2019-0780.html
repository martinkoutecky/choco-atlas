The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_o\in[0,1]\) for item classes \(O\), and additive utilities \(u_t(o)\), find \(z_{t,B}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(B\subseteq O\) satisfying \(\sum_B z_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B\ni o}z_{t,B}=\rho_o\), such that for every ordered type pair \(t,s\) and every supported bundles \(B,D\), either \(u_t(B)\ge u_t(D)\) or some \(o\in B\cup D\) satisfies \(u_t(B\setminus\{o\})\ge u_t(D\setminus\{o\})\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

An instance has finite sets of types \(T\) and item classes \(O\), nonnegative rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational supplies \(\rho_o\in[0,1]\), and additive utilities \(u_t(B)=\sum_{o\in B}u_t(o)\).
A continuous allocation is numbers \(z_{t,B}\geq0\), for \(t\in T\) and \(B\subseteq O\), with \(\sum_B z_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B\ni o}z_{t,B}=\rho_o\) for every \(o\in O\). A bundle is supported for type \(t\) when \(z_{t,B}>0\).
It is EF1 if, for every ordered pair \(t,s\) and supported \(B,D\), either \(u_t(B)\geq u_t(D)\), or some \(o\in B\cup D\) satisfies \(u_t(B\setminus\{o\})\geq u_t(D\setminus\{o\})\).

Target

Is feasibility of a continuous EF1 allocation in \(P\), or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results

For finite agents and indivisible items with additive utilities of either sign, a complete EF1 allocation can be computed in polynomial time by double round robin.

Status and scope

I do not settle the general complexity classification. I establish three partial results:

  • With binary-encoded rational utilities, feasibility belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).
  • Feasibility is fixed-parameter tractable in the number of item classes.
  • If all utility vectors are scalar multiples of one common vector, allowing negative scalars, feasibility has a simple polynomial-time characterization.

The complexity statements assume that the item utilities are rational and encoded in binary. The existence result in Section 4 also holds for real utilities.

1. An exact pairwise condition

For bundles \(B,D\subseteq O\), define

\(\Delta_t(B,D)=u_t(D)-u_t(B)\)

and

\(h_t(B,D)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{u_t(o):o\in D\setminus B\}\cup\{-u_t(o):o\in B\setminus D\}\bigr).\)

Lemma. The ordered comparison from type \(t\), holding \(B\), to bundle \(D\) satisfies EF1 exactly when

\(\Delta_t(B,D)\leq h_t(B,D).\)

Proof. If \(\Delta_t(B,D)\leq 0\), there is no envy. Otherwise, removing an item \(o\) from both bundles changes the envy gap to

\(\Delta_t(B,D)-u_t(o)\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{o\in D}-\mathbf{1}_{o\in B}\bigr).\)

Thus removing an item of \(D\setminus B\) reduces the gap by \(u_t(o)\), and removing an item of \(B\setminus D\) reduces it by \(-u_t(o)\). Removing a shared item leaves the gap unchanged. The claimed condition follows.

In particular, whether two labeled bundles are compatible can be checked in polynomial time.

2. Polynomial-size certificates

Theorem. Every feasible instance has an EF1 allocation with at most \(|T|+|O|\) positive variables. These positive variables can be chosen rational with polynomial encoding length. Consequently, feasibility belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Proof. Let \(z\) be any feasible EF1 allocation, possibly with irrational entries, and let

\(S=\{(t,B):z_{t,B}>0\}.\)

Restrict the allocation variables to the labeled bundles in \(S\). The resulting allocation polytope is

\(P_S=\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{S}:Ax=b\},\)

where \(b\) consists of the masses and supplies. The column associated with \((t,B)\) has a \(1\) in type row \(t\), a \(1\) in each item row belonging to \(B\), and zeros elsewhere.

This polytope is nonempty because it contains \(z\). It is bounded because each variable associated with type \(t\) is at most \(\mu_t\). Hence it has an extreme point \(x\).

Every allocation in \(P_S\) is EF1: its support is a subset of \(S\), and deleting supported bundles cannot introduce a new pairwise violation.

Let \(J\) denote the positive coordinates of \(x\). The columns \(A_J\) are linearly independent. Otherwise, a nonzero vector \(d\) supported on \(J\) would satisfy \(Ad=0\). For sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), both \(x+\varepsilon d\) and \(x-\varepsilon d\) would be nonnegative and belong to \(P_S\), contradicting extremality. Therefore

\(|J|\leq \operatorname{rank}(A)\leq |T|+|O|.\)

It remains to bound the encoding length. Put \(k=|J|\). Choose \(k\) rows on which the columns \(A_J\) form an invertible matrix \(C\). The positive coordinates solve

\(Cx_J=b',\)

where \(C\) is a square zero-one matrix and \(b'\) is a subvector of the rational input.

Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators appearing in \(b\). Its binary length is at most the total encoding length of those denominators. Cramer's rule gives

\((x_J)_j=\dfrac{\det C_j(Qb')}{Q\det C},\)

where \(C_j(Qb')\) replaces column \(j\) of \(C\) by \(Qb'\). The determinant expansion yields

\(|\det C|\leq k!\)

and

\(|\det C_j(Qb')|\leq k!\max\{1,\|Qb'\|_\infty\}.\)

Thus each coordinate has polynomial encoding length.

A certificate lists these labeled bundles and their positive masses. A verifier checks the allocation equalities and all ordered comparisons between listed bundles using Section 1. There are polynomially many comparisons, and all arithmetic involves polynomial-length rational numbers. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

3. An exact algorithm parameterized by item classes

Theorem. Writing \(m=|O|\), feasibility can be decided in time \(f(m)\operatorname{poly}(L)\), where \(L\) is the input length.

Proof. Enumerate every family of bundles

\(\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^O.\)

There are \(2^{2^m}\) such families. Interpret \(\mathcal{F}\) as a candidate family containing every bundle that will be supported by any type.

For each type \(t\), define its admissible bundles by

\(\mathcal{A}_t(\mathcal{F})=\{B\in\mathcal{F}:\Delta_t(B,D)\leq h_t(B,D)\text{ for every }D\in\mathcal{F}\}.\)

For this family, solve the rational linear feasibility problem with variables \(x_{t,B}\geq 0\), for \(B\in\mathcal{A}_t(\mathcal{F})\), subject to

\(\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathcal{A}_t(\mathcal{F})}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t\in T,\)

and

\(\displaystyle\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{\substack{B\in\mathcal{A}_t(\mathcal{F})\\o\in B}}x_{t,B}=\rho_o\quad\text{for every }o\in O.\)

Accept if any of these linear programs is feasible.

For soundness, every supported bundle belongs to \(\mathcal{F}\). If \(B\) is supported for \(t\), its admissibility ensures the required comparison with every supported bundle \(D\).

For completeness, suppose an EF1 allocation exists. Choose \(\mathcal{F}\) to be exactly the union of its supported bundles over all types. Every bundle supported for \(t\) then belongs to \(\mathcal{A}_t(\mathcal{F})\), so that allocation satisfies the corresponding linear program.

Each program has at most \(|T|2^m\) variables and \(|T|+m\) equality constraints. Constructing it takes at most \(|T|4^m\) pairwise checks. Its rational encoding length is bounded by a function of \(m\) times a polynomial in \(L\). Polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore gives the asserted running time.

This proves polynomial-time solvability when the number of item classes is fixed, but not when it is unrestricted.

4. Complete characterization for collinear utility vectors

Put

\(M=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t.\)

Theorem. Suppose there is an additive function \(v\) and scalars \(\alpha_t\in\mathbb{R}\) such that

\(u_t(o)=\alpha_t v(o)\quad\text{for every }t\in T,\ o\in O.\)

The scalars may be positive, negative, or zero. An EF1 allocation exists if and only if

\(\rho_o\leq M\quad\text{for every }o\in O.\)

For rational input, this gives a polynomial-time feasibility test.

4.1 Necessity and ordinary feasibility

Every allocation satisfies

\(\displaystyle\rho_o=\sum_{t,B\ni o}z_{t,B}\leq\sum_{t,B}z_{t,B}=M.\)

Conversely, suppose these inequalities hold. If \(M=0\), every supply is zero, and the zero allocation is EF1.

Assume \(M>0\), and put \(p_o=\rho_o/M\). Define a probability distribution on bundles by

\(\displaystyle q_B=\prod_{o\in B}p_o\prod_{o\notin B}(1-p_o).\)

The allocation \(z_{t,B}=\mu_tq_B\) has the prescribed type masses and supplies. Thus the polytope \(P\) of allocations satisfying the mass and supply constraints is nonempty. It is also compact, since it is closed and every coordinate lies between zero and its type mass.

4.2 Choose an allocation by two successive optimizations

Let \(c_t=\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha_t)\). First maximize

\(\displaystyle W(z)=\sum_{t,B}c_t v(B)z_{t,B}\)

over \(P\). Among its maximizers, minimize

\(\displaystyle Q(z)=\sum_{t,B}v(B)^2z_{t,B}.\)

Both objectives are continuous functions on finite-dimensional compact sets, so such an allocation \(z^*\) exists. These optimizations are used only for the existence proof.

We prove that \(z^*\) is EF1.

4.3 Types with different signs do not envy

Let \(B\) be supported for \(t\), and \(D\) be supported for \(s\), with \(c_t\neq c_s\).

Exchange an equal positive amount \(\varepsilon\) of these two bundles between the types. Choosing \(\varepsilon\) no larger than either supporting mass makes this a feasible allocation: each type retains its mass, and aggregate item supplies remain unchanged.

The change in \(W\) is

\(\varepsilon(c_t-c_s)(v(D)-v(B)).\)

Optimality of \(W\) therefore implies

\((c_t-c_s)(v(B)-v(D))\geq 0.\)

If \(c_t=1\), then \(c_s<1\), so \(v(B)\geq v(D)\), and consequently \(u_t(B)\geq u_t(D)\).

If \(c_t=-1\), then \(c_s>-1\), so \(v(B)\leq v(D)\). Multiplication by the negative scalar \(\alpha_t\) again gives \(u_t(B)\geq u_t(D)\).

If \(c_t=0\), all utilities of type \(t\) are zero. Thus every ordered comparison between different sign groups is envy-free.

4.4 Comparisons within one nonzero sign group satisfy EF1

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that an ordered comparison from \((t,B)\) to \((s,D)\) violates EF1, with

\(c_t=c_s=c\in\{-1,1\}.\)

Define \(w=cv\). Then \(u_t=|\alpha_t|w\), so multiplying utilities by the positive constant \(|\alpha_t|\) does not change EF1. In particular,

\(\delta=w(D)-w(B)>0.\)

By Section 1, failure of EF1 implies that every positive quantity of either of the following forms is strictly less than \(\delta\):

  • \(w(o)\), for \(o\in D\setminus B\);
  • \(-w(o)\), for \(o\in B\setminus D\).

At least one such positive quantity exists, because

\(\displaystyle\delta=\sum_{o\in D\setminus B}w(o)-\sum_{o\in B\setminus D}w(o)>0.\)

Choose one and call it \(r\). Thus \(0<r<\delta\).

If \(r=w(o)>0\) for \(o\in D\setminus B\), replace equal positive masses of \(B,D\) by

\(B'=B\cup\{o\},\qquad D'=D\setminus\{o\}.\)

If \(r=-w(o)>0\) for \(o\in B\setminus D\), instead use

\(B'=B\setminus\{o\},\qquad D'=D\cup\{o\}.\)

In either case, retain each modified bundle with its original type. This preserves all type masses and item supplies, and

\(w(B')=w(B)+r,\qquad w(D')=w(D)-r.\)

The value of \(W\) remains unchanged because both types have sign \(c\). Also, \(v(X)^2=w(X)^2\) for every bundle \(X\). Consequently, the change in \(Q\), for exchanged mass \(\varepsilon>0\), is

\(\varepsilon\bigl((w(B)+r)^2+(w(D)-r)^2-w(B)^2-w(D)^2\bigr)=2\varepsilon r(r-\delta)<0.\)

This contradicts the choice of \(z^*\).

Types with zero utility never envy. Together with Section 4.3, this proves that \(z^*\) is EF1 and completes the theorem.

For rational input, collinearity itself can be checked in polynomial time: choose any nonzero utility vector as \(v\), determine each candidate scalar from a nonzero coordinate of \(v\), and check all coordinates. If every utility vector is zero, the characterization applies directly.

5. Why the stated finite-agent theorem does not settle the problem

The definition here disallows resolving envy by deleting only the other agent’s copy of a shared class. For nonnegative utilities, this is the distinction captured by fairness “without commons” in the literature on allocations with copies. See [Gafni, Huang, Lavi, and Talgam-Cohen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08671).

A concrete example demonstrates the gap. Consider identical utilities with

\(u(c)=2,\qquad u(a)=u(b)=1,\)

and bundles

\(B=\{c\},\qquad D=\{c,a,b\}.\)

Under the ordinary interpretation with distinct physical copies, deleting the copy of \(c\) from the second bundle leaves value \(2\), equal to the first bundle’s value. The allocation therefore satisfies ordinary EF1.

Under the definition in the question, deleting \(c\) removes it from both bundles, leaving values \(0\) and \(2\). Deleting either \(a\) or \(b\) leaves values \(2\) and \(3\). Thus the ordered comparison from \(B\) to \(D\) violates the required EF1 condition.

This example only disproves a direct transfer of the finite-agent guarantee. It is not an infeasible continuous instance.

6. Remaining question

The arguments above establish membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), an exact algorithm for a bounded number of classes, and a complete characterization for collinear utility vectors.

They provide neither a polynomial-time algorithm nor a hardness reduction for unrestricted utility vectors. That classification remains unresolved in this write-up.
