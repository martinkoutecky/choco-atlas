The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given additive valuation \(v\) over goods \(\mathcal{M}\), find nonnegative rational masses \(q_S\) for \(S \subseteq \mathcal{M}\) satisfying \(\sum_S q_S=1\) and \(\sum_{S\ni g}q_S=1/2\) for every good \(g\), minimizing \(\Psi(q)=\sum_S q_S\bigl(v(S)-v(\mathcal{M})/2\bigr)^2\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given \(m\geq 1\) indivisible goods \(\mathcal M\), an additive valuation specified by nonnegative binary rational item values, and a nonnegative binary rational threshold \(B\), let \(L\) be the total input bit length. Empty bundles and zero-valued goods are allowed; no normalization of \(v(\mathcal M)\) is imposed.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here types are subsets \(S\subseteq\mathcal M\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not \(2^m\). The distribution is sought, and its support is unrestricted rather than supplied as input; running-time parameters are \(m,L\), and \(\tau\) when reporting output costs.
Choose nonnegative rational masses \(q_S\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal M}q_S=1\) and \(\sum_{S\ni g}q_S=1/2\) for every \(g\in\mathcal M\), minimizing \(\Psi(q)=\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal M}q_S\bigl(v(S)-v(\mathcal M)/2\bigr)^2\).

Is deciding whether a feasible \(q\) satisfies \(\Psi(q)\leq B\) solvable in time polynomial in \(m,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Computing an ex-ante proportional distribution minimizing the sum of value variances for two agents with identical additive valuations is NP-hard; ex-ante proportionality requires each agent's expected value to be at least \(v(\mathcal M)/2\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16579v1).
No published complexity classification for the exact item-marginal-constrained problem above was located.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The existential decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when all item values are positive integers and \(B=0\). Moreover, an optimal distribution always exists with exactly two support types.

1. Exact characterization of the optimum

Write \(a_g=v(\{g\})\), \(V=\sum_{g\in\mathcal M}a_g\), and
\[ c(S)=\left(v(S)-\frac V2\right)^2, \qquad c_*=\min_{S\subseteq\mathcal M}c(S). \]
Here and below, the decision question is whether there exists a feasible distribution with objective at most \(B\).

For every feasible distribution \(q\), nonnegativity of its masses and their sum being one imply
\[ \Psi(q)=\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal M}q_Sc(S) \geq \sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal M}q_Sc_* =c_*. \]

Conversely, fix any bundle \(S\), and let \(\overline S=\mathcal M\setminus S\). Define a distribution by assigning mass \(1/2\) to each of \(S\) and \(\overline S\), and zero elsewhere. These bundles are distinct because \(m\geq1\). Every good belongs to exactly one of them, so its marginal probability is exactly \(1/2\). Thus this distribution is feasible.

Additivity gives \(v(\overline S)=V-v(S)\), hence
\[ c(\overline S) =\left(V-v(S)-\frac V2\right)^2 =c(S). \]
The distribution therefore has objective \(c(S)\). Choosing a bundle attaining the finite minimum proves
\[ \boxed{\min_{q\text{ feasible}}\Psi(q) =\min_{S\subseteq\mathcal M} \left(v(S)-\frac V2\right)^2.} \]

In particular, the decision problem is equivalent to asking whether some bundle \(S\) satisfies \(c(S)\leq B\). This argument also covers zero-valued goods and empty bundles.

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate is the incidence vector of such a bundle \(S\), using \(m\) bits. We verify its inequality using exact integer arithmetic.

Write each item value as \(a_g=p_g/d_g\), where \(p_g\geq0\) and \(d_g>0\) are binary integers, and write \(B=b/e\), where \(b\geq0\) and \(e>0\). Set
\[ D=\prod_{g\in\mathcal M}d_g,\qquad A_g=p_g(D/d_g),\qquad T=\sum_{g\in S}A_g,\qquad W=\sum_{g\in\mathcal M}A_g. \]
Then
\[ c(S)=\frac{(2T-W)^2}{4D^2}, \]
so the required comparison is precisely
\[ e(2T-W)^2\leq4D^2b. \]

The bit length of \(D\) is at most the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence is bounded by \(L\). All other integers in this computation have bit length polynomial in \(m,L\). Their construction and comparison consequently take polynomial time.

By the characterization in Section 1, a certificate exists exactly when the instance is a yes-instance. Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), despite having exponentially many possible distribution variables.

3. Polynomial-time reduction from PARTITION

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness of PARTITION: given positive binary integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\), decide whether there is a subset \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) such that
\[ \sum_{i\in I}w_i=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n w_i. \]
This is one of the problems classified in [Karp’s *Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems*](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Construct \(n\) goods with item values \(a_i=w_i\), and set \(B=0\). This construction merely copies the binary integers and adds a constant-size threshold, so it is polynomial-time and satisfies all the task’s input restrictions.

Section 1 gives the equivalences
\[ \begin{aligned} \exists q\text{ feasible with }\Psi(q)\leq0 &\iff \min_{S\subseteq\mathcal M} \left(v(S)-\frac V2\right)^2=0\\ &\iff \exists S\subseteq\mathcal M:\ v(S)=V/2\\ &\iff \text{the PARTITION instance is a yes-instance}. \end{aligned} \]
The first equivalence also uses the nonnegativity of every squared deviation. This proves a polynomial-time many-one reduction and establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness. Together with Section 2, it proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

4. Support representation and the cited result

The hardness persists even though an optimal output can always have support size \(\tau=2\), with both masses equal to \(1/2\). Once an optimal bundle is known, constructing that output requires only \(O(m)\) additional bit operations. Verifying an arbitrary supplied distribution is polynomial in \(m,\tau\), and the combined bit length of the instance and supplied masses. Finding the support is the computational obstacle.

The [cited paper’s Corollary 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16579v1#S3) establishes hardness for ex-ante proportional variance minimization. The direct reduction above establishes the classification under the exact item marginal constraints in this task. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,L\) for this decision problem exists if and only if \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
