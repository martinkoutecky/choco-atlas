The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of complete XOS buyer types \(\Theta\), masses \(\mu\), per-capita item supplies \(q\), the paper’s oracle access, and a benchmark routine \(A_\infty\) returning a feasible mass allocation \(x^A\) with XOS witnesses, compute anonymous prices \(p\) such that the denominator-compatible high-multiplicity market with \(N\mu_\theta\) buyers of each type and \(Nq_j\) perfect-substitute copies of item \(j\), with each buyer receiving an integral bundle \(B\subseteq M\), has limiting per-capita welfare at least \(\frac{1}{2}W(x^A)-\varepsilon\) for every arrival order and demand tie-breaking.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) contain \(m\geq1\) item kinds. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) complete buyer types \(\theta\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_\theta\), with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Supplies \(q_j\geq0\) and accuracy \(\varepsilon>0\) are binary rationals.
Each type has a monotone, normalized XOS valuation \(v_\theta:2^M\to[0,1]\). Buyers may consume at most one copy of each item kind. Value and demand oracles are available; an XOS oracle on \(B\) returns nonnegative rational weights \(a_j\) satisfying \(\sum_{j\in S}a_j\leq v_\theta(S)\) for every \(S\subseteq M\), with equality for \(S=B\).
A benchmark routine \(A_\infty\) returns an explicit sparse list of rational masses \(x^A_{\theta,B}\geq0\) and supporting XOS witnesses, satisfying \(\sum_Bx^A_{\theta,B}\leq\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B:j\in B}x^A_{\theta,B}\leq q_j\). Define \(W(x^A)=\sum_{\theta,B}x^A_{\theta,B}v_\theta(B)\). Let \(L\) include the bit length of all explicit input and benchmark output and a bound on oracle-answer lengths; charge oracle calls and bit operations, with benchmark execution charged separately.
For every positive integer \(N\) making all \(N\mu_\theta\) and \(Nq_j\) integral, form a market with those buyer counts and supplies. A single nonnegative rational price vector \(p\), independent of \(N\), prices every copy of kind \(j\) at \(p_j\). All copies are offered, without withholding or additional sales caps.
Buyers arrive sequentially and choose an integral bundle maximizing \(v_\theta(B)-\sum_{j\in B}p_j\) among currently available kinds, including the empty bundle. Arrival orders \(\pi\) and demand tie-breaking \(t\) are arbitrary and adversarial. Write \(\operatorname{SW}_N(p;\pi,t)\) for total realized welfare and define \(G(p)=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\inf_{\pi,t}\operatorname{SW}_N(p;\pi,t)/N\), along compatible integers.

Can prices satisfying \(G(p)\geq\tfrac12W(x^A)-\varepsilon\) always be computed in oracle time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)\), or is this approximation task NP-hard or its guarantee infeasible on some inputs?

Known discrete results: [Combinatorial Auctions via Posted Prices](https://cs.tau.ac.il/~mfeldman/papers/FGLc15.pdf) gives polynomial-time pricing with expected welfare at least half a supplied allocation benchmark, minus additive error, for independent XOS buyers and unit supplies, under arbitrary arrival orders and demand ties.
[Pricing Multi-unit Markets](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~mfeldman/papers/EFRSc18.pdf) establishes a half-optimum uniform-price guarantee for a single homogeneous good with independent symmetric XOS valuations.
[Posted Pricing sans Discrimination](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0055.pdf) gives a polynomial-time half-benchmark reduction for XOS buyers with convex production costs, choosing both prices and supply caps. These results do not directly state the fixed-supply mass guarantee above; no result settling that exact formulation was located.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result: polynomial-time pricing with an exact half guarantee

The task is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, the supplied benchmark and its supporting witnesses yield prices satisfying
\(\operatorname{SW}_N(p;\pi,t)/N\geq \tfrac12W(x^A)\)
for every compatible positive integer \(N\), every arrival order, and every demand tie-breaking rule.

1. Constructing the prices

For each listed benchmark bundle, write \(a^{\theta,B}_j\) for its supporting witness. Thus
\(\sum_{j\in S}a^{\theta,B}_j\leq v_\theta(S)\)
for every \(S\subseteq M\), with equality for \(S=B\).

Define the benchmark usage and supporting welfare contribution of kind \(j\) by

\[ b_j=\sum_{\theta,B:j\in B}x^A_{\theta,B}, \qquad c_j=\sum_{\theta,B:j\in B}x^A_{\theta,B}a^{\theta,B}_j. \]

Benchmark feasibility gives \(b_j\leq q_j\). Nonnegativity gives \(c_j\geq0\), and witness equality gives

\[ \sum_{j\in M}c_j =\sum_{\theta,B}x^A_{\theta,B}\sum_{j\in B}a^{\theta,B}_j =W(x^A). \]

Set

\[ p_j= \begin{cases} c_j/(2q_j),&q_j>0,\\ 0,&q_j=0. \end{cases} \]

These are nonnegative rational prices, independent of \(N\). If \(q_j=0\), feasibility and nonnegative benchmark masses imply \(b_j=c_j=0\).

2. Utility from kinds that remain available

Fix a compatible \(N\), an arbitrary arrival order, and arbitrary demand ties. Let \(D_i\) be buyer \(i\)'s purchased bundle and let

\[ u_i=v_{\theta(i)}(D_i)-\sum_{j\in D_i}p_j. \]

The empty bundle is feasible, so \(u_i\geq0\).

Let \(R\subseteq M\) contain exactly the kinds having at least one unsold copy after all buyers have arrived. Every kind in \(R\) was available at every earlier arrival: its inventory only decreases.

Consequently, for every buyer \(i\) of type \(\theta\) and every benchmark bundle \(B\), the bundle \(B\cap R\) was feasible when that buyer arrived. Utility maximization and the supporting-witness inequality imply

\[ u_i\geq v_\theta(B\cap R)-\sum_{j\in B\cap R}p_j \geq\sum_{j\in B\cap R}\bigl(a^{\theta,B}_j-p_j\bigr). \]

Multiply this inequality by \(x^A_{\theta,B}\) and sum over \(B\). Because \(u_i\geq0\) and \(\sum_Bx^A_{\theta,B}\leq\mu_\theta\), we obtain

\[ \mu_\theta u_i \geq \sum_Bx^A_{\theta,B} \sum_{j\in B\cap R}\bigl(a^{\theta,B}_j-p_j\bigr). \]

There are exactly \(N\mu_\theta\) buyers of type \(\theta\). Dividing the preceding inequality by the positive \(\mu_\theta\), summing over those buyers, and then summing over types yields

\[ \frac1N\sum_i u_i \geq \sum_{\theta,B}x^A_{\theta,B} \sum_{j\in B\cap R}\bigl(a^{\theta,B}_j-p_j\bigr) = \sum_{j\in R}(c_j-p_jb_j). \]

For \(j\in R\), necessarily \(q_j>0\). Therefore

\[ c_j-p_jb_j =c_j\left(1-\frac{b_j}{2q_j}\right) \geq \frac{c_j}{2}, \]

using \(b_j\leq q_j\). Hence

\[ \frac1N\sum_i u_i\geq\frac12\sum_{j\in R}c_j. \]

The benchmark masses need not become integral after multiplication by \(N\): they are used only as weights in these utility comparisons.

3. Revenue from exhausted kinds

Let \(s_j\) denote the number of copies of kind \(j\) sold, divided by \(N\). The normalized revenue is

\[ \frac{\operatorname{Rev}_N}{N}=\sum_jp_js_j. \]

For every \(j\notin R\), all \(Nq_j\) copies have been sold, so \(s_j=q_j\). Nonnegative prices therefore imply

\[ \frac{\operatorname{Rev}_N}{N} \geq\sum_{j\notin R}p_jq_j =\frac12\sum_{j\notin R}c_j. \]

The last equality also holds for zero-supply kinds because both sides then contribute zero.

Welfare equals total buyer utility plus revenue. Combining the two bounds gives

\[ \frac{\operatorname{SW}_N(p;\pi,t)}{N} = \frac{\sum_i u_i+\operatorname{Rev}_N}{N} \geq \frac12\sum_{j\in M}c_j = \frac12W(x^A). \]

Every demand maximizer satisfies the utility comparisons above. Thus the proof allows adversarial ties, including ties with the empty bundle, and arbitrary arrival orders.

Since the bound holds for every compatible \(N\), taking the infimum over orders and ties and then the prescribed limit inferior gives

\[ G(p)\geq\frac12W(x^A). \]

All copies are offered throughout this argument, subject only to actual depletion.

4. Computational complexity and relation to the discrete results

Let \(K\) be the number of benchmark records. Computing the contributions \(c_j\) requires at most \(O(Km)\) rational arithmetic operations; constructing the prices then requires at most \(m\) divisions. Since all benchmark masses, witnesses, and supplies are explicitly encoded within length \(L\), the intermediate and output rational numbers have polynomial bit length. Standard exact rational arithmetic therefore gives total bit complexity polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

After the benchmark returns its stipulated witnesses, no additional oracle calls are needed. Benchmark execution is charged separately, as requested. The construction is consequently polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\), and achieves the requested bound without any additive loss.

The supporting-welfare decomposition and utility-plus-revenue argument are also central to [Lemma 3.1 of *Combinatorial Auctions via Posted Prices*](https://cs.tau.ac.il/~mfeldman/papers/FGLc15.pdf). Here the explicit benchmark and exact type counts permit the direct deterministic proof above.
