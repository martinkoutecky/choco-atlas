Proposition C.6 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support of worker types \(t\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), and for each type a task count \(n_t\) and a binary self-predicting signal channel, choose an integer quota \(n_0(t)\in\{0,\ldots,n_t\}\) for each type to maximize \(\sum_t \mu_t\,\delta_t(n_0(t))\), where \(\delta_t(k)\) is that type's EA sensitivity at quota \(k\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) worker types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
Each type has a positive binary-encoded task count \(n_t\) and a binary-rational joint signal distribution \(J_t\) on \(\{0,1\}^2\). Write \(a_{t,i}=\sum_jJ_{t,ij}>0\), \(b_{t,j}=\sum_iJ_{t,ij}\), and \(p_{t,ij}=J_{t,ij}/a_{t,i}\); require \(p_{t,00}>p_{t,10}\) and \(p_{t,11}>p_{t,01}\) (self-prediction).
At effort \(e\in[0,1]\), the \(n_t\) signal pairs are independent with joint law \(eJ_t+(1-e)a_tb_t^{\mathsf T}\). Both workers report their signals; the peer always exerts full effort.
For quota \(k\in\{0,\ldots,n_t\}\), enforced agreement (EA) changes only the worker's reports: if their zero count \(r\) exceeds \(k\), flip a uniformly chosen subset of \(r-k\) zeros to ones; if \(r<k\), flip a uniformly chosen subset of \(k-r\) ones to zeros. Then apply output agreement against the peer's unchanged reports, obtaining score \(S_t(k,e)\).
Define \(\delta_t(k)=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial e}\mathbb E[S_t(k,e)]\right|_{e=1^-}/\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(S_t(k,1))}\), with expectation and variance over signals and enforcement randomness; restrict to instances with positive denominator for every feasible quota.
Choose one integer quota \(n_0(t)\) per type to maximize \(\sum_t\mu_t\delta_t(n_0(t))\); any maximizing vector is allowed. Let \(m=2\) be the signal alphabet size and \(L\) the total binary input length, including task counts.

Is computing a maximizing quota vector polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite binary tasks, a binomial-quantile characterization of sensitivity-maximizing EA enforcement is stated by [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02259v2#Ax3.SS3), without a bit-complexity bound.
No result settling this support-encoded mass optimization problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive masses to sum to one, interpreting the society as a distribution over types.
  • Specified the signal channel by a rational joint distribution, including the worker's prior, with positive worker signal marginals and strict self-prediction; equal worker and peer marginals are not required.
  • Evaluated sensitivity at full effort using the left derivative, consistent with the full-effort sensitivity evaluations.
  • Included both directions of minimal random enforcement and left the peer's reports unchanged.
  • Used the unconditional score variance and admitted only instances where sensitivity's denominator is positive for every quota.
  • Encoded positive task counts in binary and included them in the total input length.
  • Kept one integer quota per type and allowed any maximizing vector when quotas tie.

Partial result: exact optimization for symmetric worker marginals

The general binary-input complexity classification remains unresolved. I prove a nontrivial special case:

If every listed type has worker zero marginal $a_{t,0}=1/2$, then
$$ n_0(t)=\left\lfloor\frac{n_t}{2}\right\rfloor $$
is a maximizing vector, computable in polynomial time in the binary input length.

This requires no symmetry of the joint distribution $J_t$. For an even task count, the central quota is uniquely optimal; for an odd task count, exactly the two central quotas maximize sensitivity.

I also prove a comparative-statics result for the general single-type objective.

1. Population optimization separates

For each type $t$, let
$$ M_t=\max_{0\leq k\leq n_t}\delta_t(k). $$
These maxima exist because the feasible sets are finite. Every feasible vector satisfies
$$ \sum_t\mu_t\delta_t(n_0(t)) \leq \sum_t\mu_tM_t. $$
Since every $\mu_t$ is positive, equality holds if and only if every chosen coordinate maximizes its own type’s sensitivity.

Thus the support-encoded population problem reduces to one independent optimization per listed type. Conversely, the single-type problem is the population problem with $\tau=1$ and mass $1$. The masses introduce no coupling.

2. A self-contained derivation of the single-type objective

Suppress the type index and write
$$ a=a_0,\qquad u=p_{00},\qquad v=p_{10},\qquad d=u-v. $$
The hypotheses give $0<a<1$ and $d>0$. The peer’s zero marginal is
$$ b=au+(1-a)v=v+ad. $$

Let $A_k(e)$ be the total number of agreements after enforcement. Dividing this score by $n$ scales its expectation derivative and standard deviation by the same positive factor, so it preserves sensitivity.

Conditional score moments

Let $R$ be the worker’s observed zero count. The effort mixture preserves the worker marginal, so
$$ R\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,a) $$
at every effort level.

At full effort, conditional on $R=r$, the agreement count has distribution
$$ A_k(1)\mid R=r\sim \begin{cases} \operatorname{Bin}(r,u)+\operatorname{Bin}(k-r,v) +\operatorname{Bin}(n-k,1-v),&r\leq k,\\ \operatorname{Bin}(k,u)+\operatorname{Bin}(r-k,1-u) +\operatorname{Bin}(n-r,1-v),&r\geq k, \end{cases} $$
where the summands are independent.

Indeed, condition first on the complete worker signal vector and the chosen flips. The peer signals are independent across tasks. The three groups have the displayed sizes and agreement probabilities. Every conditioning with the same $r$ yields the same agreement-count distribution, so averaging over positions and enforcement randomness preserves this law.

Define
$$ H_k(r)=2\min(r,k)-r=k-|r-k|. $$
Expanding the conditional means gives
$$ \mathbb E[A_k(1)\mid R=r] =n(1-v)+(2v-1)k+dH_k(r). $$
Complementary Bernoulli probabilities have equal variance, so
$$ \operatorname{Var}(A_k(1)\mid R=r) =ru(1-u)+(n-r)v(1-v). $$
Consequently, the law of total variance gives
$$ \operatorname{Var}(A_k(1)) =C+d^2w_k, $$
where
$$ C=n\bigl(au(1-u)+(1-a)v(1-v)\bigr), \qquad w_k=\operatorname{Var}(H_k(R)) =\operatorname{Var}(|R-k|). $$

These variances are positive. Both $R=0$ and $R=1$ have positive probability, while $H_k(0)=0$ and $H_k(1)$ is either $1$ or $-1$. Thus $w_k>0$, and $d>0$ implies positive score variance.

Expectation derivative

At effort $e$, the conditional peer-zero probabilities are
$$ u(e)=b+e(1-a)d,\qquad v(e)=b-ead. $$
Substitution into the conditional mean, followed by averaging over $R$, gives
$$ \mathbb E[A_k(e)] =n(1-b)+(2b-1)k+2edg_k, $$
where
$$ g_k=\mathbb E[\min(R,k)]-ak. $$
The expectation is affine in $e$, so its left derivative at full effort is $2dg_k$.

Setting
$$ \lambda=\frac{C}{d^2}\geq0 $$
therefore yields
$$ \boxed{\delta(k)=\frac{2g_k}{\sqrt{\lambda+w_k}}.} $$

For completeness, $g_0=g_n=0$, and $g_k>0$ for every interior quota when $n\geq2$. To prove this, use
$$ \min(r,k)\geq\frac{kr}{n}. $$
For $0<k<n$, this inequality is strict whenever $0<r<n$. Taking expectations and using $\mathbb E[R]=na$ proves the assertion. When $n=1$, both sensitivities vanish.

Folded-binomial representation

Put
$$ f_k=\mathbb E[|R-k|],\qquad \sigma^2=na(1-a). $$
Since $|R-k|^2=(R-k)^2$,
$$ w_k=\sigma^2+(k-na)^2-f_k^2. $$
Also,
$$ 2g_k=na+(1-2a)k-f_k. $$
In particular, when $a=1/2$,
$$ \boxed{g_k=\frac n4-\frac{f_k}{2}.} $$

The variance term genuinely depends on the quota. For example, take $n=2$ and
$$ J=\frac12 \begin{pmatrix} 1&0\\ 0&1 \end{pmatrix}. $$
Then $d=1$, $C=0$, and
$$ \operatorname{Var}(A_0(1))=\frac12, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(A_1(1))=\frac14. $$
Thus the quota-independent-variance assertion underlying the cited characterization does not apply to this model. [Zhang et al., Lemma C.5 and Appendix F.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02259v2#Ax6.SS6)

3. A tail bound for finite log-concave sequences

The following elementary lemma supplies the key variance comparison.

Lemma. Let $\pi_0,\ldots,\pi_M$ be positive, where $M\geq1$, and suppose the ratios
$$ \frac{\pi_{j+1}}{\pi_j} $$
are nonincreasing in $j$. Define
$$ T_j=\sum_{i=j}^M\pi_i,\qquad T_{M+1}=0,\qquad \rho=\frac{T_1}{T_0}. $$
Let $Y$ have probabilities $\Pr(Y=j)=\pi_j/T_0$. For every integer $0\leq l<M$,
$$ \mathbb E[Y-l\mid Y>l]<\frac1{1-\rho}. $$

Proof. For $0\leq j<M$,
$$ \frac{T_j}{\pi_j} = 1+\sum_{h=1}^{M-j} \prod_{i=0}^{h-1}\frac{\pi_{j+i+1}}{\pi_{j+i}}. $$
Each product is nonincreasing when $j$ increases, and the sum then contains fewer terms. Hence $T_j/\pi_j$ is nonincreasing. Therefore $\pi_j/T_j$ is nondecreasing, and
$$ \frac{T_{j+1}}{T_j} =1-\frac{\pi_j}{T_j} \leq\rho. $$
Iterating gives
$$ \frac{T_{l+h}}{T_{l+1}}\leq\rho^{h-1}. $$
The tail-sum identity for a positive integer-valued random variable now gives
$$ \mathbb E[Y-l\mid Y>l] = \sum_{h=1}^{M-l}\frac{T_{l+h}}{T_{l+1}} \leq \sum_{h=1}^{M-l}\rho^{h-1} < \sum_{h=1}^{\infty}\rho^{h-1} = \frac1{1-\rho}. $$
The strict inequality holds because $0<\rho<1$ and the support is finite. This proves the lemma.

4. Folded-binomial variance increases away from the center

Proposition. Let $R\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,1/2)$, with $n\geq2$. Then
$$ w_{k+1}>w_k \qquad \left(\left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil\leq k<n\right), $$
where $w_k=\operatorname{Var}(|R-k|)$. Moreover, $w_{n-k}=w_k$.

Proof. Set
$$ X=R-\frac n2. $$
The distribution of $X$ is symmetric, which immediately gives $w_{n-k}=w_k$.

For an outward step, write
$$ c=k-\frac n2\geq0,\qquad q=\Pr(X>c),\qquad s=1-2q, $$
and define
$$ F(c)=\mathbb E[|X-c|],\qquad B_c=\mathbb E[X-c-\tfrac12\mid X>c]. $$
Here $q>0$. By symmetry,
$$ s=\Pr(-c\leq X\leq c)>0. $$

There are no support points strictly between $c$ and $c+1$. Thus
$$ F(c+1)-F(c)=1-2q=s. $$
Since $\mathbb E[X]=0$,
$$ F(c) =c+2\mathbb E[(X-c)_+] =c+q+2qB_c. $$
Using
$$ \operatorname{Var}(|X-c|) =\operatorname{Var}(X)+c^2-F(c)^2, $$
we obtain
$$ \begin{aligned} w_{k+1}-w_k &=2c+1-2sF(c)-s^2\\ &=4q\left(c+\frac12-sB_c\right). \end{aligned} $$
It remains to prove that the expression in parentheses is positive.

Even task count

Suppose $n=2M$. Define
$$ \pi_j=\Pr(X=j) =2^{-2M}\binom{2M}{M+j}, \qquad 0\leq j\leq M, $$
and put $p_*=\pi_0$. The ratios
$$ \frac{\pi_{j+1}}{\pi_j} =\frac{M-j}{M+j+1} $$
are decreasing, and $\pi_j\leq p_*$.

Symmetry gives
$$ T_0=\frac{1+p_*}{2}, \qquad T_1=\frac{1-p_*}{2}. $$
Here $c=l$ for an integer $0\leq l<M$. Applying the lemma to the positive-side index yields
$$ B_c < \frac1{1-T_1/T_0}-\frac12 = \frac1{2p_*}. $$
Also,
$$ s=\pi_0+2\sum_{j=1}^l\pi_j \leq(2l+1)p_*=(2c+1)p_*. $$
Therefore
$$ sB_c<\frac{s}{2p_*}\leq c+\frac12. $$

Odd task count

Suppose $n=2M+1$. Since $n\geq2$, this case has $M\geq1$. Define
$$ \pi_j=\Pr(X=j+\tfrac12) =2^{-n}\binom n{M+1+j}, \qquad 0\leq j\leq M, $$
and again put $p_*=\pi_0$. Now
$$ \frac{\pi_{j+1}}{\pi_j} =\frac{M-j}{M+j+2} $$
is decreasing, and $\pi_j\leq p_*$. Symmetry gives
$$ T_0=\frac12,\qquad T_1=\frac12-p_*. $$

Here $c=l+1/2$ for an integer $0\leq l<M$. The lemma gives
$$ B_c < \frac1{1-T_1/T_0}-\frac12 = \frac1{2p_*}-\frac12 < \frac1{2p_*}. $$
Furthermore,
$$ s=2\sum_{j=0}^l\pi_j \leq2(l+1)p_*=(2c+1)p_*. $$
Again,
$$ sB_c<c+\frac12. $$

In both cases, the variance-difference formula gives $w_{k+1}-w_k>0$. This proves the proposition.

5. Exact maximizing quotas for symmetric worker marginals

Theorem. Suppose $a=1/2$. For every permitted self-predicting joint distribution:

  • if $n$ is even, the unique maximizing quota is $n/2$;
  • if $n$ is odd, exactly the quotas $(n-1)/2$ and $(n+1)/2$ maximize sensitivity.

Proof. For $n=1$, both feasible quotas have zero sensitivity, giving the asserted pair.

Assume $n\geq2$. Reflection of the symmetric binomial distribution gives
$$ f_{n-k}=f_k. $$
Hence both $g_k=n/4-f_k/2$ and $w_k$ are symmetric about $n/2$.

For every integer $k\geq\lceil n/2\rceil$ with $k<n$, the preceding proof gives
$$ f_{k+1}-f_k=s>0. $$
Therefore
$$ g_{k+1}<g_k. $$
The proposition simultaneously gives
$$ w_{k+1}>w_k. $$
All denominators are positive, and $g_k>0$ at interior quotas. Consequently,
$$ \delta(k+1)^2 = \frac{4g_{k+1}^2}{\lambda+w_{k+1}} < \frac{4g_k^2}{\lambda+w_k} = \delta(k)^2. $$
This also covers the final step to quota $n$, whose numerator is zero. Symmetry proves strict decrease on both sides away from the central quota or central pair.

This establishes the theorem.

For a society in which every listed type satisfies $a_{t,0}=1/2$, separability now proves that choosing
$$ n_0(t)=\left\lfloor\frac{n_t}{2}\right\rfloor $$
for every type is optimal. Binary integer division by $2$ takes polynomial time in the encoding length. No binomial probabilities or sensitivity comparisons need to be evaluated.

Thus this subclass is polynomial-time solvable even when the numerical task counts are exponentially large relative to their encodings.

6. Comparative statics for arbitrary worker marginals

The normalized objective also has a useful property without symmetry.

Proposition. Fix $n\geq2$ and $0<a<1$. For $\lambda\geq0$, let
$$ F_\lambda(k)=\frac{g_k^2}{\lambda+w_k}. $$
If $0\leq\lambda_1<\lambda_2$ and $k_i$ is any maximizer of $F_{\lambda_i}$, then
$$ g_{k_2}\geq g_{k_1}, \qquad w_{k_2}\geq w_{k_1}. $$

Proof. Maximizers are interior because interior values are positive and endpoint values vanish. Set
$$ A_i=g_{k_i}^2>0,\qquad W_i=w_{k_i}>0. $$
Optimality is equivalently expressed by
$$ \frac{\lambda_1+W_1}{A_1} \leq \frac{\lambda_1+W_2}{A_2}, \qquad \frac{\lambda_2+W_2}{A_2} \leq \frac{\lambda_2+W_1}{A_1}. $$
Adding these inequalities and cancelling the variance terms yields
$$ (\lambda_2-\lambda_1) \left(\frac1{A_2}-\frac1{A_1}\right)\leq0. $$
Thus $A_2\geq A_1$, and positivity of the $g$ values gives $g_{k_2}\geq g_{k_1}$.

The first optimality inequality additionally gives
$$ W_2 \geq \frac{A_2}{A_1}W_1 +\lambda_1\left(\frac{A_2}{A_1}-1\right) \geq W_1. $$
This proves the proposition, including arbitrary choices among tied maximizers.

7. Remaining open question

The results above settle the binary-encoded problem for symmetric worker marginals and establish an ordering property as the quota-independent variance parameter changes.

They do not classify the general problem with arbitrary $a$. The remaining single-type optimization is
$$ \max_{k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}} \frac{\bigl(\mathbb E[\min(R,k)]-ak\bigr)^2} {\lambda+\operatorname{Var}(|R-k|)}, \qquad R\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,a), $$
with binary-encoded $n$.

No polynomial-time algorithm in the binary input length, and no valid hardness reduction for this general optimization, is established here.
