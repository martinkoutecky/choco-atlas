The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given goods \(G\), finite buyer and seller type sets, and rational masses \(\mu\) over those types, with each type specifying per-agent valuations or reservation prices and integral capacities, compute a common nonnegative price vector \(p\) and a nonnegative type-level trade flow \(x_{ajk}\). Require each type’s average bundle to maximize its individual utility at \(p\), subject to its per-agent capacity, and require the flow to respect type masses and clear each good’s market. Equivalently, find a welfare-maximizing feasible flow supported by such equilibrium prices.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) goods, and let \(B,S\) be disjoint nonempty buyer and seller type sets. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|B|+|S|\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Buyer type \(a\) has constant marginal valuations \(v_{aj}\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) and total per-agent capacity \(d_a\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Seller type \(k\) has reservation prices \(r_{jk}\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) and per-agent stocks \(q_{jk}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). Require \(\sum_k\mu_kq_{jk}>0\) for every good. All numerical inputs are binary encoded; let \(L\) be their total bit length.
A feasible aggregate flow \(x_{ajk}\ge0\) satisfies \(\sum_{j,k}x_{ajk}\le\mu_ad_a\) and \(\sum_ax_{ajk}\le\mu_kq_{jk}\). Define average purchases \(b_{aj}=\mu_a^{-1}\sum_kx_{ajk}\) and average sales \(s_{jk}=\mu_k^{-1}\sum_ax_{ajk}\).
At common prices \(p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{m}\), require each \(b_a\) to maximize \(\sum_j(v_{aj}-p_j)b_j\) over \(b\ge0,\ \sum_jb_j\le d_a\), and each \(s_k\) to maximize \(\sum_j(p_j-r_{jk})s_j\) over \(0\le s_j\le q_{jk}\). Quantities are real; any maximizer at a tie is allowed. Individual optimization uses only these per-agent constraints. The same flow records purchases and sales, so each good clears; unsold stock is allowed.
Compute such an equilibrium pair \((p,x)\), or report that none exists. Welfare is \(\sum_{a,j,k}(v_{aj}-r_{jk})x_{ajk}\).

Is exact computation of this continuous equilibrium solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite agents and integral trades, equilibrium prices are characterized by linear inequalities expressing individual optimality at a fixed welfare-maximizing assignment; Jaume, Massó and Neme also establish equilibrium existence and computation using primal and dual LPs. [Discrete characterization](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/190614), [discrete computation](https://bse.eu/sites/default/files/working_paper_pdfs/389revised.pdf).
Sotomayor establishes equilibrium existence in a different finite-agent model with divisible labor and zero seller reservation prices. No result settling the exact rational-support problem above was identified. [Divisible-labor model](https://www.repec.eae.fea.usp.br/documentos/MarildaSotomayor29WP.pdf).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize the positive masses jointly over buyer and seller types to sum to one.
  • Use the equilibrium notion based solely on individual capacities, matching the graded question; individual demands and supplies have no aggregate-market bounds.
  • Specify nonnegative rational constant marginal valuations and reservation prices, positive integral buyer capacities, nonnegative integral seller stocks, and positive aggregate stock of every good.
  • Interpret flow entries as aggregate traded quantities and obtain each type's average bundle by dividing its incident flow by its mass; average quantities are real.
  • Interpret market clearing as equality of traded purchases and sales, allowing unsold stock and unused buyer capacity.
  • Allow every utility-maximizing choice at ties, including partial trade at zero net utility.
  • Require exact equilibrium computation, with a no-equilibrium report if necessary, and measure running time using the binary input length.

Result. Exact equilibrium computation is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Every instance satisfying the stated hypotheses has an equilibrium with rational prices and rational flows. The equilibrium constructed below also maximizes welfare.

1. The welfare LP and its dual.

Write \(D_a=\mu_a d_a\), \(Q_{jk}=\mu_k q_{jk}\), and \(c_{ajk}=v_{aj}-r_{jk}\). Consider the primal linear program

\( \displaystyle \text{maximize }\sum_{a,j,k}c_{ajk}x_{ajk} \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle \sum_{j,k}x_{ajk}\le D_a\quad(a\in B),\qquad \sum_a x_{ajk}\le Q_{jk}\quad(j\in G,\ k\in S),\qquad x_{ajk}\ge0. \)

Its dual is

\( \displaystyle \text{minimize }\sum_a D_a u_a+\sum_{j,k}Q_{jk}w_{jk} \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle u_a+w_{jk}\ge v_{aj}-r_{jk}\quad(a\in B,\ j\in G,\ k\in S),\qquad u_a,w_{jk}\ge0. \)

The primal is feasible because the zero flow is feasible. Its feasible region is closed and bounded: each coordinate satisfies \(0\le x_{ajk}\le Q_{jk}\). Therefore it attains a finite optimum.

The dual is feasible: setting every \(u_a\) equal to \(V=\max_{a,j}v_{aj}\) and every \(w_{jk}=0\) satisfies its constraints, since reservations are nonnegative. Strong LP duality consequently gives optimal solutions \(x\) and \((u,w)\) with equal objective values.

For completeness, their duality gap expands as

\( \displaystyle 0= \sum_a u_a\left(D_a-\sum_{j,k}x_{ajk}\right) +\sum_{j,k}w_{jk}\left(Q_{jk}-\sum_a x_{ajk}\right) +\sum_{a,j,k}x_{ajk}\left(u_a+w_{jk}-v_{aj}+r_{jk}\right). \)

Every summand is nonnegative by primal and dual feasibility. Hence each vanishes. In particular,

\( \displaystyle u_a\left(D_a-\sum_{j,k}x_{ajk}\right)=0, \)

\( \displaystyle w_{jk}\left(Q_{jk}-\sum_a x_{ajk}\right)=0, \)

and

\( \displaystyle x_{ajk}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ u_a+w_{jk}=v_{aj}-r_{jk}. \)

These statements hold for any pair of primal and dual optimal solutions.

2. Recovering common prices.

Define, for every good,

\( \displaystyle p_j=\max\left\{0,\ \max_{a\in B}(v_{aj}-u_a)\right\}. \)

These prices are nonnegative and depend only on the good.

Their definition gives

\( \displaystyle v_{aj}-p_j\le u_a\qquad(a\in B,\ j\in G). \)

Dual feasibility also gives \(v_{aj}-u_a\le r_{jk}+w_{jk}\) for every buyer. Since \(r_{jk}+w_{jk}\ge0\), taking the maximum with zero yields

\( \displaystyle p_j\le r_{jk}+w_{jk}\qquad(j\in G,\ k\in S). \)

If \(x_{ajk}>0\), complementary slackness implies

\( \displaystyle v_{aj}-u_a=r_{jk}+w_{jk}. \)

The price definition and the preceding upper bound therefore force

\( \displaystyle p_j=v_{aj}-u_a=r_{jk}+w_{jk}\qquad\text{whenever }x_{ajk}>0. \)

This equality is the link between the welfare LP and individual optimization.

3. Buyer optimality under the stated per-agent constraints.

Define \(b_{aj}=\mu_a^{-1}\sum_k x_{ajk}\). Because \(\mu_a>0\), primal feasibility implies \(b_{aj}\ge0\) and \(\sum_j b_{aj}\le d_a\).

For any alternative feasible per-agent bundle \(z\ge0\) with \(\sum_jz_j\le d_a\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_j(v_{aj}-p_j)z_j \le u_a\sum_jz_j \le u_a d_a. \)

The first inequality uses \(v_{aj}-p_j\le u_a\); the second uses \(u_a\ge0\).

Whenever \(b_{aj}>0\), some \(x_{ajk}>0\), so the price equality proved above gives \(v_{aj}-p_j=u_a\). Thus

\( \displaystyle \sum_j(v_{aj}-p_j)b_{aj}=u_a\sum_jb_{aj}. \)

Dividing the buyer complementary-slackness equation by \(\mu_a>0\) gives

\( \displaystyle u_a\left(d_a-\sum_jb_{aj}\right)=0. \)

Consequently the realized buyer objective equals \(u_a d_a\), attaining the upper bound for every feasible alternative. Hence \(b_a\) is an individual maximizer. This proof uses exactly the buyer’s per-agent capacity constraint.

4. Seller optimality, including unsold stock.

Define \(s_{jk}=\mu_k^{-1}\sum_a x_{ajk}\). Primal feasibility gives \(0\le s_{jk}\le q_{jk}\).

For any alternative feasible seller vector \(z\) with \(0\le z_j\le q_{jk}\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_j(p_j-r_{jk})z_j \le \sum_jw_{jk}z_j \le \sum_jw_{jk}q_{jk}. \)

Here the first inequality follows from \(p_j-r_{jk}\le w_{jk}\), and the second from \(w_{jk}\ge0\).

If \(s_{jk}>0\), some \(x_{ajk}>0\), giving \(p_j-r_{jk}=w_{jk}\). If \(s_{jk}=0\), both corresponding products vanish. Therefore

\( \displaystyle (p_j-r_{jk})s_{jk}=w_{jk}s_{jk}. \)

Dividing seller complementary slackness by \(\mu_k>0\) gives

\( \displaystyle w_{jk}(q_{jk}-s_{jk})=0. \)

It follows that

\( \displaystyle \sum_j(p_j-r_{jk})s_{jk} =\sum_jw_{jk}s_{jk} =\sum_jw_{jk}q_{jk}. \)

Thus \(s_k\) attains the upper bound for every feasible alternative and is an individual maximizer.

In particular, if \(p_j>r_{jk}\), then \(w_{jk}>0\), forcing \(s_{jk}=q_{jk}\). If \(p_j<r_{jk}\), positive sales would contradict \(p_j=r_{jk}+w_{jk}\), so sales are zero. At equality, any realized quantity within the stock bound is optimal. Zero stocks cause no difficulty.

Finally, the same flow records both sides, so for each good,

\( \displaystyle \sum_a\mu_a b_{aj} =\sum_{a,k}x_{ajk} =\sum_k\mu_k s_{jk}. \)

Hence \((p,x)\) satisfies every equilibrium requirement.

5. Exact polynomial-time computation.

The algorithm is:

  • Solve the rational primal and dual LPs exactly.
  • Compute \(p_j=\max\{0,\max_a(v_{aj}-u_a)\}\) for each good.
  • Return \((p,x)\).

The primal has \(m|B||S|\) variables and \(|B|+m|S|\) capacity constraints. The dual has \(|B|+m|S|\) variables and \(m|B||S|\) principal inequalities. These counts are polynomial in \(m,\tau\).

All LP coefficients are rational. Multiplying a binary-encoded mass by an integer, or subtracting two rational inputs, produces a rational number with bit length polynomial in \(L\). Thus the full LP encoding has length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The standard exact rational LP theorem states that feasibility and optimization for explicitly encoded rational linear programs can be performed in polynomial time in their encoding length, returning rational optimal solutions when the optimum is finite and attained. Its hypotheses have been checked above; see [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239). The subsequent rational subtractions and comparisons also take polynomial time. Therefore the returned prices and flows have polynomial encoding length and are computed exactly in polynomial time.

This adapts the primal–dual method used for the finite assignment model in [Jaume, Massó and Neme](https://bse.eu/sites/default/files/working_paper_pdfs/389revised.pdf). The proof above establishes the rational-support case directly: masses enter only through aggregate capacities, while positive masses allow conversion back to per-agent quantities.
