The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many house categories \(H\), a finite support \(\mathcal A\subseteq 2^H\) of dichotomous preference types with rational masses \(\mu_A>0\) summing to \(1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_h\geq 0\) with \(r=\sum_{h\in H}\rho_h\leq 1\), assume each category is acceptable to a positive-mass type and, for every nonempty \(H'\subseteq H\), \(\sum_{A:A\cap H'\neq\varnothing}\mu_A>\sum_{h\in H'}\rho_h\). Compute an exact type-level allocation \(x_{A,h}\geq 0\) satisfying \(\sum_A\mu_Ax_{A,h}=\rho_h\) and \(\sum_hx_{A,h}=r\) for each type, whose per-person acceptable allocations realize the successive egalitarian bottleneck levels. At each stage, with remaining types \(\mathcal A'\), categories \(H'\), masses \(\mu_A\), and supplies \(\rho_h^{\rm rem}\), select a nonempty \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal A'\) minimizing \(\frac{\sum_{h\in H'\cap\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal B}A}\rho_h^{\rm rem}}{\sum_{A\in\mathcal B}\mu_A}\), breaking ties by largest total mass; give each type in \(\mathcal B\) that amount of acceptable supply per person, then recurse on the remaining types and categories. Measure running time in \(|H|\), support size \(\tau=|\mathcal A|\), and rational input length \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal A\subseteq 2^H\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_A\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(m=|H|\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Type \(A\) strictly prefers every category in \(A\) to every category outside \(A\), with indifference within each class. Supplies are binary rationals \(\rho_h\geq0\), with \(r=\sum_{h\in H}\rho_h\leq1\). Every category is acceptable to a positive-mass type, and \(\sum_{A:A\cap S\neq\varnothing}\mu_A>\sum_{h\in S}\rho_h\) for every nonempty \(S\subseteq H\).
Define bottleneck levels recursively, initially with remaining types \(T=\mathcal A\) and categories \(P=H\). For nonempty \(B\subseteq T\), put \(N_P(B)=P\cap\bigcup_{A\in B}A\).
Choose \(B\) minimizing \(\lambda=\sum_{h\in N_P(B)}\rho_h/\sum_{A\in B}\mu_A\), breaking ties by largest total mass; set \(\ell_A=\lambda\) for every \(A\in B\), remove \(B\) and \(N_P(B)\), and repeat until no types remain. Masses and supplies of remaining categories retain their original values.
The computational problem is to find an exact rational allocation \(x_{A,h}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_A\mu_Ax_{A,h}=\rho_h\), \(\sum_hx_{A,h}=r\), and \(\sum_{h\in A}x_{A,h}=\ell_A\) for every type, or determine that no such allocation exists.

Is this allocation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents, balanced trading mechanisms with stepwise equal treatment of equal endowments compute the dichotomous egalitarian solution. [Yu and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09340v2)
For finite agents and unit objects, a polynomial-time algorithm computes the egalitarian bottleneck allocation; its stated model does not use binary rational type masses. [Katta and Sethuraman](https://www.columbia.edu/~js1353/pubs/ks-fair.pdf)
No result for exactly the support-encoded problem above was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the recursion explicit: remove the chosen types and their entire remaining acceptable neighborhood, retain original masses and supplies elsewhere, and continue even when the category set is empty.
  • Specified that bottleneck levels are final total acceptable allocations, rather than increments.
  • Allowed an infeasibility answer while retaining every stated input condition and allocation constraint: the shortage promise alone does not guarantee compatibility of the bottleneck levels with the required per-person total r.

Result

The allocation problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). One exact maximum-flow computation suffices, without expanding rational masses into individual agents.

The key observation is that the prescribed bottleneck levels account for all supply. Consequently, a feasible allocation must give every type liked-category quantity exactly \(r\).

1. Conservation of the bottleneck levels

Write \(\mu(B)=\sum_{A\in B}\mu_A\) and \(\rho(S)=\sum_{h\in S}\rho_h\).

Let the successive blocks removed by the recursion be \(B_1,\ldots,B_k\), with levels \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k\). Let \(P_i\) be the categories remaining before block \(B_i\) is removed, and put \(C_i=N_{P_i}(B_i)\). By definition,

\[ \lambda_i\mu(B_i)=\rho(C_i). \]

The blocks \(B_i\) partition \(\mathcal A\), and the sets \(C_i\) are disjoint. Moreover, the sets \(C_i\) partition \(H\): for any category \(h\), choose a type \(A\) containing \(h\), which exists by the acceptability assumption. When \(A\) is removed, either \(h\) has already been removed or it belongs to the neighborhood removed at that step.

Therefore,

\[ \sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A\ell_A =\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i\mu(B_i) =\sum_{i=1}^k\rho(C_i) =r. \]

Now suppose that an allocation satisfying the question exists. Nonnegativity and the row-sum constraint imply, for every type \(A\),

\[ \ell_A=\sum_{h\in A}x_{A,h}\leq\sum_{h\in H}x_{A,h}=r. \]

Since the masses are positive and sum to \(1\),

\[ \sum_A\mu_A(r-\ell_A)=r-\sum_A\mu_A\ell_A=0. \]

Every summand is nonnegative, so every summand vanishes. Hence

\[ \ell_A=r\qquad\text{for every }A. \]

It follows also that

\[ \sum_{h\notin A}x_{A,h}=r-\ell_A=0. \]

Thus every feasible allocation uses only categories liked by its recipient type.

2. The flow network

Construct a directed network with source \(s\), sink \(t\), one vertex for each type \(A\in\mathcal A\), and one vertex for each category \(h\in H\). Its arcs and capacities are:

| Arc | Condition | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| \(s\to A\) | Every type \(A\) | \(r\mu_A\) |
| \(A\to h\) | \(h\in A\) | \(2\) |
| \(h\to t\) | Every category \(h\) | \(\rho_h\) |

Both the total capacity leaving \(s\) and the total capacity entering \(t\) equal \(r\). The capacity \(2\) is sufficient because \(r\leq1\).

We prove that the requested allocation exists if and only if this network has a flow of value \(r\).

An allocation produces a flow

By Section 1, any feasible allocation has \(x_{A,h}=0\) when \(h\notin A\). Set

\[ y_{A,h}=\mu_Ax_{A,h}. \]

Then

\[ \sum_{h\in A}y_{A,h}=r\mu_A, \qquad \sum_{A:h\in A}y_{A,h}=\rho_h. \]

Send \(r\mu_A\) along \(s\to A\), send \(y_{A,h}\) along \(A\to h\), and send \(\rho_h\) along \(h\to t\). These quantities satisfy conservation. Also, \(y_{A,h}\leq r\leq1<2\), so the internal capacities are respected. This is a flow of value \(r\).

A flow forces the prescribed levels to equal \(r\)

Conversely, suppose a flow of value \(r\) exists, and let \(y_{A,h}\) denote its flow on \(A\to h\).

Every source arc and every sink arc is saturated: their respective total capacities equal the flow value, so their nonnegative slacks sum to zero. Consequently,

\[ \sum_{h\in A}y_{A,h}=r\mu_A, \qquad \sum_{A:h\in A}y_{A,h}=\rho_h. \]

For every nonempty \(B\subseteq\mathcal A\), all flow leaving its type vertices enters \(N_H(B)\). Thus

\[ r\mu(B) =\sum_{A\in B}\sum_{h\in A}y_{A,h} \leq\rho(N_H(B)). \]

Since \(\mu(B)>0\), every initial bottleneck ratio is at least \(r\).

For \(B=\mathcal A\), category coverage gives \(N_H(\mathcal A)=H\), and therefore its ratio is

\[ \frac{\rho(H)}{\mu(\mathcal A)}=\frac r1=r. \]

The initial minimum is consequently \(r\). The set \(\mathcal A\) attains that minimum and has mass \(1\); every proper subset has smaller mass because all masses are positive. The tie-break therefore selects all types in the first step, giving \(\ell_A=r\) for every \(A\).

Define

\[ x_{A,h}= \begin{cases} y_{A,h}/\mu_A,&h\in A,\\ 0,&h\notin A. \end{cases} \]

The flow equations give the required column supplies and row sums. Because only liked categories receive positive quantities, the liked-category sum is \(r=\ell_A\). This completes the converse.

The argument includes \(r=0\): all supplies, bottleneck levels, and output entries are then zero.

3. Algorithm and an infeasibility certificate

Compute a maximum flow of value \(F\) in the network above.

  • If \(F=r\), output the allocation constructed in Section 2.
  • If \(F<r\), report that no requested allocation exists.

The equivalence already proved establishes correctness. A minimum cut also provides an explicit certificate in the second case.

Let \(U\) be the source side of a minimum cut, and define

\[ B=U\cap\mathcal A,\qquad S=U\cap H. \]

Its capacity is \(F<r\leq1\), so it cannot cross any type-to-category arc, whose capacity is \(2\). Hence \(N_H(B)\subseteq S\), and its capacity equals

\[ r(1-\mu(B))+\rho(S)<r. \]

Therefore,

\[ \rho(N_H(B))\leq\rho(S)<r\mu(B). \]

In particular, \(B\neq\varnothing\). This certifies an initial bottleneck ratio strictly below \(r\), contradicting the necessary condition from Section 1 that every prescribed level equal \(r\).

Equivalently, feasibility is characterized by the weighted Hall inequalities

\[ \rho(N_H(B))\geq r\mu(B) \qquad\text{for every }B\subseteq\mathcal A. \]

The flow computation checks these inequalities implicitly.

4. Exact arithmetic and polynomial bit complexity

Write the input rationals as

\[ \mu_A=\frac{p_A}{q_A}, \qquad \rho_h=\frac{a_h}{b_h}, \]

with positive integer denominators, and define

\[ D=\left(\prod_{A\in\mathcal A}q_A\right) \left(\prod_{h\in H}b_h\right). \]

The binary length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). Multiplying every network capacity by \(D\) gives integer capacities: \(D\rho_h\), \(Dr\mu_A\), and \(2D\) are all integers. For \(Dr\mu_A\), this follows because a denominator for \(r\) divides \(\prod_h b_h\).

All scaled capacities are at most \(2D\), so their binary lengths are \(O(L)\). The network has

\[ v=\tau+m+2,\qquad e=\tau+m+\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}|A| \leq\tau+m+\tau m. \]

The standard shortest-augmenting-path maximum-flow algorithm uses \(O(ve^2)\) arithmetic operations, independently of capacity magnitudes. Applied here to nonnegative integer capacities of \(O(L)\) bits, it therefore runs in polynomial bit time. See [Edmonds and Karp, *Theoretical Improvements in Algorithmic Efficiency for Network Flow Problems*](https://doi.org/10.1145/321694.321699).

Let \(f_{A,h}\) be the resulting integer flow on a type-to-category arc. When the scaled flow value is \(Dr\), output

\[ x_{A,h}=\frac{f_{A,h}}{D\mu_A} \]

on liked pairs, and zero elsewhere. The denominator \(D\mu_A\) is a positive integer. Moreover, \(f_{A,h}\leq Dr\leq D\) and \(D\mu_A\leq D\). Thus every output rational has \(O(L)\) bits, and the full output has polynomial length.

5. The scarcity assumptions do not guarantee feasibility

Consider \(H=\{a,b\}\), with types and masses

\[ \mu_{\{a\}}=\frac14,\qquad \mu_{\{b\}}=\frac34, \]

and supplies

\[ \rho_a=\rho_b=\frac18,\qquad r=\frac14. \]

Every category is liked by a positive-mass type. The required strict scarcity inequalities hold: for the singleton category sets they are \(\frac14>\frac18\) and \(\frac34>\frac18\), and for \(H\) they are \(1>\frac14\).

The three initial bottleneck ratios are

\[ \frac{\rho_a}{\mu_{\{a\}}}=\frac12,\qquad \frac{\rho_b}{\mu_{\{b\}}}=\frac16,\qquad \frac{\rho_a+\rho_b}{1}=\frac14. \]

The recursion therefore assigns \(\ell_{\{b\}}=\frac16\), followed by \(\ell_{\{a\}}=\frac12\). The latter exceeds the required row sum \(r=\frac14\), so no allocation exists.

Thus the infeasibility outcome is substantive. The polynomial-time algorithm handles it exactly, and its proof uses only positive masses, category coverage, and the stated supply bounds; the strict scarcity promise introduces no additional difficulty.
