The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses of requester and qualified-device types, each with an integer capacity and an explicitly listed DMR marginal bid or ask schedule, compute the type-level traded task mass, allocations, and payments under QUAD’s split-market pricing rule. Split each type’s mass between the two arenas; find clearing prices allowing fractional participation by marginal mass when needed; use each arena’s price to determine eligible trade in the other, with ties resolved by a fixed rule.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Masses sum to \(1\), with positive requester and device mass. Devices are already qualified; all tasks belong to one homogeneous category.
Type \(t\) has positive integer capacity \(Q_t\) and an explicitly listed nonnegative rational schedule. Requester bids satisfy \(b_{t,1}\ge\cdots\ge b_{t,Q_t}\); device execution costs satisfy \(a_{t,1}\le\cdots\le a_{t,Q_t}\), the reverse of its DMR retained-unit marginal values. Let \(m=\max_t Q_t\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Split every type's mass equally between arenas \(A\) and \(B\). Each marginal slot carries the arena's mass of its type and permits fractional allocation, with earlier slots filled before later ones.
In each arena choose the least nonnegative clearing price: all requester slots with bid strictly above the price and device slots with cost strictly below it participate fully; slots equal to the price may participate fractionally so demand equals supply.
For trade in \(A\), use \(B\)'s price, and conversely. At this posted price \(p\), eligible bids satisfy \(b>p\) and eligible costs satisfy \(a<p\). If their total masses are \(D\) and \(S\), trade \(q=\min(D,S)\), selecting highest bids and lowest costs. Break equal-value ties by input type order, then slot order; a boundary slot may be partially selected.
Use the infinitesimal-replica convention for displacement fees: on a rationed side let \(g\) be the largest gain among rejected eligible marginal mass, where gain is \(b-p\) for requesters and \(p-a\) for devices. Each traded unit on that side pays fee \(g\); all other fees are zero. A requester allocated mass \(x\) pays \(px\) plus its fee; a device receives \(px\) minus its fee.

Is exact computation of the type-level traded task masses, allocations, and payments under this rule possible in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Discrete QUAD is claimed polynomial-time under polynomial bounds on capacities and the number of price-search iterations ([QUAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06647)).
Finite Vickrey-MUDA uses opposite-market equilibrium prices, marginal-value rationing, and displacement fees ([MUDA](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/11450/11309)); no result settling this exact mass computation problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize the joint requester-device distribution to total mass one, with both sides having positive mass.
  • Use one homogeneous category and take device qualification as supplied; distinct categories can be processed independently.
  • Interpret the independent fair discrete split as an equal split of every type's mass.
  • Specify positive integer capacities, nonnegative binary-rational schedules, and schedules through the full capacity despite the valuation-domain endpoint slip.
  • Interpret device asks as execution-order costs, reversing the DMR marginal values of retained units as dictated by the seller utility.
  • Replace the unspecified price increment with the least nonnegative clearing price, allowing fractional participation only by indifferent marginal mass.
  • Use strict eligibility at the opposite arena's posted price, consistent with the demand and supply definitions; correct the reversed winner-selection inequalities and restrict allocation to eligible slots.
  • Resolve ties by input type order followed by marginal slot order.
  • Extend the prose displacement fee using infinitesimal replicas: the aggregate fee is traded mass times the highest rejected eligible gain on the rationed side.
  • Define m as maximum capacity because a nonatomic society has no finite requester count; L includes all schedules and masses.

Result

Exact computation is possible in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, because every marginal schedule is explicitly listed, it is polynomial in \(L\) alone.

The proof below computes the least clearing price, every selected marginal mass, and every payment using exact rational arithmetic. It includes ties, partially selected slots, and zero trade.

1. Weighted marginal slots

Let \(R\) and \(E\) denote the requester and device types, respectively. Write \(\mu_t>0\) for type \(t\)'s society mass and put \(w_t=\mu_t/2\), its mass in either arena.

There are \(N=\sum_t Q_t\le m\tau\) explicitly listed marginal slots. A slot of type \(t\) has available task mass \(w_t\).

Both arenas have identical weighted marginal schedules. Consequently, their least clearing prices coincide, and their posted-price outcomes coincide under the specified tie rule. We may therefore compute one arena and double its masses and payments to obtain society totals.

This symmetry does not eliminate rationing: clearing permits participation at equality, whereas posted-price trading excludes equality.

2. Characterizing clearing prices

For a nonnegative price \(z\), define

\(D_{>}(z)=\sum_{t\in R}\sum_{j:\,b_{t,j}>z}w_t,\qquad D_{\ge}(z)=\sum_{t\in R}\sum_{j:\,b_{t,j}\ge z}w_t,\)

\(S_{<}(z)=\sum_{t\in E}\sum_{j:\,a_{t,j}<z}w_t,\qquad S_{\le}(z)=\sum_{t\in E}\sum_{j:\,a_{t,j}\le z}w_t.\)

Lemma 1. The price \(z\) clears if and only if

\(D_{>}(z)\le S_{\le}(z)\quad\text{and}\quad S_{<}(z)\le D_{\ge}(z).\)

Proof. Mandatory requester participation has mass \(D_{>}(z)\). Including arbitrary fractional participation from slots equal to \(z\) gives every total in the interval \([D_{>}(z),D_{\ge}(z)]\).

To verify attainability while respecting slot order, observe that each requester's equal-price slots form a consecutive block after its strictly eligible slots. Filling that block sequentially, with at most one partially filled slot, attains every participation mass between the type's mandatory and maximum masses. Summing these intervals across types gives the stated aggregate interval: the sum of intervals \([\ell_i,u_i]\) is \([\sum_i\ell_i,\sum_i u_i]\).

Device costs are nondecreasing, so the same argument gives the supply interval \([S_{<}(z),S_{\le}(z)]\).

Clearing is therefore equivalent to intersection of these two intervals. Two closed intervals intersect exactly when each lower endpoint is at most the other's upper endpoint, yielding the two inequalities. \(\square\)

3. Computing the least clearing price

Let \(Z\) consist of \(0\) and all listed bids and costs, with duplicates removed and values sorted increasingly. Define

\(p_*=\min\{z\in Z:D_{>}(z)\le S_{\le}(z)\}.\)

This minimum exists: at the largest element of \(Z\), no bid is strictly greater than the price, so \(D_{>}=0\).

Lemma 2. The price \(p_*\) is the least nonnegative clearing price.

Proof. By definition, \(D_{>}(p_*)\le S_{\le}(p_*)\). We verify the other inequality from Lemma 1.

If \(p_*=0\), nonnegative costs imply \(S_{<}(0)=0\le D_{\ge}(0)\).

Otherwise, let \(z^-\) be the preceding element of \(Z\). Because it was not selected,

\(D_{>}(z^-)>S_{\le}(z^-).\)

There is no listed value strictly between \(z^-\) and \(p_*\). Hence

\(D_{>}(z^-)=D_{\ge}(p_*),\qquad S_{\le}(z^-)=S_{<}(p_*).\)

It follows that \(S_{<}(p_*)<D_{\ge}(p_*)\). Thus \(p_*\) clears.

Finally, take any \(0\le r<p_*\), and let \(z\) be the largest element of \(Z\) satisfying \(z\le r\). No listed value lies in \((z,r]\), so

\(D_{>}(r)=D_{>}(z),\qquad S_{\le}(r)=S_{\le}(z).\)

Because \(z<p_*\), its defining test failed. Therefore \(D_{>}(r)>S_{\le}(r)\), which rules out clearing by Lemma 1. This proves minimality. \(\square\)

Algorithm. Sort the price events. Start with all requester-slot mass in a demand accumulator and zero in a supply accumulator. At each event value \(z\):

  • Remove all requester-slot mass whose bid equals \(z\).
  • Add all device-slot mass whose cost equals \(z\).
  • Test whether demand is at most supply.

After these updates, the accumulators are precisely \(D_{>}(z)\) and \(S_{\le}(z)\). Process \(0\) even if no schedule contains it. The first successful event is \(p_*\).

Sorting takes \(O(N\log(N+2))\) comparisons; the sweep takes \(O(N)\) arithmetic operations. No numerical price iteration is needed.

4. Computing allocations

Set \(p=p_*\), \(D=D_{>}(p)\), \(S=S_{<}(p)\), and \(q=\min(D,S)\).

Sort eligible requester slots by decreasing bid, then increasing input type order, then increasing slot order. Sort eligible device slots by increasing cost, with the same tie ordering.

On either side, let the resulting ordered slots have weights \(w_1,\ldots,w_k\), and define \(C_0=0\), \(C_i=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}w_\ell\). Select from slot \(i\) the mass

\(x_i=\min\{w_i,\max\{0,q-C_{i-1}\}\}.\)

This fills the ordered prefix up to mass \(q\). Since each side's eligible mass is at least \(q\), its selected mass is exactly \(q\). All slots before the boundary are full, all later slots are empty, and at most one slot on each side is partial.

Slot-order feasibility. If requester slots \(j<k\) belong to the same type, then \(b_{t,j}\ge b_{t,k}\). The sorting rule consequently places \(j\) before \(k\), including when their bids tie. For devices, \(a_{t,j}\le a_{t,k}\) gives the same conclusion. Eligibility also forms a prefix within each type.

Thus any positively selected slot has every earlier slot of its type fully selected, as required.

Returning \(x_{t,j}\), with zero for ineligible slots, specifies the marginal allocations. The type's traded task mass is

\(X_t=\sum_{j=1}^{Q_t}x_{t,j}.\)

Society totals are \(2x_{t,j}\) and \(2X_t\).

If a requester-to-device type assignment is also desired, one feasible assignment can be constructed by repeatedly matching the first requester type and device type with positive remaining selected masses, assigning the minimum of those masses, and subtracting it from both. Each step exhausts at least one type, so there are at most \(|R|+|E|-1\) positive entries when \(q>0\). Homogeneity and prior qualification make every such pairing feasible. The stated rule determines the marginal allocations, but does not prescribe a unique pairing.

5. Computing displacement fees and payments

Define the per-unit requester fee by

\(g_R=\begin{cases}\max\{b_{t,j}-p:b_{t,j}>p,\ x_{t,j}<w_t\},&D>S,\\0,&D\le S,\end{cases}\)

and the per-unit device fee by

\(g_E=\begin{cases}\max\{p-a_{t,j}:a_{t,j}<p,\ x_{t,j}<w_t\},&S>D,\\0,&S\le D.\end{cases}\)

Whenever a maximum is called for, its set is nonempty. Indeed, a rationed side has eligible mass strictly larger than \(q\), so some eligible slot has positive rejected mass.

The requester ordering is also decreasing order of gain \(b-p\); the device ordering is decreasing order of gain \(p-a\). Therefore the first slot with positive rejected mass attains the appropriate maximum.

A partially selected boundary slot counts as rejected eligible mass. If \(0<x_{t,j}<w_t\), its rejected remainder has positive mass and the same marginal gain as its selected portion. Including it is exactly what the stated infinitesimal-replica convention requires.

Hence both fees are obtained by one scan of the selected prefixes. Type payments in one arena are

\(P_t^R=(p+g_R)X_t\quad(t\in R),\qquad P_t^E=(p-g_E)X_t\quad(t\in E).\)

Here \(P_t^R\) is the requester's payment and \(P_t^E\) the device's receipt. These formulas apply the prescribed fee to every traded unit on the rationed side and zero fee elsewhere. When \(q=0\), every payment is zero. Society payments are twice the arena payments.

6. Exact bit complexity

Counting arithmetic operations is insufficient unless the operands have polynomial bit length. We now establish that bound.

Write \(\mu_t=n_t/d_t\), with numerator and positive denominator explicitly encoded in binary, and choose

\(H=2\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}d_t.\)

Every arena slot weight is an integer multiple of \(1/H\). Moreover,

\(\log_2 H\le 1+\sum_t\log_2 d_t=O(L).\)

All demand, supply, prefix, selected, and rejected masses are obtained by addition, subtraction, and comparison of such multiples. They therefore retain denominator \(H\).

The total task capacity in one arena is

\(\sum_tQ_tw_t=\frac12\sum_tQ_t\mu_t\le m/2,\)

using \(Q_t\le m\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Thus the corresponding integer mass numerators have \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits.

Next let \(K\) be the product of the denominators of all explicitly listed bids and costs. Again \(\log_2K=O(L)\). Every listed value is an integer multiple of \(1/K\). Lemma 2 shows that \(p\) is either zero or a listed value, and each nonzero fee is a difference between listed values. Accordingly, prices and fees can be stored with denominator \(K\) and \(O(L)\)-bit numerators.

Every type payment can consequently be stored with denominator \(HK\), and its numerator has \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits. Doubling for society totals preserves this bound. Reduced fractions, if required, can be obtained by polynomial-time greatest-common-divisor computations.

Let \(B=O(L+\log(m+1))\). Constructing these common denominators, converting inputs, sorting, scanning, and forming outputs all use polynomially many operations on \(O(B)\)-bit integers. For example, using ordinary quadratic-time integer arithmetic gives a conservative bound

\(O\bigl(N\log(N+2)\,B^2\bigr)\)

bit operations for the slot allocations and aggregate payments.

Since \(N\le m\tau\), this is polynomial in the requested parameters. Since schedules are explicitly listed, \(N=O(L)\) and \(m\le N\), so it is also polynomial in \(L\) alone. Small masses never require enumerating their implied replicas.

7. Relation to the cited mechanisms

QUAD's computational-efficiency discussion analyzes its discrete routines using assumptions on capacities and price-loop iteration counts. The proof here replaces iterative price adjustment with a finite event sweep and supplies an exact bit-complexity bound for the given mass representation. [QUAD, Lemma 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.06647)

MUDA uses opposite-market equilibrium prices, and its Vickrey variant selects the long side by marginal value. Its finite-trader fees depend on the gains of traders displaced by removing a selected trader. Here the fee is explicitly specified by the infinitesimal-replica convention, and Section 5 computes that convention directly. No theorem transferring finite-trader payments to this model is needed. [MUDA, Sections 1 and 3](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/11450/11309)

Together, Sections 2–6 establish a complete polynomial-time algorithm for the exact rule in the question.
