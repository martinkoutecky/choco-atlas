The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational support points \(x_1,\ldots,x_\tau\in\mathbb{Q}^2\), rational masses \(\mu_j\ge0\) summing to \(1\), and a precision parameter \(\varepsilon\), compute the lower weighted coordinate-wise median \(c_\mu\), an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal geometric median, and \(R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu)=\frac{\sum_j\mu_j\lVert c_\mu-x_j\rVert_2}{\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^2}\sum_j\mu_j\lVert y-x_j\rVert_2}\); then determine \(\sup_\mu R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu)\) and provide a witness family.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by its support: distinct points \(x_1,\ldots,x_\tau\in\mathbb Q^2\) with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_j\), where \(\sum_j\mu_j=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; the dimension is \(m=2\).
Use the standard coordinate axes and define \(c_\mu^{(k)}=\min\{t\in\{x_j^{(k)}\}_j:\sum_{j:x_j^{(k)}\le t}\mu_j\ge 1/2\}\) for \(k\in\{1,2\}\).
Let \(F_\mu(y)=\sum_j\mu_j\lVert y-x_j\rVert_2\), \(F_\mu^*=\min_{y\in\mathbb R^2}F_\mu(y)\), and \(R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu)=F_\mu(c_\mu)/F_\mu^*\); set the ratio to \(1\) when both costs vanish and to \(\infty\) when only the denominator vanishes.
For binary rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), the associated computational question asks for \(c_\mu\), a rational point \(y\) satisfying \(F_\mu(y)\le F_\mu^*+\varepsilon\), and a rational estimate of \(R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu)\) with absolute error at most \(\varepsilon\). State running times in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length.
What is the sharp worst-case approximation ratio \(\sup_\mu R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu)\) over all such societies, and what family attains or approaches it?
For an odd number \(n\) of equally weighted agents, the coordinate-wise median has sharp minisum approximation ratio \(\sqrt{2}\sqrt{n^2+1}/(n+1)\). For even \(n\), choosing the lower median in each coordinate has sharp ratio \(\sqrt{2}\). Both facts are established in [Goel and Hann-Caruthers](https://whanncar.github.io/papers/median.pdf).
The search located no separate result explicitly formulated for binary rational masses represented by support.

Result

The sharp worst-case ratio is \(\sup_\mu R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu)=\sqrt{2}\), and it is attained by a society with two support points.

All three requested outputs are computable in deterministic time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon))\). In particular, this is polynomial time for the stipulated dimension \(m=2\).

The proof below works directly with weighted support; it does not expand rational masses into individual agents.

1. The weighted upper bound

Define the auxiliary objective \(G_\mu(y)=\sum_j\mu_j\lVert y-x_j\rVert_1\).

We first prove that \(c_\mu\) minimizes \(G_\mu\). For coordinate \(k\), let \(q_-\), \(q_0\), and \(q_+\) be the masses strictly below, equal to, and strictly above \(c_\mu^{(k)}\), respectively. The definition of the lower weighted median gives \(q_-<1/2\) and \(q_+\le 1/2\).

The convex function \(g_k(t)=\sum_j\mu_j|t-x_j^{(k)}|\) has left and right derivatives at \(c_\mu^{(k)}\) equal to \(2q_--1\) and \(1-2q_+\). These are respectively nonpositive and nonnegative, so \(c_\mu^{(k)}\) minimizes \(g_k\). Since \(G_\mu(y)=\sum_{k=1}^m g_k(y^{(k)})\), it follows that \(G_\mu(c_\mu)\le G_\mu(y)\) for every \(y\).

A minimizer \(y^*\) of \(F_\mu\) exists: the function is continuous, and the triangle inequality gives
\(\displaystyle F_\mu(y)\ge \lVert y\rVert_2-\sum_j\mu_j\lVert x_j\rVert_2\),
which tends to infinity as \(\lVert y\rVert_2\) does.

Using \(\lVert v\rVert_2\le\lVert v\rVert_1\le\sqrt m\,\lVert v\rVert_2\), we obtain
\[ \text{} \]
\(F_\mu(c_\mu)\le G_\mu(c_\mu)\le G_\mu(y^*)\le\sqrt m\,F_\mu(y^*)\).

Thus, for \(m=2\) and positive optimal cost, \(R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu)\le\sqrt2\).

If \(F_\mu^*=0\), positivity of every mass forces every support point to equal \(y^*\). Because support points are distinct, this occurs exactly when \(\tau=1\). Then \(c_\mu=x_1\), both costs vanish, and the stipulated ratio is \(1\). The case of a zero denominator and positive numerator therefore never occurs.

2. Attainment and a family approaching the bound

Exact attainment

Let \(a>0\) be rational, and take \(x_1=(a,0)\), \(x_2=(0,a)\), with masses \(\mu_1=\mu_2=1/2\).

In each coordinate, the cumulative mass at \(0\) is exactly \(1/2\); hence the lower coordinate median is \(c_\mu=(0,0)\). Its cost is \(F_\mu(c_\mu)=a\).

For every \(y\), the triangle inequality gives
\(\displaystyle F_\mu(y)=\frac{\lVert y-x_1\rVert_2+\lVert y-x_2\rVert_2}{2}\ge\frac{\lVert x_1-x_2\rVert_2}{2}=\frac a{\sqrt2}\).
Equality holds for every \(y\) on the segment joining \(x_1\) and \(x_2\). Consequently, \(F_\mu^*=a/\sqrt2\) and \(R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu)=\sqrt2\).

Rational translations of this family also attain the bound.

Approach with three noncollinear support points

The same supremum is approached without a half-mass tie at either coordinate median. For dyadic rational \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), place mass \(\alpha\) at \((0,0)\) and mass \((1-\alpha)/2\) at each of \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\).

In each coordinate, the mass at \(0\) is \((1+\alpha)/2>1/2\), so \(c_{\mu_\alpha}=(0,0)\), with \(F_{\mu_\alpha}(c_{\mu_\alpha})=1-\alpha\).

At \(y=(1/2,1/2)\), all three distances equal \(1/\sqrt2\). Therefore \(F_{\mu_\alpha}^*\le1/\sqrt2\), and
\(\sqrt2(1-\alpha)\le R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu_\alpha)\le\sqrt2\).
Taking \(\alpha=2^{-r}\) and letting \(r\) increase proves convergence to \(\sqrt2\).

This agrees with the stated even-agent result of [Goel and Hann-Caruthers](https://whanncar.github.io/papers/median.pdf). The three-point family also shows why support size \(\tau\) cannot replace agent count \(n\) in the odd-agent formula.

3. Computing the coordinate median

For each coordinate, sort the \(\tau\) rational coordinate values and accumulate the masses until the cumulative sum first reaches \(1/2\). All comparisons and sums can be performed exactly.

A common denominator for the masses can be formed by multiplying their input denominators. Its bit length is \(O(L)\), so this operation does not require enumerating the potentially enormous corresponding population.

Sorting uses \(O(m\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational comparisons. Including exact arithmetic, the running time is \(O(m\tau\log(\tau+1)\operatorname{poly}(L))\).

4. Computing a rational additive approximation

We establish a subroutine that, for any rational \(\eta\in(0,1)\), returns rational \(y\) satisfying \(F_\mu(y)\le F_\mu^*+\eta\), in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\eta))\).

Set \(M=1+\max_{j,k}|x_j^{(k)}|\) and \(T=8mM\). Consider the bounded second-order cone program with variables \(y\in\mathbb R^m\) and \(t\in\mathbb R^\tau\):

  • Minimize \(\sum_j\mu_jt_j\).
  • Require \(-2M\le y^{(k)}\le2M\) for every coordinate.
  • Require \(0\le t_j\le T\) and \(\lVert y-x_j\rVert_2\le t_j\) for every support point.

Its optimum equals \(F_\mu^*\). Indeed, clamping any coordinate of \(y\) to the corresponding support-coordinate interval weakly decreases every distance. Thus a minimizer can be chosen with \(|y^{(k)}|\le M\). At that minimizer, setting \(t_j=\lVert y-x_j\rVert_2\le2mM<T\) gives feasibility and objective \(F_\mu^*\). Conversely, every feasible point has objective at least \(F_\mu(y)\).

For completeness, we verify the hypotheses needed for polynomial-time convex optimization.

Boundedness and interior

Write \(D=m+\tau\) for the number of variables, and let \(K\) be the feasible set. It is closed and convex, lies in a ball of radius \(R=D(T+2M)\), and contains the Euclidean ball of radius \(1\) centered at \(y=0\), \(t_j=4mM\).

To check the latter assertion, a perturbation of norm at most \(1\) changes \(y\) by norm at most \(1\) and each \(t_j\) by at most \(1\). Hence
\(\lVert y-x_j\rVert_2\le1+mM\le4mM-1\le t_j\).
The box constraints also hold. The objective lies in \([0,T]\), and \(\log R,\log T=O(L+\log(m+\tau))\).

Rational separation

Linear box violations have immediate rational separating inequalities. Otherwise, at a queried rational point \((y,t)\), every cone constraint can be tested exactly by comparing \(\lVert y-x_j\rVert_2^2\) with \(t_j^2\), since \(t_j\ge0\).

Suppose a cone constraint is violated. Put \(d=y-x_j\) and \(A=\lVert d\rVert_2^2>t_j^2\). Choose rational \(s\ge\sqrt A\) such that \(st_j<A\). Then the rational inequality
\(d\cdot(y'-x_j)\le s\,t_j'\)
holds for every feasible \((y',t')\), by Cauchy–Schwarz, but fails at the queried point.

Such \(s\) is computable in polynomial time. If \(t_j=0\), take \(s=A+1\). Otherwise, set \(v=A-t_j^2>0\) and compute a rational upper approximation to \(\sqrt A\) with error at most \(v/(4(t_j+1))\). This suffices because
\(\displaystyle A-t_j\sqrt A=\frac{\sqrt A}{\sqrt A+t_j}v>\frac v2\),
whereas the approximation increases \(t_js\) by less than \(v/4\). The rational gap \(v\) has polynomial encoding length in the input and query, so the required precision does too.

We now apply the standard rational-arithmetic ellipsoid optimization theorem: a bounded convex body with a known interior-radius bound and a polynomial-time rational separation oracle admits additive approximation of a rational linear objective in polynomial time in the dimension, encoding lengths, and logarithm of inverse accuracy. See [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Chapter 4](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1988.pdf). The preceding checks establish its hypotheses here.

The resulting rational feasible point has \(\sum_j\mu_jt_j\le F_\mu^*+\eta\), and therefore its \(y\)-component satisfies the claimed guarantee.

5. Estimating the ratio to absolute error

An additive approximation to the denominator alone requires a scale bound. We obtain one directly from the support.

Assume \(\tau\ge2\). Choose distinct support points \(x_i,x_j\) and a coordinate \(k\) in which they differ. Define the positive rational number
\(b=\min(\mu_i,\mu_j)|x_i^{(k)}-x_j^{(k)}|\).

For every \(y\),
\(\displaystyle F_\mu(y)\ge\min(\mu_i,\mu_j)\bigl(\lVert y-x_i\rVert_2+\lVert y-x_j\rVert_2\bigr)\ge\min(\mu_i,\mu_j)\lVert x_i-x_j\rVert_2\ge b\).
Thus \(F_\mu^*\ge b>0\). Moreover, \(b\) has encoding length \(O(L)\), so \(\log(1/\min\{1,b\})=O(L)\).

Set \(\delta=\varepsilon\min\{1,b\}/(8(m+1))\). Run the preceding subroutine with accuracy \(\delta/2\), obtaining rational \(y\).

At rational points, each distance is the square root of a nonnegative rational. Rational square-root approximation therefore computes:

  • A rational \(\widehat A\) satisfying \(|\widehat A-F_\mu(c_\mu)|\le\delta\).
  • A rational \(\widehat B\) satisfying \(|\widehat B-F_\mu(y)|\le\delta/2\).

Because \(F_\mu^*\le F_\mu(y)\le F_\mu^*+\delta/2\), we have \(|\widehat B-F_\mu^*|\le\delta\). Also, \(\widehat B\ge b-\delta\ge b/2>0\).

Output the rational estimate \(\widehat R=\widehat A/\widehat B\). Writing \(A=F_\mu(c_\mu)\) and \(B=F_\mu^*\), the weighted upper bound gives \(A/B\le\sqrt m\le m\). Consequently,
\(\displaystyle \left|\widehat R-\frac AB\right|\le\frac{|\widehat A-A|+(A/B)|B-\widehat B|}{\widehat B}\le\frac{2(m+1)\delta}{b}\le\frac{\varepsilon}{4}<\varepsilon\).

The returned point also satisfies the requested additive error because \(\delta/2<\varepsilon\).

Finally, \(\log(1/\delta)=O(L+\log(m+1)+\log(1/\varepsilon))\). Thus optimization, cost evaluation, and ratio estimation all take \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon))\) bit operations. When \(\tau=1\), simply return \(c_\mu=y=x_1\) and ratio estimate \(1\).
