The continuous mirror question, as graded

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to one; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq1\) divisible items, rational per-capita supplies \(s_j>0\), nonzero rational valuation vectors \(v_t\geq0\), and rational per-agent budgets \(B_t>0\).
Let \(x_t\geq0\) be the bundle per agent of type \(t\), and \(\delta_t\geq0\) unspent budget. Define \(S_j(x)=\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}\) and \(C_k(x)=\sum_{t,j}\mu_t a_{ktj}x_{tj}\). Input constraints are \(C_k(x)\leq b_k\) for \(k\in I\) and \(C_k(x)=b_k\) for \(k\in E\), with arbitrary signed rational coefficients, \(K=|I|+|E|\), and joint feasibility with \(S(x)\leq s\).
The constrained quasi-linear EG problem maximizes \(F(x,\delta)=\sum_t\mu_t[B_t\log(v_t^{\mathsf T}x_t+\delta_t)-\delta_t]\) over these constraints and positive logarithm arguments.
Supporting prices are supply multipliers \(p_j\geq0\); interventions are \(\bar p_{tj}=\sum_k a_{ktj}\lambda_k\), with \(\lambda_k\geq0\) for \(k\in I\) and unrestricted otherwise. Result

The approximation problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\).

In fact, the algorithm below produces rational outputs with exact primal feasibility and
\(0\leq g(p,\lambda)-F(x,\delta)<\varepsilon\).
The proof permits arbitrary signed constraint coefficients and a feasible polytope of any dimension.

1. Eliminate unspent budget

Let
\(P=\{x\geq0:S(x)\leq s,\ C_k(x)\leq b_k\ (k\in I),\ C_k(x)=b_k\ (k\in E)\}\).

By hypothesis, \(P\) is nonempty. Moreover, every \(x\in P\) satisfies
\(0\leq x_{tj}\leq s_j/\mu_t\).
Thus \(P\) is a compact rational polytope.

Fix a type \(t\), and write \(w=v_t^{\mathsf T}x_t\). For fixed \(x\), its objective contribution is maximized over \(\delta_t\geq0\) at
\(\delta_t=(B_t-w)_+\).
Indeed, the derivative with respect to \(\delta_t\) is \(B_t/(w+\delta_t)-1\), which changes sign at \(w+\delta_t=B_t\); if \(w\geq B_t\), the maximum occurs at zero.

Define, for \(w\geq0\),
\(h_t(w)=B_t\log(\max\{B_t,w\})-(B_t-w)_+\).

The original optimization problem therefore has the same optimum as
\(\max_{x\in P}H(x)\), where
\(H(x)=\sum_t\mu_t h_t(v_t^{\mathsf T}x_t)\).

The function \(h_t\) is continuously differentiable, with
\(h_t'(w)=B_t/\max\{B_t,w\}\).
This derivative is nonincreasing, so \(h_t\), and hence \(H\), is concave. Furthermore,
\(0<h_t'(w)\leq1\), and
\(\lvert h_t'(w)-h_t'(w')\rvert\leq \lvert w-w'\rvert/B_t\).

At every rational \(x\in P\), define
\(u_t=\max\{B_t,v_t^{\mathsf T}x_t\}\) and
\(\beta_t=B_t/u_t\).
Then \(\beta_t\) is rational, \(0<\beta_t\leq1\), and
\(\frac{\partial H}{\partial x_{tj}}(x)=\mu_t\beta_t v_{tj}\).
Consequently, the gradient is exactly computable using rational arithmetic.

2. Polynomial-time objective approximation

We first establish that, for any rational \(0<\eta<1\), a rational \(x\in P\) satisfying
\(H(x)\geq H^*-\eta\), where \(H^*=\max_{z\in P}H(z)\),
can be computed in polynomial time in the input length and \(\log(1/\eta)\).

The standard algorithmic ingredients are exact rational linear programming and the rational ellipsoid method. The latter maintains containing ellipsoids under central cuts, reduces their volume by a factor \(\exp(-c/d)\) per iteration in dimension \(d\), and uses polynomial bit complexity when the initial radius, target radius, and cut descriptions have polynomial encoding length. These are classical results; see the ellipsoid and rational-polyhedron chapters of [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4). We verify the needed geometric and oracle hypotheses here.

Removing lower-dimensional degeneracy

Write \(P=\{x:Ax\leq b\}\), converting each equality into two inequalities and including nonnegativity.

For every row \(i\), solve the rational linear program
\(\min_{x\in P}A_i x\).
The row is tight throughout \(P\) precisely when this minimum equals \(b_i\). For every other row, obtain a rational feasible witness with strict slack.

Average these witnesses together with one rational feasible point to obtain \(x^0\in P\). It has strict slack in every inequality that is not tight throughout \(P\). All these points, and their average, have polynomial encoding length by exact rational linear programming.

Let \(V\) be the affine space defined by the universally tight rows. A sufficiently small neighborhood of \(x^0\) within \(V\) satisfies all remaining inequalities. Hence
\(V=\operatorname{aff}(P)\).

Gaussian elimination provides rational coordinates
\(x=x^0+Dz\), with \(z\in\mathbb R^d\),
where \(D\) has full column rank and polynomial encoding length. Choose the coordinates \(z\) as deviations of the free original coordinates. If \(d=0\), \(P\) is a singleton and the optimization is immediate.

Suppose \(d>0\), and let
\(Q=\{z:x^0+Dz\in P\}\).
The original coordinate bounds give a rational outer radius \(R\geq1\), of polynomial encoding length, such that
\(Q\subseteq B(0,R)\).

Every nonuniversal inequality becomes
\(c_i^{\mathsf T}z\leq\sigma_i\), with \(\sigma_i>0\).
Thus the rational number
\(r=\min\left\{1,\min_i\frac{\sigma_i}{1+\sum_{\ell=1}^d|c_{i\ell}|}\right\}\)
satisfies
\(B(0,r)\subseteq Q\).
The encoding length of \(r\), including \(\log(1/r)\), is polynomial in the original input length.

First-order cuts and a retained ball

Set \(f(z)=H(x^0+Dz)\). Since the original gradient coordinates are bounded by \(\mu_t v_{tj}\), a rational Lipschitz bound on \(Q\) is
\(M=1+\sum_{t,j}\mu_t v_{tj}\sum_{\ell=1}^d|D_{tj,\ell}|\).
In particular,
\(|f(z)-f(z')|\leq M\|z-z'\|_2\)
for \(z,z'\in Q\). Both \(M\) and \(\log M\) have polynomial encoding length.

Let \(z^*\) maximize \(f\), and put
\(\alpha=\eta/(8MR)\).
By convexity of \(Q\), the ball
\(\mathcal B=(1-\alpha)z^*+\alpha B(0,r)\)
lies in \(Q\). Its radius is \(\alpha r\). Every \(y\in\mathcal B\) satisfies
\(\|y-z^*\|_2\leq2\alpha R\), and therefore
\(f(y)\geq f(z^*)-\eta/4\).

Run the rational central-cut ellipsoid method from \(B(0,R)\). At a query point \(z\):

  • If \(z\notin Q\), an explicitly violated rational inequality gives a central cut retaining all of \(Q\).
  • If \(z\in Q\), compute its exact rational gradient and retain the halfspace

\(\nabla f(z)^{\mathsf T}(y-z)\geq0\).
If this gradient is zero, concavity proves that \(z\) is optimal.

Suppose every feasible query point had value less than \(f(z^*)-\eta/2\). Concavity would then imply, for every \(y\in\mathcal B\),
\(\nabla f(z)^{\mathsf T}(y-z)\geq f(y)-f(z)>\eta/4\).
Thus every cut would retain \(\mathcal B\).

After
\(O\!\left(d^2\log\frac{R}{\alpha r}\right)\)
iterations, the containing ellipsoid has volume smaller than a ball of radius \(\alpha r\), contradicting retention of \(\mathcal B\). Hence some feasible query point has objective error at most \(\eta/2\).

All cut coefficients are rational and polynomial-time computable. The rational ellipsoid implementation keeps query points of polynomial encoding length.

Finally, evaluate \(f\) at the feasible query points to absolute error \(\eta/8\), and select the largest evaluated value. The selected point has objective error at most \(3\eta/4\), hence at most \(\eta\).

These evaluations have polynomial bit complexity. For completeness, for a positive rational \(a\), write \(a=2^kq\) with \(1\leq q<2\), and use
\(\log q=2\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2\ell+1}}{2\ell+1}\), where \(z=(q-1)/(q+1)\).
Here \(0\leq z<1/3\), so the truncation error decreases geometrically. The same series at \(z=1/3\) evaluates \(\log2\). Accounting for the rational objective weights requires only polynomially many terms and bits.

This proves the asserted polynomial-time objective approximation, with exact membership in \(P\).

3. Objective accuracy yields a small linearization gap

Define
\(W_t=\sum_j v_{tj}s_j/\mu_t\)
and
\(A=\sum_t\mu_t W_t^2/B_t\).

For every \(x,z\in P\), both type-\(t\) utilities lie in \([0,W_t]\). The derivative bound from Section 1 therefore implies, for \(0\leq\theta\leq1\),
\(H(x+\theta(z-x))\geq H(x)+\theta\nabla H(x)^{\mathsf T}(z-x)-A\theta^2/2\).

To justify this explicitly, the derivative of the function
\(\phi(\theta)=H(x+\theta(z-x))\)
changes by at most
\(\theta\sum_t\mu_t\bigl(v_t^{\mathsf T}(z_t-x_t)\bigr)^2/B_t\leq A\theta\).
Integrating from zero to \(\theta\) gives the displayed inequality.

Choose
\(\eta=\varepsilon^2/[4(A+1)]\)
and compute \(x\in P\) with \(H^*-H(x)\leq\eta\).

Let
\(G(x)=\max_{z\in P}\nabla H(x)^{\mathsf T}(z-x)\).
This maximum exists because \(P\) is compact. Applying the preceding inequality to a maximizing \(z\), and using \(H(x+\theta(z-x))\leq H^*\), gives
\(\theta G(x)-A\theta^2/2\leq\eta\).

Set \(\theta=\varepsilon/[2(A+1)]\), which lies in \((0,1)\). Then
\(G(x)\leq\eta/\theta+A\theta/2 =\varepsilon/2+A\varepsilon/[4(A+1)] <3\varepsilon/4\).

The rational number \(A\) has polynomial encoding length. Consequently,
\(\log(1/\eta)\)
is polynomially bounded in the input length and \(\log(1/\varepsilon)\).

4. Obtain rational multipliers by linear programming

At the computed \(x\), retain the rational numbers
\(u_t=\max\{B_t,v_t^{\mathsf T}x_t\}\),
\(\beta_t=B_t/u_t\), and
\(\delta_t=(B_t-v_t^{\mathsf T}x_t)_+\).

Solve the rational linear program
\(\max_{z\in P}\sum_{t,j}\mu_t\beta_t v_{tj}z_{tj}\).

Its dual minimizes
\(D(p,\lambda)=\sum_jp_js_j+\sum_k\lambda_kb_k\)
subject to the required multiplier domains and
\(q_{tj}:=p_j+\sum_k a_{ktj}\lambda_k\geq\beta_t v_{tj}\)
for every \(t,j\).

The factors \(\mu_t>0\) cancel from these dual constraints. In particular, no replacement of the mass instance by a potentially enormous finite population is needed.

The primal linear program is feasible and bounded. Its dual is also explicitly feasible: take \(\lambda=0\) and
\(p_j=\max_t\beta_t v_{tj}\).
Thus exact rational linear programming returns polynomial-length optimal multipliers, and linear-programming strong duality gives
\(D(p,\lambda)-\sum_{t,j}\mu_t\beta_t v_{tj}x_{tj}=G(x)\).

5. Verify the original Lagrange dual certificate

For these multipliers, the original dual is
\(g(p,\lambda)=D(p,\lambda)+ \sum_t\mu_t\sup_{y,d\geq0,\ v_t^{\mathsf T}y+d>0} [B_t\log(v_t^{\mathsf T}y+d)-d-q_t^{\mathsf T}y]\).

For every positive \(u\), concavity of the logarithm gives the tangent inequality
\(B_t\log u\leq B_t\log(B_t/\beta_t)-B_t+\beta_t u\).

Since \(q_{tj}\geq\beta_t v_{tj}\) and \(\beta_t\leq1\), it follows that
\(B_t\log(v_t^{\mathsf T}y+d)-d-q_t^{\mathsf T}y \leq B_t\log(B_t/\beta_t)-B_t\).

Therefore \(g(p,\lambda)\) is bounded above by a finite number. Each supremum is also bounded below by its value at \(y=0,d=B_t\), so \(g(p,\lambda)\) is finite.

Because \(B_t/\beta_t=u_t\),
\(g(p,\lambda)\leq D(p,\lambda)+\sum_t\mu_t[B_t\log u_t-B_t]\).

On the other hand,
\(F(x,\delta)=H(x)=\sum_t\mu_t[B_t\log u_t-\delta_t]\).
The definitions imply
\(B_t-\delta_t=\beta_t v_t^{\mathsf T}x_t\):
when the utility is below \(B_t\), \(\beta_t=1\); when it is at least \(B_t\), \(\delta_t=0\).

Subtracting gives
\(g(p,\lambda)-F(x,\delta) \leq D(p,\lambda)-\sum_{t,j}\mu_t\beta_t v_{tj}x_{tj} =G(x)<3\varepsilon/4\).

Finally, \(x\) is exactly feasible, and the multiplier signs are correct. Weak duality therefore gives
\(g(p,\lambda)\geq F(x,\delta)\).
Combining the bounds,
\(0\leq g(p,\lambda)-F(x,\delta)<\varepsilon\).

All logarithm arguments satisfy
\(v_t^{\mathsf T}x_t+\delta_t=u_t\geq B_t>0\).
All primal constraint residuals are zero.

6. Complexity conclusion

The algorithm consists of rational-polytope preprocessing, polynomial-precision concave optimization, and one exact rational linear program for the multipliers. Every intermediate bound and every output has polynomial encoding length.

Small masses can make \(s_j/\mu_t\), curvature bounds, and multiplier magnitudes large, but only their logarithms enter the bit-complexity bounds. Arbitrary signed coefficients affect neither the concavity argument nor the linear-programming certificate.

Thus the requested rational primal–dual approximation is computable in the stated polynomial time.
