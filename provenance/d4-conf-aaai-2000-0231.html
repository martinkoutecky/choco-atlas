Conclusion

The revenue-threshold problem is in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Divisibility of the accepted population mass makes the stated problem an exact rational linear-feasibility problem.

1. Exact linear formulation

For each listed type \(t\), introduce one real variable \(x_t\), representing its accepted mass. Consider the following system:

  • Population bounds: \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\).
  • Supply constraints: \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}q_{tg}x_t\le s_g\) for every \(g\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\).
  • Revenue threshold: \(-\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}p_tx_t\le-R\).

This system has exactly \(\tau\) variables and \(m+2\tau+1\) linear inequalities.

Equivalence. If the auction instance admits feasible accepted masses with revenue at least \(R\), those masses satisfy all the displayed inequalities. Conversely, any solution of the system gives allowed accepted masses, respects every supply constraint, and has revenue at least \(R\). Thus the answer to the decision problem is yes exactly when this system is feasible.

No additional integrality condition is needed. By the premises, every accepted member of type \(t\) receives the entire bundle \(q_t\), while the mass of accepted members may be any \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\). Its aggregate consumption is therefore exactly \(q_{tg}x_t\) for good \(g\).

2. Polynomial encoding size and exact decision

We use the standard polynomial-time linear-feasibility theorem: for a binary-encoded rational matrix \(A\) and rational vector \(b\), feasibility of \(Ax\le b\) over real variables can be decided deterministically in time polynomial in the dimensions and total encoding length. This is an exact feasibility theorem, applicable also to systems whose feasible sets have empty interior. See [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239).

We verify the encoding hypothesis explicitly. Let \(D\) be the product of the positive denominators of all rational numerical inputs. Because their binary encodings contribute to \(L\), the binary length of \(D\) is \(O(L+1)\). Multiplying every inequality by the positive integer \(D\) preserves feasibility and gives integer coefficients.

Every resulting coefficient has bit length \(O(L+1)\): each is \(D\) times an input rational, an input integer, or one of \(0,1,-1\). The augmented matrix consequently has encoding length at most

\(O\bigl((m+2\tau+1)(\tau+1)(L+1)\bigr).\)

Computing \(D\) and the scaled coefficients uses polynomially many operations on integers of polynomial bit length, so the construction itself takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

Applying the linear-feasibility theorem therefore decides the original threshold problem in polynomial time. In particular, equality at the threshold is handled exactly; the argument does not depend on a numerical tolerance.

For completeness, the revenue maximum also exists. The original feasible set contains the zero vector, is closed, and lies inside the bounded box \(\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}[0,\mu_t]\). It is therefore compact, and the continuous linear revenue function attains its maximum.

3. Why the discrete-auction results do not imply hardness here

CAMUS concerns selecting a feasible subset of discrete bids, and its completeness theorem guarantees an optimal allocation in that setting. [CAMUS paper, problem definition and Theorem 2](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2000/AAAI00-009.pdf). The nearby work uses linear programming as a fractional relaxation to bound the discrete optimum. [Gonen and Lehmann, Section 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0202016).

For the present model, the continuous variables are themselves the permitted decisions, so the linear formulation is exact.

A small instance makes the distinction explicit. Take \(m=\tau=1\), \(\mu_1=1\), \(q_{11}=2\), \(s_1=1\), and \(p_1=1\). Feasibility requires \(0\le x_1\le\tfrac12\), so the optimal revenue is \(\tfrac12\). Every accepted bidder still receives two units; only half the population is accepted. Replacing the permitted interval by the discrete requirement \(x_1\in\{0,1\}\) would instead give optimal revenue zero.

Thus the polynomial-time result applies to the full stated problem, with arbitrary binary-encoded quantities and prices and one variable per listed type.
