The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a graph \(H=(T,F)\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), and affinities \(w_{ts}=-1\) for \(\{t,s\}\in F\) and \(0\) otherwise, does there exist a stable allocation among exactly three nonempty coalitions? An allocation is \(x_{ti}\geq 0\), with \(\sum_{i=1}^{3}x_{ti}=\mu_t\) and positive total mass in each coalition; type-\(t\) utility in coalition \(i\) is \(u_t(i)=\sum_{s\in T}w_{ts}x_{si}\). Require individual rationality \(u_t(i)\geq 0\) for each occupied type-coalition pair, and no blocking deviation of the paper's exact-\(k\) form interpreted over mass, in which every participating mass strictly benefits while exactly three coalitions remain.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

The input is a finite simple undirected graph \(H=(T,F)\) and a society given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|T|\geq1\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; set \(m=|T|=\tau\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Affinities are fixed: \(w_{ts}=-1\) when \(\{t,s\}\in F\), and \(w_{ts}=0\) otherwise, including \(w_{tt}=0\). Coalitions need not be connected.
An allocation consists of real masses \(x_{ti}\geq0\), for \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\), satisfying \(\sum_i x_{ti}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{ti}>0\) for each \(i\). Types may split across coalitions. Define \(u_t(i)=\sum_s w_{ts}x_{si}\).
A blocking deviation selects real participating masses \(0\leq y_{ti}\leq x_{ti}\) and a destination \(r\in\{1,2,3\}\), with \(y_{tr}=x_{tr}\) for all \(t\), \(\sum_{t,i\ne r}y_{ti}>0\), and \(\sum_t(x_{ti}-y_{ti})>0\) for every \(i\ne r\).
These masses form one coalition with type masses \(b_t=\sum_i y_{ti}\); all nonparticipants remain in their original coalitions. The deviation blocks precisely when \(\sum_s w_{ts}b_s>u_t(i)\) for every pair \((t,i)\) with \(y_{ti}>0\), including participants from the destination coalition.
An allocation is stable if it admits no blocking deviation and is individually rational: \(u_t(i)\geq0\) whenever \(x_{ti}>0\).

Is deciding whether a stable allocation among exactly three nonempty coalitions exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite indivisible agents, existence in the corresponding exact-\(k\) core is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for every fixed \(k>2\); the three-coalition reduction uses only affinities \(-1\) and \(0\). The [journal extension](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/michael.wooldridge/pubs/aij2018a.pdf) retains this result and requires deviations to absorb one whole coalition while leaving every other coalition nonempty.
No result settling the continuous problem defined above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the mass deviation explicit using participating mass from each original type-coalition pair; one entire destination coalition participates, receives positive outside mass, and every other coalition retains positive mass.
  • Allowed allocation and deviation masses to be arbitrary nonnegative reals, while input masses remain binary rationals.
  • Specified a simple undirected graph and zero within-type affinity, following the exclusion of self loops.
  • Allowed any nonempty type support, including fewer than three types: divisible masses can populate three coalitions, so the discrete bound on coalition count does not impose a support-size bound.
  • Defined m as the number of graph vertices, equal here to the support size, and L as the total input bit length.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. More precisely, for every permitted positive mass vector, a stable allocation among exactly three nonempty coalitions exists if and only if \(\ H\ \) is three-colorable. Thus splitting types does not remove the computational obstruction.

Here, “three-colorable” means that there exists a map \(\ c:T\to\{1,2,3\}\ \) assigning different colors to adjacent vertices; the map need not use all three colors.

1. No allowed deviation can block

Fix any feasible allocation \(\ x\ \), without assuming individual rationality. Consider a deviation satisfying the stated participation constraints, with destination \(\ r\ \) and resulting masses \(\ b_t=\sum_i y_{ti}\ \).

Because the deviation absorbs the entire destination coalition, for every \(\ s\in T\ \),
\[ b_s=x_{sr}+\sum_{i\ne r}y_{si}\geq x_{sr}. \]
All affinities are nonpositive. Consequently, for every \(\ t\in T\ \),
\[ \sum_s w_{ts}b_s\leq \sum_s w_{ts}x_{sr}=u_t(r). \]

The destination is nonempty, so some \(\ t\ \) satisfies \(\ x_{tr}>0\ \). For this type, \(\ y_{tr}=x_{tr}>0\ \), and blocking requires the strict inequality \(\ \sum_s w_{ts}b_s>u_t(r)\ \). This contradicts the preceding inequality.

Therefore every feasible allocation admits no blocking deviation. Under the definition in the question, stability is equivalent to individual rationality.

2. Individual rationality is equivalent to independent coalition supports

Define the support of coalition \(\ i\ \) by \(\ S_i=\{t\in T:x_{ti}>0\}\ \), and define the neighborhood of \(\ t\ \) by \(\ N_H(t)=\{s\in T:\{t,s\}\in F\}\ \). The prescribed affinities give
\[ u_t(i)=-\sum_{s\in N_H(t)}x_{si}\leq0. \]

Suppose the allocation is individually rational. Whenever \(\ x_{ti}>0\ \), individual rationality requires \(\ u_t(i)\geq0\ \), hence
\[ \sum_{s\in N_H(t)}x_{si}=0. \]
Every summand is nonnegative, so \(\ x_{si}=0\ \) for every neighbor \(\ s\ \) of \(\ t\ \). Thus no edge has both endpoints in \(\ S_i\ \): each coalition support is an independent set.

Conversely, suppose every \(\ S_i\ \) is independent. If \(\ x_{ti}>0\ \), no neighbor of \(\ t\ \) has positive mass in coalition \(\ i\ \). The displayed utility formula then yields \(\ u_t(i)=0\ \). This proves individual rationality.

Therefore an allocation is stable exactly when all three coalition supports are independent.

3. Equivalence with three-colorability

A stable allocation yields a proper coloring. Since \(\ \sum_i x_{ti}=\mu_t>0\ \), each type occupies at least one coalition. Choose any such coalition and set \(\ c(t)=i\ \).

If an edge \(\ \{t,s\}\in F\ \) had \(\ c(t)=c(s)=i\ \), both endpoints would belong to the independent set \(\ S_i\ \), a contradiction. Hence \(\ c\ \) is a proper three-coloring. This argument applies even when types split across several coalitions.

A proper coloring yields a stable allocation with exactly three nonempty coalitions. Initially allocate every type entirely to its color:
\[ x_{t,c(t)}=\mu_t,\qquad x_{ti}=0\quad\text{for }i\ne c(t). \]
All coalition supports are independent.

If a coalition label \(\ j\ \) is empty, choose any positive entry \(\ x_{ti}=a>0\ \) in an occupied coalition. Replace \(\ x_{ti}\ \) by \(\ a/2\ \) and set \(\ x_{tj}=a/2\ \). This preserves every type’s total mass. Coalition \(\ i\ \) remains nonempty, its support remains independent, and the newly occupied coalition has singleton support \(\ \{t\}\ \), which is independent because the graph is simple.

Repeat for any remaining empty label. There are at most two repetitions. The resulting allocation has exactly three nonempty coalitions and independent supports, so Sections 1–2 prove that it is stable.

This also handles support sizes \(\ \tau=1\ \) and \(\ \tau=2\ \).

4. Complexity and binary mass encoding

We use the standard theorem that deciding three-colorability of finite simple undirected graphs is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; see [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer, *Some simplified NP-complete graph problems*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1).

For a polynomial reduction, let \(\ G\ \) be a nonempty input graph with \(\ n\ \) vertices. Set
\[ q=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil,\qquad N=2^q. \]
Construct \(\ H\ \) by adding \(\ N-n\ \) isolated vertices to \(\ G\ \), and assign every vertex the mass \(\ \mu_t=2^{-q}\ \).

These masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one. Moreover, \(\ N\leq2n\ \), and each mass has encoding length \(\ O(1+\log n)\ \), so the construction has polynomial total bit length. Adding isolated vertices preserves three-colorability. By Section 3,
\[ G\text{ is three-colorable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (H,\mu)\text{ admits a stable allocation}. \]
This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness even for uniform dyadic masses.

For membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), a certificate is simply a proper three-coloring of \(\ H\ \). It has length \(\ O(m)\ \), and checking every edge takes polynomial time. Section 3 proves that this certificate characterizes stable-allocation existence; no certificate involving arbitrary real masses is needed.

Therefore the problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, and a running time polynomial in \(\ m,\tau,L\ \) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
