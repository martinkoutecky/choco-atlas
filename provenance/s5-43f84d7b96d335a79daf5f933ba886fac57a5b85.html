Theorem 4.2 (Sample Complexity) — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_t\) over \(\tau\) approval-interval types \(I_t\) and i.i.d. issue samples \(S=(x_1,\ldots,x_q)\) from \(P\), what sample size and running time suffice to return an interval \(J\) with \(\Phi_\mu(J)\ge \sup_K\Phi_\mu(K)-2\epsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\), where \(\ell_\mu(x)=\sum_t\mu_t(2\mathbf 1\{x\in I_t\}-1)\) and \(\Phi_\mu(J)=\mathbb E_{x\sim P}[\ell_\mu(x)\mathbf 1\{x\in J\}]\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\ge1\) approval-interval types \(I_t=[a_t,b_t]\subset\mathbb R\), with finite binary-rational endpoints \(a_t\le b_t\), and positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(\mathcal H=\{[a,b]:a,b\in\mathbb R,\ a\le b\}\); singleton intervals and intervals containing no observed issues are allowed. Define \(\ell_\mu(x)=\sum_t\mu_t(2\mathbf1\{x\in I_t\}-1)\) and \(\Phi_\mu(J)=\mathbb E_{x\sim P}[\ell_\mu(x)\mathbf1\{x\in J\}]\).
The unknown \(P\) is an arbitrary Borel probability distribution on \(\mathbb R\), possibly with atoms; access consists solely of \(m=q\ge1\) i.i.d. issue samples. Real sample coordinates permit exact comparisons and copying; count coordinate comparisons separately from rational bit operations. Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the rational input, including rational \(0<\epsilon<1\) and \(0<\delta<1\).
The required output is \(J\in\mathcal H\) satisfying \(\Phi_\mu(J)\ge\sup_{K\in\mathcal H}\Phi_\mu(K)-2\epsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\), uniformly over societies and \(P\).

Does this learning problem admit polynomial sample complexity and polynomial running time, or is obtaining this guarantee NP-hard? What sample size \(m\) and running time suffice, as functions of \(\tau,L,\epsilon^{-1},\log(1/\delta)\), with running time also expressed in \(m\)?

For \(n\) equally weighted discrete voters with unnormalized labels, uniform convergence to accuracy \(\epsilon\) is established using \(O(n^2\epsilon^{-2}(\log(n/\epsilon)+\log(1/\delta)))\) samples, yielding additive \(2\epsilon\) accuracy for a genuine empirical maximizer. A polynomial ERM implementation is claimed, but its sample-endpoint restriction omits intervals containing no samples. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21811v1)
The checked version contains no theorem for arbitrary rational type masses.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize the positive type masses to sum to one, interpreting the society as a probability distribution over types.
  • Use nonempty bounded closed approval intervals with finite binary-rational endpoints; allow singleton intervals.
  • Retain all nonempty bounded closed hypothesis intervals, including intervals containing no samples, without imposing the algorithm's sample-endpoint restriction.
  • Allow arbitrary Borel issue distributions, including atoms, and preserve closed-endpoint membership.
  • Use exact comparison and copying access for real sample coordinates, with rational bit operations measured separately using L; identify the sample-size notation m with q.
  • Take rational accuracy and confidence parameters in (0,1), and require the guarantee uniformly over the unknown issue distribution.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The problem admits polynomial sample complexity and polynomial running time, including for arbitrary rational type masses and distributions with atoms.

A sufficient sample size is
\( m=\left\lceil 18\epsilon^{-2}\ln(2/\delta)\right\rceil. \)
The sample bound is independent of \(\tau\) and \(L\).

For any sample size \(m\), an exact empirical maximizer can be computed using
\( O((m+\tau)\log(m+1)) \)
coordinate comparisons and
\( O\!\left(L^3+(m+\tau)(L+\log(m+1))\log(m+1)\right) \)
bit operations. These are conservative bounds using elementary integer arithmetic. The algorithm requires only comparisons and copying of sampled real coordinates; it performs no arithmetic on those coordinates.

1. Uniform convergence, including atoms

Let \(P_m\) be the empirical probability measure of the \(m\) independent samples, and define
\( \widehat{\Phi}_m(J)=\int \ell_\mu(x)\mathbf1\{x\in J\}\,dP_m(x). \)

Write
\( F(z)=P((-\infty,z]),\qquad F_m(z)=P_m((-\infty,z]), \)
and
\( D_m=\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}|F_m(z)-F(z)|. \)

The Dvoretzky–Kiefer–Wolfowitz inequality with Massart’s constant states that, for independent identically distributed real random variables with any distribution function and every \(u>0\),
\( \Pr(D_m>u)\le 2\exp(-2mu^2). \)
Its hypotheses apply here: \(P\) is a Borel probability measure on \(\mathbb R\), and the samples are independent with common law \(P\). Continuity of \(F\) is not required. See [Massart’s theorem](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176990746) and [Dudley’s statement covering arbitrary distribution functions](https://math.mit.edu/~rmd/Research/semtalk.pdf).

For a closed interval \(A=[c,d]\), including a singleton,
\( P(A)=F(d)-F(c-),\qquad P_m(A)=F_m(d)-F_m(c-). \)
Taking left limits in the inequality \(|F_m(z)-F(z)|\le D_m\) gives
\( |F_m(c-)-F(c-)|\le D_m. \)
Consequently,
\( |P_m(A)-P(A)|\le 2D_m. \)
This calculation explicitly retains atoms at the left endpoint.

Since the masses sum to one,
\( \ell_\mu(x)=2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\mathbf1\{x\in I_t\}-1. \)
Thus, for every \(J\in\mathcal H\),
\( \Phi_\mu(J)=2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tP(I_t\cap J)-P(J), \)
with the analogous identity for \(\widehat{\Phi}_m(J)\).

Each intersection \(I_t\cap J\) is either a closed interval or empty. An empty intersection has discrepancy zero. Therefore,
\( \begin{aligned} |\widehat{\Phi}_m(J)-\Phi_\mu(J)| &\le 2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t |P_m(I_t\cap J)-P(I_t\cap J)| +|P_m(J)-P(J)|\\ &\le 4D_m\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t+2D_m =6D_m. \end{aligned} \)

This bound holds simultaneously for every \(J\). Applying the preceding probability inequality with \(u=\epsilon/6\) yields
\( \Pr\!\left( \sup_{J\in\mathcal H}|\widehat{\Phi}_m(J)-\Phi_\mu(J)|>\epsilon \right) \le 2\exp(-m\epsilon^2/18). \)
Hence the stated sample size makes this probability at most \(\delta\).

The argument uses the rational masses directly; it does not expand them into an equally weighted voter population.

2. A complete empirical candidate set

Sort and group the samples into distinct coordinates
\( y_1<y_2<\cdots<y_d, \)
with positive multiplicities \(c_1,\ldots,c_d\), where
\( \sum_{j=1}^{d}c_j=m. \)
Define
\( v_j=c_j\ell_\mu(y_j). \)

If an interval contains samples, its included distinct coordinates form a consecutive block \(y_p,\ldots,y_q\). Indeed, containing \(y_p\) and \(y_q\) forces it to contain every coordinate between them. Conversely, \([y_p,y_q]\) realizes that block. Its empirical objective is
\( m^{-1}\sum_{j=p}^{q}v_j. \)

Intervals containing no samples have empirical objective zero. This value must be included in the optimization.

A legal zero-score candidate can be constructed without arithmetic on sampled coordinates. Among the \(m+1\) integer coordinates
\( 0,1,\ldots,m, \)
at least one is absent from the sample, because there are at most \(m\) distinct sampled coordinates. Choose such an integer \(r\) and use the singleton interval \([r,r]\). Its empirical objective is zero.

It follows that the empirical optimum is exactly
\( \max_{J\in\mathcal H}\widehat{\Phi}_m(J) = \frac1m\max\left\{0,\max_{1\le p\le q\le d} \sum_{j=p}^{q}v_j\right\}. \)
Grouping duplicates ensures that every block considered by the algorithm corresponds to an actual interval.

The omitted zero candidate is consequential. For example, take \(\tau=1\), \(I_1=[0,0]\), \(\mu_1=1\), and all samples equal to \(1\). Every interval with sample endpoints has empirical objective \(-1\), whereas \([0,0]\) has empirical objective zero. Thus the sample-endpoint assertion in [Proposition 3.1 of the linked source](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21811v1) needs this additional candidate.

3. Exact polynomial-time optimization

Integer representation of the masses

Write each input mass as
\( \mu_t=p_t/d_t \)
with positive integers \(p_t,d_t\). Set
\( D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}d_t,\qquad W_t=p_t(D/d_t). \)
Then
\( W_t=D\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}W_t=D. \)

The bit length of \(D\) is at most one plus the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Moreover, \(W_t\le D\), so every \(W_t\) also has \(O(L)\) bits. Although the numerical value of \(D\) can be large, its representation has polynomial length.

Labels by binary searches and a difference array

For each type \(t\), binary-search the sorted distinct samples to find
\( s_t=\min\{j:y_j\ge a_t\},\qquad e_t=\max\{j:y_j\le b_t\}. \)
If either set is empty or \(s_t>e_t\), that type covers no samples.

Otherwise, in an initially zero difference array, add \(W_t\) at index \(s_t\) and subtract \(W_t\) at index \(e_t+1\). Prefix sums then give
\( A_j=\sum_{t:y_j\in I_t}W_t. \)
The searches use inclusive inequalities, so endpoint atoms and singleton approval intervals are handled correctly.

Define the integer label
\( h_j=2A_j-D=D\ell_\mu(y_j), \)
and block weight
\( z_j=c_jh_j=Dv_j. \)
Maximizing block sums of the \(z_j\) is equivalent to maximizing block sums of the \(v_j\), since \(D>0\).

Maximum consecutive sum

Define prefix sums
\( S_0=0,\qquad S_k=\sum_{j=1}^{k}z_j. \)
For every nonempty block,
\( \sum_{j=p}^{q}z_j=S_q-S_{p-1}. \)
For fixed \(q\), the largest such sum is therefore
\( S_q-\min_{0\le i<q}S_i. \)

A single scan maintains the minimum preceding prefix sum, its index, and the best block found. Initialize the overall best value to zero with candidate \([r,r]\). Replace that candidate whenever a strictly positive, larger block sum is found. If the stored minimizing prefix index is \(i\), the corresponding interval is \([y_{i+1},y_q]\).

This proves that the returned interval is a genuine empirical maximizer over all of \(\mathcal H\).

Complexity

Sorting and grouping take
\( O(m\log(m+1)) \)
coordinate comparisons. The endpoint searches take
\( O(\tau\log(m+1)) \)
comparisons. The missing integer \(r\) can be found by merging the sorted samples with the increasing integer candidates, using \(O(m)\) further comparisons.

For arithmetic, let
\( B=L+\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil. \)
Since \(0\le A_j\le D\), we have \(|h_j|\le D\). Every prefix sum satisfies
\( |S_k| \le \sum_{j=1}^{d}c_j|h_j| \le mD, \)
so \(B+O(1)\) bits suffice.

Computing \(D\) and all \(W_t\) costs \(O(L^3)\) bit operations using elementary multiplication and division; here \(\tau\le L\) because every type occupies positive input length. Difference-array updates and prefix scans use \(O((m+\tau)B)\) bit operations. Each block weight may be formed by repeated addition, with at most \(m\) additions in total.

Including binary-search indices and sorting bookkeeping gives the conservative bound
\( T_{\mathrm{bit}} = O\!\left(L^3+(m+\tau)B\log(m+1)\right). \)

4. From empirical optimality to the required guarantee

On the uniform-convergence event proved in Section 1, let \(\widehat J\) be the empirical maximizer constructed above. For every \(K\in\mathcal H\),
\( \begin{aligned} \Phi_\mu(\widehat J) &\ge \widehat{\Phi}_m(\widehat J)-\epsilon\\ &\ge \widehat{\Phi}_m(K)-\epsilon\\ &\ge \Phi_\mu(K)-2\epsilon. \end{aligned} \)
Taking the supremum over \(K\) proves
\( \Phi_\mu(\widehat J) \ge \sup_{K\in\mathcal H}\Phi_\mu(K)-2\epsilon. \)
This argument does not assume that the population supremum is attained. The event has probability at least \(1-\delta\).

For a sample-count rule using only exact integer arithmetic, one may instead choose
\( k=\lceil\log_2(2/\delta)\rceil,\qquad m=\lceil18k/\epsilon^2\rceil. \)
Compute \(k\) as the smallest integer satisfying \(2^k\delta\ge2\). This sample size is sufficient because \(k\ge\ln(2/\delta)\).

Finally, with
\( s=\epsilon^{-2}\ln(2/\delta), \)
the sufficient sample size is \(O(s)\), and the bounds become
\( O((\tau+s)\log(s+1)) \)
coordinate comparisons and
\( O\!\left(L^3+(\tau+s)(L+\log(s+1))\log(s+1)\right) \)
bit operations. Thus all requested bounds are polynomial in \(\tau,L,\epsilon^{-1}\), and \(\log(1/\delta)\).
