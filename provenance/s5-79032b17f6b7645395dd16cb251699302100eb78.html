The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of \(d\) policies, \(k\) criteria, evaluator types \(t\) specifying a complete ranking of the policies on every criterion, and rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), form \(P^\mu_j(a,b)=\sum_t\mu_tQ^t_j(a,b)\). There are \(m=d\ge2\) policies and \(k\ge1\) criteria. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) evaluator types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Each type specifies a strict complete ranking on each criterion; \(L\) is the total binary input length.
Set \(Q^t_j(a,b)=1\) when type \(t\) ranks \(a\) above \(b\), \(0\) when below, and \(Q^t_j(a,a)=1/2\). Define \(P^\mu_j(a,b)=\sum_t\mu_tQ^t_j(a,b)\) and \(P^\mu(\pi,\pi')=(\pi^\top P^\mu_j\pi')_{j=1}^k\), with \(\pi,\pi'\in\Delta_d\).
A valid target set \(S\subseteq[0,1]^k\) is nonempty, closed, convex, and upward closed within the cube. Fix \(q\in[1,\infty]\); access to \(S\) is through an exact distance oracle \(D_S(z)=\inf_{s\in S}\|z-s\|_q\) for \(z\in\mathbb R^k\).
For binary rational \(\epsilon>0\), put \(v_\mu(\pi)=\max_{\pi'\in\Delta_d}D_S(P^\mu(\pi,\pi'))\). The required output satisfies \(v_\mu(\pi)\le\min_{\hat\pi\in\Delta_d}v_\mu(\hat\pi)+\epsilon\).
Does this additive approximation problem admit an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L,\epsilon^{-1}\), counting distance-oracle calls and real arithmetic operations, or is it NP-hard in this oracle model?
Known results: for arbitrary preference tensors satisfying \(P_j(a,b)+P_j(b,a)=1\), an additive \(\epsilon\) approximation uses \(O(k^{2/q}d^2\log^2(d)/\epsilon^2)\) distance-oracle calls, with \(k^{2/q}=1\) for \(q=\infty\). This result already allows population comparison probabilities and therefore covers the stated mass model; it is not limited to finite societies. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01850).

The problem admits an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L,\epsilon^{-1}\) in the stated oracle model.

The approximation theorem supplied in the question already applies to the aggregated preference tensor. Below is also a self-contained deterministic proof with a looser polynomial bound. This explicitly accounts for arithmetic operations and guarantees that the returned distribution satisfies the required inequality.

1. Aggregating the listed types

For every criterion \(j\) and policies \(a,b\), strict completeness gives

\(Q_j^t(a,b)+Q_j^t(b,a)=1\).

Consequently,

\(P_j^\mu(a,b)+P_j^\mu(b,a)=\sum_t\mu_t=1\),

and \(P_j^\mu(a,a)=1/2\). Moreover, every entry lies in \([0,1]\). Thus the aggregated tensor satisfies the hypotheses of the supplied approximation theorem.

The tensor can be constructed using \(O(\tau km^2)\) arithmetic operations, after converting each ranking into positions. This uses only the listed types. In particular, rational masses need not be expanded into a population of individual voters.

Exact rational preprocessing also has polynomial bit complexity: the product of the input mass denominators is a common denominator whose binary length is at most the sum of their binary lengths, hence \(O(L)\).

For the remainder, write \(P_j=P_j^\mu\).

2. A convex objective with an exact value oracle

Let \(\Delta_m=\{x\in\mathbb R^m:x_a\ge0,\ \sum_a x_a=1\}\), and let \(e_b\) denote its \(b\)-th vertex. Define the linear map

\(A_bx=\bigl(\sum_{a=1}^m x_aP_j(a,b)\bigr)_{j=1}^k\),

and extend the objective to all of \(\mathbb R^m\) by

\(F(x)=\max_{b\in[m]}D_S(A_bx)\).

Because \(S\) is nonempty and compact, every distance minimization defining \(D_S\) attains its minimum. Convexity of \(S\), followed by the triangle inequality, gives

\(D_S(\lambda z+(1-\lambda)w)\le\lambda D_S(z)+(1-\lambda)D_S(w)\)

for \(0\le\lambda\le1\): take closest points to \(z,w\), and use their convex combination as a candidate closest point. Thus \(D_S\) is convex. The triangle inequality also gives

\(\lvert D_S(z)-D_S(w)\rvert\le\|z-w\|_q\).

It follows that \(F\) is convex. Define \(C=k\) when \(q<\infty\), and \(C=1\) when \(q=\infty\). Since the tensor entries belong to \([0,1]\),

\(\|A_bx-A_by\|_q\le C\|x-y\|_1\),

so

\(\lvert F(x)-F(y)\rvert\le C\|x-y\|_1\).

For \(x,\pi'\in\Delta_m\),

\(P^\mu(x,\pi')=\sum_b\pi'_bA_bx\).

Convexity of distance therefore implies

\(D_S(P^\mu(x,\pi'))\le\sum_b\pi'_bD_S(A_bx)\le F(x)\).

Conversely, choosing \(\pi'=e_b\) gives each term defining \(F(x)\). Hence

\(v_\mu(x)=F(x)\qquad(x\in\Delta_m)\).

Thus one evaluation of \(F\) requires \(m\) distance-oracle calls and \(O(km^2)\) arithmetic operations. This also holds outside the simplex, because the oracle accepts every point of \(\mathbb R^k\).

Finally, continuity of \(F\) and compactness of \(\Delta_m\) ensure the existence of a minimizer.

3. Deterministic approximate subgradients from values

We prove the value-oracle lemma needed for the algorithm.

Lemma. Suppose \(f:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R\) is convex and satisfies

\(\lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert\le C\|x-y\|_1\),

where \(C\ge1\). For \(x\in\Delta_m\) and \(\delta>0\), one can deterministically construct \(g\in\mathbb R^m\) satisfying

\(\|g\|_\infty\le C\)

and

\(f(z)\ge f(x)+g^\top(z-x)-\delta\qquad\text{for every }z\in\Delta_m\),

using \(O(m(1+C/\delta))\) evaluations of \(f\) and polynomially many arithmetic operations.

Proof. Set

\(\eta=\delta/4,\quad N=\lceil4C/\eta\rceil,\quad M=\lceil8C/\eta\rceil+2,\quad r_1=\delta/(8C)\),

and recursively set \(r_{i+1}=r_i/[M(N+1)]\). Start with \(y^0=x\).

At stage \(i\), put \(h_i=r_i/(N+1)\), and evaluate

\(f(y^{i-1}+jh_ie_i),\qquad j=0,\ldots,N+1\).

Define consecutive slopes

\(a_j=\bigl[f(y^{i-1}+(j+1)h_ie_i)-f(y^{i-1}+jh_ie_i)\bigr]/h_i,\qquad j=0,\ldots,N\).

Convexity along this line gives \(a_0\le\cdots\le a_N\), while Lipschitz continuity gives \(\lvert a_j\rvert\le C\). Therefore,

\(\sum_{t=1}^N(a_t-a_{t-1})=a_N-a_0\le2C\).

Choose \(t_i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\) with \(a_{t_i}-a_{t_i-1}\le2C/N\), and set

\(y^i=y^{i-1}+t_ih_ie_i,\qquad g_i=(a_{t_i}+a_{t_i-1})/2\).

In particular, \(\lvert g_i\rvert\le C\).

Let \(y=y^m\). A finite convex function on all of \(\mathbb R^m\) has a subgradient at every point, by the supporting-hyperplane theorem; its hypotheses hold here because \(f\) is convex and Lipschitz continuous. Choose \(u\in\partial f(y)\). The Lipschitz bound implies \(\|u\|_\infty\le C\), by applying the subgradient inequality to \(y+se_i\) and \(y-se_i\).

For each coordinate, the subgradient inequality gives

\(\bigl[f(y)-f(y-h_ie_i)\bigr]/h_i\le u_i\le\bigl[f(y+h_ie_i)-f(y)\bigr]/h_i\).

Replacing \(y\) by \(y^i\) changes either slope by at most \(2C\|y-y^i\|_1/h_i\). At \(y^i\), the two slopes are exactly \(a_{t_i-1}\) and \(a_{t_i}\). Consequently,

\(\lvert u_i-g_i\rvert\le C/N+2C\|y-y^i\|_1/h_i\).

Every subsequent coordinate shift has length at most its corresponding radius. Since the radii decrease by a factor of at least two,

\(\|y-y^i\|_1\le2r_{i+1},\qquad \|y-x\|_1\le2r_1\).

Using \(r_{i+1}/h_i=1/M\), we obtain

\(\lvert u_i-g_i\rvert\le C/N+4C/M\le\eta/4+\eta/2\le\eta\).

For any \(z\in\Delta_m\), the subgradient inequality now yields

\(f(z)\ge f(y)+u^\top(z-y)\ge f(x)+g^\top(z-x)-2C\|y-x\|_1-\eta\|z-x\|_1\).

The simplex has diameter at most two in the \(\ell_1\) norm. Hence the error is at most

\(4Cr_1+2\eta=\delta\).

There are \(m(N+2)\) evaluations. All query points, slopes, and comparisons use arithmetic operations alone. This proves the lemma.

4. The approximation algorithm and its guarantee

Put \(e=\min\{\epsilon,1\}\), and choose

\(\delta=e/4,\qquad \gamma=e/(2mC^2),\qquad T=\lceil4mC^2/e^2\rceil\).

Start with any \(x_1\in\Delta_m\). At each iteration, apply the lemma to \(F\) at \(x_t\), obtaining \(g_t\), and update

\(x_{t+1}=\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta_m}(x_t-\gamma g_t)\),

where the projection is Euclidean. Return

\(\bar x=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T x_t\).

This projection uses arithmetic and comparisons: sort the coordinates of the vector being projected and find the threshold \(\lambda\) for which the projected coordinates are \(\max\{w_i-\lambda,0\}\) and sum to one.

Let \(x^*\) minimize \(F\) on the simplex. The projection optimality condition implies

\(\|x_{t+1}-x^*\|_2^2\le\|x_t-x^*\|_2^2-2\gamma g_t^\top(x_t-x^*)+\gamma^2\|g_t\|_2^2\).

The lemma gives

\(F(x_t)-F(x^*)\le g_t^\top(x_t-x^*)+\delta\),

and \(\|g_t\|_2^2\le mC^2\). Summing the projection inequality and using \(\|x_1-x^*\|_2^2\le2\) gives

\(T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T[F(x_t)-F(x^*)]\le1/(\gamma T)+\gamma mC^2/2+\delta\).

Our parameter choices bound the three terms by \(e/2,e/4,e/4\), respectively. Convexity of \(F\) therefore gives

\(F(\bar x)\le F(x^*)+e\le F(x^*)+\epsilon\).

Since \(F=v_\mu\) on the simplex, this is exactly the required output guarantee.

5. Complexity and the sharper supplied result

The construction above uses

\(O(m^3C^3e^{-3})\)

distance-oracle calls. Its arithmetic-operation count, including tensor contractions and simplex projections, is bounded by

\(O\bigl(\operatorname{poly}(L)+\tau km^2+km^4C^3e^{-3}\bigr)\).

Because \(C\le k\) and \(e^{-1}=\max\{1,\epsilon^{-1}\}\), both bounds are polynomial in the requested parameters. The small radii in the lemma require only polynomially many arithmetic operations to construct; the stipulated model allows exact real arithmetic.

The sharper known result supplied in the question gives \(O(k^{2/q}m^2\log^2(m)/\epsilon^2)\) oracle calls, with \(k^{2/q}=1\) for \(q=\infty\). Its preference-tensor and distance-oracle hypotheses match this problem, as verified above. See [Proposition 5 and Appendix D.2 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01850).

Thus the polynomial-algorithm alternative holds in the stated oracle model, including for societies represented by binary rational masses.
