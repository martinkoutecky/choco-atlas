The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given registered candidates \(C\), unregistered candidates \(D\), target \(p\in C\), an agenda on \(C\cup D\), a rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete rankings of \(C\cup D\), and a budget \(k\), does there exist \(D'\subseteq D\) with \(|D'|\leq k\) such that, after restricting each ranking and the agenda to \(C\cup D'\), \(p\) is the earliest candidate in the agenda whose mass of types ranking it above all its successors exceeds \(1/2\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given disjoint finite sets of registered candidates \(C\) and unregistered candidates \(D\), a target \(p\in C\), a strict linear agenda on \(C\cup D\) in which \(p\) is not first, and a binary-encoded nonnegative integer budget \(k\), put \(m=|C\cup D|\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) distinct strict complete rankings of \(C\cup D\) with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For any retained candidate set, restrict every ranking and the agenda to that set, preserving masses. A candidate qualifies if the total mass ranking it above every agenda successor is strictly greater than \(1/2\); equality does not qualify. The candidate with no successors qualifies vacuously. The successive winner is the earliest qualifying candidate.
The decision problem asks whether some \(D'\subseteq D\) with \(|D'|\leq k\) makes \(p\) the successive winner on \(C\cup D'\). Only candidates are added; the society is fixed.

Is this decision problem in \(\mathsf{P}\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), and \(k\).

For finite explicitly listed electorates, constructive control by adding candidates under successive voting is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete and \(\mathsf{W[2]}\)-hard parameterized by \(k\), even with two registered candidates and the target not first in the full agenda. [Yang](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-025-01304-0)

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete, even with exactly two registered candidates and the target last in the full agenda. It is also \(\mathsf{W[2]}\)-hard parameterized by \(k\). Thus a polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).

1. Exact verification and membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\). Write the support rankings as \(R_1,\ldots,R_\tau\), with masses \(a_i/b_i\), where the positive integers \(a_i,b_i\) are encoded in binary. Define

\(B=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}b_i,\qquad w_i=a_i(B/b_i).\)

Then \(w_i/B=a_i/b_i\) and \(\sum_iw_i=B\). The integers \(B,w_1,\ldots,w_\tau\) have \(O(L)\) bits: the bit length of the product is bounded by the sum of the denominator bit lengths, and \(w_i\leq B\). Schoolbook multiplication and division compute them in \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations.

Fix a proposed addition \(D'\subseteq D\), and put \(S=C\cup D'\). For each retained candidate \(c\), define

\(Q_S(c)=\sum_{\{i:\ R_i\text{ ranks }c\text{ above every agenda successor in }S\}}w_i.\)

The qualification test is exactly \(2Q_S(c)>B\). In particular, equality fails, as required.

These sums can be computed in \(O(\tau mL)\) bit operations. For each ranking, scan the retained agenda from last to first, maintaining the smallest ranking position among the successors already scanned. The current candidate ranks above every successor precisely when its position is smaller than this minimum. Initialize the minimum to \(m+1\), so the last candidate passes vacuously. Each successful test contributes \(w_i\) to the current candidate’s sum. All accumulated sums are at most \(B\).

Consequently, checking whether \(p\) is the earliest qualifying candidate takes

\(O(\tau L^2+\tau mL)\)

bit operations. A certificate specifying \(D'\) has at most \(|D|\) bits as an incidence vector; checking \(|D'|\leq k\) also takes polynomial time, regardless of the numerical size of the binary-encoded budget. This proves membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\).

2. A direct polynomial reduction from Set Cover. An instance of Set Cover consists of a universe \(U\), explicitly listed sets \(S_1,\ldots,S_s\subseteq U\), and a budget \(k\). It asks whether at most \(k\) of the sets cover \(U\). This is a standard \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete problem.

We may restrict to \(n=|U|\geq1\): an empty-universe instance is always positive and can be replaced by a fixed positive instance with nonempty universe.

Create registered candidates \(C=\{q,p\}\) and unregistered candidates \(D=\{d_1,\ldots,d_s\}\). Use the agenda

\(q,\ d_1,\ldots,d_s,\ p.\)

Thus \(p\) is last and is not first. Construct the society from the following weighted rankings:

  • One ranking of mass \(1/2\), ordered \(q\succ p\succ d_1\succ\cdots\succ d_s\).
  • For each \(e\in U\), one ranking of mass \(1/(2n)\), ordered

\(\{d_j:e\in S_j\}\succ q\succ p\succ\{d_j:e\notin S_j\}\).
Within each displayed block, order candidates by increasing index.

Every ranking is strict and complete. The masses are positive and sum to \(1/2+n/(2n)=1\).

If some constructed rankings coincide, merge them by summing their masses. This produces precisely the required representation by distinct support rankings, with \(\tau\leq n+1\). Merging preserves the mass of every event determined by a ranking, including every qualification event after restricting candidates.

Now fix an arbitrary addition \(D'\subseteq D\), and define

\(J=\{j:d_j\in D'\},\qquad u(J)=\left|U\setminus\bigcup_{j\in J}S_j\right|.\)

We check all possible winners.

First, the ranking of mass \(1/2\) places \(q\) above every successor. The ranking associated with \(e\) places \(q\) above every retained successor exactly when no selected set contains \(e\): \(p\) and all noncovering candidates lie below \(q\), while every selected covering candidate lies above it. Therefore \(q\)’s qualifying mass is exactly

\(\frac12+\frac{u(J)}{2n}.\)

Hence \(q\) qualifies if and only if \(u(J)>0\). Since \(q\) is first in the agenda, it wins whenever any element remains uncovered.

Second, every added candidate \(d_j\) has \(p\) as an agenda successor. In the ranking of mass \(1/2\), \(p\) lies above \(d_j\). Consequently, \(d_j\)’s qualifying mass is at most \(1/2\), so no added candidate qualifies.

Finally, \(p\) is last and qualifies vacuously. These observations prove the equivalence

\(p\text{ wins on }C\cup D'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u(J)=0.\)

The budget is unchanged. Thus a successful addition of at most \(k\) candidates exists exactly when the Set Cover instance has a cover using at most \(k\) sets.

The construction is polynomial in the explicitly listed Set Cover input. In particular, \(m=s+2\), and every merged mass can be written with denominator \(2n\) and numerator at most \(2n\). The output bit length is bounded by

\(O\!\left((n+1)m\log m+(n+1)\log(n+1)+\log(k+2)\right).\)

Constructing, sorting, and aggregating the rankings takes polynomial time. This establishes \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness and, together with the verifier, \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness.

3. Why the stated electorate hardness also transfers. Yang’s Theorem 26 establishes \(\mathsf{W[2]}\)-hardness for explicitly listed electorates, with two registered candidates and \(p\) not first. Its successive rule uses the same strict-majority qualification test. [Yang, definitions and Theorem 26](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-025-01304-0)

For an explicitly listed nonempty electorate with \(N\) voters, group identical complete rankings. Give a ranking occurring \(t\) times the mass \(t/N\). There are at most \(N\) support rankings, and each mass has \(O(\log(N+1))\) bits, so this conversion takes polynomial time and produces polynomial output size.

For every retained candidate set and candidate, qualifying mass equals the number of qualifying voters divided by \(N\). Thus qualifying mass is strictly greater than \(1/2\) exactly when the qualifying voters form a strict majority. Every winner is preserved, including cases of equality. The conversion also preserves \(k\), the candidates, and the agenda. It is therefore a parameter-preserving reduction, proving \(\mathsf{W[2]}\)-hardness for the rational-support formulation.

4. Explicit deterministic running times. Put \(d=|D|\), \(K=\min\{k,d\}\), and

\(H(d,K)=\sum_{j=0}^{K}\binom dj.\)

Precompute the integer weights once, enumerate all additions of size at most \(K\), and apply the verifier. The total running time is

\(O\!\left(\tau L^2+H(d,K)\tau mL\right)\)

bit operations. There is no expansion into an electorate whose size depends on the rational denominators.

Since \(H(d,K)\leq2^d\), this also gives the bound

\(O\!\left(\tau L^2+2^m\tau mL\right).\)

Furthermore, \(H(d,K)\leq(d+1)^K\): encode each subset by its increasing list of indices, padded with zeros to length \(K\). Hence another bound is

\(O\!\left(\tau L^2+(m+1)^K\tau mL\right).\)

The problem is therefore polynomial-time solvable for each fixed \(k\), with an exponent depending on \(k\). Its \(\mathsf{W[2]}\)-hardness rules out a running time \(f(k)L^{O(1)}\) unless \(\mathsf{FPT}=\mathsf{W[2]}\).
