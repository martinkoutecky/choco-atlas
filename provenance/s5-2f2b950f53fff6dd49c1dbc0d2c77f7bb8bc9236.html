The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(d\) item kinds, rational per-capita supplies \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^d\) with \(\sum_j q_j\geq 1\), and a normalized, nonnegative, \(\sigma\)-subadditive valuation \(v:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^d\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\) whose one-item marginals have absolute value at most \(\Lambda\), find a finite-support mass allocation \(x_z\geq 0\) over integral bundles \(z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^d\) such that \(\sum_z x_z=1\), \(\sum_z x_z z_j=q_j\) for every kind \(j\), \(x_0=0\), and \(\max_{x_z>0}v(z)-\min_{x_z>0}v(z)\leq 5\Lambda+1\).

Status: partial, with stronger polynomial-time cases

I do not settle the general complexity classification. I prove three improvements:

  • Half-integral supplies admit a polynomial-time algorithm with spread at most $\Lambda$.
  • Arbitrary supplies admit a polynomial-time algorithm with spread at most $5\Lambda$ whenever their total residual above coordinatewise downward half-integral rounding is at most $2$.
  • Separable valuations admit a polynomial-time algorithm for every supply vector, with spread at most $2\Lambda$. Monotone separable valuations admit spread at most $\Lambda$.

All these algorithms have polynomial bit complexity, including when supplies and $\Lambda$ are exponentially large in magnitude. None requires subadditivity.

1. Basic bounds and exact cumulative rounding

Write
$$ |z|_1=\sum_{j=1}^d z_j. $$

The marginal assumption implies
$$ |v(z)-v(w)|\leq \Lambda |z-w|_1 $$
for any nonnegative integral bundles $z,w$. Indeed, decrease coordinates where necessary and then increase the remaining coordinates, obtaining a path of exactly $|z-w|_1$ unit steps within the domain. Each step changes the valuation by at most $\Lambda$.

Normalization and nonnegativity consequently give
$$ 0\leq v(z)\leq \Lambda |z|_1. $$

The following rounding lemma will be used twice.

Lemma 1. Given any rational vector $r\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^d$, one can construct, in polynomial time, a distribution on at most $d+1$ integral vectors with mean $r$. Every supported vector $a$ satisfies
$$ a_j\in\{\lfloor r_j\rfloor,\lceil r_j\rceil\}, \qquad |a|_1\leq \left\lceil\sum_j r_j\right\rceil. $$
If $\sum_jr_j\geq1$, every supported vector is nonempty.

Proof. Choose an ordering of the coordinates and define
$$ S_0=0,\qquad S_j=\sum_{i=1}^j r_i. $$
For $u\in(0,1)$, set
$$ a_j(u)=\lceil S_j-u\rceil-\lceil S_{j-1}-u\rceil. $$
Since $S_j\geq S_{j-1}$, these coordinates are nonnegative integers.

For any real $t$ and nonnegative $r_j$, the difference
$$ \lceil t+r_j\rceil-\lceil t\rceil $$
belongs to $\{\lfloor r_j\rfloor,\lceil r_j\rceil\}$. This proves the coordinate assertion. Telescoping gives
$$ |a(u)|_1=\lceil S_d-u\rceil, $$
because $\lceil-u\rceil=0$. Thus the asserted cardinality bound holds; if $S_d\geq1$, the cardinality is at least one.

Let $U$ be uniform on $(0,1)$. For every $S\geq0$,
$$ \mathbb E[\lceil S-U\rceil]=S. $$
To verify this, write $S=n+b$, where $n$ is integral and $0\leq b<1$. The ceiling equals $n+1$ on an interval of length $b$, and $n$ elsewhere, apart from an irrelevant endpoint. Therefore
$$ \mathbb E[a_j(U)]=S_j-S_{j-1}=r_j. $$

The vector $a(u)$ changes only at fractional parts of the cumulative sums $S_j$. Partition $(0,1)$ at these distinct interior breakpoints. For each resulting interval, evaluate $a$ at its midpoint and assign that vector the interval’s length as its mass. There are at most $d+1$ intervals, and the expectation calculation proves exact feasibility.

Cumulative sums, fractional parts, sorting, ceilings, midpoints, and interval lengths all have polynomial encoding length and can be computed by polynomial-time rational arithmetic. No enumeration of the integer parts is required. $\square$

2. Half-integral supplies: spread at most $\Lambda$

Theorem 2. If $2q$ is integral, the required allocation can be found in polynomial time with at most two supported bundles and spread at most $\Lambda$.

Proof. Put
$$ B=2q,\qquad M=\sum_j B_j. $$
Since $\sum_jq_j\geq1$, we have $M\geq2$.

Order the conceptual items by kind. Let $P(t)$ be the count vector of the first $t$ items, for integers $0\leq t\leq M$. It can be computed directly:
$$ P_j(t)=\min\{B_j,\max\{0,t-C_{j-1}\}\}, \qquad C_{j-1}=\sum_{i<j}B_i. $$
Thus even exponentially many conceptual items require no expansion.

For $1\leq t\leq M-1$, define
$$ F(t)=v(P(t))-v(B-P(t)). $$
Both bundles are nonempty. Increasing $t$ by one transfers one item between them, so
$$ |F(t+1)-F(t)|\leq2\Lambda. $$

If $M=2$, the split at $t=1$ consists of two singletons. Both values belong to $[0,\Lambda]$, so their spread is at most $\Lambda$.

Suppose $M\geq3$. If $F(1)\geq0$, then
$$ 0\leq v(B-P(1))\leq v(P(1))\leq\Lambda, $$
because $P(1)$ is a singleton. This split therefore suffices. Likewise, if $F(M-1)\leq0$, the complementary singleton proves that the split at $M-1$ suffices.

Otherwise,
$$ F(1)<0<F(M-1). $$
Apply integer bisection, preserving endpoints $\ell<h$ with
$$ F(\ell)<0<F(h). $$
A zero midpoint gives an exactly equal split. Otherwise replace the endpoint having the midpoint’s sign. This requires no monotonicity of $F$.

After at most $\lceil\log_2M\rceil$ iterations, the endpoints are adjacent. Write
$$ a=-F(\ell)>0,\qquad b=F(h)>0. $$
The adjacent-step bound gives
$$ a+b=F(h)-F(\ell)\leq2\Lambda. $$
Consequently,
$$ \min\{a,b\}\leq\Lambda. $$
Choose whichever endpoint has the smaller absolute value of $F$. The corresponding two bundles have spread at most $\Lambda$.

Assign mass $1/2$ to each chosen bundle. Their mean is
$$ \frac12P(t)+\frac12(B-P(t))=q. $$
Merge coincident bundles if necessary.

The algorithm uses $O(1+\log M)$ oracle calls. All query coordinates are bounded by the binary-encoded vector $B$, and $\log M$ is polynomially bounded in the input length. The oracle-answer assumption therefore gives polynomial total bit complexity. $\square$

The improvement over the previous argument is the choice of the better of both adjacent endpoints.

3. Extending balanced allocations by small residual supplies

Lemma 3. Suppose a supply vector $s$ has a feasible allocation on $N$ nonempty bundles with spread at most $W$. Let $r\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^d$ and put
$$ k=\left\lceil\sum_jr_j\right\rceil. $$
Then one can construct a feasible allocation for $s+r$ on at most $N(d+1)$ nonempty bundles with spread at most
$$ W+2k\Lambda. $$
The construction takes polynomial time in the encodings of $r$ and the supplied allocation.

Proof. Apply Lemma 1 to $r$, allowing the empty vector in this auxiliary distribution. Write the resulting vectors and masses as $a^t,y_t$. Every $a^t$ has cardinality at most $k$.

Write the original allocation as $z^i,x_i$. Output
$$ z^i+a^t \quad\text{with mass}\quad x_i y_t. $$
The masses sum to one, and the mean is
$$ \sum_{i,t}x_i y_t(z^i+a^t) = \sum_i x_i z^i+\sum_t y_t a^t = s+r. $$
Every output bundle is nonempty because $z^i$ is nonempty.

The lattice Lipschitz bound gives
$$ |v(z^i+a^t)-v(z^i)|\leq k\Lambda. $$
Thus any two supported output values differ by at most
$$ k\Lambda+W+k\Lambda. $$
The number of vectors and all rational mass products have polynomial encoding length. $\square$

Define the coordinatewise downward half-integral rounding and its residual by
$$ s_j=\frac{\lfloor2q_j\rfloor}{2}, \qquad r_j=q_j-s_j, \qquad \rho(q)=\sum_jr_j. $$

Theorem 4. If $\rho(q)\leq2$, the required allocation can be found in polynomial time with at most $2(d+1)$ supported bundles and spread at most $5\Lambda$.

Proof. First suppose $\sum_js_j\geq1$. Theorem 2 supplies an allocation for $s$ with at most two bundles and spread at most $\Lambda$. Since $\rho(q)\leq2$, Lemma 3 applies with $k\leq2$ and gives spread at most
$$ \Lambda+4\Lambda=5\Lambda. $$

If instead $\sum_js_j<1$, then
$$ \sum_jq_j=\sum_js_j+\rho(q)<3. $$
Apply Lemma 1 directly to $q$. Its supported bundles are nonempty and have cardinality at most three. Their values lie in $[0,3\Lambda]$, so their spread is at most $3\Lambda$.

Both constructions have the asserted support and bit-complexity bounds. $\square$

This covers instances outside the previously proved families. For example, for every $d\geq12$, take
$$ q_j=\frac12+\frac{1}{4d} \qquad\text{for all }j. $$
Then every coordinate is nonintegral, the total supply exceeds five, and the supplies are not half-integral. Nevertheless,
$$ \rho(q)=\frac14, $$
so Theorem 4 applies to every admissible valuation.

4. Arbitrary denominators for separable valuations

Here an additional valuation hypothesis is imposed explicitly.

Let
$$ p_j=\lfloor q_j\rfloor, $$
and define the rounding box
$$ \mathcal B(q)= \{z\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^d: z_j\in\{\lfloor q_j\rfloor,\lceil q_j\rceil\}\}. $$

Suppose $v$ is affine on this box: there are coefficients $c_j$ such that
$$ v(z)=v(p)+\sum_j c_j(z_j-p_j) \qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal B(q). $$
For nonintegral coordinates, these coefficients are necessarily
$$ c_j=v(p+e_j)-v(p). $$
For integral coordinates, set $c_j=0$, since that coordinate never varies within the box. Hence
$$ -\Lambda\leq c_j\leq\Lambda. $$

Every separable valuation
$$ v(z)=\sum_j f_j(z_j) $$
satisfies this local affine hypothesis, regardless of whether the functions $f_j$ are monotone.

Theorem 5. Under the local affine hypothesis, every supply vector admits a polynomial-time construction with at most $d+1$ nonempty bundles and spread at most $2\Lambda$. If all the relevant coefficients are nonnegative, the spread is at most $\Lambda$.

Proof. Obtain the coefficients using at most $d+1$ oracle queries. Order the coordinates so that
$$ c_1\geq c_2\geq\cdots\geq c_d. $$
Apply Lemma 1 to $q$ in this ordering. Its allocation is exactly feasible, nonempty, and contained in $\mathcal B(q)$.

It remains to bound its value spread. Write
$$ A_j(u)=\lceil S_j-u\rceil, \qquad S_j=\sum_{i=1}^j q_i, $$
so that $z_j(u)=A_j(u)-A_{j-1}(u)$ and $A_0(u)=0$.

For $0<u<w<1$, put
$$ I_j=A_j(u)-A_j(w). $$
Because $0<w-u<1$, each $I_j$ belongs to $\{0,1\}$. The local affine formula and telescoping yield
$$ \begin{aligned} v(z(u))-v(z(w)) &=\sum_{j=1}^d c_j\bigl(I_j-I_{j-1}\bigr)\\ &=c_d I_d+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}(c_j-c_{j+1})I_j. \end{aligned} $$
All coefficients in the sum on the right are nonnegative, and their sum is $c_1-c_d$.

If $c_d\geq0$, the displayed difference lies in
$$ [0,c_d+(c_1-c_d)]=[0,c_1]\subseteq[0,\Lambda]. $$

If $c_d<0$, it lies in
$$ [c_d,c_1-c_d]. $$
The absolute values of both endpoints are at most $2\Lambda$, since
$$ -c_d\leq\Lambda, \qquad c_1-c_d\leq2\Lambda. $$
Thus every pair of supported values differs by at most $2\Lambda$.

The coefficients have polynomial bit length by the oracle-answer assumption. Sorting them and performing cumulative rounding therefore takes polynomial time. $\square$

This is a promise-case algorithm: it does not attempt to verify separability or the local affine identity.

A sharp example for this construction

The $2\Lambda$ bound can be attained by the construction even for an admissible separable valuation. Take
$$ v(z_1,z_2)=\Lambda\bigl((z_1\bmod2)+(z_2\bmod2)\bigr), \qquad q=\left(\frac12,\frac32\right). $$
The valuation is normalized and nonnegative. Each marginal has absolute value $\Lambda$. It is subadditive because, for nonnegative integers $a,b$,
$$ (a+b)\bmod2\leq(a\bmod2)+(b\bmod2), $$
and the coordinate inequalities add.

Here $p=(0,1)$ and the coefficients are $(\Lambda,-\Lambda)$. Sorted cumulative rounding outputs $(1,1)$ and $(0,2)$, each with mass $1/2$. Their values are $2\Lambda$ and zero.

This is a limitation of that construction, not a lower bound for the search problem: the bundles $(1,0)$ and $(0,3)$, each with mass $1/2$, have the same mean and equal value $\Lambda$.

5. Remaining complexity gap

The proofs above resolve arbitrary denominators for separable valuations and additional arbitrary-valuation instances with many nonintegral coordinates.

They do not resolve general valuations when the residual-rounding condition fails. The cited discrete guarantee also does not directly close this gap: clearing supply denominators produces $D$ agents and $M=D\sum_jq_j$ conceptual items, whereas its running time is polynomial in the numerical quantities $D,M,\Lambda$. Those quantities can be exponentially large in the finite input length. [Discrete guarantee, Theorem 17](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14609v4#Thmtheorem17).

No general polynomial-time algorithm or hardness reduction is established here.
