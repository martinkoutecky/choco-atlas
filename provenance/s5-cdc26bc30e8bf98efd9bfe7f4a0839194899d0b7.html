Theorem 2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) supported user types, where each type \(t\) supplies a finite set \(J_t\) of instance comparisons \((a,b)\) over \(X=\{0,1\}^n\), fixed rational kernel parameters \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n\), and a rational soft-margin parameter \(C\), compute for every type a minimum-norm margin utility by minimizing \(\sum_t \mu_t(\tfrac12\lVert w_t\rVert_2^2+C\sum_{(a,b)\in J_t}\xi_{t,a,b})\), subject to \(w_t\cdot(\Phi_\lambda(a)-\Phi_\lambda(b))\ge 1-\xi_{t,a,b}\) and \(\xi_{t,a,b}\ge0\). Output each utility in kernel form using \(K(x,x')=\sum_{\ell=1}^n\lambda_\ell(\vec{x}\cdot\vec{x}')^\ell\); determine the running time in \(n\), \(\tau\), the total number of comparisons, and the input bit length.

Result

The exact utility-computation problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time. This includes inconsistent comparison lists, empty lists, zero kernel parameters, and singular kernel matrices.

The algorithm solves one rational convex quadratic program for each supported type. Its running time is polynomial in \(n,\tau,M,L\), and hence polynomial in the total binary input length \(L\).

1. The types optimize independently

Write \(m_t=|J_t|\). For fixed \(w_t\), the optimal slack for comparison \((a,b)\) is

\( \xi_{t,a,b}=\max\{0,1-w_t\cdot(\Phi_\lambda(a)-\Phi_\lambda(b))\}, \)

because \(C>0\). Thus the objective for type \(t\), before multiplication by its mass, is

\( F_t(w)=\tfrac12\|w\|_2^2+ C\sum_{(a,b)\in J_t}\max\{0,1-w\cdot(\Phi_\lambda(a)-\Phi_\lambda(b))\}. \)

This function is continuous, coercive, and strictly convex: the squared norm is strictly convex, while every hinge term is convex. Consequently, it has a unique minimizer.

The feasible sets have no constraints between types. Moreover, every \(\mu_t\) is positive. Therefore a tuple minimizes the society’s objective if and only if each component minimizes \(F_t\). Indeed, replacing any nonminimizing component by its minimizer strictly decreases the total objective.

In particular, the masses do not affect the minimizing utilities. If \(J_t\) is empty, its unique minimizing weight is zero, and its utility is identically zero.

2. A comparison-sized rational quadratic program

Fix a type with \(m>0\) comparisons, indexed as \((a_i,b_i)\). Define

\( d_i=\Phi_\lambda(a_i)-\Phi_\lambda(b_i), \qquad Q_{ij}=d_i\cdot d_j. \)

The kernel identity gives

\( Q_{ij}=K(a_i,a_j)-K(a_i,b_j)-K(b_i,a_j)+K(b_i,b_j). \)

Thus \(Q\) is rational and can be constructed without explicitly constructing feature vectors. It is positive semidefinite, because for every real vector \(z\),

\( z^\mathsf TQz=\left\|\sum_i z_i d_i\right\|_2^2\ge0. \)

Consider the quadratic program

\( \text{maximize }\; h(\alpha)=\mathbf1^\mathsf T\alpha-\tfrac12\alpha^\mathsf TQ\alpha \quad\text{subject to }\;0\le\alpha_i\le C. \)

Equivalently, minimize the convex quadratic function \(-h\) over the box \([0,C]^m\). The box is nonempty and compact, so an optimum exists.

The cited paper establishes the kernel identity and the corresponding hard-margin kernel formulation; it also explicitly permits the standard soft-margin extension. The box constraints above account for the slack penalties. [Domshlak and Joachims](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~tj/publications/domshlak_joachims_05a.pdf)

3. Every box optimum yields the minimizing utility

We prove this directly, including singular cases.

For any primal-feasible pair \((w,\xi)\) and any box-feasible \(\alpha\), expansion gives the identity

\( \begin{aligned} &\tfrac12\|w\|_2^2+C\sum_i\xi_i-h(\alpha)\\ &=\tfrac12\left\|w-\sum_i\alpha_i d_i\right\|_2^2 +\sum_i\alpha_i(d_i\cdot w-1+\xi_i) +\sum_i(C-\alpha_i)\xi_i. \end{aligned} \)

Every term on the right is nonnegative. Hence \(h(\alpha)\) is a lower bound on every feasible primal objective.

Now let \(\alpha\) maximize \(h\), and set

\( w=\sum_i\alpha_i d_i, \qquad g_i=1-(Q\alpha)_i=1-d_i\cdot w, \qquad \xi_i=\max\{0,g_i\}. \)

This pair is primal-feasible. Since \(\partial h/\partial\alpha_i=g_i\), optimality along each coordinate implies:

  • If \(\alpha_i=0\), then \(g_i\le0\).
  • If \(0<\alpha_i<C\), then \(g_i=0\).
  • If \(\alpha_i=C\), then \(g_i\ge0\).

For example, at the lower bound a positive derivative would permit an improving positive step; the other cases follow in the same way.

These signs imply

\( \alpha_i(\xi_i-g_i)=0, \qquad (C-\alpha_i)\xi_i=0 \)

for every \(i\). The squared-norm term in the identity also vanishes. Therefore the constructed primal objective equals \(h(\alpha)\). Since this value is a lower bound on every feasible primal objective, the constructed pair is optimal.

Taking its inner product with \(\Phi_\lambda(x)\) gives precisely the required representation:

\( U_t(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m_t}\alpha_{t,i} \bigl(K(a_i,x)-K(b_i,x)\bigr). \)

Although the optimal coefficient vector may be nonunique, the minimizing weight, and consequently the utility, is unique by Section 1.

4. Exact rational computation

We use the following classical theorem.

Exact convex quadratic programming theorem. A quadratic program with rational coefficients, positive semidefinite quadratic objective matrix, and rational linear constraints can be solved exactly in deterministic time polynomial in its binary encoding length. When the minimum is attained, the algorithm returns a rational optimal point. Positive definiteness is not required. [Kozlov, Tarasov, and Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189)

All hypotheses hold here: \(Q\) is rational and positive semidefinite, the box constraints are rational, and an optimum is attained on the compact box. Thus the theorem returns exact rational coefficients.

For completeness, the polynomial size of suitable coefficients can also be seen directly. Choose any real optimum \(\alpha^*\), and partition its coordinates into \(I_0,I_C,I_F\), according as their values are zero, \(C\), or strictly between them. Consider the rational polytope defined by

\( 0\le z\le C,\qquad z_i=0\;(i\in I_0),\qquad z_i=C\;(i\in I_C), \)

together with

\( (Qz)_i\ge1\;(i\in I_0),\qquad (Qz)_i\le1\;(i\in I_C),\qquad (Qz)_i=1\;(i\in I_F). \)

It is nonempty because it contains \(\alpha^*\), and bounded because it lies in the box. Every point in it satisfies the coordinate optimality conditions used in Section 3, so every point is optimal.

A nonempty bounded polyhedron has a vertex. At a vertex, \(m\) linearly independent active constraints determine the coordinates. After clearing denominators, Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s determinant bound give polynomial bit length for these rational coordinates.

This argument establishes existence of a small exact output; the polynomial-time algorithm is supplied by the theorem, without enumerating coordinate partitions.

5. Bit lengths and running time

Define

\( B=L+n\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil+1. \)

All kernel and matrix entries have \(O(B)\)-bit rational representations. To verify this, write \(\lambda_\ell=p_\ell/q_\ell\) and use the common denominator

\( D=\prod_{\ell=1}^n q_\ell. \)

Its bit length is at most \(L\). For two alternatives, the indicator inner product is

\( s=\vec x\cdot\vec x'= |\{i:x_i=x_i'\}|\in\{0,\ldots,n\}. \)

Hence

\( K(x,x')= \frac{\sum_{\ell=1}^n p_\ell(D/q_\ell)s^\ell}{D}. \)

The numerators \(p_\ell(D/q_\ell)\) have \(O(L)\) bits, and \(s^\ell\le n^n\). Summing the terms therefore yields the claimed \(O(B)\) bound. The four-term expression defining each \(Q_{ij}\) preserves this bound.

Computing \(s\) takes \(O(n)\) bit comparisons. Evaluating the numerator by Horner’s rule takes \(O(n)\) arithmetic operations on \(O(B)\)-bit integers. With schoolbook arithmetic, constructing all matrices consequently takes

\( O\!\left(L+\tau+n\left(1+\sum_t m_t^2\right)B^2\right) \)

bit operations, including common-denominator preparation.

Each type’s explicitly encoded quadratic program has length

\( S_t=O((m_t^2+m_t+1)B). \)

Let \(p\) be a fixed monotone polynomial bounding the exact quadratic-programming algorithm’s bit running time. A total bound is therefore

\( O\!\left( L+\tau+n\left(1+\sum_t m_t^2\right)B^2 +\sum_{t:m_t>0}p(S_t) \right). \)

Since \(\sum_t m_t^2\le M^2\), a bound expressed solely in the requested parameters is

\( O\!\left( L+\tau+n(M^2+1)B^2 +\tau\,p\!\left(c(M^2+M+1)B\right) \right) \)

for a fixed constant \(c\). This is polynomial in \(n,\tau,M,L\). Under the stated explicit encoding, \(n,\tau,M\) are themselves bounded by \(O(L)\), so it is also polynomial in \(L\).

The vertex argument above gives coefficients with \(O(m_t(B+\log(m_t+1)))\) bits each, and hence total coefficient-output length

\( O\!\left(\tau+M^2(B+\log(M+1))\right). \)

6. Degenerate inputs and conclusion

No consistency or rank assumption was used. Inconsistent reports remain feasible through slacks. If all kernel parameters vanish, then \(Q=0\), choosing every coefficient equal to \(C\) solves the box problem, and the represented utility is zero. Empty comparison lists also yield zero utility.

Thus the exact distribution-over-types formulation is polynomial-time solvable: positivity of the masses makes it a collection of independent exact rational convex quadratic programs.
