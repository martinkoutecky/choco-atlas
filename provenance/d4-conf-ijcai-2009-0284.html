The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu\) summing to one, a coalition-slot limit \(K\), and explicit rational piecewise-linear nonnegative coalition values \(u(x,\Pi)\) satisfying positive externalities, find a feasible mass partition \(\Pi=\{x^1,\ldots,x^r\}\), with \(r\le K\) and \(\sum_j x^j=\mu\), whose value \(W(\Pi)\) is at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/K\). Can this guarantee be achieved by optimizing over the one- and two-block mass partitions?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by its support \(T\): the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(m=\tau=|T|\) is the mass-vector dimension, not the number of possible types.
Given an integer \(K\ge1\), a feasible partition is an unordered multiset \(\Pi=[x^1,\ldots,x^r]\), where \(1\le r\le K\), each \(x^j\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\) is nonzero, and \(\sum_jx^j=\mu\); identical vectors represent distinct blocks and are counted separately.
Represent partitions using \(K\) ordered slots, padding with zero vectors. The input gives nonnegative continuous piecewise-linear functions \(f_j(x^1,\ldots,x^K)\), explicitly by rational polyhedral cells and rational affine formulas covering the feasible slot domain. Slot permutations permute the values correspondingly; zero slots have value zero, and their placement does not affect other values. Write \(u(x^j,\Pi)=f_j(x^1,\ldots,x^K)\).
Positive externalities mean that merging any two nonzero blocks \(y,z\) into \(y+z\) weakly increases the value of every other block: \(u(x,\Pi')\ge u(x,\Pi)\). No restriction is imposed on the merged block's value.
Define \(W(\Pi)=\sum_{j=1}^r u(x^j,\Pi)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_{\Pi\text{ feasible}}W(\Pi)\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including all value descriptions and \(K\).

Can a feasible rational partition satisfying \(W(\Pi)\ge\mathrm{OPT}/K\) be found in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is attaining this approximation guarantee NP-hard?
Does maximizing \(W\) over feasible partitions with at most \(\min\{2,K\}\) nonzero blocks always achieve that guarantee?

For finite societies of \(n\) agents with nonnegative coalition values and positive externalities, exhaustive search over all one- and two-block partitions guarantees value at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/n\), using \(2^{n-1}\) partitions; this is an approximation result, not a polynomial-time claim.   • The objective was undefined; set it to the sum of block values, matching the discrete definition.
  • Require K to be a positive integer; when K is one, the restricted search includes only the one-block partition.
  • Use nonzero mass vectors for actual coalitions and a multiset of blocks, allowing distinct coalitions with identical type compositions.
  • Make the unspecified piecewise-linear representation explicit: continuous functions described by rational polyhedral cells and affine formulas on a K-slot domain.
  • Use zero slots only as padding, with zero value and no effect on other blocks; require permutation symmetry. This supplies a compact domain while excluding empty coalitions as actual blocks.
  • Permit real mass allocations in the optimization domain and require rational allocations as computational output.

Result

The requested approximation is polynomial-time solvable under the stated explicit representation. Moreover, maximizing welfare over partitions with at most \(\min\{2,K\}\) nonzero blocks always achieves welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/K\).

The proof separates the approximation guarantee from the algorithm for optimizing over two blocks.

1. Existence of an optimal partition

Let the feasible ordered-slot domain be
\[ D=\left\{(x^1,\ldots,x^K)\in(\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T})^K:\ \sum_{j=1}^{K}x^j=\mu\right\}. \]

This set is nonempty, closed, and bounded: every coordinate satisfies \(0\le x_t^j\le\mu_t\). Consequently, \(D\) is compact.

The function \(F(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{K}f_j(X)\) is continuous on \(D\), so it attains a maximum. Removing zero slots produces a feasible partition, because their values are zero and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\) ensures that some slot is nonzero. Conversely, every feasible partition has an ordered-slot representation. Permutation equivariance makes its welfare independent of the ordering.

Thus the maximum of \(F\) on \(D\) is precisely \(\mathrm{OPT}\).

2. The two-block approximation guarantee

Choose an optimal partition \(\Pi^\star=[x^1,\ldots,x^r]\), where \(1\le r\le K\). There is an occurrence \(i\) such that
\[ u(x^i,\Pi^\star)\ge\frac{1}{r}\sum_{j=1}^{r}u(x^j,\Pi^\star)=\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{r}. \]

If \(r\le2\), the optimal partition itself already has at most \(\min\{2,K\}\) blocks.

Suppose \(r\ge3\). Keep the selected occurrence \(x^i\) separate and successively merge all other blocks. Each merge involves two nonzero blocks, and \(x^i\) is an unaffected block. Positive externalities therefore imply that its value never decreases.

The resulting partition is
\[ \widehat\Pi=\left[x^i,\ \sum_{j\ne i}x^j\right]. \]
Both blocks are nonzero. By nonnegativity of the second block’s value,
\[ W(\widehat\Pi)\ge u(x^i,\widehat\Pi) \ge u(x^i,\Pi^\star) \ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{r} \ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{K}. \]

Identical vectors cause no difficulty: the argument selects and preserves one block occurrence, as required by the multiset definition.

Hence the maximum welfare over partitions with at most \(\min\{2,K\}\) nonzero blocks satisfies the requested guarantee.

3. Exact optimization over at most two blocks

For \(K=1\), output the unique partition \([\mu]\). It is optimal.

Assume \(K\ge2\). Define
\[ B=\{x\in\mathbb R^{T}:0\le x_t\le\mu_t\text{ for every }t\in T\}, \qquad S(x)=(x,\mu-x,0,\ldots,0). \]

Every partition with at most two nonzero blocks has a representation \(S(x)\) for some \(x\in B\). The choices \(x=0\) and \(x=\mu\) represent the one-block partition. Permutation equivariance justifies placing the active blocks in the first two slots.

Since the remaining slots have value zero, the welfare represented by \(S(x)\) is
\[ G(x)=f_1(S(x))+f_2(S(x)). \]

Write the explicit descriptions of these two functions as follows:

  • \(f_1=\ell_{1,a}\) on the rational polyhedral cell \(C_{1,a}\), for \(1\le a\le N_1\).
  • \(f_2=\ell_{2,b}\) on the rational polyhedral cell \(C_{2,b}\), for \(1\le b\le N_2\).

Here each \(\ell_{j,a}\) is a rational affine function. For every pair \((a,b)\), form the rational linear program
\[ \text{maximize }\ell_{1,a}(S(x))+\ell_{2,b}(S(x)) \quad\text{subject to }x\in B,\ S(x)\in C_{1,a},\ S(x)\in C_{2,b}. \]

These are linear constraints and an affine objective because \(S\) is an affine map. Every feasible region is bounded by \(B\).

The pairs cover \(B\): for every \(x\in B\), the point \(S(x)\) belongs to a cell for \(f_1\) and a cell for \(f_2\). On each pair’s feasible region, its objective equals \(G(x)\). Therefore, the largest optimum among the feasible linear programs equals \(\max_{x\in B}G(x)\).

For boundary conventions, rational polyhedral cells can be taken closed. If the input instead uses relatively open cells, first intersect each cell with \(D\), discard empty intersections, and take closures. Continuity extends its affine formula to that closure. These operations are polynomial-time: emptiness with strict inequalities is checked by maximizing a common positive slack; for a nonempty intersection, replacing strict inequalities by weak ones gives its closure. Thus this convention does not change the argument.

4. Running time and rational output

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: feasibility and bounded optimization for explicitly given rational linear programs can be solved exactly in time polynomial in their binary encoding length, returning a rational optimizer of polynomial encoding length. This applies also to feasible regions of lower dimension. See the treatment of feasibility and optimization in [MIT’s linear-programming notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-251j-introduction-to-mathematical-programming-fall-2009/e13f5e6650f7be1e21101a2e7bd0d5b8_MIT6_251JF09_lec18.pdf).

There are \(N_1N_2\) linear programs. Because the cell lists are explicit parts of the input, this number is polynomial in \(L\). Each program has \(m=\tau\) variables, and its encoding length is polynomial in \(m,K,L\): substituting \(S(x)\) requires only rational arithmetic on the supplied coefficients and \(\mu\).

Solve these programs exactly, discard infeasible ones, and choose a rational optimizer \(x^\star\) with the largest objective value. Output the multiset \([x^\star,\mu-x^\star]\), deleting zero vectors but retaining both occurrences if the vectors are identical.

This is a feasible rational partition maximizing welfare over at most two blocks. Section 2 proves that its welfare is at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/K\). The entire algorithm runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\).
