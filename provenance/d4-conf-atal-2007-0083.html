The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite agenda \(A\), underlying logic \(L\), roles \(R\) with fixed rational shares \(q_r\), a finite-support rational society \(\mu_0\) over types \((r,J)\) with \(J\in J(A,L)\), an agenda item \(p\), a formula \(\phi\), and a polynomial-time representation of a discrete rule \(f\), decide whether \(\mathcal F_f,\mu_0,p\models\phi\). Define \(D_r(\mu)\) as the tie-broken judgment set of greatest mass within role \(r\), set \(\mathcal F_f(\mu)=f(D_1(\mu),\ldots,D_n(\mu))\), interpret each role atom using \(D_r(\mu)\), and let \(\Box\) range over all rational societies with those role shares.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Take the underlying logic \(\mathsf L\) to be classical propositional logic. Let \(A\) be a nonempty finite agenda consisting of formula–negation pairs, with \(m=|A|\), and let \(J(A,\mathsf L)\) denote its complete, consistent judgment sets; assume this set is nonempty.
Let \(R=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), where \(n\geq2\), with positive binary rational shares \(q_r\) satisfying \(\sum_r q_r=1\). A society \(\mu\) is a rational probability distribution on \(R\times J(A,\mathsf L)\) satisfying \(\sum_J\mu(r,J)=q_r\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Order the agenda as part of the input. Define \(D_r(\mu)\) as the judgment set maximizing \(\mu(r,J)\), breaking ties by the lexicographically least membership vector in that order; absent types have mass zero.
The input rule \(f:J(A,\mathsf L)^n\to J(A,\mathsf L)\) is represented by a Boolean circuit on judgment-set membership vectors, promised to return an admissible judgment set on every admissible profile. Set \(\mathcal F_f(\mu)=f(D_1(\mu),\ldots,D_n(\mu))\).
Formulas have grammar \(\phi::=r\mid\sigma\mid h_a\mid\neg\phi\mid(\phi\land\phi)\mid\Box\phi\mid\blacksquare\phi\), with \(r\in R\) and \(a\in A\). At \((\mu,p)\), atoms \(r,\sigma,h_a\) mean respectively \(p\in D_r(\mu)\), \(p\in\mathcal F_f(\mu)\), and \(p=a\); Boolean connectives have their usual semantics.
The operator \(\Box\) quantifies over every rational society with the prescribed role shares, keeping the agenda item fixed; \(\blacksquare\) quantifies over every agenda item, keeping the society fixed. Neither operator changes the rule or shares.
Given these data, an initial society \(\mu_0\), \(p\in A\), and \(\phi\), is deciding \(\mathcal F_f,\mu_0,p\models\phi\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,n,|\phi|\), and the total input bit length \(L\).
Discrete judgment aggregation model checking with succinct polynomial-time rule evaluation is \(\Delta_2^p\)-hard, and is already \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for \(\Diamond\psi\) with no further profile modalities, where \(\Diamond=\neg\Box\neg\). Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with \(\,n=\tau=2\), equal shares, an agenda of independent propositional atoms and their negations, and the fixed formula \(\Diamond\sigma\).

Moreover, using the discrete \(\Delta_2^p\)-hardness result stipulated in the question, the full problem is \(\Delta_2^p=\mathrm P^{\mathrm{NP}}\)-complete. The rational societies introduce no additional semantic complexity: every admissible discrete profile is realizable, and the language observes a society only through that profile.

All complexity statements below concern the promised valid inputs.

1. Exact reduction to the discrete semantics

Write \(\mathcal J=J(A,\mathsf L)\), and define the effective profile of a society by

\[ \pi(\mu)=(D_1(\mu),\ldots,D_n(\mu)). \]

Because \(q_r>0\), some type belonging to role \(r\) has positive mass. Consequently, the maximum defining \(D_r(\mu)\) is positive, so absent types cannot attain it. Thus \(D_r(\mu)\) can be computed entirely from the supplied support, including the prescribed lexicographic tie-breaking.

Lemma 1. For every profile \(\gamma=(J_1,\ldots,J_n)\in\mathcal J^n\), there is a rational society \(\mu_\gamma\) with support size exactly \(n\) such that \(\pi(\mu_\gamma)=\gamma\).

Proof. Define

\[ \mu_\gamma(r,J)= \begin{cases} q_r,&J=J_r,\\ 0,&J\ne J_r. \end{cases} \]

The masses are rational, positive on exactly \(n\) distinct role–judgment types, and sum to \(\sum_r q_r=1\). Each role has the required marginal \(q_r\). For role \(r\), \(J_r\) is the unique positive-mass judgment set, hence the unique maximizer. Therefore \(D_r(\mu_\gamma)=J_r\). \(\square\)

Let \(f,\gamma,p\models_{\mathrm{disc}}\theta\) denote the corresponding discrete semantics, in which \(\Box\) quantifies over \(\mathcal J^n\) and \(\blacksquare\) quantifies over \(A\).

Lemma 2. For every society \(\mu\), item \(p\), and formula \(\theta\),

\[ \mathcal F_f,\mu,p\models\theta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f,\pi(\mu),p\models_{\mathrm{disc}}\theta. \]

Proof. We use structural induction on \(\theta\).

For role atoms, the equivalence is the definition of \(\pi\). For \(\sigma\), it follows from \(\mathcal F_f(\mu)=f(\pi(\mu))\). For \(h_a\), both semantics test \(p=a\). Negation and conjunction preserve the induction hypothesis.

For \(\blacksquare\psi\), both semantics quantify over precisely the same items \(a\in A\), while retaining respectively \(\mu\) and \(\pi(\mu)\). Apply the induction hypothesis at every such item.

For \(\Box\psi\), the induction hypothesis gives

\[ \mathcal F_f,\mu,p\models\Box\psi \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{for every rational society }\nu,\ f,\pi(\nu),p\models_{\mathrm{disc}}\psi. \]

Every \(\pi(\nu)\) is admissible, and Lemma 1 shows that every admissible profile occurs as some \(\pi(\nu)\). The last condition is therefore exactly the discrete interpretation of \(\Box\psi\). \(\square\)

In particular, rational mass precision, support multiplicity, and tie-breaking do not change the set of profiles quantified over.

2. Direct \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness with two roles

We give a reduction from propositional satisfiability that is independent of the cited discrete theorem.

Let \(G(x_1,\ldots,x_t)\) be a SAT instance. A dummy variable may be added to ensure \(t\geq1\). Introduce a fresh atom \(y\), and take

\[ A=\{x_i,\neg x_i:1\leq i\leq t\}\cup\{y,\neg y\}. \]

Thus \(m=2(t+1)\). Since all agenda issues are independent atoms, admissible judgment sets correspond exactly to truth assignments to \(x_1,\ldots,x_t,y\).

Set \(n=2\) and \(q_1=q_2=1/2\). Define \(f\) by the following output choices:

  • On every issue \(x_i\), copy role \(1\)'s choice.
  • Accept \(y\) exactly when \(G\) evaluates to true on role \(1\)'s choices for \(x_1,\ldots,x_t\).
  • On each negated item, output the complement of its positive item's output.

This is a Boolean circuit of size \(O(|G|+t)\). Its output specifies a truth assignment to the independent agenda atoms, so it is admissible on every admissible input profile. The rule promise is therefore satisfied.

Let \(J^-\) contain all the negative agenda items. Supply the initial society

\[ \mu_0(1,J^-)=\mu_0(2,J^-)=1/2, \]

which has \(\tau=2\). Choose \(p=y\) and \(\phi=\Diamond\sigma=\neg\Box\neg\sigma\).

We claim that

\[ G\text{ is satisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathcal F_f,\mu_0,y\models\Diamond\sigma. \]

If \(G\) is satisfiable, choose an admissible \(J_1\) whose \(x_i\)-choices give a satisfying assignment, and choose any admissible \(J_2\). Lemma 1 realizes this profile as a society. By construction, its aggregate accepts \(y\), witnessing \(\Diamond\sigma\).

Conversely, a society witnessing \(\Diamond\sigma\) has \(y\in f(D_1(\mu),D_2(\mu))\). The definition of \(f\) then says that role \(1\)'s \(x_i\)-choices satisfy \(G\).

The construction has polynomial input length. This proves the stated restricted \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm for the full problem would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

3. A polynomial-time algorithm with an \(\mathrm{NP}\) oracle

Put \(s=|\phi|\), and let \(b\leq s\) be the number of occurrences of \(\Box\).

For each occurrence \(u=\Box\psi\), its truth value depends on the agenda item but not on the current society. We compute its table

\[ T_u(a)= \begin{cases} 1,&f,\gamma,a\models_{\mathrm{disc}}\Box\psi,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad a\in A, \]

processing occurrences from the inside out.

Suppose all strict descendant \(\Box\)-occurrences have already been tabulated. For each \(a\), ask whether there exists an admissible profile \(\gamma\) at which \(\psi\) is false, interpreting descendant occurrences using their tables. Negating this existential answer gives \(T_u(a)\).

This existential question belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). To verify this explicitly, let \(v\) be the number of distinct propositional variables occurring in the agenda. Guess one assignment to these variables for each role, using \(nv\) bits. Evaluate every agenda formula under each assignment to obtain the profile.

These guesses cover exactly all admissible profiles:

  • A truth assignment yields a complete, consistent judgment set.
  • Conversely, a complete, consistent judgment set has a satisfying assignment. Completeness forces that assignment's agenda membership vector to equal the supplied judgment set.

Evaluate \(f\) once on the guessed profile. After replacing descendant \(\Box\)-occurrences by their tables, the remaining formula has only Boolean connectives and \(\blacksquare\). It can be evaluated by storing, for each subformula, its truth values at all \(m\) agenda items. A \(\blacksquare\)-node takes the conjunction of its child's \(m\) entries. Thus arbitrary nesting of \(\blacksquare\) does not require repeated branching.

If \(C\) is the circuit size, verification takes polynomial time, with a basic-operation bound

\[ O(nL+nm+C+ms). \]

There are at most \(mb\leq ms\) oracle queries. Once their answers are tabulated, evaluate \(\phi\) at \(\pi(\mu_0),p\) by the same finite-item computation. Hence the full problem belongs to \(\mathrm P^{\mathrm{NP}}\).

The same certificate argument proves that the fragment \(\Diamond\psi\), where \(\psi\) has no profile modalities, belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). Together with Section 2, that fragment is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

4. Running times and the role of the support size

The following bounds distinguish the input support size \(\tau\) from the number of possible judgment sets.

Extracting the initial profile

Scan the \(\tau\) support entries, retaining each role's greatest mass and lexicographically least judgment vector among ties. Comparing binary rational masses uses cross-multiplication; schoolbook arithmetic gives the conservative bound

\[ O\bigl(\tau(m+L^2)+nm\bigr). \]

There is no enumeration of absent types.

If \(\phi\) contains no \(\Box\), the complete computation therefore takes

\[ O\bigl(\tau(m+L^2)+nm+C+ms\bigr) \]

basic operations, apart from polynomial indexing overhead. In particular, this fragment is polynomial-time solvable.

For unrestricted formulas, the oracle algorithm uses the same preprocessing, polynomial additional work in \(L,m,n,s\), and at most \(ms\) \(\mathrm{NP}\)-oracle calls.

An explicit deterministic algorithm

Let \(k=|\mathcal J|\). With \(m/2\) formula–negation pairs,

\[ 1\leq k\leq 2^{m/2}. \]

Enumerate all \(2^v\) truth assignments to agenda variables, evaluate their membership vectors, and deduplicate them. As proved above, this produces exactly \(\mathcal J\).

Next enumerate its \(k^n\) profiles and evaluate the rule once per profile. Compute a truth table for every subformula on all \(k^n m\) profile–item states. At a \(\Box\)-node, conjoin over profiles; at a \(\blacksquare\)-node, conjoin over items.

A basic-operation bound is

\[ O\bigl( \tau(m+L^2)+nm+2^vL+ k^n(C+nm+ms) \bigr). \]

Allowing polynomial overhead for bit-level indexing, a conservative deterministic bit-time bound is

\[ \bigl(\tau+2^v+2^{mn/2}\bigr)\operatorname{poly}(L), \qquad v\leq L. \]

The exponential profile count concerns possible societies, not the \(\tau\) types listed in the initial society. Indeed, the hardness construction has initial support size only two.

5. Transfer of the stipulated stronger hardness

The discrete \(\Delta_2^p\)-hardness theorem supplied in the question transfers through Lemma 2. Given a discrete initial profile, use equal shares \(q_r=1/n\) and place mass \(1/n\) on its judgment set for each role. This produces a support-\(n\) society of polynomial encoding length, preserving every formula's truth.

The cited construction uses dictatorship, \(f(J_1,\ldots,J_n)=J_1\), which has an immediate Boolean-circuit representation and satisfies the admissibility promise. Thus its rule representation fits the present input model. [Ågotnes, van der Hoek and Wooldridge, Theorem 2](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/michael.wooldridge/pubs/jaamas2011a.pdf).

Taking that stipulated discrete theorem as a premise, the transferred lower bound and Section 3 give \(\Delta_2^p\)-completeness. The requested \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness conclusion also has the independent complete proof in Section 2.
