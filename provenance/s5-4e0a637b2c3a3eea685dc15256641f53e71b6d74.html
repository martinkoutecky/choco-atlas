The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) types, where each type \(t\) has a strict ranking \(\succ_t\) of \(m\) house categories and a per-capita endowment vector \(e_t\) with \(\sum_j e_{tj}=1\), compute a common per-capita allocation \(p_t\) for each type. Require \(\sum_j p_{tj}\le 1\), aggregate use \(\sum_t\mu_t p_{tj}\le w_j=\sum_t\mu_t e_{tj}\), rank-prefix individual rationality for every type, and no justified envy between any pair of types. The allocation must be ordinally efficient: no feasible reassignment weakly improves every type by first-order stochastic dominance and strictly improves a positive-mass type.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\ge1\) house categories. Each type consists of a strict ranking \(\succ_t\) of all categories and a binary rational endowment \(e_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^m\) with \(\sum_j e_{tj}=1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Write \(x\succeq_t^{\mathrm{SD}}y\) for first-order stochastic dominance under \(\succ_t\), and \(x\succ_t^{\mathrm{SD}}y\) for dominance with at least one strict rank-prefix inequality.
A feasible allocation gives every member of type \(t\) the same nonnegative per-capita vector \(p_t\), with \(\sum_jp_{tj}\le1\) and \(\sum_t\mu_tp_{tj}\le w_j:=\sum_t\mu_te_{tj}\) for every category \(j\).
Individual rationality requires \(p_t\succeq_t^{\mathrm{SD}}e_t\) for every type. No justified envy requires, for every ordered pair \(t,s\), that \(p_t\succeq_t^{\mathrm{SD}}p_s\) or \(p_t\not\succeq_s^{\mathrm{SD}}e_s\).
Ordinal efficiency means that no feasible allocation \(q\) satisfies \(q_t\succeq_t^{\mathrm{SD}}p_t\) for every type and \(q_t\succ_t^{\mathrm{SD}}p_t\) for some positive-mass type; competing allocations need not satisfy the fairness conditions.

Can an individually rational, ordinally efficient allocation with no justified envy always be computed exactly as rational vectors in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite lists of agents, the controlled-consuming mechanism is ordinally efficient and computes individually rational allocations with no justified envy in polynomial time. [Athanassoglou and Sethuraman](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/24351/1/MPRA_paper_24351.pdf)
No result settling this exact support-encoded mass problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one and required nonempty support and at least one category.
  • Specified nonnegative binary rational endowments and exact rational output vectors.
  • Used the original justified-envy definition: failure of dominance of the other allocation, together with the envious type's allocation being individually rational for the other type.
  • Interpreted ordinal efficiency against all feasible common per-type reallocations, without requiring competing reallocations to satisfy individual rationality or no justified envy.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length and a type as its ranking together with its per-capita endowment.

Result

An allocation satisfying all three requirements can always be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

We give a direct construction and proof. The construction uses the flow idea underlying [controlled consuming](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/24351/1/MPRA_paper_24351.pdf), with capacities weighted by type masses. The argument below establishes correctness and bit complexity directly for the support representation.

1. A flow representation of stochastic dominance

Write \(h_t(k)\) for type \(t\)’s \(k\)-th ranked category, and define
\(H_t(k)=\{h_t(1),\ldots,h_t(k)\}\). Also set \(H_t(0)=\varnothing\) and
\(E_t(k)=\sum_{\ell=1}^k e_{t,h_t(\ell)}\).

We will maintain nonnegative numbers \(u_{tk}\), with
\(\sum_{k=1}^m u_{tk}=1\). Define their prefixes by
\(U_t(k)=\sum_{\ell=1}^k u_{t\ell}\), including \(U_t(0)=0\).

For any such \(u\), construct a network with source \(o\), sink \(d\), rank nodes \(v_{tk}\), and category nodes \(j\). Its arcs and capacities are:

  • \(o\longrightarrow v_{tk}\), of capacity \(\mu_tu_{tk}\);
  • \(v_{tk}\longrightarrow j\), for every \(j\in H_t(k)\), of capacity \(2\);
  • \(j\longrightarrow d\), of capacity \(w_j\).

Both the total source capacity and the total category capacity equal \(1\). Thus a flow of value \(1\) saturates every source arc and every category-to-sink arc.

If \(f\) is such a flow, define
\(z_{tj}=\mu_t^{-1}\sum_k f(v_{tk},j)\).
Then \(z\) is feasible, every row sums to \(1\), and
\(z_t(H_t(k))\ge U_t(k)\): all flow originating at the first \(k\) rank nodes must go to \(H_t(k)\).

We also need the converse.

Flow representation lemma. Suppose a feasible allocation \(z\) has row sums \(1\) and satisfies \(z_t(H_t(k))\ge U_t(k)\) for every \(t,k\). Then \(z\) is represented by a flow of value \(1\) in this network.

Proof. For each type separately, give rank node \(k\) supply \(u_{tk}\), give category \(j\) receiving capacity \(z_{tj}\), and allow rank node \(k\) to send only to \(H_t(k)\).

Consider a cut that does not cross a rank-to-category arc. If its source side contains a nonempty set \(I\) of rank nodes and \(r=\max I\), its category set \(Y\) contains \(H_t(r)\). Consequently,

\( \sum_{k\in I}u_{tk}\le U_t(r)\le z_t(H_t(r))\le z_t(Y). \)

Its cut capacity, \(1-\sum_{k\in I}u_{tk}+z_t(Y)\), is therefore at least \(1\). Cuts with no rank nodes also have capacity at least \(1\); cuts crossing a capacity-\(2\) arc have capacity at least \(2\). The max-flow/min-cut theorem gives a flow of value \(1\).

Multiply this flow by \(\mu_t\) and combine the flows of all types. Feasibility of \(z\) makes the combined category flows admissible. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

2. The algorithm

Maintain a time \(a\in[0,1]\), the numbers \(u_{tk}\), and a best-rank index \(b_t\).

Initially,

\( a=0,\qquad u_{tk}=e_{t,h_t(k)},\qquad b_t=1. \)

The initial network admits a flow of value \(1\): send each endowment directly to its category.

At the beginning of a stage, define

\( n_t=\min\{k>b_t:u_{tk}>0\}, \)

using \(n_t=m+1\) if this set is empty. We will prove the invariant \(U_t(b_t)\ge a\). Call type \(t\) active when \(U_t(b_t)=a\), and claiming when \(U_t(b_t)>a\).

If \(a<1\), an active type necessarily has \(n_t\le m\), since its positive mass after rank \(b_t\) totals \(1-a>0\).

On the current stage, active types transfer capacity from their next rank to their best rank at unit per-capita speed. Define

\( v_{tk}=\begin{cases} 1,&t\text{ is active and }k=b_t,\\ -1,&t\text{ is active and }k=n_t,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \)

For a candidate time \(\lambda\), use
\(u_{tk}(\lambda)=u_{tk}+(\lambda-a)v_{tk}\).
Claiming types’ capacities remain fixed.

Let \(\beta\) be the earliest of:

  • time \(1\);
  • \(U_t(b_t)\) for any claiming type, when it becomes active;
  • \(a+u_{t,n_t}\) for any active type, when its donor capacity becomes zero.

Every listed nonterminal time is strictly greater than \(a\). On \([a,\beta]\), all capacities remain nonnegative and every type’s capacities still sum to \(1\).

Solve the rational linear program that maximizes \(\lambda\in[a,\beta]\), subject to the corresponding network admitting a flow of value \(1\). Denote its optimum by \(\theta\). This LP is feasible at \(a\).

Update \(u_{tk}\leftarrow u_{tk}(\theta)\) and \(a\leftarrow\theta\).

If \(\theta=\beta\), recompute the stage data, or terminate if \(\theta=1\).

If \(\theta<\beta\), choose the minimum cut immediately to the right of \(\theta\), breaking ties by taking the largest source side. Section 6 explains how to compute this using an exact rational perturbation. Write its source side as

\( \{o\}\cup X\cup Y, \)

where \(X\) contains rank nodes and \(Y\) contains category nodes.

For each type, \(X\) contains a prefix of its rank nodes. Write its length as \(r_t\). For every active type satisfying

\( b_t\le r_t<n_t, \)

increment \(b_t\) by \(1\). Then start the next stage.

At termination, output

\( p_{t,h_t(k)}=u_{tk}. \)

We now prove that these steps are well-defined and that the output has the required properties.

3. Invariants and permanent tight cuts

Prefix invariants

Every capacity transfer moves mass from rank \(n_t\) to the better rank \(b_t\). Hence every \(U_t(k)\) is nondecreasing throughout the algorithm. In particular,

\( U_t(k)\ge E_t(k). \)

A second invariant is crucial:

\[ U_t(k)>a\quad\Longrightarrow\quad U_t(k)=E_t(k). \tag{1} \]

Initially this holds because \(U_t(k)=E_t(k)\).

During a stage, an active type has no positive capacity strictly between \(b_t\) and \(n_t\). Thus, for \(b_t\le k<n_t\),

\( U_t(k;\lambda)=U_t(b_t;a)+(\lambda-a)=\lambda. \)

All other prefixes remain constant. A constant prefix exceeding the new time \(\lambda\) also exceeded the previous time \(a\), so it equals its endowment prefix by induction. Claiming types’ prefixes are all constant. This proves (1).

The invariant \(U_t(b_t)\ge a\) is also preserved: an active best prefix equals the current time, while a claiming best prefix remains above it until its activation time. When an active best rank is advanced at time \(\theta\), the new best prefix is at least the old one, which equals \(\theta\).

Structure of the obstruction cut

At \(\theta<\beta\), feasibility fails immediately to the right of \(\theta\). We choose a cut that is tight at \(\theta\) and whose capacity has negative right derivative.

Its capacity is less than \(1\) just to the right, so it cannot cross a capacity-\(2\) arc. Therefore every rank node in \(X\) has all its neighboring categories in \(Y\).

The largest-source-side rule implies prefix closure. Indeed, if \(v_{tk}\in X\) and \(\ell<k\), adding \(v_{t\ell}\) introduces no outgoing rank-to-category arc and removes a nonnegative source capacity from the cut. The resulting cut is also minimum, so maximality requires \(v_{t\ell}\in X\).

Consequently \(X\) consists of prefixes of lengths \(r_t\), and \(H_t(r_t)\subseteq Y\). Its capacity is

\( C(\lambda)=1-\sum_t\mu_tU_t(r_t;\lambda)+w(Y), \)

where \(w(Y)=\sum_{j\in Y}w_j\). Tightness at \(\theta\) gives

\[ \sum_t\mu_tU_t(r_t;\theta)=w(Y). \tag{2} \]

Its right derivative is

\( C'=-\sum_{\substack{t\text{ active}\\b_t\le r_t<n_t}}\mu_t. \)

Since this derivative is negative, at least one best index is advanced. Thus every obstruction stage makes discrete progress.

A tight cut remains tight

Fix a cut recorded at time \(\theta\). At every later state, feasibility of a flow of value \(1\) implies

\( \sum_t\mu_tU_t(r_t)\le w(Y), \)

because all flow from these rank prefixes must enter \(Y\). Each prefix on the left is nondecreasing, and equality held at \(\theta\). Since every \(\mu_t>0\), each individual prefix remains constant:

\[ U_t(r_t;\text{later})=U_t(r_t;\theta). \tag{3} \]

Moreover, in any later flow of value \(1\), these source prefixes together fill all capacity in \(Y\). No rank node outside \(X\) can send positive flow into \(Y\). Thus the allocation \(z\) represented by any such flow satisfies

\[ z_t(Y)=U_t(r_t;\theta)\qquad\text{for every }t. \tag{4} \]

Advancing a best rank freezes its allocation prefix

Suppose type \(t\)’s rank \(k=b_t\) is advanced at time \(\theta\). It is active and \(k\le r_t<n_t\), so

\( U_t(k;\theta)=U_t(r_t;\theta)=\theta. \)

After the advance, all subsequent transfers for this type involve ranks greater than \(k\). Hence \(U_t(k)\) stays equal to \(\theta\).

For any subsequent represented allocation \(z\),

\( z_t(H_t(k))\ge U_t(k)=\theta. \)

But \(H_t(k)\subseteq Y\), and (4) gives \(z_t(Y)=\theta\). Therefore

\[ z_t(H_t(k))=\theta. \tag{5} \]

4. Feasibility, individual rationality, and ordinal efficiency

First suppose the algorithm has reached time \(1\), and consider any flow of value \(1\) in its final network.

For every \(k<b_t\), rank \(k\) was advanced earlier, so (5) gives
\(z_t(H_t(k))=U_t(k)\).

For \(k\ge b_t\), the invariant \(U_t(b_t)\ge1\), together with the unit row sum, gives \(U_t(k)=1\). The flow representation then gives \(z_t(H_t(k))=1\).

Thus every rank prefix of \(z_t\) equals the corresponding prefix of the final \(u_t\). Taking differences of consecutive prefixes yields

\[ z_{t,h_t(k)}=u_{tk}. \tag{6} \]

The maintained existence of a flow of value \(1\) therefore proves that the output \(p\) is feasible. Prefix monotonicity gives
\(p_t(H_t(k))=U_t(k)\ge E_t(k)\), proving individual rationality.

Now suppose a feasible allocation \(q\) stochastically dominates \(p\) for every type. The full-prefix inequality and the per-capita upper bound imply that every \(q_t\) sums to \(1\). Since \(p\) equals the final \(u\), the flow representation lemma represents \(q\) by a flow of value \(1\) in the final network. Equation (6) forces \(q=p\).

Therefore no feasible allocation can weakly improve all types and strictly improve any type. This proves ordinal efficiency against the full feasible set.

5. No justified envy

Fix an ordered pair \(t,s\). Suppose

\( p_t\not\succeq_t^{\mathrm{SD}}p_s. \)

Then some prefix \(A=H_t(k)\) satisfies

\[ p_s(A)>p_t(A). \tag{7} \]

This rank \(k\) must have been advanced: every prefix at or beyond the final \(b_t\) has \(p_t\)-mass \(1\).

Let \(\theta\) be its advancement time, and take the associated tight cut with category set \(Y\) and rank-prefix lengths \(r_v\). Equations (4) and (5) give

\( A\subseteq Y,\qquad p_t(A)=p_t(Y)=\theta. \)

Consequently, by (7),

\( U_s(r_s;\theta)=p_s(Y)\ge p_s(A)>\theta. \)

Invariant (1), applied at time \(\theta\), yields

\[ E_s(r_s)=U_s(r_s;\theta)>\theta. \tag{8} \]

If \(p_t\succeq_s^{\mathrm{SD}}e_s\), then

\( p_t(H_s(r_s))\ge E_s(r_s)>\theta. \)

Yet \(H_s(r_s)\subseteq Y\) and \(p_t(Y)=\theta\), a contradiction. Therefore

\( p_t\not\succeq_s^{\mathrm{SD}}e_s. \)

We have proved exactly the required disjunction for every ordered pair.

6. Polynomial running time and exact arithmetic

Number of stages

Each obstruction stage advances at least one \(b_t\). Each best index advances at most \(m-1\) times.

Each donor-exhaustion event increases some \(n_t\). Donor indices never decrease: with a fixed best index, an exhausted donor is replaced by a later positive rank; when the best index advances, the next positive rank cannot move earlier. Using \(m+1\) for an absent donor, there are at most \(m\) such increases per type.

Finally, with a fixed best index, a claiming type becomes active at most once. After activation, its best prefix equals the current time throughout subsequent stages until its best index changes.

Every nonterminal stage ends with an obstruction, donor exhaustion, or activation. Hence there are \(O(\tau m)\) stages, including stages with no time increase. An active type always has a donor before time \(1\), so an advancement cannot move its best index beyond \(m\). The algorithm therefore terminates at time \(1\).

Size of each optimization problem

The network has \(\tau m+m+2\) nodes and \(O(\tau m^2)\) arcs.

On an affine stage, the conditions for a flow of value \(1\), together with \(a\le\lambda\le\beta\), form a rational LP of polynomial size. It is nonempty and bounded. Exact rational linear programming computes its optimum in polynomial time in its encoding length.

It remains to bound that encoding length throughout the algorithm.

Denominator growth

Choose integers \(Q,R\) clearing, respectively, all input mass denominators and all endowment denominators. Products of the supplied denominators suffice, so

\( \log Q+\log R=O(L). \)

Maintain an integer \(D\), divisible by \(R\), that clears all current \(u_{tk}\) and the current time. Initially \(D=R\).

Activation times and donor-exhaustion times are sums of current quantities, so their denominators divide \(D\).

On an affine stage, every cut capacity has the form

\( C(\lambda)=A+\frac{z}{Q}\lambda, \)

where \(A\) has denominator dividing \(QD\), and \(z\) is an integer with \(|z|\le Q\). For the slope bound, each type contributes either \(0\), \(\mu_t\), or \(-\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).

If an obstruction occurs at \(\theta<\beta\), a tight cut has nonzero slope and satisfies \(C(\theta)=1\). Writing \(A=c/(QD)\) gives

\( \theta=\frac{QD-c}{Dz}. \)

Thus replacing \(D\) by \(D|z|\) clears \(\theta\) and every updated capacity. Each obstruction adds at most \(\log Q\) bits to this common denominator. There are \(O(\tau m)\) obstructions, so throughout the computation,

\( \log D=O\!\left((1+\tau m)L\right). \)

All maintained capacities and times lie in \([0,1]\), so their numerators also have polynomial bit length. The LP coefficients consequently have polynomial encoding length at every stage.

Computing the right-hand cut exactly

At an obstruction time \(\theta<\beta\), let \(D\) already clear the updated quantities and put \(H=QD\). Every cut capacity at \(\theta\) has denominator dividing \(H\). Since the network is feasible there, any cut not tight at \(1\) has capacity at least \(1+1/H\).

Choose the rational perturbation

\( \varepsilon=\min\left\{\frac{\beta-\theta}{2},\frac{1}{4H}\right\}. \)

Every cut slope has absolute value at most \(1\). Thus a cut that was not tight at \(\theta\) still has capacity greater than \(1\) at \(\theta+\varepsilon\). Since \(\theta\) is the largest feasible time and \(\theta+\varepsilon<\beta\), the minimum cut there has capacity less than \(1\). It is therefore tight at \(\theta\) and has negative slope, as required.

Compute its largest source side by exact maximum flow. This cut is unique: cut-capacity submodularity implies that unions of minimum-cut source sides remain minimum-cut source sides, so their union is the unique largest one. It can also be obtained from the residual graph by excluding every node that can reach the sink.

The perturbation and network capacities have polynomial bit length. Exact maximum flow is therefore polynomial-time executable, for example after clearing denominators.

Combining these bounds proves polynomial total running time in \(m,\tau,L\). The output coordinates are the final \(u_{tk}\), whose bit lengths are polynomially bounded.
