The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite worker-type and firm-type sets \(A,B\), rational masses \((\mu_a)_{a\in A}\), \((\nu_b)_{b\in B}\), and strict rankings over acceptable opposite-side types, find a stable mass matching. The variables \(x_{ab}\ge 0\), \(u_a\ge 0\), and \(v_b\ge 0\) must satisfy \(\sum_b x_{ab}+u_a=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}+v_b=\nu_b\), with \(x_{ab}=0\) for unacceptable pairs. Each unit of mass receives one integral partner or remains unmatched, while aggregate masses may be fractional. For each acceptable pair \((a,b)\), let \(R_{ab}=u_a+\sum_{c:\,b\succ_a c}x_{ac}\) and \(S_{ab}=v_b+\sum_{d:\,a\succ_b d}x_{db}\); require \(R_{ab}=0\) or \(S_{ab}=0\), so no positive masses on both sides prefer to match with each other.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be disjoint finite worker-type and firm-type supports. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) types with positive masses, each encoded as a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Masses need not sum to one or have equal totals on the two sides.
Each type strictly ranks its acceptable opposite-side types above being unmatched. Let \(E\subseteq A\times B\) contain the mutually acceptable pairs and let \(m=|E|\); there are no side payments.
A mass matching consists of nonnegative rational \(x_{ab},u_a,v_b\), with \(x_{ab}=0\) outside \(E\), satisfying \(\sum_{b\in B}x_{ab}+u_a=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_{a\in A}x_{ab}+v_b=\nu_b\). Each individual receives one partner or remains unmatched; a type's mass may be divided among these outcomes.
For \((a,b)\in E\), define \(R_{ab}=u_a+\sum_{c:\,b\succ_a c}x_{ac}\) and \(S_{ab}=v_b+\sum_{d:\,a\succ_b d}x_{db}\), where the sums range over acceptable partners. Stability requires \(R_{ab}=0\) or \(S_{ab}=0\) for every such pair.
Is finding a stable mass matching solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length, or is it NP-hard?
The discrete anchor establishes finite termination of a stable-outcome algorithm for bounded hereditary discrete-concave allocation domains, without a polynomial running-time bound.

  • The output is an exact rational aggregate matching, representing division of each type's mass among individual partner assignments and unmatched individuals.

Conclusion

Finding a stable mass matching is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). This follows from the corrected Baïou–Balinski stable-allocation theorem. Below is an explicit reduction, including the rational-output and bit-complexity arguments.

1. The theorem being applied

Baïou–Balinski consider finite sets \(I,J\), positive real quotas \(s_i,d_j\), nonnegative pair capacities \(c_{ij}\), and strict preferences. An allocation satisfies

\(\sum_j x_{ij}\le s_i,\qquad \sum_i x_{ij}\le d_j,\qquad 0\le x_{ij}\le c_{ij}.\)

Their stability condition is that, for every pair,

\(x_{ij}<c_{ij}\ \Longrightarrow\ \sum_{k:\,k\succeq_i j}x_{ik}=s_i\ \text{or}\ \sum_{h:\,h\succeq_j i}x_{hj}=d_j,\)

where each weak preference includes the partner itself. Their inductive algorithm finds such an allocation using polynomially many arithmetic operations and comparisons, independently of quota and capacity magnitudes. See Section 2, Theorem 2 and Corollary 1 of the [corrected publication](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

2. Construction and feasibility

If \(m=0\), assigning every type’s entire mass to unmatched is stable. Hence assume \(m>0\).

Set \(I=A\), \(J=B\), \(s_a=\mu_a\), and \(d_b=\nu_b\). Define

\(C=1+\sum_{a\in A}\mu_a+\sum_{b\in B}\nu_b.\)

Give every pair in \(E\) capacity \(C\), and every pair outside \(E\) capacity zero. Preserve the given preference order on mutually acceptable partners; if complete preference lists are required, append the remaining partners in any strict order. These added partners have zero allocation and zero pair capacity, so their positions have no effect.

The hypotheses of the theorem hold: the sets are finite, all supported masses are positive, capacities are nonnegative, and preferences are strict. Neither equal total quotas nor normalization is required.

The resulting allocation constraints are precisely

\(x_{ab}\ge 0,\qquad x_{ab}=0\ \text{outside }E,\qquad \sum_bx_{ab}\le\mu_a,\qquad \sum_ax_{ab}\le\nu_b.\)

Indeed, the capacity \(C\) imposes no additional restriction: every feasible coordinate satisfies \(x_{ab}\le\mu_a<C\).

Define the unmatched masses by

\(u_a=\mu_a-\sum_bx_{ab},\qquad v_b=\nu_b-\sum_ax_{ab}.\)

They are nonnegative and give exactly the mass-balance equalities required in the task. Conversely, those equalities imply the allocation inequalities. Thus the feasible objects correspond.

3. Exact equivalence of stability

Fix \((a,b)\in E\). Strict preferences partition the mutually acceptable partners of \(a\) into those better than \(b\), \(b\) itself, and those worse than \(b\). Using mass balance,

\(R_{ab}=u_a+\sum_{c:\,b\succ_a c}x_{ac}=\mu_a-\left(x_{ab}+\sum_{c:\,c\succ_a b}x_{ac}\right).\)

Consequently,

\(R_{ab}=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \sum_{c:\,c\succeq_a b}x_{ac}=\mu_a.\)

The same calculation on the firm side gives

\(S_{ab}=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \sum_{d:\,d\succeq_b a}x_{db}=\nu_b.\)

Because \(x_{ab}<C\) always holds, the theorem’s stability implication applies to every pair in \(E\). Its conclusion is therefore exactly \(R_{ab}=0\) or \(S_{ab}=0\).

For a pair outside \(E\), its capacity and allocation are both zero, so the implication’s antecedent is false. It imposes no additional stability requirement.

Thus stable allocations in the constructed instance correspond exactly to stable mass matchings in the question.

4. Rational output and polynomial bit complexity

Write the input masses as \(p_t/q_t\), with positive integer denominators, and let \(Q=\prod_tq_t\). Since the denominator encodings are part of the input,

\(\log_2 Q\le L.\)

Multiply all quotas and capacities by \(Q\). They become integers with bit length \(O(L+\log(\tau+1))\). Positive scaling preserves feasibility and stability: both \(R_{ab}\) and \(S_{ab}\) are multiplied by \(Q\), so their zero conditions are unchanged.

The inductive algorithm’s numerical updates use addition, subtraction, minima, and comparisons. Thus, on this scaled instance, every intermediate value and every output coordinate is an integer. These operations appear explicitly in Section 2 of the [corrected paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

To check bit complexity, let \(P\) be its polynomial bound on the number of elementary operations, expanding finite sums into binary additions. If initial integers have absolute value at most \(H\), each addition or subtraction can at most double the largest absolute value seen so far; minima and comparisons do not increase it. Hence every intermediate integer has absolute value at most \(2^P H\), and therefore bit length

\(O(L+\log(\tau+1)+P).\)

Exact integer arithmetic consequently takes polynomial total time. Dividing the output by \(Q\) yields rational allocations; computing the residual unmatched masses yields rational \(u_a,v_b\), also in polynomial time.

Finally, the construction uses only supported types. Even a complete matrix has at most \(|A||B|\le\tau^2\) entries. No expansion into individuals, or enumeration of unsupported possible types, is needed.

This proves the requested polynomial-time bound. The discrete finite-termination anchor is unnecessary for this formulation.
