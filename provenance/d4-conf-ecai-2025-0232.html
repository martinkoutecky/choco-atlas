Theorem 4 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_a\), a coalition-size bound \(\alpha\), a per-capita coalition-count bound \(\kappa\), and unknown symmetric type-pair utilities \(w_{ab}\), does there exist a learner with expected regret \(o(T)\) against the minimum achievable maximum pairwise envy? In each round it chooses masses \(x_q\) over nonempty integer group compositions \(q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{\tau}\) with \(\sum_a q_a\le\alpha\), subject to \(\sum_q q_a x_q=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_q x_q\le\kappa\), then receives one independent sub-Gaussian report for each type pair that co-occurs in positive mass (including \((a,a)\) when a group contains at least two type-\(a\) members). For a type-\(a\) person in composition \(q\), own utility is \(\sum_c(q_c-\mathbf{1}[c=a])w_{ac}\); replacing a type-\(b\) member of composition \(r\) gives utility \(\sum_c(r_c-\mathbf{1}[c=b])w_{ac}\). The envy objective is the maximum positive difference between these utilities over types and positive-mass compositions. Can the learner achieve this regret with an \(f(\tau,\alpha)\operatorname{poly}(L,T,1/\varepsilon)\)-time \(\varepsilon\)-accurate implementation, where \(L\) is the input bit length?

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), normalized by \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here \(m=\tau\), since there is no separate underlying ground set.
Let \(\alpha\ge2\) be an integer and \(\kappa\) a binary rational with \(1/\alpha\le\kappa<1\). Write \(Q=\{q\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\tau}:1\le\sum_aq_a\le\alpha\}\). A feasible action is a nonnegative mass vector \(x\) satisfying \(\sum_{q\in Q}q_ax_q=\mu_a\) for every \(a\) and \(\sum_{q\in Q}x_q\le\kappa\); denote its feasible set by \(X\).
Utilities \(w_{ab}=w_{ba}\in\mathbb R\) are fixed and unknown, with a known rational bound \(W>0\) satisfying \(|w_{ab}|\le W\). The value \(w_{aa}\) describes two distinct people of type \(a\), not self-utility; self-utility is zero.
Define \(u_a(q)=\sum_c(q_c-\mathbf1[c=a])w_{ac}\) and \(v_{a,b}(r)=\sum_c(r_c-\mathbf1[c=b])w_{ac}\). Set \(E(x,w)=\max\{0,\max_{x_qx_r>0,\ q_a>0,\ r_b>0}(v_{a,b}(r)-u_a(q))\}\); comparisons concern distinct group instances, allowing \(q=r\). Assume \(E(\cdot,w)\) is nonconstant on \(X\).
At round \(t\), a learner chooses a rational feasible \(x^t\) using only previous feedback. It receives one report \(w_{ab}+\eta^t_{ab}\) for each unordered pair occurring in a positive-mass composition, including \((a,a)\) precisely when some such composition has \(q_a\ge2\). Noises are independent, mean-zero, and identically distributed across rounds for each pair, with known sub-Gaussian proxy \(\sigma^2\).

Use binary computation with reports accessible to requested absolute precision; charge their encoding and precision costs. Let \(L\) count the supplied rational input and processed report bits, and state running times in \(m,\tau,\alpha,L,T,1/\varepsilon,W,\sigma\).

Is achieving expected regret \(R_T=o(T)\) in this model polynomial-time solvable, or is it NP-hard?

Result and interpretation

The answer depends on the quantifiers in “\(R_T=o(T)\).” The following statements hold for the model in the question.

  • Static action selection is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with utilities in \(\{0,1,2\}\), dyadic masses, and the required nonconstancy promise. There is a gap between \(E^\star=0\) and \(E^\star\ge 1\).
  • A learner with polynomial computation and a uniform guarantee that reaches small average regret within a polynomial horizon would imply \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\). In particular, this applies to a uniform bound of order \(T^{2/3}(\log T)^{1/3}\) with a polynomial coefficient.
  • The requested implementation with time \(f(\tau,\alpha)\operatorname{poly}(m,L,T,1/\varepsilon,W,\sigma)\) exists. It preserves feasibility exactly and has a regret bound with numerical overhead at most \(\varepsilon T\).
  • Bare pointwise \(R_T=o(T)\) is achievable in polynomial total time in the stated variables. Its convergence can have an exponential transient depending on the society.

Thus, for the substantive interpretation suggested by the cited regret rate, there is computational hardness. For the literal asymptotic requirement alone, there is a polynomial-time learner. Both assertions are proved below.

Actions are encoded as explicit sparse lists of compositions and positive rational masses. Report encoding and requested precision are charged throughout.

1. A finite family of rational actions suffices

Write \(|q|=\sum_a q_a\), and let

\(N=|Q|=\binom{\tau+\alpha}{\alpha}-1,\qquad r=\tau+1.\)

Introduce a slack variable \(s=\kappa-\sum_q x_q\). Feasibility is equivalent to

\(\sum_{q\in Q}(q,1)x_q+(0,1)s=(\mu,\kappa),\qquad x_q\ge 0,\quad s\ge 0.\)

Let \(A\) denote this integer matrix, with \(N+1\) columns and \(r\) rows. It has rank \(r\): the columns corresponding to \(e_1,\ldots,e_\tau\), together with the slack column, are linearly independent.

The feasible set is nonempty. Indeed, assigning mass \(\mu_a/\alpha\) to composition \(\alpha e_a\) gives total group mass \(1/\alpha\le\kappa\).

Support reduction

For any feasible augmented vector \(y=(x,s)\), suppose its positive columns are linearly dependent. There is then a nonzero vector \(h\), supported on those columns, with \(Ah=0\).

Every column of \(A\) has last coordinate \(1\), so \(\sum_i h_i=0\). Consequently, \(h\) has both positive and negative entries. Set

\(\theta=\min_{h_i>0}y_i/h_i.\)

Then \(y-\theta h\) is feasible, nonnegative, and has strictly smaller support. Repeating this operation yields a feasible vector whose positive columns are independent. Extend these columns to a basis of \(A\), assigning zero to the additional basic variables.

This produces a basic feasible action \(x'\) satisfying

\(\operatorname{supp}(x')\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(x).\)

Removing supported compositions removes envy comparisons. Therefore,

\(E(x',w)\le E(x,w).\)

Enumerate every nonsingular set of \(r\) columns of \(A\), solve its system against \((\mu,\kappa)\), and retain the nonnegative solutions. Let \(\mathcal A\) be the resulting finite family of actions. The preceding argument proves

\(E^\star(w)=\min_{x\in\mathcal A}E(x,w).\)

In particular, the infimum is attained by a rational action supported on at most \(\tau+1\) compositions. This conclusion does not require continuity of \(E\).

Encoding and computation

Let \(L_0\) denote the supplied rational input length. Clear the denominators of \((\mu,\kappa)\) using a common denominator of bit length at most \(L_0\). A basis matrix has dimension \(r\) and entries of absolute value at most \(\alpha\). Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound \(r!\alpha^r\) show that its solution coordinates have bit length

\(O\!\left(L_0+r\log r+r\log\alpha\right).\)

Thus each basis can be processed using polynomially many bit operations. There are at most

\(M=\binom{N+1}{r}\le (N+1)^r\)

bases. Compositions can be indexed and recovered using binomial counts, without first materializing all of \(Q\).

For rational utilities, a certificate for \(E^\star\le h\) consists of one such sparse rational action. Its feasibility and every relevant envy comparison can be checked in polynomial time.

2. A constant-gap hardness reduction

We reduce from the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete clique problem. Its classical hardness is established in [Karp’s original paper](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Start with a graph \(G_0\) and target clique size \(k_0\), with \(2\le k_0\le |V(G_0)|\). To make all supplied masses and \(\kappa\) dyadic, let \(\alpha\) be the least power of two at least \(k_0+1\), and put \(k=\alpha-1\). Add \(k-k_0\) new vertices adjacent to every other vertex, obtaining \(G\).

Then \(G\) has a \(k\)-clique exactly when \(G_0\) has a \(k_0\)-clique: a \(k\)-clique contains at least \(k_0\) original vertices, and an original \(k_0\)-clique extends using the new vertices.

Let \(n=|V(G)|\). This padding is polynomial in the original input size.

Construction

The types are the \(n\) graph vertices and three additional types \(p,s,z\). Thus

\(m=\tau=n+3,\qquad \alpha=k+1,\qquad \kappa=1/\alpha,\qquad W=2.\)

Choose a dyadic rational \(\lambda\) satisfying

\(0<\lambda<\frac{1}{2\alpha(n+2)}.\)

Set

\(\mu_a=\lambda\quad(a\in V(G)),\qquad \mu_s=\mu_z=\lambda,\qquad \mu_p=1-(n+2)\lambda.\)

All masses are positive, normalized, and have polynomial encoding length.

Define symmetric utilities as follows:

  • For graph vertices, \(w_{aa}=0\), and \(w_{ab}=1\) exactly when distinct \(a,b\) are adjacent.
  • For every graph vertex \(a\), \(w_{ap}=w_{as}=1\) and \(w_{az}=0\).
  • Set \(w_{pp}=1\), \(w_{ss}=0\), \(w_{zz}=2\), and \(w_{sz}=1\).
  • Set \(w_{ps}=w_{pz}=0\).

These specify all unordered pairs. The value \(w_{zz}=2\) concerns two distinct people of type \(z\); the subtraction in \(u_z\) still excludes self-utility.

We prove

\(G\text{ has a }k\text{-clique}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ E^\star(w)=0,\)

and otherwise \(E^\star(w)\ge 1\).

Every supported group has full size

For any feasible action,

\(1=\sum_q |q|x_q\le\alpha\sum_q x_q\le\alpha\kappa=1.\)

Equality throughout implies \(\sum_q x_q=1/\alpha\), and every positive-mass composition has size \(\alpha\).

Moreover, every feasible action contains a positive-mass pure \(p\) composition \(\alpha e_p\). Otherwise every supported composition would contain at most \(k\) copies of \(p\), giving

\(\mu_p\le k\sum_q x_q=k/\alpha.\)

But the chosen masses satisfy \(\mu_p>k/\alpha\).

An action with envy below \(1\) yields a clique

Suppose \(E(x,w)<1\).

For any supported composition \(q\) containing types \(a,b\), the allowed comparison with another instance of the same composition gives

\(v_{a,b}(q)-u_a(q)=w_{aa}-w_{ab}.\)

If a composition contained \(z\) and another type \(b\), this difference for \(a=z\) would be at least \(1\). Thus every composition containing \(z\) is pure. Since \(\mu_z>0\), composition \(\alpha e_z\) has positive mass.

Now take any supported composition \(q\) containing \(s\). Comparing an \(s\)-person with a \(z\)-person in the pure \(z\) composition gives prospective utility \(k\). Hence

\(k-u_s(q)<1.\)

The utility \(u_s(q)\) is an integer at most \(k\), so \(u_s(q)=k\). Every one of the \(k\) other occupants must therefore have utility \(1\) for \(s\).

There can be no additional \(s\), no \(p\), and no \(z\): the first two have utility \(0\) for \(s\), and mixed \(z\) compositions were excluded. Thus \(q\) contains one \(s\) and \(k\) graph vertices, initially possibly with repetitions.

For any graph type \(a\) occurring in \(q\), comparison with the positive-mass pure \(p\) composition gives

\(k-u_a(q)<1.\)

Again \(u_a(q)\) is an integer at most \(k\), so it equals \(k\). Every other occupant must have utility \(1\) for \(a\). Because \(w_{aa}=0\), graph vertices cannot repeat. Because nonedges have utility \(0\), all graph vertices in \(q\) are mutually adjacent.

Consequently, \(q\) contains a \(k\)-clique.

A clique yields an exactly feasible zero-envy action

Conversely, let \(C\) be a \(k\)-clique. Use the following compositions and masses:

  • \(e_s+\sum_{a\in C}e_a\), with mass \(\lambda\);
  • \(e_a+k e_p\), with mass \(\lambda\), for each \(a\notin C\);
  • \(\alpha e_z\), with mass \(\lambda/\alpha\);
  • \(\alpha e_p\), with mass \((\mu_p-k(n-k)\lambda)/\alpha\).

The last mass is positive, since

\(\mu_p-k(n-k)\lambda=1-[n+2+k(n-k)]\lambda>0,\)

using \(n+2+k(n-k)\le\alpha(n+2)\).

Each graph type, \(s\), and \(z\) receives exactly its prescribed mass. The remaining \(p\)-mass is assigned to pure \(p\) groups. Since every composition has size \(\alpha\), the total group mass is exactly \(1/\alpha\). Feasibility is exact.

Every graph person, \(s\)-person, and \(p\)-person has utility \(k\). Every \(z\)-person has utility \(2k\). All entries in a non-\(z\) row are at most \(1\), and all entries in the \(z\)-row are at most \(2\). Thus prospective utilities are respectively at most \(k\) and \(2k\). No comparison has positive envy.

Therefore \(E(x,w)=0\).

The nonconstancy promise holds in every constructed instance

The pure-type action \(x_{\alpha e_a}=\mu_a/\alpha\) has envy exactly \(k\). Graph people have own utility \(0\) and prospective utility \(k\) in pure \(p\) groups; \(s\)-people similarly see utility \(k\) in pure \(z\) groups. The row bounds above exclude larger envy.

For a second action, choose any graph vertex \(a_0\). Assign:

  • mass \(\lambda\) to \(e_s+e_{a_0}+(k-1)e_p\);
  • mass \(\lambda\) to \(e_a+k e_p\) for every other graph vertex;
  • mass \(\lambda/\alpha\) to \(\alpha e_z\);
  • all remaining \(p\)-mass to \(\alpha e_p\).

The remaining \(p\)-mass is positive because its numerator is

\(1-[n+2+kn-1]\lambda>0.\)

Graph people have utility \(k\); \(s\)-people have utility \(1\); \(p\)-people have utility \(k\) or \(k-1\); and \(z\)-people have utility \(2k\). Their maximum envies are respectively \(0,k-1,1,0\). Since \(k\ge2\), this action has envy exactly \(k-1\).

Hence \(E\) is nonconstant, independently of whether a clique exists.

Static consequence

The reduction proves the promised gap. Section 1 supplies polynomial-size certificates, so deciding whether \(E^\star=0\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for rational utility input.

In particular, producing an action with

\(E(x,w)\le E^\star(w)+\varepsilon\)

for a fixed \(\varepsilon<1\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard: on a yes-instance its output has envy below \(1\), which certifies and explicitly exhibits a clique.

3. Consequence for uniformly efficient learning

Consider a learner with polynomial total bit cost and the following uniform convergence guarantee: for the constructed instances of encoding length at most \(S\), a computable polynomial horizon \(H(S)\) satisfies

\(R_{H(S)}/H(S)\le 1/4.\)

Simulate the learner on a clique instance. Use identically zero noise, which satisfies the noise assumption with, for example, known proxy \(\sigma^2=1\). Every requested report is a known integer utility.

On a yes-instance, \(E^\star=0\). Choose a round \(J\) uniformly from \(\{1,\ldots,H(S)\}\). Then

\(\mathbb E[E(x^J,w)]\le1/4.\)

By Markov’s inequality,

\(\Pr(E(x^J,w)\ge1)\le1/4.\)

Accept precisely when \(E(x^J,w)<1\). This test takes polynomial time on the explicit output. By the reduction, no no-instance can be accepted, while a yes-instance is accepted with probability at least \(3/4\). Thus clique belongs to \(\mathrm{RP}\), implying \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\).

Requested precision costs must be included in this polynomial bit budget; allowing exponentially expensive report requests would not yield a polynomial-time simulation.

For example, a guarantee

\(R_T\le P(m,\alpha,L_0,W,\sigma)\,T^{2/3}(\log T)^{1/3}\)

with polynomial \(P\), together with polynomial computational and observation costs, satisfies the polynomial-horizon condition. An implementation overhead of \(\varepsilon T\) does not avoid the conclusion: take a fixed sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), and choose the horizon so that the remaining average-regret bound is also sufficiently small.

This establishes hardness for the intended uniform interpretation of efficient sublinear regret.

4. A parameterized learner with explicit regret and bit costs

One feasible action reveals every utility

Let \(d=\tau(\tau+1)/2\), and define

\(\rho=\frac{\min_a\mu_a}{2\alpha(d+1)}.\)

For each \(a<b\), assign mass \(\rho\) to

\(q^{ab}=(\alpha-1)e_a+e_b.\)

Define residual type masses

\(r_a=\mu_a-\rho\sum_{i<j}q^{ij}_a,\)

and assign mass \(r_a/\alpha\) to \(\alpha e_a\).

Since \(\rho\alpha d<\min_a\mu_a/2\), every residual is positive. Every composition has size \(\alpha\), so this action is exactly feasible with total group mass \(1/\alpha\).

Every off-diagonal pair occurs in its designated composition. Every diagonal pair occurs in a pure composition, since \(\alpha\ge2\). Thus each play provides one report for every unordered pair, irrespective of how small the positive masses are.

The action has at most \(\tau+\binom{\tau}{2}\) compositions and polynomial encoding length.

Lipschitz bound

For every relevant comparison, both \(q-e_a\) and \(r-e_b\) are nonnegative vectors with coordinate sums at most \(\alpha-1\). Therefore the comparison, as a linear function of utilities, has coefficient norm at most \(2(\alpha-1)\).

Taking maxima preserves this bound:

\(|E(x,w)-E(x,\widetilde w)|\le2(\alpha-1)\|w-\widetilde w\|_\infty.\)

Also,

\(0\le E(x,w)\le B,\qquad B=2(\alpha-1)W.\)

Explore, estimate, and select

For a given horizon \(T\), play the exploration action for

\(n=\min\{T,\lceil T^{2/3}\rceil\}\)

rounds. Access each report to absolute error at most \(\delta\), average the accessed reports for each pair, and project each average onto \([-W,W]\). Denote the resulting rational symmetric matrix by \(\widehat w\).

The true unrounded sample mean for each pair has centered sub-Gaussian proxy \(\sigma^2/n\). Applying the exponential-moment bound to both signs of all \(d\) errors gives

\(\mathbb E\|\widehat w-w\|_\infty\le \sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2d)}{n}}+\delta.\)

Projection cannot increase error because every true utility lies in \([-W,W]\).

For the remaining rounds, choose exactly a minimizer

\(\widehat x\in\arg\min_{x\in\mathcal A}E(x,\widehat w).\)

If \(x^\star\in\mathcal A\) minimizes true envy, the Lipschitz bound and estimated optimality imply

\(E(\widehat x,w)-E^\star(w)\le4(\alpha-1)\|\widehat w-w\|_\infty.\)

Consequently,

\(R_T\le Bn+4(\alpha-1)(T-n)\left(\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2d)}{n}}+\delta\right).\)

Choosing \(\delta\le\varepsilon/[4(\alpha-1)]\) yields

\(R_T=O\!\left((\alpha-1)\bigl(W+\sigma\sqrt{\log(2d)}\bigr)T^{2/3}\right)+\varepsilon T.\)

Selection on the rational estimates is exact. The numerical term is at most \(\varepsilon T\).

To obtain sublinear regret, use a vanishing accuracy schedule, for example

\(\delta_T\le\frac{\min\{\varepsilon,T^{-1/3}\}}{4(\alpha-1)}.\)

The resulting bound is \(o(T)\). An unknown horizon can be handled by doubling epochs; fresh exploration in each epoch preserves the same order of regret.

Running time

Enumerate the basic feasible actions from Section 1 once, and compare their envy values using exact rational arithmetic. Feasibility is never approximated.

For instance, a valid parameter factor is

\(f(\tau,\alpha)=\left[\binom{\tau+\alpha}{\alpha}\right]^{\tau+1}\operatorname{poly}(\tau,\log\alpha).\)

The total bit time is

\(f(\tau,\alpha)\operatorname{poly}(m,L,T,1/\varepsilon,W,\sigma).\)

Requested fractional precision requires only

\(O\!\left(\log(2+\alpha/\varepsilon)+\log(2+T)\right)\)

bits per accessed report. Unbounded report integer parts and all processed report bits are charged through \(L\). No exact comparison of unknown real utilities is used.

This proves the requested parameterized implementation exists.

5. Why pointwise sublinear regret alone is polynomial-time achievable

For completeness, here is an explicit learner establishing the literal asymptotic claim.

At each round:

  • Process one further basis from Section 1 and retain its action if feasible.
  • At square-numbered rounds, play the full-observation exploration action.
  • At other rounds, minimize estimated envy over the feasible actions retained so far; use the exploration action if none has yet been retained.

At exploration round \(j^2\), access reports with absolute error at most \(1/j\), implemented using dyadic precision. Estimate utilities using these exploration reports only, and project the averages onto \([-W,W]\).

After at most \(M\) rounds, every basis has been processed. The retained family then contains a true optimal action. Before this time, regret is at most \(BM\).

By round \(t\), the number of previous scheduled observations per pair is \(\lfloor\sqrt{t-1}\rfloor\). Averaged numerical error is bounded by

\(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^n\frac1j\le\frac{1+\log n}{n}.\)

The statistical error bound and the Lipschitz argument therefore give, after enumeration is complete, expected exploitation regret per round of order

\(O\!\left((\alpha-1)\left[\sigma\sqrt{\log(2d)}\,t^{-1/4}+(\log t)t^{-1/2}\right]\right).\)

There are at most \(\sqrt T+1\) exploration rounds. Summing yields

\(R_T\le BM+O\!\left(B\sqrt T+(\alpha-1)\sigma\sqrt{\log(2d)}\,T^{3/4}+(\alpha-1)\sqrt T\log(2+T)\right).\)

For every fixed society, \(M\) is finite, so this is \(o(T)\).

Meanwhile, only one basis is processed per round, and at most \(T\) retained actions are evaluated in any round. Total computation is bounded by

\(T^2\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,\alpha,L,W,\sigma).\)

Thus the literal pointwise requirement has a polynomial-time solution. Its potentially exponential transient \(BM\) explains why it does not contradict the uniform hardness result.
