1. The optimal-face identity and preservation of assignments

Let \(V\) denote the original primal optimum and let \(F\) denote the original optimal dual face. Define \(V'\) and \(F'\) analogously.

The primal feasible set is nonempty and compact: the zero assignment is feasible, and every variable is bounded by the corresponding positive capacities. The dual is feasible, for example by taking every seller rate equal to \(\max_{s,b}a_{sb}\) and all buyer rates zero. Strong linear-programming duality therefore gives attained, finite optima.

Moreover, \(F\) is compact. On this face, nonnegativity and positivity of every mass give

\(\displaystyle 0\le u_s\le V/r_s,\qquad 0\le v_b\le V/q_b.\)

Thus all coordinate extrema are attained.

Proposition 1. Under \(0\le c\le\bar u_k\),

\(\displaystyle V'=V-r_kc,\)

and the entire new optimal face is

\(\displaystyle F'=\{(u-ce_k,v):(u,v)\in F,\ u_k\ge c\},\)

where \(e_k\) is the seller-coordinate unit vector.

Proof. Choose an original optimal dual vector satisfying \(u_k\ge c\), which exists because \(c\le\bar u_k\). Subtracting \(c\) from \(u_k\) gives a feasible new dual vector. Indeed, for the changed row:

  • If \(a_{kb}\ge c\), then \(u_k-c+v_b\ge a_{kb}-c=a'_{kb}\).
  • If \(a_{kb}<c\), then \(a'_{kb}=0\), and feasibility follows from nonnegativity.

Its objective is \(V-r_kc\), so \(V'\le V-r_kc\).

For any original optimal assignment \(x\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{s,b}a'_{sb}x_{sb} =V-\sum_b\min\{a_{kb},c\}x_{kb} \ge V-c\sum_bx_{kb} \ge V-r_kc.\)

Hence \(V'=V-r_kc\). The same calculation applies to every original optimal assignment, proving that each remains optimal.

It also proves that every original optimal dual vector with \(u_k\ge c\), after translation, belongs to \(F'\).

Conversely, if \((u',v')\in F'\), then \((u'+ce_k,v')\) is feasible for the original dual, since

\(\displaystyle u'_k+c+v'_b\ge a'_{kb}+c\ge a_{kb}.\)

Its objective is \(V'+r_kc=V\), so it belongs to \(F\). This proves the face identity. \(\square\)

There is a stronger assignment statement.

Proposition 2. If \(c<\bar u_k\), the original and new optimal-assignment sets are identical. At \(c=\bar u_k\), the new set can be strictly larger.

Proof. Take an original optimal dual vector with \(u_k=\bar u_k\). Its translated new rate satisfies \(u'_k=\bar u_k-c>0\). Complementary slackness with any new optimal assignment \(x'\) therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_bx'_{kb}=r_k.\)

Every positive changed-row entry also satisfies

\(\displaystyle a'_{kb}=u'_k+v'_b>0,\)

so \(a_{kb}>c\). Consequently its original objective is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{s,b}a_{sb}x'_{sb} =\sum_{s,b}a'_{sb}x'_{sb}+r_kc =V.\)

Thus \(x'\) was originally optimal.

For strict enlargement at the endpoint, take one seller and one buyer, both of mass \(2\), with surplus \(A>0\). Originally only \(x=2\) is optimal and \(\bar u_k=A\). At \(c=A\), the new surplus is zero, so every \(0\le x\le2\) is optimal. \(\square\)

2. Simultaneous extrema of the weighted dual

The familiar lattice property continues to hold with arbitrary positive weights.

Lemma 3. If \((u,v)\) and \((U,W)\) belong to \(F\), then both

\(\displaystyle (\min\{u,U\},\max\{v,W\})\)

and

\(\displaystyle (\max\{u,U\},\min\{v,W\})\)

belong to \(F\), with coordinatewise minima and maxima.

Proof. For the first vector, whichever seller rate realizes the minimum is paired with a buyer rate at least as large as its original partner rate. Thus every dual inequality remains satisfied. The second vector is feasible by the symmetric argument, starting from whichever buyer rate realizes the minimum.

Their weighted objectives sum to \(2V\), because coordinatewise minimum plus maximum equals the sum of the original coordinates. Each feasible dual objective is at least \(V\), so both equal \(V\). \(\square\)

Combining finitely many coordinate extremizers using this lemma yields simultaneous optimal vectors

\(\displaystyle (\underline u,\bar v)\in F,\qquad (\bar u,\underline v)\in F.\)

In particular, \((\bar u,\underline v)\) satisfies \(u_k\ge c\). Proposition 1 therefore immediately gives

\(\displaystyle \bar u'_k=\bar u_k-c,\qquad \bar u'_s=\bar u_s\quad(s\ne k),\qquad \underline v'_b=\underline v_b\quad(b\in B).\)

The remaining extrema require understanding the restriction \(u_k\ge c\).

3. A graph giving every exact response

Put

\(\displaystyle \ell_s=\underline u_s,\qquad h_b=\bar v_b,\qquad d=(c-\ell_k)_+.\)

Fix any original optimal assignment \(x^*\). Construct a directed graph on the supported types \(S\cup B\):

  • For every pair \((s,b)\), add an arc \(b\to s\) of length

\(\displaystyle w_{sb}=\ell_s+h_b-a_{sb}.\)

  • Whenever \(x^*_{sb}>0\), add an arc \(s\to b\) of length zero.

All lengths are nonnegative, since \((\ell,h)\) is dual feasible. On a positive-support pair, complementary slackness gives \(\ell_s+h_b=a_{sb}\), so the reverse arc \(b\to s\) also has length zero.

Let \(\lambda_j\) be the shortest-path distance from \(k\) to type \(j\), allowing \(\lambda_j=\infty\) when no path exists. Define

\(\displaystyle \rho_j= \begin{cases} (d-\lambda_j)_+,&\lambda_j<\infty,\\ 0,&\lambda_j=\infty. \end{cases}\)

In particular, \(\lambda_k=0\) and \(\rho_k=d\).

Theorem 4. The exact changes are:

| Type | Change in minimum | Change in maximum | Change in midpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seller \(k\) | \(-\min\{c,\ell_k\}\) | \(-c\) | \(-c+d/2\) |
| Seller \(s\ne k\) | \(\rho_s\) | \(0\) | \(\rho_s/2\) |
| Buyer \(b\) | \(0\) | \(-\rho_b\) | \(-\rho_b/2\) |

These formulas hold for every choice of the original optimal assignment \(x^*\).

Proof of the graph formula

For an original optimal vector \((u,v)\), define nonnegative movement coordinates

\(\displaystyle z_s=u_s-\ell_s,\qquad z_b=h_b-v_b.\)

They are nonnegative by the definitions of \(\ell_s\) and \(h_b\).

We first characterize exactly which movement vectors correspond to original dual optima. They satisfy:

  • \(z_j\ge0\) for every type \(j\).
  • \(z_b\le h_b\) for every buyer.
  • For every graph arc \(i\to j\) of length \(w\), \(z_j\ge z_i-w\).
  • \(z_j=0\) whenever type \(j\)’s capacity is not saturated in \(x^*\).

For the last condition, an unsaturated seller has \(u_s=\ell_s=0\), while an unsaturated buyer has \(v_b=h_b=0\), by complementary slackness.

The arc \(b\to s\) expresses the original dual inequality:

\(\displaystyle u_s+v_b-a_{sb}=w_{sb}+z_s-z_b\ge0.\)

On a positive-support pair, the two zero-length arcs impose \(z_s=z_b\), which expresses equality in that dual constraint.

Conversely, any movement vector satisfying these conditions produces a nonnegative feasible dual vector

\(\displaystyle u_s=\ell_s+z_s,\qquad v_b=h_b-z_b.\)

It is tight on every positive-support pair and has zero rate at every unsaturated type. Hence it is optimal. Explicitly, the duality gap is

\(\displaystyle \sum_sr_su_s+\sum_bq_bv_b-\sum_{s,b}a_{sb}x^*_{sb} = \sum_su_s\Bigl(r_s-\sum_bx^*_{sb}\Bigr) +\sum_bv_b\Bigl(q_b-\sum_sx^*_{sb}\Bigr) +\sum_{s,b}x^*_{sb}(u_s+v_b-a_{sb}),\)

and every term on the right is zero.

The restriction \(u_k\ge c\) is equivalent to \(z_k\ge d\). Along any directed path from \(k\) to \(j\) of length \(D\), the arc inequalities imply

\(\displaystyle z_j\ge z_k-D\ge d-D.\)

Together with nonnegativity, this gives \(z_j\ge\rho_j\) for every vector in the restricted face.

We now prove that \(z=\rho\) itself is admissible. For any arc \(i\to j\) of length \(w\), the shortest-path inequality gives

\(\displaystyle \lambda_j\le\lambda_i+w\)

whenever \(\lambda_i\) is finite. Since \(w\ge0\), it follows that

\(\displaystyle \rho_j\ge\rho_i-w.\)

If \(\lambda_i=\infty\), then \(\rho_i=0\), so the same inequality follows from \(\rho_j\ge0\).

It remains to check buyer upper bounds and forced-zero coordinates. The restricted face is nonempty: it contains \((\bar u,\underline v)\). Let \(\widehat z\) be its movement vector. The preceding path argument shows \(\rho_j\le\widehat z_j\) for every \(j\). Therefore \(\rho_b\le h_b\), and every forced-zero coordinate of \(\rho\) is zero. Also \(\rho_k=d\).

Thus \(\rho\) is the coordinatewise least admissible movement vector satisfying the restriction. Consequently the restricted original face has simultaneous seller minima and buyer maxima

\(\displaystyle u_s=\ell_s+\rho_s,\qquad v_b=h_b-\rho_b.\)

Translate its \(k\)-coordinate by Proposition 1. This proves every entry of the table. \(\square\)
