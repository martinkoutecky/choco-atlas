The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) object categories with rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\) summing to \(1\), known rational masses of ordinal ranking types, and an explicit finite menu of consistent rational unit-sum utility vectors, the actual distribution \(\nu\) over complete ranking–utility types is hidden. A deterministic protocol precommits to one threshold \(q_{rj}\) for each acceptable ranking–object pair, observes the mass \(y_{r,z}\) of each ranking and threshold-response class, then returns mass flows \(x_{r,z,j}\) assigning each unit to at most one acceptable category within its supply. Require the allocation to be Pareto efficient under the ordinal rankings, and measure loss against the full-information welfare optimum over Pareto-efficient allocations for the true \(\nu\), in the worst case over distributions consistent with the known ranking masses and observed responses. What is the best achievable worst-case ratio as a function of \(m\), the finite type-menu size \(\tau\), and input bit length \(L\); in particular, is a polynomial-time \(O(m^{2/3})\) protocol possible, and does an \(\Omega(\sqrt{m})\) lower bound hold?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) complete types \(t=(r,u)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\nu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. The protocol knows this support menu and the ranking masses \(a_r=\sum_{t:r(t)=r}\nu_t\), but not the individual masses.
There are \(m\) categories with positive rational supplies \(b_j\), where \(\sum_j b_j=1\). Each ranking \(r\) is a weak order on a nonempty acceptable set \(A_r\); unmatched is strictly below every acceptable category. Utilities satisfy \(u_j\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\), \(u_j=0\) outside \(A_r\), \(\sum_{j\in A_r}u_j=1\), and \(j\succeq_r k\iff u_j\ge u_k\) for acceptable categories; unmatched has utility zero.
A deterministic protocol chooses rational thresholds \(q_{rj}\in[0,1]\) before receiving any responses, one for each acceptable ranking–category pair. Type \(t=(r,u)\) responds with \(z_j=\mathbf1\{u_j\ge q_{rj}\}\); the protocol receives only the positive class masses \(y_{r,z}=\sum_{t:r(t)=r,\,z(t)=z}\nu_t\).
It returns rational flows \(x_{r,z,j}\ge0\), zero for unacceptable categories, with \(\sum_jx_{r,z,j}\le y_{r,z}\) and \(\sum_{r,z}x_{r,z,j}\le b_j\). Within a class, every hidden type receives the same per-capita allocation: \(f_{t,j}=\nu_t x_{r,z,j}/y_{r,z}\); the residual is unmatched.
Require \(f\) to be ordinally Pareto efficient for every compatible \(\nu\): no feasible allocation indexed by complete types weakly improves every type’s per-capita allocation by stochastic dominance under its ranking and strictly improves some type. Ties remain indifferences.
Let \(W_\nu(x)=\sum_{t,j}u_{t,j}f_{t,j}\), and let \(\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)\) be the maximum welfare among ordinally Pareto-efficient feasible allocations that can distinguish complete types. Loss is \(\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)/W_\nu(x)\), with positive-over-zero equal to \(+\infty\) and zero-over-zero equal to \(1\); take the supremum over public inputs, response transcripts, and compatible positive rational masses.
What is the best achievable worst-case approximation ratio as a function of \(m,\tau,L\)? In particular, does a deterministic protocol running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) achieve \(O(m^{2/3})\), and must every deterministic protocol incur \(\Omega(\sqrt m)\) loss on some family of inputs?
Here \(L\) is the total binary encoding length of the public input and observed class masses; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and any additional parameters.
For discrete societies with \(n\) agents and \(n\) individual objects, deterministic non-adaptive protocols using at most one threshold query per agent–object pair have worst-case loss \(\Omega(\sqrt n)\), while a polynomial-time protocol achieves \(O(n^{2/3})\) with Pareto-optimal output. 1. Stochastic dominance and class-level efficiency

For each ranking $r$, let its acceptable indifference tiers, ordered from best to worst, be
$$ B_{r,1},\ldots,B_{r,h_r}, $$
and define
$$ P_{r,\ell}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}B_{r,i}. $$

Because unmatched is strictly below every acceptable category, an allocation $g$ weakly improves type $t$ over allocation $f$ exactly when
$$ \frac{g_t(P_{r,\ell})}{\nu_t} \ge \frac{f_t(P_{r,\ell})}{\nu_t} \qquad \text{for every }\ell. $$
A strict improvement requires a strict inequality for at least one prefix of at least one type.

Fix thresholds and a transcript. Write $c=(r,z)$ for a nonempty response class and $y_c$ for its positive mass.

Lemma 1. A feasible class allocation $x$ is ordinally Pareto efficient among class allocations if and only if its induced complete-type allocation is ordinally Pareto efficient. Consequently, this efficiency condition does not depend on the hidden distribution within classes.

Proof. Suppose a complete-type allocation $g$ dominates the allocation induced by $x$. For every $t\in c$ and every acceptable prefix,
$$ g_t(P_{r,\ell}) \ge \frac{\nu_t}{y_c}x_c(P_{r,\ell}). $$
Define aggregated flows
$$ G_{c,j}=\sum_{t\in c}g_{t,j}. $$
Aggregation preserves row capacities, category capacities, and acceptability. Summing the preceding inequalities gives
$$ G_c(P_{r,\ell})\ge x_c(P_{r,\ell}). $$
A strict type-prefix inequality remains strict after summation, since all other inequalities for that class and prefix are weak. Thus $G$ dominates $x$.

Conversely, if a feasible class allocation $G$ dominates $x$, distribute it proportionally:
$$ g_{t,j}=\frac{\nu_t}{y_c}G_{c,j} \qquad(t\in c). $$
This allocation is feasible and transfers all class-prefix inequalities to the types in that class. Positivity of the type masses ensures that a strict class-prefix improvement gives a strict type improvement. This proves both implications. $\square$

Stochastic dominance also weakly increases cardinal welfare. To verify this explicitly, let $v_\ell$ be a type’s utility on tier $B_{r,\ell}$ and set $v_{h_r+1}=0$. For any per-capita allocation $p$,
$$ \sum_j u_jp_j = \sum_{\ell=1}^{h_r} (v_\ell-v_{\ell+1})p(P_{r,\ell}). $$
The identity follows by telescoping the coefficient of each category. All coefficients are nonnegative. It remains valid when the bottom acceptable tier has utility zero.

2. A polynomial-time protocol and its welfare bound

Let
$$ b_{\min}=\min_j b_j. $$
Choose all thresholds equal to zero. Every supported type then reports one on every acceptable coordinate, so there is one response class per ranking, with public mass $a_r$.

Start with
$$ x^0_{r,j}= \begin{cases} a_rb_j,&j\in A_r,\\ 0,&j\notin A_r. \end{cases} $$
This allocation is feasible because
$$ \sum_jx^0_{r,j} = a_r\sum_{j\in A_r}b_j \le a_r $$
and
$$ \sum_rx^0_{r,j} = b_j\sum_{r:j\in A_r}a_r \le b_j. $$

To obtain ordinal efficiency, solve the rational linear program over feasible class flows that imposes
$$ x_r(P_{r,\ell})\ge x^0_r(P_{r,\ell}) $$
for every ranking and acceptable prefix, and maximizes
$$ \Phi(x)=\sum_r\sum_{\ell=1}^{h_r}x_r(P_{r,\ell}). $$
The baseline makes this program feasible, and row capacities make its feasible region bounded. Any allocation dominating a maximizer would satisfy the baseline constraints and strictly increase $\Phi$. Hence every maximizer is ordinally Pareto efficient. Lemma 1 gives the required efficiency for every compatible hidden distribution.

Define the population’s aggregate utility coordinates by
$$ U_j=\sum_t\nu_tu_{t,j}. $$
Normalization gives
$$ U_j\ge0, \qquad \sum_jU_j=1. $$
The baseline welfare is exactly
$$ W_\nu(x^0)=\sum_jb_jU_j. $$
By prefix dominance and the utility decomposition,
$$ W_\nu(x)\ge\sum_jb_jU_j\ge b_{\min}. $$
Every feasible allocation has welfare at most $1$. Therefore
$$ \frac{\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)}{W_\nu(x)} \le \frac1{b_{\min}}. $$

There are $O(m\tau)$ variables and constraints, with coefficients and right-hand sides of polynomial encoding length. The standard exact rational linear-programming theorem therefore gives a deterministic $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ running time and rational output of polynomial bit length.

Under ordinary binary numerator-denominator encoding,
$$ b_{\min}\ge 2^{-O(L)}. $$
Thus this protocol has ratio at most $2^{O(L)}$.

3. A factor-$2$ guarantee for two categories

The preceding protocol has a substantially stronger guarantee when $m=2$.

Proposition 2. For $m=2$, the protocol in Section 2 has approximation ratio at most $2$, for every support size and every positive supply vector.

Proof. For any feasible complete-type allocation $g$, its category-$j$ welfare contribution satisfies both
$$ \sum_tu_{t,j}g_{t,j}\le b_j $$
and
$$ \sum_tu_{t,j}g_{t,j}\le U_j. $$
The first inequality uses $u_{t,j}\le1$ and the category capacity. The second uses $g_{t,j}\le\nu_t$. Consequently,
$$ \operatorname{OPT}(\nu) \le \min(b_1,U_1)+\min(b_2,U_2). $$

Put $s=b_1$ and $t=U_1$. Then
$$ b_2=1-s,\qquad U_2=1-t, $$
and the baseline welfare is
$$ D=st+(1-s)(1-t). $$
We claim that
$$ \min(s,t)+\min(1-s,1-t)\le2D. $$

Both sides are symmetric in $s,t$, so assume $s\le t$. The left side is then $1-t+s$, and
$$ 2D-(1-t+s)=1-3s-t+4st. $$
If $s\ge1/4$, this expression is nondecreasing in $t$, so its minimum over $t\in[s,1]$ occurs at $t=s$ and equals
$$ (1-2s)^2\ge0. $$
If $s<1/4$, it is decreasing in $t$, so its minimum occurs at $t=1$ and equals $s\ge0$. This proves the claim.

The output welfare is at least $D$, by Section 2. Hence
$$ \operatorname{OPT}(\nu)\le2D\le2W_\nu(x). $$
The running time is $\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)$. $\square$

For $m=1$, the sole supply is $1$ and every normalized type values that category at $1$; full allocation achieves ratio $1$.

4. Two categories do not always permit exact welfare

The constant bound above cannot be replaced by ratio $1$ for all supports.

Proposition 3. For every fixed $\tau\ge4$, every deterministic protocol has worst-case loss at least $7/6$ when $m=2$, taking the supremum over public inputs and encoding lengths.

More quantitatively, there are instances of length
$$ O(k+\tau\log(\tau+1)) $$
on which every deterministic protocol has loss at least
$$ \frac76-6\cdot2^{-k}. $$

Proof. Fix $k\ge6$, put $\varepsilon=2^{-k}$, and define four core types by
$$ v_i=\varepsilon+i\left(\frac16-\varepsilon\right), \qquad i=0,1,2,3, $$
$$ u^{(i)}=(1-v_i,v_i). $$
These parameters are strictly increasing, positive, and smaller than $1/2$. All four types therefore accept both categories and have the common strict ranking
$$ 1\succ2. $$
Set
$$ b_1=b_2=\frac12. $$

If $\tau>4$, add $\tau-4$ types with parameters
$$ w_\ell=\frac14+\frac{\ell}{16\tau}, \qquad \ell=1,\ldots,\tau-4, $$
and utilities $(1-w_\ell,w_\ell)$. These parameters lie strictly between $1/4$ and $5/16$. For $k\ge6$, none equals a core parameter. Thus all supported types are distinct and share the same ranking. Its public mass is $1$.

For the four core types, the two response bits are
$$ z_1=\mathbf1\{v_i\le1-q_1\}, \qquad z_2=\mathbf1\{v_i\ge q_2\}. $$
Along their ordered parameters, each bit changes at most once. Therefore the four core types occupy at most three response classes. Two share a class. Call them $H,L$, with
$$ v_H>v_L, \qquad \Delta=v_H-v_L\ge\frac16-\varepsilon. $$

Choose their hidden masses to be
$$ \nu_H=\nu_L=\frac12-\varepsilon. $$
Give all remaining types positive dyadic masses summing to $2\varepsilon$. Such masses exist: if there are $N=\tau-2$ remaining types, put $d=\lceil\log_2N\rceil$, assign $2\varepsilon/2^d$ to the first $N-1$, and give the last the positive remainder.

Let $c$ be the class containing $H,L$. Its mass satisfies
$$ y_c\ge1-2\varepsilon. $$
Write
$$ \bar v=\frac{v_H+v_L}{2}. $$
Their combined welfare under any feasible class allocation is
$$ \frac{1-2\varepsilon}{y_c} \left((1-\bar v)x_{c,1}+\bar v x_{c,2}\right). $$
Since both category supplies are $1/2$, this is at most $1/2$. The other types have total mass $2\varepsilon$ and utilities at most $1$, so
$$ W_\nu(x)\le\frac12+2\varepsilon. $$

For a benchmark, give category $1$ to all mass of $L$ and category $2$ to all mass of $H$. Give each remaining type half of its mass in each category. This allocation fills both supplies and fully allocates every type. Its welfare is
$$ \left(\frac12-\varepsilon\right)(1+\Delta)+\varepsilon = \frac12+\left(\frac12-\varepsilon\right)\Delta. $$

It is ordinally Pareto efficient. Indeed, all types have the same ranking and both supplies are exhausted. A strict stochastic-dominance improvement would strictly increase the aggregate mass of some acceptable prefix beyond its supply.

Therefore
$$ \frac{\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)}{W_\nu(x)} \ge \frac{1+(1-2\varepsilon)(1/6-\varepsilon)} {1+4\varepsilon}. $$
The difference between $7/6$ and this last expression is
$$ \frac{6\varepsilon-2\varepsilon^2}{1+4\varepsilon} \le6\varepsilon. $$
Thus the loss is at least $7/6-6\cdot2^{-k}$.

The four core utility vectors have $O(k)$-bit descriptions. The added vectors require $O(\log(\tau+1))$ bits each. There are at most three response classes, whose masses have $O(k+\log(\tau+1))$-bit descriptions. This gives the stated length bound. Letting $k$ tend to infinity proves the supremum lower bound. $\square$

Thus, for two categories and $\tau\ge4$, the optimal worst-case ratio is between $7/6$ and $2$. Its order of growth is constant; its exact value remains open here.

5. Exponential obstruction for every $m\ge3$, $\tau\ge4$

The following construction extends the verified four-type obstruction while keeping track of the cost of additional categories and support types.

Proposition 4. There is an absolute encoding constant $C$ such that, for every $m\ge3$, $\tau\ge4$, and integer $k\ge2$, every deterministic protocol has an instance with loss at least
$$ \frac{2^k}{7} $$
and encoding length at most
$$ C\left( k+m\tau+\tau\log_2(\tau+1)+m\log_2(m+1) \right). $$

Proof. Put
$$ s=2^{-k}, \qquad v_i=\frac{s^{2i}}4\quad(i=0,1,2,3), \qquad \beta=\frac{s^8}{4}=s^2v_3. $$
The four core types accept categories $1,2,3$ and have utilities
$$ u^{(i)}=(1-v_i-\beta,\ v_i,\ \beta), $$
with zero utilities outside these three categories. Since $k\ge2$,
$$ 1-v_i-\beta>v_i>\beta>0. $$
Thus all four have the common strict ranking
$$ 1\succ2\succ3. $$

If $\tau>4$, add types indexed by $\ell=1,\ldots,\tau-4$ with utilities
$$ u^{(\ell)}_1=\frac{13}{16}-\frac{\ell}{32\tau}, \qquad u^{(\ell)}_2=\frac18+\frac{\ell}{32\tau}, \qquad u^{(\ell)}_3=\frac1{16}, $$
and zero utilities elsewhere. They are normalized, distinct, and have the same strict ranking. They are distinct from the core types because their third utility is $1/16$, whereas the core third utility is $\beta<1/16$.

For $m=3$, set
$$ b_1=\beta,\qquad b_2=s,\qquad b_3=1-s-\beta. $$
For $m>3$, set
$$ b_1=\beta,\qquad b_2=s,\qquad b_3=\frac34-s-\beta, $$
and
$$ b_j=\frac1{4(m-3)} \qquad(j=4,\ldots,m). $$
All supplies are positive and sum to $1$. Every type finds only categories $1,2,3$ acceptable. The sole ranking has public mass $1$.

5.1 A collision survives every threshold choice

For a core type with parameter $v$, its response is
$$ z_1=\mathbf1\{v\le1-\beta-q_1\}, \qquad z_2=\mathbf1\{v\ge q_2\}, \qquad z_3=\mathbf1\{\beta\ge q_3\}. $$
The third bit is constant over the four core types. The first and second bits each change at most once along their ordered parameters. Consequently, at most three response classes contain core types.

Choose two colliding core types with parameters $v_H>v_L$. Their geometric spacing gives
$$ v_L\le s^2v_H. $$

Now choose hidden masses
$$ \nu_H=s,\qquad \nu_L=1-s-\beta, $$
and give all other types positive dyadic masses summing to $\beta$. As in Proposition 3, this can be done with denominators having $O(k+\log(\tau+1))$ bits.

This adversarial choice is legitimate: the public menu and ranking mass were fixed before the thresholds, and these masses preserve that public information.

Their shared class $c$ has mass
$$ y_c\ge\nu_H+\nu_L=1-\beta\ge\frac12. $$

5.2 Every feasible output has small welfare

Category $1$ contributes at most $\beta$.

For category $2$, identical per-capita allocation within $c$ implies
$$ f_{H,2} = \frac{s}{y_c}x_{c,2} \le \frac{s^2}{y_c} \le2s^2. $$
Thus $H$ contributes at most $2s^2v_H$. Type $L$ contributes at most $sv_L$, because $f_{L,2}\le x_{c,2}\le s$. All other types together contribute at most their total mass $\beta$.

The core types’ category-$3$ contribution is at most $\beta$, since their utility there is $\beta$. The added types’ contribution is at most their total mass, hence at most $\beta$. Thus category $3$ contributes at most $2\beta$.

All other categories are unacceptable. Therefore every feasible output satisfies
$$ W_\nu(x)\le4\beta+2s^2v_H+sv_L. $$
Using
$$ \beta=s^2v_3\le s^2v_H, \qquad v_L\le s^2v_H, $$
we obtain
$$ W_\nu(x) \le(6+s)s^2v_H \le7s^2v_H. $$

5.3 An efficient benchmark has larger welfare

Give all category $2$, of mass $s$, to type $H$, and all category $1$, of mass $\beta$, to type $L$.

If $m=3$, give all remaining population mass category $3$. Its total is exactly
$$ 1-s-\beta=b_3, $$
so this is feasible.

If $m>3$, give all category $3$ to $L$. Its total allocation is
$$ \beta+b_3=\frac34-s \le1-s-\beta=\nu_L, $$
where the inequality follows from $\beta<1/4$.

In both cases, the benchmark exhausts every acceptable category and has welfare at least $sv_H$.

To verify ordinal efficiency, suppose a feasible allocation weakly improves every type and strictly improves one. Because all types have the same ranking, some acceptable prefix has a strict type improvement. Summing its mass inequalities over types would give an aggregate prefix allocation strictly exceeding the benchmark’s value. That value already equals the prefix’s full supply, a contradiction.

Consequently,
$$ \operatorname{OPT}(\nu)\ge sv_H, $$
and
$$ \frac{\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)}{W_\nu(x)} \ge \frac{sv_H}{7s^2v_H} = \frac{2^k}{7}. $$
If output welfare is zero, the prescribed infinite-loss convention preserves this bound.

5.4 Encoding length

The core parameters are
$$ s=2^{-k}, \qquad v_i=2^{-2ik-2}, \qquad \beta=2^{-8k-2}, $$
so their utility vectors and the first three supplies have $O(k)$-bit descriptions.

Each added type has $O(\log(\tau+1))$-bit nonzero utilities. Explicitly listing the utility vectors, including zero entries, costs
$$ O(m\tau+\tau\log(\tau+1)+k). $$
The extra supplies cost $O(m\log(m+1))$ bits.

There are only three queried coordinates and one ranking, hence at most eight response classes. Their masses are dyadic with $O(k+\log(\tau+1))$-bit descriptions. Combining these bounds proves the stated encoding estimate. $\square$

The obstruction already holds at $m=3$, $\tau=4$, with all categories acceptable and all utilities strictly positive. The extra categories and types serve only to extend it to other parameter values.

6. Consequences for the encoding-length minimax ratio

For precision, let $R(m,\tau,L)$ denote the infimum over deterministic protocols of their worst-case loss on inputs and transcripts of total encoding length at most $L$. Protocols remain subject to the required efficiency guarantee for every compatible distribution.

Define
$$ H(m,\tau) = m\tau+\tau\log_2(\tau+1)+m\log_2(m+1). $$
Let $C$ be the constant from Proposition 4. For $m\ge3$, $\tau\ge4$, suppose
$$ L\ge2C H(m,\tau). $$
Choose
$$ k=\left\lfloor\frac{L}{2C}\right\rfloor. $$
Increasing $C$ if necessary ensures $k\ge2$. Proposition 4 gives an instance of length at most
$$ C(k+H(m,\tau))\le L. $$
Its loss is at least
$$ \frac{2^k}{7} \ge \frac1{14}2^{L/(2C)}. $$

Together with Section 2, this proves
$$ 2^{\Omega(L)} \le R(m,\tau,L)\le2^{O(L)} $$
in this regime, with constants independent of $m,\tau,L$ under the displayed length condition. The upper bound is achieved in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ time; the lower bound applies even to protocols without computational restrictions.

In particular, for every fixed $m\ge3$, $\tau\ge4$,
$$ R(m,\tau,L)=2^{\Theta(L)} $$
as $L$ tends to infinity.

The sharp joint dependence when $L$ is close to the minimum size needed to describe the support is not established by these bounds.

7. The two specifically requested conclusions

No uniform $O(m^{2/3})$ guarantee exists. At the fixed values $m=3$, $\tau=4$, Proposition 4 gives loss at least $2^k/7$ for arbitrarily large $k$. Thus no finite bound depending only on $m$ and $\tau$ is possible in that regime. This rules out the proposed guarantee even without a running-time restriction.

Every deterministic protocol incurs $\Omega(\sqrt m)$ loss. Use Proposition 4 with $\tau=4$ and
$$ k=\max\left\{2,\left\lceil\frac12\log_2m\right\rceil\right\}. $$
Then
$$ \frac{2^k}{7}\ge\frac{\sqrt m}{7}. $$
These instances have encoding length $O(m\log(m+1))$. Increasing $k$ gives arbitrarily larger losses while retaining the same $m$ and $\tau$.

8. Exact recovery with at most three types per ranking

For completeness, the exact-recovery result used in the parameter classification is proved here.

Proposition 5. If every ranking supports at most three distinct complete types, a deterministic non-adaptive protocol achieves ratio $1$ in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ time.

Proof. Thresholds can distinguish all supported types within each ranking.

For two distinct utility vectors, choose an acceptable coordinate on which they differ and place its threshold strictly between their values.

For three distinct vectors, choose a nonconstant coordinate. A threshold between consecutive distinct values separates one vector from the other two. The remaining pair differs in at least two coordinates: if it differed in exactly one coordinate, equality of its coordinate sums would force equality there as well. Therefore it differs on a coordinate other than the first queried coordinate. A threshold between its values on that second coordinate separates the pair.

All unused thresholds can be zero. The separating thresholds can be rational midpoints, with polynomial encoding length, and are chosen entirely from the public menu before responses arrive.

Every response class is now a singleton, so the transcript reveals every individual mass. Maximize cardinal welfare over the complete-type flow polytope. Then, among welfare maximizers, maximize
$$ \sum_t\sum_{\ell=1}^{h_{r(t)}}f_t(P_{r(t),\ell}). $$
Both programs are feasible, bounded rational linear programs.

If a feasible allocation stochastically dominated the resulting allocation, its welfare would weakly increase by the utility decomposition in Section 1. A strict welfare increase contradicts the first maximization. If welfare stayed equal, a strict prefix improvement would strictly increase the second objective, contradicting the second maximization.

Thus the output is ordinally Pareto efficient and attains the unrestricted maximum feasible welfare. It therefore attains $\operatorname{OPT}(\nu)$.

Threshold construction and both linear programs take $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ time. $\square$

In particular, the optimal ratio is $1$ whenever $\tau\le3$.
