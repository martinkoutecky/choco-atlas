The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(K\) arms and \(\tau\) types with known positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, each type has an unknown reciprocal preference matrix \(P_t\) with a Condorcet winner \(a_t^\star\). Each round, a learner chooses two policies \(\pi,\pi' \in \Delta_K\), samples an arm from each, and receives one independent preference response from each type. Define \(u_t(\pi)=\sum_{a=1}^K \pi(a)\,2P_{t,a,a_t^\star}\) and \(\Phi_\mu(\pi)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\log u_t(\pi)\right)\), with zero welfare if any positive-mass type has zero utility. What is the minimax expected cumulative regret over horizon \(H\), as a function of \(H,K,\tau,\mu\), and the preference gaps, and can an algorithm attain the resulting rate?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) type labels with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to one; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(m=K\), let \(K,\tau\geq4\) be even, and let \(H\geq1\) be an integer horizon; \(L\) is the bit length of the supplied data. State running times in \(K,\tau,L,H\) and any additional parameters.
Each type has a fixed unknown matrix \(P_t\in[0,1]^{K\times K}\), with \(P_{t,i,j}+P_{t,j,i}=1\) and \(P_{t,i,i}=1/2\), and a unique strict Condorcet winner \(a_t^\star\). Other pairwise ties and probabilities zero or one are allowed.
At round \(h\), the learner adaptively chooses \(\pi_h,\pi'_h\in\Delta_K\), independently samples a shared pair \(i_h\sim\pi_h,j_h\sim\pi'_h\), and observes the pair and one response \(Y_{h,t}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(P_{t,i_h,j_h})\) from every type. Conditional on the pair and past, responses are independent across types and use fresh randomness each round; equal sampled arms are permitted.
Define \(u_t(\pi)=\sum_a\pi(a)2P_{t,a,a_t^\star}\) and \(\Phi_\mu(\pi)=\exp(\sum_t\mu_t\log u_t(\pi))\), with value zero when any utility is zero. Let \(\pi^\star\in\arg\max_{\pi\in\Delta_K}\Phi_\mu(\pi)\) and \(R_H=\sum_{h=1}^H[\Phi_\mu(\pi^\star)-(\Phi_\mu(\pi_h)+\Phi_\mu(\pi'_h))/2]\).
For a supplied rational gap bound \(g\in[0,1/2]\), let \(\mathcal P_g\) contain exactly the above preference tensors satisfying \(\min_{t,i\ne j}|P_{t,i,j}-1/2|\geq g\). The learner knows \(K,\tau,H,\mu,g\), but neither the matrices nor their winners. Define \(\mathcal R^*(H,K,\tau,\mu,g)=\inf_A\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_g}\mathbb E_{A,P}[R_H]\), where the infimum ranges over randomized adaptive learners.

Partial minimax characterization

The full joint minimax rate remains unresolved. However, the previous results can be strengthened in two directions:

  • The small-gap $H^{2/3}$ lower bound holds for every supplied mass vector in a larger regime, without a mass-partition condition.
  • For sufficiently large gaps, the minimax horizon rate is logarithmic, rather than $H^{2/3}$.

All results below concern the supplied support of size $\tau$. Distinct supported types provide independent responses even when their preference matrices coincide.

1. Proven bounds

Define
$$ \mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t,\qquad \delta=1-\max_t\mu_t,\qquad b=\min\{K,\tau\}, $$
$$ B_0=\min\{\tau,K-2\},\qquad Q=\min\left\{\binom K2,\tau(K-1)\right\}, $$
and
$$ \Lambda=\left\lceil\log_2\bigl(16\tau K^2(H+1)\bigr)\right\rceil. $$

The following statements are proved below.

Without a gap promise,
$$ \frac{\delta H}{2048} \leq \mathcal R^*(H,K,\tau,\mu,0) \leq H. $$
Consequently, for every fixed supplied positive mass vector,
$$ \mathcal R^*(H,K,\tau,\mu,0)=\Theta_\mu(H). $$
The joint dependence on the masses is not determined by these bounds.

For every positive gap, an explicit algorithm gives
$$ \mathcal R^*(H,K,\tau,\mu,g) \leq \min\left\{ H,\; C\left[ \frac{Q\Lambda}{g^2} + K^{1/3}b\,\Lambda^{1/3}H^{2/3} +1 \right] \right\}, $$
where $C$ is universal.

For small positive gaps, if
$$ 0<g<\frac{1-1/B_0}{2}, $$
then, for every fixed supplied mass vector,
$$ \mathcal R^*(H,K,\tau,\mu,g) = \widetilde\Theta_{K,\tau,\mu,g}(H^{2/3}). $$
Here the upper bound hides logarithms in $H$, and the lower-bound constant may depend on the fixed parameters. This improves the previous partition-dependent lower bound.

For large gaps, suppose
$$ 0<1-2g<\mu_{\min}. $$
Set
$$ c=1-2g,\qquad \kappa=\frac{\mu_{\min}-c}{1-c},\qquad \rho=1-c(b-1). $$
Then $\kappa,\rho>0$, and an explicit algorithm gives
$$ \mathcal R^*(H,K,\tau,\mu,g) \leq \min\left\{ H,\; C\left[ \frac{Q\Lambda}{g^2} + \frac{\tau b^3}{\mu_{\min}\rho^2\kappa^6} \log(H+1) +1 \right] \right\}. $$
For every fixed $0<g<1/2$, there is also a logarithmic lower bound. Therefore, in this large-gap regime,
$$ \mathcal R^*(H,K,\tau,\mu,g) = \Theta_{K,\tau,\mu,g}(\log(H+1)). $$

At the deterministic endpoint,
$$ \frac1{16}\min\{H,K\} \leq \mathcal R^*(H,K,\tau,\mu,1/2) \leq \min\{H,Q\}. $$

For example, if the masses are equal and $K\geq\tau+2$, these results determine the horizon rate for every positive gap except the boundary:
$$ \begin{cases} \widetilde\Theta(H^{2/3}), & 0<g<(1-1/\tau)/2, \\[2mm] \Theta(\log(H+1)), & (1-1/\tau)/2<g<1/2, \\[2mm] \Theta(1), & g=1/2, \end{cases} $$
with $K,\tau,g$ fixed.

The cited [Ahmed–Ghasemi result](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01961v3) concerns product welfare, while the cited [Zhang–Vuong–Luo result](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/6a183ab792c56627d4c9dc45c1f5a616-Paper-Conference.pdf) uses absolute-reward feedback. No transfer of either theorem is needed below.

2. Objective properties and first-order conditions

Write
$$ s_{t,a}=2P_{t,a,a_t^\star}, \qquad u_t(\pi)=\sum_a s_{t,a}\pi(a). $$
Strict Condorcet optimality gives
$$ s_{t,a_t^\star}=1,\qquad 0\leq s_{t,a}<1 \quad(a\neq a_t^\star). $$
Under a positive gap, the stronger restriction is
$$ s_{t,a}\leq c:=1-2g \quad(a\neq a_t^\star). $$
Thus welfare and each round’s regret belong to $[0,1]$.

For positive coordinates, the weighted geometric mean
$$ G_\mu(u)=\prod_tu_t^{\mu_t} $$
satisfies
$$ D^2G_\mu(u)[v,v] = G_\mu(u) \left[ \left(\sum_t\mu_t\frac{v_t}{u_t}\right)^2 - \sum_t\mu_t\left(\frac{v_t}{u_t}\right)^2 \right] \leq0. $$
The inequality is weighted Cauchy–Schwarz, using $\sum_t\mu_t=1$. Continuity extends concavity to the nonnegative orthant. Hence $\Phi_\mu$ is continuous and concave on the simplex.

The uniform policy has every utility at least $1/K$. Compactness therefore gives an optimizer with positive utilities. We may equivalently maximize
$$ f(\pi)=\sum_t\mu_t\log u_t(\pi). $$

At any optimizer $\pi^\star$, differentiation in the feasible direction toward arm $a$ gives
$$ C_a:=\sum_t\mu_t\frac{s_{t,a}}{u_t(\pi^\star)} \leq1. $$
Moreover,
$$ \sum_a\pi^\star(a)C_a=1. $$
Consequently, every arm receiving positive optimal mass has $C_a=1$.

Taking $a=a_t^\star$ and keeping its nonnegative $t$-th summand gives
$$ u_t(\pi^\star)\geq\mu_t. $$
These arguments also apply to any nonnegative score matrix with one designated unit score in each row.

We use the usual adaptive relative-entropy chain rule and Pinsker’s inequality. Their application here is valid because, conditional on the recorded past and chosen policies, the pair-sampling kernel is the same in both environments; only the conditional response distributions can differ.

3. Linear regret without a gap promise

3.1 Choosing a substantial group

There is a nonempty proper subset $A$ of types whose mass $w$ satisfies
$$ \frac{\delta}{4}\leq w\leq\frac12. $$

Indeed, if $\max_t\mu_t>1/2$, take all types except one of maximum mass; then $w=\delta$. Otherwise, take a single mass in $[1/4,1/2]$ if one exists. If none exists, accumulate masses until their sum first reaches $1/4$; since each added mass is below $1/4$, the resulting sum is below $1/2$.

Let $r=|A|$.

3.2 Two nearly indistinguishable environments

Give every type outside $A$ winner $3$, with scores equal to one at arm $3$ and zero elsewhere.

For every type in $A$, use one of two matrices, differing only on $\{1,2\}$:
$$ P^{(0)}_{1,2}=\frac12+\varepsilon, \qquad P^{(1)}_{1,2}=\frac12-\varepsilon. $$
In both environments set
$$ P_{1,3}=1,\qquad P_{2,3}=\frac58, $$
and let arms $1,2$ beat every arm beyond $3$ deterministically. Complete all unspecified comparisons identically and reciprocally.

The selected types have winner $1$ in environment $0$ and winner $2$ in environment $1$. Their score vectors on arms $1,2,3$ are respectively
$$ (1,1-2\varepsilon,0), \qquad (1-2\varepsilon,1,3/4). $$
All extra-arm scores are zero.

For a policy, put
$$ x=\pi(1)+\pi(2),\qquad z=\pi(3). $$
The limiting welfare functions as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ are
$$ F_0(\pi)=x^wz^{1-w}, \qquad F_1(\pi)=(x+3z/4)^wz^{1-w}. $$
Since $x+z\leq1$, increasing $z$ to $1-x$ cannot decrease either expression. On that boundary, write
$$ f_0(x)=x^w(1-x)^{1-w}, $$
$$ f_1(x)=\left(\frac34+\frac{x}{4}\right)^w(1-x)^{1-w}. $$
Their optimal values are
$$ M_0=w^w(1-w)^{1-w}, \qquad M_1=(3/4)^w. $$
The first maximum occurs at $x=w$. For the second,
$$ -\frac{d}{dx}\log f_1(x) = \frac{1-w}{1-x}-\frac{w}{3+x} \geq1-\frac{4w}{3} \geq\frac13, $$
so its maximum occurs at $x=0$.

3.3 Separation of the required policies

Both $M_0$ and $M_1$ are at least $1/2$.

If $x\leq w/2$, monotonicity of $f_0$ on $[0,w]$ gives
$$ M_0-F_0(\pi)\geq M_0-f_0(w/2). $$
Furthermore,
$$ \log\frac{f_0(w)}{f_0(w/2)} = \int_{w/2}^w\frac{w-y}{y(1-y)}\,dy \geq\frac{w}{16}. $$
For the last inequality, restrict the integral to $[w/2,3w/4]$, where the integrand is at least $1/4$. Using $1-e^{-w/16}\geq w/32$,
$$ M_0-F_0(\pi)\geq\frac{w}{64}. $$

If $x>w/2$, the derivative bound for $f_1$ instead gives
$$ M_1-F_1(\pi) \geq M_1(1-e^{-w/6}) \geq\frac{w}{24}. $$
Therefore, for every policy,
$$ [M_0-F_0(\pi)]+[M_1-F_1(\pi)] \geq\frac{w}{64}. $$

Let $F_j^\varepsilon,V_j^\varepsilon$ be the actual welfare functions and optimal values. Since
$$ |a^w-b^w|\leq|a-b|^w \quad(0\leq a,b\leq1), $$
we have
$$ \sup_\pi|F_j^\varepsilon(\pi)-F_j(\pi)| \leq(2\varepsilon)^w. $$
Also $F_j^\varepsilon\leq F_j$, so
$$ V_j^\varepsilon-F_j^\varepsilon(\pi) \geq M_j-F_j(\pi)-(2\varepsilon)^w. $$
Choose $\varepsilon$ so small that
$$ (2\varepsilon)^w\leq\frac{w}{256}. $$
Then the sum of the two actual policy regrets is at least $w/128$.

3.4 Information bound

For $\varepsilon\leq1/4$, the relative entropy between the two Bernoulli laws on the differing pair is at most $32\varepsilon^2$. There are $r$ independently responding selected types. Thus the full interaction laws satisfy
$$ \operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_0\Vert\mathbb P_1) \leq32rH\varepsilon^2, $$
and their total variation distance is at most
$$ 4\varepsilon\sqrt{rH}. $$
Further choose
$$ \varepsilon\leq\frac{w}{1024\sqrt{rH}}. $$
The distance is then at most $w/256$.

The pointwise separation applies separately to both policies each round. Changing the law of a $[0,H]$-valued regret functional changes its expectation by at most $H$ times total variation. Hence
$$ \mathbb E_0R_H+\mathbb E_1R_H \geq \frac{wH}{128}-\frac{wH}{256} = \frac{wH}{256}. $$
One environment has expected regret at least $wH/512$, which is at least $\delta H/2048$.

The matrices are not restricted to supplied finite precision, so these arbitrarily small, horizon-dependent choices of $\varepsilon$ are admissible.

4. A general positive-gap algorithm

Assume $g>0$.

4.1 Identify winners

Compare either every unordered pair or run a sequential knockout tournament separately for each type, choosing the method with fewer comparison blocks. There are at most $Q$ blocks.

Use
$$ n_0=\left\lceil\frac{2\Lambda}{g^2}\right\rceil $$
fresh rounds per block, playing its two arms deterministically. Hoeffding’s inequality and a union bound show that all relevant empirical probabilities differ from their true values by less than $g/2$, except with probability at most $1/[4(H+1)]$.

On that event, every comparison sign is correct. A strict Condorcet winner survives a sequential tournament once encountered.

Operationally, denote the identified winner by $\widehat a_t$, and let
$$ W=\{\widehat a_t:t\},\qquad B=|W|\leq b. $$
Only on the successful identification event do we identify $\widehat a_t$ with $a_t^\star$.

Before beginning this stage, if $Qn_0>H$, use a fixed policy for the whole horizon.

4.2 Estimate winner columns

Let $M$ be the number of unordered pairs with at least one endpoint in $W$. Then
$$ M\leq KB,\qquad M\leq Q. $$
Set
$$ n= \left\lceil \left(\frac{B^2H^2\Lambda}{M^2}\right)^{1/3} \right\rceil. $$
After identification, check whether $Mn$ fits in the remaining horizon. If it does not, use a fixed policy for the remaining rounds. This check requires no retrospective change of earlier policies.

Otherwise, estimate each of these pairs using $n$ fresh repetitions. Choose a rational radius satisfying
$$ \sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{2n}} \leq r_n \leq2\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{2n}}. $$
Another conditional Hoeffding union bound gives simultaneous error at most $r_n$, except with probability at most $1/[4(H+1)]$.

Form optimistic scores
$$ U_{t,a}= \begin{cases} 1,&a=\widehat a_t,\\ \min\{1,\,2(\widehat P_{t,a,\widehat a_t}+r_n)\}, &a\neq\widehat a_t. \end{cases} $$
On the joint successful event,
$$ s_{t,a}\leq U_{t,a}\leq s_{t,a}+\epsilon, \qquad \epsilon=4r_n. $$

4.3 Optimize and smooth

Compute a policy $\widehat\pi$ within additive $\eta=1/H$ of optimal welfare for $U$. Let $q$ be uniform on $W$, and define
$$ \gamma=\min\{1,B\epsilon\}, \qquad \widetilde\pi=(1-\gamma)\widehat\pi+\gamma q. $$
Play $\widetilde\pi$ on both sides thereafter.

Every true type obtains utility at least $1/B$ from $q$. If $\gamma=B\epsilon<1$, then
$$ \begin{aligned} u_t(\widetilde\pi) &\geq (1-\gamma) \left(\sum_aU_{t,a}\widehat\pi(a)-\epsilon\right) +\frac{\gamma}{B}\\ &\geq (1-\gamma)\sum_aU_{t,a}\widehat\pi(a). \end{aligned} $$
Monotonicity and degree-one homogeneity of $G_\mu$ imply
$$ \Phi_\mu(\widetilde\pi) \geq (1-\gamma)\bigl(\Phi_\mu(\pi^\star)-\eta\bigr). $$
Thus exploitation regret per round is at most $B\epsilon+\eta$. If $\gamma=1$, the same bound follows from the unit regret bound.

Charging one per exploration round and $H$ on failure gives
$$ \mathbb ER_H \leq Qn_0+Mn+4BH r_n+O(1). $$
The choice of $n$ yields
$$ \mathbb ER_H \leq C\left[ \frac{Q\Lambda}{g^2} + (MB^2\Lambda)^{1/3}H^{2/3} +1 \right]. $$
Since $MB^2\leq Kb^3$, this proves the claimed upper bound.

The fallback branches satisfy the same bound: the identification branch has $H<Qn_0$; the later fallback has $H$ below the sum of identification and proposed estimation budgets. Ceiling costs are absorbed using $M\leq Q$.

4.4 Finite implementation and running time

One explicit optimizer enumerates the simplex grid with denominator
$$ J=\lceil4K^2H\rceil. $$
To verify its approximation guarantee, let $\pi_U^\star$ be an optimizer and put $d=2K/J$. Mix $\pi_U^\star$ with the uniform policy with weight $Kd$, then round to the grid with $\ell_1$ error at most $d$.

Each row has a unit score, so the uniform mixture adds at least $d$ utility to every row. Rounding removes at most $d$. The rounded policy therefore has welfare at least
$$ (1-Kd)\Phi_{\mu,U}(\pi_U^\star), $$
whose loss is at most $1/(2H)$.

Evaluate grid welfare to additive accuracy $1/(4H)$ and select the largest estimate. If any utility is zero, assign welfare zero directly. Otherwise, evaluate weighted logarithms and exponentiation. Positive rational utilities have logarithms of polynomially bounded bit length, so the required evaluations use polynomial bit time.

With
$$ N_{\mathrm{grid}}=\binom{J+K-1}{K-1}, $$
a conservative running-time bound is
$$ O\left( L+ \bigl(H\tau+N_{\mathrm{grid}}K\tau\bigr) \operatorname{poly}\left( L+\log(H+1)+\log K+\log\tau+\log(1/g) \right) \right). $$
This implementation is exponential in $K$.

5. A small-gap lower bound for every mass vector

We now prove the new lower-bound regime
$$ c=1-2g>\frac1{B_0}. $$

5.1 Baseline instance

Put $B=B_0$. Partition the supported types into any $B$ nonempty groups, with masses
$$ \nu_1,\ldots,\nu_B>0,\qquad \sum_j\nu_j=1. $$
There are enough arms for $B$ winner arms and two additional arms. Denote the additional arms by $C,D$.

Choose
$$ \frac1B<b_0<c. $$
For group $j$, give winner arm $j$ score one, other winner arms score zero, and both $C,D$ score $b_0$. Extra arms have score zero.

These scores are realized by setting each winner-column probability to half its score and completing all unspecified comparisons deterministically. All off-diagonal probabilities satisfy the promised gap.

For a policy write
$$ x=\pi(C)+\pi(D). $$
The grouped utilities and baseline welfare are
$$ u_j=\pi(j)+b_0x, \qquad F_0(\pi)=\prod_{j=1}^Bu_j^{\nu_j}. $$
Let $V_0$ be its maximum.

No optimizer uses extra arms: removing their mass and renormalizing the remaining policy strictly increases every positive utility. Also, all optimizers have the same utility vector $u^\star$. Indeed, $\sum_j\nu_j\log u_j$ is strictly concave in positive utility vectors, so two distinct optimal utility vectors would have a strictly better midpoint.

At an optimizer,
$$ \sum_ju_j^\star=1+(Bb_0-1)x^\star. $$
Since $Bb_0>1$, the optimal total compromise mass $x^\star$ is consequently unique.

Moreover, $x^\star>0$. If it were zero, the best winner-only policy would be $\pi(j)=\nu_j$. The directional derivative of logarithmic welfare toward $C$ would then equal
$$ b_0\sum_j\frac{\nu_j}{\nu_j}-1 = Bb_0-1>0, $$
contradicting optimality.

The first-order conditions give
$$ \frac{\nu_j}{u_j^\star}\leq1, \qquad b_0\sum_j\frac{\nu_j}{u_j^\star}=1. $$
Because $1/b_0<B$, at least one index $j_0$ satisfies
$$ \eta_0:=1-\frac{\nu_{j_0}}{u_{j_0}^\star}>0. $$
Thus arm $j_0$ has zero mass in every baseline optimizer.

Define positive constants
$$ a=V_0\nu_{j_0}, \qquad d=\frac{V_0\eta_0}{2}. $$

5.2 Perturb an unplayed winner comparison

Construct environments $-$ and $+$ by changing only the score of arm $C$ for group $j_0$:
$$ s_{j_0,C}^{\pm}=b_0\pm\varepsilon. $$
All other scores and comparisons remain fixed.

For
$$ 0<\varepsilon\leq \min\{b_0/2,(c-b_0)/2\}, $$
both instances lie in $\mathcal P_g$. They differ only on comparisons $\{j_0,C\}$ for the $r$ types in group $j_0$.

In the minus instance, moving mass from $C$ to $D$ cannot decrease welfare. The baseline optimum with all compromise mass on $D$ is unchanged. Hence
$$ V_-=V_0. $$
Its welfare tangent has coefficient gap at least $2d$ for winner arm $j_0$ and at least $a\varepsilon$ for $C$. Concavity therefore gives
$$ r_-(\pi):=V_--F_-(\pi) \geq d\pi(j_0)+a\varepsilon\pi(C). $$

For sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, the plus instance satisfies
$$ r_+(\pi):=V_+-F_+(\pi) \geq d\pi(j_0)+a\varepsilon\pi(D). $$
Here are the required details.

The perturbed objectives converge uniformly to $F_0$, with error at most $\varepsilon^{\nu_{j_0}}$. Compactness implies that every limit of perturbed optimizers is a baseline optimizer. Thus their utility vectors converge to $u^\star$, and their total compromise masses converge to $x^\star>0$.

For sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, the welfare-gradient gap for winner $j_0$ is therefore at least $d$. Every plus optimizer puts positive mass on $C$ and zero mass on $D$: shifting positive mass from $D$ to $C$ strictly increases its positive welfare.

Consequently, at a plus optimizer the coefficient of $C$ is $V_+$, while the coefficient gap for $D$ is
$$ V_+\frac{\nu_{j_0}\varepsilon}{u_{j_0}^+} \geq V_0\nu_{j_0}\varepsilon = a\varepsilon. $$
We used $V_+\geq V_0$ and $u_{j_0}^+\leq1$. All other coefficient gaps are nonnegative by optimality, proving the asserted tangent bound.

5.3 Near-optimal policies must use compromise arms

The compact set
$$ \{\pi:\pi(C)+\pi(D)\leq x^\star/2\} $$
contains no baseline optimizer. Hence
$$ \Delta_0 := V_0- \max_{\pi:\,\pi(C)+\pi(D)\leq x^\star/2}F_0(\pi) >0. $$
Since $F_-\leq F_0$,
$$ r_-(\pi)\geq\Delta_0 \quad\text{whenever }\pi(C)+\pi(D)\leq x^\star/2. $$
It follows, for every policy, that
$$ \pi(C)+\pi(D) \geq \frac{x^\star}{2} \left(1-\frac{r_-(\pi)}{\Delta_0}\right). $$
If the right side is positive, the preceding implication applies; if it is nonpositive, the inequality is automatic.

Choose a constant $\varepsilon_0>0$ sufficiently small that all preceding conclusions hold and $\varepsilon_0\leq\Delta_0$.

5.4 Information and regret

Let $S_j,S_C,S_D$ be the sums over rounds of the averages of the two policies’ respective arm masses.

For the informative pair, the differing Bernoulli probabilities are
$$ p_\pm=(b_0\pm\varepsilon)/2. $$
The elementary Bernoulli relative-entropy bound
$$ \operatorname{kl}(p_-\Vert p_+) \leq \frac{(p_--p_+)^2}{p_+(1-p_+)} $$
is at most $4\varepsilon^2/b_0$. The informative-pair probability is at most the sum of the two policies’ masses on $j_0$. Conditional independence across the $r$ types therefore gives the conservative bound
$$ \operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_-\Vert\mathbb P_+) \leq \frac{32r\varepsilon^2}{b_0}\mathbb E_-S_j \leq \frac{32r\varepsilon^2}{b_0d}\mathbb E_-R_H. $$

Take
$$ \varepsilon= \min\left\{ \varepsilon_0,\; \left(\frac{b_0d x^\star}{128rH}\right)^{1/3} \right\}. $$
If
$$ \mathbb E_-R_H\geq\frac{a x^\star\varepsilon H}{16}, $$
we already have the desired bound.

Otherwise,
$$ \operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_-\Vert\mathbb P_+) \leq\frac{a(x^\star)^2}{64} \leq\frac{(x^\star)^2}{64}. $$
Pinsker gives total variation less than $x^\star/8$.

The compromise-mass inequality and $\varepsilon\leq\Delta_0$ yield
$$ \mathbb E_-(S_C+S_D) \geq \frac{x^\star}{2} \left(H-\frac{\mathbb E_-R_H}{\Delta_0}\right) \geq\frac{x^\star H}{4}. $$
Since $0\leq S_D\leq H$,
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E_-R_H+\mathbb E_+R_H &\geq a\varepsilon\bigl(\mathbb E_-S_C+\mathbb E_+S_D\bigr)\\ &\geq a\varepsilon \left[ \mathbb E_-(S_C+S_D) -H\,\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb P_-,\mathbb P_+) \right]\\ &\geq \frac{a x^\star\varepsilon H}{8}. \end{aligned} $$
Thus in either case,
$$ \mathcal R^* \geq \frac{a x^\star H}{16} \min\left\{ \varepsilon_0,\; \left(\frac{b_0d x^\star}{128rH}\right)^{1/3} \right\}. $$
For fixed parameters this is $\Omega(H^{2/3})$, completing the small-gap horizon characterization.

6. Logarithmic regret for sufficiently large gaps

Assume
$$ 0<c=1-2g<\mu_{\min}. $$

6.1 Optimal policies reveal all winner comparisons

Let $W$ be the true winner set, with $B=|W|\leq b$, and let
$$ \nu_a=\sum_{t:a_t^\star=a}\mu_t \quad(a\in W). $$

At an optimizer, summing the first-order coefficients over winner arms gives
$$ \sum_{a\in W}C_a = \sum_t\mu_t \frac{\sum_{a\in W}s_{t,a}}{u_t(\pi^\star)} \geq \sum_t\frac{\mu_t}{u_t(\pi^\star)}. $$
Since each $C_a\leq1$,
$$ \sum_t\frac{\mu_t}{u_t(\pi^\star)}\leq B. $$
For a nonwinner arm $a$,
$$ C_a\leq cB<1. $$
It therefore cannot receive positive optimal mass.

For a winner arm $a$, every type having that winner satisfies
$$ u_t(\pi^\star)\leq c+(1-c)\pi^\star(a). $$
Thus
$$ 1\geq C_a \geq \frac{\nu_a}{c+(1-c)\pi^\star(a)}. $$
Consequently,
$$ \pi^\star(a) \geq \frac{\nu_a-c}{1-c} \geq \kappa. $$
Every optimizer is supported on $W$ and gives every winner arm mass at least $\kappa$.

The same floor holds for an optimizer of any estimated score matrix having designated winner scores one and all other scores in $[0,c]$.

6.2 Uniform strong concavity

Restrict policies to $W$. Choose one representative type for each winner arm. Its score row has a unit diagonal and off-diagonal entries at most $c$.

For any vector $v$, choose a coordinate of maximum absolute value. Its representative row satisfies
$$ |s_t\cdot v| \geq [1-c(B-1)]\|v\|_\infty \geq \frac{\rho}{\sqrt b}\|v\|_2. $$
Because every representative type has mass at least $\mu_{\min}$ and all utilities are at most one,
$$ -D^2f(\pi)[v,v] = \sum_t\mu_t\frac{(s_t\cdot v)^2}{u_t(\pi)^2} \geq \lambda\|v\|_2^2, $$
where
$$ \lambda=\frac{\mu_{\min}\rho^2}{b}. $$
Thus logarithmic welfare is $\lambda$-strongly concave wherever its utilities are positive.

6.3 Epoch algorithm and estimation error

Identify winners as in Section 4, with failure probability at most $1/[4(H+1)]$. Apply the same preliminary horizon check.

On the remaining rounds use epochs of lengths $1,2,4,\ldots$, truncating the last epoch. Throughout an epoch, play the same policy on both sides. Restrict policies to
$$ \mathcal D= \left\{\pi\in\Delta(W):\pi(a)\geq\kappa/2 \text{ for all }a\in W\right\}. $$
Start with the uniform policy on $W$.

After each epoch, estimate winner-column scores using only that epoch’s observations. Set the designated diagonal scores to one and clip other estimates to $[0,c]$. For the next epoch, choose a policy in $\mathcal D$ whose estimated logarithmic welfare is within $H^{-2}$ of its maximum.

Conditional on correct identification and the policy selected for an epoch of length $n$, each distinct winner pair has sampling probability at least $\kappa^2/2$. Its count $N$ is binomial. Conditional on $N\geq1$, a doubled Bernoulli sample mean has mean squared error at most $1/N$; clipping cannot increase that error. If $N=0$, use any score in $[0,c]$, incurring squared error at most one.

Using
$$ \mathbb E\frac1{N+1} = \frac{1-(1-p)^{n+1}}{(n+1)p}, \qquad \frac1{N\vee1}\leq\frac2{N+1}, $$
each score estimate has mean squared error at most
$$ \frac4{\kappa^2(n+1)}. $$
For the maximum score error $e$, summing over at most $\tau B$ relevant entries gives
$$ \mathbb Ee^2 \leq \frac{4\tau B}{\kappa^2(n+1)}. $$

6.4 Quadratic conversion from estimation error to regret

Let $\widehat f$ be the estimated logarithmic objective. On $\mathcal D$, both true and estimated utilities are at least $\kappa/2$. Therefore,
$$ \|\nabla f-\nabla\widehat f\|_2 \leq \frac{6\sqrt B}{\kappa^2}e. $$
Indeed, for each coordinate, subtract the fractions defining the two gradients and use utility error at most $e$, score error at most $e$, and the utility floor.

Let $\widehat\pi$ be the approximate estimated maximizer and put
$$ \Delta=f(\pi^\star)-f(\widehat\pi). $$
Writing $\zeta=\sup_{\mathcal D}\|\nabla f-\nabla\widehat f\|_2$,
$$ \Delta \leq H^{-2}+\zeta\|\pi^\star-\widehat\pi\|_2. $$
Strong concavity and optimality of $\pi^\star$ give
$$ \Delta\geq\frac{\lambda}{2} \|\pi^\star-\widehat\pi\|_2^2. $$
Combining these inequalities, and using
$$ \zeta\sqrt{2\Delta/\lambda} \leq \Delta/2+\zeta^2/\lambda, $$
yields
$$ \Delta \leq 2H^{-2}+\frac{2\zeta^2}{\lambda}. $$
Because true logarithmic welfare is nonpositive, exponentiation converts logarithmic loss to welfare loss without increasing it. Hence expected per-round regret in the following epoch is at most
$$ \frac{288\tau B^2} {\lambda\kappa^6(n+1)} + 2H^{-2}. $$
The following epoch has length at most $2n$, so its expected regret is bounded by
$$ \frac{576\tau B^2}{\lambda\kappa^6} $$
plus its negligible optimization contribution.

There are $O(\log(H+1))$ epochs. Identification failures contribute $O(1)$ expected regret. Substituting $\lambda$ proves the large-gap upper bound.

6.5 Running time

Use the transformed grid
$$ \pi(a)=\frac{\kappa}{2} + \left(1-\frac{B\kappa}{2}\right)q(a), $$
where $q$ ranges over a simplex grid with denominator
$$ J_{\mathrm{gap}}= \left\lceil\frac{16bH^2}{\kappa}\right\rceil. $$
Rounding has $\ell_1$ error at most $2B/J_{\mathrm{gap}}$. Since the estimated logarithmic gradient has coordinate magnitudes at most $2/\kappa$, the resulting objective loss is at most $1/(4H^2)$. Evaluation to accuracy $1/(8H^2)$ gives the required $H^{-2}$ optimization guarantee. All utilities here are positive.

With
$$ N_{\mathrm{gap}} = \binom{J_{\mathrm{gap}}+b-1}{b-1}, $$
a conservative running time is
$$ O\left( L+ \left[ H\tau+ N_{\mathrm{gap}}b\tau\log(H+1) \right] \operatorname{poly}\left( L+\log(H+1)+\log K+\log\tau +\log(1/g)+\log(1/\kappa) \right) \right). $$
Again, this finite implementation is exponential in the number of winner arms.

7. A logarithmic lower bound for every nonendpoint positive gap

Fix $0<g<1/2$. Consider two environments in which all types share the same matrix. Set
$$ P^{(+)}_{1,2}=\frac12+g, \qquad P^{(-)}_{1,2}=\frac12-g, $$
and let both arms $1,2$ beat all other arms deterministically. Complete remaining comparisons deterministically and identically.

The winners are $1$ and $2$, respectively. Since all types have identical utilities, welfare is linear:
$$ F_+(\pi)=\pi(1)+(1-2g)\pi(2), $$
$$ F_-(\pi)=(1-2g)\pi(1)+\pi(2). $$

Let $R$ be the larger expected regret in these two environments. Put
$$ k_g= 2g\log\frac{1/2+g}{1/2-g}, \qquad C_g=\frac{\tau k_g}{g}. $$
Only pair $\{1,2\}$ is informative. Under the plus environment, its probability is at most the sum of the policies’ masses on arm $2$, which is at most the current regret divided by $g$. Therefore,
$$ \operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_+\Vert\mathbb P_-) \leq C_gR. $$

Let $S_1$ be the horizon sum of average policy mass on arm $1$, and consider the event $E=\{S_1\geq H/2\}$. Pointwise,
$$ R_H^{(+)}\geq2g(H-S_1), \qquad R_H^{(-)}\geq2gS_1. $$
Markov’s inequality gives
$$ \mathbb P_+(E)\geq1-\frac{R}{gH}, \qquad \mathbb P_-(E)\leq\frac{R}{gH}. $$
If $R\leq gH/2$, data processing to this event and the binary entropy bound give
$$ C_gR \geq \frac12\log\frac{gH}{R}-\log2. $$

For $gH\geq256$, either $R>\sqrt{gH}$ or
$$ R\geq\frac{\log(gH)}{8C_g}. $$
Thus, with $g,\tau$ fixed,
$$ \mathcal R^*=\Omega_{g,\tau}(\log H). $$
Together with Section 6, this proves the logarithmic horizon rate there.

8. The deterministic endpoint

At $g=1/2$, every off-diagonal comparison is deterministic and every score row is the indicator of its winner.

All winners can be identified without error in at most $Q$ rounds. Define
$$ \nu_a=\sum_{t:a_t^\star=a}\mu_t. $$
Then
$$ \Phi_\mu(\pi)=\prod_{a:\nu_a>0}\pi(a)^{\nu_a}. $$
The optimizer is $\pi=\nu$, because
$$ \sum_{a:\nu_a>0}\nu_a\log\frac{\pi(a)}{\nu_a} \leq \log\sum_{a:\nu_a>0}\pi(a) \leq0. $$
Playing $\nu$ thereafter gives the upper bound $\min\{H,Q\}$. A conservative bit running time is
$$ O\left( L+(H\tau+Q\tau) \operatorname{poly}(L+\log(H+1)+\log K+\log\tau) \right). $$

For the lower bound, choose a common winner $J$ uniformly among the arms. Start from any fixed deterministic auxiliary tournament and force $J$ to beat every other arm; all types share the resulting matrix.

Couple interaction with this environment to interaction with the auxiliary tournament. Histories agree until a sampled pair contains $J$. In the auxiliary process, $J$ is independent of the transcript, so the probability of encountering it before round $h$ is at most $2(h-1)/K$.

The expected average policy mass on $J$ in round $h$ is consequently at most
$$ \frac1K+\frac{2(h-1)}K. $$
Optimal welfare is one, and policy welfare equals its mass on $J$. Each of the first $\min\{H,\lceil K/8\rceil\}$ rounds therefore has expected regret at least $1/2$. This proves
$$ \mathcal R^*\geq\frac1{16}\min\{H,K\}. $$
These deterministic instances also belong to every other gap class.

9. What remains unresolved

The proved horizon regimes leave genuine gaps. In particular, the small-gap argument requires
$$ 1-2g>\frac1{\min\{\tau,K-2\}}, $$
whereas the logarithmic algorithm requires
$$ 1-2g<\mu_{\min}. $$
The intervening regimes and their boundaries are not settled here.

The bounds also do not determine sharp joint dependence on the masses, support size, arm count, gap, and horizon. The explicit algorithms attain the proved upper bounds, but their exhaustive optimization implementations do not establish polynomial running time jointly in all supplied parameters.
