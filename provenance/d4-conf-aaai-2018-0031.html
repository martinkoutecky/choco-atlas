The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\beta,\alpha\), a finite type set \(T\) with \(p_t\in[\beta,1-\beta]\), a mass vector \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), and a finite network template specifying approved incidences and overlaps, compute the limiting GREEDYCAP mass flow on high-multiplicity realizations with \(C(N)\to\infty\) and \(C(N)=o(\sqrt{\log N})\), and decide whether its limiting correctness probability weakly dominates direct voting for every admissible \(\mu\) and is strictly better for some \(\mu\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

A type \(t\in T\) has competence \(p_t\in[\beta,1-\beta]\), where \(0<\beta<1/2\), and \(0<\alpha<1-2\beta\); a mass vector is \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\).
For each \(N\), a network template yields an approved graph \(G_N\) on \(n_t(N)\) voters of type \(t\), with \(n_t(N)/N\to\mu_t\); an approval \(x\to y\) requires \(p_y>p_x+\alpha\).
Votes are independent and correct with their type competence. GREEDYCAP repeatedly chooses a remaining voter with the most remaining approved in-neighbors, delegates up to \(C(N)-1\) such neighbors to that voter, and removes all selected voters, where \(C(N)\to\infty\) and \(C(N)=o(\sqrt{\log N})\).
Let \(P_{\mathrm{GC}}(\mu)\) and \(P_{\mathrm{D}}(\mu)\) be the resulting limiting correctness probabilities; the required predicate is \(P_{\mathrm{GC}}(\mu)\ge P_{\mathrm{D}}(\mu)\) for every admissible \(\mu\), with strict inequality for some \(\mu\).

Target

Is the continuous GREEDYCAP mass-flow-and-dominance problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results

For finite voter graphs, GREEDYCAP has constant positive gain on some arbitrarily large instances and asymptotically nonnegative worst-case gain under the stated bounds on competences and \(C\).
No result for this exact high-multiplicity type-mass formulation was verified.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

Status

The stated premises do not yet define a decision problem to which a polynomial-time or NP-hardness classification can be assigned. In particular, the limiting probabilities need not exist.

Below are rigorous counterexamples and a concentration lemma that narrows the dominance question for any formulation in which the limits do exist. These results do not establish either complexity classification.

1. Conventions

Write \(P_{\mathrm D,N}\) and \(P_{\mathrm{GC},N}\) for the finite-population correctness probabilities. We use weighted majority: a representative selected with \(k\) delegators casts a vote of weight \(k+1\). Any convention for an exact majority tie is allowed; the estimates below cover all such conventions.

All constructions have exactly \(N\) voters. GREEDYCAP makes its choices independently of the realized votes. When needed, “up to the cap” means selecting the minimum of the available number of in-neighbors and the cap minus one.

2. Proven: mass convergence does not ensure correctness limits

Fix any allowed \(\beta,\alpha\). Take two types with competences \(p_L=\beta\) and \(p_H=1-\beta\), and let every graph be edgeless. Such graphs satisfy the approval restriction vacuously.

For all sufficiently large \(N\), set
\( n_H(N)=\left\lfloor N/2+(-1)^N N^{3/4}\right\rfloor \)
and \(n_L(N)=N-n_H(N)\). Choose arbitrary valid counts for the finitely many smaller populations. Both type proportions converge to \(1/2\).

Let \(S_N\) be the number of correct votes, and put \(g=1-2\beta>0\). Independence gives
\( \mathbb E S_N-N/2=g\bigl(n_H(N)-N/2\bigr) \)
and
\( \operatorname{Var}(S_N)=N\beta(1-\beta)\le N/4. \)

For even \(N\), the expectation exceeds \(N/2\) by at least \(g(N^{3/4}-1)\). Chebyshev’s inequality therefore gives
\( \Pr(S_N\le N/2)\le N/\bigl(4g^2(N^{3/4}-1)^2\bigr)\longrightarrow0. \)
Hence \(P_{\mathrm D,N}\longrightarrow1\) along even populations.

For odd \(N\), the expectation lies below \(N/2\) by at least \(gN^{3/4}\). The same inequality gives
\( \Pr(S_N\ge N/2)\le N/(4g^2N^{3/2})\longrightarrow0. \)
Hence \(P_{\mathrm D,N}\longrightarrow0\) along odd populations.

Because the graphs are edgeless, GREEDYCAP leaves every voter with weight one. Consequently \(P_{\mathrm{GC},N}=P_{\mathrm D,N}\) for every \(N\), and neither limiting probability exists.

There is also a failure of determination by mass alone even when limits exist. Replacing \((-1)^N\) by the constant \(1\) gives both limits equal to \(1\); replacing it by the constant \(-1\) gives both limits equal to \(0\). The limiting mass and the edgeless graph rule remain identical.

Thus a critical-mass discretization rule, or additional information beyond limiting mass, is necessary.

3. Proven: a concentration bound for GREEDYCAP

Assume finitely many types. Fix any GREEDYCAP execution and let its final blocks be \(B_1,\ldots,B_r\), with representatives \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\) and weights \(w_j=|B_j|\). Then
\( \sum_jw_j=N \)
and \(1\le w_j\le C(N)\).

Let \(X_j\) indicate whether representative \(v_j\) votes correctly. The total correct weight is \(W_N=\sum_jw_jX_j\). Distinct representatives have independent votes, so
\( \operatorname{Var}(W_N) =\sum_jw_j^2p_{v_j}(1-p_{v_j}) \le\frac14\sum_jw_j^2 \le\frac{NC(N)}4. \)

Let \(d_N=N-r\) be the number of delegated voters. Every delegated voter \(u\) approves its representative, so \(p_{v_j}>p_u+\alpha\). It follows that
\( \mathbb E W_N-\sum_up_u =\sum_j\sum_{u\in B_j\setminus\{v_j\}}(p_{v_j}-p_u) \ge\alpha d_N. \)

Define the population-average competence \(m_N=N^{-1}\sum_up_u\). For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), if
\( m_N+\alpha d_N/N\ge1/2+\varepsilon, \)
then
\( \Pr(W_N\le N/2) \le C(N)/(4\varepsilon^2N)\longrightarrow0. \)
Here the last implication follows from the stated bound on \(C(N)\), which implies \(C(N)/N\longrightarrow0\).

In particular, write \(m(\mu)=\sum_t\mu_tp_t\). Since there are finitely many types, \(m_N\longrightarrow m(\mu)\).

  • If \(m(\mu)>1/2\), both direct voting and GREEDYCAP have correctness tending to \(1\). For GREEDYCAP, use the bound above with \(d_N\ge0\); for direct voting, use variance at most \(N/4\).
  • If \(m(\mu)<1/2\), direct-voting correctness tends to \(0\), again by Chebyshev’s inequality. Whenever the GREEDYCAP limit exists, weak dominance follows because a probability is nonnegative.

Therefore, any failure of weak dominance among masses with existing limits must lie on the hyperplane \(m(\mu)=1/2\). This conclusion does not establish existence of the GREEDYCAP limit away from that hyperplane.

4. Proven: unrestricted templates can prevent convergence away from the critical hyperplane

Use the same two competences, now with \(n_H(N)=\lfloor N/4\rfloor\). The limiting average competence is
\( m(\mu)=\beta+(1-2\beta)/4=1/4+\beta/2<1/2. \)
Thus \(P_{\mathrm D,N}\longrightarrow0\).

For even \(N\), use the complete directed bipartite graph from low-type voters to high-type voters. Every edge is approved because \(1-2\beta>\alpha\). For odd \(N\), use an edgeless graph. This is a fixed, explicit template rule permitted by the stated restrictions.

On even populations, let \(c_N=\lfloor C(N)\rfloor\). For sufficiently large \(N\), we have \(c_N\ge2\). While both types remain, each high-type voter has positive in-degree and each low-type voter has zero in-degree. GREEDYCAP therefore selects a high-type representative and delegates \(\min\{c_N-1,\text{remaining low voters}\}\) voters to it.

The number of high-type representatives needed to absorb all low-type voters is
\( \left\lceil n_L(N)/(c_N-1)\right\rceil=o(N), \)
whereas \(n_H(N)/N\longrightarrow1/4\). Hence all low-type voters are absorbed before the high-type voters run out. Every final representative consequently has competence \(1-\beta\).

Thus \(\mathbb E W_N=(1-\beta)N\), and the variance bound proves \(P_{\mathrm{GC},N}\longrightarrow1\) along even populations. Along odd populations, the graph is edgeless, so \(P_{\mathrm{GC},N}=P_{\mathrm D,N}\longrightarrow0\).

This counterexample uses ordinary rounding of fixed proportions. A restriction on templates is therefore needed in addition to a discretization rule.

5. What remains open

A complexity classification requires a specified class of finitely encoded instances: the template representation, numerical representation, admissible masses, and GREEDYCAP tie-breaking and neighbor-selection rules. The formulation must also guarantee the relevant limits or explicitly replace them with another asymptotic predicate.

The counterexamples establish defects in the present definition. The concentration lemma advances any repaired formulation by localizing possible failures of weak dominance to critical masses. Neither result decides the complexity of a suitably specified continuous mass-flow problem.
