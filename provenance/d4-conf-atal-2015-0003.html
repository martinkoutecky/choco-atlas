Detecting Possible Manipulators in Elections (d4-conf-atal-2015-0003)

NEW RESULT — discrete: hard → continuous: hard · adversarial review: proved

Answer: \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete

The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 4.7 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(C\), \(T=\mathcal{L}(C)\), rational \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{T}\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational \(\rho\le\mu\), and fixed STV tie-breaking, let \(x=\operatorname{STV}(\mu)\). Decide whether there exist \(y\ne x\) and rational \(\eta\ge0\) with \(\sum_t\eta_t=\sum_t\rho_t\), \(\eta_t>0\Rightarrow x\succ_t y\), and \(\operatorname{STV}(\mu-\rho+\eta)=y\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) have \(m\ge 2\) candidates and let \(T=\mathcal L(C)\) be the strict complete rankings.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not \(m!\). The reported society \(\mu\) satisfies \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\).
For each listed type, the input specifies a binary rational suspect mass \(0\le\rho_t\le\mu_t\); unlisted coordinates are zero. Put \(s=\sum_{t\in T}\rho_t\).
Use single-winner STV: each ranking contributes its entire mass to its highest remaining candidate; eliminate one candidate of minimum total mass each round until one remains. A fixed total order on candidate labels selects the first tied candidate for elimination, identically in every election.
Put \(x=\operatorname{STV}(\mu)\). The decision problem asks whether there exist \(y\in C\setminus\{x\}\) and \(\eta\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{T}\) such that \(\sum_t\eta_t=s\), \(\eta_t>0\Rightarrow x\succ_t y\), and \(\operatorname{STV}(\mu-\rho+\eta)=y\). Replacement mass may be split arbitrarily among all rankings, including previously absent types.
State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the total input bit length \(L\).

Is this continuous STV coalitional possible-manipulators decision problem in \(\mathsf P\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard?

For finite unweighted electorates, the corresponding problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete even with one specified suspect voter ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.2367)).
No result settling exactly this divisible-mass version was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the suspect-mass domain explicit as 0 <= rho_t <= mu_t, since rho represents a subset of the reported society.
  • Specified a fixed total order that selects the first tied candidate for elimination, resolving the unspecified STV tie-breaking convention.
  • Required at least two candidates because the decision problem seeks a winner distinct from the current winner.
  • Specified that replacement mass can split among all strict complete rankings, including types absent from the input support.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length and represented rho on the listed support; zero suspect mass remains allowed, as the discrete subset definition does not exclude an empty coalition.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete under the stated sparse-support encoding.

The hardness proof below handles arbitrary splitting among previously absent rankings. Its key property is stronger than needed: in the constructed election, every replacement profile of the prescribed mass has winner either \(x\) or \(y\), and \(y\) can win only if the source exact-cover instance is positive.

The construction adapts the selector-and-cover-check structure behind the discrete hardness result in [Dey, Misra, and Narahari, Theorem 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.2367). The numerical parameters and the fractional soundness argument are given explicitly below.

1. Polynomial-time feasibility for a fixed elimination order

Write \(\nu=\mu-\rho\). Fix a proposed elimination order \(e_1,\ldots,e_m\), with proposed winner \(y=e_m\ne x\), and let \(x=e_h\).

For round \(i\), put \(R_i=\{e_i,\ldots,e_m\}\). Let \(b_i(c)\) be the tally contributed to \(c\in R_i\) by \(\nu\). These constants can be computed from the \(\tau\) listed rankings in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) bit operations.

Introduce replacement-tally variables \(z_i(c)\). Impose:

  • \(z_i(c)\ge 0\) and \(\sum_{c\in R_i}z_i(c)=s\);
  • \(z_{i+1}(c)\ge z_i(c)\) for every surviving \(c\in R_{i+1}\);
  • \(z_i(y)=0\) for \(i\le h\).

The last condition is necessary because a replacement ranking with \(x\succ y\) cannot contribute to \(y\) while \(x\) remains.

Let \(\triangleleft\) denote the fixed priority order for elimination. The required tally comparisons are

\[ \]
\(b_i(e_i)+z_i(e_i)\le b_i(c)+z_i(c)\qquad(c\in R_i\setminus\{e_i\}).\)

The comparison must be strict whenever \(c\triangleleft e_i\), since otherwise the tie-breaking rule would eliminate \(c\).

These constraints are also sufficient to realize the replacement tallies by complete rankings. Indeed, define

\(f_{i,c}=z_{i+1}(c)-z_i(c)\ge 0\qquad(c\in R_{i+1}).\)

The total-mass equations give

\(\sum_{c\in R_{i+1}}f_{i,c}=z_i(e_i).\)

Thus the initial allocations \(z_1(c)\), together with transfers \(f_{i,c}\), form a flow on the acyclic graph whose candidate arcs go from earlier to later positions in the elimination order. Decompose this flow into source-to-\(y\) paths.

For each path, rank its candidates in path order. If the path contains \(x\), it already ranks \(x\) above \(y\). If it omits \(x\), its predecessor of \(y\) must be eliminated after \(x\): transfers into \(y\) before \(x\)'s elimination are zero. Insert \(x\) immediately before \(y\). This insertion does not change the path's voting behavior, because \(x\) has already been eliminated when that path reaches \(y\). Append every unused candidate after \(y\).

Assign each resulting complete ranking its path-flow mass. This realizes all \(z_i(c)\) and satisfies \(x\succ y\).

To implement the strict comparisons, introduce \(0\le\epsilon\le 1\), replace each strict comparison by

\(b_i(e_i)+z_i(e_i)+\epsilon\le b_i(c)+z_i(c),\)

and maximize \(\epsilon\). The prescribed order is feasible exactly when this rational LP is feasible and its optimum is positive. A genuine election supplies a positive minimum over its finitely many required strict gaps; conversely, positive \(\epsilon\) enforces every required strict comparison.

There are \(O(m^2)\) variables and constraints, with polynomial encoding length. Exact rational linear programming therefore decides fixed-order feasibility in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time. Its feasible region is bounded, so positive feasibility also has a polynomial-bit rational witness.

Consequently:

  • guessing an elimination order and solving this LP proves membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\);
  • enumerating all orders gives a deterministic algorithm with running time \(m!\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\).

The flow decomposition uses \(O(m^2)\) paths, so a positive instance also admits a replacement profile supported on \(O(m^2)\) complete rankings with polynomial-bit masses.

2. Source problem and construction

Reduce from Exact Cover by 3-Sets. Let the universe be \(\{1,\ldots,3q\}\), and let its three-element subsets be \(S_1,\ldots,S_k\). An exact cover consists of \(q\) of these subsets covering the universe.

We may assume \(q\ge 1\) and \(k\ge q\). Instances with \(k<q\) are immediately negative and can be mapped to any fixed negative target instance.

Work first with unnormalized weights. Set

\(\delta=\frac14,\qquad H=20k,\qquad B_i=10k+4i.\)

The candidates are

\(C=\{x,y,d_0,d_1,\ldots,d_{3q}\}\cup\{a_i,\bar a_i,b_i,\bar b_i,g_i:1\le i\le k\}.\)

Thus \(m=5k+3q+3\).

The honest profile \(v\) consists of the following weighted rankings. Every displayed prefix is completed by appending the remaining candidates in a fixed order.

| Weight | Ranking prefix | Indices |
|---|---|---|
| \(H-1\) | \(x\succ y\) | |
| \(H\) | \(y\succ x\) | |
| \(H-2(k-q)\) | \(d_0\succ x\succ y\) | |
| \(H-2\) | \(d_j\succ x\succ y\) | \(1\le j\le 3q\) |
| \(H\) | \(g_i\succ x\succ y\) | \(1\le i\le k\) |
| \(B_i-5\) | \(b_i\succ\bar b_i\succ x\succ y\) | \(1\le i\le k\) |
| \(2\) | \(b_i\succ d_j\succ x\succ y\) | \(j\in S_i\) |
| \(B_i-1\) | \(\bar b_i\succ b_i\succ x\succ y\) | \(1\le i\le k\) |
| \(2\) | \(\bar b_i\succ d_0\succ x\succ y\) | \(1\le i\le k\) |
| \(B_i-3\) | \(a_i\succ g_i\succ x\succ y\) | \(1\le i\le k\) |
| \(1\) | \(a_i\succ b_i\succ g_i\succ x\succ y\) | \(1\le i\le k\) |
| \(2\) | \(a_i\succ\bar a_i\succ g_i\succ x\succ y\) | \(1\le i\le k\) |
| \(B_i-3\) | \(\bar a_i\succ g_i\succ x\succ y\) | \(1\le i\le k\) |
| \(1\) | \(\bar a_i\succ\bar b_i\succ g_i\succ x\succ y\) | \(1\le i\le k\) |
| \(2\) | \(\bar a_i\succ a_i\succ g_i\succ x\succ y\) | \(1\le i\le k\) |

All weights are positive.

We will analyze \(v+\zeta\) for every nonnegative replacement profile \(\zeta\) of total weight \(\delta\), without imposing any preference restriction. At every round, its contribution to any candidate lies between \(0\) and \(\delta\).

3. The selector phases survive arbitrary splitting

For each \(i\), the initial honest tallies are

\(v(a_i)=v(\bar a_i)=B_i,\qquad v(b_i)=v(\bar b_i)=B_i+1.\)

We prove that the first \(3k\) eliminations occur in \(k\) consecutive stages. Stage \(i\) eliminates:

  • one of \(a_i,\bar a_i\);
  • one of \(b_i,\bar b_i\);
  • the remaining member of \(a_i,\bar a_i\).

At the start of stage \(i\), the pair \(a_i,\bar a_i\) has honest tally \(B_i\), whereas its \(b\)-pair has honest tally \(B_i+1\). Replacement weight cannot bridge this gap, since \(\delta<1\).

Untouched later selector pairs have honest tally at least \(B_i+4\). Every guard initially has honest tally at least \(H-2k=18k\), and guards only gain honest weight during these stages. The candidates \(x,y,g_j\), and earlier surviving \(b\)-candidates also have larger tallies. The relevant bound is

\(B_i+2+\delta\le 14k+2+\delta<18k.\)

Therefore the next elimination is one of \(a_i,\bar a_i\), regardless of replacement splitting or tie-breaking.

If \(a_i\) is eliminated, it transfers honest weight \(2\) to \(\bar a_i\) and \(1\) to \(b_i\). Hence \(\bar b_i\), with honest tally \(B_i+1\), is uniquely the next minimum: both \(\bar a_i\) and \(b_i\) have honest tally \(B_i+2\).

If \(\bar a_i\) is eliminated, the symmetric calculation makes \(b_i\) uniquely the next minimum.

After that \(b\)-candidate is eliminated, its counterpart has honest tally either \(2B_i-3\) or \(2B_i+1\), both exceeding \(H\). The remaining \(a\)-candidate has honest tally \(B_i+2\), so it is eliminated before the next stage begins, because

\(B_i+2+\delta<B_i+4.\)

This proves the stage structure inductively.

Define the selected index set

\(J=\{i:b_i\text{ is eliminated during stage }i\}.\)

Each stage therefore makes a discrete set choice, even though the replacement weight is arbitrarily divisible.

4. Exact tallies after the selector phases

After the first \(3k\) eliminations, both \(a\)-candidates of every stage are gone, and precisely one \(b\)-candidate per stage remains.

Let

\(c_j=|\{i\in J:j\in S_i\}|.\)

The honest tallies at this checkpoint are:

| Remaining candidate | Honest tally |
|---|---|
| \(x\) | \(H-1\) |
| \(y\) | \(H\) |
| \(d_j\), \(1\le j\le 3q\) | \(H-2+2c_j\) |
| \(d_0\) | \(H+2(q-|J|)\) |
| \(g_i\) | \(H+2B_i-1\) |
| \(\bar b_i\), if \(i\in J\) | \(2B_i-3\) |
| \(b_i\), if \(i\notin J\) | \(2B_i+1\) |

For the guards, each eliminated \(b_i\) sends weight \(2\) to every element of \(S_i\), whereas each eliminated \(\bar b_i\) sends weight \(2\) to \(d_0\).

For a surviving \(b\)-candidate, add its initial \(B_i+1\), the weight \(1\) from the first eliminated \(a\)-candidate, and the eliminated counterpart's bulk weight, respectively \(B_i-5\) or \(B_i-1\).

For \(g_i\), the two \(a\)-candidates contribute their two bulk blocks, their two mutual-transfer blocks, and the weight \(1\) whose designated \(b\)-candidate has already disappeared. Their total contribution is

\(2(B_i-3)+4+1=2B_i-1.\)

These calculations also show that no honest weight has yet reached \(x\) or \(y\).

The useful bounds are:

  • every guard's honest tally lies between \(18k\) and \(22k\);
  • every surviving \(b\)-candidate's honest tally lies between \(20k+5\) and \(28k+1\);
  • every \(g_i\)'s honest tally lies between \(40k+7\) and \(48k-1\).

A guard's honest tally is at least \(H\) exactly when its associated condition holds. Thus all guards have honest tally at least \(H\) if and only if

\(|J|\le q\quad\text{and}\quad c_j\ge 1\text{ for every }j.\)

Because each selected set has three elements, covering all \(3q\) elements requires \(|J|\ge q\). Consequently these conditions hold exactly when \(J\) is an exact cover.

If \(J\) is not an exact cover, at least one guard has honest tally at most \(H-2\).

5. Winner determination for arbitrary replacement profiles

Failed cover check forces \(x\) to win

Suppose \(J\) is not an exact cover.

A deficient guard has total tally at most \(H-2+\delta<H-1\), while \(x\)'s total tally is at least \(H-1\). The \(b\)- and \(g\)-candidates exceed \(H+\delta\). Hence the next eliminated candidate is a guard.

Its entire honest tally transfers to \(x\), giving \(x\) honest tally at least

\((H-1)+18k=38k-1.\)

Meanwhile \(y\)'s total tally is at most \(H+\delta\). Every surviving \(b\)- or \(g\)-candidate has honest tally strictly above \(H+\delta\). Therefore, until \(y\) is eliminated, only other guards can be eliminated first; each such elimination increases \(x\)'s honest tally. In particular, \(x\) cannot be eliminated before \(y\).

When \(y\) is eliminated, its honest weight \(H\) transfers to \(x\), giving \(x\) honest tally at least

\(58k-1.\)

Every other remaining candidate has total tally at most \(48k-1+\delta\), which is strictly smaller.

At this point every other remaining honest ballot transfers next to \(x\): both \(a\)-candidates and the other member of each \(b\)-pair are gone. Thus no other candidate can acquire further honest weight while \(x\) remains. Its replacement contribution is always at most \(\delta\). The strict dominance persists, and \(x\) wins.

This argument did not assume any restriction on the replacement rankings.

A successful cover check forces \(y\) to win

Suppose \(J\) is an exact cover.

Every guard has honest tally at least \(H\), whereas

\(\operatorname{tally}(x)\le H-1+\delta<H.\)

The \(b\)- and \(g\)-candidates have still larger honest tallies. Thus \(x\) is uniquely the next eliminated candidate.

Its honest weight \(H-1\) transfers to \(y\), giving \(y\) honest tally at least \(2H-1=40k-1\).

The next eliminated candidate is a guard or a surviving \(b\)-candidate. Indeed, a guard has total tally at most \(22k+\delta\), below both \(y\)'s tally and every \(g\)-candidate's tally. Every guard and surviving \(b\)-candidate has honest tally at least \(H\), and its honest ballots now transfer directly to \(y\).

Therefore, after this next elimination, \(y\)'s honest tally is at least

\(3H-1=60k-1>48k-1+\delta.\)

As in the preceding case, every remaining honest ballot transfers next to \(y\); other candidates cannot acquire further honest weight. The dominance persists, and \(y\) wins.

We have proved, for every replacement profile of weight \(\delta\),

\(\operatorname{STV}(v+\zeta)=y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad J\text{ is an exact cover},\)

and otherwise the winner is \(x\). These conclusions hold for any fixed candidate-label tie-breaking order.

6. Reported profile, completeness, and normalization

Define the reported suspect ranking by the prefix

\(r_0:\bar a_1\succ\bar a_2\succ\cdots\succ\bar a_k\succ x\succ y.\)

Give this ranking weight \(\delta\).

During stage \(i\), this ballot protects \(\bar a_i\), so \(a_i\) is eliminated first and \(\bar b_i\) second. After \(\bar a_i\) is eliminated, the ballot moves to \(\bar a_{i+1}\). Thus its selected set is \(J=\varnothing\). Since \(q\ge 1\), the cover check fails, and the reported winner is \(x\).

Now suppose an exact cover \(J^\ast\) exists. Define

\(u_i=a_i\text{ if }i\in J^\ast,\qquad u_i=\bar a_i\text{ otherwise},\)

and use the replacement ranking with prefix

\(r^\ast:u_1\succ u_2\succ\cdots\succ u_k\succ g_1\succ x\succ y.\)

Give it weight \(\delta\). In each stage it protects \(u_i\), selecting precisely the indices in \(J^\ast\). Hence \(y\) wins. The ranking explicitly satisfies \(x\succ y\), as required.

Conversely, if the X3C instance has no exact cover, Section 5 shows that every replacement profile of weight \(\delta\) elects \(x\). This includes profiles with arbitrary support and profiles satisfying the preference restriction for any proposed alternative winner.

Finally, put

\(N=\sum_t v_t+\delta,\qquad \mu=\frac{v+\delta e_{r_0}}{N},\qquad \rho=\frac{\delta e_{r_0}}{N}.\)

Here \(e_{r_0}\) denotes unit mass on ranking \(r_0\). These are valid rational input profiles, with \(\rho\le\mu\), total reported mass \(1\), and suspect mass \(s=\delta/N\).

Multiplying all weights by the same positive number preserves every tally comparison and tie. Therefore normalized replacement profiles \(\eta\) correspond exactly to unnormalized profiles \(\zeta=N\eta\), and the reduction's equivalence is unchanged.

7. Encoding and running times

The reduction has

\(m=5k+3q+3,\qquad \tau\le 13k+3q+4.\)

Its unnormalized weights are integers of magnitude \(O(k)\), except for \(\delta=1/4\). After normalization, numerators and denominators have \(O(\log(k+q))\) bits. Writing every complete ranking gives total encoding length

\(L=O((k+q)^2\log(k+q)).\)

The reduction therefore takes polynomial time in the source-instance length.

For arbitrary target instances, fixed-order feasibility takes \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time, and exhaustive order enumeration takes \(m!\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time. The polynomial-time fixed-order verifier establishes membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\); the reduction establishes \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness.

Thus the divisible-mass problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete, including when the reported suspect mass is supported on just one ranking.
