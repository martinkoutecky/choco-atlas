The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of rational processing times \(P\) and rational masses \(\mu_p\ge 0\), with \(\sum_{p\in P}\mu_p=1\) in each of two departments, decide whether a proportionally fair Pareto schedule exists and construct one if it does. A schedule is a measurable assignment \(q_{i,p}(s)\) on \(s\in[0,2]\), with \(q_{i,p}(s)\ge 0\), \(\sum_{i\in\{A,B\},p\in P}q_{i,p}(s)=1\), and \(\int_0^2 q_{i,p}(s)\,ds=\mu_p\); its clock is \(C(s)=\int_0^s\sum_{i,p}p\,q_{i,p}(r)\,dr\), and department \(i\)'s cost is \(F_i(q)=\int_0^2\sum_p q_{i,p}(s)C(s)\,ds\). Define Pareto schedules, utilities, and proportional fairness from \((F_A,F_B)\) as in the paper.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P\subset\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\) be nonempty, with \(m=|P|\), and let \(\mu_p>0\) be binary rational masses satisfying \(\sum_{p\in P}\mu_p=1\). Departments \(A\) and \(B\) have identical distributions.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here types are department–processing-time pairs, so \(\tau=2m\); this is support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
A feasible schedule consists of measurable functions \(q_{i,p}:[0,2]\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}\), with \(\sum_{i,p}q_{i,p}(s)=1\) almost everywhere and \(\int_0^2q_{i,p}(s)\,ds=\mu_p\) for every \(i,p\).
Define \(C_q(s)=\int_0^s\sum_{i,p}p\,q_{i,p}(r)\,dr\) and \(F_i(q)=\int_0^2\sum_pq_{i,p}(s)C_q(s)\,ds\).
Let \(\Sigma_P\) contain the feasible schedules for which no feasible schedule weakly decreases both costs and strictly decreases at least one. Set \(F_i^{\infty}=\max_{r\in\Sigma_P}F_i(r)\) and \(u_i(q)=F_i^{\infty}-F_i(q)\).
A proportionally fair Pareto schedule is \(q^*\in\Sigma_P\) with \(u_A(q^*),u_B(q^*)>0\) such that \(\sum_{i\in\{A,B\}}(u_i(r)-u_i(q^*))/u_i(q^*)\le0\) for every \(r\in\Sigma_P\).

Is deciding whether a proportionally fair Pareto schedule exists solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? Can one be constructed exactly in polynomial time whenever it exists?

For finite, indivisible, nonpreemptive jobs, deciding existence is NP-hard even when the departments have identical processing-time multisets; finding such a schedule when one exists is also NP-hard ([Agnetis et al.](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/112370/7/WRAP-price-fairness-two-agent-single-machine-scheduling-problems-Chen-2018.pdf)).
No result settling this exact measurable mass model was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted processing times to strictly positive rationals, matching positive job lengths and excluding zero-length degeneracy.
  • Discarded zero-mass processing times, making P the nonempty positive support; retained identical masses in both departments and unit total mass per department.
  • Counted department–processing-time pairs as society types, so τ=2m, where m is the number of supported processing times.
  • Interpreted pointwise feasibility almost everywhere, as appropriate for measurable schedules and integral costs.
  • Required strictly positive utilities for proportional fairness, as required by its denominators and the discrete definition.

Result

Every instance in the stated measurable mass model admits a proportionally fair Pareto schedule. One can construct it exactly in polynomial time: order the processing times increasingly and assign half of the rate within each processing-time block to each department.

The proof below also establishes that the maxima defining the utilities exist.

1. A lower bound for internal scheduling costs

Write \(P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_m\}\), where \(p_1<\cdots<p_m\), and let \(\mu_j=\mu_{p_j}\). Define

\(M=\sum_{j=1}^m p_j\mu_j\),

\(a=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^m p_j\mu_j^2+\sum_{j<k}p_j\mu_j\mu_k\).

We first prove an inequality for nonnegative integrable functions \(h_j\) on \([0,2]\), with masses \(w_j=\int_0^2h_j(s)\,ds\). Set

\(J(h)=\int_0^2\left(\sum_kh_k(s)\right)\left(\int_0^s\sum_jp_jh_j(t)\,dt\right)\,ds\).

For each ordered pair, define

\(T_{jk}=\int_0^2h_k(s)\int_0^sh_j(t)\,dt\,ds\).

Tonelli’s theorem applies because the integrands are nonnegative. Splitting the square \([0,2]^2\) into its two strict triangles, whose omitted diagonal has measure zero, gives

\(T_{jj}=\frac12w_j^2,\qquad T_{jk}+T_{kj}=w_jw_k\quad(j\ne k)\).

Consequently,

\(J(h)=\frac12\sum_jp_jw_j^2+\sum_{j<k}\bigl(p_jT_{jk}+p_kT_{kj}\bigr)\)

\(=\frac12\sum_jp_jw_j^2+\sum_{j<k}\left(p_jw_jw_k+(p_k-p_j)T_{kj}\right)\)

\(\geq\frac12\sum_jp_jw_j^2+\sum_{j<k}p_jw_jw_k.\)

Equality holds whenever the processing-time types occur in increasing order: then \(T_{kj}=0\) for every \(j<k\).

For a feasible schedule, put \(q_i=(q_{i,p_j})_{j=1}^m\). Its department costs decompose as

\(F_i(q)=J(q_i)+X_{\bar i,i}(q)\),

where

\(X_{\bar i,i}(q)=\sum_{j,k}p_j\int_0^2q_{i,p_k}(s)\int_0^sq_{\bar i,p_j}(t)\,dt\,ds\geq0.\)

Each department has type masses \(\mu_j\), so the inequality just proved yields

\(F_A(q)\geq a,\qquad F_B(q)\geq a.\)

Moreover, \(h_j=q_{A,p_j}+q_{B,p_j}\) has mass \(2\mu_j\), and

\(F_A(q)+F_B(q)=J(h)\geq4a.\)

These bounds hold for every feasible measurable schedule.

2. The Pareto reference values are exactly \(a+M\)

Consider the schedule that processes all of department \(A\) on \([0,1]\), in increasing processing-time order, and then all of department \(B\) on \([1,2]\), in the same order. Its cost vector is

\((F_A,F_B)=(a,a+M)\).

Indeed, each department’s internal cost is \(a\), and every unit of \(B\)-mass encounters the preceding workload \(M\) of department \(A\).

We show that this schedule is Pareto.

Suppose a feasible schedule \(q\) satisfies \(F_A(q)\leq a\). The preceding lower bound forces \(F_A(q)=a\), and its cost decomposition forces \(X_{B,A}(q)=0\). Every coefficient in this cross term is strictly positive. Hence, for every \(j,k\),

\(\int_0^2q_{A,p_k}(s)\int_0^sq_{B,p_j}(t)\,dt\,ds=0.\)

The same triangle identity used above, now for these two department–type pairs, implies

\(\int_0^2q_{B,p_j}(s)\int_0^sq_{A,p_k}(t)\,dt\,ds=\mu_j\mu_k.\)

Thus the cross contribution from \(A\) to \(B\) is exactly

\(X_{A,B}(q)=\sum_{j,k}p_k\mu_j\mu_k=M.\)

Since \(J(q_B)\geq a\), we obtain \(F_B(q)\geq a+M\). Therefore no feasible schedule dominates \((a,a+M)\). Interchanging the departments proves that \((a+M,a)\) is also a Pareto cost vector.

Next, any feasible schedule has \(F_B\geq a\). Therefore a schedule with \(F_A>a+M\) is dominated by the Pareto schedule having cost vector \((a+M,a)\). Such a schedule cannot belong to \(\Sigma_P\). Similarly, every Pareto schedule satisfies \(F_B\leq a+M\).

Both bounds are attained by the two schedules above. Hence the maxima in the question exist, and

\(F_A^\infty=F_B^\infty=a+M.\)

This argument does not require any compactness assumption about the Pareto set.

3. Exact construction and proof of proportional fairness

Define rational breakpoints

\(b_0=0,\qquad b_j=2\sum_{\ell=1}^j\mu_\ell\quad(1\leq j\leq m).\)

Because the masses sum to one, \(b_m=2\). On every interval \([b_{j-1},b_j)\), set

\(q^*_{A,p_j}(s)=q^*_{B,p_j}(s)=\frac12\),

and set every other rate to zero. Endpoint values may be assigned arbitrarily, since they do not affect feasibility or costs.

The rates sum to one almost everywhere, and each department receives mass

\(\int_{b_{j-1}}^{b_j}\frac12\,ds=\mu_j\)

of type \(p_j\). Thus \(q^*\) is feasible.

Its aggregate processing-time types occur in increasing order. Equality in the bound from Section 1 therefore gives

\(F_A(q^*)+F_B(q^*)=4a.\)

The department rates agree pointwise, so their costs agree. Consequently,

\(F_A(q^*)=F_B(q^*)=2a.\)

This schedule is Pareto: any schedule weakly reducing both costs and strictly reducing one would have total cost strictly below \(4a\), contradicting the universal lower bound.

Its two utilities are

\(u_A(q^*)=u_B(q^*)=M-a.\)

They are strictly positive, because

\(M-a=\frac12\sum_jp_j\mu_j^2+\sum_{j<k}p_k\mu_j\mu_k>0.\)

Here the first sum is strictly positive, since the support is nonempty and all processing times and masses are positive.

For any \(r\in\Sigma_P\), the required proportional-fairness expression is

\(\sum_{i\in\{A,B\}}\frac{u_i(r)-u_i(q^*)}{u_i(q^*)} =\frac{4a-F_A(r)-F_B(r)}{M-a}\leq0.\)

The numerator is nonpositive by Section 1, and the denominator is positive. This proves proportional fairness.

4. Running time and exact representation

For the promised valid input class, the decision algorithm always returns yes. Including input reading, this takes \(O(L)\) time.

The construction requires:

  • sorting the \(m\) rational processing times;
  • computing \(m\) rational prefix sums of the masses;
  • outputting \(m\) intervals, each with two nonzero rates equal to \(\frac12\).

Every breakpoint has \(O(L)\) bits. Indeed, a common denominator can be taken as the product of the input mass denominators, whose bit length is \(O(L)\); multiplying the prefix sums by two preserves this bound.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, rational comparisons and prefix-sum operations take at most \(O(L^2)\) bit operations each. Thus exact construction takes

\(O\!\left(m\log(m+1)L^2\right) =O\!\left(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2\right)\)

bit operations, since \(\tau=2m\). The sparse schedule description has \(O(mL)=O(\tau L)\) bits.

The construction uses the divisible mass rates explicitly permitted here. The finite indivisible-job hardness result in [Agnetis et al.](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/112370/7/WRAP-price-fairness-two-agent-single-machine-scheduling-problems-Chen-2018.pdf) therefore concerns a different feasible-schedule model.
