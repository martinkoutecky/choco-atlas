The continuous mirror question, as graded

For rational \(c>0\), let a society have preference types \(A,B\) with masses \(\mu_A+\mu_B=1\). Find a neutral lottery rule \(P_A(a,b)\) that is pointwise maximal for every state \(a\ge b\), subject to the mass-scaled group false-name-proof constraints: for every \(A\)-preferring coalition of mass \(\kappa>0\), background ballot masses \(z_A,z_B\ge0\), and added false-ballot masses \(\delta_A,\delta_B\ge0\), require \(P_A(z_A+\kappa,z_B)\ge P_A(z_A+\kappa+\delta_A,z_B+\delta_B)-c(\delta_A+\delta_B)/\kappa\); impose the symmetric constraint for \(B\), along with neutrality and the corresponding participation and strategy-proofness conditions. Is the pointwise-optimal rule \(P_A(a,b)=\min\{1,\tfrac12+c\ln(a/b)\}\) for \(a\ge b>0\), with \(P_A(a,0)=1\) for \(a>0\) and \(P_A(0,0)=1/2\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m=2\) alternatives, \(A,B\), and two strict preference types; utility per unit mass is \(1\) for the preferred outcome and \(0\) otherwise. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, with \(\mu_A+\mu_B=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and other relevant parameters, where \(L\) is the input bit length.
For rational \(c>0\), seek an anonymous lottery rule \(P_A:\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2\to[0,1]\), with \(P_B=1-P_A\) and neutrality \(P_A(a,b)=1-P_A(b,a)\). States are ballot masses, need not sum to one, and all constraints below apply universally on this domain; no continuity is assumed.
Genuine ballot mass is free, abstention costs nothing, and added false-ballot mass \(\delta_A+\delta_B\) costs \(c(\delta_A+\delta_B)\). Coalitions of one preference type can commit to transfers and share this cost equally per unit mass.
Participation and strategy-proofness require, for all \(x,y\ge0\) and \(h>0\), \(P_A(x+h,y)\ge P_A(x,y)\) and \(P_A(x+h,y)\ge P_A(x,y+h)\), with the symmetric requirements for \(B\).
Group false-name-proofness requires, for every \(\kappa>0\) and \(z_A,z_B,\delta_A,\delta_B\ge0\), \(P_A(z_A+\kappa,z_B)\ge P_A(z_A+\kappa+\delta_A,z_B+\delta_B)-c(\delta_A+\delta_B)/\kappa\), and symmetrically for \(B\).
An admissible rule is strongly optimal if, for every admissible \(Q\), \(P_A(a,b)\ge Q_A(a,b)\) at every \(a\ge b\ge0\).

Does a strongly optimal admissible rule exist, and is it given by \(P_A(a,b)=\min\{1,\tfrac12+c\ln(a/b)\}\) for \(a\ge b>0\), \(P_A(a,0)=1\) for \(a>0\), and \(P_A(0,0)=\tfrac12\), extended by neutrality, or does this characterization fail?

For integer ballot counts, the strongly optimal neutral group false-name-proof rule satisfying participation has majority probability \(\min\{1,\tfrac12+c\sum_{k=b}^{a-1}1/k\}\) for \(a\ge b>0\), with unanimous and empty-state values as above. Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result

The characterization holds: there is a unique strongly optimal admissible rule, and it is the proposed logarithmic rule. No continuity assumption is needed.

Fix \(c>0\). Define the clipping function
\(C(t)=\max\{0,\min\{1,t\}\}\), and set

\(P_A(a,b)=C\!\left(\tfrac12+c\ln(a/b)\right)\) for \(a,b>0\),

with \(P_A(a,0)=1\) for \(a>0\), \(P_A(0,b)=0\) for \(b>0\), and \(P_A(0,0)=\tfrac12\). Set \(P_B=1-P_A\).

For \(a\ge b>0\), this is exactly
\(P_A(a,b)=\min\{1,\tfrac12+c\ln(a/b)\}\).

2. A universal bound without continuity

Let \(Q\) be any admissible rule. For every \(u>0\), \(b\ge0\), and \(d\ge0\), group false-name-proofness, applied with
\(\kappa=u\), \(z_A=0\), \(z_B=b\), \(\delta_A=d\), and \(\delta_B=0\), gives

\(Q_A(u+d,b)-Q_A(u,b)\le cd/u.\)

Participation gives the corresponding lower bound. Thus

\(0\le Q_A(u+d,b)-Q_A(u,b)\le cd/u.\)

We claim that, for every \(v>u>0\),

\(Q_A(v,b)-Q_A(u,b)\le c\ln(v/u).\)

To prove this, fix a positive integer \(n\), put \(\Delta=(v-u)/n\), and define \(u_i=u+i\Delta\). Each \(u_i\) is positive, so the preceding inequality applies to every consecutive pair. Summing gives

\(Q_A(v,b)-Q_A(u,b) =\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\bigl(Q_A(u_{i+1},b)-Q_A(u_i,b)\bigr) \le c\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Delta/u_i.\)

The sum on the right is a left Riemann sum for the continuous function \(t\mapsto1/t\) on \([u,v]\). Consequently, its limit is
\(\int_u^v dt/t=\ln(v/u)\). Since the displayed bound holds for every \(n\), taking the limit proves the claim. No limit or regularity property of \(Q_A\) was used.

Neutrality forces \(Q_A(b,b)=\tfrac12\). Therefore, for \(a\ge b>0\),

\(Q_A(a,b)\le\tfrac12+c\ln(a/b).\)

Combining this with \(Q_A(a,b)\le1\) yields the universal upper bound

\(Q_A(a,b)\le\min\{1,\tfrac12+c\ln(a/b)\}.\)

3. Admissibility of the proposed rule

The clipping function satisfies three elementary properties:

  • It is nondecreasing.
  • For \(s\le t\), \(0\le C(t)-C(s)\le t-s\).
  • \(C(1-t)=1-C(t)\).

The second property follows by considering intervals lying below zero, inside \([0,1]\), or above one, and splitting any interval that crosses an endpoint. On each piece, the change in \(C\) is either zero or the length of that piece. The third property follows directly from the same three regions.

Neutrality. For positive coordinates,
\(\tfrac12+c\ln(b/a)=1-(\tfrac12+c\ln(a/b))\). The third clipping property therefore gives
\(P_A(a,b)=1-P_A(b,a)\). The specified axis and empty-state values satisfy this identity as well.

Participation. For fixed \(b>0\), the positive-coordinate formula is nondecreasing in \(a\), because the logarithm and \(C\) are nondecreasing. Its value at \(a=0\) is zero, so monotonicity also holds there. For \(b=0\), the value is \(\tfrac12\) at \(a=0\) and one for every \(a>0\), again nondecreasing.

Neutrality then implies that \(P_A\) is nonincreasing in its second coordinate: if \(b'\ge b\), then

\(P_A(a,b')=1-P_A(b',a)\le1-P_A(b,a)=P_A(a,b).\)

These two coordinate monotonicities establish participation for both preference types.

Strategy-proofness. For every \(x,y\ge0\) and \(h>0\), coordinate monotonicity gives

\(P_A(x+h,y)\ge P_A(x,y)\ge P_A(x,y+h).\)

This is precisely the required inequality for type \(A\); exchanging the alternatives proves the requirement for type \(B\).

Group false-name-proofness. Fix arbitrary \(\kappa>0\) and nonnegative \(z_A,z_B,\delta_A,\delta_B\). Write \(a=z_A+\kappa>0\) and \(b=z_B\). Since additional mass for \(B\) cannot increase \(P_A\),

\(P_A(a+\delta_A,b+\delta_B)-P_A(a,b) \le P_A(a+\delta_A,b)-P_A(a,b).\)

If \(b=0\), the right-hand side is zero because both probabilities equal one.

If \(b>0\), the second clipping property gives

\(P_A(a+\delta_A,b)-P_A(a,b) \le c\ln\!\left(1+\delta_A/a\right) \le c\delta_A/a.\)

Here the last inequality follows from
\(\ln(1+t)=\int_0^t(1+s)^{-1}\,ds\le t\) for \(t\ge0\).

Finally, \(a\ge\kappa\), so

\(c\delta_A/a\le c\delta_A/\kappa \le c(\delta_A+\delta_B)/\kappa.\)

Combining these inequalities proves exactly the stated constraint for type \(A\), including all boundary cases. Because \(P_B(a,b)=P_A(b,a)\), exchanging coordinates and alternatives proves the symmetric constraint for type \(B\).

Thus \(P\) is admissible.

4. Strong optimality and uniqueness

For every admissible \(Q\) and every \(a\ge b>0\), the bound proved in Section 2 gives

\(Q_A(a,b)\le\min\{1,\tfrac12+c\ln(a/b)\}=P_A(a,b).\)

For \(a>0=b\), the inequality follows from \(Q_A(a,0)\le1=P_A(a,0)\). At the empty state, neutrality forces
\(Q_A(0,0)=\tfrac12=P_A(0,0)\). Hence \(P\) is strongly optimal on the entire required domain.

If another admissible rule \(R\) were strongly optimal, strong optimality of \(P\) and \(R\) would give equality at every \(a\ge b\). Neutrality would then give equality at every \(a<b\). Therefore the strongly optimal rule is unique.

5. Scope

The discrete harmonic characterization appears in [Wagman–Conitzer (2008), Theorem 3](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/costly_votingAAAI08.pdf). The proof above establishes the mass characterization directly from the stated constraints.

The normalized society has \(m=2\) and support size \(\tau\in\{1,2\}\). Its outcome is obtained by evaluating this rule at \((\mu_A,\mu_B)\), treating an absent type as having zero mass. Normalization is not used in the proof: admissibility and optimality hold universally on \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2\).
