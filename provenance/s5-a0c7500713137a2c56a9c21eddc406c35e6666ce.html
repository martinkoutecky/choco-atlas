The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two finite type sets \(I,J\), rational masses \(\mu_i,\nu_j\) with each side’s total mass normalized to \(1\), integer capacities, and incomplete rankings with ties over acceptable opposite-side types, can one find an exact rational assignment in polynomial time? Represent the assignment by masses of agents receiving integral bundles of opposite-side types, with equal type-pair incidence on both sides. Require pairwise stability under the paper’s blocking conditions and two-sided Pareto efficiency: no feasible reassignment weakly improves almost every agent and strictly improves a positive mass. Measure running time and output size in the number of types \(\tau\), the preference description, the binary capacity encodings, and the mass bit length \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I,J\) be nonempty finite type sets, with \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) and \(m=\max\{|I|,|J|\}\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Masses satisfy \(\sum_i\mu_i=\sum_j\nu_j=1\). Each type has a binary-encoded capacity \(c_x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) and an incomplete transitive ranking with ties over opposite-side types. Only mutually acceptable pairs may be assigned, and every acceptable partner is strictly preferred to an empty slot.
A bundle is an integral multiset of mutually acceptable opposite-side types, of size at most \(c_x\); repeated types represent distinct partners. For bundle comparison, sort partners from best to worst and pad to \(c_x\) with empty slots: \(b'\succeq_x b\) exactly when every coordinate is weakly better, and \(b'\succ_x b\) when additionally some coordinate is strictly better. Other bundle pairs are incomparable.
An assignment consists of nonnegative masses \(a_{i,b},d_{j,e}\) of agents receiving bundles, satisfying \(\sum_ba_{i,b}=\mu_i\), \(\sum_ed_{j,e}=\nu_j\), and \(\sum_ba_{i,b}b_j=\sum_ed_{j,e}e_i\) for every \(i,j\). These equations and the bundle restrictions define feasibility.
Type \(x\) receiving bundle \(b\) strictly accepts a new partner of type \(y\) if \(|b|<c_x\), or if it strictly prefers \(y\) to some partner in \(b\). Pairwise stability forbids mutually acceptable \(i,j\) and bundles \(b,e\) with \(a_{i,b}>0\), \(d_{j,e}>0\) for which both strictly accept each other. Having another partner of the same type does not exclude a block; the proposed partner is a distinct individual.
Two-sided Pareto efficiency means that no feasible reassignment admits, within each type, a coupling of old and new bundle masses that weakly improves almost every agent and strictly improves a positive total mass on either side. Competing reassignment masses and couplings may be real.
The computational problem is to find a pairwise stable, two-sided Pareto-efficient assignment, or report that none exists, represented by an explicit list of nonzero rational bundle masses with binary-encoded multiplicities. Let \(P\) be the preference-description length, \(B\) the total capacity-encoding length, and \(L\) the total mass-encoding length.

Is this construction problem solvable in polynomial time, with polynomial output size, in \(m,\tau,P,B,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite individual societies with these bundle preferences, a pairwise stable and two-sided Pareto-efficient assignment always exists and is computable in polynomial time ([discrete algorithm](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/47031/1/MPRA_paper_47031.pdf)); the [2019 journal result](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304406819300400) also establishes an efficient discrete construction.
No result settling this exact distribution-over-types construction problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The graded question omitted bundle preferences; used the coordinatewise partial order on sorted, empty-slot-padded bundles dictated by the discrete lattice preference model.
  • Specified nonnegative integer capacities, including zero, which the discrete model explicitly permits for dummy agents; capacities apply to each individual of a type.
  • Interpreted integral bundles as multisets: different individuals of the same opposite-side type may occupy multiple slots, without a capacity bound based on support size.
  • Defined population-level blocking through positive-mass bundle classes whose members both strictly benefit from adding a distinct partner or replacing a strictly worse partner; existing incidence with that partner's type does not preclude blocking.
  • Made individual Pareto comparisons precise through within-type couplings, allowing real competing assignments and requiring a positive mass of strict improvements on either side.
  • Specified an explicit rational output representation and reporting nonexistence, since continuous existence is not supplied by the discrete theorem.
  • Defined the otherwise unspecified size variable m and separated preference, capacity, and mass encoding lengths.

The construction problem is polynomial-time solvable with polynomial output size. A pairwise stable, two-sided Pareto-efficient assignment always exists.

The crucial feature is the specified blocking convention: an existing partner of the proposed type does not prevent a block. This makes stability depend only on aggregate match quantities.

1. Aggregate quantities and preference cutoffs. Write \(\rho_x=\mu_x\) for \(x\in I\), and \(\rho_x=\nu_x\) for \(x\in J\). Let \(E\subseteq I\times J\) be the mutually acceptable pairs, and define the aggregate capacity \(\displaystyle C_x=\rho_xc_x\).

For each type \(x\), list its mutually acceptable partner types in ordered indifference classes \(\displaystyle T_{x,1},\ldots,T_{x,r_x}\), best first. This uses the ranking interpretation implicit in the question’s sorting rule: incompleteness specifies unacceptable types.

For a bundle \(b\), define its cumulative counts by
\(\displaystyle N_{x,k}(b)=\sum_{y\in T_{x,1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{x,k}}b_y\).

An assignment determines an edge vector \(f\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E}\), where
\(\displaystyle f_{ij}=\sum_ba_{i,b}b_j=\sum_ed_{j,e}e_i\).

When summing over neighbors of either endpoint, write \(f_{xy}\) for the corresponding edge variable. Every feasible assignment therefore satisfies
\(\displaystyle \sum_{y:xy\in E}f_{xy}\le C_x\quad\text{for every }x.\)

Let \(\mathcal F\) denote the polytope defined by these inequalities and nonnegativity. Define
\(\displaystyle F_{x,k}(f)=\sum_{y\in T_{x,1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{x,k}}f_{xy}\).

If \(p_{x,b}\) denotes the bundle mass for type \(x\), then
\(\displaystyle F_{x,k}(f)=\sum_bp_{x,b}N_{x,k}(b).\)

We next prove that every rational vector in \(\mathcal F\) has a small rational bundle realization.

2. Constructive bundle realization. Fix a type \(x\), abbreviate \(\rho=\rho_x\) and \(c=c_x\), and enumerate its acceptable neighbors as \(y_1,\ldots,y_d\). Given rational \(f\in\mathcal F\), set
\(\displaystyle z_\ell=\frac{f_{xy_\ell}}{\rho}\quad(1\le\ell\le d),\qquad z_{d+1}=c-\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}z_\ell.\)

The final coordinate represents empty slots. All coordinates are nonnegative, and their sum is the integer \(c\).

Let
\(\displaystyle S_0=0,\qquad S_\ell=\sum_{h=1}^{\ell}z_h.\)

For \(0\le t<1\), define
\(\displaystyle v_\ell(t)=\lfloor S_\ell+t\rfloor-\lfloor S_{\ell-1}+t\rfloor\quad(1\le\ell\le d+1).\)

These are nonnegative integers. Moreover,
\(\displaystyle \sum_{\ell=1}^{d+1}v_\ell(t)=\lfloor c+t\rfloor-\lfloor t\rfloor=c.\)

Discarding the empty-slot coordinate therefore gives a permitted integral bundle.

For every nonnegative real \(s\),
\(\displaystyle \int_0^1\lfloor s+t\rfloor\,dt=s.\)
Indeed, writing \(s=n+\alpha\), with integer \(n\) and \(0\le\alpha<1\), the integrand equals \(n\) on an interval of length \(1-\alpha\) and \(n+1\) on an interval of length \(\alpha\). Consequently,
\(\displaystyle \int_0^1v_\ell(t)\,dt=S_\ell-S_{\ell-1}=z_\ell.\)

The vector \(v(t)\) changes only at the interior breakpoints
\(\displaystyle 1-\operatorname{frac}(S_\ell)\quad\text{with }\operatorname{frac}(S_\ell)\ne0,\quad1\le\ell\le d.\)

Thus there are at most \(d+1\) intervals of constancy. For each interval of length \(\lambda\), output its bundle with mass \(\rho\lambda\). These masses are rational, sum to \(\rho\), and reproduce every incident quantity \(f_{xy_\ell}\).

Apply this independently to every type. Both endpoint decompositions reproduce the same \(f_{ij}\), so all balance equations hold. Identical bundles may be merged.

Hence every rational \(f\in\mathcal F\) has a rational realization using at most
\(\displaystyle \sum_x(d_x+1)=2|E|+\tau\)
nonzero bundle masses. The construction also covers zero capacities and types without acceptable neighbors.

3. Stability is determined by aggregates. For an acceptable neighbor \(y\) of \(x\), let \(k_x(y)\) be its indifference-class index.

A bundle \(b\) strictly accepts \(y\) exactly when
\(\displaystyle N_{x,k_x(y)}(b)<c_x.\)

To verify this equivalence, if the bundle is under capacity, its cumulative count is smaller than \(c_x\), and it strictly accepts \(y\). If the bundle is full, the inequality says precisely that some assigned partner is strictly worse than \(y\).

For any realization of \(f\),
\(\displaystyle C_x-F_{x,k_x(y)}(f)=\sum_bp_{x,b}\bigl(c_x-N_{x,k_x(y)}(b)\bigr).\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore some positive-mass bundle of type \(x\) strictly accepts \(y\) if and only if
\(\displaystyle F_{x,k_x(y)}(f)<C_x.\)

It follows directly from the blocking definition that an assignment is stable exactly when, for every \(ij\in E\),
\[\displaystyle F_{i,k_i(j)}(f)=C_i\quad\text{or}\quad F_{j,k_j(i)}(f)=C_j.\tag{1}\]

In particular, all realizations of the same aggregate vector have the same stability status.

A useful consequence is monotonicity: if \(f^0\) satisfies (1), and \(f\in\mathcal F\) satisfies
\[\displaystyle F_{x,k}(f)\ge F_{x,k}(f^0)\quad\text{for every }x,k,\tag{2}\]
then \(f\) also satisfies (1). Each equality witnessing stability of an edge remains an equality, because every cumulative quantity is bounded above by \(C_x\).

4. Computing a stable aggregate starting point. We use the following established stable-allocation theorem.

For a bipartite graph with positive vertex quotas, nonnegative edge upper capacities, and strict preference orders at vertices, a stable allocation exists and can be found in strongly polynomial time. Stability means that every edge either reaches its upper capacity or has an endpoint whose quota is entirely allocated to neighbors ranked at least as high as the other endpoint. With integer data, the inductive algorithm produces integer quantities. This is the stable-allocation result of [Baïou and Balinski, corrected 2002 paper, Section 2](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

Here is an explicit application, including the encoding considerations.

Let \(D\) be the product of all denominators in the input masses. Then
\(\displaystyle Q_x=DC_x=D\rho_xc_x\)
is an integer. Remove zero-quota vertices temporarily; any edge incident to such a vertex automatically satisfies (1).

Break ties arbitrarily within each original indifference class. Give every remaining acceptable edge the upper capacity
\(\displaystyle H=1+\sum_xQ_x.\)

The theorem applies: the graph is bipartite, retained quotas are positive integers, edge bounds are positive integers, and rankings are strict. Missing edges can equivalently be represented by upper capacity zero.

Let \(h\) be its stable output. No acceptable edge reaches \(H\), since
\(\displaystyle h_{xy}\le Q_x<H.\)

Thus every acceptable edge has an endpoint whose full quota is assigned to neighbors at least as good under the refined strict order. All these neighbors are also at least as good under the original ranking. Restoring zero-quota vertices and setting
\(\displaystyle f^0_{ij}=h_{ij}/D\)
therefore gives \(f^0\in\mathcal F\) satisfying (1).

If either retained side is empty, simply take \(f^0=0\).

5. A linear program ensures Pareto efficiency. Solve the rational linear program
\(\displaystyle \text{maximize }U(f)=\sum_x\sum_{k=1}^{r_x}F_{x,k}(f)\)
subject to
\[\displaystyle f\in\mathcal F,\qquad F_{x,k}(f)\ge F_{x,k}(f^0)\quad\text{for every }x,k.\tag{3}\]

Its feasible region is nonempty because it contains \(f^0\). It is closed and bounded, since each edge quantity is bounded by its endpoint capacities. Therefore an optimum exists, and rational linear programming yields a rational optimum \(f^\ast\) in polynomial time.

By Section 3, every feasible vector in (3), including \(f^\ast\), is stable.

Realize \(f^\ast\) using Section 2. We prove that this realization is two-sided Pareto-efficient against the permitted real-mass reassignments and real couplings.

Suppose otherwise. Let \(\widehat f\) be the aggregate vector of a feasible Pareto-improving reassignment, and let \(\gamma_x(b,b')\) be its coupling of old bundle \(b\) with new bundle \(b'\) within type \(x\).

Whenever \(b'\succeq_x b\), coordinatewise comparison of the sorted, padded bundles gives
\[\displaystyle N_{x,k}(b')\ge N_{x,k}(b)\quad\text{for every }k.\tag{4}\]

Indeed, improving a sorted coordinate cannot change its indicator of membership in a preference prefix from one to zero.

If \(b'\succ_x b\), choose a coordinate that improves strictly and let \(k\) be the new partner’s indifference-class index. That coordinate changes its prefix indicator from zero to one, while every other coordinate’s indicator weakly increases. Hence
\[\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{r_x}\bigl(N_{x,k}(b')-N_{x,k}(b)\bigr)\ge1.\tag{5}\]

Using the coupling marginals and (4), we obtain
\(\displaystyle F_{x,k}(\widehat f)\ge F_{x,k}(f^\ast)\ge F_{x,k}(f^0).\)

Thus \(\widehat f\) is feasible for (3). Furthermore,
\(\displaystyle U(\widehat f)-U(f^\ast)=\sum_x\sum_{b,b'}\gamma_x(b,b')\sum_{k=1}^{r_x}\bigl(N_{x,k}(b')-N_{x,k}(b)\bigr).\)

All terms are nonnegative. By (5), the difference is at least the total coupling mass of strictly improving transitions, which is positive by hypothesis. This contradicts optimality of \(f^\ast\).

This argument uses neither rationality of the competing masses nor rationality of the couplings. It therefore proves precisely the requested two-sided Pareto efficiency.

6. Polynomial running time and output size. Let \(q=|E|\). Then
\(\displaystyle q\le |I||J|\le m^2,\qquad \sum_xr_x\le\sum_xd_x=2q.\)

The stable-allocation instance has at most \(\tau\) vertices and \(q\) acceptable edges. The integer scaling factor \(D\) has bit length at most \(L\), and \(Q_x\) and \(H\) have bit lengths polynomial in \(B,L,\tau\). The stable-allocation algorithm processes these encoded quantities without expanding them into individual agents or slots.

The linear program has \(q\) variables and polynomially many constraints, with polynomial encoding length. Standard rational linear-programming bounds give an optimal vertex \(f^\ast\) of polynomial bit length.

For each type, decomposition requires only rational sums, floors, and sorting at most \(d_x\) breakpoints. Every output multiplicity is at most \(c_x\), so its binary length is bounded by the input capacity length. All interval masses have polynomial bit length.

The final output contains at most
\(\displaystyle \tau+2q\le\tau+2m^2\)
nonzero rational bundle masses and has total size polynomial in \(m,\tau,P,B,L\).

Thus the algorithm always constructs the required assignment; the “none exists” outcome never occurs under the stated premises.
