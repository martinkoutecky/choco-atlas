The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(T\) with masses \(\mu_t\), entitlements \(e_t\), additive values \(v_t(g)\), and supplies \(\sigma_g\) of indivisible item categories \(g\in G\), find a type-level configuration law \(\lambda_{t,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{G}\), with \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=1\) and \(\sum_{t,B}\mu_t\lambda_{t,B}B_g=\sigma_g\), whose marginals \(x_{t,g}=\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}B_g\) maximize \(\sum_t\mu_te_t\log v_t(x_t)\), satisfy \(v_t(x_t)/e_t\ge v_t(x_u)/e_u\) for all positive-mass types \(t,u\), and induce a measurable integral assignment that is Pareto optimal and gives every supported bundle \(B\) weighted proportionality up to one item: \(v_t(B)\ge e_tV_t\) or \(v_t(B)+v_t(g)\ge e_tV_t\) for some available \(g\), where \(V_t=\sum_g\sigma_gv_t(g)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless probability space partitioned into types \(T\). Its input is the support list of the \(\tau=|T|\) positive-mass types, with binary-rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|G|\ge1\) item categories, binary-rational supplies \(\sigma_g>0\), entitlement densities \(e_t>0\) normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_te_t=1\), and nonnegative binary-rational additive values \(v_t(g)\), with \(V_t=\sum_g\sigma_gv_t(g)>0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length and \(v_t(z)=\sum_gv_t(g)z_g\).
Supplies measure aggregate masses of copies; individuals receive integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\), without individual capacity bounds. An outside copy of any positive-supply category may witness proportionality up to one item, including a category already represented in the bundle.
Seek a finitely supported rational configuration law \(\lambda_{t,B}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=1\), \(x_t=\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}B\), and \(\sum_t\mu_tx_{t,g}=\sigma_g\) for every \(g\).
Its marginals must maximize \(\sum_t\mu_te_t\log v_t(x_t)\) over all nonnegative fractional allocations satisfying those supply equalities, and satisfy weighted envy-freeness: \(v_t(x_t)/e_t\ge v_t(x_u)/e_u\) for all \(t,u\in T\).
Every supported bundle must satisfy weighted proportionality up to one item: \(v_t(B)\ge e_tV_t\), or \(v_t(B)+v_t(g)\ge e_tV_t\) for some \(g\in G\).
The law must admit a measurable integral realization assigning bundle \(B\) to mass \(\mu_t\lambda_{t,B}\) of type \(t\), which is Pareto optimal: no other measurable integral assignment with the same supplies weakly improves utility almost everywhere and strictly improves it on a set of positive mass.

Can such a law be computed, or its nonexistence determined, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finitely many agents and explicitly listed goods, a strongly polynomial algorithm computes weighted-Nash-maximizing marginals and a lottery satisfying ex-ante weighted envy-freeness and Pareto optimality, and ex-post weighted proportionality up to one item and Pareto optimality. [Discrete result](https://evi-micha.github.io/papers/wbobw.pdf)
Subsequent discrete work also obtains these guarantees in strongly polynomial time; neither verified source treats the atomless configuration problem above, and no result settling that exact problem was located. [Subsequent result](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/988549/files/988549.pdf)

  • Normalized population mass to one and interpreted entitlements as positive densities with mass-weighted total entitlement one.
  • Required positive entitlements and positive total value for every type, excluding undefined divisions and logarithms; retained nonnegative additive valuations.
  • Specified binary-rational values, entitlements, and supplies, and defined L as total input bit length.
  • Interpreted category supplies as aggregate masses of copies, retaining the stated unrestricted nonnegative integral bundle domain rather than adding individual capacity bounds.
  • Resolved the outside-item witness using the atomless stock interpretation: every positive-supply category has copies outside any individual's finite bundle, even when that category is already represented there.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, and every valid instance admits a law satisfying all the requirements. The law can be chosen to have at most \(m+1\) supported bundles per type.

I use the intended finite rational-input interpretation: the entitlement densities \(e_t\) are rational numbers whose encodings are included in \(L\).

The construction combines exact linear Fisher-market computation with an explicit rounding lemma. Pareto optimality holds for every measurable realization of the resulting law.

1. Computing the fractional marginals

Let \(H\subseteq G\) contain the categories valued positively by at least one type. Construct a linear Fisher market with one buyer per type and one unit-supply good per category in \(H\). Define the budgets and utilities by

\(b_t=\mu_te_t,\qquad a_{t,g}=\sigma_gv_t(g).\)

Every budget is positive, every buyer values some retained good because \(V_t>0\), and every retained good is valued by some buyer.

We use the following known algorithmic result: a linear Fisher market with positive rational budgets, nonnegative rational utilities, unit supplies, and a positive utility in every row and column has an exactly computable rational equilibrium in polynomial bit time. Orlin gives a strongly polynomial algorithm for this market model. Its integer-input formulation applies here by clearing utility denominators and budget denominators separately; uniform utility scaling preserves demands, and uniform budget scaling merely rescales equilibrium prices. These transformations have polynomial encoding length. [Orlin, *Improved Algorithms for Computing Fisher’s Market Clearing Prices*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/16dagstuhl-seminar-proceedings/dsp-vol10171/DagSemProc.10171.2/DagSemProc.10171.2.pdf)

Let \(y_{t,g}\) be the equilibrium fractions and \(P_g>0\) the equilibrium prices. Thus

\(\sum_t y_{t,g}=1,\qquad \sum_{g\in H}P_gy_{t,g}=b_t.\)

For each buyer, put

\(\alpha_t=\max_{g\in H}\frac{a_{t,g}}{P_g}>0.\)

The equilibrium condition says

\(a_{t,g}\leq\alpha_tP_g,\)

with equality whenever \(y_{t,g}>0\).

For each category outside \(H\), assign its entire fractional supply to any fixed type. Define the per-copy prices and per-capita allocations by

\(p_g=P_g/\sigma_g,\qquad x_{t,g}=\sigma_gy_{t,g}/\mu_t\quad(g\in H),\)

and set \(p_g=0\) outside \(H\), using the chosen fractional allocation there.

These quantities satisfy

\(\sum_t\mu_tx_{t,g}=\sigma_g,\qquad p\cdot x_t=e_t,\)

and

\(v_t(g)\leq\alpha_tp_g,\)

with equality whenever \(x_{t,g}>0\). For categories outside \(H\), this equality follows because both sides are zero.

Consequently, writing \(U_t=v_t(x_t)\), we have

\(U_t=\alpha_t e_t>0,\qquad p\cdot\sigma=\sum_t\mu_te_t=1.\)

All these quantities are rational and computable with polynomial encoding length.

2. Nash optimality, weighted envy-freeness, and proportionality

Nash optimality

Let \(z\) be any feasible nonnegative fractional allocation. The price inequalities imply

\(\sum_t\mu_t\frac{e_t}{U_t}v_t(z_t) =\sum_t\mu_t\frac{v_t(z_t)}{\alpha_t} \leq\sum_t\mu_t p\cdot z_t =p\cdot\sigma=1.\)

If some \(v_t(z_t)=0\), its Nash objective is negative infinity, so it cannot improve upon \(x\). Otherwise, applying \(\log r\leq r-1\) for \(r>0\) gives

\(\sum_t\mu_te_t\log\frac{v_t(z_t)}{U_t} \leq\sum_t\mu_te_t\left(\frac{v_t(z_t)}{U_t}-1\right) \leq 1-\sum_t\mu_te_t=0.\)

Thus \(x\) maximizes exactly the required weighted Nash objective.

Weighted envy-freeness

For every pair \(t,u\),

\(v_t(x_u)\leq\alpha_t p\cdot x_u=\alpha_te_u.\)

Since \(U_t/e_t=\alpha_t\), this proves

\(\frac{v_t(x_t)}{e_t}\geq\frac{v_t(x_u)}{e_u}.\)

Weighted proportionality of the marginals

For every type,

\(V_t=\sum_g\sigma_gv_t(g) \leq\alpha_t\sum_g\sigma_gp_g=\alpha_t.\)

Therefore,

\(U_t=\alpha_te_t\geq e_tV_t.\)

3. Explicit rounding with at most \(m+1\) bundles

We prove a rounding lemma for a single type.

Order its categories so that their values satisfy

\(a_1\geq a_2\geq\cdots\geq a_m\geq0,\qquad a_1>0.\)

Let \(x_i\geq0\) denote its fractional quantities in that order, and define

\(S_0=0,\qquad S_k=\sum_{i=1}^k x_i.\)

For \(r\in[0,1)\), define the integral bundle

\(B_i(r)=\lfloor S_i+r\rfloor-\lfloor S_{i-1}+r\rfloor.\)

Because the prefix sums are nondecreasing, every \(B_i(r)\) is a nonnegative integer.

Preservation of every marginal

If \(r\) is uniform on \([0,1)\), then for every real \(s\),

\(\mathbb E[\lfloor s+r\rfloor]=s.\)

Indeed, writing \(s=n+f\) with \(n\) integral and \(f\in[0,1)\), the floor equals \(n+1\) on a set of length \(f\) and \(n\) otherwise. Hence

\(\mathbb E[B_i(r)]=S_i-S_{i-1}=x_i.\)

Utility loss is less than one maximum-value copy

Define

\(D_k(r)=\lfloor S_k+r\rfloor-S_k.\)

Then \(D_k(r)>-1\). Set \(a_{m+1}=0\). Telescoping yields

\(\sum_{i=1}^m a_i(B_i(r)-x_i) =\sum_{k=1}^m(a_k-a_{k+1})D_k(r).\)

All coefficients on the right are nonnegative, and their sum is \(a_1>0\). Therefore,

\(\sum_{i=1}^m a_iB_i(r)>\sum_{i=1}^m a_ix_i-a_1.\)

Apply this construction to type \(t\), and let

\(M_t=\max_{g\in G}v_t(g)>0.\)

Every produced bundle satisfies

\(v_t(B)+M_t>U_t\geq e_tV_t.\)

A category attaining \(M_t\) has positive supply. Under the stipulated outside-copy convention, it is an admissible witness even when that category already appears in \(B\). Thus every produced bundle satisfies weighted proportionality up to one item.

Producing a finite rational law

The function \(r\mapsto B(r)\) changes only when some \(S_k+r\) crosses an integer. Its possible internal breakpoints are

\(1-\{S_k\}\quad\text{for those }k\text{ with }\{S_k\}>0,\)

where \(\{s\}=s-\lfloor s\rfloor\).

There are at most \(m\) distinct internal breakpoints. Together with \(0\) and \(1\), they partition the parameter interval into at most \(m+1\) intervals.

For each interval:

  • Choose its midpoint and compute the corresponding bundle.
  • Give that bundle probability equal to the interval length.

Merge repeated bundles if necessary. Since \(x\) is rational, all breakpoints and probabilities are rational. The resulting law has at most \(m+1\) supported bundles and preserves every coordinate of \(x\).

Doing this separately for all types produces \(\lambda_{t,B}\) with

\(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=1,\qquad \sum_B\lambda_{t,B}B=x_t.\)

In particular, all supply equalities remain exact.

4. Measurable realization and Pareto optimality

Measurable realization

Use the divisibility theorem for atomless finite measures: if \(A\) is measurable and \(c\in[0,\mu(A)]\), there exists a measurable subset of \(A\) of measure \(c\).

Each type set inherits atomlessness from the society. Repeated application of this theorem partitions type \(t\) into measurable pieces of measures

\(\mu_t\lambda_{t,B}.\)

Assign bundle \(B\) on its corresponding piece. This gives a measurable integral assignment \(A\), and its aggregate supplies are

\(\int A_g\,d\mu =\sum_t\mu_t\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}B_g =\sum_t\mu_tx_{t,g} =\sigma_g.\)

Pareto optimality against all measurable integral assignments

Define the positive type weights

\(c_t=\frac{1}{\alpha_t}=\frac{e_t}{U_t}.\)

The price inequalities imply, for every nonnegative integral bundle \(C\),

\(c_tv_t(C)\leq p\cdot C.\)

Consequently, every measurable integral assignment \(A'\) with the same supplies satisfies

\(\int c_{t(\omega)}v_{t(\omega)}(A'(\omega))\,d\mu(\omega) \leq\sum_gp_g\int A'_g\,d\mu =p\cdot\sigma=1.\)

For the constructed realization, equality holds:

\(\int c_{t(\omega)}v_{t(\omega)}(A(\omega))\,d\mu(\omega) =\sum_t\mu_tc_tU_t =\sum_t\mu_te_t=1.\)

Suppose \(A'\) weakly improves utility almost everywhere and strictly improves utility on a set of positive measure. Because every \(c_t>0\), the weighted utility difference is nonnegative almost everywhere and positive on a set of positive measure. It therefore has strictly positive integral.

The relevant utilities are integrable: supplies are finite, there are finitely many categories and types, and all values and weights are finite. Thus \(A'\) would have weighted utility integral strictly greater than \(1\), contradicting the preceding bound.

This proves Pareto optimality. The argument applies to every measurable realization of the law.

5. Polynomial running time and output size

The Fisher-market transformation and exact equilibrium computation take polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

For each type, rounding requires sorting the categories, forming prefix sums, sorting at most \(m\) breakpoints, and constructing at most \(m+1\) bundles. These are polynomially many exact rational operations on numbers of polynomial encoding length.

Large supplies do not require expanding copies. Indeed,

\(x_{t,g}\leq\sigma_g/\mu_t,\qquad B_g(r)\leq\lceil x_{t,g}\rceil\leq\lceil\sigma_g/\mu_t\rceil.\)

Hence bundle multiplicities have polynomial binary encoding length even when their numerical values are large.

The output has at most \(\tau(m+1)\) supported type-bundle pairs, each containing \(m\) binary-encoded multiplicities and one rational probability. Its total encoding length is polynomial.

The algorithm therefore computes the requested law in polynomial bit time; under the stated hypotheses, a nonexistence outcome never occurs.
