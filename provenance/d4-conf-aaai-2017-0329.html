The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of types \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita chore supplies \(q_j\), and nonnegative additive burdens \(a_{tj}\), define \(b_t=\min_{\pi}\max_{B:\pi_B>0} a_t\cdot B\), where \(\pi\) ranges over finite-support distributions on integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{J}\) with \(\sum_B\pi_B=1\) and \(\sum_B\pi_B B=q\). Determine whether there is a finite-support mass assignment \(x_{t,B}\ge0\) with \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B}B_jx_{t,B}=q_j\) for every chore category \(j\), and \(x_{t,B}>0\) only if \(a_t\cdot B\le b_t\); if so, output such an assignment, and otherwise report infeasibility.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) chore categories \(J\), binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\geq0\), and binary-rational additive burdens \(a_{tj}\geq0\), with \(a_t\cdot q>0\) for every type. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Bundles are vectors \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{J}\), including the empty bundle, with burden \(a_t\cdot B\).
Define \(b_t=\min_{\pi}\max_{B:\pi_B>0}a_t\cdot B\), where \(\pi\) ranges over finite-support probability distributions satisfying \(\sum_B\pi_B B=q\). These benchmarks are defined by the input, not supplied separately.
The computational problem is to output a finite list of bundles and nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,B}\) satisfying \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B}B_jx_{t,B}=q_j\) for every \(j\), and \(x_{t,B}>0\Rightarrow a_t\cdot B\leq b_t\), or report infeasibility.

Is this computational problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite agents and indivisible chores, computing a maximin-share allocation when one exists is strongly \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and weakly \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even for two agents ([Aziz–Rauchecker–Schryen–Walsh](https://arxiv.org/html/1604.01435)).
No result settling this exact mass-assignment problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative supplies and binary-rational burdens, since their signs and finite encoding were not fully specified.
  • Required at least one type and one category, and positive total burden a_t·q for every type, carrying over the anchor's exclusion of identically zero chore valuations on supplied chores.
  • Allowed empty integral bundles, as finite-agent allocations may leave agents empty.
  • Specified rational masses in the output; the benchmark distributions retain the stated finite-support probability-distribution domain.
  • Made explicit that benchmarks are derived from the input rather than provided as additional input.

Result

The required output problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(\tau=1\), \(\mu_1=1\), every supply is \(q_j=\tfrac12\), and all burdens are positive integers.

We prove this by a polynomial-time reduction from PARTITION using one call to the proposed solver. Every instance constructed below is feasible and admits a polynomial-size rational output.

1. Source problem and construction

Binary-encoded PARTITION is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete: given positive integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\), decide whether some subset sums to half their total. See [Theorem 6.4 in this complexity text](https://hardness.mit.edu/drafts/2022-09-21.pdf). We may restrict to even total \(W=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\), because doubling every integer preserves the answer and makes the total even.

Define

\[ R=n(W+1)+1,\qquad H=R^{n+1}. \]

Create one type of mass \(1\), and two chore categories \((i,0),(i,1)\) for each \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\). Set

\[ q_{i,0}=q_{i,1}=\tfrac12,\qquad a_{i,0}=H+R^i,\qquad a_{i,1}=H+R^i+w_i. \]

Suppress the type index. The expected burden prescribed by the supplies is the integer

\[ C=a\cdot q=nH+\sum_{i=1}^n R^i+\frac W2. \]

This is a valid input: every burden is positive, all supplies and the type mass are binary-rational, and \(a\cdot q=C>0\).

The construction has polynomial bit length. Indeed, \(\log R=O(\log n+\log W)\), so every burden requires \(O(n(\log n+\log W))\) bits, and there are \(2n\) categories. No benchmark is supplied.

2. Encoding lemma: exact burden forces a partition

Lemma. There exists a nonnegative integral bundle \(B\) with \(a\cdot B=C\) if and only if the PARTITION instance is a yes-instance. Furthermore, every such bundle satisfies

\[ B_{i,0}+B_{i,1}=1 \quad\text{for every }i, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^n w_iB_{i,1}=\frac W2. \]

Proof. First observe that \(nW<R\) and \(n+1<R\). Consequently,

\[ n(R^n+W)<(n+1)R^n<R^{n+1}=H. \]

Writing \(d=\sum_{i=1}^nR^i+W/2\), we therefore have \(0<d<H\), and hence

\[ nH<C<(n+1)H. \]

Suppose \(a\cdot B=C\), and define

\[ k=\sum_{i=1}^n(B_{i,0}+B_{i,1}),\qquad c_i=B_{i,0}+B_{i,1},\qquad r=\sum_{i=1}^n w_iB_{i,1}. \]

Since every chore has burden at least \(H\), we have \(kH\le C<(n+1)H\), so \(k\le n\). Expanding the burden gives

\[ a\cdot B=kH+\sum_{i=1}^n c_iR^i+r. \]

The term after \(kH\) lies between \(0\) and \(k(R^n+W)\), and thus is strictly less than \(H\). Comparing the integer quotients upon division by \(H\) in \(a\cdot B=C=nH+d\) yields \(k=n\). Therefore,

\[ \sum_{i=1}^n c_iR^i+r = \sum_{i=1}^nR^i+\frac W2. \]

Here \(0\le c_i\le n<R\), while \(0\le r\le nW<R\) and \(0\le W/2<R\). Both sides are thus base-\(R\) representations without carries. Uniqueness of that representation gives \(c_i=1\) for every \(i\), and \(r=W/2\).

Thus each \(B_{i,1}\) belongs to \(\{0,1\}\), and the indices with \(B_{i,1}=1\) constitute a partition subset.

Conversely, given a subset \(S\) with \(\sum_{i\in S}w_i=W/2\), set \(B_{i,1}=1\) and \(B_{i,0}=0\) when \(i\in S\), and reverse these values otherwise. Direct substitution gives \(a\cdot B=C\). This proves the lemma.

3. The benchmark detects yes-instances

Let \(b\) denote the benchmark of the single type.

For any finite-support distribution with mean \(q\), linearity gives

\[ \sum_B\pi_B(a\cdot B)=a\cdot q=C. \]

Its maximum supported burden is therefore at least \(C\). Hence \(b\ge C\).

If PARTITION has a solution, let \(B\) be its bundle from the lemma, and let \(\overline B=\mathbf1-B\), where \(\mathbf1\) has every coordinate equal to \(1\). Both bundles are nonnegative integral, and

\[ a\cdot\overline B=a\cdot\mathbf1-a\cdot B=2C-C=C. \]

Giving each bundle probability \(\tfrac12\) produces mean \(q\) and maximum burden \(C\). Thus \(b=C\).

Conversely, if a distribution with mean \(q\) has maximum burden at most \(C\), then every positive-probability bundle must have burden exactly \(C\): their average is \(C\), and any strictly smaller supported burden would make that average smaller. The encoding lemma then produces a partition subset.

We have proved

\[ b=C \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{PARTITION is a yes-instance}. \]

Attainment of \(b\), including on no-instances, is established next.

4. Every constructed instance has a short rational output

The distribution assigning probability \(\tfrac12\) to the empty bundle and probability \(\tfrac12\) to \(\mathbf1\) has mean \(q\) and maximum burden \(2C\).

Because all burdens are integers, the maximum burden of every finite-support distribution is a nonnegative integer. The nonempty set of achievable maxima therefore has a least element \(b\), achieved by some distribution, with \(b\le2C\).

There are only finitely many bundles of burden at most \(b\). Indeed, every coordinate of such a bundle satisfies

\[ B_jH\le a\cdot B\le b\le2C<2(n+1)H, \]

so \(B_j\le2n+1\).

Choose a distribution with mean \(q\), supported on these bundles, whose support size \(s\) is smallest. Its augmented bundle vectors \((1,B)\) are linearly independent. Otherwise, a nonzero dependence would give coefficients summing to zero and preserving every supply coordinate. Such coefficients have both positive and negative entries. Subtracting a suitable positive multiple of them from the positive distribution weights preserves all equations and nonnegativity while making at least one weight zero, contradicting minimality.

Consequently, \(s\le2n+1\). The weights solve a rational linear system with independent columns, so an invertible \(s\)-row subsystem and Cramer’s rule show that all weights are rational.

They also have polynomial bit length. The subsystem’s integer matrix entries are at most \(2n+1\). Multiplying the weights by \(2\) makes its right-hand side integral with entries at most \(2\). The determinants in Cramer’s rule have absolute value at most \(s!(2n+1)^s\), giving \(O(n\log n)\) bits per weight.

These rational weights, used as the single type’s assignment masses, form a valid output. Thus infeasibility cannot be reported on any constructed instance.

5. Recovering PARTITION from any valid output

Suppose a polynomial-time algorithm solves the problem as stated. Run it on the constructed instance and compute

\[ M=\max_{B:x_{1,B}>0}a\cdot B. \]

The output weights form a finite-support distribution with mean \(q\), so the benchmark definition gives \(M\ge b\). Output validity gives \(M\le b\). Therefore \(M=b\).

By Section 3, accept the PARTITION instance exactly when \(M=C\). Construction and postprocessing take polynomial time, and the hypothetical solver’s output has polynomial length because its running time is polynomial.

This would solve PARTITION in polynomial time.

6. Conclusion

The exact mass-assignment output problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard already for one supported type. A solver running in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The hardness concerns producing an allocation satisfying the input-defined benchmark: all instances used in the reduction are feasible.
