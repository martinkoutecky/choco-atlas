The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a candidate set \(C\), committee size \(k\), distance bound \(\ell\), a Thiele rule with OWA function \(\lambda\), and a binary-rational distribution \(\mu\) over pairs of nonempty ballots \((A,B)\), define the stage marginals \(\mu^0_A=\sum_B\mu_{A,B}\) and \(\mu^1_B=\sum_A\mu_{A,B}\). Given a size-\(k\) committee \(S\) maximizing the stage-0 score, does there exist a size-\(k\) committee \(S'\) maximizing the stage-1 score \(F_\lambda(\mu^1,S')=\sum_B\mu^1_B\sum_{j=1}^{|S'\cap B|}\lambda(j)\) with \(k-|S\cap S'|\le\ell\)? For the greedy variant, replace score maximizers with committees obtainable by greedy marginal-score choices, allowing any tie resolution.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here types are pairs \((A,B)\) of nonempty approval ballots contained in \(C\), and \(\sum_{A,B}\mu_{A,B}=1\). The support size \(\tau\) counts listed pairs, not possible types.
For each fixed polynomial-time computable OWA function \(\lambda:\mathbb N^+\to\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\), require \(\lambda(1)=1\) and \(\lambda(j+1)\leq\lambda(j)\).
Define \(\mu^0_A=\sum_B\mu_{A,B}\), \(\mu^1_B=\sum_A\mu_{A,B}\), and \(F_\lambda(\nu,T)=\sum_D\nu_D\sum_{j=1}^{|T\cap D|}\lambda(j)\), with an empty inner sum equal to zero.
An exact winning committee maximizes \(F_\lambda\) over size-\(k\) subsets. A greedy winning committee is obtainable from the empty set by repeatedly adding an unselected candidate maximizing the score increment, for \(k\) steps; every resolution of ties is allowed.
For either variant, the input includes a committee \(S\) promised to win for \(\mu^0\) under that variant. Decide whether some committee \(S'\) winning for \(\mu^1\) under the same variant satisfies \(k-|S\cap S'|\leq\ell\).
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L,k,\ell\).

Classification

For every fixed admissible \(\lambda\), the rational-mass problem has the following classification under polynomial-time many-one reductions:

| OWA function | Exact variant | Greedy variant |
|---|---|---|
| \(\lambda(j)=1\) for every \(j\) | \(\mathsf{P}\) | \(\mathsf{P}\) |
| Any other admissible \(\lambda\) | \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard and \(\mathsf{coNP}\)-hard | \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete |

The exact variant’s \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness requires an additional argument: it does not follow merely from the supplied finite-electorate \(\mathsf{coNP}\)-hardness result. Section 4 gives a direct reduction.

These are classifications with \(k\) and \(\ell\) included in the input. Some restrictions are easy: \(\ell=k\) always gives a yes-instance, as does \(k=m\). Moreover, whenever \(\lambda(j)=1\) for every \(j\leq k\), both variants coincide with Approval Voting.

1. Exact arithmetic and finite-electorate transfer

Write the support as \((A_i,B_i,\mu_i)\), for \(i\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\). Directly from the definition of the marginal,

\( F_\lambda(\mu^1,T) =\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i\sum_{j=1}^{|T\cap B_i|}\lambda(j). \)

Thus all computations can use the listed pairs directly; aggregation of identical second-stage ballots is optional.

To check the arithmetic bounds, write \(\mu_i=p_i/q_i\) and \(\lambda(j)=a_j/d_j\), with positive denominators. Define

\( Q=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}q_i,\qquad D=\prod_{j=1}^{k}d_j,\qquad w_i=p_iQ/q_i,\qquad v_j=a_jD/d_j. \)

Every committee score has the integer representation

\( QD\,F_\lambda(\mu^1,T) =\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\sum_{j=1}^{|T\cap B_i|}v_j. \)

The bit length of \(Q\) is bounded by the input length. Since \(\lambda\) is fixed and polynomial-time computable, the total encoding length of its first \(k\) values is polynomial in \(k\). Also, \(0\leq F_\lambda(\mu^1,T)\leq k\). Consequently, all score and marginal comparisons have polynomial bit complexity in \(L+k\).

Below, \(P_\lambda(L+k)\) denotes a fixed polynomial large enough to cover these arithmetic costs and the computation of \(\lambda(1),\ldots,\lambda(k)\).

Transfer lemma

Consider a finite electorate of \(n\) voters, whose two ballots are \((A_i,B_i)\). For each distinct pair \((A,B)\), let \(n_{A,B}\) be its multiplicity and output the positive mass

\( \mu_{A,B}=n_{A,B}/n. \)

There are at most \(n\) listed pairs, and each mass has encoding length \(O(\log n)\). This transformation is polynomial-time in the explicit finite-electorate input.

For every committee, its rational-mass score equals its finite-electorate score divided by \(n\). The same identity holds for every greedy marginal increment. Therefore:

  • The exact winning committees are preserved.
  • Every greedy maximizing choice, including every tie, is preserved at every step.
  • The promise concerning \(S\), and the distance condition, are preserved.

Hence the supplied finite-electorate hardness results transfer to rational masses. In particular, this proves greedy \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness and exact \(\mathsf{coNP}\)-hardness for every non-AV function. The quoted results appear as Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 4.3 of [Zech, Boehmer, Elkind, and Teh, *Multiwinner Temporal Voting with Aversion to Change*](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11017v1).

The supplied exact \(\mathsf{coW[1]}\)-hardness also transfers, because the transformation leaves \(k\) unchanged.

2. Approval Voting: a polynomial-time algorithm

Suppose \(\lambda(j)=1\) throughout. Define each candidate’s second-stage approval mass by

\( a(c)=\sum_{i:c\in B_i}\mu_i. \)

Then

\( F_\lambda(\mu^1,T)=\sum_{c\in T}a(c). \)

The greedy marginal of \(c\) is always \(a(c)\), independent of previously selected candidates. Thus the exact and greedy winning committees coincide.

Let \(b\) be the \(k\)-th largest approval mass, and define

\( H=\{c:a(c)>b\},\qquad E=\{c:a(c)=b\}. \)

Every winning committee contains all of \(H\), exactly \(k-|H|\) candidates from \(E\), and no other candidates. Indeed, violating this description permits replacing a selected candidate by an unselected candidate with strictly greater approval mass.

The largest overlap with \(S\) among winning committees is therefore

\( r_{\max} =|H\cap S|+\min\{k-|H|,\ |E\cap S|\}. \)

This bound is attainable: choose as many members of \(E\cap S\) as the remaining capacity permits, and then fill any remaining positions from \(E\setminus S\).

The answer is yes precisely when

\( r_{\max}\geq k-\ell. \)

Using a common denominator for the masses, schoolbook integer arithmetic, and sorting, a valid bit-operation bound is

\( O\!\left( L+(m\tau+m\log(m+1)+\tau+k)(L+1)^2 \right). \)

The algorithm uses the listed support; it never expands masses into voters.

3. Greedy membership and general running times

Greedy membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\)

A certificate is an ordered list of distinct candidates \(c_1,\ldots,c_k\). Put \(U_0=\varnothing\) and \(U_t=\{c_1,\ldots,c_t\}\).

At step \(t\), the marginal of an unselected candidate \(c\) is

\( \Delta(c\mid U_{t-1}) =\sum_{i:c\in B_i}\mu_i\lambda(|U_{t-1}\cap B_i|+1). \)

Check that \(c_t\) has maximum marginal among all unselected candidates, permitting equality. Finally, check \(|U_k\cap S|\geq k-\ell\).

These checks take

\( O\!\left(km\tau P_\lambda(L+k)\right) \)

time. Acceptance is equivalent to a greedy winning committee satisfying the distance bound. Combined with the transfer lemma, this proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness for every non-AV greedy rule.

Exact enumeration

Enumerate all \(\binom{m}{k}\) committees. Evaluate each score, and retain the largest overlap with \(S\) among committees achieving the largest score. This gives the bound

\( O\!\left( \left[m\tau+\tau k\binom{m}{k}\right]P_\lambda(L+k) \right). \)

In particular, the exact problem is fixed-parameter tractable in \(m\) and belongs to \(\mathsf{XP}\) parameterized by \(k\).

It also belongs to \(\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}\): binary-search the optimal integer-scaled score using an oracle for the existence of a committee meeting a score threshold, then ask whether a committee achieves that optimum and the required overlap. The search takes polynomially many queries because the scaled scores have polynomial bit length.

Greedy enumeration using \(\ell\)

The number of size-\(k\) committees within distance \(\ell\) of \(S\) is

\( N_\ell =\sum_{r=0}^{\min\{\ell,m-k\}} \binom{k}{r}\binom{m-k}{r}. \)

For each such committee \(T\), test whether it is greedy-obtainable by dynamic programming over subsets of \(T\). Mark \(\varnothing\) reachable. From a reachable \(U\subsetneq T\), mark \(U\cup\{c\}\) reachable for every \(c\in T\setminus U\) whose marginal is maximal among all of \(C\setminus U\).

This dynamic program is correct because all future marginals depend on the selected set \(U\), rather than its selection order. Its transitions are exactly the permitted greedy additions. Thus \(T\) is obtainable if and only if its full-set state is reachable.

The resulting running time is

\( O\!\left( \left[m\tau+N_\ell 2^k m\tau\right]P_\lambda(L+k) \right). \)

Alternatively, applying the same dynamic program to all subsets of \(C\) of size at most \(k\) gives

\( O\!\left( m\tau \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{m}{i} \,P_\lambda(L+k) \right). \)

These bounds again depend on the listed support size \(\tau\), rather than the number of possible ballot pairs.

4. Direct \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness for the exact variant

Fix an admissible function \(\lambda\) that is not AV. Let \(s\geq1\) be the smallest index satisfying

\( \lambda(s+1)<1. \)

By monotonicity and minimality,

\( \lambda(1)=\cdots=\lambda(s)=1,\qquad \delta:=1-\lambda(s+1)>0. \)

Both \(s\) and \(\delta\) are constants associated with the fixed rule.

We reduce from Independent Set. Its input is a simple graph \(G=(V,E_G)\), with \(n=|V|\), and a target \(\kappa\) satisfying \(1\leq\kappa\leq n\). Restricting to these bounds retains \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness, since instances outside them are trivial.

4.1 Candidate sets and comparison graph

Add a new vertex \(p\), isolated from \(V\), and put

\( X=V\cup\{p\},\qquad r=\kappa+1. \)

Let \(D\) be a disjoint set of \(r\) dummy vertices. Construct a graph \(H\) on \(X\cup D\) with:

  • the original edges \(E_G\) inside \(V\);
  • every edge between \(X\) and \(D\);
  • no edges inside \(D\), and no additional edges inside \(X\).

Write \(h=|X\cup D|\).

Finally, add a disjoint set \(F\) of \(s-1\) padding candidates. The election’s candidate set and committee size are

\( C=X\cup D\cup F,\qquad k=r+s-1=\kappa+s. \)

Choose any \(\kappa\)-element subset \(R\subseteq V\), and supply

\( S=F\cup\{p\}\cup R,\qquad \ell=r-1=\kappa. \)

Thus \(S\) has the required size \(k\).

4.2 Second-stage ballots and normalization

First specify unnormalized integer ballot weights:

  • For each edge \(\{u,v\}\) of \(H\), give ballot \(F\cup\{u,v\}\) weight \(1\).
  • For each vertex \(v\) of \(H\), give ballot \(F\cup\{v\}\) weight \(h-\deg_H(v)\).

Every weight in the second group is positive because \(\deg_H(v)\leq h-1\). Let \(W\) be the total weight of these two groups, and put \(M=W+1\).

  • For each \(f\in F\), give singleton ballot \(\{f\}\) weight \(M\).

Let \(Z=W+(s-1)M\) be the total weight. For each weighted second-stage ballot \(B\) of weight \(w\), output the type

\( (A,B)=(S,B),\qquad \mu_{S,B}=w/Z. \)

All ballots are nonempty, all listed masses are positive, and their sum is \(1\). If any types coincide, combine their weights.

Every first-stage ballot is \(S\). Hence for every size-\(k\) committee \(T\),

\( F_\lambda(\mu^0,T) =\sum_{j=1}^{|T\cap S|}\lambda(j) \leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda(j) =F_\lambda(\mu^0,S), \)

because all weights \(\lambda(j)\) are nonnegative. The promised first-stage optimality of \(S\) is therefore satisfied.

Multiplying second-stage scores by \(Z\) does not change their maximizers. We henceforth use these unnormalized scores.

4.3 Every exact winner contains all padding candidates

Suppose a size-\(k\) committee \(T\) omits \(f\in F\). Since \(k>|F|\), it contains some candidate \(x\notin F\). Replace \(x\) by \(f\).

The singleton ballot \(\{f\}\) increases the score by exactly \(M\). The other padding singleton ballots have unchanged scores.

For each of the first two ballot groups, replacing one candidate by another changes its approval count by at most one. Its score can consequently decrease by at most its weight, because every marginal weight lies in \([0,1]\). The total loss from those groups is at most \(W\).

The replacement therefore increases the score by at least

\( M-W=1>0. \)

Thus an exact winner cannot omit any member of \(F\). For \(s=1\), this assertion is vacuous.

4.4 Exact score formula after padding

Consider a committee \(F\cup T\), where \(T\subseteq X\cup D\) and \(|T|=r\).

Every ballot in the first two groups receives a constant contribution \(s-1\) from \(F\). Beyond this:

  • a selected endpoint contributes \(1=\lambda(s)\);
  • when both endpoints of an edge ballot are selected, their combined contribution is \(1+\lambda(s+1)=2-\delta\).

Therefore an edge ballot’s contribution beyond padding is

\( |T\cap\{u,v\}|-\delta\,\mathbf{1}[\{u,v\}\subseteq T]. \)

The weighted linear contribution of each selected vertex \(v\) is

\( \deg_H(v)+(h-\deg_H(v))=h. \)

Let \(e_H(T)\) denote the number of edges induced by \(T\) in \(H\). Adding all contributions gives

\( \operatorname{score}(F\cup T) =(s-1)W+(s-1)M+rh-\delta e_H(T). \)

The first three terms are independent of \(T\). Since \(\delta>0\), maximizing the score is equivalent to minimizing \(e_H(T)\).

The dummy set \(D\) has size \(r\) and induces no edges. Together with Section 4.3, this proves the exact characterization:

The second-stage exact winning committees are precisely \(F\cup T\), where \(T\) is an independent set of size \(r\) in \(H\).

4.5 Equivalence to Independent Set

Because every vertex of \(X\) is adjacent to every vertex of \(D\), an independent set of size \(r\geq2\) cannot meet both sets. Consequently, a size-\(r\) independent set in \(H\) is either \(D\) or lies entirely in \(X\).

If \(G\) has an independent set \(I\subseteq V\) of size \(\kappa\), then \(I\cup\{p\}\) is an independent set of size \(r\) in \(X\). Thus

\( S'=F\cup I\cup\{p\} \)

is an exact winner. It shares at least \(F\cup\{p\}\) with \(S\), so

\( k-|S\cap S'| \leq k-s =\kappa =\ell. \)

The constructed instance is therefore a yes-instance.

Conversely, suppose \(G\) has no independent set of size \(\kappa\). Then \(X\) has no independent set of size \(\kappa+1\): deleting \(p\), if present, from such a set would leave at least \(\kappa\) independent vertices of \(G\).

The only size-\(r\) independent set in \(H\) is consequently \(D\), so the unique second-stage exact winner is \(F\cup D\). Its overlap with \(S\) is exactly \(F\), and hence

\( k-|S\cap(F\cup D)| =k-(s-1) =r =\kappa+1 >\ell. \)

The constructed instance is a no-instance. This proves both directions of the reduction.

4.6 Encoding size

For fixed \(\lambda\), \(s\) is constant. The construction has

\( m=n+\kappa+s+1=O_\lambda(n),\qquad \tau=O_\lambda(n^2). \)

Furthermore,

\( W=|E(H)|+\sum_v(h-\deg_H(v)) =h^2-|E(H)| \leq h^2. \)

Thus \(M\) and \(Z\) have \(O_\lambda(\log(n+1))\) bits, as do every mass numerator and denominator. Ballots and the whole support have polynomial encoding length. The reduction is therefore polynomial-time.

This completes the missing \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness proof for every non-AV exact rule. It also preserves the parameter up to the fixed additive constant \(s\), since \(k=\kappa+s\).

5. Conclusion

Binary rational masses preserve the finite-electorate hardness results, and their scores admit polynomial-bit exact comparisons. Approval Voting remains polynomial-time solvable for both variants. For every other admissible OWA function, the exact variant is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard by the direct reduction above, and the greedy variant is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete.
