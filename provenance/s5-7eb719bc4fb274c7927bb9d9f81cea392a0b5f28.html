Equivalence of masses and probabilities

Let \(T\) be the explicitly listed type set, with masses \(\mu_t\), and suppose \(M=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t>0\). Define type probabilities \(f(t)=\mu_t/M\).

For a reported type \(t\), let \(x_{tj}\) be its probability of receiving item \(j\), and let \(p_t\) be its expected payment. A total supply constraint
\(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_{tj}\le S_j\)
is equivalent, by division by \(M\), to the ex ante allocation cap
\(\sum_{t\in T}f(t)x_{tj}\le S_j/M\).

Likewise, total revenue satisfies
\(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p_t=M\sum_{t\in T}f(t)p_t\).
Multiplication by the positive constant \(M\) preserves the maximizing mechanisms.

For additive, risk-neutral types with valuation vectors \(v_t\), truthfulness in expectation requires
\(v_t\cdot x_t-p_t\ge v_t\cdot x_s-p_s\)
for every pair \(t,s\in T\). These inequalities are unchanged by normalizing the masses. Thus feasibility, incentives, and optimality are preserved.
