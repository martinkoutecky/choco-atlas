Proposition 4 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a continuum consortium with scale \(H>0\), type support \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, per-member quantities \(q_t>0\), and \(P(z)=a-bz\ge0\) on \([0,H\bar q]\), where \(\bar q=\sum_t\mu_tq_t\), define \(x_\nu=\sum_t\nu_tq_t\) and normalized coalition cost \(C_H(\nu)=x_\nu P(Hx_\nu)\) for \(0\le\nu\le\mu\). Compute exact per-member allocation densities \(r_t\) equal to the Aumann–Shapley path allocation, and determine whether they are efficient and coalitionally stable: \(\sum_t\mu_tr_t=C_H(\mu)\) and \(\sum_t\nu_tr_t\le C_H(\nu)\) for every \(\nu\le\mu\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(T\): the list of its \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There is one commodity, \(m=1\). Inputs \(H>0\), \(q_t>0\), \(a>0\), and \(b\ge0\) are binary rationals; \(L\) is their total encoding length together with the support. Put \(\bar q=\sum_t\mu_tq_t\) and require \(a\ge2bH\bar q\), so total purchasing cost is nondecreasing on the feasible interval.
A coalition is any real vector \(\nu\) satisfying \(0\le\nu_t\le\mu_t\). Its normalized cost is \(C_H(\nu)=x_\nu P(Hx_\nu)\), where \(x_\nu=\sum_t\nu_tq_t\), \(P(z)=a-bz\), and \(C_H(0)=0\).
The Aumann–Shapley allocation density is \(r_t=\int_0^1\partial_{\nu_t}C_H(s\mu)\,ds\); the total allocation to type \(t\) is \(\mu_tr_t\). Efficiency means \(\sum_t\mu_tr_t=C_H(\mu)\), and coalitional stability means \(\sum_t\nu_tr_t\le C_H(\nu)\) for every coalition.

Can these densities be computed exactly and their efficiency and coalitional stability decided in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finite purchasing games with linear discounts, the equal-price allocation \(q_iP(\sum_jq_j)\) equals both the Shapley value and the nucleolus and belongs to the core. [García-Martínez, Meca and Vergara](https://doi.org/10.3390/math10224195).
Divisible-demand cost-sharing literature gives the Aumann–Shapley path-integral definition; no result settling this exact support-encoded computational problem was verified. [Wang](https://research.sabanciuniv.edu/311/1/30118000001012.pdf).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified binary-rational encoding for scale, quantities, and price coefficients, and defined the total input bit length.
  • Specified a positive price intercept and a nonnegative discount slope, allowing constant prices as the linear-discount definition does.
  • Restored the general purchasing model's limited-growth condition on the feasible interval: for a linear price it becomes a ≥ 2bH times the mean quantity.
  • Defined the allocation densities through differentiation with respect to normalized coalition masses, so type allocations are mass times density.
  • Allowed all real partial coalitions, including the empty coalition, and set its cost to zero.
  • Set m=1 because the model has one commodity; permitted a single supported type because a continuum can contain many members of that type.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. For every admissible input, the densities are exactly computable, efficient, and coalitionally stable.

  • Exact computation of the densities.

Write \(\bar q=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tq_t\). The given cost function is the polynomial
\[ C_H(\nu)=ax_\nu-bHx_\nu^2, \]
where \(x_\nu=\sum_{t\in T}\nu_tq_t\). Its polynomial extension supplies derivatives even at boundary points. By the chain rule,
\[ \partial_{\nu_t}C_H(\nu)=q_t(a-2bHx_\nu). \]
Along the prescribed path, \(x_{s\mu}=s\bar q\). Consequently, the stipulated path-integral definition—also given as formula (3) in [Wang](https://research.sabanciuniv.edu/311/1/30118000001012.pdf)—yields
\[ r_t=\int_0^1q_t(a-2bHs\bar q)\,ds =q_t(a-bH\bar q) =q_tP(H\bar q). \]
Thus each density is rational and has an explicit exact formula.

  • Efficiency.

Substituting the computed densities gives
\[ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_tr_t =(a-bH\bar q)\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tq_t =a\bar q-bH\bar q^2 =C_H(\mu). \]
This proves efficiency.

  • Coalitional stability for every real coalition.

Let \(\nu\) be any real vector satisfying \(0\le\nu_t\le\mu_t\). Since every \(q_t>0\), summing these inequalities after multiplication by \(q_t\) gives
\[ 0\le x_\nu\le\bar q. \]
The coalition’s allocation is
\[ \sum_{t\in T}\nu_tr_t=x_\nu(a-bH\bar q). \]
Subtracting this allocation from its cost gives the exact identity
\[ C_H(\nu)-\sum_{t\in T}\nu_tr_t =ax_\nu-bHx_\nu^2-x_\nu(a-bH\bar q) =bHx_\nu(\bar q-x_\nu). \]
Every factor on the right is nonnegative: \(b\ge0\), \(H>0\), and \(0\le x_\nu\le\bar q\). Therefore
\[ \sum_{t\in T}\nu_tr_t\le C_H(\nu) \]
for every feasible real coalition, proving coalitional stability.

This includes \(b=0\), when equality holds for every coalition. If \(b>0\), the inequality is strict whenever \(0<x_\nu<\bar q\). The promised condition \(a\ge2bH\bar q\) also ensures that \(a-2bHx\ge0\) throughout the feasible interval, as required for nondecreasing total cost.

  • Polynomial bit complexity.

Compute \(\bar q\) by exact rational multiplication and addition, then compute \(a-bH\bar q\), and finally multiply this common factor by each \(q_t\). This requires \(O(\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations.

To bound their bit cost, represent each input rational by an integer numerator and positive integer denominator. A common denominator for \(\bar q\) is the product of the denominators of all \(\mu_t\) and \(q_t\). Its bit length is \(O(L)\), because the sum of those input denominator lengths is at most \(L\). After conversion to this denominator, each summand’s numerator has \(O(L)\) bits; summing \(\tau\) such numerators uses \(O(L+\log\tau)\) bits. Subsequent formulas involve only a fixed number of products and differences of these quantities and input rationals, so their bit lengths remain \(O(L+\log\tau)\).

Integer arithmetic and rational reduction on numbers of these lengths take polynomial time. Hence the full output has polynomial length and is computable in time polynomial in \(\tau,L\), and therefore in \(m,\tau,L\) for the stipulated \(m=1\). Efficiency and stability always receive an affirmative answer on admissible inputs, justified by the identities above; no coalition enumeration is required.
