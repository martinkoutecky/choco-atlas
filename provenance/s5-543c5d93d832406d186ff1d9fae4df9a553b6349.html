The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support of complete agent types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), utilities \(u_{tj}\), and disagreement utilities \(c_t\), each type-\(t\) agent receives \(x_{tj}\ge 0\) units of good \(j\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}\le b_j\) and \(v_t=\sum_j u_{tj}x_{tj}\). Is there an exact algorithm polynomial in \(g\), \(|T|\), and the binary input length that decides whether \(v_t>c_t\) for every type, and, when feasible, computes an allocation maximizing \(\sum_t\mu_t\log(v_t-c_t)\) with supporting prices, or otherwise returns an infeasibility certificate?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) complete types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=g\ge1\) divisible goods; \(L\) is the total binary input length.
Each type specifies rational utilities \(u_{tj}\ge0\) and a disagreement utility \(c_t\ge0\); supplies \(b_j>0\) are rational. Every type values some good positively, and every good is valued positively by some type. Disagreement utilities need not be jointly attainable.
An allocation gives each type the same per-capita bundle \(x_{tj}\ge0\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}\le b_j\) and \(v_t=\sum_j u_{tj}x_{tj}\). Feasibility means \(v_t>c_t\) for every type.
For feasible instances, the objective is \(\sum_t\mu_t\log(v_t-c_t)\). Supporting prices satisfy \(p_j\ge0\), \(p_j(b_j-\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj})=0\), and \(p_j\ge u_{tj}/(v_t-c_t)\), with equality whenever \(x_{tj}>0\). Any maximizing allocation is permitted.
The computational problem decides feasibility and returns an exact rational maximizing allocation and supporting prices when feasible, or a rational infeasibility certificate otherwise; it need not output the logarithmic objective value.

Is this decision-and-search problem solvable exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Finite symmetric ADNB admits a polynomial-time algorithm for feasibility and exact allocation and price computation ([Vazirani](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/NBalg.pdf)).
Finite nonsymmetric ADNB, with arbitrary positive objective weights, also admits an exact polynomial-time algorithm ([Végh](https://www.laszlovegh.eu/papers/vegh-concave-genflow.pdf)); no theorem explicitly stated for this distribution-support input was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted utilities and disagreement utilities to nonnegative rationals, following the final ADNB definition.
  • Required positive supplies and nonempty type and good sets, and retained the condition that every type and every good participates in a positive utility entry.
  • Allowed disagreement utilities that are not jointly attainable, following the generalized ADNB definition.
  • Defined supporting prices as multipliers of the mass-weighted supply constraints, fixing their normalization through the displayed conditions.
  • Interpreted exact computation as returning rational allocation and price coordinates, without requiring an exact representation of the logarithmic objective value.
  • Allowed any maximizing allocation, since allocations need not be unique; set the general size parameter to the number of goods.

1. Conclusion and finite theorem used.

The problem is solvable exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The reduction uses one buyer per supported type; it does not expand rational masses into populations.

We use the finite nonsymmetric ADNB theorem supplied in the question: with unit supplies, nonnegative integer utilities and disagreement utilities, and positive integer objective weights, an exact rational maximizing allocation can be computed in polynomial time whenever the instance is feasible. Every buyer and good must have a positive utility incident to it. Végh’s §8.1 and Theorem 8 use these hypotheses without requiring joint attainability of the disagreement utilities; the paragraph following Theorem 8 discusses implementation with rational numbers of polynomial encoding length. [Végh, *Concave Generalized Flows with Applications to Market Equilibria*](https://www.laszlovegh.eu/papers/vegh-concave-genflow.pdf).

We establish the reduction, feasibility certificates, and supporting prices explicitly.

2. Exact feasibility testing and rational certificates.

Consider the rational linear program

\( \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad &\delta\\ \text{subject to}\quad &\sum_j u_{tj}x_{tj}-\delta\ge c_t &&\text{for every }t,\\ &\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}\le b_j &&\text{for every }j,\\ &x_{tj}\ge0,\qquad \delta\in\mathbb R. \end{aligned} \)

Its resource-feasible allocation set is compact: each coordinate satisfies \(0\le x_{tj}\le b_j/\mu_t\). The program is feasible by taking \(x=0\) and \(\delta=-\max_t c_t\). Its optimum is therefore attained and finite.

Write its optimal value as \(\delta^*\). An allocation with all \(v_t>c_t\) gives the positive margin \(\min_t(v_t-c_t)\), so \(\delta^*>0\). Conversely, an LP solution with \(\delta>0\) is feasible for the task. Thus feasibility is equivalent to \(\delta^*>0\).

The dual is

\( \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad &\sum_j b_jq_j-\sum_t c_t\lambda_t\\ \text{subject to}\quad &\mu_tq_j\ge\lambda_tu_{tj} &&\text{for every }t,j,\\ &\sum_t\lambda_t=1,\qquad \lambda_t\ge0,\quad q_j\ge0. \end{aligned} \)

Standard exact polynomial-time rational linear programming applies: both programs have polynomially many variables and constraints and rational coefficients of polynomial encoding length. Strong duality gives a rational optimal dual solution.

When \(\delta^*\le0\), return such a dual solution. It is an independently verifiable infeasibility certificate. Indeed, every resource-feasible allocation satisfies

\( \sum_t\lambda_t(v_t-c_t) \le \sum_jq_j\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}-\sum_t\lambda_tc_t \le \sum_jb_jq_j-\sum_t\lambda_tc_t \le0. \)

If every \(v_t-c_t>0\), the left side would be positive because \(\lambda_t\ge0\) and \(\sum_t\lambda_t=1\). This contradiction certifies infeasibility, including cases where only weak improvement is attainable.

3. Reduction to finite nonsymmetric ADNB.

Henceforth suppose the feasibility test succeeds. Define

\( z_{tj}=\frac{\mu_t x_{tj}}{b_j}, \qquad a_{tj}=\frac{u_{tj}b_j}{\mu_t}. \)

Because \(\mu_t,b_j>0\), this is a bijective change of allocation variables, with inverse \(x_{tj}=b_jz_{tj}/\mu_t\). The constraints and utilities become

\( z_{tj}\ge0,\qquad \sum_tz_{tj}\le1,\qquad v_t=\sum_ja_{tj}z_{tj}. \)

Thus there are \(\tau\) finite buyers, \(m\) unit-supply goods, disagreement utilities \(c_t\), and objective weights \(\mu_t\).

To obtain integer data, let \(H\) be the product of the denominators of the masses, and put \(w_t=H\mu_t\). These are positive integers. For each \(t\), let \(D_t\) be the product of the denominators of \(c_t,a_{t1},\ldots,a_{tm}\), and put

\( A_{tj}=D_ta_{tj},\qquad C_t=D_tc_t. \)

The finite integer-data problem maximizes

\( G(z)=\sum_tw_t\log\left(\sum_jA_{tj}z_{tj}-C_t\right) \)

under the unit-supply constraints. Its surplus for buyer \(t\) is \(D_t(v_t-c_t)\), so its strict feasibility condition is exactly the original one. Furthermore,

\( G(z) = H\sum_t\mu_t\log(v_t-c_t) + H\sum_t\mu_t\log D_t. \)

The second term is independent of \(z\), and \(H>0\). Consequently, the two problems have exactly the same maximizing allocations under the change of variables.

All hypotheses of the finite theorem hold. The weights \(w_t\) are positive, utilities and disagreements are nonnegative integers, and \(A_{tj}>0\) exactly when \(u_{tj}>0\). Thus every buyer and good has a positive utility incident to it.

The transformation also preserves polynomial encoding size. Each \(a_{tj}=u_{tj}b_j/\mu_t\) has encoding length \(O(L)\). The binary length of a product of positive integer denominators is at most the sum of their binary lengths. Hence \(\log H=O(L)\), each \(\log D_t=O((m+1)L)\), and the whole transformed instance has length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). These products are computed in binary; no replication by \(H\) occurs.

Apply the finite theorem to obtain an exact rational maximizing \(z^*\), and return

\( x^*_{tj}=\frac{b_jz^*_{tj}}{\mu_t}. \)

This allocation is rational, has polynomial encoding length, and maximizes the original objective.

4. Computing the required supporting prices.

We can compute prices directly from the returned allocation. Let

\( s_t=v_t^*-c_t>0, \qquad p_j=\max_t\frac{u_{tj}}{s_t}. \)

These prices are rational and polynomial-time computable. They are positive because every good has some positive utility.

We prove the required equalities. In the normalized variables, the original objective is

\( F(z)=\sum_t\mu_t\log\left(\sum_ja_{tj}z_{tj}-c_t\right), \)

and at \(z^*\),

\( \frac{\partial F}{\partial z_{tj}} = \frac{\mu_ta_{tj}}{s_t} = b_j\frac{u_{tj}}{s_t}. \)

First, every good is fully allocated. Otherwise, some good \(j\) has \(\sum_tz^*_{tj}<1\). Giving a positive amount of the unused good to a type with \(u_{tj}>0\) preserves feasibility and strictly increases the objective, contradicting optimality. Therefore

\( \sum_t\mu_tx^*_{tj}=b_j. \)

Next, suppose \(z^*_{tj}>0\), and let \(r\) be any type. Transfer an amount \(\varepsilon>0\) of good \(j\) from \(t\) to \(r\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), the donor’s allocation stays nonnegative and its surplus remains positive; all resource constraints are preserved. Optimality implies that the directional derivative is nonpositive:

\( b_j\left(\frac{u_{rj}}{s_r}-\frac{u_{tj}}{s_t}\right)\le0. \)

Since \(b_j>0\) and \(r\) was arbitrary, \(u_{tj}/s_t=p_j\). Also, \(x^*_{tj}>0\) is equivalent to \(z^*_{tj}>0\).

We have therefore established all stipulated price conditions:

\( p_j\ge0,\qquad p_j\left(b_j-\sum_t\mu_tx^*_{tj}\right)=0,\qquad p_j\ge\frac{u_{tj}}{v_t^*-c_t}, \)

with equality in the last inequality whenever \(x^*_{tj}>0\).

5. Resulting algorithm and complexity.

The algorithm first solves the rational margin LP. If its optimum is nonpositive, it returns the rational dual certificate. Otherwise, it constructs the polynomial-size finite integer ADNB instance, computes an exact rational optimum, transforms the allocation back, and computes prices by the displayed maximum formula.

Every step takes time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The algorithm outputs neither logarithms nor the logarithmic objective value.
