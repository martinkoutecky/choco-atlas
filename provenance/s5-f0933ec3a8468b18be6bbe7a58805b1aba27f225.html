The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), seat count \(k\), a rational-mass distribution \(\mu\) over submitted ballot types, fixed tie-breaking and weighted-inclusive Gregory rules, and a target RCV structure \(\sigma\), find the minimum added ballot mass \(\sum_b x_b\), where \(x_b\geq 0\), such that running RCV on \(\mu+x\) yields \(\sigma\). The quota is the total mass divided by \(k+1\). The instance may restrict added ballots by a ranking-length cap or require that specified ballot prefixes still yield \(\sigma\) under every legal suffix.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq1\) candidates and let \(1\leq k\leq m\). Ballot types are strict lists of distinct candidates, possibly partial or empty. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Assume \(\mu(C)>0\), where here \(\mu(C)\) denotes total ballot mass; masses need not sum to one.
For added masses \(x_b\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}\), fix the quota throughout counting at \(Q=(\mu(C)+\sum_b x_b)/(k+1)\). Each ballot's current weight supports its first active candidate. If the maximum tally \(v\) satisfies \(v\geq Q\), remove that candidate as a round winner and multiply each supporting ballot's current weight by \((v-Q)/v\) before transferring it; otherwise remove a minimum-tally candidate and transfer its supporting weights unchanged. Transfers skip inactive candidates and exhaust when no active preference remains. A supplied priority order resolves all tally ties.
Perform \(m-1\) removals, placing each round winner in the highest vacant position of an order \(f\), and each eliminated candidate in the lowest; place the remaining candidate in the vacant position. The structure is \(\sigma=(f,s)\), where \(s\in\{W,L\}^{m-1}\) records removal outcomes; the first \(k\) positions of \(f\) are the election winners.
Given a target structure and a binary rational budget \(B\geq0\), exact budget feasibility asks whether additions with \(\sum_b x_b\leq B\) realize that structure. Additions may use types absent from the original support, including empty ballots, whose mass counts toward the quota.
Consider unrestricted additions, additions restricted to length at most \(\ell\), and suffix-robust additions: choose prescribed prefixes and their masses so that every completion preserves the target structure. Every prefix mass may be split arbitrarily among suffixes listing previously unranked candidates, including the empty suffix; only added ballots are completed. An optional cap \(1\leq\ell\leq m\) bounds completed ballot length. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is exact budget feasibility in \(\mathrm P\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for each of these continuous ballot-addition variants, with running time measured in \(m,\tau,L,k,\ell\)?

For integer ballot additions to a specified structure, polynomial-time optimal algorithms are claimed for unrestricted, suffix-robust, and length-restricted strategies ([Deshpande–Garg–Jacobson](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42001-026-00470-7)). The foundational formulation explicitly keeps additions integral despite fractional transfers ([Optimal Strategies in Ranked Choice Voting](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13661v2)); no complexity result for the exact continuous variants above was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Used arbitrary positive total original mass rather than probability normalization, matching ballot counts; added masses range over nonnegative reals.
  • Made empty ballots legal, including as additions, because the foundational definition explicitly uses empty-ballot padding to raise the quota.
  • Specified maximum-tally winner selection, exact transfers without rounding, exhaustion, fixed-quota counting, and tie resolution; preserved the threshold comparison at equality.
  • Resolved the structure's final-round convention by recording the first m−1 removals and placing the remaining candidate in the sole vacant position, preserving the complete-order formulation.
  • Expressed the minimum-addition question through exact rational-budget feasibility, preserving actual realizability when a continuous minimum is unattained.
  • Interpreted suffix robustness as optimizing prescribed prefixes, with universal independent completions of all portions of their masses; original ballots remain fixed.
  • When robustness and a length cap are combined, applied the cap to completed ballots; without a cap, suffixes may extend to all remaining candidates.

The following results are proved below:

  • All three variants belong to $\exists\mathbb R$ through polynomial-size quadratic formulations.
  • Suffix-robust feasibility is equivalent to feasibility using additions of exactly the maximum permitted length.
  • All three variants admit polynomial-time feasibility tests when the transfer multipliers of the early winners are prescribed rational numbers. This includes testing for strategies in which every early winner has zero surplus.
  • Unrestricted and uncapped suffix-robust feasibility are polynomial-time solvable when no original ballot supports an early winner along the prescribed trajectory.
  • Length-restricted and suffix-robust feasibility with $\ell=1$ are polynomial-time solvable when original ballots supporting early winners immediately exhaust.
  • Every feasible strategy can be replaced by one using at most $m(m+1)/2$ added types.
  • Conservation gives necessary restrictions on the number and placement of round wins.

Here an early winner means a winner whose removal precedes another counted decision, namely a winning removal in one of rounds $1,\ldots,m-2$.

The distinction from the cited integral formulations matters: the [foundational paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13661v2) explicitly keeps additions integral despite fractional transfers, and [Proposition 1 of the later paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42001-026-00470-7) claims polynomial-time extensions. Those statements do not establish a complexity classification for the exact continuous problems here.

1. The prescribed trajectory and its exact inequalities

Let $M>0$ be the original total mass. A valid target structure determines a removal order
$$ c_1,\ldots,c_{m-1} $$
and a remaining candidate $c_m$: at a prescribed win, take the candidate in the highest vacant position of $f$; at an elimination, take the candidate in the lowest vacant position.

Immediately before round $r$, the prescribed active set is
$$ A_r=\{c_r,\ldots,c_m\}. $$

Write $v_{r,j}$ for the tally of $c_j$ in round $r$, where $j\geq r$. Write $c_i\prec c_j$ when the supplied priority order selects $c_i$ in a tally tie with $c_j$.

For a prescribed win in round $r$, the exact conditions are
$$ v_{r,r}\geq Q, $$
together with, for every $j>r$,
$$ \begin{cases} v_{r,r}\geq v_{r,j},&c_r\prec c_j,\\ v_{r,r}>v_{r,j},&c_j\prec c_r. \end{cases} $$

For a prescribed elimination, the exact conditions are
$$ v_{r,j}<Q\qquad\text{for every }j\geq r, $$
together with, for every $j>r$,
$$ \begin{cases} v_{r,r}\leq v_{r,j},&c_r\prec c_j,\\ v_{r,r}<v_{r,j},&c_j\prec c_r. \end{cases} $$

These conditions are necessary and sufficient for that round. The quota comparisons determine whether the rule selects a winner or eliminates a candidate. The pairwise comparisons determine which candidate is selected, including the prescribed tie rule. Pairwise comparisons exclude self-comparisons.

If $m=1$, there are no decisions, and the unique valid structure is feasible with zero additions. Henceforth assume $m\geq2$.

2. Suffix robustness is equivalent to exact-length additions

Proposition 1. For $1\leq\ell\leq m$, suffix-robust feasibility with completed length bounded by $\ell$ is equivalent to ordinary feasibility using only added ballots of length exactly $\ell$. Without a cap, use $\ell=m$.

Proof. Suppose a robust prefix strategy is feasible. Extend every positive-mass prefix to length exactly $\ell$ by appending distinct previously unranked candidates. Assign all its mass to that particular extension. This is an allowed completion, so robustness guarantees that the resulting additions realize the target. Their total mass is unchanged.

Conversely, prescribe every ballot of a feasible exact-length strategy as a prefix. Under the cap, a prefix of length $\ell$ admits only the empty suffix. Without a cap, a full ranking admits only the empty suffix because every candidate is already ranked. The strategy is therefore suffix-robust. $\square$

This proposition uses the permission to choose prefixes freely. It does not apply to a problem with an externally supplied prefix menu.

3. A polynomial-size quadratic formulation

I construct a formulation for additions of length at most $\ell$, then modify it for exact length $\ell$.

3.1 Increasing histories

Along the prescribed trajectory, the candidates successively supported by a ballot have strictly increasing removal indices. After $c_i$ is removed, its next active preference, if any, belongs to $\{c_{i+1},\ldots,c_m\}$.

Deleting preferences that are skipped because they are already inactive leaves the counting trajectory unchanged. Thus an added ballot can be represented by an increasing history of visited candidates. A ballot of length at most $\ell$ visits at most $\ell$ candidates.

Only transfers in rounds $1,\ldots,m-2$ affect a later counted decision. The formulation therefore omits all transfers and bookkeeping updates after the final decision.

3.2 Variables and conservation

Introduce nonnegative variables:

  • $a_j$ for initial added mass supporting $c_j$;
  • $a_{\varnothing}$ for added empty-ballot mass;
  • $F_{i,h,j}$ for weight transferred from $c_i$, as the $h$th visited candidate, to $c_j$, where $i\leq m-2$, $j>i$, and $h<\ell$;
  • $E_{i,h}$ for weight exhausted after removing $c_i$, where $i\leq m-2$ and $h\leq\ell$;
  • $z_{i,h}$ for added weight at $c_i$ in layer $h$ immediately before its removal.

Define total added mass $H$ and quota $Q$ by
$$ H=a_{\varnothing}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_j,\qquad 0\leq H\leq B,\qquad (k+1)Q=M+H. $$
In particular, $Q>0$.

Incoming weights satisfy
$$ z_{i,1}=a_i, $$
and, for $2\leq h\leq\ell$,
$$ z_{i,h} = \sum_{\substack{p<i\\p\leq m-2}}F_{p,h-1,i}. $$

For each $i\leq m-2$, introduce $\alpha_i\in[0,1]$. At an elimination impose
$$ \alpha_i=1. $$
At a win impose
$$ \alpha_i v_{i,i}=v_{i,i}-Q. $$
Because the round conditions require $v_{i,i}\geq Q>0$, this equation gives exactly
$$ \alpha_i=\frac{v_{i,i}-Q}{v_{i,i}}. $$

For each $i\leq m-2$ and $h\leq\ell$, impose
$$ E_{i,h}+\sum_{j>i}F_{i,h,j} = \alpha_i z_{i,h}, $$
where the sum is absent when $h=\ell$.

3.3 Original ballots and tallies

Index the original support by $t=1,\ldots,\tau$, with masses $\mu_t$. Let $p_t(r)$ be the first candidate on type $t$ belonging to $A_r$, or $\bot$ if none exists. These values depend only on the original lists and the prescribed trajectory.

Introduce original bookkeeping weights $u_{t,r}\geq0$, for $1\leq r\leq m-1$, with
$$ u_{t,1}=\mu_t. $$
For $i\leq m-2$, impose
$$ u_{t,i+1} = \begin{cases} \alpha_i u_{t,i},&p_t(i)=c_i,\\ u_{t,i},&p_t(i)\neq c_i. \end{cases} $$
A bookkeeping weight with $p_t(r)=\bot$ contributes to no tally.

For every counted round $r$ and every $j\geq r$, impose
$$ v_{r,j} = \sum_{t:\,p_t(r)=c_j}u_{t,r} + a_j + \sum_{\substack{i<r\\1\leq h<\ell}}F_{i,h,j}. $$

The added terms are correct because weight transferred to $c_j$ remains there until $c_j$ is removed. Finally impose the exact round inequalities from Section 1.

3.4 Exact-length modification

For additions of length exactly $\ell$, additionally impose
$$ a_{\varnothing}=0 $$
and
$$ E_{i,h}=0 \qquad\text{whenever }i<\ell. $$

The latter condition is necessary: after removal $i<\ell$, fewer than $\ell$ candidates are inactive. A ballot containing $\ell$ distinct candidates must therefore still contain an active preference. Its post-multiplication positive weight cannot exhaust.

3.5 Proof of equivalence

Theorem 2. The formulation is feasible exactly when the corresponding addition problem is feasible.

Proof of necessity. Follow each added ballot through the prescribed removals. Record its initial candidate, increasing visited history, transferred weights, and exhausted weights. Aggregation gives the variables above.

At each node, multiplication by $\alpha_i$ precedes transfer or exhaustion, proving the conservation equation. The original recurrence and tally equations follow directly from the counting rule. Realizing the target gives the round inequalities.

For exact-length ballots, the argument in Section 3.4 supplies the additional zero constraints.

Proof of sufficiency. Start with added mass $a_j$ on each initial history $(c_j)$. Also include mass $a_{\varnothing}$ on the empty ballot; this mass contributes to $H$ and $Q$ throughout.

Process the nodes in increasing removal order. At node $(i,h)$, incoming histories have total current weight $z_{i,h}$. If $\alpha_i z_{i,h}>0$, split every incoming history in the proportions
$$ \frac{F_{i,h,j}}{\alpha_i z_{i,h}} \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{E_{i,h}}{\alpha_i z_{i,h}}. $$
Conservation makes these proportions sum to one. Append $c_j$ to portions assigned to $F_{i,h,j}$, and terminate portions assigned to exhaustion.

Splitting a history means splitting its initial added mass in the same proportions. Thus this operation is realizable by a finite collection of ballot types. Even when incoming histories have different prior multipliers, splitting each in the same proportions produces the specified aggregate outgoing weights.

If $\alpha_i=0$, every supporting history becomes weightless. Nonnegativity and conservation force all outgoing quantities to be zero, and subsequent preferences are immaterial. A node with no positive current weight requires no positive-weight routing.

Every positive-weight transfer increases both the removal index and the layer. The resulting histories consequently contain at most $\ell$ distinct candidates.

For the at-most-length problem, use these histories directly, together with the empty-ballot mass already included.

For exact length $\ell$, pad terminated histories:

  • A history exhausting with positive weight at $c_i$ has $i\geq\ell$. Append unused candidates among $\{c_1,\ldots,c_i\}$ until its length is $\ell$. At least $\ell-h$ unused candidates are available because $i-h\geq\ell-h$. All are inactive when reached.
  • After a zero multiplier, append any unused candidates. Subsequent weight is zero.
  • A history ending at $c_{m-1}$ or $c_m$ can be padded arbitrarily. Its last visited candidate shields the appended preferences throughout all counted decisions in which it remains active, and no decision follows removal of $c_{m-1}$.

Each padding is possible because $\ell\leq m$, and none changes a counted tally.

Inductively, the constructed additions realize the specified flows, and the original ballots realize the bookkeeping recurrence. The actual tallies therefore equal $v_{r,j}$. The imposed round conditions force the prescribed selections and multipliers in every counted round. Hence the target structure is realized. $\square$

3.6 Complexity consequence

The formulation has
$$ O(\tau m+m^2\ell) $$
variables and constraints, each of degree at most two. Its complete written size and rational coefficient encoding lengths are polynomial in $m,\tau,L,\ell$. The budget and quota constraints incorporate $k$ directly.

Existential real feasibility permits strict as well as weak polynomial inequalities. Thus all three variants belong to $\exists\mathbb R$, with Proposition 1 handling suffix robustness.

This upper bound does not establish polynomial-time solvability, $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness, or membership in $\mathrm{NP}$.

4. Polynomial-time tests with prescribed multipliers

Theorem 3. Suppose a rational multiplier $\widehat\alpha_i$ is prescribed for each early winner. Feasibility with these multipliers is polynomial-time decidable for all three variants, including the encoding length of the prescribed multipliers in the input length.

Proof. Use the formulation in Section 3 and replace each $\alpha_i$ by its prescribed constant. At eliminations use $\widehat\alpha_i=1$.

At an early win, a prescribed value outside $[0,1)$ is impossible because $Q>0$. For a value in $[0,1)$, impose the linear equality
$$ (1-\widehat\alpha_i)v_{i,i}=Q. $$
It ensures that the actual winner multiplier equals the prescribed value.

All original weights are now computable rational constants: each is its original mass multiplied by a product of prescribed multipliers. Their binary encoding lengths are polynomial, since the encoding length of a product is bounded by the sum of the encoding lengths of its factors, up to polynomial bookkeeping.

Every added-flow equation, quota equation, and round condition is linear. No final multiplier or final bookkeeping update is present.

To handle strict inequalities exactly, write them as $g_a(x)>0$, introduce $0\leq\delta\leq1$, replace them by
$$ g_a(x)\geq\delta, $$
and maximize $\delta$, retaining all other constraints.

A point satisfying the original strict inequalities has finitely many positive gaps, so it permits some $\delta>0$. Conversely, a feasible point with $\delta>0$ satisfies all original strict inequalities. The objective is bounded, and rational linear programming determines whether the closed system is feasible and its optimum is positive in polynomial time. If there are no strict inequalities, ordinary linear feasibility suffices.

Proposition 1 supplies the exact-length formulation for suffix robustness. $\square$

Two consequences follow.

Corollary 3.1. For every target, one can decide in polynomial time whether a feasible strategy exists in which every early winner has tally exactly $Q$.

Indeed, prescribe $\widehat\alpha_i=0$ at every early win. This is a test for a restricted class of strategies.

It does not decide general feasibility. For example, take $m=3$, $k=2$, original mass $1$ on $(a,b)$, budget $B=0$, and target wins by $a$ and then $b$. The quota is $Q=1/3$. Candidate $a$ wins with tally $1$ and transfers weight $2/3$ to $b$, which then wins. The target is feasible, but the first win necessarily has positive surplus.

Corollary 3.2. All three variants are polynomial-time solvable whenever
$$ s_1=\cdots=s_{m-2}=L. $$
There are then no early winner multipliers to prescribe. The final outcome may be either $L$ or $W$. This includes every instance with $m\leq2$.

5. A new polynomial-time class with unrestricted early surpluses

The next result does not prescribe the early winner multipliers.

Hypothesis. Along the prescribed trajectory,
$$ p_t(i)\neq c_i $$
for every original type $t$ and every early winning round $i$.

This hypothesis is polynomial-time checkable from the input lists and target.

Theorem 4. Under this hypothesis, unrestricted exact budget feasibility and uncapped suffix-robust exact budget feasibility are polynomial-time solvable.

Proof. No original ballot supports an early winner. Consequently, no original weight affecting a counted tally is multiplied at a win. Elimination transfers preserve weights. The original tally contributions are therefore the fixed rational constants
$$ o_{r,j}=\sum_{t:\,p_t(r)=c_j}\mu_t. $$
In particular,
$$ o_{i,i}=0 $$
at every early winning round.

We can omit the history layers because unrestricted additions have no relevant length bound. Introduce initial masses $a_j,a_{\varnothing}\geq0$, flows $F_{i,j}\geq0$ for $i\leq m-2$ and $j>i$, and exhausted weights $E_i\geq0$. Put
$$ z_i=a_i+\sum_{p<i}F_{p,i}. $$

The tallies are
$$ v_{r,j} = o_{r,j}+a_j+\sum_{i<r}F_{i,j}. $$
Use the same budget, quota, and exact round conditions as before.

At an early elimination impose
$$ E_i+\sum_{j>i}F_{i,j}=z_i. $$

At an early win, all supporting weight is added weight, so $v_{i,i}=z_i$. Its total post-multiplication weight is exactly
$$ \frac{z_i-Q}{z_i}z_i=z_i-Q. $$
Hence impose the linear equation
$$ E_i+\sum_{j>i}F_{i,j}=z_i-Q. $$

This is a polynomial-size linear system. Necessity follows by aggregation of an actual strategy.

For sufficiency, construct increasing histories as in Theorem 2. At an elimination, split according to the outgoing quantities divided by $z_i$ when $z_i>0$. At a win with $z_i>Q$, the actual multiplier is $(z_i-Q)/z_i$; split according to the outgoing quantities divided by $z_i-Q$. These proportions realize every modeled flow. At $z_i=Q$, all supporting histories become weightless. The increasing histories have length at most $m$, so they are valid unrestricted ballots. Include $a_{\varnothing}$ as empty-ballot mass.

For uncapped suffix robustness, Proposition 1 asks for full-ranking additions. Impose
$$ a_{\varnothing}=0,\qquad E_i=0 \quad\text{for all }i\leq m-2. $$
Every positive-weight history then reaches $c_{m-1}$ or $c_m$, unless it first becomes weightless at a zero-surplus winner. Pad it to length $m$ as in Theorem 2. The resulting full rankings realize the same counted tallies.

Thus both variants are exactly described by linear systems with the required strict inequalities. The linear-programming procedure from Theorem 3 decides them in polynomial time. $\square$

The reason this argument works is specific: at these winners, aggregate added surplus is $z_i-Q$. With original supporting weight, only part of the surplus belongs to additions. With shorter length restrictions, different history layers must also receive the same proportional multiplier, so this aggregate equation alone is insufficient.

6. A polynomial-time class for single-choice additions

A different condition allows original ballots to support early winners.

Hypothesis. Whenever an original type supports an early winner, it has no active preference after that removal:
$$ p_t(i)=c_i\ \text{ and }\ s_i=W \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_t(i+1)=\bot, \qquad i\leq m-2. $$

Theorem 5. Under this hypothesis, length-restricted feasibility with $\ell=1$ and suffix-robust feasibility with cap $\ell=1$ are polynomial-time solvable.

Proof. An original ballot that is multiplied at an early win contributes to no later tally, by the hypothesis. An original ballot contributing to a later tally therefore has never previously supported a winner; its current weight remains its initial mass. Hence
$$ o_{r,j}=\sum_{t:\,p_t(r)=c_j}\mu_t $$
gives every original tally contribution as a fixed rational constant.

With single-choice additions, let $a_j\geq0$ be the mass on $(c_j)$. It contributes $a_j$ to $c_j$ until that candidate is removed and contributes nothing afterward. Thus
$$ v_{r,j}=o_{r,j}+a_j. $$

For length at most one, also allow $a_{\varnothing}\geq0$. For capped suffix robustness, Proposition 1 requires exact length one, so impose $a_{\varnothing}=0$.

The budget, quota, and exact round conditions are all linear. Apply the strict-inequality linear-programming procedure from Theorem 3. $\square$

In particular, this theorem applies whenever every original ballot is empty or single-choice, regardless of the locations of the prescribed wins.

7. Conservation restrictions

Let $w$ be the total number of prescribed round wins.

Proposition 6. Every feasible instance satisfies
$$ w\leq k+1. $$
If $w=k+1$, there is no initially empty mass, no positive exhausted weight, and the remaining candidate has zero weight after the final removal. The last round winner has tally exactly $Q$, and all later counted tallies are zero.

Proof. Let $T=M+H=(k+1)Q$. Let $D_0$ be initially empty mass, and let $D_r$ be post-multiplication weight exhausted at removal $r$. Let $R$ be the final remaining active weight.

At a win, precisely $Q$ is removed from the supporting weights before transfer: if the winner tally is $v$, post-multiplication total weight is $v-Q$. At an elimination, no weight is consumed before transfer. Therefore
$$ T=wQ+D_0+\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}D_r+R. $$
All terms other than $wQ$ are nonnegative. Since $Q>0$, this gives $w\leq k+1$.

If $w=k+1$, equality forces every other term to vanish. Immediately before the last win, exactly $k$ quotas have been consumed and no weight has exhausted, so active total weight is $Q$. A winner tally at least $Q$ must then equal $Q$, and every other active tally is zero. Its multiplier is zero, leaving no active weight. All subsequent removals are eliminations of zero-tally candidates, selected according to the priority order. $\square$

Let $M_{\varnothing}$ be the original empty-ballot mass. Since initially active weight is at most $M-M_{\varnothing}+H$,
$$ w\frac{M+H}{k+1}\leq M-M_{\varnothing}+H. $$
For $w<k+1$, this yields the necessary budget bound
$$ B\geq H\geq \max\left\{ 0,\, \frac{(k+1)M_{\varnothing}}{k+1-w}-M \right\}. $$
If $w=k+1$ and $M_{\varnothing}>0$, feasibility is impossible.

There is an additional restriction when all original and added ballots are full rankings. Let $w_r$ be the number of wins before round $r$, and let $n_r=m-r+1$. No positive weight exhausts while a candidate remains active, so the active total is
$$ (k+1-w_r)Q. $$
A prescribed elimination requires every active tally to be strictly below $Q$, and consequently requires
$$ (k+1-w_r)Q<n_rQ, $$
or equivalently
$$ n_r+w_r>k+1. $$
By Proposition 1, this condition applies to uncapped suffix-robust feasibility when the original ballots are full rankings.

8. Polynomial support and exact-boundary behavior

Proposition 7. Every feasible instance of any of the three variants admits a realizing strategy with at most
$$ d=\frac{m(m+1)}2 $$
positive-mass added types.

Proof. For the robust variant, first use Proposition 1 to obtain exact-length additions.

Fix a realizing strategy and its actual multipliers. For each allowed added ballot $b$, let $g_b$ contain a first coordinate equal to $1$ and its per-unit-mass contribution to every active candidate in every counted round, evaluated along the prescribed trajectory with those fixed multipliers. Its dimension is
$$ 1+\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}|A_r| = 1+\sum_{j=2}^{m}j = d. $$

If more than $d$ types have positive masses $\lambda_b$, their vectors are linearly dependent. Choose coefficients $\beta_b$, not all zero, satisfying
$$ \sum_b\beta_bg_b=0. $$
The first coordinate gives $\sum_b\beta_b=0$, so both positive and negative coefficients occur. Set
$$ t=\min_{\beta_b>0}\frac{\lambda_b}{\beta_b}, \qquad \lambda'_b=\lambda_b-t\beta_b. $$
All new masses are nonnegative, at least one positive mass disappears, and the total feature vector is preserved. Repetition leaves at most $d$ types.

Preserving the first coordinate preserves $H$ and therefore $Q$. Preserving the tally coordinates under the fixed multipliers preserves actual counting inductively: the first-round tallies agree, so its selection and multiplier agree; the same argument then applies to each successive round. The target is unchanged. Only existing types were removed, so length restrictions remain valid. Exact-length types remain robust as prefixes. $\square$

This proves a support bound, not a polynomial-bit certificate bound.

Strict inequalities also make budget attainment substantive. Take $m=2$, $k=1$, original mass $1$ on $(a)$, priority favoring $a$, and target a win by $b$. Its tally must strictly exceed $a$’s tally, which is at least $1$. Hence every realizing addition has total mass greater than $1$.

Conversely, adding mass $h>1$ on $(b)$ gives
$$ Q=\frac{1+h}{2}<h, $$
and realizes the target. For full-ranking additions use $(b,a)$, which has the same first-round contribution. Thus the feasible budgets are exactly
$$ B>1. $$
The infimum is $1$, but budget $1$ is infeasible.

9. What remains unresolved

The general formulations retain two coupled requirements at an early winner:
$$ \alpha_i v_{i,i}=v_{i,i}-Q $$
and
$$ E_{i,h}+\sum_{j>i}F_{i,h,j}=\alpha_i z_{i,h}. $$
The same multiplier also scales original ballots whose later preferences may differ.

The linear cases proved above remove this coupling by fixing the multipliers, making their later original effects irrelevant, or pooling all added weight at winners with no original supporting weight. None of these arguments eliminates the coupling in general. No hardness reduction is established either.

Consequently, the requested general classifications remain open in this write-up.
