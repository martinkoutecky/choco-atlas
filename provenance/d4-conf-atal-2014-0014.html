The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), a type compatibility graph \(H=(T,E_H)\), rational interaction weights \(w_{tu}\), \(\alpha>0\), and fixed \(\gamma\ge 2\), maximize the sum of coalition payoffs over finite mass partitions \(x^1,\ldots,x^k\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{T}\), where \(\sum_j x_t^j=\mu_t\), each \(\operatorname{supp}(x^j)\) is connected in \(H\), and each payoff is \(F(x^j)=\frac12\sum_t w_{tt}(x_t^j)^2+\sum_{\{t,u\}\in E_H}w_{tu}x_t^jx_u^j-\alpha(\sum_t x_t^j)^\gamma\). Determine the exact optimum or return an additive-\(\varepsilon\) approximation.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \(T\) with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Input also includes a connected simple undirected compatibility graph \(H=(T,E_H)\), signed rational weights \(w_{tt}\) and \(w_{tu}=w_{ut}\) for \(\{t,u\}\in E_H\), and a positive rational \(\alpha\). Fix a rational constant \(\gamma\ge2\).
A feasible mass partition consists of any finite number \(k\ge1\) of nonzero vectors \(x^j\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\), with \(\sum_{j=1}^k x_t^j=\mu_t\), such that each induced graph \(H[\{t:x_t^j>0\}]\) is connected; singleton supports are permitted. Types may split among coalitions, and no bound on \(k\) is imposed.
The payoff is \(F(x)=\frac12\sum_t w_{tt}x_t^2+\sum_{\{t,u\}\in E_H}w_{tu}x_tx_u-\alpha(\sum_t x_t)^\gamma\); nonedges contribute nothing. Define \(V^*=\sup\{\sum_{j=1}^kF(x^j): (x^1,\ldots,x^k)\text{ is feasible}\}\).
Exact value computation is understood through the decision problem \(V^*\ge b\), for a binary rational threshold \(b\). Additive approximation means computing a rational \(z\) with \(|z-V^*|\le\varepsilon\), for a binary rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\). Let \(m=|E_H|\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including the threshold or accuracy input.

Is deciding \(V^*\ge b\) in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
Is additive-\(\varepsilon\) value approximation possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\), or is it NP-hard even when \(1/\varepsilon\) is polynomially bounded in \(L\)?

For discrete connected-coalition partitions, an objective decomposing into a part increasing under coalition mergers and a part decreasing under mergers admits an efficiently evaluable subtree upper bound, combining the decreasing part at the current partition with the increasing part at a computable coarsening. This supports exact branch and bound without establishing polynomial-time optimization ([verified discrete result](https://iris.univr.it/retrieve/e14ff6e3-ab38-0209-e053-6605fe0ad24c/2017tist.pdf)).
No result settling this precise continuous mass-partition problem was identified in the literature search.

  • Removed zero-mass types so that the supplied type list is exactly the positive-mass support; retained total mass one.
  • Specified a connected simple undirected compatibility graph, carrying over the discrete connected-graph convention; singleton supports remain feasible.
  • Allowed either sign for rational interaction weights, imposed symmetry on undirected interactions, and specified that nonedges contribute nothing. Retained the stated diagonal interaction term as interaction within a type.
  • Specified alpha as a positive binary rational and gamma as a fixed rational constant at least two, providing a finite computational convention for the otherwise unspecified exponent.
  • Excluded empty coalitions while retaining arbitrary finite coalition counts and splitting of types.
  • Defined the optimal value as a supremum because the unrestricted finite-partition formulation does not guarantee attainment.
  • Interpreted exact computation through rational-threshold decision and additive approximation as approximation of the optimal value, rather than requiring an unspecified representation of a real optimizer.
  • Defined m as the number of compatibility edges and L as total input bit length; distinguished polynomial dependence on inverse accuracy from polynomial dependence on its encoding length.

Result

For every fixed rational \(\gamma\ge2\), both problems are \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard:

  • deciding whether \(V^*\ge b\);
  • computing an additive approximation, even when \(1/\varepsilon\) is polynomially bounded in the input length \(L\).

The hardness holds when \(H\) is complete, all masses are equal, and every weight has absolute value less than \(1\). Thus the proposed polynomial-time algorithms cannot exist unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Arbitrarily fine splitting and the supremum

First, the possibility of using arbitrarily many coalitions must be handled explicitly.

Let \(r\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\) be any residual mass vector. For a positive integer \(N\), split each positive coordinate \(r_t\) into \(N\) singleton coalitions, each with vector \((r_t/N)e_t\). These coalitions are feasible because singleton supports are permitted. Their total payoff is

\( D_N(r)=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_t w_{tt}r_t^2-\frac{\alpha}{N^{\gamma-1}}\sum_t r_t^\gamma. \)

Because \(\gamma\ge2\), both terms tend to zero as \(N\) tends to infinity.

Consequently:

  • Taking \(r=\mu\) proves \(V^*\ge0\). In particular, thresholds \(b\le0\) are trivial.
  • If \(x\le\mu\) coordinatewise and \(x\) has nonempty connected support, adjoining the singleton partition of \(r=\mu-x\) gives feasible partitions with values tending to \(F(x)\). Therefore \(V^*\ge F(x)\).

These statements concern a supremum; they do not require its attainment.

The supremum is also finite. Let \(B\) be the largest absolute value of an input weight. For a coalition of total mass \(s\), its quadratic contribution is at most \(Bs^2/2\), so \(F(x)\le Bs^2/2\). Coalition masses sum to \(1\), hence every partition has value at most \(B/2\).

2. A clique characterization of a quadratic maximum

Let \(G\) be a simple graph on vertex set \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), with clique number \(\omega(G)\). Define

\( A_G(p)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}p_ip_j \)

for \(p\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_i p_i=1\). We prove

\( \max_{p\ge0,\ \sum_i p_i=1}A_G(p)=\frac12\left(1-\frac1{\omega(G)}\right). \)

Proof. Suppose two nonadjacent vertices \(a,b\) both have positive coordinates. Write

\( U_a=\sum_{v:\{a,v\}\in E(G)}p_v,\qquad U_b=\sum_{v:\{b,v\}\in E(G)}p_v. \)

Because \(a,b\) are nonadjacent, neither quantity depends on \(p_a\) or \(p_b\). The part of \(A_G(p)\) involving these coordinates is \(p_aU_a+p_bU_b\).

If \(U_a\ge U_b\), replace \((p_a,p_b)\) by \((p_a+p_b,0)\). This increases the objective by \(p_b(U_a-U_b)\ge0\). If \(U_b>U_a\), merge into \(b\) instead. Each operation decreases the support size.

After finitely many operations, the support is a clique, of some size \(r\le\omega(G)\). On that support,

\( A_G(p)=\frac12\left(1-\sum_i p_i^2\right) \le\frac12\left(1-\frac1r\right) \le\frac12\left(1-\frac1{\omega(G)}\right), \)

where \(\sum_i p_i^2\ge1/r\) follows from Cauchy–Schwarz. Since the operations never decreased the objective, this bounds the original vector as well.

Conversely, assigning mass \(1/\omega(G)\) to each vertex of a maximum clique attains the stated bound. This proves the characterization.

3. Reduction from CLIQUE

We reduce from CLIQUE: given \(G\) and an integer \(K\), decide whether \(\omega(G)\ge K\). Its \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness is the standard result of [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

We may restrict to \(2\le K\le |V(G)|\): instances outside this range are immediately decidable and can be mapped to fixed yes- or no-instances.

Pad \(G\) with isolated vertices until its vertex count \(n\) is a power of two. This at most doubles its size and preserves its clique number. The padding ensures that the masses and the thresholds below are dyadic rationals.

For each positive integer \(r\), define

\( a_r=\frac12\left(1-\frac1r\right). \)

Set

\( c=\frac{a_K+a_{K-1}}2,\qquad \delta=\frac{a_K-a_{K-1}}2=\frac1{4K(K-1)},\qquad \alpha=\frac{\delta}{2}. \)

Construct the society as follows:

  • \(T=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), with \(\mu_i=1/n\);
  • \(H\) is the complete graph on \(T\);
  • \(w_{ii}=-2c\);
  • for distinct vertices,

\( w_{ij}= \begin{cases} 1-2c,&\{i,j\}\in E(G),\\ -2c,&\{i,j\}\notin E(G). \end{cases} \)

The source graph \(G\) encodes the weights; the compatibility graph \(H\) is complete. Thus every nonempty coalition support is connected.

Writing \(s=\sum_i x_i\), direct expansion gives

\( \frac12\sum_i w_{ii}x_i^2+\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}x_ix_j =\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}x_ix_j-cs^2. \)

Therefore the constructed payoff is

\( F(x)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}x_ix_j-cs^2-\alpha s^\gamma. \)

All constructed data are rational. Since \(0<c<1/2\), every weight has absolute value less than \(1\).

4. No-instances have value exactly zero

Suppose \(\omega(G)\le K-1\). Consider any nonzero coalition \(x\), and normalize it by \(p=x/s\), where \(s=\sum_i x_i>0\).

The characterization in Section 2 gives

\( \sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}x_ix_j =s^2A_G(p) \le a_{K-1}s^2. \)

Since \(c-a_{K-1}=\delta\),

\( F(x)\le-\delta s^2-\alpha s^\gamma<0. \)

This inequality holds for every coalition vector, including arbitrary real vectors produced by splitting types. Every finite feasible partition therefore has negative total payoff, implying \(V^*\le0\).

Section 1 proves \(V^*\ge0\). Hence

\( \omega(G)\le K-1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V^*=0. \)

In these instances the supremum is not attained, which is why arbitrarily fine splitting matters.

5. Yes-instances have an inverse-polynomial positive value

Suppose \(\omega(G)\ge K\), and choose a clique \(C\) with exactly \(K\) vertices. Define one coalition by

\( x_i= \begin{cases} 1/n,&i\in C,\\ 0,&i\notin C. \end{cases} \)

This vector satisfies \(x\le\mu\), and its support is connected in \(H\). Its total mass is \(s=K/n\le1\). Uniformity on the clique gives

\( \sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}x_ix_j =\frac{K(K-1)}{2n^2} =a_Ks^2. \)

Since \(a_K-c=\delta\),

\( F(x)=\delta s^2-\alpha s^\gamma. \)

For \(0<s\le1\) and \(\gamma\ge2\), we have \(s^\gamma\le s^2\). Using \(\alpha=\delta/2\), it follows that

\( F(x)\ge\frac{\delta}{2}s^2 =\frac{K}{8(K-1)n^2} \ge\frac1{8n^2}. \)

By Section 1, the remaining masses can be partitioned into singleton coalitions whose total payoff tends to zero. Therefore

\( \omega(G)\ge K\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V^*\ge\frac1{8n^2}. \)

Together, Sections 4 and 5 establish the gap

\( \omega(G)<K\ \Longrightarrow\ V^*=0, \qquad \omega(G)\ge K\ \Longrightarrow\ V^*\ge\frac1{8n^2}. \)

6. Exact-decision hardness

Choose the rational threshold

\( b=\frac1{16n^2}. \)

The established gap proves the exact equivalence

\( \omega(G)\ge K\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V^*\ge b. \)

The construction has \(\tau=n\) and \(m=n(n-1)/2\). Every rational parameter has bit length \(O(\log n)\), and the entire instance can be constructed in polynomial time.

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from CLIQUE. Thus deciding \(V^*\ge b\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for every fixed rational \(\gamma\ge2\).

7. Additive-approximation hardness

For the same society, request accuracy

\( \varepsilon=\frac1{64n^2}. \)

Suppose an algorithm returns a rational \(z\) satisfying \(|z-V^*|\le\varepsilon\).

In a no-instance,

\( z\le\frac1{64n^2}<b. \)

In a yes-instance,

\( z\ge\frac1{8n^2}-\frac1{64n^2} =\frac7{64n^2}>b. \)

Comparing \(z\) with \(b\) therefore decides CLIQUE using one approximation call.

The explicit input lists \(n\) positive masses, so its total bit length satisfies \(L\ge n\). Consequently,

\( \frac1\varepsilon=64n^2\le64L^2. \)

Thus additive approximation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even when inverse accuracy is polynomially bounded in \(L\). An algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The reduction proves both requested hardness results without imposing any bound on coalition count or forbidding type splitting. It makes no assertion of membership of the exact decision problem in \(\mathrm{NP}\).
