The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite candidate set \(C\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), integer voting weights \(q_t\), fixed types \(F\) with rankings \(\rho_t\), manipulable types \(M\), and a target \(p\), decide whether there are masses \(x_{t,r} \ge 0\) for reports \(r\) with \(\sum_r x_{t,r}=\mu_t\) for every \(t \in M\), such that \(p\) is a veto winner. For each \(c \in C\), its veto score is \(\sum_{t \in F} \mu_t q_t \mathbf{1}[c \ne \operatorname{last}(\rho_t)] + \sum_{t \in M} q_t \sum_r x_{t,r}\mathbf{1}[c \ne \operatorname{last}(r)]\); require this score to be at least every other candidate's.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set with \(m=|C|\ge 3\), and let \(p\in C\). A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Each type has a positive integer voting weight \(q_t\), encoded in binary; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
The support is partitioned into fixed types \(F\) and manipulable types \(M\). Each fixed type has a known strict linear ranking \(\rho_t\) of all candidates. Write \(\mathcal R(C)\) for all strict linear rankings of \(C\), and \(\operatorname{last}(r)\) for the last candidate in ranking \(r\).
A manipulation assigns real masses \(x_{t,r}\ge 0\) to reports \(r\in\mathcal R(C)\), with \(\sum_{r\in\mathcal R(C)}x_{t,r}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\in M\). Every report is permitted, and each assigned portion retains weight \(q_t\).
Under veto, define \(s(c)=\sum_{t\in F}\mu_tq_t\mathbf 1[c\ne\operatorname{last}(\rho_t)]+\sum_{t\in M}q_t\sum_{r\in\mathcal R(C)}x_{t,r}\mathbf 1[c\ne\operatorname{last}(r)]\). Success means \(s(p)\ge s(c)\) for every \(c\in C\); ties count as success.

Is deciding whether a successful manipulation exists in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For indivisible weighted voters, constructive coalitional veto manipulation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for every fixed \(m\ge 3\), including when ties count as success ([Conitzer–Sandholm–Lang](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/manipulationJACM07.pdf)).
No result settling this exact divisible-mass decision problem was located in the literature search.

  • Specified that fixed rankings and permitted reports are strict linear orders of the entire candidate set, with all such reports allowed.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, a successful manipulation exists exactly when
\[ \sum_{c\in C\setminus\{p\}}\max\{0,f(p)-f(c)\}\le W, \]
where
\[ f(c)=\sum_{\substack{t\in F\\ \operatorname{last}(\rho_t)=c}}\mu_tq_t, \qquad W=\sum_{t\in M}\mu_tq_t. \]
Here \(f(c)\) is the fixed voting weight vetoing \(c\), and \(W\) is the total manipulable voting weight.

The proof below uses precisely the stated model: each manipulable type may split its mass among arbitrary reports, with every portion retaining its type’s weight.

1. Expressing success through veto weights

Let \(T=\sum_t\mu_tq_t\) be the total voting weight. Given any manipulation, define its additional veto weight against \(c\) by
\[ z(c)=\sum_{t\in M}q_t \sum_{\substack{r\in\mathcal R(C)\\\operatorname{last}(r)=c}}x_{t,r}. \]
Every ranking has exactly one last candidate. Consequently,
\[ z(c)\ge 0, \qquad \sum_{c\in C}z(c) =\sum_{t\in M}q_t\sum_{r\in\mathcal R(C)}x_{t,r} =W. \]

Every unit of voting weight contributes to a candidate’s score unless it vetoes that candidate. Thus
\[ s(c)=T-f(c)-z(c). \]
It follows that success is equivalent to
\[ f(p)+z(p)\le f(c)+z(c) \qquad\text{for every }c\ne p. \]

2. Necessity of the criterion

For each rival \(c\ne p\), put
\[ d(c)=\max\{0,f(p)-f(c)\}, \qquad D=\sum_{c\ne p}d(c). \]

Suppose a successful manipulation exists. Its success inequalities imply
\[ z(c)\ge f(p)+z(p)-f(c)\ge f(p)-f(c). \]
Since also \(z(c)\ge 0\), we obtain \(z(c)\ge d(c)\). Summing over the rivals gives
\[ D\le\sum_{c\ne p}z(c)=W-z(p)\le W. \]
Therefore \(D\le W\) is necessary, including for manipulations that veto \(p\).

3. Sufficiency and an explicit manipulation

Assume \(D\le W\).

If \(W=0\), positivity of all masses and weights implies \(M=\varnothing\). Since the nonnegative numbers \(d(c)\) sum to \(D=0\), every rival satisfies \(f(c)\ge f(p)\). Hence \(p\) already has score at least that of every rival.

Now suppose \(W>0\). Choose any \(a\in C\setminus\{p\}\), which exists because \(m\ge 3\). Define
\[ z(p)=0,\qquad z(a)=d(a)+(W-D),\qquad z(c)=d(c)\quad(c\notin\{p,a\}). \]
These numbers are nonnegative, sum to \(W\), and satisfy
\[ f(c)+z(c)\ge f(c)+d(c)\ge f(p) =f(p)+z(p) \qquad(c\ne p). \]

It remains to realize these aggregate veto weights while respecting every type’s mass and weight.

For each rival \(c\ne p\), choose a strict ranking \(r_c\) placing \(p\) first and \(c\) last; order the other candidates arbitrarily. These rankings are distinct because their last candidates differ. For every \(t\in M\), assign
\[ x_{t,r_c}=\mu_t\frac{z(c)}{W} \qquad(c\ne p), \]
and assign zero mass to every other ranking.

For each type,
\[ \sum_r x_{t,r} =\frac{\mu_t}{W}\sum_{c\ne p}z(c) =\mu_t, \]
so this assignment is permitted. Moreover, its veto weight against each rival is exactly
\[ \sum_{t\in M}q_tx_{t,r_c} =\frac{z(c)}{W}\sum_{t\in M}\mu_tq_t =z(c). \]
No assigned ranking vetoes \(p\). The preceding inequalities therefore prove success.

This establishes both directions of the criterion.

4. Polynomial bit complexity

The algorithm computes all \(f(c)\), computes \(W\), and accepts exactly when \(D\le W\). No rankings need to be enumerated.

To justify exact arithmetic, write each input mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), with positive binary integers \(a_t,b_t\), and let
\[ B=\prod_t b_t. \]
The binary length of \(B\) is at most one plus the sum of the binary lengths of the denominators, hence is bounded by \(L+1\). Define the integer scaled voting weights
\[ A_t=a_tq_t(B/b_t). \]
Then compute
\[ F_c=\sum_{\substack{t\in F\\\operatorname{last}(\rho_t)=c}}A_t, \qquad V=\sum_{t\in M}A_t. \]
Because \(F_c=Bf(c)\) and \(V=BW\), the acceptance test is exactly the integer comparison
\[ \sum_{c\ne p}\max\{0,F_p-F_c\}\le V. \]

Since \(\mu_t\le 1\), each \(A_t=B\mu_tq_t\) is at most \(Bq_t\). Thus each \(A_t\) has bit length bounded by \(2L+2\). Summing over types and candidates adds at most logarithmic terms in \(\tau\) and \(m\). All intermediate integers consequently have bit length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Reading the fixed rankings takes at most polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\); the remaining computation uses polynomially many integer additions, multiplications, exact divisions, and comparisons on these polynomial-length integers. Standard binary arithmetic therefore gives the required polynomial running time.

The [cited indivisible-voter hardness result](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/manipulationJACM07.pdf) concerns a different feasibility constraint. Here, the explicit proportional assignment in Section 3 realizes the required veto weights even when they require splitting individual types.
