The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a succinct feasible set \(S\), a finite type set \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and nonnegative utilities \(u_t:S\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), define \(Q_x(q)=\inf\{v:\sum_{t:u_t(x)\le v}\mu_t\ge q\}\) for \(0<q\le1\). For \(\alpha\in(0,1]\) and \(\varepsilon\ge0\), seek a feasible \(x\) for which there are no feasible \(y\) and \(q\in(0,1]\) with \(Q_y(r)\ge Q_x(r)\) for every \(0<r<q\) and \(Q_y(q)>(Q_x(q)+\varepsilon)/\alpha\). Can a weighted ordered-tail adaptation of Algorithm 1, using lower-tail integrals \(\int_0^q Q_x(r)\,dr\), return such a solution with the guarantee \((\alpha^2/(1-\alpha+\alpha^2),\,\varepsilon/(1-\alpha+\alpha^2))\) in time polynomial in the decision description, \(\tau=|T|\), and the mass bit length \(L\), given approximate solvers for its ordered-tail subproblems?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(T\) lists the \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(S\subseteq\mathbb Q^m\) be a nonempty succinctly represented feasible set, with feasible decisions encoded in at most \(B\) bits and polynomial-time evaluable utilities \(u_t:S\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\). Let \(N\) be the decision and utility description length and \(L\) the total mass bit length.
Define \(Q_x(q)=\inf\{v\in\mathbb R:\sum_{t:u_t(x)\le v}\mu_t\ge q\}\) for \(0<q\le1\), and \(A_x(q)=\int_0^q Q_x(r)\,dr\), with \(A_x(0)=0\). Equal utilities are pooled; quantiles use this endpoint convention without interpolation.
A feasible \(x\) is \((a,e)\)-approximately leximin-optimal if no \(y\in S\) and \(q\in(0,1]\) satisfy both \(Q_y(r)\ge Q_x(r)\) for every \(0<r<q\) and \(Q_y(q)>(Q_x(q)+e)/a\).
Fix binary-rational \(\alpha\in(0,1]\) and \(\varepsilon\ge0\). An ordered-tail oracle receives rational \(0=q_0<q_1<\cdots<q_k<q\le1\) and bounds \(b_0=0,b_1,\ldots,b_k\in\mathbb Q\), and maximizes \(F(x)=(A_x(q)-b_k)/(q-q_k)\) subject to \(x\in S\) and \(A_x(q_j)\ge b_j\) for \(1\le j\le k\).
On nonempty subproblems with nonnegative optimal value, the oracle returns a feasible \(x\) satisfying \(F(x)\ge\alpha\max F-\varepsilon\), in polynomial time in its encoded input length; feasibility is exact and the returned objective is the actual \(F(x)\).
Can an iterative adaptation using these ordered-tail oracles return an \(\left(\alpha^2/(1-\alpha+\alpha^2),\varepsilon/(1-\alpha+\alpha^2)\right)\)-approximately leximin-optimal decision in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,N,L,B\) and the bit lengths of \(\alpha,\varepsilon\), uniformly over all admissible oracle outputs?
For a finite list of equally weighted agents, the ordered-outcomes iteration obtains exactly these approximation parameters from approximate residual ordered-sum solvers. [Hartman et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.12506v4)
No result settling this positive-rational-mass oracle problem was identified in the literature search.

  • Made alpha and epsilon binary-rational inputs and specified all running-time parameters.
  • Defined the otherwise unspecified weighted ordered-tail subproblem by retaining prior integral lower bounds and maximizing the residual integral divided by the newly considered mass. This normalization preserves utility units for the additive error and recovers the discrete residual ordered-sum objective for equal masses.
  • Required deterministic oracle guarantees, exact feasibility, and actual attained objective values; the guarantee must hold for every admissible oracle output.

Status and new partial result

The requested uniform polynomial-time guarantee remains unresolved. The following result extends the exact-oracle case from the notes to approximate oracles.

Theorem. For every admissible $\alpha>0$ and $\varepsilon\ge0$, the supplied ordered-tail oracle suffices to compute an exactly leximin-optimal decision, uniformly over all admissible oracle outputs, in time polynomial in
$$ m,\tau,N,L,B,\operatorname{bits}(\alpha),\operatorname{bits}(\varepsilon),\frac1\alpha. $$
Consequently, the requested approximation guarantee is obtainable in polynomial time whenever $1/\alpha$ is polynomially bounded in the input length. In particular, it is obtainable for $\alpha=1$ and arbitrary $\varepsilon$, and for every fixed positive $\alpha$.

The dependence on the numerical value $1/\alpha$ prevents this theorem from settling the general question. The proof below establishes that dependence explicitly.

1. Finite mass grid and rational separation

We use the natural interpretation that each decision encoding determines one decision. Since encodings have length at most $B$, the feasible set is finite. Every nonempty optimization problem considered below therefore attains its optimum.

Write the masses in reduced form as $\mu_t=a_t/d_t$, and define
$$ D=\operatorname{lcm}_{t\in T}d_t, \qquad \delta=\frac1D. $$
Every mass and every sum of masses is an integer multiple of $\delta$. Moreover,
$$ \log_2D\le\sum_{t\in T}\log_2d_t\le L. $$
Thus $D$ has polynomial encoding length, although its numerical value may be exponential.

Pooling equal utilities, every quantile breakpoint is a cumulative mass of types. The stipulated endpoint convention consequently makes $Q_x$ constant on every cell
$$ ((i-1)\delta,i\delta], \qquad 1\le i\le D. $$
In particular,
$$ A_x(i\delta)-A_x((i-1)\delta) = \delta Q_x(i\delta). $$

Let $P$ be a polynomial upper bound on the bit lengths of utility numerators and denominators. Such a bound follows from the assumed polynomial-time utility evaluation on decision encodings of length at most $B$. Increase $P$ if necessary so that
$$ 0\le u_t(x)\le U:=2^P $$
for every feasible $x$ and every type $t$. Hence, for $0\le r\le q\le1$,
$$ 0\le A_x(q)-A_x(r)\le U(q-r). $$

We also need a uniform separation bound. Fix a rational $p=a/d\in(0,1]$ in reduced form. If the utility denominators at $x$ are $e_1,\ldots,e_\tau$, the denominator of $A_x(p)$ divides
$$ Dd\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}e_t. $$
Indeed, its full utility-group contributions have masses with denominator dividing $D$, and its final partial contribution has mass $p-c$, where $c$ has denominator dividing $D$.

Since $e_t\le2^P$, every reduced denominator of $A_x(p)$ is at most
$$ W(p):=Dd\,2^{\tau P}. $$
Two distinct attainable values of $A_x(p)$ therefore differ by at least
$$ g(p):=\frac1{W(p)^2}. $$
To justify this, distinct reduced fractions with denominators $v_1,v_2\le W(p)$ differ by a nonzero integer divided by $v_1v_2$.

Both $g(p)$ and all integrals used below have polynomial bit length when $p$ does.

2. Residual tightening removes additive error

The key new step is to place the objective quantile very close to a retained checkpoint.

Lemma. Let $X$ be a nonempty feasible set defined by ordered-tail constraints whose last checkpoint is $s$, taking $s=0$ when there are no constraints. Suppose a decision $x_0\in X$ is known. For rational $s<r<1$ and $0<\eta\le U$, one can obtain $x\in X$ satisfying
$$ A_x(r)\ge\max_{y\in X}A_y(r)-\eta $$
using
$$ n= \left\lceil\frac1\alpha\right\rceil \left\lceil\log_2\frac{2U}{\eta}\right\rceil $$
oracle calls. All calls satisfy the oracle promise.

Proof. Put
$$ M=\max_{y\in X}A_y(r), \qquad b_0=A_{x_0}(r). $$
Choose the positive rational
$$ h= \min\left\{ \frac{1-r}{2}, \frac{\alpha\eta}{2(U+\varepsilon)} \right\}. $$
At iteration $j$, retain the original constraints defining $X$, append the checkpoint $r$ with bound $b_j$, and query at $q=r+h$. Let the returned decision be $x_{j+1}$, and set
$$ b_{j+1}=A_{x_{j+1}}(r). $$
The checkpoint at $r$ is replaced at the next iteration; duplicate checkpoints are never introduced.

The subproblem is nonempty because the preceding decision satisfies its constraints. Its objective is nonnegative for every feasible decision:
$$ A_x(r+h)\ge A_x(r)\ge b_j. $$
Thus the oracle promise applies.

A maximizer $y$ of $A_y(r)$ over the original set $X$ remains feasible, since $b_j\le M$. Its residual objective is at least $(M-b_j)/h$. Therefore the oracle guarantee gives
$$ A_{x_{j+1}}(r+h)-b_j \ge \alpha(M-b_j)-\varepsilon h. $$
Using the utility upper bound,
$$ b_{j+1} \ge b_j+\alpha(M-b_j)-h(U+\varepsilon). $$
Exact feasibility also gives $b_{j+1}\ge b_j$.

For $G_j=M-b_j$, we obtain
$$ 0\le G_{j+1} \le (1-\alpha)G_j+h(U+\varepsilon) \le (1-\alpha)G_j+\frac{\alpha\eta}{2}. $$
Since $G_0\le U$, induction yields
$$ G_n\le(1-\alpha)^nU+\frac{\eta}{2}. $$

For $0<\alpha<1$,
$$ (1-\alpha)^{\lceil1/\alpha\rceil} \le e^{-1}<\frac12. $$
For $\alpha=1$, the left side is zero. With the stated choice of $n$,
$$ (1-\alpha)^nU \le 2^{-\lceil\log_2(2U/\eta)\rceil}U \le\frac{\eta}{2}. $$
Hence $G_n\le\eta$, proving the lemma.

The checkpoints $r,r+h$ have polynomial encoding length. Each bound $b_j$ is an actual integral of a returned decision at the fixed rational $r$, so its bit length does not grow with the number of iterations. ∎

3. Recovering exact integral maximizers

Proposition. Suppose $X$ is defined by retained constraints, its last checkpoint is $s<p\le1$, and a decision in $X$ is known. An exact maximizer of $A_x(p)$ over $X$ can be computed with a number of oracle calls polynomial in the encoding lengths and $1/\alpha$.

Case $p<1$

Apply the lemma with
$$ r=p, \qquad \eta=\frac{g(p)}2. $$
Its output satisfies
$$ 0\le\max_{y\in X}A_y(p)-A_x(p)\le\frac{g(p)}2. $$
If this difference were positive, rational separation would make it at least $g(p)$, a contradiction. Thus $x$ is an exact maximizer.

Case $p=1$

The oracle cannot put its objective quantile beyond $1$. Instead, perturb the integral being optimized slightly to the left.

Write $g=g(1)$, and choose
$$ \zeta= \min\left\{ \frac{1-s}{2}, \frac{g}{4U} \right\}, \qquad r=1-\zeta. $$
Then $s<r<1$. Apply the lemma at $r$ with $\eta=g/4$, obtaining $x$ such that
$$ A_x(r)\ge\max_{y\in X}A_y(r)-\frac g4. $$
Let $y$ maximize $A_y(1)$ over $X$. Since
$$ A_y(r)\ge A_y(1)-U\zeta $$
and $A_x(1)\ge A_x(r)$, we get
$$ A_x(1) \ge A_y(1)-U\zeta-\frac g4 \ge A_y(1)-\frac g2. $$
Rational separation at $1$ forces equality. Thus $x$ exactly maximizes $A_x(1)$.

In both cases, the logarithm of the required inverse accuracy is polynomial in $\tau,P,L$ and the checkpoint encoding lengths. The lemma therefore supplies the claimed call bound. ∎

This proposition uses only nonempty subproblems with nonnegative objective. It neither queries an unknown feasibility condition nor relies on oracle behavior outside its promise.

4. Compressing exact leximin optimization

For completeness, the exact-prefix construction from the notes is proved here, with explicit terminal returns.

First obtain a known feasible decision by an unconstrained oracle call at $q=1$. Its objective is nonnegative. Thereafter, use the proposition whenever an exact integral maximizer is required.

Initialization

Exactly maximize $A_x(\delta)$ over $S$. Since every positive mass is at least $\delta$,
$$ \frac{A_x(\delta)}{\delta} = \min_{t\in T}u_t(x). $$
Let its optimal value be $\delta v$. Retain
$$ A_x(\delta)\ge\delta v, $$
and put
$$ s=\delta, \qquad b=\delta v. $$
Every retained decision has minimum utility exactly $v$: the constraint gives a minimum at least $v$, and maximality of $v$ gives the reverse inequality.

If $s=1$, return the maximizing decision.

Phase invariant

Let $X$ be the set defined by the retained constraints. Maintain:

  • A feasible decision in $X$ is known.
  • All decisions in $X$ have the same quantile function on $(0,s]$.
  • Every decision in $X$ satisfies $A_x(s)=b$.
  • The common endpoint value is $Q_x(s)=v$.

Initialization establishes these properties.

For $x\in X$, define
$$ c_x=\sum_{t:u_t(x)\le v}\mu_t, \qquad c=\min_{x\in X}c_x. $$
The minimum exists because $X$ is finite and nonempty. Also, $c_x\ge s$, and every $c_x$ is a grid point.

Monotonicity and the endpoint convention give
$$ Q_x(r)=v \quad\text{for }s<r\le c_x, $$
and
$$ Q_x(r)>v \quad\text{for }c_x<r\le1. $$

Finding the end of the common plateau

For a grid point $q>s$, define
$$ M(q)=\max_{x\in X}A_x(q), \qquad H(q)=b+v(q-s). $$
The proposition computes $M(q)$ exactly. We have
$$ M(q)>H(q) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q>c. $$

Indeed, if $q\le c$, every retained decision has quantile $v$ throughout $(s,q]$, so its integral is $H(q)$. If $q>c$, a decision attaining $c_x=c$ has quantile strictly larger than $v$ on the positive-length interval $(c,q]$, making its integral strictly larger than $H(q)$.

Compute $M(1)$. If $M(1)=H(1)$, then $c=1$ and every decision in $X$ has the same complete quantile function. Return the maximizer obtained in this query.

Otherwise, binary search over grid indices finds the smallest $j$ for which
$$ M(j\delta)>H(j\delta). $$
The predicate is monotone, and
$$ c=(j-1)\delta. $$
The search requires $O(1+\log D)$ exact-maximization requests.

Fixing the next utility level

Put
$$ s'=j\delta=c+\delta, \qquad b'=M(s'). $$
For every $x\in X$, grid constancy gives
$$ A_x(s') = b+v(c-s)+\delta Q_x(s'). $$
Consequently, maximizing $A_x(s')$ exactly maximizes $Q_x(s')$ over $X$.

Define
$$ w=\frac{b'-b-v(c-s)}{\delta}. $$
Since $b'>H(s')$, we have $w>v$.

Append the constraint
$$ A_x(s')\ge b'. $$
The new feasible set $X'$ contains the returned maximizer. Because $b'$ is the maximum over $X$, every decision in $X'$ has integral exactly $b'$ and hence endpoint utility exactly $w$.

Thus all decisions in $X'$ share the extended prefix: the previous prefix, followed by $v$ through $c$, followed by $w$ on $(c,s']$. Replace the state by
$$ (X,s,b,v)\leftarrow(X',s',b',w). $$
If $s'=1$, return the retained maximizer immediately. Otherwise begin another phase.

Termination

Each successful extension introduces a new, strictly larger utility value on a cell of positive mass. The fixed prefix belongs to every retained decision, and each decision has at most $\tau$ distinct utilities. There can therefore be at most $\tau-1$ successful extensions after initialization.

The total number of exact-maximization requests is
$$ O\bigl(\tau(1+\log D)\bigr). $$

Exact leximin correctness

Let $x^\star$ be the returned decision. Suppose there were $y\in S$ and $q\in(0,1]$ with
$$ Q_y(r)\ge Q_{x^\star}(r) \quad\text{for every }0<r<q, $$
and
$$ Q_y(q)>Q_{x^\star}(q). $$
By common-grid constancy, the first grid cell on which the two quantile functions differ must favor $y$.

Such a first favorable difference is impossible:

  • In the initial cell, $v$ is the maximum possible minimum utility.
  • If $y$ agrees with a previously fixed prefix, it satisfies every retained constraint and therefore belongs to that phase’s set $X$.
  • Within the subsequently fixed plateau through $c$, every decision in $X$ has value $v$.
  • In the next cell, the algorithm chooses the maximum possible value $w$ over $X$.
  • On termination with $M(1)=H(1)$, every remaining retained suffix is identical.

These cases cover the entire quantile domain. Thus the alleged first favorable difference cannot exist, and $x^\star$ is exactly leximin-optimal.

5. Running time and requested parameters

The official retained checkpoints are grid points and number at most $\tau$. Their encoding lengths are polynomial in $L$. Their bounds are actual integrals, whose encoding lengths are polynomial in $\tau,P,L$.

Every exact-maximization request uses a polynomial number of approximate-oracle calls times $1/\alpha$, by Sections 2 and 3. Its temporary checkpoints and bounds also have polynomial encoding length. The complete construction therefore has the running time stated in the theorem, uniformly over all admissible oracle outputs.

Set
$$ d_\alpha=1-\alpha+\alpha^2, \qquad a=\frac{\alpha^2}{d_\alpha}, \qquad e=\frac{\varepsilon}{d_\alpha}. $$
Since $0<a\le1$ and $e\ge0$,
$$ \frac{Q_{x^\star}(q)+e}{a}\ge Q_{x^\star}(q). $$
Any forbidden witness for the requested parameters would therefore also be a forbidden witness for exact leximin optimality. The constructed decision satisfies the requested guarantee.

The unresolved issue is exclusively the running time when $1/\alpha$ is exponentially large relative to its encoding length.

6. Why the tightening argument does not resolve the general case

The dependence on $1/\alpha$ cannot simply be removed from the preceding tightening analysis.

Consider one type of mass $1$, feasible decisions
$$ S=\{0,1,\ldots,M\}, $$
utility $u(j)=j$, and parameters
$$ \alpha=\frac1M, \qquad \varepsilon=0. $$
At a tightening checkpoint $r=1/2$, suppose the current decision is $c<M$ and the bound is $b=rc$. For any permitted $h>0$, the residual objective is
$$ F(j)=\frac rh(j-c)+j. $$
It is maximized at $j=M$. Nevertheless, returning only $c+1$ is admissible, because
$$ \alpha F(M) = \frac rh\left(1-\frac cM\right)+1 \le \frac rh+1 \le F(c+1). $$
It also satisfies the retained constraint exactly as required.

Thus, even arbitrarily small choices of $h$ allow an admissible sequence that advances the decision by only one unit per call. Starting from $c=1$, exact recovery can require $M-1$ such calls, while $\alpha=1/M$ has encoding length logarithmic in $M$.

This example concerns the exact-recovery subroutine, not a lower bound for the task’s weaker approximation target. In fact, the one-type approximation problem is handled by a single unconstrained oracle call. A general solution may therefore need to avoid exact recovery rather than accelerate it.
