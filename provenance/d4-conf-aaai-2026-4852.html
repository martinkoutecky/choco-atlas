The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), an axis \(\lhd\), a balanced group-separating tree \(\mathcal T\), and a rational mass distribution \(\mu\) over \(\mathcal D=\mathrm{SP}(\lhd)\cap\mathrm{GS}_{\mathrm{bal}}(\mathcal T)\), decide whether there exist \(r_1,r_2\in\mathcal L(C)\) such that \(\sum_{v\in\mathcal D}\mu_v\min\{\mathrm{swap}(v,r_1),\mathrm{swap}(v,r_2)\}\le q\), for rational \(q\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given a candidate set \(C\) with \(m\ge 2\), let \(\mathcal L(C)\) be its strict linear orders. The input includes a single-peaked axis \(\lhd\) and a rooted, ordered binary tree \(\mathcal T\) whose leaves are bijectively labeled by \(C\); every level except possibly the last is completely filled.
A ranking belongs to \(\mathrm{GS}_{\mathrm{bal}}(\mathcal T)\) if it is obtained by independently reversing the order of the two children at any internal nodes and then reading the leaves from left to right. Set \(\mathcal D=\mathrm{SP}(\lhd)\cap\mathrm{GS}_{\mathrm{bal}}(\mathcal T)\), where \(\mathrm{SP}(\lhd)\) is the single-peaked domain on the supplied axis. Write \(\mathrm{swap}\) for Kendall tau distance.
A society is given by its support: a list \(S\subseteq\mathcal D\) of \(\tau\) distinct types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_v\), satisfying \(\sum_{v\in S}\mu_v=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. The threshold is \(q\in\mathbb Q\), encoded in binary; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
The decision problem asks whether there exist two distinct rankings \(r_1,r_2\in\mathcal L(C)\) satisfying \(\sum_{v\in S}\mu_v\min\{\mathrm{swap}(v,r_1),\mathrm{swap}(v,r_2)\}\le q\). The centers need not belong to \(\mathcal D\); equal distances require no tie-breaking.

Is this decision problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite voter lists, deciding whether the \(2\)-Kemeny score is at most a given integer is NP-complete even when all votes are simultaneously single-peaked on one axis and consistent with one balanced group-separating tree.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one, with positive masses only on its explicitly listed support.
  • Required two distinct centers, following the discrete definition as a set of two rankings, and imposed at least two candidates so that such a set exists.
  • Defined L as the total binary input bit length, including the masses and rational threshold.
  • Retained arbitrary rational thresholds, as stated in the continuous question.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. In particular, a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. The finite-list hardness result applies with supplied witnesses

Use the finite-list \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness result stated in the question. Its hardness construction explicitly provides a common single-peaked axis and a balanced binary group-separating tree. The definitions of tree consistency and balance agree with those in this task. Consequently, supplying these witnesses preserves the hardness. See [Theorem 3.1 and its construction](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15812v1#S3.SS1).

Thus we start with a hard instance consisting of a candidate set \(C\), an explicit voter list \(V=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)\), its axis \(\lhd\), its balanced tree \(\mathcal T\), and an integer threshold \(K\). Here \(n\geq 1\), and every vote belongs to \(\mathrm{SP}(\lhd)\cap\mathrm{GS}_{\mathrm{bal}}(\mathcal T)\). The question is whether two unrestricted centers achieve total distance at most \(K\).

2. Polynomial reduction to support encoding

Let \(S\) be the set of distinct rankings occurring in \(V\). For each \(v\in S\), define its multiplicity and mass by

\[ n_v=\bigl|\{i:v_i=v\}\bigr|, \qquad \mu_v=\frac{n_v}{n}. \]

Set the new threshold to \(q=K/n\), and retain \(C,\lhd,\mathcal T\).

Every mass is a positive rational, and \(\sum_{v\in S}\mu_v=1\). Removing duplicate occurrences does not change any ranking, so the required domain condition remains satisfied.

For every pair of centers \(r_1,r_2\in\mathcal L(C)\), grouping identical votes gives the exact identity

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{v\in S}\mu_v \min\{\mathrm{swap}(v,r_1),\mathrm{swap}(v,r_2)\} &= \frac1n\sum_{v\in S}n_v \min\{\mathrm{swap}(v,r_1),\mathrm{swap}(v,r_2)\}\\ &= \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n} \min\{\mathrm{swap}(v_i,r_1),\mathrm{swap}(v_i,r_2)\}. \end{aligned} \]

Since \(n>0\), the left-hand side is at most \(q=K/n\) exactly when the original total is at most \(K\). Ties between the centers do not affect this identity.

The transformation has polynomial cost. Distinct rankings and their multiplicities can be computed by sorting the explicit voter list. Moreover, \(\tau=|S|\leq n\), each mass uses \(O(\log n)\) bits for its numerator and denominator, and encoding \(K/n\) requires only the encoding length of \(K\) plus \(O(\log n)\) bits. Hence all output parameters, including \(L\), are polynomially bounded in the source input length.

This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

3. Requiring distinct centers causes no obstruction

If the source convention permits one center or two identical centers, its feasibility question is still equivalent to the distinct-center version here.

Indeed, because \(m\geq2\), for every ranking \(r\) there exists another ranking \(s\neq r\). For every vote \(v\),

\[ \min\{\mathrm{swap}(v,r),\mathrm{swap}(v,s)\} \leq \mathrm{swap}(v,r). \]

Thus a feasible single center can be extended to two distinct centers without increasing the score. Conversely, two distinct centers are admissible whenever at most two centers are permitted. Therefore the reduction works under either convention.

4. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate consists of two distinct permutations \(r_1,r_2\) of \(C\), requiring \(O(m\log m)\) bits. Their validity and distinctness are directly checkable.

Kendall tau distance counts candidate pairs whose relative orders disagree. Comparing all pairs computes each distance in \(O(m^2)\) time. Therefore all distances needed for verification take \(O(\tau m^2)\) time.

The weighted comparison can also be performed exactly in polynomial time. Write the input rationals as \(\mu_v=a_v/b_v\) and \(q=A/B\), with positive denominators, and let

\[ d_v=\min\{\mathrm{swap}(v,r_1),\mathrm{swap}(v,r_2)\}, \qquad D=B\prod_{v\in S}b_v. \]

The verifier checks the integer inequality

\[ \sum_{v\in S}a_vd_v\frac{D}{b_v} \leq A\frac{D}{B}. \]

The bit length of \(D\) is at most the sum of the input denominator bit lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Also, \(0\leq d_v\leq\binom m2\). All integers in this calculation therefore have polynomial bit length, so exact verification takes polynomial time.

Together with the reduction, this establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.
