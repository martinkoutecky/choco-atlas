Result and intended hypothesis

I assume, as intended, that every listed valuation \(u_t\) satisfies the displayed GM-concavity axiom.

Under this assumption, the auction reaches an equilibrium price from every integer start, for every choice of maximizing directions. A rational mass allocation supporting its final price can then be computed in polynomial time.

Define
\[ \Phi=\max_{t,z\in\Omega}|u_t(z)|,\qquad P=\|p^0\|_\infty,\qquad H=\max\{P,2\Phi\}. \]
The number of rounds, including the two maximizations that end the respective phases, is at most
\[ 4nH+2. \]
Consequently, the worst-case round complexity is \(2^{\Theta(L)}\), in the sense of matching exponential upper and lower bounds in total binary input length. Nevertheless, computing a mass Walrasian equilibrium directly is polynomial-time solvable under the complete-table encoding.

1. Structural facts used

Write \(r=Up\), and define \(h_t(w)=u_t(Uw)\) on the integer box \(U\Omega\). Substitution into the given exchange axiom shows that \(h_t\) is \(M^\natural\)-concave. Its demand at \(r\) is \(UD_t(p)\).

We use the following standard consequences of \(M^\natural\)-concavity:

  • Its indirect utility is submodular: \(g(x)+g(y)\ge g(x\wedge y)+g(x\vee y)\).
  • The convex hull of its demand is a generalized polymatroid.
  • Generalized polymatroids are closed under Minkowski addition.

The first follows from the conjugacy theorem and translation submodularity in [Murota–Shioura–Yang, Section 3.1](https://www.keisu.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/data/2014/METR14-39.pdf). The other facts appear in [Shioura–Yang, Propositions 6.4 and 6.18](https://orsj.org/wp-content/or-archives50/pdf/e_mag/Vol.58_04_410.pdf).

Here a generalized polymatroid admits a description
\[ Q=\{x:\mu(A)\le x(A)\le\rho(A)\text{ for every }A\subseteq N\}, \]
where \(x(A)=\sum_{i\in A}x_i\), with a defining supermodular/submodular pair satisfying the cross inequality. Positive scaling preserves this description and its defining inequalities.

Therefore
\[ G(r)=\mathcal L_\lambda(Ur) \]
is submodular: positive weighted sums preserve submodularity, and its linear term satisfies the submodular inequality with equality. Also, for every price \(p\),
\[ Q_p=\sum_t\lambda_t\operatorname{conv}(UD_t(p)) \]
is a generalized polymatroid. All these applications concern functions on finite integer boxes and positive masses, as required.

2. The auction expression equals the exact unit-step decrease

We first establish the local improvement property needed below.

Local improvement lemma. If \(h\) is \(M^\natural\)-concave on a finite domain and \(x\) does not maximize \(h(w)-a^\top w\), then some feasible elementary move
\[ x'=x\pm e_i \quad\text{or}\quad x'=x-e_i+e_j \]
strictly improves that objective.

Proof. Put \(f(w)=h(w)-a^\top w\); subtracting a linear function preserves the exchange axiom. Choose a maximizer \(y\) minimizing \(\|y-x\|_1\).

If \(x_k>y_k\) for some \(k\), exchange between \(x,y\) gives
\[ f(x)+f(y)\le f(x-e_k+e_\ell)+f(y+e_k-e_\ell), \]
where \(\ell=0\) or \(x_\ell<y_\ell\). The second new point is strictly closer to \(x\). Since \(y\) maximizes \(f\), the first new point has value at least \(f(x)\). Equality would force the second new point to be another maximizer, contradicting the choice of \(y\). Thus the first move strictly improves.

If there is no such \(k\), then \(x\le y\), with strict inequality somewhere. Apply exchange to \(y,x\) at a coordinate where \(y_k>x_k\). The permitted exchange index is necessarily \(0\), and the same argument gives the improving move \(x+e_k\). This proves the lemma.

Now fix integer \(p\), a direction \(d\in C\cup(-C)\), and
\[ z^*\in\arg\min_{z\in D_t(p)}d^\top z. \]
I claim that \(z^*\) remains demanded at \(p+\alpha d\) for every \(0\le\alpha\le1\).

Otherwise, the local improvement lemma, applied in transformed coordinates, supplies an elementary neighbor \(z'\) strictly improving its utility at \(p+\alpha d\). Such a neighbor satisfies
\[ |d^\top(z'-z^*)|\le1, \]
because \(Ud\) has all coordinates in \(\{0,1\}\), or all in \(\{0,-1\}\).

The choice of \(z^*\) excludes a strict improvement by any \(z'\in D_t(p)\). Thus \(z'\notin D_t(p)\). Integer valuations and integer \(p\) imply
\[ u_t(z')-p^\top z' \le u_t(z^*)-p^\top z^*-1. \]
Consequently,
\[ u_t(z')-(p+\alpha d)^\top z' -\bigl(u_t(z^*)-(p+\alpha d)^\top z^*\bigr) \le-1+\alpha\le0, \]
a contradiction.

It follows that
\[ V_t(p+d)=V_t(p)-\min_{z\in D_t(p)}d^\top z. \]
Multiplying by the masses and adding the supply term gives the exact identity
\[ \boxed{\mathcal L_\lambda(p)-\mathcal L_\lambda(p+d)=F_p(d)} \qquad(d\in C\cup(-C)). \]

In particular, maximizing \(F_p\) is precisely minimizing the next Lyapunov value over the permitted unit-step cube.

3. Uniform price bounds and finite termination

The following argument bounds every auction price without using mass denominators.

Suppose \(p_i\ge2\Phi\). Removing one unit of good \(i\) from any bundle changes its net utility by at least
\[ -2\Phi+p_i\ge0. \]
Hence every demanded bundle can be replaced by a demanded bundle with the same other coordinates and coordinate \(i\) equal to zero.

For a permitted direction with \(d_i=1\), let \(d'\) be obtained by setting that coordinate to zero. Then
\[ \min_{z\in D_t(p)}d^\top z =\min_{z\in D_t(p)}(d')^\top z, \]
so
\[ F_p(d)=F_p(d')-\omega_i<F_p(d'). \]
Thus no maximizing direction raises coordinate \(i\) when \(p_i\ge2\Phi\).

Similarly, if \(p_i\le-2\Phi\), adding units until coordinate \(i\) equals \(\omega_i\) never decreases net utility. For \(d_i=-1\), therefore,
\[ F_p(d)=F_p(d')-(M-1)\omega_i<F_p(d'), \]
using \(M\ge2\). No maximizing direction lowers that coordinate.

Since prices and moves are integral, induction gives
\[ -H\le p_i\le H \]
throughout both phases.

In transformed coordinates \(r=Up\), every update in the first phase increases coordinates, and every update in the second phase decreases coordinates. A positive maximum requires a nonzero move, so each update changes the coordinate sum by at least one in its phase’s direction.

Let \(r^1\) be the switching price and \(r^f\) the final price. The number of updates is at most
\[ \sum_i(r_i^1-r_i^0)+\sum_i(r_i^1-r_i^f) \le4nH. \]
Each phase consequently ends in finite time. Adding its terminal maximization gives at most \(4nH+2\) rounds.

4. Every final price supports market clearing

At the start of the descending phase, the stopping condition and the identity from Section 2 give
\[ G(r+a)\ge G(r) \qquad\text{for every }a\in\{0,1\}^n. \]
This property persists after any maximizing descending update.

Indeed, let \(r'=r-b\), where \(b\in\{0,1\}^n\) minimizes \(G(r-b)\). Suppose some \(a\in\{0,1\}^n\) satisfies \(G(r'+a)<G(r')\). Put
\[ x=r\vee(r'+a),\qquad y=r\wedge(r'+a). \]
Then \(x\in r+\{0,1\}^n\), so \(G(x)\ge G(r)\), while \(y\in r-\{0,1\}^n\). Submodularity implies
\[ G(y)\le G(r)+G(r'+a)-G(x) \le G(r'+a)<G(r'). \]
This contradicts the minimizing choice of \(b\).

Thus, at the final price \(p^f\), both stopping conditions hold:
\[ F_{p^f}(d)\le0 \qquad\text{for every }d\in C\cup(-C). \]

Set \(w=U\omega\) and \(Q=Q_{p^f}\). For each \(A\subseteq N\), choose \(a=\mathbf1_A\). Separability of linear optimization over a Minkowski sum gives
\[ F_{p^f}(Ua)=\min_{x\in Q}x(A)-w(A), \]
and
\[ F_{p^f}(-Ua)=w(A)-\max_{x\in Q}x(A). \]
Hence
\[ \min_{x\in Q}x(A)\le w(A)\le\max_{x\in Q}x(A) \qquad\text{for every }A. \]

These inequalities imply \(w\in Q\). To check this explicitly, take any defining generalized-polymatroid description with bounds \(\mu,\rho\). Its validity gives
\[ \mu(A)\le\min_{x\in Q}x(A),\qquad \max_{x\in Q}x(A)\le\rho(A). \]
Thus \(w\) satisfies every defining inequality.

Undoing the transformation, we obtain
\[ \omega\in\sum_t\lambda_t\operatorname{conv}(D_t(p^f)). \]
Write the corresponding convex combinations as
\[ \omega=\sum_{t,z\in D_t(p^f)}\lambda_t\theta_{t,z}z, \qquad \theta_{t,z}\ge0,\qquad \sum_z\theta_{t,z}=1. \]
Then \(q_{t,z}=\lambda_t\theta_{t,z}\) is a market-clearing mass allocation supported on demands.

The defining feasibility system has rational coefficients, so it has a rational feasible solution. Thus the integer terminal price admits the required rational equilibrium allocation. The proof applies to every maximizing choice.

5. The exponential round bound is unavoidable

Take
\[ n=\tau=1,\qquad S_1=N,\qquad \omega_1=1,\qquad \lambda_1=2, \]
with \(u_1(0)=u_1(1)=0\), and start at \(p^0=K>0\). This valuation satisfies GM-concavity.

At every positive price, demand is \(\{0\}\). The ascending phase therefore stops immediately. In the descending phase,
\[ F_p(-1)=1 \]
whenever \(p>0\), so the price decreases by exactly one per update.

At price zero, both bundles are demanded and
\[ F_0(-1)=-1, \]
so the auction stops. It uses exactly \(K+2\) rounds.

Choosing \(K=2^b\) gives input length \(L=\Theta(b)\) and \(2^{\Omega(L)}\) rounds, already with fixed \(n=\tau=1\). Conversely, binary encoding gives \(H\le2^{O(L)}\), so the proved upper bound is \(2^{O(L)}\).

Each round itself can be implemented in polynomial time under the full-table encoding: if \(B=|\Omega|\), then \(B\ge2^n\). Enumerating all \(2^n\) directions and scanning the \(\tau B\) table entries therefore takes polynomial time in \(n,\tau,L\), with polynomial-length rational arithmetic.

6. Direct equilibrium computation is polynomial-time solvable

Consider the rational linear program
\[ \max\sum_{t,z}u_t(z)q_{t,z} \]
subject to
\[ q_{t,z}\ge0,\qquad \sum_zq_{t,z}=\lambda_t,\qquad \sum_{t,z}zq_{t,z}=\omega. \]

It is feasible: for each type, put mass \(\lambda_t/M\) on \(\omega\), and mass \(\lambda_t(1-1/M)\) on zero. The aggregate consumption is exactly \(\omega\). Its feasible region is bounded because \(0\le q_{t,z}\le\lambda_t\).

Its dual is
\[ \min_{\alpha,p}\sum_t\lambda_t\alpha_t+p^\top\omega \]
subject to
\[ \alpha_t+p^\top z\ge u_t(z) \qquad\text{for every }t,z, \]
with unrestricted \(\alpha_t\) and \(p\). It is feasible, for example at \(p=0\) and \(\alpha_t=\max_z u_t(z)\).

The classical polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore produces rational optimal primal and dual solutions. Since every \(\lambda_t>0\), dual optimality forces
\[ \alpha_t=\max_z(u_t(z)-p^\top z)=V_t(p). \]
Complementary slackness gives
\[ q_{t,z}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ \alpha_t+p^\top z=u_t(z) \ \Longrightarrow\ z\in D_t(p). \]
Thus the optimal solutions form a mass Walrasian equilibrium.

There are \(\tau|\Omega|\) primal variables and \(\tau+n\) equality constraints; all coefficients have polynomial total bit length in the supplied tables and other input data. The resulting running time and output length are polynomial in \(n,\tau,L\).

This linear-programming conclusion holds even without GM-concavity. GM-concavity is what guarantees convergence of the specified unit-step auction.
