Partial resolution: a complete implementation for two item classes

I do not settle the polynomial-time versus NP-hardness question for unrestricted $m$.

I prove two new results:

  • The entire implementation problem is polynomial-time solvable when $m\le 2$, with arbitrary support size, binary multiplicities, and unrestricted binary-encoded $\varepsilon$.
  • For arbitrary $m$, a total-variation sampler with polynomial dependence on log-accuracy would suffice. A padding construction converts such a sampler into a law containing the exact ideal distribution as a component. This supplies the prescribed decomposition and allocation privacy without an accuracy requirement involving $e^\varepsilon$.

The missing step for unrestricted $m$ is therefore an allocation sampler of this kind, or an applicable hardness reduction.

1. The ideal labeled distribution

Write $c=\varepsilon/2$. Fix the $N$ labeled agents and $N$ labeled items, and let $\mathcal R$ be their perfect matchings. For $r\in\mathcal R$, let $y(r)$ be its type-by-class flow and define
$$ S_v(r)=\sum_{t,j}y_{tj}(r)v_t(j)=NW(y(r)/N). $$

For a feasible integer flow $y$, partitioning the labeled agents of each type among the item classes gives
$$ \frac{\prod_t a_t!}{\prod_{t,j}y_{tj}!} $$
possibilities. Matching the assigned agents to the labeled items within each class then gives $\prod_j b_j!$ possibilities. Consequently,
$$ |\{r:y(r)=y\}|=\Omega_N(y/N). $$
Every labeled matching induces exactly one flow, so
$$ \sum_y\Omega_N(y/N)=N!, \qquad Z(v)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}e^{cS_v(r)}. $$

Thus the uniform lift of $P_v$ is
$$ D_v(r)=\frac{e^{cS_v(r)}}{Z(v)}. $$

If one labeled agent changes report, the type grouping can change, but $\mathcal R$ remains the same. Moreover,
$$ |S_v(r)-S_{v'}(r)|\le 1 $$
for every matching under such a replacement. Therefore both the unnormalized weights and their normalizers change by factors between $e^{-c}$ and $e^c$, and
$$ D_v(r)\le e^{2c}D_{v'}(r)=e^\varepsilon D_{v'}(r). $$
Summing over any labeled allocation event proves that the ideal lift is $\varepsilon$-DP.

2. A padding construction that resolves the privacy-transfer issue

For distributions $R,P$ on a finite set, use
$$ \|R-P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\frac12\sum_x|R(x)-P(x)|. $$
In particular, $\|R-P\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\alpha$ implies
$$ R(x)\ge P(x)-\alpha $$
for every point $x$.

2.1 An easy full-support distribution on feasible flows

Let
$$ d=\tau m, \qquad k=\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil, \qquad u=2^{-kd}. $$

First compute a feasible integer flow $y^0$ by greedily assigning the minimum remaining row and column demand. Integer margins make every assignment integral. Each assignment exhausts a row or column, so this takes polynomially many binary-arithmetic operations.

Define a distribution $U_v$ as follows:

  • Draw each of the $d$ entries independently using $k$ fair bits, giving an integer between $0$ and $2^k-1$.
  • If the resulting matrix has the prescribed margins, output it.
  • Otherwise output $y^0$.

This uses exactly $kd$ random bits. Every feasible flow has entries at most $N$, so its entries belong to the drawing range. Its particular array of entries is drawn with probability $u$. Hence
$$ U_v(y)\ge u $$
for every feasible flow.

The construction requires neither rejection until feasibility nor an expanded population.

2.2 Exact ideal-law minorization

Choose a dyadic number $h=2^{-r}$, where $r\ge 1$ is the least integer satisfying
$$ h\le\min\{\delta,\gamma/16,1/2\}. $$
This $h$ depends only on the common public parameters, so adjacent profiles use the same value.

Suppose a sampler produces a feasible-flow law $R_v$ with
$$ \|R_v-P_v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\alpha, \qquad \alpha=\frac{hu}{2}. $$
Implement the law
$$ Q_v=(1-h)R_v+hU_v. $$
The mixing coin is implementable with $r$ fair bits.

For every feasible flow,
$$ \begin{aligned} Q_v(y) &\ge (1-h)\bigl(P_v(y)-\alpha\bigr)+hu\\ &\ge (1-h)P_v(y). \end{aligned} $$
Therefore
$$ B_v(y)=\frac{Q_v(y)-(1-h)P_v(y)}h $$
is nonnegative and sums to one. We obtain the exact decomposition
$$ Q_v=(1-h)P_v+hB_v. $$

This is an analytical decomposition; the algorithm does not sample $P_v$ as a branch. It samples $R_v$ or $U_v$.

Taking
$$ \beta=h,\qquad Q_g=P_v,\qquad Q_b=B_v $$
satisfies the prescribed approximation requirement for every value of $\eta$ in the question.

Uniform lifting is linear within each profile. Consequently, the labeled allocation law has the decomposition

For adjacent profiles and any labeled allocation event $A$,

The argument does not require the two profiles to have the same support.

$$ \log_2(1/\alpha)=r+kd+1 $$
is polynomial in the succinct input parameters. In particular, it has no dependence on the magnitude of $\varepsilon$.

3. A succinct allocation sampler for two item classes

Assume $m=2$. Write
$$ k_t=y_{t1}, \qquad y_{t2}=a_t-k_t, \qquad B=b_1. $$
The feasible states are
$$ \mathcal K= \left\{ k\in\mathbb Z^\tau: 0\le k_t\le a_t,\ \sum_t k_t=B \right\}. $$

Set
$$ \theta_t=c\bigl(v_t(1)-v_t(2)\bigr), \qquad f_t(k)=\binom{a_t}{k}e^{\theta_t k}. $$
Indeed,
$$ \Omega_N(y/N)=b_1!b_2!\prod_t\binom{a_t}{k_t}, $$
and
$$ S_v(y)=\sum_t a_tv_t(2) +\sum_t k_t\bigl(v_t(1)-v_t(2)\bigr). $$
The terms independent of $k$ cancel upon normalization. Thus the ideal law is
$$ \pi(k)=\frac1{\mathcal Z}\prod_t f_t(k_t), \qquad k\in\mathcal K. $$

Each sequence $f_t$ is positive on its integer interval and log-concave, since
$$ \frac{f_t(k+1)}{f_t(k)} =e^{\theta_t}\frac{a_t-k}{k+1} $$

3.1 Pair conditionals can be sampled with logarithmic accuracy dependence

A pair conditional has unnormalized weights
$$ f(k)=\binom{A}{k}\binom{C}{s-k}e^{\lambda k} $$
on the integer interval
$$ \ell=\max\{0,s-C\}, \qquad u=\min\{A,s\}. $$
Here $A,C,s\le N$, and $\lambda$ is a binary rational.

I prove that this distribution can be sampled within total-variation error $\xi$ in time polynomial in its input bit length, $\log N$, and $\log(1/\xi)$. The argument includes finite precision and bounded running time.

Computing log-weights

The logarithm of an adjacent ratio is
$$ d(k)= \lambda+\ln(A-k)-\ln(k+1) +\ln(s-k)-\ln(C-s+k+1). $$
It is decreasing wherever defined.

Both $d(k)$ and $\ln f(k)$ can be approximated to absolute error $2^{-p}$ in time polynomial in the input length, $\log N$, and $p$. Here is a justification of the factorial part of that assertion.

For positive integer $n$, Stirling’s expansion gives
$$ \ln(n!)= \left(n+\frac12\right)\ln n-n+\frac12\ln(2\pi) +\sum_{j=1}^{K-1} \frac{B_{2j}}{2j(2j-1)n^{2j-1}} +R_K(n), $$
where the remainder is bounded by the first omitted term. The positive-real hypothesis applies to every $n$ used here. These expansion and remainder statements are given in [DLMF §5.11](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11).

The Bernoulli-number identity
$$ |B_{2K}|=\frac{2(2K)!\zeta(2K)}{(2\pi)^{2K}} $$
and $\zeta(2K)\le 2$ imply the sufficient bound
$$ |B_{2K}|\le 4(2K)^{2K}. $$
The identity is recorded in [DLMF §25.6](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.6).

Take $K=p+10$ and $n_0=8K$. For $n\ge n_0$,
$$ |R_K(n)| \le 32K\,16^{-K} \le 2^{-K}. $$
To avoid computing the constant $\frac12\ln(2\pi)$, subtract the expansion at $n_0$ from that at $n$, using the directly computed integer $n_0!$. The combined remainder is at most $2^{1-K}$. For $n<n_0$, compute $n!$ directly.

The Bernoulli coefficients are computable by their rational recurrence in polynomial time in $K$; their bit lengths are polynomial in $K$. Logarithms of positive binary rationals are computable to the necessary precision by scaling to $r\in[1,2]$ and using
$$ \ln r= 2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}, \qquad z=\frac{r-1}{r+1}, \qquad |z|\le\frac13. $$
The tail decreases geometrically. Multiplication by $n$ requires only an additional $O(\log N)$ guard bits.

Thus $\ln(n!)$, and hence $\ln f(k)$, have the asserted bit complexity. The rational term $\lambda k$ is handled directly; large $\lambda$ increases the bit length of the calculation, not the number of agents processed.

Finding an approximate mode without exact transcendental comparisons

Put
$$ \rho=\frac1{16(N+1)}. $$
Binary-search the decreasing sequence $d(k)$, evaluating each queried value to absolute error at most $\rho$.

If the approximation is greater than $\rho$, its true value is positive. If it is less than $-\rho$, its true value is negative. If its absolute value is at most $\rho$, return that index $M$; then $|d(M)|\le 2\rho$.

If no such ambiguous comparison occurs, ordinary binary search returns an actual mode. In either case, the returned $M$ satisfies
$$ d(k)\ge-2\rho\quad(k<M), \qquad d(k)\le 2\rho\quad(k\ge M), $$
for the ratios that exist.

It follows that, for any two points on the same side of $M$, with one farther from $M$ than the other,
$$ f(\text{farther})\le e^{2\rho N}f(\text{nearer})<2f(\text{nearer}). $$
This follows by summing the appropriate adjacent log-ratios. The search uses $O(\log(N+1))$ evaluations.

A rejection envelope with constant acceptance probability

Partition the interval into the singleton $\{M\}$ and the nonempty blocks on either side whose distances from $M$ lie in
$$ [2^j,2^{j+1}-1], \qquad j=0,1,\ldots. $$
There are $O(\log(N+1))$ blocks.

For a block $I$, let $n_I$ be its endpoint nearest $M$, and define
$$ g(k)=2f(n_I)\quad(k\in I). $$
Use $g(M)=2f(M)$ for the central singleton. The preceding inequality proves $g(k)\ge f(k)$.

Let
$$ Z_f=\sum_k f(k), \qquad M_g=\sum_k g(k). $$
For a block at distances $[2^j,2^{j+1}-1]$ with $j\ge 1$, the preceding block on that side has exactly $2^{j-1}$ points. Every such point has weight at least $f(n_I)/2$. Therefore
$$ \sum_{k\in I}g(k) \le 2^{j+1}f(n_I) \le 8\sum_{k\text{ in preceding block}}f(k). $$
The first block on either side contributes at most $4f(M)$. Summing gives
$$ M_g\le 10f(M)+8\sum_{k\ne M}f(k)\le 10Z_f. $$

An ideal rejection trial draws a block with probability proportional to
$$ 2|I|f(n_I), $$
draws uniformly within it, and accepts $k$ with probability $f(k)/g(k)$. Its acceptance probability is $Z_f/M_g\ge 1/10$. Conditional on acceptance, its output has exactly the target distribution.

Finite-precision implementation

The following elementary numerical observations provide the required bounded-time version.

For a categorical distribution on $J$ points with computable log-weights, one can sample within total-variation error $\kappa$ in time polynomial in $J$, the log-weight input lengths, and $\log(1/\kappa)$:

  • Approximate every log-weight to sufficiently small absolute error. If the error is at most $\vartheta$, the resulting normalized probabilities differ pointwise by factors between $e^{-2\vartheta}$ and $e^{2\vartheta}$.
  • Subtract the largest approximate log-weight. Discard exponentials below $\kappa/(32J)$ and compute the others to absolute error at most $\kappa/(32J)$. The sum before normalization is at least one, so these errors give a controlled normalization error.
  • Round cumulative probabilities to a dyadic grid with spacing at most $\kappa/(4J)$.

Taking $\vartheta\le\kappa/16$ makes the total-variation error less than $\kappa$. Only exponentials with arguments in an interval of length $O(\log(J/\kappa))$ need evaluation. Taylor expansion with its factorial remainder bound evaluates them in polynomial time at the required precision.

A uniform integer from an interval of length $H$ can likewise be sampled within error $\kappa$: draw $b$ bits with $2^b\ge H/\kappa$, reduce modulo $H$, and translate to the interval. The total-variation error is at most $H/2^b$. A Bernoulli coin with computable probability can be approximated within error $\kappa$ by dyadic rounding.

Now cap rejection at
$$ T_0=10\left\lceil\log_2(2/\xi)\right\rceil $$
trials, outputting $M$ upon failure. The ideal capped algorithm has error at most
$$ (9/10)^{T_0}\le e^{-T_0/10}\le \xi/2. $$
Implement the block choice, within-block choice, and acceptance coin with errors at most
$$ \kappa=\frac{\xi}{6T_0} $$
each. Coupling the ideal and implemented trials shows that their outputs disagree with probability at most $3T_0\kappa=\xi/2$. Thus the implemented output has total-variation error at most $\xi$.

All loops are bounded. Their lengths and precision requirements are polynomial in $\log N$, input bit length, and $\log(1/\xi)$. This proves the pair-sampling claim.

3.2 A covering coupling for pair conditionals

The next lemma gives the mixing bound.

Lemma. Let $f_i,f_j$ be positive log-concave sequences on integer intervals $\{0,\ldots,a_i\}$ and $\{0,\ldots,a_j\}$. Let $K_s$ have law
$$ \Pr[K_s=k]\ \propto\ f_i(k)f_j(s-k). $$
For consecutive feasible sums $s,s+1$, there is a coupling such that
$$ K_s\le K_{s+1}\le K_s+1 $$
almost surely.

Proof. On the common support, the ratio of the two probability masses, up to a positive constant independent of $k$, is
$$ \frac{f_j(s+1-k)}{f_j(s-k)}. $$
Log-concavity says that $f_j(r+1)/f_j(r)$ decreases with $r$. Consequently this ratio increases with $k$. Also, the lower and upper support endpoints both weakly increase when $s$ increases.

These properties imply that the difference of the two probability masses changes sign at most once, from negative to positive. Since its total sum is zero, every lower prefix sum is nonpositive. Therefore
$$ K_s\le_{\mathrm{st}}K_{s+1}. $$

For the second inequality, compare the law of $K_{s+1}-1$ with that of $K_s$. On their common support the mass ratio, up to a positive constant, is
$$ \frac{f_i(k+1)}{f_i(k)}, $$
which decreases with $k$. The support endpoints for $K_{s+1}-1$ weakly decrease relative to those for $K_s$. The same prefix-sum argument, with the direction reversed, gives
$$ K_{s+1}-1\le_{\mathrm{st}}K_s. $$

Use one common uniform variable for the quantile functions of $K_s$ and $K_{s+1}$. The two stochastic inequalities then give both asserted pointwise inequalities. $\square$

3.3 Pair resampling mixes in logarithmic population time

For $\tau\ge2$, consider this heat-bath chain on $\mathcal K$:

  • Choose an unordered pair of distinct coordinates uniformly.
  • Preserve their sum and all other coordinates.
  • Resample the pair from its conditional law under $\pi$.

Every update preserves $\pi$: conditional on the untouched coordinates and pair sum, it replaces the pair by precisely its $\pi$-conditional distribution.

Define the integer metric
$$ d(k,k')=\frac12\sum_t|k_t-k'_t|. $$
It is the graph distance for moves transferring one unit between coordinates. To see this, each transfer changes the distance by at most one; conversely, repeatedly transfer from a coordinate exceeding its target value to one below its target value. Such transfers respect the box constraints and reach the target in exactly $d(k,k')$ moves. The diameter is at most $N$.

Consider neighboring states
$$ k'=k+e_p-e_q. $$
Couple their updates using the same chosen pair.

  • If the chosen pair is $\{p,q\}$, its sum and all outside coordinates agree, so use identical conditional draws. The states coalesce.
  • If the pair contains $p$ but not $q$, its sums differ by one. The covering lemma couples its draws so that the additional unit is in exactly one of the two updated coordinates. The opposite discrepancy at $q$ remains, so the new distance is one.
  • The case containing $q$ but not $p$ is identical with the roles reversed.
  • If the pair contains neither, use identical draws; the distance remains one.

With
$$ C=\binom{\tau}{2}, $$
the expected distance after one update is therefore at most $1-1/C$.

For arbitrary states, concatenate these neighboring-state couplings along a shortest path. The couplings can be joined using their common intermediate marginals. The triangle inequality then bounds the expected distance between the endpoint draws by
$$ (1-1/C)d(k,k'). $$

Start one chain from any feasible state and a second from stationarity. Apply such a coupling at each step. After $T$ steps,
$$ \mathbb E[d(K_T,K'_T)] \le N(1-1/C)^T \le Ne^{-T/C}. $$
Distinct states have distance at least one, so the coupling inequality gives
$$ \|\mathcal L(K_T)-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le Ne^{-T/C}. $$

For target error $\alpha\in(0,1)$, take
$$ T=C\left\lceil\log_2(2N/\alpha)\right\rceil. $$
The exact chain then has error at most $\alpha/2$.

Implement each pair choice within error $\alpha/(4T)$ and each conditional draw within error $\alpha/(4T)$, using Section 3.1. Each implemented transition differs from its exact counterpart by at most $\alpha/(2T)$ in total variation. Coupling all $T$ transitions contributes at most another $\alpha/2$.

Hence the final flow law is within $\alpha$ of $P_v$, and the running time is polynomial in
$$ \tau,L,\log N,\log(1/\alpha). $$
When $\tau=1$, the feasible flow is unique and no chain is needed.

This proves the required allocation-sampling result for two item classes. It remains valid for every support arising from a unilateral report change.

4.1 Ideal payments are bounded and exactly truthful

Fix a labeled agent $i$ whose report is $u=v_t$. Let $v^\lambda$ replace only this agent’s report by $\lambda u$, retaining that agent. Define
$$ F(\lambda)=\frac1c\ln Z(v^\lambda), \qquad f(\lambda)=\mathbb E_{D_{v^\lambda}}[u(r(i))]. $$
The partition sum is finite and positive. Differentiation therefore gives
$$ F'(\lambda)=f(\lambda), \qquad f'(\lambda)=c\,\operatorname{Var}_{D_{v^\lambda}}(u(r(i)))\ge0. $$
Thus $f$ is nondecreasing and belongs to $[0,1]$.

Exchangeability at $\lambda=1$ identifies $f(1)$ with the allocation-value expectation in the question. Hence
$$ p_t=f(1)-\int_0^1f(\lambda)\,d\lambda, \qquad 0\le p_t\le1. $$

For truthfulness, let $H(D)=-\sum_rD(r)\ln D(r)$ and define
$$ \mathcal F_v(D)=\mathbb E_D[S_v]+\frac{H(D)}c. $$
Direct substitution of $D_v(r)=e^{cS_v(r)}/Z(v)$ gives
$$ \mathcal F_v(D)=\frac{\ln Z(v)}c-\frac{\operatorname{KL}(D\Vert D_v)}c. $$
Relative entropy is nonnegative: applying $-\ln z\ge1-z$ on the positive support of $D$ proves this even when $D$ has zero probabilities. Thus $D_v$ maximizes $\mathcal F_v$.

If agent $i$ reports $w_i$ while her true valuation is $v_i$, let $w$ be the resulting profile and let $Z_0$ zero only this agent. Her expected ideal utility is
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E_{D_w}[v_i(r(i))]-p_i(w) &=\mathbb E_{D_w}[v_i(r(i))-w_i(r(i))] +\frac{\ln Z(w)-\ln Z_0}{c}\\ &=\mathcal F_v(D_w)-\frac{\ln Z_0}{c}. \end{aligned} $$
The last equality follows from the preceding entropy identity applied to $D_w$. Since $Z_0$ is independent of her report, maximization by $D_v$ proves exact incentive compatibility.

4.2 Payment estimation from total-variation samples

Assume the allocation sampler is available on the original and auxiliary profiles. For $m=2$, Section 3 provides it.

Let
$$ \alpha_p=\min\{1/8,\gamma/64\}. $$
Choose $H$ to be the least power of two satisfying
$$ H\ge\max\{1,16/\gamma\}, $$
and choose $K$ to be the least integer satisfying
$$ K\ge\max\{1,256/\gamma^2\}. $$
In particular,
$$ H<2\max\{1,16/\gamma\}, \qquad K<1+\max\{1,256/\gamma^2\}. $$

For an auxiliary profile $v^\lambda$, let $s$ denote the reported type containing the distinguished agent, after equal reports are merged. For a sampled flow define
$$ h_\lambda(y)=\frac1{a_s}\sum_jy_{sj}u(j). $$
This statistic uses the agent’s original valuation $u$. Under an ideal sample and uniform lifting, agents in group $s$ have the same conditional class probabilities, so
$$ \mathbb E[h_\lambda(y)]=f(\lambda). $$
Also $0\le h_\lambda(y)\le1$.

For each of $K$ independent repetitions:

  • Sample $A_k=h_1(y)$ from a flow law within $\alpha_p$ of $P_v$.
  • Choose $\ell$ uniformly from $\{0,\ldots,H-1\}$ and sample $B_k=h_{\ell/H}(y')$ from a law within $\alpha_p$ of $P_{v^{\ell/H}}$.

Set
$$ \overline D=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K(A_k-B_k), \qquad \widehat p_t=\min\{1,\max\{0,\overline D\}\}. $$

Monotonicity gives the Riemann-sum bound
$$ 0\le \int_0^1f(\lambda)\,d\lambda -\frac1H\sum_{\ell=0}^{H-1}f(\ell/H) \le\frac1H. $$
Total-variation error changes expectations of $[0,1]$-valued statistics by at most $\alpha_p$. Consequently,
$$ |\mathbb E\overline D-p_t|\le\frac1H+2\alpha_p. $$
Each summand is in $[-1,1]$, so independence gives
$$ \operatorname{Var}(\overline D)\le\frac1K. $$
Projection onto $[0,1]$ cannot increase distance from $p_t\in[0,1]$. Therefore
$$ \begin{aligned} |\mathbb E\widehat p_t-p_t| &\le\mathbb E|\overline D-p_t|\\ &\le\frac1{\sqrt K}+\frac1H+2\alpha_p\\ &\le\frac{5\gamma}{32}<\frac\gamma4. \end{aligned} $$

Splitting off the distinguished agent increases support by at most one. Scaling by $\ell/H$ adds only polynomially many bits. The uniform grid choice needs $\log_2H$ fair bits, and the grid is not explicitly enumerated. There are
$$ O\!\left(\tau\max\{1,\gamma^{-2}\}\right) $$
sampling calls.

4.3 Approximate truthfulness

The padded allocation satisfies
$$ Q_v=(1-h)P_v+hB_v, \qquad \|Q_v-P_v\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le h\le\gamma/16. $$
Uniform lifting preserves this total-variation bound because compatible-matchings sets for distinct flows are disjoint.

Thus, for any fixed true valuation in $[0,1]^m$, the allocation-value expectation changes by at most $\gamma/16$. The payment bias is at most $\gamma/4$. Approximate expected utility differs from ideal expected utility by at most $5\gamma/16$ at every report.

Comparing truthful and deviating reports and using ideal truthfulness bounds the deviation gain by
$$ 2\cdot\frac{5\gamma}{16}=\frac{5\gamma}{8}<\gamma. $$
This applies also to deviations changing the support.

5. Welfare and the resulting running time

For $s\ge0$, define
$$ A_s= \left\{ x: W(x)<W^*-\gamma-\frac{\ln(N!)+s}{cN} \right\}. $$
An optimal feasible flow has at least one compatible matching, so
$$ Z(v)\ge e^{cNW^*}. $$
Every matching whose flow belongs to $A_s$ has weight less than
$$ e^{cNW^*-cN\gamma-\ln(N!)-s}. $$
There are at most $N!$ such matchings. Therefore
$$ P_v(A_s)\le e^{-cN\gamma-s}. $$
The padded law satisfies
$$ Q_v(A_s) \le(1-h)e^{-cN\gamma-s}+h \le e^{-s}+\delta. $$
Since $1/(cN)=2/(\varepsilon N)$, this is exactly the requested threshold and tail bound.

For $m=2$, the allocation call in Section 2 uses accuracy
$$ \alpha=2^{-(r+k\tau m+1)}. $$
Section 3 samples to this accuracy in polynomial time. The payment calls also have polynomial support size, bit length, and log-accuracy. All random draws and rejection loops have bounded polynomial running time.

Consequently, for two item classes the entire implementation runs in randomized time polynomial in
$$ m,\tau,L,\log N,\varepsilon^{-1}, \gamma^{-1},\log(\delta^{-1}). $$
In fact, its sampling component needs no dependence on the magnitude of $\varepsilon$ beyond its encoding length.

For $m=1$, the flow is unique, the labeled allocation can be uniform independently of reports, and the ideal payments are zero. This gives the trivial exact implementation.

6. What remains unresolved

The preceding argument completely handles $m\le2$.

For unrestricted $m$, the padding, payment, incentive, privacy, and welfare arguments remain valid conditional on a feasible-flow total-variation sampler with polynomial dependence on log-accuracy. The two-class sampler works because flows become coordinate counts subject to a single sum constraint. Its covering coupling has not been extended here to general transportation tables.

Thus this write-up establishes neither an implementation for unrestricted $m$ nor NP-hardness. The earlier unrestricted-$\varepsilon$ privacy-transfer obstacle is resolved by the padding construction; the remaining difficulty is sampling the general factorial-weighted transportation distribution succinctly.
