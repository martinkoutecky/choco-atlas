The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) items, a distribution \(\mu\) over observable worker types \(t\), type-specific Gaussian response variances \(\sigma_t^2\), per-capita task capacities \(b_t\), a Gaussian prior with variance \(\sigma_x^2\), and a total task budget \(B\), choose query masses \(q_{ijt}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{i<j}q_{ijt}\le b_t\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,i<j}q_{ijt}=B\) to maximize \(\frac12\log\det\!\left(I+\sigma_x^2\sum_{t,i<j}\frac{q_{ijt}}{\sigma_t^2}d_{ij}d_{ij}^{\top}\right)\), where \(d_{ij}=e_i-e_j\). Does this continuous design problem admit an efficient \(\varepsilon\)-optimal algorithm, and how does its optimum compare with the paper’s sequential greedy EIG rule?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m=n\ge2\) items. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) observable worker types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has binary-rational variance \(\sigma_t^2>0\) and per-capita capacity \(b_t\ge0\). The prior is \(x\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_x^2 I_m)\), with binary-rational \(\sigma_x^2>0\); a query of type \(t\) on \(i<j\) observes \(x_i-x_j\) plus independent Gaussian noise of variance \(\sigma_t^2\). Every pair is available, and repeated queries are allowed.
The binary-rational budget satisfies \(0<B\le\sum_t b_t\mu_t\). Choose real masses \(q_{ijt}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{i<j}q_{ijt}\le b_t\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,i<j}q_{ijt}=B\).
With \(d_{ij}=e_i-e_j\), maximize \(F(q)=\frac12\log\det\bigl(I_m+\sigma_x^2\sum_{t,i<j}(q_{ijt}/\sigma_t^2)d_{ij}d_{ij}^{\top}\bigr)\); logarithms are natural. Write \(F^*=\max_q F(q)\). Additive \(\varepsilon\)-optimality means \(F(q)\ge F^*-\varepsilon\), where \(0<\varepsilon<1\) is binary rational; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
For comparison, on homogeneous instances \(\tau=1\) with integer \(B\), sequential greedy EIG starts with no observations and makes \(B\) unit queries, each maximizing the posterior-predictive expectation of KL divergence from the current posterior to the updated posterior. Ties use lexicographic order; let \(q^{\mathrm{gr}}\) be its query counts.

Does this continuous problem admit an additive \(\varepsilon\)-optimal algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\), or is achieving this accuracy NP-hard?
On the homogeneous integer-budget instances, what is the sharp worst-case gap \(F^*-\mathbb E[F(q^{\mathrm{gr}})]\) as a function of \(m,B,\sigma_x^2/\sigma_1^2\)?

For discrete Gaussian queries, [Xu et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11619/11478) give rank-one posterior updates and constant-time pairwise EIG evaluation given stored posterior parameters; this is a local computation result, not a global optimality guarantee.
For continuous designs, [Alexanderian](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.11466v3) establishes the Gaussian EIG–posterior-log-determinant identity, and [Boyd–Vandenberghe](https://www.seas.ucla.edu/~vandenbe/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf) treat ordinary fractional D-optimal design as convex optimization; these results do not state the capacity-constrained problem and greedy-gap comparison above.

  • Defined greedy EIG through posterior-predictive expected KL rather than the erroneous displayed simplification.

2. Proven: an exact polynomial-time continuous optimum

Write
$$ r=m-1,\qquad M=\binom m2,\qquad a_t=b_t\mu_t. $$
Order the supported types so that
$$ \sigma_1^2\le\cdots\le\sigma_\tau^2. $$
Define
$$ Q_t^*= \min\left\{ a_t,\, \max\left\{0,B-\sum_{u<t}a_u\right\} \right\}, \qquad S^*=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\frac{Q_t^*}{\sigma_t^2}. $$
Then
$$ q_{ijt}^*=\frac{Q_t^*}{M} $$
is an exact optimum, with value
$$ F^*=\frac r2\log\left(1+\frac{2\sigma_x^2S^*}{r}\right). $$

Proof. For any feasible design, let
$$ Q_t=\sum_{i<j}q_{ijt}, \qquad S=\sum_t\frac{Q_t}{\sigma_t^2}, \qquad L_q=\sum_{t,i<j}\frac{q_{ijt}}{\sigma_t^2}d_{ij}d_{ij}^{\top}. $$
The matrix $L_q$ is positive semidefinite, annihilates $\mathbf1$, and has trace $2S$. Consequently, its $r$ eigenvalues on $\mathbf1^\perp$, including zeros, have sum $2S$. Concavity of the logarithm gives
$$ F(q)\le \frac r2\log\left(1+\frac{2\sigma_x^2S}{r}\right). $$

Put
$$ P=I_m-\frac{\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top}{m}. $$
Directly comparing diagonal and off-diagonal entries gives
$$ \sum_{i<j}d_{ij}d_{ij}^{\top}=mP. $$
Therefore uniform allocation within each type gives
$$ L_q=\frac{mS}{M}P=\frac{2S}{r}P, $$
and attains the preceding bound.

It remains to maximize $S$ over
$$ 0\le Q_t\le a_t,\qquad \sum_tQ_t=B. $$
If a type of smaller variance has unused capacity while a type of larger variance receives positive mass, transferring mass to the former does not decrease $S$. Successively making these transfers produces the displayed $Q_t^*$. The assumption $B\le\sum_ta_t$ ensures that their sum is $B$.

All operations producing $q^*$ are rational comparisons and arithmetic. Products and sums of input rationals have polynomial bit length, and the output has $M\tau$ entries. Thus the algorithm runs in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$. It is additive $\varepsilon$-optimal for every allowed $\varepsilon$, without evaluating any logarithm. $\square$

3. Proven: greedy is deterministic, including under the literal KL direction

For homogeneous instances, set
$$ \alpha=\frac{\sigma_x^2}{\sigma_1^2}. $$
Let $L_k$ be the multigraph Laplacian after $k$ unit queries. Gaussian conditioning gives posterior covariance
$$ C_k=\sigma_x^2(I_m+\alpha L_k)^{-1}. $$
In particular, this covariance depends on the query counts and not on the observed values.

Here is the precise KL identity needed below. For positive definite covariance matrices $V_1,V_2$,
$$ D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left( \mathcal N(h_1,V_1)\,\middle\|\,\mathcal N(h_2,V_2) \right) = \frac12\left[ \log\frac{\det V_2}{\det V_1} -m+\operatorname{tr}(V_2^{-1}V_1) +(h_2-h_1)^\top V_2^{-1}(h_2-h_1) \right]. $$
This identity is also stated in [Alexanderian’s Gaussian D-optimality derivation](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.11466v3).

Suppose the current posterior is $\mathcal N(h,C)$ and the next query uses $d=d_{ij}$. Write $v=\sigma_1^2$ and
$$ s=\frac{d^\top Cd}{v}. $$
The updated covariance and mean are
$$ C'=C-\frac{Cdd^\top C}{v+d^\top Cd}, \qquad h'=h+\frac{Cd}{v+d^\top Cd}(y-d^\top h). $$
Under the posterior-predictive distribution,
$$ \mathbb E[h'-h]=0,\qquad \mathbb E[(h'-h)(h'-h)^\top]=C-C'. $$
Also,
$$ C'^{-1}=C^{-1}+\frac{dd^\top}{v}, \qquad \frac{\det C}{\det C'}=1+s. $$
Substituting into the KL identity yields
$$ \mathbb E[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\text{updated}\,\|\,\text{current})] =\frac12\log(1+s). $$
Indeed, the expected trace and mean terms sum to $m$.

In the literal direction requested in the question,
$$ \mathbb E[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\text{current}\,\|\,\text{updated})] =s-\frac12\log(1+s), $$
because
$$ \operatorname{tr}(C'^{-1}C)=m+s, \qquad \operatorname{tr}(C'^{-1}(C-C'))=s. $$
Both functions are strictly increasing in $s\ge0$. Thus either convention selects precisely the pairs maximizing
$$ R_k(i,j)=d_{ij}^\top(I_m+\alpha L_k)^{-1}d_{ij}. $$

Lexicographic tie-breaking consequently makes the entire greedy query sequence deterministic. Hence
$$ \mathbb E[F(q^{\mathrm{gr}})]=F(q^{\mathrm{gr}}). $$
The continuous benchmark is
$$ F^*=\frac r2\log\left(1+\frac{2\alpha B}{r}\right). $$

At fixed $m,B,\alpha$, every feasible homogeneous instance has this same greedy sequence and benchmark: its capacity affects only feasibility. Therefore each exact gap below is also the sharp worst-case gap for its stated parameters.

4. Proven: exact gaps for every $m$ through budget $m+1$

4.1. The path-merging phase

There is a unique $\rho\in(0,1)$ satisfying
$$ \alpha=\frac{\rho}{(1-\rho)^2}, $$
because the right-hand side increases continuously from zero to infinity.

For a path on $s$ vertices, define
$$ D_s=\det(I_s+\alpha L_{P_s}) = \frac{1-\rho^{2s}} {(1-\rho^2)(1-\rho)^{2s-2}}. $$
In particular, $D_1=1$.

Starting with $m$ component sizes equal to $1$, repeatedly replace the two smallest sizes by their sum. Let $\mathcal S_B$ be the resulting multiset after $B$ replacements. For $B\le m-1$,
$$ \boxed{ F^*-F(q^{\mathrm{gr}}) = \frac r2\log\left(1+\frac{2\alpha B}{r}\right) -\frac12\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_B}\log D_s. } $$

The following calculation proves the asserted greedy structure.

Let
$$ K_s=(I_s+\alpha L_{P_s})^{-1}. $$
For consecutively indexed path vertices,
$$ (K_s)_{ij} = \frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho} \frac{ \rho^{|i-j|} +\rho^{i+j-1} +\rho^{2s+1-i-j} +\rho^{2s-|i-j|} } {1-\rho^{2s}}. $$
For verification when $s\ge2$, put
$$ f_i=\rho^{-i}+\rho^{i-1}, \qquad g_i=\rho^i+\rho^{2s+1-i}, \qquad \kappa=\frac{1-\rho}{(1+\rho)(1-\rho^{2s})}. $$
For $i\le j$, the proposed entry is $\kappa f_i g_j$. The relation
$$ 1+2\alpha=\alpha(\rho+\rho^{-1}) $$
verifies the interior recurrence. The endpoint conditions follow from $f_0=f_1$ and $g_{s+1}=g_s$. The diagonal equation follows from
$$ \alpha(f_{j+1}g_j-f_jg_{j+1}) = \frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}(1-\rho^{2s}) =\kappa^{-1}. $$
The case $s=1$ is direct.

The maximum diagonal entry occurs at an endpoint and equals
$$ v_s= \frac{(1-\rho)(1+\rho^{2s-1})}{1-\rho^{2s}} = 1-\rho+ \frac{(1-\rho^2)\rho^{2s-1}}{1-\rho^{2s}}. $$
To see endpoint maximality, the position-dependent diagonal terms are
$$ \rho^{2i-1}+\rho^{2s+1-2i}. $$
Their exponents have fixed sum; convexity of the exponential makes their sum largest at an endpoint. The last expression for $v_s$ also shows that $v_s>1-\rho$ and that $v_s$ strictly decreases with $s$.

The covariance between path endpoints is
$$ c_s=\frac{(1-\rho^2)\rho^{s-1}}{1-\rho^{2s}}. $$
Their pair score is therefore
$$ R_{\mathrm{end}}(s) = 2(v_s-c_s) = 2(1-\rho)\frac{1-\rho^{s-1}}{1+\rho^s} <2(1-\rho). $$

Every within-path pair has score below $2(1-\rho)$. Indeed, remove the edges attaching its intervening subpath to the rest of the path. This decreases the positive definite precision matrix, so it increases the inverse in positive semidefinite order. The original score is at most the endpoint score of that isolated subpath.

Conversely, endpoints in different components of sizes $s,t$ have score
$$ v_s+v_t>2(1-\rho). $$
Thus greedy joins different components whenever more than one remains. It chooses endpoints, preserving the path structure, and chooses the two smallest components because $v_s$ decreases with $s$. This proves the path-merging rule.

For completeness, the determinant calculation uses leading tridiagonal determinants $h_k$ satisfying
$$ h_0=1,\qquad h_1=1+\alpha,\qquad h_k=(1+2\alpha)h_{k-1}-\alpha^2h_{k-2}. $$
Solving this recurrence gives
$$ h_k= \frac{1+\rho^{2k+1}}{(1+\rho)(1-\rho)^{2k}}. $$
For $s\ge2$,
$$ D_s=(1+\alpha)h_{s-1}-\alpha^2h_{s-2}, $$
which gives the displayed expression for $D_s$. Determinants multiply across path components, completing the gap proof.

4.2. Budget $m$: the cycle

Assume $m\ge3$. After $m-1$ queries, greedy has one path on all vertices. The function $R_{\mathrm{end}}(s)$ strictly increases with $s$: writing $t=\rho^s$, its variable factor is
$$ \frac{1-t/\rho}{1+t}, $$
which strictly decreases with $t$.

Every pair other than the full path’s endpoints spans a shorter subpath. The preceding edge-removal bound therefore proves that the full path’s endpoints uniquely maximize the next score. Greedy closes the path into $C_m$.

Its determinant is
$$ D_{C_m} = \prod_{j=0}^{m-1} \frac{1-2\rho\cos(2\pi j/m)+\rho^2}{(1-\rho)^2} = \frac{(1-\rho^m)^2}{(1-\rho)^{2m}}. $$
The last equality follows by factoring the numerator over the $m$th roots of unity. Hence
$$ \boxed{ F^*-F(q^{\mathrm{gr}}) = \frac r2\log\left(1+\frac{2\alpha m}{r}\right) -\log\left(\frac{1-\rho^m}{(1-\rho)^m}\right). } $$

4.3. New result: budget $m+1$

For vertices at cyclic distance $h$, the cycle inverse has entry
$$ (K_{C_m})_{uv} = \frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho} \frac{\rho^h+\rho^{m-h}}{1-\rho^m}. $$
One verification is to periodize the infinite-line inverse kernel
$$ \frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}\rho^{|u-v|}. $$
That kernel satisfies the tridiagonal equations because
$$ 1+2\alpha=\alpha(\rho+\rho^{-1}), \qquad \frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}\bigl(1+2\alpha(1-\rho)\bigr)=1. $$
Summing its shifts by multiples of $m$ gives the displayed cycle entry.

Thus the score at distance $h$ is
$$ R_{C_m}(h) = \frac{2(1-\rho)}{1+\rho} \frac{(1-\rho^h)(1-\rho^{m-h})}{1-\rho^m}. $$
For integer $h<\lfloor m/2\rfloor$,
$$ \bigl(\rho^{h+1}+\rho^{m-h-1}\bigr) -\bigl(\rho^h+\rho^{m-h}\bigr) = (1-\rho)(\rho^{m-h-1}-\rho^h)<0. $$
Therefore the score strictly increases up to maximum cyclic distance. All pairs at that distance have the same score.

Put
$$ h_*=\left\lfloor\frac m2\right\rfloor. $$
By the matrix determinant lemma, the next query multiplies the determinant by $1+\alpha R_{C_m}(h_*)$. Consequently, for every $m\ge3$,
$$ \boxed{ F^*-F(q^{\mathrm{gr}}) = \frac r2\log\left(1+\frac{2\alpha(m+1)}r\right) -\frac12\log D_{C_m} -\frac12\log\bigl(1+\alpha R_{C_m}(h_*)\bigr) \quad\text{when }B=m+1. } $$
For $m=3$, this query repeats an existing pair, as permitted.

4.4. An unbounded-gap consequence

When $B\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor$, greedy produces a matching, so
$$ F^*-F(q^{\mathrm{gr}}) = \frac r2\log\left(1+\frac{2\alpha B}{r}\right) -\frac B2\log(1+2\alpha). $$

More generally, the path determinant formula gives
$$ \frac{D_s}{\alpha^{s-1}} = \frac{1-\rho^{2s}}{1-\rho^2}\rho^{-(s-1)} \longrightarrow s \qquad\text{as }\alpha\longrightarrow\infty. $$
Since $\mathcal S_B$ has $m-B$ components,
$$ F^*-F(q^{\mathrm{gr}}) = \frac{r-B}{2}\log\alpha +\frac r2\log\left(\frac{2B}{r}\right) -\frac12\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_B}\log s +o(1). $$
Thus the gap is unbounded in $\alpha$ whenever $B<m-1$. Taking $\alpha$ through powers of two respects the rational-input requirement.

5. Proven: exact all-budget gaps for $m\le6$

5.1. Two and three vertices

For $m=3$, let the three edge counts be $a,b,c$, with sum $B$. Expansion by principal minors gives
$$ \det(I_3+\alpha L) = 1+2\alpha B+3\alpha^2(ab+ac+bc). $$
Increasing the count $a$ increases the quadratic term by $b+c=B-a$. Greedy therefore increments a smallest count. The final counts differ by at most one, and
$$ ab+ac+bc=\left\lfloor\frac{B^2}{3}\right\rfloor. $$

The following bounds apply to every $m,B,\alpha$, but are not asserted to be sharp.

$$ \Delta=F^*-F(q^{\mathrm{gr}}), \qquad a=\frac r{2\alpha}. $$

$$ 0\le\Delta \le \frac12\sum_{j=1}^{r-1} \log\left( \frac{(a+B)(a+j)}{a(a+B+j)} \right). $$

Proof. Let $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r$ be the eigenvalues of $I_m+\alpha L_k$ on $\mathbf1^\perp$. Their sum is $r+2\alpha k$. Averaging scaled pair scores gives
$$ \frac1M\sum_{i<j}\alpha R_k(i,j) = \frac{2\alpha}{r}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\lambda_\ell^{-1}. $$
Cauchy–Schwarz yields
$$ \sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\lambda_\ell^{-1} \ge\frac{r^2}{r+2\alpha k}. $$
The selected query therefore has scaled score at least $r/(a+k)$, and increases $F$ by at least
$$ \frac12\log\left(1+\frac r{a+k}\right). $$
Summing and cancelling common factors gives
$$ F(q^{\mathrm{gr}}) \ge \frac12\sum_{j=0}^{r-1} \log\left(\frac{a+B+j}{a+j}\right). $$
Subtracting this from $F^*=\frac r2\log((a+B)/a)$ proves the bound. $\square$

There is also a lower bound applying to every integral design. Write
$$ B=Mk+s,\qquad 0\le s<M. $$
Then
$$ \Delta\ge \frac{\alpha^2s(M-s)} {2M(1+2\alpha B)^2}. $$

Proof. Put
$$ H=1+2\alpha B,\qquad c=1+\frac{2\alpha B}{r}. $$
Every contrast precision eigenvalue lies in $[1,H]$, and their average is $c$. Since $(\log x)''\le-H^{-2}$ on this interval,
$$ \log\lambda \le \log c+\frac{\lambda-c}{c} -\frac{(\lambda-c)^2}{2H^2}. $$
Summing cancels the linear terms. Therefore, for any integral design $q$,
$$ F^*-F(q) \ge \frac{\alpha^2}{4H^2} \left\|L_q-\frac{2B}{r}P\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2 \ge \frac{\alpha^2}{2H^2} \sum_{i<j}\left(q_{ij}-\frac BM\right)^2. $$
The last inequality uses the two symmetric off-diagonal entries for each pair.

For integer counts summing to $B$, transferring one unit between counts differing by at least two strictly decreases the final squared sum. Its minimum is attained by $s$ counts equal to $k+1$ and $M-s$ counts equal to $k$, giving minimum $s(M-s)/M$. This proves the assertion. $\square$
