The continuous mirror question, as graded

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) preference types with positive binary rational masses \(\rho_t\), where \(\sum_t\rho_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The cake is \(C=[0,1]\), partitioned into \(q\geq1\) interval cells \(E_k\) with rational endpoints and positive lengths. Type \(t\) has constant rational density \(d_{tk}\geq0\) on \(E_k\), defining \(\nu_t(B)=\sum_k d_{tk}\lambda(B\cap E_k)\), where \(\lambda\) is Lebesgue measure and \(\nu_t(C)=1\).
Assume mutual absolute continuity: on every cell, either all types have zero density or all have positive density. Cells are completely divisible.
An allocation \((B_t)_{t\in T}\) is a measurable partition of the entire cake; pieces may be disconnected. Compute exactly \(z^*=\max_{(B_t)}\min_{t\in T}\nu_t(B_t)/\rho_t\) and an attaining allocation, represented by finite unions of intervals with rational endpoints. Any attaining allocation is acceptable.
Is this exact optimization problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m=q\), \(\tau\), and the total binary input length \(L\).
For finitely many recipients, a subgradient convergence result under complete divisibility and mutual absolute continuity gives no polynomial running-time bound. [Dall’Aglio and Di Luca](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.4241).

1. Result

The exact optimization problem is polynomial-time solvable in the binary Turing model. Both \(z^*\) and an attaining allocation with rational endpoints can be computed in time \((m\tau+L)^{O(1)}\), where \(m=q\).

The algorithm uses the \(\tau\) explicitly listed support types directly. Binary rational masses do not require expanding types into individual recipients.

2. An exact linear program

Order the cells from left to right, and write their endpoints as \(a_k,b_k\), with length \(\ell_k=b_k-a_k>0\). Endpoint ownership does not affect any valuation because every valuation is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure.

Introduce a variable \(x_{tk}\) for the length of cell \(k\) allocated to type \(t\), and a variable \(z\). Consider the following linear program:

Maximize \(z\), subject to

  • \(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}x_{tk}=\ell_k\) for every \(k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\);
  • \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{m}d_{tk}x_{tk}\geq \rho_t z\) for every \(t\in T\);
  • \(x_{tk}\geq0\) for every \(t,k\), and \(z\geq0\).

All coefficients are rational. Keeping \(\rho_t\) on the right avoids even the need to divide density coefficients by masses.

Feasibility. Set \(x_{tk}=\rho_t\ell_k\) and \(z=1\). The cell constraints hold because \(\sum_t\rho_t=1\). For each type,

\(\displaystyle \sum_k d_{tk}x_{tk}=\rho_t\sum_k d_{tk}\ell_k=\rho_t\nu_t(C)=\rho_t.\)

Thus this is feasible.

Boundedness and attainment. The cell constraints imply \(0\leq x_{tk}\leq\ell_k\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \rho_tz\leq\sum_kd_{tk}x_{tk}\leq\sum_kd_{tk}\ell_k=1.\)

Choose a type with maximum mass. Its mass is at least \(1/\tau\), so \(z\leq\tau\). The feasible region is therefore nonempty, closed, and bounded. It is a compact polytope, and the objective attains its maximum.

3. Equivalence and construction of the allocation

Every measurable allocation gives an LP solution

Given a measurable partition \((B_t)_{t\in T}\), define

\(\displaystyle x_{tk}=\lambda(B_t\cap E_k).\)

The partition property gives \(\sum_tx_{tk}=\ell_k\). Constancy of the densities gives

\(\displaystyle \nu_t(B_t)=\sum_kd_{tk}x_{tk}.\)

Therefore, setting \(z=\min_t\nu_t(B_t)/\rho_t\) gives a feasible LP solution with exactly the allocation’s objective value. Hence every allocation’s objective value is at most the LP optimum.

Every feasible length matrix is realizable

Fix an ordering \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\). Given any feasible length matrix, define cutpoints

\(\displaystyle s_{k,0}=a_k,\qquad s_{k,t}=a_k+\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{ik}\quad(1\leq t\leq\tau).\)

Nonnegativity makes these cutpoints nondecreasing, and the cell constraint gives \(s_{k,\tau}=b_k\).

Assign the half-open interval \([s_{k,t-1},s_{k,t})\) to type \(t\), omitting empty intervals. These intervals partition \([0,1)\). Assign the point \(1\) to the type owning the last nonempty interval of the final cell, by closing that interval at its right endpoint.

This constructs an exact measurable partition of the entire cake. Each type receives at most \(m\) intervals, and

\(\displaystyle \lambda(B_t\cap E_k)=x_{tk},\qquad \nu_t(B_t)=\sum_kd_{tk}x_{tk}.\)

Apply this construction to an optimal LP solution \((x^*,z_{\mathrm{LP}})\). Every resulting ratio is at least \(z_{\mathrm{LP}}\). Together with the preceding upper bound, this proves

\(\displaystyle z^*=z_{\mathrm{LP}}.\)

If \(x^*\) is rational, every constructed cutpoint is rational. Thus exact rational LP optimization supplies precisely the requested output representation.

4. Exactness, bit lengths, and running time

Polynomial-time LP theorem and its hypotheses

The standard rational linear-programming theorem provides exact optimization in time polynomial in the numbers of variables and constraints and the total binary encoding length. For a nonempty bounded rational polytope, an optimal vertex can also be obtained in polynomial time. See [Yang Cai’s thesis, Section 2.6, Theorem 1](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/cai/publication/thesis/thesis.pdf).

The hypotheses hold here: the coefficients and objective are rational, and Section 2 established nonemptiness and boundedness. Lower dimensionality caused by the cell equalities is permitted.

Our LP has

\(\displaystyle N=m\tau+1\)

variables. Replacing each equality by two inequalities gives

\(\displaystyle R=2m+\tau+N\)

inequalities, including nonnegativity.

Computing the lengths \(\ell_k\) increases their total encoding length by only a constant factor: each is a difference of two input rationals, and each endpoint participates in at most two such differences. The remaining nonconstant coefficients are copied from the input. A dense LP encoding therefore has length

\(\displaystyle S=O(L+NR).\)

Thus exact LP optimization takes \(\operatorname{poly}(N,R,S)\), hence \(\operatorname{poly}(m\tau,m+\tau,L)\), bit operations.

Polynomial bit lengths of the exact output

For completeness, rational output size can be bounded directly.

At a vertex, the active constraint normals span the full variable space. Indeed, otherwise a nonzero vector orthogonal to all active normals would allow sufficiently small perturbations in both directions while preserving all constraints, contradicting extremality. Select \(N\) independent active constraints.

Clear denominators in these selected equations. Each row’s rational data has total encoding length \(O(L)\), so the resulting integer coefficients and right-hand sides have absolute value at most \(2^{cL}\) for an absolute constant \(c\). Let \(A\) be the resulting nonsingular square matrix.

By Hadamard’s inequality, both its determinant and every determinant obtained by replacing one column with the right-hand side satisfy

\(\displaystyle |\det A|,\ |\det A_i|\leq N^{N/2}2^{cNL}.\)

Cramer’s rule therefore expresses all vertex coordinates using a common positive denominator \(D=|\det A|\), with numerator and denominator bit lengths bounded by

\(\displaystyle H=O\bigl(N(L+\log N)\bigr).\)

The constructed endpoints have the same asymptotic bit bound. To see this, write \(x^*_{tk}=p_{tk}/D\) and \(a_k=u_k/v_k\). Then

\(\displaystyle s_{k,t}=\frac{u_kD+v_k\sum_{i=1}^{t}p_{ik}}{v_kD}.\)

The sum adds at most \(O(\log\tau)\) bits, and the input endpoint contributes \(O(L)\) bits. Both are absorbed into \(O(H)\).

There are at most \(m\tau\) intervals in the output. Its total binary length is therefore \(O(m\tau H)\), which is polynomial.

Overall running time

The stages have the following bounds:

  • LP construction: polynomial bit time, with \(O(m\tau+m)\) coefficient-writing and rational-arithmetic steps.
  • Exact optimization: \(\operatorname{poly}(m\tau,m+\tau,L)\) bit operations.
  • Interval reconstruction: \(O(m\tau)\) rational additions, or \(O(m\tau H^2)\) bit operations using ordinary integer arithmetic.

Consequently, the entire computation takes \((m\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations. Since the explicit density table contains \(m\tau\) rational entries, this is also polynomial in \(L\). This is a bit-complexity bound; no bound independent of \(L\) is asserted.
