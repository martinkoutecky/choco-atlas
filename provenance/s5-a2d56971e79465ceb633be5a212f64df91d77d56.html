The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), side, acceptable opposite-side types, weak rankings, and integer per-person quotas \((\ell_t,u_t)\), consider atomless cohorts whose members are interchangeable. A feasible outcome is a measurable matching relation in which almost every person's degree is an integer at most their upper quota. Call it critical if it minimizes \(D(M)=\int_A\max\{0,\ell(a)-\deg_M(a)\}\,da+\int_B\max\{0,\ell(b)-\deg_M(b)\}\,db\); call it relaxed-stable if almost every potential opposite-side pair that blocks satisfies one of Definition 6's endpoint justifications. Does an algorithm polynomial in the number of types \(\tau\), mass bit length \(L\), and quota parameters return a critical relaxed-stable matching with mass cardinality at least \(\frac{2}{3}\) of the maximum?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a side, mutually acceptable opposite-side types, a weak ordering of all acceptable types, and integer quotas \(0\leq\ell_t\leq u_t\), with \(u_t\geq1\). Let \(m\) count acceptable type pairs, \(L\) be the total input bit length, and \(U=\max_t u_t\).
Represent each cohort by a disjoint interval of length \(\mu_t\) with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\); their unions are \(A\) and \(B\). People inherit their type's quotas and preferences and are indifferent between people of the same type.
An admissible matching is a measurable relation \(M\subseteq A\times B\) using only acceptable pairs, with finite partner sets \(M(v)\) and integer degrees \(d_M(v)=|M(v)|\leq u(v)\). Partners must be distinct people, but may share a type. Require mass balance: for every measurable \(X\subseteq A\), \(Y\subseteq B\), \(\int_X|M(a)\cap Y|\,d\lambda(a)=\int_Y|M(b)\cap X|\,d\lambda(b)\). Degree conditions hold almost everywhere.
Define \(D(M)=\int_{A\cup B}\max\{0,\ell(v)-d_M(v)\}\,d\lambda(v)\) and \(C(M)=\int_A d_M(a)\,d\lambda(a)=\int_B d_M(b)\,d\lambda(b)\). A matching is critical if it minimizes \(D\) over all admissible matchings; lower quotas are not hard feasibility constraints.
An acceptable unmatched pair \((a,b)\) blocks if each endpoint either has degree below its upper quota or strictly prefers the other endpoint to some current partner. Ties do not constitute strict improvement.
A blocking pair is justified at \(a\) if \(d_M(a)=u(a)\) and every \(b'\in M(a)\) with \(b\succ_a b'\) satisfies \(d_M(b')\leq\ell(b')\); justification at \(b\) is symmetric. A matching is relaxed-stable if the set of blocking pairs justified at neither endpoint has \((\lambda|_A)\otimes(\lambda|_B)\)-measure zero.
Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the supremum of \(C(N)\) over critical relaxed-stable admissible matchings. An algorithm represents its output by a finite union of graphs of translations between equal-length cohort subintervals with binary-rational endpoints.
Does an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,U)\) always return a critical relaxed-stable matching \(M\) with \(C(M)\geq\frac{2}{3}\mathrm{OPT}\), or is achieving this guarantee NP-hard?
For finite societies, critical relaxed-stable matchings always exist and a polynomial-time \(\frac{2}{3}\)-approximation is known; exact maximum cardinality is NP-hard. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.12325v3)

1. A realization lemma

Write $T_A,T_B$ for the type sets and $E\subseteq T_A\times T_B$ for the acceptable type pairs. For nonnegative edge masses $x_{ts}$, put

$$ z_t=\sum_{s:\{t,s\}\in E}x_{ts}. $$

The same edge variable contributes to both endpoints.

Lemma 1. Suppose $z_t\leq u_t\mu_t$ for every type. Then the edge masses are realizable by an admissible matching in which almost every person of type $t$ has degree

$$ \left\lfloor\frac{z_t}{\mu_t}\right\rfloor \quad\text{or}\quad \left\lceil\frac{z_t}{\mu_t}\right\rceil. $$

For rational data, the matching has a rational translation representation constructible in polynomial time in the data size and in $m,\tau,U$. Dyadic data admit a dyadic construction.

Proof. Write

$$ z_t=k_t\mu_t+r_t, \qquad k_t=\left\lfloor\frac{z_t}{\mu_t}\right\rfloor, \qquad 0\leq r_t<\mu_t. $$

Make $k_t$ full copies, called slots, of the cohort interval, together with one partial slot of length $r_t$ when $r_t>0$. A full slot will supply one incidence to every person; the partial slot will supply one incidence to a set of measure $r_t$. The upper-capacity inequality ensures that the resulting degrees are allowed.

Concatenate each type’s slots and partition them among its acceptable neighbors according to $x_{ts}$. For each acceptable type pair, align its two allocated stub intervals by translation. Cutting at slot boundaries gives

$$ H=O(m+\tau U) $$

pieces.

The remaining issue is distinct partners. Keep the maps from $A$-slots to people fixed. Within each $B$-cohort, rotate each slot by a separately chosen offset, interpreting the coordinate modulo the cohort length. A rotation preserves the incidence supplied by a full slot and the measure supplied by a partial slot.

Pieces using the same target slot cannot duplicate a pair: that slot’s coordinate map is injective, and its allocated stub pieces are disjoint. For pieces using different slots of one target cohort, duplication can occur only if their translation constants coincide. After cutting at wrap points, these constants have the form

$$ \gamma+c_j-h\mu_s, \qquad h\in\{0,1\}. $$

Thus the offsets need avoid finitely many equalities

$$ c_j-c_{j'}=\eta. $$

There are at most $3H^2$ relevant forbidden values for each sequential choice. Choose offsets from a grid of more than $3H^2$ points in $[0,\mu_s)$. A grid whose size is a power of two preserves dyadic coordinates when the initial data are dyadic. Each rotation adds at most one cut to a piece.

Finally, a translation $T:I\to J$ between equal-length intervals satisfies, for measurable $X,Y$,

$$ \int_X \mathbf 1_I(a)\mathbf 1_Y(T(a))\,d\lambda(a) = \lambda\bigl(T(X\cap I)\cap Y\bigr) = \int_Y \mathbf 1_J(b)\mathbf 1_X(T^{-1}(b))\,d\lambda(b). $$

Because repeated pairs have been excluded, summing these identities gives precisely the required mass balance. The number of pieces and their encoding lengths are polynomially bounded. ∎

We also need the following extension.

Lemma 2. Partition the cohorts into finitely many positive-mass classes. Prescribe each class’s degree and its number of partners in every opposite-side type-degree category. If nonnegative incidence masses satisfy the prescribed balances at both endpoints, these data are realizable by translations.

For rational data, the construction is rational and polynomial in the number of classes, nonzero incidence masses, $U$, and their encoding lengths.

Proof. For each class, create full slots, one for each prescribed partner incidence, labelled by the partner’s type and degree. Partition each slot category among the prescribed opposite-side classes and align equal masses.

Apply the rotation argument of Lemma 1 within each target class. Every slot is full, so rotation preserves every person’s number of partners in each labelled category. Rotations never move a person outside their class. Thus the prescribed profiles, distinct-partner condition, and mass balance are all preserved. ∎

2. Computing the exact criticality requirement

Define

$$ \mathcal F= \left\{ x\geq0: z_t\leq u_t\mu_t\text{ for every }t \right\}, $$

and

$$ Y_*= \max_{x\in\mathcal F} \sum_t\min\{\ell_t\mu_t,z_t\}. $$

Equivalently, $Y_*$ is the optimum of the linear program

$$ \max\sum_t y_t $$

subject to

$$ x_{ts}\geq0,\qquad z_t\leq u_t\mu_t,\qquad 0\leq y_t\leq z_t,\qquad y_t\leq\ell_t\mu_t. $$

Proposition 3. The minimum deficiency is

$$ D_*=\sum_t\ell_t\mu_t-Y_*. $$

It is computable in polynomial time, and a critical matching can be constructed in the format of Lemma 1.

Proof. For any admissible matching, define

$$ x_{ts}=\int_{I_t}|M(v)\cap I_s|\,d\lambda(v). $$

Mass balance identifies the two endpoint definitions. Also,

$$ z_t=\int_{I_t}d_M(v)\,d\lambda(v). $$

Its actual credited degree satisfies

$$ \int_{I_t}\min\{\ell_t,d_M(v)\}\,d\lambda(v) \leq \min\{\ell_t\mu_t,z_t\}. $$

Consequently,

$$ D(M)\geq\sum_t\ell_t\mu_t-Y_*. $$

Conversely, realize an optimal $x$ using Lemma 1. If $z_t/\mu_t\leq\ell_t$, both possible degrees are at most $\ell_t$. If $z_t/\mu_t\geq\ell_t$, both are at least $\ell_t$. This uses the fact that $\ell_t$ is an integer. Hence

$$ \int_{I_t}\min\{\ell_t,d_M(v)\}\,d\lambda(v) = \min\{\ell_t\mu_t,z_t\}, $$

and the lower bound is attained. ∎

A useful arithmetic consequence follows from a circulation formulation. Use source-to-$A$ arcs with capacities $\ell_t\mu_t$ and $(u_t-\ell_t)\mu_t$, carrying rewards one and zero. Use analogous $B$-to-sink arcs, acceptable-pair arcs, and a sink-to-source return arc. All remaining capacities can be bounded by $U$. For fixed $x$, the maximum reward is exactly its total credited degree.

Let $Q$ clear the input mass denominators. After multiplying capacities by $Q$, they are integers. The integral optimal-circulation theorem applies to this directed network. Thus optimal edge masses and $D_*$ have denominators dividing $Q$. Moreover, $\log Q$ is polynomially bounded by $L$; for dyadic masses, $Q$ can be chosen as a power of two.

3. Eliminating the profile-to-profile edge variables

A profile $p$ of type $t$ consists of nonnegative integers

$$ n_p(s,k), \qquad s\text{ acceptable to }t,\quad 1\leq k\leq u_s, $$

with

$$ d_p=\sum_{s,k}n_p(s,k)\leq u_t. $$

Here $n_p(s,k)$ counts partners of type $s$ and degree $k$. Degree-zero people cannot be partners, so no degree-zero partner category is needed.

Let $P_t$ be the finite set of these profiles, and let $P=\bigcup_tP_t$. Define

$$ W_p(s) \iff d_p<u_t \ \text{or}\ n_p(r,k)>0\text{ for some }r,k\text{ with }s\succ_t r, $$

and

$$ J_p(s) \iff d_p=u_t \ \text{and}\ k\leq\ell_r \text{ whenever }s\succ_t r\text{ and }n_p(r,k)>0. $$

Opposite-side profiles $p,q$, of acceptable types $t,s$, are bad if

$$ W_p(s),\quad W_q(t),\quad \neg J_p(s),\quad \neg J_q(t) $$

all hold.

Introduce only profile masses $w_p\geq0$. Impose the type-mass equations

$$ \sum_{p\in P_t}w_p=\mu_t. $$

For every $(t,s)\in E$, $1\leq d\leq u_t$, and $1\leq k\leq u_s$, impose

$$ \sum_{\substack{p\in P_t\\d_p=d}}n_p(s,k)w_p = \sum_{\substack{q\in P_s\\d_q=k}}n_q(t,d)w_q. $$

These equations equate the total incidence masses between the type-degree classes $(t,d)$ and $(s,k)$.

Proposition 4. These linear equations, together with

$$ w_pw_q=0 \qquad\text{for every bad pair }p,q, $$

characterize exactly the profile masses of relaxed-stable admissible matchings. Deficiency and cardinality are

$$ D(w)= \sum_p\max\{0,\ell_{\operatorname{type}(p)}-d_p\}w_p, $$

and

$$ C(w)=\sum_{p\text{ on }A}d_pw_p. $$

Proof: necessity. Profile classes are measurable under admissibility: their defining counts are section-count functions appearing in the required integrals. Exceptional null sets can be discarded without changing almost-everywhere profiles. Indeed, mass balance with a null set at one endpoint shows that almost every person has no partner in that set.

The displayed incidence equations follow by mass balance between type-degree classes.

Every admissible matching has product-null graph, because its sections are finite. If $p,q$ are bad, every unmatched pair between their classes is an unjustified blocking pair. Therefore relaxed stability requires $w_pw_q=0$.

The objective formulas follow directly from the definitions.

Proof: sufficiency. Fix $(t,d;s,k)$. The left side of its balance equation lists demands

$$ n_p(s,k)w_p \qquad (p\in P_t,\ d_p=d), $$

and the right side lists demands

$$ n_q(t,d)w_q \qquad (q\in P_s,\ d_q=k). $$

Their totals agree. A greedy transportation construction pairs these demands into nonnegative masses between individual profiles. Repeating this for every type-degree pair supplies the data required by Lemma 2.

The resulting matching has the prescribed profiles. Its unjustified blocking pairs lie in the finite union of rectangles corresponding to bad profile pairs. Every such rectangle has measure zero by the zero-product conditions. ∎

This eliminates the profile-to-profile edge variables entirely. Put

$$ B=\sum_{(t,s)\in E}u_tu_s, \qquad R=\tau+B+1. $$

The type-mass equations, incidence equations, and criticality equation $D(w)=D_*$ have at most $R$ rows, where

$$ R\leq\tau+mU^2+1. $$

5. A polynomial-size optimal rational representation

Theorem 5. There is an optimal critical relaxed-stable matching using at most

$$ R=\tau+\sum_{(t,s)\in E}u_tu_s+1 $$

positive-mass profiles. It admits $O(RU)$ rational translation graphs, whose endpoints have encoding lengths polynomial in $m,\tau,L,U$.

Proof: sparse support. Choose a compatible constituent polytope on which the global optimum is attained, and choose an optimal vertex $w$ of that polytope.

Its equality matrix has at most $R$ rows. If more than $R$ coordinates of $w$ were positive, their columns would be linearly dependent. There would be a nonzero vector $h$ supported on those positive coordinates with

$$ Ah=0. $$

For sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, both $w+\varepsilon h$ and $w-\varepsilon h$ would remain nonnegative and satisfy every equality. They remain within the same compatible support. This contradicts the vertex property. Hence at most $R$ coordinates are positive.

Proof: encoding length. All matrix coefficients are integers with absolute value at most $U$. The right-hand side consists of cohort masses and $D_*$, with a common denominator $Q$ whose bit length is polynomial in $L$.

Let $k\leq R$ be the number of positive coordinates. Their columns are independent, so choose $k$ rows forming a nonsingular integer matrix $H$. Hadamard’s inequality gives

$$ |\det H|\leq k^{k/2}U^k. $$

By Cramer’s rule, all positive coordinates have denominators dividing $Q|\det H|$. Their numerator and denominator lengths are therefore

$$ O\bigl(L+R\log(RU+1)\bigr). $$

This also bounds the encoding length of $\mathrm{OPT}$.

Proof: translation count. In the transportation construction of Proposition 4, a profile has at most $d_p\leq U$ nonzero partner categories. Across the at most $R$ positive profiles, there are at most $RU$ nonzero margins.

For each transportation problem, greedy pairing creates no more positive entries than the total number of its positive margins. Consequently there are $O(RU)$ positive profile-to-profile incidence masses altogether. There are also at most $RU$ full slots. Lemma 2 therefore uses $O(RU)$ pieces before rotations, and the same order afterward.

Transportation operations use sums, differences, and minima of rationals sharing the denominator $Q|\det H|$. Rotations introduce only a polynomial-size power-of-two grid factor. Endpoint encoding lengths remain polynomially bounded. ∎

This supplies an exact algorithm with a single exponential bound in the requested numerical parameters.

Indeed, if

6. Unit upper quotas: an explicit characterization

Assume now that

$$ u_t=1 \qquad\text{for every type }t. $$

Then $\ell_t\in\{0,1\}$. For aggregate edge masses $x$, define

$$ z_t=\sum_s x_{ts}\leq\mu_t. $$

For an acceptable opposite-side type $s$, put

$$ K_t(s)= \mu_t-z_t+ \sum_{\substack{r\text{ acceptable to }t\\s\succ_t r\\\ell_r=0}} x_{tr}. $$

Proposition 6. A unit-quota matching is relaxed-stable exactly when

$$ K_t(s)K_s(t)=0 \qquad\text{for every }(t,s)\in E. $$

This condition depends only on its aggregate edge masses.

Proof. The unmatched people of type $t$ have mass $\mu_t-z_t$. They are willing to match with $s$ and cannot justify a blocking pair.

A matched person of type $t$ has one partner, of some type $r$, whose degree is one. This person is willing to match with $s$ precisely when $s\succ_t r$. When willing, justification holds precisely when

$$ 1\leq\ell_r, $$

equivalently $\ell_r=1$.

Thus $K_t(s)$ is exactly the mass of type-$t$ people who are willing to match with type $s$ and would not justify the pair. The analogous set on side $s$ has mass $K_s(t)$. Their rectangle consists of unjustified blocking pairs except for possibly matched pairs, whose graph is product-null. The asserted equivalence follows. ∎

Every summand defining $K_t(s)$ is nonnegative. Therefore

$$ K_t(s)=0 $$

is equivalent to the linear restrictions

$$ z_t=\mu_t, $$

and

$$ x_{tr}=0 \quad \text{whenever }s\succ_t r\text{ and }\ell_r=0. $$

Choose, for each acceptable pair $(t,s)$, one endpoint whose $K$-value is required to vanish. Call this an orientation choice. It imposes only:

  • full subscription of certain types;
  • deletion of certain type edges.

Every relaxed-stable aggregate solution satisfies some orientation choice, and every solution satisfying an orientation choice is relaxed-stable.

7. Dyadic optimality for unit quotas

Theorem 7. Suppose every upper quota is one, and let $Q$ clear all input mass denominators. Then an exact optimum exists with every edge mass an integer multiple of $1/Q$.

It can be represented by at most $m$ translation graphs with endpoints having denominators dividing $Q$. In particular, dyadic inputs admit an exact dyadic optimum.

Proof. Fix an orientation choice. Its aggregate feasible set is a flow network:

  • a source-to-$A$ arc of upper capacity $\mu_t$;
  • the surviving acceptable-pair arcs;
  • a $B$-to-sink arc of upper capacity $\mu_s$;
  • a sink-to-source return arc.

For a type required to be fully subscribed, give its incident source or sink arc lower capacity equal to its upper capacity. Other such lower capacities are zero. All other capacities can be bounded by one.

For unit quotas,

$$ D(x)=\sum_t\ell_t(\mu_t-z_t). $$

Thus criticality is equivalent to maximizing

$$ Y(x)=\sum_t\ell_tz_t. $$

Place reward $\ell_t$ on each corresponding source or sink arc. Place a secondary reward one on acceptable-pair arcs to maximize cardinality.

After scaling by $Q$, all capacities and lower bounds are integers. Lexicographic optimization of credited degree and cardinality has an integral optimum. For example, reward

$$ (Q+1)Y+C $$

on scaled flows implements the lexicographic objectives: credited degree is integer, and scaled cardinality is at most $Q$.

If an orientation admits a critical solution, its maximum credited degree equals the unrestricted maximum $Y_*$. The integral lexicographic optimum is therefore critical and maximizes cardinality among critical solutions satisfying that orientation.

There are finitely many orientations. Every critical relaxed-stable matching belongs to one of them. Choosing the best critical orientation proves the first assertion.

Finally, partition each cohort into intervals of lengths $x_{ts}$, one for each incident edge, leaving an unmatched remainder. Map the two intervals assigned to each edge by translation. Each person has at most one partner, so no rotation is needed. All endpoints have denominators dividing $Q$, and Proposition 6 proves relaxed stability. ∎

$$ h_t=Q\mu_t $$

$$ \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal G_Q) = Q\,\mathrm{OPT}. $$

$$ D_{*,\mathrm{fin}}=QD_*. $$
