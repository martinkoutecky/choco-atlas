The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given chore categories \(O\), a supported type distribution \(\mu\) over types \(t\in T\) specified by binary supermodular costs \(c_t:2^O\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) and positive rational weights \(w_t\), and feasible rational per-capita supplies \(q_o\), choose masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) for integral bundles \(S\subseteq O\), with \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:o\in S}x_{t,S}=q_o\). Compute a feasible assignment whose histogram \((\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_K)\) is lexicographically minimum, where \(a_1<\cdots<a_K\) are the possible utility levels \(-c_t(S)/w_t\) and \(\rho_j=\sum_{t,S:-c_t(S)/w_t=a_j}x_{t,S}\). Determine its computational complexity in \(m=|O|\), support size \(\tau\), rational input length, and the specified cost-access model.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(O\) contain \(m\) chore categories. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a positive binary-rational weight \(w_t\) and a value oracle for a supermodular cost function \(c_t:2^O\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), with \(c_t(\emptyset)=0\) and \(c_t(S\cup\{o\})-c_t(S)\in\{0,1\}\) whenever \(o\notin S\). Each oracle query returns the exact cost.
Supplies are binary rationals \(q_o\in[0,1]\). Each individual receives an integral bundle \(S\subseteq O\), possibly empty, hence at most one unit of each category.
An assignment consists of nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,S}\), with zero entries implicit, satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq O}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:o\in S}x_{t,S}=q_o\) for every category.
Let \(a_1<\cdots<a_K\) be the distinct values in \(\{-c_t(S)/w_t:t\in T,\ S\subseteq O\}\), and define \(\rho_j=\sum_{t,S:-c_t(S)/w_t=a_j}x_{t,S}\). The optimization problem is to compute an assignment minimizing \((\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_K)\) lexicographically; any assignment attaining the minimum is allowed.
Let \(L\) be the total binary encoding length of the support list, masses, weights, and supplies; measure bit operations and oracle queries separately.

Is this exact histogram-minimization problem solvable with polynomially many bit operations and value-oracle queries in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard even for costs evaluable in polynomial time?

For finite societies, weighted leximin allocations under binary supermodular costs are claimed computable in polynomial time by [Viswanathan and Zick](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.06212v1).
[Barman, Narayan, and Verma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11530) establish polynomial-time value-oracle algorithms for discrete Lorenz-dominating allocations. No result settling this exact supported-mass histogram problem was identified in the search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the type distribution to total mass one and restricted per-capita supplies to binary rationals in the closed unit interval.
  • Specified exact value-oracle access to costs, following the model of the discrete algorithm's underlying allocation subroutine; defined L for the explicitly encoded data.
  • Made the cost normalization and binary-marginal condition explicit, as required by the discrete definition.
  • Retained empty bundles and the restriction to at most one unit of each category per individual, inherited from bundles being subsets.
  • Used rational assignment masses with zero entries implicit, avoiding an obligatory exponential listing of zero entries.
  • Defined utility levels as all distinct attained type-bundle utilities and allowed arbitrary ties between optimal assignments.
  • Distinguished polynomial oracle complexity from NP-hardness on polynomial-time evaluable cost families.

Exact polynomial-time solvability

The problem is solvable with polynomially many bit operations and value-oracle queries in \(m,\tau,L\). An optimal assignment can be returned with at most \(m+\tau\) nonzero entries.

The proof has three ingredients: cost sublevel sets are matroids; the lexicographic objective admits an exact scalarization of polynomial bit length; and the resulting linear program has a polynomial-time dual separation oracle.

1. Cost sublevel sets are matroids

Fix a type and write its cost function as \(c\). For every integer \(k\geq 0\), define
\[ \mathcal I_k=\{S\subseteq O:c(S)\leq k\}. \]

Lemma 1. The family \(\mathcal I_k\) is the independent-set family of a matroid. Membership requires one value-oracle query.

Proof. Nonnegative marginal costs imply monotonicity of \(c\). Consequently, \(\emptyset\in\mathcal I_k\), and \(\mathcal I_k\) is hereditary.

It remains to prove augmentation. Let \(A,B\in\mathcal I_k\) satisfy \(|A|<|B|\).

If \(c(A)<k\), then for every \(e\in B\setminus A\), the unit marginal bound gives
\[ c(A\cup\{e\})\leq c(A)+1\leq k. \]

Suppose instead that \(c(A)=k\), and assume for contradiction that no element of \(B\setminus A\) augments \(A\). Every such element then has marginal cost \(1\) at \(A\). By supermodularity and the unit marginal bound, its marginal remains exactly \(1\) at every larger set not containing it. Adding the elements of \(B\setminus A\) successively therefore gives
\[ c(A\cup B)=k+|B\setminus A|. \]

Adding the elements of \(A\setminus B\) to \(B\), on the other hand, gives
\[ c(A\cup B)\leq c(B)+|A\setminus B|\leq k+|A\setminus B|. \]

Thus \(|B\setminus A|\leq|A\setminus B|\), contradicting \(|B|>|A|\). This proves augmentation. \(\square\)

2. Polynomial-time pricing for increasing cost penalties

Fix rational element weights \(\beta_o\), which may have either sign, and an increasing rational function \(f\) on \(\{0,\ldots,c(O)\}\). We will compute
\[ \min_{S\subseteq O}\left(f(c(S))-\sum_{o\in S}\beta_o\right). \]

For each \(k\in\{0,\ldots,c(O)\}\), define
\[ H_k=\max_{S\in\mathcal I_k}\sum_{o\in S}\beta_o, \]
and obtain a maximizing set \(S_k\) by matroid greedy optimization.

For completeness, discard elements of nonpositive weight, sort the remaining elements as \(e_1,\ldots,e_p\) with weights \(b_1\geq\cdots\geq b_p>0\), and accept each element whenever independence is preserved.

Let \(E_i=\{e_1,\ldots,e_i\}\), and let \(G\) be the resulting set. Each \(G\cap E_i\) is maximal independent within \(E_i\): an element rejected earlier cannot become feasible after more elements have been accepted, by heredity. Augmentation therefore implies, for every independent set \(I\),
\[ |I\cap E_i|\leq|G\cap E_i|. \]

With \(b_{p+1}=0\), the identity
\[ \sum_{e_j\in I}b_j =\sum_{i=1}^{p}(b_i-b_{i+1})|I\cap E_i| \]
proves that greedy maximizes weight. Discarding nonpositive-weight elements is valid because removal preserves independence and does not decrease weight.

Lemma 2. The pricing optimum is
\[ \min_{S\subseteq O}\left(f(c(S))-\sum_{o\in S}\beta_o\right) =\min_{0\leq k\leq c(O)}\bigl(f(k)-H_k\bigr). \]

Proof. For any bundle \(S\), put \(\ell=c(S)\). Since \(S\in\mathcal I_\ell\),
\[ f(c(S))-\sum_{o\in S}\beta_o\geq f(\ell)-H_\ell \geq\min_k(f(k)-H_k). \]

Conversely, let \(k\) minimize the right-hand side. Since \(c(S_k)\leq k\) and \(f\) is increasing,
\[ f(c(S_k))-\sum_{o\in S_k}\beta_o \leq f(k)-H_k. \]

Together these inequalities prove equality and show that \(S_k\) attains the pricing optimum. \(\square\)

Because \(c(O)\leq m\), this computation uses at most \(m+1\) greedy runs, each requiring at most \(m\) independence queries. Its bit complexity is polynomial in \(m\) and the encoding lengths of the supplied weights and penalties.

3. Feasibility and vertex denominators

Let \(P\) be the polytope of assignments, temporarily allowing real masses. It has the form
\[ P=\{x\geq0:Ax=b\}, \]
where \(A\) has \(d=m+\tau\) rows and entries in \(\{0,1\}\), and \(b\) consists of the type masses and supplies.

The polytope is nonempty. For example, the rational assignment
\[ x_{t,S}=\mu_t\prod_{o\in S}q_o\prod_{o\notin S}(1-q_o) \]
has the required type totals and category supplies, by summing the corresponding product distribution.

Moreover, \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}=1\), so \(P\) is bounded.

Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of all masses and supplies, and put
\[ D=d!,\qquad M=QD. \]
Then \(Qb\) is integral, and \(\log Q=O(L)\).

At a vertex, the columns corresponding to positive coordinates must be linearly independent. Otherwise a nonzero dependence supported on those coordinates would permit small feasible perturbations in both directions, contradicting extremality. Hence every vertex has at most \(d\) positive coordinates.

Select a nonsingular square submatrix determining these coordinates. Its order is at most \(d\), and its determinant has absolute value at most \(D\), by the determinant expansion for a binary matrix. Cramer’s rule shows that all coordinates of this vertex have a common denominator dividing \(Q|\det|\).

Consequently, every histogram coordinate at a vertex has denominator at most \(M\). Any nonzero difference between histogram coordinates of two vertices therefore has absolute value at least
\[ \delta=M^{-2}. \]

4. Exact scalarization with polynomial bit length

First, all possible utility levels can be identified efficiently.

Query \(h_t=c_t(O)\) for each type. Along any chain from \(\emptyset\) to \(O\), costs start at zero, end at \(h_t\), and increase by zero or one. Thus the attainable costs are exactly \(\{0,\ldots,h_t\}\). The utility levels are therefore precisely the distinct elements of
\[ \bigcup_{t\in T}\{-k/w_t:0\leq k\leq h_t\}. \]

In particular, \(K\leq\tau(m+1)\), and these rational levels can be generated and sorted in polynomial time.

Choose the integer base
\[ B=M^2+2, \]
and define the scalar objective
\[ F(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{K}B^{K-j}\rho_j(x). \]

Lemma 3. On vertices of \(P\), minimizing \(F\) orders distinct histograms exactly as lexicographic minimization does.

Proof. Let vertices \(u,v\) first differ at histogram coordinate \(h\), with \(\rho_h(u)<\rho_h(v)\). The denominator bound gives
\[ \rho_h(v)-\rho_h(u)\geq\delta. \]

Every histogram coordinate lies in \([0,1]\). Therefore
\[ F(v)-F(u) \geq\delta B^{K-h}-\sum_{j=h+1}^{K}B^{K-j}. \]

The trailing sum is strictly less than \(B^{K-h}/(B-1)\). Since \(B-1>M^2=\delta^{-1}\), the displayed difference is positive. \(\square\)

This suffices for the entire polytope, not just its vertices. Successively minimizing the histogram coordinates defines a nonempty face of \(P\), so a lexicographically optimal histogram \(H^*\) is attained at a vertex. Lemma 3 implies that the scalar-minimizing vertices are exactly the vertices with histogram \(H^*\).

Every scalar minimizer is a convex combination of scalar-minimizing vertices. Since histogram coordinates are linear, every such combination also has histogram \(H^*\). Hence every minimizer of \(F\) is lexicographically optimal.

Finally, the scalar coefficients have polynomial encoding length:
\[ \log B=O(L+d\log d),\qquad \log(B^{K-1})=O\bigl(K(L+d\log d)\bigr). \]

No numerical approximation or infinitesimal coefficient is needed.

5. A polynomial-time dual separation oracle

For each type and attainable cost \(k\), let \(j(t,k)\) be the index satisfying \(a_{j(t,k)}=-k/w_t\), and define
\[ f_t(k)=B^{K-j(t,k)}. \]

Because \(w_t>0\), increasing \(k\) decreases utility and therefore increases its scalar penalty. Thus \(f_t\) is increasing.

The scalarized assignment problem is the linear program
\[ \min\sum_{t,S}f_t(c_t(S))x_{t,S} \quad\text{subject to}\quad Ax=b,\quad x\geq0. \]

Its dual has unrestricted variables \(\alpha_t,\beta_o\), objective
\[ \max\left(\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t+\sum_o q_o\beta_o\right), \]
and constraints
\[ \alpha_t+\sum_{o\in S}\beta_o\leq f_t(c_t(S)) \qquad(t\in T,\ S\subseteq O). \]

Given a rational candidate \((\alpha,\beta)\), apply Lemma 2 separately to each type to compute
\[ z_t=\min_{S\subseteq O}\left(f_t(c_t(S))-\sum_{o\in S}\beta_o\right), \]
together with an attaining bundle.

If \(\alpha_t>z_t\), that bundle identifies a violated defining inequality. If \(\alpha_t\leq z_t\) for every type, all dual constraints hold.

This is an exact separation oracle. Each call uses \(O(\tau(m+1)^2)\) value queries and polynomially many bit operations in the candidate’s encoding length and \(m,\tau,L\). Every returned inequality has polynomial encoding length.

6. Recovering an assignment and concluding

We use the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem with certificate recovery: a rational linear program in polynomial dimension, with uniformly bounded inequality encoding length and a polynomial-time separation oracle returning violated defining inequalities, can be optimized exactly with polynomially many bit operations and oracle calls. For a finite optimum, one can also recover an optimal dual certificate supported on polynomially many returned inequalities. This includes unbounded feasible regions and degeneracy; see [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, *Geometric Algorithms and Combinatorial Optimization*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4), and the explicit discussion of certificate recovery in [Dadush–Végh–Zambelli, *On finding exact solutions of linear programs in the oracle model*](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35149/35149.pdf).

Apply this theorem to the dual in Section 5. Its hypotheses hold:

  • Its dimension is \(d=m+\tau\).
  • Its objective and all defining inequalities have polynomial encoding length.
  • The separation oracle returns an actual violated defining inequality in polynomial time.
  • It is feasible, since \(\alpha=\beta=0\) satisfies every constraint.
  • Its optimum is finite and attained, by strong duality with the nonempty bounded primal.

The recovered certificate consists of nonnegative multipliers \(x_{t,S}\) on polynomially many bundle inequalities. Its coefficient identity is exactly \(Ax=b\), and its objective identity certifies scalar optimality. Thus it gives an explicit rational optimal assignment.

To obtain at most \(d\) nonzero entries, solve the primal restricted to those recovered columns and return an optimal basic feasible solution. This restricted program is feasible and has the same optimum; its basic solution has at most \(d\) positive coordinates. Its masses have polynomial encoding length by the denominator argument in Section 3.

By Lemma 3 and its extension to all of \(P\), this assignment minimizes the requested histogram lexicographically. The algorithm’s bit operations and value-oracle queries are both polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with no expansion into a finite society.
