The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item categories, a finite set \(T\) of normalized monotone submodular valuation types with \(|T|=\tau\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), and type-level valuation-query access, determine the best worst-case welfare approximation achievable by a fixed valuation-independent continuous MIR range \(\mathcal R\) under a communication budget \(B=O(e^{m^{1/15}})\). The decision variables are masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) of type \(t\) receiving integral bundle \(S\subseteq M\), with \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le q_j\), maximizing \(W_\mu(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) contain \(m\ge2\) item categories. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\ge1\) type labels with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a normalized monotone submodular valuation \(v_t:2^M\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), with \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\). Public binary-rational supplies satisfy \(0\le q_j\le1\). Public labels identify oracle endpoints and reveal no valuation information.
Let \(L\) bound both the total encoding length of masses and supplies and the encoding length of each valuation answer. A value query specifies \((t,S)\) and returns \(v_t(S)\) exactly; count all request and response bits toward communication.
The feasible set \(F(\mu,q)\) consists of real masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le q_j\). Each recipient receives an integral bundle; only recipient masses split.
Define \(W_\mu(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}=\max_{x\in F(\mu,q)}W_\mu(x)\).
A deterministic continuous MIR algorithm fixes a nonempty compact range \(\mathcal R\subseteq F(\mu,q)\) using only public data, independently of valuations and query answers, and returns an exact rational maximizer of \(W_\mu\) over \(\mathcal R\). Any maximizing tie may be selected; output uses a finite list of nonzero coordinates.
Only oracle communication is restricted; local computation is unrestricted. State any running-time bounds in \(m,\tau,L,B\).

What is the smallest worst-case approximation factor \(\rho(m,\tau,L,B)\) achievable by such continuous MIR algorithms using at most \(B\le C e^{m^{1/15}}\) communication bits, for a fixed constant \(C>0\), where the guarantee is \(W_\mu(x)\ge\operatorname{OPT}/\rho\) on every admissible input?

Discrete MIR allocation has an approximation lower bound \(\Omega(\min\{n,m^{1/6}\})\) under \(O(e^{m^{1/15}})\) communication, including randomized and nondeterministic protocols ([Dobzinski–Nisan](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00493-011-2528-4)).
Later discrete bounds with \(2^k\) communication are \(\Omega(\sqrt{m/(k\log(m/k))})\) for \(n=\Omega(\sqrt{m/k})\) and \(k=\Omega(\log m)\), and an MIR upper bound is \(O(\sqrt{m/k})\) ([Qiu–Weinberg](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00831v1)).
No result settling this exact mass-allocation, value-query, continuous MIR approximation question was identified in the search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one because the question specifies a distribution and per-capita supplies; restricted supplies to [0,1], since each recipient can receive each category at most once.
  • Interpreted normalized valuations as having zero empty-bundle value, without imposing unit grand-bundle value.
  • Made type labels and masses public while keeping valuations accessible only through exact type-level value queries; the range may depend on public feasibility data but not valuation information.
  • Restricted valuation answers to nonnegative binary rationals and added a public answer-length bound L so that the communication budget counts actual bits, including queries and responses.
  • Required a nonempty compact range and an exactly encoded rational maximizing output to make continuous MIR maximization and communication operationally defined.
  • Chose deterministic algorithms and unrestricted local computation, retaining the anchor's communication model rather than adding a polynomial-time restriction.
  • Defined approximation against the unrestricted feasible mass optimum and uniformly over all admissible masses, supplies, and valuation profiles; retained B as a parameter with a fixed constant in its stated asymptotic bound.

Status and main results

The general optimum approximation factor remains unresolved. The following bounds are proved below. They improve the previous positive-budget upper bound on an important class of public supplies and establish a constant lower bound for positive budgets, already with one supported type.

Let $\rho^*(m,\tau,L,B)$ denote the infimum of the worst-case approximation factors achievable in the stated model. Let
$$ N=\{j\in M:q_j>0\},\qquad d=|N|. $$
If $d=0$, then every feasible allocation has zero welfare, and the problem is trivial. Henceforth assume $d\ge1$, and define
$$ a=\min_{j\in N}q_j,\qquad b=\max_{j\in N}q_j,\qquad \kappa=\frac ba. $$

The proved upper bounds are:

  • With zero oracle communication,

$$ \rho^*(m,\tau,L,B)\le \frac{1}{1-e^{-1/\tau}}. $$

  • For $1\le k\le d$, querying all nonempty subsets of $N$ of size at most $k$, together with $N$ itself, gives a continuous MIR algorithm with factor

$$ \min\left\{\frac dk,\ \Phi\left(\frac{\kappa d}{k}\right)\right\}, $$
where
$$ \Phi(D)= \begin{cases} 1+D/4,&1\le D\le4,\\ \sqrt D,&D\ge4. \end{cases} $$
In particular, equal positive supplies give factor $\sqrt{d/k}$ when $d/k\ge4$.

The new lower bound is:

  • Along an explicit sequence with $\tau=1$ and $q_j=1/2$ for every item, every deterministic algorithm using

$$ B\le Ce^{m^{1/15}} $$
has worst-case factor at least
$$ \frac43-o(1). $$
This lower bound applies even without the MIR restriction. The constructed valuation answers have encoding length $O(\log m)$.

These bounds do not match in general. In particular, they do not establish the smallest factor as a function of all four parameters.

1. A sampling lemma and the zero-query upper bound

We first prove the zero-query bound, including the zero-supply case.

Sampling lemma

Let $f:2^M\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ be normalized, monotone, and submodular. For $p\in[0,1]^M$, let
$$ F(p)=\mathbb E[f(R_p)], $$
where $R_p$ includes the items independently with probabilities $p_j$.

Suppose $A$ is any random subset with marginals
$$ a_j=\Pr(j\in A), $$
and suppose $a_j\le cp_j$ for every item, where $c\ge1$. Then
$$ F(p)\ge (1-e^{-1/c})\mathbb E[f(A)]. $$

Proof. Put $V=\mathbb E[f(A)]$ and $H(s)=F(sp)$ for $0\le s\le1$.

For deterministic sets $A,R$, monotonicity and diminishing returns give
$$ f(A)\le f(R\cup A) \le f(R)+\sum_{j\in A}\bigl(f(R\cup\{j\})-f(R)\bigr). $$
The second inequality follows by adding the elements of $A\setminus R$ one at a time.

Take $A$ and $R_{sp}$ independently. Since all marginal increments are nonnegative,
$$ V\le H(s)+c\sum_jp_j\, \mathbb E\bigl[f(R_{sp}\cup\{j\})-f(R_{sp})\bigr]. $$
Differentiating the independent-sampling polynomial gives
$$ H'(s)=\sum_jp_j\, \mathbb E\bigl[ f((R_{sp}\setminus\{j\})\cup\{j\}) -f(R_{sp}\setminus\{j\}) \bigr]. $$
For each realization, the increment in this derivative is at least the corresponding increment in the preceding display. They are equal when $j\notin R_{sp}$, while the latter increment is zero when $j\in R_{sp}$. Consequently,
$$ V\le H(s)+cH'(s). $$
Thus
$$ \frac{d}{ds}\bigl(e^{s/c}H(s)\bigr) \ge \frac Vc e^{s/c}. $$
Integration from zero to one, using $H(0)=0$, proves the claim. $\square$

Public product allocation

For each item define
$$ d_{tj}=\min\{\mu_t,q_j\}, \qquad D_j=\sum_t d_{tj}. $$
If $q_j=0$, set $p_{tj}=0$. Otherwise set
$$ p_{tj}=\frac{q_jd_{tj}}{\mu_tD_j}. $$

For $q_j>0$,
$$ q_j\le D_j\le\tau q_j. $$
Indeed, if some $\mu_t\ge q_j$, that type alone contributes $q_j$ to $D_j$. Otherwise $D_j=\sum_t\mu_t=1\ge q_j$. The upper bound follows from $d_{tj}\le q_j$.

These inequalities and $d_{tj}\le\mu_t$ imply $0\le p_{tj}\le1$. Define
$$ x^0_{t,S} = \mu_t\prod_{j\in S}p_{tj} \prod_{j\notin S}(1-p_{tj}). $$
Its type totals are $\mu_t$, and its item consumption is
$$ \sum_{t,S\ni j}x^0_{t,S} = \sum_t\mu_tp_{tj} = q_j. $$
For zero-supply items, both sides are zero. Thus $x^0$ is feasible.

The singleton range $\{x^0\}$ is compact, rational, and determined entirely by public data. Returning its sole point is therefore a continuous MIR algorithm using zero oracle communication.

Let $x^*$ be an optimal allocation. Such an allocation exists because the feasible set is a nonempty compact polytope. Write
$$ y^*_{tj}=\sum_{S\ni j}x^*_{t,S}. $$
For positive-supply items,
$$ y^*_{tj}\le d_{tj} \le\tau\mu_tp_{tj}. $$
For zero-supply items, feasibility gives $y^*_{tj}=0$, so the same final comparison holds.

For each type, apply the sampling lemma with $c=\tau$ to the distribution assigning probability $x^*_{t,S}/\mu_t$ to bundle $S$. Multiplying the resulting inequalities by $\mu_t$ and summing yields
$$ W_\mu(x^0)\ge(1-e^{-1/\tau})\operatorname{OPT}. $$

Explicitly constructing the output takes
$$ 2^m\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
local time and produces at most $\tau2^m$ nonzero coordinates. All coordinates are rational with polynomial encoding length. This output size is permitted by the model.

2. The query range and the truncation guarantee

Every positive coordinate of a feasible allocation uses a subset of $N$: a bundle containing a zero-supply item cannot have positive mass.

Fix $1\le k\le d$. Define the public family
$$ \mathcal A_k=\{S\subseteq N:|S|\le k\}\cup\{N\}, $$
and the range
$$ \mathcal R_k^+ = \{x\in F(\mu,q):x_{t,S}=0\text{ for }S\notin\mathcal A_k\}. $$
This is a nonempty compact rational polytope. Query the value of every nonempty bundle in $\mathcal A_k$ for every type, and solve the resulting linear program exactly.

The number of queries is
$$ Q_k^+ = \tau\left( \sum_{i=1}^k\binom di+\mathbf1_{\{k<d\}} \right). $$

We will use the following truncation property.

Lemma. For a normalized monotone submodular function $f$, a set $S$ of size $s>k$, and a uniformly random $k$-element subset $T$ of $S$,
$$ \mathbb E[f(T)]\ge\frac ks f(S). $$

Proof. Order $S$ as $j_1,\ldots,j_s$ and define
$$ w_{j_\ell} = f(\{j_1,\ldots,j_\ell\}) -f(\{j_1,\ldots,j_{\ell-1}\}). $$
Monotonicity gives $w_j\ge0$, and telescoping gives $\sum_{j\in S}w_j=f(S)$.

For any $T\subseteq S$, add its elements in the inherited order. The preceding elements at each addition form a subset of the corresponding full prefix of $S$. Diminishing returns therefore gives
$$ f(T)\ge\sum_{j\in T}w_j. $$
Every element belongs to a uniformly random $k$-element subset with probability $k/s$, proving the lemma. $\square$

Replace every bundle of size greater than $k$ in an optimal allocation by the uniform distribution over its $k$-element subsets. This preserves type masses and cannot increase item consumption. Retain all smaller bundles. The resulting allocation belongs to $\mathcal R_k^+$ and has welfare at least
$$ \frac kd\operatorname{OPT}. $$
Hence the query algorithm has factor at most $d/k$.

3. An improved bound using full bundles

The addition of $N$ to the queried range gives a stronger guarantee when the positive supplies are comparable.

Let $H$ be the optimal welfare over $\mathcal R_k^+$. Let $H_k$ denote the optimum over the subrange containing only bundles of size at most $k$, and let $H_N$ denote the optimum over the subrange containing only $\varnothing$ and $N$. Both are subranges of $\mathcal R_k^+$, so
$$ H\ge H_k,\qquad H\ge H_N. $$

Fix an optimal allocation $x^*$. Define
$$ W_0=\sum_{t,S:\,|S|\le k}x^*_{t,S}v_t(S), $$
$$ W_1=\sum_{t,S:\,|S|>k}x^*_{t,S}v_t(S), $$
and
$$ A=\sum_{t,S:\,|S|>k} x^*_{t,S}\frac{k}{|S|}v_t(S). $$
The truncation lemma proves
$$ H_k\ge W_0+A. $$

Next define, for every type,
$$ z_t=\frac{a}{db}\sum_Sx^*_{t,S}|S|. $$
These masses can receive $N$, with the remaining mass of each type receiving $\varnothing$. Indeed,
$$ z_t\le\frac ab\mu_t\le\mu_t, $$
and
$$ \sum_tz_t = \frac{a}{db}\sum_{t,S}x^*_{t,S}|S| \le \frac{a}{db}\sum_{j\in N}q_j \le a. $$
Thus every item consumes at most $a\le q_j$.

By monotonicity,
$$ H_N \ge\sum_tz_tv_t(N) \ge \frac{1}{\kappa d} \sum_{t,S}x^*_{t,S}|S|v_t(S). $$

Apply Cauchy–Schwarz to the nonnegative weights
$$ w_{t,S}=x^*_{t,S}v_t(S) $$
on bundles of size greater than $k$. It gives
$$ W_1^2 \le \left(\sum_{|S|>k}w_{t,S}\frac{k}{|S|}\right) \left(\sum_{|S|>k}w_{t,S}\frac{|S|}{k}\right). $$
The first factor is $A$. The second is at most $(\kappa d/k)H_N$. Consequently,
$$ W_1^2 \le \frac{\kappa d}{k}\,H(H-W_0). $$

If $H=0$, this inequality and $W_0\le H$ imply $\operatorname{OPT}=0$. Otherwise put
$$ D=\frac{\kappa d}{k}, \qquad u=\frac{W_0}{H}\in[0,1]. $$
Then
$$ \frac{\operatorname{OPT}}H = \frac{W_0+W_1}{H} \le u+\sqrt{D(1-u)}. $$
For $D\ge4$, this expression is decreasing on $[0,1]$ and its maximum is $\sqrt D$. For $1\le D\le4$, its maximum occurs at $u=1-D/4$ and equals $1+D/4$. This proves
$$ H\ge \frac{\operatorname{OPT}}{\Phi(\kappa d/k)}. $$

Combining this result with the truncation bound gives the claimed factor
$$ \min\left\{\frac dk,\Phi\left(\frac{\kappa d}{k}\right)\right\}. $$

Communication and local running time

One concrete encoding uses a type index, an $m$-bit incidence vector, and a length-prefixed rational response. Its per-query cost is at most
$$ \beta = m+\lceil\log_2\tau\rceil+L +\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil+c_0 $$
for a fixed protocol constant $c_0$.

Whenever
$$ \beta Q_k^+\le B, $$
the algorithm fits the communication budget. Exact rational linear programming takes
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,Q_k^+) $$
local time, hence polynomial time in $m,\tau,L,B$ when the displayed budget condition holds. An optimal basic feasible solution has at most $\tau+d$ nonzero allocation coordinates, because adding supply slacks gives $\tau+d$ equality constraints.

Combining with the zero-query algorithm yields
$$ \rho^*(m,\tau,L,B) \le \min\left\{ \frac{1}{1-e^{-1/\tau}}, \frac dk, \Phi\left(\frac{\kappa d}{k}\right) \right\} $$
whenever that query budget is available. The algorithm attaining this bound can be chosen using public data before any oracle answers are received.

4. A constant lower bound for positive budgets

We now prove a lower bound that explicitly permits arbitrary finite output support and unrestricted local computation.

Take one type with mass one. Let
$$ m=2r,\qquad q_j=\frac12\quad(j\in M). $$
Let $0<\varepsilon<1$ be rational.

For a hidden set $A\subseteq M$ of size $r$, define
$$ x_A(S)=\frac{|S\cap A|}{r}, \qquad y_A(S)=\frac{|S\setminus A|}{r}. $$
For $x,y\in[0,1]$, write
$$ a(x,y)=\frac{x+y}{2} $$
and define
$$ F_\varepsilon(x,y) = 2a(x,y)-a(x,y)^2 + \frac{\bigl(\max\{|x-y|-\varepsilon,0\}\bigr)^2}{4}. $$
Our hidden valuation is
$$ f_A(S)=F_\varepsilon(x_A(S),y_A(S)). $$
The common comparison valuation is
$$ g(S) = \frac{|S|}{r}-\frac{|S|^2}{4r^2}. $$
These valuations agree whenever
$$ \left|2|S\cap A|-|S|\right|\le\varepsilon r. $$

4.1 Verification of the valuation hypotheses

We verify monotonicity and submodularity directly.

Put $d=x-y$. In the region $|d|<\varepsilon$,
$$ \frac{\partial F_\varepsilon}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial F_\varepsilon}{\partial y} = 1-\frac{x+y}{2}. $$
All three second derivatives in the two coordinate directions are $-1/2$.

In the region $d>\varepsilon$,
$$ \frac{\partial F_\varepsilon}{\partial x} = 1-y-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}, \qquad \frac{\partial F_\varepsilon}{\partial y} = 1-x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. $$
The same-coordinate second derivatives are zero, and the mixed derivative is $-1$.

In the region $d<-\varepsilon$,
$$ \frac{\partial F_\varepsilon}{\partial x} = 1-y+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}, \qquad \frac{\partial F_\varepsilon}{\partial y} = 1-x-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. $$
Again the same-coordinate second derivatives are zero and the mixed derivative is $-1$.

The first derivatives match continuously at both boundaries. They are nonnegative throughout the square: for example, $x-y>\varepsilon$ implies $y<1-\varepsilon$, so $1-y-\varepsilon/2>0$; the other cases follow from the displayed formulas.

Moreover, each first derivative is nonincreasing as either coordinate increases. On each region this follows from the nonpositive second derivatives; continuity preserves the comparison when a boundary is crossed.

Therefore $F_\varepsilon$ is coordinatewise monotone and has diminishing coordinate increments. Adding an item of $A$ increases $x$ by $1/r$, and adding an item of its complement increases $y$ by $1/r$. Integrating the derivative comparison over this increment proves the diminishing-returns inequality for $f_A$. Thus $f_A$ is submodular.

Finally,
$$ F_\varepsilon(0,0)=0, \qquad F_\varepsilon(1,1)=1. $$
Monotonicity implies that $f_A$ takes values in $[0,1]$. It is therefore normalized, monotone, nonnegative, and submodular.

The comparison valuation $g$ has the same properties: its cardinality increments are
$$ g(S\cup\{j\})-g(S) = \frac1r-\frac{2|S|+1}{4r^2}, $$
which are nonnegative and decrease with $|S|$.

4.2 Welfare bounds

For any feasible allocation $z$,
$$ \sum_Sz_S|S|\le r, \qquad \sum_Sz_S=1. $$
Let $U=|S|/(2r)$ under this distribution. Then $\mathbb E[U]\le1/2$. Since $2u-u^2$ is increasing and concave on $[0,1]$,
$$ \sum_Sz_Sg(S) = \mathbb E[2U-U^2] \le 2\mathbb E[U]-(\mathbb E[U])^2 \le\frac34. $$

For $f_A$, allocate mass $1/2$ to $A$ and mass $1/2$ to $M\setminus A$. This consumes exactly $1/2$ of every item and gives welfare
$$ F_\varepsilon(1,0) = F_\varepsilon(0,1) = 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}. $$
Hence
$$ \operatorname{OPT}(f_A) \ge 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}. $$

4.3 Indistinguishability of queried bundles

Choose $A$ uniformly from the $r$-element subsets of $M$. For every fixed $S\subseteq M$, we claim
$$ \Pr\left( \left|2|S\cap A|-|S|\right|>\varepsilon r \right) \le \delta, $$
where
$$ \delta=2(m+1)e^{-\varepsilon^2r/4}. $$

To prove this, first assign independent uniform signs $\sigma_j\in\{-1,1\}$. Condition on exactly $r$ positive signs. Their positive positions then form a uniform $r$-element set, and
$$ 2|S\cap A|-|S|=\sum_{j\in S}\sigma_j. $$
The conditioning event has probability
$$ \frac{\binom{2r}{r}}{2^{2r}}\ge\frac1{2r+1}=\frac1{m+1}, $$
because the central binomial coefficient is the largest of the $m+1$ coefficients whose sum is $2^m$.

For $s=|S|>0$, independent signs satisfy
$$ \Pr\left(\left|\sum_{j\in S}\sigma_j\right|>u\right) \le2e^{-u^2/(2s)}. $$
Indeed, their moment-generating function is $(\cosh\lambda)^s\le e^{s\lambda^2/2}$; applying Markov's inequality with $\lambda=u/s$ gives each one-sided bound. Taking $u=\varepsilon r$, using $s\le2r$, and dividing by the probability of the conditioning event proves the claim. For $s=0$, the probability is zero.

4.4 Controlling the entire output

Run any deterministic algorithm on the comparison valuation $g$. Suppose this run makes $Q$ queries and returns the feasible allocation $z$.

Let $E$ be the event that every queried set satisfies
$$ \left|2|S\cap A|-|S|\right|\le\varepsilon r. $$
A union bound gives
$$ \Pr(E)\ge1-Q\delta. $$
On $E$, running the algorithm on $f_A$ produces the same transcript and the same output $z$. This follows inductively: every query on the comparison transcript receives the same answer, so determinism gives the same next query or termination decision.

Define the correction to the output welfare by
$$ Z_A=\sum_Sz_S\bigl(f_A(S)-g(S)\bigr). $$
For every fixed $S$, this difference is nonnegative, at most $1/4$, and zero unless the event in Section 4.3 occurs. Therefore
$$ \mathbb E_A[Z_A] \le \frac{\delta}{4}\sum_Sz_S = \frac{\delta}{4}. $$
This calculation uses the allocation masses as weights. It does not require a union bound over the output support.

If $Q\delta<1$, there exists an $A$ for which $E$ holds and
$$ Z_A\le\frac{\delta}{4(1-Q\delta)}. $$
For that valuation, the algorithm's welfare is at most
$$ \frac34+\frac{\delta}{4(1-Q\delta)}. $$
Combining with the feasible allocation from Section 4.2 proves a worst-case factor of at least
$$ \frac{ 1-\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon^2/4 }{ 3/4+\delta/[4(1-Q\delta)] }. $$

Every completed query communicates at least one bit, so $Q\le\lfloor B\rfloor$. In particular, whenever $B\delta<1$, the following weaker bound holds uniformly for algorithms with budget $B$:
$$ \rho^*(m,1,L,B) \ge \frac{ 1-\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon^2/4 }{ 3/4+\delta/[4(1-B\delta)] }, $$
provided the displayed valuations and public data are admissible under $L$.

4.5 Specialization to the requested communication regime

Let $r$ range over powers of two tending to infinity, put $m=2r$, and choose
$$ h=\left\lfloor\frac{\log_2r}{4}\right\rfloor, \qquad \varepsilon=2^{-h}. $$
Then
$$ r^{-1/4}\le\varepsilon<2r^{-1/4}, \qquad \varepsilon^2r\ge\sqrt r. $$
Consequently,
$$ \delta\le2(m+1)e^{-\sqrt r/4}. $$
For every budget satisfying $B\le Ce^{m^{1/15}}$,
$$ B\delta\longrightarrow0, \qquad \varepsilon\longrightarrow0. $$
The preceding lower bound therefore tends to $4/3$.

All constructed valuation answers are dyadic rationals. Their denominators divide
$$ 4r^2\,2^{2h}, $$
and their values lie in $[0,1]$, so their encoding lengths are $O(\log m)$. The public supplies are all $1/2$, and the sole mass is one. Thus the construction is admissible for suitable $L_m$ accommodating the public data; under conventional binary rational encodings one may take $L_m=O(m\log m)$.

We have proved
$$ \liminf_{m\to\infty} \rho^*(m,1,L_m,B_m)\ge\frac43 $$
along this sequence, for every choice $B_m\le Ce^{m^{1/15}}$.

5. A lower bound when few types can be queried

For completeness, there is also a simple lower bound relevant when the support size exceeds the budget.

Let $h=\lceil\log_2\tau\rceil$. Choose dyadic masses consisting of $2^h-\tau$ masses of size $2^{1-h}$ and $2\tau-2^h$ masses of size $2^{-h}$. They are positive, sum to one, and are all at least $2^{-h}$. Set
$$ q_1=2^{-h},\qquad q_j=0\quad(j\ne1). $$
Assume $L$ accommodates these public numbers and valuation answers zero and one.

Run a deterministic algorithm on the all-zero valuation profile. Let $T$ be the types queried on a bundle containing item $1$. Since each completed query communicates at least one bit,
$$ |T|\le\lfloor B\rfloor. $$
If $u=\tau-|T|>0$, the returned allocation consumes item $1$ on some unqueried type $t^*$ in mass at most $q_1/u$.

Change only that type's valuation to
$$ v_{t^*}(S)=\mathbf1_{\{1\in S\}}. $$
This is an additive, hence submodular, valuation. The entire transcript remains unchanged, so the output remains unchanged. Its welfare is at most $q_1/u$, while the optimum is $q_1$, because $\mu_{t^*}\ge q_1$.

Thus, on these public data,
$$ \rho^*(m,\tau,L,B)\ge\tau-\lfloor B\rfloor $$
whenever $\lfloor B\rfloor<\tau$. This argument also does not use MIR.

6. What remains unresolved

The improved query range gives a square-root upper bound for equal positive supplies, and the hidden-partition construction gives a constant positive-budget lower bound even for one type. Neither result establishes a matching characterization.

For example, for one type the proved bounds in the stated communication regime still leave, asymptotically,
$$ \frac43-o(1) \le \rho^*(m,1,L_m,B_m) \le \frac{e}{e-1}. $$
For larger support sizes, the optimal dependence on $B$, $\tau$, and heterogeneous supplies remains undetermined. No discrete MIR lower bound is transferred to arbitrary continuous ranges in this proof.
