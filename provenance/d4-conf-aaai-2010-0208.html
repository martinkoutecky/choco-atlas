The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a binary-encoded population scale \(N\), rational masses \(\mu_t=k_t/N\) over \(\tau\) piecewise-uniform valuation types \(t\), and rational interval descriptions of each type’s reference set \(U_t\), does there exist a succinct mechanism, evaluable in time polynomial in \(\tau\), the description length \(L\), and \(\log N\), that assigns each of the \(N\) individuals a cake piece? The mechanism must produce a partition with free disposal for every report profile, satisfy proportionality and envy-freeness at truthful profiles, and be dominant-strategy truthful for every individual and every alternative piecewise-uniform report. Its representation must specify assignments by type and within-type rank, so a unilateral report change updates the corresponding type masses by \(1/N\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

The cake is \([0,1]\). A type \(t\) has a reference set \(U_t\), a finite union of intervals with binary rational endpoints and positive total length, and valuation \(V_t(X)=\operatorname{len}(X\cap U_t)/\operatorname{len}(U_t)\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(N\geq1\) is a binary-encoded integer, \(\mu_t=k_t/N\), \(k_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and \(\sum_t k_t=N\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(m\) be the number of elementary intervals induced by all reported endpoints and \(L\) the total bit length of the support description. Individuals have a fixed public order; an individual’s within-type rank is its position among individuals currently reporting that type.
A deterministic mechanism without payments maps every report profile to pairwise disjoint cake pieces, each a finite union of intervals; unassigned cake may be discarded. Its succinct representation specifies a piece \(A_{t,r}\) for each type and rank \(1\leq r\leq k_t\), and evaluation returns that piece’s rational interval endpoints.
At every truthful profile, require \(V_i(A_i)\geq1/N\) and \(V_i(A_i)\geq V_i(A_j)\) for all individuals \(i,j\). Require dominant-strategy truthfulness against every alternative positive-length, rational-endpoint piecewise-uniform report, for arbitrary reports of the other individuals.
A deviation preserves the individual’s identity, decreases its old type’s mass by \(1/N\), increases its new type’s mass by \(1/N\), and recomputes ranks using the fixed public order; types may enter or leave the support.

Can such a mechanism’s allocation representation be constructed and evaluated uniformly in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,\log N\), or is constructing such succinct allocations NP-hard?

For explicitly listed individuals, a deterministic polynomial-time mechanism satisfying truthfulness, proportionality, and envy-freeness is known ([Chen–Lai–Parkes–Procaccia](https://procaccia.info/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/justruth.pdf)). Result

Such a mechanism can be constructed and evaluated uniformly in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,\log N\).

The construction compresses the minimum-ratio allocation structure of Mechanism 1 in [Chen–Lai–Parkes–Procaccia](https://procaccia.info/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/justruth.pdf). Below, I prove the compressed running-time bound and verify truthfulness directly, including deviations that change the support and within-type ranks.

Throughout, interval endpoints are immaterial to valuations. We use half-open allocation intervals and discard the endpoint \(1\), so pieces are literally pairwise disjoint.

1. Minimum-ratio blocks can be represented by types

At an intermediate stage, let \(T\) be the remaining types, with their original counts \(k_t\), and let \(X\) be the remaining cake. Define, for \(H\subseteq T\),

\[ k(H)=\sum_{t\in H}k_t, \qquad D_X(H)=X\cap\bigcup_{t\in H}U_t, \qquad d_X(H)=\operatorname{len}(D_X(H)). \]

The next allocation rate will be

\[ \lambda=\min_{\varnothing\ne H\subseteq T}\frac{d_X(H)}{k(H)}. \]

Here a rate means unnormalized cake length per individual.

Lemma 1. The same minimum is obtained by minimizing over all nonempty subsets of the remaining individuals. Moreover, the unique largest minimizing individual set consists of all copies of a set of types.

Proof. Given an individual set \(S\), add every remaining individual whose reported type occurs in \(S\). This leaves its desired union unchanged and weakly increases its cardinality. Consequently, its desired length divided by cardinality weakly decreases. Thus the individual and type minima coincide.

It remains to justify “unique largest.” The function \(d_X\) is submodular: for type sets \(A,B\),

\[ d_X(A\cup B)+d_X(A\cap B) \leq d_X(A)+d_X(B). \]

Indeed, the desired union of \(A\cup B\) is the union of their desired unions, whereas the desired union of \(A\cap B\) is contained in their intersection.

Set \(F(H)=d_X(H)-\lambda k(H)\), including \(F(\varnothing)=0\). By minimality of \(\lambda\), \(F(H)\geq0\) for every \(H\). If \(A,B\) minimize the ratio, submodularity gives

\[ 0\leq F(A\cup B)+F(A\cap B) \leq F(A)+F(B)=0. \]

Hence \(A\cup B\) also minimizes. Taking the union of all minimizers produces the unique largest minimizer.

The identical argument applies to individual sets. Completing any individual minimizer to whole types still gives a minimizer, by the first paragraph. Therefore the largest individual minimizer contains every copy of each type it contains. \(\square\)

We always select this unique largest minimizing block. This choice is essential to the truthfulness proof below.

2. A polynomial-size flow oracle

Partition the cake at all reported endpoints and at \(0,1\). Write its positive-length elementary intervals as \(I_j=[a_j,b_j)\), with lengths \(\ell_j=b_j-a_j\). Membership in each reported reference set is constant inside each elementary interval.

The algorithm will remove only whole elementary intervals, so \(X\) remains a union of them.

Let \(K=k(T)\). For a rational \(c\in[0,2]\), construct a network with source \(s\), sink \(z\), one node per remaining type, and one node per remaining elementary interval:

  • \(s\to t\) has capacity \(k_t c\);
  • \(t\to I_j\) has capacity \(M=2N+2\) whenever type \(t\) desires \(I_j\);
  • \(I_j\to z\) has capacity \(\ell_j\).

Lemma 2. This network has a flow saturating every source edge if and only if \(c\leq\lambda\).

Proof. A cut consisting of the source alone has capacity \(Kc\leq2N<M\). Thus a minimum cut cannot cut any type-to-interval edge.

For a prescribed set \(H\) of type nodes on the source side, all intervals desired by \(H\) must also lie on that side. Putting any additional interval there increases the cut capacity. Therefore the minimum cut with type set \(H\) has capacity

\[ c\bigl(K-k(H)\bigr)+d_X(H). \]

By the max-flow min-cut theorem, a flow of value \(Kc\) exists exactly when every such cut has capacity at least \(Kc\). This is equivalent to

\[ d_X(H)\geq c\,k(H) \quad\text{for every }H\subseteq T, \]

which is equivalent to \(c\leq\lambda\). \(\square\)

The network has \(O(m+\tau)\) vertices and \(O(m\tau+m+\tau)\) edges. Its size is independent of the explicit population size.

3. Finding the exact rate and the largest block

Choose a common denominator \(Q\) for the input endpoints. For example, their denominators’ product suffices. Its binary length is \(O(L)\).

Every remaining desired union has length \(A/Q\) for an integer \(A\). Thus every candidate ratio has the form

\[ \frac{A}{Qh}, \qquad 1\leq h\leq N. \]

Two distinct candidate ratios differ by at least

\[ \left|\frac{A}{Qh}-\frac{B}{Qh'}\right| =\frac{|Ah'-Bh|}{Qhh'} \geq\frac{1}{QN^2}. \]

Use Lemma 2 to bisect an interval whose lower endpoint is feasible and upper endpoint is infeasible. Initially, \(0\) is feasible and \(2\) is infeasible, since the cake has length at most \(1\) and \(K\geq1\).

After \(O(\log Q+\log N)\) iterations, obtain endpoints \(c_-,c_+\) satisfying

\[ c_-\leq\lambda<c_+, \qquad c_+-c_-<\frac{1}{QN^2}. \]

A minimum cut at the infeasible value \(c_+\) supplies a nonempty type set \(H\) with

\[ \frac{d_X(H)}{k(H)}<c_+. \]

This ratio cannot exceed \(\lambda\): otherwise its separation from \(\lambda\) would be at least \(1/(QN^2)\). Therefore it equals \(\lambda\), determining the optimum exactly.

To obtain the largest minimizing block, compute a maximum flow at this exact \(\lambda\). In its residual graph, let \(Z\) be the vertices from which the sink cannot be reached. Then \(Z\) is the largest source side of a minimum cut:

  • the source belongs to \(Z\), since the flow is maximum;
  • no positive-capacity residual edge leaves \(Z\);
  • every minimum-cut source side has no positive-capacity residual edge leaving it, so it is contained in \(Z\).

Let \(B\) be the type nodes in \(Z\). The cut formula from Lemma 2 gives

\[ d_X(B)=\lambda k(B). \]

Every minimizing type set defines a minimum cut and is therefore contained in \(B\). Hence \(B\) is precisely the unique largest minimizing block. It is nonempty because a nonempty minimizer exists.

4. Allocation and succinct evaluation

At each stage:

  • Compute \(\lambda\), the largest minimizing block \(B\), and a maximum flow at \(\lambda\).
  • Allocate \(D_X(B)\) exactly to the individuals of types in \(B\), as described below.
  • Remove \(B\) and \(D_X(B)\), and repeat.

At least one type disappears at every stage, so there are at most \(\tau\) stages.

Let \(f_{jt}\) denote the flow from type \(t\in B\) to elementary interval \(I_j\). Every source edge is saturated, giving

\[ \sum_j f_{jt}=k_t\lambda. \]

The block sends total flow \(k(B)\lambda=d_X(B)\) into intervals whose total capacity is exactly \(d_X(B)\). Consequently, it fills every interval in \(D_X(B)\) completely.

Fix a deterministic ordering of the types, obtained from their canonical interval descriptions. Inside each such elementary interval, place the segments of lengths \(f_{jt}\) consecutively in this order. Let \(p_{jt}\) be the start of type \(t\)’s segment.

Divide that segment into \(k_t\) equal parts. Rank \(r\) receives

\[ \left[ p_{jt}+\frac{(r-1)f_{jt}}{k_t}, \; p_{jt}+\frac{rf_{jt}}{k_t} \right). \]

Its complete piece \(A_{t,r}\) is the union of these intervals over positive \(f_{jt}\). Every individual in the block therefore receives desired length exactly

\[ \sum_j\frac{f_{jt}}{k_t}=\lambda. \]

The representation stores the counts and the positive entries \((p_{jt},f_{jt})\). It specifies every type-and-rank piece without enumerating individuals. Evaluation requires at most \(m\) interval formulas.

Use a fixed ordering of network vertices and edges for all flow computations. Together with the unique largest block and the segment ordering, this defines a deterministic mechanism on every report profile.

Bit complexity

The bisection performs \(O(L+\log N)\) flow computations per stage. Its rational capacities have polynomial bit length.

At the exact rate, write

\[ \lambda=\frac{A}{Qh}, \qquad h=k(B)\leq N. \]

All capacities are integer multiples of \(1/(Qh)\). An augmenting-path implementation starting at zero therefore returns flows with this denominator. Their bit lengths are \(O(L+\log N)\). The segment offsets have the same bound.

Dividing a segment among \(k_t\) ranks introduces a denominator dividing \(Qhk_t\leq QN^2\). Thus every returned endpoint also has bit length \(O(L+\log N)\).

For example, deterministic Edmonds–Karp gives a polynomial number of augmentations regardless of capacity magnitudes. Consequently, construction takes

\[ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log N) \]

time and space, and evaluation takes polynomial time in the same parameters. No operation iterates over \(N\) individuals.

5. Envy-freeness and proportionality

First, allocation rates are nondecreasing.

Suppose block \(B\) receives rate \(\lambda\), and put \(X'=X\setminus D_X(B)\). For any nonempty set \(C\) of remaining types,

\[ d_X(B\cup C)=d_X(B)+d_{X'}(C). \]

Minimality of \(B\)’s ratio implies

\[ d_X(B)+d_{X'}(C) \geq\lambda\bigl(k(B)+k(C)\bigr). \]

Since \(d_X(B)=\lambda k(B)\), we obtain \(d_{X'}(C)\geq\lambda k(C)\). Therefore the next rate is at least \(\lambda\).

Now consider individual \(i\), allocated at rate \(q_i\). Its entire allocated length is desired.

An earlier individual \(j\) receives length at most \(q_i\), so

\[ \operatorname{len}(A_j\cap U_i)\leq\operatorname{len}(A_j)\leq q_i. \]

An individual allocated in the same block receives length \(q_i\), giving the same inequality. A later individual receives no positive-length portion of \(U_i\), because all remaining cake desired by \(i\) is removed when \(i\)’s block is allocated.

Thus \(i\) values its own piece at least as much as every other piece. This proves envy-freeness.

Every positively desired elementary interval is assigned to a type that desires it: an interval is removed at the first stage whose block desires it, and every reported type is eventually allocated. Hence all of \(U_i\) is assigned, up to endpoints. Envy-freeness then gives

\[ \operatorname{len}(U_i) =\sum_{j=1}^N\operatorname{len}(A_j\cap U_i) \leq Nq_i. \]

Dividing by \(\operatorname{len}(U_i)>0\) yields \(V_i(A_i)\geq1/N\).

6. Dominant-strategy truthfulness, including rank changes

Fix an individual \(i\), its true reference set \(U_i\), and arbitrary reports of all other individuals. Compare the run where \(i\) reports \(U_i\) with the run where it reports any allowed alternative \(U_i'\).

For this proof only, view the mechanism on the fixed set of individual identities. Lemma 1 shows that the compressed algorithm selects exactly the unique largest minimizing individual block.

Compare the runs while their selected blocks agree and exclude \(i\). At every such stage, the block’s reports are identical in both runs. Its entire desired union is therefore identical and is removed in both runs. Consequently, both runs reach the same remaining individual set and the same remaining cake \(X\).

Stop at the first stage where either the selected blocks differ or the truthful block contains \(i\). Such a stage exists because the truthful run eventually allocates \(i\).

Let \(B,\lambda\) be the truthful block and rate, and \(B',\lambda'\) the alternative block and rate at this common state.

Case 1: The truthful block excludes \(i\)

The blocks differ at this stage. Since \(B\) excludes \(i\), its ratio is unchanged by the deviation. Therefore \(\lambda'\leq\lambda\).

I claim that \(i\in B'\). Otherwise, \(B'\)’s ratio would also be unchanged, giving \(\lambda'\geq\lambda\). Thus both rates would equal \(\lambda\). Then \(B'\) would be a truthful minimizer and \(B\) an alternative-profile minimizer. Largest-minimizer selection would imply both \(B'\subseteq B\) and \(B\subseteq B'\), contradicting their difference.

The deviation therefore allocates \(i\) immediately, with total length \(\lambda'\leq\lambda\). Its truly desired length is no greater than this total length. In the truthful run, \(i\) is allocated later at a rate at least \(\lambda\). The deviation cannot improve its utility.

Case 2: The truthful block contains \(i\)

Truthful reporting gives \(i\) desired length exactly \(\lambda\).

If \(\lambda'<\lambda\), the alternative block must contain \(i\): every set excluding \(i\) has unchanged ratio at least \(\lambda\). Hence \(i\)’s alternative piece has total length \(\lambda'<\lambda\), which cannot improve its utility.

It remains to consider \(\lambda'\geq\lambda\). Put

\[ D=X\cap\bigcup_{j\in B}U_j. \]

Here \(B\) is an individual set, so

\[ \operatorname{len}(D)=|B|\lambda. \]

For every \(j\in B\setminus\{i\}\), its report is unchanged. In the alternative run:

  • its piece lies inside \(U_j\cap X\), up to endpoints, and therefore inside \(D\);
  • it receives length at least \(\lambda'\), by nondecreasing allocation rates.

Their pieces are disjoint. The truly desired portion of \(i\)’s alternative piece is also contained in \(D\). Consequently,

\[ \operatorname{len}(A_i'\cap U_i) \leq |B|\lambda-(|B|-1)\lambda' \leq\lambda. \]

Again, the deviation cannot improve \(i\)’s utility.

These cases exhaust all possibilities. Dividing desired lengths by the fixed positive quantity \(\operatorname{len}(U_i)\) proves dominant-strategy truthfulness.

The argument tracks the same identity throughout. It places no restriction on the individual’s new type or rank, and allows types to enter or leave the support. The rank merely determines which geometric subsegments implement the guaranteed lengths.

Conclusion

The mechanism above satisfies deterministic dominant-strategy truthfulness, proportionality, and envy-freeness for the stated finite-population model. Its allocation representation and evaluator have uniform polynomial complexity in \(m,\tau,L,\log N\). This settles the support-encoded construction question affirmatively.
