The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given goods \(G\), rational masses \(\beta_b\) of buyer types \(b\), and rational masses \(\sigma_s\) of seller types \(s\), with \(\sum_b\beta_b+\sum_s\sigma_s=1\), each buyer type specified by a complete valuation vector \(v_{bj}\) and integer capacity \(d_b\), and each seller type by complete reservation-price and stock vectors \(r_{js}\) and \(q_{js}\), compute a competitive equilibrium. The decision variables \(x_{bjs}\ge 0\) are masses of units of good \(j\) traded from seller type \(s\) to buyer type \(b\); they satisfy \(\sum_{j,s}x_{bjs}\le\beta_b d_b\) and \(\sum_bx_{bjs}\le\sigma_s q_{js}\), and maximize \(\sum_{b,j,s}(v_{bj}-r_{js})x_{bjs}\). Return a nonnegative price vector \(p\) such that each buyer type's trades are a mass average of individually optimal integral demand plans at \(p\), each seller type's supply follows its individual price-taking supply rule, and the trades clear the market.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) goods, and let \(B,S\) be nonempty sets of buyer and seller types. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|B|+|S|\) types with positive masses, each a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses satisfy \(\sum_b\beta_b+\sum_s\sigma_s=1\); let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Buyer type \(b\) has complete nonnegative rational per-unit valuations \(v_{bj}\) and capacity \(d_b\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Seller type \(s\) has complete nonnegative rational reservation prices \(r_{js}\) and stocks \(q_{js}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). Require \(q_{js}=0\Rightarrow r_{js}=0\), and positive stock at some seller type for every good.
At prices \(p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{m}\), define individual buyer demand by \(D_b(p)=\operatorname*{argmax}_{a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m},\,\sum_j a_j\le d_b}\sum_j(v_{bj}-p_j)a_j\). Demand plans impose no availability restrictions beyond the buyer's capacity; all maximizers are allowed.
Individual seller supply of good \(j\) is \(q_{js}\) when \(p_j>r_{js}\), zero when \(p_j<r_{js}\), and any integer between zero and \(q_{js}\) when prices are equal; arbitrary mixtures of optimal plans are allowed.
A feasible aggregate trade array \(x\ge0\) satisfies \(\sum_{j,s}x_{bjs}\le\beta_b d_b\) and \(\sum_bx_{bjs}\le\sigma_s q_{js}\). It is optimal if it maximizes \(\sum_{b,j,s}(v_{bj}-r_{js})x_{bjs}\) over these arrays.
An equilibrium is an optimal feasible array together with prices such that \((\sum_sx_{bjs}/\beta_b)_{j\in G}\in\operatorname{conv}D_b(p)\) for every buyer type, and \(\sum_bx_{bjs}/\sigma_s\) equals \(q_{js}\), zero, or an element of \([0,q_{js}]\), respectively, when \(p_j>r_{js}\), \(p_j<r_{js}\), or \(p_j=r_{js}\). The common trade array clears every market.
Is computing an exact rational equilibrium pair \((p,x)\) polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
For finite individual agents, equilibrium prices are characterized by optimal dual solutions, and every equilibrium price supports every optimal integral assignment; equilibria can be computed using linear programming. [Jaume, Massó, and Neme](https://bse.eu/sites/default/files/working_paper_pdfs/389revised.pdf).

Result

Computing an exact rational equilibrium pair is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\).

The proof works directly with the supported types and their rational masses. It uses the LP-duality principle underlying the finite-agent result of [Jaume, Massó, and Neme](https://bse.eu/sites/default/files/working_paper_pdfs/389revised.pdf), with an explicit price construction and verification of the required mixtures.

1. A compact primal–dual pair

Write the aggregate buyer capacities and seller stocks as

\( \displaystyle C_b=\beta_b d_b,\qquad Q_{js}=\sigma_s q_{js}. \)

Consider the welfare linear program

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad& \sum_{b,j,s}(v_{bj}-r_{js})x_{bjs}\\ \text{subject to}\quad& \sum_{j,s}x_{bjs}\le C_b &&(b\in B),\\ &\sum_bx_{bjs}\le Q_{js} &&(j\in G,\ s\in S),\\ &x_{bjs}\ge0 &&(b\in B,\ j\in G,\ s\in S). \end{aligned} \)

This is exactly the optimization problem in the question. Its feasible set is nonempty because the zero array is feasible. It is compact: it is closed, and every coordinate satisfies \(0\le x_{bjs}\le C_b\). Consequently, an optimum exists.

The dual is

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad& \sum_b C_b\alpha_b+\sum_{j,s}Q_{js}\gamma_{js}\\ \text{subject to}\quad& \alpha_b+\gamma_{js}\ge v_{bj}-r_{js} && (b,j,s),\\ &\alpha_b\ge0,\qquad\gamma_{js}\ge0. \end{aligned} \)

The dual is feasible: take \(\alpha_b=\max_j v_{bj}\) and \(\gamma_{js}=0\), using \(r_{js}\ge0\). By strong LP duality, primal and dual optima exist and have equal objective values.

Choose any optimal primal solution \(x\) and any optimal dual solution \((\alpha,\gamma)\). Complementary slackness gives

\( \displaystyle \alpha_b\left(C_b-\sum_{j,s}x_{bjs}\right)=0, \qquad \gamma_{js}\left(Q_{js}-\sum_bx_{bjs}\right)=0, \)

and

\( \displaystyle x_{bjs}\bigl(\alpha_b+\gamma_{js}-v_{bj}+r_{js}\bigr)=0. \)

These relations hold for any such pair of optima: the dual objective minus the primal objective is the sum of the displayed nonnegative products, and that difference is zero.

2. Constructing common prices

Define, for every good,

\( \displaystyle p_j=\max\left\{0,\ \max_{b\in B}(v_{bj}-\alpha_b)\right\}. \)

Then \(p_j\ge0\), and its definition gives

\( \displaystyle v_{bj}-p_j\le\alpha_b \qquad\text{for every }b,j. \)

Dual feasibility also gives \(v_{bj}-\alpha_b\le r_{js}+\gamma_{js}\) for every buyer \(b\). Since \(r_{js}+\gamma_{js}\ge0\), taking the maximum yields

\( \displaystyle p_j\le r_{js}+\gamma_{js}\qquad\text{for every }j,s. \)

If \(x_{bjs}>0\), complementary slackness implies

\( \displaystyle \alpha_b+\gamma_{js}=v_{bj}-r_{js}. \)

Combining this equality with the two preceding inequalities gives

\( \displaystyle p_j\ge v_{bj}-\alpha_b=r_{js}+\gamma_{js}\ge p_j. \)

Thus every positive trade satisfies

\[ \displaystyle v_{bj}-p_j=\alpha_b, \qquad p_j-r_{js}=\gamma_{js}. \tag{*} \]

3. Verifying buyer demand, including integer-plan mixtures

For buyer type \(b\), define its normalized aggregate bundle and per-unit surpluses by

\( \displaystyle y_{bj}=\frac{\sum_sx_{bjs}}{\beta_b}, \qquad u_{bj}=v_{bj}-p_j. \)

The positive mass \(\beta_b\) makes this normalization well-defined. Primal feasibility implies

\( \displaystyle y_{bj}\ge0,\qquad \sum_jy_{bj}\le d_b. \)

We already know \(u_{bj}\le\alpha_b\). Moreover, if \(y_{bj}>0\), some \(x_{bjs}>0\), so \((*)\) gives \(u_{bj}=\alpha_b\).

We verify membership in \(\operatorname{conv}D_b(p)\) by explicitly constructing a mixture. Let \(e_j\) denote the \(j\)-th unit vector.

Case 1: \(\alpha_b>0\). Buyer-capacity complementary slackness gives

\( \displaystyle \sum_jy_{bj}=d_b. \)

Every feasible integer plan \(a\) satisfies

\( \displaystyle \sum_j u_{bj}a_j \le\alpha_b\sum_j a_j \le\alpha_b d_b. \)

For every \(j\) with \(y_{bj}>0\), the feasible integer plan \(d_b e_j\) attains this bound because \(u_{bj}=\alpha_b\). Hence each such plan belongs to \(D_b(p)\). The representation

\( \displaystyle y_b=\sum_{j:y_{bj}>0}\frac{y_{bj}}{d_b}\,(d_b e_j) \)

is a convex combination because its weights sum to one.

Case 2: \(\alpha_b=0\). All surpluses satisfy \(u_{bj}\le0\), so the zero plan is optimal. Whenever \(y_{bj}>0\), we have \(u_{bj}=0\), and \(d_b e_j\) is also optimal. Now

\( \displaystyle y_b= \left(1-\frac{\sum_jy_{bj}}{d_b}\right)0 +\sum_{j:y_{bj}>0}\frac{y_{bj}}{d_b}\,(d_b e_j) \)

is a convex combination of optimal integer plans.

Therefore \(y_b\in\operatorname{conv}D_b(p)\) for every buyer type. This argument uses precisely the stated demand domain: each plan \(d_b e_j\) is available without a stock restriction.

4. Verifying seller supply and market clearing

Fix \(j,s\), and write \(T_{js}=\sum_bx_{bjs}\).

If \(p_j>r_{js}\), then

\( \displaystyle \gamma_{js}\ge p_j-r_{js}>0. \)

Seller-stock complementary slackness therefore gives \(T_{js}=Q_{js}\), and hence \(T_{js}/\sigma_s=q_{js}\).

If \(p_j<r_{js}\), positive trade is impossible: \(x_{bjs}>0\) would imply, by \((*)\),

\( \displaystyle \gamma_{js}=p_j-r_{js}<0, \)

contradicting dual nonnegativity. Thus \(T_{js}=0\).

If \(p_j=r_{js}\), primal feasibility gives

\( \displaystyle 0\le T_{js}/\sigma_s\le q_{js}, \)

as required. Any real quantity in this interval is realizable by a mixture of the integer supply plans \(0\) and \(q_{js}\); when \(q_{js}=0\), the only quantity is zero. Choices for different goods can be combined by taking the product of their finite distributions, since seller optimization is separable across goods.

Finally, the same array \(x\) describes purchases and sales, so every market clears. The array is welfare-optimal by construction. Consequently, \((p,x)\) is an equilibrium under every condition in the question.

The proof also establishes that the prices constructed from any optimal dual solution support every optimal aggregate trade array.

5. Exact polynomial-time computation

Use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a rational LP with a nonempty feasible set and finite optimal value admits an exact rational optimal solution computable in time polynomial in its binary encoding length.

The primal has \(m|B||S|\) variables and \(|B|+m|S|\) capacity constraints. The dual has \(|B|+m|S|\) variables and \(m|B||S|\) covering constraints. These sizes are polynomial in \(m,\tau\).

Their coefficients are input rationals or the products and differences

\( \displaystyle \beta_b d_b,\qquad \sigma_s q_{js},\qquad v_{bj}-r_{js}. \)

Each has binary encoding length bounded by a constant multiple of \(L\). Thus both LP encodings have length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), even when capacities and stocks are numerically large.

Solve the primal and dual exactly, then compute each \(p_j\) by rational subtraction and comparison. Exact rational LP algorithms return solutions of polynomial encoding length, and the price construction preserves that bound. This produces the complete rational array \(x\) and rational price vector \(p\) in polynomial time.
