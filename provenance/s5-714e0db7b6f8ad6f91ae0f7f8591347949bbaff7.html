The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a directed network \(D=(V,E;s,t)\) and rational type masses \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{E}\) with \(\sum_{e\in E}\mu_e=1\) and \(F_D(\mu)>0\), where type \(e\) consists of interchangeable suppliers whose mass provides capacity on arc \(e\), let \(F_D(y)\) be the maximum \(s\)-to-\(t\) flow under capacities \(y\). Find payment rates \(p_e\ge 0\) and the largest \(\varepsilon\) satisfying \(\sum_e\mu_ep_e=1\) and \(\sum_ep_ey_e\ge F_D(y)/F_D(\mu)+\varepsilon\) for every coalition mass vector \(y\in\prod_e[0,\mu_e]\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(D=(V,E;s,t)\) be a finite weakly connected directed network with distinct source and sink, no public arcs, and \(m=|E|\).
A society is given by its support \(T\subseteq E\): the list of its \(\tau=|T|\) positive-mass types, each with binary-rational mass \(\mu_e>0\). Set \(\mu_e=0\) outside \(T\), and require \(\sum_e\mu_e=1\). Thus \(\tau\) counts present types, not possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Assume the support network contains at least two edge-disjoint directed source-to-sink paths, carrying over the empty-core restriction for the discrete path game.
Type \(e\) consists of interchangeable divisible suppliers whose contributed mass supplies capacity on arc \(e\). For real capacities \(y\ge0\), let \(F_D(y)\) denote the maximum source-to-sink flow; require \(F_D(\mu)>0\).
A coalition is any real vector \(y\in\prod_{e\in E}[0,\mu_e]\), including the empty and grand coalitions, with worth \(F_D(y)/F_D(\mu)\).
Choose real payment rates \(p_e\ge0\) for \(e\in T\), set \(p_e=0\) otherwise, and maximize \(\varepsilon\in\mathbb R\) subject to \(\sum_e\mu_ep_e=1\) and \(\sum_ep_ey_e\ge F_D(y)/F_D(\mu)+\varepsilon\) for every coalition \(y\).

Is exact computation of the optimal \(\varepsilon\) and an attaining payment vector polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,|V|,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For the discrete unit-capacity edge path game, a coalition earns one exactly when its arcs contain a source-to-sink path. Its least-core allocations are precisely the convex hull of minimum-cut incidence vectors divided by the maximum-flow value, yielding polynomial-time computation. Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact computation is polynomial-time solvable, and the optimal value is always \(\varepsilon^*=0\). A minimum cut in the network with capacities \(\mu\) yields an attaining payment vector.

1. Cut bound and upper bound on the objective

Write \(c=F_D(\mu)>0\). For any vertex set \(S\subseteq V\) with \(s\in S\) and \(t\notin S\), define its outgoing cut by \(\delta^+(S)=\{(u,v)\in E:u\in S,\ v\notin S\}\).

For every nonnegative capacity vector \(y\),

\[ F_D(y)\leq \sum_{e\in\delta^+(S)}y_e. \]

Indeed, summing flow conservation over the vertices in \(S\) shows that any feasible flow has value equal to its total flow leaving \(S\) minus its total flow entering \(S\). The latter is nonnegative, and each outgoing arc carries at most its capacity.

The empty-coalition constraint gives \(0\geq\varepsilon\). Independently, the grand-coalition constraint and payment normalization give \(1\geq1+\varepsilon\). Thus every feasible solution satisfies \(\varepsilon\leq0\).

2. A payment vector attaining zero

By the max-flow/min-cut theorem, choose \(S^*\subseteq V\), containing \(s\) and excluding \(t\), such that

\[ \sum_{e\in\delta^+(S^*)}\mu_e=c. \]

The theorem applies to this finite directed network with nonnegative capacities; zero capacities on arcs outside \(T\) are permitted.

Define the payment rates by

\[ p_e= \begin{cases} 1/c,&e\in T\cap\delta^+(S^*),\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

These rates are nonnegative and vanish outside \(T\). Since \(\mu_e=0\) outside \(T\), they satisfy the required normalization:

\[ \sum_{e\in E}\mu_ep_e =\frac{1}{c}\sum_{e\in\delta^+(S^*)}\mu_e =1. \]

Now let \(y\) be any real coalition satisfying \(0\leq y_e\leq\mu_e\). In particular, \(y_e=0\) outside \(T\). Applying the cut bound from Section 1 gives

\[ \sum_{e\in E}p_ey_e =\frac{1}{c}\sum_{e\in\delta^+(S^*)}y_e \geq\frac{F_D(y)}{c}. \]

Consequently, this payment vector satisfies every coalition constraint with \(\varepsilon=0\), including all fractional coalitions. Combined with the upper bound, this proves \(\varepsilon^*=0\).

3. Exact computation and bit complexity

The construction requires one exact minimum-cut computation. To verify polynomial bit complexity, write each present mass as \(\mu_e=a_e/b_e\), with positive integers encoded in binary. Set

\[ Q=\prod_{e\in T}b_e, \qquad A_e=Q\mu_e\quad(e\in T), \]

and set \(A_e=0\) outside \(T\). This also covers the special case of dyadic rational inputs.

Each \(A_e\) is an integer. The binary length of \(Q\) is at most one plus the sum of the binary lengths of the denominators, hence is \(O(L)\). Moreover,

\[ \sum_{e\in E}A_e=Q, \]

so every integer capacity has \(O(L)\) bits.

Run the Edmonds–Karp algorithm on these integer capacities. Its standard bound is \(O(|V|m^2)\) arithmetic operations, independent of the capacity magnitudes. Its flow and residual-capacity arithmetic uses integers of \(O(L)\) bits, so its bit complexity is polynomial in \(|V|,m,L\). The final residual graph supplies a minimum cut \(S^*\).

Let

\[ k=\sum_{e\in\delta^+(S^*)}A_e. \]

Scaling all capacities by \(Q\) scales every feasible flow value by \(Q\), so \(k=Qc\). The assumptions give \(1\leq k\leq Q\). Therefore the output rates are exactly

\[ p_e= \begin{cases} Q/k,&e\in T\cap\delta^+(S^*),\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \]

and each has \(O(L)\) bits. Computing and writing the entire vector is polynomial in \(m,\tau,|V|,L\).

4. Why the path-game restriction does not prevent this result

The two-edge-disjoint-path hypothesis is compatible with the proof and does not create an empty core for the stated worth function. Here, coalition worth is normalized flow value. The capacity of one fixed minimum cut bounds that worth for every real coalition, and paying for the coalition’s capacity on that cut meets the bound.

This distinction is also present in the cited paper, which separately discusses flow games and their minimum-cut core descriptions. [Fang et al., Introduction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.04575) The argument above directly verifies the needed property for the support-encoded masses and all real coalitions.
