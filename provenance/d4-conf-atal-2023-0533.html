The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a weighted road graph \(G\), ordered PoI categories \(V_1,\ldots,V_k\), and a finite support \(T\) of origin–destination types \(t=(o_t,d_t)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), find a valid itinerary \(P=(v_1,\ldots,v_k)\) minimizing \(J_\mu(P)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\left[d_G(o_t,v_1)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}d_G(v_j,v_{j+1})+d_G(v_k,d_t)\right]\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G=(V,E,w)\) be a finite connected undirected road graph with \(n=|V|\), \(m=|E|\), and nonnegative binary-rational edge lengths; let \(d_G\) denote shortest-path distance. There are \(k\geq 1\) ordered, nonempty, pairwise disjoint PoI categories \(V_1,\ldots,V_k\subseteq V\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) distinct origin–destination types \(t=(o_t,d_t)\in V\times V\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A valid itinerary is a single common tuple \(P=(v_1,\ldots,v_k)\) with \(v_j\in V_j\); travel between successive designated stops uses shortest paths. Incidental visits to other PoIs and repeated road vertices are allowed. Any minimizing itinerary is acceptable.
Minimize \(J_\mu(P)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\bigl[d_G(o_t,v_1)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}d_G(v_j,v_{j+1})+d_G(v_k,d_t)\bigr]\).

Is exact minimization of \(J_\mu\) solvable in time polynomial in \(n,m,k,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a finite list of agents, minimizing the unweighted sum of their travel distances over common itineraries with prescribed category order admits a polynomial-time algorithm.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified an undirected graph, following the road-network model, and nonnegative binary-rational edge lengths to make distances and input complexity precise.
  • Required connectivity and nonempty categories so every itinerary has finite travel cost and a feasible itinerary exists.
  • Specified at least one category, with pairwise disjoint categories partitioning the designated PoIs; other road vertices may exist.
  • Allowed incidental PoI visits and repeated road vertices because validity constrains the designated stops and permits arbitrary connecting paths.
  • Allowed any minimizing itinerary because the aggregate-distance problem imposes no tie-breaking rule.
  • Defined n as the vertex count, m as the edge count, and L as total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact minimization is polynomial-time solvable, including when the masses are arbitrary positive binary-rational numbers. The key is that the travel cost between designated stops is common to all types.

  • Separate the objective.

Define
\(A(v)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t d_G(o_t,v)\) and
\(B(v)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t d_G(v,d_t)\).

Since \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), every valid itinerary satisfies
\[ J_\mu(v_1,\ldots,v_k) =A(v_1)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}d_G(v_j,v_{j+1})+B(v_k). \]
Thus the society affects only the costs at the first and last designated stops. This identity uses a single common itinerary, exactly as required.

  • Compute an optimal itinerary by dynamic programming.

First compute all shortest-path distances. For \(v\in V_1\), set \(F_1(v)=A(v)\). For each \(j\in\{2,\ldots,k\}\) and \(v\in V_j\), set
\[ F_j(v)=\min_{u\in V_{j-1}}\bigl(F_{j-1}(u)+d_G(u,v)\bigr). \]
Return a tuple attaining
\[ \min_{v\in V_k}\bigl(F_k(v)+B(v)\bigr), \]
recording a minimizing predecessor in each recurrence and tracing backward.

To prove correctness, interpret \(F_j(v)\) as the minimum of
\(A(v_1)+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}d_G(v_i,v_{i+1})\)
over all choices \(v_i\in V_i\) with \(v_j=v\). For \(j=1\), this is precisely the initialization. For \(j\geq2\), every such tuple has a predecessor \(u=v_{j-1}\in V_{j-1}\). Once \(u\) is fixed, its minimum prefix cost is \(F_{j-1}(u)\), and extending to \(v\) adds exactly \(d_G(u,v)\). Conversely, every minimizing prefix can be extended to \(v\). This proves the recurrence by induction. Adding \(B(v_k)\) gives the original objective by step 1.

All minima exist because the categories are finite and nonempty. Connectivity makes every distance finite. The same argument covers \(k=1\), where the answer minimizes \(A(v)+B(v)\) over \(V_1\). Incidental PoI visits and repeated road vertices impose no additional constraints under the stated premises.

  • Verify polynomial bit complexity.

Write each edge length as \(w_e=p_e/q_e\), with integer \(p_e\geq0\) and integer \(q_e>0\), and each mass as \(\mu_t=r_t/s_t\), with positive integer numerator and denominator. Let
\(D=\prod_{e\in E}q_e\) and \(M=\prod_{t\in T}s_t\), taking an empty product to be \(1\).

Both products have bit length \(O(L)\): the logarithm of each product is the sum of the logarithms of its input denominators. Define integer edge lengths \(a_e=Dw_e\) and integer masses \(b_t=M\mu_t\). Then \(\sum_t b_t=M\).

Let \(\delta(x,y)\) be the shortest-path distance with edge lengths \(a_e\). Scaling every walk’s length by \(D>0\) preserves its ordering, so \(\delta(x,y)=D\,d_G(x,y)\). Consequently,
\[ DM\,J_\mu(P) =\sum_t b_t\delta(o_t,v_1) +M\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\delta(v_j,v_{j+1}) +\sum_t b_t\delta(v_k,d_t). \]
We can therefore perform the entire algorithm using integers: use endpoint costs \(\sum_t b_t\delta(o_t,v)\) and \(\sum_t b_t\delta(v,d_t)\), and transition costs \(M\delta(u,v)\). Multiplication of the objective by the positive constant \(DM\) preserves all minimizers.

Set \(H=\max(\{1\}\cup\{a_e:e\in E\})\). Nonnegative lengths imply that deleting cycles cannot increase a walk’s length. Hence every shortest-path distance is attained by a simple path and is at most \(nH\). The bit length of \(H\) is \(O(L)\).

Each scaled endpoint cost is at most \(MnH\), because \(\sum_t b_t=M\). Every dynamic-programming value and every candidate final cost is at most \(M(k+1)nH\), so their bit lengths are \(O(L+\log n+\log(k+1))\). Thus all arithmetic and comparisons have polynomial cost.

For example, Floyd–Warshall computes the integer distances with \(O(n^3)\) additions and comparisons. Its recurrence considers whether a shortest path uses the newly allowed internal vertex; cycle deletion justifies choosing a simple shortest path, including when some edges have length zero. Computing the endpoint costs takes \(O(\tau n)\) arithmetic operations. The dynamic program takes
\(O\bigl(\sum_{j=2}^k |V_{j-1}||V_j|\bigr)\subseteq O(kn^2)\)
operations. Constructing the scaled integers and recovering the tuple also take polynomial time.

Therefore, the complete algorithm runs in time polynomial in \(n,m,k,\tau,L\). It processes the support directly; rational masses do not require expanding the society into a potentially enormous list of agents.
