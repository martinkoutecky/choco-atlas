The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two atomless role populations, each with a finite action set and a common conditional ranking map ρ_i(a_{-i}), a scoring rule ν, and an iteration count \(T\), compute the WFP action-mass distributions \(x_T^{(1)},x_T^{(2)}\) and certify the stated ε_T bound. If the guarantee is measured against latent cardinal payoffs, the instance also supplies bounds \(D_i\) on the paper’s ̅d_i^+ terms.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Two atomless role populations each have total mass \(1\), finite nonempty action sets \(A_1,A_2\), and explicitly tabulated common conditional weak rankings \(\rho_i:A_{-i}\to\mathcal P(A_i)\). Set \(m=\max_i|A_i|\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here a type specifies its role and initial rational action distribution; masses sum to \(1\) within each role. Rankings and latent utilities are common within each role.
The scoring rule \(\nu\) is supplied by a nonnegative rational score vector \(s_i(r,\cdot)\) for each occurring ranking \(r\), with identical rankings receiving identical vectors. Define \(BR_i(q)=\Delta\bigl(\arg\max_{a\in A_i}\sum_{b\in A_{-i}}q(b)s_i(\rho_i(b),a)\bigr)\).
Let \(y_{i,1}\) be the initial aggregate action distribution. Unregularized mass WFP simultaneously sets \(x_t^{(i)}=t^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^t y_{i,k}\) and \(y_{i,t+1}\) to the uniform distribution over the winning actions against \(x_t^{(-i)}\); atomless masses may split among these actions. The requested output is \((x_T^{(1)},x_T^{(2)})\), for an integer \(T\ge1\).
Latent utilities \(u_i:A_1\times A_2\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) strictly respect strict rankings and equate tied actions, with positive context sums and \(\max_b\sum_a u_i(a,b)=1\). Supplied binary rational bounds \(D_i\ge0\) are promised to satisfy \(\max_{z\in\Delta(A_i)}u_i(z,q)-u_i(w,q)\le D_i\) for every \(q\in\Delta(A_{-i})\) and \(w\in BR_i(q)\).
Measure approximation by \(\epsilon(x)=\max_i\bigl[\max_{z\in\Delta(A_i)}u_i(z,x^{(-i)})-u_i(x)\bigr]\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; utilities are latent, and the guarantee is conditional on the supplied promises.

Can mass WFP be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,T\) with the universal guarantee \(\epsilon(x_T)\le\frac{T+1}{2T}+\frac{T-1}{2T}\max_i D_i\), or can this approximation guarantee fail?

For two finite strategic players, the ordinal WFP result gives this bound using uniform best-response distortion bounds ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07996v1)). Exact fictitious play with utilities in \([0,1]\) has the tight guarantee \((T+1)/(2T)\) ([source](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/fictitiousAllerton09.pdf)).
No result settling this specific atomless mass formulation was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized each role population to mass one and made types specify role and initial action distribution, since population support and initialization were undefined.
  • Used explicitly tabulated weak rankings, allowing indifference, and an explicit rational score-vector representation of the scoring rule.
  • Specified simultaneous responses to historical aggregate play, arithmetic averaging, and uniform splitting over tied winners; used unregularized responses because the bound assumes them.
  • Made latent utilities common within each role and evaluated exploitability through independent role action distributions, preserving the two-player utility model.
  • Required nonnegative utilities, positive context sums, and maximum context sum one, carrying over the normalization needed by the numerical bound.
  • Expressed the supplied distortion bounds as promises covering every opponent distribution and every scoring best response; certification is conditional on these promises.
  • Measured runtime polynomially in T itself, rather than only its binary encoding length.

Result

The guarantee holds. Under the stated promises, the prescribed mass WFP trajectory can be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,T\), and it satisfies the stronger role-specific bounds
\[ \epsilon_i(x_T)\le \frac{T+1}{2T}+\frac{T-1}{2T}D_i. \]
Here and below, \(U_i(p,q)\) denotes role \(i\)’s expected latent utility when its own action distribution is \(p\) and the other role’s distribution is \(q\).

1. Aggregation and utility bounds

Let \(H_i\) be the supported types belonging to role \(i\). For each type \(h\), write its mass as \(\lambda_h\) and its initial action distribution as \(p_h\). Then
\[ y_{i,1}(a)=\sum_{h\in H_i}\lambda_h p_h(a). \]
Because the masses within each role sum to one, this is an action distribution.

All types within a role share the rankings, scoring rule, and latent utilities. Consequently, the prescribed subsequent updates depend only on the two aggregate distributions. Assigning every type in a role the uniform distribution over that role’s winning actions realizes the required aggregate update; atomlessness permits these mass splits.

Nonnegativity and normalization imply that every pure payoff lies in \([0,1]\): each payoff is at most its context sum, and every context sum is at most one. Expected utilities are convex combinations of pure payoffs, so
\[ 0\le U_i(p,q)\le 1 \]
for all action distributions \(p,q\).

2. Proof of the approximation guarantee

Fix a role \(i\), a horizon \(T\ge1\), and an arbitrary deviation distribution \(z\in\Delta(A_i)\). Define
\[ g_{s,t}=U_i(z,y_{-i,t})-U_i(y_{i,s},y_{-i,t}), \qquad 1\le s,t\le T. \]
The utility bounds give \(g_{s,t}\le1\).

For every \(s\ge2\), the update is supported on scoring-rule winners, hence
\[ y_{i,s}\in BR_i(x_{s-1}^{(-i)}). \]
Apply the promised distortion bound with \(q=x_{s-1}^{(-i)}\) and \(w=y_{i,s}\). Since the maximum over deviations is at least the payoff from \(z\),
\[ U_i(z,x_{s-1}^{(-i)})-U_i(y_{i,s},x_{s-1}^{(-i)})\le D_i. \]
Using \(x_{s-1}^{(-i)}=(s-1)^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{s-1}y_{-i,t}\) and linearity in the opponent’s distribution yields
\[ \sum_{t=1}^{s-1}g_{s,t}\le (s-1)D_i. \]
For the remaining \(T-s+1\) terms, use \(g_{s,t}\le1\). Thus
\[ \sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{s,t}\le (s-1)D_i+T-s+1. \]
This inequality also holds for \(s=1\), because its prefix is empty.

Bilinearity now expresses regret at the product of the two averages:
\[ U_i(z,x_T^{(-i)})-U_i(x_T^{(i)},x_T^{(-i)}) =\frac1{T^2}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}g_{s,t}. \]
Therefore,
\[ U_i(z,x_T^{(-i)})-U_i(x_T^{(i)},x_T^{(-i)}) \le \frac1{T^2}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\bigl((s-1)D_i+T-s+1\bigr). \]
The two sums are \(T(T-1)/2\) and \(T(T+1)/2\), respectively. Hence
\[ U_i(z,x_T^{(-i)})-U_i(x_T^{(i)},x_T^{(-i)}) \le \frac{T+1}{2T}+\frac{T-1}{2T}D_i. \]
Taking the maximum over \(z\), then over the two roles, proves
\[ \boxed{\epsilon(x_T)\le \frac{T+1}{2T}+\frac{T-1}{2T}\max_i D_i.} \]

The argument includes \(T=1\), when the bound is simply one. It requires neither pure round-by-round actions nor rational latent utilities.

This is the mixed-distribution extension of the triangular summation used in [Conitzer’s finite-player proof](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/fictitiousAllerton09.pdf) and [the ordinal WFP calculation in Appendix C.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07996v1#A3.SS1).

3. Exact computation and bit complexity

The following implementation uses only the supplied rational data.

Let \(Q\) be the product, with multiplicity, of the denominators of all supported masses and initial-distribution coordinates. Set
\[ H=Q\,m!. \]
Every product \(\lambda_h p_h(a)\) has denominator dividing \(Q\). Also, every possible number of winning actions belongs to \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\) and therefore divides \(H\).

Maintain integer vectors
\[ N_{i,t}(a)=H\sum_{k=1}^{t}y_{i,k}(a). \]
Initialization is exact:
\[ N_{i,1}(a)=\sum_{h\in H_i}H\lambda_h p_h(a). \]

Let \(P\) be the product of all denominators in the supplied score vectors, and precompute the integer score tables
\[ c_i(a,b)=P\,s_i(\rho_i(b),a). \]
At round \(t\), calculate
\[ V_{i,t}(a)=\sum_{b\in A_{-i}}N_{-i,t}(b)c_i(a,b). \]
Indeed, the expected score against \(x_t^{(-i)}\) equals \(V_{i,t}(a)/(HtP)\). Thus the winning set is determined by exact integer comparisons:
\[ W_{i,t}=\arg\max_{a\in A_i}V_{i,t}(a). \]

Compute both winning sets from the current vectors before updating either role. If \(k_{i,t}=|W_{i,t}|\), update
\[ N_{i,t+1}(a)= \begin{cases} N_{i,t}(a)+H/k_{i,t},&a\in W_{i,t},\\ N_{i,t}(a),&a\notin W_{i,t}. \end{cases} \]
The increments are integers. Induction verifies that these vectors represent exactly the prescribed simultaneous mass WFP trajectory. After \(T-1\) updates, output
\[ x_T^{(i)}(a)=\frac{N_{i,T}(a)}{HT}. \]

To check bit complexity:

  • The binary lengths of \(Q\) and \(P\) are \(O(L)\).
  • The binary length of \(H\) is \(O(L+m\log(m+1))\).
  • Since \(0\le N_{i,t}(a)\le Ht\), each maintained coordinate has \(O(L+m\log(m+1)+\log(T+1))\) bits.
  • The integer scores and their weighted sums also have polynomially bounded binary length.

Initialization uses \(O(\tau m)\) coordinate operations, and each round uses \(O(m^2)\) integer arithmetic operations and comparisons. Standard integer arithmetic therefore gives total running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,T\), including exact ties and output encoding.

The algorithm never accesses latent utilities or verifies the distortion promises. Its guarantee holds for every latent utility pair satisfying those promises.
