The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given distinct rational bichromatic signal distributions \(P_i=(p_i,1-p_i)\) for \(i\in[n]\), choose a common row-stochastic policy \(x_{c,j}\), where \(x_{c,j}\) is the fraction of signal-type \(c\) mass assigned a ballot for urn \(j\). For each possible correct urn \(i\), let \(v^{(i)}_j=\sum_c p_{i,c}x_{c,j}\). Maximize \(\delta=\min_{i,\ j\ne i}(v^{(i)}_i-v^{(i)}_j)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given \(m\ge2\) urns with binary rational probabilities \(0\le p_1<\cdots<p_m\le1\), urn \(i\) induces the signal distribution \(P_i=(p_i,1-p_i)\) on blue and red.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here each possible correct urn specifies a conditional society of total mass \(1\); let \(\tau\le2\) be the maximum of these support sizes, omitting zero-mass types from each list.
Choose one common policy \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{2\times m}\), with \(\sum_{j=1}^m x_{c,j}=1\) for each color \(c\). The fraction \(x_{c,j}\) of color-\(c\) mass casts a single ballot for urn \(j\); the policy cannot depend on which urn is correct.
When urn \(i\) is correct, its society produces ballot masses \(v_j^{(i)}=p_i x_{\mathrm{blue},j}+(1-p_i)x_{\mathrm{red},j}\). Plurality uses these masses; a tie does not constitute a unique correct winner.
Define \(\delta^*=\max_x\min_{i\ne j}(v_i^{(i)}-v_j^{(i)})\). The decision input additionally includes a binary rational threshold \(d\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Is deciding whether \(\delta^*\ge d\) in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite voters, Chierichetti and Kleinberg give a common randomized signal-to-ballot policy ensuring that the correct urn uniquely wins with probability at least \(1-\eta\), using \(O(m^3\epsilon^{-2}\log\eta^{-1})\) voters, where \(\epsilon=\min_{1\le i<m}(p_{i+1}-p_i)\) and \(0<\eta\le1/2\). This is a sufficient-voter guarantee, not an exact margin-optimization result.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Renamed the number of urns from n to m; no finite voter count is an input to the continuous problem.
  • Required at least two urns and ordered the distinct probabilities within the closed interval [0,1], retaining the endpoint distributions allowed by the discrete definition.
  • Normalized each conditional society to total mass one and defined tau as the maximum conditional support size; zero-mass colors are omitted from support lists.
  • Allowed arbitrary nonnegative real policy fractions, with both color rows summing to one and the same policy used under every possible correct urn.
  • Retained unique plurality victory as the success condition; ties do not count as unique correct victories.
  • Added a binary rational threshold d to express the exact optimization question as a decision problem, and included its encoding in L.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). It is exactly feasibility of a rational linear program with \(\,2m\,\) variables and \(\,O(m^2)\,\) constraints.

This conclusion uses the stated ballot-mass formula. Under that formula, the conditional society lists impose no additional constraints on the policy; their support sizes therefore do not affect the optimization.

Exact linear formulation

Write \(\,b_j=x_{\mathrm{blue},j}\,\) and \(\,r_j=x_{\mathrm{red},j}\,\). Consider the following system of linear constraints:

  • \(\,b_j\ge0,\quad r_j\ge0\quad(1\le j\le m)\,\).
  • \(\,\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_j=1,\quad\sum_{j=1}^{m}r_j=1\,\).
  • \(\,p_i(b_i-b_j)+(1-p_i)(r_i-r_j)\ge d\quad(1\le i,j\le m,\ i\ne j)\,\).

There is just one pair of vectors \(\,(b,r)\,\), shared across all possible correct urns. Thus these constraints enforce precisely the requirement of a common policy.

To prove equivalence with the decision question, define the policy space

\(\displaystyle X=\left\{(b,r)\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{2m}:\sum_jb_j=\sum_jr_j=1\right\}\)

and the margin function

\(\displaystyle F(b,r)=\min_{i\ne j}\left[p_i(b_i-b_j)+(1-p_i)(r_i-r_j)\right].\)

The set \(\,X\,\) is nonempty: take \(\,b_j=r_j=1/m\,\). It is closed and bounded, because every coordinate lies in \(\,[0,1]\,\), and hence compact. The function \(\,F\,\) is continuous, being the minimum of finitely many linear functions. Consequently, its maximum on \(\,X\,\) is attained.

For each ordered pair \(\,i\ne j\,\), the given ballot-mass formula yields

\(\displaystyle v_i^{(i)}-v_j^{(i)}=p_i(b_i-b_j)+(1-p_i)(r_i-r_j).\)

Therefore:

  • If the displayed system is feasible, its policy satisfies \(\,F(b,r)\ge d\,\), so \(\,\delta^*\ge d\,\).
  • If \(\,\delta^*\ge d\,\), a maximizing policy exists by compactness and satisfies every constraint in the system.

Thus the system is feasible if and only if \(\,\delta^*\ge d\,\). This equivalence includes equality at the threshold.

Polynomial bit complexity

Write each input probability as \(\,p_i=u_i/w_i\,\), with \(\,w_i>0\,\), and write \(\,d=s/t\,\), with \(\,t>0\,\). Multiplying the constraint for \(\,(i,j)\,\) by the positive integer \(\,tw_i\,\) gives the equivalent integer inequality

\(\displaystyle tu_i(b_i-b_j)+t(w_i-u_i)(r_i-r_j)\ge sw_i.\)

Every resulting coefficient has \(\,O(L+1)\,\) bits: it is formed by subtraction and multiplication of integers from the input. The normalization equalities can each be replaced by two inequalities.

The resulting integer system has \(\,2m\,\) variables and \(\,m(m-1)+2m+4\,\) inequalities. Even a dense encoding has total bit length \(\,O(m^3(L+1))\,\), and it can be constructed in polynomial time.

The polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming decides feasibility of a finite rational linear-inequality system over the reals in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. It applies to this system, including when its feasible region has lower dimension. See [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239).

Hence the required decision runs in time polynomial in \(\,m,\tau,L\,\). The argument actually applies to arbitrary support sizes under the same ballot-mass formula.

Ties and the finite-voter guarantee

The decision asks for the non-strict inequality \(\,\delta^*\ge d\,\). At \(\,d=0\,\), the uniform policy above is always feasible and produces ties. For \(\,d>0\,\), every feasible policy gives the correct urn a strictly positive margin against every competitor, hence a unique correct winner in every conditional society.

The finite-voter guarantee is unnecessary for this exact decision: the rational linear system directly captures the prescribed ballot masses and margin threshold.
