The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(I,J\), rational mass distributions \(a,b\), rational tournament kernels \(W,U\) with \(W_{ii'}+W_{i'i}=1\) and \(U_{jj'}+U_{j'j}=1\), and a rational threshold \(\epsilon\), decide whether there is a coupling \(\pi\) with marginals \(a,b\) such that \(\frac12\sum_{i,i',j,j'}\pi_{ij}\pi_{i'j'}|W_{ii'}-U_{jj'}|\le\epsilon\). For the continuous ordered and cyclic kernels corresponding to \(T_{\Ord}\) and \(T_{\Rps}\), determine whether the minimum equals the normalized limit \(1/8\) from Proposition 4.1 or is smaller under fractional couplings.

A coupling is a real nonnegative matrix \(\pi\) satisfying \(\sum_j\pi_{ij}=a_i\) and \(\sum_i\pi_{ij}=b_j\). Define \(D(W,U;a,b)=\min_\pi\frac12\sum_{i,i',j,j'}\pi_{ij}\pi_{i'j'}|W_{ii'}-U_{jj'}|\).

Is deciding \(D(W,U;a,b)\le\epsilon\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Results

The finite decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even for equal uniform masses, tournament kernels, and one transitive kernel. For the specified atomless kernels, \(D_\infty=1/8\).

The proofs below account explicitly for fractional couplings.

1. Fractional couplings cannot improve the transitive tournament optimum

Let \(H\) be a tournament on \(N\) vertices, with kernel \(U\). Let \(W\) be the transitive tournament kernel:

\(W_{ii'}=1\) if \(i<i'\), \(W_{ii'}=0\) if \(i>i'\), and \(W_{ii}=1/2\).

Give every type in both societies mass \(1/N\). Define \(\operatorname{fas}(H)\) as the minimum number of backward edges over all total orders of the vertices of \(H\). We prove

\[ D(W,U;a,b)=\frac{\operatorname{fas}(H)}{N^2}. \]

Here and below, write \(F(\pi)\) for the objective evaluated at a particular coupling.

For an arbitrary feasible coupling \(\pi\), put \(p_{ij}=N\pi_{ij}\). Each column of \(p\) sums to one. Independently for each vertex \(j\) of \(H\), choose a random rank \(R_j\) with \(\Pr(R_j=i)=p_{ij}\). Break equal ranks using independent continuous uniform random variables. This produces a total order of all vertices.

Let \(B\) be its number of backward edges. For distinct vertices \(j,j'\), conditional on their sampled ranks:

  • If \(R_j<R_{j'}\), vertex \(j\) precedes vertex \(j'\).
  • If \(R_j>R_{j'}\), vertex \(j'\) precedes vertex \(j\).
  • If the ranks coincide, either order has probability \(1/2\).

Consequently, the conditional probability of disagreement with the edge of \(H\) is precisely \(\lvert W_{R_jR_{j'}}-U_{jj'}\rvert\). Independence of the sampled ranks gives

\[ \mathbb E B = \frac12\sum_{j\ne j'}\sum_{i,i'} p_{ij}p_{i'j'}\lvert W_{ii'}-U_{jj'}\rvert. \]

The terms with \(j=j'\) in the coupling objective are nonnegative. More precisely, because \(U_{jj}=1/2\),

\[ N^2F(\pi) = \mathbb E B+ \frac14\sum_j\left(1-\sum_i p_{ij}^2\right). \]

Indeed, \(\lvert W_{ii'}-1/2\rvert\) is zero for \(i=i'\) and \(1/2\) otherwise, while \(\sum_i p_{ij}=1\).

Every realization of the random order has at least \(\operatorname{fas}(H)\) backward edges. Also, \(\sum_i p_{ij}^2\le 1\). Hence

\[ F(\pi)\ge \frac{\operatorname{fas}(H)}{N^2}. \]

Conversely, choose an order attaining \(\operatorname{fas}(H)\), and couple the type at position \(i\) in the transitive tournament to the vertex at position \(i\) in that order, assigning mass \(1/N\). This is feasible. Each backward edge contributes \(1/N^2\) to the objective, and all other terms vanish. It attains the asserted lower bound.

2. Complexity of the finite problem

Hardness

We use the known \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness of deciding whether a tournament has a total order with at most \(K\) backward edges. This is the tournament feedback arc set problem proved hard by [Alon, *Ranking Tournaments*](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/050623905).

Given a tournament \(H\) on \(n\) vertices, let \(N\) be the smallest power of two with \(N\ge n\). Add \(N-n\) vertices which precede every original vertex and form a transitive tournament among themselves. Call the resulting tournament \(H^+\).

Restriction of any order of \(H^+\) to the original vertices proves \(\operatorname{fas}(H^+)\ge\operatorname{fas}(H)\). Prepending the added vertices in their transitive order to an optimal order of \(H\) proves the reverse inequality. Thus \(\operatorname{fas}(H^+)=\operatorname{fas}(H)\).

Construct the instance from Section 1 using \(H^+\), and set \(\epsilon=K/N^2\). Then

\[ D(W,U;a,b)\le\epsilon \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{fas}(H)\le K. \]

All kernel entries satisfy the required hypotheses. The masses \(1/N\) are dyadic, so the reduction works even if “binary rational” is interpreted as requiring a finite binary expansion.

Here \(m=2N\), \(\tau=N\), and the output length is \(L=O(m^2+m\log m)\), assuming the nontrivial range \(0\le K\le n(n-1)/2\). Construction takes \(O(m^2\log m)\) bit operations. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under polynomial-time many-one reductions.

Membership and an exact algorithm

For completeness, the general problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), despite allowing real couplings.

Put \(k=\tau_I\tau_J\), and flatten a coupling into a vector \(z\) of length \(k\). Let \(Az=c\) express its marginal constraints, where \(A\) has \(m\) rows and entries in \(\{0,1\}\). Define

\[ C_{(i,j),(i',j')}=\lvert W_{ii'}-U_{jj'}\rvert. \]

The complement identities imply that \(C\) is symmetric. The objective is therefore \(q(z)=\tfrac12z^{\mathsf T}Cz\), on the nonempty compact polytope \(Az=c,\ z\ge0\).

Choose a minimizer \(z_*\), and let \(S\) be its positive support. On this support, every direction \(v\) satisfying \(A_Sv=0\) permits sufficiently small perturbations in both signs. First-order optimality therefore gives \(v^{\mathsf T}C_Sz_*=0\). By elementary linear algebra, some multiplier vector \(\lambda_*\) satisfies

\[ A_Sz_*=c,\qquad C_Sz_*=A_S^{\mathsf T}\lambda_*. \]

Consider the rational linear feasibility system

\[ A_Sz=c,\qquad C_Sz=A_S^{\mathsf T}\lambda,\qquad z\ge0, \]

with \(\lambda\) unrestricted. It is feasible. Splitting each multiplier into a difference of nonnegative variables puts it in standard linear-programming form. A basic feasible solution then supplies rational \(z,\lambda\) of polynomial encoding length, by the determinant bounds for rational linear systems.

Every feasible solution of this system has the same objective value as \(z_*\). Indeed, symmetry gives

\[ q(z)-q(z_*) = \frac12(z-z_*)^{\mathsf T}C_S(z+z_*) = \frac12\bigl(A_S(z-z_*)\bigr)^{\mathsf T}(\lambda+\lambda_*) =0. \]

Extending \(z\) by zeros outside \(S\) thus yields a rational global minimizer of polynomial encoding length. A verifier checks its marginals, nonnegativity, and objective threshold using polynomial-time rational arithmetic. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), and hence \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

The same argument gives an exact algorithm: enumerate all supports \(S\), solve the displayed linear feasibility system, and evaluate one solution whenever it is feasible. Every candidate is a feasible coupling, and at least one candidate attains the global minimum.

There are at most \(2^k\) supports. The running time is

\[ 2^{\tau_I\tau_J}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \subseteq 2^{O(\tau^2)}\operatorname{poly}(L). \]

A candidate objective uses \(O(k^2)\) rational arithmetic operations. These bounds depend on the listed supports; no additional possible types are introduced.

3. The atomless value

Lower bound for every coupling

Let \(\gamma\) be any Borel probability coupling with Lebesgue marginals. For each fixed \((x,y)\), the integral triangle inequality gives

\[ \int \lvert W_{\mathrm{ord}}(x,x')-W_{\mathrm{cyc}}(y,y')\rvert \,d\gamma(x',y') \ge \left| \int W_{\mathrm{ord}}(x,x')\,dx' - \int W_{\mathrm{cyc}}(y,y')\,dy' \right|. \]

The two marginal identities justify replacing the respective integrals against \(\gamma\) by Lebesgue integrals. Directly from the kernel definitions,

\[ \int W_{\mathrm{ord}}(x,x')\,dx'=1-x, \qquad \int W_{\mathrm{cyc}}(y,y')\,dy'=\frac12. \]

The exceptional diagonal and antipodal points have Lebesgue measure zero. Integrating the lower bound against \(\gamma(x,y)\), and using its first marginal, yields

\[ F_\infty(\gamma) \ge \frac12\int_0^1\left|\frac12-x\right|\,dx = \frac18. \]

Thus this bound applies to arbitrary fractional or singular couplings.

Attainment

Take the identity coupling, the law of \((X,X)\) for uniform \(X\in[0,1)\). It has the required marginals.

For this coupling, away from the null sets where \(x=x'\) or \(\lvert x-x'\rvert=1/2\), the two kernels disagree exactly when \(\lvert x-x'\rvert>1/2\). Therefore

\[ F_\infty(\gamma_{\mathrm{id}}) = \frac12\int_0^1\int_0^1 \mathbf1_{\{\lvert x-x'\rvert>1/2\}}\,dx'\,dx = \int_0^{1/2}\left(\frac12-x\right)\,dx = \frac18. \]

Combined with the universal lower bound, this proves \(D_\infty=1/8\).
