The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many location types \(T\), coordinates \(p_t\in\mathbb{R}^3\), and positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), represent the population by an identity space \(\Omega_\mu=\bigsqcup_{t\in T}(\{t\}\times[0,\mu_t))\) with Lebesgue measure. Does there exist a measurable partition of this population into rooms of exactly three individuals such that, for every other rooming distinct modulo null sets, the measure of individuals whose sum of distances to their roommates decreases is strictly greater than the measure whose sum increases?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) location types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Locations \(p_t\in\mathbb R^m\), with \(m=3\), have real-algebraic coordinates encoded by integer minimal polynomials and rational isolating intervals; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
The population is \(\Omega_\mu=\bigsqcup_{t\in T}(\{t\}\times[0,\mu_t))\), with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\); write \(p(x)=p_t\) for an individual of type \(t\).
An admissible rooming \(\pi\) partitions this space into three-element sets, with Borel-measurable maps enumerating each individual's two roommates. Rooms contain distinct individuals, but repeated types and coincident locations are allowed.
Roomings \(\pi,\pi'\) are distinct modulo null sets exactly when \(\lambda\{x:\pi(x)\ne\pi'(x)\}>0\); individual identities, including roommate identities, are retained in this comparison.
An individual's cost is \(c_\pi(x)=\sum_{y\in\pi(x)\setminus\{x\}}\|p(x)-p(y)\|_2\). Lower cost is preferred; equal costs give indifference, with no tie-breaking.
A rooming \(\pi\) is strictly popular if every admissible \(\pi'\) distinct modulo null sets satisfies \(\lambda\{x:c_\pi(x)<c_{\pi'}(x)\}>\lambda\{x:c_\pi(x)>c_{\pi'}(x)\}\).

Is deciding whether a strictly popular rooming exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, with \(m=3\) and room size fixed to three?

For finite societies, existence of a strictly popular partition into triples under these distance-sum preferences is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hard; the [2025 published result](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-2845-2_13) confirms this bound.

Conclusion

The problem is polynomial-time solvable: every valid instance has answer NO. No admissible rooming is strictly popular.

This is not a special case involving small support or coincident locations. For every admissible rooming, we construct another admissible rooming that differs on positive measure and gives every individual exactly the same cost. The proof applies to arbitrary finite support and arbitrary locations.

1. A positive-measure set containing at most one individual per room

Fix an admissible rooming \(\pi\), and let \(r_1,r_2:\Omega_\mu\to\Omega_\mu\) be its Borel roommate maps.

Choose an ordering of the finite set \(T\), and order \(\Omega_\mu\) lexicographically by type and interval coordinate. This is a Borel strict total order. Define an individual’s rank within their room by

\[ \operatorname{rank}_\pi(x) = 1+\mathbf 1_{\{r_1(x)<x\}}+\mathbf 1_{\{r_2(x)<x\}}. \]

Each room has exactly one individual of each rank in \(\{1,2,3\}\), because its three individuals are distinct. The rank function is Borel.

For each \(u\in T\), define

\[ n^\pi_u(x)=|\pi(x)\cap(\{u\}\times[0,\mu_u))|. \]

The vector \(n^\pi(x)=(n^\pi_u(x))_{u\in T}\) records the room’s type multiplicities. Its coordinates are Borel, since they can be computed from \(x,r_1(x),r_2(x)\). It takes only finitely many values: its coordinates belong to \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) and sum to \(3\).

Consequently, the labels

\[ \bigl(\operatorname{type}(x),\operatorname{rank}_\pi(x),n^\pi(x)\bigr) \]

give a finite Borel partition of \(\Omega_\mu\). Since \(\lambda(\Omega_\mu)=1\), some cell \(A\) has positive measure. Fix its common labels \(t_0,j_0,n_0\).

Thus:

  • every member of \(A\) has type \(t_0\);
  • every member has room-type multiplicity vector \(n_0\);
  • every room contains at most one member of \(A\), because all members have rank \(j_0\).

Write \(A=\{t_0\}\times A_0\), where \(A_0\subseteq[0,\mu_{t_0})\) is Borel and has positive Lebesgue measure.

2. An explicit Borel swap within that set

We construct two disjoint, positive-measure subsets of \(A_0\) that are translates of one another.

Let \(\ell\) denote Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb R\), and put \(a=\ell(A_0)>0\). Tonelli’s theorem and the change of variables \((s,d)\mapsto(s,s+d)\) give

\[ \int_0^{\mu_{t_0}}\ell\bigl(A_0\cap(A_0-d)\bigr)\,dd = \ell^{\,2}\{(s,v)\in A_0^2:s<v\} = \frac{a^2}{2}>0. \]

Here \(A_0-d=\{s:s+d\in A_0\}\). The final equality follows because exchanging the two coordinates interchanges the regions \(s<v\) and \(v<s\), while the diagonal has two-dimensional measure zero.

Hence there exists \(d>0\) such that \(D=A_0\cap(A_0-d)\) has positive measure. Partition the real line into the half-open intervals \([kd,(k+1)d)\), with \(k\in\mathbb Z\). At least one of these intervals has positive-measure intersection with \(D\). Fix such an interval and set

\[ B=D\cap[kd,(k+1)d). \]

Then \(B\) and \(B+d\) are Borel, disjoint, contained in \(A_0\), and have the same positive measure.

Define \(h:\Omega_\mu\to\Omega_\mu\) by

\[ h(t,s)= \begin{cases} (t_0,s+d),&t=t_0,\ s\in B,\\ (t_0,s-d),&t=t_0,\ s\in B+d,\\ (t,s),&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

This is a Borel involution: \(h^{-1}=h\). It preserves types and fixes precisely the complement of

\[ S=\{t_0\}\times\bigl(B\cup(B+d)\bigr). \]

In particular, \(S\subseteq A\) and \(\lambda(S)=2\ell(B)>0\).

3. The competing rooming preserves every cost

Transport the partition through \(h\). Explicitly, define

\[ \pi'(x)=h[\pi(h^{-1}(x))]. \]

Because \(h\) is a bijection, the images of the rooms of \(\pi\) form a partition into three distinct individuals. Moreover,

\[ r'_i=h\circ r_i\circ h^{-1},\qquad i\in\{1,2\}, \]

are Borel maps enumerating the new roommates. Thus \(\pi'\) is admissible.

Since \(h\) preserves every individual’s type,

\[ n^{\pi'}(x)=n^\pi(h^{-1}(x)). \]

If \(x\notin S\), then \(h^{-1}(x)=x\). If \(x\in S\), both \(x\) and \(h^{-1}(x)\) belong to \(A\), so both have multiplicity vector \(n_0\). Therefore

\[ n^{\pi'}(x)=n^\pi(x)\qquad\text{for every }x\in\Omega_\mu. \]

For an individual \(x\) of type \(t\), the cost is determined entirely by their type and their room’s type multiplicities:

\[ c_\pi(x) = \sum_{u\in T} \bigl(n^\pi_u(x)-\mathbf 1_{\{u=t\}}\bigr) \|p_t-p_u\|_2. \]

It follows that

\[ c_{\pi'}(x)=c_\pi(x)\qquad\text{for every }x\in\Omega_\mu. \]

4. The competing rooming differs on positive measure

Take \(x\in S\), and put \(z=h(x)\). Then \(z\ne x\), and both belong to \(A\). Since each original room contains at most one member of \(A\), the rooms \(\pi(x)\) and \(\pi(z)\) are distinct and therefore disjoint.

The two roommates of \(z\) lie outside \(A\), hence outside \(S\), so \(h\) fixes them. Consequently,

\[ \pi'(x)=\{x\}\cup\bigl(\pi(z)\setminus\{z\}\bigr)\ne\pi(x). \]

Thus \(\pi'\) differs from \(\pi\) on all of \(S\), a set of positive measure. The disagreement set is Borel: equality of two three-element rooms can be tested by finitely many comparisons of their Borel enumerations. Therefore \(\pi\) and \(\pi'\) are distinct modulo null sets.

Nevertheless, exact equality of costs gives

\[ \lambda\{x:c_\pi(x)<c_{\pi'}(x)\} = \lambda\{x:c_\pi(x)>c_{\pi'}(x)\} = 0. \]

The required strict inequality fails. Since \(\pi\) was arbitrary, no strictly popular rooming exists.

5. Decision complexity and the finite result

An algorithm can return NO on every valid input. Its decision work is constant; even including an input scan, its running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). No algebraic-coordinate computations are needed.

Admissible roomings do exist: for each type \(t\), use the triples

\[ \{(t,s),(t,s+\mu_t/3),(t,s+2\mu_t/3)\}, \qquad 0\le s<\mu_t/3. \]

These give Borel roomings with zero costs. The obstruction proved above concerns strict popularity under the specified comparison of individual identities.

The cited [2025 finite-society result](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-2845-2_13) establishes \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness in its finite setting. Here, the positive-measure supply of individuals with identical type and room-type composition permits the cost-preserving swap proved above.
