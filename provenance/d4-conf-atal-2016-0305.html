The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given hospitals with qualities \(q_j\), per-patient costs \(c_j\), a per-capita budget \(b\), and a society described by \(\tau\) patient-value types \(v_t\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), compute a budget-feasible stable assignment maximizing average social welfare. The decision variables are masses \(x_{tj}\ge 0\) assigned from each type to each hospital and common waiting times \(w_j\ge 0\); require \(\sum_j x_{tj}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,j}c_jx_{tj}\le b\), and every hospital receiving positive mass from type \(t\) maximizes that type's nonnegative utility \(v_tq_j-w_j\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge 2\) hospitals with positive binary-rational qualities \(q_j\) and per-unit-mass costs \(c_j\), and a binary-rational per-capita budget \(b\) satisfying \(\min_j c_j\le b<\max_j c_j\).
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge 1\) distinct positive binary-rational patient values \(v_t\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
An assignment consists of real masses \(x_{tj}\ge 0\) and common real waiting times \(w_j\ge 0\), with \(\sum_jx_{tj}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,j}c_jx_{tj}\le b\). Every patient must be served; hospitals have no additional capacity constraints.
Stability requires that whenever \(x_{tj}>0\), \(v_tq_j-w_j\ge 0\) and \(v_tq_j-w_j\ge v_tq_k-w_k\) for every hospital \(k\), including hospitals receiving no mass. Mass may split arbitrarily among tied maximizers.
Average social welfare is \(W(x,w)=\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}(v_tq_j-w_j)\). Given a binary-rational threshold \(K\), the decision problem asks whether a feasible stable assignment has \(W(x,w)\ge K\).

Is this continuous stable-assignment decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete indivisible patients with proportional valuations, exact optimal stable assignment is NP-hard, its welfare-threshold decision version is NP-complete, and an FPTAS exists. [Source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2016/pdfs/p278.pdf)
For continuous populations, a common zero-wait lottery can be optimized by an LP; this uses expected-utility choice of lotteries rather than the stability requirement above. No result settling this exact support-encoded mass problem was located. [Source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2016/pdfs/p278.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total mass to one, so the budget and welfare are per capita.
  • Use positive binary-rational qualities, costs, patient values, and budget, retaining the discrete model's positivity while allowing rational units; the discrete hardness construction itself uses rational patient values.
  • Carry over the nontrivial feasible-budget restriction as minimum hospital cost at most b and b strictly below maximum hospital cost; this also implies at least two hospitals.
  • Define welfare as average utility after subtracting waiting time, rather than average gross treatment value.
  • Allow arbitrary splitting only among utility-maximizing hospitals tied for a type, and compare utility against every hospital, including unused ones.
  • Introduce a binary-rational welfare threshold K to state the exact decision problem, and include it in total input bit length L.

Result

The continuous stable-assignment decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even when every hospital satisfies \(c_j=q_j\), all qualities and costs are positive integers, and \(K=b\). The remaining input numbers in the construction are dyadic rationals.

The key is a welfare bound whose equality condition forces each support type to use a single hospital. Thus arbitrary mass splitting does not invalidate the reduction.

A welfare bound that rules out splitting

Order the support values as \(v_1>\cdots>v_n>0\), and write
\[ \]
\(M_i=\sum_{k=1}^{i}\mu_k.\)

For this section, suppose the values satisfy

\(M_i(v_i-v_{i+1})=\mu_i\quad\text{for }i<n,\qquad v_n=\mu_n.\)

Consider any stable assignment with \(c_j=q_j\). Define

\(u_i=\max_j(v_iq_j-w_j),\qquad r_i=\min\{q_j:x_{ij}>0\},\qquad \bar q_i=\frac{1}{\mu_i}\sum_jq_jx_{ij}.\)

These quantities are well defined because every type has positive mass and must be served. Stability implies that every hospital serving type \(i\) gives utility \(u_i\). Consequently,

\(W=\sum_i\mu_i u_i,\qquad C=\sum_i\mu_i\bar q_i,\)

where \(C\) denotes total cost.

Choose a hospital of quality \(r_i\) serving type \(i\). Type \(i+1\) can compare its utility with that hospital, so

\(u_{i+1}\ge u_i-(v_i-v_{i+1})r_i.\)

Hence

\(u_i-u_{i+1}\le(v_i-v_{i+1})r_i.\)

For the last type, nonnegative waiting times give

\(u_n\le v_nr_n.\)

Since \(M_n=1\), telescoping yields

\(\displaystyle W=u_n+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}M_i(u_i-u_{i+1})\le v_nr_n+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}M_i(v_i-v_{i+1})r_i=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_i r_i.\)

Also, \(\bar q_i\ge r_i\). Therefore

\(\boxed{W\le\sum_i\mu_i r_i\le C.}\)

If \(W=C\), then

\(\sum_i\mu_i(\bar q_i-r_i)=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative and every \(\mu_i\) is positive. Thus \(\bar q_i=r_i\) for every type. A weighted average of served qualities equals their minimum only when every positively served quality equals that minimum.

Equality condition. If all hospital qualities are distinct and \(W=C\), every support type sends all its mass to one hospital.

Polynomial reduction from subset sum

Use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem \(\mathrm{SUBSET\ SUM}\): given positive integers \(s_1,\ldots,s_n\) and a target \(T\), decide whether some subset sums to \(T\). This is also the source problem in [Chan and Chen’s discrete reduction](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2016/pdfs/p278.pdf).

We may restrict to \(n\ge2\) and \(0<T<S\), where \(S=\sum_i s_i\). The excluded cases are directly decidable and can be mapped to fixed restricted yes or no instances.

Values and masses

Set

\(D=2^{n-1},\qquad a_1=1,\qquad a_i=2^{i-2}\text{ for }2\le i\le n,\qquad \mu_i=\frac{a_i}{D}.\)

Then

\(\sum_i a_i=D,\qquad M_i=\frac{2^{i-1}}{D}.\)

Choose the distinct positive values

\(v_1=\frac{n+1}{2},\qquad v_i=\frac{n-i+1}{2}\text{ for }2\le i\le n.\)

Their successive differences satisfy

\(v_1-v_2=1,\qquad v_i-v_{i+1}=\frac12\text{ for }2\le i<n.\)

It follows that

\(M_i(v_i-v_{i+1})=\mu_i\quad\text{for every }i<n,\qquad v_n=\frac12=\mu_n.\)

Thus the preceding welfare bound applies. All values and masses are dyadic rationals.

Hospitals and threshold

Choose a power of two \(R\) satisfying

\(R>D^2S.\)

For each \(i\), introduce two hospitals, indexed by \((i,0)\) and \((i,1)\), and put

\(h_i=\frac{D}{a_i}s_i,\)

\(q_{i,0}=c_{i,0}=R^{n-i+1},\qquad q_{i,1}=c_{i,1}=R^{n-i+1}+h_i.\)

Every \(h_i\) is a positive integer because \(a_i\) divides \(D\). Moreover,

\(h_i\le DS<R.\)

For \(i<n\),

\(q_{i+1,1}<R^{n-i}+R\le R^{n-i+1}=q_{i,0}.\)

Therefore all qualities are distinct, with ordering

\(q_{1,1}>q_{1,0}>q_{2,1}>q_{2,0}>\cdots>q_{n,1}>q_{n,0}.\)

Set the per-capita budget and welfare threshold to

\(\displaystyle b=K=\frac1D\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_iR^{n-i+1}+T.\)

This satisfies the required budget hypothesis. The minimum hospital cost is \(R\), and the baseline weighted average is strictly larger than \(R\), so \(b>R\).

For the upper bound, \(\mu_1=1/D\le1/2\), while every other baseline quality is at most \(R^{n-1}\). Hence

\(\displaystyle b\le\frac{R^n+R^{n-1}}2+T<R^n<q_{1,1}.\)

The strict middle inequality follows from

\(\displaystyle T<S<\frac{R(R-1)}2\le\frac{R^n-R^{n-1}}2,\)

using \(R>D^2S\ge4S\) and \(n\ge2\).

Thus \(\min_jc_j<b<\max_jc_j\).

The construction has polynomial bit length. We can choose \(R\) with

\(\log_2R=O(n+\log S).\)

The largest quality has bit length \(O(n(n+\log S))\); there are only \(2n\) hospitals and \(n\) support types. Every remaining input number also has polynomial bit length.

Correctness of the reduction

A subset-sum solution gives a stable assignment

Suppose \(\varepsilon_i\in\{0,1\}\) satisfy

\(\sum_i\varepsilon_i s_i=T.\)

Assign all mass of type \(i\) to hospital \((i,\varepsilon_i)\), whose quality we denote by \(Q_i\). The ordering above gives \(Q_1>\cdots>Q_n\).

Set waiting times at these selected hospitals recursively:

\(w_n=0,\qquad w_i=w_{i+1}+v_{i+1}(Q_i-Q_{i+1})\quad\text{for }i<n.\)

These waiting times are nonnegative. To check stability, take \(i<k\). Then

\(\displaystyle w_i-w_k=\sum_{\ell=i}^{k-1}v_{\ell+1}(Q_\ell-Q_{\ell+1}).\)

Type \(i\)'s utility advantage over hospital \(k\) is

\(\displaystyle (v_iQ_i-w_i)-(v_iQ_k-w_k)=\sum_{\ell=i}^{k-1}(v_i-v_{\ell+1})(Q_\ell-Q_{\ell+1})\ge0.\)

Type \(k\)'s utility advantage over hospital \(i\) is

\(\displaystyle (v_kQ_k-w_k)-(v_kQ_i-w_i)=\sum_{\ell=i}^{k-1}(v_{\ell+1}-v_k)(Q_\ell-Q_{\ell+1})\ge0.\)

These comparisons cover every pair of selected hospitals. Furthermore,

\(\displaystyle w_i\le v_i\sum_{\ell=i}^{n-1}(Q_\ell-Q_{\ell+1})=v_i(Q_i-Q_n),\)

so each assigned utility is at least \(v_iQ_n>0\).

Set every unselected hospital’s waiting time to \(v_1q_{\max}\). Its utility for every support type is then nonpositive, so it cannot improve upon the assigned utility. The assignment is stable against all hospitals.

Its cost is

\(\displaystyle C=\sum_i\mu_iQ_i=\frac1D\sum_i a_iR^{n-i+1}+\frac1D\sum_i a_i\varepsilon_i h_i=\frac1D\sum_i a_iR^{n-i+1}+\sum_i\varepsilon_i s_i=b.\)

Writing \(u_i=v_iQ_i-w_i\), the waiting-time recursion gives

\(u_i-u_{i+1}=(v_i-v_{i+1})Q_i,\qquad u_n=v_nQ_n.\)

Therefore

\(\displaystyle W=u_n+\sum_{i<n}M_i(u_i-u_{i+1})=\sum_i\mu_iQ_i=C=b=K.\)

The constructed decision instance is a yes instance.

A threshold-achieving assignment gives a subset-sum solution

Conversely, suppose a feasible stable assignment satisfies \(W\ge K=b\). The welfare bound gives

\(b\le W\le\sum_i\mu_i r_i\le C\le b.\)

Every inequality is therefore an equality. Since all qualities are distinct, the equality condition forces each type to use exactly one hospital.

Let type \(k\) choose hospital \((j(k),\varepsilon_k)\), where \(\varepsilon_k\in\{0,1\}\). Since \(C=b\), multiplying the cost equality by \(D\) gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{n}a_kR^{n-j(k)+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_k\varepsilon_k h_{j(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_jR^{n-j+1}+DT.\)

Define

\(\displaystyle N_j=\sum_{\{k:j(k)=j\}}a_k,\qquad E=\sum_k a_k\varepsilon_k h_{j(k)}.\)

These coefficients obey

\(0\le N_j\le D<R,\qquad 0\le E\le D^2S<R,\qquad 0<DT<R.\)

Consequently, both sides of

\(\displaystyle \sum_jN_jR^{n-j+1}+E=\sum_j a_jR^{n-j+1}+DT\)

are valid base-\(R\) expansions with no carries between the displayed positions. Uniqueness of base-\(R\) representation implies

\(N_j=a_j\text{ for every }j,\qquad E=DT.\)

Since every \(a_j\) is positive, every hospital pair receives at least one type. There are \(n\) types and \(n\) pairs, so every pair receives exactly one type.

We also need to identify which type uses which pair. For any \(i<k\), stability of their chosen hospitals implies

\(v_iQ_i-w_i\ge v_iQ_k-w_k,\qquad v_kQ_k-w_k\ge v_kQ_i-w_i.\)

Adding yields

\((v_i-v_k)(Q_i-Q_k)\ge0.\)

Since \(v_i>v_k\), we have \(Q_i\ge Q_k\). Hospital pairs are strictly ordered by quality, and each pair is used exactly once. Thus the pair indices must occur in increasing order:

\(j(k)=k\quad\text{for every }k.\)

Finally,

\(\displaystyle DT=E=\sum_i a_i\varepsilon_i h_i=D\sum_i\varepsilon_i s_i.\)

Dividing by \(D\) gives

\(\sum_i\varepsilon_i s_i=T.\)

This is a subset-sum solution, completing the polynomial reduction and proving \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

The real-valued variables do not prevent polynomial-size certificates.

Let \(M=v_{\max}q_{\max}\). Any feasible stable assignment can be assumed to satisfy

\(0\le w_j\le M,\qquad 0\le u_t\le M,\qquad 0\le x_{tj}\le1.\)

Indeed, a used hospital has \(w_j\le v_tq_j\le M\) for some served type, by nonnegative utility. Set every unused hospital’s wait to \(M\); its utility for every type becomes nonpositive, preserving stability.

Guess a set \(F\) of allowed type–hospital pairs. Introduce variables \(x_{tj},w_j,u_t\), and impose the bounded linear system

\(x_{tj}=0\text{ for }(t,j)\notin F,\qquad x_{tj}\ge0,\qquad \sum_jx_{tj}=\mu_t,\)

\(\sum_{t,j}c_jx_{tj}\le b,\qquad 0\le w_j\le M,\qquad 0\le u_t\le M,\)

\(u_t\ge v_tq_j-w_j\text{ for all }t,j,\)

\(u_t=v_tq_j-w_j\text{ for }(t,j)\in F,\qquad \sum_t\mu_tu_t\ge K.\)

A real stable witness makes this system feasible when \(F\) is its positive-mass support. Conversely, any feasible point of the system defines a stable assignment: positive mass occurs only on pairs in \(F\), where utility equals the common maximum \(u_t\). Its welfare equals \(\sum_t\mu_tu_t\).

This is a bounded rational polyhedron with polynomially many variables and constraints and polynomial-size coefficients. If nonempty, it has a vertex with polynomial-size rational coordinates. Explicitly, a vertex is determined by linearly independent active constraints; after clearing denominators, Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound give polynomial bit length for its coordinates.

Thus every yes instance has a polynomial-size rational certificate, whose feasibility, stability, and welfare can be checked by exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time. The decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Combining membership with the reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness. A general algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
