The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a fixed finite set of network roles \(T\), a fixed signal accuracy \(q\), a role-incidence rule generating graphs \(G_N\), and rational role distributions \(\mu^{(N)}\) with \(N\mu_t^{(N)}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\), determine whether there is an ordering family \(\sigma_N\) for which Bayesian network learning quality \(L_{\sigma_N}(G_N)\to 1\), and construct such a family when it exists. In particular, characterize the hub-and-seed regime with a hub \(v_N\), an independent neighbor set \(S_N\), \(|S_N|\to\infty\), \(|S_N|/N\to0\), and a component containing all but \(o(N)\) agents after removing \(S_N\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
A family has a finite role set \(T\), with \(m=|T|\), and a symmetric matrix \(A\in\{0,1\}^{T\times T}\). Distinct agents of roles \(t,u\) are adjacent exactly when \(A_{tu}=1\); diagonal entries specify whether a role induces a clique or an independent set.
Specify population growth by densely encoded polynomials \(p_t(k)\) with nonnegative integer coefficients, allowing zero polynomials, with \(N(k)=\sum_t p_t(k)\to\infty\). At integer scale \(k\geq1\), role \(t\) has \(p_t(k)\) agents and mass \(\mu_t^{(k)}=p_t(k)/N(k)\); write the resulting simple undirected graph as \(G_k\). Roles and incidence remain fixed as \(k\) grows, but vary between inputs.
Fix a rational \(q\in(1/2,1)\). The state \(\theta\) is uniform on \(\{0,1\}\); conditional on it, private binary signals are independent and satisfy \(\Pr(s_v=\theta\mid\theta)=q\). An ordering is a permutation chosen before signals are realized. Each agent knows the graph, ordering, and signal model, observes its own signal and earlier neighbors' actions, and announces a posterior-maximizing binary action, breaking ties with an independent fair coin.
Define \(L_{\sigma_k}(G_k)=N(k)^{-1}\sum_v\Pr(a_v=\theta)\). The hub-and-seed regime means there exist \(v_k\) and an independent set \(S_k\subseteq N_{G_k}(v_k)\) with \(|S_k|\to\infty\), \(|S_k|/N(k)\to0\), and the component of \(v_k\) in \(G_k-S_k\) omitting only \(o(N(k))\) remaining agents; this is a subclass, not an input promise.
Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the family description and \(d=\max_t\deg p_t\). State decision running times in \(m,\tau,L,d\), and ordering-construction running times additionally in \(N(k)\) and the bit length of \(k\).

Is deciding whether there exists an ordering family \((\sigma_k)\) with \(L_{\sigma_k}(G_k)\to1\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? When such a family exists, can it be constructed within the stated running-time parameters?

Result: the decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\)

There is a complete structural criterion, and every accepted family admits a successful ordering constructible in time polynomial in the description parameters, the bit length of \(k\), and \(N(k)\).

Let
\[ \]
\(U=\{t\in T:p_t\not\equiv0\}\), and let \(R\) be the undirected graph on \(U\) whose edges are the pairs of distinct roles \(t,u\) with \(A_{tu}=1\). Thus diagonal entries do not create edges in \(R\).

Since the coefficients are nonnegative, every role in \(U\) is populated at every integer scale \(k\geq1\). Moreover, \(N(k)\to\infty\) implies \(d\geq1\).

Call a component \(C\) of \(R\) dominant if it contains a role \(t\) with \(\deg p_t=d\).

The criterion is: a successful ordering family exists if and only if every dominant component \(C\) satisfies both conditions:

  • \(|C|\geq2\).
  • Some role \(t\in C\) satisfies \(A_{tt}=0\) and \(\deg p_t\geq1\).

In words, every role component carrying positive limiting population mass must contain a growing independent role connected to another role.

The substantial converse is an information bound for clique roles, proved next.

1. A uniform obstruction for clique roles

Consider any finite graph of the prescribed role form. Suppose it has \(h\) populated clique roles, and its independent roles together contain at most \(b\) agents.

We prove that, for every ordering and every agent \(v\),
\(\Pr(a_v\neq\theta)\geq\delta(q,h,b)>0\),
where the bound is independent of the clique populations.

Throughout this proof, logarithms are natural. Define
\(c_q=\frac12q(1-q)(2q-1)^2>0\)
and
\(D_q=(2q-1)\log\frac{q}{1-q}>0\).

A bound that will suffice is
\(\delta(q,h,b)=\frac14\exp\!\left[-D_q\left(\frac{2h\log2}{c_q}+b\right)\right]\).

1.1. When an action can reveal a fresh signal

Number agents in their decision order. Let \(F_i\) be the vector of earlier neighbors’ actions observed by agent \(i\), and put
\(r_i=\Pr(\theta=1\mid F_i)\).

Conditional on \(\theta\), the current private signal is independent of all earlier actions: those actions depend only on earlier private signals and independent tie coins.

Bayes’ rule therefore gives the following policy at any observation history with social posterior \(r\):

  • If \(r<1-q\), the action is always \(0\).
  • If \(r>q\), the action is always \(1\).
  • If \(1-q<r<q\), the action equals the private signal.
  • At \(r=1-q\), signal \(0\) gives action \(0\), while signal \(1\) produces a fair tie.
  • At \(r=q\), signal \(1\) gives action \(1\), while signal \(0\) produces a fair tie.

Define \(Z_i=\mathbf1\{1-q\leq r_i\leq q\}\). Thus \(Z_i=1\) precisely at histories where the action’s distribution depends on the fresh signal.

We claim
\(I(\theta;a_i\mid F_i)\geq c_q\,\mathbb E Z_i\).

To verify this, fix a history with \(r\in[1-q,q]\). Write
\(\alpha_s=\Pr(a_i=1\mid F_i,s_i=s)\).
The policy above implies
\(\alpha_1-\alpha_0\geq\frac12\).

If \(b_j=\Pr(a_i=1\mid F_i,\theta=j)\), conditional independence gives
\(b_1-b_0=(2q-1)(\alpha_1-\alpha_0)\).

Put \(\bar b=rb_1+(1-r)b_0\). The conditional mutual information at this history is
\(rD(\operatorname{Ber}(b_1)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(\bar b))+(1-r)D(\operatorname{Ber}(b_0)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(\bar b))\).

The elementary binary relative-entropy inequality
\(D(\operatorname{Ber}(x)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(y))\geq2(x-y)^2\)
then gives the lower bound
\(2r(1-r)(b_1-b_0)^2\geq\frac12q(1-q)(2q-1)^2=c_q\).

For completeness, the binary inequality follows by differentiating in \(x\): the difference between its two sides has value and derivative zero at \(x=y\), and second derivative
\(\frac1{x(1-x)}-4\geq0\).
Boundary cases follow by continuity. Averaging over histories proves the claim.

1.2. Each clique role has a bounded information budget

Fix a clique role \(t\). Consider the chronological action stream consisting of all agents of role \(t\) and all agents of roles adjacent to \(t\).

Immediately before an agent of role \(t\) acts, the preceding part of this stream is exactly its observed action vector \(F_i\). This uses both defining properties of a role:

  • All agents of role \(t\) have the same neighbors outside that role.
  • Since \(A_{tt}=1\), each agent sees every earlier agent of its own role.

By the mutual-information chain rule, the sum of the contributions from agents of role \(t\) is at most the information in the entire stream. All other chain-rule contributions are nonnegative. Consequently,
\(\sum_{i:\,t_i=t}I(\theta;a_i\mid F_i)\leq H(\theta)=\log2\).

Combining this with the preceding bound yields
\(\mathbb E\sum_{i:\,t_i=t}Z_i\leq\frac{\log2}{c_q}\).

The expectation here uses the uniform mixture of the two states. Hence, under \(\theta=1\),
\(\mathbb E_1\sum_{i:\,t_i=t}Z_i\leq\frac{2\log2}{c_q}\).

Summing over clique roles and adding the at most \(b\) independent-role agents gives
\(\mathbb E_1\sum_i Z_i\leq\frac{2h\log2}{c_q}+b\).

1.3. The entire transcript has bounded relative entropy

Let \(P_1,P_0\) denote the laws of the complete action transcript under the respective states. They have common support: every private-signal assignment and every relevant tie-coin assignment has positive probability under both states.

Fix a complete earlier action history. The current private signal remains independent of that history conditional on the state. The current action is obtained from that signal through a channel determined by its observed subhistory and the tie rule.

If \(Z_i=0\), this channel produces the same deterministic action under both states, contributing zero relative entropy. If \(Z_i=1\), the log-sum inequality, equivalently relative-entropy contraction under a common channel, bounds its contribution by
\(D(\operatorname{Ber}(q)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(1-q))=D_q\).

The relative-entropy chain rule therefore gives
\(D(P_1\Vert P_0)\leq D_q\,\mathbb E_1\sum_iZ_i\leq K\),
where
\(K=D_q\left(\frac{2h\log2}{c_q}+b\right)\).

1.4. Bounded relative entropy forces a positive error

Let \(\rho=\sum_x\sqrt{P_1(x)P_0(x)}\). Jensen’s inequality gives
\(\rho=\mathbb E_1\exp\!\left[-\frac12\log\frac{P_1(X)}{P_0(X)}\right]\geq e^{-K/2}\).

Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\(\operatorname{TV}(P_1,P_0)\leq\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\).

Under the uniform prior, the minimum error attainable from the full transcript is therefore at least
\(\frac{1-\operatorname{TV}(P_1,P_0)}2\geq\frac{\rho^2}{4}\geq\frac{e^{-K}}4\).

Every individual action is an estimator available from that transcript. Its error cannot be smaller than the optimal transcript-based error. This proves the claimed uniform obstruction.

2. Necessity of the criterion

For each role, let \(c_{t,d}\) be its coefficient of \(k^d\), possibly zero. For a component \(C\) of \(R\), write
\(n_C(k)=\sum_{t\in C}p_t(k)\).

Nonnegativity of the coefficients gives
\(\frac{n_C(k)}{N(k)}\longrightarrow w_C=\frac{\sum_{t\in C}c_{t,d}}{\sum_{t\in U}c_{t,d}}\).

Thus \(w_C>0\) exactly when \(C\) is dominant.

There are no edges between different role components. Restricting any ordering to one role component leaves its learning process unchanged: agents there observe only actions from that component, and the externally specified ordering carries no state information.

Suppose a dominant component fails the criterion.

First case: it is a singleton independent role. Its agents have no neighbors, so every agent acts on its private signal and has accuracy exactly \(q\). Therefore, for every ordering family,
\(\liminf_{k\to\infty}(1-L_{\sigma_k}(G_k))\geq w_C(1-q)>0\).

Second case: it contains no growing independent role. This includes a singleton clique role. Every independent role in this component has a constant population, because its nonzero polynomial has degree zero. Let \(b_C\) be their total, and let \(h_C\) be the number of clique roles.

The obstruction from Section 1 applies uniformly at every scale and under every ordering. Hence
\(1-L_{\sigma_k}(G_k)\geq\frac{n_C(k)}{N(k)}\delta(q,h_C,b_C)\),
and consequently
\(\liminf_{k\to\infty}(1-L_{\sigma_k}(G_k))\geq w_C\delta(q,h_C,b_C)>0\).

These cases exhaust failures of the criterion. Necessity follows.

3. Sufficiency and an explicit ordering

Assume every dominant component passes the criterion. We construct its ordering separately.

Fix such a component \(C\). Choose a role \(t\in C\) with \(A_{tt}=0\) and \(\deg p_t\geq1\). Since \(|C|\geq2\), choose a neighboring role \(u\), and choose one agent \(v\) of role \(u\).

At scale \(k\), put
\(b_C(k)=\min\{p_t(k)-1,\lfloor\sqrt{p_t(k)}\rfloor\}\),
and choose \(b_C(k)\) agents of role \(t\) as the seed set \(S_C(k)\).

Then \(b_C(k)\to\infty\), while at least one agent of role \(t\) remains outside the seed set.

Use this order:

  • All agents in \(S_C(k)\).
  • The hub \(v\).
  • One representative of every other role in \(C\), following a rooted spanning tree of \(R[C]\) with root \(u\), with parents before children.
  • All remaining agents of the component.

For role \(t\), choose its representative outside the seed set.

3.1. The hub learns

The seeds are independent and act before any of their neighbors. Their actions equal their private signals.

The hub consequently observes \(b_C(k)\) independent signals, in addition to its own. Its Bayesian action is their majority, with the specified fair tie rule.

Let \(n=b_C(k)+1\), and let \(X\) count correct signals among these \(n\) signals. Conditional on either state, \(X\) is binomial with mean \(nq\) and variance \(nq(1-q)\). A wrong majority, including a possible wrong tie outcome, requires \(X\leq n/2\). Applying Markov’s inequality to \((X-nq)^2\) gives
\(\Pr(a_v\neq\theta)\leq\varepsilon_C(k)=\min\left\{1,\frac{q(1-q)}{(b_C(k)+1)(q-\frac12)^2}\right\}\to0\).

3.2. Accuracy propagates through the component

If an agent observes an earlier neighbor’s action, copying that action is a feasible decision rule. Posterior maximization therefore implies
\(\Pr(a_x=\theta)\geq\Pr(a_y=\theta)\)
whenever \(y\) is an earlier neighbor of \(x\). This argument requires no independence between the observed actions.

Every nonroot representative observes its parent representative. Induction along the spanning tree shows that every representative has error at most \(\varepsilon_C(k)\).

Every role in the nontrivial connected graph \(R[C]\) has a neighboring role. Once all representatives have acted, every remaining agent observes a representative of such a neighboring role. It too has error at most \(\varepsilon_C(k)\).

This is a componentwise form of the seed aggregation and propagation construction in [Lu et al., Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04317v1).

3.3. The whole population learns

Concatenate these orders over dominant components, and append all nondominant components in any order. Let \(n_{\mathrm{ign}}(k)\) be the total population of the latter.

The construction gives
\(1-L_{\sigma_k}(G_k)\leq\frac{n_{\mathrm{ign}}(k)}{N(k)}+\sum_{C\text{ dominant}}\left(\frac{b_C(k)}{N(k)}+\frac{n_C(k)}{N(k)}\varepsilon_C(k)\right)\).

Every term tends to zero:

  • Nondominant components have total population of degree strictly less than \(d\), so \(n_{\mathrm{ign}}(k)/N(k)\to0\).
  • Each \(b_C(k)\leq\sqrt{p_t(k)}\leq\sqrt{N(k)}\).
  • Each \(\varepsilon_C(k)\to0\), and there are only finitely many role components.

Therefore \(L_{\sigma_k}(G_k)\to1\), proving sufficiency.

4. Decision and construction running times

The decision algorithm scans the dense polynomials to find their supports and degrees, computes components of \(R\), and checks the criterion. It never enumerates an ambient universe of possible types; only the encoded support is relevant.

A conservative bit-operation bound, displaying the requested parameters, is
\(\widetilde O(L+m^2+m(d+1)+\tau)\),
where the tilde suppresses logarithmic factors. In particular, the decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\).

For an explicit ordering at scale \(k\), label agents by their role and their index within that role. Let \(\ell(k)\) be the bit length of \(k\), and set
\(R_0=L+m^2+m(d+1)+\tau+1\)
and
\(B_k=L+(d+1)\ell(k)+\left\lceil\log_2((m+\tau+1)(d+1)+1)\right\rceil+3\).

This bounds the bit lengths of the evaluated populations, \(N(k)\), and the required indices. Using schoolbook arithmetic:

  • Horner evaluation costs \(O(m(d+1)B_k^2)\).
  • Each seed-size square root can be computed by binary search in \(O(B_k^3)\).
  • Role spanning trees use the encoded role matrix.
  • Writing all \(N(k)\) agent labels costs \(O(N(k)B_k)\).

Thus a valid construction bound is
\(O(R_0B_k^2+mB_k^3+N(k)B_k)\).

This constructs the ordering itself; it requires no computation of agents’ Bayesian posteriors.

5. Scope of the conclusion

The criterion covers families outside the single hub-and-seed regime. For example, two disjoint stars, each with one hub and \(k\) leaves, satisfy it and learn componentwise. They fail the stated global hub condition because every hub’s component omits approximately half the population.

The [finite directed-network threshold hardness result](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12704v1) concerns a different decision problem. Here the uniform error obstruction, together with the fixed role structure across scales, yields the polynomial-time criterion proved above.
