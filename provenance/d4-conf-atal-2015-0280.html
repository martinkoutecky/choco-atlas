The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a society μ over complete feature and cost types, with binary-rational masses summing to one, a marked target type of mass α and feature vector \(x^\star\), a budget \(B\), costs \(C\), and a rationally described allowed weight set \(W\), decide whether there is a common cohort update \(Δ ≥ 0\), with \(C · Δ ≤ B\), such that for every \(w ∈ W\), the mass whose score exceeds \(w · (x^\star+Δ)\) is less than a given threshold \(q\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct feature-and-cost types \((x_i,C_i)\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_i\), with \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(m\geq1\) is the feature dimension. Initial features lie in \(\mathbb Q^m\cap[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]^m\), and costs lie in \(\mathbb Q_{>0}^m\).
A marked support type \(t\) has mass \(\alpha=\mu_t\), features \(x^\star=x_t\), and costs \(C=C_t\). The input also gives binary rationals \(B\geq0\) and \(q\in[0,1]\), and a nonempty allowed weight set \(W=\{w\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m:\sum_jw_j=1,\ Aw\leq b\}\), with \(k\) rational linear inequalities.
A common update \(\Delta\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m\) changes the entire marked cohort to \(x^\star+\Delta\); all other types remain fixed. Feasibility means \(C\cdot\Delta\leq B\), with budget interpreted per cohort member and no upper bound on updated features. Scores are \(w\cdot x\); equal scores tie and do not count as exceeding the target.
Decide whether some feasible \(\Delta\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\ne t:\,w\cdot x_i>w\cdot(x^\star+\Delta)}\mu_i<q\) for every \(w\in W\). State running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length.

Is this continuous cohort-update decision problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

The discrete minimax-rank threshold decision problem is stated to be \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete ([source](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/~magdon/ps/conference/RankingsAAMAS2015.pdf)). No result settling exactly the mass-based cohort-update problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Use m for feature dimension, since the support representation replaces the finite number of agents.
  • Encode features, costs, budget, threshold, and weight constraints as binary rationals; retain the initial feature interval and strictly positive costs from the discrete model.
  • Interpret the rationally described weight set as a nonempty polytope specified by weak linear inequalities within the nonnegative normalized weight simplex.
  • Apply the common update to the entire marked support type, leaving all other types unchanged.
  • Interpret B and C as a per-member budget and cost vector, so feasibility is C·Δ≤B without multiplication by α.
  • Allow real nonnegative updates without an upper feature cap, following the discrete spending-plan definition; retain B=0 as an allowed case.
  • Take q in [0,1] as a mass threshold and retain strict exceedance and strict threshold comparison, with ties excluded.

1. Proven classification

The problem is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions, even with a strictly positive budget. It admits an exact algorithm with running time
\((\tau^2+k+m)^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(L)\), so it is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed feature dimension.

Consequently, the requested dichotomy requires a reduction convention:

  • Under polynomial-time Turing reductions, the problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.
  • Under polynomial-time many-one reductions, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard if and only if \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\).
  • It belongs to \(\mathrm P\) if and only if \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The cited paper states the related discrete rank result in Theorem 3. The proof below establishes the mass-based result directly, including positive budgets. [Computing Manipulations of Ranking Systems](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/~magdon/ps/conference/RankingsAAMAS2015.pdf)

2. The strict mass condition defines closed halfspaces

Let \(I=\{i:i\ne t\}\) and \(\beta=\sum_{i\in I}\mu_i=1-\alpha\).

Two boundary cases are immediate:

  • If \(q=0\), the answer is NO, since exceeding mass is nonnegative and \(W\) is nonempty.
  • If \(q>\beta\), the answer is YES: the feasible update \(\Delta=0\) suffices.

Henceforth assume \(0<q\leq\beta\). In particular, \(I\) is nonempty.

For \(w\in W\), define
\[ \]
\(h(w)=\max_{\substack{S\subseteq I\\ \sum_{i\in S}\mu_i\geq q}}\ \min_{i\in S}w\cdot x_i.\)

The maximum exists because its indexing family is finite and nonempty.

Lemma 1. For every \(y\in\mathbb R^m\) and \(w\in W\),
\[ \]
\(\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\w\cdot x_i>w\cdot y}}\mu_i<q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w\cdot y\geq h(w).\)

Proof. Suppose first that \(w\cdot y<h(w)\). A maximizing subset \(S\) has mass at least \(q\), and every member satisfies
\(w\cdot x_i\geq h(w)>w\cdot y\).
Thus the exceeding mass is at least \(q\).

Conversely, if the exceeding mass is at least \(q\), take
\(S=\{i\in I:w\cdot x_i>w\cdot y\}\).
This is a nonempty finite set, so
\(\min_{i\in S}w\cdot x_i>w\cdot y\).
It follows that \(h(w)>w\cdot y\). These two implications prove the equivalence. \(\square\)

In particular, ties are handled exactly as required. Successful updates are precisely the points of
\[ \]
\(P_0\cap\bigcap_{w\in W}\{\Delta:w\cdot\Delta\geq h(w)-w\cdot x^\star\},\)
where
\[ \]
\(P_0=\{\Delta\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m:C\cdot\Delta\leq B\}.\)

Thus the set of successful updates is convex. The strict mass threshold produces a nonstrict score inequality, rather than an open halfspace.

3. A finite linear program and its running time

Write \(r=|I|=\tau-1\). Consider the following hyperplanes in weight space:

  • The \(k\) boundary hyperplanes of \(Aw\leq b\).
  • The \(m\) coordinate hyperplanes \(w_j=0\).
  • The \(\binom r2\) score-equality hyperplanes \(w\cdot(x_i-x_\ell)=0\), for distinct \(i,\ell\in I\).

Their number is at most
\(H=k+m+\binom r2\).
Identically zero equations can be ignored.

For each choice of \(m-1\) of these hyperplanes, combine their equations with \(\sum_jw_j=1\). If this system has a unique solution and that solution belongs to \(W\), retain it. Let \(V\) be the resulting finite set. Then
\[ \]
\(|V|\leq\binom H{m-1}.\)

For \(m=1\), the empty choice gives the unique simplex weight.

Lemma 2. An update satisfies the mass condition for every \(w\in W\) if and only if it satisfies
\[ \]
\(v\cdot\Delta\geq h(v)-v\cdot x^\star\qquad\text{for every }v\in V.\)

Proof. Necessity follows from \(V\subseteq W\).

For sufficiency, let \(\sigma\) be a permutation of \(I\), and consider the possibly empty polytope
\[ \]
\(P_\sigma=\{w\in W:w\cdot x_{\sigma(1)}\geq\cdots\geq w\cdot x_{\sigma(r)}\}.\)

These polytopes cover \(W\), including all score ties. Let \(\ell_\sigma\) be the smallest index satisfying
\[ \]
\(\sum_{a=1}^{\ell_\sigma}\mu_{\sigma(a)}\geq q.\)

For every \(w\in P_\sigma\),
\[ \]
\(h(w)=w\cdot x_{\sigma(\ell_\sigma)}.\)

Indeed, the first \(\ell_\sigma\) types form a subset of sufficient mass whose minimum score is the displayed value. Conversely, the total mass of types with strictly larger score is at most
\(\sum_{a=1}^{\ell_\sigma-1}\mu_{\sigma(a)}<q\).
Therefore every subset of sufficient mass contains a type with score no larger than that value.

Every vertex of a nonempty \(P_\sigma\) belongs to \(V\). To verify this even when \(P_\sigma\) has lower dimension, suppose that the simplex equality and the active boundary equations at a vertex do not have rank \(m\). There is then a nonzero direction preserving all these equations. Moving a sufficiently small distance in either direction also preserves the inactive inequalities, contradicting extremality. Consequently, \(m-1\) active equations can be chosen together with the simplex equality to determine the vertex uniquely. All these equations occur in the enumerated list.

On \(P_\sigma\), the required inequality becomes
\[ \]
\(w\cdot(x^\star+\Delta-x_{\sigma(\ell_\sigma)})\geq0.\)

This is linear in \(w\). Every nonempty \(P_\sigma\) is a bounded polytope, hence the convex hull of its vertices. If the inequality holds at those vertices, it holds throughout \(P_\sigma\). Since the polytopes cover \(W\), the result follows. \(\square\)

The decision problem therefore reduces exactly to the rational linear program
\[ \]
\(\Delta\geq0,\qquad C\cdot\Delta\leq B,\qquad v\cdot\Delta\geq h(v)-v\cdot x^\star\quad(v\in V).\)

Each retained weight has encoding length polynomial in \(m,L\): it solves a full-rank rational linear system of dimension \(m\), so the claim follows from determinant bounds and Cramer’s rule.

For each \(v\), compute \(h(v)\) by sorting rival scores in nonincreasing order and finding the first cumulative mass reaching \(q\). Exact rational arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity here.

Enumerating the candidate systems and solving the final rational linear program takes
\[ \]
\(\left(\binom H{m-1}+1\right)^{O(1)} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L) \ \subseteq\ (\tau^2+k+m)^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(L).\)

This proves polynomial-time solvability for every fixed \(m\).

4. Membership in \(\mathrm{coNP}\)

We now show that a NO instance has a polynomial-size certificate, without enumerating all of \(V\).

For \(v\in V\), write
\[ \]
\(K_v=\{\Delta:v\cdot\Delta\geq h(v)-v\cdot x^\star\}.\)

By Lemma 2, a NO instance satisfies
\(P_0\cap\bigcap_{v\in V}K_v=\varnothing\).

We apply Helly’s theorem: a finite family of convex subsets of \(\mathbb R^m\) with empty intersection has a subfamily of at most \(m+1\) sets with empty intersection. Its hypotheses hold because \(P_0\) and every \(K_v\) are convex and the family is finite.

Moreover, the halfspaces \(K_v\) alone have a common point. Let \(\mathbf1\) denote the vector with every coordinate equal to one. Since all initial features lie in the given cube and \(v\) is a simplex weight,
\[ \]
\(h(v)-v\cdot x^\star\leq\tfrac12-(-\tfrac12)=1 =v\cdot\mathbf1.\)

Thus \(\mathbf1\in K_v\) for every \(v\). Any subfamily with empty intersection must consequently include \(P_0\).

It follows that there exist \(s\leq m\) weights \(v^{(1)},\ldots,v^{(s)}\in V\) such that
\[ \]
\(P_0\cap K_{v^{(1)}}\cap\cdots\cap K_{v^{(s)}}=\varnothing.\)

These weights form a polynomial-size NO certificate: each has polynomial encoding length by Section 3. A verifier:

  • Checks that every supplied weight belongs to \(W\).
  • Computes its weighted threshold \(h(v^{(a)})\).
  • Checks infeasibility of the rational linear program consisting of the budget constraints and these at most \(m\) halfspaces.

All three steps take polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\). Soundness follows from Lemma 1 even if the supplied weights are not vertices. Completeness follows from Helly’s theorem and the finite formulation.

The case \(q=0\) is directly recognizable. Therefore the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\).

5. Direct \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness with positive budget

We give a polynomial-time many-one reduction from 3UNSAT, the complement of 3SAT. Use formulas with \(n\geq1\) variables and \(c\geq1\) clauses, each having three literal occurrences. This restricted form remains \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete.

5.1 Linear predicates encoding satisfiability

Introduce \(z\in[0,1]^n\). For every variable \(j\), introduce two predicates:
\[ \]
\(z_j>\tfrac9{10},\qquad z_j<\tfrac1{10}.\)

For every clause, introduce the predicate
\[ \]
\(\ell_1(z)+\ell_2(z)+\ell_3(z)>\tfrac12,\)
where a positive literal has value \(z_j\), and a negative literal has value \(1-z_j\).

There are \(N=2n+c\) predicates. Write them as
\(a_i\cdot z-b_i>0\), for \(1\leq i\leq N\). Their coefficients satisfy
\[ \]
\(|a_{ij}|\leq3,\qquad |b_i|\leq\tfrac52.\)

Lemma 3. There exists \(z\in[0,1]^n\) satisfying at least \(n+c\) predicates if and only if the formula is satisfiable.

Proof. For each variable, at most one of its two predicates can hold. Thus at most \(n\) variable predicates and at most \(c\) clause predicates hold.

Reaching \(n+c\) forces exactly one predicate for every variable and all clause predicates. Assign variable \(j\) true when \(z_j>\tfrac9{10}\), and false when \(z_j<\tfrac1{10}\). If a clause were false under this assignment, each of its three literal values would be less than \(\tfrac1{10}\). Their sum would be less than \(\tfrac3{10}<\tfrac12\), contradicting its predicate. Hence the assignment satisfies the formula.

Conversely, represent any satisfying assignment by \(z_j\in\{0,1\}\). Exactly one variable predicate holds for every variable, and every clause has literal sum at least one. All \(c\) clause predicates therefore hold. \(\square\)

5.2 Embedding the predicates into legal features and weights

Set \(m=n+1\), indexing coordinates by \(0,1,\ldots,n\). Define
\[ \]
\(W=\{w\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{n+1}: \sum_{j=0}^nw_j=1,\ w_j\leq\tfrac1{2n}\text{ for }1\leq j\leq n\}.\)

This uses \(k=n\) additional inequalities and is nonempty. The map
\(z_j=2nw_j\) maps \(W\) onto \([0,1]^n\): for any such \(z\), choose
\[ \]
\(w_j=\tfrac{z_j}{2n}\quad(1\leq j\leq n),\qquad w_0=1-\sum_{j=1}^n\tfrac{z_j}{2n}\geq\tfrac12.\)

Let \(P\) be the least power of two satisfying \(P\geq20(n+1)\), and put \(\rho=1/P\).

The marked type has features \(x^\star=0\) and costs \(C=\mathbf1\). For predicate \(i\), create a rival with features
\[ \]
\((x_i)_0=-\rho b_i,\qquad (x_i)_j=\rho(2na_{ij}-b_i)\quad(1\leq j\leq n).\)

Then, for corresponding \(w,z\),
\[ \]
\(w\cdot x_i=\rho(a_i\cdot z-b_i).\)

Thus rival \(i\) strictly exceeds the unchanged target exactly when its predicate holds.

All initial features satisfy the required bounds, since
\[ \]
\(|(x_i)_j|\leq\rho(6n+\tfrac52) \leq\tfrac{6n+\tfrac52}{20(n+1)}<\tfrac12,\)
and the coordinate-zero bound is smaller.

Give rival \(i\) costs \(C_i=(i+1)\mathbf1\). These costs are positive, and all feature-and-cost types are distinct even if some predicates coincide.

5.3 Masses and a strictly positive budget

Let \(D\) be the least power of two strictly greater than \(N\). Set
\[ \]
\(\mu_i=\tfrac1D\quad(1\leq i\leq N),\qquad \alpha=1-\tfrac ND,\qquad q=\tfrac{n+c}{D},\qquad B=\tfrac{\rho}{32}>0.\)

All masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one. The threshold and budget are also dyadic rationals. The support size is \(\tau=N+1\).

For the zero update, exceeding mass is at least \(q\) exactly when at least \(n+c\) predicates hold.

5.4 Correctness, including all feasible updates

If the formula is unsatisfiable, Lemma 3 shows that every \(w\in W\) has at most \(n+c-1\) rivals exceeding the unchanged target. Consequently,
\[ \]
\(\sum_{\substack{i\ne t\\w\cdot x_i>0}}\mu_i \leq\tfrac{n+c-1}{D}<q.\)

The feasible update \(\Delta=0\) succeeds, so the constructed instance is YES.

If the formula is satisfiable, choose its Boolean vector \(z\) and the corresponding \(w\). Exactly \(n\) variable predicates hold, each with value \(a_i\cdot z-b_i=\tfrac1{10}\). Every clause predicate has value at least \(\tfrac12\). Hence at least \(n+c\) rivals have score at least \(\rho/10\).

For every feasible update, the marked cost vector gives
\(\sum_j\Delta_j\leq B\). Therefore
\[ \]
\(w\cdot(x^\star+\Delta)=w\cdot\Delta \leq\sum_j\Delta_j \leq B=\tfrac{\rho}{32}<\tfrac{\rho}{10}.\)

Those \(n+c\) rivals still strictly exceed the updated target. Their mass is at least \(q\), so this weight defeats every feasible update. The constructed instance is NO.

We have proved
\[ \]
\(\text{formula is unsatisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{constructed cohort-update instance is YES}.\)

All constructed rational numbers have polynomial encoding length, and the construction has polynomial size. This establishes \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness, including for \(B>0\). Together with Section 4, it proves \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-completeness.

6. What this establishes about the requested alternatives

Let \(\mathcal D\) denote this decision language.

Because \(\mathcal D\) is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete,
\[ \]
\(\mathcal D\in\mathrm P\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}.\)

For the forward implication, polynomial-time decidability of \(\mathcal D\) would imply \(\mathrm{coNP}\subseteq\mathrm P\); taking complements yields \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm P\). The converse follows because \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\) also implies \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{coNP}\).

Likewise,
\[ \]
\(\mathcal D\text{ is }\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard under many-one reductions} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}.\)

Indeed, many-one hardness together with \(\mathcal D\in\mathrm{coNP}\) would imply \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{coNP}\), since \(\mathrm{coNP}\) is closed under polynomial-time many-one preimages. Taking complements gives the reverse inclusion. Conversely, equality of the classes makes \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-completeness imply \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

Under Turing reductions, hardness is unconditional: construct the instance above, query the cohort-update oracle once, and negate its answer to decide 3SAT.

Thus the exact \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-classification and the fixed-dimension algorithm are fully proved. Under the conventional many-one interpretation, I do not claim an unconditional resolution of the literal “\(\mathrm P\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard” alternatives.
