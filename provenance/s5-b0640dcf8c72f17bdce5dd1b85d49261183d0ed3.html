The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given resource categories \(G\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and a finite set \(T\) of unary profiles with rational masses \(\mu_t\), compute each profile's continuous MMS benchmark \(h_t(q)\) and maximize \(\alpha\). A type's bundle distribution \(P_t\) may split its mass among individual bundles; every bundle gives value at least \(\alpha h_t(q)\), uses whole units of categories that type regards as indivisible, and may use real amounts of categories it regards as divisible. Aggregate use must satisfy \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\,\mathbb{E}_{P_t}[b_g]\le q_g\) for every \(g\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) contain \(m\geq1\) resource categories with supplies \(q_g\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\). Each unit has value \(1\) to every recipient. A type \(t\) specifies its divisible categories \(D_t\subseteq G\).
A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
An admissible bundle belongs to \(B_t=\{b\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^{G}:b_g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}\text{ for }g\notin D_t\}\), with value \(u_t(b)=\sum_g b_g\). Categories contain interchangeable units; an individual bundle has no supply bound beyond aggregate feasibility.
Define the continuous MMS benchmark by \(h_t(q)=\sup\{z\geq0:\exists P\text{ on }B_t,\ P[u_t(b)\geq z]=1,\ \mathbb{E}_P[b_g]\leq q_g\ \forall g\}\). Thus the benchmark distributes supplies across a unit mass consisting entirely of type \(t\).
For \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), an allocation consists of bundle distributions \(P_t\) satisfying \(P_t[u_t(b)\geq\alpha h_t(q)]=1\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t\mathbb{E}_{P_t}[b_g]\leq q_g\) for every category. Unused resources are allowed; zero benchmarks impose no positive requirement. Algorithmic allocations are represented by finite lists of rational bundles and rational probabilities.

What is the tight universally achievable continuous MMS approximation ratio \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), and can its guarantee, including computation of the benchmarks \(h_t(q)\), be attained in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For finite societies with unary valuations and subjective divisibility, the tight universal MMS guarantee is \(\frac{2}{3}\), attained by a polynomial-time algorithm. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13057v1)
Earlier discrete results establish a \(\frac{1}{2}\) guarantee for general valuations and a tight \(\frac{2}{3}\) guarantee for two or three agents. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00976)
No result settling the precise continuous model defined above was identified in the search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one, so the supplied per-capita quantities are also aggregate supplies.
  • Defined each unary profile solely by its divisibility subset, with additive value one per whole unit and proportional value for divisible amounts.
  • Defined the unspecified continuous MMS benchmark using a unit mass of identical-type recipients, aggregate supplies q, and a bundle-value guarantee holding almost surely.
  • Interpreted categories as interchangeable unit resources, allowing multiple whole units per bundle and nonnegative real amounts for divisible categories; no individual bound b_g <= q_g is imposed.
  • Allowed nonnegative rational supplies, including zero supplies, and retained zero MMS benchmarks without imposing a positivity promise.
  • Restricted approximation factors to [0,1], as in the discrete definition, including when every benchmark is zero.
  • Used almost-sure guarantees for probability distributions and finite rational bundle lists as the computational allocation representation.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The tight universal approximation ratio is \(\boxed{\alpha=1}\). The benchmarks and an exact allocation can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with at most \(2m\) rational bundles per type.

The crucial feature is that feasibility constrains expected consumption. Once each type has a benchmark-attaining distribution with expected consumption at most \(q\), their mass-weighted combination is feasible. We prove benchmark attainment and give explicit constructions below.

1. An explicit benchmark formula

Write
\[ \]
For clarity, all subsequent equations use inline mathematical delimiters. Define \(Q=\sum_{g\in G}q_g\), \(K=\lfloor Q\rfloor\), and \(r=Q-K\), so \(0\leq r<1\). For a fixed type \(t\), put

\(I=G\setminus D_t,\qquad d=\sum_{g\in D_t}q_g,\qquad i=\sum_{g\in I}q_g=Q-d.\)

We claim that

\(\displaystyle \boxed{h_t(q)=K+\min\left\{r,\frac{d}{1-r+d}\right\}.}\)

The denominator is positive because \(1-r+d>0\).

To prove the upper bound, consider any feasible distribution guaranteeing value at least \(z\). For its random bundle \(b\), let

\(N=\sum_{g\in I}b_g,\qquad Y=\sum_{g\in D_t}b_g.\)

Then \(N\) is a nonnegative integer, \(Y\geq0\), and feasibility gives \(\mathbb E[N]\leq i\) and \(\mathbb E[Y]\leq d\). Consequently,

\(z\leq\mathbb E[N+Y]\leq Q.\)

If \(z\leq K\), the claimed upper bound already holds. Otherwise write \(z=K+s\), where \(0<s\leq r<1\). Every admissible bundle of value at least \(z\) satisfies

\(Y\geq s(K+1-N).\)

Indeed, when \(N\leq K\), the value requirement gives \(Y\geq K+s-N\), and

\(K+s-N-s(K+1-N)=(1-s)(K-N)\geq0.\)

When \(N\geq K+1\), the right-hand side is nonpositive, so the inequality follows from \(Y\geq0\).

Taking expectations yields

\(\displaystyle d\geq\mathbb E[Y]\geq s(K+1-\mathbb E[N])\geq s(K+1-i)=s(1-r+d).\)

Thus \(s\leq d/(1-r+d)\), proving the claimed upper bound for every feasible \(z\), and therefore for the supremum defining \(h_t(q)\).

We next attain this bound explicitly.

2. Rational distributions attaining the benchmark

Let \(e_g\) denote the unit vector of category \(g\), and let \(q^D\) be the vector equal to \(q_g\) on \(D_t\) and zero elsewhere. In every case below, the constructed distribution has expectation exactly \(q\).

Case A: \(Q=0\). Use the zero bundle with probability \(1\). The benchmark is \(0\).

Case B: \(Q>0\) and \(r=0\). Here \(Q=K\) is a positive integer. For each category with \(q_g>0\), use bundle \(K e_g\) with probability \(q_g/K\).

These probabilities sum to \(1\), all bundles are admissible and have value \(K\), and their expectation is \(q\). Hence the benchmark is \(K\), as claimed.

Case C: \(r>0\) and \(d\geq r\). In this case \(i\leq K\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \frac{d}{1-r+d}\geq r,\)

because this inequality is equivalent to \((1-r)(d-r)\geq0\). The claimed benchmark is therefore \(Q\).

If \(K=0\), then \(i=0\), and the deterministic bundle \(q\) is admissible and has value \(Q\).

Suppose instead that \(K>0\). For every \(g\in I\) with \(q_g>0\), assign probability \(q_g/K\) to

\(\displaystyle b^{(g)}=K e_g+\frac{r}{d}q^D.\)

Assign the remaining probability \(1-i/K\) to

\(\displaystyle b^{(0)}=\frac{Q}{d}q^D.\)

These probabilities are nonnegative and sum to \(1\). All bundles are admissible. Each \(b^{(g)}\) has value \(K+r=Q\), and \(b^{(0)}\) also has value \(Q\).

For every \(g\in I\), expected consumption is \(q_g\). The expected divisible vector is

\(\displaystyle \left[\frac{i}{K}\frac{r}{d}+ \left(1-\frac{i}{K}\right)\frac{Q}{d}\right]q^D =\frac{Q-i}{d}q^D=q^D, \)

where the first equality uses \(Q-r=K\). Thus the expectation is exactly \(q\), and value \(Q\) is attained.

Case D: \(r>0\) and \(d<r\). Here \(K<i<K+1\). Define

\(\displaystyle p=K+1-i=1-r+d,\qquad s=\frac{d}{p}.\)

Then \(0<p<1\) and \(0\leq s<r\); the latter follows from

\(rp-d=(1-r)(r-d)>0.\)

Thus the claimed benchmark is \(K+s\).

For each \(g\in I\) with \(q_g>0\), use the following two bundles:

  • \(K e_g+q^D/p\), with probability \(p q_g/i\);
  • \((K+1)e_g\), with probability \((1-p)q_g/i\).

The probabilities sum to \(1\), since \(\sum_{g\in I}q_g=i>0\). The first bundle has value \(K+s\), and the second has value \(K+1>K+s\). Both are admissible.

For each \(g\in I\), expected consumption is

\(\displaystyle \frac{q_g}{i}\bigl(pK+(1-p)(K+1)\bigr) =\frac{q_g}{i}(K+1-p)=q_g. \)

The first class of bundles has total probability \(p\), so the expected divisible vector is \(p(q^D/p)=q^D\). Again the expectation is exactly \(q\).

These cases prove the formula and show that its supremum is always attained.

3. Combining types gives an exact allocation

For every supported type \(t\), take its distribution \(P_t\) constructed above. Every bundle in its support satisfies

\(u_t(b)\geq h_t(q).\)

For every category \(g\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\mathbb E_{P_t}[b_g] =\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t q_g =q_g, \)

using \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Hence these distributions constitute a feasible allocation with \(\alpha=1\).

Since the requested ratio belongs to \([0,1]\), this is the tight universal guarantee.

The cited finite-society result establishes a tight \(\frac23\) bound for its model. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13057v1) Here, expected category feasibility and the absence of individual supply bounds permit the constructions above, so that bound does not transfer to the model in the question.

4. Polynomial computation and output size

Compute \(Q,K,r\) once. For each supported type, compute \(d,i\), evaluate the benchmark formula, and output the appropriate distribution.

Each distribution contains at most \(2m\) bundles. All probabilities and bundle coordinates are rational expressions involving input rationals, their sums, and a constant number of arithmetic operations. Their binary encoding lengths are polynomial in \(m,L\). The integer \(K\) is represented in binary; the algorithm never enumerates individual units.

A dense representation therefore uses at most \(2\tau m^2\) bundle coordinates, together with polynomially many probability bits. Exact rational arithmetic computes the complete allocation and all benchmarks in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
