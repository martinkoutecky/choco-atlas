The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a characteristic-zero representable matroid whose elements are partitioned into categories and whose feasible bases are invariant under relabeling elements within each category, let \(\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{T}\) be its feasible integral basis profiles. For identical recipients of total mass \(1\), a rational per-capita supply vector \(q\in\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{B})\), and nonnegative category values \(w\), compute a probability distribution \(\pi\) over \(\mathcal{B}\) with \(\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\pi_b b=q\) that minimizes \(\max_{\pi_b>0}w\cdot b-\min_{\pi_b>0}w\cdot b\). Each recipient receives an integral basis; \(q\) is the average bundle profile.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) be a rank-\(r\) matroid on \(m\) elements, with \(1\le r\le m\), given by a full-row-rank matrix over a number field encoded by an irreducible polynomial over \(\mathbb Q\), with entries in its power basis.
The ground set is partitioned into \(T\) nonempty categories \(E_1,\ldots,E_T\); every permutation within categories preserves the basis family. Define \(\mathcal B=\{(|B\cap E_t|)_{t=1}^{T}:B\text{ is a basis of }M\}\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here \(\tau=1\), with identical recipients of total mass \(1\).
The input includes binary rational vectors \(w\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{T}\) and \(q\in\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal B)\). Each recipient receives an integral basis profile; supply conservation means that the average profile is \(q\), with no disjointness requirement between recipients' template bases.
For a finitely supported rational probability distribution \(\pi\) on \(\mathcal B\), require \(\sum_b\pi_b b=q\), and define \(R(\pi)=\max_{\pi_b>0}w\cdot b-\min_{\pi_b>0}w\cdot b\). The optimization problem is to compute a distribution minimizing \(R(\pi)\).
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including the field encoding and, for the decision version, a threshold \(D\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\).

Is exact minimum-range allocation solvable in time polynomial in \(m,T,r,\tau,L\), or is deciding whether some feasible \(\pi\) satisfies \(R(\pi)\le D\) NP-hard?

For characteristic-zero representable matroids whose ground set partitions into \(k\ge1\) bases, a partition with range at most the largest element weight in its heaviest basis can be found in randomized expected time polynomial in \(m,W\) for integer weights bounded by \(W\), using unit-time field operations. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16021v3)
For arbitrary matroids and rational nonnegative weights, weighted equitability and identical-valuation EF1 partitions admit strongly polynomial algorithms. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13983v1)
These results provide bounded-range partitions, rather than exact minimum-range distributions with a prescribed mean; no result settling this exact continuous problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The characteristic-zero representation had no computational encoding; use an explicitly encoded number field and matrix, with their encodings included in L.
  • The category values had no finite encoding; take nonnegative binary rationals.
  • Require positive matroid rank so that the anchor's maximum element weight in a basis is defined, and use nonempty categories.
  • Interpret the matroid as a bundle template, with supply conserved through the prescribed average q rather than disjointness of template elements across recipients.
  • Use finitely supported rational allocation probabilities and a nonnegative rational range threshold to define the decision version.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(\tau=1\), every category has exactly two elements, and the representing matrix contains only \(0\) and \(1\). Hardness holds for the fixed threshold \(D=0\), and also for the fixed positive threshold \(D=1\).

Consequently, exact minimum-range allocation cannot be computed in time polynomial in \(m,T,r,\tau,L\) unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Reduction from PARTITION

We use the standard binary-encoded PARTITION problem: given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), decide whether some subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i=A/2\), where \(A=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\). This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. [MIT lecture notes](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/6.1400-2025/lec19-before-class.pdf)

Construct the matrix \(H=[\,I_n\ I_n\ I_n\ I_n\,]\). Its columns are indexed by elements \(e_{i,j}\), with \(1\le i\le n\) and \(1\le j\le4\); the column of \(e_{i,j}\) is the \(i\)-th standard unit vector.

For the required number-field encoding, use the fixed field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), defined by the irreducible polynomial \(z^2-2\), and encode the matrix entries in the power basis \((1,\alpha)\). All entries are rational constants. The matrix has full row rank \(n\).

Partition the ground set into the categories \(E_i^+=\{e_{i,1},e_{i,2}\}\) and \(E_i^-=\{e_{i,3},e_{i,4}\}\). Thus \(m=4n\), \(r=n\), and \(T=2n\).

A basis consists of exactly one element from each four-element set \(E_i^+\cup E_i^-\). Indeed, choosing two elements with the same index \(i\) gives repeated columns and hence dependence; choosing one for every index gives the standard basis.

Every permutation within a category preserves the basis family: it exchanges identical columns. Thus the category-symmetry hypothesis holds with nonsingleton categories.

Ordering the profile coordinates as \((1,+),(1,-),\ldots,(n,+),(n,-)\), we obtain exactly the profiles
\(b(x)=(x_1,1-x_1,\ldots,x_n,1-x_n)\), for \(x\in\{0,1\}^n\).
Every such profile is attainable by choosing one element from the indicated category for each index.

Set \(w_{i,+}=a_i\), \(w_{i,-}=0\), and \(q_{i,+}=q_{i,-}=1/2\). Take one recipient type of mass \(1\), so \(\tau=1\), and set \(D=0\).

The promised condition \(q\in\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal B)\) holds explicitly: writing \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\), we have
\(q=\frac12 b(\mathbf1)+\frac12 b(\mathbf0)\).

Moreover, \(w\cdot b(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_ix_i\), and \(w\cdot q=A/2\).

The construction has polynomial encoding length. The matrix has \(4n^2\) constant-size entries; the field encoding is fixed; the weights copy the source integers; and every coordinate of \(q\) has denominator \(2\).

2. Correctness of the reduction

A PARTITION solution gives a zero-range distribution

Suppose \(S\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i=A/2\), and let \(x\) be its indicator vector.

Give probability \(1/2\) to each of \(b(x)\) and \(b(\mathbf1-x)\). Coordinatewise,
\(\frac12b(x)+\frac12b(\mathbf1-x)=q\), so supply conservation holds.

Their scores are respectively \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i=A/2\) and \(\sum_{i\notin S}a_i=A/2\). Therefore this finitely supported rational distribution has range \(0\).

A zero-range distribution gives a PARTITION solution

Conversely, suppose a feasible distribution \(\pi\) has \(R(\pi)\le0\). Since a range is nonnegative, \(R(\pi)=0\), so every positively supported profile has the same score, say \(c\).

Feasibility and linearity give
\(c=\sum_b\pi_b(w\cdot b)=w\cdot\sum_b\pi_b b=w\cdot q=A/2\).

The support is nonempty because the probabilities sum to \(1\). Choose any supported profile \(b(x)\). Then \(\sum_i a_ix_i=A/2\), so \(\{i:x_i=1\}\) solves PARTITION.

Thus the source instance is a YES-instance if and only if the constructed allocation instance admits a feasible distribution of range at most \(0\). This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

3. Exact optimum on the constructed instances

The construction also permits an exact characterization:
\(R_{\min}=\min_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}\left|2\sum_{i=1}^n a_ix_i-A\right|\).

To prove this, define \(\mu=A/2\) and \(\delta=\min_x|w\cdot b(x)-\mu|\).

For any \(x\), the equal mixture of \(b(x)\) and \(b(\mathbf1-x)\) is feasible. Its scores are symmetric about \(\mu\), and its range is \(2|w\cdot b(x)-\mu|\). Choosing a minimizer proves \(R_{\min}\le2\delta\).

If \(\delta=0\), this already proves equality because ranges are nonnegative. If \(\delta>0\), no profile has score \(\mu\). Every feasible distribution must have a supported score below \(\mu\) and one above \(\mu\): otherwise its average score could not equal \(\mu\). By the definition of \(\delta\), these scores are at most \(\mu-\delta\) and at least \(\mu+\delta\), respectively. Hence its range is at least \(2\delta\).

This proves the identity. In particular, allowing arbitrary rational mixtures cannot improve upon the best complementary two-profile mixture in these instances.

4. Positive threshold and relation to the cited results

For hardness at the fixed threshold \(D=1\), replace \(w\) by \(2w\). Every profile score is then an even integer. Consequently, every positive support range is at least \(2\), so a range at most \(1\) is equivalent to a range of \(0\). The same reduction applies.

The cited [representable-matroid result](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16021v3) and [weighted-equitability result](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13983v1) provide bounded-range partitions, which do not require attaining the exact minimum established above. Their guarantees therefore do not contradict this reduction. Also, the reduction uses binary-encoded PARTITION and does not establish strong \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness; running time polynomial in the numerical weight bound can be exponential in its binary encoding length.
