The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_\alpha,\mu_\beta\) with \(\mu_\alpha+\mu_\beta=1\), rational additive values \(v_\alpha(g),v_\beta(g)\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\) of indivisible copies of each item kind \(g\), output a finite-support rational mass allocation \(\lambda_{r,b}\) over \(r\in\{\alpha,\beta\}\) and integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^m\) satisfying \(\sum_b\lambda_{r,b}=\mu_r\), \(\sum_{r,b}b_g\lambda_{r,b}=q_g\), and \(v_r(a)\ge v_r(b-e_g)\) for every positive-mass pair \((r,a),(s,b)\) and every \(g\) with \(b_g>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses, where \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here the support is \(T\subseteq\{\alpha,\beta\}\), with \(\sum_{r\in T}\mu_r=1\).
There are \(m\) item kinds, with per-capita supplies \(q_g\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) and additive values \(v_r(g)\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\). Zero values and supplies are allowed; no nondegeneracy assumption is imposed.
Each individual receives an integral bundle \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\), valued at \(v_r(b)=\sum_{g=1}^m b_gv_r(g)\). Supplies constrain aggregate consumption, not individual bundle coordinates.
The search problem is to output finitely many positive rational masses \(\lambda_{r,b}\), with bundle coordinates and masses encoded in binary, satisfying \(\sum_b\lambda_{r,b}=\mu_r\) and \(\sum_{r\in T,b}b_g\lambda_{r,b}=q_g\) for every kind \(g\).
Require \(v_r(a)\ge v_r(b-e_g)\) whenever \(\lambda_{r,a}>0\), \(\lambda_{s,b}>0\), and \(b_g>0\), where \(e_g\) is the unit vector. This includes comparisons within a type and removal of zero-valued copies; empty envied bundles impose no inequalities.
Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Can this complete EFX mass-allocation search problem be solved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies with at most two additive valuation types, complete EFX allocations always exist, with a constructive existence proof ([Mahara](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08798)).
Existence also holds for finite societies with at most two general valuation types ([Mahara](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09901)). No result settling this exact rational-mass search problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Allowed an absent type by listing only positive-mass types, so the support size is one or two.
  • Retained weak EFX inequalities for removal of every copy, including copies valued at zero, and imposed comparisons within types.
  • Did not impose nondegeneracy: it is an intermediate proof assumption, not a restriction of the existence theorem.

1. General feasibility and polynomial solution length

Throughout, $\tau=|T|\le 2$. A supported class is a pair $(r,b)$ whose assigned mass is positive.

$$ N\mu_r\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad Nq_g\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}, \qquad \log N=O(L). $$

Form a finite society with $N\mu_r$ agents of type $r$ and $Nq_g$ distinguished copies of kind $g$. There are exactly $N$ agents.

Mahara’s theorem guarantees a complete EFX allocation for finite societies with at most two nonnegative additive valuation types. Its EFX definition requires the inequality after removal of every item, including zero-valued items. Thus its hypotheses and fairness convention match this expanded instance. [Mahara, Theorem 1 and Definition 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.08798v4).

For each type $r$ and count vector $b$, let $n_{r,b}$ count the agents receiving $b$. Set
$$ \lambda_{r,b}=\frac{n_{r,b}}{N} $$
when $n_{r,b}>0$. Completeness gives
$$ \sum_b\lambda_{r,b}=\mu_r, \qquad \sum_{r,b}b_g\lambda_{r,b}=q_g. $$

Choose representatives of any two supported classes. Removing a physical copy of kind $g$ changes the envied count vector from $b$ to $b-e_g$. Finite EFX therefore gives the required mass-allocation inequality. When the same representative serves on both sides, the inequality follows directly from nonnegative additive values.

This proves feasibility. The numerical size of the expanded society can be exponential in $L$, so this argument alone does not give a polynomial algorithm.

1.2 Support reduction preserves EFX

Put $d=m+\tau$. Associate with each labeled bundle the column
$$ c(r,b)=(e_r,b)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{d}. $$
Here $e_r$ is the unit vector in the type-coordinate block. The balance equations are
$$ \sum_{r,b}\lambda_{r,b}c(r,b)=h, \qquad h=\bigl((\mu_r)_{r\in T},(q_g)_{g=1}^{m}\bigr). $$

Suppose the supported columns are linearly dependent. Because these columns are integer vectors, there is a nonzero rational dependence $z$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{r,b}z_{r,b}c(r,b)=0. $$
Summing its type-coordinate equations gives
$$ \sum_{r,b}z_{r,b}=0. $$
Consequently, $z$ has both positive and negative entries.

Set
$$ t=\min_{z_{r,b}>0}\frac{\lambda_{r,b}}{z_{r,b}}, \qquad \lambda'_{r,b}=\lambda_{r,b}-tz_{r,b}. $$
Every new mass is nonnegative, at least one becomes zero, and all balances remain valid.

Only existing classes survive. Thus every EFX comparison required after this operation was already required before it. EFX is preserved.

Repeating the operation yields independent supported columns, hence at most $d$ positive classes.

1.3 Encoding bounds

Start with the finite allocation from Section 1.1. Every retained coordinate satisfies
$$ 0\le b_g\le Nq_g. $$
Define
$$ K=\max\bigl(\{1,N\}\cup\{Nq_g:g=1,\ldots,m\}\bigr). $$
Then $\log K=O(L)$.

Suppose the compressed support has $k\le d$ independent columns. Choose $k$ rows forming a nonsingular integer matrix $C$. On those rows,
$$ C\lambda=h_R. $$
Since $Nh_R$ is integral, Cramer’s rule gives
$$ \lambda_j=\frac{\det C_j}{N\det C}, $$
where $C_j$ replaces column $j$ of $C$ by $Nh_R$.

All entries of $C$ and $C_j$ have absolute value at most $K$. The determinant expansion therefore implies
$$ |\det C|,\ |\det C_j|\le k!K^k. $$
Each mass has encoding length
$$ O\bigl(L+d(L+\log d)\bigr). $$

It follows that every instance admits a solution with at most $m+\tau$ classes and total encoding length
$$ O\bigl((m+\tau)^2(L+\log(m+\tau))\bigr). $$

Positivity, balances, and all EFX inequalities for such a solution can be checked by exact rational arithmetic in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ time.

2. A compressed finite theorem for two item kinds

The new polynomial-time result uses the following published theorem.

Known finite theorem. For strictly positive additive valuations and two item kinds with integer multiplicities, Davidiuk, Dementiev, Ignatiev, and Sagunov construct a complete EFX and Pareto-optimal allocation. After preprocessing and ordering agents by their valuation ratios, their Theorem 5 and Algorithm 1 use $O(\log n+\log M)$ arithmetic operations, where $n$ is the agent count and $M$ is the item count. The output consists of constantly many index intervals receiving specified bundles. [Theorem 5, Algorithm 1, and its proof](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03438#S3).

Pareto optimality means that no reallocation weakly increases every agent’s utility and strictly increases at least one agent’s utility.

The compressed output is essential: we will apply this theorem to exponentially many agents without listing them.

3. Two item kinds: a polynomial algorithm including zero values

I first prove the result for at most two active kinds, where “active” means positive supply. Kinds with zero supply can be omitted and restored with coordinate zero: nonnegative consumption and positive class masses force that coordinate to be zero in every valid solution.

3.1 Zero or one active kind

With no active kinds, give every type its mass on the empty bundle.

For one active kind, retain the integer $N$ from Section 1.1 and write
$$ Nq=Nk+R, \qquad k=\lfloor q\rfloor, \qquad 0\le R<N. $$
Order the $N$ agents in contiguous type blocks. Give $k$ copies to the first $N-R$ agents and $k+1$ copies to the remaining $R$ agents.

Intersect these two index intervals with the type blocks and divide their lengths by $N$. This produces $O(\tau)$ classes with the exact masses and supply.

Every supported bundle has $k$ or $k+1$ copies. After removing a copy from any nonempty bundle, at most $k$ remain. Thus EFX holds for every nonnegative valuation of this kind.

Moreover, a class with the minimum copy count is unenvied: no supported individual strictly prefers that class’s bundle to their own.

3.2 A polynomially encoded perturbation

Now suppose both kinds have positive supply. Let
$$ M_g=Nq_g, \qquad M=M_1+M_2. $$
These are positive integers, and $\log M=O(L)$.

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the two-kind valuations. Define integer values
$$ A_r(g)=Dv_r(g) $$
and strictly positive perturbed values
$$ w_r(g)=A_r(g)+\varepsilon, \qquad \varepsilon=\frac{1}{M+1}. $$
All these numbers have $O(L)$ bits.

I claim that any EFX allocation for $w$ is EFX for the original valuations.

Indeed, suppose original EFX fails for finite bundles $a,b$ and a copy of kind $g$ in $b$. Since $A_r$ is integer-valued,
$$ A_r(b-e_g)-A_r(a)\ge 1. $$
Writing $|a|$ for the number of copies in $a$, we obtain
$$ w_r(b-e_g)-w_r(a) = A_r(b-e_g)-A_r(a) +\varepsilon\bigl(|b|-1-|a|\bigr). $$
Every bundle contains at most $M$ copies, so
$$ w_r(b-e_g)-w_r(a) \ge 1-\varepsilon M =\frac{1}{M+1}>0. $$
Thus the original violation would also violate perturbed EFX.

The same calculation without removal shows that every original strict envy is preserved:
$$ A_r(b)>A_r(a) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad w_r(b)>w_r(a). $$

The argument explicitly covers removal of a copy whose original value is zero.

3.3 Simulating the finite algorithm without expanding the society

Normalize each perturbed valuation by its positive first-kind value and form the ratio
$$ \rho_r=\frac{w_r(2)}{w_r(1)}. $$
Sort the $\tau$ type blocks by this ratio.

An indexed agent’s valuation can now be obtained from the cumulative integer block sizes $N\mu_r$. There is no need to store $N$ valuation rows.

First consider $M\le N$. Give kind $1$ to the first $M_1$ indexed agents and kind $2$ to the last $M_2$ agents. Everyone receives at most one item, so the allocation is EFX.

This allocation is also Pareto optimal for the normalized positive valuations. Any Pareto improvement must keep each of the $M$ initially nonempty agents nonempty. Since there are only $M$ items, those agents must still receive exactly one item each. Total normalized utility in any such allocation is
$$ M_1+\sum_{i\in S_2}\rho_i, \qquad |S_2|=M_2. $$
This is maximized by selecting the last $M_2$ agents, which is the allocation already constructed. A Pareto improvement would strictly increase total normalized utility, a contradiction.

For $M>N$, perform the preprocessing required by the finite theorem. Partition the agents into groups of sizes $n_1,n_2$, with first-kind-preferring agents in the first group. If
$$ M_1n_2<M_2n_1, $$
interchange the kinds and the groups, and reorder the type blocks. After this operation,
$$ n_2>0, \qquad M_1n_2\ge M_2n_1. $$
Positivity, normalized first-kind values, sorted ratios, and the required count inequalities now all hold.

Apply the finite theorem through indexed access to the type blocks. Its split and reallocation formulas use bounded-depth arithmetic on counts and valuation ratios, including integer division and floors or ceilings of count-ratio products. Consequently their operands have $O(L)$ bits in this application.

There are $O(\log N+\log M)=O(L)$ arithmetic steps. Indexed valuation access and preprocessing take polynomial time in $\tau,L$. Hence the simulation has polynomial bit complexity.

Finally, intersect the constantly many output intervals with the $\tau$ type blocks. An intersection containing $n_j>0$ agents produces a class of mass
$$ p_j=\frac{n_j}{N}. $$
This gives $O(\tau)$ classes, with polynomial encoding length, exact balances, and EFX for the original valuations by Section 3.2.

3.4 The construction also supplies an unenvied class

For the two-kind construction, consider the finite envy graph under the original valuations. An edge from agent $i$ to agent $j$ means that $i$ strictly prefers $j$’s bundle.

This graph is acyclic. Otherwise, original strict envy along a directed cycle would remain strict under the perturbation. Cyclically transferring the bundles would then Pareto improve the perturbed allocation, contradicting its Pareto optimality.

The same argument applies to the graph on supported classes: a cycle of distinct classes can be realized by distinct representative agents.

Every finite directed acyclic graph has a vertex with no incoming edge. Therefore some supported class, with bundle $b^*$, satisfies
$$ v_r(a)\ge v_r(b^*) $$
for every supported class $(r,a)$.

The zero-kind and one-kind constructions also have such a class, as shown above.

Conclusion. With at most two active kinds, we can compute in $\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)$ time both a complete EFX mass allocation and an unenvied supported class.

4. Merging identical valuation columns and splitting them back

We now allow arbitrary $m$.

For an active kind $g$, define its valuation column
$$ \nu(g)=\bigl(v_r(g)\bigr)_{r\in T}. $$
Suppose the nonzero columns take at most two distinct values, denoted $u_1,u_2$. Let
$$ G_h=\{g:\nu(g)=u_h\}, \qquad Q_h=\sum_{g\in G_h}q_g. $$
Temporarily omit the jointly zero-valued kinds.

Replace each nonempty $G_h$ by a merged kind with supply $Q_h$ and valuation column $u_h$. Section 3 gives a polynomially computed allocation
$$ (r_j,c_j,p_j), \qquad j=1,\ldots,s, $$
where $s=O(\tau)$,
$$ p_j=\frac{n_j}{N}, \qquad c_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{\,k}, \qquad k\le 2. $$

It remains to realize each merged count using the original kinds. The following construction does so exactly.

4.1 Allocate fractional average counts within each class

Fix a group $G_h$. Find nonnegative integers $t_{j,g}$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{g\in G_h}t_{j,g}=n_jc_{j,h}, \qquad \sum_jt_{j,g}=Nq_g. $$
The total row and column demands agree because
$$ \sum_jn_jc_{j,h}=NQ_h =\sum_{g\in G_h}Nq_g. $$

A greedy transportation construction suffices: match a row with a column, assign the smaller remaining demand, and repeat. Each assignment exhausts a row or column. Thus the construction uses polynomially many operations on $O(L)$-bit integers.

For class $j$, set
$$ y_{j,g}=\frac{t_{j,g}}{n_j}. $$
Then
$$ \sum_{g\in G_h}y_{j,g}=c_{j,h}, \qquad \sum_jp_jy_{j,g}=q_g. $$
These are average counts; we next replace them by finitely many integral bundles.

4.2 An explicit integral rounding lemma

Let $y_1,\ldots,y_a$ be nonnegative rationals whose sum is an integer $C$. Write
$$ d_i=\lfloor y_i\rfloor, \qquad f_i=y_i-d_i, \qquad R=\sum_i f_i=C-\sum_i d_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}. $$
Define prefix sums
$$ s_0=0, \qquad s_i=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}f_\ell. $$

For $0\le\theta<1$, set
$$ z_i(\theta) = d_i+\lfloor s_i+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor s_{i-1}+\theta\rfloor. $$
Since $0\le f_i<1$, each floor difference is either zero or one. Therefore every $z_i(\theta)$ is a nonnegative integer.

The sum telescopes:
$$ \sum_i z_i(\theta) = \sum_i d_i+\lfloor R+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor\theta\rfloor =C. $$

For any nonnegative real $s$,
$$ \int_0^1\lfloor s+\theta\rfloor\,d\theta=s. $$
To verify this, write $s=a+f$ with integer $a$ and $0\le f<1$; the integrand equals $a$ on an interval of length $1-f$ and $a+1$ on an interval of length $f$.

Consequently,
$$ \int_0^1z_i(\theta)\,d\theta =d_i+s_i-s_{i-1} =y_i. $$

The vector $z(\theta)$ changes only at the rational thresholds
$$ 1-\{s_i\} $$
whose fractional parts are nonzero. Cutting $[0,1)$ at these thresholds gives at most $a+1$ intervals. Their lengths and the corresponding constant integral vectors form a finite rational distribution with mean $y$ and total count exactly $C$ in every outcome.

4.3 Apply the rounding simultaneously to both groups

For each original class $j$, apply Section 4.2 to its vector $(y_{j,g})_{g\in G_h}$ in each group. Use the same parameter $\theta$ for both groups and cut at the union of their thresholds.

There are at most $m+1$ resulting intervals. If an interval has length $\ell>0$, assign its integral bundle mass
$$ p_j\ell. $$

Every resulting bundle $b$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{g\in G_h}b_g=c_{j,h}. $$
Its average consumption of kind $g$ is $y_{j,g}$, so all original supply balances hold.

This construction is entirely rational. In fact, all thresholds have denominator dividing $n_j$, so each resulting positive mass is an integer multiple of $1/N$. The construction has polynomial encoding length and running time.

4.4 EFX survives disaggregation

Every bundle derived from class $j$ has, for every supported type $r$, the same utility:
$$ v_r(b)=\sum_hu_h(r)c_{j,h}. $$

Suppose $a$ comes from merged class $i$, $b$ comes from merged class $j$, and $b_g>0$ for $g\in G_h$. Then $c_{j,h}>0$, and
$$ v_r(b-e_g) = \sum_\ell u_\ell(r)c_{j,\ell}-u_h(r). $$
This is exactly the utility of the merged bundle $c_j-e_h$. Hence the required inequality follows from merged EFX:
$$ v_r(a)=u_r(c_i)\ge u_r(c_j-e_h)=v_r(b-e_g). $$

This covers comparisons within a type and removal of a kind valued at zero by the comparing type.

Moreover, a child of the merged unenvied class remains unenvied: disaggregation preserves its utility under every supported valuation.

5. Adding all jointly zero-valued kinds

Let $G_0$ contain the omitted kinds whose valuation columns are zero. Choose an unenvied supported class of mass $p>0$ and bundle $b^*$.

For each $g\in G_0$, write
$$ k_g=\left\lfloor\frac{q_g}{p}\right\rfloor, \qquad a_g=q_g-pk_g, \qquad 0\le a_g<p. $$

Parameterize this class by $0\le t<p$. Add $k_g+1$ copies of kind $g$ when $t<a_g$, and $k_g$ copies otherwise. Cut the interval at the distinct thresholds $a_g$ and output the resulting bundles with interval-length masses.

Consumption is exact:
$$ pk_g+a_g=q_g. $$
The original type masses and all previously allocated supplies remain unchanged.

All utilities remain unchanged. Removing a previously present copy therefore gives an inequality already established before this step.

Removing a newly added zero-valued copy leaves the original utility of $b^*$. Since $b^*$ was unenvied,
$$ v_r(a)\ge v_r(b^*) $$
for every supported own bundle $a$. Thus these new removal comparisons also satisfy EFX.

The construction adds at most $|G_0|$ classes. All arithmetic has polynomial bit length.

Combining Sections 3–5 proves:

Polynomial-time subclass theorem. If the positive-supply kinds have at most two distinct nonzero valuation columns, complete EFX mass allocation is computable in
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
time, with $O(\tau(m+1))$ output classes. Zero values and jointly zero-valued kinds are included.

6. General search bound and the hardness qualification

6.1 A finite exponential-time algorithm

For the unrestricted instance, use the $N$ from Section 1 and enumerate labeled bundles in the box
$$ 0\le b_g\le Nq_g. $$
The number of candidates is

$$ 2^{O((m+\tau)(mL+\log\tau))} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

6.2 Totality constrains an NP-hardness argument

Section 1 supplies a polynomially bounded and polynomially verifiable solution relation that is total on valid inputs. With a trivial convention for invalid encodings, this relation belongs to $\mathrm{TFNP}$.

For a precise conditional statement, suppose a polynomial-time reduction maps each Boolean formula $x$ to a valid allocation instance $I(x)$, and a polynomial-time decoder decides satisfiability correctly from every valid allocation of $I(x)$.

Then unsatisfiability has polynomial certificates: provide a polynomial-size valid allocation and verify that the decoder answers “unsatisfiable.” Such a certificate exists for every unsatisfiable formula by totality, and cannot exist for a satisfiable formula by the decoder’s correctness. Thus
$$ \mathrm{UNSAT}\in\mathrm{NP}, $$
which implies
$$ \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}. $$

This does not rule out other search-hardness notions. It shows why a hardness claim for this problem needs an explicit reduction definition.

7. What remains open in this argument

The general feasibility and encoding bounds do not provide a polynomial method for discovering compatible bundles.

The additional construction resolves two item kinds and, more generally, at most two distinct nonzero valuation columns. It does not handle arbitrary two nonproportional valuation types with three or more distinct nonzero columns.

I therefore establish neither polynomial-time solvability nor NP-hardness for the complete task.
