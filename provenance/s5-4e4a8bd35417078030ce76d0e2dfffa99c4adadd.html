$$ c_{C,t}(x)=\sum_{y\in X}P_{xy}v_{C,t}(y). $$

From every state outside $C$, a simple directed path reaches $z$ in at most $m-1$ transitions. Thus, conditional on not having reached $C$, the probability of reaching it within the next $m$ transitions is at least $p_{\min}^{m}>0$. The probability of avoiding $C$ for $jm$ transitions is consequently at most

$$ (1-p_{\min}^{m})^j, $$

which tends to zero.

It follows that

$$ \min_{z\in C}u_t(z) \le v_{C,t}(x),c_{C,t}(x) \le \max_{z\in C}u_t(z). $$

Writing $R=X\setminus C$, the values satisfy

$$ v_{C,t}(x)=u_t(x)\qquad(x\in C) $$

and

$$ (I-P_{RR})v_{C,t}|_R=P_{RC}u_t|_C. $$

The hitting argument shows that $P_{RR}$ is transient, so

$$ (I-P_{RR})^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}P_{RR}^{j} $$

exists and is entrywise nonnegative.

These values can be computed exactly in polynomial time. For completeness, let $d$ be the product of all input denominators, so $\log_2 d\le L$, and put

$$ H=dI_R-dP_{RR}. $$

The equation for the unknown values becomes

$$ H\bigl(dv_{C,t}|_R\bigr)=d^2P_{RC}u_t|_C, $$

whose right-hand side is integral. If $r=|R|$, then the integral matrix $H$ has entries of absolute value at most $d$, and

$$ 0<|\det H|\le r!d^r. $$

Cramer’s rule therefore bounds every value’s denominator by $d|\det H|$. Together with the utility bound, this gives polynomial binary length. Exact rational linear algebra computes the values in polynomial time.

If $D(C)$ is empty, stop. Otherwise replace $C$ by $C\setminus D(C)$.

Finally use $(C,0,C)$ as the main plan and every punishment plan.

$$ c_{C,t_0}(z)\le\max_{y\in C}u_{t_0}(y)=u_{t_0}(z). $$

$$ v(x)\le(Pv)(x). $$

$$ v(x)=u_t(x)<c_{C,t}(x)=(Pv)(x). $$

$$ u_t(x)<c_{\text{old},t}(x). $$

$$ u_t(x)<c_{C,t}(x) \qquad\text{for every }t. $$

The strict-support mass at this prescribed rejection is therefore zero, which is less than $q_x$.

$$ u_t(x)\ge c_{C,t}(x). $$

Its mass alone is at least $\mu_{\min}\ge q_x$, so the weak-support mass reaches the quota. If $q_x=0$, the acceptance inequality holds automatically.

The collective inequalities hold at every proposal history because the strategy is stationary. Section 1.2 supplies subgame perfection.

5. Proven counter compression for deterministic transitions

Assume now that each row of $P$ has exactly one positive entry. Since the row sums are one, there is a function $\sigma:X\to X$ such that

$$ P_{x,\sigma(x)}=1. $$

Irreducibility forces $\sigma$ to be a single cycle containing all proposals. In particular,

$$ P^m=I. $$

The following statement concerns equilibrium on all formal proposal histories, including histories inconsistent with this deterministic cycle.

5.1. Values of a two-step plan

For plan $i=(A_i,k_i,B_i)$, let $v_{i,t}^{(r)}(x)$ denote its prescribed-play value with $r$ first-phase rounds remaining, including the current round.

The terminal-phase values are $v_{i,t}^{(0)}=v_{B_i,t}$, with the convention $v_{\varnothing,t}=0$. For $r\ge1$,

$$ v_{i,t}^{(r)}(x)= \begin{cases} u_t(x),&x\in A_i,\\ v_{i,t}^{(r-1)}(\sigma(x)),&x\notin A_i. \end{cases} $$

Following prescribed play triggers no punishment: its first prescribed acceptance ends the game.

If $A_i\ne\varnothing$, the deterministic cycle encounters $A_i$ within the first $m$ proposals. Thus

$$ v_{i,t}^{(r)}=v_{A_i,t} \qquad(r\ge m). $$

If $A_i=\varnothing$, then

$$ v_{i,t}^{(r)}=P^r v_{B_i,t}. $$

Because $P^m=I$, these values depend only on $r$ modulo $m$.

5.2. Compression rule

Replace every counter by

$$ k_i'= \begin{cases} \min\{k_i,m\},&A_i\ne\varnothing,\\ k_i\bmod m,&A_i=\varnothing. \end{cases} $$

Keep both target sets unchanged.

Section 5.1 gives

$$ v_{i,t}^{(k_i')}=v_{i,t}^{(k_i)} $$

for every plan, type, and current proposal. Therefore the continuation utility after rejection of any prescribed acceptance is unchanged: rejecting proposal $x$ still gives

$$ V_t=v_{x,t}^{(k_x)}(\sigma(x)), $$

and the compressed punishment gives the same value.

It remains to check prescribed rejections and formal-history reachability.

5.3. Reachability on formal histories

Index the main plan by $\star$ and each punishment by its proposal. Make a graph on these indices with edges

$$ i\longrightarrow x \qquad(x\in B_i). $$

A plan index occurs at some formal history exactly when it is reachable from $\star$ in this graph.

For necessity, switching from $i$ to punishment $x$ requires a prescribed acceptance of $x$, and hence $x\in B_i$.

For sufficiency, every $x\in B_i$ can be prescribed for acceptance. If $k_i=0$ or $A_i=X$, offer $x$ immediately. Otherwise repeat a proposal outside $A_i$ for $k_i$ prescribed rejections and then offer $x$. Counterfactually rejecting it enters punishment $x$. All these words are allowed formal histories, regardless of transition probabilities.

Compression leaves this graph unchanged.

Within a reachable plan:

  • If $k_i>0$ and $A_i=X$, only its initial first-phase state occurs.
  • Otherwise, every remaining-counter value from zero through $k_i$ occurs, with every current proposal.

For the second assertion, prescribed rejections of a proposal outside $A_i$ reach each desired counter, and the next current proposal may be arbitrary.

5.4. Preservation of the collective inequalities

At every prescribed acceptance in the compressed tuple, the rejection continuation is unchanged by Section 5.2. The same proposal was prescribed for acceptance somewhere in the corresponding reachable original plan, so its original acceptance inequality applies.

Consider a prescribed rejection in a compressed plan.

$$ v_{i,t}^{(r-1)}(\sigma(x)). $$

Since $r\le k_i'\le k_i$, the same remaining-counter state occurred in the original plan, unless $A_i=X$. That exceptional case has no prescribed rejections. The value above depends on $A_i,B_i,r$, not on the plan’s initial counter, and is therefore unchanged.

6. Polynomial-time verification and a conditional hardness barrier

6.1. A polynomial-time verifier for deterministic transitions

A tuple with counters at most $m$ has encoding length

$$ O\bigl(m^2+m\log(m+1)\bigr). $$

The reachability graph and phase characterization in Section 5.3 identify every active state that must be checked. There are at most

$$ (m+1)^2m $$

such states.

For each plan, compute $v_{i,t}^{(r)}$ for $0\le r\le m$ using the recurrence in Section 5.1. Under deterministic transitions, every coordinate is either an input utility or zero: prescribed play has a deterministic terminal outcome, or never accepts.

At an acceptance, compare utilities with the appropriate punishment value. At a rejection, compare them with the next-phase value. Sum the strict or weak supporters’ masses and check the relevant quota inequality.

All computations have polynomial binary length and polynomial running time. Section 1.2 then supplies the subgame-perfect-equilibrium requirement.

6.2. Totality and polynomial balance

Use the existence theorem supplied in the question: finite-player collective stopping games with nonnegative utilities, irreducible proposal transitions, and nonempty monotone decisive-coalition families admit two-step collective equilibria.

Its hypotheses hold here. The delegates form a finite player set, and each family

$$ \mathcal C_x= \left\{ S\subseteq T:\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\ge q_x \right\} $$

is monotone and contains the full coalition. Zero quotas permit the empty coalition and are allowed by the theorem. [Herings–Predtetchinski, Theorem 9.2](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/economics/seminars/seminars/conferences/ctn/herings_predtetchinski.pdf).

Existence followed by compression proves that every deterministic-transition instance has a solution with counters at most $m$.

Consequently, the relation consisting of these bounded-counter solutions is total, polynomially balanced, and polynomial-time verifiable. In standard terminology, it is a $\mathrm{TFNP}$ relation.

6.3. Conditional consequence for Turing hardness

Here “robust” means that the reduction must succeed for every oracle returning a valid solution on every queried instance.

Suppose a deterministic polynomial-time robust Turing reduction decides Boolean satisfiability using only deterministic-transition instances of this search problem.

We show that unsatisfiability then has polynomial-size, polynomial-time verifiable certificates.

A certificate supplies the oracle replies in an execution of the reduction, choosing only solutions with counters at most $m$. A verifier:

  • Simulates the reduction.
  • Checks every supplied reply with the polynomial-time verifier above.
  • Requires identical replies to repeated identical queries.
  • Accepts exactly when the simulated reduction declares the formula unsatisfiable.

The transcript has polynomial length because the reduction has polynomial running time and all chosen replies have polynomial length.

If the formula is unsatisfiable, a valid transcript exists: every query has a bounded-counter solution.

Conversely, every accepted transcript is consistent with a valid total oracle. Its replies are valid and agree on repeated queries; choose any bounded-counter solution for each unqueried instance. Robust correctness implies that the formula is indeed unsatisfiable.

Thus Boolean unsatisfiability belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$. By the $\mathrm{NP}$-completeness of satisfiability, this implies

$$ \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}. $$

Conditional conclusion. An $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness proof using only deterministic-transition instances, under robust polynomial-time Turing reductions, would imply $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$.

This does not establish a corresponding barrier for arbitrary stochastic transitions: the deterministic compression and polynomial-time verification arguments do not extend here.

11. Section 6.3: conditional hardness barrier

The transcript argument is correct under the explicitly stated robust oracle convention.

Bounded valid replies exist for every queried instance. Polynomial running time and polynomial reply length give polynomial-size transcripts. Requiring identical replies to repeated queries makes each accepted transcript extendible to a fixed valid total oracle.

Robust correctness then ensures that an accepted transcript can only occur for an unsatisfiable formula. Conversely, totality supplies a valid transcript for every unsatisfiable formula. The resulting membership of Boolean unsatisfiability in $\mathrm{NP}$ implies $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$.
