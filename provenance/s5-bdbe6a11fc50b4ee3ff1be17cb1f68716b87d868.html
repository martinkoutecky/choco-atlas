The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite defeasible theory \(T\), a finite literal universe \(U\), and rational choice types \(C_1,\ldots,C_\tau\subseteq U\) with masses \(\mu_i>0\) summing to \(1\), determine whether there is a proposal \(P\subseteq U\) and a superiority relation under which every literal in \(P\) is defeasibly derivable, such that \(\sum_{i:C_i\subseteq P}\mu_i>1/2\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(U\) be a finite, complement-closed propositional literal universe, with \(m=|U|\); write \(\bar q\) for the complement of \(q\). A background theory \(T=(F,R,>_0)\) has facts \(F\subseteq U\), uniquely labelled strict rules, defeasible rules and defeaters, each with a finite antecedent set \(A(r)\subseteq U\) and one head \(h(r)\in U\). Empty antecedents are allowed. Write \(R_s\) and \(R_{sd}\) for the strict rules and the strict-or-defeasible rules, and \(R[q]\) for rules with head \(q\).
A selectable superiority relation \(>\) is an acyclic relation between rules with complementary heads; it may replace \(>_0\) completely. Facts and rules remain fixed. Derivability uses the following finite-proof semantics, with all tagged premises established earlier in the proof.
Derive \(+\Delta q\) when \(q\in F\) or some \(r\in R_s[q]\) has every antecedent tagged \(+\Delta\). Derive \(+\partial q\) when \(+\Delta q\) holds, or when \(-\Delta\bar q\) holds, some \(r\in R_{sd}[q]\) has every antecedent tagged \(+\partial\), and every \(s\in R[\bar q]\) either has an antecedent tagged \(-\partial\) or is outranked by some \(t\in R_{sd}[q]\) whose antecedents are all tagged \(+\partial\).
The conditions for \(-\Delta q\) and \(-\partial q\) are the strong negations of their positive conditions: interchange conjunction/disjunction and universal/existential quantifiers, swap positive/negative tags, and negate untagged membership and superiority tests. A missing positive tag alone is not a negative tag.
A choice is a contradiction-free subset of \(U\), possibly empty; it is rational if some selectable superiority relation derives \(+\partial q\) for every member \(q\). The society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) distinct rational choices \(C_i\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_i\), where \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\). Individual rationality is promised and may use different superiority relations; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,|R|,L\).
A proposal may be any contradiction-free \(P\subseteq U\). Type \(i\) supports it exactly when \(C_i\subseteq P\); a tie at half the total mass loses.

Is deciding whether there exist a proposal \(P\) and one selectable superiority relation deriving every member of \(P\), with \(\sum_{i:C_i\subseteq P}\mu_i>1/2\), solvable in polynomial time or NP-hard?

The discrete majority winning-proposal problem is reported NP-hard; evaluating supplied rational proposals against rational choices is polynomial. [Discrete results](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336816150_Non-monotonic_Collective_Decisions)
Changing a specified literal's derivability by revising only rule superiority is NP-complete. [Preference-revision result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.5833)
No result settling this exact rational-mass problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the literal universe propositional, complement-closed and inclusive of every theory literal; defined m and total input bit length L.
  • Required choices and proposals to be contradiction-free, as the discrete definition requires; retained its allowance for empty choices and proposals.
  • Interpreted rational input types as a promise of individual rationality, with independently selectable superiority relations.
  • Allowed complete replacement of the initial superiority relation while fixing facts and rules; proposals need not equal an input type.
  • Used acyclic superiority between complementary-head rules, following the cited preference-revision formalism and the intended consistent conflict-resolution semantics.
  • Corrected the unrestricted defeating-rule quantifier to strict-or-defeasible rules with the desired head, following the intended explanation and the cited formalism.
  • Specified constructive negative tags through strong negation, so failure to derive a literal is not automatically its refutation.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even with exactly two distinct singleton choices, each of mass \(1/2\), and with no facts, strict rules, or defeaters.

With only one supported type, the answer is always yes by the individual-rationality promise. Thus hardness already occurs at the first nontrivial support size.

The cited [preference-revision paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.5833) establishes a related complexity result. The reduction below proves hardness directly for the precise semantics and promise in this question.

Polynomial verification

Write \(r=|R|\). A certificate consists of a proposal \(P\subseteq U\) and a selectable superiority relation \(>\), represented by an \(r\times r\) Boolean matrix. Its size is \(O(m+r^2)\).

First verify that \(P\) is contradiction-free, that every superiority edge joins rules with complementary heads, and that the relation is acyclic. These checks take \(O(m+r^2)\) Boolean-table operations.

For the fixed relation, compute derivability by saturation. Start with no established tags, and repeatedly add every tag whose inference condition holds using previously established tags.

There are only \(4m\) possible tags. Moreover, every tagged premise in both the positive conditions and their strong negations tests the presence of a tag; untagged membership and superiority tests have fixed truth values. Consequently, the inference operator is monotone.

This saturation computes exactly finite-proof derivability:

  • Every added tag has a finite proof: inductively, concatenate proofs of its previously established premises and then apply its inference condition.
  • Every tag in a finite proof is eventually added: induct on the position of that tag in the proof.

In particular, saturation never treats a missing positive tag as a negative tag.

With rules indexed by their heads, one complete scan costs \(O((m+1)(r+1)^2)\) Boolean-table operations. This bound allows checking all opposing-rule and supporting-rule pairs and scanning their antecedents. There are at most \(4m+1\) scans, so saturation costs \(O((m+1)^2(r+1)^2)\).

Finally, check that every member of \(P\) has tag \(+\partial\), and compute the exact supporting mass. Testing all inclusions \(C_i\subseteq P\) costs \(O(\tau(m+1))\) table operations. Sequential rational addition uses intermediate integers with \(O(L)\) bits: their denominators divide the product of the input denominators, whose total bit length is \(O(L)\). Schoolbook arithmetic therefore gives \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations, including comparison with \(1/2\).

A conservative bit-cost random-access bound for the whole verifier is

\(B=O\!\left(L(m+1)^2(r+1)^2+\tau L(m+1)+(\tau+1)L^2\right).\)

Thus the promised problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). For comparison, exhaustive enumeration of proposals and superiority matrices gives a deterministic upper bound \(O(2^{m+r^2}B)\).

Reduction from satisfiability

Let \(\Phi=D_1\land\cdots\land D_k\) be a Boolean formula in conjunctive normal form with at most three literals per clause, over variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\). Satisfiability for this class is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

Introduce fresh atoms \(z,a,b,c_1,\ldots,c_k\). Let \(U\) consist of these atoms, the original variable atoms, and all their complements. Set \(F=\varnothing\) and \(>_0=\varnothing\). Every rule below is defeasible and has its own distinct label.

For every \(y\in\{x_1,\ldots,x_n,z\}\), introduce the two rules

\(u_y:\varnothing\Rightarrow y,\qquad v_y:\varnothing\Rightarrow\bar y.\)

For every clause \(D_j\) and every literal \(\ell\) occurring in it, introduce

\(w_{j,\ell}:\{\ell\}\Rightarrow c_j.\)

Finally, introduce

\(r_a:\{z\}\Rightarrow a,\qquad r_b:\{\bar z\}\Rightarrow b,\qquad r_\Phi:\{c_1,\ldots,c_k\}\Rightarrow b.\)

The society has precisely the two choices

\(C_1=\{a\},\qquad C_2=\{b\},\qquad \mu_1=\mu_2=\frac12.\)

Both choices are contradiction-free and distinct.

Derivability properties of the construction

Fix any selectable superiority relation for the constructed theory.

Because there are neither facts nor strict rules, \(+\Delta q\) is impossible for every \(q\in U\). Conversely, \(-\Delta q\) has a premise-free derivation for every \(q\), since its fact-membership condition is false and there are no strict rules to examine.

Variable literals encode consistent partial assignments

For each \(y\in\{x_1,\ldots,x_n,z\}\),

\(+\partial y\text{ is derivable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u_y>v_y,\)

and

\(+\partial\bar y\text{ is derivable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_y>u_y.\)

Indeed, the sole supporting rule and sole opposing rule both have empty antecedents. The opposing rule cannot be discarded through a negative antecedent, so deriving the positive literal requires exactly the displayed superiority edge. Conversely, that edge, together with the already available \(-\Delta\) tag for the complement, supplies all premises for derivation.

Acyclicity prohibits both edges simultaneously. Therefore \(+\partial y\) and \(+\partial\bar y\) cannot both be derived.

Clause and goal literals have their stated meaning

There are no rules headed by \(\bar c_j,\bar a,\bar b\). Hence the opposing-rule conditions for \(c_j,a,b\) are vacuous. Inspection of their supporting rules gives

\(+\partial c_j\text{ is derivable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{some }\ell\in D_j\text{ has derivable }+\partial\ell,\)

\(+\partial a\text{ is derivable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad +\partial z\text{ is derivable},\)

and

\(+\partial b\text{ is derivable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \left(+\partial\bar z\text{ is derivable}\right) \text{ or } \left(\text{every }+\partial c_j\text{ is derivable}\right).\)

For necessity, the \(+\Delta\) alternative is unavailable, so a derivation must use one of the listed supporting rules with all its antecedents established earlier. For sufficiency, concatenate finite proofs of those antecedents, establish the required \(-\Delta\) tag, and apply the corresponding rule.

Verification of the rationality promise

The promise holds for every source formula, whether satisfiable or unsatisfiable.

For \(C_1\), choose the relation consisting solely of \(u_z>v_z\). It is acyclic and derives \(+\partial z\), followed by \(+\partial a\).

For \(C_2\), choose the relation consisting solely of \(v_z>u_z\). It is acyclic and derives \(+\partial\bar z\), followed by \(+\partial b\).

Thus both listed choices are individually rational, although their witnessing relations differ.

Correctness of the reduction

Because the two masses are exactly \(1/2\), a proposal wins precisely when it contains both \(a\) and \(b\). Supporting only one type gives a losing tie.

A satisfying assignment yields a winning rational proposal

Suppose \(I\) satisfies \(\Phi\). Choose \(u_z>v_z\). For each variable \(x_i\), choose \(u_{x_i}>v_{x_i}\) when \(I(x_i)\) is true, and choose the reverse edge when it is false. Include no other edges.

This relation is acyclic: every edge lies within a separate two-rule pair, and each pair contains only one direction.

First derive \(-\Delta q\) for every \(q\in U\). Then derive \(+\partial z\) and all variable literals made true by \(I\). Every clause has such a literal, so derive every \(+\partial c_j\). Finally derive \(+\partial a\) through \(r_a\) and \(+\partial b\) through \(r_\Phi\).

The contradiction-free proposal \(P=\{a,b\}\) is therefore rational and has supporting mass \(1\).

A winning rational proposal yields a satisfying assignment

Conversely, suppose a winning proposal and one selectable relation derive every member of that proposal. Winning forces the proposal to contain both \(a\) and \(b\).

Deriving \(+\partial a\) forces \(+\partial z\). By the variable-pair property and acyclicity, \(+\partial\bar z\) cannot also be derived. Consequently, deriving \(+\partial b\) must use \(r_\Phi\), so every \(+\partial c_j\) is derivable.

For each clause \(D_j\), the clause property supplies a literal \(\ell_j\in D_j\) with derivable \(+\partial\ell_j\). These literals are mutually consistent: no original variable and its complement can both have positive tags.

Define an assignment by setting \(x_i\) true whenever \(+\partial x_i\) is derivable, false whenever \(+\partial\bar x_i\) is derivable, and arbitrarily when neither is derivable. This is well-defined by consistency. Each chosen \(\ell_j\) is true under this assignment, so every clause is satisfied. Hence \(\Phi\) is satisfiable.

Size and conclusion

Writing \(s=\sum_j|D_j|\), the construction has

\(m=2(n+k+3),\qquad \tau=2,\qquad |R|=2n+s+5\leq 2n+3k+5.\)

Its total antecedent size is \(s+k+2\), and the masses have constant bit length. With indexed identifiers, its output length and construction time are \(O((n+k)\log(n+k+2))\).

This is a polynomial reduction whose outputs always satisfy the individual-rationality promise. Together with polynomial verification, it proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness. A polynomial-time algorithm for the stated problem would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proposed certificate contains both objects required by the task: a contradiction-free proposal \(P\) and one selectable superiority relation \(>\).

An \(r\times r\) Boolean matrix represents an arbitrary relation on the \(r=|R|\) rules. Its size, together with the proposal representation, is \(O(m+r^2)\).

The checks enforce the exact restrictions imposed by the task:

  • \(P\) contains no complementary pair.
  • Every superiority edge joins rules with complementary heads.
  • The directed relation is acyclic.

Acyclicity can be checked in \(O(r^2)\) time with the matrix representation. The combined \(O(m+r^2)\) table-operation bound is valid. There is no requirement to preserve \(>_0\), because the task explicitly permits replacing it completely.

2. Saturation and finite-proof semantics

The monotonicity claim is correct. Strong negation replaces positive tagged premises with negative tagged premises and conversely; it does not introduce tests for the absence of an established tag. All membership and superiority tests remain fixed for the chosen theory and relation.

Consequently, adding established tags cannot invalidate an inference condition.

Both directions of the correspondence with finite proofs are justified:

  • A newly added tag has a finite proof because its tagged premises already have finite proofs that can be concatenated before its inference.
  • Induction on proof position ensures that every finitely derivable tag eventually appears in saturation.

This also correctly handles cycles that cannot initiate a finite proof. The procedure never infers a negative tag merely because the corresponding positive tag is absent.

There are \(4m\) possible tags. Each nonterminal scan adds at least one previously absent tag, so at most \(4m+1\) scans are required.

3. Runtime and exact mass comparison

The scan bound \(O((m+1)(r+1)^2)\) is conservative and valid. Antecedent sets have size at most \(m\), and all supporting/opposing rule-pair checks can be bounded by \(r^2\) pairs. The negative inference conditions have the same bounded structure.

Thus the stated saturation bound
\[ O((m+1)^2(r+1)^2) \]
is valid.

Testing all support inclusions takes \(O(\tau(m+1))\) table operations.

The rational-arithmetic argument is also sound. Intermediate denominators can divide the product of the input denominators, whose bit length is \(O(L)\). Since all masses are positive and sum to one, partial-sum numerators have \(O(L)\) bits as well. Sequential schoolbook arithmetic therefore fits the stated \(O(\tau L^2)\) bound.

The conservative total bit-cost bound is polynomial in the requested parameters. Hence the promised problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). The verifier need not independently establish the individual-rationality promise.

4. Construction and definite tags

The reduction uses only defeasible rules and fixes \(F=\varnothing\) and \(>_0=\varnothing\). All introduced atoms are fresh, and all rules receive distinct labels.

With no facts or strict rules, \(+\Delta q\) is impossible for every literal. The strong-negative definite condition gives \(-\Delta q\) without tagged premises: \(q\notin F\), and the requirement concerning strict rules is vacuous.

These observations apply to every literal in the constructed universe, including complements of clause and goal atoms.

5. Variable-pair equivalences

For each variable atom \(y\), the only rule supporting \(y\) is \(u_y\), and the only opposing rule is \(v_y\). Both have empty antecedents.

The opposing rule cannot be discarded through a negative antecedent, because it has no antecedents. The defeat condition therefore requires exactly \(u_y>v_y\). Conversely, that edge and the available \(-\Delta\bar y\) establish \(+\partial y\).

The complementary equivalence follows identically. Acyclicity excludes the simultaneous edges \(u_y>v_y\) and \(v_y>u_y\), which would form a directed cycle.

Thus the claimed consistency of derivable variable literals is established directly for this construction.

6. Clause and goal equivalences

There are no rules with heads \(\bar c_j,\bar a,\bar b\). Their opposing-rule conditions are therefore vacuous.

Since the definite-positive alternative is unavailable, the listed supporting rules give exactly the three claimed equivalences:

  • A clause literal \(c_j\) is derivable precisely when some literal in its clause is derivable.
  • \(a\) is derivable precisely when \(z\) is derivable.
  • \(b\) is derivable precisely when either \(\bar z\) is derivable or all clause literals are derivable.

The necessity arguments respect the requirement that antecedent tags appear earlier in a finite proof. The sufficiency arguments correctly concatenate finite antecedent proofs.

7. Individual-rationality promise

The two supplied witnesses satisfy all selectable-relation restrictions.

The single edge \(u_z>v_z\) derives \(z\), then \(a\). The reverse single edge derives \(\bar z\), then \(b\). Each relation is acyclic and connects complementary heads.

Consequently, both singleton choices are rational for every source formula. The promise verification does not assume satisfiability.

8. Forward direction

Given a satisfying assignment, the selected superiority edges connect disjoint two-rule pairs, with only one direction chosen per pair. The resulting relation is acyclic.

The assignment’s true literals become derivable. Every clause has a true literal, so every \(c_j\) becomes derivable. The selected \(z\) direction derives \(a\), and the clause rule derives \(b\).

The proposal \(\{a,b\}\) is contradiction-free because \(a\) and \(b\) are distinct fresh atoms. It contains both supported choices and therefore receives mass one.

9. Reverse direction

With two masses equal to \(1/2\), strictly exceeding half requires supporting both types. Hence any winning proposal contains both \(a\) and \(b\).

Derivability of \(a\) forces derivability of \(z\). The variable-pair equivalences and acyclicity then exclude derivability of \(\bar z\). Therefore the derivation of \(b\) must use \(r_\Phi\), forcing every clause literal to be derivable.

Each clause consequently contains a derivable original-variable literal. No variable and its complement can both be derivable, so the proposed assignment is well-defined. Arbitrary values for variables with neither tag do not alter the truth of the already selected clause witnesses.

Every clause is satisfied. The reverse implication is therefore valid.

10. Edge cases and construction size

Empty clauses cause no problem: their clause atoms have no supporting rules, so they cannot become derivable. The reverse argument consequently excludes a winning proposal.

If there are no clauses, \(r_\Phi\) has an empty antecedent set, and its universal antecedent requirement is vacuous. This agrees with satisfiability of the empty conjunction.

The counts are correct:
\[ m=2(n+k+3),\qquad \tau=2,\qquad |R|=2n+s+5, \]
and the total antecedent size is \(s+k+2\). Since \(s\leq 3k\), the stated output-size and construction-time bounds are polynomial.
