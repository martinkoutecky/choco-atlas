The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite schools \(C\), overlapping labels \(T\), complete applicant types \(\Theta\) containing membership sets, strict preferences, and priority blocks at every school, rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), and per-capita bounds \(\bar q_c\) and \(\bar p_{c,t}\), compute the mass assignment \(x_{\theta,c,t}\) produced by DA-OT: schools first accept highest-priority proposal mass up to each \(\bar p_{c,t}\), then fill capacity up to \(\bar q_c\), splitting mass when a quota cuts through a type block. Determine whether this stable, student-optimal assignment is computable in time polynomial in the compressed input size and rational bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be finite schools and \(T\) overlapping labels. Each complete applicant type \(\theta\in\Theta\) has rational mass \(\mu_\theta\), a nonempty membership set \(\tau(\theta)\subseteq T\), and a strict preference over eligible contracts \((c,t)\) with \(t\in\tau(\theta)\). Each school strictly orders the contract-type priority blocks \((\theta,c,t)\); mass within a block is indistinguishable. All contracts are acceptable.
Each school has rational capacity \(\bar q_c\) and soft reserves \(\bar p_{c,t}\), with \(\sum_t\bar p_{c,t}\leq\bar q_c\) and \(\sum_c\bar q_c\geq\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\). Its choice rule first retains the highest-priority proposed mass for each \(t\), up to \(\bar p_{c,t}\), then retains the highest-priority remaining proposed mass up to total capacity \(\bar q_c\). A quota may split a block. Each unit of mass occupies at most one contract, so membership in several labels does not fill several reserves.
In student-proposing DA-OT, rejected mass proposes to its next preferred contract; schools reconsider held and new proposals by this choice rule. Let \(x_{\theta,c,t}\) be the resulting assignment mass.

Is exact computation of \(x_{\theta,c,t}\) from the compressed rational input in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

For finite individual applicants, DA-OT computes the student-optimal stable matching in \(O(|X|^3)\) time, where \(X\) is the contract set. Continuum deferred acceptance has also been studied with adaptive priority rules, but that result uses a different school choice rule.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact computation is polynomial-time solvable in the compressed rational input. The key is to replace each contract by two consecutive alternatives—reserve and open—and apply the strongly polynomial algorithm for stable allocation in ordered polymatroid intersection. The proof below verifies that this computes the specified DA outcome.

1. Scaling and the expanded contracts

Let \(E_0\) be the set of eligible contract-type blocks, and write \(e=(\theta,c,t)\).

Choose a common denominator \(D\) for all masses, capacities, and reserves. Set

\(m_\theta=D\mu_\theta,\qquad q_c=D\bar q_c,\qquad p_{c,t}=D\bar p_{c,t}.\)

These are nonnegative integers. Taking the product of the input denominators suffices, so their binary encoding lengths are polynomial in the input length. Scaling commutes with every choice operation.

For each \(e\in E_0\), introduce two coordinates \(e^R,e^O\). Replace each applicant preference list

\(e_1\succ_\theta e_2\succ_\theta\cdots\)

by

\(e_1^R\succ_\theta e_1^O\succ_\theta e_2^R\succ_\theta e_2^O\succ_\theta\cdots.\)

Let \(E\) denote this expanded ground set. Thus \(|E|=2|E_0|\).

Write \(E_\theta\) for the coordinates belonging to type \(\theta\), \(E_c\) for those belonging to school \(c\), \(R_{c,t}\) for its reserve coordinates of label \(t\), and \(O_c\) for its open coordinates.

The expanded feasibility constraints are

\(z(E_\theta)\le m_\theta\quad(\theta\in\Theta),\)

\(z(E_c)\le q_c\quad(c\in C),\qquad z(R_{c,t})\le p_{c,t}\quad(c\in C,\ t\in T),\)

together with \(z\ge0\). Here \(z(A)=\sum_{a\in A}z_a\).

Each school ranks all reserve coordinates above all open coordinates. Within either class, it uses its original strict block priority. It chooses greedily in this order, respecting its displayed capacity constraints.

The applicant constraint counts both versions together. Consequently, the expansion does not duplicate applicant mass.

2. The expansion preserves deferred acceptance

We first establish this independently of any polynomial-time algorithm.

Temporarily interpret the scaled population as \(m_\theta\) individual copies of each type. This potentially enormous expansion is used only for the proof. Within each block, break priority ties by increasing copy index, consistently throughout.

Tag each contract currently held in original DA as reserved or open according to its school’s two-stage choice. We claim that an original school update can be simulated by expanded DA, with these tags indicating which version is held.

Suppose a school receives new original proposals. Let \(B\) comprise these proposals and its previous holdings.

The following invariant holds before the update:

  • For every label \(t\), the reserved holdings are the highest-priority holdings of that label, up to \(p_{c,t}\).
  • If any open holding has label \(t\), then the reserve for \(t\) is full, and every reserved holding of that label has higher priority than every open holding of that label.

Submit each new proposal first to its reserve version. Reserve proposals are considered before all open proposals. Moreover,

\(\sum_t p_{c,t}\le q_c,\)

so total school capacity never prevents a reserve selection that satisfies all label reserve bounds.

It follows that the new reserve holdings are precisely the highest-priority \(p_{c,t}\) proposals of each label in \(B\), or all such proposals if fewer exist. Indeed, previous open holdings cannot improve this reserve selection: whenever such holdings exist, the previous reserve was already full with higher-priority proposals.

Now send every rejected reserve proposal—including displaced reserve holdings—to its immediately following open version. Together with previous open holdings, these are exactly the proposals in \(B\) remaining after reserve selection. Greedy open selection therefore retains their highest-priority members up to

\(q_c-\sum_t z(R_{c,t}).\)

This is exactly the original second stage.

For completeness, intermediate open rejections cause no discrepancy. Reserve holdings never decrease in number: each label retains its highest-priority proposals up to a fixed bound, and reserve choices are unaffected by open proposals. Thus residual open capacity never increases. Adding further open proposals cannot rescue an already rejected open proposal. We may therefore finish all reserve-to-open transitions before advancing any open rejection to its next original contract.

This proves the invariant inductively and simulates every original DA update. A unit advances to its next original contract exactly when its open version is rejected. Hence, if \(d\) is the expanded DA allocation, the original scaled outcome satisfies

\(D x_{\theta,c,t}=d_{e^R}+d_{e^O}.\)

The same argument applies to block batches: the aggregate choice depends only on their counts. It therefore also applies directly to the rational-mass DA in the question.

3. The expanded constraints are two polymatroids

For a normalized, nondecreasing, submodular function \(g:2^E\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), write

\(P(g)=\{z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^E:z(A)\le g(A)\text{ for every }A\subseteq E\}.\)

Define the applicant rank function by

\(h(A)=\sum_{\theta:\,A\cap E_\theta\ne\varnothing}m_\theta.\)

This is a weighted coverage function, hence normalized, nondecreasing, and submodular. Its polymatroid is exactly

\(P(h)=\{z\ge0:z(E_\theta)\le m_\theta\text{ for every }\theta\}.\)

For each school, define

\(f_c(A)=\min\!\left\{q_c,\ q_c\mathbf 1_{\{A\cap O_c\ne\varnothing\}}+\sum_t p_{c,t}\mathbf 1_{\{A\cap R_{c,t}\ne\varnothing\}}\right\},\qquad A\subseteq E_c.\)

The expression inside the minimum is another weighted coverage function. Truncation at \(q_c\) preserves monotonicity and submodularity. In this particular formula, this can be checked directly: adding a coordinate contributes zero if its class is already represented; otherwise it contributes the smaller of its class weight and the remaining distance to \(q_c\). This marginal contribution cannot increase when the starting set grows.

We claim that

\(P(f_c)=\{z\ge0:z(E_c)\le q_c,\ z(R_{c,t})\le p_{c,t}\text{ for every }t\}.\)

The forward implication follows by applying the rank inequalities to \(E_c\) and each nonempty \(R_{c,t}\). For the reverse implication, consider any \(A\subseteq E_c\).

  • If \(A\) contains an open coordinate, then \(f_c(A)=q_c\), and the school capacity bounds \(z(A)\).
  • Otherwise \(A\) contains only reserve coordinates. Its mass is bounded both by \(q_c\) and by the sum of the reserve bounds of the labels represented in \(A\). Their minimum is \(f_c(A)\).

Taking the direct sum,

\(f(A)=\sum_c f_c(A\cap E_c),\)

gives precisely all expanded school constraints.

Finally, choose a total order \(\succ_H\) on \(E\) by concatenating the expanded applicant lists, and choose a total order \(\succ_F\) consistent with every expanded school order. The instance is now a pair of totally ordered polymatroids,

\((E,h,\succ_H)\quad\text{and}\quad(E,f,\succ_F).\)

4. The algorithmic theorem and the relevant stability condition

Iwata and Yokoi give an algorithm that computes a stable allocation for two totally ordered polymatroids in \(O(n^3\gamma)\) time, where \(n\) is the ground-set size and \(\gamma\) is the time for a saturation or exchange-capacity calculation. For integer-valued rank functions its output is integral. Their output \(y\) also dominates every stable allocation \(z\) in every prefix of the first order:

\(y(\{a:a\succeq_H b\})\ge z(\{a:a\succeq_H b\})\quad(b\in E).\)

See the algorithmic bound and Theorem 7.7 in [Iwata–Yokoi, *Finding a Stable Allocation in Polymatroid Intersection*](https://www.keisu.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/data/2017/METR17-02.pdf).

It remains essential to identify this output with expanded DA.

In the ordered-polymatroid definition, a side is willing to increase coordinate \(e\) if it can either increase that coordinate alone or increase it while decreasing a strictly worse coordinate. Stability means that no coordinate has both sides willing.

For our constraints, unwillingness has a useful explicit certificate:

A side is unwilling to increase \(e\) if and only if some displayed capacity set \(S\) containing \(e\) is tight and every positive coordinate in \(S\) is ranked at least as high as \(e\).

Here the applicant capacity sets are \(E_\theta\); the school capacity sets are \(E_c\) and \(R_{c,t}\).

To prove the characterization, note that all capacity sets containing a fixed coordinate form a chain. If none is tight, a sufficiently small increase is feasible. Otherwise let \(S\) be the smallest tight set containing \(e\). If \(S\) contains a positive, strictly worse coordinate \(v\), increasing \(e\) and decreasing \(v\) by a sufficiently small common amount preserves every tight constraint. It also preserves all initially slack constraints when the amount is small enough. Conversely, a tight set containing only coordinates ranked at least as high as \(e\) prevents both kinds of improvement.

This proves the characterization, including boundary cases involving zero capacities.

5. Why the computed optimum is exactly expanded DA

We supply the finite-to-compressed identification to avoid assuming that an arbitrary stable allocation suffices.

First, consider the finite expanded instance from Section 2. Each school’s feasible sets form a matroid: take the direct sum of uniform matroids on the reserve classes and the free matroid on open contracts, then truncate at \(q_c\).

We use the following finite DA fact, with its proof.

Finite DA lemma. With unit-demand applicants and greedy ordered-matroid choices on the receiving side, applicant-proposing DA is stable and gives every applicant its best contract among all stable outcomes.

For a greedy matroid choice \(G\), a contract is rejected from a set exactly when that set contains a circuit in which the contract has lowest priority. Thus rejection persists as the offered set grows. Also, deleting rejected contracts does not change a greedy scan. Consequently,

\(G(G(A)\cup B)=G(A\cup B).\)

DA holdings therefore equal the choice from cumulative proposals. At termination every contract an applicant prefers to its assignment has been rejected, which proves stability.

To prove optimality, fix a stable outcome \(M\). At each school, let \(B\) contain its contracts in \(M\) and every contract applicants prefer to their assignments in \(M\). Stability and the displayed identity imply \(G(B)=M_c\): adding any one of these desired contracts leaves \(M_c\) unchanged, and hence so does adding all of them.

Suppose a contract belonging to \(M\) is rejected for the first time. Before that rejection, every proposal belongs to \(B\), since an applicant cannot reach a contract worse than its assignment in \(M\) without first having that assignment rejected. Rejection persistence would then reject the same contract from \(B\), contradicting \(G(B)=M_c\). No contract of \(M\) is ever rejected. Each applicant’s DA assignment is therefore at least as good as its assignment in \(M\). This proves the lemma.

This argument does not require a school’s choice on hypothetical proposal sets to enforce applicant unit demand; actual DA holdings satisfy unit demand because only unheld applicants propose.

We next relate finite stable outcomes to compressed stable allocations.

Aggregation. The aggregate vector of any stable finite expanded outcome is stable for the two compressed polymatroids.

Otherwise some coordinate \(e\) would have both sides willing. Applicant willingness supplies a copy assigned below \(e\), or unmatched. School willingness permits adding one unit at \(e\), possibly removing a contract from a strictly lower-priority block. The amount can be one because all capacities and allocations are integral. This produces a blocking contract in the finite instance, a contradiction.

Lifting. Every integral compressed stable allocation \(z\) can be realized as a stable finite expanded outcome.

For each type, assign copies in increasing index order to its expanded contracts in decreasing preference order, using the prescribed counts \(z_e\); leave remaining copies unmatched.

Suppose copy \(i\) wants a contract in block \(e\). The applicant side is then willing to increase \(e\) at \(z\). Compressed stability supplies a school certificate \(S\) from Section 4: \(S\) is full and all its occupied blocks rank at least as high as \(e\).

Every copy actually assigned to block \(e\) has smaller index than \(i\), by the construction. Thus all contracts filling \(S\) have strictly higher finite priority than the proposed contract of copy \(i\). The school rejects that proposal. No blocking contract exists, proving the lifting claim.

Now let \(d\) be the aggregate expanded DA outcome, and let \(y\) be the Iwata–Yokoi output.

The vector \(d\) is compressed stable by aggregation. Therefore the algorithm’s optimality gives, for every prefix \(P\) of \(\succ_H\),

\(y(P)\ge d(P).\)

The vector \(y\) is integral, so lift it to a finite stable outcome. The finite DA lemma says that each copy weakly prefers its DA assignment to its assignment in this lift. Consequently, for every prefix of every expanded applicant list, the DA count is at least the count under \(y\).

Because \(\succ_H\) concatenates those lists, summing these inequalities gives

\(d(P)\ge y(P)\)

for every prefix \(P\) of \(\succ_H\). Thus all prefix sums coincide. Taking differences of consecutive prefix sums yields

\(d=y.\)

Combined with Section 2, this proves that the required output is exactly

\(x_{\theta,c,t}=\dfrac{y_{e^R}+y_{e^O}}{D}.\)

6. Polynomial complexity on the compressed input

No finite population expansion is performed by the algorithm.

For either constructed polymatroid, write its explicit constraints as \(z(S)\le b_S\). Saturation capacity at coordinate \(u\) is

\(\widehat c(z,u)=\min_{S:\,u\in S}(b_S-z(S)).\)

For distinct coordinates \(u,v\), feasible exchange capacity is

\(\overline c(z,u,v)=\min\!\left\{z_v,\ \min_{S:\,u\in S,\ v\notin S}(b_S-z(S))\right\},\)

where an empty inner minimum is \(+\infty\).

These formulas follow directly by substituting \(z+\alpha\mathbf 1_u\) and \(z+\alpha(\mathbf 1_u-\mathbf 1_v)\) into the explicit inequalities and nonnegativity constraints.

After discarding empty sets, there are \(O(|E|)\) constraints with \(O(|E|)\) total incidences. Each oracle calculation therefore takes \(O(|E|)\) arithmetic operations by direct summation. The cited algorithm consequently uses \(O(|E|^4)\) arithmetic operations with this straightforward implementation.

The scaled integer data have polynomial binary length, and these are explicit polynomial-bit capacity oracles for a strongly polynomial algorithm. Hence the computation has polynomial bit complexity as well. Division by \(D\) gives the exact rational output.

The finite-applicant runtime alone would not establish this conclusion: its population expansion can be exponentially large. The ordered-polymatroid reduction supplies the required algorithm on compressed blocks.
