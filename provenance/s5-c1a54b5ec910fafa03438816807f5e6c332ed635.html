The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a fixed set of \(N\) samples and \(L\) labels, a finite support \(\Theta\) of worker types with masses \(\mu_\theta\), and each type's group and complete update-relevant annotation profile, find matrices \(D_\theta\) minimizing the mass-weighted analogue of the paper's \(D_w\)-update objective: the expected squared fitting loss plus the within-group pairwise \(\operatorname{HSIC}\) term integrated against \(\mu\), plus the mass-weighted ADMM penalty, with group-commonality matrices, group weights, and ADMM states fixed. What convexity condition and algorithmic complexity hold as functions of \(N\), \(L\), support size \(\tau\), and input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) worker types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Write \(m\) for the number of labels and \(L\) for total input bit length.
There are \(N\geq1\) samples, \(m\geq2\) labels, and \(M\) nonempty groups. Each type has a group \(g(\theta)\) and a nonzero annotation matrix \(A_\theta\in\{-1,0,1\}^{N\times m}\), where zero denotes an unspecified annotation.
Fixed rational inputs are \(C_g\in\mathbb Q^{m\times m}\), group weights \(\lambda_g>0\) summing to \(1\), coefficients \(\beta,\rho>0\), and ADMM states \(S_\theta,T_\theta\in\mathbb Q^{m\times m}\); every row of \(S_\theta\) has Euclidean norm \(1\).
Use linear kernels on matrix rows: \(H=I_m-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/m\), \(K(X)=XX^\top\), and \(h(X,Y)=(m-1)^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(K(X)HK(Y)H)\).
Jointly optimize unrestricted matrices \(D_\theta\in\mathbb R^{m\times m}\), with objective
\(F(D)=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\lambda_{g(\theta)}^2\|A_\theta-A_\theta(D_\theta+C_{g(\theta)})\|_F^2-\beta\sum_{\theta,\phi:g(\theta)=g(\phi)}\mu_\theta\mu_\phi h(D_\theta,D_\phi)+\rho\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\|D_\theta-S_\theta+T_\theta\|_F^2.\)
The pair sum is ordered and includes equal types: it represents independent draws of distinct individuals from a nonatomic population, which may share a type. The threshold problem asks, for rational \(q\), whether some \(D\) satisfies \(F(D)\leq q\).

Under what input conditions is \(F\) jointly convex, and is its threshold decision problem in \(\mathrm P\) or NP-hard?
What is the complexity of finding a global minimizer when one exists, with running times stated in \(N,m,\tau,M,L\) and any accuracy parameter?

For finite workers, AMCC states a sufficient convexity condition \(\rho/\beta\geq4m(W-1)\) for its worker-update subproblem, where \(W\) is the worker count. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.02789).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Renamed the label count to m so L can denote total input bit length.
  • Normalized worker masses to a probability distribution; pairwise terms use the unconditioned product measure without group-mass normalization.
  • Specified linear kernels on matrix rows because the kernel and observation orientation are unspecified; retained the empirical HSIC normalization using the label count.
  • Interpreted the graded request as simultaneous optimization of all type matrices, since only commonality matrices, weights, and ADMM states were fixed.
  • Used the unrestricted D-domain of the ADMM update; row normalization applies to fixed auxiliary matrices S, rather than imposing additional probability or row-sum constraints on D.
  • Included equal-type pairs because excluding the same individual in a nonatomic society does not exclude distinct individuals of the same type.
  • Assigned each fitting term its type's own group commonality and weight, resolving inconsistent group summation notation according to the intended worker partition.
  • Fixed the group-weight exponent at two, the experimental setting, and required rational fixed inputs.
  • Defined a threshold decision problem and asked for minimizers only when they exist, since the unrestricted update domain does not itself guarantee attainment.

Result for the stated formulation

For every admissible input, \(F\) is nonconvex and unbounded below. Consequently, every rational threshold has answer YES, so the threshold problem is in \(\mathrm P\). No admissible instance has a global minimizer.

This conclusion uses the expressly stated unrestricted domain and inclusion of equal-type pairs. The unit row norms of \(S_\theta\) impose no constraint on \(D_\theta\).

1. The interaction contains a negative quartic term

The centering matrix satisfies \(H^\top=H\) and \(H^2=H\). By cyclic invariance of trace,

\[ h(X,Y)=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(XX^\top HYY^\top H)}{(m-1)^2} =\frac{\|X^\top HY\|_F^2}{(m-1)^2}. \]

In particular, \(h(X,0)=0\), while \(h(X,X)\) can be positive.

Choose any supported type \(\theta_0\), and let \(e_1\) denote the first standard basis vector in \(\mathbb R^m\). Define \(E=e_1e_1^\top\), and consider the line

\[ D_{\theta_0}(t)=tE,\qquad D_\theta(t)=0\quad(\theta\ne\theta_0). \]

Because \(e_1^\top He_1=(m-1)/m\),

\[ E^\top HE=\frac{m-1}{m}E, \qquad h(tE,tE)=\frac{t^4}{m^2}. \]

Every interaction involving another type vanishes on this line. Thus the ordered pair sum contributes exactly \(-\beta\mu_{\theta_0}^2t^4/m^2\).

For explicit coefficients, write \(R_\theta=A_\theta(I_m-C_{g(\theta)})\), \(U_\theta=S_\theta-T_\theta\), and \(g_0=g(\theta_0)\). Expanding the squared norms gives

\[ F(D(t))=c+at^2+bt-kt^4, \]

where

\[ c=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta \left(\lambda_{g(\theta)}^2\|R_\theta\|_F^2+\rho\|U_\theta\|_F^2\right), \]

\[ a=\mu_{\theta_0} \left(\lambda_{g_0}^2\|A_{\theta_0}E\|_F^2+\rho\right)>0, \]

\[ b=-2\mu_{\theta_0} \left(\lambda_{g_0}^2\langle R_{\theta_0},A_{\theta_0}E\rangle_F +\rho\langle U_{\theta_0},E\rangle_F\right), \qquad k=\frac{\beta\mu_{\theta_0}^2}{m^2}>0. \]

Here \(\langle X,Y\rangle_F=\operatorname{tr}(X^\top Y)\). All four coefficients are rational.

2. Nonconvexity and absence of minimizers

Since \(k>0\), the negative quartic term dominates the lower-degree terms:

\[ \lim_{t\to+\infty}F(D(t))=-\infty. \]

Therefore \(\inf_D F(D)=-\infty\). Every finite collection of real matrices has a finite objective value, so no global minimizer exists.

Unboundedness alone would not establish nonconvexity. To prove that separately, let \(p(t)=F(D(t))\). Convexity of \(F\) would imply convexity of its restriction to this line, and hence the midpoint inequality \(p(0)\leq(p(t)+p(-t))/2\). But

\[ \frac{p(t)+p(-t)}2-p(0)=at^2-kt^4<0 \quad\text{whenever}\quad t^2>a/k. \]

This violates the midpoint inequality.

Thus there are no input conditions within the stated admissible class that make \(F\) jointly convex. In particular, increasing the finite ratio \(\rho/\beta\) cannot restore convexity or boundedness.

3. Threshold witnesses and running times

For every rational \(q\), the limit above proves that some \(D\) satisfies \(F(D)\leq q\). A rational witness can also be constructed explicitly.

Using the coefficients from Section 1, set

\[ B=a+|b|+|c-q|, \qquad t=\left\lceil 1+\frac{B}{k}\right\rceil. \]

Then \(t\geq1\) and \(kt^2\geq B\). Consequently,

\[ \begin{aligned} F(D(t))-q &=c-q+at^2+bt-kt^4\\ &\leq |c-q|+at^2+|b|t-kt^4\\ &\leq Bt^2-kt^4\\ &\leq0. \end{aligned} \]

Hence the integer matrix \(tE\), together with zero matrices for the other types, is a threshold witness.

The complexity statements are as follows.

  • Threshold decision on promised admissible inputs: return YES, taking \(O(1)\) time after input access. Reading the complete input takes \(O(L)\) time.
  • Constructing an explicit witness: computing the coefficients requires \(O(\tau Nm^2+\tau m^2+1)\) rational arithmetic operations. Writing all matrices requires \(O(\tau m^2)\) entries.
  • Bit complexity: the coefficients and \(t\) have \(O(L)\)-bit representations. One conservative bound for witness construction, including reading the input and writing the matrices, is \(O((\tau Nm^2+(\tau+M)m^2+1)L^3)\) bit operations.

For the bit-length assertion, take the product of all input denominators as a common denominator. Its bit length is at most \(O(L)\). The coefficients above are sums of polynomially many terms of bounded degree in the input rationals, so a fixed power of that common denominator suffices. Their numerators also have \(O(L)\) bits. Rational division and the ceiling defining \(t\) preserve this bound. The displayed running time allows \(O(L^3)\) bit operations per exact rational operation and is not intended to be optimal.

There is no minimizer-finding case under the given hypotheses, so no accuracy parameter can yield an additive approximation to a finite global optimum. For any desired finite objective target, however, the witness construction applies.

4. Why the cited worker-update condition does not transfer

The cited paper’s Theorem 1 concerns a worker-update subproblem; its Equation (16) excludes the worker’s self-pair. Its unit row constraints are placed on the auxiliary matrix. These are material differences from the present joint objective. [AMCC, Equation (16) and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.02789)

With the other matrices fixed and self-pairs excluded, the interaction is quadratic in the updated matrix. Here the included self-pair is quartic. Even freezing every other type therefore leaves a negative quartic leading term in the single-type subproblem.

Moreover, deleting equal-type pairs would still not generally repair joint optimization. If two distinct types share a group, setting both matrices to \(tE\) and all others to zero produces the interaction

\[ -\frac{2\beta\mu_\theta\mu_\phi}{m^2}t^4, \]

while the remaining terms grow at most quadratically. Thus that modified joint problem would also be unbounded below whenever such a pair exists. A nonvacuous global-minimization problem requires an additional change to the domain or objective.
