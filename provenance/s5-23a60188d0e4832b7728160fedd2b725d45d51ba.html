The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given doctor types \(I\) and hospital types \(J\), rational masses \(a_i,b_j\), acceptable type pairs \(E\subseteq I\times J\), complete weak rankings over acceptable partner types, and a closure set \(S\subseteq J\), decide whether a rational flow \(x_{ij}\ge 0\) exists with \(\sum_j x_{ij}\le a_i\) and \(\sum_i x_{ij}\le b_j\) that is stable. For each type pair \((i,j)\), let \(u_i=a_i-\sum_k x_{ik}\) and \(v_j=b_j-\sum_\ell x_{\ell j}\), and define \(D^w_{ij}=u_i+\sum_{k:\,j\succeq_i k}x_{ik}\), \(D^s_{ij}=u_i+\sum_{k:\,j\succ_i k}x_{ik}\), \(H^w_{ij}=\sum_{\ell:\,i\succeq_j\ell}x_{\ell j}+\mathbf{1}_{j\notin S}v_j\), and \(H^s_{ij}=\sum_{\ell:\,i\succ_j\ell}x_{\ell j}+\mathbf{1}_{j\notin S}v_j\). Stability requires that neither \(D^s_{ij}>0\) and \(H^w_{ij}>0\), nor \(D^w_{ij}>0\) and \(H^s_{ij}>0\). For the anchored restricted case, require at most three acceptable partner types per type and require each doctor type to rank every adjacent hospital type in \(S\) above every adjacent type outside \(S\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: disjoint finite sets of doctor types \(I\) and hospital types \(J\), with positive binary rational masses \(a_i,b_j\). Masses need not sum to one or have equal totals on the two sides. Let \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) be the support size, not the number of possible types, \(m=|E|\), and \(L\) the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Input includes acceptable pairs \(E\subseteq I\times J\), a closure set \(S\subseteq J\), and complete transitive weak rankings over each type's acceptable partners. Every acceptable partner is strictly preferred to being unmatched; ties are retained. Each type has at most three acceptable partners, and each doctor strictly prefers every adjacent hospital in \(S\) to every adjacent hospital outside \(S\).
A feasible flow has \(x_{ij}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) for \((i,j)\in E\), with \(x_{ij}=0\) otherwise, \(\sum_jx_{ij}\le a_i\), and \(\sum_ix_{ij}\le b_j\). Define \(u_i=a_i-\sum_kx_{ik}\) and \(v_j=b_j-\sum_\ell x_{\ell j}\).
For each \((i,j)\in E\), define \(D^w_{ij}=u_i+\sum_{k:j\succeq_i k}x_{ik}\) and \(D^s_{ij}=u_i+\sum_{k:j\succ_i k}x_{ik}\).
Define \(H^w_{ij}=\sum_{\ell:i\succeq_j\ell}x_{\ell j}+\mathbf{1}_{j\notin S}v_j\) and \(H^s_{ij}=\sum_{\ell:i\succ_j\ell}x_{\ell j}+\mathbf{1}_{j\notin S}v_j\). All preference sums range over acceptable partners.
A flow is stable if, for every acceptable pair, neither \((D^s_{ij}>0\ \land\ H^w_{ij}>0)\) nor \((D^w_{ij}>0\ \land\ H^s_{ij}>0)\) holds. These tests apply also when \(x_{ij}>0\); unused hospital mass in \(S\) cannot participate in blocking.

Is deciding whether such a rational stable flow exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

The discrete one-to-one counterpart is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete under the same degree and doctor-ranking restrictions. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02666v1#S3)
No result settling this exact continuous flow problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Masses are strictly positive binary rationals, with no normalization or equality of side totals imposed, preserving the unrestricted sizes of the two discrete groups.
  • Every acceptable partner is strictly preferred to being unmatched, as required by the discrete definition; ties among acceptable partners are retained without tie-breaking.
  • Flow is zero on unacceptable pairs, and all blocking tests and preference sums are restricted to acceptable pairs and partners.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when every mass equals \(1\).

The key point is a rounding theorem: under the stated blocking tests, every stable flow with unit masses can be rounded to a stable one-to-one matching. Thus the discrete hardness result does transfer to this continuous problem.

1. Positive-flow edges force saturation and ties

For a feasible flow \(x\), define its positive support by \(F=\{(i,j)\in E:x_{ij}>0\}\). Call a type active if it is incident to \(F\).

Lemma 1. If \(x\) is stable, then:

  • Every active doctor is fully assigned.
  • All positive-flow partners of any doctor are tied in that doctor's ranking.
  • All positive-flow partners of any hospital are tied in that hospital's ranking.
  • Every active hospital outside \(S\) is fully assigned.

Proof. Fix \((i,j)\in F\). The weak preference sums include \(x_{ij}\), so \(D^w_{ij}\ge x_{ij}>0\) and \(H^w_{ij}\ge x_{ij}>0\). Stability consequently requires \(D^s_{ij}=H^s_{ij}=0\).

All summands in these quantities are nonnegative. Therefore \(D^s_{ij}=0\) implies \(u_i=0\), proving the first assertion.

If two positive-flow partners \(j,k\) of doctor \(i\) were not tied, completeness of the weak ranking would give, after possibly exchanging them, \(j\succ_i k\). Then \(D^s_{ij}\ge x_{ik}>0\), a contradiction. The same argument using \(H^s\) proves the third assertion.

Finally, if \(j\notin S\), then \(H^s_{ij}\) includes \(v_j\). Hence \(v_j=0\). \(\square\)

This lemma uses explicitly the requirement that blocking tests apply to positive-flow edges.

2. A sufficient condition for stability-preserving rounding

Lemma 2. Let \(x\) be stable, with positive support \(F\). Suppose \(y\) is a feasible flow satisfying:

  • \(y_{ij}=0\) whenever \((i,j)\notin F\);
  • every doctor active in \(x\) remains fully assigned in \(y\);
  • every hospital outside \(S\) active in \(x\) remains fully assigned in \(y\).

Then \(y\) is stable.

Proof. We compare the four blocking quantities for every acceptable pair, including pairs outside \(F\).

For a doctor inactive in \(x\), both flows assign zero mass, so both doctor quantities equal \(a_i\).

For an active doctor \(i\), Lemma 1 says that its positive-flow partners belong to a single tie class and its assigned mass equals \(a_i\). The same holds under \(y\): its assigned mass is still \(a_i\), and its partners form a subset of that tie class. Completeness and transitivity of the ranking therefore give, for every adjacent \(j\), the exact equalities \(D^w_{ij}(y)=D^w_{ij}(x)\) and \(D^s_{ij}(y)=D^s_{ij}(x)\). Indeed, each preference sum includes either the entire assigned tie class or none of it.

Now consider a hospital \(j\notin S\). If it is inactive in \(x\), it is inactive in \(y\), and both hospital quantities equal \(b_j\) because of vacancy. If it is active, both flows fill it completely, and all assigned doctors belong to the same tie class by Lemma 1. Thus \(H^w_{ij}(y)=H^w_{ij}(x)\) and \(H^s_{ij}(y)=H^s_{ij}(x)\).

For a hospital \(j\in S\), vacancy contributes nothing. If it is inactive in \(x\), both hospital quantities vanish under both flows. Otherwise, its positive-flow doctors under \(x\) belong to one tie class, and those under \(y\) form a subset of that class. Each hospital preference sum therefore includes either all of the assigned mass or none. Consequently, for \(r\in\{w,s\}\),

\(H^r_{ij}(y)>0\ \Longrightarrow\ H^r_{ij}(x)>0\).

This implication also covers the possibility that \(y\) assigns zero mass to \(j\).

If either forbidden conjunction held under \(y\), the equalities for the doctor quantities and the implications for the hospital quantities would make the same conjunction hold under \(x\). That contradicts stability of \(x\). \(\square\)

3. Integral rounding and a polynomial-size rational certificate

We first prove the rounding assertion for arbitrary positive integer masses.

Lemma 3. With integer masses, every stable flow has a stable flow whose edge values are integers.

Proof. Let \(x\) be stable, and retain its positive support \(F\). Construct a directed circulation network with vertices \(s,t\), the doctors, and the hospitals. Its arcs and bounds are:

| Arc | Lower bound | Upper bound |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(s\to i\), doctor \(i\) active | \(a_i\) | \(a_i\) |
| \(s\to i\), doctor \(i\) inactive | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(i\to j\), for \((i,j)\in F\) | \(0\) | \(\min\{a_i,b_j\}\) |
| \(j\to t\), hospital \(j\notin S\) active | \(b_j\) | \(b_j\) |
| \(j\to t\), hospital \(j\in S\) active | \(0\) | \(b_j\) |
| \(j\to t\), hospital \(j\) inactive | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(t\to s\) | \(0\) | \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i\) |

The original flow supplies a feasible circulation: put \(x_{ij}\) on doctor-to-hospital arcs, its row and column totals on the corresponding outer arcs, and its total assigned mass on \(t\to s\). Lemma 1 verifies all prescribed saturation bounds.

We use the standard integral-circulation theorem: a directed network with integer lower and upper bounds has an integer feasible circulation whenever it has a feasible circulation. Its hypotheses hold here. The theorem follows by subtracting lower bounds, reducing feasibility to a maximum-flow problem with integer capacities, and applying maximum-flow integrality.

Take such an integer circulation, and let \(y\) be its values on doctor-to-hospital arcs. The arc bounds make \(y\) feasible, supported on \(F\), and saturated at every vertex required by Lemma 2. Thus \(y\) is stable. \(\square\)

For rational masses, write \(a_i=p_i/q_i\) and \(b_j=r_j/s_j\), with positive integer numerators and denominators. Set \(Q=\prod_{i\in I}q_i\prod_{j\in J}s_j\).

Multiplying every mass and flow value by \(Q\) multiplies every blocking quantity by \(Q\), preserving stability. Lemma 3 applies to the resulting integer masses. Dividing its integer stable flow by \(Q\) proves:

Corollary. Whenever a rational stable flow exists, one exists of the form \(x_{ij}=z_{ij}/Q\), where every \(z_{ij}\) is a nonnegative integer.

The binary length of \(Q\) is \(O(L)\). Each scaled mass also has \(O(L)\) bits, and \(z_{ij}\le Qa_i\). Thus all edge numerators together require \(O(mL)\) bits.

4. Unit masses give exactly the discrete existence problem

Suppose every mass equals \(1\). Lemma 3 turns any stable flow into an integer stable flow. Feasibility then forces every edge value to lie in \(\{0,1\}\), with at most one positive edge incident to each type. Its positive edges therefore form a one-to-one matching.

Conversely, let \(M\) be a one-to-one matching and let \(x\) be its incidence flow. For an edge outside \(M\):

  • \(D^w_{ij}\) and \(D^s_{ij}\) indicate, respectively, weak and strict doctor willingness to deviate; both equal \(1\) for an unmatched doctor.
  • At a matched hospital, \(H^w_{ij}\) and \(H^s_{ij}\) indicate the corresponding weak and strict hospital comparisons.
  • At an unmatched hospital outside \(S\), both hospital quantities equal \(1\).
  • At an unmatched hospital in \(S\), both hospital quantities equal \(0\).

Hence the two forbidden conjunctions are exactly the discrete blocking condition: both endpoints weakly favor the deviation, at least one strictly favors it, and an unmatched hospital in \(S\) cannot participate.

For an edge in \(M\), \(D^s_{ij}=H^s_{ij}=0\), so the additional tests on matched edges cause no problem.

We have proved that, with unit masses, a rational stable flow exists if and only if a discrete stable matching exists.

5. Complexity conclusion

Kamiyama's Theorem 1 proves that the discrete existence problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete when every type has degree at most \(3\) and every doctor strictly prefers adjacent hospitals in \(S\) to adjacent hospitals outside \(S\). These are precisely the restrictions needed here. [The Strongly Stable Matching Problem with Closures, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02666v1#S3)

Reduce that problem by retaining its graph, rankings, and closure set and assigning mass \(1\) to every type. Section 4 proves equivalence in both directions. The reduction preserves \(m\) and \(\tau\), appends only \(O(\tau)\) mass-encoding bits, and takes \(O(m+\tau+L)\) bit operations in the resulting instance's parameters. Therefore the flow problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

For membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), use the numerators \(z_{ij}\) from Section 3 as a certificate. Compute \(Q\), scale the input masses, and verify feasibility and all blocking tests using integer arithmetic. The certificate has \(O(mL)\) bits. Because degrees are at most \(3\), verification uses a constant number of sums per edge and can be performed in \(O((m+\tau+1)(L+1)^2)\) bit operations using ordinary arithmetic.

For completeness, once a stable support \(F\) is supplied, the circulation construction has \(O(\tau+1)\) vertices and \(O(m+\tau+1)\) arcs. Lower-bound reduction followed by a standard maximum-flow algorithm gives a conservative rounding bound of \(O((m+\tau+1)(\tau+1)^2(L+1)^2)\) bit operations. Finding an appropriate support is the hard part; this rounding procedure does not provide a polynomial-time existence algorithm.

Thus the target decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, already with unit masses. It admits a polynomial-time decision algorithm if and only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
