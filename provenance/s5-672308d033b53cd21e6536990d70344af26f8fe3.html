The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu\in\Delta^{\tau}\), additive cost vectors \(c_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{q}\), rational per-capita chore supplies \(r\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{q}\), and a fixed workload bound \(k\), let \(\mathcal{B}_k=\{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{q}:\|b\|_1\le k\}\). Compute masses \(\lambda_{t,b}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_b\lambda_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}\lambda_{t,b}b_j=r_j\), such that every supported integral bundle satisfies support-wise \(\mathrm{EF1}\), and no same-worker measurable reassignment of integral bundles with the same supplies weakly lowers every worker’s cost while strictly lowering the cost of positive mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless probability space partitioned into type sets \(I_t\). It is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=q\ge1\) chore kinds, costs \(c_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{m}\), and per-capita supplies \(r\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{m}\). Fix a positive integer workload bound \(k\), and set \(\mathcal B_k=\{b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}:\|b\|_1\le k\}\). Bundle cost is \(c_t\cdot b\); empty bundles and zero costs are allowed.
An allocation consists of rational masses \(\lambda_{t,b}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_k}\lambda_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}\lambda_{t,b}b_j=r_j\) for every chore kind \(j\).
Support-wise EF1 requires, for every \((t,b),(s,d)\) with positive allocation mass, \(c_t\cdot b-\max(\{c_{tj}:b_j>0\}\cup\{0\})\le c_t\cdot d\).
Realize these masses by a measurable assignment \(f\) of bundles to individual workers. Same-worker PO means there is no measurable assignment \(g\) into \(\mathcal B_k\), preserving every supply, such that \(c_t\cdot g(x)\le c_t\cdot f(x)\) almost everywhere on every \(I_t\), with strict inequality on a set of positive mass. Competing assignments need not satisfy EF1 and may have real allocation masses.
The computational problem is to produce an allocation satisfying both properties, or report that none exists; let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

For each fixed \(k\), can this problem be solved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies with additive chore costs, EF1 and PO allocations always exist and can be computed in polynomial time for a constant number of agents ([Mahara](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09544v2)). General polynomial-time computation remains open in that source. The searches found no verified NP-hardness or W[1]-hardness claim for that discrete search problem, and no result settling the exact atomless problem defined above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Resolved the simplex notation by requiring strictly positive listed masses summing to one, and modeled each type by an atomless set of workers.
  • Used m=q for the number of chore kinds; the rational per-capita supplies specify their quantities.
  • Interpreted the fixed workload bound as a positive integer constant applying to both the allocation and all competing reassignments.
  • Made support-wise EF1 compare every ordered pair of positively supported type-bundle combinations, including combinations within the same type; removing at most one chore from the worker's own bundle handles empty bundles.
  • Defined same-worker efficiency using almost-everywhere individual cost comparisons, positive-mass strict improvement, unrestricted measurable splitting of workers, and competitors that need not satisfy fairness.
  • Required rational output masses while allowing real masses in Pareto comparisons.
  • Included reporting nonexistence because the workload restriction is additional to the discrete model and does not inherit its unconditional existence guarantee.
  • Kept the support size variable as in the graded question; a constant number of discrete agents does not impose a constant support size on this continuous input.

Partial classification

The unrestricted classification for fixed $k\ge2$ remains unresolved. I prove two stronger existence results:

  • For every fixed $k$, all instances with total supply $\sum_j r_j\le1$ admit a polynomial-time construction of EF1 and same-worker PO, including arbitrary zero costs.
  • For every fixed $k$, if all cost vectors are nonnegative multiples of one common vector, every feasible instance admits a polynomial-time construction, including zero-cost types.

The first result follows from a more general, explicit LP criterion that also covers some instances with total supply greater than one. I also reprove polynomial-time verification and polynomial-size existence certificates.

1. Preliminaries

Write
$$ R=\sum_{j=1}^m r_j, \qquad N=|\mathcal B_k|=\binom{m+k}{k}, $$
and define
$$ h_t(b)=c_t\cdot b-\max\bigl(\{c_{tj}:b_j>0\}\cup\{0\}\bigr). $$
For fixed $k$, all bundles can be enumerated in polynomial time, and their costs have polynomial binary encoding length.

I use the standard atomless measure-splitting theorem: if $E$ is measurable in an atomless finite measure space, every number in $[0,\mu(E)]$ is the measure of a measurable subset of $E$. Repeated application realizes any finite list of prescribed nonnegative masses summing to $\mu(E)$.

The restriction of an atomless measure to a measurable subset remains atomless. Thus allocation masses can be realized inside each type set, and reassignment masses can be realized inside each original type–bundle cell. This applies to real reassignment masses as well as rational ones.

Ignoring fairness and optimality, allocation feasibility is equivalent to
$$ R\le k. $$
Necessity follows by integrating the workload bound. For sufficiency, assign masses
$$ \lambda_{t,k e_j}=\frac{\mu_t r_j}{k}, \qquad \lambda_{t,0}=\mu_t\left(1-\frac{R}{k}\right). $$
Their type totals are $\mu_t$, and their supplies are $r_j$.

2. A polynomial LP criterion sufficient for EF1 and PO

For each type, let
$$ P_t=\{j:c_{tj}>0\}, \qquad Z_t=\{j:c_{tj}=0\}, $$
and consider the bundle class
$$ H_t= \left\{ b\in\mathcal B_k: \sum_{j\in P_t}b_j\le1 \right\}. $$
Thus a worker receiving a bundle in $H_t$ performs at most one occurrence of a chore that has positive cost to that worker. Every such bundle satisfies
$$ h_t(b)=0. $$
Consequently, every allocation using only these bundles is EF1.

Consider the following system in aggregate type–kind quantities $x_{tj}$:
$$ \begin{aligned} x_{tj}&\ge0,\\ \sum_t x_{tj}&=r_j &&\text{for every }j,\\ \sum_j x_{tj}&\le k\mu_t &&\text{for every }t,\\ \sum_{j\in P_t}x_{tj}&\le\mu_t &&\text{for every }t. \end{aligned} $$

Theorem 1. If this system is feasible, an EF1 and same-worker PO allocation can be produced in polynomial time. The system exactly characterizes feasibility of allocations supported on the classes $H_t$.

The central optimality assertion is that a minimum-total-cost allocation within these classes is PO against competitors using all of $\mathcal B_k$, not merely the classes $H_t$.

2.1. Constructing bundle masses from aggregate quantities

An allocation supported on $H_t$ necessarily gives aggregate quantities satisfying the displayed system.

Conversely, suppose $x$ satisfies it. Fix a type $t$ and put
$$ A_t=\sum_{i\in P_t}x_{ti}, \qquad B_t=\sum_{j\in Z_t}x_{tj}, \qquad D_t=k\mu_t-A_t. $$
The constraints imply
$$ A_t\le\mu_t, \qquad 0\le B_t\le D_t. $$

If $B_t=0$, give mass $x_{ti}$ bundle $e_i$ for each $i\in P_t$, and give the remaining mass $\mu_t-A_t$ the empty bundle.

If $B_t>0$, then $D_t>0$. Define
$$ \eta_t=\frac{B_t}{D_t}, \qquad \rho_{tj}=\frac{x_{tj}}{B_t}\quad(j\in Z_t). $$
Here $0<\eta_t\le1$ and $\sum_{j\in Z_t}\rho_{tj}=1$. Use the following bundle–mass contributions, merging coincident bundles:
$$ \begin{array}{c|c} \text{bundle}&\text{mass}\\ \hline e_i&x_{ti}(1-\eta_t)\quad(i\in P_t)\\ e_i+(k-1)e_j&x_{ti}\eta_t\rho_{tj} \quad(i\in P_t,\ j\in Z_t)\\ k e_j&(\mu_t-A_t)\eta_t\rho_{tj} \quad(j\in Z_t)\\ 0&(\mu_t-A_t)(1-\eta_t). \end{array} $$
Every listed bundle lies in $H_t$.

The contributions containing a positive-cost chore have total mass $A_t$; the other contributions have total mass $\mu_t-A_t$. Each positive-cost kind $i$ has aggregate quantity $x_{ti}$. Each zero-cost kind $j$ has aggregate quantity
$$ \eta_t\rho_{tj} \bigl((k-1)A_t+k(\mu_t-A_t)\bigr) = \eta_t\rho_{tj}D_t = x_{tj}. $$
This proves the claimed equivalence.

Moreover, the total cost of any allocation with aggregate quantities $x$ is exactly
$$ \sum_{t,j}c_{tj}x_{tj}. $$
Therefore minimizing this expression over the aggregate LP, followed by the construction above, produces a minimum-total-cost allocation supported on $H_t$.

The LP has $\tau m$ variables and polynomially many rational constraints. It is bounded because $x_{tj}\le k\mu_t$. Exact rational LP optimization provides an optimum with polynomial encoding length. The construction uses at most polynomially many rational arithmetic operations and produces at most $O(\tau m^2)$ bundle–mass contributions.

It remains to establish unrestricted PO.

3. Why the restricted minimum is unrestricted PO

Lemma 2. Every minimum-total-cost allocation supported on the classes $H_t$ is same-worker PO against all assignments into $\mathcal B_k$.

3.1. Dual prices and the structure of supported bundles

The restricted allocation LP has dual variables $\alpha_t\in\mathbb R$ and $\pi_j\in\mathbb R$, with constraints
$$ \alpha_t+\pi\cdot b\le c_t\cdot b \qquad(t,\ b\in H_t). $$
Its objective is
$$ \sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t+\sum_jr_j\pi_j. $$

The primal is a nonempty bounded finite rational LP. Strong duality supplies an optimal dual solution. Complementary slackness gives
$$ \alpha_t+\pi\cdot b=c_t\cdot b $$
for every positively allocated pair $(t,b)$.

Define
$$ z_t=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{\pi_j:j\in Z_t\}\bigr), \qquad \beta_t=-\alpha_t-(k-1)z_t. $$
Testing the empty bundle, bundles $k e_j$ with $j\in Z_t$, and bundles consisting of one positive-cost chore and up to $k-1$ zero-cost chores gives
$$ \alpha_t+kz_t\le0, \qquad \pi_i\le c_{ti}+\beta_t\quad(i\in P_t). $$
In particular,
$$ \beta_t\ge z_t\ge0. $$

There are two possibilities for a supported bundle $b$.

Zero-cost bundle. If $c_t\cdot b=0$, complementary slackness and the preceding bound yield
$$ -\alpha_t=\pi\cdot b\le kz_t\le-\alpha_t. $$
All inequalities are equalities. Hence:

  • If $z_t>0$, the bundle contains exactly $k$ chores, all at price $z_t$.
  • If $z_t=0$, every chore occurring in the bundle has price zero.

Positive-cost bundle. Such a bundle contains exactly one positive-cost occurrence, say of kind $i$, with cost
$$ C=c_{ti}>0. $$
Its other occurrences have zero cost. Complementary slackness gives
$$ \alpha_t+\pi_i+ \sum_{j\in Z_t}b_j\pi_j=C. $$
Since the last sum is at most $(k-1)z_t$, while
$$ \alpha_t+\pi_i+(k-1)z_t\le C, $$
both bounds must be tight. Therefore
$$ \pi_i=C+\beta_t>z_t, \qquad \sum_{j\in Z_t}b_j\pi_j=(k-1)z_t. $$
Consequently:

  • If $z_t>0$, the bundle contains $k-1$ zero-cost occurrences, all at price $z_t$.
  • If $z_t=0$, every zero-cost occurrence in the bundle has price zero.

3.2. Every weak improvement decreases a price-count vector lexicographically

List the distinct strictly positive prices as
$$ v_1>v_2>\cdots>v_\ell>0. $$
For any bundle $b$, define
$$ n_a(b)=\sum_{j:\pi_j=v_a}b_j, \qquad n(b)=(n_1(b),\ldots,n_\ell(b)). $$

I claim that whenever $b$ is supported for type $t$ and
$$ c_t\cdot d\le c_t\cdot b, $$
one has
$$ n(b)\succeq_{\mathrm{lex}} n(d). $$
Furthermore, strict cost improvement implies strict lexicographic decrease.

For a supported zero-cost bundle, every chore in $d$ must also have zero cost to type $t$. Its price is therefore at most $z_t$. If $z_t>0$, the original bundle already has $k$ occurrences at this highest available zero-cost price. If $z_t=0$, neither bundle contains a positive-price occurrence. This proves the claim in this case.

Now suppose the original bundle has positive cost $C$, and write
$$ P=C+\beta_t $$
for its unique positive-cost chore’s price. Its zero-cost chores have prices at most $z_t<P$.

If $d$ contains at least two positive-cost occurrences, each of their costs is strictly less than $C$, because their positive costs sum to at most $C$. Each such occurrence of kind $j$ satisfies
$$ \pi_j\le c_{tj}+\beta_t<P. $$
Its zero-cost occurrences also have price below $P$. Thus $d$ loses the original occurrence at price $P$ and introduces nothing at that price or above: its count vector is strictly smaller.

If $d$ contains no positive-cost occurrence, the same conclusion holds.

Finally, suppose $d$ contains exactly one positive-cost occurrence, of kind $j$. Then
$$ \pi_j\le c_{tj}+\beta_t\le C+\beta_t=P. $$
If $\pi_j<P$, the vector again strictly decreases. If $\pi_j=P$, both inequalities are equalities, so
$$ c_{tj}=C. $$
The positive-cost occurrence retains its price. The remaining occurrences have prices at most $z_t$. When $z_t>0$, the original bundle already has the maximum possible $k-1$ occurrences at that level; when $z_t=0$, those occurrences contribute nothing to the positive-price vector. Hence the vector cannot increase.

This also proves the strictness assertion: a strict cost improvement cannot retain the positive-cost occurrence’s price $P$.

If there are no positive prices, the supported-bundle descriptions imply that every allocated bundle has zero cost to its recipient. Such an allocation is immediately PO. Thus assume $\ell\ge1$.

3.3. Supply preservation excludes strict improvement

Let $f$ realize the allocation, and suppose $g$ weakly improves every worker. The preceding argument gives
$$ n(f(x))\succeq_{\mathrm{lex}}n(g(x)) $$
almost everywhere.

Supply preservation gives, for every $a$,
$$ \int n_a(f(x))\,d\mu(x) = \sum_{j:\pi_j=v_a}r_j = \int n_a(g(x))\,d\mu(x). $$

Lexicographic dominance first implies
$$ n_1(f(x))-n_1(g(x))\ge0. $$
Its integral is zero, so it vanishes almost everywhere. On that full-measure set, lexicographic dominance implies the same nonnegativity for the second coordinate. Its integral is also zero. Continuing through the finitely many coordinates proves
$$ n(f(x))=n(g(x)) $$
almost everywhere.

A strict cost improvement would instead require a strict lexicographic decrease. It therefore cannot occur on a set of positive measure. This proves Lemma 2, and hence Theorem 1.

4. Consequences: all zero costs are allowed when total supply is at most one

Corollary 3. For every fixed $k$, every instance with $R\le1$ admits a polynomial-time construction of EF1 and same-worker PO, without any positivity assumption.

Indeed, the aggregate quantities
$$ x_{tj}=\mu_t r_j $$
satisfy
$$ \sum_jx_{tj}=\mu_t R\le\mu_t\le k\mu_t $$
and
$$ \sum_{j\in P_t}x_{tj} \le\mu_t R\le\mu_t. $$
Theorem 1 applies.

This is stronger than minimizing cost among singleton allocations: the construction may pack several zero-cost chores onto one worker, while still assigning at most one positive-cost occurrence to each worker.

Corollary 4. For $k=1$, the unrestricted problem is polynomial-time solvable.

If $R>1$, no allocation is feasible. If $R\le1$, Corollary 3 constructs the required allocation. Thus this fully resolves the case $k=1$.

For $R>1$, feasibility of the aggregate LP remains a sufficient condition. Its infeasibility does not imply that the original problem has no solution.

5. Proportional costs, including zero-cost types

Theorem 5. Suppose
$$ c_t=a_t c \qquad\text{for every }t, $$
where $c\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m$ and $a_t\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$. For every fixed $k$, an EF1 and same-worker PO allocation exists exactly when $R\le k$, and it can be constructed in polynomial time.

If all costs are zero, every feasible allocation has both properties. Otherwise, a representation of the stated form can be found and checked by choosing a nonzero cost row as $c$ and checking rational coordinate ratios.

Assume henceforth that $c\ne0$, and let
$$ T_+=\{t:a_t>0\}. $$

5.1. Two linear objectives

Over the full allocation polytope, first minimize
$$ A(\lambda)= \sum_{t\in T_+}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_k} \lambda_{t,b}\,c\cdot b. $$
Among the minimizers, minimize
$$ V(\lambda)= \sum_t\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_k} \lambda_{t,b}(c\cdot b)^2. $$

Both are linear functions of the allocation variables. For fixed $k$, the polytope and objective coefficients have polynomial encoding size. Solve the first LP, impose its optimal objective value as an equality, and solve the second LP.

Every minimizer of $A$ is same-worker PO. For a positive-factor type, weak improvement in its actual cost is equivalent to weak improvement in $c\cdot b$. Zero-factor types have cost zero and cannot strictly improve. Thus any same-worker Pareto improvement would strictly decrease $A$, contradicting its minimum. This applies equally to competitors with real masses.

It remains to prove EF1 for a minimizer of both objectives.

5.2. An EF1 violation permits a feasible exchange

Suppose supported pairs $(t,b)$ and $(s,d)$ violate EF1. Necessarily $t\in T_+$, because a zero-factor type has trimmed cost zero.

Dividing by $a_t$, put
$$ X=c\cdot b,\qquad Y=c\cdot d,\qquad M=\max\{c_j:b_j>0\}. $$
The violation says
$$ \Delta:=X-Y>M. $$
In particular, $M>0$. Choose an occurrence of kind $i$ in $b$ with $c_i=M$.

If $\|d\|_1<k$, transfer that occurrence:
$$ b'=b-e_i, \qquad d'=d+e_i. $$
The common-vector costs change from $(X,Y)$ to
$$ (X-\delta,Y+\delta), \qquad \delta=M, $$
where $0<\delta<\Delta$.

If $\|d\|_1=k$, choose an occurrence of kind $j$ in $d$ having minimum common cost $v=c_j$. One must have $M>v$: otherwise
$$ X\le\|b\|_1M\le kM\le kv\le Y, $$
contradicting $X>Y$.

Swap the chosen occurrences:
$$ b'=b-e_i+e_j, \qquad d'=d-e_j+e_i. $$
Now
$$ \delta=M-v $$
satisfies
$$ 0<\delta\le M<\Delta. $$

Both operations preserve supplies and respect the workload bound.

Choose a sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, remove mass $\varepsilon$ from each original supported cell, and add it to the corresponding new bundle in the same type. The original cells are distinct because $X>Y$, so this produces a feasible allocation.

If $s\notin T_+$, its change is excluded from $A$, while the type-$t$ contribution decreases. Hence
$$ A(\lambda')-A(\lambda)=-\varepsilon\delta<0, $$
contradicting primary optimality.

If $s\in T_+$, the two common-vector cost changes cancel, so $A$ is unchanged. But
$$ \begin{aligned} V(\lambda')-V(\lambda) &=\varepsilon\bigl((X-\delta)^2+(Y+\delta)^2-X^2-Y^2\bigr)\\ &=-2\varepsilon\delta(\Delta-\delta)<0, \end{aligned} $$
contradicting secondary optimality.

Therefore no EF1 violation exists. This proves Theorem 5.

In particular, for one supported type, every feasible supply vector admits a polynomial-time construction; there is no additional existence obstruction.

6. General verification and polynomial-size certificates

These results do not settle how to select successful supports for arbitrary cost vectors. Nevertheless, the general existence decision problem has polynomial certificates for every fixed $k$.

6.1. Exact verification of same-worker PO

Fix a rational allocation $\lambda$. For every triple with
$$ c_t\cdot d\le c_t\cdot b, $$
introduce a nonnegative transition variable $\gamma_{t,b,d}$. Impose
$$ \sum_{d:c_t\cdot d\le c_t\cdot b}\gamma_{t,b,d} =\lambda_{t,b} $$
and
$$ \sum_{t,b,d}\gamma_{t,b,d}d_j=r_j \qquad\text{for every }j. $$
Maximize
$$ \sum_{t,b,d} \gamma_{t,b,d}\bigl(c_t\cdot b-c_t\cdot d\bigr). $$

The identity reassignment is feasible with objective zero. Every measurable weak improvement gives feasible transition masses. Strict improvement on positive mass gives a positive objective: there are finitely many transition costs, and at least one strictly improving transition must have positive mass.

Conversely, atomless splitting realizes any feasible transition masses inside the original cells. A positive objective then gives a same-worker Pareto improvement.

Thus PO holds exactly when the LP optimum is zero. The LP is bounded and has at most $\tau N^2$ variables. Verification is polynomial in the combined encoding length of the instance and the supplied allocation. EF1 is verified by checking the supported inequalities.

6.2. Reducing support preserves both properties

EF1 plainly survives support reduction.

For PO, suppose a feasible allocation $\lambda'$ has support contained in that of a PO allocation $\lambda$, and suppose $\lambda'$ admits an improvement. Let its transition masses be $a_{t,b,d}$. They satisfy
$$ \sum_{t,b,d}a_{t,b,d}(d-b)=0 $$
and have strictly positive total cost decrease.

Every source cell with positive outgoing mass is supported by $\lambda$. Choose $\varepsilon>0$ small enough that
$$ \varepsilon\sum_d a_{t,b,d}\le\lambda_{t,b} $$
for every source cell. Perform those scaled transitions in $\lambda$ and leave the remaining workers unchanged.

The displayed balance equation preserves supplies. Every transition weakly improves its worker, and the total strict decrease remains positive. Atomless splitting realizes the transitions, contradicting PO of $\lambda$.

Therefore PO also survives feasible support reduction.

6.3. Polynomial-size existence certificates

Suppose a successful allocation exists. Restrict the allocation polytope to its support and choose a vertex. This is a nonempty bounded rational polytope with $\tau+m$ equality constraints.

A standard-form vertex has at most the rank of its equality matrix many positive variables, hence at most
$$ \tau+m. $$
Its support is contained in the original successful support, so it remains EF1 and PO.

The equality matrix has integer entries bounded by $k$. Choosing an invertible basis and applying Cramer’s rule gives polynomial encoding length for the vertex coordinates: after clearing the input denominators, determinant sizes are bounded using
$$ |\det B|\le d!\,k^d, \qquad d\le\tau+m. $$
The logarithm of this bound is polynomial in the input size for fixed $k$.

Guessing this allocation and applying the preceding verification procedure proves that the existence decision problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

7. Unresolved classification

The new LP construction resolves all instances with $R\le1$, including zero costs. The proportional-cost construction resolves all feasible supplies in that family.

For each fixed $k\ge2$, the unrestricted problem with variable $m,\tau$ remains unclassified. In particular, no polynomial algorithm or $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness reduction is established here for arbitrary nonproportional costs with
$$ 1<R\le k $$
when the sufficient LP criterion in Section 2 fails.
