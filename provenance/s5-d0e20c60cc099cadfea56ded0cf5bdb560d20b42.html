The continuous mirror question, as graded

For fixed \(k\ge2\), given a finite support \(T\) of explicitly listed types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), and a succinct polynomial-time value representation of a monotone, Lipschitz DR-submodular function \(\Phi:[0,1]^T\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}\), determine the best polynomial-time approximation for maximizing \(\Phi(y)\) subject to \(0\le y_t\le1\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t y_t\le1/k\), with additive accuracy \(\eta\). In particular, does the discrete barrier \(1-(1-1/k)^k\) persist when type proportions are explicit?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix an integer \(k\ge2\). A society is given by its support \(T\): explicitly listed types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Write \(\tau=|T|\) for the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(m\) be the maximum type-description length and \(L\) the total input bit length.
The input also supplies a succinct representation of a coordinatewise nondecreasing, DR-submodular function \(\Phi:[0,1]^T\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), and a positive binary-rational bound \(B\) satisfying \(|\Phi(x)-\Phi(z)|\le B\|x-z\|_\infty\). No differentiability or gradient access is assumed, and \(\Phi(0)\) need not vanish.
The representation includes an evaluator that, given rational \(x\) and precision \(p\in\mathbb N\), returns a rational approximation to \(\Phi(x)\) with error at most \(2^{-p}\), in time polynomial in \(L\), the encoding length of \(x\), and \(p\).
For binary-rational \(0<\eta<1\), define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max\{\Phi(y):y\in[0,1]^T,\ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t y_t\le1/k\}\). An approximation factor \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) means a feasible rational output \(y\) satisfying \(\Phi(y)\ge\alpha\mathrm{OPT}-\eta\), with probability at least \(2/3\); polynomial time means polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\eta\), for fixed \(k\) and \(\alpha\).

What is the supremum polynomial-time approximation factor for this problem? In particular, can any fixed factor exceeding \(1-(1-1/k)^k\) be achieved, or is every such improvement impossible unless \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\)?

For succinctly represented nonnegative monotone submodular set functions under \(|S|\le n/k\), approximation above \(1-(1-1/k)^k\) is impossible unless \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\) ([Dobzinski–Vondrák](https://theory.stanford.edu/~jvondrak/data/querytocc.pdf)).
For continuous monotone DR-submodular maximization over general down-closed polytopes, a \(1-1/e\) guarantee is known with gradient access and a Lipschitz derivative assumption; general-polytope inapproximability above \(1-1/e\) is also known unless \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\) ([Bian et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v54/bian17a/bian17a.pdf)). These results do not state the fixed normalized mass-budget, value-only problem above.

Result

Write \(\gamma_k=1-(1-1/k)^k\).

The supremum is \(\gamma_k\), assuming \(\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{RP}\). More precisely:

  • Every fixed \(\alpha<\gamma_k\) is achievable in the stated model.
  • A polynomial-time algorithm for any fixed \(\alpha>\gamma_k\) would imply \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\).
  • If \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\), factor \(1\), with the prescribed additive error, is achievable.

Thus the conditional supremum is exactly the proposed threshold. Identifying the supremum does not require proving attainment at the endpoint.

The proof below addresses both representation issues: the algorithm uses neither gradients nor a discretization proportional to the numerical value of \(B\), and the hardness instances have deterministic evaluators running in time polynomial in the requested precision.

1. Consequences of diminishing returns

We use the following definition of DR-submodularity: whenever \(x\le z\) coordinatewise and both increments remain in the domain,

\(\Phi(x+h e_t)-\Phi(x)\ge \Phi(z+h e_t)-\Phi(z)\), for \(h\ge0\).

Telescoping over coordinates gives the corresponding inequality for any nonnegative increment vector \(d\):

\(\Phi(x+d)-\Phi(x)\ge\Phi(z+d)-\Phi(z)\),

provided all four points belong to the cube.

Consequently, for every \(y\ge0\), the function \(s\mapsto\Phi(sy)\) is concave on its domain. Indeed, the preceding inequality proves midpoint concavity; continuity, supplied by the Lipschitz hypothesis, extends this to concavity. In particular,

\(\Phi((1-s)y)\ge(1-s)\Phi(y)+s\Phi(0)\ge(1-s)\Phi(y)\), for \(0\le s\le1\).

This also explains why allowing \(\Phi(0)>0\) causes no difficulty.

2. A value-only dense-cardinality lemma

Define \(c(d)=1-(1-d)^{1/d}\), for \(0<d\le1/2\).

Lemma. Let \(f:2^E\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) be monotone submodular, let \(n=|E|\), and let \(1\le q\le n/2\). Suppose rational approximations to \(f(S)\), with error at most \(2^{-p}\), can be computed in polynomial time in the input size and \(p\). Put

\(O=\max_{|S|\le q}f(S)\), and \(d=q/n\).

For rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\) and \(\delta>0\), a randomized algorithm returns \(S\) with \(|S|\le q\) and

\(\mathbb E[f(S)]\ge(c(d)-\varepsilon)O-\delta\).

Its running time is polynomial in the input size, \(n\), \(1/\varepsilon\), and \(\log^+(1/\delta)\), where \(\log^+(a)=\max\{0,\log a\}\). Neither normalization \(f(\varnothing)=0\) nor exact value access is required.

The underlying idea is measured continuous greedy, whose density-dependent guarantee appears in [Feldman–Naor–Schwartz](https://www.cs.haifa.ac.il/~moranfe/publications/FOCS2011Full.pdf). We give the numerical implementation needed here.

Proof: normalization and derivative estimates

Submodularity and nonnegativity imply

\(f(E)\le f(\varnothing)+\sum_{i\in E}(f(\{i\})-f(\varnothing))\le nO\).

Compute \(a\) with \(|a-f(E)|\le\delta\), and set

\(K=\max\{\delta,a+\delta\}\).

Then

\(f(E)\le K\le nO+2\delta\).

Let \(g=f/K\), and let \(H\) be its multilinear extension:

\(H(x)=\mathbb E[g(R_x)]\),

where each element \(i\) belongs to \(R_x\) independently with probability \(x_i\). Thus \(0\le H\le1\). Its derivatives satisfy

\(0\le\partial_i H\le1,\qquad -1\le\partial_{ij}H\le0\ \text{for }i\ne j,\qquad \partial_{ii}H=0\).

These follow directly by expressing derivatives as expectations of discrete marginal gains and differences of marginal gains.

For rational \(x\), estimate

\(w_i(x)=(1-x_i)\partial_iH(x)\)

by sampling marginal gains \(g(R\cup\{i\})-g(R)\), where \(R\) is sampled on \(E\setminus\{i\}\). Bounded sampling, Hoeffding’s inequality, and approximate evaluations give additive error \(\zeta\) using polynomially many calls in \(1/\zeta\) and the logarithm of the inverse failure probability.

This is a finite-time implementation: Bernoulli probabilities can be rounded to sufficiently accurate dyadic rationals. Their total variation error is at most the sum of the individual errors. Evaluation errors and this sampling bias can each be assigned a fixed fraction of \(\zeta\). Since \(K\ge\delta\), the required evaluator precision is polynomially bounded in \(\log^+(1/\delta)\), \(\log n\), and \(\log(1/\zeta)\).

Proof: progress

Start at \(x^{(0)}=0\). At each iteration choose a vector \(v\in\{0,1\}^E\) having exactly \(q\) ones and maximizing the estimated sum of weights. Update

\(x_i^{(j+1)}=x_i^{(j)}+h\,v_i(1-x_i^{(j)})\).

When all weight estimates are accurate within \(\zeta\), this direction loses at most \(2q\zeta\) against the best true direction.

For an optimal set \(S^*\), diminishing returns and monotonicity give

\(\sum_{i\in S^*}w_i(x)=\sum_{i\in S^*}\bigl(H(x\vee e_i)-H(x)\bigr)\ge H(x\vee\mathbf1_{S^*})-H(x)\ge O/K-H(x)\).

The Hessian bounds therefore imply

\(H(x^{(j+1)})\ge(1-h)H(x^{(j)})+hO/K-2qh\zeta-h^2n^2/2\).

After \(r\) iterations, writing \(T=rh\), we obtain

\(H(x^{(r)})\ge(1-e^{-T})O/K-T(2q\zeta+hn^2/2)\).

Here we used \(H(0)\ge0\) and \((1-h)^r\le e^{-T}\).

Proof: feasibility and rounding

Set \(T_d=-\log(1-d)/d\). Choose a rational \(T\) satisfying

\(T_d-\varepsilon/8\le T\le T_d-\varepsilon/16\).

Such a rational is computable efficiently using the convergent series for \(-\log(1-d)\); because \(d\le1/2\), its tail decreases geometrically.

If coordinate \(i\) was selected \(a_i\) times, then

\(x_i^{(r)}=1-(1-h)^{a_i},\qquad \sum_i a_i=rq\).

Concavity of \(a\mapsto1-(1-h)^a\) gives

\(\frac1n\sum_i x_i^{(r)}\le1-(1-h)^{rd}\).

For \(h\le1/2\),

\(-\log(1-h)\le h+h^2\).

Choose \(h=T/r\le\varepsilon/64\). Since \(T<2\), we have \(T(1+h)\le T_d\). Consequently,

\((1-h)^{rd}\ge e^{-dT_d}=1-d\),

and hence \(\sum_i x_i^{(r)}\le q\).

Randomized pipage rounding now produces an integral feasible set without decreasing the expected value of \(H\). For completeness, along a swap direction \(e_i-e_j\),

\(\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(x+t(e_i-e_j))=-2\partial_{ij}H\ge0\).

Randomly choosing the two feasible endpoints with mean equal to the current point therefore does not decrease expected value. Each swap makes a coordinate integral. If one fractional coordinate remains, rounding it upward preserves feasibility because \(q\) is an integer, and monotonicity preserves value.

Proof: error and running time

Take \(\sigma=\varepsilon/(4n)\), choose

\(h\le\min\{\varepsilon/64,\sigma/(32n^2)\},\qquad \zeta\le\sigma/(64n)\),

and make the probability of any inaccurate weight estimate at most \(\sigma/8\). These choices require polynomially many iterations and samples.

The accumulated progress error is at most \(3\sigma/32\). Failed estimation contributes at most \(\sigma/8\), since \(H\in[0,1]\). Using dyadic approximations to the pipage probabilities can contribute at most another \(\sigma/8\), by coupling the successive choices. Thus the total normalized loss is less than \(\sigma\).

Also \(1-e^{-T}\ge c(d)-\varepsilon/8\). We conclude that

\(\mathbb E[f(S)]\ge(c(d)-\varepsilon/8)O-\sigma K\ge(c(d)-\varepsilon)O-\delta\).

All maintained rationals have polynomial encoding length. This proves the lemma.

3. Lifting the society to polynomially many copies

Fix \(\alpha<\gamma_k\). Choose fixed rational \(s,\varepsilon>0\), sufficiently small that

\(\beta=(\gamma_k-2\varepsilon)(1-s)>\alpha\).

Choose an integer \(N\), divisible by \(k\), satisfying

\(N\ge\max\{2k\tau/s,\;2\tau/\varepsilon\}\).

For each type \(t\), create \(q_t=\lceil N\mu_t\rceil\) copies. Let \(E\) be the resulting ground set and \(n=|E|\). Then

\(N\le n<N+\tau\).

For a set \(S\subseteq E\), define

\(x_t(S)=|S\cap E_t|/q_t,\qquad f(S)=\Phi(x(S))\).

This is a monotone, nonnegative submodular set function. Indeed, adding a copy of type \(t\) increases precisely coordinate \(t\) by \(1/q_t\), so its discrete diminishing-returns inequality is exactly a DR inequality for \(\Phi\). Its approximate evaluator queries the supplied evaluator at a rational vector of polynomial encoding length.

Use the cardinality bound \(q=N/k\).

Feasibility

Since \(q_t\ge N\mu_t\),

\(\sum_t\mu_t x_t(S)=\sum_t\frac{\mu_t}{q_t}|S\cap E_t|\le |S|/N\).

Thus every set with \(|S|\le q\) gives a feasible society output.

Comparison of optima

Continuity and compactness ensure an optimal \(y^*\) exists. Set

\(a_t=\lceil q_t(1-s)y_t^*\rceil\).

Because \(q_t<N\mu_t+1\),

\(\sum_t a_t\le(1-s)(N/k+\tau)+\tau\le N/k\).

The last inequality follows from \(sN/k\ge2\tau\). Choose a set \(S\) containing exactly \(a_t\) copies of each type. Then \(x(S)\ge(1-s)y^*\), and Section 1 gives

\(O\ge f(S)\ge(1-s)\mathrm{OPT}\).

Density comparison

Put \(d=q/n\). We have \(0<d\le1/k\le1/2\) and

\(1/k-d\le\tau/N\).

On \(0<d\le1/2\), the function \(c\) satisfies \(0\le c'(d)\le2\). One way to see this is to differentiate

\(\log(1-d)/d=-\sum_{j\ge1}d^{j-1}/j\);

the absolute derivative of this expression is at most \(1/(1-d)\le2\), and its exponential is at most \(1\). Therefore,

\(c(d)\ge\gamma_k-2\tau/N\ge\gamma_k-\varepsilon\).

Apply the lemma with additive error \(\eta/8\). The resulting feasible society point \(Y=x(S)\) satisfies

\(\mathbb E[\Phi(Y)]\ge\beta\,\mathrm{OPT}-\eta/8\).

The number of copies is polynomial in \(\tau\), with constants depending only on fixed \(k,\alpha\). In particular, neither mass denominators nor the numerical value of \(B\) are expanded.

Obtaining the required success probability

Let \(g=\beta-\alpha>0\). Every returned point is feasible, so its value lies in \([0,\mathrm{OPT}]\).

If \(a=\alpha\mathrm{OPT}-\eta/2>0\), the expectation bound implies

\(\Pr[\Phi(Y)\ge a]\ge\frac{g\,\mathrm{OPT}+3\eta/8}{(1-\alpha)\mathrm{OPT}+\eta/2}\ge\min\{g,1/2\}\).

The second inequality follows by bounding the denominator above by \(\mathrm{OPT}+\eta/2\). If \(a\le0\), the event holds automatically.

Repeat independently a constant number of times so that at least one output meets this threshold with probability at least \(2/3\). Evaluate the outputs within \(\eta/8\) and choose the largest approximate value. Selection loses at most \(\eta/4\), giving

\(\Phi(y)\ge\alpha\mathrm{OPT}-\eta\).

This proves achievability of every fixed \(\alpha<\gamma_k\) in the required running time.

4. Hardness with a precision-efficient fractional evaluator

Fix \(\alpha>\gamma_k\). We construct a reduction using the smoothing and coding ingredients underlying the cited hardness result, rather than assuming that an arbitrary succinct set function has an efficiently evaluable multilinear extension.

Standard ingredients and their hypotheses

Consider the fixed set function \(f_0(S)=\min\{|S|,1\}\) on \([k]\). It is nonnegative, monotone, submodular, and invariant under every permutation. Its multilinear extension and symmetrization are

\(F(z)=1-\prod_{j=1}^k(1-z_j),\qquad G(z)=1-(1-\bar z)^k,\qquad \bar z=k^{-1}\sum_jz_j\).

The smoothing lemma, applied to this fixed function, supplies, for every fixed positive rational \(e\), nonnegative monotone DR functions \(U,V\) and a constant \(r_0>0\) such that:

  • \(|U(z)-F(z)|\le e\);
  • \(V(z)=U(\bar z\mathbf1)\), hence \(|V(z)-G(z)|\le e\);
  • \(U(z)=V(z)\) whenever \(\|z-\bar z\mathbf1\|_2\le r_0\).

These are the applicable conclusions of [Vondrák’s Lemma 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.4860). Its construction has continuous, absolutely continuous first derivatives and nonpositive second partial derivatives, including diagonal ones. Integrating along coordinate lines, and extending by continuity, verifies DR-submodularity.

The construction also meets the computational requirement here. Its fixed-dimensional piecewise formulas use polynomials, computable fixed constants, and fixed rational powers with nonsingular arguments. Values and first derivatives agree at branch boundaries. Thus approximating the constants and formulas to sufficiently many bits gives evaluation polynomial in the rational argument length and precision. Ambiguous branch comparisons can use either nearby branch, with error controlled by a fixed derivative bound. Fix an integer \(M\) bounding all first derivatives of both functions; such an effective bound follows from these explicit formulas.

Choose a fixed rational \(0<\gamma<\min\{r_0/k^2,1/4\}\). The coding ingredient used in [Dobzinski–Vondrák, Section 4.2](https://theory.stanford.edu/~jvondrak/data/querytocc.pdf) provides an injective, polynomial-time encoding into \([k]^n\), with \(n\) polynomial in message length, and a polynomial-time list decoder for relative Hamming radius \(1-(1+\gamma)/k\). The alphabet and decoding slack are fixed, as required.

Boolean assignments can be treated as messages over \([k]\); messages using other symbols are rejected when checking the formula.

The hidden-partition objective

Given a Boolean formula \(\varphi\) promised to have either zero or one satisfying assignment, let \(E(z)\in[k]^n\) denote the codeword of its unique assignment, if one exists.

Identify the symbols with integers modulo \(k\). That codeword determines a partition of

\(T=[n]\times[k]\)

into \(k\) parts of size \(n\):

\(Y_j=\{(a,E(z)_a+j):a\in[n]\}\).

For a fractional vector \(u\in[0,1]^T\), define

\(\xi_j(u)=n^{-1}\sum_{t\in Y_j}u_t\).

In the uniquely satisfiable case set \(\Phi_\varphi(u)=U(\xi(u))\). In the unsatisfiable case set

\(\Phi_\varphi(u)=V(\bar\xi(u)\mathbf1),\qquad \bar\xi(u)=(kn)^{-1}\sum_{t\in T}u_t\).

The latter expression does not depend on a partition.

Both promised objectives are nonnegative, monotone, and DR-submodular. For the satisfiable objective this follows by composition with the block-averaging map: a coordinate increment of \(u\) is a coordinate increment of \(\xi\) divided by \(n\). For the unsatisfiable objective, express it as \(V\) composed with any fixed block-averaging map and apply the same argument.

Furthermore,

\(|\Phi_\varphi(u)-\Phi_\varphi(v)|\le kM\|u-v\|_\infty\).

Indeed, block averaging contracts the sup norm, and the sum of the absolute first derivatives of \(U\) or \(V\) is at most \(kM\). Thus \(B=kM\) is a valid positive integer bound.

Deterministic decoding from fractional inputs

It remains to evaluate the satisfiable objective without knowing the assignment.

For each row \(a\), define rational probabilities

\(p_{a,b}=\frac1k+\frac{u_{a,b}}k-\frac1{k^2}\sum_{c=1}^k u_{a,c}\).

They are nonnegative and sum to \(1\). For each row, partition \([0,1]\) into intervals of these lengths. Use one common parameter \(\theta\in[0,1]\) to choose a symbol in every row. Enumerating all interval endpoints produces only polynomially many candidate strings.

Suppose some hidden part \(Y_j\) satisfies \(\xi_j(u)-\bar\xi(u)>\gamma\). The expected number of matches between this random string and the shifted codeword defining \(Y_j\) is

\(\sum_a p_{a,E(z)_a+j}=\frac nk+\frac nk(\xi_j(u)-\bar\xi(u))>\frac{n(1+\gamma)}k\).

Hence an enumerated string has Hamming distance less than \(n(1-(1+\gamma)/k)\) from that shifted codeword. Enumerate all cyclic shifts of the candidate strings, apply the list decoder, and check the decoded messages against \(\varphi\). This recovers the satisfying assignment.

Whenever \(\|\xi(u)-\bar\xi(u)\mathbf1\|_2>r_0\), such a positively deviating coordinate exists. To verify this, let \(A=\max_j(\xi_j-\bar\xi)\). The deviations sum to zero, so every negative deviation has absolute value at most \((k-1)A\). Therefore,

\(\|\xi-\bar\xi\mathbf1\|_2\le k^{3/2}A\le k^2A\).

If all positive deviations were at most \(\gamma\), this norm would be less than \(r_0\).

The evaluator is consequently:

  • Generate the candidate strings, decode, and check assignments.
  • If a satisfying assignment is found, reconstruct its partition and evaluate \(U(\xi(u))\).
  • Otherwise, evaluate \(V(\bar\xi(u)\mathbf1)\).

On promised instances this is correct: failure to recover the unique assignment implies that \(u\) is in the region where \(U=V\). All decoding operations are polynomial, and the final fixed-dimensional evaluation is polynomial in the requested precision.

This establishes the evaluator required by the question, on every rational fractional query.

Normalized dyadic masses and the optimization gap

The reduction can even use dyadic masses. Put \(N=kn\), choose a power of two \(D\ge N/e\), and assign each type a mass equal to either

\(\lfloor D/N\rfloor/D\) or \(\lceil D/N\rceil/D\),

using the appropriate number of each so that their sum is exactly \(1\). These masses are positive and satisfy

\((1-e)/N\le\mu_t\le(1+e)/N\).

If \(\varphi\) is uniquely satisfiable, take

\(u=(1+e)^{-1}\mathbf1_{Y_1}\).

This is feasible, because \(Y_1\) has \(n\) elements. Its macro-vector is \((1+e)^{-1}e_1\), so

\(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{YES}}\ge(1+e)^{-1}-e\ge1-2e\).

If \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable, feasibility implies

\(\bar\xi(u)=N^{-1}\sum_tu_t\le1/[k(1-e)]\).

The scalar function \(a\mapsto1-(1-a)^k\) has derivative at most \(k\) on \([0,1]\). For \(e<1/2\), therefore,

\(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{NO}}\le\gamma_k+\frac{e}{1-e}+e\le\gamma_k+3e\).

Choose fixed positive dyadic \(e,\eta\) so small that

\(e<(\alpha-\gamma_k)/16,\qquad \eta<(\alpha-\gamma_k)/16\).

Then

\(\alpha(1-2e)-\eta>\gamma_k+3e\).

An alleged \(\alpha\)-approximation thus produces, on a uniquely satisfiable formula, a feasible output of value greater than \(\gamma_k+3e\), with probability at least \(2/3\). Running the fractional decoding procedure on that output must recover a satisfying assignment: otherwise its objective value would be the symmetric value, bounded above by \(\gamma_k+3e\).

Acceptance is based solely on verifying a recovered satisfying assignment, so unsatisfiable formulas are never accepted. On formulas with multiple assignments, the constructed function need not satisfy the promise; the algorithm can be stopped after its promised polynomial running-time bound, and acceptance still requires a verified witness.

The [Valiant–Vazirani randomized isolation theorem](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(86)90135-0) now yields an RP algorithm for SAT: polynomially many isolation trials find a uniquely satisfiable formula with sufficient probability, while preserving unsatisfiability. Hence \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\).

All support labels have length \(O(\log N)\), and the complete representation has polynomial input length. The reduction therefore respects \(m,\tau,L\), not an implicit universe of possible types.

5. The case where NP equals RP

For completeness, if \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\), the supremum becomes \(1\).

Choose a power of two \(D\ge\max\{1,8B/\eta\}\). Rounding an optimal point downward to the grid with coordinates in \(\{0,1/D,\ldots,1\}\) preserves feasibility and loses at most \(\eta/8\).

Every grid point has polynomial encoding length, since \(\log D\) is polynomially bounded by the input length and \(\log(1/\eta)\). Fix evaluator precision giving error at most \(\eta/16\). The predicate

“there exists a feasible grid point whose evaluator output is at least a specified rational threshold”

belongs to NP: a grid point is a polynomial-size certificate, and its feasibility and evaluator output are checkable in polynomial time.

Under \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\), amplify these decision procedures and use binary search followed by certificate self-reduction. Polynomially many queries suffice to find a grid point whose evaluator value is within \(\eta/4\) of the largest grid evaluator value. A union bound gives overall success probability at least \(2/3\). The grid loss and evaluation errors together are less than \(\eta\), yielding

\(\Phi(y)\ge\mathrm{OPT}-\eta\).

The dependence on \(B\) here is through its encoding length.

6. Conclusion

The value-only problem with a fixed normalized mass budget has the sharp conditional supremum \(\gamma_k\). Every fixed factor below it is achievable with polynomial dependence on the explicitly listed support and binary input length. Every fixed improvement above it implies \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\), even for nearly uniform dyadic masses.
