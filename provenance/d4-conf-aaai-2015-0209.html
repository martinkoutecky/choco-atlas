The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-horizon aggregate resource MDP with an explicitly listed state and transition model, τ customer types of positive rational masses μ_t summing to 1, and per-capita allocations subject to mass-scaled resource constraints, compute an MMEU-optimal policy and a valid lower and upper certificate for the MEMU optimum. Each member of type t receives the same trajectory-dependent per-capita cumulative reward G_t^π, so the objectives are max_π min_t E[G_t^π] and max_π E[min_t G_t^π]. For a requested tolerance ε, certify the MMEU policy as ε-optimal whenever the certificate gap is at most ε; otherwise report that the certificate is inconclusive.

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.

For \(m\geq1\) resources, action \(a\) specifies nonnegative rational per-capita allocations \(q_{tj}(a)\); it is feasible in state \(s\) exactly when \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tq_{tj}(a)\leq b_j(s)\) for every resource \(j\), where budgets are nonnegative rationals. Policies are centralized randomized nonstationary Markov policies choosing feasible actions. All members of a type share the same realized reward trajectory; expectations include policy and transition randomness.
Define \(G_t^\pi=\sum_{h=1}^{H}r_t(s_h,a_h,s_{h+1})\), \(F(\pi)=\min_t\mathbb E[G_t^\pi]\), and \(J(\pi)=\mathbb E[\min_tG_t^\pi]\). Both maxima below range over the specified policy class.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact MMEU-based MEMU certification is NP-hard

The specified problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with one resource, one action, and rewards in \(\{0,1\}\). Computing an MMEU optimizer and its upper endpoint is polynomial-time solvable. The difficulty lies in the exact lower endpoint; even deciding whether the prescribed certificate is conclusive is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

1. Polynomial-time MMEU optimization

Let \(S\) be the state set, and let \(A_s\) denote the feasible actions in state \(s\). These sets can be computed exactly: first calculate each action’s resource loads

\(\displaystyle w_j(a)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tq_{tj}(a),\)

then compare them with every state’s budgets. This takes \(O(mK(\tau+n))\) rational arithmetic operations. Every \(A_s\) is nonempty by hypothesis.

Define the expected one-step reward

\(\displaystyle \bar r_t(s,a)=\sum_{s'\in S}P(s'\mid s,a)r_t(s,a,s').\)

Computing all these coefficients takes \(O(\tau n^2K)\) rational arithmetic operations.

Introduce occupancy variables \(x_h(s,a)\), for \(a\in A_s\), and a scalar \(z\). Solve the rational linear program maximizing \(z\), subject to

\(\displaystyle x_h(s,a)\geq0,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a\in A_s}x_1(s,a)=\mathbf 1_{\{s=s_{\mathrm{init}}\}},\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a\in A_s}x_h(s,a)=\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{a\in A_v}P(s\mid v,a)x_{h-1}(v,a)\quad(2\leq h\leq H),\)

and

\(\displaystyle z\leq\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{s\in S}\sum_{a\in A_s}x_h(s,a)\bar r_t(s,a)\quad(1\leq t\leq\tau).\)

Every admissible policy induces a feasible occupancy vector through

\(\displaystyle x_h(s,a)=\Pr(s_h=s,a_h=a).\)

Conversely, given feasible occupancies, set \(d_h(s)=\sum_{a\in A_s}x_h(s,a)\), and, whenever \(d_h(s)>0\), define

\(\displaystyle \pi_h(a\mid s)=\frac{x_h(s,a)}{d_h(s)}.\)

At states with \(d_h(s)=0\), choose any feasible action. Induction on \(h\), using the initial and flow constraints, shows that this policy has precisely the prescribed occupancies. In particular,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[G_t^\pi]=\sum_{h,s,a}x_h(s,a)\bar r_t(s,a).\)

Thus the LP optimum equals \(\max_\pi F(\pi)\), and normalization of an optimal occupancy vector produces an MMEU optimizer.

For completeness, let \(R=\max_{t,s,a,s'}|r_t(s,a,s')|\). Flow conservation implies that occupancies at each stage sum to \(1\), so every type’s expected total reward lies in \([-HR,HR]\). Adding \(-HR\leq z\leq HR\) therefore preserves the optimum and makes the feasible region bounded. It is nonempty because feasible actions exist in every state.

The standard rational linear-programming theorem gives an exact rational optimum and optimizer in polynomial bit time. Its hypotheses hold here: the LP is explicitly represented, rational, feasible, and bounded. It has at most \(HnK+1\) variables and \(O(HnK+Hn+\tau)\) constraints, with total encoding length polynomial in the input parameters. Consequently, computing \(\pi_F\) and \(u=F(\pi_F)\) takes

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,n,K,H,L)\)

bit operations, and the returned policy has polynomial encoding length.

The support masses enter this construction through feasibility; no expansion into discrete individuals is necessary.

2. Validity of the certificate

For every policy \(\pi\) and every type \(t\), the pointwise inequality \(\min_vG_v^\pi\leq G_t^\pi\) gives

\(\displaystyle J(\pi)\leq\mathbb E[G_t^\pi].\)

Taking the minimum over \(t\) yields \(J(\pi)\leq F(\pi)\). Since \(\pi_F\) maximizes \(F\),

\(\displaystyle J(\pi)\leq F(\pi)\leq F(\pi_F)=u.\)

The policy space is a nonempty finite product of simplices. Both objectives are continuous, because trajectory expectations are finite sums of products of policy probabilities. Hence their maxima exist, and

\(\displaystyle \ell=J(\pi_F)\leq\max_\pi J(\pi)\leq u.\)

It follows that

\(\displaystyle \max_\pi J(\pi)-J(\pi_F)\leq u-\ell.\)

Therefore \(u-\ell\leq\varepsilon\) is a valid certificate of additive \(\varepsilon\)-optimality.

3. Hardness with a unique policy

We reduce from \(\mathrm{3SAT}\) with exactly three distinct variables per clause, an NP-complete restriction. [Tovey (1984)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(84)90081-7)

Let the formula be

\(\displaystyle \varphi=\bigwedge_{t=1}^{c}C_t,\)

with variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_d\), where \(c\geq1\). Each clause contains three literals on distinct variables.

Society and feasibility

Create one type for each clause and one additional type, called the anchor. Thus \(\tau=c+1\).

Choose the positive dyadic masses

\(\displaystyle \mu_t=2^{-t}\quad(1\leq t<\tau),\qquad \mu_\tau=2^{-(\tau-1)}.\)

Their sum is \(1\), since

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau-1}2^{-t}+2^{-(\tau-1)}=1.\)

Their total binary encoding length is \(O(\tau^2)\).

Set \(m=K=1\), give the sole action allocation \(0\) to every type, and give every state budget \(0\). The sole action is feasible everywhere. Consequently, there is exactly one policy in the specified class, denoted \(\pi^\circ\); it is necessarily MMEU-optimal.

Random assignment generation

Use the states

\(\displaystyle S=\{s_0\}\cup\{s_{i,b}:1\leq i\leq d,\ b\in\{0,1\}\},\)

with initial state \(s_0\) and horizon \(H=d\). Thus \(n=2d+1\).

From \(s_0\), transition to each of \(s_{1,0},s_{1,1}\) with probability \(1/2\). From every \(s_{i,b}\) with \(i<d\), transition to each of \(s_{i+1,0},s_{i+1,1}\) with probability \(1/2\). The final-layer states are absorbing.

A horizon-length trajectory therefore determines an assignment \(\alpha\in\{0,1\}^d\), where entering \(s_{i,b}\) sets \(\alpha_i=b\). Every assignment has probability \(2^{-d}\).

Rewards and objectives

For clause type \(t\), the reward on a transition entering layer \(i\) is \(1\) exactly when the assignment to \(x_i\) satisfies a literal of \(C_t\); otherwise it is \(0\).

Because each clause uses distinct variables,

\(\displaystyle G_t^{\pi^\circ}(\alpha)=\#\{\text{true literals of }C_t\text{ under }\alpha\}.\)

The anchor receives reward \(1\) on the first transition and \(0\) thereafter. Its total reward is always \(1\). Set all other tabulated rewards to \(0\), including rewards on absorbing transitions.

Each clause literal is true with probability \(1/2\). By linearity of expectation,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[G_t^{\pi^\circ}]=\frac32\quad(1\leq t\leq c),\qquad \mathbb E[G_\tau^{\pi^\circ}]=1.\)

Hence the required upper endpoint is

\(\displaystyle u=F(\pi^\circ)=1.\)

If \(\alpha\) fails to satisfy \(\varphi\), some clause type has total reward \(0\); all rewards are nonnegative, so the realized minimum is \(0\). If \(\alpha\) satisfies \(\varphi\), every clause type has integer total reward at least \(1\), and the anchor has total reward exactly \(1\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \min_tG_t^{\pi^\circ}(\alpha)=\mathbf 1_{\{\alpha\models\varphi\}}.\)

Writing \(N(\varphi)\) for the number of satisfying assignments, the exact lower endpoint is consequently

\(\displaystyle \ell=J(\pi^\circ)=\frac{N(\varphi)}{2^d}.\)

In particular, exact endpoint computation determines satisfiability by testing whether \(\ell>0\).

The certificate status itself decides satisfiability

Choose the positive dyadic tolerance

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=1-2^{-(d+1)}.\)

Its binary encoding length is \(O(d)\). The certificate is conclusive precisely when

\(\displaystyle u-\ell\leq\varepsilon \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \ell\geq2^{-(d+1)}.\)

If \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable, then \(\ell=0\), so the certificate is inconclusive. If it is satisfiable, then \(N(\varphi)\geq1\), giving

\(\displaystyle \ell\geq2^{-d}>2^{-(d+1)},\)

so the certificate is conclusive.

Thus the required certificate status equals the satisfiability answer. This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction to the certificate decision on instances with a unique policy.

All tables are explicitly constructible in polynomial time: their sizes are \(O(d^2)\) for transitions and \(O(cd^2)\) for rewards, alongside the polynomial mass encoding and unary horizon. The reduction satisfies every promised input condition. Therefore the full certification problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, independently of the freedom to return any MMEU optimizer.

4. Exact running-time upper bound and tractable cases

After computing a rational MMEU optimizer by the LP, enumerate all trajectories. There are at most \((nK)^H\). For a trajectory \(\omega=(s_1,a_1,\ldots,a_H,s_{H+1})\), compute

\(\displaystyle p_\omega=\prod_{h=1}^{H}\pi_{F,h}(a_h\mid s_h)P(s_{h+1}\mid s_h,a_h)\)

and

\(\displaystyle v_\omega=\min_t\sum_{h=1}^{H}r_t(s_h,a_h,s_{h+1}).\)

Then \(\ell=\sum_\omega p_\omega v_\omega\), exactly.

The arithmetic has polynomial bit cost per trajectory. To see this, let \(D_P,D_r,D_\pi\) be the products of all denominators in the transition, reward, and returned-policy tables. Every summand’s denominator divides

\(\displaystyle D_r(D_PD_\pi)^H.\)

This common denominator has polynomial bit length because the policy encoding is polynomial and \(H\) is unary. Moreover, every partial sum has absolute value at most \(HR\): its terms satisfy \(|p_\omega v_\omega|\leq p_\omega HR\), and trajectory probabilities sum to \(1\). Hence numerator lengths also remain polynomial.

Computing each trajectory’s rewards and minimum takes \(O(\tau H)\) rational operations. A complete exact algorithm therefore has bit running time bounded by

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,n,K,H,L) +(nK)^H\,\tau H\,\operatorname{poly}(L,HnK,\tau).\)

Once the endpoints are computed, comparison with \(\varepsilon\) takes polynomial bit time.

There are useful tractable boundaries:

  • If \(\tau=1\), then \(J(\pi)=F(\pi)\) for every policy. The LP solves the complete task, and the certificate gap is \(0\).
  • For any fixed constant \(H\), trajectory enumeration is polynomial in the remaining parameters.

For variable unary \(H\) and variable support size, the hardness reduction applies. Thus polynomial-time MMEU optimization does not imply polynomial-time exact certification unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\). This is consistent with Zhang and Shah’s proposal to estimate the lower endpoint by Monte Carlo; that proposal does not supply the exact rational endpoint required here. [Zhang and Shah (2015), Proposition 1 and subsequent discussion](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9699/9558)
