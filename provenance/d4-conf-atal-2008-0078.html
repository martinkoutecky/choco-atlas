Given a one-agent CBG template with Boolean variables [Phi[0m (that is, [0m\(\Phi\)), costs \(c\), and goal [gamma[0m (that is, [0m\(\gamma\)), together with a finite-support rational distribution [x_xi[0m (that is, [0m\(x_\xi\)) over local valuations [xi subset Phi[0m (that is, [0m\(\xi\subseteq\Phi\)), decide whether it is in the atomless core: whether there is no measurable coalition of positive mass whose members can change only their private Boolean variables so that every member strictly prefers their resulting local valuation under the template's goal-first, cost-second preferences.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(\Phi\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) Boolean variables, let \(\gamma\) be an explicitly represented propositional formula over \(\Phi\), and let \(c:\Phi\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\) give binary rational costs.
Each individual controls a private copy of all variables in \(\Phi\); its goal is evaluated solely on its own local valuation. For \(\xi\subseteq\Phi\), set \(k(\xi)=\sum_{p\in\xi}c(p)\) and \(u(\xi)=(1+\sum_{p\in\Phi}c(p))\mathbf{1}_{\xi\models\gamma}-k(\xi)\). Strict preference means strictly greater utility; equal utilities are indifference.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) distinct local valuations with positive mass, each mass \(x_\xi\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_\xi x_\xi=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number \(2^m\) of possible types.
Realize the society on \([0,1]\) with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\), partitioned into measurable sets \(A_\xi\) of measure \(x_\xi\); the initial valuation map is \(v(t)=\xi\) on \(A_\xi\).
A blocking coalition is a measurable set \(C\) with \(\lambda(C)>0\), together with a measurable map \(w:[0,1]\to 2^\Phi\), such that \(w(t)=v(t)\) outside \(C\) and \(u(w(t))>u(v(t))\) for every \(t\in C\). Members may choose different valuations, including valuations outside the input support. Atomless core membership means that no such coalition exists.
Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including the formula, costs, support valuations, and masses; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is atomless core membership in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete CBG core membership is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete even with one agent and the empty initial valuation ([Dunne et al., 2008](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/michael.wooldridge/pubs/aamas2008d.pdf)).
No result settling this exact atomless problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Costs lacked a finite encoding and an explicit sign convention; use binary rational nonnegative costs, allowing zero as the discrete model does.
  • Specify a nonempty variable set and an explicitly represented, unrestricted propositional goal formula.
  • Interpret the one-agent template as giving each individual a private copy of every template variable, with entirely local goal evaluation.
  • Normalize masses to one and realize each positive-mass type as a measurable subset of an atomless probability space.
  • Allow coalition members to choose different measurable local valuations, including valuations absent from the initial support, while leaving outsiders unchanged.
  • Use the template's utility formula to specify strict preference and indifference without tie-breaking.
  • Define L as the total encoded input length.

Result.

Atomless core membership is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions, even when \(\tau=1\), the supported valuation is empty, and every variable has cost one.

Consequently, it belongs to \(\mathrm{P}\) if and only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). It is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. Under polynomial-time many-one reductions, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard if and only if \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\). Thus the question’s proposed dichotomy needs this reduction-type qualification.

1. Characterizing blocking coalitions.

Write \(S\subseteq 2^\Phi\) for the input support, and define \(U^*=\max_{\eta\subseteq\Phi}u(\eta)\). This maximum exists because \(2^\Phi\) is finite.

We claim that the society belongs to the atomless core if and only if \(u(\xi)=U^*\) for every \(\xi\in S\).

Suppose first that some \(\xi\in S\) and \(\eta\subseteq\Phi\) satisfy \(u(\eta)>u(\xi)\). Take \(C=A_\xi\), and define \(w(t)=\eta\) on \(A_\xi\) and \(w(t)=v(t)\) elsewhere. Because \(A_\xi\) is measurable and \(v\) is a measurable map with finite range, \(w\) is measurable. Moreover, \(\lambda(C)=x_\xi>0\), and every member of \(C\) strictly improves. Hence \(C\) blocks.

Conversely, suppose every supported valuation attains \(U^*\). For every individual \(t\) and every alternative valuation \(\eta\), we have \(u(\eta)\leq U^*=u(v(t))\). Thus no individual can strictly improve, so no blocking coalition exists.

In particular, nonmembership is equivalent to the existence of a pair \((\xi,\eta)\) with \(\xi\in S\), \(\eta\subseteq\Phi\), and \(u(\eta)>u(\xi)\). The alternative \(\eta\) need not belong to the support. This characterization also proves that membership is independent of the particular measurable realization and of the positive masses.

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{coNP}\).

A certificate of nonmembership consists of a support index and an alternative valuation \(\eta\), using \(O(\log(\tau+1)+m)\) bits. The verifier evaluates \(\gamma\) at the two valuations and compares their utilities exactly.

To justify polynomial-time rational arithmetic, write \(c(p)=a_p/b_p\), where \(a_p\) is a nonnegative integer and \(b_p\) a positive integer. Let \(Q=\prod_{p\in\Phi}b_p\) and \(d_p=a_p(Q/b_p)\). Multiplication by the positive integer \(Q\) preserves strict utility comparisons, and

\(Q\,u(\eta)=\left(Q+\sum_{p\in\Phi}d_p\right)\mathbf{1}_{\eta\models\gamma}-\sum_{p\in\eta}d_p.\)

All these integers have \(O(L)\) bits: the bit length of \(Q\) is bounded by the total denominator encoding length, and multiplication by a numerator and summation add at most \(O(L)\) further bits. Schoolbook integer arithmetic therefore suffices for polynomial-time verification.

A conservative verification bound, including reading the input, is \(O((\tau+1)mL^2)\) bit operations. Hence nonmembership belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), and membership belongs to \(\mathrm{coNP}\).

3. Hardness with one supported valuation.

We give a polynomial-time many-one reduction from propositional unsatisfiability, which is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete.

Let \(F\) be an arbitrary propositional formula on a variable set \(X\). Introduce a fresh variable \(q\), and construct the instance with \(\Phi=X\cup\{q\}\), \(\gamma=F\land q\), and \(c(p)=1\) for every \(p\in\Phi\). Its sole supported valuation is \(\varnothing\), with mass one. Thus \(\tau=1\), and \(\Phi\) is nonempty.

Since \(q\) is false at \(\varnothing\), its utility is \(u(\varnothing)=0\).

If \(F\) is satisfiable, choose a satisfying valuation \(\zeta\subseteq X\) and put \(\eta=\zeta\cup\{q\}\). Then \(\eta\models\gamma\), and, writing \(m=|\Phi|\),

\(u(\eta)=1+m-|\eta|\geq 1>0=u(\varnothing).\)

By Section 1, the society is outside the core.

If \(F\) is unsatisfiable, no valuation satisfies \(\gamma\). Therefore every \(\eta\subseteq\Phi\) has utility \(u(\eta)=-|\eta|\leq 0=u(\varnothing)\). Again by Section 1, the society belongs to the core.

We have proved \(F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}\) if and only if its constructed society belongs to the atomless core. The construction has polynomial size, establishing \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hardness. Together with Section 2, this proves \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-completeness.

This reduction follows the same formula construction used for the discrete result in [Dunne et al., Theorem 1](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/michael.wooldridge/pubs/aamas2008d.pdf); Section 1 supplies the explicit justification for the present atomless model.

4. Running time and the meaning of hardness.

An exact deterministic algorithm computes \(b=\min_{\xi\in S}u(\xi)\), enumerates all \(2^m\) valuations, and rejects membership if it finds an \(\eta\) with \(u(\eta)>b\). The characterization in Section 1 proves correctness.

Using the integer scaling above, a conservative running-time bound is \(O((2^m+\tau+1)mL^2)\) bit operations. Thus fixed \(m\) gives polynomial time, but fixed support size does not remove the hardness: the reduction already has \(\tau=1\).

Finally:

  • A polynomial-time membership algorithm would decide \(\mathrm{SAT}\) by applying the reduction and reversing its answer. Hence membership belongs to \(\mathrm{P}\) exactly when \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
  • The same single-query procedure proves polynomial-time Turing \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.
  • If membership were many-one \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, its inclusion in \(\mathrm{coNP}\), together with closure under polynomial-time many-one preimages, would imply \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{coNP}\), hence \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\). Conversely, if those classes coincide, its \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-completeness makes it many-one \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.
