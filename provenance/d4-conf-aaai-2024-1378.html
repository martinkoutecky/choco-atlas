Result

The supported mass-threshold problem is \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. In particular, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. A polynomial-time algorithm would therefore imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

A deterministic algorithm runs in time \(O(\tau\,8^m\,\operatorname{poly}(L))\). Thus bounded support size alone does not remove the hardness, whereas fixed \(m\) gives polynomial-time solvability.

1. Applicability of the single-framework result

Write \(C(D,a)\) for the statement that some \(\Theta\)-complete extension of \(D\) contains \(a\).

The cited paper’s Theorem 50 and Table 1 establish that deciding \(C(D,a)\) is \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\)-complete for finite flat ABA+ frameworks. Its Definitions 3–6 use the same derivation trees, preferences, attack reversal, and admissibility as the task. Definitions 45 and 47 give the same \(\Theta\)-defense and completeness condition. In particular, they quantify over primitive normal or reversed attacks, exactly as the definition using \(B_D\) does. All the stated hypotheses therefore apply. [Dimopoulos et al., *Redefining ABA+ Semantics via Abstract Set-to-Set Attacks*](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/28918/29747)

We use this established theorem for both hardness and membership.

2. Hardness, including a two-type society

A singleton support already transfers the single-framework hardness: give \((D,a)\) mass \(1\) and choose threshold \(1/2\).

The reduction also works with two distinct types, equal positive masses, and a threshold strictly between \(0\) and \(1\).

Given \((D,a)\), choose fresh sentences \(z,\bar z\) and define \(D_0\) by

  • \(\mathcal L_0=\{z,\bar z\}\);
  • \(A_0=\{z\}\);
  • \(\mathcal R_0=\{\bar z\leftarrow\}\);
  • \(c_0(z)=\bar z\);
  • \(\preceq_0=\{(z,z)\}\).

This is finite and flat, its assumption set is nonempty, and its preference relation is reflexive and transitive.

The empty-body rule gives \(\varnothing\vdash\bar z\), so
\((\varnothing,\{z\})\in B_{D_0}\). Consequently, every set containing \(z\) attacks itself. No admissible set, and hence no \(\Theta\)-complete extension, contains \(z\). Thus \(C(D_0,z)\) is false.

Construct the society
\[ \]
with support \(\{(D,a),(D_0,z)\}\), masses \(\mu_1=\mu_2=1/2\), and threshold \(q=1/2\). The fresh sentences ensure that the types are distinct. Its accepted mass is exactly

\(\displaystyle \frac12\,\mathbf 1[C(D,a)]+\frac12\,\mathbf 1[C(D_0,z)] =\frac12\,\mathbf 1[C(D,a)].\)

It reaches the threshold precisely when \(C(D,a)\) holds.

The construction has polynomial size and leaves \(m\) unchanged, because the input already has at least one assumption. Therefore the supported problem is \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\)-hard even when \(\tau=2\), both masses are \(1/2\), and \(q=1/2\).

3. Membership in \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\)

For each type, put \(x_t=\mathbf 1[C(D_t,a_t)]\). Positivity of the masses gives the equivalence

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t x_t\ge q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\tau\}: \sum_{t\in J}\mu_t\ge q \ \text{and}\ C(D_t,a_t)\text{ holds for every }t\in J. \)

For the forward implication, take \(J=\{t:x_t=1\}\). For the reverse implication, every selected type contributes to the full accepted mass, and every additional contribution is nonnegative.

Since single-framework acceptance belongs to \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\), there is a polynomial-time predicate \(R\), with polynomially bounded strings \(u,v\), such that

\(\displaystyle C(D,a)\Longleftrightarrow\exists u\,\forall v\,R(D,a,u,v).\)

Accordingly, the society condition can be written as

\(\displaystyle \exists J,(u_t)_{t=1}^{\tau}\ \forall(v_t)_{t=1}^{\tau}: \left[\sum_{t\in J}\mu_t\ge q\right] \land \bigwedge_{t\in J}R(D_t,a_t,u_t,v_t). \)

This formula is equivalent to the preceding subset condition. In particular, for any selected \(t\), universal quantification over the tuple includes every possible \(v_t\), so the selected \(u_t\) certifies \(C(D_t,a_t)\).

The support is explicitly listed, so \(\tau\le L\). After padding the individual strings to suitable polynomial bounds, the total existential and universal string lengths are polynomial in \(L\).

The rational comparison is also polynomial-time. Write \(\mu_t=b_t/d_t\) and \(q=r/s\), with positive denominators, and use the common denominator \(Q=s\prod_t d_t\). Its binary length is bounded by the sum of the input denominator lengths. The comparison becomes

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in J} b_t(Q/d_t)\ge r(Q/s),\)

using integers of polynomial binary length.

Thus the quantified formula has a polynomial-time matrix and polynomially bounded quantifiers. The problem belongs to \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\), completing the classification.

4. An explicit deterministic running-time bound

The exact-support requirement in \(U\vdash p\) matters: ordinary Horn closure alone does not enumerate those supports. The following algorithm preserves them.

Fix one framework, with \(n=|A|\le m\) and \(N=|\mathcal L|\).

4.1 Enumerating exact derivation supports

For each sentence \(p\), maintain a family \(F(p)\subseteq 2^A\). Initialize \(F(a)=\{\{a\}\}\) for assumptions \(a\), and initialize all other families to be empty.

Repeatedly apply each rule \(p\leftarrow p_1,\ldots,p_k\), adding to \(F(p)\) every union \(U_1\cup\cdots\cup U_k\) with \(U_i\in F(p_i)\). An empty-body rule adds \(\varnothing\).

Every inserted support has a derivation tree: join the trees witnessing the selected body supports beneath the rule. Conversely, induction on tree height shows that every finite derivation tree contributes its support after finitely many rounds. Hence, at termination,

\(\displaystyle F(p)=\{U\subseteq A:U\vdash p\}.\)

There are at most \(N2^n\) sentence-support pairs. Each nonfinal round inserts at least one new pair, so there are at most \(N2^n+1\) rounds.

For one rule, compute its possible unions one body sentence at a time. Each intermediate family contains at most \(2^n\) sets; combining two families examines at most \(4^n\) pairs. Therefore one round costs \(O(4^n\operatorname{poly}(L))\), and the whole saturation costs \(O(8^n\operatorname{poly}(L))\). This argument also covers cyclic rules.

4.2 Checking extensions

The support families determine \(B_D\) directly. There are at most \(n2^n\) primitive attacks, because every attack arises from a pair \((U,b)\).

Precompute \(S\rightsquigarrow T\) for all \(4^n\) ordered pairs of assumption sets by scanning \(B_D\). Including subset-test costs, this takes \(O(n^2 8^n)\) time.

Now enumerate every candidate \(E\subseteq A\). Using the table:

  • Check \(E\not\rightsquigarrow E\).
  • Check every \(T\subseteq A\) for the implication \(T\rightsquigarrow E\Rightarrow E\rightsquigarrow T\).
  • Compute \(\Theta_D(E)\) by scanning the primitive attacks and check \(\Theta_D(E)\subseteq E\).
  • Check whether the queried assumption lies in \(E\).

These are exactly the defining conditions, so the search decides \(C(D,a)\). Its additional cost fits within \(O(8^n\operatorname{poly}(L))\).

Applying this procedure to all supported types and then comparing their accepted mass exactly gives total running time

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(\tau\,8^m\,\operatorname{poly}(L)\right).\)

The algorithm depends on the explicitly listed support, rather than on the number of possible types.
