The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(T_M,T_W\), rational masses \(p_i,q_j\), strict rankings of opposite-side types, and hidden utilities \(u_i(j),v_j(i)\) consistent with those rankings, define \(\mathcal S_\infty\) as the closure of aggregate pair-mass matrices of stable one-to-one matchings in rational blow-ups under a fixed refinement of within-type ties. An ordinal algorithm sees only the masses and type rankings and outputs an ex-post stable mass plan \(x\); determine \(\inf_A\sup_I \frac{\max_{y\in\mathcal S_\infty(I)}SW_I(y)}{SW_I(A(I))}\), in particular whether it equals \(2\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(T_M,T_W\) be nonempty finite sets of types, with positive binary rational masses \(p_i,q_j\) satisfying \(\sum_i p_i=\sum_j q_j=1\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|T_M|+|T_W|\) positive-mass types, not the number of possible types. Put \(m=\max\{|T_M|,|T_W|\}\); state any running times in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the binary input length.
Each type has a complete strict ranking of opposite-side types. Hidden utilities \(u_i(j),v_j(i)\) are finite nonnegative reals, weakly decreasing along these rankings, with no utility normalization; all individuals of a type share its utilities.
For every positive integer \(N\) making all \(Np_i,Nq_j\) integral, form a one-to-one stable marriage instance with those numbers of copies. Copies inherit type rankings; within each opposite-side type, every individual ranks copies by increasing copy index. This public refinement is fixed independently of utilities.
For a stable perfect matching \(\mu\) in such a blow-up, let \(z_{ij}(\mu)\) be its number of type-\((i,j)\) pairs divided by \(N\). Define \(\mathcal S_\infty(I)\) as the Euclidean closure of all these matrices over all admissible \(N\).
An ex-post stable mass plan is the barycenter \(x\) of a probability distribution supported on \(\mathcal S_\infty(I)\), equivalently \(x\in\operatorname{conv}\mathcal S_\infty(I)\); it satisfies \(x\geq0\), \(\sum_jx_{ij}=p_i\), and \(\sum_ix_{ij}=q_j\). Define \(SW_I(x)=\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}(u_i(j)+v_j(i))\).
An ordinal algorithm \(A\) sees only masses and rankings and returns such a barycenter \(A(I)\). The infimum below ranges over all such algorithms without a running-time restriction; the supremum ranges over all finite supports, masses, rankings, and consistent hidden utilities. Interpret \(0/0=1\) and a positive numerator divided by zero as \(+\infty\).

What is \(D_\infty=\inf_A\sup_I\frac{\max_{y\in\mathcal S_\infty(I)}SW_I(y)}{SW_I(A(I))}\)? In particular, does \(D_\infty=2\), or does it have a different value?

For finite individual societies, every ordinal randomized stable algorithm has distortion at least \(2\), and choosing the two proposer-optimal stable matchings equiprobably achieves \(2\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14961v1).
No result settling this precise blow-up-closure distortion problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required nonempty supports and equal total mass, normalized to one on each side, reflecting the balanced discrete market.
  • Specified complete rankings, nonnegative finite real utilities, weak consistency allowing utility ties, and no utility normalization.
  • Made rational blow-ups precise using every common integral scaling N and pair counts divided by N, with Euclidean closure.
  • Fixed the unspecified within-type refinement to increasing copy index, publicly and independently of utilities.
  • Interpreted ex-post stable output as a distribution over limiting stable matrices, represented by its barycenter, rather than requiring the barycenter itself to belong to the unconvexified stable set.
  • Specified the welfare formula, the unrestricted algorithm class, the scope of the supremum, and zero-denominator conventions.
  • Defined m, total support size tau, and binary input length L for any running-time statements.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The exact value is \(\boxed{D_\infty=2}\).

The key point is that taking all blow-ups and then closing does not enlarge the set of attainable barycenters: their convex hull already equals the convex hull of the stable type matrices in the smallest integral blow-up. This requires a proof specific to the fixed copy-index refinement.

1. Stable type plans and their refined realizations

Fix the masses and rankings. Write \(j'\succ_i j\) when male type \(i\) prefers female type \(j'\) to \(j\), and \(i'\succ_j i\) for the corresponding female ranking.

For a feasible mass matrix \(x\), define
\[ r_{ij}(x)=\sum_{j':\,j\succ_i j'}x_{ij'}, \qquad s_{ij}(x)=\sum_{i':\,i\succ_j i'}x_{i'j}. \]
Thus \(r_{ij}(x)\) is the mass of type \(i\) assigned to types strictly worse than \(j\), and \(s_{ij}(x)\) is the mass of type \(j\) assigned to types strictly worse than \(i\).

Let \(\mathcal W\) be the set of feasible matrices satisfying \(r_{ij}(x)s_{ij}(x)=0\) for every type pair \((i,j)\).

Lemma 1. Every matrix in \(\mathcal S_\infty(I)\) belongs to \(\mathcal W\).

Proof. First consider a stable matching in an admissible blow-up. If both \(r_{ij}(x)>0\) and \(s_{ij}(x)>0\), some man of type \(i\) has a partner of a type strictly worse than \(j\), and some woman of type \(j\) has a partner of a type strictly worse than \(i\). These two individuals strictly prefer each other to their partners, regardless of copy indices. They therefore block the matching.

Consequently every finite-blow-up matrix satisfies the stated equations. Feasibility constraints and the equations \(r_{ij}(x)s_{ij}(x)=0\) are closed conditions, so they also hold throughout the Euclidean closure. ∎

The converse for integral plans uses the public refinement essentially.

Lemma 2. Suppose \(x\in\mathcal W\), and \(N\) is admissible with every \(n_{ij}=Nx_{ij}\) integral. Then \(x\) is realized by a stable matching in the specified \(N\)-blow-up.

Proof. Construct a matching as follows.

  • For each male type \(i\), partition its copies, in increasing index order, into consecutive blocks of sizes \(n_{ij}\), with the female types ordered from best to worst according to \(i\).
  • For each female type \(j\), partition its copies similarly into blocks of sizes \(n_{ij}\), with the male types ordered according to \(j\).
  • For every \((i,j)\), match the two corresponding blocks in increasing index order.

The marginal constraints ensure that these partitions exhaust all copies and that corresponding blocks have equal sizes.

Consider an unmatched man \(a\) of type \(i\) and woman \(b\) of type \(j\). Let their current partner types be \(j_0\) and \(i_0\), respectively. If \(j_0\succ_i j\), or \(i_0\succ_j i\), at least one individual rejects the proposed deviation. The remaining possibilities are:

  • If both current partner types are strictly worse, then \(r_{ij}(x)>0\) and \(s_{ij}(x)>0\), contradicting \(x\in\mathcal W\).
  • If \(j\succ_i j_0\) and \(i_0=i\), man \(a\) lies after every type-\(i\) copy assigned to type \(j\). In particular, his index exceeds that of woman \(b\)'s current partner. Woman \(b\) therefore rejects him.
  • If \(j_0=j\) and \(i\succ_j i_0\), the symmetric argument shows that man \(a\) rejects woman \(b\).
  • If \(j_0=j\) and \(i_0=i\), both individuals belong to the corresponding \((i,j)\) blocks. Let their positions within those blocks be \(h\) and \(k\). Increasing-index matching pairs equal positions. Man \(a\) prefers woman \(b\) to his partner exactly when \(k<h\), whereas woman \(b\) prefers man \(a\) exactly when \(h<k\). Both cannot hold.

Thus there is no blocking pair. ∎

2. The smallest blow-up already generates every barycenter

Let \(d\) be the least common multiple of the denominators of all masses, in reduced form. The admissible population sizes are precisely the positive multiples of \(d\).

Let \(\mathcal B_d\) be the finite set of normalized type matrices of stable matchings in the \(d\)-blow-up.

Lemma 3. Every \(x\in\mathcal W\) belongs to \(\operatorname{conv}\mathcal B_d\).

Proof. Fix \(x\in\mathcal W\). Consider the transportation polytope
\[ P_x=\left\{t\geq0: \sum_jt_{ij}=dp_i,\quad \sum_it_{ij}=dq_j,\quad t_{ij}=0\text{ whenever }x_{ij}=0 \right\}. \]
It is nonempty because it contains \(dx\), and it is bounded.

Every vertex of \(P_x\) is integral. Here is a direct justification. Form the bipartite graph of its positive coordinates. A cycle would permit a sufficiently small alternating increase and decrease around that cycle, preserving all marginals and nonnegativity. This would express the point as the midpoint of two distinct feasible points, contradicting vertexhood. Thus a vertex has forest support.

In a forest, an edge incident to a leaf must equal that leaf's remaining marginal. All initial marginals are integral. Repeatedly removing leaves and subtracting their incident edge values therefore proves that every positive coordinate is integral.

Express \(dx\) as a convex combination of vertices of \(P_x\), say \(dx=\sum_{\ell=1}^R\alpha_\ell t^\ell\), where \(\alpha_\ell\geq0\) and \(\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell=1\). Put \(y^\ell=t^\ell/d\).

Each \(y^\ell\) has support contained in that of \(x\). This implies \(y^\ell\in\mathcal W\): if both strictly-worse masses were positive for \(y^\ell\) at some pair, the corresponding masses would also be positive for \(x\), contradicting its defining condition.

Since \(dy^\ell\) is integral, Lemma 2 realizes \(y^\ell\) by a stable matching in the \(d\)-blow-up. Hence \(y^\ell\in\mathcal B_d\), and \(x=\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell y^\ell\). ∎

It follows that
\[ \operatorname{conv}\mathcal S_\infty(I)=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal B_d. \]
Indeed, Lemmas 1 and 3 give one inclusion; the reverse inclusion follows from \(\mathcal B_d\subseteq\mathcal S_\infty(I)\).

In fact, the lemmas also prove \(\mathcal S_\infty(I)=\mathcal W\). To see the missing inclusion, take the decomposition from Lemma 3 and approximate its coefficients by nonnegative rational coefficients summing to one. If their common denominator is \(k\), the resulting matrix has integral counts at population size \(kd\). Its support remains contained in that of \(x\), so it belongs to \(\mathcal W\) and Lemma 2 gives a stable realization. These matrices converge to \(x\).

3. An ordinal algorithm with distortion at most two

Run men-proposing and women-proposing deferred acceptance on the \(d\)-blow-up. Let their normalized type matrices be \(z^M\) and \(z^W\). Define \(A(I)=(z^M+z^W)/2\). This is an admissible barycenter.

We use the finite proposer-optimality theorem: with complete strict preferences, deferred acceptance returns a stable matching in which every proposer weakly prefers their partner to their partner in any other stable matching. This is the property underlying the finite distortion bound in the [supplied paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14961v1).

Its hypotheses hold here: the blow-up is finite and balanced, and the public refinement gives complete strict individual rankings. Moreover, individual utilities are weakly decreasing along those refined rankings, because they depend only on partner type.

For a mass matrix \(y\), write
\[ U_M(y)=\sum_{i,j}y_{ij}u_i(j), \qquad U_W(y)=\sum_{i,j}y_{ij}v_j(i). \]
Proposer optimality gives \(U_M(z^M)\geq U_M(b)\) and \(U_W(z^W)\geq U_W(b)\) for every \(b\in\mathcal B_d\). Linearity extends both inequalities to \(\operatorname{conv}\mathcal B_d\), and thus to every \(y\in\mathcal S_\infty(I)\).

Using nonnegativity,
\[ SW_I(A(I)) =\frac{SW_I(z^M)+SW_I(z^W)}2 \geq\frac{U_M(z^M)+U_W(z^W)}2 \geq\frac{SW_I(y)}2. \]
The set \(\mathcal S_\infty(I)\) is nonempty and compact: it is a closed subset of the compact feasible transportation polytope. Therefore the welfare maximum exists, and the displayed inequality proves distortion at most \(2\).

If the algorithm's welfare is zero, the same inequality forces the benchmark welfare to be zero, giving ratio \(1\) under the prescribed convention.

This is a finite algorithm. Since \(d\leq2^L\), a direct implementation that expands the smallest blow-up and runs both deferred-acceptance procedures takes \(O(4^L\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L))\) bit operations. No polynomial-time claim is needed.

4. A matching lower bound on four types

Take male types \(a,b\), female types \(c,e\), and give every type mass \(1/2\). Their rankings are
\[ a:\ c\succ e,\qquad b:\ e\succ c,\qquad c:\ b\succ a,\qquad e:\ a\succ b. \]

At admissible population size \(2\), there is one copy of each type. Both matchings are stable:

  • Pairing \(a\) with \(c\) and \(b\) with \(e\) gives every man his first choice.
  • Pairing \(a\) with \(e\) and \(b\) with \(c\) gives every woman her first choice.

Their normalized matrices consequently belong to \(\mathcal S_\infty(I)\).

Fix any ordinal algorithm and let \(x\) be its output on these masses and rankings. The female-type-\(c\) marginal gives \(x_{ac}+x_{bc}=1/2\). Hence at least one of \(x_{ac}\) and \(x_{bc}\) is at most \(1/4\).

Consider two possible hidden utility profiles:

  • Only \(u_a(c)=1\) is nonzero. These utilities respect all rankings. Welfare equals \(y_{ac}\), whose stable maximum is \(1/2\), attained by the first matching.
  • Only \(v_c(b)=1\) is nonzero. These utilities also respect all rankings. Welfare equals \(y_{bc}\), whose stable maximum is \(1/2\), attained by the second matching.

The algorithm sees identical ordinal input in both cases, so its output remains \(x\). Choosing the profile corresponding to the smaller coordinate yields distortion at least \((1/2)/(1/4)=2\). If that coordinate is zero, the distortion is infinite.

Thus every ordinal algorithm has worst-case distortion at least \(2\). Combined with the upper bound, this settles the target: \(\boxed{D_\infty=2}\).

The main substantive contribution is the reduction of all blow-up barycenters to the convex hull of stable type matrices in the smallest integral blow-up. The finite distortion theorem alone would not justify that reduction; Lemmas 1–3 supply it.

Step-by-step verification

1. Definitions and Lemma 1

The definitions of \(r_{ij}(x)\) and \(s_{ij}(x)\) correctly measure assignments to strictly worse partner types.

For a finite-blow-up matrix, simultaneous positivity provides a man of type \(i\) and a woman of type \(j\), each assigned to a strictly worse type. They cannot already be partners, and both strictly prefer the proposed deviation regardless of copy indices. Thus they constitute a blocking pair.

Each marginal constraint is closed, and each product \(r_{ij}(x)s_{ij}(x)\) is continuous. Passing these conditions to the Euclidean closure is valid. Consequently, \(\mathcal S_\infty(I)\subseteq\mathcal W\).

Assessment: Correct.

2. Lemma 2: realization under the public refinement

The integral marginal constraints ensure that the prescribed blocks exhaust the copies and that corresponding blocks have equal sizes. Pairing equal positions therefore gives a perfect matching with exactly the required type counts.

The blocking-pair analysis exhausts the possibilities:

  • If either current partner type is strictly preferred to the proposed partner type, that individual rejects the deviation.
  • If both current partner types are strictly worse, the defining condition of \(\mathcal W\) is violated.
  • If only the man would improve partner type, while the woman already has a type-\(i\) partner, the man’s copy index exceeds her current partner’s index. This follows from the ordering of the male blocks. She rejects him.
  • The symmetric case follows from the ordering of the female blocks.
  • If both current partners have the proposed types, the individuals lie in the same corresponding blocks. Equal-position matching makes their desired index inequalities opposite: \(k<h\) and \(h<k\).

Zero-size blocks cause no difficulty: any case involving an individual in a block already implies that block is nonempty.

Assessment: Correct. The proof uses precisely the refinement imposed by the task.

3. Smallest admissible blow-up

For a reduced rational mass, integrality of its product with \(N\) is equivalent to its denominator dividing \(N\). Hence the admissible sizes are exactly the positive multiples of the stated least common multiple \(d\).

Assessment: Correct.

4. Lemma 3: transportation-polytope decomposition

The polytope \(P_x\) is nonempty because it contains \(dx\). It is bounded because all coordinates are nonnegative and have fixed finite marginals.

The vertex-integrality argument is valid:

  • A cycle in the graph of positive coordinates permits opposite alternating perturbations. Taking the perturbation smaller than every coordinate on the cycle preserves nonnegativity and all imposed zero coordinates.
  • Those perturbations preserve every row and column marginal, contradicting vertexhood.
  • Thus the positive-support graph is a forest.
  • At a leaf, the incident edge equals the remaining marginal. Integral initial marginals and repeated subtraction preserve integrality throughout leaf removal.

Isolated vertices do not undermine the argument: after previous removals, feasibility forces their remaining marginal to be zero.

The use of a convex decomposition into vertices is the standard finite-dimensional bounded-polytope property, and its hypotheses are satisfied here.

For each resulting matrix \(y^\ell\), support containment correctly preserves membership in \(\mathcal W\). Indeed, a nonnegative strictly-worse sum vanishes exactly when all its contributing coordinates vanish. Both sums therefore cannot become positive after restricting support.

Lemma 2 then applies at population size \(d\).

Assessment: Correct.

5. Convex-hull equality and the stronger closure claim

The two inclusions establishing
\(\operatorname{conv}\mathcal S_\infty(I)=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal B_d\)
follow directly from Lemmas 1 and 3 and the inclusion \(\mathcal B_d\subseteq\mathcal S_\infty(I)\).

The additional claim \(\mathcal S_\infty(I)=\mathcal W\) also follows as written. Rational approximations to the finite coefficient vector can remain nonnegative and sum to one. With common denominator \(k\), their resulting matrix has integral counts at size \(kd\), preserves the required marginals, and has support contained in that of \(x\). Lemma 2 supplies stable realizations converging to \(x\).

The proof does not incorrectly assume that \(\mathcal W\) itself is convex.

Assessment: Correct.

6. Upper bound

The proposer-optimality theorem is stated sufficiently precisely. Its hypotheses hold: the expanded instance is finite, balanced, and has complete strict individual preferences. Complete preferences and equal populations also ensure that its stable matchings are perfect. The cited [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14961v1) confirms the deferred-acceptance optimality property used here.

Type-dependent utilities are weakly decreasing along the refined preferences because utilities are equal within each partner type. Thus individual proposer optimality implies the claimed aggregate utility inequalities after normalization by \(d\).

Linearity extends these inequalities from \(\mathcal B_d\) to its convex hull. The established convex-hull equality therefore makes them applicable to every benchmark matrix in \(\mathcal S_\infty(I)\).

The welfare calculation uses nonnegativity correctly when dropping the women’s welfare from \(z^M\) and the men’s welfare from \(z^W\). It establishes
\(SW_I(A(I))\geq SW_I(y)/2\)
for every benchmark matrix.

The benchmark set is nonempty by deferred acceptance and compact because it is a closed subset of the bounded feasible transportation polytope. Finite utilities make welfare continuous, so the maximum exists.

If algorithm welfare is zero, the inequality forces maximum benchmark welfare to be zero. The stipulated ratio convention then gives \(1\).

Assessment: Correct, including the zero-welfare case.

7. Running time

The bound \(d\leq 2^L\) follows because the least common multiple is at most the product of the encoded denominators, whose total binary length is at most \(L\).

The smallest blow-up has \(d\) individuals on each side. Explicit preference construction and deferred acceptance require at most quadratic work in \(d\), with polynomial overhead for indexing, arithmetic, and the type input. Thus
\(O(4^L\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L))\)
bit operations is a valid upper bound.

This establishes a finite algorithm, without asserting polynomial time in the binary support encoding. The task permits that.

Assessment: Correct.

8. Lower bound

Both displayed size-\(2\) matchings are stable. In the first, every man receives his first-choice type; in the second, every woman does. Hence neither permits a blocking pair.

Every admissible algorithm output has the female-\(c\) marginal
\(x_{ac}+x_{bc}=1/2\).
Consequently, at least one coordinate is at most \(1/4\), independently of any further restrictions on attainable barycenters.

In each profile, the benchmark value is exactly \(1/2\): feasibility bounds the relevant coordinate by \(1/2\), and the corresponding stable matching attains it.

The algorithm sees identical ordinal data in both profiles. Selecting the profile associated with its smaller coordinate therefore gives distortion at least \(2\), or infinity if that coordinate is zero.
