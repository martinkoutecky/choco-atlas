The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite rational agent types \(T\) with masses \(\mu_t\), weight densities \(\eta_t>0\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t\eta_t=1\), chore categories \(O\) with rational per-capita supplies \(q_o\), and additive costs \(v_t(o)\), choose masses \(y_{t,b}\ge0\) over whole integer bundles \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{O}\) satisfying \(\sum_b y_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}b_o=q_o\), minimizing \(\alpha\) subject to the support-wise constraints \(v_t(b)\le\alpha W_t^\infty\) whenever \(y_{t,b}>0\), where \(W_t^\infty=\eta_t\inf_z\sup\{v_t(b)/\eta_r:z_{r,b}>0\}\) and \(z_{r,b}\) denotes mass of recipient type \(r\) receiving bundle \(b\).

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types. There are \(m\ge1\) chore categories \(O\), binary-rational supplies \(q_o\ge0\), weight densities \(\eta_t>0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t\eta_t=1\), and binary-rational additive costs \(v_t(o)\ge0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A bundle is \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{O}\), including the empty bundle, with \(v_t(b)=\sum_o b_o v_t(o)\). Bundles have no exogenous size bound. A feasible allocation is a nonnegative real array \(x_{t,b}\) satisfying \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_o=q_o\); denote all such arrays by \(\mathcal F\).
Define \(W_t^\infty=\eta_t\inf_{z\in\mathcal F}\sup\{v_t(b)/\eta_r:r\in T,\ z_{r,b}>0\}\). The benchmark uses the evaluating type's costs on every recipient's bundle. For \(\alpha\ge1\), an allocation \(y\in\mathcal F\) is \(\alpha\)-WMMS if \(v_t(b)\le\alpha W_t^\infty\) whenever \(y_{t,b}>0\), including when \(W_t^\infty=0\).

Does every instance admit an \(O(\log(1+\tau))\)-WMMS allocation computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this approximation guarantee impossible, existentially or computationally?

1. Result and input convention

The requested guarantee holds. More strongly, a \(48\)-WMMS allocation can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The output has at most \(\tau(m+1)\) positive allocation entries, all with rational masses.

For the computational statement, I interpret the densities \(\eta_t\) as binary-encoded rational input numbers included in \(L\). Without specifying their encoding, the bit-complexity question is undefined. The existence conclusion also holds for arbitrary positive real densities, as explained below.

The proof uses the following finite-agent result: every finite instance with positive normalized weights and nonnegative additive chore costs admits a \(12\)-WMMS allocation. This follows from Theorems 1 and 3 of [Li, Wang and Xing](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06581v2). We use only its existence guarantee; the algorithm below never expands the population into individual agents.

2. A rounding lemma for bundle distributions

Lemma. Let \(u\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{m}\) be a vector of desired mean category counts, and let \(c\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{m}\) be additive costs. Define

\(D=\max\{c_o:u_o>0\}\),

with \(D=0\) when \(u=0\). In polynomial time, one can construct a rational probability distribution on at most \(m+1\) integer bundles such that its mean is exactly \(u\), and every supported bundle \(b\) satisfies

\(\sum_o c_ob_o\le \sum_o c_ou_o+D.\)

Proof. Coordinates with \(u_o=0\) will always be zero. Order the remaining coordinates so that \(c_1\ge\cdots\ge c_k\), where \(k\le m\). Write

\(u_j=a_j+p_j,\qquad a_j=\lfloor u_j\rfloor,\qquad 0\le p_j<1,\)

and set \(S_0=0\) and \(S_j=\sum_{i=1}^{j}p_i\).

For a uniformly distributed \(U\in[0,1)\), define

\(X_j(U)=\lfloor S_j+U\rfloor-\lfloor S_{j-1}+U\rfloor,\qquad b_j(U)=a_j+X_j(U).\)

Because \(S_j-S_{j-1}<1\), each \(X_j\) belongs to \(\{0,1\}\). The identity \(\mathbb E[\lfloor a+U\rfloor]=a\) gives \(\mathbb E[X_j]=p_j\), so \(\mathbb E[b_j]=u_j\).

Let \(K_j=\sum_{i=1}^{j}X_i=\lfloor S_j+U\rfloor\), and put \(c_{k+1}=0\). Since \(K_j\le S_j+1\), summation by parts gives

\(\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_jX_j=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(c_j-c_{j+1})K_j\le \sum_{j=1}^{k}(c_j-c_{j+1})(S_j+1)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_jp_j+c_1.\)

Adding the costs of the integer parts proves the claimed bound, because \(c_1=D\). If there are no remaining coordinates, the empty bundle suffices.

Each function \(\lfloor S_j+U\rfloor\) changes at most once inside \([0,1)\). Thus at most \(k\) breakpoints divide this interval into at most \(k+1\) intervals on which the entire bundle is constant. Their lengths are rational probabilities. Computing and sorting these breakpoints requires polynomial time, and all output numbers have polynomial bit length. \(\square\)

Applying the lemma with mean \(q\), and giving the resulting distribution to every type, shows that \(\mathcal F\) contains a finite-support allocation. Consequently, every benchmark in the question is finite.

3. Computing upper estimates of the benchmarks

Fix an evaluating type \(t\), and abbreviate \(c_o=v_t(o)\). Write

\(\lambda_t=\inf_{z\in\mathcal F}\sup\{v_t(b)/\eta_r:z_{r,b}>0\},\qquad W_t^\infty=\eta_t\lambda_t.\)

Consider the following relaxation. For a proposed value \(s\ge0\), seek nonnegative category totals \(f_{r,o}\) satisfying

\(\sum_{r\in T}f_{r,o}=q_o\quad\text{for every }o,\)

\(\sum_o c_of_{r,o}\le \mu_r\eta_rs\quad\text{for every }r,\)

and

\(f_{r,o}=0\quad\text{whenever }c_o>\eta_rs.\)

Let \(h_t\) be the infimum of feasible values \(s\). We will prove that it is attained, polynomial-time computable, and satisfies

\(h_t\le\lambda_t\le 2h_t.\)

The relaxation is a lower bound

Take any \(z\in\mathcal F\) whose benchmark objective is a finite value \(s\), and define

\(f_{r,o}=\sum_b z_{r,b}b_o.\)

These sums are finite because their sum over recipients equals the finite supply \(q_o\). Nonnegativity permits rearranging the sums, so

\(\sum_o c_of_{r,o}=\sum_b z_{r,b}v_t(b)\le \eta_rs\sum_bz_{r,b}=\mu_r\eta_rs.\)

If \(f_{r,o}>0\), some supported bundle has \(b_o>0\). Additivity and nonnegative costs imply

\(c_o\le v_t(b)\le\eta_rs.\)

Thus \(f\) satisfies the relaxation. Taking the infimum over such allocations gives \(h_t\le\lambda_t\).

The relaxed optimum is computable by polynomially many LPs

Form the rational threshold set

\(A_t=\{0\}\cup\{c_o/\eta_r:r\in T,\ o\in O\}.\)

For every \(a\in A_t\), solve the rational linear program that minimizes \(s\), subject to

\(s\ge a,\qquad f_{r,o}\ge0,\qquad \sum_r f_{r,o}=q_o,\)

\(\sum_o c_of_{r,o}\le\mu_r\eta_rs,\)

and the fixed restrictions

\(f_{r,o}=0\quad\text{if }c_o>a\eta_r.\)

Every feasible solution of one of these LPs is feasible for the original relaxation: \(s\ge a\) makes each allowed category eligible at \(s\).

Conversely, suppose \((s,f)\) is feasible for the relaxation. Choose the largest element \(a\in A_t\) with \(a\le s\). Every positive \(f_{r,o}\) has \(c_o/\eta_r\le s\); because this ratio belongs to \(A_t\), it is at most \(a\). Hence \((s,f)\) is feasible for the LP indexed by \(a\).

Therefore the minimum of the LP optima equals \(h_t\). At least one LP is feasible: at the largest threshold all recipient-category pairs are allowed, and sufficiently large \(s\) accommodates any assignment of the finite supplies. Each feasible LP has an attained finite optimum. It follows that \(h_t\) is attained and rational.

There are at most \(m\tau+1\) such LPs, each with at most \(m\tau+1\) variables. Standard polynomial-time rational linear programming computes an optimal solution of polynomial bit length.

Rounding gives an upper benchmark estimate

Take an optimal relaxed solution \(f\). For each recipient type \(r\), apply the rounding lemma with

\(u_o=f_{r,o}/\mu_r,\qquad c_o=v_t(o).\)

Its mean cost is at most \(\eta_rh_t\). Every category with positive mean has \(c_o\le\eta_rh_t\). Consequently, every supported bundle satisfies

\(v_t(b)\le 2\eta_rh_t.\)

Multiply the probabilities of this distribution by \(\mu_r\), obtaining allocation masses \(z^{(t)}_{r,b}\). These masses have the required type totals, and their aggregate category totals are exactly \(q\). Thus \(z^{(t)}\in\mathcal F\), and

\(\lambda_t\le2h_t.\)

Define the computable estimate

\(\widehat W_t=2\eta_th_t.\)

We have proved

\(W_t^\infty\le\widehat W_t\le2W_t^\infty.\)

We have also constructed a rational finite-support allocation \(z^{(t)}\) satisfying

\(z^{(t)}_{r,b}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ v_t(b)\le \eta_r\widehat W_t/\eta_t.\)

These statements include \(h_t=0\); no division by a benchmark occurs.

4. Using the finite-agent theorem to prove LP feasibility

Compute \(\widehat W_t\) and its witness \(z^{(t)}\) for every supported type.

Choose an integer \(N\ge2\) clearing the denominators of all \(\mu_r\), all \(q_o\), and every positive mass in every witness \(z^{(t)}\). Construct a finite instance with:

  • \(N\mu_r\) agents of each type \(r\);
  • weight \(\eta_r/N\) for each such agent;
  • \(Nq_o\) individually labelled copies of category \(o\);
  • cost \(v_r(o)\) for each category-\(o\) copy, evaluated by a type-\(r\) agent.

The agent counts and item counts are integers. Every weight is positive, and their sum is

\(\sum_r(N\mu_r)(\eta_r/N)=\sum_r\mu_r\eta_r=1.\)

Costs are nonnegative and additive, as required by the finite theorem.

For a fixed evaluating type \(t\), its witness \(z^{(t)}\) yields a finite partition: give exactly \(Nz^{(t)}_{r,b}\) type-\(r\) agents bundle \(b\). The witness’s feasibility ensures the correct numbers of agents and items.

For every bundle in this partition,

\(\frac{v_t(b)}{\eta_r/N}\le N\widehat W_t/\eta_t.\)

Multiplying by the evaluating agent’s weight \(\eta_t/N\), we conclude that every finite type-\(t\) agent’s finite WMMS benchmark, denoted \(B_t^N\), satisfies

\(B_t^N\le\widehat W_t.\)

The finite \(12\)-WMMS theorem therefore provides a partition in which every type-\(t\) agent receives a bundle of own cost at most \(12\widehat W_t\). If some agent has zero cost for all items, this conclusion holds directly by assigning all items to that agent and empty bundles to everyone else; empty item sets are also immediate.

Aggregate this partition by setting \(g_{t,o}\) equal to the number of category-\(o\) items assigned to type-\(t\) agents, divided by \(N\). Then

\(\sum_tg_{t,o}=q_o,\)

\(\sum_o v_t(o)g_{t,o}\le12\mu_t\widehat W_t,\)

and

\(g_{t,o}=0\quad\text{whenever }v_t(o)>12\widehat W_t.\)

The last assertion follows because any bundle containing such an item would have own cost greater than \(12\widehat W_t\).

We have therefore proved feasibility of the following polynomial-size rational LP:

\(g_{t,o}\ge0,\qquad \sum_tg_{t,o}=q_o,\)

\(\sum_o v_t(o)g_{t,o}\le12\mu_t\widehat W_t,\)

\(g_{t,o}=0\quad\text{if }v_t(o)>12\widehat W_t.\)

The potentially enormous finite instance is used only in this existence argument. The algorithm solves this LP directly.

5. Constructing the final allocation

Find a rational feasible solution \(g\) to the LP above.

For each type \(t\), apply the rounding lemma to the mean vector

\(u^{(t)}_o=g_{t,o}/\mu_t\)

and its own costs \(v_t(o)\). The mean cost is at most \(12\widehat W_t\), and every category with positive mean costs at most \(12\widehat W_t\). Hence every bundle in the resulting distribution satisfies

\(v_t(b)\le24\widehat W_t\le48W_t^\infty.\)

Multiply the distribution probabilities by \(\mu_t\), and call the resulting allocation masses \(y_{t,b}\). Their type totals are \(\mu_t\). Preservation of means gives, for every category,

\(\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}b_o=\sum_t\mu_tu^{(t)}_o=\sum_tg_{t,o}=q_o.\)

Thus \(y\in\mathcal F\), and the preceding bundlewise inequality proves that \(y\) is \(48\)-WMMS.

If \(W_t^\infty=0\), then \(\widehat W_t=0\). The LP assigns that type only zero-cost categories, and the rounding lemma produces only zero-cost bundles. Thus the original requirement at zero benchmarks is satisfied exactly.

6. Complexity and conclusion

Computing all benchmark estimates requires at most \(\tau(m\tau+1)\) rational LPs, each of polynomial size. The final allocation LP also has polynomial size. All coefficients and computed rational solutions have polynomial bit length.

Each rounding operation produces at most \(m+1\) bundles. Their coordinates may be numerically large, but their binary encodings have polynomial length: they are floors or ceilings of rational means of polynomial bit length. Consequently, the final allocation has at most \(\tau(m+1)\) positive entries and is computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

For arbitrary positive real densities, the same existence argument remains valid. The threshold LPs still attain their optima, and rounding still produces finite-support witnesses. Such a witness can be replaced by a rational one on the same support: its type-mass and supply equations have rational coefficients and rational right-hand sides, so their nonempty finite-dimensional rational polytope contains a rational point. This replacement preserves every supported bundle’s cost bound and enables the replication argument. Only the computational statement requires a specified numerical encoding.

Finally, because \(\tau\ge1\), a universal constant guarantee is also an \(O(\log(1+\tau))\) guarantee. The target question is therefore resolved affirmatively under the stated bit-model interpretation.
