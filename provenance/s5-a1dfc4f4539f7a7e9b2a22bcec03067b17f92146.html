The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(A,B\), rational masses \(\mu_a,\nu_b\) summing to \(1\), and weak rankings of opposite-side types and \(\bot\), find an exact rational flow \(x_{ab},u_a,v_b\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{ab}+u_a=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}+v_b=\nu_b\), with positive \(x_{ab}\) only for mutually acceptable types. For each type pair require \(D_a(b;x)=0\) or \(D_b(a;x)=0\), where \(D_a(b;x)=u_a\mathbf 1[b\succ_a\bot]+\sum_{b':\,b\succ_a b'}x_{ab'}\), and define \(D_b\) symmetrically. Also require Pareto efficiency: among reassignments of the same atomless individuals, none weakly improves everyone and strictly improves a positive measure. Is such a flow computable in time \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)\), where \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) and \(L\) is the input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be nonempty finite sets of types on two sides of a matching market. A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Write \(\tau=|A|+|B|\), \(m=\max\{|A|,|B|\}\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible rankings. Each side has total mass \(1\).
Each type has a complete, transitive weak preference relation over opposite-side types and the unmatched option \(\bot\); \(\succ\) denotes its strict part. Ties, including ties with \(\bot\), are allowed and are not broken. A pair \((a,b)\) is mutually acceptable when \(b\succeq_a\bot\) and \(a\succeq_b\bot\).
A feasible flow consists of nonnegative entries \(x_{ab},u_a,v_b\) satisfying \(\sum_{b\in B}x_{ab}+u_a=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_{a\in A}x_{ab}+v_b=\nu_b\), with \(x_{ab}=0\) for pairs that are not mutually acceptable.
Define \(D_a(b;x)=u_a\mathbf 1_{\{b\succ_a\bot\}}+\sum_{b'\in B:\,b\succ_a b'}x_{ab'}\) and \(D_b(a;x)=v_b\mathbf 1_{\{a\succ_b\bot\}}+\sum_{a'\in A:\,a\succ_b a'}x_{a'b}\). The flow is weakly stable when, for every \((a,b)\in A\times B\), \(D_a(b;x)=0\) or \(D_b(a;x)=0\).
Each type consists of an atomless population whose individuals share its ranking. A realization of a flow partitions these populations into matched subsets of measures \(x_{ab}\), paired by measure-preserving bijections, and unmatched subsets of measures \(u_a,v_b\).
A flow is Pareto efficient if no measurable reassignment of the same individuals weakly improves almost every individual on both sides and strictly improves a set of positive measure. Comparisons use partner types or \(\bot\); competing reassignments may have real masses and need not be stable.
Is finding an exact rational weakly stable, Pareto-efficient flow solvable in time \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)\), equivalently \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), or is this search problem NP-hard?
For finite individual agents with incomplete weak preferences, a polynomial-time mechanism computes a weakly stable, Pareto-optimal matching and is strategyproof for one side. [Domaniç, Lam, and Plaxton](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10598).
No result settling this exact atomless search problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Defined mutual acceptability using weak preference over being unmatched, so partners tied with the unmatched option remain acceptable.
  • Specified rankings as complete, transitive weak relations over all opposite-side types and the unmatched option, with no prescribed tie-breaking.
  • Made the individual interpretation of a flow explicit using atomless populations and measure-preserving pairings.
  • Interpreted everyone in measurable Pareto comparisons as almost everyone; strict improvement must affect positive measure on either side.
  • Specified that Pareto competitors may use real masses and need not satisfy stability, matching unrestricted Pareto comparison in the discrete definition.
  • Defined m as the larger side's support size; it is bounded by tau and introduces no additional input parameter.

Result

The search problem is solvable in \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)\) time.

The algorithm computes an initial weakly stable flow, then optimizes over a face of the feasible-flow polytope that preserves stability and is closed under individual Pareto improvements. The optimization is a maximum-cost transportation problem.

All stability and Pareto comparisons below use the original weak preferences, including ties with \(\bot\).

1. Pareto improvements decrease the deficit quantities

Lemma. Suppose a measurable reassignment weakly improves almost every individual relative to a realization of a feasible flow \(x\). Let \(y\) denote its aggregate flow. Then \(y\) is feasible, and for every pair \((a,b)\),
\[ D_a(b;y)\le D_a(b;x), \qquad D_b(a;y)\le D_b(a;x). \]

Proof. Every individual’s original outcome is weakly preferred to \(\bot\), because \(x\) is feasible. Consequently, almost every individual’s new outcome is also weakly preferred to \(\bot\). Any pair assigned positive mass in the reassignment must therefore be mutually acceptable. Conservation of the same populations gives the required mass-balance equations, so \(y\) is feasible.

Fix a type \(a\) and a threshold \(b\). For an individual of type \(a\), write \(o\) and \(o'\) for the old and new outcomes. Since \(o'\succeq_a o\), completeness and transitivity imply
\[ b\succ_a o' \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b\succ_a o. \]
Thus
\[ \mathbf 1_{\{b\succ_a o'\}} \le \mathbf 1_{\{b\succ_a o\}} \]
almost everywhere. Integrating over the type-\(a\) population gives the asserted inequality: the integral of the right-hand indicator is exactly \(D_a(b;x)\), including the unmatched contribution. The other side is identical. \(\square\)

In particular, any individual Pareto improvement of a weakly stable flow remains weakly stable. We will use the stronger fact that every zero deficit remains zero.

2. Computing an initial stable flow in polynomial time

We use the following established stable-allocation theorem.

Stable-allocation theorem. For a finite bipartite graph with positive vertex quotas, nonnegative edge capacities, and strict rankings of incident edges, a stable allocation can be computed using strongly polynomially many arithmetic operations. An edge blocks when its allocation is below its capacity and each endpoint either has unused quota or holds positive allocation on a less-preferred edge. The inductive algorithm uses additions, subtractions, minima, and comparisons. See Baïou and Balinski, *The Stable Allocation (or Ordinal Transportation) Problem*, corrected version, Section 2 and Theorem 2. [Source](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302)

Here is the reduction, including the treatment of \(\bot\).

Let \(E\) be the mutually acceptable pairs. Give vertex \(a\) quota \(\mu_a\), vertex \(b\) quota \(\nu_b\), and edge \((a,b)\in E\) capacity
\[ c_{ab}=\min\{\mu_a,\nu_b\}. \]
These capacities impose no additional restriction on feasible flows.

Choose auxiliary strict rankings of incident edges that preserve every original strict comparison. In this auxiliary problem, place every incident edge above unused quota. This includes originally acceptable partners tied with \(\bot\).

Apply the theorem and call the resulting allocation \(\bar x\). Set
\[ \bar u_a=\mu_a-\sum_b\bar x_{ab}, \qquad \bar v_b=\nu_b-\sum_a\bar x_{ab}. \]
The resulting flow is feasible.

Claim. This flow is weakly stable under the original preferences.

Proof. A pair outside \(E\) cannot violate weak stability. For example, if \(b\prec_a\bot\), every outcome assigned to type \(a\) is weakly above \(\bot\), so \(D_a(b;\bar x)=0\).

Suppose instead that \((a,b)\in E\) satisfies
\[ D_a(b;\bar x)>0, \qquad D_b(a;\bar x)>0. \]
The first inequality means that \(a\) has positive unmatched mass with \(b\succ_a\bot\), or positive mass assigned to an originally strictly worse partner. Either possibility gives an opportunity to improve in the auxiliary strict ranking. The same holds for \(b\).

Moreover, the positive deficits imply
\[ \bar x_{ab}<\mu_a, \qquad \bar x_{ab}<\nu_b, \]
and hence \(\bar x_{ab}<c_{ab}\). Thus \((a,b)\) blocks the auxiliary stable allocation, a contradiction. \(\square\)

The auxiliary rankings establish a seed only. They do not redefine the original preferences or determine which subsequent changes count as strict improvements.

Bit complexity. Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Every quota and capacity is a multiple of \(1/Q\). The stated arithmetic operations preserve this property, and the allocation and residual quantities have polynomial bit length. Therefore the strongly polynomial arithmetic algorithm computes \(\bar x\) exactly in \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)\) bit operations.

3. A stable face closed under Pareto improvements

Define
\[ Z_A=\{(a,b):D_a(b;\bar x)=0\}, \qquad Z_B=\{(a,b):D_b(a;\bar x)=0\}. \]

Let \(\mathcal F\) consist of all feasible flows \(y\) satisfying
\[ D_a(b;y)=0 \quad\text{for every }(a,b)\in Z_A, \]
and
\[ D_b(a;y)=0 \quad\text{for every }(a,b)\in Z_B. \]

This set has three useful properties.

  • It is nonempty: \(\bar x\in\mathcal F\).
  • Every flow in it is weakly stable: for each pair, weak stability of \(\bar x\) supplies at least one zero deficit. The corresponding defining equality of \(\mathcal F\) preserves that zero.
  • It is closed under individual Pareto improvements: if a reassignment improves a realization of \(y\in\mathcal F\), its aggregate flow \(z\) is feasible by the lemma. Every defining deficit satisfies

\[ 0\le D_a(b;z)\le D_a(b;y)=0, \]
or the analogous inequality on the other side. Hence \(z\in\mathcal F\).

Furthermore, each deficit is a sum of nonnegative flow coordinates with coefficients zero or one. Setting such a sum to zero is equivalent to setting every coordinate appearing in it to zero. Thus \(\mathcal F\) is obtained from the feasible-flow polytope simply by forbidding additional matched or unmatched coordinates.

4. Optimizing on this face gives global Pareto efficiency

For each type \(a\), assign consecutive integer scores to its indifference classes, from worst to best. Write the resulting function as
\[ r_a:B\cup\{\bot\}\longrightarrow\{0,\ldots,|B|\}. \]
It satisfies
\[ t\succeq_a s \iff r_a(t)\ge r_a(s), \qquad t\succ_a s \iff r_a(t)>r_a(s). \]
Construct analogous scores \(r_b\) on the other side.

For a flow \(y=(y_{ab},u_a^y,v_b^y)\), define
\[ W(y)= \sum_{a,b}\bigl(r_a(b)+r_b(a)\bigr)y_{ab} +\sum_a r_a(\bot)u_a^y +\sum_b r_b(\bot)v_b^y. \]

This is the integral of individuals’ outcome scores over both populations. It is independent of the particular realization of \(y\).

Compute an exact maximizer
\[ y^*\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{y\in\mathcal F} W(y). \]
The maximum exists: \(\mathcal F\) is nonempty, closed, and bounded.

Claim. Every realization of \(y^*\) is Pareto efficient.

Proof. Suppose a measurable reassignment weakly improves almost every individual and strictly improves a set of positive measure. Let \(z\) be its aggregate flow.

By Section 3, \(z\in\mathcal F\). Every individual’s score weakly increases. On the strictly improved set, the score increases by at least one, because the scores are integers representing the original indifference classes. If that set has total measure \(\delta>0\), then
\[ W(z)-W(y^*)\ge\delta>0. \]
This contradicts maximality of \(y^*\). \(\square\)

This argument covers competing reassignments with arbitrary real masses and imposes no stability requirement on them.

5. Exact optimization and output size

For completeness, the optimization in Section 4 is a polynomial-size transportation problem.

Introduce a dummy type \(a_0\) on the first side and a dummy type \(b_0\) on the second, each with mass one. Represent unmatched mass by
\[ y_{a b_0}=u_a^y, \qquad y_{a_0 b}=v_b^y. \]
Keep the real matched coordinates \(y_{ab}\), and add the dummy-to-dummy coordinate
\[ y_{a_0 b_0}=\sum_{a,b}y_{ab}. \]

These coordinates satisfy all augmented transportation balances. Indeed, if the total real matched mass is \(M\), the unmatched mass on each side is \(1-M\), so each dummy balance is \((1-M)+M=1\).

Conversely, any augmented transportation solution gives a feasible original flow through its real matched and unmatched coordinates. Its dummy-to-dummy coordinate necessarily equals its total real matched mass.

Delete all arcs forbidden by acceptability or the defining zero conditions of \(\mathcal F\). Give the remaining arcs weights

  • \(r_a(b)+r_b(a)\) for a real pair;
  • \(r_a(\bot)\) for \((a,b_0)\);
  • \(r_b(\bot)\) for \((a_0,b)\);
  • zero for \((a_0,b_0)\).

The transportation objective is exactly \(W\).

There are \(\tau+2\) transportation vertices and at most
\[ |A||B|+\tau+1 \]
arcs. After multiplying every mass by \(Q\), all supplies and demands are integers with \(O(L)\) bits, and all weights are integers bounded by \(2m\).

The standard integral minimum-cost-flow theorem applies: with integer capacities and balances, an integral optimum exists and can be computed in time polynomial in the network’s binary encoding. Equivalently, it computes an optimum over all real feasible transportation flows, with an integral optimum available after scaling.

Consequently, dividing the computed optimum by \(Q\) gives an exact rational \(y^*\). Every output coordinate is \(k/Q\) with \(0\le k\le Q\), so its binary encoding has polynomial length.

Combining the stable-allocation initialization, construction of the zero conditions, and transportation optimization gives the claimed \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)\) algorithm. Since \(m\le\tau\), this also meets the stated \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) bound.

1. Deficit monotonicity

The lemma is correct.

Feasibility ensures that almost every individual’s original outcome is weakly preferred to \(\bot\). Transitivity therefore makes every weakly improving new outcome acceptable to that individual. A positive-mass newly matched pair must be acceptable on both sides. Conservation of the populations supplies the aggregate mass balances.

The implication \(b\succ_a o'\Longrightarrow b\succ_a o\), under \(o'\succeq_a o\), follows from completeness and transitivity of the weak preference relation. It remains valid when either outcome is \(\bot\) and when ties occur.

The integrated indicator is exactly the stated deficit: matched individuals contribute through their partner types, and unmatched individuals contribute precisely when \(b\succ_a\bot\). Thus both deficit inequalities hold for arbitrary real aggregate masses.

Consequently, a zero deficit remains zero under any individual Pareto improvement. The claimed preservation of weak stability follows.
