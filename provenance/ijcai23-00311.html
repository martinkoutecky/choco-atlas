The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite slot labels \(E\), rational normalized capacities \(b_e\), a finite set of complete types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), one common strict ranking \(\succ\), and a matroid \(\mathcal F_t\) on \(E\) for each type, choose conditional bundle lotteries \(\lambda_t\in\Delta(\mathcal F_t)\). Require \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\sum_{A\in\mathcal F_t}\lambda_t(A)\mathbf 1[e\in A]\le b_e\) for every \(e\), define \(U(e)=\{e'\in E:e'\succeq e\}\) and \(R_t^\lambda(e)=\sum_A\lambda_t(A)|A\cap U(e)|\), and require no feasible \(\lambda'\) to weakly improve every \(R_t^\lambda(e)\) with one strict improvement, together with \(R_t^\lambda(e)\ge\sum_{B\in\mathcal F_u}\lambda_u(B)\max_{Y\subseteq B,\,Y\in\mathcal F_t}|Y\cap U(e)|\) for all types \(t,u\) and thresholds \(e\). Output such a family or report that none exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(E\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) slot labels, with binary-rational capacities \(b_e\in[0,1]\), and let \(\succ\) be one common strict ranking of \(E\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) complete types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type specifies a matroid whose independent-set family \(\mathcal F_t\) is given by an independence oracle. Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the explicitly listed input.
Choose conditional bundle lotteries \(\lambda_t\in\Delta(\mathcal F_t)\), represented by finite lists of bundles and rational probabilities. Feasibility means \(\sum_t\mu_t\sum_{A\in\mathcal F_t:e\in A}\lambda_t(A)\le b_e\) for every \(e\in E\); unused capacity is allowed.
Define \(U(e)=\{e'\in E:e'\succeq e\}\), where \(\succeq\) includes equality, and \(R_t^\lambda(e)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal F_t}\lambda_t(A)|A\cap U(e)|\).
Require SD-efficiency: no feasible family \(\lambda'\) satisfies \(R_t^{\lambda'}(e)\ge R_t^\lambda(e)\) for every \(t,e\), with at least one strict inequality. Comparisons range over all feasible families, without imposing envy-freeness on \(\lambda'\).
Also require constrained SD-envy-freeness: \(R_t^\lambda(e)\ge\sum_{B\in\mathcal F_u}\lambda_u(B)\max_{Y\subseteq B,\,Y\in\mathcal F_t}|Y\cap U(e)|\) for every \(t,u\in T\) and \(e\in E\).
The search problem is to output such a family or report that none exists. Oracle calls have unit cost; hardness concerns instances with polynomial-time implementable independence oracles.

Can this search problem be solved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), using polynomially many independence-oracle calls, or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many agents, unit item supplies, a common strict ranking, and heterogeneous matroid constraints, an SD-efficient and constrained SD-envy-free lottery always exists and is computable in polynomial time ([Kawase–Sumita–Yokoi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.07666)).
No prior result settling this exact support-encoded mass formulation was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted normalized capacities as binary rationals in [0,1], allowing zero capacity and unused supply.
  • Specified a nonempty slot set and a nonempty support consisting only of positive-mass types.
  • Carried over the discrete model's membership-oracle representation as independence-oracle access; polynomial time counts bit operations and oracle calls, and hardness concerns polynomial-time implementable oracles.
  • Specified finite bundle-support lists with rational probabilities as the representation of output lotteries.
  • Defined L as the total bit length of explicitly listed input data.
  • Made explicit that efficiency compares against all capacity-feasible lottery families, including those that violate envy-freeness.

Result

The search problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), using polynomially many independence-oracle calls. A solution always exists.

The construction extends the item-by-item allocation rule in [Kawase–Sumita–Yokoi, Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.07666): active types receive equal increases in their conditional allocation probabilities, and these increases consume capacity in proportion to their masses. Below, the proof addresses the support encoding, arbitrary rational capacities, and the required bundle-lottery output directly.

1. Marginals and matroid constraints

Write the common ranking as \(e_1\succ\cdots\succ e_m\), and put \(H_k=\{e_1,\ldots,e_k\}\), with \(H_0=\varnothing\).

For a lottery \(\lambda_t\), define its marginal vector by

\[ \text{} \]
\(x_{t,k}=\Pr_{A\sim\lambda_t}[e_k\in A]\), and \(x_t(S)=\sum_{e_k\in S}x_{t,k}\).

Then \(R_t^\lambda(e_k)=x_t(H_k)\).

Let \(r_t\) be the rank function of type \(t\)'s matroid. We use the standard independent-set polytope theorem:

\(P_t:=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf 1_A:A\in\mathcal F_t\}=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^E:x(S)\leq r_t(S)\text{ for every }S\subseteq E\}\).

Matroid rank functions are monotone and submodular, and \(r_t(\varnothing)=0\). The polytope characterization is [Schrijver, Corollary 40.2b](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~cornaz/Enseignement/M2_MODO/DATA/B4.PDF).

Consequently, feasible marginal families are exactly those satisfying

\(x_t\in P_t\quad(t\in T),\qquad \sum_t\mu_t x_{t,k}\leq b_{e_k}\quad(1\leq k\leq m)\).

Indeed, each row in \(P_t\) has a decomposition into independent bundles, and the capacity conditions depend only on these marginals. The envy proof below will hold for every such decomposition of the constructed rows.

2. Construction by weighted water levels

Initialize every coordinate to zero. Process the items in ranking order.

At the beginning of round \(k\), all coordinates preceding \(k\) are fixed, and all remaining coordinates are zero. For each type, let

\(p^{t,k}=\sum_{j<k}x_{t,j}\mathbf 1_{\{e_j\}}\),

and compute its maximum permissible allocation of the current item:

\(\alpha_{t,k}=\max\{a\geq0:p^{t,k}+a\mathbf 1_{\{e_k\}}\in P_t\}\).

The rank inequalities give the following exact formula:

\[\alpha_{t,k}=\min_{S\subseteq H_{k-1}}\bigl(r_t(S\cup\{e_k\})-x_t(S)\bigr).\tag{1}\]

To justify the restriction to these sets, consider any rank constraint indexed by \(V\) containing \(e_k\). Set \(S=V\cap H_{k-1}\). The already allocated vector has value \(x_t(S)\) on \(V\), and monotonicity gives \(r_t(V)\geq r_t(S\cup\{e_k\})\). Thus constraints indexed by subsets of \(H_k\) containing \(e_k\) suffice. Constraints not containing \(e_k\) remain satisfied.

In particular, feasibility of \(p^{t,k}\) and the choice \(S=\varnothing\) in (1) show that \(0\leq\alpha_{t,k}\leq1\).

Define \(M_k=\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_{t,k}\).

  • If \(b_{e_k}\geq M_k\), set \(h_k=\max_t\alpha_{t,k}\).
  • If \(b_{e_k}<M_k\), choose \(h_k\) satisfying

\(\sum_t\mu_t\min\{\alpha_{t,k},h_k\}=b_{e_k}\).

In the second case, \(h_k\) exists and is unique: the left side is continuous, starts at zero, reaches \(M_k\) at \(\max_t\alpha_{t,k}\), and is strictly increasing below that maximum because all masses are positive.

In either case, set

\[x_{t,k}=\min\{\alpha_{t,k},h_k\}.\tag{2}\]

Each updated row remains in its matroid polytope. Capacity is respected, and whenever \(b_{e_k}<M_k\), that capacity is exhausted exactly. This proves feasibility inductively.

3. Tight sets witnessing allocation differences

Call \(S\subseteq E\) tight for type \(t\) if \(x_t(S)=r_t(S)\), where \(x_t\) is the final constructed row.

First, tight sets are closed under union. If \(A,B\) are tight, then

\(r_t(A)+r_t(B)=x_t(A)+x_t(B)=x_t(A\cup B)+x_t(A\cap B)\leq r_t(A\cup B)+r_t(A\cap B)\leq r_t(A)+r_t(B)\).

The last inequality is submodularity. Equality throughout implies that \(A\cup B\) is tight. The empty set is also tight, so every finite union of tight sets is tight.

We next prove the crucial property:

If \(x_{t,i}<x_{u,i}\), there is a tight set \(K_{t,i}\subseteq H_i\) containing \(e_i\).

By (2), \(x_{u,i}\leq h_i\). Hence \(x_{t,i}<h_i\), which forces \(x_{t,i}=\alpha_{t,i}\).

Choose a minimizer \(S_{t,i}\subseteq H_{i-1}\) in (1), and let \(K_{t,i}=S_{t,i}\cup\{e_i\}\). Then

\(r_t(K_{t,i})=x_t(S_{t,i})+\alpha_{t,i}=x_t(K_{t,i})\).

Every coordinate in this set is fixed by the end of round \(i\), so the equality holds for the final row. This proves the property.

4. Constrained SD-envy-freeness

Fix arbitrary types \(t,u\) and a prefix \(H_k\). Define the deficient items by

\(D=\{e_i\in H_k:x_{t,i}<x_{u,i}\}\).

For every \(e_i\in D\), take the tight set supplied by Section 3, and put

\(K=\bigcup_{e_i\in D}K_{t,i}\).

When \(D\) is empty, take \(K=\varnothing\). In every case,

\[D\subseteq K\subseteq H_k,\qquad x_t(K)=r_t(K).\tag{3}\]

Since \(K\) contains every deficient item,

\[x_u(H_k\setminus K)\leq x_t(H_k\setminus K).\tag{4}\]

Now consider any bundle \(B\), and any \(Y\subseteq B\) independent in type \(t\)'s matroid. Because \(K\subseteq H_k\),

\(|Y\cap H_k|=|Y\cap K|+|Y\cap(H_k\setminus K)|\leq r_t(K)+|B\cap(H_k\setminus K)|\).

This bound holds for every admissible \(Y\), so it also bounds their maximum. Taking expectations under any lottery \(\lambda_u\) with marginal vector \(x_u\), and then using (3)–(4), gives

\(\sum_B\lambda_u(B)\max_{Y\subseteq B,\;Y\in\mathcal F_t}|Y\cap H_k|\leq r_t(K)+x_u(H_k\setminus K)\leq x_t(K)+x_t(H_k\setminus K)=x_t(H_k)\).

For any decomposition \(\lambda_t\) of \(x_t\), the last quantity equals \(R_t^{\lambda}(e_k)\). Thus the required constrained SD-envy-freeness inequalities hold for every pair of types and every prefix.

5. SD-efficiency against all feasible families

Suppose, for contradiction, that another feasible family has marginals \(y_t\) satisfying

\(y_t(H_j)\geq x_t(H_j)\quad\text{for every }t,j\),

with at least one strict inequality.

The marginal families differ. Let \(k\) be the first coordinate where they differ for any type. All earlier coordinates agree. Prefix dominance at \(k\) therefore implies

\(y_{t,k}\geq x_{t,k}\quad\text{for every }t\),

with strict inequality for at least one type.

Because \(y_t\in P_t\), setting its later coordinates to zero preserves all rank inequalities. Its earlier coordinates equal those used to compute \(\alpha_{t,k}\). Consequently,

\[y_{t,k}\leq\alpha_{t,k}\quad\text{for every }t.\tag{5}\]

If \(b_{e_k}\geq M_k\), the construction sets \(x_{t,k}=\alpha_{t,k}\) for every type, contradicting (5) and the existence of a strict increase.

Otherwise, the construction exhausts capacity:

\(\sum_t\mu_t x_{t,k}=b_{e_k}\).

All coordinates \(y_{t,k}\) are weakly larger, at least one is strictly larger, and every \(\mu_t\) is positive. Thus

\(\sum_t\mu_t y_{t,k}>\sum_t\mu_t x_{t,k}=b_{e_k}\),

contradicting feasibility.

This proves SD-efficiency. No envy restriction was imposed on the competing family.

6. Polynomial oracle and bit complexity

Computing the permissible amounts

For fixed \(t,k\), the function

\(S\longmapsto r_t(S\cup\{e_k\})-x_t(S),\qquad S\subseteq H_{k-1}\),

is submodular: adjoining a fixed element preserves submodularity, and subtracting a modular function preserves it.

A rank evaluation requires at most \(m\) independence-oracle calls, by greedily constructing a maximal independent subset. Exact submodular minimization is polynomial in the ground-set size and the encoding length of integer function values; see [Iwata–Fleischer–Fujishige](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0004089). Rational values here can be scaled by a common denominator.

There are exactly \(m\tau\) minimizations. Water levels are computed by sorting the \(\tau\) permissible amounts and solving a linear equation on the appropriate interval.

Bounding denominators

Let \(D\) be a common denominator of all input masses and capacities, obtained, for example, by multiplying their denominators. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Write

\(\mu_t=a_t/D,\qquad b_{e_k}=c_k/D\),

where \(a_t\) are positive integers and \(\sum_ta_t=D\).

Suppose all previously constructed coordinates have common denominator \(Q\). Equation (1) shows that every \(\alpha_{t,k}\) also has denominator dividing \(Q\); write \(\alpha_{t,k}=q_t/Q\).

When \(b_{e_k}\geq M_k\), the new coordinates need no larger common denominator.

Otherwise, let \(C=\{t:\alpha_{t,k}\leq h_k\}\) and \(A=\{t:\alpha_{t,k}>h_k\}\). Since \(b_{e_k}<M_k\), the set \(A\) is nonempty. The water-level equation gives

\(h_k=\dfrac{c_kQ-\sum_{t\in C}a_tq_t}{Q\sum_{t\in A}a_t}\).

Thus the new coordinates have a common denominator dividing \(Q\sum_{t\in A}a_t\). Since \(1\leq\sum_{t\in A}a_t\leq D\), each round increases the common denominator by a factor of at most \(D\).

Starting from \(Q=1\), we may therefore maintain \(Q\leq D^m\). All constructed coordinates have bit length \(O(mL)\).

For each minimization, multiplying the objective by the current \(Q\) produces integer values of bit length \(O(mL+\log m)\). Hence the minimizations, sorting, and rational arithmetic all have polynomial bit complexity. The number of independence-oracle calls is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

7. Producing the required lotteries

The standard oracle decomposition theorem states that a rational point in a matroid independent-set polytope can be decomposed into a rational convex combination of independent-set incidence vectors in polynomial time; see [Schrijver, Corollary 40.4a](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~cornaz/Enseignement/M2_MODO/DATA/B4.PDF).

Its hypotheses hold for every constructed row: \(x_t\) is rational, belongs to \(P_t\), and the matroid is given by an independence oracle. Applying the theorem separately to every type produces the required finite bundle lists and rational probabilities, with polynomial total output length.

Their marginals satisfy feasibility and SD-efficiency, and Section 4 proves constrained SD-envy-freeness for these decompositions. Therefore the algorithm always outputs a valid family.
