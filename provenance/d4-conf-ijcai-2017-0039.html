The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite item-type catalogue \(G\), preference-type set \(T\), integer bundle size \(k\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_g\) with \(\sum_g\sigma_g=k\), and a strict ranking \(\succ_t\) for each \(t\in T\), let \(\mathcal{B}_k=\{B\subseteq G:\lvert B\rvert=k\}\). Decide whether there exist masses \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}_k}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{B\ni g}x_{t,B}=\sigma_g\), and, whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\) and \(x_{t',B'}>0\), \(B\succeq_t^{NDD}B'\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a catalogue of \(m\) item types and let \(2\le k\le m\) be an integer. A bundle contains at most one item of each type: \(\mathcal B_k=\{B\subseteq G:|B|=k\}\).
A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\) preference types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Each type reports a strict ranking of all of \(G\). Supplies \(\sigma_g\ge0\) are binary rationals with \(\sum_{g\in G}\sigma_g=k\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Write the ranking of type \(t\) as \(g_{t,1}\succ_t\cdots\succ_t g_{t,m}\). Let \(\mathcal U_t^{DD}\) consist of strictly positive additive real utilities consistent with this ranking and satisfying \(u(g_{t,j})-u(g_{t,j+1})\ge u(g_{t,j+1})-u(g_{t,j+2})\) for \(1\le j\le m-2\). Define \(B\succeq_t^{NDD}B'\) iff \(u(B)\ge u(B')\) for every \(u\in\mathcal U_t^{DD}\); utility ties are allowed in this comparison.
The decision problem asks whether real masses \(x_{t,B}\ge0\) exist such that \(\sum_{B\in\mathcal B_k}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\), \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{B\ni g}x_{t,B}=\sigma_g\) for every \(g\), and \(x_{t,B}>0\land x_{t',B'}>0\implies B\succeq_t^{NDD}B'\) for every ordered pair, including \(t=t'\).

Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a finite list of \(n\ge3\) agents and at least \(2n\) distinct indivisible items, deciding whether a complete necessarily-DD-envy-free allocation exists is NP-complete. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.07993)
No result settling the exact mass-allocation problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted bundle size to 2 <= k <= m, carrying over the anchor's condition of at least two items per agent and the requirement that bundles are sets.
  • Required supplies to be nonnegative binary rationals; zero supplies are allowed, and no additional upper-bound promise is imposed.
  • Defined NDD using strictly positive additive real utilities, strict consistency with the item ranking, and weak diminishing-difference inequalities, as in the discrete definition.
  • Specified allocation masses as real numbers and retained comparisons between all ordered positive-mass bundle pairs, including pairs belonging to the same preference type.
  • Defined L as total input bit length and tau as positive-mass support size; no agent-count restriction is imposed on tau.

Result

The decision problem is NP-complete, already for $k=2$ and dyadic masses and supplies.

The crucial point is that the comparisons with $t=t'$ force every preference type to receive exactly one bundle. Thus allowing real allocation masses does not provide fractional freedom within a type.

1. Every type receives a single bundle

Fix a type $t$ and define the additive utility
$$ v_t(g_{t,j})=2^{m-j}. $$
Its item utilities are strictly positive and strictly decreasing. Its consecutive differences satisfy
$$ v_t(g_{t,j})-v_t(g_{t,j+1}) = 2\bigl(v_t(g_{t,j+1})-v_t(g_{t,j+2})\bigr), $$
so $v_t\in\mathcal U_t^{DD}$.

Distinct bundles have distinct $v_t$-values. Indeed, the highest power of two corresponding to an item in their symmetric difference exceeds the sum of all lower powers, so their utility difference cannot vanish.

Suppose both $x_{t,B}>0$ and $x_{t,B'}>0$. Applying the stipulated condition to both ordered pairs gives
$$ B\succeq_t^{NDD}B' \quad\text{and}\quad B'\succeq_t^{NDD}B. $$
Consequently, $v_t(B)=v_t(B')$, which implies $B=B'$.

Since $\mu_t>0$, the mass constraint therefore forces a unique bundle $B_t$ with
$$ x_{t,B_t}=\mu_t, \qquad x_{t,B}=0\quad\text{for }B\ne B_t. $$

Hence the original problem is equivalent to finding bundles $B_t\in\mathcal B_k$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{\{t:\,g\in B_t\}}\mu_t=\sigma_g \quad\text{for every }g, $$
and
$$ B_t\succeq_t^{NDD}B_s \quad\text{for every }t,s\in T. $$

2. NDD comparisons admit polynomial verification

We derive a finite characterization directly from the utility definition.

Fix a type $t$ and equal-size bundles $B,B'$. Write
$$ a_j=\mathbf 1_{\{g_{t,j}\in B\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{g_{t,j}\in B'\}}, \qquad A_\ell=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_j, \qquad D_r=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}A_\ell. $$
Equal cardinality gives $\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_j=0$.

For any $u\in\mathcal U_t^{DD}$, let
$$ d_\ell=u(g_{t,\ell})-u(g_{t,\ell+1}). $$
Then
$$ d_1\ge d_2\ge\cdots\ge d_{m-1}>0. $$
Expanding each item utility from the bottom-ranked item and using equal cardinality yields
$$ u(B)-u(B')=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}d_\ell A_\ell. $$
Since
$$ d_\ell=d_{m-1}+\sum_{r=\ell}^{m-2}(d_r-d_{r+1}), $$
we obtain
$$ u(B)-u(B') = d_{m-1}D_{m-1} + \sum_{r=1}^{m-2}(d_r-d_{r+1})D_r. $$

It follows that
$$ B\succeq_t^{NDD}B' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D_r\ge0 \quad\text{for every }1\le r\le m-1. $$

For sufficiency, every coefficient in the displayed utility difference is nonnegative.

For necessity, if $D_{m-1}<0$, take all gaps equal to $1$, with bottom utility $1$. This is an admissible utility and gives a negative difference. If instead $D_r<0$ for some $r\le m-2$, choose
$$ d_\ell= \begin{cases} 1+\lambda,&\ell\le r,\\ 1,&\ell>r, \end{cases} $$
where $\lambda>0$ is sufficiently large. With bottom utility $1$, these gaps define a strictly positive, strictly ranking-consistent DD utility, and
$$ u(B)-u(B')=D_{m-1}+\lambda D_r<0. $$

A certificate consists of the $\tau$ bundles $B_t$, using $O(\tau k\log m)$ bits. The supply equalities can be checked by exact rational arithmetic, and every ordered NDD comparison can be checked using the inequalities above. All these checks take polynomial time in $m,\tau,k,L$. Thus the problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

3. The source hardness result

We use the following restricted consequence of the cited theorem:

For a variable number $n$ of agents with strict rankings on exactly $2n$ distinct items, deciding whether a complete necessarily-DD-envy-free allocation exists is NP-hard, even when all agents have different first-ranked items.

The restriction follows from the paper’s explicit reduction: Appendix C constructs $3s$ agents and $6s$ items, with a different top dummy item for each agent. Its utility assumptions agree with those in the present question. See [Segal-Halevi, Hassidim and Aziz, Theorem 7.4 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.07993#A3).

Every complete necessarily-DD-envy-free allocation in such an instance gives each agent exactly two items. To verify this, for agent $i$ consider the admissible utilities
$$ u_i(g_{i,j})=C+(2n-j), \qquad C>0. $$
All consecutive gaps equal $1$. If $|X_i|<|X_j|$, then
$$ u_i(X_i)-u_i(X_j) = C\bigl(|X_i|-|X_j|\bigr) + \sum_{g\in X_i}(2n-\operatorname{rank}_i(g)) - \sum_{g\in X_j}(2n-\operatorname{rank}_i(g)) $$
is negative for sufficiently large $C$. This contradicts necessary DD envy-freeness. All bundle sizes must therefore agree, and completeness on $2n$ items makes their common size $2$.

4. Reduction using only dyadic data

Let $H$ be the source catalogue, with $|H|=2n$, and agents indexed by $1,\ldots,n$.

Introduce two new items $a,b$ and one additional preference type $0$. Set
$$ G=H\cup\{a,b\}, \qquad k=2, \qquad q=\left\lceil\log_2(4n)\right\rceil, \qquad \alpha=2^{-q}, \qquad \beta=1-n\alpha. $$
Then $n\alpha\le1/4$, so $\beta\ge3/4>\alpha$.

Give each original type mass $\alpha$ and type $0$ mass $\beta$:
$$ \mu_i=\alpha\quad(1\le i\le n), \qquad \mu_0=\beta. $$
Set supplies
$$ \sigma_h=\alpha\quad(h\in H), \qquad \sigma_a=\sigma_b=\beta. $$
These data satisfy
$$ \sum_t\mu_t=n\alpha+\beta=1, \qquad \sum_{g\in G}\sigma_g=2n\alpha+2\beta=2=k. $$

Each original type preserves its ranking on $H$ and places $a,b$ last, in that order. Type $0$ ranks
$$ a\succ_0 b\succ_0\text{all items of }H, $$
with any strict order on $H$.

The original rankings remain distinct because their first-ranked items are distinct; type $0$ has a new first-ranked item. Thus this construction is a valid support of preference types.

All masses and supplies are dyadic and have $O(\log n)$ encoding length. The construction has polynomial size.

Preservation of comparisons on original bundles

For any original type $i$ and bundles $X,Y\subseteq H$, extending the ranking by $a,b$ at the bottom preserves their NDD comparison exactly.

One direction follows because restricting any admissible utility on $G$ to $H$ gives an admissible utility on the original ranking.

For the other direction, take any admissible original utility $u$. Let $c>0$ be its utility for the last-ranked original item, and let $d>0$ be its last consecutive gap. Choose
$$ 0<\varepsilon<\min\{d,c/2\}, $$
and extend it by
$$ u(a)=c-\varepsilon, \qquad u(b)=c-2\varepsilon. $$
The added consecutive gaps both equal $\varepsilon$ and do not exceed $d$. The extension is strictly positive, strictly consistent with the extended ranking, and DD. It leaves the utilities of $X,Y$ unchanged.

Consequently,
$$ X\succeq_i^{NDD}Y \text{ on }H \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X\succeq_i^{NDD}Y \text{ on }G. $$

Forward implication

Suppose the source instance has a complete necessarily-DD-envy-free allocation $X_1,\ldots,X_n$. Each $X_i$ has size $2$.

Assign bundle $X_i$ to type $i$ with mass $\alpha$, and $\{a,b\}$ to type $0$ with mass $\beta$.

Every original item belongs to exactly one $X_i$, so all supply constraints hold. Comparisons between original types hold by preservation of NDD comparisons.

Every original type ranks every item in its bundle above both $a$ and $b$, so it strictly prefers its bundle to $\{a,b\}$ under every admissible utility. Conversely, type $0$ ranks both $a,b$ above every original item, so it strictly prefers $\{a,b\}$ to every original bundle. Thus all required comparisons hold.

Reverse implication

Suppose the constructed mass instance is feasible. Section 1 gives a single bundle $B_t$ for every type.

Type $0$ cannot receive any original item $h$: its mass $\beta$ alone would contribute more than that item’s supply $\alpha$. Since its bundle has size $2$, necessarily
$$ B_0=\{a,b\}. $$
This consumes the entire supplies of $a,b$, so no original type can receive either new item.

For each $h\in H$, its supply equality now reads
$$ \alpha\bigl|\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:h\in B_i\}\bigr|=\alpha. $$
Therefore every original item belongs to exactly one original bundle. The bundles $B_1,\ldots,B_n$ form a complete allocation of $H$.

Their pairwise NDD comparisons hold in the mass instance, and hence in the original instance by comparison preservation. They therefore form a complete necessarily-DD-envy-free allocation.

5. Conclusion

The construction is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from an NP-hard problem, and it uses only $k=2$ and dyadic masses and supplies. Together with membership in $\mathrm{NP}$, this proves NP-completeness. A polynomial-time algorithm for the general problem would consequently imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.
