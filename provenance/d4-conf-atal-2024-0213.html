The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational \(\gamma>1\), ordered slot click-through rates \(x_k\), per-capita capacities \(q_k\) with \(\sum_k q_k\leq 1\), and a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over types \(\theta=(v,\tau)\), does there exist an anonymous direct mechanism computable in time polynomial in \(K\), \(\tau_0=|\operatorname{supp}(\mu)|\), and the input bit length? It must assign advertisers integral slots, possibly by lottery, be truthful and individually rational for a tagged advertiser against fixed background \(\mu\), enforce \(p\leq v\) for every realized payment \(p\) to an \(\mathrm{RB}\), and agree with the replicated-market limits of VCG and Cavallo’s mechanism in the respective pure-class cases. The mechanism must guarantee expected liquid social welfare at least \(\frac{2}{3}\) of the feasible assignment optimum.

Values \(v>0\) are rational and classes \(c\in\{\mathrm{UM},\mathrm{RB}\}\) are private. Public rational data are \(\gamma>1\), \(K\geq1\) slot categories with \(0<x_1\leq\cdots\leq x_K\), and capacities \(q_k>0\) satisfying \(\sum_kq_k\leq1\); write \(m=K\) and let \(L\) denote total input bit length.
An anonymous direct mechanism gives each reported type a lottery over an integral category \(k\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\) and a nonnegative per-click payment \(p\); \(k=0\) means no assignment, with \(x_0=p=0\). Each realization assigns at most \(q_k\) mass to category \(k\), and each advertiser receives at most one category.
Realized UM utility is \((v-p)x_k\). Realized RB utility is \((\gamma v-p)x_k\) when \(p\leq v\), and \(-\infty\) otherwise. Against every fixed background \(\mu\), every tagged advertiser must maximize expected utility by reporting both coordinates truthfully, allowing any positive rational value and either class as a report; truthful payments must satisfy \(p\leq v\) almost surely for both classes.
For truthful assignment probabilities \(a_{\theta k}\), liquid social welfare is \(W=\sum_{\theta,k\geq1}\mu_\theta v_\theta x_k a_{\theta k}\). Define \(\mathrm{OPT}\) as its maximum over \(a_{\theta k}\geq0\) with \(\sum_{k\geq1}a_{\theta k}\leq1\) and \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta a_{\theta k}\leq q_k\).
Consistency requires the truthful joint category/payment law for each supported type in a pure-class society to equal its replicated-market limit: for integers \(N\) clearing all denominators, use \(N\mu_\theta\) advertisers of each type and \(Nq_k\) individual slots of CTR \(x_k\), then let \(N\to\infty\). Break equal-value ties by uniformly random, report-independent priorities, and identify replicated slots with their categories; if necessary add one zero-valued dummy advertiser to ensure a loser exists.
In the pure-UM replicas use VCG. In the pure-RB replicas assign the highest values to the highest CTRs; listing occupied slots bottom-up as \(j=1,\ldots,J\), with CTRs \(X_j\), assigned values \(b_j\), highest losing value \(b_0\), and \(X_0=P_0=0\), charge \(P_j=\min\{b_{j-1},[P_{j-1}X_{j-1}+\gamma b_{j-1}(X_j-X_{j-1})]/X_j\}\).

Conclusion

The guarantee is unattainable under the stated requirements. In fact, pure-RB consistency and tagged-advertiser truthfulness are already incompatible. This holds for every rational $\gamma>1$, with two categories and three supported types.

The obstruction uses the prescribed treatment of exact equal-value ties.

1. A family of admissible societies

Fix any rational $\gamma>1$. Set
$$ x_1=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}, \qquad x_2=1, \qquad q_1=\frac14, \qquad q_2=\frac12. $$
These data satisfy $0<x_1<x_2$ and $q_1+q_2=\frac34\leq1$.

Take the pure-RB society
$$ \mu_{(1/2,\mathrm{RB})}=\frac14, \qquad \mu_{(1,\mathrm{RB})}=\frac12, \qquad \mu_{(2,\mathrm{RB})}=\frac14. $$
All masses are positive binary rationals, and the support size is $\tau=3$.

For this fixed background $\mu$, write $D_\mu(r)$ for the joint category/payment law received by a tagged advertiser reporting type $r$. Since the mechanism is anonymous and direct, this law depends on the report and background, rather than on the advertiser’s unobserved true type.

2. Compute the required replicated-market laws

Let $N=4t$ be any admissible replication size, with $t$ a positive integer. The replica contains:

  • $t$ advertisers of value $\frac12$;
  • $2t$ advertisers of value $1$;
  • $t$ advertisers of value $2$;
  • $t$ category-$1$ slots and $2t$ category-$2$ slots.

The prescribed value ordering makes all value-$\frac12$ advertisers losers. The category-$1$ slots receive $t$ value-$1$ advertisers. The category-$2$ slots receive the other $t$ value-$1$ advertisers and all $t$ value-$2$ advertisers.

Uniform, report-independent priorities among the $2t$ value-$1$ advertisers therefore give each such advertiser probability $\frac12$ of receiving each category. Every value-$2$ advertiser receives category $2$.

We now compute the payments using precisely the recurrence in the question.

Category-$1$ payments

The highest losing value is $b_0=\frac12$. For the bottom occupied slot, the recurrence gives
$$ P_1 = \min\left\{ \frac12, \frac{\gamma(\frac12)x_1}{x_1} \right\} = \frac12. $$
Within category $1$, consecutive CTRs are equal. Every subsequent predecessor has value $1$, so, inductively,
$$ P_j=\min\{1,P_{j-1}\}=\frac12. $$
Thus every category-$1$ payment is $\frac12$.

Category-$2$ payments

The predecessor of the first category-$2$ slot has value $1$, CTR $x_1$, and payment $\frac12$. Since $x_2=1$, the payment for this first upper slot is
$$ \begin{aligned} P_{t+1} &= \min\left\{ 1,\frac12x_1+\gamma(1-x_1) \right\}\\ &= \min\left\{ 1,\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}+1 \right\}\\ &=1. \end{aligned} $$
All subsequent category-$2$ slots have the same CTR, and their predecessors have values at least $1$. Consequently, induction gives
$$ P_j=\min\{b_{j-1},P_{j-1}\}=1 \qquad \text{for }j=t+2,\ldots,3t. $$

The resulting category/payment laws are therefore exactly the same for every admissible replication size. In particular, their replicated-market limits exist and equal these laws.

Consistency forces
$$ D_\mu((1,\mathrm{RB})) = \begin{cases} (k,p)=(1,\frac12),&\text{with probability }\frac12,\\ (k,p)=(2,1),&\text{with probability }\frac12, \end{cases} $$
and
$$ D_\mu((2,\mathrm{RB})) = (k,p)=(2,1) \qquad\text{with probability }1. $$

3. A strictly profitable tagged deviation

Consider a tagged advertiser whose true type is $(1,\mathrm{RB})$.

Both truthful payment outcomes satisfy $p\leq1$. Its expected truthful utility is therefore
$$ \begin{aligned} U_{\mathrm{truth}} &= \frac12\left(\gamma-\frac12\right)x_1 +\frac12(\gamma-1)x_2\\ &= \frac12\left(\gamma-\frac12\right) \frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma} +\frac12(\gamma-1)\\ &= (\gamma-1)-\frac{\gamma-1}{4\gamma}. \end{aligned} $$

Now let this advertiser report $(2,\mathrm{RB})$, keeping the background $\mu$ fixed as required by tagged-advertiser truthfulness. Anonymity and directness imply that it receives $D_\mu((2,\mathrm{RB}))$: category $2$ at payment $1$, with probability $1$.

This payment is feasible for its true value $1$, because the RB payment constraint allows equality. Its deviation utility is
$$ U_{\mathrm{dev}}=(\gamma\cdot1-1)x_2=\gamma-1. $$
Hence
$$ U_{\mathrm{dev}}-U_{\mathrm{truth}} = \frac{\gamma-1}{4\gamma} > 0. $$
This contradicts truthful reporting against the fixed background $\mu$.

The deviation uses another supported report, so specifying outcomes for unsupported reports cannot repair the contradiction.

4. Consequence and relation to the finite-market result

The contradiction uses only anonymity, directness, tagged-advertiser truthfulness, and the stipulated pure-RB consistency law. Thus no mechanism satisfies all the requirements, regardless of its running time or welfare guarantee. In particular, the requested $\frac23$ guarantee is unattainable.

The cited paper’s preliminaries assume distinct bidder values and describe an $\epsilon$ perturbation for ties; see page 1820, footnote 1. That convention does not establish truthfulness for the exact equal-value, uniformly randomized replica laws prescribed here. [Tang et al., AAMAS 2024](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p1818.pdf)

The binding payment $p=v$ is decisive: the upper-category outcome remains feasible for the tied lower-value advertiser, who strictly prefers it to the required lottery.
