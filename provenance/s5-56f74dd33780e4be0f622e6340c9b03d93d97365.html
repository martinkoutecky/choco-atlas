The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given item categories \(J\) with per-capita supplies \(\sigma_j\), a ranking-mass vector \(\bar\mu\), and complete hidden types \(t=(r_t,v_t)\) inducing \(\bar\mu_r=\sum_{t:r_t=r}\mu_t\), compute the best rank-only aggregate matching \(x_{tj}=\mu_t y_{r_tj}\), subject to \(\sum_j y_{rj}=1\) and \(\sum_t x_{tj}\le\sigma_j\), minimizing the worst-case ratio \(\max_t u_t(x^*)/u_t(x)\) over complete populations consistent with \(\bar\mu\), where \(x^*\) maximizes \(\sum_t\mu_t\log(u_t(x)-o_t)\), \(u_t(x)=\mu_t^{-1}\sum_jv_{tj}x_{tj}\), and \(o_t=\sum_j\sigma_jv_{tj}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(J\) contain \(m\ge2\) item categories with positive binary-rational supplies \(\sigma_j\), where \(\sum_j\sigma_j=1\). The observed society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) weak rankings with positive binary-rational masses \(\bar\mu_r\) summing to one; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A hidden completion is any finite-support society of types \(t=(r_t,v_t)\), with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), nonzero nonnegative rational value vectors \(v_t\) inducing exactly the reported weak rankings, and \(\sum_{t:r_t=r}\mu_t=\bar\mu_r\). Hidden values and the subdivision into cardinal types are unavailable to the mechanism.
A rank-only matching chooses \(y_{rj}\ge0\) using only \((\sigma,\bar\mu)\), with \(\sum_j y_{rj}=1\) and \(\sum_r\bar\mu_r y_{rj}\le\sigma_j\); it allocates \(x_{tj}=\mu_t y_{r_tj}\). Truthfulness is not required.
For a completion, a cardinal matching \(z\) satisfies \(z_{tj}\ge0\), \(\sum_jz_{tj}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_tz_{tj}\le\sigma_j\). Define \(u_t(z)=\mu_t^{-1}\sum_jv_{tj}z_{tj}\) and \(o_t=\sum_j\sigma_jv_{tj}\).
Let \(F\) be the cardinal matchings satisfying \(u_t(z)\ge o_t\) for every type, and let \(A=\{t:\exists z\in F,\ u_t(z)>o_t\}\). The bargaining benchmark \(x^*\) maximizes \(\sum_{t\in A}\mu_t\log(u_t(z)-o_t)\) over \(F\); types outside \(A\) have benchmark utility \(o_t\). If \(A\) is empty, every benchmark utility equals its disagreement utility; otherwise the benchmark utility vector is unique.
The approximation factor of \(y\) is \(\sup_{(\mu,v):\,\mu\text{ induces }\bar\mu}\max_t u_t(x^*)/u_t(x)\), with a positive numerator divided by zero interpreted as \(+\infty\).
What worst-case per-type approximation factors are achievable and unavoidable for rank-only matchings in this distributional model, as functions of \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(\sigma\)? Which guarantees are achievable by algorithms running in polynomial time in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\)?
For finite societies with \(n\) agents and \(n\) items, every ordinal mechanism has worst-case factor at least \(n-1\), and random serial dictatorship has worst-case factor \(n\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.07797)
No result settling this precise distributional approximation question was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass and total supply to one, and required positive supplies; this makes the stated disagreement allocation feasible and preserves balanced perfect matching.
  • Used weak rankings with ties, and required values to induce them exactly, following the ordinal instances used in the discrete result.
  • Specified nonzero nonnegative rational values without an additional value normalization; hidden completions may have arbitrary finite support and positive binary-rational masses.
  • Defined tau as the observed ranking support size, since that is the society available to the rank-only algorithm; hidden cardinal support is not an algorithm input.
  • Made cardinal benchmark feasibility and individual rationality explicit, while retaining raw utilities in the approximation ratio.
  • Resolved zero-gain degeneracy by maximizing the weighted log objective over precisely the types that can improve within the individually rational feasible set; other types retain their fixed disagreement utilities. This agrees with the stated log objective whenever strictly positive gains for all types are feasible.
  • Used a supremum over hidden completions because limiting valuations need not attain the worst-case factor, and specified the zero-denominator convention.
  • Interpreted rank-only matching as identical per-capita allocations within each observed ranking, without a truthfulness restriction.

Sharp worst-case factors

The minimax question indexed by $m,\tau,\sigma$ can be resolved. Throughout, sharpness means the worst case over observed societies with the specified support size and supply vector. An individual observed society can admit a better guarantee.

Write
$$ \sigma_{\min}=\min_j\sigma_j. $$

For $m\ge3$, define $C(\sigma)$ as follows. For each permutation $\pi$ of the categories, put
$$ s_i=\sigma_{\pi(i)},\qquad S_i=\sum_{h=1}^i s_h. $$
Let $g_\pi$ be the unique positive solution of
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{s_i}{\min\{1,S_i+g_\pi\}}=1. $$
Then define
$$ C(\sigma)=\max_\pi\left(1+\frac{g_\pi}{s_1}\right). $$

The sharp factors are:

| Regime | Sharp worst-case factor | Polynomial-time matching |
|---|---:|---|
| $m=2$, every admissible $\tau$ | $1$ | Explicit construction below |
| $m\ge3,\ \tau=1$ | $C(\sigma)$ | The forced matching $y_j=\sigma_j$ |
| $m\ge3$, every admissible $\tau\ge2$ | $1/\sigma_{\min}$ | $y_{rj}=\sigma_j$ |

The scalar-root formula is explicit and needs only polynomially many candidate orderings. More precisely, sort the supplies as
$$ a_1\le a_2\le\cdots\le a_m. $$
It suffices to consider the $m-1$ orderings that put $a_1$ first, choose one other supply to put last, and arrange the remaining supplies increasingly.

Useful consequences are
$$ \frac1{a_1+a_2}\le C(\sigma)<\frac1{a_1}. $$
For three categories, the formula simplifies to
$$ C(\sigma)= \max\left\{ \frac{1+\sqrt{1+4a_3/a_1}}2,\, \frac1{a_1+a_2} \right\}. $$

For uniform supplies, write $C(\sigma)=1+x_m$. The defining equation becomes
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac1{\min\{m,i+x_m\}}=1, $$
and
$$ C(\sigma)=m-\sqrt{2m}+O(1). $$
Thus one shared ranking already permits a factor approaching $m$ asymptotically. For example, with four equally supplied categories,
$$ C(\sigma)=\frac{5+\sqrt{73}}6. $$

If “binary-rational” means dyadic, the uniform specialization applies literally when $m$ is a power of two. All limiting constructions below preserve admissibility by using dyadic perturbations and dyadic hidden masses.

1. Preliminary facts and universal guarantees

Every feasible matching exhausts every supply. The row sums allocate total mass one, and the supplies sum to one; therefore all column inequalities are equalities.

The proportional rank-only matching
$$ y_{rj}=\sigma_j $$
is feasible and gives every hidden type its disagreement utility:
$$ u_t(x)=o_t. $$
Because values are nonnegative, supplies are positive, and value vectors are nonzero, $o_t>0$.

Let $M_t=\max_jv_{tj}$. Every per-capita allocation is a probability vector, so
$$ u_t(x^*)\le M_t, \qquad o_t\ge \sigma_{\min}M_t. $$
Consequently, the proportional matching guarantees
$$ \frac{u_t(x^*)}{u_t(x)}\le\frac1{\sigma_{\min}}. $$
It is computable in polynomial time.

There is also a polynomial-time guarantee sensitive to the observed support. Let $T_r$ denote ranking $r$’s top indifference class, and solve
$$ \beta^*=\max_{y,\beta}\beta $$
subject to
$$ y_{rj}\ge0,\qquad \sum_jy_{rj}=1,\qquad \sum_r\bar\mu_ry_{rj}=\sigma_j, \qquad \sum_{j\in T_r}y_{rj}\ge\beta\quad\text{for every }r. $$
All top-class categories have value $M_t$, hence
$$ u_t(x)\ge\beta^*M_t. $$
The resulting factor is at most $1/\beta^*$. The proportional matching shows that
$$ \beta^*\ge\min_r\sigma(T_r)\ge\sigma_{\min}>0. $$
This is a rational linear program of polynomial encoding size, with a bounded objective, so an optimal rational solution is computable in polynomial time.

2. One reported ranking: identifying the active types

When $\tau=1$, feasibility forces
$$ y_j=\sigma_j. $$
The problem is therefore to bound each benchmark utility relative to disagreement.

Aggregate categories within the common ranking’s indifference classes. Let there be $K$ classes, ordered from best to worst, with supplies
$$ s_1,\ldots,s_K, \qquad S_j=\sum_{h=1}^j s_h. $$
This aggregation preserves all feasible utility vectors: an allocation of a class can be distributed among its constituent categories in proportion to their supplies.

For each type, subtract its value on the bottom class. This does not change bargaining gains because every per-capita allocation has total mass one. Denote the resulting value vector by $\widetilde v_t$. If $K\ge2$, then
$$ \widetilde v_{t1}>\cdots>\widetilde v_{tK}=0. $$
Here consecutive coordinates correspond to distinct indifference classes.

Lemma. Either all $\widetilde v_t$ are positive multiples of one vector and $A$ is empty, or every type belongs to $A$.

Proof. Suppose first that $\widetilde v_t=c_tw$ with $c_t>0$. Individual rationality requires
$$ w\cdot y_t\ge w\cdot s $$
for every type’s per-capita allocation $y_t$. Supply exhaustion gives
$$ \sum_t\mu_t\,w\cdot y_t=w\cdot s. $$
Positive masses force equality for every type, so no type can improve.

Otherwise, for every type $t$ there is a type $h$ whose reduced value vector is not proportional to $\widetilde v_t$. Their restrictions to the zero-sum subspace are linearly independent. Indeed, dependence would give
$$ \widetilde v_t-\lambda\widetilde v_h=c\mathbf1; $$
the bottom coordinates imply $c=0$, and the positive top coordinates imply $\lambda>0$.

Thus there is a vector $d$ satisfying
$$ \sum_jd_j=0,\qquad \widetilde v_t\cdot d>0,\qquad \widetilde v_h\cdot d<0. $$
Starting from the proportional cardinal matching, transfer $\varepsilon d$ to type $t$ and $-\varepsilon d$ to type $h$. For sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, all allocation entries remain nonnegative because the proportional matching has strictly positive entries. Row and column totals remain unchanged. Both types strictly improve, and all other types retain disagreement utility. Hence $t\in A$. This works for every $t$. $\square$

When $K\le2$, all reduced vectors are proportional, so the factor is exactly one.

In the remaining case, all types are active. Averaging finitely many individually rational improvement witnesses gives a matching with strictly positive gains for all types. Therefore the bargaining optimum has strictly positive gains for every type.

3. One reported ranking: a price bound

Assume $K\ge3$ and all types are active. At the benchmark, define
$$ d_t=u_t(x^*)-o_t>0, \qquad w_{tj}=\frac{\widetilde v_{tj}}{d_t}, \qquad b_t=\sum_js_jw_{tj}. $$
The normalized benchmark utility satisfies
$$ \sum_jw_{tj}y^*_{tj}=b_t+1. $$

The benchmark allocation maximizes the linear objective
$$ \sum_{t,j}w_{tj}z_{tj} $$
over the entire transportation polytope. To verify this, take any feasible cardinal matching $z$. A sufficiently small movement from $x^*$ toward $z$ preserves strictly positive gains. The derivative of the bargaining objective in that direction is
$$ \sum_{t,j}w_{tj}(z_{tj}-x^*_{tj}), $$
which is nonpositive by optimality.

The transportation polytope is nonempty and compact. Linear programming duality and complementary slackness therefore give category prices $P_j$ and type constants $q_t$ such that
$$ w_{tj}\le P_j+q_t, $$
with equality whenever $x^*_{tj}>0$.

Shift the dual variables so that $P_K=0$. Since $w_{tK}=0$, the dual inequalities imply $q_t\ge0$. Every category has positive supply and is exhausted, so
$$ P_j=\max_t(w_{tj}-q_t). $$
As every $w_t$ is decreasing in the common ranking,
$$ P_1\ge\cdots\ge P_K=0. $$
Put $T=P_1$. We have $T>0$: a type receiving the bottom class has $q_t=0$, and $T=0$ would force its whole reduced value vector to vanish.

Define $g=1/T$. We claim
$$ \sum_{j=1}^{K-1}\frac{s_j}{\min\{1,S_j+g\}}\ge1. $$

For a type with $q_t>0$, complementary slackness prevents consumption of the bottom class. Therefore
$$ \sum_{j<K}\frac{y^*_{tj}}{\min\{1,S_j+g\}} \ge\sum_{j<K}y^*_{tj}=1. $$

For a type with $q_t=0$, every consumed class $j$ satisfies $w_{tj}=P_j\le T$. Monotonicity and nonnegativity of values give
$$ b_t\ge S_jw_{tj}. $$
Consequently,
$$ \frac{w_{tj}}{b_t+1} \le \frac{w_{tj}}{1+S_jw_{tj}} \le \frac{T}{1+S_jT} = \frac1{S_j+g} \le \frac1{\min\{1,S_j+g\}}. $$
Multiplying by $y^*_{tj}$ and summing yields
$$ 1=\sum_{j<K}\frac{y^*_{tj}w_{tj}}{b_t+1} \le \sum_{j<K}\frac{y^*_{tj}}{\min\{1,S_j+g\}}. $$
Averaging these inequalities over types and using supply exhaustion proves the claim.

Let $g_s$ be the unique positive root of
$$ f_s(h)=\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}\frac{s_j}{\min\{1,S_j+h\}}=1. $$
Existence and uniqueness follow because $f_s$ is continuous, strictly decreasing on $[0,1-s_1]$, and
$$ f_s(0)>1,\qquad f_s(1-s_1)=1-s_K<1. $$
The preceding inequality implies
$$ g\le g_s,\qquad T\ge\frac1{g_s}. $$

Consider a target type.

If it receives positive benchmark allocation from the top class, complementary slackness gives
$$ w_{t1}=T+q_t\ge T. $$
Thus
$$ b_t\ge s_1w_{t1}\ge\frac{s_1}{g_s}. $$
Writing its original bottom value as $\gamma_t\ge0$, its ratio is
$$ \frac{\gamma_t+d_t(b_t+1)}{\gamma_t+d_tb_t} \le1+\frac1{b_t} \le1+\frac{g_s}{s_1}. $$

If it receives no top-class allocation, let $j_0\ge2$ be its best consumed class. Its original benchmark utility is at most its value on that class, whereas disagreement is at least $S_{j_0}$ times that value. Hence
$$ \frac{u_t(x^*)}{o_t}\le\frac1{S_{j_0}}\le\frac1{S_2}. $$

We have proved the following bound for every fixed common weak ranking:
$$ \frac{u_t(x^*)}{o_t} \le \max\left\{ 1+\frac{g_s}{s_1},\, \frac1{S_2} \right\}. $$

4. One reported ranking: matching lower constructions

Fix any strict category order, with supplies $s_1,\ldots,s_m$, and let $g$ solve
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{s_i}{\min\{1,S_i+g\}}=1. $$
Put
$$ h_i=\min\{1,S_i+g\}, \qquad a_i=\frac{s_i}{h_i}, \qquad i=1,\ldots,m-1. $$
The root equation gives $\sum_i a_i=1$.

First consider an auxiliary society whose type $i$ has mass $a_i$ and values
$$ v_{ij}=\mathbf1_{\{j\le i\}}. $$
These auxiliary values need not induce the strict reported ranking; admissible approximations will be supplied below.

Give type $i$ probability $h_i$ of category $i$ and probability $1-h_i$ of category $m$. Its absolute consumption of category $i$ is $a_ih_i=s_i$, and total bottom-category consumption is
$$ \sum_i a_i(1-h_i)=1-\sum_{i<m}s_i=s_m. $$
Thus this is feasible. Type $i$ has disagreement $S_i$, utility $h_i$, and positive gain
$$ d_i=h_i-S_i=\min\{g,1-S_i\}. $$

This allocation is exactly the auxiliary bargaining optimum. To prove it, set
$$ P_j=\frac1g\quad(j<m),\qquad P_m=0, \qquad q_i=\frac1{d_i}-\frac1g\ge0. $$
Then
$$ \frac{v_{ij}}{d_i}\le P_j+q_i. $$
Equality holds on category $i$. If type $i$ consumes the bottom category, then $h_i<1$, so $d_i=g$ and $q_i=0$; equality holds there as well. Consequently, the displayed allocation maximizes the linear objective with coefficients $v_{ij}/d_i$.

For any feasible allocation with positive gains, concavity of the logarithm gives
$$ \sum_i a_i\log\frac{u_i-S_i}{d_i} \le \sum_i a_i\frac{u_i-h_i}{d_i} \le0. $$
An individually rational allocation with any zero gain has logarithmic objective $-\infty$. Thus the constructed allocation is a bargaining optimum. Strict concavity in the utility vector shows that its benchmark utilities are uniquely $h_i$.

In particular, since $g<1-s_1$,
$$ \frac{h_1}{S_1}=1+\frac g{s_1}. $$

Admissible approximation and convergence

Choose positive dyadic masses $a_i^\nu$ summing to one and converging to $a_i$. Choose positive dyadic $\varepsilon_\nu\to0$, and use values
$$ v_{ij}^\nu=\mathbf1_{\{j\le i\}}+\varepsilon_\nu(m-j). $$
Every type now induces exactly the same strict ranking. All values are nonnegative, rational, and nonzero.

We verify that the benchmark utilities converge to the auxiliary utilities. This also handles the possibly irrational auxiliary masses.

Let $z^0$ be the auxiliary optimal allocation and put
$$ e_\nu=\max_i\frac{|a_i^\nu-a_i|}{a_i}. $$
For sufficiently large $\nu$, define
$$ \widehat z_{ij}^\nu = (1-e_\nu)z_{ij}^0+ \bigl(e_\nu a_i+a_i^\nu-a_i\bigr)\sigma_j. $$
All entries are nonnegative. Its row masses are $a_i^\nu$, its column totals are $\sigma_j$, and $\widehat z^\nu\to z^0$. Its gains converge to the strictly positive auxiliary gains. Hence all types are active for sufficiently large $\nu$.

Take any convergent subsequence of benchmark allocations. A limiting gain cannot be zero: otherwise its positive limiting mass would make its logarithmic contribution tend to $-\infty$, while all other contributions are bounded above and the comparison allocations $\widehat z^\nu$ have objectives converging to a finite value.

All limiting gains are therefore positive. Continuity of the objective and benchmark optimality against $\widehat z^\nu$ show that the limit is auxiliary-optimal. Uniqueness of the auxiliary utility vector implies
$$ u_i^{*,\nu}\longrightarrow h_i. $$
The proportional utility of type $1$ tends to $S_1=s_1$. Its ratio therefore tends to
$$ 1+\frac g{s_1}. $$

This proves an unavoidable factor of $1+g_\pi/s_1$ for every strict ordering $\pi$, and hence an unavoidable factor $C(\sigma)$.

5. Completing the one-ranking upper bound

Let $a_1\le a_2$ be the two smallest category supplies.

First,
$$ C(\sigma)\ge\frac1{a_1+a_2}. $$
Indeed, consider any strict ordering with first supply $p=a_1$ and last supply $r=a_2$. Write $q=1-p-r$ and test its root equation at
$$ g_0=\frac{pq}{p+r}. $$
Since
$$ p+g_0=\frac p{p+r}<1, $$
the first summand equals $p+r$. Every other summand is at least its numerator because its denominator is at most one. Therefore
$$ f_\pi(g_0)\ge p+r+q=1. $$
Thus $g_\pi\ge g_0$, and
$$ 1+\frac{g_\pi}{p}\ge\frac1{p+r}. $$

Now consider an arbitrary common weak ranking with at least three classes. Refine its classes into a strict ordering of the individual categories. Within each original nonbottom class, the refined cumulative supplies are at most the original class’s cumulative supply. Thus, at every $h\ge0$, its refined root function is at least the original root function. Refining the bottom class adds further nonnegative terms.

Consequently,
$$ g_{\mathrm{ref}}\ge g_s. $$
The refined first-category supply is at most $s_1$, so
$$ 1+\frac{g_s}{s_1} \le 1+\frac{g_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{ref},1}} \le C(\sigma). $$
Also, the first two distinct indifference classes contain at least two distinct categories, whence
$$ S_2\ge a_1+a_2. $$
Therefore
$$ \frac1{S_2}\le\frac1{a_1+a_2}\le C(\sigma). $$

Section 3’s upper bound is therefore at most $C(\sigma)$ for every completion and every common weak ranking. Together with Section 4, this proves the sharp one-ranking factor.

The strict inequality
$$ C(\sigma)<\frac1{\sigma_{\min}} $$
follows from $g_\pi<1-s_1$ for every permutation.

Polynomially many candidate orderings

Let an ordering have first supply $a$, root $g$, and factor
$$ R=1+\frac ga. $$
Move any smaller supply $b$ to the first position by exchanging it with $a$. Evaluate the new root function at $g'=b(R-1)$.

The first summand remains $1/R$, because $aR<1$ and $bR<1$. Before the exchanged position, both cumulative supply and the tested gain decrease. At the exchanged position, if it is not last, the numerator increases from $b$ to $a$ and the denominator decreases. Later denominators also decrease. Hence the new root function at $g'$ is at least one, and its factor is at least $R$. A smallest supply may therefore be placed first.

Fix the first and last supplies. For two adjacent internal supplies $a\le b$, put $t$ equal to preceding cumulative supply plus the tested gain. Putting $a$ before $b$ changes the root function by
$$ D= \frac a{\min\{1,t+a\}} +\frac b{\min\{1,t+a+b\}} -\frac b{\min\{1,t+b\}} -\frac a{\min\{1,t+a+b\}}. $$
This is nonnegative:

  • If $t+a+b\le1$, then

$$ D=\frac{ab(b-a)}{(t+a)(t+b)(t+a+b)}\ge0. $$

  • If $t+b<1\le t+a+b$, then

$$ D=(b-a)\left(1-\frac t{(t+a)(t+b)}\right)\ge0, $$
because $(t+a)(t+b)=t(t+a+b)+ab\ge t$.

  • If $t+a<1\le t+b$, then

$$ D=a\left(\frac1{t+a}-1\right)\ge0. $$

  • If $t+a\ge1$, then $D=0$.

Thus sorting the internal supplies increasingly cannot decrease the root. Only the last supply remains to be chosen, giving $m-1$ candidates.

Bisection computes $C(\sigma)$ to additive accuracy $\delta>0$ in time polynomial in $m,L,\log(1/\delta)$: root accuracy $\sigma_{\min}\delta$ suffices. Computing this factor is unnecessary for implementing the matching, which simply copies the supplies.

Three categories and uniform supplies

For an ordering $(p,q,r)$, the root equation is
$$ \frac p{p+g}+\frac q{\min\{1,p+q+g\}}=1. $$
Its two branches give
$$ 1+\frac gp = \max\left\{ \frac{1+\sqrt{1+4q/p}}2,\, \frac1{p+r} \right\}. $$
With the smallest supply first, the second summand of the root function increases with $q$, so the largest supply goes in the middle. This proves the three-category formula stated above.

For uniform supplies, put $x=mg$ and $d=m-x$, and let $k=\lfloor d\rfloor$. The root equation is equivalent to
$$ \sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{d-i}{m-d+i}=1. $$
An endpoint term with $i=d$ is zero and causes no difficulty. Define
$$ N=\sum_{i=1}^k(d-i). $$
Since the denominators lie between $m-d+1$ and $m$,
$$ m-d+1\le N\le m. $$
Writing $d=k+\theta$ with $0\le\theta<1$ gives
$$ N=\frac{d(d-1)}2+\frac{\theta-\theta^2}2. $$
These bounds imply $d=\sqrt{2m}+O(1)$, and hence
$$ C(\sigma)=1+x=m-\sqrt{2m}+O(1). $$

6. Two or more reported rankings

We next prove the sharp lower bound $1/\sigma_{\min}$.

Two rankings

Choose a category $a$ of minimum supply $s=\sigma_{\min}$. Partition the remaining categories into nonempty sets $B,C$, and write
$$ b=\sigma(B),\qquad c=\sigma(C),\qquad s+b+c=1. $$
Use rankings
$$ r_B:\ a\succ B\succ C, \qquad r_C:\ a\succ C\succ B, $$
with ties within each displayed set. Assign masses
$$ p_B=b+\frac s2,\qquad p_C=c+\frac s2. $$

For any feasible rank-only matching,
$$ p_By_{r_Ba}+p_Cy_{r_Ca}=s. $$
At least one row has $y_{ra}\le s$. Suppose it is $r_B$; the other case follows by exchanging $B,C$.

Put $\delta=s/4$. For positive dyadic $\eta<1/2$, use these hidden types:

| Type | Mass | Value on $a$ | Value on $B$ | Value on $C$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Target | $\delta$ | $1$ | $\eta$ | $0$ |
| Bulk $B$ | $b+s/4$ | $1$ | $1-\eta$ | $0$ |
| Bulk $C$ | $c+s/2$ | $1$ | $0$ | $1-\eta$ |

All values induce the required rankings, and the hidden masses reproduce the observed masses.

Give the target $\delta$ units of $a$, bulk $B$ every supply in $B$ and $s/4$ units of $a$, and bulk $C$ every supply in $C$ and $s/2$ units of $a$. This is feasible. Every utility is at least $1-\eta$, and the target’s utility is one.

The disagreements tend respectively to
$$ s,\qquad s+b,\qquad s+c, $$
all strictly below one. Thus the comparison allocation strictly improves every type for sufficiently small $\eta$.

Let $\widehat u_t^\eta$ denote its utilities and $u_t^{*,\eta}$ the benchmark utilities. Every utility is at most one. Benchmark optimality gives
$$ 0\le \sum_t\mu_t\log\frac{1-o_t^\eta}{u_t^{*,\eta}-o_t^\eta} \le \sum_t\mu_t\log\frac{1-o_t^\eta}{\widehat u_t^\eta-o_t^\eta}. $$
The right-hand side tends to zero. Every left-hand summand is nonnegative and has fixed positive mass. Therefore
$$ u_t^{*,\eta}\longrightarrow1 $$
for every type.

The target’s rank-only utility is
$$ y_{r_Ba}+\eta\sum_{j\in B}y_{r_Bj}. $$
Its ratio tends to $1/y_{r_Ba}$, with divergence if $y_{r_Ba}=0$. Hence every feasible output has factor at least $1/s$.

Every larger admissible support size

For $\tau>2$, choose $\tau-2$ additional distinct rankings. Assign them positive dyadic masses totaling a positive dyadic $\rho$, and assign the original rankings masses
$$ (1-\rho)p_B,\qquad (1-\rho)p_C. $$
A dyadic partition of $\rho$ into any prescribed finite number of positive parts exists.

For every feasible output, at least one original row satisfies
$$ y_{ra}\le\frac{s}{1-\rho}. $$
Target that row with the preceding completion, scaling its three masses by $1-\rho$.

7. Two categories: exact implementation

Let the supplies be $p,1-p$. Denote the observed masses of the three possible rankings by
$$ a=\bar\mu_{1\succ2},\qquad b=\bar\mu_{2\succ1},\qquad c=\bar\mu_{1\sim2}, \qquad a+b+c=1, $$
assigning zero mass to absent rankings.

$$ q_+=\min\{1,p/a\}, \qquad q_-=\min\{1,(1-p)/b\}. $$
Their aggregate category-$1$ demand is
$$ D=\min\{a,p\}+\max\{0,b-(1-p)\}. $$
We have $p-c\le D\le p$:

  • If $a\ge p$, then $b\le1-p$ and $D=p$.
  • If $a<p$ and $b\le1-p$, then $D=a\in[p-c,p]$.
  • If $a<p$ and $b>1-p$, then $D=p-c$.

If $c>0$, give the indifferent ranking category-$1$ probability $(p-D)/c$. If $c=0$, the displayed bounds force $D=p$. Thus the matching is feasible.

$$ v_t=(\gamma_t+d_t,\gamma_t),\qquad d_t>0. $$

$$ d_t(h_t-p). $$
For the opposite ranking, with preferred-category probability $k_t$, its gain is
$$ d_t(k_t-(1-p)). $$

If a single strict ranking has mass one, individual rationality and supply exhaustion force all gains to be zero. The proposed matching gives disagreement utility.

Otherwise every present strict group has mass below one, and
$$ q_+>p,\qquad q_->1-p $$
for the respective present groups. The proposed allocation strictly improves all nonindifferent types, so these are exactly the active types.

Consider any individually rational allocation with positive gains for all active types. If the first strict group is present, its average preferred-category probability is at most $q_+$. Weighted Jensen’s inequality gives
$$ \sum_{t:r_t=1\succ2}\mu_t\log(h_t-p) \le a\log(q_+-p). $$
For a present opposite group, similarly,
$$ \sum_{t:r_t=2\succ1}\mu_t\log(k_t-(1-p)) \le b\log(q_--(1-p)). $$
The terms $\mu_t\log d_t$ are independent of the matching. The proposed matching attains both bounds simultaneously by assigning a common preferred-category probability within each ranking.

Allocations with a zero active gain have objective $-\infty$ and cannot improve on this construction. Indifferent types have constant utility. Therefore the proposed rank-only matching implements every hidden completion’s benchmark utility vector exactly.
