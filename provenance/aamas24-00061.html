The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given alternatives \(C\), finite ranking types \(T\subseteq S_C\), and rational masses \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), compute a lottery \(p^\star\in\Delta(C)\) minimizing \(\delta(p,\mu)=\sup_u\max_{a\in C}W_u(a)/W_u(p)\), together with the optimum \(\delta^\star\), where \(W_u(a)=\sum_{t\in T}\int_0^{\mu_t}u_{t,z}(a)\,dz\) and each utility field is unit-sum and consistent with its ranking type; equivalently, by Theorem 1, the adversary may allocate each ranking mass among the finite top-\(k\) types \((t,k)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) alternatives. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) strict complete ranking types, each with positive mass \(\mu_t\) encoded as a binary rational, with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For each \(t\), the adversary chooses a Lebesgue-measurable utility field \(u_{t,z}:C\to\mathbb R_{>0}\), indexed by \(z\in[0,\mu_t]\), satisfying \(\sum_{a\in C}u_{t,z}(a)=1\) and \(u_{t,z}(a)>u_{t,z}(b)\) whenever \(t\) ranks \(a\) above \(b\), almost everywhere.
Define \(W_u(a)=\sum_{t\in T}\int_0^{\mu_t}u_{t,z}(a)\,dz\), \(W_u(p)=\sum_{a\in C}p_aW_u(a)\), and \(\delta(p,\mu)=\sup_u\frac{\max_{a\in C}W_u(a)}{W_u(p)}\) for \(p\in\Delta(C)\).

Can an optimal lottery \(p^\star\in\arg\min_{p\in\Delta(C)}\delta(p,\mu)\) and its exact value \(\delta^\star\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this problem NP-hard?

For a finite list of \(n\) agents, exact computation of the optimal lottery and its distortion is polynomial-time solvable using an LP with \(O(nm)\) variables, constraints, and nonzero coefficients ([Ebadian et al.](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~nisarg/papers/distortion-computation.pdf)). No result explicitly settling the binary-rational support-input problem above was identified in the search.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Both an optimal lottery and its exact distortion can be computed by a rational LP with \(O(\tau m)\) variables and \(O(\tau m^2)\) constraints and nonzero coefficients.

The proof below gives an explicit formulation. It uses the prefix-utility idea appearing in [Ebadian et al., Section 3](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~nisarg/papers/distortion-computation.pdf), while proving the required equivalence for measurable utility fields and binary-rational type masses directly.

1. Replacing utility fields by a compact welfare set

Write type \(t\)’s ranking as \(c_{t,1},\ldots,c_{t,m}\), from best to worst. Define

\(K_t=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\geq 0}^{C}:\sum_{a\in C}x(a)=1,\ x(c_{t,1})\geq\cdots\geq x(c_{t,m})\}.\)

This is a compact polytope. Define the compact welfare set

\(\mathcal W=\{\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_t:x_t\in K_t\text{ for every }t\}.\)

Every admissible utility field has welfare in \(\mathcal W\): its type average

\(\bar u_t(a)=\mu_t^{-1}\int_0^{\mu_t}u_{t,z}(a)\,dz\)

belongs to \(K_t\), because integration preserves the normalization, nonnegativity, and ranking inequalities.

Conversely, every point of \(\mathcal W\) is a limit of admissible welfare vectors. Indeed, fix \(x_t\in K_t\), and let

\(v_t(c_{t,j})=\dfrac{2(m-j+1)}{m(m+1)}.\)

This vector is positive, normalized, and strictly decreasing along the ranking. For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), the constant field

\(u_{t,z}^{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon)x_t+\varepsilon v_t\)

is admissible, and its welfare tends to \(\sum_t\mu_t x_t\).

Thus \(\mathcal W\) is exactly the closure of the admissible welfare set. Consequently, for every continuous linear functional, its supremum over admissible welfare vectors equals its maximum over \(\mathcal W\).

We will apply this statement only to linear functionals, avoiding division at boundary points where a lottery’s welfare might vanish.

2. Prefix utilities and robust distortion inequalities

For each \(t\) and \(k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), define

\(e_{t,k}(a)=\dfrac{\mathbf 1\{a\in\{c_{t,1},\ldots,c_{t,k}\}\}}{k}.\)

We claim that

\(K_t=\operatorname{conv}\{e_{t,1},\ldots,e_{t,m}\}.\)

To prove this, take \(x\in K_t\), put \(x_j=x(c_{t,j})\), set \(x_{m+1}=0\), and define

\(\lambda_k=k(x_k-x_{k+1}).\)

The coefficients are nonnegative, and telescoping gives

\(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_k=\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_j=1.\)

For every position \(j\),

\(\sum_{k=j}^{m}\dfrac{\lambda_k}{k} =\sum_{k=j}^{m}(x_k-x_{k+1})=x_j.\)

Hence \(x=\sum_k\lambda_k e_{t,k}\). The reverse inclusion holds because every \(e_{t,k}\) belongs to the convex set \(K_t\).

Now fix \(p\in\Delta(C)\) and a finite \(d\geq 1\), and put \(q=dp\). For every admissible field, \(W_u(p)>0\), since every alternative has positive utility almost everywhere. Therefore

\(\delta(p,\mu)\leq d\)

is equivalent to

\(W_u(a)-\sum_{b\in C}q_bW_u(b)\leq 0 \quad\text{for every admissible field and every }a\in C.\)

By Section 1, this is equivalent to the same inequalities throughout \(\mathcal W\).

Let \(r_t(a)\) denote \(a\)’s position in ranking \(t\), and define

\(S_{t,k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}q_{c_{t,j}}.\)

For a fixed \(a\), the maximum welfare excess separates across types. Each type’s linear objective is maximized at a prefix utility, so

\(\displaystyle \max_{W\in\mathcal W}\left(W(a)-\sum_{b\in C}q_bW(b)\right) = \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t \max_{1\leq k\leq m} \frac{\mathbf 1\{r_t(a)\leq k\}-S_{t,k}}{k}. \)

The separation is valid because the choices \(x_t\in K_t\) are independent; choosing a maximizer for each type simultaneously attains the sum of their maxima.

Thus the exact distortion condition is

\[\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t \max_{1\leq k\leq m} \frac{\mathbf 1\{r_t(a)\leq k\}-S_{t,k}}{k} \leq 0 \quad\text{for every }a\in C. \tag{1} \]

3. An explicit rational LP

For every lottery, distortion is at least \(1\), because its welfare is a convex combination of the alternatives’ welfares.

The uniform lottery has welfare \(1/m\) on every admissible field, while every alternative has welfare at most \(1\). Its distortion is therefore at most \(m\). We may consequently restrict distortion guarantees to \([1,m]\).

Use variables \(q_a\), \(S_{t,k}\), and \(h_{t,a}\). Minimize

\(\displaystyle d=\sum_{a\in C}q_a\)

subject to:

  • Nonnegative scaled probabilities and the distortion bound:

\(q_a\geq 0\quad(a\in C),\qquad 1\leq\sum_{a\in C}q_a\leq m.\)

  • Prefix sums:

\(S_{t,1}=q_{c_{t,1}}\quad(t\in T),\)

\(S_{t,k}=S_{t,k-1}+q_{c_{t,k}} \quad(t\in T,\ 2\leq k\leq m).\)

  • Upper bounds on every prefix excess:

\(k h_{t,a}+S_{t,k}\geq\mathbf 1\{r_t(a)\leq k\} \quad(t\in T,\ a\in C,\ 1\leq k\leq m).\)

  • Weighted robust guarantees:

\(\displaystyle\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t h_{t,a}\leq 0 \quad(a\in C).\)

  • Auxiliary bounds:

\(-m\leq h_{t,a}\leq 1 \quad(t\in T,\ a\in C).\)

To verify equivalence with (1), define

\(\displaystyle H_{t,a}(q)= \max_{1\leq k\leq m} \frac{\mathbf 1\{r_t(a)\leq k\}-S_{t,k}}{k}. \)

Constraint 3 says precisely that \(h_{t,a}\geq H_{t,a}(q)\). Since every \(\mu_t>0\), Constraint 4 then implies (1).

Conversely, if (1) holds, choose \(h_{t,a}=H_{t,a}(q)\). Constraints 3 and 4 follow immediately. Because \(0\leq S_{t,k}\leq m\), every expression defining \(H_{t,a}(q)\) lies in \([-m,1]\). Thus Constraint 5 also holds.

The LP is feasible: take \(q_a=1\) for all \(a\). Then \(S_{t,k}=k\), and every prefix excess is nonpositive, so choosing \(h_{t,a}=H_{t,a}(q)\) satisfies all constraints.

Its feasible set is compact: the \(q\) variables and \(h\) variables are bounded, and the prefix equations bound every \(S_{t,k}\). Hence the LP attains its optimum.

4. Recovering an optimal lottery and its exact value

Let an optimal LP solution have objective value \(d^\star\). Define

\(p_a^\star=q_a^\star/d^\star.\)

Since \(d^\star\geq 1\), this is well-defined and belongs to \(\Delta(C)\). The equivalence proved above gives

\(\delta(p^\star,\mu)\leq d^\star.\)

For the reverse optimality argument, take any lottery \(p\) with \(\delta(p,\mu)\leq m\). Set \(d=\delta(p,\mu)\) and \(q=dp\). The definition of the supremum implies every admissible welfare ratio is at most \(d\), so Section 2 applies. These variables extend to a feasible LP solution of objective \(d\). Therefore

\(d^\star\leq\delta(p,\mu).\)

If instead \(\delta(p,\mu)>m\), feasibility of the LP at objective \(m\) gives the same inequality. Thus \(d^\star\) is a lower bound on every lottery’s distortion. Applying this to \(p^\star\) proves

\(\delta(p^\star,\mu)=d^\star=\min_{p\in\Delta(C)}\delta(p,\mu).\)

This also proves existence of an optimal lottery, without assuming that any adversarial supremum is attained.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

The LP has \(m+2\tau m=O(\tau m)\) variables. Constraint 3 contributes \(\tau m^2\) inequalities, each with two nonzero coefficients. All remaining constraints contribute \(O(\tau m)\) nonzero coefficients. Thus its total size is \(O(\tau m^2)\).

Apart from the input masses \(\mu_t\), all coefficients are integers of magnitude at most \(m\). Repeating the rational masses in the weighted constraints increases the encoding length only polynomially in \(m,\tau,L\).

The standard exact rational linear-programming theorem computes an optimal rational solution in time polynomial in the LP’s binary encoding length. Its hypotheses hold here: the LP is explicitly represented, rational, feasible, and bounded. In particular, optimal coordinates have polynomial bit length, as follows from determinant bounds for rational basic feasible solutions.

Finally, summing the optimal \(q_a^\star\) and dividing by \(d^\star\) take polynomial time in their bit lengths. No denominator-sized expansion into individual agents is required.
