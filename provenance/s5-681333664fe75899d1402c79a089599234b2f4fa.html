The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite advertiser types \(T\), slot formats \(J\), positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) and supplies \(q_j\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=\sum_jq_j=1\), and nonnegative binary-rational values \(v_{tj}\), find the coordinatewise greatest price vector \(p\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{J}\) admitting an allocation \(x_{tj}\ge 0\) such that \(\sum_jx_{tj}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_tx_{tj}=q_j\), and \(x_{tj}>0\) only if \(v_{tj}-p_j=\max(0,\max_{k\in J}(v_{tk}-p_k))\). Return a clearing allocation as a witness.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(T\) be advertiser types and \(J\) slot formats, with \(\tau=|T|\) and \(m=|J|\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass encoded as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input comprises positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), positive binary-rational supplies \(q_j\), and nonnegative binary-rational values \(v_{tj}\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=\sum_jq_j=1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Advertisers are unit-demand with quasi-linear utility and an outside option of utility zero. Mass of one type may be divided among formats, and all utility-maximizing ties are allowed.
A price vector \(p\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{J}\) is clearing if some allocation \(x_{tj}\ge0\) satisfies \(\sum_jx_{tj}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_tx_{tj}=q_j\), and \(x_{tj}>0\Rightarrow v_{tj}-p_j=\max\{0,\max_{k\in J}(v_{tk}-p_k)\}\). Thus all advertiser mass is assigned and all supply is used, including assignments tied with the outside option.
The computational problem is to return exactly a clearing allocation and a clearing price vector that coordinatewise dominates every clearing price vector.

Is this computational problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For the discrete market with \(m\) buyers and \(m\) individually supplied goods, maximum clearing prices can be computed by a strongly polynomial descending-price algorithm using \(O(m^4)\) operations ([Su, Abernethy, Schoenebeck, and Subramanian](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.04710)).
No result settling the exact rational-mass problem above was identified in the sources checked.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The treatment of ties is made explicit: allocation may use any utility-maximizing format, including formats tied with the zero-utility outside option.
  • The computational encoding is made explicit: m counts slot formats, tau counts positive-mass advertiser types, and L is the total binary input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The problem is solvable exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). One can compute a welfare-maximizing transportation allocation, then obtain the coordinatewise greatest clearing prices from shortest-path distances. The argument below allows arbitrary rational masses and all utility-maximizing ties.

1. Welfare maximization and existence of clearing prices

Write \(F(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{j\in J}v_{tj}x_{tj}\). Consider the full-allocation linear program

\( \displaystyle W=\max\left\{F(x):x_{tj}\geq0,\ \sum_jx_{tj}=\mu_t\ \text{for every }t,\ \sum_tx_{tj}=q_j\ \text{for every }j\right\}. \)

Its feasible region is nonempty: \(x_{tj}=\mu_tq_j\) is feasible because both total masses equal \(1\). The region is closed and bounded, so an optimum exists. Fix any optimal allocation \(x^*\).

We also need the packing LP, obtained by replacing both families of equalities by inequalities:

\( \displaystyle \widehat W=\max\left\{F(x):x_{tj}\geq0,\ \sum_jx_{tj}\leq\mu_t,\ \sum_tx_{tj}\leq q_j\right\}. \)

Claim: \(\widehat W=W\). Every full allocation is packing-feasible, so \(\widehat W\geq W\). Conversely, take any packing-feasible \(x\), and define its residual masses by

\(a_t=\mu_t-\sum_jx_{tj},\qquad b_j=q_j-\sum_tx_{tj},\qquad R=\sum_ta_t=\sum_jb_j.\)

All residuals are nonnegative. If \(R=0\), the allocation is already full. If \(R>0\), set

\( \displaystyle y_{tj}=x_{tj}+\frac{a_tb_j}{R}. \)

Then \(\sum_jy_{tj}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_ty_{tj}=q_j\). Since every value is nonnegative, \(F(y)\geq F(x)\). Applying this to a packing optimum proves \(W\geq\widehat W\), establishing the claim.

The dual of the packing LP is

\( \displaystyle \min\left\{\sum_t\mu_tu_t+\sum_jq_jp_j:u_t\geq0,\ p_j\geq0,\ u_t+p_j\geq v_{tj}\ \text{for every }t,j\right\}. \)

The packing primal is feasible and bounded. Its dual is feasible, for example with \(p_j=0\) and \(u_t=\max_jv_{tj}\). Strong LP duality therefore gives a dual optimum \((u^0,p^0)\) of value \(W\).

For any full allocation \(x\) and any dual-feasible \((u,p)\), the marginal equalities give the identity

\[ \displaystyle \sum_t\mu_tu_t+\sum_jq_jp_j-F(x)=\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}(u_t+p_j-v_{tj}). \tag{1} \]

Apply (1) to \(x^*\) and \((u^0,p^0)\). The left side is zero, and every summand on the right is nonnegative. Consequently,

\(x^*_{tj}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ u^0_t+p^0_j=v_{tj}.\)

Every type has at least one such assignment because \(\mu_t>0\). For an assigned format \(j\),

\(v_{tj}-p^0_j=u^0_t\geq0,\qquad v_{tk}-p^0_k\leq u^0_t\quad\text{for every }k.\)

Thus every assigned format maximizes utility, including comparison with the outside option. Hence \((x^*,p^0)\) clears. In particular, nonnegative clearing prices exist, even when some assignments tie with the outside option.

2. Every clearing price vector supports the fixed allocation

Let \(p\) be any clearing price vector, witnessed by a full allocation \(x\). Define

\( \displaystyle u_t=\max\left\{0,\max_{k\in J}(v_{tk}-p_k)\right\}. \)

Then \((u,p)\) is dual-feasible. Clearing gives \(u_t+p_j=v_{tj}\) whenever \(x_{tj}>0\). Equation (1) therefore shows that the packing-primal objective at \(x\) equals the dual objective at \((u,p)\).

By weak duality, both are optimal, with objective value \(W\). Applying (1) again, now to \(x^*\) and \((u,p)\), yields

\(x^*_{tj}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ u_t+p_j=v_{tj}.\)

The same utility comparison as in Section 1 proves that \((x^*,p)\) clears.

This proves a useful universal compatibility property: every clearing price vector clears with every welfare-maximizing full allocation.

Let

\(S=\{(t,j):x^*_{tj}>0\}.\)

It follows that the set of all clearing price vectors is exactly the set of vectors satisfying

\(p_j\geq0\qquad\text{for every }j,\)

\(p_j\leq v_{tj}\qquad\text{for every }(t,j)\in S,\)

\[p_j-p_k\leq v_{tj}-v_{tk}\qquad\text{for every }(t,j)\in S,\ k\in J. \tag{2}\]

Indeed, necessity follows from compatibility with \(x^*\). Conversely, these inequalities say that whenever \(x^*_{tj}>0\), the assigned utility \(v_{tj}-p_j\) is nonnegative and at least \(v_{tk}-p_k\) for every format \(k\). These are precisely the required clearing conditions.

3. Shortest paths produce the coordinatewise greatest prices

Construct a directed weighted multigraph with vertex set \(\{0\}\cup J\), where \(0\) is an auxiliary source. For every \((t,j)\in S\), add:

  • an edge \(0\longrightarrow j\) of weight \(v_{tj}\);
  • for every \(k\in J\), an edge \(k\longrightarrow j\) of weight \(v_{tj}-v_{tk}\).

Set the auxiliary coordinate \(p_0=0\). Every edge \(a\longrightarrow b\) of weight \(w\) represents the inequality \(p_b\leq p_a+w\). Thus the graph encodes exactly the last two families of inequalities in (2).

The clearing vector \(p^0\) from Section 1 satisfies all these edge inequalities. Summing them around any directed cycle shows that the cycle weight is nonnegative. Therefore the graph has no negative-weight directed cycle.

Every format is reachable from \(0\): since \(q_j>0\), the equality \(\sum_tx^*_{tj}=q_j\) implies that some \((t,j)\) belongs to \(S\), supplying a direct source edge.

Let \(d_j\) be the shortest-path distance from \(0\) to \(j\), with \(d_0=0\). These distances are finite and attained: removing cycles cannot increase a path’s weight, so a shortest path can be chosen simple.

We now prove both feasibility and domination.

Domination. Let \(p\) be any clearing price vector. Along any path \(0=a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_r=j\), successive edge inequalities give

\( \displaystyle p_j\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}w(a_{\ell-1},a_\ell). \)

Taking the minimum over paths yields \(p_j\leq d_j\). This holds for every clearing vector and every coordinate. In particular, applying it to \(p^0\) gives \(d_j\geq p^0_j\geq0\).

Feasibility. For every edge \(a\longrightarrow b\) of weight \(w\), extending a shortest path to \(a\) gives \(d_b\leq d_a+w\). Hence \(d\) satisfies all graph inequalities. Together with \(d_j\geq0\), this means that \(d\) satisfies (2).

Section 2 therefore shows that \((x^*,d)\) clears. Since \(d_j\geq p_j\) for every clearing \(p\) and every \(j\), \(d\) is the coordinatewise greatest clearing price vector.

4. Exact algorithm and running time

The algorithm is:

  • Solve the full-allocation welfare LP exactly, obtaining \(x^*\).
  • Compute its support \(S\) and construct the graph above.
  • Compute exact shortest-path distances from \(0\).
  • Return \((x^*,d)\).

The welfare LP has \(\tau m\) variables and \(\tau+m\) equality constraints, together with nonnegativity constraints. All its coefficients are rational, and its encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The standard exact rational LP theorem supplies an optimal rational solution in polynomial bit time, with polynomial output length. Its hypotheses hold here because the LP is rational, feasible, and bounded. See [Goemans’s notes on LP encoding, rational solutions, and polynomial-time solvability](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-854j-advanced-algorithms-fall-2008/72e3bdf718c625bc850cdab40b416516_lec11.pdf).

Writing \(s=|S|\leq\tau m\), the graph has \(m+1\) vertices and at most \(s(m+1)\) edges. Bellman–Ford therefore computes the distances in \(O(\tau m^3)\) arithmetic operations; negative edge weights are permitted, and we proved that negative cycles are absent. See [MIT’s Bellman–Ford notes](https://people.csail.mit.edu/alinush/6.006-spring-2014/mit-fall-2010-bellman-ford.pdf).

Exact arithmetic also has polynomial cost. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input values \(v_{tj}\). Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Scaling edge weights by \(D\) makes them integers of \(O(L)\) bits. A shortest simple path uses at most \(m\) edges, so its scaled weight has \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits. A synchronous Bellman–Ford implementation uses only walks of at most \(m\) edges and therefore has the same bound on intermediate finite distances.

Consequently, the graph stage can be implemented within

\( \displaystyle O\!\left(\tau m^3\bigl(L+\log(m+1)\bigr)^2\right) \)

bit operations, including denominator scaling with elementary integer arithmetic. Together with the exact LP computation, the total running time is \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\).

The algorithm uses the explicitly listed \(\tau\) types directly. Its running time does not depend on expanding rational masses into individually represented buyers.
