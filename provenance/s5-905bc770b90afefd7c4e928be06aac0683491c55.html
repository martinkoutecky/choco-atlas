The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given schools \(S\), an unmatched option \(U\), a finite set of student types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), strict preferences over schools, school priorities over types, and per-capita capacities \(q_s\), compute a mass-valued student-proposing deferred-acceptance allocation \(y\). After school \(e\) closes, keep every assignment \(y_{t,s}\) for \(s\ne e\) fixed and let \(d_t=y_{t,e}\). Compute an assignment \(x_{t,s}\) of each displaced mass \(d_t\) to an acceptable remaining school or \(U\), with capacity additions \(\Delta_s=\max\{0,\sum_t(y_{t,s}+x_{t,s})-q_s\}\), such that the resulting allocation is stable and student-optimal among stable continuations preserving all non-displaced offers.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(S\) contain \(m\geq1\) schools, with nonnegative binary rational capacities \(q_s\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type has a strict, possibly incomplete preference list of acceptable schools, followed by the unlimited unmatched option \(U\). Each school strictly ranks all types; students within a type have equal priority, with no further tie-breaking. Mass is divisible.
Compute the original allocation \(y\) by student-proposing mass deferred acceptance: rejected mass proposes to its next choice, and each school retains its highest-priority proposing mass up to capacity, splitting the boundary type when necessary.
A specified school \(e\in S\) closes. Set \(R=(S\setminus\{e\})\cup\{U\}\) and \(d_t=y_{t,e}\). A continuation assigns nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,s}\) only to acceptable remaining schools or \(U\), with \(\sum_{s\in R}x_{t,s}=d_t\); every original non-displaced assignment, including to \(U\), stays fixed. Write \(z_{t,s}=y_{t,s}+x_{t,s}\) for \(s\in R\).
Remaining schools may expand without budget limits or expansion costs. Their resulting capacities are \(c_s=q_s+\Delta_s\), where \(\Delta_s=\max\{0,\sum_tz_{t,s}-q_s\}\).
A continuation is stable if there are no \(t,r,s\), with \(r\in R\), \(s\in S\setminus\{e\}\), \(z_{t,r}>0\), and \(s\succ_t r\), such that either \(\sum_uz_{u,s}<c_s\) or \(z_{u,s}>0\) for some type \(u\) ranked strictly below \(t\) at \(s\). This condition includes all fixed and displaced mass.
A stable continuation \(x\) is student-optimal if, for every stable continuation \(x'\) and every type \(t\) and preference cutoff \(a\in R\), \(\sum_{s\in R:s\succeq_t a}x_{t,s}\geq\sum_{s\in R:s\succeq_t a}x'_{t,s}\), comparing each continuation under its own resulting capacities.

Is computing a student-optimal stable continuation in this model polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite students with strict priorities, Stable Expansion produces the student-optimal stable matching preserving all non-displaced assignments and permitting unrestricted capacity expansion. [Discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13604v1)
Continuum mechanisms respecting assignment guarantees and priorities are known for reassignment after late cancellations, a different problem; no result settling the exact closure problem above was located. [Continuum reassignment result](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/21326/schoolchoice.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total student mass to one so that capacities are per capita; allowed nonnegative rational capacities without assuming schools fill.
  • Carried over possibly incomplete strict student preferences, with the unlimited unmatched option last, and complete strict school rankings of types.
  • Resolved priorities within a positive-mass type as equality, without additional tie-breaking; deferred acceptance splits a boundary type, and justified envy requires strictly higher priority.
  • Defined mass deferred acceptance through proposals and priority-based retention of divisible mass.
  • Preserved original unmatched mass as well as all assignments at remaining schools; only mass originally assigned to the closed school may move.
  • Specified stability using both vacancies and strictly lower-priority occupants, evaluated against the minimally expanded capacities and including fixed mass.
  • Defined student optimality by cumulative preference dominance for each displaced type across all stable continuations, each evaluated under its own capacities.
  • Made capacity expansion unrestricted and costless, as in the discrete resource-reduction model, without introducing a capacity-minimization objective.

Result

Computing a student-optimal stable continuation is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\).

The key is that fixed assignments impose a priority threshold at each remaining school. Assign every displaced type entirely to its favorite remaining school that satisfies this threshold, or to \(U\). Below is a complete proof, including the computation of the original allocation.

1. A property of the original allocation

Write \(v\triangleright_s t\) when school \(s\) ranks type \(v\) strictly above type \(t\), and put \(f_s=\sum_t y_{t,s}\).

The original mass deferred-acceptance allocation has the following property:

Lemma 1. If \(y_{t,r}>0\) and \(s\succ_t r\), then \(f_s=q_s\), and \(y_{u,s}=0\) for every type \(u\) with \(t\triangleright_s u\).

Proof. Positive mass of type \(t\) reaching \(r\) must previously have proposed to \(s\) and been rejected there. At that rejection, school \(s\) was full and retained only types ranked at least as high as \(t\).

Thereafter, \(s\) remains full. Because every update retains the highest-priority mass among its incumbents and new proposals, it cannot subsequently retain a type strictly below \(t\): its existing mass of types ranked at least as high as \(t\) already fills its capacity. Both assertions therefore hold at termination. This also covers \(q_s=0\). \(\square\)

In particular, \(y\) is stable under the original capacities. The lemma permits positive mass of type \(t\) both at \(s\) and at worse assignments, consistently with the absence of within-type tie-breaking.

2. The thresholds imposed by fixed assignments

For each remaining school \(s\), define

\(F_s=\{v:\text{ there exists }r\in R\text{ with }y_{v,r}>0\text{ and }s\succ_v r\}.\)

Thus \(F_s\) consists of types having positive fixed mass at an assignment worse than \(s\). Fixed unmatched mass is included.

Call type \(t\) eligible at \(s\) if \(s\) is acceptable to \(t\) and there is no \(v\in F_s\) with \(v\triangleright_s t\). Declare \(U\) eligible for every type.

Equality with a threshold type is allowed: mass of the same type does not have strictly higher priority.

Lemma 2. Every stable continuation \(x'\) satisfies \(x'_{t,s}=0\) whenever \(t\) is ineligible at \(s\).

Proof. Unacceptable assignments are forbidden by feasibility. Otherwise, ineligibility supplies \(v\in F_s\) with \(v\triangleright_s t\). Choose \(r\in R\) witnessing \(v\in F_s\).

If \(x'_{t,s}>0\), then \(z'_{t,s}>0\), while \(z'_{v,r}\geq y_{v,r}>0\) and \(s\succ_v r\). Consequently, type \(v\) blocks school \(s\) through its positive assignment of the strictly lower-priority type \(t\). This contradicts stability, independently of the resulting capacities. \(\square\)

Two further consequences will be useful:

  • If \(F_s\neq\varnothing\), Lemma 1 implies \(f_s=q_s\).
  • If \(v\in F_s\), Lemma 1 implies that no fixed occupant of \(s\) is strictly below \(v\).

3. Construction of the continuation

For each type \(t\), let \(b_t\) be its favorite eligible option among the acceptable remaining schools and \(U\). This option exists because \(U\) is eligible.

Define

\(x^*_{t,r}=\begin{cases}d_t,&r=b_t,\\0,&r\neq b_t.\end{cases}\)

This is a feasible rational continuation. In particular, each displaced type’s mass goes entirely to one option.

Let \(z^*_{t,r}=y_{t,r}+x^*_{t,r}\), and use exactly the prescribed capacities:

\(c^*_s=\max\{q_s,\sum_t z^*_{t,s}\}.\)

4. Proof of stability

Suppose \(z^*_{t,r}>0\) and \(s\succ_t r\), where \(s\) is a remaining school. We prove that \(s\) has no vacancy and no positive mass of a type strictly below \(t\).

Case 1: \(y_{t,r}>0\)

Here \(t\in F_s\). Lemma 1 gives \(f_s=q_s\) and excludes every strictly lower-priority type from the fixed occupants of \(s\).

A type \(u\) with \(t\triangleright_s u\) is ineligible at \(s\), because \(t\in F_s\). The construction therefore also gives \(x^*_{u,s}=0\). Thus no strictly lower-priority type has positive total mass at \(s\).

Moreover, its total load \(\ell_s=\sum_u z^*_{u,s}\) satisfies \(\ell_s\geq f_s=q_s\). Hence \(c^*_s=\ell_s\), so there is no vacancy.

Case 2: \(y_{t,r}=0\)

Then \(x^*_{t,r}>0\), so \(r=b_t\). Since \(s\succ_t b_t\), school \(s\) must be ineligible for \(t\). It is acceptable because it is strictly preferred to an assigned acceptable option or to \(U\). Therefore there exists \(v\in F_s\) with \(v\triangleright_s t\).

By Lemma 1, \(f_s=q_s\), and every fixed occupant of \(s\) is ranked at least as high as \(v\). In particular, no fixed occupant is strictly below \(t\).

If \(t\triangleright_s u\), transitivity gives \(v\triangleright_s u\). Such a type \(u\) is ineligible at \(s\), so it receives no displaced mass there either.

Finally, \(\ell_s\geq f_s=q_s\) again gives \(c^*_s=\ell_s\), excluding a vacancy.

These cases cover every positive assignment \(z^*_{t,r}\), and exclude both forms of blocking in the question. Thus \(x^*\) is stable. \(\square\)

5. Proof of student optimality

Let \(x'\) be any stable continuation, evaluated under its own resulting capacities.

By Lemma 2, its positive assignments for type \(t\) use only eligible options. Since \(b_t\) is the favorite eligible option, every such assignment is weakly worse than \(b_t\).

Fix any preference cutoff \(a\).

  • If \(b_t\succeq_t a\), the construction assigns all \(d_t\) above the cutoff. Its cumulative mass is \(d_t\), which bounds the cumulative mass of any feasible continuation.
  • If \(a\succ_t b_t\), no eligible option lies above the cutoff. Lemma 2 therefore forces the cumulative mass of \(x'\) above that cutoff to be zero, as it is for \(x^*\).

Consequently,

\(\sum_{r\in R:r\succeq_t a}x^*_{t,r}\geq\sum_{r\in R:r\succeq_t a}x'_{t,r}\)

for every type and cutoff. This is precisely the required student-optimality condition.

6. Polynomial-time computation

Computing the original mass DA allocation

It is necessary to distinguish the allocation defined by mass DA from a naive implementation: batch proposal algorithms for stable allocation can require exponentially many iterations.

We use the known algorithmic result that, for a bipartite stable-allocation instance with strict vertex preferences, vertex quotas, and edge capacities, the proposing side’s DA allocation can be computed in polynomially many arithmetic operations. Dean and Munshi establish that ordinary generalized DA and their accelerated algorithm return the same allocation in Lemmas 1 and 5; Section 3 supplies the polynomial running time. [Dean–Munshi, *Faster Algorithms for Stable Allocation Problems*](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

Here are the hypotheses and reduction for this instance.

Use one job vertex per type, with quota \(\mu_t\), and one machine vertex per school, with quota \(q_s\). Include precisely the acceptable type-school edges, with capacity \(\min\{\mu_t,q_s\}\), and inherit the specified strict preferences.

To represent unmatched mass and unused seats with exact vertex quotas, add a dummy machine representing \(U\), of quota \(1\), and a dummy job of quota \(Q=\sum_s q_s\). Connect every real job to \(U\), every real machine to the dummy job, and the two dummies to each other. Edge capacities are the minimum of their endpoint quotas. Each real vertex ranks its dummy neighbor last; both dummy vertices rank the other dummy last, with arbitrary strict orders otherwise.

A real allocation \(y\) extends to this instance by assigning:

  • each real job’s unmatched mass to \(U\);
  • each school’s unused capacity to the dummy job;
  • mass \(A\) between the dummy vertices, where \(A\) is the total mass assigned to real schools.

Indeed, unused school capacity totals \(Q-A\), while unmatched real mass totals \(1-A\), so all vertex quotas are satisfied.

Dummy pairs cannot block: a real vertex always prefers its real acceptable neighbors to its dummy neighbor. A dummy occupant at a school represents exactly an unused seat. Thus real blocking pairs have precisely the original vacancy-or-lower-priority interpretation. The real edge capacities are redundant under the endpoint quotas.

The real proposal and rejection operations are therefore the specified student-proposing mass DA operations, with dummy school occupants representing empty seats. The cited accelerated algorithm computes their terminal allocation \(y\).

Bit complexity

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses and capacities. Its binary length is at most the sum of their encoded denominator lengths, hence \(O(L)\).

Scaling by \(D\) gives integral quotas and capacities. The accelerated algorithm’s augmentations use addition, subtraction, comparison, and minima; they preserve integrality. All allocations remain bounded by the total scaled quota \(D(1+Q)\), whose binary length is polynomial in \(L\) and \(\log m\). Thus its polynomial arithmetic bound also gives polynomial bit complexity. Dividing by \(D\) recovers \(y\).

The reduced graph has \(\tau+m+2\) vertices and at most \(\tau m+\tau+m+1\) edges, so its size is polynomial in the support size.

Computing the continuation

Given \(y\), construct every \(F_s\) by scanning types and their fixed assignments. This takes \(O(\tau m^2)\) elementary checks.

If \(\rho_s(t)\) is type \(t\)’s priority rank, with smaller ranks better, put \(h_s=\min_{v\in F_s}\rho_s(v)\), using \(h_s=\tau+1\) when \(F_s\) is empty. An acceptable type \(t\) is eligible exactly when \(\rho_s(t)\leq h_s\).

Scanning each type’s preference list now finds \(b_t\). Forming \(x^*\), the school loads, and the expansions requires only polynomially many rational operations.

This also covers \(m=1\), when every displaced type goes to \(U\), and types with \(d_t=0\). Therefore the entire requested computation is polynomial-time.
