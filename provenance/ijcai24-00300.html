The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite chore-category set \(M\), rational additive costs \(c_t(S)=\sum_{e\in S}w_{t,e}\) for \(t\in\{1,2,3\}\), population masses \(\mu_t=1/3\), and per-capita chore supplies \(\rho_e=1/3\), find nonnegative masses \(x_{t,S}\) over whole bundles \(S\subseteq M\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=1/3\) and \(\sum_{t=1}^3\sum_{S\ni e}x_{t,S}=1/3\), such that whenever \(x_{t,S}>0\), \(x_{u,R}>0\), and \(e\in S\), \(c_t(S\setminus\{e\})\le2c_t(R)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

A society has chore classes \(E\), types \(T\), type masses \(\mu_t\), per-capita supplies \(\rho_e\), and normalized monotone costs \(c_t:2^E\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\cup\{+\infty\}\). Each agent may receive at most one copy of each class.
An allocation is masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq E}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T,\,S\ni e}x_{t,S}=\rho_e\). It is EFX if, whenever \(x_{t,S}>0\), \(x_{u,R}>0\), and \(e\in S\), then \(c_t(S\setminus\{e\})\le c_t(R)\).

Target

Is deciding whether such an EFX mass allocation exists solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results

For indivisible chores, deciding whether an EFX allocation exists is NP-complete, even with three agents and superadditive cost functions.

Complexity of EFX Mass Allocation

Result

Under the standard succinct encoding of additive costs by binary-encoded item weights, deciding whether an EFX mass allocation exists is weakly \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. In particular, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even with:

  • one agent type of mass \(1\);
  • finite additive costs;
  • supplies only in \(\{1/2,1\}\).

Thus divisible population masses do not eliminate the hardness of EFX existence.

The representation qualification is necessary: the original statement does not specify how an arbitrary table \(c_t:2^E\to\mathbb Q_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\}\) is encoded. The theorem below applies whenever additive functions are represented succinctly by their item weights, as is standard.

Reduction from \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\)

Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\) be a \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) instance consisting of positive integers, encoded in binary. Write

\[ A=\sum_{i=1}^m a_i. \]

The question is whether there is \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\) satisfying

\[ \sum_{i\in I}a_i=A/2. \]

Construct the following mass-allocation instance.

There is one type \(t\), with mass \(\mu_t=1\). The chore classes are

\[ E=\{z,e_1,\ldots,e_m\}. \]

The supplies are

\[ \rho_z=1,\qquad \rho_{e_i}=1/2\quad\text{for every }i. \]

Define the additive cost function

\[ c(S)=\mathbf 1[z\in S]+2\sum_{\{i:e_i\in S\}}a_i. \]

This function is normalized by \(c(\varnothing)=0\), finite, monotone, and additive. Its succinct description has size polynomial in the \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) input.

If normalization additionally requires \(c(E)=1\), divide every cost by \(1+2A\). Positive scaling preserves every EFX inequality, so the proof is unchanged.

Structural lemmas for the construction

Every supported bundle contains \(z\)

Feasibility gives

\[ \sum_{S\subseteq E}x_{t,S}=1 \]

and

\[ \sum_{\{S:z\in S\}}x_{t,S}=\rho_z=1. \]

Subtracting these equalities yields

\[ \sum_{\{S:z\notin S\}}x_{t,S}=0. \]

All variables are nonnegative, so \(x_{t,S}=0\) whenever \(z\notin S\).

All supported bundles have equal cost

Let \(S\) and \(R\) be any two supported bundles. Both contain \(z\). Applying EFX to the ordered pair \(S,R\) and removing \(z\) from \(S\) gives

\[ c(S\setminus\{z\})=c(S)-1\leq c(R). \]

Interchanging \(S\) and \(R\) gives

\[ c(R)-1\leq c(S). \]

Consequently,

\[ |c(S)-c(R)|\leq 1. \]

Because both bundles contain \(z\),

\[ c(S)-c(R) = 2\left( \sum_{\{i:e_i\in S\}}a_i- \sum_{\{i:e_i\in R\}}a_i \right). \]

Thus \(c(S)-c(R)\) is an even integer. The only even integer of absolute value at most \(1\) is \(0\). Therefore

\[ c(S)=c(R) \]

for all supported bundles.

Correctness of the reduction

A partition produces an EFX mass allocation

Suppose \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=A/2\). Define

\[ S_I=\{z\}\cup\{e_i:i\in I\}, \qquad S_{\overline I}=\{z\}\cup\{e_i:i\notin I\}. \]

Assign mass \(1/2\) to each bundle:

\[ x_{t,S_I}=x_{t,S_{\overline I}}=1/2. \]

Every ordinary chore \(e_i\) belongs to exactly one of the two bundles, so its allocated mass is \(1/2\). Chore \(z\) belongs to both bundles, so its allocated mass is \(1\).

Moreover,

\[ c(S_I)=c(S_{\overline I})=1+A. \]

For either supported bundle \(S\), every \(e\in S\), and either supported comparison bundle \(R\), monotonicity and equality of the two bundle costs give

\[ c(S\setminus\{e\})\leq c(S)=c(R). \]

Hence the allocation is EFX.

An EFX mass allocation produces a partition

Conversely, suppose an EFX mass allocation exists. By the equal-cost lemma, there is a number \(\lambda\) such that every supported bundle has cost \(\lambda\).

The average cost of the allocation is determined by the supplies:

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{S\subseteq E}x_{t,S}c(S) &= \sum_{\{S:z\in S\}}x_{t,S} + 2\sum_{i=1}^m a_i \sum_{\{S:e_i\in S\}}x_{t,S}\\ &= 1+2\sum_{i=1}^m a_i\cdot\frac12\\ &= 1+A. \end{aligned} \]

Since the total mass is \(1\) and every supported bundle has cost \(\lambda\), it follows that \(\lambda=1+A\).

Choose any supported bundle \(S\), which exists because the total mass is \(1\). It contains \(z\), and therefore

\[ 1+2\sum_{\{i:e_i\in S\}}a_i=c(S)=1+A. \]

Thus

\[ \sum_{\{i:e_i\in S\}}a_i=A/2. \]

The ordinary chores contained in \(S\) consequently define a solution to the original \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) instance.

This proves the equivalence and hence weak \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

For succinct additive costs, the problem is also in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Suppose \(x\) is feasible and let \(K\) be its finite positive support. Restrict the allocation equalities to variables indexed by \(K\). Their coefficient matrix has one row for every type and one row for every chore class. Hence it has at most \(|T|+|E|\) rows.

The resulting nonempty polytope is bounded by the type-mass equations. Choose an extreme point. At an extreme point, the columns corresponding to positive variables are linearly independent; otherwise a sufficiently small perturbation in both directions along a dependence would contradict extremality. Consequently, the extreme point has at most \(|T|+|E|\) positive variables.

Passing to this smaller support preserves EFX, because EFX only quantifies over positive-support bundles. Since the constraint matrix is a \(0\)-\(1\) matrix and the right-hand sides are rational, Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s determinant bound give polynomial encoding length for all positive masses.

A certificate therefore lists at most \(|T|+|E|\) triples \((t,S,x_{t,S})\). One can verify the mass equations, supply equations, positivity, and all EFX inequalities in polynomial time. Additive costs can be evaluated directly from their item weights.

Therefore the succinct additive restriction is weakly \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, and the general succinct problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

Overall assessment

The prover correctly establishes weak \( \mathrm{NP} \)-completeness under the explicitly stated succinct additive-cost encoding. In particular, the reduction proves \( \mathrm{NP} \)-hardness for a restricted subclass of the instances in the task.

The qualification concerning encoding is appropriate: without specifying how arbitrary set functions are represented, a polynomial-time complexity classification is not formally defined.

Step-by-step audit

1. Construction and encoding size

The reduction from \( \mathrm{PARTITION} \) uses one type of mass \( 1 \), one class \( z \) of supply \( 1 \), and classes \( e_i \) of supply \( 1/2 \). The cost function

\[ c(S)=\mathbf 1[z\in S]+2\sum_{\{i:e_i\in S\}}a_i \]

is additive, finite, monotone, and satisfies \( c(\varnothing)=0 \).

The item weights have binary encoding length polynomial in the original \( \mathrm{PARTITION} \) input. The construction is therefore a polynomial-time reduction under the stated succinct encoding.

The optional rescaling for a convention requiring \( c(E)=1 \) is valid: multiplication by a positive rational preserves all EFX inequalities, and the denominator \( 1+2A \) has polynomial binary length.

2. Every supported bundle contains \( z \)

The total mass of all bundles is \( 1 \), while the total mass assigned bundles containing \( z \) is also \( 1 \). Nonnegativity then forces every bundle not containing \( z \) to have zero mass. This is correct.

3. Equal-cost lemma

For supported bundles \( S \) and \( R \), both contain \( z \). Applying EFX with \( e=z \) gives

\[ c(S)-1\leq c(R) \]

and, after reversing the ordered pair,

\[ c(R)-1\leq c(S). \]

Hence \( |c(S)-c(R)|\leq1 \). Because the contribution of \( z \) cancels and all other weights are even integers, \( c(S)-c(R) \) is even. It must therefore be zero. This argument is complete and correct.

4. Forward direction

Given a partition, the two complementary bundles each receive mass \( 1/2 \). The supply constraints are satisfied exactly.

Both bundles have cost \( 1+A \). For every supported \( S,R \) and every \( e\in S \), monotonicity gives

\[ c(S\setminus\{e\})\leq c(S)=c(R). \]

Thus all required EFX inequalities hold. This direction is correct.

5. Reverse direction

The equal-cost lemma supplies a common supported-bundle cost \( \lambda \). Additivity and the supply constraints give the average cost

\[ 1+2\sum_i a_i\cdot\frac12=1+A. \]

Since the total mass is \( 1 \), the common cost is \( \lambda=1+A \). Any supported bundle contains \( z \), so its ordinary classes have total weight \( A/2 \). This yields a valid partition. The reasoning also correctly excludes odd-\( A \) instances.

6. Membership in \( \mathrm{NP} \)

Starting from any feasible EFX allocation, restricting to its positive support produces a nonempty bounded polytope. An extreme point has at most \( |T|+|E| \) positive coordinates because its positive columns must be linearly independent.

Shrinking the support cannot introduce new EFX comparisons, so EFX is preserved. Standard determinant bounds for rational linear systems provide polynomial-size rational coordinates. A certificate with at most \( |T|+|E| \) supported bundles can therefore be checked in polynomial time, including all pairwise EFX inequalities. This argument is sound.

Scope of the conclusion

The proof establishes weak \( \mathrm{NP} \)-completeness for succinctly represented additive costs. It does not claim strong \( \mathrm{NP} \)-hardness, and the use of \( \mathrm{PARTITION} \) supports precisely the stated weak-hardness conclusion.
