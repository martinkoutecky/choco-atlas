Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A top-ℓ ranking is a strict order of ℓ candidates, with every unranked candidate tied below them. Let δ_R(a,b)=1 if \(a\succ_R b\), \(-1\) if \(b\succ_R a\), and \(0\) otherwise. For a mass vector μ, let \(\omega_\mu(a,b)=\sum_R\mu_R\delta_R(a,b)\) and \(s_\mu(a)=\min_{b\ne a}\omega_\mu(a,b)\); the Maximin winner maximizes \(s_\mu\), with ties resolved by a fixed deterministic priority order.

Target. For every fixed \(\ell\ge2\), given \(M\), rational \(\nu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{\mathcal R_\ell(M)}\), \(\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\), and \(c\in M\), is deciding whether there is \(x\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{\mathcal R_\ell(M)}\) with \(\sum_Rx_R=\alpha\) such that \(c\) is the Maximin winner for \(\mu=\nu+x\) solvable in polynomial time, or NP-hard?

Nearest known results. For every fixed \(\ell\ge2\), the corresponding finite-profile problem, in which exactly an integer number of absent top-ℓ ballots must be chosen, is NP-complete. No verified result for the mass-vector version is stated here.

Theorem

For every fixed \(\ell\ge 2\), the mass-vector version of top-\(\ell\) WAV-Maximin is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Consequently, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and is not polynomial-time solvable unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The reduction below differs from the finite-profile reduction of [Han, Marian, and Xia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07150): it uses a positive mass threshold that prevents fractional set-cover solutions.

1. A normal form for the added mass

Let \(x\) be any feasible added mass vector of total mass \(\alpha\).

Lemma 1

If \(c\) is the Maximin winner under \(\nu+x\), then there is another added mass vector \(x'\), also of total mass \(\alpha\), under which \(c\) is the winner and every positive-mass ballot ranks \(c\) first.

Proof

Consider mass \(\lambda>0\) on one ballot \(R\).

If \(c\) is ranked but is not first, move \(c\) to first while preserving the relative order and ranked status of every other candidate. Only comparisons involving \(c\) change: margins of \(c\) weakly increase, while no rival’s Maximin score increases. Hence \(c\) remains the winner.

Suppose instead that \(c\) is unranked. Write \(R=(r_1,\ldots,r_\ell)\), and replace its mass \(\lambda\) by mass \(\lambda\) on \(R'=(c,r_1,\ldots,r_{\ell-1})\). Let \(d=r_\ell\).

Every pairwise margin of \(c\) increases by at least \(\lambda\): it increases by \(2\lambda\) against each formerly ranked candidate and by \(\lambda\) against every other formerly unranked candidate. Therefore \(s(c)\) increases by at least \(\lambda\).

For any rival \(a\), each of its pairwise margins increases by at most \(\lambda\). Indeed, the only possible increase occurs when \(a\) was unranked: its margin against \(d\) increases from \(-\lambda\) to \(0\), while its margin against \(c\) decreases by \(\lambda\). Consequently, \(s(a)\) increases by at most \(\lambda\).

Thus every score difference \(s(c)-s(a)\) weakly increases. This preserves both weak comparisons and strict comparisons required by the priority order. Applying this replacement to every ballot not ranking \(c\) first proves the lemma. \(\square\)

In this normal form, every added ballot contributes \(1\) to \(\omega(c,a)\) for every \(a\ne c\). Hence

\(\omega_{\nu+x}(c,a)=\omega_\nu(c,a)+\alpha\)

for every rival \(a\).

2. Realizing arbitrary rational margins

Lemma 2

For fixed \(\ell\ge2\), every rational skew-symmetric matrix \(A\) on a candidate set \(M\), with \(A(a,a)=0\), is the weighted-majority matrix of a nonnegative rational mass vector over top-\(\ell\) rankings. Such a vector can be constructed in polynomial time.

Proof

Let \(U\) assign mass \(1\) to every ordered top-\(\ell\) ranking. By symmetry, \(\omega_U(a,b)=0\) for every pair \(a,b\).

Fix distinct \(a,b\), and choose a ranking \(R=(b,a,r_3,\ldots,r_\ell)\). Replace one unit of mass on \(R\) by one unit on \(R'=(a,b,r_3,\ldots,r_\ell)\). The resulting nonnegative vector \(U^{a,b}\) has only one nonzero margin:

\(\omega_{U^{a,b}}(a,b)=2\).

Indeed, swapping the adjacent first two candidates changes only their mutual comparison.

For every unordered pair \(\{a,b\}\), add \(\lvert A(a,b)\rvert/2\) copies of the appropriate vector \(U^{a,b}\) or \(U^{b,a}\). The resulting mass vector realizes \(A\). There are \(O(\lvert M\rvert^\ell)\) ballot types and \(O(\lvert M\rvert^2)\) pairs, so the construction is polynomial for fixed \(\ell\). \(\square\)

3. Reduction from exact cover by \(3\)-sets

Fix \(\ell\ge2\). Consider an \(\mathrm{X3C}\) instance consisting of a universe \(X\) with \(\lvert X\rvert=3k\) and a family \(\mathcal S\) of \(3\)-element subsets of \(X\). We may assume \(k\ge\ell-1\), since \(\ell\) is fixed and the finitely many smaller values can be decided directly.

The candidates are

\(M=\{c,d,g\}\cup X\cup\mathcal S\),

where elements and sets are identified with distinct candidates.

Define

\(\alpha=2k\), \(B=2(\ell-1)\), \(T=2k+1\), \(\eta=\dfrac{B}{2(k+1)}\), and \(\sigma=-T\).

We prescribe the initial pairwise-margin matrix \(A\) as follows:

  • \(A(c,d)=-T-\alpha\).
  • \(A(c,e)=-\alpha\) for every \(e\in X\).
  • \(A(c,S)=T+\eta-\alpha\) for every \(S\in\mathcal S\).
  • \(A(c,g)=T+\eta-\alpha\).
  • \(A(d,g)=-T-\eta\).
  • \(A(e,S)=-T+B-\eta\) whenever \(e\in S\).
  • Every unlisted margin is \(0\), and \(A(b,a)=-A(a,b)\).

By Lemma \(2\), a nonnegative rational top-\(\ell\) mass vector \(\nu\) realizing \(A\) can be constructed in polynomial time.

4. Completeness of the reduction

Forward direction

Assume that \(\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal S\) is an exact cover. Then \(\lvert\mathcal C\rvert=k\).

Put equal mass on every ballot consisting of \(c\) followed by an ordered \((\ell-1)\)-tuple of distinct candidates from \(\mathcal C\). If \(N=k(k-1)\cdots(k-\ell+2)\) is the number of these tuples, assign mass \(\alpha/N\) to each ballot.

The total added mass is \(\alpha\). Each selected set candidate occurs in a fraction \((\ell-1)/k\) of this mass, so its total ranked mass is

\(\alpha\dfrac{\ell-1}{k}=2(\ell-1)=B\).

Because \(c\) is first everywhere, its final margins are

\(\omega(c,d)=-T=\sigma\), \(\omega(c,e)=0\), and \(\omega(c,S)=\omega(c,g)=T+\eta\).

Therefore \(s(c)=\sigma\).

For each element \(e\), let \(S\in\mathcal C\) be its unique covering set. Candidate \(e\) is unranked on all added ballots, while \(S\) has total ranked mass \(B\). Hence the added margin from \(e\) against \(S\) is \(-B\), giving

\(\omega(e,S)=(-T+B-\eta)-B=\sigma-\eta<\sigma\).

Every set candidate and \(g\) has margin \(-T-\eta=\sigma-\eta\) against \(c\). Candidates \(d\) and \(g\) are unranked on all added ballots, so \(\omega(d,g)=\sigma-\eta\).

Thus every rival has Maximin score strictly below \(\sigma=s(c)\). Candidate \(c\) is the unique winner, independently of the priority order.

Reverse direction

Suppose some added mass vector makes \(c\) the winner. By Lemma \(1\), assume every added ballot ranks \(c\) first.

The final margins of \(c\) are then exactly those computed above, so \(s(c)=\sigma=-T\).

For every set candidate \(S\), let \(z_S\) be the total added mass of ballots on which \(S\) is ranked. Each ballot has only \(\ell-1\) positions below \(c\), whence

\(\sum_{S\in\mathcal S}z_S\le(\ell-1)\alpha=kB\).

Fix an element candidate \(e\). Since \(c\) wins, \(s(e)\le\sigma\). Its margin against \(c\) is \(0>\sigma\), so there must be another candidate \(b\) satisfying \(\omega(e,b)\le\sigma\).

If \(b\) is not a set containing \(e\), then its initial margin against \(e\) is \(0\). Any pairwise margin can change by at most the total added mass \(\alpha\). Therefore

\(\omega(e,b)\ge-\alpha=-2k>-2k-1=\sigma\),

a contradiction. Thus \(b=S\) for some set \(S\ni e\).

Let \(\Delta_x(e,S)\) denote the added margin. On a ballot where \(S\) is unranked, the contribution to \(\Delta_x(e,S)\) is nonnegative. On a ballot where \(S\) is ranked, it is at least \(-1\). Consequently,

\(\Delta_x(e,S)\ge-z_S\).

On the other hand, \(\omega(e,S)\le\sigma\) and \(A(e,S)=\sigma+B-\eta\), so

\(\Delta_x(e,S)\le-B+\eta\).

It follows that \(z_S\ge B-\eta\).

Define \(\mathcal C'=\{S\in\mathcal S:z_S\ge B-\eta\}\). The preceding argument shows that \(\mathcal C'\) covers every element. Moreover,

\((k+1)(B-\eta)=(k+1)B-\dfrac B2=kB+\dfrac B2>kB\).

Since \(\sum_Sz_S\le kB\), this implies \(\lvert\mathcal C'\rvert\le k\). A family of at most \(k\) sets of size \(3\) can cover a \(3k\)-element universe only if it consists of exactly \(k\) pairwise disjoint sets. Therefore \(\mathcal C'\) is an exact cover.

This proves the equivalence and hence \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

5. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

There are \(O(\lvert M\rvert^\ell)\) variables \(x_R\), which is polynomial for fixed \(\ell\).

Nondeterministically guess:

  • a candidate \(d_c\ne c\) that realizes the minimum defining \(s(c)\);
  • for every \(a\ne c\), a candidate \(d_a\ne a\) witnessing that \(a\)’s score does not defeat \(c\).

Feasibility is then expressed by the rational linear constraints

\(\sum_Rx_R=\alpha\), \(x_R\ge0\), and \(\omega(c,d_c)\le\omega(c,b)\) for every \(b\ne c\),

together with

\(\omega(a,d_a)\le\omega(c,d_c)\)

when \(c\) wins a tie against \(a\), and the strict version

\(\omega(a,d_a)<\omega(c,d_c)\)

when \(a\) has higher priority.

These conditions are necessary and sufficient for the guessed witnesses. Feasibility of rational linear inequalities with strict constraints is polynomial-time decidable: introduce a variable \(\varepsilon\), replace every strict inequality by one with slack at least \(\varepsilon\), impose \(0\le\varepsilon\le1\), and test whether the maximum possible \(\varepsilon\) is positive.

Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Conclusion

For every fixed \(\ell\ge2\), deciding whether a mass \(\alpha\) of top-\(\ell\) ballots can make \(c\) the deterministic-priority Maximin winner is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. The hardness holds even though the successful solution in the forward construction makes \(c\) the unique winner.

Verification report

Overall assessment

The reduction is mathematically sound. The normalization lemma, margin-realization construction, X3C equivalence, and NP-membership argument all withstand verification. No central step is false, and the continuous mass-vector problem is established to be NP-hard and, with the membership argument, NP-complete.

There are minor omissions concerning the domain of Lemma 2, bit-complexity bookkeeping, and the treatment of finitely many small X3C instances.

1. Normal-form lemma

The two transformations are valid.

  • If \(c\) is ranked below another candidate, moving \(c\) to first place changes only comparisons involving \(c\). Every affected margin of \(c\) increases, while affected rivals have their margin against \(c\) decreased. Thus no rival’s Maximin score increases.
  • If \(c\) is unranked in \(R=(r_1,\ldots,r_\ell)\), replacing \(R\) by

\(R'=(c,r_1,\ldots,r_{\ell-1})\) has the claimed effects:

  • Against each retained ranked candidate and against \(d=r_\ell\), the margin of \(c\) increases by \(2\lambda\).
  • Against every other formerly unranked candidate, it increases by \(\lambda\).
  • For a rival formerly unranked, its only positive margin change is the increase by \(\lambda\) against \(d\); its margin against \(c\) decreases by \(\lambda\).
  • Retained candidates and \(d\) have no pairwise margin increase exceeding \(\lambda\).

Consequently,
\[ s_{\mathrm{new}}(c)\ge s_{\mathrm{old}}(c)+\lambda \]
and
\[ s_{\mathrm{new}}(a)\le s_{\mathrm{old}}(a)+\lambda \]
for every rival \(a\). Hence \(s(c)-s(a)\) cannot decrease. This preserves both weak score comparisons when \(c\) has priority and strict comparisons when the rival has priority.

Because there are finitely many ballot types, repeated application yields the asserted normal form.

2. Realization of arbitrary margins

For \(|M|\ge\ell\), the construction is correct.

The uniform vector \(U\) has zero margins by reversal symmetry. Swapping adjacent candidates \(a,b\) in
\[ R=(b,a,r_3,\ldots,r_\ell) \]
changes only their mutual comparison, producing margin \(2\) for \(a\) against \(b\). Scaling and summing these nonnegative vectors realizes each prescribed entry independently.

The lemma is overstated as written for \(|M|<\ell\), since no top-\(\ell\) rankings then exist and an arbitrary nonzero matrix cannot be realized. This does not affect the reduction, whose candidate set satisfies \(|M|\ge\ell\).

The proof counts the number of ballot types but does not explicitly bound rational bit lengths. For the actual reduction, all prescribed values have polynomial bit length, and summing polynomially many such rationals preserves polynomial output length. Thus this is an expositional omission, not a substantive obstacle.

3. Forward direction

Assuming an exact cover \(\mathcal C\) of size \(k\), the proposed added mass is well-defined because \(k\ge\ell-1\).

Each selected set occurs in the same proportion
\[ \frac{\ell-1}{k}, \]
so its ranked mass is
\[ \alpha\frac{\ell-1}{k} =2k\frac{\ell-1}{k} =2(\ell-1) =B. \]

Since every added ballot ranks \(c\) first, every margin of \(c\) increases by \(\alpha\). Therefore:
\[ \omega(c,d)=-T,\qquad \omega(c,e)=0,\qquad \omega(c,S)=\omega(c,g)=T+\eta. \]
Thus \(s(c)=-T=\sigma\).

For each element \(e\), its uniquely covering selected set \(S\) is ranked with total mass \(B\), while \(e\) is always unranked. Hence:
\[ \omega(e,S)=(-T+B-\eta)-B=-T-\eta=\sigma-\eta. \]

Each set candidate and \(g\) has margin \(\sigma-\eta\) against \(c\), and \(d\) has unchanged margin \(\sigma-\eta\) against \(g\). Therefore every rival’s score is strictly below \(s(c)\), so \(c\) is the unique winner.

4. Reverse direction

After applying Lemma 1, every added ballot ranks \(c\) first. Hence the same calculation gives:
\[ s(c)=\sigma=-T. \]

Let \(z_S\) be the total added mass of ballots ranking \(S\). Each ballot has only \(\ell-1\) positions below \(c\), so:
\[ \sum_{S\in\mathcal S}z_S \le(\ell-1)\alpha =2k(\ell-1) =kB. \]

For each element \(e\), \(c\)’s victory implies \(s(e)\le\sigma\). Since
\[ \omega(e,c)=0>\sigma, \]
a minimizing candidate for \(e\) cannot be \(c\).

If the witness \(b\) is not a set containing \(e\), then its relevant initial margin is zero. Added mass of total size \(\alpha\) can decrease a pairwise margin by at most \(\alpha\), so:
\[ \omega(e,b)\ge-\alpha=-2k>-2k-1=\sigma. \]
Thus the necessary witness must be some \(S\ni e\).

For such an \(S\), ballots not ranking \(S\) contribute nonnegatively to the added \(e\)-versus-\(S\) margin, while ballots ranking \(S\) contribute at least \(-1\). Therefore:
\[ \Delta_x(e,S)\ge-z_S. \]

The condition \(\omega(e,S)\le\sigma\), combined with
\[ A(e,S)=\sigma+B-\eta, \]
gives:
\[ \Delta_x(e,S)\le-B+\eta. \]
Consequently \(z_S\ge B-\eta\).

Thus
\[ \mathcal C'=\{S:z_S\ge B-\eta\} \]
covers \(X\). Moreover:
\[ (k+1)(B-\eta) =(k+1)B-\frac B2 =kB+\frac B2 >kB. \]
Since \(\sum_S z_S\le kB\), there can be at most \(k\) members of \(\mathcal C'\). At most \(k\) three-element sets can cover \(3k\) distinct elements only when there are exactly \(k\) sets and their sets of elements are pairwise disjoint. Therefore \(\mathcal C'\) is an exact cover.

5. Complexity and NP membership

For fixed \(\ell\), the number of top-\(\ell\) rankings is
\[ \frac{|M|!}{(|M|-\ell)!}=O(|M|^\ell), \]
so the continuous vector \(x\) has polynomially many coordinates.

The guessed witnesses give a correct finite disjunctive description:

  • The constraints for \(d_c\) ensure that \(\omega(c,d_c)=s(c)\).
  • For a rival \(a\) below \(c\) in priority, the existence of \(d_a\) with

\[ \omega(a,d_a)\le s(c) \]
is equivalent to \(s(a)\le s(c)\).

  • For a rival above \(c\) in priority, the strict version is equivalent to \(s(a)<s(c)\).

The strict-inequality transformation is also correct. Since there are finitely many strict inequalities, a feasible point has a positive minimum slack. Conversely, a solution with \(\varepsilon>0\) satisfies all original strict inequalities. Exact rational linear programming can decide whether the optimum is positive in polynomial time.

Minor issues requiring clarification

  • Lemma 2 should explicitly assume \(|M|\ge\ell\).
  • Its polynomial-time claim should mention rational bit lengths, not only the number of ballot types.
  • The statement that instances with \(k<\ell-1\) “can be decided directly” does not fully specify a many-one reduction on those inputs. The write-up should state that such inputs are mapped, according to their directly computed answer, to fixed yes- and no-instances of the target problem.

None of these issues affects the main reduction for sufficiently large X3C instances.
