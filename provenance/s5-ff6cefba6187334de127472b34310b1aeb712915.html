The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an agenda of \(m\) alternatives, a finite support of \(\tau\) complete three-tier ballot types with binary rational masses summing to \(1\), and a rational threshold \(q\), does there exist an ordered partition \(P\) into three nonempty tiers such that \(\sum_{a\succ_P b}\Delta_\mu(a,b)\ge q\), where \(\Delta_\mu(a,b)=\sum_t\mu_t(\mathbf{1}[a\succ_t b]-\mathbf{1}[b\succ_t a])\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) be an agenda of \(m\geq3\) alternatives. Each ballot type \(t\) is a complete weak order represented by an ordered partition \(T_t>M_t>B_t\) of \(A\) into exactly three nonempty tiers; \(a\succ_t b\) means that \(a\) lies in a higher tier than \(b\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) distinct types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible ballot types.
The input also contains a binary rational threshold \(q\in\mathbb{Q}\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Define \(\Delta_\mu(a,b)=\sum_t\mu_t\bigl(\mathbf{1}[a\succ_t b]-\mathbf{1}[b\succ_t a]\bigr)\).
A feasible outcome is an ordered partition \(P=(T>M>B)\) of \(A\) into exactly three nonempty tiers, with score \(S_\mu(P)=\sum_{a\succ_P b}\Delta_\mu(a,b)\). Tied alternatives contribute zero, and each strictly ordered pair contributes once.

Is deciding whether there exists a feasible \(P\) with \(S_\mu(P)\geq q\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathit{NP}\)-hard?

For finite lists of voters casting complete three-tier ballots, computing an optimal three-tier outcome under this score is \(\mathit{NP}\)-hard. The corresponding threshold problem for arbitrary integer-weighted tournaments, max \(3\)-OP, is \(\mathit{NP}\)-complete.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted complete three-tier ballot types as weak orders with exactly three nonempty indifference classes, matching the discrete definition; consequently required at least three alternatives.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length, including ballot descriptions, rational masses, and the threshold.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathit{NP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. Hardness holds even when the support size satisfies \(\tau\leq m(m-1)\).

The proof uses the stated \(\mathit{NP}\)-completeness of the corresponding integer-weighted max \(3\)-OP threshold problem. The key step is an explicit realization of arbitrary integer pairwise weights by a polynomial-size support of valid three-tier ballots.

1. Membership in \(\mathit{NP}\)

Fix a labeling \(A=\{1,\ldots,m\}\). For any ballot or outcome \(R\), define \(\varepsilon_R(a,b)\) to be \(1\) if \(a\succ_R b\), \(-1\) if \(b\succ_R a\), and \(0\) if they are tied.

A certificate specifies the tier of each alternative. Checking that all three tiers are nonempty takes \(O(m)\) time.

For a proposed outcome \(P\), its score can be written as

\(\displaystyle S_\mu(P)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t K_t(P),\qquad K_t(P)=\sum_{a<b}\varepsilon_P(a,b)\varepsilon_t(a,b).\)

Indeed, for each unordered pair, multiplication by \(\varepsilon_P(a,b)\) selects its margin with the correct orientation, or contributes zero when the outcome ties that pair.

Each integer \(K_t(P)\) has absolute value at most \(\binom m2\). Computing all these integers requires \(O(\tau m^2)\) elementary operations.

The comparison with \(q\) is exact: take as common denominator the product of the denominators of all masses and of \(q\). Its bit length is \(O(L)\), since their encoding lengths are included in \(L\). The resulting integer arithmetic uses \(O(L+\log(m+1))\)-bit numbers.

Using elementary integer arithmetic, a sufficient verifier running-time bound is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(\tau m^2\log(m+1)+(\tau+1)(L+\log(m+1))^2\right).\)

Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathit{NP}\).

2. Two ballots isolate one pairwise margin

Let \(a,b\in A\) be distinct, and put \(R=A\setminus\{a,b\}\). Because \(m\geq3\), the set \(R\) is nonempty.

Consider the two ballots

\(\displaystyle U_{ab}=(\{a\}>\{b\}>R),\qquad V_{ab}=(R>\{a\}>\{b\}).\)

Both have exactly three nonempty tiers. Give each ballot one unit of unnormalized mass.

Their combined margin is \(2\) on the ordered pair \((a,b)\) and zero on every other unordered pair. To verify this:

  • Both ballots rank \(a\) above \(b\), giving combined margin \(2\).
  • For each \(r\in R\), the first ballot ranks \(a\) and \(b\) above \(r\), while the second ranks \(r\) above both. These contributions cancel.
  • Every pair within \(R\) is tied in both ballots.

Reversing the roles of \(a\) and \(b\) therefore isolates margin \(-2\) on \((a,b)\).

3. Reduction from integer-weighted max \(3\)-OP

Take an instance of the stated \(\mathit{NP}\)-complete threshold problem. Write its antisymmetric integer weights as \(w_{ab}\) for \(a<b\), with \(w_{ba}=-w_{ab}\). Its outcome score and threshold are

\(\displaystyle H_w(P)=\sum_{a<b}\varepsilon_P(a,b)w_{ab},\qquad Q.\)

We must decide whether some feasible three-tier outcome satisfies \(H_w(P)\geq Q\).

Set \(W=\sum_{a<b}|w_{ab}|\), and first suppose \(W>0\).

For each pair \(a<b\):

  • If \(w_{ab}>0\), include \(U_{ab}\) and \(V_{ab}\), each with unnormalized mass \(w_{ab}\).
  • If \(w_{ab}<0\), include \(U_{ba}\) and \(V_{ba}\), each with unnormalized mass \(-w_{ab}\).
  • If \(w_{ab}=0\), include no ballots for that pair.

The total unnormalized mass is \(2W\). Merge identical ballot types by adding their masses, and divide every resulting mass by \(2W\). This produces a support of distinct types with positive rational masses summing to \(1\), as required.

By the calculation in Section 2, the unnormalized margin on \((a,b)\) is exactly \(2w_{ab}\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \Delta_\mu(a,b)=\frac{w_{ab}}{W}.\)

For every feasible outcome \(P\), it follows that

\(\displaystyle S_\mu(P)=\frac{H_w(P)}{W}.\)

Set the output threshold to \(q=Q/W\). Since \(W>0\), we obtain the exact equivalence

\(\displaystyle S_\mu(P)\geq q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_w(P)\geq Q.\)

The agenda and feasible outcomes are identical in the two instances. Hence the reduction preserves both yes-instances and no-instances.

If \(W=0\), every source score is zero. The source answer is therefore determined by whether \(Q\leq0\). Map it to a fixed yes-instance or no-instance: on three alternatives, a society consisting of the single ballot \(\{a\}>\{b\}>\{c\}\) has maximum score \(3\), so thresholds \(3\) and \(4\) provide these two fixed instances.

4. Encoding size and nonempty tiers

The construction creates at most \(2\binom m2=m(m-1)\) ballot occurrences before merging. Thus its support size satisfies \(\tau\leq m(m-1)\), irrespective of the magnitudes of the source weights.

Let \(B\) denote the source input bit length. The integers \(W\) and all aggregated masses have \(O(B+\log(m+1))\) bits. The output masses and threshold therefore have polynomial-length binary rational encodings. Explicitly listing the ballots gives the sufficient output-size bound

\(\displaystyle L=O\!\left(m^3\log(m+1)+m^2(B+\log(m+1))\right).\)

Construction, merging, and rational arithmetic all take polynomial time in \(m\) and \(B\). No weight is expanded into an explicit list of repeated voters.

If the stated source problem permits *at most* three nonempty tiers, its optimum still equals the optimum with *exactly* three when \(m\geq3\). To prove this, take an outcome with fewer than three tiers. Some tier contains at least two alternatives. Split it into two nonempty sets. The two possible orders of those sets produce score changes \(c\) and \(-c\), because all relations with outside alternatives remain unchanged and every newly ordered internal pair reverses orientation. Choose the order whose change is nonnegative. Repeat until there are three nonempty tiers. Thus the exact-tier requirement does not weaken the reduction.

The reduction proves \(\mathit{NP}\)-hardness, and Section 1 proves membership in \(\mathit{NP}\). Therefore the requested threshold problem is \(\mathit{NP}\)-complete.
