The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given parties \(C\), coalition \(A\), rational threshold \(t\), target \(\phi\), per-capita budget \(B\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) ranking types with linear coalition-shift costs, decide whether there are nonnegative transfers \(x_{r,r'}\) with \(\sum_{r'}x_{r,r'}=\mu_r\), each from \(r\) to a ranking \(r'\) reachable under the paper’s coalition-shift rule, such that \(\sum_{r,r'}\gamma(r,r')x_{r,r'}\le B\) and \(A\)’s seat share is at least \(\phi\). Compute plurality scores from the resulting distribution \(\mu'\), activate each party whose score is at least \(t\), and allocate seats among active parties in proportion to their scores.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given parties \(C\) with \(m=|C|\geq2\), a nonempty coalition \(A\subseteq C\), and binary rationals \(0<t\leq1\), \(0\leq\phi\leq1\), and \(B\geq0\), consider the following continuous coalition-bribery problem.
A society is given by its support: the list \(R\) of the \(\tau\) strict ranking types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_r\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_{r\in R}\mu_r=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Each supported type also has an input binary-rational cost slope \(\lambda_r\geq0\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Write \(p\succ_r q\) when \(r\) ranks \(p\) above \(q\). A destination ranking \(r'\) is allowed exactly when every reversed pair \(p\succ_r q\), \(q\succ_{r'}p\) has \(q\in A\); swaps within \(A\) are permitted. Let \(K(r,r')=|\{(p,q):p\succ_r q, q\succ_{r'}p\}|\) and \(\gamma(r,r')=\lambda_r K(r,r')\), including zero cost for leaving a ranking unchanged.
Transfers \(x_{r,r'}\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}\) may split each source mass among any allowed destination rankings, including rankings outside the input support, and must satisfy \(\sum_{r'}x_{r,r'}=\mu_r\) and \(\sum_{r,r'}\gamma(r,r')x_{r,r'}\leq B\). Set \(\mu'_{r'}=\sum_r x_{r,r'}\).
Use plurality scores \(s_c\) from \(\mu'\). The active set is \(H=\{c\in C:s_c\geq t\}\), including equality; seats are divisible and allocated proportionally among active parties. A successful outcome requires \(D=\sum_{c\in H}s_c>0\) and \(\sum_{c\in A\cap H}s_c/D\geq\phi\).

Is deciding whether such transfers exist solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

The finite-voter version with a positive electoral threshold is NP-hard even with multiplicative shift costs; with threshold \(t=0\), it is polynomial-time solvable. [Discrete results](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14707v1)

Result

The decision problem is NP-complete. The hardness proof allows arbitrary real-valued transfers and works even when all numerical inputs are dyadic rationals.

The [discrete paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14707v1) also uses an exact-cover reduction. The proof below includes a quantitative bound that prevents fractional transfers from circumventing the construction.

1. Reducing ranking transfers to plurality transfers

For a supported ranking \(r\), let \(u_r\) be its first-ranked party, and let \(\operatorname{pos}_r(c)\) denote the position of \(c\), starting at one.

Lemma. The possible first-ranked parties of an allowed destination are precisely \(A\cup\{u_r\}\). For every \(c\in A\), the minimum cost of making \(c\) first is
\(\lambda_r(\operatorname{pos}_r(c)-1)\). Keeping \(u_r\) first has minimum cost zero.

Proof. Suppose a destination ranks \(c\ne u_r\) first. The pair \(u_r\succ_r c\) is then reversed, so the allowedness condition requires \(c\in A\).

If \(c\in A\) becomes first, every party originally preceding \(c\) forms a reversed pair with \(c\). Thus there are at least \(\operatorname{pos}_r(c)-1\) reversals. Moving \(c\) to the front and preserving the relative order of all other parties achieves exactly these reversals. Every reversed pair has \(c\) as its promoted party, so this destination is allowed. Leaving \(r\) unchanged costs zero. This proves the lemma.

Consequently, we may use variables \(y_{r,c}\) for the mass from source \(r\) whose destination has first-ranked party \(c\), where \(c\in A\cup\{u_r\}\). Write \(d_{r,c}\) for the minimum cost given by the lemma. The transfer constraints become
\(\sum_c y_{r,c}=\mu_r\), \(y_{r,c}\geq0\), and
\(\sum_{r,c}d_{r,c}y_{r,c}\leq B\), with scores
\(s_c=\sum_r y_{r,c}\).

This formulation is equivalent to the original problem: aggregating destination rankings by their first party and replacing them by minimum-cost destinations preserves scores and cannot increase cost. Conversely, every such assignment is realizable using those destinations.

2. Reduction from Exact Cover by 3-Sets

We use the standard NP-complete problem Exact Cover by 3-Sets. Its input is a universe \(U=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), where \(n\geq3\) is divisible by three, and a nonempty family \(S_1,\ldots,S_q\) of three-element subsets. The question is whether some subfamily partitions \(U\).

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\geq4n\), and set \(M=8nq\).

Parties and supported rankings

Create:

  • Coalition parties \(A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_q\}\), one for each set.
  • Outside parties \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\), one for each element.
  • An outside party \(h\).
  • A set \(F\) of \(M\) additional outside parties.

For each element \(i\), create one supported ranking \(r_i\), with mass \(1/N\) and slope one. Its blocks, from first to last, are:

  • \(e_i\);
  • all \(a_j\) such that \(i\in S_j\);
  • all parties in \(F\);
  • every remaining party.

Fix any order within each block.

Create one additional supported ranking \(r_h\), whose first party is \(h\), with mass \((N-n)/N\) and slope \(M\). Complete this ranking arbitrarily.

These are distinct strict rankings, and their masses sum to one. Set
\(t=3/N\), \(\phi=n/N\), and \(B=nq/N\).

The construction has support size \(\tau=n+1\) and
\(m=n+q+1+M\) parties. Since \(M=8nq\) and \(N<8n\), listing every ranking takes polynomial space and time. All numerical inputs are dyadic rationals.

Cost properties

The lemma gives three useful bounds.

First, if \(i\in S_j\), then \(a_j\) appears in the block immediately following \(e_i\), so
\(d_{r_i,a_j}\leq q\).

Second, if \(i\notin S_j\), then all \(M\) parties in \(F\) precede \(a_j\). Hence
\(d_{r_i,a_j}\geq M\).

Third, moving mass from \(r_h\) to any coalition party costs at least \(M\) per unit mass: its slope is \(M\), and at least one pair must be reversed.

3. An exact cover gives a successful outcome

Suppose \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}\) indexes an exact cover.

For each element \(i\), transfer its entire mass \(1/N\) to the coalition party corresponding to the unique selected set containing \(i\). Leave the mass of \(r_h\) unchanged.

Each selected coalition party receives exactly \(3/N=t\), and is therefore active, including under the stipulated equality convention. Every unselected coalition party has score zero. All element parties and all parties in \(F\) have score zero.

Party \(h\) has score \((N-n)/N\) and is active because \(N-n\geq3n\geq3\). Thus all mass is assigned to active parties, and the coalition’s seat share is exactly \(n/N=\phi\).

The total cost is at most \(nq/N=B\). The outcome is successful.

4. A successful outcome gives an exact cover

Now suppose arbitrary real-valued transfers produce a successful outcome within budget.

For this section, measure mass in units of \(1/N\). Each element source then has mass one, the source \(r_h\) has mass \(N-n\), the activation threshold is three, and the budget is \(nq\).

Let \(J\) index the active coalition parties, and let \(S\) be their total score in these units.

Define two nonnegative real quantities:

  • \(a\): the total mass from \(r_h\) assigned to coalition parties, active or inactive.
  • \(b\): the total mass from element sources assigned to active coalition parties along nonincident routes—that is, from source \(i\) to \(a_j\) with \(j\in J\) and \(i\notin S_j\).

The two categories involve disjoint source masses. Each costs at least \(M\) per unit, so the budget implies
\(M(a+b)\leq nq\). Therefore,
\(a+b\leq1/8\), and in particular \(a\leq1/8\).

The outside party remains active

By the lemma, no source can give plurality mass to an outside party other than its own original first party. Thus each \(e_i\) has score at most one, and every party in \(F\) has score zero. These parties are inactive.

Party \(h\) has score exactly \(N-n-a\), which satisfies
\(N-n-a\geq3n-1/8>3\).
It remains active. Consequently, the active outside score is exactly \(N-n-a\).

Success therefore requires
\(S/(S+N-n-a)\geq n/N\).
The denominator is positive, and multiplying through gives
\((N-n)S\geq n(N-n-a)\).

Put \(\alpha=n/(N-n)\). Since \(N\geq4n\), we have \(0<\alpha\leq1/3\). The success condition is thus
\(S\geq n-\alpha a\).

At most \(n/3\) coalition parties can be active

The total score of all coalition parties is at most \(n+a\): at most \(n\) units come from element sources, and exactly \(a\) units come from \(r_h\).

Every active coalition party has score at least three. Hence
\(3|J|\leq n+a\leq n+1/8<n+3\).

Because \(n\) is divisible by three and \(|J|\) is an integer, it follows that
\(|J|\leq n/3\).

The selected sets cover every element

Let \(V=\bigcup_{j\in J}S_j\). Suppose, for contradiction, that \(V\ne U\). Then \(|V|\leq n-1\).

Element sources indexed by \(V\) contribute at most \(|V|\) units to active coalition parties. Every contribution from an element outside \(V\) to an active coalition party uses a nonincident route, and is therefore counted in \(b\). The source \(r_h\) contributes at most \(a\).

It follows that
\(S\leq|V|+b+a\leq n-1+b+a\).

Combining this with \(S\geq n-\alpha a\) yields
\(1\leq(1+\alpha)a+b\).
But
\((1+\alpha)a+b\leq(1+\alpha)(a+b)\leq(4/3)(1/8)=1/6\),
a contradiction. Therefore \(V=U\).

Finally, the sets indexed by \(J\) cover \(n\) elements, each has cardinality three, and \(|J|\leq n/3\). Thus
\(n=|V|\leq3|J|\leq n\).
Equality holds throughout, forcing \(|J|=n/3\) and forbidding any overlap between the selected sets. They form an exact cover.

This proves the converse for arbitrary real transfers, and establishes NP-hardness.

5. Membership in NP

The reduced plurality formulation has at most \(\tau(m+1)\) variables and rational coefficients of polynomial bit length.

Guess a nonempty active set \(H\subseteq C\). Its required score constraints are
\(s_c\geq t\) for \(c\in H\), and \(s_c<t\) for \(c\notin H\).
The success constraint is the linear inequality
\(\sum_{c\in A\cap H}s_c\geq\phi\sum_{c\in H}s_c\).
Because \(H\ne\varnothing\) and \(t>0\), these conditions ensure a positive denominator.

To handle the strict inequalities, introduce a variable \(\delta\) with \(0\leq\delta\leq1\), and replace each inactive constraint by
\(s_c\leq t-\delta\).

An outcome with active set exactly \(H\) exists if and only if this rational system has a feasible point with \(\delta>0\). Indeed, finitely many strict inactive inequalities have a common positive slack. If there are no inactive parties, choose \(\delta=1\).

The resulting polytope is bounded: each transfer variable lies between zero and its source mass, and \(\delta\in[0,1]\). If a positive-\(\delta\) point exists, maximizing \(\delta\) over this polytope gives a vertex with positive \(\delta\).

Such a vertex has polynomial-size rational coordinates. To justify this, a vertex is determined by a full-rank subsystem of active rational constraints. Clearing denominators gives integer coefficients of polynomial bit length; Cramer’s rule and determinant bounds then give polynomial bit length for every coordinate.

Thus a certificate consists of \(H\), rational transfer variables, and a positive rational \(\delta\), all of polynomial size. Every constraint can be verified by exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time. The problem belongs to NP.

Together with the reduction, this proves NP-completeness.
