Theorem 2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a non-bipartite weighted graph \(H=(R,F;w)\), consider an atomless unit mass of identical modules indexed by \(q\in[0,1]\), each containing one player for every role \(v\in R\), with interactions only along the copy of \(F\) inside that module. For a measurable coalition \(A\subseteq[0,1]\times R\), let \(S_A(q)=\{v:(q,v)\in A\}\), and let \(\kappa_H(S)\) be the paper’s minimum integral edge-cover cost for \(S\). Define \(c(A)=\frac{1}{|R|}\int_0^1\kappa_H(S_A(q))\,dq\) and \(a(A)=\frac{1}{|R|}\int_0^1\sum_{v\in S_A(q)}a_v\,dq\). Can one efficiently compute nonnegative role rates \(a_v\) satisfying \(a(A)\le c(A)\) for every measurable \(A\) and \(a([0,1]\times R)\ge\frac{\ell(H)}{1+\ell(H)}c([0,1]\times R)\), and is this factor sharp as a guarantee over edge costs?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(H=(R,F;w)\) be a finite simple undirected non-bipartite graph without isolated vertices, with nonnegative binary-rational edge costs; set \(m=|R|\), and let \(\ell(H)\) be its shortest odd-cycle length.
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) denotes support size, not the number of possible types. Here types are roles \(v\in R\), each of mass \(1/m\), so \(\tau=m\); masses sum to one. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The society is realized as \(\Omega=[0,1]\times R\), with Lebesgue measure divided by \(m\) on each role. Each module \(q\) contains all roles, with interactions only along its copy of \(F\).
For \(S\subseteq R\), define \(\kappa_H(S)=\min\{\sum_{e\in K}w_e:K\subseteq F,\ \forall v\in S\ \exists e\in K\text{ incident to }v\}\), with \(\kappa_H(\varnothing)=0\). Edges may have an endpoint outside \(S\); only vertices in \(S\) must be covered.
For a measurable coalition \(A\subseteq\Omega\), put \(S_A(q)=\{v:(q,v)\in A\}\), \(c(A)=m^{-1}\int_0^1\kappa_H(S_A(q))\,dq\), and \(a(A)=m^{-1}\int_0^1\sum_{v\in S_A(q)}a_v\,dq\), where role rates \(a_v\geq0\) are constant across modules.

Can rates satisfying \(a(A)\leq c(A)\) for every measurable \(A\) and \(a(\Omega)\geq\frac{\ell(H)}{1+\ell(H)}c(\Omega)\) always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,|F|,L\)?
For each fixed \(H\), is \(\frac{\ell(H)}{1+\ell(H)}\) the largest factor guaranteed uniformly over nonnegative rational edge costs with \(c(\Omega)>0\)?

For finite edge-cover games, polynomial-time computation at this factor is established, and sharpness over edge costs is asserted. [Discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.11222v1)
No result addressing exactly this atomless module model was identified in the literature search.

  • Specified a simple undirected graph without isolated vertices so integral covers exist for every coalition.
  • Used nonnegative binary-rational edge costs, allowing zero costs, to make polynomial-time computation precise.
  • Resolved the conflicting induced-subgraph wording by requiring coverage only of coalition vertices and allowing edges to outside endpoints, following the explicit minimum-edge-set definition.
  • Treated roles as society types, each with mass one divided by the number of roles; retained the module structure as additional input semantics.
  • Specified Lebesgue measurability and constant nonnegative role rates, retaining the stated normalization.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The computational answer is yes. The sharpness answer for every fixed graph is no. In fact, the exact factor depends on which odd cycles can coexist with an edge cover of the remaining vertices.

Define \(g(H)\) to be the minimum length of an odd cycle \(C\) such that the induced graph \(H[R\setminus V(C)]\) has no isolated vertices. The empty graph is allowed here. Set \(g(H)=\infty\) if no such cycle exists.

The largest factor guaranteed uniformly over the stated edge costs is
\[ \beta(H)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{g(H)}{g(H)+1},&g(H)<\infty,\\ 1,&g(H)=\infty. \end{cases} \]
Below, this formula and the polynomial-time guarantee are proved directly.

1. Exact reduction of the module model. For a rate vector \(a\geq 0\), write \(s_a(S)=\sum_{v\in S}a_v\). Then the following three conditions are equivalent:

  • \(a(A)\leq c(A)\) for every measurable coalition \(A\).
  • \(s_a(S)\leq\kappa_H(S)\) for every \(S\subseteq R\).
  • \(a_u+a_v\leq w_{uv}\) for every \(uv\in F\).

For the implication from the first condition to the second, take \(A=[0,1]\times S\). The resulting inequality is \(s_a(S)/m\leq\kappa_H(S)/m\).

Conversely, the second condition applies to \(S_A(q)\) in each module. Because there are finitely many roles, both integrands are measurable finite-valued functions. Integrating the pointwise inequality proves the first condition.

The second condition implies the third because the single edge \(uv\) covers \(\{u,v\}\), so
\(a_u+a_v\leq\kappa_H(\{u,v\})\leq w_{uv}\).

Finally, suppose the third condition holds, and let \(K\subseteq F\) cover \(S\). Denote by \(d_K(v)\) the number of selected edges incident to \(v\). Nonnegativity of the rates and \(d_K(v)\geq1\) for \(v\in S\) give
\[ s_a(S)\leq\sum_{v\in R}d_K(v)a_v =\sum_{uv\in K}(a_u+a_v) \leq\sum_{e\in K}w_e. \]
Minimizing over \(K\) proves the second condition.

Thus all measurable-coalition constraints have the finite description
\[ D_w=\{a\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{R}:a_u+a_v\leq w_{uv}\text{ for every }uv\in F\}. \]

Let \(I_w=\kappa_H(R)\), and define the fractional edge-cover optimum
\[ \varphi_w=\min\left\{\sum_{e\in F}w_ex_e: x\geq0,\ \sum_{e\ni v}x_e\geq1\text{ for every }v\in R\right\}. \]
Its linear-programming dual is exactly
\[ \max\left\{\sum_{v\in R}a_v:a\in D_w\right\}. \]
The primal is feasible because there are no isolated vertices, and its objective is bounded below by zero. The dual is feasible at zero and bounded: for any edge \(uv\), nonnegativity implies \(a_v\leq w_{uv}\). Strong duality therefore applies.

Consequently, the maximum recoverable total in the module model is \(\varphi_w/m\), whereas \(c(\Omega)=I_w/m\).

2. Structure of fractional extreme points. Let
\[ P=\left\{x\geq0:\sum_{e\ni v}x_e\geq1\text{ for every }v\in R\right\}. \]
Every extreme point of \(P\) has positive support consisting of vertex-disjoint stars with edge values \(1\), and vertex-disjoint odd cycles with edge values \(1/2\).

Here is a proof. Fix an extreme point \(x\), let \(E^+=\{e:x_e>0\}\), and let \(T\) be the set of vertices whose covering constraints are tight.

The columns of the incidence matrix restricted to rows \(T\) and columns \(E^+\) must be linearly independent. Otherwise there is a nonzero vector \(d\), supported on \(E^+\), whose incidence sums vanish on \(T\). For sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), both \(x+\varepsilon d\) and \(x-\varepsilon d\) preserve positivity on the support and all slack inequalities. They would be distinct feasible points with midpoint \(x\), contradicting extremality.

Consider a connected support component with \(n\) vertices, \(p\) edges, and \(t\) tight vertices. Independence gives \(p\leq t\leq n\); connectivity gives \(p\geq n-1\). Hence the component is a tree or is unicyclic.

For a tree, at most one vertex is slack. A slack leaf would have its unique edge value greater than \(1\), making its neighbor slack too. Thus every leaf is tight and its edge value is \(1\). A tree of diameter at least three has two distinct vertices adjacent to terminal leaves and to another support edge. Both are slack, which is impossible. The tree is therefore a star, with every edge value \(1\).

For a unicyclic component, \(p=n\), so every vertex is tight. It cannot have a leaf: that leaf’s edge would have value \(1\), making its neighbor slack. A connected unicyclic graph without leaves is a cycle. An even cycle admits a nonzero alternating perturbation preserving every incidence sum, contradicting independence. Thus the cycle is odd. Its equations \(x_{e_{i-1}}+x_{e_i}=1\) then force every edge value to be \(1/2\).

Every vertex belongs to a support component because its covering constraint requires positive incident support. This completes the structural proof.

An optimal extreme point exists even when some costs vanish. Indeed, capping each coordinate of a feasible solution at \(1\) preserves feasibility and cannot increase its nonnegative objective, so the minimum is attained on a compact set. The polyhedron \(P\) contains no line, since it lies in the nonnegative orthant. The standard extreme-point theorem for linear programming therefore supplies an optimal extreme point.

3. Rounding bound and polynomial-time computation. Take an optimal extreme point \(x\). Retain all its star edges. Consider one of its odd cycles, of length \(t=2k+1\).

There are \(t\) cyclic rotations of an edge cover consisting of \(k+1\) alternating cycle edges. Each cycle edge occurs in exactly \(k+1\) of these covers. Their average cost is therefore
\[ \frac{k+1}{t}\sum_{e\in C}w_e =\left(1+\frac1t\right)\frac12\sum_{e\in C}w_e. \]
At least one cover has cost no greater than this average. Choose such a cover on every cycle. Together with the star edges, these choices cover all vertices.

Write \(B\) for the cost of the star edges and \(Q_C=\frac12\sum_{e\in C}w_e\) for each fractional cycle’s contribution. Since every odd cycle has length at least \(\ell(H)\),
\[ I_w\leq B+\sum_C\left(1+\frac1{|C|}\right)Q_C \leq\left(1+\frac1{\ell(H)}\right) \left(B+\sum_CQ_C\right) =\left(1+\frac1{\ell(H)}\right)\varphi_w. \]

Now compute an optimal solution \(a^*\) of the dual LP. By Section 1, it satisfies every measurable-coalition inequality, and
\[ a^*(\Omega)=\frac{\varphi_w}{m} \geq\frac{\ell(H)}{1+\ell(H)}\frac{I_w}{m} =\frac{\ell(H)}{1+\ell(H)}c(\Omega). \]

This LP has \(m\) variables, \(|F|\) edge inequalities, and \(m\) nonnegativity inequalities. Its rational encoding length is polynomial in the input length \(L\). Polynomial-time rational linear programming produces an exact optimal rational vector with polynomial output bit length. Thus the requested computation is polynomial in \(m,\tau,|F|,L\), with \(\tau=m\). Computing \(\ell(H)\) is unnecessary for this algorithm.

The argument also covers \(c(\Omega)=0\).

4. Exact factor for a fixed graph. Every fractional support cycle \(C\) in Section 2 satisfies the defining condition for \(g(H)\): after deleting \(V(C)\), the remaining support components still cover every remaining vertex using edges entirely within the remaining vertex set. Hence \(H[R\setminus V(C)]\) has no isolated vertices.

If \(g(H)<\infty\), the rounding calculation consequently strengthens to
\[ I_w\leq\left(1+\frac1{g(H)}\right)\varphi_w. \]
If \(g(H)=\infty\), no extreme point can contain a fractional cycle. An optimal extreme point is then integral, so \(I_w=\varphi_w\). These statements prove the claimed factors are always achievable.

For sharpness when \(g(H)<\infty\), choose an admissible odd cycle \(C\) of length \(g=g(H)\). Assign costs as follows:

  • Cycle edges have cost \(1\).
  • Edges with both endpoints outside \(V(C)\) have cost \(0\).
  • Every other edge has cost \(g+1\).

These are nonnegative integer costs, hence valid binary-rational inputs.

The complement of \(V(C)\) has an edge cover of cost zero. Combining it with a cycle edge cover gives total cost \((g+1)/2\). Since this is smaller than \(g+1\), an optimal integral cover uses no edge of cost \(g+1\). It must therefore cover the cycle vertices using cycle edges. Each such edge covers at most two cycle vertices, so
\(I_w=(g+1)/2\).

Assigning value \(1/2\) to every cycle edge and selecting a zero-cost edge cover outside the cycle gives \(\varphi_w\leq g/2\). Conversely, the dual vector assigning rate \(1/2\) on \(V(C)\) and zero elsewhere is feasible: cycle-edge inequalities hold with equality, zero-cost inequalities have both rates zero, and every remaining inequality has right-hand side \(g+1\). Thus \(\varphi_w\geq g/2\).

Therefore \(\varphi_w/I_w=g/(g+1)\), proving sharpness. When \(g(H)=\infty\), factor \(1\) is achievable and the grand-coalition inequality prohibits any larger factor when \(c(\Omega)>0\).

In particular, the proposed factor is sharp for a fixed graph precisely when \(g(H)=\ell(H)\).

5. Explicit counterexample to universal fixed-graph sharpness. Take a triangle on vertices \(x,y,z\), and attach one pendant vertex \(p\) to \(x\). This graph is simple, non-bipartite, and has no isolated vertices. Its only odd cycle is the triangle, whose deletion leaves \(p\) isolated. Hence \(\ell(H)=3\), but \(g(H)=\infty\), and its exact uniform factor is \(1\).

Full recovery can also be checked directly for arbitrary costs. The pendant edge \(xp\) is mandatory, and
\(I_w=w_{xp}+\min\{w_{yz},w_{xy}+w_{xz}\}\).
Put \(t=\min\{w_{yz},w_{xy}+w_{xz}\}\), and choose
\[ a_x=0,\qquad a_p=w_{xp},\qquad a_y=\min\{w_{xy},t\},\qquad a_z=t-a_y. \]
Then \(a_z\geq0\), \(a_z\leq w_{xz}\), and \(a_y+a_z=t\leq w_{yz}\). All edge inequalities hold, and the total rate is exactly \(I_w\).

This also exposes the issue with the cited sharpness argument. Equation (10) assigns cost \(1\) to a shortest odd cycle and zero elsewhere, but zero-cost edges outside that cycle can cover its vertices. On this example, those costs give \(I_w=\varphi_w=1\), rather than the claimed cycle-only values. Thus that argument does not establish sharpness for every fixed graph.
