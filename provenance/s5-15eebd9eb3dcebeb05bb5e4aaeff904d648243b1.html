The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(R\), rational masses \(\mu_r>0\) summing to \(1\), initial product sets \(P_r\), product-independent thresholds \(\theta_r\), and rational influence shares \(\alpha_{s,r}\geq 0\) with \(\sum_s\alpha_{s,r}\leq 1\), consider states \(x_{r,q}\) recording the mass of type \(r\) that has adopted product \(q\). An unadopted portion of type \(r\) may adopt \(q\in P_r\) when \(\sum_s\alpha_{s,r}x_{s,q}/\mu_s\geq\theta_r\); types with no incoming influence may adopt as in the paper. Each adoption is irreversible, and any permitted portion of a type may adopt. Among states reachable by such adoption schedules in which no further adoption is possible, what is the minimum of \(\sum_r x_{r,q^\star}\) for a designated target product \(q^\star\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(R\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_r\), where \(\sum_r\mu_r=1\). Here \(\tau=|R|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq3\) products \(Q\), nonempty initial sets \(P_r\subseteq Q\), and a designated product \(q^\star\in Q\).
Each type has a binary-rational, product-independent threshold \(\theta_r\in(0,1]\). Binary-rational influence shares satisfy \(\alpha_{s,r}\geq0\), \(\alpha_{r,r}=0\), and \(\sum_s\alpha_{s,r}\leq1\); write \(N(r)=\{s:\alpha_{s,r}>0\}\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
A state has \(x_{r,q}\geq0\), with \(x_{r,q}=0\) outside \(P_r\) and \(\sum_qx_{r,q}\leq\mu_r\). Initially, if \(P_r=\{q\}\), its entire mass has already adopted \(q\); all other types are wholly unadopted.
A move assigns any real amount \(0<\delta\leq\mu_r-\sum_qx_{r,q}\) to one product \(q\in P_r\), provided \(N(r)=\varnothing\) or \(\sum_s\alpha_{s,r}x_{s,q}/\mu_s\geq\theta_r\) before the move. Adoption is irreversible; different portions of a type may adopt different products, and equality permits adoption without a priority rule.
Reachability means reachability by finitely many moves. A reachable state is terminal if no move is possible. The objective is \(\sum_rx_{r,q^\star}\); let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be its infimum over reachable terminal states, agreeing with the minimum whenever attained. Use multiplicative approximation, with value zero required when \(\mathrm{OPT}=0\).

Does this minimum-spread problem admit a polynomial-time multiplicative approximation with ratio \(o(\tau)\), or is achieving such a ratio NP-hard?

For discrete networks with \(n\) nodes, minimum spread is NP-hard to approximate within \(o(n)\), already with three products and product-independent thresholds; with two products it is computable in \(O(n^2)\) arithmetic operations. [Apt–Markakis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.5636v4)
No result settling this precise divisible-mass approximation problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified at least three products, nonempty initial product sets, and rational thresholds strictly greater than zero and at most one, following the discrete definition and anchor.
  • Singleton initial product sets represent fully adopted types; other types initially have no adopted mass.
  • Excluded influence from a type to itself, following the discrete graph's exclusion of self-loops.
  • Since the graded input supplies shares rather than an edge set, defined incoming neighbours by strictly positive shares; an all-zero incoming column permits unconditional adoption.
  • Made mass conservation and mutually exclusive individual adoption explicit while allowing portions of one type to choose different products.
  • Specified finite schedules of arbitrary real positive adoption amounts, with eligibility evaluated before each move and no priority among eligible products.
  • Used the infimum as the optimal value when a minimum is not attained, without adding unreachable limit states to the feasible set.
  • Transferred the anchor's linear approximation scale from the number of discrete nodes to support size, and specified the multiplicative convention at zero optimum.

Result

Achieving a polynomial-time multiplicative approximation ratio \(o(\tau)\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. This holds already with three products, equal dyadic masses, product-independent dyadic thresholds, and an acyclic influence graph.

The proof below handles arbitrary real move amounts. Both optima in the reduction are attained, so the distinction between an infimum and a minimum causes no difficulty.

1. Reduction parameters

We reduce from binary-encoded PARTITION: given positive integers \(b_1,\ldots,b_n\), decide whether some \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i\in S}b_i=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n b_i.\)

This is the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem used in the discrete reductions of [Apt–Markakis, Section 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.5636v4). The guards introduced below enforce the needed choices in the divisible model.

Let \(T=\sum_i b_i\), and let \(D\) be the least power of two satisfying \(D\geq T\).

Use products \(Q=\{A,B,C\}\), with designated product \(q^\star=C\). The types are:

  • One seed \(z\).
  • Sources \(s_1,\ldots,s_n\).
  • Two aggregators \(a,b\).
  • Guards \(g_1,\ldots,g_n,c,d\).
  • One collector \(e\).
  • A chain \(w_1,\ldots,w_M\).

There are \(t_0=2n+6\) types before adding the chain. Let \(\tau\) be the least power of two satisfying \(\tau\geq2t_0\), and set \(M=\tau-t_0\). Thus the support size is exactly \(\tau\), and

\(\displaystyle M\geq\frac{\tau}{2},\qquad 2t_0\leq\tau<4t_0.\)

Give every type mass \(\mu_r=1/\tau\). These masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one.

Write \(y_{r,q}=x_{r,q}/\mu_r\) for normalized adoption. Then \(0\leq y_{r,q}\leq1\), and normalized adoption is nondecreasing throughout every move sequence.

2. Products, thresholds, and influence shares

All unlisted influence shares are zero.

Seed and sources. Set \(P_z=\{C\}\), so \(y_{z,C}=1\) initially. Each source has \(P_{s_i}=\{A,B\}\). The seed and sources have no incoming influence; give them threshold \(1/2\).

Aggregators. Set \(P_a=P_b=\{A,B\}\), and, for every \(i\), set

\(\displaystyle \alpha_{s_i,a}=\alpha_{s_i,b}=\frac{b_i}{D},\qquad \theta_a=\theta_b=\frac{T}{2D}.\)

Source guards. For each \(i\), set

\(\displaystyle P_{g_i}=\{A,B,C\},\qquad \alpha_{s_i,g_i}=\alpha_{z,g_i}=\frac12,\qquad \theta_{g_i}=\frac12.\)

Aggregator guards. Set

\(\displaystyle P_c=\{A,C\},\qquad \alpha_{a,c}=\alpha_{z,c}=\frac12,\qquad \theta_c=\frac12,\)

and

\(\displaystyle P_d=\{B,C\},\qquad \alpha_{b,d}=\alpha_{z,d}=\frac12,\qquad \theta_d=\frac12.\)

Collector. Let \(G=\{g_1,\ldots,g_n,c,d\}\), so \(|G|=k=n+2\). Let \(K\) be the least power of two satisfying \(K\geq k\), and put \(\gamma=1/(2K)\). Set

\(\displaystyle P_e=\{A,C\},\qquad \alpha_{z,e}=\frac12,\qquad \alpha_{g,e}=\gamma\ \text{for every }g\in G,\qquad \theta_e=\frac12+\gamma.\)

Chain. Every \(w_j\) has \(P_{w_j}=\{A,C\}\) and threshold \(1\). Set \(\alpha_{e,w_1}=1\), and \(\alpha_{w_{j-1},w_j}=1\) for \(j>1\).

All thresholds are positive, at most one, and product-independent. Incoming shares sum to \(T/D\leq1\) at each aggregator, to one at each guard and chain type, and to \(1/2+k\gamma\leq1\) at the collector. There are no self-loops, and the graph is acyclic.

Only \(z\) has a singleton product set. Consequently, the stipulated initial state consists precisely of its full adoption of \(C\).

3. The source guards detect every genuine split

Consider any reachable terminal state.

Every source is fully adopted: it has no incoming neighbors, so any positive remaining mass would permit another move. Hence

\(\displaystyle y_{s_i,A}+y_{s_i,B}=1.\)

Suppose a source genuinely splits, meaning \(0<y_{s_i,A}<1\) and \(0<y_{s_i,B}<1\) in the terminal state. At every earlier state, both fractions were also strictly below one, by irreversibility.

The support for \(A\) at \(g_i\) is \(y_{s_i,A}/2\); the support for \(B\) is \(y_{s_i,B}/2\). Both therefore remain strictly below \(\theta_{g_i}=1/2\) throughout the sequence. Thus \(g_i\) never adopts \(A\) or \(B\).

Conversely, the seed supplies support \(1/2\) for \(C\) from the beginning. Since equality permits adoption, terminality forces

\(\displaystyle y_{g_i,C}=1.\)

Therefore, any source split forces an entire guard onto \(C\), irrespective of how small one of the portions is.

4. A fully adopted guard forces the collector and chain onto \(C\)

At every state, support for \(A\) at \(e\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{g\in G}\gamma y_{g,A}\leq k\gamma\leq\frac12<\theta_e.\)

Thus \(e\) can never adopt \(A\).

If some guard has \(y_{g,C}=1\) in a terminal state, then support for \(C\) at \(e\) is at least

\(\displaystyle \frac12+\gamma=\theta_e.\)

Terminality forces \(e\) to be fully adopted; since it never adopts \(A\), this means \(y_{e,C}=1\).

No chain type can ever adopt \(A\). Indeed, \(w_1\) would require \(y_{e,A}=1\), which is impossible; inductively, \(w_j\) would require \(y_{w_{j-1},A}=1\). Once the collector is fully on \(C\), terminality therefore forces \(w_1\) fully onto \(C\), and then successively forces every remaining chain type onto \(C\).

It follows that any terminal state with a full \(C\)-guard has spread at least

\(\displaystyle \frac{1+1+1+M}{\tau}=\frac{M+3}{\tau}.\)

The counted types are the seed, one guard, the collector, and the chain.

5. Yes-instances have optimum \(1/\tau\)

Suppose \(S\) solves PARTITION. Use the following finite sequence of whole-mass moves:

  • Sources in \(S\) adopt \(A\); all other sources adopt \(B\).
  • Aggregator \(a\) adopts \(A\), and aggregator \(b\) adopts \(B\).
  • Each \(g_i\) adopts its source’s product.
  • Guard \(c\) adopts \(A\), and guard \(d\) adopts \(B\).

The aggregator moves are legal because their respective supports equal \(T/(2D)\), exactly their threshold. Each subsequent guard move has support \(1/2\), also exactly its threshold.

No guard adopts \(C\). Hence the collector has support \(1/2<\theta_e\) for \(C\), and support at most \(1/2<\theta_e\) for \(A\). It remains wholly unadopted. The chain also remains wholly unadopted because its first predecessor has adopted nothing.

All other types are full, so this state is terminal. Its \(C\)-spread is \(1/\tau\). The seed contributes that much in every reachable state, proving

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{YES}}=\frac1\tau.\)

This minimum is attained by the explicit finite sequence above.

6. No-instances have optimum \((M+3)/\tau\)

Suppose PARTITION has no solution, and consider an arbitrary reachable terminal state.

If any source splits, Section 3 supplies a full \(C\)-guard, and Section 4 gives spread at least \((M+3)/\tau\).

Otherwise every source is entirely on \(A\) or entirely on \(B\). Define

\(\displaystyle U=\sum_{\{i:y_{s_i,A}=1\}}b_i.\)

Because there is no partition, \(U\neq T/2\).

If \(U>T/2\), final support for \(B\) at either aggregator is \((T-U)/D<T/(2D)\). By monotonicity, support for \(B\) was below threshold throughout the sequence. Neither aggregator ever adopts \(B\). Final support for \(A\) exceeds threshold, so terminality forces both aggregators fully onto \(A\). Guard \(d\) consequently never has support for \(B\), while its seed always enables \(C\). Thus \(d\) is fully on \(C\).

If \(U<T/2\), the symmetric argument forces both aggregators fully onto \(B\), and guard \(c\) fully onto \(C\).

Every case supplies a full \(C\)-guard, proving the lower bound for every reachable terminal state, including states obtained through arbitrary real moves.

The bound is attained: let all sources and both aggregators adopt \(A\); let all \(g_i\) and \(c\) adopt \(A\); let \(d,e,w_1,\ldots,w_M\) adopt \(C\), in that order. Every move is legal, every type becomes full, and exactly \(M+3\) types adopt \(C\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{NO}}=\frac{M+3}{\tau}\geq\frac12.\)

This minimum is also attained.

7. Approximation consequence and running time

Assume a polynomial-time algorithm returns a reachable terminal state of spread \(V\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle V\leq\rho(\tau)\,\mathrm{OPT},\qquad \rho(\tau)=o(\tau).\)

For all sufficiently large \(\tau\), we have \(\rho(\tau)<\tau/2\). On the constructed instances:

  • In a yes-instance, \(V\leq\rho(\tau)/\tau<1/2\).
  • In a no-instance, every feasible terminal state has \(V\geq1/2\).

Comparing the returned spread with \(1/2\) therefore decides PARTITION. The same argument applies to an upper estimate satisfying \(\mathrm{OPT}\leq V\leq\rho(\tau)\mathrm{OPT}\).

For completeness, the asymptotic qualification causes no problem: instances with support below the fixed cutoff have a bounded number of PARTITION items and can be decided by enumerating their subsets in polynomial bit time.

Let \(B\) be the input bit length of the PARTITION instance. The construction has \(m=3\), \(\tau=O(n)\), and dyadic coefficients of polynomial bit length. A conservative bound, allowing a dense influence matrix, is

\(\displaystyle L=O(B^2+\tau^2\log\tau),\)

with the same bound on construction bit time. Thus an approximation algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) would yield a polynomial-time algorithm for PARTITION.

The resulting gap is

\(\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{NO}}}{\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{YES}}}=M+3\geq\frac{\tau}{2}.\)

Consequently, achieving a polynomial-time multiplicative ratio \(o(\tau)\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The reduction has strictly positive optima, so it does not rely on the convention concerning zero-valued instances.
