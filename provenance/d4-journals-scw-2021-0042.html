The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a set of alternatives \(A\) with \(|A|\le 5\) and a finite support \(S\) of complete rankings, each with positive rational mass \(\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t=1\), decide whether there are distinct alternative positions \(x_a\in\mathbb{R}\) and type positions \(y_t\in\mathbb{R}\) such that, for every \(t\in S\) and distinct \(a,b\in A\), \(a\succ_t b\) if and only if \(|x_a-y_t|<|x_b-y_t|\); return such an embedding if one exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) contain \(m\) alternatives, with \(1\le m\le5\). A society is given by its support: a list \(S\) of \(\tau\) distinct strict complete rankings \(\succ_t\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|S|\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
An embedding consists of pairwise distinct alternative positions \(x_a\in\mathbb R\) and type positions \(y_t\in\mathbb R\) satisfying \(a\succ_t b\iff |x_a-y_t|<|x_b-y_t|\) for every \(t\in S\) and distinct \(a,b\in A\). Type positions may coincide with alternative positions. Decide whether an embedding exists and return binary rational coordinates when it does.

Is this recognition problem in \(\mathrm P\), with an embedding computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is its decision version \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete profiles with at most five alternatives, one-dimensional Euclidean representability is equivalent to being single-peaked and single-crossing. [Knoblauch](https://media.economics.uconn.edu/working/2008-52.pdf) gives polynomial-time recognition and embedding construction for arbitrary finite profiles of strict complete rankings. This covers the stated embedding constraints, which impose no conditions involving masses; consequently, the stated complexity target is already settled.

  • Clarify that type positions may coincide with alternative positions, as the discrete definition restricts distinctness only among alternatives.
  • Define L as the total binary input length, including rankings and masses.

Result

The problem is in \(\mathrm P\), and an embedding can be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, whenever an embedding exists with \(m\le5\), one exists with integer alternative coordinates and type coordinates whose denominators divide \(4\).

The cited [Knoblauch paper](https://media.economics.uconn.edu/working/2008-52.pdf) gives polynomial-time recognition and construction for arbitrary finite profiles. Its Definition 2 uses precisely the strict distance comparisons required here and imposes no separation between voter and alternative positions. Below is a self-contained proof for the stated bound, including binary-coordinate guarantees.

1. Fixing the alternative order

For \(m=1\), set the alternative position and every type position to \(0\). All comparison requirements are vacuous.

Assume henceforth that \(2\le m\le5\). Enumerate every permutation \((a_1,\ldots,a_m)\) of the alternatives. For this permutation, seek an embedding satisfying

\[ x_{a_1}<\cdots<x_{a_m}. \]

For brevity, write \(x_i=x_{a_i}\). When \(i<j\), squaring the nonnegative distances gives

\[ |x_i-y|<|x_j-y| \iff (x_j-x_i)(x_i+x_j-2y)>0 \iff 2y<x_i+x_j. \]

Thus preference for the left alternative imposes an upper bound on \(2y\), while preference for the right alternative imposes a lower bound.

For each type \(t\), define

\[ \mathcal L_t=\{(i,j):i<j,\ a_j\succ_t a_i\}, \qquad \mathcal U_t=\{(i,j):i<j,\ a_i\succ_t a_j\}. \]

These sets partition all alternative pairs because each ranking is strict and complete. The required comparisons for type \(t\) are exactly

\[ 2y_t>x_i+x_j\quad((i,j)\in\mathcal L_t), \qquad 2y_t<x_k+x_\ell\quad((k,\ell)\in\mathcal U_t). \]

If both sets are nonempty, such a \(y_t\) exists exactly when every lower bound is smaller than every upper bound. If either set is empty, a sufficiently small or sufficiently large \(y_t\) always works.

Consequently, this alternative order admits an embedding if and only if the following strict linear system is feasible:

\[ x_{i+1}-x_i>0\quad(1\le i<m), \]

\[ x_k+x_\ell-x_i-x_j>0 \quad \bigl(t\in S,\ (i,j)\in\mathcal L_t,\ (k,\ell)\in\mathcal U_t\bigr). \tag{1} \]

This equivalence eliminates all type-position variables.

2. Replacing strict inequalities by unit margins

Every expression in (1) is homogeneous and invariant under translating all alternative coordinates by the same amount. We may therefore impose \(x_1=0\).

There are finitely many inequalities. If (1) is feasible, let \(\delta>0\) be the minimum of their left-hand sides at a feasible point. Dividing every coordinate by \(\delta\) makes every left-hand side at least \(1\). Conversely, a left-hand side at least \(1\) is positive.

Hence feasibility is equivalent to

\[ x_1=0,\qquad x_{i+1}-x_i\ge1, \]

\[ x_k+x_\ell-x_i-x_j\ge1 \quad \bigl(t\in S,\ (i,j)\in\mathcal L_t,\ (k,\ell)\in\mathcal U_t\bigr). \tag{2} \]

After eliminating \(x_1\), write (2) as \(Hz\ge\mathbf1\), where \(z=(x_2,\ldots,x_m)\) has dimension \(d=m-1\le4\). The entries of \(H\) are integers of absolute value at most \(2\).

Let \(N\) be its number of rows. Then

\[ N\le m-1+\tau\binom m2^2. \]

3. Exact feasibility by enumerating active constraints

We prove that, if (2) is feasible, some feasible point is uniquely determined by \(d\) linearly independent active inequalities.

Let \(P=\{z:Hz\ge\mathbf1\}\), and suppose \(z^0\in P\). The order inequalities imply \(x_i\ge i-1\); in particular, all coordinates of \(z\) are nonnegative. Therefore

\[ P\cap\left\{z:\sum_{i=2}^m x_i\le\sum_{i=2}^m x_i^0\right\} \]

is nonempty and compact. It follows that \(\sum_{i=2}^m x_i\) attains its minimum on \(P\), and its minimizer set is compact.

Within that set, minimize \(x_2\), then \(x_3\), and so on. Each minimization occurs over a nonempty compact set. After all coordinates have been fixed, the resulting point \(v\) is unique.

This point is an extreme point of \(P\): if \(v=(p+q)/2\) for \(p,q\in P\), the minimum-sum condition forces both \(p,q\) to minimize the sum. The successive coordinate-minimum conditions then force each coordinate of both points to equal the corresponding coordinate of \(v\). Thus \(p=q=v\).

The active row vectors at \(v\) must span the entire space. Otherwise, choose a nonzero vector \(w\) orthogonal to all active rows. Every inactive inequality has positive slack, so, because there are finitely many rows, sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\) makes both \(v+\varepsilon w\) and \(v-\varepsilon w\) feasible. This contradicts extremality.

Therefore the following algorithm decides feasibility exactly:

  • Enumerate every subset of \(d\) rows of \(H\).
  • Discard subsets whose row matrix \(B\) is singular.
  • Solve \(Bz=\mathbf1\) using exact rational arithmetic.
  • Accept a candidate precisely when it satisfies every row of \(Hz\ge\mathbf1\).

Every accepted candidate is feasible. The argument above proves that some candidate is accepted whenever \(P\) is nonempty.

4. Constructing binary rational coordinates

For an accepted candidate, let \(D=|\det B|\), a positive integer. Cramer’s rule shows that

\[ X_{a_1}=0,\qquad X_{a_i}=D x_i\quad(2\le i\le m) \]

are integers. Positive scaling preserves all strict inequalities, and the order constraints give

\[ X_{a_{i+1}}-X_{a_i}\ge D>0. \]

Thus the alternative positions are pairwise distinct.

For each type, when the indicated sets are nonempty, define the integers

\[ \lambda_t=\max_{(i,j)\in\mathcal L_t}(X_{a_i}+X_{a_j}), \qquad \upsilon_t=\min_{(i,j)\in\mathcal U_t}(X_{a_i}+X_{a_j}). \]

If both exist, (2) implies \(\lambda_t<\upsilon_t\). Choose

\[ Y_t= \begin{cases} (\lambda_t+\upsilon_t)/4, &\mathcal L_t\ne\varnothing,\ \mathcal U_t\ne\varnothing,\\ (\lambda_t+2)/2, &\mathcal L_t\ne\varnothing,\ \mathcal U_t=\varnothing,\\ (\upsilon_t-2)/2, &\mathcal L_t=\varnothing,\ \mathcal U_t\ne\varnothing. \end{cases} \]

In the first case, \(2Y_t\) lies strictly between the maximum lower bound and minimum upper bound. In the second or third case, it lies strictly beyond every required bound. The midpoint equivalence from Section 1 therefore proves every prescribed distance comparison.

All output coordinates are integers or quarter-integers, so they have finite binary representations. No restriction prevents a chosen type position from coinciding with an alternative.

There is also an explicit size bound. By the determinant expansion, each relevant determinant—including those with one column replaced by \(\mathbf1\)—has absolute value at most

\[ C=d!\,2^d\le384. \]

Thus \(|X_a|\le C\), and the displayed formulas give \(|Y_t|\le C+1\le385\). The output coordinates consequently require only a constant number of bits each.

5. Correctness and running time

An existing embedding orders its pairwise distinct alternative positions according to one of the enumerated permutations. Sections 1–3 guarantee that this permutation is accepted. Conversely, Sections 1 and 4 prove that every accepted permutation yields a valid embedding. Rejecting all permutations therefore correctly certifies nonexistence.

There are at most \(m!\le120\) permutations. For each, there are at most \(\binom Nd\le N^4\) candidate systems, each of dimension at most \(4\), and checking a candidate requires \(N\) inequalities. All numerical calculations involve bounded-size integers. Together with reading the input and producing the coordinates, this is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The masses impose no embedding constraints and play no role in these calculations. The decision and construction problems are therefore both polynomial-time solvable.
